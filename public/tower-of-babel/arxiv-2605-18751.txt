Kernel Characterisations of Stochastic Orders Within Parametric Density Families
Zakaria Derbazi

Abstract
We develop kernel criteria for the likelihood-ratio, hazard-rate, usual stochastic, and relative log-concavity orders in parametric families of univariate probability laws with densities. The score is the derivative of the log density with respect to the parameter, and a kernel equals the score up to an additive term depending only on the parameter. Kernel monotonicity gives likelihood-ratio order, kernel concavity gives relative log-concavity, and two tail-conditional mean inequalities give the hazard-rate and usual stochastic orders. The same construction applies along joint-parameter paths and to comparisons between two laws whose densities admit parameter-dependent factors, where the log-factor ratio is used as the kernel. For compound sums with a random number of i.i.d. terms, the induced kernel is the posterior mean of the kernel of the summand count. The applications recover standard one-parameter orderings, give likelihood-ratio comparisons for compound laws, and handle nonmonotone examples through the tail-conditional criteria.

1
Introduction

This paper develops kernel criteria for the likelihood-ratio, relative log-concavity, hazard-rate, and usual stochastic orders in parametric families of univariate probability measures. For densities differentiable in the parameter, the score is the derivative of the log density with respect to that parameter. A kernel is a function on the support whose centred version under the current law equals the score.

The kernel yields characterisations of all four stochastic orders between laws in the family, and is often simpler to analyse than the likelihood ratio. Monotonicity gives the likelihood-ratio order, concavity gives relative log-concavity, and two tail-conditional mean inequalities give the hazard-rate and the usual stochastic orders. The construction also applies to direct comparisons of two laws whose densities admit parameter-dependent factors. The difference of the two log factors then replaces the derivative of the log density with respect to the parameter in the same tests.

The kernel lives at the level of parameter-dependent factors, before centring produces the score. This makes the same criteria portable to joint-parameter paths, pairwise factor-form comparisons, and compound laws.

Our main contributions are as follows:

(i)

Kernel criteria for comparing two members of the same parametric family in the likelihood-ratio, relative log-concavity, hazard-rate, and usual stochastic orders, via a uniform four-order characterisation in terms of the kernel and its tail-conditional means (Proposition

(ii)

A superlevel-set criterion that establishes hazard-rate and usual stochastic ordering when the kernel is nonmonotone or nonconcave (Proposition

, Corollary
3.9
).

(iii)

Extensions to joint-parameter and interpolation paths, and to comparisons of laws not belonging to a common parametric family, where the relevant kernel is either the path derivative or the difference of two log factors.

A compound-construction extension by which the compound law inherits a kernel from the underlying counting law through a posterior-averaging identity, extending the compound-geometric comparison of

Notation and Preliminaries

2.1
Conventions

Throughout, the probability measures under consideration are dominated by a
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
on a totally ordered space
E
, and densities are understood as Radon–Nikodym derivatives with respect to
\mu
. The standard choices are counting measure on an integer interval and Lebesgue measure on an interval of
\mathbb{R}
. We write
\delta_{x}
for the unit point mass at
x
. In examples with both an atom at zero and an absolutely continuous component, we use the dominating measure
\delta_{0}+{\mathrm{d}}{x}
. For a probability measure
P
on
E
we write
f_{P}\coloneqq{\mathrm{d}}{}P/{\mathrm{d}}{\mu}
for its density,
\bar{F}_{P}(x)\coloneqq P([x,\infty)\cap E)
for its survival function,
h_{P}(x)\coloneqq f_{P}(x)/\bar{F}_{P}(x)
for its hazard rate (where
\bar{F}_{P}(x)>0
), and
\supp(P)\coloneqq\{f_{P}>0\}
up to
\mu
-null sets.

For a pair of probability measures
P,Q
, set
\rho\coloneqq P+Q
and
S\coloneqq\supp(\rho)
. Their likelihood ratio is

\ell(x)\coloneqq\frac{({\mathrm{d}}{}P/{\mathrm{d}}{}\rho)(x)}{({\mathrm{d}}{}Q/{\mathrm{d}}{}\rho)(x)},\qquad x\in S,

(1)

with the conventions
a/0\coloneqq+\infty
for
a>0
and
0/0\coloneqq 0
. Where both
\mu
-densities are positive, this agrees with the ordinary density ratio
f_{P}/f_{Q}
.

A parametric family of probability measures is written
(P_{\vartheta})_{\vartheta\in\mathcal{V}}
, where
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is specified by a corresponding family of
\mu
-densities
(f_{\vartheta})_{\vartheta\in\mathcal{V}}
, so that for measurable
A\subseteq E

P_{\vartheta}(A)=\int_{A}f_{\vartheta}(x)\,\mu({\mathrm{d}}{x}).

We reserve
parametric family
for the indexed probability measures being compared, and
density family
for their densities with respect to
\mu
. Terms such as exponential family, generalised power-series family (abbreviated GPS), and generalised hypergeometric family are used in their usual sense, as classes of parametric families with a common density or mass-function structure.

The criteria below are one-dimensional. Thus, when
d>1
, all parameters except the one under consideration are held fixed, and the resulting parametric density family is written
(f_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{I}}
, with associated probability measures
(P_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{I}}
and
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an interval. If several parameters vary together, we write the resulting one-parameter path as
(P_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{T}}
, with
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an interval. The common support of the laws under comparison is denoted
J\subseteq E
. We use
X,Y
for random variables with values in
E
, distributed according to the law under consideration, and take expectations under that law.

We write
\mathbb{N}_{0}\coloneqq\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
. In the discrete case, sets of the form
[a,b]\coloneqq\{a,\dots,b\}
and
[a,\infty)\coloneqq\{a,a{+}1,\dots\}
are intervals in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Forward differences are
\Delta h(k)\coloneqq h(k+1)-h(k)
and
\Delta^{2}h(k)\coloneqq h(k+2)-2h(k+1)+h(k)
. We call
k
admissible for
\Delta^{2}
when
k,k+1,k+2
all lie in the support. We use the Pochhammer symbol
(a)_{k}\coloneqq a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1)
with
(a)_{0}\coloneqq 1
. Recall that
\partial_{a}\log(a)_{k}=\psi(a+k)-\psi(a)
, where
\psi(x)\coloneqq\Gamma^{\prime}(x)/\Gamma(x)
is the digamma function.
The indicator of a set
A\subseteq E
is
\mathds{1}\{A\}
.

Assumption 2.1

(Differentiation under the integral)
.

For every parametric density family
(f_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{I}}
considered below, with associated probability measures
(P_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{I}}
and
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an interval, the density
f_{\nu}(x)
is positive on a common interval
J\subseteq E
and
\nu\mapsto\log f_{\nu}(x)
is
C^{1}
on
\mathcal{I}
for every
x\in J
. For every compact interval
B\subseteq\mathcal{I}
, there exists a majorant
m\in L^{1}(J,\mu)
such that

\sup_{\nu\in B}\bigl|\partial_{\nu}f_{\nu}(x)\bigr|\leq m(x)\qquad\mu\text{-a.e.}

This justifies differentiation under the integral (or sum) sign, which we use without further mention. In particular, once the score is defined in Section
3
, the maps
\nu\mapsto s_{\nu}(x)
and
\nu\mapsto\partial_{\nu}\log\bar{F}_{\nu}(x)
are continuous at every fixed
x\in J
with
\bar{F}_{\nu}(x)>0
. This continuity is deployed in the necessity direction of Theorem
3.5
.

2.2
Shape notions and stochastic orders

Definition 2.2

(Log-concavity, PF
2
, and TP
2
)
.

A positive function
a:J\to(0,\infty)
on an interval
J\subseteq E
is
log-concave
if
\log a
is concave on
J
. In the discrete case this is equivalent to
\Delta^{2}\log a(k)\leq 0
for every admissible
k\in J
and in the continuous case it is equivalent to
(\log a)^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
wherever the second derivative exists.

For a sequence
b=(b_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we say that
b
is a
Pólya frequency sequence of order 2
(PF
2
) if
b_{k}\geq 0
for all
k
, the support
\{k:b_{k}>0\}
is an interval in
\mathbb{Z}
, and
b
is log-concave on its support. A probability distribution
F
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
is PF
2
if its mass sequence, extended by zero outside its support, is PF
2
.

A nonnegative kernel
M:I\times J\to[0,\infty)
on two totally ordered sets is
totally positive of order 2
(TP
2
) if

M(i,j)M(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\geq M(i,j^{\prime})M(i^{\prime},j)

whenever
i\leq i^{\prime}
and
j\leq j^{\prime}
. See
[
3
]
for the general total positivity theory.

We recall the four orders used throughout. See
[
7
,
8
]
for further background.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
P
and
Q
be probability measures on
E
, with likelihood ratio
\ell
as in (
1
).

(i)

Usual stochastic order
(
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
):
P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
if

\bar{F}_{P}(x)\leq\bar{F}_{Q}(x)
for every
x\in E
.

(ii)

Hazard-rate order
(
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
):
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q
if
\bar{F}_{P}(x)/\bar{F}_{Q}(x)
is nonincreasing wherever
\bar{F}_{Q}(x)>0
(equivalently,
h_{P}(x)\geq h_{Q}(x)
wherever both hazard rates are defined).

(iii)

Likelihood-ratio order
(
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
):
P\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q
if
\ell
is nonincreasing on
\supp(P)\cup\supp(Q)
.

(iv)

Relative log-concavity
(
\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}
): when
\supp(P)
is an interval contained in
\supp(Q)
,
P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q
if
\log\ell
is concave on
\supp(P)
.

In Section
3
, the criteria are stated in the order
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}},\leq_{\mathrm{lc}},\leq_{\mathrm{st}},\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
, matching the corresponding kernel conditions: monotonicity, concavity, and two tail-conditional inequalities.

3
Kernel Criteria and Compound Laws

Throughout this section, let
(f_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{I}}
be a parametric density family satisfying Assumption
2.1
, with associated probability measures
(P_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{I}}
and common support interval
J\subseteq E
. The
score
is

s_{\nu}(x)\coloneqq\partial_{\nu}\log f_{\nu}(x),\qquad x\in J,

(2)

and is centred under
P_{\nu}
:

\int_{J}s_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}=0.

A
kernel
(with respect to
P_{\nu}
) is any measurable,
P_{\nu}
-integrable function
K_{\nu}:J\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

s_{\nu}(x)=K_{\nu}(x)-\int_{J}K_{\nu}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}(y),\qquad x\in J.

(3)

Since the centring term
\int_{J}K_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}
is a constant independent of
x
,
K_{\nu}
and
s_{\nu}
have the same properties on
J
whenever those properties are invariant under additive constants. The score itself is the unique centred kernel. Kernels are determined only up to addition of a function of the parameter. We state the ordering criteria in kernel form and pass to the score whenever a density-level proof is more convenient.

Remark 3.1
.

Suppose the density can be expressed as
f_{\nu}(x)={w_{\nu}(x)}/{A(\nu)}
, where
A(\nu)\coloneqq\int_{J}w_{\nu}(y)\,\mu({\mathrm{d}}{y})
. Then

K_{\nu}(x)\coloneqq\partial_{\nu}\log w_{\nu}(x),\qquad x\in J,

(4)

is a kernel. Indeed, differentiating
A(\nu)=\int_{J}w_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mu}
under the integral gives
\partial_{\nu}\log A(\nu)=\int_{J}K_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}
, hence

s_{\nu}(x)=\partial_{\nu}\log w_{\nu}(x)-\partial_{\nu}\log A(\nu)=K_{\nu}(x)-\int_{J}K_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}.

3.1
Density, survival, and hazard identities

Definition 3.2

(Regularity condition)
.

A measurable function
u:J\to[0,\infty)
satisfies the
regularity condition
if, for every compact interval
B\subseteq\mathcal{I}
, the integral
\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}
is finite and positive for every
\nu\in B
, and there exists a majorant
m\in L^{1}(J,\mu)
such that

\sup_{\nu\in B}\bigl|u(x)\,\partial_{\nu}f_{\nu}(x)\bigr|\leq m(x)\qquad\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in J.

By Assumption
2.1
, every bounded measurable
u
satisfies the majorant condition of Definition
3.2
. Thus, tail indicators
u_{y}(x)=\mathds{1}\{x\geq y\}
satisfy the regularity condition whenever
\bar{F}_{\nu}(y)>0
for every
\nu
in the compact parameter interval under consideration.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
u:J\to[0,\infty)
satisfies the regularity condition, and write
P_{\nu}^{u}
for the probability measure defined by

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}^{u}}{{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}}(x)=\frac{u(x)}{\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}},\qquad x\in J.

Then

\partial_{\nu}\log\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}=\int_{J}s_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}^{u}=\int_{J}K_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}^{u}-\int_{J}K_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}.

(5)

If
v:J\to[0,\infty)
also satisfies the same regularity condition, and
P_{\nu}^{v}
is defined analogously, then

\partial_{\nu}\log\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}}{\displaystyle\int_{J}v\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}}=\int_{J}s_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}^{u}-\int_{J}s_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}^{v}=\int_{J}K_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}^{u}-\int_{J}K_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}^{v}.

Proof.

Since the regularity condition on
u
justifies differentiating under the integral sign, it follows that

\partial_{\nu}\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}=\int_{J}u(x)\,\partial_{\nu}f_{\nu}(x)\,\mu({\mathrm{d}}{x})=\int_{J}u(x)\,s_{\nu}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}.

Dividing by
\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}
yields

\partial_{\nu}\log\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}=\frac{\int_{J}u(x)\,s_{\nu}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}}{\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}}=\int_{J}s_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}^{u}.

Substituting (
3
) gives the kernel form. The general case follows by applying (
5
) to
u
and

v
and taking the difference.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be a random variable with law
P_{\nu}
. For every
x\in J
with
\bar{F}_{\nu}(x)>0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\log f_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=s_{\nu}(x)=K_{\nu}(x)-\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)],

(6)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\log\bar{F}_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x]=\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x]-\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)],

(7)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\log h_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=s_{\nu}(x)-\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x]=K_{\nu}(x)-\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x].

(8)

Proof.

The density-level identity corresponds to definitions (
2
) and (
3
) expressed through
X
. For the survival identity, take
u(y)=\mathds{1}\{y\geq x\}
in Proposition
3.3
. Then
\int_{J}u\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\nu}=\bar{F}_{\nu}(x),

and the corresponding new law is the conditional law of
X
given
X\geq x
. Hence
\partial_{\nu}\log\bar{F}_{\nu}(x)=\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x].
Substituting
s_{\nu}
with its expression in (
6
) gives the right-hand side of (
7
).
Finally, subtract (
7
) from (
6
) to get (
8
). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma
3.4
expresses each of the density, survival, and hazard derivatives as a difference between two of the three quantities
K_{\nu}(x)
,
\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x]
, and
\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)]
. Integrating these local identities over a parameter interval gives the pairwise comparison functions

L_{\nu_{2},\nu_{1}}(x)\coloneqq\log\frac{f_{\nu_{2}}(x)}{f_{\nu_{1}}(x)},\quad T_{\nu_{2},\nu_{1}}(x)\coloneqq\log\frac{\bar{F}_{\nu_{2}}(x)}{\bar{F}_{\nu_{1}}(x)},\quad H_{\nu_{2},\nu_{1}}(x)\coloneqq\log\frac{h_{\nu_{2}}(x)}{h_{\nu_{1}}(x)},

defined wherever the corresponding denominators are positive. In particular, for
\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}
in

and

, the log-likelihood ratio is given by

L_{\nu_{2},\nu_{1}}(x)=\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}s_{\nu}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}{\nu}=\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\bigl(K_{\nu}(x)-\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)]\bigr)\,{\mathrm{d}}{\nu},

(9)

with analogous representations of

and

via the kernel and its tail-conditional means. These representations lead to the ordering criteria of the next subsection.

3.2
Comparing laws in parametric families

Theorem 3.5
.

(i)

P_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}P_{\nu_{2}}
for all

in

if and only if

.

(ii)

and

3.4
, this is the value of
\partial_{\nu}\log\bar{F}_{\nu}(x_{0})
at
\nu=\nu_{0}
, which is continuous in
\nu
. Hence there is an
\varepsilon>0
and an interval
I_{\varepsilon}
containing
\nu_{0}
such that
\partial_{\nu}\log\bar{F}_{\nu}(x_{0})<-\varepsilon
for every
\nu\in I_{\varepsilon}
. Choosing
\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}
in
I_{\varepsilon}
gives
T_{\nu_{2},\nu_{1}}(x_{0})<0
, contradicting
P_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\nu_{2}}
.

Assertion
(iv)
.
Suppose
P_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\nu_{2}}
for all
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
and assume
K_{\nu_{0}}(x_{0})-\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu_{0}}(X)\mid X\geq x_{0}]>0
at some
(\nu_{0},x_{0})
with
\bar{F}_{\nu_{0}}(x_{0})>0
. By (
3
) and (
11
), this difference equals

s_{\nu_{0}}(x_{0})-\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu_{0}}(X)\mid X\geq x_{0}]=\partial_{\nu}\log h_{\nu}(x_{0})\Big|_{\nu=\nu_{0}}

by Lemma
3.4
, which is continuous in
\nu
. Hence there is an
\varepsilon>0
and an interval
I_{\varepsilon}
containing
\nu_{0}
such that
\partial_{\nu}\log h_{\nu}(x_{0})>\varepsilon
for every
\nu\in I_{\varepsilon}
. Choosing
\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}
in
I_{\varepsilon}
then contradicts
H_{\nu_{2},\nu_{1}}(x_{0})\leq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

The simplest sufficient condition combines part (i) of Theorem
3.5
with the standard chain
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\Rightarrow\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}\Rightarrow\leq_{\mathrm{st}}

[
7
, Theorem 1.C.1]
.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
x\mapsto K_{\nu}(x)
, equivalently
x\mapsto s_{\nu}(x)
, is nondecreasing for every
\nu\in\mathcal{I}
, then for all
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
in
\mathcal{I}
,

P_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}P_{\nu_{2}},\qquad P_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\nu_{2}},\qquad P_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\nu_{2}}.

If
K_{\nu}
is nonincreasing for every
\nu
, the three orders reverse.

Proof.

Assertion (i) of Theorem
3.5
gives
P_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}P_{\nu_{2}}
. The chain
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\Rightarrow\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}\Rightarrow\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
extends to the remaining orders.
∎

3.3
Beyond monotone and concave kernels

Theorem
3.5
(i) and (ii) characterise
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}
through monotonicity and concavity of
K_{\nu}
on
J
. When these global shape properties fail, the tail-conditional criteria (iii) and (iv) can still yield
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
. The next results give shape conditions on the score
s_{\nu}
or kernel
K_{\nu}
that imply these tail-conditional inequalities. The superlevel-set, concave-kernel, and unimodal-kernel hypotheses below are kernel-level analogues of pairwise conditions on the likelihood ratio in
[
2
]
. The half-Student and zero-inflated examples of Section
4.2
illustrate each in turn.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
in
\mathcal{I}
, and assume
J
has finite left endpoint
x_{0}\coloneqq\inf J
. If for every
\nu\in[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]
, the set
A_{\nu}\coloneqq\{x\in J:s_{\nu}(x)\geq 0\}
is a nonempty initial interval of
J
, then

P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\nu_{1}}.

If, in addition,
s_{\nu}
is nonincreasing on
J\setminus A_{\nu}
for every
\nu\in[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]
, then

P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\nu_{1}}.

Proof.

Fix
\nu\in[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]
. Since
A_{\nu}
is a nonempty initial interval of
J
, its complement
J\setminus A_{\nu}
is a final interval. Thus,
s_{\nu}
is nonnegative on
A_{\nu}
and negative on its complement. Consequently,
s_{\nu}
has at most one sign change on
J
, from nonnegative to negative. Since
\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}(X)]=0
for
X\sim P_{\nu}
, Lemma 2.6 of
[
2
]
, applied with
\phi=s_{\nu}
under
P_{\nu}
, gives

\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x]\leq 0

for every
x\in J
with
\bar{F}_{\nu}(x)>0
.

Now, by Lemma
3.4
, the left-hand side equals
\partial_{\nu}\log\bar{F}_{\nu}(x)
. Integrating over
[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]
gives
\bar{F}_{\nu_{2}}(x)\leq\bar{F}_{\nu_{1}}(x)
, which is
P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\nu_{1}}
.

For the hazard-rate part, we need to show that
s_{\nu}(x)\geq\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x]
holds pointwise on
\{x\in J:\bar{F}_{\nu}(x)>0\}
. If
x\in A_{\nu}
, then the tail-mean bound proved in the first part gives
\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x]\leq 0\leq s_{\nu}(x)
. If
x\in J\setminus A_{\nu}
, then every
u\geq x
also lies in
J\setminus A_{\nu}
, and the right-tail monotonicity hypothesis gives
s_{\nu}(u)\leq s_{\nu}(x)
. In either case,
\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}(X)\mid X\geq x]\leq s_{\nu}(x)
. By Lemma
3.4
, the difference equals
\partial_{\nu}\log h_{\nu}(x)
, and integrating over
[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]
gives
h_{\nu_{2}}(x)\geq h_{\nu_{1}}(x)
, which is
P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\nu_{1}}
, as required.
∎

The superlevel-set hypothesis is the natural shape condition on
s_{\nu}
at fixed
\nu
. Two corollaries follow, recovering kernel-level versions of the endpoint reductions of
[
2
]
under relative log-concavity and under unimodality of the likelihood ratio.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
in
\mathcal{I}
. Suppose
K_{\nu}
is concave on
J
and that
J
has finite left endpoint
x_{0}\coloneqq\inf J
. If
s_{\nu}(x_{0})\geq 0
for every
\nu\in[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]
, then
P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\nu_{1}}
and
P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\nu_{1}}
.

Proof.

By definition,
s_{\nu}
inherits concavity from
K_{\nu}
and is centred under
P_{\nu}
. A superlevel set of a concave function on an interval is an interval. Since
s_{\nu}(x_{0})\geq 0
, the set
A_{\nu}=\{s_{\nu}\geq 0\}
is therefore a nonempty initial interval of
J
. Concavity then implies that
s_{\nu}
is nonincreasing on
J\setminus A_{\nu}
. Invoking Proposition
3.7
concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
in
\mathcal{I}
, and suppose
J
has finite left endpoint
x_{0}\coloneqq\inf J
. Suppose further that there exists a single point
c\in J
, independent of
\nu
, such that for every
\nu\in[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]
:

(i)

K_{\nu}
is nondecreasing on
J\cap(-\infty,c]
.

(ii)

K_{\nu}
is nonincreasing on
J\cap[c,\infty)
.

(iii)

s_{\nu}(x_{0})\geq 0
.

Then
P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\nu_{1}}
and
P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\nu_{1}}
.

Proof.

The score is nondecreasing on
J\cap(-\infty,c]
and nonincreasing on
J\cap[c,\infty)
, because subtracting a constant does not affect either monotonicity property. With
s_{\nu}(x_{0})\geq 0
and
\mathbb{E}[s_{\nu}]=0
, the set
A_{\nu}=\{s_{\nu}\geq 0\}
is a nonempty initial interval of
J
. Therefore,
s_{\nu}
is nonincreasing on
J\setminus A_{\nu}\subseteq J\cap[c,\infty)
. Now invoke Proposition
3.7
to complete the proof.
∎

3.4
Compound-law kernels

The compound laws considered here are discrete, so throughout this subsection we take
E=\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Definition 3.10

(Compound laws)
.

Let
Q
and
F
be probability measures on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\mathbb{N}
, respectively.

Define

X=\sum_{i=1}^{N}J_{i}

(12)

where
N
has law
Q
, and
(J_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with law
F
, each independent of
N
.

Let
q_{n}\coloneqq Q(\{n\})
. Conditioning on
N
gives

\mathbb{P}(X=k)=\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}\,F^{*n}(\{k\}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
F^{*0}\coloneqq\delta_{0}
and
F^{*n}\coloneqq F^{*(n-1)}*F
for
n\geq 1
.

For a family of probability measures
(Q_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{I}}
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and a fixed summand law
F
on
\mathbb{N}
, write
C_{\nu}
for the law of the corresponding compound sum and
f_{\nu}\coloneqq dC_{\nu}/d\mu
for its mass function.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
X
be the compound sum defined in
(
12
)
, and assume
F
is PF
2
. Then the posterior kernel
(n,k)\mapsto\mathbb{P}(N=n\mid X=k)
is TP
2
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\supp(X)
. In particular, the conditional laws of
N
given
X=k
are stochastically increasing in
k
, and
k\mapsto\mathbb{E}[N\mid X=k]
is nondecreasing on
\supp(X)
.

Proof.

Since
F
is PF
2
, its convolution powers
M(n,k)\coloneqq F^{*n}(\{k\})
,
n,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, form a TP
2
kernel
[
3
]
. Multiplying the
n
th row of
M
by
q_{n}
preserves the TP
2
property, and normalising each column preserves TP
2
as well. Hence the posterior kernel is TP
2
, so the posterior laws of
N
given
X=k
are increasing in
k
in the monotone likelihood-ratio order, and therefore stochastically increasing. Their means are consequently nondecreasing.
∎

The compound law inherits a kernel from the counting law
Q_{\nu}
. The general statement uses only the score of
Q_{\nu}
and produces the compound score. When
Q_{\nu}
is a member of the power-series family or admits a factorisation involving a parameter-dependent function, the same construction transports to a kernel.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(Q_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathcal{I}}
be a family of probability measures on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
with common support
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}
, so that
q_{\nu}(n)\coloneqq Q_{\nu}(\{n\})>0
for
n\in S
. Denote the density of the compound law
C_{\nu}
by

f_{\nu}(k)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in S}q_{\nu}(n)F^{*n}(\{k\}).

If
\nu\mapsto q_{\nu}(n)
is differentiable on
\mathcal{I}
for every
n\in S
, then for
k
in the common support of
C_{\nu}
, the compound score is

s_{\nu}(k)\coloneqq\partial_{\nu}\log f_{\nu}(k)=\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{\nu}(N)\mid X=k],

(13)

where
\gamma_{\nu}(n)\coloneqq\partial_{\nu}\log q_{\nu}(n)
and the expectation is taken under the joint compound law at the current parameter value.

Proof.

For fixed
k
, the sum defining
f_{\nu}(k)
is finite, because
F^{*n}(\{k\})=0
for
n>k
. Since the atoms of
Q_{\nu}
are differentiable in
\nu
, differentiating
f_{\nu}(k)
gives

\partial_{\nu}f_{\nu}(k)=\sum_{n\in S}\gamma_{\nu}(n)q_{\nu}(n)F^{*n}(\{k\}).

For
k
in the support of
C_{\nu}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(N=n\mid X=k)=\frac{q_{\nu}(n)F^{*n}(\{k\})}{f_{\nu}(k)}.

Dividing the derivative identity by
f_{\nu}(k)
yields

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\log f_{\nu}(k)=\sum_{n\in S}\gamma_{\nu}(n)\frac{q_{\nu}(n)F^{*n}(\{k\})}{f_{\nu}(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in S}\gamma_{\nu}(n)\mathbb{P}(N=n\mid X=k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{\nu}(N)\mid X=k].

∎

Corollary 3.13
.

Suppose the hypotheses of Proposition
3.12
hold and that
q_{\nu}(n)=w_{n}(\nu)/A(\nu)
, where
w_{n}(\nu)>0
for
n\in S
,
A(\nu)=\sum_{n\in S}w_{n}(\nu)
, and
\partial_{\nu}A(\nu)=\sum_{n\in S}\partial_{\nu}w_{n}(\nu)
. Set
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(n)\coloneqq\partial_{\nu}\log w_{n}(\nu)
. Then

K_{\nu}(k)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(N)\mid X=k]

is a kernel of
C_{\nu}
.

Proof.

Since
q_{\nu}(n)=w_{n}(\nu)/A(\nu)
, the score of
Q_{\nu}
is
\gamma_{\nu}(n)=\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(n)-\partial_{\nu}\log A(\nu).
Proposition
3.12
therefore implies that

s_{\nu}(k)=\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{\nu}(N)\mid X=k]=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(N)\mid X=k]-\partial_{\nu}\log A(\nu)=K_{\nu}(k)-\partial_{\nu}\log A(\nu).

The differentiability condition on
A
gives

\partial_{\nu}\log A(\nu)=\sum_{n\in S}\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(n)q_{\nu}(n)=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(N)]=\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)].

Hence
K_{\nu}
is a kernel of
C_{\nu}
.
∎

The compound kernel
K_{\nu}
is the posterior expectation of
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}
. Since centring does not affect monotonicity, the compound score and compound kernel have the same monotonicity properties in
k
. Combining Lemma
3.11
with Proposition
3.12
or Corollary
3.13
gives a likelihood-ratio criterion for the compound law in terms of the score or kernel of
Q_{\nu}
alone.

Proposition 3.14
.

Assume the summand law
F
is PF
2
, and that the hypotheses of Corollary
3.13
hold.

(i)

If
n\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(n)
is nondecreasing on
S
for every
\nu\in\mathcal{I}
, then
C_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}C_{\nu_{2}}
for
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
.

(ii)

If
n\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(n)
is nonincreasing on
S
for every
\nu\in\mathcal{I}
, then
C_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}C_{\nu_{1}}
for
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.11
, the conditional laws of
N
given
X=k
are stochastically increasing in
k
. Hence, the conditional expectation of any nondecreasing function of
N
is nondecreasing in
k

[
7
, Theorem 1.C.5]
. By Corollary
3.13
,
K_{\nu}(k)=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(N)\mid X=k]
, which is therefore nondecreasing in
k
under hypothesis (i) and nonincreasing in
k
under (ii).

For (i), fix
k_{1}\leq k_{2}
in the common support of
C_{\nu}
, and set

G_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\nu)\coloneqq\log f_{\nu}(k_{2})-\log f_{\nu}(k_{1}).

Since
K_{\nu}
is nondecreasing in
k
and
s_{\nu}=K_{\nu}-\mathbb{E}[K_{\nu}(X)]
, Proposition
3.12
gives

G^{\prime}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\nu)=s_{\nu}(k_{2})-s_{\nu}(k_{1})\geq 0.

Thus
G_{k_{1},k_{2}}
is nondecreasing in
\nu
. For
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
,

\log\frac{f_{\nu_{2}}(k_{2})}{f_{\nu_{1}}(k_{2})}-\log\frac{f_{\nu_{2}}(k_{1})}{f_{\nu_{1}}(k_{1})}=G_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\nu_{2})-G_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\nu_{1})\geq 0.

Hence
f_{\nu_{2}}/f_{\nu_{1}}
is nondecreasing, equivalently
f_{\nu_{1}}/f_{\nu_{2}}
is nonincreasing, so
C_{\nu_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}C_{\nu_{2}}
. The proof of (ii) is identical with all inequalities reversed, giving
C_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}C_{\nu_{1}}
.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

When
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}
is affine,
\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(n)=a(\nu)+b(\nu)n
, Corollary
3.13
and Lemma
3.11
give the explicit form

and monotonicity of

reduces to the sign of

. The five classical counting laws fall under this case (Section
4.5
). Harmonic kernels such as
\psi(\alpha+n)-\psi(\alpha)
, which are nondecreasing in

without being affine, fall under Proposition
3.14
directly.

4.1
Ordering in parametric density families

To make the relation between score and kernel explicit, each template writes
f_{\nu}=w_{\nu}/Z(\nu)
and reads off the score
s_{\nu}=\partial_{\nu}\log f_{\nu}
and the kernel
K_{\nu}=\partial_{\nu}\log w_{\nu}
.

(i)

Natural parameter

\nu
, through
w_{\nu}(x)=h(x)\exp\{\nu T(x)\}
and
Z(\nu)=e^{A(\nu)}
:

s_{\nu}(x)=T(x)-A^{\prime}(\nu),\qquad K_{\nu}(x)=T(x),

the sufficient statistic. The normalisation term
A^{\prime}(\nu)=\mathbb{E}[T(X)]
is the centring constant.

(ii)

Power, rate, or scale parameter
. In the discrete case the parameter-dependent factor is
w_{\theta}(k)=a(k)\theta^{k}
with
Z(\theta)=\sum_{k}a(k)\theta^{k}
, so

s_{\theta}(k)=\frac{k-\mathbb{E}[X]}{\theta},\qquad K_{\theta}(k)=\frac{k}{\theta},

the affine power-series kernel. In the continuous rate case the parameter-dependent factor is
w_{\rho}(x)=h(x)e^{-\rho R(x)}
with
Z(\rho)=\int h\,e^{-\rho R}\,{\mathrm{d}}{x}
, so

s_{\rho}(x)=-R(x)+\mathbb{E}[R(X)],\qquad K_{\rho}(x)=-R(x).

In the continuous scale case
f_{\sigma}(x)=\sigma^{-1}f_{0}(x/\sigma)
on
(0,\infty)
, take
w_{\sigma}(x)=f_{0}(x/\sigma)
, with the
\sigma^{-1}
prefactor represented by
Z(\sigma)=\sigma
. Then

s_{\sigma}(x)=-\tfrac{1}{\sigma}-\tfrac{x}{\sigma^{2}}(\log f_{0})^{\prime}(x/\sigma),\qquad K_{\sigma}(x)=-\tfrac{x}{\sigma^{2}}(\log f_{0})^{\prime}(x/\sigma).

(iii)

Shape parameter

\nu
, through a shape-dependent factor
w_{\nu}
, for instance an upper Pochhammer block
(\nu)_{k}
:

s_{\nu}(x)=\partial_{\nu}\log w_{\nu}(x)-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{\nu}\log w_{\nu}(X)],\qquad K_{\nu}(x)=\partial_{\nu}\log w_{\nu}(x).

When
\nu
enters through an upper Pochhammer block, the kernel evaluates to
\psi(\nu+k)-\psi(\nu)
, with lower and finite-support variants in Table
1
. The continuous analogue is the logarithmic kernel
\log x
.

(iv)

Location parameter

\mu
, through the translate
w_{\mu}(x)=f_{0}(x-\mu)
. The translate has
Z(\mu)=1
independent of
\mu
, so the normalisation term vanishes and score and kernel coincide:

s_{\mu}(x)=K_{\mu}(x)=-(\log f_{0})^{\prime}(x-\mu).

The discrete case replaces the derivative by the forward difference of
\log f_{0}
.

(Standard discrete orderings)

4.2
Beyond monotone-kernel applications

When the kernel is neither monotone nor concave on the full support, the
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}
routes are unavailable but the criteria of Section
3.3
can still deliver
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
. Two patterns recur. The half-line pattern restricts to
[0,\infty)
or to an interval where the kernel is unimodal with a positive value at the boundary, bringing Corollary
3.9
into reach. The tail-conditional pattern verifies the inequalities of Theorem
3.5
(iii)–(iv) directly, comparing the kernel value at
m
with its conditional expectation on
\{X\geq m\}
. This pattern fits zero-inflated laws, where atom-inflation breaks monotonicity of the kernel at the origin while the tail-conditional inequalities remain explicit.

Example 4.3

(Half-Student in degrees of freedom)
.

Let
(P_{\nu})_{\nu>0}
be the half-Student family on
E=[0,\infty)
, with density

f_{\nu}(x)=\frac{2\,\Gamma((\nu+1)/2)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\,\Gamma(\nu/2)}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{x^{2}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{-(\nu+1)/2},\qquad x\geq 0.

A direct calculation gives

(s_{\nu})^{\prime}(x)=\frac{x(1-x^{2})}{(\nu+x^{2})^{2}},\qquad x>0,

so
s_{\nu}
is unimodal in
x
on
[0,\infty)
with common mode
c=1
for every
\nu>0
. Writing
z=\nu/2
,

s_{\nu}(0)=\partial_{\nu}\log f_{\nu}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\{\psi(z+\tfrac{1}{2})-\psi(z)\}-\frac{1}{4z}>0,

because concavity of
\psi
and
\psi(z+1)-\psi(z)=1/z
imply
\psi(z+\tfrac{1}{2})-\psi(z)>1/(2z)
. Both hypotheses of Corollary
3.9
(common-mode unimodality and
s_{\nu}(x_{0})>0
) hold uniformly in
\nu
, so

\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\nu_{1}}\text{ and }P_{\nu_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\nu_{1}}.

On
\mathbb{R}
the underlying Student-
t
kernel is symmetric and neither monotone nor concave, so the global criteria of Theorem
3.5
do not apply.

Example 4.4

(Zero-inflated Poisson)
.

Take the Poisson base law
Q_{\theta}=\mathrm{Poi}(\theta)
with
\theta>0
, and use the superscript
Q
to mark its kernel and score. A kernel of
Q_{\theta}
is the affine GPS kernel
K_{\theta}^{Q}(k)=k/\theta
, nondecreasing in
k
. The Poisson family is therefore
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
-,
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
-, and
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
-monotone in
\theta
. Its score is
s_{\theta}^{Q}(k)=k/\theta-1
, so
c_{Q}(\theta)=K_{\theta}^{Q}(k)-s_{\theta}^{Q}(k)=1
. Zero-inflation gives, for
\pi\in(0,1)
,

f_{\theta}(0)=(1-\pi)+\pi e^{-\theta},\qquad f_{\theta}(k)=\pi e^{-\theta}\theta^{k}/k!\quad(k\geq 1).

Differentiating in
\theta
gives the score of the zero-inflated law. Adding the constant
c_{Q}(\theta)=1
produces a kernel that agrees with
K_{\theta}^{Q}
at every
k\geq 1
:

K_{\theta}(k)=k/\theta\quad(k\geq 1),\qquad K_{\theta}(0)=1-\alpha_{\theta},\qquad\alpha_{\theta}\coloneqq\frac{\pi e^{-\theta}}{(1-\pi)+\pi e^{-\theta}}.

Now
1-\alpha_{\theta}=(1-\pi)e^{\theta}/[(1-\pi)e^{\theta}+\pi]
, so as
\theta\to\infty
,
1-\alpha_{\theta}\to 1
and
K_{\theta}(0)\to 1
, while
K_{\theta}(1)=1/\theta\to 0
. For large
\theta
,
K_{\theta}(0)>K_{\theta}(1)
, so
\Delta K_{\theta}
changes sign and the zero-inflated family is not
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
-ordered in
\theta
. Similarly
\Delta^{2}K_{\theta}(0)>0
rules out
\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}
. The
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
directions can nevertheless be checked directly from Theorem
3.5
(iii)–(iv). Since
K_{\theta}
is a kernel,
\mathbb{E}[K_{\theta}(X)]=1
. At
m=0
,
\mathbb{E}[K_{\theta}(X)\mid X\geq 0]=1
. For
m\geq 1
, if
N\sim\mathrm{Poi}(\theta)
, the event
\{X\geq m\}
excludes the inflated atom, so

\mathbb{E}[K_{\theta}(X)\mid X\geq m]=\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbb{E}[N\mid N\geq m]=\frac{\mathbb{P}(N\geq m-1)}{\mathbb{P}(N\geq m)}\geq 1.

Thus condition (iii) holds. Moreover,

K_{\theta}(m)=\frac{m}{\theta}\leq\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbb{E}[N\mid N\geq m]=\mathbb{E}[K_{\theta}(X)\mid X\geq m]\qquad(m\geq 1),

while
K_{\theta}(0)=1-\alpha_{\theta}\leq 1
, so condition (iv) holds as well. Therefore
P_{\theta_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\theta_{2}}
and
P_{\theta_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\theta_{2}}
for all
\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}
, by Theorem
3.5
(iii)–(iv).

Example 4.5

(Zero-inflated exponential)
.

The continuous analogue takes the base law
Q_{\theta}=\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)
on
(0,\infty)
and forms the atom-inflated law

P_{\theta}=(1-\pi)\delta_{0}+\pi\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)\qquad\text{on }[0,\infty).

With respect to the dominating measure
\delta_{0}+{\mathrm{d}}{x}
, the score is

s_{\theta}(0)=0,\qquad s_{\theta}(x)=\frac{1}{\theta}-x,\quad x>0.

Since
\mathbb{E}[s_{\theta}(X)]=0
, the score
s_{\theta}
is itself a kernel, and we write
K_{\theta}\coloneqq s_{\theta}
. For
m>0
, the event
\{X\geq m\}
excludes the atom at
0
, so the conditional law coincides with the exponential tail. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[K_{\theta}(X)\mid X\geq m]

\displaystyle=-m\leq 0=\mathbb{E}[K_{\theta}(X)],

\displaystyle K_{\theta}(m)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta}-m\geq-m=\mathbb{E}[K_{\theta}(X)\mid X\geq m].

At
m=0
, both inequalities hold with equality. Thus the inequalities of Theorem
3.5
(iii)–(iv) hold with the reversed sign, so the family is
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
- and
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
-decreasing in
\theta
:
P_{\theta_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}P_{\theta_{1}}
and
P_{\theta_{2}}\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}P_{\theta_{1}}
whenever
\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}
.

The likelihood-ratio direction still fails. With respect to the dominating measure
\delta_{0}+{\mathrm{d}}{x}
, the density of
P_{\theta}
is

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}{}P_{\theta}}{{\mathrm{d}}{\delta_{0}+{\mathrm{d}}{x}}}(x)=\begin{cases}1-\pi,&x=0,\\
\pi\theta e^{-\theta x},&x>0.\end{cases}

For
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
, the corresponding likelihood ratio equals

1\quad\text{at }x=0,\qquad\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}}e^{-(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})x}\quad\text{for }x>0.

Since
\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}>1
, the likelihood ratio jumps upward at
0
, so it is not nonincreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Therefore the family is not
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
-ordered in
\theta
, even though the
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
- and
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
-orders survive atom inflation.

4.3
Joint-parameter and interpolation paths

The density-family criteria apply directly to any
C^{1}
path
t\mapsto P_{t}
with common support and satisfying Assumption
2.1
, with
t
in place of
\nu
. Treat
t
as the varying parameter and use the path score
\partial_{t}\log f_{t}
. When a reduced factor
w_{t}
is available, the path kernel is
K_{t}(x)=\partial_{t}\log w_{t}(x)
.

For a path
t\mapsto(\theta_{1}(t),\dots,\theta_{d}(t))
inside a
d
-parameter family with parameter-dependent factor
w_{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d}}
, the chain rule gives the path kernel as a weighted combination of single-parameter kernels:

K_{t}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta_{i}^{\prime}(t)\,K^{(i)}_{\theta(t)}(x),\qquad K^{(i)}_{\theta(t)}(x)\coloneqq\left.\partial_{\theta_{i}}\log w_{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d}}(x)\right|_{(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})=\theta(t)}.

(14)

The joint-parameter examples (Examples
4.6
,
4.7
, and
4.9
) read off the single-parameter kernels from Table
1
and combine them through (
14
). The interpolation example (Example
4.8
) connects two laws of distinct factor form along a constructed path on which the kernel varies nontrivially with the path parameter. The degenerate case, in which the path kernel is constant along the path, is treated separately in Section
4.4
.

Example 4.6

(Negative binomial along a two-parameter path)
.

Let
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
and
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
with
q_{i}=1-p_{i}
. Along the linear path
r(t)=(1-t)r_{1}+tr_{2}
,
q(t)=(1-t)q_{1}+tq_{2}
, the chain rule (
14
) combines the harmonic kernel
\psi(r+k)-\psi(r)
in the shape parameter (upper Pochhammer row of Table
1
) with the affine kernel
k/q
in the success-failure parameter
q=1-p
(discrete power-series row):

K_{t}(k)=r^{\prime}(t)\,\bigl[\psi(r(t)+k)-\psi(r(t))\bigr]+q^{\prime}(t)\,\frac{k}{q(t)},

nondecreasing and concave in
k
for
r^{\prime}(t),q^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
. Theorem
3.5
applied along the path gives
\mathrm{NegBin}(r_{1},p_{1})\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{NegBin}(r_{2},p_{2})
and
\mathrm{NegBin}(r_{2},p_{2})\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}\mathrm{NegBin}(r_{1},p_{1})
.

Example 4.7

(Beta-binomial along a two-parameter path)
.

Let
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
and
s_{1}\geq s_{2}
, with all parameters positive and
n\geq 1
fixed. The Beta-binomial mass function has parameter-dependent factor
w_{r,s}(k)\propto(r)_{k}(s)_{n-k}
, with single-parameter kernels
\psi(r+k)-\psi(r)
(upper Pochhammer) and
\psi(s+n-k)-\psi(s)
(finite-support Pochhammer) from Table
1
. Along the linear path
r(t)=(1-t)r_{1}+tr_{2}
,
s(t)=(1-t)s_{1}+ts_{2}

14
) gives a path kernel with forward difference

\Delta K_{t}(k)=\frac{r^{\prime}(t)}{r(t)+k}-\frac{s^{\prime}(t)}{s(t)+n-k-1}\geq 0,\qquad k=0,\dots,n-1,

because
r^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
and
s^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
. Theorem
3.5
applied along the path gives
\mathrm{BB}(n,r_{1},s_{1})\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{BB}(n,r_{2},s_{2})
.

Example 4.8

(Beta-binomial to binomial interpolation)
.

If
p\geq(r+n-1)/(r+s+n-1)
, then
\mathrm{BB}(n,r,s)\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
.

Consider the path of factors
w_{c}(k)=\binom{n}{k}(r+cp)_{k}(s+c(1-p))_{n-k}
,
c\geq 0
, and let
P_{c}
be the law obtained by normalising
w_{c}
. At
c=0
this is
\mathrm{BB}(n,r,s)
. As
c\to\infty
,
(a+cq)_{k}=(cq)^{k}(1+O(1/c))
uniformly in
k\leq n
, so
w_{c}(k)/c^{n}\to\binom{n}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k}
pointwise in
k
, and
P_{c}\to\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
after normalisation. The path kernel is
K_{c}(k)=\partial_{c}\log w_{c}(k)
, so differentiating each Pochhammer factor through
\partial_{c}\log(r+cp)_{k}=p[\psi(r+cp+k)-\psi(r+cp)]
and the analogous identity for the second factor gives

K_{c}(k)=p\bigl[\psi(r+cp+k)-\psi(r+cp)\bigr]+(1-p)\bigl[\psi(s+c(1-p)+n-k)-\psi(s+c(1-p))\bigr].

Its forward difference is

\Delta K_{c}(k)=\frac{p}{r+cp+k}-\frac{1-p}{s+c(1-p)+n-k-1}=\frac{p(s+n-1)-(1-p)r-k}{(r+cp+k)(s+c(1-p)+n-k-1)}.

The stated condition
p\geq(r+n-1)/(r+s+n-1)
is exactly the requirement that the numerator be nonnegative at
k=n-1
, so
\Delta K_{c}(k)\geq 0
for every
k=0,\dots,n-1
. Theorem
3.5
(i) gives
P_{0}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}P_{c}
for every
c>0
. The likelihood-ratio order is preserved under pointwise limits of densities, so letting
c\to\infty
yields
\mathrm{BB}(n,r,s)\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
.

Example 4.9

(Gamma along a shape–scale path)
.

For a continuous analogue, take a
C^{1}
path
t\mapsto(r(t),\beta(t))
with
r^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
and
\beta^{\prime}(t)\geq 0

f_{r,\beta}(x)\propto x^{r-1}e^{-x/\beta}
has parameter-dependent factor
w_{r,\beta}(x)=x^{r-1}e^{-x/\beta}
, with single-parameter kernels

K^{(r)}_{r}(x)=\partial_{r}\log w_{r,\beta}(x)=\log x,\qquad K^{(\beta)}_{\beta}(x)=\partial_{\beta}\log w_{r,\beta}(x)=x/\beta^{2}.

Both are nondecreasing on
(0,\infty)
. The first is the shape-parameter row of Table
1
; the second comes from differentiating
-x/\beta
in
\beta
. The chain-rule combination (
14
) gives the path kernel

K_{t}(x)=r^{\prime}(t)\log x+\beta^{\prime}(t)\,x/\beta(t)^{2},

which is once more nondecreasing on
(0,\infty)

3.5
(i) therefore gives

\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{0},\beta_{0})\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{1},\beta_{1})\qquad\text{whenever }r_{0}\leq r_{1}\text{ and }\beta_{0}\leq\beta_{1},

on taking any monotone path with
r^{\prime}\geq 0
and
\beta^{\prime}\geq 0
between the endpoints, for instance the linear path. In the rate parametrisation

again nondecreasing. So increasing the shape

r_{0}\leq r_{1}
and
\rho_{0}\geq\rho_{1}

\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{0},\rho_{0})\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{1},\rho_{1}).

4.4
Comparisons via the pairwise kernel

The joint-parameter comparisons of the previous subsection use an explicit one-parameter path. When two laws admit compatible factorisations, the algebraic log-ratio of their factors can be used directly, without choosing a cumbersome and application-specific path. Fix two laws
P,Q
on a common discrete support
J\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}
with positive factors
w^{P}_{k},w^{Q}_{k}
, so
f_{P}(k)\propto w^{P}_{k}
and
f_{Q}(k)\propto w^{Q}_{k}
. The path
w_{t}(k)=(w^{P}_{k})^{t}(w^{Q}_{k})^{1-t}
,
t\in[0,1]
, interpolates from
Q
at
t=0
to
P
at
t=1
, and its path score is constant in
t
and equal to the log-factor ratio:

\partial_{t}\log w_{t}(k)=\log\frac{w^{P}_{k}}{w^{Q}_{k}}.

The
pairwise kernel

{\cal K}(k)\coloneqq\log\frac{w^{P}_{k}}{w^{Q}_{k}},\qquad k\in J,

is therefore a path kernel along this interpolation, defined up to an additive constant absorbed by the normalising ratio. Theorem
3.5
applied along the path with
t_{0}=0
and
t_{1}=1
reads

Q\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}P\iff{\cal K}\text{ is nondecreasing on }J,\qquad P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q\iff{\cal K}\text{ is concave on }J,

with the discrete tests
\Delta{\cal K}(k)\geq 0
and
\Delta^{2}{\cal K}(k)\leq 0
. Under this concavity condition, the hazard-rate and usual stochastic orders reduce to endpoint checks at
k_{*}=\min J
by
[
2
]
. Each pairing of factor forms below decomposes
{\cal K}
into transparent blocks.

Shared GPS representation.

For
w^{P}_{k}=a_{k}\theta_{P}^{k}
and
w^{Q}_{k}=b_{k}\theta_{Q}^{k}
,

{\cal K}(k)=\log\frac{a_{k}}{b_{k}}+k\log\frac{\theta_{P}}{\theta_{Q}},\qquad\Delta^{2}{\cal K}(k)=\Delta^{2}\log\frac{a_{k}}{b_{k}}.

The affine power term shifts
\Delta{\cal K}
but drops out of
\Delta^{2}{\cal K}
, so
\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}
is determined by the coefficient-ratio shape and
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
follows under a sign condition on
\Delta{\cal K}
.

Example 4.10

(Katz-class pairs)
.

The binomial, Poisson, and negative-binomial laws share the GPS-power representation with coefficients
\binom{n}{k}
,
1/k!
, and
(r)_{k}/k!
. The GPS comparison gives:

(i)

\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}\mathrm{Poi}(\lambda)
, with
\mathrm{Bin}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{Poi}\iff np\leq(1-p)\lambda
and
\mathrm{Bin}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}\mathrm{Poi}\iff(1-p)^{n}\geq e^{-\lambda}
.

(ii)

\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}\mathrm{NegBin}(r,\pi)
, with
\mathrm{Bin}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{NegBin}\iff np\leq r(1-\pi)
and
\mathrm{Bin}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}\mathrm{NegBin}\iff(1-p)^{n}\geq\pi^{r}
.

(iii)

\mathrm{Poi}(\lambda)\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}\mathrm{NegBin}(r,p)
, with
\mathrm{Poi}\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}\mathrm{NegBin}\iff\lambda\leq r(1-p)
and
\mathrm{Poi}\leq_{\mathrm{st}}\mathrm{NegBin}\iff e^{-\lambda}\geq p^{r}
.

The coefficient ratios are log-concave. The binomial–Poisson ratio is the falling factorial
\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(n-j)
, the binomial–negative-binomial ratio multiplies this by
1/(r)_{k}
, and the Poisson–negative-binomial ratio is
1/(r)_{k}
.

Shared exponential-family representation.

Consider

w^{P}_{k}=h_{P}(k)e^{\theta_{P}T_{P}(k)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad w^{Q}_{k}=h_{Q}(k)e^{\theta_{Q}T_{Q}(k)}.

Then

{\cal K}(k)=\log\frac{h_{P}(k)}{h_{Q}(k)}+\theta_{P}T_{P}(k)-\theta_{Q}T_{Q}(k).

A shared carrier
h_{P}=h_{Q}
cancels the carrier block, leaving
{\cal K}=\theta_{P}T_{P}-\theta_{Q}T_{Q}
. A shared affine statistic
T_{P}=T_{Q}=T
cancels the statistic block under
\Delta^{2}
, so
(h_{P}/h_{Q})
log-concave on
J
implies
P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q
.

(CMP comparisons)

Example 4.13

(Beta-binomial vs hypergeometric)
.

Assume

and that the hypergeometric law has support

The pairwise kernel
{\cal K}(k)=\log(w^{P}_{k}/w^{Q}_{k})
collects the four Pochhammer blocks, and its forward difference simplifies to

\Delta{\cal K}(k)=\log\frac{(B-k)(s+n-k-1)}{(W-n+k+1)(r+k)},

\Delta{\cal K}(k)\geq\Delta{\cal K}(n-1)

\Delta{\cal K}(n-1)\geq 0

The shared-statistic exponential-family comparison admits a dual reading.

\mathrm{Poi}(\lambda)\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}\mathrm{NegBin}(r,p)
from Example

5
Conclusion

We have shown that the four stochastic orders considered here can be read from the score, or equivalently from any kernel
K_{\nu}
on the support. The score is the unique centred kernel, and any other kernel differs from it only by a parameter-dependent constant. Thus, the score and kernel can be used interchangeably in the criteria, while the kernel form often removes normalising terms and leaves a simpler state-dependent expression.

This equivalence extends beyond comparisons within a parametric family. Along joint-parameter paths, the path kernel is obtained by the chain rule. In pairwise factor-form comparisons, the kernel is the log ratio of the factors and gives a direct comparison without first choosing an interpolating family. More generally, when a law is induced from parameter-dependent factors or atom weights, the kernel can be obtained at the factor level, before centring produces the score. In compound laws, the kernel of the counting law is the parameter derivative of the log atom weights, and the compound kernel is its posterior average given the compound sum.
