Tschirnhausen bundles of sextic covers of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}
Sam Frengley, Sameera Vemulapalli

Abstract
. A degree d d genus g g cover of the complex projective line by a smooth irreducible curve C C yields a vector bundle on the projective line by pushforward of the structure sheaf. We classify the bundles that arise this way when d = 6 d=6 . Interestingly, our methods show that all constraints on the pushforward are “explained” by multiplication in an algebra. Finally, we show that all possible pushforwards are realized by covers with a nontrivial proper subcover.

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth irreducible projective curve of genus
g
over
\mathbb{C}
equipped with a finite morphism
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
d
.
The restriction of functions gives an inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\rightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
.
Define the
Tschirnhausen bundle of
\pi
to be
\mathcal{E}_{\pi}\coloneqq(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})^{\vee}
. By the Birkhoff-Grothendieck theorem, vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
split completely, so we may write (uniquely up to non-unique isomorphism)

\mathcal{E}_{\pi}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{d-1})

with
e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\dots\leq e_{d-1}
. Because
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C})=h^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})=1
, we have
e_{1}\geq 1
. The integers
\vec{e}_{\pi}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{d-1})
are called the
scrollar invariants
of
\pi
, and encode a good deal of information about the curve
C
. For the rest of this article, assume
1\leq e_{1}\leq\dots\leq e_{d-1}
.

Question 1.1

(Tschirnhausen realization problem)
.

Which
(d-1)
-tuples
\vec{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
arise as the scrollar invariants of a smooth irreducible degree
d
cover
\pi\colon C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
?

For
2\leq d\leq 5
, the answer to the Tschirnhausen realization problem is known completely (combining
[
17
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
, and
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
) using the parametrizations of low degree covers
[
5
,
4
]
. Moreover, when
d
is prime there is a conjectural answer to the Tschirnhausen realization problem
[
23
, Conjecture 1.3]
; no such conjecture exists for composite
d>5
. In
Theorem

1.3
we give a complete answer to the Tschirnhausen realization problem in the case
d=6
.

Define
\mathcal{P}_{2},\mathcal{P}_{3}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{5}
by the following conditions

\mathcal{P}_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(e_{1},\dots,e_{5})\in\mathbb{Z}^{5}\quad\Bigg|\quad\begin{aligned} &1\leq e_{1}\leq\dots\leq e_{5},\;e_{5}\leq e_{1}+e_{4},\\
&e_{5}\leq e_{2}+e_{3},\;e_{3}\leq e_{1}+e_{2},\;e_{4}\leq 2e_{2}\end{aligned}\right\}

\mathcal{P}_{3}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(e_{1},\dots,e_{5})\in\mathbb{Z}^{5}\quad\Bigg|\quad\begin{aligned} &1\leq e_{1}\leq\dots\leq e_{5},\;e_{5}\leq e_{1}+e_{4},\;\\
&e_{5}\leq e_{2}+e_{3},\;e_{2}\leq 2e_{1},\;e_{4}\leq e_{1}+e_{3}\end{aligned}\right\}.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\vec{e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{5})\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
. We say a partition
\{i,j\}\sqcup\{k,\ell\}=\{2,3,4,5\}
is
admissible for
\vec{e}
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

e_{i}+e_{j}\leq e_{k}+e_{\ell}
,

(2)

e_{i}\leq e_{j}\leq e_{i}+e_{1}
,

(3)

e_{k}\leq e_{\ell}\leq e_{k}+e_{1}
, and

(4)

e_{k}+e_{\ell}+e_{1}\leq 2e_{i}+2e_{j}
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the set of points
(e_{1},\dots,e_{5})\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{3}
admitting an admissible partition.

Theorem 1.3
.

A quintuple
\vec{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{5}
arises as the scrollar invariants of a smooth irreducible sextic cover if and only if
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{Q}\cup\mathcal{P}_{3}
.

1.1.
Constraints on scrollar invariants

The scrollar invariants of a degree
d
cover
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
must satisfy constraints arising from multiplication. More precisely,
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
-algebra and this forces
\vec{e}_{\pi}
to satisfy some linear inequalities
[
22
, Section 1.3]
(e.g., see
Example

2.1
for a proof that
e_{2}\leq 2e_{1}
when
d=3
). One significant feature is that the linear inequalities imposed on
\vec{e}_{\pi}
depend on the degrees of the subcovers of
\pi
. In the case
d\leq 5
, the necessary constraints arising from multiplication in
[
22
, Section 1.3]
are known to be sufficient
[
17
,
13
,
23
,
11
]
.

Moreover, when
\pi
is
primitive
(that is, if
\pi
does not factor through a nontrivial proper subcover) it is known from
[
22
, Section 1.3]
that
e_{i+j}\leq e_{i}+e_{j}
for all
i
and
j
. A conjecture of the second author
[
23
, Conjecture 1.3]
asserts that this is a
sufficient
condition, i.e., every
\vec{e}
satisfying
e_{i+j}\leq e_{i}+e_{j}
arises as the scrollar invariants of a smooth primitive cover. This conjecture is known for each
d\leq 5
(combining
[
17
,
23
,
11
]
).

In the imprimitive case, the constraints in
[
22
, Section 1.3]
are significantly more complicated. Regardless, they are worked out explicitly for all
d<18

[
22
, Theorem 1.26]
. One might wonder if these are
sufficient
conditions, but this turns out to be false (as we show in the case
d=6
). There is a good reason for this insufficiency: if
\pi\colon C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
factors through a subcover as
\tau\circ\gamma
, then
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
is an algebra over the intermediate cover and this forces the slopes of
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
to satisfy some linear inequalities. This in turn imposes constraints on
\vec{e}_{\pi}
.

Indeed, in the case
d=6
, if
\pi
is a sextic cover factoring as
\tau\circ\gamma
through a degree
2
cover
\tau\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then
\vec{e}_{\pi}
satisfies additional linear inequalities coming from the multiplicative structure of
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
as a cubic
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra. This constraint is truly new in the sense that it is
not
implied by those in
[
22
, Section 1.3]
. We prove via explicit construction that this new constraint, combined with the constraints of
[
22
, Section 1.3]
, are necessary and sufficient conditions for a quintuple
\vec{e}
to arise as the scrollar invariants of a smooth sextic cover.

1.2.
Main ideas of the proof of
Theorem

1.3

If
\pi\colon C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a degree
6
cover
[
22
, Section 1.3]
gives an explicit set of linear inequalities on the scrollar invariants
\vec{e}_{\pi}
, depending on how
\pi
factors. It turns out that in this case (when
d=6
) there are quintuples
\vec{e}
which satisfy the constraints of
[
22
, Section 1.3]
but which
do not
arise as the scrollar invariants of a sextic curve. For example if
e_{2}>2e_{1}
, then one can show (
Lemma

2.4
) that
C
is a triple cover of a hyperelliptic curve
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, e.g., there is a factorization

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}

and thus
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
is a cubic
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra. The multiplication structure of
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
places nontrivial constraints on the slopes of the vector bundle
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
, which in turn places constraints on
\vec{e}_{\pi}
. These constraints, which do not follow from

, play a central role in this article.

After proving these constraints (which we do in

), it remains to show that all

satisfying these constraints actually arise as the scrollar invariants of a sextic cover. Remarkably it turns out that such a quintuple

can always be realized as the scrollar invariants of a cover which factors (that is, a double cover of a triple cover or a triple cover of a double cover).

1.3.
Future directions

We now pose several open questions for interested reader which may be tractable via the methods of this paper.

1.3.1.
Tschirnhausen bundles of primitive degree
6
covers

A conjecture of the second author
[
23
, Conjecture 1.3]
asserts that every
\vec{e}
satisfying
e_{i+j}\leq e_{i}+e_{j}
arises as the scrollar invariants of a smooth primitive cover. The case
d=6
is the smallest degree in which this conjecture is not known. In
[
23
, Theorem 1.4]
it is shown that if
\vec{e}_{\pi}
is the scrollar invariants of a primitive cover, then
e_{i+j}\leq e_{i}+e_{j}
for all
i
and
j
. Thus the difficulty of proving
[
23
, Conjecture 1.3]
lies in showing the existence of smooth
primitive
covers with prescribed scrollar invariants. In
Appendix

A
we sketch how
Theorem

1.3
can be to prove the existence of primitive curves with some specified scrollar invariants in the case
d=6
.

1.3.2.
Maroni loci of larger than expected dimension

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d,g}
be the moduli stack of degree
d
covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by genus
g
smooth irreducible curves. Define the
Maroni locus

\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})=\{\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}:\vec{e}_{\pi}=\vec{e}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{d,g}.

An application of Serre duality shows that
\sum e_{i}=d+g-1
, so henceforth assume this. If the Tschirnhausen bundle of a cover behaves like a “random” vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of rank
d-1
and degree
d+g-1
, one might expect that:

(1)

\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})
is nonempty precisely when
h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})))\leq\dim(\mathcal{H}_{d,g})
; and

(2)

when
\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})
is nonempty, we have
\codim\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})=h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})))
.

Unfortunately, both of these guesses are false in general. Regarding
(1)
, the algebra structure on
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
sometimes forces
\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})
to be empty when
h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})))\leq\dim(\mathcal{H}_{d,g})
(for example take
d=3
and
\vec{e}=(1,3)
). Regarding
(2)
, in the cases
d=4,5
it is known that the dimension of
\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})
can be
larger
than the expected dimension when the elements of
\vec{e}
are spread out; see
[
23
,
11
]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{fac}}(\vec{e})\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})
be the locus of covers factoring through a nontrivial proper subcover. In
Section

3
and
Section

4
, we explicitly construct elements of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{fac}}(\vec{e})
when possible. We do not compute
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{fac}}(\vec{e})
, but in many cases such a computation should be possible. Now, the inequality

\dim\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})\geq\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{fac}}(\vec{e})

yields a
lower bound
on the dimension of
\mathcal{M}(\vec{e})
. In cases where the elements of
\vec{e}
are spread out, we expect that

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{fac}}(\vec{e})\geq\dim(\mathcal{H}_{6,g})-h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e}))).

Combining these two inequalities, one may be able to show that when
d=6
many Maroni loci have dimension larger than the expected dimension.

1.3.3.
Making a conjecture for the Tschirnhausen realization problem for all
d

It is natural to wonder if, for general degree
d
, all constraints on the scrollar invariants
\vec{e}_{\pi}
“come from” multiplication over some (possibly trivial) subcover of
\pi
. If this is true then it may be possible to combine the ideas of
Section

2
with
[
22
, Section 1.3]
to generalize
[
23
, Conjecture 1.3]
to give a conjectural answer to the Tschirnhausen realization problem for composite
d>6
. This would be a substantial step forward towards the Tschirnhausen realization problem.

1.4.
History

There has been a great deal of work on the Tschirnhausen realization problem. We highlight here two ways (out of many) of constructing curves with prescribed scrollar invariants. In
[
23
, Theorem 1.5]
a construction is given for a cover with specified scrollar invariants whenever
\vec{e}
is
concave
; the proof involves desingularizing curves in Hirzebruch surfaces and keeping track of how the scrollar invariants change. In
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
it is shown that for any
\vec{e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{d-1})
, there exists an integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
(e_{1}+k,\dots,e_{d-1}+k)
is realized as the scrollar invariants of some cover; the proof involves deforming curves in projective bundles. For further constructions of curves with specified scrollar invariants, see
[
13
,
6
,
2
,
7
,
8
,
19
]
. For constraints on scrollar invariants of curves in general degree, see
[
18
,
22
,
23
]
.

1.5.
Conventions

We work over the complex numbers, but all results in this paper hold over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. All curves are assumed to be smooth, projective, and irreducible unless otherwise specified. Throughout we drop the subscript and write
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
for the structure sheaf of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. If
X
is a curve, then a degree
d
cover of
X
means a finite morphism
C\rightarrow X
of degree
d
, where
C
is smooth and irreducible.

2.
Constraints on the Tschirnhausen bundle

We begin with a warm-up showing how multiplication imposes constraints on the Tschirnhausen bundle of a trigonal cover.

Example 2.1
.

Suppose that
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
has degree
d=3
. We claim that
e_{2}\leq 2e_{1}
.
Let

m\colon\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}\otimes\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}

denote the multiplication map. Fix a splitting
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-e_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(-e_{2})
and write
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-e_{1})
. If
m(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{A}
then
\mathcal{A}
is a quadratic subalgebra of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
, and therefore the trigonal cover
\pi\colon C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
factors through the degree
2
morphism
\Spec_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, the multiplication map
m
induces some nonzero morphism
\mathcal{O}(-e_{1})\otimes\mathcal{O}(-e_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(-e_{2})
(composing
m
with the projection onto
\mathcal{O}(-e_{2})
) and thus
e_{2}\leq 2e_{1}
.

In the remainder of this section, we will prove the following theorem (which is the “only if” direction of
Theorem

1.3
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a sextic cover with scrollar invariants
\vec{e}_{\pi}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{5})
. Then
\vec{e}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{Q}\cup\mathcal{P}_{3}
.

The bounds in
[
22
, Theorem 1.26]
imply that
\vec{e}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\cup\mathcal{P}_{3}
. Therefore to prove
Theorem

2.2
it is sufficient to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
\vec{e}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{3}
, then
\vec{e}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{Q}
.

We first give a brief outline of the proof of
Proposition

2.3
before giving the proof in
Section

2.1
. Let
\pi\colon C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a sextic cover with
\vec{e}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{3}
.
Lemma

2.4
shows that
\pi
factors as
\tau\circ\gamma
through a degree
2
cover
\tau\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
; consider the vector bundle
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
. This vector bundle splits as
\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus{V}
for some rank
2
vector bundle
V
on
X
. The vector bundle
V
is either semistable or unstable.

If
{V}
is semistable,
Lemma

2.5
bounds the difference of summands of
\tau_{*}V
. In
Lemma

2.7
we give an explicit combinatorial construction of an admissible partition to conclude that
\vec{e}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{Q}
.

If
{V}
is unstable then its Harder–Narasimhan filtration has the form
0\subseteq{L}\subseteq{V}
for a line bundle
L
on
X
. In this case, the exact sequence of vector bundles
0\rightarrow L\rightarrow V\rightarrow V/L\rightarrow 0
pushes forward to an exact sequence of vector bundles

(2.1)

0\rightarrow\tau_{*}L\rightarrow\tau_{*}V\rightarrow\tau_{*}(V/L)\rightarrow 0.

It is possible that sequence (
2.1
) does not split. Nevertheless, using properties of the Harder–Narasimhan filtration and the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra structure of
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
, we directly prove several linear inequalities on the summands of
\tau_{*}L
and
\tau_{*}(V/L)
. Now, explicit manipulation of these linear inequalities (
Lemma

2.8
) shows that
\vec{e}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{Q}
.

respectively, and identify

at the generic point of

. Denote by

at the generic point

. Note that
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}
and
M_{\eta}
are
k
-subvector spaces of
K
of dimension
2
and
4
respectively and that the specialization
m\colon\mathcal{A}_{\eta}\otimes_{k}M_{\eta}\rightarrow K
is multiplication in the field
K
. But (
2.2
) implies that
m(\mathcal{A}_{\eta}\otimes M_{\eta})=M_{\eta}
, and so
M
is a vector space over the subfield
k(\mathcal{A}_{\eta})\subseteq K
. It follows that
k(\mathcal{A}_{\eta})/k
has degree dividing
2
since
\dim_{k}M_{\eta}=4
and
\dim_{k}K=6
. But
\deg k(\mathcal{A}_{\eta})\geq\dim_{k}\mathcal{A}_{\eta}=2
and therefore
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}=k(\mathcal{A}_{\eta})
which implies that
\mathcal{A}
is a quadratic subalgebra of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
.
∎

For a vector bundle
V
on a curve, let
\lambda(V)=\deg(V)/\rank(V)
denote the slope of
V
. In the case when
V
is semistable the following
Lemmas

2.5
and
2.7
will be used to deduce
Proposition

2.3
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\pi\colon C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a degree
d
cover of the projective line by a genus
g
curve, and let
{E}
be a semistable vector bundle of rank
r
on
C
. Write

\pi_{*}{E}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}(a_{i})

for
a_{1}\leq\dots\leq a_{r}
. Then
a_{r}-a_{1}\leq\frac{2}{d}(g+d-1)
.

Remark 2.6
.

We note that the result
[
3
, Theorem 1.6]
is the arithmetic analogue of
Lemma

2.5
(in the case where
E=\mathcal{O}_{C}
) under the dictionary between degree
d
number fields
K/\mathbb{Q}
and degree
d
covers
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
(see
[
23
, Section 8]
for a precise description of this dictionary).

Proof of
Lemma

2.5
.

Fix a splitting
\pi_{*}{E}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}(a_{i})
. Since
\pi
is flat, the inclusion
\mathcal{O}(a_{r})\subseteq\pi_{*}{E}
induces an inclusion
\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(a_{r})\subseteq\pi^{*}\pi_{*}{E}
. Composing this with the projection formula
\pi^{*}\pi_{*}{E}\rightarrow{E}
yields a morphism

\phi\colon\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(a_{r})\rightarrow{E}.

We first claim that
\phi
is nonzero. Let
K
and
k
be the function fields of
C
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}
respectively. The fiber
{E}_{\xi}
of
E
at the generic point
\xi\in C
is an
r
-dimensional
K
-vector space. The morphism
\pi^{*}\pi_{*}{E}\rightarrow{E}
arising from the projection formula specializes to the multiplication map
K\otimes_{k}E_{\xi}\rightarrow E_{\xi}
given by
x\otimes y\mapsto xy
. The fiber of
\mathcal{O}(a_{r})
at the generic point
\eta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a
1
-dimensional
k
-vector subspace
W\subseteq E_{\xi}
. Therefore on the generic point,
\phi
specializes to the nonzero linear map
K\otimes_{k}W\rightarrow E_{\xi}
sending
x\otimes y\mapsto xy
. Therefore,
\phi
is nonzero.

A nonzero morphism from a line bundle to a vector bundle is an inclusion and therefore
\phi(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(a_{r}))
is a subbundle of
{E}
. Since
{E}
is semistable, we have

\lambda(\phi(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(a_{r})))\leq\lambda({E}).

On the other hand

\lambda(\phi(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(a_{r})))\geq\lambda(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(a_{r}))=da_{r}.

Combining the above two inequalities, we obtain

(2.3)

da_{r}\leq\lambda({E}).

Let
\omega_{C/\mathbb{P}^{1}}
be the relative dualizing sheaf of
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Since
E
is semistable and
\omega_{C/\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is a line bundle the bundle
{E}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{C/\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is semistable. Relative Serre duality implies that

\pi_{*}({E}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{C/\mathbb{P}^{1}})\simeq(\pi_{*}{E})^{\vee}.

A similar argument to the above shows that
-da_{1}\leq\lambda(\pi_{*}E)^{\vee}=\lambda({E}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{C/\mathbb{P}^{1}})
. Since the slope is additive over the tensor product with a line bundle we have

(2.4)

-da_{1}\leq-\lambda({E})+\deg(\omega_{C/\mathbb{P}^{1}}).

Taking degrees in the relation
\omega_{C/\mathbb{P}^{1}}=\omega_{C}\otimes(\pi^{*}\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})^{\vee}
yields

(2.5)

\deg(\omega_{C/\mathbb{P}^{1}})=2g-2+2d.

Combining (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) we obtain

(2.6)

\lambda({E})\leq da_{1}+2(g+d-1).

and combining (
2.3
) and (
2.6
) gives
a_{r}-a_{1}\leq\frac{2}{d}(g+d-1)
, as required.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{3}
has
e_{5}\leq e_{1}+e_{2}
. Then
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

Because
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{3}
, we must have either
e_{2}>2e_{1}
or
e_{4}>e_{1}+e_{3}
. By assumption
e_{5}\leq e_{1}+e_{2}
so
e_{4}\leq e_{1}+e_{3}
and hence
e_{2}>2e_{1}
.

It suffices to show that
\{2,3\}\sqcup\{4,5\}
is an admissible partition for
\vec{e}
(in the sense of
1.2
). Conditions
(1)
,
(2)
, and
(3)
of
1.2
follow immediately.

To see Condition
(4)
, we claim that
(e_{k}-e_{i})+(e_{\ell}-e_{j})+e_{1}\leq e_{i}+e_{j}
. Since
e_{5}\leq e_{2}+e_{1}
we have
e_{k}-e_{i}\leq e_{1}
and
e_{\ell}-e_{j}\leq e_{1}
. Moreover,
e_{2}>2e_{1}
gives
4e_{1}<e_{i}+e_{j}
. Therefore

(e_{k}-e_{i})+(e_{\ell}-e_{j})+e_{1}\leq 3e_{1}<e_{i}+e_{j}

as required.
∎

Lemmas

2.5
and
2.7
will be sufficient to deduce
Proposition

2.3
when
V
is semistable. When
V
is unstable we will need the following lemma instead.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{3}
and write
E=\mathcal{O}(e_{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{4})\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{5})
. Let

(2.7)

0\rightarrow F\rightarrow E\rightarrow G\rightarrow 0

be an exact sequence of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
\rank(F)=\rank(G)=2
and
\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(F)
. Write
F=\mathcal{O}(f_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(f_{2})
and
G=\mathcal{O}(g_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(g_{2})
for integers
f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq e_{1}+f_{1}
and
g_{1}\leq g_{2}\leq e_{1}+g_{1}
. If
f_{1}+f_{2}+e_{1}\leq 2g_{1}+2g_{2}
then
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

The exactness of the sequence (
2.7
) implies that

(2.8)

g_{1}+g_{2}+f_{1}+f_{2}=e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{4}+e_{5}.

The exact sequence (
2.7
) gives an extension class
\alpha\in\Ext^{1}(G,F)
. Consider the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
where the restriction to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\{t\}
is given by the extension class
t\alpha\in\Ext^{1}(G,F)
. Because all nonzero scalar multiples of
\alpha
give rise to isomorphic vector bundles if
t\neq 0
, we have an isomorphism
\mathcal{E}_{t}\simeq E
. On the other hand, the special fiber at
t=0
is
\mathcal{E}_{0}\simeq F\oplus G
.

Now, the upper semi-continuity theorem implies that for any integer
n
we have

(2.9)

h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},E(n))\leq h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(F\oplus G)(n)).

Write
F\oplus G=\mathcal{O}(c_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}(c_{4})
ordered so that
c_{1}\leq\dots\leq c_{4}
. From (
2.9
), a standard argument now implies that

(2.10)

c_{1}\leq e_{2},\quad\text{ and }\quad c_{1}+c_{2}\leq e_{2}+e_{3},\quad\text{ and }\quad c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3}\leq e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{4}.

There are four possibilities for
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4})
. Either

(i)

(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4})=(g_{1},g_{2},f_{1},f_{2})
,

(ii)

(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4})=(g_{1},f_{1},f_{2},g_{2})
,

(iii)

(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4})=(f_{1},g_{1},g_{2},f_{2})
,

(iv)

(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4})=(g_{1},f_{1},g_{2},f_{2})
.

In case
(i)
, combining
eqs.

2.8
and
2.10
gives

e_{4}+e_{5}+e_{1}\leq f_{1}+f_{2}+e_{1}\leq 2g_{1}+2g_{2}\leq e_{2}+e_{3},

and thus
\{2,3\}\sqcup\{4,5\}
is an admissible partition for
\vec{e}
, as required.

In case
(ii)
, we have
g_{1}\leq f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq g_{2}
, so
c_{4}-c_{1}=g_{2}-g_{1}\leq e_{1}
. This implies
e_{5}-e_{2}\leq e_{1}
and

2.7
implies that
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{Q}
. An analogous argument holds in case
(iii)
.

In case
(iv)
, we claim that
\{2,4\}\sqcup\{3,5\}
is an admissible partition. The fact that

is surjective implies that
e_{2}\leq g_{1}
. Combining this with (
2.10
) implies that
e_{2}=g_{1}
. Dualizing the exact sequence (
2.7
) and applying the same argument implies that
e_{5}=f_{2}
. Therefore we have

(2.11)

e_{2}=g_{1}\leq f_{1}\leq e_{3}\leq e_{4}\leq g_{2}\leq f_{2}=e_{5}.

The inequality
g_{2}-g_{1}\leq e_{1}
implies
e_{4}-e_{2}\leq e_{1}
, and similarly
f_{2}-f_{1}\leq e_{1}
implies
e_{5}-e_{3}\leq e_{1}
. Because
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{3}
, we have either
e_{4}>e_{1}+e_{3}
or
e_{2}>2e_{1}
. From
eq.

2.11
, we have
e_{4}-e_{3}\leq g_{2}-g_{1}\leq e_{1}
, so indeed
e_{2}>2e_{1}
. Now

(e_{5}-e_{4})+(e_{3}-e_{2})+e_{1}\leq 3e_{1}\leq e_{2}+e_{3}

and therefore
\{2,4\}\sqcup\{3,5\}
is an admissible partition, as required.
∎

3.
Double covers of triple covers

The purpose of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Every quintuple
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{P}_{3}
is realized as the scrollar invariants of a cover
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
where
\pi=\tau\circ\gamma
factors as a double cover of a triple cover (that is
\deg(\tau)=3
and
\deg(\gamma)=2
).

The basic strategy is as follows. First, we construct a cubic cover
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
with specified scrollar invariants
(e_{1},e_{2})
. Then we construct a double cover
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
such that
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L
for some line bundle
L
on
X
with
\tau_{*}L=\mathcal{O}(-e_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}(-e_{4})\oplus\mathcal{O}(-e_{5})
.

The key technical difficulty is producing a double cover
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
such that
C
is smooth and its structure sheaf has the desired pushforward. As a result, the Brill–Noether theory of trigonal covers (
[
14
]
) plays an essential role in our proof.

We first recall the solution to the Tschirnhausen realization problem in degree
d=3
.

Theorem 3.2

(Maroni
[
17
]
)
.

Let
1\leq e_{1}\leq e_{2}
be integers. The pair
(e_{1},e_{2})
arises as the scrollar invariants of a cubic cover if and only if
e_{2}\leq 2e_{1}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a cubic cover and let
L\not\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
be a line bundle on
X
such that
L^{\otimes-2}
is basepoint-free. Then there exists a double cover
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
by a smooth curve such that
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L
.

Proof.

Choose a generic section
s\in H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes-2})
. From Bertini’s theorem, the basepoint-freeness of
L^{\otimes-2}
implies that the divisor
Z(s)
, cut out by the vanishing of
s
, is reduced. Define the quadratic
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L
with multiplication map
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
given by

(x_{1},y_{1})\otimes(x_{2},y_{2})\mapsto(x_{1}x_{2}+sy_{1}y_{2},x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{1}).

Writing
C=\Spec_{X}(\mathcal{A})
we obtain a finite flat morphism
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
of degree
2
, and by definition
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{A}
. The divisor
Z(s)
is easily seen to be the ramification divisor of the morphism
\gamma
. In particular, since
Z(s)
is reduced
C
is smooth.

Finally, if
C
were reducible and smooth, then
C\simeq X\sqcup X
and
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}
. But
L\not\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
by assumption, so
C
is irreducible and hence
\gamma
is a double cover.
∎

The key technical proposition is now
Proposition

3.4
whose proof we delay to
Section

3.1
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\vec{e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{5})\in\mathcal{P}_{3}
and let
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a trigonal curve with scrollar invariants
\vec{e}_{\tau}=(e_{1},e_{2})
. There exists a line bundle
L
on
X
such that:

(1)

\tau_{*}L=\mathcal{O}(-e_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}(-e_{4})\oplus\mathcal{O}(-e_{5})
; and

(2)

L^{\otimes-2}
is basepoint-free.

Assuming
Proposition

3.4
, we now give a proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\vec{e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{5})\in\mathcal{P}_{3}
. By
Theorem

3.2
, there exists a trigonal curve
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
with scrollar invariants
\vec{e}_{\tau}=(e_{1},e_{2})
. By
Proposition

3.4
, there exists a line bundle
L
on

satisfying
(1)
and
(2)
.

Now by

there exists a double cover
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
with

. Thus

is a smooth irreducible sextic cover with scrollar invariants

.
∎

3.1.

3.4

We begin by recalling some basic properties of

of line bundles on finite covers of the projective line.

.

Given a degree

\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
and a line bundle
L
on
X
, the

of

is the unique

-tuple
\vec{a}(L)=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
such that
a_{1}\leq\dots\leq a_{d}
and
\tau_{*}L\simeq\mathcal{O}(a_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}(a_{d})
.

Definition 3.6
.

Given a degree
d

\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and a
d
-tuple of integers
\vec{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
with

, define

.

Let
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a degree

be a line bundle on

. If

has splitting type
\vec{a}(L)=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})

, then

Proof.

For any closed point

, the inclusion

pushes forward to an inclusion

. Since

the splitting type of

. We then have

\displaystyle h^{0}(X,L(-P))

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\tau_{*}L(-P))

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(a_{i}-1))+\sum_{j\neq i}h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(a_{j}))

\displaystyle=-1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(a_{j}))

\displaystyle=-1+h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\tau_{*}L)

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},L).

Therefore

For the rest of this section, we assume that

4.
Triple covers of double covers

The purpose of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Every quintuple
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{Q}
is realized as the scrollar invariants of a cover
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
where
\pi=\tau\circ\gamma
factors as a triple cover of a double cover (that is
\deg(\tau)=2
and
\deg(\gamma)=3
).

The basic strategy is similar to the proof of
Theorem

3.1
. Namely, fix
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{Q}
and let
\{i,j\}\sqcup\{k,\ell\}
be an admissible partition for
\vec{e}
. First, choose a hyperelliptic curve
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
with scrollar invariant
e_{1}
(i.e., a hyperelliptic curve of genus
e_{1}-1
). Next, we choose line bundles
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
on
X
with splitting types
(-e_{j},-e_{i})
and
(-e_{\ell},-e_{k})
respectively. The line bundles will be chosen (carefully) so that there is a cubic cover
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
with
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
.

4.1.
Constructing triple covers of hyperelliptic curves

Let
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a hyperelliptic curve. In this subsection we discuss how, given appropriate line bundles
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
on
X
, one may construct a triple cover
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
with
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
. The key proposition is the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

Fix
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{Q}
and let
\{i,j\}\sqcup\{k,\ell\}
be an admissible partition for
\vec{e}
. Fix a hyperelliptic curve
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
of genus
g_{X}=e_{1}-1
. Suppose
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are line bundles on
X
with splitting types
(-e_{i},-e_{j})
and
(-e_{k},-e_{\ell})
respectively. Then there exists a triple cover
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
with
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
.

We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of
Proposition

4.2
we have
h^{0}(X,L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{\otimes-2})>0
.

Proof.

First note that

\deg(L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{\otimes-2})=\deg(L_{1})-2\deg(L_{2}).

Because
e_{i}+e_{j}\leq e_{k}+e_{\ell}
, we have
\deg(L_{2})\leq\deg(L_{1})
. Therefore

\deg(L_{1})-2\deg(L_{2})\geq-\deg(L_{1}).

A short calculation shows that
-\deg(L_{1})=e_{i}+e_{j}-e_{1}\geq e_{1}=g_{X}+1
. In particular we have
\deg(L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{\otimes-2})\geq g_{X}+1
and so by the Riemann–Roch theorem
h^{0}(X,L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{\otimes-2})>0
.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

4.2
.

Write
V=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
and consider the vector bundle

\Sym^{3}V^{\vee}\otimes\det(V)\simeq(L_{2}\otimes L_{1}^{\otimes-2})\oplus L_{1}^{\vee}\oplus L_{2}^{\vee}\oplus(L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{\otimes-2}).

Upon fixing a splitting
V=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
, a section
f\in\Sym^{3}V^{\vee}
can be written as a binary cubic form

f(x,y)=f_{0}x^{3}+f_{1}x^{2}y+f_{2}xy^{2}+f_{3}y^{3}

where
f_{0}\in H^{0}(X,(L_{2}\otimes L_{1}^{\otimes-2}))
,
f_{1}\in H^{0}(X,L_{1}^{\vee})
,
f_{2}\in H^{0}(X,L_{2}^{\vee})
and
f_{3}\in H^{0}(X,(L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{\otimes-2}))
. The vanishing of
f
gives rise to a variety

C=Z(f)\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{X}(V).

Observe that
h^{0}(X,L_{2}\otimes L_{1}^{\otimes-2})>0
by assumption and
h^{0}(X,L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{\otimes-2})>0
follows from Lemma
4.3
. But this implies that the linear series
\Sym^{3}V^{\vee}\otimes\det(V)
is basepoint-free on
\mathbb{P}_{X}(V)
. Indeed, for a closed point
p\in X
, the fiber
\mathbb{P}_{X}(V)
above
p
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and the restriction

(\Sym^{3}V^{\vee}\otimes\det(V))|_{p}

contains the binary cubic forms
ax^{3}+by^{3}
for arbitrary
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
. As a result, Bertini’s theorem implies that a general section
f
gives rise to a smooth curve
C
. The natural map
\gamma\colon C\rightarrow X
is easily seen to be a cubic cover and by construction
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus V
.
∎

4.2.
Line bundles on hyperelliptic curves

We interlude with a brief discussion of standard facts about line bundles on hyperelliptic curves. Let
\tau\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
. Let
H\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\tau^{*}[0:1]
be a divisor representing the fiber class. The main fact we need is
Proposition

4.6
, which relates divisors of a certain form to line bundles with fixed splitting type.

Definition 4.4
.

A
semireduced divisor
on
X
is an effective divisor which does not contain any fibers of
\tau
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\tau\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g_{X}
and let
D
be a semireduced divisor on
X
of degree
\deg(D)\leq g_{X}+1
. Then we have
h^{0}(X,D-H)=0
where
H=\tau_{*}[0:1]
.

Proof.

We first handle the case where
\deg(D)\leq g_{X}
. Let
K_{X}
be a canonical divisor on
X
. In this case the Riemann–Roch theorem implies

h^{0}(X,D)=\deg(D)-g_{X}+1+h^{0}(X,K_{X}-D).

Because
X
is hyperelliptic, the canonical map factors as
X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
where the second map is given by the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(g_{X}-1)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. By definition, we have

h^{0}(X,K_{X}-D)=\dim\{f\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(g_{X}-1)):\tau^{*}Z(f)\geq D\}

where the vanishing set
Z(f)
is considered with multiplicity. However, since
D
is semireduced, this is equal to

\dim\{f\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(g_{X}-1)):Z(f)\geq\tau^{*}D\}.

In particular
h^{0}(X,K_{X}-D)=g_{X}-\deg(D)
, and so
h^{0}(X,D)=1
. Because
h^{0}(X,H)=2
, the linear system
\lvert D\rvert
cannot contain
\lvert H\rvert
as a sublinear series, and hence
h^{0}(X,D-H)=0
, as required.

When
\deg(D)=g_{X}+1
a similar calculation shows that
h^{0}(X,D)=2=h^{0}(X,H)
. Suppose there exists a nonzero section
s\in H^{0}(X,D-H)
. Multiplication by
s
gives an isomorphism
H^{0}(X,H)\rightarrow H^{0}(X,D)
. Choose
f\in H^{0}(X,D)
such that
Z(f)=D
. Then
f=sh
for some
h\in H^{0}(X,H)
. In other words,
D=H+Z(s)
for some effective divisor
Z(s)
, which contradicts the assumption that
D
is semireduced.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
L
be a line bundle on a hyperelliptic curve
\tau\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of genus
g_{X}
. Then
L
has splitting type
(a,b)
if and only if
L\simeq\tau^{*}\mathcal{O}(b)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
for some semireduced divisor
D
of degree
g_{X}+1-(b-a)
on
X
.

Proof.

First suppose that
L
has splitting type
(a,b)
. From the projection formula, we obtain

\tau_{*}(L\otimes\tau^{*}\mathcal{O}(-b))\simeq\tau_{*}L\otimes\mathcal{O}(-b)\simeq\mathcal{O}(a-b)\oplus\mathcal{O}.

Taking global sections yields

h^{0}(X,L\otimes\tau^{*}\mathcal{O}(-b))=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(a-b)\oplus\mathcal{O})\geq 1.

Therefore
L\otimes\tau^{*}\mathcal{O}(-b)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
for some effective divisor
D
. Now note that

h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-H))=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(\mathcal{O}(a-b)\oplus\mathcal{O})(-1))=0,

so
D
contains no fibers of
\tau
. Finally we have

\deg(D)=\deg(L)-\tau^{*}\mathcal{O}(b)=a+b+g_{X}+1-2b=g_{X}+1-(b-a).

as required.

Conversely suppose that
a\leq b
are integers and
D
is a semireduced divisor on
X
of degree
g_{X}+1-(b-a)
. Let
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
be the splitting type of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
. Because
D
is effective, one obtains

\min\{0,a^{\prime}+1\}+\min\{0,b^{\prime}+1\}=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\tau_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\geq 1

which implies
b^{\prime}\geq 0
. Moreover, the push-pull formula and
Lemma

4.5
yields:

\displaystyle\min\{0,a^{\prime}\}+\min\{0,b^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1)\otimes\tau_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\tau_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-H))

\displaystyle=h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-H))

\displaystyle=0

and hence
b^{\prime}\leq 0
. Thus
b^{\prime}=0
. Finally, we have

a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+g_{X}+1=\deg(D)=g_{X}+1-(b-a),

which yields
a^{\prime}=-(b-a)
. Therefore
\tau_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)=\mathcal{O}(-(b-a))\oplus\mathcal{O}
and another use of the push-pull formula yields

\tau_{*}(\tau^{*}\mathcal{O}(b)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\mathcal{O}(b)\otimes\tau_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)=\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}(b)

as required.
∎

Let
\vec{e}\in\mathcal{Q}

and choose
D_{1}
and

so that

for effective divisors

and

. Further, choose

Now, because

(4.1)

\Delta=(2e_{i}-e_{k}-\deg(D_{1}^{\prime}))H+D_{2}^{\prime}

Appendix A

In this section, we sketch a potential application of

towards the following conjecture in the case

.

Conjecture A.1
.

(
[

)
A
(d-1)
-tuple
\vec{e}
arises as the scrollar invariants of a smooth primitive degree
