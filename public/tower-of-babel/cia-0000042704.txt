FULL TEXT OF ARTICLE:

1. [Text] San Salvador, 19 Nov (EFE)--Salvadoran President Alfredo
Cristiani stated today that the Armed Forces High Command ‘‘does not
dismiss’’ the possibility that those who instigated the assassination
of the six Jesuits on 16 November could have been military.

2. ‘*The High Command members do not dissmiss the possibility that ~
the perpetrators could been Army members, and are ready to get to the~-
bottom of things so that they will be punished,’’ the Salvadoran
president said in statements to EFE and other media.

3. Cristiani, who insisted on the necessity of ‘‘not jumping to
hasty conclusions,’! added that ‘‘the Armed Forces do not want the
institute, per se, to suffer the consequences of what its members do
as individuals.’’

4. On 16 November six Jesuit priests, five of them Spaniards, were
assassinated by uniformed men who machinegunned them in their
residence that morning, while a rigid curfew was in effect in El
Salvador.

5. The Jesuits, who had lived in Rl Salvador for many years, were
stanch critics of the military regimes and of the extreme rightist
sectors of the country.

6. According to Cristiani, *‘the Armed Forces High Command, of which
the president is a part, are totally determined to investigate and
clear up the assassination of the Jesuits.’’

7. Interviewed shortly after meeting with Inocencio Arias, Spanish
under secretary of foreign relations, Cristiani asked that Spain, the
United States, and Great Britain participate in the investigation of
the assassination ‘‘by sending experts. ,

8. The Salvadoran president described the attitude of those persons
in the United States who have hinted at the possibility of suspending
the $1 million daily aid given to El Salvador as ‘‘very simplistic.’’

9. ‘*I trust that this will not be so and I have information that
the large majority of U.S. political leaders do not feel that way,’’
the president added.

10. Thus far, the autopsies performed on the Jesuits by three U.S.
pathologists ‘‘are not conclusive in determinining guilt,’’ the
president added.

11. Cristiani had words of praise for Ignacio Ellacuria, one of the
Jesuits killed, *‘‘who had become a constructive opposer of the
Salvadoran Government,’’ according to him.

12. Salvadoran television, which has been state-controlled since the
guerrilla offensive started 1 week ago, carried some clips of
Ellacuria in which he requested applause for Cristiani at an event
held some months ago in the University of El Salvador.

13. Some Salvadoran rightist sectors have publicly accused the :
Farabundo Marti National Liberation Front (FMLN) guerillas of being
behind the Jesuits’ killing.

14. Ellacuria told EFE, 4 days before he was killed, that Cristiani
represents ‘‘the civilian sector’’ of the ruling ARENA [Nationalist
Republican Alliance] Party, in contrast with two others whom he
described as ‘‘militarists’’ and ‘‘death squad-oriented.’’

15. ‘‘ARENA, per se, has nothing to do with violent incidents,’’
Cristiani said, commenting on Ellacuria’s appraisal and adding that
‘‘obviously there are violent groups in the country, but they are not
known to the government or to ARENA.
