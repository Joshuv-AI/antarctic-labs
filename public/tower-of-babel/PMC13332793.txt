Proresolving mediators as cardiovascular biomarkers: solo players or an orchestra?
Authors: Luís M Cardoso dos Santos, Magnus Bäck
Published: 2026

This editorial refers to ‘Sex-specific associations of perioperative resolvin D1 and D2 dynamics with postoperative atrial fibrillation in cardiac surgery: a secondary analysis of a prospective observational study’, by J. Heine et al. https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjopen/oeag095.

Although omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (n-3 PUFA) have long been extensively studied for their cardioprotective and anti-arrhythmic properties, clinical trial have generated heterogeneous results.1 Furthermore, large clinical trials and meta-analyses have raised concerns regarding a potential association between n-3 PUFA supplementation and an increased risk of atrial fibrillation.2 This paradox highlights the need to better understand dose-dependent effects and the underlying electrophysiological mechanisms.

Downstream metabolism of n-3 PUFA yields specialized proresolving mediators (SPM), including resolvins (Rv), maresins, protectins, and lipoxins (Figure 1).3 Within the Rv family, RvD1 and RvD2 are generated via the sequential action of 15-lipoxygenase followed by 5-lipoxygenase on the n-3 PUFA docosahexaenoic acid. Potential effects in atrial fibrillation may include acting through specific receptors to dampen cardiovascular inflammation3 and exert direct effects on cardiac potassium channels.4 Indeed, others have shown that RvD1 administration could attenuate atrial fibrillation in a rat model by resolving inflammation.5,6

(A) Individual analysis, where one or few metabolites are chosen, e.g. RvD1 and RvD2 (dashed arrows), are measured and correlated with a certain disease without considering the overall lipid mediator profile. In this issue of the European Heart Journal Open, Heine et al.7 link post-operative atrial fibrillation (POAF) after open-heart surgery with dynamic SPM changes in the perioperative period. Specifically, the authors employed a mixed-effects Tobit regression to establish an overall association of temporal post-operative trends of predicted serum levels of RvD1 and RvD2 with POAF. The results confirmed that POAF is common following open-heart surgery with an incidence of 51% in this study. Subjects experiencing POAF exhibited lower predicted RvD2 levels, whereas predicted RvD1 levels were higher. Given their common role in resolving inflammation,3 the differential directions of RvD1 and RvD2 may point to distinct regulatory mechanisms. Moreover, and although RvD1 and RvD2 share most of their biosynthetic pathway, cellular-specific expression of unique enzymatic machinery determining the endpoint product may promote certain cells to preferentially produce one over the other.8 This raises the question of whether the higher levels of RvD1 reflect a dysregulation of the metabolic pathway, or whether it represents a compensatory response to impaired receptor-mediated downstream signalling.

Of note, the interpretation of the repeated perioperative Rv measurements should be interpreted with caution. The results are presented as predicted mean values rather than absolute values, and the analysis incorporates all time points with different number of subjects at each point.7 At no single time point, statistically significant differences in either RvD1 or RvD2 serum levels were observed between the POAF and no-POAF groups. This could be a consequence of the decrease in the number of samples measured post-operatively, due to early intensive care discharge, which may introduce a bias in the Rv levels results.

Nonetheless, and withstand the lack of statistical significance, a key finding in this work is the inverse association of temporal RvD1 and RvD2 trends to predict POAF after cardiac surgery. This illustrates a fundamental question on how to interpret SPMs as cardiovascular biomarkers: should individual metabolites be considered separately—often focusing on one or a few metabolites, as in the current study (Figure 1A)—or should SPMs be evaluated at the family or whole-class level (Figure 1B)? In previous studies performing a full lipidomic profiling, SPMs have been grouped as total proresolving mediators, PUFA metabolome families or SPM classes.9 When considered as individual mediators, SPM measures after ST-elevation myocardial infarction also present opposing trajectories,9 illustrating the need for an optimal depth for SPM biomarker associations to obtain a full image of the resolution activity in with cardiovascular disease, as illustrated in Figure 1.

Following subgroup analysis, Heine et al.7 also provide compelling evidence regarding the impact of sex on SPM dynamics, since the association of RvD1 and RvD2 with POAF was stronger in women compared with men. The latter is consistent with studies on systemic inflammation-induced endothelial dysfunction, in which women exhibited an accelerated resolution of inflammation compared with men, specifically via neutrophils, mediated by an elevation of the D-Rv pathway.10

Another interesting perspective on the intrinsic capacity of individuals to resolve inflammation in relation to RvD1 and RvD2 levels is also introduced by the authors, with patients being classified as fast or slow C-reactive protein decline profiles. The higher predicted RvD1 serum levels in subjects with compared to without POAF being most pronounced in the slow C-reactive protein decline group,7 may point to that RvD1 increase is a second line protection activated in the absence of an effective resolution by RvD2.

Taken together, the results from D-series Rv measures in relation to POAF illustrate the complexity of inflammation resolution after cardiac surgery. In particular in light of the following aspects; differential regulation and cellular distribution of SPMs biosynthesis, a complete or targeted overview of the proinflammatory and proresolving lipid mediator profiles, sex-differences, and a potential sequential activation depending on the degree of resolution obtained.
