Mass relations in heavy hadrons from Jensen-like inequalities
Wen-Xuan Zhang 1,2,3, Wen-Nian Liu 4,5, Duojie Jia 2,6

Abstract
We demonstrate that mass inequalities for hadrons with one or more heavy quarks arise primarily from the concavity of binding energies in the quark model, reflecting short-range Coulombic interactions and long-range confinement. Empirical two-body bindings B i ​ j ¯ B_{i\bar{j}} are extracted from spin-averaged meson masses, ensuring model independence and direct use of experimental data. Fitting these as functions of reduced mass μ i ​ j \mu_{ij} reveals a critical confinement scale of 1.34 fm where bindings turn positive. The concave B ⁡ ( 1 / μ ) B(1/\mu) justifies Jensen-like inequalities under flavor permutation, reproducing relations like m x ​ y ¯ > 1 2 ​ ( m x ​ x ¯ + m y ​ y ¯ ) m_{x\bar{y}}>\frac{1}{2}(m_{x\bar{x}}+m_{y\bar{y}}) and baryon analogs, including m x ​ y ​ z > 1 3 ​ ( m x ​ x ​ x + m y ​ y ​ y + m z ​ z ​ z ) m_{xyz}>\frac{1}{3}(m_{xxx}+m_{yyy}+m_{zzz}) . Hadron mass decomposition validates Δ ​ M EXP ≈ Δ ​ B + Δ ​ C \Delta M_{\textrm{EXP}}\approx\Delta B+\Delta C with σ ∼ 2.07 \sigma\sim 2.07 MeV for mesons and baryons. Promoting inequalities to equalities, we predict masses for unobserved heavy baryons (e.g., M ⁡ ( Ω b ∗ ) = 6076.6 M(\Omega_{b}^{\ast})=6076.6\, MeV, M ⁡ ( Ξ c ​ c ∗ ) = 3703.6 M(\Xi_{cc}^{\ast})=3703.6\, MeV and M ⁡ ( Ω c ​ c ∗ ) = 3802.4 M(\Omega_{cc}^{\ast})=3802.4\, MeV) and identify favored quark-exchange scattering channels.

I
Introduction

Understanding the nonperturbative regime of strong interactions remains a central challenge in quantum chromodynamics (QCD).
At short distances, the interaction exhibits Coulomb-like behavior with asymptotic freedom,
potentially leading to concave forms in binding energies from inverse-mass dependence.
In contrast, at long ranges, confinement manifests as a linear potential, driving string-like bindings that may break at critical scales,
introducing strong condensation and unquenched effects and complicating mass predictions for heavy hadronic states.
This is timely given the recent LHCb discovery of the doubly-charmed baryon
\Xi_{cc}^{+}

Aaij et al. 2026

and the surge in triply-heavy baryon mass predictions
Zhou et al. 2025
.

Empirical mass inequalities among hadrons containing heavy or strange quarks provide valuable insights into these dynamics.
Mixed-flavor mesons and baryons systematically exceed the mean value of same-flavor counterparts, as in

m_{x\bar{y}}>\frac{1}{2}(m_{x\bar{x}}+m_{y\bar{y}}),\quad m_{xxy}>\frac{1}{2}(m_{xxx}+m_{xyy}),

(1)

with
x\neq y
and at least one heavy flavor
Bertlmann and Martin 1980
;
Richard and Taxil 1983
;
Weingarten 1983
;
Witten 1983
;
Nussinov 1984
;
Lieb 1985
;
Martin et al. 1986
;
Anwar et al. 2018
;
Karliner and Rosner 2020a
.
These relations, valid for pseudoscalar, vector mesons as well as ground-state baryons, indicate concavity in the mass function with respect to energy scale,
stemming from flavor-dependent interactions in the quark model.

In this work, we derive these inequalities from the concavity of chromoelectric binding energies
B_{i\bar{j}}
,
extracted empirically from spin-averaged hadron masses to ensure model independence and reliance on experimental data.
By fitting
B(\mu)
and
B(1/\mu)
, interpreted as the mean values of the Cornell potential,
we explore their concave dependence on reduced mass
\mu_{ij}
, yielding Jensen-like inequalities under flavor permutation.
This fitting further reveals a critical confinement scale upon positive bindings, aligning with predictions for string-breaking distances.
We validate the approach with
\Delta M_{\textrm{EXP}}\approx\Delta B+\Delta C
,
predict masses for unobserved doubly and triply heavy baryons,
and discuss potential decay and scattering processes.

II
Mass inequalities and their origin

The mass inequalities (
1
) can be understood in terms of a Jensen-like inequality

f(\sum_{i}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{i})>\sum_{i}^{n}\alpha_{i}f(x_{i}),

(2)

for a concave function
f(x)
, with weights
\alpha_{i}
summing to 1.
For
n=2
,
\alpha_{i}=1/2
,
x_{1}=x
,
x_{2}=y
gives
m_{x\bar{y}}>\frac{1}{2}(m_{x\bar{x}}+m_{y\bar{y}})

and
m_{xxy}>\frac{1}{2}(m_{xxx}+m_{xyy})
. For
n=3
, weights
\alpha_{i}=1/3
yield
m_{xyz}>\frac{1}{3}(m_{xxx}+m_{yyy}+m_{zzz})
.
These relations hold for mesons with at least one heavy quark and baryons
where the
xy
pair exceeds the
ud
diquark mass
Karliner and Rosner 2020a
.
Such inequalities arise from concavity of flavor-dependent interactions
Nussinov 1984
;
Lieb 1985
;
Martin et al. 1986
,
particularly when exchanging heavy-light flavors, indicating flavor symmetry breaking.

The concavity of mass function stems from the underlying quark model dynamics.
To demonstrate this, consider the hadron mass in second-order perturbation theory:

\displaystyle H=H_{0}+\lambda V

\displaystyle,\quad H_{0}|\psi_{0}\rangle=E_{0}|\psi_{0}\rangle,

\displaystyle\textrm{d}_{\lambda}E_{0}=\langle\psi_{0}|V|\psi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle,\quad\textrm{d}^{2}_{\lambda}E_{0}=2\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{|\langle\psi_{n}|V|\psi_{0}\rangle|^{2}}{E_{0}-E_{n}}\leq 0,

(3)

where
H_{0}
is the leading-order,
V
denotes perturbative interactions,
and variational parameter
\lambda
is related to inverse reduced mass
1/\mu
.
The concavity can be shown by analyzing the second derivatives of the mass with respect to parameter
\lambda
,
ensuring the condition for Jensen-like inequality in its concave form.
In the quark model, this mass incorporates both leading-order and perturbative interactions:

m=\sum_{i}m_{i}+E_{\textrm{BD}}+E_{\textrm{CMI}},

(4)

where

\displaystyle E_{\textrm{BD}}=\sum_{i<j}B_{ij(\bar{j})},

(5)

\displaystyle E_{\textrm{CMI}}=-\sum_{i<j}\langle\mathbf{\lambda}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{\lambda}_{j}\rangle\langle\mathbf{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{j}\rangle C_{ij(\bar{j})}.

(6)

Here,
E_{\textrm{BD}}
captures confinement and short-range chromoelectric binding, while
E_{\text{CMI}}
represents the perturbative chromomagnetic interaction.
The binding energies
B_{ij(\bar{j})}
are regarded as mean values of Cornell potential for
\mathbf{1}_{c}
(
i\bar{j}
) and
\mathbf{\bar{3}}_{c}
(
ij
).
The coupling parameters
C_{ij(\bar{j})}=A_{ij(\bar{j})}/m_{i}m_{j}
are taken from Ref.
Zhang et al. 2025
,
where
A_{i\bar{j}}
is fitted to a convex function with respect to the reduced mass
\mu
.
Further investigation for concavity of interaction energies stems from the condition (
3
) when
\lambda
represents
1/\mu
,
leading to the fitting of binding energies
B(1/\mu)
.

From another perspective, the mass inequalities (
1
) can be related to Eqs.(
4
)–(
6
), by decomposing the differences:

\displaystyle\Delta M=m(x\bar{y})-\frac{1}{2}[m(x\bar{x})+m(y\bar{y})]

\displaystyle\Delta B=E_{\textrm{BD}}(x\bar{y})-\frac{1}{2}[E_{\textrm{BD}}(x\bar{x})+E_{\textrm{BD}}(y\bar{y})]

\displaystyle\Delta C=E_{\textrm{CMI}}(x\bar{y})-\frac{1}{2}[E_{\textrm{CMI}}(x\bar{x})+E_{\textrm{CMI}}(y\bar{y})]

\displaystyle\Delta M=\Delta B+\Delta C,

(7)

This decomposition enables us to confirm the mass inequalities and therefore predict mass relations by assuming
\Delta M=\Delta B+\Delta C
,
upon evaluation of
\Delta B
and
\Delta C
.

V
Summary and conclusions

In this work, we investigate a set of mass inequalities for hadrons containing one or more heavy quarks,
demonstrating that they arise from the concavity of binding energies
B_{ij(\bar{j})}
as the mean values of Cornell potential.
By employing the quark model and decomposing the hadron mass into leading-order, binding contributions (
5
) from confinement,
and chromomagnetic interactions (
6
), we establish a mass relation
\Delta M=\Delta B+\Delta C
under flavor permutations.
This approach yields Jensen-like inequalities for distinct flavors, such as
m_{xyz}>\frac{1}{3}(m_{xxx}+m_{yyy}+m_{zzz})
for averages among three baryons,
attributing the concavity to flavor-dependent interactions with respect to
1/\mu
in second-order perturbation theory.

We extract effective two-body binding energies
B_{i\bar{j}}
and hyperfine couplings
C_{i\bar{j}}
from meson masses,
including an extra binding of -40.6 MeV for the
s\bar{s}
system.
These bindings are parametrized as functions of the reduced mass
\mu_{ij}
with logarithmic scaling,
generating a concave form
B(1/\mu)
that satisfies the second-derivative condition (
3
).
This fitting reveals a critical confinement scale of 1.34 fm, beyond which bindings become positive,
aligning with predictions for the string-breaking distance and indicating the onset of strong unquenched effects.

For baryonic systems, we adopt scaling ratios of
B_{ij}=B_{i\bar{j}}/2
from color factors and
C_{ij}=0.85C_{i\bar{j}}

to minimize the standard error to
\sigma=2.07
MeV across
\Delta M_{\textrm{EXP}}\approx\Delta B+\Delta C
.
This close agreement confirms that mass inequalities are primarily driven by the concavity of binding energies and chromomagnetic interactions.
Using the relation
\Delta M=\Delta B+\Delta C
, we predict mass spectra for unobserved doubly and triply heavy baryons,
as well as potential scattering channels like
D\bar{D}\to\eta_{c}+\pi
and
\Xi_{c}^{*}\Xi_{c}^{*}\to\Xi^{*}+\Xi_{cc}^{*}
.
Inequalities with large differences support physical pictures of the quark-exchange model,
due to strong bindings and positive differences. These predictions are expected to be validated in future experiments.
