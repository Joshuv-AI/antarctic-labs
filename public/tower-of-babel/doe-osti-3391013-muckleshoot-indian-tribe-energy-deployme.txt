1.0 Executive Summary
The Muckleshoot Indian Tribe (MIT) collaborated with our Project Partner, GRID Alternatives (GRID), to install 132
kilowatts of direct current (kW-DC) of rooftop solar on three Tribal facilities. The three facilities are the Tribe’s
Youth Drop-In Center, Canoe Shed, and Water Treatment Facility. The solar PV systems were originally anticipated
to offset approximately 45% of the aggregate annual electricity usage of the three buildings.

A major aspect of the MIT-ED project was providing hands-on paid training to five Muckleshoot Building
Maintenance workers in solar PV installations, operations, and maintenance. The scope of work aligns with the
Tribe’s goals of building local capacity and providing real world work experience and potential career opportunities
in solar PV to its citizens. The Building Maintenance Department committed five of its current FTE employees to
the project. GRID provided guidance for the MIT project team on identifying paid trainees as well as end goals of
skill development through training, including a long-term Operations and Maintenance (O&M) plan tailored to the
Tribe’s goals of local capacity building and stewardship of natural resources.

2.0 Background
Established in 1857, MIT is located in King County, Washington, approximately 20 miles southeast of the City of
Seattle on a plateau between the Green and White Rivers. The reservation is checkerboard shaped, spanning over
six square miles, adjacent to the town of Auburn, Washington. The goal of the MIT-ED project is to promote energy
development on Muckleshoot Tribal land.

3.0 Project Objectives
The Muckleshoot Indian Tribe (MIT) collaborated with GRID by entering into an enforceable installation contract
and installed 132 kW-DC of grid-tied, rooftop solar on three Tribal facilities. GRID Alternatives worked closely with
the Tribe’s MIT-ED project team to develop a comprehensive project management plan including, but not limited
to, design and engineering, work force development goals, and equipment procurement and installation. The
project scope was successful in design and implementation and directly aligns with the Muckleshoot Tribe’s goal
of building local capacity and providing real work experience and potential career opportunities in solar PV to
workers of the Building Maintenance Program – MIT-ED Project Team. GRID provided guidance and expertise as
a project partner to the MIT-ED project team by assisting in the identification of paid trainees and the achievement
of end goals including skills development through training and the development of a long-term O&M plan tailored
to the Tribe’s goals of local capacity building.

The Building Maintenance team is responsible for operations and maintenance of all 92 properties making up the
Tribal Administration and Government Operations. The Muckleshoot Indian Tribe conducts all electrical
permitting through the Department of Labor and Industries. The Building Maintenance Director, MIT-ED Project
Lead, is a qualified Journeyman Electrician and has over 35 years of experience in residential and commercial
electrical work. Brook Boone was able to conduct site surveys and connection integrity in all facets of the solar
technology and operations of the selected buildings. Once the permits were received from the Labor and
Industries permitting process, Building Maintenance staff invited PSE to conduct site surveys and sign off on the
integrity of the solar systems installed. PSE then turned the meters on in conjunction with the Interconnection
Agreements which allows for excess energy not used to be fed back into the electrical grid, serving not only the
Muckleshoot Indian Tribe but the adjacent municipalities of Auburn, Enumclaw, and Black Diamond. Energy
harvested through the solar panels offsets the energy consumption and costs of energy for the target facilities
with the remaining power being fed back into the grid.

5.0 Conclusion and Recommendations
Goal: The MIT-ED Project has been successful in promoting energy development on Muckleshoot Tribal lands. The
Muckleshoot Indian Tribe (MIT) partnered with GRID Alternatives to install 132 kW-DC of rooftop solar on three
Tribal facilities. As shown in the charts below, the solar arrays on the target facilities are functioning properly and
providing electricity sufficient to significantly reduce the energy drawn from the grid through PSE and reduce the
costs associated with that energy. The results of this project are close to the initial projections.

To obtain the data for the 12 months prior to commissioning of the PV arrays and 12 months after commissioning,
PSE provided charts for each month that compared the target month to the same month in the previous year.
Overall, the entire project demonstrated an annual decrease of 42% in kWh usage, from 324,192.92kWh to
187,846.62kWh and a 33% decrease in actual costs, from $39,355.58 to $26,258.60. The imbalance in percentage
decreases may be attributed to an estimated 9.4% increase in the price per kWh for electricity in King County, WA,
from 2023 to 20242 and further 12% increase from 2024 to 20253.

Water Treatment Facility

The Water Treatment Facility is the largest facility included in the project. The original estimate for the solar array
was more than 200kW to allow the array to generate enough electricity to offset all of the energy drawn from the
grid. The State of Washington limits solar arrays to 100kW or less, and the installed system was rated at 58.32 kW.
Even with the size limitation, the new solar array has offset 35% of the usage (193,888.85 to 125,442.12kWh) and
28% of the costs ($24,105.23 to $17,412.19) associated with energy drawn from the grid. The 58.32kWp system
produced 66,883.22kWh to the facility in its first year of operation.

6.0 Lessons Learned
The MIT-ED Project was a success and furthered the efforts of the Tribes Long Term Energy Goal: Securing Tribal
sovereignty by enhancing and strengthening the Tribe’s energy and economic infrastructure.

In our project completion meeting with the MIT-ED Project team, the following lessons learned were highlighted
in the discussion:
 • Existing Infrastructure
 o Building selected did not have existing “tie offs” which required installation of tie offs to increase safety
 for the workforce development and training component of the grant.
 ▪ This was an added lesson to performing the scope of work and completing our proposed
 activities, which enhanced the training and workforce development component.
 ▪ Additionally, the training furthered Building Maintenance Department capacity. Five FTE
 employees received training and are positioned to be the ongoing operation and maintenance
 crews for the solar panel arrays.
 • Grants Reporting
 o The Annual Program Review created an opportunity for Building Maintenance employees to attend and
 present the project. For the majority, this was the first experience in conducting such work.
 • Installation of Solar Array on existing infrastructure
 o The Project Team consisted of Project Director, two Journeyman Electricians, one Electrician
 Apprentice, and one trainee. During the implementation of the proposed activities, we were able to
 secure knowledge of all aspects of the project deliverables. This included, but was not limited to,
 procurement, engineering, material handling, panel and bracket installation, electrical maintenance and
 engineering, safety which included OSHA 10 certificates and safety testing, testing solar array systems,
 operations and maintenance of solar array, weatherproofing, code compliance, man lift operations, and
 organizational partnerships.
 o Monitor all solar panels closely to ensure they are working properly and do routine check more
 frequently. The Drop-in Center’s solar panel monitor is not functioning properly, but the panels are
 working efficiently according to the data provided.

All proposed activities and phases of the project were carried out in collaboration with the project partner, GRID
Alternatives. Originally, procuring materials and equipment for solar arrays was unknown territory, but because
of the MIT-ED Project, we now have a repository of vendors to work with for future solar arrays and carrying out
other activities beyond the project period.
