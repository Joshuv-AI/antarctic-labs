Heterogeneous-Horizon Exact-Weight Local SGD
Dmitry Pasechnyuk-Vilensky, Martin Takáč

Abstract
We study adaptive aggregation for heterogeneous local SGD in convex finite-sum optimization, allowing heterogeneous local horizons, minibatch sizes, gradient noise, and participation. We introduce HEW-Local SGD, a corrected local-SGD method that chooses nodewise server weights by minimizing an explicit one-round upper bound on the next objective value. This yields an exact local-control formulation with a threshold simplex update, separable amplitude updates, and a one-step guarantee under arbitrary predictable participation. We also introduce two post-local variants: a corrected heterogeneous method and a simpler homogeneous specialization. We establish one-step guarantees and global benchmark-style convergence results. In the regimes where comparison is appropriate, the theory matches the qualitative communication-efficient picture of recent LocalSGD/SCAFFOLD analyses, while also giving explicit guarantees for unequal local horizons.

A key but usually fixed component in this literature is server aggregation. Standard choices include uniform averaging, data-size weighting, and normalized aggregation
[
9

10
]
. We study adaptive aggregation when clients differ simultaneously in horizon, noise, and update scale.

Our starting point is
Heterogeneous-Horizon Exact-Weight Local SGD
(HEW-Local SGD). It is a corrected local-SGD method with heterogeneous local horizons, nodewise amplitudes, and exact simplex-constrained server weights. Its central object is an explicit one-round certificate for the next expected objective value, obtained from endpoint mean–variance bounds for heterogeneous corrected local branches. This gives an exact local-control baseline in which the server weights are theorem-backed minimizers.

This baseline is conservative for practice because it optimizes before local randomness is realized and is driven by upper bounds. We therefore introduce two post-local variants. The first keeps corrected heterogeneous local branches and optimizes aggregation over realized corrected endpoints. The second further specializes to a homogeneous plain-local regime with a simpler post-local rule. The paper thus studies one idea — endpoint-adaptive aggregation — in three forms.

Contribution.

(1) Adaptive aggregation for heterogeneous local SGD as a single exact endpoint-based principle, and derive from it one local-control method and two post-local methods. (2) Convergence rates that, in the structured comparison regimes used in recent LocalSGD/SCAFFOLD theory, recover the same communication-efficient scaling picture. (3) Explicit guarantees for unequal local horizons, giving a new rate statement for heterogeneous numbers of local steps.

Limitations.

The strongest comparisons to prior SOTA hold in the structured regimes where such comparisons are standard; the general heterogeneous theory is less sharp. The local-control baseline is intentionally conservative, which motivates the post-local variants. The analysis is restricted to convex finite-sum models.

Assumption 2.1

(Convex smooth sum-of-sums model with invariant ball)
.

Let

F(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}(x),\qquad F_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{m_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\phi_{ij}(x),

(1)

Assume there exists

i

Assumption 2.2

(Minibatch stochastic gradients)
.

For each node
i

v_{i}^{2}:=\sup_{x\in B(x_{\star},R)}\frac{1}{m_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\left\lVert\nabla\phi_{ij}(x)-\nabla F_{i}(x)\right\rVert^{2}.

Assumption 2.3

(Amplitude range and executable variance proxies)

There exist constants

\mu_{i}(\theta_{i};U_{t},Q_{t}):=s_{i}(U_{t}^{\sharp};\theta_{i})-\rho_{i}(\theta_{i};U_{t},Q_{t}).

Moreover, if the executable variance proxies

Theorem 3.2

(Exact one-step heterogeneous local control law)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, let

(w_{t}^{\mathrm{id}},\theta_{t}^{\mathrm{id}})\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\Delta_{S_{t}}\\

Then

\mathbb{E}[F(x_{t+1})-F_{\star}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(w_{t}^{\mathrm{id}},\theta_{t}^{\mathrm{id}})\leq\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{ex}}(w_{t}^{\mathrm{ex}},\theta_{t}^{\mathrm{ex}}).

(5)

Moreover, for every predictable feasible benchmark pair

\mathbb{E}[F(x_{t+1})-F_{\star}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(\bar{w}_{t},\bar{\theta}_{t})-\Gamma_{t},\qquad\Gamma_{t}\geq 0,

\Gamma_{t}=\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(\bar{w}_{t},\bar{\theta}_{t})-\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(w_{t}^{\mathrm{id}},\theta_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}).

For fixed amplitudes, the weight subproblem is a strictly convex quadratic
program with the exact KKT law

w_{i,t}^{\star}=\frac{\bigl(\mu_{i}(\theta_{i};U_{t},Q_{t})-\lambda_{t}\bigr)_{+}}{L\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i};U_{t},Q_{t})},

where
\lambda_{t}
is chosen so that the weights sum to one.

\mathcal{S}_{t}=[n]

6
Conclusion

We studied adaptive aggregation for heterogeneous local SGD with unequal local horizons. On the theory side, we derived an exact one-step control formulation and global convergence guarantees under full participation, including convex, PL, and higher-order regimes. On the algorithmic side, we introduced post-local variants that retain the same endpoint-based viewpoint while avoiding the conservatism of the original local-control surrogate. The experiments indicate that adaptive weighting is most useful when heterogeneity induces a meaningful ordering of post-local client endpoints.

Appendix A
Scalar lemmas and deterministic caps

Lemma A.1

(Gradient-gap inequalities and deterministic cap)
.

Under Assumption
B.1
,

f_{t}\leq R\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert,\qquad\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}\geq\frac{f_{t}^{2}}{R^{2}}.

(35)

Moreover, for every
x\in B(x_{\star},R)
,

\left\lVert\nabla F(x)\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2L(F(x)-F_{\star}),

(36)

and

F(x)-F_{\star}\leq\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{LR^{2}}{2}=\bar{f}.

(37)

Proof.

By convexity of
F
,

F(x_{t})-F_{\star}\leq\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),x_{t}-x_{\star}\right\rangle\leq\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert\left\lVert x_{t}-x_{\star}\right\rVert\leq R\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert,

which proves (
35
). For the upper gradient bound, define
y:=x-\frac{1}{L}\nabla F(x)
.
By
L
-smoothness,

F(y)\leq F(x)+\left\langle\nabla F(x),y-x\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert y-x\right\rVert^{2}=F(x)-\frac{1}{2L}\left\lVert\nabla F(x)\right\rVert^{2}.

Since
F_{\star}\leq F(y)
, rearranging proves (
36
).
Finally, because
x_{\star}
minimizes the differentiable convex function
F
,

\nabla F(x_{\star})=0
. Applying
L
-smoothness between
x
and
x_{\star}
gives

F(x)\leq F(x_{\star})+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{\star}),x-x_{\star}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}=F_{\star}+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}.

The ball condition then implies (
37
).
∎

Definition A.2

(

operator)
.

and

Lemma A.3

(Quadratic descent is majorized by

.

For every

Proof.

T_{a}(u)-(u-au^{2})=\frac{u}{1+au}-u+au^{2}=\frac{a^{2}u^{3}}{1+au}\geq 0.

Lemma A.4

(Composition)
.

For all

Proof.

T_{a_{2}}(T_{a_{1}}(u))=\frac{u/(1+a_{1}u)}{1+a_{2}u/(1+a_{1}u)}=\frac{u}{1+(a_{1}+a_{2})u}.

(

For every

, the map

is increasing on

Lemma A.6

(Branch telescoping)

Lemma A.7

(Quadratic-linear scalar recursion)

Let

(41)

(Deterministic surrogate upper state)
.

Under Assumption
B.9
, define

g_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[F(x_{t})-F_{\star}].

A deterministic surrogate upper state at round
t
is a deterministic pair

(u_{t},\chi_{t})\in[0,\infty)^{2}
such that

g_{t}\leq u_{t}\leq\bar{f},\qquad\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\chi_{t}.

Definition B.11

(Deterministic surrogate local objectives)
.

For deterministic
(u,\chi)
with
0\leq u\leq\bar{f}
, define

A_{i}(\theta_{i}):=\frac{\theta_{i}}{2LR^{2}},\qquad s_{i}(u;\theta_{i}):=u-T_{A_{i}(\theta_{i})}(u),

\rho_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi):=32e^{2\theta_{i}}\theta_{i}^{3}u+\frac{16\theta_{i}+64e^{2\theta_{i}}\theta_{i}^{3}}{L}\chi+8e^{2\theta_{i}}\frac{\theta_{i}^{3}v_{i}^{2}}{LH_{i}b_{i}},

\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi):=16e^{2\theta_{i}}\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}}{L}u+32e^{2\theta_{i}}\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}}{L^{2}}\chi+\left(2\theta_{i}^{2}+4e^{2\theta_{i}}\theta_{i}^{4}\right)\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}},

\mu_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi):=s_{i}(u;\theta_{i})-\rho_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi),

and

\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(w,\theta;u,\chi):=u-\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\mu_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi)+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}^{2}\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi),

The executable surrogate objective

\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{ex}}(w,\theta;u,\chi)
is defined by replacing every
occurrence of
v_{i}^{2}
by
\widehat{v}_{i,t}^{2}
.

Theorem B.12

(Global surrogate exact local system)
.

Assume Assumption
B.9
. Let

u_{0}\in[g_{0},\bar{f}],\qquad\chi_{0}\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{i,0}\right\rVert^{2},

(44)

For every
t\geq 0
, let

(w_{t}^{\mathrm{id}},\theta_{t}^{\mathrm{id}})\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\Delta_{n}\\
\theta\in[\underline{\theta},\bar{\theta}]^{n}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(w,\theta;u_{t},\chi_{t}),

and define

u_{t+1}:=\min\!\left\{\bar{f},\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(w_{t}^{\mathrm{id}},\theta_{t}^{\mathrm{id}};u_{t},\chi_{t})\right\}.

(45)

\chi_{t+1}:=A_{\chi}+B_{\chi}u_{t}+C_{\chi}\chi_{t},

where

A_{\chi}:=6\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{H_{i}b_{i}},\qquad B_{\chi}:=144L\bar{\theta}^{2},\qquad C_{\chi}:=288\bar{\theta}^{2}.

Then
(u_{t},\chi_{t})
is a deterministic surrogate upper state for every

t\geq 0
, i.e.

g_{t}\leq u_{t}\leq\bar{f},\qquad\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\chi_{t}.

(46)

The same upper-state domination holds if the idealized surrogate objective is
replaced by the executable surrogate objective.

Definition B.13

(Comparator convex coefficients)
.

Fix a deterministic feasible comparator pair
(\bar{w},\bar{\theta})
. Define

\underline{A}_{i}(\bar{\theta}_{i}):=\frac{A_{i}(\bar{\theta}_{i})}{1+A_{i}(\bar{\theta}_{i})\bar{f}}=\frac{\bar{\theta}_{i}}{2LR^{2}(1+\bar{\theta}_{i}/4)},

a_{\mathrm{cvx}}(\bar{w},\bar{\theta}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{w}_{i}\,\underline{A}_{i}(\bar{\theta}_{i}),\quad\beta_{\mathrm{cvx}}(\bar{w},\bar{\theta}):=96\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{w}_{i}\bar{\theta}_{i}^{2}+32\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{w}_{i}^{2}\bar{\theta}_{i}^{4},

\gamma_{\mathrm{cvx}}(\bar{w},\bar{\theta}):=\frac{256}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{w}_{i}\bar{\theta}_{i}+\frac{64}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{w}_{i}^{2}\bar{\theta}_{i}^{4},\;\delta_{\mathrm{cvx}}(\bar{w},\bar{\theta}):=\frac{32}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{w}_{i}\bar{\theta}_{i}^{2}\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{H_{i}b_{i}}+\frac{16}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{w}_{i}^{2}\bar{\theta}_{i}^{2}\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{H_{i}b_{i}}.

Theorem B.14

(Comparator convex recursion for the exact optimized controller)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
B.12
, let

(\bar{w},\bar{\theta})
be any deterministic feasible comparator pair. Then the
exact optimized controller satisfies

u_{t+1}\leq u_{t}-a_{\mathrm{cvx}}(\bar{w},\bar{\theta})\,u_{t}^{2}+\beta_{\mathrm{cvx}}(\bar{w},\bar{\theta})\,u_{t}+\gamma_{\mathrm{cvx}}(\bar{w},\bar{\theta})\,\chi_{t}+\delta_{\mathrm{cvx}}(\bar{w},\bar{\theta}).

(47)

Consequently, if

\bar{\chi}:=\max\left\{\chi_{0},\frac{A_{\chi}+B_{\chi}\bar{f}}{1-C_{\chi}}\right\},

(48)

then
\chi_{t}\leq\bar{\chi}
for all
t
, and therefore

u_{t+1}\leq u_{t}-a_{\mathrm{cvx}}\,u_{t}^{2}+\beta_{\mathrm{cvx}}\,u_{t}+d_{\mathrm{cvx}},\qquad d_{\mathrm{cvx}}:=\gamma_{\mathrm{cvx}}\bar{\chi}+\delta_{\mathrm{cvx}},

(49)

with the comparator-dependent coefficients suppressed for readability.
If the positive root

m_{\mathrm{cvx}}:=\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{cvx}}+\sqrt{\beta_{\mathrm{cvx}}^{2}+4a_{\mathrm{cvx}}d_{\mathrm{cvx}}}}{2a_{\mathrm{cvx}}}

also satisfies

2a_{\mathrm{cvx}}m_{\mathrm{cvx}}\leq 1+\beta_{\mathrm{cvx}},

then for every
T\geq 0
,

g_{T}\leq u_{T}\leq m_{\mathrm{cvx}}+\frac{1}{((u_{0}-m_{\mathrm{cvx}})_{+})^{-1}+a_{\mathrm{cvx}}T}.

In particular,

g_{T}\leq u_{T}\leq m_{\mathrm{cvx}}+\frac{1}{u_{0}^{-1}+a_{\mathrm{cvx}}T}.

This is the main closed heterogeneous global theorem for the local viewpoint. It is stated
for the exact optimized controller itself, not for a frozen comparator or a
uniformized proxy. The price of this generality is that the statement is a
surrogate convex recursion rather than a sharp direct Bellman PL theorem.

Assumption B.15

(Uniform-controller common-schedule specialization)
.

Assume, in addition to Assumption
B.9
, that

H_{i}\equiv H,\qquad b_{i}\equiv b,\qquad\theta_{i,t}\equiv\vartheta,\qquad w_{i,t}\equiv\frac{1}{n},\qquad 288\vartheta^{2}<1.

Define
v^{2}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}v_{i}^{2}
.

Definition B.16

(Deterministic Bellman state of the uniform-controller branch)
.

Under Assumption
B.15
, define

q_{t}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}.

The deterministic Bellman state of this branch is
(g_{t},q_{t})
.

Theorem B.17

(Direct one-step Bellman inequality)
.

Under Assumption
B.15
,

g_{t+1}\leq g_{t}-a_{\mathrm{dir}}g_{t}^{2}+\beta_{\mathrm{dir}}g_{t}+\gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}q_{t}+\delta_{\mathrm{dir}},

where

a_{\mathrm{dir}}:=\frac{\vartheta}{2LR^{2}},\qquad\beta_{\mathrm{dir}}:=48\vartheta^{3}+\frac{32\vartheta^{4}}{n},

\gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}:=\frac{96\vartheta^{3}}{L}+\frac{64\vartheta^{4}}{Ln},\qquad\delta_{\mathrm{dir}}:=\left(16\vartheta^{3}+\frac{16\vartheta^{2}}{n}\right)\frac{v^{2}}{LHb}.

In the same regime,

q_{t+1}\leq A_{q}+B_{q}g_{t}+C_{q}q_{t},

with

A_{q}:=6\frac{v^{2}}{Hb},\qquad B_{q}:=144L\vartheta^{2},\qquad C_{q}:=288\vartheta^{2}.

The special structure of the uniform controller is what enables the direct
Bellman closure. In this regime, the aggregate control-variate mismatch cancels
exactly, and the local drift remainder can be analyzed directly at the
averaged-update level.

Assumption B.18

(PL benchmark condition)
.

Assume

\left\lVert\nabla F(x)\right\rVert^{2}\geq 2\mu(F(x)-F_{\star})\qquad\text{for every iterate }x.

Theorem B.19

(Global stochastic PL contraction in the uniform-controller branch)
.

Assume Assumptions
B.15
and
B.18
. Define

a_{\mathrm{PL}}:=\mu\frac{\vartheta}{L}-\beta_{\mathrm{dir}}.

Suppose

a_{\mathrm{PL}}>0,\qquad a_{\mathrm{PL}}<1-C_{q},\qquad a_{\mathrm{PL}}(1-C_{q})>B_{q}\gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}.

Define

\rho_{\mathrm{PL}}:=\frac{a_{\mathrm{PL}}+1-C_{q}-\sqrt{(1-C_{q}-a_{\mathrm{PL}})^{2}+4B_{q}\gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}}}{2},

\lambda_{\mathrm{PL}}:=\frac{2\gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}}{1-C_{q}-a_{\mathrm{PL}}+\sqrt{(1-C_{q}-a_{\mathrm{PL}})^{2}+4B_{q}\gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}}}.

Then

s_{t}:=g_{t}+\lambda_{\mathrm{PL}}q_{t}

satisfies

s_{t+1}\leq(1-\rho_{\mathrm{PL}})s_{t}+\delta_{\mathrm{dir}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{PL}}A_{q},

and therefore

g_{t}\leq(1-\rho_{\mathrm{PL}})^{t}\bigl(g_{0}+\lambda_{\mathrm{PL}}q_{0}\bigr)+\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{dir}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{PL}}A_{q}}{\rho_{\mathrm{PL}}}.

In the readable safe regime

\mu\frac{\vartheta}{L}\leq\frac{1}{2},\qquad\vartheta^{2}\leq\min\!\left\{\frac{\mu}{400L},\frac{1}{576}\right\},

one has

\rho_{\mathrm{PL}}\geq\frac{\mu\vartheta}{8L},\qquad\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{dir}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{PL}}A_{q}}{\rho_{\mathrm{PL}}}=O\!\left(\frac{\vartheta v^{2}}{\mu nHb}+\frac{\vartheta^{2}v^{2}}{\mu Hb}\right).

This is the sharpest PL statement in the paper for the local viewpoint. The comparison with the recent
stochastic SCAFFOLD benchmark line is made only in this uniform-controller
regime and under the same PL-type benchmark condition. In that comparison
class, the theorem gives a geometric contraction to an explicit stochastic
floor of order (
8
), which is the same benchmark-style
communication-efficient scaling emphasized by recent stochastic SCAFFOLD
analyses, including the linear-speedup / higher-order bias perspective of

8
; see also the broader benchmark program of

7
.

Assumption B.20

(Higher-order benchmark regularity)
.

For every node
i\in[n]
, the function
F_{i}
is twice continuously
differentiable on
B(x_{\star},R)
. There exist constants

\mathcal{H}\geq 0
and

Theorem B.21

(Global higher-order convex benchmark bounds in the uniform-controller branch)
.

Under Assumptions
B.15
and
B.20
, define

K_{\mathrm{ho}}:=(\mathcal{H}+MR)^{2},\qquad a_{\mathrm{ho}}:=\frac{\vartheta}{2LR^{2}},\qquad\underline{a}_{\mathrm{ho}}:=\frac{\vartheta}{2LR^{2}(1+\vartheta/4)}.

Then the one-round recursion satisfies

g_{t+1}\leq T_{a_{\mathrm{ho}}}(g_{t})+\frac{2\vartheta}{L}K_{\mathrm{ho}}\Delta_{t}^{2}+\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2LnH}\frac{v^{2}}{b},

where

\Delta_{t}^{2}:=\frac{96\vartheta^{2}}{L}g_{t}+\frac{32\vartheta^{2}v^{2}}{L^{2}Hb}+\frac{192\vartheta^{2}}{L^{2}}q_{t}.

Moreover, if

\bar{q}:=\max\left\{q_{0},\frac{A_{q}+B_{q}\bar{f}}{1-C_{q}}\right\},

then
q_{t}\leq\bar{q}
for all
t
, and therefore

g_{t+1}\leq g_{t}-\underline{a}_{\mathrm{ho}}g_{t}^{2}+\beta_{\mathrm{ho}}g_{t}+\delta_{\mathrm{ho}},

where

\beta_{\mathrm{ho}}:=\frac{2\vartheta}{L}K_{\mathrm{ho}}\frac{96\vartheta^{2}}{L},\qquad\delta_{\mathrm{ho}}:=\frac{2\vartheta}{L}K_{\mathrm{ho}}\left(\frac{32\vartheta^{2}v^{2}}{L^{2}Hb}+\frac{192\vartheta^{2}}{L^{2}}\bar{q}\right)+\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2LnH}\frac{v^{2}}{b}.

Consequently, for every integer
T\geq 1
,

\min_{0\leq t\leq T-1}g_{t}\leq\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{ho}}}{\underline{a}_{\mathrm{ho}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{ho}}}{\underline{a}_{\mathrm{ho}}}}+\sqrt{\frac{g_{0}}{\underline{a}_{\mathrm{ho}}T}}.

In the homogeneous quadratic benchmark subcase

\mathcal{H}=0
,
M=0
,
one has
K_{\mathrm{ho}}=0
and therefore

\min_{0\leq t\leq T-1}g_{t}\leq C_{1}\sqrt{\frac{LR^{2}g_{0}}{\vartheta T}}+C_{2}Rv\sqrt{\frac{\vartheta}{nHb}}

for absolute constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
.

This higher-order branch is where the paper aligns most closely with the
recent higher-order benchmark program for LocalSGD / SCAFFOLD, especially the
Hessian-similarity and Lipschitz-Hessian regimes emphasized by

7
. The comparison is again only for the
uniform-controller specialization together with the higher-order assumptions
stated above. Under those conditions, the theorem yields the same qualitative
benchmark picture: a higher-order correction term plus a best-iterate convex
rate, with the homogeneous quadratic subcase reducing to the clean

T^{-1/2}
-plus-noise-floor form. The difference is that the present route uses
a direct averaged-branch Bellman mechanism tailored to the corrected-local-SGD
specialization.

Arbitrary-active-set identities and certificate proofs.

Lemma B.22

(Server-average identity)
.

Under Assumption
B.5
,

c_{t}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i,t}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction. At
t=0
, it is exactly
(
43
). Assume it holds at time
t
. By the inactive-node convention,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(c_{i,t+1}-c_{i,t})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}(c_{i,t+1}-c_{i,t}).

Hence the server update gives

c_{t+1}=c_{t}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(c_{i,t+1}-c_{i,t})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i,t}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(c_{i,t+1}-c_{i,t})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i,t+1}.

∎

Lemma B.23

(Control-variates average identity)
.

For every active node
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
,

c_{i,t+1}=\frac{1}{H_{i}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}g_{i,t,\ell}.

Proof.

Summing the branch recursion over
\ell=0,\dots,H_{i}-1
gives

x_{t}-y_{i,t}^{(H_{i})}=\eta_{i,t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}(g_{i,t,\ell}-c_{i,t}+c_{t}).

Substitute this identity into the control-variate update:

c_{i,t+1}=c_{i,t}-c_{t}+\frac{1}{H_{i}\eta_{i,t}}(x_{t}-y_{i,t}^{(H_{i})})=\frac{1}{H_{i}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}g_{i,t,\ell}.

∎

Lemma B.24

(Tracking mismatch)
.

Define

\delta_{i,t}:=\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})-\nabla F(x_{t})-(c_{i,t}-c_{t}).

Then

\left\lVert\delta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 4Q_{t}
almost surely.

Proof.

Since

c_{t}-\nabla F(x_{t})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j,t}
,
one has

\delta_{i,t}=-e_{i,t}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j,t}
.
Hence

\left\lVert\delta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\left\lVert e_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j,t}\right\|^{2}\leq 2\left\lVert e_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}+2\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert e_{j,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 4Q_{t}.

∎

Definition B.25

(Conditional branch radius)
.

For every active node
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, define

R_{i,t}^{2}:=\max_{0\leq\ell\leq H_{i}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

Lemma B.26

(Uniform branch radius)
.

For every active node
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
,

R_{i,t}^{2}\leq\frac{24\theta_{i,t}^{2}}{L}U_{t}+\frac{8\theta_{i,t}^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}+\frac{48\theta_{i,t}^{2}}{L^{2}}Q_{t}\qquad\text{almost surely.}

Proof.

Fix
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
and
t
. For every
\ell\in\{0,\dots,H_{i}\}
,

y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}=-\eta_{i,t}\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\nabla F(x_{t})+\delta_{i,t}+\bigl(\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(s)})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})\bigr)+\varepsilon_{i,t,s}\right),

where
\varepsilon_{i,t,s}:=g_{i,t,s}-\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(s)})
.
Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta_{i,t}^{2}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\nabla F(x_{t})+\delta_{i,t}+\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(s)})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})\right)\right\|^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+2\eta_{i,t}^{2}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\varepsilon_{i,t,s}\right\|^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

(50)

For the deterministic part,

\left\|\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}a_{s}\right\|^{2}\leq\ell\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\left\lVert a_{s}\right\rVert^{2}\leq H_{i}^{2}\max_{0\leq s\leq H_{i}-1}\left\lVert a_{s}\right\rVert^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\nabla F(x_{t})+\delta_{i,t}+\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(s)})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})\right)\right\|^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq H_{i}^{2}\left(3\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}+3\left\lVert\delta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}+3L^{2}R_{i,t}^{2}\right).

(51)

For the stochastic part, the sequence
(\varepsilon_{i,t,s})_{s=0}^{H_{i}-1}
is a
martingale-difference sequence with respect to
(\mathcal{G}_{i,t,s})_{s=0}^{H_{i}-1}
,
so the cross terms vanish conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Therefore

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\varepsilon_{i,t,s}\right\|^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert\varepsilon_{i,t,s}\right\rVert^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq H_{i}\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{b_{i}}.

(52)

Substituting (
51
) and (
52
) into
(
50
), and using
\eta_{i,t}=\theta_{i,t}/(LH_{i})
,
produces

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq\frac{6\theta_{i,t}^{2}}{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{6\theta_{i,t}^{2}}{L^{2}}\left\lVert\delta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}+6\theta_{i,t}^{2}R_{i,t}^{2}+\frac{2\theta_{i,t}^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}.

Define

r_{i,t,\ell}:=\Bigl(\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\Bigr)^{1/2}.

By Minkowski’s inequality, the decomposition above, and the conditional orthogonality of the noise terms,

r_{i,t,\ell}\leq\eta_{i,t}\ell\bigl(\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\delta_{i,t}\right\rVert\bigr)+\eta_{i,t}L\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}r_{i,t,s}+\eta_{i,t}\sqrt{\ell}\,\frac{v_{i}}{\sqrt{b_{i}}}.

Since
\ell\leq H_{i}
and
\eta_{i,t}=\theta_{i,t}/(LH_{i})
,

r_{i,t,\ell}\leq\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L}\bigl(\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\delta_{i,t}\right\rVert\bigr)+\frac{\theta_{i,t}v_{i}}{L\sqrt{H_{i}b_{i}}}+\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{H_{i}}\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}r_{i,t,s}.

Apply Lemma
A.9
with
\beta=\theta_{i,t}/H_{i}
to obtain

r_{i,t,\ell}\leq\exp(\theta_{i,t})\left[\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L}\bigl(\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\delta_{i,t}\right\rVert\bigr)+\frac{\theta_{i,t}v_{i}}{L\sqrt{H_{i}b_{i}}}\right].

Squaring, using
(a+b+c)^{2}\leq 4a^{2}+4b^{2}+2c^{2}
, and then Lemmas
A.1
and
B.24
, yields

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq 8e^{2\theta_{i,t}}\frac{\theta_{i,t}^{2}}{L}U_{t}+16e^{2\theta_{i,t}}\frac{\theta_{i,t}^{2}}{L^{2}}Q_{t}+2e^{2\theta_{i,t}}\frac{\theta_{i,t}^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}.

Taking the maximum over
\ell
proves the claim.
∎

Lemma B.27

(Endpoint decomposition)
.

For every active node
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
,

\Delta_{i,t}=-\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L}\nabla F(x_{t})-\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L}\delta_{i,t}+r_{i,t}+\xi_{i,t},

(53)

where

r_{i,t}:=-\eta_{i,t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}\bigl(\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(\ell)})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})\bigr),\quad\xi_{i,t}:=-\eta_{i,t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}\bigl(g_{i,t,\ell}-\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(\ell)})\bigr).

Define

m_{i,t}:=\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{i,t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}],\qquad\zeta_{i,t}:=\Delta_{i,t}-m_{i,t}.

Then

m_{i,t}=-\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L}\nabla F(x_{t})-\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L}\delta_{i,t}+\bar{r}_{i,t},\qquad\bar{r}_{i,t}:=\mathbb{E}[r_{i,t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}],

(54)

\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{i,t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0,

(55)

and for
i\neq j
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\langle\zeta_{i,t},\zeta_{j,t}\right\rangle\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=0.

(56)

Proof.

Summing the branch recursion over
\ell=0,\dots,H_{i}-1
yields

\Delta_{i,t}=-\eta_{i,t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}(g_{i,t,\ell}-c_{i,t}+c_{t}).

Insert and subtract
\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(\ell)})
and
\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})
to obtain
(
53
). Taking conditional expectation gives
(
54
). Equation (
55
) is immediate
from the definition, and (
56
) follows from conditional
independence of the nodewise randomness given
\mathcal{F}_{t}
.
∎

Lemma B.28

(Conditional mean remainder bound)
.

For every active node
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
,

\left\lVert\bar{r}_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert r_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\theta_{i,t}^{2}R_{i,t}^{2}\qquad\text{almost surely.}

(57)

Consequently,

\left\lVert\bar{r}_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 24\frac{\theta_{i,t}^{4}}{L}U_{t}+8\frac{\theta_{i,t}^{4}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}+48\frac{\theta_{i,t}^{4}}{L^{2}}Q_{t}.

Proof.

By Jensen’s inequality for conditional expectation,

\left\lVert\bar{r}_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[r_{i,t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\right\rVert^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert r_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}].

By the definition of
r_{i,t}
,

\left\lVert r_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\eta_{i,t}^{2}H_{i}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}\left\lVert\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(\ell)})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\eta_{i,t}^{2}H_{i}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}L^{2}\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}.

Using
\eta_{i,t}=\theta_{i,t}/(LH_{i})
yields

\left\lVert r_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{\theta_{i,t}^{2}}{H_{i}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\theta_{i,t}^{2}\max_{0\leq\ell\leq H_{i}}\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}.

Taking conditional expectation proves (

); the expanded bound
follows from Lemma
B.26
.

Lemma B.29

(Centered endpoint variance)
.

For every active node
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
,

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\zeta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i,t};U_{t},Q_{t})\qquad\text{almost surely.}

Proof.

Since

\zeta_{i,t}=(r_{i,t}-\mathbb{E}[r_{i,t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}])+\xi_{i,t},

one has

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\zeta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert r_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]+2\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\xi_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}].

The first term is controlled by Lemma
B.28
. For the second term,
conditional orthogonality across local steps gives

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\xi_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\eta_{i,t}^{2}H_{i}\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{b_{i}}=\frac{\theta_{i,t}^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}.

Combining the two estimates and using
\theta_{i,t}\leq 1
gives the stated

Definition B.30

(Aggregated mean and centered residual)
.

For a predictable feasible pair
(w,\theta)
, define

\bar{m}_{t}(w,\theta):=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}w_{i}m_{i,t},\qquad\bar{\zeta}_{t}(w,\theta):=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}w_{i}\zeta_{i,t}.

Theorem B.31

(Exact aggregated server one-step inequality)
.

For every predictable feasible pair
(w,\theta)
with
w\in\Delta_{S_{t}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(x_{t+1})-F_{\star}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t},w,\theta]

\displaystyle\leq f_{t}+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\bar{m}_{t}(w,\theta)\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bar{m}_{t}(w,\theta)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}w_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\zeta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}].

In particular,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(x_{t+1})-F_{\star}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t},w,\theta]

\displaystyle\leq f_{t}+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\bar{m}_{t}(w,\theta)\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bar{m}_{t}(w,\theta)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}w_{i}^{2}\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i,t};U_{t},Q_{t}).

Proof.

Write
x_{t+1}=x_{t}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}w_{i}\Delta_{i,t}=x_{t}+\bar{m}_{t}+\bar{\zeta}_{t}
.
By
L
-smoothness,

F(x_{t}+\bar{m}_{t}+\bar{\zeta}_{t})\leq F(x_{t})+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\bar{m}_{t}+\bar{\zeta}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bar{m}_{t}+\bar{\zeta}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}.

Taking
\mathbb{E}[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{t},w,\theta]
and using

\mathbb{E}[\bar{\zeta}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
gives

\mathbb{E}[F(x_{t+1})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t},w,\theta]\leq F(x_{t})+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\bar{m}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bar{m}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\bar{\zeta}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}].

Now

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\bar{\zeta}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=\sum_{i}w_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\zeta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]+\sum_{i\neq j}w_{i}w_{j}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\langle\zeta_{i,t},\zeta_{j,t}\right\rangle\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

The cross terms vanish by Lemma
B.27
. Applying
Lemma
B.29
yields the second display.
∎

Proposition B.32

(Direct nodewise mean certificate)
.

For every active node
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
,

\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),m_{i,t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq-\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{2LR^{2}}f_{t}^{2}+\rho_{i}(\theta_{i,t};U_{t},Q_{t})\qquad\text{almost surely.}

Proof.

Fix
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
. Write

\theta:=\theta_{i,t},\qquad g:=\nabla F(x_{t}),\qquad\delta:=\delta_{i,t},\qquad\bar{r}:=\bar{r}_{i,t},\qquad m:=m_{i,t}.

By Lemma
B.27
,
m=-\frac{\theta}{L}g-\frac{\theta}{L}\delta+\bar{r}
.
Set
\nu:=-\frac{\theta}{L}\delta+\bar{r}
,
so that
m=-(\theta/L)g+\nu
. Then

\displaystyle\left\langle g,m\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\theta-\theta^{2}/2}{L}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+(1-\theta)\left\langle g,\nu\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}.

By Young’s inequality,

(1-\theta)\left\langle g,\nu\right\rangle\leq\frac{\theta}{4L}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L}{\theta}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}.

Since
0<\theta\leq 1
,

-\frac{\theta-\theta^{2}/2}{L}+\frac{\theta}{4L}\leq-\frac{\theta}{2L},\qquad\frac{L}{\theta}+\frac{L}{2}\leq\frac{2L}{\theta}.

Thus

\left\langle g,m\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m\right\rVert^{2}\leq-\frac{\theta}{2L}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{2L}{\theta}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}.

(58)

Next,

\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\frac{\theta^{2}}{L^{2}}\left\lVert\delta\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\lVert\bar{r}\right\rVert^{2}.

Apply Lemma
B.24
and Lemma
B.28
, then use

\theta\leq 1
to obtain

\frac{2L}{\theta}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}\leq 256\frac{\theta}{L}Q_{t}+96\theta^{2}U_{t}+32\frac{\theta^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{LH_{i}b_{i}}.

Substituting into (
58
) and invoking

\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}\geq f_{t}^{2}/R^{2}
from Lemma
A.1
proves the claim.
∎

Lemma B.33

(Nodewise upper certificate)
.

For every active node
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
,

f_{t}+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),m_{i,t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq T_{A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})}(U_{t}^{\sharp})+\rho_{i}(\theta_{i,t};U_{t},Q_{t})\qquad\text{almost surely.}

Proof.

By Proposition
B.32
,

f_{t}+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),m_{i,t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq f_{t}-A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})f_{t}^{2}+\rho_{i}(\theta_{i,t};U_{t},Q_{t}).

Define
\varphi(z):=z-A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})z^{2}
. Then

\varphi^{\prime}(z)=1-2A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})z.

For
0\leq z\leq\bar{f}
,

2A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})z\leq 2A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})\bar{f}=\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2},

so
\varphi^{\prime}(z)\geq 1/2>0

\varphi
is increasing on
[0,\bar{f}]

f_{t}-A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})f_{t}^{2}=\varphi(f_{t})\leq\varphi(U_{t}^{\sharp})=U_{t}^{\sharp}-A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})(U_{t}^{\sharp})^{2}.

Lemma
A.3
gives

U_{t}^{\sharp}-A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})(U_{t}^{\sharp})^{2}\leq T_{A_{i}(\theta_{i,t})}(U_{t}^{\sharp}),

which proves the claim.
∎

Proposition B.34

(Existence and measurable selection of exact one-step minimizers)
.

For every realization of the round-
t
information, the feasible set

\Delta_{S_{t}}\times[\underline{\theta},\bar{\theta}]^{S_{t}}
is compact and the

\omega

state is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.

Proposition B.36

(KKT law in the weights)
.

Fix
\theta
. If
\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i};U_{t},Q_{t})>0
for all
i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, then
the unique minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(\cdot,\theta)
over

\Delta_{S_{t}}

where
\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
is the unique threshold such that

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}w_{i,t}^{\star}=1
.

Proof.

For fixed
\theta
, the objective is

w\mapsto U_{t}^{\sharp}-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}w_{i}\mu_{i}(\theta_{i};U_{t},Q_{t})+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i};U_{t},Q_{t})w_{i}^{2}

on the simplex. This is strictly convex because every
\kappa_{i}>0
. The KKT
conditions are

L\kappa_{i}w_{i}-\mu_{i}+\lambda-\nu_{i}=0,\qquad\nu_{i}\geq 0,\qquad\nu_{i}w_{i}=0,\qquad\sum_{i}w_{i}=1.

Hence
w_{i}=\frac{(\mu_{i}-\lambda)_{+}}{L\kappa_{i}}
.
The threshold
\lambda_{t}
is uniquely determined by the simplex constraint
because the map
\lambda\mapsto\sum_{i}\frac{(\mu_{i}-\lambda)_{+}}{L\kappa_{i}}

is continuous and strictly decreasing from
+\infty
to
0
.
∎

Lemma C.1

(Expectation-level mismatch bound under a surrogate upper state)

B.9

Definition C.2

(Deterministic branch radius under a surrogate upper state)
.

For every node
i\in[n]

Lemma C.3

(Deterministic branch radius bound under a surrogate upper state)
.

Assume Assumption
B.9
, and let
(u_{t},\chi_{t})
be a surrogate
upper state. Then, for every deterministic feasible amplitude vector

\theta\in[\underline{\theta},\bar{\theta}]^{n}
and every node
i\in[n]

\widetilde{R}_{i,t}^{2}\leq\frac{24\theta_{i}^{2}}{L}u_{t}+\frac{8\theta_{i}^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}+\frac{48\theta_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}}\chi_{t}.

(60)

Proof.

Fix
i\in[n]
and a deterministic feasible amplitude vector
\theta
. For

\ell\in\{0,\dots,H_{i}\}

y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}=-\eta_{i}\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\nabla F(x_{t})+\delta_{i,t}+\bigl(\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(s)})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})\bigr)+\varepsilon_{i,t,s}\right),

\eta_{i}:=\frac{\theta_{i}}{LH_{i}}

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert y_{i,t}^{(\ell)}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\nabla F(x_{t})+\delta_{i,t}+\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(s)})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})\right)\right\|^{2}

\left\|\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}a_{s}\right\|^{2}\leq\ell\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\left\lVert a_{s}\right\rVert^{2}\leq H_{i}^{2}\max_{0\leq s\leq H_{i}-1}\left\lVert a_{s}\right\rVert^{2}.

\widetilde{R}_{i,t}^{2}

61

\ell
. By Lemma
A.1

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2Lg_{t}\leq 2Lu_{t}

C.1

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\delta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 4\chi_{t}

A.9

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert r_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 24\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}}{L}u_{t}+8\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}+48\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}}{L^{2}}\chi_{t}.

For the stochastic term,

\xi_{i,t}=-\eta_{i}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}\bigl(g_{i,t,\ell}-\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(\ell)})\bigr),\qquad\eta_{i}=\frac{\theta_{i}}{LH_{i}}.

The summands are conditionally centered and conditionally orthogonal across the
local steps, so

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\xi_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\eta_{i}^{2}H_{i}\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{b_{i}}=\frac{\theta_{i}^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}.

Combining the last two bounds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\zeta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 48\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}}{L}u_{t}+16\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}+96\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}}{L^{2}}\chi_{t}+2\frac{\theta_{i}^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq 64\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}}{L}u_{t}+32\frac{\theta_{i}^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}+128\frac{\theta_{i}^{4}}{L^{2}}\chi_{t},

because
\theta_{i}\leq 1
. This is exactly (
62
).
∎

Proposition C.6

(Deterministic nodewise mean certificate under a surrogate upper state)
.

Assume Assumption
B.9
, and let
(u_{t},\chi_{t})
be a surrogate
upper state. Then, for every deterministic feasible amplitude vector

\theta\in[\underline{\theta},\bar{\theta}]^{n}
and every node
i\in[n]
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),m_{i,t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq-\frac{\theta_{i}}{2LR^{2}}g_{t}^{2}+\rho_{i}(\theta_{i};u_{t},\chi_{t}).

(63)

Proof.

Fix
i\in[n]
and write

\theta:=\theta_{i},\qquad g:=\nabla F(x_{t}),\qquad\delta:=\delta_{i,t},\qquad\bar{r}:=\bar{r}_{i,t},\qquad m:=m_{i,t}.

By Lemma
B.27
,
m=-\frac{\theta}{L}g-\frac{\theta}{L}\delta+\bar{r}
.
Set
\nu:=-\frac{\theta}{L}\delta+\bar{r}
,
so that
m=-(\theta/L)g+\nu
. Then

\displaystyle\left\langle g,m\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\theta}{L}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+\left\langle g,\nu\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left(\frac{\theta^{2}}{L^{2}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}-2\frac{\theta}{L}\left\langle g,\nu\right\rangle+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\theta-\theta^{2}/2}{L}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+(1-\theta)\left\langle g,\nu\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}.

(64)

By Young’s inequality,

(1-\theta)\left\langle g,\nu\right\rangle\leq\frac{\theta}{4L}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L}{\theta}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}.

Since
0<\theta\leq 1
,

-\frac{\theta-\theta^{2}/2}{L}+\frac{\theta}{4L}\leq-\frac{\theta}{2L},\qquad\frac{L}{\theta}+\frac{L}{2}\leq\frac{2L}{\theta}.

Hence (
64
) gives

\left\langle g,m\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m\right\rVert^{2}\leq-\frac{\theta}{2L}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{2L}{\theta}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}.

(65)

Taking expectations,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\langle g,m\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq-\frac{\theta}{2L}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{2L}{\theta}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}.

(66)

Next,
\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\frac{\theta^{2}}{L^{2}}\left\lVert\delta\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\lVert\bar{r}\right\rVert^{2}
.
Taking expectations and using Lemma
C.1
and
Lemma
C.4
,

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\frac{\theta^{2}}{L^{2}}\cdot 4\chi_{t}+2\left(24\frac{\theta^{4}}{L}u_{t}+8\frac{\theta^{4}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}+48\frac{\theta^{4}}{L^{2}}\chi_{t}\right).

Therefore

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}\leq 8\frac{\theta^{2}}{L^{2}}\chi_{t}+48\frac{\theta^{4}}{L}u_{t}+16\frac{\theta^{4}v_{i}^{2}}{L^{2}H_{i}b_{i}}+96\frac{\theta^{4}}{L^{2}}\chi_{t}.

Multiplying by
2L/\theta
, and using
\theta\leq 1
, yields

\frac{2L}{\theta}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}\leq 256\frac{\theta}{L}\chi_{t}+96\theta^{2}u_{t}+32\frac{\theta^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{LH_{i}b_{i}}.

Substituting into (
66
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\langle g,m\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq-\frac{\theta}{2L}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{2}+256\frac{\theta}{L}\chi_{t}+96\theta^{2}u_{t}+32\frac{\theta^{2}v_{i}^{2}}{LH_{i}b_{i}}.

Finally, Lemma
A.1
and Jensen imply
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}\geq\frac{1}{R^{2}}\mathbb{E}[f_{t}^{2}]\geq\frac{g_{t}^{2}}{R^{2}}
.
Substituting this lower bound proves (
63
).
∎

Proposition C.8

(Deterministic surrogate executable domination)
.

For every deterministic feasible pair
(w,\theta)
,

\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(w,\theta;u,\chi)\leq\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{ex}}(w,\theta;u,\chi).

(68)

Proof.

Since
\widehat{v}_{i,t}^{2}\geq v_{i}^{2}
, one has

\widehat{\rho}_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi,\widehat{v}_{i,t})\geq\rho_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi),\qquad\widehat{\kappa}_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi,\widehat{v}_{i,t})\geq\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi).

Because the score
s_{i}(u;\theta_{i})
is unchanged,

\widehat{\mu}_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi,\widehat{v}_{i,t})=s_{i}(u;\theta_{i})-\widehat{\rho}_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi,\widehat{v}_{i,t})\leq s_{i}(u;\theta_{i})-\rho_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi)=\mu_{i}(\theta_{i};u,\chi).

Substituting these inequalities into the definitions of

\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}
and
\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{ex}}
proves
(
68
).

Theorem C.9

(Deterministic one-step surrogate majorant)
.

Assume Assumption
B.9
, and let
(u_{t},\chi_{t})
be a surrogate
upper state. Fix any deterministic feasible pair

w\in\Delta_{n}
,
\theta\in[\underline{\theta},\bar{\theta}]^{n}
. Then

g_{t+1}\leq\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{id}}(w,\theta;u_{t},\chi_{t}).

Consequently,
g_{t+1}\leq\mathfrak{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{ex}}(w,\theta;u_{t},\chi_{t})
.

Proof.

Under full participation,

x_{t+1}=x_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\Delta_{i,t}=x_{t}+\bar{m}_{t}+\bar{\zeta}_{t}.

Applying
L
-smoothness and taking total expectation yields

g_{t+1}\leq g_{t}+\mathbb{E}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\bar{m}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\bar{m}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\bar{\zeta}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}.

(69)

By convexity of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{2}
,
\left\lVert\bar{m}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left\lVert m_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}
,
so

g_{t}+\mathbb{E}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\bar{m}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\bar{m}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\mathbb{E}\!\left[f_{t}+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),m_{i,t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\right].

Apply Corollary
C.7
to each node:

\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\mathbb{E}\!\left[f_{t}+\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),m_{i,t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert m_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left(T_{A_{i}(\theta_{i})}(u_{t})+\rho_{i}(\theta_{i};u_{t},\chi_{t})\right).

Since
T_{A_{i}(\theta_{i})}(u_{t})=u_{t}-s_{i}(u_{t};\theta_{i})
, the right-hand side is

u_{t}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\mu_{i}(\theta_{i};u_{t},\chi_{t}),

For the centered term,

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\bar{\zeta}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\zeta_{i,t}\right\rVert^{2},

because the cross terms vanish by Lemma
B.27
. Apply
Lemma
C.5
to get

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\bar{\zeta}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}^{2}\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i};u_{t},\chi_{t}).

Substituting into (
69
) proves the idealized bound,
and executable domination proves the second statement.
∎

Proposition C.10

(Tracking recursion under a surrogate upper state)
.

Assume Assumption
B.9
, and let
(u_{t},\chi_{t})
be a surrogate
upper state. Let
w\in\Delta_{n}
and
\theta\in[\underline{\theta},\bar{\theta}]^{n}

be any deterministic feasible control pair at round
t
. Then

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{i,t+1}\right\rVert^{2}\leq A_{\chi}+B_{\chi}u_{t}+C_{\chi}\chi_{t}.

Proof.

For each
i\in[n]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{i,t+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left\|\frac{1}{H_{i}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}\bigl(g_{i,t,\ell}-\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(\ell)})\bigr)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+3\mathbb{E}\left\|\frac{1}{H_{i}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{H_{i}-1}\bigl(\nabla F_{i}(y_{i,t}^{(\ell)})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})\bigr)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+3\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\nabla F_{i}(x_{t})-\nabla F_{i}(x_{t+1})\right\rVert^{2}.

(70)

The first term is bounded by
\max_{j}v_{j}^{2}/(H_{j}b_{j})
by conditional
orthogonality across local steps. The second term is bounded by

L^{2}\widetilde{R}_{i,t}^{2}
. The third term is bounded by

L^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}
, and

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Delta_{j,t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\widetilde{R}_{j,t}^{2}.

Applying Lemma
C.3
, then using
\theta_{i}\leq\bar{\theta}
and

\sum_{j}w_{j}=1
, yields the common upper bound

24L\bar{\theta}^{2}u_{t}+8\bar{\theta}^{2}\max_{j}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{H_{j}b_{j}}+48\bar{\theta}^{2}\chi_{t}

for both
L^{2}\widetilde{R}_{i,t}^{2}
and
L^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}
. Plugging
this into (
70
) gives

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{i,t+1}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 3\max_{j}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{H_{j}b_{j}}+48\bar{\theta}^{2}\max_{j}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{H_{j}b_{j}}+144L\bar{\theta}^{2}u_{t}+288\bar{\theta}^{2}\chi_{t}.

Since
48\bar{\theta}^{2}\leq 1
, the first two terms are bounded by

6\max_{j}v_{j}^{2}/(H_{j}b_{j})
, which proves the claim.
∎

Theorem C.27

\displaystyle=R_{a}^{\theta}(R_{b}^{\theta}(s)),\qquad a,b,s\geq 0,

\displaystyle\leq s,\qquad a,s\geq 0,

(115)

\displaystyle s_{1}\leq s_{2}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow R_{a}^{\theta}(s_{1})\leq R_{a}^{\theta}(s_{2}),\qquad a\geq 0,

(116)

and, for each

, the map

is concave on

Proof.

Fix

For

and

, one has

by (

Let

Then, for

|R_{t}^{\theta}(s)-R_{r}^{\theta}(s)|=\left|\int_{r}^{t}\varphi_{\theta}(R_{\tau}^{\theta}(s))\,d\tau\right|\leq M_{T,S}|t-r|.

For

, let

u(\tau):=R_{\tau}^{\theta}(s_{1}),\qquad v(\tau):=R_{\tau}^{\theta}(s_{2}).

Then

\frac{d}{d\tau}(u(\tau)-v(\tau))=-\varphi_{\theta}(u(\tau))+\varphi_{\theta}(v(\tau)).

Hence

\frac{d}{d\tau}|u(\tau)-v(\tau)|\leq L_{T,S}|u(\tau)-v(\tau)|.

Gronwall yields

|R_{t}^{\theta}(s_{1})-R_{t}^{\theta}(s_{2})|\leq e^{L_{T,S}t}|s_{1}-s_{2}|.

Thus
(a,s)\mapsto R_{a}^{\theta}(s)
is jointly continuous.

Global existence follows from
0\leq v(\tau;s)\leq s
.
Equations (
112
), (
113
), and (
114
) follow from the definition, uniqueness, and the constant zero solution.
Equation (
115
) follows from
\dot{v}(\tau)\leq 0
.
For (

), let
u(\tau):=R_{\tau}^{\theta}(s_{1})
and
v(\tau):=R_{\tau}^{\theta}(s_{2})
with
s_{1}\leq s_{2}
.
If

for some
\tau_{0}>0
, let

Then

and

Uniqueness implies

for

, contradiction.

For concavity, define

for
\tau>0
,

\partial_{\tau}p(\tau,s)=-\varphi_{\theta}^{\prime}(u(\tau,s))\,p(\tau,s),\qquad p(0,s)=1,

Differentiating again,

\partial_{\tau}q(\tau,s)=-\varphi_{\theta}^{\prime\prime}(u(\tau,s))\,p(\tau,s)^{2}-\varphi_{\theta}^{\prime}(u(\tau,s))\,q(\tau,s),\qquad q(0,s)=0.

Since

, one gets

.
Thus
R_{a}^{\theta}
is concave on
(0,\infty)
, hence on
[0,\infty)
by continuity.

\vartheta_{t}^{\mathrm{eff}}
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
t
that

c_{i,t}=c_{t}\qquad\text{for all }i\in[n].

For
t=0
, this is exactly Assumption
G.1
(iv).

Assume now that, for some fixed round
t\geq 0
,

c_{i,t}=c_{t}\qquad\text{for all }i\in[n].

Since
H=1
, Algorithm 1 uses

\eta_{i,t}=\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L},\qquad y_{i,t}^{(0)}=x_{t},\qquad y_{i,t}^{(1)}=x_{t}-\eta_{i,t}(g_{i,t,0}-c_{i,t}+c_{t}).

By Assumption
G.1
(v),

g_{i,t,0}=\nabla F(x_{t}).

Using the induction hypothesis
c_{i,t}=c_{t}
, we obtain

y_{i,t}^{(1)}=x_{t}-\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L}\nabla F(x_{t}),\qquad\Delta_{i,t}=-\frac{\theta_{i,t}}{L}\nabla F(x_{t}).

Next, Lemma B.23 gives, since
H=1
,

c_{i,t+1}=g_{i,t,0}=\nabla F(x_{t}),\qquad i\in[n].

Thus all vectors
c_{i,t+1}
are equal. Lemma B.22 implies

c_{t+1}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i,t+1},

and hence

c_{i,t+1}=c_{t+1}\qquad\text{for all }i\in[n].

This closes the induction on
t
.

Finally,

x_{t+1}=x_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i,t}\Delta_{i,t}=x_{t}-\frac{1}{L}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i,t}\theta_{i,t}\right)\nabla F(x_{t})=x_{t}-\frac{\vartheta_{t}^{\mathrm{eff}}}{L}\nabla F(x_{t}).

This proves the claim.
∎

Proposition H.1

(Solver properties)
.

Consider any local objective of the quadratic-risk / linear-gain form

\mathcal{O}(w,\theta)=\text{constant}-\sum_{i}w_{i}\mu_{i}(\theta_{i})+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i}w_{i}^{2}\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i})

over a simplex in
w
and a box in
\theta
, where each
\kappa_{i}>0
and each

\mu_{i},\kappa_{i}
depends only on
\theta_{i}
. Then:

1.

every weight subproblem is a strictly convex quadratic program with the closed-form KKT threshold law of Proposition
B.36
;

2.

for fixed weights
w
, the amplitude subproblem is separable across the nodes and reduces to independent one-dimensional convex minimizations;

3.

exact block-coordinate descent produces a monotonically nonincreasing sequence of objective values;

4.

every limit point is a coordinatewise minimum.

Proof.

Item (1) is Proposition
B.36
. For item (2), the objective decomposes
as a sum of scalar coordinate functions because each
\mu_{i}
and
\kappa_{i}

depends only on
\theta_{i}
. It remains to prove convexity of the scalar block.
In all local objectives used in the paper, the only non-polynomial part is

-s_{i}(u;\theta_{i})
, where

s_{i}(u;\theta_{i})=u-T_{A_{i}(\theta_{i})}(u)=\frac{A_{i}(\theta_{i})u^{2}}{1+A_{i}(\theta_{i})u}=\frac{c_{i}\theta_{i}}{1+d_{i}\theta_{i}}

for nonnegative constants
c_{i},d_{i}
depending on
u,L,R
. Differentiating
shows that
s_{i}
is concave in
\theta_{i}
, hence
-s_{i}
is convex. The
remaining terms are nonnegative linear, quadratic, or quartic monomials in

\theta_{i}
, hence convex. Item (3) holds because each block is minimized
exactly. Item (4) follows from compactness of the feasible set and continuity
of the objective.
∎
