Increased point shear wave elastography values of the placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver in patients with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy
Authors: Mehmet Özer, Pınar Tuğçe Özer, Süleyman Cemil Oğlak, Hakan Gölbaşı, Alper İleri, Zübeyde Emiralioğlu Çakır, Mesut Ali Halisçelik, Gökhan Bolluk, Emine Zeynep Yılmaz, Atalay Ekin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe application of point shear wave elastography (pSWE) in obstetrics gained popularity in examining the elasticity of the placenta and maternal tissues in high-risk pregnancies This study aimed to utilize pSWE to determine the elasticity values of these tissues in pregnant women with or without intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP), and to investigate its potential as a novel, non-invasive diagnostic approach beyond conventional methods.Materials and methodsThis prospective case-control study, conducted between February 2022 and June 2023, included 120 singleton pregnant women (60 with ICP and 60 body mass index [BMI]- and gestational age-matched healthy controls). All participants underwent B-mode ultrasonography, Doppler examination, and pSWE at 36 weeks of gestation in a single session. Placental (central and peripheral), fetal lung, and fetal liver stiffness values were measured, and the mean of ten valid measurements was used for analysis. Correlations between stiffness values and biochemical parameters were evaluated. Logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of ICP.ResultsCases with ICP had a significantly higher mean stiffness value in both the peripheral (9.29 ± 2.45 kPa) and the central (9.03 ± 2.79 kPa) part of the placentas, fetal lung (10.86 ± 3.33 kPa), and fetal liver (4.85 ± 1.17 kPa) than those of the control cases (4.92 ± 2.26 kPa, 4.61 ± 2.11 kPa, 5.76 ± 2.62 kPa, and 3.81 ± 0.99 kPa, respectively, p < 0.001). There was a significant positive correlation between the mean elasticity values of the placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver and AST, ALT, and TBA values at diagnosis, and a significant negative correlation between the mean elasticity values of the placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver and the gestational week at diagnosis.ConclusionWe showed increased stiffness values on pSWE at the peripheral and central parts of the placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver in ICP cases compared to healthy pregnancies. Also, higher stiffness values of the placenta and fetal organs were significantly and positively associated with AST, ALT, and TBA values at diagnosis, and significantly and negatively associated with the gestational week at diagnosis. The pSWE might provide evidence of histopathological findings in ICP.

Introduction

Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) is the most common pregnancy-specific liver disease, with a reported incidence between 0.2% and 2%. This pregnancy complication is characterized by generalized skin pruritus that is frequently localized at the palms and soles which cannot be explained by any other reason, and biochemical evidence of elevated bile acids (BAs), with or without elevated liver function tests (LFTs) [1, 2]. The maternal symptoms commonly manifest in the second or third trimester of pregnancy and rapidly resolve after delivery. However, ICP might also present as early as the first trimester under extraordinary situations [3]. Although the etiology of cholestasis is not definitively established, genetic, hormonal, immunological, and environmental factors are thought to contribute to the development of ICP [4, 5]. ICP has been related to severe adverse pregnancy outcomes, including an increased risk of both spontaneous and iatrogenic preterm birth and related complications, meconium-stained amniotic fluid, fetal distress, and stillbirth, for which no useful treatment is yet acknowledged [6]. Commonly, earlier development of ICP is correlated with more severe adverse prenatal outcomes. Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA), which can decrease BA levels and relieve pruritus, is currently the front-line medication for the ICP treatment, although its positive impact on fetal outcomes is controversial [3]. Signs and symptoms of ICP typically resolve following delivery. However, cases who have experienced ICP have an increased risk of developing hepatobiliary, immune-mediated, and cardiovascular diseases later in life [7]. Despite the increased risk of adverse outcomes, strategies differ concerning proper diagnostic criteria, maternal and fetal surveillance, treatment, and delivery timing. The comparative rarity of this disorder, along with dissimilar guidelines, makes management decisions challenging for healthcare providers [8].

The placenta is the crucial organ that serves as the primary interface between maternal and fetal circulations. Placental dysfunction in complicated pregnancies has been related to adverse fetal outcomes. Experimental studies demonstrated different levels of substantial histopathological changes in the placentas, fetal liver, and fetal lung of mothers with ICP, including increased terminal villous and capillary surface area, and number of syncytial knots [9, 10]. In a daily routine medical setting, it is hard to define the morphological changes in organs on an ultrasonographic (US) exam because of the insidious nature of these changes. Also, placental histological changes are generally manifested only after delivery, and it is impossible to assess these changes during delivery. These microstructural changes in the placenta and fetal organs might change the elasticity and stiffness of tissue parenchyma during ICP. Thus, improved evaluation of the placenta and fetal organs enables earlier recognition and improved management alternatives.

Elasticity is the soft tissue stiffness that associates with the changes due to the physiopathologic processes of the organs [11]. The elasticity of superficial and deep tissues, which is the tissue’s tendency to resist deformation following an applied force, has been measured to investigate the inflammation, fibrosis, and benign and malignant masses in various parenchymal organs [12]. Point shear-wave elastography (pSWE) is a novel, noninvasive, convenient-to-use, reproducible, and rapid US method that measures the elastic properties of tissue in real-time through an elastogram, where a high shear wave speed indicates an increased tissue stiffness. Using SWE, shear waves that are induced by mechanical vibrations propagate perpendicular to compressive waves; tissue motion provided by shear waves is monitored at multiple sites along the US probe, allowing shear wave velocity (SWV) calculation. Young’s modulus can be algebraically obtained from the SWV [13]. SWE effectively investigates deep organs because this procedure does not need any compression-relaxation sequence on the studied organ. The generation of high-frequency US impulses in the SWE procedure is operator-independent, which provides quantitative data with high reproducibility [14]. Also, previous studies reported that SWE could be safely used on human fetuses without obvious histopathological changes and immediate adverse outcomes [15, 16]. Thus, the application of this technique in obstetrics gained popularity in examining the elasticity of the placenta and maternal liver in high-risk pregnancies [17, 18]. However, to the best of our knowledge, the application of pSWE for the assessment of the placenta, fetal liver, and fetal lung in ICP has not been investigated previously.

This study aimed to utilize pSWE to determine the elasticity values of these tissues in body mass index (BMI)- and gestational age-matched pregnant women with or without ICP, and to investigate its potential as a novel, non-invasive diagnostic approach beyond conventional methods.

Materials and methods

This prospective case-control study was performed with 120 singleton pregnant women at Tepecik Training and Research Hospital (Izmir, Turkey) between February 2022 and June 2023. The study group included 60 pregnant women presenting with a singleton pregnancy diagnosed with ICP in their second or third trimester of pregnancy. Also, 60 healthy BMI- and gestational age-matched singleton pregnant women with no history of liver dysfunction or any complication in their current or previous pregnancies were randomly selected and constituted the control group. All ICP cases and healthy individuals signed informed consent. Izmir Tepecik Training and Research Hospital Ethics Committee’s approval was obtained before the study was initiated (2022/02–46). Maternal demographic and clinical characteristics, laboratory parameters, Doppler US and pSWE findings, and delivery outcomes of the participants were recorded.

ICP was diagnosed according to the diagnostic criteria of the Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, based on the presence of otherwise unexplained maternal pruritus associated with elevated serum total BA (TBA) levels above the upper limit of normal (> 10 µmol/L), after exclusion of other causes of liver dysfunction [19]. If we suspected the ICP diagnosis but serum TBA levels and serum transaminases were in the normal range at first admission, laboratory parameters were analyzed again one week after, and the patient was diagnosed as having ICP if the following levels of these variables reached abnormal values. The medical histories, previous pregnancies, and current pregnancies of all participants were questioned comprehensively. Upper abdominal US imaging was performed for the differential diagnosis of gallbladder diseases and other liver disorders.

Exclusion criteria for all participants were as follows: multiple pregnancies, major fetal abnormalities, pruritic dermatoses of pregnancy with normal TBA levels, acute or chronic viral or non-viral hepatitis and other hepatic diseases, hepatotoxic drug consumption, extrahepatic biliary obstruction, gallstones or gallbladder diseases, chronic inflammatory diseases (vasculitis, rheumatologic diseases), and pregnancy complications that affect liver tests, including gestational hypertensive disorders and gestational diabetes. Cases with any history of hepatotoxic drug consumption, hepatitis, gallbladder diseases, or any other diseases associated with abnormal serum transaminases were also excluded.

Following the diagnosis of ICP, gestational week, serum transaminase levels, and serum TBA levels of the cases at the diagnosis time were recorded. The gestational age was determined based on the first day of the last menstrual period and crown-rump length measurement during the first trimester. The ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) with a dose of 300 mg twice daily was initiated and further increased to a maximum dose of 1,500 mg/day gradually for those whose laboratory tests worsened or who were not relieved clinically. All participants in both the study and control groups underwent B-mode sonography, Doppler US, and pSWE during the same session at 36 weeks of pregnancy, performed by a single maternal-fetal medicine specialist (M.Ö.) with 10 years of experience in sonography. This gestational age was selected to ensure uniformity of measurements and to minimize the potential influence of gestational age–related variability in tissue elasticity.

A Samsung Ultrasound System HS70A (Samsung Medison Company, Republic of Korea) equipped with an abdominal convex probe of 1–7 MHz (CA1-7 A) was used for all US measurements in this study. This system provides a real-time visual image of an elastogram coupled with a B-mode visualization. Prior to pSWE, conventional grayscale 2-D imaging was performed to visualize the fetus and placenta, and Doppler US was performed to measure the pulsatility index (PI) and peak systolic/end-diastolic velocity ratios (S/D). Estimated fetal weight (EFW), placental thickness, and maternal abdominal subcutaneous fat thickness (ASFT) were recorded. The ASFT was measured in millimeters from the external face of the rectus abdominis muscle to the skin surface [20]. During the US examination, the targeted organs (placenta, fetal liver, and fetal lung) were centered in the line of vision. The confirmed-sized region of interest (ROI) of 5 × 10 mm was located at the targeted organs, and the quantitative stiffness value of these organs was displayed over a B-mode sonogram in real-time.

The pSWE was performed following the manufacturer’s recommended protocol (S-Shear wave Imaging™ Non-invasive quantification of tissue stiffness. Samsung Medison CO., LTD 2018. Available at https://samsunghealthcare.com.) in the supine position. The patient was asked to temporary apnea for 5 s in the natural end-inspiratory phase, and stop coughing or any movement during the measurements. The operator confirmed that the placental tissue was as horizontal as possible, and fetal extremities did not push against the placental tissue during the procedure. The lowest possible pressure was applied to the transducer to standardize the findings and avoid elasticity variabilities because of the compression. During the pSWE, tracking pulses were utilized to quantify shear wave displacements between two points inside an ROI that is positioned perpendicular to the probe and gives an SWV at a definite moment. Placental elastograms were captured from the two sampling sites as central (at least 2 cm away from the umbilical cord insertion) and peripheral (close to the lateral border of the placenta) areas of the placenta, where fetal movements minimally impact the placenta (Figs. 1 and 2). The sites of the placental margin and cord insertion were not included in the measurement site. Vascular structures, lacunae, and heterogeneous parts of the placenta were avoided during the examination. Fetal lung elasticity was measured on the most proximal lung (located closest to the US probe) in the region behind the plane passing through the atria after obtaining a B-mode image of a four-chamber fetal heart view (Fig. 3). Fetal liver elasticity (Fig. 4) was measured in the region inside the hepatic portal sinus after visualizing a B-mode abdominal circumference image [21]. In real-time, the US software displays elastograms superimposed on grayscale images simultaneously. The operator performed a minimum of ten measurements at the central placenta, peripheral placenta, right or left lung, where appropriate, and liver. The values of elasticity were quantified from ten rectangular ROI of 5 × 10 mm and obtained the elasticity values in kilopascals (kPa) [22]. The mean of ten measurements of all organs was used for statistical analysis. The distance between the probe and the target area was measured automatically by the application and recorded as placental depth, lung depth, and liver depth. The duration of the pSWE examination did not exceed 7 min in any case. The thermal indices did not exceed 1.7, and the mechanical indices did not exceed 1.5 in any case.

Samsung’s S-Shearwave Imaging™ provides a reliable measurement index (RMI) determining whether the elasticity value has been correctly quantified. This index is calculated by analyzing how much the measured shear wave deviates from the theoretical behavior. RMI is a quality control parameter calculated by the weighted sum of two variables: the size of the shear wave and the residual of the wave equation. An RMI of 1.0 indicates no error, whereas an RMI of 0.0 indicates a significant error. Based on the manufacturer’s recommendations, measurements with RMI < 0.4 were removed to improve reliability. The median value and interquartile range (IQR) of valid measurements are calculated automatically with the application. Reliable pSWE measurements were described as the median value of at least 10 measurements with RMI ≥ 0.4 and IQR/M ≤ 30%.

Statistical analysis

The IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0 (Armonk, NY) program was used for the statistical analysis. Descriptive statistical values (mean, standard deviation, median, frequency, percentage, 25%, and 75% percentiles) were used for the evaluation of the study data. The normality of the distribution of the numerical values was tested with a Shapiro-Wilk test and graphic evaluations. The Student’s t-test and Mann-Whitney U test were used for the comparison of quantitative variables, respectively with and without normal distribution, between the two groups. Pearson’s Chi-square test and Fisher’s Exact test were used for the comparison of qualitative variables. Spearman’s correlation coefficient and the related p-values were used to examine relationships. In addition to statistical significance, the magnitude and direction of the correlation coefficients (r) were carefully interpreted to assess their clinical relevance. Correlation analyses were performed to explore the biological gradient rather than merely testing statistical association. The results were also summarized with scatter plots. The level of statistical significance was accepted as p < 0.05.

Univariate and multiple logistic regression were applied for the primary outcome variables of the study. Univariate logistic regression analysis and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed by using the Enter and Backward LR approaches. Variables that remained significant (p < 0.05) in the multivariate model were considered independent predictors of Cholestasis. Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit statistics were used to assess the model fit. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for each predictor.

The comparison of pSWE findings of different tissues between the groups was summarized in Table 2. Cases with ICP had a significantly higher mean stiffness value in both the peripheral (9.29 ± 2.45 kPa) and the central (9.03 ± 2.79 kPa) part of the placentas than the control cases (4.92 ± 2.26 kPa and 4.61 ± 2.11 kPa, respectively, p < 0.001). The mean fetal lung stiffness and fetal liver stiffness values were significantly higher in the ICP cases (10.86 ± 3.33 kPa and 4.85 ± 1.17 kPa, respectively) than those of the control group (5.76 ± 2.62 kPa and 3.81 ± 0.99 kPa, respectively). These findings suggest that ICP is associated with increased tissue stiffness, which may reflect underlying pathological processes and may have potential clinical utility in the non-invasive assessment of disease involvement.

We demonstrated the correlational relationships between pSWE findings of different tissues and serum AST, ALT, TBA values, and the gestational week at diagnosis in Table 3; Fig. 5. The Spearman’s correlation coefficient test indicated that peripheral and central placental elasticity values demonstrated a moderately positive correlation with serum AST (r = 0.405 and p = 0.001, r = 0.392 and p = 0.002, respectively) and ALT values at diagnosis (r = 0.329 and p = 0.010 and p = 0.012, respectively), a highly positive correlation with serum TBA value (r = 0.714 and p < 0.001, r = 0.717 and p < 0.001, respectively), and a highly negative correlation with the gestational week at ICP diagnosis (r= -0.744 and p < 0.001, r= -0.608 and p < 0.001, respectively). Lung stiffness on pSWE showed a highly positive correlation with serum AST (r = 0.527 and p < 0.001) and TBA levels at diagnosis (r = 0.817 and p < 0.001), a moderately positive correlation with serum ALT level (r = 0.383 and p = 0.003), and a highly negative correlation with the gestational week at diagnosis (r= -0.709 and p < 0.001). Liver elasticity values had a moderately positive correlation with serum AST (r = 0.399 and p = 0.002), ALT (r = 0.369 and p = 0.004), and TBA levels at diagnosis (r = 0.471 and p < 0.001), and a moderately negative correlation with the gestational week at diagnosis (r= -301 and p = 0.019). The strength and direction of these correlations suggest that increasing tissue stiffness is aligned with worsening biochemical markers of ICP, supporting a clinically meaningful relationship beyond statistical significance alone.

We performed univariate and multivariate logistic regression models to clarify whether the stiffness values of different tissues on pSWE were independently associated with the diagnosis of ICP (Table 4). The stiffness values on pSWE of the peripheral and central parts of the placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver were found to be significant in the diagnosis of ICP as a result of univariate logistic regression analysis. However, as a result of the multiple logistic regression model, only central placental stiffness and fetal lung stiffness values on pSWE were detected to be effective in the diagnosis of ICP. The exclusion of some variables from the final multivariate model likely reflects shared variance among correlated pSWE measurements rather than lack of association, indicating that retained variables represent the strongest independent predictors. We observed that a 1 unit increase in central placental stiffness value increases the risk of ICP 1.66 times (p = 0.003), and a 1 unit increase in fetal lung stiffness value increases the risk of ICP 1.45 times (p = 0.009), which were statistically significant.

Discussion

In the current study, we assessed the pSWE findings on the peripheral and central placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver in connection with the presence or absence of ICP. Our findings indicate that the peripheral and central parts of the placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver of cases with ICP were stiffer than those of healthy pregnancies. The stiffness values on pSWE at the peripheral and central parts of the placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver were significantly and positively correlated with AST, ALT, and TBA values at diagnosis. Also, a negative and statistically significant correlation was detected between the stiffness value on pSWE at the peripheral and central parts of the placenta, fetal lung, and fetal liver and gestational week at diagnosis.

SWE has been utilized in medical practice to assess the elastic properties of parenchymal organs for more than a decade. The elastographic evaluation of the liver, thyroid, breast, and other organs provides a broad idea regarding the histological changes in tissues to identify and diagnose the diseases [12, 13, 23]. This non-invasive tool is a new complementary procedure in obstetrics for assessing soft tissue elasticity during pregnancy or immediately after delivery. Investigations studying the mechanical properties of pathological and healthy tissues are continuing to identify the diagnostic function of this method. Numerous studies regarding elastography data on the placenta and fetal organs demonstrated increased tissue stiffness measured via SWE in cases with complicated pregnancies [17, 18, 21, 23]. To the best of our knowledge, a study quantifying the elasticity of the placenta and fetal organs on pSWE in ICP has not been previously published. According to our findings, elastographic outcomes at the 36th week of pregnancy were significantly different between pregnancies complicated with ICP and healthy pregnancies.

The BA excretion is carried out by the maternal liver and the placenta. Thus, biliary excretion impairment might induce BA accumulation, especially in the maternal liver-placenta-fetal liver trio [9]. In pregnant rats with cholestasis, BA accumulation leads to placental antioxidant system impairment and oxidative damage. Also, BA accumulation in the fetal compartment induces significant oxidative damage and apoptosis in the fetal liver [24]. The BA accumulation in the systemic circulation might also have harmful consequences on placental cells and fetal extrahepatic tissues, including the lung and kidney. Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome is related to ICP, and BAs might play a crucial role in neonatal BA pneumonia. Zecca et al. demonstrated that the BAs were present in the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid of infants affected by respiratory distress syndrome who were born to ICP mothers, which confirms the idea of damaging impacts of BA on the lung epithelium [25]. Wikström Shemer et al. examined the morphological abnormalities in placentas of cases with ICP and showed that ICP influences the placenta morphologically, as demonstrated by an increased terminal villous and capillary surface area, and the number of syncytial knots [10]. Imaging of the placenta and fetal tissues is a crucial point during the assessment of ICP cases. It is essential to detect and examine the structural changes in the placenta and parenchymal organs that might assist clinicians in the diagnosis of ICP. Thus, a new US quantitative analysis of the histopathological changes of the placenta and fetal organs might help to identify ICP cases by giving information. We consider that the increased mean placental, fetal lung, and fetal liver elasticity values detected in the current study might be due to the histopathological changes that have been demonstrated in the ICP cases. Also, the positive correlation between serum TBA levels and higher mean elasticity values, and a high negative correlation between gestational week at diagnosis and pSWE findings of different tissues examined in this study could be explained by the prolonged exposure of tissues to the increased serum levels of TBA. Our findings indicate that elastographic examination provides indirect yet meaningful information regarding the morphological alterations of tissues in cases with ICP. The potential clinical implication of pSWE in ICP is that it may provide non-invasive information regarding placental and fetal involvement beyond maternal biochemical markers alone, potentially offering complementary information regarding disease severity when used alongside conventional US and laboratory evaluation. This observation is supported by previous studies demonstrating the utility of pSWE in detecting tissue changes associated with inflammation, fibrosis, infarction, and placental pathologies [12, 26].

The main benefits of pSWE are that this method is non-invasive, can be conducted during the corresponding period with the same US tool utilized for fetal anatomy scanning and Doppler imaging, and its findings are operator-independent [11]. The pSWE procedure relies on acoustic push pulses that create lateral shear waves locally inside tissue without requiring dynamic external compression [17]. Also, various researchers investigated the safety of pSWE during pregnancy and observed no apparent histological damage to the placental tissue because of mechanical or thermal injury. They stated that SWE maintained the identical thermal impact as pulsed Doppler US already allowed in obstetrics [27, 28]. The acoustic output regulation in pSWE is less than the limit determined by the FDA [29]. Based on these findings, pSWE is considered to be not harmful to the fetal organs and placenta.

The main strength of this study is that, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first investigation to examine the elasticity of the placenta and fetal parenchymal tissues in vivo utilizing the pSWE procedure in ICP cases. We included data from at least 10 measurements with RMI ≥ 0.4 and IQR/M ≤ 30% to meet the reliable measurement criteria determined by the manufacturer, which provides a highly reliable quantification. All pSWE measurements were conducted by an experienced single maternal-fetal medicine specialist to eliminate the possible bias associated with the interobserver agreement. Also, as pSWE findings might vary with BMI and gestational week, we included cases that were matched for BMI and gestational age to the control and ICP groups to eliminate these parameters that could introduce potential bias. The main limitation of this study is the lack of histopathological validation of the pSWE findings, which restricts definitive interpretation of the observed stiffness changes. Although our results are consistent with previously reported histological alterations in ICP, future studies incorporating direct placental and fetal tissue pathology are essential to confirm these associations and establish the biological basis of elastographic findings. Additionally, pSWE assessments were performed at a single time point (36 weeks of gestation) to ensure standardization; however, given that the mean gestational age at diagnosis was earlier, this approach does not capture the dynamic progression of ICP. Serial pSWE measurements from diagnosis to delivery could better reflect temporal changes in tissue elasticity and provide more comprehensive data. Further studies incorporating longitudinal assessment are warranted. An important limitation is that all ICP patients received UDCA prior to pSWE assessment; although this reflects routine clinical practice, a potential modifying effect of UDCA on tissue stiffness cannot be excluded, and future prospective studies evaluating measurements before and after treatment are warranted to clarify its impact.

Conclusion

This study provides a novel perspective on the imaging of the placenta and fetal tissues in ICP through elastographic quantification. We demonstrated significantly increased stiffness values on pSWE in the peripheral and central parts of the placenta, the fetal lung, and the fetal liver in ICP cases compared to healthy pregnancies. Also, higher stiffness values of the placenta and fetal organs were positively correlated with AST, ALT, and TBA values at diagnosis, and negatively correlated with gestational week at diagnosis, suggesting a relationship between elastographic findings and disease severity.

These findings indicate that pSWE may serve as a non-invasive adjunctive imaging modality for the assessment of placental and fetal involvement in ICP and may provide complementary information regarding disease severity beyond maternal biochemical markers when used alongside conventional gray-scale and Doppler US. Although clinically applicable cut-off values cannot be defined based on the present data, the observed increase in tissue stiffness may reflect disease severity and could support clinical assessment in selected cases. Future studies with larger cohorts and longitudinal designs are warranted to validate these findings, establish diagnostic thresholds, and determine the prognostic value of pSWE in predicting perinatal outcomes in ICP.
