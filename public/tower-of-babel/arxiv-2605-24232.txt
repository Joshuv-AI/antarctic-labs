Stability of optimal transport maps and second variation of the 2 2 -Monge–Kantorovich distance
F.-U. Caja-Lopez, Matias G. Delgadino, Jun Kitagawa

Abstract
. We establish several quantitative stability estimates for optimal transport maps between non-degenerate densities on uniformly convex domains for the quadratic cost. Under Hölder regularity assumptions, we prove Lipschitz L 2 L^{2} (respectively 𝒞 1 , α \mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha} ) stability estimates for optimal transport maps in terms of the 2 2 -Monge–Kantorovich distance (respectively L p L^{p} distances) between pairs of source and target densities. When the continuity assumption is removed, we obtain a Lipschitz L 2 L^{2} stability estimate for the Brenier potentials in terms of the L 2 L^{2} distance between the source and target densities. The proofs rely on a precise characterization of the linear response of the Brenier potential along smooth interpolations of the data, obtained by linearizing the Monge–Ampère equation in divergence form. As a further application of this approach, we derive an explicit formula for the second variation of the quadratic Monge–Kantorovich distance.

Remark 2

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\left(D^{2}\phi_{t}\right)^{-1}\nabla\xi_{t},\nabla\xi_{t}\right\rangle f_{t}

\displaystyle=\Lambda\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\left(\nabla u_{t}-\left(D^{2}\phi_{t}\right)^{-1}\nabla v_{t}(\nabla\phi_{t})\right),\nabla\xi_{t}\right\rangle f_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\xi_{t}\right|^{2}f_{t}+\Lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|^{2}f_{t}+\Lambda^{4}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v_{t}(\nabla\phi_{t})\right|^{2}f_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\xi_{t}\right|^{2}f_{t}+\Lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|^{2}f_{t}+\Lambda^{4}\int_{\Omega_{*}}\left|\nabla v_{t}\right|^{2}g_{t}.

Rearranging and using the Benamou–Brenier formula (

) implies

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\xi_{t}\right|^{2}f_{t}dt\leq C\left[\mathrm{d}_{2}(f_{0},f_{1})^{2}+\mathrm{d}_{2}(g_{0},g_{1})^{2}\right],

where

depends on
\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})},\left\|g_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})}
,
a
,
\Omega
, and
\Omega_{*}
. Since

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\xi_{t}\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\nabla\xi_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}dt\leq C\left[\mathrm{d}_{2}(f_{0},f_{1})+\mathrm{d}_{2}(g_{0},g_{1})\right].

We now turn to the proof of the estimate (

Since by Lemma

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\left\|D^{2}\phi_{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,1])}^{2}+1\right)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{t}(x)|^{2}f_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt+2\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\xi_{t}(x)|^{2}f_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\left(2\left\|D^{2}\phi_{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,1])}^{2}+1\right)\mathrm{d}_{2}(f_{0},f_{1})^{2}+2\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\xi_{t}(x)|^{2}f_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt.

Now recall that Lemma

together with Lemma

3.
Proof of
theorem

1.2

The proof is based on a version of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality
[
4
]
, which can be seen to hold for log-concave measures supported on a bounded, convex domain. Specifically, if
\mu_{F}:=e^{-F(x)}\mathds{1}_{\Omega}dx
where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded and convex,
F\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, and
D^{2}F(x)>0
for all
x\in\Omega
, then for any
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega)
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{F}}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\int u\;d\mu_{F}\right)^{2}\,d\mu_{F}\leq\int_{\Omega}\left\langle(D^{2}F)^{-1}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,d\mu_{F}.

This form of inequality has previously been used to study stability of optimal transport in
[
17
, Theorem 2.3]
, where it is attributed to
[
33
, Corollary 1.3]
and
[
32
, Section 3.1.1]
, which requires at least
\mathcal{C}^{2}
regularity of
\partial\Omega
. However, it is possible to obtain this inequality from only convexity of
\Omega
with no additional assumption of regularity by following the argument on
[
14
, p. 161]
. Indeed, in the notation there, both
[
14
, (20)]
and the integration by parts formula before it hold for
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
(the former by integrating the standard Bochner’s identity for the operator
L
). Next by
[
25
, Theorem 8.3]
, there exists a weak solution
w\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
to the equation
\Delta w-\left\langle\nabla F,\nabla w\right\rangle=u-\int_{\Omega}ud\mu_{F}
. Since
\left\langle\nabla F,\nabla w\right\rangle\in L^{2}(\Omega;dx)
and
\Omega
is convex, by
[
27
, Theorem 3.2.1.2]
there exists a strong solution
v\in W^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega)
of
\Delta v=\left\langle\nabla F,\nabla w\right\rangle+u-\int_{\Omega}ud\mu_{F}
, by the comparison principle
[
25
, Theorem 8.2]
, we see
v=w
a.e. on
\Omega
. Thus there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
\Delta\varphi-\left\langle\nabla F,\nabla\varphi\right\rangle-(u-\int_{\Omega}ud\mu_{F})
has arbitrarily small
L^{2}(\Omega;dx)
norm, hence small
L^{2}(\Omega;\mu_{F})
norm, from the boundedness of
F
. Thus the argument can be concluded exactly as on
[
14
, p. 161]
to obtain (
3.1
).

Proof of
theorem

1.2
.

Again the proof will follow from the intuition outlined in
section

1.1
. In this case, we consider the linear interpolation of the densities

f_{t}=(1-t)f_{0}+tf_{1},\qquad g_{t}=(1-t)g_{0}+tg_{1}.

First assume the densities are
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
regular, then
t\mapsto f_{t}
and
t\mapsto g_{t}
are clearly smooth as curves valued in
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})
respectively, so that we can apply Theorem
1.5
. In particular, if
\tilde{\phi}_{t}
is the Brenier potential from
f_{t}
to
g_{t}
with
\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\phi}_{t}=0
for all

we have that
\tilde{\phi}_{t}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, is differentiable with respect to
t
, and
\tilde{\xi}_{t}:=\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}_{t}
solves, in the weak sense (using that
f_{t}
and
g_{t}
are linear interpolations),

(3.2)

\begin{cases}{\displaystyle-\div\left[f_{t}\big(D^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{t}\big)^{-1}\nabla\tilde{\xi}_{t}\right]=\frac{g_{1}(\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{t})-g_{0}(\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{t})}{g_{t}(\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{t})}f_{t}-(f_{1}-f_{0}),}&\textnormal{in }\Omega,\\
\left\langle f_{t}\big(D^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{t}\big)^{-1}\nabla\tilde{\xi}_{t},\vec{n}\right\rangle=0,&\textnormal{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Now define
\phi_{t}:=\tilde{\phi}_{t}+\log\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{-\tilde{\phi}_{t}}\right)
, then we see
\int_{\Omega}e^{-\phi_{t}(x)}\,dx=1
and
\nabla\phi_{t}=\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{t}
. Since
\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}_{t}
is bounded on
\overline{\Omega}
and
\int_{\Omega}e^{-\tilde{\phi}_{t}}\neq 0
, we can justify differentiating under the integral to see that
\phi_{t}
is also differentiable in
t
, and if
\xi_{t}:=\partial_{t}\phi_{t}
we have
\nabla\xi_{t}=\nabla\tilde{\xi}_{t}
. Thus (
3.2
) holds with
\xi_{t}
and
\phi_{t}
replacing
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{t}
.

Next, since
\nabla\phi_{t}(\Omega)=\Omega_{*}
, we see
\phi_{t}
is uniformly Lipschitz independent of
t
, thus
\left\|\phi_{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
is bounded in terms of
\Omega
and
\Omega_{*}
; in particular there are
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending on
\Omega
and
\Omega_{*}
such that
c_{1}\leq\rho_{t}(x):=e^{-\phi_{t}(x)}\leq c_{2}
. For the rest of the computation
C>0
will denote a constant depending on
d
,
\textnormal{diam}(\Omega)
,
\textnormal{diam}(\Omega_{*})
,
a
, and
A
which may change from line to line. For each

we can apply (
3.1
) with
F=\phi_{t}
and

to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\left(D^{2}\phi_{t}\right)^{-1}\nabla\xi_{t},\nabla\xi_{t}\right\rangle f_{t}

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\left(D^{2}\phi_{t}\right)^{-1}\nabla\xi_{t},\nabla\xi_{t}\right\rangle\,d\rho_{t}

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right)^{2}\,d\rho_{t}\geq C^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right)^{2}\,dx.

Taking
\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}
as a test function in

3.2
, and combining with the above, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right)^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\left(D^{2}\phi_{t}\right)^{-1}\nabla\xi_{t},\nabla\xi_{t}\right\rangle f_{t}

\displaystyle=-C\int_{\Omega}(f_{1}-f_{0})\left(\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right)+C\int_{\Omega}\frac{g_{1}(\nabla\phi_{t})-g_{0}(\nabla\phi_{t})}{g_{t}(\nabla\phi_{t})}f_{t}\left(\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}(f_{1}-f_{0})^{2}+C\int_{\Omega_{*}}\left(g_{1}-g_{0}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right)^{2}

which shows

(3.3)

\left\|\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\left\|f_{1}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|g_{1}-g_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{*})}\right).

For a fixed

we calculate,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\xi_{t}(x)-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{0}(x)-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi_{t}(y)e^{-\phi_{t}(y)}dydt

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{0}(x)+\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}\left(e^{-\phi_{t}(y)}\right)dtdy

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{0}(x)+\int_{\Omega}\left(e^{-\phi_{1}(y)}-e^{-\phi_{0}(y)}\right)dy=\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{0}(x).

Now the stability bound is a consequence of Minkowski’s integral inequality combined with (
3.3
):

\left\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\left(\xi_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\xi_{t}d\rho_{t}\right)\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\left\|f_{1}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|g_{1}-g_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{*})}\right).

This finishes the proof of

1.2
for smooth densities.

Now note that the regularity of

,

was only used to justify the differentiability of

with respect to

and the bound obtained at the end is independent of such regularity. Therefore, an approximation argument allows us to extend the inequality to discontinuous densities: for general

,

as in the hypotheses, let

Finally, (

.

As before, we consider

\phi_{0}

.

Let us first show the one dimensional bound

\left\|\phi_{1}^{\prime}-\phi_{0}^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\left\|f_{1}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}(I)}+\left\|g_{1}-g_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}(J)}\right),

which we stated in (

). We use that in one dimension the optimal transport map can be simply expressed as

\phi_{i}^{\prime}=G_{i}^{-1}\circ F_{i},

where
F_{i}
,
G_{i}
are the cumulative distribution functions of
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
. From here the bound is an elementary computation. Indeed, for
x_{1}\leq x_{2}
in

,

\displaystyle\lvert G_{i}(x_{1})-G_{i}(x_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}g_{i}\geq a\lvert x_{2}-x_{1}\rvert,

hence the
G_{i}^{-1}
are
\frac{1}{a}
-Lipschitz and we have

\displaystyle\left|G_{1}^{-1}\left(F_{1}(x)\right)-G_{0}^{-1}\left(F_{0}(x)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|G_{1}^{-1}\left(F_{1}(x)\right)-G_{1}^{-1}\left(F_{0}(x)\right)\right|+\left|G_{1}^{-1}\left(F_{0}(x)\right)-G_{0}^{-1}\left(F_{0}(x)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a}\left\|F_{1}-F_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\left\|G_{1}^{-1}-G_{0}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a}\left\|f_{1}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}(I)}+\left\|G_{1}^{-1}-G_{0}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}.

For the second term, we note that given

\left|G_{1}^{-1}(y)-G_{0}^{-1}(y)\right|=\left|G_{0}^{-1}\left(G_{0}\left(G_{1}^{-1}(y)\right)\right)-G_{0}^{-1}(y)\right|\leq\frac{1}{a}\left|G_{0}\left(G_{1}^{-1}(y)\right)-y\right|.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|G_{1}^{-1}-G_{0}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a}\sup_{y\in[0,1]}\left|G_{0}\left(G_{1}^{-1}(y)\right)-y\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\sup_{x\in J}\left|G_{0}(x)-G_{1}(x)\right|\leq\frac{1}{a}\left\|g_{1}-g_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}(J)},

which proves (

Next, we show that, if we remove the lower bound hypothesis

We prove this with a one dimensional example on

and

g_{a}(x)=\begin{cases}(p+1)a^{-p}(a-x)^{p},&0\leq x\leq a\\

In this case, the optimal transport map between
f
and

is given by
G_{a}^{-1}
, where
G_{a}
is the cumulative function of
g_{a}
. We will consider

G_{a}^{-1}(y)=\begin{cases}a\left(1-\left(1-\frac{y}{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\right),&0\leq y\leq a,\\
a+(1-a)\left(\frac{y-a}{1-a}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+1}},&a\leq y\leq 1.\end{cases}

Since the

we have

\left\|G_{a}^{-1}-G_{1/2}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\geq\left|G_{a}^{-1}(1/2)-G_{1/2}^{-1}(1/2)\right|=\left|\frac{1-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}}{2(1-\varepsilon)}-\frac{1}{2}\right|\gtrsim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}.

On the other hand, a simple computation shows that for each fixed

Therefore, for any

\displaystyle 0

Lemma 6.2

Given a fixed time

\displaystyle{\left\langle(D^{2}\phi_{t})^{-1}\nabla\xi,\vec{n}\right\rangle=\left|(D^{2}\phi_{t})^{-1}\vec{n}\right|q},&\textnormal{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Let us first re-write the boundary condition. Consider the function

, which verifies

As a consequence, we see that

which shows the equivalence between the boundary conditions. Next, observe that

Lemma 6.3
.

Consider a fixed
t
and

,

Proof.

The previous Lemma

tells us that
D_{\phi}\Gamma(t,\phi_{t})\xi=(p,q)
corresponds to the problem

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\div\left[f_{t}(D^{2}\phi_{t})^{-1}\nabla\xi\right]=pf_{t}},&\textnormal{in }\Omega\\
\displaystyle{\left\langle(D^{2}\phi_{t})^{-1}\nabla\xi,\vec{n}\right\rangle=\left|(D^{2}\phi_{t})^{-1}\vec{n}\right|q},&\textnormal{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\div\left[f_{t}\left(D^{2}\phi_{t}\right)^{-1}\nabla\xi\right]\,dx

Proof of Theorem

The previous analysis shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})\mid\int_{\Omega}\xi=0\right\}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(p,q)\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{C}^{1,\beta}(\partial\Omega)\mid\int_{\Omega}pf_{t}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|(D^{2}\phi_{t})^{-1}\vec{n}\right|qf_{t}\right\}.

is a smooth map in a neighborhood of

Appendix A
Regularity of compositions

All results stated here are taken straight out of
[
15
]
with some simplifications. The main goal is to study the regularity of composition of maps in terms of the regularity of each factor.

Basic definitions and notation.
We will consider Banach spaces
E
,
F
,
G
and maps

f:U\subset E\longrightarrow F,\qquad g:V\subset F\longrightarrow G,

where
U
,
V
are open sets so that
f(U)\subset V
. Hölder spaces will be denoted with only one real index so that
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}\equiv\mathcal{C}^{k+\alpha}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\alpha<1
. This notation makes it easier to state the required results.

We remind the reader that a map
f:U\subset E\longrightarrow F
is said to be
Fréchet differentiable
at
x\in U
if there is a bounded linear operator
df(x):E\longrightarrow F
such that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\left\|f(x+th)-f(x)-tdf(x)h\right\|_{F}}{t}=0.

Then, by induction,
f
is said to be
n
times differentiable if
x\mapsto df(x)
is
n-1
times differentiable and we set
d^{n}f=d(d^{n-1}f)
, where
d^{n}f(x):E^{n}\longrightarrow F
is identified with a multilinear map.

When
n
is a natural number, we define
\mathcal{C}^{n}(U)
as the set of
n
-times continuously differentiable maps
f:U\subset E\longrightarrow F
and equip it with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{n}(U)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sup_{x}\left\|d^{k}f(x)\right\|_{F}.

When
r>0
is a nonnegative number of the form
r=n+\alpha
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\alpha<1
, we define
\mathcal{C}^{r}(U)
as the set subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{n}(U)
such that the
n
-th derivative is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous. We equip
\mathcal{C}^{r}(U)
with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{r}(U)}=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{n}(U)}+\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\left\|d^{n}f(x)-d^{n}f(y)\right\|_{F}}{\left\|x-y\right\|_{E}}.

Frechet differentiability in relation with regularity in Euclidean space.

In this work, we consider functions
f:[0,1]\times\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
open, and view them as a curve in a Banach space

\mathcal{F}:[0,1]\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),\qquad\mathcal{F}(t)(x)=f(t,x).

It is not immediately obvious how classical regularity of
f
with respect to
(t,x)
translates into regularity for the map
\mathcal{F}
. For example, requiring that
f
be differentiable in
t
, that
\mathcal{F}(t)\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t)\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
is not sufficient to ensure Frechet differentiability of
\mathcal{F}
, since this also requires convergence of incremental quotients in
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
. Nevertheless, we may still conclude that
\mathcal{F}
is Frechet differentiable if we slightly strengthen these assumptions.

Proposition A.1
.

Consider
f:[0,1]\times\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

There is
0<\alpha<1
with
f(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for each
t
.

(2)

f
is differentiable in
t
over
(0,1)\times\overline{\Omega}
, and
\partial_{t}f
is continuous over
[0,1]\times\overline{\Omega}
.

(3)

For each
t
,
\partial_{t}f(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
with
M:=\sup_{t\in(0,1)}\|\partial_{t}f(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}<\infty
.

Then, for any
0<\beta<\alpha
, the mapping

\mathcal{F}:[0,1]\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega}),\qquad\mathcal{F}(t)(x):=f(t,x),

is Frechet differentiable with continuous differential.

Proof.

The clear candidate for differential
d\mathcal{F}(t):\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
is
\left\langle d\mathcal{F}(t),h\right\rangle=\partial_{t}f(t,\cdot)h
, which we can identify with
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t):=\partial_{t}f(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
. We must show that for
t\in(0,1)
,

(A.1)

\lim_{h\to 0}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{F}(t+h)-\mathcal{F}(t)}{h}-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}=0.

Using the inequality
\|g\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|g\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}^{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}
, we obtain when
0<t+h<1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\mathcal{F}(t+h)-\mathcal{F}(t)}{h}-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\partial_{t}f(t+sh,\cdot)-\partial_{t}f(t,\cdot)\Big)\,ds\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\partial_{t}f(t+sh,\cdot)-\partial_{t}f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}^{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\,ds.

Note that
{\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}}\left\|\partial_{t}f(t+sh,\cdot)-\partial_{t}f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}=0
for each
s
due to the uniform continuity of
\partial_{t}f
, so the dominated convergence theorem shows (
A.1
) as desired. The continuity of the differential follows from a similar calculation, as

\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t+h)-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq CM^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left\|\partial_{t}f(t+h,\cdot)-\partial_{t}f(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}^{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\overset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0.\qed

In particular, if
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
up to the boundary with respect to
(t,x)

Appendix B
A quantitative
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
estimate for the Brenier potential

In this appendix we prove Lemma
1.7
. Our argument only relies on the
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
estimate from
[
8
]
and the fact that the Brenier potential is
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
when the densities are
\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}
, which was proven in
[
11
]
. Let us begin by motivating the main idea behind the proof.

Consider
f\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
,
g\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})
, let
\phi
be the solution of (
1.14
). Assume we are able to prove that

(f,g)\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})\mapsto\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})

is continuous as a map between Banach spaces. Then the conclusion of Lemma
1.7
would immediately follow, as a result of the compactness of the following set in
\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})
:

\left\{(f,g)\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})\mid f,g\geq a,\;\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})},\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})}\leq M\right\}.

Then, one may attempt to show the continuity (smoothness) of
(f,g)\mapsto\phi
through the implicit function theorem. This would involve the definition of a functional similar to one in Section
6
, which would be of the form

\displaystyle\Gamma:\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})\times\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)

\displaystyle\Gamma(f,g,\phi):=\left(\log\det D^{2}\phi+\log g(\nabla\phi)-\log f,\Gamma^{(2)}(f,g,\phi)\right),

where the coordinate
\Gamma^{(2)}
is chosen to enforce the boundary condition
\nabla\phi(\Omega)=\Omega_{*}
. However this runs into a technical problem: in order to apply the implicit function theorem, we need
\Gamma
to be at least
\mathcal{C}^{1}
as a map between Hölder spaces. Unfortunately, for
(g,\phi)\mapsto g(\nabla\phi)
to be a smooth map,
g
needs to belong to a regularity class better than
\mathcal{C}^{1}
(see Appendix
A
or
[
15
]
for further details on the regularity of composition).

In this manuscript we avoid this problem by first considering the case where
g
is constant. To be precise, we consider the mapping

f\in\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\mapsto\phi_{f}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),

where
\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
denotes the subset of
\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
formed by functions bounded away from
0
and
\phi_{f}
is the unique convex function with average
0
solving

(B.1)

\begin{cases}\det D^{2}\phi_{f}=\left\langle f\right\rangle_{\Omega}^{-1}|\Omega_{*}|f,&\textnormal{in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\phi_{f}(\Omega)=\Omega_{*},\end{cases}

where
\left\langle f\right\rangle_{\Omega}:=\int_{\Omega}f
. Note that the factor
\left\langle f\right\rangle_{\Omega}^{-1}|\Omega_{*}|
only appears so that the mass balance condition is satisfied for the optimal transport problem. Since
\partial\Omega
and
\partial\Omega_{*}
are
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
regular, by
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
, for any
f\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}_{>0}(\overline{\Omega})
, the the above solution
\phi_{f}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, which is uniformly convex by Remark
2
; in particular this shows the map
f\mapsto\phi_{f}
is well-defined on
\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}_{>0}(\overline{\Omega})
. In this setting, we are able to prove the following lemma:

Lemma B.1
.

Assume
\Omega
,
\Omega_{*}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
are convex sets where
\partial\Omega,\,\partial\Omega_{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\beta}
. Then for any
\alpha<\beta
,
f\in\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\mapsto\phi_{f}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
when viewed as a mapping between Banach spaces.

We will also make use of the following estimate taken from
[
8
]
:

Lemma B.2

(Caffarelli)
.

Let
\Omega,\Omega_{*}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded convex sets and consider two probability densities
f
,
g
over
\Omega
,
\Omega_{*}
such that
a\leq f,g\leq A
for some constants
a,A>0
. Let
\phi
be the unique convex potential with average
0
inducing the optimal map
(\nabla\phi)_{\sharp}f=g
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that

\left\|\phi\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\delta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C,

where both
\delta
and
C
depend on
a
,
A
and the maximum and minimum diameter of
\Omega
,
\Omega_{*}
.

Before proving Lemma
B.1
, let us show that it implies Lemma
1.7
.

Proof of Lemma
1.7
.

Let
\phi
be the solution of (
1.14
) and fix
\alpha<\beta
. By Lemma
B.2
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\delta}
with an estimate on
\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\delta}(\overline{\Omega})}
. Therefore, letting
\tilde{\alpha}:=\min(\alpha,\delta\beta)<\beta
, Lemmas
A.2
and
A.4
show that

\left\|f/g(\nabla\phi)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\tilde{\alpha}}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\left(d,a,\Omega,\Omega_{*},\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})},\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})}\right)=:M.

Now note that
\phi=\phi_{f/g(\nabla\phi)}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
in the notation of Lemma
B.1
. Since the set

\left\{h\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\tilde{\alpha}}(\overline{\Omega})\mid h\geq a/\lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})},\;\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\tilde{\alpha}}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq M\right\}

is compact as a subset of
\mathcal{C}^{0,\tilde{\alpha}-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega})
, for
0<\varepsilon<\tilde{\alpha}
arbitrary, applying Lemma
B.1
shows

(B.2)

\left\|\phi\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\tilde{\alpha}-\varepsilon}(\Omega)}=\left\|\phi_{f/g(\nabla\phi)}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\tilde{\alpha}-\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(d,a,\Omega,\Omega_{*},\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})},\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})},\alpha,\beta\right).

In particular this implies a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
bound on
\phi
, thus we can bootstrap and use Lemmas
A.2
,
A.4
again to conclude

\left\|f/g(\nabla\phi)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\left(d,a,\Omega,\Omega_{*},\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})},\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega}_{*})},\alpha,\beta\right),

which can be combined with Lemma
B.1
again to produce the desired estimate.
∎

Now it only remains to prove Lemma
B.1
, which follows the same outline used in Section
6
and in
[
26
]
.

Proof of Lemma
B.1
.

The idea is to apply the implicit function theorem to the functional

\Gamma:\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega),\qquad\Gamma(f,\phi):=\left(\det D^{2}\phi-|\Omega_{*}|\left\langle f\right\rangle_{\Omega}^{-1}f,\omega_{*}(\nabla\phi)\right),

where recall that
\omega_{*}
is a convex defining function of
\Omega_{*}
. The domain of
\Gamma
is defined by the open sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\mid\inf_{\Omega}f>0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{u}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})

\displaystyle:=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\mid\inf_{x\in\Omega,|e|=1}\left\langle D^{2}\phi(x)e,e\right\rangle>0\right\}.

From Lemma
A.3
we immediately see that
\Gamma
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
with differential

D_{\phi}\Gamma(f,\phi)\xi=\left(\textnormal{tr}\left[A[\phi]D^{2}\xi\right],\left\langle\vec{n}_{*}\left(\nabla\phi\right),\nabla\xi\right\rangle\right),\qquad A[\phi]=(\det D^{2}\phi)\left(D^{2}\phi\right)^{-1},

where
A[\phi]
is the cofactor matrix of
D^{2}\phi
. Therefore, showing that
D_{\phi}\Gamma(f,\phi)
is a diffeomorphism is equivalent to showing that for each
p\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
,
q\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
, there is a unique solution
\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
of

(B.3)

\begin{cases}\textnormal{tr}\left[A[\phi]D^{2}\xi\right]=p,&\textnormal{in }\Omega\\
\left\langle\vec{n}_{*}(\nabla\phi),\nabla\xi\right\rangle=q,&\textnormal{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

To study this problem, we will re-write its boundary condition and note that it is equivalent to the same problem written in divergence form. This would be immediately obvious if
\phi
were
\mathcal{C}^{3}
, since it is known that cofactor matrices are divergence free.

Claim
.

Let
\phi=\phi_{f}
be the solution of (
B.1
). A function
\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
solves (
B.3
) if and only if it is a weak solution of

(B.4)

\begin{cases}\div\left(A[\phi]\nabla\xi\right)=p,&\textnormal{in }\Omega\\
\left\langle A[\phi]\nabla\xi,\vec{n}\right\rangle=\left|A[\phi]\vec{n}\right|q,&\textnormal{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

As a consequence, (
B.3
) has a solution if and only if the following compatibility condition holds:

\int_{\Omega}p+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|A[\phi]\vec{n}\right|q=0.

Moreover, such a solution is unique up to additive constants.

Proof of the claim.

Noting that
D^{2}\phi>0
, the boundary condition in (
B.4
) is equivalent to
\left\langle(D^{2}\phi)^{-1}\nabla\xi,\vec{n}\right\rangle=\left|(D^{2}\phi)^{-1}\vec{n}\right|q
, hence is equivalent to the boundary condition in (
B.3
) as in the proof of Lemma
6.2
.
Assume we have a solution
\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
of (
B.3
) and let
\phi_{\varepsilon}
be a mollification of
\phi
. Then, using that
\div(A[\phi_{\varepsilon}])=0
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\textnormal{tr}\left(A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]D^{2}\xi\right)\theta=\int_{\Omega}\div\left(A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]\nabla\xi\right)\theta=-\int_{\Omega}\left\langle A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]\nabla\xi,\nabla\theta\right\rangle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left\langle A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]\nabla\xi,\vec{n}\right\rangle\theta.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we see that
\xi
satisfies the weak formulation of (
B.4
). Now assume
\xi
is a weak solution of (
B.4
) and consider
\xi_{\varepsilon}
where for each
\varepsilon>0
we have

\begin{cases}\div\left(A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]\nabla\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)=p,&\textnormal{in }\Omega\\
\left\langle A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]\nabla\xi_{\varepsilon},\vec{n}\right\rangle=\left|A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]\vec{n}\right|q,&\textnormal{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Using that the previous equation is also written in non-divergence form, the classical Schauder estimates (see
[
25
, Thm. 6.30]
) tell us that
\xi_{\varepsilon}
has a bound
\left\|\xi_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C
independent of
\varepsilon
. Then, a simple compactness argument tells us that
\xi_{\varepsilon}\to\xi
in
\mathcal{C}^{2}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Then, we can let
\varepsilon\to 0
in the equalities

\textnormal{tr}\left(A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]D^{2}\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)=p\textnormal{ in }\Omega,\qquad\qquad\left\langle A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]\nabla\xi_{\varepsilon},\vec{n}\right\rangle=\left|A[\phi_{\varepsilon}]\vec{n}\right|q\textnormal{ on }\partial\Omega,

which hold pointwise. Finally, since
\partial\Omega
is
\mathcal{C}^{2,\beta}
regular, the existence of globally regular solutions under the compatibility condition is a simple consequence of the Lax-Milgram Theorem and elliptic regularity.
∎

Next, we consider a fixed
f_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, its corresponding potential
\phi_{f_{0}}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
and will show that the mapping
f\mapsto\phi_{f}
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
in a neighborhood of
f_{0}
. To do this, first note the previous claim shows that
D_{\phi}\Gamma(f_{0},\phi_{f_{0}})
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{Y}_{\phi_{0}}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\mid\int_{\Omega}\xi=0\right\},

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{Y}_{\phi_{0}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(p,q)\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)\mid\int_{\Omega}p+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|A[\phi_{f_{0}}]\vec{n}\right|q=0\right\}.

Then define, writing
\Gamma(f,\phi)=(\Gamma^{(1)}(f,\phi),\Gamma^{(2)}(f,\phi))
,

\tilde{\Gamma}:\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times\left(\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{C}_{u}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\phi_{0}},\qquad\tilde{\Gamma}(f,\phi)=\left(\Gamma^{(1)}(f,\phi)-C_{f,\phi}\left\langle f\right\rangle_{\Omega}^{-1}f,\Gamma^{(2)}(f,\phi)\right),

where
C_{f,\phi}
is a constant chosen to ensure
\tilde{\Gamma}(f,\phi)\in\mathcal{Y}_{\phi_{0}}
, i.e.

C_{f,\phi}=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma^{(1)}(f,\phi)+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|A[\phi_{0}]\vec{n}\right|\Gamma^{(2)}(f,\phi).

We now check that
\tilde{\Gamma}
satisfies the hypothesis of the implicit function theorem. The smoothness of
\tilde{\Gamma}
is a consequence of the smoothness of
\Gamma
, and we calculate

D_{\phi}\tilde{\Gamma}(f,\phi)\xi=D_{\phi}\Gamma(f,\phi)\xi+\left(\int_{\Omega}D_{\phi}\Gamma^{(1)}(f,\phi)\xi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|A[\phi_{0}]\vec{n}\right|D_{\phi}\Gamma^{(2)}(f,\phi)\xi,0\right)\left\langle f\right\rangle_{\Omega}^{-1}f.

Note that the second term is
0
at
(f_{0},\phi_{0})
because
D_{\phi}\Gamma(f_{0},\phi_{0})\xi\in\mathcal{Y}_{\phi_{0}}
, hence

D_{\phi}\tilde{\Gamma}(f_{0},\phi_{0})=D_{\phi}\Gamma(f_{0},\phi_{0})|_{\mathcal{X}},

showing that
D_{\phi}\tilde{\Gamma}(f_{0},\phi_{0}):\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\phi_{0}}
is a diffeomorphism and the implicit function theorem applies. Thus there is a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{C}_{>0}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
of
f_{0}
and a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
mapping

f\in\mathcal{U}\mapsto\tilde{\phi}_{f}\in\mathcal{C}_{u}^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{X},\quad\textnormal{such that }\tilde{\Gamma}(f,\tilde{\phi}_{f})=0.

Finally, we verify that
\tilde{\phi}_{f}=\phi_{f}
. From
\tilde{\Gamma}(f,\tilde{\phi}_{f})=0
we conclude that for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{cases}\det D^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{f}=\left\langle f\right\rangle_{\Omega}^{-1}\left(|\Omega_{*}|-C\right)f,&\textnormal{in }\Omega\\
\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{f}(\Omega)=\Omega_{*}.\end{cases}

Since
\tilde{\phi}_{f}
is strictly convex,
\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{f}
is a one-to-one mapping and we can perform the change of variables
y=\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{f}(x)
, which shows

|\Omega_{*}|=\int_{\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{f}(\Omega)}1\,dy=\int_{\Omega}\det D^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{f}(x)\,dx=\left\langle f\right\rangle_{\Omega}^{-1}\left(|\Omega_{*}|-C\right)\int_{\Omega}f=|\Omega_{*}|-C.

Therefore, we have
C=0
and
\tilde{\phi}_{f}=\phi_{f}
, finishing the proof.
∎
