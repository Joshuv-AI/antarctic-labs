The complete mitochondrial genome of Myotis chinensis (Chiroptera: Vespertilionidae) and its phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Xinyu Miao, Lizhi Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe vesper bat, Myotis chinensis, is a member of the family Vespertilionidae and is found in Southeast Asia. There are, to date, no reports of the complete mitochondrial genome of this species. In this study, the mitochondrial genome of Myotis chinensis collected from Henan Province, China, was sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. The genome was found to be 16,812 bp in length, with a typical closed circular double-stranded structure. It contained 22 tRNA genes, 2 rRNA genes, 13 protein-coding genes, and 1 non-coding control region. The overall A + T content was 64.7%, representing a distinct AT bias in the nucleotide composition. Phylogenetic analysis based on 40 Myotis species revealed that M. chinensis is most closely related to M. nattereri and M. bombinus.Keywords: Myotis chinensis, mitochondrial genome, gene arrangement, evolutionary relationships

Introduction

Myotis chinensis (Thomas, 1857) is a vesper bat belonging to the family Vespertilionidae. This large bat is similar in appearance to M. blythii, and was once regarded as a subspecies of M. myotis (Zhang et al. 1997). Myotis chinensis is listed as Near Threatened (NT) in RCBM (Jiang 2021). Its distribution is predominantly in central, southern, and eastern China, with additional records from Southeast Asia, including Thailand, Myanmar, and northern Vietnam. Notably, Henan Province in China represents a new recorded distribution for this species (Yue et al. 2025). Currently, only the cytochrome C oxidase subunit 1 (COI) and cytochrome B (CYTB) genes and control region sequences of the M. chinensis mitochondrial genome have been reported. Therefore, the objectives of this study were to sequence and characterize the complete mitochondrial genome of M. chinensis to clarify its taxonomic status and improve understanding of the phylogenetic relationships within the Vespertilionidae. The findings provide a valuable reference for further research on this species.

Materials and methods

The M. chinensis sample (Figure 1) used in this study was collected in December 2023 from a cave at Yangrangfang, Fengji Town, Shangcheng County, China (31.8428°N, 115.5042°E, 95.01 m). This specimen has been deposited in the School of Resources and Environmental Engineering at Anhui University (voucher number: ADBF052, Lizhi Zhou, zhoulz@ahu.edu.cn). Genomic DNA was isolated from samples of muscle tissue using the MagPure Bacterial DNA Kit (Magen, Guangzhou, China). Library construction was then performed using the Hieff NGS®MaxUp II DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina® (YEASEN, Shanghai, China), followed by sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) using a 2 × 150 bp paired-end kit. Quality assessment and filtering of all raw reads were performed with FastQC v 0.11.2, generating 17.6 Gb of clean data. The read coverage depth map is depicted in Figure S1. SPAdes v3.15 (Bankevich et al. 2012) and MITObim v1.9.1 software were employed for sequence assembly and iterative mapping. Finally, the circular gene map of the M. chinensis mitochondrial genome was drawn using Circos.

M. chinensis sample (ADBF052) collected from Yangrangfang, Fengji Town, Shangcheng County, Henan Province of China (the photo was taken by Xinyu Miao).

The mitochondrial genomes of 40 Myotis species were retrieved from the GenBank database. Vespertilio sinensis (NC024558) and Hipposideros armiger (NC018540) were used as outgroups (Table S1). A Bayesian inference (BI) phylogenetic tree was constructed based on the concatenated sequences of 13 protein-coding genes and 24 RNA genes. All selected gene sequences were aligned using the MAFFT v7 plugin (Katoh and Standler 2013) in Phylosuite v1.2.3 (Xiang et al. 2023). The protein-coding and RNA gene datasets were trimmed using the Gblock and trimAl plugins, respectively, and the aligned sequences were concatenated. ModelFinder was used to select GTR+F + I + G4 as the most appropriate evolutionary model (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017). The BI phylogenetic tree was reconstructed using MrBayes v3.2.7 and was visualized using the online tool iTOL.

Results

The complete mitochondrial genome of M. chinensis was found to be 16,812 bp in length (Figure 2). The genome contained 37 genes, including 22 tRNA genes, 2 rRNA genes, 13 protein-coding genes, and 1 non-coding control region (Table S2). Its structural arrangement was consistent with those of previously reported mitochondrial genomes of the genus Myotis. The overall nucleotide composition was as follows: A: 34.1%, T: 30.6%, G: 12.8%, and C: 22.5%; the A + T content (64.7%) was significantly higher than the G + C content (35.3%), with an AT-skew of 0.054 and a GC-skew of −0.275, indicating a distinct AT bias. The genome was compact, with multiple intergenic spacers and overlapping regions. The largest gene overlap (43 bp) was detected between ATP8 and ATP6, while the longest intergenic spacer (32 bp) was found between tRNAAsn and tRNACys. All 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs) contained typical ATN start codons. Specifically, ND3 and ND5 used ATA as the start codon, and ND2 used ATT, while the remaining 10 PCGs all had ATG as the start codon. COX3 terminated with TGA, while ND1, ND3, ND2, and ND4 had incomplete stop codons (TA- or T–), and all other PCGs terminated with TAA as the stop codon.

The phylogenetic results are shown in Figure 3. It can be seen that the genus Myotis is monophyletic and essentially split into two major clades, with M. myotis and M. blythii forming one clade and representing sister species, while M. chinensis was clustered with M. nattereri and M. bombinus on a single evolutionary branch, indicating a closer evolutionary relationship with these two species.

Discussion and conclusion

This study analyzed the complete mitochondrial genome of M. chinensis, thereby filling the gap in mitochondrial genome data for this species. The structure of the genome was observed to be consistent with the published mitochondrial genomes of Myotis species.

Mitochondrial genomes provide more information on nucleotide sites and gene order than nuclear genomes, which is helpful in constructing a more robust evolutionary framework. In this study, the phylogenetic tree based on the mitochondrial sequences of 40 Myotis species indicated that the Myotis genus is monophyletic, with all species originating from a single common ancestor. This result is consistent with the conclusions of previous studies (Korstian et al. 2022; Vargas-Trejo et al. 2023). The Myotis genus divided into two major clades at an early evolutionary stage. Kawai et al. (2003) reported that M. chinensis and M. nattereri were clustered within one clade, indicating a close genetic relationship between the two species. However, the present study observed that M. chinensis did not cluster with M. nattereri; instead, M. nattereri was first clustered with M. bombinus, with the two species then clustered with M. chinensis to form an evolutionary branch. This phenomenon may be associated with the discrepancies in the selected gene fragments as well as the differences in the scale of phylogenetic tree construction.

The complete mitochondrial genome of M. chinensis was sequenced and characterized in this study. These genetic data can serve as fundamental resources for further investigations into the phylogenetic position of M. chinensis, as well as the evolution of species within the genus Myotis and the family Vespertilionidae.
