Pick-up Sticks and the General Fibonacci Numbers
Tian Cao Lin

Abstract
In the article by Edward et al. [ 6 ] , it was shown that the probability that no three sticks randomly chosen from the unit interval can form a triangle equals the reciprocal of the product of the first n n Fibonacci numbers. The authors further suggested a generalization to higher ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -gons ( k ≥ 4 ) (k\geq 4) . This note proves that, indeed, for any k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , the probability that no k + 1 k+1 of n n independent uniform [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] lengths can form a ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -gon is expressed as a product whose factors involve a k k -step Fibonacci-type recurrence. The method follows closely the original argument of [ 6 ] , while making explicit the algebraic structure that governs the general case.

Introduction

The problem of random sticks is one of the classic problems in geometric probability with a long history, dating back to the “broken stick problem” posed by Lemoine in 1873
[
3
]
: a stick of unit length is broken at two random points, and one asks for the probability that the three resulting segments can form a triangle. Since then, many generalizations of this basic setting have been studied. D’Andrea and Gomez
[
1
]
extended the problem to the case of
n
pieces, proving that the probability that a stick broken randomly into
n
pieces can form an
n
-gon is
1-n/2^{n-1}
. More generally, Verreault
[
8
,
7
]
and Mukerjee
[
4
]
systematically studied the “
k
-gon” version: given a stick broken into
n
pieces, what is the probability that every subset of
k
pieces can form a
k
-gon? Remarkably, these probability formulas are deeply connected with the
(k-1)
-step Fibonacci numbers.

In a different direction, Petersen and Tenner
[
5
]
introduced a model that is fundamentally different in its assumptions — the
pick-up stick model
. In this model, the lengths of
n
sticks are drawn independently from the uniform distribution on
[0,1]
, and there is no constraint that they sum to one. They proved that the probability that
n
such sticks cannot form an
n
-gon is
\frac{1}{(n-1)!}

[
5
]
.

In the article
[
6
]
, the authors proved that the probability that no three sticks can form a triangle is the reciprocal of the product of the first
n
Fibonacci numbers. They extended this method to quadrilaterals, where the probability expression involves the Tribonacci sequence and includes a correction term, and they discussed further extensions to general
k+1
-gons for
k\geq 2
. Their results hint at a general pattern: the avoidance of
k+1
-gons corresponds to a
k
-step Fibonacci-type recurrence. This note proves this generalization.

The method used in this paper is essentially the same as that in the article
[
6
]
.

2
Main result

We first denote

\displaystyle A_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&0&k-1\\
1&0&\dots&0&k-2\\
0&1&\dots&0&k-3\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&1&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}.

(1)

and define the recurrence that will appear.

Definition 1
.

For a fixed integer
k\geq 2
, the sequence
\bigl\{F_{k}^{l}\bigr\}_{l=2-k}^{\infty}
is called a
k
-step Fibonacci‑type recurrence
if it satisfies

F_{k}^{l}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}F_{k}^{l-j},\qquad l=2,3,\dots,

with the initial conditions

F_{k}^{1}=1,\quad F_{k}^{0}=F_{k}^{-1}=\dots=F_{k}^{-k+2}=0.

In particular, when
k=2
, the sequence
\{F_{2}^{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
is the well-known Fibonacci sequence.
Theorem 1
is our the main result.

Theorem 1
.

If
n
real numbers are chosen uniformly from the unit interval
[0,1]
, for
k\geq 3
,
the probability that no
k+1
of the numbers can form a
k+1
-gons is

P_{n}^{(k)}=\prod_{l=1}^{n-k+2}\frac{1}{F^{l}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k-2}\frac{1}{F^{n-i+1}_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-i-1}(k-i-j)F_{k}^{n-k+j}},

where
F_{k}^{l}
is the
l
-th term in
k
-step Fibonacci-type recurrence.

Before proving the theorem we state a lemma that captures the algebraic relations generated by the integration process.

Lemma 2
.

Let matrix
A_{k}
defined by (
1
),
R^{1}=(1,1,\dots,1)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
R^{l}=(R_{1}^{l},R^{l}_{2},\dots,R_{k}^{l})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
satisfy

R^{l}=A^{l-1}_{k}R^{1},l=1,2,3,\dots

Then,

R_{k}^{l}=F_{k}^{l},R_{k-1}^{l}=F^{l+1}_{k}

and

\quad R_{i}^{l}=F^{l+k-i}_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-i-1}(k-i-j)F_{k}^{l+j-1},i=1,2,\dots,k-2.

where
F_{k}^{l}

((l=1,2,3,\dots,)
is
k
-step Fibonacci-type recurrence.

Now, assuming the lemma holds, we prove the main theorem.

Proof.

Let
U_{(1)}\leq U_{(2)}\leq\dots\leq U_{(n)}
be the order statistics of
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
. The condition that no
k+1
lengths can form a
(k+1)
-gon is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=l}^{l+k-1}U_{(i)}\leq U_{(l+k)},\qquad\text{for all }1\leq l\leq n-k.

(2)

A classical fact
[
2
]
states that the order statistics of a uniform sample can be represented via normalized sums of independent exponential variables, if
x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\operatorname{Exp}(1)
with density
f(x)=e^{-x}
and
S=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n+1}
, then

U_{(i)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{x_{1}+\dots+x_{i}}{S},\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Substituting this into (
2
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{i+l}}{S}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{i+k}}{S}

\displaystyle(k-1)\sum_{l=1}^{i}x_{l}+(k-2)

\displaystyle x_{i+1}+\dots+x_{i+k-2}\leq x_{i+k},

where
1\leq i\leq n-k
. With
x_{l}

(l=1,2,\dots,k)
being unbounded between
0
and infinity.

The successive inequalities define a nested region in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the required probability corresponds to the volume of this region under the exponential density. To evaluate it, we integrate successively from
x_{n}
down to
x_{1}
, each step producing a factor determined by the recurrence relation that will lead to the
k
step Fibonacci pattern.

To make the subsequent formulas more concise, we introduce the notation
L_{j}
, that

\displaystyle L_{j}=(k-1)\sum_{l=1}^{j-k}x_{l}+(k-2)x_{j-k+1}+\dots+x_{j-2},

(3)

for
j=k+1,k+2,\dots,n
. And
L_{j}=0
for
j=1,2,\dots,k
.

the probability we need to calculate is

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(k)}=\int_{L_{1}}^{\infty}\int_{L_{2}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\dots\int_{L_{n}}^{\infty}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}dx_{n}\dots dx_{2}dx_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{L_{1}}^{\infty}\int_{L_{2}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\cdots\int^{\infty}_{L_{n-1}}e^{-k\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}x_{l}+(k-1)x_{n-k+1}+\dots+2x_{n-2}+x_{n-1}}dx_{n-1}\dots dx_{2}dx_{1}

For convenience, we introduce the vector
R^{1},R^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
to rewrite this integral,

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(k)}=\int_{L_{1}}^{\infty}\int_{L_{2}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\dots\int_{L_{n}}^{\infty}e^{-R_{1}^{1}\sum_{l=1}^{n-k+1}x_{l}-R_{2}^{1}x_{n-k+2}-\dots-R_{k}^{1}x_{n}}dx_{n}\dots dx_{2}dx_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{L_{1}}^{\infty}\int_{L_{2}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\cdots\int^{\infty}_{L_{n-1}}e^{-R_{1}^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}x_{l}-R_{2}^{2}x_{n-k+1}-\dots-R_{k-1}^{2}x_{n-2}-R_{k}^{2}x_{n-1}}dx_{n-1}\dots dx_{2}dx_{1}

Here, the last term
x_{n-k}
in the
\sum
-summation has been taken out separately to compensate for the missing integrated
x_{n}
.
Let the integral obtained after
j
integrations be denoted
I_{j}^{(k)}
, for example,

\displaystyle I_{1}^{(k)}=

\displaystyle\int_{L_{n}}^{\infty}e^{-R_{1}^{1}(\sum_{t=1}^{n-k}x_{t}+x_{n-k+1})-R_{2}^{1}x_{n-k+2}-\dots-R_{k}^{1}x_{n}}dx_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R_{k}^{1}}e^{-R_{1}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{n-k}x_{t}-R_{2}^{2}x_{n-k+1}-\dots-R_{k-1}^{2}x_{n-2}-R_{k}^{2}x_{n-1}}.

where
L_{n}
is given by formula (
3
) and

\left\{\begin{aligned} R_{1}^{2}&=R_{1}^{1}+(k-1)R_{k}^{1},\\
R^{2}_{2}&=R_{1}^{1}+(k-2)R_{k}^{1},\\
R_{3}^{2}&=R_{2}^{1}+(k-3)R_{k}^{1},\\
\cdots\\
R^{2}_{k}&=R_{k-1}^{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Now extend the relationship between
R^{1}
and
R^{2}
to the general case, i.e., the properties that
R^{l}
possesses after
l
integrations. By induction,

\displaystyle I_{l}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{l-1}\frac{1}{R^{t}_{k}}\int_{L_{n-l+1}}^{\infty}e^{-R_{1}^{l}(\sum_{t=1}^{n-l-k+1}x_{t}+x_{n-l-k+2})-R_{2}^{l}x_{n-l-k+3}-\dots-R_{k}^{l}x_{n-l+1}}dx_{n-l+1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{l}\frac{1}{R_{k}^{t}}e^{-R_{1}^{l+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n-l-k+1}x_{t}-R_{2}^{l+1}x_{n-l-k+2}-\dots-R_{k-1}^{l+1}x_{n-l-1}-R_{k}^{l+1}x_{n-l}}.

In the
\Sigma
-summation term of the second integrand, a factor is extracted outside, ensuring that the exponent always contains
k
terms. Then

\displaystyle R_{1}^{l+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n-l-k+1}x_{t}

\displaystyle+R_{2}^{l+1}x_{n-l-k+2}+\dots+R_{k-1}^{l+1}x_{n-l-1}+R_{k}^{l+1}x_{n-l}

\displaystyle=R^{l}_{k}\times L_{n-l+1}+R_{1}^{l}(\sum_{t=1}^{n-l-k+1}x_{t}+x_{n-l-k+2})+R_{2}^{l}x_{n-l-k+3}+\dots+R_{k-1}^{l}x_{n-l}.

By observing the relationship between the expression of
L_{n-l+1}
and the exponential term, we can obtain that
R^{l}
satisfies the following recurrence relation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} R_{1}^{l+1}&=R_{1}^{l}+(k-1)R_{k}^{l},\\
R_{2}^{l+1}&=R_{1}^{l}+(k-2)R_{k}^{l},\\
R_{3}^{l+1}&=R_{2}^{l}+(k-3)R_{k}^{l},\\
\cdots\\
R_{k}^{l+1}&=R_{k-1}^{l}.\end{aligned}\right.

The transformation matrix
A_{k}
we obtained here is identical to the matrix in (
1
).

Denote
R^{l}=(R_{1}^{l},R_{2}^{l},\dots,R_{k}^{l})^{T}
, we have

R^{l+1}=A_{k}R^{l}=A^{l}_{k}R^{1}.

This relationship is the same as the condition stated in the lemma
2
. After
n-k
integrations we are left with

\displaystyle I_{n-k}^{(k)}=\prod_{l=1}^{n-k}\frac{1}{R^{k}}e^{{-R_{1}^{n-k+1}x_{1}-R_{2}^{n-k+1}x_{2}-\dots-R_{k-1}^{n-k+1}x_{k-1}-R_{k}^{n-k+1}x_{k}}.}

Then, by integrating successively with respect to
x_{n-k},x_{n-1},\dots x_{2}
and
x_{1}
, we obtain

P_{n}^{(k)}=\prod_{l=1}^{n-k}\frac{1}{R^{l}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{R^{n-k+1}_{i}}.

Note that in the second product, the terms involving
R^{n-k+1}_{k}
and
R^{n-k+1}_{k-1}=R^{n-k+2}_{k}
can be incorporated into the first product. Applying the lemma
2
, we complete the proof.
∎

Now we prove the lemma.

Proof.

R^{l}=A^{l-1}_{k}R^{1}
tell us that
R^{l}=A_{k}R^{l-1}
, that is to say

\left\{\begin{aligned} R_{1}^{l}&=R_{1}^{l-1}+(k-1)R_{k}^{l-1},\\
R_{2}^{l}&=R_{1}^{l-1}+(k-2)R_{k}^{l-1},\\
R_{3}^{l}&=R_{2}^{l-1}+(k-3)R_{k}^{l-1},\\
\cdots\\
R_{k}^{l}&=R_{k-1}^{l-1}.\end{aligned}\right.

then we can get

\displaystyle R^{l+1}_{k}

\displaystyle=R^{l}_{k-1}

\displaystyle=R_{k-2}^{l-1}+R_{k}^{l-1}

\displaystyle=R^{l-2}_{k-3}+2R^{l-2}_{k}+R_{k}^{l-1}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=R_{1}^{l-k+2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}iR_{k}^{l-i}.

other hand, from the first two formulas, we can get

R_{2}^{l}-R_{1}^{l}=-R_{k}^{l-1},

which means

R_{1}^{l}=R_{k}^{l-1}+R_{2}^{l}.

Then we get

\displaystyle R_{k}^{l+1}

\displaystyle=R_{1}^{l-k+2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}iR_{k}^{l-i}

\displaystyle=R_{k}^{l+1-k}+R_{2}^{l-k+2}+(k-2)R^{l-k+2}_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-3}iR_{k}^{l-i}

\displaystyle=R_{k}^{l+1-k}+R_{k}^{l-k+2}+R_{3}^{l-k+3}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-3}iR_{k}^{l-i}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}R_{k}^{l+1-i}.

Let
R^{-k+2}=(1,0,0,\dots,0)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then

R^{t+2-k}=A^{t}_{k}R^{2-k}=(1,1,\dots,1,0,0,\dots,0)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{k},t<{k-1}.

the elements from the first to the
t+1
-th position of the vector are all
1
, and the remaining elements are
0
. and

R^{1}=A_{k}^{k-1}R^{2-k}.

A_{k}
is invertible, which show that
R_{k}^{-k+2}=\cdots=R_{k}^{0}=0
. Then this proves that
R_{k}^{l}

(l=1,2,\dots)
is precisely the
k
-step Fibonacci-type recurrence by definition. That’s to say,
R_{k}^{l}=F_{k}^{l}

(l=1,2,\dots)
.

Now we use
R_{k}^{m}

(m=1,2,\dots)
to present
R_{i}^{l}
. Come back to the relationship,

\left\{\begin{aligned} R_{1}^{l}&=R_{1}^{l-1}+(k-1)R_{k}^{l-1},\\
R_{2}^{l}&=R_{1}^{l-1}+(k-2)R_{k}^{l-1},\\
R_{3}^{l}&=R_{2}^{l-1}+(k-3)R_{k}^{l-1},\\
\cdots\\
R_{k}^{l}&=R_{k-1}^{l-1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Firstly, we get

R_{k-1}^{l}=R_{k}^{l+1}=F_{k}^{l+1},

and
R_{k-2}^{l}=R_{k-1}^{l+1}-R_{k}^{l}=R_{k}^{l+2}-R_{k}^{l}
, similarly

R_{k-3}^{l}=R^{l+1}_{k-2}-2R_{k}^{l}=R_{k}^{l+3}-R^{l+1}_{k}-2R_{k}^{l},

and

R_{k-4}^{l}=R_{k-3}^{l+1}-3R_{k}^{l}=R^{l+4}_{k}-3R_{k}^{l}-2R_{k}^{l+1}-R^{l+2}_{k}.

Since

R_{i}^{l}=R_{i+1}^{l+1}-(k-i-1)R_{k}^{l},i\leq k-1.

By induction, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{i}^{l}

\displaystyle=R^{l+k-i}_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-i-1}(k-i-j)R_{k}^{l+j-1}

\displaystyle=F^{l+k-i}_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-i-1}(k-i-j)F_{k}^{l+j-1},i\leq k-2.

∎

Conclusion

(k+1)
