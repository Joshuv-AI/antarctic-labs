The investigation on tensile failure of active waveguide structure by acoustic emission measurements
Authors: Zhihui Wu, Yongxin Yu, Cong Zhao, Lingjun Zhang, Yunlong Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Based on the principle of active waveguide monitoring for rock slope, the physical test was designed, and the acoustic emission (AE) characteristics of the active waveguide structure were studied. The results show that the signal distribution range in the parameter correlation diagram expands from the low-amplitude to the high-amplitude region with tensile load increase. The AE amplitude rises from 35 to 55 dB to 60–85 dB, accompanied by a continuous increase in AE energy. Subsequently, continuous signals appear in the frequency bands of 50–150 kHz, accounting for 15% of the total AE events. When the tensile stress is near the peak stage, the AE signal increases sharply, the signal amplitude is distributed in the range of 40-90dB, and a large number of high-frequency signals appear in the range of 300–400 kHz, accounting for 32% of the total effective AE events. The energy amplitude level of the high-frequency band is in the range of 30%-35%. Through a series of tensile load (0.1-1.0kN/s) tests, it is found that the statistical AE counts are sensitive to the loading rate, which can effectively distinguish the tensile stage and cracking evolution trend of the active waveguide structure. The active waveguide structure can monitor the internal deformation and failure characteristics of the rock slope, so as to provide some reference for the early warning.

Introduction

The slope geological disasters are increasing in recent years, which seriously threaten the property safety of the people. Therefore, the early warning of slope instability is particularly important. The early warning requires effective monitoring of slope instability1–3, and then provides a reliable reference for slope disaster prevention. Therefore, the research on slope monitoring technology has considerable economic and social benefits. In the process of slope deformation and failure, the rock mass cracks and releases energy, and the signal wave propagates in the rock slope. The characteristics of AE signals have important reference indexes for slope monitoring and early warning. Existing research has verified that multiple acoustic emission parameters can effectively reflect the internal damage evolution of rock masses. For instance, hit count and cumulative energy are adopted to identify crack initiation and propagation in rock slopes4. Amplitude and frequency characteristics are analyzed to distinguish different fracture modes5. Meanwhile, signal duration and peak frequency are applied to evaluate the stability and early-warning threshold of geotechnical engineering6,7. Therefore, these AE parameters provide important reference indices for slope monitoring and early warning.

Because the AE signals are easy to attenuate in rock slopes and are greatly affected by environmental factors, it is not conducive to the slope stability analysis. Some scholars have proposed using waveguide technology to monitor slope stability and reflect slope deformation characteristics by the evolution law of AE signals. Subsequently, active waveguide technology has gradually received extensive attention in the process of slope monitoring. This technology can monitor the deformation characteristics of rock and soil mass inside the slope. By the AE signal generated by the active waveguide, the deformation state and stress evolution characteristics of the slope can be effectively identified8–10. Some scholars have carried out some research on slope stability based on waveguide monitoring technology, from a single sensor monitoring to a multi-sensor monitoring system. Dixon applies acoustic emission to quantify the deformation behavior and systematically analyses the relationship between AE signals and landslide movement characteristics, providing an effective method for landslide activity assessment11. Japanese researchers have proposed the use of waveguide rods buried in the slope to achieve the purpose of continuous monitoring of slope deformation12. In the 1990 s, some researchers adopted steel rods as a waveguide structure to monitor the deformation of slopes13–16. In addition, some scholars buried the waveguide rod in the soil slope and installed AE sensors on the top of the waveguide rod to monitor the slope stability17. With the application of the waveguide rod, some scholars have improved the waveguide monitoring device, using the active waveguide monitoring device in the field monitoring test18. Dae-Sung Cheon and Yo-Seph Byun19,20 studied the evolution of AE parameters during slope deformation based on an improved acoustic emission monitoring device.

There is almost no obvious deformation before the instability failure of the rock slope, and macroscopic deformation and instability failure often occur in a very short time, resulting in slope disasters often showing significant suddenness, concealment and catastrophic consequences. In fact, in the early stage of rock slope failure, there will be many microcracks in the rock mass. The instability process of the rock slope is the cumulative process of internal rock mass rupture11,21,22. The AE monitoring method can identify the micro-deformation and fracture evolution process inside the rock mass, and detect the acoustic emission phenomenon accompanying the fracture process of the rock mass. It is the basis of the early warning research. Some researchers investigate the acoustic signals generated by the rock fracture process18,23, which is a theoretical foundation for the development of monitoring and early warning technology. There are a few studies on the application of active waveguide technology in rock slope monitoring. Rock slopes generally exhibit no obvious deformation before instability and failure. Macroscopic deformation and collapse usually occur within a very short time. During the instability of unstable rock masses, the mortar part of the waveguide structure is also subjected to tensile stress from the surrounding rock mass, resulting in corresponding cracking, deformation and failure, which in turn generates high-intensity acoustic emission signals. The acoustic emission characteristics are closely related to the evolution of microcracking damage inside the mortar of the waveguide structure, enabling the AE behavior of the waveguide structure to be linked with the unstable state of the unstable rock mass. It is necessary to further study the AE characteristics of active waveguide structures in the deformation process of rock slopes, and provide a reference for early warning13,24–26.

Recent advances in acoustic emission monitoring have greatly improved the accuracy and reliability of damage detection in engineering materials and structures. Advanced signal processing and machine learning methods have been successfully applied in AE monitoring to enhance the extraction of damage features and realize intelligent fault diagnosis in complex structural systems27. Meanwhile, significant progress has also been made in the quantitative characterization of dynamic damage evolution using AE technology, with new models proposed to describe the relationship between AE signals and the mechanical responses of materials under external loading28. These studies demonstrate the strong capability of AE techniques in capturing subtle damage information and provide important methodological support for the research conducted in this work29,30.

Based on the monitoring principle of the active waveguide structure of the rock slope, this paper uses mortar as the filling material of the active waveguide structure, and carries out the acoustic emission monitoring test of tensile loading failure. The evolution law of AE characteristic parameters, parameter correlation and time-frequency characteristics in the fracture process of active waveguide structure is studied. The precursor law of acoustic emission is studied, and the correlation between statistical AE parameters and load rate is further explored.

Monitoring principle of active waveguide structure for rock slope

In the process of instability evolution of dangerous rock mass, the crack propagation will occur in the rock mass under tensile stress. With the continuous cracking of the rock mass, a dangerous rock mass is produced. In the monitoring process of dangerous rock mass by active waveguide structure, it is appropriate to use the mortar to couple the waveguide rod with the surrounding rock mass to monitor the deformation and failure process of the slope. The monitoring method is shown in Fig. 1. This study realizes real-time and high-sensitivity monitoring of tensile damage in active waveguide structures by means of acoustic emission technology. It establishes quantitative AE thresholds for different failure stages and reveals the correlation between AE characteristics and structural instability. The proposed method has strong engineering applicability and provides a reliable technical approach for early warning of rock slope instability. Furthermore, this monitoring technique can be integrated with wireless communication technologies to form a local monitoring network, demonstrating strong practical applicability.

Physical experiment design

The tensile loading tests were carried out to monitor the AE signals during the fracture process of the active waveguide structure. The test system was mainly composed of a loading system and an AE monitoring system, as shown in Fig. 3.

Fig. 3Test system.

Test system.

Loading system

The WDAJ-600 testing machine is used. The testing machine adopts intelligent control technology, which can realize loading tests under different conditions, and can measure the mechanical parameters such as tensile and compressive strength. The test system is mainly composed of a control computer, a hydraulic system, multi-channel static strain system.

AE monitoring system

The acoustic emission system is a PCI-28-channel device, which can collect the AE count, energy, amplitude, frequency domain, and other information during the fracture process of the active waveguide structure, and can collect the AE signal waveform. The system uses a PCI board for noise reduction, which can minimize the impact of noise in the signal acquisition process. The system can display and monitor the characteristics of AE parameters in real time, and has the characteristics of multi-channel calculation optimization. The sampling frequency of acoustic emission in the test process is 1 MHz, and the threshold value is set to 40dB. The acoustic emission signals were collected using a Nano-30 high-sensitivity piezoelectric sensor. This sensor has a frequency range of 50–750 kHz with a peak sensitivity of approximately. It is widely used in rock and material fracture tests due to its high sensitivity to high-frequency microcracking signals, making it suitable for monitoring the tensile damage process of the waveguide structure. The sensor is installed at the end of the waveguide rod to accurately collect the AE signal during the loading process. In order to better receive the AE signal, the contact surface between the sensor and the waveguide rod is coated with a coupling agent.

Active waveguide structure

The active waveguide structures are used to carry out experimental research. The cement mortar with a cement-sand ratio of 1:1 is used. The size of the cylindrical mortar specimen is 260 mm in length and 80 mm in diameter. The geometric dimensions of the specimen were determined based on the actual length and diameter of field anchor bolts used in rock slope engineering, ensuring the test model is consistent with real-world engineering conditions and can truly reflect the mechanical response and acoustic emission characteristics of the active waveguide structure in practical applications. The waveguide rod is made of rebar with a diameter of 18 mm and a length of 400 mm. It is installed in the longitudinal center of the mortar specimen. The completed specimen is shown in Fig. 4.

Results and analysis

Analysis of failure characteristics of active waveguide structure

The AE characteristics of active waveguide structure under tensile load are studied through experiments, which can provide a reference for analyzing the instability evolution state of slope rock mass. The loading rates of the test were set to 0.10 kN/s, 0.25 kN/s, 0.50 kN/s, 0.75 kN/s and 1.00 kN/s, respectively, to simulate the different expansion states of the slope rock mass. Under the tensile loading, the final failure mode of the active waveguide structure is shown in Fig. 6.

Fig. 6Failure mode of the active waveguide structure (a) initial loading stage (b) stable development stage (c) near the fracture stage.

Failure mode of the active waveguide structure (a) initial loading stage (b) stable development stage (c) near the fracture stage.

Figure 6 shows tensile failure modes of the active waveguide structure at different deformation stages: (a) In the initial loading stage, a small amount of cracks began to form inside the specimen, resulting in a small amount of AE signals; (b) In the stable development stage, the internal cracks continue to expand, the AE signal is gradually active, and the cumulative AE energy begins to show an obvious upward trend; (c) Near the fracture stage, the cracks are in the unstable expansion stage, and more and more internal micro-fractures are gradually formed, forming macroscopic cracks. Finally, the AE counts reach their peak.

AE characteristics during the tensile failure test

Based on the tensile failure test of the active waveguide structure, the AE parameters, parameter correlation and time-frequency evolution characteristic are studied, and the correlation between AE statistical counts and tensile load rate is explored. Quantitative thresholds and statistical characterization are adopted to improve the reliability of stage identification.

Analysis of AE parameters

Fig. 7AE parameters characteristics (a) Distribution of AE count; (b) Distribution of energy (mv×ms); (c) Distribution of amplitude (dB); (d) Distribution of frequency domain (kHz).

AE parameters characteristics (a) Distribution of AE count; (b) Distribution of energy (mv×ms); (c) Distribution of amplitude (dB); (d) Distribution of frequency domain (kHz).

As shown in Fig. 7, the tensile fracture process of the specimen can be divided into three stages: the initial loading stage, the stable development stage and the fracture stage. From the diagram, it can be seen that in the initial loading stage, a small number of micro-cracks are initiated inside the specimen, the AE count is small, the cumulative energy curve rises slowly, the signal amplitude is distributed in 40 ~ 70 dB, and the frequency domain is distributed in a band near 50 kHz and 150 kHz. With the gradual increase of tensile stress, it enters a stable development stage. The crack propagation begins to intensify inside the specimen, the AE count increases, and the cumulative energy curve begins to show a clear upward trend. The amplitude distribution is in the range of 40 ~ 85 dB. It is concentrated in the range of 40–60 dB. At this stage, a small number of high-frequency signals begin to appear.

Prior to testing, the acoustic emission system was properly calibrated with standard parameters suitable for cement-sand mortar and steel rod composite waveguide structures. The AE threshold was set to 40 dB to suppress background noise interference. The peak definition time (PDT) was determined as 200 µs, the hit definition time (HDT) as 800 µs, and the hit locking time (HLT) as 1000 µs. These timing parameters were optimized to accurately identify AE signals generated from matrix cracking and interfacial debonding, avoid false or repeated hits, and ensure stable and consistent data collection during the entire loading process.

As the specimen approaches the fracture stage, a large number of AE signals are generated, and the cumulative energy curve rises sharply. The amplitude is distributed in 40–90 dB, and a large number of high-frequency signals appear, mainly concentrated in the range of 300–400 kHz. With the continuous expansion of the internal cracks in the specimen, the specimen breaks instantaneously, and the stress level almost drops vertically. Subsequently, the specimen was subjected to tensile failure, forming obvious tensile cracks, and the AE count decreased significantly. The above results show that the AE characteristics of the active waveguide structure are related to the structure fracture stage. The AE monitoring can provide a reference for judging the fracture evolution stage of the active waveguide structure.

Correlation analysis of statistical AE counts and loading rate

Based on a series of tensile loading rates, the variation characteristics of statistical AE counts during the test process are analyzed.

Fig. 12Characteristics of statistical AE counts under series tensile loading rates.

Characteristics of statistical AE counts under series tensile loading rates.

As shown in Fig. 12, in the initial loading stage and the stable development stage, the fluctuation range of statistical AE counts is small, and remains at the same level basically. Near the peak state, the microcracks begin to expand, and the AE events are extremely active. The statistical AE counts begin to increase significantly and show a sharp rise. Under the condition of 0.10 kN/s low loading rate, the peak value of AE statistical count is about 110,000, the peak time is about 730 s, and the duration time of the test process is the longest, indicating that the material damage evolves slowly under low loading rate. When the loading rate is increased to 0.25 kN/s, the peak value of AE statistical count is about 200,000, the peak time is advanced to 170 s, the signal intensity and release rate are significantly improved, and the damage development process is accelerated. When the loading rate further increases to 0.50 kN/s, the peak value of AE statistical count rises to 300,000, and the rising section of the curve is steeper, indicating that the crack propagation is more rapid at a high loading rate. When the loading rate continues to increase to 1.0 kN/s, the peak value of AE statistical count is about 350 000, and the peak time is further shortened to 50 s. The faster loading rate leads to brittle fracture of the sample. The regression fitting of the AE count-time series is carried out, and the fitting degree of each working condition is greater than 0.90. The measured data under different loading rates are in good agreement with the fitting curves, which indicates that the fitting method can better describe the evolution characteristics of AE statistical count under different loading rates, and provides a reliable basis for quantitative analysis of the influence of loading rate on failure characteristics.

The correlation between statistical AE counts and loading rates. (a) The relationship between statistical AE counts and loading rates. (b) The correlation between the statistical AE counts and the loading rates during the fracture stage.

As shown in Fig. 13a, the statistical AE counts under different loading rates remain at a low level in the early stage, and begin to increase sharply near the fracture stage, which is significantly higher than the previous statistical values. Figure 13b reflects the correlation between the AE statistical count and the loading rate during the fracture stage. It can be seen that the statistical AE counts and the loading rates have a typical positive correlation trend. By analyzing the evolution trend of statistical AE counts under different loading rates, it can be used to judge the possible tensile stress state of the active waveguide structure. The statistical AE counts in the fracture stage can reflect the influence of loading rate on the fracture behavior of the waveguide structure, which can be used as the characteristic parameter to predict the fracture process, and provide important support for monitoring and early warning based on acoustic emission technology.

Discussion

At present, there are many methods and monitoring approaches for slope stability analysis. The mechanical methods rely on a large number of geotechnical parameters, which makes it difficult to realize real-time dynamic monitoring and early warning. Displacement monitoring technologies such as GNSS, GPS, and remote sensing can monitor surface deformation, but they usually fail to identify microcrack activities before macroscopic deformation. In contrast, the active waveguide structure shows significant advantages in monitoring rock slope collapse, which can track the progressive failure process and internal damage evolution of rock slopes. When the rock slope tends to collapse, the waveguide structure is subjected to tension loading and produces continuous AE signals during the whole process of microcrack initiation, stable propagation, and unstable fracture. Compared with displacement monitoring, it can capture early precursor information before macroscopic deformation and improve the timeliness of early warning. Therefore, the active waveguide monitoring technology can make up for the deficiencies of existing monitoring methods, and provide a reliable early warning.

Acoustic emission has been used as an effective monitoring technique for capturing microcracks in geotechnical materials. Typical AE parameters, including hit count, cumulative energy, amplitude and duration, have been adopted to identify fracture evolution modes9,10. Nevertheless, conventional AE monitoring for slopes has the disadvantage of severe signal attenuation and environmental interference, which restricts warning reliability and field applicability. To overcome these limitations, waveguide-based AE monitoring has been developed and gradually applied to slope monitoring. With improved device integration, active waveguide systems have been used in field monitoring. Previous research mainly concentrates on the AE signals induced by soil slope deformation, focusing on the empirical relationship between AE activity and displacement rate11,13,16,19. The slope stability analysis methods are complicated and require extensive soil parameters and field monitoring data. In recent years, machine learning methods have been increasingly adopted to establish slope stability assessment models, providing significant guidance for slope hazard monitoring26,27. However, investigations focusing on tensile failure during rock slope collapse remain quite limited. The rock slope collapse is dominated by tensile stress near the main fracture surface, and the tensile fracture mechanism and the AE characteristics of active waveguide structures are still unclear. There is relatively little research content regarding the evolution of amplitude distributions, parameter correlation patterns, and high-frequency precursor features during progressive tensile failure. The study reveals the AE evolution characteristics of the active waveguide structures under tension loading, and explores the mechanical correlation between waveguide tensile damage and rock slope instability. Furthermore, the wavelet transform is used to analyze the time-frequency characteristics of the AE signal, and the energy percentage of each frequency band component can be calculated, so as to accurately analyze the frequency band energy properties under the different loading stages. It constructs a multi-parameter comprehensive classification criterion integrating AE count, amplitude, frequency, energy, and parameter correlations, supporting reliable field identification of rock slope failure.

The AE precursor indicators can provide some reference for field monitoring of active waveguide systems. When the proportion of 300–400 kHz high-frequency signals exceeds 32% of total effective AE events, and the high-frequency energy ratio reaches 30%−35%, the active waveguide structure is approaching tensile fracture, indicating a high risk of slope instability. The relationship between loading rate and statistical AE counts allows a certain estimation of rock slope collapse state, and a sharp increase in AE counts indicates accelerating crack propagation, which can be used to determine that the rock slope is in an unstable state. The abundant high-frequency AE signals generated in the tensile failure stage of cement mortar are probably governed by multiple internal microscopic damage. As a typical brittle multiphase composite material, massive microcracks continuously initiate and expand under tensile loading. In the tensile fracture stage, severe tensile stress induces widespread debonding at the interface transition zone between aggregate and cement paste. These small-scale and rapid fracture behaviors continuously generate high-frequency elastic waves, thereby concentrating the occurrence of high-frequency AE signals. With the sharp increase in microscopic fracture events, such dynamic fracture behaviors further enrich the high-frequency components of stress waves in the later failure stage.

These AE indicators are suitable for long-term monitoring of the high-risk rock slopes. The study adopts idealized tensile loading tests, while real rock slopes are subjected to complex geological conditions and environmental disturbances, such as rainfall and seismic loading. Further research is required to apply this method to a field monitoring strategy for early warning. In addition, integrating AE parameters with machine learning classification will improve the intelligent recognition of rock slope failure stages and the prediction accuracy of instability probability.

Conclusions

Based on the acoustic emission monitoring principle of active waveguide structure for the rock slope, a series of physical experiments was carried out. The main conclusions are as follows :

At the initial stage, the AE amplitude is concentrated in the range of 40 ~ 50dB. The signals are mainly concentrated near the 50 ~ 150 kHz frequency bands, accounting for 15% of the total effective AE events. And the signal energy is concentrated in the low-frequency domain. The signal points in the parameter correlation diagram are concentrated in a relatively small range in the initial stage. As the tensile stress gradually increases, the AE signal count, energy, and amplitude gradually increase, and some high-frequency signals begin to appear.
