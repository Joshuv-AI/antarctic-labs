Origin of Edge Currents in Chiral Active Liquids
Faisal Alsallom, David T. Limmer

Abstract
Chiral active liquids exhibit unidirectional edge currents when confined to simple geometries, but the origin of this phenomenon has defied explanation. Starting from the microscopic equations of motion of a simple two-dimensional model, we find that localized edge currents emerge as a consequence of global angular momentum conservation in dense systems. From these underlying equations, we derive an Ohmic-like conductance law for the mean edge current in the dense phase, and we find it to be intensive, depending only on the density, active torque and substrate drag. For simple geometries, we find the distribution of the edge currents has a closed Gaussian form, with a variance that is intensive, depending only on temperature, density and the aspect ratio of the system. These results are validated numerically using extensive molecular dynamics simulations. These results provide a new perspective for studying the collective phenomena in active matter through the global balance of conserved quantities.

Here we demonstrate that localized edge currents have a simple physical origin– the global balance of angular momentum. We derive effective hydrodynamic equations that explain the localization length of the current as a balance of dissipation through the substrate friction and internal shear viscosity. However, the amplitude of the current emerges as a consequence of the efficient conversion of locally injected spin into orbital angular momentum and requires a particle framework to describe. Starting from microscopic equations of motion for chiral active dimers, we derive a relationship between the mean edge current and microscopic system parameters, leading to a chiral edge current conductance law analogous to Ohm’s law. This conductance law is expected to be general for interparticle interactions that conserve angular momentum. Additionally, for the model we consider, we can solve for the full statistics of the edge currents in simple geometries. These statistics are equilibrium-like, with activity shifting only the mean of the distribution.

The angular momentum of this system can be stored as either spin or orbital angular momenta. In the dilute limit, where collisions between dimers are rare, approximately all of the injected angular momentum is stored in the spin degrees of freedom and dissipated through them, with a spin steady-state average value of
\expectationvalue{S}/A\approx m\tau_{a}/\gamma
. However, as the system’s density increases, collisions between the dimers act to transfer spin angular momentum into orbital angular momentum. Numerically, we find that
\expectationvalue{L}/\expectationvalue{S}\sim\mathcal{O}(100)
in dense systems
\rho\sigma^{2}/m\gtrsim 0.5
, see the supplementary materials. In the limit of high density, the dimers’ spin becomes frustrated and unable to build up, transferring approximately all of the injected angular momentum into orbital angular momentum. In the incompressible limit, the steady-state orbital angular momentum can be written as

\expectationvalue{L}=\rho\int\textbf{r}\times\textbf{v}(\textbf{r})\;dA,

where
\textbf{v}(\textbf{r})

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{L}

\displaystyle\approx\rho\int\textbf{r}_{\text{e}}(l)\times\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{t}v(b)\;dl\;db,

\expectationvalue{I}=\rho\int v(b)\;db.

(9)

Therefore, in steady state, the mean current in a dense system is directly proportional to the total mean angular momentum density

\expectationvalue{I}=\frac{\expectationvalue{j}}{2}=\frac{\rho}{4\gamma}\tau_{a}.

(10)

This result is akin to Ohm’s law as derived through the Drude model

Equation

3
(a) and (b) show the mean edge current measured in simulations across a large range of parameters, showing good agreement with our predictions. The relation relies on global angular momentum balance and the dense-limit spin frustration, not on the specific form of the active torque, suggesting these results extend to a broad class of chiral active systems. This result is accurate as long as the localization assumption is valid, since the first-order correction is proportional to
(\kappa_{\gamma}a)^{-1}

a

\sigma\ll\kappa_{\gamma}^{-1}

The fluctuations in the edge current are related to those of the total angular momentum, but not equivalent as in the case of the mean value. Unlike the mean, which is localized at the boundary, fluctuations in the angular momentum are distributed throughout the bulk, so the approximation connecting the mean current to the mean angular momentum is not valid for their fluctuations. Starting from microscopic definitions of edge currents in these two geometries, we can derive governing stochastic equations for the edge currents, as detailed in the supplementary materials. In this letter, we elucidated the physical origin of edge currents in chiral active liquids by revealing a direct relationship between the global injection of angular momentum and its dissipation through the spontaneous edge currents. This approach resulted in an unambiguous connection between the mean edge current and activity, expressed as an Ohm-like conductance law. This simple result reveals an unusual physical picture, where a locally applied driving force leads to an intensive global current in the form of a linear response that is valid arbitrarily far from equilibrium. Moreover, we showed that the statistics of the total angular momentum are closely related in character to those of the edge current. In both cases, we saw that the nonequilibrium steady-state distribution is a shifted equilibrium distribution. Arriving at these seemingly simple results starting from the fluctuating hydrodynamic equations would be a formidable task. We validated our predictions using molecular dynamics simulations of large systems of chiral active dimers. Our work paves the way for using the balance of globally conserved quantities as a starting point in analyzing collective behavior in systems driven far from equilibrium.
