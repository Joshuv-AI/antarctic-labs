Carsten Carstensen & Ngoc Tien Tran

Abstract
. The heart of the a priori and a posteriori error control in convex minimization problems is the sharp control of the differences of discrete and exact minimal energy. Conforming finite element discretizations for p-Laplace type minimization problems provide upper bounds of the energy difference with optimal convergence rates. Even for smooth solutions, known convergence rates for higher-order non-conforming finite element discretizations for the same problem class with 2 < p < ∞ 2<p<\infty , however, are exclusively suboptimal. Thus the popular a posteriori error control within the two-energy principle, that generalize hyper-circle identities, appears unbalanced. The innovative point of departure in a refined analysis of two discontinuous Galerkin (dG) schemes exploits duality relations between a discrete primal and a semi-discrete dual problem. The infinite-dimensional dual problem leads to a tiny duality gap that even vanishes for polynomial low-order terms. For a class of degenerated convex minimization problems with two-sided p p growth, the novel duality provides improved a priori convergence rates for the error in the minimal energies. This closes the misfit of convergence rates for the conforming and nonconforming schemes at least for the local discontinuous Galerkin schemes at hand. The motivating two-energy principle and some post-processing for a Raviart-Thomas dual variable provides an a posteriori error control, that also may drive adaptive mesh-refining. Computational benchmarks provide striking numerical evidence for improved convergence rates of the adaptive beyond uniform mesh-refining.

1.
Introduction

This paper develops novel techniques to establish duality relations for higher-order nonconforming methods for convex minimization problems with application to the error analysis of local discontinuous Galerkin methods (LDG).

1.1.
Model problem

Given an open bounded polyhedral Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the continuous problem minimizes the energy

(1.1)

\displaystyle E(v)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}(W(\nabla v)+\psi(\bullet,v))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{among }v\in V\coloneqq W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{D}}(\Omega)

with a convex energy density
W\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and with
a measurable function
\psi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, convex
in the second variable: At a.e.
x\in\Omega
let

\psi(x,\bullet):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a proper lower semi-continuous convex function.
Underlying non-displayed growth conditions lead to

1<p<\infty
and the test space

V=W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{D}}(\Omega)
of Sobolev functions in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with homogeneous boundary data on a compact part
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\subset\partial\Omega
of the boundary
\partial\Omega
with
positive surface measure.

The convexity in the functions inside (
1.1
) gives rise to a dual energy:
Let
W^{*}
(resp.
\psi^{*}(x,\bullet)
) denote the convex conjugate of
W
(resp.
\psi(x,\bullet)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
) and
the Hölder conjugate

1<p^{\prime}\coloneqq p/(p-1)<\infty
of
p
,
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
.
The dual problem of (
1.1
) maximizes the dual energy

(1.2)

\displaystyle E^{*}(\tau)\coloneqq-\int_{\Omega}(W^{*}(\tau)+\psi^{*}(\bullet,\mathrm{div}\tau))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{among }\tau\in\Sigma\coloneqq W^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega).

The space
\Sigma
consists
of vector fields

\tau\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)^{n}
with a distributional divergence
\mathrm{div}\,\tau
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)

and normal traces
\tau\cdot\nu=0
, that vanish along the
(relativly open and possibly empty) Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\coloneqq\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
. It is a Banach space
under the graph norm as, e.g., in the Hilbert case
W^{2}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)=H_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
.
Throughout this paper, the subsequent general conditions

(A1)

-\infty<\inf E(V)\leq E(v_{0})<\infty
for some
v_{0}\in V
and
E
is continuous at
v_{0}
,

(A2)

E(v)\to\infty
as
\|\nabla v\|_{p}\to\infty
for
v\in V

first imply existence of solutions

and
\sigma\in\arg\max E^{*}(\Sigma)
), as well as, second, no duality gap

[
32
, Chapter 3, Theorem 4.2]
viz.

(1.3)

\displaystyle E(u)=\min E(V)=\max E^{*}(\Sigma)=E^{*}(\sigma).

1.2.
Motivation

The a priori error analysis of conforming finite element methods (FEM)
is well understood in the literature
[
37
,
20
,
19
]
.
We illustrate the main results on the a priori convergence rates from a class
of problems with

in the subsequent assumptions.

(B1)

(smoothness of

)

(B2)

(B3)

\displaystyle d(a,b)\coloneqq\frac{|\mathrm{D}W(a)-\mathrm{D}W(b)|^{2}}{1+|a|^{p-2}+|b|^{p-2}}\leq c_{5}\left(W(b)-W(a)-\mathrm{D}W(a)\cdot(b-a)\right).

(B4)

(linear low-order term)
\psi(x,a)\coloneqq-f(x)a

The assumptions (B1)–(B3) define a class of degenerate convex minimization problems:
The dual variable

is uniquely defined, i.e.,

belongs to

]

Examples include an optimal design problem
[

[

]

The condition (B4) allows for explicit error control, but the analysis of this paper extends to other right-hand sides as well.

Given a discrete minimizer

The main motivation to apply nonconforming methods comes from a direct

1.3.
Contributions of this paper

This paper establishes the analog of (
1.4
) for
local discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) methods
[
22
,
12
,
25
]
, viz.

(1.5)

\displaystyle E_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u)-\min E_{h}(V_{h})+|E(u)-\min E_{h}(V_{h})|=O(h_{\max}^{2k}),

with novel strong duality relations on the discrete level by employing continuous objects in the dual ansatz space.
This leads in the a priori error analysis to

\displaystyle 0\leq E_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u)-\min E_{h}(V_{h})\leq E_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u)-E(u)+\gamma_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}^{*}\sigma)/r^{\prime}.

Here and in (

),

denotes a dicrete version of (
1.1
),

is the discrete ansatz space,
and
\mathrm{I}_{h}
and
\mathrm{I}_{h}^{*}
are interpolation operators.
Up to the stabilization
\gamma_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}^{*}\sigma)/r^{\prime}
on the dual level, the discrete energy of the interpolation of

is an
upper bound
for the exact energy. Under the assumptions (B1)-(B4) and

smoothness assumptions

and

, we provide quadratic convergence rates (

).
This enables

, Section 4]
, e.g.,

for

in strongly monotone problems.
This closes the theoretical gap between conforming and nonconforming discretizations.

Our results carry over to hybridizable methods with HDG/WG stabilizations
[
21
]
with suboptimal polynomial consistency
[
23
, Remark 2.9]
; however, it does not apply
for Lehrenfeld-Schöberl stabilization.

A conforming dual Raviart-Thomas finite element functions approximation
enables guaranteed energy error bounds. We suggest a post-processing by

for linear problems.
A localization of the resulting error estimator drives an

2.1.
Polytopal Mesh

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite collection of closed polytopes of positive volume with overlap of measure zero that covers
\overline{\Omega}=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}K
.
A face
S
of the mesh

is a closed connected subset of a hyperplane

with positive

-dimensional surface measure such that either (a) there exist
K_{+},K_{-}\in\mathcal{M}
with

(boundary face). We refer to
[
23
, Section 1.1]
for further details.

Let

be a finite collection of faces with overlap of

-dimensional surface measure zero that covers the skeleton

with the split

into the set of interior faces
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
and the set of boundary faces
\mathcal{F}(\partial\Omega)
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}\coloneqq\{S\in\mathcal{F}:S\subset\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\partial\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}
) denote the set of Dirichlet (resp. Neumann) faces. For
K\in\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{F}(K)
is the set of all faces of
K
.
The normal vector
\nu_{S}
of an interior face
S\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
is fixed in its orientation beforehand and set
\nu_{S}\coloneqq\nu|_{S}
for boundary faces
S\in\mathcal{F}(\partial\Omega)
.
For
S\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
,
K_{+}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
(resp.
K_{-}\in\mathcal{M}
) denotes the unique cell with

(resp.
S\subset\partial K_{-}
) and

(resp.
\nu_{K_{-}}|_{S}=-\nu_{S}

For

is the unique cell with

The jump

For theoretical purposes, let

The differential operators

2.3.
Modified local discontinuous Galerkin method

For any

,

The discrete gradient

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}v_{h}\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}v_{h}\,\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}\int_{S}\{v_{h}\}_{S}[\Phi]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}v_{h}\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}[v_{h}]_{S}\{\Phi\}_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Lemma 2.1

(discrete consistency error)
.

Any

satisfies

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}-\nabla_{h}v_{h}\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}h_{S}^{1-p}\|[v_{h}]_{S}\|_{L^{p}(S)}^{p}.

Proof.

Given any
v_{h}\in V_{h}
and
\Phi_{h}\in P_{k-1}(\mathcal{M})^{n}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}-\nabla_{h}v_{h})\cdot\Phi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\Big(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}h_{S}^{1-p}\|[v_{h}]_{S}\|_{L^{p}(S)}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\|\Phi_{h}\|_{p^{\prime}}.

Since
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\Phi\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|\Phi\|_{p^{\prime}}
for any

from stability of the

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}-\nabla_{h}v_{h}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\Phi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}-\nabla_{h}v_{h})\cdot\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x/\|\Phi\|_{p^{\prime}}.\qed

Let
\psi_{h}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

3.
Duality relations on discrete level

The main tools for the analysis of this paper are duality relations of the discrete problem (
2.3
) to a dual maximization problem with the ansatz space

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(\tau_{\mathcal{M}},\tau_{\mathcal{F}})\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)^{n}\times L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M}):\tau_{\mathcal{F}}|_{S}\equiv 0\text{ for all }S\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}\}.

Given any function
\tau\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)^{n}\cap\Sigma
,
we define the interpolation

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{h}^{*}\tau\coloneqq(\tau,(\tau\cdot\nu_{S})_{S\in\mathcal{F}})\in Y.

The divergence reconstruction
\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
of
\tau=(\tau_{\mathcal{M}},\tau_{\mathcal{F}})\in Y
is the unique solution to

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau\,\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}\tau_{S}[\phi]_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s

for any
\phi\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
.
The operator
\mathrm{div}_{h}
is consistent in the following sense.

Lemma 3.1

(consistency)
.

Any
\tau\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)^{n}\cap\Sigma
satisfies

\mathrm{div}_{h}\mathrm{I}_{h}^{*}\tau=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\mathrm{div}\,\tau
.

Proof.

The right-hand side of (
3.1
) is equal to
(\mathrm{div}\,\tau,\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for any
\phi\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
, which concludes the assertion.
∎

For any
\tau=(\tau_{\mathcal{M}},\tau_{\mathcal{F}})\in Y
, consider the following dual energy

(3.2)

\displaystyle E^{*}_{h}(\tau)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\int_{\Omega}(W^{*}(\tau_{\mathcal{M}})+\psi_{h}^{*}(\bullet,\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau))\,\mathrm{d}x-\gamma_{h}(\tau)/r^{\prime},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\gamma_{h}(\tau)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}h^{s/(r-1)}\|\tau_{S}-\{\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(S)}^{r^{\prime}}.

The following duality relation between the LDG method (
2.3
) and the dual problem of (
3.2
) holds. For strong duality, we assume the following condition.

(B5)

(polynomial low-order term)

\psi_{h}(x,\bullet)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
at a.e.
x\in\Omega
and
\partial_{u}\psi_{h}(\bullet,v_{h})\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
for any
v_{h}\in V_{h}=P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
.

Theorem 3.2

(duality of LDG)
.

It holds
\sup E_{h}^{*}(Y)\leq\min E_{h}(V_{h})
; (B1) and (B5) imply

\max E_{h}^{*}(Y)=\min E_{h}(V_{h})
.

Proof.

Given
\tau=(\tau_{\mathcal{M}},\tau_{\mathcal{F}})\in Y
and
v_{h}\in V_{h}
,
(
2.2
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}\cdot\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}[v_{h}]_{S}\{\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}
can be omitted in the integral
(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h},\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
this and the definition of the divergence reconstruction in (
3.1

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}[v_{h}]_{S}(\tau_{S}-\{\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S}\cdot\nu_{S})\,\mathrm{d}s.

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{h}y=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\partial_{u}\psi_{h}(\bullet,u_{h})=\partial_{u}\psi_{h}(\bullet,u_{h})

Theorem 4.2

(a priori)

Suppose (B1), (B4), (

\displaystyle E_{h}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\sigma\cdot(\nabla_{h}\mathrm{I}_{h}u-\nabla J_{h}\mathrm{I}_{h}u)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}f(J_{h}\mathrm{I}_{h}u-\mathrm{I}_{h}u)\,\mathrm{d}x+\gamma_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}^{*}\sigma)/r^{\prime}.

\displaystyle 0\leq E_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u)-\min E_{h}(V_{h})

\displaystyle=E_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u)-E(u)+\gamma_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}^{*}\sigma)/r^{\prime}.

under regularity assumptions based on the natural distance

Remark 4.12

(
1<p\leq 2
)
.

If

,
we assume (B3) with
d(a,b)\coloneqq|\mathrm{D}W(a)-\mathrm{D}W(b)|^{p^{\prime}}
, which implies (

) for the error quantity

\displaystyle\delta(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\|\mathrm{D}W(\alpha)-\mathrm{D}W(\beta)\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}.

The choice

in (

) and a Hölder inequality lead to

\displaystyle\|\sigma-\mathrm{D}W(\nabla_{h}\mathrm{I}_{h}u)\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|\nabla u-\nabla\mathrm{I}_{h}u\|_{p}^{p-1}.

Suppose that

and
\sigma\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)^{n}\cap W^{k,\max\{p^{\prime},r^{\prime}\}}(\mathcal{M})^{n}

4.2
concludes

\displaystyle|E(u)-\min E_{h}(V_{h})|+E_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u)-\min E_{h}(V_{h})\lesssim h_{\max}^{kp}

for

then any

From (

]
for hybridizable and
[

]
for conforming methods for strongly monotone problems.

A
\Sigma

For the sake of brevity, we assume that

Recall

Let

Theorem 4.13

(post-processing)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a regular triangulation of
\Omega
into simplices.
Suppose (B1) and
\psi_{h}(x,\bullet)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Then
\sigma_{\mathrm{RT}}\in\mathrm{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{M})\cap\Sigma
from (

) satisfies

\mathrm{div}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{RT}}=\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h}=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\partial_{u}\psi_{h}(\bullet,u_{h}).

Proof.

Integration by parts, (
4.17
), and (
3.1
) show, for any
\phi\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
, that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{RT}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{\mathrm{RT}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}[\phi]_{S}\sigma_{\mathrm{RT}}\cdot\nu_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}[\phi]_{S}\sigma_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h}=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\partial_{u}\psi_{h}(\bullet,u_{h})
by (
3.8
), this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.14

(a posteriori error control)
.

Suppose that the lower-order term
\psi
has the explicit representation (B4) and the assumptions of
Theorem

4.13
hold.
Furthermore, we assume that

is a piecewise polynomial.
Given a conforming postprocessing

, then the energy error

can be bounded by

\displaystyle E(v_{C})-E(u)\leq E(v_{C})-E^{*}(\sigma_{\mathrm{RT}}).

In the numerical examples below,

is obtained from the discrete minimizer

of

in

by nodal averaging in the conforming subspace

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta(\nabla u,\nabla v_{C})&\lesssim\int_{\Omega}(W(\nabla v_{C})-W(\nabla u)-\sigma\cdot\nabla(v_{C}-u))\,\mathrm{d}x\\

If

In this section, we briefly extend the analysis of
Section

4
to a hybridizable method using the techniques of
[

]
. For the sake of simplicity, we retain the notation of

2
on the discrete level.
Given
k\geq 1
, let

\displaystyle V_{h}\coloneqq P_{k}(\mathcal{M})\times P_{k}(\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}})

denote the discrete ansatz space. Given
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\mathcal{F}})\in V_{h}
, the discrete gradient
\nabla_{h}v_{h}\in P_{k-1}(\mathcal{M})^{n}
of
v_{h}
is the unique solution to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}v_{h}\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}v_{\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}\int_{S}v_{S}[\Phi\cdot\nu_{S}]\,\mathrm{d}s

for any
\Phi\in P_{k-1}(\mathcal{M})
,
where
v_{S}=v_{\mathcal{F}}|_{S}
abbreviates the restriction of
v_{\mathcal{F}}
along the side
S
. The discrete problem minimizes

(5.1)

\displaystyle E_{h}(v_{h})\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}(W(\nabla_{h}v_{h})+\psi_{h}(\bullet,v_{\mathcal{M}}))\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathrm{s}_{h}(v_{h})/r

among
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\mathcal{F}})\in V_{h}

with the stabilization

\displaystyle\mathrm{s}_{h}(v_{h})\coloneqq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}(K)}h_{S}^{-s}\int_{S}T_{K,S}v_{h}(v_{S}-v_{K})\,\mathrm{d}s

and
T_{K,S}v_{h}\coloneqq|v_{S}-v_{K}|^{r-2}(v_{S}-v_{K})
for any
K\in\mathcal{M}
,
S\in\mathcal{F}
.
The corresponding dual problem is (
3.2
),
but with the stabilization

\displaystyle\gamma_{h}(\tau)\coloneqq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}h^{s/(r-1)}\|\tau_{S}-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{K}\cdot\nu_{S}\|^{r^{\prime}}_{L^{r^{\prime}}(S)}

for any
\tau=(\tau_{\mathcal{M}},\tau_{\mathcal{F}})\in Y

instead of (
3.3
)
to reflect the hydridization of the ansatz space.
Here,
\tau_{K}=\tau_{\mathcal{M}}|_{K}
is the restriction of
\tau_{\mathcal{M}}
to
K
.

Remark 5.1

(suboptimal polynomial consistency)
.

For the Lehrenfeld-Schöberl stabilization, we can use the discrete ansatz space
P_{k}(\mathcal{M})\times P_{k-1}(\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}})
, reducing the computational cost of the method. However, the analysis of this section does not carry over because (
3.1
) forfeits to hold.

The following
Theorem

5.2
allows for the extension of
all results of
Section

4
to the hybrid method this section.

Theorem 5.2

(duality of hybridizable methods)
.

It holds
\sup E^{*}_{h}(Y)\leq\min E_{h}(V_{h})
;
(B1) and (B5) imply

\max E_{h}^{*}(Y)=\min E_{h}(V_{h})
.

Proof.

For any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\mathcal{F}})\in V_{h}
and
\tau=(\tau_{\mathcal{M}},\tau_{\mathcal{F}})\in Y
, the proof departs from the integration by parts formula

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}v_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}\int_{S}(v_{S}-\{v_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S})\cdot[\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nu_{S}]_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s

(5.2)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}[v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot(\tau_{S}-\{\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nu_{S}\}_{S})\,\mathrm{d}s

This follows from arguments similar to
[
46
, Lemma 3.2]
.
Furthermore, the final two sums on the right-hand side can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}(K)}(\nu_{S}\cdot\nu_{K})\int_{S}(v_{S}-v_{K})\cdot(\tau_{S}-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\tau_{K}\cdot\nu_{S})\,\mathrm{d}s,

cf.
[
46
, Proof of Theorem 3.1]
. This, (
5
), the Hölder inequality, and
\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h}\leq W(\nabla_{h}v_{h})+W^{*}(\tau_{\mathcal{M}})
as well as
\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau\,v_{h}\leq\psi_{h}(\bullet,v_{h})+\psi_{h}^{*}(\bullet,\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau_{h})
a.e. in
\Omega
conclude
\sup E^{*}_{h}(Y)\leq\min E_{h}(V_{h})
.

Assuming the differentiability of
W
and
\psi_{h}
, we can define the stress variable
y=(\sigma_{\mathcal{M}},\sigma_{\mathcal{F}})\in Y
as

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}&\coloneqq\mathrm{D}W(\nabla_{h}u_{h}),\\
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}|_{S}&\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}+h_{S}^{-s}(T_{K_{+},S}u_{h}-T_{K_{-},S}u_{h})/2&\mbox{if }S\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega),\\
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nu_{S}+h_{S}^{-s}T_{K_{+},S}u_{h}&\mbox{if }S\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}.\end{cases}\end{split}

The computations in
[
46
, Corollary 5.1 and Lemma 5.2]
carry over and show that

(5.4)

\displaystyle[\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\nu_{S}]_{S}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M},S\in\mathcal{F}(K)}h_{S}^{-s}T_{K,S}u_{h}\quad\text{for any }S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{h}y

\displaystyle=\partial_{u}\psi_{h}(\bullet,u_{\mathcal{M}}).

Thus, the discrete Euler-Lagrange equations and (
5
) imply

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{h}y\,u_{\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{u}\psi_{h}(\bullet,u_{\mathcal{M}})u_{\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{d}x

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{h}u_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h}).

An explicit computation with the definition of
y
in (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) show, for any
K\in\mathcal{M}
and
S\in\mathcal{F}(K)
, that

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}-\Pi_{K}^{k-1}\sigma_{K}\cdot\nu_{S}

\displaystyle=-(\nu_{S}\cdot\nu_{K})[\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}/2+h_{S}^{-s}(T_{K_{+},S}u_{h}-T_{K_{-},S}u_{h})/2

\displaystyle=(\nu_{S}\cdot\nu_{K})h^{-s}T_{K,S}u_{h}.

for interior sides
S\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}-\Pi_{K}^{k-1}\sigma_{K}\cdot\nu_{S}=h_{S}^{-s}T_{K,S}u_{h}

for boundary sides
S\in\mathcal{F}(\partial\Omega)
.
This and
s/(r-1)-sr^{\prime}=-s
imply

(5.6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{h}(y)=\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h}).

From (
5
)–(
5.6
),
\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nabla_{h}u_{h}=W(\nabla_{h}u_{h})+W^{*}(\sigma_{\mathcal{M}})
, and
\mathrm{div}_{h}y\,u_{\mathcal{M}}=\psi_{h}(\bullet,u_{\mathcal{M}})+\psi_{h}^{*}(\bullet,\mathrm{div}_{h}y)
a.e. in
\Omega
, we conclude
E_{h}^{*}(y)=E_{h}(u_{h})
and thus,
\max E_{h}^{*}(Y)=\min E_{h}(V_{h})
.
∎
