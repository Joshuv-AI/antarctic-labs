Series for 1 / π 1/\pi arising from Cauchy product
Roman Le Lan

Abstract
. In this note, we evaluate a series for 1 / π 1/\pi conjectured by Sun. Our proof uses the Cauchy product and hypergeometric transformations. From this result, we derive two additional analogous series involving polynomials of degree 3 3 . Further identities can be proved using our method; these are presented in a table at the end of the note.

2.
Preliminary results

To prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, we need several intermediate results. The first lemma establishes a useful transformation.

Lemma 1
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. Then

(2.1)

108^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{-1/3}{k}\binom{-1/6}{k}\binom{-1/3}{n-k}\binom{-1/6}{n-k}=\binom{2n}{n}^{2}\binom{3n}{n}

Proof.

It is easy to see that (
2.1
) holds for
n=0
and
n=1
.

Let
u_{n}
denote the left-hand side of (
2.1
). Using Zeilberger’s algorithm on
u_{n}
, we obtain the recurrence relation

(n+1)^{3}u_{n+1}-6(3n+2)(2n+1)(3n+1)u_{n}=0.

Moreover, one can easily verify that
\binom{2n}{n}^{2}\binom{3n}{n}
also satisfies this recurrence, which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We can now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|z|<1
and let
P
be a function defined for all
z
by

(3.1)

P(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{-1/3}{k}\binom{-2/3}{n-k}\binom{-1/6}{k}\binom{-5/6}{n-k}.

By the Cauchy product of two series, we obtain a new expression for
P
.

P(z)=\Biggr(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}\binom{-1/3}{k}\binom{-1/6}{k}\Biggl)\Biggl(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}\binom{-2/3}{k}\binom{-5/6}{k}\Biggr)

The series
P
can thus be expressed as a product of two hypergeometric functions
{}_{2}F_{1}
. Using Euler’s transformation, we obtain

\displaystyle P(z)

\displaystyle={}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{6};1;z\biggr){}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(\frac{2}{3},\frac{5}{6};1;z\biggr)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{6};1;z\biggr)^{2}.

Then, upon applying another Cauchy product, it follows that

P(z)=(1-z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{-1/3}{k}\binom{-1/6}{k}\binom{-1/3}{n-k}\binom{-1/6}{n-k}.

Using identity (
2.1
) from Lemma 1, this becomes

(3.2)

P(z)=(1-z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{z}{108}\biggr)^{n}\binom{2n}{n}^{2}\binom{3n}{n}.

Let us apply the operator

-1+3z\frac{d}{dz}\Bigg|_{z=\frac{1}{2}}

to both sides of (
3.1
) to obtain the relation

\frac{3}{2}P^{\prime}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)-P\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{3n-1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{-1/3}{k}\binom{-2/3}{n-k}\binom{-1/6}{k}\binom{-5/6}{n-k},

where the
′
stands for the derivative with respect to
z
. Hence, using equation (
3.2
) in the above relation, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{3n-1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}

\displaystyle\binom{-1/3}{k}\binom{-2/3}{n-k}\binom{-1/6}{k}\binom{-5/6}{n-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{216^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}^{2}\binom{3n}{n}+3\sqrt{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n}{216^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}^{2}\binom{3n}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(6k+1)\frac{\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)_{k}\bigl(\frac{1}{3}\bigr)_{k}\bigl(\frac{2}{3}\bigr)_{k}}{2^{k}k!^{3}}.

Identity (
2.2
) from Lemma 2 completes the proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{3n-1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{-1/3}{k}\binom{-2/3}{n-k}\binom{-1/6}{k}\binom{-5/6}{n-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\times\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\sqrt{6}}{2\pi}

Theorem
1.1
is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Set

a_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{-1/3}{k}\binom{-2/3}{n-k}\binom{-1/6}{k}\binom{-5/6}{n-k}.

Using Zeilberger’s algorithm on
a_{n}
, we obtain the following recurrence relation.

144(n+2)^{3}a_{n+2}-4(2n+3)(18n^{2}+54n+47)a_{n+1}+(n+1)(6n+5)(6n+7)a_{n}=0

Hence,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)(6n+5)(6n+7)a_{n}-4\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2n+3)(18n^{2}+54n+47)a_{n+1}

\displaystyle\quad+144\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+2)^{3}a_{n+2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)(6k+5)(6k+7)a_{k}-4\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2k+1)(18k^{2}+18k+11)a_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+144\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}k^{3}a_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigl((k+1)(6k+5)(6k+7)-4(2k+1)(18k^{2}+18k+11)+144k^{3}\bigr)a_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+44a_{0}-144a_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(36k^{3}-108k^{2}-53k-9)a_{k}.

By subtracting or adding this to (
1.1
), we obtain the identities (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) as desired. Theorem
1.3
is proved.
∎
