1.0 Summary
 The Fluorescent Particle Vertical Profiles at CoURAGE (FPVPC) campaign was designed to characterize
 the vertical distribution of bioaerosol-relevant particles at the urban-rural interface sampled during the
 U.S. Department of Energy Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility’s
 Coast-Urban-Rural Atmospheric Gradient Experiment (CoURAGE). Bioaerosols, including bacteria,
 fungi, viruses, pollen, spores, and biological fragments, can affect cloud formation, disease transmission,
 allergen exposure, and biogeochemical cycling.[1] Although ground-level bioaerosol abundance and
 composition have been studied extensively, their vertical distributions remain poorly constrained across
 different ecosystems, source regions, and meteorological regimes. The CoURAGE deployment addresses
 this gap by pairing ARM tethered balloon system (TBS) [2] vertical profiling with in situ single-particle
 fluorescence measurements and offline single-particle chemical analysis.

 The scientific objectives were to obtain high-resolution vertical profiles of bioaerosol-relevant fluorescent
 particles and coarse-mode aerosol up to approximately 1-2 km, assess the role of diurnal, seasonal, and
 meteorological variability in controlling bioaerosol vertical structure, and provide observational
 constraints for improving parameterizations of bioaerosol vertical profiles in urban-rural environments.

2.0 Measurement Approach
 The campaign centered on deployment of a wideband integrated bioaerosol sensor (WIBS) [3] on the
 ARM TBS during the July, 2025 intensive operational period. The WIBS measures individual particle
 size, shape, and intrinsic fluorescence using multiple excitation and emission wavelength bands. These
 measurements provide real-time constraints on fluorescent biological aerosol proxies, including particles
 consistent with pollen, fungal spores, and bacteria, while also allowing comparison against the broader
 coarse-particle population.

 Offline sampling was planned using a Sandia four-stage cascade impactor to collect coarse-mode particles
 on gold-plated transmission electron microscopy (TEM) grids. These samples are intended for
 single-particle chemical analysis using optical photothermal infrared spectroscopy (O-PTIR) and Raman
 microspectroscopy at the Brookhaven National Laboratory Center for Functional Nanomaterials. This
 offline analysis will provide particle-level chemical and functional-group information needed to interpret
 WIBS fluorescence signals and distinguish biological particles from non-biological fluorescent or
 coarse-mode aerosol.

3.0 Preliminary Results

 Figure 1. TBS vertical profiles of WIBS coarse-mode particles at CoURAGE in July, 2025 presented
 as 2D histograms (color indicates number of observations). Left: total WIBS particle
 concentrations (> 500 nm). Right: fluorescent WIBS particle concentrations.

 Figure 1 shows vertically resolved total WIBS particle concentration and fluorescent particle
 concentration as a function of altitude. Total WIBS particle concentrations were observed through much
 of the sampled column, with coarse-mode particles extending from near the surface to approximately
 1 km, but low total concentrations throughout the column. Fluorescent particle concentrations were very
 low throughout most of the profile. This result indicates that while coarse-mode aerosol was present
 above the surface, only a small fraction of the coarse-particle population detected by WIBS was
 fluorescent.

 The presence of coarse-mode particles aloft does not by itself imply substantial vertically transported
 biological aerosol. Instead, the preliminary WIBS data suggest that aloft coarse-mode aerosol during this
 case was largely non-fluorescent, potentially reflecting dust, aged aerosol, haze droplets, or other
 non-biological particle types. Conversely, the near-surface fluorescent signal is more consistent with local
 biological or biogenic sources, although fluorescence remains a proxy and requires confirmation using
 offline composition and morphology measurements.

4.0 Synthesis and Next Analysis Steps
 The CoURAGE results provide an important contrast to ARM’s forested-site Bankhead National Forest
 (BNF, in Alabama) measurements where biological sources may be more directly coupled to the local
 boundary layer. At the CoURAGE urban-rural interface, coarse aerosol can be vertically distributed
 without a corresponding strong fluorescent-particle signal. This implies that source type, particle aging,
 meteorology, and transport history all affect whether bioaerosol-relevant particles are lofted and detected
 aloft.

 2

 The preliminary finding that very little aloft coarse-mode aerosol was fluorescent suggests that
 inlet-compatible fluorescence measurements can identify periods when coarse aerosol layers are not
 dominated by strongly fluorescent biological particles. It also highlights the need for offline
 single-particle analysis to determine whether non-fluorescent coarse particles are dust, smoke-processed
 material, sea-salt-like particles, anthropogenic material, or weakly fluorescent biological fragments.

5.0 Summary
 The FPVPC campaign used ARM TBS profiling during CoURAGE to examine fluorescent and total
 coarse-mode particle vertical distributions at an urban-rural interface. Preliminary WIBS results show that
 coarse-mode particles were detected aloft, but fluorescent particle concentrations were generally very low
 above the near-surface layer. These measurements indicate that aloft coarse aerosol during the observed
 case was not strongly fluorescent and therefore was not readily attributable to fluorescent biological
 aerosol based on WIBS alone. Ongoing offline O-PTIR and Raman microspectroscopy analyses of
 impactor samples will be needed to determine the chemical identity of the coarse particles and to evaluate
 whether weakly fluorescent or non-fluorescent biological material contributed to the observed aerosol
 population.
