Equivariant formality and the cohomology of subgroups of right-angled Coxeter groups
Steven Amelotte, Vladimir Gorchakov

Abstract
. We construct models for the classifying spaces of coabelian subgroups of right-angled Coxeter groups as homotopy orbit spaces of real moment-angle complexes, generalizing well-known models for the classifying space of a right-angled Coxeter group and its commutator subgroup. This identifies the cohomology of these groups with the Borel equivariant cohomology of elementary abelian 2 2 -group actions on cubical subcomplexes of a cube [ − 1 , 1 ] m [-1,1]^{m} . We then characterize equivariant formality for these actions, leading to a simple graph-theoretic criterion for when the cohomology of a coabelian subgroup is free as a module over the cohomology of the quotient by the commutator subgroup of the right-angled Coxeter group.

Introduction

Given a simple graph
\Gamma

[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}

W_{\Gamma}=\left\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\,:\,g_{i}^{2}=1\text{ for all }i\in[m]\text{ and }[g_{i},g_{j}]=1\text{ for }\{i,j\}\in E(\Gamma)\right\rangle.

Unlike the right-angled Artin group

m

T^{m}
by a result of Charney and Davis
[
8
]
,

contains torsion, and hence is not of finite cohomological dimension. It is, however, of finite virtual cohomological dimension (meaning it contains a finite-index subgroup of finite cohomological dimension), as witnessed by the commutator subgroup
[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]
, which admits a finite classifying space in the form of a subcomplex of the
m
-cube
[-1,1]^{m}
.

More precisely, classifying spaces of each of the groups above can be obtained as special cases of a common construction at the foundations of toric topology known as the polyhedral product functor. For each pair of spaces
(X,A)
and simplicial complex
K
on
m
vertices, this functor carves out a topological space

from the cartesian product

(see

). In particular, the
real moment-angle complex

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}=(D^{1},S^{0})^{K}
and the
real Davis–Januszkiewicz space

\mathbb{R}DJ_{K}=(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty},\,pt)^{K}
are well-known models for the classifying spaces of
[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]
and
W_{\Gamma}
, respectively, when
K
is the flag simplicial complex with underlying graph
K^{(1)}=\Gamma
(see
[
15
]
or
Theorem

2.1
below); here
D^{1}=[-1,1]
and
S^{0}=\partial D^{1}
.

By definition,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a cubical subcomplex of the cube
(D^{1})^{m}
, from which it inherits a canonical action of the elementary abelian
2
-group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
by coordinatewise reflection. The homotopy orbits (i.e., Borel construction) of this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
-space is homotopy equivalent to the real Davis–Januszkiewicz space:
(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}}=E\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\simeq\mathbb{R}DJ_{K}
. Thus, while the right-angled Coxeter group

does not admit a finite classifying space, it does admit one which is the homotopy quotient of a finite

-invariant subcomplex of
(D^{1})^{m}
. In this paper, we observe that the same is true of all coabelian subgroups
G

(

) and use this to study the cohomology of these groups as modules over the polynomial ring given by the cohomology of the quotient
G/[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]

Note that if

is a simplicial complex with underlying graph
\Gamma
, then by considering restricted actions of subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
on the real moment-angle complex

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{homotopy quotients of}\\
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\text{by subgroups of }\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\end{subarray}\Bigr\}\;

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\;\Bigl\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{coabelian subgroups}\\
\text{of the RACG }W_{\Gamma}\end{subarray}\Bigr\}

(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{A}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\quad\text{\small$\pi_{1}\bigl((\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{A}\bigr)$}

(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{G/[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]}

\displaystyle\longmapsfrom\quad\text{\small$G$}

where the right-to-left assignment can be identified with the classifying space functor when

can be viewed as a subgroup of

via the injection of fundamental groups induced by a natural fibre inclusion

Our main result is the following combinatorial classification of coabelian subgroups

for which

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a simple graph on the vertex set
[m]
, and let
G
be a coabelian subgroup of
W_{\Gamma}
. Let
I\subseteq[m]
be the minimal subset for which
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{I}
contains the subgroup
G/[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]
of
W_{\Gamma}/[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
(see

). Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\mathrm{H}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a free module over the polynomial ring
\mathrm{H}^{*}(G/[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}];\mathbb{F}_{2})
;

(b)

\mathrm{H}^{*}(G^{\rtimes};\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a free module over
\mathrm{H}^{*}(G^{\rtimes}/[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}];\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{i}:i\in I]

G^{\rtimes}=\overline{W}_{\Gamma_{I}}\rtimes[W_{\Gamma_{J}},W_{\Gamma_{J}}],\quad J=[m]\setminus I;

(c)

the induced subgraph

\{i,j_{1}\},\{i,j_{2}\}\in E(\Gamma)

of

.

Here

which can be identified with the Serre spectral sequence of the Borel fibration

2.
Classifying spaces of subgroups of RACGs via real moment-angle complexes

In this section we use a fundamental construction from toric topology to describe models for the classifying spaces of coabelian subgroups of right-angled Coxeter groups. Generalizing well-known models for the classifying spaces of right-angled Coxeter groups and their commutator subgroups, this identifies the group cohomology of these subgroups with the equivariant cohomology of certain elementary abelian
2
-group actions on cubical subcomplexes of a cube
[-1,1]^{m}
.

2.1.
Real moment-angle complexes

Let
K
be an abstract simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, and let
(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{A})=\left\{(X_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(X_{m},A_{m})\right\}
be a sequence of pairs of topological spaces
A_{i}\subseteq X_{i}
. For any
\sigma\subseteq[m]
, we write
(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{A})^{\sigma}
for the subspace of the cartesian product
\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}
given by

(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{A})^{\sigma}=\big\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}\,:\,x_{i}\in A_{i}\text{ for }i\notin\sigma\big\}.

The
polyhedral product

(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{A})^{K}
is then the subspace of
\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}
defined by the colimit

(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{A})^{K}=\operatorname*{colim}_{\sigma\in K}\,(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{A})^{\sigma}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in K}(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{A})^{\sigma}.

When
(X_{i},A_{i})=(X,A)
for all
i\in[m]
, we denote
(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{A})^{K}
simply by
(X,A)^{K}
.

Key examples of polyhedral products in toric topology include the
moment-angle complex

\mathcal{Z}_{K}=(D^{2},S^{1})^{K}
and the
Davis–Januszkiewicz space

DJ_{K}=(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},\,pt)^{K}
. (Here
D^{2}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leqslant 1\}
and
S^{1}=\partial D^{2}
.) These spaces fit in a homotopy fibration

\mathcal{Z}_{K}\longrightarrow DJ_{K}\longrightarrow BT^{m},

which can be identified up to homotopy with the Borel fibration of the natural
T^{m}=(S^{1})^{m}
-action on
\mathcal{Z}_{K}\subseteq(D^{2})^{m}
defined by coordinatewise multiplication; see
[
7
, Theorem 4.3.2]
.

Real analogues of these spaces are given by the
real moment-angle complex

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}=(D^{1},S^{0})^{K}
and the
real Davis–Januszkiewicz space

\mathbb{R}DJ_{K}=(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty},\,pt)^{K}
. (Here
D^{1}=[-1,1]
and
S^{0}=\partial D^{1}
.) By definition,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a cubical subcomplex of the
m
-dimensional cube
(D^{1})^{m}
, and the action of the elementary abelian
2
-group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
on the cube by coordinatewise reflection restricts to an action on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
. As above, these spaces fit in a homotopy fibration

(1)

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}DJ_{K}\longrightarrow B\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m},

which is a polyhedral product model for the Borel fibration of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
-action on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.

Thus for every simplicial complex
K
on
m
vertices, the real moment-angle complex
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is naturally a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
-space. Given any subgroup
A
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
, the
homotopy quotient
(or
homotopy orbit space
)
(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{A}
will always refer to the Borel construction

(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{A}\coloneqq EA\times_{A}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}

of the restricted action of
A
on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, which is the total space of the Borel fibration
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\to(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{A}\to BA
.

Since any subgroup
A
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
is itself an elementary abelian
2
-group, we have
A\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}
for some
r\leqslant m
, and the equivariant cohomology
H_{A}^{*}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{F}_{2})\coloneqq H^{*}((\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{A};\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a graded module over the polynomial ring
H^{*}(BA;\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
, with
|x_{i}|=1
, via the map induced by the projection
(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})_{A}\to BA
.

The following summarizes some well-known results on the fundamental group and asphericity of real moment-angle complexes and real Davis–Januszkiewicz spaces. Recall that a simplicial complex
K
is called a
flag complex
if every set of pairwise connected vertices spans a face of
K
. Equivalently,
K
is flag if it is the clique complex of the underlying graph
\Gamma=K^{(1)}
given by the
1
-skeleton of

.

Theorem 2.1

(
[

; see also
[

,

Let
K
be a simplicial complex with underlying graph

.

(a)

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}DJ_{K})\cong W_{\Gamma}
.

(b)

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})\cong[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]
.

(c)

\pi_{i}(\mathbb{R}DJ_{K})\cong\pi_{i}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})
for

(d)

Both spaces

We therefore have
\mathbb{R}DJ_{K}\simeq BW_{\Gamma}

For a subset

of a right-angled Coxeter group

K

3.
Equivariant formality for real moment-angle complexes

The goal of this section is to prove
Theorem

1.2
by establishing a real analogue of the following equivariant formality criterion for ordinary moment-angle complexes from
[
3
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
3
, Theorem C]
)
.

Let
K
be a flag complex on the vertex set
[m]
, and let
I\subseteq[m]
. Then the coordinate
T^{I}
-action on
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is equivariantly formal over a field
k
if and only if
I\in K
and
K_{\{i,j\}}\ast K_{I\setminus\{i,j\}}\subseteq K
for every missing edge
\{i,j\}\notin K
.

Rather than redoing the work of
[
3
]
in the context of elementary abelian
2
-group actions on real moment-angle complexes
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, instead of torus actions on
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, we identify the equivariant formality of coordinate subgroup actions on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
by describing their fixed points and applying the criteria of
Theorems

A.4
and
A.6
.

3.1.
Reduction to coordinate subgroup actions

To classify the subgroups
A\leqslant\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
that act equivariantly formally on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
for a given simplicial complex
K
, we first show that the problem can be reduced to the case of coordinate subgroups
A=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{I}
(see (
4
)).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A
be a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
. The
coordinate hull
of
A
, denoted
\operatorname{hull}(A)
, is the smallest coordinate subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
containing
A
. More precisely,

\operatorname{hull}(A)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{I}\quad\text{for}\quad I=\big\{i\in[m]\,:\,\mathrm{proj}_{i}(A)\neq\{1\}\big\},

where
\mathrm{proj}_{i}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the
i^{\text{th}}
coordinate projection.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]
, and let
A
be a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
. Then there is an equality of fixed points

(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{A}=(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{\operatorname{hull}(A)}.

Proof.

Clearly
(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{\operatorname{hull}(A)}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{A}
since
A\subseteq\operatorname{hull}(A)
. Conversely, suppose
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\subseteq(D^{1})^{m}
is fixed by every
a\in A
. Then
\mathrm{proj}_{i}(a)\cdot x_{i}=x_{i}
for all
i\in[m]
and
a\in A
, since the
A
-action on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a restriction of the coordinatewise
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
-action on
(D^{1})^{m}
. It follows that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
is fixed by each coordinate generator of
\operatorname{hull}(A)
, and hence lies in
(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{\operatorname{hull}(A)}
.
∎

The following is a real analogue of
[
3
, Proposition 5.6]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]
, and let
A
be a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
. Then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is an equivariantly formal
A
-space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
if and only if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is an equivariantly formal
\operatorname{hull}(A)
-space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{A}=(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{\operatorname{hull}(A)}
by
Lemma

3.3
, the proposition follows from the Betti sum criterion for equivariant formality in
Theorem

A.4
.
∎

3.2.
Detecting equivariant formality via fixed points

We recall that for a simplicial complex
K
on the vertex set
[m]
, the
link
of a face
\sigma\in K
is the subcomplex

\lk_{K}(\sigma)=\{\tau\in K\,:\,\tau\cap\sigma=\varnothing\text{ and }\tau\cup\sigma\in K\}

on the vertex set
[m]\setminus\sigma
.

Recall also that the ordinary moment-angle complex
\mathcal{Z}_{K}\subseteq(D^{2})^{m}
comes with a standard action of the
m
-torus
T^{m}
. The diagonal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action on the polydisc
(D^{2})^{m}
defined by complex conjugation also restricts to an involution on
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
I\subseteq[m]
. Then the fixed point set
(\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{T^{I}}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariantly homeomorphic to
\mathcal{Z}_{\lk_{K}(I)}
with respect to the complex conjugation action if
I\in K
, and is empty otherwise.

Proof.

First note that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in(D^{2})^{m}
is fixed by
T^{I}
if and only if
x_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
. Let
J=[m]\setminus I
. Then the fixed point set of the
T^{I}
-action on
(D^{2})^{m}
is
(D^{2},0)^{J}
. Hence,

(\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{T^{I}}=\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cap(D^{2},0)^{J}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in K}\big((D^{2},S^{1})^{\sigma}\cap(D^{2},0)^{J}\big).

The intersection
(D^{2},S^{1})^{\sigma}\cap(D^{2},0)^{J}
is nonempty if and only if
I\subseteq\sigma
. Therefore, the fixed point set
(\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{T^{I}}
is nonempty if and only if
I\in K
. For
I\in K
, we have

(6)

(\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{T^{I}}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in K,\,I\subseteq\sigma}\big((D^{2},S^{1})^{\sigma}\cap(D^{2},0)^{J}\big).

For
\sigma\in K
with
I\subseteq\sigma
, let
J^{\prime}=\sigma\setminus I
so that
\sigma=J^{\prime}\sqcup I
. Observe that
J^{\prime}\in\lk_{K}(I)
, and every face of
\lk_{K}(I)
arises in this way from some
\sigma\in K
containing
I
. We have

(D^{2},S^{1})^{\sigma}\cap(D^{2},0)^{J}=\prod_{i\in J^{\prime}}D^{2}\times\prod_{i\in I}\{0\}\times\prod_{i\notin\sigma}S^{1}\cong\prod_{i\in J^{\prime}}D^{2}\times\prod_{i\notin\sigma}S^{1},

and since (
6
) can be written as a union over all faces of the link
\lk_{K}(I)
, this gives the desired
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant homeomorphism
(\mathcal{Z}_{K})^{T^{I}}\cong\mathcal{Z}_{\lk_{K}(I)}
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]
, and let
I\subseteq[m]
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is an equivariantly formal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{I}
-space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
;

(b)

\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is an equivariantly formal
T^{I}
-space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
;

(c)

K_{J}\setminus(I\cap J)\hookrightarrow K_{J}
induces the trivial map on
\widetilde{H}^{*}(-;\mathbb{F}_{2})
for all

.

Moreover, if
K
is flag, then the conditions above are equivalent to:

(d)

I\in K
and
\{i,j_{1}\},\{i,j_{2}\}\in K
for every missing edge
\{j_{1},j_{2}\}\notin K
and every
i\in I\setminus\{j_{1},j_{2}\}
.

Proof.

The equivalence of conditions
(b)
and
(c)
is contained in
[
3
, Theorem B]
, and the equivalence of
(b)
and
(d)
when

is flag is

3.1
. (Note that since a flag complex is determined by its underlying graph,

is equivalent to the condition that

and

for every

.) It therefore suffices to prove the equivalence of

and
(b)
.

Consider the complex conjugation
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action on
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
. It is easily checked that

. Moreover, it follows from the well-known additive isomorphisms

that

. This, together with the fixed point criterion of

, implies that the

-action on

is equivariantly formal. On the other hand, by

, we have that

as

-spaces if

. So in this case the complex conjugation

-action on

is equivariantly formal by the same argument as before, and

implies that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is an equivariantly formal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{I}
-space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
if and only if
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is an equivariantly formal

-space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Finally, if

, then the actions by the coordinate subgroups

and

on

and

, respectively, have no fixed points. Therefore, neither action is equivariantly formal, making conditions

and

equivalent in this case as well.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

.

Conditions

and

of

\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
-modules

\mathrm{H}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\Biggl(\bigoplus_{J\subseteq[m]}\widetilde{H}^{*-1}(K_{J};\mathbb{F}_{2})\Biggr)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}],

where
K
is the flag complex of
\Gamma
, and
K_{J}
is the full subcomplex of
K
on the vertex set
J\subseteq[m]
.

Proof.

This follows from the collapse of the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of the short exact sequence
1\to[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]\to G\to G/[W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}]\to 1
(see also
Theorem

A.4
) and the Hochster decomposition
\mathrm{H}^{n}([W_{\Gamma},W_{\Gamma}];\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong H^{n}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\bigoplus_{J\subseteq[m]}\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(K_{J};\mathbb{F}_{2})
of
[
7
, Corollary 8.3.6]
.
∎

Appendix A
Betti number criteria for equivariant formality

Here we collect various general criteria for equivariant formality over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
(
Theorem

A.4
) and prove a general result (
Theorem

A.6
) on compatible group actions that allows us to relate the equivariant formality of certain torus and elementary abelian
2
-group (or “
2
-torus”) actions over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. The arguments are standard and follow
[
2
]
.

We use the same assumptions on topological spaces as in
[
1
]
: all spaces
X
are assumed to be compact, Hausdorff, second-countable, and locally contractible. Examples include topological manifolds, complex algebraic varieties, and countable, locally finite CW-complexes. Furthermore, we let
T=(S^{1})^{r}
and assume that
all stabilizers
T_{x}
are connected
for any
T
-space
X
.

Let
X
be a
T
-space and let
k
be a field. Let
R=H^{*}(BT;k)
and let
p^{*}\colon R\to H^{*}_{T}(X;k)
be the map induced by the projection map
p\colon X_{T}\to BT
. For each orbit
Tx
, we have
(Tx)_{T}\simeq BT_{x}
, and hence the inclusion
j_{x}\colon Tx\hookrightarrow X
induces a map
j_{x}^{*}\colon H^{*}_{T}(X;k)\to H^{*}(BT_{x};k)
. For each point
x\in X
, we thus have a map
j_{x}^{*}\circ p^{*}\colon R\to H^{*}(BT_{x};k)
.
For a multiplicative set
S\subseteq R
, define

X^{S}=\big\{x\in X\,:\,(j_{x}^{*}\circ p^{*})(s)\neq 0\text{ for all }s\in S\big\}.

With these conventions, we can now state the localization theorem.

Theorem A.1

(Localization Theorem
[
2
, Theorem 3.2.6]
)
.

Let
k=\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, where
p
is prime. Let
\phi^{*}\colon H^{*}_{T}(X;k)\to H_{T}^{*}(X^{S};k)
be the map induced by the inclusion
\phi\colon X^{S}\hookrightarrow X
. Then the induced localized homomorphism

S^{-1}\phi^{*}\colon S^{-1}H_{T}^{*}(X;k)\longrightarrow S^{-1}H_{T}^{*}(X^{S};k)

is an isomorphism of
S^{-1}R
-algebras.

The next lemma is the key point, where we use that all stabilizers are connected.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
S=R\setminus\{0\}
be the multiplicative set of
R
, and let
X^{T}
be the fixed points of the
T
-space
X
. Then
X^{S}=X^{T}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X^{T}
. Then
j_{x}^{*}\circ p^{*}=\mathrm{id}
and hence
x\in X^{S}
. Conversely, suppose
x\notin X^{T}
. Since all stabilizers are connected,
T_{x}
is a subtorus of
T
and
T_{x}\neq T
, hence
\rk T_{x}<\rk T
. Therefore,
\ker(j_{x}^{*}\circ p^{*})\neq 0
, but this implies
x\notin X^{S}
since
S=R\setminus\{0\}
.
∎

Proposition A.3
.

Let
X
be a
T
-space with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})<\infty
. Then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{p})\leqslant\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Moreover, the Serre spectral sequence
E_{2}^{i,j}=H^{i}(BT,H^{j}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p}))\Rightarrow H_{T}^{i+j}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
of the fibration
X\to X_{T}\to BT
degenerates at
E_{2}
if and only if
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{p})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Proof.

Let
R=H^{*}(BT;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Since the rows of
E^{*,*}_{r}
are
R
-modules and the differentials
d_{r}
,
r\geqslant 2
, are
R
-module homomorphisms, one can localize the spectral sequence with respect to
S=R\setminus\{0\}
. Let
K=R_{(0)}
be the field of fractions. The spectral sequence
\{S^{-1}E_{r}\}_{r\geqslant 2}
becomes a sequence of complexes of
vector spaces over
K
with
S^{-1}E_{2}\cong K\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Now we have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}(S^{-1}E_{2})\geqslant\dim_{K}(S^{-1}E_{3})\geqslant\cdots

\displaystyle\geqslant\dim_{K}(S^{-1}E_{\infty})=\dim_{K}\bigl(S^{-1}H_{T}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}\bigl(S^{-1}H_{T}^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{p})\bigr)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{p}),

where the equality
\dim_{K}\bigl(S^{-1}H_{T}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})\bigr)=\dim_{K}\bigl(S^{-1}H_{T}^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{p})\bigr)
follows from
Theorem

A.1
and
Lemma

A.2
. Therefore,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{p})\leqslant\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
with equality if and only if
E_{2}=E_{\infty}
.
∎

We next recall a general criterion for equivariant formality which generalizes the Smith–Thom inequality for spaces with an involution. For a
G
-space
X
, let
i^{*}\colon H^{*}_{G}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})
denote the forgetful map induced by the fibre inclusion of the Borel fibration
X\xrightarrow{i}X_{G}\to BG
.

Theorem A.4
.

Let
G=T^{r}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}
. Let
X
be a
G
-space with the same assumptions as in the beginning of this section.
Then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{G};\mathbb{F}_{2})\leqslant\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Moreover,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{G};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})
if and only if the following equivalent conditions hold:

(a)

i^{*}\colon H_{G}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})
is surjective;

(b)

H_{G}^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a free module over the polynomial ring
H^{*}(BG;\mathbb{F}_{2})
;

(c)

G
acts trivially on
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and the Serre spectral sequence with mod
2
coefficients associated to the fibration
X\to X_{G}\to BG
degenerates at the second page.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Theorem 3.10.4]
and
[
1
, Theorem 10.2]
for the
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}
case, and
[
1
, Theorem 8.3]
and
Proposition

A.3
for the
G=T^{r}
case.
∎

Definition A.5
.

A
G
-space
X
is called
equivariantly formal over
\mathbb{F}_{2}

if any of the equivalent conditions of Theorem
A.4
hold.

Suppose now that
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle\tau\rangle
acts on a
T
-space
X
and that this action is
compatible
with the torus action in the sense that
\tau(g\cdot x)=g^{-1}\cdot\tau(x)
for all
x\in X
and
g\in T
. Such actions arise in algebraic and symplectic geometry, where examples include toric varieties and symplectic toric manifolds with anti-symplectic involutions; see, e.g.,
[
4
]
. Under this compatibility assumption,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
acts on
X^{T}
, and the maximal elementary abelian
2
-subgroup
T_{2}
(consisting of all elements
g\in T
with
g^{2}=1
) acts on
X^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
. We can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem A.6
.

Assume that
X
and
X^{T}
are equivariantly formal as
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-spaces over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Then
X
is an equivariantly formal
T
-space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
if and only if
X^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
is an equivariantly formal
T_{2}
-space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-actions on
X
and
X^{T}
are equivariantly formal and
(X^{T})^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}=X^{T_{2}}
, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{T_{2}};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{2})
by
Theorem

A.4
. We therefore have

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{2})&\leqslant&\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})\\
\|&&\|\\
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{T_{2}};\mathbb{F}_{2})&\leqslant&\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}};\mathbb{F}_{2}).\end{array}

So
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{T_{2}};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}};\mathbb{F}_{2})
if and only if
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X^{T};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})
, which completes the proof, again by
Theorem

A.4
.
∎
