On the K-theory of matroids with Tutte coverings
Luigi Caputi, Sabino Di Trani

Abstract
. The aim of this work is to explicitly compute the K K -theory of the category of matroids with respect to the covering family of Tutte coverings. In particular, we show that this is equivalent to the K K -theory spectrum of the category of graphic matroids on looped forests, with the covering family generated by isomorphisms. Further, we show that this yields an equivalence of C 2 C_{2} -spectra.



Introduction

Matroids provide a combinatorial framework that unifies several notions of independence arising in algebra, geometry, and graph theory. The most common numerical and algebraic invariants of matroids satisfy recursive descriptions in terms of deletion and contraction operations. Tutte-Grothendieck invariants play a fundamental role in this regard, providing universal invariants compatible with the deletion-contraction relations. In fact, the Tutte polynomial is a central object in matroid theory, encoding several important specializations, including the chromatic polynomial, the flow polynomial, and the characteristic polynomial (see
[
Whi92
, Chapter 6]
for a complete exposition).

In recent years, understanding polynomial invariants related to the Tutte polynomial from a categorical and homotopical perspective has gained increasing attention. An example is given by the process of categorification of the Tutte polynomial of a graph, that describes such a polynomial as a graded Euler characteristic
[
JHR06
]
. Among others, there are also categorifications of the chromatic polynomial
[
HGR05
]
, of the dichromatic polynomial
[
Sto08
]
, and of the signed chromatic polynomial of graphs
[
CLWZ22
]
. Related ideas appear in recent approaches to categorify classical matroid invariants, such as the characteristic polynomial
[
SY24
]
. Another direction is to use homotopic theoretic methods. The framework of categories with covering families, introduced in
[
BGM+24
]
, has provided a natural setting for defining a
K
-theory spectrum associated to combinatorial structures as graphs, posets or matroids. Recent developments in this direction include the work
[
CG25
]
on the edge reconstruction conjecture in graph theory, and the study of categorical realizations of Tutte-type recursions
[
Lop25
]
, which both inspired this work.

The deletion–contraction recursion satisfied by the Tutte polynomial suggests a natural covering family structure on the category of matroids. Roughly speaking, a matroid
M
can be covered by the pair of minors
M\setminus e
and
M/e
associated to a non-degenerate element
e
. Iterating this process produces a deletion–contraction tree whose leaves correspond to matroids obtained by successive deletions and contractions. Following
[
Lop25
]
, these trees define the
Tutte coverings
of a matroid and determine a covering family structure. This yields a category with covering families, denoted
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
.
The goal of this paper is to compute the
K
-theory spectrum associated to this category. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1

(cf. Corollary
4.8
)
.

There is an equivalence of spectra

K(\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}})\simeq\bigvee_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{m})\wedge\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{n})\ ,

where
S_{p}
denotes the symmetric group on
p
elements.

The key observation is that every matroid admits a Tutte covering whose domains are
indecomposable
matroids (see Definition
3.3
). Such matroids have a particularly simple structure: they are precisely the direct sums of loops and coloops, and can be identified with graphic matroids associated to looped forests
[
Lop25
]
.
Using this description, we show that the category of matroids with Tutte coverings
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
has refinements (see Proposition
3.7
). This allows us to apply the Dévissage theorem for categories with covering families of
[
CG25
]
. As a consequence, the computation of the
K
-theory of
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
can be reduced to the computation of the
K
-theory of the full subcategory of indecomposable matroids, with respect to the covering family given by isomorphisms of matroids. This gives Theorem
1
.

In view of Theorem
1
, the recursive combinatorics underlying the Tutte polynomial investigated in
[
Lop25
]
translates into a homotopy-theoretic description of the associated
K
-theory spectrum. We further show that the equivalence on
K
-theory is an equivalence of
C_{2}
-spectra (see Corollary
4.10
) and that this can be done also for graphic matroids, seen as a full category of
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
(see Theorem
4.4
).

1.
Matroids and their Algebraic Invariants

In this sectionwe recall some basic facts of matroids needed in the follow-up. We refer to
[
Oxl11
]
and
[
Whi86
]
for complete references.

Recall taht a (finite)
matroid

M
is a pair
(X,\mathcal{I})
, where
X
is a (finite) set and
\mathcal{I}
is a family of independent sets for
X
, i.e. a family of subsets of
X
that satisfies the independence axioms:

(I1)

the empty set is in
\mathcal{I}
;

(I2)

if
A\in\mathcal{I}
and
B\subset A
, then
B\in\mathcal{I}
;

(I3)

if
A,B\in\mathcal{I}
and
|A|>|B|
then there exists
x\in A
such that
B\cup\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}
.

The set
X
is the
ground set
of
M
. A maximal independent set is called a
basis
.

Given a matroid
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
, its
rank function

r_{M}\colon\mathcal{P}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
is the function which associates to each
S\subseteq X
the size of a maximal independent set contained in
S
.

Example 1.1

(Uniform Matroids)
.

Let
X
be a finite set with
n
elements and
k\leq n
a non negative integer. The family

\mathcal{I}=\{I\subset X\,|\,|I|\leq k\}
satisfies the axioms of a family of indipendents. The matroid
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
is the uniform matroid of subsets of cardinality
k
in a set with
n
elements. It is denoted by
U_{k,n}
.

Example 1.2

(Graphic Matroids)
.

Let
G
be a finite undirected graph, and
\mathcal{C}
the set of its (undirected) cycles. Identifying a subgraph of
G
with the set of its edges, the set

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}=\{S\subseteq E(G)\mid C\nsubseteq S,\text{ for each }C\in\mathcal{C}\}\

satisfies the independence axioms, where
E(G)
denotes the set of edges of
G
,.
The pair
M_{G}=(E(G),\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}})
is the graphic matroid associated to
G
.

Let
M_{1}=(X_{1},\mathcal{I}_{1})
and
M_{2}=(X_{2},\mathcal{I}_{2})
be two matroids. A matroid morphism
\varphi\colon M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
is an injective map
\varphi\colon X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
such that
\varphi(I)\in\mathcal{I}_{2}
for every
I\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
. Matroids and morphisms of matroids yield a category that we denote by
\mathbf{Mat}
.
The matroids
M_{1}=(X_{1},\mathcal{I}_{1})
and
M_{2}=(X_{2},\mathcal{I}_{2})
are isomorphic, and in such case we write
M_{1}\cong M_{2}
, if there exists a bijective map
f\colon X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
such that
f^{-1}(I)\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
if and only if
I\in\mathcal{I}_{2}
.

Definition 1.3

(Direct Sum Matroid)
.

Let
M_{1}=(X_{1},\mathcal{I}_{1})
and
M_{2}=(X_{2},\mathcal{I}_{2})
be matroids. The direct sum
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
is the matroid having ground set
X_{1}\sqcup X_{2}
and
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}\mid I_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{1},I_{2}\in\mathcal{I}_{2}\}
as family of independent sets.

Let
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
be a matroid and
B
the set of its bases.
Set

B^{*}=\{X\setminus J\,|\,J\in B\}\ ,

I^{*}=\{A\in X\,|\,A\subset J\mbox{ for certain }J\in B^{*}\}\ .

Observe that the set
I^{*}
satisfies the axioms of family of indipendent sets for a matroid.

Definition 1.4

(Dual Matroid)
.

Let
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
be a matroid. The matroid
M^{*}=(X,I^{*})
is the
dual matroid
of
M
.

We will use the following terminology;

Definition 1.5
.

Let
M=(X,I)
be a finite matroid and
e\in X
.

•

e
is a
loop
if
\{e\}
is not an independent set;

•

e
is a
coloop
(or an isthmus) of
M
if
e
is contained in every independent set;

•

e
is
non-degenerate
if it is not a loop nor a coloop for
M
.

There are two operations on matroids of importance in the follow-up: deletion and contraction. We now recall the definitions.
Consider a matroid
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
and
T
any subset of
X
.

Definition 1.6

(Deletion Matroid)
.

The deletion of
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
with respect to
T
is the matroid
M\setminus T=(X^{\prime},\mathcal{I^{\prime}})
defined by:

(DM1)

the ground set
X^{\prime}
is the set
X\setminus T
;

(DM2)

the independent set
\mathcal{I^{\prime}}
is the sets of
I\in\mathcal{I}
such that
I\subset X^{\prime}
.

Definition 1.7

(Contraction Matroid)
.

The matroid
M/T
is defined as the matroid
(M^{*}\setminus T)^{*}

Remark 1.8
.

If
\{x\}
is an independent for
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
it is equivalent to say that
M/x
is the matroid defined by the data:

(CM1)

the ground set
X
is the set
X\setminus\{x\}
;

(CM2)

the independent set
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is given by the sets
I\subset X\setminus\{x\}
such that
I\cup\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}
.

Otherwise, if
x
is a loop for
M
, then
M/x=(X\setminus\{x\},\mathcal{I})
.

We recall the following property of deletions and contractions – see, e.g.
[
Oxl11
, Proposition 3.1.25]
:

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
M=(X,I)
be a matroid and
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
disjoint subsets of
X
. Then:

(1)

(M\setminus T_{1})\setminus T_{2}=M\setminus(T_{1}\cup T_{2})=(M\setminus T_{2})\setminus T_{1}
;

(2)

(M/T_{1})/T_{2}=M/(T_{1}\cup T_{2})=(M/T_{2})/T_{1}
;

(3)

(M\setminus T_{1})/T_{2}=(M/T_{2})\setminus T_{1}
.

Let
M
be a matroid. Any matroid obtained from
M
via a sequence
of deletions and/or contractions is called a
minor
of
M
.
Observe that, in view of Proposition
1.9
the minors of a matroid are well defined objects.

Remark 1.10
.

Let
M=(X,\mathcal{I}(M))
be a matroid and
x\in X
. The inclusion of
X\setminus\{x\}
induces two maps,

M\setminus x\rightarrow M\ \qquad\text{and}\qquad M/x\rightarrow M\ ,

that are matroid morphisms. In fact, for the first map, by definition we have
I\in\mathcal{I}(M\setminus x)
if and only if
I
is an independent set for
M
contained in
X\setminus\{x\}
. The second map is obviously defined if
x
is a loop. If
x
is not a loop, we have
I\in\mathcal{I}(M/x)
if and only if
I\cup\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}(M)
; in particular
I\in\mathcal{I}(M)
. Furthermore, observe that if
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are disjoint subset of
X
, then the ground set inclusion induces a morphism from
M^{\prime}=M\setminus T_{1}/T_{2}
to
M
.

For the class of graphic matroids, we have the following:

Proposition 1.11

(
[
Oxl11
, 3.1.2 & 3.2.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
M_{G}
be the associated graph matroid. Let
T\subseteq E(G)
a subset of edges of
G
. Then:

(1)

M_{G}\setminus T=M_{G\setminus T}
;

(2)

M_{G}/T=M_{G/T}
.

where
G\setminus T
and
G/T
denotes the classical deletion and contraction operations on graphs.

By Proposition
1.11
, minors of graphic matroids are graphic matroids as well.

Let
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
be a matroid with rank function
r
.
The
corank
of a subset
A\subset X
is the difference
z(A)\coloneqq r(M)-r(A)
. The difference
n(A)\coloneqq|A|-r(A)
is called the
nullity
of
A
.
Using the corank and the nullity functions, we can now recall the definition of Tutte polynomial for a matroid. For more details we refer to
[
Whi92
, Chapter 6]
.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
M=(X,I)
be a finite matroid. Then, its Tutte polyonomial is defined as:

T_{M}(x,y)=\sum_{A\subseteq X}(x-1)^{z(A)}(y-1)^{n(A)}\ .

By convention, if
X=\emptyset
then
T_{M}(x,y)=1
.

We recall that the Tutte polynomial is well-behaved with respect to direct sums,

(1.1)

T_{M_{1}\oplus M_{2}}(x,y)=T_{M_{1}}(x,y)\,T_{M_{2}}(x,y)\ ,

and it can be recursively computed using deletions and contractions. These properties of the Tutte polynomial are essentially the main motivation for investigating the category of matroids with Tutte coverings introduced in
[
Lop25
]
, and that we are going to study in this work.

2.
K
-theory of categories with covering families

In this section we recall the notion of a category with covering families.
We follow
[
BGM+24
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
BGM+24
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a small category.

(1)

A
multi-morphism
in
\mathbf{C}
is an object
B\in\mathbf{C}
and a finite (possibly empty) family of maps in
\mathbf{C}
with target
B
:

\{f_{i}\colon A_{i}\to B\}_{i\in I}\ .

(2)

A
covering family structure
on
\mathbf{C}
is a collection of multi-morphisms, called the
covering families
,
such that:

(a)

each singleton containing an identity map
\{A=A\}
is a covering family;

(b)

given a covering family
\{g_{j}\colon B_{j}\to C\}_{j\in J}
and, for each
j\in J
a covering family
\{f_{ij}\colon A_{ij}\to B_{j}\}_{i\in I_{j}}
, then the collection of all composites
\{g_{j}\circ f_{ij}\colon A_{ij}\to C\}_{j\in J,i\in I_{j}}
forms a covering family.

(3)

A
category with covering families
is a small category
\mathbf{C}
, a covering family structure on
\mathbf{C}
, and a
distinguished basepoint object
\ast\in\mathbf{C}
, such that:

(i)

\Hom(\ast,\ast)=\{1_{\ast}\}
and
\Hom(c,\ast)=\emptyset
if
c\neq\ast
;

(ii)

For every finite (possibly empty) set
I
, the family
\{\ast\to\ast\}_{i\in I}
is a covering family.

A morphism of categories with covering families is a functor
F\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{D}
that preserves both the covering families and the basepoint; this means that
F(\ast_{C})=\ast_{D}
and
F
sends covering families of
\mathbf{C}
to covering families of
\mathbf{D}
. Categories with covering families and morphisms of categories with covering families yield a category
\mathbf{CatFam}
.

If
\mathbf{C}
is a small category without basepoint, we can always add one as
disjoint base point
. In fact, from
\mathbf{C}
we can form a new category
\mathbf{C}_{+}
by adding an object
\ast
to
\mathbf{C}
with the property that
\Hom(\ast,\ast)=\{1_{\ast}\}
and
\Hom(c,\ast)=\emptyset
if
c\neq\ast
. This new category
\mathbf{C}_{+}
becomes a category with covering families if all families of the form
\{\ast\to\ast\}_{i\in I}

are covering families
[
BGM+24
, Remark 2.3]
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a small category with basepoint
\ast
. Then, the collection
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{C}}^{\cong}
of all isomorphisms of
\mathbf{C}
yields a category with covering families that we denote by
(\mathbf{C},\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{C}}^{\cong},\ast)
or, more concisely by
\mathbf{C}^{\cong}
. In particular, we have:

(i)

for every finite (possibly empty) set
I
, the family
\{\ast\to\ast\}_{i\in I}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{\cong}
;

(ii)

if
f\colon C\to D
is an isomorphism in
\mathbf{C}
, then
\{f\colon C\to D\}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{\cong}
.

Example 2.3

(
[
Lop25
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{Mat}
be the category of matroids. Denote by
\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
the category of matroids and matroids morphisms, with a disjoint distinguished point
\ast
. Then, the category with covering families
(\mathbf{Mat}_{+},\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{Mat}}^{\cong},\ast)
shall be denoted by
\mathbf{Mat}^{\cong}
.

We are interested in the
K
-theory of categories with covering families, and in particular in the
K
-theory of the category of matroids. Its definition is based on the following notion of categories of covers:

Definition 2.4

(
[
BGM+24
, Definition 2.12]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a category with covering families. The
category of covers

W(\mathbf{C})
has objects any finite collection
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of non-distinguished objects of
\mathbf{C}
, and a morphism

\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\to\{B_{j}\}_{j\in J}

in
W(\mathbf{C})
is a map
f\colon I\to J
of finite sets along with morphisms
f_{i}\colon A_{i}\to B_{f(i)}
for all
i\in I
, such that

\{f_{i}\colon A_{i}\to B_{j}\}_{i\in f^{-1}(j)}

is a covering family for each
j\in J
. Composition of morphisms in
W(\mathbf{C})
is defined by composing the maps of sets and the covering families.

We observe here that the category of covers construction yields a functor by
[
BGM+24
, Lemma 2.16]
, and that
W(\mathbf{C})
has a natural base-point object, corresponding to
I=\emptyset
. Using the category of covers associated to a category with covering families
\mathbf{C}
, it is possible to define the
K
-theory spectrum
K(\mathbf{C})
– see
[
BGM+24
, Definition 2.17]
for the details of this construction. The computation of the
K
-theory groups is in general quite difficult, but the case of the
0
-th group is well understood. In fact, we have the following:

Proposition 2.5

(
[
BGM+24
, Proposition 3.8]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a category with covering families. Then,
K_{0}(\mathbf{C})
is the free abelian group

K_{0}(\mathbf{C})={\raisebox{2.04439pt}{$\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{ob}(\mathbf{C})]$}\left/\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$[A]=\sum_{i\in I}[A_{i}]$}\right.}

for every covering family
\{A_{i}\to A\}_{i\in I}
.

The
K
-theory of categories with covering families satisfies fundamental theorems valid also for the classical
K
-theory of categories. We let
\mathbf{D}\subseteq\mathbf{C}
be a full subcategory of the category with covering families
\mathbf{C}
; that is,
\mathbf{D}
is a full subcategory of
\mathbf{C}
, and
\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{C}
is a morphism of categories with covering families. Following
[
CG25
]
, we say that a cover
\{f_{i}\colon A_{i}\to A\}_{i\in I}
is a

of a cover

if for every

there is

and a map

in
\mathbf{C}

such that
f_{i}=g_{j}\circ h
.

Definition 2.6

(
[
CG25
, Definition 2.7]
)
.

A category with covering families
\mathbf{C}
has refinements if any pair of covers
\{A_{i}\to A\}_{i\in I}
and
\{A_{j}^{\prime}\to A\}_{j\in J}
of the same object

has a common refinement.

We can now recall the Dévissage Theorem for categories with covering families.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
CG25
, Theorem 2.8]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a category with covering families that has refinements and let
i\colon\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{C}
be the inclusion of a full subcategory with covering families.
If every object
C\in\mathbf{C}
has a choice of a cover
\{D_{i}\to C\}_{i\in I}
with
D_{i}\in\mathbf{D}
for all
i\in I
, then

Ki\colon K(\mathbf{D})\to K(\mathbf{C})

is an equivalence.

Recall from Example
2.2
that to any small category with basepoint
\mathbf{C}
we can associate the category with covering families
\mathbf{C}^{\cong}
where the covering families are given by the isomorphisms. Then, we have a complete description of the associated
K
-theory:

(
[
CG25
, Proposition 2.14]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{C}^{\cong}
be a small category with basepoint. Then, there is an equivalence of spectra

K(\mathbf{C}^{\cong})\simeq\bigvee_{[C]}\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{C}}(C))

where the wedge ranges across the isomorphism classes of objects in
\mathbf{C}
and
\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{C}}(C)
is the
group of
\mathbf{C}
-isomorphisms of an object
C\in\mathbf{C}
.

As a consequence, Proposition
2.8
gives us the full computation of the category of matroids
\mathbf{Mat}^{\cong}
with coverings given by the isomorphisms. In the next sections we shall focus on a different structure of covering families, the Tutte coverings developed in
[
Lop25
]
.

3.
Matroids with Tutte coverings

In this section we first recall from
[
Lop25
]
the definition of the covering category of matroids with Tutte coverings, and then we shall focus on indecomposable matroids. However, to define the notion of Tutte coverings we first need to recall the definition of deletion-contraction trees.

Let
T
be a binary tree with root
r_{T}
. We can naturally associate a category to
T
as follows. First, we consider the order relation
\leq_{T}
on the vertices of
T
with
w\leq_{T}v
if and only if
w=v
or
w
is a descendant of
v
. As
T
is a tree, this describes a poset, hence a category. We denote this resulting category by
\mathcal{C}_{T}
.

Recall from Example
2.3
that
\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
denotes the category of matroids and matroids morphisms, with a disjoint distinguished point
\ast
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
Lop25
, Definition 4.4 & 4.5]
)
.

Let
T
be a rooted binary tree with root
r_{T}
. An
elementary deletion-contraction tree of shape
T
is a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{C}_{T}\to\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
such that:

(1)

\mathcal{F}(r_{T})\neq\ast
;

(2)

if
v
is an internal vertex of
T
with only one child
w
, then
\mathcal{F}(v)=\mathcal{F}(w)
and if
w\to v
is the morphism in
\mathcal{C}_{T}
induced by the relation
w\leq_{T}v
, we have
\mathcal{F}(w\to v)=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}(v)}
;

(3)

if
v
is an internal vertex with children
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, then there is a non-degenerate element
e
of the matroid
M\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(v)
for which
\mathcal{F}
maps the subdiagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{2}}

in
\mathcal{C}_{T}
to one of the following ones:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M/e}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\setminus e}

or

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\setminus e}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M/e}

where
M/e
and
M\setminus e
denote the contraction and deletion of
M
on
e
, and the maps
M/e\to M
and
M\setminus e\to M
denote the standard inclusions.

We say that a functor
\mathcal{G}\colon\mathcal{C}_{T}\to\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
is a
deletion-contraction tree
of shape
T
if it is naturally isomorphic to an elementary deletion-contraction tree of shape
T
.

Following
[
Lop25
, Definition 4.11]
, we can now define the covering family structure on the category of matroids induced by the Tutte morphism.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tc}}
be the collection of multi-morphisms on
\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
defined as follows:

(1)

For every finite (possibly empty) set
I
, the family
\{\ast\to\ast\}_{i\in I}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tc}}
.

(2)

Given a matroid
M\neq\ast
, a multi-morphism
\{f_{j}\colon M_{j}\to M\}_{j=1,\dots,p}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tc}}
if and only if there exists a rooted binary tree
T
with root
r_{T}
, leaves
v_{1},\dots,v_{p}
and
a deletion-contraction tree
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{C}_{T}\to\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
, with
\mathcal{F}(r_{T})=M
, such that
f_{j}=\mathcal{F}(i_{v_{j}})
for each
j=1,\dots,p
, and where
i_{v_{j}}
is the unique morphism
i_{v_{j}}\colon v_{j}\to r_{T}
in
\mathcal{C}_{T}
.

A multi-morphism
\{f_{j}\colon M_{j}\to N\}_{j=1,\dots,p}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tc}}
is called a
Tutte covering
of
M
.

By
[
Lop25
, Proposition 4.12]
, the triple
(\mathbf{Mat}_{+},\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{tc}},\ast)
is a category with covering families that we shall shortly denote by
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
.

3.1.
Indecomposable Coverings

In this subsection we recall the properties of the so-called
indecomposable matroids
. Recall from
[
Lop25
, Definition 2.9]
that, if
\mathbf{C}
is a category with covering families, then an object
C\in\mathbf{C}
is
indecomposable
if the only coverings of
C
are singletons of the form
\{A\xrightarrow{\cong}C\}
.

Definition 3.3
.

An
indecomposable matroid
is a matroid
M=(X,\mathcal{I})
such that every
x\in X
is degenerate.

Observe that an indecomposable matroid is the graphic matroid associated to a forest, possibly with loops. In fact,
M
is an indecomposable matroid if it is the direct sum (possibly empty) of finitely many coloops
and loops. As by
[
Lop25
, Definition 4.15]
, we say that a Tutte covering
\{A_{i}\rightarrow M\}_{i\in I}
for a matroid
M\neq\ast
is
indecomposable
if all the matroids
A_{i}
, for
i\in I
, are indecomposable.
Every matroid admits a covering by indecomposable. In fact, we have the following:

Proposition 3.4

(
[
Lop25
, Proposition 4.14]
)
.

Any matroid
M\neq*
admits a Tutte covering
\{g_{i}\colon N_{i}\rightarrow M\}_{i\in I}
where each
N_{i}
is an indecomposable matroid.

By a multi-set of matroids we shall mean an indexed family of matroids
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
with
I\neq\emptyset
. An isomorphism of multi-sets of matroids
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}\to\{N_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is a bijection
f\colon I\to J
such that
M_{i}\xrightarrow{\cong}N_{f(i)}
is an isomorphism of matroids for all
i\in I
.
Given an indecomposable Tutte covering
A=\{A_{i}\rightarrow M\}_{i\in I}
, the set
\mathrm{Ind}_{M}(A)
will denote the multi-set of domains – that is, indecomposable matroids – appearing in
A
. Then, it is proven in
[
Lop25
, Proposition 4.16]
that indecomposable Tutte coverings have isomorphic domains. More precisely, we have:

Proposition 3.5

(
[
Lop25
, Proposition 4.16]
)
.

Let
A\coloneqq\{A_{i}\rightarrow M\}_{i\in I}
and
B\coloneqq\{B_{j}\rightarrow M\}_{j\in J}
be indecomposable Tutte coverings of a matroid
M\neq\ast
. Then
\mathrm{Ind}_{M}(A)
and
\mathrm{Ind}_{M}(B)
are isomorphic as multi-sets.

As a main step towards the computation of the
K
-theory of matroids with Tutte coverings, we want to show that the category with covering families
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
has refinements.
We will use the following proposition;

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
A\coloneqq\{A_{i}\rightarrow M\}_{i\in I}
be a Tutte covering. Then there exist a refinement
A^{\prime}\coloneqq\{A^{\prime}_{k}\rightarrow M\}_{k\in K}
of
A
that is an indecomposable Tutte covering.

Proof.

If every domain
A_{i}
of
A
is an indecomposable matroid, the assertion is immediate. Otherwise, there exists an index
j
and a matroid
A_{j}
such that
A_{j}
has at least a non-degenerate element
e
.
By Remark
1.10
, we have the morphisms

f_{j}^{d}\coloneqq A_{j}\setminus e\rightarrow A_{j}\rightarrow M,\qquad f_{j}^{c}\coloneqq A_{j}/e\rightarrow A_{j}\rightarrow M\ ,

and, consequently, we can consider the multi-morphism

A^{\prime}\coloneqq\{A_{i}\rightarrow M\}_{i\neq j}\cup\{A_{j}\setminus e\xrightarrow{f_{j}^{d}}M\}\cup\{A_{j}/e\xrightarrow{f_{j}^{d}}M\}\ .

We want to show that
A^{\prime}
is a Tutte covering family. To see it, let
\mathcal{F}_{A}\colon T_{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
be a deletion-contraction tree for the covering family
A
and let
\ell_{j}
be a leaf of
T_{A}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{A}(\ell_{j})=A_{j}
. Consider the tree
T_{A^{\prime}}
obtained from
T_{A}
by adding the children
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
to
\ell_{j}
.
The assignment

\mathcal{F}_{A^{\prime}}(v)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}_{A}(v)&\mbox{ if }v\in T_{A}\\
M\setminus e&\mbox{ if }v=w_{1}\\
M/e&\mbox{ if }v=w_{2}\end{cases}

provides the desired functor
\mathcal{F}_{A^{\prime}}\colon T_{A^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
.
By construction,
A^{\prime}
refines
A
. We can now iterate this process until every matroid in the domain of the newly constructed Tutte covering has no non-degenerate elements. This proves the statement.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

The category with covering families
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
has refinements.

Proof.

Let
\{A_{i}\rightarrow M\}_{i\in I}
and
\{B_{j}\rightarrow M\}_{j\in J}
be covering families in
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
. We want to prove that there exists a common refinement for
A
and
B
. By Proposition
3.6
, there exist indecomposable Tutte coverings
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
refining
A
and
B
, respectively, via refining morphisms
h_{A}
and
h_{B}
.
Now, by Proposition
3.5
, we have that the multi-sets of matroids
\mathrm{Ind}_{M}(A^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{Ind}_{M}(B^{\prime})
are isomorphic. This means that there exists a bijective map
h\colon\mathrm{Ind}_{M}(A^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{M}(B^{\prime})
such that if
h(A^{\prime}_{j})=B^{\prime}_{i}
, then
h_{j}\colon A^{\prime}_{j}\rightarrow B^{\prime}_{i}
is an isomorphism of matroids. The composition
h\circ h_{B}
is then a refinement morphism and
A^{\prime}
refines both
A
and
B
. This is enough to show that the category
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
has refinements.
∎

4.
The
K
-theory of (indecomposable) matroids

In this section we introduce the subcategories of
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
of graphic matroids and indecomposable matroids. Our aim is to prove that these categories share the same
K
-theoretic invariants. As a main application, we provide a computation of the
K
-theory of
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
.

We start with the category with covering families of graphic matroids. Denote by
\mathbf{Graph}
the full subcategory of
\mathbf{Mat}
on graphic matroids. Therefore, its objects are matroids of the form
M_{G}
, for
G
a finite graph, and the morphisms are the induced morphisms as (graphic) matroids.
By Proposition
1.11
, graphic matroids are closed under taking deletions and contractions. Therefore, the covering family structure on
\mathbf{Mat}_{+}
with multi-morphisms the Tutte coverings also yields a category with covering families, that we denote by

\mathbf{Graph}^{\mathrm{tc}}\coloneqq(\mathbf{Graph}_{+},\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{Graph}}^{\mathrm{tc}},\ast)\ .

Remark 4.1
.

The inclusion

\mathbf{Graph}^{\mathrm{tc}}\to\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
is a morphism of categories with covering families.

We now focus of indecomposable matroids and recall that an indecomposable matroid is the graphic matroid associated to a forest, possibly with loops (see Definition
3.3
). We denote by
\mathbf{IMat}
the full subcategory of
\mathbf{Mat}
on indecomposable matroids. The morphisms are all matroid morphisms between indecomposable matroids.

Remark 4.2
.

The category
\mathbf{IMat}
is closed under taking minors and duals.

Consider the category
\mathbf{IMat}_{+}
of indecomposable matroids with a disjoint distinguished point
*
. By Example
2.2
, the isomorphisms of matroids yield a category with covering families that we denote by

\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}\coloneqq(\mathbf{IMat}_{+},\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{IMat}}^{\cong},\ast)\ .

Observe that, because of Remark
4.2
, Tutte covering families of indecomposable matroids yield a covering family
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{IMat}}^{\mathrm{tc}}
in
\mathbf{IMat}
, which we still call the Tutte coverings. We get a category with covering families

\mathbf{IMat}^{\mathrm{tc}}\coloneqq(\mathbf{IMat}_{+},\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{IMat}}^{\mathrm{tc}},\ast)\ .

Remark 4.3
.

The inclusion functors
\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}\to\mathbf{Mat}^{\cong}
,
\mathbf{IMat}^{\mathrm{tc}}\to\mathbf{Graph}^{\mathrm{tc}}
and
\mathbf{IMat}^{\mathrm{tc}}\to\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
are all morphisms of categories with covering families. All domain categories are full in the codomain.

Consider the category with covering families
\mathbf{IMat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
. By definition, a covering family in
\mathbf{IMat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
is either of the form
\{\ast\to\ast\}_{I}
or it is a singleton of the form
\{A\xrightarrow{\cong}C\}
. As a consequence, the category with covering families
\mathbf{IMat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
is in fact equivalent to the category with covering families given by the isomorphisms, that is
\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}
.
As a consequence of this observation we have the following:

Theorem 4.4
.

There are equivalences of spectra

K(\iota_{1})\colon K(\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong})\to K(\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}})

and

K(\iota_{2})\colon K(\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong})\to K(\mathbf{Graph}^{\mathrm{tc}})

where
\iota_{1}
and
\iota_{2}
are the inclusion functors.

Proof.

The category
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
has refinements by Corollary
3.7
and
\iota_{1}\colon\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}\to\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
is the inclusion of a full subcategory with covering families. Every object
M\in\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
has a choice of cover
M_{i}\to M
where all
M_{i}
are indecomposable matroids by Proposition
3.4
– the coverings of the basepoint
\ast
being the obvious ones, given by
\{\ast\rightarrow\ast\}
. Then, the first part of the statement follows by Theorem
2.7
.
For the second part of the statement, observe that
\iota_{1}
factors through
\iota_{2}
. By naturality of the
K
-theory construction, also the second equivalence holds.
∎

Using this equivalence of spectra, we can now decompose the
K
-theory spectrum of matroids as follows:

Corollary 4.5
.

There is an equivalence of spectra

K(\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}})\simeq\bigvee_{[M]}\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{IMat}}(M))

where the wedge ranges across the isomorphism classes of indecomposable matroids and
\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{IMat}}(M)
is the
group of
\mathbf{IMat}
-isomorphisms of
M
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.8
, we have the equivalence of spectra

K(\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong})\simeq\bigvee_{[M]}\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{IMat}}(M))

where the wedge ranges across the isomorphism classes of indecomposable matroids and the group
\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{IMat}}(M)
is the
group of isomorphisms of
M
in the category
\mathbf{IMat}
. The statement follows by Theorem
4.4
.
∎

We can provide a more explicit computation, but we first need a preliminary lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
M=(X,\mathcal{I})\in\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}
be an indecomposable matroid of rank
r
, and let
n
be the cardinality of the ground set
X
. Then

\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{IMat}}(M)\cong S_{r}\times S_{n-r}\ ,

where
S_{r}
and
S_{n-r}
denote the symmetric groups on
r
and
n-r
elements.

Proof.

The elements of
\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{IMat}}(M)
are exactly the automorphisms of
M
in the category
\mathbf{Mat}
, i.e. bijective maps
\varphi\colon X\rightarrow X
such that for any
I\in\mathcal{I}
we have that
\varphi(I)\in\mathcal{I}
.

Recall that a matroid in
\mathbf{IMat}
has no non-degenerate elements. As a consequence,
X=X_{c}\sqcup X_{\ell}
, where
X_{c}
and
X_{\ell}
are the sets of coloops and loops of
M
, respectively. Observe that, by definition,
X_{c}\in\mathcal{I}
and it is the unique bases of
M
. In particular we have that
|X_{c}|=r
and
|X_{\ell}|=n-r
.
Furthermore, note that the condition

I\in\mathcal{I}\Longrightarrow\varphi(I)\in\mathcal{I}
is satisfied only if
\varphi({\{x\}})\in X_{i}
for every
x\in X_{c}
, i.e.
\varphi
acts as a permutation of
S_{r}
when restricted to
X_{c}
. Analogously, this condition forces
\varphi({\{x\}})
to be in
X_{\ell}
for any
x\in X_{\ell}
. Then,
\varphi
acts as an element of
S_{n-r}
over
X_{\ell}
. The statement follows.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

We remark that, if
M
is an indecomposable matroid, then its rank is the number of its coloops and
n-r
is the number of its loops. In particular
M
can be written in the form
M\simeq U_{0,1}^{\oplus n-r}\oplus U_{1,1}^{\oplus r}
, where
U_{k,h}
is the uniform matroid of cardinality
k
subsets over a set of
h
elements.
Furthermore, observe that for any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
the matroid
M\simeq U_{0,1}^{\oplus m}\oplus U_{1,1}^{\oplus n}
is an indecomposable matroid with exactly
m
loops and
n
coloops.

We observe here that there is an equivalence of spectra

\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(G\times H)\simeq\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(G)\wedge\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(H)

whenever
G
and
H
are finite groups. In fact, first we have an homotopy equivalence of classifying spaces
B(G\times H)\simeq B(G)\times B(H)
. Further, if
X,Y
are topological spaces there is a homeomorphism
(X\times Y)_{+}\cong X_{+}\wedge Y_{+}
of based spaces; see e.g.
[
Rie14
, Construction 3.3.14 & Lemma 3.3.16]
, applied to the convenient category of compactly generated spaces
\mathbf{CGTop}
. This implies that the associated category of based spaces
\mathbf{CGTop}_{+}
is closed symmetric monoidal with respect to the smash
product
[
Rie14
, Theorem 6.1.12]
. Taking the suspension spectrum of a product of based spaces yields a strong symmetric monoidal functor (with respect to the smash product of spectra) – cf.
[
Lur17
, Section 4.8.2]
. As a consequence of this fact, we get the description of the
K
-theory of the category of matroids with Tutte coverings as a wedge decomposition of suspension spectra (cf.
[
BGM+24
, Example 2.20]
):

Corollary 4.8
.

There are equivalences of spectra

K(\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}})\simeq\bigvee_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{m})\wedge\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{n})\simeq K(\mathbf{Graph}^{\mathrm{tc}})\ .

Proof.

As
K(\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}})
is equivalent to
K(\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong})
, and this is equivalent to
\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{IMat}}(M))
, we only neeed to specify the automorphism groups

\mathrm{iso}_{\mathbf{IMat}}(M)
for indecomposable matroids.
Recall that, by Remark
4.7
, an indecomposable matroid can be written in the form
U_{0,1}^{\oplus m}\oplus U_{1,1}^{\oplus n}
, where
m
and
n
are the number of loops and coloops, respectively.
Hence, we get an equivalence of spectra

K(\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}})\simeq\bigvee_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{m}\times S_{n})\ .

Now, using the decomposition

\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{m}\times S_{n})\simeq\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{m})\wedge\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{n})\ ,

the result follows.
∎

As a consequence of the splitting in
K
-theory of Corollary
4.8
, we get that
K_{0}(\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}})
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}
, as established in
[
Lop25
]
, and used to realise the universal Tutte-Grothendieck invariant
for matroids as the
K_{0}
-homomorphism induced by a map of
K
-theory spectra. From the same splitting we also have that all higher homotopy groups encode the stable homology groups of the symmetric groups. Furthermore, we can depict the relation between the
K
-theory of
\mathbf{Mat}^{\cong}
and
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
in the following commutative diagram:

.

Is there a categorical interpretation of evaluations of the Tutte polynomial?

We conclude with the description of the

-theory of matroids with a further structure reflecting duality of matroids. Let
C_{2}
be the cyclic group with two elements:

Corollary 4.10
.

The
K
-theory spectra
K(\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong})
and
K(\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}})
are equivalent as
C_{2}
-spectra.

Proof.

Consider the category
\mathbf{Mat}
. This is a category with duality, the duality being given by taking dual matroids. Also the category
\mathbf{IMat}
is a category with duality by Remark
4.2
. Since an element
x
of a matroid
M
is non-degenerate if and only if
x
is non-degenerate in the dual matroid
M^{*}
, it yields duality functors

D_{\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}}\colon\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}\to\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}\text{ and }D_{\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}}\colon\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}\to\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}

which are morphisms of categories with covering families. That is, the category with Tutte coverings
\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
and the category
\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}
are
C_{2}
-categories with covering families (cf.
[
BGM+24
, Definition 4.1]
). Note that the duality functors commute with the inclusion functor
\iota_{1}\colon\mathbf{IMat}^{\cong}\to\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}
, that is the diagram

(4.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Mat}^{\mathrm{tc}}}

is commutative. We now focus on the left part of this diagram. The duality functor

induces an equivalence

\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{m})\wedge\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{n})\to\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{n})\wedge\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{m})

for all

, and it yields a spectrum involution. This extends to the whole spectrum
\bigvee_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{m})\wedge\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}B(S_{n})
which inherits the structure of a

. By naturality of the

∎
