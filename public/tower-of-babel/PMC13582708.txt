Effects of taurine on skeletal muscle in senescence-accelerated mice and C2C12 myoblasts and myotubes
Authors: Yiying Huang, Hatasu Kobayashi, Xiaoying Zhou, Keiya Matsuoka, Jun Kawanokuchi, Kaoru Midorikawa, Shinji Oikawa, Zhe Zhang, Ning Ma, Mariko Murata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Taurine is a semi-essential amino acid with diverse cytoprotective effects and is associated with anti-aging. This study explores the role and molecular mechanism of taurine in muscle aging. Senescence-accelerated mouse prone 8 (SAMP8) and its resistant (senescence-accelerated mouse resistant 1, SAMR1) mice were given 1% taurine water from 5 to 10 months of age, while control mice were given distilled water (DW). At 10 months of age, a rotarod test was performed to assess muscle endurance. SAMP8 mice had a shorter running time on the rotarod test than SAMR1 mice. Taurine significantly extended running time compared with that of DW-drinking group in SAMP8 mice, a similar trend was observed in SAMR1 mice. The gastrocnemius muscle was used for the RNA-sequencing analysis. Among differentially expressed genes between taurine and DW, Tbx5 and Alb showed some significant differences between the treatment by RT-qPCR. Myoblast C2C12 cells underwent cellular senescence induction with D-galactose (D-gal), which was detected by SA-β-gal staining. Taurine reduced SA-β-gal-positive area induced by D-gal, suggesting its protective effect against cellular senescence. Myotube induction was inhibited by D-gal treatment and restored by taurine, as detected by immunocytochemistry of myosin heavy chain (MHC) and Western blot of myogenin. D-gal-induced decrease trend in protein level of TBX5 was significantly increased in C2C12 myotubes treated with taurine. Taurine may have a protective effect on muscle senescence and myotube regeneration.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00726-026-03542-0.

Introduction

As human life expectancy increases, skeletal muscle aging is becoming a major challenge, and its negative impact on human health is becoming more evident. Senescence causes a decline in muscle mass and function, worsening the quality of life of the elderly population. Taurine is one of the nutrients being studied for its potential anti-aging properties. Taurine is present in high concentrations in several mammalian tissues, including skeletal muscle. Singh et al. (Singh et al. 2023) found that circulating taurine levels declined with age in mice, monkeys, and humans, suggesting taurine deficiency as a driver of senescence. On the contrary, Fernandez et al. (Fernandez et al. 2025) showed through longitudinal observation that circulating taurine does not decrease with age in healthy individuals (concentrations increase or remain unchanged), concluding that low circulating taurine levels are unlikely to serve as a good biomarker of aging. Regarding skeletal muscle senescence, it has been reported that muscle taurine levels gradually and significantly decreased (–24%) in senescence-accelerated prone 8 mice (SAMP8) mice from 10 to 60 weeks of age (Derave et al. 2008). On the other hand, Wu et al. (Wu et al. 2025) observed a significant increase in taurine concentration in the skeletal muscle of aging SAMP8 mice, but suggested that this increase was insufficient to completely counteract muscle atrophy induced by oxidative stress and metabolic dysfunction associated with aging. The effectiveness of taurine as an anti-aging agent for lifespan and skeletal muscle remains debatable.

Therefore, we conducted in vivo and in vitro experiments, to clarify the efficacy of taurine in muscle senescence. We set up a DW drinking (DW) group and a 1% taurine drinking (Tau) group of SAMP8, a model of enhanced aging, and senescence-accelerated resistant 1 mice (SAMR1), a normal control. In addition, we investigated the effects of taurine supplementation on D-galactose (D-gal)-induced senescence of murine C2C12 myoblast and myotube cells.

Materials and methods

Animals and experimental protocol

Four-week-old male SAMP8 and its healthy control SAMR1 were purchased from Japan SLC (Shizuoka, Japan). The mice were individually housed (one mouse per cage with wood-derived bedding materials) under standard housing conditions (temperature: 21 ± 1 °C; humidity: 55 ± 2%) in a specific pathogen free facility with a 12 h light/dark cycle, and given food and water ad libitum.

A total of 22 SAMP8 mice and 20 SAMR1 mice were used in this study. At 20 weeks of age, each strain was randomly divided into two groups (9–11 mice per group): a taurine group and a distilled water (DW) group. Mice in the taurine administration (Tau) group were given 1% taurine containing drinking water, and mice in DW administration (DW) group were given DW. It is estimated that mice ingest 20 mg/day of taurine based on their water intake. Body weights were measured monthly. From At 42 weeks of age, rotarod test was performed, as described below. There was no significant difference in body weight between the Tau group and DW group in SAMP8 mice (30.1 ± 2.8 and 28.2 ± 3.9 g, respectively), while a significant difference in body weight between the Tau group and the DW group in SAMR1 mice (40.9 ± 3.5 and 45.5 ± 3.6 g, respectively, P = 0.00091) at 42 weeks of age. After the experiment, three mice from each group were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection (200 µL) of 2% phenobarbital sodium, and then euthanized by cardiac perfusion with physiological saline. The gastrocnemius muscle was removed and kept in − 80 °C until use.

All experimental protocols in this study were approved by the Suzuka University Medical Science Board Committee for Animal Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (Approval No. 259).

Rotarod test

Mice were trained on a rotarod (LE8300; Letica LSI, Pan lab Scientific Instruments, Barcelona, Spain) at a speed of 20 rotations per min for 5 consecutive days. On the final day of the experiment, the fall latency (the time from the moment the mouse was placed on the rod until it fell) was recorded. This procedure was repeated a total of three times, with a 10-minute interval between trials. Between trials, the rotator rod was washed with diluted ethanol.

RNA sequencing and analysis

The muscle (3 mice per group) was treated with RNAlater-ICE, and was commissioned to TaKaRa (Japan) for RNA sequencing. The 150-bp paired-end RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq X Plus system. Gene expression levels were quantified and normalized to Transcripts Per Million (TPM), which was analyzed according to the instruction manual. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified based on the cut-off of |log2FC|>1 and P value < 0.05. Jonckheere-Terpstra test (P < 0.05) was used to identify trend genes. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were implemented by DAVID bioinformatics resources, and visualized in https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/. The RNA-seq raw data were deposited to the Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database under the accession number PRJNA1447588.

Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

QuantiTect Reverse Transcription Kit (205311, QIAGEN, Germany) was used to reverse RNA (0.05 µg) to cDNA. StepOnePlus RT- PCR System (Applied Biosystem, USA) and the QuantiTect SYBR® Green PCR Kits (204143, QIAGEN, Germany) were used to detect the relative gene expression. The primer of Chrm3, Prkg1, Alb, Tbx5, Zfhx4, Syt12, Rdh9, Mmp27, Ctcfl, Pcdhga5, Zfp316, Zfp462 and Gapdh were provided by QIAGEN.

Cell culture and treatment

A mouse skeletal myoblast cell line (C2C12) was obtained from the Cell Bank of the RIKEN BioResource Research Center (RCB0987, Japan) maintained at 70% confluence and used for experiments within 10 passages. The cells were cultured in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 at 37 °C.

C2C12 cells were grown in growth medium (GM) consisting of high-glucose DMEM (11995065, Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (P/S; 09367-34, Nacalai Tesque, Japan). To induce cellular senescence, C2C12 cells were treated with 30 mg/mL D-gal (G5388-100G, Sigma, USA) in GM for 3 days. To investigate the effect of taurine on D-gal-induced senescence, taurine (1mM) was added to the culture medium 12 h before D-gal treatment.

To obtain differentiated myotubes, C2C12 cells were grown in GM until reaching approximately 90% confluence was reached. The medium was then replaced with differentiation medium (DM) consisting of high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 2% horse serum (HS; 116050130, Gibco, USA) and 1% P/S. D-gal treatment for 5 days in DM, and taurine treatment was started 12 h before D-gal treatment.

SA-β-gal staining

SA-β-gal staining was performed using the senescence-galactosidase staining kit (9860, Cell Signaling, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. To avoid false-positive staining of SA-β-gal due to high cell density, we performed the experiment using C2C12 myoblast cells under70-80% confluence. Cells were photographed and analyzed using the all-in-one fluorescence microscope (BZ-X700, KEYENCE, Japan).

Immunofluorescence staining

Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (163-20145, FUJIFILM, Japan) for 15 min, treated with 0.15% Triton X-100/PBS for 10 min, and then blocked at room temperature using 1% BSA/PBST containing glycine 22.52 mg/mL for 30 min. Cells were incubated with anti-myosin heavy chain (MHC) primary antibody (3 µg/mL, ab37484, Abcam, UK) overnight at 4 °C, followed by Alexa 488-labeled secondary antibody (1:1000, A11001, Invitrogen, USA) in the dark at room temperature for 1 h. They were then incubated with DAPI (1:1000, 19178-91, nacalai tesque, Japan) for 3 min. Cells were washed with PBS three times for 5 min each, before proceeding to the next step. Images were captured and analyzed using the all-in-one fluorescence microscope.

Western blotting

Cells were lysed on ice using 1×RIPA buffer (9806, Cell Signaling, USA) containing protease inhibitor cocktail. Protein concentrations were measured using Pierce™ 660 nm Protein Assay Reagent (22660, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Equal amounts of 60 µg protein were loaded onto a 4–12% Bis-Tris gel (NP0336BOX, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), then separated and transferred onto PVDF membrane using NuPAGE® Electrophoresis System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). the membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk in TBS-T at room temperature for 1 h. The membrane was incubated with primary antibody at 4℃ overnight, and then secondary antibody at room temperature for 1 h. The following primary antibodies were used: TBX5 (1:1000, ab259980, Abcam), myogenin (1:1000, ab1835, Abcam), β-tubulin (1:4000, 66240-1-Ig, Proteintech, USA), and GAPDH (1:2000, 2118, Cell Signaling Technology, USA). The secondary antibodies were HRP-conjugated anti-mouse IgG (1:2000, 7076 S, Cell Signaling Technology) and HRP-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG (1:2000, 5127 S, Cell Signaling Technology). Protein bands were visualized using Amersham ECL Prime (RPN2232, Cytiva, UK) and imaged with the ImageQuant 800 system (Cytiva). Band intensity was quantified using ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. Student’s t-test and chi-square test were used to compare between two groups. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. All results were expressed as mean values ± standard deviation (SD).

Results

Taurine treatment improves the survival rate of SAMP8 mice

The survival rate in SAMP8-DW group (54.5%, P = 0.04185) was significantly lower than SAMP8-Tau group (100%), while there was no significant difference between SAMR1-DW group (100%, P = 0.9179) and SAMR1-Tau group (90.9%).

Taurine treatment improves the motor performance of mice in rotarod test

To observe the effects of taurine on senescence-related decline of skeletal muscle endurance, we used SAMP8 and SAMR1 mice. As shown in Fig. 1, SAMP8 mice had a shorter running duration on the rotarod test than SAMR1 mice in DW groups (SAMP8 vs. SAMR1 P = 0.0022). In SAMP8 mice, the Tau group showed a significantly greater difference compared to the DW group (P = 0.0071), and in SAMR1 mice, although no significant difference was observed, a trend was seen (P = 0.0845), suggesting a protective effect against senescence-related decline in motor function. We observed the cross-sectional H&E-stained sections of the gastrocnemius muscle, showing a reduction in muscle fiber diameter in SAMP8-DW mouse muscle, and taurine treatment improved it, and no or little atrophy in SAMR1 muscle (Supplementary Figure S1).

RNA-seq analysis of muscle tissue of mouse treated with taurine and verifying expression of candidate genes

To explore the effects of taurine on muscle senescence, the mRNA expression profiles of muscle samples were analyzed via RNA-seq. When |log2FC|>1 and P value < 0.05 are used as cut-off values, a total of 229 and 349 differentially expressed genes between taurine and control in SAMP8 and SAMR1, respectively (Fig. 2A). Although no shared genes were found by Venn analysis, Jonckheere-Terpstra test (Fig. 2B and Table S1) revealed 65 trending genes (P < 0.05). Among them, 25 up-regulated genes and 9 down-regulated genes were identified in both SAMP8 and SAMR1 mice. GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis (Fig. 2C; Table 1) showed 21 genes, as candidate genes. We selected 12 genes, including Chrm3, Prkg1, Alb, Tbx5, Zfhx4, Syt12, Rdh9, Mmp27, Ctcfl, Pcdhga5, Zfp316, and Zfp462, and their expression levels were verified by RT-qPCR. The selection was based on an integrated assessment of gene function descriptions sourced from the GEO database and published literature. We hypothesize that these candidates may be involved in governing muscle-specific biological processes.

Effect of taurine on D-gal-induced C2C12 myoblast senescence

To investigate the effect of taurine on D-gal-induced senescence in C2C12 myoblast, we performed SA-β-gal staining, as shown in Fig. 3. The positive area of SA-β-gal staining was increased after D-gal treatment (P = 0.0036). The SA-β-gal positive area was significantly decreased in co-treatment of D-gal and taurine compared to D-gal alone (P = 0.00008).

Effect of taurine on differentiation ability

To characterize the effect of taurine supplementation on D-gal-induced senescence and its impact on the differentiation ability of C2C12 cells, differentiation medium was used to induce myoblasts into myotubes, which were identified by MHC, a marker of myotubes. As shown in Fig. 4A, the MHC immunofluorescence-positive area in the D-gal treatment showed a reduction of approximately 57% (P = 0.0006) compared to the control, and taurine treatment significantly restored the MHC area (P = 0.0063). Western blot analysis (Fig. 4B) showed that the protein level of myogenin, a myogenic differentiation marker, in the D-gal treatment decreased by 36% (P = 0.0013) compared with the control. There was an improvement (P = 0.0008) by co-treatment of D-gal and taurine compared with D-gal treatment alone. These results indicate that taurine exerts a protective effect against D-gal–induced myotube differentiation inhibition. Furthermore, the protein level of TBX5 showed a decreasing trend in D-gal treatment compared with the control, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.0582), and taurine treatment significantly restored the reduction of TBX5 (P = 0.0361).

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrated that SAMP8, an established model of skeletal muscle senescence (Derave et al. 2005), showed impaired motor performance compared with a normal control SAMR1 in the rotarod test, and taurine improved motor performance in SAMP8 as well as SAMR1 mice. However, the difference of body weight between DW and Tau groups in SAMR1 mice could be a kind of confounding factor on the motor performance. Notably, Morita et al. reported that the herbal medicine Juzentaihoto improved rotarod performance concomitantly with amelioration of skeletal muscle atrophy in SAMP8 (Morita et al. 2021), suggesting that the improvement in motor performance may be linked to the alleviation of aging-related skeletal muscle atrophy in this model. Furthermore, taurine treatment restored the survival rate of SAMP8 at 42 weeks of age, which recapitulates the survival improvement previously observed in normal mice at 28 months of age (Singh et al. 2023). Several in vitro and in vivo studies have reported that taurine maintains skeletal muscle health through multiple mechanisms, including regulating calcium ion balance, stabilizing membranes, modulating energy metabolism, reducing oxidative stress, and preventing muscle damage (Ito et al. 2014; Liu et al. 2025; McIntosh et al. 1998; Mughal et al. 2024; Singh et al. 2023; Terrill et al. 2016; Thirupathi et al. 2020). Scicchitano and Sica (Scicchitano and Sica 2018) suggested the potential use of taurine as a therapeutic molecule for the amelioration of skeletal muscle function and performance severely compromised during aging. These results collectively suggest that taurine exerts anti-aging effects under senescence-accelerating conditions, improving motor function.

To gain insight into the mechanism of the protective effect of taurine, the mRNA expression profile of skeletal muscle tissue was analyzed. RNA-seq analysis and validation by RT-qPCR identified candidate genes, Tbx5 and Alb, both of which tended to increase after taurine treatment in SAMP8. Studies on the relationship between Alb and skeletal muscle aging have mainly consisted of epidemiological analyses examining the association between serum albumin and age-related loss of muscle mass (sarcopenia) (Baumgartner et al. 1996; Silva-Fhon et al. 2021; Snyder et al. 2012), whereas studies investigating the mechanistic role of Alb in muscle aging remain limited. In contrast, Tbx5 plays an essential role in the development and patterning of skeletal muscle tissue (Hasson et al. 2010; Huang 2017; Steimle and Moskowitz 2017). Therefore, we prioritized Tbx5 for further analysis in this study. TBX5 was identified as the causative gene for Holt-Oram syndrome, an autosomal dominant disorder with complex heart and limb deformities (Li et al. 1997). TBX5 is a member of the T-box transcription factor family and is primarily known for its role in cardiac and forelimb development (Steimle and Moskowitz 2017). TBX5 acts in connective tissues to regulate muscle and tendon patterning and is required for proper organization of the soft tissues of the musculoskeletal system (Hasson et al. 2010). Our results and these previous reports suggest that TBX5 may be associated with taurine-mediated protection of skeletal muscle against aging.

Muscle stem cell senescence and impaired muscle regeneration have been known to be associated with skeletal muscle aging (Englund et al. 2021; Hong et al. 2022; Yamakawa et al. 2020). Therefore, in vitro experiments were also performed using C2C12 myoblasts and their differentiated myotubes. Although we used SA-β-gal staining to detect cellular senescence, additional senescence markers, such as p16 and p21, would substantially strengthen the interpretation of the in vitro data. As a result, taurine attenuated D-gal-induced myoblast senescence (SA-β-gal staining) and restored impaired myotube differentiation (MHC and myogenin). The proliferation and differentiation ability of myoblasts plays a crucial role in myofiber repair and regeneration (Yu et al. 2021). A recent review indicated that taurine has the physiological effects that improve muscle mass and function in older adults (He et al. 2025). Notably, taurine supplementation protected C2C12 myoblasts from loss of viability, facilitated intracellular ROS clearance, and partially preserved their ability to differentiate into myotubes after cisplatin exposure (Zhou et al. 2021). Accordingly, taurine exhibits protective effects on the ability of myoblasts to differentiate into myotubes impaired by senescence, suggesting that taurine contributes to muscle regeneration.

We also investigated the effects of taurine on Tbx5 gene expression in myoblasts cultured in differentiation medium with D-gal. The protein levels of TBX5 showed a decreasing tendency following D-gal treatment, and the reduction of TBX5 was significantly restored by taurine supplementation. Interestingly, Kaur et al. used a modified mRNA gene delivery platform of cardiac-reprogramming genes including TBX5, and demonstrated that it leads to neovascularization in ischemic-limb injury, indicating vascular regeneration in skeletal muscle in addition to cardiac muscle (Kaur et al. 2021). Such a mechanism may contribute to the improvement of motor function by taurine. Although the present findings are exploratory and do not establish a definitive mechanism, we discuss below several possible mechanisms based on previous reports. To the best of our knowledge, no direct evidence has clarified how taurine upregulates TBX5. However, because STAT3 has been reported to induce TBX5 expression (Snyder et al. 2010), activation of AKT and mTOR induced by taurine may upregulate TBX5 through AKT-mTOR-STAT3 signaling (Mughal et al. 2024). In addition, Yamak et al. (Yamak et al. 2015) identified a novel alternatively spliced TBX5 isoform (TBX5c), which is predominant in skeletal myoblasts. Overexpression of TBX5c, but not the canonical TBX5 isoform, enhanced myoblast differentiation into myotubes (Yamak et al. 2015). Therefore, our results raise the possibility that TBX5c may be involved in the protective effects of taurine. However, specific detection of TBX5c remains difficult because no isoform-specific antibody is currently available. TBX5 has been identified as a candidate based on our preliminary observations, and therefore further investigation is needed, including functional verification of the role of TBX5.

The small sample size is a limitation of this study. Increasing the sample size may reveal a statistically significant difference. We will consider increasing the sample size in future research to enhance statistical power. We used only male mice, since Yang et al. reported that the level of testosterone was obviously increased by taurine supplementation (1% taurine drinking) in male rats (Yang et al. 2010), which may contribute to increased skeletal muscle. The mechanism of taurine effects on skeletal muscle is still unclear, and sex differences should be clarified in future studies.

In conclusion, we demonstrated that taurine improved skeletal muscle function and myotube differentiation in vivo and in vitro models of senescence acceleration, respectively. We found TBX5 as a candidate gene for further investigation clarifying the mechanisms of muscle aging.
