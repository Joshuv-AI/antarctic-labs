Note b* Letter from Senator McClellan on Paris Negotiations

The Chairman stated that Mr. Hollister received a letter from
Senator McClellan dated May 28 wherein the Senator made reference to negotiations
then under way in Paris and indicated that he felt it was important that Congress
be kept informed on the current status of that situation. The Chairman noted that
the draft reply from Mr. Hollister (sent June 5>) indicated that negotiations have
been in progress, that the Senator had received a briefing prior to their start,
that the negotiations were not yet complete and no evaluation had yet been made of
the UK decision, that State was planning further discussions on the matter with
Congressional leaders, and, finally, that the Department of State would be notified
of the Senators interest in the matter. (The text of the letter from
Senator McClellan to Mr. Hollister and Mr. Hollister’s reply will be circulated as
EDAC D-67A8.) ^

1. Report of Team on CHINCOM Meeting

Decision

Mr. George, Commerce, and Mr. Knoll, State, will prepare a draft letter com-
mending Mir. William Cargo, USR0/ST, for submission by the Executive Committee to
the EDAC Chairman.

Discussion

The Chairman, on behalf of the Executive Committee, commended the members of
the team who went to Paris to assist the USDEL for the excellence of their services
and for the clear and concise reports which they transmitted to Washington. He
stated that the written reports submitted by the team would constitute the record
of this meeting. He then asked the team members to make any additional comments on
their trip to Paris and the proceedings there as would be of interest to the
Committee.

Mr. George briefly outlined the course of events to the Committee. He concluded
his remarks with the request that the Executive Committee send a letter of commenda-
tion, to be included in the personnel file of Mr. William Cargo, USDEL, for the out-
standing job he had done under circumstances which were more trying and difficult
than had ever been faced before in the GG structure. It was Mr. George’s belief
that to a large extent any prestige lost in CHINCOM by the U. S. in the past year
a half had been recovered because of the ability, sincerity, add discreetness of
Mr. Cargo in presenting the U. S. position and his effective use of restraint in his

Colonel Green and Mr. Knoll supported Mr. George’s comments and concurred in
the desirability of a letter for Mr. Cargo. Colonel Green pointed out that at the
opening of the meeting the U. S. was supported only by the Turks, and the subsequent
shift of several of the countries in favor of our position was due largely to the
efforts of Mr. Cargo in presenting the U. S. position. Mr. Knoll added that many
of the delegates had commented not only on Mr. Cargo’s performance at these sessions
but his handling, in prior sessions, of complex exception cases.

Mr. George said he would particularly like to mention the fact that the prompt
and complete replies from Washington in response to requests for guidance from the
team during the Paris meetings tended to ease their very difficult job.

The Chairman stated that the Chairman of EDAC, with that Committee's support,
is prepared to endorse a letter commending Mr. Cargo.

2 . Discussion of Future Activities

Decision

The Executive Committee will review at the earliest possible time the current
economic defense policy with the intent of making recommendations to EDAC on
changes which appear warranted by the current situation. A meeting will be called
as soon as the members are prepared to meet after receiving the anticipated summary
report on the Paris situation. It was the Chairman's hope that the meeting would
be held not later than Thursday, June 6.
