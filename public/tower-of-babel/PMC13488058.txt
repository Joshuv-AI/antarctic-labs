Integrated multi-omics analysis reveals molecular regulation of the hypothalamus–pituitary–ovary axis underlying egg production in indigenous chickens
Authors: Hang Liu, Yinghui Wei, Yanlu Wang, Yuanhao Zhou, Lei Zhang, Weiwu Zhang, Jinghui Fan, Huanhuan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study was conducted to systematically elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying egg production traits in indigenous chickens. Using a self-bred White-feathered Green-shelled (WG) chicken line as the research subject, high-yield and low-yield individuals were selected based on egg numbers at 43 weeks (EN43). Hypothalamus, pituitary, and ovarian tissues were collected from these individuals for targeted metabolomics profiling and transcriptome sequencing. The results showed a significant difference in EN43 between the two groups (P < 0.05). Targeted metabolomics of ovarian tissue detected a total of 17 steroid hormones, among which pregnenolone, progesterone, and 17β-estradiol were identified as differentially expressed steroid hormones (DESHs) between the two groups. Differential expression analysis of transcriptomes identified 666, 149, and 69 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the hypothalamus, pituitary, and ovary, respectively. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that hypothalamic DEGs were primarily involved in transcriptional regulation, neuropeptide signaling, and neurodevelopmental processes. Pituitary DEGs were mainly enriched in synaptic plasticity, calcium ion binding, and neurotransmitter transport, while ovarian DEGs were predominantly associated with signal transduction, calcium signaling regulation, and the cGMP signaling pathway. A molecular regulatory network (MRN) was constructed based on DEGs and DESHs. Through modular analysis, four key regulatory modules were identified, involving neuroendocrine regulation, neurotransmitter transport, steroid hormone synthesis, and transcriptional/developmental regulation. Combined with comprehensive network topology scoring, 25 potential candidate genes were identified, including POMC, NPY, CRH, SLC18A2, CD36, EGF, and WNT3A. In conclusion, this study integrated multi-omics data to reveal the molecular mechanisms of the HPO axis regulating egg production traits from a multi-tissue and multi-level perspective, providing a theoretical foundation for molecular breeding in indigenous poultry.Keywords: Chicken, Egg production, Multi-omics, HPO axis, Candidate gene

Introduction

Eggs are rich in various nutrients, including proteins, lipids, vitamins, and minerals, making them one of the essential animal-derived food sources in the human diet (Sun et al., 2019). Compared with commercial laying hen breeds, eggs from indigenous chicken breeds show no significant differences in protein or fat content; however, they often exhibit superior sensory qualities, such as better taste and flavor (Gao, et al., 2022; Yang, et al., 2023; Zhang, et al., 2024). Nevertheless, the annual egg production of indigenous breeds is far lower than that of commercial layers, which directly limits the economic profitability of indigenous poultry farming (Shi, et al., 2023). Therefore, genetic improvement of egg production traits in indigenous chickens not only helps satisfy market demand for diverse egg quality but also enhances the economic value and breeding efficiency of these breeds. Egg production traits are typical quantitative traits co-regulated by minor polygenes and complex molecular networks, with heritability generally ranging from low to moderate (h2 = 0.1–0.6) (Chen, et al., 2024; Ni, et al., 2023; Zhang, et al., 2025). Although numerous candidate genes related to egg production have been identified, most studies have focused on commercial breeds, and the regulatory mechanisms may vary significantly across different genetic backgrounds. For instance, Li et al. utilized expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) analysis to demonstrate that 28.9% of eGenes were breed-specific in three pig breeds, and that these breed-specific eGenes captured additional regulatory signals that could not be explained by breed-shared eGenes (Li, et al., 2026). Consequently, systematic identification of key candidate genes associated with egg production traits in indigenous chicken populations is of great significance for elucidating their unique molecular regulatory mechanisms and guiding molecular breeding programs.

The hypothalamic-pituitary-ovary (HPO) axis regulates the synthesis and secretion of reproductive hormones through precise positive and negative feedback mechanisms, serving as the core endocrine regulatory system that determines egg production performance in chickens. Hypothalamic neurons secrete Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH) in a pulsatile manner, stimulating the pituitary gland to synthesize and release Luteinizing Hormone (LH) and Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which in turn act upon the ovaries to regulate follicular development, the process of ovulation, and the synthesis of gonadal steroid hormones (Wang, et al., 2025). Beyond the classical regulation of the HPO axis, recent studies have demonstrated that several canonical signaling pathways and their key genes are also involved in the fine-tuning of the egg-laying process. Energy metabolism-related pathways (e.g., the AMPK signaling pathway and fatty acid synthesis and oxidation pathways) play fundamental regulatory roles in follicular development and yolk deposition. Specifically, genes such as PPARG, FASN, and SREBP1 influence ovarian function and egg production efficiency by modulating lipid metabolism (Cui, et al., 2022; Tian, et al., 2025; Weaver, et al., 2025). The insulin/insulin-like growth factor (IGF) signaling pathway is crucial for follicular cell proliferation and differentiation, where genes like IGF1 and INSR indirectly affect ovulation rhythm by regulating granulosa cell function (Hayes, et al., 2024). The transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β/SMAD) signaling pathway, a vital regulatory network for follicle development, involves members such as BMP15, GDF9, and SMAD4 that play key roles in follicle selection and dominant follicle formation (Bertani, et al., 2026). Collectively, egg production traits in chickens are not determined by a single pathway but are regulated by a complex molecular network involving the synergy of the HPO axis and various metabolic, developmental, and immune-related signaling pathways. Although certain key regulatory factors have been identified, their systematic molecular regulatory mechanisms across multiple tissues and levels remain to be further elucidated.

In recent years, multi-omics technologies—including genomics, transcriptomics, epigenomics, proteomics, and metabolomics—have been extensively applied to investigate the genetic mechanisms underlying complex economic traits in livestock and poultry (Choudhary, et al., 2024; Wadood, et al., 2025). Compared with single-omics analysis, multi-omics integration systematically captures regulatory information across different biological levels and effectively reduces information bias inherent in individual data layers. Consequently, constructing a molecular regulatory network (MRN) through multi-omics integration allows facilitating the dissection of the genetic basis of complex traits at a systems biology level. For instance, Shen et al. integrated genomic, epigenomic, 3D genomic, and transcriptomic data to construct a comprehensive 3D chromatin regulatory network map, and discovered that the variant rs734209466 remotely enhances the transcription of IGFBP2 and IGFBP5 by binding to the transcription factor IRF4, subsequently influencing abdominal fat deposition in chickens (Shen, et al., 2024). Similarly, Ding et al. integrated genomic, transcriptomic, and metabolomic data to identify 22 key genes affecting egg weight and revealed that rs315726522 may regulate the expression of MAP2K2 and FSHB by modulating transcription factor binding, thus influencing egg weight (Ding, et al., 2025). Multi-omics integration offers distinct advantages in identifying key candidate genes and constructing regulatory networks, providing a more systematic framework to analyze the genetic regulatory mechanisms of egg production traits in indigenous chickens.

Based on these considerations, the white-feathered green-shelled (WG) chicken line, self-bred by the Hangzhou Academy of Agricultural Sciences, was used as the experimental population in this study. This line was developed through crossbreeding and multi-generational directional selection of the Jiangshan silky and Dongxiang green-shelled chicken breeds. It possesses both the white-feather phenotype and green-shell quality characteristics, while exhibiting high egg production rates and superior egg quality. In this study, high-yielding and low-yielding individuals were selected based on egg numbers at 43 weeks (EN43). Tissues from the hypothalamus, pituitary, and ovary were collected for RNA-seq, and the ovarian tissues were further subjected to targeted metabolomics analysis. By integrating these multi-omics data, a molecular regulatory network (MRN) was constructed to systematically analyze the complex regulatory mechanisms of egg production traits from multi-tissue and multi-level perspectives. This research aims to provide a theoretical foundation for the molecular breeding of egg production traits in indigenous chickens.

Materials and methods

Animals and sample collection

This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Hangzhou Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Hangzhou, China), and all procedures were conducted in accordance with its guidelines for animal research.

The WG line population used in this study was raised at the Shuangjiang Base of the Hangzhou Academy of Agricultural Sciences. A total of 578 hens were transferred to a single closed poultry house at 12 weeks of age and housed in individual cages. Throughout the rearing period, the hens had ad libitum access to feed and water. Egg number was recorded daily for each hen from the onset of laying to 43 weeks of age, from which age at first egg (AFE) and total egg number at 43 weeks (EN43) were derived for each individual. Individuals ranked in the top 5% and bottom 5% for EN43 were defined as the high-yield (H) and low-yield (L) groups, respectively. Six healthy individuals were randomly selected from each group (n = 6 per group) and humanely slaughtered. Tissue samples from the hypothalamus, pituitary, and ovary were collected, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C for subsequent transcriptomic sequencing and targeted metabolomic determination.

mRNA sequencing and analysis

Total RNA extraction and RNA-seq library construction were performed by Wuhan Yingzi Gene Technology Co., Ltd. Total RNA was extracted using the Trizol method, and RNA purity was assessed using a NanoDrop ND-1000 (NanoDrop, USA). RNA integrity was assessed using the Agilent 5400 system (RIN: 7.1–9.4; 94% > 8.0), with quality metrics provided in Supplementary Table S3. Paired-end sequencing (read length of 150 bp) was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Raw data quality control was conducted using FASTP (v0.23.2) to filter low-quality sequences (Chen, et al., 2018). Clean reads were aligned and uniquely mapped to the reference chicken genome (GRCg7b) using HISAT2 (v2.2.1). Gene counts were quantified using featureCounts (v2.0.3) to generate a gene expression matrix for each sample(Kim, et al., 2015; Liao, et al., 2014). Transcripts with a total read count of less than 200 across all samples were excluded prior to downstream analysis. Normalization and differential expression analysis were performed using the DESeq2 (v1.20) R package (Love, et al., 2014). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified based on a threshold of adjusted P < 0.05 and |log2(fold change (FC))| > 1.

Targeted metabolite determination and analysis

Metabolite extraction and detection were performed by Wuhan Yingzi Gene Technology Co., Ltd. Briefly, thawed samples were pulverized, and the supernatant was extracted using methanol (Darmstadt, Germany) for subsequent LC-MS/MS analysis. A total of 43 steroid hormones in ovarian tissues were quantified using an Ultra-Performance Liquid Chromatography (UPLC) system coupled with Tandem Mass Spectrometry (MS/MS), following the protocol described by Yu et al. (Yu, et al., 2025). Steroid hormone levels were normalized using Z-score transformation. Differential abundance analysis was performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Differentially expressed steroid hormones (DESHs) were identified based on a threshold of P < 0.05 and |log2(FC)| > 0.58.

Molecular regulatory network (MRN) analysis

To elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms of egg production traits, a molecular regulatory network was constructed by integrating DEGs and DESHs. A Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) network of DEGs was generated using the STRING database, while a Gene-Metabolite Interaction (GMI) network between DEGs and DESHs was constructed through the MetaboAnalyst platform (Pang, et al., 2024; Szklarczyk, et al., 2021). The GMI and PPI networks were merged to form the MRN. A community clustering algorithm (GLay) implemented in Cytoscape was used to partition the network into modules. Small modules (containing < 10 genes and connected to only one large module) were merged, and invalid modules (not connected to DESHs or other modules) were filtered. The cytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape was used to calculate the Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC), Degree, and Betweenness for each node (Ono, et al., 2025). These parameters reveal distinct structural features: MCC measures local cohesiveness, Degree reflects overall connectivity, and Betweenness indicates the pivotal regulatory capacity of a node. After Min-Max normalization (0–1), a comprehensive score was calculated by summing these three values. The top 10% of genes in each module based on the comprehensive score were identified as hub genes.

RT-qPCR validation

To validate the RNA-seq data, five DEGs were selected from each tissue for RT-qPCR analysis. Primers were designed using Primer 6.0 (Table S1). Total RNA from 36 samples was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript RT kit (Takara, Dalian, China). RT-qPCR was performed using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). GAPDH served as housekeeping gene, and the 2^–ΔΔCT method was used to calculate the gene relative expression levels.

Functional enrichment analysis

To explore the biological roles of the HPO axis tissues and the regulatory effects of biological processes on ovarian hormones, KEGG pathway and Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analyses were performed using the KOBAS online tool (http://bioinfo.org/kobas) for DEGs from each tissue and each module (Bu, et al., 2021). Enrichment P-values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg method, and results with adjusted P < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

Statistical analysis

Basic statistical analysis, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, and principal component analysis (PCA) were performed using custom R scripts. Student’s t-test was used to compare the AFE and EN43 between the H and L groups, with significance set at P < 0.05. Data visualization was conducted using the R package ggplot2 (v3.5.1) and Cytoscape (v3.10.2).

Results

Phenotypic and targeted metabolomic analyses

Phenotypic analysis revealed a significant difference in AFE and EN43 between the H and L groups of the WG line (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1A). In the targeted metabolomic analysis, a total of 43 steroid hormones were targeted, and 27 were successfully detected. To ensure data reliability, steroid hormones with a missing rate exceeding 50% in both the H and L groups were excluded, leaving 17 steroid hormones for subsequent analysis (Table S2). Based on the Wilcoxon rank-sum test and differential abundance analysis, three steroid hormones were identified as significantly different between the two groups: progesterone, 17β-estradiol, and pregnenolone (Fig. 1B; Table S5).

DEGs identification and functional enrichment analysis

To investigate the DEGs in HPO axis tissues of the WG line, 36 cDNA libraries were constructed and sequenced. After quality control, approximately 240 Gb of high-quality sequencing data were obtained. An average of 44,980,274 clean reads was generated per sample, with an average GC content of 49.8%, a Q30 value of 96.8%, and an average alignment rate to the reference genome of 80.81% (Table S4). These metrics indicate that the transcriptome sequencing data were of high quality and suitable for subsequent analysis. The results of PCA showed that, within each HPO axis tissue, samples exhibited distinct separation between the H and L groups (Fig. 2A–C). The separation was most pronounced in the hypothalamic tissue, suggesting its pivotal role in the regulation of egg production traits (Fig. 2A). Differential expression analysis identified 666 DEGs (225 down-regulated, 441 up-regulated) in the hypothalamus, 149 DEGs (17 down-regulated, 132 up-regulated) in the pituitary, and 69 DEGs (52 down-regulated, 17 up-regulated) in the ovary (Fig. 2D–F). These results reflect tissue-specific transcriptional responses across the HPO axis during the regulation of egg production.

GO functional enrichment analysis was performed to further elucidate the potential biological functions of the identified DEGs. A total of 213, 27, and 43 GO terms were significantly enriched (P < 0.05) in the hypothalamus, pituitary, and ovary, respectively. In the hypothalamus, significantly enriched GO terms were primarily related to transcriptional regulation and neurofunctional processes, including RNA polymerase II cis-regulatory region sequence-specific DNA binding, positive regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II, synapse structure, neuron differentiation, neuropeptide signaling pathway, central nervous system development, and neuropeptide hormone activity, highlighting its core role in neuroendocrine regulation (Fig. 2G). In the pituitary, significantly enriched GO terms were mainly associated with neural signal transmission and substance transport, such as regulation of synaptic plasticity, calcium ion binding, RNA polymerase II-specific transcription factor activity, gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) transport, and transmembrane transport (Fig. 2H). In the ovary, significantly enriched GO terms were predominantly involved in signal transduction and ion regulation, including signal transduction, small molecule binding, negative/positive regulation of calcium ion transport, and the cGMP-mediated signaling pathway (Fig. 2I).

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis identified four significantly enriched pathways in the hypothalamus, and one each in the pituitary and ovary (Table 1). Key pathways closely related to egg production traits included Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, Calcium signaling pathway, Wnt signaling pathway, Focal adhesion, and Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction. The enrichment of these pathways across different tissues further underscores the critical roles of neural signaling, hormone secretion, and intercellular communication in regulating egg production performance in laying hens.

Construction and analysis of the MRN

To elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying egg production traits in indigenous chickens, an MRN was constructed. The MRN comprised 296 nodes and 564 edges. Using the community clustering algorithm, the MRN was partitioned into 32 molecular modules. After filtering and merging, four key regulatory modules (Module 1, 3, 4, and 5) associated with 17β-estradiol and progesterone were identified, containing 86, 12, 30, and 102 genes, respectively (Fig. 3A).

Functional enrichment analysis revealed that different modules subserve distinct biological functions in the regulation of egg production (Table 2). Module 1 was primarily enriched in neuroendocrine regulation processes, including the neuropeptide signaling pathway, neuropeptide hormone activity, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, and the calcium signaling pathway. Module 3 was mainly involved in neurotransmitter transport and vesicle secretion, such as neurotransmitter transport, SNARE binding, tyrosine metabolism, and SNARE interactions in vesicular transport. Module 4 was enriched in metabolism and microenvironment regulation, including the VEGF signaling pathway, long-chain fatty acid transport, MAPK signaling pathway, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, suggesting its involvement in ovarian nutrient supply and local regulation. Module 5 was associated with transcriptional regulation and developmental processes, encompassing positive regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II, RNA polymerase II-specific DNA-binding transcription factor activity, and the Wnt and mTOR signaling pathways.

To validate the reliability of the RNA-seq data, five DEGs from each tissue were selected for RT-qPCR analysis (hypothalamus: OXT, CBLN1, AVP, GPC3, and CALB1; pituitary: PER3, NES, ZIC2, MLIP, and MPZ; ovary: TSPAN1, PDE9A, SAA, PAK6, and ALK). Discussion

Steroid hormones play a central role in the regulation of egg production in laying hens (Yu, et al., 2025). We identified three DESHs in ovarian tissue of WG line individuals from the H and L groups: pregnenolone, progesterone, and 17β-estradiol. These DESHs occupy the upstream precursor, midstream intermediate, and downstream terminal effector positions, respectively, within the steroidogenesis pathway.

Pregnenolone, the common precursor for all steroid hormones, is synthesized from cholesterol by the catalysis of cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzyme (CYP11A1/P450scc), the rate-limiting step of steroidogenesis (Vallée, 2016; Wan, et al., 2026). In this study, the progesterone content in ovarian tissue of group H was significantly elevated, suggesting a higher efficiency in the initiation of steroid hormone synthesis. Progesterone is primarily synthesized and secreted by follicular granulosa cells, and serves as a key endocrine signal that induces the preovulatory LH surge and promotes ovulation (Nie, et al., 2024). The magnitude of progesterone upregulation in the H group was the largest among the three DESHs, suggesting that progesterone may serve as a key endocrine marker distinguishing high- from low-yield individuals. 17β-Estradiol participates in follicle recruitment, growth, and selection, and promotes hepatic synthesis of yolk precursors required for yolk deposition and oocyte maturation (Hanlon, et al., 2022). Previous studies have shown that high-yielding laying hens typically maintain elevated levels of 17β-estradiol during the laying period to support rapid follicular development and yolk deposition, which is consistent with the findings of the present study (Çiftci, 2017). The coordinated upregulation of the three DESHs in the H group reflects an overall enhancement of steroidogenic capacity in the ovarian tissue. To dissect the transcriptional mechanisms underlying these hormonal differences, we performed transcriptomic profiling across the three tissues of the HPO axis.

In the hypothalamus, 666 DEGs were identified and enriched in neuropeptide signaling, neuroactive ligand–receptor interaction, calcium signaling, and the Wnt pathway. Neuropeptides such as kisspeptin, neurokinin B (NKB), and dynorphin, released by KNDy neurons in the arcuate nucleus, regulate GnRH pulse generation through modulating intracellular calcium concentrations, while Wnt signaling affects GnRH neuron differentiation efficiency (Alhajeri, et al., 2022; Constantin, 2017; Delli, et al., 2026; Meng, et al., 2024; Wang, et al., 2022). Co-enrichment of these pathways indicates that the hypothalamus of H and L group individuals operates under distinct neuropeptide–calcium signaling states, and the resulting difference in GnRH release constitutes the first-tier input that regulates downstream steroid hormone synthesis. GnRH reaches the anterior pituitary via the hypophyseal portal system, triggering the second tier. In the pituitary, 149 DEGs were identified, enriched in GABA transport, calcium ion binding, astrocyte development, and extracellular matrix organization. GABA modulates LH/FSH release thresholds by altering pituitary cell membrane potential (Mayerhofer, et al., 2001). Intracellular calcium dynamics drive secretory vesicle exocytosis (Al-Suhaimi, et al., 2022). ECM remodeling influences intercellular communication and directional transport of secretory granules (Kumar, 2023; Liu, et al., 2025). Astrocytes modulate GnRH neuron responsiveness(Abbara, et al., 2024). The predominance of upregulated DEGs (89%) in the H group indicates enhanced functional activity of gonadotroph cells, with heightened sensitivity to GnRH stimulation: equivalent GnRH pulses elicit stronger LH/FSH secretion, thereby amplifying the steroidogenic drive transmitted to the ovary.

The amplified LH/FSH signal reaches the ovary and drives the third regulatory tier. In the ovary, 69 DEGs were identified, enriched in the cGMP signaling pathway, calcium homeostasis, and cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction. These three pathways influence steroidogenic efficiency at distinct levels: the cGMP pathway regulates CYP11A1 (P450scc) expression and activity, and CYP11A1 catalyzes the rate-limiting conversion of cholesterol to pregnenolone (Pei, et al., 2023). Calcium homeostasis genes regulate granulosa cell proliferation and steroidogenic enzyme expression (Chen, et al., 2024; Demczuk, et al., 2016). Cytokine signals shape the follicular microenvironment through paracrine and autocrine regulation of granulosa–oocyte interactions (Huang, et al., 2022; Li, et al., 2023). The enrichment pattern indicates systematic differences between H and L group individuals in granulosa cell function, follicular microenvironment, and steroidogenic efficiency, which jointly affect hormone output and egg production performance.

The DEG enrichment pattern described above delineates a three-tier signal relay along the HPO axis. However, the co-enrichment of multiple pathways within each tissue, together with the functional coupling across tissues, indicates that egg production is not governed by a single gene or linear pathway but by multi-tissue, multi-pathway cooperation. To capture this lateral coordination, we constructed an MRN (296 nodes, 564 edges) integrating DEGs and DESHs, which was partitioned by community clustering into four modules with distinct functional features.

Module 1 contained 86 genes enriched in neuropeptide signaling and calcium signaling pathways. Nine hub genes were identified; all except TH were upregulated in the hypothalamus of the H group. OXT, whose avian ortholog mesotocin is a key regulator of oviposition, showed marked upregulation, suggesting enhanced mesotocin-mediated oviduct contraction and egg expulsion efficiency (Chokchaloemwong, et al., 2013). Sustained high-frequency oviposition requires granulosa cells to maintain robust progesterone synthesis to trigger the preovulatory LH surge (Bernardi, et al., 2024). NPY, the most potent orexigenic peptide in birds, stimulates GnRH and LH secretion via the median eminence, and LH is the core upstream signal driving granulosa cell synthesis of progesterone and 17β-estradiol (Muroi, et al., 2016). TRH upregulation elevates basal metabolic rate by promoting thyroid hormone secretion, providing the energetic basis for sustained cholesterol mobilization and steroidogenesis in the ovary (De Groef, et al., 2005; Stincic, et al., 2022). TH encodes tyrosine hydroxylase, the rate-limiting enzyme for dopamine synthesis (Lehrer, et al., 2022). Its downregulation in the H group reduces dopamine–prolactin (PRL) pathway-mediated inhibition of GnRH, permitting sustained LH/FSH secretion and uninterrupted progesterone and 17β-estradiol synthesis. Module 1 thus constitutes the first and most upstream signaling layer that regulates downstream steroid hormone synthesis.

Module 3 contained 12 genes enriched in neurotransmitter transport, the SNARE complex, and tyrosine metabolism. Both hub genes, SLC18A2 and SLC18A3, were downregulated in the hypothalamus of the H group. They encode the vesicular monoamine transporter (VMAT2) and vesicular acetylcholine transporter (VAChT), respectively, which load neurotransmitters such as dopamine into synaptic vesicles; tyrosine metabolism supplies precursors for dopamine synthesis (Daubner, et al., 2011; Lawal, et al., 2013). The concurrent downregulation of SLC18A2 and SLC18A3 likely reduces dopamine-mediated inhibitory input, thereby disinhibiting GnRH neurons and promoting ovarian steroidogenesis. Module 3 thus functions primarily as a modulator of neurotransmitter transmission patterns and signal gain.

Module 4 contained 30 genes enriched in long-chain fatty acid transport, the VEGF signaling pathway, the MAPK signaling pathway, and cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction. Its hub genes exhibited tissue-specific differential expression: CD36 was downregulated in the hypothalamus, EGF upregulated in the pituitary, and ALB upregulated in the ovary. CD36, a key receptor for long-chain fatty acid uptake and sensing, showed reduced expression, suggesting altered central energy perception in the H group that may facilitate the energy intake and metabolic supply required for egg production (Tao, et al., 2025). EGF promotes ovarian granulosa cell proliferation via the MAPK pathway while enhancing the responsiveness of pituitary gonadotrophs to GnRH pulses, serving as a critical amplification node that couples central GnRH signaling with peripheral gonadal function (Wu, et al., 2019). ALB, the principal carrier protein for lipophilic steroid hormones, was upregulated in the H group ovary, potentially enhancing the transport and utilization efficiency of progesterone and 17β-estradiol in follicular fluid and the local tissue environment (Hammond, 2016). The VEGF pathway participates in ovarian angiogenesis, regulating the supply of oxygen, nutrients, and hormone precursors to follicles (Guzmán, et al., 2023). Module 4 thus integrates energy sensing, granulosa cell proliferation, and microenvironmental regulation to coordinately drive ovarian steroidogenesis.

Module 5 hub genes also displayed tissue-specific expression patterns. TBR1, FOXG1, FGF8, ISL1, WNT3A, CALB1, and PAX6 were upregulated in the H group hypothalamus, whereas FOXA2 was downregulated; concurrently, OLIG2, SOX10, and GFAP were upregulated in the H group pituitary. Most of these genes are recognized as key developmental transcription factors, suggesting that the HPO axis of the H group may enhance its central drive for steroidogenesis through coordinated "hypothalamic neural remodeling–pituitary glial activation". FGF8, a critical regulator of GnRH neuron development and functional maintenance, showed sustained high expression, which may contribute to the stability of the hypothalamic reproductive neural network (Wilmerding, et al., 2022). The coordinated upregulation of FOXG1, TBR1, ISL1, PAX6, and WNT3A likely reflects enhanced neuronal transcriptional programs, synaptic connectivity, and cellular plasticity (Ba, et al., 2023; Liao, et al., 2022). CALB1 upregulation indicates elevated neuronal calcium signaling activity, which facilitates the vesicular release and secretion of neuropeptides such as kisspeptin (Vastagh, et al., 2019). In contrast, FOXA2 regulates the differentiation and functional specialization of ventral hypothalamic KNDy neurons (Yu, et al., 2022). Its downregulation in the H group may reflect functional remodeling of hypothalamic neuronal populations to better accommodate the sustained reproductive endocrine output required for high egg production. In the pituitary, the upregulation of OLIG2, SOX10, and GFAP indicates enhanced glial activation. GFAP is an astrocyte marker; SOX10 and OLIG2 participate in glial differentiation and functional maintenance (Huang, et al., 2026; Jurga, et al., 2021; Wang, et al., 2025). Pituitary glial cells can modulate gonadotroph sensitivity to GnRH and LH/FSH release, thereby contributing to reproductive regulation (Prevot, et al., 2022). Thus, enhanced glial support in the H group may improve pituitary responsiveness to GnRH, providing a more stable gonadotropin drive for follicular development. Module 5 reveals that hypothalamic neural remodeling and pituitary glial activation act in concert, potentially enhancing GnRH–LH/FSH signaling efficiency and promoting ovarian steroidogenesis. These findings highlight the role of neurodevelopmental genes in maintaining HPO axis function and offer a new molecular perspective on central regulatory mechanisms underlying egg production performance in poultry.

In summary, by integrating multi-omics data, this study constructed a multi-level, multi-pathway molecular regulatory network that progressively transmits from central neuroendocrine regulation to peripheral steroid hormone synthesis with coordinated interactions. This work systematically revealed the underlying molecular mechanisms contributing to the differences in egg-laying performance between high-yielding and low-yielding WG lines, providing a theoretical basis and candidate gene resources for molecular breeding of egg-laying traits.

Conclusion

This study systematically elucidated the hierarchical molecular regulatory network underlying egg production in indigenous chickens by integrating targeted metabolomics and transcriptomics. We identified pregnenolone, progesterone, and 17β-estradiol as pivotal steroid hormones driving production differences. Our findings reveal a tissue-specific functional partitioning within the HPO axis: the hypothalamus initiates neuroendocrine signaling, the pituitary integrates these signals and modulates secretion, and the ovary executes steroidogenesis and follicular development. Modular analysis of the MRN further highlights that egg production is governed by the synergistic coordination of neuropeptide signaling, neurotransmitter transport, and lipid metabolism. Furthermore, 25 genes were identified from the MRN as primary candidates for modulating laying performance. These results provide a robust theoretical foundation and essential candidate genes for the molecular breeding and genetic improvement of egg production traits in poultry.
