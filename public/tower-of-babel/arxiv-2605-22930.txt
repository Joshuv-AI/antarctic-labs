Sharp Bohr-Type inequalities for certain classes of close-to-convex functions
Shalini Rana, Naveen Kumar Jain*

Abstract
. In this article, we determine the Rogosinski radii for certain subclasses of close-to-convex functions defined on open unit disc 𝔻 = { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } \mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\} . Furthermore, we establish improved versions of the classical Bohr inequality and the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality pertaining to these subclasses. We demonstrate that all results derived in the study are sharp.

30C45, 30C50, 30C80

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the class of analytic functions
g
with the Taylor series expansion
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. The classical Bohr inequality
[
8
]
says that if
g\in\mathcal{A}
and
g
maps the unit disc into itself, then

(1.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}

and the radius
1/3
cannot be improved. The radius
1/3
is known as Bohr radius, while the inequality (
1.1
) is known as Bohr inequality. Initially, the inequality (
1.1
) was established by Harald Bohr for radius
r\leq 1/6
and later Weiner, Riesz and Schur obtained the inequality for
r\leq 1/3
. It is easy to see that
(1-|a_{0}|)
is equal to
d(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D}))
, where
d
is the Euclidean distance and
\partial g(\mathbb{D})
is the boundary of
g(\mathbb{D})
. Therefore, the Bohr inequality can be re-written in the following Euclidean distance form

(1.2)

d\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|,|a_{0}|\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|=d(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})).

The inequality (
1.2
) can be generalized to class
\mathcal{G}
consisting of functions
g
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
g(\mathbb{D})\subset\Delta
, where
\Delta
is any given domain. The class
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the Bohr phenomenon if there exists largest
r_{\Delta}\in(0,1)
such that

d\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|,|a_{0}|\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq d(g(0),\partial\Delta)

holds for all
|z|=r\leq r_{\Delta}
and for all the functions
g\in\mathcal{G}
. It is interestingly
enough, for the convex domain
\Delta
, the radius
r_{\Delta}=1/3
satisfying (
1.2
) coincides with Bohr radius
[
4
]
. The Bohr radius has been obtained for the class
\mathcal{G}
when
\Delta
has positive real part
[
5
]
and Janowski starlike
[
7
]
(see also
[
3
]
). Several Bohr radii have been obtained for the classes of analytic functions
g
satisfying the differential
subordination
g(z)+\beta zg^{\prime}(z)+\gamma z^{2}g^{\prime\prime}(z)\prec k(z)
, when the function
k
is convex and starlike
[
1
]
, convex and starlike of order
\alpha

[
12
]
and Janowski starlike
[
7
]
.

The Bohr inequality has emerged as an active research area for operator algebraists after Dixon
[
10
]
established a connection between the inequality and the characterization of Banach Algebra that satisfies Von Neumann inequality. Improved versions of Bohr inequality for various classes of analytic functions becomes now a days an active research area. Kayumov et al.
[
16
]
obtained several different improved versions of the classical Bohr inequality. Recently, Ahamed and Ahammed
[
2
]
obtain sharp refined Bohr–Rogosinski-type
inequalities for the subordination class of univalent and convex function. In 2021, Liu et al.
[
17
]
generalized and improved several Bohr-type inequalities for bounded analytic functions. Recently, Rana and Jain
[
21
]
has established several improved Bohr-type inequalities for starlike functions of order
\alpha
, functions whose derivative has positive real part and the functions convex in the direction of imaginary axis, see also
[
19
,
11
,
18
,
9
]
.

In 1952, Kaplan
[
13
]
introduced the class of close-to-convex functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying the condition

(1.3)

Re\left(\frac{zf^{{}^{\prime}}}{g}\right)>0\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
g
is a function starlike with respect to origin of the form

(1.4)

g(z)=b_{1}z+...Re(b_{1})>0.

The class is denoted by
\mathcal{C}
. Later, Silverman and Telage
[
22
]
introduced the three subclasses,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
\mathcal{C}_{3}
of class
\mathcal{C}
.
A function
f
is said to be in the class
\mathcal{C}_{1}
if there exists a convex function
g
of the form (
1.4
) such that (
1.3
) is satisfied.
A function
f
is said to be in the class
\mathcal{C}_{2}
if there exists a convex function
g
of the form (
1.4
) satisfying

Re\left(\frac{(zf^{{}^{\prime}})^{{}^{\prime}}}{g^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)>0\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

A function
f
is said to be in the class
\mathcal{C}_{3}
if

Re\left(\frac{z((zf^{{}^{\prime}})^{{}^{\prime}})^{{}^{\prime}}}{(zg^{{}^{\prime}})^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)>0\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
g
is a convex function of the form (
1.4
). They proved that
\mathcal{C}_{3}\subset\mathcal{C}_{2}\subset\mathcal{C}_{1}\subset\mathcal{C}
.
In 2017,
Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
14
]
introduced and found the following Bohr-Rogosinski inequality and Bohr–Rogosinski radius for the class of functions mapping unit disc to unit disc.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
14
]

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then

|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}r^{n}|\leq 1\quad\text{for}\,|z|=r\leq R_{N},

where
R_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
2(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
. The radius
R_{N}
is the best possible. Moreover,

|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}r^{n}|\leq 1\quad\text{for}\,|z|=r\leq R^{\prime}_{N},

where
R^{\prime}_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
. The radius
R^{\prime}_{N}
is the best possible.

For the results on Bohr-Rogosinski inequality, we refer to the articles
[
6
,
15
,
20
,
19
]
.

In the present work, we extend this line of research by determining the sharp Bohr–Rogosinski radius for the classes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
\mathcal{C}_{3}
together with improved
versions of the classical Bohr’s inequality . In section 2, section 3, and section 4, we find the sharp Bohr–Rogosinski radius and its various versions for the classes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
\mathcal{C}_{3}
respectively.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}

(2.4)

|f(z)|+|f^{{}^{\prime}}(z)||z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})

is the solution of

\log 2-1-r(-6+r(2+r-\log 2)+\log 4)+2(1-r)^{2}\log(1-r)=0.

Proof.

We begin the proof by evaluating

From growth estimate of

d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})=\liminf\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 1}|f(z)-f(0)|\geq\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 1}\left(\frac{2r}{1+r}-\log(1+r)\right)

which gives

(2.5)

For

, by
(\ref{coef1})

, we have

(2.6)

|f(z)|+|f^{{}^{\prime}}(z)||z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq\frac{2r}{1-r}+\log(1-r)+\frac{r(1+r)}{(1-r)^{2}}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)r^{n}

Define a function

by

P(r)=\log 2-1-r(-6+r(2+r-\log 2)+\log 4)+2(1-r)^{2}\log(1-r).

It can be easily seen that
P(0)=\log 2-1\simeq-0.306853<0
and
P(1/2)={11}/{8}-{\log 2}/{4}\simeq 1.20171>0
. Therefore by intermediate value theorem
P(r)
has a root in
(0,1/2)
. Next to show the uniqueness of root in
(0,1/2)
, it is sufficient to show that the function
P
is a strictly increasing function in
(0,1/2)

It is easy to see that
P^{\prime}(r)=(2+6r-5r^{2}+r^{3})/{(1-r)^{3}}>(2+6r-5r+r^{3})/{(1-r)^{3}}=(2+r+r^{3})/{(1-r)^{3}}>0
for all

, which shows that
P
is strictly increasing function. Therefore,
P(r)
has the unique root in
(0,1/2)
, say
r_{11}=0.110377
.
Thus,

(2.7)

P(r_{11})=\log 2-1-r_{11}(-6+r_{11}(2+r_{11}-\log 2)+\log 4)+2(1-r_{11})^{2}\log(1-r_{11})=0

and
P(r)\leq 0
for

.
In view of the above argument together with (

) and (

), for

\displaystyle|f(z)|+

\displaystyle|f^{{}^{\prime}}(z)||z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}-d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})

\displaystyle\leq 0

Note that the power series expansion of

is given by

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)z^{n}.

At

\displaystyle|f(z)|+|f^{{}^{\prime}}(z)||z|+

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}

Next, by considering powers of the coefficient
(|a_{n}|)
, we evaluate the corresponding sharp Bohr radii for the Bohr inequality for class
\mathcal{C}_{1}

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
, then

(2.8)

|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}|z|^{np}\leq d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})

for
|z|\leq r_{p}
and
p\geq 1
, where
r_{p}
is the solution of

(1-r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p}r^{pn}-(1-3r+r\log 2-\log(2-2r)+r\log(1-r))=0.

The result is sharp.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, it follows that

(2.9)

d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})=\liminf\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 1}|f(z)-f(0)|\geq\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 1}\left(\frac{2r}{1+r}-\log(1+r)\right)

which gives
d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})\geq(1-\log{2})
for
f\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
. By Lemma
2.1
, for
|z|\leq r
and
p\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}|z|^{np}\leq r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)r^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p}r^{pn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p}r^{pn}-\frac{(1-3r+r\log 2-\log(2-2r)+r\log(1-r))}{1-r}.

Let
Q
be a function defined by

\displaystyle Q(r)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p}r^{pn}-\frac{1-3r+r\log 2-\log(2-2r)+r\log(1-r)}{1-r},\quad r\in[0,1).

Since,
Q(0)=-(1-\log 2)<0
and

Q(1/2)=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p}\frac{1}{2^{pn}}>0

by the Intermediate Value Theorem it follows that
Q(r)=0
has a root in
(0,1/2)
.
The uniqueness of the root
r
in
(0,1/2)
follows from the fact that the derivative of the function

Q^{\prime}(r)=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)nr^{n-1}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p}pnr^{pn-1}>0.

Thus,

\frac{2r+(1-r)\log(1-r)}{1-r}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p}r^{pn}\leq(1-\log{2}).

for
r\leq r_{p}
and
p\geq 1
, where
r_{p}
is the solution of the equation
Q(r)=0
in
(0,1).

To prove the sharpness, we assume

(2.10)

f(z)=\frac{2z}{(1+z)}-\log(1+z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)z^{n}.

For
z=r=r_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}|z|^{np}

\displaystyle=|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|(-1)^{n+1}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)z^{n}\right|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}{\left|(-1)^{n+1}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)\right|}^{p}|z|^{np}

\displaystyle=|r|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)r^{n}\right|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}{\left|\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)\right|}^{p}|r|^{np}

\displaystyle=r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)r^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}{\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)}^{p}r^{np}

\displaystyle=\frac{2r+(1-r)\log(1-r)}{1-r}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}{\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)}^{p}r^{np}

\displaystyle=1-\log 2

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 1}\left(\frac{2r}{1+r}-\log(1+r)\right)

\displaystyle=d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})

Hence, the result is sharp.
∎

Next, we state the second result determining the sharp Bohr-Rogosinski radius for the class
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
N\geq 2
be an integer. Then

(2.13)

|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})

for
|z|\leq r_{2,N}
, where
r_{2,N}
is the solution of

\left(\frac{2r}{1-r}+\log(1-r)\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)r^{n}-(1-\log{2})=0.

The result is sharp.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, for
|z|\leq r
,
we have

|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq\left(\frac{2r}{1-r}+\log(1-r)\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)r^{n}.

To find the radius
r
satisfying the inequality

|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})\quad(|z|\leq r),

we define the function
S:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

S(r)=\left(\frac{2r}{1-r}+\log(1-r)\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)r^{n}-(1-\log{2}).

We claim that
S(r)\leq 0
for
r\leq r_{2,N}
, where
r_{2,N}
is the solution of the equation

(2.14)

\left(\frac{2r}{1-r}+\log(1-r)\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)r^{n}-(1-\log{2})=0.

An easy calculation shows that
S(0)=\log 2-1<0
and

S\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=4+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{2^{n}}-(1-\log{2})\geq 4-(1-\log 2)=1+\log 2>0

Clearly, by the Intermediate Value Theorem, there exists a root
r\in(0,1/2)
of the equation
S(r)=0
. Note that, for
r\in(0,1/2)
,

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{(1-r)^{2}}-\frac{1}{1-r}\right)^{2}+\frac{2r^{N-1}(N+r-Nr)}{(-1+r)^{2}}-\frac{r^{N-1}}{1-r}

\frac{(3-r)r}{3(1-r)}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{3n^{2}}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)^{p}r^{np}\leq\frac{1}{36}\left(12+\pi^{2}\right).

Now, we define a function
Q:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

Q(r)=\frac{(3-r)r}{3(1-r)}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{3n^{2}}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)^{p}r^{np}-\frac{1}{36}\left(12+\pi^{2}\right).

An easy calculation shows that

Q(0)=-\frac{1}{36}\left(12+\pi^{2}\right)<0

and

\displaystyle Q\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{(\log 2)^{2}}{6}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2^{-n}}{3n^{2}}\right)^{p}\frac{1}{2^{np}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\left(2-(\log 2)^{2}\right)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2^{-n}}{3n^{2}}\right)^{p}\frac{1}{2^{np}}

\displaystyle>0.

Since
Q(0).Q(1/2)<0
, by Intermediate Value Theorem, there exists a root
r\in(0,1/2)
satisfying the equation
Q(r)=0
. Moreover,

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{3-2r+r^{2}}{3(1-r)^{2}}-\frac{r+\log(1-r)}{3r}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)^{p}{np}r^{{np}-1}>0.

Thus, there exists the unique root, say
R_{p}\in(0,1/2)
satisfying the equation (
4.11
).
In order to prove the sharpness, consider the function

f(z)=\frac{2z}{3(1-z)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\log(1-\zeta)}{\zeta}d\zeta.

At
z=r=R_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}|z|^{np}

\displaystyle=|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)z^{n}\right|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)\right|^{p}|z|^{np}

\displaystyle=r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)r^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)^{p}r^{np}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt

\displaystyle=d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})}).

Hence, the result is sharp.
∎

We find Bohr-Rogosinski radius for the class
\mathcal{C}_{3}
in next two theorems.

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose that
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{3}
, then

(4.14)

|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})

for
|z|\leq R_{1,N}
, where
R_{1,N}
is the solution of

\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{3n^{2}}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt-\frac{12+\pi^{2}-36r-\pi^{2}r-24r^{N}}{36(1-r)}=0.

The result is sharp.

Proof.

Using Lemma
4.1
and growth estimate (
4.2
), for
|z|\leq r
, we get

|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)r^{n}.

It follows from the above inequality that the equation (
4.14
) holds if and only if

\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(1+t)}{t}dt.

In order to find the radius
R_{1,N}\in(0,1)
satisfying the inequality
(\ref{2.3.3})
, we define the function
R:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

R(r)=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{3n^{2}}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt-\frac{12+\pi^{2}-36r-\pi^{2}r-24r^{N}}{36(1-r)}.

It is easy to see that

R(0)=-1/3-\pi^{2}/36<0
and

\displaystyle R\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)+\frac{1}{36}\left(\pi^{2}-6(\log 2)^{2}\right)+\frac{2}{3}\left(1+2^{1-N}\right)-\frac{1}{36}\left(12+\pi^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)+\frac{1}{6}\left(2+2^{3-N}-(\log 2)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle>0.

In view of the above and using Intermediate Value Theorem it follows that there exists a root
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
such that
R(\varepsilon)=0.

Since

R^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2}{3(1-r)^{2}}-\frac{\log(1-r)}{3r}+\frac{2r^{N-1}(N+r-Nr)}{3(1-r)^{2}}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{3n}>0,

there exists the unique
R_{1,N}\in(0,1/2)
such that
R(R_{1,N})=0.

Thus, equation
(\ref{2.3.3})
holds for
r\leq R_{1,N}
, where
R_{1,N}
is the solution of the equation

\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)r^{n}-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(1+t)}{t}dt=0.

To prove the sharpness, we assume

f(z)=\frac{2z}{3(1-z)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\log(1-\zeta)}{\zeta}d\zeta.

At
z=r=R_{1,N}
, we have

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{2z}{3(1-z)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt\right|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left|\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)\right||z|^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt

\displaystyle=d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})}).

Hence, the result is sharp.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose that
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{3}
, then

(4.15)

|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq d(f(0),\partial{f(\mathbb{D})})

for
|z|\leq R_{2,N}
, where
R_{2,N}
is the solution of

\left(\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{3n^{2}}+\frac{24r^{N}+(12+\pi^{2})r-12-\pi^{2}}{36(1-r)}=0.

The result is sharp.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
that

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{3n^{2}}+\frac{2r^{N}}{3(1-r)}

for
|z|\leq r
.

Define a function
S:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

S(r)=\left(\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\right)r^{n}-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(1+t)}{t}dt.

Note that

S(0)=-{1}/{3}-{\pi^{2}}/{36}
.
Also, an easy calculation yields

\displaystyle S(1/2)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3n^{2}2^{n}}+\frac{2^{2-N}}{3}-\frac{1}{36}\left(12+\pi^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(24+\pi^{2}-6(\log 2)^{2}\right)^{2}}{1296}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3n^{2}2^{n}}+\frac{2^{2-N}}{3}-\frac{1}{36}\left(12+\pi^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(24+\pi^{2}-6(\log 2)^{2}\right)^{2}}{1296}-\frac{1}{36}\left(12+\pi^{2}\right)+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3n^{2}2^{n}}+\frac{2^{2-N}}{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{144+12\pi^{2}+\pi^{4}-288(\log 2)^{2}-12\pi^{2}(\log 2)^{2}+36(\log 2)^{4}}{1296}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3n^{2}2^{n}}+\frac{2^{2-N}}{3}

\displaystyle>0.

In view of the above,
S(0)S(1/2)<0
, and by the Intermediate Value Theorem there exists a root
\zeta\in(0,1/2)
such that
S(r)\leq 0
for
r\leq\zeta
. Furthermore, Since

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{2r}{3(1-r)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}dt\right)\left(\frac{2}{3(1-r)^{2}}-\frac{\log(1-r)}{3r}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2r^{-1+N}(N+r-Nr)}{3(1-r)^{2}}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{3n}

\displaystyle>0,

there exists the unique
R_{2,N}\in(0,1/2)
such that the inequality
(\ref{2.3.4})
holds for
r\leq R_{2,N}
, and satisfying the equation
S(R_{2,N})=0.

The result is sharp for the function

f(z)=\frac{2z}{3(1-z)}-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\log(1-\zeta)}{\zeta}d\zeta.

∎
