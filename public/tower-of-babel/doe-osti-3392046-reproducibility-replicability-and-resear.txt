Reproducibility, Replicability, and Research Quality in
 Homogeneous Catalysis

 Executive Summary
Researchers from all sectors of homogeneous catalysis convened in response to concerns regarding
reproducibility in science to analyze the issue and provide recommendations. In addition to an in-
person workshop, the group engaged the broader homogeneous catalysis community through a
webinar series and virtually during the workshop. Results of the project affirm that homogeneous
catalysis is not in a reproducibility crisis, as evidenced by the field’s current and past contributions
to society that have led to economic growth and advances in a range of industries from agriculture
to consumer goods to human health. However, it is not uncommon for researchers to encounter
obstacles related to reproducibility. Ensuring reproducibility remains the responsibility of the
community, both in current work and in training future researchers. This report is intended to
engage key stakeholders, including disciplinary societies, publishers, employers, research leaders,
and researchers, in practices that maximize reproducible homogeneous catalysis and ensure
continued innovation and translatable discoveries. Recommendations made herein are also framed
to be applicable beyond homogeneous catalysis, empowering the broader chemical if not scientific
community.

 Front matter
Context
This report was initiated in response to the National Science Foundation Dear Colleague Letter
“Reproducibility and Replicability in Science,” publication number NSF 23-018 dated October 25,
2022. That call was part of a larger effort among federal agencies that support scientific research
to proactively protect the highly successful enterprise of domestic scientific research. In 2019, the
National Academies consensus study report “Reproducibility and Replicability in Science” was
released. While that report has a primary focus on the increased computational and digital aspects
of scientific research, the ideas and recommendations are broadly applicable. These national efforts
came at a time when several high-profile examples of irreproducibility in science were circulating
in mainstream media. With this backdrop, the core members of this group responded to the Dear
Colleague Letter recognizing that current and future domestic leadership in homogeneous catalysis
is predicated on high-quality, including reproducible, research.

As this project developed, colleagues in the heterogeneous catalysis community had convened a
similar workshop to consider related issues in that field. The result of that work, a report titled
“Addressing Rigor and Reproducibility in Thermal, Heterogeneous Catalysis” provides specific
guidelines at the heart of the heterogeneous catalysis community.1 The homogeneous catalysis
community has a broad scope for which there are very few common experimental methods or
procedures. That fact informed the approach to this project and report, which focuses on the most
uniform activities that will support reproducibility in homogeneous catalysis by addressing the
most common potential sources of irreproducibility and making suggestions toward reasonable
expectations of experimental rigor. Many of these ideas extend to other areas of chemistry and
other sciences, and it was the aim of the workshop participants to provide content that is as broadly
applicable as possible.

Structure of the Project
A successful workshop typically has an optimal number of participants to bring a range of
perspectives and engage in meaningful dialog to address the issues at hand. Given the size of the
homogeneous catalysis community, a workshop alone would not fully capture the community’s
thinking. Therefore, this project was executed in three major parts: a series of webinars were

Webinars
Two webinars were held in which speakers provided some context for issues of reproducibility
related to homogeneous catalysis. Community attendees engaged in robust discussion after the
presentations, and the content of the presentations and discussion informed workshop activities.

Workshop
A group of 50 participants selected by application convened in Denver during January 5–7, 2026.
Workshop participants used break-out sessions to devise and discuss key issues in the topic area.
Once issues were defined, solutions and other opportunities were identified. These concepts were
elaborated upon during the meeting. Some of this report was drafted on site, and the majority of
the draft was completed after the in-person event.
External Participation
Individuals not selected to participate at the in-person workshop, webinar registrants, and others
who contacted organizers were invited to digitally provide feedback during the workshop. Several
topic areas were shared digitally with feedback on each solicited from the community.
