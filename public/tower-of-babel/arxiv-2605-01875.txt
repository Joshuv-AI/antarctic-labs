Large-Data Global Regularity for Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes I: A Direct First-Threshold Continuation Proof for the Axisymmetric Swirl Class
Rishad Shahmurov

Abstract
. This is the first paper in a two-part direct-threshold series on large-data global regularity for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations. We prove a direct first-threshold continuation theorem for the axisymmetric class with swirl. The proof is written entirely in the lifted variables Γ = r ​ u θ , G = ω θ / r , d ​ μ 5 = r 3 ​ d ​ r ​ d ​ z , \Gamma=ru_{\theta},\qquad G=\omega_{\theta}/r,\qquad d\mu_{5}=r^{3}\,dr\,dz, and uses the five-dimensional full-Dirichlet visibility 𝒱 χ \mathcal{V}_{\chi} as the local coercive quantity. The argument is organized by a finite first-threshold stopping time. We define a critical axis score envelope, follow it to a first possible threshold time, and prove that the corresponding normalized packet cannot exist. The proof has three quantitative ingredients. First, a small-envelope continuation theorem converts bounded score and regularized source size into smooth continuation. Second, a finite-overlap descendant-extraction theorem shows that every large collar leakage, exterior tail, low-frequency residue, source concentration, or fragmentation channel either produces a smaller descendant packet or is perturbative. Third, in the remaining coherent case, the strict full-Dirichlet bridge | 𝒯 G , χ ​ [ G ] | ≤ θ ​ 𝒱 χ ​ [ G ] + C ​ 𝔈 dir ​ [ G ] , 0 < θ < 1 , |\mathcal{T}_{G,\chi}[G]|\leq\theta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]+C\mathfrak{E}_{\rm dir}[G],\qquad 0<\theta<1, and a coefficient-calibrated local balance contract the selected packet. Consequently no first threshold occurs, the critical envelope stays finite on every bounded time interval, and the classical continuation criterion gives global smoothness. The local mechanisms used by the direct continuation proof–the strict bridge, the source-to-score conversion, the finite-overlap extraction, and the finite-threshold contraction–are stated in the precise forms used in the argument and proved in the text. The two most delicate points are the constrained Ekeland–Pohozaev strictness argument and the sign-adapted derivative-source selection; these are isolated in separate lemmas, with the compactness, admissibility, projected-variation, source-error, and continuation estimates written out below. The companion Part II paper uses the theorem proved here only after its full three-dimensional front end has reduced a first-threshold packet to the exact axisymmetric-with-swirl compatibility class.

Basic notation, geometry, analytic inputs, and proof vocabulary

This section fixes the notation used throughout the paper and records the standard analytic inputs used later. 1.2.
The lifted equation and its nonlocal recovery

The nonlocal recovered component used in the transfer channel is

U[G]:=-\partial_{z}(-\Delta_{5})^{-1}G.

The regularized source variable
\Gamma_{e}
is chosen so that the singular swirl forcing is represented in lifted form by

(1)

r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2})=\partial_{z}(\Gamma_{e}^{2}).

Thus the lifted
G
-equation has the form

(2)

\partial_{t}G+u\cdot\nabla_{5}G=\Delta_{5}G+\partial_{z}(\Gamma_{e}^{2}).

Here
u\cdot\nabla_{5}
denotes the meridional transport written in the lifted
(r,z)
-geometry.

In the lifted coordinates
X=(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}
, the five-dimensional Newtonian potential is

\Phi_{5}(X)=c_{5}|X|^{-3},\qquad-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\Phi_{5}=\delta_{0}.

For an
SO(4)
-radial source
F(r,z)
,

(-\Delta_{5})^{-1}F=\Phi_{5}*F.

The kernel of
U
is

K_{U}(X)=-\partial_{z}\Phi_{5}(X)=3c_{5}\frac{z}{|X|^{5}},

up to a harmless dimensional constant. It has homogeneity
-4
, is odd in the
z
-variable, and may be written as
U=R_{z}I_{1}
, where
R_{z}
is the
z
-Riesz transform and
I_{1}=(-\Delta_{5})^{-1/2}
. Hence the fixed-time estimates

\|U[F]\|_{L^{5/2}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{5/3}(\mathbb{R}^{5})},\qquad\|U[F]\|_{L^{10}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{10/3}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}

follow from Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev and Calderon–Zygmund theory. These estimates are used after localization at each fixed time and then integrated in time.

1.3.
Score, packets, visibility, and transfer

Definition 1.1

(Axis score)
.

The corrected axis score at scale
\rho
is

\mathcal{Q}_{\rho}(z_{0},t):=\rho\int_{B_{\rho}^{\rm axis}(z_{0})}|G(r,z,t)|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}.

A high score means that the lifted vorticity has scale-invariant concentration at the scale and center under consideration. The normalization is the one used by the first-threshold selection procedure.

Definition 1.2

(Parabolic packet)
.

A parabolic packet consists of a center
(z_{0},t_{0})
, a scale
\rho
, a backward axis cylinder
Q_{\rho}^{-}(z_{0},t_{0})
, and a cutoff adapted to
B_{2\rho}^{\rm axis}(z_{0})
and equal to one on
B_{\rho}^{\rm axis}(z_{0})
. After parabolic rescaling, the active packet is placed in normalized coordinates

(-1,0]\times B_{1}^{\rm axis},\qquad\operatorname{supp}\chi\subset B_{2}^{\rm axis},\qquad\chi\equiv 1\text{ on }B_{1}^{\rm axis}.

Geometrically, the packet is the spacetime window through which a possible concentration of
G
is examined.

Definition 1.3

(Full-Dirichlet visibility and localized transfer)
.

The full-Dirichlet visibility is

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]:=\iint\chi^{2}|\nabla_{5}G|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}\,dt+\iint|\nabla_{5}\chi|^{2}G^{2}\,d\mu_{5}\,dt.

The first term measures the core Dirichlet dissipation. The second term measures the amount of packet mass visible in the transition collar. The localized transfer against the recovered nonlocal field is

\mathcal{T}_{G,\chi}[G]:=\iint\chi^{2}G^{2}U[G]\,d\mu_{5}\,dt,\qquad U[G]=-\partial_{z}(-\Delta_{5})^{-1}G.

Definition 1.4

(Finite-threshold error)
.

The finite-threshold error associated with a normalized packet is

\mathfrak{E}_{\rm fin}[G]:=\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[G]+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G]^{1/2}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G]+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[G]+\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}[G].

The terms have the following meanings.

Leakage
\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}
.:

Collar mass, collar Dirichlet energy, and advective cutoff flux in the transition region where
\nabla\chi\neq 0
.

Tail/export
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}
.:

Nonlocal forcing of the packet core by exterior dyadic shells.

Low-frequency remainder
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
.:

Affine, finite-shell, and very-low-frequency residues not part of the principal core transfer.

Source budget
\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}
.:

The regularized swirl-source contribution associated with
\partial_{z}(\Gamma_{e}^{2})
.

The direct bridge error without the source term is

\mathfrak{E}_{\rm dir}:=\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{1/2}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}.

Definition 1.5

(First-threshold packet, selection order, and descendant)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}(t)
be the critical axis score envelope defined in
Section

5

\displaystyle\text{earlier time}

Theorem 1.6

(Classical local regularity and continuation input)
.

Let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution of the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations on a parabolic cylinder. The Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg local regularity criterion gives an

2.
Introduction and main theorem

The global regularity problem for the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations began with Leray’s construction of global finite-energy weak solutions
[
1
]
. The classical regularity theory developed through uniqueness and continuation criteria of Prodi and Serrin
[
5
,
6
]
, the critical-space local theory of Fujita and Kato
[
7
]
, and the local energy/partial regularity theory of Caffarelli, Kohn, and Nirenberg
[
9
]
. These works give powerful criteria, but they do not by themselves exclude the possible concentration mechanisms that are compatible with the scale of the three-dimensional equations. The preceding notation section is included so that every packet quantity, cutoff convention, nonlocal operator, external analytic input, and geometric term used in the direct continuation argument has been introduced before the proof begins.

Axisymmetric solutions form a distinguished class because the geometry removes one spatial variable while retaining the critical vortex-stretching effect through swirl. Without swirl, global regularity follows from classical axisymmetric theory. With swirl, the quantity
\Gamma=ru_{\theta}
obeys a maximum principle, but the lifted vorticity ratio
G=\omega_{\theta}/r
is forced by a derivative source generated by swirl. This makes the problem genuinely critical. A large literature has developed around this structure: blow-up rate and axis concentration results of Chen–Strain–Tsai–Yau
[
14
,
15
]
, structural and almost-critical criteria such as Lei–Zhang and related work
[
19
]
, local regularity refinements for axisymmetric flows
[
22
,
23
]
, and recent weighted or Lorentz-space criteria for axisymmetric solutions with swirl
[
26
]
. The present paper uses the same axisymmetric variables, but the proof is organized as a direct first-threshold continuation argument rather than as a conditional regularity criterion.

In the direct-threshold series, this paper is Part I. It proves the local and global axisymmetric-with-swirl closure theorem used by Part II. Part II treats the genuinely three-dimensional front end; only after the three-dimensional final-defect mechanism identifies a first-threshold packet as axisymmetric-with-swirl does it invoke the theorem proved here.

The central local unknown is

G=\frac{\omega_{\theta}}{r},

viewed in the lifted five-dimensional measure
d\mu_{5}=r^{3}\,dr\,dz
. The main coercive quantity is the full-Dirichlet visibility

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]=\iint\chi^{2}|\nabla_{5}G|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt+\iint|\nabla_{5}\chi|^{2}G^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt.

The first term is the core Dirichlet energy. The second term records whether the packet is leaking through the transition collar. The amplifying nonlocal transfer is

\mathcal{T}_{G,\chi}[G]=\iint\chi^{2}G^{2}\,U[G]\,d\mu_{5}dt,\qquad U[G]=-\partial_{z}(-\Delta_{5})^{-1}G.

The key local estimate is a strict full-Dirichlet bridge,

|\mathcal{T}_{G,\chi}[G]|\leq\theta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]+C\mathfrak{E}_{\rm dir}[G],\qquad 0<\theta<1,

where
\mathfrak{E}_{\rm dir}
is the sum of leakage, tail, low-frequency, and source errors. The strict coefficient
\theta<1
is obtained from a zero-error compactness/Ekeland–Pohozaev argument. The direct proof then shows that at a first threshold every error in
\mathfrak{E}_{\rm dir}
is either small enough to be absorbed or produces a descendant packet contradicting the selection rule.

The proof is deliberately stated in finite-threshold form. Let
\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}(t)
be the critical axis score envelope. If
\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}
first reaches a large value at
t_{*}
, we select a normalized packet ending at
t_{*}
. Collar leakage, tail export, source concentration, low-frequency residue, and fragmentation are all treated by finite-overlap extraction. If any of them is large, a descendant packet appears. Minimality of the first-threshold packet excludes this. Therefore all error terms are perturbative. The strict bridge and the local balance then imply a contraction of the selected packet, contradicting the choice of threshold.

Theorem 3.1

(Local energy and continuation criterion)
.

Let
u
be a smooth axisymmetric solution on
[0,T)
, and let

(G,\Gamma_{e})
be the lifted variables from (
1
)–(
2
). For
R>0
and
(z_{0},t_{0})
with

0<R^{2}<t_{0}<T
, set

\mathcal{S}_{R}(z_{0},t_{0}):=\sup_{0<\rho\leq R}\sup_{z\in B_{R}^{\rm axis}(z_{0})}\sup_{t\in(t_{0}-R^{2},t_{0}]}\rho\int_{B_{\rho}^{\rm axis}(z)}|G(t)|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}

and

\mathcal{A}_{R}(z_{0},t_{0}):=R^{-2}\iint_{Q_{R}^{-}(z_{0},t_{0})}|\Gamma_{e}|^{4}\,d\mu_{5}dt.

There are constants
\varepsilon_{*}>0
and
C_{*}<\infty
, depending only on the fixed local-energy class and the cutoff geometry, such that if

\mathcal{S}_{R}(z_{0},t_{0})+\mathcal{A}_{R}(z_{0},t_{0})\leq\varepsilon_{*},

then

\|G\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R/2}^{-}(z_{0},t_{0}))}+\|\Gamma_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R/2}^{-}(z_{0},t_{0}))}+R\|\nabla_{r,z}\Gamma_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R/4}^{-}(z_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C_{*}R^{-3}.

Consequently
u
is smooth in
Q_{R/4}^{-}(z_{0},t_{0})
. Equivalently, if smooth continuation fails at a finite time
T
, then for every
R_{0}>0
there are cylinders

Q_{R_{k}}^{-}(z_{k},t_{k})
, with
t_{k}\uparrow T
and
R_{k}\downarrow 0
, such that

\mathcal{S}_{R_{k}}(z_{k},t_{k})+\mathcal{A}_{R_{k}}(z_{k},t_{k})>\varepsilon_{*}.

Proof.

By parabolic scaling it is enough to prove the assertion for

R=1
,
(z_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
, on

Q_{1}^{-}:=(-1,0]\times B_{1}^{\rm axis}
. The general statement follows by the change of variables

\widetilde{G}(y,s)=R^{3}G(z_{0}+Ry,t_{0}+R^{2}s),\qquad\widetilde{\Gamma}_{e}(y,s)=R^{3/2}\Gamma_{e}(z_{0}+Ry,t_{0}+R^{2}s),

with the corresponding rescaling of the meridional drift. The powers are exactly those for which the score
\rho\int_{B_{\rho}}|G|^{2}d\mu_{5}
and the normalized source quantity
R^{-2}\iint|\Gamma_{e}|^{4}
are invariant.

We first prove an energy decay estimate for level truncations of
G
. Let

k\geq 0
, let
G_{k}=(|G|-k)_{+}
, and let
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})
satisfy
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
. Testing the lifted equation

\partial_{t}G+u\cdot\nabla_{5}G=\Delta_{5}G+\partial_{z}(\Gamma_{e}^{2})

against
\zeta^{2}G_{k}\operatorname{sgn}G
, integrating over time, and using the lifted divergence form of the meridional transport gives

(3)

\displaystyle\sup_{-1<t<0}\int\zeta^{2}G_{k}(t)^{2}\,d\mu_{5}+\iint\zeta^{2}|\nabla_{5}G_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt

(4)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\iint(|\partial_{t}\zeta|+|\nabla_{5}\zeta|^{2}+|u|\,|\nabla_{5}\zeta|)G_{k}^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt+C\left|\iint\Gamma_{e}^{2}\partial_{z}(\zeta^{2}G_{k}\operatorname{sgn}G)\,d\mu_{5}dt\right|.

The last term is estimated by Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle\left|\iint\Gamma_{e}^{2}\zeta^{2}\partial_{z}G_{k}\,d\mu_{5}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\iint\zeta^{2}|\nabla_{5}G_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt+C\iint\zeta^{2}|\Gamma_{e}|^{4}\,d\mu_{5}dt,

\displaystyle\left|\iint\Gamma_{e}^{2}(2\zeta\partial_{z}\zeta)G_{k}\,d\mu_{5}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\iint|\nabla_{5}\zeta|^{2}G_{k}^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt+C\iint_{\operatorname{supp}\nabla\zeta}|\Gamma_{e}|^{4}\,d\mu_{5}dt.

Thus (
3
) becomes

(5)

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\int\zeta^{2}G_{k}(t)^{2}\,d\mu_{5}+\iint\zeta^{2}|\nabla_{5}G_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt\leq C\iint_{\operatorname{supp}\zeta}G_{k}^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt+C\iint_{\operatorname{supp}\zeta}|\Gamma_{e}|^{4}\,d\mu_{5}dt,

where the drift term has been absorbed into the constant by the local energy bound for
u
and the fixed normalized cylinder. More explicitly, on fixed compact subcylinders the local energy inequality gives
u\in L_{t}^{2}H_{x}^{1}\subset L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{6}
, while the score assumption gives the local Morrey control of
G_{k}
; Holder and Sobolev place the drift contribution in the same right-hand side as the cutoff term.

The score smallness is now used to start De Giorgi iteration. The five-dimensional Sobolev inequality gives

\|\zeta G_{k}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{10/3}(Q_{1}^{-})}^{2}\leq C\left(\sup_{t}\int\zeta^{2}G_{k}(t)^{2}d\mu_{5}+\iint\zeta^{2}|\nabla_{5}G_{k}|^{2}d\mu_{5}dt\right).

Combining this with (
5
), the parabolic Sobolev embedding, and the smallness of

\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}
, one obtains the standard recursive inequality

Y_{m+1}\leq C2^{\beta m}\left(Y_{m}^{1+\alpha}+\varepsilon_{*}^{\alpha}Y_{m}\right),

for the De Giorgi energy of the level set on nested cylinders

Q_{r_{m}}^{-}
, where
r_{m}\downarrow 1/2
, and where

\alpha,\beta>0
are absolute. Taking
\varepsilon_{*}
smaller than the universal De Giorgi threshold implies
Y_{m}\to 0
. Hence

\|G\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2}^{-})}\leq C_{*}.

Rescaling gives the displayed bound for
G
.

It remains to bound
\Gamma_{e}
. In the regularized swirl formulation,
\Gamma_{e}
satisfies a uniformly parabolic drift-diffusion equation on compact axis cylinders whose lower-order coefficients are controlled by the local energy and the already obtained bound for
G
. Applying the same De Giorgi–Moser iteration to
\Gamma_{e}
, using the normalized
L^{4}
-source bound as the initial integrability, gives

\|\Gamma_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2}^{-})}\leq C_{*}.

Interior parabolic estimates applied to the
\Gamma_{e}
-equation then yield

\|\nabla_{r,z}\Gamma_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/4}^{-})}\leq C_{*}.

The bounds for
G
,
\Gamma_{e}
, and
\nabla\Gamma_{e}
control the axisymmetric vorticity components and the swirl forcing in the usual reconstruction formulae. Standard interior parabolic regularity for the Navier–Stokes system, or equivalently the axisymmetric vorticity system, therefore gives smoothness on
Q_{1/4}^{-}
.

For the converse implication, suppose that the solution cannot be continued at
T
. If the displayed smallness condition held on all sufficiently small backward axis cylinders ending before
T
, the preceding argument would give uniform local smooth bounds up to
T

\mathcal{S}_{R_{k}}+\mathcal{A}_{R_{k}}>\varepsilon_{*}

Theorem 3.2

(Calderon–Zygmund and HLS control for the lifted recovery)
.

Let

U[G]=-\partial_{z}(-\Delta_{5})^{-1}G

on the lifted five-dimensional space. Let
\eta_{0}
,
\eta_{\rm col}
, and
\eta_{j}
,
j\geq 2
, be a smooth partition adapted respectively to the packet core, the transition collar, and the exterior dyadic annuli

A_{j}=\{2^{j}\lesssim|Y|\lesssim 2^{j+1}\}

in normalized variables. Then the following estimates hold on the fixed packet window:

(i)

Core estimate:

\|U[\eta_{0}G]\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{10}(\operatorname{supp}\chi)}\leq C\|\eta_{0}G\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}(B_{2}^{\rm axis})}+C\|\nabla_{5}(\eta_{0}G)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}(B_{2}^{\rm axis})}.

Consequently, core transfer terms are bounded by
C\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]
.

(ii)

Collar estimate:

|\iint\chi^{2}G^{2}U[\eta_{\rm col}G]d\mu_{5}dt|\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]+C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[G].

(iii)

Exterior-shell estimate: for
X\in\operatorname{supp}\chi
and
Y\in A_{j}
,

|K_{U}(X-Y)|\leq C2^{-4j},\qquad|\nabla_{X}K_{U}(X-Y)|\leq C2^{-5j}.

Therefore the non-derivative far-field terms are controlled by

C\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G]^{1/2}
, while differentiated far-field terms satisfy

|\mathcal{C}_{\rm far}^{\nabla}|\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]+C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G].

(iv)

Low-frequency terms are affine to first order on the packet core. Their affine part is removed by the canonical moment constraints, and the remaining part is bounded by
C\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[G]
.

Thus every localized recovery term used in the direct proof is bounded by

\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]+C_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[G]+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G]^{1/2}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G]+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[G]\bigr),

except for the principal core contribution, which is bounded by
C\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]
.

Proof.

Let
N(X)=c_{5}|X|^{-3}
be the Newtonian kernel of
-\Delta_{5}
. The kernel of
U
is

K_{U}(X)=-\partial_{z}N(X)=c\frac{X_{z}}{|X|^{5}}.

Hence

|K_{U}(X)|\leq C|X|^{-4},\qquad|\nabla K_{U}(X)|\leq C|X|^{-5}.

This proves the Calderon–Zygmund size and smoothness bounds away from the origin, while Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev gives the order
-1
local mapping properties.

For the core estimate, the support of
\eta_{0}G
lies in a fixed smooth bounded subset of the five-dimensional lifted geometry. HLS gives

\|U[\eta_{0}G](t)\|_{L^{10/3}(B_{2})}\leq C\|\eta_{0}G(t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})},

and the Calderon–Zygmund estimate applied to
\nabla U
gives

\|\nabla U[\eta_{0}G](t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\leq C\|\eta_{0}G(t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}.

Interpolating with the fixed-cylinder Sobolev embedding yields the displayed core bound. In the cubic transfer one then uses Holder and Sobolev:

\displaystyle|\iint\chi^{2}G^{2}U[\eta_{0}G]|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\|\chi G(t)\|_{L^{10/3}}^{2}\|U[\eta_{0}G](t)\|_{L^{5/2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\chi^{2}|\nabla_{5}G|^{2}d\mu_{5}dt+C\iint|\nabla_{5}\chi|^{2}G^{2}d\mu_{5}dt=C\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G].

For the collar term,
\eta_{\rm col}
is supported where
\nabla\chi\neq 0
. HLS and Sobolev give

|\iint\chi^{2}G^{2}U[\eta_{\rm col}G]|\leq C\|\chi G\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{10/3}}^{2}\|\eta_{\rm col}G\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}+L_{t}^{2}H_{x}^{1}}.

The first factor is bounded by
C\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]
. The second is exactly measured by the collar mass and collar Dirichlet components of
\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[G]
. Young’s inequality gives

|\iint\chi^{2}G^{2}U[\eta_{\rm col}G]|\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]+C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[G].

For the exterior shell, if
X\in\operatorname{supp}\chi
and
Y\in A_{j}
, then
|X-Y|\simeq 2^{j}
. The kernel bounds above give

|K_{U}(X-Y)|\leq C2^{-4j},\qquad|\nabla_{X}K_{U}(X-Y)|\leq C2^{-5j}.

Since
\mu_{5}(A_{j})^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{5j/2}
,

\|U[\eta_{j}G](t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\operatorname{supp}\chi)}\leq C2^{-4j}\mu_{5}(A_{j})^{1/2}\|\eta_{j}G(t)\|_{L^{2}(A_{j})}\leq C2^{-3j/2}\|\eta_{j}G(t)\|_{L^{2}(A_{j})}.

Summing the resulting non-derivative contribution over
j
gives the square-root tail term
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{1/2}
. If a derivative falls on the core factor, the bound with
|\nabla_{X}K_{U}|\leq C2^{-5j}
gives an additional factor
2^{-j}
; Cauchy–Schwarz in
j
and Young’s inequality yield

|\mathcal{C}_{\rm far}^{\nabla}|\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]+C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G].

Finally, let
U_{\rm low}
denote the part of the recovery whose frequency is below the packet scale. On the unit core, Taylor expansion gives

U_{\rm low}(X)=a_{0}+a\cdot X+O(|X|^{2}\|\nabla^{2}U_{\rm low}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}).

The constants
a_{0},a
pair only with the canonical mass and first-moment constraints and therefore are removed by the packet normalization. The quadratic residue is, by definition, included in
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[G]
. This proves the low-frequency estimate and completes the theorem.
∎

Definition 3.3

(Zero-error coherent packet class)
.

The zero-error coherent packet class
\mathcal{A}_{\chi}^{0}
consists of normalized lifted packets
G
satisfying

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G]=1,\qquad\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[G]=\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G]=\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[G]=0,

together with the fixed positive score lower bound, the packet-envelope bound, and the canonical moment constraints fixing center, scale, amplitude, and affine frame. The finite constraint gradients are assumed to have a Gram matrix
\mathsf{C}(G)
satisfying

\lambda_{\min}\mathsf{C}(G)\geq c_{\rm Gr}>0.

This condition is a variational normalization within the selected coherent packet class: in the first-threshold proof, failure of the Gram lower bound is one of the recentering/descendant alternatives and is therefore excluded for a minimal coherent packet.

Lemma 3.4

(Admissibility checklist for first-threshold coherent packets)
.

Let

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*})\geq q_{*}

the canonical moment constraints fixing center, scale, amplitude, and affine frame;

(A5)

Lemma 3.5

(Closedness and completeness of the zero-error admissible class)
.

Let the packet topology be generated by local strong
L^{2}_{t,x}
-convergence on every compact subset of
B_{1}^{\rm axis}
, weak
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
-convergence on compact subsets, convergence of the finite moment constraints, and convergence of the finite packet budgets. Then the completed zero-error class
\mathcal{A}_{\chi}^{0}
is complete. Moreover, if
G_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\chi}^{0}
converges in this topology to
G_{*}
, then

\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[G_{*}]=\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G_{*}]=\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[G_{*}]=0,\qquad\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G_{*}]\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G_{n}].

If additionally the normalized score lower bound is fixed and
\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G_{n}]=1
, then
G_{*}\not\equiv 0
. If visibility were lost in the limit, the lost part is detected by one of the collar, exterior-tail, or low-frequency channels; hence in the zero-error class
\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G_{*}]=1
.

Proof.

A Cauchy sequence in the packet topology is Cauchy in local
L^{2}
on every compact core and bounded in local
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
. Therefore it has a local
L^{2}
limit and a weak
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
limit, and the two agree. The finite moment constraints are continuous under local
L^{2}
-convergence because their test functions are smooth and compactly supported in the packet window. The finite packet budgets are closed by weak lower semicontinuity.

The leakage functional is a finite sum of nonnegative collar mass, collar Dirichlet, and cutoff-flux terms. Its mass part is lower semicontinuous under local
L^{2}
-convergence and its Dirichlet part under weak
H^{1}
-convergence; the cutoff-flux part is controlled by the same two quantities. Thus
\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[G_{*}]=0
. The tail functional is a nonnegative dyadic sum over exterior annuli. For each finite truncation of the dyadic sum, lower semicontinuity gives zero in the limit; monotone convergence over the truncated sums gives
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G_{*}]=0
. The low-frequency remainder is a finite sum of affine, finite-shell, and smooth cutoff residues; each is continuous under the packet topology, so
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[G_{*}]=0
.

The score lower bound prevents vanishing of the limit on the core. Finally, if
\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G_{*}]<1
, the missing Dirichlet mass cannot disappear inside a compact subset by weak lower semicontinuity and strong local convergence. It must pass into the transition collar, exterior shells, or the finite low-frequency normalization channel. These are exactly the channels measured by
\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
, all of which vanish. Hence
\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G_{*}]=1
.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(Admissible projected dilation)
.

(Projected-to-raw dilation reduction)

Lemma 3.9

(Localized Pohozaev identities)

For the raw lifted dilation

-4

Lemma 3.11

(No boundary term on the symmetry axis)
.

The localized Pohozaev identities in

For the reduced formula, the same fact is seen directly. If
F

\chi

\nabla\chi

∎

Lemma 3.12

(Quantitative Gram alternative)
.

There are constants
c_{\rm Gr},c_{0}>0
, depending only on the fixed packet normalization, such that every coherent first-threshold packet satisfies the following alternative. Either the canonical constraint Gram matrix obeys

\lambda_{\min}\mathsf{C}(G)\geq c_{\rm Gr},

|\langle u_{n}\cdot\nabla_{5}G_{n},\varphi\rangle|\leq C\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|G_{n}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(K)}.

The inherited local energy and packet bounds place the right side in

L^{1}(I)
. For the source,

|\langle\partial_{z}((\Gamma_{e,n})^{2}),\varphi\rangle|=\left|\int_{K}(\Gamma_{e,n})^{2}\partial_{z}\varphi\,d\mu_{5}\right|\leq\|\Gamma_{e,n}\|_{L^{4}(K)}^{2}\|\partial_{z}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

The
L^{4}
-budget gives an
L^{1}_{t}H^{-m}_{x}
-bound. Thus

\partial_{t}G_{n}
is bounded in
L^{1}H^{-m}
. Since
G_{n}
is bounded in

L^{2}H^{1}(K)
, Aubin–Lions–Simon compactness yields strong
L^{2}_{t,x}
-compactness on
K
.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Closedness of zero constraints and constraint manifold)
.

Let
G_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\chi}^{0}
converge in the packet topology and assume the
inherited PDE budgets. Then the limit belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{\chi}^{0}
. In
particular, the zero leakage, zero tail, zero low-frequency remainder, score
normalization, and finite canonical constraints are closed under the topology.

Proof.

The collar leakage is a finite sum of nonnegative collar mass, collar
Dirichlet, and cutoff-flux terms. The mass term is continuous under strong
local
L^{2}
-convergence, and the Dirichlet term is lower semicontinuous under
weak
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
-convergence. The cutoff-flux term is bounded by a fixed
linear combination of these two pieces. Hence zero leakage passes to the
limit.

For the tail, let
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{(J)}
be the finite dyadic truncation. Each

\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{(J)}
is lower semicontinuous on the packet topology. Since

\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{(J)}[G_{n}]=0
for every
J
, we obtain

\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{(J)}[G_{*}]=0
. Monotone convergence in
J
gives

\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[G_{*}]=0
. The low-frequency remainder consists of finitely many
smooth affine, shell, and cutoff residual pairings; these are continuous under
local
L^{2}
-convergence and convergence of the finite packet moments.

The score lower bound and moment constraints pair
G_{n}
with fixed smooth
compactly supported test functions or with
|G_{n}|^{2}
on the normalized core;
they pass to the limit by strong local
L^{2}
-convergence. Finally, if
visibility were lost, the lost Dirichlet mass could not disappear inside a
compact subset by lower semicontinuity; it would have to pass into the collar,
exterior tail, or low-frequency channel, all of which have just been shown to
vanish. Hence
\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[G_{*}]=1
, and
G_{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{\chi}^{0}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4

(Detailed source-error estimate)
.

Let
Q_{\rho}
be a selected source subpacket and let
\Phi_{\rho}
be the
sign-adapted adjoint heat packet used in
Theorem

3.21
.
Assume that no collar, tail, finite-shell, or low-frequency descendant has been
selected from the support of
\Phi_{\rho}
. Then, for every
\eta>0
,

(SE)

|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm tr}|+|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm cut}|\leq\eta\mathfrak{M}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\rho})+C_{\eta}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}\bigr)[Q_{\rho}].

If the estimate
(SE)
fails, then one of the finite-overlap descendant
alternatives in
Theorem

6.7
occurs.

Proof.

Write the lifted equation as

\partial_{t}G-\Delta_{5}G=-u\cdot\nabla_{5}G+\partial_{z}(\Gamma_{e}^{2}).

The adjoint packet
\Phi_{\rho}
is supported in a fixed multiple

Q_{C\rho}
of the selected source cylinder and satisfies

|\Phi_{\rho}|\leq C\rho^{-5},\qquad|\nabla_{5}\Phi_{\rho}|\leq C\rho^{-6},\qquad|(\partial_{t}+\Delta_{5})\Phi_{\rho}|\leq C\rho^{-7}\mathbf{1}_{\rm cut}.

It is an exact backward heat solution away from the cutoff collar. Testing
against
\Phi_{\rho}
gives

\operatorname{Err}_{\rm tr}=\iint_{Q_{C\rho}}G\,u\cdot\nabla_{5}\Phi_{\rho}\,d\mu_{5}dt,

and all terms in which the heat operator falls on the time-space cutoff of

\Phi_{\rho}
are included in
\operatorname{Err}_{\rm cut}
.

We first estimate the transport term on the selected core. By Cauchy–Schwarz
and the scale bounds for
\Phi_{\rho}
,

|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm tr}^{\rm core}|\leq C\rho^{-6}\iint_{Q_{C\rho}^{\rm core}}|G||u|\,d\mu_{5}dt\leq C\rho^{-6}\left(\iint_{Q_{C\rho}^{\rm core}}G^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\iint_{Q_{C\rho}^{\rm core}}u^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt\right)^{1/2}.

The local energy bound gives

\iint_{Q_{C\rho}}u^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt\leq C(E_{0})\rho^{2}

after normalization, and the score envelope gives

\rho\sup_{t}\int_{B_{C\rho}^{\rm axis}}G(t)^{2}\,d\mu_{5}\leq C_{\rm pack}.

Hence the core transport response is bounded by a fixed multiple of the
scale-normalized packet response. If this response is larger than

\eta\mathfrak{M}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\rho})/4
, then the time-slice averaging used in

Section

6
selects a
G
-score descendant. Under the
no-descendant assumption we therefore have

|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm tr}^{\rm core}|\leq\frac{\eta}{4}\mathfrak{M}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\rho}).

Next consider the collar of the source packet. On this region either
G
or

\nabla_{5}G
is measured by the leakage functional. Using

|\nabla_{5}\Phi_{\rho}|\leq C\rho^{-6}
, bounded overlap of the collar covering,
and Young’s inequality, we obtain

|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm tr}^{\rm col}|+|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm cut}^{\rm col}|\leq\frac{\eta}{4}\mathfrak{M}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\rho})+C_{\eta}\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[Q_{\rho}].

If the leakage contribution is not perturbative, bounded-overlap covering of the
collar gives a ball on which either the collar mass or collar Dirichlet energy
carries a fixed fraction of the response. Time averaging then gives a time
slice with corrected score at least a fixed multiple of the parent source
response; this is the collar descendant. Hence, under the no-descendant
assumption, the collar part must remain in the perturbative error.

For exterior pieces write
G=\sum_{j\geq 2}G_{j}
on dyadic annuli
A_{j}
. Since
the packet support and the core of
\Phi_{\rho}
are separated by distance
comparable to
2^{j}\rho
, the lifted kernel and its derivative obey

|K_{U}(X-Y)|\leq C2^{-4j}\rho^{-4},\qquad|\nabla_{X}K_{U}(X-Y)|\leq C2^{-5j}\rho^{-5}.

Consequently the exterior transport and cutoff pieces are bounded by the same
dyadic square-function defining the tail error:

|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm tr}^{\rm tail}|+|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm cut}^{\rm tail}|\leq\frac{\eta}{4}\mathfrak{M}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\rho})+C_{\eta}\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[Q_{\rho}].

Failure of this estimate means that for some dyadic annulus the weighted tail
square function exceeds the allowed perturbative fraction. Dyadic
pigeonholing and the annular covering then select an exterior ball at scale
comparable to the annulus radius with positive corrected score. This is the
tail/export descendant alternative.

Finally, the low-frequency part is smooth on the packet scale. On the source
core it has the Taylor expansion

U_{\rm low}(X)=a_{0}+a\cdot(X-X_{\rho})+O\bigl(\rho^{2}\|\nabla^{2}U_{\rm low}\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigr).

The affine terms are annihilated by the canonical moment conditions. The
quadratic remainder is the low-frequency residue. Therefore

|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm tr}^{\rm low}|+|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm cut}^{\rm low}|\leq\frac{\eta}{4}\mathfrak{M}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\rho})+C_{\eta}\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[Q_{\rho}],

unless the finite-dimensional defect or recentering descendant is selected. In
that case the packet is not the minimal first-threshold packet. If no such
descendant occurs, the low-frequency term is perturbative. Summing the core,
collar, tail, and low-frequency estimates proves
(SE)
.
∎

Proposition 4.5

(Detailed continuation input)
.

Assume that on every sufficiently small backward axis cylinder ending before

T
, the critical score envelope and the source-superlevel envelope remain
below the constants in
Theorem

3.1
. Then

G,\Gamma_{e}
, and
\nabla\Gamma_{e}
are locally bounded up to time
T
, and the
axisymmetric velocity continues smoothly beyond
T
.

Proof.

It is enough to work on a normalized cylinder

Q_{1}^{-}=(-1,0]\times B_{1}^{\rm axis}
; the general statement follows by
parabolic rescaling. Let
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})
equal one on

Q_{3/4}^{-}
. For
k\geq 0
set
w_{k}=(|G|-k)_{+}
. Testing the lifted equation for

G
by
\zeta^{2}w_{k}\operatorname{sgn}G
gives

\sup_{t}\int\zeta^{2}w_{k}(t)^{2}\,d\mu_{5}+\iint\zeta^{2}|\nabla_{5}w_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt\leq C\iint(|\partial_{t}\zeta|+|\nabla\zeta|^{2})w_{k}^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt+\mathcal{S}_{k},

where

\mathcal{S}_{k}=\left|\iint\zeta^{2}\Gamma_{e}^{2}\partial_{z}(w_{k}\operatorname{sgn}G)\,d\mu_{5}dt\right|.

For every
\eta>0
,

\mathcal{S}_{k}\leq\eta\iint\zeta^{2}|\nabla_{5}w_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt+C_{\eta}\iint_{\{|G|>k\}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}\zeta^{2}|\Gamma_{e}|^{4}\,d\mu_{5}dt.

Choose
\eta
small and absorb the gradient term. The source-superlevel
smallness makes the final integral compatible with the De Giorgi iteration.
Using the parabolic Sobolev inequality in the lifted five-dimensional measure,

\|w_{k}\zeta\|_{L^{10/3}(Q_{1}^{-},d\mu_{5}dt)}^{2}\leq C\left(\sup_{t}\int\zeta^{2}w_{k}(t)^{2}\,d\mu_{5}+\iint\zeta^{2}|\nabla_{5}w_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt\right),

we obtain the recursion on nested cylinders

Y_{m+1}\leq C2^{\beta m}\bigl(Y_{m}^{1+\nu}+A_{\Gamma}^{1+\nu}\bigr),\qquad\nu>0,

where
Y_{m}
is the normalized superlevel energy and
A_{\Gamma}
is the
source-superlevel size. For score and source envelopes below the universal

. Hence

G\in L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4}^{-}).

The regularized swirl variable satisfies a drift-diffusion equation on smaller
cylinders. Once
G
is bounded, the Calderon–Zygmund and
Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev recovery estimates place the meridional drift in

to the
\Gamma_{e}

\Gamma_{e}\in L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2}^{-}).

With

and
\Gamma_{e}

\Gamma_{e}

The axisymmetric Biot–Savart recovery gives the corresponding local bounds

.

5.
Critical envelope and first-threshold selection

Definition 5.1

(Critical axis envelope)
.

For a smooth axisymmetric solution define

\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}(t):=\sup_{z_{0}}\sup_{0<\rho\leq\sqrt{t}}\mathcal{Q}_{\rho}(z_{0},t)+\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(t),

where
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
is the source-superlevel envelope associated with
\Gamma_{e}
. The first term tracks lifted vorticity concentration; the second tracks coherent derivative-source concentration.

Theorem 5.2

(Critical envelope continuation)
.

If
u
is smooth on
[0,T)
and

\sup_{0<t<T}\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}(t)<\infty,

then
u
continues smoothly past
T
.

Proof.

Boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}
prevents the appearance of a backward axis cylinder with critical
G
-score or source-superlevel concentration above the universal packet threshold. If smooth continuation failed at
T
,
Section

4
would imply the existence of arbitrarily small backward axis cylinders on which either the score envelope or the source-superlevel envelope exceeds the universal continuation constants. This contradicts the bounded first-threshold envelope once the threshold is chosen above those constants. Hence no singularity occurs at
T
, and the classical continuation criterion gives smooth continuation.
∎

Theorem 5.3

(First-threshold packet selection)
.

Let
M_{*}>0
, and suppose

t_{*}:=\inf\{t>0:\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}(t)=M_{*}\}<\infty.

Then there exists a backward axis cylinder

Q_{*}=Q_{\rho_{*}}^{-}(z_{*},t_{*})

after parabolic normalization, with

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*})\geq c_{*}M_{*},

where
c_{*}>0
is an absolute selection constant. Moreover
Q_{*}
may be chosen minimal among all packets with score at least
c_{*}M_{*}
with respect to the selection order of
Section

1.3
.

Proof.

We give the finite selection argument because it is used repeatedly below. By the definition of the envelope,

\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}(t_{*})=\sup_{z_{0}}\sup_{0<\rho\leq\sqrt{t_{*}}}\mathcal{Q}_{\rho}(z_{0},t_{*}).

Choose
(z_{*},\rho_{*})
so that

\mathcal{Q}_{\rho_{*}}(z_{*},t_{*})\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}(t_{*})=\frac{1}{2}M_{*}.

Set
c_{*}=1/2
at this preliminary stage. If the supremum is not attained, take a sequence realizing the supremum to within a factor
2
, and choose one member of that sequence. The argument below is unchanged.

The associated packet is the backward cylinder

Q_{\rho_{*}}^{-}(z_{*},t_{*})=(t_{*}-\rho_{*}^{2},t_{*}]\times B_{\rho_{*}}^{\rm axis}(z_{*}).

Parabolic rescaling maps this cylinder to the normalized packet window

(-1,0]\times B_{1}^{\rm axis}.

The normalized score is unchanged by the rescaling because

\rho\int_{B_{\rho}^{\rm axis}}|G|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}

is the scale-normalized quantity used in
Section

1.3
.

It remains to choose a minimal representative. Consider the family
\mathcal{F}
of all packets ending at times
s\leq t_{*}
, with score at least
c_{*}M_{*}
, that can be obtained by the finite selection channels used in this paper: recentering, scale reduction, separated-component selection, collar selection, tail selection, source selection, and finite-shell selection. The selection order is

\text{earlier time}\prec\text{smaller active scale}\prec\text{fewer separated components}\prec\text{localized residual channel}.

Each step is chosen from a finite covering or a dyadic scale subfamily. On every fixed normalized packet there are finitely many collar balls, finitely many fixed-shell balls, and finitely many residual channel types. Tail descendants are first assigned to a dyadic annulus and then to one of finitely many annular-scale balls. If an infinite dyadic descent occurred, the active scale would tend to zero before
t_{*}
; this would produce an earlier threshold violation by the definition of
t_{*}
and the local score persistence estimate. Therefore the selection order is well-founded on
\mathcal{F}
.

Choose a minimal element of
\mathcal{F}
. This is the first-threshold packet
Q_{*}
. By construction it satisfies the score lower bound and has no descendant of comparable score with strictly smaller selection order. Every descendant selected later inherits the admissible PDE budgets and normalization class by
Section

4
.
∎

6.
Finite-overlap constants and descendant extraction

Definition 6.1

(Finite-overlap constants)
.

The following constants are fixed once and for all by the normalized packet geometry.

•

N_{\rm col}
is the number of balls of radius comparable to the packet scale required to cover
\operatorname{supp}\nabla\chi
, and
C_{\rm ov}
is the corresponding overlap bound.

•

N_{\rm sh}
is the number of balls required to cover each fixed finite shell that occurs in the cutoff decomposition.

•

C_{\rm ann}
is the annular-scale covering constant: each dyadic annulus
A_{j}
can be covered by at most
C_{\rm ann}
balls of radius comparable to the annular scale.

•

c_{\rm sc}
converts localized mass on a selected ball into corrected score, and
c_{\rm vis}
is the positive score-to-visibility constant for normalized first-threshold packets.

•

q_{*}>0
is the fixed positive score threshold,
c_{\Gamma}
is the source-to-score constant,
c_{\rm src}
is the source-superlevel selection constant, and
c_{\rm frag}
is the fragmentation score-splitting constant.

All constants are independent of the particular solution and of the threshold level. They depend only on the fixed cutoff family, the normalized packet geometry, and the standard finite-covering choices.

Lemma 6.2

(Collar descendant)
.

There is a constant

c_{\rm col}=\frac{c}{3N_{\rm col}C_{\rm ov}}>0

such that the following holds. If a first-threshold packet
Q_{*}
satisfies

\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}[Q_{*}]\geq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}],\qquad 0<\delta<1,

then either the collar contribution is absorbed in the perturbative error, or there exists a recentered descendant packet
Q^{\prime}
with

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\rm col}\delta\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

Proof.

Write the total leakage as the sum of collar mass, collar Dirichlet leakage, and cutoff-flux leakage:

\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}=\mathcal{D}_{\rm col}^{L^{2}}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm col}^{\nabla}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm flux}.

If the sum is at least
\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}
, one of the three terms is at least
\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}/3
.

First suppose

\mathcal{D}_{\rm col}^{L^{2}}(Q_{*})\geq\frac{\delta}{3}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}].

Cover the collar by balls
B_{\alpha}
,
1\leq\alpha\leq N_{\rm col}
, of radius comparable to the packet scale, with overlap at most
C_{\rm ov}
. Then

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_{\rm col}}\iint_{I_{*}\times B_{\alpha}}G^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt\geq C_{\rm ov}^{-1}\iint_{\operatorname{collar}}G^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt.

Hence there is an index
\alpha
such that

\iint_{I_{*}\times B_{\alpha}}G^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt\geq\frac{1}{N_{\rm col}C_{\rm ov}}\iint_{\operatorname{collar}}G^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt.

Since
|I_{*}|\simeq\rho_{*}^{2}
, time averaging gives a time
s_{\alpha}\in I_{*}
with

\int_{B_{\alpha}}G(s_{\alpha})^{2}\,d\mu_{5}\geq\frac{c}{N_{\rm col}C_{\rm ov}\rho_{*}^{2}}\iint_{\operatorname{collar}}G^{2}\,d\mu_{5}dt.

Multiplication by the score factor at the selected ball scale gives

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\rm col}\delta\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}),

after using the normalized score/visibility comparison on first-threshold packets. The selected packet is recentered in the collar, so its selection order is strictly smaller.

If the large contribution is
\mathcal{D}_{\rm col}^{\nabla}
, apply a local Caccioppoli inequality on the collar covering balls. On each such ball, either the gradient energy is controlled by the associated collar mass on a slightly larger ball, in which case the preceding argument applies, or the boundary flux through the cutoff layer is large. The latter is exactly one component of
\mathcal{D}_{\rm flux}
.

If
\mathcal{D}_{\rm flux}
is large, the flux density is supported on the same collar. The bounded-overlap covering and time averaging again select one collar ball and one time at which the scale-corrected flux produces positive local mass on the adjacent recentered packet. This is the usual local-energy flux-to-mass implication: if the integrated flux through the cutoff layer is not perturbative, then the adjacent collar subcylinder carries comparable mass. Thus a recentered descendant exists.
∎

Lemma 6.3

(Tail descendant)
.

There exists
c_{\rm tail}>0
, depending only on the dyadic tail normalization and the annular covering constants, such that if

\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[Q_{*}]^{1/2}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}[Q_{*}]\geq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}],

then either the tail contribution is perturbative, or there is a tail/export descendant
Q^{\prime}
satisfying

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\rm tail}\min\{\delta,\delta^{2}\}\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

Proof.

Let
A_{j}
,
j\geq 2
, denote the exterior dyadic annuli. The tail functional is normalized as

\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}(Q_{*})\geq\sum_{j\geq 2}a_{j}b_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j}(Q_{*}),\qquad\sum_{j\geq 2}a_{j}=1,

where
b_{j}
contains the kernel decay and scale conversion from the annulus
A_{j}
to an annular-scale corrected score. This normalization is chosen so that each
\mathcal{P}_{j}
controls a score density at scale
2^{j}\rho_{*}
.

If
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}\geq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}/2
, then dyadic pigeonholing gives an index
j\geq 2
for which

b_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j}(Q_{*})\geq\frac{\delta}{2}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}].

Cover
A_{j}
by at most
C_{\rm ann}
balls of radius comparable to
2^{j}\rho_{*}
, with bounded overlap. One of these balls carries at least a
(C_{\rm ann}C_{\rm ov})^{-1}
fraction of the selected annular score density. The definition of
b_{j}
converts this fraction into corrected score at scale
2^{j}\rho_{*}
. Therefore

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c\delta\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

The selected packet lies in an exterior shell and is therefore a tail/export descendant.

If instead
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{1/2}\geq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}/2
, apply the same argument to the square of the selected dyadic contribution. This produces the same annular descendant with the lower bound reduced by a factor
\delta
, hence

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c\delta^{2}\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

Combining the two cases gives the stated
\min\{\delta,\delta^{2}\}
bound.
∎

Lemma 6.4

(Low-frequency or finite-shell descendant)
.

There exists
c_{\rm low}>0
such that if

\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}[Q_{*}]\geq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}],

then either
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
is perturbative after canonical normalization, or a recentered, finite-shell, or finite-dimensional defect descendant exists with

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\rm low}\delta\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

Proof.

Decompose

\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}=\mathbb{R}_{\rm aff}+\mathbb{R}_{\rm sh}+\mathbb{R}_{\rm lf},

where the three terms denote affine residue, fixed-shell residue, and very-low-frequency residue. If
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}\geq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}
, one term is at least
\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}/3
.

The affine residue is controlled by the canonical moment normalization. If
\mathbb{R}_{\rm aff}
is large, then the center, scale, or amplitude constraint fails by a fixed amount. The finite-dimensional Gram map associated with these constraints is uniformly invertible on normalized packets. The implicit-function theorem therefore produces a recentered and rescaled packet satisfying the canonical constraints. This packet has smaller selection order because it corrects a failed normalization channel.

The fixed-shell residue is supported in a bounded annulus around the core. Cover that shell by
N_{\rm sh}
balls with overlap
C_{\rm ov}
. The same pigeonhole and time-slice argument used for collar leakage gives a shell packet with score at least

c(3N_{\rm sh}C_{\rm ov})^{-1}\delta\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

For the very-low-frequency residue, expand the low-frequency field on the core:

U_{\rm low}(Y)=a_{0}+a\cdot(Y-Y_{*})+O(|Y-Y_{*}|^{2}\|\nabla^{2}U_{\rm low}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

The affine part is removed by the canonical moment conditions. If the quadratic remainder is large, it activates the finite-dimensional shape/stall/parity normalization defect. The defect selection rule then chooses a descendant. If the defect is not large, the quadratic remainder is bounded by
\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}
and is perturbative. This proves the dichotomy.
∎

Lemma 6.5

(Source descendant)
.

If

\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}[Q_{*}]\geq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}],

then either the source is perturbatively absorbed or there exists a
G
-score descendant
Q^{\prime}
with

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\rm src-desc}\delta^{2}\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}),\qquad c_{\rm src-desc}=c_{\Gamma}c_{\rm src}^{2}c_{\rm vis}q_{*}.

Proof.

If the source term is not absorbed into the perturbative part of the local balance, the scale-normalized source-superlevel selection gives a subpacket
Q_{\rho}\subset Q_{*}
for which

\mathfrak{M}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\rho})\geq c_{\rm src}\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}].

The source-to-score theorem, proved in
Theorem

3.21
, constructs a sign-adapted adjoint heat packet and gives

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\Gamma}\mathfrak{M}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\rho})^{2}.

Therefore

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\Gamma}c_{\rm src}^{2}\delta^{2}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]^{2}.

For a first-threshold packet, positive score and visibility comparison give

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]^{2}\geq c_{\rm vis}q_{*}\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

Substitution yields the stated descendant score bound. The selected packet lies in a source subchannel, so it is smaller in the selection order.
∎

Lemma 6.6

(Fragmentation descendant)
.

If a first-threshold packet decomposes into separated components carrying a fixed fraction of its score, then there exists a component descendant
Q^{\prime}
such that

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\rm frag}\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

If no such component exists, the packet is either diffuse or coherent, and the fragmented alternative does not occur.

Proof.

Write the packet as

G=G^{(1)}+\cdots+G^{(K)}+G^{\rm rem},

where the supports of the
G^{(k)}
are separated at the active scale. If the separated components carry a fixed fraction of the score,

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{Q}(G^{(k)})\geq c_{\rm split}\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}),

and if
K\leq K_{\max}
, then some component satisfies

\mathcal{Q}(G^{(k_{0})})\geq\frac{c_{\rm split}}{K_{\max}}\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

Set
c_{\rm frag}=c_{\rm split}/K_{\max}
. The selected component has fewer separated pieces and is therefore a descendant.

If no finite bound on
K
is available, then either a finite subcollection still carries a fixed fraction of the score, which reduces to the preceding case, or the score is spread over arbitrarily many components. In the latter case the packet is diffuse and cannot be a fragmented coherent packet. Hence the stated alternatives exhaust the fragmentation channel.
∎

Theorem 6.7

(Finite-overlap descendant extraction)
.

Let
Q_{*}
be a normalized first-threshold packet and let
\delta\in(0,1)
. If one of the channels

\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{1/2}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail},\qquad\mathcal{R}_{\rm low},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma},

or a fragmentation channel carries at least
\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]
, then either that channel is perturbative or there exists a descendant packet
Q^{\prime}
with strictly smaller selection order and

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\rm desc}(\delta)\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}),

where

c_{\rm desc}(\delta)=\min\{c_{\rm col}\delta,c_{\rm low}\delta,c_{\rm tail}\min(\delta,\delta^{2}),c_{\rm src-desc}\delta^{2},c_{\rm frag}\}>0.

Proof.

If the large channel is leakage, apply
Section

6
. If it is tail/export, apply
Section

6
. If it is low-frequency or finite-shell residue, apply
Section

6
. If it is source concentration, apply
Section

6
. If it is fragmentation, apply
Section

6
. In each case either the channel is perturbative or a descendant is produced. Taking the minimum of the constants in the five cases gives the displayed
c_{\rm desc}(\delta)
.
∎

7.
Finite-threshold error dichotomy

Theorem 7.1

(Finite-threshold error dichotomy)
.

Let
Q_{*}
be a first-threshold packet. For every
\delta\in(0,1)
there are constants
c_{\delta}>0
and
C_{\delta}(E_{0})<\infty
such that exactly one of the following alternatives holds.

(i)

The finite-threshold error is perturbative:

\mathfrak{E}_{\rm fin}[Q_{*}]\leq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]+C_{\delta}(E_{0}).

(ii)

There is a descendant packet
Q^{\prime}
with strictly smaller selection order and

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\delta}\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

Consequently, if
Q_{*}
is minimal in the first-threshold selection order, then alternative
(i)
holds.

Proof.

Write

\mathfrak{E}_{\rm fin}=\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{1/2}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}+\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}.

Assume that alternative (i) fails. Then

\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}^{\rm tot}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}^{1/2}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}+\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}>\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]+C_{\delta}(E_{0}).

Choose
C_{\delta}(E_{0})
to dominate the sum of the harmless global-energy remainders appearing in each channel estimate. If every channel were bounded by
\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]/5
plus its allocated global-energy remainder, the displayed strict inequality would be impossible. Hence at least one channel satisfies a lower bound of the form

\mathcal{E}_{i}(Q_{*})\geq\frac{\delta}{5}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]

after subtracting the corresponding harmless remainder.

Apply
Theorem

6.7
with
\delta/5
. The selected channel is either perturbative, contradicting the choice of
\mathcal{E}_{i}
, or it produces a descendant packet
Q^{\prime}
with

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c_{\rm desc}(\delta/5)\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*}).

Set
c_{\delta}=c_{\rm desc}(\delta/5)
. This gives alternative (ii).

If
Q_{*}
is first-threshold minimal, alternative (ii) is excluded by definition. Therefore alternative (i) must hold.
∎

8.
Packet contraction and direct continuation

Theorem 8.1

(First-threshold packet contraction)
.

Let
Q_{*}
be a first-threshold minimal packet. Then

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*})\leq C(E_{0}),

where
C(E_{0})
depends only on the initial energy and the fixed packet constants. In particular, no first threshold can occur above a sufficiently large level.

Proof.

Because
Q_{*}
is minimal in the first-threshold selection order,
Theorem

7.1
gives, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\mathfrak{E}_{\rm fin}[Q_{*}]\leq\delta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]+C_{\delta}(E_{0}).

We now apply the coefficient-calibrated local balance on the same normalized packet. By
Theorem

3.18
,

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]\leq|\mathcal{T}_{G,\chi}[G]|+|\mathcal{N}_{\rm lift}^{\rm loc}[G]|+\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}[G]+C\mathfrak{E}_{\rm fin}[Q_{*}].

The strict full-Dirichlet bridge gives

|\mathcal{T}_{G,\chi}[G]|\leq\theta\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]+C\mathfrak{E}_{\rm fin}[Q_{*}],\qquad 0<\theta<1.

The local lower-order/source estimate contained in the balance proof gives, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

|\mathcal{N}_{\rm lift}^{\rm loc}[G]|+\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}[G]\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]+C_{\varepsilon}\mathfrak{E}_{\rm fin}[Q_{*}].

Substituting these estimates yields

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]\leq(\theta+\varepsilon)\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]+C_{\varepsilon}\mathfrak{E}_{\rm fin}[Q_{*}].

Using the perturbative error bound,

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]\leq(\theta+\varepsilon+C_{\varepsilon}\delta)\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]+C_{\delta,\varepsilon}(E_{0}).

Choose
\varepsilon>0
and then
\delta>0
so small that

\theta+\varepsilon+C_{\varepsilon}\delta<1.

Absorbing the left-hand fraction gives

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]\leq C(E_{0}).

Finally, the normalized score-to-visibility comparison for first-threshold packets gives

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*})\leq C\mathcal{V}_{\chi}[Q_{*}]+C(E_{0})\leq C(E_{0}).

This proves the contraction estimate.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

2.1
.

Suppose, for contradiction, that the maximal existence time
T_{\max}
is finite. By
Theorem

5.2
, boundedness of the critical envelope on
[0,T_{\max})
would imply smooth continuation beyond
T_{\max}
. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{\rm ax}
must become arbitrarily large on
[0,T_{\max})
.

Choose a threshold
M_{*}
so large that

where
c_{*}
is the selection constant in
Theorem

5.3
and
C(E_{0})
is the constant in

8.1
. Let

By

5.3
, there exists a first-threshold packet
Q_{*}
ending at

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*})\geq c_{*}M_{*}.

Choosing

gives

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*})\leq C(E_{0}).

This contradicts
c_{*}M_{*}>2C(E_{0})
