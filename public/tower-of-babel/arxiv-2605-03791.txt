An Affine invariant Minkowski problem
Antoine Ablondi

Abstract
. In Euclidean space, the generalised Minkowski problem asks, for a given finite Radon measure μ \mu on the unit sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} , to find a compact convex set K K with area measure μ \mu . For convex sets in the Minkowski space invariant under an affine deformation of a uniform lattice of SO 0 ​ ( d , 1 ) \mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1) , the analogous Minkowski problem was considered and solved by Barbot–Béguin–Zeghib (partially) and Bonsante–Fillastre. By a theorem of Mess–Barbot–Bonsante, that also solves the Minkowski problem in flat Lorentzian spacetimes with compact hyperbolic Cauchy surface. We consider convex domains of the oriented real affine space ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} which are invariant under a subgroup of affine transformations obtained by adding translation parts to a discrete subgroup of SL ⁡ ( ℝ d + 1 ) \mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}) dividing a convex cone. We prove that those convex domains satisfy a local Steiner Formula, allowing to introduce natural area measures and define an affine invariant Minkowski problem. We then solve that Minkowski problem through a variational method using the convexity of a covolume functional. We also give an interpretation of those results in some “affine spacetimes”, which were introduced by the author in a preceding work.



Introduction

The Minkowski problem in Euclidean space

Let
d\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{E}^{d+1}
denote the
(d+1)
-dimensional
Euclidean affine space
and let
\mathbb{S}^{d}
be the
unit sphere
in the Euclidean vector space
(\mathbb{V}^{d+1},\|\cdot\|_{euc})
of translations of
\mathbb{E}^{d+1}
. Given a bounded convex domain
K
of
\mathbb{E}^{d+1}
and a Borel subset
b
of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, the
parallel
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)
consists of all the points
P\in\mathbb{E}^{d+1}
at distance less then
\varepsilon
from
\partial K
having orthogonal projection
P^{\prime}
onto
\partial K
such that
\overrightarrow{P^{\prime}P}
is collinear to a vector
\vec{n}\in b
. Notice that we have

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(\mathbb{S}^{d})=K+\varepsilon\mathbb{S}^{d}\,.

Fenchel and Jessen have proved
[
20
]
that the volume of such neighbourhoods satisfies a
local Steiner Formula
: it is uniquely decomposed as

\mathrm{vol}_{euc}{\bigl({\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)}\bigr)}=\frac{1}{d+1}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\varepsilon^{d+1-i}\binom{d+1}{i}S_{i}(K)(b)\,,

where each
S_{i}(K)
is a finite Radon measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, called the
area measure of order
i
of
K
. The measure
S_{0}(K)
is always
\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}
the volume on the Riemannian unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset(\mathbb{V}^{d+1},\|\cdot\|_{euc})
.

We shall say that the
d
-th area measure
S_{d}(K)
is
“the” area measure
of
K
and denote it by
\mathrm{A}(K)
. Notice that it can equivalently be seen as some sort of derivative of the volume of parallel neighbourhoods of
K
:

\mathrm{A}(K)=S_{d}(K)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{euc}{\bigl({\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)}\bigr)}}{\varepsilon}\,.

The area measure generalises the notion of
Gaussian curvature
: if
K
is a bounded convex domain of
\mathbb{E}^{d+1}
with a locally uniformly convex
C^{2}
boundary, its area measure
\mathrm{A}(K)
is the measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
of the form

\mathrm{A}(K)=\frac{1}{\phi}\,\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\,,

where
\phi(\vec{n})
is the Gaussian curvature of
\partial K
at the point
P(\vec{n})\in\partial K
with exterior normal vector
\vec{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

The
generalised Euclidean Minkowski problem
is the question of the existence of a bounded convex domain
K
with prescribed area measure
\mathrm{A}(K)
, generalising the
classical Euclidean Minkowski problem

[
39
]
asking for the existence of a
C^{2}
bounded of
\mathbb{E}^{d+1}
having specified
Gaussian curvature
.

It has been solved by Alexandrov
[
2
]
, who has shown that for any finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
with support not included in any hemisphere and satisfying an “equilibrium” condition

(Eq)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\vec{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\vec{n})=0\,,

there exists a unique (up to translation) bounded convex domains
K
inside the Euclidean affine space with area measure
\mathrm{A}(K)=\mu
.

Alexandrov’s proof of the existence of a solution relies on polyhedral approximation and the fact that Minkowski had already solved the problem for area measures coming from bounded polyhedra via a variational method
[
38
]
. A completely variational proof of that fact has later been found by Carlier
[
15
]
. That proof is based on the study of
volume functional
on bounded convex domains of
\mathbb{E}^{d+1}
through the use of their
support functions
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

The uniqueness, up to translation, of the solution of the generalised Minkowski problem is then an independent convex geometry fact, which is a consequence of the Brunn–Minkowski inequality
[
47
, Theorem 8.1.1]
.

The invariant Minkowski Problem in Minkowski space

Let
d\geq 1
and let
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
denote the
(d+1)
-dimensional
time-oriented Minkowski affine space
, that is the oriented real affine space endowed with a complete flat Lorentzian metric and a time orientation. Let
\mathbb{H}^{d}
denote the one-sheeted hyperboloid consisting of all unit future timelike vectors of the Minkowski vector space
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}=(\mathbb{V}^{d+1},\|\cdot\|_{d,1})
of translations of the Minkowski affine space
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
. The metric
\|\cdot\|_{d,1}
induces a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, giving the classical hyperboloid model of the
d
-dimensional
hyperbolic space
.

An
F
-convex domains
of
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
[
22
,
13
]
is a convex domain
K
equal to its own future (denoted by
J^{+}(K)
) and such that every spacelike linear hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
directs a supporting affine hyperplane of
K
. Notice that the condition
K=J^{+}(K)
imposes that any supporting hyperplane
H
of
K
is either
spacelike
, i.e. the restriction of
\|\cdot\|_{d,1}
onto
H
has signature
(d,0,0)
, or
null
, i.e. the restriction of
\|\cdot\|_{d,1}
onto
H
has signature
(d-1,0,1)
.

Bonsante has shown
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]
that if
K
is an
F
-convex domain of
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
, there is an orthogonal (for the ambient Lorentzian metric) projection from
K
to its boundary
\partial K
, closely related to the Lorentzian cosmological time on
(K,\|\cdot\|_{d,1})

[
4
]
. Hence, like in the Euclidean case, one can define parallel neighbourhoods. Consider
K
an
F
-convex domain of
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
and
b
a Borel subset of
\mathbb{H}^{d}
. The
parallel
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)
consists of all the points
P\in K
at Lorentzian distance at most
\varepsilon
from
\partial K
having orthogonal projection
P^{\prime}
onto
\partial K
such that
\overrightarrow{P^{\prime}P}
is collinear to a vector
\vec{n}\in b
. Those parallel neighbourhoods now satisfy

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(\mathbb{H}^{d})=K\setminus(K+\varepsilon\mathbb{H}^{d})\,.

The volume of such neighbourhoods also satisfies a
local Steiner Formula

[
22
]
: it uniquely decomposes as

\mathrm{vol}_{d,1}{\bigl({\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)}\bigr)}=\frac{1}{d+1}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\varepsilon^{d+1-i}\binom{d+1}{i}S_{i}(K)(b)\,,

where each
S_{i}(K)
is a Radon measure on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{d}
and
S_{0}(K)
is always
\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, the hyperbolic volume on the hyperboloid. Once again the area measure
\mathrm{A}(K)=S_{d}(K)
generalises Gaussian curvature: if
K
is an
F
-convex domain of
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
with a locally uniformly convex
C^{2}
boundary, its area measure
A(K)
is a Radon measure on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
satisfying

\mathrm{A}(K)=\frac{1}{\phi}\,\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\,,

where
\phi(\vec{n})
is the
extrinsic Gaussian curvature
of the hypersurface
\partial K
inside
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
at the point
P(\vec{n})\in\partial K
with exterior normal vector
\vec{n}\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
.

In that setting, Bonsante and Fillastre
[
13
]
have solved and
invariant generalised Minkowski problem
(concerning prior results, see the paragraph preceding the statement of Theorem
IV
). They have considered
F
-convex domains of
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
invariant under a subgroup
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
of affine transformations obtained by adding translations parts to a uniform lattice
\Gamma<\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)
. The study of such groups was motivated by the work of Mess
[
37
,
3
]
, Bonsante
[
14
]
, and Barbot
[
7
]
, proving that they describe all the

(d+1)
-dimensional flat Lorentzian spacetimes with compact hyperbolic Cauchy surface.

Then, the area measure
A(K)
of a
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
F
-convex domain is a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, thus descending to a finite measure on the closed hyperbolic manifold
\mathbb{H}^{d}/\Gamma
. Bonsante and Fillastre have proved that for any finite measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}^{d}/\Gamma
, there exists a unique
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
F
-convex domain in
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
with area measure descending to
\mu
. Note that unlike the Euclidean case, the measure
\mu
does not have to satisfy any “equilibrium” condition (
Eq
).

Their proof is variational and similar to Carlier’s proof of the Euclidean case: it relies on the use of the
covolume functional
on invariant
F
-convex domains and of their
hyperbolic support functions
on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
.

Affine Steiner Formula

Let
d\geq 2
, let
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
denote the
(d+1)
-dimensional
oriented real affine space
endowed with a parallel volume form
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}
. Let
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
denote the vector space of translations of
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
, which is naturally endowed with the linear volume form
\mathit{vol}
inducing
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}
on
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
. Let
\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
denote the space of volume and orientation preserving linear transformations of
(\mathbb{V}^{d+1},\mathit{vol})
.

An open convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
is said to be
proper
if it is non-empty and its closure does not contain any entire line.
Given a proper open convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
, we shall consider the so-called
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains
of the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
(see Figure
2.1
). A
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
is a convex domain having
asymptotic cone
(or
recession cone
) equal to
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
, the closure of
\mathcal{C}
, i.e. it is a convex domain
K\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
such that

\left\{\vec{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;\forall P\in K,\ P+\mathbb{R}_{+}\vec{v}\subseteq K\right\}=\overline{\mathcal{C}}\,.

Note that the set of linear hyperplanes of
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
that do not intersect
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
is given by
\overline{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})}
, the closure of the projectivisation of the proper open convex cone
\mathcal{C}^{*}
dual to
\mathcal{C}
:

\mathcal{C}^{*}\coloneqq\mathrm{int}\left\{\varphi\in(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}\;\middle|\;\varphi(\vec{v})\leq 0,\ \forall\,\vec{v}\in\mathcal{C}\right\}\,,

where
\mathrm{int}
denotes the interior of a set. Thus, any supporting hyperplane of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain must be directed by an element in
\overline{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})}
. An affine hyperplane directed by an element of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
will be called
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike
.

A central idea in the study of convex subsets of
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
is convex duality: one should see convex subsets as intersections of half-spaces rather than sets of points.
In the Euclidean and Minkowski cases described above, the “spheres”
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
\mathbb{H}^{d}
allow to define a normal direction from a supporting hyperplane of a convex set. In our affine setting,
this “sphere” will be
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
, the
Cheng–Yau unit affine sphere
associated with
\mathcal{C}\subset(\mathbb{V}^{d+1},\mathit{vol})

[
16
,
35
]
. It is a uniquely defined smooth convex hypersurface asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, endowed with a canonical volume form
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}
, see subsection
4.1
.

Remark 0.1
.

Note that when
\mathcal{C}
is the cone of future timelike vectors in the Minkowski vector space
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
, the
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains are exactly the
F
-convex domains from
[
22
,
13
]
, and the affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
is
\mathbb{H}^{d}
. Then, we shall recover all the tools from convex geometry in the Minkowski space.

Thanks to that normal direction, we have proved
[
1
, Section 6]
that, similarly to the Minkowski setting, every
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
admits a well-defined projection from itself to its boundary
\partial K
, that gives a foliation of
K
by the hypersurfaces
(\partial(K+t\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}))_{t>0}
. That allows to define the parallel
\varepsilon
-neighbourhoods for
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains similarly to the Minkowski and Euclidean cases (see subsection
3.1
). Using duality, that is the correspondence between a
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike hyperplane and its normal vector given by
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
, they are defined in the following way. Consider
K
an
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain of
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
and
b
a Borel subset of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
,
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)
consists of all the points
P\in K\setminus(K+\varepsilon\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}})
such that at
P^{\prime}
, the normal projection of
P
onto
\partial K
,
K
has a supporting hyperplane directed by an element in
b
.

Then, the volume
V_{\varepsilon}(K)
of the parallel
\varepsilon
-neighbourhoods will be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
(Proposition
3.2
). Similarly, using duality, the support function of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain, which indicates the position of its supporting hyperplanes, will be a function on the hypersurface
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
dual to
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
, which happens to be the Cheng–Yau affine sphere asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}^{*}

[
25
]
, and which is radially identified with
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
.

Remark 0.2
.

That might look different from the Euclidean and Minkowski cases where no duality seems to be involved. Actually, it is not. Using its bilinear form, the Euclidean vector space
(\mathbb{V}^{d+1},\|\cdot\|_{euc})
(respectively the Minkowski vector space
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
) is usually implicitly identified with its dual space, then the unit sphere (resp. the unit hyperboloid) is an self-dual hypersurface:
\mathbb{S}^{d}=(\mathbb{S}^{d})^{*}
(resp.
\mathbb{H}^{d}=(\mathbb{H}^{d})^{*}
). Hence, support functions and area measures are often seen as supported by
\mathbb{S}^{d}
(resp.
\mathbb{H}^{d}
) rather than
(\mathbb{S}^{d})^{*}
(resp.
(\mathbb{H}^{d})^{*}
).

We then prove that those measures satisfy a local Steiner Formula.

Theorem I

(Local Steiner Formula)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
be a proper open convex cone and
K\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
be a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain. Then, there exist a unique family of Radon measures
S_{0}(K)
,
\dots
,
S_{d}(K)
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
, such that for all
\varepsilon>0
and
b
a Borel set of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
,

V_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)=\mathrm{vol}{\bigl({\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)}\bigr)}=\frac{1}{d+1}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\varepsilon^{d+1-i}\binom{d+1}{i}S_{i}(K)(b)\,.

Moreover, one always has
S_{0}(K)=\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}}
, where
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}}
is the canonical volume form on the affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
radially identified with
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
.

Remark 0.3
.

In general, the affine sphere has no reason to be self-dual, that is, there might not be an diffeomorphism
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}\to\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
sending the
affine invariants
of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
onto those of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
. Nevertheless, there is always a canonical smooth diffeomorphic identification
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
given by the
Blaschke conormal
(see
[
35
]
for more details). This diffeomorphism actually sends
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}
onto
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}}
, so from now we shall abusively denote by
\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}
the canonical volume on
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}\simeq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
given by the affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
.

We thus define the
area measure
of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
as the Radon measure
\mathrm{A}(K)\coloneqq S_{d}(K)
given by Theorem
I
.

In the case where
K
is a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain with a locally uniformly convex
C^{2}
boundary, the situation is similar to the Euclidean and Minkowski cases: we prove that its area measure
A(K)
is of the form

\mathrm{A}(K)=\frac{1}{\phi}\,\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}\,,

where
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}
is the canonical volume on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
given by the affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
(see the remark above), and
\phi
is (the push-forward by the Gauss map of) the
Gaussian curvature of
\partial K
relative to the affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
, or more precisely
relative to the pair
(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}},\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*})
, which we shall call
\mathcal{C}
-curvature
. That curvature is
relative
in the sense of
affine differential geometry

[
33
]
, and is obtained in the following way. First, endow
\partial K
with the following transverse vector field
N
, which we shall call the
\mathcal{C}
-normal vector field on
\partial K
: for every point
P\in\partial K\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
, let
N(P)
be the vector
\vec{n}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d}
such that the tangent hyperplane of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
at
\vec{n}
has the same direction as the tangent hyperplane of
\partial K
at
P
(see Figure
4.1
). Then, as for the usual extrinsic Gaussian curvature in a Riemannian setting,
\mathcal{C}
-curvature is obtained by considering the infinitesimal variation of the
\mathcal{C}
-normal field on the hypersurface
\partial K
.

Remark 0.4
.

That construction of
\mathcal{C}
-curvature actually generalises Euclidean and Minkowski geometry:

•

In the Euclidean affine space
\mathbb{E}^{d+1}
, the Gauss structural equations of an immersed hypersurface come from its affine differential geometry relative to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
(which is an affine sphere); the normal vector to any affine hyperplane
H
is the vector of
\vec{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
having tangent hyperplane with the same direction as
H
.

•

In the Minkowski affine space
\mathbb{M}^{d,1}
, the Gauss structural equations of an immersed spacelike hypersurface come from its affine differential geometry relative to the hyperboloid
\mathbb{H}^{d}
(which is an affine sphere); the normal vector to any spacelike affine hyperplane
H
is the vector of
\vec{n}\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
having tangent hyperplane with the same direction as
H
.

An affine invariant Minkowski problem

We now consider the case when the cone
\mathcal{C}
is
divisible
by a discrete torsion-free subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
, that is the projective action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C})
is free, properly discontinuous and cocompact. Let
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
be an
affine deformation
of
\Gamma
, that is a discrete subgroup of orientation and volume preserving affine transformations obtained by adding translation parts to elements of
\Gamma
. Then, one checks that the area measures of
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains descend to Radon measures on the projective manifold
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})/\Gamma
, given by the quotient of the proper convex projective domain
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
by the dual projective action of
\Gamma
, which is also free, properly discontinuous and cocompact
[
10
, Lemma 2.8]
.

In that affine invariant setting, we solve the following Minkowski problem.

Theorem II
.

Let
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
be an affine deformation of a subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
dividing a proper open convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
. For every finite Radon measure
\mu
on the closed convex projective manifold
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})/\Gamma
, there exists a unique
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
with area measure
\mathrm{A}(K)=\mu
.

Even though the
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains are unbounded, the divisibility condition, which is a cocompactness property, and the volume preserving nature of the transformations we study, allow to introduce their
covolume
. Inspired by Carlier’s variational approach and the work of Bonsante and Fillastre, we show that, in some sense area measures are derivatives of the covolume functional.

Theorem III
.

The Gateau derivative of the covolume functional at a
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
is the area measure
\mathrm{A}(K)
.

For a more rigorous and precise statement of Theorem
III
, we refer to Theorem
6.8
and Remark
6.11
.

Using Theorem
III
, we proceed and get a variational proof that the affine invariant Minkowski problem always admits a solution.

It is worth noticing that there is a Monge–Ampère equation underlying that affine Minkowski problem. Indeed, by arbitrarily choosing an affine chart to identify
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
with a bounded open convex domain
\Omega^{*}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, support functions of
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains can be seen as convex functions on
\Omega^{*}
. We show (Proposition
4.15
) that the area measure of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
satisfies

\mathrm{A}(K)=(-\omega_{\mathcal{C}})\mathrm{MA}(s_{K})\,,

where
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}:\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
is the support function of the affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathrm{MA}(s_{K})
is the Monge–Ampère measure of
s_{K}:\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
, the support function of
K
. Hence, prescribing the area measure
\mu
is equivalent to finding a solution of a Monge–Ampère equation:

\mathrm{MA}(s)=\frac{1}{(-\omega_{\mathcal{C}})}\mu\,.

Then, the uniqueness of the solution in Theorem
II
is a consequence from standard arguments of Monge–Ampère equation theory.

II
extends a long series of results:

•

\Gamma_{\tau}=\Gamma<\mathrm{SO}(d,1)
is a uniform lattice,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathbb{H}^{d}
, and
\mu
of the form
\phi\,\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, with
\phi
a smooth positive function on
\mathbb{H}^{d}/\Gamma

[
43
]
,

•

Barbot, Béguin and Zeghib have proved it for any affine deformation
\Gamma_{\tau}
of a Fuchsian subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SO}(2,1)
,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathbb{H}^{2}
, and
\mu
of the form
\phi\,\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
, with
\phi
a positive smooth function on
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Gamma

[
5
]
,

•

Bonsante and Fillastre have proved it for any affine deformation
\Gamma_{\tau}
of a uniform lattice
\Gamma<\mathrm{SO}(d,1)
,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathbb{H}^{d}
, and
\mu
any finite Radon measure on
\mathbb{H}^{d}/\Gamma

[
13
]
,

•

Labourie
[
30
, Theorem 8.2.1]
and later Nie and Seppi
[
41
]
have proved it, with different approaches, for any affine deformation
\Gamma_{\tau}
of a group
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{3})
dividing a convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{3}
, and
\mu=k\,\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}}
, where
k>0
is constant
[
41
]
.

In fact, Labourie and Nie and Seppi were not considering
\mathcal{C}
-curvature, but the distinct
affine Gaussian curvature
. Nevertheless, if the boundary of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain has constant
\mathcal{C}
-curvature, we prove that it also has a constant (with a different value) affine Gaussian curvature.

Theorem IV
.

Let
K
be convex domain in
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
with
C^{2}
boundary. If
\partial K
has constant
\mathcal{C}
-curvature
\kappa>0
, then it has constant affine Gaussian curvature
\kappa^{2(d+1)/(d+2)}
.

Note that affine Gaussian curvature is only defined for
C^{2}
hypersurfaces, while the notion of
\mathcal{C}
-curvature can naturally be generalised to the boundary of any
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain via area measures.

Relations to affine spacetimes

In
[
1
]
, we have introduced a notion of
affine spacetime
structure, with which the quotients of the invariant domains considered above are naturally endowed. If
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
is an affine deformation of a discrete subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
dividing a proper open convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
, by the work of Nie and Seppi
[
41
]
, and Choi
[
18
]
, there exist a maximal
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
D_{\tau}\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
. Then, we have shown that
(D_{\tau}/\Gamma_{\tau},\mathcal{C})
is a
Maximal Globally Hyperbolic affine spacetimes admitting a
C^{2}
locally uniformly Convex and Compact Cauchy surface
, denoted as
MGHCC affine spacetimes
and that, conversely, any MGHCC affine spacetime can be built in that way
[
1
, Theorems I and II]
.

When the cone
\mathcal{C}
is quadratic, that is a theorem on
flat Lorentzian spacetimes
due to Mess
[
37
,
3
]
for
d=2
, and Bonsante
[
14
]
and Barbot
[
7
]
for
d\geq 2
.

Hence, Theorem
II
can produce
Cauchy surfaces
inside MGHCC affine spacetimes with prescribed
\mathcal{C}
-curvature. But one has to be careful, as the boundary
S=\partial K
of a
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
might not completely be included inside the open convex set
D_{\tau}
: one could have
\partial K\cap\partial D_{\tau}\neq\emptyset
. Still, like in the flat Lorentzian case
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
, if the
total mass
of the area measure
\mathrm{A}(K)
is big enough, that cannot happen.

Theorem V
.

Let
M=(D_{\tau}/\Gamma_{\tau},\mathcal{C})
be an MGHCC affine spacetime. Then, there exists a constant
m(M)\geq 0
such that if
\mu
is a Radon measure on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})/\Gamma
with total mass
\mu(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})/\Gamma)>m(M)
, there is a unique convex Cauchy surface
S
in
M
with area measure equal to
\mu
.

When that total mass condition is satisfied, one can also use regularity results on the
Monge–Ampère equation
underlying the Minkowski problem to get information on the regularity of the solution.

Theorem VI
.

Let
M=(D_{\tau}/\Gamma_{\tau},\mathcal{C})
be an MGHCC affine spacetime. Then, there exists a constant
\varepsilon(M)\in(0,+\infty]
such that if
\phi
is a positive
C^{k+1}
function on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})/\Gamma
with
k\geq 2
, and such that
\phi<\varepsilon(M)
, then there is a unique
C^{k+2}
Cauchy surface
S
in
M
with
\mathcal{C}
-curvature equal to
\phi
.

If
d=2
or
D_{\tau}
is simple (see Definition
7.9
), the constant
\varepsilon(M)
can be taken to be
+\infty
.

Note that the total mass condition cannot be removed from the statement of Theorem
V
. Indeed, for all
d\geq 3
, Bonsante and Fillastre
[
13
, subsection 3.7]
have provided examples, based on the work of Pogorelov
[
44
]
, of an affine deformation
\Gamma_{\tau}
of a uniform lattice
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)
, and a positive measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}^{d}/\Gamma
(that is such that
\mu\geq m\,\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
with
m>0
) such that the future-convex domain with area measure
\mu
given by Theorem
II
meets the boundary
\partial D_{\tau}
.

Dimension
2+1
and higher Teichmüller theory

Finally, let us write a few words about the special case when
d=2
, and the relation between the work of the present article and (higher) Teichmüller theory.

First, let us start by considering the case when the cone
\mathcal{C}
is quadratic, i.e. convex geometry inside the Minkowski space
\mathbb{M}^{2,1}
. That setting is closely related to
classical Teichmüller theory
. Indeed, if
S_{g}
is a closed oriented and connected surface of genus
g\geq 2
, its
Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}(S_{g})
can be seen as a connected component of the character variety

\chi{\bigl({\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1)}\bigr)}\coloneqq\text{Hom}{\bigl({\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1)}\bigr)}\big/\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1)\,,

where the quotient is by conjugation. More precisely,
\mathcal{T}(S_{g})
is diffeomorphic to
\chi^{fd}(\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1))
one of the two connected components of the character variety consisting of conjugacy classes of faithful and discrete representations
[
26
]
, i.e. representations
\rho
such that
\rho(\pi_{1}(S_{g}))
is a uniform lattice of
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1)
.

If
[\rho]\in\mathcal{T}(S_{g})
, through the special identification between the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(2,1)
and the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{2,1}
, an affine deformations of
\rho(\pi_{1}(S_{g}))
can actually be seen as vector in the tangent space
\mathrm{T}_{[\rho]}\mathcal{T}(S_{g})
. By the work of Mess
[
37
,
3
]
, an affine deformation
\Gamma_{\tau}
of
\Gamma=\rho(\pi_{1}(S_{g}))
can also be identified with a
measured geodesic lamination
on the hyperbolic surface
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\rho(\pi_{1}(S_{g}))
, which is another model for tangent vectors of
\mathrm{T}_{[\rho]}\mathcal{T}(S_{g})
.

The work of Barbot and Fillastre
[
6
]
has highlighted how that fact can be recovered through convex geometry in the Minkowski space. The first area measure
S_{1}(D_{\tau})
of the maximal
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
F
-convex domain
D_{\tau}\subset\mathbb{M}^{2,1}
(given by the Steiner Formula, Theorem
I
) induces a finite measure on
\mathbb{H}^{d}/\Gamma
supported by a geodesic lamination, from which one can recover the measured lamination.

Another tool from Teichmüller theory which can be recovered through convex geometry in the affine Minkowski space
\mathbb{M}^{2,1}
is
grafting
. Indeed, let
\Gamma_{\tau}
be an affine deformation of a Fuchsian subgroup. Then, the surface
\partial(D_{\tau}+\mathbb{H}^{2})\subset\mathbb{M}^{2,1}
is a
C^{1,1}
surface endowed with a
C^{0,1}
Riemannian metric induced by the ambient Lorentzian metric of
\mathbb{M}^{2,1}
. By the work of Benedetti and Bonsante
[
9
]
, it actually is the Riemannian surface obtained by the grafting of the hyperbolic surface
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Gamma
along the measured geodesic lamination associated with the affine deformation
\Gamma_{\tau}
described above.

Now, let us consider the more general case of a divisible cone
\mathcal{C}
. That setting is closely related to
higher Teichmüller theory
. Indeed, if
S_{g}
is a closed oriented and connected surface of genus
g\geq 2
, a theorem of Choi and Goldman
[
17
]
states that the
Hitchin component

[
29
]
of the representation space of
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
into
\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{3})
is also the
moduli space of convex projective structures on
S_{g}
, and it can be described as

\mathrm{Hit}(S_{g})=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\bigl({\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{3})}\bigr)}\Big/\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{3})\,,

where the quotient is by conjugation and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}(\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{3}))
is the set of faithful and discrete group representations
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{3}))
such that there is a proper open convex cone
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{V}^{3}
divisible by
\rho(\pi_{1}(S_{g}))
.

In that setting, the area measure
S_{1}(D_{\tau})
and the surface
\partial(D_{\tau}+\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}})\subset\mathbb{A}^{2+1}
are still-well defined. Hence, we can hope for a natural generalisation of measured geodesic lamination and a generalised grafting process for convex projective surfaces.

Note that we have based our study of
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains on affine spheres, which were used independently by Loftin
[
34
]
and Labourie
[
30
]
in order to get a holomorphic parametrisation of the
\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{3})
-Hitchin component. Thus, we could hope that a generalised grafting process obtained in that way would have a nice behaviour in the Labourie–Loftin parametrisation of
\mathrm{Hit}(S_{g})
.

It would also be interesting to see if we can relate our work with the one of Bobb and Farre
[
11
]
concerning the correspondence between affine deformation of dividing groups and affine laminations
[
28
]
.

Organisation of the article

In Section
1
, we describe the setting of this article and introduce a parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
of the affine space which will be useful for computations. In Section
2
, we introduce and study
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains and the Cheng–Yau affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
. Section
3

is dedicated to the proof, via an approximation argument, of the local Steiner Formula (first part of Theorem
I
), allowing to define area measures for
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains. Then, in Section
4
, we use affine differential geometry relative to the Cheng–Yau affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
in order to introduce a notion of
\mathcal{C}
-curvature for
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains with enough regularity. We then proceed to exhibit the relation between area measures and
\mathcal{C}
-curvature, and prove the second part of Theorem
I
and a stronger version of Theorem
IV
. We also highlight the relation between the area measure of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain and the Monge–Ampère measure of its support function. Section
5
focuses on the case of
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains invariant under an affine deformation
\Gamma_{\tau}
of a discrete subgroup
\Gamma
dividing
\mathcal{C}
. In Section
6
, we introduce the covolume functional on such invariant domains, we prove its strict convexity and that, in some sense, its derivative is the area measure (Theorem
III
). Finally, in Section
7
, we solve the Minkowski problem for such invariant domains (Theorem
II
), and explain how classical facts from Monge–Ampère equation theory provide some regularity results implying Theorems
V
and
VI
.

In addition, Appendix
A
presents Dirichlet–Lee fundamental domains for the actions of affine deformation of dividing subgroups. Those convex fundamental domains appear in the proof of the strict convexity of the covolume. Appendix
B
provides, for the convenience of the reader, the proof of an approximation lemma for
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain due to Choi, which is used in the proof of Theorem
III
.

Setting and parametrisation of the affine space

1.1.
Setting of the article

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer. Let
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
be the
(d+1)
-dimensional real vector space endowed with an orientation and a linear volume form
\mathit{vol}
. Let
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
be the
(d+1)
-dimensional real affine space with
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
as its vector space of translations. It is endowed with an orientation, a flat affine connection
D
and a parallel differential volume form
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}
, induced by
\mathit{vol}
on
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
. We shall denote by
\mathrm{vol}
the measure on
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
obtained by integrating the form
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}
.

The dual vector space
(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
is the space of real linear forms on
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
. It is naturally endowed with a linear volume form
\mathit{vol}^{*}
defined in the following way: the volume of a basis
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d+1}
of
(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
is

\mathit{vol}^{*}(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d+1})=\frac{1}{\mathit{vol}(\vec{e}_{d+1},\dots,\vec{e}_{1})}\,,

where
\vec{e}_{1},\dots,\vec{e}_{d+1}
is the basis of
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
pre-dual to
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d+1}
.

Definition 1.1

(Proper convex cone)
.

A
cone
in the vector space
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
is a subset
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
such that for all
\vec{v}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\lambda>0
, one has
\lambda\vec{v}\in\mathcal{C}
.

A cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
is
convex
if for all
\vec{v},\vec{w}\in\mathcal{C}
and
t\in[0,1]
, one has
(1-t)\vec{v}+t\vec{w}\in\mathcal{C}
. A convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
is said to be
proper
if it is non-empty and its closure does not contain any entire line.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
be a proper open convex cone and
\mathcal{C}^{*}\subset(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
be the open
polar cone
to
\mathcal{C}
. That is, the proper open convex cone in
(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
defined as

(1.1)

\mathcal{C}^{*}\coloneqq\mathrm{int}\left\{\varphi\in(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}\;\middle|\;\varphi(\vec{v})\leq 0,\ \forall\,\vec{v}\in\mathcal{C}\right\},

where
\mathrm{int}
denotes the interior of a set. Using the identity (
1.1
) and identifying each elements
[\varphi]\in\mathbb{P}((\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*})
with the linear hyperplane
\ker\varphi\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
, the set
\overline{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})}
can be seen as the set of
supporting hyperplanes
of the convex cone
\mathcal{C}
, that is of linear hyperplanes
H\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
such that
H\cap\mathcal{C}=\emptyset
and
H\cap\partial\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
. Among those hyperplanes,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
is the set of linear hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
such that
H\cap\overline{\mathcal{C}}=\{0\}
. As a set of linear hyperplanes in
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
can also be seen as a set of directions of affine hyperplanes in
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
. Let us then introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.2

(
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike hyperplane)
.

An affine hyperplane of
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
is said to be
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike
if it is directed by an element in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
.

Remark 1.3
.

The adjective “spacelike” comes from the special case where the cone
\mathcal{C}
is quadratic. In that case, the vector space
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
can be endowed with a quadratic form of signature
(d,1)
with isotropic cone
\partial\mathcal{C}\cup(-\partial\mathcal{C})
. Then,
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
is endowed with a flat Lorentzian metric and
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike hyperplanes are spacelike in the Lorentzian sense.

Let
\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
denote the group of volume preserving linear automorphisms of
(\mathbb{V}^{d+1},\mathit{vol})
and
\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
denote the group of volume preserving affine automorphisms of
(\mathbb{A}^{d+1},\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol})
. We shall denote by
\star
the
dual action
of
\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
on
(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
; for all
\varphi\in(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
and
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
,

\gamma\star\varphi\coloneqq\varphi\circ\gamma^{-1}\,,

and by
*
the
projective dual action
of
\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
; for all
\varphi\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
and
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
,

\gamma*[\varphi]\coloneqq[\gamma\star\varphi]=[\varphi\circ\gamma^{-1}]\,.

Note that for all
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
,
\varphi\in(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*}
and
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
,

(1.2)

(\gamma\star\varphi)(\gamma\cdot\vec{v})=\varphi(\vec{v})\,.

2.1.

A convex domain of the affine space

is a non-empty open set

such that for all

[PQ]

A

is an affine hyperplane

such that

and

. For such a hyperplane, we shall write that
“

Definition 2.1

(
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain)
.

A
\mathcal{C}
-convex

K\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
such that:

•

K+\mathcal{C}=K
,

•

the set
\mathcal{H}(K)\subset\mathbb{P}((\mathbb{V}^{d+1})^{*})
of directions of supporting hyperplanes of
K

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})\subseteq\mathcal{H}(K)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})}\,.

The
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike boundary
of
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain

, denoted by
\partial_{sp}K
, is the set of points

having a supporting hyperplane with direction in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
.

A
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain

is said to be
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike
if
\partial K=\partial_{sp}K
.

Remark 2.2
.

Using a classical fact from convex analysis
[
46

,
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains can be equivalently be characterised in the following way. A
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain is a convex domain
K\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
having
asymptotic cone
(or

) equal to
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
, the closure of

Then, it is straightforward to notice that in the parametrisation

Proposition 2.6

In the parametrisation

The

-convex domain

In

{\tilde{s}_{K}}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{C}^{*}}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{R}}\\

One checks that it is well-defined (i.e. the supremum is always finite) by definition of a

The

-support function

Definition 2.7

(Legendre–Fenchel transform)

Let

be a convex domain. The

using the usual scalar product

\mathbb{R}^{d}
to identify

\mathbb{R}^{d}

Proposition 2.8

(
[
1

In the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
, let

be a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain of

, the Legendre–Fenchel transform of
s

Conversely, any convex function

gives a

Here is a classical convex geometry fact

Proposition 2.9

(

In the parametrisation

.

Note that for every point

The polar affine sphere

3.1.
Parallel neighbourhood volume measures

In this subsection, we shall introduce the
parallel
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood volume measures
of
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains which are
Radon measures
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
. So here is a reminder concerning measure theory in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. On an open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a Radon measure is just a Borel measure which is finite on compact sets. Such a measure
\mu
is:

•

inner regular
, that is for any Borel set
b\subseteq U

\mu(b)=\sup\left\{\mu(A)\;\middle|\;A\subseteq b\ \text{and $A$ is compact}\right\},

•

outer regular
, that is for any Borel set
b\subseteq U
,

\mu(b)=\inf\left\{\mu(O)\;\middle|\;b\subseteq O\ \text{and $O$ is open}\right\}.

Given a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we can introduce the
parallel
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood volume measure
of
K
as the measure
V_{\varepsilon}(K)
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
defined in the following way. For any Borel subset
b\subseteq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
,
V_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)
is defined as the volume of the
parallel
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)\subset\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
, which is the set of points of the form
P+t\,\vec{n}
where

and
\vec{n}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
both have a common supporting hyperplane direction in

, and

.

Using the Gauss map
\mathcal{G}_{K}
(Definition
2.4
) and the
\mathcal{C}
-normal field

on

(Definition

), it can equivalently be described in the following way. For any Borel subset

is the volume of the

\varepsilon
-neighbourhood

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)\coloneqq\left\{P+t\,N_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}[\varphi]\;\middle|\;\ [\varphi]\in b,P\in\mathcal{G}_{K}^{-1}[\varphi],\ t\in(0,\varepsilon)\right\}\subset K\,,

where
P\in\mathcal{G}_{K}^{-1}[\varphi]
means that
P\in\partial_{sp}K
is such that

.
In the case where
K
is a
C^{2}_{+}

\mathcal{C}
-convex domain, and thus

is a genuine bijective map, that becomes

(3.1)

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(b)=\left\{\mathcal{G}_{K}^{-1}[\varphi]+t\,N_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}[\varphi]\;\middle|\;[\varphi]\in b,\ t\in(0,\varepsilon)\right\}\,.

Remark 3.1
.

Using the causal distance on

given by the affine sphere

[

,

can be interpreted as set of points

of

-normal projection

onto

is collinear to an element of

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
K
be a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain and
\varepsilon>0
. Then,
V_{\varepsilon}(K)
is a Radon measure on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
.

Proof.

Let us work in the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
described in Section
1
, where
V_{\varepsilon}(K)
is a measure on the bounded convex domain
\Omega^{*}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
s
be the support function of
K
.

We shall prove that for all
A\subset\Omega^{*}
compact,
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(A)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is bounded, and thus that its Lebesgue volume
V_{\varepsilon}(K)(A)
is finite. Let
A
be a compact subset of
\Omega^{*}
. As
N_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}
is continuous on
\Omega^{*}
, the image
N_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}(A)
is compact. Hence, as
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(A)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{K}^{-1}(A)+[0,\varepsilon]\,N_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}(A)
, we just have to show that
\mathcal{G}_{K}^{-1}(A)
is bounded.

By contradiction, assume that there exists an unbounded sequence
(X_{n})_{n}=(x_{n},s^{*}(x_{n}))_{n}\in\mathcal{G}_{K}^{-1}(A)^{\mathbb{N}}
. That means that for all

,
\partial s^{*}(x_{n})\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Then, by the Fenchel Inequality Theorem
[

, each

is in
\partial s(A)
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
X_{n}\in\graph s^{*}
. Hence, as the sequence
(X_{n})_{n}
is unbounded and

is continuous (it is convex and finite over the whole

), the sequence of

is necessarily unbounded. But, by
[

, the compactness of

imply that
\partial s(A)
is compact, giving a contradiction.

In the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})

\mathcal{C}
-convex domain,

Then, using the local Steiner Formula defining the area measures (Theorem

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

In the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
, let
K
be a
C^{2}_{+}

\mathcal{C}
-convex domain with support function

{\Phi}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{\Omega^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}}&\longrightarrow&{K}\\

induces a diffeomorphism between

and

and

\left|\Jac_{\Phi}(y,t)\right|=-\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\det{\bigl({\Hess(s+t\omega_{\mathcal{C}})(y)}\bigr)}\,.

Proof.

For all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, let us denote
K_{t}=K+t\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
. It is a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain domain (see Remark
2.15
) and by Proposition
2.9
, it is actually
C^{2}_{+}
with support function
s_{t}\coloneqq s+t\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
. Notice that, using (
2.1
), the diffeomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{K_{t}}^{-1}
can be expressed as

{\mathcal{G}_{K_{t}}^{-1}}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{\Omega^{*}}&\longrightarrow&{\partial K_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\\
{y}&\longmapsto&{\left(\begin{matrix}\grad s(y)+t\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\\
\grad s(y)\cdot y+t\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\cdot y-(s+t\omega_{\mathcal{C}})(y)\end{matrix}\right)}\end{matrix}\right.\,.

By Lemma
3.7
, for any open subset
U
of
\Omega^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have
\Phi(U\times(0,\varepsilon))=\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(U)
. In order to conclude, we only have to compute the determinant of the Jacobian matrix of
\Phi
at any point, and notice that it never vanishes.

Let
y\in\Omega^{*}
and

. On one side,

\partial_{t}\Phi(y,t)=\partial_{t}\mathcal{G}_{K_{t}}^{-1}(y)=\left(\begin{matrix}\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\\
\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\cdot y-\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\end{matrix}\right)\,.

On the other side, for all

\partial_{y_{i}}\Phi(y,t)=\left(\begin{matrix}\partial_{y_{i}}\grad s_{t}(y)\\
\partial_{y_{i}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y+\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot e_{i}-\partial_{y_{i}}s_{t}(y)\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\partial_{y_{i}}\grad s_{t}(y)\\
\partial_{y_{i}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y\end{matrix}\right)\,.

Thus,

\det\Jac_{\Phi}(y,t)=\det\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Hess s_{t}(y)&\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\\
\hline\cr\partial_{y_{1}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y\ \vline\ \cdots\ \vline\ \partial_{y_{d}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y&\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\cdot y-\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\end{array}\right)\,.

By

\det\Jac_{\Phi}(y,t)=\det\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c|c|c}\partial_{y_{1}}\grad s_{t}(y)&\cdots&\partial_{y_{d}}\grad s_{t}(y)&\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\\
\hline\cr\partial_{y_{1}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y&\cdots&\partial_{y_{d}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y&\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\cdot y\end{array}\right)\\
-\det\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Hess s_{t}(y)&0\\
\hline\cr\partial_{y_{1}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y\ \vline\ \cdots\ \vline\ \partial_{y_{d}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y&\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\end{array}\right)\,.

Because its lines satisfy

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c|c|c}\partial_{y_{1}}\grad s_{t}(y)&\cdots&\partial_{y_{d}}\grad s_{t}(y)&\grad\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\\

Moreover, we have

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Hess s_{t}(y)&0\\
\hline\cr\partial_{y_{1}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y\ \vline\ \cdots\ \vline\ \partial_{y_{d}}\grad s_{t}(y)\cdot y&\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\end{array}\right)=\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\det{\bigl({\Hess s_{t}(y)}\bigr)}<0\,.

So we finally get

\left|\Jac_{\Phi}(y,t)\right|=-\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\det{\bigl({\Hess s_{t}(y)}\bigr)}=-\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\det{\bigl({\Hess(s+t\omega_{\mathcal{C}})(y)}\bigr)}>0\,.\qed

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Let us work in the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
. By Lemma
3.8
, for any open subset

V_{\varepsilon}(K)(U)=\int_{\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(K)(U)}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{U}\left|\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\Jac_{\Phi}(y,t)\right|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y=\int_{U}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(-\omega_{\mathcal{C}})(y)\det{\Bigl({\Hess(s+t\omega_{\mathcal{C}})(y)}\Bigr)}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y\,.

Thus, by the uniqueness part of the Carathéodory’s Extension Theorem on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that the following measures are equal

(3.2)

V_{\varepsilon}(K)={\left({\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(-\omega_{\mathcal{C}})\det{\bigl({\Hess(s+t\omega_{\mathcal{C}})}\bigr)}\mathrm{d}t}\right)}\mathcal{L}\,,

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\Omega^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As for all
y\in\Omega^{*}

\Hess(s)(y)
and
\Hess(\omega_{\mathcal{C}})(y)
are positive definite symmetric matrices, the determinant

is a degree
d

f(\varepsilon)\coloneqq-\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\det{\bigl({\Hess(s+t\omega_{\mathcal{C}})(y)}\bigr)}\mathrm{d}t

is then a degree

\varepsilon

4.2

where the

are non-negative continuous functions. Setting

\mathbb{R}

(

Let

Proof.

3.2.3.
Proof of the Steiner formula

We now have all the tools needed to prove the local Steiner Formula in the most general setting.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Let
K
be a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain. By Lemma
3.6
, we can use a Lagrange polynomial interpolation with
d+1
fixed distinct positive real numbers
\varepsilon_{0},\dots,\varepsilon_{d}
, in order to get real numbers
(a_{ik})_{0\leq i,k\leq d}
(independent of any choice of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain), such that for any
C^{2}_{+}

\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K^{\prime}
we have

(3.6)

S_{i}(K^{\prime})=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{ik}V_{\varepsilon_{k}}(K^{\prime})\,.

Thus, we set
S_{i}(K)
to be the (a priori signed) Radon measure on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
defined by

(3.7)

S_{i}(K)\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{ik}V_{\varepsilon_{k}}(K)\,.

By Lemma
3.12
, we can set
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be a sequence of
C^{2}_{+}

\mathcal{C}
-convex epi-converging towards
K
. By Lemma
3.15
, for any
\varepsilon
, the measures for

converges vaguely to
V_{\varepsilon}(K)
. Thus, by (
3.6
) and (

), the

’s converges vaguely to the

’s, which are non-negative Radon measures because the

are Radon measures by Proposition
3.2
. At the vague limit of the Steiner Formula for the
\mathcal{C}^{2}_{+}

\mathcal{C}
-convex domains
(K_{n})_{n}
we get:

V_{\varepsilon}(K)=\frac{1}{d+1}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\varepsilon^{d+1-i}\binom{d+1}{i}S_{i}(K)\,.\qed

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
K
be a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain. Then, its area measure

Proposition 3.17
.

Let

4.
Relation between area measures, curvature and Monge–Ampère measures

In this section we shall exhibit the relation between the area measures introduced in Section

In order to introduce

Consider a

of

Theorem 4.7

That generalises the Euclidean
[

[

Finally, let us notice that like Euclidean and Minkowski Gaussian curvature, the

-curvature comes from a relative differential geometry, and thus differs from the classical

given by the affine differential geometry theory

(also called

4.2.
Relation between area measures and Monge–Ampère measures

Let us end this section by highlighting how the area measure of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain is also very closely related to the
Monge–Ampère measure
of its support function, which is a classical measure associated with any convex function.

Definition 4.13

(Monge–Ampère measure of a convex function)
.

Let
U
be an open convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s:U\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function. The
Monge–Ampère measure
of
s
, denoted by
\mathrm{MA}(s)
, is a measure on
U
defined in the following way. For all Borel subset
b\subseteq U
,

\mathrm{MA}(s)(b)\coloneqq\mathcal{L}{\bigl({\partial s(b)}\bigr)}\,,

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Lebesgue measure on
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and we recall that

\partial s(b)\coloneqq\bigcup_{x\in b}\partial s(x)\,.

Proposition 4.14

(
[
21
, Theorem A.31]
)
.

If
s
is a
C^{2}
convex function on an open convex subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then

\mathrm{MA}(s)=\det(\Hess s)\,\mathcal{L}\,.

Here is the relation between the area measure of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain and the Monge–Ampère measure of its support function.

Proposition 4.15
.

In the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
, for every
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
with support function
s
, we have

\mathrm{A}(K)=(-\omega_{\mathcal{C}})\mathrm{MA}(s)\,.

Proof.

In the
C^{2}_{+}
-case, that is a direct consequence from Proposition
4.3
, Corollary
4.9
and Proposition
4.14
. In the general case, consider
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of
C^{2}_{+}
support function converging locally uniformly to
s
given by Lemma
3.12
. On one side, by Proposition
3.17
, we have that
A(K_{n})
converges vaguely to
A(K)
. On the other side, it is known
[
21
, Proposition 2.6]
that the local uniform convergence of
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
towards
s
implies the vague convergence of
(\mathrm{MA}(s_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
towards
\mathrm{MA}(s)
, giving the wanted equality at the vague limit.
∎

5.

In this section we shall consider the case where the convex cone

Definition 5.1

\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})

\Gamma

Proposition 5.2

(
[

)
.

A proper open convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
is divisible by a subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
if and only its polar cone
\mathcal{C}^{*}
is divisible by
\Gamma
(acting with the dual action
\star

Definition 5.3

(Affine deformation of a subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
)
.

An
affine deformation of a subgroup

\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
is a subgroup
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
such that the linear action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
on
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
, the vector space of translation in
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
, is exactly the one of
\Gamma
, i.e. there is a group isomorphism

\Phi_{\tau}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\tau}

such that for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
\gamma_{\tau}\coloneqq\Phi_{\tau}(\gamma)\in\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
satisfies

\mathrm{d}\gamma_{\tau}=\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})\,.

In the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
described in Section
1
, an affine deformation of a subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
is a subgroup

(5.1)

\Gamma_{\tau}=\left\{{\bigl({\gamma,\tau(\gamma)}\bigr)}\;\middle|\;\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

obtained by adding translation parts to elements of
\Gamma
through a translation function
\tau:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. As
\Gamma_{\tau}
is a group, the translation function
\tau
must satisfy the following
cocycle relation
: for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma

\tau({\alpha\beta})=\alpha\tau(\beta)+\tau(\alpha)\,.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let

be two
\tau

Proof.

be the support function of respectively

As the Legendre–Fenchel transform is order reversing, that is equivalent to

s+\varepsilon\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}\leq s^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad s^{\prime}+\varepsilon\omega_{\mathcal{C}}\geq s\,,

which is just

\varepsilon\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}\leq s^{\prime}-s\leq-\varepsilon\omega_{\mathcal{C}}\,.

As

and

are two

. Consider the function

{\Psi}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{[0,1]}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{R}}\\
{t}&\longmapsto&{\mathrm{covol}(K_{t})-\mathrm{covol}(K_{0})}\end{matrix}\right.,

where for all
0\leq t\leq 1
,

K_{t}\coloneqq(1-t)K_{0}+tK_{1}.

Using the foliation given by the graphs of the functions
(s_{t}^{*})_{t\in[0,1]}
, a computation similar to the one in the proof of Lemma
3.8
gives that for all
t\in[0,1]

(6.1)

\Psi(t)=\mathrm{covol}(K_{t})-\mathrm{covol}(K_{0})=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s_{1}-s_{0}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{l}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,\mathrm{d}l\,.

By convexity of the covolume (Theorem
6.3
),
\Psi
is convex. Thus, we have

\Psi^{\prime}(0)\leq\Psi(1)-\Psi(0)\leq\Psi(1)\,,

which becomes, using the expression of
\Psi
given by (
6.1
),

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s_{1}-s_{0}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{0}/\Gamma_{\tau})\leq\mathrm{covol}(K_{1})-\mathrm{covol}(K_{0})\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s_{1}-s_{0}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{1}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

For the general case, using the approximation lemma given in Appendix
B
, Proposition
6.2
, and Proposition
5.17
, one shows that this last inequality extends to every pair of
\tau
-convex domains
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
such that
K_{1}\subseteq K_{0}
(or equivalently such that
s_{1}\leq s_{0}
).
∎

6.2.
Area measures as derivatives of the covolume

In this subsection we shall only work in the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
described in Section
1
. Our goal is to compute the “derivative” of the covolume. Hence, we first need to consider small deformations of
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains, or equivalently of support functions on
\Omega^{*}
in “every direction”.

At first it can seem difficult. Indeed, let
s
be a
\tau
-support function, let
f
be any continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant function on
\Omega^{*}
, and let
t>0
. Then, the function
s+tf
is
\tau
-equivariant but has no reason to be convex, even for
t
small enough (for example when
f\geq 0
and
s=s_{\tau}
is the support function of the maximal
\tau
-convex domain
D_{\tau}
).

Nonetheless, there is a way to get rid of that problem. Remember that any continuous
\tau
-equivariant function
g:\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
(not necessarily convex) continuously extends to a function on
\overline{\Omega^{*}}
with boundary values given by
g_{\tau}
(Proposition
5.8
). Given such a function
g
, let us introduce

K_{g}\coloneqq\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\cdot(y,-1)<g(y),\ \forall y\in\overline{\Omega^{*}}\right\}\,.

The domains defined in that way satisfy the following key property.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
g:\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
\tau
-equivariant function. Then,
K_{g}
is a
\tau
-convex domain with support function
g^{**}=(g^{*})^{*}
, the
Legendre–Fenchel biconjugate
of
g
which also is its
convex hull
, that is the pointwise largest convex function bounded above by
g
.

Proof.

The fact that the biconjugate of a function is its convex hull is a standard convex analysis result.

Note that as
g
continuously extends to
\overline{\Omega^{*}}
, it is bounded and thus its convex hull
g^{**}
is finite on the whole
\overline{\Omega^{*}}
. Moreover, the
\tau
-equivariance of
g
directly implies that
K_{g}
is
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant and that
g^{**}
is
\tau
-equivariant. Using the definition of
K_{g}
, we have

\displaystyle K_{g}

\displaystyle=\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\cdot(y,-1)<g(y),\ \forall y\in\overline{\Omega^{*}}\right\}=\left\{(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;x\cdot y-g(y)<\lambda,\ \forall y\in\overline{\Omega^{*}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;\lambda>\sup_{y\in\Omega^{*}}{\bigl({x\cdot y-g(y)}\bigr)}\right\}=\left\{(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;\lambda>g^{*}(x)\right\}

\displaystyle=\epi(g^{*})\,.

Its support function
s
is thus defined by: for all
y\in\Omega^{*}
,

s(y)=\sup_{(x,\lambda)\in K_{g}}(x,\lambda)\cdot(y,-1)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}x\cdot y-g^{*}(x)=g^{**}(y)\,.\qed

Proposition
6.6
solves our problem: let
s
be a
\tau
-support function,
f
is any continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant function on
\Omega^{*}
, and
t>0
, then
(s+tf)^{**}
will be convex, i.e. will be a
\tau
-support function. The following lemma tells us that the path
(K_{s+tf})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a Hausdorff continuous path of
\tau
-convex domains.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
K
be a
\tau
-convex domain with support function
s
, and
f
be a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant function on
\Omega^{*}
. Then, the family of
\tau
-convex domains
(K_{s+tf})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is continuous for the Hausdorff distance.

Proof.

Up to setting
K_{0}^{\prime}=K_{s+tf}
, we just have to prove the continuity at
t=0
. Moreover, up to replacing
f
by
-f
(which is also
\Gamma
-equivariant), we just have to consider the case when
t
tends to
0^{+}
. Let
t>0
. As
s
and
f^{**}
are convex, so too is
s+tf^{**}
. Moreover,
f^{**}\leq f
, so
s+tf^{**}
is convex and satisfies
s+tf^{**}\leq s+tf
. Hence

s+tf^{**}\leq(s+tf)^{**}\leq s+tf\,.

Thus, as
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}\leq 0
, we get

t\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\leq\frac{(s+tf)^{**}-s}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\leq t\frac{f^{**}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,.

That concludes the proof, as then

d_{H}(K,K_{s+tf})\leq t\max{\left({\left\|\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\frac{f^{**}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\,.\qed

We shall thus prove that, just like in the Euclidean case
[
15
, Proposition 2]
, area measures are derivatives of the covolume in the following sense.

Theorem 6.8

(Theorem
III
)
.

Let
K=K_{s}
be a
\tau
-convex domain with support function
s
, and
f
be a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant function on
\Omega^{*}
(remember that, by Proposition
5.6
,
\bar{f}=f/\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
is then
\Gamma
-invariant). Then, we have

\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf})-\mathrm{covol}(K)}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to 0]{}\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

First, let us prove a useful convex analysis lemma concerning Monge–Ampère measures of convex hulls.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be a bounded convex domain,
g:\overline{U}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function and
g^{**}
be its convex hull. Then, the Borel set

b=\left\{x\in U\;\middle|\;g^{**}(x)\neq g(x)\right\}

is negligible for the Monge–Ampère measure of
g^{**}
, i.e.
\mathrm{MA}(g^{**})(b)=0
.

Proof.

As
g^{**}
, the convex hull of
g
, satisfies
g^{**}\leq g
,
b
is the set

b=\left\{x\in U\;\middle|\;g^{**}(x)<g(x)\right\}\,.

We claim that
\partial g^{**}(b)
is included in the set of non-differentiable points of
g^{*}
, which will conclude the proof as, by Rademacher’s Theorem, the convex function
g^{*}
is differentiable almost everywhere, so that

\mathrm{MA}(g^{**})(b)=\mathcal{L}{\bigl({\partial g^{**}(b)}\bigr)}=0\,.

Now, let us prove our claim. Let
x\in b
and
y\in\partial g^{**}(x)
. By the Fenchel Inequality Theorem
[
46
, Theorem 23.5]
,
x
is a subgradient of
g^{*}
at
y
. As

g^{*}(y)=\sup_{x\in U}{\bigl({x\cdot y-g(x)}\bigr)}\,,

there exist
x^{\prime}\in\overline{U}
such that

g^{*}(y)=x^{\prime}\cdot y-g(x^{\prime})\,.

Thus, as
g^{**}\leq g
, we have

(6.2)

g^{*}(y)=x^{\prime}\cdot y-g(x^{\prime})\leq x^{\prime}\cdot y-g^{**}(x)\,.

As
g^{**}=(g^{*})^{*}
is convex, by the Fenchel Inequality Theorem
[
46
, Theorem 23.5]
, the equality must be achieved in (
6.2
), so that
g^{**}(x)=g(x)
, and
x^{\prime}
is a subgradient of
g^{*}
at
y
. Thus,
x^{\prime}\notin b
, and
g^{*}
has two distinct subgradients
x\neq x^{\prime}
at
y
, i.e.
g^{*}
is not differentiable at
y
.
∎

Proposition
4.15
and Lemma
6.9
directly imply the following.

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
g:\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
\tau
-equivariant function. Then, the following Borel set (which is well-defined by
\tau
-equivariance of
g
and
g^{**}
)

b=\left\{[y]\in\Omega^{*}/\Gamma\;\middle|\;g^{**}(y)\neq g(y)\right\}

is negligible for the area measure of
K_{g}/\Gamma_{\tau}
, i.e.
\mathrm{A}(K_{g}/\Gamma_{\tau})(b)=0
.

Now we have all the tools needed to prove Theorem
6.8
.

Proof of Theorem
6.8
.

Up to replacing
f
by
-f
(which is also
\Gamma
-equivariant), we just have to consider the case when
t
tends to
0^{+}
.

• First, let us start with the case where
f\geq 0
. Let
t>0
. As
s
is convex and satisfies
s\leq s+tf
, we have

s\leq(s+tf)^{**}\leq s+tf\,.

Thus, as
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}\leq 0
, we deduce that

(6.3)

0\leq\frac{s-(s+tf)^{**}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\leq-t\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,.

Now, let us apply apply Lemma
6.5
with
K_{0}=K_{s+tf}
and
K_{1}=K
. We get

(6.4)

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s-(s+tf)^{**}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})\leq\mathrm{covol}(K)-\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf})\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s-(s+tf)^{**}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

By Corollary
6.10
, the first term in (
6.4
) is

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s-(s+tf)^{**}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})=\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s-(s+tf)}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})=-t\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

Inequalities (
6.3
) imply that the last term in (
6.4
) satisfies

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s-(s+tf)^{**}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\leq-t\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

Thus, we have

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\leq\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf})-\mathrm{covol}(K)}{t}\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

By Lemma
6.7
,
(K_{s+tf})_{t}
Hausdorff converges to
K
as
t
tends to
0
. Hence, letting
t
tend to
0
in the previous inequalities, and using the weak convergence of area measures given by Proposition
5.17
, we get the wanted result.

• Now, let us consider the case where
f\leq 0
. Let
t>0
. We now have

(s+tf)^{**}\leq s+tf\leq s\,.

Thus, as
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}\leq 0
, we deduce that

(6.5)

0\leq t\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\leq\frac{(s+tf)^{**}-s}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,.

Now, let us apply apply Lemma
6.5
with
K_{0}=K
and
K_{1}=K_{s+tf}
. We get

(6.6)

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{(s+tf)^{**}-s}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\leq\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf})-\mathrm{covol}(K)\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{(s+tf)^{**}-s}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

By Corollary
6.10
, the last term in (
6.6
) is

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{(s+tf)^{**}-s}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})=t\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

Inequalities (
6.5
) imply that the first term in (
6.6
) satisfies

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{(s+tf)^{**}-s}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\geq t\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

Thus, we have

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\leq\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf})-\mathrm{covol}(K)}{t}\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

By Lemma
6.7
,
(K_{s+tf})_{t}
Hausdorff converges to
K
when
t
tends to
0
. Again, letting
t
tend to
0
in the previous inequalities, and using the weak convergence of area measures given by Proposition
5.17
, we get the wanted result.

• Finally, in the general case, decompose
f
as

f=f^{+}+f^{-}\,

where
f^{+}=(f+|f|)/2\geq 0
and
f^{-}=(f-|f|)/2\leq 0
. For all
t>0
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf})-\mathrm{covol}(K)}{t}=\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf^{+}+tf^{-}})-\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf^{-}})}{t}+\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf^{-}})-\mathrm{covol}(K)}{t}\,.

Using what was done in the first point (the case
f\geq 0
), we get that for all
t>0
,

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f^{+}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf^{-}}/\Gamma_{\tau})\leq\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf^{+}+tf^{-}})-\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf^{-}})}{t}\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f^{+}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s+tf^{+}+tf^{-}}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

Letting
t
tend to
0
in the previous inequalities, and using Lemma
6.7
and Proposition
5.17
, we get

\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf^{+}+tf^{-}})-\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf^{-}})}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to 0^{+}]{}\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f^{+}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

By the second point (the case
f\leq 0
), we have

\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf^{-}})-\mathrm{covol}(K)}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to 0^{+}]{}\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f^{-}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

That concludes the proof as we hence have

\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s+tf})-\mathrm{covol}(K)}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to 0^{+}]{}\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f^{+}+f^{-}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.\qed

Remark 6.11

(On the “Gateaux derivative” in Theorem
III
)
.

Let
C^{0}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})
denote the space of
\tau
-equivariant continuous function on
\Omega^{*}
. It has a structure of infinite-dimensional real affine space with associated vector space
C^{0}(\Omega^{*}/\Gamma)
, given by the transitive action

\left\{\begin{matrix}{C^{0}(\Omega^{*}/\Gamma)\times C^{0}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})}&\longrightarrow&{C^{0}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})}\\
{(\bar{f},g)}&\longmapsto&{g+\omega_{\mathcal{C}}\bar{f}}\end{matrix}\right.\,.

The metric on
C^{0}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})
induced by the
L^{\infty}
-norm on the underlying vector space
C^{0}(\Omega^{*}/\Gamma)
is the Hausdorff distance
d_{H}
.
The set
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})
of
\tau
-equivariant convex functions is a closed convex subset of
C^{0}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})
. Note that
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})
can equivalently be seen as the set of all
\tau
-convex domains (Propositions
2.8
and
5.5
).
Lemma
6.7
states that the map

{\mathrm{hull}}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{C^{0}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})}&\longrightarrow&{\mathcal{K}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})}\\
{g}&\longmapsto&{g^{**}}\end{matrix}\right.

is continuous on
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})
.
Theorem
6.8
states that the extension of the covolume to
C^{0}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})
given by

\Psi\coloneqq\mathrm{covol}\circ\mathrm{hull}:C^{0}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

admits a continuous linear
Gateaux derivative
at every point of
\mathcal{K}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})
, expressed as

{\mathrm{d}^{\mathrm{Gat}}\Psi}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{K}_{\tau}(\Omega^{*})\times C^{0}(\Omega^{*}/\Gamma)}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{R}}\\
{(s,\bar{f})}&\longmapsto&{\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{s}/\Gamma_{\tau})}\end{matrix}\right.\,.

7.
The Minkowski problem for
\tau
-convex domains

Throughout this whole section, we shall again assume that
\mathcal{C}
is divisible by a subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
and let
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
be an affine deformation of
\Gamma
.

7.1.
Existence and uniqueness of a solution

We shall prove that the Minkowski problem, asking for a
\tau
-convex domain with prescribed area measure, always has a unique solution.

Theorem 7.1

(Theorem
II
)
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})/\Gamma
. Then, there exists a unique
\tau
-convex domain with area measure
\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})=\mu
.

The proof of the existence part of Theorem
7.1
is variational. To any Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})/\Gamma
, we will associate a functional
L_{\mu}
, involving the covolume, on the space of
\tau
-convex domains. We shall show that
L_{\mu}
attains a minimum at a
\tau
-convex domain
K_{\mu}
. Then, using that the derivative of
L_{\mu}
must vanish at
K_{\mu}
and that we have computed the derivative of the covolume in Section
6
, we will get that
K_{\mu}
satisfies a particular condition: it is a solution of the Minkowski problem
\mathrm{A}(K/\Gamma_{\tau})=\mu
.

The uniqueness part of Theorem
7.1
is then a direct consequence of the comparison principle
[
21
, Theorem 2.10]
for the underlying Monge–Ampère equation.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Let us work in the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
described in Section
1
. Given the finite measure
\mu
on
\Omega^{*}/\Gamma
, let us introduce the following continuous functional
L_{\mu}
on the space of
\tau
-convex domains: for every
\tau
-convex domain
K
with support function
s
,

L_{\mu}(K)\coloneqq\mathrm{covol}(K)-\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s-s_{\tau}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

• First, let us prove that
L_{\mu}
is coercive for the Hausdorff distance, i.e. if
d_{H}(K_{n},D_{\tau})\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}+\infty
, then
L_{\mu}(K_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}+\infty
.

Let
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
\tau
-convex domains with respective support functions
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
d_{H}(K_{n},D_{\tau})\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}+\infty
. By Corollary
5.19
, we have that
a_{n}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{\min}(K_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}+\infty
. On one side, by Lemma
5.20
, there exist a constant
\delta
such that for
n
big enough we have

\frac{s_{n}-s_{\tau}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\leq\delta a_{n}\,,

so that
\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}(s_{n}-s_{\tau})/\omega_{\mathcal{C}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
grows at most linearly in
a_{n}
.

On the other side, as
a_{n}=\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{\min}(K_{n})
, we have
K_{n}\subseteq D_{\tau}+a_{n}\,\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
, so

\mathrm{covol}(K_{n})\geq\mathrm{covol}(D_{\tau}+a_{n}\,\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}})\,.

By Theorem
5.14
,
\mathrm{covol}(D_{\tau}+a_{n}\,\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}})=V_{a_{n}}(D_{\tau}/\Gamma_{\tau})(\Omega^{*}/\Gamma)
is a polynomial of degree
d+1
in
a_{n}
. Thus, we have that

L_{\mu}(K_{n})=\mathrm{covol}(K_{n})-\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{s_{n}-s_{\tau}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{d}\mu\,\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}+\infty.

• By
[
46
, Theorem 10.9]
, we can extract a converging subsequence from a minimising sequence for
L_{\mu}
. Thus,
L_{\mu}
attains its minimum at some
\tau
-convex domain
K_{\mu}
with support function
s_{\mu}
.

• Let
\bar{f}:\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
\Gamma
-invariant function. Then,
f\coloneqq\bar{f}\,\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
is a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant function. By Theorem
6.8
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{covol}(K_{s_{\mu}+tf})-\mathrm{covol}(K_{\mu})}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to 0]{}\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{\mu}/\Gamma_{\tau})\,.

Thus, as
K_{\mu}
is a minimum of
L_{\mu}
, we have

(7.1)

0\leq\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{L_{\mu}(K_{s_{\mu}+tf})-L_{\mu}(K_{\mu})}{t}\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{\mu}/\Gamma_{\tau})-\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{(s_{\mu}+tf)^{**}-s_{\mu}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

As
(s_{\mu}+tf)^{**}\leq s_{\mu}+tf
and
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}\leq 0
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have that,

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{(s_{\mu}+tf)^{**}-s_{\mu}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{d}\mu\geq t\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

and thus

(7.2)

\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{(s_{\mu}+tf)^{**}-s_{\mu}}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\frac{f}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

As
\bar{f}=f/\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
, inequalities (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) imply

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{\mu}/\Gamma_{\tau})\geq\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

Applying the same reasoning to
-\bar{f}
, we get the reverse inequality. Hence, for every continuous function
\bar{f}:\Omega^{*}/\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{A}(K_{\mu}/\Gamma_{\tau})=\int_{\Omega^{*}/\Gamma}\bar{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

By the uniqueness part of the Riesz Representation Theorem, that implies that
\mathrm{A}(K_{\mu}/\Gamma_{\tau})=\mu
.

• For the uniqueness of the solution, just notice that, by Proposition
4.15
, being a solution of the invariant Minkowski problem is the same as having support function satisfying the following Monge–Ampère equation:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{MA}(s)=(-\omega_{\mathcal{C}})^{-1}\mu&\text{in}\ \Omega^{*}\,,\\
s_{|\partial U}=g_{\tau}\,.\end{cases}

Then, the uniqueness of the solution is a consequence of the comparison principle for Monge–Ampère equations
[
21
, Theorem 2.10 and Corollary 2.11]
.
∎

7.2.
Regularity of the solution

In order to get regularity results concerning the solution of Theorem
7.1
, we shall use various facts from the Monge–Ampère equation theory.

Here is a theorem concerning the regularity of strictly convex solutions of Monge–Ampère equations.

Theorem 7.2

(
[
48
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain, and
\varphi\in C^{1,1}(U)
be a positive function. Then, any strictly convex solution of
\mathrm{MA}(s)=\varphi\mathcal{L}
is
C^{3,\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Furthermore, if
\varphi
is
C^{k,\alpha}
for some
k\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then any strictly convex solution of
\mathrm{MA}(s)=\phi\,\mathcal{L}
is
C^{k+2,\alpha}
.

7.2.1.
Dimension 2

In dimension

, there is a particular strict convexity result for solutions of a Monge–Ampère equation due to Alexandrov and Heinz.

Theorem 7.3

(Alexandrov–Heinz
[

, Remark 3.2]
)

Let

such that on

Then,

is strictly convex.

Theorems
7.2
and

imply the following theorem.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded convex domain, and
\varphi\in C^{1,1}(U)

for any

Furthermore, if
\varphi
is
C^{k,\alpha}
for some

and

\mathrm{MA}(s)=\varphi\mathcal{L}

Using the parametrisation

, a

and

Theorem 7.5
.

7.2.2.
Higher dimension

In higher dimension, the following result is due to Caffarelli.

Theorem 7.6

(Caffarelli
[
21
, Theorem 4.10]
)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain and
s:U\to\mathbb{R}
a convex function such that there exist

such that on

Fix

(2)

Theorem 7.7

(Multidimensional Alexandrov–Heinz Theorem
[
13
, Theorem 2.34]
)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain and

\mathrm{MA}(s)\geq m\mathcal{L}\,.

-plane around some point of

7.2
and

imply the following theorem (see the proof of

, Corollary 2.37]

Theorem 7.8
.

Let

be a bounded convex domain,

and
\varphi\in C^{k,\alpha}(U)

, be a positive function. If

(

)
.

There exists a non-negative number

such that for every

-convex domain

First let us prove that there exists a non-negative number

such that every
\tau
-convex domain

Appendix A
Dirichlet–Lee fundamental domains

In this first appendix, we show that the action of an affine deformation of a group dividing a cone on its maximal domain admits a convex fundamental domains. This fact is used in the proof of the strict convexity of the covolume (Theorem
6.3
).

Let
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
be an affine deformation of a subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{V}^{d+1})
dividing a proper open convex cone
\mathcal{C}
. The construction of a convex fundamental domain for the action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
on
D_{\tau}
, the maximal
\tau
-convex domain is due to Lee
[
32
]
(Lee actually only considered the case where
\tau=0
, but the construction immediately extends to our frame of work). Another study of these domains (again in the case
\tau=0
) using
Vinberg hypersurfaces
instead of affine spheres, can be found in
[
36
]
.

Definition A.1

(Dirichlet–Lee domain)
.

Let
[\varphi]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
. The
Dirichlet–Lee domain of
D_{\tau}
given by
[\varphi]
is the convex set
\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
, closed in
D_{\tau}
, defined as

\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}=\left\{P\in D_{\tau}\;\middle|\;\forall\gamma_{\tau}\in\Gamma_{\tau},\ \varphi{\left({\overrightarrow{P(\gamma_{\tau}P)}}\right)}\leq 0\right\}\,,

where
\overrightarrow{P(\gamma_{\tau}P)}\in\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
is the vector from
P\in\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
to
\gamma_{\tau}\cdot P\in\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
.

In the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
, if
y\in\Omega^{*}
and
Y\coloneqq(y,-1)\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
. The Dirichlet–Lee domain given by
y
is

\mathcal{D}_{y}=\left\{X\in D_{\tau}\;\middle|\;\forall(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\tau},\ X\cdot Y\geq(\gamma X+\tau)\cdot Y\right\}\,.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
[\varphi]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
and
A
be a compact subset of
D_{\tau}
. If
(\gamma^{n}_{\tau})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a diverging sequence of elements of
\Gamma_{\tau}
, then for all
P\in\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
,

\sup_{Q\in A}\varphi{\left({\overrightarrow{P(\gamma^{n}_{\tau}Q)}}\right)}\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}-\infty\,.

Proof.

Let us work in the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
. We now let
Y=(y,-1)\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
and
(\gamma_{n},\tau_{n})_{n}
be a diverging sequence of elements of
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SL}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. We want to show that

\sup_{X\in A}(\gamma_{n}X+\tau_{n})\cdot Y\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}-\infty\,.

Using
[
1
, Proposition 6.7]
, let us decompose any element
X\in A
as
X=\Pi(X)+tN(X)
, where
\Pi(X)\in\partial D_{\tau}
,
t>0
, and
N(X)\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
. By
[
1
, Proposition 6.9]
,
N(A)
is compact as the image of the compact set
A
by a continuous map. Then, for all
X\in A
, we have,

(A.1)

(\gamma_{n}X+\tau_{n})\cdot Y=(\gamma_{n}\Pi(X)+\tau_{n})\cdot Y+\gamma_{n}N(X)\cdot Y\,.

As
P\in\partial D_{\tau}
, by
[
1
, Proposition 5.15]
, we have

(A.2)

(\gamma_{n}\Pi(X)+\tau_{n})\cdot Y=(\gamma_{n}\Pi(X)+\tau_{n})\cdot(y,-1)\leq g_{\tau}(y)\,.

As the affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
is
\Gamma
-invariant, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\gamma_{n}N(A)\subset\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
. As the action of
\Gamma
on
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
is properly discontinuous and the sequence
(\gamma_{n},\tau_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
diverges, the compact sets
(\gamma_{n}N(A))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
escape from every compact subset of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
. Then, as
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
is asymptotic to
\partial\mathcal{C}
and
Y\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
, we have

(A.3)

\sup_{X\in A}\gamma_{n}N(X)\cdot Y\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}-\infty\,.

That concludes the proof, as equations (
A.1
), (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) give

\sup_{X\in A}(\gamma_{n}X+\tau_{n})\cdot Y\leq g_{\tau}(y)+\sup_{X\in A}\gamma_{n}N(X)\cdot Y\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}-\infty\,.\qed

Corollary A.3
.

Let
y\in\Omega^{*}
. The Dirichlet–Lee domain
\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
of
D_{\tau}
satisfies

D_{\tau}=\bigcup_{\gamma_{\tau}\in\Gamma_{\tau}}(\gamma_{\tau}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]})\,.

Proof.

Let
P\in D_{\tau}
. Lemma
A.2
applied with
A=\{P\}
and the proper discontinuity of the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
, imply that

\sup_{\gamma_{\tau}\in\Gamma_{\tau}}\varphi{\left({\overrightarrow{P(\gamma_{\tau}P)}}\right)}

is achieved for some
\gamma_{\tau}^{P}\in\Gamma_{\tau}
. Hence,
P\in\gamma_{\tau}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
, with
\gamma_{\tau}=(\gamma_{\tau}^{P})^{-1}\in\Gamma_{\tau}
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
[\varphi]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
. The Dirichlet–Lee domain
\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
of
D_{\tau}
has interior

\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]})=\left\{P\in D_{\tau}\;\middle|\;\forall\gamma_{\tau}\in\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{\mathrm{Id}\},\ \varphi{\left({\overrightarrow{P(\gamma_{\tau}P)}}\right)}<0\right\},

where
\mathrm{Id}
is the identity in
\mathrm{SA}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1})
.

Proof.

The direct inclusion
\subseteq
is trivial. Let us then prove the reverse inclusion.

Let us work in the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
. We now let
Y=(y,-1)\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
and we want to show that

\left\{X\in D_{\tau}\;\middle|\;\forall(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{(I,0)\},\ X\cdot Y>(\gamma X+\tau)\cdot Y\right\}\subseteq\,\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{D}_{y})\,.

Let
X\in D_{\tau}
such that for all
(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{(I,0)\}
, we have
X\cdot Y>(\gamma X+\tau)\cdot Y
. Let
A
be a compact neighbourhood of
X
in
D_{\tau}
, such that

(A.4)

\inf_{X^{\prime}\in A}X^{\prime}\cdot Y\geq X\cdot Y-1\,.

Lemma
A.2
and the proper discontinuity of the action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
on
D_{\tau}
imply that there is only a finite family of elements
(\gamma_{i},\tau_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
of
\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{(I,0)\}
such that for all
(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{(I,0),(\gamma_{1},\tau_{1}),\dots,(\gamma_{n},\tau_{n})\}
,

(A.5)

X\cdot Y-1>\sup_{X^{\prime}\in A}(\gamma X^{\prime}+\tau)\cdot Y\,.

Thus, using equations (
A.4
) and (
A.5
), for all
X^{\prime}\in A
and
(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{(I,0),(\gamma_{1},\tau_{1}),\dots,(\gamma_{n},\tau_{n})\}
,

(A.6)

X^{\prime}\cdot Y>(\gamma X^{\prime}+\tau)\cdot Y\,.

Now, for all
1\leq i\leq n
, we have, by assumption, that
X\cdot Y>(\gamma_{i}X+\tau_{i})\cdot Y
, so there is a neighbourhood
U_{i}\subseteq A
of
X
such that for all
X^{\prime}\in U_{i}
,

(A.7)

X^{\prime}\cdot Y>(\gamma_{i}X^{\prime}+\tau_{\gamma})\cdot Y\,.

Equations (
A.6
) and (
A.7
) imply the neighbourhood
U\coloneqq\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}U_{i}\subseteq A
of
X
in
D_{\tau}
satisfies that for all
X^{\prime}\in U
and
(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\tau}
,

X^{\prime}\cdot Y\geq(\gamma X^{\prime}+\tau)\cdot Y\,.

That means that
X
admits a neighbourhood included in
\mathcal{D}_{y}
, i.e.
X\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{D}_{y})
.
∎

Corollary A.5
.

Let
[\varphi]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
. The Dirichlet–Lee domain
\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
of
D_{\tau}
satisfies that for all
(\gamma_{\tau})\in\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{\mathrm{Id}\}
,

\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]})\cap{\bigl({\gamma_{\tau}\cdot\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]})}\bigr)}=\emptyset\,.

Corollaries
A.3
and
A.5
give the following Theorem.

Theorem A.6
.

Let
[\varphi]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
. The Dirichlet–Lee domain
\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
is a convex fundamental domain of
D_{\tau}
for the action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
.

Finally, let us end this appendix by noticing a nice property satisfied by Dirichlet–Lee domains, which is useful in the proof of the strict convexity of the covolume.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
[\varphi]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}^{*})
and
\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
be the Dirichlet–Lee domain of
D_{\tau}
given by it. For all
P\in\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
and
t>0
, we have that
P+tN_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}[\varphi]\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]})
.

Proof.

Let
P\in\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
,
t>0
and
\gamma_{\tau}\in\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{\mathrm{Id}\}
. Let us denote
\vec{n}\coloneqq N_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}[\varphi]\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
. As
P\in\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]}
, we have

(A.8)

\varphi{\left({\overrightarrow{P(\gamma_{\tau}P)}}\right)}\leq 0\,.

Moreover,
\gamma\cdot\vec{n}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
and
[\varphi]
directs the supporting hyperplane to
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
at
\vec{n}=N_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}[varphi]
, so, as
\gamma N\neq N
and the affine sphere is a locally uniformly convex hypersurface, for
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{\mathrm{Id}\}
we have

(A.9)

\varphi(\gamma\cdot\vec{n})<\varphi(\vec{n}).

Let
t>0
and set
P^{\prime}=P+t\vec{n}=P+tN_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}[\varphi]
. For all
\gamma_{\tau}\in\Gamma_{\tau}\setminus\{\mathrm{Id},\}
, we have

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{P^{\prime}(\gamma_{\tau}P^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\overrightarrow{P^{\prime}P}+\overrightarrow{P(\gamma_{\tau}P)}+\overrightarrow{(\gamma_{\tau}P)(\gamma_{\tau}P^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\overrightarrow{P(\gamma_{\tau}P)}+t(\gamma\cdot\vec{n})-t\vec{n}\,.

As

, with (
A.8
) and (

) that implies that

\varphi{\left({\overrightarrow{P(\gamma_{\tau}P)}}\right)}<0\,.

By Lemma
A.4
, that means that for all

P^{\prime}=P+tN_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}[\varphi]\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{D}_{[\varphi]})
.
∎

Appendix B
Smooth approximation of
\tau
-convex domains

In this second appendix, we provide the proof of a
C^{2}_{+}
-approximation lemma for
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains, which we use in the proof of Lemma
6.5
, in order to get Theorem
6.8
and Theorem
7.1
.

The following approximation result is due to Choi
[
18
, Theorem 4.4.4]
, who managed to adapt the proof of a classical convex geometry approximation result for bounded convex domains in the Euclidean affine space, presented in
[
24
, Proposition 2.1]
. For the sake of completeness, we give a quick proof of it, using the same language and notation as in the rest of this article.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
K
be a
\tau
-convex. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
C^{2}_{+}

\tau
-convex domain
K^{\prime}
such that
d_{H}(K,K^{\prime})\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let us work in the parametrisation
(\mathbf{P})
and let
s
be the support function of
K
. For any support function
f
, let us denote by
K(f)
the
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain with support function
f
.

First, note that
K
can already be approximated by the
\tau
-convex domains
K(s+t\omega_{\mathcal{C}})=K+t\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
, with
t>0
. Thus, we can assume that we are working with
K
a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain with a strictly convex support function
s
, and such that
\partial_{sp}K=\partial K\subset D_{\tau}

[
1
, Lemma 6.4]
and
\partial K/\Gamma_{\tau}
is compact
[
1
, Lemma 7.6]
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let us now show that there exists a
\tau
-convex domain
K^{\prime}
with smooth and locally uniformly convex boundary, and such that
K(s+\varepsilon\omega_{\mathcal{C}})\subseteq K^{\prime}\subseteq K
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth function, locally uniformly convex on
(0,+\infty)
and vanishing on
(-\infty,0]
. For instance, consider

{g}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{\mathbb{R}}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{R}}\\
{t}&\longmapsto&{\begin{cases}t^{2}\exp{\left({-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}\right)}&\text{if}\ x>0\,,\\
0&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}}\end{matrix}\right.

Then, for
Y=-(y,-1)/\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, set
\phi_{Y}
to be the following smooth convex function:

{\phi_{Y}}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{K}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\\
{X}&\longmapsto&{g{\bigl({X\cdot Y-(\tilde{s}+\varepsilon\tilde{\omega}_{\mathcal{C}})(Y)}\bigr)}}\end{matrix}\right.\,,

where
\tilde{s}
and
\tilde{\omega}_{\mathcal{C}}
are the
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-support function of respectively
K
and
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
. Note that the function
\phi_{Y}
is smooth and convex as the composition of
g
and an affine map, and that it vanishes on
K(s+\varepsilon\omega_{\mathcal{C}})
but is positive in the
\mathcal{C}
-past half-space
\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\cdot(y,-1)>(s+\varepsilon\omega_{\mathcal{C}})(y)\right\}
.

Let
X\in\overline{K}\subset D_{\tau}
. By
