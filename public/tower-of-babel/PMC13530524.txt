Poster 157. Validity and Responsiveness of the Two-Question Universal Pain and Function Score for Common Sports Medicine Surgeries
Authors: Andrew Haskell, Garet Comer, Diane McGrew, Todd Kim, Sien Deng, John Cooper
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs) quantify responses to treatment, but they burden patients, clinicians, and health care systems with often lengthy questionnaires. We developed the Universal Pain and Function score (UPAF) with only two questions to address this burden. It combines two single-question surveys that have been validated to measure musculoskeletal pain and function: the Visual Analog Scale for pain (VAS) and the Single Assessment Numerical Evaluation for function (SANE). We tested the hypothesis that UPAF is both valid and responsive for arthroscopic rotator cuff repair (RCR), arthroscopic meniscectomy (MNSC), and arthroscopic anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction (ACL).Methods:Between August 2023 and June 2025, VAS, SANE, PROMIS Global (10 questions), and either American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons Score (ASES) (11 questions) for RCR patients or Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score for Joint Replacement (KOOS-JR) (7 questions) for MNSC and ACL patients were prospectively collected from 1648 patients before and at least 60 days after orthopedic surgery. In addition, Satisfaction with the Outcome of Surgery (SOS) was prospectively collected after surgery. UPAF was calculated as [10*(10-VAS) + SANE]/2. For each surgery, validity was measured using the concepts of convergent, discriminant, and concurrent criteria validity by calculating Spearman rank order correlation (ρ) between UPAF and ASES or KOOS-JR, PROMIS Global mental health, and SOS. Responsiveness was measured for each surgery using Cohen's d, standardized response mean (SRM), and the correlation between change in UPAF and change in ASES or KOOS-JR.Results:UPAF strongly to very strongly correlates with ASES and KOOS-JR confirming convergent validity (RCR ρ = 0.91; MNSC ρ = 0.73; ACL ρ = 0.77). It has weak to no correlation with PROMIS Global mental health demonstrating strong discriminant validity (RCR ρ = 0.16; MNSC ρ = n/a; ACL ρ = 0.19). UPAF moderately correlates with SOS indicating concurrent criteria validity (RCR ρ = 0.50; MNSC ρ = 0.62; ACL ρ = 0.48), which is comparable to ASES and KOOS-JR (Table 1). UPAF has large to very large effect size to measure changes after surgery by Cohen's d (RCR 1.47; MNSC 1.16; ACL 1.09) and SRM (RCR 1.14; MNSC 0.95; ACL 1.05), which are both also comparable to ASES and KOOS-JR (Table 2). The change in UPAF after surgery strongly to very strongly correlates with the change in ASES and KOOS-JR (RCR ρ = 0.86; MNSC ρ = 0.68; ACL ρ = 0.70), which suggests UPAF is capable of measuring expected changes after surgery.Conclusions:UPAF only requires two questions to deliver both valid and responsive outcomes measures after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair, arthroscopic meniscectomy, and ACL reconstruction. It correlates well with ASES, KOOS-JR, and SOS, and appropriately does not correlate with PROMIS Global mental health. Its effect size is comparable to ASES and KOOS-JR, making it suitable for studies requiring limited sample sizes. With only two questions, it reduces survey burden by 82% compared to ASES (11 questions vs 2 questions) and 71% compared to KOOS-JR (7 questions vs 2 questions) without sacrificing validity or responsiveness. Surgeons and health care systems wishing to measure surgical outcomes should consider this two-question score given its decreased survey burden, validity, and responsiveness.
