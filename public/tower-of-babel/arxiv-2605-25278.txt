Exact Variance and Fano Factor for Arbitrary Level Crossings in Stationary Gaussian Processes
Shivang Rawat 1 2, Flaviano Morone 2 3, David J. Heeger 3 4, and Stefano Martiniani 1 2 3 5

Abstract
Understanding the statistics of level crossings in stochastic processes is crucial across many scientific disciplines. The traditional Kac-Rice formula gives the mean rate of level crossings and has found broad use. However, that mean rate captures only a coarse summary of the crossing process. It depends entirely on local properties of the stochastic process at a given instant and is therefore blind to the correlation structure of the process over time. To understand whether crossing events, such as neuronal spikes, tend to cluster in time, spread apart, or exhibit more complex temporal organization, one must go beyond the mean rate and study higher-order crossing statistics. Here we go beyond the mean by deriving the exact analytical formulae for the variance and Fano factor of arbitrary level crossings in smooth stationary Gaussian processes. Our exact solution reveals how the full temporal correlation structure dictates whether crossings cluster or become regular. In systems with oscillatory correlations, such as a stochastic damped harmonic oscillator, a recent crossing suppresses an immediate subsequent one, producing sub-Poissonian statistics. However, as damping increases and oscillations disappear, a large and slow excursion above the threshold can produce multiple closely spaced crossings, yielding super-Poissonian statistics. In purely relaxational, non-oscillatory systems, such as a mean-reverting process driven by Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise, the competition between the timescales of the driving noise and system relaxation produces a richer landscape, including reentrant transitions between sub- and super-Poissonian statistics as the threshold level is varied. Taken together, the exact variance and Fano factor derived here complement the Kac-Rice mean rate, enabling more robust parameter estimation and model selection across any setting where Gaussian processes are used.



The study of level crossings in stochastic
processes is of fundamental importance across
a range of scientific and engineering disciplines.
In neuroscience, the rate and variability of
threshold crossings by membrane potential fluctuations
determine neuronal firing patterns, with the Fano
factor serving as a standard diagnostic for
distinguishing regular from bursty spiking
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
In structural and reliability engineering,
the number of times a load exceeds a critical
threshold directly governs fatigue life estimates
[
5
]
, where knowledge
of the mean crossing rate alone can underestimate
failure risk if crossings are clustered. In finance,
the variability and clustering of extreme-event
crossings carry implications for risk management
and policy
[
6
,
7
]
.

Across all these domains, the mean crossing
rate alone, as quantified by the celebrated
Kac-Rice formula
[
8
,
9
]
,
is insufficient: it is the higher-order statistics,
and in particular the variance, that capture
the degree of clustering or regularity of
crossing events.
Yet computing the variance of crossing counts
has remained an open problem for crossings
at arbitrary threshold levels, due to the
asymmetric Gaussian integrals that arise when
the threshold is nonzero.
In this work we close this gap by deriving exact
analytical expressions for the variance and Fano
factor of level crossings in smooth stationary
Gaussian processes.

The Fano factor is the ratio of the variance
to the mean of the crossing count and serves
as a natural diagnostic to classify the
statistics of level crossings. A Fano factor
of 1 corresponds to Poisson-distributed crossings,
while values below or above unity signal sub-Poissonian regularity or super-Poissonian clustering, respectively.
Our results reveal that this quantity is entirely
governed by the temporal correlation structure of
the process. Specifically, oscillatory correlations
typically enforce regularity by suppressing immediate
subsequent crossings, while overdamped dynamics allow
long excursions above the threshold to generate bursts
of closely spaced crossings. In purely relaxational
systems, the competition between the timescales of
the driving noise and the system’s relaxation produces
a richer landscape, including reentrant transitions
between sub-Poissonian and super-Poissonian regimes as the
threshold is varied.

, an

X_{t}

. Similarly,

. Our primary objective is to derive exact analytical expressions for the variance of these counting processes, specifically
\text{Var}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]
and
\text{Var}[N_{u}(T)]
, and for the Fano factor, a dimensionless metric that quantifies the regularity of crossing events relative to a Poisson process.

The study of higher-order moments of level
crossings has a rich history, comprehensively
surveyed in
[
10
]
, with general

as multiple integrals involving the process’s derivatives.
For the specific case of mean-level upcrossings (
u=0
),
Steinberg
et al.

[
11
]

derived an exact analytical solution expressed as
a single integral over time, a result dating back
to the 1950s. However, extending such a compact
single-integral form to an arbitrary level
u
has
remained a longstanding open problem, owing to the
asymmetric Gaussian integrals that arise when
u\neq 0
.
Azaïs
et al.

[
12
]
and
Cierco-Ayrolles
et al.

[
13
]

obtained expressions for the variance of upcrossings
at arbitrary levels as a byproduct of their work on
the statistics of extreme values of Gaussian processes,
but these were left in the form of multiple integrals
rather than reduced to a compact single-integral
expression. The present work provides this missing
solution by deriving a closed-form, single-integral
expression for the variance of arbitrary level
upcrossings and total crossings, expressed in terms
of the error function and Owen’s
T
function.

The paper is structured as follows: Section
I
provides a precise mathematical formulation of the level-crossing problem for stationary Gaussian processes and states key results for the mean and variance. In Section
II
, we present our central contribution: exact analytical formulae for the variance and Fano factor of arbitrary level upcrossings and total crossings. Simplified expressions for mean-level crossings are detailed in Appendix
D
. Finally, Section
III
demonstrates the utility of our findings by applying them to three stochastic models, the damped harmonic oscillator, a mean-reverting process with Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise, and a process with a rational quadratic correlation function, revealing a rich landscape of sub-Poissonian and super-Poissonian crossing statistics controlled by the correlation structure.

I
Description of the Problem

Let
\{X_{t};\,t\in[0,T]\}
denote a smooth (i.e., differentiable), stationary, zero-mean, Gaussian process characterized by the autocorrelation function
r(t)
, defined as

r(t)\coloneqq R_{X}(s,s+t)=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{s}\,X_{s+t}\right].

(1)

The function
r(t)
is real-valued for all
t\,\in(-\infty,\infty)
and satisfies the following properties:

•

|r(t)|<r(0)
for all
\,t\notin\{0\}
,

•

0<r(0)<\infty
,

•

r(t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Due to stationarity,
r(t)
depends only on the time difference
t
and satisfies the symmetry property
r(-t)=r(t)
. Additionally, we assume that
X_{t}
is smooth, i.e.,
r(t)
is twice-differentiable (
r^{\prime\prime}(t)
exists).

In this work, we find explicit expressions for the variance of the counting processes
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
,
N_{u}^{\downarrow}(T)
, and
N_{u}(T)
, which represent the number of upcrossings, downcrossings, and the total number of crossings, respectively, of a specified level
u
by the process
X_{t}
within the interval
t\in(0,T]
. All results derived for
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
also hold for
N_{u}^{\downarrow}(T)
. Therefore, to avoid redundancy, we will primarily focus on
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
, with the corresponding results for
N_{u}^{\downarrow}(T)
implied. The total number of crossings is simply given by the sum
N_{u}(T)=N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)+N_{u}^{\downarrow}(T)
. Additionally, we explore the Fano factors for these counting processes, a crucial metric that quantifies the dispersion, defined as the ratio between the variance and the mean of the counting process in the long-time limit. Formally, we have,

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}^{\uparrow}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\text{Var}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]}{\mathbb{E}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]}\;\text{and}\;\mathrm{F}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\text{Var}[N_{u}(T)]}{\mathbb{E}[N_{u}(T)]}.\end{split}

(2)

The counting processes
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
and
N_{u}(T)
can be expressed as integrals of the underlying generator stochastic process
X_{t}
:

\begin{split}N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)&=\int_{0}^{T}\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right)\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1},\\
N_{u}(T)&=\int_{0}^{T}\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\left|\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}.\end{split}

(3)

Here,
\dot{X}_{t_{1}}
denotes the derivative of the stochastic process,
\delta(x)
represents the Dirac delta function, and
\mathrm{H}(x)
is the Heaviside step function. The term
\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)
ensures that only the crossings of level
u
are counted. The presence of
\mathrm{H}(\dot{X}_{t_{1}})
restricts the count to upward crossings, while
|\dot{X}_{t_{1}}|
accounts for both upward and downward crossings.

Since the autocorrelation function
r(t)
is assumed to be twice differentiable, the expected values of
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
and
N_{u}(T)
remain finite over a bounded interval
T

[
14
,
15
]
. Furthermore, Geman
[
16
]
established that a necessary and sufficient condition for the variance of zero-level crossings,
\text{Var}\left[N_{0}(T)\right]
, to be finite in a bounded interval
T
is given by:

\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{r^{\prime\prime}(t)-r^{\prime\prime}(0)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,<\infty;\qquad\text{for some }\epsilon>0.

(4)

Later, Kratz
[
17
]
demonstrated that this condition remains both necessary and sufficient for ensuring a finite variance of crossings at an arbitrary level, i.e., for
\text{Var}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]
to be finite over a bounded interval
T
. Consequently, this also guarantees a finite
\text{Var}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]
. Throughout this work, we assume that this condition is satisfied by the correlation function.

I-A

Mean of the Counting Processes

The mean number of upcrossings follows from the Kac-Rice formula
[
8
,
9
]
. We define the random vector
\mathbf{P}=[X_{0},\,\dot{X}_{0}]^{\top}
, whose joint probability density function (p.d.f.)
f_{\mathbf{P}}(x,\,y)
is a zero-mean bivariate Gaussian with covariance matrix

\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{P}}=\begin{bmatrix}r(0)&0\\
0&-r^{\prime\prime}(0)\end{bmatrix},

(5)

where the off-diagonal entries vanish because
r^{\prime}(0)=0
for a stationary process (see Appendix
A-A
for details). The Kac-Rice formula then gives
[
9
]

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]=T\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
=T\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{-r^{\prime\prime}(0)}{r(0)}}\,e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2r(0)}}.

(6)

Since
\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]
accounts for both upward and downward crossings, it is simply
2\,\mathbb{E}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]
:

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]=T\,\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{-r^{\prime\prime}(0)}{r(0)}}\,e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2r(0)}}.

(7)

Notably, the mean crossing rate depends only on the local properties of the autocorrelation function, its value and curvature at the origin, and is independent of the detailed shape of
r(t)
at finite lag times.

I-B

Variance of the Counting Processes

In contrast to the mean, which depends only on the local properties
r(0)
and
r^{\prime\prime}(0)
, the variance encodes information about the full correlation structure of the process. To compute the variance, we consider the joint p.d.f. of the random vector
\mathbf{Q}=[X_{t_{1}},\,X_{t_{2}},\,\dot{X}_{t_{1}},\,\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]^{\top}
at two distinct times, which is a zero-mean multivariate Gaussian with the
4\times 4
covariance matrix:

\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{Q}}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}r(0)&r(t)&0&r^{\prime}(t)\\
r(t)&r(0)&-r^{\prime}(t)&0\\
0&-r^{\prime}(t)&-r^{\prime\prime}(0)&-r^{\prime\prime}(t)\\
r^{\prime}(t)&0&-r^{\prime\prime}(t)&-r^{\prime\prime}(0)\\
\end{bmatrix},

(8)

where
t=t_{2}-t_{1}
. Using the second factorial moment and a change of variables (see Appendix
A-B
for a detailed derivation), the finite-time variance of
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
can be expressed as
[
18
,
19
]
:

\text{Var}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]+2\,T\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(1-\frac{t}{T}\biggr)y_{1}y_{2}\\
\times\bigl(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,u,y_{1},y_{2};\,t)\\
-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{2})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(9)

The integrand measures the excess joint probability of two crossings at lag
t
relative to the independent (Poisson) case. It can be further shown
[
19
]
that the asymptotic variance rate
\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\text{Var}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]/T
is finite provided
\int_{0}^{\infty}t\left(\left|r(t)\right|+\left|r^{\prime}(t)\right|+\left|r^{\prime\prime}(t)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
, yielding:

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\text{Var}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]}{T}=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]}{T}\\
+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}y_{1}y_{2}\,\bigl(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,u,y_{1},y_{2};t)\\
-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{2})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(10)

The Fano factor, defined as the ratio of variance to mean in the long-time limit, serves as a dimensionless measure of the regularity of crossing events. A Fano factor of unity corresponds to Poisson-distributed crossings; values below unity (
\mathrm{F}^{\uparrow}<1
) indicate sub-Poissonian regularity (anti-bunching), while values above unity (
\mathrm{F}^{\uparrow}>1
) signal super-Poissonian clustering (bunching). For upcrossings:

\mathrm{F}^{\uparrow}=1+\frac{2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}y_{1}y_{2}\,G^{\uparrow}(t)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}y}\,,

(11)

where
G^{\uparrow}(t)=f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,u,y_{1},y_{2};t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{1})f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{2})
.
Using a similar approach, we can derive these quantities for the total crossing process
N_{u}(T)
(see Appendix
B
for details):

\text{Var}[N_{u}(T)]=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]+2\,T\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\biggl(1-\frac{t}{T}\biggr)\\
\times\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\bigl(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,u,y_{1},y_{2};\,t)\\
-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{2})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t,

(12)

\mathrm{F}=1+\frac{2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|G(t)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|y|\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}y}\,,

(13)

where
G(t)=f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,u,y_{1},y_{2};t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{1})f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{2})
.

While these expressions are exact, the double integrals over velocities (
y_{1}
,
y_{2}
) remain to be evaluated explicitly for a given level
u
. This is the central challenge addressed in the next section.

II
Main Result

We first present the main result of this work as a theorem, and prove it subsequently.

Theorem II.1
.

Consider a zero-mean stationary Gaussian stochastic process
X_{t}
with the autocorrelation function
r(t)
satisfying the properties defined in Section
I
. Let
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
denote the number of upcrossings of an arbitrary level
u
.
Denoting
r(t)\rightarrow r
,
r(0)\rightarrow r_{0}
,
r^{\prime}(t)\rightarrow p
,
r^{\prime}(0)\rightarrow p_{0}
,
-r^{\prime\prime}(t)\rightarrow q
,
-r^{\prime\prime}(0)\rightarrow q_{0}
, and

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=-\frac{r+r_{0}}{2\left(p^{2}+(q-q_{0})(r+r_{0})\right)},

(14)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=-\frac{r_{0}-r}{2\left(p^{2}+(q+q_{0})(r-r_{0})\right)},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,p}{r+r_{0}}\,u,

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r+r_{0}}\,.

The mean of
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
is given by:

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]=T\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{q_{0}}{r_{0}}}\,e^{-u^{2}/(2r_{0})},

(15)

and the finite-time variance of
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
is:

\textnormal{Var}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]+2T\int_{0}^{T}\biggl(1-\frac{t}{T}\biggr)I^{\uparrow}\mathrm{d}t\,,

(16)

where
I^{\uparrow}
is a function of the time-dependent quantities
r,\,\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\delta
given by:

I^{\uparrow}=\frac{e^{-\delta\,u^{2}}}{4\pi^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-r^{2}}}\Biggl[\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}\biggl(1\\
+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\sqrt{\alpha{+}\beta}\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\operatorname{Erf}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha{+}\beta}}\Bigr)\biggr)\\
+\pi\biggl(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\alpha\beta}\biggr)\mathcal{T}\Bigl(\gamma\!\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha{+}\beta}},\,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\Bigr)\Biggr]\\
-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{q_{0}}{r_{0}}\,e^{-u^{2}/r_{0}},

(17)

where
\operatorname{Erf}(z)
is the error function defined as:

\operatorname{Erf}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{z}e^{-t^{2}}\mathrm{d}t,

(18)

and
\mathcal{T}(h,a)
is Owen’s T function
[
20
]
defined as:

\mathcal{T}(h,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{a}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}(1+t^{2})}}{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(19)

Proof sketch.
The key challenge is the explicit evaluation of the double integral over the velocity variables
(y_{1},y_{2})
in Eq. (
9
) for an arbitrary level
u
. To the best of our knowledge, this has only been accomplished for mean-level crossings (
u=0
)
[
11
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
, but not for
u\neq 0
due to the asymmetric Gaussian integral that arises. We overcome this difficulty as follows. First, we introduce a
\pi/4
rotation of the velocity variables,
(y_{1},y_{2})\rightarrow(z_{1},z_{2})
, which maps the positive quadrant onto the region
|z_{1}|<z_{2}
and transforms the product
y_{1}y_{2}
into
(z_{2}^{2}-z_{1}^{2})/2
. In the rotated frame, the quadratic form in the exponent of the joint p.d.f.
f_{\mathbf{Q}}
can be simplified by completing the square in
z_{1}
, yielding
S=-\delta u^{2}-\alpha(z_{1}-\gamma)^{2}-\beta z_{2}^{2}
. The parameters
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
(their positivity is established via a Cauchy–Schwarz argument on the spectral measure; see Theorem
G.1
), while
\gamma\propto u\,r^{\prime}(t)
captures the asymmetry introduced by a nonzero threshold. The resulting integral takes the canonical form
\int\!\!\int(z_{2}^{2}-z_{1}^{2})\exp(-\alpha(z_{1}-\gamma)^{2}-\beta z_{2}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}
, which we evaluate analytically (Theorem
G.2
) using integration by parts combined with known identities for the error function and Owen’s
T
function. The full derivation is provided in Appendix
A-D
.

In the long-time limit (
T\to\infty
), the variance rate and Fano factor take the compact forms:

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\textnormal{Var}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]}{T}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{q_{0}}{r_{0}}}\,e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2r_{0}}}+2\int_{0}^{\infty}I^{\uparrow}\mathrm{d}t,

(20)

\mathrm{F}^{\uparrow}=1+4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}}{q_{0}}}\,e^{\frac{u^{2}}{2r_{0}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}I^{\uparrow}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(21)

Several important limits of these expressions deserve comment. First, when
u=0
(mean-level crossings), the parameter
\gamma
vanishes and Owen’s
T
function reduces to an arctangent, recovering the classical results of Steinberg
[
11
]
and Leadbetter
[
21
]
(see Appendix
D
). Second, in the limit
u/\sqrt{r_{0}}\to\infty
, crossings become increasingly rare and uncorrelated, so the Fano factor approaches unity, consistent with the well-known result that rare threshold crossings become Poisson-distributed
[
18
]
. Finally, the single-integral formulation in Eqs. (
16
)–(
21
) is straightforward to evaluate numerically, requiring only standard quadrature and readily available implementations of the error function and Owen’s
T
function.

Theorem II.2

(See Appendix
C
for proof)
.

Consider a zero-mean stationary Gaussian stochastic process
X_{t}
with the autocorrelation function
r(t)
satisfying the properties defined in Section
I
. Let
N_{u}(T)
denote the number of total crossings of an arbitrary level
u
.
The mean of
N_{u}(T)
is given by:

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]=T\,\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{q_{0}}{r_{0}}}\,e^{-u^{2}/(2r_{0})},

(22)

and the finite-time variance of
N_{u}(T)
is:

\textnormal{Var}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]+2T\int_{0}^{T}\biggl(1-\frac{t}{T}\biggr)I\mathrm{d}t\,,

(23)

where
I
is a function of the time-dependent quantities
r,\,\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\delta
given by:

I=\frac{e^{-\delta\,u^{2}}}{4\pi^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-r^{2}}}\Biggl[\frac{2e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}\biggl(1\\
+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\sqrt{\alpha{+}\beta}\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\operatorname{Erf}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha{+}\beta}}\Bigr)\biggr)\\
+4\pi\biggl(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\alpha\beta}\biggr)\\
\times\biggl(\mathcal{T}\Bigl(\gamma\!\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha{+}\beta}},\,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\Bigr)-\frac{1}{8}\biggr)\Biggr]\\
-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\frac{q_{0}}{r_{0}}\,e^{-u^{2}/r_{0}}.

(24)

Corollary II.3

(See Appendix
C
for proof)
.

In the long-time limit, the variance becomes:

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\textnormal{Var}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]}{T}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{q_{0}}{r_{0}}}\,e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2r_{0}}}+2\int_{0}^{\infty}I\mathrm{d}t

(25)

The corresponding Fano factor is:

\mathrm{F}=1+2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}}{q_{0}}}\,e^{\frac{u^{2}}{2r_{0}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}I\,\mathrm{d}t

(26)

III-B

Mean Reverting Process with Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Noise

We consider a mean-reverting process driven by Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise. This model is relevant in various fields, including finance for modeling stochastic volatility and in neuroscience for describing the membrane equation with filtered synaptic inputs
[
33
]
. The dynamics of the system are given by the following set of stochastic differential equations:

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}&=-\frac{x}{\tau_{f}}+\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{\tau_{f}}}\,\eta(t)\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}&=-\frac{1}{\tau_{e}}\Bigl(y-x\Bigr).\end{split}

(31)

Here,
x(t)
is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process obtained by exponentially low-pass filtering the white-noise process
\eta(t)
, where
\mathbb{E}\left[\eta(t)\,\eta(s)\right]=\delta(t-s)
. The OU process
x(t)
has a characteristic correlation time
\tau_{f}
, and its fluctuations are scaled by the parameter
\sigma
. The process
y(t)
receives
x(t)
as its input and exhibits mean-reverting behavior toward
0
with a characteristic timescale
\tau_{e}
. Since
y(t)
is a linear transformation of the Gaussian process
x(t)
, it is also a stationary Gaussian process.

To analyze the level-crossing statistics of
y(t)
, we first need its autocorrelation function. Following a procedure similar to that used for the stochastic damped harmonic oscillator (i.e., by solving the system in the Fourier domain), the autocorrelation function
r_{y}(t)=\mathbb{E}[y(s)y(s+t)]
for the process
y(t)
is found to be:

r_{y}(t)=\frac{\sigma^{2}\kappa}{1-\kappa^{2}}\left(e^{-|t|/\tau_{e}}-\kappa\,e^{-|t|/(\kappa\tau_{e})}\right),

(32)

where
\kappa=\tau_{f}/\tau_{e}
is the dimensionless ratio of the input noise correlation timescale
\tau_{f}
to the system’s relaxation timescale
\tau_{e}
. The variance of this process is
r_{y}(0)=\sigma^{2}\kappa/(1+\kappa)
.

Using this autocorrelation function
r_{y}(t)
, we can apply Theorem
II.1
to compute the mean, variance, and Fano factor for level upcrossings of
y(t)
. The parameter
\kappa=\tau_{f}/\tau_{e}
controls how filtered the noise appears to the mean-reverting process: when
\kappa\ll 1
, the driving noise varies much faster than the system
relaxes and therefore appears nearly white, whereas
when
\kappa\sim 1
or larger the noise varies slowly
relative to the system’s relaxation timescale.

Figure
5
illustrates these quantities
as functions of the normalized threshold
u/\sigma

and
\kappa
. The Fano factor (Fig.
5
c)
spans a broad range from sub-Poissonian to super-Poissonian, mirroring the behavior of the overdamped harmonic oscillator.
This similarity arises because the bi-exponential form
of
r_{y}(t)
in Eq. (
32
) can be
mapped exactly to the overdamped SDHO correlation function (Eq. (
30
)), illustrating how physically distinct systems can exhibit identical crossing statistics
when their correlation structures coincide.
A particularly striking feature of Fig.
5

is the reentrant behavior observed for intermediate
values of
\kappa
: as the threshold
u/\sigma
is
increased, the Fano factor transitions from the sub-Poissonian
regime to the super-Poissonian regime and back. This
reentrant behavior highlights how the interplay between
the threshold level and the correlation structure can
produce a rich statistical landscape even in a simple
two-timescale system.

IV
Discussion

In this work, we presented exact analytical expressions for the variance and Fano factor of
arbitrary
level crossings in smooth, stationary Gaussian processes. Our formulae, encapsulated in Theorems
II.1
and
II.2
, provide a single-integral formulation for the variance, analogous to the well-established result for mean-level upcrossings
[
11
]
, but now generalized to any level
u
. This was achieved by explicitly solving the requisite asymmetric Gaussian integrals, expressed in terms of known special functions: the error function and Owen’s
T
function.

Our analysis highlights that the mean crossing rate and the crossing variability capture distinct properties of the underlying stochastic process. While the Kac-Rice mean depends only on the local properties of the autocorrelation function at the origin (
r(0)
and
r^{\prime\prime}(0)
), the variance and Fano factor probe the entire correlation structure at all lag times. This distinction is vividly illustrated by the stochastic damped harmonic oscillator, where the mean upcrossing rate is completely independent of the damping ratio
\zeta
, yet the Fano factor varies dramatically, from strongly sub-Poissonian in the underdamped regime to super-Poissonian in the overdamped regime. The physical mechanism underlying this transition is transparent: oscillatory correlations impose regularity on crossing times (anti-bunching), while monotonically decaying correlations enable clustering (bunching).
This sub-to-super-Poissonian transition appears to be a
general feature of processes whose correlation function
transitions from oscillatory to monotone decay, as further
illustrated by the mathematical equivalence between the
mean-reverting OU-noise model and the overdamped oscillator.
The analytical expressions offer versatile tools with several important practical implications. First, they significantly enhance the toolkit for parameter estimation and inference in systems modeled by stationary Gaussian processes. While the Kac-Rice formula for the mean number of crossings is invaluable, the incorporation of exact second-order statistics can lead to more robust and accurate parameter determination, particularly when the mean rate alone is insufficient to discriminate between models, for instance, processes with identical
r(0)
and
r^{\prime\prime}(0)
but different
\zeta
values. The Fano factor, as a single dimensionless summary statistic, is particularly attractive for experimental comparisons: it can be estimated from empirical data without requiring full time-series analysis and compared directly with our analytical predictions. Second, our results provide a solid foundation for developing and validating tighter upper and lower bounds on the variance of level crossings, extending previous work on mean-crossing statistics
[
35
]
to arbitrary levels.

In neuroscience, the Fano factor of spike trains is a standard metric for distinguishing regular from bursty firing patterns
[
3
,
4
]
; our theory provides exact predictions for Gaussian-modeled membrane potential fluctuations. In structural and reliability engineering, the variance of threshold crossings governs fatigue life predictions, where the mean crossing rate alone can significantly underestimate failure risk. In environmental and climate science, the clustering of extreme events (super-Poissonian crossings of temperature or precipitation thresholds) has direct implications for risk assessment and policy.

Our analytical framework currently assumes that the underlying process is stationary, Gaussian, and smooth.
Extending these exact results to non-stationary or non-Gaussian regimes would require higher-order spectral information beyond the autocorrelation function.
Future work will be directed toward developing parameter estimation techniques that directly incorporate the analytical variance of level crossings derived herein, as well as exploring extensions to higher-order crossing moments and to multivariate Gaussian processes.

Appendix A
Theory for the Upcrossing Process

We assume that all the properties of the autocorrelation function
r(t)
described in Section
I
are followed. The upcrossing counting process
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
can be written as the integral of the underlying generator stochastic process
X_{t}
,

N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right)\mathrm{d}X_{t_{1}}

(A.1)

where
\dot{X}_{t}
is the derivative process,
\delta(x)
is the Dirac delta function, and
\mathrm{H}(x)
is the Heaviside step function. The condition
\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)
ensures that only the crossings of level
u
are counted, and the condition
\mathrm{H}(\dot{X}_{t_{1}})
ensures that only upcrossings are counted. Writing the expression above as an integral in time, we get,

N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right)\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}

(A.2)

A-A

Mean Number of Upcrossings

We take the expectation of
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
to get the mean number of upcrossings of
X_{t}
in time
T
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right)\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\right]\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right)\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\end{split}

(A.3)

We define the random vector
\mathbf{P}=[X_{t_{1}},\,\dot{X}_{t_{1}}]^{\top}
with probability density function (p.d.f.) given by
f_{\mathbf{P}}(x,\,y)
. Since
X_{t}
is a stationary process, the distribution of
\mathbf{P}
does not depend on
t_{1}
, implying that we can write
\mathbf{P}=[X_{0},\,\dot{X}_{0}]^{\top}
. Therefore,
f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)
is independent of time
t_{1}
and we can rewrite
Eq. (
A.3
) as,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]&=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right)\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\left(x-u\right)\,\mathrm{H}\left(y\right)y\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(x,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{H}\left(y\right)y\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,.\end{split}

(A.4)

Since
f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)
does not depend on time,
t_{1}
, we have,

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]=T\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}y\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

(A.5)

If we further assume that
X_{t}
is zero-mean and Gaussian distributed, this implies that
\mathbf{P}
is a zero-mean multivariate Gaussian stationary process with the correlation matrix defined as,

\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{P}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}[X_{0}X_{0}]&\mathbb{E}[X_{0}\dot{X}_{0}]\\
\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{0}X_{0}]&\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{0}\dot{X}_{0}]\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}r(0)&0\\
0&-r^{\prime\prime}(0)\end{bmatrix}

(A.6)

We have calculated the entries of this matrix as follows:

•

\mathbb{E}\left[X_{0}X_{0}\right]=r(0)
by definition.

•

\mathbb{E}[X_{0}\dot{X}_{0}]=\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]_{t_{2}=t_{1}}
. Taking the derivative outside of the expectation, we get,

\mathbb{E}[X_{0}\dot{X}_{0}]=\frac{\partial\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}X_{t_{2}}]}{\partial t_{2}}\bigr|_{t_{2}=t_{1}}=\frac{\partial r(t_{2}-t_{1})}{\partial t_{2}}\bigr|_{t_{2}=t_{1}}=r^{\prime}(t_{2}-t_{1})|_{t_{2}=t_{1}}=r^{\prime}(0)=0
.

•

\mathbb{E}\left[\dot{X}_{0}X_{0}\right]=\mathbb{E}[X_{0}\dot{X}_{0}]=0
.

•

\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{0}\dot{X}_{0}]=\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]_{t_{2}=t_{1}}
. Taking the derivatives outside of the expectation, we get,

\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{0}\dot{X}_{0}]=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}X_{t_{2}}]}{\partial t_{1}\partial t_{2}}\bigr|_{t_{2}=t_{1}}=\frac{\partial^{2}r(t_{2}-t_{1})}{\partial t_{1}\partial t_{2}}\bigr|_{t_{2}=t_{1}}=-r^{\prime\prime}(t_{2}-t_{1})|_{t_{2}=t_{1}}=-r^{\prime\prime}(0)
.

Using this covariance matrix, we can define the p.d.f.
f_{\mathbf{P}}(x,y)
as,

\begin{split}f_{\mathbf{P}}(x,y)&=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\left|\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{P}}\right|}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}[x,y]\,\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{P}}^{-1}\,[x,y]^{\top}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{r(0)\,(-r^{\prime\prime}(0))}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{r(0)}+\frac{y^{2}}{(-r^{\prime\prime}(0))}\right)\right)\,.\end{split}

(A.7)

Substituting the expression for
f_{\mathbf{P}}(x,\,y)
into the integral in Eq. (
A.5
) yields

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]=T\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{-r^{\prime\prime}(0)}{r(0)}}\,e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2r(0)}}.

(A.8)

A-B

Variance of Upcrossings

The variance of
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
is given by,

\text{Var}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]^{2}

(A.9)

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]
is known from the previous section, therefore, we focus on
\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)^{2}\right]
. Consider the counting process
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
as the integral over time of the differential process, i.e.,

N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}N^{\uparrow}_{u}(t_{1})

(A.10)

Therefore, we can write
\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)^{2}\right]
as,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)^{2}\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{2})\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{2})\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}=t_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{2})+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{2})\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{2})\right]\end{split}

(A.11)

Since there can only be zero or one crossing at any given time,
\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})
is either
0
or
1
, so
\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})^{2}=\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})
. Substituting this into the expression above, we get,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)^{2}\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{2})\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\uparrow}_{u}}(t_{2})\right]}_{\text{\normalsize$M_{2}$}}\end{split}

(A.12)

Hence,
\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]+M_{2}
, where
M_{2}
is the second factorial moment of
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
. Now, we use the definition of
N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)
from Eq. (
A.2
) to get the following expression for
M_{2}
,

M_{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right)\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{2}}\right)\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\delta\left(X_{t_{2}}-u\right)\,\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\,\dot{X}_{t_{2}}\right]\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}

(A.13)

We define the random vector
\mathbf{Q}=[X_{t_{1}},\,X_{t_{2}},\,\dot{X}_{t_{1}},\,\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]^{\top}
with p.d.f. given by
f_{\mathbf{Q}}(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{1},\,t_{2})
. Since
X_{t}
is a stationary process,
\mathbf{Q}
only depends on
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
through their difference
\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)
or
f_{\mathbf{Q}}(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{2}-t_{1})
. Further, since
X_{t}
is zero-mean and Gaussian distributed,
\mathbf{Q}
is a zero-mean multivariate Gaussian stationary process with the correlation matrix defined as,

\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{Q}}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}X_{t_{1}}]&\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}X_{t_{2}}]&\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}\dot{X}_{t_{1}}]&\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]\\
\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{2}}X_{t_{1}}]&\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{2}}X_{t_{2}}]&\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{2}}\dot{X}_{t_{1}}]&\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{2}}\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]\\
\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{1}}X_{t_{1}}]&\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{1}}X_{t_{2}}]&\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\dot{X}_{t_{1}}]&\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]\\
\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{2}}X_{t_{1}}]&\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{2}}X_{t_{2}}]&\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{2}}\dot{X}_{t_{1}}]&\mathbb{E}[\dot{X}_{t_{2}}\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}r(0)&r(t)&0&r^{\prime}(t)\\
r(t)&r(0)&-r^{\prime}(t)&0\\
0&-r^{\prime}(t)&-r^{\prime\prime}(0)&-r^{\prime\prime}(t)\\
r^{\prime}(t)&0&-r^{\prime\prime}(t)&-r^{\prime\prime}(0)\\
\end{bmatrix},

(A.14)

where
t=t_{2}-t_{1}
. The following properties emerge from the fact that
r(t)
is a symmetric function about the origin:
r(-t)=r(t)
,
r^{\prime}(-t)=-r^{\prime}(t)
, and
r^{\prime\prime}(-t)=r^{\prime\prime}(t)
. We used these properties to simplify the covariance matrix above. Using the p.d.f., we can write
M_{2}
from Eq. (
A.13
) as,

\begin{split}M_{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}&\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right)\mathrm{H}\left(\dot{X}_{t_{2}}\right)\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\delta\left(X_{t_{2}}-u\right)\,\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\,\dot{X}_{t_{2}}\right]\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\biggl(\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{H}\left(y_{1}\right)\mathrm{H}\left(y_{2}\right)\delta\left(x_{1}-u\right)\,\delta\left(x_{2}-u\right)\,\\
&\qquad y_{1}y_{2}\,f_{\mathbf{Q}}(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{2}-t_{1})\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{H}\left(y_{1}\right)\mathrm{H}\left(y_{2}\right)y_{1}y_{2}\,f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{2}-t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y_{1}y_{2}\,f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{2}-t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}\end{split}

(A.15)

Central Limit Theorem for Variance

We consider the infinite time limit for the variance per unit time, i.e.,
\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\text{Var}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]}{T}
. We can split the integral in Eq. (
A.27
) as follows,

\begin{split}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\text{Var}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]}{T}=&\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]}{T}+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y_{1}y_{2}\bigl(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{2})\bigr)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&-\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2}{T}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,y_{1}y_{2}\,\left(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{2})\right)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t}_{\text{\normalsize$I^{\prime\prime}$}}\end{split}

Consider the second integral in the expression above:

in the infinite time limit,

\mathrm{F}^{\uparrow}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\text{Var}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]}{\mathbb{E}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]}

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}^{\uparrow}&=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]+2\,T\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y_{1}y_{2}\,\left(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{2})\right)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{E}[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)]}\\

Appendix B
Theory for the Crossing Process

In this section, we derive the formulae for the mean, variance, and Fano factor for the crossing process, i.e., including both
up
and
down
crossings. The steps involved in deriving these formulae are similar to those used for the upcrossing process in Section
A
; therefore, we offer a brief discussion of the proof. The counting process for the total number of crossings can be written as,

N_{u}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\left|\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right|\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}

(B.1)

B-A

Mean Number of Crossings

Taking the expectation of the equation above, we get,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]&=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\left|\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right|\right]\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|y|\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\end{split}

(B.2)

where
f_{\mathbf{P}}(x,\,y)
is the p.d.f. of the random vector
\mathbf{P}=[X_{t_{1}},\,\dot{X}_{t_{1}}]^{\top}
.

B-B

Variance and Fano Factor of Crossings

Using an argument similar to that in Section
A-B
we can write
\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)^{2}\right]
as the following integral,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)^{2}\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}{N_{u}}(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}{N_{u}}(t_{2})\right]}_{\text{\normalsize$M_{2}$}}\end{split}

(B.3)

Now using Eq. (
B.1
),
M_{2}
can be written as,

\begin{split}M_{2}&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\,\delta\left(X_{t_{1}}-u\right)\,\delta\left(X_{t_{2}}-u\right)\left|\dot{X}_{t_{1}}\right|\left|\dot{X}_{t_{2}}\right|\right]\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\,f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{2}-t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}\end{split}

(B.4)

where
f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{2}-t_{1})
is the p.d.f. of the random vector
\mathbf{Q}=[X_{t_{1}},\,X_{t_{2}},\,\dot{X}_{t_{1}},\,\dot{X}_{t_{2}}]^{\top}
. The variance of
N_{u}(T)
is given by,

\begin{split}\text{Var}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]^{2}\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\,f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{2}-t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]^{2}\end{split}

(B.5)

We use Eq. (
B.2
) to write
\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]^{2}
as a double integral, which gives us,

\begin{split}\text{Var}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]^{2}\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\bigl(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t_{2}-t_{1})-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{2})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}\end{split}

(B.6)

We define:

I^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\,\left(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,u,y_{1},y_{2};t_{2}-t_{1})-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,y_{2})\right)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}

(B.7)

Making the variable transformation
t_{2}-t_{1}=t
,
t_{2}=s
and using the arguments about the integral from Section
A-B
, we can write

as,

I^{\prime}=\,2\,T\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{t}{T}\right)\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\left(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{2})\right)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

(B.8)

Therefore, the variance is given by,

\text{Var}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]+2\,T\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{t}{T}\right)\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\bigl(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{2})\bigr)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

(B.9)

In the long-time limit, if the condition in Eq. (
A.29
) is satisfied, we have,

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\text{Var}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]}{T}=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}(T)\right]}{T}+2\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\bigl(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{2})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}\mathrm{d}t

(B.10)

Finally, the Fano factor is given by,

\mathrm{F}=1+\frac{2\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|y_{1}y_{2}\right|\,\left(f_{\mathbf{Q}}(u,\,u,\,y_{1},\,y_{2};\,t)-f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{1})\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y_{2})\right)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|y|\,f_{\mathbf{P}}(u,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}y}.

(B.11)

Appendix E
Note About the Dimensionality

In this section, we note the units of various variables (
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
) and expressions (
I^{\uparrow},I
) used to define our main results.
We note that the autocorrelation function
r(t)
will be of the form,

r\coloneq r(t)=\sigma^{2}\,g\left(t/\tau;\,\bm{\theta}\right)

(E.1)

where
\sigma
defines the dimension of the stochastic process,
\tau
defines the timescales of the process, and
\bm{\theta}
denotes dimensionless variables associated with the correlation function. Therefore,
g\left(t/\tau;\,\bm{\theta}\right)
is a dimensionless function. Taking the derivatives with respect to time on both sides, we get,

p=r^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\tau}\,g^{\prime}\left(t/\tau;\,\bm{\theta}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad q=-r^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\,g^{\prime\prime}\left(t/\tau;\,\bm{\theta}\right)

(E.2)

Substituting
t=0
in these expressions,

r_{0}=\sigma^{2}\,g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{0}=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\,g^{\prime\prime}\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)

(E.3)

Consider the mean, variance, and Fano factor results for level upcrossings, downcrossings, and total crossings. All of the results depend on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
, and
\delta
. Therefore, first, we determine the dimensionality of these variables by plugging in the expressions above. First, consider
\alpha
,

\begin{split}\alpha&=-\frac{r+r_{0}}{2\left(p^{2}+(q-q_{0})(r+r_{0})\right)}\\
&=-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{g\left(t/\tau;\,\bm{\theta}\right)+g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}{2\left((g^{\prime}\left(t/\tau;\,\bm{\theta}\right))^{2}-(g^{\prime\prime}\left(t/\tau;\,\bm{\theta}\right)-g^{\prime\prime}\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right))(g\left(t/\tau;\,\bm{\theta}\right)+g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right))\right)}\right)\end{split}

(E.4)

where the expression in the bracket is dimensionless; therefore,

\alpha=\frac{\tau^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\,h_{\alpha}\left(\tilde{t};\,\bm{\theta}\right)

(E.5)

where
\tilde{t}=t/\tau
is a dimensionless time and
h_{\alpha}\left(\tilde{t};\,\bm{\theta}\right)
is the dimensionless expression. A similar analysis reveals,

\beta=\frac{\tau^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\,h_{\beta}\left(\tilde{t};\,\bm{\theta}\right),\qquad\gamma=\frac{u}{\tau}\,h_{\gamma}\left(\tilde{t};\,\bm{\theta}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\,h_{\delta}\left(\tilde{t};\,\bm{\theta}\right)

(E.6)

where
h_{\beta}\left(\tilde{t};\,\bm{\theta}\right)
,
h_{\gamma}\left(\tilde{t};\,\bm{\theta}\right)
and
h_{\delta}\left(\tilde{t};\,\bm{\theta}\right)
are dimensionless functions. Using these expressions, it can be shown that
I^{\uparrow}
and
I
can be separated into the following dimensional and dimensionless parts,

I^{\uparrow}=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\,h_{I^{\uparrow}}\left(\tilde{t};\,\psi,\,\bm{\theta}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad I=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\,h_{I}\left(\tilde{t};\,\psi,\,\bm{\theta}\right)

(E.7)

where we additionally define the dimensionless parameter
\psi=u/\sigma
. Separating the dimensional and dimensionless expression for mean of upcrossings, we get,

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]=T\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{q_{0}}{r_{0}}}\,e^{-u^{2}/(2r_{0})}=\frac{T}{\tau}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{-g^{\prime\prime}\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}{g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}}\,e^{-\psi^{2}/(2g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right))}\right).

(E.8)

Similarly, for variance in finite time, we have,

\begin{split}\text{Var}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]&=T\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{q_{0}}{r_{0}}}\,e^{-u^{2}/(2r_{0})}+2\int_{0}^{T}\left(1-\frac{t}{T}\right)\,I^{\uparrow}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\\
&=\frac{T}{\tau}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{-g^{\prime\prime}\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}{g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}}\,e^{-\psi^{2}/(2g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right))}+2\int_{0}^{T/\tau}\left(1-\frac{\tau}{T}\,\tilde{t}\right)\,h_{I^{\uparrow}}\left(\tilde{t};\,\psi,\,\bm{\theta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\right),\end{split}

(E.9)

and in the long-time limit,

\text{Var}\left[N_{u}^{\uparrow}(T)\right]=\frac{T}{\tau}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{-g^{\prime\prime}\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}{g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}}\,e^{-\psi^{2}/(2g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right))}+2\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{I^{\uparrow}}\left(\tilde{t};\,\psi,\,\bm{\theta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\right).

(E.10)

Finally, the Fano factor (Eq. (
A.33
)) can be shown to be independent of
\tau

\mathrm{F}^{\uparrow}=1+4\pi\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}}{q_{0}}}\,e^{u^{2}/(2r_{0})}\int_{0}^{\infty}I^{\uparrow}\,\mathrm{d}t=1+4\pi\sqrt{\frac{g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}{-g^{\prime\prime}\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right)}}\,e^{\psi^{2}/(2g\left(0;\,\bm{\theta}\right))}\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{I^{\uparrow}}\left(\tilde{t};\,\psi,\,\bm{\theta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}

(E.11)

Appendix F
Derivation of SDHO Autocorrelation Functions

Here we derive the autocorrelation functions for the stochastic damped harmonic oscillator stated in the main text (Eqs. (
28
)–(
30
)). The SDE (Eq. (
27
)) can be recast as a two-dimensional linear stochastic dynamical system:

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}&=v\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t}&=-\omega_{0}^{2}x-2\zeta\omega_{0}v+\sqrt{4\zeta\omega_{0}\vartheta}\,\eta(t),\end{split}

(F.1)

where
v(t)=\dot{x}(t)
is the velocity of the oscillator.
This system conforms to the general form of a linear stochastic dynamical system:

\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{J}\mathbf{x}\mathrm{d}t+\mathbf{L}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}

(F.2)

where
\mathbf{x}=[x,v]^{\top}
is the state vector, and
\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}=[\mathrm{d}W_{1},\mathrm{d}W_{2}]^{\top}
is a vector of independent standard Wiener increments. The Jacobian matrix
\mathbf{J}
and the noise diffusion matrix
\mathbf{L}
are given by:

\mathbf{J}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-\omega_{0}^{2}&-2\zeta\omega_{0}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{L}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&\sqrt{4\zeta\omega_{0}\vartheta}\end{bmatrix}.

(F.3)

The power spectral density matrix of the state vector
\mathbf{x}
can be obtained using the formula
[
32
]
:

\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{x}}(\omega)=(\dot{\iota}\omega\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J})^{-1}\,\mathbf{L}\,\mathbf{L}^{\top}(-\dot{\iota}\omega\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J})^{-\top}.

(F.4)

Extracting the (1,1) component of
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbf{x}}(\omega)
yields the power spectral density of the position variable
x(t)
:

\mathcal{S}_{x}(\omega)=\frac{4\zeta\omega_{0}\vartheta}{(\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2})^{2}+(2\zeta\omega_{0}\omega)^{2}}.

(F.5)

According to the Wiener–Khinchin theorem, the autocorrelation function of the stationary process
x(t)
, denoted by
r_{x}(t)=\mathbb{E}[x(t^{\prime})x(t^{\prime}+t)]
, is the inverse Fourier transform of its power spectral density:

r_{x}(t)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left\{\mathcal{S}_{x}(\omega)\right\}(t).

(F.6)

Calculating the inverse Fourier transform of Eq. (
F.5
) provides the correlation functions
r_{x}(t)
in the three distinct damping regimes stated in the main text (Eqs. (
28
)–(
30
)). Note that
r_{x}(0)=\vartheta/\omega_{0}^{2}
in all cases, representing the variance of the position
x(t)
as determined by the equipartition theorem.

Appendix G

Theorem G.1
.

Let
r(t)
be the autocorrelation function of a one-dimensional real Gaussian stationary process. For brevity, we introduce the following notations:
r(t)\rightarrow r
,
r(0)\rightarrow r_{0}
,
r^{\prime}(t)\rightarrow p
,
r^{\prime}(0)\rightarrow p_{0}
,
-r^{\prime\prime}(t)\rightarrow q
, and
-r^{\prime\prime}(0)\rightarrow q_{0}
. Then, for all
t\geq 0
the following expressions are strictly positive,

\begin{split}&-\frac{r+r_{0}}{2\left(p^{2}+(q-q_{0})(r+r_{0})\right)}>0\\
&-\frac{r_{0}-r}{2\left(p^{2}+(q+q_{0})(r-r_{0})\right)}>0\end{split}

(G.1)

Proof.

Since
r(t)
is the autocorrelation function of a real stationary process, it has the spectral representation

r(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos(\omega t)\,dF(\omega),

(G.2)

where
F
is a nonnegative measure. Differentiating with respect to time, we obtain

r^{\prime}(t)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega\sin(\omega t)\,dF(\omega)\quad\text{and}\quad r^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\cos(\omega t)\,dF(\omega).

(G.3)

In the notation defined in the theorem and using the integral formulae above, we have,

\begin{split}r&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos(\omega t)\,dF(\omega)\\
p&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega\sin(\omega t)\,dF(\omega)\\
q&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\cos(\omega t)\,dF(\omega)\\
r_{0}&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dF(\omega)\\
p_{0}&=0\\
q_{0}&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\,dF(\omega)\end{split}

(G.4)

Using these relations, we have the following integral expressions for the relevant quantities,

r+r_{0}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl(1+\cos(\omega t)\bigr)\,dF(\omega)\quad\text{and}\quad r_{0}-r=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl(1-\cos(\omega t)\bigr)\,dF(\omega),

(G.5)

and,

q_{0}-q=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\Bigl(1-\cos(\omega t)\Bigr)dF(\omega)\quad\text{and}\quad q_{0}+q=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\Bigl(1+\cos(\omega t)\Bigr)dF(\omega).

(G.6)

Now, writing

p^{2}=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega\sin(\omega t)\,dF(\omega)\right)^{2},

(G.7)

and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\left(\int_{S}f(x)g(x)\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{S}f(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x)\right)\left(\int_{S}g(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x)\right),

(G.8)

gives us,

p^{2}\leq\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}(\omega t)}{1+\cos(\omega t)}\,dF(\omega)\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1+\cos(\omega t))\,dF(\omega)\right).

(G.9)

A short calculation shows that

\frac{\sin^{2}(\omega t)}{1+\cos(\omega t)}=\frac{4\sin^{2}\bigl(\frac{\omega t}{2}\bigr)\cos^{2}\bigl(\frac{\omega t}{2}\bigr)}{2\cos^{2}\bigl(\frac{\omega t}{2}\bigr)}=2\sin^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\omega t}{2}\Bigr)=1-\cos\left(\omega t\right).

(G.10)

Therefore,

p^{2}\leq\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\left(1-\cos\left(\omega t\right)\right)dF(\omega)\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1+\cos(\omega t))\,dF(\omega)\right),

(G.11)

or,

p^{2}\leq\left(q_{0}-q\right)\left(r_{0}+r\right).

(G.12)

Since equality in the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality can occur only if the integrands are proportional, which is excluded for a nondegenerate spectral measure, i.e., a spectral measure not concentrated on just a finite set of points, we have,

p^{2}-(q_{0}-q)(r+r_{0})<0.

(G.13)

Further, since
r+r_{0}>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0
, we have,

-\frac{r+r_{0}}{2\left(p^{2}+(q-q_{0})(r+r_{0})\right)}>0

(G.14)

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz, we can also write,

\begin{split}p^{2}&\leq\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}(\omega t)}{1-\cos(\omega t)}\,dF(\omega)\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\omega t))\,dF(\omega)\right)\\
p^{2}&\leq\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\left(1+\cos(\omega t)\right)\,dF(\omega)\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\omega t))\,dF(\omega)\right)\\
p^{2}&\leq\left(q_{0}+q\right)\left(r_{0}-r\right)\\
\end{split}

(G.15)

Since the spectral measure is nondegenerate, strict inequality follows,

p^{2}-\left(q_{0}+q\right)\left(r_{0}-r\right)<0

(G.16)

Further, since
r_{0}-r>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0
, we have,

-\frac{r_{0}-r}{2\left(p^{2}+(q+q_{0})(r-r_{0})\right)}>0

(G.17)

Theorem G.2
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result holds:

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}(y^{2}-x^{2})\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=&\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{2\alpha\beta}\left(1+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)\\
&+\pi\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{(\alpha\beta)^{3/2}}\right)\mathcal{T}\left(\gamma\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}},\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)\end{split}

(G.18)

where
\operatorname{Erf}(z)
is the error function defined as

\operatorname{Erf}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{z}e^{-t^{2}}\mathrm{d}t,

(G.19)

and
\mathcal{T}(h,a)
is Owen’s T function
[
20
]
defined as

\mathcal{T}(h,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{a}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}(1+t^{2})}}{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(G.20)

Proof.

Denoting the integral that we have to evaluate by,

I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}(y^{2}-x^{2})\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

(G.21)

Integrating the inner integral in
y
and using the result from Lemma
G.6
, we have,

\begin{split}I&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\beta}|x|\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta x^{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\beta^{3/2}}\left(2\beta x^{2}-1\right)e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\operatorname{Erfc}\left(\sqrt{\beta}|x|\right)\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{2\beta}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{1}$}}-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\beta^{3/2}}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2\beta x^{2}-1\right)e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\operatorname{Erfc}\left(\sqrt{\beta}|x|\right)\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{2}$}}\end{split}

(G.22)

I_{1}
is shown to be given by the following expression in Lemma
G.7
,

I_{1}=\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\gamma\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)

(G.23)

We make the variable change of
\sqrt{\beta}\,x=k
in the integral
I_{2}
which gives us,

\begin{split}I_{2}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2k^{2}-1\right)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\operatorname{Erfc}\left(|k|\right)\mathrm{d}k\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2k^{2}-1\right)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\mathrm{d}k}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{21}$}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2k^{2}-1\right)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(|k|\right)\mathrm{d}k}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{22}$}}\end{split}

(G.24)

The integral in
I_{21}
is given by Lemma
G.8
, upon simplification, we get,

I_{21}=\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+2\beta\gamma^{2}-1\right)

(G.25)

and
I_{22}
can be written as,

\begin{split}I_{22}&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2k^{2}-1\right)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(|k|\right)\mathrm{d}k\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(k\right)\mathrm{d}k+\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(-k\right)\mathrm{d}k\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(k\right)\mathrm{d}k+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(k\right)\mathrm{d}k\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\right)\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(k\right)\mathrm{d}k\end{split}

(G.26)

Applying integration by parts, we get

\begin{split}I_{22}=&\int_{0}^{\infty}\underbrace{\left(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\right)\left(2k^{2}-1\right)}_{\text{\normalsize second part}}\,\underbrace{\operatorname{Erf}\left(k\right)}_{\text{\normalsize first part}}\mathrm{d}k\\
=&\underbrace{\left[\operatorname{Erf}\left(k\right)\int\left(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\right)\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\mathrm{d}k\right]_{0}^{\infty}}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{221}$}}\\
&-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-k^{2}}\right)\left(\int\left(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\right)\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\mathrm{d}k\right)\mathrm{d}k}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{222}$}}\end{split}

(G.27)

First, the indefinite integral can be evaluated using the standard results on the normal integrals
[
20
]
,

\begin{split}\int&\biggl(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\biggr)\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\mathrm{d}k=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}}\left(\gamma\sqrt{\beta}-\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)e^{\frac{4\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\beta}}k}-k\right)\\
&+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)\left(\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)\right)\end{split}

(G.28)

Let us denote the integral above by
v(k)
. We have the following:

\biggl[\operatorname{Erf}(k)\int\biggl(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\biggr)\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\mathrm{d}k\biggr]_{0}^{\infty}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Erf}(k)\,v(k)-\lim_{k\rightarrow 0}\operatorname{Erf}(k)\,v(k)

(G.29)

Using the fact that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Erf}(k)=1
and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(k\,e^{-a\,k}\right)=0
, we have,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Erf}(k)\,v(k)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right),

(G.30)

and using the fact that
\lim_{k\rightarrow 0}\operatorname{Erf}(k)=0
, we have,

\lim_{k\rightarrow 0}\operatorname{Erf}(k)\,v(k)=0.

(G.31)

Therefore, the first term of
I_{22}
in Eq. (
G.27
) is,

I_{221}=\biggl[\operatorname{Erf}(k)\int\biggl(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\biggr)\left(2k^{2}-1\right)\mathrm{d}k\biggr]_{0}^{\infty}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)

(G.32)

Using the integral in Eq. (
G.28
), we can write
I_{222}
as the following definite integral,

\begin{split}I_{222}=&\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-k^{2}}\right)\biggl(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}}\left(\gamma\sqrt{\beta}-\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)e^{\frac{4\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\beta}}k}-k\right)\\
&+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)\left(\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)\right)\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}k\end{split}

(G.33)

Upon expanding, we get,

\begin{split}I_{222}=&\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-k^{2}}\left(\gamma\sqrt{\beta}-\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)e^{\frac{4\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\beta}}k}-k\right)\mathrm{d}k}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{2221}$}}\\
&+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-k^{2}}\left(\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left(k-\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}k}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{2222}$}}\end{split}

(G.34)

Using Lemma
G.9
, we have the following expression for
I_{2221}
,

I_{2221}=-\frac{\beta\,e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\biggl(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\,(2\alpha+\beta)\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\biggr).

(G.35)

Finally, using Lemma
G.10
, we have the following expression for
I_{2222}
,

I_{2222}=4\sqrt{\pi}\,\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\gamma,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)

(G.36)

Now, we go backward to find a simplified expression for
I
. First, we find
I_{222}
using the definition in Eq. (
G.34
),

\begin{split}I_{222}=&\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\beta}{\alpha}I_{2221}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)I_{2222}\\
=&-\frac{2\beta^{2}\,e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\,(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\biggl(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\,(2\alpha+\beta)\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\biggr)\\
&+\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\gamma,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)\end{split}

(G.37)

Next, we find
I_{22}
using the definition in Eq. (
G.27
),

\begin{split}I_{22}=&I_{221}-I_{222}\\
=&\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)+\frac{2\beta^{2}\,e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\,(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\biggl(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\,(2\alpha+\beta)\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\biggr)\\
&-\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\gamma,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)\end{split}

(G.38)

Using the definition in Eq. (
G.24
),
I_{2}
is given by,

\begin{split}I_{2}=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\left(I_{21}-I_{22}\right)\\
=&\cancel{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+2\beta\gamma^{2}-1\right)}-\cancel{\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\alpha^{3/2}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)}+\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{\alpha^{3/2}}\left(\beta-\alpha+2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}\right)\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\gamma,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)\\

(G.39)

Finally, using the definition in Eq. (
G.22
), we get the expression for

,

\begin{split}I=&\frac{1}{2\beta}I_{1}-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\beta^{3/2}}I_{2}\\
=&\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{2\beta\,(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\gamma\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)\\

&+\frac{\,e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{2\alpha\,(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\biggl(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\,(2\alpha+\beta)\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\biggr)\end{split}

(G.40)

Simplifying the expression above gives us the final result,

I=\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{2\alpha\beta}\left(1+\sqrt{\pi}\gamma\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)+\pi\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{(\alpha\beta)^{3/2}}\right)\mathcal{T}\left(\gamma\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}},\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)

(G.41)

Theorem G.4
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result holds:

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|y^{2}-x^{2}\right|\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=&\frac{2e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{\alpha\beta}\left(1+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)\\
&+4\pi\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\left(\alpha\beta\right)^{3/2}}\right)\left(\mathcal{T}\left(\gamma\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}},\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)-\frac{1}{8}\right)\end{split}

(G.46)

Proof.

Let us denote the integral we have to evaluate by
I
.

I=\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|y^{2}-x^{2}\right|\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

(G.47)

The integral is evaluated over
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We split
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into two disjoint regions (Fig.
8
), defined as follows:

•

\mathcal{R}_{+}:\{(x,y):|y|\geq|x|\}
, i.e.,
y^{2}\geq x^{2}
, and

•

\mathcal{R}_{-}:\{(x,y):|x|>|y|\}
, i.e.,
x^{2}>y^{2}
.

Writing the integral over these disjoint regions gives us,

\begin{split}I&=\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}\left|y^{2}-x^{2}\right|\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}\left|y^{2}-x^{2}\right|\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\underbrace{\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{1}$}}-\underbrace{\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{2}$}}\end{split}

(G.48)

Splitting of
\mathcal{R}_{+}
into two disjoint regions.

\mathcal{R}_{+}^{1}
is the region in blue satisfying
y\geq|x|
and
\mathcal{R}_{+}^{2}
is the region in purple satisfying
y\leq-|x|
.

Therefore,
I=I_{1}-I_{2}
. For evaluating the integral
I_{1}
, we split
\mathcal{R}_{+}
into two disjoint subregions (Fig.
9
) defined as follows:

•

\mathcal{R}_{+}^{1}:\{(x,y):y\geq|x|\}
, and

•

\mathcal{R}_{+}^{2}:\{(x,y):y\leq-|x|\}
.

Therefore,
I_{1}
can be written as,

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{+}^{1}}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{+}^{2}}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{-|x|}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(G.49)

Since the integrand in the second integral is independent of the sign of
y
, we have,

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=2\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{0}$}}\end{split}

(G.50)

We know the result of
I_{0}
from Theorem
G.2
. Therefore, we have
I_{1}=2I_{0}
. Now consider the following integral, the result of which is known (Lemma
G.11
),

\begin{split}I^{\prime}&=\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\int_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(G.51)

We recognize these integrals from Eq. (
G.48
) and have the following relation,

I^{\prime}=I_{1}+I_{2}\quad\text{or}\quad I_{2}=I^{\prime}-I_{1}

(G.52)

Therefore,
I
can be expressed in terms of the known integrals as follows,

\begin{split}I=&I_{1}-I_{2}\\
=&I_{1}-\left(I^{\prime}-I_{1}\right)\\
=&2I_{1}-I^{\prime}\\
=&4I_{0}-I^{\prime}\\
=&4\left(\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{2\alpha\beta}\left(1+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)+\pi\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{(\alpha\beta)^{3/2}}\right)\mathcal{T}\left(\gamma\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}},\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)\right)\\
&-\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\left(\alpha\beta\right)^{3/2}}\right)\\
=&\frac{2e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{\alpha\beta}\left(1+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)+4\pi\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\left(\alpha\beta\right)^{3/2}}\right)\left(\mathcal{T}\left(\gamma\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}},\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)-\frac{1}{8}\right)\end{split}

(G.53)

∎

Lemma G.6
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result holds:

\begin{split}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{2\beta}|x|e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta x^{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\beta^{3/2}}\left(2\beta x^{2}-1\right)e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\operatorname{Erfc}\left(\sqrt{\beta}|x|\right)\end{split}

I=\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y.

(G.59)

x
is treated as a constant with respect to the integration variable
y
, we can factor out the constant exponential term,

I=e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\left(\int_{|x|}^{\infty}y^{2}e^{-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y-x^{2}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}e^{-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\right).

\operatorname{Erfc}(z)=1-\operatorname{Erf}(z)

\int_{|x|}^{\infty}y^{2}e^{-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{|x|}{2\beta}e^{-\beta x^{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\beta^{3/2}}\operatorname{Erfc}\left(\sqrt{\beta}|x|\right).

(G.62)

Substituting these results back into the expression for
I
, we get,

I=\frac{1}{2\beta}|x|e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta x^{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\beta^{3/2}}\left(2\beta x^{2}-1\right)e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\operatorname{Erfc}\left(\sqrt{\beta}|x|\right)

(G.63)

Lemma G.7
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result holds:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\gamma\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)

(G.64)

Proof.

We denote the integral by
I
and split it at
0
to get,

\begin{split}I&=\int_{-\infty}^{0}(-x)\,e^{-\beta x^{2}-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\beta x^{2}-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\beta x^{2}-\alpha(x+\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{1}$}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\beta x^{2}-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{2}$}}.\end{split}

(G.65)

We complete the square in
x
for both
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, which gives us,

I_{1}=e^{-\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\,x+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2}=e^{-\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\,x-\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x

(G.66)

These are well-known integrals
[
20
]
and are given by,

I_{1}=\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{2(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}-\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\gamma\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erfc}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)

(G.67)

I_{2}=\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{2(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\gamma\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erfc}\left(-\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)

(G.68)

Substituting
\operatorname{Erfc}(z)=1-\operatorname{Erf}(z)
and noting that
\operatorname{Erf}(-z)=-\operatorname{Erf}(z)
, we get the following expression for
I
,

\begin{split}I&=I_{1}+I_{2}\\
&=\frac{e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\gamma\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)\end{split}

(G.69)

∎

Lemma G.8
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result holds:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2x^{2}-1\right)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(x-\gamma\sqrt{\beta})^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+2\beta\gamma^{2}-1\right)

(G.70)

Proof.

We denote the integral under consideration by
I
and make the following variable transformation,
x=k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}
. This gives us

\begin{split}I&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2\left(k+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-1\right)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}k^{2}}\mathrm{d}k\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2k^{2}+4\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\,k+2\beta\gamma^{2}-1\right)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}k^{2}}\mathrm{d}k\\
&=2\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}k^{2}}\mathrm{d}k}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{1}$}}+4\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k\,e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}k^{2}}\mathrm{d}k}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{2}$}}+\left(2\beta\gamma^{2}-1\right)\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}k^{2}}\mathrm{d}k}_{\text{\normalsize$I_{3}$}}\end{split}

(G.71)

The integrand of
I_{2}
is an odd function; therefore,
I_{2}=0
.
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
are standard integrals given by,

I_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{3/2}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{3}=\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}

(G.72)

Therefore,
I
is given by,

\begin{split}I&=\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{3/2}+\left(2\beta\gamma^{2}-1\right)\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\\
&=\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+2\beta\gamma^{2}-1\right)\end{split}

(G.73)

∎

Lemma G.9
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result holds:

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(x+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-x^{2}}\biggl(\gamma\sqrt{\beta}&-\left(x+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)e^{\frac{4\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\beta}}x}-x\biggr)\mathrm{d}x=\\
&-\frac{\beta\,e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\biggl(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\,(2\alpha+\beta)\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\biggr)\end{split}

(G.74)

Proof.

We denote the integral by
I
and expand to get the following four integrals,

\begin{split}I=&\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(x+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(x+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-x^{2}+\frac{4\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\beta}}x}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&-\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(x+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(x+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-x^{2}+\frac{4\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\beta}}x}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(G.75)

Forming a square in
x
in the exponential terms, we get,

\begin{split}I=&\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\,e^{-\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\beta}}\,x+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\,e^{-\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\beta}}\,x-\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&-e^{-\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\beta}}\,x+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x-e^{-\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\beta}}\,x-\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(G.76)

These are standard integrals of normal functions. Using the results in
[
20
]
, we get,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\beta}}\,x+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}\left(1-\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)

(G.77)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\beta}}\,x-\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}\left(1+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)

(G.78)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\beta}}\,x+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\beta}{2(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\,e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}-\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\gamma\,\left(1-\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)\right)

(G.79)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,e^{-\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\beta}}\,x-\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\beta}{2(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\,e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\alpha\gamma\,\left(1+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\right)\right)

(G.80)

Substituting these expressions in Eq. (
G.76
) and simplifying, we get,

I=-\frac{\beta\,e^{-\alpha\gamma^{2}}}{(\alpha+\beta)^{3/2}}\biggl(\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}+\sqrt{\pi}\,\gamma\,(2\alpha+\beta)\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\biggr)

(G.81)

∎

Lemma G.10
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result holds:

\begin{split}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}\left(\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left(x+\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left(x-\gamma\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=4\sqrt{\pi}\,\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\gamma,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)\end{split}

(G.82)

Proof.

Consider the result “
10,016.6
” from Owen’s table of normal integrals
[
20
]
. It states that,

\int_{0}^{\infty}G^{\prime}(x)\,G(a+bx)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}G\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}}}\right)+\mathcal{T}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}}},b\right)

(G.83)

where
G(z)
is the cumulative distribution function of a standard normal Gaussian random variable and
G^{\prime}(z)
is its derivative, i.e.,

G(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{z}e^{-t^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{and}\quad G^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^{2}/2},

(G.84)

and
\mathcal{T}(h,a)
is Owen’s T function defined as,

\mathcal{T}(h,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{a}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}(1+t^{2})}}{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(G.85)

First, we note the following relation,

G(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)

(G.86)

We substitute this expression into the
LHS
of Eq. (
G.83
),

\begin{split}\text{LHS}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}G^{\prime}(x)\,G(a+bx)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}\right)\,\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{a+bx}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{a+bx}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{a+bx}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(G.87)

Making the variable change
k=x/\sqrt{2}
gives us,

\text{LHS}=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-k^{2}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(bk+\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}k

(G.88)

Now, the
RHS
of Eq. (
G.83
) can be written as,

\begin{split}\text{RHS}&=\frac{1}{2}G\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}}}\right)+\mathcal{T}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}}},b\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\left(1+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2(1+b^{2})}}\right)\right)+\mathcal{T}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}}},b\right)\end{split}

(G.89)

Equating
\text{LHS}=\text{RHS}
gives us the following relation,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-k^{2}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(bk+\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}k=\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2(1+b^{2})}}\right)+2\,\mathcal{T}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}}},b\right)\right)

(G.90)

Finally, we replace
a/\sqrt{2}
by
c
on both sides to get the relation,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-k^{2}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(bk+c\right)\,\mathrm{d}k=\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}}}\right)+2\,\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+b^{2}}}\,c,b\right)\right)

(G.91)

Replacing
c\rightarrow-c
and using the properties that
\operatorname{Erf}(-z)=-\operatorname{Erf}(z)
and
\mathcal{T}(-h,a)=\mathcal{T}(h,a)
, we also have the relation,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-k^{2}}\operatorname{Erf}\left(bk-c\right)\,\mathrm{d}k=\sqrt{\pi}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Erf}\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}}}\right)+2\,\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+b^{2}}}\,c,b\right)\right)

(G.92)

Adding these two equations gives the following result,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-k^{2}}\left(\operatorname{Erf}\left(bk+c\right)+\operatorname{Erf}\left(bk-c\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}k=4\sqrt{\pi}\,\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+b^{2}}}\,c,b\right)

(G.93)

Substituting
b\rightarrow\sqrt{\alpha/\beta}
and
c\rightarrow\gamma\sqrt{\alpha}
into the equation above gives us,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-k^{2}}\left(\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\,k+\gamma\sqrt{\alpha}\right)+\operatorname{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\,k-\gamma\sqrt{\alpha}\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}k=4\sqrt{\pi}\,\mathcal{T}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}}\,\gamma,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)

(G.94)

∎

Lemma G.11
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result holds:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)\,e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\left(\alpha\beta\right)^{3/2}}\right)

(G.95)

Proof.

Let the integral we need to evaluate be denoted by
I
. First, we integrate in
y
assuming
x
is constant,

\begin{split}I&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{2}\,e^{-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y-x^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\beta y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\beta^{3/2}}-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta}}\,x^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\beta^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2}e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(G.96)

Evaluating the integral in
x
and simplifying the expressions, we get the final expression for
I
,

\begin{split}I&=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\beta^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2}e^{-\alpha(x-\gamma)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\beta^{3/2}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\alpha^{3/2}}\left(1+2\alpha\gamma^{2}\right)\right)\\
&=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{\alpha}-2\gamma^{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta-2\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}}{\left(\alpha\beta\right)^{3/2}}\right)\end{split}

(G.97)

∎
