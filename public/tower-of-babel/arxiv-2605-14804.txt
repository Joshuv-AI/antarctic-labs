Uniquely 2-colourable 4-cycle decompositions
Andrea C. Burgess, David A. Pike, Shahriyar Pourakbar Saffar

Abstract
A cycle system of order n n is a decomposition of the edges of the complete graph K n K_{n} into cycles of a fixed length. A cycle system is said to be k k -colourable if we can assign k k colours to its vertices so that no cycle is monochromatic. A k k -colourable cycle system is uniquely k k -colourable if its colouring is unique up to the permutation of colour classes. In this paper, we construct uniquely 2 2 -colourable 4 4 -cycle systems of order n n for all admissible n ≥ 49 n\geq 49 , and also uniquely 2 2 -colourable 4 4 -cycle decompositions of K n − I K_{n}-I , for all admissible n ≥ 50 n\geq 50 . These constructions contribute to the broader study of uniquely colourable cycle systems and open new directions for future research.



Keywords
: cycle systems, graph colouring, unique colouring, combinatorial design

\frac{n(n-1)}{2}

[

,

,

,

. When

-cycle decomposition of the cocktail party graph

For a positive integer

The history of colouring cycle systems starts with Steiner triple systems, which also happen to be

One may notice that the count of different ways to colour a cycle decomposition does not increase when we reduce the number of colours. Let
{\cal D}
be an

is uniquely

That is where the prior knowledge of uniquely colourable cycle decompositions ends. In this paper, we push it further by affirmatively answering the questions “Do any uniquely 2‑colourable 4‑cycle systems exist?” and “Do any uniquely 2-colourable 4-cycle decompositions of cocktail party graphs exist?”. Although being exclusively alt-colourable does not necessarily imply possessing a unique 2-colouring, an exclusively alt-colourable cycle decomposition yields predictable colour patterns, which we will use to our advantage.

If

Theorem 1.2
.

For all admissible

n
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For all admissible
n\geq 50
, there exists a uniquely 2-colourable 4-cycle decomposition of the cocktail party graph
K_{n}-I
.

2
Exclusively alt-colourable 4-cycle decompositions

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. To do so, we define a total ordering for the parts of a complete multipartite graph and we will explicitly form an exclusively alt-colourable 4-cycle decomposition for that graph.

Let us start by introducing an ordered set for the labelling of each part. Consider the set of triples with labels in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for a positive integer
\ell
. We denote this set of points by
L_{\ell,2,2}
. For the sake of clarity, for any positive integer
n
,
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
consists of the elements
0,\ldots,(n-1)
(with the natural order
0<1<\cdots<(n-2)<(n-1)
), but the usual algebraic ring operations of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
can be used if necessary (for example,
(n-1)+1=0
).
We set the primary order of
L_{\ell,2,2}
to be the lexicographic order that inherits the order of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and the order of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. So, for all
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in L_{\ell,2,2}
and
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in L_{\ell,2,2}
,
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\prec(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
if and only if there exists
i\in\{1,2,3\}
such that
a_{i}<b_{i}
, and
a_{j}=b_{j}
whenever
j<i
. We denote the
successor
of

by
x^{\triangleright}
, which is the element of
L_{\ell,2,2}
such that
x
and
x^{\triangleright}
are consecutive and
x\prec x^{\triangleright}
. We also adopt the convention
(\ell-1,1,1)^{\triangleright}=(0,0,0)
. For a subset
A\subseteq L_{\ell,2,2}
, we denote the set
\{x^{\triangleright}\mid x\in A\}
by
A^{\triangleright}
.

Let
A=\{x,y\}
be a pair of distinct points in
L_{\ell,2,2}
.
If
y=x^{\triangleright}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\{0\}
, then we call
A
an
\alpha
-set
.
If
y=x^{\triangleright}
and

, then we call
A
a
\beta
-set
.
If
y-x=(0,1,0)
, i.e.,
A=\{(i,0,c),(i,1,c)\}
for some

and
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, then we call
A
a
\gamma
-set
. We denote the
\gamma
-set
\{(i,0,c),(i,1,c)\}
by
\Gamma_{i,c}
.

One can observe that since we have an even number of points in
L_{\ell,2,2}
, the family of all
\alpha
-sets partitions
L_{\ell,2,2}
into 2-subsets. This family forms what we call the
\alpha
-partition
of
L_{\ell,2,2}
. In the same fashion, we define the
\beta
-partition
and the
\gamma
-partition
of
L_{\ell,2,2}
as the family of all
\beta
-sets and the family of all
\gamma
-sets, respectively, and they both partition
L_{\ell,2,2}
into 2-subsets.

Now, view the elements of
L_{\ell,2,2}
as vertices and suppose
L_{\ell,2,2}
is a coclique on
4\ell
vertices, totally ordered as we have described. As we will see in Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, a 2-colouring of the vertices of
L_{\ell,2,2}
, namely
\varphi
, can interact curiously with some partitions of
L_{\ell,2,2}
. With respect to
\varphi
we call a pair of vertices
\{x,y\}
a
twin-pair of type c
or a
{\mathrm{TPT}}_{c}^{\varphi}
if
\varphi(x)=\varphi(y)=c
. When the colouring is fixed, we instead use
{\mathrm{TPT}}_{c}
and when
\{x,y\}
is an
\alpha
-set (
\beta
-set,
\gamma
-set, resp.), we may use the term
\alpha
-twin-pair (
\beta
-twin-pair,
\gamma
-twin-pair, resp.). We call
\varphi
an
\alpha
-free
(
\beta
-free
,
\gamma
-free
, resp.) colouring if it contains no
\alpha
-twin-pairs (
\beta
-twin-pairs,
\gamma
-twin-pairs, resp.) of any type. With this terminology, we may assume
L_{\ell,2,2}
is a single part of a multipartite graph and characterise an alt-colouring on the vertices of this graph as a 2-colouring that is both
\alpha
-free and
\beta
-free, i.e., for all
x\in L_{\ell,2,2}
,
\varphi(x)\neq\varphi(x^{\triangleright})
. This assertion mostly follows by definition, but to check whether or not it is also true that
\varphi((\ell-1,1,1))\neq\varphi((0,0,0))
when
\varphi
is an alt-colouring, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\varphi:L_{\ell,2,2}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a 2-colouring of the vertices of
L_{\ell,2,2}
. For every
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, let
a_{c}
be the number of
\alpha
-
{\mathrm{TPT}}_{c}
and
b_{c}
be the number of
\beta
-
{\mathrm{TPT}}_{c}
in
L_{\ell,2,2}
. Then
a_{0}+b_{1}=a_{1}+b_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
C_{0}
be the set of all vertices with colour
0
. We have a total of
\frac{4\ell}{2}-(a_{0}+a_{1})

\alpha
-sets and
\frac{4\ell}{2}-(b_{0}+b_{1})

\beta
-sets that are not twin-pairs. Note that every non-twin-pair 2-subset of
L_{\ell,2,2}
must contain exactly one vertex with colour 0. Hence,

2(a_{0})+(2\ell-(a_{0}+a_{1}))=\lvert C_{0}\rvert=2(b_{0})+(2\ell-(b_{0}+b_{1}))\Rightarrow a_{0}+b_{1}=a_{1}+b_{0}.

∎

If
\varphi
is an alt-colouring, then there are no
\alpha
-twin-pairs and no
\beta
-twin-pairs except possibly
\{(\ell-1,1,1),(0,0,0)\}
, which cannot be the only
\beta
-twin-pair; otherwise, we would have
a_{0}+b_{1}\neq a_{1}+b_{0}
. As a result, the aforementioned characterisation of an alt-colouring is valid and we may use it hereafter. In fact, Lemma
2.2
contains additional characterisations for an alt-colouring of
L_{\ell,2,2}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
\varphi
is a 2-colouring of the vertices of
L_{\ell,2,2}
. The following statements are equivalent.

1.

\varphi
is an alt-colouring.

2.

For all
x\in L_{\ell,2,2}
,
\varphi(x)\neq\varphi(x^{\triangleright})
.

3.

\varphi
is both
\alpha
-free and
\beta
-free.

4.

{\cal X}_{0}=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\{0\}
and
{\cal X}_{1}=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\{1\}
are the two colour classes of
\varphi
.

Proof.

We have already covered the equivalency of the first three statements. We prove the second and fourth statements are equivalent. For
i\in\{0,1\}
, if
x\in{\cal X}_{i}
, then
x^{\triangleright}\not\in{\cal X}_{i}
and
(x^{\triangleright})^{\triangleright}\in{\cal X}_{i}
. If for a set
A\subseteq L_{\ell,2,2}
,
A=(A^{\triangleright})^{\triangleright}
and
x\in A
, then
(x^{\triangleright})^{\triangleright}\in A
. Iteratively, as a result, the only sets with the property that
A=(A^{\triangleright})^{\triangleright}
are
\emptyset
,
{\cal X}_{0}
,
{\cal X}_{1}
, and
L_{\ell,2,2}
.
Suppose
\{A,B\}
is a partition of
L_{\ell,2,2}
such that
A^{\triangleright}=B
. Let
C
be a set such that
C^{\triangleright}=A
. If
x\in A\cap C
, then, contradictively,
x^{\triangleright}\in A^{\triangleright}\cap C^{\triangleright}=B\cap A=\emptyset
. Therefore,
C\subseteq A^{c}=B
and since
\lvert C\rvert=\lvert C^{\triangleright}\rvert=\lvert A\rvert=\lvert A^{\triangleright}\rvert=\lvert B\rvert
,
C=B
and so
A=(A^{\triangleright})^{\triangleright}
.
Hence,
\{{\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}_{1}\}
is the unique partition of
L_{\ell,2,2}
such that
{\cal X}_{0}^{\triangleright}={\cal X}_{1}
. This property precisely characterises the colour classes described in the second statement. The uniqueness yields the equivalency.
∎

This lemma will give us a powerful tool, because if
\varphi
is an alt-colouring, then all
\gamma
-sets will be twin-pairs and for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
,
\Gamma_{i,0}
and
\Gamma_{i,1}
are
\gamma
-twin-pairs of different types.

Since we want to use the join of some copies of the coclique
L_{\ell,2,2}
to construct a part-wise totally ordered complete multipartite graph, we may add an additional variable to
L_{\ell,2,2}
so we will be able to distinguish between different parts of the constructed complete multipartite graph. We use the new notation
L^{i}_{\ell,2,2}
for
\{i\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong\{i\}\times L_{\ell,2,2}
.

A complete multipartite graph can be decomposed into complete bipartite subgraphs, each of which is induced by a pair of maximal cocliques. So, to decompose a multipartite graph into 4-cycles, we may start with these bipartite subgraphs. A complete bipartite graph is 4-cycle decomposable, i.e., it can be decomposed into 4-cycles, if and only if each of its parts has an even number of vertices
[
17
]
. If even-sized subsets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
partition a part of a 4-cycle decomposable complete bipartite graph
G
, and if
{\cal D}_{1}
and
{\cal D}_{2}
are 4-cycle decompositions of induced subgraphs on
V(G)\setminus X_{2}
and
V(G)\setminus X_{1}
, respectively, then
{\cal D}_{1}\cup{\cal D}_{2}
decomposes
G
into 4-cycles. But each of these induced subgraphs is again a complete bipartite graph. So we may continue breaking the parts of these smaller bipartite graphs in the same manner until we end up with subgraphs isomorphic to
K_{2,2}
—which is a 4-cycle. In particular, let
G=K_{m,n}
be a 4-cycle decomposable complete bipartite graph with parts
A
and
B
and suppose
\lvert A\rvert=m
and
\lvert B\rvert=n
. Let
{\cal A}
be a partition of the vertices of
A
into 2-subsets and
{\cal B}
be a partition of the vertices of
B
into 2-subsets. The family of 4-cycles

\mathrm{BD}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\left\{(a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\mid\{a,a^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{A},\{b,b^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{B}\right\}

is a 4-cycle decomposition of
G
.

For a positive integer
\ell
, we define
\mathrm{BG}_{\ell,2,2}
as the complete bipartite graph with parts
L^{0}_{\ell,2,2}
and
L^{1}_{\ell,2,2}
. Since
\lvert L^{0}_{\ell,2,2}\rvert=\lvert L^{1}_{\ell,2,2}\rvert=\lvert L_{\ell,2,2}\rvert=4\ell
,
\mathrm{BG}_{\ell,2,2}
is 4-cycle decomposable and indeed we will introduce three different decompositions for this graph. Suppose

Lemma 2.4

Let

Discussion

The results of this paper give rise to a natural question: can we use the method we have presented here to obtain results for cycles of other lengths or more colours? With a slight modification and a simple computer search (when feasible) we can find a suitable
{\gothfamily F}_{r}({\cal D},\widehat{{\cal D}}_{1})
to “uniquify” an exclusively alt-colourable
m
-cycle decomposition of a complete multipartite graph (
m>3
), in a similar way as we did for the case
m=4
in Section
3
.
However, the real problem is to find such an exclusively alt-colourable
m
-cycle decomposition. As one might notice, what we achieved in Section
2
relied heavily on Lemma
2.1
which gave us an equation between the number of monochromatic sets of two non-intersecting partitions.
Unfortunately, Lemma
2.1
does not naturally extend to the cases in which the number of colours and the size of the sets in each partition are not 2.
Therefore, we have begun to explore and develop other sophisticated methods of construction to find such decompositions.

In
[
3
]
, Burgess and Pike established that a 3-chromatic 4-cycle system of order
n
exists for all admissible
n\geq 49
. To this day, 49 is the smallest order for which a 3-chromatic 4-cycle system is known to exist. Curiously, our results prove the existence of uniquely 2-colourable 4-cycle systems of all admissible orders
n\geq 49
. Although we have yet to determine whether 49 is in fact the smallest order for which both problems have a solution, this coincidence cannot be overlooked.
Is there some connection in general between the smallest order of a
k
-chromatic
m
-cycle system and the smallest order of a uniquely
(k-1)
colourable
m
-cycle system?
