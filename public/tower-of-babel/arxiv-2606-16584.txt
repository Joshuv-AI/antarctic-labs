The Perron-Bremermann envelope for q q -plurisubharmonic functions on unbounded domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n}
Pawlaschyk, Thomas

Abstract
. Let D D be an unbounded domain in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} , and let f f be a bounded continuous function prescribed on the boundary of D D . We show that if D D has r r -peak points on its boundary and is of bounded type, f f extends to a maximal bounded continuous function F F on D ¯ \overline{D} that is q q -plurisubharmonic and ( n − q − 1 ) (n-q-1) -plurisuperharmonic (i.e., ( d ​ d c ​ F ) n = 0 (dd^{c}F)^{n}=0 ) on D D , and that coincides with the Perron-Bremermann envelope created with respect to bounded q q -plurisubharmonic functions on D ¯ \overline{D} .

1.
Introduction

The Dirichlet-Bremermann problem for plurisubharmonic functions is of special interest in pluripotential theory and several complex variables. It was solved by Bremermann
[
Bre59
]
for bounded strictly pseudoconvex domains with continuous boundary data using the Perron envelope. Continuity of the solution is due to Walsh
[
Wal69
]
. Bedford and Taylor
[
BT82
]
showed that it is the unique solution satisfying
(dd^{c}F)^{n}=0
. Recent developments on bounded domains can be found in
[
Nil25
]
and
[
NTT26
]
. For unbounded domains, additional assumptions are required. Simioniuc and Tomassini
[
ST08
]
presented a constructive method for obtaining a continuous solution on strictly convex and special strictly pseudoconvex domains. Nguyen and Hung
[
NH08
]
proved results on unbounded
B
-regular domains and domains of so-called
bounded type
using Jensen measures.

In this note, we study the Dirichlet-Bremermann problem for
q
-plurisubharmonic
functions in the sense of
[
HM78
]
from the viewpoint of the
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope
given by

\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}(z)=\sup\{\psi(z):\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap{\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})},\ \psi\leq f\ \text{on}\ \partial D,\ \psi\leq M\ \text{on}\ \overline{D}\},

where
D
is a possibly unbounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
f
is a is continuous function on
\partial D
, and
M
is a constant with
\sup_{\partial D}f\leq M\leq+\infty
. The aim is to find a maximal continuous
q
-plurisubharmonic extension of
f
on
\overline{D}
.

Hunt-Murray
[
HM78
]
solved the issue on bounded strictly
q
-pseudoconvex domains, extending continuous boundary data to a so-called
q
-Bremermann
function on
\overline{D}
, i.e., a function that is continuous on
\overline{D}
, and
q
-plurisubharmonic and
(n-q-1)
-plurisuperharmonic on
D
. Kalka
[
Kal79
]
presented a more general version on strictly
r
-pseudoconvex domains. Słodkowski
[
Sło84
]
developed approximation techniques for
q
-plurisubharmonic functions and deduced the uniqueness of the solution. Simioniuc and Tomassini
[
ST08
]
extended their results from plurisubharmonic functions (
q=0
) to the
q
-plurisubharmonic setting on unbounded strictly convex domains.

In the first section, we establish basic properties of
q
-Bremermann and
q
-maximal functions. In the second one, we study the
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope introduced above. In the third and final section, we present classical results on the
q
-Dirichlet-Bremermann problem and our main Theorem
4.19
. We prove that
D
admits a unique maximal continuous extension of
f
to
\overline{D}
that is
q
-Bremermann on
D
, provided that
D
has
r
-peak points (
0\leq r\leq q\leq n-r-1
) on its boundary and is of bounded type.

2.
Properties of q-Bremermann functions

We begin by listing main properties of
q
-Bremermann functions. Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We write
\mathcal{USC}(A)
for the set of upper semi-continuous functions
u:A\to[-\infty,+\infty)
, and
\mathcal{C}(A)
for the set of real-valued continuous functions on
A
. Unless stated otherwise,
D
denotes a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, possibly unbounded.
B_{r}(p)
is used for the ball centered at
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with radius
r>0
. By
U\Subset A
we mean that
\overline{U}
is a compact subset of
A
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f:A\to\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued function on an arbitrary subset
A
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. By
f^{*}(z):=\limsup_{\zeta\to z}f(\zeta)
we mean the
upper semi-continuous regularization
of
f
on
A
, and by
f_{*}(z):=\liminf_{\zeta\to z}f(\zeta)
the
lower semi-continuous regularization
of
f
on
A
. We denote by
\mathcal{USC}(A)
the set of all upper semi-continuous functions on
A
, and by
\mathcal{C}(A)
the set of all continuous functions on
A
.

Remark 2.2
.

f^{*}
is upper semi-continuous and
f_{*}
is lower semi-continuous, satisfying
f_{*}\leq f\leq f^{*}
on
A
. Furthermore,
(-f)^{*}=-f_{*}
. The function
f
is upper semi-continuous on
A
if and only if
f^{*}=f
. It is lower semi-continuous on
A
if and only if
f_{*}=f
. Therefore,
f\in\mathcal{C}(A)
if and only if
f_{*}=f=f^{*}
.

The following notion of
q
-plurisubharmonicity is in the sense of
[
HM78
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
q\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
and let
\psi:U\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be an upper semi-continuous function on an open set
U
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(1)

The function
\psi
is
q
-plurisubharmonic
on
U
if for every complex
(q+1)
-dimensional affine subspace
\Pi
, every ball
B\Subset U
and every pluriharmonic function
h
on defined on an open neighborhood of
\overline{B}
with
\psi\leq h
on
\partial B\cap\Pi
we have that
u\leq h
on
\overline{B}\cap\Pi
.

1

This type of functions was called
pseudoconvex of order
n-q
by O. Fujita
[
Fuj92
]
. Smooth
q
-plurisubharmonic functions are exactly the
weakly
(q+1)
-convex
ones in the sense of Grauert.

(2)

If
m\geq n
, every upper semi-continuous function on
U
is by convention
m
-plurisubharmonic.

(3)

\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)
donotes the family of
q
-plurisubharmonic functions on
U
.

(4)

For
q=0
, we have the classical
plurisubharmonic
functions, and the
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic functions are also called
subpluriharmonic
.

The next property follows directly from the definition of
q
-plurisubharmonicity.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a collection of
q
-plurisubharmonic functions on an open subset
U
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j}
is locally bounded, then the upper semi-continuous regularization
\Psi^{*}
of
\Psi:=\sup_{j\in J}\{\psi_{j}\}
is
q
-plurisubharmonic on
U
. If
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a decreasing sequence, then
\inf_{j}\{\psi_{j}\}=\lim_{j}\psi_{j}
is
q
-plurisubharmonic on
U
.

The maximum principle holds true for
q
-plurisubharmonic functions
[
HM78
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(Maximum principle)
.

Let
q\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
and
U\Subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then any function
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{U})
fulfills

\max\{\psi(z):z\in\overline{U}\}=\max\{\psi(z):z\in\partial U\}.

The next result is Theorem 5.1 in
[
Sło84
]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{r}(U)
. Then
\psi+\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q+r}(U)
.

The following important technique is from
[
Ngu06
]
, but was also already used in some proofs in
[
HM78
]
and
[
Sło84
]
.

Lemma 2.7

(Glueing lemma)
.

Let
W\subset U
,
w\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(W)
and
u\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)
such that
\limsup_{\zeta\to z}w(\zeta)\leq u(z)
for every
z\in\partial W\cap U
. Then

\psi(z):=\begin{cases}\max\{w(z),u(z)\},&z\in\overline{W}\\
u(z),&z\in U\setminus W\end{cases}

is
q
-plurisubharmonic on
U
.

We have the following characterization of smooth
q
-plurisubharmonic functions.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
q\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
and let
\psi
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth function on an open subset
U
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)
if and only if the complex Hessian
\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z_{k}\partial\overline{z}_{l}}(p)\right)_{k,l=1}^{n}
has at most
q
negative eigenvalues at every point
p
in
U
.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that
\psi
is
strictly
q
-plurisubharmonic
on
U
if it is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth and its complex Hessian has at most
q
non-positive eigenvalues.

Using approximation techniques from
[
Sło84
]
and
[
Bun90
]
, we obtain the subsequent characterization of
q
-plurisubharmonicity (see Corollary 3.5.5 in
[
Paw15
]
).

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
q\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and
U
be open in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)
if and only if for every open subset
V\subseteq U
and every
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{n-q-1}(V)\cap\mathcal{C}(V)
the sum
\psi+\varphi
is subpluriharmonic on
V
.

The next notion is from
[
Sło84
]
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
q\in\{0,1,\ldots,n{-}1\}
and
A
be an arbitrary set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
F:A\to\mathbb{R}
be real-valued. We say that
F
is
almost
q
-Bremermann
on
A
, if
F^{*}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(A^{\circ})
and
-F_{*}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{n-q-1}(A^{\circ})
. Here,
A^{\circ}
denotes the interior of
A
. The function
F
is called
q
-Bremermann
on
A
if
F
is continuous on
A
and almost
q
-Bremermann on
A^{\circ}
.

Remark 2.12
.

Every
q
-Bremermann function is almost
q
-Bremermann, and every continuous almost
q
-Bremermann function is
q
-Bremermann, since
F^{*}=F
and
F_{*}=F
if
F
is continuous. If
F
is
q
-Bremermann and
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth, then its complex Hessian has at least one zero eigenvalue. In this sense,
q
-Bremermann functions
F
satisfy the complex Monge-Ampère equation
(\partial\overline{\partial}F)^{n}=0
. By this reason,
q
-Bremermann functions were called
q
-complex Monge-Ampère
or
q
-CMA
in
[
HM78
]
. Therefore, every pluriharmonic function on an open subset
U
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is
q
-Bremermann on
U
for every
q\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. If
F
is
0
-Bremermann, then
F^{*}
is plurisubharmonic and
-F_{*}
is subpluriharmonic.

Example 2.13
.

Let
\|z\|
be any complex norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then
\log\|z\|
is plurisubharmonic on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
-\log\|z\|
is subpluriharmonic on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
(see
[
PZ13
]
). This means that
\log\|z\|
is
0
-Bremermann on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. As a consequece,
\log\|F\|
is
0
-Bremermann on
\{F\neq 0\}
for every holomorphic map
F:U\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
and
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
open.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If
\{F_{j}\}_{j}
is a locally bounded increasing sequence of
q
-Bremermann functions on
\overline{D}
, then
F:=\sup_{j}F_{j}
is almost
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
. If
\{G_{j}\}_{j}
is a locally bounded decreasing sequence of
q
-Bremermann functions on
\overline{D}
, then
G:=\inf_{j}G_{j}
is almost
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
,
F^{*}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)
. Since
F_{j}
’s are continuous,
F
is lower semi-continuous on
D
, so
-F
is upper semi-continuous. Moreover,
-F=\inf_{j}\{-F_{j}\}
, so
-F
is the limit of a decreasing sequence of functions
-F_{j}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{n-q-1}(D)
. Again, by Proposition
2.4
,
-F=-F_{*}
is also in
\mathcal{PSH}_{n-q-1}(D)
.

Now notice that, in general, a function
H
is (almost)
q
-Bremermann if and only if
-H
is (almost)
(n-q-1)
-Bremermann. Then
\{-G_{j}\}_{j}
is an increasing sequence of
(n-q-1)
-Bremermann functions. Thus, by the first part,
-G=\sup_{j}\{-G_{j}\}
is almost
(n-q-1)
-Bremermann on
D
, so that
G
is
q
-Bremermann on
D
.
∎

The next result can be found in
[
Sło84
]
.

Proposition 2.15

(Uniqueness property)
.

Let
D
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and let
F,G
be two almost
q
-Bremermann functions on
\overline{D}
such that
F,G\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
and
F=G
on
\partial D
. Then
F=G
on
\overline{D}
.

Proof.

By Thoerem
2.6
and since
F=F_{*}=F^{*}
and
G=G_{*}=G^{*}
on
\partial D
,
F^{*}-G_{*}
and
G^{*}-F_{*}
are
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic on
D
such that
F^{*}-G_{*}=0
and
G^{*}-F_{*}=0
on
\partial D
. Since
F=G
on
\partial D
, we can apply the maximum principle (Theorem
2.6
) in order to obtain
F\leq F^{*}\leq G_{*}\leq G
and
G\leq G^{*}\leq F_{*}\leq F
on
\overline{D}
.
∎

This motivates to study the maximal property of
q
-Bremermann functions in the subsequent sense.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
F:\overline{D}\to\mathbb{R}
a real-valued function. We say that
F
is
q
-maximal on
\overline{D}
if
F^{*}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})
and if for every
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})
with
\psi\leq F_{*}
on
\partial D
we have that
\psi\leq F_{*}
on
\overline{D}
. We say that
F
is
q
-maximal on (bounded) open subsets of
U
if it is
q
-maximal on
\overline{U}
for every (bounded) open subset
U
of
D
.

Remark 2.17
.

If
F
is
q
-maximal on open subsets of
D
, then it is
q
-maximal on
\overline{D}
and on bounded open subsets of
D
. If
D
is bounded,
q
-maximality on open sets and
q
-maximality on bounded open sets are the same.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
F\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
. Then
F
is
q
-maximal on
\overline{D}
if and only if it is
q
-maximal on open subsets of
D
.

Proof.

Let
F
be
q
-maximal on
\overline{D}
and let
U
be an open subset of
D
. We pick a function
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{U})
with
\psi\leq F
on
\partial U
. Then by the Glueing Lemma
2.7
,

\Psi:=\begin{cases}\max\{\psi,F\},&\text{on}\ \overline{U}\\
F,&\text{on}\ \overline{D}\setminus U\end{cases}

is in
\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{U})
and fulfills
\Psi\leq F
on
\partial D
. By the
q
-maximality of
F
, it follows that
\Psi\leq F
on
\overline{D}
, so that
\psi\leq\Psi\leq F
on
U
.
∎

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
F:\overline{D}\to\mathbb{R}
be an almost
q
-Bremermann function on
\overline{D}
. Then
F
is
q
-maximal on bounded open subsets of
D
.

Proof.

Let
U
be a bounded open set in
D
and
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{U})
with
\psi\leq F_{*}
on
\partial U
. Then
\psi-F_{*}
is
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic due to Theorem
2.6
, and
\psi-F_{*}\leq 0
on
\partial U
. The maximum principle yields
\psi-F_{*}\leq 0
on
U
.
∎

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
F\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
. Then
F
is
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
if and only
F
is
q
-maximal on bounded open subsets of
D
.

Proof.

Recall that
F^{*}=F=F_{*}
if
F
is continuous. Then the first direction follows immediately from Proposition
2.19
.

Now assume that
F
is
q
-maximal on
\overline{D}
. By the definition of
q
-maximality, we already have
F\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)
, so that it only remains to show that
-F\in\mathcal{PSH}_{n-q-1}(D)
. Assume that this is not the case. According to Lemma
2.10
, there exist a ball
B\Subset D
and a continuous
q
-plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
defined on an open neighbourhood of
\overline{B}
such that
-F+\varphi<0
on
\partial B
and
-F(p)+\varphi(p)>0
for some
p\in B
. This means that
\varphi<F
on
\partial B
and
\varphi(p)>F(p)
. Now define

\Phi:=\begin{cases}\max\{F,\varphi\},&\text{on}\ \overline{B}\\
F,&\text{on}\ \overline{D}\setminus B\end{cases}

By the Glueing Lemma
2.7
, the function
\Phi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
satisfying
\Phi=F
on
\partial D
and
\Phi(p)=\varphi(p)>F(p)
. This contradicts the
q
-maximality of
F
on
\overline{D}
. The proof is finished due to Proposition
2.18
.
∎

3.
Properties of the q-Perron-Bremermann envelopes

The solution of the Dirichlet-Bremermann problem for plurisubharmonic functions uses the Perron-Bremermann envelope which easily extends to
q
-plurisubharmonic functions. Notice that, in this section,
M\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, unless otherwise stated.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
. Fix a constant
M
with
\sup_{\partial D}f\leq M\leq+\infty
. Then the
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope
on
\overline{D}
is defined at each point
z
of
\overline{D}
by

\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}(z):=\sup\{\psi(z):\ \psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{D}),\ \psi\leq f\ \text{on}\ \partial D,\ \psi\leq M\ \text{on}\ \overline{D}\}.

If
M=+\infty
, we simply write
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
rather than
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,+\infty}
because in this case, the condition
\psi\leq M
is superfluous.

Remark 3.2
.

It is obvious that
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\leq M
and
\sup_{M<+\infty}\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\leq\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
. Notice that
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\leq f
on
\partial D
, but the upper semi-continuous regularization
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}^{*}
might jump at the boundary and exceed
f
there. This means that we cannot conclude immediately that
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}^{*}=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
. If the latter identity holds true,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
is continuous on
\overline{D}
.

If
D
is bounded, by the maximum principle, the assumption
\psi\leq f\leq M
on
\partial D
implies
\psi\leq M
on
\overline{D}
. In this case, for any
M\geq\sup_{\partial D}f
, we have,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}\leq\sup_{\partial D}f
.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
is lower semi-continuous, it is
q
-maximal with respect to
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
such that
\psi\leq f
on
\partial D
and
\psi\leq M
on
\overline{D}
.

Remark 3.3
.

In the literature, the definition of the
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope (
q=0
) is slightly different. E.g., in
[
NH08
]
or
[
NTT26
]
(for
q=0
) the following is used:

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}(z):=\sup\{\psi(z):\ \psi\in\mathcal{PSH}(D),\ \psi^{\star}\leq f\ \text{on}\ \partial D\},

where
\psi^{\star}(z):=\limsup_{\zeta\to z}\psi(\zeta)
for
z\in\overline{D}
. It is clear that it coincides with the definition in
[
HM78
]
,

\sup\{\psi(z):\ \psi\in\mathcal{PSH}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D}),\ \psi\leq f\ \text{on}\ \partial D\}.

The reason why we chose our definition is, that in contrary to
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
, the function
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}
need not to be lower semi-continuous on
D
, which we use in several arguments. In general, we have
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}
. We will discuss conditions on their equality later.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope
\mathcal{P}:=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
is lower semi-continuous with
\mathcal{P}\leq f
on
\partial D
, and it is almost
q
-Bremermann outside
E_{\infty}:=\{z\in\overline{D}:\mathcal{P}(z)=+\infty\}
in
\overline{D}
. Moreover, it is continuous and
q
-Bremermann outside
E
in
\overline{D}
, where
E:=E_{\infty}\cup E_{d}
and
E_{d}:=\{z\in\overline{D}:\mathcal{P}(z)<\mathcal{P}^{*}(z)\}
. Hence,
\mathcal{P}
is
q
-maximal on bounded open subsets of
D\setminus E
. Since
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
, we have
E\cap\partial D=\emptyset
. If
M<+\infty
or if
D
is bounded, then
E_{\infty}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

\mathcal{P}
is the supremum of continuous functions on
\overline{D}
, so it is lower semi-continuous, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}_{*}=\mathcal{P}
, and
\mathcal{P}^{*}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}((D\setminus E_{\infty})^{\circ})
. It remains to show that
-\mathcal{P}
is
(n-q-1)
-plurisubharmonic on
(D\setminus E_{\infty})^{\circ}
. Assume that this is not the case. Then by Lemma
2.10
there exist a ball
B\Subset D\setminus E_{\infty}
and a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(B)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{B})
such that
-\mathcal{P}+\varphi<0
on
\partial B
, but
-\mathcal{P}(p)+\varphi(p)>0
for some point
p\in B
. In other words,
\varphi<\mathcal{P}\leq M
on
\partial B
and
\mathcal{P}(p)<\varphi(p)
. By the maximum principle,
\varphi(p)\leq M
. By the continuity of
\varphi
, the lower semi-continuity of
\mathcal{P}
and the definition of the
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope, there exists a
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
such that
\mathcal{P}\geq\psi>\varphi
on
\partial B
,
\psi(p)\leq\mathcal{P}(p)<\varphi(p)
and
\psi\leq f
on
\partial D
. Now define

\Psi:=\begin{cases}\max\{\psi,\varphi\},&\text{on}\ \overline{B}\\
\psi,&\text{on}\ \overline{D}\setminus B\end{cases}.

By the Glueing Lemma
2.7
,
\Psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
,
\Psi=\psi\leq f
on
\partial D
,
\Psi(p)=\varphi(p)>\mathcal{P}(p)
and
\Psi\leq M
on
\overline{D}
. By the definition of the
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope, we obtain
\Psi\leq\mathcal{P}
on
\overline{D}
. In particular,
\Psi(p)\leq\mathcal{P}(p)
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
-\mathcal{P}
is
(n-q-1)
-plurisubharmonic on
(D\setminus E_{\infty})^{\circ}
. The rest is obvious, since
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{*}
and
E_{d}
is the set of discontinuity of
\mathcal{P}
on
\overline{D}
.
∎

Example 3.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and define

s_{n}(z):=n(|\exp(\exp(z))|-1)=n(e^{e^{x}\cos(y)}-1),

on
D:=\mathbb{R}\times(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\subset\mathbb{C}
. Then
s_{n}=0
on
\partial D
and
s_{n}>0
on
D
. Let
f:=0
on
\partial D
. Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
s_{n}\leq\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}=+\infty
on
D
, and
E_{\infty}=D
. By the Riemann mapping theorem and the maximum principle,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}=0
on
\overline{D}
for any
M\geq 0
. Hence,
\sup_{M<+\infty}\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}<\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
on
D
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(1)

If there exists a function
F
on
\overline{D}
such that
F\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
and
F
is
q
-maximal with respect to
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})
with
\psi\leq f
on
\partial D
and
\psi\leq M
on
\overline{D}
, where
f:=F|_{\partial D}
and
M\geq\sup_{\partial D}f
, then
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}=F
is continuous on
\overline{D}
.

(2)

\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
is
q
-maximal on bounded open subsets of
D
.

Proof.

(1) Obviously,
F\leq\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
on
\overline{D}
. On the other hand, for every
\psi
as above in (1), by
q
-maximality of
F
, we have
\psi\leq F
on
\overline{D}
. Thus,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\leq F
on
\overline{D}
, and therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}=F
on
\overline{D}
.

(2) Since
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
, it is
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
, and therefore
q
-maximal on bounded open subsets of
D
due to Proposition
2.20
.
∎

4.
Solution of the q-Dirichlet-Bremermann problem

The
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope attains the prescribed values at the boundary when the domain permits peak points at its boundary.

Definition 4.1
.

An open set
U
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
has a
q
-peak point
at a boundary point
p\in\partial U
if there is a
q
-peak function

\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(U)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{U})
such that
\psi(p)=0
and
\psi<0
on
\overline{U}\setminus\{p\}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Every
local

q
-peak point for
U
is a
q
-peak point for
U
. Indeed, assume that there is an open neighbourhood
V
of
p
such that
U\cap V
has a
q
-peak point at
p
. Take an open ball
B\Subset V
centered at
p
and let
C:=\max_{\partial B}\psi
. Then
\varphi:=\max\{\psi,a\}
extends by the constant
C<a<0
from
\overline{U}\cap\overline{B}
to a
q
-plurisubharmonic function on the whole of
U
that is upper semi-continuous on
\overline{U}
and peaks at
p
.

The next lemma clarifies the relevance of
q
-peak points for the
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p\in\partial D
be a
q
-peak point for the domain
D
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
q
-peak function
\psi
, and let
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
with
f\geq 0
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}(p)=f(p)
for every
M\geq\sup_{\partial D}f
.

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is a radius
r>0
such that
\Psi_{a,\varepsilon}:=a\psi+f(p)-\varepsilon<f
on the closure of the ball
B=B_{r}(p)
in
\partial D
for all
a\geq 1
. If
a\geq 1
is large enough,
\Psi_{a,\varepsilon}<0
on
\partial B\cap\overline{D}
. By the Glueing Lemma
2.7
,
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a,\varepsilon}:=\max\{0,\Psi_{a,\varepsilon}\}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
such that
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a,\varepsilon}\leq f
on
\partial D
and
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a,\varepsilon}\leq\sup_{\partial D}f\leq M
on
\overline{D}
. Therefore,
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a,\varepsilon}(p)=f(p)-\varepsilon\leq\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}(p)\leq f(p)
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was chosen arbitrarily, we conclude
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}(p)=f(p)
.
∎

Domains that admit peak points at its boundary are given by the following important class of sets.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that a domain
D
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is
strictly (Levi)
q
-pseudoconvex
at
p\in\partial D
if there is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth strictly
q
-plurisubharmonic function
\psi
on an open neighbourhood
U
of
p
such that
D\cap U=\{z\in U:\psi<0\}
and
d\psi(p)\neq 0
.

Remark 4.5
.

The classical strict pseudoconvexity is strict
0
-pseudoconvexity in our sense. This means that the result in Theorem
4.6
always holds true in the case of
r=0
for any
q\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. If
D
is strictly Levi
q
-pseudoconvex at
p\in\partial D
, then it has a (local)
q
-peak point at
p
. In view of Definition
4.4
we simply can take
\varphi:=\psi-\varepsilon|z-p|^{2}
as a local peak function for
D
at
p
, for
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

The following theorem is due to
[
HM78
]
in the case
r=q
and in its general version to
[
Kal79
]
. Uniqueness of the solution has been shown in
[
Sło84
]
. Notice also the example and the comment before Theorem 3.7 in
[
Bun90
]
on the extra condition
r\leq q\leq n-r-1
. In
[
Ngu06
]
, it was pointed out that
r
-peak points (or
B_{q}
-regular points) at the boundary of the given domain are sufficient to get a solution of the
q
-Dirichlet-Bremermann problem.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and let
D
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that admits an
r
-peak point at each of its boundary points. Then for
r\leq q\leq n-r-1
and
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
, the
q
-Perron-Bremermann envelope
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}
is continuous on
\overline{D}
. Moreover,
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}
is the unique
q
-Bremermann function on
D
such that
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}=f
on
\partial D
. Therefore, it is
q
-maximal on open subsets of
D
.

Remark 4.7
.

It follows from the definition that
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}
. Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}
is continuous on
\overline{D}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}=f
on
\partial D
, we also obtain
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}\leq\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D}
under the assumptions made in Theorem
4.6
.

Corollary 4.8
.

If
D
is a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
F\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
.
F
is
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
if and only if
F=\mathcal{P}_{F|_{\partial B},q,B}
for every ball
B\subseteq D
.

Proof.

One direction follows from the uniqueness of
q
-Bremermann functions (Proposition
2.15
). If
F=\mathcal{P}_{F|_{\partial B},q,B}
on
B
for every ball
B
in
D
, then
F
is locally
q
-Bremermann. Since
q
-plurisubharmonicity is a local property, the proof is finished.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

The boundedness of
D
guarantees that
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}\leq\max_{\partial D}f<+\infty
on
\overline{D}
by the maximum principle, and Walsh’s technique
[
Wal69
]
can then be applied to establish the continuity of
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
on
\overline{D}
. For an unbounded domain
D
, however, it is not immediate that
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
is bounded, and even when boundedness does hold, continuity remains the most delicate issue. As we shall see later, this issue plays a central role in our main result, Theorem
4.19
.

Consider also the following Example 8.2 from
[
ST99
]
on the boundedness of the boundary data.

Example 4.10
.

Consider the domain
D=\{(z,u+iv)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:v>|z|^{2}+u^{2}\}
. It is strictly convex in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and there exists a function
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
with
f\leq 0
such that for each
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}(\overline{D})
with
\psi\leq f
on
\partial D
we have
\psi\equiv-\infty
on
D
. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{f,{q=0},D}=-\infty
on
D
.

In virtue of the previous example, from now on, we only consider continuous boundary values that are bounded below. The next result is Theorem 24 and Theorem 25 in
[
ST08
]
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
D
be an unbounded strictly convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
with
f\geq 0
. Then there exists a function
\Phi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})
such that
\Phi\geq 0
,
\Phi|_{\partial D}=f
and
\Phi\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
. Moreover,
\Phi
is
q
-maximal on
\overline{D}
. If
f
is bounded,
\Phi
is continuous and
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
.

Remark 4.12
.

It follows from Proposition
3.6
that
\Phi=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D}
on
\overline{D}
, and from Proposition
3.4
that
\Phi
is almost
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
.

Now we construct a continuous
q
-plurisubharmonic extension out of given continuous boundary data (see also
[
HST21
]
).

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that admits an
r
-peak point at each of its boundary points. Fix
r\leq q\leq n-r-1
. Then, for every
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
with
f\geq 0
, there exists
F\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
such that
F|_{\partial D}=f
,
F\geq 0
and
\sup_{\overline{D}}F=\sup_{\partial D}f
.

Proof.

Let
\{B_{i}\}_{i}
,
\{B_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i}
and
\{B_{i}^{\prime\prime}\}_{i}
be locally finite coverings of
\partial D
by balls centered in
p_{i}\in\partial D
with
B_{i}\Subset B_{i}^{\prime}\Subset B_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. Let
D_{i}:=B^{\prime\prime}_{i}\cap D
. For each
i
, we pick a continuous function
f_{i}
on
\overline{D_{i}}
such that
f_{i}=f
on
\partial D\cap\overline{B}_{i}
,
f_{i}<0
on
\partial D_{i}\setminus\overline{B_{i}^{\prime}}
,
f_{i}\leq f
on
\partial D\cap B_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. Then
\sup_{\overline{D_{i}}}f_{i}=\sup_{\overline{D_{i}}}f
. Since all boundary points of
D_{i}
are
r
-peak points, it follows from Theorem
4.6
that for each
f_{i}
there is a
q
-Bremermann function
F_{i}
on
\overline{D_{i}}
such that
F_{i}=f_{i}
on
\partial D_{i}
and
\sup_{\overline{D_{i}}}F_{i}=\sup_{\overline{D_{i}}}f
. We set
F_{i}^{\prime}:=\max\{F_{i},0\}
. Then
F_{i}^{\prime}
is
q
-plurisubharmonic on
D_{i}
and continuous on
\overline{D_{i}}
, and
F_{i}^{\prime}<0
near
\partial D_{i}\setminus B_{i}^{\prime}
. Therefore, the function
F_{i}^{\prime\prime}:\overline{D}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
F_{i}^{\prime\prime}:=F_{i}^{\prime}
on
\overline{D_{i}}
and
F_{i}^{\prime\prime}:=0
on
\overline{D}\setminus B_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is continuous on
\overline{D}
and
q
-plurisubharmonic on
D
. Since
\{B_{i}\}_{i}
is a locally finite covering of
\partial D
, the function
F:=\sup_{i}\{F_{i}^{\prime\prime}\}=\max_{i}\{F_{i}^{\prime\prime}\}
is
q
-plurisubharmonic on
D
such that
F=f_{i}=f
on
\partial D\cap B_{i}
. It is evident that

\sup_{\overline{D}}F\leq\sup_{i}\sup_{\overline{D_{i}}}F_{i}^{\prime\prime}\leq\sup_{i}\sup_{\overline{D_{i}}}F_{i}\leq\sup_{\overline{D}}f.

∎

Remark 4.14
.

Observe that
F=0
on
D\setminus\bigcup_{i}B_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. Since the covering
\{B_{i}^{\prime\prime}\}_{i}
can be chosen arbitrarily small, we can construct
F
to vanish on any closed subset
A\subset D
with
A\cap\partial D=\emptyset
.

The next notion can be found in
[
NH08
]
.

Definition 4.15
.

An unbounded domain
D
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is of
bounded type
if there exist
\Psi\in\mathcal{PSH}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(D)
such that
\Psi<0
on
D
and
\Psi(z)\to-\infty
as
|z|\to+\infty
, i.e., for every
C\geq 0
there exists
R>0
such that
\Psi<-C
on
D\setminus B_{R}(0)
.

Example 4.16
.

If a domain
D
contains a copy of
\mathbb{C}
, then it is not of bounded type by the Liouville property for plurisubharmonic functions. For example, the subsequent domain
D
is strictly pseudoconvex, but not of bounded type, since it contains
\mathbb{C}\times\{0\}
,

\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\log|w|+|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}<1\}.

If a domain
D
is contained in
\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|p(z)|^{2}>(1+|z|^{2})^{\deg p}\}
for a complex polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
, then
D
is of bounded type (see
[
NH08
]
).

The maximum principle holds true on unbounded domains of bounded type.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
q\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
and let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of bounded type. Then any bounded above function
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})
satisfies

\sup\{\varphi(z):z\in\overline{D}\}=\sup\{\varphi(z):z\in\partial D\}.

Proof.

Assume that the assumption is false, and that there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})
such that
m:=\sup_{\partial D}\varphi<M:=\sup_{\overline{D}}\varphi<+\infty
. Let
\Psi
be from Definition
4.15
, and
\widehat{\Psi}
its continuous extension to
\overline{D}
. We fix
\varepsilon>0
and define
\Phi_{\varepsilon}:=\varphi+\varepsilon\widehat{\Psi}
. Let
R_{0}>0
be so large that
\varepsilon\widehat{\Psi}<m-M
on
\overline{D}\setminus B_{R_{0}}(0)
for every
R\geq R_{0}
. Then
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\leq m
on
\overline{D}\setminus B_{R}(0)
, and by the maxmum principle on bounded domains,
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\leq m
on
\overline{D}\cap\overline{B_{R}(0)}
for every
R\geq R_{0}
. Therefore,
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\leq m
on
\overline{D}
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrarily chosen,
\varphi\leq m
on
\overline{D}
. But this contradicts to
M\leq\sup_{\overline{D}}\varphi\leq m
.
∎

Remark 4.18
.

If
D
is of bounded type and
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
is bounded above, then
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M_{0}}
for every
M
with
M_{0}:=\sup_{\partial D}f\leq M<+\infty
.

We present our main result on the solution of the
q
-Dirichlet-Bremermann problem on unbounded domains of bounded type.

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
r
,
q
,
D
be as in Theorem
4.6
, except that
D
is unbounded. Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(\partial D)
with
0\leq f\leq\sup_{\partial D}f\leq M
for some upper bound
M<+\infty
. Then there exists an almost
q
-Bremermann function
\Phi=\Phi_{M}
on
\overline{D}
such that

(1)

\Phi=f
on
\partial D
, and
0\leq\Phi\leq M
on
\overline{D}
.

(2)

\Phi
is
q
-maximal with respect to
q
-plurisubharmonic functions
\psi
with
\psi\leq f
on
\partial D
and
\psi\leq M
on
\overline{D}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\leq\Phi
.

(3)

\Phi
is continuous on
\partial D
.

(4)

If
D
is of bounded type,
\Phi
is continuous and
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
. Thus,
\Phi=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M_{0}}
for
M_{0}:=\sup_{\partial D}f
.

(5)

If
D
is of bounded type,
\Phi
is the unique
q
-Bremermann function on
\overline{D}
with
\Phi=f
on
\partial D
.

Proof.

Claim 1:
Existence of an upper semi-continuous almost
q
-Bremermann
\Phi
with
\Phi=f
on
\partial D
and
0\leq\Phi\leq M
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define
D_{j}:=D\cap B_{j}(0)
,
E_{j}:=\partial B_{j}(0)\cap\overline{D}
and
A_{j}:=\partial D\cap\overline{B_{j}(0)}
. We may assume that
j
is so large that
D_{0}
is not empty.

For
j\geq 1
, let
\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{D_{j}})
such that
0\leq f\leq\varphi_{j}\leq M
on
\partial D_{j}\cap\partial D
,
\varphi_{j}=f
on
\partial D_{j-1}\cap\partial D
and
\varphi_{j}=M
on
E_{j}
. Let
\Phi_{j}
be the solution of the
q
-Dirichlet-Bremermann problem with boundary data
\varphi_{j}
on
D_{j}
. Then by Theorem
4.6
,
\Phi_{j}=\mathcal{P}_{\varphi_{j},q,D_{j}}
is
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D_{j}}
,
\Phi_{j}=\varphi_{j}
on
\partial D_{j}
,
q
-maximal on open subsets of
D_{j}
, and fulfills
0\leq\Phi_{j}\leq M
on
\overline{D_{j}}
.

Since
\Phi_{j+1}=f
on
\partial D_{j}\cap\partial D
and
\Phi_{j+1}\leq M
on
D_{j}
, we have that
\Phi_{j+1}\leq\varphi_{j}
on
\partial D_{j}
. Since
\Phi_{j+1}
is
q
-plurisubharmonic on
D_{j}
and
\Phi_{j}
is
q
-maximal on
\overline{D_{j}}
, we have that
\Phi_{j+1}\leq\Phi_{j}
on
\overline{D_{j}}
. Hence, for each
j_{0}
, we obtain a locally bounded, decreasing sequence of
q
-Bremermann functions
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{j\geq j_{0}}
on
\overline{D_{j_{0}}}
such that
\Phi_{j}=f
on
\partial D\cap\partial D_{j-1}
. Since
\{D_{j}\}_{j}
is an increasing sequence of domains such that
D=\bigcup_{j}D_{j}
, the function
\Phi:=\inf_{j}\Phi_{j}
is well-defined on the whole of
\overline{D}
. Furthermore, it fulfills
\Phi=f
on
\partial D
and
0\leq\Phi\leq M
on
\overline{D_{j}}
for each
j
, so
\Phi\leq M
on
\overline{D}
. By Proposition
2.4
,
\Phi
is almost
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
, and by the continuity of
\Phi_{j}
’s,
\Phi\in\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})
.

Claim 2:

\Phi
is
q
-maximal with respect to
q
-plurisubharmonic functions
\psi
with
\psi\leq f
on
\partial D
and
\psi\leq M
on
\overline{D}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\leq\Phi
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})
such that
\psi\leq f
on
\partial D
and
\psi\leq M
on
D
. Define
\psi_{j}:=\psi|_{\overline{D_{j}}}
. Then, by the construction of
\varphi_{j}
and
\Phi_{j}
, we have
\psi_{j}\leq\varphi_{j}=\Phi_{j}
on
\partial D_{j}
for each
j
. Since
\Phi_{j}
is
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D_{j}}
, we conclude that
\psi=\psi_{j}\leq\Phi_{j}
on
\overline{D_{j}}
for every
j
, so that
\psi\leq\Phi
on
\overline{D}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\leq\Phi
on
\overline{D}
.

Claim 3:

\Phi
is continuous on
\partial D
.

We only need to show that
\Phi
is lower semi-continuous on
\partial D
, since it is already upper semi-continuous on
\overline{D}
. By Lemma
4.13
, there exists a function
F\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{D})
such that
F=f
on
\partial D
and
0\leq F\leq\sup_{\overline{D}}F=\sup_{\partial D}f\leq M
. By
q
-maximality of
\Phi
, we have
F\leq\Phi
on
\overline{D}
. Thus, if
p\in\partial D
, then

\liminf_{z\to p}\Phi(z)\geq\liminf_{z\to p}F(z)=\lim_{z\to p}F(z)=F(p)=f(p)=\Phi(p)

Claim 4:
If
D
is of bounded type,
\Phi
is continuous and
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
, and
\Phi\leq\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
.

We use similar arguments as in the proof of Lemma 2 in
[
ST08
]
. It suffices to show that
\Phi
is lower semi-continuous on
D
. Since
D
is of bounded type, there is a continuous negative plurisubharmonic function
\Psi
on
D
such that
\Psi(z)\to-\infty
as
|z|\to+\infty
.

We fix a point
p_{0}\in D
, an upper bound
M\in[\sup_{\partial D}f,+\infty)
and an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. We choose a constant
a>0
so small that
-\varepsilon/2<a\Psi(p_{0})
. We replace
\Psi
by
a\Psi
, so that we can assume
-\varepsilon/2<\Psi(p_{0})
. Since
\Psi
is continuous on
D
, we obtain that
-\varepsilon/2<\Psi
on a ball
B_{\rho}(p_{0})\Subset D
for some
\rho>0
.

Let
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
be so large that
B_{\rho}(p_{0})\Subset B_{r_{1}}(0)
and
\Psi+M<0
on
\overline{D}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)
. We set
B^{\prime}:=B_{r_{1}}(0)
,
B^{\prime\prime}:=B_{r_{2}}(0)
and
D^{\prime\prime}:=D\setminus\overline{B^{\prime\prime}}
.
Since
\Phi
is continuous on
\partial D
, it is uniformly continuous on the compact set
\partial D\cap\overline{B^{\prime\prime}}
. Hence, there is a
\delta^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for every
p\in\partial D\cap\overline{B^{\prime\prime}}
and
z\in\overline{D}\cap\overline{B^{\prime\prime}}
with
|p-z|<2\delta^{\prime\prime}
we have

|\Phi(p)-\Phi(z)|<\varepsilon/4.

Let
D_{1}:=\{z\in D\cap B^{\prime\prime}:d(z,\partial D))\geq\delta/3\}
and
D_{2}:=\{z\in D\cap B^{\prime\prime}:d(z,\partial D))<\delta/3\}
.

We fix a positive constant
\delta<\min\{\rho,r_{2}-r_{1},\delta^{\prime\prime}\}
. For
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
|w|<\delta/3
we define

\widetilde{\Phi}(z):=\begin{cases}\max\{\Phi(z),\Phi(z+w)+\Psi(z)-\varepsilon/2\},&z\in D_{1}\\
\Phi(z)&z\in D_{2}\cup(\overline{D}\setminus B^{\prime})\end{cases}

It is clear that, if
z\in D_{1}
, then
z+w\in\overline{D}
. Now let
z\in D\cap B^{\prime}
such that
d(z,\partial D)=\delta/3
. Then there exists a
p\in\partial D
such that
z\in B_{\delta}(p)
. Then
z+w\in B_{2\delta}(p)
, so that
|\Phi(p)-\Phi(z)|<\varepsilon/2
and
|\Phi(p)-\Phi(z+w)|<\varepsilon/2
due to uniform continuity of
\Phi
on
\partial D
discussed above. Therefore, since
\Psi<0
on
D
,

\Phi(z)>\Phi(z+w)-\varepsilon/2>\Phi(z+w)+\Psi(z)-\varepsilon/2.

Thus,
\widetilde{\Phi}
is
q
-plurisubharmonic on
D\cap B^{\prime\prime}
by the Glueing Lemma
2.7
.

If
z\in\partial B^{\prime\prime}\cap\overline{D}
with
d(z,\partial D)\geq\delta/3
, then
z+w\in\overline{D}\setminus B^{\prime}
and therefore,

\Phi(z+w)+\Psi(z)-\varepsilon/2\leq M+\Psi(z)<0\leq\Phi(z),

so
\widetilde{\Phi}
is
q
-plurisubharmonic on
D
and upper semi-continuous on
\overline{D}
again by the Glueing Lemma
2.7
. It is easy to see that
0\leq\widetilde{\Phi}\leq M
and
\widetilde{\Phi}=\Phi=f
on
\partial D
. By the
q
-maximality of
\Phi
on
\overline{D}
(Claim 2), we have
\widetilde{\Phi}\leq\Phi
on
\overline{D}
. Recall that we fixed a point
p_{0}\in D
such that
B_{\rho}(p_{0})\Subset B^{\prime}\cap D
, and
-\varepsilon/2<\Psi
on
B_{\rho}(p_{0})
. Hence,
d(p_{0},\partial D)>\rho>\delta
. Then
z=p_{0}-w\in D_{1}
for
|w|<\delta/3
, so that

\Phi(p_{0})-\varepsilon=\Phi(z+w)-\varepsilon<\Phi(z+w)+\Psi(z)-\varepsilon/2\leq\widetilde{\Phi}(z)\leq\Phi(z).

This means that
\Phi
is lower semi-continuous at
p_{0}
. Since
p_{0}
was an arbitrary point in
D
, we conclude that
\Phi
is lower semi-continuous on the whole of
\overline{D}
. Since we already deduced that
\Phi
is upper semi-continuous on
\overline{D}
, we finally get that
\Phi
is continuous on the whole of
\overline{D}
. Since
\Phi
is almost
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
, it is
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
. Moreover, also by the continuity and the properties of
\Phi
, we have
\Phi\leq\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
. It follows from the maximum principle of domains of bounded type that
\Phi=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M_{0}}
for every
M\geq M_{0}:=\sup_{\partial D}f
(see Remark
4.18
).

Claim 5:

\Phi=\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M_{0}}
is the unique
q
-Bremermann function on
\overline{D}
that equals
f
on
\partial D
.

Assume that another
q
-Bremermann
F
exists on
\overline{D}
with
F=f
on
\partial D
. It is clear that
F\leq\Phi
on
\overline{D}
by the maximum principle on domains of bounded type and the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M_{0}}
. Recall that
F
is
q
-maximal on bounded open subsets of
D
, since it is
q
-Bremermann on
\overline{D}
(see Proposition
2.20
).

For arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
define
\Phi_{\varepsilon}:=\Phi+\varepsilon\widehat{\Psi}
(where
\Psi
is from Claim 4 and
\widehat{\Psi}
is the continuous extension of
\Psi
into the whole of
\overline{D}
). If the ball
B=B_{r_{0}}(0)
is large enough (
r_{0}>1/\varepsilon
), then
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\leq F
on
\overline{D}\setminus B
, so
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\leq F
on
\partial(D\cap B_{r}(0))
for every
r\geq r_{0}
. By the
q
-maximality of
F
on bounded open sets, we deduce
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\leq F
on
\overline{D}
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was chosen to be arbitrary, we obtain
\Phi\leq F
on
\overline{D}
.
∎

Remark 4.20
.

In view of Remark
3.3
, for
M\geq\sup_{\partial D}f
and
z\in D
, we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D,M}(z):=\sup\{\psi(z):\ \psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D}),\ \psi\leq f\ \text{on}\ \partial D,\ \psi\leq M\ \text{on}\ \overline{D}\}.

Then we can replace
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D,M}
in Claim 2 in order to get
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D,M}\leq\Phi
. If
D
is of bounded type, we get
\Phi=\mathcal{P}_{q,f,D,M}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D,M}\leq\Phi
, so that
\mathcal{P}_{q,f,D,M}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{f,q,D,M}=\Phi
in this case.

Remark 4.21
.

The case
M=+\infty
appears to be more delicate. This case was studied in
[
NH08
]
and
[
ST08
]
. The discussion preceding Theorem 23 of the latter paper suggests that the
unbounded plurisubharmonic hull

\overline{D}^{\wedge}
of the closure
\overline{D}
of the domain
D
may be relevant. It is defined by

\overline{D}^{\wedge}:=\bigcup_{r>0}\big(\overline{D\cap B_{r}(0)}\big)^{\wedge}
, where
K^{\wedge}
denotes plurisubharmonic hull of a compact set
K\subset\overline{D}
. This motivates to define the
q
-hull
of
K
in
\overline{D}
via

K^{\wedge}_{q}:=\{z\in\overline{D}:\psi(z)\leq\max_{K}\psi\ \text{for\ all}\ \psi\in\mathcal{PSH}_{q}(D)\cap\mathcal{USC}(\overline{D})\}.

Then
K^{\wedge}_{0}=K^{\wedge}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}\leq\max_{\partial D\cap\overline{B_{r}(0)}}f\leq M
holds true at least on the
unbounded
q
-hull

\overline{D}^{\wedge}_{q}:=\bigcup_{r>0}\big(\overline{D\cap B_{r}(0)}\big)^{\wedge}_{q}
for every sufficiently large
r>0
such that
D\cap B_{r}(0)\neq\emptyset
. More precisely,
\overline{D}^{\wedge}_{q}\subseteq\overline{D}\setminus E_{\infty}
(see Proposition
3.4
). In general, we have
\overline{D}^{\wedge}_{q}\subsetneq\overline{D}
, but it is not immediate that
\mathcal{P}_{f,q,D,M}
is continuous on
\overline{D}^{\wedge}_{q}
, as pointed out in
[
ST08
]
in the case
q=0
.
