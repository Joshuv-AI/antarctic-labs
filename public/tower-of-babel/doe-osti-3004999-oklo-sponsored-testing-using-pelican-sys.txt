Nature of Work
Describe the research (summary of Scope of Work and principal objectives of the CRADA):
Argonne National Laboratory (the Contractor), located in Lemont IL, and Oklo, Inc., (the Participant), headquartered
in Santa Clara, CA, propose to enter into a Cooperative Research and Development Agreement (CRADA) to perform
a gap analysis of thermal hydraulic data, perform prototypical fuel assembly pressure drop and cavitation model
validation, generate the experimental data as well as the corresponding uncertainties for this matrix and, finally,
develop the validation models with the Argonne system level code SAS4A/SASSYS-1, the Argonne
subchannel analysis code DASSH, the Argonne high fidelity code Nek5000, and/or the Idaho National
Laboratory code Pronghorn Subchannel. The work outlined below will significantly improve the experimental and
validation database currently available for liquid metal fast reactors, thus making it a viable part of a
comprehensive reactor design and licensing suite to be used by the participant.

Conclusions drawn from this CRADA; include any major accomplishments:
A model for the fuel assembly, designed by Oklo and jointly fabricated by Oklo and Argonne was assembled and
installed in the Pressure drop Experimental Loop for the Investigations of Core Assemblies in Nuclear reactors
(PELICAN). A series of experimental tests were performed to gather pressure drop data across flow rates that
span the laminar, transition, and turbulent flow regimes. A total of 13 experimental tests were completed, Test050-
Test062. With 5 differential pressure (DP) transmitters installed, these 13 provided a total of 65 pressure drop vs.
flow rate data points. Of those 65 measurements, 41 measurements were unique while the remaining 24
measurements were repeats. A comparison was made between the experimental pressure drop data from the
PELICAN facility and simulation results from a SAS model. Across the tested flow range, the SAS model with both
the UCTD and PCTD correlations shows strong agreement with the experimental data, with pressure drop
increasing quadratically with flow rate. The PCTD correlation consistently predicts a slightly lower pressure drop
than the UCTD correlation at each corresponding flow rate. At the upper flow range, UCTD and PCTD predictions
are in excellent agreement with the normalized experimental value over the entire wire-wrapped region and also
show good agreement with the fully developed region value, with UCTD slightly closer than PCTD.
