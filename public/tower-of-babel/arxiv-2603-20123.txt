Early emission characterization of TDE2025aarm
Andrea Simongini, 1,2 Maria Kherlakian, 3 Alicia López-Oramas 4, Josefa Becerra 4,5

Abstract
In this Letter, we present early emission data analysis of the tidal disruption event TDE2025aarm, including optical, UV and X-ray data. At a redshift of z = 0.01368 z=0.01368 , TDE2025aarm is the second closest TDE ever discovered, offering an unprecedented opportunity to study such phenomena in great details. We observed TDE2025aarm in optical with the Liverpool Telescope for a total of three epochs, and complemented our dataset with ancillary spectroscopic and photometric data. The early optical spectra are characterized by a blue-continuum and helium, hydrogen and possibly Bowen lines typical of H+He events. The optical light curves peak at M g ∼ − 18.63 M_{g}\sim-18.63 mag and are well described by fallback of a M ⋆ ∼ 0.16 ​ M ⊙ M_{\star}\sim 0.16M_{\odot} star onto a M BH ∼ 2 × 10 7 ​ M ⊙ M_{\rm BH}\sim 2\times 10^{7}M_{\odot} black hole. We report Swift -XRT detection in the 0.3 − 10 0.3-10 keV range, with a total flux of F X ∼ 1.42 × 10 − 14 ​ erg ​ cm − 2 ​ s − 1 F_{X}\sim 1.42\times 10^{-14}\rm\,erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1} , fitted by a black-body with k B ​ T ∼ 0.39 k_{B}T\sim 0.39 keV. This makes TDE2025aarm a new event among optical/UV bright TDEs detected in soft X-rays. Our analysis suggests that the early emission from TDE2025aarm is powered by circularization shocks, and that the delayed accretion scenario best describes the observed features.



1
Introduction

Tidal disruption events (TDEs) are transient phenomena that occur when stars wander sufficiently close to a supermassive black hole (BH) to be torn apart by tidal forces.
A luminous flare of radiation, with typical luminosities of
10^{42}-10^{44}\rm\,erg\,s^{-1}
is expected from the fraction of stellar debris that, being gravitationally bound after disruption, falls back onto the BH forming an accretion disk
(
20
;
35
)
.
Over the past decade, wide-field optical and X-ray surveys have discovered more than 100 TDEs, establishing them as a distinct class of luminous nuclear transients (e.g.
19
).
These events typically exhibit blue optical/UV (OUV) continua and light curves that rise over days to weeks and decline over weeks to months, often approximated by a power-law decay consistent with the fallback rate of stellar debris onto the accretion disk (e.g.
29
).
Spectroscopically, they are divided into three main classes: He, He+H and H (e.g.
8
).
In particular, TDE H+He appear to prefer more UV luminous flares with more compact radii than TDE H, while TDE He appear in a larger range of radii and luminosities
(
14
)
.

Despite TDEs are expected to be mainly powered by accretion, almost 50
\%
of discovered TDEs exhibit faint X-ray radiation or none at all.
There are two main models trying to explain this diversity.
In the first scenario, the accretion disk formation is delayed, while the OUV emission is powered by shocks from debris stream–stream collisions near apocenter during the circularization process; the dissipated kinetic energy is thermalized and diffuses through the infalling debris, producing bright OUV emission that is largely decoupled from the X-ray emission from the inner accretion flow
(
33
)
.
In the second scenario, the circularizing debris form a viscous accretion disk around the SMBH which can effectively obscure the X-ray emission and reprocess it into OUV wavelengths
(
35
)
, with viewing angle effects potentially intervening
(
10
)
.

4
Analysis

4.1
Spectral analysis

We identify the spectral emission features of TDE2025aarm by comparing our spectra with those of AT 2023clx
(
7
)
.
In both cases, the spectra exhibit a blue-continuum on top of broad emission lines, which cools down tens of days after peak (Fig.
1
).
Both sources display the typical emission lines of the H+He class, being the Balmer series and the helium ions He
i

\lambda\lambda 5876,6678
and He
ii

\lambda 4684
.
The presence of Bowen fluorescence doublets cannot be rule out but neither robustly confirmed.
The N
iii

\lambda 4640
is potentially blended with He
iii
, while N
iii

\lambda 4100
with H
\delta
.

We subtracted the host light using the spectrum from DESI-DR1 (ID: 2842392105320449;
1
).
We fit the spectral features of the continuum-subtracted spectra to extract their relevant information, luminosity, full width-half maximum (FWHM), offset, and to track their evolution (Fig.
6
).
In general, the lines in exam exhibit a similar behavior.
They are relatively broad, with FWHM of the order of
10^{3}-10^{4}\rm\,km\,s^{-1}
, and do not show significant variation over time.
The luminosity of each line increases with time, reaching the peak between 10 – 23 days post
t_{\rm p}
, and remains stable afterwards or slightly decreases.
Finally, apart from He
ii
, which exhibits a large negative offset (potentially due to the blending with N
iii
), all the lines are positively offset with a decreasing trend after peak, reaching an almost null offset in our last spectrum.

The behavior after maximum is in line with the described properties of TDEs H+He by
8
.
Both the H
\alpha
and He
i

\lambda 5876
lines peak
\geq 24
days from
t_{p}
, although sparse sampling hinders the exact estimation of the time lag.
The lag time roughly follows
\tau\sim r/c

(
8
)
.
For TDE2025aarm this translates into
r=7\times 10^{16}
cm, two orders of magnitude higher than the black-body radius.

8
noticed that the evolution of the spectral lines and the photospheric evolution are correlated, and the lag can be interpreted as low electron density reprocessing material with high recombination times.
The correlation between spectral lines and photosphere can also be seen in the He
ii
/H
\alpha
ratio, which becomes stronger as the photospheric radius recedes, with values
\leq 1
.
This hints at a stratified emitting structure, with Helium closer to the BH that becomes stronger when the photosphere recedes.

4.3
Fallback modeling

After tidal disruption, a fraction of the stellar mass is gravitationally bound to the BH.
The subsequent fallback onto the BH can be subdivided into two phases: circularization and accretion.
The resulting radiation will directly depend on the BH mass
M_{\rm BH}
by:

\frac{dM}{dE}\frac{dE}{dt}=\frac{2\pi}{3}\left(GM_{\rm BH}\right)^{2/3}\frac{dM}{dE}t^{-5/3}

(1)

This equation describes a complete fallback; if the fallback is partial, we expect a steeper rate of
t^{-9/4}
.
Depending on the mass of the BH, the accretion rate can be in the super-Eddington or in the sub-Eddington regime.
In particular, the relation that describes the Eddington luminosity is:

L_{\rm Edd}=\eta\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}c^{2}=1.33\times 10^{44}\left(\frac{M_{\rm BH}}{10^{6}M_{\odot}}\right)\ \rm erg\,s^{-1}

(2)

where
\eta
is the accretion efficiency.
See e.g.
14
for additional details on this model.

We modeled our light curves using the
tde
module from
MOSFiT

(
17
)
.
We show the results in Fig.
8
and report prior and posterior of the free parameters in Table
5
.
The model well reproduces most of the observed data, with the lowest chi-square yielded for the
g
-band
\chi^{2}_{g}
/d.o.f. = 0.22 and the
uvm2
-band
\chi^{2}_{w1}
/d.o.f. = 0.99.
The
U
-band yields the highest residuals and, in general, the UVOT filters are the hardest to reproduce, as seen in
29
.
The fit yields a total reduced chi-square of
\chi^{2}
/d.o.f. = 1.54.
Our results suggest a low-mass star
M_{\star}=0.16^{+0.03}_{-0.03}\,M_{\odot}
disrupted by a high mass BH,
M_{\rm BH}=1.87^{+0.3}_{-0.2}\times 10^{7}M_{\odot}
, with a scaled impact factor of
b\sim 0.5
and a relatively low efficiency
\epsilon\sim 0.05
, in line with the population of H+He TDEs studied in
29
.
These results indicate sub-Eddington accretion, with
L_{\rm OUV}/L_{\rm Edd}=0.02
and
L_{X}/L_{\rm Edd}=3\times 10^{-6}
, revealing an extremely under-luminous X-ray emission.

5
Discussion and Conclusions

TDE2025aarm adds to the diversity of OUV bright TDEs that have been detected in the soft X-ray band
(
44
;
19
)
.
Remarkably, TDE2025aarm exhibits one of the highest OUV to X-ray luminosity ratios at peak ever found
(
19
)
, with
L_{\rm OUV}/L_{X}=6.4\times 10^{3}
, indicating that the two components must arise from well distinguished emitting regions and are uncorrelated.
The faint X-ray emission observed in TDE2025aarm may reflect inefficient accretion from a still-circularizing debris stream.
This behavior is potentially already hinted by the increasing counts in the XRT analysis, although the statistics is still too low to provide solid evidence.
Alternatively, the X-ray emission could be obscured by optically thick material, although the early detection by Chandra may suggest only partial veiling, as discussed for other TDEs
(
13
)
.

Interestingly, the delayed accretion scenario is potentially probed by the bumps in the temperature evolution (Fig.
2
).
Recent works by
23
,
7
, and
18
suggested that the first bump reflects radiation coming from the shocks produced during circularization, while the subsequent re-brightening would be due to the formation of the accretion disk, and consequently an increase in reprocessing X-rays to OUV light.
This behavior is mirrored in the light curves, as the UV peaks earlier than the optical.
The spectral lines evolution provides an additional argument.
For Bowen lines to emerge there must be a source of X-rays.
In the orientation dependent model, where the reprocessing happens around the accretion disk, the line width and the blueshift positively correlates with X-ray emission, that should rise while the photosphere becomes transparent.
This is the opposite of what we are observing with TDE2025aarm: the blueshift diminishes, while X-ray flux is increasing, indicating that the orientation scenario is not (the only) contributing factor.
In the delayed accretion scenario, instead, blueshift is unrelated with changes in the X-ray flux.
Future X-ray observations of TDE2025aarm will be crucial to test these interpretations, as the delayed emergence of a luminous soft X-ray component is expected once a circularized accretion disk forms and viscous accretion becomes efficient, or when the accreting material becomes optically thin (e.g.
13
;
19
).

In conclusion, we have presented an early characterization of TDE2025aarm.
Its proximity has enabled an exceptionally deep multi-wavelength characterization, including the detection of soft X-ray emission that would likely have remained undetected at larger distances, other than making the event well suited for long-term monitoring well beyond first light.
The high-cadence optical photometric and spectroscopic monitoring provides an unusually detailed view of its temporal and spectral evolution.
Future analyses will provide a more complete characterization of TDE2025aarm.
For now, this Letter establishes an initial observational framework that can serve as a reference for future studies, with particular emphasis on the importance of coordinated multi-wavelength follow-up observations.

Appendix C
extrabol
fitting

Figure 5:
Blackbody fits to the SEDs.
We show the epochs at which all five filters are available within a window of
\pm
1 day.
The top left sub-panel is instead obtained with interpolated points.

Here we summarize the workflow of the
extrabol
code
(
42
)
.
Once a set of light curves is given, the first step is to smoothly interpolate each of them using Gaussian process regression techniques.
We excluded the UVM2,
o
, and
c
bands to avoid redundancy, as their bandpasses significantly overlap with those of other filters.
They use a 2-dimensional kernel to interpolate the curves both in the time and wavelength domains.
The interpolation is self-regulated and, if it results in large errors, for example when the data are very sparse, it uses template libraries to reduce the uncertainties, although the farthest from the data points, the highest the errros.
For this reason, the absence of UV data at times earlier than the first point produces systematically higher errors, until the fit explodes.
This is why we executed the fit starting from
-40
days before
t_{p}
instead of utilizing the entire time range.
Once the interpolation is done, magnitudes are converted into fluxes at every epoch, building an SED (Fig.
5
).
The bolometric luminosity is then obtained by integrating the SED, while temperature and radius are obtained by fitting a black-body model to it using Monte Carlo sampling.
The default priors are
T_{0}=9000
K and
R_{0}=1\times 10^{15}
cm.
Temperature is constrained to positive values below
T_{\max}=40000
K, while radius can assume any positive values.
See
42
for more details.

Appendix D
Line fitting details

Figure 6:
Luminosity, FWHM and velocity offset of the Balmer series and the helium lines. Note that a negative offset corresponds to a redshift of the line.
We excluded H
\gamma
because of low signal-to-noise ratio.

We fit the host+continuum subtracted spectra using a custom Python script.
We follow the same procedures as
8
;
7
.
Each emission line is modeled independently with a Gaussian profile.
For each feature, we first define a spectral region centered on the expected line centroid and apply a smooth interpolation to reduce noise.
Within this region, we identify the most prominent peak closest to the centroid and use it as an initial guess for the fit.
We then iteratively fit a Gaussian function, updating the priors at each step based on the relative chi-squared of the fit to improve convergence.
From the best-fit model, we derive the FWHM, estimate the line luminosity by integrating over the fitted region, and compute the wavelength offset as the difference between the Gaussian peak and the expected line centroid.
If the feature is heavily blended, resulting in a very broad line, we use
pyspeckit
3
3

3

https://pyspeckit.readthedocs.io/en/latest/index.html
to fit a multi-gaussian profile centered on the rest-frame centroid of the individual features.
This method is applied to the He
ii
+ H
\beta
+ N
iii
blend in all the post-peak spectra.
In our earliest spectrum, H
\beta
is not clearly distinguishable, thus we treat He
ii
as a single line.
Results of line fitting are shown in Fig.
6
.
Notably, the He
ii
/ H
\alpha
luminosity ratio increases with time, assuming the following values:
0.41\pm 0.07
,

,
0.57\pm 0.15
, and
1.22\pm 0.19
