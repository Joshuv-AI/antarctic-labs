Intuitionistic Justification Logic, Semantically
Sonia Marin Paaras Padhiar Ian Shillito

Abstract
Justification logics are explicit versions of modal logic. In the classical setting, this means boxes are refined with explicit proof terms and interact with each other through proof operations. This exercise was extended to intuitionistic modal logic with native diamonds. In this setting, diamonds are refined to satisfier terms and come equipped with additional operations. Justification logic enjoys a connection to its corresponding modal logic through a realisation theorem. In the classical setting, this is achieved through either proof-theoretic or semantic methodology. So far, intuitionistic justification logic with satisfiers has only been presented syntactically with a proof-theoretic realisation theorem. We present two classes of semantics for intuitionistic justification logic with soundness and completeness results: basic modular models, which extend possible world semantics for intuitionistic propositional logic; modular models which contain Kripke-style machinery to promote “backwards compatibility” to modal logic. Using modular models, we present a realisation theorem to establish a connection between intuitionistic justification logic and its corresponding intuitionistic modal logic.

Justification logic makes knowledge explicit by attaching reasons to statements.
It refines modalities in standard modal logic: the usual

The first proposed justification logic is the Logic of Proofs (

The realisation theorem was first proved by Artemov using a proof-theoretic (and constructive) methodology

Semantics for justification logic.

Originally, the logic of proofs was interpreted using proofs in Peano Arithmetic (

)

]

Later, simple and flexible semantic models akin to epistemic (possible-world) models were created,

These models helped apply justification logic in a more general fashion

Intuitionistic justification logic.

Since

)

Though

Intermediate variants of

Intuitionistic diamonds.

In classical modal logic,

Furthermore, the addition of certain

Diamonds in justification logic.

In justification logic, even intuitionistic variants,

The first justification logic which makes the

We continue this line of work with a semantic exploration of intuitionistic justification logics (with

Building on these results, our investigation is crowned by an adaptation of the semantic realisation procedure in

Theorem 2.11

(Deduction Theorem)
.

Let
\Gamma\cup\{A,B\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}

Then:

\Gamma,A\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}B\iff\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A\mathbin{\to}B

Proof.

This is a standard argument

]

from right to left we simply use

given proof of

.
∎

Next, we show that
\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}
is closed under a notion of substitution modifying proof and satisfier terms.

Definition 2.12

(Justification substitution)
.

A
(justification) substitution

and
\sigma_{s}:\mathsf{SatVar}\rightarrow\mathsf{SatTm}
.

as a justification substitution.
We define as expected the applications

,
\mathtt{m}\sigma
and

of

to, respectively, the proof term
\mathtt{t}
, the satisfier term
\mathtt{m}
and the formula

We write

to designate the set

.

Lemma 2.13

(Substitution Lemma)
.

Let
\mathsf{CS}
be a
schematic constant specification,

\Gamma\cup\{A\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}
,
and

a substitution.
Then:

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A\implies\Gamma\sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A\sigma

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
29
, Lemma 2.27]
,
and goes by induction on the structure of the proof of
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.
The critical cases are when the last rule applied is

or
can

In the former case, it suffices to notice that if

is an instance of an axiom, then so is

.
In the latter case, we make use of the fact that
\mathsf{CS}
is
schematic:
with

and
[\mathtt{{!}^{\mathnormal{n}}\mathtt{\mathtt{c}}}]\dots{[\mathtt{\mathtt{c}}]A}
appearing in our consecution,
we obtain a proof of

via

by noticing that

as

is schematic.
∎

The next lemma expresses an interesting feature of justification logic:
its (object) language allows for the reflection of its own (meta) proofs
within formulas.
On top of its conceptual interest, we leverage this lemma in many places
throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.14

(Lifting Lemma)
.

Let
\mathsf{CS}
be axiomatically appropriate.

,

\{A_{1},\dots,A_{k},B,A\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}

As in
[

]
, we proceed by induction on the structure of the proof of

Our requirement that
\mathsf{CS}
is axiomatically appropriate shows in the base case when

the existence of a constant

We expand on the case for

In this case

and formula

∎

The proof of the above lemma informs us that the terms

and
\mathtt{m}
.

The technical lemma below generalises the axioms governing the operators

and

,
a useful tool in the completeness proof we provide later on.

\mathsf{CS}

2.4
From Modal to Justification – and back

We now proceed to formalise the connection between

and

in one direction, through translating their respective languages;
in the other, by using

.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}
.
If
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
then
{\Gamma}^{\mathsf{f}}\vdash_{\mathsf{IK}}{A}^{\mathsf{f}}
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the forgetful projection on axioms of
\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}
and conclusions of the

.
∎

Corollary 2.18
.

\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}
is consistent.

Proof.

Suppose
\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\mathord{\mathord{\bot}}
.
Then by Theorem

,

∎

The reverse direction is expressed via realisation maps.

Definition 2.19

(Realisation map)
.

A
realisation map
is a function
r:\mathcal{L}_{\Box}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}
such that
{r(G)}^{\mathsf{f}}=G
for each

.

In the remaining of the paper, we set ourselves to prove the next theorem.

Theorem 2.20

(Realisation Theorem)
.

For schematic
\mathsf{CS}
, there is a realisation map
r
such that:

\forall G\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box}.\;\;\;\vdash_{\mathsf{IK}}G\implies\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}r(G).

This

map
is precisely what
embeds the modal logic
\mathsf{IK}
into its corresponding justification logic
\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}
.
The condition
{r(G)}^{\mathsf{f}}=G
ensures that each
\Box
(and each
\Diamond
) occurring in
G
is replaced by exactly one proof term (and satisfier term, respectively).
In other words, a realisation map takes a modal formula
G
into a justification formula with the same formula tree.

Basic modular models were first introduced by Mkrtychev

3.2
Completeness

In this section we prove the reverse direction of the last result, i.e. completeness.

Theorem 3.6

(Completeness)
.

For any formula
A
:
\vDash_{\textsf{b}}{A}\implies\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.

Our proof exploits the construction of a canonical model for intuitionistic justification logic.
This syntactic structure uses prime sets as worlds.

Definition 3.7

(Prime Set)
.

A

\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}
is a set of formulas satisfying the following:

•

Deductive closure
: if
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
then
A\in\Gamma
.

•

Primeness
: if
A\mathbin{\vee}B\in\Gamma
then
A\in\Gamma
or
B\in\Gamma
.

•

Consistency
:
\bot\notin\Gamma
.

Notation 3.8
.

Let
\Gamma,\Delta\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}
.
We write
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Delta

if there is a finite set of formulas
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\}\subseteq\Delta
such that

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n}
.
We also write
\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Delta
when it does not hold that

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Delta
.

The completeness proof relies on projecting unprovable consecutions
into the canonical model.

.

such that

.

is a maximal set (w.r.t. inclusion) satisfying these conditions.

Proof sketch.

The proof goes via a standard argument: we exploit an enumeration of
formulas to extend
\Gamma
step-by-step
while ensuring
that the extension does not entail
\Delta
.
The limit of this process can be shown to be a prime set
\Gamma^{\prime}
not
entailing
\Delta
.
The maximality of
\Gamma^{\prime}
follows from the fact that
if a formula could be added without entailing
\Delta
,
then it would indeed have been added when
enumerated.
∎

Next, we define a structure which we show to be a basic (modular) model.
To define parts of this structure, we use the sets of formulas

\mathtt{t}^{-1}{\Gamma}:=\{A\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}\mid[\mathtt{\mathtt{t}}]A\in\Gamma\}

and

\mathtt{m}^{-1}{\Gamma}:=\{A\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}\mid\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle A\in\Gamma\}
.

Definition 3.10

(Canonical basic model)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{c}
be the tuple
(W^{c},\leq^{c},*^{c})
where:

\bullet

W^{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\Gamma\mid\text{$\Gamma$ is a prime set}\}
;

\bullet

{\mathtt{t}}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathtt{t}^{-1}{\Gamma}
;

\bullet

\leq^{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\subseteq
;

\bullet

{\mathtt{m}}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathtt{m}^{-1}{\Gamma}
.

\bullet

{p}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}=1
iff
p\in\Gamma
;

Proposition 3.11
.

\mathcal{B}^{c}
is a basic model.

Proof.

We are required to prove that this structure satisfies all
the properties from Definition
3.1
.
As an example, we show that
\mathtt{{s}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathtt{{t}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}}\subseteq{(\mathtt{s}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathtt{t})}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}
.
Let
B\in\mathtt{{s}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathtt{{t}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}}
.
By definition, there is a formula
A
such that
A\mathbin{\to}B\in{s}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}
and
A\in{t}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}
.
This means that
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{s}](A\mathbin{\to}B)
and
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t}]A
.
Using the
\mathsf{{jk}_{1}}
axiom we get
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{\mathtt{s}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathtt{t}}]B
, hence
[\mathtt{\mathtt{s}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathtt{t}}]B\in\Gamma
by deductive closure.
Therefore
B\in\mathtt{(}\mathtt{s}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathtt{t})^{-1}{\Gamma}={(\mathtt{s}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathtt{t})}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}
.
∎

Lemma 3.12

(Truth Lemma)
.

Let
A
be a formula and
\Gamma
be a prime set.
Then:

A\in\Gamma\iff\mathcal{B}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash A
.

Proof.

By induction on
A
.
All cases are covered in
[
31
]
, but the case for
A=\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle B
which we show:

\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle B\in\Gamma

\iff

B\in\mathtt{m}^{-1}{\Gamma}
by definition of
\mathtt{m}^{-1}{\Gamma}

\iff

B\in{\mathtt{m}}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}
by definition of the model
\mathcal{B}^{c}

\iff

\mathcal{B}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle B
by definition of truth in a basic model.

∎

With these results in hand, we can prove completeness.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

We prove the contrapositive.
Suppose
\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.
Then by the Prime Lemma
3.9
, there exists a prime set
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.
So
A\notin\Gamma
, and by the Truth Lemma
3.12
we have
\mathcal{B}^{c},\Gamma\not\Vdash A
.
Finally, Proposition
3.11
ensures that the
\mathcal{B}^{c}
belongs to the adequate class of models.
∎

4
Modular Models

In the classical setting, modular models
[
6
]
are epistemic models extending basic modular models with a modal accessibility relation to model the notion of
knowledge
.
A key feature of these models is
justification yields belief
, which says that if a formula of the form
[\mathtt{t}]A
holds at a world, then
A
must hold in all accessible worlds.
This behaviour imitates that of the
\Box
operator in modal logic and hence the feature promotes “backwards compatibility” from the justification logic to its corresponding modal logic.

In our setting, we must make several adaptations.
First, satisfaction of a modal formula
\Box G
is not defined locally, but globally along the intuitionistic pre-order, so the justification yields belief principle must account for this.
Second, to deal with satisfiers we introduce
satisfaction yield possibility
, which says that if a formula of the form
\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle A
holds at a world, then
A
must hold in some accessible world.
Finally, to promote “backwards compatibility”, we have to ensure that the additional frame conditions of a birelational frame (Definition
2.3
)
[
18
]
also hold.

4.1
Definition and Soundness

We define models for intuitionistic justification logic
that bridge it with intuitionistic modal logic via the modal relation.
To flesh out this connection, we make the interpretation of terms resonate with the modal relation by imposing conditions on the former using the latter and the notion of truth.
To get there, we need to introduce preliminary structures on which we evaluate formulas.

Definition 4.1

(Intuitionistic quasi-model)
.

An (intuitionistic) quasi-model is a tuple
\mathcal{M}=(W,\leq,R,*)
where
\mathcal{B}=(W,\leq,*)
is a basic model and
R
is a binary relation on
W
.

Definition 4.2

(Truth in a quasi-model)
.

For a quasi-model
\mathcal{M}=(W,\leq,R,*)
, a point
w\in W
and a formula
A
, we define the
truth of
A
at
w
in
\mathcal{M}
as:

\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
iff

\mathcal{B},w\Vdash A

where
\mathcal{B}=(W,\leq,*)
.

As the notion of truth is the same in basic and quasi-models, the following obviously follows.

Lemma 4.3

(Monotonicity Lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(W,\leq,R,*)
be a quasi-model.
Let
w,v\in W
such that
w\leq v
.
Let
A
be a formula.
Then:
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A\implies\mathcal{M},v\Vdash A

Proof.

By definition
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
implies
\mathcal{B},w\Vdash A
where
\mathcal{B}=(W,\leq,*)
.
Using the Monotonicity Lemma
for basic modular models we get
\mathcal{B},v\Vdash A
.
By definition here again, we obtain
\mathcal{M},v\Vdash A
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Definition
4.2
exhibits the
locality of truth

[
31
,
29
]
.
This notion in quasi-models helps us use these intermediate structures to
bridge justification and modal logics:
we obtain the adequate models by restricting the interpretation of terms by
R
and truth as shown in the next definition.

Definition 4.5

(Intuitionistic modular model)
.

An (intuitionistic modular) model is a quasi-model
\mathcal{M}=(W,\leq,R,*)
such that
\mathcal{M}_{\Box}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(W,\leq,R,*_{\mathsf{Prop}})
is a birelational intuitionistic model together with:

•

Justification yields belief
(JYB) principle:
for all proof terms
\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}

{\mathtt{t}}^{*}_{w}\subseteq\Box_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{A\mid\text{for all $w^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in W$ with $w\leq w^{\prime}Rv^{\prime}$, $\mathcal{M},v^{\prime}\Vdash A$}\}

•

Satisfaction yields possibility
(SYP) principle:
for all satisfiers
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}

{\mathtt{m}}^{*}_{w}\subseteq\Diamond_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{A\mid\text{there exists $v\in W$ such that $wRv$ and $\mathcal{M},v\Vdash A$}\}

We write
\vDash_{\textsf{m}}A
if for any model
\mathcal{M}
and world
w\in W
we have
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
.

In contrast to the classical setting or other intuitionistic variants
[
6
,
31
,
34
]
, the definition of (JYB) takes into account the global interpretation of
\Box
via the pre-order
\leq
.

Exploiting soundness with respect to basic models, the result follows for models.

Theorem 4.6

(Soundness of modular models)
.

For any formula
A
:

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A\implies\vDash_{\textsf{m}}{A}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(W,\leq,R,*)
be a modular model and

w
a world.
Since
\mathcal{B}=(W,\leq,*)
is a basic modular model, we have

\mathcal{B},w\Vdash A
by Theorem
.
Hence,
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
by Definition
4.5
.
∎

Remark 4.7

(Differences between Modular models and Fitting models)
.

The first epistemic models for classical justification logics were introduced by Fitting
[
19
]
.
These differ from Artemov’s modular models
[
6
]
on the definition of satisfaction for justification formulas of the form
[\mathtt{t}]A
.
Fitting models additionally require
A
to hold in all
modally accessible
worlds, i.e.,

\mathcal{M},w\Vdash[\mathtt{t}]A
iff
A\in{\mathtt{t}}^{*}_{w}
and for all
v\in W
with
wRv
,
\mathcal{M},v\Vdash A
.
In contrast, modular models make an
ontological separation
with the modal accessibility relation which plays no role on the satisfaction of formulas.
Yet, both classes of models ensure “backwards compatibility” with modal logic
[
29
]
;
in fact, they are equivalent
[
28
]
.

4.2
Completeness

We prove completeness w.r.t. the semantics on models,
assuming that
\mathsf{CS}
is axiomatically appropriate.

Theorem 4.8

(Completeness)
.

For any formula
A
:
\vDash_{\textsf{m}}{A}\implies\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.

We construct once more a canonical model
using the basic canonical model from Definition
3.10
as a basis.
In essence, we aim to show that an adequate modal relation
can be grafted onto this basic model.

We first need to define, for a given
\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}
, certain sets of formulas:

\Gamma^{\sharp}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{A\mid\text{ for some }\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm},\,[\mathtt{t}]A\in\Gamma\}

and
\Gamma^{\flat}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{A\mid\text{for all }\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm},\,\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle A\notin\Gamma\}
.

Definition 4.9

(Canonical intuitionistic modular model)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{c}
be the tuple
(W^{c},\leq^{c},R^{c},*^{c})
where

\mathcal{B}^{c}=(W^{c},\leq^{c},*^{c})
is the canonical basic model,
and

\Gamma R^{c}\Delta

is defined as:
if
[\mathtt{\mathtt{t}}]A\in\Gamma
then
A\in\Delta

and for each
A\in\Delta
there exists
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle A\in\Gamma
.

It is immediate that
\mathcal{M}^{c}
is a quasi-model, as
\mathcal{B}^{c}
is a basic model by Proposition
3.11
.
Utilising the locality of truth, this allows us to directly prove the Truth Lemma for
\mathcal{M}^{c}
.

Lemma 4.10

(Truth Lemma)
.

Let
A
be a formula and
\Gamma
be a prime set.
Then:
A\in\Gamma\iff\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash A
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{B}^{c}
is a basic model, we have

\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash A\iff\mathcal{B}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash A\iff A\in\Gamma
by Lemma
3.12
.
∎

The canonical model also displays the following strong properties which ensure all collections of formulas made true by the same satisfier
\mathtt{m}
(or false by the same proof term
\mathtt{t}
) must be in fact true (or false, respectively) at the same world.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\Pi\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}

and

\Gamma\in W^{c}
.

1.

If
for all
\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}
and for all
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\Pi
,

\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t}](A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})
,
then there are
\Gamma^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}\in W^{c}
such that
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}R^{c}\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Pi
.

2.

If for some
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
and for all
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\Pi
,

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle(A_{1}\and\dots\and A_{n})
,
then there is
\Delta\in W^{c}
such that
\Gamma R^{c}\Delta
and
\Delta\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
for all
A\in\Pi
.

Proof.

1. Let
\tilde{\Pi}=\{[\mathtt{t}](A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})\mid\text{$\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}$ and $A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\Pi$}\}
.
Since for all
\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}
and
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\Pi
we have
\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t}](A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})
,
we can establish that
\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\tilde{\Pi}
.
Indeed, the primeness of
\Gamma
informs us that if it entailed a
finite disjunction of elements of
\tilde{\Pi}

then it would entail one of its disjuncts.
Then, by the Prime Lemma
3.9
we get a maximal
prime set
\Gamma^{\prime}
with
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
and

\Gamma^{\prime}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\tilde{\Pi}
.
We can then show that
\Gamma^{\prime\sharp}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Pi
.
Otherwise, there would be
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\in\Gamma^{\prime\#}
with each
[\mathtt{t_{i}}]C_{i}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
and
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\Pi
such that

C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n}
,
which by the Lifting Lemma
2.14

would give
\Gamma^{\prime}\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t}](A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})

for some
\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}
.
Then, we can apply the Prime Lemma
3.9
again on
\Gamma^{\prime\sharp}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Pi
to construct a prime set
\Delta^{\prime}
with
\Gamma^{\prime\sharp}\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Pi
.

Finally we need to check that
\Gamma^{\prime}R^{c}\Delta^{\prime}
.
Since
\Gamma^{\prime\sharp}\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}
by construction we have that if
[\mathtt{\mathtt{t}}]A\in\Gamma^{\prime}
then
A\in\Delta^{\prime}
, hence it is enough to show that
for each
A\in\Delta^{\prime}
there exists
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle A\in\Gamma^{\prime}
.
For such
A\in\Delta^{\prime}
, suppose for a contradiction that for all
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
,
\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle A\notin\Gamma^{\prime}
.
Then, for all
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\Pi
we have
\Gamma^{\prime},\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle A\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t}](A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})

for each
\mathtt{m}
and
\mathtt{t}
,
otherwise using the Deduction Theorem
2.11
and
\mathsf{{jk}_{4}}
we would have

\Gamma^{\prime}\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{\mathtt{m}\mathbin{\triangleright}\mathtt{t}}](A\mathbin{\to}(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n}))

and hence
A\mathbin{\to}(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})\in\Gamma^{\prime\#}\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}
,
so we could deduce from
A\in\Delta^{\prime}
and primeness of
\Delta^{\prime}
that

A_{i}\in\Delta^{\prime}
for some
i\leq n
.
However, we have
\Gamma^{\prime}\subsetneq\Gamma^{\prime}\cup\{\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle A\}
and so the Prime Lemma
3.9
would give a strictly larger prime set satisfying the same conditions as
\Gamma^{\prime}
, contradicting its maximality
– a similar use of maximality to e.g.
[
18
, Lemma 3 and Theorem 7]
.

2.
Let us fix
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
such that for all
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\Pi
,

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle(A_{1}\and\dots\and A_{n})
,
hence by deductive closure of
\Gamma
,

\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle(A_{1}\and\dots\and A_{n})\in\Gamma
.
We first show that

\Pi\cup\Gamma^{\sharp}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Gamma^{\flat}
.
Otherwise, there would be
A_{1},\dots,A_{l}\in\Pi
,
B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\in\Gamma^{\sharp}
with
[\mathtt{t_{i}}]B_{i}\in\Gamma
for some
\mathtt{t_{i}}
, and
C_{1},\dots,C_{p}\in\Gamma^{\flat}
such that

A_{1},\dots,A_{l},B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}C_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}C_{p}
,
and therefore
A_{1}\and\ldots\and A_{l},B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}C_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}C_{p}
.
By the Lifting Lemma
2.14
this
yields some
\mathtt{n}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
such that
\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle(A_{1}\and\ldots\and A_{l}),[\mathtt{t_{1}}]B_{1},\dots,[\mathtt{t_{n}}]B_{n}\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{n}\rangle(C_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}C_{p})
, hence

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{n}\rangle(C_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}C_{p})
.
Now, using the
\mathsf{{jk}_{3}}
axiom as well as the deductive closure and primeness of
\Gamma
, we get

\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{n}}\rangle C_{j}\in\Gamma
for some
j\leq p
, which contradicts
C_{j}\in\Gamma^{\flat}
.
Then, we can apply the Prime Lemma
3.9
on
\Pi\cup\Gamma^{\sharp}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Gamma^{\flat}

to get a prime set
\Delta\supseteq\Pi\cup\Gamma^{\sharp}
with

\Delta\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Gamma^{\flat}
.
Finally,
\Gamma R^{c}\Delta
because as
\Gamma^{\sharp}\subseteq\Delta
by construction we have that if
[\mathtt{\mathtt{t}}]A\in\Gamma
then
A\in\Delta
, and as
\Delta\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\Gamma^{\flat}
we have that
for each
A\in\Delta
there must exist
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle A\in\Gamma
.
∎

As our next step in showing that
\mathcal{M}^{c}
is an intuitionistic modular model,
we show that
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box}
is an intuitionistic birelational model.
We therefore proceed to establish the confluence properties.

Lemma 4.12

(Forwards and backwards confluence in
\mathcal{M}^{c}
)
.

Let
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime},\Delta\in W^{c}
.

(FC)

If
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
and
\Gamma R^{c}\Delta
,
then there exists
\Delta^{\prime}\in W^{c}
with
\Delta\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}R^{c}\Delta^{\prime}
.

(BC)

If
\Gamma R^{c}\Delta\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}
,
then there exists
\Gamma^{\prime}\in W^{c}
with
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}R^{c}\Delta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

While our proof is similar to the one for modal birelational models for
\mathsf{IK}
in
[
18
]
,
there are some subtleties in handling the satisfier terms.
We expand on this point in the remark below.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

The use of proof terms in classical justification logic is known to introduce some non-normal behaviours of the modality
[
5
,
7
]
.

Similarly,
in the intuitionistic setting,
the
\mathsf{IK}
theorem
\Diamond(G_{1}\and\dots\and G_{n})\mathbin{\to}(\Diamond G_{1}\and\dots\and\Diamond G_{n})
does not distinguish the information the distinct diamonds are carrying.
The justification version of this theorem,

\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle(A_{1}\and\dots\and A_{n})\mathbin{\to}(\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{t_{1}}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{m}}\rangle A_{1}\and\dots\and\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{t_{n}}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{m}}\rangle A_{n})

where
\mathtt{m}
is a satisfier variable and each
\mathtt{t_{i}}
is a ground term obtained by applying the Lifting Lemma
2.14
to
\vdash_{\mathsf{IK}}(A_{1}\and\dots\and A_{n})\mathbin{\to}A_{i}
,
is more constrained due to the specific structure of the
\mathtt{}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{}
-satisfier terms on the right-hand-side.

We finally establish that
\mathcal{M}^{c}
is a modular model
by proving that it obeys the JYB and SYP principles.

Justification yields belief
is already achieved from the definition of
R^{c}
,
following the lines of
[
31
]
.

Lemma 4.14

(JYB in
\mathcal{M}^{c}
)
.

Let
\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}
and
\Gamma\in W^{c}
.
Then:

A\in{\mathtt{t}}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}\implies
for all
\Gamma^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}\in W^{c}
such that
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}R^{c}\Delta^{\prime}
we have
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Delta^{\prime}\Vdash A
.

Proof.

Suppose
A\in{\mathtt{t}}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}
, then
[\mathtt{t}]A\in\Gamma
by Definition
3.10
.
So, for
\Gamma^{\prime}\supseteq\Gamma
we have
[\mathtt{t}]A\in\Gamma^{\prime}
too.
Hence, for
\Delta^{\prime}
with
\Gamma^{\prime}R^{c}\Delta^{\prime}
, we have
A\in\Delta^{\prime}
by Definition
4.9
.
By the Truth Lemma
4.10
, we have
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Delta^{\prime}\Vdash A
.
∎

The remaining principle of
satisfaction yields possibility
is slightly more involved, as shown below.

Lemma 4.15

(SYP in
\mathcal{M}^{c}
)
.

Let
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
and
\Gamma\in W^{c}
. Then:

A\in{\mathtt{m}}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}\implies
there exists
\Delta\in W^{c}
such that
\Gamma R^{c}\Delta
and
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Delta\Vdash A
.

Proof.

Suppose
A\in{\mathtt{m}}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}
, then
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle A\in\Gamma
by definition.
So,
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle A
.
Therefore, by Lemma
4.11
there is
\Delta\in W^{c}
with
\Gamma R^{c}\Delta
and
\Delta\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.
By deductive closure
A\in\Delta
and apply the Truth Lemma
4.10
for
\mathcal{M}^{c}
to get
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Delta\Vdash A
.
∎

The next proposition reveals the nature of
\mathcal{M}^{c}
by gathering our results.

Proposition 4.16
.

\mathcal{M}^{c}
is an intuitionistic modular model.

Proof.

First,
\mathcal{M}^{c}
is a quasi-model as
\mathcal{B}^{c}
is a basic model by Proposition
3.11
.
Then, Lemma
4.12

shows that forwards and backwards confluence hold in
\mathcal{M}^{c}
.
Finally, Lemma
4.14
and Lemma
4.15

respectively prove that the properties JYB and SYP are satisfied in
\mathcal{M}^{c}
.
∎

With this in hand, completeness follows.

Proof of Theorem
4.8
.

We reason contrapositively and assume
\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.
The Prime Lemma
3.9
informs us of the existence of a prime set
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.
So
A\notin\Gamma
, which gives
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\not\Vdash A

by the Truth Lemma
4.10
.
Finally, Proposition
4.16
ensures that the
\mathcal{M}^{c}
belongs to the adequate class of models.
∎

5
Realisation

We recall that our goal is to prove the following.

Theorem
2.20
.

For schematic
\mathsf{CS}
, there is a realisation map
r
such that for all
G\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
:

\vdash_{\mathsf{IK}}G\implies\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}r(G).

This realisation function is constructed methodically in two steps:

1.

Firstly, we reason semantically that a theorem
G
of
\mathsf{IK}
can be
pre-realised
into a disjunction of justification formulas which have “roughly” the same structure as
G
;

2.

Secondly, we
condense
a pre-realisation into a realisation by syntactical reasoning and substitutions to remove the extraneous content.

Our goal is more specifically to define
normal
realisation map which differentiates modalities based on their polarity.
Recall that
a subformula of
G
is
positive
if its position in the formula tree of
G
is reached from the root by following the left branch of an implication an even number of times; and call it
negative
otherwise.

Definition 5.1

(Normal realisation)
.

A realisation map

r:\mathcal{L}_{\Box}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}

is
normal
if,
for any
G\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
,
its negative modal subformulas
\Box F
(or
\Diamond F
) are realised as

[\mathtt{\mathtt{x}}]B
(or
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a}}\rangle B
) with
\mathtt{x}\in\mathsf{PrfVar}
(or
\mathtt{a}\in\mathsf{SatVar}
, respectively)
occurring in
r(G)
exactly once.

Remark 5.2
.

The choice of a normal realisation
ensures that no independent subformulas are unintensionally identified.
For example, the
\mathsf{IK}
-theorem
(\Diamond p\mathbin{\vee}\Diamond q)\mathbin{\to}\Diamond(p\mathbin{\vee}q)
can be realised as

(\langle\mathtt{a}\rangle p\mathbin{\vee}\langle\mathtt{a}\rangle q)\mathbin{\to}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{\mathtt{t_{1}}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{a}}\mathbin{\sqcup}\mathtt{\mathtt{t_{2}}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{a}}}\rangle(p\mathbin{\vee}q)\quad\text{or}\quad(\langle\mathtt{a}\rangle p\mathbin{\vee}\langle\mathtt{b}\rangle q)\mathbin{\to}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{\mathtt{t_{1}}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{a}}\mathbin{\sqcup}\mathtt{\mathtt{t_{2}}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{b}}}\rangle(p\mathbin{\vee}q)

where
\mathtt{t_{1}}
and
\mathtt{t_{2}}
are ground terms
obtained by applying the Lifting Lemma
2.14
to
\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}p\mathbin{\to}(p\mathbin{\vee}q)
and
\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}q\mathbin{\to}(p\mathbin{\vee}q)
, respectively.
But, the first instance cannot be the result of a normal realisation.

For this reason, we need to do some bookkeeping on
\Box
s and
\Diamond
s:
depending on their (positive or negative) polarity, they will be replaced by a variable in
\mathsf{PrfVar}\cup\mathsf{SatVar}
or a compound term in
\mathsf{PrfTm}\cup\mathsf{SatTm}
.
So, we first annotate by natural numbers

\Box
s and
\Diamond
s in the modal logic formulas.
Each annotation is unique. Formally:

Definition 5.3

(Annotated modal formulas)
.

An
annotated modal formula
is built up like a standard modal formula but instead of single modalities
\Box
and
\Diamond
, there is an infinite family of indexed modalities
\Box_{k}
and
\Diamond_{k}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We assume that
no index occurs more than once in any given annotated formula.

Example 5.4
.

Given the modal formula
\Box\Diamond G\mathbin{\to}\Box(\Diamond J\and\Diamond\Box(H\mathbin{\vee}I))
, a possible annotated version is

\Box_{1}\Diamond_{2}G\mathbin{\to}\Box_{3}(\Diamond_{4}J\and\Diamond_{5}\Box_{6}(H\mathbin{\vee}I))
.

Additionally, we need to track the signs of modal formulas.

Definition 5.5

(Signed modal formulas)
.

Signed modal formulas
are annotated modal formulas assigned with a polarity
\mathsf{T}
or
\mathsf{F}
.
We denote this as
\mathsf{T}{G}
or
\mathsf{F}{G}
.
The set of signed formulas is
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}^{\mathsf{ann}}
.

We stress that these annotations do not add semantic information: they are bookkeeping devices.
Where it is clear, we do not distinguish between a modal formula and its annotated version.

5.1
Pre-realisation

In this subsection,
we adapt the methodology of
[
19
,
29
,
34
]

and reason semantically on both the canonical model
\mathcal{M}^{c}
and its induced birelational intuitionistic model
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box}
(recall Definition
4.5
) to
pre-realise
modal formulas.

Definition 5.6

(Pre-realiser)
.

The
pre-realiser
is a function

\llangle\cdot\rrangle:\mathcal{L}_{\Box}^{\mathsf{ann}}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}})

defined as:

\begin{array}[]{c @{\hspace{0.02cm}}c @{\hspace{0.02cm}} l@{\quad}c @{\hspace{0.02cm}} c @{\hspace{0.02cm}} l}\llangle\mathsf{T}{p}\rrangle\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\{p\}&\llangle\mathsf{F}{p}\rrangle\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\{p\}\\

\llangle\mathsf{T}{G\mathbin{\to}H}\rrangle\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\{A\mathbin{\to}B\mid A\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle,B\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{H}\rrangle\}&\llangle\mathsf{F}{G\mathbin{\to}H}\rrangle\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\hbox{\multirowsetup$\{(A_{1}\and\dots\and A_{n})\mathbin{\to}(B_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}B_{m})\mid\phantom{A}\,\,A_{i}\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle,B_{j}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle\}$}\\
\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&&\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt\\
\llangle\mathsf{T}{\Box_{n}G}\rrangle\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\{[\mathtt{\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{n}}}}]A\mid A\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle\}&\llangle\mathsf{F}{\Box_{n}G}\rrangle\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\hbox{\multirowsetup$\{[\mathtt{t}](A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{m})\mid A_{i}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle,\newline
\phantom{A}\,\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}\}$}\\
\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&&\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt\\
\llangle\mathsf{T}{\Diamond_{n}G}\rrangle\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\{\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{n}}}}\rangle(A_{1}\and\dots\and A_{m})\mid A_{i}\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle\}&\llangle\mathsf{F}{\Diamond_{n}G}\rrangle\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\hfil\hskip 0.56917pt&\{\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle A\mid A\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle,\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}\}\end{array}

Definition 5.7
.

A modal formula
G
is
pre-realisable
in
\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}
if

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
,
i.e.,
there exist some
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
such that
\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n}
.

The following is the key result of this subsection
and corresponds to the first step of the realisation proof described above and provides an initial but imperfect connection between theorems of
\mathsf{IK}
and theorems of
\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}
.
It leverages the semantic machinery developed up to now.

Theorem 5.8

(Pre-realisation)
.

Let
\mathsf{CS}
be axiomatically appropriate. For
G\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
,
if
\vdash_{\mathsf{IK}}G
,
then
G
is
pre-realisable
in
\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}
.

This is shown through
the semantic connection between a pre-realiser and its modal formula which is understood in the following:

Theorem 5.9
.

For every annotated modal formula
G
.
For a prime set
\Gamma
.

1.

If for all
A\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle
we have

\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash A
, then
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Gamma\Vdash G
.

2.

If for all
A\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
we have

\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\not\Vdash A
, then
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Gamma\not\Vdash G
.

Proof.

We prove both simultaneously by induction on
G
.
The cases when
G=p,H\and I,H\mathbin{\vee}I,H\mathbin{\to}I
follow the argument in
[
34
]
.
We extend the proof with an adapted case for
\Box
and a new one for
\Diamond
.

If
G=\Box_{n}H
then
A\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle
is of the form
[\mathtt{x_{n}}]B
for
B\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{H}\rrangle
and
A\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
is of the form
[\mathtt{t}](B_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}B_{m})
for
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle
and
\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}
.

1.

Suppose for all
A\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle
,

\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash A
, that is,
for all
B\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{H}\rrangle
,
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash[\mathtt{\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{n}}}}]B
.
So,
B\in{\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{n}}}}^{*^{c}}_{\Gamma}
and by Lemma
4.14
, for all
\Gamma^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}
with
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}R^{c}\Delta^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Delta^{\prime}\Vdash B
.
By (IH) on 1.,
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Delta^{\prime}\Vdash H
and therefore
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Gamma\Vdash\Box_{n}H
.

2.

Suppose for all
A\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
,

\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\not\Vdash A
, that is,
for all
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle
and
\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}
,
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\not\Vdash[\mathtt{t}](B_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}B_{m})
.
So by the Truth Lemma
4.10
,
[\mathtt{t}](B_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}B_{m})\notin\Gamma
, hence
\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t}](B_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}B_{m})
.
By Lemma
4.11
, there exist
\Gamma^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}\in W^{c}
such that
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}R^{c}\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle
.
So for all
B\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle
,
B\notin\Delta^{\prime}
and by the Truth Lemma
4.10
again,
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Delta^{\prime}\not\Vdash B
.
By (IH) on 2.,
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Delta^{\prime}\not\Vdash H
, hence
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Gamma\not\Vdash\Box_{n}H
.

If
G=\Diamond_{n}H
then
A\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle
is of the form
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{n}}}}\rangle(B_{1}\and\dots\and B_{m})
for
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{H}\rrangle
, and
A\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
is of the form
\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle B
for
B\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle
and
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
.

1.

Suppose for all
A\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle
,

\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash A
, that is,
for all
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{H}\rrangle
,
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\Vdash\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{n}}}}\rangle(B_{1}\and\dots\and B_{n})
.
By Lemma
4.11
, there exists
\Delta
with
\Gamma R^{c}\Delta
and
\Delta\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}B
for all
B\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{H}\rrangle
.
By deductive closure
B\in\Delta
and by the Truth Lemma
4.10
we get
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Delta\Vdash B
for all
B\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{H}\rrangle
.
Hence, by (IH) on 1.,
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Delta\Vdash H
and so
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Gamma\Vdash\Diamond_{n}H
.

2.

Suppose for all
A\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
,

\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\not\Vdash A
, that is,
for all
B\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle
and
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
,
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\not\Vdash\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle B
,
and by the Truth Lemma
4.10
,
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle B\notin\Gamma
.
By definition of
R^{c}
(Definition
4.9
),
it means, for all
\Delta\in W^{c}
with
\Gamma R^{c}\Delta
, that
B\notin\Delta
,
and by the Truth Lemma
4.10
again,
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Delta\not\Vdash B
for all
B\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle
.
Hence, by (IH) on 2.,
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Delta\not\Vdash H
and so
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Gamma\not\Vdash\Diamond_{n}H
.
∎

Now we can prove the Pre-realisation Theorem
5.8
.

Proof of Theorem
5.8
.

Suppose
G
is not pre-realisable.
Then
\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
.
By the Prime Lemma
3.9
, there exists a prime set
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
.
Hence, for all
A\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
,
A\notin\Gamma
, so
by the Truth Lemma
4.10
,
\mathcal{M}^{c},\Gamma\not\Vdash A
.
By Theorem
5.9
,
\mathcal{M}^{c}_{\Box},\Gamma\not\Vdash G
, so
G
is not a theorem of
\mathsf{IK}
.
∎

5.2
From Pre-realisation to Realisation

This subsection performs the second step of the realisation proof sketched above.
From any finite subset of pre-realisers for a given modal formula
G
, through a process called
condensing

[
21
]
which syntactically
removes the extra disjunctions and conjunctions,
we construct a justification formula that
potentially
realises
G
, i.e. respects its syntax but not necessarily its semantics.
Eventually, the realisation map, will choose a valid justification formula from the set of potential realisers, guided by the Pre-realisation theorem
5.8
from the previous section.

Definition 5.10

(Potential realiser)
.

The
potential realiser
is a map

\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket:\mathcal{L}_{\Box}^{\mathsf{ann}}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}})

defined as:

\begin{array}[]{c@{\colonequals}l@{\quad}c@{\colonequals}l}\llbracket\mathsf{T}{p}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{p\}&\llbracket\mathsf{F}{p}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{p\}\\
\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G\and H}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{A\and B\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G}\rrbracket,B\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{H}\rrbracket\}&\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G\and H}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{A\and B\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket,B\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{H}\rrbracket\}\\
\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G\mathbin{\vee}H}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{A\mathbin{\vee}B\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G}\rrbracket,B\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{H}\rrbracket\}&\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G\mathbin{\vee}H}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{A\mathbin{\vee}B\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket,B\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{H}\rrbracket\}\\
\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G\mathbin{\to}H}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{A\mathbin{\to}B\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket,B\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{H}\rrbracket\}&\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G\mathbin{\to}H}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{A\mathbin{\to}B\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket,B\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{H}\rrbracket\}\\
\llbracket\mathsf{T}{\Box_{n}G}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{[\mathtt{\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{n}}}}]A\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G}\rrbracket\}&\llbracket\mathsf{F}{\Box_{n}G}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{[\mathtt{t}]A\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket,\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}\}\\
\llbracket\mathsf{T}{\Diamond_{n}G}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{n}}}}\rangle A\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G}\rrbracket\}&\llbracket\mathsf{F}{\Diamond_{n}G}\rrbracket\hfil\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}&\{\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle A\mid A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket,\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}\}\end{array}

The idea is that a realisation function on a modal formula
G
would choose a suitable justification formula
A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket
.
The choice of a formula from
\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket
, rather than
\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G}\rrbracket
, ensures that for any negative subformula of
A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket
of the shape
[\mathtt{t}]B
(or
\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle B
), we have
\mathtt{t}\in\mathsf{PrfVar}
(or
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatVar}
respectively), which is required to reach a normal realisation.
The difference in the definition of
\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G\mathbin{\to}H}\rrbracket
,
\llbracket F{\Box_{n}G}\rrbracket
and
\llbracket\mathsf{T}{\Diamond_{n}G}\rrbracket
compared to the pre-realiser ensures the justification formula does not contain additional conjunctions and disjunctions and exactly matches the structure of the modal formula.
More formally:

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
G
be an annotated modal formula.
Then for every
A\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G}\rrbracket\cup\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket
,
{A}^{\mathsf{f}}=G
,
where
{(\cdot)}^{\mathsf{f}}:\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{J}}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
is the forgetful projection from Definition
2.16
.

Proof.

This is a routine proof by induction on
G
.
∎

The pre-realiser from Definition
5.6
is converted into a potential realiser using substitutions and syntactic reasoning – this process is called
condensing
by Fitting
[
21
, p. 640]
.

To construct adequate substitutions we use the following auxiliary definitions.

Definition 5.12

(No new variable condition)
.

Let
\sigma
be a substitution.
The
domain
of
\sigma
is the set

\text{dom}(\sigma)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{}}}\in\mathsf{PrfVar}\mid\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{}}}\sigma\neq\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{}}}\}\cup\{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{}}}\in\mathsf{SatVar}\mid\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{}}}\sigma\neq\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{}}}\}
.

A substitution
\sigma

meets the no new variable condition
if for each variable
\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{}}}\in\text{dom}(\sigma)
(or
\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{}}}\in\text{dom}(\sigma)
),
\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{}}}\sigma
(or
\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{}}}\sigma
) contains no other variables than
\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{}}}
(or
\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{}}}
).

Definition 5.13

(Lives on)
.

Let
G
be an annotated modal formula
and
\sigma
be a substitution.
We say that

\sigma

lives on

G
if for every
\mathtt{x_{\mathnormal{k}}}\in\text{dom}(\sigma)
,
\Box_{k}
occurs in
G
and for every
\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}\in\text{dom}(\sigma)
,
\Diamond_{k}
occurs in
G
.

With these definitions in hand, we tackle the condensing theorem.

Theorem 5.14

(Condensing Theorem)
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box}^{\mathsf{ann}}
.

1.

For each collection
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{G}\rrangle
, there exist
A\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{G}\rrbracket
and
\sigma
that lives on
G
and meets the no new variable condition such that
A\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}(A_{1}\and\dots\and A_{n})\sigma
.

2.

For each collection
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle
, there exist
A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket
and
\sigma
that lives on
G
and meets the no new variable condition such that
(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})\sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.

Proof.

We prove both simultaneously by induction on
G
.
Except for diamonds, all cases are covered by Pischke
[
34
]
.
So, we are left to consider the case when
G=\Diamond_{k}H
.

1. Let
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle(A_{1}^{1}\and\dots\and A_{n_{1}}^{1}),\dots,\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle(A_{1}^{m}\and\dots\and A_{n_{m}}^{m})\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{\Diamond_{k}H}\rrangle
with
A_{1}^{1},\dots,A_{n_{1}}^{1},\dots,A_{1}^{m},\dots,A_{n_{m}}^{m}\in\llangle\mathsf{T}{H}\rrangle
.
By the inductive hypothesis, there exist
A\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{H}\rrbracket
and
\sigma
living on
H
which meets the no new variable condition such that

A\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}(A_{1}^{1}\and\dots\and A_{n_{1}}^{1}\and\dots\and A_{1}^{m}\and\dots\and A_{n_{m}}^{m})\sigma
.
So, for each
i\leq m
,

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A\mathbin{\to}(A_{1}^{i}\and\dots\and A_{n_{i}}^{i})\sigma
.
By the Lifting Lemma
2.14
, there exist ground terms
\mathtt{t}_{1},\dots,\mathtt{t}_{n}\in\mathsf{PrfTm}
such that

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t_{i}}](A\mathbin{\to}(A_{1}^{i}\and\dots\and A_{n_{i}}^{i})\sigma)
.
Set
\mathtt{t}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathtt{t_{1}}\mathbin{+}\mathtt{\mathtt{\dots}\mathbin{+}\mathtt{t_{m}}}
which is also a ground term, and by repetitive use of
\mathsf{j}{\mathtt{}\mathbin{+}\mathtt{}}_{l}
and
\mathsf{j}{\mathtt{}\mathbin{+}\mathtt{}}_{r}
, we have

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t}](A\mathbin{\to}(A_{1}^{i}\and\dots\and A_{n_{i}}^{i})\sigma)

for all
i\leq m
.

Now, define
\sigma^{\prime}

as
\sigma^{\prime}(\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}})=\mathtt{t}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}
and as the identity otherwise – note as
\mathtt{t}
is a ground term,
\sigma^{\prime}
meets the no new variable condition.
By the Substitution Lemma
2.13
, Definition
2.12
and the fact that
\mathtt{t}
is a ground term, so
\mathtt{t}\sigma^{\prime}=\mathtt{t}
, we have

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}[\mathtt{t}](A\sigma^{\prime}\mathbin{\to}(A_{1}^{i}\and\dots\and A_{n_{i}}^{i})\sigma\sigma^{\prime})

for all
i=1,\dots,m
.
Using the
\mathsf{{jk}_{2}}
axiom and
mp
:

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle A\sigma^{\prime}\mathbin{\to}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{t}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}}\rangle(A_{1}^{i}\and\dots\and A_{n_{i}}^{i})\sigma\sigma^{\prime}
.
Which can be rewritten as

\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle(A\sigma^{\prime})\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}(\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle(A_{1}^{i}\and\dots\and A_{n_{i}}^{i}))\sigma\sigma^{\prime}

since
\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}=\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}\sigma^{\prime}=\mathtt{t}\mathbin{\star}\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}
.
Propositional reasoning gives

\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle(A\sigma^{\prime})\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}(\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle(A_{1}^{1}\and\dots\and A_{n_{1}}^{1})\and\dots\and\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle(A_{1}^{m}\and\dots\and A_{n_{m}}^{m}))\sigma\sigma^{\prime}

where indeed
\sigma\sigma^{\prime}
is a substitution which lives on
\Diamond_{k}H
, meets the no new variable condition and
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{a_{\mathnormal{k}}}}\rangle(A\sigma^{\prime})\in\llbracket\mathsf{T}{\Diamond_{k}H}\rrbracket
.

2. Let
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}_{1}}\rangle A_{1},\dots,\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}_{n}}\rangle A_{n}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{\Diamond_{k}H}\rrangle
with
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{H}\rrangle
and
\mathtt{m}_{1},\dots,\mathtt{m}_{n}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
.
By Proposition
2.15
, there exists a satisfier
\mathtt{m}\in\mathsf{SatTm}
such that

(*)

\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}_{1}}\rangle A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}_{n}}\rangle A_{n}\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})
.
By the inductive hypothesis, there exist
A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{H}\rrbracket
and
\sigma
that lives on
H
(and meets the no new variable condition) such that
(**)

(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})\sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.
Note that by definition
\sigma
also lives on
\Diamond_{k}H
trivially.
By the Substitution Lemma
2.13
applied to
(*)
, we have

(\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}_{1}}\rangle A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}_{n}}\rangle A_{n})\sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}(\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n}))\sigma
.
By the Lifting Lemma
2.14
applied to
(**)
, there exists
\mathtt{n}\in\mathsf{SatTm}

such that

\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}\sigma}\rangle(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})\sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{n}}\rangle A
,
equivalently (by Definition
2.12
)

(\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}}\rangle(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n}))\sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{n}}\rangle A
.
Hence,
(\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}_{1}}\rangle A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{m}_{n}}\rangle A_{n})\sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{n}}\rangle A
where indeed
\langle\mathtt{\mathtt{n}}\rangle A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{\Diamond_{k}H}\rrbracket
and
\sigma
is a substitution living on
\Diamond_{n}H
and meets the no new variable condition.
∎

Finally, our central Realisation Theorem falls as a corollary of the results harvested this far:
from pre-realisers which are semantically justified by the Pre-realisation theorem
5.8
we extract potential realisers which are also syntactically designed through the Condensing theorem
5.14
to follow precisely the original structure of the modal formula.

Proof of Theorem
2.20
.

Suppose
\vdash_{\mathsf{IK}}G
.
By the Pre-realisation Theorem
5.8
, there exist
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\llangle\mathsf{F}{G}\rrangle

such that

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n}
.
By the Condensing Theorem
5.14
, there exists
A\in\llbracket\mathsf{F}{G}\rrbracket
and a substitution
\sigma
such that

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})\sigma\mathbin{\to}A
.
By the Substitution Lemma
2.13
,

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}(A_{1}\mathbin{\vee}\dots\mathbin{\vee}A_{n})\sigma

and so by modus ponens

\vdash_{\mathsf{JIK}_{\mathsf{CS}}}A
.
Let
r(G)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A
and
we have
{r(G)}^{\mathsf{f}}=G
by Proposition
5.11
.
∎

6
Conclusion

We have presented basic modular and modular models for the justification counterparts to
\mathsf{IK}
which let us derive an alternative semantic proof of the realisation theorem from
\mathsf{IK}
to
\mathsf{JIK}
.
This method could be further adapted on the one hand to
sublogics of
\mathsf{IK}
such as the constructive modal logic
\mathsf{CK}
and on the other to
extensions of
\mathsf{IK}

such as the other logics in the modal
\mathsf{IS5}
cube by following in the classical case
[
9
,
36
,
20
,
28
,
22
,
11
]
,
or the family of Geach/Scott-Lemmon logics
[
21
]
.
For example, the modal logic
\mathsf{IS4}
extends
\mathsf{IK}
with the following axioms.

\mathsf{t}_{\Box}

:

\Box G\mathbin{\to}G

\mathsf{t}_{\Diamond}

:

G\mathbin{\to}\Diamond G

\mathsf{4}_{\Box}

:

\Box G\mathbin{\to}\Box\Box G

\mathsf{4}_{\Diamond}

:

\Diamond\Diamond G\mathbin{\to}\Diamond G

Semantically, this means imposing
R
is reflexive and transitive in any model
(W,\leq,R,*)
.
In the corresponding justification logic
[
30
]
, we add the following axioms to
\mathsf{Base}
.

\mathsf{jt}_{\Box}

:

[\mathtt{t}]A\mathbin{\to}A

\mathsf{jt}_{\Diamond}

:

A\mathbin{\to}\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle A

\mathsf{j4}_{\Box}

:

[\mathtt{t}]A\mathbin{\to}[\mathtt{{!}^{\mathnormal{}}\mathtt{t}}][\mathtt{t}]A

\mathsf{j4}_{\Diamond}

:

\langle\mathtt{m}\rangle\langle\mathtt{n}\rangle A\mathbin{\to}\langle\mathtt{n}\rangle A

An adaptation to the basic modular models in Section
3
in line with models for the justification counterpart to
\mathsf{S4}

[
33
,
6
]
, and imposing
R
is reflexive and transitive, should be sufficient.
Similarly, we conjecture that one can find justification counterparts to the family of logics defined by extending
\mathsf{IK}
with
\Diamond^{k}\Box^{l}G\mathbin{\to}\Box^{m}\Diamond^{n}G
where
k,l,m,n\in{\rm Nature}
,
by using the frame conditions given in
[
35
]
.
The challenge could be defining additional operations on proof terms and satisfier terms.

Regarding sublogics such as
\mathsf{CK}
, the canonical model construction for modular models would need to be generalised to build over not only prime sets, but pairs of prime sets and
segments
following the methodology introduced by
[
40
]
and developed for all the logics between
\mathsf{CK}
and
\mathsf{IK}
by
[
23
]
.

Some variants of intuitionistic justification logic can be seen as extending the typed
\lambda
-calculus with reflective capabilities
[
4
]
. Applications of intuitionistic justification logic could extend to
\lambda
-calculi and type theory
[
10
]
,
and lead to more expressive modal
\lambda
-calculi, notably for type constructors corresponding to the
\Diamond
modality
[
39
]
.
