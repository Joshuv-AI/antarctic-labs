Generalization of Zeroth-Order Method for Quotients of Quadratic Functions
Jonas Bresch bresch@math.tu-berlin.de

Abstract
Optimization of quadratic functions and the quotient of those are relevant in subspace and iterative optimization methods. In this paper, the calculation of the generalized operator norm and extremal generalized Rayleigh quotient is considered. In contrast to recent works an unconstrained sampling approach on the entire sphere for the random search direction in each iteration is proposed. Furthermore, the link to zeroth-order methods for Riemannian first- and second-order optimization methods is provided in the sense that the Riemannian gradient and Hessian are estimated by the specific surrogates. Even though the tangent space is not used in this construction the optimal step size problem can be computed in a closed form. The subproblems of this and recent works are illuminated in the context of sub-generalized Rayleigh quotient problems on specific Gram matrices. Together the achieved theory allows to construct an accelerated algorithm which shows state-of-the-art behavior and outperforms recent works.

Introduction

The computation of extremal spectral quantities of linear operators under restricted access models
remains a fundamental challenge in applied mathematics, scientific computing, and data analysis.
Quantities such as the operator norm, generalized Rayleigh quotients, numerical radius,
and pseudospectral measures govern stability, transient amplification, and admissible step sizes
in optimization and inverse problems

In particular, for non-normal operators, eigenvalues alone fail to capture transient growth phenomena,
making Rayleigh-type extremal formulations and pseudospectral diagnostics indispensable

A central object in this context is the generalized Rayleigh quotient,
which generalizes the operator norm and appears in a variety of applications.
In statistics and machine learning, it underlies

define operators

In signal processing, related formulations arise in beamforming and detection theory

In inverse problems and PDE-constrained optimization, generalized Rayleigh quotients govern stability
constants and convergence rates when weighted norms or preconditioners are involved

[

Despite their importance, computing these quantities becomes highly nontrivial
when only black-box access to the operators is available.

Adjoint-free and matrix-free constraints.

In many modern applications, linear maps
A:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
B:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}

are accessible only through forward evaluations.
This setting arises, for example, in large-scale PDE solvers, time-stepping schemes,
tomographic forward models, and legacy simulation codes

In such scenarios, forming matrices, computing adjoints, or inverting operators is infeasible.
Moreover, discretized adjoints may be inconsistent with the forward operator,

,

These constraints rule out standard methods such as power-schemes,
Lanczos procedures, and Krylov subspace methods,
which rely on repeated adjoint applications or dense linear algebra

,

,

Relying on inexact adjoints

This has motivated the development of

which use only function evaluations and random perturbations

,

Recent approaches combine random search, sketching, and consensus-based dynamics

However, existing methods and novel, substantially improved algorithms

often rely on tangent-space sampling on manifolds,
require access to adjoint operators or metric information,
or employ heuristic curvature approximations.

Unconstrained sampling and zeroth-order quasi-Newton methods.

This work introduces a fundamentally different approach based on
sampling on the entire unit sphere.
Therefore, it can be seen as an unconstrained sampling approach compared to
[

,
9
,
8
]

Instead of drawing search directions from tangent spaces of a Riemannian manifold,
we sample directions globally on the unit sphere,
which removes the need for conditional sampling or metric-dependent constructions.
This seemingly simple modification has significant algorithmic consequences:
it decouples the sampling step from the operators,
simplifies implementation,
and enables unbiased zeroth-order estimators
for both gradient and curvature information.

Building on this sampling strategy, we develop a zeroth-order optimization framework
for generalized Rayleigh quotient maximization that operates entirely with forward evaluations.
The first component is a stochastic gradient estimator derived from random directional evaluations.
The second and main novelty is a
zeroth-order quasi-Newton method

in which the curvature model is given by an
unbiased estimator of the Riemannian Hessian

This distinguishes the approach from classical quasi-Newton methods,
which rely on secant conditions or deterministic approximations

[
18
,
2
]

Here, curvature information is obtained directly from randomized function evaluations,
yielding a principled second-order method in a fully derivative-free setting.

Preliminaries

In this section,

2.1
Optimization Problem

The optimization problem

\|A/B\|\coloneqq\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\;\frac{\|Av\|}{\|Bv\|}

(1)

for two rectangular matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}

and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times d}
,
which is considered in this paper,
is a generalization of the operator norm

\|A\|\coloneqq\|A/I_{d}\|=\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\nicefrac{{\|Av\|}}{{\|v\|}}

where
B=I_{d}
is chosen.
Here, we have to guarantee that
B^{\tT}B\succ 0
,
so that (
1
) is well defined.
Hence,
\ell\geq d
is a necessary assumption
on the dimension of the space of the domain and range of
B
.
The sufficient condition on
B

is equivalently given by
\det(B^{\tT}B)\neq 0
,

\rank(B)=d
, or
\ker(B)=\{0\}
,
whenever
\ell\geq d
.
Considering the (generalized) spheres
given by

\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1}\coloneqq\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\|v\|_{D}\coloneqq\sqrt{\left\langle{v},{Dv}\right\rangle}=1\},\quad\text{where}\quad D\succ 0,

and we write for the unit sphere,
i.e. for
D=I_{d}
,
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\coloneqq\mathbb{S}_{I_{d}}^{d-1}
,
yields

\|A/B\|^{2}=\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\;\frac{\left\langle{v},{A^{\tT}Av}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{v},{B^{\tT}Bv}\right\rangle}=\max_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\;\frac{\left\langle{v},{A^{\tT}Av}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{v},{B^{\tT}Bv}\right\rangle}=\max_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{B^{\tT}B}^{d-1}}\;\left\langle{v},{A^{\tT}Av}\right\rangle.

(2)

The latter is due to the scale invariance of the considered (squared) objective

f(v)\coloneqq\frac{\left\langle{v},{A^{\tT}Av}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{v},{B^{\tT}Bv}\right\rangle}=\frac{\|Av\|^{2}}{\|Bv\|^{2}}

(3)

from (
1
), respectively (
2
).
The function in (
3
)
is also known as the generalized Rayleigh quotient of
(A^{\tT}A,B^{\tT}B)
.
Furthermore,
(
1
) is a generalization
of the extremal generalized Rayleigh quotient computation

\mathcal{R}(C,D)\coloneqq\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left\langle{v},{Cv}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{v},{Dv}\right\rangle}=\max_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1}}\left\langle{v},{Cv}\right\rangle
,
where
C\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is an arbitrary, quadratic matrix
and
D\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}
is Hermitian positive definite,
considered in
[
9
]
.
Here, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\,\{X\in\mathcal{S}^{d}\mid\left\langle{v},{Xv}\right\rangle>0\;\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\}\subset\mathcal{S}^{d}\coloneqq\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\mid X=X^{\tT}\}.

More precisely,
we have the following relation

\|A/B\|^{2}=\mathcal{R}(A^{\tT}A,B^{\tT}B)
,
if

B^{\tT}B\succ 0
.
If we are not aware of the adjoint
B^{\tT}
of
B

[
12
,
46
,
19
,
7
]

then the computation and construction for solving
the extremal generalized Raleigh quotient
and hence our considered problem (
1
)
via the generalized
B^{\tT}B
-sphere, i.e.
\mathbb{S}_{B^{\tT}B}^{d-1}
,
from
[
9
]
can be not applied.
Therefore,
we focus on the optimization problem

\|A/B\|^{2}=\max_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\;f(v)=\max_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\;\frac{\|Av\|^{2}}{\|Bv\|^{2}},

(4)

where we are also interested in the set of maximizer.

2.2
Riemannian Optimization Techniques

The problem (
2
)
could be solved by Riemannian optimization methods
[
5
]

via the generalized
B^{\tT}B
-sphere.
Therefore,
we would relay on the tangent space of the (generalized) sphere
\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1}

at some sphere point
v\in\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1}
given by

\mathbb{T}_{Dv}\coloneqq\ker(Dh_{C}[v])=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\left\langle{x},{Dv}\right\rangle=0\}=\spa\{Dv\}^{\perp}\quad\text{for}\quad v\in\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1},

where
h_{D}(v)\coloneqq\left\langle{v},{Dv}\right\rangle-1

Hence
\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1}=h_{D}^{-1}(\{0\})

is a smooth, embedded,
(d-1)

Here,
for solving (
4
),
we relay on
D=I_{d}
such that
\mathbb{T}_{v}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\left\langle{x},{v}\right\rangle=0\}=\spa\{v\}^{\perp}

for
v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
The orthogonal projection onto the tangent space
for the generalized sphere
is then defined by

P_{Dv}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}_{Dv},\quad x\mapsto\Bigl(I_{d}-\frac{Dv(Dv)^{\tT}}{\|Dv\|^{2}}\Bigr)x\quad\text{for}\quad v\in\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1},

and simplifies for (
4
)
to
P_{v}(x)=(I_{d}-vv^{\tT})x
for
v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
We further relay
on the retraction
at some sphere point
v\in\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1}

defined by

R_{Dv}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{S}_{D}^{d-1},\quad x\mapsto\frac{v+x}{\|v+x\|_{D}^{2}}.

(5)

and simplifies to
R_{Dv}(x)=\nicefrac{{v+x}}{{\sqrt{1+\|x\|_{D}^{2}}}}

if
x\in\mathbb{T}_{Dv}
.
For solving
the optimization problem (
4
),
two possible iterative approaches are

and zeroth-order Riemannian ascent methods.
We roughly outline them in the following.

2.2.1
First-Order Riemannian Ascent Methods

The general construction
of first-order Riemannian gradient ascent methods

\grad f(v)=P_{v}\circ\nabla f(v),\quad v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

\nabla f(v)=\frac{2}{\|Bv\|^{4}}\bigl(\|Bv\|^{2}A^{\tT}Av-\|Av\|^{2}B^{\tT}Bv\bigr).

Plugging (

\nabla f(v)\in\spa\{v\}^{\perp}=\ker(x\mapsto(I_{d}-vv^{\tT})x)

The iterative algorithm breaks down to

4

A

The critical points of (

are therefore given by

\displaystyle 0=\grad f(v)

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\|Bv\|^{2}A^{\tT}Av=\|Av\|^{2}B^{\tT}Bv\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad(B^{\tT}B)^{-1}A^{\tT}Av=\frac{\|Av\|^{2}}{\|Bv\|^{2}}v

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad(B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2}A^{\tT}A(B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2}w=\frac{\|A(B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2}w\|^{2}}{\|w\|^{2}}w,

(8)

by a change of variables

Hence, the critical points

The generalized eigen spaces are defined by

G_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\mid A^{\tT}Av=\lambda B^{\tT}Bv\}

where

generalized eigen value given in the set of generalized eigen values

\displaystyle\spec((B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2}A^{\tT}A(B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\exists v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},A^{\tT}Av=\lambda B^{\tT}Bv\}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{d}\},\quad\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq...\geq\lambda_{d},

by the symmetry of
(B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2}A^{\tT}A(B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}

Therewith,
it is

\eqref{eq: prob}^{2}=\eqref{eq: prob sphere}=\lambda_{1}=\max_{\lambda\in\spec((B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2}A^{\tT}A(B^{\tT}B)^{-1/2})}\lambda\quad\text{and}\quad\arg\eqref{eq: prob}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}=G_{\lambda_{1}}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

2.2.2
Zeroth-Order Riemannian Ascent Methods

To avoid the adjoints
A^{\tT}
and
B^{\tT}
,
for calculating the Riemannian (
6
), i.e. Euclidean (

due to (

\widehat{\grad}_{1}f(v)\coloneqq\frac{f(R_{v}(\mu\tilde{x}))-f(v)}{\mu}\tilde{x},\quad\text{where}\quad\tilde{x}=P_{v}x,\quad x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}).

The numerical gradient is an unbiased estimator

meaning that

\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}[\widehat{\grad}_{1}f(v)]=\frac{1}{\mu d}\grad f(v).

\widehat{\grad}_{m}

in order

More precisely holds

\trace(\mathbb{C}\text{ov}[\widehat{\grad}_{m}f(v)])=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\grad}_{m}f(v)]-\widehat{\grad}_{m}f(v)\|^{2}\bigr]\in\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{m}).

The resulting ascent method is enrolled as in Alg.

Second-Order Riemannian Ascent Methods

For capturing even more information concerning the curvature of the considered objective,

Here, for the function of interest

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f(v)=\tfrac{2}{\|Bv\|^{2}}\Bigl[

\mathbb{E}[(\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{d_{k}})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{v^{k}\sim V^{k}}[\mathbb{E}_{x^{k}\sim\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\mid V^{k}=v^{k}}[(\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{d_{k}})^{2}]]=\tfrac{1}{d}\mathbb{E}_{v^{k}\sim V^{k}}[d_{k}^{2}\|\grad f(v^{k})\|^{2}]\to 0,\quad k\to\infty.

Due to the boundedness

,

such that by Cor.

Lemma

\tau=0

it suffices to take

Proposition

For the second assertion
