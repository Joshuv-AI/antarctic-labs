Abstract
. The main aim of the paper is to give a formula for computing the separation Łojasiewicz exponents for two real analytic set germs via the Newton–Puiseux expansions of their defining functions. Moreover, we present an effective exponent for the case of two real algebraic sets in terms of their degrees.

The Łojasewicz inequalities impressively appeared in the 50s years of last century to solve the question of L. Schwartz for distribution division
[
13
,
20
,
21
]
. They have many relations and applications to other branches of mathematics, such as research on local singularities of analytic functions
[
26
]
, the proof of Thom’s Gradient Conjecture
[
19
]
, study of infinitely dimensional version in partial differential equations
[
3
]
, applications in polynomial and tame optimization
[

,

]
, …

Let
A,B
be real semi-analytic subsets of an open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in A\cap B
. Then we have the following inequality
[
21
]
:

•

There exist
C,r>0
and
\beta\geq 1
such that

(1)

d(x,A)+d(x,B)\geq Cd(x,A\cap B)^{\beta},\text{for all}\ \|x\|\leq r,

where
d(x,X)
is the Euclidean distance of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the set
X
(
d(x,X)=1
if
X=\emptyset
).

•

The infimum of such exponents
\beta
is called
separation Łojasiewicz exponent
of semi-analytic subsets
A
and
B
(at the origin), it is denoted by
\mathscr{L}(A,B)
.

Note that the separation Łojasiewicz exponent
\mathscr{L}(A,B)
is a rational number
[
1
, Corollary 2]
. The Łojasiewicz exponent represents and compares the growths of functions on two sides of the inequalities
[
21
]
.

The Łojasiewicz exponents are used to study some important problems, such as computing some topological invariants
[
23
,
25
,
26
]
, investigating the singularity at infinity
[
8
]
, the study of the separation of real algebraic sets
[
4
,
18
]
proof of effective Nullstellensatz
[
14
,
15
]
, etc. There are many works providing computations and estimations of Łojasiewicz exponents
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
5
,
18
,
23
,
24
]
.

In this paper, we study the separation Łojasiewicz exponents of the zeros of the real analytic functions in two variables. More precisely, assuming
f
and
g
are real analytic functions in two variables, we give a formula to compute the separation Łojasiewicz exponent
\mathscr{L}(f^{-1}(0),g^{-1}(0))
in (
1
) in terms of the approximations of the Newton–Puiseux roots of
f\cdot g
(see Definition
2.6
and Theorem
3.1
). If
f
and
g
are polynomials, then we give an effective formula for the separation Łojasiewicz exponent.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Newton polygon relative to an arc

In this section, we recall the technique of the Newton polygon relative to an arc or sliding-technique due to Kuo-Parusinski
[
17
]
(see also
[
10
]
). This technique plays a key role in this article. Let
f:(\mathbb{K}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
(
\mathbb{K}
is
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{R}
) be an analytic function germ. Suppose that
f
is mini-regular in
x
of order
m
, i.e. in the Taylor expansion of
f
:

f(x,y)=f_{m}(x,y)+f_{m+1}(x,y)+\dots,

we have
f_{m}(1,0)\neq 0
, where
f_{k}(x,y)
is the homogeneous component of degree
k
.

For a
Puiseux series

\displaystyle x=\phi(y)=c_{1}y^{n_{1}/N}+c_{2}y^{n_{2}/N}+\cdots\in\mathbb{K}\{y^{1/N}\}

with
N\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
being positive integers, where
c_{1}\neq 0
. We define the order of a Puiseux series
x=\phi(y)
:

\mathrm{ord}\phi=\frac{n_{1}}{N}.

The series
\phi
is said to be real if all coefficients
c_{i}
of
\phi
are real. Let us define

M(X,Y):=f(X+\phi(Y),Y):=\sum c_{ij}X^{i}Y^{j/N}.

For each
c_{ij}\neq 0,
let us plot a dot at
(i,j/N)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and call it a
Newton dot
. The set of Newton dots is called the
Newton diagram
, They generate a convex hull, whose boundary is called the
Newton polygon of
f
relative to
\phi,
to be denoted by
\mathbb{P}(f,\phi).
Note that this is the Newton polygon of
M
in the usual sense.

Algorithm 2.1

(The sliding)
.

Input.

An analytic function germ
f:(\mathbb{K}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{K},0)
, mini-regular in
x
, and an initial Puiseux series
\phi_{0}(y)
. Set
k:=0
and
\phi_{0}:=\phi
.

Step 1.

Define

M_{k}(X,Y):=f(X+\phi_{k}(Y),Y)=\sum c^{(k)}_{ij}X^{i}Y^{j/N}

and consider the Newton polygon
\mathbb{P}(f,\phi_{k})
of
M_{k}
. Let
(0,h_{k})
be the lowest Newton dot on
X=0
and
E_{k}
be the compact edge of
\mathbb{P}(f,\phi_{k})
having
(0,h_{k})
as a vertex. Then

\operatornamewithlimits{ord}f(\phi_{k}(y),y)=h_{k}.

Step 2.

•

If
\mathbb{P}(f,\phi_{k})
has no Newton dot on
X=0
, then
f(\phi_{k}(y),y)=0
and the algorithm stops with output

\phi_{\infty}(y):=\phi_{k}(y).

•

If
\mathbb{P}(f,\phi_{k})
has a Newton dot on
X=0
, choose a nonzero root
c_{k}
of the edge polynomial

\mathcal{E}_{E_{k}}(z):=\sum_{(i,j/N)\in E_{k}}c^{(k)}_{ij}z^{i}

and set

\phi_{k+1}(y):=\phi_{k}(y)+c_{k}\,y^{\tan\theta_{E_{k}}},

where
\theta_{E_{k}}
is the angle of the edge
E_{k}
.

Step 3.

Increase
k:=k+1
and return to
Step 1
.

Output.

The sequence
\{\phi_{k}(y)\}_{k}
has diagram
\phi_{0}\rightarrow\phi_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\phi_{k}\rightarrow\cdots
which produces a Puiseux series
\phi_{\infty}
satisfying

f(\phi_{\infty}(y),y)=0.

The series
\phi_{\infty}
is called a
final result of the sliding of
\phi
along
f
. The above algorithm is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\phi
is not a root of
f=0
. Consider a series

\psi:x=\phi(y)+cy^{\rho}+o(\rho),

where
c\in\mathbb{K},0<\rho\in\mathbb{Q}
and
o(\rho)
denotes a Puiseux series of order greater than
\rho
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If
\tan\theta_{E_{1}}<\rho
or
\tan\theta_{E_{1}}=\rho
and
\mathcal{E}_{E_{1}}(c)\neq 0
then

\mathbb{P}(f,\phi)=\mathbb{P}(f,\psi),
and, therefore

\mathrm{ord}f(\phi(y),y)=\mathrm{ord}f(\psi(y),y).

(ii)

If
\tan\theta_{E_{1}}=\rho
and
\mathcal{E}_{E_{1}}(c)=0
then
\mathrm{ord}f(\phi(y),y)<\mathrm{ord}f(\psi(y),y)
.

Proof.

For a detailed proof, we refer to
[
9
]
. In fact, the special case where
\psi:x=\phi(y)+cy^{\tan\theta_{E_{1}}}
was proved in
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
. The lemma is then deduced by applying the special case (infinitely) many times.
∎

We define

\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f}):=\begin{cases}\max\limits_{j}\{\mathrm{ord}(\phi(y)-\beta_{j}(y))\},\ \text{$\beta_{j}(y)$ are the real Newton-Puiseux roots of $f$,}\\
1\ \text{ if $f$ has no real roots,}\end{cases}

where
V_{f}
denotes the zero locus
f(x,y)=0
of
f
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\phi
is not a root of
f=0
. Then

(i)

\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})\leq\tan\theta_{E_{1}}.

(ii)

Let
\psi
be a series of form

\psi:x=\phi(y)+cy^{\rho}+o(\rho),\ c\in\mathbb{R}.

Suppose that
\rho>\mathrm{ord}d(\psi,V_{f})
, or
\rho\geq\mathrm{ord}d(\psi,V_{f})
and
c
is generic. Then

\mathrm{ord}d(\psi,V_{f})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f}).

Proof.

(i) Assume by contradiction that
\rho:=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})>\tan\theta_{E_{1}}
, then taking a real Newton-Puiseux root
\beta(y)
such that
\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})=\mathrm{ord}(\phi(y)-\beta(y))
we obtain

\beta(y)=\phi(y)+cy^{\rho}+o(\rho),c\neq 0.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\mathrm{ord}f(\phi(y),y)=\mathrm{ord}f(\beta(y),y)=+\infty
, which is a contradiction.

(ii) Let
\beta
be any Newton-Puiseux root of
f
. Then

\psi(y)-\beta(y)=\phi(y)-\beta(y)+cy^{\rho}+o(\rho).

Since
\mathrm{ord}\left(\psi(y)-\beta(y)\right)\leq\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})\leq\rho
, it follows that

\mathrm{ord}\left(\psi(y)-\beta(y)\right)=\mathrm{ord}\left(\phi(y)-\beta(y)\right).

Therefore

\mathrm{ord}d(\psi,V_{f})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f}).

∎

The following lemma is similar to
[
10
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\mathrm{ord}\phi(y)\geq 1
. Then

d((\phi(y),y),V_{f})\simeq|y|^{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})},\text{ as }y\to 0

Proof.

(cf.
[
16
, Lemma 2.16]
). Let us consider the following two cases.

If
f=0
does not have any real Newton–Puiseux root, then
V_{f}=(0,0)
. Taking
\epsilon>0
small sufficiently such that
|y|<\epsilon
, we have

d((\phi(y),y),V)=d((\phi(y),y),(0,0))=\|(\phi(y),y)\|=\sqrt{(\phi(y))^{2}+y^{2}}.

Since the assumption
\mathrm{ord}\phi(y)\geq 1
, then

(2)

\displaystyle d((\phi(y),y),V)\simeq|y|^{1}\ \text{for all}\ |y|<\epsilon.

If
f=0
has some real Newton–Puiseux roots, then there exists
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small such that
f(x,y)
can be represented as follows

\displaystyle f(x,y)=u(x,y)\prod_{j=1}^{k}(x-\beta_{i}(y)),

where
u(x,y)
has no real roots and
|y|<\epsilon
. Hence

\displaystyle d((\phi(y),y),V)=\inf_{j}\|(\phi(y),y)-(\beta_{j}(y),y)\|=\inf_{j}|\phi(y)-\beta_{j}(y)|.

It follows that

\displaystyle\inf_{j}|\phi(y)-\beta_{j}(y)|\simeq|y|^{\max_{j}\{\mathrm{ord}(\phi(y)-\beta_{j}(y))\}}.

Therefore,
d((\phi(y),y),V)\simeq|y|^{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V)}
for all
|y|<\epsilon
. Combining this with (
2
), we deduce the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.2.
Approximations of Puiseux series

Let
x=\gamma(y)
be a Puiseux series in the following form:

\displaystyle\gamma(y)=a_{1}y^{n_{1}/N}+a_{2}y^{n_{2}/N}+\cdots+a_{s-1}y^{n_{s-1}/N}+c_{s}y^{n_{s}/N}+\cdots,

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{s}
is the first non-real coefficient, if there is one. Let us replace
c_{s}
by a generic real number
g,
and call

(3)

\gamma^{\mathbb{R}}(y):=a_{1}y^{n_{1}/N}+a_{2}y^{n_{2}/N}+\cdots+a_{s-1}y^{n_{s-1}/N}+gy^{n_{s}/N},

a
real approximation of
\gamma
. In case
s=+\infty,
let
\gamma^{\mathbb{R}}=\gamma.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{R},0)
be a real analytic function germ, which is mini-regular in
x
of order
m
. Suppose that
\phi
is not a Newton–Puiseux root of
f=0
and
\gamma
is a final result of the sliding of
\phi
along
f
. Then

\gamma(y)=\phi(y)+cy^{\tan\theta_{E_{1}}}+\text{higher order terms},

where
\mathcal{E}_{H_{M}}(c)=0
and
M(X,Y)=f(X+\phi(Y),Y)
. If
c\notin\mathbb{R}
, then by the definition of the real approximation (
3
), the real approximation
\gamma^{\mathbb{R}}
of the series
\gamma
also has the form

(4)

\gamma^{\mathbb{R}}(y)=\phi(y)+gy^{\tan\theta_{E_{1}}},

where
g\in\mathbb{R}
is generic. For
f\in\mathbb{K}\{x,y\}
which is regular in
x
, let
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
be the set of all real approximations of all the Newton-Puiseux roots of
f
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a Puiseux series
\phi(y)=\sum a_{i}y^{\alpha_{i}}
. For each positive real number
\rho
, the
\rho
-approximation
of
\phi(y)
is defined by series

\sum_{\alpha_{i}<\rho}a_{i}y^{\alpha_{i}}+cy^{\rho},

where
c
is a generic real number.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\phi(y)
be a non-real Puiseux series. Then the real approximation
\phi^{\mathbb{R}}(y)
of
\phi(y)
is the
\rho
-approximation of
\phi
, where
\rho
is the smallest exponent occurring in
\phi
with non-real coefficient.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
be any two distinct Puiseux series. The series
\phi_{1,2}
is called the
approximation of
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
if it is the
\rho
-approximation of
\phi_{1}
(also the
\rho
-approximation of
\phi_{2}
), where
\rho:=\text{ord}(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})
. Let
f\colon(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{R},0)
be a real analytic function germ, which is mini-regular in
x
. Let
x=\phi_{i}(y),i=1,\ldots,s
be the set of the Newton-Puiseux roots of
f
. We denote by
\mathcal{V}_{a}(f)
the set of all approximations
\phi_{i,j}
of
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}
such that
\phi_{i,j}
is a real Puiseux series. Note that
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{R}}(f)\subset\mathcal{V}_{a}(f)
if
f\in\mathbb{R}\{x,y\}
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\phi_{1}(y)=y^{\frac{3}{2}}-2y^{\frac{5}{2}}+3iy^{\frac{7}{2}}+\dots
and
\phi_{2}(y)=y^{\frac{3}{2}}-2y^{\frac{5}{2}}-3y^{\frac{7}{2}}+iy^{\frac{9}{2}}+\dots
. Then, the approximation of series
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
is
\phi_{1,2}(y)=y^{\frac{3}{2}}-2y^{\frac{5}{2}}+gy^{\frac{7}{2}}
, where
g\in\mathbb{R}
and generic.

3.
Separation Łojasewicz exponent of two real analytic subsets

Let
f,g\colon(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{R},0)
be reduced real analytic functions germs in two variables, which are mini-regular in
x
of order
m
. Put
V_{f}=f^{-1}(0),V_{g}=g^{-1}(0)
. Then, by the Łojasiewicz inequality (
1
), there exist
C,r>0
and
\beta\geq 1
such that

(5)

d(x,V_{f})+d(x,V_{g})\geq Cd(x,V_{f}\cap V_{g})^{\beta}\text{ for all}\ \|x\|\leq r.

Let
\varphi\colon[0,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a semi-algebraic analytic curve such that
\varphi(0)=0
. Then there is a rational number
\ell(\varphi)
such that

d(\varphi(t),V_{f})+d(\varphi(t),V_{g})\sim\left(d(\varphi(t),V_{f}\cap V_{g})\right)^{\ell(\varphi)}\text{ as }t\to 0.

Let
\mathscr{L}(V_{f},V_{g})
denote the separation Łojasiewicz exponent of
V_{f}
and
V_{g}
. Using the Curve Selection Lemma (see
[
22
]
), we can easily prove the following equality

(6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(V_{f},V_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi}\ell(\phi),

where the supremum is taken over all semi-algebraic analytic curves passing through the origin, which do not contain in
V_{f}\cap V_{g}
.

Suppose that the curve
\varphi
is parametrized by a Puiseux series
x=\phi(y)
. We can see that

(7)

\ell(\varphi)=\ell(\phi):=\frac{\min\{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f}),\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{g})\}}{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{\gcd(f,g)})}.

We define the number

(8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{f},V_{g}):=\max\{\ell(\gamma)|\gamma\in\mathcal{V}_{a}(f\cdot g)\},

where the set
\mathcal{V}_{a}(f\cdot g)
is defined in Definition
2.6
. Let us denote by
\overline{f},\overline{g}
the real analytic function germs defined by
\overline{f}(x,y):=f(x,-y)
and
\overline{g}(x,y):=g(x,-y)
, and set
\mathscr{L}_{-}(V_{f},V_{g}):=\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{\overline{f}},V_{\overline{g}})
. Now, we establish the formula for the separation Łojasiewicz exponent
\mathscr{L}(V_{f},V_{g})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f,g\colon(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{R},0)
be non-zero reduced real analytic function germs, which are mini-regular in
x
of order
m
. Then

\mathscr{L}(V_{f},V_{g})=\max\{\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{f},V_{g}),\mathscr{L}_{-}(V_{f},V_{g})\},

where
\mathscr{L}_{-}(V_{f},V_{g}):=\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{\overline{f}},V_{\overline{g}})
and
\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{\overline{f}},V_{\overline{g}})
is defined by (
8
).

First, we need some lemmas. Put
h:=\gcd(f,g)
, where
\gcd(f,g)
is the greatest common division of
f
and
g
. Let us consider the Newton polygons relative to any arc
x=\phi(y)
, they are
\mathbb{P}(f,\phi)
,
\mathbb{P}(g,\phi)
and
\mathbb{P}(h,\phi)
with the highest Newton edges
F_{1}
,
G_{1}
and
H_{1}
, respectively. Assume that
\mathcal{E}_{F_{1}}
,
\mathcal{E}_{G_{1}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{H_{1}}
are their associated polynomials. Let
\theta_{F_{1}}
,
\theta_{G_{1}}
and
\theta_{H_{1}}
be the Newton angles of
F_{1}
,
G_{1}
and
H_{1}
, respectively. It is easy to see that

(9)

\displaystyle\min\{\tan\theta_{F_{1}},\tan\theta_{G_{1}}\}\geq\tan\theta_{H_{1}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\ell(\phi)>\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{f},V_{g})
. Then

(i)

\tan\theta_{F_{1}}=\tan\theta_{G_{1}}.

(ii)

The polynomial
\mathcal{E}_{F_{1}}\mathcal{E}_{G_{1}}
has only one root, which is a real value.

Proof.

By contradiction we assume that
\tan\theta_{F_{1}}\neq\tan\theta_{G_{1}}.
Without loss of generality suppose that
\tan\theta_{F_{1}}>\tan\theta_{G_{1}}.
Let
\phi_{1,\infty}
and
\phi_{2,\infty}
be final results of sliding
\phi
along
f
and
g
, respectively. Then we have

(10)

\displaystyle\phi_{1,\infty}(y)

\displaystyle=\phi(y)+\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}y^{\alpha_{i}},a_{1}\neq 0

(11)

\displaystyle\phi_{2,\infty}(y)

\displaystyle=\phi(y)+\sum_{i\geq 1}b_{i}y^{\beta_{i}},b_{1}\neq 0,

where
\tan\theta_{F_{1}}=\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\ldots
and
\tan\theta_{G_{1}}=\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\ldots
.

Let
\phi_{1,2}
be the approximation of
\phi_{1,\infty}
and
\phi_{2,\infty}
. Then
\phi_{1,2}\in\mathcal{V}_{a}(fg)
and

\phi_{1,2}(y)=\phi(y)+cy^{\tan\theta_{G_{1}}}+o(\tan\theta_{G_{1}}),

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is generic. It follows from Lemma
2.2
that

(12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}d(\phi_{1,2},V_{g})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{g})\text{ and }\mathrm{ord}d(\phi_{1,2},V_{h})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{h}).

We will show that
\ell(\phi_{1,2})=\ell(\phi)
by considering the following two cases.

•

Case 1:
\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})\leq\tan\theta_{G_{1}}
. Then
\mathrm{ord}\ d(\phi_{1,2},V_{f})=\mathrm{ord}\ d(\phi,V_{f})
due to Lemma
2.2
. Hence
\ell(\phi_{1,2})=\ell(\phi)
.

•

Case 2:
\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})>\tan\theta_{G_{1}}
. Take a real Newton-Puiseux root
\gamma
of
f
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}(\phi-\gamma)=\mathrm{ord}\ d(\phi,V_{f}).

Since

\phi_{1,2}(y)-\gamma(y)=\phi(y)-\gamma(y)+cy^{\tan\theta_{G_{1}}}+o(\tan\theta_{G_{1}}),

it follows that
\mathrm{ord}d(\phi_{1,2}-\gamma)=\tan\theta_{G_{1}}
. Therefore,

(13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}d(\phi_{1,2},V_{f})\geq\tan\theta_{G_{1}}\geq\mathrm{ord}d(\phi_{1,2},V_{g}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\ell(\phi_{1,2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\min\{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi_{1,2},V_{f}),\mathrm{ord}d(\phi_{1,2},V_{g})\}}{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi_{1,2},V_{h})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{g})}{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{h})}=\ell(\phi).

The equality
\ell(\phi_{1,2})=\ell(\phi)
contradicts the assumption that
\ell(\phi)>\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{f},V_{g})
. This proves (i).

(ii) By (i) one has
\rho=\tan\theta_{F_{1}}=\tan\theta_{G_{1}}
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{F_{1}}\mathcal{E}_{G_{1}}
is the polynomial associated with the highest Newton edge of
\mathbb{P}(fg,\phi)
with the angle
\rho
. Suppose by contradiction that
\mathcal{E}_{F_{1}}\mathcal{E}_{G_{1}}
has two distinct roots
c_{1},c_{2}
. Let
\varphi_{i}=\phi+c_{i}y^{\rho}
for each
i=1,2
, and let
\varphi_{i,\infty}
be a final result of sliding
\varphi_{i}
along
fg
. Let
\varphi_{1,2}
be the approximation of
\varphi_{1,\infty}
and
\varphi_{2,\infty}
. Then
\varphi_{1,2}\in\mathcal{V}_{a}(fg)
and

\varphi_{1,2}(y)=\phi(y)+cy^{\rho}+o(\rho),

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is a generic number. Note that

\tan\theta_{H_{1}}\leq\tan\theta_{F_{1}}=\tan\theta_{G_{1}}=\rho.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that

\mathrm{ord}d(\varphi_{1,2},V_{f})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f}),\ \mathrm{ord}d(\varphi_{1,2},V_{g})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{g})

and

d(\varphi_{1,2},V_{h})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{h}).

Hence
\ell(\phi_{1,2})=\ell(\phi)
, which is a contradiction. The lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By the formula (
6
), it is obvious that

\mathscr{L}(V_{f},V_{g})\geq\max\{\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{f},V_{g}),\mathscr{L}_{-}(V_{f},V_{g})\}.

Suppose for contradiction that

\mathscr{L}(V_{f},V_{g})>\max\{\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{f},V_{g}),\mathscr{L}_{-}(V_{f},V_{g})\}.

Then there is a real analytic arc
\phi
passing through the origin and not lying the
x
-axis such that

(14)

\displaystyle\ell(\phi)>\max\{\mathscr{L}_{+}(V_{f},V_{g}),\mathscr{L}_{-}(V_{f},V_{g})\}

and the parameter forms of
\phi
is

(x=\phi(t),y=t)\ \text{or}\ (x=\phi(t),y=-t),\ \text{where}\ \phi(t)\in\mathbb{R}\{t^{1/N}\}.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\phi
can be parametrized by
(x=\phi(t),y=t)
.
By Lemma
3.2
, then
\theta_{F_{1}}=\theta_{G_{1}}
and the polynomial
\mathcal{E}_{F_{1}}\mathcal{E}_{G_{1}}
has a unique root
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Define

\tilde{\phi}(y):=\phi(y)+c_{0}y^{\tan\theta_{F_{1}}}.

Claim 3.1
.

We have

\ell(\tilde{\phi})\geq\ell(\phi).

Proof.

We first see that

\min\{\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f}),\ \mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{g})\}>\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f}\cap V_{g})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{h}),

because
\ell(\phi)>1
. Then

\tan\theta_{F_{1}}\geq\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})>\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{h}).

Applying Lemma
2.2
and its proof, one has

\mathrm{ord}d(\tilde{\phi},V_{h})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{h}),\ \mathrm{ord}d(\tilde{\phi},V_{f})\geq\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})\text{ and }\mathrm{ord}d(\tilde{\phi},V_{g})\geq\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{g}).

Hence
\ell(\tilde{\phi})\geq\ell(\phi).

∎

Consider Newton polygons
\mathbb{P}(f,\tilde{\phi})
and
\mathbb{P}(g,\tilde{\phi})
. The polygon
\mathbb{P}(f,\tilde{\phi})
has the highest Newton edge
\widetilde{F}_{1}
with angle
\theta_{\widetilde{F}_{1}}
and the associated polynomial
\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{F}_{1}}
; the polygon
\mathbb{P}(g,\tilde{\phi})
has the highest Newton edge
\widetilde{G}_{1}
with angle
\theta_{\widetilde{G}_{1}}
and the associated polynomial
\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{G}_{1}}
. Combining Claim
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
we obtain

Claim 3.2
.

If
\tilde{\phi}
is not a Newton–Puiseux root of
h=0
, then

(1)

\tan\theta_{\widetilde{F}_{1}}=\tan\theta_{\widetilde{G}_{1}}
;

(2)

The polynomial
\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{F}_{1}}\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{G}_{1}}
has only real root.

Let
\phi_{0}:=\phi
and

\phi_{1}(y)=\tilde{\phi}(y)=\phi_{0}(y)+c_{0}y^{\tan\theta_{F_{1}}}.

Then
\phi_{1}
is the unique sliding of
\phi_{0}
along
f
(as well as
g
and
h
). Applying this process, we obtain a sequence

(15)

\displaystyle\phi\to\gamma_{1}\to\gamma_{2}\to\dots\to\gamma_{n}\to\dots\to\phi_{\infty},

where
\phi_{k+1}
be the unique sliding of
\phi_{k}
along
h
for each
k\geq 1
. The series
\phi_{\infty}
is a real Newton-Puiseux root of
h
. It yields the following identities

\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{f})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{g})=\mathrm{ord}d(\phi,V_{h})=\mathrm{ord}(\phi-\phi_{\infty})=\tan\theta_{F_{1}}.

This implies
\ell(\phi)=1
, which is a contradiction. So, the theorem is proved.
∎

4.
Effective estimation for separation Łojasiewicz exponent

Suppose that
f
and
g
are polynomials of two real variables, then there is the following effective version of the separation Łojasiewicz exponent.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be polynomials of two real variables such that their degrees are not greater than
d
and
f(0)=g(0)=0
. Then there exist positive numbers
C
and
r
such that

(16)

d(x,V_{f})+d(x,V_{g})\geq Cd(x,V_{f}\cap V_{g})^{\frac{(2d-1)^{2}+1}{2}},\ \text{for all}\ \|x\|\leq r.

Proof.

(cf.
[
18
]
)
Put
F:=f^{2}+g^{2}
, then
\deg F\leq 2d
, where
d=\max\{\deg f,\deg g\}
. Moreover,
V_{F}=V_{f}\cap V_{g}
.

If
V_{f}\subset V_{g}
, then
\mathscr{L}(V_{f},V_{g})=1
. Assume that
V_{f}\not\subset V_{g}
. Applying the classical Łojasiewicz inequality to
F
, there exists
\alpha,c>0
such that

|F(x)|\geq cd(x,V_{F})^{\alpha},\forall x\in U\cap(V_{f}\setminus V_{g}),

where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a neighbourhood of
(0,0)
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(F)
be the classical Łojasiewicz exponent of the polynomial
F
, then, by
[
23
, Theorem 4.4]
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(F)\leq(2d-1)^{2}+1
. Hence, the effective optimal version of classical Łojasiewicz inequality is

|F(x)|\geq cd(x,V_{F})^{(2d-1)^{2}+1},\forall x\in U\cap(V_{f}\setminus V_{g}),

Note that
x\in U\cap(V_{f}\setminus V_{g})
, then

(17)

\displaystyle|F(x)|=|g(x)|^{2}.

Therefore,

(18)

\displaystyle|g(x)|\geq cd(x,V_{f}\cap V_{g})^{\frac{(2d-1)^{2}+1}{2}},\forall x\in U\cap(V_{f}\setminus V_{g}),

It is easy to see that there exists
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that

(19)

\displaystyle Cd(x,V_{g})\geq C^{\prime}|g(x)|.

Combining (
17
) with (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle d(x,V_{g})\geq Cd(x,V_{f}\cap V_{g})^{\frac{(2d-1)^{2}+1}{2}},\forall x\in U\cap(V_{f}\setminus V_{g}).

This implies inequality (
16
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The exponent in inequality (
16
) is sharper than the exponent in
[
18
, Corollary 8]
in the case of two variables, where
0\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is not necessarily isolated point of
V_{f}\cap V_{g}
.
