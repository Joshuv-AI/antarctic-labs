Complete interpolating sequences for Fock type spaces
Karim Kellay, Youssef Omari

Abstract
. We obtain a characterization of complete interpolating sequences in a class of Fock-type spaces with radial weights for which such sequences exist. Our criterion is formulated in terms of logarithmic separation and controlled perturbations of a reference sequence satisfying an Avdonin-type condition. This provides a geometric description of complete interpolating sequences and extends previous results of Borichev–Lyubarskii and Baranov–Belov–Borichev on Riesz bases of reproducing kernels in Fock-type spaces. It also yields explicit density criteria for sampling and interpolating sequences.

1.
Introduction

Sampling and interpolation problems play a central role in the theory of spaces of analytic functions.
Given a Hilbert space of analytic functions with reproducing kernel, a natural question is to determine which discrete sequences allow one to reconstruct functions from their values on the sequence and which sequences allow arbitrary interpolation.
Sequences possessing the first property are called
sampling sequences
, while those possessing the second are called
interpolating sequences
.
Sequences enjoying both properties are known as
complete interpolating sequences
.
The existence and structure of such sequences reflect a delicate interaction between the geometry of the space and the growth of its reproducing kernels.

A classical example is provided by the Paley–Wiener space of entire functions of exponential type.
In this setting the Kadets–Ingham theorem describes the stability of exponential Riesz bases under small perturbations of the integer lattice (see
[
16
,
28
]
).
More generally, in de Branges spaces complete interpolating sequences correspond exactly to Riesz bases of reproducing kernels
[
14
]
.
These ideas have strongly influenced the modern theory of interpolation in spaces of analytic functions.

Weighted Fock spaces provide another natural framework in which sampling and interpolation problems arise.
For the classical Gaussian Fock space, Seip and Wallstén obtained a complete description of sampling and interpolating sequences in terms of density conditions
[
25
,
26
]
.
In particular, although sampling sequences and interpolating sequences both exist in this space, no sequence can possess both properties simultaneously. Further results in
[
13
,
11
]
show that there are no sequences which are simultaneously sampling and interpolating, even when unbounded multiplicities are allowed.

A different phenomenon appears in Fock-type spaces associated with weights of slower growth.
In particular, Borichev and Lyubarskii showed that certain Fock-type spaces admit Riesz bases of reproducing kernels
[
12
]
.
Later, Baranov, Belov, and Borichev established a deep connection between these spaces and de Branges spaces
[
5
]
, providing a structural explanation for the existence of Riesz bases in this setting. More general weights, whose Laplacian is a doubling measure, were considered by Berndtsson and Ortega-Cerdà
[
9
]
and Marco, Massaneda, and Ortega-Cerdà
[
21
]
. Related questions for rapidly growing radial weights were studied by Lyubarskii and Seip
[
19
]
and in
[
10
]
. In this case, complete interpolating sequences do not exist. For small Fock spaces with slowly growing radial (logarithmic) weights, complete descriptions of complete interpolating sequences are available; see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
17
,
22
,
23
]
for various settings and extensions.

The purpose of this paper is to give a complete characterization of complete interpolating sequences in a class of weighted Fock spaces with slowly growing radial weights.
We show that, in this setting, the existence of such sequences is governed by a critical density condition.
In particular, the role of the reference lattice is played by a sequence whose geometry naturally reflects the underlying Fock-space weight.

We now introduce the Fock-type spaces and the associated notions of sampling and interpolation that will be used throughout the paper. Let
\varphi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
, and extend
\varphi
to the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
by putting
\varphi(z)=\varphi(|z|)
. For every
0<p\leq\infty
and every weight
\varphi
we associate the Fock spaces
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
of all entire functions
f
satisfying

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\varphi,\infty}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}|f(z)|e^{-\varphi(z)}<\infty,\quad p=\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\varphi,p}^{p}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)|^{p}e^{-p\varphi(z)}\,dm(z)<\infty,\quad 0<p<\infty,

where
dm
is the area Lebesgue measure. We assume that
\log|z|=o(\varphi(z))
as
|z|\to\infty
, ensuring that the space is not limited to polynomials.

For any
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we consider the linear maps

L_{z}\ :\ f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}\longmapsto f(z)\in\mathbb{C}.

In the case
p=2
, the boundedness of
L_{z}
and the Riesz representation theorem ensure that there exists a unique function
K_{z}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\varphi}
, called the reproducing kernel at
z
, such that

L_{z}(f)=\langle f,K_{z}\rangle,\quad f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\varphi}.

These reproducing kernels are central to the study of Fock-space structure; for sufficiently regular weights
\varphi(r)\gg(\log r)^{2}
,
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\varphi}
does not admit a Riesz basis of normalized kernels
[
10
,
12
]
. The classical Fock space
\varphi(r)=r^{2}
had been treated earlier by Seip
[
25
,
26
]
. By contrast, when
\varphi(r)=(\log r)^{\alpha}
with
1<\alpha\leq 2
,
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\varphi}
admits Riesz bases of normalized reproducing kernels supported on the real line
[
12
]
, making these spaces de Branges spaces.

We now introduce the notions of sampling and interpolation sequences. Let
\Lambda
be a sequence of
\mathbb{C}
.
For
0<p<\infty
, the sequence
\Lambda
is called a
sampling sequence
for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
if there exist constants
0<c_{\Lambda}\leq C_{\Lambda}
such that

c_{\Lambda}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{|f(\lambda)|^{p}}{\|L_{\lambda}\|^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{\varphi,p}^{p}\leq C_{\Lambda}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{|f(\lambda)|^{p}}{\|L_{\lambda}\|^{p}},\quad f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}.

The sequence
\Lambda
is called an
interpolating sequence
for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
if for every sequence

v=(v_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
such that

\left(v_{\lambda}/\|L_{\lambda}\|\right)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{p}
,
there exists
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
satisfying

f(\lambda)=v_{\lambda},\quad\lambda\in\Lambda.

For
p=\infty
, the sequence
\Lambda
is called
sampling
for
{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}_{\varphi}
if there exists
C_{\Lambda}\geq 1
such that

\|f\|_{\varphi,\infty}\leq C_{\Lambda}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|f(\lambda)|e^{-\varphi(\lambda)},\quad f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}_{\varphi}.

It is called
interpolating
for
{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}_{\varphi}
if for every sequence

v=(v_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
satisfying

\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|v_{\lambda}|e^{-\varphi(\lambda)}<\infty,

there exists
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}_{\varphi}
such that

f(\lambda)=v_{\lambda},\quad\lambda\in\Lambda.

Finally,
\Lambda
is called a
complete interpolating sequence
for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
,
0<p\leq\infty
, if it is both sampling and interpolating.

When
p=2
, the Hilbert space structure allows one to interpret these notions in terms of the reproducing kernels
K_{\gamma}
. Indeed, a complete interpolating sequence
\Gamma
corresponds precisely to a Riesz basis of normalized reproducing kernels

\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}:=\{K_{\gamma}/\|K_{\gamma}\|_{\varphi,2}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\varphi}
.

In the setting of general sufficiently regular weights, one can describe conditions ensuring the existence of Riesz bases of reproducing kernels. For a weight
\varphi
, set
\psi(t)=2\varphi(e^{t}).

Baranov, Belov and Borichev
[
5
]
proved that if
\psi
satisfies suitable regularity conditions (see (
1.1
) below),
then
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\varphi}
admits a Riesz basis of normalized reproducing kernels supported on the real line. The basis points are given by

\sigma_{n}=e^{y_{n}},\quad\psi^{\prime}(y_{n})=2n+2.

They also showed that if
\psi^{\prime\prime}
tends to infinity, then
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\varphi}
does not admit a Riesz basis of normalized reproducing kernels. See also the related works of Isaev and Yulmukhametov, where conditions on
\varphi
are established to study the existence of Riesz bases of normalized reproducing kernels in weighted Fock spaces
[
15
]
and the references therein.

1.1.
Reference sequence and complete interpolation sequences for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}

Under these assumptions, the geometry of the space is determined by a radial reference sequence. Throughout the paper, we assume that

\psi(t)=p\,\varphi(e^{t})

satisfies the above regularity conditions:

(1.1)

\psi^{\prime}(t)\to\infty,\quad\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)>0\text{ is non-increasing, and }|\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)|=O\bigl((\psi^{\prime\prime}(t))^{5/3}\bigr),\quad t\to\infty.

Before stating the main theorem, we introduce the reference sequence
\Sigma_{\varphi,p}
, which represents the ideal distribution of points in
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
, for
0<p<\infty
. Namely,
\Sigma_{\varphi,p}
is given by

(1.2)

\Sigma_{\varphi,p}=\{\sigma_{n}=e^{y_{n}}:\ \psi^{\prime}(y_{n})=pn+2,\ n\geq 0\}.

This sequence will serve as a model geometry for the space
\mathcal{F}^{p}_{\varphi}
.

We also define a logarithmic distance

d_{\log}(z,w)=\frac{|z-w|}{1+\min(|z|,|w|)},\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{C},

and say that a sequence
\Gamma
is
logarithmically separated
, if there exists
d_{\Gamma}>0
such that

\inf\{d_{\log}(\gamma,\gamma^{*}):\gamma,\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma,\ \gamma\neq\gamma^{*}\}\geq d_{\Gamma}.

For a sequence
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}
and
N
a positive integer, we define
\Delta_{N}(\Gamma)
in (
1.3
) to measure the average radial deviation from the reference sequence over
N
consecutive points.
The following theorem characterizes complete interpolating sequences in terms of logarithmic separation and bounded radial perturbation relative to
\Sigma_{\varphi,p}
given by an Avdonin-type condition.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of
\mathbb{C}
such that
|\gamma_{n}|\leq|\gamma_{n+1}|
and write
\gamma_{n}=e^{y_{n}}e^{\delta_{n}}e^{i\theta_{n}}
, for some real sequences
(\delta_{n})
and
(\theta_{n})
. Then
\Gamma
is a complete interpolating sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
,
0<p<\infty
, if and only if

(1)

\Gamma
is logarithmically separated.

(2)

\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n})\delta_{n}\right)
is bounded.

(3)

There exists a positive integer
N
such that

(1.3)

\Delta_{N}(\Gamma):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{y_{n+N}-y_{n}}\left|\sum_{k=n+1}^{n+N}\delta_{k}\right|<\frac{1}{2}.

The following result characterizes complete interpolating sequences in
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varphi}
. This result reveals the deep link between complete interpolation in
{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}_{\varphi}
and the Hilbertian structure of
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\varphi}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}
be a sequence and let
\gamma^{*}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma\cup\{\gamma^{*}\}
is a complete interpolating sequence for
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varphi}
if and only if the family of reproducing kernels
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}=\{K_{\gamma}/\|K_{\gamma}\|_{\varphi,2}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
forms a Riesz basis for
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\varphi}
.

To illustrate these results, we now consider the special case

\varphi_{2}(z)=(\log^{+}|z|)^{2}
,
for which a complete description of complete interpolating sequences for

{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\varphi_{2}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}_{\varphi_{2}}
was obtained in
[
4
]
.
For general
p\geq 1
, this was extended in
[
23
]
, while the case
0<p<1
was treated by Mironov in
[
22
]
.
Belov and Mironov
[
8
]
also provided a complete characterization of shift-invariant sampling in terms of lower logarithmic density.
The case
\varphi_{\alpha}(z)=(\log^{+}|z|)^{\alpha}
, where
1<\alpha<2
, was studied in
[
17
]
for
p=2
and
p=\infty
.
Baranov, Belov, and Gröchenig
[
3
]
, and more recently Baranov and Belov
[
2
]
, established a connection between Gabor systems and the space
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{\varphi_{2}}
.

Finally, if a Fock-type space admits a Riesz basis of normalized reproducing kernels

\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}:=\{K_{\gamma}/\|K_{\gamma}\|_{\varphi,2}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
with
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}
, then it coincides (with equivalence of norms) with a de Branges space (see
[
7
]
). All de Branges spaces that arise as Fock spaces were explicitly described in
[
6
]
.

1.2.
Density results

Let
{\mathcal{C}}(r,R)
be the annulus centered at the origin with inner and outer radii
r
and
R
,
{\mathcal{C}}(r,R):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:r\leq|z|<R\}
. For a given sequence
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
n_{\Gamma}(B)
the number of points of
\Gamma
contained in the set
B
. Let
\eta(t)=(\psi^{\prime})^{-1}(t)
. We then define the lower and upper
\varphi
-densities of
\Gamma
as

(1.4)

{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{-}(\Gamma):=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\inf_{r\geq 0}\frac{n_{\Gamma}\bigl({\mathcal{C}}(e^{\eta(r)},e^{\eta(r+R)})\bigr)}{R},

and

(1.5)

{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{+}(\Gamma):=\limsup_{R\to\infty}\sup_{r\geq 0}\frac{n_{\Gamma}\bigl({\mathcal{C}}(e^{\eta(r)},e^{\eta(r+R)})\bigr)}{R}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\Gamma
be a logarithmically separated sequence.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{+}(\Gamma)<\frac{1}{p}
then
\Gamma
can be completed in such a way to become a complete interpolating sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{-}(\Gamma)>\frac{1}{p}
then
\Gamma
contains a complete interpolating sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
.

(3)

If
\Gamma
is an interpolating sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{+}(\Gamma)\leq\frac{1}{p}
.

(4)

If
\Gamma
is a sampling set for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
, then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{-}(\Gamma)\geq\frac{1}{p}
.

Preliminary Results

2.1.
First evaluation estimates.

We begin with the following lemmas, which provide estimates for the norm of the evaluation map

L_{z}:f\in\mathcal{F}^{p}_{\varphi}\longmapsto f(z)\in\mathbb{C}.

These results will be used repeatedly throughout the paper.
An exact estimate of
\|L_{z}\|
will be given later (see Theorem
4.1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
p>0
we have

\|L_{z}\|^{p}\lesssim\frac{\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(\log|z|)\right)^{1/2}}{1+|z|^{2}}e^{p\varphi(z)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
, and define

F(r)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\frac{d\theta}{\pi}=\exp(\omega(\log r)).

Then we have

\|f\|^{p}_{\varphi,p}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)|^{p}e^{-p\varphi(z)}dm(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(r)e^{-p\varphi(r)}rdr=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\omega(t)-\psi(t)+2t}dt.

Let
u=\log|z|
. Using
\psi(t)=\psi(u)+\psi^{\prime}(u)(t-u)+O(1)
for
|t-u|\leq\varepsilon=:\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\right)^{-1/2}
, we get

(2.1)

\|f\|^{p}_{\varphi,p}\geq\int_{u-\varepsilon}^{u+\varepsilon}e^{\omega(t)-\psi(t)+2t}dt\gtrsim\ e^{-\psi(u)+u\psi^{\prime}(u)}\int_{u-\varepsilon}^{u+\varepsilon}e^{\omega(t)-\psi^{\prime}(u)t+2t}dt.

Thanks to the convexity of
\omega(t)
, the function

\displaystyle v(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{u-\varepsilon}^{u+\varepsilon}e^{\omega(t)-\psi^{\prime}(u)t+2t}dt

is increasing in
(0,\infty)
and hence for small positive
\kappa
we get

\int_{u-\varepsilon}^{u+\varepsilon}e^{\omega(t)-\psi^{\prime}(u)t+2t}dt\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{\kappa}\int_{u-\kappa}^{u+\kappa}e^{\omega(t)-\psi^{\prime}(u)t+2t}dt.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{\varphi,p}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\ \left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)\right)^{-1/2}e^{-\psi(u)+u\psi^{\prime}(u)}\int_{u-\kappa}^{u+\kappa}e^{\omega(t)-\psi^{\prime}(u)t+2t}dt

\displaystyle=\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)\right)^{-1/2}e^{-\psi(u)+u\psi^{\prime}(u)}\int_{{\mathcal{C}}(e^{u-\kappa},e^{u+\kappa})}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|\xi|^{\psi^{\prime}(u)}}dm(\xi).

Using, now, the mean inequality in
D(z,\frac{\kappa|z|}{4})\subset{\mathcal{C}}(e^{u-\kappa},e^{u+\kappa})
, we then get

\displaystyle\frac{|f(z)|^{p}}{|z|^{\psi^{\prime}(u)}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\int_{D(z,\frac{\kappa|z|}{4})}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|\xi|^{\psi^{\prime}(u)}}dm(\xi)\lesssim\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\int_{{\mathcal{C}}(e^{u-\kappa},e^{u+\kappa})}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|\xi|^{\psi^{\prime}(u)}}dm(\xi).

Finally, we have

|f(z)|^{p}\lesssim\frac{|z|^{\psi^{\prime}(u)}}{|z|^{2}}\int_{{\mathcal{C}}(e^{u-\varepsilon},e^{u+\varepsilon})}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{|\xi|^{\psi^{\prime}(u)}}dm(\xi)\lesssim\frac{\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\right)^{1/2}}{|z|^{2}}e^{\psi(u)}\|f\|_{\varphi}^{p}.

Hence

\|L_{z}\|^{p}\lesssim\frac{\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\right)^{1/2}}{1+|z|^{2}}e^{\psi(u)}=\frac{\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(\log|z|)\right)^{1/2}}{1+|z|^{2}}e^{p\varphi(z)}.

This completes the proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have for every positive integer

(2.2)

\|z^{n}\|^{p}_{\varphi,p}\asymp\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n})\right)^{-1/2}e^{(pn+2)y_{n}-\psi(y_{n})},

(2.3)

\left\|L_{e^{y_{n}}}\right\|\asymp\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n})\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}e^{\frac{1}{p}\psi(y_{n})-\frac{2}{p}y_{n}},\quad n\geq 0.

\displaystyle\|z^{n}\|^{p}_{\varphi,p}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{np}e^{-p\varphi(z)}dm(z)\asymp\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(pn+2)t-\psi(t)}dt\asymp\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n})\right)^{-1/2}e^{(pn+2)y_{n}-\psi(y_{n})}.

2.2.
Canonical products estimates

We begin with the following lemma

Lemma 2.4
.

We have

-p\sum_{k=1}^{m}y_{k}=-\psi^{\prime}(y_{m})y_{m}+\psi(y_{m})-\frac{p}{2}y_{m}(1+o(1))+O(1).

Proof.

If
f
is an increasing
C^{1}
- function, then

f(t)=f(m)+f^{\prime}(m)(t-m)(1+o(1))
, and

\displaystyle\int_{m-1}^{m}f(t)dt=f(m)+f^{\prime}(m)\left(\frac{-1}{2}+o(1)\right),

whence

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}f(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{m}f(t)dt+\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\sum_{k=1}^{m}f^{\prime}(m)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{m}f(t)dt+\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\int_{0}^{m}f^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{m}f(t)dt+\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)(f(m)-f(0)).

Applying this formula to the function
f(t)=(\psi^{\prime})^{-1}(pt+2)
, we get

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}y_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(\psi^{\prime})^{-1}(pk+2)=\int_{0}^{m}(\psi^{\prime})^{-1}(pt+2)dt+\frac{y_{m}}{2}(1+o(1))

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{m}(\psi^{\prime})^{-1}(pt+2)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{0}}^{y_{m}}x\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)\frac{dx}{p}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{m}}{p}\psi^{\prime}(y_{m})-\frac{\psi(y_{m})}{p}+O(1).

Thus, we obtain the desired result by (
2.4
) and (
2.2
), which completes the proof of the lemma.

Let

\Gamma:=\left\{\gamma_{n}:=e^{y_{n}+\delta_{n}}e^{i\theta_{n}}\quad:\ n\geq 0\right\}

We associate to
\Gamma
the canonical product

(2.6)

G_{\Gamma}(z):=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(1-\frac{z}{\gamma}\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

For every positive integer
N
, we define the following quantity, provided that the limit exists

(2.7)

\Delta_{N}:=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{y_{n+N}-y_{n}}\left|\sum_{k=n+1}^{n+N}\delta_{k}\right|.

Lemma 2.5
.

The infinite product in (
2.6
) converges uniformly on every compact set of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
to an analytic function
G_{\Gamma}
. Moreover, for every positive integer
N
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C=C(N,\varepsilon)\geq 1
such that the following estimates hold:

(2.8)

\frac{1}{C}\frac{\mathrm{dist}(z,\Gamma)^{p}}{(1+|z|)^{2+\frac{p}{2}+p\Delta_{N}+\varepsilon}}\leq\left|G_{\Gamma}(z)\right|^{p}e^{-p\varphi(z)}\leq C\frac{\mathrm{dist}(z,\Gamma)^{p}}{(1+|z|)^{2+\frac{p}{2}-p\Delta_{N}-\varepsilon}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

5.
Separated sequences

A sequence
\Gamma
is said to be
logarithmically separated
if there exists a constant
d_{\Gamma}>0
such that

\inf\{d_{\log}(\gamma,\gamma^{*}):\gamma,\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma,\ \gamma\neq\gamma^{*}\}\geq d_{\Gamma},

where

d_{\log}(z,w)=\frac{|z-w|}{1+\min(|z|,|w|)},\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{C}.

One of the main tools we rely on is the following Bernstein-type result (see Lemma
5.2
).
We begin with the following lemma, which shows that the inequality below allows us to prove that the sequence is a finite union of logarithmically separated sequences.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{n}\ :\ n\geq 0\}
be a sequence of
\mathbb{C}
such that
\gamma_{n}=e^{y_{n}}e^{\delta_{n}}e^{i\theta_{n}}
, for some real sequences
(\delta_{n})
and
(\theta_{n}),
and
|\gamma_{n}|\leq|\gamma_{n+1}|
. There exists
C(\Gamma)>0
such that

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{|f(\gamma)|^{p}}{\|L_{\gamma}\|^{p}}\leq C(\Gamma)\,\|f\|^{p}_{\varphi,p},\quad f\in\mathcal{F}^{p}_{\varphi},

if and only if
\Gamma
is a finite union of logarithmic-separated sequences.

Proof.

Let

\Sigma_{p}=\Sigma_{\varphi,p}=\{\sigma_{n}=e^{y_{n}}e^{i\theta_{n}}:\psi^{\prime}(y_{n})=pn+2,\ n\geq 0\}
,
the reference sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
. Consider

g_{\sigma}(z)=\|L_{\sigma}\|\,\frac{G_{\Sigma_{p}}(z)}{G^{\prime}_{\Sigma_{p}}(\sigma)\,(z-\sigma)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

We have
\|g_{\sigma}\|_{\varphi,p}\asymp 1
. For every
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we write
|z|=e^{t}
. If
y_{m}\leq t<y_{m+1}
, and suppose that
\mathrm{dist}(z,\Sigma_{p})=|z-\sigma_{m}|
. We have by (
3.5
)

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left|\|L_{\sigma_{n}}\|\frac{G_{\Sigma_{p}}(z)}{G^{\prime}_{\Sigma_{p}}(\sigma_{n})(z-\sigma_{n})}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\psi(y_{n})-\ell(y_{n})}e^{\ell(t)-2t}\frac{\mathrm{dist}(z,\Sigma_{p})^{p}}{|z-\sigma_{n}|^{p}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{|g_{\sigma}(\gamma)|^{p}}{\|L_{\gamma}\|^{p}}\ \gtrsim\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap D(\sigma,\varepsilon\sigma)}\frac{|g_{\sigma}(\gamma)|^{p}}{\|L_{\gamma}\|^{p}}\ \gtrsim\operatorname{Card}(\Gamma\cap D(\sigma,\varepsilon\sigma)).

to

. By Lemma
2.6
,
\Gamma
is a uniqueness set for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
under the condition
\Delta_{N}<\frac{1}{2}
, hence,
T_{\Sigma_{p}}
is injective. On the other hand, fo every
v=(v_{n})\in\ell^{p}
, consider the function

(6.1)

f_{v}(z):=\sum_{n}v_{n}\|L_{\gamma_{n}}\|\frac{G_{\Gamma}(z)}{G^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{n})(z-\gamma_{n})},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

The estimates of the function
G_{\Gamma}
ensure that the last series converges uniformly on every compact set of the complex plane to an entire function
f_{v}
, which satisfies the interpolation condition
{f_{v}(\gamma_{n})}/{\|L_{\gamma_{n}}\|}=v_{n}
, for every integer
n
. It remains to prove that
f_{v}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|f_{v}\|_{\Sigma_{\varphi,p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}\left|\sum_{n}v_{n}\frac{\|L_{\gamma_{n}}\|}{\|L_{\sigma_{m}}\|}\frac{G_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{m})}{G^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{n})(\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{n})}\right|^{p}.

The last quantity is finite, for every
v\in\ell^{p}
, if and only if the matrix

(6.2)

A_{n,m}:=\frac{\|L_{\gamma_{n}}\|}{\|L_{\sigma_{m}}\|}\frac{G_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{m})}{G^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{n})(\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{n})}

defines a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}
.

Recall the estimates of the function
G_{\Gamma}

\left|G_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{m})\right|\asymp\frac{|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{n_{m}}|}{|\gamma_{n_{m}}|}\prod_{k=0}^{n_{m}-1}\left|\frac{\sigma_{m}}{\gamma_{k}}\right|,\quad\left|G^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{n})\right|\asymp\frac{1}{|\gamma_{n}|}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\left|\frac{\gamma_{n}}{\gamma_{k}}\right|,

where the integer
n_{m}
is the unique integer for which
|\gamma_{n_{m}}|\leq\sigma_{m}<|\gamma_{n_{m}+1}|
.

Since
(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n})\delta_{n})
is bounded, there exists
M>0
such that
|\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n})\delta_{n}|\leq M
for all
n
.
From

y_{n_{m}}+\delta_{n_{m}}\leq y_{m}<y_{n_{m}+1}+\delta_{n_{m}+1},

the monotonicity of
\psi^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime}(y_{m})=pm+2
, we obtain

\psi^{\prime}(y_{n_{m}}+\delta_{n_{m}})\leq pm+2\leq\psi^{\prime}(y_{n_{m}+1}+\delta_{n_{m}+1}).

Hence

pn_{m}+\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n_{m}})\delta_{n_{m}}(1+o(1))\leq pm\leq p(n_{m}+1)+\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n_{m}+1})\delta_{n_{m}+1}(1+o(1))\\

and therefore

|m-n_{m}|\leq 2M.

In particular,
n_{m}=m+j
for some integer
j\in\left[-\frac{M}{p},\frac{M}{p}+1\right]
.
Let
x_{n}=y_{n}+\delta_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|A_{n,m}|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(x_{n})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{m})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{m+j}|^{p}}{|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{n}|^{p}}\frac{\sigma_{m}^{p(m+j)}}{|\gamma_{n}|^{pn}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{m+j}\frac{1}{|\gamma_{k}|^{p}}\right)\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n}|\gamma_{k}|^{p}\right)e^{\psi(x_{n})-\psi(y_{m})-2x_{n}+2y_{m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(x_{n})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{m})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{m+j}|^{p}}{|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{n}|^{p}}\times e^{\theta(n,m)},

where

\displaystyle\theta(n,m)

\displaystyle=\psi(y_{n}+\delta_{n})-\psi(y_{m})+\psi^{\prime}(y_{m+j})y_{m}-\psi^{\prime}(y_{n})(y_{n}+\delta_{n})-p\sum_{0}^{m+j}y_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+p\sum_{0}^{n}y_{k}-p\sum_{0}^{m+j}\delta_{k}+p\sum_{0}^{n}\delta_{k}.

By Lemma
2.4
, we know that

-p\sum_{0}^{m}y_{k}=\psi(y_{m})-\psi^{\prime}(y_{m})y_{m}-y_{m}\left(\frac{p}{2}+o(1)\right)+O(1).

It follows that

\displaystyle\theta(n,m)

\displaystyle=\psi(y_{n}+\delta_{n})-\psi(y_{n})-\psi^{\prime}(y_{n})\delta_{n}+\psi(y_{m+j})-\psi(y_{m})+\psi^{\prime}(y_{m+j})(y_{m}-y_{m+j})

\displaystyle\qquad+(y_{n}-y_{m+j})\left(\frac{p}{2}+o(1)\right)+p\left(\sum_{0}^{n}\delta_{k}-\sum_{0}^{m+j}\delta_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{n})\delta_{n}^{2}-\psi^{\prime\prime}(y_{m+j})(y_{m}-y_{m+j})^{2}\right)+(y_{n}-y_{m+j})\left(\frac{p}{2}+o(1)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+p\left(\sum_{0}^{n}\delta_{k}-\sum_{0}^{m+j}\delta_{k}\right).

And then

\displaystyle\theta(n,m)

\displaystyle=(y_{n}-y_{m+j})\left(\frac{p}{2}+o(1)\right)+(p+o(1))\left(\sum_{0}^{n}\delta_{k}-\sum_{0}^{m+j}\delta_{k}\right).

If
n\leq m+j
, then
\mathrm{dist}(\sigma_{m},\Gamma)=|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{m+j}|\leq|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{n}|
.

Recall that

\Delta_{N}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{y_{n+N}-y_{n}}\left|\sum_{k=n+1}^{n+N}\delta_{k}\right|.

Thus, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\left|\sum_{k=n+1}^{n+N}\delta_{k}\right|\leq(\Delta_{N}+\varepsilon)\,(y_{n+N}-y_{n}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{0}^{n}\delta_{k}-\sum_{0}^{m+j}\delta_{k}=\sum_{n+1}^{m+j}\delta_{k}\leq(\Delta_{N}+\varepsilon)(y_{m+j}-y_{n}).

Consequently

\left|A_{n,m}\right|^{p}\lesssim\exp\left(-\left[\frac{p}{2}-p\Delta_{N}-p\varepsilon\right]|y_{m+j}-y_{n}|\right)

if and only if

\left|A_{n,m}\right|\lesssim\exp\left(-\left[\frac{1}{2}-\Delta_{N}-\varepsilon\right]|y_{m+j}-y_{n}|\right).

A similar estimate holds in the case
m+j<n
,

\frac{|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{m+j}|^{p}}{|\sigma_{m}-\gamma_{n}|^{p}}\lesssim e^{(p+o(1))(y_{m+j}-y_{n})}.

Therefore,

(6.3)

\left|A_{n,m}\right|\lesssim\exp\left(-\left[\frac{1}{2}-\Delta_{N}-\varepsilon\right]|y_{m+j}-y_{n}|\right).

Finally, if
\Delta_{N}<\frac{1}{2}
then the matrix
(A_{n,m})
maps continuously
\ell^{p}
on itself.

Proof of Theorem

\Gamma

Note that

M

. Indeed

\displaystyle n_{\Sigma_{p}}({\mathcal{C}}_{m,M})

\displaystyle={\rm Card}\left(\left\{n\geq 0\ :\ mM\leq n\leq M(m+1)-1\right\}\right)=M

Consequently, there exist
\delta>0
and
s_{m}>0
such that the annulus

B_{m}={\mathcal{C}}(s_{m},s_{m}+\delta)\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{m,M}

contains no point from
\Gamma
.

The idea is to complete
\Gamma
by a sequence
\Gamma^{\prime}
(will be chosen later) in such a way that the sequence
\widetilde{\Gamma}=\Gamma\cup\Gamma^{\prime}
satisfies conditions of Theorem
1.1
. We denote the points of
\Gamma\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{m,M}
by
\gamma_{1}^{m},\gamma_{2}^{m},\cdots,\gamma_{l_{m}}^{m}
, for some
0\leq l_{m}\leq M-1
(where
|\gamma_{j}^{m}|\leq|\gamma_{j+1}^{m}|
), and write

\gamma_{l}^{m}=\sigma_{mM+l}e^{\delta_{mM+l}}e^{i\theta_{mM+l}},\quad 1\leq l\leq l_{m}.

Recall that each annulus
{\mathcal{C}}_{m,M}
contains at least one point from
\Lambda
for which no thing is associated (free).

Take now
N
a large integer, and consider the grouping of annuli :

{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}}_{k}\ :=\ \bigcup_{m=kN+1}^{kN+N}\ {\mathcal{C}}_{m,M},\quad k\geq 0.

It follows that, every set
{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}}_{k}
contains at least
N
free points from
\Sigma_{p}
, which will assign to
\Gamma^{\prime}
. We will construct such points in such a way that

(8.1)

\frac{1}{y_{(kN+2)M}-y_{(kN+1)M}}\left|\sum_{n=(kN+1)M+1}^{(kN+N+1)M}\ \delta_{n}\right|\ \leq\ C.

for some absolute constant
C
. Such construction implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y_{(kN+N+1)M}-y_{(kN+1)M}}\left|\sum_{n=(kN+1)M+1}^{(kN+N+1)M}\ \delta_{n}\right|\leq C\frac{y_{(kN+2)M}-y_{(kN+1)M}}{y_{(kN+N+1)M}-y_{(kN+1)M}}<\frac{1}{2}

for large
N
.Thus, from now on,
k
will be fixed.

The points of
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}}_{m}
will be chosen within the annuli
B_{m}={\mathcal{C}}(s_{m},s_{m}+\delta)
. Note that we could even place all missing points in a single annulus
B_{m}
and still obtain logarithmically separation sequence. However, in that case the separation
constant would depend on
\delta
,
M
, and
N
and may therefore be rather small. Let us consider all possible sequences

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\bigcup_{m=kN+1}^{kN+N}B_{m}

with separation constants uniformly bounded away from zero, and write the elements of
\widetilde{\Gamma}=\Gamma\cup\Gamma^{\prime}
as
\gamma_{n}=\sigma_{n}e^{\delta_{n}}e^{i\theta_{n}}
.
Note that for any
m
and
n=mM+1,\cdots,mM+l_{m}
the values
\delta_{n}
are already fixed. Moreover, since for these
n
the corresponding
\sigma_{n}
are in the same annulus
{\mathcal{C}}_{m,M}
. We have

\eta(pmM+2-\frac{p}{2})<y_{n}<\eta(pM(m+1)+2-\frac{p}{2})

and

\eta(pmM+2-\frac{p}{2})<y_{n}+\delta_{n}<\eta(pM(m+1)+2-\frac{p}{2}).

Hence

(8.2)

|\delta_{n}|<\eta(pM(m+1)+2-\frac{p}{2})-\eta(pmM+2-\frac{p}{2})

whence

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m=kN+1}^{kN+N}\sum_{n=mM+1}^{mM+l_{m}}\delta_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=kN+1}^{kN+N}l_{m}\left(\eta(pM(m+1)+2-\frac{p}{2})-\eta(pmM+2-\frac{p}{2})\right)

\displaystyle\leq M\left(y_{M(kN+N)}-y_{M(kN+1)-1}\right).

Now, choose all the points of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in the annulus
B_{kN+1}
. Then for

kNM+jM+l_{kN+j}+1<n\leq kNM+(j+1)M,\quad 2\leq j\leq N-1,

we have

y_{kNM+jM+l_{kN+j}+1}<y_{n}\leq y_{kNM+(j+1)M}

and the corresponding
\sigma_{n}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{kN+1,M}
and then

y_{(kN+1)M-1}<\eta(p(kN+1)M+2-\frac{p}{2})<y_{n}+\delta_{n}<\eta(pM(kN+2)+2-\frac{p}{2})\leq y_{M(kN+2)}.

Thus

\delta_{n}=\delta_{n}+y_{n}-y_{n}\leq y_{M(kN+2)}-y_{(kN+j)M+l_{kN+j}+1}\leq y_{M(kN+2)}-y_{(kN+j)M+1}.

Recall that we have at least
N
free point and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}\sum_{n=kNM+jM+l_{kN+j}+1}^{kNM+(j+1)M}\delta_{n}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}(y_{M(kN+2)}-y_{(kN+j)M+1})\leq N(y_{M(kN+2)}-y_{(kN+N-1)M+1}).

With this choice, and for for large
N\gg M
. we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=(kN+1)M+1}^{(kN+N+1)M}

\displaystyle\delta_{n}=\sum_{m=kN+1}^{kN+N}\left(\sum_{n=mM+1}^{mM+l_{m}}\delta_{n}+\sum_{n=mM+l_{m}+1}^{(m+1)M}\delta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N(y_{M(kN+2)}-y_{(kN+N-1)M+1})+M\left(y_{M(kN+N)}-y_{M(kN+1)-1}\right)<0.

Now, if instead we choose the terms of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in the annulus
B_{kN+N}
, we obtain for every
kNM+jM+l_{kN+j}+1<n\leq kNM+(j+1)M
,
0\leq j\leq N-3,

that

(8.3)

y_{kNM+jM+l_{kN+j}+1}<y_{n}\leq y_{kNM+(j+1)M}.

Moreover

(8.4)

y_{(kN+N)M-1}<y_{n}+\delta_{n}\leq y_{M(kN+N+1)}.

Hence

\displaystyle\delta_{n}=\delta_{n}+y_{n}-y_{n}

\displaystyle\geq y_{(kN+N)M-1}-y_{kNM+(j+1)M}.

This ensures that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=(kN+1)M+1}^{(kN+N+1)M}\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=kN+1}^{kN+N}\left(\sum_{n=mM+1}^{mM+l_{m}}\delta_{n}+\sum_{n=mM+l_{m}+1}^{(m+1)M}\delta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=kN+1}^{kN+N}\sum_{n=mM+l_{m}+1}^{(m+1)M}\delta_{n}-M\left(y_{M(kN+N)}-y_{M(kN+1)-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N-3}\sum_{n=kNM+jM+l_{kN+j}+1}^{kNM+(j+1)M}\delta_{n}-O\left(M\left(y_{M(kN+N)}-y_{M(kN+1)-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq(N-3)\left(y_{(kN+N)M}-y_{kNM+2M}\right)-O\left(M\left(y_{M(kN+N)}-y_{M(kN+1)-1}\right)\right)>0.

It follows that the
\gamma_{n}
may be chosen so that

\left|\sum_{n=(kN+1)M+1}^{(kN+N+1)M}\ \delta_{n}\right|<1=\frac{y_{(kN+2)M}-y_{(kN+1)M}}{y_{(kN+2)M}-y_{(kN+1)M}}\leq(y_{(kN+2)M}-y_{(kN+1)M}).

Proof of (
2
).

Suppose that
{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\varphi}(\Gamma)>\frac{1}{p}
, then there exists a large positive integer
M
such that

n_{\Gamma}({\mathcal{C}}(r,r+pM))>\frac{pM}{p}=M,\quad r>0.

This ensures that for every integer
m
, the annulus
{\mathcal{C}}_{m,M}
contains at least
M+1
points from
\Gamma
. Recall that it contains
M
points from
\Sigma_{p}.
Take
N
such that
N\gg M
and choose
j_{0}
an integer so that
3j_{0}M<N\leq 3(j_{0}+1)M
.

For
j_{0}\leq j\leq N-j_{0}
and
kNM+jM+1\leq n\leq kNM+(j+1)M
we choose in an arbitrary way
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{j,M}
and write them as

\gamma_{n}=\sigma_{n}e^{\delta_{n}}e^{i\theta_{n}}.

Then

(8.5)

|\delta_{n}|<\eta(pM(j+1)+2-\frac{p}{2})-\eta(pjM+2-\frac{p}{2})

and

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{N-j_{0}}\sum_{n=kNM+jM+1}^{kNM+(j+1)M}\delta_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{N-j_{0}}M\left(\eta(pM(j+1)+2-\frac{p}{2})-\eta(pjM+2-\frac{p}{2})\right)

\displaystyle\leq M\left(y_{M(N-j_{0}+1)}-y_{Mj_{0}-1}\right).

On the other hand, recall that we still have
N
free points from the sequence
\Gamma
in each
{\mathcal{C}}_{m,M}
. We now consider two possible choices of
\gamma_{n}
for the remaining indices, namely for

kNM+1\leq n\leq kNM+j_{0}M

and

kNM+(N-j_{0})M+1\leq n\leq kNM+NM.

\bullet
For the first choice.
Assign points
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{j,M}
to
kNM+jM+1\leq n\leq kNM+(j+1)M
and
N-j_{0}\leq j\leq N-1
.
On the other hand, since we have
N/3>j_{0}M
free points in
\displaystyle\cup_{j>2N/3}{\mathcal{C}}_{j,M}
, we choose
j_{0}M
points
\gamma_{n}
in
\displaystyle\cup_{j>2N/3}{\mathcal{C}}_{j,M}
, for
kNM+1\leq n\leq kNM+j_{0}M
. Then,

\displaystyle\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=y_{n}+\delta_{n}-y_{n}\leq y_{M(j+1)}-y_{kNM+1}\leq y_{M(N+1)}-y_{kNM+1}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{n=(kN+1)M+1}^{(kN+N+1)M}\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}\sum_{n=kNM+jM+1}^{kNM+(j+1)M}\delta_{n}+\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{N-1}\sum_{n=kNM+jM+1}^{kNM+(j+1)M}\delta_{n}

\displaystyle\leq j_{0}M(y_{M(N+1)}-y_{kNM+1})+O\left(M\big(y_{M(N-j_{0}+1)-y_{Mj_{0}+1}}\big)\right)<0.

\bullet

Second choice.
Choose
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{j,M}
for

kNM+jM+1\leq n\leq kNM+(j+1)M,\qquad 0\leq j\leq j_{0}-1,

and select the remaining
\gamma_{n}
so that
\gamma_{n}\in\bigcup_{j<N/3}{\mathcal{C}}_{j,M}
whenever

kNM+(N-j_{0})M+1\leq n\leq kNM+NM.

Arguing as above, we obtain that the corresponding sum of the
\delta_{n}
is positive.
This completes the proof of (
2
), as in the proof of (
1
).

Proof of (
3
) and (
4
)

It suffices to observe that the lower and upper
\varphi
-densities of a sequence
\Gamma
can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{-}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}\ \inf_{r\geq 0}\ \frac{n_{\Gamma}\bigl({\mathcal{C}}(e^{r},e^{r+R})\bigr)}{R\,\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{+}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{R\rightarrow\infty}\ \sup_{r\geq 0}\ \frac{n_{\Gamma}\bigl({\mathcal{C}}(e^{r},e^{r+R})\bigr)}{R\,\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)}.

The result then follows by applying Lemma
8.1
and comparing the density of
\Gamma
with that of the corresponding reference sequence.

If
\Gamma
is an interpolating sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
, consider the reference sequence
\Sigma_{(1+\varepsilon)\varphi,2}
, which is a sampling sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{(1+\varepsilon)\varphi}
with density

{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{+}(\Sigma_{(1+\varepsilon)\varphi,2})={\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{-}(\Sigma_{(1+\varepsilon)\varphi,2})=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{p}.

Then, by Lemma
8.1
and comparing the densities of interpolating and sampling sequences, we obtain

{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{+}(\Gamma)\leq\frac{1}{p}.

Similarly, if
\Gamma
is a sampling sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\varphi}
, consider the reference sequence
\Sigma_{(1-\varepsilon)\varphi,2}
, which is an interpolating sequence for
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{(1-\varepsilon)\varphi}
with density

{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{+}(\Sigma_{(1-\varepsilon)\varphi,2})={\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{-}(\Sigma_{(1-\varepsilon)\varphi,2})=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{p}.

Then, again by Lemma
8.1
and comparing densities, we obtain

{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}^{-}(\Gamma)\geq\frac{1}{p}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
