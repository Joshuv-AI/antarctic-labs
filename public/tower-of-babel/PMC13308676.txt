Profiles and Disease Burden of Patients with COPD Initiating Triple Therapy in China
Authors: Xin Li, Jie Cao, Sune Hao, Stephen G Noorduyn, Zhiliu Tang, Afisi S Ismaila, Iris Chen, Jiaxin Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo describe the patient profile and disease burden for patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) in China who initiated triple therapy.MethodsThis retrospective, observational cohort study used real-world data from the Tianjin database to identify patients with COPD who initiated triple therapy between 1 January 2019 and 30 June 2023. Index date was the first prescription for single-inhaler triple therapy (SITT; fluticasone furoate/umeclidinium/vilanterol or budesonide/glycopyrronium/formoterol fumarate) or multiple-inhaler triple therapy (MITT). Patient demographics, clinical characteristics, baseline medications, healthcare resource utilisation and costs were described.ResultsIn total, 7871 patients were included; 3582 received MITT and 4289 received SITT. In the overall population, the mean age was 65.8 years and 64.8% of patients were male. Most patients were non-smokers (95.6%) and had a visit to a Tier 3 hospital at index (89.8%). Hypertension (36.8%) and asthma (33.0%) were the most common comorbidities at index. Inhaled corticosteroid/long-acting β2-agonist (19.3%) was the most used treatment prior to initiating triple therapy. The median (interquartile range) number of COPD-related emergency department visits, inpatient admissions and outpatient admissions in the year prior to and including the index date was 1 (1), 1 (0) and 3 (5), respectively. The median COPD-related total direct medical costs were 2068 Renminbi per patient. The mean proportion of days covered (PDC) for baseline medications was 0.35. The median eosinophil count was 120 cells/μL.ConclusionThese results provide an overview of the profiles of patients with COPD in China. The high rates of COPD-related healthcare resource utilization and costs demonstrate the considerable burden of COPD in China, whilst the PDC results indicate medication adherence needs to be improved. These findings could help reduce the burden of COPD in China by supporting the planning and adjustment of patients’ treatment strategies.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a common respiratory disease characterised by persistent, often progressive, airflow limitation.1 It is one of the top three causes of death in China and accounted for 89.3 deaths per 100 000 population in 2021.2

Patients with COPD in China have been shown to have reduced quality of life.3 There were approximately 480 million cases of COPD globally in 2020, which are expected to rise to approximately 590 million cases by 2050.4 The increased prevalence of COPD could lead to increased healthcare resource utilisation (HCRU) and costs globally. A systematic literature review investigating the disease burden of COPD in China found that direct medical costs for patients with COPD ranged from $72–3565 per-capita per-year, which accounted for 33.33–118.09% of the local average annual income in China.3 COPD exacerbations substantially increase HCRU and costs; over 90% of patients with ≥3 exacerbations experience ≥1 hospitalisation compared with 60% of patients without exacerbations.5 Additionally, patients with COPD often have other comorbidities which can increase HCRU, costs and the risk of exacerbations.6

The Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) 2026 report recommends a step-up from dual therapy to triple therapy comprising an inhaled corticosteroid (ICS), a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA) and a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) for patients with ≥1 moderate or severe exacerbations in the previous year and blood eosinophil (EOS) counts of ≥300 cells/µL.1 Prompt initiation of triple therapy has been shown to improve outcomes of patients with COPD who experience exacerbations.7–9

Blood EOS counts are a clinical biomarker or indicator of how effectively COPD is managed as high blood EOS levels predict an increased risk of future exacerbations and an improved response to ICS-containing treatments.10 Therefore, before initiating triple therapy, it is important to understand the distribution of blood EOS levels in patients, to help analyse the profiles of potential patients at risk of exacerbations in current practice and predict the potential COPD-related HCRU burden among them.

The GOLD 2026 report also recommends single-inhaler triple therapy (SITT) over multiple-inhaler triple therapy (MITT), due to the more convenient dosing strategy of SITT, which has been shown to improve adherence.1 Patients receiving SITT have also reported improved outcomes compared with patients receiving MITT.11,12 A previous study conducted in China found that patients with COPD treated with SITT had improved adherence and persistence to treatment, and reduced moderate-to-severe and severe exacerbations, compared with patients receiving MITT.12

Despite the reported benefits of prompt initiation of triple therapy, many patients with COPD in China who are at risk of exacerbations have not been prescribed triple therapy. Whilst previous studies have investigated treatment patterns and triple therapy utilisation in patients with COPD in China, these patients were not required to have received triple therapy prior to study initiation; therefore, the profiles of patients specifically receiving triple therapy were unknown.13,14 Obtaining and understanding the profiles of patients who have been prescribed triple therapy in China can help to identify other patients who would benefit from promptly initiating triple therapy. The aim of this study was to describe the demographics, clinical characteristics and disease burden for patients with COPD in China who initiated triple therapy.

Methods

Data Source

This study used electronic medical record (EMR) data from the Tianjin database, which is one of the available pilot regional medical claims databases in the Tianjin area of North China. The Tianjin database has high-quality data and wide coverage. Hospitals and health centres in China are placed in one of three tiers; primary (Tier 1; general care), secondary (Tier 2; more comprehensive services) and tertiary (Tier 3; specialist care). The Tianjin database contains EMR data from approximately 15.6 million patients from 33 Tier 2 hospitals, 39 Tier 3 hospitals and several community health centres in Tianjin. A feasibility assessment identified 93 152 patients with COPD were included in the database between 1 January 2019 and 31 December 2021.

Study Design

This was a retrospective, descriptive, observational cohort study using real-world data from the Tianjin database to identify patients with COPD who initiated triple therapy between 1 January 2019–30 June 2023 (patient identification period) (Figure 1). The index date was the first date of a pharmacy prescription for the SITTs fluticasone furoate/umeclidinium/vilanterol (FF/UMEC/VI) or budesonide/glycopyrronium/formoterol fumarate (BUD/GLY/FOR), or the first date with all three triple-therapy components prescribed and the patient had all three components on hand, for patients treated with MITT. Study design.

Study Population

To be included in this study, patients must have been aged ≥40 years on the index date and had ≥2 EMRs with a diagnosis code for COPD during the study period (1 January 2018–30 June 2023). Patients were identified as having COPD if they had an International Classification of Diseases 10th revision J44 diagnosis code or Chinese diagnosis words of COPD with Chinese characteristics (including formal and informal terminology such as long and short names, and colloquialisms). Patients also had to have initiated FF/UMEC/VI or BUD/GLY/FOR for ≥30 consecutive days, or MITT for ≥30 consecutive days of overlap in the days’ supply of ICS, LABA and LAMA during the patient identification period. The final inclusion criteria required patients to have ≥12 months’ continuous enrolment prior to the index date.

Patients were excluded if they had ≥1 EMR with a diagnosis code for cystic fibrosis or lung cancer during the study period, ≥1 day of overlapping days’ supply of an ICS, LABA and LAMA during the baseline period, excluding the index date, and >1 pharmacy prescription for triple therapy on the index date.

Study Objectives

The primary objective of this study was to describe the patient profile (demographics, clinical characteristics and baseline medications) and disease burden (HCRU and costs) of patients with COPD who initiated triple therapy (MITT or SITT [FF/UMEC/VI or BUD/GLY/FOR]) on the index date. Demographics, clinical characteristics and disease burden were also described by patients’ triple-therapy prescriptions.

There were two secondary objectives of this study: to describe the distribution of proportion of days covered (PDC; assessment for adherence) for baseline medication, and patient profiles and disease burden by PDC levels (PDC analysis); and to describe the distribution of blood EOS levels, and patient profiles and disease burden by EOS levels (EOS analysis).

Data Analysis

For the description of patient profiles and disease burden across the objectives, categorical variables were summarised as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables were summarised as the number of observations, percentages, mean (standard deviation [SD]) and median (interquartile range [IQR], quarter [Q] 1, 3). The variables for the study outcomes related to patient profiles, disease burden and baseline medication are shown in Supplementary Tables 1 and 2.

Demographics and clinical characteristics were evaluated on the index date or during the baseline period if the information was not available at index. GOLD groups were based on the 2023 GOLD guidelines. Baseline medications were defined as the medication prescribed to manage COPD during the baseline period, and were identified as the latest medication used by patients for COPD management that covered a continuous treatment period of ≥30 days during the 12 months prior to the index date (Supplementary Figure 1). When there was more than one medication prescribed on the same day, all medications were included.

HCRU was calculated for emergency department (ED) visits, inpatient admissions and stays, and outpatient visits during the baseline period. Inpatient stays were measured in days. Healthcare costs were calculated during the baseline period using total direct medical costs that included expenses on general medical service, general treatment operation, nursing, pathological examination, laboratory tests, imaging examination, medications, blood transfusions and medical consumables.

Annualised exacerbation rates (any, moderate and severe) were summarised using two methods: the time-based annualised exacerbation rate, calculated as the total number of exacerbations divided by the total person-year of all patients; and the patient-based annualised exacerbation rate (also noted as per-patient per-year [PPPY]), calculated as the total number of exacerbations divided by person-year for each patient who had at least one exacerbation. Due to the study design, each patient had a fixed 12-month retrospective period, so that the total person-years was equal to the total patient number.

Baseline medication adherence was assessed as PDC, which was summarised as mean (SD) and median (IQR). PDC was calculated as the number of days with the baseline medication in possession during the period of the denominator, divided by the total number of days from the initiation of the baseline medication until the index date. Patients with PDC ≥0.8 and <0.8 during the treatment period were considered adherent and non-adherent, respectively, based on previous literature.11,15 The non-PDC population included patients from the overall population who were baseline medication naïve and were therefore considered as having no PDC rather than a PDC of 0. Patients with EOS counts of <100, 100–<300 and ≥300 cells/μL on the index date were placed in the low, moderate and high EOS groups, respectively.

The study period, including the baseline period, included the entire coronavirus disease (COVID-19) pandemic period in China. Previous studies have shown varying patterns in disease burden including hospital admissions, acute exacerbations and mortality for acute exacerbations between the pre-COVID-19 and COVID-19 periods.16,17 Therefore, an exploratory analysis was performed to estimate the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on disease burden, comparing the pre-COVID-19 (1 January 2018–31 December 2019), COVID-19 (1 January 2020–31 December 2022) and post-COVID-19 (1 January 2023–30 June 2023) pandemic periods. As the post-COVID-19 pandemic period only spanned the first half of 2023, the pre-COVID-19 and COVID-19 periods were also summarised by only using the first half of every year’s data to control the seasonal impact. The annualised number of exacerbations (any, moderate and severe) in this exploratory analysis was calculated using the time-based annualised exacerbation method only.

Patient Demographics, Clinical Characteristics and Rescue Medication

In the overall population, the mean (SD) age was 65.8 (10.35) years and 64.8% of patients were male (Table 1). Height and weight data were not described due to large amounts of missing data in the database. Most patients (95.6%) in the overall population were non-smokers. A total of 45.5% of patients in the overall population reported owning insurance (Table 1). Most patients in the overall population (89.8%) had a visit to a Tier 3 hospital at index; no patients had visited a Tier 1 hospital at index (Table 2). Impact of COVID-19 on HCRU, Costs and Exacerbation Rate

The total follow-up time was 1596, 6216 and 60 years for the pre-COVID-19, COVID-19 and post-COVID-19 pandemic periods, respectively. All-cause HCRU and costs stratified by the COVID-19 pandemic period are included in Supplementary Table 11. From the pre-COVID-19 period to the COVID-19 period, the annualised rate of COPD-related HCRU decreased for ED visits (0.19 to 0.13), inpatient admissions (0.19 to 0.14) and outpatient admissions (6.12 to 1.82) (Supplementary Table 12). The annualised total cost also decreased from 9849 RMB during the pre-COVID-19 period to 4847 RMB during the COVID-19 period (Supplementary Table 12). The annualised rate of any exacerbation decreased from 0.51 during the pre-COVID-19 period to 0.30 during the COVID-19 period (Supplementary Figure 2).

Discussion

The results of this study describe the patient profile and disease burden of patients with COPD who initiated triple therapy in China. Most patients who initiated triple therapy in 2021 and 2022 received SITT; possibly because previous studies have demonstrated improved adherence and a reduced risk of exacerbations with SITT compared with MITT.12

Patients had a mean age of 65.8 years, the most common comorbidities (in order) were hypertension, asthma and coronary heart disease, and 21% of patients experienced ≥1 acute moderate or severe exacerbation in the baseline period. A total of 84.1% of patients were identified as being in GOLD group A/B, which suggests that most patients in this study initiated triple therapy early. Of the patients identified as being in GOLD group A/B, 86.1% received SITT and 81.8% received MITT. The demographic and clinical characteristics of patients are generally consistent with previous studies of patients with COPD in China.14,18,19

The proportion of patients with ≥1 acute moderate or severe exacerbation during the baseline period in the MITT and FF/UMEC/VI subgroups was 24.3% and 34.2%, respectively. This is lower than what has been previously reported in a United States (US) study, which found that 61% and 60.3% of patients receiving MITT and FF/UMEC/VI, respectively, had ≥1 exacerbation at baseline.11

A total of 7.0%, 10.8% and 62.4% of patients in the overall population had ≥1 COPD-related ED visit, inpatient admission and outpatient admission, respectively, during the baseline period. A previous US study reported a higher proportion of patients with an ED visit (24.4% for patients receiving FF/UMEC/VI and 24.9% for patients receiving MITT), but a lower proportion of patients with an outpatient admission (36.2% for patients receiving FF/UMEC/VI and 35.8% for patients receiving MITT).11

The median annual individual COPD-related total direct medical costs were 2068 RMB. In Tianjin, the per-capita disposable income among residents was 53 003 RMB in 2022, while general healthcare expenditure only accounted for 3555 RMB of this income.20 Previous studies have shown that hospitalisation costs account for the largest share of COPD-related expenditure.3,21–23 This is consistent with our study, which found that COPD-related inpatient admissions accounted for 76.9% of the COPD-related total direct medical costs in the overall population.

Most patients in our study (90.2%) had poor adherence, which is similar to a previous study conducted in China.24 Treatment adherence is particularly low in patients with COPD receiving triple therapy, with reports of one in six patients remaining adherent to treatment over two years.5 Improved medication adherence has been shown to improve outcomes among patients on long-term medication.25 Our study suggests that there were fewer exacerbations of any severity in the year before triple-therapy initiation among adherent versus non-adherent patients, despite a numerically higher proportion of adherent patients having comorbidities than non-adherent patients. Adherent patients also incurred lower costs. It is also worth noting that a numerically higher proportion of patients in the non-PDC analysis group had no previous medication use than patients in the adherent group (36.6% and 15.2%, respectively).

Patients with high EOS counts (≥300 cells/μL) did not experience more exacerbations (of any severity) than patients with low counts (<100 cells/μL). Previous studies have reported varied results;26,27 future studies should explore the suitable threshold levels for EOS counts.

General limitations of this study include those commonly associated with database studies, such as missing data (in this case related to body mass index, which is an important risk factor for COPD). The Tianjin database does not include data from retail pharmacies. The density of health insurance-designated retail pharmacies in Tianjin has continued to increase, and many patients choose to purchase drugs from retail pharmacies. Subsequently, the lack of retail pharmacy prescription records led to a large number of patients with baseline medication gaps before prescription of triple therapy. Additionally, prescription costs and medical insurance reimbursement rates in different tier hospitals may have impacted which triple therapy treatment patients received, with lower distribution rates of triple therapies often reported in Tier 1 hospitals compared with Tier 2 and 3 hospitals.13 Medical insurance reimbursement rates may have also impacted HCRU. The COVID-19 pandemic may have limited HCRU, leading to the low estimates of disease burden for patients with COPD, especially as much of the patient identification period occurred during the pandemic. Additionally, most patients receiving SITT were identified during the COVID-19 period, whereas approximately 70% of patients receiving MITT were identified pre-COVID-19. Comparisons of exacerbation rates, baseline medications and HCRU between SITT and MITT should be interpreted with caution due to the confounding impact of COVID-19.

Conclusions

Patients with COPD who were prescribed triple therapy in China had a mean age of 65.8 years, 64.8% were male and over half of patients with reported EOS had EOS >100 cells/µL. Most patients who initiated triple therapy received SITT from 2021, which may have been a result of FF/UMEC/VI and BUD/GLY/FOR receiving approval for marketing in China at the end of 2019 and the start of 2020, respectively. The proportion of patients who initiated triple therapy with MITT continuously decreased from 2021. Patients who initiated FF/UMEC/VI and BUD/GLY/FOR had median total direct medical costs of 1265 and 1556 RMB, respectively. Most patients were identified as being in GOLD group A/B, suggesting that patients in this study initiated triple therapy early. By providing an overview of the profiles of patients who were prescribed triple therapy by physicians, the results of this study could support the planning and adjustment of treatment strategies for patients with COPD in China. However, treatment adherence and insurance coverage needs to be improved to further reduce the burden of COPD.
