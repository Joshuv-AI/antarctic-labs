Adjudicating Heart Failure Events in Participants Receiving Hemodialysis: Findings from Evaluation of Cinacalcet Hydrochloride Therapy to Lower Cardiovascular Events (EVOLVE) Trial
Authors: Guson Kang, Safa Abdalla, Patrick S. Parfrey, Renato D. Lopes, Uptal D. Patel, Gerard London, Glenn M. Chertow, Kenneth W. Mahaffey, on behalf of the EVOLVE trial investigators
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe Evaluation of Cinacalcet Hydrochloride Therapy to Lower Cardiovascular Events (EVOLVE) trial compared the effects of cinacalcet against placebo on major cardiovascular events in 3,883 hemodialysis patients with secondary hyperparathyroidism. In this manuscript, we describe heart failure events in EVOLVE and the role of centralized adjudication on heart failure reporting.MethodsA central clinical events classification committee (CEC) defined and adjudicated suspected heart failure events reported by the site investigators. The treatment effect was analyzed by intention to treat.ResultsOverall, 3,883 participants were enrolled, 1,366 heart failure events were reported by site investigators, and 40 potential events were identified independently by the CEC. Among site investigator-reported events, 653 (47.8%) were adjudicated by the CEC as heart failure endpoints. Of the 40 potential events identified independently by the CEC, 17 (42.5%) were adjudicated as heart failure endpoints. The effect of cinacalcet compared with placebo on time to first heart failure event using CEC-adjudicated and site investigator-reported events (10.6% and 12.2% for CEC-adjudicated events [relative hazard 0.82, 95% CI: 0.68–0.99] and 20.3% and 22.1% for site investigator-reported events [relative hazard 0.88, 95% CI: 0.77–1.01]) was similar, although CEC data included fewer events but nominally statistically significant results.ConclusionRoughly half of heart failure events reported by site investigators were not adjudicated as heart failure by the CEC. Site investigators failed to report additional events that were later adjudicated as heart failure endpoints. These findings suggest that, in the hemodialysis population, identifying and classifying heart failure events is challenging and highlight the importance of centralized adjudication to ensure specificity and consistency in clinical events ascertainment.

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease is the primary cause of death in the hemodialysis population, accounting for approximately 50% of deaths. Rates of cardiovascular events, including heart failure, and cardiovascular death, exceed those in the general population by several fold [1]. Heart failure is a major contributor to morbidity and mortality in the hemodialysis population and is present in over 30% of patients at initiation of dialysis [2].

Distinguishing acute heart failure from volume overload due to salt and water retention from insufficient ultrafiltration in the patient receiving hemodialysis is challenging. Both acute heart failure and volume overload typically present with pulmonary edema, jugular venous distension, and peripheral edema. The long-term prognosis is significantly worse in patients with comorbid heart failure and end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), with a reported median survival of 18–36 months, as compared to the general dialysis population, which has a median survival of 1.9–6.5 years in patients over age 50 [2–4]. Similarly, patients with heart failure and ESKD fare far worse than the general heart failure population, with 5-year survival as low as 12.5% compared to 46%–60% in patients with normal or near normal kidney function, according to recent Framingham data [4, 5].

Formal, systematic, standardized classification of clinical events has become standard practice in modern clinical trial design. Few studies, however, have explored the impact of the adjudication process on event reporting and assessment of relative safety and efficacy [6–15]. Due to its subjectivity and complexity, heart failure event reporting is assumed to benefit from adjudication, but no studies have described the adjudication of acute heart failure events in the hemodialysis population and there is no standard definition of acute heart failure events in patients receiving hemodialysis. Using data from the Evaluation of Cinacalcet Hydrochloride Therapy to Lower Cardiovascular Events (EVOLVE) trial, we described the adjudication process of heart failure events. We then assessed the impact of this approach on the treatment effect of cinacalcet versus placebo.

Methods

The EVOLVE trial was a global, multicenter, prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled trial that tested the effect of cinacalcet on death and major cardiovascular events in patients receiving hemodialysis with moderate to severe secondary hyperparathyroidism. The trial was sponsored by Amgen Inc. and supervised by an academic executive committee, which oversaw trial design and operations. Safety data and interim analyses were periodically reviewed by an independent data monitoring committee. All clinical end points were adjudicated by an independent clinical events classification committee (CEC) group at the Duke Clinical Research Institute. The study protocol was approved by Institutional Review Boards at all participating study sites and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT00345839). The design, rationale, protocol, endpoint definitions, and primary results of EVOLVE have been published [16–18]. The trial included 3,883 participants who were randomized to receive either cinacalcet (n = 1,948) or placebo (n = 1,935) and were followed for up to 64 months. The primary composite endpoint included death, myocardial infarction, and hospitalization for unstable angina, peripheral vascular disease (including revascularization and non-traumatic amputation), or heart failure.

Heart Failure Definition

The trial standardized the definition of a heart failure event using a combination of clinical, laboratory, radiographic, and invasive pressure monitoring criteria (online suppl. Material A; for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000553103). We provided site investigators with event definitions online and as hard copies in study materials. Criteria were periodically reviewed at in-person investigator meetings and Web-based teleconferences.

Data Collection

Site investigators submitted endpoint worksheets for any suspected heart failure events and were encouraged to do so even in equivocal cases. The worksheets collected information about the patient’s clinical presentation, including pulmonary rales, jugular venous distention, and chest radiograph findings, as well as which treatment modalities were used, such as unplanned mechanical fluid removal or intravenous inotropes (online suppl. Material B). We instructed site investigators to submit selected relevant medical records, including admission notes, discharge summaries, progress notes, diagnostic test reports, and baseline laboratory studies. All available information was to be sent to a commercial vendor (IQVIA, Durham, NC, USA) and collated into event packages and sent to the Duke Clinical Research Institute (DCRI) for review and distribution to physicians for adjudication. If, during review of the available medical records by DCRI physicians or staff, another possible endpoint was identified for which an endpoint worksheet had not been submitted, we contacted the site investigator to complete an endpoint worksheet and submit appropriate medical records so that the newly discovered potential event could be processed.

Event Adjudication

All heart failure event packages were reviewed independently by 2 physicians. If the physicians agreed that an event did or did not occur, the event was considered completed and the result entered into the trial database. If the physicians did not agree, the suspected event was reviewed by a committee of physicians and a final decision was made by consensus.

Statistical Analysis

We described the presenting clinical characteristics and interventions for events adjudicated as heart failure endpoints by the CEC using frequency distributions. We cross-tabulated heart failure events reported by site investigators and those endpoints reported by the CEC to examine their agreement. To characterize the differences between the CEC-adjudicated and site investigator-reported data, we compared baseline patient data among 3 different subgroups: (1) participants who had at least 1 heart failure endpoint reported by the CEC; (2) participants who had site-reported events but the CEC did not agree that a heart failure endpoint occurred; and (3) participants with no site-reported heart failure events or CEC-determined heart failure endpoints. We employed the Kruskal-Wallis test and the Pearson chi-square text to compare continuous and categorical variables, respectively.

We calculated the annualized event rate of heart failure in the 2 randomization arms based on CEC-adjudicated events and site investigator-reported events. To determine the relative hazard (cinacalcet versus placebo) of heart failure using CEC-adjudicated and site investigator-reported results, we employed proportional hazards (“Cox”) regression with stratification by site and whether the patient had diabetes. We fitted unadjusted and multivariable-adjusted models, with the latter accounting for the following baseline variables: age, sex, designated race/ethnicity, geographical region, tobacco use, history of coronary artery disease, cardiac arrhythmia, diabetes, dyslipidemia, heart failure, hypertension, retinopathy, transient ischemic attack, statin use at baseline, serum albumin, bone-specific alkaline phosphatase, LDL, and PTH concentrations, and nutritional vitamin D use at baseline. We used the Andersen-Gill modification of the Cox model to examine the relative hazard of repeated heart failure events. We considered p values <0.05 as nominally significant. We conducted all statistical analyses using SAS v9.3 (Cary, NC, USA).

Results

In total, 1,406 suspected heart failure events were prepared for CEC adjudication by aggregating events reported by site investigators (n = 1,366) and events identified on medical record review (n = 40). Of these 1,406, 670 (47.6%) were determined by the CEC to be heart failure endpoints.

Site investigators reported 97.2% of suspected events (1,366 of 1,406). The CEC did not agree with the site investigator in 713 of the 1,366 (52.2%) site investigator-reported events (Fig. 1; Table 1). The remaining 2.8% of events (40 of 1,406) submitted for adjudication were identified on medical record review and were not initially reported by site investigators. While site investigator- and CEC-reported annualized rates of heart failure trended similarly in both cinacalcet and placebo groups, site investigators reported significantly higher rates of heart failure (Fig. 2).

Clinical Characteristics Associated with Adjudicated Heart Failure Events

Table 2 shows differences in baseline characteristics among participants with and without at least 1 CEC-adjudicated heart failure event. Participants with adjudicated heart failure events had higher mean baseline systolic blood pressure than those with no reported events or with site-reported events but no CEC-adjudicated events (147 mm Hg vs. 142 mm Hg vs. 140 mm Hg, respectively, p < 0.0001). Participants with adjudicated heart failure events were more likely to have had a history of AICD implantation (2.0% vs. 1.5% vs. 0.7%, p = 0.01), coronary artery bypass grafting (12.2% vs. 9.7% vs. 6.5%, p < 0.0001), percutaneous coronary intervention (10.4% vs. 8.7% vs. 6.0%, p = 0.0006), myocardial infarction (19.2% vs. 17.6% vs. 10.8%, p < 0.0001), type 2 diabetes mellitus (42.3% vs. 39.4% vs. 26.5%, p < 0.0001), and a prior history of heart failure (39.8% vs. 30.7% vs. 20.0%, p < 0.0001). Participants with adjudicated heart failure events were more likely to have been treated with β-blockers (59.3% vs. 51.7% vs. 44.6%, p < 0.0001), calcium channel blockers (46.8% vs. 37.1% vs. 34%, p < 0.0001), nitrates (16.3% vs. 12.0% vs. 8.8%, p < 0.0001), aldosterone antagonists (1.4% vs. 1.0% vs. 0.4%, p = 0.01), and amiodarone (5.0% vs. 3.1% vs. 2.5%, p = 0.01). Treatment with angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers did not differ significantly among the 3 groups, nor did baseline electrocardiogram QRS duration (data not shown).

Presenting Clinical Features of CEC-Adjudicated Heart Failure Events

Figure 3 shows the clinical features evident among events that were adjudicated as heart failure by the CEC. Overall, 634 of 670 (94.6%) adjudicated heart failure events were associated with bilateral bibasilar rales or crepitus on presentation, 566 (84.5%) were associated with radiographic evidence of pulmonary edema, 163 (24.3%) were associated with jugular venous distention, and 21 (3.1%) had elevated central venous pressures transduced via central venous catheterization. These features were seen in similar proportion across treatment arms.

Presenting clinical characteristics in positively adjudicated heart failure events. JVP, jugular venous pressure.

The most common therapeutic intervention among participants with decompensated heart failure was unplanned mechanical fluid removal, which occurred in 574 (85.7%) participants (online suppl. Fig. 1). Invasive therapies such as mechanical ventilation (22.0%), pulmonary arterial catheterization (1.3%), or hemodynamic support with either inotropes (5.5%) or an intra-aortic balloon pump (0.4%) were less frequent. Medical therapy with intravenous diuretics was administered in 18% of events, and intravenous vasodilators were given at a similar frequency (15%). Again, the approach to managing heart failure events was similar in both treatment arms.

Evaluating the Effect of Cinacalcet versus Placebo on Heart Failure Events

Online supplementary Table 1 shows the relative hazard (cinacalcet versus placebo) of heart failure using CEC-adjudicated and site investigator-reported events. Site investigators reported more first and recurrent events than the CEC reported after adjudication. The treatment effect was similar for first HF events using site investigator vs. CEC data, although unadjusted analyses had a p value of 0.035 using CEC data compared with a p value of 0.075 using site-reported events.

Discussion

Our results show that more than half of events reported by site investigators were deemed not to represent heart failure as defined in the trial protocol. Analyses of treatment efficacy with cinacalcet compared with placebo were similar using site-reported and CEC-reported heart failure events. Even though the adjudication process resulted in fewer events, the treatment effect using CEC-adjudicated events reached nominal statistical significance, while the effect using site investigator-reported events did not. Overall, these data suggest that adjudication is associated with improved specificity for heart failure in ESKD when a rigorous definition is applied systematically across all trial participants.

Determining acute heart failure is challenging in the hemodialysis population because of confounding by volume overload due to salt and water retention. Both conditions typically present with pulmonary edema, jugular venous distension, and/or peripheral edema. The clinical overlap is further confounded by the increasing recognition of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, as well as confusion over the utility of traditional heart failure biomarkers such as NT-proBNP, making it more difficult to distinguish the two [19–23]. Clinical trial design in the hemodialysis population should be informed by our observation that heart failure events may be over-reported by site investigators. Refinement in approaches in site investigator reporting of heart failure events to increase specificity without losing sensitivity may improve efficiency of CEC efforts.

Collister et al. [24] recently reported results of a meta-epidemiologic systematic review of 561 randomized clinical trials in patients receiving dialysis among which 36 (6.4%) considered heart failure as a primary or secondary endpoint, with variable definitions. In no study was heart failure explicitly distinguished from volume overload secondary to diminished adherence or intensity of dialysis or ultrafiltration. In only five studies (fewer than one in seven, including EVOLVE) was heart failure independently adjudicated. In the Proactive IV irOn Therapy in hemodiALysis patients (PIVOTAL) trial of alternative intravenous iron administration strategies [24–26], adjudication of all cardiovascular events including heart failure yielded results that enhanced the apparent efficacy of the proactive iron administration strategy, providing another example of where – in all likelihood – power was enhanced despite documentation of fewer overall events.

These analyses have several limitations. Investigators were encouraged to report any suspected heart failure event, even in equivocal cases, which undoubtedly contributed to the observed event over-reporting. The trial was neither designed nor powered to detect treatment differences for heart failure or any of the individual components of the primary composite endpoint. With respect to the current analysis, a great deal of time and effort was devoted to establishing rigorous criteria to define heart failure in this setting. We were successful in establishing a more specific definition, in that roughly half of site investigator-reported events were adjudicated negative. Since we did not have routine echocardiography or other objective methods to assess the presence or absence of structural or functional cardiac disease, we cannot precisely determine the sensitivity and specificity of our heart failure event criteria. Additional validation efforts may help to further refine assessment of heart failure in patients receiving hemodialysis who participate in cardiovascular clinical trials. We did not track the reason(s) why the CEC and site investigators disagreed. Doing so might have allowed us to understand how site investigator reporting can be improved in the future. Finally, EVOLVE was conducted more than a decade ago. Diagnostic approaches and therapeutic strategies in patients with heart failure but without ESKD have changed, although as care becomes more complex, 1 might anticipate even greater value of adjudication in clinical trials.

In summary, using data from the EVOLVE clinical trial conducted in the hemodialysis population, our analyses suggest that identifying and classifying heart failure events by investigators is challenging and highlight the importance of a centralized adjudication process to assure high specificity and consistency in clinical events ascertainment. We endorse the development of uniform definitions of heart failure in patients with ESKD and adjudication of heart failure events in clinical trials wherever feasible.
