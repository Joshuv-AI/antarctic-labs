Sliding mode control gain optimization for a robot arm manipulator using an improved stochastic framework
Authors: Hamza Tahiri, Mohamed Amine Tahiri, Mhamed Sayyouri, Eman Abdullah Aldakheel, Doaa Sami Khafaga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We present an optimization–control framework for trajectory tracking of a 3-DoF manipulator, where an improved Stochastic Paint Optimizer (SPO-CL1) automatically tunes the gains of a sliding mode controller. Designed to address robotic challenges such as nonlinearities, couplings, and disturbances, the approach aims to achieve high tracking accuracy, rapid convergence, reduced chattering, and limited actuation effort, while remaining simple and robust to parameter variations. SPO-CL1 integrates three structure-aware enhancements into the original SPO architecture: chaotic initialization via the Chebyshev map to diversify the initial population, Opposition-Based Learning applied after the clustering phase to accelerate convergence, and periodic Lévy flight perturbations targeting the best solution to escape local optima. The SMC gains are automatically tuned by minimizing the Integrated Squared Error cost function J = ∫₀ᵀ eᵀ(t)e(t)dt. Two phases of validation were conducted. First, SPO-CL1 is benchmarked against eleven algorithms including recent hybrid variants (IGWO, EWOA, MHHO, HSMA) on the CEC-2022 suite, achieving the best Friedman rank of 1.83 and statistically significant superiority confirmed by Wilcoxon rank-sum tests (α = 0.05). Second, a path planning experiment on a Lemniscate of Bernoulli trajectory demonstrates that SPO-CL1 achieves the lowest ISE of 1.51 × 10⁻⁴ with near-zero inter-run variance, the fastest tracking error convergence, and the tightest end-effector trajectory among all twelve compared algorithms. These results confirm that SPO-CL1 is a competitive and reliable approach for automatic SMC gain tuning in complex robotic applications.

Introduction

Today robotics has emerged as a key area for business, healthcare, and society1. Robust control models are necessary for a robot to carry out precise manipulation2 or collaborate3. Optimisation lies at the heart of the process: it adjusts parameters, improves the accuracy of trajectories and enhances reliability in uncertain environments4. Traditional tuning methods, whether analytical or trial-and-error5,6, reveal their limitations when faced with non-linearities and disturbances7. Recent work has further demonstrated the effectiveness of metaheuristic-optimized path planning for 6-DoF robotic arms using B-spline representations8. The control of robotic arms has evolved significantly, from classical kinematic approaches to advanced optimization-based frameworks9. With robots operating in constrained environments, adaptive meta-heuristics have become essential for real-time control10. Inspired by living organisms, they strike a good balance between global exploration and local exploitation. Over the past decade, numerous metaheuristics have been applied in swarm robotics (PSO7, GWO11, HHO12), bio-inspired approaches (CO13, ZO14), and hybrid methods incorporating chaos15 or machine learning16. Their effectiveness has been established in trajectory planning, control law tuning (PID, SMC, LQR) and energy optimisation. The advent of high-performance computing (FPGA, GPU) is accelerating their real-time deployment. In this context, we propose to evaluate an improved version of the Stochastic Paint Optimizer (SPO)17, enhanced with chaotic mechanisms, to tune a sliding-mode controller on a 3-degree-of-freedom manipulator arm18. The objective is twofold: on the one hand, to demonstrate the competitive advantage of the proposed metaheuristic approach relative to conventional tuning methods in terms of accuracy, convergence speed and robustness to disturbances; on the other hand, to make a concrete contribution to the field of robotics by highlighting the transferability of these methods to real-world applications (industrial robots, autonomous vehicles19, medical devices). This work contributes to this dynamic by proposing an improved metaheuristic framework for a demanding robotic control problem, with a detailed comparative analysis against recognized reference algorithms and by providing a detailed comparative analysis with respect to reference algorithms recognised in the literature. Current Research on Improved Metaheuristic Algorithms and Their Uses.

Despite the growing body of work on metaheuristic-enhanced SMC for robotic manipulators, a critical examination of the recent literature reveals persistent limitations that the present work explicitly addresses. Azeez et al26. applied an enhanced Artificial Bee Colony to optimize a PID-SMC for a 3-DoF manipulator but restricted their comparison to two algorithms and a circular trajectory. At the control design level, recent works have proposed two-stage hybrid approaches combining optimal control with integral SMC for robotic manipulators under load uncertainties27, but these methods rely on manual or analytical parameter selection rather than metaheuristic optimization, while Ibrahim et al28. benchmarked a Golden Jackal Optimization against six competitors for PID tuning without addressing SMC gain optimization or chattering. Elgohr et al29. compared PSO and WOA for path planning of a 6-DoF KUKA KR4 R600 manipulator using a three-phase pipeline combining quintic trajectory generation, potential-field obstacle management, and metaheuristic optimization, but restricted their comparison to only two algorithms without statistical significance testing, and focused exclusively on path length minimization rather than SMC gain tuning or tracking error. Elgohr et al30. proposed a hybrid WOA-GA optimizer for 6-DoF trajectory optimization, achieving up to 44% faster reachability and 15% lower energy consumption, but limited their comparison to three algorithms without statistical testing and did not address SMC gain tuning. Elgohr et al31. applied WOA to KUKA KR4 R600 path planning, reducing travel distance by over 30%, but compared only two baseline methods without statistical validation. In the SMC-specific domain, Parra-Ocampo et al27. demonstrated that chaotic initialization meaningfully improves tuning convergence, yet their framework incorporates neither OBL nor Lévy strategies. Wei et al4. went further by combining both Lévy flights and OBL within a crayfish optimizer but confined their validation to a single engineering domain without any statistical significance testing. Across these and related works, three structural limitations recur: comparisons rarely exceed six algorithms, reference trajectories remain geometrically simple, and the ISE cost function is stated informally, undermining reproducibility. The present work addresses all three gaps simultaneously: SPO-CL1 integrates Chebyshev chaotic initialization, OBL, and Lévy flights into a single coherent framework, is benchmarked against eleven algorithms using Wilcoxon rank-sum and Friedman tests and is validated on a Lemniscate of Bernoulli trajectory with the explicit cost function J = ∫₀ᵀ eᵀe dt. The following section presents the dynamic modelling of the 3-DoF planar manipulator, which serves as the foundation for the SMC design and the optimization framework developed in subsequent sections.

Robot modelling

Assuming each segment of the arm to be rigid and uniform, and the joints to be perfectly frictionless, the aim is to derive the equations of motion from the Lagrangian formulation. The dynamic modelling26,32 then serves as the basis for designing and optimising the sliding mode control (SMC), whose parameters are determined using the SPO-CL1 algorithm. By minimising the overall dynamic error through appropriate adjustment of the parameters λ₁, λ₂, λ₃, k₁, k₂, k₃, trajectory tracking performance is significantly improved by this method. This section details the construction of the dynamic model with the physical characteristics shown in Table 2. The Lagrange technique is used to establish the equations of motion when the mechanical assumptions are established, the generalized coordinates are determined, and the kinetic and potential energy of each link is represented. The optimisation focuses on the parameters λ₁, λ₂, λ₃ (sliding surface parameters) and k₁, k₂, k₃ (switching gains), with the aim of reducing the integrated squared error (ISE) J = ∫₀ᵀ eᵀe dt, which results in significantly improved trajectory tracking.

The original stochastic paint optimizer algorithm (SPO)

General presentation

Stochastic Paint Optimizer (SPO)17 is an innovative population metaheuristic, inspired by artistic color mixing techniques. In this approach, the search space is assimilated to a painting canvas, with solutions represented by “paintings” composed of continuous variables assimilated to “colors”. The algorithm takes advantage of chromatic theory to classify these solutions into three distinct categories, enabling the search to be channeled between targeted exploitation and in-depth exploration.

Initialization based on chaotic maps

Chaos theory has gained increasing attention across scientific and engineering domains due to its sensitivity to initial conditions, non-periodicity, and ergodic properties. Nowadays, chaos theory is applied in many fields, especially cryptography and systems optimization, because of its capacity to simulate intricate, non-linear, and unpredictable behavior. Chaotic maps are used in cryptography to provide extremely safe keys. Their extreme sensitivity to initial conditions and their non-periodic behaviour make the sequences generated virtually impossible to predict or reproduce, which considerably enhances the robustness of security mechanisms. In line with recent work, initialising solutions using chaotic maps improves initial diversity and reduces premature convergence, for example, Tahiri et al33. have proposed an improved version called Chaos-Artemisinin Optimizer (CAO) injects ten maps (CAO1-CAO10) to enrich exploration while stabilising the search on a manipulator robot. In order to demonstrate the efficacy of chaotic dynamics in protecting sensitive data from cyberattacks, a technique for encrypting color images using fractional Krawchouk transformations and a hybrid optimization approach was presented by Tahiri et al.24. In the optimization field, chaotic maps have also shown a demonstrated capacity to diversify the solutions generated and prevent becoming stuck in local optima. An enhanced Kepler technique that incorporates chaotic maps was proposed by El Ghouate et al.34, who achieved better results on a number of test functions and real-world applications. Conventional optimisation algorithms often suffer from premature convergence towards local minima, limiting their overall efficiency. The inclusion of chaotic dynamics, because of their non-linear and unpredictable nature, helps to broaden the spectrum of solutions examined and prevents this kind of impasse. Several popular metaheuristic algorithms have been successful with this method. For example Fister et al.35 demonstrated that adding chaotic dynamics to the Firefly Algorithm (FA) intensified agent movements, thereby improving global and local search. Similarly, to enhance the exploration stage and prevent stagnation in suboptimal solutions, Chaotic maps were included to the Moth Flame Optimization (MFO) algorithm by H. Li et al.36. Similar advancements for other techniques, such Artificial Bee Colony (ABC), have been documented37, or Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO)38, where the introduction of chaotic perturbations encourages deeper exploration and prevents stagnation in local minima. Lastly, Tahiri et al.39 combined separable moments with a firefly-based chaotic algorithm in the context of optimal 3D reconstruction to show the usefulness of non-linear dynamics in computer vision applications.

Ten one-dimensional chaotic maps were selected for this study because of their methodological importance and the range of dynamic behaviors they offer (see Table 3). These maps were chosen due to their widespread application and proven efficacy in earlier research on chaos-enriched optimization techniques. They cover a wide range of dynamic properties, including density distribution throughout the interval [0,1], symmetry, responsiveness to initial conditions, bifurcations, and ergodicity. Instead of creating a comprehensive inventory of every map that currently exists, the goal is to create a representative and diverse collection that allows for pertinent statistical comparisons to be performed.

Comparison with other optimizers

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed SPO-CL1 algorithm, a comparative study was conducted against eleven state-of-the-art metaheuristic algorithms, including both classical optimizers and recent hybrid or enhanced variants. The comparison is organized into two parts. The section “Tests Based on the CEC-2022 Benchmark” presents the quantitative results obtained on the CEC-2022 benchmark suite, reporting key statistical indicators over 30 independent runs. The section “Nonparametric statistical analysis” provides a rigorous nonparametric statistical analysis using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test and the Friedman test to assess the significance of the observed performance differences and establish a global ranking of the compared algorithms.

The proposed SPO-CL1 algorithm was evaluated against eleven state-of-the-art metaheuristic algorithms on the CEC-2022 benchmark suite of 12 optimization functions. Each algorithm was run 30 times independently with a population of 30 individuals over 250 iterations. The algorithms compared are AO, AOA, GWO, HHO, ZOA, COA, CSBO, IGWO, EWOA, MHHO, and HSMA. As reported in Table 7, SPO-CL1 achieves the best mean on five of twelve functions (F1, F2, F3, F9, F12) and remains competitive elsewhere. The advantage is sharpest on unimodal functions: a near-optimal mean of 6.01 × 10² on F3 and the lowest standard deviation across all algorithms on F2 (1.03 × 10⁰) reflect both solution quality and exceptional consistency. On hybrid functions (F7–F9), SPO-CL1 ranks in the top two throughout, peaking at 2.53 × 10³ on F9. F6 is the one function where it yields ground while AOA and CSBO post catastrophic means of 4.20 × 10⁸ and 2.26 × 10⁷, exposing their vulnerability to multimodal landscapes. On composition functions (F10–F12), SPO-CL1 leads on F12 (2.86 × 10³) and finishes in the top three on F10–F11; EWOA, competitive on F6, deteriorates sharply here. IGWO and GWO are the most credible rivals throughout, while AOA and CSBO consistently trail. Across all function types, the complementary action of chaotic initialization, OBL, and Lévy perturbations sustains both exploration breadth and solution quality (Figure 6and figure 7).

Beyond solution quality, computational overhead is a practical consideration in algorithm selection. All twelve algorithms were executed under identical conditions (population size = 30, MaxIter = 250, 30 independent runs, Intel Core i7-8550U, 1.80 GHz, 16 GB RAM). The average wall-clock time per run on the CEC-2022 suite was comparable across most algorithms, ranging from approximately 0.8 s (GWO, ZOA) to 1.4 s (AOA, HSMA). SPO-CL1 recorded an average runtime of 1.1 s per run, reflecting a modest overhead relative to the standard SPO (0.9 s) attributable to the OBL evaluation and the periodic Lévy perturbation step. This overhead remains negligible in the context of the robotic application, where each function evaluation already requires a full closed-loop simulation via RK4 integration, making the optimizer’s internal cost a minor fraction of the total computation time. The overall complexity of SPO-CL1 is O (Tmax · Np · nc), consistent with most population-based metaheuristics of comparable design.

Nonparametric statistical analysis

Wilcoxon tests (α = 0.05) confirm the dominance of SPO-CL1: 12 out of 12 victories against AOA, COA and CSBO, and at least 10 victories against the other algorithms, with only one defeat on F6. The majority of the results are highly significant (p < 0.001), demonstrating consistent and non-random superiority. Combined with the Friedman ranking, SPO-CL1 emerges as the overall best-performing algorithm on CEC-2022 (Figure 8and figure 9).

Conclusion

This work presented a unified optimization-control framework in which SPO-CL1 automatically tunes the six SMC gains (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃, K₁, K₂, K₃) for a 3-DoF manipulator by minimizing J = ∫₀ᵀ eᵀe dt, evaluated through full closed-loop simulation via 4th-order Runge-Kutta integration. On CEC-2022, SPO-CL1 achieves the best Friedman rank of 1.83 among twelve algorithms, with Wilcoxon rank-sum tests (α = 0.05) confirming statistically significant superiority over the majority of competitors. The robotic validation on a Lemniscate of Bernoulli trajectory shows that, out of the twelve algorithms, SPO-CL1 has the narrowest end-effector trajectory, the fastest tracking error convergence, and the lowest ISE of 1.51 × 10⁻² with almost nil inter-run variance. While COA and HSMA show noticeably higher ISE values because of inferior λ and K configurations, IGWO and AO are the strongest competitors among the analyzed methods, continuously recovering near-optimal gain configurations. These findings demonstrate that SPO-CL1 is a competitive and dependable method for automatically adjusting SMC gain in intricate robotic applications. Future research directions include evaluation on 6-DoF manipulators with 3D trajectories, real-time experimental validation on a physical platform, and extension to multi-objective formulations considering energy consumption and actuator saturation. Extending the comparison to include surrogate-assisted or reinforcement learning-based optimizers also represents a natural direction for future investigation. As with most simulation-based SMC tuning studies, the simplified rigid-link 3-DoF model and the absence of hardware validation limit direct transferability to real platforms, motivating the experimental extension identified as primary future work.
