1.
Introduction and main results

Euler’s pentagonal-number theorem

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j-1)/2},

stands as the foundational cornerstone of
q
-series and combinatorics. Multiplying it by
(q;q)_{\infty}^{-1}=\sum_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}
yields the well-known alternating recurrence relation for the partition function
p(n)
.
Shanks
[
29
]
obtained a finite form of the pentagonal series in 1951. Researchers such as Warnaar
[
30
]
and Chapman
[
10
]
have extensively explored other related finite forms and partial-sum identities. The systematic study of coefficientwise positivity for such truncations began with the following identity due to Andrews and Merca

[
4
]

\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j+1)/2}(1-q^{2j+1})=1+(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{\binom{k}{2}+(k+1)n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{k-1}_{\!q}.

They interpreted the nonconstant coefficients as follows,

(1.1)

(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\bigl(p(n-j(3j+1)/2)-p(n-j(3j+5)/2-1)\bigr)=M_{k}(n),

where
M_{k}(n)
is
the number of partitions of
n
in which
k
is the least positive integer that
does not occur as a part and the number of parts larger than
k
is greater than
the number of parts smaller than
k
.
This immediately yields the corresponding nonnegativity of the truncated
pentagonal sums. We refer to
[
20
,
33
,
37
]
for the subsequent bijective and analytic work clarified this interpretation which produced companion inequalities.

Guo and Zeng
[
15
]
then established truncations of Gauss’s identities on triangular numbers and
square numbers, which lead to inequalities for overpartitions and
partitions with odd parts distinct. Andrews and Merca
[
5
]
showed
that both truncations given by Euler and Gauss admit a unified treatment through the Rogers–Fine identity. Xia, Yee, and Zhao
[
34
]
later
constructed broader families of truncated forms for these three classical theta
identities. These developments made it natural to seek
a truncation theory at the level of the Jacobi triple product, which reduces to
Euler and Gauss’ identities as special cases.

Andrews–Merca and Guo–Zeng independently conjectured that appropriately
normalized finite truncations of this bilateral series have nonnegative
coefficients. More precisely, for
1\leq S<R/2
they asked whether every positive-degree
coefficient of

(1.2)

(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}q^{Rj(j+1)/2-Sj}\left(1-q^{(2j+1)S}\right)}{\left(q^{S},q^{R-S},q^{R};q^{R}\right)_{\infty}},

is nonnegative. Note that the complete series over
j\geq 0
on the numerator is just the Jacobi triple product seris which equals to

(q^{S},q^{R-S},q^{R};q^{R})_{\infty}
. It have been certified analytically by Mao
[
24
]
and combinatorially by Yee in 2015

[
36
]
. Wang and Yee
[
31
]
subsequently obtained an explicit multiple-sum form based on the
Bailey pair methods and a
q
-series expansion of Liu
[
22
]
. The bilateral variants, averaged truncations, Hecke–Rogers analogues,
and various combinatorial interpretations have since been developed in
[
16
,
19
,
28
,
32
,
35
]
.

Merca observed that the preceding finite-truncation statement (
1.2
) can be
strengthened substantially. In
[
26
,
27
]
, Merca proposed a positivity conjecture
after removing the factor
(q^{R};q^{R})_{\infty}
from the denominator of (
1.2
) and extending the
parameter range to
1\leq S<R
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For positive integers
k,R,S
with
1\leq S<R
, the coefficient of
q^{n}
with
n\geq 1
in

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{R,S,k}(q)=\frac{\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-k}q^{Rj(j+1)/2-Sj}\left(1-q^{(2j+1)S}\right)}{\left(q^{S},q^{R-S};q^{R}\right)_{\infty}}

is nonnegative.

Ballantine and
Feigon proved the first three truncation levels
k=1,2,3
and obtained several
related identities
[
8
]
. Ding and Sun
[
12
]
proved the
specialization
R=3S
by applying Bailey lattice. Mao
[
23
]
used Wright’s circle method to obtain
asymptotic formulas for the relevant Jacobi and quintuple-product tails,
thereby proving the corresponding conjectures for sufficiently large
coefficients. In a separate analytic treatment,
Ding and Sun
[
13
]
further established eventual positivity with an effective procedure for
a threshold
N(R,S,k)
and also proved the positivity when the truncation
parameter is sufficiently large.
Zhou
[
37
]
obtained further tail-positivity results and
specializations in a systematic study of pentagonal tails. Thereby this conjecture remains open in full generality until now.

The terminology “tail" also carries a natural physical interpretation. In two-dimensional conformal field theory and solvable lattice models,
q
-series represent graded partition functions where
q
tracks energy and
z
acts as a charge fugacity
[
1
,
11
]
. Truncating these series corresponds to a finite-volume restriction or an ultraviolet cutoff
[
3
,
25
]
. Consequently, the tail” is the high-energy remainder of the spectrum. In this paper, we consider the normalized Jacobi triple product tails as follows

(1.4)

J_{k}(z,q)=\frac{\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-k}q^{T_{j}}\chi_{j}(z)}{(zq,q/z;q)_{\infty}},

where
k\geq 1
,
T_{j}=\binom{j+1}{2}
and

\chi_{j}(z)=\sum_{u=-j}^{j}z^{u}
. The Laurent variable
z
records the difference between the numbers of

A
-colored parts and
B
-colored parts in the two colored-partitions. Combinatorially, we prove the coefficientwise positivity of
J_{k}(z,q)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every integer
k\geq 1
,

(1.5)

J_{k}(z,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[z,z^{-1}][[q]].

Equivalently,
[q^{n}z^{s}]\,J_{k}(z,q)\geq 0
for all
n\geq 0
and

s\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The above theorem leads to inequalities on partition pairs and two-colored partitions immediately.Moreover, by substituting
(z,q)=(q^{S},q^{R})
into the above theorem, it directly reduces to Merca’s stronger conjecture
1.1
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Merca’s stronger conjecture
1.1
holds.

Our proof is based on the minimal excludant of integer partitions, which is denoted by mex in short. The statistic mex has long
been used in combinatorial theory, see, for example, Fraenkel and
Peled
[
14
]
. Andrews and Newman
[
6
,
7
]
introduced it
systematically into partition theory and related its moments to classical
partition functions. The parity
of the mex was then connected with Dyson’s crank, and
Hopkins, Sellers and Stanton
[
17
]
formulated a shifted version that records the first
missing part above a prescribed level, see also
[
6
,
7
,
9
,
17
,
18
,
21
]
.

For a partition
\lambda
,
let

\operatorname{mex}_{r}(\lambda)=\min\{t>r:t\text{ is not a part of }\lambda\},

and let
\operatorname{Top}(\lambda)
denote the set of the elements contained in the top row of its Frobenius symbol
[
2
]
. For
r\geq 0
, define

\mathcal{M}_{r}=\{\lambda:\operatorname{mex}_{r}(\lambda)-r\text{ is odd}\},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{r}=\{\lambda:r\notin\operatorname{Top}(\lambda)\}.

Konan
[
21
]
established an explicit bijection for these generalized mex classes.

Theorem 1.4

(Konan
[
21
]
)
.

For every
r\geq 0
there is a bijection

\mathsf{K}_{r}:\mathcal{M}_{r}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{r}

that preserves both size and length

|\mathsf{K}_{r}(\lambda)|=|\lambda|,\qquad\ell(\mathsf{K}_{r}(\lambda))=\ell(\lambda).

To give our combinatorial proof, first we construct a bidegree-preserving,
sign-reversing involution on set of quadruples.
Then the unpaired elements are organized into
invariant subsets according to the mex
\mathcal{C}_{r}
with the corresponding generating function
C_{r}(z,q)
. Based on Konan’s bijection, we derive an
injection
\Theta_{r}
on this subset which makes each negative block in the invariant subset is injected into a preceding positive block by
\Theta_{r}
. The argument therefore proves coefficientwise positivity of
J_{k}(z,q)
.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the basic definitions and
notations for partitions and the formal power series. Section
3
derives the
invariant subset according to the mex decomposition combinatorially and records its direct

q
-series counterpart. Section
4
constructs the
Frobenius-arm lift and the injection between consecutive invariant subsets, thereby

. Section
5

concluding remarks. In Appendix

2.
Partitions, minimal excludants, and colored indices

In this paper, we assume that
|q|<1
and all the power formal series are interpreted in

\mathscr{R}=\mathbb{Z}[z,z^{-1}][[q]]
, where the coefficient of each
q^{n}
is a
Laurent polynomial in
z
. Thereby, for a
fixed bidegree
(n,s)
only finitely many summands can contribute to the coefficient of
q^{n}t^{s}
.

Let

(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{u=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{u}),\qquad(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n}),

and
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{t};q)_{\infty}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(a_{i};q)_{\infty}
.

We write
F\succeq G
, or equivalently
G\preceq F
, if every coefficient
of
F-G
is nonnegative.

A partition
[
2
]
of a nonnegative integer
n
is a finite weakly decreasing sequence
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}
of positive integers such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=n
, which can be denoted by
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
.
The set of all partitions, including the empty partition, is denoted by

\mathcal{P}
. For
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, let
|\lambda|
,
\ell(\lambda)
, and

m_{t}(\lambda)
denote its size, length, and multiplicity of the part
t
.
Multiset union is written as
\lambda\sqcup\mu
. Denote the staircase in the set of partitions by

\delta_{r}=(r,r-1,\ldots,1),\qquad\delta_{0}=\varnothing,

so that
|\delta_{r}|=T_{r}
,
\ell(\delta_{r})=r
and we call
r
the level of this staircase.

If
d
is the size of the Durfee square of
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
, that is,
d=\max\{i\geq 1\colon\lambda_{i}\geq i\}
, and
\lambda^{\prime}
is its
conjugate, then the Frobenius symbol of
\lambda
is given by
[
2
]

\lambda\longleftrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{d}\\
b_{1}&b_{2}&\cdots&b_{d}\end{pmatrix},

where
a_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i
and
b_{i}=\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-i
for
1\leq i\leq d
. The two rows are strictly decreasing sequences of nonnegative integers, which represent the Frobenius arms and legs, respectively, such that

(2.1)

|\lambda|=d+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(a_{i}+b_{i}),\qquad\ell(\lambda)=b_{1}+1\quad(\lambda\neq\varnothing).

Denote
\operatorname{Top}(\lambda)=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
and

\operatorname{Top}(\varnothing)=\varnothing
. See Figure
1
for an example.

\lambda=(7,5,4,2,1)
,
d=3

a_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i,\qquad b_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-i,

\lambda\longleftrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}6&3&1\\
4&2&0\end{pmatrix}.

The cells enclosed by the black border form the Durfee square. The lightly shaded cells give the Frobenius arms 6, 3, 1; the cells
below the diagonal give the legs 4, 2, 0.

For
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, define the ordinary and shifted minimal excludants by

\operatorname{mex}(\lambda)=\min\{t\geq 1:m_{t}(\lambda)=0\},\qquad\operatorname{mex}_{r}(\lambda)=\min\{t>r:m_{t}(\lambda)=0\}.

For example, if
\lambda=(7,5,4,2,1)
, then
\operatorname{mex}_{1}(\lambda)=\operatorname{mex}_{2}(\lambda)=3
.

Recall that

\mathcal{M}_{r}=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}:\operatorname{mex}_{r}(\lambda)-r\text{ is odd}\},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{r}=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}:r\notin\operatorname{Top}(\lambda)\}.

The condition defining
\mathcal{F}_{r}
concerns a Frobenius arm, not the presence
of
r
as an ordinary part. For example,
\lambda=(7,5,4,2,1)
, then

\operatorname{Top}(7,5,4,2,1)=(6,3,1)
, hence it lies in
\mathcal{F}_{r}
precisely when

r\notin\{1,3,6\}
.

Assume that for a partition with two colors
A
and
B
, an
A
-colored part of size
i
, denoted by
i^{A}
, has weight
q^{i}z
, whereas a
B
-colored part of size
i
, denoted by
i^{B}
, has weight
q^{i}z^{-1}
. Note that
the superscripts
A
and
B
are just color labels, not powers. Thus a pair
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{P}^{2}
, where
\alpha
records the
A
-colored parts and
\beta
records the
B
-colored parts, has weight

\operatorname{wt}(\alpha,\beta)=q^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}z^{\ell(\alpha)-\ell(\beta)}.

Consequently,

(2.2)

\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{P}}\operatorname{wt}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{(zq,q/z;q)_{\infty}}.

We also set the weight of an uncolored atom
U
to be
zq^{0}=z
.
For example, for a two colored partition pair
(\alpha=(5,2,2),\beta=(4,3,1))
, we have
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha,\beta)=q^{17}z^{3-3}=q^{17}
.

3.
Preliminary cancellations and the invariant subset decomposition

We first interpret
J_{k}(z,q)
, both combinatorially and analytically. Since

\chi_{j}(z)=z^{-j}(1+z+\cdots+z^{2j}),

equation (
1.4
) can be rewritten as

J_{k}(z,q)=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-k}\frac{q^{T_{j}}z^{-j}(1+z+\cdots+z^{2j})}{(zq,q/z;q)_{\infty}},

where the factor
q^{T_{j}}z^{-j}
can be seen as the weight of a
B
-colored staircase

\delta_{j}=(j,j-1,\ldots,1).

Let
\mathcal{X}_{k}
be the set of quadruples

\mathcal{X}_{k}=\{y=(j,m,\alpha,\beta)\colon\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{P},\ j\geq k,\ 0\leq m\leq 2j,\ {\rm mex}(\beta)>j\},

where
\alpha,\beta
are colored by
A
and
B
, respectively. Define

\operatorname{sgn}(y)=(-1)^{j-k},\qquad\operatorname{wt}(y)=q^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}z^{m+\ell(\alpha)-\ell(\beta)}.

Here
m
records the choice of the monomial
z^{m}
in

1+z+\cdots+z^{2j}
, or equivalently
m
copies of the
atom
U
. Combining with (
2.2
), it is easy to see that

J_{k}(z,q)=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}_{k}}\operatorname{sgn}(y)\operatorname{wt}(y).

For
\beta\in\mathcal{P}
, let

h(\beta):=\max\{t\geq 0:\delta_{t}\subseteq\beta\}=\operatorname{mex}(\beta)-1,

where the staircase containment is understood with multiplicity. For
m\geq 0
, set

L(m):=\max\left\{k,\left\lceil\frac{m}{2}\right\rceil\right\}.

Once
m,\alpha,\beta
are given, the admissible values of
j

are precisely the integers in the interval

L(m)\leq j\leq h(\beta).

We now define a map

\displaystyle\iota:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{k}

by

\iota(j,m,\alpha,\beta)=\begin{cases}(j+1,m,\alpha,\beta),&j-L\ \text{is even and }j<h,\\[5.69054pt]
(j-1,m,\alpha,\beta),&j-L\ \text{is odd},\\[5.69054pt]
(j,m,\alpha,\beta),&j=h\ \text{and }h-L\ \text{is even},\end{cases}

where
y=(j,m,\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{\chi}_{k}
, and

L=L(m),h=h(\beta)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The map
\iota
is an involution, and the invariant subset of this involution is

\mathcal{I}_{\iota}=\left\{(j,m,\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{\chi}_{k}:j=h(\beta),\ \operatorname{mex}(\beta)=L(m)+2d+1\text{ for some }d\geq 0\right\}.

Proof.

In the first case,

j<h
implies

\delta_{j+1}\subseteq\beta.

In the second case,
j-L
is odd, so
j\geq L+1
. Hence

j-1\geq L\geq k,\qquad\delta_{j-1}\subseteq\beta\qquad\text{and}\qquad m\leq 2L\leq 2(j-1).

For any element in the first case,
the staircase under
\iota
is mapped to of level
j+1
which has odd difference from
L
, so applying
\iota
again simply maps it back to itself at level
j
. Conversely, for any element in the second
case, applying
\iota
twice clearly maps it back to itself at level
j
. The elements in the third case consist precisely of the fixed points of
\iota
. Moreover, since
\iota
changes only
j
, it preserves

\operatorname{wt}(y)
. On every nonfixed orbit,
j
is changed by
one, and therefore

\operatorname{sgn}(\iota(y))=-\operatorname{sgn}(y).

Thus
\iota
is a weight-preserving sign-reversing involution.

Note that an element
y=(j,m,\alpha,\beta)
is fixed under
\iota
if and only if

j=h(\beta),\qquad h(\beta)-L(m)\equiv 0\pmod{2},

and
\operatorname{sgn}(y)=(-1)^{L(m)-k}
.
Equivalently, there exists
d\geq 0
such that

\operatorname{mex}(\beta)=L(m)+2d+1,

which completes the proof.
∎

For
r\geq 1
, define the invariant subset according to the mex

(3.1)

\mathcal{C}_{r}=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{P}^{2}:\operatorname{mex}(\beta)=r+2d+1\text{ for some }d\geq 0\right\},

and let

C_{r}(z,q)=\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{C}_{r}}q^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}z^{\ell(\alpha)-\ell(\beta)}.

By applying the involution
\iota
, we obtain the following decomposition of
J_{k}(z,q)
according to the invariant subset of
\iota
. Here we present both combinatorial proof and anlaytic proof of such a decomposition.

Lemma 3.2

(Invariant subset decomposition of
J_{k}(z,q)
)
.

For every
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle J_{k}(z,q)={}

\displaystyle(1+z+\cdots+z^{2k})C_{k}(z,q)

(3.2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty}(-1)^{r-k}z^{2r-1}(1+z)C_{r}(z,q).

Combinatorial proof.

Under the involution
\iota
, only its fixed points remain. Set
r=L(m)
.

If
r=k
, then
0\leq m\leq 2k
, then every surviving element has
positive sign, and the coresponding
(\alpha,\beta)
belongs to

\mathcal{C}_{k}
. Summing over the possible values of
m
gives

(1+z+\cdots+z^{2k})C_{k}(z,q).

If
r>k
, then
L(m)=\lceil m/2\rceil=r
, and hence

m\in\{2r-1,2r\}.

The sign of the fixed point is
(-1)^{r-k}
, while the two possible
values of
m
contribute to

z^{2r-1}+z^{2r}=z^{2r-1}(1+z).

Summing over all
r\geq k+1
gives
(
3.2
). Conversely, every
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{C}_{r}
, together with an admissible value of
m
described above,
determines the unique fixed point
j=h(\beta)
.
∎

Analytic proof.

Formula (
3.2
) also follows from (
1.4
). Let

F_{j}(z,q)=\frac{q^{T_{j}}z^{-j}}{(zq,q/z;q)_{\infty}},

and let

A_{j}(z)=1+z+\cdots+z^{2j},\qquad D_{r}(z)=z^{2r-1}(1+z).

Then

J_{k}(z,q)=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-k}A_{j}(z)F_{j}(z,q),\qquad A_{j}(z)=A_{k}(z)+\sum_{r=k+1}^{j}D_{r}(z).

Collecting equal blocks in the region
k+1\leq r\leq j
gives

\displaystyle J_{k}(z,q)={}

\displaystyle A_{k}(z)\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-k}F_{j}(z,q)+\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty}D_{r}(z)\sum_{j=r}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-k}F_{j}(z,q)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle A_{k}(z)C_{k}(z,q)+\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty}(-1)^{r-k}D_{r}(z)\widehat{C}_{r}(z,q),

where

\widehat{C}_{r}(z,q)=\sum_{j=r}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-r}F_{j}(z,q).

Pairing up the consecutive summands, we have

\widehat{C}_{r}(z,q)=\sum_{d\geq 0}\bigl(F_{r+2d}(z,q)-F_{r+2d+1}(z,q)\bigr).

Noting that
T_{j+1}-T_{j}=j+1
, it implies that

\displaystyle F_{j}(z,q)-F_{j+1}(z,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{T_{j}}z^{-j}(1-q^{j+1}z^{-1})}{(zq,q/z;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(zq;q)_{\infty}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{q^{i}z^{-1}}{1-q^{i}z^{-1}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=j+2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{i}z^{-1}}\right).

The first factor
1/(zq;q)_{\infty}
permits the generating function of arbitrary
A
-colored partitions. According to the following two brackets, the
B
-colored partitions must have parts

1,2,\ldots,j
occurring at least once,
j+1
being absent, and all larger
parts being unrestricted. Thus
F_{j}(z,q)-F_{j+1}(z,q)
is the generating function
for pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
with
{\rm{mex}}(\beta)=j+1
, that is to say,
j+1
is the first missing
B
–colored part. Taking
j=r+2d
shows that

\widehat{C}_{r}(z,q)=C_{r}(z,q)
, and this proves
(
3.2
).
∎

4.
Frobenius operation and the combinatorial proof

In this section, we will construct a Frobenius-arm lift operator as well as an injection which can be combined with Konan’s bijection to build an injection from the invariant subset
\mathcal{C}_{r}
to
\mathcal{C}_{r-1}
.

Taking
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{C}_{r}
, since
\operatorname{mex}(\beta)>r
, by removing one copy of each
part among
1,2,\ldots,r
, it yields a unique decomposition for
\beta
:

\beta=\delta_{r}\sqcup\gamma,

such that

\operatorname{mex}_{r}(\gamma)=\operatorname{mex}(\beta)=r+2d+1
, and hence
\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
.
Conversely, adjoining
\delta_{r}
to any
\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
produces a

B
-colored partition in the invariant subset
\mathcal{C}_{r}

To give the combinatorial proof of our main result, we need to introduce the a Frobenius operation associated with the following sets

Lemma 4.1
.

For a given

, there exists a bijection

\mathsf{U}_{r}:D_{r}\longrightarrow E_{r},

where for

\mathsf{U}_{r}(\lambda)
is obtained by replacing the unique top-row entry
r-1
with

and
leaving the bottom row unchanged.

Proof.

For a partition
\lambda\in D_{r}
, suppose its Frobenius symbol is as follows

\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&\cdots&a_{d}\\
b_{1}&\cdots&b_{d}\end{pmatrix},

and let

. Since
\lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
, the integer
r

does not occur in the top row. Hence

a_{t-1}\geq r+1\quad(t>1),\qquad a_{t+1}\leq r-2\quad(t<d).

Thus replacing
a_{t}=r-1
with

preserves the strictly decreasing order of
the top row, and thereby

\mathsf{U}_{r}(\lambda)\in E_{r}
.

Conversely, if
\mu\in E_{r}
, then its top row contains a unique entry

r
and contains no entry
r-1
. Replacing that entry
r
with
r-1

again preserves the strictly decreasing order and produces a partition in

.
This lowering operation is the inverse of
\mathsf{U}_{r}
. Thus

\mathsf{U}_{r}
is a bijection.

the Frobenius-arm lift operator since it extends one of the arms of

by 1. From the properties of the Frobenius symbol (

Lemma 4.2

The map

\mathsf{B}_{r}:\mathcal{P}\times\{\widehat{r}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{F}_{r-1}

\mathsf{B}_{r}(\alpha,\widehat{r},\lambda)=\begin{cases}(\alpha\sqcup(r),\lambda),&\mbox{if}\ r-1\notin\operatorname{Top}(\lambda),\\[2.84526pt]

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\{(\alpha^{\prime},\mu):r\notin\operatorname{Top}(\mu),\ m_{r}(\alpha^{\prime})\geq 1\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\{(\alpha^{\prime},\mu):r\in\operatorname{Top}(\mu),\ m_{r-1}(\alpha^{\prime})\geq 1\},

Proof.

It is obvious that

. The first branch of

lands in

r
, and the newly inserted part
r
ensures that

. The second

\mathcal{I}_{r}^{(1)}

into the output top row

contains the newly inserted part

Conversely, for

(\alpha,\widehat{r},\lambda)=\begin{cases}(\alpha^{\prime}\setminus(r),\widehat{r},\mu),&r\notin\operatorname{Top}(\mu),\\[2.84526pt]

Here

We can see that the size and length statements are obvious from the definition of

Concluding remarks

Our proof divides the coefficientwise positivity problem into two combinatorial
stages. First, the involution on quadruples transforms our target into invariant subsets according to the mex. Second, the injection
\Theta_{r}
maps each negative invariant subset associated to
z^{2}C_{r}(z,q)
into the preceding positive one associated to
C_{r-1}(z,q)
which preserves the bidegree of
(q,z)
in the generating functions.
A natural next question is whether the invariant subset inequality

z^{2}C_{r}(z,q)\preceq C_{r-1}(z,q)

admits a direct map on the colored pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
which gives an explicit partition-theoretic interpretation of the coefficientwise positivity of

C_{r-1}(z,q)-z^{2}C_{r}(z,q)
. Moreover, it is also interesting to consider which kind of truncated theta series admits an analogous generalized-mex–Frobenius injection.

This appendix is included to introduce the detailed procedures of Konan’s bijection

\mathcal{M}_{r}=\{\lambda:\operatorname{mex}_{r}(\lambda)-r\text{ is odd}\},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{r}=\{\lambda:r\notin\operatorname{Top}(\lambda)\},

A.1.
The forward and inverse maps

We adopt the notation of the staircase as given in
[
21

:

\Delta_{r,t}=(r+t,r+t-1,\ldots,r+1),\qquad\Delta_{r,0}=\varnothing.

It is also compatible with the terminal state used in the bijection.

For a partition
\lambda
, denote

Then

and

\lambda_{d_{s}(\lambda)}-d_{s}(\lambda)=s
.

Let

and write

\operatorname{mex}_{r}(\gamma)=r+2t+1,\qquad\gamma=\Delta_{r,2t}\sqcup\lambda,

where the part
r+2t+1
is absent from

. For

and

. Konan’s

Move K1:

.

Let
\lambda_{d}=d+s
. Delete this critical part, add one to the first

d-1

\displaystyle(\Delta_{r,2t};\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d-1},d+s,\lambda_{d+1},\ldots)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\Delta_{r,2t};\lambda_{1}+1,\ldots,\lambda_{d-1}+1,s+1,\lambda_{d+1},\ldots).

Under this move, the staircase keeps unchanged, and the residual partition corresponding to
\lambda
keeps both the size
and the length.

Move K2:
s\notin\operatorname{Top}(\lambda)
and

.

Remove the two largest parts

from the staircase, subtract one from the first

d

\displaystyle(\Delta_{r,2t};\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d},\lambda_{d+1},\ldots)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\Delta_{r,2t-2};\lambda_{1}-1,\ldots,\lambda_{d}-1,d+s,s-1,\lambda_{d+1},\ldots),

with the residual parts reordered. Under this move, the size of
\lambda
increases by

and its

Iteration stops at a state
(\Delta_{r,0},\nu)
with
