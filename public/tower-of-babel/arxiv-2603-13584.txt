Abstract
. Deep learning has achieved recognition for its impact within natural sciences, however scientists are inhibited by the prohibitive technical cost and computational complexity of training project specific models from scratch. Following software engineering community guidance, natural scientists are reusing pre-trained deep learning models (PTMs) to amortize these costs. While prior works recommend PTM reuse patterns, to our knowledge, little work has been done to empirically evaluate their usage and impact within the natural sciences. We present the first empirical study of PTM reuse patterns in the natural sciences, quantifying the utilization and impact of conceptual, adaptation, and deployment reuse within the scientific process. Leveraging an automated large language model driven pipeline, we analyze 17,511 peer reviewed, open access papers to identify PTM reuse by scientific field, associated reuse patterns, and the impact of PTM integration into the scientific process from January 1st, 2000 to December 10th, 2025. Our results show that “Biochemistry, Genetics and Molecular Biology” has outpaced other natural scientific fields in PTM reuse, “adaptation” reuse is the most prevalent PTM reuse pattern identified across all natural science fields, and the “Test” stage of the scientific process has been most impacted by PTM integration. This aligns with the growing interest of leveraging computational methods to conduct high throughput, data driven scientific research. Our work characterizes and identifies current PTM reuse practices within the natural sciences, evaluates their impact on the scientific process, and establishes a foundation for future work into the implementation and broader scientific implications of PTM reuse.

4.1.
Methods

To identify PTM reuse patterns within the natural sciences, we developed an automated, large language model (LLM) driven pipeline to analyze academic works.
First, we identify candidate peer reviewed, open access works published in mega-journals and normalize their metadata.
Second, we then filter for cited natural science works and extract the prose into a form suitable for LLMs.
Third, using a LLM we autonomously analyze and extract DL and PTM reuse features from the works.
We report the methodology for each step in this section.

5.1.
Methods

To identify how PTM reuse has augmented the scientific process within natural sciences, we developed a pipeline designed to transition from specific identification of PTMs to the greater context in which they are reused.
First, we isolate sections of the prose specifically mentioning PTMs as identified from our previous analysis (
Section

5.1.1

We subsequently employ a large language model to map these isolated prose segments against a formalized taxonomy of the scientific process (
Section

5.1.2

This allows us to categorize the specific functional role of each PTM utilization instance and determine which research phase is being augmented by model reuse.
We report the methodology for each step in this section.

5.1.1.
Isolation of PTM Reuse Prose

To identify at what stage PTM reuse is augmenting the scientific process, we first identify section’s of a paper’s prose that specifically mention previously identified PTMs (
Section

4.3.3
).
While our previous methods to extract PTM model names and reuse patterns returned the most salient prose to confirm the output, it did not return the section in which that prose is located in.
To remedy this, we parse the Markdown version of the document for the PTM name, and return a set of all sections of the prose that reference the model.

Discussion

This study examines how natural scientists integrate pre-trained deep learning models (PTMs) into their scientific workflows to facilitate data driven discovery.
We present a qualitative analysis of 17,511 peer reviewed, open access papers in
biology,

This dataset serves as a proxy for evaluating the current landscape of PTM reuse across the scientific community.

6.1.
Implications For Science

PTM Supply Chain Enabling “Adaptation” Reuse

The findings in
Section

4
suggest a few notable patterns regarding PTM adoption in the natural sciences.
First, there is a visible increase in deep learning (DL) and PTM reuse, particularly within “Biochemistry, Genetics and Molecular Biology” (
Figures

4
and
5
).
Second, the data shows that the “adaptation” pattern is used more frequently than “deployment” or “conceptual” reuse (
Figure

6
).
By utilizing “adaptation”, researchers are able to lower the technical and computational barriers typically associated with DL methods.

Given the prevalence of the PTM “adaptation” reuse pattern, we posit that scientists leverage this approach to realize the benefits of DL methodologies without incurring the prohibitive financial and computational costs of training models from scratch.
In this capacity, PTMs serve as reusable scientific infrastructure that minimizes the need for domain specific specialization.
The broader impact is a shift away from training DL models per project, towards the greater integration of existing PTM supply chains into the natural sciences.

Limited PTM Integration in Scientific Processes

As detailed in
Section

5
, PTM integration is concentrated within the “Test” stage of the scientific process (
Figure

7
).
This concentration suggests that PTMs are currently utilized as evaluative or benchmarking instruments rather than as foundational components of early stage experimental design.
Furthermore, the limited reuse of PTMs in other stages of the scientific process implies the existence of persistent integration barriers, highlighting a potential missed opportunity to leverage PTMs earlier in the scientific process
2
2

2

It is also possible that PTMs are used elsewhere in the process but not disclosed,
e.g
, through undocumented use of large language models (LLMs) in study design.
.

To address this missed opportunity, future research must prioritize the expansion of PTM utility across the broader scientific process.
By limiting PTM usage to data evaluation, researchers may be overlooking a significant opportunity to leverage these models during the formative stages of inquiry, such as hypothesis generation or experimental modeling.
By shifting the focus toward these earlier stages, the scientific community can move toward a more holistic integration of PTMs that accelerates the entire hypothetico-deductive process.

6.2.
Future Directions

Expanding Beyond Natural Science

We plan to expand the scope of analysis to additional academic disciplines.
By examining works from a broader range of fields, we can assess the current state of practice regarding PTM models and reuse in science more generally.
This large scale analysis will allow us to determine whether common PTM reuse patterns emerge across disciplines.

Exploring PTM Integration into the Scientific Process

Future work should investigate the technical implementation of PTM reuse patterns within scientific software to determine their influence on the scientific process.
By analyzing how researchers integrate PTM packages into their code, we can discern whether these models are utilized as drivers of scientific reasoning or merely as tools for data automation.
Delineating these software engineering practices is essential for identifying the process level frameworks that enable effective, reproducible model reuse across scientific domains.

Research Into Agentic Scientific Workflows

This paper does not evaluate the prevalence of agentic scientific workflows, as they represent an emerging form of PTM reuse.
However, scientific practice is becoming progressively automated through tools such as autonomous laboratories and large language models for hypothesis generation and experimental design.
Understanding how researchers integrate agents into their workflows will be critical for assessing their impact on scientific outcomes.
Future research should examine how agentic systems are embedded within domain specific workflows and how such integration reshapes experimental design, validation, and knowledge production.

7.
Threats To Validity

Construct Threats

Construct threats are those that affect the measurements of our study.
First, we rely on OpenAlex generated topic field assignments per paper to identify natural science papers.
As these fields are inferred using deep learning, misclassifications may affect our selection of papers for our dataset.
Second, our research is subject to reporting bias as researchers may not disclose PTM reuse in their works, thereby limiting our ability to identify PTM reuse.
Additionally, due to the how researchers describe their methods, it is possible that we were unable to capture every PTM reuse and deep learning usage candidate from the academic prose.
Third, we leveraged GPT-OSS 120:b to analyze prose for our analysis.
GPT-OSS 120:b is limited in its reasoning capacity compared to current commercial offerings from frontier labs, but was selected to enable reproducibility in our analysis methodology.

Internal Threats

Internal threats to validity are those that effect the cause-effect relationships between variables.
First, while we posit that the increasing reuse of PTMs in natural science is tied to the increasing usage of deep learning methods, we acknowledge that the reverse could hold true.
PTM reuse could be driving the increasing deep learning usage we measured.
Second, the increasing deep learning usage and PTM reuse that we measured could be due to several factors.
These include the
increasing availability of GPU and supercomputing resources,
lower financial and technical barriers for reusing PTMs, and
better library support and maturation of the PTM supply chain.
Third, our usage of GPT-OSS 120:b to analyze natural science works may be inhibited due to the stochasticity of the large language model.
To mitigate this, we specified parameters and seed values in
Section

4.2
.
However, the potential for misclassifications and hallucinations is present in our analysis.

External Threats

External threats are those that affect the generalization of our study.
First, our study is limited to a selection of four open-access mega-journals.
PTM reuse patterns measured in these venues may not reflect the patterns in other academic journals.
Second, our analysis is focused on PTM reuse within the fields of
biology,
chemistry,
physics, and
environmental science.
As such, the results of our study may not generalize well to other scientific disciplines.
Third, our study relies on the analysis of academic works available in the Journal Article Tag Suite (JATS) XML format returned using a keyword search strategy.
As such, potential candidate works may have fallen outside of our search criteria due to
not supporting a JATS XML formatted version of the document or
not being captured by our keyword search,
thereby constraining our pool of candidate papers for this study.

8.
Conclusion

To our knowledge, this work presents the first empirical study of pre-trained deep learning model (PTM) reuse patterns within the natural sciences.
By examining how scientists
reuse PTMs and
report how they reused them,
we characterize the impact that PTM reuse patterns have made on the scientific process.
Our findings highlight the increasing adoption of
PTM reuse to enable data-driven scientific discoveries,
the overwhelming usage of the “adaptation” reuse pattern to enable PTM reuse, and
that the primary integration of PTMs within the scientific process has occurred within the “Test” stage.
Collectively, these findings highlight a maturing pattern of PTM integration within the natural sciences characterized by adaptation focused reuse and downstream evaluative application.
