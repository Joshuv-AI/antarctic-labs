Toeplitz operators on pluriharmonic Fock spaces
Vladan Jaguzović, Djordjije Vujadinović

Abstract
. In this paper, we study Toeplitz operators with a positive symbol on pluriharmonic Fock spaces over ℂ n . \mathbb{C}^{n}. We characterize the conditions under which the Toeplitz operator T μ T_{\mu} is bounded, compact, or belongs to the Schatten class S 1 S_{1} . Furthermore, we give a necessary condition for a Toeplitz operator to belong to S p S_{p} for p ≥ 1 , p\geq 1, and under an additional assumption, we prove the sufficiency of the condition.

1.
Introduction

By
L_{\alpha}^{p}
we denote the space of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for which

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,\alpha}^{p}=\frac{p^{n}}{\left(2\pi\alpha\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(z)<\infty.

The holomorpic Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{p}
is the closed subset of
L_{\alpha}^{p}
consisting of all holomorphic functions belonging to
L_{\alpha}^{p}.
These spaces play a central role in quantum physics, with their applications discussed in
[
7
]
. It is known that the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{2}
is given by
K_{\alpha}(z,w)=e^{\frac{z\bar{w}}{\alpha}}
(see
[
11
]
). Moreover, the projection
P_{\alpha}:L_{\alpha}^{p}\to\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{p},
given by

P_{\alpha}f(z)=\frac{1}{\pi\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)e^{\frac{z\bar{w}}{\alpha}}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(z)

is bounded for
p\geq 1
(see
[
11
]
). Similarly, the boundedness of the orthogonal projection from
L_{\alpha}^{p}
to the harmonic Fock space was established in
[
9
]
.

The main focus of this paper is the study of Toeplitz operators acting on
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).
We will provide necessary and sufficient conditions for such operators to be bounded, compact, and belong to the trace class. Those problems for holomorphic Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{2}
are considered in
[
4
]
. To prove our boundedness and compactness results, we will derive a characterization of Carleson measures analogous to the holomorphic case presented in
[
11
]
and the harmonic case on
\mathbb{C}
developed in
[
10
]
.

It remains an open problem to find sufficient conditions for the operator
T_{\mu}
to be in the Schatten class
S_{p}.

We conjecture that for a positive Borel measure
\mu,
the operator
T_{\mu}\in S_{p}
if and only if
\mu(B(\cdot,r))\in L^{p}(dA)
for every
r>0.
While we have yet to provide a complete proof of this statement, we establish the necessity of the condition
\mu(B(\cdot,r))\in L^{p}(dA).
Furthermore, we prove the sufficiency of this condition under the additional assumption that
\mu
is a positive regular radial Borel measure.

2.
Preliminares

A pluriharmonic function
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is a function such that

\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}=0,

for all
j,k=\{1,\dots,n\}.
It is known that for each such function
f,
there exist unique holomorphic functions
g,h
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
h(0)=0
and
f=g+\overline{h}.
We denote by
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
the set of all pluriharmonic functions belonging to
L^{p}_{\alpha}.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a closed subspace of
L^{p}_{\alpha}.

2.1.
Reproducing kernel

In the paper
[
2
]
it is stated that the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is given by

K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)=K_{\alpha}(z,w)+K_{\alpha}(w,z)-1,

where
K_{\alpha}(z,w)=e^{\frac{z\bar{w}}{\alpha}}
is the kernel for holomorphic Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\alpha}.

We also define the normalized pluriharmonic reproducing kernel
k_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)
by

k_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)=\frac{K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)}{\left\lVert K_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,w)\right\rVert_{2,\alpha}}=\frac{K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)}{\sqrt{K_{ph,\alpha}(w,w)}}=\frac{K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)}{\sqrt{2e^{\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1}}.

Furthermore, it is easy to show that
K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e_{n}(z)\overline{e_{n}(w)},
for any orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),
following the same argument as in
[
12
, Theorem 4.19]
.

The set
\mathrm{Lin}\,\left\{K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)\mid w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\right\}
is a dense subset of
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).
Indeed, if
f
is orthogonal to the set, we have

f(z)=\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(w)=\langle f,K_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\rangle=0.

Moreover, the set
\mathrm{Lin}\ \{k_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)\mid w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}
is dense in
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).
Therefore,
f_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}0,
if and only if
(f_{m})
is bounded and
\langle f_{m},k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,w)\rangle\to 0,
for every
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n},
by the Banach-Steinhaus theorem.

2.2.
Some inequalities for pluriharmonic functions

In this subsection we will give some useful estimates for the integral average of holomorphic and harmonic functions. The first lemma, we state is a standard lemma found in almost all textbooks and papers on Fock spaces. In the second lemma, we deal with an appropriate estimate for harmonic functions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty,\alpha>0
and
R>0
are positive fixed paramethers. There exists a positive constant
C(p,\alpha,R)
such that

\left|g(a)e^{-\frac{|a|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\leq\frac{C}{r^{2n}}\int_{B(a,r)}\left|g(z)\right|^{p}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}p}{2\alpha}}\,dA(z)

for every
0<r<R,a\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and every entire function
g
defined on
\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Proof.

It follows by applying the same idea from
[
11
, Lemma 2.32]
for
C=2ne^{\frac{Rp}{2\alpha}}.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
be a harmonic function and
\alpha>0,p>0,
and
R
are positive fixed parametheres. Then, there exists a positive constant
C(|a|,p,\alpha,R)
such that

\left|f(a)e^{-\frac{|a|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\leq\frac{C}{r^{2n}}\int_{B(a,r)}\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(z)

holds for all
0<r\leq R.

The constant
C
is given by

C=C_{p}e^{p\frac{R^{2}+2R|a|}{2\alpha}}

where
C_{p}
depends only on
p.

Proof.

Using the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{B(a,r)}\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-p\frac{(|a|+r)^{2}}{2\alpha}}\int_{B(a,r)}|f(z)|^{p}\,dA(z)

and by Lemma
[
1
, Lemma 2, p. 172]
, we obtain

\int_{B(a,r)}\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(z)\geq e^{-p\frac{(|a|+r)^{2}}{2\alpha}}|f(a)|^{p}\frac{r^{2n}}{C_{p}}=\frac{r^{2n}}{C_{p}}e^{-p\frac{R^{2}+2R|a|}{2\alpha}}\left|f(a)e^{-\frac{|a|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}.\qed

2.3.
The Berezin transform of operators

Here we introduce the Berezin transform with respect to the pluriharmonic kernel. Moreover, we establish several basic properties of this transform, for latter use.

For every bounded linear operator
T:\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),
we define the Berezin transform (the Berezin symbol) of
T
as

\widetilde{T}(z)=\langle Tk_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z),k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\rangle.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
T
is a compact operator, then
\widetilde{T}(z)\to 0,
as
|z|\to\infty.

Proof.

Notice

\langle k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z),k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,w)\rangle=\frac{1}{\left(2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right)^{1/2}\left(2e^{\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right)^{1/2}}K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)\to 0,

as
|z|\to 0,
therefore
k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)
converges weakly to
0
as
z\to\infty.
Since
|\widetilde{T}(z)|\leq\left\lVert Tk_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\right\rVert
, by
[
12
, Thm 1.14]
we have that
\widetilde{T}(z)\to 0,
as
|z|\to\infty.

∎

Theorem 2.4
.

If
T
is a positive operator or
T
is a trace-class operator then

\tr(T)=\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\widetilde{T}(z)\left(2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(z).

Also, a positive operator
T
belongs to the trace-class if and only if the integral converges.

Proof.

Analogous to the proof in
[
11
, Prop. 3.3]
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
T\in S_{p}.
Then
\widetilde{T}
belongs to
L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},(2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}dA(z)).

Proof.

Notice that if
T
is a bounded operator, that is,
T\in S_{\infty},
we have
|\widetilde{\mu}(z)|\leq\left\lVert T\right\rVert.
Also, if
T\in S_{1}
is positive, we have that

\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\widetilde{T}(z)\left(2e^{\frac{|z|}{\alpha}}-1\right)e^{-\frac{|z|}{\alpha}}\,dA(z)=\tr(T)=\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{S_{1}}.

If
T\in S_{1}
is a self-adjoint then
T=T_{1}-T_{2},
where
T_{1}=\frac{|T|+T}{2},
and
T_{2}=\frac{|T|-T}{2}
are positive operators. Since
|\widetilde{T}(z)|\leq\widetilde{T_{1}}(z)+\widetilde{T_{2}}(z),
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|\widetilde{T}(z)\right|\frac{2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1}{e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}}\,dA(z)\leq\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\widetilde{|T|}(z)\frac{2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1}{e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}}\,dA(z)=\tr(|T|)=\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{S_{1}}.

If
T\in S_{1}
is an arbitary operator, then
T=T_{1}+iT_{2}=\frac{T+T^{*}}{2}+i\frac{T-T^{*}}{2i},
where
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are self-adjoint operators. Since

\left|\widetilde{T}(z)\right|\leq\left|\widetilde{T_{1}}\right|+\left|\widetilde{T_{2}}\right|,
by the previous discussion, we have

\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|\widetilde{T}(z)\right|\frac{2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1}{e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}}\,dA(z)\leq\left\lVert T_{1}\right\rVert+\left\lVert T_{2}\right\rVert\leq 2\left\lVert T\right\rVert.

From the complex interpolation we have if
T\in S_{p}
then
T\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\frac{2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1}{e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}}dA(z)).

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

If
p\geq 1,
and
T\in S_{p}.
Then
\widetilde{T}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dA(z)).

For a positive operator on a Hilbert space, by
[
12
, Prop. 1.31]
, we have
\langle T^{p}x,x\rangle\leq\langle Tx,x\rangle^{p},
for every unit vector
x.
This implies that for a positive operator we have
\widetilde{T^{p}}\leq\widetilde{T}^{p}.

2.4.
p
-Carleson measures

For a non-negative Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
1\leq p<\infty,
we say that
\mu
is
p
-Carleson measure for the pluriharmonic Fock space
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,\alpha}.

Here we give a characterization of the
p
-Carleson measures. These results will yield a characterisation of boundedness of Toeplitz operators on
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).
For the mentioned characterisation, we will use only the case
p=2.
However, since the same approach applies to all
p\geq 1,
we prove the result for the general case. Before stating the theorem, we briefly review the tools required for the proof.

Let us denote with
P_{p,\alpha}:L_{\alpha}^{p}\to\mathcal{F}^{p}_{\alpha}
projection onto the holomorphic Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{p}_{\alpha}.
One can easy show that
P_{p,\alpha}
is bounded, using the same idea as in
[
11
, Thm 2.20]
. Furthermore, the same proof give us that
\left\lVert P_{\alpha,p}\right\rVert\leq 2^{n}.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n},
and
p\geq 1,\alpha>0
and
0<r<\infty
fixed. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mu
is a
p
-Carleson measure;

(2)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that
\mu(B(a,r))\leq C
for every
a\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a
p
-Carleson measure. Then for
f(z)=e^{\frac{z\bar{a}}{\alpha}-\frac{|a|^{2}}{2\alpha}},
we obtain

\int_{B(a,r)}\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{-\frac{p|z-a|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\,dA(z)\leq C.

Since
\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}=e^{-\frac{p|z-a|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\geq e^{-\frac{pr^{2}}{2\alpha}},
we conclude that

\mu(B(a,r))\leq Ce^{\frac{pr^{2}}{2\alpha}}.

Let
\mu
satisfies the second condition. For
r>0
and lattice
r\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
one can see that there exists
M
such that for every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
belongs to at the most
M
balls
B(z_{k},2r),
where
\left\{z_{k}\right\}_{k}=r\mathbb{Z}^{2n}.
Then for every holomorphic function
g\in\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|g(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z_{k}}\int_{B(z_{k},r)}\left|g(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{2n}}\sum_{z_{k}}\int_{B(z_{k},r)}\int_{B(z_{k},2r)}\left|g(w)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(w)\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{2n}}\sum_{z_{k}}\mu(B(z_{k},r))\int_{B(z_{k},2r)}\left|g(w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ce^{\frac{pr}{2\alpha}}}{r^{2n}}\sum_{z_{k}}\int_{B(z_{k},2r)}\left|g(w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{MCe^{\frac{pr}{2\alpha}}}{r^{2n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|g(w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(z).

Let
f=g+\bar{h}\in\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
where
g,h\in\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
are entire functions such that
h(0)=0.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|g(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)+\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|h(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{MCe^{\frac{pr}{2\alpha}}}{r^{2n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|g(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}+\left|h(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dA(z)

By boundedness of
P_{p,\alpha}
we have that
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{p,\alpha}=\left\lVert P_{p,\alpha}f\right\rVert_{p,\alpha}\leq\left\lVert P_{p,\alpha}\right\rVert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,\alpha}
and

\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{p,\alpha}=\left\lVert\overline{P_{p,\alpha}(\bar{f})}-\overline{f(0)}\right\rVert\leq(\left\lVert P_{p,\alpha}\right\rVert+1)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,\alpha}.

As a result,
\mu
is a
p
-Carleson measure.
∎

For a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
we say that it is a vanishing
p
-Carleson measure if

(2.1)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)=0,

for every sequence
\{f_{m}\},
bounded in
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that converges to 0 uniformly on compact subsets. The following results will give us a characterization of the compactness of Toeplitz operators on
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
p\geq 1,\alpha>0,r>0
and
\mu
is a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}.
Then
\mu
is a vanishing
p
-Carleson measure if and only if
\mu\left(B(z,r)\right)\to 0,
as
z\to\infty.

Proof.

Notice that
\mu
is a vanishing
p
-Carleson measure, then (
2.1
) holds for every bounded sequence from
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{p}
(holomorphic Fock space). Consequently, by
[
11
, Thm 3.30]
, we have that
\mu(B(z,r))\to 0,
as
z\to\infty.

Let
\mu(B(z,r))\to 0,
as
z\to\infty,\{z_{k}\}=r\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
and
f_{m}=g_{m}+h_{m}
a bounded sequence from
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
f_{m}
converges uniformly to 0 on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Since
\mu(B(z_{k},r))\to\infty,
when
k\to 0,
we can choose
N
such that
\mu(B(z_{k},r))<\epsilon
for
k>N.
Taking into account that
\left\{\mu(B(z_{k},r))\right\}
is bounded and following the idea from Theorem
2.7
we obtain

(2.2)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\leq&\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{B(z_{k},r)}|f_{m}(z)|^{p}e^{-p\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)\\
&+\frac{C\epsilon}{r^{2n}}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\int_{B(z_{k},2r)}\left(\left|g_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}+\left|h_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\right)\,dA(z).\end{split}

Using Lemma
2.2
in the first term of the previous expression, we derive the following upper bound

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{B(z_{k},r)}|f_{m}(z)|^{p}e^{-p\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{B(z_{k},r)}C_{p}\frac{e^{\frac{r^{2}+2r|z|}{2\alpha}}}{r^{2n}}\int_{B(z,r)}|f(w)|^{p}e^{-p\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\,dA(w)\,d\mu(z).

Let
R
be such that
\cup_{k=1}^{N}B(z_{k},2r)\subset B(0,R)
and let
M
be such number that every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is contained in most
M
ball
B(z_{k},2r).
Then for every
z\in B(z_{k},r),k\in\{1,\dots,N\},
the inequality
e^{\frac{r^{2}+2r|z|}{2\alpha}}\leq e^{\frac{r^{2}+2rR}{2\alpha}}
holds; hence

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{B(z_{k},r)}|f_{m}(z)|^{p}e^{-p\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\frac{e^{\frac{r^{2}+2rR}{2\alpha}}}{r^{2n}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{B(z_{k},2r)}|f_{m}(w)|^{p}e^{-p\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle MC_{p}\frac{e^{\frac{r^{2}+2rR}{2\alpha}}}{r^{2n}}\int_{B(0,R)}|f_{m}(w)|^{p}e^{-p\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\,dA(w)\to 0,

as
m\to\infty.
For the second term in (
2.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{C\epsilon}{r^{2n}}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\int_{B(z_{k},2r)}\left(\left|g_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}+\left|h_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{MC\epsilon}{r^{2n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left(\left|g_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}+\left|h_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(2\left\lVert P_{\alpha}\right\rVert+1)MC\epsilon}{r^{2n}}\left\lVert f_{m}\right\rVert_{p,\alpha}\leq C^{\prime}\epsilon.

Therefore,
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f_{m}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\alpha}}\right|^{p}d\mu(z)\leq C^{\prime}\epsilon,
which implies that
\mu
is vanishing. ∎

and

3.
Toeplitz operators

For every
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),
we define
T_{g}:\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
with

T_{g}(f)=P_{p,\alpha}^{ph}(fg),

where
P_{p,\alpha}^{ph}
is an orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}dA(z))
to
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).
The previously defined operator we call Toeplitz operator with symbol
g.
One can easily see that
T_{g}
is bounded and
\left\lVert T_{g}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\infty}.
Moreover,

T_{g}(f)(z)=\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)g(w)K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(w).

The previous formula motivates the definition of Toeplitz operators for a wider class of symbols.
If
\mu
is a Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n},
then we define

(3.1)

T_{\mu}(f)(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)f(w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

In order that the previous expression is defined for
f\in\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),
we consider only measures such that

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d|\mu|(w)<\infty,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Notice that if (
3.2
) holds, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)\right|e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d|\mu|(w)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d|\mu|(w)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d|\mu|(w)\right)^{1/2},

where the finiteness of the second factor we obtain from (
3.2
) for
z=0.

Therefore,
T_{\mu}(f)
is defined whenever
f
is a finite linear combination of kernel functions, i.e., on the fundamental subset of
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

If
d\mu=\varphi dA
, then we simply write
T_{\varphi}
instead of
T_{\mu}
. From now on, we will restrict our attention to Toeplitz operators with positive symbols.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}.
The operator
T_{\mu}
is bounded if and only if
\mu
is a
2
-Carleson measure.

Proof.

Let
T_{\mu}
is bounded. By the boundedness of
T_{\mu}
, we have
|\langle T_{\mu}f,f\rangle|\leq\left\lVert T_{\mu}\right\rVert\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}
for every
f\in\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and by Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|f(w)|^{2}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w)\leq\left\lVert T_{\mu}\right\rVert\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f(w)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(w).

Hence,
\mu
is a
2
-Carleson measure.

If
\mu
is a
2
-Carleson measure, then

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|f(w)|^{2}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f(w)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(w),

Using the previous inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain that

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)

is a bounded sesquilinear form on
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n});
therefore, there exists a bounded operator
T:\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
\langle Tf,g\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle_{\mu}.
Using the reproducing kernels we obtain

Tf(z)=\langle Tf,K_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\rangle=\langle f,K_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\rangle_{\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)K_{ph,\alpha}(w,z)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w)=T_{\mu}f(z),

hence
T_{\mu}
is bounded.
∎

In
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
the condition (
2.1
) is equivalent to weak convergence of the sequence of functions
(f_{m})
to the zero function. Indeed,
f_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}0
is equivalent to
(f_{m})
being bounded and
\langle f_{m},k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,w)\rangle\to 0,
for every
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n};
that is,
\left(f_{m}\right)
is bounded and
f_{m}(w)\to 0
for every
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Since

\left|f_{m}(z)\right|=\left|\langle f_{m},K_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\rangle\right|\leq\left\lVert f_{m}\right\rVert_{2,\alpha}\cdot\left\lVert K_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\right\rVert_{2,\alpha}\leq\left\lVert f_{m}\right\rVert_{2,\alpha}\sqrt{2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1},

we have that
f_{m}
is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}.
By the compactness principle (see
[
5
, Thm 2.2.11]
), we have that
(f_{m})
is normal. Since,
f_{n}(w)\to 0
for every
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and since
(f_{n})
is normal, we conclude that
f_{n}
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Operator
T_{\mu}
is a compact operator if and only if
\mu
is a vanishing
2
-Carleson measure.

Proof.

Let
T_{\mu}
be a compact operator and
\left(f_{m}\right)
be a bounded sequence of functions from
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, such that it converges to 0 uniformly on compact subsets, that is, by the previous discussion,
f_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}0
. Since
T_{\mu}
is compact, it follows that
\left\lVert T_{\mu}f_{m}\right\rVert\to 0
(see
[
12
, Thm 1.14]
), hence

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f_{m}(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)=\langle T_{\mu}f_{m},f_{m}\rangle\to 0,

as
m\to\infty.
Therefore,
\mu
is a vanishing
2
-Carleson measure.

If
\mu
is a vanishing
2
-Carleson measure, then
\mu
is a
2
-Carleson measure, and
T_{\mu}
is bounded. For every
f\in\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{\mu}f\right\rVert_{2,\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\mu}f,T_{\mu}f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}T_{\mu}f(z)\overline{f(z)}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|T_{\mu}f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|T_{\mu}f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(z)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert T_{\mu}\right\rVert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2,\alpha}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(z)\right)^{1/2}.

Hence, if
(f_{m})
is a bounded sequence functions from
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
f_{m}
converge uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n},
that is, by the previous discussion
f_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}0,
we have that
\left\lVert T_{\mu}f_{m}\right\rVert_{2,\alpha}\to 0.
As a consequence we conclude that
T_{\mu}
is compact (see
[
12
, Thm 1.14]
).
∎

3.1.
Berezin transform of a measure

If
\mu
is a measure satisfying condition (
3.2
), we define the Berezin transform of
\mu
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\frac{\left|K_{ph,\alpha}(z,u)\right|^{2}}{e^{\frac{|u|^{2}}{\alpha}}\left(2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right)}\,d\mu(u),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Also, if
T_{\mu}:\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
defined with (
3.1
) is bounded, then

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}K_{ph,\alpha}(z,w)f(w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w)=T_{\mu}f(z)=\langle T_{\mu}f,K_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\rangle,\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n});

hence

\widetilde{\mu}(z)=\langle T_{\mu}k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z),k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\rangle.

Then
|\widetilde{\mu}(z)|=|\langle T_{\mu}\left(k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\right),k_{ph,\alpha}(\cdot,z)\rangle|\leq\left\lVert T_{\mu}\right\rVert,
hence
\widetilde{\mu}
is a bounded function.

3.2.
Trace class

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mu\geq 0.
Then
T_{\mu}\in S_{1}
if and only if
\mu(\mathbb{C}^{n})<\infty.

Proof.

Since
\mu\geq 0,
we have that
T_{\mu}
is a positive operator. Therefore, by Theorem
2.4
,
T_{\mu}\in S_{1}
if and only if

\tr(T_{\mu})=\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\widetilde{\mu}(z)\left[2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right]e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(z)<\infty.

By Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\widetilde{\mu}(z)\left[2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right]e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\frac{|K_{ph,\alpha}(z,u)|^{2}}{e^{\frac{|u|^{2}}{\alpha}}\left(2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right)}\,d\mu(u)\left[2e^{\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right]e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|K_{ph,\alpha}(z,u)|^{2}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,dA(z)e^{-\frac{|u|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}K_{ph,\alpha}(u,u)e^{-\frac{|u|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left[2e^{\frac{|u|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1\right]e^{-\frac{|u|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(u).

Since
1\leq\frac{2e^{\frac{|u|^{2}}{\alpha}}-1}{e^{\frac{|u|^{2}}{\alpha}}}\leq 2,
the desired conclusion follows.
∎

3.3.
Schatten class

In this subsection, we provide some partial results about characterisation of the condition
T_{\mu}\in S_{p}
for
p>1.
We begin by considering absolutely continuous measures.
For every
p\in[1,\infty)
and for every
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dA)
, the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
is bounded. Indeed,
\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}+i\varphi_{3}-i\varphi_{4},
where
\varphi_{k}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dA)
are positive functions. Then for every
a\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
r>0
we have

\int_{B(a,r)}\varphi_{k}(z)\,dA(z)\leq m(B(a,r))^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{B(a,r)}|\varphi_{k}(z)|^{p}\,dA(z)\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\varphi(z)|^{p}\,dA(z)\right)^{1/p},

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. By Theorem
2.7
we have that
d\mu_{k}=\varphi_{k}dA
is a
p
-Carleson measure: Therefore,
T_{\varphi_{k}}
is a bounded operator by Proposition
3.1
, which implies that
T_{\varphi}
is bounded.

Also, similarly we can obtain that if
\varphi\in L^{1}(dA)
has compact support, then
T_{\mu}
is compact.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
p\geq 1
and
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dA(z)),
then
T_{\varphi}
is in Schatten class
S_{p}.

Proof.

Decomposing
\varphi
as a linear combination of four nonnegative functions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dA),
we can assume that
\varphi
is nonnegative. Assume also that
\varphi
has compact support in
\mathbb{C}^{n}.
Then
T_{\varphi}
is a positive compact operator. Therefore, it has a canonical decomposition of the form

T_{\varphi}f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\langle f,e_{n}\rangle e_{n},

where
\{e_{n}\}
is an orthonormal set in
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Following the same idea from
[
12
, Prop. 7.11]
, we obtain that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{n}|^{p}\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\varphi(z)|^{p}K_{ph,\alpha}(z,z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,\frac{dA(z)}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\leq\frac{2}{\left(\alpha\pi\right)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\varphi(z)|^{p}\,dA(z).

Therefore,
T_{\varphi}
is in
S_{p}.

The general case follows from monotone convergence and approximating
\varphi
with
\varphi\chi_{B(r,0)}.

∎

In the remainder of this paper, we present some results supporting the conjecture stated in the introduction.

Let
M=\mathcal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
N=\mathcal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\ominus\mathcal{A}
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the subspace set of constant functions. Then, we have
\mathcal{PH}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=M\oplus N
and
M\bot N.

We decompose
T_{\mu}
as

\begin{split}T_{\mu}(f)(z)&=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)e^{\frac{z\bar{w}}{\alpha}}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w)+\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)\left(e^{\frac{\bar{z}w}{\alpha}}-1\right)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w)\\
&=T_{M,\mu}(f)(z)+T_{N,\mu}(f)(z).\end{split}

One can easily notice that

\begin{split}\langle T_{M,\mu}f,g\rangle=\langle f,P_{M}g\rangle_{\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)\overline{P_{M}g(w)}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w);\\
\langle T_{N,\mu}f,g\rangle=\langle f,P_{N}g\rangle_{\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)\overline{P_{N}g(w)}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w).\end{split}

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
T_{\mu}\in S_{p}
then
\mu(B(\cdot,r))\in L^{p}(dA)
for every
r>0.

Proof.

If
(e_{n})
is a basis for
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{2},
then

\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle T_{\mu}e_{n},e_{n}\rangle|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq\left\lVert T_{\mu}\right\rVert_{p}

By the identities mentioned earlier, we have

\langle T_{\mu}e_{n},e_{n}\rangle=\langle T_{M,\mu}e_{n},e_{n}\rangle+\langle T_{N,\mu}e_{n},e_{n}\rangle=\langle T_{M,\mu}e_{n},e_{n}\rangle.

Therefore, we conclude that
T_{M,\mu}:\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{2}\to\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{2}
belongs to the Schatten class
S_{p}.
By
[
4
, Thm 4.4]
, it follows that
\mu(B(\cdot,r))\in L^{p}(dA),
for every
r>0.

∎

Sufficiency of the condition
Assume that
\mu(B(\cdot,r))\in L^{p}(dA).

Let us decompose
T_{\mu}=T_{M,\mu}P_{M}+T_{M,\mu}P_{N}+T_{N,\mu}P_{M}+T_{N,\mu}P_{N}.
Since
T_{M,\mu}:\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{2}\to\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{2}
is in class
S_{p},
we have
T_{M,\mu}P_{M}\in S_{p}
(as they have the same canonical decomposition). Futhermore, it is straightforward to see that
T_{N,\mu}:N\to N
is in
S_{p}.
Moreover, since

\langle T_{M,\mu}P_{N}x,y\rangle=\langle P_{N}x,P_{M}y\rangle_{\mu}=\langle x,T_{N,\mu}P_{M}y\rangle,

we conclude that
(T_{M,\mu}P_{N})^{*}=T_{N,\mu}P_{M},
and, hence it sufficies to prove that
T_{M}P_{N}
is in
S_{p}.

Assume additionally that
\mu
is a positive regular radial Borel measure. Then, the measure
\mu
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w)<\infty.
Following the method from
[
6
, Thm 2.4]
, we obtain that there exists a Borel measure
\nu
on
[0,\infty),
such that

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}f(r\zeta)\,d\sigma_{{2n-1}}(\zeta)\,d\nu(r).

Note that if
f
is a harmonic function, the previous formula implies that

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(w)e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu=f(0)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w).

In this case, we have

T_{M,\mu}P_{N}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{\frac{z\bar{w}}{\alpha}}\overline{P_{N}f(w)}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{\alpha}}\,d\mu(w)=0.

Therefore,
T_{M,\mu}P_{N}
is the zero operator; hence,
T_{M,\mu}P_{N}\in S_{p}
for every
p.
