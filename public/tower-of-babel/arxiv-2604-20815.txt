A dichotomy for hypergraph Zarankiewicz problems on axis-parallel boxes
Ting-Wei Chao, Zichao Dong, Hong Liu, Xichao Shu, Shuaichao Wang

Abstract
We study the Zarankiewicz problem for r r -partite, r r -uniform intersection hypergraphs arising from r r families of axis-parallel boxes in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with prescribed directions F 1 , … , F r ⊆ { 1 , … , d } F_{1},\dots,F_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,d\} . This extends the problems studied by Chan and Har-Peled on points and d d -dimensional boxes in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , corresponding to ( F 1 , F 2 ) = ( ∅ , [ d ] ) (F_{1},F_{2})=(\varnothing,[d]) , as well as by Chan, Keller, and Smorodinsky on r r families of d d -dimensional boxes, corresponding to ( F 1 , … , F r ) = ( [ d ] , … , [ d ] ) (F_{1},\dots,F_{r})=([d],\dots,[d]) . Our main result establishes a sharp dichotomy for the Zarankiewicz number in this setting: it is either Θ r ​ ( t ​ n r − 1 ) \Theta_{r}(tn^{r-1}) or at least Ω ⁡ ( t ​ n r − 1 ⋅ log ⁡ n log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) \Omega\bigl(tn^{r-1}\cdot\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\bigr) , depending only on a simple set-theoretic condition on ( F 1 , … , F r ) (F_{1},\dots,F_{r}) , which we call 2 2 - coherence . Informally, 2 2 -coherence captures whether the configuration contains an underlying two-dimensional incidence structure, which is precisely what gives rise to the extra polylogarithmic factor. Our proof proceeds via a sequence of reductions and a geometric slicing argument that reduces the problem to planar incidence bounds.

Introduction

In 1951, Zarankiewicz

25

[
18
,
2

4

5
]
show it is tight for
K_{s,t}
in place of
K_{t,t}
when
s\gg t

In 1964, Erdős
[
13

proposed a natural hypergraph analogue: determine the maximum number

r

r

[
20

12

are known when the largest parameter

is much larger than
t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1}
, for
K_{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}}^{(r)}
in place of
K_{t,\dots,t}^{(r)}
.

Zarankiewicz problems on axis-parallel boxes.

Bipartite graphs naturally model geometric incidences or intersections. Consider the incidence graph
G
between
n
points and
n
lines, where an edge connects a point
p
and a line
\ell
if
p\in\ell
. Since two points determine a unique line,
G
contains no
K_{2,2}
. The Kővári–Sós–Turán theorem then yields
e(G)=O(n^{3/2})
, while the Szemerédi–Trotter theorem
[
22
]
, a central result in incidence geometry, improves this bound to the tight
O(n^{4/3})
. The incidence problems are closely related to algorithmic problems in theoretical computer science such as range searching
[
1
]
and the so-called Hopcroft’s problem
[
10
]
. We refer to
[
21
,
7
,
9
]
for related studies on simplices, balls, pseudo-disks, and other geometric objects.

This paper studies Zarankiewicz problems for incidences between axis-parallel boxes. For any positive integer
n
, we use the standard notation
[n]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{1,\dots,n\}
. Throughout the whole paper, every

refers to an
axis-parallel box
. We require several notations.

In the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, an
axis-parallel box
is the product of
d
closed intervals
\bm{b}=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\dots\times[a_{d},b_{d}]
, where
a_{i}\leq b_{i}\,(i=1,\dots,d)
are real numbers. If
a_{i}=b_{i}
, then
\bm{b}

collapses
along the
i
-th coordinate axis. We call the set
\bigl\{i\in[d]:a_{i}<b_{i}\bigr\}
the

of
\bm{b}
. For example, the box
\{0\}\times[e,\pi]\times\{\frac{2}{3}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
collapses in the
1
-st and
3
-rd coordinates and has direction set
\{2\}
. In the literature, axis-parallel boxes usually exclude the case
a_{i}=b_{i}
. However, we allow such degeneracies and study a strictly more general class of configurations. We call boxes with direction set
[d]
(i.e., without any

)

.

Let
d\geq 1,\,r\geq 2
. In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, every
r
-
direction
-
vector

\vec{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}(F_{1},\dots,F_{r})
refers to an
r
-tuple of subsets of

\vec{F}

the maximum number of edges in

The Zarankiewicz problem between points and full boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\vec{F}=(\varnothing,[d])
, has been extensively studied. In the complexity analysis of orthogonal range reporting, Chazelle
[
11

constructed random points and boxes in

to deduce
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;2)=\Omega\bigl(n(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n})^{d-1}\bigr)
. This construction was later reformulated in mathematical language and generalized by Tomon

z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)=\Omega\bigl(tn(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n})^{d-1}\bigr)
. Basit, Chernikov, Starchenko, Tao, and Tran
[
3
]
obtained the upper bound
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)=O_{d,t}\bigl(n(\log n)^{2d}\bigr)
. This upper bound was later improved by Chan–Har-Peled
[
7
]
to
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)=O_{d}\bigl(tn(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n})^{d-1}\bigr)
, matching the lower bound and thereby resolving the problem.

The Zarankiewicz problem for full boxes has also attracted much attention. Keller and Smorodinsky
[
17
]
established
z_{2}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)=\Theta_{t}\bigl(n\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\bigr)
for rectangles, where
\vec{F}=([2],[2])
. Chan, Keller, and Smorodinsky
[
8

later extended this to incidence

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and improved the dependence on

z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)=\Theta_{r,d}\bigl(tn^{r-1}(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n})^{d-1}\bigr)
for
\vec{F}=\bigl(\underbrace{[d],\dots,[d]}_{r}\bigr)
.

For an arbitrary
\vec{F}
, the lower bound
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)\geq(t-1)\cdot n^{r-1}
is straightforward. Indeed, suppose each box in
\mathcal{B}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{B}_{r-1}
contains the origin. In
\mathcal{B}_{r}
, choose
t-1
boxes containing the origin and place the remaining
n-t+1
boxes far away so that they intersect no others. Then the intersection hypergraph is
K_{n,\dots,n,t-1}^{(r)}
, which contains no
K_{t,\dots,t}^{(r)}
, and thus yields
(t-1)\,n^{r-1}
hyperedges.

In some cases, this lower bound is tight. For example, for intervals
(\{1\},\{1\})
in
\mathbb{R}^{1}
and for planar vertical and horizontal segments
(\{1\},\{2\})
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it was previously established in
[
7
,
17
]
that

z_{1}^{(\{1\},\{1\})}(n;t)=\Theta(tn)
and
z_{2}^{(\{1\},\{2\})}(n;t)=\Theta(tn)
. On the other hand, an additional logarithmic factor is sometimes unavoidable: for the aforementioned
(\varnothing,[2])
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
([3],[3],[3])
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, it is known
[
7
,
9
]
that

z_{2}^{(\varnothing,[2])}(n;t)=\Theta\bigl(tn\tfrac{\log n}{\log\log n}\bigr)
and

z_{3}^{([3],[3],[3])}(n;t)=\Theta\bigl(tn^{2}(\tfrac{\log n}{\log\log n})^{2}\bigr)
.

Motivated by these results on
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)
for different direction-vectors
\vec{F}
, a natural question arises:

Under what conditions on
\vec{F}
does a polylogarithmic factor emerge in
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)
?

Our main finding is that this phenomenon admits a sharp dichotomy depending only on a simple set-theoretic property of the direction sets. Informally, a polylogarithmic factor emerges in
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)
when all but at most one of the families share at least two common coordinate directions. Formally, we refer to an
r
-direction-vector
\vec{F}=(F_{1},\dots,F_{r})
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
2
-
coherent
if there is some
k\in[r]
such that
\bigl|\bigcap_{j\in[r]\setminus\{k\}}F_{j}\bigr|\geq 2
, and
non
-
2
-
coherent
otherwise. The theorem below formalizes this dichotomy.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 1,\,r\geq 2,\,t\geq 2
be integers. For any
r
-direction-vector
\vec{F}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)=\begin{cases}\Theta_{r}(tn^{r-1})&\text{if $\vec{F}$ is non-$2$-coherent},\\
\Omega\bigl(tn^{r-1}\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\bigr)\qquad&\text{if $\vec{F}$ is $2$-coherent}.\end{cases}

In particular, this yields a complete characterization of those direction-vectors for which the trivial lower bound
\Omega(tn^{r-1})
is asymptotically optimal, identifying
2
-coherence as the threshold for the emergence of superlinear behavior. The four examples above fit this dichotomy perfectly: the interval case
(\{1\},\{1\})
in
\mathbb{R}^{1}
and the planar vertical-horizontal segment case
(\{1\},\{2\})
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are non-
2
-coherent and satisfy the linear bound
\Theta(tn)
, whereas the point-box case
(\varnothing,[2])
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the full-box case
([3],[3],[3])
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are
2
-coherent and exhibit an extra polylogarithmic factor.

At a high level, our proof reveals that the appearance of a polylogarithmic factor is driven by an underlying two-dimensional incidence structure. When
\vec{F}
is
2
-coherent, such a structure is explicitly present, allowing us to embed planar constructions with polylogarithmic growth and obtain the lower bound. In contrast, the main difficulty lies in the non-
2
-coherent case, where no such structure is a priori visible. To overcome this, we develop a sequence of reductions that gradually impose geometric structure on the problem. A key role is played by the
canonical
case
\vec{F}=([d]\setminus\{1\},\dots,[d]\setminus\{d\})
, where each box in
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional box perpendicular to the
i
-th coordinate direction. This case extends earlier work of Chan–Keller–Smorodinsky
[
17
,
9
]
on vertical and horizontal segments in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, corresponding to
z_{2}^{(\{1\},\{2\})}(n;t)
, and serves as a central intermediate step in our argument. The key technical step is a geometric slicing argument that decomposes high-dimensional intersections into collections of planar incidence problems, to which sharp two-dimensional bounds can be applied. This ultimately allows us to recover the optimal
O(tn^{r-1})
upper bound. We defer a more detailed proof sketch to
Section

3
. This reduction framework, which isolates and exploits hidden low-dimensional structures, may be of independent interest for other geometric Zarankiewicz-type problems.

Related Zarankiewicz problems.

When
\vec{F}
is non-
2
-coherent, our result shows that the upper bound matches the trivial lower bound asymptotically, hence yields a tight characterization of the extremal behavior in this regime. This contrasts with the general theory of semi-algebraic incidence graphs, where one typically cannot obtain bounds of order
n^{r-1}
. Recall that a bipartite graph
G
is
semi-algebraic
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if its vertex sets are point sets
P,Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and an edge
(p,q)
is present if and only if it satisfies a Boolean combination of finitely many polynomial equalities and inequalities. For such graphs, in a seminal work Fox, Pach, Sheffer, Suk, and Zahl
[
14
]
proved that if
G
is
K_{t,t}
-free, then
e(G)=O\Bigl(n^{\frac{2d}{d+1}+\varepsilon}\Bigr)
, independently of
t
. The
\varepsilon
-loss was later removed by Tidor and Yu
[
23
]
, who showed
e(G)=O\Bigl(n^{\frac{2d}{d+1}}\Bigr)
, with extensions to incidence hypergraphs as well. These bounds remain significantly larger than the linear-type bound
O(tn^{r-1})
in our non-
2
-coherent setting, reflecting the greater rigidity of incidences involving axis-parallel boxes.

3
The upper bound—overview of ideas

For every
r
-size-vector
\vec{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
of positive integers and every integer
t\geq 2
, write

g_{t}(\vec{n})=g_{t}(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}tn_{1}\cdots n_{r}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\dots+\frac{1}{n_{r}}\Bigr).

Then
g_{t}\bigl(\underbrace{n,\dots,n}_{r}\bigr)=trn^{r-1}
.
We will prove a strengthening of the upper bounds in
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 1,\,r\geq 2,\,t\geq 2
, and
\vec{F}
be a non-
2
-coherent
r
-direction-vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then there exists a constant
\Lambda_{r}>0
such that
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(\vec{n};t)\leq\Lambda_{r}\cdot g_{t}(\vec{n})
holds for every
r
-size-vector
\vec{n}
.

As noted in
Section

1
, the theorem is tight up to a constant factor in the
n_{1}=\dots=n_{r}
balanced case. In general,
Theorem

3.1
is again tight up to a constant factor, as shown by the following construction.
Partition
\mathcal{B}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{B}_{r}
into
r
disjoint groups so that, for each
j\in[r]
, the
j
-th group contains
\frac{n_{j}}{r}
boxes from
\mathcal{B}_{j}
. In the
j
-th group, arrange
n_{j}-t
boxes from
\mathcal{B}_{j}
to be far away so that they are disjoint with others, while every other box from
\mathcal{B}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{B}_{r}
in the same group contains a fixed point. Summing over all groups yields
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{n_{1}}{r}\cdots\frac{n_{j-1}}{r}\cdot t\cdot\frac{n_{j+1}}{r}\cdots\frac{n_{r}}{r}=\frac{g_{t}(\vec{n})}{r^{r-1}}
hyperedges.

Our proof proceeds through several reduction steps, ultimately reducing the problem to a planar incidence bound.

Observation 4.2

Suppose

and let

(

†
†

†

by a union of

\vec{F}_{d}
-family

\Gamma_{s}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. Write
n_{1}^{(s)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\bigl|\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(s)}\bigr|,\,n_{2}^{(s)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\bigl|\mathcal{B}_{2}^{(s)}\bigr|
. It follows that

\text{$\sum_{s\in S}n_{1}^{(s)}=n_{1},\ \sum_{s\in S}n_{2}^{(s)}=n_{2}$, \, and $\bigl|\mathcal{B}^{(s)}_{i}\bigr|\leq n_{i}$ holds for $i=3,4,\dots,r$ and every $s\in S$}.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{(s)}
be the incidence hypergraph of
\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{(s)}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{B}^{(s)}_{r}\bigr)
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{(s)}
is a subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
, and hence it is again
K_{t,\dots,t}^{(r)}
-free. Since these subgraphs
\mathcal{H}^{(s)}\,(s\in S)
cover
\mathcal{H}
, we obtain
e(\mathcal{H})\leq\sum_{s\in S}e\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{(s)}\bigr)
.

We first verify that the induction hypothesis is applicable to
\mathcal{H}^{(s)}
.

Claim 4.8
.

The direction vector
\vec{F}^{\prime}
is non-2-coherent.

Proof.

For any
k\in[r]
, from
F^{\prime}_{j}\subseteq F_{j}\,(\forall j\in[r])
we deduce that

\Biggl|\bigcap_{j\in[r]\setminus\{k\}}F^{\prime}_{j}\Biggr|\leq\Biggl|\bigcap_{j\in[r]\setminus\{k\}}F_{j}\Biggr|\leq 1.\qed

So, the inductive hypothesis implies that
e\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{(s)}\bigr)\leq\Lambda_{r}\cdot g_{t}\bigl(n_{1}^{(s)},n_{2}^{(s)},n_{3},\dots,n_{r}\bigr)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in S}e\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{(s)}\bigr)\leq\Lambda_{r}\cdot\sum_{s\in S}g_{t}\bigl(n_{1}^{(s)},n_{2}^{(s)},n_{3},\dots,n_{r}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{r}\cdot t\cdot\sum_{s\in S}n_{1}^{(s)}n_{2}^{(s)}n_{3}\cdots n_{r}\cdot\biggl(\frac{1}{n_{1}^{(s)}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}^{(s)}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}+\dots+\frac{1}{n_{r}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{r}\cdot t\cdot\biggl(\sum_{s\in S}n_{2}^{(s)}n_{3}\cdots n_{r}+\sum_{s\in S}n_{1}^{(s)}n_{3}\cdots n_{r}+\sum_{s\in S}n_{1}^{(s)}n_{2}^{(s)}n_{3}\cdots n_{r}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n_{3}}+\dots+\frac{1}{n_{r}}\Bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{r}\cdot t\cdot\biggl(n_{2}n_{3}\cdots n_{r}+n_{1}n_{3}\cdots n_{r}+n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}\cdots n_{r}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n_{3}}+\dots+\frac{1}{n_{r}}\Bigr)\biggr)=\Lambda_{r}\cdot g_{t}(\vec{n}),

where at the last “
\leq
” we used the facts
\sum_{s\in S}n_{1}^{(s)}=n_{1},\,\sum_{s\in S}n_{2}^{(s)}=n_{2}
, and
\sum_{s\in S}n_{1}^{(s)}n_{2}^{(s)}\leq n_{1}n_{2}
.

Case 2: for every
i\in[d]
there is at most one
j\in[r]
such that
i\notin F_{j}
.
Denote
F_{j}^{\mathsf{c}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}[d]\setminus F_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,r
. In this case, we know that
F_{1}^{\mathsf{c}},\dots,F_{r}^{\mathsf{c}}
are mutually disjoint. Moreover, we have the following equivalent formulation of the non-2-coherent condition on
\vec{F}
:

\Biggl|\bigcap_{j\in[r]\setminus\{k\}}F_{j}\Biggr|\leq 1\,(\forall k\in[r])\iff\Biggl|\bigcup_{j\in[r]\setminus\{k\}}F_{j}^{\mathsf{c}}\Biggr|\geq d-1\,(\forall k\in[r]).

(14)

From (
14
) and the mutual disjointness of
F_{j}^{\mathsf{c}}
, we see that
|F_{j}^{\mathsf{c}}|\leq 1
for all
j\in[r]
. Moreover,
F_{1}^{\mathsf{c}},\dots,F_{r}^{\mathsf{c}}
have to cover
[d]
. Indeed, if not, then

•

either
F_{j}^{\mathsf{c}}=\varnothing
for all
j\in[r]
, which is impossible due to (
14
) and
d\geq 2
,

•

or there exists
k
with
\lvert F_{k}^{\mathsf{c}}\rvert=1
, yielding a contradiction against (
14
).

Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume that

F_{j}=\begin{cases}[d]\setminus\{j\}\qquad&\text{if $j\in\{1,\dots,d\}=[d]$},\\
[d]\qquad&\text{if $j\in\{d+1,\dots,r\}=[r]\setminus[d]$}.\end{cases}

Now, we “transfer” the family
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{B}_{r})
into
\mathbb{R}^{r}
in two steps as follows.

•

First, embed each
\bm{b}\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\mathcal{B}_{j}
into
\mathbb{R}^{r}
by identifying it with
\bm{b}\times\underbrace{\{0\}\times\dots\times\{0\}}_{r-d}
.

•

Next, for any
\bm{b}\in\mathcal{B}_{j}
with
j>d
, thicken it in the
(d+1)
-th through the
r
-th coordinates, except the
j
-th coordinate, by arbitrary lengths.

This process above yields a canonical family in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
with exactly the same intersection hypergraph. Applying
Proposition

4.6
to the “transferred” family, we obtain
e(\mathcal{H})\leq M_{r}\cdot g_{t}(\vec{n})
.

Combining the above case analysis, the inductive proof of
Theorem

3.1
is complete.
∎

Concluding remarks

Given an
\vec{F}
-family of boxes
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{B}_{r})
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we establish in

a sharp dichotomy for
\vec{F}
that precisely characterizes when a polylogarithmic factor appears in

. In particular, we have

z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)=\Omega\bigl(tn^{r-1}\tfrac{\log n}{\log\log n}\bigr)

for every

-coherent

. However, it has been shown

]
that

holds for

and that

holds for

. These naturally lead to the following question concerning the correct asymptotics of

in general.

Question 5.1
.

Determine the exact order of magnitude of
z_{d}^{\vec{F}}(n;t)
when
\vec{F}
is
2
-coherent.

To address
5.1
, we single out the following two cases as fundamental instances.

Question 5.2

Determine the asymptotics of

for
\vec{F}=(\{1\},\{2,3\})
.

Question 5.3
.

for

.

Appendix A
Proof of
Lemma

In
\mathbb{R}^{1}
, there are only two possible direction sets,
\varnothing
or
\{1\}
. A box family with direction set

is a collection of points, and a box family with direction set

, we begin with the

4.7
for any
r
sets of segments. Finally, we can thicken all the points in
\mathcal{B}_{i}
with direction sets

r

Throughout this appendix, every set refers to a multiset.

We begin with the case of points and segments, corresponding to

in
[

, Lemma 2.1]
. We include a proof here to remove the assumption

in their statement.

Proposition A.1
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{1}
, let
\mathcal{A}
be a set of
m
points and
\mathcal{B}
be a set of
n
segments. Denote by
G
the intersection graph between
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}

is

-free (

from the
\mathcal{A}

Proof.

Order

. Set
\ell\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lceil\frac{m}{s}\rceil
and partition
\mathcal{A}
into

\mathcal{A}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\bigl\{a_{(i-1)s+1},a_{(i-1)s+2},\dots,a_{is}\bigr\}\qquad&\text{if $i\in[\ell-1]$},\\
\bigl\{a_{(\ell-1)s+1},a_{(\ell-1)s+2},\dots,a_{m}\bigr\}\qquad&\text{if $i=\ell$}.\end{cases}

Write
\mathcal{B}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{\bm{b}\in\mathcal{B}:\bm{b}\cap\mathcal{A}_{i}\neq\varnothing\}
and denote by

the intersection graph between
\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{B}_{i}
. From the definitions we deduce that

It remains to bound

. Suppose

\displaystyle e(G)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}e(G_{i})=e(G_{\ell})+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}e(G_{i})

\displaystyle\leq ns+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\bigl((t-1)s+n_{i}(s-1)\bigr)
