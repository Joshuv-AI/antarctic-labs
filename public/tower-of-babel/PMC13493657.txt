Abrocitinib Achieves Early and Complete/Near-Complete Skin Clearance Plus Itch-Free State in Atopic Dermatitis: Phase 3 Pooled Post Hoc Analysis
Authors: Stephen Weidinger, Kristian Reich, Naiem Issa, H. Chih-ho Hong, Christopher G. Bunick, Pinaki Biswas, Gary Chan, Erman Güler, Melissa Watkins, Justine Alderfer

Introduction

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic inflammatory skin condition characterized by recurring eczematous lesions and intense itch [1]. Patients with moderate-to-severe AD unresponsive to topical treatment may receive systemic therapies, including dupilumab, a subcutaneous monoclonal antibody against interleukin (IL)-4 receptor alpha, or abrocitinib, an oral, once-daily, Janus kinase 1 (JAK1)-selective inhibitor [1–3].

The Aiming High in Eczema/Atopic Dermatitis (AHEAD) recommendations to clinicians established an approach for minimal disease activity targets, combining patient-reported outcomes with clinician-reported outcomes [4]. Attaining stringent outcomes such as ≥ 90% improvement from baseline in the Eczema Area and Severity Index score (EASI-90) and Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating score of 0 to 1 (PP-NRS0/1) is associated with disease suppression and clinically meaningful improvements in quality of life, although the clinical validity of this combined endpoint has not yet been established [4–6]. The goal of optimal treatment targets is to elevate the standard of care for patients with AD. Across the JADE phase 2 and 3 monotherapy trials, high proportions of patients achieved stringent outcomes, including EASI-90, 100% improvement from baseline in EASI score (EASI-100), and PP-NRS0/1 with abrocitinib 100 or 200 mg [5]. In phase 3 trials with concomitant topical therapy, greater proportions of patients achieved stringent outcomes with abrocitinib 200 mg versus dupilumab (e.g., EASI-100; ≥ 4-point reduction from baseline in PP-NRS score [PP-NRS4]) or abrocitinib 100 mg versus dupilumab (e.g., EASI-100; Investigator’s Global Assessment score of 0 [IGA 0]; PP-NRS0/1) [6, 7].

In the post hoc analysis reported here, we assessed patients with AD receiving abrocitinib with respect to achieving the stringent composite outcome of EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 in the JADE DARE (NCT04345367) and JADE COMPARE (NCT03720470) clinical trials, including the impact of baseline itch, and in those who switched to abrocitinib after inadequate response to dupilumab in JADE EXTEND (NCT03422822).

Methods

Detailed methods were described in previous reports for JADE DARE [6], JADE COMPARE [7], and JADE EXTEND [8]. In the randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled, parallel-treatment JADE DARE clinical trial, adults with moderate-to-severe AD received abrocitinib 200 mg/day or dupilumab 300 mg/2 weeks for 26 weeks with background topical therapy. In the multicenter, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, placebo-controlled JADE COMPARE trial, adults received abrocitinib 100 or 200 mg/day or dupilumab 300 mg/2 weeks for 16 weeks with background topical therapy. JADE EXTEND is an ongoing long-term extension safety trial for eligible patients who completed a qualifying parent study for abrocitinib. Patients were randomized to abrocitinib 100 or 200 mg/day with or without topical corticosteroids.

All study documents and procedures were approved by the appropriate institutional review boards/ethics committees at each study site. The studies were conducted in compliance with the ethical principles from the Declaration of Helsinki and all International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All local regulatory requirements were followed. Informed consent was obtained from all individual participants included in the studies.

Abrocitinib response using EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 was evaluated post hoc across JADE DARE, COMPARE, and EXTEND. Patients were also stratified by baseline itch score (PP-NRS4-10) in JADE COMPARE. Change from baseline using the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) was assessed at weeks 12 and 16 in JADE COMPARE among patients who achieved EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1. In JADE EXTEND, EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 was evaluated in dupilumab inadequate responders (i.e., did not achieve EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 by week 16 in JADE COMPARE). Similar analyses were assessed for EASI-75 + PP-NRS4 and EASI-90 + PP-NRS4. Data are summarized using descriptive statistics with number and proportion of patients. If patients withdrew, they were considered inadequate responders after withdrawal. EASI, PP-NRS, and DLQI information were used with permission.

Results

Patient demographics and disease characteristics were described in previous reports for JADE DARE [6] and COMPARE [7] and were comparable across patients who received abrocitinib 200 mg, abrocitinib 100 mg, dupilumab, or placebo.

Overall, greater proportions of patients treated with abrocitinib in JADE DARE and COMPARE achieved the stringent composite endpoint. In JADE DARE, higher proportions of patients achieved EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 as early as week 2 of treatment with abrocitinib 200 mg (5.8%) compared with dupilumab (1.1%) (Fig. 1a). The proportion of patients achieving EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 continued to increase and was maintained through week 26. Numerically greater proportions of patients in JADE COMPARE achieved EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 as early as week 4 of treatment with abrocitinib 200 mg (18.3%) or 100 mg (7.6%) compared with dupilumab (2.6%) or placebo (1.6%) (Fig. 1b). Greater proportions of patients continued to achieve EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 with abrocitinib 200 mg relative to those receiving dupilumab or placebo through week 16, while proportions were numerically higher with overlapping confidence intervals for patients receiving abrocitinib 100 mg and dupilumab (Fig. 1b). Among patients with severe itch (PP-NRS7-10) at baseline, EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 response rates were numerically higher, with overlapping confidence intervals for abrocitinib 200 or 100 mg versus dupilumab or placebo at week 16 (Fig. 1c). Among patients with moderate itch (PP-NRS 4–6), both doses of abrocitinib and dupilumab achieved similar responses; however, when focusing on patients with moderate itch scores of PP-NRS 5 or PP-NRS 6 separately, response rates again showed a numerical separation for abrocitinib 200 or 100 mg versus dupilumab or placebo with overlapping confidence intervals (Fig. 1c). Discussion

Abrocitinib 200 and 100 mg demonstrated early onset of response and an advantage over dupilumab in achieving the endpoint EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1. Response rates were observed as early as week 2 in JADE DARE and week 4 in JADE COMPARE. The proportion of patients achieving the stringent composite endpoint was comparable with previously reported efficacy outcomes in JADE DARE and COMPARE, including EASI-90, EASI-100, PP-NRS4, PP-NRS0/1, and IGA 0 [6, 7]. Differences in response rates between abrocitinib and dupilumab, up to approximately 12% for EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1, were analogous with those in the head-to-head LEVEL UP phase 3b/4 study in which an approximately 11% increased response rate was observed for upadacitinib, a JAK inhibitor, and dupilumab [9]. Safety outcomes have been previously reported and are consistent with those previously observed for abrocitinib across the JADE clinical development program [6, 7, 10].

The EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 response rates in patients receiving abrocitinib 200 and 100 mg and the advantages of abrocitnib at these doses over dupilumab were durable and sustained through the end of each study. Similar proportions of patients in JADE EXTEND achieved and sustained EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 up to week 112 of treatment with abrocitinib [11]. Stringent response rates achieved with both doses of abrocitinib were consistent across baseline itch and inadequate responder subgroups. In JADE COMPARE, numerically greater proportions of patients with more severe baseline itch who received abrocitinib achieved EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 by week 16 compared with dupilumab or placebo. Dupilumab response was greatest in patients with more moderate baseline itch (PP-NRS4). Dupilumab inadequate responders achieved both moderate and stringent endpoints after switching to abrocitinib 200 or 100 mg, suggesting that attaining minimal disease activity with abrocitinib is possible for patients regardless of prior dupilumab exposure. These results further support real-world comparative data demonstrating the efficacy of abrocitinib in patients with inadequate response to dupilumab [12].

Our data reinforce AHEAD’s recommendations [4] and demonstrate that higher threshold outcomes, including both clinician- and patient-reported outcomes, can elevate the standard-of-care in patients with AD. These stringent outcomes allow providers to observe and record improvements in quality-of-life outcomes (e.g., DLQI) over time. Our data suggest that patients achieving an optimal endpoint, such as EASI-90 + PP-NRS0/1 scores [4], had greater, clinically meaningful DLQI improvements (≥ 4-point improvement from baseline). Numerically greater improvements were observed after 16 weeks of treatment with abrocitinib compared with dupilumab [7]. Those patients on placebo who achieved this composite endpoint also demonstrated similar changes in DLQI scores comparable to abrocitinib 100 mg, indicating that other factors may also impact quality of life measures. This knowledge can aid healthcare providers in optimizing AD management.

Patients randomized to either dose of abrocitinib achieved early stringent responses compared with dupilumab and these generally remained numerically higher through to study end. Patients randomized to abrocitinib 200 mg often achieved better response rates than those randomized to abrocitinib 100 mg, dupilumab, or placebo. Our results align with previous studies in suggesting that abrocitinib may support disease suppression, with previous results demonstrating that abrocitinib improved skin and itch symptoms and downregulated genes associated with AD pathology (e.g., T-helper 2 and 22 cytokines) in a dose-dependent manner [13]. Given the strong efficacy of abrocitinib 200 mg across time points, patients not responsive to abrocitinib 100 mg after 4 weeks may consider dose escalation [14]. These data also support the recommendations from the European Union, which suggest treatment initiation of abrocitinib 200 mg for patients with AD who are not at higher risk of venous thromboembolism, major adverse cardiac events, or malignancies [15].

This analysis was limited by its post hoc nature and lack of formal statistical testing; all findings should be considered exploratory. The duration of treatments across JADE DARE and COMPARE were relatively short (16–26 weeks). Further analyses using consistent comparators over a longer treatment period will provide a more complete characterization of abrocitinib’s efficacy in achieving stringent endpoints.

Conclusion

Overall, substantial proportions of patients treated with abrocitinib 200 or 100 mg achieved a composite stringent response of near complete/complete skin clearance and an itch-free state. Patients with severe itch and inadequate response to dupilumab also achieved these optimal endpoints with both abrocitinib doses (100 or 200 mg). Treatment with abrocitinib in patients may provide a complete and robust response in those with moderate-to-severe AD, regardless of prior dupilumab exposure, and support the role of abrocitinib in AD suppression and elevation of treatment standards.
