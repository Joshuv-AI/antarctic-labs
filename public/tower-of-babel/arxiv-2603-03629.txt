Well-posedness and mean-field limit of discontinuous weighted dynamics via the relative entropy method
Immanuel Ben-Porat, José A. Carrillo, Alexandra Holzinger

Abstract
We consider deterministic particle dynamics with time evolving weights and their associated Kolmogorov equation and mean-field equation. We prove existence and uniqueness for the limit PDE alongside estimates on the growth of the logarithmic gradient as well as existence of weak solutions for the Kolmogorov equation satisfying an appropriate entropy inequality. We then apply these estimates and the relative entropy method as developed in 17 , in order to derive the associated equation as a mean field limit. Our results cover both interactions and influence kernels with mild regularity assumptions.

Introduction

In this paper we are concerned with the mean-field limit
of the following system of ODEs

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\dot{x_{i}}^{N}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j}^{N}(t)\mathbf{a}(x_{j}^{N}(t)-x_{i}^{N}(t)),\ x_{i}^{N}(0)=x_{i}^{0,N}\\
\dot{m}_{i}^{N}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}S(x_{i}^{N}(t),m_{i}^{N}(t),x_{j}^{N}(t),m_{j}^{N}(t)),\ m_{i}^{N}(0)=m_{i}^{0,N}.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

These kind of systems find applications in opinion dynamics
1
;
2
;
14
, neuroscience
13
and other scientific fields, see
7
and the references therein.

Here
\mathbf{a}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a given
interaction kernel and
S:(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a given
influence kernel. We are interested in the large population limit of (
1.1
) as
N\to\infty
of the distribution
\psi_{N}
of
(x_{i},m_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
, which fulfils the
Kolmogorov equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi_{N}+\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\textnormal{div}_{x_{i}}\bigg(\psi_{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j}\mathbf{a}(x_{i}-x_{j})\bigg)+\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\partial_{m_{i}}\bigg(\psi_{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}S(x_{i},m_{i},x_{j},m_{j})\bigg)=0\\
\psi_{N}(0,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})=\psi_{N,0}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Here, the unknown
\psi_{N}(t,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})

is a time dependent probability density

\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
and we apply the shorthand notation
(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\coloneqq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N},m_{1},\ldots,m_{N})
. The reason of viewing the ODE system in
(x,m)
lies in the fact that the Kolmogorov equation for the spatial distribution of
\mathbf{x}_{N}
in (
1.1
) is not a closed equation.

The ultimate goal of this article is to show a propagation of chaos result for
\psi_{N}
in the large population limit
N\to\infty
towards the product measure
\psi^{\otimes N}
, where
\psi
is a solution of the following non-local PDE on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi+\textnormal{div}_{x}\big(\psi\,\mathbf{a}\star_{x}\mu[\psi]\big)+\partial_{m}(\psi\,\mathbf{S}[\psi])=0,\\
\psi(0,x,m)=\psi_{0}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

Here,
\mu[\psi]
is defined as

\mu[\psi](t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}n\psi(t,x,n)\ {\rm d}n\,,

(1.4)

designating the first moment with respect to the weight variable and

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}[\psi](x,m)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}S(x,m,y,n)\psi(y,n)\ {\rm d}n{\rm d}y.

The solution to (
1.3
) is a time-dependent probability density
\psi(t,\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
. The propagation of chaos property of (
1.1
) is understood in the following
L^{1}
-sense: For
1\leq k\leq N
set

\displaystyle\psi_{N:k}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{m}_{k})\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d(N-k)}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N-k}}\psi_{N}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{N-k},\mathbf{m}_{k},\mathbf{m}_{N-k})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N-k}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N-k}.

(1.5)

The density
\psi_{N:k}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{dk}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k})
is called the
k
-th marginal of
\psi_{N}
. Typically, one aims to show that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\psi_{N:k}
converges in
L^{1}((\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{k})
to the product measure
\psi^{\otimes k}
, i.e.

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\psi_{N:k}(t,\cdot)-\psi^{\otimes k}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{1}((\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{k})}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0

(1.6)

on a suitable time interval
[0,T]
.
This convergence also implies the more classical probabilistic propagation of chaos, that is,
the weak convergence of the empirical measure

\mu_{N}(t):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{(x,m)=(x_{i}(t),m_{i}(t))}
towards
\psi
.
The underlying strategy of our work is to adapt the well-known relative entropy approach for quantitative estimates of the mean-field limit as in
17
to the present settings. We aim at analyzing the distance between
\psi_{N:k}
and
\psi^{\otimes k}
by showing the vanishing as
N\rightarrow\infty
of the relative entropy, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{H}(\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)|\psi^{\otimes N}(t,\cdot))

(1.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\psi_{N}(t,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\log\bigg(\frac{\psi_{N}(t,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})}{\psi^{\otimes N}(t,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})}\bigg)\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0,

uniformly in
t
on some short time interval. It is classical to deduce (
1.6
) and the mean-field limit from (
1.7
) via the Csiszár-Kullback-Pinsker (aka CKP) inequality, which provides an estimate of the form

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{N:k}-\psi^{\otimes k}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2k\mathcal{H}(\psi_{N}\mid\psi^{\otimes N})}.

1.1
Previous results

The study of mean-field limits with time evolving
weights has recently drawn the attention of the mathematical community.
Let us first remark that if
S
takes the form
S(x,m,y,n)=mns(x,y)
for some given
s:\mathbb{T}^{2d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then, by taking the first moment of (
1.3
)
with respect to the weight variable
m
, one readily checks that
\mathcal{\mu}(t,x)
(as defined in (
1.4
))
is governed by the non-local transport equation with source term

\partial_{t}\mu+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\mu\,\mathbf{a}\ast\mu)=h\left[\mu\right]

(1.8)

where the source term
h[\mu]
is given by

h\left[\mu\right](x)\coloneqq\mu(x)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}s(x,y)\mu(y)\ {\rm d}y.

In accordance, one considers the
weighted
empirical measure

\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{i}(t)\delta_{x=x_{i}(t)}
.
Assuming that
\mathbf{a}
and
s
are Lipschitz and that
s
satisfies an appropriate parity condition, in
22

the mean-field limit equation (
1.8
) on the whole space has been derived directly from the trajectories (
1.1
) by proving
W_{1}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(t,\cdot),\mu(t,\cdot))\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0

uniformly in time provided this is true initially, i.e. provided
W_{1}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(0,\cdot),\mu(0,\cdot))\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0
. In the same work
the well posedness of the Cauchy problem associated with (
1.8
) has been
also addressed. The well-posedness theory of equations of
the type (
1.8
), or variants thereof, has been also handled in
21
;
20
. The mean field limit and well posedness for the 1D
whole space case where
\mathbf{a}
exhibits an attractive Coulombic singularity

\mathbf{a}(x)=\mathrm{sgn}(x)
has been handled in
4
via
the theory of entropy solutions for 1D conservations laws. Recently,
the same problem where
\mathbf{a}
exhibits a repulsive Coulombic singularity in higher
dimensions has been studied in
6
using a commutator estimate
approach. The case where
\mathbf{a}
is Lipschitz but
s

has a jump discontinuity at the origin has been introduced in
18

where it is referred to as the pairwise competition model, alongside
the study of long time behavior. The weak mean field limit of the
pairwise competition model has been established in
5
as a
consequence of the graph limit of the underlying dynamics. The major
limitation of this approach is that it necessitates choosing the initial
datum (both at the microscopic and macroscopic level) in a specific
manner – in particular,
L^{p}
initial data is not admissible with
this approach. Other works which consider the graph limit and its link with the mean field limit include

1
;
8
;
19
. The case where the weights form a non-symmetric matrix is also interesting, as it renders the system to be non-exchangeable, and has been considered in
3
;
14
;
15
. An extensive overview of opinion dynamic models and their various exchangeable and non-exchangeable limits can be found in
2
;
19
.

A different active area in the theory of mean field limits is the relative entropy method, which has been initiated in the work
16
, and further developed within
9
;
10
;
17
. Let us briefly recall the main idea of the relative entropy method for the simpler case where
S=0
and
m_{j}=1
for all
1\leq j\leq N
. The associated Kolmogorov equation in this case is also known as the Liouville equation and writes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{N}+\frac{1}{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\textnormal{div}_{x_{i}}\bigg(\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\mathbf{a}(x_{i}-x_{j})\psi_{N}\bigg)=0,\ \psi_{N}(0,\mathbf{x}_{N})=\psi_{N,0}

(1.9)

while the limit equation is the homogeneous non-local transport equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\psi\,\mathbf{a}\star\psi)=0,\ \psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}.

(1.10)

The quantity whose evolution is studied is the
relative entropy
, defined as follows. Given
\psi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

we define
\overline{\psi_{N}}\coloneqq\psi^{\otimes N}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{dN})
and for
each
\psi_{N}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{dN})

we define the relative entropy analogously to the case of varying weights by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\psi_{N}\left|\overline{\psi_{N}}\right.)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{dN}}\psi_{N}(\mathbf{x}_{N})\log\left(\frac{\psi_{N}(\mathbf{x}_{N})}{\overline{\psi_{N}}(\mathbf{x}_{N})}\right)\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}.

A remarkable observation made in
16
is that if
\mathbf{a}
is bounded and divergence free (
\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{a})=0
), the time dependent relative entropy

\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)\coloneqq\mathcal{H}(\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)\left|\overline{\psi_{N}}(t,\cdot)\right.)
, where
\psi(t,\cdot)
is taken to be the solution of (
1.10
) and
\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)

the solution of (
1.9
), satisfies the evolution estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)\leq\left(\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)e^{t}.

(1.11)

Depending on the specifics of the problem, the above estimate is valid either on any time interval on which the solution exists or on a time interval possibly shorter than the maximal existence time.

1.2
Main results and assumptions

In this work we are concerned with the mean field limit leading from (
1.2
) to (
1.3
) in the case where both
\mathbf{a}
and
S
exhibit some roughness. More specifically,
\mathbf{a}
is merely bounded and divergence free, which is essentially the same class of kernels considered in
16
, and
S
exhibits
W^{1,1}
regularity in the variables
x,y
. However, the utility of the
W^{1,1}
regularity of
S
is used in the proof that
\psi_{N}
satisfies an appropriate entropy inequality, and is not needed for what concerns the existence and uniqueness of the limit PDE. The relative entropy approach allows us to derive the mean-field limit
of the system (
1.1
) for the case where
\mathbf{a}
and
S
exhibit very mild regularity, which clearly extends
22
.
We start by stating the assumptions on the interaction kernel
a
, influence kernel
S
and the initial data:

Assumption

(
H1
)
.

We impose the following assumptions on the kernels in (
1.2
).

i.

Regularity of interaction kernel:
\mathbf{a\in}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathrm{div}_{x}(\mathbf{a})=0
.

ii.

Regularity of
S
:

S\in L^{\infty}_{x,y,n}W^{2,\infty}_{m}

\overline{S}_{0},\overline{S}_{1},\overline{S}_{2}>0

\displaystyle\left|S(x,m,y,n)\right|\leq\overline{S}_{0},\ \left|\partial_{m}S(x,m,y,n)\right|\leq\overline{S}_{1},\ \left|\partial_{mm}S(x,m,y,n)\right|\leq\overline{S}_{2}.

In addition, for any

\mathrm{supp}(S(x,\cdot,y,n))\Subset(0,\infty)

Furthermore, we assume that for all

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{m}^{\alpha}S(x,m,y,n)\psi(y,n)\ {\rm d}y{\rm d}n\right|=\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{m}^{\alpha}S(x,m,y,n)\nabla_{y}\psi(y,n)\ {\rm d}y{\rm d}n\right|.

Condition (

Assumption

(
H2
)
.

We require the following regularity of the initial condition of the limiting equation (

).

i.

Regularity:

\psi_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap W^{1,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

ii.

Initial positivity, bound of logarithmic gradient and moments:
There exists a constant
\mathcal{C}>1
such that for all

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{m}\psi_{0}(x,m)\right|\leq\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}e^{-m}

\displaystyle,\qquad\left|\nabla_{x}\psi_{0}(x,m)\right|\leq\mathcal{C}e^{-m}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}}e^{-m}\leq\psi_{0}(x,m)\leq\mathcal{C}e^{-m}.

In addition there exists

\displaystyle\underset{b\geq 1}{\sup}\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{b,1}^{\frac{1}{b}}}{b}\leq n_{0}

\mathfrak{M}_{b,p}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+m^{bp})\psi_{0}^{p}(x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

Remark 1.2
.

We demonstrate an initial data realizing (i) – (ii). Take any
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\cap W^{1,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\cap\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
f>0
and let
\psi_{0}=e^{-m}f(x)
. To see that (ii) is satisfied note that
\psi_{0}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
as a tensor product of probability densities. In addition,
\left\|\partial_{m}\psi_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}e^{-m}
and
\left\|\nabla_{x}\psi_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\infty}e^{-m}
. Furthermore, notice that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{b}\psi_{0}(x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m=\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{b}e^{-m}\ {\rm d}m=\Gamma(b+1)=b!.

Note also that point ii. in particular implies that
\mu[\psi_{0}]\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with the bound
\left\|\mu[\psi_{0}]\right\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}}\leq\overline{\mu}
for some
\overline{\mu}>0
, an observation which will be used in the sequel.

Assumption

(
H3
)
.

Concerning the initial data of (
1.2
), we fix

\psi_{N,0}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{T}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})

i.

Exponential decay:
There exist some

ii.

There is some constant

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{dN}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\psi_{N,0}(\mathbf{x_{N}},\mathbf{m}_{N})\log(\psi_{N,0}(\mathbf{x_{N}},\mathbf{m}_{N}))\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}=:\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{N}<\infty.

iii.

Propagation of chaos at

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)=\mathcal{H}(\psi_{N,0}|\psi_{0}^{\otimes N})\to 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \textnormal{as }N\to\infty.

(1.15)

Assumptions (
1.13

A function

Theorem 1.4

(Limiting PDE (
1.3
))
.

Let assumptions

-
H2

Existence:
There exists some

to (

) on

.

2.

Let

)

Proposition 1.6
.

Let assumptions

H3

\partial_{m}S\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})

\displaystyle\psi_{N}(t,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\log(\psi_{N}(t,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N}))\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}

(2.6)

\psi

\overline{\psi_{N}}=\psi^{\otimes N}\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega^{N})

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

be a solution in the sense of Theorem

to (

) with

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{\psi_{N}}+L_{N}\overline{\psi_{N}}=(\mathcal{R}_{N}+\mathcal{S}_{N})\overline{\psi_{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{N}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left(m_{j}\mathbf{a}(x_{i}-x_{j})-\mathbf{a}\star\mu[\psi](t,x_{i})\right)\nabla_{x_{i}}\log(\psi(t,x_{i},m_{i}))

(2.8)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{N}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\partial_{m_{i}}S(x_{i},m_{i},x_{j},m_{j})+S(x_{i},m_{i},x_{j},m_{j})\partial_{m_{i}}\log(\psi(x_{i},m_{i}))

\displaystyle-\partial_{m}\mathbf{S}[\psi](x_{i},m_{i})-\partial_{m_{i}}\log(\psi(x_{i},m_{i})\mathbf{S}[\psi](x_{i},m_{i})).

(2.9)

Proof.

Using the notation (
2.4

2.5

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\overline{\psi_{N}}=\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\partial_{t}\psi(x_{i},m_{i})\underset{j\neq i}{\prod}\psi(x_{j},m_{j})=-\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left(\mathbf{a}_{\infty}\psi+S_{\infty}\psi\right)(x_{i},m_{i})\frac{\overline{\psi_{N}}}{\psi(x_{i},m_{i})}.

Comparing
\mathbf{a}_{N}
with
a_{\infty}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{N}\overline{\psi_{N}}-\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left(\mathbf{a}_{\infty}\psi\right)(x_{i},m_{i})\frac{\overline{\psi_{N}}}{\psi(x_{i},m_{i})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j}\mathbf{a}(x_{i}-x_{j})\nabla_{x_{i}}\psi(x_{i},m_{i})\frac{\overline{\psi_{N}}}{\psi(x_{i},m_{i})}-\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\mathbf{a}\star\mu[\psi](x_{i})\nabla_{x_{i}}\psi(x_{i},m_{i})\frac{\overline{\psi_{N}}}{\psi(x_{i},m_{i})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}m_{j}\mathbf{a}(x_{i}-x_{j})\nabla_{x_{i}}\log(\psi(x_{i},m_{i}))\overline{\psi_{N}}-\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\mathbf{a}\star\mu[\psi](x_{i})\nabla_{x_{i}}\log(\psi(x_{i},m_{i}))\overline{\psi_{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\left(m_{j}\mathbf{a}(x_{i}-x_{j})-\mathbf{a}\star\mu[\psi](x_{i})\right)\nabla_{x_{i}}\log(\psi(x_{i},m_{i}))\overline{\psi_{N}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{N}\overline{\psi_{N}}.

Secondly, we compare

\displaystyle\overline{\psi_{N}}-\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}(S_{\infty}\psi)(x_{i},m_{i})\frac{\overline{\psi_{N}}}{\psi(x_{i},m_{i})}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{m_{i}}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}S(x_{i},m_{i},x_{j},m_{j})\overline{\psi_{N}}\bigg)

\displaystyle-\mathrel{{\mathop{\sum}\limits}}\partial_{m_{i}}\left(\psi(x_{i},m_{i})\mathbf{S}[\psi](x_{i},m_{i})\right)\frac{\overline{\psi_{N}}}{\psi(x_{i},m_{i})}

2.2
Well-posedness of limiting PDE

We start by establishing preliminary estimates showing the propagation of moments property and estimates on the growth of the logarithmic gradient of a solution to (
2.6
) (Theorem
1.4
3.). These estimates will play an important role for the mean-field limit.
We start with a general Lemma providing upper and lower bounds on the supremum and infimum of solutions (or subsolutions) to a linear transport equation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
and let
\mathbf{S},\mathbf{S}^{\prime},\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. Let
\Psi
be a smooth subsolution to the following linear transport equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi+\nabla_{x}\Psi\cdot\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{S}\partial_{m}\Psi+\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\Psi+\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\leq 0,\ \Psi(0,x,m)=\Psi_{0}.

(2.10)

Then,

i.

For all
t\in[0,T]
we have

\displaystyle\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\sup}\Psi(t,x,m)\leq e^{\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}T}\left(\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\sup}\Psi_{0}(x,m)+T\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\right).

ii.

If in addition
\mathbf{S^{\prime\prime}}\equiv 0
and
\Psi
is a smooth solution, then for all
t\in[0,T]
we have

\displaystyle\inf_{(x,m)\in\Omega}\Psi(t,x,m)\geq e^{-\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}T}\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\inf}\Psi_{0}(x,m).

Proof.

Step 1
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\chi_{n}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a smooth cut-off function such that:

•

\chi_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

•

\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{n})\subset[\frac{1}{2n},2n]
.

•

0\leq\chi_{n}\leq 1
and
\chi_{n}\equiv 1
on
[\frac{1}{n},n]
.

Multiplying (
2.10
) by
\chi_{n}
we arrive at:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\Psi\chi_{n})+\nabla_{x}(\chi_{n}\Psi)\cdot\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{S}\partial_{m}(\chi_{n}\Psi)-\partial_{m}\chi_{n}\Psi\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{S}^{\prime}(\chi_{n}\Psi)+\mathbf{S^{\prime\prime}}\chi_{n}\leq 0.

Setting
\Psi_{n}\coloneqq\chi_{n}\Psi
the above inequality writes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi_{n}+\nabla_{x}\Psi_{n}\cdot\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{S}\partial_{m}\Psi_{n}-\partial_{m}\chi_{n}\Psi\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\Psi_{n}+\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\chi_{n}\leq 0.

(2.11)

Since
\Psi_{n}(t,\cdot)
is a smooth compactly supported function, for any
t\in[0,T]
it attains a global maximum
(x_{\max}^{t},m_{\max}^{t})\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
. Evaluating (
2.11
) at
(x_{\max}^{t},m_{\max}^{t})
and using that
\nabla_{x}\Psi_{n}(x_{\max}^{t},m_{\max}^{t})=0
and
\partial_{m}\Psi_{n}(x^{t}_{\max},m_{\max}^{t})=0
we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi_{n}(t,x_{\max}^{t},m_{\max}^{t})-\partial_{m}\chi_{n}\Psi\mathbf{S}(t,x_{\max}^{t},m_{\max}^{t})

\displaystyle+(\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\Psi_{n})(t,x^{t}_{\max},m_{\max}^{t})+(\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\chi_{n})(t,x_{\max}^{t},m_{\max}^{t})=0.

Since
\mathrm{supp}(\partial_{m}\chi_{n})\subset[\frac{1}{2n},\frac{1}{n}]\bigcup[n,2n]
and
\mathbf{S}
is compactly supported in
m
, it follows that for
n
sufficiently large we have
\partial_{m}\chi_{n}\mathbf{S}\equiv 0
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Psi_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}+\mathbf{S}^{\prime}(t,x_{\max}^{t},m_{\max}^{t})\left\|\Psi_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}+(\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\chi_{n})(t,x_{\max}^{t},m_{\max}^{t})=0.

Therefore we conclude the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Psi_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\Psi_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{\infty},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq e^{\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}T}\left(\left\|\Psi_{n}(0,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}+T\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\right).

Therefore for all
(t,x,m)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\chi_{n}\Psi(t,x,m)\leq e^{\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}T}\left(\chi_{n}\Psi_{0}(x,m)+T\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\right).

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
and using that
\chi_{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}1
pointwise we find

\displaystyle\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\sup}\Psi(t,x,m)\leq e^{\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}T}\left(\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\sup}\Psi_{0}(x,m)+T\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\right).

Step 2
. Fix
\eta>0
and set
\Phi_{\eta}=\frac{1}{\eta+\Psi}
. Dividing the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi+\nabla_{x}\Psi\cdot\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{S}\partial_{m}\Psi+\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\Psi=0

by
(\Psi+\eta)^{2}
we get

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\Phi_{\eta}-\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{S}\partial_{m}\Phi_{\eta}+\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\Psi}{\Psi+\eta}\Phi_{\eta}=0,

which is recast as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi_{\eta}+\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{S}\partial_{m}\Phi_{\eta}-\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\Psi}{\Psi+\eta}\Phi_{\eta}=0.

Note that
\left\|\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\Psi}{\Psi+\eta}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}
and therefore by step 1 we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\sup}\Phi_{\eta}(t,x,m)\leq e^{\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}T}\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\sup}\Phi_{\eta}(0,x,m).

Since

\displaystyle\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\sup}\Phi_{\eta}(t,x,m)=\frac{1}{\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\inf}\Psi(t,x,m)+\eta}

it follows that

\displaystyle\inf_{(x,m)\in\Omega}\Psi(t,x,m)+\eta\geq e^{-\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}T}\left(\underset{(x,m)\in\Omega}{\inf}\Psi_{0}(x,m)+\eta\right).

Letting
\eta\rightarrow 0
yields the announced inequality.
∎

\displaystyle\ \mathbf{A}_{R}[\psi](x)\coloneqq\mathbf{a}_{R}\star\mu[\psi](x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbf{S}_{R}[\psi](x,m)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}S_{R}(x,m,y,n)\psi(y,n)\ {\rm d}y{\rm d}n\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega),

.
A particular consequence of Lemma

, Lemma

Let

We can now prove existence for the limiting PDE (

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
and

satisfy the assumptions of

, let
\psi_{0}
satisfy the assumptions of

\psi

Proof.

Consider the solution
\psi_{R}
of the regularized equation (

(2.39)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}_{R}[\psi_{R}](t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\mu[\psi_{R}](t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{\infty}(1+\overline{S}_{0}t),\ \left\|\mathbf{S}_{R}[\psi_{R}]\right\|_{\infty}\leq\overline{S}_{0}.

),(
2.40

The last part of this section is devoted to show uniqueness of solutions to (

). Recall from the previous section that
\mu[\psi]=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}n\psi(x,n)\ {\rm d}n

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
and
S
satisfy assumption

. Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
be strong solutions to (

) on

Proof
.

Step 1
.
Calculation of
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|(\mu[\psi_{1}]-\mu[\psi_{2}])(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}
.
By equation (
2.42
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mu_{1}(t,\cdot)-\mu_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\mathrm{div}_{x}(\mu_{2}\mathbf{a}\star\mu_{2})-\mathrm{div}_{x}(\mu_{1}\mathbf{a}\star\mu_{1})\right)(t,x)\mathrm{sgn}(\mu_{1}(t,x)-\mu_{2}(t,x))\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(h\left[\psi_{1}\right]-h\left[\psi_{2}\right])(t,x)\mathrm{sgn}(\mu_{1}(t,x)-\mu_{2}(t,x))\ {\rm d}x=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Put
\mathrm{s}(t,x)\coloneqq\mathrm{sgn}(\mu_{1}(t,x)-\mu_{2}(t,x))
. We start by estimating
I_{1}
. Using that
\mathrm{div}_{x}(\mathbf{a})=0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{div}_{x}(\mu_{2}\mathbf{a}\star\mu_{2})-\mathrm{div}(\mu_{1}\mathbf{a}\star\mu_{1})\right)(t,x)\mathrm{s}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{div}_{x}((\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})\mathbf{a}\star\mu_{2})(t,x)\mathrm{s}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{div}_{x}(\mu_{1}\mathbf{a}\star(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}))(t,x)\mathrm{s}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\left|\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}\right|(t,x)\mathbf{a}\star\mu_{2}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\mu_{1}(t,x)\mathbf{a}\star(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})(t,x)\mathrm{s}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x.

(2.43)

Integrating by parts the first term and using that
\mathrm{div}_{x}(\mathbf{a})=0
, we find that the first term in the right-hand side of (
2.43
) vanishes, and thus

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\mu_{1}(t,x)\mathbf{a}\star(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})(t,x)\mathrm{s}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x\leq\left\|\nabla_{x}\mu_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1},

and so we get

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim\left\|(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(2.44)

Next we estimate
I_{2}
. First, note that we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega^{2}}S(x,m,y,n)\psi_{1}(t,x,m)\psi_{1}(t,y,n)\mathrm{s}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m{\rm d}y{\rm d}n

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega^{2}}S(x,m,y,n)\psi_{2}(t,x,m)\psi_{2}(t,y,n)\mathrm{s}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m{\rm d}y{\rm d}n\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega^{2}}\left|S(x,m,y,n)(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,x,m)\right|\left|\psi_{1}(t,y,n)\right|\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m{\rm d}y{\rm d}n

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega^{2}}\left|S(x,m,y,n)(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,y,n)\right|\left|\psi_{2}(t,x,m)\right|\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m{\rm d}y{\rm d}n\leq 2\overline{S}_{0}\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1},

(2.45)

where we used that
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are probability densities. Hence, we conclude that

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq 2\overline{S}_{0}\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(2.46)

Gathering (
2.44
) and (
2.46
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\left\|(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}+\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(2.47)

Step 2
.
Calculation of
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}
. Put
\mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\coloneqq\mathrm{sgn}(\psi_{1}(t,x,m)-\psi_{2}(t,x,m))
. We compute that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,x,m)\mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{x}\left(\mathbf{A}[\psi_{2}]\psi_{2}-\mathbf{A}[\psi_{1}]\psi_{1}\right)(t,x,m)\mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{m}\left(\mathbf{S}[\psi_{2}]\psi_{2}-\mathbf{S}[\psi_{1}]\psi_{1}\right)(t,x,m)\mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m\coloneqq J_{1}+J_{2}.

We start with
J_{1}
. First, we have

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{x}\left((\mathbf{A}[\psi_{2}]-\mathbf{A}[\psi_{1}])\psi_{2}\right)\mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{x}(\left(\psi_{2}-\psi_{1}\right)\mathbf{A}[\psi_{1}])\ \mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m\coloneqq J_{1}^{1}+J_{1}^{2}.

Since

\displaystyle J_{1}^{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{A}[\psi_{2}]-\mathbf{A}[\psi_{1}])\nabla_{x}\psi_{2}(t,x,m)\mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla_{x}\psi_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

Furthermore, one has

\displaystyle J_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}\left|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\right|(t,x,m)\mathbf{A}[\psi_{1}](t,x)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\right|(t,x,m)\mathrm{div}_{x}\mathbf{A}[\psi_{1}](t,x)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle J_{1}\lesssim\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(2.48)

We continue by estimating
J_{2}
. By adding and subtracting

we have

\displaystyle J_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{m}(\mathbf{S}[\psi_{2}]-\mathbf{S}[\psi_{1}])\psi_{2}(t,x,m)\mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}[\psi_{2}]-\mathbf{S}[\psi_{1}])\partial_{m}\psi_{2}(t,x,m)\mathbf{s}(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}[\psi_{1}](t,x,m)\partial_{m}\left|\psi_{2}-\psi_{1}\right|(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{m}\mathbf{S}[\psi_{1}](t,x,m)\left|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\right|(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m=\sum_{k=1}^{4}J_{2}^{k}.

We bound each of the

\displaystyle J_{2}^{1}\leq 2\overline{S}_{1}\left\|(\psi_{2}-\psi_{1})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(2.49)

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle J_{2}^{2}\leq 2\overline{S}_{0}\left\|\partial_{m}\psi_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(2.50)

Moreover, integrating by parts reveals that

\displaystyle J_{2}^{4}=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{m}\mathbf{S}[\psi_{1}]\left|\psi_{2}-\psi_{1}\right|(t,x,m)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}m,

and therefore, we get

\displaystyle J_{2}^{3}+J_{2}^{4}=0.

(2.51)

Gathering (
2.49
)-(
2.51
) we find that

\displaystyle J_{2}\lesssim\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(2.52)

Combining (

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

(2.53)

The combination of (

) and (

) entails

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\left(\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}+\left\|(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\right)\lesssim\left\|(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}+\left\|(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}.

∎

(

3
The mean field limit

In this part, we introduce the relative entropy (as defined in (
1.7
)) and prove rigorously a propagation of chaos property via the relative entropy. The proof is split into 3 steps, summarized below:

(I)

We study the evolution of
\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)
and obtain an inequality of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)\leq\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)-\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{N}}\psi_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}+\mathcal{S}_{N})(\tau,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}{\rm d}\tau.

(3.1)

This inequality relies crucially on the fact that the weak solution we constructed in the previous section is an entropy solution.

(II)

We prove a "cancellation Lemma" which ultimately shows that the quantity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega_{N}}\exp(\left|\mathcal{R}_{N}+\mathcal{S}_{N}\right|)\overline{\psi_{N}}(t,\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}

(3.2)

is of order
\frac{1}{N}
. This is where the smallness of the parameters
\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|_{\infty},\overline{S}_{0},\overline{S}_{1},T_{\ast\ast}
comes into play.

(III)

We apply a "change of laws lemma" in order to control the integral in (
3.1
) by means of (
3.2
) and the relative entropy. This eventually leads to an evolution inequality of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}(t)\leq\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}_{N}(\tau)\ {\rm d}\tau+O(\frac{1}{N}),

which concludes the proof.

We proceed by proving (I), (III) and finally (II).

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{N}}\exp\left(\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\chi(x_{i},m_{i},x_{j},m_{j})\right|\right)\overline{\psi_{N}}(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}\leq C

(3.13)

for some constant

. In particular, the integral in the left-hand side of (

) is well defined.

Remark 3.7

The original statement in

Lemma 3.8

(Cancellation Lemma)
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
and
S
satisfy
H1
and suppose that
\psi\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}(\Omega)
. Let

\displaystyle\phi(x,m,y,n)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\,\partial_{m}S(x,m,y,n)+S(x,m,y,n)\partial_{m}\log(\psi(x,m))

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{m}S(x,m,y^{\prime},n^{\prime})\psi(y^{\prime},n^{\prime})\ {\rm d}y^{\prime}{\rm d}n^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\partial_{m}\log(\psi(x,m))\int_{\Omega}S(x,m,y^{\prime},n^{\prime})\psi(y^{\prime},n^{\prime})\ {\rm d}y^{\prime}{\rm d}n^{\prime}

(3.14)

and

\displaystyle\theta(x,m,y,n)=(m\mathbf{a}(y-x)-\mathbf{a}\star\mu[\psi](y))\nabla_{y}\log\psi(y,n).

(3.15)

Then, for any
I_{2k}\in\mathscr{R}_{N,2k}
and
J_{2k}\notin\mathcal{J}_{m_{I_{2k}},n_{I_{2k}}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{N}}\phi(x_{i_{1}},m_{i_{1}},x_{j_{1}},m_{j_{1}})\dots\phi(x_{i_{2k}},m_{i_{2k}},x_{j_{2k}},m_{j_{2k}})\overline{\psi_{N}}(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}=0

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{N}}\theta(x_{i_{1}},m_{i_{1}},x_{j_{1}},m_{j_{1}})\dots\theta(x_{i_{2k}},m_{i_{2k}},x_{j_{2k}},m_{j_{2k}})\overline{\psi_{N}}(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}=0.

Proof.

Define the effective set to be the set of all
I_{2k}
with multiplicity different from
1
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N,2k}\coloneqq\left\{I_{2k}\in\mathcal{T}_{N,2k}\left|\forall 1\leq l\leq N:a_{l}\neq 1\right.\right\}.

There are three possible (potentially non-disjoint) cases for
I_{2k}\in\mathscr{R}_{N,2k}
and
J_{2k}\notin\mathcal{J}_{m_{I_{2k}},n_{I_{2k}}}
:

i.

I_{2k}\notin\mathcal{E}_{N,2k}
,
I_{2k}\in\mathscr{R}_{N,2k}
and there exist some
1\leq l\leq m_{I_{2k}}
such that
b_{l}=0
.

ii.

I_{2k}\notin\mathcal{E}_{N,2k}
,
I_{2k}\in\mathscr{R}_{N,2k}
and there is some
l>m_{I_{2k}}+n_{I_{2k}}
such that
b_{l}=1
.

iii.

I_{2k}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,2k}\cap\mathscr{R}_{N,2k}
.

Step 1. Cancellation for
\phi
.

Case 1
. Suppose that i. holds. Since
I_{2k}\notin\mathcal{E}_{N,2k}
, there exists some
1\leq r\leq N
such that
a_{r}=1
(note that
1\leq r\leq m_{I_{2k}}
because
a_{r}\neq 1
for all
r>m_{I_{2k}}
). Therefore case i. implies that for all
1\leq r\leq m_{I_{2k}}
(which is a non-empty inequality due to
m_{I_{2k}}\geq 1
) it holds
a_{r}=1
and there exist
1\leq l\leq m_{I_{2k}}
such that
b_{l}=0
.

Taking
r=l
, due to the definitions of
a_{r}
and
b_{l}
, this means that there exists a
\nu
such that
i_{\nu}=l
and
i_{\nu^{\prime}}\neq r
for all
\nu^{\prime}\neq\nu
and
j_{\nu^{\prime}}\neq l
for all
1\leq\nu^{\prime}\leq 2k
. Therefore, by the Leibniz rule it holds that

\displaystyle\phi(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}},x_{j_{\nu}},m_{j_{\nu}})\psi(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}})=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{m_{i_{\nu}}}(S(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}},x_{j_{\nu}},m_{j_{\nu}})\psi(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}}))

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{N}}\partial_{m_{i_{\nu}}}(S(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}},y,n)\psi(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}}))\psi(y,n)\ {\rm d}y{\rm d}n

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{m_{i_{\nu}}}\varphi(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}},x_{j_{\nu}},m_{j_{\nu}}),

(3.16)

where we have set

\displaystyle\varphi(x,m,y,n)\coloneqq S(x,m,y,n)\psi(x,m)-\int_{\Omega}S(x,m,y^{\prime},n^{\prime})\psi(x,m)\psi(y^{\prime},n^{\prime})\ {\rm d}y^{\prime}{\rm d}n^{\prime}.

Recognizing that the product
\prod_{\nu^{\prime}\neq\nu}\phi(x_{i_{\nu^{\prime}}},m_{i_{\nu^{\prime}}},x_{j_{\nu^{\prime}}},m_{j_{\nu^{\prime}}})\prod_{l\neq i_{\nu}}\psi(x_{l},m_{l})
is independent of
m_{i_{\nu}}
we can invoke (
3.16
) and integrate by parts in order to find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{N}}

\displaystyle\phi(x_{i_{1}},m_{{i_{1}}},x_{j_{1}},m_{j_{{1}}})\cdots\phi(x_{i_{1}},m_{i_{i_{1}}},x_{j_{1}},m_{j_{{1}}})\overline{\psi_{N}}(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{N}}\partial_{m_{i_{\nu}}}\varphi(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}},x_{j_{\nu}},m_{j_{\nu}})\prod_{\nu^{\prime}\neq\nu}\phi(x_{i_{\nu^{\prime}}},m_{i_{\nu^{\prime}}},x_{j_{\nu^{\prime}}},m_{j_{\nu^{\prime}}})\prod_{l\neq i_{\nu}}\psi(x_{l},m_{l})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}=0.

Case 2
. Suppose that either ii. or iii. hold.

If ii. holds, then we know that
m_{I_{2k}}\geq 1
and there is some
l>m_{I_{2k}}+n_{I_{2k}}
such that
b_{l}=1
.
By definition of
b_{l}
and the fact that
I_{2k}\in\mathscr{R}_{N,2k}
, this means that there is some
\nu
such that
j_{\nu}=l
and
j_{\nu^{\prime}}\neq l
for all
\nu^{\prime}\neq\nu
as well as
i_{\nu^{\prime}}\neq l
for all
1\leq\nu^{\prime}\leq 2k
.

If iii. holds then
m_{I_{2k}}=0
and therefore
J_{2k}\notin\mathcal{J}_{m_{I_{2k}},n_{I_{2k}}}
implies that there exist some
l>n_{I_{2k}}
such that
b_{l}=1
. So again, this means that there is
\nu
such that
j_{\nu}=l
such that
j_{\nu^{\prime}}\neq l
for all
\nu^{\prime}\neq\nu
and
i_{\nu^{\prime}}\neq l
for all
1\leq\nu^{\prime}\leq 2k
.
We then have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{N}}

\displaystyle\phi(x_{i_{1}},m_{i_{1}},x_{j_{1}},m_{j_{1}})\cdots\phi(x_{i_{2k}},m_{i_{2k}},x_{j_{2k}},m_{j_{2k}})\ \overline{\psi_{N}}(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{N-1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}},x_{j_{\nu}},m_{j_{\nu}})\psi(x_{j_{\nu}},m_{j_{\nu}})\ {\rm d}x_{j_{\nu}}{\rm d}m_{j_{\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{l\neq j_{\nu}}\psi(x_{l},m_{l})\prod_{\nu^{\prime}\neq\nu}\phi(x_{i_{\nu^{\prime}}},m_{i_{\nu^{\prime}}},x_{j_{\nu^{\prime}}},m_{j_{\nu^{\prime}}})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{N-1}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}^{\prime}_{N-1}.

Here we apply the notation

\displaystyle{\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N-1}^{\prime}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N-1}^{\prime}\coloneqq{\rm d}x_{j_{1}}{\rm d}m_{j_{1}}\cdots\hat{{\rm d}x_{j_{\nu}}}\hat{{\rm d}m_{j_{\nu}}}\cdots{\rm d}x_{N}{\rm d}m_{N}.

Note that, due to direct inspection of (
3.14
), for all

\int_{\Omega}\phi(x,m,y,n)\psi(y,n)\ {\rm d}y{\rm d}n=0.\

It follows that

\int_{\Omega_{N}}\phi(x_{i_{1}},m_{i_{1}},x_{j_{1}},m_{j_{1}})\cdots\phi(x_{i_{2k}},m_{i_{2k}},x_{j_{2k}},m_{j_{2k}})\overline{\psi_{N}}(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}=0.

Step 2. Cancellation for
\theta
. As before we distinguish between 2 cases.

Case 1
.
As discussed in Step 1, if i. holds there is some

such that
i_{\nu^{\prime}}\neq i_{\nu}

\displaystyle\theta(x_{i_{1}},m_{i_{1}},x_{j_{1}},m_{j_{1}})\cdots\theta(x_{i_{2k}},m_{i_{2k}},x_{j_{2k}},m_{j_{2k}})\overline{\psi_{N}}(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{m}_{N})\ {\rm d}\mathbf{x}_{N}{\rm d}\mathbf{m}_{N}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{N-1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\theta(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}},x_{j_{\nu}},m_{j_{\nu}})\psi(x_{i_{\nu}},m_{i_{\nu}})\ {\rm d}x_{i_{\nu}}{\rm d}m_{i_{\nu}}\right)

where we used that for each
