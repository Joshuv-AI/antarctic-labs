Factorization of Additive Polynomials and van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves in characteristic 2 2
Tetsushi Ito, Daichi Takeuchi, Takahiro Tsushima

Abstract
. In our previous work, we gave a formula for the Frobenius eigenvalues of van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves in characteristic 2 2 by considering suitable quotients of the curve. Although the formula is explicit, it depends on many choices, which makes the formula complicated. In this article, we take a different approach using a factorization of additive polynomials, and prove a new formula. The resulting formula is simpler and is useful for explicit computations. As applications, we provide a method for constructing maximal and minimal van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves, and show that every such curve arises from this construction. We also compute various examples of van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves and study their periods.

In this paper, we start with a pair

The adjoint

(i)

We have

(iii)

We have

For such a pair

\displaystyle R_{F}(x):=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{e}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x],\quad\textrm{with }a_{i}:=\sum_{j=0}^{e-i}(b_{j}b_{j+i})^{p^{-j}},

1.1.
An Explicit Formula for the Frobenius Eigenvalues of
D_{F,t}

Let
(F,t)
be a pair as above, and let
D_{F,t}
be the associated curve. Let
\overline{D}_{F,t}
denote the smooth compactification of
D_{F,t}
. The
L
-polynomial of
\overline{D}_{F,t}
is described as follows.

Let
W_{2}
denote the Witt vector scheme of length two.
Let
\ell
be an odd prime and
let

Q_{q}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\xrightarrow{x\mapsto(x,0)}W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\xrightarrow{\Tr_{q/2}}W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})\xrightarrow{\xi_{2}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}_{\ell},

where
\Tr_{q/2}\colon W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\to W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
denotes the trace map and

\xi_{2}
is a fixed faithful character of the group
W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
. Set
\sqrt{-1}:=\xi_{2}(1,0)
, which is a primitive
4
-th root of unity.

The function
Q_{q}
is an even-characteristic analogue of the quadratic form
\frac{1}{2}x^{2}
, in the sense that

Q_{q}(x+y)Q_{q}(x)^{-1}Q_{q}(y)^{-1}=(-1)^{\Tr_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}(xy)}.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.12
)
.

The
L
-polynomial

L(\overline{D}_{F,t}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},T):=\det(1-{\rm Fr}_{q}T\mid H^{1}(\overline{D}_{F,t,{\mathbb{F}}},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}))

satisfies

L(\overline{D}_{F,t}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},T)=\prod_{v\in W_{F}^{\ast}}(1-\tau_{v}T)^{p-1},

where
\tau_{v}:=Q_{q}(t+v)^{-1}(-1-\sqrt{-1})^{[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{2}]}
.

The above formula is simpler than the one obtained in
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
. In loc. cit., we impose a certain condition on
C_{R}
. One can show that this condition is equivalent to the existence of a pair
(F,t)
as above such that
R(x)=R_{F,t}(x)
. See Theorem
4.14
for details.

1.2.
Maximal and Minimal Twists

Fix a polynomial

R(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{e}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}

with
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
a_{e}\neq 0
. For each
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, set
R_{a}(x):=R(x)+ax
, and consider the van der Geer–van der Vlugt curve
C_{a}:=C_{R_{a}}
.
Its compactification is denoted by
\overline{C}_{a}
.
The curves
C_{a}
,
\overline{C}_{a}

are called the
twists
of
C_{0}
,
\overline{C}_{0}
, respectively.
We regard
C_{a}
as a family of van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves parametrized by
a
.

Let
T_{R,\max}
(resp.
T_{R,\min}
) be the set of

a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for which
the twist
\overline{C}_{a}
is

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal (resp.
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal). Here, a smooth projective curve over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is said to be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal
(resp.
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal
) if it attains the Hasse–Weil upper bound (resp. lower bound). We also say that
\overline{C}_{a}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal
if it is either
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal or
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal.
For the precise definition, see Definition
5.2
.

For each
x\in{\mathbb{F}}
, define

E(x):=R(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{e}(a_{i}x)^{p^{-i}},

and

V:=\ker(E\colon{\mathbb{F}}\to{\mathbb{F}}).

It is known that, if either of
T_{R,\max}
or
T_{R,\min}
is nonempty, then
V\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Moreover, the condition
V\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
implies that
[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
is even.
For this reason, we henceforth assume that
V\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
2\mid[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
. In this case, van der Geer and van der Vlugt
[
5
, (6.2)]
determine the cardinalities of the sets
T_{R,\max}
and
T_{R,\min}
, at least when
p=2
. In this paper, we describe these sets by using Theorem
1.1
as follows.

Assume that
V\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Then, by Lemma
5.6
, there exists
F
satisfying the above conditions such that
R=R_{F}
. Choose and fix such an
F
.
Let
\Tr_{q/2}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
denote the trace map and set

\psi_{q}(x):=(-1)^{\Tr_{q/2}(x)}
for
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
We define

\displaystyle S_{F}:

\displaystyle=\{t\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\mid\forall v\in W_{F}^{\ast},\ Q_{q}(v)=\psi_{q}(tv)\},

\displaystyle S_{F,-}:

\displaystyle=\left\{t\in S_{F}\mid Q_{q}(t)=-(\sqrt{-1})^{[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{2}]/2}\right\},

\displaystyle S_{F,+}:

\displaystyle=\left\{t\in S_{F}\mid Q_{q}(t)=(\sqrt{-1})^{[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{2}]/2}\right\}.

In the following theorem,
we give a parametrization of maximal and minimal twists.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.11
)
.

We have

\alpha_{F}(S_{F,-})=T_{R_{F},\max},\qquad\alpha_{F}(S_{F,+})=T_{R_{F},\min}.

In other words, the curve
\overline{C}_{a}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal if and only if
a=\alpha_{F}(t)
for some
t\in S_{F,-}
. A similar statement holds for
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal twists.

In certain cases,
S_{F,-}
and
S_{F,+}
admit a more explicit description; see Theorems
6.3
and
6.5
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain a recipe for constructing
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves from linear-algebraic data. Let
p
be a power of
2
, and
q
be a power of
p
. Assume that
[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
is even.

Consider a pair
(W,t)
, where

•

W
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
such that
1\in W
,

•

t
is an element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
such that

Q_{q}(v)=\psi_{q}(tv)\qquad\text{for all }v\in W.

To such a pair
(W,t)
, we define

f_{W}(x):=\prod_{v\in W}(x-v),\qquad F_{W}:=(\tau^{-e}f_{W})^{\ast},

where

. One can check that

satisfies conditions (i)–(iii) stated above.
Let

be the associated

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem

)
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

The

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

where

and

is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal. Then there exists a pair
(W,t)
as above and

such that

*

*

.

The results in Subsection

•

2.
Computations on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Linearized Polynomials

Let
p_{0}
be a prime number, and let
p
be a power of
p_{0}
. Let
k
be a perfect field containing
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
Let
k\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
denote the
k
-algebra

\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}k\tau^{i},

whose multiplication is determined by

(a\tau^{i})\cdot(b\tau^{j})=ab^{p^{i}}\tau^{i+j}\text{ \hskip 8.53581pt for $i,j\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $a,b\in k$.}

For
f=\sum_{i}a_{i}\tau^{i}\in k\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
, we define the corresponding polynomial

f(x):=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in k[x^{p^{-\infty}}],

where
k[x^{p^{-\infty}}]
denotes the perfection of
k[x]
.
Conversely, given a polynomial of the form
f(x)=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in k[x^{p^{-\infty}}]
, we write
f
for the corresponding element
\sum_{i}a_{i}\tau^{i}\in k\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
.

A polynomial of the form
\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}
is called an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial
.
By abuse of terminology, we also call the corresponding element
\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\tau^{i}\in k\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial.

An
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial
f(x)=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}
is said to be
separable
if
a_{0}\neq 0
. Equivalently, all roots of
f(x)
are simple.

Let
\overline{k}
be an algebraic closure of
k
. To an element
f=\sum_{i}a_{i}\tau^{i}\in k\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
, we associate the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear map

\overline{k}\to\overline{k},\quad x\mapsto f(x)=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}.

By abuse of notation, we also denote this linear map by
f
. This construction defines a ring homomorphism
k\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}\to{\rm End}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(\overline{k})
. We write
\ker f
for the kernel of the associated map
\overline{k}\to\overline{k}
. An element of the kernel is called a
root
of
f
.

We recall some basic results.
Let
{\mathcal{V}}
denote the set of finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-subspaces of
\overline{k}
. For
W\in\mathcal{V}
, define

f_{W}(x):=\prod_{v\in W}(x-v).

Then
f_{W}(x)
is a monic separable
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial with
\ker f_{W}=W
. Furthermore, the element
f_{W}\in k\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
is uniquely characterized by these properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

The map

\overline{k}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathcal{V}}\to\overline{k}\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}\setminus\{0\},\qquad(a,n,W)\to a\tau^{n}f_{W}

is bijective. In particular, for every nonzero
f\in\overline{k}\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
, there exists a unique triple
(a,n,W)
such that

f=a\tau^{n}f_{W}.

Moreover, we have
\ker f=W
.

(ii)

Let
f=\sum_{i=r}^{s}a_{i}\tau^{i}

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\ker f=s-r.

(iii)

For

(a)

.

(b)

We have

Proof.

We first prove (i). It is clear that

lies in

\overline{k}\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}\setminus\{0\}
. We construct the inverse map. Take a nonzero element

, and write

where

b_{s-r}=1

Let

. Since

acts as an automorphism,

\sum_{i=0}^{s-r}b_{i}x^{p^{i}}

Since this polynomial is monic and separable, the uniqueness of

Therefore, the assignment

We now prove (iii). The implication
\text{(a)}\Rightarrow\text{(b)}
is clear, since
\overline{k}\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}\to{\rm End}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(\overline{k})
is a ring homomorphism. We prove
\text{(b)}\Rightarrow\text{(a)}
. If

, then we may take

. Assume now that

. Set

and

and

is finite-dimensional. In particular, we have

. Therefore, we may write

where

are units in
\overline{k}\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
.

Let

Then

Hence,

for some

. Since
\alpha_{f}
and

are units, it follows that

for some

.
∎

Assume that

-valued points induced by the morphism

Lemma 2.2
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

Let

f=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\tau^{i}\in k\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}

with
a_{0}\neq 0
.

(a)

The morphism
f\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k}
is finite étale.

(b)

The morphism

is a Galois covering if and only if

\ker f\subset k
. In this case,

is an abelian Galois covering with Galois group
\ker f
.

(ii)

Let

be nonzero elements of

. Assume that

Proof.

(i)(a) Finiteness is immediate. Étaleness follows from the Jacobian criterion.

(i)(b) Let

be the base change of

. For each

, consider the automorphism of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{\overline{k}}
given by

. Since
f_{\overline{k}}(x+a)=f_{\overline{k}}(x)
, we obtain a group homomorphism

\ker f\to{\rm Aut}(f_{\overline{k}}).

This homomorphism is injective.
Since the degree of
f_{\overline{k}}
is equal to the cardinality of
\ker f
, it follows that the above map is an isomorphism. Hence
f_{\overline{k}}
is an abelian Galois covering with Galois group
\ker f
.

Therefore,

is Galois over

if and only if each automorphism

is defined over

, which is equivalent to
\ker f\subset k
. This proves the assertion.

(ii) For each

, choose an integer

such that
\tau^{n_{i}}f_{i}
defines a separable
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial. Put

\widetilde{f}:=\tau^{n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}}f.

Then
\widetilde{f}
also defines a separable
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial. By (i)(b), the

-morphism

is an abelian Galois covering. Since

\widetilde{f}=(\tau^{n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}}f_{1}\tau^{-n_{2}-n_{3}})\cdot(\tau^{n_{3}+n_{2}}f_{2}\tau^{-n_{3}})\cdot(\tau^{n_{3}}f_{3}),

is an intermediate covering of

. Hence it is also a Galois covering. Applying (i)(b) again, we obtain

Since

\ker f_{2}=(\ker g)^{p^{-n_{3}}}:=\{a\in\bar{k}\mid a^{p^{n_{3}}}\in\ker g\},

∎

Definition 2.3
.

For

The adjoint defines an anti-involution on

(fg)^{\ast}=g^{\ast}f^{\ast},\qquad(f^{\ast})^{\ast}=f.

The following observation will be useful.

Lemma 2.4
.

For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-algebra

Let

in

Let

Lemma 2.5
.

For every nonzero element

\displaystyle=g(x,y)+g(z,y),

\displaystyle g(x,y+z)

\displaystyle=g(x,y)+g(x,z),

\displaystyle g(ax,y)

\displaystyle=ag(x,y)=g(x,ay)\qquad\text{for all }a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}.

We first show uniqueness. Let

be another element satisfying the same conditions. Then we have

in

We now prove existence. Since

We prove

Let

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be as in Lemma

.
For

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

Assume further that

, and hence

. Then

(iv)

Proof.

(i) The assertion follows from the identity

\omega(u,u^{\ast})^{p}-\omega(u,u^{\ast})=u^{\ast}F(u)-uF^{\ast}(u^{\ast})=0.

(ii), (iii) The assertions other than nondegeneracy follow from Lemma

We prove nondegeneracy. Let

vanishes on

(iii), there exists

Since

If the additive map
(
4.1
)
is zero, then

|C_{a}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|=q\pm(p-1)\sqrt{qp^{d}},

where

.

Proof.

Let
\psi_{p}
be the character
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times},x\mapsto(-1)^{\Tr_{p/2}(x)}
. Then any additive character
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
can be written uniquely as
\psi_{p,b}:=\psi_{p}(b-)
for some
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
The sequence

0\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\to{\mathbb{F}}\xrightarrow{x^{p}-x}{\mathbb{F}}\to 0

is exact. Taking Galois cohomology
H^{i}({\rm Gal}({\mathbb{F}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}),-)
, we obtain an exact sequence

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\xrightarrow{x^{p}-x}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\xrightarrow{\Tr_{q/p}}{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\to 0.

Therefore,
for
z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, the equation
y^{p}-y=z
has a solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if and only if
\Tr_{q/p}(z)=0
. This is equivalent to
\psi_{p,b}(\Tr_{q/p}(z))=1
for all
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Furthermore, if
y^{p}-y=z
has a solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, then it has exactly
p
solutions.

Thus we have

\displaystyle|C_{a}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\psi_{p,b}\bigl(\Tr_{q/p}(xR_{a}(x))\bigr)

\displaystyle=q+\sum_{b\neq 0}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\psi_{p,b}\bigl(\Tr_{q/p}(xR_{a}(x))\bigr).

Note that

\psi_{p,b}\bigl(\Tr_{q/p}(xR_{a}(x))\bigr)=\psi_{p}\bigl(\Tr_{q/p}(bxR_{a}(x))\bigr)=\psi_{p}\bigl(\Tr_{q/p}((b^{1/2}x)R_{a}(b^{1/2}x))\bigr).

Here
b^{1/2}
exists in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
since
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is perfect, and therefore the last equality holds.
Consequently, the sum
\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
for
\psi_{p,b}
with
b\neq 0
is equal to that for
\psi_{p}
. Hence, we obtain

|C_{a}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|=q+(p-1)\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(-1)^{\Tr_{q/2}(xR_{a}(x))}.

Hence, we are reduced to determining the sum of
(-1)^{\Tr_{q/2}(xR_{a}(x))}
. This is done in
[
5
, (5.2)]
and
[
5
, (5.3)]
.
∎

is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

V_{\psi}\cong H^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}},\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(xR(x))).

This follows from the decomposition

\phi_{\ast}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\cong\bigoplus_{\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}}\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(xR(x))

and the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(xR(x))
is the
\psi
-isotypic component of
\phi_{\ast}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Consequently, by
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
, we have
\dim V_{\psi}=p^{e}
.

We need the following result.

Lemma A.3
.

Assume that
S={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
for a finite field extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then the following hold:

(i)

The
H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})
-representation
V_{\psi}
is irreducible. The group homomorphism

\rho_{\psi}|_{H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})}\colon H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})\to GL(V_{\psi})

is injective.

(ii)

Assume that
C_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal. Then the étale covering
H_{R}\to S
splits completely. We have
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Proof.

(i) We first prove irreducibility. We apply the Stone–von Neumann theorem for a finite two-step nilpotent group; for this theorem, we refer to
[
2
, Exercises 4.1.4–4.1.8]
.

Since the character
\psi
is nontrivial, it is generic in the sense of loc. cit. Therefore, by
[
2
, Exercise 4.1.8]
, there exists a unique irreducible representation
\pi_{\psi}
of
H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})
whose central character is
\psi
. Moreover, its dimension is equal to
p^{e}
.

Since the center
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
acts on
V_{\psi}
via
\psi
, the uniqueness of
\pi_{\psi}
implies that

V_{\psi}\cong\pi_{\psi}^{\oplus n}

for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Since both
V_{\psi}
and
\pi_{\psi}
have dimension
p^{e}
, it follows that
n=1
. Hence,
V_{\psi}
is irreducible.

We now prove that
\rho_{\psi}
is injective on
H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})
. Let
g=(a,b)\in H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})
lie in the kernel. If
a=0
, then
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and hence
g\in Z(H_{R}({\mathbb{F}}))
. Thus, for each nontrivial character
\psi
, the element
g
acts on
V_{\psi}
by scalar multiplication by
\psi(b)
. Since
\rho_{\psi}(g)=1
, it follows that
\psi(b)=1
. As
\psi
is arbitrary, we conclude that
b=0
, and hence
g=1
.

Assume now that
a\neq 0
. By the nondegeneracy of
\omega_{R}
, there exists an element
h\in H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

[g,h]=(0,c)\qquad\text{for some }c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\setminus\{0\}.

Choose a nontrivial character
\psi
such that
\psi(c)\neq 1
. Then
[g,h]
acts nontrivially on
V_{\psi}
. In particular,
\rho_{\psi}(g)\neq 1
. This proves injectivity.

(ii) We first prove that
H_{R}
splits. It suffices to show that
{\rm Fr}_{q}
commutes with
H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})
in
G
.

By the assumption that
C_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal, the image
\rho_{\psi}({\rm Fr}_{q})
is a scalar matrix. In particular, it commutes with
\rho_{\psi}(H_{R}({\mathbb{F}}))
. On the other hand, by (i), the representation
\rho_{\psi}
is injective on
H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})
. Therefore,
{\rm Fr}_{q}
commutes with
H_{R}({\mathbb{F}})
in
G
. This proves that
H_{R}
splits.

We now show that
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Since
H_{R}
splits, the intermediate covering
V_{R}
also splits. Thus, the case where
p_{0}=2
is proved in Lemma
5.5
. Assume that
p_{0}
is odd. In this case,
[
12
, Corollary A.8]
implies that, for each nontrivial character
\psi
, the value

-\zeta_{p,\psi}\cdot\left(\frac{c}{q}\right)\cdot G_{\psi},\qquad(G_{\psi}:=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\psi(x^{2}))

appears as an
{\rm Fr}_{q}
-eigenvalue of
C_{R}
, where
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
is independent of
\psi
, and
\zeta_{p,\psi}
is a certain
p
-th root of unity.
Since
C_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal, all
{\rm Fr}_{q}
-eigenvalues on
H_{c}^{1}
are equal. For every
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
, let
\psi_{a}
denote the character
x\mapsto\psi(ax)
. Then

\zeta_{p,\psi}G_{\psi}=\zeta_{p,\psi_{a}}G_{\psi_{a}}=\zeta_{p,\psi_{a}}\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)G_{\psi},

and therefore
\zeta_{p,\psi}=\zeta_{p,\psi_{a}}(\frac{a}{q})
. Taking
p
-th powers, we obtain
(\frac{a}{q})=1
for all
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
. Thus every element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}
is a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, which implies that
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
∎

(Proof of Theorem
A.1
)

The uniqueness of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
follows from the Chebotarev density theorem. We prove the existence by constructing them explicitly.

By Lemma
A.3
, for any morphism
s\to S
from the spectrum of a finite field such that
C_{s}
is
s
-extremal, the finite étale
s
-scheme
H_{R,s}
splits completely.
Therefore, the Galois coverings
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
with the required properties should trivialize the finite étale
S
-scheme
H_{R}
.

Let
S_{1}\to S
be the
smallest connected Galois covering that trivializes
H_{R}
. Replacing
S_{1}
by
S
, we may assume that
H_{R}
is trivial over
S
.
Since
S
is connected, the
S
-scheme
H_{R}
can be identified with the constant group scheme associated with the group
H_{R}(S)
.

Since
V_{R}\to S
is an intermediate covering of
H_{R}\to S
,
V_{R}
is also trivial. We also identify it with the constant
S
-scheme associated with the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-vector space
V_{R}(S)
. By abuse of notation,
we write
V_{R}
and
H_{R}
for
V_{R}(S)
and
H_{R}(S)
, respectively.

One checks that the action of
H_{R}
on
C_{R}
is free and that the quotient morphism
C_{R}\to C_{R}/H_{R}
is identified with
E_{R}\circ\phi\colon C_{R}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{S}
. In the case where
S
is the spectrum of a finite field, this is proved in
[
12
, Lemma 2.7]
. The general case follows by the same argument.

Fix a maximal totally isotropic subspace
\overline{A}\subset V_{R}
and set
A:=\{(a,b)\in H_{R}\mid a\in\overline{A}\}
, which is a maximal abelian subgroup of
H_{R}
. Put
F_{A}(x):=\prod_{a\in\overline{A}}(x-a)
. The quotient morphism
C_{R}\to C_{R}/A
is identified with
F_{A}\circ\phi\colon C_{R}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{S}
.

Let
f\colon C_{R}\to S
denote the structure morphism. We compute
R^{1}f_{!}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
as follows. First we have

\phi_{!}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\cong\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\oplus\bigoplus_{\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(xR(x)).

Let
g\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{S}\to S
denote the structure morphism. Since

R^{1}g_{!}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}=0
, we have

R^{1}f_{!}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\cong\bigoplus_{\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\}}R^{1}g_{!}({\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(xR(x)))=\bigoplus_{\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\}}R^{1}g_{!}\mathcal{M}_{\psi},

where we set
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}:={\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(xR(x))
.
We claim that
R^{1}g_{!}\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is a smooth
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaf on
S
. Indeed, consider the canonical compactification

{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{S}\xhookrightarrow{j}\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}\xrightarrow{\overline{g}}S.

Then

R^{1}g_{!}\mathcal{M}_{\psi}\cong R^{1}\overline{g}_{!}j_{!}\mathcal{M}_{\psi}.

Consequently, by
[
9
, Corollaire 2.1.2]
applied to
j_{!}\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
, smoothness follows once we check that the function

S\to{\mathbb{Z}},\quad s\mapsto{\rm Sw}_{\infty}(j_{!}\mathcal{M}_{\psi}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{s}}\bigr)

is constant, where
{\rm Sw}_{\infty}
denotes the Swan conductor at the infinity. This conductor is equal to
p^{e}+1
by
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
, and hence constant.

Hence, the sheaf
R^{1}g_{!}\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is smooth. Moreover, it carries an action of
H_{R}
.
Take and fix a geometric point
\eta\to S
lying over a closed point. Then the stalk

V_{\psi}:=(R^{1}g_{!}\mathcal{M}_{\psi})_{\eta}

becomes a continuous
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-representation of the group
\pi_{1}(S,\eta)\times H_{R}
. By the proper base change theorem,
V_{\psi}
is isomorphic to

H^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{\eta},\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(xR(x))|_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{\eta}}).

Thus,
V_{\psi}
is irreducible as an
H_{R}
-representation by Lemma
A.3
(i). Since the actions of
\pi_{1}(S,\eta)
and
H_{R}
commute, the group
\pi_{1}(S,\eta)
acts on
V_{\psi}
through a character

\chi_{\psi}\colon\pi_{1}(S,\eta)\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}.

Using these characters, we define the
S
-scheme
T_{1}
to be the Galois covering corresponding to the intersection of the kernels of the characters

\chi_{\psi}\cdot\chi_{\psi^{\prime}}^{-1}\qquad\text{for all }\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\}.

Similarly, we define
T_{2}
to be the Galois covering corresponding to the intersection of the kernels of the characters

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}(-1/2)\cdot\chi_{\psi}^{-1}\qquad\text{for all }\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\},

where
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}(-1/2)
denotes the
character defined by the composite

\pi_{1}(S)^{\mathrm{ab}}\to\pi_{1}({\rm Spec}({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}))\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}.

Here, the first map is induced by the structure morphism
S\to{\rm Spec}({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}})
, whose existence follows from Lemma
A.3
and the Chebotarev density theorem, and the
second map sends the geometric Frobenius element to
p
.

To verify that these definitions indeed give
finite
coverings, it suffices to show that the displayed characters have finite order. For this, it is enough to prove that the character

\theta_{\psi}\coloneqq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}(-1/2)\cdot\chi_{\psi}^{-1}

has finite order. In fact, we show that
\theta_{\psi}^{4p_{0}}
is trivial. Since this can be checked at closed points, we may assume that
S={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
for some finite field extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

This follows from
[
12
, Theorem 1.2(1)]
, and the proof is complete.
∎
