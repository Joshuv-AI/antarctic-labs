Algebraic planar torsion in contact manifolds
Zhengyi Zhou

Abstract
. We demonstrate that the functorial properties of the symplectic field theory under strong cobordisms and surgery cobordisms can produce finite algebraic (planar) torsions from simple examples, which gives a unified treatment of most of the known computations of algebraic (planar) torsions. In addition, we obtain many families of new examples, notably including (1) stably fillable examples in all dimensions ≥ 5 \geq 5 with algebraic (planar) torsion precisely k k for any given k ∈ ℕ + k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+} , confirming a conjecture of Latschev and Wendl; (2) contact structures on spheres of all dimensions at least 5 5 with finite algebraic planar torsion at least 1 1 , which implies that tight not weakly fillable contact structures are ubiquitous in higher dimensions. We also explain that all known examples of contact manifolds without strong/weak fillings in dimension ≥ 5 \geq 5 have algebraic planar torsion.

Introduction

A fundamental dichotomy in contact topology distinguishes between
overtwisted

tight
contact structures. As established in
[

Tight contact structures, in contrast, are inherently geometric and generally more difficult to construct and classify. An important sufficient condition for tightness is symplectic fillability: every contact manifold that is

,
Eli90a
,

,

,

,

fillings (where the symplectic form is compatible with the canonical conformal symplectic structure on the contact hyperplanes). These notions yield the hierarchy

\{\text{strongly fillable}\}\subseteq\{\text{weakly fillable}\}\subseteq\{\text{tight}\},

which is strict in all odd dimensions
\geq 3

[
Eli96
,
EH02
,
MNW13
,
BGM22
]
.

Obstructions to strong or weak fillings in dimension at least
5
often arise from ‘‘wrong cobordisms’’ to contact manifolds that exhibit strong filling rigidity.

Symplectic field theory (SFT), introduced by Eliashberg, Givental, and Hofer
[

This makes SFT a natural tool for studying symplectic fillings, which are special cases of cobordisms. Many geometric properties find algebraic interpretations through SFT: for example, overtwisted manifolds have vanishing contact homology

Finiteness of algebraic (planar) torsion obstructs augmentations in the corresponding algebraic theory. Since augmentations arise from counting curves in symplectic fillings, finiteness of torsion implies an obstruction to fillings. Our first theorem demonstrates that, in dimension at least
5

Theorem A
.

All known examples of contact manifolds without strong or weak fillings in dimension at least

admit finite algebraic planar torsion (with twisted coefficients).

Question 1.1

In general dimensions (or in dimension at least

Are there obstructions to strong and weak fillings that go beyond SFT augmentation obstructions? Are there obstructions arising from higher-dimensional moduli spaces, in the spirit of Floer homotopy theory?

Given the high flexibility of contact structures in higher dimensions, affirmative answers to both questions seem likely. However, both the theoretical construction of those invariants and the geometric construction of potential examples are missing in the literature and are very challenging.

Although algebraic (planar) torsion is defined in all dimensions, it has been studied mostly in dimension

Theorem D

We will obtain more precise upper bounds for

We will introduce them in the main text, but the list will not be exhaustive. The rule of thumb is that if the convex boundary exhibits some filling rigidity (uniqueness, non-existence, etc.) via rational holomorphic curves (more precisely, if it can be phrased in RSFT), then a “wrong cobordism” violating those properties will force finite algebraic planarity on the concave boundary.

For

for

. This yields a Weinstein cobordism with convex boundary

Definition 2.1
.

A contact
(+1)
-surgery along a Legendrian sphere

to denote the resulting contact manifold, and

Remark 2.2

The

on
M=\partial\Sigma
. Blowing down the Giroux domain consists of gluing
({\mathbb{D}}\times M,\alpha_{M}+r^{2}{\rm d}\theta)
for

to
(0,\epsilon)_{s}\times S^{1}_{t}\times M
via

. This blow-down produces a contact submanifold

from

of the blow-down. It is clear from the construction that blowing down the Giroux domain in

recovers

. Moreover, this blow-down process can be described by a strong cobordism obtained from a round handle attachment as in
[
MNW13
, §5.1]
.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
MNW13
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Assume

has a Giroux domain
\Sigma\times S^{1}
, and let

denote the manifold obtained by blowing down. There is a strong cobordism
(W,\omega)
from
Y
to

obtained by attaching
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{D}}
to

\Sigma\times\{0\}\subset W
is a codimension

. The symplectic form

has a primitive defined outside

\Sigma\times\{0\}
the non-exact co-core of the round handle.

Example 2.8

(
S^{1}
-invariant contact structures)
.

In
[
DG12
]
, Ding and Geiges showed that any
S^{1}_{\tau}
-invariant contact form
\alpha

admits a decomposition

(W_{+}\cup_{\Gamma}(-W_{-}))\times S^{1}_{\tau}
with

\alpha=f{\rm d}\tau+\beta,\quad f\in C^{\infty}(S),\quad\beta\in\Omega^{1}(S),

such that on the interior
W^{\circ}_{\pm}
, we have
\pm f>0
and
\beta_{\pm}=\pm\beta/f\in\Omega^{1}(W_{\pm}^{\circ})
defining Liouville forms. The symplectic forms

then orient
W_{+}
(respectively

. It also follows that
\alpha
is a contact form when restricted to
\Gamma=\{f=0,\tau=\tau_{0}\}
, and we write
\xi_{\Gamma}=\ker\alpha|_{\Gamma}
. With this contact structure,
(\Gamma,\xi_{\Gamma})
is the ideal boundary of
(W_{\pm},\beta_{\pm})

(W_{\pm},\beta_{\pm},\ker\beta|_{\Gamma})
are ideal Liouville domains.

Conversely, let
(W_{+},\omega_{+},\xi_{+})
and
(W_{-},\omega_{-},\xi_{-})
be two ideal Liouville domains. Given a contactomorphism
\phi:(\partial W_{+},\xi_{+})\to(\partial W_{-},\xi_{-})

-invariant contact structure on

and

[

\DG(W_{+},W_{-},\phi)
for such an

-invariant contact manifold. Then we can view it as obtained from transverse surgeries:

\DG(W_{+},W_{-},\phi)=\partial(W_{+}\times{\mathbb{D}})_{\Gamma,W_{-},\phi^{-1}}=\partial(W_{-}\times{\mathbb{D}})_{\Gamma,W_{+},\phi}.

The cobordism in
Proposition

2.7
produces cobordisms from
\DG(W_{+},W_{-},\phi)

\partial(W_{+}\times{\mathbb{D}})
or

In higher dimensions, we not only have greater flexibility in choosing symplectic fillings for transverse surgery, but also the extra freedom of choosing the contactomorphism
\phi
, although our knowledge of the contact mapping class group is very limited. One special case of contactomorphisms are those lifted from the symplectic mapping class groups of certain contactization pieces. The following is a prominent example.

Example 2.9

(Bourgeois contact structures)
.

Let
(\Sigma,\beta_{\Sigma})
be a Liouville domain and
\phi\in\symp_{c}(\Sigma,\beta_{\Sigma})
a compactly supported symplectomorphism. Then
\phi^{*}\beta_{\Sigma}=\beta_{\Sigma}+\eta
, where
\eta
is a closed
1
-form that is zero near
\partial\Sigma
. The Thurston–Winkelnkemper construction endows the total space
Y=(\partial\Sigma\times{\mathbb{D}})\cup\Sigma_{\phi}
of the open book with a contact form
\alpha
, where
\Sigma_{\phi}=\Sigma\times[0,1]_{t}/(x,1)\sim(\phi(x),0)
is the mapping torus. More precisely,

\alpha=\begin{cases}\beta_{\Sigma}|_{\partial\Sigma}+Kr^{2}{\rm d}\theta&\text{along }\partial\Sigma\times{\mathbb{D}},\\
\beta_{\Sigma}+b(\theta)\eta+2\pi K{\rm d}t&\text{along }\Sigma_{\phi},\theta=2\pi t,\end{cases}

for
K\gg 0
and
b=b(\theta)
a function equal to
1
near
\theta=2\pi
and
0
near
\theta=0
. Such a contact open book is denoted by

(Y,\xi)=\Openbook(\Sigma,\phi)

Strictly speaking, the contact open book has a corner at
S^{1}\times\partial\Sigma
, since a neighborhood with the contact form is identified with a neighborhood of the corner in the boundary of the product
(\Sigma\times{\mathbb{D}},Kr^{2}{\rm d}\theta+\lambda)
. One can round the corner following, e.g.,
[
Zho23b
, §2.1]
.

The smooth structure near the corner is inherited from the submanifold smooth structure in
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{D}}
after smoothing the corner. Therefore, the projection to
{\mathbb{D}}
is a smooth map; we use
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
to denote the two coordinates. Hence
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}):Y\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
has
0
as a regular value with inverse image
\partial\Sigma\times\{0\}
, which is the binding of the open book for
Y
. Bourgeois
[
Bou02
]
showed that

\alpha_{\mathrm{BO}}:=\alpha+\Phi_{1}{\rm d}x+\Phi_{2}{\rm d}y

is a contact form on
Y\times T^{2}=\Openbook(\Sigma,\phi)\times T^{2}
, where
(x,y)
are coordinates on
T^{2}
. The associated contact structure is independent of choices (e.g.
K
,

, and
\phi
up to isotopy). We refer to such contact manifolds as

, denoted by
\BO(\Sigma,\phi)
. Observe that this contact form (and hence the contact structure) is

-invariant. In particular, each

is a convex hypersurface with respect to the contact vector field
\partial_{y}
. Therefore we have
[
AZ24
, Proposition 2.6]

\BO(\Sigma,\phi)=\DG(W,W,\phi_{\BO}),

where
W=\Sigma\times D^{*}S^{1}
, and
\phi_{\BO}
on
\partial(\Sigma\times D^{*}S^{1})
is given by
\phi^{-1}
on
\Sigma\times\{1\}\times S^{1}
and identity elsewhere. By
Example

, we can view

\BO(\Sigma,\phi)=\partial(W\times{\mathbb{D}})_{\partial(\Sigma\times D^{*}S^{1}),W,\phi_{\BO}}.

Moreover, we obtain a strong cobordism from

to

via

[

]

2.3.
Spinal open books

Spinal open books generalize the concept of contact open books and were introduced by Lisi, Van Horn-Morris, and Wendl
[
LHMW18
]
. Heuristically, spinal open books arise as the contact boundary of a Lefschetz fibration over a surface with boundary. Such contact manifolds, especially in dimension
4
, have been studied systematically in
[
LHMW18
,
LHMW20
,
LW21
,
MRW24
]
. Moreover, spinal open books can be used to construct contact manifolds with infinitely many fillings
[
BvHM15
,
Zho24a
]
.

In this paper, we will only consider special cases of spinal open books. Let
S_{k}
be
S^{2}
with
k
disks removed, viewed as a Liouville surface with Liouville form
\lambda_{S}
. Let
M
be a contact manifold, let
\Sigma_{1},\ldots,\Sigma_{k}
be
k
Liouville fillings of
M
, each equipped with a contactomorphism
\psi_{i}:\partial\Sigma_{i}\to M
, and let
\phi_{i}\in\pi_{0}(\symp_{c}(\Sigma_{i}))
. We can endow the space

\left(S_{k}\times M\displaystyle\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}(\Sigma_{i})_{\phi_{i}}\right)/\sim

with a contact structure, where the gluing
\sim
is given by

S^{1}\times M\to\partial\Sigma_{\phi_{i}}=S^{1}\times\partial\Sigma_{i},\quad(t,x)\mapsto(t,\psi_{i}^{-1}(x)),

and where
S^{1}
is the
i
th boundary component of
S_{k}
. We endow this space with a contact structure via a generalization of the Thurston–Winkelnkemper construction, see
[
LHMW18
, §2.3]
. More precisely, let
\alpha_{M}
be a contact form on
M
and
\lambda_{i}
a Liouville form on
\Sigma_{i}
that restricts to
\phi_{i}^{*}\alpha_{M}
on the boundary. Then the contact form is given by

(2.1)

\alpha=K\lambda_{S}+\alpha_{M}\text{ on }S_{k}\times M,\quad\alpha=K{\rm d}t+\lambda_{i}+\beta(t)\eta_{i}\text{ on }(\Sigma_{i})_{\phi_{i}}.

Here we assume that
\lambda_{S}
restricts to
{\rm d}t
on each boundary component
S^{1}=[0,1]_{t}/0\sim 1
and that
\phi_{i}^{*}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i}+\eta_{i}
. Strictly speaking, we need to smooth the corners for both the smooth structure and the contact form as before. We use
\SOB(\bm{\Sigma},\bm{\phi},\bm{\psi})
(with bold font) to denote this contact manifold. By a standard argument using Gray stability, the contact structure up to isotopy is well-defined for all
K\gg 0
and for
\lambda_{S},\alpha_{M},\lambda_{i},\psi_{i},\phi_{i}
up to suitable homotopy. If
\Sigma_{1}=\ldots=\Sigma_{k}=\Sigma
and
\psi_{1}=\ldots=\psi_{k}
, then we use
\SOB(\Sigma,\bm{\phi})
to denote the special case. When
k=1
, this specializes to the contact open book
\OB(\Sigma,\phi)
. We can suppress
\psi
here, as different
\psi
yield contactomorphic open books.

Remark 2.10
.

A few remarks on spinal open books are in order.

(1)

is called a

5
5

5

Analogs of rational open books would exist, if the boundary of the page admits a Reeb flow generating an

(4)

The presence of

up to isotopy. However, this is no longer the case in higher dimensions. In dimension

Given a spinal open book

2.4.
Liouville connected sum

In this part, we review Avdek’s Liouville connected sum
[
Avd21
]
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
(Y^{2n-1},\xi)
be a contact manifold. A Liouville hypersurface
i:\Sigma\subset Y
is a Liouville domain
(\Sigma,\lambda)
for which there exists a contact form
\alpha
on
Y
such that
i^{*}\alpha=\lambda
.

For example, a page of a spinal open book is a Liouville hypersurface. Transverse surgery yields a Liouville hypersurface
\Sigma
in
Y_{M,\Sigma,\phi}
.

Example 2.15

(
[
Avd21
, Example 1.5]
)
.

Let
S\subset Y^{2n-1}
be an isotropic submanifold, and assume the symplectic normal bundle
TS^{{\rm d}\alpha|_{\xi}}/TS\subset\xi
is a trivial symplectic bundle. Then by Morse’s trick, a neighborhood of
S
is modeled on a neighborhood of the zero section in the contactization
(\mathbb{R}_{z}\times T^{*}S\times\mathbb{R}^{2n-2-2\dim S},{\rm d}z+\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}+\lambda_{\rm std})
, where
\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}
is the canonical Liouville form on
T^{*}S
and
\lambda_{\rm std}
is the standard Liouville form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n-2-2\dim S}
. This produces a Liouville hypersurface of the form
D^{*}S\times{\mathbb{D}}^{n-1-\dim S}
. A special case is the Liouville hypersurface
{\mathbb{D}}^{n-1}
in any contact manifold, which is the positive half of the convex boundary after deleting a Darboux ball.

The main theorem of
[
Avd21
]
is that we can cut and paste contact manifolds along Liouville hypersurfaces. More precisely, given two disjoint Liouville hypersurfaces
i_{1},i_{2}:(V,\lambda)\to(Y,\xi)
, we can cut along the images of
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
to produce two isomorphic convex hypersurface boundaries. Gluing these two convex hypersurfaces yields a new contact manifold, denoted by
\#_{((V,\lambda),(i_{1},i_{2}))}(Y,\xi)
. This procedure is called
Liouville connected sum
. The significance of this construction is the following natural cobordism:

Proposition 2.16

(
[
Avd21
, Theorem 1.8]
)
.

There is a Liouville cobordism from
Y
to
\#_{((V,\lambda),(i_{1},i_{2}))}(Y,\xi)
. If
V
is Weinstein, then so is the cobordism. More precisely, a
(k+1)
-Weinstein handle in the cobordism corresponds to a
k
-handle in
V
.

Many classical constructions, e.g., Weinstein handle attachment, can be viewed as Liouville connected sums, see
[
Avd21
, Example 1.7]
. Another example is the natural cobordism between spinal open books.

It was shown in
[
Avd21
,
Bal12
,
BEVHM12
,
Klu18
]
that there is an exact cobordism from
\OB(\Sigma,\phi)\sqcup\OB(\Sigma,\psi)
to
\OB(\Sigma,\phi\circ\psi)
, which is a Weinstein cobordism if
\Sigma
is Weinstein. In terms of the Liouville connected sum
[
Avd21
, Proposition 8.2]
,
\OB(\Sigma,\phi\circ\psi)
is
\#_{(\Sigma,(i_{1},i_{2}))}(\OB(\Sigma,\phi)\sqcup\OB(\Sigma,\psi))
, where
i_{1},i_{2}:\Sigma\to\OB(\Sigma,\phi),\OB(\Sigma,\psi)
are inclusions of pages. Similar to the proof of
[
Avd21
, Proposition 8.2]
, we have the following generalization.

Proposition 2.17
.

Given two spinal open books
\SOB\left(\bm{\Sigma},\bm{\phi},\bm{\psi}\right)
,
\SOB\left(\bm{\Sigma^{\prime}},\bm{\phi^{\prime}},\bm{\psi^{\prime}}\right)
, assume that
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}=\Sigma
and
\psi_{1}=\psi^{\prime}_{1}=\psi
, and
i,i^{\prime}:\Sigma\to\SOB\left(\bm{\Sigma},\bm{\phi},\bm{\psi}\right),\SOB\left(\bm{\Sigma^{\prime}},\bm{\phi^{\prime}},\bm{\psi^{\prime}}\right)
are inclusions of the first pages as Liouville hypersurfaces. Then

\displaystyle\#_{\Sigma,(i,i^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\left(\SOB\left(\bm{\Sigma},\bm{\phi},\bm{\psi}\right)\sqcup\SOB\left(\bm{\Sigma^{\prime}},\bm{\phi^{\prime}},\bm{\psi^{\prime}}\right)\right)=

\displaystyle\SOB\left(\bm{\Sigma}\cup\left(\bm{\Sigma^{\prime}}\backslash\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{1}\}\right),\{\phi_{1}\circ\phi^{\prime}_{1}\}\cup\left(\bm{\phi}\backslash\{\phi_{1}\}\right)\cup\left(\bm{\phi^{\prime}}\backslash\{\phi^{\prime}_{1}\}\right),\bm{\psi}\cup\left(\bm{\psi^{\prime}}\backslash\{\psi^{\prime}_{1}\}\right)\right).

Remark 2.18
.

We can also apply the Liouville connected sum to embeddings of
\Sigma
into the same spinal open book, either in the same paper component or in different paper components. We may also twist the Liouville embedding by elements in
\symp_{c}(\Sigma)
. This will result in a spinal open book with possibly different vertebrae and different monodromy. For example, we can perform the Liouville connected sum for two pages in
\OB(\Sigma,\phi)
with suitable twists from
\symp_{c}(\Sigma)
to obtain
\SOB(\Sigma,\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\})
, such that
\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{2}=\phi
. Moreover, we can twist the Liouville embedding by elements in
\symp(\Sigma)
that restrict to a contactomorphism on the boundary. The Liouville connected sum will then also induce twists on the
\{\psi\}
part.

2.5.
Weak fillings and stable fillings

Here we recall the notion of weak filling and stable filling from
[
MNW13
,
LW11
]
.

Definition 2.19

(
[
MNW13
, Definition 4]
)
.

Let
\xi
be a co-oriented contact structure on a manifold
Y
. Let
(W,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold with
\partial W=Y
as oriented manifolds. We say that
(W,\omega)
is a weak filling of
(Y,\xi)
, and
\omega
weakly dominates
\xi
if

\alpha\wedge({\rm d}\alpha+\omega_{\xi})^{n-1},\quad\alpha\wedge\omega_{\xi}^{n-1}

are positive volume forms on
Y
for any positive contact form
\alpha
and
\omega_{\xi}=\omega|_{\xi}
.

These conditions are equivalent to the condition that
t\omega+(1-t){\rm d}\alpha
defines a symplectic structure on
\xi=\ker\alpha
for any
t\in[0,1]
. In dimension
3
, they are equivalent to the compatibility of the orientations on
\xi
induced by
\omega
and
{\rm d}\alpha
.

A closely related notion from the perspective of symplectic field theory is stable fillings of contact manifolds, see
[
LW11
, Definition 1.9]
. A fundamental theorem relating them is that a weak filling can be deformed into a stable filling
[
MNW13
, Proposition 6]
. The following simple lemma is needed when we consider weak fillings under contact surgeries and cobordisms.

Lemma 2.20
.

Assume
W
is an exact cobordism from
Y_{-}
(concave boundary) to
Y_{+}
(convex boundary) such that
H^{2}(W;\mathbb{R})\to H^{2}(Y_{-};\mathbb{R})
is surjective. If
Y_{-}
is weakly fillable, then so is
Y_{+}
.

Proof.

Let
(V,\omega)
be a weak filling of
(Y,\alpha_{-})
. By
[
MNW13
, Lemma 1.6]
, a neighborhood of the boundary of
V
is modeled on
(-\epsilon,0]_{t}\times Y_{-},{\rm d}(t\alpha_{-})+\omega|_{Y_{-}})
. On the exact cobordism
(W,\lambda)
, we have a closed
2
-form
\omega_{W}
extending
\omega|_{Y_{-}}
and a collar neighborhood of
Y_{-}
modeled on
([1,1+\epsilon)_{r}\times Y_{-},\lambda=r\alpha_{-})
. We may assume
\omega_{W}
on this collar neighborhood is
\omega|_{Y_{-}}
. Now we choose a function
f:[1,1+\epsilon)_{r}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, such that
f=r-1
near
1
and
f^{\prime}>0
and
f=Kr
for
K\gg 0
near
1+\epsilon
. Then by setting
t=1-r
, we can glue
V
to
W
equipped with a symplectic form
\omega_{W}+{\rm d}(\frac{f(r)}{r}\lambda).
To see it is a symplectic form, on the collar neighborhood in
W
, the form is
\omega|_{Y_{-}}+{\rm d}(f(r)\lambda)
, which is symplectic by the weak filling property. Outside the collar, the form is
K{\rm d}\lambda+\omega_{W}
, which is symplectic if
K\gg 0
. Finally,
K{\rm d}\lambda+\omega_{V}
weakly dominates the contact structure on
Y_{-}
if
K\gg 0
. Hence,
Y_{+}
is weakly fillable.
∎

Remark 2.21
.

It is clear from the proof that
Lemma

2.20
holds for a symplectic cobordism
(W,\omega)
that is strong on the negative boundary and weak on the positive boundary. Moreover, the weak filling can be glued as long as
\omega|_{Y_{-}}
extends to a closed
2
-form on
W
.

Example 2.22
.

By
[
LMN19
, Theorem A]
, if
(W,\omega)
is a weak filling of
\OB(\Sigma,\phi)
, then
(W\times T^{2},\omega\oplus{\rm d}\mathrm{vol}_{T^{2}})
is a weak filling of
\BO(\Sigma,\phi)
.

3.1.2.
Morphisms

We now recall morphisms between

algebras. Consider a family of operators
\{\phi^{k,l}:S^{k}V\to S^{l}V^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1,l\geq 0}

from the following tree description. To represent

but replace

on

and

is the identity in such a case, i.e.,

Definition 3.2

(
[

, Definition 2.10]
)
.

3.1.3.
Augmentations

The trivial vector space
V=\{0\}
has a unique trivial
BL_{\infty}
algebra structure with
p^{k,l}=0
. We use
\mathbf{0}
to denote this trivial
BL_{\infty}
algebra.

Definition 3.3

(
[
MZ25
, Definition 2.13]
)
.

A
BL_{\infty}
augmentation is a
BL_{\infty}
morphism
\epsilon:V\to\mathbf{0}
, i.e., a family of operators
\epsilon^{k}:S^{k}V\to{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying Definition
3.2
.

For a
BL_{\infty}
algebra
V
, we let

(3.5)

E^{k}V=\overline{B}^{k}SV:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}S^{j}SV,

which is a filtration (sentence length filtration) on
EV
compatible with the differential
\widehat{p}
. We have
E\mathbf{0}={\mathbb{Q}}\oplus S^{2}{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus+\ldots
with
\widehat{p}=0
, and
H_{*}(E^{k}\mathbf{0})=E^{k}\mathbf{0}
for all
k\geq 1
. We define
1_{\mathbf{0}}
to be the generator in
E^{1}\mathbf{0}
; then
1_{\mathbf{0}}\neq 0\in H_{*}(E^{k}\mathbf{0})
for all
k\geq 1
. We define
1_{V}\in H_{*}(E^{k}V)
to be the image of
1_{\mathbf{0}}
under the chain map
E^{k}\mathbf{0}\to E^{k}V
induced by the trivial
BL_{\infty}
morphism
\mathbf{0}\to V
. Note that the existence of an augmentation implies that
H_{*}(EV,\widehat{p})\neq 0
, as
\epsilon
descends to homology and
\epsilon_{*}1_{V}=1_{\mathbf{0}}\neq 0\in H_{*}(E\mathbf{0},0)
.

Definition 3.4

(
[
MZ25
, Definition 2.15]
)
.

We define the torsion of a
BL_{\infty}
algebra

to be

\mathrm{T}(V):=\min\{k-1\mid 1_{V}=0\in H_{*}(E^{k}V),k\geq 1\}.

Here, the minimum of an empty set is defined to be
\infty
.

By definition, we have that
\mathrm{T}(V)=0
iff
1_{V}\in H^{*}(SV,\widehat{p}^{1})
is zero. Since
H^{*}(SV,\widehat{p}^{1})
is an algebra with
1_{V}
a unit, in this case we have
H^{*}(SV,\widehat{p}^{1})=0
. In general, a finite torsion is an obstruction to augmentations, and the hierarchy of torsion is functorial with respect to
BL_{\infty}
morphisms, i.e., if there is a
BL_{\infty}
morphism from
V
to
V^{\prime}
, then
\mathrm{T}(V)\geq\mathrm{T}(V^{\prime})
, see
[
MZ25
, §2.5]
for more discussions. In the context of RSFT, this torsion invariant is the algebraic planar torsion
\APT(Y)
.

Given a
BL_{\infty}
augmentation
\epsilon
, we use a change of coordinates on
EV
to assemble a new
BL_{\infty}
structure
\{p^{k,l}_{\epsilon}\}
with the property that
p^{k,0}_{\epsilon}=0
for all

. We will call such a procedure the linearization w.r.t.

,
[
MZ25
, Propositions 2.17, 2.18]
. In particular,

form an

structure (after fixing suitable sign conventions; see
[
MZ25
, §2.1, 2.2]
) on

. There is a notion of pointed map on
BL_{\infty}
algebras, to model the structure arising from counting rational holomorphic curves in the symplectization with a marked point passing through a closed submanifold in the contact manifold (or more generally a closed chain), with a typical case a point constraint, see
[
MZ25
, Definition 2.19]
. Along with the linearization w.r.t. all possible augmentations, we can derive another type of numerical invariants
[
MZ25
, Definition 2.21]

; see
[
MZ25
, §3.5]
for details.

3.1.4.
Completions and Twisted Coefficients

We define the Novikov field
\Lambda
as

\Lambda=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}\left|a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}},\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=+\infty\right.\right\}.

Let
\omega:G\to\mathbb{R}
be a group homomorphism. The Novikov completion
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}[G/\ker\omega]}
of the group ring
{\mathbb{Q}}[G/\ker\omega]
is

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}[G/\ker\omega]}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}T^{g_{i}}\left|a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}},g_{i}\in G/\ker\omega,\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\omega(g_{i})=+\infty\right.\right\}.

These coefficient rings are equipped with a decreasing filtration and are complete with respect to the filtration.

Now let
V
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space with filtration degree
0
; then
V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Lambda
and
V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}[G/\ker\omega]}
have induced decreasing filtrations, and so do the base changes for
SV
and
EV
. We use
\overline{V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Lambda}
,
\overline{SV\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Lambda}
, etc., to denote the corresponding completions. For example,

\overline{V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Lambda}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}v_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}\left|v_{i}\in V,\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=+\infty\right.\right\}.

The filtration on
\overline{V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Lambda}
is given by

(\overline{V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Lambda})_{\rho}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}v_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}\left|v_{i}\in V,\lambda_{i}\geq\rho,\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=+\infty\right.\right\},

so that

(\overline{V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Lambda})_{\rho}\subset(\overline{V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Lambda})_{\rho^{\prime}},\text{ when }\rho\geq\rho^{\prime}.

Definition 3.5
.

A filtered completed
BL_{\infty}
algebra structure on
V
over
R=\Lambda,\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}[G/\ker\omega]}
consists of
R
-linear maps
p^{k,l}:\overline{S^{k}V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}R}\to\overline{S^{l}V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}R}

for
k\geq 1,l\geq 0
, such that the assembled map
\widehat{p}
is well-defined on
\overline{EV\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}R}
, has degree
1
, squares to zero, and preserves the filtration. The filtered completed versions for morphisms, augmentations, etc., are similar.

In the context of SFT, a typical case for
G
is the second homology of the contact manifold and
\omega
comes from certain closed
2
-forms that are positive on the contact structure; see §
3.2.3
for details.

3.1.5.
Maurer-Cartan Elements

Definition 3.6
.

A Maurer-Cartan element of a filtered completed

It is clear that

to
\overline{EV\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}R}

We can represent an element

3.2.
Rational symplectic field theory

3.2.1.
Punctured holomorphic curves in completions

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a co-oriented contact manifold and
\alpha
a contact form such that all Reeb orbits are non-degenerate. We fix an
\mathbb{R}
-translation invariant almost complex structure
J
on the symplectization
\widehat{Y}=\mathbb{R}_{t}\times Y
, such that
J\partial_{t}=R
(the Reeb vector field) and
J
preserves
\xi
and is
{\rm d}\alpha
-compatible on
\xi
. Let
\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}
be two ordered multisets (i.e., allowing duplicates of elements) of good Reeb orbits of
\alpha
.
{\mathcal{M}}_{Y,A}(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-})
is defined to be the moduli space of rational
J
-holomorphic curves in homology class
A\in H_{2}(Y,\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-})
with
|\Gamma_{+}|
ordered positive punctures and
|\Gamma_{-}|
ordered negative punctures, all equipped with asymptotic markers, such that the curve converges to orbits in
\Gamma_{+}/\Gamma_{-}
near the positive/negative punctures and converges to pre-fixed base points on the image of Reeb orbits along the direction of asymptotic markers, modulo reparameterization and
\mathbb{R}
-translation; see
[
MZ25
, §3.2]
for details. The following are compactifications of the moduli spaces above, as well as variants of them; all of them can be found in
[
MZ25
,
MZ23
]
.

(1)

We use
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{Y,A}(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-})
to denote the compactification of
{\mathcal{M}}_{Y,A}(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-})
; for formal discussions, one can either use the original SFT compactification in
[
BEH+03
]
or the reduced version by throwing out trivial cylinders used in
[
Par19
,
BH23
,
MZ25
]
. However, the definition of virtual count differs in these two settings, and it is currently carried out only using the reduced compactification. We could have
\Gamma_{-}=\emptyset
, but
\Gamma_{+}
has to be non-empty to have a non-empty moduli space.

(2)

Let
W
be a cobordism, not necessarily Liouville; we use
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{W,A}(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-})
to denote the compactified moduli space of rational curves of class
A
with asymptotics
\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}
in the completion
\widehat{W}
, i.e., similar to

except that there is no
\mathbb{R}
-translation symmetry anymore. When

is not exact, we can have
\Gamma_{-}=\emptyset
as well.

(3)

Let

be a point in

to denote the compactified moduli space of rational curves of class

in
\widehat{Y}

\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}
and a free marked point going through

. More generally, let

. Let

be a cobordism and

be a smooth map from an oriented manifold

(4)

or

The virtual dimension of

\left(\widehat{S}_{k}\times M\times[1,\infty)_{r}\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}[1,\infty)_{p}\times\Sigma_{\phi_{i}},\quad\lambda:=\left(\widehat{\lambda}_{S}+r\alpha_{M}\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}Kp{\rm d}t+\beta(t)\eta_{i}+\lambda_{i}\right)\right).

In this part, we will justify

6.1.
Examples in dimension

Even though the main focus of this paper is higher dimensional contact manifolds, we will first review briefly examples in dimension

Example 6.1
.

Contact manifolds Giroux torsion, e.g. the first example of weakly not strongly fillable contact manifolds
[
Eli96
]
, or more generally, planar
k
-torsion
[

, Definition 2.13]

[

, Theorem 1]
. Those manifolds are not strongly fillable
[

, Theorem 1]
, and not weakly fillable if they have fully separating planar

-torsion
[

, Corollary 3]
. By
[

, Theorem 6]
, those manifolds have algebraic torsion at most
k
and fully twisted algebraic torsion at most

if the planar torsion is fully separating. As explained in
[
MZ25
, Theorem 3.17]
, those manifolds also have algebraic planar torsion
k
, and fully twisted algebraic torsion

, respectively. In view of

, those manifolds arise as the concave boundaries of torsion cobordisms of type I. When the planar torsion is fully twisted, by
[
Wen13b
, Theorem 2]
, the torsion cobordism will satisfy conditions in

4.12
, hence the concave boundary has fully twisted finite algebraic torsion by the results in this paper.

Example 6.2
.

The first example of tight and not weakly fillable contact manifolds in
[
EH02
]
used filling obstructions in
[
Lis98
]
derived from Seiberg-Witten theory. Similar filling obstructions were also used in
[

]
to obtain, for example, a contact

yields tight contact manifold that is not weakly fillable. In
[

]
, such a contact manifold is shown to be algebraically overtwisted, i.e. with algebraic (planar) torsion

. In
[

, we showed that the contact homology of

is zero, where

such that the Thurston–Bennequin number is not

[

]
were very different from those in this paper, in particular, there is no obvious torsion cobordism for examples in
[

]
. In the example of torsion cobordisms from
+1
surgeries considered in this paper, we only use the homology class of

, while proofs in
[
ALZon
]
used Legendrian properties of

.

By
[

, Theorem 3]
, connected

Example 6.4

(Algebraically overtwisted contact manifolds)
.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, the following is the list of all known examples of contact manifolds with vanishing contact homology, i.e.
\APT=\AT=0
.

(1)

Overtwisted contact manifolds, when viewed as in
Example

2.3
, can always be realized as the negative boundary of a Liouville torsion cobordism.

(2)

Niederkrüger
[
Nie06
]
introduced the concept of plastikstufe, which is a higher-dimensional generalization of an overtwisted disk in dimension
3
. The bordered Legendrian open book (bLob) was then introduced by Massot-Niederkrüger-Wendl
[
MNW13
]
as a further generalization. Contact manifolds that contain those structures are called PS-overtwisted, which are generalizations of overtwisted contact manifolds by the work of Casals-Murphy-Presas
[
CMP19
, Theorem 1.1-(4)]
. By an unpublished work of Bourgeois and Niederkrüger sketched in
[
Bou09
, Theorem 4.10]
, PS-overtwisted contact manifolds also have vanishing contact homology. The idea is considering the degeneration of the Bishop family of holomorphic disks in the symplectization, as opposed to symplectic fillings in
[
Nie06
]
, one boundary is a degenerated holomorphic disk on the core of the bLob, and the other boundary will give rise to holomorphic planes killing the contact homology. In other words, the proof is phrasing the celebrated proof of the Weinstein conjecture for overtwisted contact manifolds in dimension
3
by Hofer
[
Hof93
]
in the context of contact homology. However, bLobs are codimension
n
objects in a
2n+1
dimensional contact manifold, where the torsion cobordism perspective is essentially codimension
0
. The latter is more or less necessary in order to have a higher
\APT
. Therefore, the connection between bLobs and torsion cobordisms is probably not very strong. PS-overtwisted contact manifolds are the ”but one” part in the abstract.

(3)

Contact manifolds arose as the concave boundary of
exact
torsion cobordisms of type I or II are algebraically overtwisted by the main theorem in
[
Zho23a
]

(4)

Contact
3
manifolds derived from applying contact
1/k
surgery to the right–handed trefoil knot with the maximal Thurston–Bennequin number in
(S^{3},\xi_{std})
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
are algebraically overtwisted by
[
Avd23b
]
. More generally, based on the algorithm in
[
Avd23b
]
, we showed in
[
LZ25
]
that the same holds for all positive torus knots (besides the unknot) with the maximum Thurston-Bennequin number. Those results are generalized vastly with a completely different method in
[
ALZon
]
to all dimensions.

(5)

Open books with monodromy certain negative fibered Dehn twists were shown to have vanishing contact homology
[
CDvK14
,
CDvK16
]
.

Example 6.5

(Higher dimensional Giroux torsion)
.

It was shown in
[
MNW13
, Theorem B]
that if a contact manifold contains a connected codimension
0
submanifold with nonempty boundary obtained by gluing together two Giroux domains, then it is not strongly fillable. As the domain has a non-trivial boundary, at least one of the Giroux domains is
S^{1}\times V
with
V
a Liouville domain with disconnected boundary. By blowing up the other Giroux domain, we obtain a strong cobordism to a contact manifold containing a bLob
[
MNW13
, Proposition 4.9]
. Then, by the functoriality of
\APT
under strong cobordisms, we know that those contact manifolds have finite
\APT
.
[
MNW13
, Theorem B]
can be applied to all non-strongly/weakly fillable examples in
[
MNW13
]
, e.g.
[
MNW13
, Theorems A, E, F and G]
, except those that contain bLobs only.

We can also understand
[
MNW13
, Theorem B]
in terms of a generalization of torsion cobordism of type III. Assume there are two Giroux domains
S^{1}\times V_{1},S^{1}\times V_{2}
glued along the common boundary
M\subset\partial V_{1},\partial V_{2}
, such that
\partial V_{1}\backslash M\neq\emptyset
. If
M=\partial V_{2}
, by capping off
S^{1}\times V_{1}
, we get a strong cobordism to
Y\sqcup\partial(V_{2}\times{\mathbb{D}})
for a non-empty contact manifold
Y
, that is, we get a torsion cobordism of type III. Similar to the proof of
Theorem

4.9
(the ruling curve intersects once with the non-exact co-core
V_{1}\times\{0\}
in the capping cobordism in
Proposition

2.7
), we get that the contact manifold we start with has
\APT\leq 1
. If
V_{2}\backslash M\neq\emptyset
, then we need to cap off both
S^{1}\times V_{1},S^{1}\times V_{2}
to get a strong cobordism
W
to
Y\sqcup M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
has ruling curves without positive punctures from the
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
-component, whose intersection with each of the two non-exact co-cores of the capping cobordism is one. Similar to the proof of
Theorems

4.15
and
4.9
, by considering the moduli space of curves passing through a path in
W
connecting a point in
M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and a point in
Y
, we can deduce that the algebraic planar torsion of the concave boundary is at most
1
. In some sense, this is phrasing the proof of
[
MNW13
, Theorem 6.1]
in terms of rational SFT. Moreover, for planar torsions in dimension
3
, we can cap off the planar torsion domain as in
[
Wen13b
]
to obtain a strong cobordism to
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\sqcup Y
behaving like a
k
-torsion cobordism. Therefore, we can use the functorial perspective in this paper to give an alternative proof to
[
LW11
, Theorem 6]
.

Using Liouville domains in the form of
M\times[-1,1]
, a higher-dimensional Giroux torsion was introduced in
[
MNW13
, before Corollary 8.2]
. Contact manifolds with such a Giroux torsion are special cases of
[
MNW13
, Theorem B]
recalled above. For contact manifolds with (higher-dimensional) Giroux torsions, it was already established in
[
Mor24
, Theorem 1.6]
that they have algebraic torsion at most
1
. The proof in
[
Mor24
]
was based on a direct computation of holomorphic curves and adiabatic limit methods relating “Morse-Bott” and Morse settings of contact forms.

Example 6.6

(Moreno’s example in
[
Mor24
]
)
.

Let
M
be a manifold with a Liouville pair
[
MNW13
, Definition 1]
, in particular,
M\times I
can be equipped with a Liouville structure
\lambda
, whose boundary is
(M,\alpha_{+}),(M,\alpha_{-})
with
\alpha_{\pm}
are two contact forms with opposite orientation. In
[
Mor24
]
, the following example was considered (after smoothing the corner):

Y:=\left((M\times S_{k})\cup_{i=1}^{k}(M\times I\times S^{1})\cup(M\times\Sigma_{g,k}),\quad(\alpha_{+}+\lambda_{S_{k}})\cup_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda+{\rm d}\theta)\cup(\alpha_{-}+\lambda_{\Sigma_{g,k}})\right)

where
\lambda_{S_{k}},\lambda_{\Sigma_{g,k}}
are Liouville forms on genus
0
, respectively
g
curve with
k
disks removed, such that the restriction of the boundary circle is
{\rm d}\theta
.
[
Mor24
, Theorem 1.3]
asserted such manifolds have algebraic torsion at most
k-1
if
g\geq k
. From the respective in this paper, we can cap off the
k

M\times I\times S^{1}
regions to obtain a strong cobordism
W
from
Y
to stable Hamiltonian manifold
(M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\alpha_{+},{\rm d}\alpha_{+}+\omega_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}})\sqcup(M\times\Sigma_{g},\alpha_{-},{\rm d}\alpha_{-}+\omega_{\Sigma_{g}})
.
M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is uniruled by closed curves in the
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
-direction, while
M\times\Sigma_{g}
has no non-trivial closed rational curves if
g>0
. Moreover, in the completion
\widehat{M}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the product almost complex structure, there are no curves (possibly with genus) without positive punctures but with negative punctures by projection to
\widehat{M}
and the maximum principle. Therefore, similar to the proofs of
Theorems

4.15
and
4.9
by considering degeneration of holomorphic curves in
W
without positive puncture and passing through a path connecting a point in
M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and a point in
M\times\Sigma_{k}
, we see that the algebraic planar torsion of
Y
is at most
k-1
if
g>0
. Moreover, we can argue similarly that the algebraic torsion is also at most
k-1
if
g>0
, instead of
g\geq k
.

Example 6.7

(Bourgeois contact manifolds)
.

In
[
BGM22
, Theorem C]
, if
\Sigma\hookrightarrow\OB(\Sigma,\phi)
does not induce an injection on rational homology, the Bourgeois contact manifold
\BO(\Sigma,\phi)
is not strongly fillable. More general results hold for some
S^{1}
-invariant contact structures by
[
BGM22
, Theorem B]
. Those manifolds arose as the concave boundary of
1
-torsion cobordism by
Example

2.8
, hence they have algebraic planar torsion at most
1
by
Theorem

4.9
.

Example 6.8

(Exotic contact structures in
[
BGMZ22
]
)
.

By Theorems
E
and
F
, exotic contact spheres as well as the connected sum with a general contact manifold considered in
[
BGMZ22
]
have finite fully twisted algebraic planar torsions.

6.3.
One more application

Let
\Sigma
be a Weinstein domain and
\phi\in\pi_{0}(\symp_{c}(\Sigma))
. By
[
BC23
, Lemma 2.2]
, the inclusion of the page
\Sigma\hookrightarrow\OB(\Sigma,\phi)
has a non-trivial kernel on the induced rational homology if and only if
\mathrm{var}(\phi)\neq 0
, which occurs in degree
\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\Sigma
.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
\Sigma
be a Weinstein domain and
\phi\in\pi_{0}(\symp_{c}(\Sigma))
such that
\mathrm{var}(\phi)\neq 0
. Then

(1)

At least one of
\OB(\Sigma,\phi)
and
\OB(\Sigma,\phi^{-1})
is algebraically overtwisted;

(2)

If
\OB(\Sigma,\phi)
is not algebraically overtwisted, then
\phi^{k}\neq\Id\in\pi_{0}(\symp_{c}(\Sigma))
for
k>0
.

Proof.

By
[
Avd21
, Proposition 8.2]
, there is a Weinstein cobordism
W
from
\OB(\Sigma,\phi)\sqcup\OB(\Sigma,\phi^{-1})
to
\OB(\Sigma,\Id)
. By assumption, the inclusion of the page
\Sigma
into
\OB(\Sigma,\Id)
and then into
W
has a non-trivial kernel on the induced rational homology. Then, by the Liouville cobordism case of
D
in §
4.3
, we have
\CH(\OB(\Sigma,\phi)\sqcup\OB(\Sigma,\phi^{-1}))=0
; that is, either
\CH(\OB(\Sigma,\phi))=0
or
\CH(\OB(\Sigma,\phi^{-1}))=0
. If
\phi^{k}=\Id
for
k>0
, we have a Weinstein cobordism
V
from
\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}\OB(\Sigma,\phi)
to
\OB(\Sigma,\Id)
such that the inclusion of the page
\Sigma
into
\OB(\Sigma,\Id)
and then into
V
has a non-trivial kernel on the induced rational homology because
\mathrm{var}(\phi)\neq 0
. Then we can deduce from the Liouville cobordism case of
D
that
\CH(\OB(\Sigma,\phi))=0
, contradicting the assumption.
∎

Example 6.10
.

The Dehn-Seidel twist
\phi_{DS}
on
D^{*}S^{n}
has non-trivial
\mathrm{var}(\phi_{DS})
. Since
\OB(D^{*}S^{n},\phi_{DS})
is
(S^{2n+1},\xi_{\rm std})
, which is not algebraically overtwisted because it is fillable, it follows that
\OB(D^{*}S^{n},\phi_{DS}^{-1})
, i.e., the overtwisted contact sphere, has vanishing contact homology. Moreover,
\phi_{DS}^{k}\neq\Id
in
\pi_{0}(\symp_{c}(D^{*}S^{n}))
for any
k>0
. Both are well-known, classical results regarding the Dehn-Seidel twist. More generally, if
L
is a Lagrangian sphere in a Weinstein domain
\Sigma
such that
[L]
is non-trivial in rational homology, then
\mathrm{var}(\phi_{L})\neq 0
for the Dehn-Seidel twist
\phi_{L}
around
L
. Then we have
\CH(\Sigma,\phi_{L}^{-1})=0
and
\phi_{L}^{k}\neq\Id\in\pi_{0}\symp_{c}(\Sigma)
for any
k>0
. The former result was established in
[
Zho23a
]
, and the latter result was established in
[
BGZ19
]
without the homology assumption
12

The homology assumption may be tautological in view of the regular Lagrangian conjecture in
[
EGL20
, Problem 2.5]
.

Another source of elements in symplectic mapping class groups is fibered Dehn twists on Weinstein domains whose boundaries are circle bundles of pre-quantization bundles. Contact open books from such monodromies and their contact homology were studied extensively by Chiang, Ding, and van Koert
[
CDvK14
,
CDvK16
]
. The following corollary provides more examples in the same flavor.

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a smooth projective variety, and let
D
be an ample divisor such that the map
H_{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{*-2}(D;{\mathbb{Q}})
given by
A\mapsto A\cap[D]
is not surjective. Then the ideal contact boundary of
X\backslash D
is the circle bundle of the normal bundle of
D
, and
\phi
is the fibered Dehn twist on
X\backslash D
such that

(1)

\CH(\OB(X\backslash D,\phi^{-1}))=0
;

(2)

\phi^{k}\neq\Id
in
\pi_{0}\symp_{c}(X\backslash D)
for
k\neq 0
.

Proof.

By
[
CDvK16
, Theorem 1.1]
, the circle bundle
Y
of the prequantization bundle
{\mathcal{O}}(-D)
over
X
, as a contact manifold, admits an open book
\OB(X\backslash D,\phi)
. Note that this contact manifold
Y
has a strong filling by
{\mathcal{O}}(-D)
; therefore,
\CH(Y)\neq 0
. To apply
Theorem

6.9
, we need that the map
H_{*}(X\backslash D)\to H_{*}(Y)
has a non-trivial kernel. By the hard Lefschetz theorem and the Gysin exact sequence, the map
H^{*}(X)\to H^{*}(Y)
is surjective for
*\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}X
, because
H^{i}(Y)
is spanned by the pullback of primitive
i
-forms on
X
for
i\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}X
. Dually, the map
H_{*}(Y)\to H_{*}(X)
is injective for
*\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}X
. Since
H_{*}(X\backslash D)
is supported in degree at most
\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}X
and the composition
H_{*}(X\backslash D)\to H_{*}(Y)\to H_{*}(X)
coincides with
\iota_{*}
induced by the inclusion
X\backslash D\hookrightarrow X
, it follows that
H_{*}(X\backslash D)\to H_{*}(Y)
is non-injective if and only if
\iota_{*}:H_{*}(X\backslash D)\to H_{*}(X)
is non-injective. Consider the long exact sequence,

\ldots\to H_{k+1}(X)\to H_{k+1}(X,X\backslash D)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{*}}}{{\to}}H_{k}(X\backslash D)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{*}}}{{\to}}H_{k}(X)\to\ldots.

Then
\iota_{*}
is injective if and only if
\partial_{*}=0
, which is equivalent to the surjectivity of
H_{k+1}(X)\to H_{k+1}(X,X\backslash D)
. Via the Thom isomorphism,
H_{k+1}(X,X\backslash D)\simeq H_{k-1}(D)
, and the map
H_{k+1}(X)\to H_{k+1}(X,X\backslash D)
corresponds to the map
H_{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{*-2}(D;{\mathbb{Q}})
given by
A\mapsto A\cap[D]
. Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

6.11
