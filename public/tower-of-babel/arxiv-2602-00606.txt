Actor-Dual-Critic Dynamics for Zero-sum and Identical-Interest Stochastic Games
Ahmed Said Donmez, Yuksel Arslantas and Muhammed O. Sayin

Abstract




Abstract

We propose a novel independent and payoff-based learning framework for stochastic games that is model-free, game-agnostic, and gradient-free. The learning dynamics follow a best-response-type actor-critic architecture, where agents update their strategies (actors) using feedback from
two
distinct critics: a fast critic that intuitively responds to observed payoffs under limited information, and a slow critic that deliberatively approximates the solution to the underlying dynamic programming problem. Crucially, the learning process relies on non-equilibrium adaptation through smoothed best responses to observed payoffs. We establish convergence to (approximate) equilibria in two-agent zero-sum and multi-agent identical-interest stochastic games over an infinite horizon. This provides one of the first payoff-based and fully decentralized learning algorithms with theoretical guarantees in both settings. Empirical results further validate the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed approach across both classes of games.

1
Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved remarkable success in domains such as board games
(
Silver et al. 2017
)
, autonomous driving
(
Shalev-Shwartz et al. 2016
)
, city-scale navigation
(
Mirowski et al. 2018
)
, and robotic control
(
Gu et al. 2023
)
. Many of these applications inherently involve multiple interacting agents, underscoring the importance of
multi-agent reinforcement learning
(MARL) as a framework for sequential decision-making in shared environments
(
Zhang et al. 2021
)
. Despite its empirical progress, MARL still relies heavily on heuristics and parameter tuning, often lacking strong theoretical guarantees.

Game theory provides a principled foundation for analyzing strategic interactions among intelligent agents. When combined with sequential decision-making, it gives rise to
stochastic games
(or Markov games), a natural and expressive model for MARL
(
Shapley 1953
;
Zhang et al. 2021
)
. However, stochastic games inherit and amplify the distinct challenges of both reinforcement learning and multi-agent interaction. Properties that hold in single-agent MDPs or in repeated matrix games may fail to extend to stochastic games, complicating both analysis and algorithm design. Recent work has sought to overcome these challenges and provide theoretical insight into algorithmic learning in stochastic games
(
Daskalakis et al. 2020
;
Sayin et al. 2021
;
Daskalakis et al. 2023
;
Ding et al. 2022
;
Jin et al. 2022
)
.

1.1
Contributions

In this paper, we focus on decentralized learning settings where agents update independently, without access to opponent actions, or environment models. Such minimal-information scenarios are highly relevant in practice yet challenging. Our goal is to develop learning algorithms that operate under these constraints while retaining convergence guarantees. This leads us to the question:

Can independent agents, relying solely on payoff feedback and non-equilibrium adaptation,
converge to equilibrium in key classes of stochastic games?

We provide an
affirmative
answer for two benchmark cases: zero-sum and identical-interest stochastic games.

To address the challenge of decentralized learning in stochastic games, we introduce a cognitively inspired, payoff-based actor-critic framework. Our key contributions are as follows:

A novel actor-dual-critic architecture:
We propose a new learning design where each agent independently maintains two critics: a
fast critic
, which rapidly estimates local rewards and transitions based on local observations, and a
slow critic
, which solves a fixed-point equation to approximate long-term values. Motivated by dual-process theories of human cognition
(
Kahneman 2011
)
, blending fast, reactive intuition with slow, deliberate planning, this dual decomposition addresses the challenge of non-stationarity in MARL: as each agent’s strategy evolves, it alters the environment faced by others. Treating this environment as stationary, as in single-agent RL, can lead to instability. Our architecture separates short-term reactions from long-term stabilization, allowing for more robust adaptation to such non-stationary issues.

Gradient-free, best-response-type strategy updates:
Our actor improves its strategy using
best-response-type
updates rather than relying on policy gradients. This avoids the limitations of gradient-based actor-critic methods
(
Konda and Tsitsiklis 1999
)
, which often converge only to local optima. Our approach is
gradient-free
, enabling simple and efficient updates,
game-agnostic
, avoiding assumptions about structure, and
provably convergent
to equilibria in structured game classes.
Furthermore, it aligns with theoretical arguments that equilibrium behavior can emerge from non-equilibrium adaptation processes
(
Fudenberg and Levine 2009
)
. Our analysis demonstrates that even under minimal information, such payoff-driven best-response-type dynamics can robustly converge to equilibrium—thereby reinforcing the predictive power of equilibrium in stochastic games.

Convergence to equilibrium in stochastic games:
We prove convergence to approximate Nash equilibria in two foundational settings: two-agent zero-sum stochastic games, and multi-agent identical-interest stochastic games. The approximation error is proportional to the exploration rate
\epsilon
. Convergence in identical-interest games is especially challenging. Unlike the zero-sum case, these games may lack unique game values for different equilibria, and value iteration may not have the contraction property. Our analysis leverages and extends recent advances in
quasi-monotonicity
techniques
(
Baudin and Laraki 2022
;
Sayin et al. 2022b
)
to ensure convergence in both settings under fully independent, payoff-based dynamics.

1.2
Related Work

Algorithmic learning in stochastic games has recently received significant attention due to their applications on MARL
(
Ozdaglar et al. 2022
)
. For example,
Leslie et al. 2020
presented a continuous-time generalization of fictitious play and best response dynamics to two-agent zero-sum stochastic games.
Sayin et al. 2022a
introduced uncoupled discrete-time fictitious-play dynamics in which each agent independently estimates continuation values based on their own beliefs.
Baudin and Laraki 2022
showed the convergence of
Leslie et al. 2020
’s dynamics also for identical-interest stochastic games.
Sayin et al. 2022b
showed the convergence of
Sayin et al. 2022a
’s dynamics also for identical-interest stochastic games with single controllers. Notably,
Sayin et al. 2021
presented independent and payoff-based dynamics generalizing
Leslie and Collins 2005
’s individual Q-learning to stochastic games with convergence guarantees for zero-sum games.
Chen et al. 2023
presented another independent and payoff-based dynamics generalizing
Leslie and Collins 2006
’s actor-critic dynamics to stochastic games with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for zero-sum games.

Several decentralized actor-critic methods have also been proposed under structural assumptions.

Arslan and Yüksel 2017
introduced decentralized Q-learning for pure-strategy equilibria, transforming stochastic games into a strategic-form game over Markov stationary deterministic strategies. While convergence is shown for weakly acyclic games, the restriction to pure strategies limits generality—particularly in zero-sum games where no pure-strategy equilibrium may exist.
Perolat et al. 2018
developed actor-critic learning for a class of multi-stage games, where each state is visited only once. In the zero-sum setting,
Daskalakis et al. 2020
studied independent policy gradient methods with two-timescale step sizes across agents, achieving non-asymptotic guarantees. Our actor-dual-critic dynamics differ from these works with provable convergence in both two-agent zero-sum and multi-agent identical-interest stochastic games with no access to opponent actions, game model, and dynamics.

On the other hand, for Markov potential games, which generalize identical-interest games, convergence of independent
policy gradient dynamics
has been established in several works
(
Leonardos et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2024
;
Fox et al. 2022
;
Ding et al. 2022
)
. By contrast, general—especially zero-sum—stochastic games lack such a Lyapunov structure, so researchers resort to coupled-Lyapunov or extra-gradient updates that still require cross-agent sampling coordination
(
Wei et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022
)
. Finite-time convergence is achieved by optimistic gradient descent-ascent and related extra-gradient variants, yet only under strong assumptions like state-space irreducibility or double-timescale updates that limit practical deployment
(
Wei et al. 2021
;
Zhao et al. 2022
)
. Notably,
Ding et al. 2022
shows the convergence of independent policy gradient for both zero-sum stochastic games and Markov potential games.

The literature on learning in general-sum stochastic games mostly focus on sample efficient algorithms with regret guarantees that finds coarse correlated equilibria
(
Jin et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Bai and Jin 2020
;
Song et al. 2021
;
Mao and Başar 2023
;
Tian et al. 2021
)
since finding a Nash equilibria is a PPAD-complete task
(
Daskalakis et al. 2023
)
.
Cai et al. 2023
showed a weaker concept of convergence, path convergence, on general stochastic games. On the other hand,
Maheshwari et al. 2024
introduced Markov Near-Potential Function idea to approximate general-sum games with potential functions and shows certain bounds on closeness to Nash equilibria.
Yongacoglu et al. 2023
showed convergence on symmetric stochastic games. With more structural constraints,
Park et al. 2023
demonstrated convergence on multi-agent zero-sum stochastic games with networked separable interactions, which is a generalization of polymatrix games to the stochastic game setting.

Stochastic Games

We consider an
n
-agent discounted stochastic game
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},(\mathcal{A}^{i})_{i\in[n]},(\mathsf{R}^{i})_{i\in[n]},p,\gamma)
, where
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}:=\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}^{i}
are
finite
state and action sets,
p(\cdot\mid s,a)
is the state transition kernel, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the discount factor. For each agent
i\in[n]
,
\mathsf{R}^{i}(\cdot\mid s,a)
is a reward kernel on
\mathbb{R}
, induced by a reward function
r^{i}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times Z^{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and an exogenous i.i.d. noise process
(\zeta_{k}^{i})_{k\geq 0}
with
finite
support
Z^{i}
, independent across agents, over time, and independent of the state process. Given that agents take joint action
a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
and the state is
s_{k}\in\mathcal{S}
at stage
k
, agent
i
receives reward
r_{k}^{i}=r^{i}(s_{k},a_{k},\zeta_{k}^{i})
. This formulation allows exploration-perturbed action execution to be equivalently modeled via stochastic rewards while treating actions as pure decisions.

Let each agent
i
randomize their actions contingent on the current state
s
via a Markov stationary strategy
\pi^{i}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta(\mathcal{A}^{i})
,
independent
of others. The agent
i
’s utility under strategy profile
\pi:=(\pi^{i})_{i\in[n]}
is the discounted sum of the rewards collected over infinite horizon and given by

U^{i}(\pi)=\mathrm{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\overline{r}^{i}(s_{k},a_{k})\right],

(1)

where
a_{k}\sim\pi(s_{k})
,
s_{k+1}\sim p(\cdot\mid s_{k},a_{k})
, the expectation is taken over the state transitions and strategy-induced randomness, and
\overline{r}^{i}(s,a):=\mathrm{E}_{\zeta}[r^{i}(s,a,\zeta)]
denotes the expected reward for the pair
(s,a)
with the expectation taken over the reward noise.

Definition 1 ([)

\varepsilon
-Nash Equilibrium]
A strategy profile
\pi_{*}
is an
\varepsilon
-Nash equilibrium (also known as
\varepsilon
-approximate Markov stationary equilibrium) of stochastic game
\mathcal{M}
with
\varepsilon\geq 0
provided that

U^{i}(\pi_{*}^{i},\pi_{*}^{-i})\geq U^{i}(\pi^{i},\pi_{*}^{-i})-\varepsilon\quad\forall\pi^{i}\mbox{ and }i,

(2)

where
\pi_{*}^{-i}:=(\pi_{*}^{j})_{j\neq i}
. Such equilibria exist for any
\mathcal{M}
and
\varepsilon\geq 0

(
Fink 1964
)
.

A common justification for the game-theoretical equilibrium analysis or computation is the
belief
that learning agents would reach equilibrium through non-equilibrium adaptations based on the feedback received across dynamic interactions
(
Fudenberg and Levine 2009
)
. In the following, we present independent and payoff-based learning dynamics to strengthen the predictive power of equilibrium for stochastic games.

3
Actor-Dual-Critic Learning Dynamics

In complex multi-agent environments, agents must make decisions under uncertainty and limited observability, often needing to balance short-term reactions with long-term strategic reasoning. In such cases, human decision-making uses the interplay between a
fast
system that reacts quickly based on observations, and a
slow
system that engages in deeper reasoning and planning
(
Kahneman 2011
)
. Motivated by this, we design a novel actor-critic architecture in which each agent is guided by two critics: a
fast critic
that responds fast to immediate observations, and a
slow critic
that slowly evaluates the future impact. This separation enables agents to adapt rapidly to changing local observations while simultaneously optimizing their long-term goals through slow, structured learning.

Let
v_{\pi}^{i}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
q_{\pi}^{i}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}^{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, resp., denote the value function and local q-function for strategy profile
\pi
. Then, the agent utility (
1
) yields that

\displaystyle q_{\pi}^{i}(s,a^{i})=\mathrm{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{-i}\sim\pi^{-i}(s)\\
s^{\prime}\sim p(\cdot\mid s,a^{i},a^{-i})\end{subarray}}\left[\overline{r}^{i}(s,a^{i},a^{-i})+\gamma\cdot v_{\pi}^{i}(s^{\prime})\right]

(3a)

\displaystyle v_{\pi}^{i}(s)=\mathrm{E}_{a^{i}\sim\pi^{i}(s)}[q_{\pi}^{i}(s,a^{i})].

(3b)

Given how the others play, i.e.,
\pi^{-i}
, the local q-function depends on the expected immediate reward and the expected value of the next state while the value function depends on the agent’s play, i.e.,
\pi^{i}
, and the local q-function. We propose a new learning algorithm in which a fast critic, an actor, and a slow critic, resp., learn these three parameters
q^{i},\pi^{i}
, and
v^{i}
.

The fast critic responds fast to learn
q_{\pi}^{i}
based on the local observations and the slow critic’s value function estimate by solving (
3a
) via stochastic approximation iterations and one-stage lookahead. At stage
k
, the local q-update for the previous pair
(s,a^{i})=(s_{k-1},a_{k-1}^{i})

q_{k}^{i}(s,a^{i})=q_{k-1}^{i}(s,a^{i})+\lambda_{k-1}\cdot\frac{r_{k-1}^{i}+\gamma\cdot v_{k-1}^{i}(s_{k})-q_{k-1}^{i}(s,a^{i})}{\pi_{k-1}^{i}(a^{i}\mid s)}

(4)

with some step size
\lambda_{k-1}\in(0,1)
decaying slowly, where
r_{k-1}^{i}
is the local immediate reward received at stage
k-1
,
s_{k}
is the (next) state at stage
k
, and
\pi_{k-1}^{i}(a^{i}\mid s)\in(0,1)
denotes the probability that
a^{i}
gets played at state
s
according to the strategy
\pi_{k-1}^{i}
, with a slight abuse of notation. The agent does not update the q-value for the other state-action pairs, i.e.,
q_{k}^{i}(s,a^{i})=q_{k-1}^{i}(s,a^{i})

\pi_{k-1}^{i}(a^{i}\mid s)\in(0,1)

(
Leslie and Collins 2005
)
.

For each agent
i

\mathcal{E}^{i}(\tilde{a}^{i}\mid a^{i}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1-\epsilon+\frac{\epsilon}{|\mathcal{A}^{i}|}&\mbox{if }\tilde{a}^{i}=a^{i}\\
\frac{\epsilon}{|\mathcal{A}^{i}|}&\mbox{if }\tilde{a}^{i}\neq a^{i}\end{array}\right.

(5)

for some exploration rate
\epsilon\in(0,1)

Then, the actor (determining how the agent plays) can iteratively improve its strategy
\pi^{i}_{k}
based on the
\epsilon

\pi_{k}^{i}(s)=\pi_{k-1}^{i}(s)+\alpha_{k-1}\cdot(\mathrm{br}_{\epsilon}(q_{k-1}^{i}(s,\cdot))-\pi_{k-1}^{i}(s))

(6)

for all

\alpha_{k-1}\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{br}_{\epsilon}(q^{i}_{k-1}(s,\cdot)):=\mathcal{E}^{i}(\cdot\mid\mathrm{br}(q^{i}_{k-1}(s,\cdot)))

(7a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{br}(q^{i}_{k-1}(s,\cdot))\in\argmax_{\tilde{a}^{i}\in\mathcal{A}^{i}}\{q^{i}_{k-1}(s,\tilde{a}^{i})\}.

(7b)

The actor-dual-critic dynamics are aligned with the existing independent and payoff-based methods proposed for stochastic games, such as those in
(
Sayin et al. 2021
)
and
(
Chen et al. 2023
)
.
Sayin et al. 2021
extends the individual-Q learning framework of
Leslie and Collins 2005
by integrating value iteration in a two-timescale setup, where values evolve more slowly. However, their agents do not follow an actor; instead, they directly respond to the local q-function through a smoothed best response. In contrast,
Chen et al. 2023
builds on the actor-critic dynamics of
Leslie and Collins 2006
, adapting them to stochastic games using a nested loop where value updates occur only at the end of each inner loop.

Our actor-dual-critic dynamics differ from
Chen et al. 2023
in three key ways: (i) value functions are updated continuously at a slower timescale rather than in episodic batches; (ii) actor strategies are improved using
\epsilon
-greedy responses instead of smoothed ones; and (iii) local q-function estimates use normalized step sizes for each action, yielding a more tractable and robust formulation. Furthermore, both
Sayin et al. 2021
and
Chen et al. 2023
provide convergence guarantees for their algorithms in two-agent zero-sum stochastic games using asymptotic and non-asymptotic analyses, respectively. In the following section, we establish the convergence of the actor-dual-critic dynamics in
both
two-agent zero-sum and multi-agent identical-interest stochastic games.

4
Main Results

To present the convergence results, we first address the exploration-perturbed execution and coupled dynamics across the actor and critics.

4.1
Effective Stochastic Game Formulation

The actor-update in
Algorithm

1
relies on
\epsilon
-best responses, which induce persistent and independent exploration in action execution. Rather than modeling this exploration explicitly through mixed strategies, we equivalently incorporate it into the reward and transition mechanisms so that we can view
\epsilon
-best responses as pure best responses in an
effective stochastic game
, characterized by the tuple
\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}=(\mathcal{S},(\mathcal{A}^{i})_{i\in[n]},(\mathsf{R}_{\epsilon}^{i})_{i\in[n]},p_{\epsilon},\gamma)
. Due to independent exploration, define the joint exploration kernel by
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{a}\mid a):=\prod_{j\in[n]}\mathcal{E}^{j}(\tilde{a}^{j}\mid a^{j})
. Then, the
effective
reward and transition kernels are given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{\epsilon}^{i}(\cdot\mid s,a):=\sum_{\tilde{a}\in\mathcal{A}}\mathsf{R}^{i}(\cdot\mid s,\tilde{a})\mathcal{E}(\tilde{a}\mid a),

(11a)

\displaystyle p_{\epsilon}(\cdot\mid s,a):=\sum_{\tilde{a}\in\mathcal{A}}p(\cdot\mid s,\tilde{a})\mathcal{E}(\tilde{a}\mid a).

(11b)

Correspondingly, the effective
expected
rewards and transitions are given by

\overline{r}_{\epsilon}^{i}(s,a)=\mathrm{E}_{\tilde{a}\sim\mathcal{E}(\cdot\mid a)}[\overline{r}^{i}(s,\tilde{a})]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{p}_{\epsilon}^{i}(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)=\mathrm{E}_{\tilde{a}\sim\mathcal{E}(\cdot\mid a)}[p_{\epsilon}(s^{\prime}\mid s,\tilde{a})].

(12)

Let
\mu:=(\mu^{i}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta(\mathcal{A}^{i}))_{i\in[n]}
be the
exploration-free
joint strategy associated with the exploration-perturbed execution strategy
\pi
in the original game, i.e.,
\pi^{i}(s)=(1-\epsilon)\mu^{i}(s)+\epsilon\mathrm{Uniform}(\mathcal{A}^{i})
.
When agents play according to
\pi
in the original stochastic game, the effective utility of agent
i
in the effective stochastic game is given by

U_{\epsilon}^{i}(\mu)=\mathrm{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{r}_{\epsilon}^{i}(s_{k},a_{k})\right],

(13)

where
a_{k}\sim\mu(s_{k})
and
s_{k+1}\sim\overline{p}_{\epsilon}(\cdot\mid s_{k},a_{k})
. Therefore, exploration-free strategies in this effective stochastic game
\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}
are distributionally equivalent to exploration-perturbed strategies in the original game
\mathcal{M}
.

4.2
Three-timescale Dynamics

In the underlying stochastic game, the interplay between the fast critic, the actor, and the slow criticyields a cyclic interconnection between them. The timescale separation of their evolution can break this cyclic nature by weakening the coupling between them. Particularly,
Algorithm

1
uses three step sizes
\lambda_{k}
,
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
, decaying at different rates to ensure such timescale separation.

Assumption 1 (Step Sizes)

The step sizes are given by

\lambda_{k}=(k+1)^{-\rho_{\lambda}}
,
\alpha_{k}=(k+1)^{-\rho_{\alpha}}
,
\beta_{k}=(k+1)^{-1}
for some
\frac{1}{2}<\rho_{\lambda}<\rho_{\alpha}<1
.

Section

4.2
ensures that the step sizes satisfy the standard stochastic approximation conditions, i.e., square-summable yet not summable, while
\alpha_{k}\in o(\lambda_{k})
and
\beta_{k}\in o(\alpha_{k})
, implying three-timescale dynamics. In particular, the step size
\lambda_{k}
decays to zero at the slowest rate yielding that the strategies and value estimates are
quasi-stationary
in the local q-update such that
q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)
can track
Q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\pi_{k}^{-i}
, where

Q_{k}^{i}(s,a):=\overline{r}^{i}(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)v_{k}^{i}(s^{\prime})\quad\forall(s,a)

(14)

is the underlying global Q-function associated with the value function estimate
v_{k}^{i}
. In the actor update, the step size
\alpha_{k}
decays to zero at a moderate rate yielding that the value estimates are
quasi-stationary
while local q-function estimates
q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)
are tracking
Q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\pi_{k}^{-i}
. Therefore, in each stage
k
, actors are approximately playing in an induced stage game whose payoffs are
Q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)
’s associated with the current state. The quasi-stationarity of value function estimates implies the quasi-stationarity of (implicit) global Q-function estimates by (
14
). Correspondingly, actors are
\epsilon
-greedy responding to each other in the repeated play of quasi-stationary stage games and can track equilibrium of the stage game if it has certain structures. In the slow-critic update, the step size
\beta_{k}
decays to zero at the fastest rate yielding that the target term
\pi_{k}^{i}(s)^{T}q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)
can track equilibrium associated with the current value function estimates.

4.3
Convergence Results

We define the
Nash gap
and
effective Nash gap
for exploration-perturbed
\pi
and exploration-free
\mu
strategy profiles, resp., by

\displaystyle\mathrm{NG}(\pi):=\max_{i\in[n]}\Big(\max_{\hat{\pi}^{i}}U^{i}(\hat{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i})-U^{i}(\pi^{i},\pi^{-i})\Big),

(15a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{NG}_{\epsilon}(\mu):=\max_{i\in[n]}\Big(\max_{\hat{\mu}^{i}}U_{\epsilon}^{i}(\hat{\mu}^{i},\mu^{-i})-U_{\epsilon}^{i}(\mu^{i},\mu^{-i}).\Big).

(15b)

Given Markov stationary strategies
\pi^{-i}
or
\mu^{-i}
, the problems
\max_{\hat{\pi}^{i}}U^{i}(\hat{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i})
or
\max_{\hat{\mu}^{i}}U_{\epsilon}^{i}(\hat{\mu}^{i},\mu^{-i})
induce Markov decision processes, for which Markov stationary solutions always exist
(
Sutton and Barto 2018
)
. Furthermore, these gaps are non-negative by definition.

Proposition 1.

Consider an exploration-free Markov stationary strategy profile
\mu
and the associated exploration-perturbed strategy profile
\pi
, i.e.,
\pi^{i}(s)=(1-\epsilon)\mu^{i}(s)+\epsilon u^{i}(s)
for all
i
and
s
. If
\mathrm{NG}_{\epsilon}(\mu)=0
, then
\mathrm{NG}(\pi)\leq\varepsilon
, where

\varepsilon:=\frac{2\epsilon}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\cdot\max_{(i,s,a)}|\overline{r}^{i}(s,a)|

(16)

and, therefore,
\pi
is
\varepsilon
-Nash equilibrium.

Proof.

Fix an agent
i
and consider an arbitrary Markov stationary strategy profile
\tilde{\mu}^{i}
in the effective game and the associated exploration-perturbed strategy profile
\tilde{\pi}
in the original game, i.e., we have
\tilde{\pi}^{i}(s)=(1-\epsilon)\tilde{\mu}^{i}(s)+\epsilon u^{i}
, where
u^{i}:=\frac{\mathbf{1}}{|\mathcal{A}^{i}|}
denotes the uniform distribution over
\mathcal{A}^{i}
for notational simplicity. By the construction of the effective expected reward and transitions (
12
) in the effective utility (
13
) and
\mathrm{NG}_{\epsilon}(\mu)=0
as assumed in the proposition statement, we have

U^{i}(\pi)=U_{\epsilon}^{i}(\mu)\geq U_{\epsilon}^{i}(\tilde{\mu}^{i},\mu^{-i})=U^{i}(\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}).

(17)

Given a Markov stationary strategy profile
\bar{\pi}
, a standard solution for the value of each state in the original stochastic game is given by

\mathbf{v}_{\bar{\pi}}^{i}=(1-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\bar{\pi}})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{\bar{\pi}}^{i},

(18)

where
\mathbf{v}_{\bar{\pi}}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
is the value vector,
\mathbf{P}_{\bar{\pi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|}
is the (stochastic) transition matrix, and
\mathbf{r}_{\bar{\pi}}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
is the reward vector. For example, the entry
\mathbf{P}_{\bar{\pi}}(s,s^{\prime})=\mathrm{E}_{a\sim\bar{\pi}(s)}[p(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)]
and
\mathbf{r}_{\bar{\pi}}^{i}(s)=\mathrm{E}_{a\sim\bar{\pi}(s)}[\overline{r}^{i}(s,a)]
. Correspondingly, we have the identity

\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}-\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}=(1-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}})^{-1}(\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}-\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}+\gamma(\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}})\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}).

Taking the infinity norm of both hand side and applying triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}-\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\;\|(1-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}})^{-1}\|\|\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}-\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\gamma\|(1-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}})^{-1}\|\|(\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}})\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}\|_{\infty}.

(19)

Since
\tilde{\pi}^{i}(s)=(1-\epsilon)\tilde{\mu}^{i}(s)+\epsilon u^{i}
, we have
\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}=(1-\epsilon)\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}+\epsilon\mathbf{r}_{u^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}=(1-\epsilon)\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}+\epsilon\mathbf{P}_{u^{i},\pi^{-i}}
. This yields that

\|\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}-\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}\|_{\infty}=\epsilon\|\mathbf{r}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}-\mathbf{r}_{u^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\epsilon\max_{(j,s,a)}|\overline{r}^{j}(s,a)|

(20)

and

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}})\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\|(\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}-\mathbf{P}_{u^{i},\pi^{-i}})\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}\|_{\infty}

(21)

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon\|\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{1-\gamma}\max_{(j,s,a)}|\overline{r}^{j}(s,a)|

(22)

as the induced norm
\|\mathbf{P}_{\pi}\|\leq 1
for all
\pi
since the associated transition matrices are stochastic matrices for any strategy profile. Furthermore, the Neumann series gives

\|(1-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}})^{-1}\|=\left\|\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}^{t}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

(23)

Plugging the bounds (
20
), (
22
), and (
23
) into (
19
), we obtain

\|\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i}}-\mathbf{v}_{\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\max_{(j,s,a)}|\overline{r}^{j}(s,a)|=\varepsilon.

(24)

By the definition of value and utility functions, (
24
) yields that
U^{i}(\tilde{\pi}^{i},\pi^{-i})\geq U^{i}(\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i})-\varepsilon
for all
\tilde{\mu}^{i}
. Thus, by (
17
), we can conclude that
U^{i}(\pi)\geq U^{i}(\tilde{\mu}^{i},\pi^{-i})-\varepsilon
for all
\tilde{\mu}^{i}
, which completes the proof.

\square

5.1
Proof for Two-agent Zero-sum Stochastic Games

The proof for the zero-sum games is relatively straight-forward based on
(

(
Leslie and Collins 2006

as

\mu_{k+1}^{i}(s)-(1-\alpha_{k})\cdot\mu_{k}^{i}(s)\in\alpha_{k}\cdot\mathrm{BR}_{k}(\mu_{k}^{-i}(s)),

(31)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{BR}_{k}(\mu_{k}^{-i}(s)):=\{\mu^{i}\in\Delta(\mathcal{A}^{i}):

\displaystyle\;(\mu^{i})^{T}\overline{Q}_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\mu_{k}^{-i}(s)\geq(\tilde{\mu}^{i})^{T}\overline{Q}_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\mu_{k}^{-i}(s)-z_{k}^{i}(s),

\displaystyle\forall\tilde{\mu}^{i}\in\Delta(\mathcal{A}^{i})\}

4.2
and
4.3

Since the stage games become zero-sum games asymptotically and (

\left|\mu_{k}^{i}(s)^{T}\overline{Q}_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\mu_{k}^{-i}(s)-\max_{\mu^{i}\in\Delta(\mathcal{A}^{i})}\min_{\mu^{-i}\in\Delta(\mathcal{A}^{-i})}(\mu^{i})^{T}\overline{Q}_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\mu^{-i}\right|\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\pi_{k}^{i}(s)^{T}Q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\pi_{k}^{-i}(s)

Proof for Multi-agent Identical-interest Stochastic Games

Recall the challenge that in identical-interest games (where

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}^{i}(s,a)=(1-\beta_{k})Y_{k}^{i}(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)(\hat{v}_{k+1}^{i}(s^{\prime})-\hat{v}_{k}^{i}(s^{\prime})).

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{k+1}^{i}(s)-\hat{v}_{k}^{i}(s)=

\displaystyle\;\beta_{k}\pi_{k}^{i}(s)^{T}Y_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\pi_{k}^{-i}(s)+\alpha_{k}(\hat{\pi}_{k}^{i}(s)-\pi_{k}^{i}(s))^{T}(q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)+\delta_{k}^{i}(s))

\displaystyle+\alpha_{k}(\hat{\pi}_{k}^{-i}(s)-\pi_{k}^{-i}(s))^{T}\big(q_{k}^{-i}(s,\cdot)+\delta_{k}^{-i}(s)+\Delta_{k}(s)\pi_{k}^{i}(s)\big)

as a
|\mathcal{A}^{i}|\times|\mathcal{A}^{-i}|

is due to
Section

4.2

In the policy update (

-greedy response in which each action

(\hat{\pi}_{k}^{i}(s)-\pi_{k}^{i}(s))^{T}q_{k}^{i}(s,\cdot)\geq 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\hat{\pi}_{k}^{-i}(s)-\pi_{k}^{-i}(s))^{T}q_{k}^{-i}(s,\cdot)\geq 0

Combining (

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}^{i}(s,a)\geq(1-\beta_{k})Y_{k}^{i}(s,a)

\displaystyle+\gamma\beta_{k}x_{k}^{i}+e_{k}^{i}(s,a)-z_{k+1}^{i}(s,a)+z_{k}^{i}(s,a),

where we define

(48a)

\displaystyle e_{k}^{i}(s,a):=\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}}p(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\Big(\alpha_{k}(\hat{\pi}_{k}^{i}(s^{\prime})-\pi_{k}^{i}(s^{\prime}))^{T}\delta_{k}^{i}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 43.36243pt+\alpha_{k}(\hat{\pi}_{k}^{-i}(s^{\prime})-\pi_{k}^{-i}(s^{\prime}))^{T}\big(\delta_{k}^{-i}(s^{\prime})+\Delta_{k}(s^{\prime})\pi_{k}^{i}(s^{\prime})\big)+O(\alpha_{k}^{2})\Big),

5.2.3
Limit Characterization

Since the global Q-values are almost surely convergent, let

denote the limit, i.e.,

almost surely for each

)
as

\mu_{k+1}^{i}(s)-(1-\alpha_{k})\cdot\mu_{k}^{i}(s)\in\alpha_{k}\cdot\overline{\mathrm{BR}}_{k}(\mu_{k}^{-i}(s)),

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{BR}}_{k}(\mu^{-i}):=\{\mu^{i}\in\Delta(\mathcal{A}^{i}):

\displaystyle\;(\mu^{i})^{T}\overline{Q}_{*}^{i}(s,\cdot)\mu^{-i}\geq(\tilde{\mu}^{i})^{T}\overline{Q}_{*}^{i}(s,\cdot)\mu^{-i}-\overline{z}_{k}^{i}(s),

7
Concluding Remarks

We introduced a new Actor-Dual-Critic learning framework for stochastic games. In this framework, inspired by dual-process cognition, agents maintain a fast critic for immediate payoff response and a slow critic for long-term value approximation. We proved that this independent and payoff-based learning dynamic converges to approximate Nash equilibria in both two-agent zero-sum and multi-agent identical-interest stochastic games over an infinite horizon. Our analysis further characterizes the approximation error in terms of the exploration rate. We further conducted numerical experiments demonstrating the effective convergence of the framework across both game classes. Future research could explore extending this dual-critic architecture to broader classes of general-sum games or integrating function approximation to handle large-scale state spaces.
