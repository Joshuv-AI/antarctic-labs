 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 The purpose of this project is to aggregate zoning and permitting data for utility-scale solar PV
 and battery storage projects.

 CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

 • Enhances the laboratory’s core competencies
 • Uses the laboratory’s core competencies

 Summary of Research Results:

 The purpose of this research project was to aggregate zoning and permitting data for utility-scale
 solar PV and battery energy storage systems. The ultimate goal of this collaboration is to lower
 solar and battery energy storage system soft costs by streamlining regulatory due diligence and
 reducing the burden of conducting feasibility assessments for solar and storage systems. The
 below sections describe the specific research completed by NLR (the contractor) in furtherance
 of the agreement with Vanox (the participant).

 Task 1:

 1.1 Contractor will compile data on solar and battery storage permitting and siting requirements
 for utility-scale projects into a query-able database.

 1.2 Contractor will research zoning ordinances and land use restrictions for solar and battery
 storage across state, municipal, and utility levels.

 1.3 Contractor will detail general permitting requirements for discretionary permits issued by
 state and municipal governments for solar and battery storage.

 2

 Task 1 Results:

 In furtherance of Task 1 NLR compiled data on solar permitting, siting, and zoning requirements
 at the state and municipal levels for Texas, California, Maryland, New Jersey, Pennsylvania,
 Ohio, Virginia and West Virginia. The utility-scale solar permitting data relied heavily on NLR’s
 existing Continuous Ordinance Mapping for Siting and Planning Systems (COMPASS) Large
 Language Model (LLM)-based data extraction method. Data collected using COMPASS
 included:

 • Setbacks (structures, property lines, roads, railroads, transmission lines, and water
 bodies)
 • Noise limits
 • Structure height
 • Minimum and maximum lot size
 • Density limits
 • Prohibitions (bans, moratoria)
 • Maximum project size (in MW)
 • Maximum lot coverage (in acres)
 • Decommissioning requirements
 • Visual impact assessment requirements
 • Glare restrictions
 • Permitted use districts
 • Primary/secondary use districts
 • Ordinance enactment date

 Figure 1. COMPASS Ordinance Retrieval Process

 After leveraging COMPASS to extract the data for each state, NLR manually researched and
 compiled additional information on eligible zoning districts, including applicable definitions and
 links to zoning maps. NLR identified 204 county and/or municipal jurisdictions with
 requirements across the eight states (see Table 1). The data was provided to Vanox in a machine-
 readable and query-able Excel database.

 NLR had not previously developed COMPASS for battery energy storage systems and therefore,
 battery energy storage system permitting and siting information was manually collected for
 Texas only based on a review of Authorities Having Jurisdiction (AHJs) that NLR had
 previously identified via COMPASS as having solar and/or wind ordinances. In addition, NLR
 completed a desktop review using commercial search engines to identify additional battery
 energy storage system permitting and siting information in Texas. NLR identified three
 municipal requirements in Texas. The data was provided to Vanox in a machine-readable and
 query-able Excel database.

 NLR also completed research on general permitting requirements for discretionary permits
 issued by Texas at a state-level and provided a summary memo to Vanox. NLR did not have the
 resources under the agreement to extend this research to other states, as Texas was determined to
 be the primary state of interest.

 Vanox, through its portfolio company Distill Energy, initially incorporated NLR’s state and
 municipal level solar ordinance data into its forecasting models to sharpen predictions of utility-
 scale solar buildout across the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT). By ingesting
 zoning restrictions, setback requirements, permitting timelines, and local incentive structures,
 Distill will enable customers to anticipate where new solar capacity is most likely to emerge, and
 where regulatory barriers would constrain it. This granular, location-specific ordinance layer will
 enhance Distill's probabilistic forecasts by capturing a key driver of supply-side uncertainty that
 traditional models often overlook.

 Task 2:

 Utilizing data from task 1, the Contractor will support the Participant to validate and improve the
 accuracy of solar regulatory data retrieved through Large Language Models through techniques
 like prompt engineering.

 Task 2 Results:

 NLR and the participant did not complete any joint validation of LLM produced data under this
 Task. Instead, NLR worked at improving LLM extraction techniques and validation of the
 produced data using COMPASS. NLR provided Vanox with a validation synthesis and the
 COMPASS source code to allow the participant to replicate the NLR process and attempt to
 improve accuracy of solar regulatory data retrieval. The COMPASS source code is available at:
 https://github.com/NatLabRockies/COMPASS.
