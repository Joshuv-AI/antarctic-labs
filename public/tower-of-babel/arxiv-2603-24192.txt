𝚪 {\bf\Gamma} -convergence of convolution-type functionals for free discontinuity problems
Giuseppe C. Brusca, Davide Donati, Sergio Scalabrino, Chiara Trifone, Edoardo Voglino

Abstract
. We prove compactness with respect to Γ \Gamma -convergence for a general class of non-local energies modelled after the ones considered in [Gobbino, CPAM (1998)]. We give an integral representation result for the limits, which are free discontinuity functionals defined on the space of generalised special functions of bounded variation. We then characterise the bulk and surface energy densities of the obtained limits by means of minimisation problems on small cubes for the approximating energies. MSC codes: 49J45, 49Q20, 65K10 Keywords: Free discontinuity problems, Γ \Gamma -convergence, non-local functionals



1.
Introduction

The necessity of treating numerically
minimisation problems related to free discontinuity functionals has stimulated the development of a wide literature of
approximation results, which offer insights on the structure of minimisers to such problems. Indeed, although general compactness and lower semicontinuity theorems due to Ambrosio
[
7
,
10
]
(see also
[
38
,
39
]
) allow to prove the existence of solutions to minimisation problems, very little can be inferred on the properties of the obtained minimisers relying on these arguments alone.
In this setting, the prototypical example is the
Mumford-Shah
functional, which for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
open and bounded and for
u\in{\rm GSBV}(\Omega)
(see
[
37
]
and
[
8
, Chapter 4]
) takes the form

(1.1)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}({J_{u}}).

Here,
\nabla u
is the
approximate gradient
of
u
and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})
denotes the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the
jump set

J_{u}
.

A natural approach is to approximate, in a variational sense, the functionals under consideration. This can be achieved in a remarkably wide variety of fashions, all sharing a common feature: the replacement of the surface part of the energy, which is in general very hard to treat, by means of a computationally more favourable expression.

A first result in this direction was obtained in
[
9
]
by Ambrosio and Tortorelli, who proposed to introduce an additional phase-field variable to approximate (
1.1
) by means of elliptic energies. This result was later employed by Bellettini and Coscia
[
13
]
to provide a discrete approximation of (
1.1
), obtained by replacing the elliptic functionals considered in
[
9
]
with discrete sums.
Other approximations involving discretisation procedures have then been considered, for instance, by Chambolle
[
27
]
and Braides and Gelli
[
21
,
20
]
. A different strategy was adopted by Alicandro, Braides, and Gelli
[
4
]
(see also
[
1
]
) and more recently by Solci
[
48
]
, who used singular perturbations of integer order
k\geq 2
of non-convex energies to approximate

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}}|[u]|^{1/k}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where
[u]:=u^{+}-u^{-}
is the difference of the unilateral traces
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
of
u
on
J_{u}
.

Several non-local approximations of the Mumford-Shah functional have been proposed. In
[
16
]
Braides and Dal Maso considered functionals of the form

(1.2)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}f\Bigl(\varepsilon\fint_{\Omega\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x)}|\nabla u(y)|^{2}\,{\rm d}y\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x,\qquad u\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where
f
is a non-negative increasing continuous function such that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(t)}{t}=a,\qquad\lim_{t\to+\infty}f(t)=b

for some
a,b>0
; see also
[
19
,
30
,
29
,
42
,
43
]
for generalisations to the
{\rm BV}
setting and
[
45
,
44
,
47
]
for related works in the setting of functions of
bounded deformation
.

A different approach was pursued by Gobbino, who proved that (
1.1
) could be approximated by sequences of convolution-type functionals depending on difference quotients, as conjectured by De Giorgi in 1996. More precisely, in
[
41
]
the author proves that, up to multiplicative constants and assuming some regularity on
\Omega
, the sequence of functionals defined by

(1.3)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+1}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\arctan\Big(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{\varepsilon}\Big)e^{-|\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y,\qquad u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

\Gamma
-converges as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
in the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
topology to the Mumford-Shah functional. This is obtained by a careful analysis of the one-dimensional case, in which the convolution kernel does not appear and (
1.3
) takes a simpler form. Such result is then extended to the
d
-dimensional case using slicing techniques. In higher dimensions, the convolution kernel is introduced in order to avoid the anisotropies that one would obtain if only increments with respect to the coordinate axes were considered. No particular role is played by the specific choice of the integrand and of the convolution kernel appearing in (
1.3
), and the arguments of
[
41
]
are suitable also to treat functionals given by

(1.4)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\min\Big\{\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big\}\rho\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y,

for
\rho\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
any smooth kernel with compact support (see
[
41
, Section 7]
and
[
22
, Chapter 5]
). This result was later adapted by Chambolle
[
28
]
, who proved a discrete version of De Giorgi’s conjecture.

Gobbino and Mora in
[
40
]
further discussed a variant of
(
1.3
) in which more general convex-concave integrands
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
are considered and slightly different scaling conditions are taken into account.
The choice of the integrands
\{\varphi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
determines the expression of a free discontinuity functional appearing in the limit, which is of the form

(1.5)

\int_{\Omega}f_{\rm bulk}(|\nabla u|)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}}f_{\rm surf}(|[u]|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

for suitable integrands
f_{\rm bulk}
and
f_{\rm surf}
, with
f_{\rm surf}
that may also be superlinear near
0
.
Moreover, the authors prove that, for specific choices of
f_{\rm bulk}
and
f_{\rm surf}
, the functional (
1.5
) can be approximated via non-local energies associated to appropriately chosen integrands
\{\varphi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
, for which they provide an explicit expression.

A further variant of Gobbino’s result was recently considered in
[
6
]
by Almi, Davoli, Kubin, and Tasso, who studied a vectorial analogue of (
1.3
) and showed its
\Gamma
-convergence to the Griffith functional, whose domain is a subspace of
{\rm GSBD}(\Omega)
(see
[
35
, Definition 4.2]
) and whose expression can be formally obtained by replacing in (
1.1
) the approximate gradient
\nabla u
with the approximate symmetric gradient
\mathcal{E}u
.

The aim of this paper is to prove
\Gamma
-compactness and integral representation results for a general class of non-local functionals modelled after (
1.4
).
We study functionals defined for
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
by

(1.6)

F_{\varepsilon}(u):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}\Big(x,\frac{y-x}{\varepsilon},\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y,

where the integrands

f_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfy suitable assumptions and growth conditions making the resulting energies comparable with (
1.4
) (see Sections
3
and
2
).
To some extent, our energies may be regarded as a continuum counterpart of those considered in
[
12
]
(see also the related work
[
3
]
), where the authors detect sufficient conditions implying the
\Gamma
-convergence of certain discrete energies to free discontinuity functionals as in (
1.7
) below.

The first main result we obtain is Theorem
3.3
, which states that, given a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
converging to
0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
, the sequence
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
given by (
1.6
) admits a subsequence that
\Gamma
-converges in the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
-topology to a functional
F
, which for
u\in{\rm GSBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
takes the form

(1.7)

F(u)=\int_{\Omega}f_{\rm bulk}(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}}f_{\rm surf}(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where the integrands
f_{\rm bulk}
and
f_{\rm surf}
are determined by auxiliary minimisation problems on small cubes.

In Section
5
we use the localisation method for
\Gamma
-convergence (see
[
24
, Section 3.3]
or
[
34
, Chapter 18]
) to prove
\Gamma
-compactness. The starting point of this technique is introducing the set functions
U\mapsto F_{\varepsilon}(u,U)
, defined on the class of open subsets of
\Omega

by a suitable localisation of the functionals (
1.6
). The second step requires to produce a non-local version of the so called
fundamental estimate
(see
[
34
, Chapter 18]
), whose proof, in our case, crucially relies on an argument, borrowed from
[
2
]
and developed in Section
4
, that allows us to consider only finite-range interactions in (
1.6
). Finally, exploiting the fundamental estimate we prove that, if
U
is sufficiently regular, up to subsequences
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\cdot,U)\}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
to
a certain
F(\cdot,U)
and that this limit functional can be extended to a Borel measure in the set variable. We point out that if
U
is not regular, the
\Gamma
-convergence may fail also for the reference functionals (
1.4
) (see
[
41
, Remark 7.1]
).

We will then exploit in Section
6
the integral representation on
{\rm SBV}^{2}
of Bouchitté, Fonseca, Leoni, and Mascarenhas
[
14
]
.
However, we cannot directly apply the results of
[
14
]
to the limit functional
F
. Indeed, those results require in particular the existence of a constant
C>0
such that

C\Bigl(\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}\!\!\!\!\!(1+|[u]|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\Bigr)\leq F(u,U)

for every
U\subseteq\Omega
open and
u\in{\rm SBV^{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, while our growth conditions only yield the weaker estimate

C\Bigl(\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap U)\Bigr)\leq F(u,U).

To deal with this problem, we resort to well-known truncation techniques employed, for instance, in
[
25
]
and
[
33
]
. We consider the perturbations
F^{\delta}(u):=F(u)+\delta\|[u]\|_{L^{1}(J_{u};\mathbb{R}^{m})}
for
\delta>0,
which satisfy the required lower bound, and therefore admit an integral representation, and then let
\delta\to 0^{+}
recovering equality (
1.7
) for all
u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Finally, arguing by density, we obtain the desired integral representation (
1.7
) for all functions
u
in

{\rm GSBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

We mention that a general integral representation result for functionals on
{\rm GSBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
has recently been established by Crismale, Friedrich, and Solombrino in
[
31
]
, but also in this instance, requiring growth conditions not suitable to a direct application in our setting.

In Section
7
we analyse the asymptotic behaviour of the minimum values of Dirichlet problems for
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
and compare them with the minimum values of the corresponding problems for the
\Gamma
-limit
F
. Combining this analysis with the compactness and integral representation results described above, we prove our second main result in Theorem
3.4
, which provides necessary and sufficient conditions for
\Gamma
-convergence of sequences of functionals of the form (
1.6
). These conditions are expressed as the existence, independently of the sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
, of limits of infima of minimisation problems for
F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\cdot,Q)
on small cubes
Q
(see (
7.16
) and (
7.17
)).

This kind of condition characterising
\Gamma
-convergence is particularly useful when dealing with the homogenisation of free discontinuity functionals (see, for instance, the works
[
17
,
25
,
26
]
in the local setting). In our non-local framework, a simple example may be given by

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\min\Big\{\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big\}\rho\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y,

where
a\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,+\infty)
is
1
-periodic and bounded.
It may be interesting to study this problem, which in the Sobolev setting has already been addressed in
[
2
]
.

Our work can be compared with a previous paper by Cortesani
[
30
]
, who dealt with energies modelled after (
1.2
). The arguments presented therein are flexible enough to infer that a “generalised fundamental estimate” also holds for non-local energies as (
1.4
), upon assuming that the interaction kernel
\rho
is bounded, compactly supported, and lower semicontinuous. Then, the localisation method of
\Gamma
-convergence is employed in the
L^{1}
-topology. In this work, we pursue an approach more similar to that presented in
[
2
]
, which allows us to encompass a wider class of admissible kernels and more general growth conditions, performing the asymptotic analysis with respect to the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
-convergence.

Finally, we remark that, in recent years, there has been a growing interest in non-local approximations of local functionals. Starting from the seminal work of Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu
[
15
]
on the approximation of the Dirichlet energy, several significant results of similar fashion have been obtained in the setting of functions of bounded variation and deformation. In particular, shortly after the publication of
[
15
]
, Dávila has addressed in
[
36
]
the problem of approximating the total variation of the distributional derivative
Du
of a function
u\in{\rm BV}(\Omega)
, and a similar problem has been considered by Arroyo-Rabasa and Bonicatto
[
11
]
for the symmetric part of the derivative of vector fields of bounded deformation. A remarkable example of non-local approximation that fits in the above-mentioned framework is the one obtained using Gagliardo-seminorms, which are introduced via fractional kernels. We point out that such class of kernels is not taken into account in our analysis. In this vein, in a more related recent work
[
5
]
, the authors investigate the
\Gamma
-convergence of functionals defined on partitions through Riesz-type fractional variations.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

In this section we fix the notation and introduce some preliminary results.

Notation.
For fixed positive integers
d
and
m
we let
|\cdot|
denote the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
k\in\{d,m,m\times d\}
. Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, we let
B_{r}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y-x|<r\}
denote the open ball of center
x
and radius
r
, the dependence on the center
x
being omitted if
x=0
. The canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\{e_{1},...,e_{d}\}
and the
i
-th component of a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to this basis is denoted by
x_{i}
. The unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|=1\}
and we let
SO(m)
denote the group of rotations of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\varepsilon>0
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we also introduce the notation

E_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\{x\in E:x+\varepsilon\xi\in E\}.

We let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. The collection of all open subsets of
\Omega
is denoted by
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
, while we let
\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
denote the collection of all open subsets of
\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary. We let
\mathcal{L}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
denote the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure and the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.

Jump set.
Given a measurable function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, a measurable set
E\subset\Omega
, and a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(2.1)

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{r}(x)\cap E)}{r^{d}}>0,

we say that
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the approximate limit in
E
of
u
at
x
, in symbols

\text{ap}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\to x\\
y\in E\end{subarray}}u(y)=a,

if for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{y\in E\cap B_{r}(x):|u(y)-a|>\varepsilon\})}{r^{d}}=0.

It follows from (
2.1
) that, if the approximate limit exists, it is unique.

Given a function
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the
jump set

J_{u}
of
u
is a Borel set made up of all points
x\in\Omega
for which there exists a triple
(u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x),\nu_{u}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that, setting

H^{\pm}(x):=\{y\in\Omega:\pm y\cdot\nu_{u}(x)\geq 0\},

we have

\text{ap}\!\!\!\!\!\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\to x\\
y\in H^{\pm}(x)\end{subarray}}u(y)=u^{\pm}(x).

The triple
(u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x),\nu_{u}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is uniquely defined up to changing the roles of
u^{+}(x)
and
u^{-}(x)
and the sign of
\nu_{u}(x)
. Having fixed
\nu_{u}\colon J_{u}\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we set
[u](x):=u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x)
for every
x\in J_{u}
.

Functions of (generalised) bounded variation.

We here recall the main properties of the space
{\rm BV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
of
functions of bounded variation
. The interested reader may find a proof of all the results stated below in
[
8
,
7
]
. A function
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is said to be a function of bounded variation if
u\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and its distributional derivative
Du
is a bounded Radon measure with values in
\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
.

The measure
Du
admits a decomposition as the sum of three mutually singular measures

Du=\nabla u\mathcal{L}^{d}+D^{c}u+([u]\otimes\nu_{u})\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u},

where

(i)

\nabla u\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})
denotes the
approximate gradient
of
u
,

(ii)

D^{c}u
, called the
Cantor part
of
Du
, is a measure singular with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
that vanishes on every Borel set
B\subseteq\Omega
which is
\sigma
-finite with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
,

(iii)

[u]\otimes\nu_{u}
denotes the tensor product between
[u]
and
\nu_{u}
.

The space of
special functions of bounded variation
{\rm SBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, is the subspace of functions
u\in{\rm BV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
D^{c}u=0
. We will often work with its subspace

{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\Big\{u\in{\rm SBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})<+\infty\Big\}.

We now recall the definition of
{\rm GSBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
-functions introduced by De Giorgi and Ambrosio in
[
37
]
and that constitute the natural domain where to set the study of minimisation problems related to the Mumford-Shah functional.

{\rm GSBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the space of all
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-measurable functions
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\Phi\circ u\in{\rm SBV}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
for every
C^{1}
function
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
whose gradient has compact support, where we have set

{\rm SBV}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in{\rm SBV}(\Omega^{\prime};\mathbb{R}^{m})\text{ for all }\Omega^{\prime}\text{ open},\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega\}.

When
m=1
, the space
{\rm GSBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
can also be characterised by means of
{\it rough}
vertical truncations. More precisely,
u\in{\rm GSBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
if and only if the function
u^{M}:=(u\lor-M)\land M
belongs to
{\rm SBV_{loc}}(\Omega)
, where we have set
s\lor t:=\max\{s,t\}
, and
s\land t:=\min\{s,t\}
for every
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

We recall that every function
u\in{\rm GSBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
admits an approximate gradient
\nabla u
, i.e., an
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-measurable function
\nabla u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
such that

\text{ap}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\to x\\
y\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\frac{u(y)-u(x)-\nabla u(x)(y-x)}{|y-x|}=0

for
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
.

Finally, we introduce the subspace

\displaystyle{\rm GSBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\Big\{u\in{\rm GSBV}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})<+\infty\Big\},

and also set

{\rm SBV}^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in{\rm SBV}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})<+\infty\}.

For the sake of notation, in all the previous functional spaces we omit the target space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
when
m=1
.

In the following lemma, whose proof can be found in
[
32
, Proposition 2.3]
, we observe that, even in the vector-valued case
m>1
, the space
{\rm GSBV^{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
can be characterised by means of
rough
vertical truncations.

Lemma 2.1
.

{\rm GSBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a vector space. Moreover, if
u\in{\rm GSBV^{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
then
u_{i}\in{\rm GSBV^{2}}(\Omega)
for all
i\in\{1,...,m\}
. In particular, for all
M>0
, if
u^{M}
denotes the function whose
i
-th component is given by
u^{M}_{i}:=(u_{i}\lor-M)\land M
, then
u^{M}\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Justified by the previous lemma, for every
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
M>0
,
u^{M}
will always denote the truncated function whose
i
-th component is given by
u^{M}_{i}:=(u_{i}\lor-M)\land M
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
u\in{\rm GSBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
One can check that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\nabla u^{M}=\nabla u
for
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
such that
|u(x)|\leq M
so that
\nabla u^{M}
converges
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. in
\Omega
(and in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
) to
\nabla u
as
M\to+\infty
, and
|\nabla u^{M}|
converges monotonically to
|\nabla u|
;

(ii)

for every
M>0
the jump set
J_{u^{M}}
is contained in
J_{u}
, up to a
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-negligible set, and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\setminus\bigcup_{M>0}J_{u^{M}})=0
.
Moreover, for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
x\in J_{u^{M}}
, we have
(u^{M}(x))^{\pm}=(u^{\pm}(x))^{M}
;

(iii)

the map
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{M}
is
1
-Lipschitz,
(\zeta^{M+1})^{M}=\zeta^{M}
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
M>0
; hence,
|[u^{M}]|=|[(u^{M+1})^{M}]|\leq|[u^{M+1}]|
for all
M>0
and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e. in
J_{u^{M}}
.

\mathbf{\Gamma}
-convergence.

We recall the definition of
\Gamma
-convergence in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
. For a thorough introduction to the general theory of
\Gamma
-convergence we refer the reader to
[
23
,
34
]
.

Given a positive sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
tending to
0
and functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\to[-\infty,+\infty]
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\to[-\infty,+\infty]
, we say that
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is the
\Gamma
-limit with respect to the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
-convergence of the sequence
\{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
n\to+\infty
, it holds that

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\geq\mathcal{F}_{0}(u);

(ii)

for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
there exists
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
such that
v_{n}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
n\to+\infty
and

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n})\leq\mathcal{F}_{0}(u).

In such case, we write

\mathcal{F}_{0}=\underset{n\to+\infty}{\Gamma\text{-}\lim}\,\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}.

We recall that the functionals
\Gamma
-
\liminf
and
\Gamma
-
\limsup
are respectively defined as follows:

(2.2)

\begin{gathered}\Gamma\text{-}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u):=\inf\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n}):u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\};\\
\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u):=\inf\{\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n}):u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\}.\end{gathered}

Both functionals in (
2.2
) are lower semicontinuous with respect to the convergence in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, they coincide if and only if
\Gamma
-
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
exists, and, in this case, the
\Gamma
-limit coincides with their common value.

Given a family of functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\to[-\infty,+\infty]
for
\varepsilon>0
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\to[-\infty,+\infty]
, we say that
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is the
\Gamma
-limit with respect to the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
-convergence of the family
\{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
if, for any positive sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
tending to
0
, it holds

\mathcal{F}_{0}=\underset{n\to+\infty}{\Gamma\text{-}\lim}\,\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}};

and we write

\mathcal{F}_{0}=\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}.

We point out that, throughout the rest of the work,
\Gamma
-convergence shall always be meant with respect to the convergence in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Reference functionals.

We now introduce some families of non-local functionals that constitute upper and lower bounds for the general family of energies that we mean to study.

For
i\in\{1,2\}
we let
\rho_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty]
be a radially symmetric kernel; in particular, we suppose that

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|\xi|+|\xi|^{2})\rho_{i}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi<+\infty,

(2.4)

\rho_{1}(\xi)\geq c_{0}\qquad\text{for }\mathcal{L}^{d}\text{-a.e.\ }\xi\in B_{r_{0}}

for some positive constants
c_{0},r_{0}
. We let
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be a family of non-negative radially symmetric kernels such that

(2.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi=1\quad\text{ for every }\varepsilon>0,

for every
\delta>0
there exists
r_{\delta}>0
such that

(2.6)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{\delta}}}|\xi|\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi<\delta,

and

(2.7)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi<+\infty.

For
\varepsilon>0
,
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
, and
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we define the functionals

G_{i,\varepsilon}(u,U):=\int_{U\times U}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\rho_{i}\Bigl(\frac{y-x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\min\Bigl\{\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Bigr\}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\qquad\text{for }i\in\{1,2\},

and

P_{\varepsilon}(u,U):=\int_{U\times U}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{y-x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y.

Recalling that
U_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\{x\in U:x+\varepsilon\xi\in U\}
, we perform the change of variables
\xi:=(y-x)/\varepsilon
to obtain

G_{i,\varepsilon}(u,U)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{i}(\xi)\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}g_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi\qquad\text{for }i\in\{1,2\},

where we set

(2.8)

g_{\varepsilon}(z):=\min\Big\{|z|^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big\}\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

and

P_{\varepsilon}(u,U)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\frac{|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi.

Conditions (
2.3
)-(
2.6
) play distinct roles in our analysis. On the one hand, inequalities (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) guarantee that the
\Gamma
-limits of
\{G_{i,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon},i\in\{1,2\}
, and
\{P_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
are finite. On the other, (
2.4
) is useful in order to obtain suitable coerciveness conditions in
{\rm GSBV}^{2}
. Assumption (
2.6
) corresponds to the assumption (H2) introduced in
[
2
]
and ensures that short-range interactions are favoured when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
; this condition is in fact stronger than the analogous hypothesis appearing in
[
36
,
46
]
, which reads as

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{\frac{r}{\varepsilon}}}|\xi|\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi=0\quad\text{ for every }r>0.

We mention some useful results concerning the above functionals. For
i\in\{1,2\}
and
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
, we introduce
the Mumford-Shah functional

{\rm MS}_{i}(u,U):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\mu_{i}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap U)&\text{ if }u\in L^{1}_{\text{\rm loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap{\rm GSBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\setminus{\rm GSBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{i}
and
\mu_{i}
are two positive constants given by

(2.9)

\lambda_{i}:=\frac{1}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2}\rho_{i}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi,\qquad\quad\mu_{i}:=2\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|\rho_{i}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi,

and we introduce (a multiple of) the total variation

{\rm TV}_{\kappa}(u,U):=\kappa|Du|(U)\qquad\text{if }u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap{\rm BV}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m}),

where the constant
\kappa
is given by

(2.10)

\kappa:=\Bigl(\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|e_{1}\cdot\sigma|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\sigma)\Bigr).

Finally, we introduce the reference functionals

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(u,U):=G_{2,\varepsilon}(u,U)+P_{\varepsilon}(u,U)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),

\displaystyle H(u,U):={\rm MS}_{2}(u,U)+{\rm TV}_{\kappa}(u,U)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap{\rm BV}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m}).

We summarize in the next theorem the needed auxiliary results concerning the scalar case
m=1
, which is the setting of the works of Ponce
[
46
]
, Dávila
[

]
, and Gobbino

.

.

Let
U\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)

Then the following two conditions hold:

(i)

3.
Setting of the Problem and Main results

In this section we introduce the functionals that are going to be our main object of investigation and state the main results of the paper. We recall that
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are radially symmetric non-negative kernels satisfying (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), that the family of radially symmetric non-negative kernels
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
2.5
)-(
2.7
) and that, for all
\varepsilon>0,
the functions
g_{\varepsilon}
are those introduced in (
2.8
).

Setting of the problem.
We fix a family of non-negative functions
\{\eta_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
such that for every
\delta>0
there exists
r_{\delta}>0
such that

(3.1)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{\delta}}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi<\delta,

and

(3.2)

\Lambda:=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<+\infty.

For every
\varepsilon>0
we consider an integrand
f_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

(
measurability
)
f_{\varepsilon}
is Borel measurable;

(ii)

(
growth conditions
) for almost every
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
it holds

(3.3)

\rho_{1}(\xi)g_{\varepsilon}(z)\leq f_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi,z)\leq\rho_{2}(\xi)g_{\varepsilon}(z)+\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|z|+\eta_{\varepsilon}(\xi);

(iii)

(
monotonicity
) for almost every
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
|z_{1}|\leq|z_{2}|
it holds

(3.4)

f_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi,z_{1})\leq f_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi,z_{2}).

We study functionals

(3.5)

F_{\varepsilon}(u,U):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}f_{\varepsilon}\Big(x,\xi,\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi,

where
\varepsilon>0
and
(u,U)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Thanks to the upper and lower bounds in (
3.3
), it follows immediately that

(3.6)

G_{1,\varepsilon}(u,U)\leq F_{\varepsilon}(u,U)\leq H_{\varepsilon}(u,U)+\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|U|.

We also note that the presence of the term
P_{\varepsilon}
in the upper bound allows us to treat limit energies that, contrary to the Mumford-Shah functional, admit linear growth with respect to the size of the jump
|[u]|
.

Remark 3.2
.

Hypothesis (iii) above was already considered in the discrete setting in
[
12
]
.
It will be repeatedly used throughout the work to allow us to restrict our attention to functions in
L^{\infty}
.
More in general, given
(u,U)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and a
1
-Lipschitz function
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, it follows immediately from (
3.4
) that

(3.7)

F_{\varepsilon}(\Phi\circ u,U)\leq F_{\varepsilon}(u,U).

As a consequence, we obtain that

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u+a,U)

\displaystyle=F_{\varepsilon}(u,U)\quad\text{ for every }a\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(Ru,U)

\displaystyle=F_{\varepsilon}(u,U)\quad\text{ for every }R\in SO(m),

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u^{M},U)

\displaystyle\leq F_{\varepsilon}(u,U)\quad\text{ for every }M>0.

We now present the main results of the paper. To this aim, we first introduce some further notation.
Given a functional
\mathcal{F}\colon{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
, a function
w\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and
U\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
we set

(3.8)

\mathfrak{m}^{\mathcal{F}}(w,U):=\inf\{\mathcal{F}(u,U):\,u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})\text{ with }u=w\text{ in a neighbourhood of }\partial U\}.

Given
L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, with a slight abuse of notation, we also let
L
denote the corresponding linear map
L:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we introduce the function
u_{x,\zeta,\nu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

u_{x,\zeta,\nu}(y):=\begin{cases}\zeta&\text{ if }(y-x)\cdot\nu\geq 0,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We let the symbol
Q^{\nu}(x,r)
denote an open cube of side length
r
and centre
x
with two faces orthogonal to
\nu
, and we assume that
Q^{\nu}(x,r)=Q^{-\nu}(x,r)
. We also set
Q(x,r):=x+(-\frac{r}{2},\frac{r}{2})^{d}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be as in (
3.5
), and let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
be a positive sequence converging to
0
as
n\to+\infty
. Then, there exists a subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
and a functional
F\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
such that

\underset{n\to+\infty}{\Gamma\text{-}\lim}\,F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u,U)=F(u,U)

for every
(u,U)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
. In particular,

F(u,U)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{U}\!\!f_{{\rm bulk}}(x,\nabla u){\rm d}x+\!\!\int_{J_{u}\cap U}\!\!\!\!\!f_{{\rm surf}}(x,[u],\nu_{u}){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\!\!&\text{if }u\in L^{1}_{\text{\rm loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}){\cap}\,{\rm GSBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\\
+\infty\!\!&\text{if }u\in L^{1}_{\text{\rm loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}){\setminus}{\rm GSBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m}).\end{cases}

for every
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and
u\in{\rm GSBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
where

(3.9)

\displaystyle f_{{\rm bulk}}(x,L):=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(L,Q(x,r))}{r^{d}}\quad\text{for all $x\in\Omega$ and $L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$},

(3.10)

\displaystyle f_{{\rm surf}}(x,\zeta,\nu):=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q^{\nu}(x,r))}{r^{d-1}}\quad\text{ for all $x\in\Omega$, $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$, and $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$.}

Letting
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
as in (
2.9
),
\kappa
as in (
2.10
),
c_{1},c_{2}
as in Remark
2.4
, and
\Lambda
as in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle c_{1}\lambda_{1}|L|^{2}\leq f_{\rm bulk}(x,L)\leq c_{2}(\lambda_{2}|L|^{2}+\kappa|L|)+\Lambda\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all $x\in\Omega$ and $L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$},

\displaystyle c_{1}\mu_{1}\leq f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq c_{2}(\mu_{2}+\kappa|\zeta|)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all $x\in\Omega$, $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$, and $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$}.

Moreover, for all
x\in\Omega
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have

\displaystyle f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{1})\leq f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{2})\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if $|L_{1}|\leq|L_{2}|$},

\displaystyle f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{1},\nu)\leq f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{2},\nu)\quad

if
|\zeta_{1}|\leq|\zeta_{2}|
,

so that

\displaystyle f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{1})=f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{2})\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if $|L_{1}|=|L_{2}|$},

\displaystyle f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{1},\nu)=f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{2},\nu)\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if $|\zeta_{1}|=|\zeta_{2}|$},

\displaystyle f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)=f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,-\nu)\quad

\displaystyle\text{ for all $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$}.

Proof.

It is sufficient to combine Propositions
5.5
and
6.1
.
∎

We now introduce the minimisation problems for the non-local functionals we are interested in.
Given
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
,
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and
s>0
, we set

\mathcal{D}_{s,w}(U):=\big\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):\,u=w\text{ for $\mathcal{L}^{d}$-a.e.\ $x\in U$ with dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U)<s\big\},

and consider the minimisation problems

\mathfrak{m}_{s}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(w,U):=\inf\big\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u,U):u\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon_{n}s,w}(U)\big\}.

We observe that for every
0<s_{1}<s_{2}
we clearly have the inclusion
\mathcal{D}_{s_{2},w}(U)\subset\mathcal{D}_{s_{1},w}(U)
, which implies

\mathfrak{m}_{s_{1}}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(w,U)\leq\mathfrak{m}_{s_{2}}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(w,U).

For every
x\in\Omega
,
L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

Lemma 4.1

and Fubini’s Theorem, it follows

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{j_{0}}}

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{v(x_{0}+\varepsilon\xi)-v(x_{0})}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\widetilde{\varepsilon}}\right)^{d}k^{p-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}^{j_{\ell-1}}}a_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{v(x_{\ell-1}+\varepsilon\xi^{\prime})-v(x_{\ell-1})}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x_{\ell-1}\,{\rm d}\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{d+3}}{r}\Bigr)^{d}k^{p-1}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{S_{\varepsilon}(j_{0})}a_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{v(x+\varepsilon\xi^{\prime})-v(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi^{\prime},

where
S_{\varepsilon}(j_{0}):=\cup_{\ell=0}^{k-1}Q_{\varepsilon}^{j_{\ell}}
and we note that
S_{\varepsilon}(j_{0})\subset V_{\varepsilon,\xi}
for every
j_{0}\in\mathcal{I_{\varepsilon}}
.

Since the sets
\{S_{\varepsilon}(j_{0}):j_{0}\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\}
overlap at most
2k-1
times, summing over the indices
j_{0}\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
and recalling that
k=\lceil\varepsilon|\xi|/\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,\rceil
, we get

\int_{V}a_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{v(x+\varepsilon\xi)-v(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x\leq C(|\xi|^{p}+1)A_{\varepsilon}^{r}(v,V_{\varepsilon,\xi})

and the proof is concluded.
∎

Combining Lemmas
4.3
and
4.5
we obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
and let
r>0
. There exist two positive constants
C_{3}=C_{3}(r,U)
and
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(r,U)
such that

\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}a_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr){\rm d}x\leq C_{3}(|\xi|^{p}+1)(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})}+A_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U))

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
u\in L^{p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and
\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{U},r_{1}
, and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
be as in the statement of Theorem
4.3
and, in light of Remark
4.4
, suppose that
\text{co}(U)\subset\subset\widetilde{U}
, where we let co
(U)
denote the closed convex hull of
U
, and let

\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(r,U):=\min\Bigl\{\varepsilon^{\prime},\frac{\text{dist}(\text{co}(U),\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\widetilde{U})}{r_{1}}\Bigr\}>0.

Since we have that

U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)+(0,\varepsilon)\xi\subseteq\text{co}(U)

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that

\varepsilon r_{1}<\text{dist}(U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)+(0,\varepsilon)\xi,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\widetilde{U})

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
. We get that the set
(U_{\varepsilon}(\xi))_{\varepsilon,\xi}=U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)+(0,\varepsilon)\xi+B_{\varepsilon r_{1}}
is contained in
\widetilde{U}
; and then, applying Lemma
4.5
with

r=r_{1},\quad W=\widetilde{U},\quad V=U_{\varepsilon}(\xi),\quad v=Eu,

and Theorem
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}a_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(|\xi|^{p}+1)A_{\varepsilon}^{r_{1}}(Eu,(U_{\varepsilon}(\xi))_{\varepsilon,\xi})

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(|\xi|^{p}+1)A_{\varepsilon}^{r_{1}}(Eu,\widetilde{U})

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}(|\xi|^{p}+1)(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})}+A_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U)),

which yields the thesis.
∎

We introduce further reference functionals. For every
\varepsilon,r>0
and
(u,U)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we set

\displaystyle G^{r}_{\varepsilon}(u,U)

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\min\Bigl\{\Bigl|\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Big|^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Bigr\}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi

(4.17)

\displaystyle\hphantom{:}=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}g_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr){\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi,

and

P^{r}_{\varepsilon}(u,U):=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\Bigl|\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi,

and finally

H_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U):=G_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U)+P_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U).

According to the following elementary lemma,
g_{\varepsilon}
fulfils (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) with
p=2
and, therefore, all the previous results apply to functionals
\{G^{r}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
. The same conclusion clearly holds for
\{P_{\varepsilon}^{r}\}_{\varepsilon}
with
a_{\varepsilon}(z)=|z|
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
\{z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

g_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}\Bigr)\leq k\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{\varepsilon}(z_{i}).

Proof.

If there exists an index
i^{*}\in\{1,...,k\}
such that
g_{\varepsilon}(z_{i^{*}})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
, then

k\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{\varepsilon}(z_{i})\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\geq\min\Bigl\{\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}\Bigr|^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Bigr\}=g_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}\Bigr),

otherwise,

k\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{\varepsilon}(z_{i})=k\sum_{i=1}^{k}|z_{i}|^{2}\geq\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}\Bigr|^{2}\geq\min\Bigl\{\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}\Bigr|^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Bigr\}=g_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}\Bigr),

which concludes the proof.
∎

The next proposition shows that for sequences of functions
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
that are essentially bounded, a uniform bound on
G_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)
implies a uniform bound on
H_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\,U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega),
and
\varepsilon,r>0
. We have

H_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U)\leq(1+2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})})G_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U)+(|U||B_{r}|G_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U))^{1/2}.

Proof.

We claim that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\frac{|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq|U|^{1/2}|B_{r}|^{1/2}\Bigl(\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\min\Bigl\{\Bigl|\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Bigr\}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi\Bigr)^{1/2}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\quad+2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\min\Bigl\{\Bigl|\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Bigr\}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi.

To prove this, we let

U(\varepsilon,\xi):=\{x\in U_{\varepsilon}(\xi):|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}\}

for all
\varepsilon>0,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By Hölder’s inequality, we infer that

(4.19)

\int_{U(\varepsilon,\xi)}\frac{|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\leq|U|^{1/2}\Bigl(\int_{U(\varepsilon,\xi)}\Bigl|\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Bigr)^{1/2}

and, by the boundedness of
u
,

(4.20)

\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\setminus U(\varepsilon,\xi)}\frac{|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\setminus U(\varepsilon,\xi)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x.

Summing (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) and integrating with respect to
\xi
, we apply Jensen’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\frac{|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq|U|^{1/2}\int_{B_{r}}\Bigl(\int_{U(\varepsilon,\xi)}\Bigl|\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|^{2}{\rm d}x\Bigr)^{1/2}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\quad+2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\setminus U(\varepsilon,\xi)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq|U|^{1/2}|B_{r}|^{1/2}\Bigl(\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U(\varepsilon,\xi)}\Bigl|\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\setminus U(\varepsilon,\xi)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi.

This proves the claim.

By (
4.18
), we conclude

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U)

\displaystyle=G_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U)+\int_{B_{r}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\frac{|u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)|}{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq G_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U)+|U|^{1/2}|B_{r}|^{1/2}(G_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U))^{1/2}+2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}G_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u,U),

which is the thesis.
∎

.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
, let

be as in (

(4.21)

.
Then,
S\coloneqq\sup_{n}H^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},U)<+\infty

(4.22)

4.8

To prove (

F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},U)=F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(u_{n},U)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{T}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\xi)}f_{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big(x,\xi,\frac{u_{n}(x+\varepsilon_{n}\xi)-u_{n}(x)}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi

and, by (

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},U)

\displaystyle\leq F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(u_{n},U)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{T}}\rho_{2}(\xi)\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\xi)}g_{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big(\frac{u_{n}(x+\varepsilon_{n}\xi)-u_{n}(x)}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big){\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\xi

4.6
with

, we make an observation that will come to use multiple times.

Remark 4.10
.

Consider
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
be such that

Note that an analogous property holds if we replace the
\Gamma
-
\liminf
with the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
; that is, considering
F^{\prime\prime}
in place of

. Moreover, the same conclusions hold for

and

if we consider

in place of

.

We are finally ready to prove Proposition
4.2
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

We only prove the statement for
F^{\prime}
, the other case being analogous.

Since
F_{\varepsilon}^{T}(u,U)\leq F_{\varepsilon}(u,U)
for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\varepsilon,T>0
, one inequality is trivial; therefore, we only prove the opposite inequality assuming first that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Given
T>r_{0}
, let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
be such that

(4.23)

u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(u_{n},U)\to F^{\prime,T}(u,U)\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

It is not restrictive to suppose that
F^{\prime,T}(u,U)<+\infty
and, by Remark
4.10
, we may also assume

(4.24)

\sup_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq\sqrt{m}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}.

Thanks to (
4.23
) and (
4.24
), inequality (
4.21
) holds. Hence, by Lemma
4.9
, for any
\delta>0
it holds

F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},U)\leq F^{T}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},U)+C^{*}\delta

for every
T>T^{*}
and
n
large enough. We now pass to the liminf as

and obtain

F^{\prime}(u,U)\leq F^{\prime,T}(u,U)+C^{*}\delta,

for every

;
the conclusion follows letting first

and then
\delta\to 0^{+}
.

If

\displaystyle=\sup_{M}\,F^{\prime}(u^{M},U)=\sup_{M}\,\sup_{T}\,F^{\prime,T}(u^{M},U)

\displaystyle=\sup_{T}\,\sup_{M}\,F^{\prime,T}(u^{M},U)=\sup_{T}\,F^{\prime,T}(u,U)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\,F^{\prime,T}(u,U),

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})

such that
U^{\prime}\subset\subset U

U^{\prime}\cup V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)

F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime}\cup V^{\prime})\leq F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)+F^{\prime\prime}(u,V).

Proof.

Assume first that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and, without loss of generality, also assume that
F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)
and
F^{\prime\prime}(u,V)
are both finite.
Consider two sequences
u_{n},v_{n}\to u

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},U)=F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)\ \quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n},V)=F^{\prime\prime}(u,V),

and, in light of Remark
4.10

\sup_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq\sqrt{m}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})},\quad\sup_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq\sqrt{m}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}.

be a positive integer, and let

\displaystyle U_{i}\colon=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\text{dist}(x,U^{\prime})<\frac{iR}{N}\Big\},\quad i\in\{1,...,N\}.

For all

U^{\prime}\subset\subset U_{i}\subset\subset U_{i+1}\subset\subset U

(5.3)

\begin{cases}\varphi_{i}=1&\text{in }\overline{U}_{i},\\
\varphi_{i}=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U_{i+1},\\

and note that, for any

and

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{i}(x+\varepsilon_{n}\xi)-w_{n}^{i}(x)}{\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{i}(x)\frac{u_{n}(x+\varepsilon_{n}\xi)-u_{n}(x)}{\varepsilon_{n}}+(1-\varphi_{i}(x))\frac{v_{n}(x+\varepsilon_{n}\xi)-v_{n}(x)}{\varepsilon_{n}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\varphi_{i}(x+\varepsilon_{n}\xi)-\varphi_{i}(x)}{\varepsilon_{n}}\left(u_{n}(x+\varepsilon_{n}\xi)-v_{n}(x+\varepsilon_{n}\xi)\right)

and, in particular,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(u,U)

\displaystyle F(u+a,U)

\displaystyle=F(u,U)\quad\text{ for every }a\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

\displaystyle F(Ru,U)

\displaystyle=F(u,U)\quad\text{ for every }R\in SO(m),

\displaystyle F(u^{M},U)

Proof.

Consider the functionals
F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime}:L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
given by

(5.24)

F^{\prime}(u,U):=\Gamma\text{-}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u,U),\qquad F^{\prime\prime}(u,U):=\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u,U)

and, for fixed

, define the inner regular envelopes

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{-}(u,U):=\sup\{F^{\prime}(u,U^{\prime}):U^{\prime}\subset\subset U\text{ with }U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\},

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}_{-}(u,U):=\sup\{F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime}):U^{\prime}\subset\subset U\text{ with }U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\}

for every

. By

, there exists a subsequence

as

such that

for every

, where

in (

) are obtained replacing

in (

) with the subsequence

.

Using Lemma

, we obtain that

for every

, which proves property
(a)
.

Property

follows by the lower semicontinuity of the
\Gamma
-liminf and (

). Locality property

follows by the locality of

and
[

, Proposition 16.15]
.

In order to prove the measure property

we use De Giorgi-Letta criterion for measures (see, e.g.,
[

, Theorem 10.2]
or
[

, Theorem 14.21]
). The proof then amounts to show that, for every

, the set function

is increasing, inner regular on
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
, superadditive, and subadditive.

Let us fix

.
By Lemma
5.3
, (

), and
[

, Remark 14.5]
the set function

is increasing, while it is inner regular on

by definition.

We now prove that

is subadditive. Let

. By inner regularity of

, it suffices to show that

for any

such that

. We choose
U^{\prime},U^{\prime\prime},V^{\prime},V^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
with
U^{\prime}\subset\subset U^{\prime\prime}\subset\subset U
and

and such that

. Using Lemma

and Remark

, we obtain that

F(u,W)\leq F(u,U^{\prime}\cup V^{\prime})\leq F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime}\cup V^{\prime})\leq F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime\prime})+F^{\prime\prime}(u,V^{\prime\prime})\leq{F}(u,U)+{F}(u,V).

We are left with proving that

is superadditive. Let

with

. One can easily check that

and

are disjoint, for every

converging to

in

we obtain

which, letting

For

, property

holds by Proposition

resorting to

6.
Integral Representation

In this section we complete the proof of Theorem
3.3
by proving its integral representation part. We recall the notation set in (
3.8
). Given a functional
\mathcal{F}\colon{\rm SBV^{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
, a function
w\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and
U\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
, we set

\mathfrak{m}^{\mathcal{F}}(w,U):=\inf\{\mathcal{F}(u,U):\,u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})\text{ with }u=w\text{ in a neighbourhood of }\partial U\}.

Given
L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, with a slight abuse of notation, we also let
L
denote the corresponding linear map
L:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we introduce the function
u_{x,\zeta,\nu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

u_{x,\zeta,\nu}(y):=\begin{cases}\zeta&\text{ if }(y-x)\cdot\nu\geq 0,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We let the symbol
Q^{\nu}(x,r)
denote an open cube of side length
r
and centre
x
with two faces orthogonal to
\nu
, and we assume that
Q^{\nu}(x,r)=Q^{-\nu}(x,r)
. We also set
Q(x,r):=x+(-\frac{r}{2},\frac{r}{2})^{d}
.

The main result of this section is as follows.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
F\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
be a functional satisfying properties
(
b
\,)
-
(
f
\,)
of Proposition

.
Then for every

and

we have

where

(6.1)

f_{{\rm bulk}}(x,L):=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(L,Q(x,r))}{r^{d}}

for all
x\in\Omega,

L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, and

(6.2)

f_{{\rm surf}}(x,\zeta,\nu):=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q^{\nu}(x,r))}{r^{d-1}}

for all
x\in\Omega
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle c_{1}\lambda_{1}|L|^{2}\leq f_{\rm bulk}(x,L)\leq c_{2}(\lambda_{2}|L|^{2}+\kappa|L|)+\Lambda\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all $x\in\Omega$ and $L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$},

\displaystyle c_{1}\mu_{1}\leq f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq c_{2}(\mu_{2}+\kappa|\zeta|)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all $x\in\Omega$, $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$, and $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$},

and

\displaystyle f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{1})\leq f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{2})\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if $|L_{1}|\leq|L_{2}|$},

\displaystyle f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{1},\nu)\leq f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{2},\nu)\quad

if
|\zeta_{1}|\leq|\zeta_{2}|
.

for all
x\in\Omega
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. In particular, for all
x\in\Omega
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
it holds

\displaystyle f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{1})=f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{2})\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if $|L_{1}|=|L_{2}|$},

\displaystyle f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{1},\nu)=f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{2},\nu)\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if $|\zeta_{1}|=|\zeta_{2}|$},

\displaystyle f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)=f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,-\nu)\quad

\displaystyle\text{ for all $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$}.

Remark 6.2
.

Thanks to
(f)
of Proposition
5.5
, the functional
F
considered in Proposition
6.1
is invariant under vertical translations. In particular, for every
x\in\Omega
and
L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
we have

(6.3)

f_{\rm bulk}(x,L):=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(L(\cdot-x),Q(x,r))}{r^{d}}.

The proof of this proposition is based on a result of Bouchitté, Fonseca, Leoni, and Mascarenhas
[
14
]
, who gave sufficient conditions for a free discontinuity functional to be representable as the sum of bulk and surface energies. For the reader’s convenience, we here give a complete, slightly modified, statement of this theorem.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\colon{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
be a functional that satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

for every
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
the functional
\mathcal{F}(\cdot,U)
is
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
-lower semicontinuous;

(ii)

for every
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and

with
u=v

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. on
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
we have
\mathcal{F}(u,U)=\mathcal{F}(v,U)
;

(iii)

there exists a positive constant

such that for every
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and

we have

\frac{1}{C}\Bigl(\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}(1+|[u]|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\Bigr)\leq\mathcal{F}(u,U)\\
\leq C\Bigl(\int_{U}(1+|\nabla u|^{2})\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}\!\!\!\!(1+|[u]|)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\Bigr);

(iv)

for every
u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
the set function
\mathcal{F}(u,\cdot)
is the restriction to
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
of a Borel measure on
\Omega
;

(v)

for every

,
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
, and

we have

.

Then for every
u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and

it holds

\mathcal{F}(u,U)=\int_{U}f_{{\rm bulk}}(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}f_{{\rm surf}}(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where
{f}_{{\rm bulk}}
and
{f}_{{\rm surf}}
are given by (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) with

replaced by
\mathcal{F}
.

With this result at our disposal, we are now ready to prove Proposition
6.1
.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

It follows immediately from
(b),(c),(e)
, and

that the restriction of the functional

to

satisfies conditions

By (

[
33

(see also
[
25

\delta>0
we introduce the functional
{F}^{\delta}
defined by

{F}^{\delta}(u,U):={F}(u,U)+\delta\int_{J_{u}\cap U}|[u]|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

for every

and

, which satisfies conditions (

)-(

Hence, we deduce the integral representation

{F}^{\delta}(u,U)=\int_{U}f^{\delta}_{{\rm bulk}}(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}f^{\delta}_{{\rm surf}}(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

for all

, where

and
f^{\delta}_{\rm surf}
are defined as in (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) with
{F}
replaced by
{F}^{\delta}
.

At this point, we aim to pass to the limit as

in (

) in order to obtain an integral representation for
{F}
on
{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
. Clearly,

as

We preliminarily observe that, using

and

, from the upper bound in (

\displaystyle f_{\rm bulk}(x,L)\leq c_{2}(\lambda_{2}|L|^{2}+\kappa|L|)+\Lambda\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\Omega\text{ and }L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},

(6.5)

\displaystyle f^{\delta}_{\rm bulk}(x,L)\leq c_{2}(\lambda_{2}|L|^{2}+\kappa|L|)+\Lambda\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\Omega\text{ and }L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}

Analogously, we have

\displaystyle c_{1}\mu_{1}\leq f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq c_{2}(\mu_{2}+\kappa|\zeta|)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\text{ and }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

(6.6)

\displaystyle f^{\delta}_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq c_{2}\mu_{2}+(c_{2}\kappa+\delta)|\zeta|\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\text{ and }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

Now we prove that

(6.7)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}f^{\delta}_{\rm bulk}(x,L)=f_{\rm bulk}(x,L)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega\text{ and }L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},

(6.8)

for every

\displaystyle f^{\delta}_{\rm bulk}(x,L)\leq f_{\rm bulk}(x,L)\quad\text{ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$},

and that there exists a constant

, such that

\displaystyle f^{\delta}_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq(1+C|\zeta|\delta)f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)\quad\text{ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$, and $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$}.

and

, for

\displaystyle{F}^{\delta}(u_{r},Q(x,r))

\displaystyle={F}(u_{r},Q(x,r))+\delta\int_{J_{u_{r}}\cap Q(x,r)}|[u_{r}]|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle{F}^{\delta}(u_{r},Q^{\nu}(x,r))

\displaystyle={F}(u_{r},Q^{\nu}(x,r))+\delta\int_{J_{u_{r}}\cap Q^{\nu}(x,r)}\!\!\!\!|[u_{r}]|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

(6.12)

{F}(u,U)=\int_{U}f_{{\rm bulk}}(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}f_{{\rm surf}}(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

for every
(u,U)\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
. Note that, by
(c)
, this integral representation yields an identical integral representation on every
U\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
and
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap{\rm SBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Indeed, if
U\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
, any function
u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
can be extended to a function
\widetilde{u}\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and by locality we then have
F(u,U)=F(\widetilde{u},U)
.

In order to extend the integral representation to
{\rm GSBV}^{2}
, we begin showing that

(6.13)

|L_{1}|\leq|L_{2}|\implies f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{1})\leq f_{\rm bulk}(x,L_{2})

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

(6.14)

|\zeta_{1}|\leq|\zeta_{2}|\implies f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{1},\nu)\leq f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta_{2},\nu)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We prove only the first inequality, the proof of the latter being obtained with an analogous argument.
Let us fix
L_{1},L_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
and
r>0
and let
v_{2}\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(Q(x,r);\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
v_{2}=L_{2}
in a neighbourhood of
\partial Q(x,r)
. As
|L_{1}|\leq|L_{2}|
, there exists a
1
-Lipschitz map
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
so that
v_{1}:=\Phi\circ v_{2}
equals
L_{1}
in a neighbourhood of
\partial Q(x,r)
and therefore, by
(f)
, we obtain

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(L_{1},Q(x,r))\leq F(v_{1},Q(x,r))\leq F(v_{2},Q(x,r)).

From of the arbitrariness of
v_{2}
and of
r
we deduce (
6.13
).

We are now ready to conclude the proof of the integral representation. Let us fix

U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and
u\in{\rm GSBV^{2}}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Since
u^{M}\in{\rm SBV^{2}}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
for every
M>0
, by (
6.12
) we obtain that

(6.15)

{F}(u^{M},U)=\int_{U}f_{{\rm bulk}}(x,\nabla u^{M})\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u^{M}}\cap U}f_{{\rm surf}}(x,[u^{M}],\nu_{u^{M}})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

Combining
(b)
with
(f)
, we deduce that

\lim_{M\to+\infty}F(u^{M},U)=F(u,U).

Then, recalling Remark
2.2
, (
6.13
) and (
6.14
), and using the Monotone Convergence Theorem, we let
M\to+\infty
in (
6.15
) to get

{F}(u,U)=\int_{U^{\prime}}f_{{\rm bulk}}(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}f_{{\rm surf}}(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

which is the thesis.
∎

7.
Convergence of minima and
\Gamma
-convergence

In this section we fix a positive sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
converging to
0
as
n\to+\infty
and
a sequence
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
given by (
3.5
), with
\{f_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
satisfying conditions (i)-(iii) of Section
3
. We show that thanks to general properties of
\Gamma
-convergence and to results obtained in previous sections, the asymptotic behaviour of minimisation problems associated to
F_{\varepsilon_{n}}
on small cubes determines the energy densities in the integral representation of the
\Gamma
-limit
F
.

We first state a compactness result for sequences with equi-bounded energy, originally proved by Gobbino in
[
41
, Theorem 5.4]
(see also
[
22
, Theorem 5.23]
).

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, let
r>0
, and assume that

\sup_{n}\big(\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}+G^{r}_{1,\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\big)<+\infty,

where
G^{r}_{1,\varepsilon_{n}}
are the functionals defined by (
2.14
).
Then there exists a subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
and a function
u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
\{u_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}\}_{k}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
k\to+\infty
.

We now introduce the minimisation problems for the non-local functionals we are interested in.
Given
U\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
,
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and
s>0
, we set

\mathcal{D}_{s,w}(U):=\big\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):\,u=w\text{ for $\mathcal{L}^{d}$-a.e.\ $x\in U$ }\text{with dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U)<s\big\},

and consider the minimisation problems

\mathfrak{m}_{s}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(w,U):=\inf\big\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u,U):u\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon_{n}s,w}(U)\big\}.

We recall that

\mathfrak{m}_{s_{1}}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(w,U)\leq\mathfrak{m}_{s_{2}}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}(w,U)

for every
0<s_{1}<s_{2}
.

The following result shows that, if
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to
F
on a regular open set
U\subset\Omega
and if
V\subset\subset U
, the minimum value of
\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)
can be controlled from above by the asymptotic value of the minimisation problems
\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,V)
and
H(w,U\setminus V)
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
w\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and let
U,V\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
with
V\subset\subset U
. Assume that
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\cdot,U)\}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to
F(\cdot,U)
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Then

(7.1)

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)\leq\sup_{s>0}\,\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,V)+c_{2}H(w,U\setminus V)+\Lambda|U\setminus V|.

Proof.

We consider a sequence of functions
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon_{n}s,w}(V)
such that

(7.2)

F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},V)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,V)+\varepsilon_{n},

and which, by truncation, we may assume to be satisfying
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq{\sqrt{m}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}
.

Let us fix
s>2r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is the constant introduced in (
2.4
). Thanks to (
2.4
) and (
3.6
), from this inequality we obtain

c_{0}G^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},V)\leq F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},V)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,V)+\varepsilon_{n}\leq H_{\varepsilon_{n}}(w,V)+\|\eta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|V|+\varepsilon_{n},

where
c_{0}
is the positive constant introduced in (
2.4
) and
G_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{r_{0}}
is as in (
4.17
). Recalling (
2.13
), this chain of inequalities implies

(7.3)

\sup_{n}G^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},V)\leq\sup_{n}\frac{1}{c_{0}}\Big(H_{\varepsilon_{n}}(w,U)+\|\eta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|U|+1\Big)<+\infty

upon assuming
\varepsilon_{n}<1
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with the right hand side being independent of
s>2r_{0}
.

We introduce the extension
\widetilde{u}_{n}
of the function
u_{n}
obtained by setting
\widetilde{u}_{n}:=u_{n}
on
V
and

\widetilde{u}_{n}:=w
on
U\setminus V
. We observe that

\displaystyle G^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\widetilde{u}_{n},U)\leq G^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}({u}_{n},V)+G^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}\big(w,\big((U\setminus V)+B_{\varepsilon_{n}r_{0}}\big)\cap U\big),

which by (
7.3
) implies

(7.4)

\sup_{n}G^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\widetilde{u}_{n},U)\leq\big(1+\frac{1}{c_{0}}\big)\sup_{n}\Big(H_{\varepsilon_{n}}(w,U)+\|\eta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|U|+1\Big)<+\infty.

Observe that by Proposition
4.8
, inequality (
7.4
) also implies that

S:=\sup_{n}H^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\widetilde{u}_{n},U)<+\infty.

We now pass to a subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
such that

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,V)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}}_{s}(w,V).

Thanks to (
7.4
), we may apply Lemma
7.1
to obtain a further subsequence, not relabelled, such that
\{\widetilde{u}_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
converges strongly in
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
to a function
u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, which also satisfies the boundary conditions
u=w

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. in
U\setminus V
.

For every
T>0
let us consider the functional
F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}
defined by (
4.1
). Thanks to Lemma
4.9
, there is a constant
C^{*}
, depending only on
S
and on
\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}
, and which is therefore independent of
s
, such that for every
\delta>0
it holds

F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\widetilde{u}_{n},U)\leq F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(\widetilde{u}_{n},U)+C^{*}\delta

for every
T>T^{*}=T^{*}(\delta)
and
n
large enough.
Hence, given
\delta>0
and further assuming that
s>T^{*}
, recalling that
u
is a competitor for the minimisation problem
\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)
, by
\Gamma
-convergence we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)

\displaystyle\leq F(u,U)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}(\widetilde{u}_{n_{k}},U)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\Big(F_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}^{T}(\widetilde{u}_{n_{k}},U)+C^{*}\delta\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\Big(F_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}^{T}({u}_{n_{k}},V)+F^{T}_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}\big(w,\big((U\setminus V)+B_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}T})\big)\cap U\big)\Big)+C^{*}\delta,

for every
T\in(T^{*},s]
.
Using (
3.6
) and (
2.13
), from this last chain of inequalities we obtain that for every
\sigma>0

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}({u}_{n_{k}},V)+c_{2}H\big(w,\big((U\setminus V)+B_{\sigma}\big)\cap U\big)+\Lambda|((U\setminus V)+B_{\sigma})\cap U)|+C^{*}\delta.

By arbitrariness of
\sigma>0
and by (
7.2
) we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}({u}_{n_{k}},V)+c_{2}H(w,U\setminus V)+\Lambda|U\setminus V|+C^{*}\delta

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}}_{s}(w,V)+c_{2}H(w,U\setminus V)+\Lambda|U\setminus V|+C^{*}\delta.

Taking the limit as
s\to+\infty
and as
\delta\to 0^{+}
, we obtain (
7.1
), concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

Following the above proof, it is easily seen that the statement of the previous lemma can be improved upon assuming that there exists
T>0
such that
f_{\varepsilon_{n}}(x,\xi,z)=0
for a.e
x\in\Omega,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{T},z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case, we obtain that

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,V)+c_{2}H(w,U\setminus V)+\Lambda|U\setminus V|

for all
s\geq T
.

In the following lemma we show that, if
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to
F
on a regular open set
U\subset\Omega
, the minimum value of
\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)
is always larger than the asymptotic value of the minimisation problems
\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,U)
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
w\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, let
s>0
, and let
U\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
. Assume that
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\cdot,U)\}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to
F(\cdot,U)
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
n\to+\infty
. Then

(7.5)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,U)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U).

Proof.

Since
w\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we have that
H(w,U)<+\infty
, which by (
3.6
) clearly implies
\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)<+\infty
.
Let us fix
\sigma>0
and
u\in{\rm SBV}^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
F(u,U)<\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)+\sigma
and
u=w
in a neighbourhood of
\partial U
. Observe that, since
F
satisfies property
(f)
of Proposition
5.5
on
U
, it is not restrictive to assume that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

We pass to a not relabelled subsequence such that the limsup in (
7.5
) is actually a limit and consider a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, such that

(7.6)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},U)=F(u,U)<\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)+\sigma.

By Lemma
4.1
we can also assume that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq{\sqrt{m}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so that, possibly passing to a further subsequence, we can assume that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
by Lemma
7.1
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(U)
be obtained intersecting
U
with a neighbourhood of
\partial U
on which
u=w

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. and let
U^{\prime}\subset\subset U^{\prime\prime}\subset\subset U
be open sets such that
U\setminus U^{\prime}\subset V
. Having fixed
T>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we may argue as in the proof of Lemma
5.2
to find a sequence of cutoff functions
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}
compactly supported in
U^{\prime\prime}
such that
\varphi_{n}=1
in
\overline{U^{\prime}}
and, setting
v_{n,N}:=\varphi_{n}u_{n}+(1-\varphi_{n})u
, we have

(7.7)

F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(v_{n,N},U)\leq F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},U)+F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(w,V)+\frac{C}{N-4}+C(N^{2}+N)\|u_{n}-w\|_{L^{1}(V;\mathbb{R}^{m})},

for a positive constant
C>0
, independent of
n
and
N
, and for
n
large enough. Note that
\{v_{n,N}\}_{n}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
n\to+\infty
and that
\|v_{n,N}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq{2\sqrt{m}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, we observe that, since the supports of
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}
are compactly contained in
U^{\prime\prime}
,
U\setminus U^{\prime}\subset V
, and
u=w

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. on
V
, we have
v_{n,N}\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon_{n}s,w}(U)
for every
n
large enough.

Then, by (
3.6
) and (
2.13
) we get

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(w,V)\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}H_{\varepsilon_{n}}(w,V)+\Lambda|V|\leq c_{2}H(w,V)+\Lambda|V|.

Recalling that
\|u_{n}-w\|_{L^{1}(V;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
and (
7.6
), we may pass to the limsup as
n\to+\infty
in (
7.7
) and obtain

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(v_{n,N},U)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)+\sigma+c_{2}H(w,V)+\Lambda|V|+\frac{C}{N-4}

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and by arbitrariness of
\sigma
and
V
, we get

(7.8)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(v_{n,N},U)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)+\frac{C}{N-4}.

To conclude, we show that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(7.9)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,U)\leq\sup_{T>0}\,\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(v_{n,N},U).

To see this, we begin noting that from
(
7.8
) and Lemma
4.9
it follows that

S:=\sup_{n}H^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n,N},U)<+\infty

and that there is a positive constant
C^{*}
, depending only on
S
,
N
, and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}
, such that for every
\delta>0

F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n,N},U)\leq F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(v_{n,N},U)+C^{*}\delta,

for every
T>T^{*}(\delta)
and
n
large enough.
Recalling that
v_{n,N}\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon_{n}s,w}(U)
, this implies that

\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(w,U)\leq F_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{T}(v_{n,N},U)+C^{*}\delta

for every
T>T^{*}(\delta)
. Passing to the limsup as
n\to+\infty
and to the limit as
T\to+\infty
and
\delta\to 0^{+}
, we obtain (
7.9
).
Finally, combining (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) and passing to the limit as
N\to+\infty
, we get (
7.5
), concluding the proof.
∎

We recall the definition of the functions

(7.10)

\displaystyle f_{\rm bulk}^{\prime}(x,L)

The following two propositions prove Theorem
3.4
.

Proposition 7.5
.

Assume that there exists a functional
F\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]

the sequence
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\cdot,U)\}_{n}

with respect the topology of

Then the functions

and
f_{\rm surf}
defined in (

) and (

(7.14)

\displaystyle f_{\rm bulk}(x,L)=f^{\prime}_{\rm bulk}(x,L)=f^{\prime\prime}_{\rm bulk}(x,L),

(7.15)

\displaystyle f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)=f^{\prime}_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)=f^{\prime\prime}_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu),

for all
x\in\Omega
,
L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and

.

Let us fix

,
L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We begin proving (
7.14
). Given
r>0
, we set
\sigma:=r+r^{2}
and apply Lemma
7.2
with
w=L
,

, and

\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(L,Q(x,\sigma))}{\sigma^{d}}\leq\sup_{s>0}\,\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(L,Q(x,r))}{\sigma^{d}}+[c_{2}(\lambda_{2}|L|^{2}+\kappa|L|)+\Lambda]\Big(1-\frac{r^{d}}{\sigma^{d}}\Big).

Since

as

, from the previous inequality we get
f_{\rm bulk}(x,L)\leq f^{\prime}_{\rm bulk}(x,L)
. The inequality

is obtained applying Lemma

.

To prove (

), we apply again Lemma
7.2
with

,

, and

to get

\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q^{\nu}(x,\sigma))}{\sigma^{d-1}}\leq\sup_{s>0}\,\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}}_{s}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q^{\nu}(x,r))}{\sigma^{d-1}}+c_{2}(\mu_{2}+\kappa|\zeta|)\Big(1-\frac{r^{d-1}}{\sigma^{d-1}}\Big)+\Lambda\Big(\frac{\sigma^{d}-r^{d}}{\sigma^{d-1}}\Big),

Since
\sigma/r\to 1
as

, from the previous inequality we get
f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq f^{\prime}_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)
. The inequality
f^{\prime\prime}_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq f_{\rm surf}(x,\zeta,\nu)
follows from an application of Lemma
7.4
.
∎

Proposition 7.6
.

Assume that there exist functions
\hat{f}_{\rm bulk}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
and
\hat{f}_{\rm surf}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
