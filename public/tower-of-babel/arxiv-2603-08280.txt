Gordan-Rankin-Cohen operators on the spaces of weighted densities in superdimension 1 | 1 1|1
Victor Bovdi a,∗ , Dimitry Leites b

Abstract
. The modular forms and weighted densities over the 1-dimensional manifold M M are transformed “alike” under the group of linear fractional changes of coordinates, so the classifications of differential operators between spaces of (A) modular forms and (B) weighted densities are sometimes identified, although they are different. Here, we solve problem B for superstrings in superdimension ( 1 | 1 ) (1|1) — superizations of the result of arXiv:2404.18222. Open problems are offered.

1.1.
Weighted densities vs. modular forms

Recall that on any finite-dimensional supermanifold
{\mathcal{M}}

(for its precise form, see eq. (

(1)

Equivalently, the weighted densities can be described in terms of any subsupergroup

of diffeomorphisms

On the line,

dz
. Since

1.1.1.
Vital difference and a cause of confusions.

The modular forms and weighted densities over the 1-dimensional manifold
M

of linear fractional changes of coordinates, so the following two problems are sometimes identified, which is wrong

(8)

“classify differential operators between spaces of

(9)

3.3.1.
The 4 cases.

Only the following cases are possible:

(i)

(ii)

\lambda\neq 0

(iii)

The last condition is true only for

(31)

3.6.

-singular vectors in Case
(i)

Let

(resp.,

can be divided into the even (with coefficients

and
D
) and odd (with coefficients
B
and
C

are homogeneous under the action of
H_{2}
, then the spaces of singular vectors are also direct sums of subspaces homogeneous with respect to parity and
H_{2}
-weight. Hence, we can consider the summands with coefficients

(of
H_{2}
-weight 0) and the summands with coefficients
D
(of
H_{2}
-weight
-2

3

(A)

, where

(D)

with basis vectors
({\partial}^{\prime})^{k}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k-2}\delta^{\prime}\delta^{\prime\prime}u

;

(BC)

with basis vectors

, and
({\partial}^{\prime})^{k}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k-1}\delta^{\prime\prime}u
, where

In case (A)
, we have

X_{+}({\partial}^{\prime})^{k}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k}u=-k({\partial}^{\prime})^{k-1}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k}\delta^{\prime}u-(n-k)({\partial}^{\prime})^{k}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k-1}\delta^{\prime\prime}u.

The summands of images of different basis elements are also different basis elements, and none of the images vanishes, except for the case of
n=0
. Hence, the kernel of
X_{+}
on the space of
A
-vectors is zero.

In case (D)
, we have

(38)

\begin{split}\textstyle X_{+}({\partial}^{\prime})^{k}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k-2}\delta^{\prime}\delta^{\prime\prime}u=&0;\\
s_{\xi}({\partial}^{\prime})^{k}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k-2}\delta^{\prime}\delta^{\prime\prime}u=&(-k+\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}\mu_{1})({\partial}^{\prime})^{k}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k-2}\delta^{\prime\prime}u\\
&-(-(n-k-2)+\textstyle\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}\mu_{2})({\partial}^{\prime})^{k}({\partial}^{\prime\prime})^{n-k-2}\delta^{\prime}u.\end{split}

The images of different basis elements are still different basis elements, but in this case, the image of the
k
-th basis element can vanish if
-k+\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}\mu_{1}
and

and

are non-negative even numbers and

.
We have

If

{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbin{\hbox{\raise 1.20552pt\hbox{\bf.}}}}
and
\Pi({\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbin{\hbox{\raise 1.20552pt\hbox{\bf.}}}})
.

Solution to problem A

4.1.
Further open problems and conjectures

1) In the case of 1-dimensional varieties over the ground field of characteristic
p>0
, all bilinear invariant operators between spaces of weighted densities are classified, see
[
BL
]
, thus providing with examples of GRC-operators as well. To list all GRC-operators invariant under the maximal subalgebra
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2)
in the Lie algebra of vector fields seems to be a feasible open problem;
however, unlike the open problems listed in
[
BL
]
, no interpretation of this problem is known at the moment.

2) In an unfinished draft of the description of GRC-operators between spaces of weighted densities on
(1|N)
-dimensional superstrings with a contact structure (by Bouarroudj et al.) many interesting new
{\mathfrak{k}}(1|N)
-invariant oprators, hence GRC-operators, are found. In particular, several incomplete for
N=2
results of
[
BLO
]
dealing with problem B are completed: (i) for
N=2
, the weight is a
pair
of numbers, (ii) a contact structure with odd time is possible — both cases were never previously considered.

3) In
[
OR
]
, analogs of GRC-operators invariant under
{\mathfrak{o}}(n+2)\subset{\mathfrak{vect}}(n|0)
between spaces of weighted densities are described (not classified) for a real form of
{\mathfrak{o}}(n+2)
; for the classification in the case of
n=1
and over
{\mathbb{C}}
, see
[
BoLe
]
.

It seems also feasible to classify GRC-operators invariant under other maximal finite-dimensional simple Lie sub(super)algebras of simple infinite-dimensional vectorial Lie (super)algebras. For example, if we generalize the setting of problem B by considering binary operators invariant under
{\mathfrak{pgl}}(a+1|b)\subset{\mathfrak{vect}}(a|b)
between spaces of tensor fields with multidimensional fibers, then Grozman’s classification of the
{\mathfrak{vect}}(a)
-invariant operators, see
[
Gr
]
, gives us a part of the answer. To tackle the problem in such generality is hardly feasible, see Grozman’s proof and Subsection
3.4
.
