Some criteria for Gorensteinness via Gorenstein projective cotorsion pairs
Souvik Dey, Jian Liu, Xue-Song Lu

Abstract
. Let R R be a noetherian algebra over a Cohen–Macaulay ring admitting a canonical module, and assume that R R is maximal Cohen–Macaulay over the base ring. We provide a characterization of when R R is left weakly Gorenstein. We further show that the category of finitely generated Gorenstein projective R R -modules coincides with the left Ext \Ext -orthogonal class of the thick subcategory generated by finitely generated R R -modules of finite projective or finite injective dimension. As a consequence, finitely generated Gorenstein projective R R -modules generate a hereditary cotorsion pair. Moreover, we show that a Cohen–Macaulay local ring is Gorenstein if and only if the right Ext \Ext -orthogonal class of finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules coincides with the category of finitely generated modules of finite projective dimension.

1.
Introduction

The study of Gorenstein homological properties has played a central role in modern representation theory and commutative algebra. In 1967, M. Auslander
[
5
]
introduced the notion of finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules in his four lectures at the Séminaire Pierre Samuel, under the name “modules of G-dimension zero”.
In
[
17
]
, E. E. Enochs and O. M. G. Jenda defined and studied Gorenstein projective and injective modules for arbitrary rings. For an Iwanaga–Gorenstein ring, R.-O. Buchweitz
[
10
]
established a triangle equivalence between the stable category of finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules and the singularity category, demonstrating the deep connections between the Gorenstein projective modules and the singularity theory.

The notion of
left weakly Gorenstein rings
was introduced by C. M. Ringel and P. Zhang
[
34
]
. It was also independently introduced under different names by L. W. Christensen and H. Holm
[
12
]
, as well as by R. Marczinzik
[
31
]
. By definition, a left weakly Gorenstein ring is a ring for which the left Ext-orthogonal class of the ring itself coincides with the class of finitely generated Gorenstein projective left modules. In
[
34
]
, several equivalent characterizations of left weakly Gorensteinness for Artin algebras were established.
Recently, the weakly Gorensteinness has garnered increasing interest and has been studied in
[
20
,
24
,
30
]
.

The first result of this article is
Theorem

1.1
, which provides a characterization of left weakly Gorenstein rings for certain noetherian algebras over Cohen–Macaulay rings.
It reveals that the left weakly Gorenstein condition is governed by a single module
X
.
It is worth noting that any Artin algebra or complete Cohen–Macaulay local ring satisfies the hypothesis on

R
in
Theorem

1.1
; see more examples in

Example

3.8
.

2.
preliminaries

In this article, a noetherian ring is assumed to be two-sided noetherian.
Throughout,
R
is always a noetherian ring. Denote by
\mathsf{Mod}(R)
(resp.
\mathsf{mod}(R)
) the category of all (resp. finitely generated) left
R
-modules, and by
\mathsf{Proj}(R)
(resp.
\mathsf{Inj}(R)
) the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}(R)
consisting of all projective (resp. injective)
R
-modules. Denote by
R^{\rm op}
the opposite ring of
R
. Consequently, a right
R
-module can be viewed as a module in
\mathsf{Mod}(R^{\rm op})
. We will also write
{}_{R}M
(resp.
N_{R}
) to indicate that
M
(resp.
N
) is a left (resp. right)
R
-module.

We write
\mathsf{proj}(R)
(resp.
\mathsf{inj}(R)
) for the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Proj}(R)
(resp.
\mathsf{Inj}(R)
) consisting of all finitely generated projective (resp. injective) modules.
We use
{\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
(resp.
\mathcal{I}^{<\infty}(R)
) to denote the full subcategory of
\mathsf{mod}(R)
consisting of all finitely generated modules of finite projective (resp. injective) dimension.

When
R
is commutative, we denote by
\dim(R)
the Krull dimension of
R
.
If, moreover,
R
is a local ring, then for each
M\in\mathrm{mod}(R)
, we denote by

\depth(M)
the depth of
M
over
R
, that is, the length of a maximal

M
-regular sequence contained in the maximal ideal of
R
.

2.1
.

Noetherian algebras.

For a commutative noetherian ring
S
,
R
is said to be a
noetherian
S
-algebra
if there exists a ring homomorphism
\varphi\colon S\rightarrow R
such that the image of
\varphi
is in the center of
R
and
R
is finitely generated as an
S
-module.

.

2.3
.

Thick subcategories of an abelian category.
A full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is called
thick
if it is closed under direct summands,
and if two out of three terms in a short exact sequence are in
\mathcal{C}
, then so is the third one. For a class of objects
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{A}
, we write

to be the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
containing
\mathcal{S}
. If
{\mathcal{S}}
is a class of modules in
\mathsf{mod}(R)
, we will simply use
\Thick({\mathcal{S}})
to represent
\Thick_{\mathsf{mod}(R)}({\mathcal{S}})
in this article.

2.4
.

Torsionless modules and reflexive modules.

For each
M\in\mathsf{mod}(R)
, consider the map

\varphi_{M}\colon M\rightarrow\Hom_{R^{\rm op}}(\Hom_{R}(M,R),R);m\mapsto(f\mapsto f(m)).

A module
M
is called
torsionless
if
\varphi_{M}
is a monomorphism, and
reflexive
if
\varphi_{M}
is an isomorphism.
It is known that
M
is torsionless if and only if
M
is a submodule of some projective module.

2.5
.

Maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules and Cohen–Macaulay rings.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. A module
M\in\mathsf{mod}(R)
is called
maximal Cohen–Macaulay
if
\depth(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. We refer the readers to
[
9
, 2.1]
for more details.

Let
\MCM(R)
denote the full subcategory of
\mathsf{mod}(R)
consisting of all maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules.
The ring
R
is said to be
Cohen–Macaulay
provided that
R\in\MCM(R)
.

For an
R
-module
M
, we write
\id_{R}(M)
to denote the injective dimension of
M
over
R
.

2.6
.

Canonical modules.

For a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal and
k
is the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
, a module
\omega\in\mathsf{mod}(R)
is said to be
canonical
if
\Ext^{d}_{R}(k,R)\cong k
and
\Ext^{i}_{R}(k,R)=0
for
i\neq d
, where
d=\dim(R)
; see
[
9
, 3.3]
. In this case,
\id_{R}(\omega)<\infty
.

In general, for a Cohen–Macaulay ring
R
, a finitely generated module
\omega
is called

provided that
\omega_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a canonical module over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. In this case, if furthermore
\dim(R)<\infty
, then
\id_{R}(\omega)<\infty
; see, for example,
[
9
, Theorem 3.1.17]
.

Canonical modules are also referred to as dualizing modules in the literature; see, for example,
[
4
]
.

As an example, a commutative Artin ring is always a Cohen–Macaulay ring, and the module
J
as in
2.2
is a canonical module.

2.7
.

(Iwanaga–)Gorenstein rings.

A noetherian ring
R
is called
Iwanaga–Gorenstein
provided that
R
has finite injective dimension over both sides. By
[
38
, Lemma A]
, if

is Iwanaga–Gorenstein, then
\id_{R}(R)=\id_{R^{\rm op}}(R)<\infty
.

A commutative noetherian ring

is said to be

provided that

is Iwanaga–Gorenstein for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. For a commutative noetherian ring
R
, if

is Iwanaga–Gorenstein, then

is Gorenstein. The converse holds if, in addition,

[
9
, Theorem 3.17]
.

2.8
.

Gorenstein projective (resp. injective) modules.

An
R
-module

is called
Gorenstein projective
(resp.
Gorenstein injective
) if there exists an acyclic complex of projective (resp. injective)

\mathbf{P}\colon\cdots\longrightarrow P_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}P_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}P_{-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\quad\text{(resp. }\mathbf{I}\colon\cdots\longrightarrow I_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}I_{0}\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}I_{-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\text{)}

such that

(resp.

) is acyclic for each

(resp.

), and

is isomorphic to the image of

(resp.
\partial_{0}
).
The complex
\mathbf{P}
(resp.
\mathbf{I}
) will be called

of projective (resp. injective) modules.
We write
\mathsf{GProj}(R)
(resp.
\mathsf{GInj}(R)
) to be the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}(R)
consisting of Gorenstein projective (resp. Gorenstein injective) modules. We refer the readers to
[
18

for more details.

We write
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)
to be the full subcategory of
\mathsf{mod}(R)
consisting of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
R
-modules.

Virtually Gorenstein rings.

In

2.11
.

Left approximations and covariantly finite subcategories.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a full subcategory of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
. For each

and a morphism

with

, the morphism

is said to be a

-approximation
if any morphism

with

factors through

. The subcategory
\mathcal{C}
is said to be a

subcategory of

provided that any object

has a left
\mathcal{C}
-approximation. Dually, there are notions of right approximations and contravariantly finite subcategories.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be a complete cotorsion pair of
\mathcal{A}
. Then,
\mathcal{X}
is contravariantly finite and
\mathcal{Y}
is covariantly finite.

Let
M
be an
R
-module and
\add M
be the full subcategory of
\mathsf{mod}(R)
consisting of modules that are direct summands of finite direct sums of
M
. When
R
is an Artin algebra or a commutative noetherian ring, it is known that
\add M
is both covariantly finite and contravariantly finite in
\mathsf{mod}(R)
. See for example,
[
3
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Let
R

R
-modules

0\rightarrow M\xrightarrow{d_{0}}P_{-1}\xrightarrow{d_{-1}}P_{-2}\xrightarrow{d_{-2}}\cdots\rightarrow P_{-n}\rightarrow\cdots,

where
P_{-i}\in\mathsf{proj}(R)
and the natural embedding
\Ima d_{-(i-1)}\rightarrow P_{-i}
is a left
\mathsf{proj}(R)
-approximation for each

. Then

.

Indeed, splicing a projective resolution of

with the projective coresolution of

constructed above produces a totally acyclic complex of finitely generated projective

-modules. Consequently,

is Gorenstein projective.

3.
Characterizations of Gorenstein projective modules

The main result of this section is
Theorem

1.2
from the introduction; see
Corollary

3.10
. It follows as an application of
Theorem

3.6
, which provides a description of the category of finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules for a certain class of rings.

The following lemma is well-known. We provide a proof for convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring. In
\mathsf{mod}(R)
, there are

(1)

\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}=\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{\infty}}
.

(2)

\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}
is a thick subcategory.

Proof.

(1)
It suffices to show that for
X\in\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}
,
G\in\mathsf{Gproj}(R)
and
t\geq 2
, there is
\Ext_{R}^{t}(G,X)=0
. Choose a projective resolution of
G

\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{n+1}}P_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{2}}P_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}P_{0}\xrightarrow{\pi}G\rightarrow 0,

where
P_{i}\in\mathsf{proj}(R)
for each
i\geq 0
. Note that
\Ima f_{i}\in\mathsf{Gproj}(R)
for each
i\geq 0
. Thus,
\Ext_{R}^{t}(G,X)=\Ext_{R}^{1}(\Ima f_{t-1},X)=0
.

(2)
Combining with (1), the statement follows from
[
35
, Proposition 4.4]
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For a noetherian ring
R
, there is an inclusion in
\mathsf{mod}(R)

\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup{\mathcal{I}}^{<\infty}(R))\subseteq\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}.

Proof.

Note that in
\mathsf{Mod}(R)
, for
G\in\mathsf{GProj}(R)
and
X\in\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup{\mathcal{I}}^{<\infty}(R)
, there is
\Ext_{R}^{1}(G,X)=0
. It follows that
\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup{\mathcal{I}}^{<\infty}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}
in
\mathsf{mod}(R)
. The desired result now follows from

3.1
(2).
∎

Lemma 3.5

3.3

Conversely, let
X\in\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.65295pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.65295pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.83716pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.78491pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\{R^{\dagger}\})

\mathsf{proj}(R)
-approximation, where

is a left
\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\{R^{\dagger}\})

\textstyle{Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Remark 3.11
.

The cotorsion pair

Let

By

for all

4.
Weakly Gorensteinness and virtually Gorensteinness

The main result of this section is
Theorem

1.1
from the introduction; see also
Theorem

4.5
.
It provides a characterization of left weakly Gorenstein rings for certain noetherian algebras over Cohen–Macaulay rings.
We also study virtually Gorenstein rings and obtain an equivalent characterization related to a question of X.-W. Chen
[
11
]
; see the discussion in
4.9
.

4.1
.

Following C. M. Ringel and P. Zhang
[
34
, 1.1]
, a noetherian ring

is said to be
left weakly Gorenstein
if
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R=\mathsf{Gproj}(R)
in
\mathsf{mod}(R)
. We will say that
R
is
weakly Gorenstein
if both
R
and
R^{\rm op}
are left weakly Gorenstein.

Note that
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R=\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 12.77512pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.2918pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}({\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))
in
\mathsf{mod}(R)
. By
2.9
, any Iwanaga–Gorenstein ring is weakly Gorenstein. Also,
Theorem

3.6
yields that any commutative Gorenstein ring is weakly Gorenstein.

The following example is a weakly Gorenstein Cohen–Macaulay local ring but not Gorenstein.

Example 4.2
.

Let

be a commutative local Artin ring with

and

is not Gorenstein. For example,

, where

is a weakly Gorenstein Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Indeed, there is
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)=\mathsf{proj}(R)=\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R

[
37
, Proposition 2.4]
.

The following proposition is a characterization of left weakly Gorenstein rings. The equivalences in
Proposition

4.3
are essentially proved by C. Huang and Z. Y. Huang
[
23
, Claim 5.1]
. When

is an Artin algebra, the equivalences in

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring. The following are equivalent.

(1)

is left weakly Gorenstein.

(2)

Any module in
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R

(3)

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R
is torsionless.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. If

is weakly Gorenstein, then any module in
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R

The implication

is trivial.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)

X\in\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\{R^{\dagger}\})
. Combining this with
Remark

3.7
, we get
X\in\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{\infty}}=(^{\perp_{\infty}}R)^{\perp_{\infty}}
. By
Theorem

4.5
,
R
is left weakly Gorenstein.
∎

4.9
.

Let
R
be an Artin algebra such that
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)=\mathsf{proj}(R)
.
In
[
11
, Problem C]
, X.-W. Chen asked the following question: does it follow that
\mathsf{GProj}(R)=\mathsf{Proj}(R)
?

Proposition

4.12
will yield that this question is equivalent to asking whether
R
is virtually Gorenstein.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
S
be a Cohen–Macaulay ring that admits a canonical module
\omega
. Assume that
R
is a noetherian
S
-algebra and that
R
, viewed as an
S
-module, is maximal Cohen–Macaulay. Then
\mathsf{GProj}(R)=\mathsf{Proj}(R)
if and only if
\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\{R^{\dagger}\})=\mathsf{mod}(R)
, where
(-)^{\dagger}=\Hom_{S}(-,\omega)
.

Proof.

First, we recall that
R^{\dagger}
is the dualizing complex of the pair
\langle R,R\rangle
in the sense of
[
27
, Definition 3.3.1]
. Note that
\mathsf{GProj}(R)=\mathsf{Proj}(R)
if and only if
\mathsf{K}_{\rm tac}(\mathsf{Proj}(R))=0
, where
\mathsf{K}_{\rm tac}(\mathsf{Proj}(R))
is the full subcategory consisting of totally acyclic complexes of projective modules in the homotopy category of complexes of
R
-modules. By
[
27
, Theorems 5.3(1) and 5.12]
,
\mathsf{K}_{\rm tac}(\mathsf{Proj}(R))
is compactly generated. Thus,
\mathsf{K}_{\rm tac}(\mathsf{Proj}(R))=0
if and only if
\mathsf{K}_{\rm tac}^{\rm c}(\mathsf{Proj}(R))=0
, where
\mathsf{K}_{\rm tac}^{\rm c}(\mathsf{Proj}(R))
is the full subcategory of compact objects in
\mathsf{K}_{\rm tac}(\mathsf{Proj}(R))
. Combining
[
27
, Theorems 5.3(2) and 5.12]
with
[
29
, Theorem 1]
, we conclude that
\mathsf{K}_{\rm tac}^{\rm c}(\mathsf{Proj}(R))=0
if and only if
\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\{R^{\dagger}\})=\mathsf{mod}(R)
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.11
.

Let
\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)
denote the stable category of
\mathsf{GProj}(R)
modulo projective modules. The objects of
\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)
are the same as those of
\mathsf{GProj}(R)
. For any
M,N\in\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)
, the morphism space

\Hom_{\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom_{R}(M,N)/{\mathcal{P}}(M,N),

where
\mathcal{P}(M,N)
consists of morphisms in
\Hom_{R}(M,N)
which factor through a projective module.
It is known that
\mathsf{GProj}(R)
is a Frobenius category whose projective–injective objects are precisely the projective modules, and hence its stable category
\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)
is triangulated. We refer the reader to
[
21
]
for further details on Frobenius categories and the triangulated structure of their stable categories.

When
R
is an Artin algebra, A. Beligiannis
[
8
, Theorem 6.6]
observed that
\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)
is a compactly generated triangulated category; this result also holds for more general rings that admit dualizing complexes; see
[
27
, Theorems 5.3 and 5.12]
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
R
be an Artin algebra and assume
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)=\mathsf{proj}(R)
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

R
is virtually Gorenstein.

(2)

\mathsf{GProj}(R)=\mathsf{Proj}(R)
.

(3)

(\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.65295pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.65295pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.83716pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.78491pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\mathsf{inj}(R)),\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\mathsf{inj}(R)))

is a cotorsion pair.

Proof.

(1)\iff(2)
. By
[
8
, Theorem 8.2]
,
R
is virtually Gorenstein if and only if
\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)^{c}=\underline{\mathsf{Gproj}}(R)
, where
\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)^{c}
is the full subcategory of compact objects in
\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)
. Combining this with
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)=\mathsf{proj}(R)
, we get that
R
is virtually Gorenstein if and only if
\underline{\mathsf{GProj}}(R)=0
. The latter is equivalent to
\mathsf{GProj}(R)=\mathsf{Proj}(R)
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. Assume
\mathsf{GProj}(R)=\mathsf{Proj}(R)
. By Proposition
4.10
, we get that
\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\mathsf{inj}(R))=\mathsf{mod}(R)
, and hence
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.65295pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.65295pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.83716pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.78491pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\mathsf{inj}(R))=\mathsf{proj}(R)
. The desired result follows as
(\mathsf{proj}(R),\mathsf{mod}(R))
is a cotorsion pair.

(3)\Rightarrow(2)
. Assume
(\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 7.65295pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.65295pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 10.83716pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.78491pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\Thick}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\mathsf{inj}(R)),\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\mathsf{inj}(R)))

is a cotorsion pair. Combining this with
2.2
, we have
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}=\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\mathsf{inj}(R))
. Thus, the assumption
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)=\mathsf{proj}(R)
will yield that

\mathsf{mod}(R)=\Thick(\mathsf{proj}(R)\cup\mathsf{inj}(R).

By Proposition
4.10
,
\mathsf{GProj}(R)=\mathsf{Proj}(R)
.
∎

5.
Gorensteinness via cotorsion pairs

If
R
is an Iwanaga–Gorenstein ring
R
, then it is known that
(\mathsf{Gproj}(R),{\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))
forms a cotorsion pair in
\mathsf{mod}(R)
; see
2.9
. The main result of this section shows that the converse holds for Cohen–Macaulay local rings; see
Theorem

5.6
. This corresponds to
Theorem

1.3
in the introduction. Before stating the result, we need some preparations.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
S
be a Cohen–Macaulay ring that admits a canonical module
\omega
. Assume that
R
is a noetherian
S
-algebra and that
R
, viewed as an
S
-module, is maximal Cohen–Macaulay. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
.

(2)

(\mathsf{Gproj}(R),{\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))
is a cotorsion pair.

Proof.

The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
is by definition.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Assume
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
. By
Corollary

3.10
,

(\mathsf{Gproj}(R),\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}})

is a cotorsion pair. The desired result follows.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Keep the assumptions as in
Lemma

5.1
. If
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
, then
R
is left weakly Gorenstein.

Proof.

Note that
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R=\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 12.77512pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.2918pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}({\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))
. Thus, we have

\mathsf{Gproj}(R)\subseteq\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R=\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 12.77512pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.2918pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}({\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))\subseteq\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 9.65283pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.65283pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.3723pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.88898pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}({\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))

It follows from Lemma
5.1
that the assumption
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)

implies
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)=\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 9.65283pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.65283pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.3723pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.88898pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{1}}}}}({\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))
. Hence, we conclude from the above inclusions that
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)=\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.05565pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.85739pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.79617pt\mathopen{\vphantom{R}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\perp_{\infty}}}}}R
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. Assume that there is a finitely generated
R
-module
X
with finite injective dimension, and
X_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. If
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
, then
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

By
Corollary

3.2
,
X\in\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}
. Combining this with
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
, we get that
X\in{\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
. Thus,
X
is a finitely generated
R
-module with both finite projective dimension and finite injective dimension. For each prime ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
,
X_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
by assumption. We conclude from this that
X_{\mathfrak{m}}
has finite projective dimension and finite injective dimension over
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
. It follows from a result of Foxby
[
19
, Corollary 4.4]
that
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is Gorenstein. For each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
,
\mathfrak{p}
is contained in a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. Note that
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a prime ideal of
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong(R_{\mathfrak{m}})_{(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{m}})}
. Thus,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein as
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is. We conclude that
R
is Gorenstein. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay ring with a canonical module
\omega
and
\dim(R)<\infty
. If
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
, then
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Since
\dim(R)<\infty
, there is
\id_{R}(\omega)<\infty
; see
2.6
. By definition,
\omega_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a canonical module over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
, and hence
\omega_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
. By
Lemma

5.3
,
R
is Gorenstein.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. If
\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
, then
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Any Cohen–Macaulay local ring admits a nonzero finitely generated module of finite injective dimension; see the discussion following
[
9
, Corollary 9.6.2]
. The desired result now follows from
Lemma

5.3
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. The following are equivalent.

(1)

R
is Gorenstein.

(2)

\mathsf{Gproj}(R)^{\perp_{1}}={\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R)
.

(3)

(\mathsf{Gproj}(R),{\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))
is a cotorsion pair.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
. Since
R
is Gorenstein and
\dim(R)<\infty
, we have
\id_{R}(R)<\infty
. Then
(\mathsf{Gproj}(R),{\mathcal{P}}^{<\infty}(R))
is a cotorsion pair; see
2.9
.

The implication
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
is trivial, and the implication
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
follows from
Corollary

5.5
.
∎
