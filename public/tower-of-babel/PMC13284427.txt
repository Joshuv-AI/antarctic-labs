Polycythemia, a radiologic mimic in plain CT head: A case report
Authors: Jayaj Adhikari, Kushal Poudel, Rashmi Burlakoti, Radhika Thagunna, Shivali Rao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Polycythemia is a condition with abnormal increase in red blood cell mass and number in circulation. Increased attenuation with diffuse involvement of Circle of Willis and cerebral venous sinuses mimicking a contrast enhanced study is one of the early noncontrast CT findings in polycythemia. It can mimic venous sinus thrombosis which is its complication and thus warrants further imaging studies. Further imaging studies are needed for the dense vessel appearance in NCCT where the CT number is above a threshold value.

Introduction

Polycythemia is a hematological condition with increased red blood cell mass in the circulation. Clinically, it can be asymptomatic or present with nonspecific symptoms like dizziness, headache, fatigue as well as symptoms of hyperviscosity like amaurosis fugax, transient ischemic attack, symptoms of venous sinus thrombosis or other thrombotic complications. CT head is not recommended in the workup of polycythemia as such. But the complications of polycythemia like dural venous sinus thrombosis and transient ischemic attack warrants CT head. Diffuse hyperdense vessels is a finding in NCCT head in early polycythemia which can mimic CT of contrast studies. Here we present a case of secondary polycythemia whose unenhanced CT head mimicked the contrast enhanced study.

Case report

A 23-year-old male with no known co-morbidities was brought to our emergency department with gasping and decreased level of consciousness. He had been taking over the counter cough syrups (containing Dextromethorphan, Chlorpheniramine, Phenylephrine) as SOS basis for nonproductive cough that he had been having for 2 days. He was admitted for a similar presentation 6 months back in another hospital and diagnosed as acute liver failure secondary to drug (cough syrup) overdose with aspiration pneumonia. However no documented chronic lung or cardiac diseases were present.

At presentation, the patient was unresponsive with Glasgow Coma Score (GCS) of E1V2M5, his oxygen saturation was unrecordable (not detectable by pulse oximeter). Then he was managed with positive pressure ventilation, INJ Hydrocortisone 200 mg and INJ MgSO4 2gm. His blood pressure dropped to 90/22 mmHg, and he was started on vasopressors and inotropes. An emergency plain CT of head was done, which showed diffuse hyperdensity along the course of intracranial arteries, veins and sinuses (Figs. 1A, B, C and D). This was interpreted initially as residual contrast from previous imaging study as reliable history not available due to altered consciousness of the patient. Blood investigation reports revealed a high hemoglobin of 21.6 g/dL (Reference value 12-18g/dL), high hematocrit of 61.2% (Reference value 37.5%-45%), elevated total leukocyte count of 16,300/cu.mm (Reference value 4000-11,000 /cu.mm), elevated INR of 2.04 and elevated liver enzymes : ALT 1400 U/L, AST 2033 U/L (Reference value, AST/ALT : 0-50 U/L). Urine analysis for drugs was positive for morphine, tramadol, phencyclidine but he later denied illicit drug use. Eight hours later, the CT head was repeated with angiogram which showed similar findings without obvious abnormality in the cerebral angiogram (Fig. 1E), and also showed the presence of consolidation with ground glass opacities in posterior segment of right upper lobe and superior segment of visualized right lower lobe, patchy consolidation in apicoposterior segment of left upper lobe and dilated main pulmonary artery (34.6 mm) with pulmonary trunk to aortic ratio >1. Further history clarification revealed that no contrast imaging was done prior to presentation. For the findings in NCCT head, final impression of hyperdense vessels due to polycythemia was made. Ophthalmological evaluation showed grade IV papilledema with blurring and elevation of disc margin. However, the patient couldn’t undergo MRI of the brain with MR venography and other investigations due to financial constraints. Follow-up investigation after correction of dehydration with IV fluid showed a drop in hemoglobin levels to 18.8 g/dL and hematocrit to 55.4%. Follow-up plain CT head 4 days later showed decrease in the attenuation of the intracranial vessels (Fig. 1F). Discussion

Polycythemia, also known as erythrocytosis, is a hematological condition with increased red blood cell mass which appears on laboratory investigation as increased hemoglobin levels and hematocrit [1]. Polycythemia can be classified as spurious polycythemia and true polycythemia. Spurious polycythemia is caused by volume contraction whereas true polycythemia is due to absolute increase in RBC mass. True polycythemia is further classified into primary (low EPO levels) and secondary (high EPO levels) polycythemia [1].

Patients usually present with nonspecific symptoms like dizziness, headache, fatigue as well as symptoms of hyperviscosity like amaurosis fugax, transient ischemic attack, symptoms of venous sinus thrombosis or other thrombotic complications [1]. Loss of consciousness as a presentation of polycythemia has not been reported, albeit our case presented with loss of consciousness which was probably due to drug intoxication.

Imaging studies of an unconscious patient prioritizes CT of the brain to rule out structural pathology, intracranial hemorrhage or stroke [2]. Noncontrast CT head findings of a patient with polycythemia includes but is not limited to increased attenuation of cerebral arteries, veins and dural sinuses (mimicking dural venous thrombosis) [3]. CT values of the blood pool on noncontrast-enhanced scans are linearly correlated with serum hematocrit levels [4].

There is a linear relationship between hemoglobin and the attenuation value of dural sinuses, suggesting that increased attenuation value of cerebral vasculature on NCCT is a sign of increased hemoglobin level. Flowing blood at a hematocrit of 45% measures approximately 40HU. Cerebral vasculature appears isodense to minimally hyperdense in NCCT scan [3]. A study from Black et al. [5] shows the strong correlation of hematocrit and hemoglobin levels with CT attenuation numbers of blood in dural venous sinus. The CT number of 70 has been suggested as a cutoff for the further workup of venous sinus thrombosis. With CT attenuation values higher than 70 HU indicating high likelihood for thrombosis and needs further imaging studies for confirmation.

Other differential diagnoses for similar CT appearance in this age group are diffuse brain edema, dural venous thromboses and acute subarachnoid or subdural hemorrhage [6].

Out of these differentials, dural venous sinus thromboses might be difficult to differentiate in NCCT alone. The involvement of vessels in dural venous sinus thrombosis is nondiffuse and selective giving the infamous “cord sign” [6]. Also, dural venous sinus thrombosis being a complication of polycythemia, might coexist. Follow up imaging with CT venography, MR venography or digital subtraction angiography is needed to differentiate those entities [7].

Our patient has polycythemia secondary to dehydration. However, the urine studies have detected significant levels of morphine, tramadol, phencyclidine. The role of drugs in determining attenuation values of blood in CT has not been studied yet. This article aims to welcome new research insights into the studies on the effect of drugs in blood CT attenuation numbers. Via this case report we would also like to emphasize that although a rare finding, polycythemia and its complication dural venous sinus thrombosis be kept in differential diagnosis for diffusely increased attenuation of vasculature in noncontrast CT study as the diagnostic dilemma might be detrimental to the patient.

Conclusion

Diffuse hyperdensity of cerebral vessels can be due to various causes. Polycythemia should be one of them in differentials. Polycythemia, albeit rare, can confuse the radiologists for diagnosis of dural venous sinus thrombosis and leftover contrast from previous studies. Follow up scan with CT venography, MR venography or digital subtraction angiography should be done to rule out the condition which can have grave consequences for the patient if missed.
