Compositional Dynamics in Learning and Mechanics
David I. Spivak

Abstract
We give a single compositional setting in which gradient-based learning and Hamiltonian-style mechanics appear as functorial semantics. The syntax is an operad Arr Sm whose objects are input-output interfaces (pairs of manifolds) and whose morphisms are smooth adaptive arrangements , which consist of a reactive parameter space, a lens given by smooth output and input maps, and a real-valued potential. The main technical result of the paper is what we call lens internalization , a lax symmetric monoidal functor Lens 𝒞 → 𝒞 {\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{C} associated to any symmetric monoidal closed category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . Using it, we provide two functors Φ phase , Φ conf : Arr Sm → PC {\hyperlink{term.Phiphase}{{\Phi_{\mathrm{phase}}}}},{\hyperlink{term.Phiconf}{{\Phi_{\mathrm{conf}}}}}\colon{\hyperlink{term.srwd}{{\mathbb{A}\mathbf{rr}_{\mathrm{Sm}}}}}\to{\hyperlink{term.pc}{{\mathbb{P}\mathbf{C}}}} into the 2-category of polynomial coalgebras—input-output discrete dynamical systems—which we take as the semantics category. Φ phase stores both position and momentum, whereas Φ conf stores only position. When applied to a parameterized function, Φ conf recovers the gradient descent training algorithm, with backpropagation as the lens’ backward pass. When applied to harmonic particles wired together—in series, or according to any finite directed graph—one diagram yields two different regimes, both of which are governed by the graph Laplacian: Φ phase gives the discrete wave equation, which is conservative and second-order, and Φ conf gives the discrete heat equation, which is dissipative and first-order. They are two semantics of one adaptive arrangement, e.g. with the same potential in each case. And because Arr Sm is an operad, such diagrams nest—larger systems wired from smaller ones—and each semantics assembles a system’s dynamics functorially from its parts. These dynamics are moreover executable: a parameterized neural network and a graph of particles both compile, by the same construction, to explicit state machines one can run.

Chapter 0
Introduction

There is a commonality in Hamiltonian dynamics and gradient descent, namely a map
T^{*}M\to TM
from a cotangent bundle to a tangent bundle, transforming covectors into vectors that change the state. In each case, the covector it transforms is the differential
dU
of a potential
U
—the potential energy in Hamiltonian dynamics or the loss in gradient descent—so it is the potential that drives the state. We refer to vector spaces with this sort of covector-to-vector structure as
reactive
. In Hamiltonian dynamics, the reactive structure is the
symplectic sharp
map on phase space
[
cannasdasilva2008lectures
, §§18.1–18.2]
; in gradient descent it is the Riemannian metric, which determines the learning rate on parameter space. In both cases, the dynamics is compositional: local subsystems—each with their own dynamics—exchange information through which their dynamics assemble into an integrated system.

The goal of this paper is to build a compositional syntax for such smooth reactive, adaptive systems—one in which the couplings between subsystems can themselves adapt—and a functorial semantics that turns this syntax into explicit discrete update rules.

From smooth data to dynamics

The bridge from manifolds to polynomial functors is the cotangent functor

For a polynomial

is a set and

f_{1}\colon S\to p(1),\qquad f[{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}]\colon\prod_{s:S}S^{p[f_{1}(s)]},

(6)

where

returns a position and

A morphism

, which is independent of the readout. Such a coalgebra is called an
(A,B)
-

[

]

A

]

[
jacobs2017introduction
]

The monoidal 2-category
PC
of polynomial coalgebras

The semantics category of this paper, called
\mathbb{O}\mathbf{rg}
in
[
spivak2021learners
,
shapiro2022dynamic
]
, arises naturally from a simple categorical construction:
change of base of enrichment
along a lax monoidal functor.
3
3

3

I thank Maximilien Péroux for pointing out that this construction is a change of base of enrichment.

Recall that for any monoidal category
(\mathcal{V},I,\otimes)
, a
\mathcal{V}
-category

\mathcal{C}
has objects as usual, but instead of having sets of morphisms, it has
\mathcal{V}
-objects:
\mathcal{C}(c_{1},c_{2})\in\ob\mathcal{V}
. The identities and compositions are given by maps

I\to\mathcal{C}(c,c)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{C}(c_{1},c_{2})\otimes\mathcal{C}(c_{2},c_{3})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}(c_{1},c_{3})

in
\mathcal{V}
, which satisfy unitality and associativity equations in
\mathcal{V}
. For example, any monoidal closed category—having an internal hom
[{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}},{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}]
—is self-enriched:
\mathcal{C}(c,c^{\prime})\coloneqq[c,c^{\prime}]\in\ob\mathcal{C}
and there are natural maps
I\to[c,c]
and
[c_{1},c_{2}]\otimes[c_{2},c_{3}]\to[c_{1},c_{3}]
that satisfy unitality and associativity.

The category of
\mathcal{V}
-categories is denoted
\mathcal{V}\textnormal{-}{\hyperlink{term.smcat}{{\mathbf{Cat}}}}
. For any lax monoidal functor
F\colon\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{W}
, there is an induced functor
F_{*}\colon\mathcal{V}\textnormal{-}{\hyperlink{term.smcat}{{\mathbf{Cat}}}}\to\mathcal{W}\textnormal{-}{\hyperlink{term.smcat}{{\mathbf{Cat}}}}
that carries each
\mathcal{V}
-enriched category to a
\mathcal{W}
-enriched category with the same objects, applying
F
to the hom-objects and transporting the identities and composites along the lax structure maps of
F
. When
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}
are symmetric monoidal and
F
is symmetric lax monoidal,
F_{*}
carries monoidal (resp. monoidal closed)
\mathcal{V}
-categories to monoidal (resp. monoidal closed)
\mathcal{W}
-categories.

For a polynomial
q
, let
q\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Coalg}
denote the category of
q
-coalgebras
(S,f\colon S\to q(S))
and their morphisms; the assignment
q\mapsto q\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Coalg}
is a lax symmetric monoidal functor, which we write

\text{{Coalg}}\colon({\hyperlink{term.poly}{{\mathbf{Poly}}}},{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}},\otimes)\to({\hypertarget{term.smcat}{{\mathbf{Cat}}}},1,\times).

Indeed, given
S{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}^{S}\to p
and
T{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}^{T}\to q
, one obtains
ST{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}^{ST}\cong S{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}^{S}\otimes T{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}^{T}\to p\otimes q
.

Since
({\hyperlink{term.poly}{{\mathbf{Poly}}}},{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}},\otimes,[{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}},{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}])
is monoidal closed (

1
), it is self-enriched and since

-enriched category

{\hypertarget{term.pc}{{\mathbb{P}\mathbf{C}}}}\coloneqq\text{{Coalg}}_{*}{\hyperlink{term.poly}{{\mathbf{Poly}}}}.

Its objects are polynomials and its hom-objects are

{\hyperlink{term.pc}{{\mathbb{P}\mathbf{C}}}}(p,p^{\prime})\coloneqq[p,p^{\prime}]\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Coalg}.

The identity

in

is the one-state coalgebra

.

This 2-category has been discussed under the name

Proposition 2.7

We often elide the difference between

as a monoidal 2-category and

{\hyperlink{term.pc}{{\mathbb{P}\mathbf{C}}}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{K};p^{\prime})\coloneqq[p_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes p_{K},p^{\prime}]\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Coalg}.

Another instance of the construction

It is the wide sub-

-category of

The Para construction

Recall that an
action
of a symmetric monoidal category
(\mathcal{A},I,\otimes)
on a category
\mathcal{D}
is a functor
(\cdot)\colon\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
equipped with coherent natural isomorphisms
I\cdot d\cong d
and
(a\otimes b)\cdot d\cong a\cdot(b\cdot d)
; such data is also called an
\mathcal{A}
-actegory

[
capucci2024actegories
]
. When
\mathcal{D}
too is symmetric monoidal and the action functor
(\cdot)
is itself strong monoidal,
\mathcal{D}
is called a
monoidal
\mathcal{A}
-actegory

[
capucci2024actegories
, Def. 5.1.1]
.

Lemma 4.1

(Strong monoidal functors give actions)
.

Any strong monoidal functor
J\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{D}
with
\mathcal{D}
symmetric monoidal induces an action of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{D}
, making
\mathcal{D}
a monoidal
\mathcal{A}
-actegory, by left multiplication,

a\cdot d\coloneqq J(a)\otimes d.

We use the following form of the Para construction. If a symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{A}
acts on a category
\mathcal{D}
(
Section

4
), the category
{\hypertarget{term.Para}{{\mathbb{P}\mathbf{ara}}}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D})
has the same objects as
\mathcal{D}
, and a morphism
d_{1}\to d_{2}
in
{\hyperlink{term.Para}{{\mathbb{P}\mathbf{ara}}}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D})
is a pair
(a,f)
with
a:\mathcal{A}
and

f\colon a\cdot d_{1}\to d_{2}

in
\mathcal{D}
. We call such morphisms
parameterized maps
in
\mathcal{D}
. The identity on
d_{1}
has parameter
I
and action given by the unitor
I\cdot d_{1}\cong d_{1}
. The composite of
(a,f)\colon d_{1}\to d_{2}
and
(b,g)\colon d_{2}\to d_{3}
has parameter
b\otimes a
and uses

(b\otimes a)\cdot d_{1}\cong b\cdot(a\cdot d_{1})\xrightarrow{b\cdot f}b\cdot d_{2}\xrightarrow{g}d_{3},

i.e.
(b,g)\circ(a,f)=(b\otimes a,g\circ(b\cdot f))
.

(Para of a monoidal actegory

\mathcal{A}
-actegory.

f^{-}\colon c^{+}\otimes d^{-}\to c^{-}
any map in
\mathcal{C}
, with identity and composition as above.

It is symmetric monoidal with unit
\binom{I}{I}
and

\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}\boxtimes\binom{d^{-}}{d^{+}}\coloneqq\binom{c^{-}\otimes d^{-}}{c^{+}\otimes d^{+}}.

Our main example will be
\mathcal{C}={\hyperlink{term.mfd}{{\mathbf{Mfd}}}}
with its cartesian product. But the following is simple, instructive, and will come in handy.

Example 1.2
.

The category
{\hypertarget{term.finset}{{\mathbf{Fin}}}}^{\textnormal{op}}
, opposite to the category of finite sets, is cartesian monoidal where the product is given by the disjoint union
(0,+)
, i.e. the coproduct in
Fin
. Thus every object carries a canonical comonoid structure
A\leftarrow 0
and
A\leftarrow A+A
, and every morphism preserves it automatically.

An interface in
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{{\hyperlink{term.finset}{{\mathbf{Fin}}}}^{\textnormal{op}}}
is just a pair of finite sets
\binom{A^{-}}{A^{+}}
. A morphism
\binom{f^{-}}{f^{+}}\colon\binom{A^{-}}{A^{+}}\to\binom{B^{-}}{B^{+}}
consists of two maps, together sometimes called a
prism
:

A^{+}\xleftarrow{f^{+}}B^{+}\qquad\text{and}\qquad A^{+}+B^{-}\xleftarrow{f^{-}}A^{-},

(2)

with no further homomorphism condition, as it is automatic.

Proposition 1.3
.

The
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}}
construction is functorial in symmetric monoidal categories and strong monoidal functors.

Proof 1.4
.

If
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is colax monoidal, it preserves comonoid structures, so we can define
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}
on objects. If
F
is lax monoidal, it has a productor
F_{2}\colon F(A)\otimes F(B)\to F(A\otimes B)
from which we can define
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}
on morphisms: given a lens morphism
\binom{f^{-}}{f^{+}}
we set
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\binom{f^{-}}{f^{+}}\coloneqq\binom{F(f^{-})\circ F_{2}}{F(f^{+})}
. So if
F
is strong, we can define
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}
on objects and morphisms and the invertibility of the coherence maps assures functoriality; we leave the details to the reader.

Example 1.5
.

The functor
{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}\colon({\hyperlink{term.smset}{{\mathbf{Set}}}},1,\times)\to({\hyperlink{term.poly}{{\mathbf{Poly}}}},{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}},\otimes)
sending
A\mapsto A{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}
is strong monoidal:
A{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}\otimes B{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}\cong(A\times B){\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}
. As such it induces a strong monoidal functor

{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}{\hyperlink{term.yon}{{\mathcal{y}}}}}\colon{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{{\hyperlink{term.smset}{{\mathbf{Set}}}}}\to{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{{\hyperlink{term.poly}{{\mathbf{Poly}}}}}.

(3)

Since
{\hyperlink{term.finset}{{\mathbf{Fin}}}}^{\textnormal{op}}
is the free cartesian category on one object, any object
M
in a cartesian category
\mathcal{M}
determines a strong monoidal functor
M^{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}\colon{\hyperlink{term.finset}{{\mathbf{Fin}}}}^{\textnormal{op}}\to\mathcal{M}
sending
A\mapsto M^{A}
.

Lemma 1.6
.

For any cartesian category
\mathcal{M}
and object
M:\mathcal{M}
, the functor
M^{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}\colon{\hyperlink{term.finset}{{\mathbf{Fin}}}}^{\textnormal{op}}\to\mathcal{M}
is strong monoidal, hence induces a strong monoidal functor

{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{M^{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}}\colon{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{{\hyperlink{term.finset}{{\mathbf{Fin}}}}^{\textnormal{op}}}\to{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{M}}.

2

Backward monads on lenses

Our potentials will arise from a commutative monoid, via the following standard fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a symmetric monoidal category and
(R,0,+)
is a commutative monoid object in it, then
R\otimes{\mathord{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfont\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfonts\char 104\relax}}{\mbox{\MnSyCfontss\char 104\relax}}\mkern 2.0mu}}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
carries a monoidal monad structure, functorially in
(\mathcal{C},R)
.

Proof 2.2
.

The monad unit and multiplication are
0\otimes\mathrm{id}
and
(+)\otimes\mathrm{id}
. The monoidal structure has unitor
0\colon I\to R\cong R\otimes I
and productor

(R\otimes X)\otimes(R\otimes Y)\cong R\otimes R\otimes X\otimes Y\xrightarrow{(+)\otimes\mathrm{id}}R\otimes X\otimes Y.

(6)

The coherence diagrams arise from the monoid laws and commutativity of
R
. Functoriality in
(\mathcal{C},R)
is straightforward.

We will add a notion of potentials to our lenses by proving a more general result: any monad-with-strength on
\mathcal{C}
induces a comonad on
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}
by applying it to the input / backward-pass part. We pun on the word “backwards” to refer to the fact that it applies to the backward pass and the fact that, as such, it turns a monad on
\mathcal{C}
into a comonad on
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Recall that a
strength
on a monad
(\text{{T}},\eta,\mu)
on a monoidal category
(\mathcal{C},I,\otimes)
is a natural map
\sigma\colon X\otimes\text{{T}}Y\to\text{{T}}(X\otimes Y)
satisfying four diagrams
[
kock1972strong
]
; a monad equipped with one is a
monad-with-strength
. The monad is
monoidal
if
T
is lax monoidal (with unitor
\tau_{0}\colon I\to\text{{T}}(I)
and productor
\tau_{X,Y}\colon\text{{T}}(X)\otimes\text{{T}}(Y)\to\text{{T}}(X\otimes Y)
) and whose natural transformations are monoidal. Each monoidal monad carries a canonical strength, with
\sigma_{X,Y}\colon X\otimes\text{{T}}Y\to\text{{T}}(X\otimes Y)
the composite

X\otimes\text{{T}}Y\xrightarrow{\eta_{X}}\text{{T}}(X)\otimes\text{{T}}(Y)\xrightarrow{\tau_{X,Y}}\text{{T}}(X\otimes Y).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(\text{{T}},\eta,\mu,\sigma)
be a monad-with-strength on a symmetric monoidal category
(\mathcal{C},I,\otimes)
. Then there is a comonad
{\hypertarget{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}
on
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}
sending
\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}\mapsto\binom{\text{{T}}c^{-}}{c^{+}}
. We denote its coKleisli category by
{\hypertarget{term.Lcokl}{{\mathbf{Lens}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{{T}}}}}}\coloneqq({\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}})_{{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}}
.

If moreover
T
is a monoidal monad, then
{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}
is a colax monoidal comonad, thus the coKleisli category
({\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{{T}}},I,\otimes)
is naturally monoidal.

Proof 2.4
.

On a lens map
\binom{f^{-}}{f^{+}}
with
f^{-}\colon c^{+}\otimes d^{-}\to c^{-}
, define the forward component of
{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}\binom{f^{-}}{f^{+}}
to be
f^{+}
and the backward component to be the following composite:

c^{+}\otimes\text{{T}}d^{-}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\text{{T}}(c^{+}\otimes d^{-})\xrightarrow{\text{{T}}f^{-}}\text{{T}}c^{-}.

The counit and comultiplication of
{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}
are given by the identity on the forward map and, on the backward map, by the unit and multiplication of the monad, together with the counit
\varepsilon_{c^{+}}
:

c^{+}\otimes c^{-}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{c^{+}}\otimes\mathrm{id}}c^{-}\xrightarrow{\eta}\text{{T}}c^{-},

c^{+}\otimes\text{{T}}\text{{T}}c^{-}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{c^{+}}\otimes\mathrm{id}}\text{{T}}\text{{T}}c^{-}\xrightarrow{\mu}\text{{T}}c^{-}.

We need to prove that
{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}
preserves identity and composition (is a functor) and that the functor is counital and coassociative. These four axioms correspond to the four axioms of a monad strength.

We include only the least trivial here: preservation of composition. For

and
g\colon\binom{d^{-}}{d^{+}}\to\binom{e^{-}}{e^{+}}
, it suffices to show that the following commutes (at which point we post-compose with
\text{{T}}f^{-}\colon\text{{T}}(c^{+}\otimes d^{-})\to\text{{T}}c^{-}
to obtain equality of maps

):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{+}\otimes\text{{T}}e^{-}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(c^{+})^{\otimes 2}\otimes\text{{T}}e^{-}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{+}\otimes d^{+}\otimes\text{{T}}e^{-}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{+}\otimes\text{{T}}(d^{+}\otimes e^{-})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{+}\otimes\text{{T}}d^{-}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{{T}}(c^{+}\otimes e^{-})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{{T}}((c^{+})^{\otimes 2}\otimes e^{-})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{{T}}(c^{+}\otimes d^{+}\otimes e^{-})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{{T}}(c^{+}\otimes d^{-})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{-}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{+}}

\sigma
in its first argument, the middle triangle is the associator axiom of strength
[
kock1972strong

, and the last square is naturality of
\sigma
in its second argument.

When
T
is moreover a monoidal monad, we obtain a colax monoidal structure on
{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}
: the counitor
\binom{\text{{T}}(I)}{I}\to\binom{I}{I}
amounts to
\tau_{0}\colon I\to\text{{T}}I
and the coproductor map
\binom{\text{{T}}(c^{-}\otimes d^{-})}{c^{+}\otimes d^{+}}\to\binom{\text{{T}}(c^{-})\otimes\text{{T}}(d^{-})}{c^{+}\otimes d^{+}}
amounts to
\tau_{c^{-},d^{-}}\colon\text{{T}}(c^{-})\otimes\text{{T}}(d^{-})\to\text{{T}}(c^{-}\otimes d^{-})
.

A map in the coKleisli category
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{{T}}}
is a map of the form
\binom{\text{{T}}c^{-}}{c^{+}}\to\binom{d^{-}}{d^{+}}
in
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}
, i.e.

f^{+}\colon c^{+}\to d^{+}\qquad\text{and}\qquad f^{-}\colon c^{+}\otimes d^{-}\to\text{{T}}c^{-}

where
f^{+}
is a comonoid homomorphism.

Lemma 2.5
.

The construction of
Section

2
is functorial in
(\mathcal{C},\text{{T}})
: a strong symmetric monoidal
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and a morphism of monads-with-strength
\theta\colon F\circ\text{{T}}\Rightarrow\text{{T}}^{\prime}\circ F
induce

{\hypertarget{term.LensMor}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}^{\theta}\colon{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{{T}}}\to{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{\text{{T}}^{\prime}}\;,\qquad\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}\mapsto\binom{F(c^{-})}{F(c^{+})},\qquad\binom{f^{-}}{f^{+}}\mapsto\binom{\theta\circ F(f^{-})\circ F_{2}}{F(f^{+})},

where
F_{2}
is
F

\text{{T}},\text{{T}}^{\prime}
are monoidal monads and
\theta
is a morphism of monoidal monads,
{\hyperlink{term.LensMor}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}^{\theta}
is strong symmetric monoidal.

Proof 2.6
.

By
Section

1
,
F
induces
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\colon{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}\to{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
. By
Section

2
,
T
and
\text{{T}}^{\prime}
induce comonads
{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}
and
{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}^{\prime}}
. The monad morphism
\theta
gives the data necessary to define a natural transformation,

\gamma\colon{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}^{\prime}}\circ{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\Rightarrow{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\circ{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}},

whose component at
\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}
has output map
\gamma_{\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}}^{+}\coloneqq\mathrm{id}_{F(c^{+})}
and input map
\gamma_{\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}}^{-}
given by

F(c^{+})\otimes F(\text{{T}}c^{-})\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{F(c^{+})}\otimes\mathrm{id}}F(\text{{T}}c^{-})\xrightarrow{\theta_{c^{-}}}\text{{T}}^{\prime}F(c^{-}).

Its naturality follows from that of
\theta
and the counit
\varepsilon_{F(c^{+})}
.

We define
{\hyperlink{term.LensMor}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}^{\theta}
on objects by
F\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}\coloneqq\binom{F(c^{-})}{F(c^{+})}
. On morphisms, recall that

{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{{T}}}\left(\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}},\binom{d^{-}}{d^{+}}\right)={\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}},\binom{d^{-}}{d^{+}}\right).

For

{\hyperlink{term.LensMor}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}^{\theta}(f)
to be the element of
{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{\text{{T}}^{\prime}}({\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}},{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\binom{d^{-}}{d^{+}})

{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}^{\prime}}\circ{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}\xrightarrow{\gamma_{\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}}}{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\circ{\hyperlink{term.LensFun}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}^{\text{{T}}}\binom{c^{-}}{c^{+}}\xrightarrow{{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}(f)}{\hyperlink{term.Lens}{{\mathbf{Lens}}}}_{F}\binom{d^{-}}{d^{+}}.

and its output component is

If the monads are monoidal and

is strong symmetric monoidal.

The following is straightforward.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},1,\times)

.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and

.

Given

When
