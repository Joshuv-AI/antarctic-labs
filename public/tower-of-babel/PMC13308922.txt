A woman in her mid-fifties presented with a decades-long history of a rash on the vulva that began in adolescence. She reported persistent pruritus, burning, and pain of the right labium majus. She denied similar lesions elsewhere on the body. Her medical history included Crohn’s disease diagnosed at age 28, but she noted that the rash had begun more than a decade earlier. Her Crohn’s disease was well controlled with infliximab, but this treatment did not improve the vulvar rash.

A decade before presentation to the vulvar dermatology clinic, a biopsy of the vulva was performed, showing suppurative folliculitis and psoriasiform dermatitis. A repeat biopsy several years later revealed chronic eczematous dermatitis with lympho-eosinophilic infiltrate, features of lichen simplex chronicus (LSC), and impetiginized scale. Over time, she was treated for both psoriasis and LSC with triamcinolone 0.1% cream and betamethasone dipropionate 0.05% ointment, with only temporary relief. Her symptoms recurred frequently and caused significant disruption in her quality of life, including sleep disturbance from nocturnal pruritus.

At her first evaluation in the vulvar dermatology clinic, she continued to describe persistent symptoms. Examination showed a linear, erythematous, and lichenified plaque on the right labium majus extending to the genitocrural crease (Fig. 1).

Linear erythematous plaque localized to the right labium majus and genitocrural crease.

Given the chronic course of her symptoms, the refractory nature of the eruption despite treatment with topical steroids, and the unilateral presentation, she was diagnosed with inflammatory linear verrucous epidermal nevus (ILVEN) of the vulva. She received intralesional triamcinolone on the right labium majus with partial, transient improvement and mild atrophy secondary to the therapy (Fig. 2). Due to a lack of sustained improvement with this approach, she subsequently underwent 2 fractional CO2 laser treatments in 2024 with significant improvement. Following laser therapy, she reported marked reduction in pruritus and pain, with near-complete resolution of nocturnal symptoms, which was sustained at 6-month follow-up. Clinically, the treated area demonstrated decreased erythema and reduced lichenification.

ILVEN is an uncommon skin disorder characterized by intensely pruritic erythematous, scaly papules coalescing into a plaque that follows the lines of Blaschko and typically arises in childhood.1 Vulvar involvement is uncommon and may resemble other chronic inflammatory conditions, including verrucous epidermal nevus, psoriasis dermatitis, LSC, and linear lichen planus.2

Histopathologic differentiation of ILVEN can be challenging, particularly in mucocutaneous sites such as the vulva, where secondary changes from chronic irritation or scratching may obscure characteristic features. The patient’s prior biopsies demonstrated psoriasiform and eczematous changes with lympho-eosinophilic infiltrates and LSC, findings that overlap with the histologic spectrum of ILVEN, which likely contributed to the delayed diagnosis.1

Treatment of ILVEN remains a challenge, as reported therapies including topical agents (tacrolimus, steroids, 5-fluorouracil), systemic options (acitretin, anti-tumor necrosis factor agents such as etanercept and adalimumab), laser modalities (308 nm and CO2 laser), and even surgery for extensive lesions have shown variable and often limited success.3

CO2 lasers have shown effectiveness in treating epidermal nevi, with studies reporting significant lesion size reduction and acceptable safety, though some recurrence, scarring, and hypopigmentation may occur.4 While outcomes for inflammatory linear verrucous epidermal nevi on the trunk, face, and extremities are limited, the CO2 laser is considered a preferred option for lesions in sensitive sites such as the anogenital region.4,5 In this case, treatment was performed using a fractionated CO2 laser set at 20% fractional coverage, utilizing ring mode at 45 mJ (fluence 8.8 J/cm2) and core mode at 60 mJ (fluence 340 J/cm2) during the initial treatment, after which the CO2 laser ring and core mode were then increased to 45.7 mJ and 62 mJ, respectively, at the second session.

This case emphasizes the importance of including ILVEN in the differential diagnosis of chronic vulvar dermatitis, especially when the eruption is longstanding, unilateral, and resistant to topical therapy. Intralesional triamcinolone and fractional CO2 laser may be considered as a therapeutic option in such patients.
