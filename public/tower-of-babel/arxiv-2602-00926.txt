1.
Introduction

The study of differential equations with piecewise constant argument (or DEPCAs, for their acronym in English) was initiated by Wiener
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, Myshkis
[
19
]
, Cooke and Wiener
[
5
]
, and Shah and Wiener
[
24
]
.
The study of DEPCAs is of great applied interest, since a delay provides a mathematical model for a physical or biological system in which the rate of change depends on its past history (see
[
8
,
21
,
22
,
kuo
,
robledo
]
).

A differential equation with piecewise constant argument is a differential equation with a discontinuous argument of the following type:

(1.1)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(t)=f(t,x(t),x([t])),

where
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{q}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
, and
[\cdot]
denotes the floor (integer part) function. Note that the equation is non-autonomous.

DEPCAs are hybrid differential equations, that is, they exhibit the structure of continuous dynamical systems over the intervals
]n,n+1[,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and of discrete dynamical systems over
\mathbb{Z}
, combining the properties of both differential and difference equations.

In what follows, we denote by
|\cdot|
the Euclidean norm.

The following definition is the natural extension of the concept of solution adapted to a DEPCA.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
x:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{q}
is a solution of (
1.1
) if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

x
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

the derivative
x^{\prime}
of
x
exists on
\mathbb{R}

except possibly at points
t=n
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where the one-sided derivatives exist;

(iii)

x
satisfies (
1.1
)
on the interval
(n,n+1)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The main objective of this paper is to establish the existence and uniqueness of
Remotely Almost Periodic (RAP)
solutions for DEPCAs, specifically we consider the equations

(1.2)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(t)z(t)

(1.3)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(t)y(t)+B(t)y([t])

(1.4)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(t)x(t)+B(t)x([t])+f(t),

where
A,B:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M_{q\times q}(\mathbb{R})
are continuous matrices and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{q}
is a bounded uniformly continuous function. In
[
36
]
, the authors seek remotely almost periodic solutions for (
1.4
) with constant matrices
A
and
B
. Additionally, in
[
35
]
, the existence of almost periodic solutions for (
1.4
) is studied.
In this article, we will address the case where
A
and
B
are remotely almost periodic matrices.

It is well known that Sarason defined the notion of a scalar-valued remotely almost periodic function in
[
23
]
. The class of vector-valued remotely almost periodic functions defined on

was introduced by Yang and Zhang in
[

, where the authors have provided several applications in the study of existence and uniqueness of remotely almost periodic solutions for parabolic boundary value problems (for some results about parabolic boundary value problems, one may refer to
[

16

and references cited therein). In Propositions 2.4–2.6 in
[

[
34
,
32
]
, where the authors have investigated almost periodic type solutions and slowly oscillating type solutions for various classes of parabolic Cauchy inverse problems). Concerning applications of remotely almost periodic functions, research articles
[
39
]
by Zhang and Piao, where the authors have investigated the time remotely almost periodic viscosity solutions of Hamilton–Jacobi equations, and
[

by Zhang and Jiang, where the authors have investigated remotely almost periodic solutions for a class of systems of differential equations with piecewise constant argument, should be mentioned; see
[

To better understand the space of Remotely Almost Periodic functions, denoted by
RAP({\mathbb{R}}:{\mathbb{C}}^{q})
, we will recall the notion of a Slowly Oscilating function (the corresponding space is denoted by
SO({\mathbb{R}}:{\mathbb{C}}^{q})
henceforth):
A uniformly continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{q}
is called
slowly oscillating
if and only if for every
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have that

\lim_{|t|\rightarrow+\infty}\|f(t+a)-f(t)\|=0,

where
\|\cdot\|
be a fixed norm in
\mathbb{C}^{q}
.

Now, we recall the notion of a remotely almost periodic function:

Definition 1.2

(
[
18
,
36
]
)
.

A uniformly continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{q}
is called
Remotely Almost Periodic
(RAP, for short) if for every
\epsilon>0
we have that the set

T(f,\epsilon):=\Biggl\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}:\limsup_{|t|\rightarrow+\infty}\|f(t+\tau)-f(t)\|<\epsilon\Biggr\}

is relatively dense in
{\mathbb{R}}.
We denote by
RAP(\mathbb{R}\ :\ \mathbb{C}^{q})
the set of all such functions.

Any number
\tau\in T(f,\epsilon)
is called a remote
\epsilon
-translation number of
f
. We know that
RAP({\mathbb{R}}:{\mathbb{C}}^{q})
is a closed subspace of

BUC({\mathbb{R}}:{\mathbb{C}}^{q})
and therefore the Banach space itself. If the functions
F_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,F_{k}
are remotely almost periodic (
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
), then for each
\epsilon>0
the set of their common
remote
\epsilon
-translation number is relatively dense in
{\mathbb{R}};
see e.g.,
[
37

.

2.
Preliminars on Remotely Almost Periodic Sequences

Analogously to remotely almost periodic functions, we will make a brief analysis of remotely almost periodic sequences. Throughout this paper, we use the variable
t
for continuous time (
t\in\mathbb{R}
) and
n
for discrete time (
n\in\mathbb{Z}
). When a function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
appears as
f(n)
, it is understood that we consider the sequence
\{f(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
obtained by restricting
f
to the integers.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
x:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a bounded sequence. We say that
x
is:

(i)

Slowly Oscillating
if for each
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lim_{\left|n\right|\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x\left(n+a\right)-x\left(n\right)\right\|=0.

We denote the set of such sequences by
\mathcal{SO}\left(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
.

(ii)

Almost Periodic
if for every
\epsilon>0
, the set

T\left(x,\epsilon\right)=\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{Z}\,\middle|\,\left\|x\left(n+\tau\right)-x\left(n\right)\right\|<\epsilon,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

is relatively dense in
\mathbb{Z}
. The number
\tau\in T\left(x,\epsilon\right)
is called an
\epsilon
-almost period
. We denote the set of such sequences by
AP\left(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
.

(iii)

Remotely Almost Periodic
if for every
\epsilon>0
, the set

T\left(x,\epsilon\right)=\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{Z}\,\middle|\,\limsup_{\left|n\right|\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x\left(n+\tau\right)-x\left(n\right)\right\|<\epsilon\right\}

is relatively dense in
\mathbb{Z}
. The number
\tau\in T\left(x,\epsilon\right)
is called a
remote
\epsilon
-translation number
. We denote the set of such sequences by
RAP\left(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
.

An example of a sequence
x\in RAP(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R})
is

\displaystyle x\left(n\right)=\cos\left(n\right)+\cos\left(\sqrt{2}n\right)+\sin(n+\sqrt{|n|})+\frac{3n^{2}}{n^{2}+1}.

We now state the following theorem to relate a remotely almost periodic sequence to a remotely almost periodic function.

Theorem 2.1
.

A necessary and sufficient condition for a bounded sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
to be remotely almost periodic is the existence of a function
f\in RAP\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
such that
f(n)=u_{n},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

As
RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a closed subalgebra of
BUC(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
generated by
AP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
SO(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
we know that given
\epsilon>0
, there exist
g_{1},\,g_{2}\in AP\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)

and
\varphi_{1},\,\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{SO}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)

such that

\left\|f-\left[g_{1}+\varphi_{1}+g_{2}\varphi_{2}\right]\right\|_{\infty}<\epsilon/4.

By Theorem 1.7.3 in
[
38
]
,
g_{1}(n),g_{2}(n)\in AP(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Let
\epsilon^{\prime}=\min\left\{\epsilon/4,\epsilon/\left(4\left\|\varphi_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\right\}

and let
\tau
be an
\epsilon^{\prime}
-translation number for
g_{1}(n)
and
g_{2}(n)
.
We will show that if
\tau
is an
\epsilon^{\prime}/2
-remote translation number
for
g_{1}(n)+\varphi_{1}(n)+g_{2}(n)\varphi_{2}(n)
,
then it is a remote
\epsilon
-translation number for
f(n)=u_{n}
. Since

\|f(n+\tau)-f(n)\|\leq\epsilon/2+(1+\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\infty})\epsilon^{\prime}+\|\Delta_{\tau}\varphi_{1}(n)\|+\|g_{2}\|_{\infty}\|\Delta_{\tau}\varphi_{2}(n)\|,

it follows that

\limsup_{\left|n\right|\rightarrow\infty}\|f(n+\tau)-f(n)\|<\epsilon.

This shows that
T\left(f(n),\epsilon\right)
is relatively dense in
\mathbb{Z}
and
f(n)
is remotely almost periodic.

Now for the converse, assume that
u_{n}
is a remotely almost periodic sequence.
Define the function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
by

f(t)=u_{n}+(t-n)(u_{n+1}-u_{n}),\quad\text{for }n\leq t<n+1,\;n\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is clear that
f(n)=u_{n}
. We will show that
f\in RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
;
we only need to show that
T(u_{n},\epsilon/3)\subset T(f,\epsilon)
.
Let
\tau\in T(u_{n},\epsilon/3)
. If
n\leq t<n+1
, then
n+\tau\leq t+\tau<n+\tau+1
.
Since

f(t+\tau)-f(t)=f(n+\tau)-f(n)+(t-n)\left\{[f(n+\tau+1)-f(n+1)]-[f(n+\tau)-f(n)]\right\},

and
0\leq t-n<1
, we obtain

\limsup_{\left|t\right|\rightarrow\infty}\|f(t+\tau)-f(t)\|<\epsilon.

Thus,
\tau\in T(f,\epsilon)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Given the continuous function
f
, it is evident that the function
f([\cdot])
is discontinuous (unless
f
is a constant function).
From this, we observe that for the function
\varphi\in RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, the function
\varphi([\cdot])
is not remotely almost periodic, since it does not satisfy uniform continuity.
As naturally expected, the discontinuities occur at the integers; furthermore, it is clear that the lateral limits exist for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In
[
36
]
, there is no precise definition of what is meant by remotely almost periodicity of the possibly discontinuous function
f(\cdot,x(\cdot),x([\cdot]))
, which leads to some inaccuracies.

Analogous to the concept introduced in the context of almost periodic and almost automorphic functions by E. Ait Dads and L. Lachimi
[
2
]
, and A. Chávez, S. Castillo, and M. Pinto
[
3
]
, respectively,
we will later see that it is sufficient to consider
\mathbb{Z}
Remotely Almost Periodic
functions to study solutions of DEPCAs.

We define a subset of the bounded functions as

\displaystyle BR(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\left|\begin{aligned} \quad f\text{ is bounded, continuous on }\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z},\\
\quad\text{and has finite lateral limits at each point in }\mathbb{Z}\end{aligned}\right.\right\}

Definition 2.2
.

A function
f\in BR(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be
\mathbb{Z}
almost periodic
if there exists a set
T(f,\epsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}
that is relatively dense in
\mathbb{R}
,
and for every
\tau\in T(f,\epsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot+\tau)-f(\cdot)\|_{\infty}<\epsilon.

We denote the set of such functions by
\mathbb{Z}AP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.3
.

A function
f\in BR(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be
\mathbb{Z}
remotely almost periodic
if there exists a relatively dense set
T_{1}(f,\epsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}
such that,
if
\tau\in T_{1}(f,\epsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}
, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\|f(t+\tau)-f(t)\|<\epsilon,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

We denote the set of such functions by
\mathbb{Z}RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The set of functions
\mathbb{Z}AP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

If
f\in\mathbb{Z}\mathrm{AP}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, then the set

T_{1}\left(f,\epsilon\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}=\left\{a\in\mathbb{R}\,\middle|\,\left\|f\left(\cdot+a\right)-f\left(\cdot\right)\right\|_{\infty}<\epsilon\right\}\cap\mathbb{Z}

is relatively dense in
\mathbb{R}
.
Since
T_{1}\left(f,\epsilon\right)\subset T\left(f,\epsilon\right)
, it follows that

f\in\mathbb{Z}\mathrm{RAP}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
∎

Remark
.

Every function in
\mathbb{Z}RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
is locally integrable.

Recall the following Lemma which it can be found in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\in AP(\mathbb{R})
. Then
T(f,\epsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq\emptyset
and it is relatively dense.

Using this we can now prove the next Lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\in RAP(\mathbb{R})
. Then
T(f,\epsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq\emptyset
, and it is relatively dense.

Proof.

As
RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
is generated by
AP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
SO(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
, given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\psi\in RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by
\psi(t)=g_{1}(t)+\varphi_{1}(t)+g_{2}(t)\varphi_{2}(t)
,
where
g_{1},g_{2}\in AP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in OS(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|f(t)-\psi(t)\right\|\leq\epsilon/4,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Consider
\tau\in T(g_{1},\epsilon/3)\cap T(g_{2},\epsilon/3)\cap\mathbb{Z}
, with

\epsilon^{\prime}=\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon}{4},\frac{\epsilon}{4\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\infty}}\right\}.

We know this set is nonempty by Lemma
2.3
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|f(t+\tau)-f(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f(t+\tau)-\psi(t+\tau)\|+\|\psi(t+\tau)-\psi(t)\|+\|\psi(t)-f(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon/2+\|\varphi_{1}(t+\tau)-\varphi_{1}(t)\|

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,\,+\|g_{2}\|_{\infty}\|\varphi_{2}(t+\tau)-\varphi_{2}(t)\|+\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\infty}\|g_{2}(t+\tau)-g_{2}(t)\|

Finally,

\displaystyle\limsup_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\|f(t+\tau)-f(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon

Thus, we have that
\tau\in T(f,\epsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq\emptyset
.
∎

If

Proof.

Let us consider

Since
[t+\tau]=[t]+\tau
.
Finally,

\displaystyle\limsup_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\|f([t]+\tau)-f([t])\|

\displaystyle\leq

Therefore,

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f\in RAP(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and assume it is Lipschitz continuous in the second variable with constant
K
.
If
\varphi\in\mathbb{Z}RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

f(\cdot,\varphi(\cdot))\in\mathbb{Z}RAP(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

The proof is straightforward. If we consider
\tau\in T(f,\epsilon^{\prime})\cap T(\varphi,\epsilon^{\prime})\cap\mathbb{Z}
, where
\epsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\epsilon}{1+K}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|f(t+\tau,\varphi(t+\tau))-f(t,\varphi(t))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f(t+\tau,\varphi(t+\tau))-f(t+\tau,\varphi(t))\|

\displaystyle+\|f(t+\tau,\varphi(t))-f(t,\varphi(t))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\|\varphi(t+\tau)-\varphi(t)\|+\|f(t+\tau,\varphi(t))-f(t,\varphi(t))\|

Thus,

\displaystyle\limsup_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\|f(t+\tau,\varphi(t+\tau))-f(t,\varphi(t))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}K\|\varphi(t+\tau)-\varphi(t)\|

4.
Remotely Almost-Periodic Solutions in Difference Equations

Difference equations arise naturally in discrete mathematics and play a key role in various areas,
such as probability, computer science, numerical methods for differential equations,
and control theory (see
[
1
,
15
,
20
]
).
The properties of these equations and their applications to discrete dynamical systems and differential equations
with piecewise constant arguments have been studied recently by several authors.

In this subsection, we study the equations

(4.1)

\displaystyle x(n+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(n)x(n),

(4.2)

\displaystyle y(n+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(n)y(n)+h(n),

where both
C:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
, an invertible square matrix,
and
h:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{q}
are remotely almost-periodic sequences.
The system (
4.2
) is thoroughly studied in
[
10
]
.

Our focus is on equation (
4.2
), since discrete remotely almost-periodic solutions have not been studied in the literature.
The almost-periodic version can be found in
[
38
]
.

In the previous chapter, exponential dichotomy played a key role in the study of remotely almost-periodic solutions in non-autonomous differential equations,
mainly because it allows us to obtain a bounded solution dependent on the Green’s kernel associated with the system.
The following definition describes the discrete version of exponential dichotomy.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that equation (
4.1
) has an
(\alpha,K,P)
-exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}
if there exist positive constants
\alpha
,
K\geq 1
, and a projection
P

(P^{2}=P)
, such that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{G}(n,m)\|\leq Ke^{-\alpha|n-m|},

where
\tilde{G}(n,m)
is the discrete Green’s function given by

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(n,m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Phi\left(n\right)P\Phi^{-1}\left(m\right)&n\geq m\\
-\Phi\left(n\right)\left(I-P\right)\Phi^{-1}\left(m\right)&n<m\end{cases}

where
\Phi(n)
is the fundamental matrix of system (
4.1
) such that
\Phi(0)=I
.

In what follows, we will study the difference equation (
4.2
) and the existence of remotely almost-periodic solutions. We have the following theorem:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
h\in RAP(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{q})
.
Suppose that the difference equation (
4.1
)
has an
(\alpha,K,P)
-exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}
, and that the Green’s function
\tilde{G}(\cdot,\cdot)
is bi-remotely almost-periodic and summable, i.e., for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a relatively dense set
T(\tilde{G},\epsilon)\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that if
\tau\in T(\tilde{G},\epsilon)
, then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{|n|\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\tilde{G}(n+\tau,k+\tau)-\tilde{G}(n,k)\right\|\leq\epsilon.

Then, the difference equation (
4.2
) has a unique remotely almost-periodic solution
y(n)
, given by

\displaystyle y(n)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{G}(n,k)h(k),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover, it is uniformly bounded by

\left\|y\left(n\right)\right\|\leq K\left(1+e^{-\alpha}\right)\left(1-e^{-\alpha}\right)^{-1}\left\|h\right\|_{\infty},\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

In
[
38
, Theorem 5.7]
, it is shown that the unique bounded solution of the discrete equation (
4.2
) is

y(n)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{G}(n,k)h(k).

Therefore, we will only prove that this solution is remotely almost-periodic.

Indeed, let us consider

\displaystyle\|y(n+\tau)-y(n)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{G}(n+\tau,k+\tau)h(k+\tau)-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{G}(n,k)h(k)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(\tilde{G}(n+\tau,k+\tau)-\tilde{G}(n,k))h(k+\tau)\right\|

\displaystyle+\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{G}(n,k)(h(k+\tau)-h(k))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\tilde{G}(n+\tau,k+\tau)-\tilde{G}(n,k)\right\|\left\|h(k+\tau)\right\|

\displaystyle+K\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\alpha\left|n-k\right|}\left\|h(k+\tau)-h(k)\right\|.

Taking advantage of the fact that the Green’s function is bi-remotely almost-periodic and summable,
that the function
h
is remotely almost-periodic, and the exponential dichotomy, we obtain the result.
Let us define

\displaystyle S_{1}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\tilde{G}(n+\tau,k+\tau)-\tilde{G}(n,k)\|\|h(k+\tau)\|

\displaystyle S_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\alpha|n-k|}\|h(k+\tau)-h(k)\|.

We know that
\|h\|_{\infty}<M
. Let us study only the case when
n\rightarrow\infty
. The case
n\rightarrow-\infty
is analogous.

Given
\epsilon>0
, let us consider

\epsilon^{\prime}=\min\{\epsilon(2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\alpha|n-k|}+4M)^{-1},\epsilon(2M)^{-1}\}
, there exists
n_{1}>0
such that if
\tau\in T(h,\epsilon^{\prime})

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\|h(n+\tau)-h(n)\right\|<\epsilon^{\prime},\ \mathrm{if}\ |n|\geq n_{1}.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{-n_{1}}^{n_{1}}e^{\alpha k}<M_{1},

since it is a finite sum.

Moreover, there exists
n_{2}>0
such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha n}M_{1}<\epsilon^{\prime},\quad\text{if }n\geq n_{2}.

Let
n_{0}=\max\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
. Then, if
n>n_{0}
, for

\displaystyle S_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{-\infty}^{-n_{1}}+\sum_{-n_{1}}^{n_{1}}+\sum_{n_{1}}^{\infty}\right)e^{-\alpha|n-k|}\left\|h(k+\tau)-h(k)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in(-\infty,-n_{1}]}\left\|h(k+\tau)-h(k)\right\|\sum_{-\infty}^{-n_{1}}e^{-\alpha|n-k|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{-n_{1}}^{n_{1}}e^{-\alpha|n-k|}\left\|h(k+\tau)-h(k)\right\|

\displaystyle+\sup_{k\in[n_{1},\infty)}\left\|h(k+\tau)-h(k)\right\|\sum_{n_{1}}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha|n-k|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\epsilon^{\prime}\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha|n-k|}+2M\sum_{-n_{1}}^{n_{1}}e^{-\alpha(n-k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\epsilon^{\prime}\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha|n-k|}+2Me^{-\alpha n}\sum_{k=-n_{1}}^{n_{1}}e^{\alpha k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}

From (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we have that

S_{2}(n)<\frac{\epsilon}{2},\quad|n|>n_{0}.

We then conclude that

\displaystyle\limsup_{|n|\rightarrow\infty}S_{2}(n)<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Then, if we consider
\tau\in T(h,\epsilon^{\prime})\cap T(\tilde{G},\epsilon^{\prime})\cap\mathbb{Z}
, we have for (
4
)

\displaystyle S_{1}(n)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|\tilde{G}(n+\tau,k+\tau)-\tilde{G}(n,k)\|.

Using that the Green’s kernel satisfies (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{|n|\rightarrow\infty}S_{1}(n)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\limsup_{|n|\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|\tilde{G}(n+\tau,k+\tau)-\tilde{G}(n,k)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}

Thus, finally we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y(n+\tau)-y(n)\right\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\epsilon,

That is,
y
is a remotely almost-periodic function.
This concludes the proof.
∎

7.
Lasota-Wazewska Model

The Lasota-Wazewska Model is an autonomous differential equation of the form

(7.1)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)=-\delta y(t)+pe^{-\gamma y(t-\tau)},\ t\geq 0.

It was used by Wazewska-Czyzewska and Lasota

In this equation,
y(t)
represents the number of red blood cells in the blood at time
t

\delta>0

p,\gamma
are positive constants related to the production of red blood cells per unit of time,
and
\tau
is the time required to produce red blood cells.

Equation (
7.1
) models various real-life situations; see
[
17
]
.

In
[
9
]
, a variation of this model is proposed by modifying the delay

(7.2)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)=-\delta y(t)+pe^{-\gamma y([t])},\ t\geq 0.

We will study the following delayed model:

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)=-\delta(t)\,y(t)+p(t)\,f(y([t])),

where
\delta(\cdot)
,
p(\cdot)
are positive remotely almost-periodic functions,
and

is a positive function that satisfies a
\gamma
-Lipschitz condition; that is, the following holds:

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\gamma|x-y|,\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

We will assume the following condition:

(LW.1)
