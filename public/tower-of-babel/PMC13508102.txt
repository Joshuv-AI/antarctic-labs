Photochemical Synthesis of β‑N-Heterocycle Alcohols via a Coordination-Induced 1,5-Boronate Sigmatropic Rearrangement
Authors: Peng Zou, Dongmin Fu, Jihao Jin, Chenyang Xu, Ruijing Cai, Shi-Jun Li, Yi Zeng, Yu Lan, Yiyun Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Excited-State Intramolecular Proton Transfer (ESIPT) is an ultrafast nonradiative relaxation pathway that frequently dominates the photophysical fate of conjugated enols, often precluding productive photochemical transformations. Here, we report that bidentate boronate coordination effectively suppresses this process, locking β-N-heterocycle enols into a rigid configuration to enable an efficient photochemical synthesis of β-N-heterocycle alcohols via a 1,5-boronate sigmatropic rearrangement. This strategy circumvents the inherent challenges of hydroxyl acidity and low π-electrophilicity characteristic of the ground-state enol manifold. Operating under mild, metal- and base-free conditions, this protocol facilitates the assembly of 61 diverse aromatic and nonaromatic β-N-heterocycle alcohols (up to 81% yield) using sp 3-, sp 2-, and sp-hybridized organoboronic acids. The methodology exhibits high chemo- and regioselectivityuniquely favoring 1,5-migration to the enol α-carbon over 1,3-addition to the imineand is well-suited for the late-stage functionalization of complex pharmaceuticals. Comprehensive mechanistic studies, integrating picosecond transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy with DFT modeling, identify a discrete excited-state triplet enol as the pivotal intermediate. Crucially, our findings demonstrate that these boronate–enol photochemical transformations proceed via concerted sigmatropic pathways rather than previously assumed photoactivated nucleophilic additions. This work establishes a robust platform for exploiting coordination-induced kinetic control to modulate photochemical outcomes in enol-based substrates.

Introduction

β-N-Heterocyclic ketones are ubiquitous motifs in medicinal chemistry, serving as essential precursors to bioactive molecules that features β-N-heterocycle tertiary alcohol moieties, including the antifungal agent Quilseconazole and various interleukin-1 receptor antagonists (Figure A). However, the direct synthesis of these β-N-heterocycle alcohols from their parent ketones faces a significant reactivity constraints rooted in the thermodynamic stability of their enol tautomers. The inherent acidity of the enol hydroxyl group, combined with the remarkably low π-electrophilicity of the conjugated system, generally precludes direct nucleophilic addition at the enol α-carbon. Consequently, these enols are often biased toward β-carbon functionalization, yielding substituted ketones rather than the high-value β-N-heterocycle alcohol targets.

Traditional strategies to overcome this bias involve the use of Lewis acids to stabilize the ketone moiety, or the employment of potent organometallic nucleophiles (e.g., Grignard or organolithium reagents) to trap transient ketone species (Figure B). However, such approaches frequently necessitate cryogenic temperatures and often exhibit restricted functional group tolerance due to the inherently harsh reaction conditions. While organoboronic acids are versatile alternatives for C–C bond formation, their application to β-N-heterocyclic enols remains relatively limited. Previous efforts in photochemical boronate rearrangement to ketones or imines have expanded the scope of these transformations; yet, they have largely been categorized as photoactivated nucleophilic additionsa classification that may not fully account for the reactivity of the predominant enol tautomer, which lacks traditional electrophilic character.

We hypothesized that the latent reactivity of these enols could be assessed by managing their photophysical relaxation. Specifically, we questioned whether boronate coordination could be leveraged to suppress Excited-State Intramolecular Proton Transfer (ESIPT) of enolsthe ultrafast (femtosecond to picosecond) nonradiative process that typically dominates the photophysical fate of conjugated enols (vide infra). By implementing bidentate coordination to the N-heterocycle, we envisioned locking the β-N-heterocycle enol substrate into a rigid enol configuration, thereby bypassing ESIPT and redirecting the excited-state energy toward a concerted 1,5-boronate sigmatropic rearrangement (Figure B).

Herein, we report the visible-light-driven 1,5-boronate sigmatropic rearrangement of β-N-heterocycle enols. Operating under mild, metal- and base-free conditions, this protocol provides a direct, sigmatropic route to over 60 diverse β-N-heterocycle alcohols (Figure C). Beyond its broad synthetic utility, this work couples picosecond transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy with DFT calculations to elucidate the underlying mechanism of boronate–enol photochemistry. Crucially, we demonstrate that these transformations proceed via a discrete excited-state triplet concerted pathway, offering a distinct alternative to classical paradigms of organoboronate reactivity.

Results and Discussion

Discovery of Photochemical 1,5-Boronate Rearrangement

We previously described a 1,3-boronate rearrangement directed by o-hydroxy acetophenone imines, wherein the phenolic hydroxyl group facilitates boronate chelation and subsequent migration to the ketimine center (Table A). Given the structural parallels between o-hydroxy imines and β-N-heterocyclic enols, we questioned whether an enol system could similarly direct a boronate rearrangement, potentially mimicking the reactivity patterns observed in 1,3-dicarbonyl systems. To test this hypothesis, thiazolinyl enol 1 was synthesized as a model substrate. 1H NMR analysis confirmed that 1 exists exclusively as the enol tautomer in solution, with no detectable ketone isomer (see Supporting Information).

Mixing enol 1 with cyclohexyl boronic acid 2 resulted in a distinct bathochromic shift in the UV–vis absorption spectrum, indicating the formation of a rigid boronate-enol complex. This chelation appears to serve a dual function: it lowers the excitation energy into the visible range (427 nm) and significantly restricts the internal degrees of freedom of the substrate. We hypothesized that this restriction would suppress the ultrafast ESIPT typically observed in free enols, effectively locking the molecule in a reactive enol configuration throughout the excited-state manifold.

Subsequent irradiation of the in situ mixture with 427 nm blue light afforded the β-thiazolinyl alcohol 4 in 60% isolated yield. Notably, the anticipated 1,3-ketimine addition product 3the expected outcome of a conventional photoactivated nucleophilic additionwas not observed. This regiochemical divergence is highly significant; it represents a 1,5-sigmatropic carbo-migration to the enol α-carbon, challenging the conventional assumption that such migrations are restricted to the traditionally electrophilic imine site under ground-state conditions. Optimization studies revealed that DMSO provided the optimal environment for this transformation in the absence of any additive. Control experiments confirmed the absolute necessity of light, as thermal activation (80 °C) resulted in no conversion, further supporting a photochemical rather than a ground-state pathway (Table B).

Expansion to Oxazoline Enols and Stereochemical Probes

To further evaluate the generality of the 1,5-migration, we prepared oxazoline enols 5a–6a as alternative N-heterocyclic scaffolds. Upon complexation with cyclohexyl boronic acid 2, these substrates underwent efficient 1,5-alkyl migration to afford alcohols 5–6 in 69–70% yields. Notably, significant remote diastereoselectivity was observed (5, 2:1 d.r.; 6, 85:15 d.r.). This degree of stereocontrol across a 1,5-systemwhere the inducing stereocenter and the forming C–C bond is separated by multiple atomsprovides preliminary evidence for a concerted, intramolecular pathway. Such stereochemical integrity is inconsistent with a stepwise radical dissociation–recombination mechanism, which would typically involve loss of diastereomeric purity through the formation of planar or rapidly inverting radical intermediates.

Broad Applicability across N-Heterocycle Enols

To evaluate the generality of the 1,5-boronate sigmatropic rearrangement, we investigated a structurally diverse library of β-substituted aromatic N-heterocycle enols (Figure ). A defining feature of this photochemical protocol is its exceptional chemoselectivity. The reaction tolerated a wide array of sensitive functional groups, including aryl halides, nitriles, trifluoromethyl groups, and esters, providing the corresponding products (14–25) in 60–75% yields. Notably, preservation of electrophilic nitriles and esters under these conditions highlights a significant advantage over traditional organometallic strategies. While conventional organometallic nucleophiles (e.g. organolithium or Grignard reagents) trigger unavoidable side reactions at these electrophilic centers even under cryogenic temperatures, this metal- and base-free approach proceeds with high fidelity at room temperature. Furthermore, pyridyl- and thiophenyl-substituted enols (26a–27a) participated effectively (55–81% yields), demonstrating that additional Lewis-basic sites do not interfere with the critical bidentate boronate chelation required for the 1,5-migration.

Scalability and Downstream Transformations

The synthetic robustness of the 1,5-boronate rearrangement was further demonstrated through a gram-scale synthesis. The reaction of 10a and 2 was scaled to 5.0 mmol, providing β-benzothiazolyl alcohol 10 in 62% yield (1.03 g) without a loss in efficiency. The resulting β-N-heterocycle alcohols serve as versatile synthetic handles. For example, the benzothiazole moiety in 10 was converted into aldehyde 28 in a one-pot sequence (61% yield), offering a streamlined route to functionalized β-hydroxy aldehydes. Additionally, 10 was smoothly hydrolyzed to lactate 29 (91% yield) or reduced to 1,2-diol 30 (86% yield). These transformations underscore the utility of the N-heterocycle as a “masked” functional group, allowing for the rapid assembly of complex 1,3-difunctionalized motifs.

Broad Compatibility with sp 3, sp 2, and sp carbo-Groups

The versatility of the 1,5-boronate sigmatropic rearrangement was further evaluated across a diverse range of organoboronic acids (Figure ). Primary alkyl boronic acids, including methyl, ethyl, and long-chain aliphatic and benzyl derivatives (31b–38b), underwent efficient photochemical 1,5-migration to yield the corresponding β-N-heterocycle alcohols (31–38) in 55–71% yields. The reaction demonstrated exceptional tolerance for steric bulk: secondary cyclic alkyl boronates (five- to 12-membered rings, 43b–47b) and acyclic variants (48b–50b) consistently afforded products in 63–70% yields. Most notably, the protocol accommodated sterically congested tertiary alkyl boronic acids; the tert-butyl-substituted product 51 (53%) and the highly hindered 52 (61%) were successfully synthesized, even when generating two adjacent quaternary carbon centers.

The utility of this method extends to sp 2-hybridized aryl and alkenyl boronates (53b–54b, 57b), yielding benzyl and tertiary allyl alcohols with high efficiency. Highlighting the unique mechanistic capabilities of this 1,5-migration, an alkynyl boronic acid (58b) successfully participated to afford the tertiary propargyl alcohol 58 (51%). Such 1,5-alkynyl migrations are infrequently observed in photochemical systems and underscore the broad applicability of the sigmatropic pathway, which extends significantly beyond the scope of traditional ground-state rearrangements or our previously reported photochemical additions. ,

Late-Stage Functionalization (LSF) of Bioactive Molecules

To evaluate the potential of this metal- and base-free transformation in a medicinal chemistry context, we applied the protocol to the late-stage functionalization of complex, drug-like scaffolds. A (−)-β-citronellol-derived boronate was incorporated to furnish terpenoid-conjugated molecule 39 (63% yield). Furthermore, amino boronic acids (40b–42b) provided efficient access to β-, γ-, or δ-pyridyl-substituted amino alcohols (40–42), motifs found in interleukin-1 receptor antagonists. The robustness of the reaction was further evidenced by the successful incorporation of a fenofibrate-derived boronic acid (a hypolipidemic drug) and a tyrosine-derived boronic acid, yielding the drug-conjugates 55 (54%) and 56 (51%), respectively. The absence of aggressive reagents or heavy metals makes this methodology a promising candidate for the diversification of complex, sensitive pharmaceuticals where structural integrity is paramount.

Mechanistic Investigations

To elucidate the nature of the C–C bond-forming step, we conducted a series of experimental probes designed to distinguish between radical-chain, dissociative, and concerted sigmatropic pathways (Figure ). First, the involvement of free-radical intermediates was scrutinized (Figure A–C). The 1,5-rearrangement of 7a proceeded with high efficiency in the presence of radical scavengers such as TEMPO (61% yield) and BHT (57% yield), indicating that a free-radical chain mechanism is unlikely (Figure A). This conclusion was corroborated by a measured quantum yield of 0.62; a value below unity suggests a radical chain propagation process unlikely (see Supporting Information). To probe for the generation of short-lived radical species, we employed cyclopropylmethylboronic acid (59) as a radical clock precursor. The reaction yielded exclusively the unrearranged product 60 (55%), with no detectable ring-opened product 61 (Figure B). Given the rapid rate of cyclopropylmethyl radical rearrangement, the exclusive formation of 60 strongly suggests the absence of any transient radical intermediates with a significant lifetime.

Crucially, when enantiopure organoboronate 62 (99:1 e.r.) was reacted with 7a, the resulting adduct 63 exhibited significant retention of stereochemical integrity (90:10 and 86:14 e.r. for the respective diastereomers, Figure C) (see Supporting Information for more examples). This high degree of enantiospecificity, coupled with the absence of radical signals in nanosecond electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy (Figure S15), strongly supports a concerted, intramolecular migration occurring within a tight solvent cage or via a sigmatropic transition state.

To confirm the intramolecular nature of the carbo-migration, a crossover experiment was performed using two boronates, 64 and 65, possessing different alkyl groups and N-heterocycle scaffolds. Under standard conditions, only the intramolecular rearrangement products 66 and 67 were observed; no intermolecular crossover products (68 or 69) were detected by HRMS analysis of the crude reaction mixture (Figure D).

The inherent regioselectivity of the excited-state process was further highlighted using the conjugated alkenyl enol 70. The reaction yielded the 1,5-migration product 71 (45%) exclusively (Figure E). The absence of 1,7-conjugate addition productstypical outcomes in radical-mediated additions to extended π-systemsfurther reinforces the classification of this transformation as a sigmatropic rearrangement. These results collectively demonstrate that the in situ-generated boronate complex undergoes a highly ordered, intramolecular 1,5-migration steered by the electronic configuration of the excited-state enol.

X-ray Crystallographic Analysis of N-Heterocycle Enol-Boronate Chelates

To definitively characterize the reactive species, β-pyridyl enol ester 7a was reacted with triethylborane, resulting in the spontaneous evolution of ethane and the formation of boronate complex 64 (Figure F). X-ray crystallographic analysis revealed a tetracoordinate boron center with 83% tetrahedral character (THC = 0.83), as determined by Höpfl’s equation. Crucially, the crystal structure confirms that the pyridyl enol ester is “locked” in its enol form upon chelation, forming a robust bidentate complex with a covalent B–O bond (1.511 Å) and a dative B ← N bond (1.643 Å). Subsequent irradiation of the isolated complex 64 with blue LEDs afforded the β-pyridyl alcohol 66 in 52% yield, confirming that this chelate is a competent intermediate in the rearrangement.

To mirror the in situ reaction conditions, the interaction between 7a and cyclohexyl boronic acid 2 was investigated. Mixing the two components resulted in the emergence of a characteristic tetracoordinate signal at 8.39 ppm in the 11B NMR spectrum, accompanied by a concomitant decrease in the intensity of the free boronic acid peak (Figures S2–S3). High-resolution mass spectrometry confirmed the dehydration-driven formation of the boronate (Figure S4). Single crystals of the resulting complex 72 were obtained from methanol and analyzed via X-ray diffraction (Figure G). Complex 72 displayed a similarly high tetrahedral character (THC = 0.87) and bond lengths (B–O: 1.499 Å; B ← N: 1.646 Å) consistent with those of complex 64. The thermodynamic stability of this in situ assembly was quantified using Benesi–Hildebrand analysis, which revealed a strong binding association constant (Ka) of 54.5 M–1 (Figures S5–S6). This robust association constant explains the protocol’s efficiency in the absence of exogenous metals or bases; the self-assembly of the boronate-enol chelate is sufficiently favorable to drive the reaction forward under mild, visible-light conditions. These structural data provide a clear physical basis for our proposed mechanism, demonstrating that the boronate moiety organizes the enol into a rigid, photoactive geometry optimized for 1,5-migration.

Spectroscopic Evidence for ESIPT Suppression

To elucidate the photophysical consequences of boronate coordination, we conducted extensive spectroscopic investigations to distinguish our findings from established keto-addition pathways (Figure A–B). The fluorescence spectrum of free N-heterocycle enol 7a exhibits dual emission peaks at 410 and 445 nm, a hallmark of Excited-State Intramolecular Proton Transfer (ESIPT) wherein the enol and its ketone tautomer coexist in the excited state. Remarkably, upon boronate complexation, the resulting complex 73 displays a single enol emission peak at 410 nm. This observation indicates that the rigid bidentate coordination effectively locks the substrate, suppressing the ultrafast photoisomerization to the ketone form.

Time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy further confirms this suppression. While the emission decay of free enol 7a is biexponentialfeaturing a long-lived ketone component (4.0 ns)boronate complex 73 exhibits a monoexponential decay with a significantly shorter lifetime (0.4 ns). This shift unequivocally confirms the circumvention of the ESIPT pathway (Figure B). These data demonstrate that the reaction proceeds through a mechanism distinct from previously reported photochemical boronate rearrangements, which relied on the inherent electrophilicity of the transient ketone tautomer (Figure C). ,

Computational Analysis of the 1,5-Sigmatropic Pathway

To quantify the observed reactivity and regioselectivity, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed at the M06-2X/6-31G* level (Figure C–D). In the ground state (S 0 ), the 1,5-boronate rearrangement of complex I to alcohol II requires a prohibitively high activation energy of 52.2 kcal/mol, rendering the thermal pathway inaccessible.

Upon photoexcitation, complex I reaches the excited singlet state S 1 (63.4 kcal/mol), followed by efficient intersystem crossing to the triplet state T1 (49.2 kcal/mol). From the T 1 surface, the reaction bypasses the high thermal barriers of the ground state, proceeding via transition state TS2 with a remarkably low activation energy of only 3.5 kcal/mol to form intermediate 3 Int 1. In this triplet intermediate, the C–B distance (2.88 Å) remains within the sum of the van der Waals radii, suggesting that the C–B bond is not fully dissociateda structural feature consistent with our experimental evidence excluding free-radical intermediates.

Spin population analysis of 3 Int 1 reveals a species with significant spin density localized on the migrating carbon (1.02) and the enol C5 (0.35). This electronic distribution explains the high regioselectivity for 1,5-migration over 1,3-addition to the N-heterocycle C3 (0.25). The process concludes via a minimal energy crossing point (MECP), located 7.3 kcal/mol above 3 Int 1, facilitating spin crossover to the singlet ground state. At the MECP, concurrent C–B cleavage and C–C bond formation (2.69 Å) affects the structural recombination, yielding the boronic acid-coordinated alcohol II. This concerted but nonsynchronous pathway represents a shift in the conceptual understanding of boronate-enol interactions.

We first examined the TA profile of the free N-heterocycle enol 7a (Figure A). The spectrum of 7a is dominated by significant stimulated emission (SE), a characteristic feature of rapid Excited-State Intramolecular Proton Transfer (ESIPT). This process occurs on the picosecond time scale, converting the enol to its ketone tautomer. The resulting excited ketone then relaxes into a long-lived ground-state trans-ketone, characterized by a persistent absorption band at 460 nm (see Figures S13–S14). The dominance of these nonradiative relaxation pathways explains the traditional lack of 1,2-addition reactivity in free enols.

Spectroscopic Evidence for the Sigmatropic Pathway

In sharp contrast, the TA spectrum of the in situ-generated boronate complex I exhibited only ESA features, with a complete absence of ketone-stimulated emission (Figure B, left panel). This provides direct evidence that boronate coordination effectively suppresses ESIPT by locking the substrate into a rigid, reactive enol configuration.

Global analysis of the transient absorption data for the boronate complex identified four distinct transient species (A–D) using a sequential fitting model (A → B → C → D). These species correlate exceptionally well with the computed intermediates alongour calculated reaction coordinate (Figure B, middle panel): 1). Species A (S1, τ = 3.0 ps): The initially populated excited singlet state, which undergoes rapid intersystem crossing to the triplet manifold. The B ← N dative bond likely facilitates this rapid spin-flip. 2). Species B (T1, τ = 4.1 ps): A triplet state localized at the C=N bond. 3). Species C (3Int1, τ = 113 ps): The key spin-separated biradical intermediate identified by DFT calculations. Formed from the decay of species B, its electronic distribution concentrates spin density on the migrating carbon and the enol C5 position, pre-organizing the scaffold to undergo the sigmatropic rearrangement over a 113 ps timeline. 4). Species D (Product-like, τ = 974 μs): The product-like state corresponds to the boronic acid-coordinated β-N-heterocycle alcohol. Populated within 113 ps from species C, nanosecond flash photolysis confirms this ground-state species possesses a long microsecond lifespan (τ = 974 μs)..

Proposed Concerted Mechanism

Based on the synchronization between these spectroscopic lifetimes and the DFT-calculated energy barriers, we propose a concerted 1,5-boronate sigmatropic rearrangement. Upon photoexcitation and coordination-induced suppression of the ESIPT pathway, the system reaches the T1 state. The subsequent formation of the spin-separated intermediate C leads to structural recombination via a minimum energy crossing point (MECP). This allows for concerted C–B bond cleavage and concurrent C–C bond formation, circumventing the need for free-radical intermediates. Final tautomerization and hydrolysis yield the β-N-heterocycle alcohol. This close alignment between transient absorption kinetics and computational modeling rationalizes boronate reactivity as a concerted sigmatropic process, offering a distinct framework for modulating excited-state tautomerization to enable noncanonical transformations.

Conclusions

In summary, we have established a visible-light-driven 1,5-boronate sigmatropic rearrangement that addresses the long-standing reactivity constraints of β-N-heterocyclic enols. By leveraging bidentate coordination to suppress ESIPT, we demonstrate an effective strategy to outcompete ultrafast nonradiative relaxation pathways. This coordination-induced kinetic lock maintains the substrate in a reactive enol configuration throughout the excited-state manifold, enabling a concerted 1,5-carbo migration that is energetically inaccessible under ground-state conditions.

This metal- and base-free protocol exhibits exceptional functional group tolerance and regioselectivity, providing efficient access to a library of 61 diverse β-N-heterocycle alcoholsincluding high-value bioactive derivativesin yields up to 81%. Our mechanistic framework, supported by picosecond TA spectroscopy and DFT calculations, identifies a discrete excited triplet enol as the pivotal intermediate. These findings demonstrate that the reaction proceeds through a concerted sigmatropic pathway, rather than a conventional photoactivated nucleophilic addition. This discovery recontextualizes boronate–enol photochemistry, demonstrating that analogous functionalizations of 1,3-dicarbonyl systems should, under appropriate conditions, be viewed through the lens of sigmatropic rearrangements. We anticipate that this ESIPT-locking strategy will serve as a valuable framework for using temporary coordination to modulate photochemical outcomes, offering a robust platform for the development of sophisticated light-driven transformations.
