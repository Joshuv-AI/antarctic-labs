Connection of hypocoercivity and hypocontractivity via the θ \theta –methods
\firstname Anton \lastname Arnold, \firstname Stefan \lastname Egger

Abstract
Recent literature shows that hypocoercivity properties of linear evolution equations (in particular their exponential decay and the sharp short time decay of their propagator norm) carry over to their discretization via the midpoint rule. This note discusses this connection for the (other) θ \theta –methods, i.e. for θ ≠ 1 2 \theta\neq\frac{1}{2} . It is shown that any implicit discretization with θ ∈ ( 1 2 , 1 ] \theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1] (pertaining to a hypocoercive continuous-time evolution equation) is contractive, and not only hypocontractive – in contrast to the midpoint rule. For a coercive continuous-time evolution equation, a discretization with θ ∈ [ 0 , 1 2 ) \theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2}) is contractive for time steps small enough.

1
Introduction

This note is concerned with the comparison of the short- and large-time behavior of linear evolution equations to the related behavior of certain time discretizations. In particular we are concerned with
semi-dissipative
systems in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
:

\dot{x}=-Bx,\quad x(0)=x_{0},\quad t>0,

(1)

with some bounded linear operator
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, where its Hermitian part satisfies
B_{H}:=\frac{1}{2}(B+B^{*})\geq 0
. Moreover we shall assume that
B
has a trivial kernel.
As a discrete counterpart we shall consider here the
\theta
-methods with
\theta\in[0,1]
and step size
\tau>0
, i.e.:

\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}{\tau}=-B\big(\theta x_{k+1}+(1-\theta)x_{k}\big),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(2)

or after rearrangement

x_{k+1}=Dx_{k},\quad\mbox{with the operator }D:=(I+\tau\theta B)^{-1}(I-\tau(1-\theta)B),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3)

Our goal is to connect hypocoercivity properties of
B
in (
1
) with hypocontractivity properties of
D
in (
3
). In §4 of
[
2
]
this connection was analyzed in detail for the midpoint rule (or Crank-Nicholson scheme), i.e. for
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
. Here we shall extend that discussion to the other
\theta
-methods.

For the continuous-time dynamical system (
1
) we recall the following definitions from
[
3
,
4
]
:

\definitionname
1.1

Let
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

(i)

B
is called
hypocoercive
if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
such that the solutions to (
1
) satisfy

\forall x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},\quad\forall t\geq 0:\quad\|x(t)\|\leq Ce^{-\lambda t}\|x_{0}\|,

(4)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the norm in
\mathcal{H}
.

(ii)

Let
-B
be semi-dissipative, i.e.
B_{H}\geq 0
. The
hypocoercivity index (HC-index)

m_{HC}
of
B
is defined as the smallest integer
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(if it exists) such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(B^{*})^{j}B_{H}B^{j}\geq\kappa I

(5)

for some
\kappa>0
.

As a key results, this hypocoercivity index characterizes the short-time behavior of the propagator norm for (
1
) (see
[
5
, Th. 2.7]
for the finite dimensional case and
[
6
, Th. 4.1]
for the infinite dimensional case):

\theoremname
1.2

Let
-B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be semi-dissipative. Then,
B
is hypocoercive (with HC-index
m_{HC}
) if and only if

\|e^{-Bt}\|=1-ct^{a}+o(t^{a})\quad\mbox{for }t\to 0,

with some
a,\,c>0
. In this case, necessarily
a=2m_{HC}+1
.

Next we recall for the discrete-time dynamical system (
3
) the analogous definitions from
[
7
,
2
]
:

\definitionname
1.3

For
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
let
\frac{1}{\theta\tau}\not\in\sigma(B)
, and hence
D\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

(i)

D
is called
contractive
if
\|D\|<1
.

(ii)

D
is called
semi-contractive
if
\|D\|\leq 1
.

(iii)

D
is called
hypocontractive
(or
asymptotically stable
) if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
0<\lambda<1
such that the solutions to the iteration (
3
) satisfy

\forall x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\quad\|x_{k}\|\leq C\lambda^{k}\|x_{0}\|.

(6)

(iv)

Let
D
be semi-contractive. Its
hypocontractivity index (dHC-index)

m_{dHC}
is defined as the smallest integer
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(if it exists) such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(D^{*})^{j}(I-D^{*}D)D^{j}\geq\kappa I

(7)

for some
\kappa>0
.

As a key results, this hypocontractivity index characterizes the short-time behavior of the iteration (
3
) (see
[
7
, Th. 40]
for the finite dimensional case and
[
2
, Th. 4.5]
for the infinite dimensional case):

\theoremname
1.4

Let
D\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be semi-contractive and hypocontractive. Its (finite) hypocontractivity index is
m_{dHC}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
if and only if

\|D^{j}\|=1\>\mbox{ for all }j=1,...,m_{dHC},\quad\mbox{and }\|D^{m_{dHC}+1}\|<1.

For the midpoint rule, i.e. (
3
) with
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
, the hypocoercivity properties of
B
are closely related to the hypocontractivity properties of
D
(see
[
7
, §4]
for the finite dimensional case and
[
2
, §4]
for the infinite dimensional case):

\theoremname
1.5

Let
B,\,D\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be well-defined and related via (
3
). Then:

(i)

B
is hypocoercive if and only if
D
is hypocontractive.

(ii)

-B
is semi-dissipative if and only if
D
is semi-contractive.

(iii)

Let
B
be hypocoercive and
-B
be semi-dissipative. Then,
m_{HC}(B)=m_{dHC}(D)
.

Here we shall discuss the analogous relation of
B
and
D
for
\theta\neq\frac{1}{2}
. As we shall see in §
3
, the clear-cut relation from
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
does not extend to the cases
\theta\neq\frac{1}{2}
.
In §
4
we shall illustrate our results on some examples (explicit and implicit Euler schemes).

2
Notation and preliminaries

Throughout this article,
\mathcal{H}
denotes a (possibly infinite dimensional) separable Hilbert space with corresponding inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|
. The set of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with the operator norm also denoted by
\|\cdot\|
, and the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
I
. For an operator
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, its spectrum is denoted by
\sigma(A)
, its adjoint by
A^{*}
and its Hermitian part by
A_{H}\coloneqq\frac{A+A^{*}}{2}
. We further denote the unit sphere in
\mathcal{H}
by
\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{H}:\|x\|=1\}
, the complex left half-plane by
\mathbb{H}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Re(z)<0\}
, and the open unit disk by
\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\lvert z\rvert<1\}
.

For the continuous-time case we recall that an operator
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is hypocoercive if and only if
\sigma(-B)\subset\mathbb{H}
, see
[
8
, Th. I.3.14]
,
[
2
, Th. 4.1]
, e.g.

3
\theta
–methods

For our subsequent analysis of the iteration (
3
) we first formalize the definition of
D=M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
, where the map
M_{\theta,\tau}
is defined as follows:

\definitionname
3.1

Let
0\leq\theta\leq 1
and
\tau>0
. We consider the following Möbius transformations

\displaystyle M_{\theta,\tau}\colon\mathbb{C}\setminus\left\{\frac{1}{\theta\tau}\right\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\longmapsto\frac{1+(1-\theta)\tau z}{1-\theta\tau z}.

The following lemma, needed in the subsequent analysis, follows from the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
. We include the proof here for completeness.

\lemmaname
3.2

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

(i)

A
is semi-contractive if and only if
I-A^{*}A
is positive semidefinite.

(ii)

A
is contractive if and only if
I-A^{*}A
is coercive.

For any fixed
\lambda\geq 0
it holds:

\displaystyle I-A^{*}A\geq\lambda I

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{H}:\langle(I-A^{*}A)x,x\rangle\geq\lambda\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{H}:\|x\|^{2}-\|Ax\|^{2}\geq\lambda\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle 1-\lambda\geq\|A\|^{2}.

The first claim follows from the case
\lambda=0
in the calculation above and the second claim follows from the case
\lambda>0
.
∎

Using the preceding lemma we can characterize (semi-)contractivity of the discrete iteration operator
M_{\theta,\tau}(A)
in a way which will be useful for our analysis.

\lemmaname
3.3

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with
\frac{1}{\theta\tau}\notin\sigma(A)
. Then

(i)

M_{\theta,\tau}(A)
is semi-contractive if and only if
-2\tau A_{H}+\tau^{2}(2\theta-1)A^{*}A\geq 0
.

(ii)

M_{\theta,\tau}(A)
is contractive if and only if
-2\tau A_{H}+\tau^{2}(2\theta-1)A^{*}A\geq\lambda I
for some
\lambda>0
.

As
\frac{1}{\theta\tau}\notin\sigma(A)
,
M_{\theta,\tau}(A)
is well-defined as an element of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Moreover, semi-contractivity (resp. contractivity) of
M_{\theta,\tau}(A)
is equivalent to positive semidefinitness (resp. coercivity) of
I-M_{\theta,\tau}(A)^{*}M_{\theta,\tau}(A)
due to Lemma
3.2
. We focus on the latter characterization in the following.

\displaystyle I-M_{\theta,\tau}(A)^{*}M_{\theta,\tau}(A)

\displaystyle=I-(I-\theta\tau A^{*})^{-1}\big(I+(1-\theta)\tau A^{*}\big)\big(I+(1-\theta)\tau A\big)(I-\theta\tau A)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(I-\theta\tau A^{*})^{-1}\big((I-\theta\tau A^{*})(I-\theta\tau A)-(I+(1-\theta)\tau A^{*})(I+(1-\theta)\tau A)\big)(I-\theta\tau A)^{-1},

which shows that
I-M_{\theta,\tau}(A)^{*}M_{\theta,\tau}(A)
and
(I-\theta\tau A^{*})(I-\theta\tau A)-(I+(1-\theta)\tau A^{*})(I+(1-\theta)\tau A)
are conjugated to each other by an invertible operator. As being positive semidefinite and being coercive is invariant under such a transformation, both results follow from the equality

\displaystyle(I-\theta\tau A^{*})(I-\theta\tau A)-(I+(1-\theta)\tau A^{*})(I+(1-\theta)\tau A)

\displaystyle=(I-\theta\tau A^{*})(I-\theta\tau A)-(I-\theta\tau A^{*}+\tau A^{*})(I-\theta\tau A+\tau A)

\displaystyle=-\tau A^{*}+\theta\tau^{2}A^{*}A-\tau A+\theta\tau^{2}A^{*}A-\tau^{2}A^{*}A

\displaystyle=-2\tau A_{H}+\tau^{2}(2\theta-1)A^{*}A.

∎

We will also need the following result about hypocoercive operators.

\lemmaname
3.4

Let
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be hypocoercive. Then
B^{*}B
is bounded and coercive, i.e. there exist constants
\lambda\geq\mu>0
such that
\lambda I\geq B^{*}B\geq\mu I
.

The only nontrivial aspect to show is coercivity of
B^{*}B
. Following a remark in §
2
, it holds that
\sigma(-B)\subset\mathbb{H}
. In particular,
0\notin\sigma(-B)=-\sigma(B)
and so
B
is invertible. Consequently, for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
we have

\displaystyle\|x\|=\|B^{-1}Bx\|\leq\|B^{-1}\|\|Bx\|

and hence

\displaystyle\langle B^{*}Bx,x\rangle=\langle Bx,Bx\rangle=\|Bx\|^{2}\geq\|B^{-1}\|^{-2}\|x\|^{2}.

∎

After these auxiliary results we are ready to investigate how the hypocoercivity structure of
B
behaves under discretization using the
\theta
-methods for
\theta\neq\frac{1}{2}
. To this end, we distinguish the cases
\theta<\frac{1}{2}
and
\theta>\frac{1}{2}
.

\theoremname
3.5

Let
\frac{1}{2}<\theta\leq 1
, let
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be hypocoercive and
-B
be semi-dissipative. Then
M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is contractive for any
\tau>0
, i.e. it is hypocontractive with hypocontractivity index
0
.

Lemma
3.3
yields that
M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is contractive if and only if
2\tau B_{H}+\tau^{2}(2\theta-1)B^{*}B
is coercive. Yet, the latter is clear because
B_{H}\geq 0
,
2\theta-1>0
, and Lemma
3.4
yields that
B^{*}B\geq\mu I
for some
\mu>0
.
∎

\theoremname
3.6

Let
0\leq\theta<\frac{1}{2}
and let
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be hypocoercive.

(i)

If
B
has hypocoercivity index
0
(i.e.
B
is coercive), then the set of step sizes
\tau>0
such that
M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is semi-contractive has the form
(0,\tau_{0}]
for some
\tau_{0}>0
. Moreover,
M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is even contractive, i.e. it is hypocontractive with hypocontractivity index
0
, for
0<\tau<\tau_{0}
.

(ii)

If
B
has hypocoercivity index greater than
0
, then
M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is not semi-contractive for any
\tau>0
.

We first note that Lemma
3.3
yields that
M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is semi-contractive if and only if

\displaystyle 2\tau B_{H}+\tau^{2}(2\theta-1)B^{*}B\geq 0,

and the latter is true if and only if

\displaystyle 2B_{H}\geq\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}B.

(8)

Thus, the set of all
\tau>0
such that
M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is semi-contractive can be written equivalently as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\coloneqq\{\tau>0:2B_{H}\geq\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}B\}.

(i)

Concerning the first part,
B
has hypocoercivity index
0
, so
B_{H}
is coercive, meaning
B_{H}\geq\kappa I
for some
\kappa>0
. Moreover,
B
is hypocoercive, so Lemma
3.4
yields
\lambda I\geq B^{*}B\geq\mu I
for some
\lambda\geq\mu>0
. Consequently, for
0<\tau\leq\frac{2\kappa}{(1-2\theta)\lambda}
we have

\displaystyle 2B_{H}\geq 2\kappa I\geq\tau(1-2\theta)\lambda I\geq\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}B,

which shows that
\mathcal{T}
is nonempty. Furthermore, let
\tilde{\tau}\in\mathcal{T}
and
0<\tau<\tilde{\tau}
. Then, using
B^{*}B\geq 0
, we deduce

\displaystyle 2B_{H}\geq\tilde{\tau}(1-2\theta)B^{*}B=\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}B+(\tilde{\tau}-\tau)(1-2\theta)B^{*}B\geq\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}B.

Thus,
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
. Finally, let
\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{T}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\tau_{n}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\tau
. Then for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
it holds

\displaystyle\langle 2B_{H}x,x\rangle\geq\langle\tau_{n}(1-2\theta)B^{*}Bx,x\rangle.

Taking the limit
n\to\infty
we deduce

\displaystyle\langle 2B_{H}x,x\rangle\geq\langle\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}Bx,x\rangle,

and so
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
. Thus, the set of
\tau>0
such that
M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is semi-contractive has the form
(0,\tau_{0}]
for some
\tau_{0}>0
. Concerning the last claim, Lemma
3.3
(ii) shows that

M_{\theta,\tau}(-B)
is contractive if and only if
2B_{H}-\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}B
is coercive. The latter follows directly from semi-contractivity of
M_{\theta,\tau_{0}}(-B)
(which implies that (
8
) holds for
\tau=\tau_{0}
) and the fact that
B^{*}B\geq\mu I
:

\displaystyle 2B_{H}-\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}B

\displaystyle=2B_{H}-\tau_{0}(1-2\theta)B^{*}B+(\tau_{0}-\tau)(1-2\theta)B^{*}B\geq(\tau_{0}-\tau)(1-2\theta)B^{*}B

\displaystyle\geq(\tau_{0}-\tau)(1-2\theta)\mu I.

(ii)

Concerning the second part, we note that
B
having hypocoercivity index greater than
0
implies the existence of a sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{S}
such that
\langle B_{H}x_{n},x_{n}\rangle\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
. Together with the fact that
B^{*}B\geq\mu I
for some
\mu>0
(which again follows from Lemma
3.4
) we deduce

\displaystyle\langle(2B_{H}-\tau(1-2\theta)B^{*}B)x_{n},x_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle B_{H}x_{n},x_{n}\rangle-\tau(1-2\theta)\langle B^{*}Bx_{n},x_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle 2B_{H}x_{n},x_{n}\rangle-\tau(1-2\theta)\langle\mu x_{n},x_{n}\rangle=\langle 2B_{H}x_{n},x_{n}\rangle-\tau(1-2\theta)\mu

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}-\tau(1-2\theta)\mu<0.

Hence (
8
) cannot hold for any
\tau>0
.

∎

4
Examples

We first want to illustrate our results by extending
[
2
, Example 5.3]
to the implicit and explicit Euler method.

\examplename
4.1

We consider
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
and the matrix

\displaystyle B=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}&1\end{pmatrix}

with corresponding Hermitian part

\displaystyle B_{H}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.

As
B_{H}
is obviously not positive definite but

\displaystyle B_{H}+B^{*}B_{H}B=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}&-\frac{1}{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}&2\end{pmatrix}

is (because its eigenvalues are positive), B has hypocoercivity index
1
. The corresponding discrete iteration operator obtained by applying the
explicit Euler method
(i.e. with
\theta=0
) is

\displaystyle D(\tau)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{\tau}{2}\\
\frac{\tau}{2}&1-\tau\end{pmatrix}.

It can be seen easily that
D(\tau)
is not semi-contractive for any
\tau>0
because

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{\tau}{2}\\
\frac{\tau}{2}&1-\tau\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right\|=\left\|\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\frac{\tau}{2}\end{pmatrix}\right\|>1.

Hence the spectral norm of
D(\tau)
must be strictly larger than
1
, as was predicted by Theorem
3.6
(ii).

If we apply the
implicit Euler method
(i.e. with
\theta=1
), we obtain

\displaystyle D(\tau)=\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1+\tau&-\frac{\tau}{2}\\
\frac{\tau}{2}&1\end{pmatrix}

as the discrete iteration operator. In order to show that
D(\tau)
is a contraction for all
\tau>0
, we note that the spectral norm of a real valued matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

is given by

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{g+\sqrt{g^{2}-4h}}{2}},

where
g=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}
and
h=(ad-bc)^{2}
(this follows from the fact that the spectral norm is the largest singular value). Applying this formula to
(1+\frac{\tau}{2})^{2}\,D(\tau)
we deduce

g=2\left(\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\right),\qquad h=\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{4}.

(9)

Consequently,

\displaystyle g^{2}-4h=4\tau^{2}+4\tau^{3}+2\tau^{4}.

(10)

Now we note that

\displaystyle\|D(\tau)\|<1\iff\|D(\tau)\|^{2}<1\iff\frac{\frac{g+\sqrt{g^{2}-4h}}{2}}{\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{4}}<1\iff\sqrt{g^{2}-4h}<2\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{4}-g

(11)

and further, using (
9
),

\displaystyle 2\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{4}-g=2\tau+\frac{3\tau^{2}}{2}+\tau^{3}+\frac{\tau^{4}}{8}>0.

Thus, we can square the last expression of (
11
) again:

\displaystyle\sqrt{g^{2}-4h}<2\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{4}-g\iff g^{2}-4h<\left(2\tau+\frac{3\tau^{2}}{2}+\tau^{3}+\frac{\tau^{4}}{8}\right)^{2},

and the latter is true because (
10
) yields

\displaystyle g^{2}-4h=4\tau^{2}+4\tau^{3}+2\tau^{4}<\left(2\tau+\frac{3\tau^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}<\left(2\tau+\frac{3\tau^{2}}{2}+\tau^{3}+\frac{\tau^{4}}{8}\right)^{2}.

This shows contractivity of
D(\tau)
for any
\tau>0
, as was predicted by Theorem
3.5
. ∎

As a second example we consider the case where the hypocoercivity index is
0
.

\examplename
4.2

We consider
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
and the matrix

\displaystyle B=\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{1}{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}&1\end{pmatrix}

with corresponding Hermitian part

\displaystyle B_{H}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

As
B_{H}
is positive definite, B has hypocoercivity index
0
. The corresponding discrete iteration operator obtained by applying the
explicit Euler method
is

\displaystyle D(\tau)=\begin{pmatrix}1-\tau&-\frac{\tau}{2}\\
\frac{\tau}{2}&1-\tau\end{pmatrix}.

Since

\displaystyle D(\tau)^{T}D(\tau)=\left((1-\tau)^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}\right)I,

we deduce that

\displaystyle\|D(\tau)\|=\sqrt{(1-\tau)^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}}.

As
f(\tau)\coloneqq(1-\tau)^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}
is a parabola pointing upwards which satisfies
f(0)=1
,
f(1)=\frac{1}{4}
, and
f\geq 0
, it is clear that the set of all
\tau>0
such that
f(\tau)\leq 1
has the form
(0,\tau_{0}]
for some
\tau_{0}>0
and then the set
\{\tau>0\,\big|\,f(\tau)<1\}
is given by
(0,\tau_{0})
. Consequently, the same holds for the set of all
\tau>0
where
D(\tau)
is (semi-)contractive, confirming Theorem
3.6
.

If we apply the
implicit Euler method
, we obtain

\displaystyle D(\tau)=\begin{pmatrix}1+\tau&\frac{\tau}{2}\\
-\frac{\tau}{2}&1+\tau\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\frac{1}{(1+\tau)^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}}\begin{pmatrix}1+\tau&-\frac{\tau}{2}\\
\frac{\tau}{2}&1+\tau\end{pmatrix}

as the discrete iteration operator. Similar to before, one computes

\displaystyle\|D(\tau)\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1+\tau)^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}}}<1

for any
\tau>0
, thus confirming Theorem
3.5
. ∎
