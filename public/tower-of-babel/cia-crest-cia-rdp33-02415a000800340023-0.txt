14 June 1967

MEMORANDUM FOR: The Executive Director-Comptroller

THRU : Chief, Historical Staff/DCI

SUBJECT : Historical Programs in the Directorates

for Intelligence, Science and Technology
and Support

1. A year has now passed since historical programs were
initiated in the Directorates for Intelligence, Science and
Technology, and Support. As you may imagine, it was not easy
to "sell" these programs. There are so many required reports
and papers of different kinds in the Agency today that with
the best will in the world officers responsible for operations
could only consider the writing of historical papers by staff
personnel an added burden which they were not anxious to under-
take. However, the three Deputy Directors, together with their
senior personnel, supported the program, and after a few months
we were under way. In face, I am pleased by a certain rather
unexpected enthusiasm which has developed in regard to the
historical enterprise.

2. During the first year, Historical Board meetings
were held as follows: DDI , six; DDS, four; and DDS&T , two.

In addition, I had a number of meetings with the Board Chair-
men, and they, in turn, met at frequent intervals with indi-
vidual members of their boards and with personnel designated
to write historical papers.

3. As you know, all Directorates have submitted their
initial programs. No purpose would be served by sending you
another detailed list of them, but I think it would interest
you to know that:

a. The DDI has included in its program some forty
historical papers ranging from narrative histories to short
monographs, with an additional thirteen under consideration.
Thirty-one persons (primarily staff), with a number of assist-
ants, have been designated to write these papers.

b. The DDS&T has incorporated eighteen papers
into its program and is considering an additional
thirty-seven. Eighteen staff employees have been
designated as writers.

c. The DDS has included one hundred and ten
papers in its program and is considering additional
monographs. To date, some seventy-nine staff em-
ployees have been designated to do the writing. In
the case of DDS, a number of the scheduled histories
are short papers which may eventually be incorporated
into one or more longer ones.

Needless to say, all programs must be considered tentative
and subject to deletions and additions as time goes on.

4. It was your desire that the number of annuitants
writing under contract should be held to a minimum. As of
1 June, the DDI has one person in an Independent Contractor
status. This contract will be renewed for one year, effec-
tive 30 June 1967. Two others are presently being cleared
for contract, one to run for a year, and one for approxi-
mately six months. DDS has two current contracts, one
expiring 30 June 1967 and the other extended to September
1967. DDS&T has no contract personnel for writing purposes.
Considering the number of papers to be written, this is a
very good showing.

5. It is obvious that the level of historical writing
will vary considerably with the individual. We are not
trying to produce smooth-flowing, finished histories, but
rather an accurate record of what has transpired since the
formation of CIA. We have stressed that histories should
be as free as possible from bias, and that they should in-
clude the record of all controversies and how these have
been resolved, if they have been resolved. Nothing should

be "brushed under the rug." This will make them more valuable,
but also harder to write, and to assure as much objectivity
as possible, senior officers in the three Directorates have
been designated to review the historical papers as they are
produced .

6. Many of the deadlines still appear to be optimistic,
but as research and drafting is progressing satisfactorily

in all areas, I think that by 30 June 1968, we should have a
large number of the proposed papers in final form.
