 ABSTRACT
 Based on the rationale presented, post-closure nuclear criticality is improbable when room closure compacts
containers disposing transuranic (TRU) waste emplaced at the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP), an operating
repository in bedded salt in southeastern New Mexico. As described in the original WIPP certification, a qualitative
estimate of the probability of post-closure criticality in TRU waste produced from atomic energy defense activities
has been low either because remote-handled TRU waste canisters are neutronically isolated by the bedded salt or
because the low fissile mass in an array of contact-handled TRU waste drums cannot be compacted sufficiently by
room closure from salt creep. These situations are still valid for the majority of TRU waste that is disposed at WIPP
without any disposal constraints, as updated herein. This report also qualitatively evaluates the probability of criticality
after disposal of TRU waste in pipe overpack containers (POC) where every POC in a shipment may have the
maximum 200 fissile gram equivalent of 239Pu content. The probability of criticality for a disposal room filled with
POCs is estimated during four representative phases of repository evolution: (1) a large salt block falls onto POCs in
the first 20 years, (2) salt creep closes a disposal room in the first 1000 years without brine seepage and subsequent
gas generation, which permits maximum compaction, (3) some brine seepage occurs into the closed room, which
initiates consumption of the fiberboard (cellulose) impact absorber in the POC in the second 1000 years, and (4) full
brine inundation of a room and consumption of all fiberboard thereafter. Salt-block fall in the first phase does not
greatly disrupt three tiers of POCs. The compacted spacing of POCs in the later three repository conditions is
calculated through high-fidelity, geomechanical modeling. Criticality evaluation of compacted 200-g 239Pu spheres at
the compacted spacing shows that neither 12-inch nor 6-inch POCs are critical after the first 1000 years, the second
1000 years, or thereafter as the sea of reflector material changes to represent the three repository conditions.
Specifically, fiberboard and iron isolates 239Pu while dry, and brine reduces the reactivity when a room is partially and
fully inundated. Because POC behavior bounds behavior of other standard TRU waste containers, post-closure
criticality caused by room closure compacting containers is omitted in the performance assessments for the 2019 and
2026 WIPP compliance re-certification applications to the US Environmental Protection Agency.

Keywords: post-closure criticality evaluation; features, events, and processes screening; transuranic radioactive waste
disposal; safety assessment; performance assessment; Waste Isolation Pilot Plant

FOREWARD
 The nuclear criticality calculations referenced within this document were originally published by Oak Ridge
National Laboratory (ORNL) to meet the requirements of the US Environmental Protection Agency’s (EPA)
implementing regulation, 40 CFR §194.32(e)(3). The ORNL calculations are relied on to justify the exclusion of
nuclear criticality impacting the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP) disposal system during the post-closure regulatory
timeframe. This justification is performed in accordance with EPA "reasonable expectation" standard of proof given
in 40 CFR §191.13(b) for the post-closure timeframe. This report is being issued outside of Sandia National
Laboratories’ Nuclear Criticality Safety Program that is governed under 10 CFR 830 and US Department of Energy
(DOE) Order 420.1C, Chg. 3 without receiving that program’s concurrence or dissension of the conclusions within.
PREFACE AND ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS
 EPA has regulatory authority for certifying the performance of WIPP in isolating radionuclides over the next
10 000 years against its long-term environmental standard specified in 40 CFR 191. The US Department of Energy
(DOE), who is responsible for WIPP, has assigned Sandia National Laboratories (Sandia) to evaluate WIPP post-
closure performance for EPA certification. Part of the performance assessment includes a formal analysis of all
features, events, and processes (FEPs) that may impact WIPP isolation. EPA does not designate the post-closure
criticality FEP for special consideration in 40 CFR 191. Rather, criticality is considered like all other FEPs.
 The analysis reported here directly supports developing a rationale that post-closure criticality in an array of pipe
overpack containers (POCs) is of low probability; thus, the criticality FEP is not necessary to include in WIPP
performance assessments. The POC low-probability rationale is part of an overall probabilistic rationale for excluding
post-closure criticality that is described in Appendix SCR of the 2026 WIPP Compliance Recertification Application
to EPA, as managed by G. Ross Kirkes, Piru Associates.
 This report updates the 2019 memorandum by R.P. Rechard, Improbability of transuranic waste compaction by
salt creep causing criticality in bedded salt repository, which supported the 2019 Compliance Recertification
Application to EPA. Importantly, discussion of the criticality control overpack (CCO) was moved to its own report.
No new criticality calculations or POC container compaction simulations were conducted for this report. The 2019
calculational work and current analysis was supported by the Surplus Plutonium Disposition (SPD) program in the
Material Management and Minimization Office within DOE. Paul E. Shoemaker, Sandia Senior Manager for Defense
Waste Management Programs and Ronald Livingston, senior technical advisor to SPD program, provided coordination
between the SPD program and Sandia for the 2019 memorandum. For this deliverable, Joshua Rutkowski, Sandia
Manager International Safeguards and Engagements, was the liaison between the SPD program and Sandia.
 The low-probability screening approach for the criticality evaluation uses geomechanical simulations to
determine spacing for neutron transport modeling. Ben Reedlunn and James E. Bean, Jr. both from Sandia provided
the geomechanical analysis, which is documented in several reports between 2018 and 2020. The most applicable is
B. Reedlunn and J.E. Bean, Jr. Simulations of pipe overpack container compaction at the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant,
SAND2019-12111. ORNL conducted the neutron transport modeling, which is documented in a B.D. Brickner, Post-
closure nuclear criticality evaluations involving 6- and 12-inch pipe overpack TRU waste containers at the Waste
Isolation Pilot Plant, ORNL/TM-2019/1222. Phil Jones and Mariah Garcia of Sandia conducted the internal review.
 The POC evaluated here is managed by the WIPP management and operating (M&O) contractor for the US
Department of Energy (DOE). The M&O is responsible for the nuclear criticality safety analysis in the Safety Analysis
Report to the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) that has shown criticality is incredible in POCs under normal
and accident conditions. NRC has certified use of POCs in the TRUPACT-II package for transporting transuranic
waste to WIPP. Furthermore, the M&O has shown criticality in POCs is incredible during normal and accident
conditions at the WIPP facility prior to closure in the Design Safety Analysis report to DOE.

VI.C. Fissile Concentration Less in Large

 A similar rationale applies to larger drums (i.e., 85-
gallon with 1.33 m3 volume and 100-gallon drums with
1.15 m3—Table III), which have the same transportation
limit of 325 FGE 239Pu in 4- and 3-packs of containers.
Replacing 6-inch POCs in a room with CH-TRU 85- and
100-gallon drums, which have a similar areal footprint
(Table III and Fig. 7), will certainly reduce room
reactivity.
 Larger drums and very large containers also have a
much smaller initial fissile concentration than the 2400

kg/m3, 0.044 kg/m3, 0.150 kg/m3, respectively.
