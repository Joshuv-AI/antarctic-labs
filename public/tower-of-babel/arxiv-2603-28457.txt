Three non-Hermitian random matrix universality classes of complex edge statistics: Spacing ratios and distributions
Gernot Akemann, Georg Angermann, Noah Aygün, Adam Mielke,

Abstract
The conjectured three generic local bulk statistics amongst all non-Hermitian random matrix symmetry classes have recently been extended to three generic local edge statistics. We study analytically and numerically complex spacing ratios and nearest-neighbour (NN) spacing distributions that characterise such local statistics. We choose the three simplest representatives of these universality classes, given by the Gaussian ensembles of complex Ginibre, complex symmetric and complex self-dual matrices, denoted by class A, AI † and AII † . In the first part, we analytically study the complex spacing ratio in class A, at finite matrix size N N . Introducing a conditional point process, we simplify existing expressions and show why an uncontrolled approximation introduced earlier converges well in the large- N N limit in the bulk. When specifying to the elliptic Ginibre ensemble, we present a parameter-dependent N = 3 N=3 surmise for the complex spacing ratio, interpolating to that of the Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE), where such a surmise is very accurate. In the second numerical part, we compare complex spacing ratios, its moments, and NN spacing distributions for all three ensembles with that of uncorrelated points, the two-dimensional (2D) Poisson process, both in the bulk and at the edge. The varying degree of repulsion within these different edge universality classes can be well understood in terms of an effective 2D Coulomb gas description, at different values of inverse temperature β \beta . We find indications that the complex spacing ratio does not fully unfold the local statistics at the edge. Finally we verify that for small argument, in all three symmetry classes the NN spacing distributions in the bulk and at the edge are consistent with the universal cubic repulsion.

\displaystyle\times\underset{1\leq j,k\leq{N-3}}{\det}\left[\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\tilde{z}\ \tilde{z}^{j-1}\overline{\tilde{z}}^{k-1}|\tilde{z}|^{2}|\tilde{z}-zz_{3}|^{2}|\tilde{z}-z_{3}|^{2}\omega(\tilde{z})\heaviside\left(|\tilde{z}|^{2}-|z_{3}|^{2}\right)\right].

For
N=3
the determinant is obviously absent. Let us now further investigate the following matrix inside the determinant for
N>3
:

A_{jk}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\tilde{z}\ \tilde{z}^{j-1}\overline{\tilde{z}}^{k-1}|\tilde{z}|^{2}|\tilde{z}-z_{3}z|^{2}|\tilde{z}-z_{3}|^{2}\omega(\tilde{z})\heaviside\left(|\tilde{z}|^{2}-|z_{3}|^{2}\right).

(2.16)

In the special case of a radially symmetric weight function
\omega(\tilde{z})=\omega(|\tilde{z}|)
, by switching to polar coordinates
\tilde{z}=r\exp[\mathrm{i}\theta]
, we obtain

A_{jk}=\int_{|z_{3}|}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\;r^{j+k-1}\omega(r)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta\;|\tilde{z}|^{2}|\tilde{z}-z_{3}z|^{2}|\tilde{z}-z_{3}|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta(j-k)}.

(2.17)

It is clear that only those terms contribute where the angular dependence cancels inside the angular integral. From expanding each factor, e.g.
|\tilde{z}-z_{3}|^{2}=r^{2}+|z_{3}|^{2}+r(z_{3}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}+\overline{z}_{3}\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}\theta})
, it is clear that this leads to a five-diagonal matrix. To be explicit we now expand

|\tilde{z}|^{2}|\tilde{z}-z_{3}z|^{2}|\tilde{z}-z_{3}|^{2}=\sum_{l,m=1}^{3}M_{lm}\tilde{z}^{l}\overline{\tilde{z}}^{m},

(2.18)

using the same notation as in
[
29
]
, with the following Hermitian matrix coefficients
M_{lm}=\overline{M}_{ml}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}M_{11}=|z_{3}|^{4}|z|^{2},&M_{12}=\overline{M}_{21}=-z_{3}|z_{3}|^{2}(|z|^{2}+z),&M_{13}=\overline{M}_{31}=z_{3}^{2}z,\\
M_{22}=|z_{3}|^{2}|1+z|^{2},&M_{23}=\overline{M}_{32}=-z_{3}(1+z),&M_{33}=1.\end{array}

This expansion allows us to evaluate the angular integral in eq. (
2.17
), hence

A_{jk}=2\pi\sum_{l,m=1}^{3}M_{lm}\delta_{l+j,m+k}\int_{|z_{3}|}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\;r^{2(j+l)-1}\omega(r).

(2.21)

Since the Kronecker delta
\delta_{l+j,m+k}
implies

l+j=m+k\iff j-k=m-l\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\},

(2.22)

we obtain a pentadiagonal matrix
A_{jk}
for a general rotationally invariant weight function
\omega(|z|)
. This pentadiagonal structure coincides with the approximation to the unconditional ensemble obtained by Dusa and Wettig
[
29
]
, for the GinUE with
\omega(z)=\exp[-|z|^{2}]
.

It was claimed in
[
29
]
that the determinant of the matrix
A
in (
2.21
) is independent of the phase of
z_{3}
, allowing the integral therein to be simplified. This property holds more generally for arbitrary rotationally invariant weight functions: the phase dependence of
z_{3}
in (
2.1
) can be made explicit,
M_{lm}=C_{lm}(z,|z_{3}|)\exp\left[\mathrm{i}\arg(z_{3})(m-l)\right]
. Combining this with the Kronecker delta constraints in (
2.22
), each matrix element can be written as

A_{jk}(z,z_{3})=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\arg(z_{3})(j-k)}\tilde{A}_{jk}(z,|z_{3}|),

(2.23)

where
\tilde{A}
depends only on the complex spacing ratio
z
and the modulus
|z_{3}|
, being entirely independent of the phase of
z_{3}
.
By the multilinearity of the determinant, it follows that

\det\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq N-3}[A(z,z_{3})]=\det\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq N-3}[\tilde{A}(z,|z_{3}|)],

(2.24)

so that the integral over the phase of
z_{3}
becomes trivial.

For further details on the efficient numerical evaluation of the remaining radial integral over (
2.24
) for large matrix size
N
we refer to
[
29
]
.

In the unconditional case (

In both the conditional and unconditional case, we have to check the positivity of the expression

and for the unconditional case we have

2.3
Hermitian limit vs. consecutive and NN GUE spacing ratio

Before taking the Hermitian limit
\tau\nearrow 1
of the conditional and unconditional surmises in the eGinUE, let us recall what is known analytically in the GUE for spacing ratios of real eigenvalues
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
of a complex
N\times N
Hermitian random matrix
H
with independent Gaussian matrix elements distributed as
P(H)\sim\exp[-{\rm Tr}H^{2}]
.
Its joint density is proportional to (see e.g.
[
27
]
)

\jpdf_{\rm GUE}^{(N)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\propto\Delta_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\exp{\left[-x_{j}^{2}\right]}.

(2.43)

In contrast to complex eigenvalues
z_{j}
, the real eigenvalues
x_{j}
can be put into consecutive order,

x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{N},

(2.44)

where the probability of two eigenvalues to be equal is vanishing. Consequently, several different spacing ratios can be defined. First, for every fixed
k
, with
1<k<N
, we can define a
consecutive spacing ratio
, following
[
30
,
31
]
:

r_{k}=\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}{x_{k}-x_{k-1}}>0,\quad k=2,\ldots,N-1.

(2.45)

Notice that by definition this ratio is always positive. In
[
30
]
, the minimum value
\tilde{r}_{k}=\min\{r_{k},1/r_{k}\}\in[0,1]
was considered. In case of additional symmetries, the distributions of
r_{k}
and
\tilde{r}_{k}
can be related, cf.
[
31
]
.
The
N=3
surmise (with
k=2
) for the consecutive spacing ratio in the GUE is thus obtained as
[
31
]
:

\displaystyle\varrho_{{\rm GUE}}^{(3)}(x)

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{x_{2}}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{3}\jpdf_{\rm GUE}^{(N=3)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\ \delta^{(1)}\left(x-\frac{x_{3}-x_{2}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right)

(2.46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{3,\rm GUE}}\frac{x^{2}(1+x)^{2}}{(1+x+x^{2})^{4}},\quad x\geq 0,

with the correct normalisation reading

\alpha_{3,\rm GUE}=\frac{4\pi}{81\sqrt{3}}

[
31
]
.
This surmise is an accurate approximation to the consecutive spacing ratio in the bulk of the spectrum for large
N
, see Fig. 1 in
[
31
]
. Corresponding surmises exist for the orthogonal, symplectic and in fact
\beta
-ensembles, too
[
37
]

4

.

s_{k}=\frac{x^{\rm NN}_{k}-x_{k}}{x^{\rm NNN}_{k}-x_{k}},\quad k=2,\ldots,N-1.

Here, the NN and NNN eigenvalues are defined as

x^{\rm NN}_{k}=\min_{j\neq k}|x_{j}-x_{k}|=x_{k_{0}},\quad x^{\rm NNN}_{k}=\min_{j\neq k,k_{0}}|x_{j}-x_{k}|=x_{k_{1}}.

, in some situations the consecutive and NN spacing ratio can be related, i.e. in Fig.

In the limit
\tau\nearrow 1

Thus, in the limit, the probability mass concentrates at

.
This can be seen explicitly in the surmises (

) respectively (

\sim(1-\tau^{2})^{4}

\sim y^{9}
, for

\lim_{\tau\nearrow 1}\varrho_{\rm A,(C)}^{(3)}(x+\mathrm{i}y;\tau)=f_{\rm(C)}(x)\Theta(1-x^{2})\delta^{(1)}(y).

(2.53)

The limiting marginal distribution of the real part

is determined by

f_{\rm(C)}(x)\Theta(1-x^{2})=\lim_{\tau\nearrow 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\;\varrho_{\rm A,(C)}^{(N=3)}(x+\mathrm{i}y;\tau).

(2.54)

In order to resolve the dependence of the denominators in
\varrho_{\rm A,(C)}^{(N=3)}(x+\mathrm{i}y;\tau)

(1-\tau^{2})
, and at the same time make a rescaling of the imaginary part as follows:

y=\tilde{y}\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}.

(2.55)

In the conditional case we have

\displaystyle\left[(1-\tau^{2})g_{\rm C}(x,y)^{2}+4\tau^{2}y^{2}\right]^{-{9}/{2}}=(1-\tau^{2})^{-{9}/{2}}\left[(1+x^{2}+\tilde{y}^{2}(1-\tau^{2}))^{2}+4\tau^{2}\tilde{y}^{2}\right]^{-{9}/{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-\tau^{2})^{-{9}/{2}}\left[(1+x^{2})^{2}+4\tilde{y}^{2}\right]^{-{9}/{2}}\left[1+\frac{(x^{2}-1)\tilde{y}^{2}}{(1+x^{2})^{2}+4\tilde{y}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left((1-\tau^{2})^{2}\right)\right],

(2.56)

and analogously for the unconditional case

\displaystyle\left[(1-\tau^{2})g(x,y)^{2}+3\tau^{2}y^{2}\right]^{-{9}/{2}}=(1-\tau^{2})^{-{9}/{2}}\left[(1-x+x^{2})^{2}+3\tilde{y}^{2}\right]^{-{9}/{2}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)\right].

(2.57)

Therefore, the surmise for the complex spacing ratio can be expanded in both cases as

\displaystyle\varrho_{\rm A,(C)}^{(3)}(x+\mathrm{i}y;\tau)=\Theta(1-x^{2}-y^{2})x^{2}(1-x)^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-\tau^{2})^{\frac{2k-1}{2}}A_{k,{\rm(C)}}(x,\tilde{y}),

(2.58)

where the leading order expansion coefficients read

3.1
Complex spacing ratios in 2D: Edge vs. bulk

Let us begin by defining the complex spacing ratio as obtained numerically from the

z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}
an

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=\frac{z_{k}^{\textsc{NN}}-z_{k}}{z_{k}^{\textsc{NNN}}-z_{k}}.

(3.5)

Here,
z_{k}^{\textsc{NN(N)}}
denotes the NN(N) of
z_{k}
in radial distance.
Despite the eigenvalue repulsion of the complex eigenvalues we consider, such a NN or NNN may not be unique, as two different complex eigenvalues may lie on the same circle of radius
|z_{k}^{\textsc{NN}}-z_{k}|
sharing the same distance. In practice, the probability for this event is negligible, and choosing one of them as NN (and the other as NNN) will not affect our results.

We observe that
\lambda_{k}
is a complex variable in 2D.
We can assign a radius
r_{k}\geq 0
and angle
\phi_{k}\in(-\pi,+\pi]
to it,
\lambda_{k}=r_{k}\exp[\mathrm{i}\phi_{k}]
. As the NNN is always further away (or at best at equal distance) than the NN, we have
r_{k}\leq 1
. One attractive feature of the complex spacing ratio is that it is claimed that no unfolding is needed. In 2D the unfolding procedure, i.e. extracting the local statistics from the averaged ones, is non-trivial, see
[
12
,
18
,
55
]

z_{k}^{\textsc{NN}}
, and at

are supposedly close, the unfolding will be (almost) the same in the numerator and denominator of eq. (
3.5
). We will see if this remains true at the edge of the spectrum, where the spectral density varies on the order of the mean level spacing.

In the following we will call the complex spacing ratio
\lambda_{k}
to belong to the bulk, if the corresponding point satisfies
|z_{k}|<r_{b}
, having a radius less than what we chose to belong to the bulk. On the other hand it belongs to the (extended) edge, if it holds
|z_{k}|>r_{-}(r_{-}^{\prime})

from our statistics, where it is not clear a priori, if they belong to the bulk or to the edge. Choosing two different values
r_{-}^{\prime}<r_{-}
for the edge tests how far the edge actually extends.
It is remarkable that even in class A only approximate expressions exist for the complex spacing ratio in the bulk, as we have seen in the previous Section

3.3
NN and NNN spacing distributions

In this subsection we turn to numerical results for the NN and NNN spacing distribution at the edge in all 3 ensembles, and compare that to known (analytical and numerical) results in the bulk of the spectrum.
To set the stage, we begin with the general definition of the
NN spacings distribution
p_{\rm NN}^{(N)}(s)
for all ensembles, at finite
N
. After recalling the known analytical results for NN and NNN for class A and 2D Poisson in the bulk, we present a comparison for all 3 ensembles between bulk and edge.
A comparison to the conjectured cubic small argument behaviour (times a logarithm for class AII
†
) of
p^{(N)}(s)
for large-
N
at bulk and edge in all 3 classes concludes this subsection.

The probability that a complex eigenvalue
z_{1}
has its NN
z_{2}
at radial distance
s
, and all other eigenvalues at further distance, is given by

p_{\rm NN}^{(N)}(s)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{1}\dots\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{N}\jpdf^{(N)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})\delta^{(1)}\left(|z_{2}-z_{1}|-s\right)\prod_{j=3}^{N}\heaviside\left(|z_{j}-z_{1}|^{2}-s^{2}\right).

(3.9)

Here,
z_{1}
can be chosen in the bulk or at the edge of the spectrum.
The normalisation constant
\gamma_{N}
ensures that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,p_{\rm NN}^{(N)}(s)=1\ .

(3.10)

In addition, below we normalise the first moment to unity, to fix the scale,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,s\,p_{\rm NN}^{(N)}(s)=1\ .

(3.11)

In analogy to Section
2
, for the bulk of the spectrum we may condition the point
z_{1}=0
to be at the origin, compare (
2.1
) vs. (
2.8
):

\displaystyle p_{\rm C,NN}^{(N)}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma_{N,\rm C}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{2}\dots\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{N}\jpdf^{(N)}(0,z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\delta^{(1)}\left(|z_{2}|-s\right)\prod_{j=3}^{N}\heaviside\left(|z_{j}|^{2}-s^{2}\right)

(3.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma_{N,\rm C}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{3}\dots\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{N}\jpdf^{(N)}(0,s\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{2}},\dots,z_{N})\prod_{j=3}^{N}\heaviside\left(|z_{j}|^{2}-s^{2}\right).

(3.13)

Following
[
1
]
, in the GinUE explicit formulas for NN and NNN spacing distribution were derived at finite-
N
in class A. The idea was to choose the origin as a generic bulk point, and then use the translational invariance of the bulk in the large-
N
limit, to obtain a formula valid in the bulk in general.
In
[
1
]
the gap probability was derived first, replacing the Dirac-delta in (
3.12
) by a Heaviside function, with one eigenvalue
z_{1}=0
at the origin and all others at distance
s
or larger from the origin, cf. Appendix
A
for more details.
Likewise, gap probabilities for higher order gaps can be obtained and
its derivatives then lead to (
3.9
), and higher NNN spacings, cf.
[
1
]
. We only quote the results here for the NN and NNN spacing, conditioned to
z_{1}=0
in class A at finite
N
:

\displaystyle p_{\rm A,C\ \rm NN}^{(N)}(s)\;

\displaystyle=\;\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{\gamma(1+k,s^{2})}{k!}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{2s^{2j+1}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}}{\Gamma(1+j,s^{2})},

(3.14)

\displaystyle p_{\rm A,C\ \rm NNN}^{(N)}(s)\;

\displaystyle=\;\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{\gamma(1+k,s^{2})}{k!}\right)\sum_{j,k=1;k\neq j}^{N-1}\frac{\gamma(1+j,s^{2})}{\Gamma(1+j,s^{2})}\frac{2s^{2k+1}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}}{\Gamma(1+k,s^{2})}.

(3.15)

Here,
\Gamma(1+k,s^{2})=\int_{s^{2}}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,t^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-t}
and

\gamma(1+k,s^{2})=\int^{s^{2}}_{0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,t^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-t}
are the integral representations of the upper and lower incomplete
Gamma-functions, respectively. These exact relations converge rapidly for large
N
. Below we will plot them using a cutoff at around
N=20
for a good graphical representation.
Notice that in (
3.14
) the first moment still has to be set to unity as in (
3.11
), by rescaling
s\to s\ const.
with
const.\approx 1.1429
calculated numerically. This sets the scale and the same rescaling has to be applied to (
3.15
).

Technically speaking, the gap probability leading to (
3.14
) (and (
3.15
)) is the product of the exact Fredholm determinant eigenvalues of the kernel of this conditional determinantal point process. In Appendix
A
we applied the same technique to
|z_{1}|=1
, conditioned to be at the edge, giving the gap probability through the Fredholm determinant of the conditional kernel. Unfortunately, its eigenvalues are not explicitly known and its expansion does not easily lead to good approximations
[
57
]
. However, it can be used to determine the small-argument expansion of
p_{\rm A,C\ NN}^{(N)}(s)
in class A at the edge which is also cubic, to which we will return below.

For completeness we also give the NN and NNN spacings distribution for 2D Poisson (Poi) in the bulk of the spectrum,

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{Poi,\,NN}}^{\rm bulk}(s)\;

\displaystyle=\;\frac{\pi}{2}s\ \mathrm{e}^{-\pi s^{2}/4},

(3.16)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{Poi,\,NNN}}^{\rm bulk}(s)\;

\displaystyle=\;\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}s^{3}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\pi s^{2}/4},

(3.17)

see e.g.
[
16
]
for a derivation. Here, the large-
N
limit and normalisation (
3.11
) have already been applied. Notice that the NN spacing distribution shows a
linear
growth at small

for Poisson.

3.3.1
Unfolding at the spectral edge

Before we present our numerical results for the NN and NNN spacing distributions at the edge, and compare with the known results in the bulk, such as (
3.14
) – (
3.17
) for class A and 2D Poisson, we have to explain how to unfold at the edge of the spectrum.

In Hermitian RMT this is a standard procedure, cf.
[
27
]
, which is typically applied to the bulk of the spectrum. Also there, for a strongly varying respectively vanishing density at the edge, some care has to be taken, see
[
58
]
. In 2D, unfolding is much more sophisticated already in the bulk of the spectrum, see
[
12
,
18
,
55
]
for different proposals. Let us first describe the problem and then present heuristic arguments that lead us to an unfolding procedure. For that we will use the gap probability
E_{k}(s)
, that the open disc
D_{s}(z_{0})
of radius
s
around an eigenvalue
z_{0}
contains
k
further eigenvalues. Differentiating this for
k=0,1
then leads to the NN respectively NNN spacing distribution.

In general, the average spacing between eigenvalues in
D
dimensions is proportional to the mean density
R(z)^{1/D}
at that point. Because of that, we cannot compare the NN spacing distribution at points with different densities, in particular between bulk and edge, see Fig.
11
, and thus we need to unfold. Because at the edge the density is varying very strongly in the scale of the mean level spacing
\sim O(1/\sqrt{N})
, we propose the following procedure which is different from
[
12
,
18
,
55
]
.

Figure 11
:

Magnification of the edge part (left: side view; right top view) of the spectral density (
3.2
) for the GinUE class A at
N=50
, rescaled to have limiting support on the unit disc. The black circle covers the same volume in the bulk in this 3D plot,
as does the red dashed circle over the edge.

Generally in a point process, given a point at
z_{0}
and its
k
-th neighbour
z_{k}
in a region
\mathcal{A}
of constant mean density
R(z_{0})
, the expected
number of points closer than
z_{k}
is
R(z_{0})
times the area of the disc of radius
|z_{k}-z_{0}|
around
z_{0}
. Now imagine a region
\mathcal{B}
of constant but
different density
R^{\prime}(z_{0}^{\prime})
. A point
z_{0}^{\prime}
and its
k
-th neighbour
z_{k}^{\prime}
shall both be in region
\mathcal{B}
. Then, rather than the distances
|z_{k}-z_{0}|
and
|z_{k}^{\prime}-z_{0}^{\prime}|
,
the expected numbers of points in the discs should be compared.
In the general case of a point
z_{0}
in a region of variable density, this expected number of points within a disc of radius
s
is the integral over the density

m(s)=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{s}(z_{0})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z\ R(z).

(3.18)

In the bulk region of constant density
R_{\rm bulk}
, we have
m(s)=R_{\rm bulk}\pi s^{2}
. In our case, where the global density is normalised on the unit disc, we have
R_{\rm bulk}=1/\pi
, and thus
m(s)=s^{2}
.

For each edge eigenvalue
z_{0}
and its NN (NNN etc.),
we take the distance
s
and calculate
m(s)
numerically, given the position of the eigenvalue. Then, we calculate an unfolded distance
s^{\prime}
from the number
of expected points within the given distance from
z_{0}
,

s^{\prime}=\sqrt{m(s)}\ .

(3.19)

Here, we use the empirical density obtained from the simulations in (
3.18
).
6
6

6

Consistently, in the bulk we have
s^{\prime}=s
when normalising to
R_{\rm bulk}=1/\pi
.

For this purpose, we subdivided the radial range of the sample eigenvalue spectra into
about 1 million equally spaced bins and counted the number of occurring eigenvalues for each bin over all samples. The bins can be thought of as
concentric annuli around the origin in the complex plane. All our ensembles have rotational symmetry for the density, so this is sufficient for integrating each of the discs around edge eigenvalues.
For each annulus, we take the central circle (same distance to inner and outer boundary) and calculate its intersection length with a given disc
D_{s}(z_{0})
around an edge point
z_{0}
.
These lengths multiplied by the number of eigenvalues in the bin, and properly normalised, then sum to the integral (
3.18
),
as a good approximation for edge eigenvalues.

Note that, compared to
[
18
]
where the unfolding is done by rescaling the distance locally, we here perform an integral between the points. This assumes that the shortest distance between the points is a straight line, which is a good approximation. A possible improvement would be to find the NN using geodesic curves, as proposed in
[
12
]
, but that is beyond the scope of this work.

3.3.3
Small-argument expansion of NN spacing distributions at edge and bulk

Last but not least we turn to the small argument expansion of all NN spacing distributions. It has been found early on
[
26
]

2\times 2

p_{\rm A\,NN}^{(N=2)}(s)\sim s^{3},

(3.20)

[

[

p_{\rm AI^{\dagger}\,NN}^{(N=2)}(s)\sim-s^{3}\log(s).

(3.21)

This has to be contrasted with 2D Poisson (
3.16

for
s\ll 1

p_{\rm Poi\,NN}^{\rm bulk}(s)\sim s,

(3.22)

N

[

one year before
[

3.12

p_{\rm A\,NN}^{\rm bulk}(s)\sim s^{3},

(3.23)

also holds for the limit
N\to\infty

N

p_{\rm A\,NN}^{\rm edge}(s)\sim s^{3},

(3.24)

see (
A.26

and AII

4
Conclusions and open problems

In this article we have studied and compared the complex spacing ratio, nearest-neighbour (NN) and next-to-nearest neighbour (NNN) spacing distribution of three different symmetry classes of non-Hermitian random matrices, that are conjectured to provide the three generic universal scaling limits. In particular, we have shed some light on what characterises these different universality classes at the edge, compared to of the bulk of the spectrum.

In the first part we have analytically studied the complex spacing ratio in symmetry class A of the complex Ginibre ensemble (GinUE), for finite matrix size
N
. Introducing a conditional point process, we have clarified why an uncontrolled approximation used in the literature converges well. For the parameter-dependent elliptic Ginibre ensemble which is Gaussian, we have provided a surmise for the complex spacing ratio for
N=3
that interpolates towards the Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE), where it approximates the large-
N
limit in the bulk very well.

In the second part, based on extensive numerics we have investigated the edge universality classes of GinUE, complex symmetric (class AI
†
) and complex self-dual (class AII
†
) and compared them to two-dimensional (2D) Poisson statistics of uncorrelated points with a Gaussian distribution. Both for the complex spacing ratios, NN and NNN spacing distributions we have found a clear effect, distinguishing edge from bulk universality classes. This effect increases with the repulsion amongst eigenvalues, measured by an effective
\beta
-value of a 2D Coulomb gas description that was found to hold on local scales by part of the authors. Here, 2D Poisson corresponds to
\beta=0
without repulsion, and class AI
†
, A and AII
†
are ordered according to their increasing values of
\beta=1.4
, 2 and 2.6, respectively.

For the NN and NNN spacing distribution we provided an unfolding procedure at the edge of the spectrum for radially symmetric densities. Whilst this showed that for 2D Poisson the bulk and edge statistics remain identical, a clear distinction remained between bulk and edge distributions for all 3 symmetry classes. Because for the complex spacing ratio we found a difference also for 2D Poisson, this raises the question if the complex spacing ratio does provide properly unfolded quantities when the density varies on the scale of the mean level spacing, as it happens at the edge. Clearly, further research is needed in this direction. For example at the origin, where products of
m
non-Hermitian random matrices of class A display a strongly varying density, with distinct universality classes labelled by
m
, it is open if taking a complex spacing ratio will lead to a fully unfolded quantity.

A further open task is to derive more analytical results for classes AI
†
and AII
†
, for example for the NN spacing in the bulk, or the distribution of the largest eigenvalue in radius at the edge, given recently developed tools.
In view of the interpolation between the class A and the GUE that is provided by the eGinUE, and that leads to a transition between the Tracy–Widom and Gumbel distribution for the largest eigenvalue, this will be very interesting to achieve for the other symmetry classes.

Appendix A
Analytical results at the edge for NN spacing in class A

In this appendix we present the analytical framework for computing the NN spacing distribution in class A at the edge of the spectrum.
Being a determinantal point process (DPP) we review some of its properties. In particular, we express the gap probability to have an eigenvalue at the edge of the spectrum and its NN at radial distance
s
. It is given by the Fredholm determinant of the conditional kernel, from which the NN spacing distribution follows by differentiation.

The point process in the complex plane of class A, given by the joint density (
2.5
) at
\beta=2
, represents a DPP given as follows:

\jpdf^{(N)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\left[K_{\rm A}^{(N)}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{j})\right].

(A.1)

The reproducing kernel of planar orthogonal polynomials
P_{k}(z)
with respect to the weight function
\omega(z)
, satisfying
\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z\,\omega(z)P_{k}(z){P_{l}(\bar{z})}=\delta_{k,l}
, is defined as

K_{\rm A}^{(N)}(z,\bar{v})=\sqrt{\omega(z)\omega(\overline{u})}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}P_{j}(z)P_{j}(\bar{u})\ .

(A.2)

The kernel is unique only up to cocycles,
K_{\rm A}^{(N)}(z,\bar{v})\to\frac{f(z)}{f(\bar{u})}K_{\rm A}^{(N)}(z,\bar{v})
, for non-vanishing functions
f(z)
, as they cancel after taking them out of the determinant in (
A.1
).
Examples include the GinUE and its kernel with respect to
\omega(z)=\exp[-|z|^{2}]
, where the orthogonal polynomials are monomials:

K_{\rm GinUE}^{(N)}(z,\overline{u})=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(|z|^{2}+|u|^{2})}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(z\overline{u})^{j}}{j!}\ .

(A.3)

A second example is the corresponding expression for the eGinUE (
2.7
), in terms of planar Hermite polynomials, cf.
[
3
]
.
In DPP all
k
-point density correlation functions (marginals) are given by the
k\times k
determinant of the corresponding kernel. In the simplest case
k=1
we have for the density

R_{\rm A}^{(N)}(z)=K_{\rm A}^{(N)}(z,\bar{z}),

(A.4)

which is normalised to
N
here. In the case of the GinUE it reads

R_{\rm GinUE}^{(N)}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-|z|^{2}}\scalebox{1.2}{e}_{N-1}(|z|^{2})=\frac{1}{\pi}Q(N,|z|^{2})\ .

(A.5)

In order to converge to the circular law, as shown in Fig.
4
, we have to rescale the eigenvalues
z\to z\sqrt{N}
.
For later convenience we defined the truncated exponential
\scalebox{1.2}{e}_{N}(x)
inside (
A.3
) as

\scalebox{1.2}{e}_{N}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}=\mathrm{e}^{x}Q(N,x)\ ,

(A.6)

which can be expressed in term of the normalised upper incomplete Gamma-function
Q(N,x)=\frac{\Gamma(N,x)}{\Gamma(N)}
. This will become convenient when considering asymptotics later.

Let us turn to conditional point processes. As in the derivation of the NN spacing distribution in the bulk
[
1
]
, or in the complex spacing ratio (
2.8
), it turns out to be convenient to condition a point to be in the bulk or at the edge. In view of the form of the Vandermonde determinant (
2.6
), conditioning point
z_{1}
to be fixed, while integrating over all remaining points, leads to the following split of variables:

|\Delta_{N}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{N})|^{2}=\left(\prod_{j=2}^{N}(z_{j}-z_{1})(\overline{z_{j}}-\overline{z_{1}})\right)|\Delta_{N-1}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{N})|^{2}.

(A.7)

This corresponds to the insertion of two complex conjugate characteristic polynomials at argument
z_{1}
respectively
\overline{z_{1}}
, or a modified weight function

\omega(z)\to(z-z_{1})(\overline{z}-\overline{z_{1}})\omega(z)
.
As shown in this example for a more general result,
[
43
, eq. (3.10)]
, the kernel of a DPP conditioned on one eigenvalue
z_{0}
is again a DPP with conditional kernel
K_{\rm A,C}^{(N)}(z,\bar{u})
given by

K_{\rm A,C}^{(N)}(z,\bar{u})=\frac{|z-z_{0}||u-z_{0}|}{(z-z_{0})(\overline{u}-\overline{z_{0}})}\frac{\begin{vmatrix}K_{\rm A}^{(N+1)}(z,\bar{u})&K_{\rm A}^{(N+1)}(z,\overline{z_{0}})\\
K_{\rm A}^{(N+1)}(z_{0},\bar{u})&K_{\rm A}^{(N+1)}(z_{0},\overline{z_{0}})\end{vmatrix}}{K_{\rm A}^{(N+1)}(z_{0},\overline{z_{0}})}.

(A.8)

Compared to
[
43
]
we have multiplied the weight functions into the determinant.
In particular, the conditional kernel can be expressed in term of the unconditional one, of one degree higher. For simplicity we drop the dependence on the conditioning
point
z_{0}
of the conditional kernel. This relation is a more general feature, valid when inserting more characteristic polynomials, or equivalently conditioning on more points, see
[
43
]
.

In the case of the GinUE, we obtain from (

) for the conditional kernel

K_{{\rm GinUE,C}}^{(N)}(z,\bar{u})=\frac{|z-z_{0}||u-z_{0}|e^{-\frac{1}{2}(|z|^{2}+|u|^{2})}}{\pi(z_{0}-z)(\overline{z_{0}}-\overline{u})\scalebox{1.2}{e}_{N+1}(|z_{0}|^{2})}\big(\scalebox{1.2}{e}_{N+1}(|z_{0}|^{2})\scalebox{1.2}{e}_{N+1}(z\overline{u})-\scalebox{1.2}{e}_{N+1}(z_{0}\overline{u})\scalebox{1.2}{e}_{N+1}(z\overline{z_{0}})\big).

When investigating the local NN spacing around the conditioning point
z_{0}

In the second step we have simplified the expressions and dropped cocycles, indicated by

In the following we are interested in the limiting behaviour at the spectral edge. First, in order to obtain a compactly supported density, we have to rescale

. In the limit

. Thanks to the rotational symmetry of the global density, it suffices to

z_{0}=1

(A.11)

Applying this to (

K_{\rm GinUE,C}^{\rm edge}(\xi_{1},\overline{\xi_{2}})=\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}(|\xi_{1}|^{2}+|\xi_{2}|^{2})}}{2\pi}\left(e^{\xi_{1}\overline{\xi_{2}}}\erfc\left[\frac{\xi_{1}+\overline{\xi_{2}}}{\sqrt{2}}+\sqrt{2}d\right]-\frac{\erfc\left[\frac{\xi_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}+\sqrt{2}d\right]\erfc\left[\frac{\overline{\xi_{2}}}{\sqrt{2}}+\sqrt{2}d\right]}{\erfc[{\sqrt{2}d}]}\right).
