Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we study the well-posedness of backward doubly stochastic differential equations (BDSDEs), both with and without reflection, under weak conditions.
First, when the generator
f
is of general growth in
y
and linear growth in
z
, we establish the existence, uniqueness, comparison principle, and the existence of maximal solutions for BDSDEs, with or without reflection.
Second, under the assumption that
f
is of linear growth in
y
and quadratic growth in
z
, and that the terminal value is bounded, we prove the existence, uniqueness, and comparison principle for reflected and non-reflected BDSDEs.
Finally, when the generator
f
is of general growth in
y
and quadratic growth in
z
, again with a bounded terminal value, we prove the existence of maximal solutions for BDSDEs in both the reflected and non-reflected situations.

Key words:

Backward doubly stochastic differential equations; reflection; general growth; quadratic growth

AMS subject classifications:
60H10; 60H20

1
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which two standard independent Brownian motions
\{W_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
and
\{B_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
, with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, respectively, are defined. Let
T>0
be a fixed terminal time and denote by
\mathscr{N}
the class of
\mathbb{P}
-null sets of
\mathscr{F}
, where

\mathscr{F}_{t}\triangleq\mathscr{F}_{t}^{W}\vee\mathscr{F}_{t,T}^{B},\quad\ \mathscr{G}_{t}=\mathscr{F}^{W}_{t}\vee\mathscr{F}^{B}_{T},\quad\ \forall t\in[0,T].

In the above, for any process
\{\eta_{t};0\leqslant t\leqslant T\},

\mathscr{F}_{s,t}^{\eta}=\sigma\{\eta_{r}-\eta_{s};s\leqslant r\leqslant t\}\vee\mathscr{N}
and
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\eta}=\mathscr{F}_{0,t}^{\eta}
.
Note that the collection
\mathbb{G}\triangleq\{\mathscr{G}_{t};t\in[0,T]\}
forms a filtration, whereas
\mathbb{F}\triangleq\{\mathscr{F}_{t};t\in[0,T]\}
does not, since it is neither increasing nor decreasing. We consider the following reflected backward doubly stochastic differential equations (RBDSDEs, for short):

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})dB_{s}+K_{T}-K_{t}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.\\
&Y_{t}\geqslant S_{t}\quad\ \hbox{and}\quad\ \int_{0}^{T}(Y_{s}-S_{s})dK_{s}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

In the above equations,
dB
-integral is a backward Itô’s integral and
dW
-integral is a forward Itô’s integral. The terminal value
\xi
is
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable, and
f:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l}
are jointly measurable.
The solution
Y
is forced to remain above a given stochastic process
S
, referred to as the obstacle.
The process
K
is a continuous increasing process, which acts to push the solution
Y
upwards in order to keep it above
S
, characterized by the
Skorokhod condition.

A special case of RBDSDEs with
S=-\infty
and
K=0
is the following
backward doubly stochastic differential equations (BDSDEs, for short):

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(1.2)

which were first introduced by Pardoux and Peng
[
36
]
. Under uniform Lipschitz assumptions on the coefficients
f
and
g
, they established that for any square-integrable terminal value

\xi
, BDSDE (
1.2
) admits a unique solution pair
(Y,Z)
.
Since then, BDSDEs have significant applications in areas such as stochastic control and partial differential equations
(see
[
7
,
20
,
17
,
42
]
).
Additionally, there have been some studies on the existence and uniqueness of BDSDEs under weaker conditions for the generator
f
. In particular, a key contribution to the theory of BDSDEs with quadratic growth came from Hu, Wen, and Xiong
[
22
]
.
Some other recent works on BDSDEs with weak conditions can be found in
[
43
,
33
,
45
]
, among others.

On the other hand, when
g=0
in RBDSDEs (
1.1
), the equations reduce to the following form:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds+K_{T}-K_{t}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.\\
&Y_{t}\geqslant S_{t}\quad\ \hbox{and}\quad\ \int_{0}^{T}(Y_{s}-S_{s})dK_{s}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

These equations, known as reflected backward stochastic differential equations (RBSDEs), were first introduced by El Karoui et al.
[
13
]
and have important applications in finance.
Specifically,
S_{t}
represents the payoff process, and the increasing process
K
can be interpreted as the cumulative effort required to keep the solution above the barrier, thus offering a dynamic representation of the cost of constraint enforcement.
Since then, the study of reflected BSDEs under non-Lipschitz conditions has grown significantly. For example, Matoussi
[
31
]
proved the existence of reflected BSDEs with a continuous coefficient. Kobylanski et al.
[
24
]
considered the quadratic growth case, while Lepeltier, Matoussi, and Xu
[
27
]
extended the study to monotone and general increasing growth conditions. For further work on reflected BDSDEs, see
[
41
,
19
,
38
]
.

In order to handle more complex stochastic environments where noise evolves in both forward and backward directions, such as in stochastic control, PDEs, and mathematical finance, there are significant motivations to study RBDSDE (
1.1
).
However, despite their importance, the literature on this topic remains limited. To the best of our knowledge, the first work in this direction is by Bahlali et al.
[
5
]
, who considered RBDSDEs under uniform Lipschitz continuity conditions. Some other results have been obtained in
[
1
,
25
,
39
,
44
,
2
,
29
]
.
However, the assumptions in the above works are relatively strong. In particular, results for RBDSDEs with generator is of general growth in
y
or quadratic growth in
z
are currently unavailable. This gap restricts the further development and application of RBDSDEs. One important reason is that the usual techniques applied in classical reflected BSDEs (as in El Karoui et al.
[
13
]
) do not apply here, since the family
\{\mathscr{F}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
is not constitute a filtration.

The aim of this paper is to address the above gaps by studying the well-posedness of RBDSDE (
1.1
) under weaker conditions, with particular emphasis on the quadratic growth in
z
.
This work is also inspired by one of the open problems proposed by Peng
[
37
]
.
In order to better introduce our main results and techniques, we would like to present some results about BSDEs with weak conditions.
For instance, On the one hand, for the non-Lipschitz condition of the generator
f
in
y
,
an earlier result was proposed by Mao
[
30
]
.
Lepeltier and Martin
[
28
]
proved the existence of BSDEs when the generator
f
is continuous in
y
. Additionally, Pardoux
[
34
]
studied the case of
f
is of general growth and monotonic in
y
.
On the other hand, for the non-Lipschitz condition of the generator
f
in
z
,
Kobylanski
[
23
]
first established the existence and uniqueness of BSDEs with quadratic growth in
z
. Lepeltier and San Martin
[
26
]
studied the case of
f
is super-linear growth in
y
and quadratic growth in
z
.
Briand, Relepeltier, and San Martin
[
6
]
studied a general existence result when
f
is of general growth and monotonic in
y
, and quadratic growth in
z
.
Moreover, for quadratic BSDEs, Briand and Hu
[
3
,
4
]
studied the case of one-dimensional situations with unbounded terminal value, and Hu and Tang
[
21
]
, Fan, Hu, and Tang
[
14
]
studied the multi-dimensional situations.
Some other recently results about quadratic BSDEs could be found in
[
22
,
16
,
9
]
, etc.

We now state the main results and methods concerning the well-posedness of RBDSDE (
1.1
) under weak conditions, which involve the following two main difficulties:

(i)

Standard techniques for addressing the general growth conditions of
f
in
y
rely on conditional expectations (see Pardoux
[
34
]
). However, these methods cannot be applied to RBDSDE (
1.1
), because the presence of the backward stochastic integral implies that
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t};t\in[0,T]\}
is not a filtration.

(ii)

The BMO method, a standard tool for handling BSDEs with quadratic growth, is inapplicable to RBDSDE (
1.1
). This limitation arises from two factors: first, RBDSDEs do not possess the martingale property; second, counterexamples constructed by Hu et al.
[
22
]
show that a bounded terminal value does not guarantee a bounded solution when
f
is of quadratic growth in
z
.

In order to prove the well-posedness of RBDSDE (
1.1
) under weak conditions, we introduce some new methods to address the above difficulties.
The main contributions and innovations of this paper are as follows:

(i)

When the generator
f
is of general growth and monotonic in
y
(see the condition
(F1)
below) and Lipschitz in
z
, we establish the existence, uniqueness, and comparison principle of solutions to RBDSDEs (
1.1
) (see
Theorem 3.7
).
It should be pointed out that, our this result is novel even in the case of RBDSDEs (
1.1
) without reflection (see
Theorem 3.2
).
The main ideas to the proof is that, in order to derive an estimate for the solution
Y
, note that the conditional expectation could not be applied here, so we start from by considering the case when the generator
f
does not depend on
Z
and the coefficient
g
does not depend on
(Y,Z)
.
For this simple case, we use a truncation technique to the solution of a given simple BDSDE firstly and then construct a sequence for the solutions of BSDEs to prove the existence and uniqueness. Next, for the general case, we use the fixed point method to prove the existence and uniqueness.

(ii)

When the generator
f
is of general growth and monotonic in
y
(see the condition
(F1)
below) but not Lipschitz in
z
, we obtain the existence of maximal solutions for RBDSDEs (
1.1
) with or without reflection, respectively (see
Theorem 3.6
and
Theorem 3.10
).
Note that the classical approach to prove the existence of maximal solutions (see Lepeltier and San Martin
[
28
]
) involves constructing a “nice” approximation for the generator
f
, which can be used easily when
f
is of linear growth in
y
.
However, this method is hard to be used in the case of
f
is of general growth in
y
.
In order to address this difficulty, we use a truncation technique and derive a priori estimate for the solution
Y
.

(iii)

When
f
is of linear growth in
y
and quadratic growth in
z
(see the condition
(F2)
below), we establish the existence, uniqueness, and comparison principle of solutions to RBDSDEs (
1.1
) with or without reflection, respectively (see
Theorem 4.2
and
Theorem 4.11
).
Additionally, In order to prove the existence and uniqueness, we impose a condition
(G2)
(see below) for the coefficient
g
, which is weaker than the related condition proposed by Hu, Wen, and Xiong
[
22
]
.
With bounded prior estimates for the solution
Y
(see
4.1
and
4.9
), we prove existence by using the monotone stability theorem and double approximation techniques.
For the uniqueness, we use a comparison principle to prove it. In detail, we first prove it under astructure condition
(STR)
(see
Theorem 4.8
), and then prove it complete through a method of change of variables.

(iv)

When
f
is of general growth in
y
and quadratic growth in
z
(see the condition
(F3)
below), we obtain the existence of maximal solutions to RBDSDEs (
1.1
) with or without reflection, respectively (see
Theorem 4.4
and
Theorem 4.13
).
For simplicity of presentation, we initially consider the case when
f
is of linear growth in
y
. By using a generalized comparison theorem, we estimate the bounds of the solution
Y
by a pair of ordinary differential equations (ODEs, for short). We then use a truncation technique to establish the existence of maximal solutions to RBDSDEs (
1.1
) with or without reflection, respectively.
Finally, for the general case of
f
is of general growth in
y
, we use a similar method as in the linear growth situation to prove the results.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In
Section 2
, some preliminaries and spaces are introduced.
In
Section 3
, we study the case when the generator
f
is of general growth in
y
and linear growth in
z
.
In
Section 4
, we firstly consider the case when
f
is of linear growth in
y
and quadratic growth in
z
, and then study the case of
f
is of general growth in
y
and quadratic growth in
z
.

Preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which two standard independent Brownian motions
\{W_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
and
\{B_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
, with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, respectively, are defined. Let
T>0
be a fixed terminal time and denote by
\mathscr{N}
the class of
\mathbb{P}
-null sets of
\mathscr{F}
, where

\mathscr{F}_{t}\triangleq\mathscr{F}_{t}^{W}\vee\mathscr{F}_{t,T}^{B},\quad\ \mathscr{G}_{t}=\mathscr{F}^{W}_{t}\vee\mathscr{F}^{B}_{T},\quad\ \forall t\in[0,T].

In the above, for any process
\{\eta_{t};0\leqslant t\leqslant T\},

\mathscr{F}_{s,t}^{\eta}=\sigma\{\eta_{r}-\eta_{s};s\leqslant r\leqslant t\}\vee\mathscr{N}
and
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\eta}=\mathscr{F}_{0,t}^{\eta}
.
Note that the collection
\mathbb{G}\triangleq\{\mathscr{G}_{t};t\in[0,T]\}
forms a filtration, whereas
\mathbb{F}\triangleq\{\mathscr{F}_{t};t\in[0,T]\}
does not, since it is neither increasing nor decreasing.
For any
p\geqslant 1
,
t\in[0,T]
and Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
, we introduce the following spaces:

\displaystyle L_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\ \Big|\ \xi\text{ is $\mathscr{F}_{T}$-measurable process and }\|\xi\|_{L^{p}}\triangleq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi|^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle L_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\ \Big|\ \xi\text{ is $\mathscr{F}_{T}$-measurable process and }\|\xi\|_{\infty}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}_{\omega\in\Omega}|\xi(\omega)|<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}([t,T];\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\ \Big|\ \varphi\text{ is $\mathscr{F}_{s}$ measurable process and }

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\|\varphi\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(t,T)}\triangleq\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{t}^{T}|\varphi_{s}|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}([t,T];\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\ \Big|\ \varphi_{s}\text{ is }\mathscr{F}_{s}\text{-measurable, continuous, and }

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\|\varphi\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(t,T)}\triangleq\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|\varphi_{s}|^{p}\Big]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}([t,T];\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\ \Big|\ \varphi_{s}\text{ is }\mathscr{F}_{s}\text{-measurable, continuous, and }

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\|\varphi\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}_{(s,\omega)\in[t,T]\times\Omega}|\varphi_{s}(\omega)|<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle A_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}([t,T];\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=\Big\{K:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\ \Big|\ K_{s}\text{ is }\mathscr{F}_{s}\text{-measurable, continuous increasing process, and }

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad K(0)=0,\quad\mathbb{E}\left[K(T)^{p}\right]<\infty\Big\}.

Definition 2.1
.

A triple of measurable processes
(Y,Z,K)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times A^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})
is called a solution of reflected BDSDE (
1.1
) with obstacle
S
, if
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, it satisfies (
1.1
).

2.1
Assumptions

In this subsection, we introduce several assumptions that will be used in
Section 3
and
Section 4
. These include the linear and quadratic growth conditions of the generator
f
with respect to
Z
, as well as the boundedness of the terminal value
\xi
, among others. We group these conditions together here to clarify their similarities and differences as comprehensively as possible.
In the following, we always let
C>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
be some constants, and
\lambda(t)
be some non-negative stochastic function,
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be some non-decreasing, continuous function,
l:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

be some strictly positive, continuous function.

\textbf{(A1)}\quad\xi\in L_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}).\qquad\qquad\textbf{(A2)}\quad\xi\in L_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}).\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\

Assume that the coefficient
f(t,y,z):[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is measurable with respect to
\mathscr{P}\otimes\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and continuous with respect to
(y,z)
, such that for any

(t,\omega,y,y^{\prime},z)\in[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
f(\cdot,y,z)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
,

(F1)

\displaystyle|f(t,y,z)|\leqslant|f(t,0,0)|+\varphi(|y|)+C|z|\hbox{ with }\varphi(0)=0,\hbox{ and }

(2.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\left(f(t,y,z)-f(t,y^{\prime},z)\right)\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)\leqslant\mu(y-y^{\prime})^{2}.

(F2)

\displaystyle|f(t,y,z)|\leqslant C(1+|y|+|z|^{2}).

(2.2)

(F3)

\displaystyle|f(t,y,z)|\leqslant\varphi(|y|)+C|z|^{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\left(f(t,y,z)-f(t,y^{\prime},z)\right)\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)\leqslant\mu(y-y^{\prime})^{2}.

(2.3)

Assume that the coefficient
g:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l}
is measurable with respect
to
\mathscr{P}\otimes\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that for any

(t,\omega,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
g(\cdot,y,z)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{l})
,

(G1)

\displaystyle|g(t,y,z)-g(t,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})|^{2}\leqslant C|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}+\alpha|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

(G2)

\displaystyle|g(t,y,z)|^{2}\leqslant|\lambda(t)|^{2}+\alpha|z|^{2}.

(G3)

\displaystyle\varphi\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}g(s,0,0)dB_{s}~\Big|~\mathscr{G}_{t}\right]\Bigg)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}).

For the obstacle
S:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we always assume that
S_{T}\leqslant\xi
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.. Additionally, we give the following assumptions:

(S1)

\displaystyle S^{+}\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi^{2}(\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}e^{\mu t}S_{t}^{+})\right]<\infty.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

(S2)

\displaystyle S\in S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}).

3
Linear growth situation

In this section, we study the existence, uniqueness, and maximal solutions for BDSDEs and reflected BDSDEs when the generator
f
is of general growth with respect to
Y
and linear growth with respect to
Z
. Moreover, on one hand, to better highlight the differences in these results, we will analyze these cases separately in four situations, as the conditions on the coefficient
f
with respect to
Z
differ among them.
On the other hand, since an exponential transformation will be frequently used in the proofs, we first provide the following remark for simplicity of presentation.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that the triple
(Y,Z,K)
is a solution of reflected BDSDE (
1.1
) if and only if the following triple

\displaystyle(\bar{Y},\bar{Z},\bar{K})\triangleq\left(e^{\mu t}Y_{t},e^{\mu t}Z_{t},\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mu s}dK_{s}\right)

(3.1)

solves reflected BDSDE (
1.1
) with
({\xi},{f},{g},S)
replaced by
(\bar{\xi},\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{S})
, where

\displaystyle\big(\bar{\xi},\bar{f}(t,y,z),\bar{g}(t,y,z),\bar{S}_{t}\big)=\big(e^{\mu T}\xi,\ e^{\mu t}f(t,e^{-\mu t}y,e^{-\mu t}z)-\mu y,\ e^{\mu t}g(t,e^{-\mu t}y,e^{-\mu t}z),\ e^{\mu t}S_{t}\big).

Moreover, the transformed coefficient

\bar{f}
satisfies the same monotonicity condition as in
(F1)

with
\mu=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left(\bar{f}(t,y,z)-\bar{f}(t,y^{\prime},z)\right)\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)\leqslant 0,\quad\ \forall(t,\omega,y,y^{\prime},z)\in[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

which implies that
\bar{f}
is decreasing in
y
.
Meanwhile, the transformed obstacle
\bar{S}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}(\bar{S}_{t}^{+})^{2}\right]<\infty\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi^{2}(\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}(\bar{S}_{t}^{+}))\right]<\infty.

Based on
3.1
, we will adopt assumptions
(F1)
and
(S1)
following a variable transformation in the proof of this section.

3.1
BDSDE: existence and uniqueness

In this part, we study the existence and uniqueness of BDSDEs when the generator
f
is of general growth with respect to
Y
, which is weaker than the conditions of Pardoux and Peng
[
36
]
, and
Wu and Zhang
[
43
]
.
However, when the generator
f
has general growth in
Y
, the classical methods for BSDEs are difficult to apply to the case of BDSDEs. The reason is that the classical approach relies on conditional expectations, whereas such conditional expectations cannot be used for BDSDEs because
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t};t\in[0,T]\}
is not a filtration.
Therefore, to overcome these difficulties, we use the truncation and construction techniques.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let the conditions
(A1)
,
(F1)
,
(G1)
and
(G3)
be satisfied. Moreover, assume that
f
is Lipschitz in
z
with the constant
C
,
then BDSDE (
1.2
) admits a unique solution

(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Before proceeding with the proof of existence and uniqueness, we first recall some results concerning the Yosida approximations of monotone and continuous functions (see Da Prato and Zabczyk
[
11
]
, and Hu
[
18
]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a continuous function satisfying

\displaystyle\left(x^{1}-x^{2},F(x^{1})-F(x^{2})\right)\leqslant 0,\qquad x^{1},x^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a unique
x=J^{\varepsilon}(y)
such that

\displaystyle x-\varepsilon F(x)=y.

Moreover, for any
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{1},\varepsilon^{2}>0
, and
x,x^{1},x^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle|J^{\varepsilon}(x^{1})-J^{\varepsilon}(x^{2})|\leqslant|x^{1}-x^{2}|\quad\hbox{and}\quad\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}J^{\varepsilon}(x)=x,

\displaystyle\left(x^{1}-x^{2},F^{\varepsilon}(x^{1})-F^{\varepsilon}(x^{2})\right)\leqslant 0\quad\hbox{and}\quad|F^{\varepsilon}(x^{1})-F^{\varepsilon}(x^{2})|\leqslant\frac{2}{\varepsilon}|x^{1}-x^{2}|,

\displaystyle|F^{\varepsilon}(x)|\leqslant|F(x)|\quad\hbox{and}\quad\big(x^{1}-x^{2},F^{\varepsilon^{1}}(x^{1})-F^{\varepsilon^{2}}(x^{2})\big)\leqslant(\varepsilon^{1}+\varepsilon^{2})(|F(x^{1})|+|F(x^{2})|)^{2},

where
F^{\varepsilon}
is the Yosida approximations of the function
F
defined by

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}(x)=F(J^{\varepsilon}(x))=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}[J^{\varepsilon}(x)-x].

Additionally,
for any set
\{x^{\varepsilon}\}_{{\varepsilon>0}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}x^{\varepsilon}=x,
then

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}F^{\varepsilon}(x^{\varepsilon})=F(x).

Before proving Theorem
3.2
, we first present the following result, which addresses the existence and uniqueness of solutions to BDSDE (
1.2
) when the generator
f
is independent of
Z
and the generator
g
is independent of
(Y,Z)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem 3.2
, the following BDSDE

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g(s)dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T

(3.2)

admits a unique solution
(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Existence.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(x)=\frac{\inf\{n,|x|\}}{|x|}x.

(3.3)

Let

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{0}=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}g(s)dB_{s}~\Big|~\mathscr{G}_{t}\right].

An extension of Itô’s martingale representation theorem yields that there exists a
\mathscr{G}_{t}
-measurable process
Z^{0}_{t}
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{0}=\int_{t}^{T}g(s)dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{0}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.4)

Similar to the work of Pardoux and Peng
[
36
, Proposition 1.2]
, one can verify that
(Y_{t}^{0},Z_{t}^{0})
is
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable and
(Y_{t}^{0},Z_{t}^{0})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Step 1
: Assume that there is a positive constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\infty}+\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|f(t,0)|\leqslant c.

Denote

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{0,n}=\rho_{n}(Y_{t}^{0})\quad\ \hbox{and}\quad\ \bar{f}_{n}(t,y)=f(t,y+Y_{t}^{0,n}).

Let
\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon>0
be the Yosida approximations of the generator
\bar{f}_{n}
. Then, the following BSDE

\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T

(3.5)

admits a unique solution

(\bar{Y}^{n,\varepsilon},\bar{Z}^{n,\varepsilon})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Applying Itô’s formula to
e^{t}|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon}|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle e^{t}|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{s}\left(|\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\ e^{T}|\xi|^{2}+2\int_{t}^{T}e^{s}\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon}\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon})ds-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{s}\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon}\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon}dW_{s}.

Note that

\displaystyle 2y\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,y)\leqslant|y|^{2}+\big(|f(t,0)|+\varphi(n)\big)^{2}.

Taking conditional expectation on both sides, we obtain that

\displaystyle e^{t}|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon}|^{2}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[e^{T}|\xi|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}e^{s}\big(|f(s,0)|+\varphi(n)\big)^{2}ds~\Big|~\mathscr{F}_{t}\right],

thus
for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon}|\leqslant\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\left[e^{T}\left(|\xi|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\big(|f(s,0)|+\varphi(n)\big)^{2}ds\right)~\Big|~\mathscr{F}_{t}\right]\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\triangleq X_{n}.

Next, we prove that the sequence
(\bar{Y}^{n,\varepsilon},\bar{Z}^{n,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
converges in
S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For this, by applying
Itô’s formula to
|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}|^{2}ds=

\displaystyle\ \int_{t}^{T}2\left(\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\left(\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon^{1}}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}})-\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon^{2}}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}})\right)ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}2\left(\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\left(\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}\right)dW_{s}.

From
3.3
, we have that

\displaystyle\left(\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\left(\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon^{1}}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}})-\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon^{2}}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}})\right)\leqslant(\varepsilon^{1}+\varepsilon^{2})\left(|\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon^{1}}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}})|+|\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon^{2}}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}})|\right)^{2}.

Therefore, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}|^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}|^{2}ds\leqslant 2(\varepsilon^{1}+\varepsilon^{2})\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\left(|\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon^{1}}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}})|+|\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon^{2}}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}})|\right)^{2}ds.

(3.6)

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|\bar{f}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,\bar{Y}^{n,\varepsilon})|\leqslant|f(t,0)|+\varphi(X_{n}+n)\ \in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}).

Thus, let
\varepsilon^{1},\varepsilon^{2}\rightarrow 0
in (
3.6
), we can get

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}|^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{1}}-\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon^{2}}|^{2}ds\longrightarrow 0.

Moreover, by using Burkholder–Davis–Gundy’s inequality, we can derive that the sequence
(\bar{Y}^{n,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is also a Cauchy sequences in
S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
.
This implies that there exists a limiting process
(\bar{Y}^{n},\bar{Z}^{n})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n,\varepsilon}-\bar{Y}^{n}_{t}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|\bar{Z}_{s}^{n,\varepsilon}-\bar{Z}^{n}_{s}|^{2}ds\right]\longrightarrow 0\quad\hbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0.

Letting
\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0
in (
3.5
) and applying the dominated convergence theorem, we can conclude that the limiting process
(\bar{Y}^{n},\bar{Z}^{n})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies

\bar{Y}_{t}^{n}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}_{n}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\bar{Z}_{s}^{n}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(3.7)

where

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{n}(t,y)\longrightarrow\bar{f}(t,y)\quad\ \hbox{with}\quad\ \bar{f}(t,y)=f(t,y+Y_{t}^{0}).

Applying the same technique from (
3.5
) to (
3.7
),
we can deduce that for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n}|\leqslant\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\left[e^{T}\left(|\xi|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\big(|f(s,0)|+\varphi(|Y_{t}^{0}|)\big)^{2}ds\right)~\Big|~\mathscr{F}_{t}\right]\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\triangleq X_{0}.

Note that under the assumptions of
Theorem 3.2
,
\bar{f}(t,y)
is continuous in
y
, and for any
(t,\omega,y)\in[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|\bar{f}(t,y)|\leqslant|\bar{f}(t,0)|+\bar{\varphi}(|y|)\quad\ \hbox{and}\quad\ \bar{f}(t,y)-\bar{f}(t,y^{\prime})(y-y^{\prime})\leqslant 0,

where
\bar{\varphi}(|y|)=\varphi(|y|+|Y_{t}^{0}|)
.
From Itô’s formula, we obtain
that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n,m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{Y}_{t}^{n}-\bar{Y}_{t}^{m}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{Z}_{s}^{n}-\bar{Z}_{s}^{m}|^{2}ds\right]=2\lim\limits_{n,m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\left(\bar{Y}_{s}^{n}-\bar{Y}_{s}^{m}\right)\left(\bar{f}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{n})-\bar{f}(s,\bar{Y}_{s}^{m})\right)ds\leqslant 0.

Then, by Burkholder–Davis–Gundy’s inequality once more, we find that the sequence
(\bar{Y}_{t}^{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is bounded in
S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, which implies uniform integrability for any
t
.
Then, note that
\bar{f}_{n}(t,y)\longrightarrow\bar{f}(t,y)
for
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely and
\varphi(|Y_{t}^{0}|)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
.
Using Vitali convergence theorem, we can take the limit as
n\longrightarrow\infty
in (
3.7
). This leads to the existence of a limiting process
(\bar{Y},\bar{Z})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies the following BSDE

\displaystyle\bar{Y}_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}(s,\bar{Y}_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\bar{Z}_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.8)

Define
Y_{t}=\bar{Y}_{t}+Y_{t}^{0},Z_{t}=\bar{Z}_{t}+Z_{t}^{0}
, then the process
(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
solves BDSDE (
3.2
).

Step 2
: The general case that
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and
f(t,0)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
.
Denote

\displaystyle\xi_{n}=\rho_{n}(\xi),\quad\ \hbox{and}\quad\ f_{n}(t,y)=f(t,y)-f(t,0)+\rho_{n}(f(t,0)).

Then,
it follows from Step 1 that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a solution
(Y^{n},Z^{n})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the following BDSDE:

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{n}=\xi_{n}+\int_{t}^{T}f_{n}(s,Y_{s}^{n})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g(s)dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{n}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.9)

From Itô’s formula, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{m}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{n}-Z_{s}^{m}|^{2}ds\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{n}-\xi_{m}|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\left(Y_{s}^{n}-Y_{s}^{m}\right)\left(f_{n}(s,Y_{s}^{n})-f_{m}(s,Y_{s}^{m})\right)ds,

where

\displaystyle 2\left(Y_{s}^{n}-Y_{s}^{m}\right)\left(f_{n}(s,Y_{s}^{n})-f_{m}(s,Y_{s}^{m})\right)\leqslant|Y_{s}^{n}-Y_{s}^{m}|^{2}+|f_{n}(s,0)-f_{m}(s,0)|^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{n}-\xi|^{2}\right]\longrightarrow 0\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|f_{n}(t,0)-f(t,0)|^{2}dt\longrightarrow 0\quad\hbox{as}\quad n\longrightarrow\infty.

It follows from Gronwall’s inequality and Burkholder–Davis–Gundy’s inequality, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|Y_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{m}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{n}-Z_{s}^{m}|^{2}ds\right]\longrightarrow 0\quad\hbox{as}\quad n,m\longrightarrow\infty.

Finally, by letting
n\longrightarrow\infty
in (
3.9
) and applying the dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that there exists a pair process
(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
which is a solution to BDSDE (
3.2
).

Uniqueness.

If there exist two solutions
(Y^{1},Z^{1}),(Y^{2},Z^{2})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
solve BDSDE (
3.2
). Applying Itô’s formula to
|Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2}|^{2}
yields that

\displaystyle|Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2}|^{2}=\int_{t}^{T}\bigg(2(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})\left(f(s,Y_{s}^{1})-f(s,Y_{s}^{2})\right)-|Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}\bigg)ds-2\int_{t}^{T}(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})dW_{s}.

Since
f
is monotonicity in
y
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2}|^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}ds\leqslant 0,

which implies that
Y^{1}=Y^{2},

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and
Z^{1}=Z^{2}
,
dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
∎

Now we give the proof of the main result in this subsection.

Proof of
Theorem 3.2
.

The uniqueness can be established similar to
3.4
. Here, we will focus on proving the existence.
Using
3.4
, for any given
(U,V)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a unique solution
(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})

such that

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},V_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,U_{s},V_{s})dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.10)

We can construct a mapping
\Theta
as follows: let
(Y,Z)=\Theta(U,V)
be the solution of BDSDE (
3.10
).
Now take
(U,V),(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and denote
(Y,Z)=\Theta(U,V)
,
(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})=\Theta(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})
, and

\displaystyle\triangle U=U-U^{\prime},\qquad\triangle V=V-V^{\prime},\qquad\triangle Y=Y-Y^{\prime},\qquad\triangle Z=Z-Z^{\prime}.

For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, applying Itô’s formula to
e^{\gamma t}|\triangle Y_{t}|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\ e^{\gamma t}|\triangle Y_{t}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(\gamma|\triangle Y_{s}|^{2}+|\triangle Z_{s}|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle=2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}\left(f(s,Y_{s},V_{s})-f(s,Y^{\prime}_{s},V^{\prime}_{s})\right)ds+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(g(s,U_{s},V_{s})-g(s,U^{\prime}_{s},V^{\prime}_{s})\right)^{2}ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}\left(g(s,U_{s},V_{s})-g(s,U^{\prime}_{s},V^{\prime}_{s})\right)dB_{s}-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}\triangle Z_{s}dW_{s}.

In light of the conditions on
f
and
g
, and taking expectation on both sides, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(\gamma|\triangle Y_{s}|^{2}+|\triangle Z_{s}|^{2}\right)ds\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}2Ce^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}\triangle V_{s}ds+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(C|\triangle U_{s}|^{2}+\alpha|\triangle V_{s}|^{2}\right)ds.

By Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle 2Ce^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}\triangle V_{s}\leqslant e^{\gamma s}\left(\frac{2C^{2}}{1-\alpha}|\triangle Y_{s}|^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}|\triangle V_{s}|^{2}\right),

Choosing
\gamma=\frac{2C^{2}}{1-\alpha}+\frac{2C}{1+\alpha}
, which gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(\frac{2C}{1+\alpha}|\triangle Y_{s}|^{2}+|\triangle Z_{s}|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(C|\triangle U_{s}|^{2}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}|\triangle V_{s}|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(\frac{2C}{1+\alpha}|\triangle U_{s}|^{2}+|\triangle V_{s}|^{2}\right)ds.

Therefore,
\Theta
is a strict contraction mapping in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a fixed point
(Y,Z)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By Burkholder–Davis–Gundy’s inequality,
we can verify that
Y\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
and the fixed point is a solution of BDSDE (
1.2
).
∎

As a result of
Theorem 3.2
, we have the following comparison theorem.

Proposition 3.5

(Comparison)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem 3.2
. Let
(Y^{i},Z^{i})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
i=1,2
, be the unique solution of the following BDSDE:

\displaystyle Y^{i}_{t}=\xi^{i}+\int_{t}^{T}f^{i}(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i}_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g^{i}(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i}_{s})dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{i}_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

If for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\xi^{1}\leqslant\xi^{2},\quad~f^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z_{t}^{2})\leqslant f^{2}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z_{t}^{2}),\quad~g^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z_{t}^{2})=g^{2}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z_{t}^{2}),\quad\hbox{a.s.},

then we have
Y^{1}_{t}\leqslant Y^{2}_{t}
for any
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s..

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, consider a
C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
function
\phi_{\varepsilon}(y):\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
as follows,

\phi_{\varepsilon}(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\varepsilon y-\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{4},\qquad y\leqslant-\varepsilon,\\
&\quad y^{2},\qquad\qquad\ \ y\geqslant 0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.11)

Then,
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
, when
\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\varepsilon}(y)\longrightarrow|y^{+}|^{2},\qquad\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)\longrightarrow 2y^{+},\qquad\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(y)\longrightarrow 2\mathbf{1}_{\{y\geqslant 0\}}.

Applying Itô’s formula to
\phi_{\varepsilon}\left(Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\varepsilon}\left(Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\ \phi_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi^{1}-\xi^{2}\right)+\int_{t}^{T}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})\left(f^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-f^{2}(s,Y_{s}^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})\left(g^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-g^{2}(s,Y^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})dW_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})\left(\left(g^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-g^{2}(s,Y_{s}^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)^{2}-(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})^{2}\right)ds.

Let
\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0
, we get

\displaystyle\left((Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\ \left((\xi^{1}-\xi^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}+2\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{1}_{s}-Y^{2}_{s})^{+}\left(f^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-f^{2}(s,Y_{s}^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{1}_{s}-Y^{2}_{s})^{+}\left(g^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-g^{2}(s,Y^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)dB_{s}-2\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{1}_{s}-Y^{2}_{s})^{+}(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})dW_{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\left(g^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-g^{2}(s,Y_{s}^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)^{2}-(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})^{2}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{s}^{1}\geqslant Y_{s}^{2}\}}ds.

Since
f
is monotonicity in
y
and Lipschitz in
z
, we have

\displaystyle\ 2(Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{1})^{+}\left(f^{1}(t,Y^{1}_{t},Z^{1}_{t})-f^{2}(t,Y_{t}^{2},Z_{t}^{2})\right)\leqslant\frac{2C^{2}}{1-\alpha}\left((Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}|Z_{t}^{1}-Z_{t}^{2}|^{2}.

Taking expectation on both sides we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left((Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}ds\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left((\xi^{1}-\xi^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}+\left(C+\frac{2C^{2}}{1-\alpha}\right)\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\left((Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}ds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left((Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}\right]=0.

Therefore, we get

,

∎

3.2
BDSDE: existence of maximal solution

In this part, we study the existence of maximal solutions for BDSDEs with continuous coefficients, which is an application of the comparison theorem (

Theorem 3.6

(A1)

(F1)

(G1)

For each

&|f_{n}(t,y,z)|\leqslant|f(t,0,0)|+\varphi(|y|)+C|z|.\end{aligned}\right.

Step 1

Next, by taking the limit as

we conclude that the pair

Taking the limit as

.

Step 2
: The general case that
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
,

and

Let

\displaystyle\xi_{n}=\rho_{n}(\xi)\quad\ \hbox{and}\quad\ f_{n}(t,y,z)=f(t,y,z)-f(t,0,0)+\rho_{n}(f(t,0,0)),

\displaystyle g_{n}(t,y,z)=g(t,y,z)-g(t,y,0)+\rho_{n}(g(t,y,0)),

3.3
Reflected BDSDE: existence and uniqueness

To establish the existence and uniqueness of reflected BDSDEs, we adopt the same conditions as in

5

Since

.
As in Step 1, applying Itô’s formula, dominated convergence theorem and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, we can show that there exists a limit process
(Y_{t},Z_{t},K_{t})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\times A^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}_{+})

.
Furthermore, since

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}(Y^{n}_{s}-S^{n}_{s})dK^{n}_{s}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}(Y_{s}-S_{s})dK_{s},

where

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

Step 3
: The general case that

Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{n}-\xi|^{2}\right]\longrightarrow 0\quad\ \hbox{and}\quad\ \mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|f_{n}(t,0)-f(t,0)|^{2}dt\longrightarrow 0,\quad\ \hbox{as}~n\longrightarrow\infty.

Using the same methodology as in Step 1, we can show that

Uniqueness.
If there exist two solutions

\displaystyle|Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\ 2\int_{t}^{T}(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})\left(f(s,Y_{s}^{1})-f(s,Y_{s}^{2})\right)ds-2\int_{t}^{T}(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})dW_{s}

\displaystyle+2\int_{t}^{T}(Y_{s}^{1}-Y_{s}^{2})d(K_{s}^{1}-K_{s}^{2})-\int_{t}^{T}(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})^{2}ds.

For any

\displaystyle e^{\gamma t}|\triangle Y_{t}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(\gamma|\triangle Y_{s}|^{2}+|\triangle Z_{s}|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{t}^{T}2e^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}\left(f(s,Y_{s},V_{s})-f(s,Y^{\prime}_{s},V^{\prime}_{s})\right)ds+\int_{t}^{T}2e^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}\left(g(s,U_{s},V_{s})-g(s,U^{\prime}_{s},V^{\prime}_{s})\right)dB_{s}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t}^{T}2e^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}\triangle Z_{s}dW_{s}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(g(s,U_{s},V_{s})-g(s,U^{\prime}_{s},V^{\prime}_{s})\right)^{2}ds+2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\triangle Y_{s}d\triangle K_{s}.

\displaystyle\ \frac{2C^{2}}{1-\alpha}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}|\triangle Y_{s}|^{2}ds+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\gamma s}\left(C|\triangle U_{s}|^{2}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}|\triangle V_{s}|^{2}\right)ds.

Choosing

\displaystyle K_{t}=Y_{0}-Y_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{0}^{t}g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

∎

As a result of

Proposition 3.9

(Comparison)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem 3.7
. Let
(Y^{i},Z^{i},K^{i})\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\times A^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}_{+})

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y^{i}_{t}=\xi^{i}+\int_{t}^{T}f^{i}(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i}_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g^{i}(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i}_{s})dB_{s}+K^{i}_{T}-K^{i}_{t}-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{i}_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.\\
&Y_{t}^{i}\geqslant S_{t}^{i}\quad\ \hbox{and}\quad\ \int_{0}^{T}(Y_{s}^{i}-S_{s}^{i})dK_{s}^{i}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

If
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\xi^{1}\leqslant\xi^{2},\quad\ S^{1}_{t}\leqslant S_{t}^{2},\quad\ f^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z_{t}^{2})\leqslant f^{2}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z_{t}^{2}),\quad\ g^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z_{t}^{2})=g^{2}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z_{t}^{2}),

is defined in (

), then let

\displaystyle\left((Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}=\left((\xi^{1}-\xi^{2})^{+}\right)^{2}+2\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{1}_{s}-Y^{2}_{s})^{+}\left(f^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-f^{2}(s,Y^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)ds

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{1}_{s}-Y^{2}_{s})^{+}\left(g^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-g^{2}(s,Y^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)dB_{s}-2\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{1}_{s}-Y^{2}_{s})^{+}(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})dW_{s}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\left(g^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-g^{2}(s,Y^{2},Z_{s}^{2})\right)^{2}-(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})^{2}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{s}^{1}\geqslant Y_{s}^{2}\}}ds+2\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{1}_{s}-Y^{2}_{s})^{+}d(K^{1}_{s}-K^{2}_{s}).

Therefore, we get

,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s..
∎

.

4.1

In this part, we investigate the existence and uniqueness of BDSDEs when the coefficient

Additionally, we consider the condition

Lemma 4.1

(A priori estimate)
.

Let the conditions
(A2)
and
(G2)
be satisfied. Moreover, let
C>0
be a constant,
a:[0,T]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

\displaystyle|f(t,y,z)|\leqslant a_{t}+b_{t}|y|+C|z|^{2}.

If
(Y,Z)\in S^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a solution of BDSDE (
1.2
),
then for any

,

\displaystyle Y_{t}\leqslant\xi^{+}\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{T}b_{s}ds\right\}+\int_{t}^{T}a_{s}\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{s}b_{r}dr\right\}ds+\frac{4C}{1-\alpha}\int_{t}^{T}|\lambda(s)|^{2}\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{s}b_{r}dr\right\}ds,\quad\ \hbox{a.s.}

(4.1)

resp.

\displaystyle\Big(Y_{t}\geqslant\xi^{-}\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{T}b_{s}ds\right\}-\int_{t}^{T}a_{s}\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{s}b_{r}dr\right\}ds-\frac{4C}{1-\alpha}\int_{t}^{T}|\lambda(s)|^{2}\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{s}b_{r}dr\right\}ds,\quad\ \hbox{a.s.}\Big)

(4.2)

Furthermore, there exists a constant
\hat{K}>0
that depends on
\alpha,C,T,\|a\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{R}_{+})},\|b\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{R}_{+})}
and
\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant\hat{K}.

(4.3)

Proof.

Consider a linear ordinary differential equation,

\displaystyle X_{t}=\xi^{+}+\int_{t}^{T}\left(b_{s}X_{s}+a_{s}+\frac{4C}{1-\alpha}|\lambda(s)|^{2}\right)ds.

(4.4)

For
M=\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R})}+\|X\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R})}
, define the function
\Phi
on the interval

by

\Phi(u)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1-\alpha}{4C}(e^{\frac{4C}{1-\alpha}u}-1),\qquad&u\in[0,M],\\
\quad 0,\qquad&u\in[-M,0].\end{aligned}\right.

(4.5)

Applying Itô’s formula to
\Phi(Y_{t}-X_{t})
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(Y_{t}-X_{t})=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(Y_{T}-X_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-X_{s})\left(f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})-b_{s}X_{s}-a_{s}-\frac{4C}{1-\alpha}|\lambda(s)|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(Y_{s}-X_{s})\left(|g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})|^{2}-|Z_{s}|^{2}\right)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-X_{s})g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})dB_{s}

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-X_{s})Z_{s}dW_{s}.

In view of the conditions on
f
and
g
, we get

\displaystyle\Phi(Y_{t}-X_{t})\leqslant

\displaystyle\Phi(Y_{T}-X_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}b_{s}|\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-X_{s})\left(Y_{s}-X_{s}\right)|ds

(4.6)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\Phi^{\prime}C-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}\right)(Y_{s}-X_{s})|Z_{s}|^{2}ds+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}-\frac{4C}{1-\alpha}\Phi^{\prime}\right)(Y_{s}-X_{s})|\lambda(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-X_{s})g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-X_{s})Z_{s}dW_{s}.

It is easy to check that
\Phi(u)=0
