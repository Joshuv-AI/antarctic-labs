Project Summary/Abstract

We propose to recruit a cohort of undergraduates from Minority Serving Institutions(MSIs) to participate
in nuclear physics (NP) research in Eastern Tennessee (ET) as part of the new NPET Fellowship
Program. The program balances mentorship from leaders in their fields with professional development
and networking workshops throughout the year. Through the activities of the program, the University of
Tennessee Knoxville (UTK) Physics and Astronomy Department and Oak Ridge National Laboratory
(ORNL) will partner to develop the physics identity and feelings of belonging and will improve the
retention of under-represented minority (URM) students in the field.

Summary of Accomplishments

Year 1 2021-2022
We recruited a cohort of 10 undergraduate students from constituencies currently underrepresented in
NP. We provided them with year-long research projects, a series of professional development
workshops as well as close mentoring by PhD scientists as well as graduate students and postdocs
(earlier in the career stage).

The time period between when we got word of funding to the beginning of the program was only a few
short weeks. We opted to do summer of 2021 virtually, as at the time of the proposal writing, we were
in the middle of the winter COVID wave and it was difficult to predict what the summer situation would
be like.

In May, we quickly created an online application, accepting unofficial transcripts and requiring only one
letter of recommendation. The program was advertised by the PI as well as other mentors across MSI
institutions where we had contacts or were trying to develop contacts. Given the short time window to
advertise and recruit, we were very pleased with 18 applications for our 10 slots. The applicants came
from several CalState campuses, University of Texas El Paso, Montclair University, San Diego State,
Spelman College, FIU, Fisk University, Chicago State University and Dillard University. We later
learned that the virtual aspect of the program was a draw (since many in-person summer research
programs were still being canceled due to COVID).

The applications were reviewed by a committee of three (PI, Vince Cianciolo, and Christine Nattrass),
using a rubric shown below.

We admitted 10 students (the offer to the Spelman student was declined as she opted to do research
locally), and were able to match them with their first or second choice for a research advisor.

Summer 2021
We began our program in the second week of June, 2021, virtually. The mentors participated in a
workshop led by Lorna Hollowell, M. Ed., the assistant director of education and development in UTK’s
office of equity and diversity. The title was “A foundational Approach to diversity” and included some
nice discussions and reflection.
 We created a Slack for students to interact with their mentors, other members of research groups and
each other. It turned out that most students did not have the level of programming/analysis experience
that mentors expected, and we organized a last-minute python bootcamp, led by a recent UT
physics/comp-sci graduate, Elliott Perryman. He met with students via zoom twice a day, and by the
end of the week, everyone had basic knowledge of how to read files, histogram data, perform simple
fits, but most importantly - how to find answers to things they did NOT know how to do. Elliott
remained available through most of the summer. I discussed the desire of a standardized python analysis
training suite with our counterparts at MSU. If some training modules were developed to the point
where a graduate student could easily provide an orientation to each new cohort - it would be extremely
helpful.

During the summer, we organized a weekly workshop (Fridays), with topics matching those in the
proposal. Students, despite being on zoom, were quite engaged, especially during the two career panel
sessions (PhD track and non-PhD track). The workshops were typically attended by three of the
mentors/organizers: PI (Fomin), Madurga, and Broussard.

Several students (Cepero, Hernandez, Collins, Villegas, McKenzie and Olvier) had made enough
progress by July to submit abstracts to the CEU DNP program, which were accepted. Additionally,
Cepero and Villegas presented their posters at the SESAPS 2021 in-person meeting in Tallahassee, FL.

Fall 2021/Spring 2022

We transitioned to part-time research and monthly workshops in the fall, following the proposal.
Several of the graduating students applied to graduate programs in the fall, two of them (Villegas and
Olesson) were admitted into UT’s program and Villegas was offered the newly created Baines
fellowship, intended to attract promising students that increase our department’s diversity, but
unfortunately, she turned us down to pursue a PhD in nuclear physics at her home institution of FIU.
Olesson is part of the 2022 UTK cohort.

The final assignment for the students was to turn in a research report, written in latex (which there was a
separate dedicated workshop for). All 9 remaining students (Eboni Collins graduated and did not
participate in spring 2022) turned in their reports. This was a challenge - it coincided with the end of the
semester (for several students, it was their last semester), so deadlines had to be extended many times.

Closeout Survey
The students were asked (repeatedly) to complete an anonymous closeout survey. We received response
from 7 of 9 who completed the full year (Eboni Collins finished in December of 2021). For most
students, this was their first research experience. All 7 said it has made them more likely to go to
graduate school, and the majority said they are more likely to consider UTK’s program. The students
also were unanimous in the agreement that having a cohort was a valuable part of the experience.

When asked to choose upto 3 aspects that they found the most useful (see below for options), students
overwhelmingly chose “gaining skills and experience” as well as “learning about the details of a career
path in physics, …”

Finally, many students commented on how interesting it was to hear us (physicists) talk amongst
ourselves and reflect on experiences, and to learn how the field works in general; how slow progress can
sometimes be, and how to get help when you need it. The full PDF of the survey is attached as well.

Year 2 2023-2024

We started the program in January of 2023, to allow gradual on-boarding of the students, so that they
would be ready to hit the ground running for the summer.

The PI (Nadia Fomin) participated in CIMER’s Entering Mentoring Facilitator Training this summer,
aimed at training those who will “deliver culturally responsive mentorship education locally”. She
implemented an adapted training for the NPET mentors.

The cohort was smaller - as many programs that were paused during the pandemic resumed, and NPET
had competition.

Final Thoughts
Several of the students from this program are now pursuing advanced degrees, but not necessarily in
physics. For example, Alejandro Cepero is enrolled in a medical physics program. Gema Villegas is a
PhD student in nuclear physics at FIU, Destynne Oliver and Vicente Corral are PhD students at the
University of Tennessee, and Penny Duran will be starting a PhD program at Duke this fall, having done
two SULI summers at Jefferson Lab after NPET.
