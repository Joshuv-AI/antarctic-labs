Typical hyperbolic surfaces have a spectral gap greater than 2 / 9 − ϵ 2/9-\epsilon
Nalini Anantharaman 1 and Laura Monk 2

Abstract
. In this article, we prove that typical hyperbolic surfaces, sampled with the Weil–Petersson probability measure, have a spectral gap at least 2 / 9 − ϵ 2/9-\epsilon . This is an intermediate result on the way to our proof of the optimal spectral gap 1 / 4 − ϵ 1/4-\epsilon , building on the results of the first part of this series, [ 2 ] . A significant part of the proof is an explicit inclusion-exclusion argument to exclude tangles at the level of precision 1 / g 1/g .

1.
Introduction

This article is part of our series of work
[

,
3
,

]
, ultimately proving that the

\forall\epsilon>0,\quad\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{1}{4}-\epsilon\right)=1

where
\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}
is the Weil–Petersson probability on the moduli space of
hyperbolic surfaces of genus

. This shows that typical hyperbolic surfaces have an optimal
spectral gap, due to a result of Huber
[
7
]
. This conclusion is reached at the end of

[
4
]
, by proving a Friedman–Ramanujan type result for the volume functions associated to
any fixed type of closed geodesics.

This shorter paper shows how the results from
[
2
]
already allow to prove the following partial
result, namely:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
,

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{2}{9}-\epsilon\right)=1.

This beats the previous record of
3/16-\epsilon
by Wu–Xue
[
14
]
and
Lipnowski–Wright
[
8
]
, but does not yet reach the optimal
1/4-\epsilon

[

. The reason behind this incremental progress is that
we perform asymptotic expansions in powers of
1/g
, the
3/16

1/g^{0}
, the
2/9
to the second order
1/g
, and the
1/4
to arbitrary

.

We prove
1.1
by a classic trace method, as sketched in
[
2
, Section 3]
, performing
all computations at the second order in the expansion in powers of
1/g

Since this article is in direct continuation with

2.
The tangle-free hypothesis and its geometric implications

In
[
2
, Section 3]
, we present a detailed trace method, which is the method we follow in
this article. We relate the spectral gap problem to the question of proving that the expansion

(f_{k}^{\mathbf{T}})_{k\geq\chi(\mathbf{T})}
of the volume functions
V_{g}^{\mathbf{T}}
satisfy the
Friedman–Ramanujan
property
. We prove that:

•

for any local topological type
\mathbf{T}
with
\chi(\mathbf{T})\leq 1
, any
k\geq\chi(\mathbf{T})
,
the function
\ell\mapsto\ell f_{k}^{\mathbf{T}}(\ell)
is a Friedman–Ramanujan function in the weak
sense (see
[
2
, Theorem 1.13]
);

•

however, the sum
\sum_{\mathbf{T}:\chi(\mathbf{T})\leq 1}f_{1}^{\mathbf{T}}(\ell)
is not a
Friedman–Ramanujan function in the weak sense (see
[
2
, Section 9]
).

We prove the second point by exhibiting a subset of surfaces of genus
g
, which are
tangled
,
and hence have a small spectral gap. We prove that the probability of this set of surfaces only
decays like
1/g
as
g\to\infty
. In particular, we observe that
even though these surfaces have vanishing
probability, they contribute large contributions to the averages over the whole moduli space,
due to their exponential proliferation of closed geodesics. This
motivates the need to make a probabilistic hypothesis, removing some “bad”
surfaces containing many geodesics. Let us introduce a set of “good surfaces”.

Notation 2.1
.

Let
0<\kappa<1
. Let us consider the counting functions

\displaystyle N_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{inj}}(X)

\displaystyle=\#\{\beta\in\mathcal{G}(X):\,\ell_{X}(\beta)\leq\kappa\}

\displaystyle N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}(X)

\displaystyle=\#\{Y\text{ embedded in }X:\,\chi(Y)=1,\ell_{X}^{\mathrm{max}}(\partial Y)\leq R\},

where:

•

\mathcal{G}(X)
is the set of primitive oriented closed geodesics on
X
, and for
\beta\in\mathcal{G}(X)
,
\ell_{X}(\beta)
denotes its length;

•

any embedded subsurface
Y
is assumed to have geodesic boundary;

•

\chi(Y)>0
denotes the absolute Euler characteristic of
Y
(i.e. the absolute value of its
Euler characteristic);

•

\ell_{X}^{\mathrm{max}}(\partial Y)
denotes the length of the longest boundary component of

Y
;

•

in
N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}
, amongst pair of pants, we only count those with three boundary components
forming a multi-curve, i.e. we exclude the possibility that two components are glued into a
once-holed torus (because this once-holed torus is already counted once in
N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}
).

We denote as
\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
the set of hyperbolic surfaces of genus
g
such
that
N_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{inj}}=N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}=0
. We call
\kappa
-
short loops
the closed
geodesics counted by
N_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{inj}}
, and
R
-
tangles
the embedded
surfaces counted by
N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The same set of good surfaces appears in
[
8
]
, and making the probabilistic
assumption that
X\in\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
is a crucial step of Lipnowski–Wright’s proof of the

3/16-\epsilon
spectral gap result.

For us, the parameter
\kappa
will be an arbitrary small fixed positive number, and we will take

R=\kappa\log(g)
. The probability of
\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
can then be estimated directly using

[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
11
, Theorem 5]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any small enough
\kappa>0
, any large enough
g
,
R=\kappa\log(g)
,

1-\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\kappa^{2}+g^{\frac{3}{2}\kappa-1}\right).

We now prove that, under the probability assumption
X\in\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
, the number of
geodesics of length
\leq L:=A\log(g)
filling any pair of pants or once-holed torus is radically
reduced, from being exponential in
L
to polynomial in
L
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<\kappa<1
,
A\geq 1
. For any large enough
g
, if we set
R=\kappa\log(g)
and

L=A\log(g)
, then there exists a set
\mathrm{Loc}_{g}^{\kappa,A}
of local topological types
such that:

•

for any

X\in\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
, any primitive closed geodesic of length
\leq L
filling a surface of absolute Euler
characteristic
1
is locally equivalent to a local type in
\mathrm{Loc}_{g}^{\kappa,A}
;

•

the cardinal of the set satisfies

(2.1)

\#\mathrm{Loc}_{g}^{\kappa,A}=\mathcal{O}_{\kappa,A}\left((\log g)^{c_{\kappa,A}}\right)

for a constant
c_{\kappa,A}>0
depending only on
\kappa
and
A
.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
, and let
\gamma
be a primitive closed geodesic on
X
of length
\leq L

filling a pair of pants
Y
. Let
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
denote the three boundary components of

Y
, ordered in non-decreasing length. By definition of
\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
,

\ell_{X}(b_{1}),\ell_{X}(b_{2})\geq\kappa\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\ell_{X}(b_{3})\geq R.

Let
\alpha
denote the simple orthogeodesic of
Y
from
b_{3}
to itself. We fix a base point

x_{0}
on
\alpha
, and
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
two simple paths based at
x_{0}
, rotating around
b_{1}
and

b_{2}
respectively, so that
\pi_{1}(Y)
is the free group generated by
a_{1}
and
a_{2}

(see

1
).

.

pants.

The geodesic
\gamma
fills

, and is in particular not simple. As a consequence, it must

\alpha

The path

into two geodesic arcs, of respective lengths
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. For

\gamma_{i}

∎

3.
Inclusion-exclusion argument

In order to be able to “make the assumption
X\in\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
”, we need to be able to compute
expectations of functions in which the indicator function

\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}}=\mathds{1}_{N_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{inj}}=0}\times\mathds{1}_{N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}=0}
appears. A good way to achieve this
is to write this indicator function in terms of counting functions, using an inclusion-exclusion
(such an argument also appears in Friedman’s proof of Alon’s conjecture
[
6
]
). The
hope is that these counting functions can then be expressed in terms of geometric functions and
integrated using Mirzakhani’s integration formula.

The indicator function
\mathds{1}_{N_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{inj}}=0}
can be rewritten in the following way, as
done by Mirzakhani
[
9
]
and
Lipnowski–Wright
[
8
]
.

Notation 3.1
.

For
j\geq 1
, we define

N_{\kappa,j}^{\mathrm{inj}}(X)=\#\{\text{unordered families }\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{j}\}\text{ of distinct }\kappa\text{-short-loops on }X\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any small enough
\kappa>0
, for any
j\geq 1
, all families of geodesics
counted by
N_{\kappa,j}^{\mathrm{inj}}
are made of simple and disjoint geodesics, and

(3.1)

\mathds{1}_{N_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{inj}}=0}=1-\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j+1}N_{\kappa,j}^{\mathrm{inj}}=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j}N_{\kappa,j}^{\mathrm{inj}}

with the convention that
N_{\kappa,0}^{\mathrm{inj}}=1
.

Proof.

We simply observe that, by the binomial theorem,

\forall n\geq 0,\quad\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n=0\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The fact that the geodesics are simple and disjoint for small enough
\kappa

is a classic application of the Collar Lemma
[
5
, Theorem 4.1.6]
.
∎

However, there is no reason to believe that all embedded surfaces contributing to
N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}
are
disjoint. For this reason, it is a priori better to treat the indicator function of
N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}=0

differently, to avoid a tedious enumeration of cases. Luckily, the following easy
inclusion-exclusion is enough for proving
1.1
.

Notation 3.3
.

We define

N_{R,2}^{\mathrm{tang}}:=\#\{\text{unordered pairs }\{Y,Y^{\prime}\}\text{ of distinct }R\text{-tangles on }X\}.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
\kappa>0
and
g\geq 2
,
R=\kappa\log(g)
,

\mathds{1}_{N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}=0}=1-N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}+\mathcal{O}\left(N_{R,2}^{\mathrm{tang}}\right).

Proof.

The bound is a direct consequence of the inequality

0\leq N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}-\mathds{1}_{N_{R}^{\mathrm{tang}}\geq 1}\leq N_{R,2}^{\mathrm{tang}}
, which is trivially true
when
X
contains
0
or
1
tangles, and otherwise true because

\forall n\geq 2,\quad 0\leq n-1\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{2}=\binom{n}{2}.

∎

4.
Reduction of the number of terms to compute

In this subsection we perform some operations which allow us to reduce the number of terms that need
to be computed when performing the inclusion-exclusion.

4.1.
Expectation against
N_{R,2}^{\mathrm{tang}}

The following lemma will allow us to discard any expectation in which
N_{R,2}^{\mathrm{tang}}
appears when
proving the
2/9-\epsilon
spectral gap result. This is the reason why the inclusion-exclusion
formula in
3.4
will be sufficient for our purposes: the error term will be
negligible. A more careful inclusion-exclusion is necessary to reach the optimal spectral gap

1/4-\epsilon
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
0<\kappa<1
, any large enough
g
,
L:=6\log(g)
, any test function
F
supported
on
[0,L]
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[N_{R,2}^{\mathrm{tang}}(X)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(X)\end{subarray}}F(\ell_{X}(\gamma))\Bigg]=\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\|F(\ell)e^{\ell}\|_{\infty}}{g^{2-19\kappa}}\right).

Proof.

First, we notice that, using the triangle inequality, we can consider the case

F(x)=e^{-x}\mathds{1}_{[0,L]}(x)
only. Let
\gamma
be a closed geodesic on
X
of length
\leq L
,
and
Y
,
Y^{\prime}
be two distinct tangles on
X
. Let
Z:=S(\gamma)
be the surface filled by

\gamma
. We define
Z^{\prime}
to be the surface obtained by adding any disk in the complement of the
union
Z\cup Y\cup Y^{\prime}
. The surface
Z^{\prime}
is not necessarily connected, and has at most three
connected components. Because the boundary of
Z^{\prime}
can be obtained from the boundary of
Z

together with
\partial Y
and
\partial Y^{\prime}
, we have

(4.1)

\ell_{X}(\partial Z^{\prime})\leq\ell_{X}(\partial Z)+6R\leq 2\ell_{X}(\gamma)+6\kappa\log(g)\leq 18\log(g)=3L.

We observe that the surface
Z^{\prime}
contains the surface
Y
of Euler
characteristic
-1
. Furthermore,
Z^{\prime}\neq Y
, because
Z^{\prime}
contains
Y^{\prime}
and

Y\neq Y^{\prime}
. Since
Z^{\prime}\setminus Y
cannot be reduced to one cylinder (by
definition of the notion of tangle),
r:=\chi(Z^{\prime})\geq 2
.

As in the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 6.1]
, we use events of extremely small probability to
bound the Euler characteristic of
Z^{\prime}
and reduce the sum to a finite number of terms. Indeed, for
any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, by the naive uniform bound on the number of closed geodesics under a specific
length, see e.g.
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
,

(4.2)

N_{R,2}^{\mathrm{tang}}(X)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(X)\\
\ell_{X}(\gamma)\leq L\end{subarray}}e^{-\ell_{X}(\gamma)}=\mathcal{O}\left((ge^{R})^{6}gL\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\log(g)g^{13}\right)

because any pair of tangles is determined by at most
6
geodesics of lengths
\leq R
. We
adapt the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 6.4]
to prove that the probability for a surface of
genus
g
to contain a surface
Z^{\prime}
such as above or Euler characteristic
>\chi
is

\mathcal{O}_{\chi}\left(\frac{(L+R)^{c(\chi)}e^{L+3R}}{g^{\chi+1}}\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\chi}\left(\frac{(\log(g))^{c(\chi)}}{g^{\chi-8}}\right).

It
follows, taking
\chi=24
, that

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[N_{R,2}^{\mathrm{tang}}(X)\mathds{1}_{[25,\infty)}(\chi(Z^{\prime}))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(X)\\
\ell_{X}(\gamma)\leq L\end{subarray}}e^{-\ell_{X}(\gamma)}\Bigg]=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(\log(g))^{c(24)+1}}{g^{24-21}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{g^{2}}\right).

We are therefore left to study the sum

(4.3)

\sum_{r=2}^{24}\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\gamma,Y,Y^{\prime})\\
\ell_{X}(\gamma)\leq L\\
\chi(Z^{\prime})=r\end{subarray}}e^{-\ell_{X}(\gamma)}\Bigg]

where the sum runs over all families
(\gamma,Y,Y^{\prime})
such that
\gamma
is a primitive closed
geodesic on
X
of length
\leq L=6\log(g)
,
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are disjoint tangles on
X
, and

.

Let us fix a filling type
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
of absolute Euler characteristic
2\leq r\leq 24
, with at

\mathbf{S}\subseteq\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
(we allow that

\mathbf{S}

\mathbf{S}^{\prime}

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(Z,Z^{\prime})\\

We once again use

where

•

\mathbf{x}
denotes the length-vector of
\partial Z^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{y}
that of
\partial Z
;

•

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1}
is the

\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 1

\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\frac{1}{n(\mathbf{T})}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{3}}\int_{[0,\kappa]^{j}}F(h(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}))\,\phi_{g,Q}^{\rho_{j}\mathbf{P}}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{3}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

The filling type

has absolute Euler characteristic
1

hence only need to compute the integral above at the leading order to estimate it up to errors
decaying in
\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}\left(\|F(\ell)e^{\ell}\|_{\infty}/g^{2-\kappa}\right)

B.2
to replace
\phi_{g,Q}^{\rho_{j}\mathbf{P}}
by a sum over a fixed set

\mathcal{O}_{Q}\left(1\right)

1

realizations of rank
1
. First, we compute the term of the connected realization, which is the
case when we glue a surface of signature
(g-2-j,2j+3)
to the pair of pants
\mathbf{P}
and the

j

\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\frac{1}{n(\mathbf{T})}\int_{\star}\int_{[0,\kappa]^{j}}\frac{V_{g-2-j,2j+3}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x})}{V_{g}}\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{3}}{\,\mathrm{d}\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{j}x_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x_{i}

\int_{\star}
runs on the level-set

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\frac{1}{n(\mathbf{T})}\frac{V_{g-2-j,2j+3}}{V_{g}}\int_{\star}\int_{[0,\kappa]^{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}2\sinh\left(\frac{\ell_{i}}{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{4}{x_{i}}\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{2}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{3}}{\,\mathrm{d}\ell}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{j}I(\kappa)^{j}}{j!}\frac{1}{n(\mathbf{T})}\frac{V_{g-2-j,2j+3}}{V_{g}}\int_{\star}\prod_{i=1}^{3}f_{1}(\ell_{i})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{3}}{\,\mathrm{d}\ell}

where

for
\kappa\leq 1
. We sum
the terms for the different values of

by
[
9

). We can similarly write the

1

\int_{\star}\prod_{i=1}^{3}f_{1}(\ell_{i})\prod_{i\in I}\mathds{1}_{[0,\kappa]}(\ell_{i})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{3}}{\,\mathrm{d}\ell}\quad\text{for }I\subseteq\{1,2,3\},

or even a mixture of both, for instance if we glue two components of

to the same

and

both satisfy

for the (constant) polynomial

or
0

. Then,
[
2

applied to the local type
\mathbf{T}
allow us to conclude that these densities are indeed
Friedman–Ramanujan in the weak sense. Since we systematically integrate only functions that are
products of
f_{1}
and
f_{1}\mathds{1}_{[0,\kappa]}
, i.e. there are
\leq 2^{3}
possible products, and by

\mathcal{F}_{w}^{c,c}

\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}\left(1\right)
.

The computation is the same for the average

\langle\mu_{\kappa}^{j}\otimes(F,\mu_{R}^{3})\rangle_{g,Q}^{{\rho_{j}\mathbf{T}^{\partial}}}
, with the difference that now,

\mathds{1}_{[0,\kappa\log(g)]}(\ell)=\mathcal{O}\left(g^{\kappa/2}e^{-\ell/2}\right)

there is
\leq 3^{3}
possible products. We then obtain, once again, a uniform bound on the

\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}\left(g^{3\kappa/2}\right)
. We will explain
further why the contribution of the last terms

\sum_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Loc}_{1,1}^{\mathbf{T},\mathrm{s},\partial}}\langle\mu_{\kappa}^{j}\otimes(F,\mu_{\kappa}^{1},\mu_{R}^{1})\rangle_{g,Q}^{{\rho_{j}\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}}
can be put in the error term, which is then enough to conclude in
the case of a type
\mathbf{T}
filling a pair of pants.

Let us now consider the local topology “simple”. Similarly, we examine each term
in
5.2
one after the other; first, let us treat all the terms except the terms
containing the summations over
\mathrm{Loc}_{1,1}^{2\mathrm{s}}
and

\mathrm{Loc}_{1,1}^{2\mathrm{s},\partial}

B.1
at the second order (
N=1
). This allows us to express the product
of the density and the function
\ell\mapsto\ell
as a linear combination of functions

\ell\mapsto f^{(\kappa)}_{m_{1}}(\ell)f^{(\kappa)}_{m_{2}}(\ell)
with bounded coefficients,
where the family of functions
(f_{i}^{(\kappa)})_{1\leq i\leq 9(a_{2}+1)}
consists of all products
of the functions

where

, with
the functions
1
,
\mathds{1}_{[0,\kappa]}
and
\mathds{1}_{[0,\kappa\log(g)]}

\frac{f(\ell)}{\sinh\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)}=p(\ell)+\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}\left(g^{c\kappa}(\ell+1)^{c}e^{-\ell/2}\right)

for a polynomial
p
and a constant

, which is enough to conclude for these terms.

Now, the last cases we need to consider are the cases where we sum over local topological types in

\mathrm{Loc}_{1,1}^{2\mathrm{s}}
,
\mathrm{Loc}_{1,1}^{2\mathrm{s},\partial}
as well as

\mathrm{Loc}_{1,1}^{\mathbf{T},\mathrm{s},\partial}
for a local type
\mathbf{T}
filling a pair of
pants. These terms are different because they correspond to situations where the geodesic
\gamma

has an intersection with one of the
\kappa
-short loops.

Let us first look at the term
\langle\mu_{\kappa}^{j}\otimes(F,\mu_{\kappa})\rangle_{g,Q}^{{\rho_{j}\mathbf{T}}}
in

5.2
, where
\mathbf{T}\in\mathrm{Loc}_{1,1}^{2\mathrm{s}}
. As a first step, we compute
the density appearing for the local topology
[\mathbf{S}_{1,1},(\gamma,\beta)]_{\mathrm{loc}}
, where

(\gamma,\beta)
is a pair of simple loops on
\mathbf{S}_{1,1}
that intersect exactly once. We pick
Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates
(\ell,\theta)
on the once-holed torus associated to the geodesic

\gamma
, with the origin of twist taken for
\beta
. As a consequence, the density we wish to
compute can be expressed as:

\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{xx_{1}\ldots x_{j}V_{g-1-j,1+2j}(x,x_{1},x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},x_{j})}{V_{g}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\kappa]}(\ell_{\ell,\theta,x}(\beta))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\theta

where
\ell_{\ell,\theta,x}(\beta)
denotes the length of
\beta
in the once-holed torus of
boundary length
x
with Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates
(\ell,\theta)
. By

B.1
with
N=0
, we need to compute

\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}I(\kappa)^{j}}{j!}\frac{V_{g-1-j,1+2j}}{V_{g}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\sinh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\mathds{1}_{[0,\kappa]}(\ell_{\ell,\theta,x}(\beta))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

It is therefore enough to prove that the integral

J_{\kappa}(\ell):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\sinh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\mathds{1}_{[0,\kappa]}(\ell_{\ell,\theta,x}(\beta))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\theta

Let us equip
\mathbf{S}_{1,1}
with the metric associated with the parameters
(x,\ell,\theta)
. We
denote as

the length of the orthogeodesic linking the two boundaries of length
\ell
on the

\mathbf{S}_{1,1}\setminus\gamma
, and

the length of
\beta
for this metric. Then,

\begin{cases}\cosh\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)=\cosh\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)/\cosh\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\\
\cosh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)=\cosh(p)\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)-\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right).\end{cases}

We perform the successive changes of variables

(x,\theta)\rightarrow(r,p)\rightarrow(R,P)
for

and

P:=\cosh\left(\frac{P}{2}\right)
, which yield

J_{\kappa}(\ell)=16\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)\iint_{\frac{1}{\tanh\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)}<P<R<\cosh\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}R\,\mathrm{d}P}{\sqrt{(R/P)^{2}-1}}.

We then check that the integral above converges to a finite limit as
\ell\rightarrow+\infty
,
and that the difference between the integral and its limit is
\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}\left(e^{-\ell/2}\right)
. This
implies that

is a Friedman–Ramanujan function.

Same goes when we examine the term
[\mathbf{S}_{1,1},(\gamma,\beta,\partial\mathbf{S}_{1,1})]_{\mathrm{loc}}
in the

\mathrm{Loc}_{1,1}^{2\mathrm{s},\partial}

. We note that

, and hence the integral we need to compute is smaller than

which implies that the corresponding density is an element of

In order to conclude, we now prove that the contributions of all other local topological types in

examine the case

.

Let us fix a simple loop
\beta
on
\mathbf{S}_{1,1}
. Then,
\beta
is a pair of pants decomposition of

\mathbf{S}_{1,1}
, and therefore we can use Dehn–Thurston coordinates to enumerate all simple loops

\mathbf{S}_{1,1}
(see

\gamma
is
entirely determined by the pair of integers
(i,t)
, where
i
is the intersection number of

\gamma
and
\beta
, and
t
the number of twists of
\gamma
around
\beta
. We denote as
x

the length of
\partial\mathbf{S}_{1,1}
and
y
the length of
\beta
. Then, the quantity we wish to
estimate can be expressed as

(5.11)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{\kappa}\int_{0}^{y}\sum_{(i,t)}F(\ell_{x,y,\theta}(\gamma_{i,t}))\frac{V_{g-1,1}(x)}{V_{g}}x\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\theta

where
(i,t)
runs over a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
\gamma_{i,t}
is the simple loop of Dehn–Thurston

(i,t)
, and
\ell_{x,y,\theta}(\gamma_{i,t})
denotes its length for the metric

(x,y,\theta)
. Note that the twist parameter
\theta
is only taken in the interval
[0,y)
,
because for any
(i,t)
, the image of
\gamma_{i,t}
by a Dehn twist around
\beta

another term
(i,t^{\prime})

We use the usual bound
V_{g-1,1}(x)/V_{g}=\mathcal{O}\left(e^{x/2}\right)
, and make the following observations. On
the one hand, for small enough

, the neighbourhood of width

of length
\leq L
and
\beta

. Similarly, we have that

. On the other hand,

\beta

\mathbf{S}_{1,1}
, and

\displaystyle\|F(\ell)e^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{L+2\kappa}\int_{0}^{\kappa}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{L^{2}}{y}\,x\,e^{y}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}\left(L^{4}g^{\kappa}\|F(\ell)e^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\|_{\infty}\right)

6.
Proof of the main result

We now have all the elements needed to proceed to the proof of
1.1
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Let us take a free parameter
\kappa\in(0,1)
(we shall make further
assumptions on
\kappa
throughout the proof). Then, for
R=\kappa\log g
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{2}{9}-\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\kappa\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{2}{9}-\epsilon\text{ and }X\in\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}\right)+\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(X\notin\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}\right)+\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\lambda_{1}\leq\kappa\right).

We saw in
2.3
that

\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(X\notin\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\kappa^{2}+g^{\frac{3}{2}\kappa-1}\right)
. Furthermore,
Mirzakhani proved in
[
9
, Theorem 4.7]
that, provided that
\kappa<0.002
,

\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\lambda_{1}\leq\kappa\right)
goes to zero as
g\rightarrow+\infty
. As a consequence, if we
prove that, for any small
\kappa>0
,

(6.1)

\lim_{g\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\kappa\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{2}{9}-\epsilon\text{ and }X\in\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}\right)=0

then we obtain that

\limsup_{g\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{2}{9}-\epsilon\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\kappa^{2}\right).

We will therefore be able to deduce
1.1
by letting
\kappa
go to zero.

Now, let
h_{L}(\ell)=h(\ell/L)
denote the test function from
[
2
, Notation 3.8]

constructed by dilatation from the smooth even function
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
supported
on
[-1,1]
such that
\hat{h}
is non-negative on
\mathbb{R}\cup i[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
.
We consider the length-scale
L:=6\log(g)
. Let us fix an integer
m\geq 0
, to be determined
later. Motivated by the discussion in
[
2
, Section 3.4]
, we shall apply the Selberg trace
formula to the function
H_{L,m}:=\mathcal{D}^{m}h_{L}
, where
\mathcal{D}=1/4-\partial^{2}
.

We use
[
2
, Lemma 3.11]
to bound
\hat{h}_{L}(r_{1})
in the event that

\lambda_{1}\leq 2/9-\epsilon
. Indeed, we have
2/9=1/4-(1/6)^{2}
, and hence taking

\alpha=1/6
, we obtain that provided
\epsilon
is small enough, there exists a constant

C_{\epsilon}>0
such that, for any
X
satisfying
\lambda_{1}(X)\leq 2/9-\epsilon
,

\hat{h}_{L}(r_{1}(X))\geq C_{\epsilon}\,e^{(\alpha+\epsilon)L}=C_{\epsilon}\,g^{1+6\epsilon}.

It follows that, if
\kappa\leq\lambda_{1}\leq 2/9-\epsilon
, then the Fourier transform

r\mapsto(1/4+r^{2})^{m}\hat{h}_{L}(r)
of
H_{L,m}
, taken at
r=r_{1}
, is larger than

C_{\epsilon}\kappa^{m}\,g^{1+6\epsilon}
. As a consequence, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\kappa\leq\lambda_{1}(X)\leq\frac{2}{9}-\epsilon\text{ and }X\in\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\hat{H}_{L,m}(r_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}}\geq C_{\epsilon}\,\kappa^{m}g^{1+6\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\big[\hat{H}_{L,m}(r_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}}\big]}{C_{\epsilon}\,\kappa^{m}\,g^{1+6\epsilon}}

by Markov’s inequality (here, we have used the fact that
\hat{H}_{L,m}(r_{1})\geq 0
, because

\lambda_{1}\geq 0
and, by hypothesis,
\hat{h}_{L}(r_{1})\geq 0
). This reduces the problem to
finding an integer
m
such that, for any small enough
\kappa
,

(6.2)

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\big[\hat{H}_{L,m}(r_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}}\big]=\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon,\kappa}\left(g^{1+5\epsilon}\right)

which shall now be our objective.

By positivity of
\hat{H}_{L,m}
on
\mathbb{R}\cup i[-1/2,1/2]
, the expectation

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\big[\hat{H}_{L,m}(r_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}}\big]
is smaller than the
expectation of the Selberg trace formula applied to
H_{L,m}
, multiplied by the indicator of

\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}
. Following the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 3.12]
with this additional

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\big[\hat{H}_{L,m}(r_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}}\big]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(X)}\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)\,H_{L,m}(\ell_{X}(\gamma))}{\exp\left(\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)}{2}\right)}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}(X)}\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left((\log g)^{2}g\right).\end{split}

Using the uniform counting bound on geodesics,
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
,
as in the proof of
[
2

, we find a constant

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(X)\\
\chi(S(\gamma))>{\chi_{+}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)\,H_{L,m}(\ell_{X}(\gamma))}{\exp\left(\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)}{2}\right)}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}(X)}\Bigg]=\mathcal{O}\left(g\right).

Hence, the sum in

For every filling type

[

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[\sum_{\gamma:S(\gamma)=\mathbf{S}}\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)\,H_{L,m}(\ell_{X}(\gamma))}{\exp\left(\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)}{2}\right)}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}(X)}\Bigg]=\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{e^{(1+\epsilon)L/2}}{g^{2}}\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}\left(g^{1+3\epsilon}\right).

The number of such filling types is
\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
. As a consequence, equations
(
6.3
) and (

) together imply that

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\big[\hat{H}_{L,m}(r_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}}\big]

(6.5)

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[\sum_{\gamma:\chi(S(\gamma))\leq 1}\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)\,H_{L,m}(\ell_{X}(\gamma))}{\exp\left(\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)}{2}\right)}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}(X)}\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}\left(g^{1+3\epsilon}\right).

We shall now split this sum by local topological type. In order to do so in a

The bound (
6.5
) can then be rewritten as

(6.7)

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\big[\hat{H}_{L,m}(r_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}}\big]\leq\sum_{\mathbf{T}\in\mathrm{Loc}_{g}^{\kappa}\cup\{\mathbf{s}\}}\mathrm{Av}_{g,m,\kappa}(\mathbf{T})+\mathcal{O}_{\kappa,\epsilon}\left(g^{1+3\epsilon}\right)

where for any

,

(6.8)

\mathrm{Av}_{g,m,\kappa}(\mathbf{T}):=\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\sim\mathbf{T}\\
\ell_{X}(\gamma)\leq L\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)\,H_{L,m}(\ell_{X}(\gamma))}{\exp\left(\frac{\ell_{X}(\gamma)}{2}\right)}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{TF}_{g}^{\kappa,R}(X)}\Bigg].

Let
\mathbf{T}\in\mathrm{Loc}_{g}^{\kappa}\cup\{\mathbf{s}\}
.

•

If
\mathbf{T}
fills a once-holed torus, then by

and use our asymptotic expansion,
[
2
, Theorem 5.15]
, at the order

2
with

•

Appendix A
Discarding sets of extremely small probability

In this appendix, we exhibit a set of extremely small probability in the moduli space, i.e. surfaces
containing a multi-curve of small lengths disconnecting the surface in many connected components.

By “extremely small probability”, we mean probability
\mathcal{O}(g^{-N})
where we can tune the
parameters to make the exponent
N
as large as we need. This stands in contrast to the set of
surfaces containing tangles, which has probability of order
\kappa^{2}+g^{\frac{3}{2}\kappa-1}
.

The reason why sets of “extremely small probability” can easily be discarded in the trace method
is that they contribute a negligible amount to our trace averages. This comes from the classical
deterministic counting estimate for periodic geodesics, see e.g.
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
. An
example of this phenomenon is the use of
[
2
, Proposition 6.4]
to prove
[
2
, Theorem
6.1]
. We here exhibit another event of extremely small probability, having short multi-curves
which separate the surface of genus
g
into many components.

Notation A.1
.

For an integer
Q
and a hyperbolic surface
X
, we denote as
{\mathrm{MC}}_{X}(Q)
the set of multi-curves
which separate the surface
X
into at most
Q
connected components.

We recall that by multi-curve we mean family of simple disjoint loops which are not homotopic to one
another. We shall estimate the probability of the following events.

Notation A.2
.

For an integer
Q\geq 1
,
g\geq 2
and
0<\kappa<1
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{g}^{\kappa,Q}:=\left\{X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}\,:\,\forall\gamma\text{ multi-curve on }X,\ell^{\mathrm{max}}(\gamma)\leq\kappa\Rightarrow\,\gamma\in\mathrm{MC}_{X}(Q)\right\}

where
\ell^{\mathrm{max}}
denotes the length of the longest component of the multi-curve
\gamma
.

We shall prove the following bound on the probability of the event
\mathcal{B}_{g}^{\kappa,Q}
.

Proposition A.3
.

For any
Q\geq 1
,
g\geq 2
and
0<\kappa<1
,

(A.1)

\displaystyle 1-\mathbb{P}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{g}^{\kappa,Q}\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\kappa,Q}\left(\frac{1}{g^{Q-1}}\right).

In other words, the events
\mathcal{B}_{g}^{\kappa,Q}
are events of extremely small probability: we can
adjust the parameter
Q
to match any desired rate of decay.

In order to prove this bound, let us introduce a counting function
\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q}
, defined for a
compact hyperbolic surface
X
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}N_{\kappa,j,Q}^{\mathrm{inj}}(X)

where
N_{\kappa,j,Q}^{\mathrm{inj}}(X)
counts unordered families of
\kappa
-short loops with
j
elements, which
disconnect the surface in at most
Q
components:

(A.2)

N_{\kappa,j,Q}^{\mathrm{inj}}(X):=\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{j})\in{\mathrm{MC}}_{X}(Q)\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu{\rm{l}}}{1\mskip-4.0mu{\rm{l}}}{1\mskip-4.5mu{\rm{l}}}{1\mskip-5.0mu{\rm{l}}}}_{[0,\kappa]}(\ell_{X}(\gamma_{i})).

More generally, for any
\beta>0
, we define a weighted version

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q,\beta}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\beta^{j}N_{\kappa,j,Q}^{\mathrm{inj}}(X).

The sum actually stops at
j=3g-3
, but we want to estimate the expectation of
\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q,\beta}

uniformly as
g
varies. We prove the following.

Proposition A.4
.

For any fixed
Q\geq 1
,
0<\kappa<1
,
\beta>0
and
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{g}\mathbb{E}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left[\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q,\beta}\right]<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{g}\mathbb{E}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left[\mathcal{Y}^{2}_{\kappa,Q,\beta}\right]<+\infty.

Proof.

We use the upper bound
[
2
, equation (2.9)]
and standard integration methods on the moduli
space, which gives

(A.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left[\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q,\beta}\right]\leq\frac{1}{V_{g,n}}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\beta^{j}I_{\kappa}^{j}}{j!}\sum_{\mathfrak{q}=1}^{Q}\sum_{\vec{g},\vec{n}}\#\mathrm{Orb}_{g,n}^{j,Q}(\vec{g},\vec{n})\prod_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{q}}V_{g_{i},n_{i}}

where:

•

the sum over
1\leq\mathfrak{q}\leq Q
is a sum over the number of connected components of
the complement of
\gamma
;

•

the sum over
\vec{g},\vec{n}
is a sum over the vectors

(g_{i},n_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\mathfrak{q}}
satisfying
2g_{i}-2+n_{i}>0
,

\sum_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{q}}n_{i}=2j\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\mathfrak{q}}(2g_{i}-2+n_{i})=2g-2+n;

•

the quantity
I_{\kappa}
is defined as
I_{\kappa}=\int_{0}^{\kappa}\ell e^{\frac{\ell}{2}}d\ell
;

•

\mathrm{Orb}_{g,n}^{j,Q}(\vec{g},\vec{n})
is the set of MCG-equivalence classes, in a
surface
S_{g,n}
of signature
(g,n)
, of multi-curves
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{j})
with
j

components, cutting the surface
S_{g,n}
into
\mathfrak{q}
numbered pieces of respective signatures

(g_{1},n_{1}),\ldots,(g_{\mathfrak{q}},n_{\mathfrak{q}})
.

We then prove that for each
\vec{g},\vec{n}
,

\#\mathrm{Orb}_{g,n}^{j,Q}(\vec{g},\vec{n})\leq\mathfrak{q}^{2j}.

To see this, we define a surjective map from a subset

\mathcal{A}\subset\{1,\ldots,\mathfrak{q}\}^{2j}
onto

\mathrm{Orb}_{g,n}^{j,Q}(\vec{g},\vec{n})
. Fix a family of surfaces

(S_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\mathfrak{q}}
of respective signatures

(g_{i},n_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\mathfrak{q}}
. Given a sequence
(i_{k},i_{k}^{\prime})_{1\leq k\leq j}
with

(i_{k},i_{k}^{\prime})\in\{1,\ldots,\mathfrak{q}\}^{2}
, glue successively, for
k=1,\ldots,j
, a
boundary curve of
S_{i_{k}}
to a boundary curve of
S_{i_{k}^{\prime}}
, and call this curve
\gamma_{k}
. The
set
\mathcal{A}
is the subset of
\{1,\ldots,\mathfrak{q}\}^{2j}
such that this succession of gluings
is actually possible and gives a connected surface of signature
(g,n)
. In this case, we obtain a
surface of signature
(g,n)
together with a muticurve
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{j})
, that can be mapped to
our reference surface
S_{g,n}
by a homeomorphism. Any multi-curve that cuts
S_{g,n}
into

\mathfrak{q}
pieces of respective signatures

(g_{1},n_{1}),\ldots,(g_{\mathfrak{q}},n_{\mathfrak{q}})
is MCG-equivalent to a multi-curve obtained
this way.

Now, for fixed
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{\mathfrak{q}})
, we know from
[
12
, Lemma 24]
that

(A.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1},\ldots,g_{\mathfrak{q}},\\
\sum_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{q}}(2g_{i}-2+n_{i})=2g-2+n\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathfrak{q}}V_{g_{i},n_{i}}\leq C\Big(\frac{D}{2g-2+n}\Big)^{{\mathfrak{q}}-1}V_{g,n}

for universal constants
C,D
.
In particular, this is bounded above by
CV_{g,n}
.
Taking into account all the possibilities for
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{\mathfrak{q}})
, we lose another
factor
(2j)^{Q}
. We end up with the upper bound

(A.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left[\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q,\beta}\right]\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\beta^{j}}{j!}Q^{2j+1}(2j)^{Q}I_{\kappa}^{j}

which is a convergent series.

The bound on the second moment comes from the inequality

\mathcal{Y}^{2}_{\kappa,Q,\beta}\leq\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q,4\beta}
, together with the previous case.
∎

We now conclude to the proof of Proposition
A.3
, by the same method.

Proof of Proposition
A.3
.

For a hyperbolic surface
X
, define
{\mathrm{MC}}^{(1)}_{X}(Q)\subset{\mathrm{MC}}_{X}(Q)
to be the set of
multi-curves which separate
X
into
exactly

Q
components, and
\mathcal{Y}^{(1)}_{\kappa,Q}

the random variable

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{(1)}_{\kappa,Q}(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{j})\in{\mathrm{MC}}^{(1)}_{X}(Q)\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu{\rm{l}}}{1\mskip-4.0mu{\rm{l}}}{1\mskip-4.5mu{\rm{l}}}{1\mskip-5.0mu{\rm{l}}}}_{[0,\kappa]}(\ell_{X}(\gamma_{i}))

which counts the total number of such multi-curves with maximal length
\leq\kappa
.

Using the bound (
A.4
) with
\mathfrak{q}=Q
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\left[\mathcal{Y}^{(1)}_{\kappa,Q}\right]\leq\frac{D^{Q}}{g^{Q-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\frac{Q^{2j}(2j)^{Q}I_{\kappa}^{j}}{j!}=\mathcal{O}_{\kappa,Q}\left(\frac{1}{g^{Q-1}}\right).

This yields the announced results, because
\mathcal{Y}^{(1)}_{\kappa,Q}\geq 1
on the complement of
\mathcal{B}_{g}^{\kappa,Q}
.

∎

We furthermore add another useful tail estimate on the sum in the definition of
\mathcal{Y}_{\kappa,Q}
.

Lemma A.5
.

For any
Q\geq 1
,
0<\kappa<1
and
N\geq 0
, any large enough
g
,

\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\mathrm{\scriptsize{WP}}}\Bigg[\sum_{j>\log g}^{+\infty}N_{\kappa,j,Q}^{\mathrm{inj}}\Bigg]=\mathcal{O}_{\kappa,Q,N}\left(\frac{1}{g^{N}}\right).

Proof.

We simply use the same proof as (
A.5
), now joined with the observation that the tail

\sum_{j>\log g}^{+\infty}(2j)^{Q}(Q^{2}I_{\kappa})^{j}/j!
goes to zero
faster than any power of
g
as
g\rightarrow+\infty
.
∎
