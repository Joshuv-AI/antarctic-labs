Abstract
. We consider a power-law thin-film equation for strongly shear-thinning fluids. Weak solutions to this equation have been constructed more than twenty years ago by Ansini and Giacomelli. Here, we pass over to analyzing strong solutions with nonzero contact angle (partial-wetting regime), and place emphasis on studying the behavior of solutions near points where the film height vanishes (the contact-line region) by considering perturbations of a linear profile. The leading-order equation in von-Mises coordinates shows similarities with the evolution equation for the p p -Laplace, though being of fourth order. Using a time discretization, we reduce the leading-order problem to finding a variational solution, and pass to the limit in the discretization scheme on suitably estimating higher-order nonlinear terms in conjunction with compactness arguments. This proves existence and asymptotic stability of strong solutions that are perturbations of the linear profile, and yields control on the contact-line velocity on carefully tracking singular terms in our estimates. While we believe that the transformed equation shows mathematical features the analysis of which stands on its own merit, it also physically corroborates shear thinning behavior as an alternative in resolving the no-slip paradox, as opposed to more standard approaches like introducing slip at the liquid-solid interface.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setting

Consider the non-Newtonian thin-film equation

(1.1a)

h_{t}+\big(h^{\alpha+2}|h_{yyy}|^{\alpha-1}h_{yyy}\big)_{y}=0\quad\text{in }\{h>0\},

where the dependent variable
h=h(t,y)
describes the height of an incompressible viscous thin film as a function of time
t\geq 0
and lateral position (base point)
y\in\mathbb{R}
. The parameter
\alpha>0
is the so-called flow-behavior exponent which features the strain-dependent rheology of the fluid. More precisely, for
0<\alpha<1
, the corresponding fluid is shear-thickening, while it is Newtonian for
\alpha=1
and shear-thinning for
\alpha>1
. In this paper, we consider the strongly shear-thinning regime in which
\alpha>2
. Equation (
1.1a
) can be derived through a lubrication approximation (small film heights
h
at moderate Reynolds number) from the underlying (Navier-) Stokes system for a non-Newtonian power-law fluid with a no-slip condition at the substrate. For a formal derivation in case of the related Ellis law, see for instance
[
28
]
.

We solve (
1.1a
) with the contact-angle condition

(1.1b)

\lim_{\{h>0\}\owns\tilde{y}\to y}|\partial_{\tilde{y}}h(t,\tilde{y})|=1\quad\text{for }y\in\partial\{h>0\},

and the condition for the contact-line velocity

(1.1c)

\lim_{\{h>0\}\owns\tilde{y}\to Y_{0}}h^{\alpha+1}(t,\tilde{y})|\partial^{3}_{\tilde{y}}h(t,\tilde{y})|^{\alpha-1}\partial^{3}_{\tilde{y}}h(t,\tilde{y})=\frac{{\rm d}Y_{0}}{{\rm d}t}\quad\text{for the contact lines }Y_{0}\in\partial\{h>0\},

which implies no flux at the triple junction (the contact line), where liquid, air, and solid meet. A special solution to (
1.1
) is the linear profile

(1.2)

h=y_{+}\coloneqq\begin{cases}y&\text{ for }y>0,\\
0&\text{ for }y\leq 0.\end{cases}

The present paper deals with perturbations of this linear profile (
1.2
). Specifically, this enables us to precisely study the properties of (
1.1
) close to the contact line
\partial\{h>0\}
, separating wetted from dry regions. Here, we prove that for
\alpha>2
strong solutions to (
1.1
) with moving free boundary exist, thus rigorously justifying shear-thinning behavior as an alternative to assuming slip at the liquid-solid interface in resolving the no-slip paradox. Indeed, for the Newtonian thin-film equation with no-slip condition at the substrate

h_{t}+(h^{3}h_{yyy})_{y}=0\quad\text{in }\{h>0\},

the contact line cannot move. See the first discussion of the no-slip paradox in
[
16
]
and the reviews
[
8
,
25
,
3
]
for formal results from the physics literature. Its regularization on introducing (nonlinear) slip at the liquid-solid interface is given by

(1.3)

h_{t}+(h^{n}h_{yyy})_{y}=0\quad\text{in }\{h>0\},

where
n\in[1,3)
. Then it is somewhat natural also from the mathematical perspective to ask for regularizations thereby incorporating the term
h_{yyy}
in the flux.
In this regard, from the physical perspective (
1.1a
) turns out to be a natural regularization.
See
[
28
,
1
]
for a discussion of the no-slip paradox in the case of Ellis’ law.

1.4.
Heuristics: leading-order estimates

On assuming that a sufficiently regular solution

to (

To this end, we test (
1.13a
) with

in space and, integrating by parts twice, we obtain

(1.14a)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}f\,u\,{\rm d}x.

Next, we test (
1.13a
) with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{t}\,\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}\,g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u))\,{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{t})\,g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u)\,{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x

the identity

\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}\,g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u))\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}f\,\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}\,g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u))\,{\rm d}x,

\frac{2}{\alpha+1}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}\,g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u))\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{0}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+4\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f|\,|u|\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t,

(A-priori estimate for the velocity)

In the situation of Theorem

2.2.
Discussion

Theorem
2.2
implies in particular that the contact-line velocity

V_{|x=0}=(\partial_{t}Y)(\cdot,0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{bc-contact-vel}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\lim_{x\downarrow 0}x^{\alpha+1}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u)

is controlled in
L^{2}(0,\infty)
. Specifically, we note that a nonzero contact-line velocity requires that
\lim_{x\downarrow 0}x^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(\partial_{x}^{2}u)(t,x)\neq 0
, which in view of (
2.1
) is an admissible choice for the initial data
u_{0}
. We have thus indeed found a solution concept, for which nonzero contact-line velocities are admissible, occur, and are controlled, and have thus rigorously resolved the no-slip paradox for the free-boundary problem (
1.1
).

This sets our work apart from previous results like
[
2
]
by Ansini and Giacomelli, in which for the first time weak solutions and their qualitative properties are derived for the more general class of PDEs

h_{t}+(h^{n}|h_{yyy}|^{\alpha-1}h_{yyy})_{y}=0\quad\text{in }\{h>0\},

where
n\geq 0
and
\alpha\geq 1
(which reduces to the no-slip case (
1.1a
) on setting
n=\alpha+2
). There, it is proved that the speed of propagation is finite (cf.
[
2
, Theorem 2]
with a propagation rate matching the self-similar scaling), and that
h^{n}|h_{yyy}|^{\alpha-1}h_{yyy}\in L^{1}(\{h>0\})
for the flux, which is insufficient for obtaining control on the contact-line velocity. The previous works
[
19
,
20
]
by King deal with formal asymptotics at the contact line and the analysis of special solutions (both in the shear-thickening and the shear-thinning case), so that our analysis can be viewed as a rigorous treatment of the contact-line asymptotics in
[
19
,
20
]
. In
[
1
]
, Ansini and Giacomelli use formal asymptotics near the contact line to show that the related Ellis thin-film equation

h_{t}+\bigl(h^{3}(1+|hh_{yyy}|^{\alpha-1})h_{yyy})_{y}=0\quad\text{in }\{h>0\},

admits advancing traveling-wave solutions, whereby they suggest shear-thinning behavior as a possible remedy for the no-slip paradox. Moreover, they derive a scaling law in time for macroscopic quantities by analyzing a class of quasi-self-similar solutions to the Ellis thin-film equation in the limit of a Newtonian rheology. Existence and uniqueness of solutions to the Ellis thin-film equation emerging from positive initial values is provided in
[
24
]
. An analysis of the long-time behavior of such solutions is provided in
[
17
]
for both the power-law thin-film equation and the Ellis thin-film equation.

We mention that a corresponding theory of classical solutions (existence and uniqueness of strong solutions) has been developed for the Newtonian thin-film equation (
1.3
) starting with
[
10
,
6
,
11
]
treating (
1.3
) with linear mobility (
n=1
) and zero contact angle in the half space, which was further upgraded to compactly supported solutions and higher dimensions in
[
14
,
18
,
26
]
, and to nonlinear mobilities in
[
9
,
15
,
12
,
13
]
. The case of nonzero contact angles was covered in
[
22
,
23
]
. Our hope is that the analysis at hand may serve as a natural first step towards a corresponding analytic theory in the non-Newtonian case.

In
[
21
]
, the authors study the no-slip thin-film equation as the asymptotic limit of models with different regularized slip conditions at the liquid-solid interface. Their results suggest that the Newtonian thin-film equation with no-slip boundary condition admits a large class of physically admissible solutions but that sufficient regularity of solutions implies that the contact line cannot move. This is in line with our findings for the free-boundary problem to the non-Newtonian thin-film equation (
1.1
) as stated in Theorem
2.2
and the heuristic discussion in §
1.5
, where a singularity drives the contact line. The afore-mentioned well-posedness theory to the Newtonian thin-film equation (
1.3
) is, unless
n=1
, in line with this finding, too.

2.3.
Outlook

In future work, we would be very interested in lifting the analysis at hand to also address uniqueness of solutions. Here, our leading-order estimates need to be more refined, and perturbations around the solution
u
including additional regularity need to be addressed in order to obtain a contraction. Notably, in case of Ellis’ law, local well-posedness of the corresponding thin-film model without contact line has been proved in
[
24
]
, and we expect this case to be more feasible compared to the situation at hand when it comes to proving uniqueness.

A future research direction concerns the limit
\alpha\downarrow 1
, or which, as pointed out in §
1.5
, the singular expansion at the contact line comes into play in a more pronounced way, and a more careful subtraction of these terms is necessary even for the leading-order estimates.

Another direction of research could be the rigorous analysis of droplet rupture. A rigorous result in this regard for the thin-film equation coming from Darcy dynamics in the Hele-Shaw cell (the PDE (
1.3
) with
n=1
) is due to Constantin, Elgindi, Nguyen, and Vicol in
[
7
]
, in which finite- or infinite-time pinch off in a confined geometry is proved. It would be desirable to extend these results to the non-Newtonian setting or nonlinear mobilities. The analysis at hand may then serve as a first step allowing for an extension of the solution beyond pinch off.

The proof follows the lines of standard approximation results of Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces.

Proof of Lemma

.

For

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{s}v^{2}\,{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\big\|x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}u\big\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(0,\infty)}^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\alpha}|(x\partial_{x}-1)x\partial_{x}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{(1-\alpha)s}|(\partial_{s}-1)\partial_{s}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}s.

By (
3.1

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\alpha}|u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{(1-\alpha)s}|v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}s<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}x|\partial_{x}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{(1-\alpha)s}|\partial_{s}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}s<\infty.

Now take
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R})
with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta|_{[-1,1]}=1
and
\eta|_{(-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)}=0
, define for
k\in\mathbb{N}
the function
\eta_{k}(s)\coloneq\eta(s/k)

by dominated convergence with dominating function

\displaystyle\big\|x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}(u-u_{k})\big\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(0,\infty)}^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{(1-\alpha)s}|1-\eta_{k}|^{\alpha+1}|(\partial_{s}-1)\partial_{s}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\phantom{\lesssim_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{k^{\alpha+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{(1-\alpha)s}(|v|^{\alpha+1}+|\partial_{s}v|^{\alpha+1})\,{\rm d}s\to 0\quad\text{as }k\to\infty,

\displaystyle\big\|x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}(u-u_{k})\big\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(0,\infty)}^{\alpha+1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{(1-\alpha)s}|(\partial_{s}-1)\partial_{s}v-\phi_{k}*((\partial_{s}-1)\partial_{s}v)|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}s.

\displaystyle\big\|x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}(u-u_{k})\big\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(0,\infty)}^{\alpha+1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w(s)-(\psi_{k}*w)(s)|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}s.

\displaystyle(w_{\ell}-\psi_{k}*w_{\ell})(s)

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha+1}}|u|\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}.

Step 2. Critical bound
\beta\uparrow\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}
.
Next we raise the value of
\beta
. Therefore, suppose
p>1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus yields

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta p}|u|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\partial_{x}\big(x^{\beta p}(u^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\big)\big|\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\beta|p\,x^{\beta p-1}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\beta p}\big|\tfrac{p}{2}(u^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}2u\partial_{x}u\big|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,p}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u|^{p-1}x^{\beta p-1}\left(|u|+x|\partial_{x}u|\right){\rm d}x.

Applying Hölder’s, Jensen’s, and Hardy’s inequality, we obtain for
p\in(1,3)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta p}|u|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,\beta}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{2(\beta p-1)}{3-p}}\left(|u|+x|\partial_{x}u|\right)^{\frac{2}{3-p}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,\beta}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{2(\beta p-1)}{3-p}}|u|^{\frac{2}{3-p}}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{2(\beta p-1)}{3-p}+\frac{2}{3-p}}|\partial_{x}u|^{\frac{2}{3-p}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,\beta}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{2\beta p}{3-p}}|\partial_{x}u|^{\frac{2}{3-p}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}.

To further estimate the second integral on the right-hand side of (
3.9
), we apply Lemma
3.3
to reduce the integral into a form suitable for Hardy’s inequality (
3.1
) of Lemma
3.1
.
Inserting estimate (
3.2
) with
\nu=\beta p
and
r=\frac{2}{3-p}
in estimate (
3.9
) leads to

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta p}|u|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,p}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{2\beta p}{3-p}}|\partial_{x}u|^{\frac{2}{3-p}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,p}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}+\frac{(3-p)(\alpha+1)}{2(\alpha+3)}(1-\theta^{k})}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma_{k}}|\partial_{x}u|^{r_{k}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{2}\theta^{k}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\phantom{\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,p}}\times\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{\alpha+3}(1-\theta^{k})},

where
\theta=\frac{\alpha-1}{2(\alpha+1)}
, and
\gamma_{k}
and
r_{k}
are as in (
3.3
). To apply Hardy’s inequality (
3.1
) to the last integral on the right-hand side of (
3.10
), we need to enforce the conditions

(3.11)

\gamma_{k}=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad r_{k}=\alpha+1.

By choosing
\beta=\beta_{k}
and
p=p_{k}
in (
3.10
), and applying (
3.11
) alongside Hardy’s inequality, we deduce

(3.12)

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta_{k}p_{k}}|u|^{p_{k}}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{k},p_{k}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p_{k}-1}{2}+\frac{(3-p_{k})(\alpha+1)}{2(\alpha+3)}(1-\theta^{k})}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-p_{k}}{\alpha+3}+\frac{(3-p_{k})(\alpha+1)}{2(\alpha+3)}\theta^{k}}.

Next, we determine
\beta_{k}
and
p_{k}
by combining (
3.3
) and (
3.11
). Starting with
p_{k}
, from (
3.3b
) and (
3.11
), we obtain

\theta^{-k}\Big(\frac{2}{3-p_{k}}-\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}\Big)+\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}=\alpha+1,

which gives

p_{k}=\frac{2(5\alpha+3)+3(\alpha-1)(\alpha+1)\theta^{k}}{(\alpha+1)(4+(\alpha-1)\theta^{k})}=\frac{5\alpha+3}{2(\alpha+1)}(1+O(\theta^{k})).

Similarly, using (
3.3a
) and (
3.11
), we solve for
\beta_{k}
:

\theta^{-k}\Big(\frac{2\beta_{k}p_{k}}{3-p_{k}}-\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}\Big)+\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}=1,

which gives

(3.13)

\beta_{k}=\frac{2(\alpha+2)-(\alpha+1)\theta^{k}}{2(5\alpha+3)+3(\alpha+1)(\alpha-1)\theta^{k}}=\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}(1+O(\theta^{k})).

Substituting
\beta_{k}
and
p_{k}
back into (
3.12
) provides the weighted estimate

(3.14)

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta_{k}}|u|\lesssim_{\alpha,k}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}+\alpha-1}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1-2\beta_{k}}{3\alpha-1}},\quad\beta_{k}\uparrow\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}.

Step 3. Combination of bounds of Steps 1 and 2.

We employ the weighted estimates (
3.8
) and (
3.14
) obtained in Steps 1 and 2
by writing
\beta
as a convex combination

\beta=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha+1}\varrho_{k}+\beta_{k}(1-\varrho_{k}),\quad\varrho_{k}\in[0,1],\quad\beta_{k}\uparrow\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3},

with
\beta_{k}
as in (
3.13
) and where

\varrho_{k}=\frac{(\alpha+1)(\beta_{k}-\beta)}{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}+\alpha-1}\quad\text{and}\quad 1-\varrho_{k}=\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta+\alpha-1}{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}+\alpha-1},

so that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta}|u|\;\quad

\displaystyle\leq_{\phantom{\alpha,k}}\;\big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha+1}}|u|\big)^{\varrho_{k}}\big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta_{k}}|u|\big)^{1-\varrho_{k}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:weight xu7},\eqref{eq:weight xu13}}}}{{\lesssim}}_{\alpha,k}\;\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\varrho_{k}}{\alpha+1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}+\alpha-1}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho_{k})}

\displaystyle\phantom{\lesssim_{\alpha,k}}\;\times\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1-2\beta_{k}}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho_{k})}

\displaystyle=_{\phantom{\alpha,k}}\;\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\gamma}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\delta},

where an straight-forward computation shows that the exponents
\gamma
and
\delta
are given by

\gamma=\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}+\alpha-1}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho_{k})=\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta+\alpha-1}{3\alpha-1},\quad\delta=\frac{\varrho_{k}}{\alpha+1}+\frac{1-2\beta_{k}}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho_{k})=\frac{1-2\beta}{3\alpha-1}.

Since
\beta_{k}\uparrow\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}
, this finishes the proof of (
3.7
) for
\beta\in\big[\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha+1},\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}\big)
.

Step 4. Extension from
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
to
U_{\alpha}
.

Steps 1–3 establish (
3.7
) for
u\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
.
For general
u\in U_{\alpha}
, Lemma
3.2
provides a sequence

(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
with
u_{k}\to u
in
U_{\alpha}
. Applying (
3.7
) to

u_{k}-u_{m}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))

yields

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta}|u_{k}-u_{m}|

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{k}-u_{m}\|_{U_{\alpha}}^{\frac{\alpha+(\alpha-1)\beta}{3\alpha-1}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }k,m\to\infty,

so that
(x^{\beta}u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
BC^{0}([0,\infty))
and thus converges uniformly to some

\varphi\in BC^{0}([0,\infty))
. Thus
x^{\beta}u
admits a representative in
\varphi\in BC^{0}([0,\infty))
, again
denoted by
x^{\beta}u
, and
the reverse triangle inequality gives

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta}|u_{k}|\to\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta}|u|.

Since

in

implies convergence of both norms on the

3.7
), passing to the limit in (
3.7
) applied to

yields the estimate for

.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
\alpha>1
and
\beta\in\big[\frac{2}{\alpha+1},\frac{3(\alpha+2)}{5\alpha+3}\big)
, we have

(3.15)

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta}|\partial_{x}u|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta-2}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-2\beta}{3\alpha-1}}

for all
u\in U_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

The proof follows a similar approach as the one for Lemma
3.4
, but we include it here for completeness. We first establish (
3.15
) for
u\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
and then extend to
u\in U_{\alpha}
by approximation in Step 4.

Step 1. Weighted estimate and lower bound for
\beta
.

Let
p>1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. In this step, we derive a weighted estimate for the critical case where the
L^{2}
-norm of
u
does not appear on the right-hand side of inequality (
3.15
).
Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta p}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\partial_{x}(x^{\beta p}((\partial_{x}u)^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}})\big|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\beta p\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\beta p-1}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\beta p}\big|\tfrac{p}{2}((\partial_{x}u)^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}2(\partial_{x}u)(\partial_{x}^{2}u)\big|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta,p}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\beta p-1}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\beta p}|\partial_{x}u|^{p-1}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\beta p-\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}|\partial_{x}u|^{p-1}x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\left(|\partial_{x}^{2}u|+x^{-1}|\partial_{x}u|\right)\,{\rm d}x

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{\beta p(\alpha+1)-(\alpha+2)}{\alpha}}|\partial_{x}u|^{\frac{(p-1)(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\left(|\partial_{x}^{2}u|+x^{-1}|\partial_{x}u|\right)^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}.

Now, applying Jensen’s inequality followed by Hardy’s inequality (
3.1
) of Lemma
3.1
, we estimate the second integral on the right-hand side of (
3.16
) and obtain

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta p}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,p}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{\beta p(\alpha+1)-(\alpha+2)}{\alpha}}|\partial_{x}u|^{\frac{(p-1)(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}.\end{split}

To apply Hardy’s inequality again to the first integral on the right-hand side of (
3.17
), we require the exponents to satisfy

\frac{(p-1)(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}=\alpha+1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\beta p(\alpha+1)-(\alpha+2)}{\alpha}=1.

Solving for
\beta
and
p
, we find

\beta=\frac{2}{\alpha+1}\quad\text{and}\quad p=\alpha+1,

and substituting these values into (
3.17
), we deduce the weighted estimate

(3.18)

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\frac{2}{\alpha+1}}|\partial_{x}u|\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}.

Step 2. Weighted estimate and upper bound for
\beta
.

We now use estimate (
3.2
) of Lemma
3.3
, where we set
\nu r=\frac{\beta p(\alpha+1)-(\alpha+2)}{\alpha}
and
r=\frac{(p-1)(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}
, and where
\gamma_{k}
and
r_{k}
are given by (
3.3
). Thus, (
3.17
) can be further estimated as

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta p}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,p,\beta}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+3}(1-\theta^{k})}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma_{k}}|\partial_{x}u|^{r_{k}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\theta^{k}}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\phantom{\lesssim_{\alpha,p,\beta}}\times\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3}{\alpha+3}-\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+3)}\theta^{k}}.

In order to use Hardy’s inequality (
3.1
) of Lemma
3.1
on the second integral on the right-hand side of (
3.19
), we impose the conditions

(3.20)

\gamma_{k}=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad r_{k}=\alpha+1

which we solve for
\beta=\beta_{k}
and
p=p_{k}
further below.
Using this in (
3.19
) and applying (
3.20
) along with Hardy’s inequality, we deduce

(3.21)

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta_{k}p_{k}}|\partial_{x}u|^{p_{k}}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{k},p_{k}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+3}(1-\theta^{k})}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3}{\alpha+3}+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+3}\theta^{k}}.

Next, we determine
\beta_{k}
and
p_{k}
by combining (
3.3
) and (
3.20
). Starting with
p_{k}
, from (
3.3b
) and (
3.20
), we obtain

\theta^{-k}\Big(\frac{(p_{k}-1)(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}-\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}\Big)+\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}=\alpha+1

which simplifies to

p_{k}=\frac{5\alpha+3+\alpha(\alpha-1)\theta^{k}}{\alpha+3}\downarrow\frac{5\alpha+3}{\alpha+3}.

Similarly, using (
3.3a
) and (
3.20
), we solve for
\beta_{k}

\theta^{-k}\Big(\frac{\beta_{k}p_{k}(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}-3\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)}{\alpha(\alpha+3)}\Big)+2\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+3}=1

and obtain

(3.22)

\beta_{k}=\frac{3(\alpha+2)-\alpha\theta^{k}}{5\alpha+3+\alpha(\alpha-1)\theta^{k}}\uparrow 3\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}.

Substituting
\beta_{k}
and
p_{k}
back into (
3.21
) provides the weighted estimate

(3.23)

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta_{k}}|\partial_{x}u|\lesssim_{\alpha,k}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}-2}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-2\beta_{k}}{3\alpha-1}}.

Step 3. Combination of estimates of Steps 1 and 2.

We write
\beta
as a convex combination

\beta=\frac{2}{\alpha+1}\varrho_{k}+\beta_{k}(1-\varrho_{k}),\quad\varrho_{k}\in[0,1],\quad\beta_{k}\uparrow 3\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3},

where
\beta_{k}
is as in (
3.22
) and where

\varrho_{k}=\frac{(\alpha+1)(\beta_{k}-\beta)}{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}-2}\quad\text{and}\quad 1-\varrho_{k}=\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta-2}{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}-2},

so that we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta}|\partial_{x}u|\;\quad

\displaystyle\leq_{\phantom{\alpha,k}}\;\big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\frac{2}{\alpha+1}}|\partial_{x}u|\big)^{\varrho_{k}}\big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta_{k}}|\partial_{x}u|\big)^{1-\varrho_{k}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:weight xu'3},\eqref{eq:weight xu'6}}}}{{\lesssim}}_{\alpha,k}\;\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\varrho_{k}}{\alpha+1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}-2}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho_{k})}

\displaystyle\phantom{\lesssim_{\alpha,k}}\;\times\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{3-2\beta_{k}}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho_{k})}

\displaystyle=_{\phantom{\alpha,k}}\;\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\gamma}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\delta}.

The exponents
\gamma
and
\delta
can be readily computed as

\gamma=\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta_{k}-2}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho_{k})=\frac{(\alpha+1)\beta-2}{3\alpha-1},\qquad\delta=\frac{\varrho_{k}}{\alpha+1}+\frac{3-2\beta_{k}}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho_{k})=\frac{3-2\beta}{3\alpha-1}.

Since
\beta_{k}\uparrow 3\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}
, this proves (
3.15
) for
\beta\in\big[\frac{2}{\alpha+1},\frac{3(\alpha+2)}{5\alpha+3}\big)
.

Step 4. Extension from
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
to
U_{\alpha}
.

The argument is analogous to Step 4 of Lemma
3.4
, that is, a sequence
u_{k}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
is such that
u_{k}\to u
in
U_{\alpha}
, which implies that
x^{\beta}\partial_{x}u_{k}\to\varphi
uniformly by (
3.15
). This implies
x^{\beta}\partial_{x}u=\varphi
and taking the limit in (
3.15
) with
u=u_{k}
finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For
\alpha>1
, the following weighted estimate holds:

(3.24)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{\alpha,\nu,p}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\gamma}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\delta}

for all
u\in U_{\alpha}
, where the exponents
\gamma
and
\delta
are given by

\gamma=\frac{(\alpha-1)p+(\alpha+1)(\nu p+1)}{3\alpha-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\delta=\frac{(1-2\nu)p-2}{3\alpha-1}

and one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

it holds
2\leq p\leq\alpha+1
and
\nu
must satisfy
\nu=0
if
p=2
and

\nu\in\Big[-\frac{\alpha(p-2)}{(\alpha-1)p},\frac{(\alpha+2)(p-2)}{(5\alpha+3)p}\Big)

if
p>2
;

(ii)

it holds
p\geq\alpha+1
and
\nu
must satisfy

\nu\in\Big[-\frac{\alpha+1+(\alpha-1)p}{(\alpha+1)p},\frac{(\alpha+2)(p-2)}{(5\alpha+3)p}\Big).

Proof.

We first establish (
3.24
) for
u\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))

and then extend to
u\in U_{\alpha}
by approximation at the end of the proof. The proof follows the same reasoning as the proofs of Lemmata
3.4
and
3.5
.

Step 1. Weighted estimate and lower bound for
\nu
.

We begin with the case
2\leq p\leq\alpha+1
and set
\nu=-\frac{(p-2)\alpha}{p(\alpha-1)}
.
Applying Hölder’s inequality followed by Hardy’s inequality (cf. Lemma
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\frac{\alpha(p-2)}{\alpha-1}}\,|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\;\,

\displaystyle=_{\phantom{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u|^{\frac{2(\alpha+1-p)}{\alpha-1}}\,x^{-\frac{\alpha(p-2)}{\alpha-1}}\,|u|^{\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-2)}{\alpha-1}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq_{\phantom{\alpha}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1-p}{\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\alpha}|u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p-2}{\alpha-1}}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:Hardy}}}}{{\lesssim}}_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1-p}{\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p-2}{\alpha-1}},

where we have used

p\geq 2\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{2(\alpha+1-p)}{\alpha-1}\leq 2\qquad\text{and}\qquad p\leq\alpha+1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-2)}{\alpha-1}\leq\alpha+1.

For
p\geq\alpha+1
, we apply Hölder’s inequality again, which gives

(3.26)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}\,|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\leq\Big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\,|u|\Big)^{p-(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p+(p-(\alpha+1))\frac{(\alpha-1)}{\alpha+1}}\,|u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x.

We require the condition

\nu p+(p-(\alpha+1))\frac{(\alpha-1)}{\alpha+1}=-\alpha

so that Hardy’s inequality (
3.1
) can be used on the second term. Solving for
\nu
, we obtain

\nu=-\frac{(\alpha-1)p+\alpha+1}{p(\alpha+1)}.

With this choice of
\nu
, it follows from (
3.26
) and Lemma
3.4

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\frac{p(\alpha-1)+\alpha+1}{\alpha+1}}\,|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\;\,

\displaystyle\leq_{\phantom{\alpha}}\Big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\,|u|\Big)^{p-(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\alpha}\,|u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x

(3.27)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:weight xu}}}}{{\lesssim}}_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p}{\alpha+1}}.

This is confirms the claimed inequality (
3.24
) for
p\geq\alpha+1
, with the critical case where the
L^{2}
-norm of
u
does not appear at the lower bound
\nu=-\frac{p(\alpha-1)+\alpha+1}{p(\alpha+1)}
.
Thus, we have proved (
3.24
) for both cases (
i
) and (
ii
) and the respective lower bounds of
\nu
, completing Step 1.

Step 2. Weighted estimate and upper bound for
\nu
.
Let
p>2
and
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.
Applying Hölder’s inequality, we arrive at

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}\,|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\leq\Big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\frac{\nu p}{p-2}}\,|u|\Big)^{p-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

For
\nu\in\big[\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-2)}{(\alpha+1)p},\frac{(\alpha+2)(p-2)}{(5\alpha+3)p}\big)
we can apply Lemma
3.4
to the first-term on the right-hand side, which gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}\,|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\,\;

\displaystyle\leq_{\phantom{\alpha}}\Big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\frac{\nu p}{p-2}}\,|u|\Big)^{p-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(3.28)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:weight xu}}}}{{\lesssim}}_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{(\alpha-1)p+(\alpha+1)(\nu p+1)}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{p-2-2\nu p}{3\alpha-1}}.

This establishes the upper bound for
\nu
in (
3.24
).

Step 3. Combination of estimates of Steps 1 and 2.
We write
\nu
as a convex combination

\nu=\begin{cases}-\frac{\alpha(p-2)}{(\alpha-1)p}\varrho+\tilde{\nu}(1-\varrho)&\text{for $2\leq p\leq\alpha+1$,}\\[11.38092pt]
-\frac{\alpha+1+(\alpha-1)p}{(\alpha+1)p}\varrho+\tilde{\nu}(1-\varrho)&\text{for $p\geq\alpha+1$,}\end{cases}

where
\varrho\in[0,1]
and
\tilde{\nu}\in\big[\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-2)}{(\alpha+1)p},\frac{(\alpha+2)(p-2)}{(5\alpha+3)p}\big)
. We can explicitly compute

\varrho=\begin{cases}\frac{(\alpha-1)(\tilde{\nu}-\nu)p}{\alpha(p-2)+(\alpha-1)\tilde{\nu}p}&\text{for $2\leq p\leq\alpha+1$,}\\[11.38092pt]
\frac{(\alpha+1)(\tilde{\nu}-\nu)p}{(\alpha-1)p+(\alpha+1)(\tilde{\nu}p+1)}&\text{for $p\geq\alpha+1$,}\end{cases}

and

1-\varrho=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha(p-2)+(\alpha-1)\nu p}{\alpha(p-2)+(\alpha-1)\tilde{\nu}p}&\text{for $2\leq p\leq\alpha+1$,}\\[11.38092pt]
\frac{(\alpha-1)p+(\alpha+1)(\nu p+1)}{(\alpha-1)p+(\alpha+1)(\tilde{\nu}p+1)}&\text{for $p\geq\alpha+1$.}\end{cases}

Using Hölder’s inequality we infer

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}\,|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\frac{\alpha(p-2)}{(\alpha-1)}}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\big)^{\varrho}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\tilde{\nu}p}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\big)^{1-\varrho}&\text{for $2\leq p\leq\alpha+1$,}\\[11.38092pt]
\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\frac{\alpha+1+(\alpha-1)p}{\alpha+1}}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\big)^{\varrho}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\tilde{\nu}p}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\big)^{1-\varrho}&\text{for $p\geq\alpha+1$,}\end{cases}

(3.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\nu,p}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\gamma}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\delta},

where estimates (
3.25
), (
3.1
), and (
3.28
) were used in the last step, and the exponents
\gamma
and
\delta
satisfy

\gamma=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha+1-p}{\alpha-1}\varrho+\frac{(\alpha-1)p+(\alpha+1)(\tilde{\nu}p+1)}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho)&\text{for $2\leq p\leq\alpha+1$,}\\[5.69046pt]
\frac{(\alpha-1)p+(\alpha+1)(\tilde{\nu}p+1)}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho)&\text{for $p\geq\alpha+1$,}\end{cases}\Bigg\}=\frac{(\alpha-1)p+(\alpha+1)(\nu p+1)}{3\alpha-1}

and

\delta=\begin{cases}\frac{p-2}{\alpha-1}\varrho+\frac{p-2-2\tilde{\nu}p}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho)&\text{for $2\leq p\leq\alpha+1$,}\\[5.69046pt]
\frac{p}{\alpha+1}\varrho+\frac{p-2-2\tilde{\nu}p}{3\alpha-1}(1-\varrho)&\text{for $p\geq\alpha+1$,}\end{cases}\Bigg\}=\frac{p(1-2\nu)-2}{3\alpha-1}.

This proves (
3.24
) provided conditions (
i
) or (
ii
) are satisfied.

Step 4. Extension from
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
to
U_{\alpha}
.

For
u\in U_{\alpha}
, let
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))

with
u_{k}\to u
in
U_{\alpha}
. Since Lemma
3.4
has been
extended to
U_{\alpha}
, applying it to
u_{k}-u
gives
u_{k}\to u
pointwise,
and Fatou’s lemma yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}|u_{k}|^{p}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\nu,p}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty))}^{\gamma}\big\|x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{k}\big\|_{L^{\alpha+1}((0,\infty))}^{\delta}=\|u\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty))}^{\gamma}\big\|x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}u\big\|_{L^{\alpha+1}((0,\infty))}^{\delta},

which is (
3.24
) for
u\in U_{\alpha}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\alpha>1
. Then the following estimate holds:

(3.30)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\gamma}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\delta}

for all
u\in U_{\alpha}
, where the exponents
\gamma
and
\delta
are given by

\gamma=\frac{\alpha+1+((\alpha+1)\nu-2)p}{3\alpha-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\delta=\frac{(3-2\nu)p-2}{3\alpha-1},

where either
p=\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
and
\nu=\frac{\alpha+2}{2(\alpha+1)}
, or
p>\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
and

\nu\in\Big[\frac{2}{\alpha+1}+\frac{2(\alpha-2)}{(\alpha+3)p},\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}\big(3-\tfrac{2}{p}\big)\Big)

Proof.

We first prove the estimate for
u\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
and extend to
u\in U_{\alpha}
at the end of the proof by approximation.

Step 1. Critical case
p=\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
.

We start by approaching the upper bound on
\nu
.
From estimate (
3.2
) of Lemma
3.2
, that is,

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{\alpha,\nu,p}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+3}(1-\theta^{k})}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma_{k}}|\partial_{x}u|^{r_{k}}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\theta^{k}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{2}{\alpha+3}(1-\theta^{k})}

and equations (
3.3
), that is,

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\theta^{-k}\Big(\nu p-\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}\Big)+\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}\quad\text{and}\quad r_{k}=\theta^{-k}\Big(p-\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}\Big)+\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3},

where
\theta=\frac{\alpha-1}{2(\alpha+1)}
, we find for the critical case
p=\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
and
\nu=\frac{\alpha+2}{2(\alpha+1)}
that
\gamma_{k}=\nu p
and
r_{k}=p
, so
that (
3.2
) simplifies to

(3.31)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}}|\partial_{x}u|^{\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+3}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{2}{\alpha+3}}.

Step 2. Super-critical Case
p>\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
.

With estimate (
3.31
) in hand, we improve upon it by considering general
p>\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
and
\nu
, allowing us to obtain a refined bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\nu p-\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}}|\partial_{x}u|^{p-\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}}\Big)\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}}|\partial_{x}u|^{\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}}\,{\rm d}x

(3.32)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\nu p-\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}}|\partial_{x}u|^{p-\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+3}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{2}{\alpha+3}}.

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.32
), we apply Lemma
3.5

and therefore choose
\nu
such that

\frac{2}{\alpha+1}\leq\frac{\nu p-\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+3}}{p-\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}}<\frac{3(\alpha+2)}{5\alpha+3}

Since
p>\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
, we get after rearranging

(3.33)

\frac{2}{\alpha+1}+\frac{2(\alpha-2)}{(\alpha+3)p}\leq\nu<\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}\big(3-\tfrac{2}{p}\big).

Thus, the combination of estimate (
3.15
) of Lemma
3.5
and (
3.32
) gives the estimate

(3.34)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu p}|\partial_{x}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\gamma}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\delta},

when either
p=\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
and
\nu=\frac{\alpha+2}{2(\alpha+1)}
, or

p>\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{\alpha+3}
and

\nu\in\Big[\frac{2}{\alpha+1}+\frac{2(\alpha-2)}{(\alpha+3)p},\frac{\alpha+2}{5\alpha+3}\big(3-\tfrac{2}{p}\big)\Big).

The exponents
\gamma
and
\delta
follow from scaling balance and satisfy

p=2\gamma+(\alpha+1)\delta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nu p+1-p=\gamma+\delta(1-\alpha).

Solving for
\gamma
and
\delta
, we obtain

\gamma=\frac{\alpha+1+((\alpha+1)\nu-2)p}{3\alpha-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\delta=\frac{(3-2\nu)p-2}{3\alpha-1}

as desired.

Step 3. Extension from
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
to
U_{\alpha}
.

This follows as in Step 4 of the proof of Lemma
3.6
, that is, for
u\in U_{\alpha}
and
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))

with
u_{k}\to u
in
U_{\alpha}
we have
\partial_{x}u_{k}\to\partial_{x}u
point-wise by (
3.15
) of Lemma
3.5
, and the claim (
3.30
) for
u
follows with Fatou’s lemma.
∎

3.2.
Asymptotics and interpolation estimates in
V_{\alpha}

In what follows, it is convenient to introduce the space

(3.35)

V_{\alpha}\coloneq\Big\{v\in U_{\alpha}\colon\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}v)))^{2}\,{\rm d}x<\infty\Big\}.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose
\alpha>1
. For
v\in V_{\alpha}
define
w\coloneq x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}v)
. Then

(3.36a)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=ax(1+o(x^{\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle\text{as }x\downarrow 0,

(3.36b)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}w

\displaystyle=a(1+o(x^{\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle\text{as }x\downarrow 0,

(3.36c)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=cx^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}(1+o(x^{\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle\text{as }x\downarrow 0,

(3.36d)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}v

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}cx^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}(1+o(x^{\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle\text{as }x\downarrow 0,

(3.36e)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}cx^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(1+o(x^{\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle\text{as }x\downarrow 0,

where
\partial_{x}w(0)=a=g_{\alpha}((1-\alpha)c/\alpha^{2})
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore,

(3.37)

w(x)=ax+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)(x^{\prime\prime})\,{\rm d}x^{\prime\prime}\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\partial_{x}w)(x)=a+\int_{0}^{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)(x^{\prime})\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}.

Proof.

We first begin by noticing that the regularity assumptions on
v
entail that
\partial_{x}^{2}w\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
, so that two integrations yield

w(x)=ax+b+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)(x^{\prime\prime})\,{\rm d}x^{\prime\prime}\,{\rm d}x^{\prime},

for constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. If
b\neq 0
, we have

x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}=x^{-\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}|w|^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}=|b|^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}x^{-\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}(1+o(1))\quad\text{as }x\downarrow 0,

which contradicts
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def-ualpha}}}{{<}}\infty
because
v\in U_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def-valpha}}}{{\subset}}V_{\alpha}
. Hence, we must have
b=0
, but do have
a\neq 0
in general (which physically corresponds to a nonzero contact-line velocity, see the heuristic discussion in §
1.5
). This yields (
3.37
). For
w
this entails the asymptotics (
3.36a
), (
3.36b
), (
3.36c
) on using

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)(x^{\prime\prime})\,{\rm d}x^{\prime\prime}\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}(0,x)}\int_{0}^{x}\Big(\int_{0}^{x^{\prime}}{\rm d}x^{\prime\prime}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}=o(x^{\frac{3}{2}})

\displaystyle\text{as }x\downarrow 0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}(\partial_{x}w)(x^{\prime})\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{2}(0,x)}\Big(\int_{0}^{x}{\rm d}x^{\prime}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=o(x^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\text{as }x\downarrow 0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle<\infty\quad\Rightarrow\quad\partial_{x}^{2}w=o(x^{-\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\text{as }x\downarrow 0.

The terms with
v
follow on identifying
a=g_{\alpha}((1-\alpha)c/\alpha^{2})
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

For
\alpha>1
it holds
|x_{1}-x_{2}|\leq 2^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}|g_{\alpha}(x_{1})-g_{\alpha}(x_{2})|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
it holds

\displaystyle(g_{\alpha}(x_{1})-g_{\alpha}(x_{2}))(x_{1}-x_{2})

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}g_{\alpha}^{\prime}(x_{2}+\tau(x_{1}-x_{2}))\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle=\alpha|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{1}\big|\tfrac{x_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}+\tau\big|^{\alpha-1}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\geq\alpha|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha+1}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\tau|^{\alpha-1}\,{\rm d}\tau=2^{1-\alpha}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha+1},

which implies the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

For
\alpha>1
suppose

. Then with the notation of Lemma

(3.38)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2\alpha}|\partial_{x}^{2}(v-cx^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}})|^{2\alpha}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2\alpha}(g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}v)-ax^{-1-\alpha})^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,

(3.39)

and verifies the second estimate in (

∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Suppose
\alpha>1
. For
v\in V_{\alpha}
and
w\coloneq x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}v)
it holds

(3.40)

\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{-\frac{3}{2}}|w-ax|+\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\partial_{x}w-a|\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
a\coloneq\partial_{x}w(0)
is as in Lemma
3.8
. Furthermore, we have

(3.41)

|(\partial_{x}w)(0)|=|a|\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{3\alpha-1}}.

Lastly, we have with
\beta\in[-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{3\alpha+4}{2(2\alpha+1)}]

(3.42)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{\beta}|w-\delta ax|\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha(2\beta+3)}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{-(\alpha+1)\beta-2}{3\alpha-1}},\quad\delta\coloneq\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\beta<-1,\\
0&\text{if }\beta\geq-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We split the proof into three parts.

Step 1. Proof of (
3.40
).

Using Lemma
3.8
, it follows

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{-3}(w-ax)^{2}\quad

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{as-w}}}}{{\leq}}\quad 3\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-4}(w-ax)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+2\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-3}|w-ax||\partial_{x}w-a|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\quad 4\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-4}(w-ax)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-2}(\partial_{x}w-a)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{-1}(\partial_{x}w-a)^{2}\quad

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{as-dxw}}}}{{\leq}}\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-2}(\partial_{x}w-a)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+2\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-1}|\partial_{x}w-a||\partial_{x}^{2}w|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\quad 2\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-2}(\partial_{x}w-a)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

where the fundamental theorem of calculus as well as Young’s and Hardy’s inequality were used.

Step 2. Proof of (
3.41
).

Observe that by the triangle and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle|a|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{2}|w-ax|\,{\rm d}x+\int_{1}^{2}|w|\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-4}(w-ax)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}},

where we have applied Hardy’s inequality in the last line. Scaling
x\mapsto\lambda x
with
\lambda>0
leads to

|a|\lesssim_{\alpha}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}},

so that after optimizing in
\lambda
we arrive at (
3.41
).

Step 3. Proof of (
3.42
).

For a cut-off function
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta|_{[0,1]}=1
, and
\eta_{|[2,\infty)}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq 0}x^{-\frac{3\alpha+4}{2\alpha}}|w-\eta ax|^{\frac{2\alpha+1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\alpha+4}{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\frac{5\alpha+4}{2\alpha}}|w-\eta ax|^{\frac{2\alpha+1}{\alpha}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{\lesssim}+\frac{2\alpha+1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\frac{3\alpha+4}{2\alpha}}|w-\eta ax|^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}|\partial_{x}(w-\eta ax)|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle S_{1}\coloneqq A^{\frac{2(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}B^{\frac{4\alpha}{3\alpha-1}}C^{\frac{\alpha+1}{3\alpha-1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad S_{2}\coloneqq A^{\frac{4(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}B^{\frac{4}{3\alpha-1}}C,

we can apply Young’s inequality to find (notice
\tfrac{\alpha+1}{4(\alpha-1)}\in(0,1)
and
1-\tfrac{\alpha+1}{4(\alpha-1)}=\tfrac{3\alpha-5}{4(\alpha-1)}
)

\displaystyle A^{\frac{7\alpha-9}{2(3\alpha-1)}}B^{\frac{\alpha+5}{3\alpha-1}}C^{\frac{5\alpha-3}{2(3\alpha-1)}}=S_{1}^{\frac{\alpha+1}{4(\alpha-1)}}\,S_{2}^{\frac{3\alpha-5}{4(\alpha-1)}}\lesssim_{\alpha}S_{1}+S_{2},

and consequently, we obtain the reduced estimate

(4.7)

\displaystyle I_{2,1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\underbrace{A^{\frac{2(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}B^{\frac{4\alpha}{3\alpha-1}}C^{\frac{\alpha+1}{3\alpha-1}}}_{I_{2,1,1},\ I_{2,1,2},\ I_{2,1,7}}+\underbrace{A^{\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}B^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{3\alpha-1}}C^{\frac{2\alpha}{3\alpha-1}}}_{I_{2,1,3},\ I_{2,1,5},\ I_{2,1,6}}+\underbrace{A^{\frac{4(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}B^{\frac{4}{3\alpha-1}}C}_{I_{2,1,4},\ I_{2,1,7}}\;\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:B2-AC}}}}{{\lesssim}}_{\alpha}A^{\frac{4(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}B^{\frac{4}{3\alpha-1}}C,

where we have used Lemma
3.16
in the last estimate.

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{i2-split}}}}{{=}}\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\big)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}\,|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{2(2\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{3\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=\;B^{\frac{2(2\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}C^{\frac{\alpha+1}{3\alpha-1}}\;\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:B2-AC}}}}{{\lesssim}}_{\alpha}A^{\frac{2(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}B^{\frac{2}{3\alpha-1}}C,

x=0

x=\infty

3.8
, since

as

for
\alpha>2
. In order to obtain vanishing

\chi_{R}(x)\coloneq\chi(x/R)

3.41

By Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
3.4

\displaystyle I_{2,3,1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\sup_{x\geq 0}|v|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\,(\partial_{x}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{lem:weight xu}}}}{{\lesssim}}_{\alpha}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\,(\partial_{x}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{-1}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{2(\alpha-1)}{3\alpha-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}v|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{2}{3\alpha-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}w)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\varepsilon I_{2,3},

For

5.
Existence of strong solutions using a time-discretization scheme

Throughout the section we assume
\alpha>2
. We address problem (
1.13
), that is,

u_{t}+\partial_{x}^{2}\big(x^{\alpha+2}\,g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u)\big)=f\quad\text{for }t,x>0,

with the nonlinear right-hand side

\displaystyle f\coloneqq N(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq(1-(1+u)^{\frac{3}{2}})\,\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}\,g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u))+(1+u)^{\frac{3}{2}}\,\partial_{x}^{2}\big((1-(1+u)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})x^{\alpha+2}\,g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u)\big),

by means of the following time-discretization scheme:

(5.1a)

\frac{u_{j}-u_{j-1}}{h}+\partial_{x}^{2}\big(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j})\big)=f_{j-1},\qquad j\in\mathbb{N},

equipped with the boundary condition

(5.1b)

u_{j}=0\quad\text{at }x=0,

where
0<h<\infty
,
t_{j}\coloneq jh
(hence
t_{0}=0
),
f_{j-1}
is given by

(5.2)

f_{j-1}\coloneq N(u_{j-1})

(cf. (
1.13b
)), and the prescribed initial data

u_{0}(\cdot)\coloneq u(0,\cdot)
. Thanks to the approximation property of Lemma
3.2
, we may first assume
u_{0}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
.
On the linear space

U_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def-ualpha}}}{{=}}\big\{u\in L^{2}(0,\infty)\colon x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}u\in L^{\alpha+1}(0,\infty),\;u=0\text{ at }x=0\big\},

we introduce the functional

(5.3)

U_{\alpha}\owns u\longmapsto J_{\alpha,j}(u)\coloneq\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}u^{2}+\tfrac{h}{\alpha+1}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}-\tilde{f}_{j-1}u\big)\,{\rm d}x,

where

(5.4)

\tilde{f}_{j}\coloneq hf_{j}+u_{j}.

Provided
\tilde{f}_{j-1}\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
(in fact, we have the stronger regularity
\tilde{f}_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
for
j=0
), the functional
J_{\alpha,j}
is well-defined and finite on
U_{\alpha}
. The existence of a solution
u_{j}
for (
5.1
) at step

can be obtained by minimizing the functional
J_{\alpha,j}
via the direct method in the calculus of variations by using the fact that the space
U_{\alpha}
is weakly closed. Observe that the boundary condition (
5.1b
) is closed under weak convergence by estimate (
3.7
) of Lemma
3.4
and letting
x\downarrow 0
.

Lemma 5.1

(Existence of minimizers for
J_{\alpha,j}
)
.

Provided
\tilde{f}_{j-1}\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
, there exists a unique minimizer
u_{j}\in U_{\alpha}
of the functional
J_{\alpha,j}
(cf. (
2.1a
), (
5.3
)).

Proof.

Step 1:
U_{\alpha}
is non-empty.
We have the inclusion

by Lemma
3.2
.

Step 2: Boundedness from below and coercivity.
We have by Young’s inequality

-\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{f}_{j-1}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}\leq\inf_{u\in U_{\alpha}}J_{\alpha,j}(u)=\inf_{u\in U_{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}u^{2}+\tfrac{h}{\alpha+1}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha+1}-\tilde{f}_{j-1}u\bigr){\rm d}x

by the definition (

) of

, so the functional is bounded from below.
Moreover, the functional is coercive in the sense that if
(u^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset U_{\alpha}
is such that
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}J_{\alpha,j}(u^{(k)})\leq C
, then by Young’s inequality

\frac{1}{4}\|u^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}+\frac{h}{\alpha+1}\|x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(k)}\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(0,\infty)}^{\alpha+1}-\|\tilde{f}_{j-1}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}\leq J_{\alpha,j}(u^{(k)})\leq C.

(u^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset U_{\alpha}
there exists a function
u_{j}\in U_{\alpha}
such that for a subsequence (not relabeled)

u^{(k)}\rightharpoonup u_{j}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,\infty)\qquad\text{and}\qquad x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{(k)}\rightharpoonup x^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}\quad\text{in }L^{\alpha+1}(0,\infty),

where
u_{j}\in U_{\alpha}
because
U_{\alpha}
is weakly closed.

Step 3: Weak lower semi-continuity.

This is ensured by the (strict) convexity of the functions
s\mapsto|s|^{2}
and
\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{\alpha+1}
. Indeed, the linear space
U_{\alpha}
is convex and the second Gateaux derivative is

(5.5)

D^{2}J_{\alpha,j}(u)(v,v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(v^{2}+\alpha hx^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{\alpha-1}(\partial_{x}^{2}v)^{2}\big)\,{\rm d}x\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad u,v\in U_{\alpha}.

Thus, we have

J_{\alpha,j}(u_{j})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}J_{\alpha,j}(u^{(k)})

and hence, if
u^{(k)}
is a minimizing sequence, then
u_{j}
is a minimizer.

Step 4: Uniqueness.

For two minimizers
u_{j},u_{j}^{\prime}\in U_{\alpha}
it holds

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=DJ_{\alpha,j}(u_{j})(u_{j}-u_{j}^{\prime})-DJ_{\alpha,j}(u_{j}^{\prime})(u_{j}-u_{j}^{\prime})\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}J_{\alpha,j}(\tau u_{j}+(1-\tau)u_{j}^{\prime})(u_{j}-u_{j}^{\prime},u_{j}-u_{j}^{\prime})\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{second_ja}}}}{{\geq}}\;\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(u_{j}-u_{j}^{\prime})^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\tau=\|u_{j}-u_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2},

so that
u_{j}=u_{j}^{\prime}
must hold almost everywhere.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Euler–Lagrange equation corresponding to
J_{\alpha,j}
)
.

Suppose
\tilde{f}_{j-1}\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
and let
u_{j}\in U_{\alpha}
be a minimizer of
J_{\alpha,j}
. Then we have the strong formulation of the Euler-Lagrange equation

(5.6)

h\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}))=\tilde{f}_{j-1}-u_{j}\quad\text{almost everywhere.}

In particular,
\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}))\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
, that is,

u_{j}\in V_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def-valpha}}}{{=}}\Big\{v\in U_{\alpha}\colon\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}v))\big)^{2}\,{\rm d}x<\infty\Big\}.

Proof.

Since
DJ_{\alpha,j}(u_{j})=0
for the Gateaux derivative
DJ_{\alpha,j}
of
J_{\alpha,j}
, we have for
v\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((0,\infty))
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}hx^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j})(\partial_{x}^{2}v)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\tilde{f}_{j-1}-u_{j})v\,{\rm d}x.

This entails the identity of weak derivatives
h\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}))=\tilde{f}_{j-1}-u_{j}\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
and that (
5.1
) is satisfied almost everywhere.
∎

By testing the equation with
u_{j}
we obtain the following lemma, which is the discretized analogue of (
1.14a
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
u_{1},\ldots,u_{j}
, constructed in Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2

\tilde{f}_{0},\ldots,\tilde{f}_{j-1}\in L^{2}(0,\infty)

(5.7)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(u_{j}^{2}-u_{j-1}^{2})\,{\rm d}x+h\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x\leq h\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{j-1}\,u_{j}\,{\rm d}x.

Proof.

Testing the discretized equation

\frac{u_{j}-u_{j-1}}{h}+\partial_{x}^{2}\left(x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}|^{\alpha-1}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}\right)=f_{j-1}

with

and integrating by parts (cf. (

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{j}-u_{j-1}}{h}u_{j}{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}|^{\alpha+1}{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{j-1}u_{j}\,{\rm d}x.

Using the elementary identity

(u_{j}-u_{j-1})u_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{j}^{2}-u_{j-1}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}(u_{j}-u_{j-1})^{2}

and multiplying by

, we arrive at (

).
∎

By testing the equation with
\partial_{x}^{2}\bigl(x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}|^{\alpha-1}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}\bigr)
we obtain the following time-discretized analogue of estimate (

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
u_{1},\ldots,u_{j}

Since

by Lemma

\frac{u_{j}-u_{j-1}}{h}+\partial_{x}^{2}\left(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j})\right)=f_{j-1}

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x+h\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}))\big)^{2}{\rm d}x-\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{j-1}\,\partial_{x}^{2}(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}\big(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j})\big)\,{\rm d}x\\

in

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{j-1}\,\partial_{x}^{2}\big(x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j})\big)\,{\rm d}x&=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j-1})\,x^{\alpha+2}g_{\alpha}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j})\,{\rm d}x\\
&\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j-1}|^{\alpha+1}\,{\rm d}x,\end{split}

so that we obtain (

∎

Proposition 5.5

(Time-discretized problem)
.

Let

\int_{0}^{\infty}(u_{0}^{2}+x^{\alpha+2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{0}|^{\alpha+1})\,{\rm d}x<\infty

\displaystyle f^{h}(t,x)

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{j\geq 1}f_{j-1}(x)\chi_{[(j-1)h,jh)}(t),\quad\text{where}\quad f_{j-1}=N(u_{j-1}),

\displaystyle\tilde{u}^{h}(t,x)

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{j\geq 1}\big(\tfrac{t-(j-1)h}{h}u_{j}(x)+\tfrac{jh-t}{h}u_{j-1}(x)\big)\chi_{[(j-1)h,jh)}(t),
