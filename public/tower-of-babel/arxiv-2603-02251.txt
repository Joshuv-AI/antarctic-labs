On the Chow ring of double EPW quartics
Carl Mazzanti

Abstract
. Double EPW quartics are hyperkähler varieties of dimension 4, first introduced by Iliev, Kapustka, Kapustka, and Ranestad. The general double EPW quartic is isomorphic to a moduli space of twisted sheaves on a K ​ 3 K3 surface. They have a rich geometry: they are equipped with an anti-symplectic involution and are related to conics in Verra fourfolds in the same way Fano varieties of lines on cubic fourfolds are related to cubic fourfolds themselves. In this work, we exploit this geometry to establish general conjectures about algebraic cycles on hyperkähler varieties in the case of double EPW quartics.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Algebraic cycles on hyperkähler varieties

Algebraic cycles on hyperkähler varieties have been the subject of much research.
Following the work of Beauville and Voisin on the Chow ring of a
K3
surface in
[
BV04
]
, Beauville conjectured that the conjectural Bloch–Beilinson filtration admits a canonical splitting in the case of hyperkähler varieties
[
Bea07
]
.
As a concrete consequence, Beauville conjectured that the subring generated by divisors maps injectively to the cohomology via the cycle class map for any hyperkähler variety
[
Bea07
]
.
Voisin considered a generalisation of this conjecture in
[
Voi08
]
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Beauville–Voisin, see
7.1
)
.

Let
T
be a hyperkähler variety. Then the cycle class map restricted to the subring generated by divisors and Chern classes,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\left\langle\chow^{1}(T),c_{i}(T)\right\rangle\to H^{\ast}(T,\mathbb{Q}),

is injective.

Another question studied following
[
BV04
]
is the Franchetta conjecture for hyperkähler varieties.
O’Grady noticed in
[
OGr13
]
that the Beauville–Voisin class constructed in
[
BV04
]
is
generically defined
, see
Definition

3.1
, and asked whether any generically defined zero-cycle on a
K3
surface is a multiple of the Beauville–Voisin class.
This was generalised to higher-dimensional hyperkähler varieties in
[
FLV19
]
, where the Franchetta property was introduced and conjectured to hold for locally complete families of hyperkähler varieties.

Conjecture 1.2

(Franchetta conjecture, see
3.3
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the moduli stack of a locally complete family of hyperkähler varieties and
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{F}
its universal family.
Then it satisfies the Franchetta property, meaning that for any
b\in\mathcal{F}
the cycle class map restricted to the subring of generically defined cycles,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\gdch^{\ast}(\mathcal{T}_{b})\to H^{\ast}(\mathcal{T}_{b},\mathbb{Q}),

is injective.

The interaction of the Franchetta conjecture with the Beauville–Voisin conjecture has been studied in
[
LV26
]
.

These conjectures are open in general, but there are two cases where significant progress has been made: the Fano variety of lines on a cubic fourfold and Hilbert schemes of points on
K3
surfaces.
The Beauville–Voisin conjecture has been proven in the case of the Fano variety of lines and for
S^{[n]}
for small
n
in
[
Voi08
]
.

The Franchetta property is known to hold for
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathcal{M}
, the universal family of Fano varieties of lines on cubic fourfolds, and for
\mathcal{S}^{n/\mathcal{F}_{2d}}\to\mathcal{F}_{2d}
, the universal family of powers of
K3
surfaces for small
n
and low degree
d
, by
[
FLV19
]
, even though the latter is not a locally complete family.
See
Remark

3.6
for details on the Franchetta property in the case of non-locally complete families.
In addition to this, a multiplicative splitting of the Chow ring of these varieties was constructed in
[
SV16
]
, verifying some results predicted by the Bloch–Beilinson conjecture.

The Chow ring of moduli spaces of stable objects on possibly twisted
K3
surfaces, of which double EPW quartics are examples, is less well understood.
The more geometric techniques used to study the Beauville–Voisin conjecture for Hilbert schemes of points on
K3
surfaces and Fano varieties of lines are not available in this setting, complicating the study of their Chow rings.

1.2.
The Beauville–Voisin conjecture for double EPW quartics

The aim of this paper is to complete the picture in the case of double EPW quartics.
These are hyperkähler fourfolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type admitting an anti-symplectic involution and were first constructed by Iliev, Kapustka, Kapustka, and Ranestad in
[
IKKR17
]
.
They form a divisor in the moduli space of
K3^{[2]}
-type varieties.
While they are constructed via Lagrangian degeneracy loci, the general double EPW quartic admits a description in terms of
(1,1)
-conics on Verra fourfolds and as a moduli space of stable objects on a twisted
K3
surface.
This rich geometry makes it possible to study their cycle-theoretic properties in depth.

We prove the following generalisation of the Beauville–Voisin conjecture proposed in
[
LV26
]
. For
K3^{[2]}
-type hyperkähler varieties, this is equivalent to both the Beauville–Voisin and the Franchetta conjecture, see
Section

7.1
for more details.

The Beauville–Voisin conjecture is known in a handful of cases
[
BV04
,
Voi08
,
Fu15
,
LV21
,
Lat25a
,
LV26
]
, see
Section

7.1
for more details.
The Franchetta conjecture, on the other hand, is known for fewer hyperkähler varieties and some Fano varieties
[
PSY17
,
FL23
,
Lu25
,
FLV19
,
FLV21
]
, see
Section

3
for more details.

Theorem 1.3

(
Theorem

7.8
)
.

Let
Y
be a double EPW quartic. Then the Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture holds for
Y
, meaning that the cycle class map restricted to the subring generated by divisors and generically defined cycles,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\left\langle\chow^{1}(Y),\gdch^{\ast}(Y)\right\rangle\to H^{\ast}(Y,\mathbb{Q}),

is injective.

In particular, this establishes the existence of a special zero-cycle
o_{Y}\in\chow_{0}(Y)
, called the Beauville–Voisin class, satisfying
\langle\chow^{1}(Y),\gdch^{\ast}(Y)\rangle_{0}=\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}
.
We prove that this is not only a zero-cycle, but is represented by a point on a constant cycle subvariety.

Theorem 1.4

(
Theorem

5.3
)
.

Let
Y
be a general double EPW quartic and
Z
the fixed locus of its involution
\iota
. Then
Z
is a constant cycle surface in
Y
and the Beauville–Voisin class
o_{Y}
is represented by any point lying on
Z
.

1.3.
Parallels to Fano varieties of lines on cubic fourfolds

As in the case of double EPW sextics, or more generally hyperkähler fourfolds with an anti-symplectic involution, we expect the Bloch–Beilinson filtration on zero-cycles to split in the following way, where the superscript indicates (anti-)invariance with respect to the involution
\iota
on
Y
,

\chow_{0}(Y)=\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}.

This bears resemblance to the case of Fano varieties of lines on cubic fourfolds, where it is known that the group of zero-cycles admits an eigenspace decomposition with respect to Voisin’s self-rational map
[
SV16
]
.

Furthermore, the general double EPW quartic
Y
is associated to a unique Verra fourfold
X
, first studied in
[
Ver04
]
, which is a Fano variety of Picard rank
2
and
K3
type.
This is similar to both the case of the Fano variety of lines on a cubic fourfold and to the case of double EPW sextics, which are also associated to Fano varieties of
K3
type: they are associated to cubic fourfolds and Gushel–Mukai fourfolds, respectively.
More precisely, the Hilbert scheme
F(X)
of conics in
X
admits the structure of a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
Y
.
The universal conic induces a correspondence between
Y
and
X
, similar to the universal line inducing a correspondence between the Fano variety of lines on a cubic fourfold and the cubic fourfold itself.
Both in the case of Fano varieties of lines and of double EPW sextics, it is known that the middle graded part of the group of zero-cycles is isomorphic to the group of homologically trivial
1
-cycles on the associated fourfold
[
SV16
,
Zha24
]
.
We prove the analogous statement and compare the Chow ring of
Y
with that of
X
.

Theorem 1.5

(
Theorems

5.6
,
5.10
and
5.11
)
.

Let
Y
be a general double EPW quartic,
X
the Verra fourfold associated to
Y
, and
P
the universal conic.
We have isomorphisms

\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\xrightarrow[\cong]{P_{\ast}}\chow_{1}(X)_{\mathrm{hom}}\xrightarrow[\cong]{P^{t}_{\ast}}\chow^{2}(Y)^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}

induced by the universal conic.
The inverse of the composition is given (up to a non-zero scalar) by intersecting with
h^{2}
.
Furthermore,

\chow^{3}(Y)^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}=h\cdot\chow^{2}(Y)^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}\oplus g\cdot\chow^{2}(Y)^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}},

where
g
is the other invariant divisor, see
Section

5.3
for the definition.

We also show that Voisin’s filtration, introduced in
[
Voi16
]
, agrees with the expected splitting of the conjectural Bloch–Beilinson filtration.

Proposition 1.6

(
Proposition

5.14
)
.

Let
Y
be a double EPW quartic.
Then Voisin’s filtration agrees with the filtration obtained from the involution
\iota
,

\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\subset\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\subset\chow_{0}(Y).

Due to the surjectivity of
H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{2})\otimes H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{2})\to H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{4})
, the Bloch–Beilinson conjecture predicts that the multiplication map
F^{2}\chow^{2}(Y)\otimes F^{2}\chow^{2}(Y)\to F^{4}\chow_{0}(Y)
is surjective on the deepest part of the conjectural filtration.
While the existence of the Bloch–Beilinson filtration is not known in general, this was shown for what it is expected to be in the case of Fano varieties of lines on cubic fourfolds
[
SV16
, Thm. 20.2]
.
We would expect that
F^{2}\chow^{2}(Y)=\chow^{2}(Y)^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}
in the case of double EPW quartics and double EPW sextics.
We prove it in this setting.

Theorem 1.7

(
Theorem

6.2
,
Theorem

A.5
)
.

Let
Y
be a general double EPW quartic or a general double EPW sextic. Then the intersection pairing

\chow^{2}(Y)^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}\otimes\chow^{2}(Y)^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}\to\chow_{0}(Y)^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}

is surjective.

1.5.
Conventions

We work over the complex numbers.
A variety refers to a separated, integral scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
and a subvariety to a closed, reduced, possibly reducible, subscheme of a variety, unless stated otherwise.
A hyperkähler variety refers to a smooth, projective variety that is simply connected in the analytic topology and carries a holomorphic symplectic form that is unique up to scalars, i.e.
H^{0}(T,\Omega_{T}^{2})=\mathbb{C}\sigma
.
Points in varieties refer to closed points, unless otherwise specified.
Chow rings will be assumed to have rational coefficients and
\chow^{\ast}(T)_{\mathrm{hom}}
always refers to homologically trivial cycles with respect to singular cohomology.
A general point in a variety refers to a point contained in a non-empty open subset, while a very general point in a variety refers to a point contained in the non-empty complement of a countable union of subvarieties.
We refer to
[
And04
]
for details on Chow motives.

A glossary of notation can be found at the end of this article.

2.
The construction of double EPW quartics

Introduced by Iliev, Kapustka, Kapustka, and Ranestad in
[
IKKR17
]
, double EPW quartics
Y
are hyperkähler fourfolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type that form a
19
-dimensional family.
They carry a polarisation
h
of degree
4
with respect to the Beauville–Bogomolov form
q
, i.e.
q(h,h)=4
, and an anti-symplectic involution
\iota
with invariant lattice isometric to
U(2)
.
Furthermore, they admit two Lagrangian fibrations.

They were first constructed in
[
IKKR17
]
, where three different constructions were given: one via Lagrangian degeneracy loci, one via conics in Verra fourfolds and one via moduli spaces of twisted sheaves on K3 surfaces.
We review these constructions in this section.
While the details of the third construction will not be used in this article, we include it for future reference.
The second and third constructions can be skipped on first read and referred back to when needed.
We first summarise the results.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
IKKR17
]
)
.

There is a
19
-dimensional family
\mathcal{U}
of hyperkähler varieties
Y
of
K3^{[2]}
-type, called double EPW quartics, that admit a polarisation of Beauville degree
4
and an anti-symplectic involution
\iota\colon Y\to Y
with invariant lattice
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})^{\iota}
isomorphic to
U(2)
.
Each double EPW quartic admits two Lagrangian fibrations
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
The general double EPW quartic
Y
admits three distinct realisations:

a.

as the base of a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\alpha\colon F(X)\to Y
, where
F(X)
is the Hilbert scheme of
(1,1)
-conics in a Verra fourfold
X=Q\cap C(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})
, where
Q
is a general quadric hypersurface. There exists a unique such Verra fourfold associated to each double EPW quartic.

b.

as the moduli space of stable sheaves twisted by a
2
-torsion Brauer class on a polarised
K3
surface of genus
2
. The general double EPW quartic admits exactly two such associated
K3
surfaces.

c.

as a double cover
Y\to D_{1}^{\overline{A}}
of a Lagrangian degeneracy locus associated to a unique Lagrangian space
A
. The base
D_{1}^{\overline{A}}\subset C(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})
is a quartic section in a cone. The projections induce the two Lagrangian fibrations
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Only construction
c.
applies to all double EPW quartics.

2.1.
Double EPW quartics via conics in Verra fourfolds

We begin by reviewing the construction of double EPW quartics via conics in Verra fourfolds, as this is the construction we will be using most, in particular in
Sections

4
,
5
and
6
.
Proofs for all statements in this section can be found in
[
IKKR17
, Sec. 3]
.

Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be three-dimensional complex vector spaces and fix volume forms on
U_{1}
and
U_{2}^{\vee}
, such that
\wedge^{2}U_{1}=U_{1}^{\vee}
and
\wedge^{2}U_{2}^{\vee}=U_{2}
.
Denote the projective cone over the Segre embedding of
\mathbb{P}(U_{1})\times\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}U_{2})
by
C(\mathbb{P}(U_{1})\times\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}U_{2}))
.
Now, define a
Verra fourfold

X
as the intersection

X\coloneq Q\cap C(\mathbb{P}(U_{1})\times\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}U_{2}))\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus U_{1}\otimes\wedge^{2}U_{2})

of the cone with a quadric hypersurface
Q
.
The general Verra fourfold is a smooth Fano variety, see
[
Lat20
, Lem. 2.3]
, and a double cover
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{P}(U_{1})\times\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}U_{2})
branched along a
(2,2)
-divisor.
Denote the composition of
\pi
with the projections
\pr_{i}
by
\pi_{i}
.

Consider the Hilbert scheme of
(1,1)
-conics on
X
,

F(X)\coloneq\left\{c\subset X\penalty\ |\penalty\ c\text{ is a conic with }\pi_{i}(c)=L_{i}\text{ for some lines }L_{i}\text{ and }i=1,2\right\}.

For the rest of this article, unless otherwise stated, any conic in
X
will be a
(1,1)
-conic.
We describe how to construct a double EPW quartic from
F(X)
.

We have that

I_{X,2}\coloneq H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{X}(2))\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus U_{1}\otimes\wedge^{2}U_{2},

which we think of as the space of quadrics in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus U_{1}\otimes\wedge^{2}U_{2})
containing
X
.
Define the following morphism

\psi\colon F(X)\to\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\wedge^{2}U_{1}\otimes U_{2}),\penalty\ c\mapsto H_{c}\coloneq\left\{\mathfrak{Q}\in I_{X,2}\penalty\ |\penalty\ X\cup\langle c\rangle\subset\mathfrak{Q}\right\},

where
\langle c\rangle
is the plane spanned by
c
.
Notice that this is well-defined, as
H_{c}
is a hyperplane in
I_{X,2}
, meaning it defines a point in the dual projective space
\mathbb{P}(I_{X,2}^{\vee})=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\wedge^{2}U_{1}\otimes U_{2})
.

Any
c\in F(X)
is contained in

c\subset D_{(L,M)}\coloneq\pi^{-1}(L\times M)

for the two lines
L
and
M
with
\pi_{1}(c)=L
and
\pi_{2}(c)=M
.
As a double cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
branched along a
(2,2)
-divisor, this is a degree 4 del Pezzo surface, and we have

D_{(L,M)}\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus L\otimes M)\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus U_{1}\otimes\wedge^{2}U_{2}),

by abuse of notation, where we also denote the two-dimensional subspaces of
U_{1}
and
\wedge^{2}U_{2}
defining
L
and
M
by the same letter.
As
c\subset D_{(L,M)}
, there is a unique quadric threefold
\tilde{Q}
containing
D_{(L,M)}

, i.e.

D_{(L,M)}\cup\langle c\rangle\subset\tilde{Q}\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus L\otimes M).

Any
\tilde{Q}
obtained in this manner can only be of rank 3 or 4, with the one defined by the general conic being of rank
4
.
There are two pencils of planes on quadric threefolds of rank
4
, which coincide for those of rank
3
.
Intersecting any plane contained in
\tilde{Q}
with
D_{(L,M)}
defines a
(1,1)
-conic in
X
.
The morphism
\psi
contracts exactly the conics obtained from the same
\tilde{Q}
.
The involution on
X
restricts to an involution on
D_{(L,M)}
, where it interchanges the two pencils of conics obtained from the two pencils of planes in
\tilde{Q}
.
Thus, the general fibre of
\psi
consists of two copies of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which are interchanged by the involution.
Denote the image of
\psi
by
D
, which is a singular Calabi–Yau fourfold and a quartic hypersurface in
C(\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}U_{1}\otimes\mathbb{P}(U_{2})))\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\wedge^{2}U_{1}\otimes U_{2})
.
Then,
\psi\colon F(X)\to D
factors through a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle

\alpha\colon F(X)\to Y,

where
Y
is a double EPW quartic and a double cover of
D
.
The double EPW quartic is a hyperkähler fourfold and is of
K3^{[2]}
-type.
The family of Verra fourfolds is
19
-dimensional, and so is the family of double EPW quartics.
Interchanging the pencils of conics contained in a
\tilde{Q}
defines an anti-symplectic involution
\iota
on
Y
.
For more details on
D
, see
Section

2.2
, where it is denoted

.

2.2.
Double EPW quartics via Lagrangian degeneracy loci and their two Lagrangian fibrations

2.3.
Double EPW quartics as moduli spaces of twisted sheaves on K3 surfaces

In this section we review the construction of double EPW quartics as moduli spaces of twisted sheaves on K3 surfaces.
While we will not use it in this article, we describe the construction for the sake of completeness.

Double EPW quartics were first realised as moduli spaces of twisted sheaves on
K3
surfaces in
[
IKKR17
, Sec. 4]
via lattice-theoretic methods by constructing an embedding of

into the Hodge structure associated to the twisted

surface and finding a vector with square
2
in the orthogonal complement.
Here, we follow the more geometric approach of
[

, Sec. 5]
.

Let

be a general Verra fourfold.
The two projections

are quadric fibrations, whose discriminant loci are smooth sextic curves
C_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Let
S_{i}
be the double covers of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
branched along
C_{i}
.
The quadric bundles

induce
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibrations on

, defining two Brauer classes

of order

.
We work with
\pi_{1}
and
S_{1}
, as the other case is analogous. For the remainder of this section, we denote
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a,b)=\pi_{1}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(a,b)
.

We know from
[
Kuz08
, Thm. 4.2]
that there is a semi-orthogonal decomposition

D^{b}(X)=\left\langle\Phi(D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})),\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1,0),\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1,0),\mathcal{O}_{X}(0,1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(1,1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2,1)\right\rangle,

where

is a fully faithful functor and

is the sheaf of even parts of the Clifford algebra associated to
\pi_{1}
, as in
[
Kuz08
]
.
Furthermore, it is shown in
[
Kuz10
, Lem. 4.2]
that the double cover

induces an equivalence

Let

be the inverse functor and let
\pr\colon D^{b}(X)\to\Phi(D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}))
be the projection functor.

Consider the map

where

is the polarisation on

, given by the composition of

with

It is shown in
[

, Thm. 5.1]
that this map is well-defined and it factors through

and induces an isomorphism

Furthermore, this can be done with both
K3
surfaces

and

associated to

, and hence we also have

.

3.
The Franchetta property

In this section we discuss the Franchetta property and prove that it holds for the family of double EPW quartics and cubes of Verra fourfolds.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\to B
be a family of smooth varieties over a smooth base
B
. Let
b\in B
be a point.
Following
[
FLV21
]
, we say that a cycle
\gamma\in\chow^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b})
is
generically defined
if it lies in the image of the pullback map from
\mathcal{X}
, i.e.

\gamma\in\im\left(\chow^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})\to\chow^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b})\right).

We denote the subring of generically defined cycles over
B
by
\gdch_{B}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b})
.

Notice that the definition of generically defined cycles depends on the family
\mathcal{X}\to B
.
It will usually be clear over which base we are working, so we will often omit the subscript
B
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\to B
be a family of smooth varieties over a smooth base
B
.
We say that the family
\mathcal{X}\to B
satisfies the Franchetta property if for any closed point, or equivalently for the very general point,
b\in B
, we have that the cycle class map restricted to the subring of generically defined cycles,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\gdch_{B}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b})\to H^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b},\mathbb{Q}),

is injective.

Beauville and Voisin proved in
[
BV04
]
the existence of a special zero-cycle on a
K3
surface
S
satisfying
D\cdot D^{\prime}=\deg(D\cdot D^{\prime})o_{S}
for any two divisors
D,D^{\prime}
and that
c_{2}(S)=\penalty\ 24o_{S}
.
The class of any point lying on a rational curve in
S
is
o_{S}
.
O’Grady noticed that this implies that the Beauville–Voisin class
o_{S}
is generically defined and asked in
[
OGr13
]
whether the Franchetta property holds for the family of
K3
surfaces.
This was extended to hyperkähler varieties in
[
FLV19
]
, and it was conjectured in
[
LV26
]
that the Generalised Franchetta conjecture holds.

Conjecture 3.3

(
[
LV26
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the moduli stack of a locally complete family of polarised hyperkähler varieties and let
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{F}
be the universal family.

(Franchetta Conjecture): The family
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{F}
satisfies the Franchetta property.

(Generalised Franchetta Conjecture): The family
\mathcal{X}^{n/\mathcal{F}}\to\mathcal{F}
satisfies the Franchetta property for any
n\geq 1
.

Since we work exclusively with varieties, we refer to
[
FLV19
]
for more details on the fact that the conjecture is stated in terms of stacks.

Remark 3.4
.

Unfortunately, what we call the Franchetta conjecture above is called the generalised Franchetta conjecture in
[
OGr13
]
,
[
PSY17
]
,
[
FLV19
]
, and
[
FLV21
]
.
We have decided to follow the convention used in
[
LV26
]
and distinguish between the two, as there is less evidence for what we call the generalised Franchetta conjecture and because it interacts differently with conjectures such as the Beauville–Voisin conjecture, see
Section

7
.

The Franchetta property was first proved for the family of
K3
surfaces of genera
g\leq\penalty\ 10
and
g=12,13,16,18,20
in
[
PSY17
]
, and more recently for

]
and

]
.
This has been extended to squares of

surfaces of genus

]
.
Furthermore, the Franchetta property is known for the Fano variety of lines on cubic fourfolds and its self-product
[
FLV19
]
, fourth powers of cubic fourfolds, and LLSS eightfolds

.
We can now add double EPW quartics and Verra fourfolds and their Hilbert schemes of
(1,1)
-conics to this list.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{U}
be the family of double EPW quartics. It satisfies the Franchetta property, meaning that for any point
b\in\mathcal{U}
, the cycle class map restricted to generically defined cycles,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\gdch^{\ast}(\mathcal{Y}_{b})\to H^{\ast}(\mathcal{Y}_{b},\mathbb{Q}),

is injective.

So does the family of Hilbert schemes of
(1,1)
-conics

Proof.

Remark 3.6

This implies that all generically defined cycles on

6.
A multiplicativity result implied by the Bloch–Beilinson conjecture

The aim of this section is to prove that the multiplication

7.
The Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture for double EPW quartics

The aim of this section is to prove the Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture for double EPW quartics.
The proof uses the results from
Section

4
and the fact that a double EPW quartic is a double cover of a quartic section in the cone
C(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})
and admits two Lagrangian fibrations, see
Theorem

2.1
for a summary and
Section

2.2
for more details.

7.1.
The Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture

In the work of Beauville and Voisin
[
BV04
]
it was proven that the product of two divisors
D
and
D^{\prime}
on a
K3
surface
S
is a multiple of the class of any point lying on a rational curve, which is now called the Beauville–Voisin class
o_{S}
.
Furthermore, it was shown that
c_{2}(S)=24o_{S}
.
This led Beauville to conjecture in
[
Bea07
]
that the subring generated by divisors
\langle\chow^{1}(T)\rangle\subset\penalty\ \chow^{\ast}(T)
should map injectively into cohomology for any hyperkähler variety
T
.
Voisin then extended this conjecture in
[
Voi08
]
to the subring generated by divisors and Chern classes.

Conjecture 7.1

(Beauville–Voisin)
.

Let
T
be a hyperkähler variety. The cycle class map restricted to the subring generated by divisors and Chern classes,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\left\langle\chow^{1}(T),c_{j}(T)\right\rangle\to H^{\ast}(T,\mathbb{Q}),

is injective.

This was proved for Hilbert schemes of
n
points on
K3
surfaces, where
n
is small, and Fano varieties of lines in cubic fourfolds
[
Voi08
]
, for generalised Kummer varieties
[
Fu15
]
, for double EPW sextics
[
LV21
,
Lat25a
]
, and partial results are known for LLSS eightfolds
[
LV26
]
.

Laterveer and Vial studied in
[
LV26
]
how the Beauville–Voisin conjecture interacts with the Franchetta conjecture and showed that the generalised Franchetta conjecture and Grothendieck’s Künneth standard conjecture imply the following, see
[
LV26
, Prop. 1.8]

Conjecture 7.2

(Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the moduli stack of a locally complete family of polarised hyperkähler varieties and
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{F}
its universal family.
Let
T
be any member of the universal family and
n
arbitrary.
Then the cycle class map restricted to the subring of
\chow^{\ast}(T^{n})
generated by
\gdch^{\ast}_{\mathcal{F}}(T^{n})
and divisors,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\left\langle\chow^{1}(T^{n}),\gdch^{\ast}(T^{n})\right\rangle\to H^{\ast}(T^{n},\mathbb{Q}),

is injective.

Let
Y
be a double EPW quartic and let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be the two divisors pulled back along the Lagrangian fibrations
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Then we know that the Franchetta property holds and that

\gdch^{\ast}(Y)=\langle h_{1},h_{2},c_{2}(Y),c_{4}(Y)\rangle,

meaning that the Beauville–Voisin conjecture and the Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture are equivalent.
In fact, the Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture for hyperkähler varieties of
K3^{[2]}
-type is equivalent to both the Beauville–Voisin conjecture and the Franchetta conjecture holding, as
\sym^{2}H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
.
For more details on the cohomology of hyperkähler varieties, see
[
GKLR22
]
.

Remark 7.3
.

The Beauville conjecture, which predicts that the subring generated by divisors maps injectively into cohomology, was proved for hyperkähler varieties admitting Lagrangian fibrations in
[

]
.
Combining this with

7.4
and
7.5
and the Franchetta property yields the Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture for double EPW quartics.
Our geometric approach allows for a stronger result, which we prove in the next section.

7.2.

We introduce some notation.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let

be a double EPW quartic.
Then the relations

We only sketch the proof of the first statement, as the others are analogous.

\displaystyle(I\cdot\pr_{1}^{\ast}a)_{\ast}(D^{3}-\lambda Dh^{2})

\displaystyle=h\cdot I_{\ast}(h\cdot(D^{3}-\lambda Dh^{2}))=0

\displaystyle=h^{2}\cdot I_{\ast}(D^{3}-\lambda Dh^{2})=0,

This is enough to prove the main result, the Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture for double EPW quartics.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let

be a double EPW quartic.
Then the Beauville–Voisin–Franchetta conjecture holds for

From

and

This must be of degree at most

Theorem A.5
.

Let
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
be a general double EPW sextic.
Then the multiplication

\chow^{2}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}\otimes\chow^{2}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}\to\chow_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}

is surjective.

Proof.

The proof of
Theorem

6.2
applies almost verbatim, after replacing
Proposition

6.1
by
Proposition

A.4
,
Lemma

5.1
by
[
Zha24
, Lemma 3.9]
, and noticing the following.
In the proof of
Theorem

6.2
, we used the Franchetta property to conclude that
\alpha_{\ast}(\Gamma\cdot h_{F})
is a multiple of
o_{Y}
.
As we do not know whether the family of double EPW sextics satisfies the Franchetta property, we need to work around this.
We know that

\alpha_{\ast}(\Gamma\cdot h_{F})=\alpha_{\ast}(E_{x}\cdot\Gamma^{\prime}\cdot h_{F}),

where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a generically defined divisor.
Since
\alpha
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle outside the locus of
\rho
- and
\sigma
-conics, we know that

\Gamma^{\prime}=\alpha^{\ast}d_{1}+a_{1}\xi+b_{2}[F^{\sigma}]\text{ and }h_{F}=\alpha^{\ast}d_{2}+a_{2}\xi+b_{2}[F^{\sigma}],

where
d_{1},d_{2}\in\chow^{1}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})
,
\xi
is the class of the relative hyperplane bundle of the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle and
[F^{\sigma}]
is the class of the locus of
\sigma
-conics.
Since
F^{\sigma}
is contracted to a point by
\alpha
, any multiple of
[F^{\sigma}]
vanishes after pushing forward.
Furthermore, the conics contained in the fibre of
\alpha
over a general point

are disjoint, meaning that
\xi\cdot E_{x}=0
.
Thus, we have

\alpha_{\ast}(\Gamma\cdot h_{F})=\alpha_{\ast}(E_{x})\cdot d_{1}\cdot d_{2}.

Now, given

, there exists a conic

with

and

.
We know that there exists

and

Thus,
\alpha_{\ast}(E_{x})
is
\iota

\alpha_{\ast}(E_{x})\cdot d_{1}\cdot d_{2}
is a multiple of

using the main theorem of
[

]
. With this, the rest of the proof goes through as in the case of double EPW quartics.
