On transposed Poisson conformal algebras
Lamei Yuan, Hao Fang

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to introduce the notion of (noncommutative) transposed Poisson conformal algebras, which serve as the conformal analogues of transposed Poisson algebras and admit a rich class of identities. We show that the tensor product of two transposed Poisson conformal algebras is also a transposed Poisson conformal algebra. Moreover, we establish a close relationship between transposed Poisson conformal algebras and Hom-Lie conformal algebras, and give the compatibility conditions between a Poisson conformal algebra and a transposed Poisson conformal algebra. In addition, we provide several constructions of transposed Poisson conformal algebras arising from related algebraic structures. Finally, a complete classification of compatible noncommutative transposed Poisson conformal algebraic structures over the Lie conformal algebra W ⁡ ( a , b ) W(a,b) is given.

1.
Introduction

Poisson algebras originally emerged from the study of Hamiltonian mechanics
[
1
,
29
]
and Poisson geometry
[
35
,
36
]
and have since appeared in various areas of mathematics and mathematical physics, including
Poisson manifolds
[
8
,
34
]
, algebraic geometry
[
7
,
31
]
, quantum groups
[
10
,
15
]
, quantization theory
[
20
,
25
]

[

,
13
]
.
A dual notion of the Poisson algebra, called the transposed Poisson algebra, was introduced by Bai et al.
[
3
]
by reversing the roles of the two bilinear operations in the Leibniz rule defining the Poisson algebra.
A transposed Poisson algebra defined in this way not only parallels the classical Poisson algebras at the algebraic, operadic and categorical levels, but also relates closely to several important algebraic structures such as Novikov-Poisson algebras, pre-Lie Poisson algebras and pre-Lie commutative algebras.

The notion of a Lie conformal algebra, introduced by Kac
[
22
]
, encodes an axiomatic description of the singular part of the operator product expansion of chiral fields in two-dimensional conformal field theory.
Closely related to vertex algebras and formal distribution Lie algebras
[
11
,
26
]
, Lie conformal algebras have crucial applications in other areas of algebra and integrable systems
[
40
,
41
]
. In particular, they provide useful tools in the study of associative algebras, infinite-dimensional Lie algebras satisfying the sole locality property
[
33
]
and Hamiltonian formalism in the theory of nonlinear evolution equations
[
5
]
.
The structure theory, representation theory and cohomology theory of finite Lie conformal algebras have been extensively investigated in
[
4
,
12
,
14
,
32
,
37
]
.
Significantly, associative conformal algebras naturally emerged from the study of the representation theory of Lie conformal algebras.

The notion of a Poisson conformal algebra was introduced by Kolesnikov
[
24
]
, which consists of a Lie conformal algebra and a commutative associative conformal algebra satisfying certain compatibility conditions.
It was shown in
[
24
,
27
]
that Poisson conformal algebras are closely connected to representations of Lie conformal algebras as well as to Poisson-Gel’fand-Dorfman algebras.
The concept of noncommutative Poisson conformal algebras was also studied in
[
27
]
, where their cohomology and deformation quantization were investigated.
As a consequence, it was proved that Poisson conformal algebras are the semi-classical limits of conformal formal deformations of commutative associative conformal algebras.
Furthermore, the notion of Poisson conformal bialgebras, characterized by Manin triples of Poisson conformal algebras, was introduced and investigated in
[
18
]
.

The study of conformal analogues of classical algebraic structures has attracted increasing attention in recent years
[
2
,
17
]
, extending classical results to the conformal setting and promoting the development of conformal algebra theory.
As previously mentioned, Poisson conformal algebras and Poisson conformal bialgebras can be regarded as the conformal analogues of Poisson algebras and Poisson bialgebras, respectively.
Moreover, a conformal analogue of
L
-dendriform algebras was presented in
[
16
]
with the notion of
L
-dendriform conformal algebras, which emerge naturally from the study of
\mathcal{O}
-operators on left-symmetric conformal algebras and solutions to the conformal
S
-equation.
Motivated by these results, it is natural to consider a conformal analogue of transposed Poisson algebras, namely transposed Poisson conformal algebras, which consist of a Lie conformal algebra and a commutative associative conformal algebra satisfying a transposed conformal Leibniz rule.

In Section

Then we show that the tensor product of two transposed Poisson conformal algebras is also a transposed Poisson conformal algebra.
Furthermore, we display a close relationship between transposed Poisson conformal algebras and Hom-Lie conformal algebras, and give the compatibility conditions between a Poisson conformal algebra and a transposed Poisson conformal algebra.
In Section
4
, we provide several constructions of transposed Poisson conformal algebras arising from related algebraic structures.
In Section

W(a,b)

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic notions and results concerning Lie conformal algebras and Poisson conformal algebras.

Definition 2.1

(
[
21
]
)
.

A
conformal algebra

(A,\circ_{\lambda})
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
A
endowed with a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map
A\otimes A\to A[\lambda]
, denoted by
a\otimes b\mapsto a\circ_{\lambda}b
, satisfying the following condition:

\displaystyle(\partial a)\circ_{\lambda}b=-\lambda a\circ_{\lambda}b,~~~a\circ_{\lambda}(\partial b)=(\partial+\lambda)a\circ_{\lambda}b.

(2.1)

A
derivation
of a conformal algebra
(A,\circ_{\lambda})
is a linear map
D:A\rightarrow A
such that
D\partial=\partial D
and
D(a\circ_{\lambda}b)=D(a)\circ_{\lambda}b+a\circ_{\lambda}D(b)
for all
a,b~\in A
.
For instance,
D=\partial
is a derivation of every conformal algebra.

Definition 2.2

(
[
22
]
)
.

An

(A,\circ_{\lambda})
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
A

\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map
A\otimes A\to A[\lambda]

a\otimes b\mapsto a\circ_{\lambda}b

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}\left(b\circ_{\mu}c\right)=\left(a\circ_{\lambda}b\right)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c,\quad\forall~a,b,c\in{A}.

(2.2)

An associative conformal algebra
(A,\circ_{\lambda})
is called

, if it satisfies

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}b=b\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}a,\quad\forall~a,b\in{A}.

(2.3)

Remark 2.3

(

The following properties always hold in an associative conformal algebra

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{-\partial-\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{-\partial-\mu}c,

(2.4)

\displaystyle a\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{-\partial-\mu}b)\circ_{-\partial+\mu-\lambda}c,

(2.5)

\displaystyle a\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}(b\circ_{-\partial-\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a_{-\partial+\mu-\lambda}b)\circ_{-\partial-\mu}c.

(2.6)

Remark 2.4

(

In every commutative associative conformal algebra

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}\left(b\circ_{\mu}c\right)=b\circ_{\mu}\left(a\circ_{\lambda}c\right),\quad\forall~a,b,c\in{A}.

(2.7)

(

Definition 2.11

(
[

]
)
.

A
Poisson conformal algebra
(PCA) is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
A
endowed with two
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear maps
A\otimes A\rightarrow A[\lambda]
, denoted by
a\otimes b\mapsto a\circ_{\lambda}b
and

, respectively, such that
(A,\circ_{\lambda})
is a commutative associative conformal algebra,
(A,[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
is a Lie conformal algebra and the following

holds:

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}c)]=[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c+b\circ_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c],\quad\forall~a,b,c\in A.

(2.17)

If the associative

(A,\circ_{\lambda},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
is noncommutative, we call it a

.

Remark 2.12

(
[
24
]
)
.

Relation (
2.17
) is equivalent to

[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\mu}c]=b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c]+a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c],\quad\forall~a,b,c\in A.

(2.18)

Definition 2.13

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
L
be a vector space equipped with two bilinear operations

\cdot,\,[~,~]:L\otimes L\rightarrow L.

The triple
(L,\cdot,[~,~])
is called a
transposed Poisson algebra
, if
(L,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra and
(L,[~,~])
is a Lie algebra that satisfy the following compatibility condition

\displaystyle 2z\cdot[x,y]=[z\cdot x,y]+[x,z\cdot y],\quad\forall~x,y,z\in L.

(2.19)

Eq. (
2.19
) is called the transposed Leibniz rule because the roles played by the two binary operations in the Leibniz rule in a Poisson algebra are switched. Further, the resulting operation is rescaled by introducing a factor 2 on the left hand side.

3.
Transposed Poisson conformal algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of a transposed Poisson conformal algebra, which is a conformal analogue of the transposed Poisson algebra. Then we present some identities and properties of transposed Poisson conformal algebras.

3.1.
Definition of transposed Poisson conformal algebras

Definition 3.1
.

A
transposed Poisson conformal algebra
(TPCA) is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{L}
endowed with two
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear maps

\circ_{\lambda},\,[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}]:\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}[\lambda],

such that
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda})
is a commutative associative conformal algebra,
(\mathcal{L},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
is a Lie conformal algebra and the following
transposed conformal Leibniz rule
holds:

\displaystyle 2(a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c])=[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c]+[b_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)],\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

(3.1)

If the associative
\lambda
-multiplication in the transposed Poisson conformal algebra
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
is noncommutative, we call it a
noncommutative TPCA
.

Remark 3.2
.

The relation (
3.1
) can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle 2([a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c)=[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]-[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)],\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

(3.2)

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
be a (noncommutative) transposed Poisson conformal algebra.
Define the
n
-th products on the (commutative) associative conformal algebra
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda})
and the Lie conformal algebra
(\mathcal{L},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, respectively, by

a\circ_{\lambda}b=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\lambda^{(n)}(a_{(n)}b),\quad[a_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\lambda^{(n)}(a_{[n]}b),

where
\lambda^{(n)}=\lambda^{n}/{n!}
and
a,b\in\mathcal{L}
. Then, the notions of (commutative) associative conformal algebra and Lie conformal algebra can be equivalently expressed in terms of their respective
n
-th products.
Moreover, the transposed conformal Leibniz rule of (noncommutative) transposed Poisson conformal algebras can be rewritten in terms of
n
-th products of associative conformal algebras and Lie conformal algebras as follows:

2(a_{(n)}(b_{[m]}c))=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(a_{(j)}b)_{[n+m-j]}c+b_{{[m]}}(a_{(n)}c),\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

(3.3)

Example 3.3
.

Let
(A,\circ,[\cdot,\cdot])
be an ordinary transposed Poisson algebra. Then
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes A
equipped with

a\circ_{\lambda}b=a\circ b,\quad[{a_{\lambda}b}]=[a,b],\quad\forall~a,b\in A

is a transposed Poisson conformal algebra.

Example 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{L}_{1},\circ_{1\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]_{1},\partial_{1})
and
(\mathcal{L}_{2},\circ_{2\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]_{2},\partial_{2})
be two TPCAs. For all
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
,
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{2},
define

\displaystyle(x_{1}+x_{2})\circ_{\lambda}(y_{1}+y_{2})

\displaystyle=x_{1}\circ_{1\lambda}y_{1}+x_{2}\circ_{2\lambda}y_{2},

\displaystyle[(x_{1}+x_{2})_{\lambda}(y_{1}+y_{2})]

\displaystyle=[{x_{1}}_{\lambda}y_{1}]_{1}+[{x_{2}}_{\lambda}y_{2}]_{2}.

Then
(\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\partial=\partial_{1}\oplus\partial_{2})
is a TPCA.

3.2.
Identities in transposed Poisson conformal algebras

There is a rich class of identities for transposed Poisson conformal algebras.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
be a transposed Poisson conformal algebra. Then the following identities hold:

\displaystyle x\circ_{\lambda}[y_{\mu}z]+y\circ_{\mu}[z_{-\partial-\lambda}x]+z\circ_{-\partial-\lambda-\mu}[x_{\lambda}y]=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle[x_{\lambda}y]\circ_{\mu}z+[y_{\mu-\lambda}z]\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}x+[z_{-\partial-\lambda}x]\circ_{-\partial-\mu+\lambda}y=0,

(3.5)

\displaystyle[(h\circ_{\lambda}[x_{\gamma}y])_{\lambda+\mu}z]+[(h\circ_{\lambda}[y_{\mu-\gamma}z])_{-\partial-\gamma}x]+[(h\circ_{\lambda}[z_{-\partial-\gamma}x])_{-\partial-\mu+\gamma}y]=0,

(3.6)

\displaystyle[[x_{\gamma}y]_{\mu}(h\circ_{\lambda}z)]+[[y_{\mu-\gamma}z]_{-\partial-\lambda-\gamma}(h\circ_{\lambda}x)]+[[z_{-\partial-\gamma}x]_{-\partial-\lambda-\mu+\gamma}(h\circ_{\lambda}y)]=0,

(3.7)

\displaystyle[h_{\lambda}x]\circ_{\lambda+\gamma}[y_{\mu-\gamma}z]+[h_{\lambda}y]\circ_{\lambda+\mu-\gamma}[z_{-\partial-\gamma}x]+[h_{\lambda}z]\circ_{-\partial-\mu}[x_{\gamma}y]=0,

\displaystyle[(u\circ_{\lambda}x)_{\mu}(v\circ_{\gamma}y)]+[(v\circ_{\gamma}x)_{\gamma+\mu-\lambda}(u\circ_{\lambda}y)]=2\left(u\circ_{\lambda}v\right)\circ_{\lambda+\gamma}[x_{\mu-\lambda}y],

for all

3.3.
Tensor products of transposed Poisson conformal algebras

Now we define the tensor product of transposed Poisson conformal algebras as follows:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{L}_{1},\circ^{1}_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1})

(\mathcal{L}_{2},\circ^{2}_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})

\lambda

(a)

3.4.
Relation with Hom-Lie conformal algebras

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a transposed Poisson conformal algebra. For any
h\in\mathcal{L}
, define a linear map

\alpha_{h}
:
\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
by

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}(x):=(h\circ_{\lambda}x)|_{\lambda=0}=h\circ_{(0)}x,\quad\forall~x\in\mathcal{L}.

Then
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\alpha_{h})
is a Hom-Lie conformal algebra.

Proof.

It is well known that
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Lie conformal algebra satisfying the axioms of conformal sesquilinearity and skew-symmetry. For all
x\in\mathcal{L}
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}(\partial x)=(h\circ_{\lambda}\partial x)|_{\lambda=0}=(\partial+\lambda)(h\circ_{\lambda}x)|_{\lambda=0}=\partial(h\circ_{(0)}x)=\partial(\alpha_{h}x).

Hence,
\alpha_{h}\partial=\partial\alpha_{h}
. By taking
\gamma:=\lambda,~\mu:=\lambda+\mu,~\lambda:=0
in Eq. (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle[[x_{\lambda}y]_{\lambda+\mu}(h\circ_{(0)}z)]+[[y_{\mu}z]_{-\partial-\lambda}(h\circ_{(0)}x)]+[[z_{-\partial-\lambda}x]_{-\partial-\mu}(h\circ_{(0)}y)]=0.

That is,

\displaystyle[[x_{\lambda}y]_{\lambda+\mu}\alpha_{h}(z)]+[\alpha_{h}(y)_{\mu}[x_{\lambda}z]]=[\alpha_{h}(x)_{\lambda}[y_{\mu}z]],

which satisfies the axiom of Hom-Jacobi identity. Therefore,
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\alpha_{h})
is a Hom-Lie conformal algebra.
∎

3.5.
Compatibility conditions between PCA and TPCA

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda})
be a commutative associative conformal algebra and
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a Lie conformal algebra.
Then
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is both a Poisson conformal algebra and a transposed Poisson conformal algebra if and only if

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]=[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]\circ_{\mu}c=[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c]=0,\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

Proof.

The “if” part can be easily verified. For the “only if” part, let
a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}
. By Eq. (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle 2[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c=[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]-[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)].

By Eq. (
2.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]=[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c+b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c],\quad[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]\circ_{\mu}c+a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c].

Thus, we have

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c+a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]-b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c]+[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]\circ_{\mu}c=0.

By Eq. (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c+a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]-b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c]=0.

Hence,
[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]\circ_{\mu}c=0

3.4
), we get
a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]=b\circ_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]
. By Eq. (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle 2a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]=[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c]+[b_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)].

By Eqs. (
2.17
) and (

\displaystyle[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c]=b\circ_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]+a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c],\quad[b_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=[b_{\mu}a]\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c+a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c].

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a transposed Poisson conformal algebra. For any
h\in\mathcal{L}

[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h}
on
\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle[x_{\lambda}y]^{h}:=(h\circ_{\mu}[x_{\lambda}y])|_{\mu=0}=h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y],\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathcal{L}.

Then
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h})
is a transposed Poisson conformal algebra.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z,h\in\mathcal{L}
. We first show that
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h})
is a Lie conformal algebra.

(a)
Conformal sesquilinearity:

\displaystyle[(\partial x)_{\lambda}y]^{h}=h\circ_{(0)}[(\partial x)_{\lambda}y]=h\circ_{(0)}(-\lambda[x_{\lambda}y])=-\lambda(h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y])=-\lambda[x_{\lambda}y]^{h}.

(b)
Skew-symmetry:

\displaystyle[x_{\lambda}y]^{h}=h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y]=h\circ_{(0)}(-[y_{-\partial-\lambda}x])=-h\circ_{(0)}[y_{-\partial-\lambda}x]=-[y_{-\partial-\lambda}x]^{h}.

(c)

By taking
\gamma:=\lambda,~\mu:=\lambda+\mu,~\lambda:=0
in Eq. (
3.6

\displaystyle[(h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y])_{\lambda+\mu}z]+[(h\circ_{(0)}[y_{\mu}z])_{-\partial-\lambda}x]+[(h\circ_{(0)}[z_{-\partial-\lambda}x])_{-\partial-\mu}y]=0.

Thus, we have

h\circ_{(0)}([(h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y])_{\lambda+\mu}z]+[(h\circ_{(0)}[y_{\mu}z])_{-\partial-\lambda}x]+[(h\circ_{(0)}[z_{-\partial-\lambda}x])_{-\partial-\mu}y])=0.

That is,

\displaystyle[{[x_{\lambda}y]^{h}}_{\lambda+\mu}z]^{h}+[{[y_{\mu}z]^{h}}_{-\partial-\lambda}x]^{h}+[{[z_{-\partial-\lambda}x]^{h}}_{-\partial-\mu}y]^{h}=0.

Next we show that transposed conformal Leibniz rule holds, by Eq. (
3.2

\displaystyle 2([x_{\lambda}y]^{h}\circ_{\mu}z)=2((h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y])\circ_{\mu}z)=2(h\circ_{(0)}([x_{\lambda}y]\circ_{\mu}z))=h\circ_{(0)}(2([x_{\lambda}y]\circ_{\mu}z))

\displaystyle=h\circ_{(0)}([x_{{\lambda}}(y\circ_{\mu-\lambda}z)]-[y_{\mu-\lambda}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)])=h\circ_{(0)}[x_{{\lambda}}(y\circ_{\mu-\lambda}z)]-h\circ_{(0)}[y_{\mu-\lambda}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)]

\displaystyle=[x_{{\lambda}}(y\circ_{\mu-\lambda}z)]^{h}-[y_{\mu-\lambda}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)]^{h}.

Therefore,
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h})
is a transposed Poisson conformal algebra.

Definition 4.2
.

(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},\ast_{\lambda})

\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{N}

\mathbb{C}

\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}[\lambda]

(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda})
is a commutative associative conformal algebra,
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c=a\circ_{\lambda}(b\ast_{\mu}c),

(4.1)

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}c)=(b\ast_{\mu}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c).

(4.2)

Remark 4.3
.

In analogy with Remark
2.3
, the following properties always hold in a Novikov-Poisson conformal algebra
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},\ast_{\lambda})

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(b\ast_{-\partial-\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\ast_{-\partial-\mu}c,

\displaystyle a\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}(b\ast_{\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{-\partial-\mu}b)\ast_{-\partial+\mu-\lambda}c,

\displaystyle a\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}(b\ast_{-\partial-\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{-\partial+\mu-\lambda}b)\ast_{-\partial-\mu}c.

Taking the commutator in a Novikov-Poisson conformal algebra, we obtain

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},\ast_{\lambda})
be a Novikov-Poisson conformal algebra. Define

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]=a\ast_{\lambda}b-b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a,\quad\forall~a,b\in\mathcal{N}.

(4.3)

Then
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a transposed Poisson conformal algebra.

Proof.

It is well known that
(\mathcal{N},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Lie conformal algebra (see Proposition
2.10
). For all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{N}
, we have

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c=(a\ast_{\lambda}b-b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c=(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\mu}c-(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c,

\displaystyle[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]=a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)-(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a,

\displaystyle[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)-(a\circ_{\lambda}c)\ast_{-\partial-\mu+\lambda}b.

By Remark
4.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\mu}c=c\circ_{-\partial-\mu}(a\ast_{\lambda}b)=(c\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\ast_{-\partial+\lambda-\mu}b=(a\circ_{\lambda}c)\ast_{-\partial-\mu+\lambda}b,

\displaystyle(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c=c\circ_{-\partial-\mu}(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)=(c\circ_{-\partial+\lambda-\mu}b)\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a=(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]-[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)-b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)+[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c.

By taking
\lambda:=\lambda,~\mu:=\mu-\lambda
in Eq. (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)=(b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c).

Since
(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c=(b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c
, it follows further that

(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\mu}c-(b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c=a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)-b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)=[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c.

Combining the above equalities, we arrive at

\displaystyle[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]-[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=2[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c,

it follows that relation (
3.2
) holds. Hence the conclusion holds.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda})
be a commutative associative conformal algebra and
D
be a derivation. Define a binary operation
\ast_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{N}
by

\displaystyle a\ast_{\lambda}b=a\circ_{\lambda}D(b),\quad\forall~a,b\in\mathcal{N}.

(4.4)

Then the following conclusions hold:

(1)
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a Novikov conformal algebra.

(2)
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},\ast_{\lambda})
is a Novikov-Poisson conformal algebra.

Proof.

(1) Let
a,b,c\in\mathcal{N}
. (a) We first show that
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a conformal algebra.

\displaystyle(\partial a)\ast_{\lambda}b=(\partial a)\circ_{\lambda}D(b)=-\lambda(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))=-\lambda(a\ast_{\lambda}b),

\displaystyle a\ast_{\lambda}(\partial b)=a\circ_{\lambda}D(\partial b)=a\circ_{\lambda}(\partial D(b))=(\partial+\lambda)(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))=(\partial+\lambda)(a\ast_{\lambda}b).

(b) Next we show that
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a left-symmetric conformal algebra. Since

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\ast_{\mu}c)=(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))\circ_{\lambda+\mu}D(c)-a\circ_{\lambda}D(b\circ_{\mu}D(c))

\displaystyle=a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}D(c))-a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}D(c))-a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D^{2}(c))=-a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D^{2}(c)),

we similarly have

\displaystyle(b\ast_{\mu}a)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu}(a\ast_{\lambda}c)=-b\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}D^{2}(c)).

By Remark
2.4
, we obtain
a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D^{2}(c))=b\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}D^{2}(c)).
Thus, we get

(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\ast_{\mu}c)=(b\ast_{\mu}a)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu}(a\ast_{\lambda}c).

(c) Finally, we show that
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a Novikov conformal algebra. Since

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}c)\ast_{-\mu-\partial}b

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{\lambda}D(c))\circ_{-\mu-\partial}D(b)=D(b)\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}D(c))

\displaystyle=a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}D(c))=(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))\circ_{\lambda+\mu}D(c)=(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c.

Hence,
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a Novikov conformal algebra.

(2) Let
a,b,c\in\mathcal{N}
. (d) Relation (
4.1
) holds. Since

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c=(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}D(c)=a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D(c))=a\circ_{\lambda}(b\ast_{\mu}c).

(e) Relation (
4.2
) holds. Since

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}c)=(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\circ_{\lambda}D(b\circ_{\mu}c)

\displaystyle=a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}c)-a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}c)-a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D(c))=-a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D(c)),

we similarly have

(b\ast_{\mu}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)=-b\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}D(c)).

By Remark
2.4
, we get
a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D(c))=b\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}D(c))
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}c)=(b\ast_{\mu}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c).

Altogether,
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},\ast_{\lambda})
is a Novikov-Poisson conformal algebra.
∎
