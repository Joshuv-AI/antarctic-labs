1.
Introduction

1.1.
The perfect-cuboid problem

An
Euler-brick
is a rectangular parallelepiped with integer edges
X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{N}
and integer face diagonals

\sqrt{X^{2}+Y^{2}},\quad\sqrt{X^{2}+Z^{2}},\quad\sqrt{Y^{2}+Z^{2}}\in\mathbb{N}.

A
perfect cuboid
(or
perfect Euler-brick
) is an Euler-brick with additionally rational space diagonal
\sqrt{X^{2}+Y^{2}+Z^{2}}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Cuboid Conjecture; “Conjecture B”)
.

No perfect cuboid exists in
\mathbb{N}^{3}
.

The conjecture has been open since at least the work of Saunderson (1740) and remains a long-standing problem in elementary number theory. Computational searches have ruled out perfect cuboids with the smallest edge below
5\cdot 10^{11}
and the odd edge below
2.5\cdot 10^{13}
(
[
14
]
,
[
11
]
).

1.2.
Master tuples

Following the standard parametrization, we associate to a 4-tuple
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}
with
\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(m,n)=1
,
a>b
,
m>n
, and
a-b,m-n
odd, the quantities

\displaystyle U_{1}

\displaystyle=a^{2}-b^{2},

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=2ab,

\displaystyle W_{1}

\displaystyle=a^{2}+b^{2},

\displaystyle U_{2}

\displaystyle=m^{2}-n^{2},

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=2mn,

\displaystyle W_{2}

\displaystyle=m^{2}+n^{2}.

The associated Euler-brick (when defined) has edges

X=U_{1}U_{2},\quad Y=V_{1}U_{2},\quad Z=U_{1}V_{2}.

We call
(a,b,m,n)
a
master tuple
if the quantity

M:=(V_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}

is a perfect square, equivalently
Y^{2}+Z^{2}
is a square. The remaining face-diagonal squares
X^{2}+Y^{2}=(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}
and
X^{2}+Z^{2}=(U_{1}W_{2})^{2}
are squares automatically.

We further introduce the polynomial

(1)

f_{1}:=(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}[a,b,m,n],

which encodes the perfect-cuboid condition: writing
g:=\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})
, the
edges of the (primitive) brick associated with the master tuple are

X=U_{1}U_{2}/g
,
Y=V_{1}U_{2}/g
,
Z=U_{1}V_{2}/g
, so a direct computation
gives

g^{2}\cdot(X^{2}+Y^{2}+Z^{2})\;=\;(U_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(V_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}\;=\;W_{1}^{2}U_{2}^{2}+U_{1}^{2}V_{2}^{2}\;=\;f_{1}.

Hence
X^{2}+Y^{2}+Z^{2}
is a rational square iff
f_{1}
is a perfect square in

\mathbb{Z}
. Throughout,
f_{1}
denotes the polynomial of (
1
); the
scaled value attached to a specific brick is
f_{1}/g^{2}
.

2.
Structural results on master tuples

2.1.
The
g_{+}\cdot g_{-}
structure theorem

Theorem 2.1

(
g_{+}\cdot g_{-}
structure)
.

Let
(a,b,m,n)
be a master tuple. Set

A=am+bn,\quad B=an+bm,\quad C=am-bn,\quad D=an-bm,

and
g_{+}=\gcd(A,B)
,
g_{-}=\gcd(|C|,|D|)
. Then

g_{+}\cdot g_{-}=\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})=:g_{\mathrm{scale}},\quad\gcd(g_{+},g_{-})=1.

In particular
g_{\pm}\mid U_{1}
,
g_{\pm}\mid U_{2}
, and consequently
g_{\pm}^{2}\mid f_{1}
.

Proof.

Step 1:
g_{\pm}\mid\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})
.
The linear combinations

mA-nB=aU_{2},\quad nA-mB=-bU_{2},\quad aA-bB=mU_{1},\quad bA-aB=-nU_{1}

show
g_{+}\mid aU_{2},bU_{2},mU_{1},nU_{1}
. Since
\gcd(a,b)=1
this gives
g_{+}\mid U_{2}
, and since
\gcd(m,n)=1
also
g_{+}\mid U_{1}
. The analogous combinations

mC-nD=aU_{2},\quad nC-mD=bU_{2},\quad aC+bD=mU_{1},\quad bC+aD=nU_{1}

yield
g_{-}\mid\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})
.

Step 2:
\gcd(g_{+},g_{-})=1
.
Since
a-b
and
m-n
are odd, exactly one of each pair
\{am+bn,\,an+bm\}
and
\{am-bn,\,an-bm\}
is odd, so both
g_{+}
and
g_{-}
are odd. Suppose an odd prime
p
divides both. Then
p\mid A,B,C,D
, hence
p\mid A\pm C=2am
and
2bn
, and
p\mid B\pm D=2an
and
2bm
. By oddness of
p
,
p
divides
am,an,bm,bn
. Then
\gcd(m,n)=1
forces
p\mid a
and
p\mid b
, contradicting
\gcd(a,b)=1
.

Step 3:
g_{+}g_{-}=\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})
.
The forward direction follows from Steps 1–2. For the reverse, let
p
be an odd prime with
p^{e}\,\|\,\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})
. Since
\gcd(a,b)=1
, the entire
p
-part of
U_{1}=(a-b)(a+b)
lies in exactly one factor, so
a\equiv\varepsilon b\pmod{p^{e}}
for some
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
; analogously
m\equiv\delta n\pmod{p^{e}}
. Reducing the four expressions mod
p^{e}
:

•

If
\varepsilon=\delta
:
am-bn\equiv\delta bn-bn=(\varepsilon\delta-1)bn\equiv 0
and
an-bm\equiv 0
, so
p^{e}\mid C,D
, hence
p^{e}\mid g_{-}
.

•

If
\varepsilon=-\delta
:
am+bn\equiv-\delta bn+bn=(\varepsilon\delta+1)bn\equiv 0
and
an+bm\equiv 0
, so
p^{e}\mid A,B
, hence
p^{e}\mid g_{+}
.

Since
\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})
is odd (both factors are odd), the product over all such primes gives
\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})\mid g_{+}g_{-}
.

Step 4:
g_{\pm}^{2}\mid f_{1}
.
From Step 1,
g_{+}\mid U_{2}\Rightarrow g_{+}\mid W_{1}U_{2}
, and
g_{+}\mid U_{1}\Rightarrow g_{+}\mid U_{1}V_{2}
. Therefore
g_{+}^{2}\mid(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}=f_{1}
. Same for
g_{-}
.
∎

2.2.
The polynomial factorization
f_{1}=L\cdot R

Theorem 2.2

(Polynomial factorization)
.

With
f_{1}=(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}
from (
1
), define

L:=W_{1}W_{2}-V_{1}V_{2},\qquad R:=W_{1}W_{2}+V_{1}V_{2}.

Then, as polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[a,b,m,n]
,
f_{1}=L\cdot R
, and each factor admits a Brahmagupta representation as a sum of two squares:

L=(am-bn)^{2}+(an-bm)^{2},\qquad R=(am+bn)^{2}+(an+bm)^{2}.

The factor
L
is divisible by
g_{-}^{2}
and
R
by
g_{+}^{2}
(Corollary
2.3
); the corresponding
primitive
sums of two squares are
L/g_{-}^{2}
and
R/g_{+}^{2}
.

Proof.

By the difference-of-squares identity,

L\cdot R=(W_{1}W_{2})^{2}-(V_{1}V_{2})^{2}.

Using
W_{i}^{2}=U_{i}^{2}+V_{i}^{2}
and expanding both this expression and
f_{1}=(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}
as polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[a,b,m,n]
, one verifies the identity

(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}=(W_{1}W_{2})^{2}-(V_{1}V_{2})^{2}.

The Brahmagupta forms follow by direct expansion: with
A=am+bn,B=an+bm,C=am-bn,D=an-bm
,

\displaystyle A^{2}+B^{2}

\displaystyle=(am+bn)^{2}+(an+bm)^{2}=(a^{2}+b^{2})(m^{2}+n^{2})+2abmn\cdot 2=W_{1}W_{2}+V_{1}V_{2}=R,

\displaystyle C^{2}+D^{2}

\displaystyle=(am-bn)^{2}+(an-bm)^{2}=(a^{2}+b^{2})(m^{2}+n^{2})-2abmn\cdot 2=W_{1}W_{2}-V_{1}V_{2}=L.

Both
L
and
R
are positive (AM–GM). The (potentially nonprimitive) sum-of-two-squares structure is sharpened by Corollary
2.3
below.
∎

Corollary 2.3

(Refined divisibility)
.

For every
(a,b,m,n)
in the universe,

g_{+}^{2}\mid R,\qquad g_{-}^{2}\mid L.

That is, the two Gaussian-style gcds live on different sides of the factorisation.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
,
g_{+}\mid A,B
, so
g_{+}^{2}\mid A^{2}+B^{2}=R
. Analogously
g_{-}^{2}\mid C^{2}+D^{2}=L
.
∎

2.3.
Reduction to master tuples

Theorem 2.4

(Reduction theorem)
.

Let
(X,Y,Z)
be a primitive Euler-brick with
X
designated as the odd edge (forced uniquely up to permutation, since exactly one edge is odd). Then there is a unique master tuple
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}
with
a>b>0
,
m>n>0
,
\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(m,n)=1
, and
a-b,m-n
odd, such that

X=\frac{U_{1}U_{2}}{g},\quad Y=\frac{V_{1}U_{2}}{g},\quad Z=\frac{U_{1}V_{2}}{g},\quad g=\gcd(U_{1},U_{2}).

Proof.

Existence.
Set
d=\gcd(X,Y)
,
e=\gcd(X,Z)
. Both are odd (as
X
is odd and
Y,Z
are even). The triples

(X/d,Y/d,f_{3}/d)
and
(X/e,Z/e,f_{2}/e)
are primitive Pythagorean. By the standard Euclid parametrization
[
16
, §1]
, there exist
unique
coprime
a>b>0
with
a-b
odd, and unique coprime
m>n>0
with
m-n
odd, such that

X/d=a^{2}-b^{2}=:U_{1},\quad Y/d=2ab=:V_{1},\qquad X/e=m^{2}-n^{2}=:U_{2},\quad Z/e=2mn=:V_{2}.

Key lemma:

\gcd(d,e)=1
. Indeed, any common prime divisor of
d,e
would divide
X
,
Y
, and
Z
, contradicting primitivity. From
X=d\cdot U_{1}=e\cdot U_{2}
and
\gcd(d,e)=1
, an elementary argument gives
d=U_{2}/g
and
e=U_{1}/g
with
g:=\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})
.

Uniqueness.
Given the assignment of
X
as the odd edge and the labelling of
(Y,Z)
, the pair
(a,b)
is uniquely determined by the primitive Pythagorean triple
(X/d,Y/d)
, and likewise
(m,n)
by
(X/e,Z/e)
— both via the bijection between primitive Pythagorean triples and pairs of coprime integers of opposite parity with the larger first.

Master-tuple condition.

V_{1}U_{2}=g\cdot Y
and
U_{1}V_{2}=g\cdot Z
, so

M=(V_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}=g^{2}(Y^{2}+Z^{2}),

and since
(X,Y,Z)
is an Euler-brick,
Y^{2}+Z^{2}=h^{2}
for some integer
h
, giving
M=(gh)^{2}
, a perfect square.
∎

2.4.
The scaling argument

Lemma 2.5

(Scaling)
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the brick
k\cdot(X,Y,Z)
is a perfect cuboid if and only if
(X,Y,Z)
is.

Proof.

The four square conditions
X^{2}+Y^{2}=\square
,
X^{2}+Z^{2}=\square
,
Y^{2}+Z^{2}=\square
,
X^{2}+Y^{2}+Z^{2}=\square
scale by
k^{2}
, which is itself a square.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds globally if and only if it holds on the family of primitive master-tuple-derived bricks.

3.1.
Explicit form
H_{m,n}

For a master tuple
(a,b,m,n)
, set
t=a/b\in\mathbb{Q}
. The conditions
M=\square
(master tuple) and
X^{2}+Y^{2}+Z^{2}\in\mathbb{Q}^{*2}
(perfect cuboid), the latter equivalent by (
1
) to
f_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}^{*2}
, translate, after taking
v
to be the product of the two square-roots, into the genus-
3
hyperelliptic curve

H_{m,n}:\quad v^{2}=P(t^{2})\cdot Q(t^{2}),

with

\displaystyle P(s)

\displaystyle=V_{2}^{2}s^{2}+(4U_{2}^{2}-2V_{2}^{2})s+V_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle Q(s)

\displaystyle=W_{2}^{2}s^{2}+2(U_{2}^{2}-V_{2}^{2})s+W_{2}^{2}.

The product
P(t^{2})Q(t^{2})
is a degree-
8
palindromic polynomial in
t
.
The discriminant identities

\disc(P)=16\,U_{2}^{2}\,(U_{2}^{2}-V_{2}^{2}),\qquad\disc(Q)=-16\,U_{2}^{2}\,V_{2}^{2},\qquad\Res(P,Q)=256\,U_{2}^{4}\,V_{2}^{4},

all of which are nonzero for
m>n>0
(since
U_{2},V_{2}\neq 0
and

U_{2}^{2}-V_{2}^{2}=(m^{2}-n^{2})^{2}-4m^{2}n^{2}
has no rational roots), show that

P(t^{2})Q(t^{2})
is separable. Hence
H_{m,n}
is a smooth hyperelliptic curve
of genus
g=(8-2)/2=3
.

Remark 3.1
.

The curve
H_{m,n}
is the
(m,n)
-explicit form of
C_{A}:w^{2}=\lambda^{8}+A\lambda^{4}+1
from
[
13

. The two are related by an explicit substitution
\lambda=\kappa(m,n)\cdot t
together with rescaling of

A
of
[

]
can be read off as a rational function of
(m,n)
. We retain the
(m,n)
-form because it is more directly tied to our database of master tuples.

3.2.
The Jacobian decomposition (recall)

By
[
13
, Proposition 4.1]
, the curve
H_{m,n}
carries the Klein-four involution group generated by

\sigma_{1}:(t,v)\mapsto(-t,v),\qquad\sigma_{2}:(t,v)\mapsto(1/t,\,v/t^{4}),

and the Jacobian decomposes (up to
\mathbb{Q}
-isogeny) as

\operatorname{Jac}(H_{m,n})\sim E_{PQ}\times E_{uV}\times E_{3}.

The three quotient curves have the following explicit
(m,n)
-form, obtained by writing the
\sigma
-invariants in terms of
u=t+1/t
,
w=t-1/t
, and
s=t^{2}
:

\displaystyle E_{PQ}:\quad

\displaystyle Y^{2}=P(s)\cdot Q(s)

\displaystyle(s=t^{2}),

\displaystyle E_{uV}:\quad

\displaystyle V^{2}=(V_{2}^{2}u^{2}+4(U_{2}^{2}-V_{2}^{2}))\cdot(W_{2}^{2}u^{2}-4V_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle(u=t+1/t),

\displaystyle E_{3}:\quad

\displaystyle V^{2}=(V_{2}^{2}w^{2}+4U_{2}^{2})\cdot(W_{2}^{2}w^{2}+4U_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle(w=t-1/t).

In the notation of
[
13
, §4.1]
,
E_{PQ}=E_{A}
,
E_{uV}=E_{A}^{\prime}
, and
E_{3}=E_{A}^{\prime\prime}
. Each quotient has genus
1
by Riemann–Hurwitz applied to the degree-
2
cover
H_{m,n}\to H_{m,n}/\langle\sigma\rangle
(
[
13
, §4]
). The isogeny decomposition is the Kani–Rosen theorem
[
9
]
.

Corollary 3.2

(Rank additivity)
.

For each master-tuple fiber
(m,n)
,

\operatorname{rk}(\operatorname{Jac}(H_{m,n})(\mathbb{Q}))=\operatorname{rk}(E_{PQ}(\mathbb{Q}))+\operatorname{rk}(E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q}))+\operatorname{rk}(E_{3}(\mathbb{Q})).

Each summand is computable via PARI’s
ellrank
.

4.
The main result

4.1.
Trivial points on
H_{m,n}

Lemma 4.1
.

For every master-tuple fiber
(m,n)
, the curve
H_{m,n}
has at least eight rational points

H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})\supseteq\{(0,\pm V_{2}W_{2}),\;(1,\pm 4U_{2}^{2}),\;(-1,\pm 4U_{2}^{2}),\;\infty_{+},\;\infty_{-}\}.

Each corresponds to a degenerate Euler-brick (one zero edge), not a perfect cuboid.

Proof.

The four affine points are verified by direct evaluation of
v^{2}=P(t^{2})Q(t^{2})
:

•

At
t=0
:
P(0)=V_{2}^{2}
and
Q(0)=W_{2}^{2}
, so
v^{2}=V_{2}^{2}W_{2}^{2}
, giving
v=\pm V_{2}W_{2}
.

•

At
t=\pm 1
:
P(1)=V_{2}^{2}+(4U_{2}^{2}-2V_{2}^{2})+V_{2}^{2}=4U_{2}^{2}
and
Q(1)=W_{2}^{2}+2(U_{2}^{2}-V_{2}^{2})+W_{2}^{2}=2(W_{2}^{2}+U_{2}^{2}-V_{2}^{2})=4U_{2}^{2}
(using
W_{2}^{2}=U_{2}^{2}+V_{2}^{2}
), so
v^{2}=(4U_{2}^{2})^{2}
, giving
v=\pm 4U_{2}^{2}
.

The two points at infinity are rational since the leading coefficient
V_{2}^{2}W_{2}^{2}=(V_{2}W_{2})^{2}
is a perfect square.

Geometric interpretation.
Recall a rational point
(t_{0},v_{0})
on
H_{m,n}
with
t_{0}=a/b
in lowest terms encodes a master tuple
(a,b,m,n)
. The trivial points correspond to:

•

t=0
corresponds to
a=0
, hence
U_{1}=-b^{2}
,
V_{1}=0
,
W_{1}=b^{2}
. The brick
(X,Y,Z)=(-b^{2}U_{2},0,-b^{2}V_{2})
has
Y=0
.

•

t=\pm 1
corresponds to
a=\pm b
, hence
U_{1}=0
,
V_{1}=2a^{2}
. The brick has
X=U_{1}U_{2}=0
and
Z=U_{1}V_{2}=0
.

•

Points at infinity correspond to
b=0
(after suitable projective lift), giving similar degenerations.

In all cases at least one edge of the brick vanishes, so the configuration cannot be a perfect cuboid (which requires three positive integer edges).
∎

4.2.
The torsion-intersection lemma

Lemma 4.2

(Torsion intersection)
.

Let
E_{q}\in\{E_{PQ},E_{uV},E_{3}\}
and let
\pi_{q}:H_{m,n}\to E_{q}
be the corresponding degree-
2
quotient. Suppose
\operatorname{rk}(E_{q}(\mathbb{Q}))=0
and the involution
\sigma
defining
\pi_{q}
has no rational fixed points on
H_{m,n}
. Then

|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|\leq 2\cdot|E_{q}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{rk}(E_{q}(\mathbb{Q}))=0
, the group
E_{q}(\mathbb{Q})=E_{q}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}
is finite. The image
\pi_{q}(H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q}))\subseteq E_{q}(\mathbb{Q})
has cardinality at most
|E_{q}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|
. Each fiber
\pi_{q}^{-1}(P)
over a non-ramification point has exactly
2
rational preimages or
0
. Since
\pi_{q}
has no rational ramification points by hypothesis, each fiber over
E_{q}(\mathbb{Q})
has
\leq 2
rational preimages. Summing,

|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|\leq 2\cdot|\pi_{q}(H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q}))|\leq 2\cdot|E_{q}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|.

∎

Lemma 4.3

(Rational fixed points of the three involutions)
.

For each of the three quotient maps
\pi_{q}
, the rational fixed points of
the corresponding involution on
H_{m,n}
are as follows:

•

\pi_{PQ}
(involution
\sigma_{1}:t\mapsto-t
): rational fixed
points at
t=0
and
t=\infty
(two points each, counted with

v
-sign).

•

\pi_{uV}
(involution
\sigma_{2}:t\mapsto 1/t
): rational fixed
points at
t=\pm 1
(two points each).

•

\pi_{3}
(involution
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}:t\mapsto-1/t
):

no
rational fixed points whatsoever (the fixed-point equation

has no rational solution).

In particular, the quotient
\pi_{3}
is unramified over
\mathbb{Q}
, and Lemma
4.2
applies to

in its sharpest form.

Proof.

Direct from the formulas for
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
given in
[
13
, §4.1]
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Lemma
4.3
shows that for the application of Lemma

, the cleanest case is

(no rational ramification anywhere). For

the four rational ramification points at

4.3.
Main theorem

Theorem 4.5

(Main result)
.

Let
(m,n)
be a master-tuple fiber. Then
|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|=8
with all rational points degenerate (and hence no perfect cuboid exists on the fiber) if either of the following holds:

(a)

(Naive case via
E_{3}
.)

\operatorname{rk}(E_{3}(\mathbb{Q}))=0
and
|E_{3}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|=4
.

(b)

(Naive case via
E_{uV}
.)

\operatorname{rk}(E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q}))=0
and
|E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|=6
.

(c)

(Refined case via
E_{uV}
.)

\operatorname{rk}(E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q}))=0
,
|E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|=8
, and an explicit enumeration of the eight rational torsion points on the
E_{uV}
-quartic shows that exactly six of them lift rationally to
H_{m,n}
(i.e. the four ramification points
(u,V)=(\pm 2,\pm 4U_{2}^{2})
, written in the
E_{uV}
-coordinate
u=t+1/t
, together with the two points at infinity).

Proof.

Case (a):
Lemma
4.3
shows
\pi_{3}
has no rational ramification points, so Lemma
4.2
gives
|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|\leq 2\cdot 4=8
. The eight trivial points of Lemma
4.1
provide the lower bound
|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|\geq 8
. Combining,
|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|=8
.

Case (b):
The map
\pi_{uV}:H_{m,n}\to E_{uV}
has four rational ramification points (at
t=\pm 1
with
V=\pm 4U_{2}^{2}
, all lying in
E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}
). For the bound, decompose
E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q})=R\cup N
where
R
is the set of four rational ramification points and
N
the remaining
|tors|-4=2
rational torsion points. Each
P\in R
has exactly one rational
H_{m,n}
-preimage; each
P\in N
has at most two. Hence
|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|\leq 4\cdot 1+2\cdot 2=8
. With the lower bound from the trivial points, equality holds.

Case (c):
Same decomposition with
|R|=4
ramification points and
|N|=4
non-ramification rational torsion points (since
|tors|=8
). The additional hypothesis says: of the
4
non-ramification torsion points, only the
2
at infinity lift rationally to
H_{m,n}
(each contributing
2
preimages); the other
2
affine non-ramification torsion points have no rational
H_{m,n}
-preimage. Thus
|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|\leq 4\cdot 1+2\cdot 2+2\cdot 0=8
, with equality enforced by the trivial-point lower bound.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Case (c) accounts for
245
of our
1{,}072
proven fibers; cases (a)+(b) account for the other

(all of these via

with

— empirically,

4.4.
Unconditional rank-zero certification

For a given fiber, the rank-zero hypothesis on
E_{q}\in\{E_{uV},E_{3}\}
is verified algorithmically. PARI’s
ellrank
(2-descent) returns a pair of bounds
[r_{\mathrm{lo}},r_{\mathrm{up}}]
with
r_{\mathrm{lo}}\leq\operatorname{rk}(E_{q}(\mathbb{Q}))\leq r_{\mathrm{up}}
; we accept rank zero on this strand only when
r_{\mathrm{lo}}=r_{\mathrm{up}}=0
. When the bounds are ambiguous — typically
r_{\mathrm{lo}}=0
but
r_{\mathrm{up}}\in\{2,4\}
owing to non-trivial
\Sel^{(2)}(E_{q})
— we apply the following:

Theorem 4.7

(Modular-symbol rank-zero certification)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a semistable elliptic curve, that is, with squarefree conductor. If

L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}\;\neq\;0,

where
\Omega_{E}
is the real period of
E
, then
\mathrm{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q}))=0
unconditionally.

Proof.

Let
c_{E}
denote the Manin constant of the optimal modular parametrisation

\varphi:X_{0}(N_{E})\to E
, fixed by
\varphi^{*}\omega_{E}=c_{E}\cdot f_{E}(q)\,\frac{dq}{q}

where
f_{E}
is the normalised newform attached to
E
. The exact rational
output of the modular-symbol algorithm is then

[f_{E}]_{0}^{i\infty}=c_{E}\cdot L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}^{+}
, where
\Omega_{E}^{+}
is the
real period of the Néron differential. By Edixhoven
[
7
]

one has
c_{E}\mid 2
for every elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
c_{E}=1
for
all semistable curves (extended to general curves
in
[
5
]
). Since
E
is semistable by hypothesis,
c_{E}=1

and the modular-symbol output equals
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}^{+}
exactly. Hence

L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}^{+}\neq 0
implies
L(E,1)\neq 0

unconditionally, i.e.
\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=0
. By the modularity
theorem
[
4
]
,
E
is modular; since

\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\leq 1
, Kolyvagin’s
theorem
[
10
]
applies and gives
\mathrm{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q}))=0
.
∎

In our scan, this certification is implemented by Sage’s

E.lseries().L_ratio()
, which returns the exact rational

L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}
for a semistable
E
, and Sage’s

E.conductor()
for the squarefree (semistability) check.
Empirically, every elliptic factor
E_{q}
encountered in our scan turns
out to be semistable, so Theorem
4.7
applies as
stated. We note in passing that the bound
c_{E}\mid 2
holds for every
elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}

[
7
]
, so even for a
non-semistable
E
the modular-symbol output is twice
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}^{+}

at worst, and a non-vanishing odd-numerator output already certifies

L(E,1)\neq 0
; we did not need to invoke this refinement on any
fiber. This certification adds
468
fibers proven via
E_{3}
on top of
the
359
already produced by sharp
ellrank
bounds, for a total
of
827
via
E_{3}
in case (a) of Theorem
4.5
. It also
unlocks
36
further fibers via
E_{uV}
in case (c) (combining with the
lift refinement of §
4.5
).

Remark 4.8
.

For some additional fibers, especially via
E_{uV}
, the modular-symbol computation
L_ratio()
did not complete within our practical compute budget (the conductor of these

exceeds

4.5.
Lift-count refinement for
|E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|=8

When
\operatorname{rk}(E_{uV})=0
and
|E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|=8
, Lemma
4.2
gives the naive bound
|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|\leq 2\cdot 8-4=12
, which is too weak to match the trivial lower bound
8
. The refinement (case (c) of Theorem
4.5
) replaces the naive bound by an exact lift count.

Concretely:
E_{uV}
is presented as the quartic

E_{uV}:\quad V^{2}=(V_{2}^{2}u^{2}+4(U_{2}^{2}-V_{2}^{2}))(W_{2}^{2}u^{2}-4V_{2}^{2}),

and
\pi_{uV}:H_{m,n}\to E_{uV}
has the explicit formula
u=t+1/t
on the affine part. A rational point
(u_{0},V_{0})\in E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q})
admits a rational

-preimage iff

is a non-negative rational square (corresponding to two solutions

of

).

We enumerate

rigorously: PARI’s

(with height bound

) finds all
|tors|
rational points (six affine plus two at infinity, when leading

is a square — which it always is). For each torsion point, we test whether

is a square. The lift count is then

\#\{\text{rational $H_{m,n}$-preimages}\}=4\cdot 1\;+\;2\cdot 2\;+\;2k,

where
4
ramification,
2
at infinity, and

counts affine non-ramification torsion points with rational lift. The fiber is proven iff
k=0
, giving the count exactly
8
.

Remark 4.9

(Why “
2
at infinity” contributes

)
.

The two rational points at infinity on the

-quartic each have

5.
Discussion

5.1.
What remains uncovered

The combined methodology covers
1{,}072
out of
2{,}040
fibers up to
M_{\max}=100
. The remaining
968
fibers fall into two categories:

(a)

Persistently ambiguous rank.
For at least one quotient
E_{q}
, PARI’s
ellrank
returns
[0,k]
with
k>0
and the modular-symbol computation
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}
either vanishes (forcing
\operatorname{rk}(E_{q})\geq 1
) or fails to terminate within our compute budget (large conductor
>10^{12}
, especially for
E_{uV}
). These fibers most likely either have
\operatorname{rk}(E_{q})\geq 1
for every quotient, or are in principle treatable by allocating more compute to the modular-symbol computation.

(b)

Hard fibers.
Fibers where all three elliptic factors have rank
\geq 1
(rigorously bounded below). Then Lemma
4.2
does not apply to any factor. Standard (linear) Chabauty–Coleman applies only when
\operatorname{rk}(\operatorname{Jac}(H_{m,n})(\mathbb{Q}))<g(H_{m,n})=3
, that is when the total rank from Corollary
3.2
is at most
2
. Empirically, total rank
\geq 3
occurs in the majority of these fibers; quadratic Chabauty would be the natural next tool.

The asymptotic regime
\max(m,n)>100
is not addressed by the present scan; a uniform statement (positive proportion of fibers with a rank-zero quotient, or a Brauer obstruction independent of
(m,n)
) would be needed to settle this.

Example 5.1

(A hard-case fiber:
(m,n)=(5,2)
)
.

With
U_{2}=21
,
V_{2}=20
,
W_{2}=29
, the three elliptic factors have ranks

\operatorname{rk}(E_{PQ}(\mathbb{Q}))=2,\quad\operatorname{rk}(E_{uV}(\mathbb{Q}))=1,\quad\operatorname{rk}(E_{3}(\mathbb{Q}))=1,

so
\operatorname{rk}(\operatorname{Jac}(H_{5,2})(\mathbb{Q}))=4\geq g(H_{5,2})=3
. Neither Lemma
4.2
nor standard Chabauty applies. PARI’s
hyperellratpoints
up to height
B=10^{6}
still finds only the eight trivial points, but this provides only empirical evidence, not a proof.

5.2.
Possible extensions

(i)

Quadratic Chabauty.
For genus-
3
hyperelliptic curves with
\operatorname{rk}(\operatorname{Jac})=g
, the methods of Balakrishnan–Dogra–Müller–Tuitman
[
2
]
give effective enumeration of rational points via
p
-adic heights. An adaptation to our family would close many of the hard fibers in (b).

(ii)

Mordell–Weil sieving.
Combining the rank-decomposition of Corollary
3.2
with reductions modulo many small primes, one can in principle bound
|H_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})|
tighter than via Coleman alone. The decomposition
\operatorname{Jac}(H_{m,n})\sim E_{PQ}\times E_{uV}\times E_{3}
makes this approach particularly attractive: each factor’s reduction is an explicit elliptic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(iii)

Brauer–Manin obstruction.
The cuboid surface
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{P}^{7}_{\mathbb{Q}}
defined by the four square conditions

X^{2}+Y^{2}=F_{3}^{2},\;X^{2}+Z^{2}=F_{2}^{2},\;Y^{2}+Z^{2}=F_{1}^{2},\;X^{2}+Y^{2}+Z^{2}=W^{2}

admits a Brauer group whose computation has been initiated by Stoll and others without finding an obstruction so far. A uniform obstruction would settle Conjecture
1.1
entirely.

(iv)

Modular and CM interpretations.
The factor
E_{3}
in our decomposition has the form
V^{2}=(V_{2}^{2}w^{2}+4U_{2}^{2})(W_{2}^{2}w^{2}+4U_{2}^{2})
, which depends on
(U_{2},V_{2},W_{2})
as a primitive Pythagorean triple. It would be of interest to determine whether this family admits a modular parametrization or CM structure that would yield uniform rank bounds.

(v)

Statistical evidence.
Goldfeld’s conjecture
[
8
]
predicts that, in a one-parameter family of elliptic curves ordered by conductor, the algebraic rank should be
0
or
1
with density
\tfrac{1}{2}
each. Our empirical data (
1{,}072/2{,}040\approx 52.5\%
proven via
\operatorname{rk}=0
on either
E_{3}
or
E_{uV}
) is broadly consistent with such heuristics, but does not yet match a precise prediction.

5.3.
Connection to Conjecture B

Combining Theorems
2.4
,
4.5
and Lemma
2.5
, we have the following precise version of our partial result:

Corollary 5.2

(Partial Conjecture B)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{100}\subset\mathbb{N}^{2}
denote the set of
1{,}072
pairs
(m,n)
listed in Appendix
A
. Then no perfect cuboid
(X,Y,Z)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
exists whose primitive form arises from a master tuple
(a,b,m,n)
with
(m,n)\in\mathcal{S}_{100}
.

The corollary covers an explicit family of
1{,}072
master-tuple fibers. For each fiber
(m,n)\in\mathcal{S}_{100}
, the result excludes a perfect cuboid arising from
any
master tuple
(a,b,m,n)
with that fixed
(m,n)
, regardless of the rank of
E_{m,n}
(which controls how many master tuples populate the fiber but does not enter the torsion-intersection argument). The full Conjecture B would follow from extending
\mathcal{S}_{100}
to all of
\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}:\gcd(m,n)=1,m-n\text{ odd}\}
.

Appendix B
Computational verification

The verification scripts are written in SageMath 10.7
[
15
]
and use PARI/GP version 2.17.3
[
12
]
. Three rigorous certificates are combined:

(1)

Sharp
ellrank
.
PARI’s
ellrank
(called with
effort = 2
) returns rank bounds
[r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{up}}]
via 2-descent; we accept rank-zero only when
r_{\mathrm{low}}=r_{\mathrm{up}}=0
.

(2)

Kolyvagin certification.
For ambiguous bounds
[0,k]
with
k>0
, we call Sage’s
analytic_rank_upper_bound
, which returns a rigorous upper bound on
\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)
. When this is
0
,
\operatorname{rk}(E(\mathbb{Q}))=0
follows from Theorem
4.7
.

(3)

Lift refinement.
For fibers with
\operatorname{rk}(E_{uV})=0
and
|tors|=8
, the eight rational torsion points on the
E_{uV}
-quartic are enumerated via PARI’s
hyperellratpoints
(with height
10^{5}
); the lift to
H_{m,n}
is verified by the discriminant test of §
4.5
.

Torsion structure is computed via Sage’s
E.torsion_subgroup()
on the Weierstrass model obtained from the quartic via PARI’s
ellfromeqn
. All scripts are reproducible from the companion repository:

https://github.com/renpe/euler-brick-obstructions/tree/main/paper3

The main pipeline
analysis/jh_torsion_full_dispatch.py
(with subprocess workers
analysis/jh_torsion_full_worker.py
) runs in roughly
5
minutes on a
30
-core workstation, producing
data/proven_fibers.csv
and a full per-fiber log
data/scan_full_M100.jsonl
. The proof of Theorem
4.5
relies only on the three certificates above; height searches via PARI’s
hyperellratpoints
up to
B=10^{6}
are an independent sanity check and find no non-trivial rational points on any fiber.
