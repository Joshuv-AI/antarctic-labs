Spectral Radius, Vertex Deletion, and Chromatic Number of Signed Graphs
Abhay Jayarajan, M. Rajesh Kannan, Priti Prasanna Mondal

Abstract
A signed graph Σ = ( G , σ ) \Sigma=(G,\sigma) is a graph G G with edges given signs 1 1 or − 1 -1 defined by the function σ \sigma . The adjacency matrix of Σ \Sigma is defined as per these signs. The relation between the largest eigenvalue of G G and G − v G-v has been studied in recent years, where G − v G-v is the graph obtained from G G by deleting the vertex v v . In 2020, Sun and Das proved that the difference of the squares of the largest eigenvalues of the graphs G G and G − v G-v is bounded above by 2 ​ d ​ ( v ) − 1 2d(v)-1 where d ⁡ ( v ) d(v) is the degree of v v . A similar result need not be true for the largest eigenvalues of signed graphs. In this paper, we prove that the result is valid for the spectral radius of signed graphs. On the other hand, the signed graph version of Hoffman’s chromatic number bound and Cvetkovic’s lower bound was proved by Wang et al. in 2021. They also discussed the difficulty in proving the extended version encompassing all eigenvalues of Σ \Sigma as was done for unsigned graphs by Wocjan et al. We give a lower bound for the chromatic number in terms of all the eigenvalues of Σ \Sigma and Σ − \Sigma_{-} , where Σ − \Sigma_{-} is the spanning subgraph induced by the negative edges.

1
Introduction

A signed graph is a graph
\Sigma=(G,\sigma);
where
G=(V,E)
is the underlying graph, and
\sigma:E\rightarrow\{-1,+1\}
is the sign function. Let
e_{ij}
denote the edge between the vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
of
G.
Let
E^{+}(\Sigma)=\{e\in E:\sigma(e)=+1\}
and
E^{-}(\Sigma)=\{e\in E:\sigma(e)=-1\}
. Let
\Sigma_{+}
be the subgraph of
\Sigma
with
V(\Sigma_{+})=V(\Sigma),~E(\Sigma_{+})=E^{+}(\Sigma)
and
\Sigma_{-}
be the subgraph of
\Sigma
with
V(\Sigma_{-})=V(\Sigma),~E(\Sigma_{-})=E^{-}(\Sigma)
. Let
C
be a cycle of signed graph
\Sigma,
the
sign of a cycle

C
is denoted by
\sigma(C)
and defined by
\sigma(C)=\prod_{e_{ij}\in C}\sigma(e_{ij}).
A cycle is said to be
positive
(respectively,
negative
) if
\sigma(C)=+1
(respectively,
\sigma(C)=-1
). A signed graph is said to be
balanced
if all its cycles are positive.
The
adjacency matrix
of a signed graph
\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
denoted by
A(\Sigma)=[a_{ij}^{\sigma}],
is an
n\times n
symmetric matrix, defined by:

a_{ij}^{\sigma}=\begin{cases}\sigma(e_{ij})&\mbox{if}~v_{i}\sim v_{j}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We denote by
\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)\geq\lambda_{2}(\Sigma)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(\Sigma)
the eigenvalues of
A(\Sigma)
. Let
\rho(\Sigma)=\max\{\lambda_{1}(\Sigma),-\lambda_{n}(\Sigma)\}
, denote the spectral radius of
A(\Sigma)
. Let
(G,1)
be a signed graph with the signs of all edges being positive. Then the eigenvalues of
(G,1)
are ordered as
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(G)
and the spectral radius is denoted by
\rho(G)
.

A function
\eta:V(G)\rightarrow\{-1,+1\}
is called a
switching function
. Two signed graphs
\Sigma_{1}=(G,\sigma_{1})
and
\Sigma_{2}=(G,\sigma_{2})
are
switching equivalent,
written as
\Sigma_{1}\sim\Sigma_{2},
if there is a switching function
\eta:V(G)\rightarrow\{-1,+1\}
such that

\sigma_{2}(e_{ij})=\eta(v_{i})^{-1}\sigma_{1}(e_{ij})\eta(v_{j}).

Suppose
S=\{v\in V(G):\eta(v)=1\}
then one can see that
\Sigma_{2}
turns out to be the graph obtained from
\Sigma_{1}
by changing signs of all the edges in
\Sigma_{1}
with one endpoint in
S
and other in it’s complement. In this case, we say that
\Sigma_{2}
is the graph obtained by switching
\Sigma_{1}
with respect to
S
. It is well known that switching equivalence preserves the spectrum of
\Sigma
.

The largest eigenvalue of graphs has been extensively studied in the last few decades. Brualdi and Hoffman
[
2
]
showed that for graphs with
m=\frac{k(k-1)}{2}
,
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq k-1
. This bound was improved by Stanley
[
8
]
who found that
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+8m}}{2}
. Later, Hong
[
12
]
improved the bound and showed that
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\sqrt{2m-n+1}
. In 2019, Guo et al.
[
3
]
gave another upper bound for the largest eigenvalue. In the paper, the authors proved that if
\frac{(k-2)(k-3)}{2}\leq m-n\leq\frac{k(k-3)}{2}
where
3\leq k\leq n
, then
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\sqrt{2m-n-k+\frac{5}{2}+\sqrt{2m-2m+\frac{9}{4}}}
with equality if and only if
G
is a complete graph or
K_{4}-e
which is the graph obtained from
K_{4}
by removing an edge. In the same paper, the authors proposed a conjecture regarding the effect on the largest eigenvalue of a graph on removing a vertex and the edges that are incident to it. The authors conjectured that for any graph
G
and a non-isolated vertex
v\in V(G)
,

\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{2}(G-v)+2d(v)-1}.

In 2020, Sun and Das
[
9
]
confirmed the conjecture and characterised all connected graphs for which this bound is attained. A short proof of this result was given by Jin et al.
[
5
]
, Liu and Ning
[
7
]
. In
[
6
]
, Lan et al. proved an analogue of this result in case of signed graphs with some restrictions on the choice of vertex. However, this result is not generally true for signed graphs. One of the main objectives of this paper is to show that the above bound for the largest eigenvalue remains valid when the largest eigenvalue is replaced by the spectral radius of a signed graph.

A proper
k
-coloring of an unsigned graph is a mapping
t:V(G)\rightarrow\{1,\dots,n\}
such that no two adjacent vertices are mapped to the same integer. The least
k
for which
G
admits a proper
k
-coloring is called the chromatic number of
G
, denoted by
\chi(G)
. For
k\geq 1,
let
M_{k}=\{\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots,\pm k/2\}
if
k
is even and
M_{k}=\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots,\pm(k-1)/2\}
if
k
is odd. A (proper)
k
-coloring of a signed graph
\Sigma
is a mapping
c:V(\Sigma)\rightarrow M_{k}
such that
c(u)\neq\sigma(uv)c(v).
The signed chromatic number of
\Sigma
, denoted by
\chi(\Sigma)
is the minimum number
k
for which
\Sigma
admits a
k
-coloring. The
Hoffman lower bound
of chromatic number
[
4
]

\chi(G)\geq 1-\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(G)}{\lambda_{n}(G)}

is a well-known result in spectral graph theory. A generalization of
Hoffman’s lower bound
for chromatic number for an unsigned graph was given by Wocjan and Elphick
[
11
]
, which comprised all the eigenvalues of the graph.

Recently, Wang et al.
[
10
]
studied the signed version of
Hoffman’s lower bound
for chromatic number and discussed the difficulty of Wocjan and Elphick’s bound for signed graphs. In this paper, in Section
4
, we give an analogous bound for signed graphs that comprises all the eigenvalues of the signed graph as well as the eigenvalues of
\Sigma_{-}
, where, as defined earlier,
\Sigma_{-}
is the spanning subgraph induced by the negative edges.

2
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

For a signed graph
\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
and a vertex
v\in V(G)
, define

N^{-}(v)=\{u\in V(G):\sigma(uv)=-1\}~~~\mbox{and}~~~N^{+}(v)=\{u\in V(G):\sigma(uv)=1\}.

Let
A
be a symmetric matrix partitioned as

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

Set

\rho_{2}=\rho(A_{22}),\qquad\mu=\rho(A_{12}A_{22}A_{21}),\qquad\nu=\rho(A_{12}A_{21}).

Consider the polynomial

g(x)=(x-\nu)^{2}(\rho_{2}^{2}+x)-\mu^{2}.

Since
g^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x>\nu
, the function
g
is strictly increasing on
[\nu,\infty)
. Furthermore,
g(\nu)=-\mu^{2}\leq 0.

Hence, if
\theta^{*}
denotes the largest root of
g
, then
\theta^{*}\geq\nu
. Moreover,
\theta^{*}=\nu
if and only if
\mu=0.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
5
]

Let
A
be a symmetric matrix partitioned as

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\rho^{2}(A)\leq\rho_{2}^{2}+\theta^{*},

where
\rho_{2}=\rho(A_{22})
,
\mu=\rho(A_{12}A_{22}A_{21})
,
\nu=\rho(A_{12}A_{21})
, and
\theta^{*}
is the largest root of

g(x)=(x-\nu)^{2}(\rho_{2}^{2}+x)-\mu^{2}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
be a signed graph and let
0\neq\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\rho(\Sigma)\geq\left|\frac{\mathbf{x}^{T}A(\Sigma)\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}}\right|.

3
Signed Spectral Radius

Guo et al.
[
3
]
presented the effect on the largest eigenvalue of a graph when a vertex is deleted and proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3.1
.

[
3
]

Let
G
be a connected graph and
v
be a non-isolated vertex in
G
. Then

\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\sqrt{\lambda^{2}(G-v)+2d(v)-1}

Sun and Das
[
9
]
gave a proof of the proposed conjecture and also characterised the connected graphs for which the bound is attained.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
9
]

Let
G
be a connected graph and
v
be a non-isolated vertex in
G
. Then

\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\sqrt{\lambda^{2}(G-v)+2d(v)-1}

Equality holds if and only if
G
is a star graph and
v
is a pendant vertex, or
G
is complete.

A short proof of this result was given by Jin et al.
[
5
]
, Liu and Ning
[
7
]
. In
[
6
]
, an analogue of this result was proven with some restrictions for a signed graph
\Sigma
. The authors proved the following:

Theorem 3.2
.

[
6
]

Let
\Sigma
be a connected signed graph. Let
v
be a vertex such that
\Sigma-v
is balanced. Then

\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{2}(\Sigma-v)+2d(v)-1}

However, the result is not true in general for signed graphs
[
6
]
. A simple example is the following: a signed graph
\Sigma
obtained from an unbalanced triangle with one negative edge and a pendant vertex attached to the vertex in the triangle having
2
positive neighbors, the result does not hold. Nevertheless, in this section, we prove that the perturbation result is true for the spectral radius of signed graphs. More precisely, we prove that,

\rho(\Sigma)\leq\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+2d(v)-1}.

For a signed graph
\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
and a vertex
v
of
G
, define
g(x)=(x-d(v))\bigl(\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+x\bigr)-4(m^{+}-m^{-})^{2}=0
, where
m^{\pm}=|E^{\pm}(\Sigma[N(v)])|
and
\Sigma[N(v)]
is the signed subgraph induced by the neighbors of the vertex
v
. Throughout this section, we shall use the above notation.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
be a signed graph and let
v
be a non-isolated vertex. Set

m^{\pm}=|E^{\pm}(\Sigma[N(v)])|,
and let
\theta^{*}
denote the largest root of
g(x)
.
Then

\rho^{2}(\Sigma)\leq\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+\theta^{*}.

Furthermore, if
m^{+}=m^{-}
, then

\rho^{2}(\Sigma)\leq\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v).

Proof.

Let
v
be a non-isolated vertex of
\Sigma

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be the signed graph obtained from
\Sigma
by switching with respect to the set
N^{-}(v)
. Then every edge incident with
v
in
\Sigma^{\prime}
is positive.

The adjacency matrix of
\Sigma^{\prime}
can be written as

A(\Sigma^{\prime})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\textbf{a}^{T}\\
\textbf{a}&B\end{pmatrix},

where
B=A(\Sigma^{\prime}-v)
and
\mathbf{a}
is the incidence vector of the neighbors of
v
in
\Sigma^{\prime}-v
.
It is easy to see that,

\mathbf{a}^{T}\mathbf{a}=d(v),

and

\mathbf{a}^{T}B\mathbf{a}=2\bigl(|E^{+}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])|-|E^{-}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])|\bigr).

Note that

\Bigl|\;\bigl|E^{+}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])\bigr|-\bigl|E^{-}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])\bigr|\;\Bigr|=|m^{+}-m^{-}|.

Applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\rho^{2}(\Sigma^{\prime})\leq\rho^{2}(\Sigma^{\prime}-v)+\theta^{*}.

Since switching preserves the spectrum, it follows that

\rho^{2}(\Sigma)\leq\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+\theta^{*}.

Finally, if
m^{+}=m^{-}
, then the largest root of

g(x)
is
\theta^{*}=d(v)
. Hence,

\rho^{2}(\Sigma)\leq\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v).

∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s=|E^{+}(\Sigma)|
and
t=|E^{-}(\Sigma)|
be the numbers of positive and negative edges of a signed graph
\Sigma
, respectively. Then

\rho^{2}(\Sigma)\geq\dfrac{4(s-t)^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}-(2n-1)

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.2
on
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{1}
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the vector of all-ones, we get,

\rho^{2}(\Sigma)\geq\bigg(\dfrac{\textbf{1}^{T}A(\Sigma)\textbf{1}}{\textbf{1}^{T}\textbf{1}}\bigg)^{2}=\dfrac{4(s-t)^{2}}{n^{2}}.

(1)

It is easy to see that
2|s-t|\leq(s+t)\leq n(n-1)
. Now,

\displaystyle 4(s-t)^{2}-n^{2}(n-1)^{2}\leq 0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\dfrac{(2n-1)(4(s-t)^{2}-n^{2}(n-1)^{2})}{n^{2}(n-1)^{2}}\leq 0;~~~~~~~~~~~~\Big[\mbox{Since}~\frac{2n-1}{n^{2}(n-1)^{2}}\geq 0.\Big]

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\dfrac{(4(s-t)^{2})(n^{2}-(n-1)^{2})-(2n-1)n^{2}(n-1)^{2}}{n^{2}(n-1)^{2}}\leq 0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\dfrac{4(s-t)^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}-(2n-1)-\dfrac{4(s-t)^{2}}{n^{2}}\leq 0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\dfrac{4(s-t)^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}-(2n-1)\leq\dfrac{4(s-t)^{2}}{n^{2}}

Substituting the above inequality in Equation (
1
) gives the desired result.
∎

Next, we present the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
be a signed graph and
v
be a non-isolated vertex. Then

\rho(\Sigma)\leq\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+2d(v)-1}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be the signed graph obtained from
\Sigma
by switching with respect to the set
N^{-}(v)
. Then the adjacency matrix of
\Sigma^{\prime}
can be written in the block form

A(\Sigma^{\prime})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\textbf{a}^{T}\\
\textbf{a}&B\end{pmatrix},

where
B=A(\Sigma^{\prime}-v)
and
a
is the column vector whose entries indicate the neighbours of
v
in
\Sigma^{\prime}-v
.

Let
\theta^{*}
be the largest root of
g(x)
. If
d(v)=1
, then
m^{+}=m^{-}=0
, and hence the above equation reduces to

(x-d(v))\bigl(\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+x\bigr)=0.

Consequently,
\theta^{*}=d(v)
, and the result follows immediately.

So assume that
d(v)\geq 2
. Since
2d(v)-1>d(v)
, Theorem
3.3
implies that it suffices to show that
g(2d(v)-1)\geq 0.

We have

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}(\Sigma^{\prime}-v)

\displaystyle\geq\rho^{2}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4\bigl(|E^{+}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])|-|E^{-}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])|\bigr)^{2}}{(d(v)-1)^{2}}-(2d(v)-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{4(m^{+}-m^{-})^{2}}{(d(v)-1)^{2}}-(2d(v)-1),

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
3.1
. The last equality follows from the fact that

|E^{+}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])|-|E^{-}(\Sigma^{\prime}[N(v)])|=m^{+}-m^{-}.

Multiplying the above inequality by
(d(v)-1)^{2}
yields

(d(v)-1)^{2}\bigl(\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+2d(v)-1\bigr)\geq 4(m^{+}-m^{-})^{2}.

Since

g(2d(v)-1)=(d(v)-1)\bigl(\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+2d(v)-1\bigr)-4(m^{+}-m^{-})^{2},

and
d(v)-1\geq 1
, it follows that

g(2d(v)-1)\geq 0.

Therefore,
\theta^{*}\leq 2d(v)-1
. Combining this with Theorem
3.3
completes the proof.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

The spectral radius bound in Theorem
3.4
is sharp.

1.

Let
\Sigma=(K_{n},+)
be the complete balanced graph on
n
vertices. Then
\rho(\Sigma)=n-1
and for any
v\in V(K_{n})
, we have
\rho(\Sigma-v)=n-2
.

2.

Let
\Sigma=(K_{n},-)
be the complete graph on
n
vertices with all edges given negative signs. Here, again
\rho(\Sigma)=n-1
and
\rho(\Sigma-v)=n-2
for any vertex
v\in V(K_{n})
.

Further, we give another upper bound for
\rho(\Sigma)
which might, at times, beat the above bound.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a signed graph and
v
be a non-isolated vertex. Then

\rho(\Sigma)\leq\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)+\dfrac{2|m^{+}-m^{-}|}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)}}}

Proof.

Let us consider the function
g(x)
at
x=d(v)+\dfrac{2|m^{+}-m^{-}|}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)}}
, we get

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~g\Bigg(d(v)+\dfrac{2|m^{+}-m^{-}|}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\dfrac{2|m^{+}-m^{-}|}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)}}\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg(\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)+\dfrac{2|m^{+}-m^{-}|}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)}}\Bigg)-4(|m^{+}-m^{-}|)^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{8(|m^{+}-m^{-}|)^{3}}{(\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\geq 0

Applying Theorem
3.3
gives the required inequality.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Sometimes Corollary
3.1
gives a better bound for spectral radius compared to Theorem
3.4
. For instance, with a vertex
v
with
d(v)\geq 2
and
m^{+}=m^{-}
, we have
\rho^{2}(\Sigma)\leq\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)+\dfrac{2|m^{+}-m^{-}|}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+d(v)}}<\rho^{2}(\Sigma-v)+2d(v)-1
.

4
Signed Chromatic Number

The well-known Hoffman’s lower bound on the chromatic number
\chi(G)
of a graph is stated as follows:

Theorem 4.1
.

[
4
]

For a non-empty graph
G
on
n
vertices

\chi(G)\geq 1+\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(G)}{-\lambda_{n}(G)}

.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
be an element of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Let
x^{\downarrow}
be the vector obtained by rearranging the coordinates of
x
in the non-increasing order. Thus, if
x^{\downarrow}=(x_{1}^{\downarrow},x_{2}^{\downarrow},\cdots,x_{n}^{\downarrow}),
then
x_{1}^{\downarrow}\geq x_{2}^{\downarrow}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n}^{\downarrow}.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The vector
x
is majorized by
y,
in symbols
x^{\downarrow}\prec y^{\downarrow},
if

\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{\downarrow}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{\downarrow}~~~~\mbox{for}~~m=1,2,\cdots,n-1;~~\mbox{and}~~\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\downarrow}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{\downarrow}.

For a matrix
B
, let
B^{*}
denote the conjugate transpose of
B
. Let
A
be a Hermitian matrix of order
n
, and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
A
. Consider the vector
\lambda^{\downarrow}(A)=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
and
\lambda^{\uparrow}(A)=(\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n-1},\cdots,\lambda_{1})
. Then the eigenvalues of any two Hermitian matrices follow the following majorisation.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
1
]

If
A,B
are Hermitian matrices, then the eigenvalues of
A,B,
and
A+B
satisfy the following majorisation relation:

\lambda^{\downarrow}(A)+\lambda^{\uparrow}(B)\prec\lambda^{\downarrow}(A+B)\prec\lambda^{\downarrow}(A)+\lambda^{\downarrow}(B)

Wocjan and Elphick
[
11
]
proved the following results and used the majorisation technique to get the chromatic number bound.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
11
]
Let
G
be a simple unsigned graph, and there exists a coloring of
G
with
c
colors. Then, there exist
c
diagonal unitary matrices
U_{1},U_{2},\cdots,U_{c}
whose entries are
c^{th}
roots of unity such that

\sum_{s=1}^{c}U_{s}(W\odot A)U_{s}^{*}=0,

where
W
is an arbitrary self-adjoint matrix and
W\odot A
denotes the entry-wise product of
W
and
A.

Corollary 4.1
.

[
11
]

Assume that there exists a coloring of
G
with
c
colors. Then, there exist
c-1
diagonal unitary matrices
U_{1},U_{2},\cdots,U_{c-1}
whose entries are
c^{th}
roots of unity such that

\sum_{s=1}^{c-1}U_{s}(-W\odot A)U_{s}^{*}=W\odot A.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
11
]

Let
G
be a simple non-empty graph with
n
vertices. Let
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(G)
be the eigenvalues of
A(G).
Then

\chi(G)\geq 1+\max_{m=1,2,\cdots,n-1}~~~\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(G)}{-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(G)},

where
\chi(G)
is the chromatic number of
G
.

In 2021, Wang et al.
[
10
]
proved the Hoffman’s lower bound version for signed graphs.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
10
]

For a non-empty signed graph
\Sigma
on
n
vertices,

\chi(\Sigma)\geq 1+\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)}{-\lambda_{n}(\Sigma)}.

where
\chi(\Sigma)
denotes the signed chromatic number of
\Sigma
.

One can observe that restricting
m=1
in Theorem
4.3
gives the Hoffman’s bound. Wang et al., in their work, noted that such an improved bound is not directly possible in the case of signed graphs. Let
\Sigma=(K_{n},-1)
be the complete graph on
n
vertices with each of the edges given negative signs. In this case, for
m=n-1
,

1+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(G)}{-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(G)}=1+\frac{-\lambda_{n}(G)}{\lambda_{1}(G)}=1+\dfrac{n-1}{1}=n>\chi(\Sigma)=2

In this section, we give a lower bound for the signed chromatic number in terms of eigenvalues of
\Sigma
and it’s subgraph
\Sigma_{-}
.

For an unsigned graph
G
and a subgraph
H,
the chromatic number satisfies the inequality
\chi(G)\geq\chi(H).
Analogous result for signed graph is as follows:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a signed graph and
\Sigma_{1}
be a subgraph of
\Sigma.
Then
\chi(\Sigma)\geq\chi(\Sigma_{1}).

Proof.

Let
\chi(\Sigma)=k.

Then, by definition, there exists a proper signed
k
-coloring

c:V(\Sigma)\rightarrow M_{k},

where
M_{k}
is the color set used in the definition of signed coloring. Consider the restricted function
c_{|V(\Sigma_{1})}
. This works as a proper
k
-coloring for the subgraph
\Sigma_{1}
. Thus,
\chi(\Sigma)\geq\chi(\Sigma_{1}).

∎

The following result gives a lower bound on the chromatic number of signed graphs.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be a signed graph with
n
vertices. Let
B=-A(\Sigma_{-})
and let
\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)\geq\lambda_{2}(\Sigma)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(\Sigma)
be the eigenvalues of
A(\Sigma)
and
\lambda_{1}(B)\geq\lambda_{2}(B)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(B)
be the eigenvalues of
B.
Then

\chi(\Sigma)\geq 1+\max_{m=1,2,\cdots,n-1}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)}{-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(\Sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)}.

(2)

Proof.

The signed graph
\Sigma_{+}
is a subgraph with all positive edges. Then
B=A(\Sigma_{+})-A(\Sigma).
If
\chi(\Sigma_{+})=c,
then, by Theorem
4.2
, there exist
c
diagonal unitary matrices
U_{1},U_{2},\cdots,U_{c}
whose entries are
c^{th}
roots of unity such that

\sum_{s=1}^{c}U_{s}A(\Sigma_{+})U_{s}^{*}=0.

By Corollary
4.1
,

\sum_{s=1}^{c-1}U_{s}(-A(\Sigma_{+}))U_{s}^{*}=A(\Sigma_{+}).

From the proof of the Theorem
4.3
,

\lambda^{\downarrow}(A(\Sigma_{+}))\prec(c-1)\lambda^{\downarrow}(-A(\Sigma_{+})).

(3)

Since
A(\Sigma_{+})=A(\Sigma)+B
, by the inequality (
3
), we get

\lambda^{\downarrow}(A(\Sigma)+B)\prec(c-1)\lambda^{\downarrow}(-A(\Sigma)-B).

By Lemma
4.1
,

\lambda^{\downarrow}(A(\Sigma))+\lambda^{\uparrow}(B)\prec(c-1)(\lambda^{\downarrow}(-A(\Sigma))+\lambda^{\downarrow}(-B))

\implies\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)\leq(c-1)\Big(-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(\Sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)\Big)

Hence,

c-1\geq\underset{m=1,2,\cdots,n-1.}{\mbox{max}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)}{-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(\Sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)}.

\implies\chi(\Sigma_{+})\geq 1+\underset{m=1,2,\cdots,n-1.}{\mbox{max}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)}{-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(\Sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)}.

Thus, by Lemma
4.2
,

\chi(\Sigma)\geq 1+\underset{m=1,2,\cdots,n-1.}{\mbox{max}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)}{-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(\Sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)}.

∎

Remark 4.1
.

The signed graph
\Sigma
in the above theorem can be replaced by any signed graph that is switching equivalent to it. Hence for a signed graph
\Sigma
the following is true:

\chi(\Sigma)\geq 1+\max_{\Sigma^{\prime}\sim\Sigma}~\max_{m=1,2,\cdots,n-1}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(-\Sigma^{\prime}_{-})}{-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(\Sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{n-i+1}(-\Sigma^{\prime}_{-})}

Next, we construct families of graphs for which the bound given in equation (
2
) is sharp. We need the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

The
join
of two graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\vee H
, is the graph obtained from the disjoint union
G\cup H
by adding all possible edges between the vertices of
G
and the vertices of
H
. Formally,

V(G\vee H)=V(G)\cup V(H),

and

E(G\vee H)=E(G)\cup E(H)\cup\{uv:u\in V(G),\;v\in V(H)\}.

Theorem 4.6
.

For the following families of graphs, the bound given in the above theorem is sharp.

1.

If
\Sigma=(G\vee G,-),
where
G\vee G
is the join of
G
with itself and if
G=K_{n}
or
G=C_{n}
for
m=2|V(\Sigma)|-1=2n-1
.

2.

If
\Sigma=(G,+),
where
G
is bipartite graph for
m=1
.

3.

If
\Sigma=(K_{n},+)
for
m=1
.

Proof.

(1) If
\Sigma=(G\vee G,-)
, then apply the switching with respect to all vertices of
G
and call the new graph
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Then the adjacency matrix of
\Sigma^{{}^{\prime}}
can be expressed as a block matrix as follows:

A(\Sigma^{{}^{\prime}})=\begin{bmatrix}-A(G)&J\\
J&-A(G)\end{bmatrix}=-I_{2}\otimes A(G)+A(K_{2})\otimes J

and

B=\begin{bmatrix}A(G)&O\\
O&A(G)\end{bmatrix}=I_{2}\otimes A(G)

Now, for
m=2n-1,

\mbox{define}~~R=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1}\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1}\lambda_{2n-i+1}(B)}{-\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1}\lambda_{2n-i+1}(\Sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1}\lambda_{2n-i+1}(B)}=\frac{-\lambda_{2n}(\Sigma)-\lambda_{1}(B)}{\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)+\lambda_{1}(B)}

Case (i):
For
G=K_{n};~~\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)=\lambda_{1}(\Sigma^{{}^{\prime}})=1,~\lambda_{2n}(\Sigma)=\lambda_{2n}(\Sigma^{{}^{\prime}})=-(2n-1).
And
\lambda_{1}(B)=\lambda_{1}(K_{n})=n-1.

So,
R=\frac{-\lambda_{2n}(\Sigma)-\lambda_{1}(B)}{\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)+\lambda_{1}(B)}=\frac{(2n-1)-(n-1)}{1+(n-1)}=1.

Case (ii):
For
G=C_{n};~~\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)=\lambda_{1}(\Sigma^{{}^{\prime}})=n-2,~\lambda_{2n}(\Sigma)=\lambda_{2n}(\Sigma^{{}^{\prime}})=-n-2.
And
\lambda_{1}(B)=\lambda_{1}(C_{n})=2.
So,
R=\frac{-\lambda_{2n}(\Sigma)-\lambda_{1}(B)}{\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)+\lambda_{1}(B)}=\frac{(n+2)-2}{(n-2)+2}=1.
Clearly
\chi(\Sigma)=2
and hence the equality holds.

The proofs of (2) and (3) are straightforward.
