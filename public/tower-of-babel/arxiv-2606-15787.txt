Ferronematics: integrality of the limiting interface in the strong coupling regime
Federico Luigi Dipasquale, Yoshihiro Tonegawa

Abstract
We consider a vectorial energy functional proposed in the physical literature as a simplified model for thin films of ferronematics — composite materials formed by dispersing magnetic nanoparticles into a nematic liquid crystals host. The model features two order parameters: a reduced 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} -tensor field 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} , describing the nematic liquid crystal component, and a vector field 𝐌 \mathbf{M} , accounting for the average polarisation vector field generated by the included particles. The energy contains a coupling term promoting alignment between 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} and 𝐌 \mathbf{M} . It has been shown in [ 13 , 14 ] that, as a small parameter ε \varepsilon tends to zero, the energy of critical pairs ( 𝐐 ε , 𝐌 ε ) (\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon},\,\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}) concentrates on distinct singular sets: a finite set of points for the 𝐐 ε \mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon} -component and the support of a ℋ 1 \mathscr{H}^{1} -rectifiable varifold for the 𝐌 ε \mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon} -component, with first variation supported on the singular set for the 𝐐 ε \mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon} -component. We show in this paper that, if the coupling constant is above a certain (explicit) threshold, then, up to rescaling its density by a material constant, such a limiting varifold has integer multiplicity. Keywords: Ginzburg-Landau functional, Allen-Cahn equation, vectorial problems, topological singularities, rectifiable sets. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35Q56 ⋅ \cdot 76A15 ⋅ \cdot 49Q15 ⋅ \cdot 26B30



Introduction

Over the past few decades, much effort has been devoted to the study of
the Ginzburg-Landau functional of superconductivity
and to the Allen-Cahn functional of phase separation,
especially concerning the asymptotic behaviour, as a small
parameter
\varepsilon
tends to zero, of their
minimising and non-minimising critical points.
Seminal works in these fields, such as, for instance,

[
4
,
5
,
1
,
22
,
33
,
25
,
36
,
21
,
19
,
6
]
,
revealed the emergence of gradient-driven singular structures of codimension 2
(in the case of Ginzburg-Landau theory) and codimension 1 (in the
Allen-Cahn case) as the sets where the energy of minimisers and
critical points concentrates, at first order, as
\varepsilon
tends to zero.
From the point of view of geometric measure theory, such
energy-concentration phenomena are extremely interesting, as they provide
a mechanism to construct non-trivial integer-multiplicity rectifiable
currents minimising the energy in an appropriate homology class and
stationary varifolds of codimension 1 and 2, both in the
Euclidean and in the Riemannian context
(e.g.,
[
3
,
27
,
20
,
10
,
11
,
30
,
29
,
16
,
31
,
23
]
).
Moreover, Pacard and Ritoré
[
28
]
and De Philippis and Pigati
[
16
]
have shown that
any non-degenerate minimal submanifold of codimension 1 or 2 of a Riemannian
manifold can be recovered as the energy-concentration set of critical
points of the Allen-Cahn functional, if of codimension 1 (see
[
28
]
), or of
the Ginzburg-Landau functional, if of codimension 2 (see
[
16
]
).

While the Ginzburg-Landau functional is inherently vectorial,
the Allen-Cahn functional makes sense for both scalar and vectorial order parameters,
depending on the application at hand (for instance, in the case of three or more
phases, the vectorial theory must be used).
The asymptotic behaviour of minimisers has been studied within the framework of

\Gamma
-convergence, both in the scalar and the vectorial
case
[
25
,
36
,
17
,
2
]
.
However, when coming to the analysis of the asymptotic behaviour of
(non-minimising, in general) critical points,
the scalar and the vectorial theories are profoundly different.
The asymptotic behaviour of general critical points in the scalar case was investigated
by Hutchinson and the second author
[
19
]

through a careful PDE analysis
in which a key role is played by the so-called
discrepancy function
,
defined as the difference between the potential energy density and the elastic
energy density. By cleverly exploiting the maximum principle, Modica
[
25
]

showed that the discrepancy of entire scalar solutions is pointwise non-negative, already
at the
\varepsilon
-level. In
[
19
]
, it is proved that,
in bounded domains, the discrepancy vanishes asymptotically in the
L^{1}
-sense
for every sequence of critical points with equibounded energy. In the vectorial case,
however, there is no reason for these properties to hold (in fact,
an example of entire solution whose discrepancy changes sign has been constructed in
[
35
]
)
and the asymptotic behaviour of non-minimising critical points has eluded precise
description up to the recent breakthrough paper
[
6
]
by Bethuel,
in which new PDEs tools for the Allen-Cahn system on two-dimensional
domains has been developed
that avoid the discrepancy function and are instead ultimately based on refined
energy estimates.

In this paper we are concerned with a model for
ferronematics
which couples
the Ginzburg-Landau theory with the vectorial Allen-Cahn theory. Ferronematics are composite materials obtained by dispersing magnetic nanoparticles into a nematic
liquid crystal hosts. The physics of such systems, firstly devised theoretically
by Brochard and de Gennes in 1970
[
9
]
, and then realised experimentally
in various stages (see, e.g.,
[
24
]
and references therein) is very rich,
and the object of ongoing investigation.

A simplified model for thin films of ferronematics in the so-called

superdilute regime
(in which the density of magnetic particles
is relatively low with respect to that of nematic molecules)
has been proposed in
[
8
]
. The model features
two order parameters: a reduced
\mathbf{Q}
-tensor field associated with the liquid crystal host,
which is a map
\mathbf{Q}
from the physical domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
\mathscr{S}_{0}^{2\times 2}
,
the space of
2\times 2
, symmetric, real matrices with trace equal to zero,
and the average polarisation vector field
\mathbf{M}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

generated by the included particles. After non-dimensionalisation, the model can be described as follows.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open, bounded, and simply connected set with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, let
\varepsilon>0
be a small parameter, and let

G\subseteq\Omega
be any measurable set. We define

(1)

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M};\,G):=\int_{G}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\mathbf{Q}\right|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\nabla\mathbf{M}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M})\right\}\,{\mathrm{d}}x,

where the potential energy density
f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M})
is given by

(2)

f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M}):=\frac{1}{4}\left(\left|\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\left(\left|\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}-\varepsilon\beta\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M}+\chi_{\varepsilon}.

Here,
\beta
is a (strictly) positive parameter depending only on the material and the additive constant
\chi_{\varepsilon}
ensures that
\inf f_{\varepsilon}=0
for each
\varepsilon>0
.

The mathematical study of the functional
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}

started in
[
15
]
and then continued in
[
13
,
14
,
12
]
,
encompassing the asymptotic behaviour as
\varepsilon\to 0

of both
minimising and non-minimising critical points under suitable boundary conditions.
The limit
\varepsilon\to 0
should be understood as a large domain limit
(the size of the domain is much larger than the typical correlation length for
the liquid crystal molecules, cf.
[
15
,
13
]
).

In this paper, we refine some of the results obtained in
[
13
,
14
]

for (non-minimising, in general) critical points.
To motivate the interest towards the study of non-minimising critical points, we
recall that, while in variational theories for physical systems observable configurations are usually identified minimisers or local minimisers
of the energy,
it has been argued on the basis of numerically and experimentally observed cases
(see, e.g.,
[
18
]
for a broader discussion) that non-minimising critical
points show up naturally, for instance, when the system decays from a higher to a lower energy state.
Moreover, numerical simulations
[
8
,
15
]
show an abundance of critical points of the functional (
1
).

As in
[
13
,
14
]
,
we assume to deal with sequences of (non-minimising, in general)
critical pairs
(\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon},\,\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon})
of
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega

and that the boundary data are
\varepsilon
-independent maps

\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega,\,\mathscr{S}_{0}^{2\times 2})
,
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{bd}}\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega,\,\mathbb{R}^{2})
that satisfy either the ‘pure’ Dirichlet boundary conditions

(3)

\left|\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{bd}}\right|=\left(\sqrt{2}\beta+1\right)^{1/2},\qquad\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}=\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{bd}}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{bd}}}{\sqrt{2}\beta+1}-\frac{\mathbf{I}}{2}\right)

or the ‘mixed’ boundary conditions

(4)

\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}=\sqrt{2}\left(\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{bd}}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{bd}}-\frac{\mathbf{I}}{2}\right),\qquad\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}=0,

where
\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{bd}}\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega,\,\mathbb{S}^{1})
is an assigned,

\varepsilon
-independent vector field. Finally, we assume that
there exists a positive constant
{\rm C}_{\rm pot}
such that, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

(5)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f(\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon},\,\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq{\rm C}_{\rm pot}.

Sufficient conditions for (
5
) are provided in
[
13
, Remark 1]
.

The Euler-Lagrange equations of the functional
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
are

(6)

\displaystyle-\Delta\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left|\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-1\right)\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon}\left(\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\left|\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}}{2}\mathbf{I}\right)=0,

(7)

\displaystyle-\Delta\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left|\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-1\right)\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{2\beta}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}=0.

Under the above assumptions, upon using (
6
), (
7
),
it was shown in
[
13
]
that

(8)

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon},\,\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon})\leq C\left|\log\varepsilon\right|,\qquad\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq C\left|\log\varepsilon\right|,\qquad\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq C,

where the constant
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

As in
[
13
,
14
]
, we define the energy densities

(9)

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\nabla\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon},\,\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon})\right),

(10)

\displaystyle\nu_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\nabla\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon},\,\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}).

From the assumption (
5
) and the energy
bounds (
8
),
the sequences of positive functions
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
,
\{\nu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
are bounded in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, hence we can find limiting Radon measures
\mu_{\star}
and
\nu_{\star}
in
\Omega
such that, after possible extraction of a subsequence,

\mu_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\mu_{\star},\qquad\nu_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\nu_{\star}\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0

in the sense of Radon measures in
\Omega
.
In the limit,
distinct
(but related) gradient-driven singular
structures show up.
Indeed, it is shown in
[
13
]
that
\spt\mu_{\star}
is a finite
set of points and in
[
14
]
that
\spt\nu_{\star}
is a union of
segments with locally constant density. The relationship between

\spt\mu_{\star}
and
\spt\nu_{\star}
is expressed by item
3

of Theorem
0
below and, in a more precise way, by

[
14
, Theorem C]
.

These results are part of the outcome of a broader analysis on
the systems (
6
), (
7
).
At first sight, both (
6
), (
7
) look like perturbed
Ginzburg-Landau systems, and this is true for (
6
), although
the relatively large perturbation term makes the analysis in
[
13
]
quite
delicate. However, as shown in
[
14
]
, the structure of (

) is
the one of a perturbed Allen-Cahn system.
To see this, which is crucial to our purposes, we argue as in
[
14
]
and, for any given pair
(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M})\in\mathscr{S}_{0}^{2\times 2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
we define the functions

(11)

\ell(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M}):=\frac{1}{4}\left(\left|\mathbf{M}\right|^{2}-1\right)^{2}-\beta\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta^{2}+\sqrt{2}\beta\right),

and

(12)

V(\mathbf{M}):=\ell(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M})-\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ell(\mathbf{Q},\,y).

As shown in
[
13
, Lemma 1.2]
, for any given
\mathbf{Q}\neq 0
,
the function
\mathbf{M}\mapsto V(\mathbf{M})
has exactly two zeroes, at
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}_{\pm}
, where

\mathbf{M}_{\pm}:=\pm\left(1+\sqrt{2}\beta\left|\mathbf{Q}\right|\right)^{1/2}\mathbf{n},

and where
\mathbf{n}
is a unit vector of
\mathbf{Q}
corresponding to its positive eigenvalue.

Denoting now
\mathbf{Q}:\Omega\to\mathscr{S}_{0}^{2\times 2}
a
\mathbf{Q}
-tensor field,

\mathbf{M}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
a vector field, and
G\subseteq\Omega

a measurable set, we define the energy functional

(13)

E_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{M};\,G):=\int_{G}\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\nabla\mathbf{M}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}V(\mathbf{M})\right\}\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

By construction, if
(\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon},\,\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon})
is a critical point of
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
,
then
\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation of
E_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.,

(14)

-\varepsilon\Delta\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\nabla_{\mathbf{M}}V(\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon})=0\qquad\mbox{in }\Omega.

It can be easily checked that (cf.
[
14
, Proposition 2.3]
) that

(15)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}E_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}<+\infty,

where the constant
\mathcal{E}_{0}
depends only on
\Omega
,
\beta
,
and the
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
- and
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
-norm of

\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}\times\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}
.

The point of introducing the auxiliary functional
E_{\varepsilon}
is that
the Equation (
14
) coincides
exactly
with (
7
),
so that both
E_{\varepsilon}
and (
14
) display an Allen-Cahn
structure. However, the results of
[
6
]

cannot
be applied directly,
because the wells of
V
are not fixed, but
move
with both
x
and
\varepsilon

due to their dependence on
\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}
. Nonetheless, one can still follow the strategy
of
[
6
]
, combining it with the estimates and convergence results for
the
\mathbf{Q}
-component from
[
13
]
to control the perturbation terms.
Thus, upon defining the potential energy densities

(16)

\zeta_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}V(\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}),

which, due to (
15
), are equibounded in
L^{1}(\Omega)
,
one has

(17)

\zeta_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\zeta_{\star},\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

and the following theorem has been obtained in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 0

(
[

)
.

The set
\spt\nu_{\star}
is
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-rectifiable, with locally
finite measure. Upon setting

(18)

\mathfrak{S}_{\star}:=\spt\nu_{\star}\setminus\spt\mu_{\star},

the following holds.

1.

The limiting potential energy measure
\zeta_{\star}
is absolutely
continuous with respect to the measure
\mathscr{H}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\mathfrak{S}_{\star}
.
In other words, there exists a function
\mathfrak{v}_{\star}:\mathfrak{S}_{\star}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
locally integrable with respect to the measure
\mathscr{H}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\mathfrak{S}_{\star}
,
such that

\zeta_{\star}=\mathfrak{v}_{\star}\,\mathscr{H}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\mathfrak{S}_{\star}.

The function
\mathfrak{v}_{\star}
is locally bounded both from above and
from below in any compact set
K\subset\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
.

2.

The measure
\zeta_{\star}
is the weight measure of the
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-rectifiable varifold

\mathbb{V}_{\star}
carried by
\mathfrak{S}_{\star}
with density function
\mathfrak{v}_{\star}
.

3.

The varifold
\mathbb{V}_{\star}
is stationary in
\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
and
its first variation as a varifold in
\Omega
is concentrated on
\spt\mu_{\star}
.

4.

The set
\spt\nu_{\star}
is locally a union of segments, open relative to
\Omega
,
each of which having constant density, given by the value of
\mathfrak{v}_{\star}
at any
point of the segment. In addition, apart from an exceptional,
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-null set, around any point
x_{0}
of

\mathfrak{S}_{\star}
, the singular set
\spt\nu_{\star}
consists of exactly one segment,
with constant density
\mathfrak{v}_{\star}(x_{0})
.

5.

As

,
\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbf{M}_{\star}
in

.

Notice that Theorem
0
says nothing about the multiplicity of the
limiting varifold
\mathbb{V}_{\star}
.
We recall that, in the scalar Allen-Cahn theory
[
19
]
, the
integrality of the limiting interface where the energy critical points concentrates
as
\varepsilon\to 0
is a characteristic feature, even for the
perturbed equation, as first shown in
[
37
,
38
]
for Sobolev type perturbations and then, in
[
26
,
32
]
for
L^{2}
type perturbations.
By way of contrast, integrality is generally

false
in the vectorial case
[
6
,
7
]
, and hence
it requires specific analysis, depending on the particular form of the
potential function at hand.
Our main result in this paper, Theorem
1
below,
establishes that in the regime

\sqrt{2}\beta>1

the varifold
\mathbb{V}_{\star}
has, after rescaling by the constant
\sigma_{\beta}

in (

Theorem 1
.

Let
\zeta_{\star}=\mathfrak{v}_{\star}\,\mathscr{H}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\mathfrak{S}_{\star}

be the limiting potential energy density and let

\mathbb{V}_{\star}=\mathbf{v}(\mathfrak{S}_{\star},\,\mathfrak{v}_{\star})
be the
varifold associated with
\zeta_{\star}
according to Theorem
0
.
Let

(19)

\sigma_{\beta}:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\left(1+\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{3/2}

and assume that
\sqrt{2}\beta>1
. Then, the varifold

\sigma_{\beta}^{-1}\mathbb{V}_{*}:=\left(\mathfrak{S_{*}},\,\frac{\mathfrak{v}_{\star}}{\sigma_{\beta}}\right)

has integer multiplicity. In other words, the function
\sigma_{\beta}^{-1}\mathfrak{v}_{\star}
takes its values into
\mathbb{N}
.

The condition
\sqrt{2}\beta>1
ensures such a fast, quantitative decay
with respect to
\varepsilon
of the
component of
\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}
in the direction orthogonal to the eigenspace
\mathbf{n}_{\varepsilon}
of

\mathbf{Q}_{\varepsilon}
relative to its positive eigenvalue, that this component
cannot
concentrate energy in the
limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
This fast decay is obtained through the study of the equation satisfied by the orthogonal
component
u_{2,\varepsilon}:=\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{\perp}_{\varepsilon}
of
\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}
,
which undergoes a structural change exactly
when
\sqrt{2}\beta>1
, see (
2.3
) below.
As a result,
although the problem remains fully vectorial at the
\varepsilon
-level,
in the regime
\sqrt{2}\beta>1
it is
quantitatively
close (in terms
of
\varepsilon
) to the
scalar problem obtained by formally neglecting the component

u_{2,\varepsilon}
of
\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}
.
More precisely, this quantitive decay allows us to look at the equation satisfied by

u_{1,\varepsilon}:=\mathbf{M}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\varepsilon}
as a
perturbed
Allen-Cahn equation of
the type considered in
[
26
]
and, thus, to infer integrality from the
results in
[
26
]
.

Theorem
1
is, to the best of our knowledge, the first
integrality result for the limiting varifold obtained in a vectorial Allen-Cahn problem,
at least for a non-trivial (i.e., not decoupled already at the
\varepsilon
-level),
physically relevant potential energy, and for non-minimising solutions.
In particular, it shows that integrality can occur
if the potential energy singles out a preferred direction, as it could be
intuitively expected. On the other
hand, even in this seemingly easy setting,
proving that the components along the orthogonal directions decay sufficiently
fast turns out to be a quite technical matter.

It would be
interesting to further study the behaviour of the multiplicity function

\mathfrak{v}_{\star}
in the opposite regime
\sqrt{2}\beta\leq 1
.
Particularly
intriguing would be a situation in which integrality does not hold in such a regime
(or ceases to be true below a certain threshold).
Indeed, besides the specific interest of such a deep structural change in this
particular problem, it is conceivable that the phenomena leading to the breakdown
and the arguments involved in proving its occurrence
could be helpful to the long-term goal of determining
sufficient conditions for integrality in the vectorial Allen-Cahn problem.

1.1
Varifolds

We recall some basic terminology, as needed in this paper,

A

G_{k}(U):=U\times G(n,\,k)

-dimensional
planes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
weight measure

\left\|\mathbb{V}\right\|
of
\mathbb{V}

is the Radon measure defined by setting

\int_{U}\phi(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\left\|\mathbb{V}\right\|(x):=\int_{G_{k}(U)}\phi(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{V}(x,\,S),

for all
\phi\in C_{c}(U)
.
A
k
-varifold is
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-rectifiable

if there exist a countably
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-rectifiable set
\mathfrak{S}
and
a locally
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-integrable function

\theta\colon\mathfrak{S}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
(called
density function

or
multiplicity
) such that

\int_{G_{k}(U)}\varphi(x,\,S)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{V}(x,\,S)=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\varphi(x,\,\mathrm{T}_{x}\mathfrak{S})\,\theta(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathscr{H}^{k}(x)

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}(G_{k}(U))
, where
\mathrm{T}_{x}\mathfrak{S}
denotes
the approximate tangent space to
\mathfrak{S}
at
x\in\mathfrak{S}
.
Following the common convention, we extend
\theta
as
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathfrak{S}
.
Thus, if
\mathbb{V}
is
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-rectifiable, then

\left\|\mathbb{V}\right\|=\theta\mathscr{H}^{k}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\mathfrak{S}.

If
\theta
takes only integer values, then
\mathbb{V}
is called

integral
.

Two pairs
(\mathfrak{S},\,\theta)
and

(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime},\,\theta^{\prime})
identify the same
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-rectifiable varifold
if and only if the symmetric difference
\mathfrak{S}\Delta\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}

is a
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-null set and
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
on
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-almost all of

\mathfrak{S}\cap\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
.
These conditions define an equivalence relation,
so that rectifiable varifolds are actually equivalence classes of
pairs
(\mathfrak{S},\,\theta)
.
We will write
\mathbb{V}=\mathbf{v}(\mathfrak{S},\,\theta)

for the rectifiable varifold carried by
\mathfrak{S}

with density function
\theta

(see e.g.
[
34
, Chapter 4]
).
The
first variation
of a varifold
\mathbb{V}

is the distribution
\delta\mathbb{V}
in
U
satisfying

\delta\mathbb{V}(\mathbf{X}):=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\div_{\mathrm{T}_{x}\mathfrak{S}}\mathbf{X}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathscr{H}^{k}(x)

for all
\mathbf{X}\in C^{1}_{c}(U,\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If it happens that
\delta{\mathbb{V}}(\mathbf{X})=0

for any
\mathbf{X}\in C^{1}_{c}(U,\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\mathbb{V}
is said to be

in

.

1.2
The zero set of the potential energy and the material constant
\kappa_{\star}

It can be checked (see
[

, Lemma B.2]
) that,
for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M})=0
if and only

(\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{M})=(\mathbf{Q}^{\rm pot}_{\varepsilon},\,\mathbf{M}^{\rm pot}_{\varepsilon})

\left|\mathbf{M}^{\rm pot}_{\varepsilon}\right|=\lambda_{\varepsilon,\beta},\qquad\mathbf{Q}^{\rm pot}_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{2}s_{\varepsilon,\beta}\left(\frac{\mathbf{M}^{\rm pot}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\mathbf{M}^{\rm pot}_{\varepsilon}}{\lambda_{\varepsilon,\beta}^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{I}}{2}\right),

Notice that

Here,

is a material-dependent, dimensionless constant, measuring the strength of the coupling
between
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{M}
in terms of how much the interaction lifts the ground level
of the potential at first order in
\varepsilon
from what it would be with the interaction switched off.

Given boundary data as in (
3
) or as in (
4
),
one easily computes that

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}},\,\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{bd}})=\kappa_{\star}^{2}+\mathrm{o}_{\varepsilon\to 0}(\varepsilon^{2}).

Step 1

Step 2
.

Let

\begin{split}\int_{B}\zeta^{2}\left\{\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{\rho-1}{\varepsilon}-\kappa_{\star}\right)^{2}\right\}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq&3\int_{B}\zeta^{2}(\sigma-2\kappa_{\star})^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+12\varepsilon^{2}\int_{B}\zeta^{2}\rho^{2}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{4}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\

From (

Next, by (

Step 1

(Basic bounds)

B=B_{1},\qquad B^{\prime}=B_{1/2},\qquad B^{\prime\prime}=B_{1/4}

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{B}\left(\frac{\rho-1}{\varepsilon}-\kappa_{\star}\right)^{2}u_{1}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{B}\left(\frac{\rho-1}{\varepsilon}-\kappa_{\star}\right)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq C_{\beta}(x_{0},\,R).

Next, as anticipated in (
3.4
), we set

\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}:=\lambda_{\varepsilon}u_{1,\varepsilon},\qquad\lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}\beta}{1+\sqrt{2\beta}+\varepsilon\sqrt{2}\beta\kappa_{\star}}\right)^{1/2}.

Rewriting (
3.10
) in terms of
\widetilde{u}=\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
,
we obtain

(3.12)

\begin{split}-\varepsilon\Delta\widetilde{u}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{u}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)\widetilde{u}=&\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{u}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)\widetilde{u}\\
&+\sqrt{2}\beta\lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\rho-1}{\varepsilon}-\kappa_{\star}\right)\widetilde{u}\\
&+\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}f_{\varepsilon}(u_{1},\,u_{2}).\end{split}

By a straightforward computation, we have

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\right)^{2}\int_{B}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\widetilde{u}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{2}\widetilde{u}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\int_{B}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{u}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{2}\widetilde{u}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

Recalling that
\left|\mathbf{u}\right|^{2}
is uniformly bounded depending only on
\beta
,
that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and that
\kappa_{\star}=\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(1+\sqrt{2}\beta\right)
,
we see that

(3.13)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\right)^{2}\int_{B}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\widetilde{u}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{2}\widetilde{u}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq C(\beta)\int_{B}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}h(\mathbf{u})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:h-bis}}}{{\leq}}C_{\beta}(x_{0},\,R),

where the constant
C(\beta)
depends only on
\beta
and
C_{\beta}(x_{0},\,R)

depends only on
\beta
,
\gamma
,
x_{0}
,
R
,
\Omega
,
{\rm C}_{\rm pot}
,
and the
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
- and the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
-norm
of
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}\times\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}
.
Upon setting

(3.14)

F_{\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{u},\,u_{2}\right):=\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\widetilde{u}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)\widetilde{u}+\sqrt{2}\beta\lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\rho-1}{\varepsilon}-\kappa_{\star}\right)\widetilde{u}+f_{\varepsilon}(u_{1},\,u_{2}).

from (
3.7
), (
3.11
), and (
3.13
)
we see that

(3.15)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{B}F_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq C_{\beta}(x_{0},\,R),

where the constant
C_{\beta}(x_{0},\,R)
depends only on

\beta
,
\gamma
,
x_{0}
,
R
,
\Omega
,
{\rm C}_{\rm pot}
,
and the
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
- and the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
-norm
of
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}\times\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}
.
Finally, let
K\subset\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
be any compact set.
Then, by (
3.15
) and a standard covering argument, we have

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{K}F_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq C_{\beta,K}

where the constant
C_{\beta,K}
, depends only on
\beta
,
K
,

\Omega
,
{\rm C}_{\rm pot}
, and the
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
- and the

L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
-norm of
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}\times\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{bd}}
.
This proves (
3.5
).
Combining (
3.12
) and (
3.14
),
we obtain (
3.3
). This concludes
the proof of the claim.

Step 2

(Inferring integrality from
[
26
]
, locally in
\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
)
.

Let
K\subset\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
be any compact set.
We observe that Equation (
3.3
) has the same form
as
[
26
, Equation (1.2)]
with
F_{\varepsilon}
that, as a function
of
x\in K
, satisfies
[
26
, Assumption (A.2)]
.
By the definition of
\widetilde{u}_{1,\varepsilon}
, the energy
bound
[
26
, (A.1)]
is satisfied because
of (
1.25
) and (
3.20
) below.
We consider the energy densities
(since at this point the component
u_{2}
has been ruled out, we further
simplify the notation writing
u
in place of
u_{1}
)

\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\varepsilon\left|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\left(\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{2}}{4\varepsilon},

as well as

\eta_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\varepsilon\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\left(u_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{2}}{4\varepsilon}.

In view of the definition (
3.4
) of
\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
,
for every
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(3.16)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\zeta}^{(h_{1})}_{\varepsilon}-\zeta^{h_{1}}_{\varepsilon}&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(h_{1}(\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon})-h_{1}(u_{\varepsilon}))\\
&=\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\left\{\left(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}u^{2}_{\varepsilon}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{2}-\left(u^{2}_{\varepsilon}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{2}\right\}\\
&=\frac{u_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\left\{\left(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1\right)^{2}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}+2\left(1-\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)^{2}\left(u_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)\right\},\end{split}

so that, by the definition of
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.4
)
and (
1.25
),

(3.17)

\int_{K}\left|\widetilde{\zeta}^{(h_{1})}_{\varepsilon}-\zeta^{(h_{1})}_{\varepsilon}\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\to 0,\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

On the other hand,

(3.18)

\left|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}=\left(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1\right)\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2},

whence

(3.19)

\int_{K}\left(\left|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\to 0,\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

From (
3.17
)
and (
3.19
) it follows that

(3.20)

\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}-\eta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\lambda\left(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1\right)\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-\frac{u^{2}_{\varepsilon}}{4\varepsilon}\left\{\left(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1\right)^{2}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}+2\left(1-\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)^{2}\left(u_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)\right\},

and thus

(3.21)

\int_{K}\left|\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}-\eta_{\varepsilon}\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\to 0,\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Since the sequences of positive functions

\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon>0}
,

\left(\eta_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon>0}
are bounded in
L^{1}(K)
,
for every
K\subset\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
, by standard compactness
properties of Radon measures, they
converge in the sense of Radon measures along a subsequence to limiting measures

\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}
,
\eta_{\star}
defined on the whole
\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
.
In view of (
3.21
), we have

(3.22)

\eta_{\star}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}K=\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}K

independently of the chosen subsequence.

Theorem 4.1 in
[
26
]
gives that
\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}
is

\mathscr{H}^{1}
-rectifiable, i.e.,

\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}=\widetilde{\theta}_{\star}\mathscr{H}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Sigma
,
where
\Sigma
is
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-rectifiable and
\widetilde{\theta}_{\star}

denotes the density of
\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}
. In particular, the limit

\widetilde{\theta}_{\star}(x_{0}):=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}(B(x_{0},\,r))}{2r}

exists at any point
x_{0}\in\Sigma
. Since rectifiability
is a local property, the rectifiability of
\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}

and (
3.22
) imply
that
\eta_{\star}
is rectifiable as well and, moreover, that

\theta_{\star}(x_{0})=\widetilde{\theta}_{\star}(x_{0}),\qquad\forall x_{0}\in K.

Furthermore, if we set

\sigma_{\beta}:=\int_{-\sqrt{1+\sqrt{2}\beta}}^{\sqrt{1+\sqrt{2}\beta}}\sqrt{\frac{h_{1}(s)}{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\left(1+\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{3/2},

then applying
[
26
, Theorem 5.1]
and
using (
3.16
)
and (
3.18
) leads to the fact that

(2\sigma_{\beta})^{-1}{\theta_{\star}(x_{0})}
is an integer for all
x_{0}\in K
.
In addition, thanks to (
3.17
)
and (
3.18
),
[
26
, Proposition 4.3]
tells
us that the discrepancy functions

\xi_{\varepsilon}^{(h_{1})}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}h_{1}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}

satisfy

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{K}\left|\xi^{(h_{1})}_{\varepsilon}\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0,

i.e., equipartition holds in the limit. More precisely, if we define
the density of the potential energy functions (dually seen as measures)

(3.23)

{\zeta}_{\varepsilon}^{(h_{1})}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}h_{1}\left({u}_{\varepsilon}\right),

we see that there exists a limiting potential energy measure

{\zeta}^{(h_{1})}_{\star}
, which satisfies

{\eta}_{\star}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}K=2{\zeta}_{\star}^{(h_{1})}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}K,

so that, in particular,

\spt\left({\eta}_{\star}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}K\right)=\spt\left({\zeta}^{(h_{1})}_{\star}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}K\right).

Moreover, the density
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\star}(x_{0})

of
{\zeta}_{\star}^{(h_{1})}

exists at every point
x_{0}\in K
and satisfies

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{{\zeta}^{(h_{1})}_{\star}(B(x_{0},\,r))}{2r}={\mathfrak{h}}_{\star}(x_{0})=\frac{{\theta}_{\star}(x_{0})}{2},

so that
\sigma_{\beta}^{-1}{\mathfrak{h}}_{\star}(x_{0})
is an integer,
for any
x_{0}\in K
.
Finally, by letting
K
vary in

\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
, we obtain

(3.24)

\sigma_{\beta}^{-1}\mathfrak{h}_{\star}(x_{0})\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\forall x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}.

Step 3

(Conclusion)
.

In order to conclude the proof, we have to show that the
integrality of the rescaled density
\sigma_{\beta}^{-1}\mathfrak{h}_{\star}

implies that the density
\mathfrak{v}_{\star}
of
\zeta_{\star}

is integer-valued as well after rescaling, i.e, that

\sigma_{\beta}^{-1}\mathfrak{v}_{\star}
takes only integer values.
In fact, we are going to prove that

(3.25)

\mathfrak{v}_{\star}(x_{0})=\mathfrak{h}_{\star}(x_{0}),\qquad\forall x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}.

To this purpose, it suffices to
show that the difference
\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{\zeta}^{(h_{1})}_{\varepsilon}

tends to zero in
L^{1}\left(K\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
By the definition (
16
) of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
, the
definition (
3.23
) of

{\zeta}^{(h_{1})}_{\varepsilon}
, (
1.32
), and (
1.28
), we have

(3.26)

\begin{split}\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\zeta^{(h_{1})}_{\varepsilon}&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(h(\mathbf{u})+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}(1-\rho)\left(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2}-1-\frac{\beta+\beta\rho}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}h_{1}(u_{1})\\
&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}h_{2}(u_{1},\,u_{2})+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\varepsilon}\right)\left(u_{1}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\varepsilon}\right)\left(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}(\rho-1)-u_{2}^{2}\right)\end{split}

All terms on the right-hand side of (
3.26
)
vanish in
L^{1}\left(K\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Indeed, by (
3.1
) (recall that
h_{2}(u_{1},\,u_{2})\geq 0
pointwise,
if
\sqrt{2}\beta\geq 1
) and a covering argument,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{K}h_{2}(u_{1},\,u_{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}x\lesssim\varepsilon^{\alpha},\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

for every
\alpha\in(0,\,2)
. Next, by Hölder’s inequality
and Lemma
1.1
,

\begin{split}\int_{K}\left|\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\varepsilon}\right)\left(u_{1}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)\right|&\leq\left(\int_{K}\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{K}\left(u_{1}^{2}-1-\sqrt{2}\beta\right)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\right)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon},\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0.\end{split}

Finally, by
[
13
, Proposition 2.6]
and
Proposition
2.1
,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{K}\left\{(\rho-1)^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\right\}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\lesssim\varepsilon,\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Combining the last three inequalities with (
3.26
),
it follows that

\int_{K}\left|\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{(h_{1})}\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}x\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon},\qquad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

As a consequence,

\zeta_{\star}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}K=\zeta_{\star}^{(h_{1})}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}K.

In particular,

\mathfrak{v}_{\star}=\mathfrak{h}_{\star}\qquad\mbox{on }K.

Since
K
was arbitrary in
\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
,
by letting
K
vary in
\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
, we have

\mathfrak{v}_{\star}(x_{0})=\mathfrak{h}_{\star}(x_{0}),\qquad\forall x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star},

i.e., we have proved (
3.25
).
In particular, by (
3.25
) and (
3.24
),
it follows that
\sigma_{\beta}^{-1}\mathfrak{v}_{\star}
is an integer-valued
function on
\Omega\setminus\spt\mu_{\star}
.
Finally, the conclusion follows because, by
[
14
, Proposition 3.18(ii)]
,

\mathfrak{v}_{\star}(a)=0
for every
a\in\spt\mu_{\star}
.

∎
