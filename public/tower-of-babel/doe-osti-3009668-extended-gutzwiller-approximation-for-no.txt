 Abstract
 Understanding electron-electron and electron-photon correlations is central to un-

 covering the fundamental mechanisms governing material properties, particularly in

 systems where strong interactions give rise to emergent phenomena such as supercon-

 ductivity, magnetism, and polaritonic effects. These correlations play a pivotal role in

 cavity quantum materials, where hybridized light-matter states enable quantum con-

 trol over electronic properties. However, capturing both local and nonlocal correlations

 in these systems presents a significant theoretical challenge. In this work, we extend

 the Gutzwiller wavefunction method to include nonlocal electron-photon and electron-

 electron interactions, providing a unified framework to study the intricate interplay

 between these effects. Our approach accurately captures the long-range correlations

 induced by photon exchange, enabling the exploration of exotic quantum phases and

 the effects of cavity coupling on electronic structure. By benchmarking the method

 across coupling regimes, we reveal the critical role of nonlocal correlations in stabilizing

 phases, such as superconducting and insulating states, that are inaccessible through

 local interactions alone. This generalized Gutzwiller framework offers a versatile tool

 for understanding and designing materials that harness the transformative potential

 of strong light-matter coupling.

1 Introduction
The study of electron-photon interactions has opened up a new frontier in materials science,
mainly through the development of cavity quantum materials [1]. In these systems, strong
coupling between photons and electronic states within an optical or microwave cavity enables
the controlled manipulation of material properties, presenting unprecedented opportunities
to induce or enhance phenomena like superconductivity, magnetism, and topological order.
When photons interact strongly with electrons, the resulting hybrid states—known as polari-
tons—can modify materials’ electronic and optical properties, creating new quantum phases
that may not exist outside the light-matter hybridized regime. Light-induced tuning of elec-
tronic properties has transformative implications, providing pathways to design quantum
materials with properties traditionally unattainable by purely electronic interactions .
 At the heart of this field lies a growing need for theoretical methods capable of accu-
rately capturing the complex interplay between electronic correlations and photon-mediated
interactions. Even in the absence of light-matter interaction, studying strongly correlated
electrons remains one of condensed matter physics’s most challenging and exciting fron-
tiers. The light-matter interactions add another layer of complexity, characterized by strong
interactions between their constituent particles, giving rise to many emergent phenomena
that conventional mean-field theories cannot adequately describe. The interplay between
electron-electron and electron-boson interactions is crucial in numerous physical systems,
from high-temperature superconductors to polaron formation in materials. While tradi-
tional approaches have successfully described electron-electron interactions and, to a limited
extent, electron-phonon interactions, they often fall short in the realm of electron-photon
coupling, particularly in capturing the nonlocal correlation effects that photons induce across
a material.
 Traditionally, the Gutzwiller Wavefunction (GWF) under the Gutzwiller Approxima-
tion (GA)[2] has been a powerful tool for investigating strongly correlated electron systems,
particularly in capturing local electron correlations [3]. However, its conventional formula-
tion is limited in addressing the complex interplay between electrons and bosons, especially
when considering inter-site correlations. This limitation becomes particularly apparent when
studying systems where electron-electron and electron-boson interactions are significant and
cannot be treated separately. Though there is an extension of the GA method to handle
electron-phonon interactions in strongly correlated systems, these extensions typically treat
only local correlations due to the short-range nature of lattice vibrations. Photons, in con-
trast, mediate correlations over extended spatial ranges, demanding a more comprehensive
approach to incorporate these long-range effects. Even in the absence of light-matter inter-
action, inter-site correlations are crucial in many strongly correlated systems. A generalized
approach would allow for a more accurate description of spatial correlation effects, often

 2

oversimplified in local approximations.
 This work addresses this challenge by extending the Gutzwiller wavefunction method to
capture strong electron-photon interactions, incorporating local and nonlocal correlations
in a unified framework. This development enables the study of polaritonic effects in cor-
related materials with unprecedented accuracy, offering a versatile tool to explore the rich
phase space of cavity quantum materials. Our method provides a generalized framework
equally applicable to other electron-boson interactions, establishing a universal approach
that bridges the physics of electron-phonon and electron-photon coupling. This framework
addresses a fundamental limitation in existing Gutzwiller approaches by explicitly account-
ing for the spatially extended correlations induced by photon exchange. The theoretical
formulation is described in the next section.

3 Concluding Remarks
Future work will address numerical computation based on the formulation developed in
section 2. The results of the computations are expected to reveal the unique role of nonlocal
photon-mediated correlations in shaping electronic properties. It offer new insights into how
cavity coupling can stabilize exotic phases, such as superconducting or insulating states,
that are not achievable through local interactions alone. The theoretical formulation for the
nonlocal Gutzwiller approach developed here will be benchmarked across a range of coupling

 18

strengths, exploring the phase diagram and ground-state properties in regimes of strong
photon-mediated correlations. The model sets a foundation for systematically investigating
the impact of light-matter coupling on electronic structure, stability, and phase transitions
in materials.
 Our work notates the Gutzwiller framework’s applicability but strengthens the theoret-
ical foundations of cavity quantum materials. By generalizing this widely used approach to
include nonlocal correlations, we provide a powerful theoretical tool for designing and under-
standing materials that leverage the quantum control offered by photons. This contribution
paves the way for future studies and applications, enabling new material paradigms driven by
the interplay between strong electron-photon coupling and intrinsic electronic correlations.
