1.
Introduction

Ideals on countable sets play a fundamental role in infinite combinatorics and study of cardinal invariants of the continuum. This article is devoted to two subjects: study of a certain ideal, called the
Splitting ideal
and of
antichain numbers
of the algebras
\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\mathcal{J}
for various definable ideals. The splitting ideal is an interesting example of an ideal that was defined by Sabok and Zapletal in
[
29
]
in order to characterize adding splitting reals by certain class of idealized forcing notions. We will be interested in the cardinal invariants of the splitting ideal as well as its position in the Katětov order among other known definable ideals. In particular, we will show that, in the Katětov order, the Splitting ideal is above
convergent ideal
, but below the
nowhere-dense ideal
, the
trace of measure zero ideal
and the
Fubini product of ideal of finite sets
. We will also show that
\mathrm{cov}^{*}(\mathcal{S}pl)
is equal to the reaping number
\mathfrak{r}
. Here, the
*
-covering number is the smallest number of elements of the ideal that are needed to infinitely intersect any infinite subset of
\omega
. As a result, we will obtain a new upper bound for the covering number of the density zero ideal, i.e.
\mathrm{cov}^{*}(\mathcal{Z})\leq\mathfrak{r}
. This gives more conviction to the open problem whether
\mathrm{cov}^{*}(\mathcal{Z})\leq\mathfrak{b}
, where
\mathcal{Z}
is the asymptotic density zero ideal. Regarding the second subject, over the years, the Van Douwen’s diagram of the algebra
\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\mathcal{J}
for different ideals
\mathcal{J}
received attention of many researchers. In particular, several people were interested in the antichain numbers of such algebras. For an ideal
\mathcal{J}
on
\omega
, by
\mathfrak{a}(\mathcal{J})
we mean the smallest size of an uncountable family of
\mathcal{J}
-positive sets, maximal with respect to the property that the intersection of any two of its elements is in
\mathcal{J}
. We will show that
\min\{\mathfrak{b},\mathrm{cov}^{+}_{h}(\mathcal{J})\}
is a lower bound for
\mathfrak{a}(\mathcal{J})
for a wide class of definable ideals, called
good
ideals. This class includes all
F_{\sigma}
-ideals, all analytic
P
-ideals and many other known examples. The classical result saying that
\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{a}
can only be partially extended for algebras of the form
\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\mathcal{J}
. For example,
\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{a}(\mathcal{J})
holds for all analytic ideals
\mathcal{J}
and for ideals like
\mathcal{F}in\times\mathcal{F}in
and
\mathcal{ED}
. However, for some ideals, the inequality
\mathfrak{a}(\mathcal{J})<\mathfrak{b}
is consistent. For example, Steprans in
[
32

A powerful tool for classifying ideals on countable sets is the following notion of Katětov reducibility.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{J}_{0}
and
\mathcal{J}_{1}
are ideals on
\omega
. We will write
\mathcal{J}_{0}\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}_{1}
, and say that
\mathcal{J}_{0}
is Katětov reducible to
\mathcal{J}_{1}
, if there is a function
\pi:\omega\rightarrow\omega
such that
\pi^{-1}[A]\in\mathcal{J}_{1}
for any
A\in\mathcal{J}_{0}
. If such a function is finite-to-one the we call this Katětov-Blass reduction and write
\mathcal{J}_{0}\leq_{KB}\mathcal{J}_{1}
. If
\mathcal{J}_{0}
is Katětov reducible to
\mathcal{J}_{1}
and vice versa, we will say that the two ideals are Katětov equivalent.

The following is well known (see
[
22
]
)

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{J}_{0}
and
\mathcal{J}_{1}
are ideals on
\omega
. Then

•

\mathcal{J}_{0}\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}_{1}
implies
\mathrm{cov}^{*}(\mathcal{J}_{1})\leq\mathrm{cov}^{*}(\mathcal{J}_{0})
,

•

\mathcal{J}_{0}\leq_{KB}\mathcal{J}_{1}
implies
\mathrm{non}^{*}(\mathcal{J}_{0})\leq\mathrm{non}^{*}(\mathcal{J}_{1})
.

1.1.
Examples of
F_{\sigma}
-ideals

A function
\phi:\mathcal{P}(\omega)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is a lower semi-continuous submeasure (l.s.c.s.m. for short) if the following conditions hold:

–

\phi(\emptyset)=0
,
\phi(\{n\})<\infty
for every
n\in\omega
,

–

\phi(X)\leq\phi(Y)
for
X\subseteq Y
,

–

\phi(X\cup Y)\leq\phi(X)+\phi(Y)
for all
X,Y\subseteq\omega
,

–

\phi(X)=\sup\{\phi(X\cap n):n\in\omega\}
for each
X\subseteq\omega
.

There is a natural way to associate an ideal to such
\phi
. Define

Fin(\phi)=\{X\subseteq\omega:\phi(X)<\infty\}

It is easy to see that this is an
F_{\sigma}

P^{+}
-ideal. The following is a well-known characterization of
F_{\sigma}
-ideals.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Mazur,
[
25
]
)
An ideal
\mathcal{J}
is
F_{\sigma}
if and only if
\mathcal{J}=Fin(\phi)
for some l.s.c.s.m.
\phi
.

Summable ideal
:

Given a function
f:\omega\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
such that
\Sigma_{n\in\omega}f(n)=\infty
we define a summable ideal corresponding to
f
as follows:

\mathcal{I}_{f}=\{A\subseteq\omega:\Sigma_{n\in A}f(n)<\infty\}

Clearly,
\mathcal{I}_{f}
is an
F_{\sigma}
-ideal with the associated submeasure defined as
\phi(A)=\Sigma_{n\in A}f(n)
. It follows that
\mathcal{I}_{f}
is a
P^{+}
-ideal. It is also a
P
-ideal.
\mathcal{I}_{f}
is tall if and only if
lim_{n}f(n)=0
. In this article, we will only consider the classical summable ideal
\mathcal{S}um
defined as
\mathcal{I}_{f}
where
f(n)=\frac{1}{n+1}
.

Eventually different ideal
:
Let
\mathcal{ED}
be the ideal on
\omega\times\omega
consisting of these sets
A
such that for some
N

n>N
the set
\{m:(n,m)\in A\}
has at most
N

\mathcal{ED}_{fin}
be the restriction

. For

one could consider an ideal

defined as restriction of

to the set of form

. It is well-known that such

is Katětov equivalent with

. It follows that the two have the same covering and uniformity numbers.

Both
\mathcal{ED}
and
\mathcal{ED}_{fin}
are

-ideals. It follows that both
\mathcal{ED}
and
\mathcal{ED}_{fin}
are

-ideals but neither
\mathcal{ED}
nor
\mathcal{ED}_{fin}
is a
P
-ideal. It is easy to see that in the definition of

, if we replace the diagonal by any increasing function, we get a Katětov equivalent ideal. It is also easy to see that every

-positive set contains
\mathcal{ED}
-positive set

Theorem 1.4
.

(Hrušák, Meza-Alcántara, Minami;
[

)
The following (in)equalities holds:

–

\mathrm{cov}^{*}(\mathcal{ED})=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})

–

\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{N})\leq\mathrm{cov}^{*}(\mathcal{ED}_{fin})\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})

Solecki ideal
:

Let
\mathcal{S}

Van der Waerden ideal
:

Let
\mathcal{W}
be the ideal on
\omega
consisting of these
A\subseteq\omega
which does not contain an arithmetical progression of arbitrary length. The ideal
\mathcal{W}
is tall but not a
P
-ideal. The
\mathcal{W}

F_{\sigma}
-ideal: for
A\subseteq\omega
, the submeasure
\phi(A)
measures how long arithmetical sequence does

\mathcal{W}
is a

-ideal. Van der Waerden’s well-known theorem says that

Random Graph ideal
:

Let
(\omega,E)
,
E\subseteq[\omega]^{2}
be the random graph, i.e. the only graph (up to isomorphism) with the following property: for every pair
A,B\subseteq\omega
of non-empty, finite, disjoint sets there is an

such that
\{\{n,a\}:a\in A\}\subseteq E
and
\{\{n,b\}:b\in b\}\cap E=\emptyset
. The random graph ideal
\mathcal{R}
is the ideal generated by homogeneous subsets (cliques and anticliques) of the random graph. The ideal
\mathcal{R}
is tall and it is an

-ideal: for
A\subseteq E
, the submeasure
\phi(A)
measures what is the minimum amount of cliques and anticliques in

\mathcal{R}
is a

-ideal but not a
P
-ideal.

1.2.
Examples of analytic
P
-ideals

The following is a well-known characterization of analytic
P
-ideals using lower semi continuous submeasures.

Theorem 1.5
.

(Solecki,
[
30
]
)
Every analytic
P
-ideal is of the form

Exh(\phi)=\{X\subseteq\omega:lim_{n}\phi(X\setminus n)=0\}

for some lower semi continuous submeasure
\phi
.

It is not difficult to see that
Exh(\phi)
is in fact an
F_{\sigma\delta}
-ideal. It is well-known, that for a l.s.c.s.m.
\phi
, the ideal
Exh(\phi)
is tall if and only if

. Our first example of analytic
P
-ideal is the already defined summable ideal
\mathcal{S}um
. In this article we will be interested in the following two analytic P-ideals.

Density zero ideal
:

The density zero ideal
\mathcal{Z}
consists of these
A\subseteq\omega
such that
lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap n|}{n}=0
. This ideal is tall and can be expressed in the form of
Exh(\phi)
for l.s.c.s.m.
\phi
defined as

\phi(A)=:sup\{\frac{|A\cap[2^{n},2^{n+1})|}{2^{n}}:n\in\omega\}

It is known that the density zero ideal is Katětov equivalent with the ideal of subsets
A\subseteq 2^{<\omega}
such that
lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap 2^{n}|}{2^{n}}=0
(see
[
18
]
, Theorem 3.4).

Trace of null ideal
:

The trace of null ideal
\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{N})
is an ideal on
2^{<\omega}
consisting of those sets
A\subseteq 2^{<\omega}
such that the set
G(A):=\{x\in 2^{\omega}:\exists^{\infty}_{n}x|_{n}\in A\}
is of Lebesgue measure zero. This is a tall analytic
P
-ideal with a l.s.c.s.m given for
A\subseteq 2^{<\omega}
by

\phi(A)=\Sigma\{\frac{1}{2^{|\sigma|}}:\sigma\in A
is
\subseteq
-minimal in
A\}

Similarly we define the trace of closed measure zero ideal
\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{E})
(even though it is not a
P
-ideal). Clearly, as
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
, we have
\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{E})\subseteq\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{N})
.

\mathrm{add}_{\omega}^{*}(\mathcal{S}pl)=\omega_{1}

Proposition 2.23

\mathrm{non}^{*}_{\omega}(\mathcal{S}pl)=\mathfrak{s}_{\omega}

Proof.

To show that

\omega

Definition 3.7

In order to prove our main lower bound for numbers
\mathfrak{a}(\mathcal{J})

Definition 3.12
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{J}
is and ideal on
\omega
. We will say that
\mathcal{J}
is

if for every uncountable family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{J}^{+}
has a subfamily
\{B_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}

\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}

\mathcal{J}

Theorem 3.13
.

If an ideal
\mathcal{J}
is good, then
\min\{\mathrm{cov}^{+}_{h}(\mathcal{J}),\mathfrak{b}\}\leq\mathfrak{a}(\mathcal{J})
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa<\min\{\mathrm{cov}^{+}_{h}(\mathcal{J}),\mathfrak{b}\}
and let
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}
be an antichain in
\mathcal{J}^{+}
. As
\kappa<\mathrm{cov}^{+}_{h}(\mathcal{J})
there are
\{B_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}^{+}
such that
B_{\alpha}\subseteq A_{\alpha}
and
B_{\alpha}\cap A_{\beta}=^{*}\emptyset
for

. Suppose that
\{B_{n}:n<\omega\}
is as in definition of goodness. Without loss of generality we may assume that the intersection

is empty for each
n\in\omega
. For each
\alpha\in\kappa\setminus\omega
define

such that

for each

. Let

be a function which dominates all of

\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}

C_{n}\subseteq B_{n}\setminus f(n)
such that
C:=\bigcup_{n}C_{n}
is
\mathcal{J}
-positive. Then, clearly the intersection of

with any
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha<\kappa
is in the ideal
\mathcal{J}
which finishes the proof.

\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{a}(\mathcal{J})
