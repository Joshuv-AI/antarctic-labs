A Knebusch trace formula for Azumaya algebras with involution
Vincent Astier, Thomas Unger

Abstract
. We establish a trace formula for signatures of hermitian forms over Azumaya algebras with involution, extending Knebusch’s work on symmetric bilinear forms over finite étale extensions of commutative base rings. As an application when the base ring is semilocal, we obtain an exact sequence for total signatures, related to Pfister’s local-global principle and the notion of stability index.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
16H05, 11E39, 13J30, 16W10, 06F25

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative ring. In the mid-1970s, Knebusch proved a trace formula
for signatures of symmetric bilinear forms over finite étale extensions, and
more generally Frobenius extensions, of
R
, cf.
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
Knebusch’s approach predates the discovery of the real spectrum
\operatorname{Sper}R
by
Coste and Roy, cf.
[
10
, Chapter 7]
and
[
20
]
, and the
signatures used are ring homomorphisms from the Witt ring
W(R)
to
\mathbb{Z}
.

In this paper we are interested in a trace formula for hermitian forms,
expressed in terms of signatures at orderings
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
. Specifically,
after the required prerequisites in Section
2
, we establish such a
formula for hermitian forms over Azumaya algebras with involution over
commutative rings in Section
3
. We proved a similar result for
central simple algebras with involution over fields in our earlier paper

[
1
]
, which is also the starting point of our development of real
algebra for “well-behaved” rings with involution.

The main thread of our approach is signatures of hermitian forms. The behaviour
of signatures is generally pleasing in terms of Witt groups and the Harrison
topology on
\operatorname{Sper}R
, cf.
[
5
]
. The primary mechanism for their
definition is hermitian Morita theory, and we provide a technical appendix on
this topic. As an application of the Knebusch trace formula, we prove the
existence of a nonsingular reference form of constant signature
2^{m}
in
Section
4
, while in Section
5
we briefly dwell on an exact
sequence for total signatures when the base ring is semilocal (and which is
connected to the stability index in the commutative case).

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper all rings are assumed unital and associative with
2
invertible,
and all fields are assumed to have characteristic different from
2
.

2.1.
Hermitian forms

Let
(A,\sigma)
be a ring with involution. A hermitian module over
(A,\sigma)
is a
pair
(M,h)
where
M
is a finitely generated projective right
A
-module and

h\colon M\times M\to A
is a hermitian form with respect to
\sigma
. We often refer
to
(M,h)
as a hermitian form and do not always indicate what
M
is. We denote
isometry of forms by
\simeq
. For
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\in\Sym(A,\sigma):=\{a\in A\mid\sigma(a)=a\}
we denote by
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\rangle_{\sigma}
the diagonal hermitian
form

A^{\ell}\times A^{\ell}\to A,\ (x,y)\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sigma(x_{i})a_{i}y_{i}.

Note that not every hermitian form is isometric to a diagonal hermitian form.

Definition 2.1
.

For
i=1,\ldots,t
,
let
(A_{i},\sigma_{i})
be rings with involution and let
(M_{i},h_{i})
be a hermitian
form over
(A_{i},\sigma_{i})
. Let
(A,\sigma):=(A_{1},\sigma_{1})\times\cdots\times(A_{t},\sigma_{t})
. We denote by
(M_{1},h_{1})\times\cdots\times(M_{t},h_{t})
the
hermitian form
(M,h)
over
(A,\sigma)
defined by

•

M=M_{1}\times\cdots\times M_{t}
, with the obvious structure of right

A
-module;

•

h((m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}),(m^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,m^{\prime}_{t})):=(h_{1}(m_{1},m^{\prime}_{1}),\ldots,h_{t}(m_{t},m^{\prime}_{t}))

(it is straightforward to check that
(M,h)
is a hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
).

Proposition 2.2
.

With the same notation as in Definition
2.1
, assume that
there are commutative rings
R_{i}
such that
A_{i}
is a finitely generated

R_{i}
-module, for
i=1,\ldots,t
. Then

(1)

h
is nonsingular if and only if every
h_{i}
is nonsingular;

(2)

if every
h_{i}
is hyperbolic, then
h
is hyperbolic.

Proof.

Let
R:=R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{t}
. Then
A
is a finitely generated
R
-module.

The first statement is a consequence of the fact that
h
is nonsingular if
and only if
h\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{m}
is nonsingular, for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of

R
, cf.
[
22
, Chapter I, Lemma 7.1.3]
.

We prove the second statement. We can write
M_{i}=L_{i}\oplus P_{i}
with

h_{i}(L_{i},L_{i})=0
and
h_{i}(P_{i},P_{i})=0
(cf.
[
14
, Section 2.2]
).
So
M=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(L_{i}\oplus P_{i})\cong(\prod_{i=1}^{t}L_{i})\oplus(\prod_{i=1}^{t}P_{i})
. Let
L=\prod_{i=1}^{t}L_{i}
and
P=\prod_{i=1}^{t}P_{i}
.
We check that
h(L,L)=0
; the proof of
h(P,P)=0
is similar. It suffices
to show that
h(\ell_{i},\ell^{\prime}_{j})=0
for each
\ell_{i}\in L_{i}
and
\ell^{\prime}_{j}\in L_{j}
. If
i\not=j
then
h(\ell_{i},\ell^{\prime}_{j})=0
and if
i=j
then

h(\ell_{i},\ell^{\prime}_{i})=h_{i}(\ell_{i},\ell^{\prime}_{i})=0
.
∎

2.2.
Finite étale algebras

Let
R
be a commutative ring. We say that an
R
-algebra is
étale
over

R
if it is a commutative, separable, flat, finitely presented
R
-algebra, and

finite étale
if it is étale and a finitely generated
R
-module.

We have the following sequence of
implications (in fact, equivalences). These are well-known, but we could not
find them in a convenient single reference.

T
is a

finite étale
R
-algebra

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\text{$T$ is a separable flat $R$-algebra and finitely
presented as
$R$-module}

(by
[
26
, first part of Remark 2.4]
)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\text{$T$ is a separable $R$-algebra and a finitely generated
projective $R$-module}

(by
[
26
, second part of Remark 2.4]
)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\text{$T$ is a separable, flat, finitely presented
$R$-algebra and is finitely}

generated as
R
-module

(since projective modules are flat and by
[
15
, Exercise
9.2.2(2)]
)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\text{$T$ is a finite \'{e}tale $R$-algebra},

so that all these statements are equivalent.

2.3.
Azumaya algebras with involution

Let
R
be a commutative ring. An

R
-algebra
A
is an
Azumaya
R
-algebra
if
A
is a faithful finitely
generated projective
R
-module and the map

A\otimes_{R}A^{\mathrm{op}}\to\End_{R}(A),\ a\otimes b^{\mathrm{op}}\mapsto[x\mapsto axb]

is an isomorphism of
R
-algebras (here
A^{\mathrm{op}}
denotes the
opposite
algebra
of
A
, which coincides with
A
as an
R
-module, but with twisted
multiplication
a^{\mathrm{op}}b^{\mathrm{op}}=(ba)^{\mathrm{op}}
). The centre
Z(A)
is equal to
R
. See

[
22
, Chapter III, (5.1)]
, for example. We recall the following results:

Proposition 2.3

(
[
25
, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.2]
)
.

(1)

If
K
is a field, then
A
is an Azumaya
K
-algebra if and only
if
A
is a central simple
K
-algebra.

(2)

If
A
is an Azumaya
R
-algebra and
f:R\rightarrow S
is a
morphism of commutative rings, then
A\otimes_{R}S
is an Azumaya
S
-algebra.

Definition 2.4

(
[
14
, Section 1.4]
)
.

We say that
(A,\sigma)
is an
Azumaya
algebra with involution over
R
if the following conditions hold:

•

A
is an
R
-algebra with
R
-linear involution
\sigma
;

•

A
is separable projective over
R
;

•

the homomorphism
R\rightarrow A
,
r\mapsto r\cdot 1_{A}
identifies

R
with the set
\Sym(Z(A),\sigma)
of
\sigma
-symmetric elements of
Z(A)
.

This definition is motivated by:

Proposition 2.5

(
[
14
, Proposition 1.1]
)
.

Let
A
be an
R
-algebra. Then

A
is Azumaya over
Z(A)
and
Z(A)
is finite étale over
R
if and only
if
A
is projective as an
R
-module and separable as an
R
-algebra.

A further link with Azumaya
R
-algebras is provided by

[
6
, Lemma 2.5]
:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A
be an
R
-algebra with
R
-linear involution
\sigma
such that
A
is an
Azumaya algebra over
Z(A)
,
Z(A)
is
R
or a quadratic étale extension of

R
, and
R=\Sym(Z(A),\sigma)
. Then
(A,\sigma)
is an Azumaya algebra with
involution over
R
.

The converse holds if
R
is connected.

Assumption for the remainder of the paper:

R
is a
commutative unital ring with
2
invertible (in particular, we identify
quadratic and symmetric bilinear forms over
R
), and
(A,\sigma)
is an Azumaya
algebra with involution over
R
.

Note that
A
is Azumaya over
Z(A)
by Proposition
2.5
, but may not
be Azumaya over
R
.

We recall the following, proved in
[
14
, Section 1.4, second
paragraph]
:

Proposition 2.7

(Change of base ring)
.

Let
T
be a commutative
R
-algebra. Then
Z(A\otimes_{R}T)=Z(A)\otimes_{R}T

and
(A\otimes_{R}T,\sigma\otimes\id)
is an
Azumaya algebra with
involution over
T
(and in particular
Z(A\otimes_{R}T)\cap\Sym(A\otimes_{R}T,\sigma\otimes\id)=T
).

Remark 2.8
.

If
R=F
is actually a field, then
(A,\sigma)
is an Azumaya algebra with
involution over
R
if and only if it is a central simple
F
-algebra with
involution in the sense of
[
23
, Sections 2.A, 2.B]
(i.e., we allow for
the possibility that
Z(A)=F\times F
). We call such algebras
F
-algebras
with involution
.

Recall that if
(B,\tau)
is an
F
-algebra with involution, then
\tau
is said
to be of the first kind if
Z(B)\subseteq\Sym(B,\tau)
, and of the second kind
(or of unitary type) otherwise. Involutions of the first kind are further
divided into those of orthogonal type and those of symplectic type, depending on
the dimension of
\Sym(B,\tau)
, cf.
[
23
, Sections 2.A and 2.B]
.

Definition 2.9

(See
[
14
, Section 1.4]
)
.

We say that the involution
\sigma
on
A

is
of orthogonal, symplectic or unitary type at

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
if

(A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),\sigma\otimes\id)
is a
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra with
involution of orthogonal, symplectic or unitary type, respectively,
where
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
denotes the
field of fractions
\mathrm{Frac}(R/\mathfrak{p})
. We also say that

\sigma
is
of orthogonal, symplectic or unitary type

if it is of orthogonal, symplectic or unitary type, respectively,
at all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.

Proposition 2.10

(See
[
14
, Proposition 1.21]
for a more detailed
statement)
.

If
R
is connected, then precisely
one of the following holds:

(1)

\sigma
is of orthogonal type,

Z(A)=R
, and
\sigma|_{Z(A)}=\id_{Z(A)}
;

(2)

\sigma
is of symplectic type,

Z(A)=R
, and
\sigma|_{Z(A)}=\id_{Z(A)}
;

(3)

\sigma
is of unitary type,
Z(A)
is a quadratic étale
R
-algebra,
and
\sigma|_{Z(A)}
is the standard involution on
Z(A)

(i.e., the unique involution
\vartheta
on
Z(A)
such that
R=\Sym(Z(A),\vartheta)
, cf.
[
22
, Chapter I, Section 1.3 and
Proposition 1.3.4]
).

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\varphi:R\rightarrow T
be a morphism of commutative rings, and let

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}T
. Then the involutions
\sigma\otimes\id
on
A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))
and
\sigma\otimes\id\otimes\id
on
A\otimes_{R}T\otimes_{T}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
are of the same type.

In particular, if
R
is connected, then the involution
\sigma\otimes\id
on
A\otimes_{R}T
is of the same type as
\sigma
.

Proof.

We first recall that the result holds in the field case: if
R
and
T
are
fields, then the involutions
\sigma
on
A
and
\sigma\otimes\id
on
A\otimes_{R}T

are of the same type.

The map
\varphi
induces an injective morphism of rings from
R/\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})
to

T/\mathfrak{p}
, and thus a morphism of fields
\bar{\varphi}:\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))\rightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
. Clearly:

\textstyle{T\otimes_{T}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))\otimes_{\bar{\varphi}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}
\textstyle{t\otimes x\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bar{t}x\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1\otimes\bar{t}x,}

which induces an isomorphism from
A\otimes_{R}T\otimes_{T}\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
to
A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))\otimes_{\bar{\varphi}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
, and thus an
isomorphism of algebras with involution

(A\otimes_{R}T\otimes_{T}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),\sigma\otimes\id\otimes\id)\rightarrow(A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))\otimes_{\bar{\varphi}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),\sigma\otimes\id\otimes\id).

Therefore it suffices to show that the involutions
\sigma\otimes\id
on
A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))
and
\sigma\otimes\id\otimes\id
on
A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))\otimes_{\bar{\varphi}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
are of the same type, which
follows from the field case recalled above.

We now prove the second statement of the Proposition. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}T
.
Observe that, by Definition
2.9
, the involutions
\sigma
on
A
and

\sigma\otimes_{R}\id
on
A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))
are of the same type. By the
first part of the Proposition,
\sigma\otimes_{R}\id
on
A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))
and
\sigma\otimes\id\otimes\id
on
A\otimes_{R}T\otimes_{T}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
are also of the same type. Therefore, the type of
\sigma\otimes\id\otimes\id
on
A\otimes_{R}T
is constant on
\operatorname{Spec}T
and equal to the type of
\sigma
.
∎

2.4.
The trace map

Let
T
be a finite étale
R
-algebra. We denote by
\Tr_{T/R}\in\Hom(T,R)

the trace map from
T
to
R
afforded by
T
, in the terminology of

[
13
, p. 91]
(this is the reference used in
[
17
, §3]
) or

[
15
, Definition 4.6.5]
. Note that if
T
is finitely generated and
free, then
\Tr_{T/R}
is the usual trace, defined via the left regular
representation of
T
, cf.
[
15
, Exercise 4.6.14]
. Furthermore, the
associated symmetric bilinear form
\tr_{T/R}:T\times T\rightarrow R
,

(x,y)\mapsto\Tr_{T/R}(xy)
is nonsingular. Indeed, it follows from the proof
of
[
15
, Corollary 4.6.8, end of first paragraph]
that the adjoint
linear map of
\tr_{T/R}
is bijective.

We record the following known result:

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
S
be a commutative
R
-algebra. Then
\Tr_{T\otimes_{R}S/S}=\Tr_{T/R}\otimes_{R}\id_{S}
.

Proof.

As observed in
[
15
, p. 153, proof of (3) implies (1)]
,
a projective dual
basis of
T
over
R
gives a projective dual basis of
T\otimes_{R}S
over
S
,
and the result follows.
∎

We define

\Tr_{A\otimes_{R}T/A}:=\id_{A}\otimes_{R}\Tr_{T/R}:A\otimes_{R}T\to A,

the map induced by the trace map
\Tr_{T/R}
. Observe that it is an involution
trace in the sense of
[
22
, Chapter I, Proposition 7.2.4 and following
paragraph]
: Checking the first two points of the definition is direct,
while the third one follows from the fact that the form that needs to be
nonsingular is actually
\langle 1\rangle_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}\tr_{T/R}
, which is nonsingular, as a
tensor product of two nonsingular forms.

It follows that if
h
is hyperbolic, or nonsingular, then
\Tr_{A\otimes_{R}T/A}^{*}(h)
is respectively hyperbolic (cf.

[
22
, Chapter I, bottom of p. 40]
), or nonsingular (cf.

[
22
, Chapter I, 10.3, top of p. 62]
).

A\otimes_{R}k(\alpha)\cong M_{n_{\alpha}}(D_{\alpha}),\text{ with }D_{\alpha}\in\{k(\alpha)\times k(\alpha),(-1,-1)_{k(\alpha)}\times(-1,-1)_{k(\alpha)}\},

for some

Note that

\dim_{k(\alpha)}A\otimes_{R}k(\alpha)\in\{n_{\alpha}^{2},2n_{\alpha}^{2},4n_{\alpha}^{2},8n_{\alpha}^{2}\}.

The maximal real spectrum:

We denote by
\Sperm R
the set of all elements of
\operatorname{Sper}R
that are maximal
for inclusion, equipped with the topology induced by
\operatorname{Sper}R
. For
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
, the set
\{\beta\in\operatorname{Sper}R\mid\alpha\subseteq\beta\}
is totally
ordered by inclusion (cf.
[
21
, Theorem 3.6.7]
), and every
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
is included in a unique
element
\alpha^{*}
of
\Sperm R
, cf.
[
21
, Corollary 3.6.8]
. The map

\operatorname{Sper}R\rightarrow\Sperm R,\ \alpha\mapsto\alpha^{*}

is continuous, cf.
[
21
, Proposition 3.6.16]
.

Product of rings:

If
R=R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{n}
is a product, it is well-known that we can identify each
\operatorname{Spec}R_{i}
with a subset of
\operatorname{Spec}R
via

\operatorname{Spec}R_{i}\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}R,\quad\mathfrak{p}\mapsto R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{i-1}\times\mathfrak{p}\times R_{i+1}\times\cdots\times R_{n}.

Under this identification, we have
\operatorname{Spec}R=\operatorname{Spec}R_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}\operatorname{Spec}R_{n}
.
Similarly, working with the support of the orderings, we have

(2.1)

\operatorname{Sper}R=\operatorname{Sper}R_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}\operatorname{Sper}R_{n},

where

\operatorname{Sper}R_{i}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sper}R,\quad\alpha\mapsto R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{i-1}\times\alpha\times R_{i+1}\times\cdots\times R_{n}.

Signatures of
\boldsymbol{R}
:

The following content, about signatures of rings, can be found in

.

A signature of

.
If

Lemma 2.14

(1)

. Then the number of extensions of

(2)

Assume that

is semilocal, let

and let

such that

(1) Let

be the map induced by

on the

and

.
Let
\gamma\in\Sperm T
be such that

. Then
\sign_{\alpha}=\sign_{\gamma}\circ\varphi^{*}
by

below,
and
\sign_{\gamma}
is a signature of

that extends

\sign_{\alpha}
. The result follows since there are only finitely many such

[

, Theorem 3.4]
.

(2) It suffices to prove the lemma for

. Assume that the result does

we obtain

, contradiction.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\varphi:R\rightarrow T
be a morphism of commutative rings, let
\gamma\in\operatorname{Sper}T
and let
\alpha:=\varphi^{-1}(\gamma)\in\operatorname{Sper}R
. Then

\sign_{\alpha}=\sign_{\gamma}\circ\varphi^{*}:W(R)\to\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
q
be a nonsingular symmetric bilinear form over
R
. Let
\mathfrak{p}:=\Supp\alpha
,
and observe that
2\not\in\mathfrak{p}
, so that
2
is invertible in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
It is well known that
q\otimes R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is diagonalizable in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
in this case
(cf.
[
22
, Chapter II, Remark 4.6.5]
):
q\otimes R_{\mathfrak{p}}\simeq\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}\rangle
with
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}\in R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Up to multiplying each
a_{i}
by the square of an element in

R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
, we can assume that the
a_{i}

are in
R
, and therefore in
R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
since
q\otimes R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
nonsingular.

If
\varepsilon_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
is the sign of
a_{i}
with respect to

\alpha
we have
\sign_{\alpha}q=\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{t}
. In particular, to
show that
\sign_{\alpha}q=\sign_{\gamma}\varphi^{*}(q)
it suffices to show, for
a\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
, that
a\in\alpha\setminus-\alpha
if and only if

\varphi(a)\in\gamma\setminus-\gamma
, but this follows immediately from the
definition of
\alpha
.
∎

2.6.
Signatures of hermitian forms

We recall the following definition from
[
6
]
:

Definition 2.16
.

Let
h
be a hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
and let
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
. Then

h\otimes\kappa(\alpha)
is a hermitian form over the

\kappa(\alpha)
-algebra with
involution
(A(\alpha),\sigma(\alpha))
, and we define the
\mathscr{M}
-signature
of
h
at
\alpha
by

\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h:=\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}(h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha)),

where
\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}
is a Morita equivalence as in

[
1
, Section 3.2]
; see
[
6
, Definition 3.1]
and the
discussion following it.

The main drawback of the
\mathscr{M}
-signature at
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
is that there
is no canonical choice of Morita equivalence
\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}
. Another
choice of Morita equivalence will lead at most to a change of sign in the
signature of a form (cf.
[
1
, Proposition 3.4]
), while an arbitrary
change in the sign of this signature can
be obtained by taking another, well-chosen, Morita equivalence (cf.

[
1
, first paragraph of Section 3.3]
). We therefore adopt the
following:

Convention:

If there are several instances of
\mathscr{M}
-signatures of forms over
(A,\sigma)
in
a statement or formula, we use the same choice of Morita equivalence for each
of them.

This lack of a canonical choice for
\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}
is in particular a problem
if we want to consider the total signature of a form as a continuous
function on
\operatorname{Sper}R
. We solved this problem with the definition of
\eta
-signatures in

[
5
]
by introducing a so-called reference form
\eta
, as follows:

Definition 2.17
.

(1)

We define
\Nil[A,\sigma]:=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\mid\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}=0\}
. It is a clopen subset of
\operatorname{Sper}R
(see

[
5
, Theorem 3.11]
).

(2)

We say that a hermitian form
\eta
is a
reference form for
(A,\sigma)

if
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta\not=0
for every
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
.

For
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
, we can now use
\eta
to fix the
sign in the computation of
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}
: We choose a Morita equivalence

\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}
such that
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta>0
. We call this
signature the
\eta
-signature at
\alpha
, denoted
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}
.
Equivalently, it can be obtained as follows, for any choice of the Morita
equivalence
\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}
:

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h:=(\sgn\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta)\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h,

for
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
and
h
hermitian form over

(A,\sigma)
, and where
\sgn
denotes the sign function.

Note that since all signatures at
\alpha
are computed by scalar extension to

\kappa(\alpha)
, we have

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h=\sign^{\eta\otimes\kappa(\alpha)}_{\bar{\alpha}}(h\otimes\kappa(\alpha)).

Remark 2.18
.

The
\mathscr{M}
-signature
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}
, and in particular the

\eta
-signature
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}
(which is a special case of it, for a
particular choice of
\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}
) inherit all the properties from the
map
\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}
defined for central simple
algebras with involution. For instance, it is additive
and
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}(q\otimes h)=\sign_{\alpha}q\cdot\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h
if

q
is quadratic over
R
and
h
hermitian over
(A,\sigma)
, cf.

[
1
, Proposition 3.6]
.

3.1.

We recall Knebusch’s trace formula for symmetric bilinear forms over commutative

\displaystyle=(\dim_{Z(B\otimes_{R}k(\alpha))}B\otimes_{R}k(\alpha))\cdot\dim_{k(\alpha)}Z(B\otimes_{R}k(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\rk_{Z(B)}B\cdot\dim_{k(\alpha)}Z(B\otimes_{R}k(\alpha)).

The first term in this product is a power of
2
by hypothesis, and the second
term is 1 or 2 (cf. Lemma
2.6
),
so that
\dim_{k(\alpha)}B\otimes_{R}k(\alpha)
is a
power of
2
. End of the proof of
the Fact.

Therefore,
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\langle u\rangle_{\tau}
is a 2-power and, up to replacing

u
by
-u
, we obtain that

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}\langle u\rangle_{\tau}
is a
2
-power for the fixed reference form

\eta
. Taking
h_{\alpha}:=\langle u\rangle_{\tau}
and observing that it is nonsingular
since
u
is invertible finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
R
be semilocal connected and
let
\alpha\in\Sperm R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
. Then there exists a nonsingular
hermitian form
h_{\alpha}
over
(A,\sigma)
such that

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h_{\alpha}
is a
2
-power.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Theorem 8.7 and Remark 8.8]

there is a connected finite étale
R
-algebra
R_{1}
of
odd rank and an
Azumaya algebra with involution
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
over
R_{1}
such that
A\otimes_{R}R_{1}
and
A_{1}
are Brauer equivalent over

S_{1}:=S\otimes_{R}R_{1}
,
\sigma
and
\sigma_{1}
are both unitary, or are both non-unitary,
and such that at least one of the following holds:

(1)

Z(A_{1})\cong R_{1}\times R_{1}
;

(2)

\deg A_{1}=1
;

(3)

\deg A_{1}
is a
2
-power
and there exists
u\in A_{1}^{\times}
such
that
\sigma_{1}(u)=-u

(and other properties that are not needed in this proof).

Observe that there is
\beta\in\Sperm R_{1}
such that
\alpha=\beta\cap R
. Indeed, by Proposition
3.2
(2), there is
\gamma\in\operatorname{Sper}R_{1}

such that
\alpha=\gamma\cap R
. Then, let
\beta\in\Sperm R_{1}
be such
that
\gamma\subseteq\beta
. It follows that

\alpha=\beta\cap R
since
\alpha

is maximal. Furthermore,
\beta\not\in\Nil[A\otimes_{R}R_{1},\sigma\otimes\id]
by
Proposition
3.2
(3).

We consider the three cases above:

(1) This case does not occur: Since
Z(A\otimes_{R}R_{1})=Z(A_{1})\cong R_{1}\times R_{1}
,
the involution
\sigma
is necessarily unitary, and we obtain

\beta\in\Nil[A\otimes_{R}R_{1},\sigma\otimes\id]
by

[
6
, Lemma 3.7]
, contradiction.

(2) In this case
A_{1}=S_{1}
.
If
\sigma_{1}
is unitary, then
S_{1}
is a
quadratic étale
R_{1}
-algebra and
\sigma_{1}
is the standard involution
on
S_{1}
by Proposition
2.10
.

Claim.

K:=A_{1}\otimes_{R_{1}}\kappa(\beta)
is a quadratic field
extension of

\kappa(\beta)
of the form
\kappa(\beta)(\sqrt{d})
with

d<_{\bar{\beta}}0
and
\iota:=\sigma_{1}\otimes\id
is
the canonical involution on
K
.

Proof of the Claim.
Indeed, if
d>_{\bar{\beta}}0
or if
K\cong\kappa(\beta)\times\kappa(\beta)
, then
K\otimes_{\kappa(\beta)}k(\beta)\cong k(\beta)\times k(\beta)
, and thus
\bar{\beta}\in\Nil[K,\iota]
by

[
6
, Lemma 3.7]
, i.e.,
\bar{\beta}\in\Nil[A_{1}\otimes_{R_{1}}\kappa(\beta),\sigma_{1}\otimes\id]
. It follows that
\beta\in\Nil[A_{1},\sigma_{1}]
, again by
[
6
, Lemma 3.7]
. Since
\sigma_{1}
is
unitary,
\sigma
is unitary by assumption, and it follows that
\sigma\otimes\id
is
unitary by Proposition
2.11
. Since
A\otimes_{R}R_{1}
and
A_{1}

are Brauer equivalent, it follows that

\Nil[A\otimes_{R}R_{1},\sigma\otimes\id]=\Nil[A_{1},\sigma_{1}]
, so that
\beta\in\Nil[A\otimes_{R}R_{1},\sigma\otimes\id]
, and thus that
\alpha\in\Nil[A,\sigma]
by
Proposition
3.2
(3), contradiction. The second statement is
clear since
\iota
is
\kappa(\beta)
-linear. End of the proof of the
Claim.

By the Claim and
[
7
, Remark 2.5]
we then obtain

\sign^{\mu}_{\beta}\langle 1\rangle_{\sigma_{1}}:=\sign^{\mu\otimes\kappa(\beta)}_{\bar{\beta}}\langle 1\rangle_{\iota}=1
. Hence,
\sign^{\eta}_{\beta}\langle 1\rangle_{\sigma_{1}}=:\delta\in\{-1,1\}
. Taking
h_{1}=\langle\delta\rangle_{\sigma_{1}}
then
yields
\sign^{\eta}_{\beta}h_{1}=1
.

If
\sigma_{1}
, and thus
\sigma
is not unitary, we have
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})=(S_{1},\iota)=(R_{1},\id)
. Therefore
\sigma_{1}
is orthogonal, and
taking
h_{1}=\langle\delta\rangle_{\sigma_{1}}
as above again yields

\sign^{\eta}_{\beta}h_{1}=1
. We now show that
\sigma
must also be orthogonal.
We have
A_{1}\otimes_{R_{1}}k(\beta)\cong k(\beta)
. Since
A\otimes_{R}R_{1}
and
A_{1}
are Brauer
equivalent,
(A\otimes_{R}R_{1})\otimes_{R_{1}}k(\beta)
and
A_{1}\otimes_{R_{1}}k(\beta)

are Brauer equivalent (cf.
[
15
, Proposition 7.3.3]
),
and thus
(A\otimes_{R}R_{1})\otimes_{R_{1}}k(\beta)
is Brauer
equivalent to
k(\beta)
. If
\sigma
is symplectic, then
\beta\in\Nil[A\otimes_{R}R_{1},\sigma\otimes\id]
by
[
5
, Remark 3.6]
, and so

\alpha\in\Nil[A,\sigma]
by Proposition
3.2
(3), contradiction.

(3) We have
S_{1}:=S\otimes_{R}R_{1}=Z(A)\otimes_{R}R_{1}=Z(A\otimes_{R}R_{1})
by
Proposition
2.7
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.6
,
S_{1}
is
a quadratic étale extension of
R_{1}
. It follows that
S_{1}
is connected,
for if not, then
S_{1}\cong R_{1}\times R_{1}
by
[
14
, Lemma 1.16]
, and the
same argument as in case (1) provides a contradiction. If
\sigma_{1}
is symplectic
then
\Int(u)\circ\sigma_{1}
is orthogonal, and if
\sigma_{1}
is orthogonal then

\Int(u)\circ\sigma_{1}
is symplectic (this follows from

[
23
, Proposition 2.7]
since it can be checked at each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
,
cf. Definition
2.9
). It follows that, up to replacing
\sigma_{1}
by

\Int(u)\circ\sigma_{1}
, we can assume that
\sigma
and
\sigma_{1}
are of the same type.
By Lemma
4.1
, there is a nonsingular hermitian form
h_{1}
over

(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
such that
\sign^{\lambda_{1}}_{\beta}h_{1}
is (up to sign) a power
of
2
, where
\lambda_{1}
is a reference form for
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
.

Therefore, in both cases (2) and (3),
S_{1}
is connected,
\sigma
and
\sigma_{1}
are
of the same type, and there is a nonsingular hermitian form
h_{1}
over
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
whose signature is (up to sign) a power of
2
. By
Theorem
4.2
, there are forms
\lambda
and
h
over
(A\otimes_{R}R_{1},\sigma\otimes\id)
with

\sign^{\lambda}_{\beta}h
is a
power of
2
. Since
\sign^{\eta\otimes R_{1}}_{\beta}h=\pm\sign^{\lambda}_{\beta}h

we can assume (up to replacing
h
by
-h
if needed), that
\sign^{\eta\otimes R_{1}}_{\beta}h=2^{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

By Lemma
2.14
(1), the subset of
\Sperm R_{1}
of all maximal
extensions of
\alpha
to
R_{1}
is finite, say equal to
\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\}
, with
\beta_{1}=\beta
. By Lemma
2.14
(2) there are

such that

. Observe that

Appendix A
Hermitian Morita equivalence

In this appendix we show that Brauer equivalent Azumaya algebras with
involution over semilocal connected rings are hermitian Morita equivalent,
and that this equivalence is preserved under semilocal connected
base change. For the convenience
of the reader we spell out most of the details.

Recall that
(A,\sigma)
denotes an Azumaya algebra with involution over
R
.
Let
\varepsilon\in Z(A)
be a unit such that
\sigma(\varepsilon)\varepsilon=1
. We denote the category
of
\varepsilon
-hermitian forms over
(A,\sigma)
by
\Herm^{\varepsilon}(A,\sigma)
. Its objects are
the
\varepsilon
-hermitian modules
(M,h)
over
(A,\sigma)
and its morphisms are the
isometries (in particular they are all isomorphisms).

Let

(B,\tau)
be a further Azumaya algebra with involution over
R
, and let
\delta\in Z(B)
be a unit such that
\tau(\delta)\delta=1
.
We recall from
[
22
, Chapter I, §8]
how the product of forms is defined.
Let
(N,\varphi)
be an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
and let
(M,\psi)
be
a
\delta
-hermitian form over
(B,\tau)

is a
B
-
A
-bimodule over
R
(i.e., the left and right actions of

on

\varphi

admits

(B,\tau)

The

-hermitian form

If
