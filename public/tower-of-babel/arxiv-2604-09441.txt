Infinitely Many Attracting Periodic Circles in Higher Dimensions
Shuntaro Tomizawa

Abstract




﻿

Abstract.

We study
C^{r}
(
5\leq r\leq\infty
) diffeomorphisms on closed manifolds of dimension at least three with a heteroclinic cycle between two hyperbolic periodic points. At each point, the unstable direction is one dimensional, and the stable and unstable eigenvalues closest to
1
in modulus are real and simple. One heteroclinic connection is transverse and the other is non-transverse, and the product of those two eigenvalues is less than
1
at one point and greater than
1
at the other. Arbitrarily close to such a map, there are open sets in which a residual subset of diffeomorphisms has infinitely many attracting normally hyperbolic periodic circles. The proof uses a rescaling to the standard Hénon map and a corrected formula for the Lyapunov coefficient on its Neimark-Sacker (Andronov-Hopf) line.

Keywords.

heteroclinic cycle, heteroclinic tangency, homoclinic tangency, standard Hénon map, Neimark-Sacker (Andronov-Hopf) bifurcation, Newhouse phenomenon.

AMS subject classification.

37G25, 37C29, 37G30, 37G35

Introduction

Homoclinic and heteroclinic tangencies are standard sources of persistent nonhyperbolic dynamics. They generate both Newhouse phenomena and invariant circle bifurcations near tangencies
[
19
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
7
,
25
,
6
]
. In this paper we study a higher-dimensional heteroclinic cycle in Figure
1.1
. Our main result shows that every diffeomorphism with such a cycle belongs to the closure of an open set in which diffeomorphisms with infinitely many attracting normally hyperbolic periodic circles form a residual subset. The proof combines a two-parameter unfolding, a Newhouse domain argument, and a rescaling to the standard Hénon map based on the corrected Lyapunov coefficient.

Let

, and let

Assume that

Theorem A
.

Let

, let

•

such that, for every

such that, for every
g\in\mathcal{R}_{\{s_{k}\}}

Remark 1.7
.

(1)

The periodic circles in Theorem

are created by a Neimark-Sacker (Andronov-Hopf) bifurcation. This is why we require

(2)

If

(3)

Even when

[

, Section 5.10.2]
.

(4)

When

, related invariant circle mechanisms were studied in
[

,
7
]
. Theorem

extends that picture to higher dimensions. We also correct the Lyapunov coefficient formula used in

1.1
Previous works and our results

The background of Theorem
A
has two complementary components. The first is the Newhouse theory of persistent tangencies and infinitely many sinks. In dimension two, Newhouse proved that persistent homoclinic tangencies generate open sets in which diffeomorphisms with infinitely many sinks are dense
[
19
]
. Higher dimensional extensions and refinements were obtained by Palis and Viana
[
20
]
and by Romero
[
22
]
.

The second component concerns quasi periodic attractors born from tangencies. In dimension two, precursor bifurcation scenarios for homoclinic tangencies to neutral saddles were studied in
[
11
]
. The birth of invariant circles and the corresponding Lyapunov coefficient computations were analyzed in
[
2
,
3
]
. These ideas were developed in
[
5
,
7
]
, yielding Newhouse domains containing diffeomorphisms with infinitely many stable and unstable invariant tori. In dimension three, related precursor configurations were studied in
[
10
,
25
]
, while
[
6
, Theorem 6]
gave multidimensional criteria for the existence of infinitely many stable invariant tori in suitable Newhouse domains.

The present paper isolates a more concrete higher dimensional mechanism: a centrally dissipative-expanding transversal and non-transversal heteroclinic cycle of type two bi-saddles with one-dimensional unstable directions. The intermediate result, stated later as Theorem
5.3
, shows that suitable two-parameter unfoldings of such an orientable cycle contain parameter values with attracting periodic circles of arbitrarily large period. Theorem
A
is then obtained by combining this local statement with the genericity of (P1)–(P3) (see Section
2.1.2
) and a Newhouse domain argument. A second point is that, after correcting the Lyapunov coefficient, the rescaled return map can be taken to be the standard Hénon map rather than a larger quadratic Hénon type family; see the next section. This makes the bifurcation analysis and the rescaling argument more direct.

1.2
Correction of the Lyapunov coefficient

For the precise discussion, see Section
4
.

Near a nonresonant Neimark-Sacker (Andronov-Hopf) point, the Lyapunov coefficient must be read from the fully normalized map

\mathrm{tr}\circ\mathrm{map}\circ\mathrm{tr}^{-1}:w\longmapsto\widetilde{w},

not from the partially transformed expression

\mathrm{tr}\circ\mathrm{map}:z\longmapsto\widetilde{w}.

Proposition
4.4
gives the formula used later. As explained in Remark
1.7
(4), omitting
\mathrm{tr}^{-1}
is the normalization error behind
[
5
, Equation (30)]
and the corresponding formula in
[
2
, the equation before Equation (50)]
. The same omission also appears in
[
16
, Section 4, Equation (4.26)]
,
[
14
, Chapter III, Section 1, Exercise 1]
, and in the quoted formula of
[
18
, Theorem 4]
.

For the standard Hénon map
F_{(M_{1},M_{2})}:(X,Y)\longmapsto(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
,

\overline{X}=Y,\qquad\overline{Y}=M_{1}-M_{2}X-Y^{2},

the Neimark-Sacker (Andronov-Hopf) line is

L^{\omega}:=\{(M_{1},M_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid M_{2}=1,\ -1<M_{1}<3\}.

The resonant points on
L^{\omega}
are

C_{1}^{\omega}=(0,1),\qquad C_{2}^{\omega}=\left(\frac{5}{4},1\right).

Proposition
4.6
shows that the corrected coefficient does not vanish on
L^{\omega}\setminus\{C_{1}^{\omega},C_{2}^{\omega}\}
. Hence the standard Hénon map already yields the nondegenerate Neimark-Sacker (Andronov-Hopf) bifurcations used in this paper. Figure
1.2
shows the graph of the coefficient and the bifurcation diagram.

2.1.2
Geometrical conditions

Since both hyperbolic periodic points have one-dimensional unstable directions,

d_{s}:=\dim W^{s}(O_{1}^{*})=\dim W^{s}(O_{2}^{*})=\dim(M_{\mathrm{ph}})-1.

For each
\ell\in\{1,2\}

E_{\ell}^{\lambda}

\lambda_{\ell}^{*}

to

]
. For

. Any two such extended unstable manifolds are tangent along the local unstable manifold.

Near

is a curve and

This condition is independent of the choices of

Remark 2.2
.

Because extended unstable manifolds are tangent along the local unstable manifold, (P2) and (P3) do not depend on the chosen manifolds or on smaller neighborhoods

2.2
Two Parameters

Let
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}}\subset\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M_{\mathrm{ph}})
be a two-parameter family with
f_{\varepsilon^{*}}=f
, where
R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a connected open neighborhood of
\varepsilon^{*}
. We assume that the parameter dependence is of class
C^{r}
, namely that the map

M_{\mathrm{ph}}\times R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}\longrightarrow M_{\mathrm{ph}},\qquad(P,\varepsilon)\longmapsto f_{\varepsilon}(P),

is
C^{r}
. Fix a sufficiently small open disk
R_{\mathrm{prm}}\subset R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}
centered at
\varepsilon^{*}
such that each
O_{\ell}^{*}
has a unique hyperbolic continuation
O_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}
.

Assume that
(f,\Gamma^{*})
satisfies (P1). Let
W^{s}\subset W^{s}(\mathrm{Orb}(O_{1}^{*}))
and
W^{u}\subset W^{u}(\mathrm{Orb}(O_{2}^{*}))
be small neighborhoods of
M_{2\to 1}^{*}
. After shrinking
R_{\mathrm{prm}}
, these sets have continuations
W^{s}_{\varepsilon}\subset W^{s}(\mathrm{Orb}(O_{1}(\varepsilon)))
and
W^{u}_{\varepsilon}\subset W^{u}(\mathrm{Orb}(O_{2}(\varepsilon)))
that depend
C^{r}
on
\varepsilon
. Choose the
splitting parameter
, namely a
C^{r}
function
\mu_{1}
on
R_{\mathrm{prm}}
given by the signed critical value of a local defining function of
W^{s}_{\varepsilon}
restricted to
W^{u}_{\varepsilon}
near
M_{2\to 1}^{*}
.

Let
\lambda_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
and
\gamma_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
be the continuations of
\lambda_{\ell}^{*}
and
\gamma_{\ell}^{*}
. Note that
\lambda_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
and
\gamma_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
are
C^{r-1}
. Set

\sigma_{\ell}(\varepsilon):=|\lambda_{\ell}(\varepsilon)\gamma_{\ell}(\varepsilon)|\qquad(\ell\in\{1,2\}).

Since the cycle is centrally dissipative-expanding,

\sigma_{1}^{*}<1<\sigma_{2}^{*}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\sigma_{2}^{*}<1<\sigma_{1}^{*}.

After shrinking
R_{\mathrm{prm}}
, the same inequalities hold for all
\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}
. Define

\mu_{2}(\varepsilon):=\frac{\log\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon)}{\log\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon)}.

Then
\mu_{2}
is
C^{r-1}
,

\mu_{2}(\varepsilon^{*})=\frac{\log\sigma_{2}^{*}}{\log\sigma_{1}^{*}}<0,

and

\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon)^{i}\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon)^{j}=\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon)^{i+\mu_{2}(\varepsilon)j}\qquad(i,j\in\mathbb{Z}).

Definition 2.4
.

We say that the family
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}}

unfolds properly at
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{*}
with respect to
\Gamma^{*}
if

\det\frac{\partial(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}{\partial\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{*})\neq 0.

A family satisfying this condition will be called a
proper unfolding family
of
(f,\Gamma^{*})
.

Remark 2.5
.

Under (P1)–(P3), local perturbations with disjoint supports near
M_{2\to 1}^{*}
and near one periodic point produce a proper unfolding family of
(f,\Gamma^{*})
.

Convention 2.6
.

Assume
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}}
unfolds properly. After shrinking
R_{\mathrm{prm}}
if necessary,

R_{\mathrm{prm}}\ni\varepsilon\longmapsto\bigl(\mu_{1}(\varepsilon),\mu_{2}(\varepsilon)\bigr)

is a
C^{r-1}
diffeomorphism onto its image. Henceforth we sometimes use
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
as parameter and write

. In particular,

\varepsilon^{*}=\left(0,\frac{\log\sigma_{2}^{*}}{\log\sigma_{1}^{*}}\right).

2.3.1
Approximately linearized coordinates

For the rest of Section
2
, assume
(f,\Gamma^{*})
satisfies (P1)–(P3), and
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}}
is a proper unfolding family with
f_{\varepsilon^{*}}=f
.

For each
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, let

T_{\ell}=T_{\ell}(\varepsilon;f,U_{\ell}^{*},\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}}):=f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{per}(O_{\ell}^{*})}|_{U_{\ell}^{*}},\qquad\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}.

Let
\lambda_{\ell,d_{s}}(\varepsilon),\lambda_{\ell,d_{s}-1}(\varepsilon),\dots,\lambda_{\ell,2}(\varepsilon)
be the continuations of the remaining stable multipliers of
O_{\ell}^{*}
. Since
O_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
is a hyperbolic fixed point of
T_{\ell}
with one-dimensional weak stable and unstable directions and a
(d_{s}-1)
-dimensional strong stable complement,
[
6
, Lemma 6]
yields the following normal form.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
6
, Lemma 6]
, adapted)
.

After shrinking
R_{\mathrm{prm}}
if necessary, for every
\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}
there exist
C^{r}
coordinates
(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell})
on
U_{\ell}^{*}
, depending on
\varepsilon
, with
x_{\ell},y_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
and
u_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}-1}
, such that

T_{\ell}:(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell})\longmapsto(\widehat{x}_{\ell},\widehat{y}_{\ell},\widehat{u}_{\ell})

has the form

\displaystyle\widehat{x}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\ell}x_{\ell}+\mathsf{P}_{1}^{(\ell)}(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell},\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\widehat{y}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\ell}y_{\ell}+\mathsf{P}_{2}^{(\ell)}(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell},\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{\ell}u_{\ell}+\mathsf{P}_{3}^{(\ell)}(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell},\varepsilon),

where
\mathbf{A}_{\ell}=\mathbf{A}_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
is a
(d_{s}-1)\times(d_{s}-1)
matrix whose eigenvalues are

\lambda_{\ell,d_{s}},\ \lambda_{\ell,d_{s}-1},\ \dots,\ \lambda_{\ell,2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{1}^{(\ell)}(0,y_{\ell},0,\varepsilon),\ \mathsf{P}_{2}^{(\ell)}(0,y_{\ell},0,\varepsilon),\ \mathsf{P}_{3}^{(\ell)}(0,y_{\ell},0,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\equiv 0,

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{1}^{(\ell)}(x_{\ell},0,u_{\ell},\varepsilon),\ \mathsf{P}_{2}^{(\ell)}(x_{\ell},0,u_{\ell},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\equiv 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{\ell}}\mathsf{P}_{1}^{(\ell)}(0,y_{\ell},0,\varepsilon),\ \partial_{x_{\ell}}\mathsf{P}_{3}^{(\ell)}(0,y_{\ell},0,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\equiv 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{\ell}}\mathsf{P}_{2}^{(\ell)}(x_{\ell},0,u_{\ell},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\equiv 0.

We call such coordinates an
approximately linearized coordinate system
. In these coordinates,
x_{\ell}
is weak stable,
y_{\ell}
is unstable, and
u_{\ell}
is the strong stable coordinate.

Remark 2.8
.

(1)

In an approximately linearized coordinate system,

W^{u}_{\mathrm{loc}}(O_{\ell}(\varepsilon))=\{x_{\ell}=0,\ u_{\ell}=0\},\qquad W^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(O_{\ell}(\varepsilon))=\{y_{\ell}=0\}.

Inside
W^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(O_{\ell}(\varepsilon))
, the local strong stable leaves are the slices

\{x_{\ell}=\mathrm{const},\ y_{\ell}=0\}.

(2)

If
(x_{\ell}^{*},y_{\ell}^{*},u_{\ell}^{*})
is any
C^{r}
coordinate system on
U_{\ell}^{*}
that does not depend on
\varepsilon
, then the coordinate change

(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell},\varepsilon)\longmapsto(x_{\ell}^{*},y_{\ell}^{*},u_{\ell}^{*})

and its first and second partial derivatives with respect to
(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell})
are
C^{r-2}
in
(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell},\varepsilon)
; see the remarks following
[
6
, Lemma 6]
.

(3)

Consequently, each
\mathsf{P}_{\imath}^{(\ell)}
is
C^{r-2}
in
(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell},\varepsilon)
, and the same holds for its first and second partial derivatives with respect to
(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},u_{\ell})
; in particular,
\mathsf{P}_{\imath}^{(\ell)}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\varepsilon)
is
C^{r}
for fixed
\varepsilon
. Indeed, if
\psi
is an
\varepsilon
-independent chart and
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is the chart from Lemma
2.7
, then

\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ T_{\ell}\circ\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi^{-1})\circ(\psi\circ T_{\ell}\circ\psi^{-1})\circ(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi^{-1})^{-1}.

The middle factor is
C^{r}
, and item (2) controls the two coordinate changes. Subtracting the linear part gives the claim.

.

Fix approximately linearized coordinate systems on
U_{1}^{*}
and
U_{2}^{*}
, and write

\mathfrak{F}_{\mathrm{obj}}:=\bigl(f,\Gamma^{*},(U_{1}^{*};x_{1},y_{1},u_{1}),(U_{2}^{*};x_{2},y_{2},u_{2}),M_{1,\mathrm{in}}^{*},M_{1,\mathrm{out}}^{*},M_{2,\mathrm{in}}^{*},M_{2,\mathrm{out}}^{*},V_{1}^{*},V_{2}^{*},\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*}}\bigr).

Unless stated otherwise, whenever

•

we shrink
R_{\mathrm{prm}}
or
\delta_{\mathrm{dom}}
(see Section
2.5.2
) or

•

enlarge
\kappa_{0}
(see the next section),

the new quantities depend only on
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathrm{obj}}
.

For

\displaystyle T_{1\to 2}=T_{1\to 2}(\varepsilon;\mathfrak{F}_{\mathrm{obj}}):=f_{\varepsilon}^{N_{1\to 2}}|_{V_{1}^{*}},\qquad T_{2\to 1}=T_{2\to 1}(\varepsilon;\mathfrak{F}_{\mathrm{obj}}):=f_{\varepsilon}^{N_{2\to 1}}|_{V_{2}^{*}}.

After shrinking

if necessary,
T_{1\to 2}(V_{1}^{*})\subset U_{2}^{*}
and
T_{2\to 1}(V_{2}^{*})\subset U_{1}^{*}
for
\varepsilon\in R_{\mathrm{prm}}
. In approximately linearized coordinates, their coordinate expressions on these neighborhoods and their first and second partial derivatives with respect to
(x_{1},y_{1},u_{1})
or
(x_{2},y_{2},u_{2})
are
C^{r-2}
in these variables and
\varepsilon
; see Remark
2.8
(2), (3).

For
y_{2}
near the
y_{2}
-coordinate
y_{2}^{*}
of

, write

\Phi(y_{2},\varepsilon):=\operatorname{pr}_{y_{1}}\bigl(T_{2\to 1}(0,y_{2},0)\bigr).

Lemma 2.12
.

After shrinking
R_{\mathrm{prm}}
if necessary, there exist unique families of points

M_{1,\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon),\ M_{1,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon)\in U_{1}^{*},\qquad M_{2,\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon),\ M_{2,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon)\in U_{2}^{*}

of class
C^{r-2}
in
\varepsilon
such that:

•

M_{1,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon)\in W^{u}_{\mathrm{loc}}(O_{1}(\varepsilon))
,

M_{2,\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon)=T_{1\to 2}(M_{1,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon))\in W^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(O_{2}(\varepsilon)),

and

M_{1,\mathrm{out}}=(0,y_{1}^{\mathrm{out}},0),\qquad M_{2,\mathrm{in}}=(x_{2}^{\mathrm{in}},0,u_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}).

•

The function
\Phi(\cdot,\varepsilon)
has a unique critical point
y_{2}^{\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon)
near
y_{2}^{*}
. Set

M_{2,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon):=(0,y_{2}^{\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon),0),\qquad T_{2\to 1}(M_{2,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon))=(x_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon),y_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon),u_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon)),

and define

M_{1,\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon):=(x_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon),0,u_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon)).

Then

y_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon)=\Phi(y_{2}^{\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon).

•

At
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{*}
,

M_{1,\mathrm{in}}=M_{1,\mathrm{in}}^{*},\quad M_{1,\mathrm{out}}=M_{1,\mathrm{out}}^{*},\quad M_{2,\mathrm{in}}=M_{2,\mathrm{in}}^{*},\quad M_{2,\mathrm{out}}=M_{2,\mathrm{out}}^{*}.

Proof.

Since the heteroclinic connection from
\mathrm{Orb}(O_{1}^{*})
to
\mathrm{Orb}(O_{2}^{*})
is transverse at
M_{1\to 2}^{*}
, after shrinking
R_{\mathrm{prm}}
the manifolds
W^{u}(\mathrm{Orb}(O_{1}(\varepsilon)))
and
W^{s}(\mathrm{Orb}(O_{2}(\varepsilon)))
meet in a unique point near
M_{1\to 2}^{*}
. Transporting that point along the orbit segment of length
N_{1\to 2}
defines unique points
M_{1,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon)
and
M_{2,\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon)
such that

M_{2,\mathrm{in}}=T_{1\to 2}(M_{1,\mathrm{out}}).

Remark
2.8
(1) gives

M_{1,\mathrm{out}}=(0,y_{1}^{\mathrm{out}},0),\qquad M_{2,\mathrm{in}}=(x_{2}^{\mathrm{in}},0,u_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}).

For the branch through
M_{2,\mathrm{out}}^{*}
,
\Phi
is
C^{r-2}
. By (P1) and Remark
2.2
,

\partial_{y_{2}}\Phi(y_{2}^{*},\varepsilon^{*})=0,\qquad\partial_{y_{2}}^{2}\Phi(y_{2}^{*},\varepsilon^{*})\neq 0.

Hence the implicit function theorem yields a unique
C^{r-2}
function
y_{2}^{\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon)
near
y_{2}^{*}
such that

\partial_{y_{2}}\Phi(y_{2}^{\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)=0.

Set

M_{2,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon)=(0,y_{2}^{\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon),0),\qquad T_{2\to 1}(M_{2,\mathrm{out}}(\varepsilon))=(x_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon),y_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon),u_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}(\varepsilon)),

and define

At

, the transverse intersection and the critical point are the starred ones, so

M_{1,\mathrm{in}}=M_{1,\mathrm{in}}^{*},\quad M_{1,\mathrm{out}}=M_{1,\mathrm{out}}^{*},\quad M_{2,\mathrm{in}}=M_{2,\mathrm{in}}^{*},\quad M_{2,\mathrm{out}}=M_{2,\mathrm{out}}^{*}.

Thus the four families are of class
C^{r-2}
in

.
∎

, define

\displaystyle A_{12}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{x_{1}}\overline{x}_{2}\right|_{M_{1,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle B_{12}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{y_{1}}\overline{x}_{2}\right|_{M_{1,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle C_{12}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{x_{1}}\overline{y}_{2}\right|_{M_{1,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle D_{12}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{y_{1}}\overline{y}_{2}\right|_{M_{1,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle E_{12}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{x_{1}}\overline{u}_{2}\right|_{M_{1,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle F_{12}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{y_{1}}\overline{u}_{2}\right|_{M_{1,\mathrm{out}}},

and at

define

\displaystyle A_{21}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{x}_{1}\right|_{M_{2,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle B_{21}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{y_{2}}\overline{x}_{1}\right|_{M_{2,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle C_{21}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{y}_{1}\right|_{M_{2,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle E_{21}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{u}_{1}\right|_{M_{2,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle F_{21}

\displaystyle=\left.\partial_{y_{2}}\overline{u}_{1}\right|_{M_{2,\mathrm{out}}},

\displaystyle G_{21}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left.\partial_{y_{2}}^{2}\overline{y}_{1}\right|_{M_{2,\mathrm{out}}}.

Because

is a critical point of

C_{21}&0\end{pmatrix}

By Taylor expansion at

and
M_{2,\mathrm{out}}

2.12

Then the following truncated Taylor representations hold:

\displaystyle\overline{x}_{20}-x_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=A_{12}x_{1i}+B_{12}(y_{1i}-y_{1}^{\mathrm{out}})+\mathsf{R}^{(1)}_{1},

\displaystyle=C_{12}x_{1i}+D_{12}(y_{1i}-y_{1}^{\mathrm{out}})+\mathsf{R}^{(1)}_{2},

Remark 2.14
.

if necessary, the geometric conditions admit the following algebraic interpretation.

•

-axis, so

4
Bifurcations of Fixed Points in the standard Hénon Map

4.1
On the Lyapunov coefficient

Let
g
be a
C^{4}
diffeomorphism of a closed
C^{4}
manifold
M_{\mathrm{ph}}
, and let
Q
be a periodic point of period
\mathrm{per}(Q)
. Assume that
Dg^{\mathrm{per}(Q)}(Q)
has simple unit multipliers

\nu=\cos\psi+\mathrm{i}\sin\psi,\qquad\overline{\nu}=\cos\psi-\mathrm{i}\sin\psi,\qquad\psi\in(0,\pi),

and that no other multiplier lies on the unit circle.
Set

\Psi_{\mathrm{reg}}:=\left\{\psi\in(0,\pi)\middle|\,\psi\notin\frac{2\pi}{j}\mathbb{Z}\text{ for every }j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\right\}:=(0,\pi)\setminus\left\{\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{2\pi}{3}\right\}.

Assume in addition that
\psi\in\Psi_{\mathrm{reg}}
. By the center manifold theorem,
Q
has a two-dimensional local center manifold
W^{c}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q)
; see
[
15
]
and
[
12
, Section 5A]
. This manifold is not unique and in general only finitely smooth; see
[
21
, Section 5.10.2]
. Hence we fix one of class
C^{4}
. After restricting to a smaller neighborhood
W^{c}\subset W^{c}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q)
and choosing a
C^{4}
complex coordinate
z
on
W^{c}
, write

g^{\mathrm{per}(Q)}|_{W^{c}}:z\longmapsto\widetilde{z}=\nu z+\sum_{2\leq p+q\leq 3}\widetilde{z}^{(pq)}z^{p}\overline{z}^{q}+O(|z|^{4}),\qquad p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Definition 4.1
.

The quadratic change

w:=z+\sum_{p+q=2}\frac{\widetilde{z}^{(pq)}}{\nu-\nu^{p}\overline{\nu}^{q}}z^{p}\overline{z}^{q}

is well defined, because
\nu-\nu^{p}\overline{\nu}^{q}\neq 0
for every
(p,q)
with
p+q=2
. It removes the quadratic terms, so in the
w
coordinate the map becomes

\widetilde{w}:=\nu w+\sum_{p+q=3}\widetilde{w}^{(pq)}w^{p}\overline{w}^{q}+O(|w|^{4}).

See, for example,
[
23
, Sections 7, 8]
,
[
14
, Chapter III]
,
[
17
, Sections 6, 6A]
, or
[
1
, Section 2.8]
.
Define

\alpha(Q;z):=\widetilde{w}^{(21)},\qquad\mathrm{LC}(Q;z):=\Re(\overline{\nu}\alpha(Q;z)).

We call
\mathrm{LC}(Q;z)
the
Lyapunov coefficient
of
Q
in the coordinate
z
.

Remark 4.2
.

Because
\psi\in\Psi_{\mathrm{reg}}
, the cubic change

\zeta:=w+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+q=3\\
(p,q)\neq(2,1)\end{subarray}}\frac{\widetilde{w}^{(pq)}}{\nu-\nu^{p}\overline{\nu}^{q}}w^{p}\overline{w}^{q}.

is well defined, because
\nu-\nu^{p}\overline{\nu}^{q}\neq 0
for every
(p,q)
with
p+q=3
and
(p,q)\neq(2,1)
. It removes the nonresonant cubic terms, so

\widetilde{\zeta}:=\nu\zeta+\alpha(Q;z)\zeta^{2}\overline{\zeta}+O(|\zeta|^{4}).

See the references above. Since
|\nu|=1
,

|\widetilde{\zeta}|^{2}=|\zeta|^{2}+2\mathrm{LC}(Q;z)|\zeta|^{4}+O(|\zeta|^{5}),

hence

|\widetilde{\zeta}|=|\zeta|+\mathrm{LC}(Q;z)|\zeta|^{3}+O(|\zeta|^{4}).

Consequently, if
\mathrm{LC}(Q;z)>0
, then
Q
is weakly repelling on
W^{c}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q)
, and if
\mathrm{LC}(Q;z)<0
, then
Q
is weakly attracting there.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume
\psi\in\Psi_{\mathrm{reg}}
. If
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are two local complex coordinates on
W^{c}
such that

g^{\mathrm{per}(Q)}|_{W^{c}}:z_{j}\longmapsto\widetilde{z}_{j}=\nu z_{j}+O(|z_{j}|^{2}),\qquad j\in\{1,2\},

then there exists
\kappa>0
such that

\mathrm{LC}(Q;z_{2})=\kappa\,\mathrm{LC}(Q;z_{1}).

In particular, the sign and the vanishing of
\mathrm{LC}(Q;z)
are independent of the chosen adapted local complex coordinate.

Proof.

For

, let

be the quadratic normalization from Definition
4.1
, and define
\zeta_{j}
from

exactly as in Remark
4.2
. Then

\widetilde{\zeta_{j}}:=\nu\zeta_{j}+\alpha_{j}\zeta_{j}^{2}\overline{\zeta}_{j}+O(|\zeta_{j}|^{4}),\qquad\alpha_{j}=\alpha(Q;z_{j}).

Definition
4.1
gives

\mathrm{LC}(Q;z_{j})=\Re(\overline{\nu}\alpha_{j}).

Write

\zeta_{1}=\phi(\zeta_{2})=A\zeta_{2}+B\overline{\zeta}_{2}+O(|\zeta_{2}|^{2}).

The linear terms in

give

A\nu\zeta_{2}+B\overline{\nu}\overline{\zeta}_{2}=\nu A\zeta_{2}+\nu B\overline{\zeta}_{2}.

Since

, we get

. Because

is a local diffeomorphism, this implies

. Therefore

Comparing quadratic terms and nonresonant cubic terms in

for every

and every

Comparing the coefficient of

in the two normal forms gives

The claim follows with

4.2
Lyapunov coefficient for the standard Hénon map

Lemma 4.5
.

For the standard Hénon map
F_{(M_{1},M_{2})}:(X,Y)\longmapsto(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
,

\overline{X}=Y,\qquad\overline{Y}=M_{1}-M_{2}X-Y^{2},

(4.3)

the fixed points are

P_{\pm}=(X_{\pm},X_{\pm}),\qquad X_{\pm}=\frac{-(1+M_{2})\pm\sqrt{(1+M_{2})^{2}+4M_{1}}}{2},

whenever
(1+M_{2})^{2}+4M_{1}>0
. The point
P_{+}
has multipliers
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\psi}
for a unique
\psi\in(0,\pi)
if and only if
M_{2}=1
and
-1<M_{1}<3
. In that case,

X_{+}=-\cos\psi,\qquad M_{1}=\cos^{2}\psi-2\cos\psi.

Moreover,
\psi\longmapsto\cos^{2}\psi-2\cos\psi
is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism from
(0,\pi)
onto
(-1,3)
.

Proof.

The fixed points satisfy
X=Y
and
X^{2}+(1+M_{2})X-M_{1}=0
. At a fixed point
P=(x,x)
,

DF_{(M_{1},M_{2})}(P)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-M_{2}&-2x\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det(\lambda I-DF_{(M_{1},M_{2})}(P))=\lambda^{2}+2x\lambda+M_{2}.

If the multipliers of
P_{+}
are
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\psi}
, then their product gives
M_{2}=1
and their sum gives
x=-\cos\psi
. Substituting this into
x^{2}+2x-M_{1}=0
yields
M_{1}=\cos^{2}\psi-2\cos\psi
. Conversely, if
M_{2}=1
and
-1<M_{1}<3
, then
X_{+}\in(-1,1)
, so
X_{+}=-\cos\psi
for a unique
\psi\in(0,\pi)
, and the characteristic polynomial is
\lambda^{2}-2(\cos\psi)\lambda+1
. Finally,

\frac{d}{d\psi}(\cos^{2}\psi-2\cos\psi)=2\sin\psi(1-\cos\psi)>0

for
\psi\in(0,\pi)
.
∎

\displaystyle\Re\left(|\widetilde{z}^{(02)}|^{2}\frac{4-2\overline{\nu}^{3}}{-2+\nu^{3}+\overline{\nu}^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2-3c+4c^{3}}{16(1+c)(-1+c)^{2}(1+2c)^{2}},

\displaystyle\Re\left(|\widetilde{z}^{(11)}|^{2}\frac{2\overline{\nu}-\overline{\nu}^{2}}{(-1+\overline{\nu})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2+c}{8(1+c)(-1+c)^{2}},

\displaystyle\Re\left(-\widetilde{z}^{(11)}\widetilde{z}^{(20)}\frac{2-6\overline{\nu}+\overline{\nu}^{2}}{(-1+\nu)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-3(-2+c)}{16(1+c)(-1+c)^{2}}.

Thus, Proposition
4.4
gives (
4.4
).

The proof of the main theorem

5.1
Attracting periodic circles arbitrarily near cycles

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
(f,\Gamma^{*})
is
orientable
if it satisfies the following condition (P4):

•

(P4) For the signature
\varsigma_{ij}
defined in Lemma
3.2
, there exists
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
such that
\varsigma_{ij}=1
.

Remark 5.2
.

(1)

The property (P4) implies infinitely many
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
such that
\varsigma_{ij}=1
. Indeed,
\varsigma_{ij}
depends only on
i,j\bmod 2
, and
\mathcal{I}
contains infinitely many pairs with any prescribed such remainders.

(2)

The property (P4) does not depend on the choice of approximately linearized coordinate systems or base points. This is clear from
-B_{21}C_{21}J_{12}=\det\mathbf{J}_{12}\det\mathbf{J}_{21}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 5}\sqcup\{\infty\}
and
\dim(M_{\mathrm{ph}})\geq 3
. Suppose that
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M_{\mathrm{ph}})
has a centrally dissipative-expanding transversal and non-transversal heteroclinic cycle
\Gamma^{*}
of type two bi-saddles with one-dimensional unstable directions, and that
(f,\Gamma^{*})
satisfies
(P1)
–
(P4)
. Then, for every sequence
\{s_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 5}
with

s_{k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\infty\text{ if }r=\infty,\qquad s_{k}=r\text{ if }r<\infty,

there exist sequences

\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset R_{\mathrm{prm}}^{*},\qquad\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{>0},

such that

\varepsilon_{k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\varepsilon^{*},\qquad\tau_{k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\infty,

and
f_{\varepsilon_{k}}
has an attracting normally hyperbolic periodic
C^{s_{k}}
circle of period
\tau_{k}
for every
k
.

Proof.

Fix
\{s_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. By Remark
5.2
, we can choose
\{(i_{k},j_{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{I}
such that

\varsigma_{i_{k}j_{k}}=1,\qquad\min\{i_{k},j_{k}\}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\infty.

Choose
M_{1}^{\dagger}\in(0,3)\setminus\{5/4\}
. For all large
k
, let
\widehat{L}_{k}^{\omega}\subset\Delta_{i_{k}j_{k}}
be the bifurcation curve corresponding to
L^{\omega}
, let
\varepsilon_{k}^{\dagger}
be the point corresponding to
(M_{1}^{\dagger},1)
under the rescaling of Lemma
3.2
, and let
Q_{k}^{\dagger}
be a fixed point of
T_{i_{k}j_{k}}(\varepsilon_{k}^{\dagger};\mathfrak{F}_{\mathrm{obj}})
whose multipliers are
\nu_{k},\overline{\nu_{k}}
and whose other multipliers have modulus
<1
.

By the center manifold theorem, the local dynamics of
T_{i_{k}j_{k}}(\varepsilon_{k}^{\dagger};\mathfrak{F}_{\mathrm{obj}})
at
Q_{k}^{\dagger}
reduces to a
C^{s_{k}}
surface map. By Lemma
3.2
and the estimate at the end of its proof, this center map is
C^{4}
-close to the standard Hénon map at
(M_{1}^{\dagger},1)
for all large
k
. Hence, by Definition
4.1
, Proposition
4.6
, and Proposition
4.3
, its Lyapunov coefficient is negative for all large
k
.

Let
\widehat{\Gamma}_{k}\subset\Delta_{i_{k}j_{k}}
be a
C^{\infty}
curve through
\varepsilon_{k}^{\dagger}
transverse to
\widehat{L}_{k}^{\omega}
. Apply the Neimark-Sacker (Andronov-Hopf) theorem to the
C^{r}
one-parameter family
T_{i_{k}j_{k}}|_{\widehat{\Gamma}_{k}}
at
\varepsilon_{k}^{\dagger}
; see
[
23
, theorem 7.2]
,
[
17
, theorem 6.2]
, or
[
16
, Section 4]
. Note that this theorem requires
r\geq 5
. Then there exists a parameter

\varepsilon_{k}\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{k}

arbitrarily close to
\varepsilon_{k}^{\dagger}
such that
T_{i_{k}j_{k}}(\varepsilon_{k};\mathfrak{F}_{\mathrm{obj}})
has an attracting invariant
C^{s_{k}}
circle
C_{k}
. Since the remaining multipliers at
Q_{k}^{\dagger}
are strictly inside the unit disk,
C_{k}
is normally hyperbolic. By Lemma
3.2
,

\sup_{\varepsilon\in\Delta_{i_{k}j_{k}}}\|\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{*}\|\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0,

so
\varepsilon_{k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\varepsilon^{*}
.

Set

\tau_{k}:=i_{k}\mathrm{per}(O_{1}^{*})+N_{1\to 2}+j_{k}\mathrm{per}(O_{2}^{*})+N_{2\to 1}.

Since

T_{i_{k}j_{k}}(\varepsilon_{k};\mathfrak{F}_{\mathrm{obj}})=f_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\big|_{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}}

is the first-return map along the fixed itinerary, we have

Hence

is an attracting normally hyperbolic periodic

Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
A
.

If
r<\infty
, set
s_{n}:=r
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If
r=\infty
, fix a sequence
\{s_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 5}
as in the statement. Replacing
s_{n}
with
\max\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
, we may assume that
\{s_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is nondecreasing.

Applying the Newhouse domain construction to our cycle, as in
[
8
]
and its multidimensional version
[
6
, Section 1.2]
, we obtain an open set
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M_{\mathrm{ph}})
and a dense subset
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cycle}}\subset\mathcal{N}
such that
f\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}
and every
g\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cycle}}
has a centrally dissipative-expanding transversal and non-transversal heteroclinic cycle of type two bi-saddles with one-dimensional unstable directions.

For each
g\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cycle}}
, Corollary
5.5
gives sequences
\{f_{g,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
,
\{\mathcal{U}_{g,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, and
\{\tau_{g,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
f_{g,k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}g
,
\tau_{g,k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\infty
, and every
h\in\mathcal{U}_{g,k}
has an attracting normally hyperbolic periodic
C^{s_{k}}
circle of period
\tau_{g,k}
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, set

\mathcal{O}_{n}:=\bigcup_{g\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cycle}}}\,\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\\
k\geq n,\ \tau_{g,k}\geq n\end{subarray}}\mathcal{U}_{g,k}.

Then
\mathcal{O}_{n}
is open. It is dense in
\mathcal{N}
: if
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{N}
is nonempty and open, choose
g\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cycle}}\cap\mathcal{W}
. Since
f_{g,k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}g
and
\tau_{g,k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\infty
, for all large
k
we have
k\geq n
,
f_{g,k}\in\mathcal{W}
, and
\tau_{g,k}\geq n
. Hence
\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{U}_{g,k}\subset\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{O}_{n}
is nonempty.

\mathcal{R}:=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}\mathcal{O}_{n}

is residual in
\mathcal{N}
. If
h\in\mathcal{R}
, then for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
there exist
g\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cycle}}
and
k\geq n
such that
h\in\mathcal{U}_{g,k}
. Thus
h
has an attracting normally hyperbolic periodic
C^{s_{k}}
circle of period at least
n
. Since
\{s_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is nondecreasing and
k\geq n
, this circle is also
C^{s_{n}}
. Taking
\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{N}
proves Theorem
A
.
∎
