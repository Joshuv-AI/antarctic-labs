Physics guided semantic consistency learning for bearing fault diagnosis in agricultural machinery under operating condition shifts
Authors: Zhenlong Li, Shanyu Piao, Haowei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRolling bearings in agricultural machinery operate under pronounced operating-condition shifts, such as speed and load fluctuations and contamination-related noise, which often induce a distribution mismatch between training and deployment signals. This work studies bearing fault diagnosis under a source-only, single-source domain generalization (DG) setting, where the model is trained and selected using only source-domain data, and samples from the target operating condition or target dataset are not used for training, validation, hyperparameter selection, band-pass selection, or early stopping. We formulate cross-condition robustness as a semantic consistency problem between two complementary representations: an analytic mechanism-oriented representation emphasizing impact-related resonance demodulation, and a data-driven temporal representation learned from raw waveforms. A dual-path framework is developed accordingly. The analytic path learns a differentiable soft band-pass mask to localize an informative resonance band and constructs an impulse-oriented descriptor from statistics of the band-pass signal, its Hilbert envelope, and squared-envelope energy. The temporal path encodes normalized raw segments using a lightweight dilated one-dimensional convolutional network with temporal-attention pooling. The two embeddings are fused by a sample-wise gate, with an entropy penalty used to discourage near-uniform averaging. This design aims to improve cross-condition generalization by using the analytic path as a mechanism-oriented semantic anchor, constraining the temporal path through cross-view agreement, and allowing the fused representation to adapt to sample-dependent reliability changes. To reduce representation drift under regime changes, the two views are aligned using a bidirectional InfoNCE objective with a learnable temperature that adapts similarity scaling across operating conditions. A mechanism-critical control is also reported: replacing the analytic path with same-dimensional non-mechanistic features consistently degrades cross-condition performance, indicating that the analytic anchor is not interchangeable with generic auxiliary branches. Experiments on CWRU, SEU, and an agricultural-machinery-relevant test-rig dataset show in-domain accuracies of 99.48%, 98.50%, and 97.53%, respectively. In strict cross-speed evaluation on the test-rig dataset, the method achieves 98.22% accuracy when trained at 1500 r/min and tested at 2000 r/min, and 98.03% accuracy in the reverse setting. In cross-dataset evaluation using the shared normal, inner-race, outer-race, and rolling-element fault classes, the proposed method achieves the best average performance among the evaluated source-only baselines, including generic DG methods and recent bearing-diagnosis generalization methods. These comparisons include DPICEN and a protocol-matched single-source adaptation of FARNet, denoted FARNet-SS, both evaluated without target-domain access during training or model selection. Introduction

Agricultural machinery (e.g., tractors, combine harvesters, seeding equipment, and plant-protection equipment) typically operates with strong seasonality, short service windows, and high-intensity duty cycles. Rolling bearings in transmission, travel, and working mechanisms are exposed to impact loads, frequent start–stop cycles, and speed/load fluctuations, while operating in contamination-rich environments (dust, mud-water mixtures, and straw debris)1. Compared with general industrial settings, agricultural bearings are more susceptible to seal-related contaminant ingress, abrasive wear, and lubrication deterioration caused by grease contamination with dust and plant fibers, which can accelerate the progression from incipient spalling/pitting to failure events2. Long-term operation under such conditions poses risks to system safety and stability3. In addition, variable-speed and variable-load regimes can attenuate fault-related impulsive components and increase masking by operating components and noise4, aggravating feature extraction for complex bearing structures under harsh environments5. Consequently, agricultural bearing diagnosis requires not only high classification accuracy but also stable generalization under strong noise and wide-range speed fluctuations.

Early mechanical fault diagnosis largely relied on handcrafted features and conventional classifiers. Time-domain statistics (e.g., mean, variance, crest factor, kurtosis) remain practical baselines due to their simplicity and interpretability6. However, under wide speed ranges and strong noise, static statistics are often insufficient to disentangle operating-condition-induced amplitude/band migration from fault-induced impact modulation, motivating a shift toward mechanism-oriented modulation extraction and robust enhancement.

For speed-varying signals, time–angle conversion techniques (time synchronous averaging, angular resampling, and order analysis) can align speed-dependent components in the angular domain and reduce harmonic smearing, thereby emphasizing periodic disturbances associated with defects7. Their applicability in agricultural scenarios is constrained by limited availability of accurate speed references and an increased proportion of transients under abrupt load changes, which can degrade synchronization quality. To reduce reliance on speed references, resonance demodulation and modulation feature extraction (e.g., envelope analysis and cepstrum) have been used to highlight impact modulation within resonance bands8. Nevertheless, strong background noise, weak impacts, and resonance-band drift due to structural/assembly variations can lead to suboptimal band selection and ineffective demodulation.

Cyclostationary analysis provides an alternative mechanism-oriented route by characterizing correlations among modulation bands (e.g., spectral correlation/cyclic spectrum), which can preserve defect-related criteria under strong noise and interference9. Related approaches such as spectral kurtosis and kurtogram exploit impact-induced non-Gaussianity to localize resonance intervals favorable for demodulation, reducing dependence on manual band selection10. Practical challenges remain under wide-range operating variations: modulation structures vary with speed/load, affecting the discriminative power of fixed-scale criteria; and multi-source field excitations (working mechanisms, road/soil inputs, gear meshing) can generate pseudo-impulses that confound non-Gaussianity indicators.

To recover weak fault impacts in low-SNR conditions, enhancement-first strategies have been developed, including minimum entropy deconvolution and its variants11, as well as correlation-kurtosis enhancement targeting periodic impacts12. Sparse representation and dictionary learning further decompose signals into transient-sensitive atoms to improve noise robustness13. These approaches introduce additional hyperparameters (regularization strength, iteration stopping criteria) and may require stronger adaptivity under wide operating ranges to avoid over- or mis-enhancement14.

As operating conditions become more complex, multi-source feature fusion is frequently adopted to improve information coverage. However, direct concatenation or fixed-weight aggregation cannot accommodate condition-dependent variation in feature reliability. Related industrial monitoring studies have also explored structure-aware modeling and staged decision pipelines (e.g., two-stage graph-convolution frameworks for detection and identification) to improve robustness under complex noise and interference15. Under low-speed heavy-load regimes, impact modulation and resonance-band energy may be more informative, whereas under high-speed light-load or strongly noisy segments, more robust statistics or temporal textures may dominate. Without per-sample adaptivity, feature fusion may either introduce condition-specific noise or increase redundancy, thereby exacerbating overfitting and reducing interpretability16.

Optimization algorithms have been used for feature selection and hyperparameter tuning to balance performance, robustness, and complexity17. Differential evolution and CMA-ES provide effective search over continuous spaces18; multi-objective strategies such as NSGA-II jointly consider accuracy and complexity19; Bayesian optimization improves sample efficiency when evaluations are costly. StMA (Sterna Migration Algorithm) has also been explored for feature selection and parameter search in complex spaces20. In agricultural monitoring, the relevant question is whether the optimization objective aligns with deployment constraints, including robustness under operating variations, computational cost, and interpretability.

Traditional machine learning methods (e.g., SVM and tree ensembles) remain competitive for small-sample, high-dimensional settings21. Deep learning has advanced fault diagnosis toward end-to-end representation learning, with CNN-based models extracting discriminative features from raw signals or transformed representations22. However, under strong noise and wide-range speed fluctuations, distribution shifts across speed, load, machine type, and sensor installation can cause end-to-end models to confound operating-condition differences with class semantics, leading to unstable generalization23. Recent deployment-oriented studies therefore emphasize mechanism-consistent inductive bias together with discriminability, under constraints of stability, efficiency, and interpretability24.

Despite these advances, several issues remain for agricultural field conditions. Speed and load fluctuations across working objects, terrain, and process stages change impact intervals, spectral energy distributions, and modulation structures within the same fault type. Mechanism-based analytical methods often require condition-specific reconfiguration (e.g., resonance bands, regularization strength, cyclic-frequency resolution) and may degrade under joint wide-range variability and noise. End-to-end models reduce manual configuration but remain sensitive to distribution mismatch between training and deployment conditions25.

Multi-domain fusion can improve information coverage, yet direct concatenation or fixed-weight aggregation makes it difficult to handle the reality that “feature contributions vary with operating-condition drift.” If the fusion mechanism cannot adaptively adjust weights, two consequences commonly occur: first, weak-contribution features introduce noise under certain operating conditions and dilute discriminative information; second, the expansion of composite feature dimensionality leads to redundant accumulation, making training more prone to overfitting and reducing interpretability26.

Online monitoring of agricultural machinery is typically constrained by edge-side computing resources, power supply, and maintenance convenience. Meanwhile, downtime losses during the operation season are substantial, and false alarms—leading to “unnecessary maintenance and spare-part waste”—also carry clear cost attributes. Hence, diagnostic algorithms must not only improve accuracy, but also reduce false-alarm/missed-detection rates, and achieve stable generalization under controllable computational costs. Deep models often require large amounts of labeled data and relatively high training/inference costs; under data scarcity or strong field noise, model robustness and real-time performance may be limited27. Which has motivated data-efficient paradigms such as optimized few-shot learning for fault diagnosis in other machining domains28.In addition, certain robustness-enhancing strategies (e.g., heavier backbones or complex generative models) may further increase deployment difficulty, conflicting with the economic objectives of edge monitoring for agricultural machinery29.

The principal limitation in existing cross-condition diagnosis studies is not the absence of individual components such as filtering, attention, fusion, or contrastive learning, but the lack of an explicitly stated source-only generalization setting and a testable mechanism hypothesis explaining why stable cross-condition performance can be obtained without using target-domain data. In this study, each speed condition or dataset is treated as a domain, and the target domain is completely held out during training and model selection. The present framework addresses this limitation by coupling a mechanism-oriented analytic descriptor with a high-capacity temporal encoder through an explicit cross-view semantic-consistency objective, while using an entropy-regularized gate to prevent degenerate near-uniform fusion under condition-dependent feature-reliability changes.

To strengthen the comparison with recent task-relevant methods, two bearing-diagnosis generalization baselines are included. DPICEN31 is used as a physical-information-consistency-based baseline for unknown-domain bearing fault diagnosis. FARNet is originally formulated for multi-source domain generalization30; therefore, we implement a protocol-matched single-source variant, denoted FARNet-SS, by retaining its frequency-guided representation design while removing the multi-source training assumption. In this setting, FARNet-SS, DPICEN, and the proposed method are all trained and selected using only the labeled source domain. This design allows recent bearing-diagnosis generalization methods to be compared under the same source-only assumption.

In this study, “toward agricultural machinery reliability” refers to the target application scenario and component relevance. The proposed framework is developed to handle operating-condition shifts motivated by agricultural use, while full in-field validation is discussed as future work.

Unlike hybrid pipelines that treat resonance demodulation as fixed pre-processing, resonance selection is parameterized as a differentiable band-pass mask and optimized jointly with the classifier under cross-condition protocols. In addition, rather than concatenating analytic and deep features or imposing a one-way teacher–student constraint, bidirectional cross-path agreement is enforced using InfoNCE, where same-sample pairs across the two paths are positives and other samples in the mini-batch act as negatives. A sample-wise gate with entropy regularization is used to avoid near-uniform fusion and to expose which path dominates the decision on each sample.

To address operating-condition drift in bearing vibrations and the limited generalization of single-path representations, we develop a dual-channel complementary representation learning framework. The framework integrates a mechanism-consistent analytical pathway with an end-to-end discriminative temporal pathway. The analytical pathway captures impact-related resonance-demodulation statistics through learnable band-pass localization and squared-envelope-energy descriptors, whereas the temporal pathway learns discriminative representations directly from normalized raw waveforms. These two representations are then adaptively fused and constrained by cross-channel semantic consistency to improve robust diagnostic performance under strong noise and wide-range speed variations.

In the analytical feature channel (Channel A), resonance-band selection is parameterized as a differentiable soft band-pass mask and optimized end-to-end, and an impulse-oriented env² statistical descriptor is constructed from the band-pass signal, its Hilbert envelope, and squared-envelope energy to yield a compact, interpretable, and noise-robust representation.

In the end-to-end temporal channel (Channel B), normalized raw segments are encoded by a lightweight dilated one-dimensional convolutional network with temporal-attention pooling, enabling the extraction of discriminative temporal embeddings under non-stationary operating regimes.

The two embeddings are integrated through sample-wise gated fusion, and an entropy-based regularization term is introduced to discourage near-uniform averaging, promoting clearer dominance of the more reliable pathway and improving interpretability.

In addition, bidirectional cross-view alignment is enforced using an InfoNCE objective with a learnable temperature, adapting similarity scaling across operating conditions and strengthening semantic consistency between the two channels for each sample.

The main contributions are summarized as follows:

1. Semantic-consistency formulation for cross-condition diagnosis.

Cross-condition robustness is cast as maintaining semantic agreement between a mechanism-oriented analytic representation and a data-driven temporal representation, trained without target-domain access. A bidirectional InfoNCE objective is used to align the two views, and a learnable temperature is introduced to adapt similarity scaling across operating regimes.

2. Differentiable resonance demodulation with an impulse-oriented env² descriptor.

Resonance-band selection is parameterized as a low-parameter soft band-pass mask and optimized end-to-end. Based on the learned band-pass signal, a compact 12-D descriptor is constructed from statistics of the band-pass signal, its Hilbert envelope, and squared envelope energy (env²), providing an interpretable mechanism-consistent anchor.

3. Sample-wise gated fusion with entropy regularization.

The analytic and temporal embeddings are fused by a per-sample gate. An entropy penalty discourages near-uniform weighting, promoting clearer dominance of the more reliable pathway under operating-condition drift and improving interpretability.

4. Rigorous cross-condition evaluation and mechanism-oriented controls.

Cross-speed and cross-dataset tasks are evaluated under strict protocols with unified label mapping for cross-dataset transfer. Domain gap and representation drift are quantified, and an anchor-replacement control shows that substituting the analytic branch with same-dimensional non-mechanistic features degrades cross-condition performance, supporting the role of a mechanism-consistent anchor beyond generic multi-branch modeling.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 introduces the overall framework and key module designs (resonance-band localization and env² statistical representation, dilated convolutions and temporal attention, gated fusion and entropy sparsity, and cross-channel InfoNCE alignment). Section 3 presents the datasets, experimental settings, and the cross-operating-condition evaluation protocol, and conducts comparative experiments, ablation studies, and robustness analyses. Section 4 concludes the paper and discusses future work.

To address the problem that, under cross-speed and cross-operating-condition settings, the distribution of bearing fault features varies markedly with operating states and the generalization capability of a single representation pathway is limited, this paper proposes a dual-channel complementary representation learning framework. The framework constructs two feature-representation pathways in parallel: one explicitly models the mechanism of bearing impact faults in a data-driven manner, and the other performs end-to-end temporal modeling on the raw vibration waveform. The embedding representations output by the two channels are fused through an adaptive gating mechanism, and a cross-channel alignment constraint is introduced to enhance the modeling of semantic consistency for the same sample. The overall model is jointly optimized in an end-to-end manner. Dual-channel complementary feature modeling

To improve robust generalization under cross-speed and cross-operating-condition settings, this paper constructs two complementary representation pathways in parallel: Channel A explicitly incorporates the resonance mechanism of impact faults and outputs low-dimensional, interpretable statistical representations; Channel B performs end-to-end temporal modeling on the raw waveform to learn high-capacity discriminative representations. The embeddings from the two channels are subsequently fused via gated fusion, and a cross-channel alignment constraint is employed to enhance the semantic consistency for the same sample.

From the perspective of representation learning, the central challenge of cross-operating-condition bearing fault identification lies in the fact that both the statistical properties and temporal structures of vibration signals can change markedly across operating states. A single feature-extraction pathway often becomes biased toward certain feature patterns, thereby weakening the model’s generalization capability under unseen operating conditions. Particularly in variable-speed and cross-platform scenarios, methods that rely on fixed time–frequency structures or purely end-to-end temporal modeling may be insufficient in terms of either robustness or discriminability.

Based on the above considerations, we develop a dual-channel feature modeling framework from two complementary perspectives, namely “mechanism consistency” and “data-driven representational capacity.” Channel A introduces the physical mechanism of bearing impact faults into the model in an explicit, low-dimensional, and interpretable manner through learnable resonance-band localization and envelope-energy statistics. This helps suppress redundant frequency bands that are highly correlated with operating-condition variations and strengthens stable characterization of impact features. Channel B, in contrast, directly performs end-to-end temporal modeling on the raw vibration waveform, learning high-capacity and context-dependent discriminative features via dilated convolutions and a temporal-attention mechanism, thereby compensating for the limited expressive power of mechanism-based statistical features in complex pattern modeling.

This paper does not assume that the two channels achieve a strict functional division or are fully decoupled. Instead, complementarity mainly arises from differences in modeling assumptions and structural inductive biases between the two representation pathways: Channel A outputs a low-dimensional, mechanism-oriented descriptor derived from resonance-demodulated env² statistics; Channel B outputs a high-capacity temporal embedding learned from raw segments. The two paths are not assumed to be functionally independent; instead, they are trained to be semantically consistent for the same sample through an explicit alignment objective, while a gate selects the dominant contribution on a per-sample basis.

Mechanism rationale. The proposed generalization mechanism is based on separating fault-related semantic cues from operating-condition-dependent nuisance variations. Speed changes, load fluctuations, sensor/platform differences, and environmental noise mainly alter amplitude scaling, resonance-band energy distribution, and local temporal texture, whereas the diagnostic label is still associated with impact-induced modulation patterns caused by bearing defects. Channel A is therefore designed as a mechanism-oriented anchor: the learnable band-pass mask localizes an informative resonance band from the source data, and the env² statistics summarize impulse-related envelope-energy behavior in a compact form. Channel B provides higher representational capacity by learning temporal patterns directly from raw waveforms, but this branch may also encode condition-specific temporal textures and thus suffer from representation drift under unseen domains. The bidirectional InfoNCE objective constrains the two views of the same sample to be close while contrasting them with other samples in the mini-batch, which encourages the temporal representation to remain semantically consistent with the mechanism-oriented analytic view. The classification loss preserves fault-class discriminability, whereas the cross-view alignment reduces condition-induced drift. The entropy-regularized gate further avoids indiscriminate averaging and allows the model to assign a clearer dominant pathway for each sample according to the reliability of the two views. Thus, cross-condition generalization is expected to arise from three coupled effects: mechanism-guided anchoring, cross-view semantic alignment, and reliability-aware gated fusion.

Mechanism hypothesis and verifiable predictions

Mechanism hypothesis. Under operating-condition shifts, the observed vibration signal can be regarded as containing both fault-related semantic information and operating-condition-dependent nuisance information. The latter includes amplitude-scale variation, resonance-band migration, background noise, and temporal-texture changes induced by speed/load/platform differences. The proposed framework assumes that the analytic view provides a relatively stable mechanism-oriented cue for bearing defects because it emphasizes resonance-demodulated impulse information through the learnable band-pass mask and env² statistics. In contrast, the temporal view has stronger discriminative capacity but is more likely to absorb condition-specific temporal patterns when trained only with classification supervision.

Based on this hypothesis, the role of bidirectional InfoNCE is not to perform target-domain alignment, because no target-domain samples are available during training. Instead, it performs source-domain cross-view semantic alignment: for each source sample, the mechanism-oriented embedding and the temporal embedding are encouraged to represent the same diagnostic semantics. This constraint is expected to reduce the tendency of the temporal pathway to drift toward condition-specific cues. The entropy-regularized gate then assigns sample-wise reliability weights to the two views, so that the fused representation can rely more clearly on the mechanism-oriented or temporal pathway according to the signal characteristics of each sample.

Operational tests. The hypothesis is examined by three complementary analyses: (i) comparing domain discrepancy before and after the learnable band-pass operation at the signal level; (ii) quantifying whether bidirectional InfoNCE reduces the MMD of Channel-B embeddings compared with training without alignment; and (iii) testing whether the gate assigns higher weight to Channel A when the impact-strength indicator , computed as the kurtosis of squared-envelope energy, increases.

Testable predictions.

(1)The band-pass signal should show a smaller PSD-based domain gap than the raw normalized signal, indicating that Channel A suppresses part of the operating-condition-dependent spectral variation.

(2) Adding bidirectional InfoNCE should decrease MMD on Channel-B embeddings compared with training without alignment, while maintaining or improving Accuracy and Macro-F1.

(3) Replacing the env² analytic anchor with same-dimensional non-mechanistic features should degrade cross-condition performance if the analytic anchor is genuinely mechanism-relevant rather than merely an auxiliary branch.

(4) The gate should assign higher weight to Channel A when impulse strength increases, measured by , and this tendency can be quantified by Spearman correlation.

Experimental validation and analysis

Experimental datasets and experimental environment

A two-tier evaluation is conducted. Public benchmarks (CWRU and SEU) are used to establish baseline performance for cross-condition generalization and cross-dataset transfer. In addition, a controlled test-rig dataset, Agricultural Machinery Bearing Simulation (AMBS), is included to evaluate speed-induced distribution shifts under a consistent hardware setting. AMBS provides two rotational regimes (1500 r/min and 2000 r/min) for strict cross-speed protocols.

Experiments use three datasets: the Case Western Reserve University bearing dataset (CWRU), obtained from the Case Western Reserve University Bearing Data Center32; the Southeast University mechanical fault dataset (SEU), released in the Mechanical-datasets repository and associated with the work of Shao et al.33; and the self-built AMBS dataset. The CWRU dataset provides seeded fault test data collected from a motor-driven bearing test rig, while the SEU dataset contains bearing and gear sub-datasets acquired on a Drivetrain Dynamic Simulator under different speed-load configurations. In this work, the bearing-related subset and the common fault categories of SEU are used for cross-dataset evaluation. The sampling protocols, fault types, and operating-condition configurations of CWRU and SEU are described in their respective subsections; this subsection details the AMBS setup, sensor placement, and data acquisition.

AMBS testbed and agricultural relevance. AMBS employs a deep-groove ball bearing (SKF 6207), representative of bearing types commonly used in rotating and supporting assemblies of agricultural machinery (e.g., transmission-related rotating components and supporting units). Although AMBS data are collected on a laboratory rig, the testbed enables controlled and reproducible validation of operating-condition shifts relevant to agricultural scenarios. In field operation, regime switches caused by throttle changes, terrain variability, and intermittent load disturbances often lead to non-stationary vibration patterns and distribution shifts. Consistent with this motivation, AMBS is configured with two rotational regimes (1500 r/min and 2000 r/min) to construct strict cross-speed protocols under an unchanged hardware platform. Vibration signals are acquired at 5120 Hz. The test-rig structure, sensor layout, and the host-computer acquisition interface are shown in Figs. 3, 4 and 5.

All experiments are conducted in a server environment with an Intel® Xeon® Gold 6338 CPU, 256 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA A100 GPU (40 GB). The software environment includes Ubuntu 20.04 LTS, Python 3.10, and PyTorch 2.1.0 (CUDA 11.8) with cuDNN 8.7. Hyperparameters are fixed across runs; experiments are repeated with multiple random seeds and the seeds are recorded.

To enlarge the sample set and improve training stability, the collected continuous vibration sequences were segmented into samples using a sliding-window scheme, with the window overlap ratio set to 50%. Each segmented sample was then subjected to Z-score normalization to mitigate the influence of amplitude-scale differences on model training and generalization. The operating conditions, fault categories, and label definitions of the AMBS dataset are summarized in Table 1. Furthermore, to reflect the differences in impact intensity associated with different damage severities, each fault type was subdivided into two labels according to the damage diameter (0.3 mm and 0.6 mm; e.g., IR1/IR2). Consequently, the AMBS dataset contains six fault categories plus one normal category, resulting in a total of seven classes.

Evaluation protocol, comparative methods, and implementation settings

A strict source-only cross-condition protocol is used to evaluate single-source DG performance. Training and testing domains are disjoint, and no target-domain samples are used during training or model selection. Two settings are considered: cross-speed and cross-dataset. In the cross-speed setting, one rotational speed of AMBS is used as the source domain and the other speed is used as the unseen target domain. In the cross-dataset setting, one dataset is used as the source domain and another dataset is used as the unseen target domain after unified label mapping. The proposed model is trained under a fixed implementation configuration. The input window length is 2048 samples, and the sliding stride is 1024 samples, corresponding to an overlap ratio of 50%. Each segment is normalized by sample-wise Z-score normalization before being fed into the model. The batch size is set to 64, and the number of training epochs is set to 40. Adam is used as the optimizer, with an initial learning rate of and a weight decay coefficient of . The embedding dimension is fixed at 128.

For fair comparison, all source-only methods use the same segmented samples, sample-wise Z-score normalization, source-domain training/validation split, and final target-domain test split. Hyperparameters are selected only on the source-domain validation subset. In FARNet-SS, the original multi-source setting is reduced to one labeled source domain, without auxiliary source domains or target-related augmentation. In DPICEN, physical-information encoding is constructed only from source-domain class semantics and source labels. Thus, the proposed method, FARNet-SS, DPICEN, and the generic DG baselines are evaluated under a unified source-only model-selection protocol.

The weight of the bidirectional InfoNCE alignment loss is set to 0.1, and the weight of the gate-entropy sparsity term is set to 0.01. The learnable temperature in the InfoNCE objective is initialized to 0.07 and is constrained to be positive through the softplus function with a numerical-stability constant of . In the 10-fold cross-validation setting, 15% of the training fold is selected in a class-stratified manner as the validation subset. The model with the best validation Macro-F1 is used for testing. For the proposed method and the source-only domain generalization baselines, only source-domain data are used for training and model selection, while target-domain data are used only for final evaluation.

Accuracy and Macro-F1 are used as primary metrics. Under cross-condition protocols, no target-domain labels are accessed during training, and hyperparameter selection is completed within the source domain; the test domain is used only for final reporting. Key settings are repeated with multiple independent runs, and results are reported as mean±standard deviation. In-domain stability analysis uses 10-fold cross-validation. Ablations remove Channel A/Channel B, the cross-channel alignment constraint, and the entropy sparsity regularization to quantify each component’s contribution.

Across the eight cross-condition tasks in Table 10, the proposed method obtains the strongest average performance among the evaluated source-only methods. Compared with CNN-BiGRU, the results indicate that the mechanism-guided dual-path design provides information beyond a purely temporal deep baseline. Compared with recent bearing-diagnosis generalization baselines, including FARNet-SS and DPICEN, the proposed method maintains higher average Accuracy and Macro-F1 under the same source-only model-selection protocol. Some individual task-level margins are small, especially against the strongest time-frequency baseline, so the results should be interpreted as an average cross-condition advantage rather than uniform superiority in every single metric. Together with the ablation and anchor-replacement analyses, these comparisons suggest that the performance gain is associated with the joint use of learnable resonance demodulation, bidirectional cross-view alignment, and sample-wise gated fusion. The task-level domain-gap indicators in Table 6 provide additional context: larger discrepancy values generally coincide with lower cross-condition accuracy across the evaluated protocols.

Complexity and statistical significance

This section reports model complexity and statistical reliability. Complexity is characterized by parameter count (Params), FLOPs for the neural-network forward pass under a fixed input length, and average training time under a unified training configuration. In addition, system-level latency considerations are discussed to clarify the gap between forward-pass FLOPs and end-to-end deployment cost.

Model complexity and efficiency analysis

Params and FLOPs are reported to describe model size and forward-pass computational cost, and average training time is reported as an empirical indicator of training overhead. Params mainly reflect storage scale, while FLOPs approximate the arithmetic cost of a single forward pass under a fixed input length and do not directly equal wall-clock latency.

Under a unified training configuration (window length win = 2048, hop size hop = 1024, batch size 64, and 40 training epochs), the full model achieves accuracies of 99.48 ± 0.2%, 98.5 ± 0.4%, and 97.5 ± 0.5% on CWRU, SEU, and AMBS, respectively, with an average training time of 2.56s. With input length win = 2048, the model size is 0.1348 M parameters and the neural forward-pass cost is 127.11 M FLOPs.

Unless otherwise stated, FLOPs are computed for the neural-network forward inference only. End-to-end inference latency may additionally include preprocessing and analytic transformations in Channel A, such as rFFT, frequency-domain masking, inverse FFT, Hilbert transform for envelope extraction, and computation of env² statistics (Energy/RMS/Peak/Kurtosis on , e, and ). These operations contribute additional time depending on hardware, implementation (CPU/GPU), kernel fusion, and batching strategy. Therefore, Params and FLOPs are reported as hardware-agnostic proxies for complexity, while end-to-end latency should be measured on the target platform when deployment constraints are strict.

Conclusions and future work

Conclusions

This study addresses bearing fault diagnosis under operating-condition shifts relevant to agricultural machinery, where vibration signals exhibit non-stationarity, cross-speed/cross-platform distribution mismatch, and degraded generalization when a single representation pathway is used. A physics–deep semantic consistency learning framework is developed by coupling a mechanism-consistent analytic pathway with an end-to-end temporal encoder within a unified architecture and evaluating it under strict source-only, single-source DG protocols without target-domain access.

Channel A performs learnable resonance-band localization and constructs a compact impulse-oriented descriptor using squared Hilbert-envelope energy (env²) statistics, providing a low-dimensional and interpretable representation. Channel B encodes raw vibration segments using dilated 1D convolutions and temporal-attention pooling to capture long-range temporal structure. Complementarity arises from their different inductive biases rather than a manually imposed functional separation.

A sample-wise gate with entropy regularization is adopted to avoid uniform fusion and to expose the dominant decision pathway per sample. Bidirectional InfoNCE alignment with a learnable temperature is further employed to encourage source-domain cross-view semantic agreement between the analytic and temporal embeddings. The resulting generalization mechanism is therefore attributed to the combination of mechanism-guided anchoring, reduced temporal-representation drift, and reliability-aware gated fusion under unseen operating conditions.

On CWRU, SEU, and AMBS, in-domain accuracies of 99.48%, 98.50%, and 97.53% are achieved. Under cross-speed evaluation on AMBS, accuracies of 98.22% (1500→2000 r/min) and 98.03% (2000→1500 r/min) are obtained. Under cross-dataset evaluation on the unified label set {N, IR, OR, RE}, the method achieves the best average performance among the evaluated source-only baselines, including generic DG methods and recent bearing-diagnosis generalization baselines such as FARNet-SS and DPICEN. Component-wise ablations demonstrate consistent degradation when learnable band-pass localization, env² statistics, entropy-sparse gating, or InfoNCE alignment is removed. The model remains lightweight (0.1348 M parameters; 127.11 M FLOPs for the neural forward pass), supporting deployment under resource-constrained monitoring settings.

Limitations and practical considerations

Although the proposed method achieves stable results across multiple datasets and cross-condition evaluations, its applicability boundaries and potential limitations should still be clarified to inform subsequent research and engineering applications.

(1) Dependence on the quality of impact information.

While the learnable resonance-band localization in Channel A is data-driven and adaptive, its effectiveness still relies on the presence of impact components in the vibration signal that can be demodulated. In scenarios with extremely low signal-to-noise ratio, early-stage incipient faults with weak signatures, or heavy masking by operation-induced impacts, the discriminability of envelope-energy statistics may deteriorate, and the analytic pathway may contribute less under such segments.

(2) Domain shift beyond the training coverage.

The proposed method exhibits strong robustness under cross-speed and cross-dataset (platform-difference) conditions. However, when the test scenario involves bearing types, installation structures, sensor locations/orientations, or drivetrain structural changes that are not covered by training, domain shift may become substantially stronger and model performance may still degrade. In such cases, it may be necessary to introduce a small amount of on-site calibration data, adopt more robust normalization/calibration strategies, or combine the framework with lightweight domain adaptation mechanisms.

(3) Robust configuration of loss weights and the temperature parameter.

The proposed method is optimized jointly with classification supervision, cross-channel alignment, and entropy sparsity regularization, where , , and the initial value and learning process of the temperature parameter affect alignment strength, gating allocation, and the final generalization performance. Although stable results are obtained under a unified setting in this study, more systematic sensitivity analyses and robust configuration strategies may still be required when the noise level, sample size, or class imbalance changes substantially, so as to avoid representation collapse caused by overly strong alignment or insufficient fusion caused by overly strong sparsity.

(4) System-level overhead and real-time constraints in engineering deployment.

Although the proposed model remains lightweight in terms of parameter count and FLOPs, the dual-channel structure may still introduce additional overhead in practice, including data preprocessing, frequency-domain transformations, and module scheduling. Under stricter edge-side real-time constraints or on low-power platforms, inference-framework optimization and operator fusion may be required to further reduce end-to-end latency and energy consumption.

(5) Gap between laboratory benchmarks and in-field agricultural environments.

Although the AMBS testbed provides a controlled platform to evaluate speed-induced distribution shifts, real-field agricultural machinery involves stronger multi-source coupling (e.g., dust/mud contamination, variable lubrication regimes, and complex drivetrain excitations). Therefore, the current results should be interpreted as a feasibility validation of the proposed mechanism under strict cross-condition protocols, and large-scale in-field agricultural datasets are required for further verification.

(6) Unified label mapping may mask fine-grained severity transfer.

In cross-dataset experiments, dataset-specific sublabels (e.g., IR1/IR2, OR1/OR2, RE1/RE2) are merged into super-classes {IR, OR, RE} to ensure a shared label space. While this enables fair transfer evaluation across datasets with different annotation granularities, it may mask fine-grained generalization limitations across fault severities (e.g., transferring between mild and severe defects). Future work will evaluate severity-aware transfer protocols and retain multi-level labels when consistent annotations are available, to quantify performance under fine-grained fault progression.

Research innovations

The main innovations of this study can be summarized as follows:

(1) A physics–deep semantic consistency learning framework is presented for cross-operating-condition bearing diagnosis, coupling a mechanism-consistent analytic descriptor with an end-to-end temporal encoder under a source-only, single-source DG protocol without target-domain access.

(2) Resonance demodulation is reformulated as a differentiable analytical chain via learnable band-pass localization, and a compact env² statistical descriptor is derived to provide an interpretable impulse-oriented representation under noisy conditions.

(3) Sample-wise gated fusion with entropy regularization is introduced to prevent near-uniform averaging and to accommodate condition-dependent feature contribution drift with improved interpretability.

(4) Bidirectional InfoNCE alignment with a learnable temperature is employed to enforce cross-path semantic agreement and to mitigate representation drift across operating regimes and platforms.

Future work

Future research will focus on improving the adaptability, robustness, and engineering usability of the proposed method under more complex field operating conditions and stricter deployment constraints, mainly along the following directions:

(1) introducing adaptive noise-aware and weak-impact enhancement mechanisms to improve the extraction of diagnosable information under extremely low signal-to-noise ratios and early-stage incipient faults;

(2) exploring lightweight domain adaptation strategies that are compatible with the domain generalization setting to address more complex distribution shifts across devices, sensors, and structural configurations;

(3) investigating edge-oriented model compression, operator fusion, and inference acceleration methods to further reduce system-level latency and energy consumption in order to meet real-time monitoring requirements;

(4) extending the proposed framework to multi-sensor fusion scenarios by combining vibration signals with other sensing modalities (e.g., current, acoustics, or temperature) to improve diagnostic reliability and anti-interference capability under complex operating conditions.

(5) Acquisition and validation of in-field agricultural datasets.

Future studies will focus on incorporating large-scale in-field vibration data collected directly from working machinery (e.g., combine harvesters or tractors during tillage). We aim to further refine the model’s domain adaptation and generalization capabilities under diverse, real-world crop-harvesting scenarios to fully bridge the gap between laboratory simulation and practical agricultural engineering.
