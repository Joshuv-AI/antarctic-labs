Executive Summary
Through this effort, X-MAT CCC sought to confirm the utility and commercialization potential of carbon-
derived building materials (CDBM) technology licensed from our partner, Semplastics. These CDBM
products contain at least 52% coal-derived carbon by mass. Including the binders within the resin, the
products contain at least 71% carbon by mass overall, meeting a key criterion for DE-FOA-0002185 – Area
of Interest (AOI) 5 – Design Research and Development, Validation, and Fabrication of a Prototype
Carbon-Based Building. The Phase II project has resulted in a technology demonstrator prototype structure,
a detailed design for a carbon-based building, and updated techno-economic analysis (TEA) including
detailed market surveys to show the commercial viability of CDBM products. We pursued the following
objectives in Phase II: (1) construction of a partial building shell as a technology demonstrator, (2) testing
of CDBM products, individually and in assemblies, (3) demonstration of bonding of CDBM and traditional
building materials (TBM) in structural applications, (4) production of a detailed design for a carbon-based
building, and (5) an update of the TEA that was performed in Phase I. In Phase II, X-MAT CCC and our
industry team performed the development and testing needed to improve the maturity of the technology
from a Technology Readiness Level (TRL) of 5 to TRL 6.

CDBM have been shown by our partner Semplastics to exhibit a number of high-performance
characteristics, including high strength (five times the flexure strength of the best commercial brick, and
more than twice the compressive strength of construction-grade concrete block), lower density, improved
mechanical durability and abrasion resistance, very high temperature stability, and resistance to chemicals,
acids, salts, and water. These properties offer significant improvements over conventional building
materials. Phase II built upon the work accomplished in Phase I by performing technical and economic
research and development to confirm the viability of CDBM as commercial products in various markets.

Introduction
X-MAT CCC conducted this research and development (R&D) project to confirm the utility and
commercialization potential of carbon-derived building materials (CDBM) technology as licensed from our
partner, Semplastics. These CDBM products contain at least 52% coal-derived carbon by mass. Including
the binders within the resin, the products contain at least 71% carbon by mass overall. The project has
resulted in a technology demonstrator prototype structure, a detailed design for a carbon-based building,
and updated techno-economic analysis (TEA) including detailed market surveys to show the commercial
viability of CDBM products. In Phase I, X-MAT CCC and our industry team performed the development
and testing needed to improve the maturity of the technology from a Technology Readiness Level (TRL)
of 4 to TRL 5, and in Phase II, we advanced this technology to TRL 6.

CDBM have been shown by our partner Semplastics to exhibit a number of high-performance
characteristics, including high strength (five times the flexure strength of the best commercial brick, and
more than twice the compressive strength of construction-grade concrete block), lower density, improved
mechanical durability and abrasion resistance, very high temperature stability, and resistance to chemicals,
acids, salts, and water. These properties offer significant improvements over conventional building
materials. Phase II built upon the work accomplished in Phase I by performing technical and economic
research and development to confirm the viability of CDBM as commercial products in various markets.

This work was undertaken to support a secure and consistent channel for the use of significant amounts of
carbon in building materials across North America. Because CDBM are fire-resistant, lightweight, and less
bulky, a major impact can be made on the building materials industry. Advantages of CDBM include: (1)
higher percentage of non-toxic fire-resistant materials in residential and commercial homes; (2) faster
installation time because of lighter materials; (3) greater design flexibility; and (4) lower total cost and
schedule because CDBM can fill both interior and exterior needs. Expected beneficiaries of this work
include owners of both coal mines and coal-based power plants, who will be able to leverage a new use for
their products and/or waste, and the construction industry, who will have access to new high-strength,
lightweight alternative materials.

Phase I Objectives
The primary goals and objectives of the Phase I project were the following:

 1. To develop a conceptual design of dwelling structure composed of coal-derived building materials
 (CDBM) (with at least two walls, a partial roof, and a partial foundation) to test the assembly of
 the prototype building under stress conditions.
 2. To establish the fastening methodology for and between CDBM and traditional building materials
 (TBM) components.
 3. To perform a techno-economic analysis (TEA) to determine economic viability and the market
 penetration possibilities.
 4. To perform a gap analysis and describe the additional work (including R&D and building code
 validation) needed to meet building code requirements for CDBM to achieve market adoption.
 5. To perform a life-cycle analysis (LCA) of CDBM dwelling structures against those composed of
 traditional materials, in terms of emission and byproduct waste reductions, as well as recyclability.
 6. To develop a financial model with theoretical production costs to verify the commercial viability
 of the business model.

Task 3.0 – Component Characterization and Fastening Methodology
The fastening methodology (mortar) to be used in construction of the coal building has been developed and
tested.

Subtask 3.1 – Confirm optimal binder / fastener for CDBM parts

Semplastics has been developing hybrid mortar based on varying sizes of ceramic-coated coal particles as
replacement for the sand in typical mortars (material which we call “ceramic aggregate”). The ceramic
aggregate was found to bond very well to Portland cement and the hybrid material behaved and cured at
the same rate as normal cement-based mortar. The hybrid-coal based mortar utilizes a mix of water, coal-
based ceramic aggregate, Portland cement, and plasticizer.

Semplastics used a 2” × 2” mold to produce industry-typical compression samples for testing. Samples
were made of a standard Type S mortar and of the hybrid coal-based mortar (Figure 1). The coal-based
mortar samples had a density of 1.678 g/cm3 while the Type S mortar samples had a density of 1.739 g/cm3.

Initial work has indicated that conventional cement-based mortar will adhere to even fully fired low porosity

Phase II Objectives
The primary goals and objectives of the Phase II project were the following:

 1. Construct a partial building shell, consisting of at least two walls, a partial roof, and a partial
 foundation, to test the assembly of materials under stress conditions.
 2. Perform testing on various carbon-derived building material (CDBM) products, individually and
 as part of the demonstration structure anchored to a foundation, to confirm that they meet all
 applicable state and federal building or housing codes (e.g., fire, earthquake, weather).
 3. Validate and demonstrate that CDBM can be used alongside traditional building materials (TBM)
 in structural applications through integration into a building structure anchored to a foundation that
 would be able to qualify under current housing codes.
 4. Design a prototype demonstration carbon-based building consisting of four walls and a roof, and
 deliver detailed design documents for the building, including all construction guidelines required
 to meet state and federal housing code regulations and insurance requirements.
 5. Update the techno-economic analysis (TEA) that was performed in Phase I with the latest
 information on how CDBM will affect material and construction costs as well as market potential,
 a revised gap analysis describing what further development is necessary to achieve market
 acceptance of CDBM, and an expanded lifecycle analysis to show how CDBM compare to TBM
 in terms of emission and byproduct waste reductions, as well as recyclability.

Task 2.0 – Scale-Up of Production Methods
Successful scale-up of production methods was deemed essential to support commercialization of this
technology. To that end, our team invested time and resources in the selection, acquisition, and prove-out
of various pieces of equipment and in developing streamlined processes for production using the equipment.

Subtask 2.1 – Scale up ceramic aggregate production process

The Energy & Environmental Research Center (EERC) at the University of North Dakota was tasked with
developing a pilot process to produce composite carbon aggregate for utilization in the X-MAT CCC
operation to produce CDBM. This pilot plant process is capable of producing tons of aggregate over many
weeks of running to deliver to X-MAT CCC. The EERC utilized existing EERC equipment to the degree
possible to produce the required composite aggregate material. The existing equipment includes a rotary
kiln capable of processing up to 100 lbs. per hour of lignite coal at temperatures in excess of 900°C, a Kason
vibratory screener capable of producing three sized product streams, and multiple crushing and sizing
systems. In addition, X-MAT CCC provided a curing oven required to produce the aggregate material.
Within the first year of Phase II, the overall process was developed, all of the systems were installed and
shaken down, and standard operating procedures (SOPs) for the aggregate production were developed.

Subtask 2.2 – Scale up CDBM production process

with some samples of their output, are depicted in Figure 7 though Figure 53.

Subtask 2.3 – Produce aggregate and CDBM using scaled-up processes

Using the processes discussed in Subtask 2.1, the EERC produced tons of composite aggregate material.
This work entailed running the pilot plant operations in a semicontinuous manner over long periods to
produce the necessary volume of composite aggregate. Nearly three tons (6,000 lbs.) of aggregate material
was produced by EERC during the project and inserted into the X-MAT CCC CDBM production process.

Subtask 3.2 – Develop X-PANEL fastening methodology

An appropriate fastening method for mounting X-PANELs was researched and sourced from the
commercial market. KEIL brand fasteners and Monarch brand clips are integrated in the X-MAT CCC
solution to provide a secure, concealed, and highly durable system for mounting X-PANELs. KEIL’s
system uses precision-engineered undercut anchors installed in the rear face of each X-PANEL via
proprietary drilling tools, allowing the fastener to engage in a way that locks mechanically without imposing
tensile stress on the visible surface. This concealed anchoring method maintains the clean architectural
appearance of the X-PANEL while delivering high pull-out resistance and exceptional performance under
wind load, thermal movement, and seismic activity. Monarch brand clips are employed in conjunction with
KEIL anchors to provide lateral restraint and alignment. The clips are designed to securely engage with
Monarch rail brackets to provide secure connection to the understructure. By combining KEIL’s precise,
stress-free anchoring with the stabilizing and adjustable features of Monarch clips, X-PANEL assemblies
can achieve both structural reliability and long-term serviceability, even in demanding environmental
conditions. This methodology was used to mount X-PANELs to the Technology Demonstrator (Figure 54
through Figure 56).

Conclusion and Future Outlook
This project successfully advanced X-MAT CCC’s CDBM products from laboratory-scale development
(TRL 4) through operational environment demonstration (TRL 6), establishing a strong technical and
economic foundation for commercialization. The project validated that these products, including X-TILEs,
X-PANELs, X-BRIX, and X-BLOX, offer significant performance advantages over conventional materials,
including higher strength-to-weight ratios, superior durability, and resistance to fire, chemicals, and
weathering. The Technology Demonstrator, built using scaled-up manufacturing methods, proved that
CDBM can be integrated seamlessly with traditional building materials (TBM), meeting structural,
aesthetic, and installation requirements. Updated techno-economic, life-cycle, and gap analyses confirmed
the potential for competitive cost structures, favorable environmental impacts, and viable market entry
strategies, aligning the work with the current state of the art in sustainable, high-performance construction
materials.

Measured against DOE success criteria, the project had favorable outcomes: pilot-scale production was
achieved with repeatable quality; fastening and bonding systems were developed and proven; component
and assembly testing validated compliance with key performance requirements; and a coal-based
Technology Demonstrator was designed and built. These results position CDBM as a potentially disruptive
entrant into the advanced building materials market while establishing a novel value-added use for coal-
derived carbon.

Looking ahead, the pathway to commercialization would involve scaling manufacturing to commercial
production lines, securing ISO 9001/14001 certifications, and completing full-scale prototype builds in real-
world environments. Expanded third-party testing, code compliance certifications, and long-term durability
studies will be critical for market acceptance. Parallel market development through early-adopter pilot
projects and strategic partnerships where available could accelerate adoption, particularly in roofing, wall,
and foundation applications where these products’ performance advantages are most compelling. By
continuing to integrate environmental, safety, and sustainability considerations, including recyclability and
end-of-life planning, X-MAT CCC’s CDBM technology is well-positioned to explore the market with a
new standard in resilient, sustainable construction materials.
