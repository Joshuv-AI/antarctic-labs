Several classes of permutation pentanomials
Zhiguo Ding

Abstract
. For each prime p p and each power q = p k q=p^{k} , we present two large classes of permutation polynomials over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} of the form X r ​ B ​ ( X q − 1 ) X^{r}B(X^{q-1}) which have at most five terms, where B ⁡ ( X ) B(X) is a polynomial with coefficients in the prime field of 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} except at most one.



1.
Introduction

A polynomial
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is called a
permutation polynomial
if the map
\alpha\mapsto f(\alpha)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In recent years, many authors have constructed classes of permutation polynomials having at most five terms and having coefficients in
\{1,-1\}
. In particular, many of these are permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with the form
X^{r}B(X^{q-1})
.

We will present several classes of permutation polynomials with at most five terms, whose coefficients are all
\pm 1
except at most one. In particular, we present several classes of permutation pentanomials with coefficients
\pm 1
, and several classes of permutation quadrinomials with coefficients
\pm 1
.

The following is our first main result, which by use of roots of unity presents two general classes of permutation polynomials with at most five terms, whose coefficients are all
\pm 1
except at most one.

.

Assume
q=p^{k}

p

p^{i}

is an integer with

v

m

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X^{Q+R+S}-X^{Q}-X^{R}-X^{S}+b,

\displaystyle B_{2}(X)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X^{Q+R}-X^{Q+S}-X^{R+S}+bX^{S}-1.

Definition 1.11
.

If

V
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces, then a function

is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly equivalent
to a function

if
f=\rho\circ g\circ\eta
for some
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space isomorphisms
\rho\colon V\to U
and
\eta\colon U\to V
.

2.
Background results

In this section we present the background results which are used in our proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. They rely on the following notation.

Notation 2.1
.

If
q
is a prime power, then we write
\mu_{q+1}
for the set of all
(q+1)
-th roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and we define
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
.

We begin with the following special case of
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Write
f(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X^{r}B(X^{q-1})
where
q
is a prime power,
r
is a positive integer, and
B(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
. Then
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if
\gcd(r,q-1)=1
and
X^{r}B(X)^{q-1}
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
.

The next two results are reformulations of
[
3
, Lemmas 2.1 and 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
satisfy
\alpha^{q+1}\neq\beta^{q+1}
, then
(\beta^{q}X+\alpha^{q})/(\alpha X+\beta)

permutes
\mu_{q+1}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\beta\in\mu_{q+1}
, then
(\alpha X+\beta\alpha^{q})/(X+\beta)
maps
\mu_{q+1}
bijectively onto
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

The following result follows from the combination of Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume
v\in\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}
with
v^{2}\neq 1
. Write
\rho(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(vX+1)/(X+v)
and
\eta(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(-vX+1)/(X-v)
. Then
\rho(X)
and
\eta(X)
are degree-
1
rational functions over
\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}
with
\rho^{-1}(X)=\eta(X)
. Moreover, both of the following statements hold:

(1)

if
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
then
\rho(X)
permutes both
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\mu_{q+1}
;

(2)

if
v\in\mu_{q+1}
then
\rho(X)
induces a bijection from
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
onto
\mu_{q+1}
and also a bijection from
\mu_{q+1}
onto
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proof.

If
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
then
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with
v^{q+1}\neq 1
, which by Lemma
2.3
implies
\rho(X)
and
\eta(X)
permute
\mu_{q+1}
. If
v\in\mu_{q+1}
then
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
-v\in\mu_{q+1}
, which by Lemma
2.4
implies
\rho(X)
and
\eta(X)
map
\mu_{q+1}
bijectively onto
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Since
\rho(X)
is the inverse of
\eta(X)
under the composition, it follows that
\rho(X)
and
\eta(X)
maps
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
bijectively onto
\mu_{q+1}
.
∎

The following result is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.5
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Assume
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\mu_{q+1}
with
v^{2}\neq 1
. Write
\rho(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(vX+1)/(X+v)
and
\eta(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(-vX+1)/(X-v)
. Let

be an integer and let
g(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\eta(X)\circ X^{n}\circ\rho(X)
. Then
g(X)
induces a map from
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
into itself and a map from
\mu_{q+1}
into itself, satisfying:

(1)

g(X)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if one of the following holds:

•

v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\gcd(n,q-1)=1
,

•

v\in\mu_{q+1}
and
\gcd(n,q+1)=1
;

(2)

g(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if one of the following holds:

•

v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\gcd(n,q+1)=1
,

•

v\in\mu_{q+1}
and
\gcd(n,q-1)=1
.

Remark 2.7
.

The formal numerator
N(X)
and denominator
D(X)
of
g(X)
in Corollary
2.6
have few terms when

is a sum of few powers of the characteristic

of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. More precisely, for any integer

, if

is a sum of
\ell

then both

and

have at most

have been studied systematically in

of
