A Scalable Monolithic Modified Newton Multigrid Framework for Time-Dependent p p -Navier-Stokes Flow
Nils Margenberg, Carolin Mehlmann

Abstract
Fully implicit tensor-product space-time discretizations of time-dependent ( p , δ ) (p,\delta) -Navier-Stokes models yield, on each time step, large nonlinear monolithic saddle-point systems. In the shear-thinning regime 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 , especially as p ↓ 1 p\downarrow 1 and δ ↓ 0 \delta\downarrow 0 , the decisive difficulty is the constitutive tangent: its ill-conditioning impairs Newton globalization and the preconditioning of the arising linear systems. We therefore develop a scalable monolithic modified Newton framework for tensor-product space-time finite elements in which the exact constitutive tangent in the Jacobian action is replaced by a better-conditioned surrogate. Picard and exact Newton serve as reference linearizations within the same algebraic framework. Scalability is achieved through matrix-free operator evaluation, a monolithic multigrid V-cycle preconditioner, order-preserving reduced Gauss-Radau time quadrature, and an inexact space-time Vanka smoother with single-time-point coefficient freezing in local patch matrices. We prove coercivity of the linearized viscous-Nitsche term in the uniformly elliptic regime ν ∞ > 0 \nu_{\infty}>0 and consistency of the reduced time quadrature. Numerical tests demonstrate robustness with respect to model parameters, nonlinear and linear iteration counts, and scalable parallel performance.

Introduction

Many geophysical, biomedical, and industrial flows are modeled by shear-thinning
or viscoplastic rheologies; see, e.g.,

[
12
,
11
,
30
,
34
,
13
]
.
We consider the time-dependent incompressible
p
-Navier-Stokes system with a
regularized
(p,\delta)
stress law

\bm{S}(\bm{D}\bm{v})=\eta\left(\bm{D}\bm{v}\right)\bm{D}\bm{v},\qquad\eta\left(\bm{D}\bm{v}\right)\coloneq\nu_{\infty}+\nu\bigl(\delta^{2}+\left\lvert\bm{D}\bm{v}\right\rvert^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{p-2}{2}},\quad 1<p<2,

(1)

where
\bm{D}\bm{v}\coloneq\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\nabla\bm{v}+(\nabla\bm{v})^{\top}\bigr)
denotes the
symmetric gradient and
\left\lvert\bm{D}\bm{v}\right\rvert^{2}\coloneq\bm{D}\bm{v}:\bm{D}\bm{v}
the Frobenius
norm.
We assume
\nu_{\infty}\geq 0
,
\nu>0
, and
\delta>0
.
The regularization
\delta
removes the singularity at vanishing shear and makes
the stress law
C^{1}
; if
\nu_{\infty}>0
, the law is moreover uniformly
elliptic.

To obtain a scalable fully implicit space-time discretization, we employ a
tensor-product DG-in-time approach. This yields, on each time step, a large
nonlinear monolithic saddle-point system that couples all temporal and spatial
degrees of freedom on that step. For such formulations, solver performance is
governed not only by the discretization but also by the nonlinear iteration,
the constitutive linearization, and the efficiency of the
multigrid-preconditioned Krylov solve. This motivates the development of a
scalable monolithic modified Newton framework tailored to this setting.

In the shear-thinning regime, in particular in the limiting case
p\downarrow 1
, the exact constitutive tangent contains viscosity-derivative terms that
generate strong anisotropy and poor local conditioning. This can impair both
Newton globalization and Krylov convergence;
cf.
[
5
,
6
]
.
Picard iterations avoid these terms, but typically at the price of slower local
convergence; see, e.g.,
[
37
]
. The central issue is
therefore the construction of a scalable, robust, monolithic space-time solver
for strongly nonlinear shear-thinning
(p,\delta)
-Navier-Stokes flow.

To achieve mesh and parameter robustness in this regime, we employ a modified
Newton solver that replaces the exact constitutive tangent in the Jacobian
action by a better-conditioned surrogate while leaving the nonlinear residual
unchanged. Picard and exact Newton serve as reference linearizations within
the same discrete residual equation. This follows the general strategy of replacing ill-conditioned exact tangents
by better-conditioned linearizations while keeping the nonlinear residual
unchanged;
see
[
30
,
35
]
.
Further improvements of the globalization strategy through the line search are
discussed in
[
33
,
36
]
.

To achieve scalability of the monolithic solver, the nonlinear iteration is
combined with a monolithic space-time multigrid preconditioner, matrix-free
time-step operator evaluation, weak imposition of Dirichlet data by Nitsche
terms, and convection-aligned CIP stabilization. The multigrid hierarchy
exploits the tensor-product structure in time and space, while the smoother
uses local Vanka patch solves. Because fully time-dependent patch assembly is
expensive, we fix the state-dependent coefficients at one representative time
point per time step when assembling the local patch matrices. This is the only
approximation inside the preconditioner; cf.
[
25
]
.

For the fully discretized system in the uniformly elliptic regime
\nu_{\infty}>0
,
we prove coercivity of the linearized viscous-Nitsche term, derive a discrete
energy estimate for the convection-free problem, and justify the reduced
Gauss-Radau time quadrature used in the implementation. The numerically most
challenging regime is, however, the nearly degenerate limit

\nu_{\infty}\downarrow 0
and
p\downarrow 1
. We therefore also include

\nu_{\infty}=0
in the numerical experiments to assess solver robustness.

Contributions and scope

We present a scalable mesh-robust multigrid-preconditioned Newton framework for

(p,\delta)
-Navier-Stokes equations.

5
and
6

Time-dependent
(p,\delta)
-Navier-Stokes system

2.1
Problem statement and weak formulation

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
let
I=(0,T)
, and assume a boundary decomposition

\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}
with
\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset
and outer unit
normal
\bm{n}
.
We write
\Gamma\coloneq\partial\Omega
.
Given are a body force
\bm{f}
, Dirichlet data
\bm{g}_{D}
, and an initial
velocity
\bm{v}_{0}
.

Spaces and pairings

Fix
p\in(1,\infty)
and set
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
.
We use the same symbol
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
for duality pairings and
L^{2}

inner products; when both arguments lie in
L^{2}
, the two coincide.
Define

\bm{V}_{0}\coloneq\{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega):\bm{v}=\bm{0}\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\},

and
Q\coloneq L^{2}(\Omega)
. If
\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset
, the pressure is determined
only up to an additive constant, and one may equivalently work in

L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. For time-dependent Dirichlet data we interpret, for a.e.
t\in I
,

\bm{V}(t)\coloneq\bm{g}_{D}(t)+\bm{V}_{0},

so the weak formulation is posed in the affine space
\bm{V}(t)\times Q
for
a.e.
t\in I
.

Constitutive law

We use the regularized
(p,\delta)
constitutive law (
1
)
and restrict the discussion to the shear-thinning regime
1<p<2
.
For brevity we often write
\bm{A}=\bm{D}\bm{v}
.

Strong form

Find
(\bm{v},\pi)
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{v}+\divv(\bm{v}\otimes\bm{v})-\divv\bm{S}(\bm{D}\bm{v})+\nabla\pi

\displaystyle=\bm{f}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega\times I,

(2)

\displaystyle\divv\bm{v}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega\times I,

\displaystyle\bm{v}

\displaystyle=\bm{g}_{D}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\times I,

\displaystyle\bigl(\bm{S}(\bm{D}\bm{v})-\pi\Id\bigr)\bm{n}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{N}\times I,

\displaystyle\bm{v}(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=\bm{v}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega.

Weak form

Testing (
2
) with
(\bm{z},q)\in\bm{V}_{0}\times Q
, integrating the
viscous and pressure terms by parts, and using
\bm{z}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0
, we obtain:
find
(\bm{v}(t),\pi(t))\in\bm{V}(t)\times Q
such that, for a.e.
t\in I
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\bm{v},\bm{z}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\bm{S}(\bm{D}\bm{v}),\bm{D}\bm{z}\right\rangle-\left\langle\bm{v}\otimes\bm{v},\nabla\bm{z}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\bm{v},\bm{z}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}

(3)

\displaystyle-\left\langle\divv\bm{z},\pi\right\rangle-\left\langle\divv\bm{v},q\right\rangle=\left\langle\bm{f},\bm{z}\right\rangle\qquad\forall(\bm{z},q)\in\bm{V}_{0}\times Q,

together with
\bm{v}(\cdot,0)=\bm{v}_{0}
in
\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Thus the convective boundary term acts only on

\Gamma_{N}
.
For later reference we write (
3
) as

\left\langle\partial_{t}\bm{v},\bm{z}\right\rangle+a(\bm{v})(\bm{z})+c(\bm{v})(\bm{z})+b(\bm{z},\pi)+b(\bm{v},q)=\left\langle\bm{f},\bm{z}\right\rangle,

with

a(\bm{v})(\bm{z})\coloneq\left\langle\bm{S}(\bm{D}\bm{v}),\bm{D}\bm{z}\right\rangle,\qquad b(\bm{z},q)\coloneq-\left\langle\divv\bm{z},q\right\rangle,

and

c(\bm{v})(\bm{z})\coloneq-\left\langle\bm{v}\otimes\bm{v},\nabla\bm{z}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\bm{v},\bm{z}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}.

Regularity and well-posedness

Existence of weak solutions for generalized Newtonian fluids with

(p,\delta)
-structure, including the case
\nu_{\infty}=0
, is covered by

[
22
]
.
For variable exponent models
p(\bm{x},t)
and fully discrete implicit schemes,
see
[
7
]
.
In the present paper we exploit that, for
\nu_{\infty}>0
and
\delta>0
, the
stress law (
1
) is strictly monotone, uniformly elliptic,
and
C^{1}
with respect to the symmetric gradient; see

[
4
,
5
]
.
For
\nu_{\infty}=0
and
\delta>0
, strict monotonicity and

-regularity
remain valid, but uniform ellipticity is lost for large shear.
Accordingly, the analysis below is carried out only for
\nu_{\infty}>0
, whereas
the case
\nu_{\infty}=0
is treated numerically. In particular, the

\bm{H}^{1}
-based weak formulation adopted above matches the uniformly

\bm{H}^{1}
-conforming
discrete velocity space also for
\nu_{\infty}=0
; in that regime the natural
coercivity scale is
\bm{W}^{1,p}
.

3.1
Spaces and meshes

Let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=T
and denote the temporal mesh by

\mathcal{M}_{\tau}\coloneq\{I_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
with

I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n}],\quad\text{and}\quad S_{n}=\Omega\times I_{n}.

(4)

We refer to
I_{n}
as a time step and to
S_{n}
as the corresponding
space-time slab.
Set
\tau_{n}\coloneq t_{n}-t_{n-1}
and
\tau\coloneq\max_{n}\tau_{n}
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
define the broken polynomial space

Y_{\tau}^{k}(I)\coloneq\{w_{\tau}:I\to\mathbb{R}\mid(w_{\tau})_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};\mathbb{R})\ \forall I_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}\}.

(5)

Let
\mathcal{M}_{h}
be a shape-regular mesh of
\Omega
with element diameter
h_{T}
and

h\coloneq\max_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{h}}h_{T}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}}
the set of interior faces, by
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}
the set of
boundary faces, and by
h_{F}
a local face diameter.
For
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}}
we fix an orientation by a unit normal
\bm{n}_{F}
and use the
standard jump notation
\mathopen{[\mkern-2.0mu[}\cdot\mathclose{]\mkern-2.0mu]}
across
F
.

Let
(\bm{V}_{h},Q_{h})
be an inf-sup stable finite element pair on
\Omega
, with

\bm{V}_{h}\subset\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
(for example Taylor-Hood, or
Scott-Vogelius on suitable meshes).
We assume that the discrete inf-sup constant and the trace and Korn constants
remain uniform under refinement, up to the standard dependence on the polynomial
degree.
Set
\bm{X}_{h}\coloneq\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
and define the discretely
divergence-free subspace

\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}\coloneq\{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\mid\left\langle\divv\bm{v}_{h},q_{h}\right\rangle=0\ \forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}\}.

The space-time trial and test space is

\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}\coloneq\big(Y_{\tau}^{k}(I)\otimes\bm{V}_{h}\big)\times\big(Y_{\tau}^{k}(I)\otimes Q_{h}\big).

For
w_{\tau}\in Y_{\tau}^{k}(I)
we denote by
w_{\tau}^{-}(t_{n})
and
w_{\tau}^{+}(t_{n})

the left and right traces at
t_{n}
, and by

\mathopen{[\mkern-2.0mu[}w_{\tau}\mathclose{]\mkern-2.0mu]}_{n}\coloneq w_{\tau}^{+}(t_{n})-w_{\tau}^{-}(t_{n})
the temporal jump.
If an initial value
w_{0}
is prescribed, we set
w_{\tau}^{-}(t_{0})\coloneq w_{0}
and

\mathopen{[\mkern-2.0mu[}w_{\tau}\mathclose{]\mkern-2.0mu]}_{0}=w_{\tau}^{+}(t_{0})-w_{0}
.
In particular, we prescribe
\bm{v}_{\tau h}^{-}(t_{0})\coloneq\bm{v}_{0,h}
.

Gauss-Radau temporal nodes

On each interval
I_{n}
we fix the right-sided
(k{+}1)
-point Gauss-Radau nodes

\{t_{n}^{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{k+1}
and use the associated Lagrange basis

\{\ell_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{k+1}\subset\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n})
satisfying

\ell_{\mu}(t_{n}^{\nu})=\delta_{\mu\nu}
.
Thus the temporal degrees of freedom on
I_{n}
are nodal values at

\{t_{n}^{\mu}\}
.
Because
t_{n}
is one of the Gauss-Radau nodes,
w_{\tau}(t_{n}^{-})
is a nodal
value on
I_{n}
, whereas
w_{\tau}(t_{n-1}^{+})
is obtained by evaluating the
Lagrange expansion at the left endpoint.

3.2
Semilinear spatial form and stabilization

We first define the semilinear spatial form and then specify its stabilization
and boundary terms.

Semilinear forms

For
\bm{u}_{h}=(\bm{v}_{h},\pi_{h})\in\bm{X}_{h}
and

\bm{w}_{h}=(\bm{z}_{h},q_{h})\in\bm{X}_{h}
, set

A(\bm{u}_{h})(\bm{w}_{h})\coloneq a(\bm{v}_{h})(\bm{z}_{h})+c(\bm{v}_{h})(\bm{z}_{h})+b(\bm{z}_{h},\pi_{h})+b(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h}),

(6)

and define

\begin{split}A_{\gamma}(\bm{u}_{h})(\bm{w}_{h})&\coloneq A(\bm{u}_{h})(\bm{w}_{h})-\left\langle(\bm{S}(\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h})-\pi_{h}\Id)\bm{n},\bm{z}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\\
&\qquad+B_{\gamma}(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{w}_{h})+s_{\mathrm{CIP}}(\bm{v}_{h};\bm{v}_{h},\bm{z}_{h}).\end{split}

(7)

We use the divergence form of the convective term:
for
\bm{v}_{h},\bm{z}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
,

c(\bm{v}_{h})(\bm{z}_{h})\coloneq-\left\langle\bm{v}_{h}\otimes\bm{v}_{h},\nabla\bm{z}_{h}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{n})\bm{v}_{h},\bm{z}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma}.

(8)

Since Dirichlet data are imposed weakly,
\bm{V}_{h}
does not vanish on
\Gamma_{D}
.
Accordingly, the boundary integral in (
8
) is taken over the full
boundary, and the inflow correction on
\Gamma_{D}
is handled by
B^{c}
in
(
10
).

CIP stabilization

On the interior skeleton
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}}
we add a convection-aligned CIP term.
For
\hat{\bm{v}}\in\bm{V}_{h}
and
\bm{v}_{h},\bm{z}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
we set

s_{\mathrm{CIP}}(\hat{\bm{v}};\bm{v}_{h},\bm{z}_{h})\coloneq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}}}\gamma_{\mathrm{CIP}}\,h_{F}^{2}\,\left\langle\left\lvert\hat{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}\right\rvert\mathopen{[\mkern-2.0mu[}\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}\mathclose{]\mkern-2.0mu]},\mathopen{[\mkern-2.0mu[}\nabla\bm{z}_{h}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}\mathclose{]\mkern-2.0mu]}\right\rangle_{F}.

(9)

This term acts only on convective transport and does not modify the
velocity-pressure coupling.
Since
(\bm{V}_{h},Q_{h})
is inf-sup stable, no pressure stabilization is added.

Boundary terms: Nitsche with fixed viscous coefficients

Dirichlet data are imposed weakly by Nitsche terms;
see
[
27
,
16
]
.
For non-Newtonian models, related weak-imposition ideas are discussed in

[
3
]
.

For
y\in\mathbb{R}
write

\left(y\right)^{\oplus}\coloneq\tfrac{1}{2}(\left\lvert y\right\rvert+y)
and

\left(y\right)^{\ominus}\coloneq\tfrac{1}{2}(\left\lvert y\right\rvert-y)
.
Let
\gamma_{1}>0
and
\gamma_{2}>0
be penalty parameters and let
h_{\Gamma_{D}}
denote a local
boundary mesh size on
\Gamma_{D}
.
Further, let
\eta\left(\cdot\right)
be the effective viscosity from
(
1
).

Let
\bm{g}_{D}
denote the prescribed Dirichlet trace on
\Gamma_{D}
and fix a
lifting
\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}
to
\Omega
.
Whenever
B_{\gamma}(\bm{g}_{D},\cdot)
appears below, it is understood
through this fixed lifting.
In the viscous Nitsche terms we evaluate both the effective viscosity and the
stress derivative at
\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}
.
This keeps the boundary contribution bilinear, while the volume term

\bm{v}\mapsto\bm{S}(\bm{D}\bm{v})
remains fully nonlinear.
Accordingly, define

B_{\gamma}(\cdot,\cdot):\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times\bm{X}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}

by

\displaystyle B_{\gamma}(\bm{v},\bm{w}_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneq B^{c}(\bm{v},\bm{w}_{h})+B^{s}(\bm{v},\bm{w}_{h})+B_{\gamma}^{r}(\bm{v},\bm{w}_{h}),

(10a)

\displaystyle B^{c}(\bm{v},\bm{w}_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneq-\left\langle\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)^{\ominus}\,\bm{v},\bm{z}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}},

(10b)

\displaystyle B^{s}(\bm{v},\bm{w}_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneq-\left\langle\bm{v},\Big(D\bm{S}\left(\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}\right)\left(\bm{D}\bm{z}_{h}\right)+q_{h}\Id\Big)\bm{n}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}},

(10c)

\displaystyle B_{\gamma}^{r}(\bm{v},\bm{w}_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneq\gamma_{1}\,h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-1}\left\langle\eta\left(\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}\right)\,\bm{v},\bm{z}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}+\gamma_{2}\,h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-1}\left\langle\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},\bm{z}_{h}\cdot\bm{n}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}.

(10d)

Consistency and adjoint consistency

If
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfies

\bm{v}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\bm{g}_{D}
in the trace sense, then

B_{\gamma}(\bm{v},\bm{w}_{h})=B_{\gamma}(\bm{g}_{D},\bm{w}_{h})
for
all
\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}
.
Indeed, all terms in (
10
) depend on
\bm{v}
only through its
trace on
\Gamma_{D}
.
Fixing the viscous coefficients at
\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}
generally
destroys adjoint consistency.
Accordingly, we do not pursue duality-based
L^{2}
-error estimates here.
For stability,
\gamma_{1}
(and
\gamma_{2}
for the normal component) is chosen
sufficiently large so that the standard Nitsche trace argument applies;
cf.
[
16
]
.

3.3
Fully discrete space-time variational problem and energy stability

Problem 3.1

(Discrete space-time variational problem)

Assume

\bm{f}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(I;\bm{W}^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)),\qquad\bm{g}_{D}\in L^{2}(I;\bm{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D})),

Let
\bm{v}_{0,h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
be an approximation of the initial velocity
\bm{v}_{0}
and

\bm{v}_{\tau h}^{-}(t_{0})\coloneq\bm{v}_{0,h}

Find
\bm{u}_{\tau h}=(\bm{v}_{\tau h},\pi_{\tau h})\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}

such that for all
\bm{w}_{\tau h}=(\bm{z}_{\tau h},q_{\tau h})\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\bm{v}_{\tau h},\bm{z}_{\tau h}\right\rangle+A_{\gamma}(\bm{u}_{\tau h}(t))(\bm{w}_{\tau h}(t))\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\langle\mathopen{[\mkern-2.0mu[}\bm{v}_{\tau h}\mathclose{]\mkern-2.0mu]}_{n-1},\bm{z}_{\tau h}^{+}(t_{n-1})\right\rangle

(11)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left\langle\bm{f},\bm{z}_{\tau h}\right\rangle\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}B_{\gamma}(\bm{g}_{D}(t),\bm{w}_{\tau h}(t))\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}t.

We adopt the residual convention
right-hand side minus left-hand side
.
For
\bm{U}=(\bm{v},\pi)\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
and

\bm{W}=(\bm{z},q)\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
, define the discrete residual
functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}(\bm{U};\bm{W})\coloneq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left\langle\bm{f},\bm{z}\right\rangle\,\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}B_{\gamma}(\bm{g}_{D}(t),\bm{W}(t))\,\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}t

(12)

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left\langle\partial_{t}\bm{v},\bm{z}\right\rangle\,\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}t-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}A_{\gamma}(\bm{U}(t))(\bm{W}(t))\,\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\langle\mathopen{[\mkern-2.0mu[}\bm{v}\mathclose{]\mkern-2.0mu]}_{n-1},\bm{z}^{+}(t_{n-1})\right\rangle.

Then
\bm{u}_{\tau h}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
solves (
11
) if and
only if

\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}(\bm{u}_{\tau h};\bm{w}_{\tau h})=0\qquad\forall\,\bm{w}_{\tau h}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}.

Remark 3.2

(Global space-time form and time stepping)
.

Problem
3.1
is written in monolithic global space-time form.
Because
\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
is discontinuous in time, the residual on a
time step
I_{n}
depends only on
\bm{u}_{\tau h}|_{I_{n}}
and on the left
trace
\bm{v}_{\tau h}^{-}(t_{n-1})
through the DG jump term. After fixing a basis
in
Y_{\tau}^{k}
and ordering unknowns in temporal order, the global nonlinear
system is therefore causal and its Jacobian is block lower bidiagonal in time.
In the implementation we therefore realize the method by time marching: on each
time step
I_{n}
we solve one monolithic space-time problem for all temporal and
spatial degrees of freedom on
I_{n}
, with the left trace from
t_{n-1}
treated
as data. The multigrid preconditioner acts on this time-marching system, not on
the global space-time system at once.

Remark 3.3

(Gauss-Radau quadrature)
.

In the implementation, time integrals involving state-dependent coefficients,
notably the
p
-stress and convection terms, are evaluated on each
I_{n}
by
the right-sided
(k{+}1)
-point Gauss-Radau rule.
The rule preserves the endpoint
t_{n}
needed by the DG jump term and is exact
for polynomial integrands up to degree
2k
.
For the present nonlinear constitutive law this is a variational crime; its
effect is analyzed in Appendix
A
.

4
Nonlinear solution strategies in each time step

Each time-step problem produced by the fully implicit space-time discretization
is a large nonlinear saddle-point system. All nonlinear variants considered
below solve one fixed discrete residual equation and differ only in the
linearization used in the correction equation. The central method is modified
Newton; Picard and exact Newton are included only as reference linearizations
within the same discretization and the same algebraic framework.

The constitutive term is the decisive difficulty.
For
1<p<2
, the exact constitutive derivative is anisotropic and its
smallest eigenvalue deteriorates as
p\downarrow 1
.
This is precisely the mechanism that can make exact Newton hard to globalize and
hard to precondition in strongly shear-thinning regimes.
We therefore use a modified Newton variant that changes only the constitutive
tangent while leaving the nonlinear residual unchanged.

4.1
Residual functional and exact Gâteaux derivative

Let
\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}(\bm{U};\bm{W})
denote the discrete residual
(
12
).
Then
\bm{u}_{\tau h}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
solves (
11
) if and
only if

\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}(\bm{u}_{\tau h};\bm{W})=0\qquad\forall\,\bm{W}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}.

(13)

The Newton-type correction equation takes the form

\mathcal{J}_{m}(\Delta\bm{U}^{m},\bm{W})=\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}(\bm{U}^{m};\bm{W})
with an
additive update.
Let
\mathcal{A}_{\tau h}
denote the left-hand side form of (
11
), so
that
\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}
is the right-hand side minus this left-hand side.
For
\bm{U},\Delta\bm{U}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
, and for states

\bm{U}
at which the directional derivative exists, define

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\tau h}(\bm{U})(\Delta\bm{U},\bm{W})\coloneq\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\mathcal{A}_{\tau h}(\bm{U}+\varepsilon\Delta\bm{U};\bm{W})\right|_{\varepsilon=0}\qquad\forall\,\bm{W}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}.

(14)

Equivalently,

D_{\bm{U}}\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}(\bm{U};\bm{W})(\Delta\bm{U})=-\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\tau h}(\bm{U})(\Delta\bm{U},\bm{W}).

For the smooth contributions to the discrete residual associated with
Problem
3.1
, the Newton derivative is evaluated exactly.
It consists of the broken DG-in-time term, the differentiated viscous block,
the differentiated convective block, and the mixed pressure-velocity coupling,
including the Nitsche boundary contributions on
\Gamma_{D}
.
Here
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\nu,\tau h}(\bm{v})
denotes the derivative of the viscous
volume term together with the state-dependent viscous boundary-flux term, while

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{c,\tau h}(\bm{v})
denotes the derivative of the convective
volume and boundary terms that are differentiated in the Newton step.
The factors
\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)^{\ominus}
on
\Gamma_{D}
and

\left\lvert\hat{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}\right\rvert
in the CIP stabilization are only
Lipschitz with respect to the advecting field and are therefore lagged in
Variant exN and Variant modN; see
Section

4.3
.
With this convention, the residual equation itself is unchanged, and the only
genuinely nonlinear elliptic contribution is the constitutive linearization

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\nu,\tau h}(\bm{v})
.

Given an iterate
\bm{U}^{m}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
, we compute a correction

\Delta\bm{U}^{m}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k}
from

\mathcal{J}_{m}(\Delta\bm{U}^{m},\bm{W})=\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}(\bm{U}^{m};\bm{W})\qquad\forall\,\bm{W}\in\bm{X}_{\tau h}^{k},

(15)

and update

\bm{U}^{m+1}=\bm{U}^{m}+\lambda_{m}\Delta\bm{U}^{m},\qquad\lambda_{m}\in(0,1].

The methods differ only in the choice of the form
\mathcal{J}_{m}
. At a fixed
iterate
\bm{v}^{m}
set

\bm{A}^{m}\coloneq\bm{D}\bm{v}^{m},\qquad\Delta^{m,2}\coloneq\delta^{2}+\left\lvert\bm{A}^{m}\right\rvert^{2},\qquad\mu^{m}\coloneq\nu(\Delta^{m,2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}},\qquad\eta^{m}\coloneq\nu_{\infty}+\mu^{m}.

At a glance, Variant Pic lags all state-dependent coefficients and omits the
constitutive derivative, Variant exN differentiates all smooth residual terms
and lags only the nonsmooth inflow and CIP weights, and Variant modN agrees
with Variant exN except that the exact constitutive tangent is replaced by a
stress-clipped symmetric rank-one surrogate.

4.2
Picard iteration (Variant Pic)

Picard freezes all state-dependent coefficients at the current iterate.
For the constitutive term this amounts to the tangent

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Pic}}^{m}(\bm{B})\coloneq\eta^{m}\bm{B}.

(16)

That is, the effective viscosity is evaluated at the current iterate and the
derivative contribution of the stress law is omitted.
In addition, the advecting velocity, the inflow factor

\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)^{\ominus}
on
\Gamma_{D}
, and the state-dependent CIP weight are
lagged at
\bm{U}^{m}
.
Hence Picard is a fixed-point iteration of the form (
15
)
with a form
\mathcal{J}_{m}
.

4.3
Exact Newton (Variant exN)

Recall (
1
).
For
\delta>0
,
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}
, the mapping
\bm{A}\mapsto\bm{S}(\bm{A})
is continuously
differentiable, with Fréchet derivative

D\bm{S}\left(\bm{A}\right)\left(\bm{B}\right)=\eta\left(\bm{A}\right)\,\bm{B}+\nu(p-2)\bigl(\delta^{2}+\left\lvert\bm{A}\right\rvert^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{p-4}{2}}(\bm{A}:\bm{B})\,\bm{A}.

(17)

The exact constitutive tangent at
\bm{A}^{m}
is the linear map
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{exN}}^{m}(\bm{B})\colon\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}
defined by

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{exN}}^{m}(\bm{B})&=\eta^{m}\bm{B}+\nu(p-2)(\Delta^{m,2})^{\frac{p-4}{2}}(\bm{A}^{m}:\bm{B})\,\bm{A}^{m}\\
&=\underbrace{\eta^{m}\bm{B}}_{\text{isotropic}}+\underbrace{(p-2)\frac{\mu^{m}}{\Delta^{m,2}}(\bm{A}^{m}:\bm{B})\,\bm{A}^{m}}_{\text{rank-one
correction in $\bm{A}^{m}$-direction}}.\end{split}

(18)

By Appendix
B
, the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{exN}}^{m}
are

\lambda_{\perp}=\eta^{m}
on the subspace defined by
\{\bm{B}:\bm{A}^{m}:\bm{B}=0\}
and

\lambda_{\parallel}=\eta^{m}+(p-2)\frac{\mu^{m}}{\Delta^{m,2}}\left\lvert\bm{A}^{m}\right\rvert^{2}=\nu_{\infty}+\mu^{m}\frac{\delta^{2}+(p-1)\left\lvert\bm{A}^{m}\right\rvert^{2}}{\Delta^{m,2}}

(19)

in the
\bm{A}^{m}
-direction.
From Appendix
B
, in particular

Lemma

B.1
, we obtain, for
1<p<2
,

\bigl(\nu_{\infty}+(p-1)\mu^{m}\bigr)\left\lvert\bm{B}\right\rvert^{2}\leq(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{exN}}^{m}(\bm{B}):\bm{B})\leq(\nu_{\infty}+\mu^{m})\left\lvert\bm{B}\right\rvert^{2}.

(20)

For
\left\lvert\bm{A}^{m}\right\rvert\gg\delta
, the anisotropy ratio behaves like

\kappa(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{exN}}^{m})\approx\frac{\nu_{\infty}+\mu^{m}}{\nu_{\infty}+(p-1)\mu^{m}},

(21)

which deteriorates as
p\downarrow 1
and
\mu^{m}\gg\nu_{\infty}
due to the
factor
p-1
in the smallest-eigenvalue direction. This is the source of
ill-conditioning in the shear-thinning regime.

For all smooth residual contributions, Variant exN uses the Gâteaux derivative,

\mathcal{J}_{m}(\Delta\bm{U},\bm{W})=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\tau h}(\bm{U}^{m})(\Delta\bm{U},\bm{W}),

with constitutive part induced by (
18
).
Thus Variant exN is exact for the differentiable part of the residual.
The factors
\left(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\right)^{\ominus}
on
\Gamma_{D}
and

\left\lvert\hat{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{F}\right\rvert
in (
9
) are only Lipschitz in
the advecting field. Accordingly, in the implementation these weights are
lagged at the current iterate rather than differentiated.

4.4
Modified Newton (Variant modN)

To obtain a more robust constitutive surrogate, we replace

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{exN}}^{m}
by a stress-clipped symmetric rank-one modification.
Define

\bm{\sigma}^{m}\coloneq\mu^{m}\bm{A}^{m},\qquad\hat{\bm{\sigma}}^{m}\coloneq s^{m}\bm{\sigma}^{m},

with

s^{m}\coloneq\begin{cases}\min\{1,\sigma_{\max}/\left\lvert\bm{\sigma}^{m}\right\rvert\},&\bm{\sigma}^{m}\neq\bm{0},\\
0,&\bm{\sigma}^{m}=\bm{0},\end{cases}

so that
\left\lvert\hat{\bm{\sigma}}^{m}\right\rvert\leq\sigma_{\max}
.
Further set

\theta^{m}\coloneq\frac{(p-2)}{\mu^{m}\Delta^{m,2}}.

Since
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}^{m}=s^{m}\bm{\sigma}^{m}
, the modified constitutive tangent is

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{modN}}^{m}(\bm{B})=\eta^{m}\bm{B}+s^{m}\theta^{m}(\bm{\sigma}^{m}:\bm{B})\,\bm{\sigma}^{m}.

(22)

Equivalently, using
\bm{\sigma}^{m}=\mu^{m}\bm{A}^{m}
,

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{modN}}^{m}(\bm{B})=\eta^{m}\bm{B}+s^{m}(p-2)\frac{\mu^{m}}{\Delta^{m,2}}(\bm{A}^{m}:\bm{B})\,\bm{A}^{m}.

Hence Lemma
B.1
applies and,
if
\bm{\sigma}^{m}\neq 0
, the eigenvalues are

\lambda_{\perp}=\eta^{m},\qquad\lambda_{\parallel}=\eta^{m}+s^{m}\theta^{m}|\bm{\sigma}^{m}|^{2},

with
\lambda_{\perp}
on the subspace

\{\bm{B}:\bm{\sigma}^{m}:\bm{B}=0\}
and

\lambda_{\parallel}
in the direction
\bm{\sigma}^{m}
.
For
\left\lvert\bm{A}^{m}\right\rvert\gg\delta
, the anisotropy ratio behaves like

\kappa(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{modN}}^{m})\approx\frac{\nu_{\infty}+\mu^{m}}{\nu_{\infty}+\bigl(1-s^{m}(2-p)\bigr)\mu^{m}},

(23)

so that
s^{m}=1
recovers (
21
), whereas
s^{m}=0
yields
the isotropic Picard value
\kappa(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{modN}}^{m})=1
. Hence, clipping
replaces the factor
p-1
by
1-s^{m}(2-p)
in the
smallest-eigenvalue direction and thereby weakens the anisotropy.

Modified Newton keeps the residual (
13
) unchanged and replaces
only the exact constitutive tangent.
More precisely,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{exN}}^{m}
is replaced by
(
22
) in the viscous volume term and in the matching
state-dependent boundary flux term.
All remaining contributions, in particular the time derivative, the mixed
pressure-velocity coupling, the convective linearization, and the
fixed-coefficient Nitsche terms, are treated as in Variant exN.
The method is therefore a quasi-Newton iteration.

4.5
Coercivity of the viscous-Nitsche term

The next corollary records the coercivity property used later in the algebraic
discussion: under pointwise lower and upper bounds on the constitutive tangent,
the linearized viscous-Nitsche term remains coercive.
Let
\eta_{D}\coloneq\eta\left(\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}\right)
on
\Gamma_{D}
(hence

\eta_{D}\geq\nu_{\infty}
), and define for
\bm{v}_{h},\bm{z}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}

\displaystyle a_{\star}^{m}(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{z}_{h})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\star}^{m}(\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}),\bm{D}\bm{z}_{h}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\star}^{m}(\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h})\bm{n},\bm{z}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}-\left\langle\bm{v}_{h},D\bm{S}\left(\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}\right)\left(\bm{D}\bm{z}_{h}\right)\bm{n}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}

(24)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{1}\,\left\langle h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-1}\eta_{D}\,\bm{v}_{h},\bm{z}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}+\gamma_{2}\,\left\langle h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-1}(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{n}),(\bm{z}_{h}\cdot\bm{n})\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}.

Here
\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}
is the lifting of
\bm{g}_{D}
to
\Omega
.

Corollary 4.1

(Coercivity of the linearized viscous-Nitsche term)
.

Fix a Newton state
\bm{U}^{m}
and a tangent

\mathcal{T}_{\star}^{m}:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}
.
Assume there exists
\overline{\eta}\geq\nu_{\infty}>0
such that, for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and all

\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}
,

\nu_{\infty}|\bm{B}|^{2}\leq\mathcal{T}_{\star}^{m}(\bm{B}):\bm{B},\qquad|\mathcal{T}_{\star}^{m}(\bm{B})|+\bigl|D\bm{S}\left(\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}\right)\left(\bm{B}\right)\bigr|\leq\overline{\eta}\,|\bm{B}|.

(25)

For the tangents used in this paper, these bounds hold in the uniformly elliptic
regime
\nu_{\infty}>0
.
There exists
C_{\mathrm{tr}}>0
, depending only on shape regularity and on

\overline{\eta}/\nu_{\infty}
, such that for all
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
,

a_{\star}^{m}(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})\geq\frac{\nu_{\infty}}{2}\,\|\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\bigl(\gamma_{1}\,\nu_{\infty}-C_{\mathrm{tr}}\bigr)\,\|h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}^{2}+\gamma_{2}\,\|h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}^{2}.

(26)

In particular, if
\gamma_{1}\geq 2C_{\mathrm{tr}}/\nu_{\infty}
then
a_{\star}^{m}
is
coercive on
\bm{V}_{h}
.
Moreover, inhomogeneous Dirichlet data enter only through the fixed
coefficients
\eta_{D}
and
D\bm{S}\left(\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}\right)\left(\cdot\right)
; once the
bounds in (
25
) hold, the coercivity argument is unchanged.

Proof.

Set
\bm{z}_{h}=\bm{v}_{h}
in (
24
).
By (
25
),

\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\star}^{m}(\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}),\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle\geq\nu_{\infty}\|\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
.
Further, by the upper bound in (
25
), Cauchy-Schwarz, and
a discrete trace inequality,

\begin{split}&\bigl|\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\star}^{m}(\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h})\bm{n},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\bigr|+\bigl|\left\langle\bm{v}_{h},D\bm{S}\left(\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}\right)\left(\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}\right)\bm{n}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\bigr|\\
&\qquad\leq C_{\mathrm{tr}}^{1/2}\|\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-1/2}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}.\end{split}

Young’s inequality yields

\begin{split}\bigl|\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\star}^{m}(\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h})\bm{n},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\bigr|&+\bigl|\left\langle\bm{v}_{h},D\bm{S}\left(\bm{D}\hat{\bm{g}}_{D}\right)\left(\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}\right)\bm{n}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\bigr|\\
&\quad\leq\frac{\nu_{\infty}}{2}\|\bm{D}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{\mathrm{tr}}\|h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-1/2}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}^{2}.\end{split}

Finally,
\eta_{D}\geq\nu_{\infty}
implies

\gamma_{1}\,\left\langle h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-1}\eta_{D}\,\bm{v}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\geq\gamma_{1}\nu_{\infty}\|h_{\Gamma_{D}}^{-1/2}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}^{2}
,
and the
\gamma_{2}
-term is nonnegative.
Collecting the estimates gives (
26
).
∎

5
Algebraic structure and assembly

We now make the algebraic structure of one time step explicit.
This is the basis for matrix-free Jacobian actions and for the monolithic
space-time multigrid preconditioner used within the time-marching realization.

5.1
Algebraic system

Fix a time step
I_{n}
(
4
).
Let
\{\bm{\varphi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M_{v}}
and
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M_{p}}
be
bases of
\bm{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
.
On
I_{n}
we use the Lagrange basis

\{\ell_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{k+1}\subset\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n})
associated with the right-sided
Gauss-Radau nodes
\{t_{n}^{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{k+1}
.
Expanding

\bm{v}_{\tau h}|_{S_{n}}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{k+1}\sum_{i=1}^{M_{v}}V_{\mu i}^{n}\,\ell_{\mu}(t)\,\bm{\varphi}_{i}(x),\qquad\pi_{\tau h}|_{S_{n}}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{p}}P_{\mu j}^{n}\,\ell_{\mu}(t)\,\psi_{j}(x),

yields a coupled nonlinear system on
S_{n}=\Omega\times I_{n}
,

\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n})=\bm{0},\qquad\bm{U}^{n}\coloneq\bigl((\bm{V}_{\mu}^{n},\bm{P}_{\mu}^{n})\bigr)_{\mu=1}^{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{(k+1)(M_{v}+M_{p})},

(27)

where
\bm{V}_{\mu}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{M_{v}}
and
\bm{P}_{\mu}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{M_{p}}

collect the spatial coefficients at the temporal node
t_{n}^{\mu}
.
Let
\mathcal{R}_{n}
denote the time-step residual functional obtained from
(
12
) by restricting to
S_{n}
and treating the left trace

\bm{v}_{\tau h}^{-}(t_{n-1})
as given data.
The associated residual vector

\bm{R}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{(k+1)(M_{v}+M_{p})}\to\mathbb{R}^{(k+1)(M_{v}+M_{p})}
is defined by

(\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n}))_{(\mu,i)}\coloneq\mathcal{R}_{n}\left(\bm{U}^{n};\,\ell_{\mu}\,\bm{\varphi}_{i},0\right),\qquad(\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n}))_{(\mu,j)}\coloneq\mathcal{R}_{n}\left(\bm{U}^{n};\,0,\ell_{\mu}\,\psi_{j}\right).

Then, finding an approximation to
3.1
is equivalent to solving
(
27
) for all
S_{n}
,
n=1,\dots,N
. The global algebraic system
corresponding to
3.1
is obtained by stacking the time-step
vectors
\bm{U}^{n}
and adding the DG jump couplings between consecutive time
steps. In the time-marching realization, the left trace

\bm{v}_{\tau h}^{-}(t_{n-1})
enters
I_{n}
only through the DG jump term and is
treated as known when solving on
I_{n}
. Accordingly, the implementation advances
from
t_{n-1}
to
t_{n}
by solving the monolithic local
system (
27
) on each time step.

5.2
Tensor-product structure and matrix-free operator evaluation

On each
S_{n}
we evaluate time integrals involving state-dependent coefficients
by the right-sided
(k{+}1)
-point Gauss-Radau rule underlying the temporal
Lagrange basis.
This preserves the endpoint
t_{n}
needed by the DG jump term and is exact on

\mathbb{P}_{2k}(I_{n})
.
For nonlinear constitutive laws this introduces a quadrature error; see
Appendix
A
.

Let
\hat{I}=(0,1)
and let
\{\hat{\xi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k+1}
be the Gauss-Radau
Lagrange basis on
\hat{I}
.
The temporal matrices on
I_{n}
are obtained from the reference interval by
affine pullback; for brevity we denote them again by

\bm{M}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1\times k+1}
,

\bm{K}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1\times k+1}
, and

\bm{m}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
.
On the reference interval they are given by

{(\bm{M}_{t})}_{i,\,j}\coloneq\int_{0}^{1}\hat{\xi}_{j}(\hat{t})\hat{\xi}_{i}(\hat{t})\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}\hat{t},\quad{(\bm{K}_{t})}_{i,\,j}\coloneq\int_{0}^{1}\hat{\xi}_{j}^{\prime}(\hat{t})\hat{\xi}_{i}(\hat{t})\mkern 3.0mu\mathrm{d}\hat{t}+\hat{\xi}_{j}(0)\hat{\xi}_{i}(0),\quad{(\bm{m}_{t})}_{i}\coloneq\hat{\xi}_{i}(0),

for
i,j=1,\dots,k+1
.
The endpoint term
\hat{\xi}_{j}(0)\hat{\xi}_{i}(0)
encodes the left-sided DG jump.
Because
\hat{\xi}_{i}\hat{\xi}_{j}\in\mathbb{P}_{2k}(\hat{I})
and the
(k{+}1)
-point
Gauss-Radau rule is exact on
\mathbb{P}_{2k}(\hat{I})
, the mass matrix

\bm{M}_{t}
coincides with the diagonal matrix of Gauss-Radau weights in this
nodal basis. Note that the left endpoint is not a quadrature node of the
right-sided Gauss-Radau rule.

Let
\bm{M}_{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{M_{v}\times M_{v}}
be the spatial velocity mass matrix,

(\bm{M}_{x})_{i,j}\coloneq(\bm{\varphi}_{j},\bm{\varphi}_{i})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Write the time-step unknown in velocity-pressure block form

\bm{U}^{n}=(\bm{V}^{n},\bm{P}^{n})
with

\bm{V}^{n}=(\bm{V}_{1}^{n},\ldots,\bm{V}_{k+1}^{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{(k+1)M_{v}},\qquad\bm{P}^{n}=(\bm{P}_{1}^{n},\ldots,\bm{P}_{k+1}^{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{(k+1)M_{p}}.

For each temporal node
\mu
, let

\bm{F}_{\mu}(\bm{V}_{\mu}^{n},\bm{P}_{\mu}^{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{M_{v}+M_{p}}
denote the assembled

spatial residual vector
at
t=t_{n}^{\mu}
, with the same sign convention as

\bm{R}_{n}
, i.e. right-hand side minus spatial left-hand side, excluding the DG
time derivative and the DG jump.
Let
\bm{J}_{\mu}
denote the corresponding spatial linearization block used in the
correction equation (
32
), i.e. the matrix representation of
the selected left-hand-side linearization on the node
t_{n}^{\mu}
.
Equivalently,
\bm{J}_{\mu}=-D\bm{F}_{\mu}
for the chosen nonlinear variant.
For Variant exN,
\bm{J}_{\mu}
differentiates all smooth left-hand-side terms,
with the nonsmooth inflow and CIP weights lagged as described in

Section

4.3
. For Variant Pic and Variant modN,
\bm{J}_{\mu}
is the
analogous spatial linearization obtained from the same residual by the
corresponding constitutive replacement from
Section

4
.

Because each
\bm{F}_{\mu}
is assembled at the single node
t_{n}^{\mu}
, it depends
only on
(\bm{V}_{\mu}^{n},\bm{P}_{\mu}^{n})
and not on nodal values at any other
Gauss-Radau node.
Consequently, the spatial Jacobian blocks decouple across temporal nodes and
form the block diagonal matrix

\diag(\bm{J}_{1},\ldots,\bm{J}_{k+1})
.
Define the stacked spatial residual

\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{n})\coloneq(\bm{F}_{1},\ldots,\bm{F}_{k+1})
and let

\bm{v}_{n-1}^{-}\in\mathbb{R}^{M_{v}}
denote the coefficient vector of the left
trace
\bm{v}_{\tau h}^{-}(t_{n-1})
.

Then the time-step residual and Jacobian can be written as

\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n})=-\begin{bmatrix}(\bm{K}_{t}\otimes\bm{M}_{x})\,\bm{V}^{n}-(\bm{m}_{t}\otimes\bm{M}_{x})\,\bm{v}_{n-1}^{-}\\[1.99997pt]
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}+(\bm{M}_{t}\otimes\bm{I}_{M_{v}+M_{p}})\,\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{n}),

(28)

\bm{J}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n})=\begin{bmatrix}(\bm{K}_{t}\otimes\bm{M}_{x})&\bm{0}\\[1.99997pt]
\bm{0}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}+(\bm{M}_{t}\otimes\bm{I}_{M_{v}+M_{p}})\,\diag(\bm{J}_{1},\ldots,\bm{J}_{k+1}).

(29)

Hence a matrix-free Jacobian action reduces to independent spatial kernel
applications
\bm{J}_{\mu}(\cdot)
at the Gauss-Radau nodes, followed by temporal
scaling with
\bm{M}_{t}
and the dense temporal coupling from
\bm{K}_{t}
.

Each spatial Jacobian block

\bm{J}_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{(M_{v}+M_{p})\times(M_{v}+M_{p})}
has the mixed form

\bm{J}_{\mu}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}_{\mu}&\bm{B}_{\mu}^{\top}\\
\bm{B}_{\mu}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix},

(30)

where
\bm{B}_{\mu}
denotes the discrete incompressibility block, including the
Nitsche boundary contribution on
\Gamma_{D}
, and
\bm{B}_{\mu}^{\top}
the
corresponding pressure coupling block.
Moreover,

\bm{A}_{\mu}=\bm{A}_{\nu,\mu}(\bm{U}^{n})+\bm{A}_{c,\mu}(\bm{U}^{n})+\bm{A}_{\mathrm{CIP},\mu}(\bm{U}^{n}).

(31)

Here
\bm{A}_{\nu,\mu}
collects the linearized viscous volume term and the
state-dependent boundary flux term with the tangent variant selected in

Section

4
; the fixed-coefficient Nitsche terms are unchanged from the
discretization.
Further,
\bm{A}_{c,\mu}
denotes the linearization of convection, including the
inflow boundary term, and
\bm{A}_{\mathrm{CIP},\mu}
the linearization of the
CIP stabilization.
If the advecting field in the CIP weight is lagged, then

\bm{A}_{\mathrm{CIP},\mu}
is symmetric positive semidefinite, because
(
9
) is an
L^{2}(F)
inner product of identical jumps with
nonnegative weight.
Thus lagged CIP cannot reduce the coercivity of the velocity terms.

A representative-state surrogate is used only inside the local Vanka patch
solves of the multigrid preconditioner.
The nonlinear residual and the global matrix-free Jacobian action always use the
physical operator associated with the selected nonlinear variant.
The surrogate affects only local patch assembly inside the smoother;
see
Section

6.3
.

6
Scalable algebraic nonlinear and linear solvers

The nonlinear iterations from
Section

4
are realized by time marching in
a matrix-free Newton-Krylov framework, with one monolithic space-time solve per
time step and one space-time multigrid V-cycle as preconditioner.
We restrict the discussion to the aspects specific to the present

(p,\delta)
-Navier-Stokes setting.
On each time step
I_{n}
, the fully implicit discretization yields a nonlinear
system

\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n})=\bm{0},

where
\bm{U}^{n}
collects all space-time degrees of freedom on
S_{n}
and

\bm{R}_{n}
is the algebraic realization of
\mathcal{R}_{\tau h}(\bm{U};\bm{W})
in
the test basis. We write
\bm{U}^{n,m}
for the
m
-th nonlinear iterate on the
slab
S_{n}
, and
\bm{J}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})\coloneq-D\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})
for the
corresponding algebraic linearization. Thus
\bm{J}_{n}
is the matrix
representation, in the chosen test basis, of the form

\mathcal{J}_{m}
from (
15
). Given an iterate
\bm{U}^{n,m}
, we compute a correction

\Delta\bm{U}^{n,m}
from

\bm{J}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})\Delta\bm{U}^{n,m}=\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m}),\qquad\bm{U}^{n,m+1}=\bm{U}^{n,m}+\lambda_{m}\Delta\bm{U}^{n,m}.

(32)

Here
\bm{J}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})
denotes the time-step linearization induced by the
selected nonlinear variant: the operator for Picard, the Jacobian of the
differentiable residual terms for Variant exN, and the quasi-Newton operator for
modified Newton (Variant modN).

6.1
Krylov solver and preconditioning

The linear systems (
32
) are solved by right-preconditioned
FGMRES with one STMG V-cycle as preconditioner.
The matrix-free operator evaluation, transfer operators, coarse-grid treatment,
and
hp
multigrid hierarchy follow

[
25
,
24
]
and
are not repeated here.

We measure algebraic residuals in the block mass-weighted norm

\left\lVert(\bm{r}_{v},\bm{r}_{p})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}\coloneq\bm{r}_{v}^{\top}\bm{M}_{v}\bm{r}_{v}+\bm{r}_{p}^{\top}\bm{M}_{p}\bm{r}_{p},

where
\bm{M}_{v}
and
\bm{M}_{p}
denote the time-step velocity and pressure mass blocks.
For exact Newton and modified Newton, FGMRES is terminated by the inexact-solve
condition

\left\lVert\bm{J}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})\Delta\bm{U}^{n,m}-\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\eta_{m}\left\lVert\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}},

(33)

with an adaptive forcing term
\eta_{m}
(cf.
[
25
]
).
For Picard, we use the same Krylov solver with a prescribed relative tolerance.
Concrete parameter values are reported in
Section

7
.

6.2
Globalization and stopping criteria

For exact Newton and modified Newton, we use Armijo backtracking on the
current slab
S_{n}
, with

\phi_{n}(\bm{U})\coloneq\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}.

Picard steps are taken with
\lambda_{m}=1
unless additional residual-based
damping is required.

The nonlinear iteration is terminated once

\left\lVert\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\left\lVert\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\left\lVert\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,0})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}.

(34)

We additionally impose a maximal number of nonlinear iterations and declare the
time-step solve unsuccessful if the line search or the linear solver fails to meet
the prescribed tolerances.

6.3
Multigrid preconditioning and surrogate patch assembly

We use one space-time multigrid V-cycle as preconditioner in each Krylov step.
Here “space-time multigrid” refers to multigrid applied to the monolithic
system on one time step
S_{n}
, including all temporal basis functions on that
interval; we do not apply multigrid to the whole time horizon at once.
The general
hp
space-time multigrid construction, including the level
hierarchy, transfer operators, and coarse-grid treatment, is the same as in

[
25
,
24
]
.
The only approximation specific to the present nonlinear setting is a
finest-level surrogate assembly of local patch matrices. On coarser levels the
patch matrices are taken from the exact Petrov-Galerkin operators.

Hierarchy and coarse operators

A level
\ell\in\{0,\dots,L\}
is specified by a spatial mesh
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}

and a temporal DG degree
k_{\ell}
, with finest-level operator

\bm{A}_{L}\coloneq\bm{J}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m}).

The interlevel transfer operators are the tensor-product prolongation and
restriction operators from

[
25
,
24
]
,
and the coarse operators are defined recursively by the Petrov-Galerkin
relation

\bm{A}_{\ell}\coloneq\bm{\Pi}_{\ell}\bm{A}_{\ell+1}\bm{P}_{\ell},\qquad\ell=L-1,\dots,0.

Vanka smoothing

On each level we use one pre- and one post-smoothing step of an overlapping
additive Schwarz method of Vanka type.
For a spatial patch
\omega_{K}
with index set
I_{K}
of velocity and pressure
degrees of freedom, the associated space-time patch on
S_{n}
contains all
temporal coefficients attached to the unknowns in
I_{K}
; equivalently, the local
Vanka solve is performed on
\omega_{K}\times I_{n}
.
With
\bm{R}_{K}
denoting the restriction to this patch, one smoothing step is

\bm{d}\leftarrow\bm{d}+\omega\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\bm{R}_{K}^{\top}(\bm{R}_{K}\bm{A}_{\ell}\bm{R}_{K}^{\top})^{-1}\bm{R}_{K}(\bm{r}-\bm{A}_{\ell}\bm{d}),

(35)

with damping parameter
\omega\in(0,1]
.
The choice of cell patches versus vertex-star patches in space is as in

[
25
,
24
]
;
in time we always use the full time-step block.

Exact patch matrices

The time-step operator
\bm{A}_{\ell}
is never assembled globally.
On the finest level its action is obtained by quadrature-based matrix-free
evaluation; on coarser levels it is defined by Petrov-Galerkin projection.
For every level, the exact patch matrix is

\bm{A}_{\ell,K}\coloneq\bm{R}_{K}\bm{A}_{\ell}\bm{R}_{K}^{\top}.

Only the finest level is locally reassembled from time-node spatial blocks.
There, the state-dependent spatial blocks in (
29
)
depend on the Gauss-Radau node, so exact local patch assembly involves

k_{L}+1
distinct spatial blocks on the same time step.
More precisely, with

\bm{J}_{L,\mu}\coloneq-D\bm{F}_{\mu}(\bm{U}^{n,m}),\qquad\mu=1,\dots,k_{L}+1,

the finest-level time-local factor contains the block diagonal matrix

\diag(\bm{J}_{L,1},\dots,\bm{J}_{L,k_{L}+1}).

Consequently, exact finest-level patch assembly would require, for every patch

K
, the family of spatial patch blocks

\bm{J}_{L,\mu,K}\coloneq\bm{R}_{K}^{\mathrm{s}}\bm{J}_{L,\mu}(\bm{R}_{K}^{\mathrm{s}})^{\top},\qquad\mu=1,\dots,k_{L}+1,

where
\bm{R}_{K}^{\mathrm{s}}
denotes the purely spatial patch restriction.

Surrogate patch assembly

The only approximation in the smoother concerns the node-wise spatial block in
(
29
) on the finest level.
With the exact patch matrix

\bm{A}_{L,K}=\bm{R}_{K}\bm{A}_{L}\bm{R}_{K}^{\top}
from the preceding paragraph,
all temporal coefficient matrices from
(
28
)–(
29
), in particular

\bm{M}_{t}
and
\bm{K}_{t}
, are kept unchanged.

Choose a representative time
t_{n}^{\mathrm{rep}}\in I_{n}
and define a
representative spatial linearization block by evaluating all state-dependent
coefficients entering the locally reassembled finest-level patch operator once
at
t_{n}^{\mathrm{rep}}
and then freezing them over the time step.
In the present computations we use the midpoint of
I_{n}
.

At the level of locally reassembled finest-level patch matrices, the surrogate
replaces the family of time-node blocks by one representative block,

\diag(\bm{J}_{L,1},\dots,\bm{J}_{L,k_{L}+1})\quad\leadsto\quad\bm{I}_{k_{L}+1}\otimes\bm{J}_{L}^{\mathrm{rep}},

(36)

with all remaining temporal factors unchanged.
The corresponding surrogate finest-level patch matrix is denoted by

\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}.

Thus the surrogate replaces the
k_{L}+1
time-dependent spatial blocks on one
time step by one representative spatial block, while preserving the temporal
coupling encoded by the original time-step matrices.
The approximation is confined to the local finest-level patch solve:

(\bm{R}_{K}\bm{A}_{L}\bm{R}_{K}^{\top})^{-1}\quad\leadsto\quad\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{-1}.

The global defect
\bm{r}-\bm{A}_{\ell}\bm{d}
, the matrix-free operator action,
and the coarse-grid operators remain exact. On coarser levels we use the exact
patch matrices
\bm{A}_{\ell,K}
extracted from the Petrov-Galerkin operators.
We show in Appendix
C
that the finest-level patch
perturbation is controlled by the time-step size.

Interpretation and refresh strategy

The surrogate is a purely local coefficient freezing in the finest-level Vanka
patch matrices. It does not change the nonlinear residual or the matrix-free
global Jacobian action; it changes only the local patch solves in the multigrid
smoother and thus reduces smoother setup costs.

Within one FGMRES solve, the patch matrices used by the Vanka smoother and
their factorizations are kept fixed. They are assembled at the beginning of
each Newton or modified Newton step. Before a subsequent linear solve, the
finest-level surrogate patch matrices are refreshed only if the preceding solve
has deteriorated. Following
[
25
]
, we
trigger a rebuild when

\frac{\left\lVert\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m+1})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}}{\left\lVert\bm{R}_{n}(\bm{U}^{n,m})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}}>\rho,

or when the FGMRES iteration count from the preceding linear solve exceeds
twice that of the previous nonlinear step; see
Section

7
for the
parameters.

7
Numerical experiments

We first verify the fully discrete implementation by a manufactured-solution
convergence test in
Section

7.1
and then study a genuinely time-dependent
benchmark problem in
Section

7.2
. Throughout, the emphasis is on the fully implicit
space-time solver: nonlinear robustness, linear iteration counts, time dependence across time steps, and computational efficiency.

In space we use inf-sup stable pairs

\mathbb{Q}_{2}/\mathbb{P}_{1}^{\mathrm{disc}}
, and in time a DG
(1)
discretization.
Nonlinear systems are solved by the framework of

Sections

4
and
6
. The manufactured-solution and
parameter studies in
Section

7.1
compare Picard, Newton, and modified Newton solvers
in the same algebraic framework. For the time-dependent DFG benchmark we focus
on the modified Newton, which was the most reliable option in the preceding tests.
We terminate the nonlinear iteration when the residual satisfies an absolute tolerance of
10^{-12}
and a relative tolerance of

10^{-10}
. These strict criteria are chosen to verify that the modified tangents do not introduce an accuracy barrier, i.e.,
they permit reduction of the residual to near machine precision rather than stagnating at a tangent-induced floor.
We use Eisenstat-Walker forcing and Armijo
backtracking as described in
Section

6
. Each linearized subproblem is treated by FGMRES preconditioned with a single

V
-cycle geometric multigrid with Vanka-type smoothing.

The numerical experiments were performed on a workstation with an Intel Xeon Gold 6254 CPU and 396GB of RAM.
The implementation is based on the deal.II library and openly available
at
[
26
]
. We call the method

iteration counts stay bounded under mesh refinement, and

FGMRES
if the linear iteration counts remain bounded under the same
refinement. We use two pre- and two post-smoothing steps throughout this
section. To quantify the effect of inertia we vary
\nu_{\infty}
. We choose a
characteristic length
L
and velocity scale

8
Conclusions and outlook

We have presented a scalable Newton-Krylov framework for fully implicit
tensor-product space-time finite element discretizations of incompressible

(p,\delta)
-Navier-Stokes flow in the shear-thinning regime
1<p<2
. The
decisive algorithmic difficulty is the constitutive tangent: as
p\downarrow 1
and
\delta\downarrow 0
, the exact tangent becomes increasingly
ill-conditioned, which impairs both Newton globalization and preconditioning.
Accordingly, the key design choice is its treatment in the nonlinear
linearization. In the parameter regimes considered here, exact Newton
stagnates and Picard converges too slowly, whereas the modified Newton method
is the only variant that remains reliable. It keeps the nonlinear residual
unchanged and replaces only the constitutive tangent in the Jacobian action by
a better-conditioned surrogate.

Numerically, we recover the expected convergence rates for a manufactured
solution and observe bounded nonlinear iteration counts under
h
-refinement for
the modified Newton solver. The method remains effective as
p
decreases and
also in the limit
\nu_{\infty}=0
. For the time-dependent DFG benchmark in
a strongly shear-thinning regime, the modified Newton solver shows robust
time-step-wise nonlinear performance over the full time interval. The main
remaining limitation lies in the linear solve: the iteration counts are not yet
fully mesh-robust, although they remain controlled on the finest levels
considered.

Taken together, these results show that fully implicit monolithic space-time
solvers for strongly shear-thinning flow are viable, provided that the
constitutive tangent is treated with care. The present framework provides a
solid basis for further development toward more demanding regimes and
large-scale simulations. Natural next steps include adaptive

(h,\tau)
refinement with robust patch rebuild criteria, and large-scale
studies of smoothing effort, rebuild frequency, and time-to-solution in
convection-dominated regimes.

48
)
can be absorbed by the viscous term after Cauchy-Young. Here we use

Lemma

A.4
only to justify that the quadrature defect is order-preserving in
\tau

and does not alter the algebraic structure in
Section

5
.

Appendix B
Spectrum of the exact
(p,\delta)
constitutive tangent

Recall the
(p,\delta)
stress law (
1
). Its Fréchet derivative
(cf. (
17
)) reads, for
\bm{A},\bm{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}
,

D\bm{S}\left(\bm{A}\right)\left(\bm{H}\right)=\eta\left(\bm{A}\right)\,\bm{H}+\beta(\bm{A})\,(\bm{A}:\bm{H})\,\bm{A},\qquad\beta(\bm{A})\coloneq\nu(p-2)\bigl(\delta^{2}+\left\lvert\bm{A}\right\rvert^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{p-4}{2}}.

(49)

Thus
\bm{H}\mapsto D\bm{S}\left(\bm{A}\right)\left(\bm{H}\right)
is a rank-one perturbation of

\eta\left(\bm{A}\right)\Id
on
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}
, and the spectrum follows from
the next standard observation.

Lemma B.1

(Rank-one perturbations)
.

Let
\eta>0
,
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}
, and
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, and define

\mathcal{T}(\bm{B})\coloneq\eta\,\bm{B}+\kappa(\bm{a}:\bm{B})\,\bm{a}
for
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym}
.
Then

(\mathcal{T}(\bm{B}):\bm{B})=\eta\,\left\lvert\bm{B}\right\rvert^{2}+\kappa\,(\bm{a}:\bm{B})^{2},\qquad\operatorname{spectrum}(\mathcal{T})=\{\eta,\ \eta+\kappa\left\lvert\bm{a}\right\rvert^{2}\},

where
\eta
acts on
\{\bm{B}:\bm{a}:\bm{B}=0\}
and
\eta+\kappa\left\lvert\bm{a}\right\rvert^{2}
on
\mathrm{span}\{\bm{a}\}
.
In particular,

\min\{\eta,\eta+\kappa\left\lvert\bm{a}\right\rvert^{2}\}\,\left\lvert\bm{B}\right\rvert^{2}\leq(\mathcal{T}(\bm{B}):\bm{B})\leq\max\{\eta,\eta+\kappa\left\lvert\bm{a}\right\rvert^{2}\}\,\left\lvert\bm{B}\right\rvert^{2},

and
\mathcal{T}\succ 0
if and only if both eigenvalues are positive, i.e.
\eta>0
and
\eta+\kappa\left\lvert\bm{a}\right\rvert^{2}>0
.

Proof.

Decompose
\bm{B}=\bm{B}_{\perp}+\alpha\bm{a}
with
\bm{a}:\bm{B}_{\perp}=0
and

\alpha=(\bm{a}:\bm{B})/\left\lvert\bm{a}\right\rvert^{2}
.
Then
\mathcal{T}(\bm{B}_{\perp})=\eta\bm{B}_{\perp}
and

\mathcal{T}(\bm{a})=(\eta+\kappa\left\lvert\bm{a}\right\rvert^{2})\bm{a}
, giving the spectrum and the bounds.
∎

Applying Lemma
B.1
to (
49
) with

\eta=\eta\left(\bm{A}\right)
,
\kappa=\beta(\bm{A})
, and
\bm{a}=\bm{A}
, we obtain:
if
\bm{A}=\bm{0}
, then
D\bm{S}\left(\bm{0}\right)\left(\bm{H}\right)=\eta\left(\bm{0}\right)\bm{H}
; if
\bm{A}\neq\bm{0}
,
the tangent has exactly two eigenvalues,

\lambda_{\perp}(\bm{A})=\eta\left(\bm{A}\right),\qquad\lambda_{\parallel}(\bm{A})=\eta\left(\bm{A}\right)+\beta(\bm{A})\left\lvert\bm{A}\right\rvert^{2},

(50)

with
\lambda_{\perp}(\bm{A})
on
\{\bm{H}:\bm{A}:\bm{H}=0\}
(multiplicity
\dim(\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\sym})-1
)
and
\lambda_{\parallel}(\bm{A})
in the direction
\bm{A}
.
Moreover, since
\eta\left(\bm{A}\right)>0
and
\delta^{2}+(p-1)\left\lvert\bm{A}\right\rvert^{2}>0
for
p>1
, we have

\lambda_{\parallel}(\bm{A})>0
, hence
D\bm{S}\left(\bm{A}\right)\left(\cdot\right)
is symmetric
positive definite for all
\bm{A}
.
Then, with
r\coloneq\left\lvert\bm{A}\right\rvert
, (
50
) yields

\lambda_{\parallel}(\bm{A})=\nu_{\infty}+\nu(\delta^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{p-4}{2}}\bigl(\delta^{2}+(p-1)r^{2}\bigr).

For
1<p<2
we have
\beta(\bm{A})\leq 0
, hence
\lambda_{\parallel}(\bm{A})\leq\lambda_{\perp}(\bm{A})
and
thus the smallest eigenvalue acts in the stress direction
\bm{A}
.

\frac{\lambda_{\perp}(\bm{A})}{\lambda_{\parallel}(\bm{A})}=\frac{\nu_{\infty}+\nu(\delta^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}}{\nu_{\infty}+\nu(\delta^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{p-4}{2}}\bigl(\delta^{2}+(p-1)r^{2}\bigr)}.

(51)

In particular, if
\nu_{\infty}=0
, then the exact constitutive tangent becomes increasingly anisotropic as
p\downarrow 1
in the
high-shear regime
r\gg\delta
:

\frac{\lambda_{\perp}(\bm{A})}{\lambda_{\parallel}(\bm{A})}=\frac{\delta^{2}+r^{2}}{\delta^{2}+(p-1)r^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{p-1}\,.

(52)

Appendix C
Characterization of local Vanka patch surrogate

To justify single-time-point coefficient surrogates in the local Vanka solves,
we compare the surrogate patch matrix with the exact patch matrix on each slab.
In this appendix we continue the finest-level notation from

Section

6.3
on a fixed slab
S_{n}
,
writing
\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n}
and
\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n}
.

Lemma C.1

(Surrogate perturbation for finest-level Vanka patches)
.

Fix a slab
S_{n}
, a patch
K\in\mathcal{M}_{L}
, and set

N_{K}\coloneq M_{K}^{\bm{v}}+M_{K}^{p}
. Let

\mathfrak{A}_{n,K}:\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{M_{K}^{\bm{v}}}\bigr)^{k_{L}+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{(k_{L}+1)N_{K}\times(k_{L}+1)N_{K}}

denote the exact finest-level patch matrix as a function of the local velocity
coefficient vectors at the Gauss-Radau points

t_{n}^{1},\dots,t_{n}^{k_{L}+1}\subset I_{n}
. Define

\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n}\coloneq\mathfrak{A}_{n,K}(\bm{U}_{n,K}^{1},\dots,\bm{U}_{n,K}^{k_{L}+1}),\qquad\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n}\coloneq\mathfrak{A}_{n,K}(\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mathrm{rep}},\dots,\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mathrm{rep}}),

where
\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{M_{K}^{\bm{v}}}
is the local velocity
coefficient vector at
t_{n}^{\mu}
, and
\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mathrm{rep}}
corresponds
to a representative time
t_{n}^{\mathrm{rep}}\in I_{n}
. All vector norms are
Euclidean, all matrix norms are the induced operator
2
-norms, and

\mathcal{W}(\bm{B})\coloneq\{\langle\bm{x},\bm{B}\bm{x}\rangle:\bm{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{(k_{L}+1)N_{K}},\ \|\bm{x}\|=1\}.

Assume that
\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n}
is invertible with

\|(\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\|\leq M_{n,K}
, and that:

(i)

there exist a bounded set

\mathcal{U}_{n,K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{M_{K}^{\bm{v}}}
containing all

\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mu}
and
\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mathrm{rep}}
, and a constant
L_{n,K}>0
,
such that

\|\mathfrak{A}_{n,K}(\bm{V}^{1},\dots,\bm{V}^{k_{L}+1})-\mathfrak{A}_{n,K}(\bm{W}^{1},\dots,\bm{W}^{k_{L}+1})\|\leq L_{n,K}\sum_{\mu=1}^{k_{L}+1}\|\bm{V}^{\mu}-\bm{W}^{\mu}\|

(53)

for all
(\bm{V}^{\bullet}),(\bm{W}^{\bullet})\in(\mathcal{U}_{n,K})^{k_{L}+1}
;

(ii)

there exists
C_{K}>0
, independent of
\tau_{n}
, such that

\|\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mu}-\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mathrm{rep}}\|\leq C_{K}\tau_{n},\qquad\mu=1,\dots,k_{L}+1.

(54)

With

\bm{E}_{n,K}\coloneq\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n}-\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n},\qquad\varepsilon_{n,K}\coloneq\|(\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\bm{E}_{n,K}\|,

the following hold:

(a)

Perturbation bound.

\|\bm{E}_{n,K}\|\leq(k_{L}+1)L_{n,K}C_{K}\tau_{n},\qquad\varepsilon_{n,K}\leq M_{n,K}(k_{L}+1)L_{n,K}C_{K}\tau_{n}.

(55)

Hence
\varepsilon_{n,K}<1
for all sufficiently small
\tau_{n}
.

(b)

Preconditioned operator.

\sigma\!\bigl((\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n}\bigr),\;\mathcal{W}\!\bigl((\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n}\bigr)\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ |z-1|\leq\varepsilon_{n,K}\}.

(56)

(c)

Surrogate stability.

If
\varepsilon_{n,K}<1
, then
\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n}
is invertible,

\|(\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\|\leq\frac{M_{n,K}}{1-\varepsilon_{n,K}},

(57)

and

(1-\varepsilon_{n,K})\,s_{\min}(\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})\leq s_{\min}(\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n})\leq s_{\max}(\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n})\leq(1+\varepsilon_{n,K})\,s_{\max}(\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n}).

(58)

Proof.

By definition and (
53
),

\|\bm{E}_{n,K}\|=\|\mathfrak{A}_{n,K}(\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mathrm{rep}},\dots,\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mathrm{rep}})-\mathfrak{A}_{n,K}(\bm{U}_{n,K}^{1},\dots,\bm{U}_{n,K}^{k_{L}+1})\|\leq L_{n,K}\sum_{\mu=1}^{k_{L}+1}\|\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mu}-\bm{U}_{n,K}^{\mathrm{rep}}\|.

Using (
54
) gives (
55
), and
hence

\varepsilon_{n,K}\leq\|(\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\|\,\|\bm{E}_{n,K}\|\leq M_{n,K}(k_{L}+1)L_{n,K}C_{K}\tau_{n}.

For (
56
), write

(\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n}=\bm{I}+\bm{F},\qquad\bm{F}\coloneq(\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\bm{E}_{n,K},

so that
\|\bm{F}\|=\varepsilon_{n,K}
. If

\lambda\in\sigma(\bm{I}+\bm{F})
and
\|\bm{x}\|=1
is a corresponding
eigenvector, then
\bm{F}\bm{x}=(\lambda-1)\bm{x}
, hence

|\lambda-1|\leq\|\bm{F}\|=\varepsilon_{n,K}
. Likewise, for

\|\bm{x}\|=1
,

|\langle\bm{x},(\bm{I}+\bm{F})\bm{x}\rangle-1|=|\langle\bm{x},\bm{F}\bm{x}\rangle|\leq\|\bm{F}\|=\varepsilon_{n,K},

which proves (
56
).

If
\varepsilon_{n,K}<1
, then

\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n}=\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n}(\bm{I}+\bm{F}),

and the Neumann series yields

\|(\bm{I}+\bm{F})^{-1}\|\leq(1-\varepsilon_{n,K})^{-1}.

Therefore,

\|(\tilde{\bm{A}}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\|\leq\frac{\|(\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n})^{-1}\|}{1-\varepsilon_{n,K}}\leq\frac{M_{n,K}}{1-\varepsilon_{n,K}},

which proves (
57
). Finally, the product inequalities
for singular values and

(1-\|\bm{F}\|)\|\bm{x}\|\leq\|(\bm{I}+\bm{F})\bm{x}\|\leq(1+\|\bm{F}\|)\|\bm{x}\|

imply

1-\varepsilon_{n,K}\leq s_{\min}(\bm{I}+\bm{F})\leq s_{\max}(\bm{I}+\bm{F})\leq 1+\varepsilon_{n,K},

and hence (
58
).
∎

Remark C.2

(Scope of the assumptions)
.

The essential structural hypothesis is the invertibility of the realized exact
patch matrix
\bm{A}_{L,K}^{n}
with bound
M_{n,K}
; the result is therefore
step-local rather than neighborhood-uniform. The localized Lipschitz condition
(
53
) is natural for the present

(p,\delta)
-Navier-Stokes setting, since
\delta>0
yields smooth dependence
of the coefficient-dependent patch contributions on bounded sets of local
velocity states. The approximation property (
54
) expresses

O(\tau_{n})
variation of the local velocity coefficients across the slab and
is used here as an assumption. The inclusion (
56
) shows
that the preconditioned surrogate patch operator remains close to the identity
in spectrum and numerical range, which is the relevant local approximation
property for the surrogate Vanka solve.
