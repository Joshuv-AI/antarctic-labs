Risk stratification of 1-year atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence after ablation in patients with persistent atrial fibrillation: ALADS-AF score
Authors: Jingcheng Chen, Liang Zhao, Kai Zhang, Jindong Chen, Yunhe Wang, Weizhu Ju, Chenyang Jiang, Jingquan Zhong, Linlin Wang, Minglong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAtrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common atrial arrhythmia with high prevalence, low awareness, and low treatment rate in China. Catheter ablation (CA) is an effective treatment, but recurrence remains common in persistent AF (PsAF). The present study aimed to develop a tool for 1-year atrial tachyarrhythmia (ATa) recurrence risk stratification after de novo CA.MethodsThis multicenter, retrospective study collected data from four Chinese hospitals between January 2015 and May 2021. Eligible patients were randomly divided in a 2:1 ratio to development (DC) and test cohorts (TC). One-year ATa recurrence status was primarily obtained from existing medical records. Candidate risk factors for recurrence were initially identified through literature review and investigator consensus. Univariable and multivariable Cox regression analyses were performed to develop the model. The model’s discrimination and calibration were evaluated in both cohorts to assess performance.ResultsA total of 1,689 patients who underwent de novo CA were included, with 1,126 in DC and 563 in TC. The mean age was 60.5 years, 71.9% were male, the mean PsAF duration was 2.6 years, and 26.1% experienced 1-year ATa recurrence within 3–12 months post-ablation. Recurrence was associated with older age, larger Left Atrial Diameter (LAD), longer AF duration, and female sex. The ALADS-AF score (Age, LAD, Duration of AF, Sex) was developed, with scores from 0 to 30 and a C-index of 0.57.ConclusionsThe ALADS-AF score is a simple and user-friendly tool for stratifying the risk of 1-year ATa recurrence in patients with PsAF after de novo CA. It may facilitate pre-procedural discussions, help set realistic expectations regarding rhythm outcomes, and support consistent follow-up planning in patients undergoing ablation for PsAF.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-06097-w.Keywords: Atrial fibrillation; Catheter ablation; Risk score, Risk stratification, One-year atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common atrial arrhythmia, with an incidence of 0.71% among the Chinese population aged 35 and older [1]. In a nationwide survey of adults in China, the awareness of having AF decreased from 65.3% in 45–54 years old to 53.9% in ≥ 75 years old and even worse in the rural population (35.3%), and only 6.0% of AF patients with high stroke risk received anticoagulation therapy [2]. AF is associated with an increased risk of thromboembolism, new-onset heart failure, and all-cause mortality [3]. Catheter ablation (CA) has emerged as an effective therapeutic intervention for alleviating symptoms in patients with AF [4]. When incorporated into an early rhythm control strategy to maintain sinus rhythm, CA may contribute to reducing the risks of ischemic stroke, heart failure, mortality, and other adverse events [5–7]. With ongoing technological advancements, the success rate of CA in paroxysmal AF (PAF) has improved considerably, establishing it as a first-line treatment for this AF subtype. However, recurrence rates following CA vary significantly across AF subtypes. Compared with PAF, persistent AF (PsAF) is generally characterized by more pronounced structural and electrical remodeling, leading to a higher likelihood of recurrence after ablation [8]. Consequently, the accurate identification of patients with PsAF who are likely to respond well to CA is crucial for enhancing procedural outcomes. The development of a model for estimating the risk of recurrence in PsAF could greatly facilitate improved patient selection and inform individualized ablation strategies.

The risk factors for atrial tachyarrhythmia (ATa) recurrence have been extensively studied, leading to the development of several predictive scores[9–13], including ALARMEc, APPLE, SUCCESS, ATLAS, BASE-AF2, CAAP-AF, HATCH, MB-LATER, and FER2CI. These scores often lack specificity for Chinese patients and local practice. Additionally, some scores are built on laboratory results or post-ablation parameters to improve their prediction performance, which may conversely impact their clinical practicality.

This study aims to address these gaps by developing a tool to assess the recurrence risk in Chinese patients with PsAF. The tool will be derived from real-world electronic medical records, including clinical characteristics, medical history, imaging data, and serum biomarkers.

Methods

This study was a multicenter, retrospective cohort study based on data from electronic medical records collected from four Chinese hospitals (Jiangsu People’s Hospital [JSRM], Shanghai Chest Hospital [SHXK], Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital affiliated with Zhejiang University School of Medicine [ZJSYF], and Qilu Hospital of Shandong University [SDQR]) between January 2015 and May 2021.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee (EC) of each hospital, including: 1.The Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University, NO: 2022-SR-070.A1. 2. The Ethics Committee of Qilu Hospital of Shandong University, NO: KYLL-2022B-010. 3. The Ethics Committee of Shanghai Chest Hospital, NO: LS22051. 4. The Ethics Committee of Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital Zhejiang University School of Medicine, NO: 20,220,086. All patients signed the informed consent form, unless a waiver was approved by the EC. All procedures were performed under the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patient population

Eligible patients were included if they were aged ≥ 18 years at the time of the procedure, diagnosed with PsAF, had undergone their de novo CA for PsAF, Exclusion criteria: (1) patients with recent acute coronary syndrome; (2) history of previous cardiac surgery;3. AF related to hyperthyroidism and active infection.

Pre-procedural preparation

AF patients received continuous anticoagulation for at least 1 month before the procedure. Anticoagulants were not discontinued prior to the procedure. LA thrombus was excluded by CT scan and/or transesophageal echocardiography within 48 h prior to the study.

Electrophysiological mapping and ablation strategy

Both the Carto system and the EnSite system were used as the three-dimensional mapping systems. The main ablation catheters included the ThermoCool® ST from Johnson & Johnson and CoolPath Duo/CoolFlexCool from Abbott. Entrance and exit blocks evaluation were all performed after circumferential pulmonary vein isolation (CPVI). At each participating hospital, the specific ablation strategy was determined by the physicians. The pulmonary vein isolation plus (PVI+) strategy encompassed three distinct approaches: STABLE-SR strategy (CPVI+ substrate modification of left atrial low-voltage areas), anatomical-guided ablation (Mitral isthmus ablation + Cavotricuspid isthmus ablation + Roof line ablation), and electrogram-guided ablation (ablation of complex fractionated electrograms).

Follow-up and 1-year ATa recurrence definition

Patients were followed according to the standard of care (SOC) at each hospital, which included follow-up visits at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after ablation. Standard evaluations included regular 12-lead ECGs (1, 3, 6, and 12 months) and 24-hour Holter monitoring (3 and 12 months). Additional visits and monitoring were arranged if patients experienced symptoms suggestive of arrhythmia recurrence. One-year ATa recurrence was defined as an electrocardiographically documented episode of AF/atrial flutter (AFL)/atrial tachycardia (AT) lasting at least 30 s after a 3-month blanking period after the de novo ablation procedure. Recurrence status was primarily obtained from existing medical records [14].

Risk factors screening and determination

Candidate predictors were pre-specified based on prior literature and investigator consensus, with the additional requirement of clinical relevance and feasibility for routine pre-procedural assessment. In the first step, a systematic search was conducted using the keywords “persistent atrial fibrillation,” “catheter ablation,” “recurrence,” and “predictor/risk factors.” This search identified a total of 1,851 potential articles. After screening for eligibility, 54 articles were included. From these articles, 31 risk factors associated with long-term recurrence were identified. In the second step, the identified 31 risk factors were then discussed and confirmed by all investigators based on clinical relevance and practicality. Finally, all 31 risk factors were determined to be significant for data collection.

Data collection

Demographic and baseline clinical data for all patients were collected. Demographics included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), current alcohol consumption status, duration of AF, medical history (obstructive sleep apnea [OSA], hypertension, diabetes, coronary artery disease, heart failure, stroke/transient ischemic attack [TIA], peripheral artery disease[PAD], chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), hyperthyroidism, moderate to severe mitral regurgitation, moderate to severe tricuspid regurgitation), CHA2DS2-VASc score, and presence of a family history of AF. Pre-operative blood tests included NT-proBNP, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR); imaging data included left atrial diameter (LAD), right atrial diameter (RAD) and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF).

Statistical methods

Study cohorts and descriptive statistics

Eligible patients were randomly divided in a 2:1 ratio to a development cohort (DC) for model development and internal model validation and a test cohort (TC) for split-sample model validation, which ensures comparable distribution of patient characteristics and center-specific practice patterns between the DC and TC. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. To enhance interpretability and facilitate practical application of the resulting scoring system, continuous baseline variables were categorized using prespecified cutoffs based on established clinical relevance and commonly used thresholds in routine clinical practice. Baseline characteristics between the DC and TC were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables and Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables.

The 1-year ATa recurrence rate was presented as frequencies and percentages across subgroups categorized by baseline characteristics.

Handling of missing data

The baseline variables with more than 50% missing data were excluded from model development. For variables with missing data of 50% or less, missing values were imputed using a single regression-based imputation method before fitting Cox proportional hazards model.

Model development

Associations between the 1-year ATa recurrence and individual risk factors were assessed using univariable Cox proportional hazards model as an exploratory screening step. Risk factors with a P value < 0.05 or with a moderate/strong effect size as determined by a hazard ratio (HR) > 1.2 or HR ≤ 0.8 were considered candidates for inclusion in the multivariable Cox proportional hazards model. Prior to multivariable Cox regression analysis, potential risk factors exhibiting high collinearity were carefully reviewed. The stepwise model selection approach was subsequently used to refine the multivariable cox proportional hazards model by excluding redundant factors and retaining variables that contributed meaningfully to model performance.

Model assessment and comparison

Model discrimination was assessed using area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) and the concordance index (C-index). The C-index quantifies the model’s ability to correctly rank-order individuals according to their estimated risk, indicating that patients assigned higher estimated risks are more likely to experience ATa recurrence than those assigned lower estimated risks.

Model calibration was assessed by comparing observed and estimated mean ATa recurrence rate. Estimated risks were first ranked and then divided into four groups based on the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles. With each group, the mean observed ATa recurrence rate was compared with the mean estimated rate. In addition, the agreement between the observed and estimated risk rates was evaluated using the calibration slope and calibration intercept. The calibration intercept reflects systematic over- or under-estimation of risk, with values close to zero indicating good overall calibration. The calibration slope evaluates the agreement in risk dispersion, where a slope of 1 indicates perfect calibration. A calibration slope greater than 1 suggests underfitting, with estimated risks being overly shrunk toward the mean and therefore less extreme than the observed risks, whereas a slope less than 1 indicates overfitting with overly extreme predictions.

Classification performance was assessed using sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy. Bootstrapping method was used to calculate the 95% confidence interval (CI). A bootstrap resampling technique was employed, in which, during each iteration, the same number of subjects as in each cohort were randomly sampled with replacement. This process was repeated 1,000 times. Selected competing candidate models were also compared using the net reclassification improvement (NRI), which evaluates improvements in risk stratification by assessing whether a model increases estimated risk among individuals who experience the event while decreasing estimated risk among those who do not, without requiring prespecified risk categories. The NRI with positive values indicates improved risk reclassification.

The final multivariable model was selected based on a balanced consideration of statistical performance, model parsimony, clinical relevance, and practicality for clinical use.

Risk score development

To create a practical risk score, each of the independent predictors was assigned a weighted points value proportional to its beta regression coefficient from the final multivariable model, rounded to the nearest integer. The smallest beta coefficient was identified and used as the common denominator, by which all other coefficients were divided to determine their respective multiplying factors for the score. The final score is defined as the sum of the points assigned to each risk factor for an individual.

One-year ATa recurrence risk stratification

Based on the developed risk score, patients in DC were classified into three risk groups using the estimated probability thresholds derived from the ROC curve of the model for 1-year ATa recurrence. A lower estimated probability threshold corresponding to a sensitivity of 95% and an upper estimated probability threshold corresponding to a specificity of 95% were used to define low-, intermediate-, and high-risk categories. Specifically, patients were classified as low risk (estimated probability below the sensitivity-based threshold), intermediate risk (estimated probability between the sensitivity- and specificity-based thresholds), or high risk (estimated probability above the specificity-based threshold). The prognostic significance of these risk groups was subsequently evaluated in TC by using Cox proportional hazards model.

All tests were 2-sided. Analyses were performed using R 4.2.1.

Results

Patients distribution

A total of 2,124 patients with PsAF who underwent de novo AF ablation between January 2015 and May 2021 from four hospitals were consecutively screened, and 1,689 patients were eligible for analysis, with 45% from JSRM, 19% from SDQR, 19% from ZJSYF, and 18% from SHXK. Eligible patients were randomly divided in a 2:1 ratio to a DC (1,126 patients) for model development and interval model validation and a TC (563 patients) for split-sample model validation. Procedural information

In the overall cohort, the majority of patients (78.7%, 1,329/1,689) underwent pulmonary vein isolation plus additional ablation outside the pulmonary veins (PVI+), with similar proportions observed in the DC (79.5%, 895/1,126) and TC (77.1%, 434/563).

One-year recurrence rate

In the overall cohort, within 3–12 months after the de novo CA, the 1-year ATa recurrence rate was 26.1% (440/1,689), with 21.1% (356/1,689) experiencing AF, 2.3% (38/1,689) AT, 3.7% (62/1,689) AFL, and 7.5% (127/1,689) requiring re-ablation.

In the DC, the 1-year ATa recurrence rate was 25.1% (283/1,126), with 20.4% (230/1,126) experiencing AF, 2.4% (27/1,126) AT, 3.1% (35/1,126) AFL, and 6.7% (75/1,126) requiring re-ablation.

In the TC, the 1-year ATa recurrence rate was 27.9% (157/563), with 20.4% (126/563) experiencing AF, 2.0% (11/563) AT, 4.8% (27/563) AFL, and 9.2% (52/563) requiring re-ablation.

There was no statistically significant difference between the DC and TC (P values > 0.05).

Model development and internal validation in the DC

In the DC, univariable Cox regression analysis was performed for exploratory screening of potential risk factors using the completed dataset generated by single regression-based imputation method. The univariable analyses indicated that a higher hazard of 1-year ATa recurrence was associated with older age, female sex, longer duration of AF, larger LAD, higher CHA2DS2-VAsc score, higher NT-proBNP, OSA, hyperthyroidism, and moderate to severe valvular insufficiency (either p-value < 0.05 or a HR > 1.2 or HR ≤ 0.8) (Table 2).

Variables identified in the univariable Cox regression analysis were subsequently entered into a multivariable Cox regression analysis, yielding an initial 9-variable model. A stepwise regression analysis was then applied to identify the most parsimonious model, resulting in a 4-variables model that included age, sex, duration of AF, and LAD (Table 3).

Calibration analysis of the two models yielded similar results, showing good agreement between estimated and observed risk of 1-year ATa recurrence. Across the quartiles of estimated risk (each containing 25% patients), the mean observed arrhythmia recurrence rate increased monotonically for both 4-varaible and 9-variable models, indicating consistent risk stratification in the DC (Supplemental Fig. 1).

NRI analysis was conducted in the DC to compare the reclassification performance of the two models. When the 9-variable model was compared with the 4-variable model, the total NRI was 0.0256, comprising an NRI of − 0.2650 among patients with 1-year ATa recurrence and 0.2906 among those without recurrence. The total NRI value close to zero (0.0256) indicated similar reclassification performance of two models, although the 9-variable model showed a marginally positive net improvement.

Development of ALADS-AF score in the DC

Although the 9-variable model incorporated additional risk factors, it yielded only a marginal improvement in the C-index—from 0.57 to 0.58 and the total NRI of 0.0256. Moreover, several variables in the larger model (e.g., NT-proBNP and OSA) are not routinely available across all participating hospitals, limiting the model’s generalizability and ease of clinical implementation.

Considering the modest performance improvement, the availability of routinely collected clinical data, and model robustness, the 4-variable model was finally selected for the scoring system, named ALADS-AF (Age, LAD, Duration of AF, Sex for 1-year ATa risk stratification).

Based on the 4-variable model, each of the independent risk factors was assigned a weighted points value proportional to its beta regression coefficient, the maximum points in the ALADS-AF score are 30 points, representing patients aged 75 years or older (5 points), with LAD greater than 45 mm (10 points), AF duration at least 3 years (10 points), and female sex (5 points). The minimum point is 0, representing patients aged 65 years or younger, with LAD less than 40 mm, AF duration less than 1 year, and male sex. The ALADS-AF score is conveniently presented using a nomogram for easy visual interpretation. (Fig. 3 and Supplemental Table 1).

Validation of ALADS-AF score in the TC

We validated the ALADS-AF score using data from the TC. The discrimination with a C-index of 0.57 (95% CI 0.55, 0.59) and the calibration analysis were consistent with the results in the DC. Estimated risks were first ranked and then divided into quartiles, and the mean observed ATa recurrence rate increased with higher estimated risk in trend, particularly among patients in the higher-risk groups. (Fig. 2 and Supplemental Fig. 1&2).

Supplemental Fig. 3 displays the relationship between estimated and observed risk probabilities, with a linear regression used to assess calibration. The 4-variable model showed a calibration slope of 1.4 and an intercept of − 0.076. The near-zero intercept indicates good overall calibration, whereas the slope greater than 1 suggests underfitting, with estimated risks tending to be less extreme than observed risks.

Comparison of ALADS-AF score with previously published scores based on pre-procedural risk factors in the TC

Given that the ALADS-AF score includes only pre-procedural variables, its performance was compared with that of two published AF risk stratification scores based on pre-procedural factors (the Apple score and the CHA2DS2-VASc score). The ALADS-AF score demonstrates sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy comparable to those of two scores when applying their recommended literature-based cutoff points, although with marginally higher values observed (Supplemental Table 2).

Risk stratification according to ALADS-AF score in the DC and its application in the TC

To define clinically interpretable risk strata, two estimated probability thresholds for 1-year ATa recurrence risk were used based on based on ROC curve in the DC. A lower threshold corresponding to a sensitivity of 95% (estimated probability = 0.192) and an upper threshold corresponding to a specificity of 95% (estimated probability = 0.330) were used to classify low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups. Accordingly, patients were classified as low risk (< 0.192), intermediate risk (0.192–0.330), or high risk (> 0.330). These probability thresholds were then mapped to the ALADS-AF point score (low risk: <5; intermediate risk: 5–20; high risk: >20) and to the nomogram points (< 45, 45–180, and > 180, respectively) to facilitate bedside use. In the DC, both the intermediate-risk and high-risk levels had a significantly higher risk of 1-year ATa recurrence after de novo CA compared to the low-risk level, with HRs of 1.62 (95% CI: 1.26, 2.08) and 2.86 (95% CI: 2.15, 3.81), respectively.

Similarly, in the TC, the HR was 1.83 (95% CI: 1.33, 2.53) for the intermediate-risk level and 2.63 (95% CI: 1.81, 3.82) for the high-risk level (Fig. 4). These findings support the ALADS-AF score as a practical tool for stratifying 1-year ATa recurrence risk and identifying patients across clinically meaningful risk levels.

While the ALADS-AF score showed overall good risk stratification across the 1-year follow-up, some overlap of the survival curves was noted during early follow-up in the TC but not in the DC. This may reflect random variation between two cohorts and more limited discrimination of recurrence risk in the early post-ablation period in the TC.

Exploratory analysis of 1-year ATa recurrence rate of PVI vs. PVI + in PsAF patients with different risk levels

Out of 1,689 patients with PsAF, 141 were excluded due to missing LAD data. This retrospective analysis ultimately included 1,548 patients. The patient distribution was 7.4% (115/1,548) for low-risk, 84.2% (1,303/1,548) for intermediate-risk, and 8.4% (130/1,548) for high-risk. Among the total population, 22.0% (340/1,548) patients underwent PVI only, with distribution as follows: 26.2% (176/672) from JSRM, 17.0% (49/289) from SDQR, 21.1% (64/303) from ZJSYF, and 18.0% (51/284) from SHXK. Across all risk levels, 27% (31/115) underwent PVI in low-risk patients, 22.6% (294/1,303) in intermediate-risk, and 11.5% (15/130) in high-risk.

There was no significant difference in 1-year ATa recurrence rate between PVI and PVI+ across all risk levels. However, the 1-year ATa recurrence rate was numerically lower with PVI+ treatment compared to PVI treatment in PsAF patients with high recurrence risk (39.1% vs. 46.7%). Patients with low risk showed a favorable 1-year ATa recurrence rate regardless of whether they received PVI or PVI+, compared to those with intermediate and high risk (Supplemental Table 3).

Discussion

To our knowledge, the ALADS-AF score is the first pragmatic, pre-procedural tool developed to stratify 1-year atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence risk in Chinese patients with PsAF undergoing de novo catheter ablation. This study yields three key findings: (1) the ALADS-AF score was derived through a systematic process and validated using a split-sample approach within a multicenter real-world dataset; (2) 1-year ATa recurrence risk increased incrementally with older age, LAD, longer AF duration, and female sex, supporting the concept that earlier diagnosis and earlier referral for rhythm control may increase the proportion of patients at lower recurrence risk; (3) given its simplicity and reliance on routinely available pre-procedural variables, ALADS-AF may facilitate personalized risk communication and follow-up planning in routine clinical practice, rather than serving as a high-precision individual prediction model.

Stratification performance of ALADS-AF score

The ALADS-AF score displayed moderate discrimination performance, with a C-index of 0.57 (95% CI: 0.54, 0.60). This level of discrimination limits strong individual-level risk predictions. Accordingly, ALADS-AF should be interpreted primarily as a pragmatic, low-complexity, pre-procedural risk stratification tool for real-world practice, intended to support risk communication and follow-up planning rather than to serve as a stand-alone decision instrument.

Notably, it was derived from real-world multicenter datasets encompassing diverse regions across China, which enhances its representativeness and relevance to routine care. The modest discriminative ability may be partly attributable to the characteristics of the study population. For instance, although LAD is a well-established predictor of atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence after ablation in multiple studies, its prognostic value is most evident in patients with significantly enlarged left atrium (≥ 47 mm) [15], In our cohort, the majority of patients had an LAD below 45 mm, and only 21.8% had substantial atrial enlargement (≥ 47 mm). This likely attenuated the contribution of LAD within the model.

It is also important to note that this risk score is developed specifically for patients with PsAF. Risk stratification in such a relatively homogeneous, high-risk population is inherently more challenging than in mixed cohorts that include paroxysmal AF, as done in many existing scores. In addition, several published scores (e.g., MB-LATER, BASE-AF2, FER2CI) that report higher discrimination often incorporate strong post-procedural predictors (e.g., early recurrence) or other parameters that may not be routinely available before the procedure. The parameters of the models are summarized in Supplemental Table 4. The main strength of ALADS-AF lies in its simplicity and exclusive reliance on readily available pre-procedural clinical variables, which facilitates implementation across diverse clinical settings.

Non-invasive electrophysiological features derived from standard surface ECGs (e.g., fibrillatory wave amplitude or dominant frequency) and advanced approaches such as machine learning may further improve performance in future work. However, any added complexity should be balanced against feasibility, missingness, interpretability, and clinical implementability, which were core design objectives of the ALADS‑AF score.

PVI vs. PVI + in PsAF: Exploratory findings and potential implications

The choice between PVI and PVI + in patients with PsAF remains inconclusive. In this study, data were retrospectively and consecutively collected, potentially reflecting the current preferences of Chinese physicians: (1) the average proportion of patients with PVI only was 21.3%, ranging from 17% to 26.2% at different participating centers; (2) patients with higher ALADS-AF scores were more likely to be selected for the PVI+ procedure. Theoretically, patients with higher scores are at greater risk of post-procedural recurrence. However, in this study, the recurrence rate was numerically lower in the PVI+ group than in the PVI only group (24.4% vs. 28.1%), indicating that PVI + may bring benefits, but the difference was not statistically significant. Given the retrospective, real-world design, strategy selection was at physicians’ discretion, confounding by indication and center-level heterogeneity cannot be fully excluded. Therefore, the PVI versus PVI+ comparison should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of strategy superiority.

Recent studies and subgroup analyses suggest that adjunctive ablation may provide incremental benefit in selected PsAF patients when guided by specific intra-procedural characteristics [16, 17]. Extended ablation strategies may be accompanied with potential disadvantages, such as longer procedure times, increased resource utilization, greater risks and development of atrial tachycardias. Therefore, Future prospective studies are needed to identify which patient profiles and standardized PVI+ workflows may improve outcomes while balancing potential drawbacks related to additional lesion sets [18–20].

Clinical implications of the ALADS-AF score

With the ALADS-AF score, clinicians can quickly identify PsAF patients with relatively lower versus higher estimated 1-year ATa recurrence risk after de novo catheter ablation using four pre-procedural factors. For example, male patients aged under 65 years, with a PsAF duration of less than one year, and a LAD of less than 40 mm may have the lowest estimated recurrence rate (ALADS-AF score 0), approaching rates reported in some paroxysmal AF populations [21].

The ALADS-AF score may support personalized risk communication. Patients with lower estimated recurrence risk may be reassured regarding the likelihood of favorable outcomes after ablation, which may facilitate acceptance of the procedure. Conversely, patients with higher estimated risk may be counseled more cautiously, allowing clinicians to discuss alternative strategies, ablation strategies, closer follow-up, or adjunctive therapies.

Recent evidence suggests that, beyond patient characteristics, ablation energy source may influence procedural outcomes and patient-reported endpoints. Emerging comparisons between pulsed-field ablation and contemporary high-power short-duration radiofrequency strategies have reported differences in efficacy/recurrence and safety profiles, reflecting an evolving ablation landscape. Additionally, ablation modality may affect symptom burden and quality of life—endpoints increasingly emphasized in AF management. As our model was derived from RF-era data and focuses on ECG-documented atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence, future work should validate and potentially recalibrate ALADS-AF in PFA-treated cohorts and assess patient-centered outcomes [22, 23].

Limitations

This study had several limitations, First, due to the retrospective design, some baseline variables were missing, and cases with unavailable key variables were excluded, which may introduce selection bias. Second, recurrence ascertainment relied on standard-of-care intermittent monitoring (scheduled ECG and 24-hour Holter), which may underestimate asymptomatic episodes and contribute to fewer observed events and attenuated discrimination. Third, although we evaluated a broad set of candidate predictors, some categories had low prevalence and limited events, which may yield unstable estimates for those variables. Fourth, ablation strategy was categorized as PVI and PVI+ without standardized, protocol-mandated workflows. Center-level heterogeneity and confounding by indication cannot be ruled out. And a longer follow-up period may be required to detect potential differences. Fifth, validation in the current manuscript is based on an split-sample design within a multicenter Chinese PsAF dataset; independent external validation in geographically distinct cohorts and in contemporary ablation workflows will be needed to confirm transportability. Finally, to prioritize e feasibility and bedside usability, ALADS-AF intentionally includes only a small number of readily available pre-procedural variables, which likely contributes to its modest discriminative performance.

Conclusions

In this multicenter real-world cohort of Chinese patients with PsAF undergoing de novo catheter ablation, the ALADS-AF score is a simple and pragmatic tool based on routinely available pre-procedural clinical variables. The score stratifies patients into clinically interpretable risk categories of 1-year ATa recurrence.

Given its modest discriminative performance, the ALADS-AF score should be viewed primarily as a risk stratification and communication tool. The score highlights the incremental association of older age, left atrial enlargement, longer AF duration, and female sex with 1-year ATa recurrence risk, reinforcing the concept that AF is a progressive condition with accumulating disease burden over time.

In clinical practice, the ALADS-AF score may facilitate pre-procedural discussions, help set realistic expectations regarding rhythm outcomes, and support consistent follow-up planning in patients undergoing ablation for PsAF. Its simplicity and reliance on widely available variables enhance its feasibility across heterogeneous care settings.
