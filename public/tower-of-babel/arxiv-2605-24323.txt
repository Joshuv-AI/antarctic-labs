Arakelov inequality for families of pairs.
Junchao Shentu

Abstract
. We establish an Arakelov-type inequality for a morphism f : ( X , Δ ) → S f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S , where ( X , Δ ) (X,\Delta) is a simple normal crossing semi-log canonical pair and S S is a smooth projective variety. As a consequence, we derive a bound on the Iitaka volumes of algebraic fiber spaces whose geometric generic fiber admits a good minimal model.



1.
Introduction

The Arakelov inequality, first established by Arakelov
[
1
]
, is a fundamental inequality governing numerical invariants of families of curves and plays a crucial role in the proof of the geometric Shafarevich conjecture-namely, the finiteness of admissible families of curves.
This paper establishes a higher-dimensional Arakelov-type inequality over the complex numbers. The main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
be a morphism from a projective simple normal crossing semi-log canonical (slc) pair
(X,\Delta)
to a smooth projective variety
S
of dimension
d
, with relative dimension
n=\dim X-\dim S
. Let
D_{f}\subset S
be a proper algebraic subset such that
f
restricts to a simple normal crossing family over
S\setminus D_{f}
, and let
D\subset S
denote the codimension-one part of
D_{f}
. Denote by
R_{f}\subset D
the ramification divisor of
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
.

Let

W\subset\left(f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}(kK_{X/S}+\lfloor k\Delta\rfloor)\right)^{\otimes r}

be a coherent subsheaf of rank
l
, for integers
k,r\geq 1
. If
K_{S}+D
is pseudo-effective, then for every movable curve class
\alpha\in N_{1}(S)
and every integer
m>0
, one has

(1.1)

\displaystyle c_{1}(W)\cdot\alpha\leq\frac{klrn}{2}\,d^{mklrn-1}\,(K_{S}+D)\cdot\alpha+\frac{2}{m}\,D\cdot\alpha+r\,\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil\cdot\alpha.

Moreover, the following refined estimates hold:

•

If
\dim S=1
, then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\deg W\leq\frac{klrn}{2}\,\deg(K_{S}+D)+r\,\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil.

•

If
(S,D_{f})
is log smooth (so that
D=D_{f}
) and
K_{S}+D
is ample, then

\displaystyle c_{1}(W)\cdot(K_{S}+D)^{d-1}\leq\frac{klrn}{d}\,(K_{S}+D)^{d}+r\,\lceil kR_{f}\rceil\cdot(K_{S}+D)^{d-1}.

The assumption that
(X,\Delta)
is a simple normal crossing pair-rather than a log smooth pair-ensures the broad applicability of Theorem
1.1
to families arising at the boundary of moduli spaces of varieties (c.f.
[
11
,
12
,
14
,
4
]
).
Note that the ramification divisor
R_{f}
, as defined in Definition
2.3
, vanishes identically when the morphism
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is strictly semistable in codimension one (Definition
2.2
) .
The inequality (
1.1
) is not sharp. A refined version is established in Theorem
2.9
and Corollary
2.10
. Moreover, (
1.2
) is sharp: equality holds for certain Shimura families (see
[
28
]
,
[
22
]
).

As a consequence, we obtain the following upper bound for the Iitaka volume (defined in (
3.1
)), adapting the argument of Lu-Tan-Zuo
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism from a smooth projective variety
X
to a smooth projective curve
S
, whose geometric generic fiber
F
has dimension
n
and admits a good minimal model. Let
D\subset S
be a reduced effective divisor such that
f
restricts to a smooth family over
S\setminus D
, and let
R_{f}\subset D
denote the ramification divisor of
f
. Let
L
be a nef line bundle on
S
. If
K_{S}+D
is nef, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ivol}\big(\omega_{X/S}\otimes f^{\ast}L\big)\leq\mathrm{Ivol}(\omega_{F})\cdot\big(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1\big)\cdot\left(\frac{n}{2}\deg(K_{S}+D)+\deg L\right)+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\big(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1\big)!}{k^{\kappa(\omega_{F})+1}}\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil.

In particular, if
\kappa(\omega_{F})>1
or
R_{f}=0
(i.e. when
f
is semistable) then

\mathrm{Ivol}\big(\omega_{X/S}\otimes f^{\ast}L\big)\leq\mathrm{Ivol}(\omega_{F})\cdot\big(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1\big)\cdot\left(\frac{n}{2}\deg(K_{S}+D)+\deg L\right).

1.1.
Comparing to recent work of Kovács-Taji
[
15
]

In
[
15
]
, Kovács and Taji established a higher dimensional Arakelove inequality when the fibers are canonically polarized manifolds. Theorem
1.1
provides an effective version of Kovács-Taji’s inequality. To illustrate the difference between the techniques of the present paper and
[
15
]
, assume for simplicity that
f^{o}:X^{o}\to S^{o}
is a family of canonically polarized manifolds admitting an extension to a family
f:X\to S
of KSB-stable varieties. In
[
15
]
, the authors construct an embedding

(1.3)

\displaystyle\xi^{\ast}\lambda_{0,r}^{\otimes r}\simeq\det f_{\ast}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(rK_{X/S})\big)^{\otimes r}\hookrightarrow f^{[\mu]}_{\ast}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X^{[\mu]}}(rK_{X^{[\mu]}/S})\big),

valid for sufficiently large
\mu
, where
\mu
depends only on the some discrete invariants. Here,
f^{[\mu]}:X^{[\mu]}\to S
denotes the
\mu
-fold fiber product of
f
over
S
. The embedding gives rise to an embedding of
\det f_{\ast}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(rK_{X/S})\big)^{\otimes r}
into a variation of Hodge structures. Then the authors apply the positivity of the logarithmic cotangent bundle
\Omega_{S}(\log D)
(
[
5
]
) to derive a higher-dimensional Arakelov-type inequality.

In contrast to
[
15
]
, the central technical innovation of this paper consists in replacing the embedding (
1.3
) with one arising from the alternating sum construction:

\det f_{\ast}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(rK_{X/S})\big)^{\otimes r}\hookrightarrow\bigotimes^{lr}f_{\ast}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(rK_{X/S})\big)\simeq f^{[lr]}_{\ast}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X^{[lr]}}(rK_{X/S})\big),

where
l=\operatorname{rank}f_{\ast}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(rK_{X/S})\big)
. By the geometric characterization of the canonical extension of admissible variations of mixed Hodge structure of geometric origin (
[
25
]
), the embeding give rise to an embedding of
\det f_{\ast}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(rK_{X/S})\big)^{\otimes r}
into the canonical extension of a variation of Hodge structure on
S^{o}
. Since
lr<\mu
in general, this construction leads to a quantitatively sharper Arakelov-type inequality. Furthermore, in addition to the positivity of
\Omega_{S}(\log D)
, we exploit the parabolic semistability of the Deligne extension of the variation of Hodge structure on
S^{o}
(
[
26
]
) to establish the optimal Arakelov inequality (
1.2
).

Notation and conventions.

•

Let
F

X
. Define its dual by
F^{\vee}:=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F,\mathcal{O}_{X})

F^{\vee\vee}

2.
Arakelov Inequality

2.1.
Ramified divisor

Definition 2.1

(Simple normal crossing family)
.

Let
S
and
X
be reduced schemes of finite type over
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
, and let
D\geq 0
be a Weil
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Let
f:X\to S
be a morphism. The morphism
f:(X,D)\to S
is said to be
simple normal crossing over
S
at a point
x\in X
if there exists a Zariski open neighborhood
U
of
x
in
X
that can be embedded into a scheme
Y
, which is smooth over
S
. In this embedding,
Y
admits a regular system of parameters
(z_{1},\dots,z_{p},y_{1},\dots,y_{r})
over
S
at the point corresponding to
x=0
, such that
U
is defined by the monomial equation
z_{1}\cdots z_{p}=0
and

D|_{U}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}(y_{i}=0)|_{U},\quad\text{where }a_{i}\geq 0,

over
S
.

The morphism
f:(X,D)\to S
is said to be a
simple normal crossing family over
S
if it satisfies the simple normal crossing condition over
S
at every point of
X
.

In the special case where
S=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
, the pair
(X,D)
is referred to as a
simple normal crossing pair
if
(X,D)
forms a simple normal crossing family over
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
.
(X,D)
is called a
simple normal crossing slc pair
if the coefficients of
\Delta
lie in
[0,1]
.
X
is referred to as a
simple normal crossing scheme
if
(X,0)
is a simple normal crossing pair. In this context,
X
has Gorenstein singularities and possesses an invertible dualizing sheaf
\omega_{X}
. The canonical divisor
K_{X}
is defined up to linear equivalence via the isomorphism
\omega_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})
.

Definition 2.2

(Semistable morphism in codimension one)
.

Let
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
be a morphism from a simple normal crossing scheme
X
to a smooth variety
S
, where
\Delta\geq 0
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. The morphism
f
is called
semistable
if there exists a (not necessarily connected) smooth divisor
D_{f}\subset S
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(X,\Delta)
is a simple normal crossing pair.

(2)

f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is a simple normal crossing family over
S\setminus D_{f}
.

(3)

f^{-1}(D_{f})
and
f^{-1}(D_{f})\cup\Delta
are simple normal crossing divisors on
X
.

(4)

For every stratum
Z
of
(f^{-1}(D_{f}))_{\mathrm{red}}\cup\Delta_{\mathrm{red}}
, the restriction
f|_{Z}\colon Z\to D_{f}
is surjective onto an irreducible component of
D_{f}
.

If, in addition,
\Delta
is horizontal (i.e., contains no component of any fiber) and
f^{-1}(D_{f})
is reduced, then
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is said to be
strictly semistable
.

The morphism
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is called
semistable in codimension one
(resp.
strictly semistable in codimension one
) if there exists a dense Zariski open subset
U\subset S
with
\mathrm{codim}_{S}(S\setminus U)\geq 2
such that the base change
f|_{f^{-1}(U)}\colon\big(f^{-1}(U),\,\Delta|_{f^{-1}(U)}\big)\to U
is semistable (resp. strictly semistable).

Given a proper morphism
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
from a simple normal crossing pair
(X,\Delta)
to a smooth variety
S
, there exist closed algebraic subsets
Z_{X}\subset X
and
Z_{S}\subset S
satisfying the following conditions:

•

\mathrm{codim}_{X}(Z_{X})\geq 2
and
\mathrm{codim}_{S}(Z_{S})\geq 2
;

•

Setting
U:=X\setminus\big(f^{-1}(Z_{S})\cup Z_{X}\big)
and
V:=S\setminus Z_{S}
, the induced morphism
f|_{U}\colon\big(U,\,\Delta|_{U}\big)\to V
is semistable.

Moreover,
\Delta
admits a canonical decomposition

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta=\Delta^{h}+\Delta^{v}+\Delta^{e},

where

•

\Delta^{h}
is the
horizontal part
of
\Delta
: the sum of all components of
\Delta
that dominate
S
;

•

\Delta^{v}
is the
vertical part
of
\Delta
: the sum of all components of
\Delta
whose image in
S
has codimension one;

•

\Delta^{e}
is the
exceptional part
of
\Delta
: the sum of all components of
\Delta
whose image in
S
has codimension at least two.

Let
D_{f}\subset S
be a closed algebraic subset such that
f
restricts to a simple normal crossing family over
S\setminus D_{f}
, and let
D\subset S
denote the codimension-one part of
D_{f}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
ramification divisor
associated with
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is defined as the smallest effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
R_{f}
on
S
satisfying

(2.2)

\displaystyle f^{-1}R_{f}\geq f^{\ast}D-(f^{\ast}D)_{\mathrm{red}}+\Delta^{v}

over
U
described as above. Here,
f^{-1}R_{f}
denotes the closure in
X
of the pullback
(f|_{U})^{\ast}R_{f}
.

The ramification divisor
R_{f}
depends only on the family
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
and is independent of the choice of
D_{f}
,
U
, or
V
. When the coefficients of
\Delta^{v}
lie in
[0,1]
, one has
0\leq R_{f}\leq D
. Moreover, if
f
is strictly semistable in codimension one, then
R_{f}=0
.

By applying semi-log canonical desingularization to the total space (c.f.
[
3
]
), any family
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
can be modified so that it becomes semistable in codimension one.
Moreover, using Kawamata’s covering construction
[
9
, Theorem 17]
, one may further alter the base and total space-via a finite surjective morphism
S^{\prime}\to S
and the associated pullback family-to achieve strict semistability in codimension one.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let the notation be as in Proposition
2.4
. Suppose further that
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is semistable and
\widetilde{f}\colon(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\Delta})\to\widetilde{S}
is strictly semistable. Let
R_{f}
denote the ramification divisor associated with
f
, and let
k>0
be an integer. Then pullback of pluricanonical forms induces an injective morphism

\sigma^{\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}\big(kK_{X/S}+\lfloor k\Delta-kf^{\ast}R_{f}\rfloor\big)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{f}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\big(kK_{\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{S}}+k\widetilde{\Delta}\big).

Proof.

Denote
S^{o}:=S\setminus D_{f}
,
\widetilde{S}^{o}:=\sigma^{-1}(S^{o})
, and
D_{\widetilde{f}}:=\widetilde{S}\setminus\widetilde{S}^{o}
. Observe that the family

\widetilde{f}^{o}\colon\big(\widetilde{X}^{o}:=\widetilde{f}^{-1}(\widetilde{S}^{o}),\,\widetilde{\Delta}|_{\widetilde{X}^{o}}\big)\to\widetilde{S}^{o}

is the base change of the simple normal crossing family

f^{o}\colon\big(X^{o}:=f^{-1}(S^{o}),\,\Delta|_{X^{o}}\big)\to S^{o}.

Consequently, there is a natural injection

(2.7)

\displaystyle f^{o}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X^{o}}\big(kK_{X^{o}/S^{o}}+k\Delta|_{X^{o}}\big)\to\sigma_{\ast}\widetilde{f}^{o}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}^{o}}\big(kK_{\widetilde{X}^{o}/\widetilde{S}^{o}}+k\widetilde{\Delta}|_{\widetilde{X}^{o}}\big).

To prove the lemma, it suffices to show that (
2.7
) extends uniquely to an injective morphism

(2.8)

\displaystyle f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}\big(kK_{X/S}+\lfloor k\Delta-kf^{\ast}R_{f}\rfloor\big)\hookrightarrow\sigma_{\ast}\widetilde{f}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\big(kK_{\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{S}}+k\widetilde{\Delta}\big).

Let
E:=f^{-1}(D_{f})
and
\widetilde{E}:=\widetilde{f}^{-1}(D_{\widetilde{f}})
denote the schematic preimages, since
f
and
\widetilde{f}
are smooth over
S^{o}
and
\widetilde{S}^{o}
, respectively, and
D_{f}
,
D_{\widetilde{f}}
are snc. The pullback of relative logarithmic canonical forms yields a nonzero morphism

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}\big(K_{X/S}+E_{\mathrm{red}}-E\big)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\big(K_{\widetilde{X}/\widetilde{S}}\big).

Decompose
\Delta=\Delta^{h}+\Delta^{v}
as in (
2.1
), where
\Delta^{h}
is the horizontal part of
\Delta
and
\mathrm{supp}(\Delta^{v})\subset E
. Since
\tau
is birational and an isomorphism over
\widetilde{S}^{o}
, and
\Delta^{h}

.

), we have

and hence

. Let

W\subset\left(f_{\ast}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}\big(kK_{X/S}+\lfloor k\Delta\rfloor\big)\right)\right)^{\otimes r}

be a coherent subsheaf, for some integers
k,r\geq 1
, with
\operatorname{rank}(W)=l
. If
K_{S}+D
is pseudo-effective, then for every movable curve class
\alpha\in N_{1}(S)
and every positive integer
m
, one has

(2.24)

\displaystyle c_{1}(W)\cdot\alpha\leq\frac{1}{m(mklrn+1)}\sum_{p=1}^{mklrn}p\,d^{p-1}\,(K_{S}+D)\cdot\alpha+\frac{2}{m}\,D\cdot\alpha+r\,\lceil kR_{f}\rceil\cdot\alpha.

Moreover, the following refined estimates hold:

•

If
\dim S=1
, then

(2.25)

\displaystyle\deg W\leq\frac{klrn}{2}\,\deg(K_{S}+D)+r\,\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil.

•

If
(S,D_{f})
is log smooth (so that
D=D_{f}
) and
K_{S}+D
is ample, then

(2.26)

\displaystyle c_{1}(W)\cdot(K_{S}+D)^{d-1}\leq\frac{klrn}{d}\,(K_{S}+D)^{d}+r\,\lceil kR_{f}\rceil\cdot(K_{S}+D)^{d-1}.

Proof.

Notice that the condition of
W
implies that there is an injective morphism

W\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}(-r\lceil kR_{f}\rceil)\to\left(f_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(kK_{X/S}+\lfloor k\Delta-kf^{-1}R_{f}\rfloor))^{\otimes r}\right)^{\vee\vee}.

Applying Theorem
2.9
to
W\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}(-r\lceil kR_{f}\rceil)
we obtain the inequality (
2.24
) and (
2.26
).
When
d=\dim S=1
, by taking
m\to+\infty
in (
2.24
) we obtain (
2.25
).
∎

3.
Application: volume inequality

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
. The Iitaka volume of
L
is defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ivol}(L):=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\kappa(L)!\,h^{0}\big(X,L^{\otimes k}\big)}{k^{\kappa(L)}}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism from a smooth projective variety
X
to a smooth projective curve
S
, with geometric generic fiber
F
of dimension
n
admitting a good minimal model. Let
D\subset S
be a reduced effective divisor such that
f
restricts to a smooth family over
S\setminus D
, and let
R_{f}\subset D
denote the ramification divisor of
f
. Let
L
be a nef line bundle on
S
. If
K_{S}+D
is nef, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ivol}\big(\omega_{X/S}\otimes f^{\ast}L\big)\leq\operatorname{Ivol}(\omega_{F})\cdot\big(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1\big)\cdot\left(\frac{n}{2}\deg(K_{S}+D)+\deg L\right)+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\big(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1\big)!}{k^{\kappa(\omega_{F})+1}}\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil.

Proof.

We adapt the argument of
[
19
]
.
The statement holds trivially when
f
is isotrivial; hence, we assume
f
is non-isotrivial. Let
g
denote the genus of
S
, and let
s=\#D
. By
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, the direct image sheaf
f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})
is ample for all integers
k\gg 1
. Since the geometric generic fiber
F
admits a good minimal model, its canonical ring
\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}^{\otimes k})
is finitely generated (see also
[
8
]
). Consequently, there exists an integer
k_{0}>0
such that, for all
k\geq k_{0}
and all integers
l>1
, the multiplication map

\operatorname{Sym}^{l}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})\to f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes kl})

is surjective over a dense Zariski open subset of
S
.

It follows that, for sufficiently large
l>1
, there exists an ample line bundle
H
on
S
with
\deg H>2g-1
and a morphism

\bigoplus^{N}H\to f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes kl}),

surjective over a dense Zariski open subset of
S
, for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. This surjectivity implies the vanishing

\displaystyle H^{1}\big(S,\,f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})\otimes L^{\otimes k}\big)=0

for all
k\gg 0
.

By the Riemann-Roch theorem for vector bundles on the curve
S
, we have

\displaystyle h^{0}\big(X,\,\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k}\otimes f^{\ast}L^{\otimes k}\big)

\displaystyle=h^{0}\big(S,\,f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})\otimes L^{\otimes k}\big)

\displaystyle=\deg f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})+\operatorname{rank}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})\cdot\big(k\deg L+1-g\big).

Applying inequality (
2.25
) to the morphism
f\colon X\to S
and the subsheaf
W=f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})
yields

\displaystyle\deg f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})\leq\frac{kn\cdot\operatorname{rank}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})}{2}\,(2g-2+s)+\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil.

Substituting this bound gives

\displaystyle h^{0}\big(X,\,\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k}\otimes f^{\ast}L^{\otimes k}\big)\leq\operatorname{rank}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k})\cdot\left(\frac{kn}{2}(2g-2+s)+k\deg L+1-g\right)+\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil.

By
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
7
, Lemma 2.3.31]
, the Kodaira dimension satisfies

\displaystyle\kappa\big(\omega_{X/S}\otimes f^{\ast}L\big)=\kappa(\omega_{F})+1.

Hence, the Iitaka volume is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ivol}\big(\omega_{X/S}\otimes f^{\ast}L\big)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\kappa\big(\omega_{X/S}\otimes f^{\ast}L\big)!\cdot h^{0}\big(X,\,\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes k}\otimes f^{\ast}L^{\otimes k}\big)}{k^{\kappa(\omega_{X/S}\otimes f^{\ast}L)}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1)!}{k^{\kappa(\omega_{F})+1}}\Bigg[h^{0}(F,\,\omega_{F}^{\otimes k})\cdot\bigg(\frac{kn}{2}(2g-2+s)+k\deg L+1-g\bigg)+\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ivol}(\omega_{F})\cdot(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1)\cdot\bigg(\frac{n}{2}(2g-2+s)+\deg L\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1)!}{k^{\kappa(\omega_{F})+1}}\Big((1-g)\cdot h^{0}(F,\,\omega_{F}^{\otimes k})+\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil\Big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ivol}(\omega_{F})\cdot(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1)\cdot\bigg(\frac{n}{2}(2g-2+s)+\deg L\bigg)+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{(\kappa(\omega_{F})+1)!}{k^{\kappa(\omega_{F})+1}}\deg\lceil kR_{f}\rceil.

∎
