Aldous-type Spectral Gaps in Generalized Symmetric Groups
Niv Levhari, Doron Puder

Abstract
We prove an analog of Aldous’ spectral gap conjecture in the generalized symmetric groups G ≀ S n G\wr S_{n} where G G is an arbitrary finite group. Moreover, we show that Caputo’s extension of the conjecture to hypergraphs transfers to these groups whenever it holds in the ordinary symmetric group.

Introduction

Aldous’ spectral gap conjecture, formulated around 1992 and proved nearly two decades later by Caputo, Liggett and Richthammer
[
6
]

n\geq 2
vertices.
The first process is the interchange process on a graph
\Gamma

n
distinct
balls are placed at the vertices of
\Gamma
, one ball at every vertex. At every step of the process, one picks an edge of
\Gamma
uniformly at random, and interchanges the two balls at the endpoints of the
chosen edge. The second process is a random walk with a single ball, which is located at one of the vertices. At each step, one
picks an edge of
\Gamma
uniformly at random as before. If the ball sits at an endpoint of this edge, it is moved to the other endpoint, and otherwise it stays in its place. Although the first process has
n!
states – significantly more than the mere
n
states of the second – Aldous conjectured that both processes have identical spectral gaps. In
[
6
]

This result has an equivalent formulation in the language of the representation theory of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, where the edges of the graph correspond to transpositions and the processes to certain representations of the group. (This was noted in
[
8
]
). Such a formulation suggests the following natural question: does this phenomenon have parallels in other groups? We should stress that it is not a priori clear what the precise generalization should be. In particular, what the analogs of transpositions may be.

This paper focuses on an analog in generalized symmetric groups.

Since the measures studied in

2

Finally, as mentioned above, the setup of the current paper is inspired by the work

2.1
The wreath product
W_{n}

Above, we considered elements of
W_{n}=G\wr S_{n}=G^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
as
n\times n
monomial matrices with entries from
G\sqcup\{0\}
. It is also useful to write them as
(\textbf{g};\sigma)\in G^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
, with
\textbf{g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})\in G^{n}
and
\sigma\in S_{n}
. The corresponding matrix
M_{\textbf{g},\sigma}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(G\sqcup{\left\{{0}\right\}})
is given by

[M_{\textbf{g},\sigma}]_{r,c}=\begin{cases}g_{r}&\mathrm{if~}r=\sigma\left(c\right)\cr 0&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

The symmetric group
S_{n}
embeds into
W_{n}
, with the embedding map given by
\sigma\mapsto(e_{G^{n}};\sigma)
.
The product
G^{n}
embeds into
W_{n}
through
\textbf{g}\mapsto(\textbf{g};e_{S_{n}})
. When writing the elements as
(\textbf{g};\sigma)
, the multiplication is given by

\left(\textbf{g};\sigma\right)\cdot\left(\textbf{h};\tau\right)=\left(\textbf{g}\cdot\left(\textbf{h}\circ\sigma^{-1}\right);\sigma\tau\right),

namely,

\left(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n};\sigma\right)\cdot\left(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n};\tau\right)\ =\ \left(g_{1}\,h_{\sigma^{-1}\left(1\right)},\ldots,g_{n}\,h_{\sigma^{-1}\left(n\right)};\ \sigma\tau\right).

(2.2)

We shall use the following decomposition into factors in the wreath product
W_{n}
repeatedly:

\left(\textbf{g};\sigma\right)=\left(\textbf{g};e_{S_{n}}\right)\left(e_{G^{n}};\sigma\right)=\left(e_{G^{n}};\sigma\right)\left(\textbf{g}\circ\sigma;e_{S_{n}}\right).

(2.3)

The projection to
S_{n}
is

\pi:W_{n}\to S_{n},\qquad\pi\left(\textbf{g};\sigma\right)=\sigma,

(2.4)

and its kernel is the normal subgroup
G^{n}\trianglelefteq W_{n}
.

2.2
The Irreducible Representations of Wreath Products

We use the following basic representation-theoretic fact repeatedly: if

and

,

\displaystyle\tilde{\tau_{\mu}}\left(J_{B}^{(G)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|W_{B}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\pi^{-1}\left(\sigma\right)\cap W_{B}\end{subarray}}\tau_{\mu}\left(\sigma\right)=\frac{1}{|S_{B}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{B}}\tau_{\mu}\left(\sigma\right)=\tau_{\mu}\left(J_{B}\right).\qed

Finally, we show that the hyperedge averages
J_{B}^{(G)}
commute with
G^{n}
,

Lemma 2.9
.

For every
a\in G^{n}
and every
B\subseteq[n]
,

\left(a;e_{S_{n}}\right)\,J_{B}^{(G)}\,\left(a;e_{S_{n}}\right)^{-1}\ =\ J_{B}^{(G)}.

Consequently, for any (unitary) representation
\rho
, the operator
\rho(J_{B}^{(G)})
commutes with each element in
\left\{{\rho\left(\textbf{g}\right):\textbf{g}\in G^{n}}\right\}
.

Proof.

Conjugation by
(a;e_{S_{n}})
preserves the subgroup
W_{B}\leq W_{n}
(it does not change the set of coordinates outside
B
), hence it is a permutation on
W_{B}
, and preserves its
\mathbb{C}[W_{n}]
-average,
J_{B}^{(G)}
.
∎

3
Proofs of the Main Results

Recall that Theorem
1.6
states that for a hypergraph
\Gamma=([n],c)
with non-negative weights, we have

\lambda_{\min}^{*}\left(\Gamma,\mathrm{Reg}_{S_{n}}\right)=\lambda_{\min}^{*}\left(\Gamma,\mathrm{Reg}_{W_{n}}\right).

The proof follows from the two propositions below.
First we establish an upper bound on the smallest eigenvalue of the lift of the standard representation of
S_{n}
:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma=([n],c)
be a weighted hypergraph. Then

\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\tau_{\left(n-1,1\right)}\right)=\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\tilde{\tau}_{\left(n-1,1\right)}\right)\leq\min_{i\in[n]}\left(\sum_{B\ni i}c_{B}\right),

and unless there is an almost-isolated vertex in
\Gamma
(see Page
1.3
), the inequality is strict.

Then we show that this upper bound is a lower bound on the eigenvalues of any irreducible representation outside
\mathrm{Irr}_{0}(W_{n})
. By Corollary
2.3
, these are the irreducible representations which are not lifts of an irreducible representation of
S_{n}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma=([n],c)
be a weighted hypergraph and let
\rho:W_{n}\to GL(V)
be an irreducible representation of
W_{n}
which is not a lift of an irreducible representation of
S_{n}
. Then

\min_{i\in[n]}\left(\sum_{B\ni i}c_{B}\right)\leq\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\rho\right).

Proof of Theorem
1.6
assuming Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
.

Let
\rho
be any irreducible representation of
W_{n}
which is not a lift of an irreducible representation of
S_{n}
. Then,

\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\tau_{(n-1,1)}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Prop.~}\ref{prop:star}}}{{\leq}}\min_{i\in[n]}\left(\sum_{B\ni i}c_{B}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Prop.~}\ref{prop:gap from triv diagonal}}}{{\leq}}\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\rho\right).

Therefore, the smallest non-trivial eigenvalue of the Laplacian of
\Gamma
in the regular representation is obtained among the irreducible representations in
\mathrm{Irr}_{0}(W_{n})
:

\lambda_{\min}^{*}\left(\Gamma,\mathrm{Reg}_{W_{n}}\right)=\min_{\mathrm{triv}_{W_{n}}\neq\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}_{0}(W_{n})}\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\rho\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Cor.~}\ref{corollary:laplacians-equal-on-lifts}}}{{=}}\min_{\mathrm{triv}_{S_{n}}\neq\tau\in\mathrm{Irr}\left(S_{n}\right)}\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\tau\right)=\lambda_{\min}^{*}\left(\Gamma,\mathrm{Reg}_{S_{n}}\right),

and by Proposition
3.1
, unless there is an almost-isolated vertex, the inequality is strict - for every
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(W_{n})\setminus\mathrm{Irr}_{0}(W_{n})
,

\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\rho\right)>\lambda_{\min}^{*}\left(\Gamma,\mathrm{Reg}_{W_{n}}\right).

∎

3.1
An upper bound on the smallest eigenvalue of
\tilde{\tau}_{(n-1,1)}

In this subsection, we prove an upper bound on the smallest eigenvalue of the (
S_{n}
standard) representation
\tilde{\tau}_{(n-1,1)}
on any weighted hypergraph
\Gamma
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Using Corollary
2.8
, we have the equality
\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\tau_{(n-1,1)}\right)=\lambda_{\min}\left(\Gamma,\tilde{\tau}_{(n-1,1)}\right)
, so it suffices to consider
\tau_{(n-1,1)}
.

We work in the usual model of the standard
S_{n}
–representation defined by
(n-1,1)
,

V\ :=\ \left\{x=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=0\right\},

with
S_{n}
acting by permuting coordinates. This realizes
\tau_{(n-1,1)}
as a unitary representation with respect to the standard Hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
restricted to
V
.

Fix
i_{0}\in[n]
and set

v_{i_{0}}\ :=\ e_{i_{0}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}\ \in\ V,

where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

For each
B\subseteq[n]
, the operator
I-\tau_{(n-1,1)}(J_{B})\in\mathrm{End}(V)
is the orthogonal projection onto
(V^{S_{B}})^{\perp}
(see Claim
2.6
), hence

0\ \leq\ \left\langle\left(I-\tau_{\left(n-1,1\right)}\left(J_{B}\right)\right)v,v\right\rangle\ \leq\ \langle v,v\rangle\qquad\forall v\in V,

(3.1)

where equality on the right occurs if and only if
v\in(V^{S_{B}})^{\perp}
.
If
i_{0}\notin B
, then
v_{i_{0}}
is constant on the coordinates in
B
, so it is fixed by
S_{B}
, namely,
v_{i_{0}}\in V^{S_{B}}
. Hence,

\left(I-\tau_{\left(n-1,1\right)}\left(J_{B}\right)\right)v_{i_{0}}=0\qquad\text{whenever }i_{0}\notin B.

(3.2)

Note that
V^{S_{B}}
is the subspace where the
B
-coordinates are all equal, that is,

V^{S_{B}}=\left\{{v\in V:\exists c~~\mathrm{s.t.}~\forall k\in B,~\langle v,e_{k}\rangle=c}\right\}.

Consequently,

\left(V^{S_{B}}\right)^{\perp}=\left\{{v\in V:\sum_{k\in B}\langle v,e_{k}\rangle=0~~\mathrm{and}~~\forall l\notin B,~\langle v,e_{l}\rangle=0}\right\}.

Thus,
v_{i_{0}}\notin(V^{S_{B}})^{\perp}
whenever
i_{0}\in B\neq[n]
, and therefore in those cases,

\langle\left(I-\tau_{\left(n-1,1\right)}\left(J_{B}\right)\right)v_{i_{0}},v_{i_{0}}\rangle<\langle v_{i_{0}},v_{i_{0}}\rangle

(3.3)

for
i_{0}\in B
. So

\displaystyle\left\langle\tau_{\left(n-1,1\right)}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}\right)v_{i_{0}},\ v_{i_{0}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\subseteq[n]}c_{B}\left\langle(I-\tau_{(n-1,1)}(J_{B}))v_{i_{0}},\ v_{i_{0}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\ni i_{0}}c_{B}\left\langle(I-\tau_{(n-1,1)}(J_{B}))v_{i_{0}},\ v_{i_{0}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{B\ni i_{0}}c_{B}\,\langle v_{i_{0}},v_{i_{0}}\rangle,

where the second equality is due to (
3.2
) and the last inequality uses (
3.1
). The last inequality is strict whenever there is a non-zero weight for some
B\subsetneq[n]
containing
i_{0}
by (
3.3
).
Dividing by
\langle v_{i_{0}},v_{i_{0}}\rangle>0
gives a Rayleigh quotient bound

\frac{\left\langle\tau_{(n-1,1)}(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma})v_{i_{0}},\ v_{i_{0}}\right\rangle}{\langle v_{i_{0}},v_{i_{0}}\rangle}\ \leq\ \sum_{B\ni i_{0}}c_{B}.

Since
\tau_{(n-1,1)}(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma})
is Hermitian and positive semidefinite, its minimal eigenvalue is the minimum of the Rayleigh quotients over non-zero vectors in
V
, hence

\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\Gamma,\tau_{(n-1,1)}\right)\ \leq\ \sum_{B\ni i_{0}}c_{B}.

Taking
i_{0}
that attains the minimum value, and assuming that it is not almost-isolated, we get a strict inequality

\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\Gamma,\tau_{(n-1,1)}\right)\ <\ \sum_{B\ni i_{0}}c_{B}=\min_{i}\sum_{B\ni i}c_{B}.\qed

3.2
A lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(W_{n})\setminus\text{Irr}_{0}(W_{n})

We now prove Proposition
3.2
.
Let
\rho:W_{n}\to GL(V)
be a
W_{n}
-representation.
As in Lemma
2.4
, consider its restriction to
G^{n}
, and let the isotypic decomposition of
\text{Res}_{G^{n}}^{W_{n}}\rho
to subspaces be

V=\bigoplus_{\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}\left(G^{n}\right)}V_{\theta}.

Since
\rho
is finite-dimensional (hence unitary), this is an orthogonal direct sum.

For
\theta=\theta_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\theta_{n}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{n})
define

\mathrm{supp}\left(\theta\right)\ :=\ \{i\in[n]:\theta_{i}\not\simeq\mathrm{triv}_{G}\}.

We view
\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
as a subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[W_{n}]
via the embedding

S_{n}\hookrightarrow W_{n}
,
\sigma\mapsto(e_{G^{n}};\sigma)
.
In particular, for
B\subseteq[n]
, the element
J_{B}\in\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
from (
1.2
)
is identified with the same average in
\mathbb{C}[W_{n}]
:

J_{B}\ =\ \frac{1}{|S_{B}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{B}}\left(e_{G^{n}};\sigma\right)\ \in\ \mathbb{C}[W_{n}].

We start by showing that
\mathrm{supp}(\theta)
determines how
J_{B}^{(G)}
acts on
V_{\theta}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{n})
and
B\subseteq[n]
,

\rho\left(J_{B}^{(G)}\right)\Big|_{V_{\theta}}\ =\ \begin{cases}\rho\left(J_{B}\right)\big|_{V_{\theta}}&\text{if }B\cap\mathrm{supp}\left(\theta\right)=\emptyset,\\[2.0pt]
0&\text{if }B\cap\mathrm{supp}\left(\theta\right)\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

Proof.

Fix
B\subseteq[n]
and recall that
W_{B}=G^{B}\rtimes S_{B}
, so
|W_{B}|=|G|^{|B|}\,|S_{B}|
. Using
(
2.3
), for
g\in G^{B}
and
\sigma\in S_{B}
,

\left(g;\sigma\right)=\left(e_{G^{n}};\sigma\right)\,\left(g\circ\sigma;e_{S_{n}}\right),

hence

J_{B}^{(G)}=\frac{1}{|W_{B}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{B}}\sum_{g\in G^{B}}\left(e_{G^{n}};\sigma\right)\,\left(g\circ\sigma;e_{S_{n}}\right)=\frac{1}{|S_{B}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{B}}\left(e_{G^{n}};\sigma)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{|G|^{|B|}}\sum_{g\in G^{B}}(g;e_{S_{n}}\right)\right),

where we used the fact that
g\mapsto g\circ\sigma
is a bijection of
G^{B}
for every fixed
\sigma\in S_{B}
.

On the
G^{n}
–isotypic component
V_{\theta}
, the restriction of
\rho
to
G^{n}
is a direct sum of copies of

\theta=\theta_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\theta_{n}
, so the operator

\frac{1}{|G|^{|B|}}\sum_{g\in G^{B}}\rho\left(g;e_{S_{n}}\right)
acts as
\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\theta_{i}(h)
on coordinates
i\in B
and as the identity on coordinates outside of
B
.
By Schur’s lemma,

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\theta_{i}(h)
is the identity if
\theta_{i}\simeq\mathrm{triv}_{G}
, and zero otherwise.
Therefore, if
B\cap\mathrm{supp}(\theta)=\emptyset
, the operator corresponding to this
G^{B}
–average is the identity on
V_{\theta}
and

\rho\left(J_{B}^{(G)}\right)\Big|_{V_{\theta}}=\frac{1}{|S_{B}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{B}}\left(\rho\left(e_{G^{n}};\sigma\right)\cdot I\right)\big|_{V_{\theta}}=\rho(J_{B})\big|_{V_{\theta}}.

On the other hand, if
B\cap\mathrm{supp}(\theta)\neq\emptyset
, there is a tensor coordinate that maps to
0
and the
G^{B}
–average vanishes. Hence
\rho(J_{B}^{(G)})\big|_{V_{\theta}}=0
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\rho:W_{n}\to GL(V)
be an irreducible
W_{n}
-representation and write the
G^{n}
–isotypic decomposition
V=\bigoplus_{\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{n})}V_{\theta}
.
Then for any hypergraph
\Gamma
, each
V_{\theta}
is
\rho(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}^{(G)})
–invariant and

\operatorname{spec}\!\left(\rho\left(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}^{(G)}\right)\right)\ =\ \bigsqcup_{\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}\left(G^{n}\right)}\operatorname{spec}\!\left(\rho\left(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}^{(G)}\right)\Big|_{V_{\theta}}\right)

(with multiplicity), where
\operatorname{spec}\left(T\right)
denotes the complex spectrum of an operator
T
.

Proof.

Assume that
\vec{\mu}
is the multi-partition such that
\rho=\rho_{\vec{\mu}}
.
It suffices to show that for every
B\subseteq[n]
and every
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{n})
,
\rho(J_{B}^{(G)})
preserves
V_{\theta}
. Indeed, by (
1.7
), this would imply that
\rho(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}^{(G)})
preserves
V_{\theta}
as well, and since the decomposition into
G^{n}
–isotypic components is orthogonal, the spectrum of
\rho(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}^{(G)})
is the disjoint union of the spectra of its restrictions to the blocks
V_{\theta}
.

Fix
B\subseteq[n]
and
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{n})
.
Taking
P_{\theta}=\frac{\dim\theta}{|G^{n}|}\sum_{g\in G^{n}}\chi_{\theta}(g^{-1})g\in\mathbb{C}[W_{n}]
– the element projecting onto the
\theta
-isotypic part of every
G^{n}
-representation – we have that
\rho\left(P_{\theta}\right)V=V_{\theta}
and by Lemma
2.9
,
J_{B}^{(G)}P_{\theta}=P_{\theta}J_{B}^{(G)}
. Hence

\rho\left(J_{B}^{(G)}\right)V_{\theta}=\rho\left(J_{B}^{(G)}\right)\rho\left(P_{\theta}\right)V=\rho\left(P_{\theta}\right)\rho\left(J_{B}^{(G)}\right)V\subseteq V_{\theta}.

∎

Finally, we prove the main proposition.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Let
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(W_{n})\setminus\text{Irr}_{0}(W_{n})
, let
\vec{\mu}
be the multi-partition corresponding to
\rho
, and let
V=\bigoplus_{\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{n})}V_{\theta}
be the decomposition as above. By Corollary
2.5
, as
\rho
is not a lift of an irreducible representation of
S_{n}
, its
G^{n}
-trivial part is
{0}
. Namely, for every
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{n})
with
V_{\theta}\neq 0
,
\mathrm{supp}(\theta)\neq\emptyset
. By Corollary
3.4
, it suffices to show that

\lambda_{\min}\left(\rho\left(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}^{(G)}\right)\Big|_{V_{\theta}}\right)\geq\min_{i\in[n]}\sum_{B\ni i}c_{B}

for every
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{n})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\theta)\neq\emptyset
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\rho\left(\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}^{(G)}\right)\Big|_{V_{\theta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{B\subseteq[n]}c_{B}\left(I-\rho\left(J_{B}^{(G)}\right)\Big|_{V_{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Lemma~\ref{lem:J-on-theta}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{B\cap\mathrm{supp}\left(\theta\right)=\emptyset}c_{B}\cdot\left(I-\rho\left(J_{B}\right)\mid_{V_{\theta}}\right)+\sum_{B\cap\mathrm{supp}\left(\theta\right)\neq\emptyset}c_{B}\cdot I

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Claim~\ref{claim: JB is an orthogonal projection}}}{{\succeq}}

\displaystyle\sum_{B\cap\mathrm{supp}\left(\theta\right)\neq\emptyset}c_{B}\cdot I.

We are done as for every
i_{0}\in\mathrm{supp}(\theta)\neq\emptyset
,

\sum_{B\cap\mathrm{supp}\left(\theta\right)\neq\emptyset}c_{B}\geq\sum_{B\ni i_{0}}c_{B}\geq\min_{i\in[n]}\sum_{B\ni i}c_{B}.\qed
