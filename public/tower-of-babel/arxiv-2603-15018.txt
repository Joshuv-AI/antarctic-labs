Scalable Self-Testing of Mutually Anticommuting Observables and Maximally Entangled Two-Qudits
Souradeep Sasmal, Ritesh K. Singh, Prabuddha Roy, A. K. Pan

Abstract
The next frontier in device-independent quantum information lies in the certification of scalable and parallel quantum resources, which underpin advanced quantum technologies. We put forth a simultaneous self-testing framework for maximally entangled two-qudit state of local dimension m ∗ = 2 ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ m_{*}=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor} (equivalently ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ \lfloor n/2\rfloor copies of maximally entangled two-qubit pairs), together with n n numbers of anti-commuting observables on one side. To this end, we employ an n n -settings Bell inequality comprising two space-like separated observers, Alice and Bob, having 2 n − 1 2^{n-1} and n n number of measurement settings, respectively. We derive the local ontic bound of this inequality and, crucially, employ the Sum-of-Squares decomposition to determine the optimal quantum bound without presupposing the dimension of the state or observables. We then establish that any physical realisation achieving the maximal quantum violation must, up to local isometries and complex conjugation, correspond to a reference strategy consisting of a maximally entangled state of local dimension of at least 2 ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ 2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor} and local observables forming an irreducible representation of the Clifford algebra. This construction thereby demonstrates that the minimal dimension compatible with n n mutually anticommuting observables is naturally self-tested by the maximal violation of the proposed Bell functional. Finally, we analyse the robustness of the protocol by establishing quantitative bounds relating deviations in the observed Bell value to the fidelity between the realised and the ideal strategies. Our results thus provide a scalable, dimension-independent route for the certification of high-dimensional entanglement and Clifford measurements in a fully device-independent framework.

Preliminaries

To set the stage for our results, we briefly outline the Bell scenario, the relevant sets of correlations, and the notion of self-testing.

II.1
Bell scenario: Local, Quantum and Nosignalling correlations

A Bell scenario involves two spatially separated and non-communicating parties, Alice and Bob, who share a common preparation
P\in\mathcal{P}
. In each experimental run, Alice performs one of
m_{A}
measurements
A_{x}
with input

. Similarly, Bob performs one of
m_{B}
measurements

with

\vec{P}:=\quantity{p\qty(a,b|x,y,P)}_{a,b,x,y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{A}m_{B}O_{A}O_{B}}

(1)

where

and
\sum_{a,b}p\quantity(a,b|x,y,P)=1
denotes the joint conditional probability of obtaining the observed outcome-pair

In quantum theory, the preparation corresponds to a shared state
\rho\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}^{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B})
, and the probabilities are governed by the Born rule,

p(a,b|x,y,\rho)=\Tr[\rho A_{a|x}\otimes B_{b|y}]

(2)

where
A_{a|x},B_{b|y}\geq 0
and
\sum_{a}A_{a|x}=\sum_{b}B_{b|y}=\openone
are the POVM elements corresponding to the outcomes

The set of all quantum behaviours forms a convex set
\mathcal{Q}
, known as the quantum set. By Neumark’s theorem

\mathcal{Q}
can be obtained through projective measurements on a pure bipartite state, possibly in a higher-dimensional Hilbert space.

In the ontological model of quantum theory, a specific preparation of
\rho
essentially prepares the system into an underlying ontic state
\lambda\in\Lambda
with a probability distribution
\mu(\lambda):\mu(\lambda)\geq 0;\ \sum_{\lambda}\mu(\lambda)=1
, where
\Lambda
denotes the ontic state space. The ontic state
\lambda
determines the outcome of a measurement of each party, which is independent of the choice of measurement settings and outcomes of the other party (locality). For a local ontic model
6
, the conditional joint probability in the ontic state space factorises as follows:

p(a,b|x,y,\lambda)=p(a|x,\lambda)\ p(b|y,\lambda)\ \ \forall\ a,b,x,y,\lambda

(3)

Now, in order to reproduce the prediction of quantum theory from the ontic model, the following reproducibility condition need to be satisfied

p(a,b|x,y,\rho)=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mu(\lambda)p(a|x,\lambda)p(b|y,\lambda)

The set of all such local correlations constitutes a polytope, denoted by

. It is known
18
;
48
;
28
;
5
that the local set

\mathcal{NS}
is the nosignalling polytope containing all behaviours consistent with relativistic causality

. Note that unlike

and

is not a polytope.

II.2
Bell functionals and inequalities

A Bell functional is a linear map
\mathscr{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{A}m_{B}O_{A}O_{B}}
acting on a behaviour

as

G(\vec{P})=\expectationvalue{\mathscr{G},\vec{P}}:=\sum_{a,b,x,y}\mathscr{G}_{a,b,x,y}p(a,b|x,y)

(5)

where
\mathscr{G}_{a,b,x,y}\in\mathbb{R}

(G)^{opt}_{\mathcal{S}}=\max\limits_{\vec{P}\in\mathcal{S}}G(\vec{P})

A Bell inequality is then expressed as

G(\vec{P})\leq(G)^{opt}_{\mathcal{L}}

(7)

and a behaviour is said to be nonlocal if it violates this bound, i.e.

commute with
\mathcal{D}
and are therefore block-diagonal in the same Schmidt decomposition

\forall y,\ \mathcal{A}_{y}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)},\ \ \ \text{and }B_{y}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\alpha}B_{y}^{(k)},\ \ \ \mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)},B_{y}^{(k)}\in\mathscr{L}\quantity(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}).

(34)

Within each block, the relation

\mathcal{A}_{y}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}B_{y}^{T}\implies\mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)}=\quantity(B_y^{(k)})^{T},\ \forall y,k,

(35)

establishing that Alice’s and Bob’s local observables coincide on each degenerate Schmidt subspace and satisfy identical algebraic relations.

If all Schmidt coefficients are distinct (
\alpha=r
,
m_{k}=1
), the observables are diagonal and commute, precluding Bell inequality violation. Conversely, if all coefficients are equal (
D=\frac{1}{r}\openone
), the shared state is maximally entangled, and the optimality conditions enforce that the observables form a family of pairwise anticommuting Hermitian involutions satisfying Eq. (
19
). These operators thus generate a representation of the complex Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{n}\quantity(\mathbb{C})
, whose minimal complex representation has local dimension
m_{*}=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
. Accordingly, each degenerate block must have dimension at least
m_{k}\geq m_{*}
.

Combining these results, any optimal quantum realisation saturating
\quantity(G_n)^{opt}_{Q}=2^{n-1}\sqrt{n}
must take the form of Eqs. (
16
-
19
). Each block realises an irreducible complex Clifford algebra representation, unique up to local unitaries, while any non-trivial degeneracy corresponds only to classical mixing between identical blocks.
∎

Remark 1
.

Note that the transpose appearing in the relation
\mathcal{A}_{y}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}B_{y}^{T}
in Eq. (
35
) arises solely from the adopted vectorisation convention. In the Schmidt basis, where the maximally entangled state is real and symmetric, transposition acts trivially, i.e.,
\quantity(\mathcal{A}_y \otimes B^T_y)\ket{\phi^{+}}=\quantity(B_y \otimes\mathcal{A}_y^T)\ket{\phi^{+}}
. Thus, the two quantum realisations
A_{x},B_{y}^{T},\quantity{A_x,B_y^T,\ket{\psi}}
and its transposed (or complex-conjugated) counterpart
A_{x}^{T},B_{y},\quantity{A_x^T,B_y,\ket{\psi}}
attains the same maximal Bell violation and are therefore physically indistinguishable with respect to all measurable Bell correlations, even though they are not, in general, related by a local unitary transformation. To distinguish between such conjugate strategies, one must impose additional constraints on the observed statistics beyond standard Bell correlations, as discussed in Ref.
3
in the context of the self-testing properties of Gisin’s elegant Bell inequality
17
.

These considerations underpin the self-testing analysis developed in Sec.
V
, where we show that attaining the maximal quantum violation uniquely identifies, up to local unitaries and complex conjugation, the underlying state and observables realising the canonical Clifford-algebraic structure.

V

Results: Device-Independent Characterisation of the Optimal Quantum Strategy - Self-Testing from the Maximal Violation of
\mathscr{G}_{n}

Having established the optimal quantum bound and characterised all realisations that saturate it, we now turn to the self-testing properties of the Bell functional
\mathscr{G}_{n}
.

Self-testing provides a DI certification of both the shared quantum state and the local observables solely from the observed violation, without assuming any knowledge of the internal workings or the underlying Hilbert space dimension of the quantum system. Consider an arbitrary physical realisation

A_{x}\in\mathcal{L}\quantity(\mathcal{H}_{A}),B_{y}\in\mathcal{L}\quantity(\mathcal{H}_B),\quantity{A_{x}\in\mathcal{L}\qty(\mathcal{H}_{A}), B_{y}\in\mathcal{L}\qty(\mathcal{H}_B), \ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B},

(36)

which achieves the optimal quantum value of the Bell functional
\mathscr{G}_{n}
in the ideal, noiseless scenario. The aim of self-testing is to show that any such physical realisation, reproducing the same correlations as the optimal quantum strategy must, up to local isometries and irrelevant degrees of freedom, be equivalent to a fixed reference experiment of fixed local dimension (up to global complex conjugation), denoted by

A^{\prime}_{x}\in\mathcal{L}\quantity(\mathcal{H}_{A'}),B^{\prime}_{y}\in\mathcal{L}\quantity(\mathcal{H}_B'),\quantity{A'_{x}\in\mathcal{L}\qty(\mathcal{H}_{A'}), B'_{y}\in\mathcal{L}\qty(\mathcal{H}_B'), \ket{\psi'}\in\mathcal{H}_{A'}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B'}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime}},

(37)

The operator relations derived for the optimal violation, particularly the anticommutation conditions and the algebraic structure of the local measurements, enable one to reconstruct the exact form of the target quantum strategy up to local isometries and complex conjugation.

The following analysis formalises this claim and establishes explicit self-testing relations for the optimal quantum strategy associated with
\mathscr{G}_{n}
.

Theorem 3

(Blockwise self-testing of the Clifford realisation)

The block-wise theorem guarantees the existence of local isometries acting within each Schmidt subspace that map the corresponding physical operators and states to their canonical Clifford-algebraic form. To extend this result to the entire Hilbert space, these block-wise isometries must be coherently combined into global maps acting on the full systems of Alice and Bob. The following lemma formalises this construction and shows that the direct sum of orthogonally defined isometries remains an isometry on the total space.

Lemma 1

(Direct-sum composition of local isometries)
.

Let
\quantity{\Phi_A^{(k)}:\mathcal{H}_A^{(k)}\to\mathcal{H}_{A'}^{(k)} \otimes\mathcal{H}_{J_A^{(k)}}}_{k=1}^{\alpha}
and
\quantity{\Phi_B^{(k)}:\mathcal{H}_B^{(k)}\to\mathcal{H}_{B'}^{(k)} \otimes\mathcal{H}_{J_B^{(k)}}}_{k=1}^{\alpha}
be families of local isometries defined on mutually orthogonal subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(k)}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{B}^{(k)}
. Then the maps

\Phi_{A}:=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha}\Phi_{A}^{(k)}:\mathcal{H}_{A}\to\quantity(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha} \mathcal{H}_{A'}^{(k)})\otimes\quantity(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha} \mathcal{H}_{J_A^{(k)}})

(67)

and similarly

\Phi_{B}:=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha}\Phi_{B}^{(k)}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\to\quantity(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha} \mathcal{H}_{B'}^{(k)})\otimes\quantity(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha} \mathcal{H}_{J_B^{(k)}})

(68)

are themselves isometries satisfying
\Phi_{A}^{\dagger}\Phi_{A}=\openone_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}
and
\Phi_{B}^{\dagger}\Phi_{B}=\openone_{\mathcal{H}_{B}}
.

Proof.

We prove the claim for Alice’s side; the argument for Bob’s side is identical. Since the full local Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}
splits into orthogonal subspaces (the Schmidt blocks),
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(k)}
, every vector
\ket{\psi_{A}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
admits a unique orthogonal decomposition

\ket{\psi_{A}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}\ket{\psi_{A}^{(k)}},\ \ \text{where }\ket{\psi_{A}^{(k)}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(k)}.

(69)

Because the decomposition is orthogonal, we have
\innerproduct{\psi_A^{(k)}}{\psi_A^{(k')}}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
. Now for two arbitrary vectors
\ket{\chi_{A}},\ket{\psi_{A}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
, their inner product in
\mathcal{H}_{A}
is given by
\innerproduct{\psi_A}{\chi_A}=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\innerproduct{\psi^{(k)}_A}{\chi^{(k')}_A}
. But because the subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(k)}
are orthogonal,
\innerproduct{\psi_A}{\chi_A}
simplifies to

\innerproduct{\psi_A}{\chi_A}=\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}\innerproduct{\psi^{(k)}_A}{\chi^{(k)}_A}.

(70)

Now apply the composed map
\Phi_{A}=\bigoplus_{k}\Phi_{A}^{(k)}
. Each
\Phi_{A}^{(k)}
acts independently on its own block:

\Phi_{A}\ket{\psi_{A}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}\Phi_{A}^{(k)}\ket{\psi_{A}^{(k)}}.

(71)

Computing the inner product of the images, we get

\innerproduct{\chi_A (\Phi_A)^{\dagger}}{\Phi_A \psi_A}=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\innerproduct{\chi_A^{(k)} (\Phi_A^{(k)})^{\dagger}}{\Phi_A^{(k')} \psi^{(k')}_A}

(72)

But again, the images of distinct
k
’s lie in orthogonal subspaces of the target, so all cross-terms vanish, i.e.,
\innerproduct{\chi_A^{(k)} (\Phi_A^{(k)})^{\dagger}}{\Phi_A^{(k')} \psi^{(k')}_A}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\chi_A (\Phi_A)^{\dagger}}{\Phi_A \psi_A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\innerproduct{\chi_A^{(k)} (\Phi_A^{(k)})^{\dagger}}{\Phi_A^{(k)} \psi^{(k)}_A}

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\innerproduct{\chi_A^{(k)}}{\psi^{(k)}_A}\ \ \text{[Since $(\Phi_{A}^{(k)})^{\dagger}\Phi_{A}^{(k)}=\openone$]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\chi_A }{\psi_A}

Hence, it follows that
\quantity(\Phi_A)^{\dagger}\Phi_{A}=\openone
. This shows that the global map
\Phi_{A}
preserves the inner products on all of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
, i.e.,
\Phi_{A}
is an isometry. The same argument applies identically to
\Phi_{B}
.

∎

Having demonstrated that the local isometries associated with each Schmidt block can be coherently assembled into a single global construction, we now proceed to establish the full self-testing statement for the entire quantum realisation.

Corollary 1

(Global self-testing from the maximal violation of
\mathscr{G}_{n}
)

VI

Robust self-testing

In the preceding Sec.
V
, we have established the existence of local isometries,
\Phi_{A}
and
\Phi_{B}

A^{\prime}_{x},B^{\prime}_{y},\quantity{A'_x,B'_y,\ket{\psi'}}
from the ideal one
A_{x},B_{y},\quantity{A_x,B_y,\ket{\psi}}
. In the absence of noise, the optimal quantum violation of the Bell functional,
\expectationvalue{\mathscr{G}_n}^{opt}=2^{n-1}\sqrt{n}
is achieved for this ideal strategy.

In practice, however, the observed violation is typically slightly below the optimal value,
\expectationvalue{\mathscr{G}_n}^{obs}=2^{n-1}\sqrt{n}-\delta
for some small
\delta>0
. This deviation reflects that the physical strategy, of unknown dimension, denoted by
\tilde{A}_{x},\tilde{B_{y}},\quantity{\tilde{A}_x,\tilde{B_y},\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}

M_{x}\ket{\psi}=0
. Instead, the corresponding relations are violated by a controlled amount, with
\tilde{M}\norm{\tilde{M}\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}
bounded by a function of
\delta
. Without loss of generality, the physical state can be considered to be pure and the observables Hermitian and dichotomic (i.e., of eigenvalues
\pm 1
), since no assumption is made on the Hilbert space dimension, which can always be extended to purify the state and make the measurements projective.

The local isometry then maps these physical objects
\tilde{A}_{x},\tilde{B_{y}},\quantity{\tilde{A}_x,\tilde{B_y},\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}
to a target reference strategy of known dimension,
\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{x},\tilde{B}^{\prime}_{y},\quantity{\tilde{A}'_x,\tilde{B}'_y,\ket{\tilde{\psi'}}}
. In the ideal (noise-free) case, one would expect
A^{\prime}_{x}=\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{x},B^{\prime}_{y}=\tilde{B}^{\prime}_{y},\quantity{A'_x=\tilde{A}'_x,B'_y=\tilde{B}'_y,\ket{\psi'}=\ket{\tilde{\psi'}}}=\ket{\tilde{\psi^{\prime}}}
, corresponding to
\delta=0
(up to complex conjugation).

However, since
\delta>0
in reality, there exists a finite deviation between the mapped physical observables and states and their ideal target counterparts. In the following, we quantify these deviations by deriving the operator norm bounds, which characterise how close the extracted strategy
\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{x},\tilde{B}^{\prime}_{y},\quantity{\tilde{A}'_x,\tilde{B}'_y,\ket{\tilde{\psi'}}}
is to the corresponding target reference quantities
A^{\prime}_{x},B^{\prime}_{y},\quantity{A'_x,B'_y,\ket{\psi'}}
as a function of the observed discrepancy
\delta
.

Specifically, we evaluate the following norm bounds

\openone\otimes\quantity(B'_y-\tilde{B}'_y)\norm{\openone\otimes\qty(B'_y-\tilde{B}'_y)\ket{\tilde{\psi}'}},\ \ \quantity(A'_x-\tilde{A}'_x)\otimes\openone\norm{\qty(A'_x-\tilde{A}'_x)\otimes\openone\ket{\tilde{\psi}'}},\ \ \norm{\ket{\tilde{\psi}'}-\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}-\ket{\tilde{\psi}}

(77)

Since every dichotomic POVM can be dilated to a projective observable on a larger space, without loss of generality we assume that all physical observables are Hermitian with spectrum contained in
\quantity{\pm 1}

VII

Summary and Discussion

In this work, we propose a scalable self-testing framework for the maximally entangled two-qudit state of local dimension
m_{*}=2^{\lceil\frac{1}{2}(n-1)\rceil}
(equivalently
\lceil\frac{1}{2}(n-1)\rceil
copies of maximally entangled two-qubit states
26
), together with
n
mutually anticommuting observables on one side. The framework is based on an
n
-settings, two-outcome Bell inequality. We first derived analytically the local ontic bound of this inequality for arbitrary
n
. We then determine the optimal quantum bound of the Bell functional
\mathscr{G}_{n}
by employing the SOS decomposition technique
4
, without assuming any restriction on the underlying Hilbert space dimension. The resulting quantum bound exceeds the classical one for all
n
, and its analytical form enables a complete characterisation of the quantum strategies saturating it.

The maximal quantum value is achieved by a state that decomposes into a direct sum of maximally entangled components, each supported on local subspaces of dimension at least
m_{*}
. On every such Schmidt block, both parties implement identical families of dichotomic, pairwise anticommuting Hermitian observables forming a representation of the complex Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{n}\quantity(\mathbb{C})
. Consequently, the full measurement operators are block-diagonal, with each block acting irreducibly on the corresponding Schmidt subspace and carrying an independent Clifford representation. Any nontrivial degeneracy of the Schmidt spectrum therefore corresponds to classical mixing between identical Clifford blocks.

Within each block, Alice’s
2^{n-1}
observables correspond geometrically to the vertices of an
n
-dimensional hypercube inscribed in the operator Bloch sphere defined by the Clifford generators,
\mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)}
, cf. by Eq. (
147
). Each observable direction can be represented by the normalised vector
\vec{v}_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\quantity((-1)^{z^x_1},\ldots,(-1)^{z^x_n})
, encoding the pattern of signs defining its relation to the
n
anticommuting axes. Bob’s
n
measurements correspond to the orthogonal Clifford directions themselves, the coordinate axes of this hypercube, while Alice’s observables form its vertices. The hypercube symmetry ensures that the averaged correlations are invariant under any parity inversion of the coefficients, thereby realising the parity-oblivious constraints relations known from the
n\to 1
parity-oblivious multiplexing task in a prepare-measure scenario
61
;
16
;
59
.

A further subtle feature of
\mathscr{G}_{n}
arises from the fact that the optimal quantum strategy is not unique under local unitaries. In particular, if the strategy
\mathcal{A}_{y}=\Gamma_{y}^{T},B_{y}=\Gamma_{y},\quantity{\mathcal{A}_y=\Gamma_y^T,B_y=\Gamma_y,\ket{\phi^+}}
attains the maximal quantum value, then its complex-conjugated counterpart
\mathcal{A}^{T}_{y}=\Gamma_{y},B_{y}^{T}=\Gamma_{y}^{T},\quantity{\mathcal{A}^T_y=\Gamma_y,B_y^T=\Gamma_y^T,\ket{\phi^+}}
does as well. This equivalence occurs because the transposition (or complex conjugation) operation maps a given irreducible representation of
\mathrm{Cl}_{n}\quantity(\mathbb{C})
to another inequivalent but correlation-preserving one, for all Hermitian combinations
\Gamma_{y}^{T}\otimes\Gamma_{y}
, the corresponding joint expectation values remain identical. Consequently, any two-outcome Bell functional composed purely of joint correlators with real coefficients, such as
\mathscr{G}_{n}
, cannot distinguish between a strategy and its complex-conjugated counterpart. Physically, this means that the Bell violation is invariant under local complex conjugation, and that any self-testing statement based on the optimal violation of such two-outcome Bell inequalities must therefore be understood up to both local isometries and local complex conjugation. This generalises the ambiguity previously identified for the Elegant Bell inequality
17
;
3
(recovered as the special case
n=3
), showing that it is not an isolated feature but a general algebraic consequence of Bell functionals whose optimal observables realise complex Clifford-algebra representations.

Within this extended equivalence framework, we have proved the existence of local isometries that extract, from any optimal physical realisation, the reference configuration composed of maximally entangled states of local dimension
m_{*}
and
n
mutually anticommuting measurement operators, up to local unitary and complex-conjugation freedoms. This formally establishes that the maximal quantum value of
\mathscr{G}_{n}
self-tests a tensor product of maximally entangled qubit pairs (of total local dimension
m_{*}=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
) together with the associated Clifford measurement structure.

We have also analysed the robustness of this self-testing scheme by examining deviations from the SOS kernel. In the ideal case, the optimal state lies entirely within the kernel subspace of the SOS operators. When the observed value deviates from the quantum maximum by a small parameter
\delta
, the physical state necessarily acquires a small component orthogonal to this kernel. Operationally, this means that the experimentally realised correlations include a residual contribution orthogonal to the ideal algebraic constraints defining the optimal quantum strategy. The distance of the physical state from this kernel subspace thus provides a direct quantitative measure of how far the observed statistics are from the ideal self-tested configuration. By bounding this orthogonal component, we obtained explicit norm inequalities quantifying how close the extracted physical strategy is to the ideal reference strategy. In particular, we show that the physical state and observables remain close (in operator norm) to their ideal counterparts within an error of order
\order{n^{1/4}}\sqrt{\delta}
. Thus, even as the number of measurement settings increases, the degradation in fidelity grows only polynomially with
n
, demonstrating that the protocol remains stable in the large-setting regime. In addition, we established that the anticommutation relations of Bob’s observables remain approximately preserved as
\order{a/2^n n}\sqrt{\delta}
, confirming that the Clifford structure is robust under small perturbations. Although these robustness bounds are derived analytically under certain approximations, they can be systematically tightened using the techniques of
23
, enabling quantitative benchmarking of different scalable self-testing schemes.

Beyond their theoretical relevance, our results have direct implications for device-independent quantum information processing. Because the inequality
\expectationvalue{\mathscr{G}_n}\leq(G_{n})^{opt}_{\mathcal{L}}
self-tests multiple copies of maximally entangled pairs using only two-outcome measurements, it naturally lends itself to applications in DI randomness expansion and quantum key distribution. The possibility of certifying an unbounded amount of randomness through a scalable number of measurement settings paves the way towards asymptotically high performance DI quantum protocols.

Appendix B

Evaluation of the optimal quantum bound of the Bell functional
\mathscr{G}_{n}
via the SOS Method

We evaluate the optimal quantum value of the Bell functional
\mathscr{G}_{n}
employing the sum-of-squares (SOS) relaxation method. This approach reformulates the non-convex optimisation over quantum states and measurement operators into a convex problem, enabling analytic characterisation of both the optimal bound and the structure of the corresponding quantum realisations.

Applying SOS relaxation, the shifted Bell operator can be written as

{G}_{n}\openone_{n}-\mathscr{G}_{n}=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\frac{\omega_{x}}{2}\ M^{\dagger}_{x}M_{x},

(104)

(105)

Using Eq. (
105

A_{x}^{2}=B_{y}^{2}=\openone
(since their eigenvalues are

), we find

M_{x}^{\dagger}M_{x}=\sum\limits_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\quantity[ \frac{\qty(\mathcal{B}_x)^2}{\omega_x^2} + \openone]-2\sum\limits_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\frac{A_{x}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{x}}{\omega_{x}}.

(106)

Substituting

and
\omega_{x}
from Eq. (
105

Comparing this with the SOS definition and taking the expectation value with respect to

For all

Lemma 2
.

Let
z^{x}=(z_{1}^{x},z_{2}^{x},\ldots,z_{n}^{x})
denote binary strings of length
n
, where each component
z^{x}_{y}\in\quantity{0,1}
,
z^{x}_{1}=0
fixed and the remaining
n-1
bits varying freely, yielding
2^{n-1}
distinct strings. Then, for all
x\in\quantity{1,\cdots,2^{n-1}}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\quantity{1,\cdots,n}
, the following identities hold

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}(-1)^{z^{x}_{y}+z^{x}_{y^{\prime}}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }y\neq y^{\prime}\\
2^{n-1}&\text{if }y=y^{\prime}\end{cases}

(114)

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}(-1)^{z^{x}_{y_{1}}+z^{x}_{y^{\prime}_{1}}}\cdot(-1)^{z^{x}_{y_{2}}+z^{x}_{y^{\prime}_{2}}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }(y_{1},y^{\prime}_{1})\neq(y_{2},y^{\prime}_{2})\\
2^{n-1}&\text{if }(y_{1},y^{\prime}_{1})=(y_{2},y^{\prime}_{2})\end{cases}

(115)

Proof.

Each index
x
corresponds to a binary string
z^{x}=\quantity(z_1^x,z_2^x,\ldots,z_n^x)\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with
z^{x}_{1}=0
. The complete set of such binary strings is

V=\quantity{z=(0,z_2,z_3,\ldots,z_n)\ | \ z_i \in\qty{0,1} },\ \ \absolutevalue{V}=2^{n-1}

(116)

Any sum over the index
x
can equivalently be expressed as a sum over the set
V
, i.e.

\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}f(z^{x})=\sum_{z\in V}f(z)

(117)

for any function
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
. The order of summation is immaterial, as these are finite sums independent of indexing.

To prove Eqs. (
114
) and (
115
), we employ the Walsh-Hadamard orthogonality. A Walsh function (or Walsh character) is defined for any subset
S\subseteq\quantity{2,3,\ldots,n}
as

\chi_{S}:V\to\quantity{+1,-1},\ \ \chi_{S}=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}z_{i}}

(118)

Each
\chi_{S}
maps a binary string
z\in V
to
\pm 1
, depending on the parity of bits indexed by
S
. When
S=\emptyset
,
\chi_{\emptyset}(z)=1
is the trivial Walsh function. The collection
\quantity{\chi_S:S\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots,n\}}
forms a complete orthogonal basis for real-valued functions on
V
. Specifically,

\sum_{z\in V}\chi_{S}(z)\chi_{S^{\prime}}(z)=\begin{cases}2^{n-1}&\text{if }S=S^{\prime}\\
0&\text{if }S\neq S^{\prime}\end{cases}

(119)

Now, for any pair
(y,y^{\prime})
, define the subset
S_{1}=\quantity{y,y}\cap S
. Then,

(-1)^{z_{y}+z_{y^{\prime}}}=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S_{1}}z_{i}}=\chi_{S_{1}}(z),

(120)

since
z_{1}=0
contributes nothing whenever
y=1
or
y^{\prime}=1
. Summing over all
z\in V
,

\sum_{z\in V}(-1)^{z_{y}+z_{y^{\prime}}}=\sum_{z\in V}\chi_{S_{1}}(z)

(121)

Applying the orthogonality of Walsh characters gives

\sum_{z\in V}\chi_{S_{1}}(z)=\begin{cases}2^{n-1},&\text{if }S_{1}=\emptyset\\
0,&\text{if }S_{1}\neq\emptyset\end{cases}

(122)

The subset
S_{1}=\emptyset
precisely when
y=y^{\prime}
, thereby proving Eq. (
114
).

Next, for any two index pairs
\quantity(y_1,y'_1)
and
\quantity(y_2,y'_2)
,

(-1)^{z_{y_{1}}+z_{y^{\prime}_{1}}}\cdot(-1)^{z_{y_{2}}+z_{y^{\prime}_{2}}}=(-1)^{z_{y_{1}}+z_{y^{\prime}_{1}}+z_{y_{2}}+z_{y^{\prime}_{2}}}.

(123)

We define another subset
S_{2}=\quantity{y_1,y'_1,y_2,y'_2}\cap S
so that so that the product again corresponds to a Walsh character
\chi_{S_{2}}(z)
. Then,

\sum_{z\in V}(-1)^{z_{y_{1}}+z_{y^{\prime}_{1}}}\cdot(-1)^{z_{y_{2}}+z_{y^{\prime}_{2}}}=\sum_{z\in V}\chi_{S_{2}}(z)=\begin{cases}2^{n-1},&\text{if }S_{2}=\emptyset\\
0,&\text{if }S_{2}\neq\emptyset\end{cases}

(124)

The subset
S_{2}=\emptyset
only when the two index pairs coincide, i.e.
\quantity(y_1,y'_1)=\quantity(y_2,y'_2)
, which establishes Eq. (
115
).
∎

Expanding Eq. (
113
) in a Taylor series gives

\displaystyle(G_{n})^{opt}_{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle=\max\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\sqrt{n+\quantity(\mathbf{C}_x \cdot\mathbf{\Lambda})}=\max\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\sqrt{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\quantity{\sqrt{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{k}\frac{\qty(\mathbf{C}_x \cdot\mathbf{\Lambda})^k}{n^k}}\frac{\quantity(\mathbf{C}_x \cdot\mathbf{\Lambda})^{k}}{n^{k}}

(125)

\displaystyle=\max\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\quantity{\sqrt{n}+\frac{\mathbf{C}_x \cdot\mathbf{\Lambda}}{2\sqrt{n}}-\frac{\qty(\mathbf{C}_x \cdot\mathbf{\Lambda})^2}{8 n^{\frac{3}{2}}} + \cdots}

Considering the linear term
\sum_{x}\mathbf{C}_{x}\cdot\mathbf{\Lambda}
from Eq. (
125
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\mathbf{C}_{x}\cdot\mathbf{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\sum_{y<y^{\prime}}c^{x}_{yy^{\prime}}\ \Lambda_{yy^{\prime}}\ \ \ [\text{From Eq.~(\ref{landc})}]

(126)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y<y^{\prime}}\quantity(\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}} c^x_{yy'})\ \Lambda_{yy^{\prime}}=0\ \ \ [\text{From Eq.~(\ref{iden1})}].

Similarly, considering the quadratic term
\sum_{x}\quantity(\mathbf{C}_x \cdot\mathbf{\Lambda})^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\quantity(\mathbf{C}_x \cdot\mathbf{\Lambda})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\quantity(\sum_{y<y'} c^x_{yy'} \ \Lambda_{yy'})^{2}\ \ \ [\text{From Eq.~(\ref{landc})}]

(127)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\quantity{\sum_{y_1<y'_1} \sum_{y_2<y'_2} c^x_{y_1y'_1}c^x_{y_2y'_2} \ \Lambda_{y_1y'_1} \ \Lambda_{y_2y'_2}}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\sum_{y\leq y^{\prime}}\Lambda_{yy^{\prime}}^{2}

More generally, for an arbitrary power

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\quantity(\mathbf{C}_x\cdot\mathbf{\Lambda})^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\quantity(\sum_{y_1<y'_1} \cdots\sum_{y_k<y'_k} \Lambda_{y_1y'_1} \cdots\Lambda_{y_ky'_k} \ c^x_{(y_1<y'_1)} \cdots c^x_{(y_k<y'_k)})

By the same reasoning as in Lemma
2
, and using Walsh–Hadamard orthogonality, the inner sum over
x

\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\quantity(\mathbf{C}_x\cdot\mathbf{\Lambda})^{k}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }k=(2m+1)\\
2^{n-1}\sum\limits_{y<y^{\prime}}\Lambda^{2m}_{yy^{\prime}},&\text{if }k=2m\end{cases}

(129)

The result in Eq. (
129
) can be understood as a direct consequence of parity symmetry within the Walsh–Hadamard basis. Each coefficient
c^{x}_{(y,y^{\prime})}
alternates sign according to the parity of the corresponding bits in the binary string
z^{x}
. When taking products of such terms, every index
y
contributes a factor of
(-1)^{z^{x}_{y}}
each time it appears. If an index appears an even number of times, these factors cancel pairwise, resulting in a net contribution of
+1
; if it appears an odd number of times, a residual
(-1)^{z^{x}_{y}}
remains. Summing over all binary strings
z^{x}
uniformly, any residual sign depending on
z^{x}_{y}
averages to zero due to the symmetry between configurations with
z^{x}_{y}\in\{0,1\}
. Consequently, only those terms where all indices appear in even multiplicities, i.e., all pairs are repeated, survive the summation. This explains why all odd-order terms vanish and why, for even orders, only diagonal contributions proportional to
\Lambda^{2m}_{yy^{\prime}}
remain.

\gradient{\mathbf{\Lambda}}=0

\Lambda_{yy^{\prime}}=0\ \forall y\neq y^{\prime}
.

Moreover, the point
\mathbf{\Lambda}=0

\Tr[\quantity{B_y,B_{y'}}\rho]=0

Evaluating

Thus, the maximal achievable value of the Bell functional
\mathscr{G}_{n}
is attained if and only if there exists an optimal state
\rho

\Tr[\quantity{B_y,B_{y'}}\rho]=0\ \forall y\neq y^{\prime}

\sum\limits_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}(-1)^{z_{y}^{x}}A_{x}
. From Eq. (

), we find

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}(-1)^{z_{y}^{x}}A_{x}\otimes\openone\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{y^{\prime}=1}^{n}(-1)^{z_{y}^{x}+z_{y^{\prime}}^{x}}\quantity(\openone\otimes B_{y'})\ket{\psi}

(146)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{y^{\prime}=1}^{n}\delta_{y,y^{\prime}}\quantity(\openone\otimes B_{y'})\ket{\psi}=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}\quantity(\openone\otimes B_y)\ket{\psi}

Define the normalised, scaled observables for Alice as

\mathcal{A}_{y}:=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}(-1)^{z_{y}^{x}}\ A_{x}\ \ \ \forall y\in[n].

(147)

Then the relationship between Alice’s and Bob’s observables on the support of
\ket{\psi}
can be re-expressed, using Eq. (

), as

\quantity(\openone\otimes B_y)\ket{\psi}=\quantity(\mathcal{A}_y \otimes\openone)\ket{\psi}\implies\expectationvalue{\qty(\mathcal{A}_y \otimes B_y)}{ \psi}=1\ \ \ \forall y\in[n].

(148)

Thus, on the support of the optimal state, Bob’s operators act as the transpose (or conjugate counterparts) of Alice’s.

B.3

Structure of the Optimal State

Let
\quantity{\ket{i}_A}_{A},\quantity{\ket{j}_B}_{B}
be fixed orthonormal bases of the local Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
, respectively. Define
\mu_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
, satisfying
\absolutevalue{\mu_{ij}}\leq 1
and
\sum_{ij}\absolutevalue{\mu_{ij}}^{2}=1
, as the complex coefficients obtained by projecting
\ket{\psi}
onto each product basis element, i.e.,
\mu_{ij}=\matrixelement{i}{\psi}{j}
. Then, any pure bipartite state
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
admits the decomposition

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\mu_{ij}\ket{i}_{A}\otimes\ket{j}_{B}=\mathrm{vec}\quantity(\mathscr{M})

(149)

where the vectorisation map
\mathrm{vec}:\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{C}^{d^{2}}
is defined as

\mathrm{vec}\quantity(\mathscr{M}):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\mu_{ij}\ket{i}_{A}\otimes\ket{j}_{B},

(150)

thereby associating the bipartite vector
\ket{\psi}
with the
d\times d
coefficient matrix
\mathscr{M}:=[\mu_{ij}]\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
, i.e.
\mathscr{M}_{ij}=\mu_{ij}
.

There always exists local unitary
U_{A}
and
V_{B}
acting on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
, respectively, that transform the local bases to the Schmidt basis, in which
\mathscr{M}
becomes diagonal and positive. This follows from the singular value decomposition,

\mathscr{M}=U_{A}\mathcal{D}V_{B}^{\dagger},\ \ \mathcal{D}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{r\times r}&\emptyset_{r\times d-r}\\
\emptyset_{d-r\times r}&\emptyset_{d-r\times d-r}\end{bmatrix},

(151)

where
D_{r\times r}=\text{diag}\quantity(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)
contains the Schmidt coefficients
\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}>0
with
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1
, and
r=\mathrm{rank}\quantity(\mathscr{M})
. The state in Eq. (
149
) then can be expressed as

\ket{\psi}=\mathrm{vec}\quantity(\mathscr{M})=\mathrm{vec}\quantity(U_A \mathcal{D} V_B^{\dagger})=\quantity(U_A \otimes V^{\dagger})\ \mathrm{vec}\quantity(\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}),\ \ \ \text{with }\mathrm{vec}\quantity(\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\ket{u_{i}}\otimes\ket{v_{i}}.

(152)

Since,

\mathrm{vec}\quantity(\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}})=\mathrm{vec}\quantity(D^{\frac{1}{2}})=\sqrt{r}\quantity(\openone\otimes D^{\frac{1}{2}})\ket{\phi^{+}},\ \ \text{with }\ket{\phi^{+}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\sum_{i}\ket{i}_{A}\ket{i}_{B},

(153)

we arrive at the following form

\ket{\psi}=\sqrt{r}\quantity(U_A \otimes V^{\dagger}_B)\quantity(\openone\otimes\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}})\ket{\phi^{+}}.

(154)

Now, applying the optimal condition from Eq. (
148
) and using the standard identity
\quantity(A \otimes B)\mathrm{vec}\quantity(Y)=\mathrm{vec}\quantity(A Y B^T)
, we find

(\mathcal{A}_{y}\otimes\openone)\ket{\psi}=\mathrm{vec}\quantity(\mathcal{A}_yU_A\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}V_B^{\dagger})=(\openone\otimes B_{y})\ket{\psi}=\mathrm{vec}\quantity(U_A \mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}} V_B^{\dagger} B_y^{T})

(155)

Injectivity of the vectorisation map implies

\mathcal{A}_{y}U_{A}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}V_{B}^{\dagger}=U_{A}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}V_{B}^{\dagger}B_{y}^{T}\implies\quantity(U_A^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}_y U_A)\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}\quantity(V_B^{\dagger} B_y^T V_B)

(156)

We now redefine the observables in the Schmidt basis as

\mathcal{A}_{y}\equiv U_{A}\mathcal{A}_{y}U_{A}^{\dagger},\ \ B_{y}\equiv V_{B}^{\dagger}B^{T}_{y}V_{B},

(157)

and define the Schmidt basis vectors by

\ket{u_{i}}:=U_{A}\ket{i}_{A},\ \ \ \ket{v_{i}}:=V_{B}\ket{i}_{B},\ \ \ \text{with }i\in\quantity{1,2,\ldots,r}.

(158)

Since
\mathcal{A}_{y},B_{y}
act on
\mathcal{H}^{d}
locally, we extend the Schmidt basis by adding
d-r
orthogonal vectors

\quantity{\ket{u_p}}_{p=1}^{d}:=\quantity{\qty{\ket{u_i}}_{i=1}^r,\qty{\ket{u_i}}_{i=r+1}^d}_{i=1}^{r},\quantity{\ket{u_i}}_{i=r+1}^{d};\ \ \ \quantity{\ket{v_q}}_{q=1}^{d}:=\quantity{\qty{\ket{v_j}}_{j=1}^r,\qty{\ket{v_j}}_{j=r+1}^d}_{j=1}^{r},\quantity{\ket{v_j}}_{j=r+1}^{d}.

(159)

In this basis, any Hermitian involutive operator acting on
\mathcal{H}^{d}
can be expressed as a block matrix respecting the decomposition
\mathbb{C}^{d}=\mathbb{C}^{r}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{d-r}

\mathcal{A}_{y}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{y}^{r}&X_{A}\\
X^{\dagger}_{A}&Z_{A}\end{bmatrix},\ \ \ B_{y}=\begin{bmatrix}B_{y}^{r}&X_{B}\\
X^{\dagger}_{B}&Z_{B}\end{bmatrix},

(160)

where
\mathcal{A}_{y}^{r},B_{y}^{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
act on the respective Schmidt subspaces
(r\times r)
, and
X\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times(d-r)}
and
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{(d-r)\times(d-r)}
are the off-diagonal and complementary blocks. Moreover, since
\mathcal{A}_{y}
and
B_{y}
are Hermitian with
\mathcal{A}_{y}^{2}=B_{y}^{2}=\openone
, the following block relations hold

\quantity(\mathcal{A}_y^r)^{2}+X_{A}X^{\dagger}=\openone_{r},\ \ X^{\dagger}X_{A}+Z_{A}^{2}=\openone_{d-r},\ \ X^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}_{y}^{r}+Z_{A}X^{\dagger}=\mathcal{A}_{y}^{r}X_{A}+X_{A}Z_{A}=0.

(161)

Now, in the extended Schmidt basis, the optimal condition of Eq. (
156
) becomes

\mathcal{A}_{y}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}B_{y}\implies\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{y}^{r}D^{\frac{1}{2}}&&\emptyset\\
X_{A}^{\dagger}D^{\frac{1}{2}}&&\emptyset\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}D^{\frac{1}{2}}B_{y}^{r}&&D^{\frac{1}{2}}X_{B}\\
\emptyset&&\emptyset\end{bmatrix}\implies\begin{cases}(i)&\mathcal{A}_{y}^{r}=D^{\frac{1}{2}}B_{y}^{r}D^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
(ii)&X_{b}=X_{A}^{\dagger}=0\end{cases}

(162)

Thus, both
\mathcal{A}_{y}
and
b_{y}
are block diagonal in the extended Schmidt basis. From Eq. (
161
), we further have
\quantity(\mathcal{A}_y^r)^{2}=\quantity(B_y^r)^{2}=\openone_{r}
. Using
\quantity(\mathcal{A}_y^r)^{2}=\openone_{r}
, we find

\openone=\quantity(\mathcal{A}_y^r){\dagger}\mathcal{A}_{y}^{r}=\quantity(D^{\frac{1}{2}}B_y^r D^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{\dagger}\quantity(D^{\frac{1}{2}}B_y^r D^{-\frac{1}{2}})=D^{-\frac{1}{2}}B_{y}^{r}DB_{y}^{r}D^{-\frac{1}{2}}\implies B_{y}^{r}DB_{y}^{r}=D\implies\quantity[B_y^r,D]=0\ \ \forall y\in[n].

(163)

Using
\quantity(B_y^r)^{2}=\openone_{r}
, A symmetric argument yields
\quantity[\mathcal{A}_y^r,D]=0\ \ \forall y\in[n]
.

Let the distinct eigenvalues of
D
be denoted by
\lambda^{(k)}
with multiplicities
m_{k}
for
k=1,\ldots,\alpha
, satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}m_{k}=r
. Then,
D=\oplus_{k=1}^{\alpha}\lambda^{(k)}\openone_{m_{k}}
, where
\openone_{m_{k}}
is the identity on the
m_{k}
-dimensional degeneracy subspace, and the state can be expressed as

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}\sqrt{\lambda^{(k)}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\ket{u_{i}}\otimes\ket{v_{i}}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\alpha}\sqrt{\lambda^{(k)}m_{k}}\ket{\phi^{+}_{m_{k}}},\ \ \ \text{with }\ \ket{\phi^{+}_{m_{k}}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\ket{u_{i}}\otimes\ket{v_{i}}

(164)

where each
\ket{\phi^{+}_{m_{k}}}\in\mathcal{H}^{m_{k}}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{m_{k}}_{B}
is a maximally entangled on the
k
-th degeneracy block. The observables can be expressed in the Schmidt basis as

\forall y,\ \mathcal{A}_{y}=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\outerproduct{u_i}{u_j},\ \ a_{ij}=\matrixelement{u_i}{\mathcal{A}_y}{u_j},\ \ \ \text{and }\ B_{y}=\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}\outerproduct{v_i}{v_j},\ \ b_{ij}=\matrixelement{v_i}{B_y}{v_j}.

(165)

Since all algebraic relations are valid only on the support of
\ket{\psi}
, whose rank is
r
, we may, without loss of generality, restrict our attention to the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{r}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{r}_{A}
. For notational simplicity, we omit the superscript
r
henceforth when referring to states, observables, or Hilbert spaces.

Using the relation obtained in Eq. (
163
), with straightforward algebra yields

[B_{y},D]=0\implies\matrixelement{v_i}{[B_y,D]}{v_j}=\quantity(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)b_{ij}=0\ \ \forall i,j

(166)

It follows that whenever
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
, the corresponding coefficient
b_{ij}=0
for for every pair
i,j
. Hence, each
B_{y}
has non-zero entries only within subspaces associated with equal Schmidt coefficients. The same argument applies to
\mathcal{A}_{y}
in the
\ket{u_{i}}
basis. Therefore, both sets of observables
\mathcal{A}_{y}
and
B_{y}
are block-diagonal with respect to the degeneracy structure of
D

\forall y,\ \mathcal{A}_{y}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)},\ \ \ \text{and }B_{y}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\alpha}B_{y}^{(k)},\ \ \ \mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)},B_{y}^{(k)}\in\mathscr{L}\quantity(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}),

(167)

where each block corresponds to a degenerate Schmidt value
\lambda_{i_{1}}=\lambda_{i_{2}}=\cdots=\lambda_{i_{m_{k}}}
, acting on the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}=\mathrm{span}\quantity{\ket{u_{i_1}}:i \in I_k}:i\in I_{k}
. In matrix form, all entries between different eigenspaces vanish, rendering each observable block-diagonal.

Using the optimality condition
\mathcal{A}_{y}D^{\frac{1}{2}}=D^{\frac{1}{2}}B_{y}
from Eq. (
162
), and noting that
D^{\frac{1}{2}}
acts as a scalar multiple of the identity on each degenerate subspace, i.e.
\evaluated{D^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{\mathcal{H}^{(k)}}=\sqrt{\lambda^{(k)}}\openone_{m_{k}}
, we get

\forall y,\ \mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)}\sqrt{\lambda^{(k)}}\openone_{m_{k}}=\sqrt{\lambda^{(k)}}\openone_{m_{k}}B_{y}^{(k)}\implies\mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)}=B_{y}^{(k)}\ \ \ \text{for every block $k$.}

(168)

Hence, on each Schmidt block, Alice’s and Bob’s observables coincide, and each block supports the same operator algebra. Consequently, the block matrices of Alice and Bob coincide in the Schmidt basis, i.e.
\mathcal{A}_{y}=B_{y}
.

Block-diagonality of
\mathcal{A}_{y}
and
B_{y}
in the Schmidt basis leads to the following situations. If all Schmidt values are distinct, i.e.,
\alpha=r
and
m_{k}=1
for every
k
, then each block is
1\times 1
. Thus, all
\mathcal{A}_{y}
(
B_{y}
) are diagonal in the Schmidt basis and hence mutually commuting. Since Bell nonlocality requires incompatible local measurements, this regime cannot yield a Bell violation.

If all Schmidt values are equal, i.e.,
\lambda_{i}=\frac{1}{r}\ \forall i
, then
D=\frac{1}{r}\openone
, and the shared state is maximally entangled state in
\mathcal{H}^{r}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{r}_{B}
. The optimality condition enforces complete anticommutation, i.e.,
\quantity{B_y,B_{y'}}=\quantity{\mathcal{A}_y,\mathcal{A}_{y'}}=\delta_{yy^{\prime}}\openone
. The minimal local Hilbert space dimension that accommodates
n
such pairwise anticommuting Hermitian involutions is the smallest complex representation of the Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{n}\quantity(\mathbb{C})
, given by
r_{min}=2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}
. However, this case is sufficient, but not necessary, in general.

In general, for optimal Bell violation, degeneracy is necessary, i.e.,
m_{k}\geq 2
to allow non-commuting observables. Now, lets analyse the algebraic properties in each block. Since each
\mathcal{A}_{y}
and
B_{y}
are block diagonal, each block will only act on the corresponding block of the shared state. Thus, all optimality conditions hold independently within each block. From
\quantity(\qty{\mathcal{A}_y,\mathcal{A}_{y'}}\otimes\openone)\ket{\psi}=\quantity(\openone\otimes\qty{B_y,B_{y'}})\ket{\psi}=0
, restricting to block
k
gives

\forall y,\ \ \quantity(\qty{\mathcal{A}_y^{(k)},\mathcal{A}_{y'}^{(k)}}\otimes\openone)\ket{\phi^{+}}=\quantity(\openone\otimes\qty{B_y^{(k)},B_{y'}^{(k)}})\ket{\phi^{+}}=0.

(169)

where
\ket{\phi^{+}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k}}\ket{i}\otimes\ket{i}
is the maximally entangled state on the
k
-th block. Using vectorisation, this becomes

\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\mathrm{vec}\quantity(\qty{\mathcal{A}_y^{(k)},\mathcal{A}_{y'}^{(k)}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\mathrm{vec}\quantity(\qty{B_y^{(k)},B_{y'}^{(k)}}^T)=0

(170)

Injectivity of the vectorisation map implies

\forall y\neq y^{\prime},\ \ \quantity{\mathcal{A}_y^{(k)},\mathcal{A}_{y'}^{(k)}}=0,\ \ \text{and }\quantity{B_y^{(k)},B_{y'}^{(k)}}^{T}=0\implies\quantity{B_y^{(k)},B_{y'}^{(k)}}=0.

(171)

Thus, within each Schmidt block, the local observables form a set of
n
Hermitian involutions that pairwise anticommute.

Consequently, the degeneracy of
D
partitions the global system into orthogonal subspaces, each supporting a maximally entangled component of
\ket{\psi}
. Within each such subspace, the observables realise a complex representation of the complex Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{n}\quantity(\mathbb{C})
. Hence, no block of smaller dimension
2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
can achieve the optimal Bell violation.

Collecting all results, every optimal quantum realisation possesses the structure

\ket{\psi}=\bigoplus_{k}\sqrt{\lambda^{(k)}m_{k}}\ket{\phi^{+}_{m_{k}}},\ \ m_{k}\geq 2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor},\ \ \text{with }\mathcal{A}_{y}^{(k)}=B_{y}^{(k)},

(172)

where each block carries an irreducible representation of the complex Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{n}\quantity(\mathbb{C})
, unique up to local unitary equivalence. The direct-sum freedom corresponds to classical probabilistic mixing between identical Clifford sectors, an unavoidable degeneracy that leaves the Bell functional value unchanged but does not affect the self-testing equivalence of the underlying irreducible blocks.

Appendix D

Bounding the distance of the physical State from the ideal state:

For the ideal case, the SOS conditions imply that the state lies entirely in the intersection of the kernels of these operators. Any physical state achieving a slightly suboptimal violation necessarily has a component outside this ideal subspace, and the magnitude of this component provides a direct measure of the deviation from the ideal state. By explicitly projecting the physical state onto the ideal subspace, we obtain quantitative bounds on the norm of the orthogonal component, thereby providing a precise characterization of state robustness.

From SOS, the relation between the ideal observables and the state

\forall x\ \ \ M_{x}\ket{\psi}=0\implies A_{x}\otimes\openone\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(\openone\otimes\mathcal{B}_{x})\ket{\psi},

(205)

The ideal SOS relation
M_{x}\ket{\psi}=0
implies that the state
\ket{\psi}
is in the kernel of
M_{x}
. The kernel is defined as

\ker(M_{x})=\quantity{\ket{\psi}:M_x\ket{\psi}=0}:M_{x}\ket{\psi}=0

(206)

representing the set of all states that satisfy the ideal SOS relation perfectly. As there are
2^{n-1}
such condition, we define the subspace of all states satisfying every constraint simultaneously as the intersection of these kernels

\mathcal{S}=\bigcap\limits_{x}\ker(M_{x})

(207)

The projection of a physical state
\ket{\tilde{\psi}}
onto this subspace yields the closest ideal state, denoted
\ket{\psi^{\prime}}

\ket{\psi^{\prime}}=\frac{\Pi\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}{\Pi\norm{\Pi\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}},\ \ \ {\Pi\norm{\Pi\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}}=\Tr[\Pi\outerproduct{\tilde{\psi}}{\tilde{\psi}}]

(208)

where,
\Pi
is the orthogonal projector onto
\mathcal{S}
. Decomposing
\ket{\tilde{\psi}}
into components parallel and orthogonal to the ideal subspace, one has

\ket{\tilde{\psi}}=\Pi\ket{\tilde{\psi}}+\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}

(209)

Here,
\Pi\ket{\tilde{\psi}}
lies within the ideal subspace, while
\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}
is the component orthogonal to it. The norm of the latter component,
\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\norm{\qty(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}
, quantifies the distance between
\ket{\tilde{\psi}}
and
\mathcal{S}
. Defining

\ket{\psi_{\parallel}}:=\frac{\Pi\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}{\Pi\norm{\Pi\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}},\ \ \text{and }\ \ket{\psi_{\perp}}:=\frac{\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}{\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\norm{\qty(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}}

(210)

we note that
\ket{\psi_{\parallel}}
and
\ket{\psi_{\perp}}
are orthogonal, i.e.,
\innerproduct{ \psi_{\parallel}}{ \psi_{\perp}}=0
, and can express
\ket{\tilde{\psi}}
as

\ket{\tilde{\psi}}=\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}\ket{\psi_{\parallel}}+\epsilon\ket{\psi_{\perp}},\ \ \ \text{where }\epsilon=\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\norm{\qty(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}

(211)

The distance between the ideal state
(\ket{\psi}\equiv\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\equiv\ket{\psi_{\parallel}})
and the physical state
\ket{\tilde{\psi}}
then becomes

\displaystyle\norm{\ket{\tilde{\psi}}-\ket{\psi'}}-\ket{\psi^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}\norm{\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2} \ket{\psi_{\parallel}}+ \epsilon\ket{\psi_{\perp}}-\ket{\psi_{\parallel}}}+\epsilon\ket{\psi_{\perp}}-\ket{\psi_{\parallel}}^{2}

(212)

\displaystyle=\quantity(\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}-1)\norm{\qty(\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}-1) \ket{\psi_{\parallel}}+ \epsilon\ket{\psi_{\perp}}}+\epsilon\ket{\psi_{\perp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\quantity(\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}-1)^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\ \ \text{[since $\ket{\psi_{\parallel}}$ and $\ket{\psi_{\perp}}$ are normalised and orthogonal]}

\displaystyle=2\quantity(1-\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2})\approx\epsilon^{2}=\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\norm{\qty(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}^{2}

Recalling the positive semidefinite operator from Eq. (
106
),

\mathcal{M}=\sum_{x}\frac{\omega_{x}}{2}M_{x}^{\dagger}M_{x},\ \ \ \text{with }\delta=\expectationvalue{\mathcal{M}}{\tilde{\psi}}

(213)

we observe that

\mathcal{M}
coincides with
\mathcal{S}

\ker(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{S}

(214)

That means the projection onto
\ker(\mathcal{M})
is exactly
\Pi

\mathcal{M}\Pi=0
. Thus, from Eq. (
209
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\ket{\tilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}\Pi\ket{\tilde{\psi}}+\mathcal{M}\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}

(215)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\mathcal{M}}{\tilde{\psi}}=\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\norm{\qty(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}^{2}

Since
\mathcal{M}
is a positive semidefinite operator acting as zero on
\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}
. Denoting the spectral decomposition of
\mathcal{M}

(216)

where

span the full space and
\lambda_{j}=0

\mathcal{S}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\min\limits_{\ket{\phi}\in\mathcal{S}^{\perp},\norm{\ket{\phi}}=1}\expectationvalue{\mathcal{M}}{\phi}

(219)

Thus,

\norm{\ket{\tilde{\psi}}-\ket{\psi'}}-\ket{\psi^{\prime}}^{2}\approx\quantity(\openone-\Pi)\norm{\qty(\openone-\Pi)\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}^{2}\leq\frac{\mathcal{M}\norm{\mathcal{M}\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}^{2}}{\lambda_{min}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{min}}\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\frac{\omega_{x}}{2}M_{x}\norm{M_x\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}^{2}=\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{min}}\sim\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{n}}\ \ [\text{from Eq.~(\ref{lminc2})}]

(220)

Therefore,

\norm{\ket{\tilde{\psi}}-\ket{\psi'}}-\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\sim\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{n}}}

(221)

Robust Bound on Bob’s Anticommutation Relations

Recalling Eq. (88) from the main text,

\tilde{\omega}_{x}^{2}=\expectationvalue{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_x^2}_{\tilde{\psi}}=n+\sum_{y<y^{\prime}}(-1)^{z^{x}_{y}+z^{x}_{y^{\prime}}}\expectationvalue{\tilde{B}_y,\tilde{B}_{y'}}_{\tilde{\psi}}=n+\Delta_{x}\ \ \ \forall x

Taking the square root and applying a Taylor expansion yields

\tilde{\omega}_{x}=\sqrt{n}\quantity(1+\frac{\Delta_x}{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}\approx\sqrt{n}\quantity(1+\frac{\Delta_x}{2n}-\frac{\Delta_x^2}{8n^2}+\order{\frac{\Delta_x^3}{n^3}})

Since
\Delta_{x}\ll n
, implying
\evaluated{\order{\frac{\Delta_x^m}{n^m}}}_{m\geq 2}\approx 0

\tilde{\omega}_{x}\approx\sqrt{n}\quantity(1+\frac{\Delta_x}{2n})

Let us now consider the operator
\tilde{M}_{x}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{x}}(\openone\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{x})-\tilde{A}_{x}\otimes\openone

\tilde{M}_{x}\norm{\tilde{M}_x \ket{\tilde{\psi}}}^{2}=\quantity(1+\frac{\expval{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_x^2}}{\tilde{\omega}_x^2}-\frac{2}{\tilde{\omega}_x}\expval{\tilde{A}_x\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_x})=2\quantity[1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\qty(1-\frac{\Delta_x}{2n})\expval{\tilde{A}_x\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_x}]

(225)

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to
\expectationvalue{\tilde{A}_x\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_x}
, we have

\absolutevalue{\expval{\tilde{A}_x\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_x}}\leq\sqrt{\expectationvalue{\tilde{A}_x^2}\expectationvalue{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_x^2}}=\sqrt{n+\Delta_{x}}\approx\sqrt{n}\quantity(1+\frac{\Delta_x}{2n})

(226)

Substituting this into the previous Eq. (
225
) gives

\tilde{M}_{x}\norm{\tilde{M}_x \ket{\tilde{\psi}}}^{2}\geq\frac{\Delta_{x}^{2}}{4n^{2}}

(227)

Summing over all
x
and invoking the SOS relation yields

\delta=\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\frac{\omega_{x}}{2}\tilde{M}_{x}\norm{\tilde{M}_x\ket{\tilde{\psi}}}^{2}\geq\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\frac{1}{2}\quantity(1+\frac{\Delta_x}{2n})\frac{\Delta_{x}^{2}}{4n^{2}}\approx\sum_{x=1}^{2^{n-1}}\frac{\Delta_{x}^{2}}{8n^{2}}

(228)

\Delta_{x}
satisfies the bound

\absolutevalue{\Delta_x}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n+1}n}\sqrt{\delta}

Now, recall the matrix

112

C_{x,(y,y^{\prime})}=(-1)^{z^{x}_{y}+z^{x}_{y^{\prime}}}

(230)

(231)

\eta=0

\max\limits_{y<y^{\prime}}|\eta_{yy^{\prime}}|\leq K_{n}\max_{x}|\Delta_{x}|

(234)

Finally, using the previously derived bound for

\max\limits_{y<y^{\prime}}|\eta_{yy^{\prime}}|\leq L_{n}\sqrt{\delta},\ \ \ L_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}n}K_{n}
