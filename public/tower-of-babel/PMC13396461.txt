A node importance evaluation method integrating static and dynamic indicators with management decision requirements
Authors: Zhengfeng Ma, Haoyun Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Focusing on the needs of road network node importance evaluation and the limitations of existing methods, a novel method to comprehensively evaluate road network node importance considering the influence of operating state changes is proposed. First, an evaluation index system combining static and dynamic indexes is constructed. Therefore, an impact evaluation model of the topological potential is proposed to reflect the operating state of the road network. Second, the integrated weighting method based on multi-objective programming is proposed to determine the final weight of the evaluation index and the sorting result of node importance; further, the particle swarm optimization algorithm is employed to solve this problem. Third, a node grading algorithm based on various sorting results is proposed. Finally, an experimental scheme is designed which verifies the rationality of our proposed methods, algorithms, and theories based on a case study in Xining City. A change in the evaluation index influences the importance of nodes and their sorting, and the dynamic index of the change in road network operation state should be considered. The change in the index weight significantly influences the evaluation of node importance and its sorting and the weight of the index should be reasonably determined; important nodes are more reliable when graded according to the internal relationship and rules of various sorting results. Our proposed method can determine the index weight, node importance sorting, and evaluate the importance of nodes in road networks.

Introduction

As an important part of the urban road network, intersections (hereinafter referred to as nodes) play an important role in the operation of urban road traffic. In the actual traffic scene, once the traffic congestion has been triggered by accidents, formulating targeted traffic control measures for important nodes to effectively prevent or significantly alleviate the traffic congestion is a solution to ensure the normal operation of urban traffic. The accurate and reasonable determination of important nodes of transportation systems by the traffic administration department is critical. Therefore, the use of a reasonable method to evaluate the importance of road network nodes has increasingly attracted the attention of traffic researchers and administrators. In recent years, studies on the evaluation of the importance of road network nodes mainly focus on three categories: the simple method of a single index model, the comprehensive evaluation method based on multiple indexes, and the attack model method based on a complex network.

The simple method based on a single index model mainly reflects the importance of nodes by constructing representative evaluation indexes. The main results are as follows: In 2015, the inverse of generalized travel time was presented as a utility function to quantify the influence of external disturbances on the transportation system and used to determine key transportation facilities in1; Zeng et al.2 proposed a novel node importance evaluation method based on attraction between nodes. According to their algorithm, the importance of a node may be measured by the product of the node trust value and the sum of the node’s attractiveness to other nodes in the network; Liu et al.3 studied the influence of the number of lanes on the importance of the node and proposed an improved spatial weighting model. In addition, by considering the correlation coefficient of traffic flow between intersections, a method of measuring the importance of urban road network nodes with H index was proposed; Xu et al.4 proposed a method to sort the importance of nodes in the urban road network through data mining of trajectories of numerous urban taxis; Lu et al.5 started from the node, combined with the topological structure around the node, and identified the node importance and its propagation ability based on the node clustering H-index mixture centrality. Kunt et al.6 adopted a node shrinking method based on network cohesion, which comprehensively considered the degree and location of nodes to determine their importance. Deng et al.7 proposed the concepts of generalized edge betweenness and generalized edge clustering coefficients based on node betweenness and node coefficient, and then proposed an evaluation method on node and edge-based node importance. Zhao et al. 8 regarded financial institutions as a node in the financial system network and measured the importance of financial institutions in the financial network by calculating the adjacency and information entropy of each node. The method is relatively simple and is easy to operate; however, it has few index factors and a certain one-sidedness.

To overcome the shortcomings of the first method, some scholars introduced the method of comprehensive evaluation to study the importance of nodes. In 2011, Wang et al.9 selected the node degree, node betweenness, and peak hour traffic flow as the indexes to evaluate the importance of nodes in a traffic network. Through these indexes, information such as network topology, shortest path, and traffic flow status may be integrated to grade the importance of nodes using C cluster analysis. Meanwhile, Song et al.10 incorporated the urban flow intensity into the node importance evaluation index system according to the characteristics of urban agglomerations and adopted factor analysis as an objective method to avoid the randomness of subjective values in the calculation of node importance. Here, the importance level of each node is distinguished owing to the adoption of the K-means clustering. Consequently, Cao et al.11 used the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) to determine the weight of each index, in which the node traffic flow was defined as the sum of the traffic flow on the roads connecting the nodes by selecting the degree and betweenness of nodes in the topology network, urban road level, and node traffic flow as the evaluation indexes of node importance. Lu et al.12 improved the traditional neighborhood structure hole index from the perspective of topology and network characteristics between nodes and their neighbors combined with the improved traditional TOPSIS method using relative entropy. The key of this method is to calculate the objective weight of the evaluation index using the Gini and Kendall coefficients. Finally, the improved index may be applied to implement the importance evaluation of network nodes and calculate the node importance. Fan et al.13 analyzed the nodes in the power communication network from the physical topology, traffic distribution, and service importance distribution. Further, a key node identification algorithm was constructed by adopting a multilayer network to identify important nodes. Despite the differences in the vulnerability indexes of different layers of the communication network, the method which comprehensively included multiple attribute indexes, that influences the evaluation of node importance, such as node degree, node betweenness, and traffic flow, may overcome the one-sidedness of the first method and has certain advantages. Unfortunately, neither of them considered the influence of node change on the cascading failures on the traffic network.

Consequently, some scholars have introduced the concept of complex network theory into various networks and determined the importance of nodes based on the impact of node changes on the network with cascading failures. In 2013, Wang et al.14 established and presented the node importance evaluation method of a transportation network considering cascading failure of the transportation network based on node deletion. Candelieri et al.15 used network connectivity and efficiency loss to determine important nodes and used multi-graph to model public transportation networks and determined the attack strategy based on node degree and betweenness. Tang et al.16 proposed a weighted K-order spreading number algorithm based on network topology to evaluate the importance of nodes. Particularly, the nodes were set as infection sources, the total number of infected nodes was defined as the K-order spreading number after the spreading time K, and the important nodes were determined by the change in the network efficiency caused by attacking the node (removing all connected edges of the node). Wang et al.17 used the average weighted efficiency of the network after cascading failures to measure the consequences of cascading failures (efficiency model) to determine key nodes in wireless sensor networks. Yu et al.18 proposed a method to evaluate the importance of nodes in the case of cascading failures, which used the degree of a node and its neighbor nodes to define the node load and provided the transmission method of the node load in the case of node failure. In this algorithm, the change in the total number of edges before and after the network was used to measure the importance of the node. In addition, the duality theory was introduced to construct a road network topology map with road segments as nodes19. The underlying principle of this method is that traffic congestion has been regarded as a node attack mode and the precision of the state vulnerability assessment is improved by considering the impact of congestion. Meanwhile, Cao et al.20 determined the attack mode according to the node degree and betweenness. Accordingly, the road network efficiency model was established to determine the network change before and after node removal and the vulnerability and node importance of the bus network were studied.

References16–18 mainly discussed local impact evaluation models, which reflect the impact of node changes on the network through self-defined evaluation indexes using the network topology as input. The other part14,15,19,20 belongs to the global impact evaluation model, which mainly reflects the impact of node changes on the network through changes in network efficiency. The common denominator of the two methods is based on the network topology and can reflect the impact of node changes on the network. However, they did not consider other node attributes. Therefore, the determination of a reasonable algorithm to evaluate the importance of the nodes by combining the comprehensive evaluation method is a key problem in the attack modes environment. Therefore, Du et al.21 proposed an improved topological potential model to calculate the importance of subway network nodes. A network adjacency matrix was constructed in the method based on the passenger flow data, and the in-degree, out-degree, closeness, maximum eigenvalue, and betweenness centralities of the nodes were calculated. The result of the comprehensive weighting of the five indexes was used as a fixed attribute of the topological potential model, and the topological potential value and importance sorting of the nodes were calculated. Finally, the proposed model was verified using the node removal method and the change in network efficiency.

In summary, scholars in various fields have performed extensive studies on the evaluation of node importance and have achieved a series of research results. However, the evaluation of node importance of urban road networks has its particularity and none of the existing methods can meet the actual needs. The main reasons are: first, it takes a process for nodes to change from “unimpeded” to “congested” and the congestion levels also differ. Existing methods do not include the impact of non-physical damage on nodes when studying the impact of node changes on the road network. It is inappropriate to use the method of direct removal. Second, the traffic congestion of the node influences the entire road network and the local impact evaluation model is apparently unreasonable. Regarding the global impact evaluation model, based on studies, with an increase in the number of road network nodes, the network efficiency model cannot properly reflect the impact of node changes on the road network. Third, owing to the surrounding environment of the node (such as restricted areas) and traffic control measures (such as traffic restrictions), the location in the road network and topology are similar and the actual traffic operation of nodes with the same impact on the road network also differs. Therefore, other indices should be comprehensively considered to evaluate the importance of nodes. Although the comprehensive evaluation and attack models have been combined by scholars and used to evaluate the node importance of the subway network, in-depth studies on the impact of index selection and index weight on the evaluation of node importance are still lacking.

Organization of this paper

To describe the novel method of the comprehensive evaluation of the importance of nodes in a road network, the remainder of the paper is divided as follows. In Section II, the concept, model, data acquisition method, and the relationship between the importance of nodes and the related static indexes of road network node importance evaluation are introduced. Second, the concept of dynamic indexes and index data acquisition method based on the topological potential model are explained. Next, for the follow-up study of the impact of the evaluation index on the node importance evaluation, a set of evaluation index alternatives is provided. Finally, the preprocessing method of the evaluation index data is introduced. In Section III, based on the subjective and objective weighting method and the combined weighting method, an integrated weighting method based on the multi-objective programming model is proposed and its solution method is provided. In Section IV, the node sorting and grading methods are introduced; a node grading algorithm based on various sorting results is proposed in particular. In Section V, based on the case of the intersection in the core area of the urban road network in Xining City, Qinghai Province, China, an experimental scheme is designed to verify the rationality of the methods, algorithms, and theories proposed in this study. Section VI provides the conclusions of this study and future research perspectives.

Evaluation indexes and data preprocessing

Evaluation indexes

Undoubtedly, the importance of road network nodes is not only related to static indexes such as node degree, clustering coefficient, and betweenness but also has a greater relationship with the impact of changes in the node traffic operation state on the road network. To scientifically evaluate the importance of road network nodes and to include static indexes such as node degree, clustering coefficient, betweenness and average traffic volume, and average saturation of nodes during peak periods, the dynamic index (process evaluation index) of the impact of the change in the traffic operation state of a node on the road network is also considered. Next, the concept of each index and the evaluation models are explained in detail.

Static evaluation index

To better distinguish static and dynamic indexes, the indexes whose evaluation data acquisition are independent of the change value in road network operation state are defined as static evaluation indexes, such as node degree, clustering coefficient, betweenness and average traffic volume, and average saturation during peak hours.

(1) Node Degree.

The node degree of a road network refers to the number of edges connected to a node, which is composed of out-degree and in-degree. Conversely, the traffic volume of the urban road network during non-peak hours is relatively small, and traffic congestion does not occur. Therefore, to improve the efficiency of traffic operation and reduce traffic congestion, studies should be focused on peak hours. Compared with other intersections, if an intersection with a large average traffic volume during peak hours fails or develops traffic congestion, it will have a greater impact on the entire road network. Therefore, the intersection with higher traffic volume is more important.

Dynamic evaluation index

Contrary to the static evaluation index, the dynamic evaluation index mainly reflects the influence of the change of the node operation state on the road network operation state. The traditional efficiency evaluation model reflecting the impact of node changes on the network is as follows:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{array}{c}E=\frac{1}{n\left(n-1\right)}\sum_{i\ne j} \frac{1}{{d}_{ij}}\end{array}$$\end{document}

In the equation, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{d}}_{\mathrm{i}\mathrm{j}}$$\end{document} is the shortest path length from node i to node j in the network.

As observed, with an increase in the number of network nodes, the influence of node changes on the network cannot be properly distinguished with the efficiency evaluation model. To solve this problem, an impact evaluation model based on topological potential is proposed. Here, the operation state of the node is determined by the saturation reference value, the operation state of the road network is reflected by the topology potential of the road network, and the influence of the change in the operation state of the node on the operation state of the road network is determined using the change value of the operation state of the road network caused by the node switching between different saturation reference values. The above three aspects will be explained in detail.

a) Saturation Reference Value of Node Operating State

Determining the saturation reference value of the operation state of nodes in a road network is the basis for the study on the impact of node operation state changes on the road network. However, most of the current methods are based on norms and experience and are not sufficiently scientific backed. Owing to the differences in urban spatial layout, road structure, traffic source distribution, and residents’ travel habits, the changing trends of traffic operation state between different cities also differ. Therefore, when studying the impact of changes in node operating status on the road network, the changing trend of the average saturation of the nodes in the urban road network under study should be determined first.

Because the urban residents’ travel time is mainly around the commute time (on and off school time), a method to determine the saturation reference value based on the regularity of residents’ travel time is proposed in this study. The specific steps are as follows:

Step 1: the peak period (7:00–9:00 and 17:30–19:00) is selected and the value of the saturation level of the node at a certain time interval (5 min) is investigated and determined;

Step 2: taking each time interval during peak hours as the horizontal axis, and taking the average saturation of each node in each time interval as the vertical axis, the time sequence diagram of the average saturation change of road network nodes is drawn.

Step 3: The unimpeded and slightly congested saturation reference values of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\alpha}_{i0}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\alpha}_{i1}$$\end{document}, respectively, of the node are determined according to the principle of "change in growth rate."

To study the impact of changes in the node operating state on the traffic operation of the road network, after the saturation reference value of the road network node operating state is determined, the representation and evaluation method of the road network operating state should be clarified.

b) Evaluation of Road Network Operation State

The concept of particle topological potential in the microscopic world is introduced to better represent the operation state of the road network, treating the nodes of the road network as microscopic particles, and using the sum of the topological potential values of all nodes to evaluate the operating state of the road network.

(4) Evaluation Model of Road Network Operation State.

The traffic congestion of a road network node is successively spread to its adjacent nodes. This has an impact on the traffic operation of the entire road network. That is, the change in the congestion situation of a local intersection not only changes its topological potential but also influences the topological potential of other intersections in the road network. To scientifically evaluate the impact of changes in the operating state of road network nodes on the road network, according to the definition of the network topology potential field, an evaluation model for the overall operating level of road network nodes is proposed, as shown in (18).11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{array}{c}\varphi =\sum_{i=1}^{n} \varphi \left({\nu}_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \left[{m}_{j}\times {e}^{-(\frac{{d}_{j\to i}}{\sigma }{)}^{2}}\right]\end{array}$$\end{document}

After the issues on the grading of node congestion level and evaluation of road network operation state are solved, the impact of node operation state changes on the road network operation state can be studied.

c) Evaluation of the influence of node operation state change on road network operation state

In part a) of the Dynamic Evaluation Index, urban traffic operational status is categorized into four states: unimpeded, slightly congested, congested, and severely congested. Considering that the transition of traffic status is a progressive process, theoretically, there should be six scenarios of status changes (unimpeded to slightly congested, slightly congested to congested, congested to severely congested, severely congested to congested, congested to slightly congested, and slightly congested to unimpeded). In practical traffic management, these six scenarios should be investigated separately to identify the critical nodes for each situation in a targeted manner. Due to manuscript space limitations, and to demonstrate the impact of bidirectional status changes (e.g., unimpeded to slightly congested and slightly congested to unimpeded) as well as different scenarios of same-direction changes (e.g., unimpeded to slightly congested and congested to severely congested) on the identification of critical nodes, three specific transitions (unimpeded to slightly congested, slightly congested to unimpeded, and congested to severely congested) were selected for illustration.

Evaluation index weight

The impact of evaluation indexes on the evaluation of node importance has its own emphasis. Therefore, if the importance of nodes is to be reasonably evaluated, the calibration of each index weight factor is particularly critical. Combination weights have been favored in recent years owing to their combination of the advantages of subjective and objective weights. However, owing to different principles, in the face of the same evaluation issue, the evaluation results obtained by different combined weighting methods also differ, which leads to difficulties in decision-making. To solve this problem, an integrated weighting method based on a multi-objective programming model is proposed. First, the G1 (order relationship method) and the CRITIC methods are selected to determine the subjective and objective weights of the evaluation indexes, respectively. Second, the product method and the maximum deviation sum of squares method are selected to integrate the subjective and objective weights, respectively, and then the combined weights of the evaluation indexes are determined. Finally, based on the combined weights and the multi-objective programming model, the final weights of the evaluation indexes are obtained. The solution to the multi-objective nonlinear programming problem is a difficult problem, and PSO has been used in solving it. PSO is characterized by fewer parameters, fast convergence speed, high precision, and easy implementation. Standard PSO27,28 is introduced to solve the multi-objective programming model of the integrated weighting method proposed in this study.

Influence and determination of parameters in the PSO algorithm

When using the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm to solve the integrated weighting method based on a multi-objective programming model, the determination of relevant parameters is particularly important. The population size (Ns) determines the algorithm’s global search capability and convergence speed; a larger population enhances global search, but increases the computational cost of each iteration, whereas a smaller population may converge faster but is prone to local optima. The acceleration factors c1 and c2 control the individual (cognitive) and social components, respectively; an excessively high c1 may lead to over-dispersion, and an excessively high c2 may cause premature convergence. The maximum number of iterations (N) directly impacts computation time; a value that is too small may result in a non-optimal outcome, while a value that is too large increases unnecessary computational burden. To determine reasonable parameter values, one can initially set the range for each parameter based on relevant literature, and then analyze and determine the values experimentally using a combinatorial comparison approach, with the convergence speed and final value of the fitness function as evaluation metrics.

Conclusion

Starting from the actual needs of evaluating the importance of nodes in a road network by considering the dynamic index of changes in the operating state of road networks caused by changes in the operating state of nodes, an evaluation index system integrating static and dynamic indicators is constructed. The selection of evaluation indexes, data acquisition methods, index weight determination, node importance sorting, node grading, and other contents have been studied in depth. The relevant methods and models improved the scientificity and rationality of the evaluation results of the importance of nodes in a road network and can be used as the basis for decision making by traffic management departments on traffic operation management. The main conclusions are as follows:This paper pioneeringly proposes a node importance calculation paradigm that fuses structure and attributes, performing node importance evaluation and grading while simultaneously maintaining network structural constraints and integrating multi-dimensional attributes. This "structure-attribute-decision" three-layer coupling mechanism is unattainable by any existing single method or simple integration.The change in the operation state of road networks caused by a change in the operation state of a node was included in this study; the evaluation index system comprising static indexes such as the road network node degree, clustering coefficient, betweenness, average traffic volume during peak hours/average saturation during peak hours, and dynamic index of the changes in the operation state the road network caused by the change in the operation state of nodes was constructed and five different evaluation index schemes were provided.To better obtain the evaluation data of dynamic indexes, first, a method to determine the saturation reference value based on residents’ travel time rule was proposed, which provided a numerical basis to reflect the operation status of nodes. Second, as regards the problems existing in the traditional efficiency evaluation model, an impact evaluation model based on topological potential was proposed and a ternary iterative algorithm was introduced to calculate the impact factor of the model. This model overcame the failure of the original model with an increase in the number of nodes. The changes in the operating state of road networks caused by the changes in the operating states of different nodes were effectively evaluated and distinguished by this model. Third, based on the two methods and models mentioned above, combined with three different node operating state changes, an algorithm to obtain dynamic indexes was proposed.Aiming at the problems existing in the current index weighting methods, an integrated weighting method based on multi-objective programming was proposed and the standard PSO was introduced to solve these problems. Three objective functions were used in the method to ensure its index weight and node sorting results were significantly better than other combined weight methods; the problem of inconsistent node sorting results caused by different principles of combined weight methods was solved and the decision making of importance sorting of nodes in a road network was effectively supported.The influence of different evaluation indexes on the evaluation of node importance was analyzed and compared. Meanwhile, to effectively integrate the node sorting results of different index schemes, a node sorting algorithm based on multiple sorting results was proposed. Limitations and future work

In the process of determining the impact of changes in node operating status on changes in the operating status of the road network, the node operating status change propagation model and algorithm adopted in this study remain relatively simple. In future work, they will be further optimized by incorporating node traffic management and control strategies, the comprehensive attributes of road sections adjacent to the nodes, and residents’ travel OD data passing through the nodes.
