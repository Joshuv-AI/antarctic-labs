Extended sleeve lobectomy after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy: postoperative outcomes differ by surgical side
Authors: Jialiang Wen, Yuzhou Wang, Tao Wang, Tao Chen, Yichen Dong, Jingyu Chen, Long Xu, Jiajun Deng, Yunlang She, Junqi Wu, Lei Jiang, Diego Gonzalez-Rivas, Xuefei Hu, Chang Chen, Chenlu Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCentrally located non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) has traditionally been managed by pneumonectomy (PN), while sleeve lobectomy has emerged as a parenchyma-sparing alternative. With neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy now standard for locally advanced NSCLC, the role of extended sleeve lobectomy in this setting remains unclear. The study evaluated whether extended sleeve lobectomy (ESL) could bring benefits to the NSCLC patients eligible for the procedure after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy.MethodsThree hundred and thirty-five centrally located NSCLC patients who underwent sleeve lobectomy or PN following neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy in Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital were retrospectively reviewed. The population was divided into three groups: the ESL, the simple sleeve lobectomy (SSL), and the PN groups. Short-term perioperative outcomes, event-free survival (EFS), and overall survival were compared among the three groups in the overall, left-sided, and right-sided cohorts, respectively.ResultsIn the overall cohort, baseline characteristics showed no significant differences among the three groups, except for the surgical side (P<0.001). The ESL group had prolonged operation time, longer drainage time, and better EFS relative to the PN group. Subsequently, the effectiveness of surgical procedures was evaluated on the left and right sides, respectively. On the right, the complication rate of the PN group is higher than in the ESL and SSL groups (P=0.054). In contrast, for left-sided procedures, no significant difference in complication rates was observed among the three groups (P=0.49). Multivariable logistic regression showed that, on the right side, ESL was independently associated with lower odds of postoperative complications compared with PN (P=0.02), whereas no significant association was noted on the left side. Comparable EFS was observed between the ESL and SSL groups on both sides. Compared to the PN group, patients in the ESL group showed a favorable EFS trend (P=0.06) on the right side, whereas no significant difference (P=0.50) was observed on the left side.ConclusionsESL might bring benefits to patients with NSCLC on the right surgical side after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy compared to PN, while the advantage was not obvious when operating on the left side.Keywords: Sleeve lobectomy, extended sleeve lobectomy, neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy, perioperative outcome, prognosis

Highlight box

Key findings

• Extended sleeve lobectomy (ESL) is safe and feasible following neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy.

• Right-sided ESL was associated with better short-term outcomes and a favorable event-free survival trend compared with pneumonectomy (PN) after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy, while the benefit of left-sided ESL was limited.

• Simple sleeve lobectomy conferred survival benefits over PN on both sides.

What is known and what is new?

• Sleeve lobectomy is an alternative to PN for centrally located non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy improves prognosis.

• This study provides the first side-stratified analysis suggesting that the benefit of ESL over PN might be predominantly driven by right-sided procedures. Left-sided ESL offers a limited advantage.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Our findings highlight the importance of side-specific considerations in surgical planning for centrally located NSCLC after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy. The potential benefits of lung-preserving procedures may vary by surgical side, suggesting that procedure selection should be individualized based on tumor status, extent of parenchymal preservation achievable, and side-specific risk profiles.

Introduction

Centrally located non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), located within the inner side of the hemithorax and often invading bronchial openings and the pulmonary artery, was considered resectable only by pneumonectomy (PN). However, with the pursuit of minimally invasive surgery and the development of surgeons’ skills, the sleeve lobectomy has become an important alternative procedure gradually (1-3). Since then, surgeons have tried to avoid PN to some extent due to the higher rates of postoperative complications and mortality after the procedure (4). Based on the simple sleeve lobectomy (SSL), the extended sleeve lobectomy (ESL) was performed to treat the centrally located NSCLC involving more than one lobe. Although previous studies (5-7) indicated that ESL could also bring better survival outcomes than PN, it is a more technically challenging procedure compared to the SSL, with a greater bronchial caliber difference and increased anastomotic tension.

Neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy has been included in the standard treatment regimen, as it improves the postoperative prognosis of patients with locally advanced NSCLC according to the phase III clinical trials (8,9). Furthermore, it might increase the likelihood of resection or preserved lung parenchyma, as tumor regression was observed in many patients. Thus, SSL or ESL is likely to gain wider application in the era of immunotherapy. Understanding whether ESL can serve as a safe and effective alternative to PN—particularly in the setting of neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy—is therefore of paramount clinical importance. Prior works (10,11) have suggested the feasibility and safety of both SSL and ESL even after neoadjuvant immunotherapy. However, the sample sizes of these studies were relatively small. Meanwhile, it was indicated that the survival advantages of ESL were more evident for right-sided central NSCLC. Considering that neoadjuvant immunotherapy and preserving more lung parenchyma represent the prevailing trends in lung cancer surgery, this study intended to further compare the postoperative outcomes among SSL, ESL and PN following chemo-immunotherapy, especially focusing on the difference in this effect between the left and right sides of the surgery. We hypothesized that ESL would confer superior short- and long-term outcomes compared to PN, but that this benefit would be more pronounced on the right side. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2026-0455/rc).

Methods

Study design

This is a retrospective, single-center cohort study. Patients receiving sleeve lobectomy or pneumonectomy for NSCLC after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy between August 2019 and December 2024 at the Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital were identified retrospectively. The inclusion criteria were: (I) pathologically confirmed NSCLC; (II) centrally located tumors; (III) receipt of at least two cycles of neoadjuvant PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor combined with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy; (IV) subsequent receipt of sleeve lobectomy or pneumonectomy. Exclusion criteria were N3 diseases, distant metastasis, carinal reconstruction, preoperative radiotherapy, and lack of detailed operative reports or clinical staging information. Clinical, pathological, and perioperative data were extracted from electronic medical records. Tumor staging was assessed with the 8th AJCC TNM system (12) before and after neoadjuvant therapy. The exposure of interest was the surgical procedure, categorized as ESL, SSL, or PN. The primary outcomes were postoperative complications (graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification) and event-free survival (EFS; defined as the time from treatment to disease recurrence or death from any cause). Secondary outcomes included overall survival (OS; defined as the time from treatment to death from any cause) and short-term perioperative and pathological outcomes. Follow-up data were collected until June, 2025. Imaging assessments were performed every three months for the first two years and every six months thereafter to monitor for disease recurrence or progression. Overall comparisons among the three surgical groups were performed as the initial analytical step. Given the known differences in perioperative outcomes between left- and right-sided procedures, the central focus of this study was the pre-specified stratification by surgical side. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the institutional review board of Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital (No. K25-493-2; April 2025). Written consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of de-identified data.

Surgical technique

Our previous studies described the surgical details for bronchial sleeve lobectomy (13,14). At the start of the operation, the surgeon first examined for pleural metastasis and extensive pleural adhesion. Then the surgical approach was further assessed based on thoracic cavity exploration combined with pre-treatment imaging. Fissures and pulmonary vessels were dissected according to the principle of simplicity to complication, while the bronchus was treated last. Systematic lymphadenectomy, as defined by prior studies (15), was routinely performed. Minimally invasive surgery was usually prioritized. Conversion to thoracotomy was warranted in cases of severe lymph node calcification, thoracoscopic dissection difficulty, or massive intraoperative bleeding. Anastomotic wrapping was decided based on the surgeon’s preference. Excessive tension and air leakage were carefully avoided during anastomosis. The operator tested the air leakage under saline at an airway pressure of 25–30 cmH2O and introduced the bronchoscopy to evaluate the anastomosis and clear the airway secretion. The ESL procedure was defined as an atypical bronchial sleeve lobectomy, removing more than one lobe. According to previous research (5,11), it can be divided into four types: Type A: the right upper lobe with the middle lobe; Type B: the left upper lobe with segment 6; Type C: the left lower lobe with segment 4/5; Type D: the right lower lobe with the middle lobe.

At Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital, the choice of surgical procedure was guided by a structured but individualized decision-making process. All patients were evaluated preoperatively by a multidisciplinary team, and surgical plans were primarily based on pre-neoadjuvant tumor status, preoperative baseline functional condition, combined with intraoperative findings. Functional operability was assessed based on predicted postoperative pulmonary function, with a minimum requirement of forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) ≥40% of predicted and diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide ≥40% of predicted, with adjustments for individual patient status. The decision algorithm prioritized lung parenchymal preservation whenever oncologically feasible. SSL was preferred for tumors confined to one lobe; ESL was considered when tumor involvement extended beyond a single lobe but remained amenable to bronchial or vascular reconstruction. In addition to these objective criteria, the surgeon’s preference, expertise and judgment—particularly regarding the degree of hilar fibrosis, fissure completeness, and anastomotic tension—also played a critical role in the final decision.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges and compared by Mann-Whitney U, while categorical variables were presented as numbers (percentages) and compared by Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test. To address potential bias, we enrolled consecutive patients, used standardized outcome assessments blinded to procedure type when feasible, and performed side-stratified analyses and multivariable analyses to control confounding. The study size was determined by the total number of consecutive cases meeting eligibility criteria within the predefined timeframe. The statistically significant independent predictors of postoperative complications were identified through logistic regression. Surgical approach and factors with P value ≤0.2 from the univariate logistic analysis were included in the multivariable logistic model. Survival outcomes were compared through the adjusted Kaplan-Meier method. Patients who were lost to follow-up were censored at the time of their last known contact in survival analyses. The Cox model was used to conduct a pairwise comparison of survival among different surgical procedures. All statistical analyses were performed with R software (version 4.5.1).

Perioperative outcomes

Analyses of the overall cohort are presented in Table S4, with pairwise intergroup comparisons summarized in Table S5. The ESL required longer operation time compared to PN, while similar operation time compared to SSL. Patients receiving ESL and SSL had lower rates of postoperative blood transfusion (10.0% and 9.6% vs. 23.1%, P=0.01) and overall complications (30.0% and 29.1% vs. 46.2%, P=0.03) than those undergoing PN. Clavien-Dindo classification also varied significantly (P=0.02), with fewer severe complications observed in ESL and SSL. Significant intergroup differences existed for 30-day (P=0.03) and 90-day mortality (P<0.001). The SSL group achieved the lowest 30- and 90-day mortality; ESL numerically outperformed PN without statistical significance. Hospitalization and drainage duration also differed globally (P<0.001): PN required longer hospital stays, and ESL had prolonged chest tube drainage relative to PN (P=0.03). No intergroup gaps were found in incomplete resection or major pathological response.

Side-stratified analyses were performed and separately presented in Table 2 (left-sided tumors) and Table 3 (right-sided tumors), with subgroup pairwise comparisons listed in Tables S6,S7. Distinct subgroup patterns of ESL were identified by tumor side. On the right side, ESL showed consistent favorable trends versus PN, including fewer complications and lower short-term mortality. On the left side, no protective trend of ESL was observed; the only significant finding was substantially longer operative time for ESL compared with PN. Due to the limited sample sizes in unilateral subgroups, the results should be interpreted cautiously.

Survival data

The median follow-up for the overall population was 22.1 months (21.2 months for left-sided and 22.4 months for right-sided procedures). In the overall cohort, ESL showed comparable EFS to SSL and a favorable trend over PN (Figure S1A,S1B).

Side-stratified survival analyses are shown in Figure 2A,2B (right side) and Figure 3A,3B (left side). On the right side, ESL achieved EFS comparable to SSL and showed a favorable trend compared with PN (P=0.06). Multivariable Cox regression also showed this protective trend for ESL versus PN (HR =0.27, P=0.07), alongside major pathological response (MPR) as a significant independent protective factor (Table S8). On the left side, ESL did not demonstrate an EFS advantage over PN (HR =0.63, P=0.54 after multivariable adjustment). SSL showed a better OS trend compared with PN on both sides.

Discussion

Considering the high difficulty of the sleeve lobectomy and procedures derived from it after neoadjuvant immunotherapy, many studies (10,11,16) have been conducted to evaluate whether these procedures can improve prognosis through avoiding PN. However, there is a lack of stratified analysis identifying the specific population that can benefit from complex sleeve lobectomies. Our study suggested that right-sided ESL may offer advantages compared to PN, while the benefit of left-sided ESL appeared limited. Otherwise, patients receiving SSL had better survival outcomes than those receiving PN on either side.

In the treatment-naive population, surgeons seemed to be more inclined to perform an extended sleeve resection for tumors on the right side. In multiple previous studies (5,7,17), right-sided ESL accounted for approximately 70% of all ESL. In our cohort, right-sided ESL accounted for 67.5% of all ESL cases, which is consistent with these prior reports. This phenomenon is unlikely to be explained by the greater technical challenges associated with left-sided procedures. Left-sided ESL, especially type B ESL, was thought to be relatively easy due to the convenience of controlling the pulmonary artery trunk, lower tension and smaller differences for bronchial anastomosis caliber (18). In clinical practice, the value of left-sided ESL was questioned in certain cases where only a limited amount of lung parenchyma could be preserved. However, this viewpoint is mainly based on subjective judgment rather than evidence-based rationale, which remains blank to the best of our knowledge.

From another perspective, it was believed that the left-sided PN was a safer procedure compared with the right-sided PN. In the early years, Simón and his colleagues (19) observed the less pronounced adverse impact of PN on cardiorespiratory function and proposed that comparing PN with lesser resections, like bronchoplastic procedures, should differentiate between right and left procedures. The survival discrepancy of PN between different surgical sides was further validated through several studies (20,21) in the context of induction therapy. Perioperative mortality and risk of severe complications, such as bronchopleural fistula, were higher for right-sided PN, despite similar long-term outcomes. The risk could be attributed to the increased load on the right ventricle, the larger mass of the right lung, the greater threat of lymphatic outflow obstruction, and so on. In line with this, our data showed that right-sided PN was associated with a higher complication rate compared with right-sided ESL and SSL, with arrhythmia being the most prominent contributor. This finding corroborates the physiological mechanism that right-sided PN imposes greater hemodynamic burden and is in proximity to the sinoatrial node (22). In contrast, on the left side, complication rates were comparable among the three groups.

Neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy has been strongly recommended for the treatment of patients with locally advanced NSCLC (23). As a result, we believe that studying surgical procedures in the setting of preoperative chemo-immunotherapy is more relevant to the real world. The study by Chen et al. (11) indicated that ESL after chemo-immunotherapy was feasible and improved survival outcomes compared to PN in an overall cohort. In their study, the type of surgery was determined based on the post-therapy staging examination, which meant that treatment response could affect the surgical method. The researchers observed that neoadjuvant therapy enabled the transition from PN to ESL, mainly for right-sided tumors, and most patients receiving ESL might not tolerate PN due to the poor predicted postoperative lung function, suggesting that the advantages of ESL were attributable to protecting lung function. Nevertheless, our side-stratified analysis revealed that the ESL benefit over PN was predominantly driven by right-sided procedures: right-sided ESL achieved EFS comparable to SSL and showed a favorable trend over PN, whereas left-sided ESL showed no such advantage. In contrast, surgeons in Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital adopted a more conservative surgery strategy. Decisions on the type of surgery were made according to the pre-neoadjuvant therapy condition. Therefore, our study was relatively less influenced by tumor response, and the survival outcomes were more determined by the surgery. Higher FEV1 and FEV1% of predicted in the PN group could also point to a similar view to Chen et al. (11) that ESL improved long-term outcome by protecting lung function, thus surgeons tended to select patients with relatively poorer lung function as candidates for ESL. Regarding the efficacy of perioperative immunotherapy, the potential oncologic advantage of PN with local greater resection might become less evident, but tumor response was what mattered more instead. In our multivariable Cox analysis, MPR emerged as an independent protective factor for EFS, reinforcing the critical role of treatment response in determining prognosis, irrespective of the surgical procedure.

The inflammatory and fibrotic changes induced by immunotherapy can lead to dense hilar fibrosis, obliterated tissue planes, and thickened bronchial and vascular structures, which considerably complicate dissection and anastomosis. Bott et al.(24) reported a conversion rate from minimally invasive surgery to thoracotomy as high as 54%, with inflammation and dense adhesions being the predominant causes. Similarly, Chaft et al. (25) and Toker et al. (26) highlighted the technical difficulties posed by hilar fibrosis and altered anatomy after checkpoint inhibitors. The favorable perioperative outcomes observed in our ESL and SSL groups—despite these inherent difficulties—reflect not only the feasibility of these procedures but also the high level of surgical expertise required. All procedures in this cohort were performed by senior thoracic surgeons with extensive experience in sleeve resection, and our results should be interpreted in this context.

It is also noteworthy that SSL brought benefits to both sides. Despite the bias from different oncologic baselines, it still indicated that lung-sparing surgery was effective in improving prognosis even on the left surgical side. The insignificant advantages of left-sided ESL appear to reflect insufficient parenchymal preservation rather than a lack of benefit to preserving left-sided lung parenchyma. Considering many institutions determine the surgery procedures based on post-treatment condition, neoadjuvant immunotherapy may offer new opportunities by shrinking the tumor. It was recommended by previous consensus (23) that PN should be avoided if patients achieved a major pathological response. Some patients demanding ESL or even PN before immunotherapy only require SSL according to the examination results after neoadjuvant immunotherapy. More aggressively, a recent study (27) also reported that lobectomy might be considered for patients who could achieve a complete pathological response after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy. Such studies indicated that, when PN could be avoided, surgical procedures that reserve sufficient lung parenchyma should be considered as an option. However, there is currently insufficient evidence to support this. We are looking forward to larger-scale studies or prospective clinical trials to validate these views.

Elderly patients warrant specific consideration. Although chronologic age is often perceived as a relative contraindication to complex sleeve resections, previous studies have demonstrated that such procedures can be performed safely with acceptable outcomes in patients aged ≥70 years (28). Our results, with a median age of approximately 62–63 years across groups, support the view that parenchymal-sparing surgery remains viable in appropriately selected older patients when based on physiologic rather than chronologic age.

Taken together, our findings extended the current literature in several respects. First, while prior studies have suggested that ESL is a viable alternative to PN after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy, our side-stratified analysis showed that this benefit might not be uniform across surgical sides. Second, the comparable EFS between ESL and SSL on the right side suggested that ESL does not compromise oncological radicality while achieving the goal of avoiding PN, which is valuable given the prohibitive risks associated with right-sided PN. Third, the absence of a clear ESL benefit on the left side highlighted the need for more nuanced preoperative decision-making, taking into account both the extent of parenchymal preservation achievable and the baseline risk of the alternative procedure. These observations underscored the importance of side-specific considerations in surgical planning for centrally located NSCLC after neoadjuvant therapy.

There are several limitations in our study. First, it was a single-institution retrospective study. The representativeness of the results still needs to be proven with more data from other centers. Due to the retrospective nature of the data, some postoperative events, such as atelectasis, were not comprehensively captured. Second, a limited number of cases were enrolled, especially for left-sided ESL and right-sided PN, although our sample size is the largest compared with previous literature. Third, selection bias is the major limitation. One particular concern is that the low proportion of right-sided PN may reflect surgeons’ preference to avoid this procedure, potentially reserving it for patients with more advanced tumors, although baseline characteristics did not reveal obvious differences between the two groups. Moreover, many considerations that influence surgical decision-making, such as the specific tumor location and anastomotic difficulty, could not be quantified. Fourth, the study period substantially overlapped with the COVID-19 pandemic, which may have influenced referral patterns, diagnostic delays, and operating room capacity, potentially affecting patient selection and treatment timing. Finally, the average follow-up time of less than two years is insufficient to draw definitive conclusions about long-term survival. A longer follow-up time is needed.

Conclusions

Our study suggested that, after neoadjuvant immunotherapy, ESL might be correlated with better short-term outcomes and survival compared to PN in the overall and right-sided cohorts, but in the left-sided cohort, the benefit might be limited.
