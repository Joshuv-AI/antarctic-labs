Abstract
. Utilising recent advances in incidence geometry for balls and tubes, and advances in sum-product theory in the discrete setting, we show that for 0 < s ≤ 1 / 2 0<s\leq 1/2 and for any A ⊂ ℝ A\subset\mathbb{R} with Hausdorff dimension s , s, either the upper-box dimension of A ​ A , AA, or the lower-box dimension of A + A A+A must be at least 29 ​ s / 23 . 29s/23. We obtain the slightly better bound of 33 ​ s / 26 33s/26 when we replace the sum-set with the smoother difference-set.



1.
Introduction

The Erdős–Volkmann ring problem
[
EV66
]
asks whether there are any (Borel) subrings of
\mathbb{R}
which do not have Hausdorff dimension
0
or
1.
In
[
Fal85
]
Falconer showed (as a corollary of progress on the distance problem) that there are no Borel subrings of Hausdorff dimension strictly between
1/2
and
1.
Motivated by the distance problem (and other problems) in
[
KT01
]
Katz and Tao suggested the discretised ring problem, which is a more quantitative version of ring problem. They asked that, for a discrete set
A,
which at scale
\delta
resembles a set of Hausdorff dimension
1/2,
to show that there exists a uniform
c_{0}>0
so that

N_{\delta}({A+A})+N_{\delta}({AA})>\delta^{-1/2-c_{0}}.

This was resolved in
[
Bou03
]
by Bourgain. Independently, using a completely different method, Edgar and Miller
[
EM03
]
found a clever proof of the (Erdős–Volkmann) ring problem.

It remains open as to how large the constant
c_{0}
ought to be. In terms of fractal dimension, the discretised ring problem may ask for
A\subset\mathbb{R}
with Hausdorff dimension
0<s<1
how large

(1.1)

\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}((A+A)\times AA)

or

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}((A+A)\times AA)

would have to be.

When
0<s\leq 2/3
the work of
[
RW23
]
combined with the argument of Elekes
[
Ele97
]
show that both are bounded below by
5s/2.
We are not able to make any improvement of this quantity in this note, however, we remark that by product formulae the quantity (
1.1
) may be bounded above by both

(1.2)

\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(A+A)}+\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{AA},

and

(1.3)

\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(A+A)}+\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{AA}.

Focussing our attention on (
1.2
), the lower bound of
5s/2
tells us that

(1.4)

\max\{\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(AA)},\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(A+A)}\}\geq 5s/4.

The aim of this note is to improve this lower bound to
29s/23
when
0<s\leq 1/2.
It was obtained (using a different technique) in
[
DO25
]
that

(1.5)

\max\{\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(A/A)},\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(A+A)}\}\geq 43s/34,

for
0<s\leq 1/2.

It is unclear what the correct lower bound ought to be. In the discrete case, for
A\subset\mathbb{R}
it is conjectured that for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
c>0
so that

|A+A|+|AA|>c|A|^{2-\epsilon}.

In the fractal case, certainly the improvement cannot be larger than
\min\{s,1-s\}.
Further, in
[
MO25
]
there is an example showing that the increase cannot be larger that
(1-s)/2.
When
s
is small, it is unclear whether to expect an improvement of
s.

For all
0<s<1
there is an example
A\subset\mathbb{R}
in
[
MO25
]
with

\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}(AA)=\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}(A+A)=s,

and so (
1.4
) cannot be improved much in the sense of replacing
\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}
with a finer definition of dimension.

As previously mentioned, the results presented here may be viewed as companion results to
[
DO25
, Theorem 1.10 and Theorem 1.11]
. There, similar results were obtained with
AA
replaced with
A/A
and
A-A
replaced with
A+A,
and with
33/26
replaced by the slightly worse
43/34.
The reader is invited to view
[
DO25
, Subsection 1.4]
for the results presented there.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<s\leq 1/2.
Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfy
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}A=s.
Then

(1.6)

\max\{\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(AA)},\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(A+A)}\}\geq 29s/23.

(1.7)

\max\{\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(AA)},\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}{(A-A)}\}\geq 33s/26.

Theorem
1.1
will follow from discretised versions below. The deduction is more-or-less identical to the one presented in
[
DO25
]
so we omit this here.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<s\leq 1/2
. There exists
C,\delta_{0},\epsilon>0
so that the following holds for all
0<\delta<\delta_{0}.

Let
A\subset[1/2,1]
be a
(\delta,s,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set. Then one of the following must hold.

(1.8)

N_{{\rho}}({AA})>\rho^{-29s/23}\text{ for some }\delta<\rho<\delta^{C\epsilon},

(1.9)

N_{\delta}({A+A})>\delta^{-29s/23+C\epsilon}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<s\leq 1/2
. There exists
C,\delta_{0},\epsilon>0
so that the following holds for all
0<\delta<\delta_{0}.

Let
A\subset[1/2,1]
be a
(\delta,s,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set. Then one of the following must hold.

(1.10)

N_{{\rho}}({AA})>\rho^{-33s/26}\text{ for some }\delta<\rho<\delta^{C\epsilon},

(1.11)

N_{\delta}({A-A})>\delta^{-33s/26+C\epsilon}.

Note that
29/23=1.26086...,
and
33/26=1.26923...

The main key ingredient is the recent Szémeredi–Trotter strength incidence inequality for balls and tubes.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0<s\leq d\leq 1
. Set
\sigma=\min\{s+d,2-s-d\}.

Assume
\mathcal{T}
is a
(\delta,s,d,K_{1},K_{2})
-quasi-product set. Consider a shading
Y
of
\mathcal{T}
such that
Y(T)
is a
(\delta,\sigma,K_{3})
-KT set for each
T\in\mathcal{T}
. Then we have

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{T},Y)\lessapprox K_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}(K_{1}K_{2})^{2/3}(\delta^{-s-d}\#\mathcal{T})^{1/{3}}\#Y(\mathcal{T})^{2/3}.

Here a shading
Y
is an assignment of a distinct collection of
\delta
-balls to each
T\in\mathcal{T}.
We write

Y(\mathcal{T})=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}Y(T).

We write

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{T},Y)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{B\in Y(\mathcal{T})}1_{B\cap T\neq\emptyset}1_{B\in Y(T)}.

The method to obtain Theorem
1.3
is quite different from the one in
[
DO25
]
, although both rely on Theorem
1.4
.
Here, we combine ideas present in sum-product literature in the discrete case.
We sketch the main ideas below, focusing on the difference-set case. The sum-set case follows
using standard modifications.

We define the
\delta
-
discretised
3
-energy
of two sets
A,B
by

(1.12)

\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,B)=\sum_{z\in\delta\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\sum_{(a,b)\in A\times B}1_{|a-b-z|\leq\delta}\Big)^{3}.

If
A=B
then we write
\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A)=\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,A).

We let
t\geq s
be the ‘discrete Hausdorff dimension’ of
A-A.

The strategy can be broken into two distinct parts.
First, we use Theorem
1.4
to upper bound
\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,A-A)
and
\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A)
in terms of

\#A,N_{\delta}({A-A}),N_{\delta}({AA})\text{ and }\delta^{-t}.

Second, we find a lower bound for some multiplicative combination of
N_{\delta}({A-A})
,
N_{\delta}({AA})
,
\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A)
, and
\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,A-A)

using a Cauchy-Schwarz argument. This framework is present in the discrete sum-product literature,
where Theorem
1.4
is now playing the role of the Szemeredi-Trotter theorem. See,
for example,
[
SS11
]
,
[
RS22
]
,
[
Blo25
]
, and
[
Cus25
]
.

For the lower bound, we follow
[
Cus25
]
quite closely.
The idea is to produce many solutions to the equation
p_{1}-p_{2}=p_{3}+O(\delta)
, where the
p_{i}

lie in a ‘popular’ separated subset of
A-A
. However, a difficulty arises in our applications
as one can not use the natural definition for the popular set. Indeed,
the energy estimates obtained using Theorem
1.4
require the popular set
to be more refined.
To handle this, we introduce two notions of popular set. We have a refined set
D_{\text{pop}}

which is suitable for our estimates obtained using Theorem
1.4
, and a weaker set
P
,
which is chosen so that
\pi^{-1}(P)
captures almost all pairs
A\times A
, where
\pi(x,y)=x-y
is
the difference map. We note that
P
is not
fit for use in Theorem
1.4
, but since we never apply energy estimates to
P
, this does not
prove to be an obstacle.
We produce solutions
to the equation
p_{1}-p_{2}=p_{3}+O(\delta)
where
p_{1},p_{2}\in D_{\text{pop}}
and
p_{3}\in P
.
Our lower bound, Lemma
3.6
, follows from a few applications of the Hölder inequality.

Preliminaries

For

\delta

Definition 2.1
.

Let

We say that

N_{\delta}({A\cap B(x,r)})\leq Cr^{s}N_{\delta}({A})\text{ for all }x\in A,r\geq\delta.

-KT-set if

(2.14)

N_{\delta}({A\cap B(x,r)})\leq C(r/\delta)^{s}\text{ for all }x\in A,r\geq\delta.

If
C\approx 1
then we will often drop the
C
from the notation and simply refer to
(\delta,s)
-sets and
(\delta,s)
-KT-sets.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
K_{1},K_{2}\geq 1
and

we call a collection
\mathcal{T}
of
\delta
-tubes a
(\delta,s,d,K_{1},K_{2})
-quasi-product set if its direction set

is a
(\delta,s,K_{1})
-KT set and for each
\theta\in\Lambda
,
\mathcal{T}_{\theta}
is a

-KT set.

The below definition is standard, and can be found at
[

Let

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<s\leq t<1,C_{1},C_{2}\geq 1,
and suppose that
2s\leq 2-t.
Let
A\subset[1/2,1]
and

be
(\delta,s,C_{1})
-KT and
(\delta,t,C_{2})
-KT, respectively. Let
C
be a
\delta
-separated subset of

Then

(3.23)

\#\{(a,b,c)\in A\times B\times C:|c-(a-b)|\lesssim\delta\}\lesssim\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}C_{1}C_{2}^{2/3}\frac{\delta^{-(s+t)/3}N_{\delta}({AA})^{2/3}\#B^{1/3}\#C^{2/3}}{\#A^{2/3}}.

Proof.

We turn this into an incidence problem, as to exploit Theorem
1.4
. We introduce the dummy variable
a\in A
:

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A,b\in B,c\in C}1_{|c-(a^{\prime}-b)|\lesssim\delta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(a^{\prime},c)\in A\times C}\sum_{(a,b)\in A\times B}1_{|c-(a^{\prime}a/a-b)|\lesssim\delta}.

This counts (some of the)
\delta
-incidences between
AA\times C,
and a family of tubes parameterised by
A\times B.
We formalise this below.

Set
\mathcal{P}:=AA\times C
and
\mathcal{L}:=\{l_{a,b}:a\in A,b\in B\},
where

is governed by the equation

(3.25)

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the

-neighbourhoods of these lines. Since the lines are parameterised by

the tubes

form a
(\delta,s,t,C_{1},C_{2})
-(quasi)-product set. Here
A^{-1}=\{1/a:a\in A\},
and the fact that

is a
(\delta,s,C_{1})
-KT-set may be readily checked by observing that the map
x\mapsto x^{-1}
is bi-Lipschitz with constant

when restricted to

Given

with the axial line

These tubes contain the points

So define a shading

Since

Returning to (

The result then following after dividing through by

Step 5:
Energy lower bounds.

We now obtain a lower bound on an algebraic combination of the quantities

\#D,\#D_{\mathrm{pop}},\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A),\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,D_{\mathrm{pop}}).

We follow quite closely the argument of
[

, where

, and

is popular by some weaker notion to be defined shortly.
We then bound from above the number
of solutions to

by some multiplicative combination of the quantities mentioned previously.

We introduce the higher
\delta
-separated energies, which
proves notationally convenient.
For any

, and

-th
\delta
-separated additive energy to be

\mathrm{E}_{k,\delta}(A,B)=\sum_{z\in\delta\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{(a,b)\in A\times B}1_{\left\lvert a-b-z\right\rvert\leq\delta}\right)^{k}.

.
Recall the definition of

Lemma 3.6
.

We have

\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\#A^{\frac{15}{2}}\lesssim\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A)\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,D_{\mathrm{pop}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\#D\#D_{\mathrm{pop}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

Proof.

We will show that the equation
p_{1}-p_{2}=p_{3}+O(\delta)

has many solutions, where the
p_{i}
are some popular differences.
We first define a new set of popular intervals for which the requirement is less strict, call

\mathcal{P}=\left\{I\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\left(D\right):\#\left(\pi^{-1}(I)\cap\left(A\times A\right)\right)\geq\frac{\#A^{2}\delta^{2\epsilon}}{100\#D}\right\}.

Then let
P=D\cap\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{P}}I
.
Note that
P
does not need to be as refined as
D_{\text{pop}}
, because in our eventual applications,
we do not use incidence estimates for any energy involving

We record that

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(D)}\#\left(\pi^{-1}(I)\cap\left(A\times A\right)\right)=\#A^{2}.

By the definition of
\mathcal{P}

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(D)\setminus\mathcal{P}}\#\left(\pi^{-1}(I)\cap(A\times A)\right)<\#\left(\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(D)\setminus\mathcal{P}\right)\cdot\frac{\#A^{2}\delta^{2\epsilon}}{100\#D}\leq\frac{\delta^{2\epsilon}}{100}\cdot\#A^{2},

and hence, subtracting from the sum over

(3.31)

\displaystyle\sum_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\#\left(\pi^{-1}(I)\cap\left(A\times A\right)\right)\geq\#A^{2}\left(1-\frac{\delta^{2\epsilon}}{100}\right).

That is, many pairs
(a_{1},a_{2})
give rise to these popular differences.

We recall that the rich elements

R_{A}=\left\{x\in A:\#\left((x-A)\cap\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(D_{\text{pop}})\right)\geq\delta^{\epsilon}\#A\right\}

\#R_{A}\gtrsim\delta^{\epsilon}\#A
. We count the number of tuples

\left\{(r,a_{1},a_{2})\in R_{A}\times A^{2}:r-a_{1},r-a_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(D_{\text{pop}}),~a_{2}-a_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P)\right\}.

We then map these tuples by the truism

(r-a_{1})-(r-a_{2})=a_{2}-a_{1}

to solutions to
p_{1}-p_{2}=p_{3}+O(\delta)
where
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(D_{\text{pop}})
and
p_{3}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P)
.
For each fixed
r_{0}\in R_{A}
, by the definition of
R_{A}
,

\#\left\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{2}:r_{0}-a_{1},r_{0}-a_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(D_{\text{pop}})\right\}\geq\delta^{2\epsilon}\#A^{2}.

Call the set of all such pairs
S_{1}
.

However, by (
3.31
), the number of pairs
(a_{1},a_{2})
with
a_{2}-a_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P)
is

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\#\left(\pi^{-1}(I)\cap\left(A\times A\right)\right)\geq\#A^{2}\left(1-\frac{\delta^{2\epsilon}}{100}\right).

Call the set of all such pairs
S_{2}
.
We have, by inclusion-exclusion,

\#(S_{1}\cap S_{2})=\#S_{1}+\#S_{2}-\#(S_{1}\cup S_{2}),

and hence

\#(S_{1}\cap S_{2})\geq\delta^{2\epsilon}\#A^{2}+\#A^{2}\left(1-\frac{\delta^{2\epsilon}}{100}\right)-\#A^{2}\gtrsim\delta^{2\epsilon}\#A^{2}.

That is, for any
r_{0}\in R_{A}
,

\#\left\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{2}:r_{0}-a_{1},r_{0}-a_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(D_{\text{pop}}),~a_{2}-a_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P)\right\}\gtrsim\delta^{2\epsilon}\#A^{2}.

Thus, taking a union over all
r\in R_{A}

\#\left\{(r,a_{1},a_{2})\in R_{A}\times A^{2}:r-a_{1},r-a_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(D_{\text{pop}}),~a_{2}-a_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P)\right\}\gtrsim\delta^{3\epsilon}\#A^{3}.

Call
X
the set of all such triples, and map them by

f:(r,a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto(r-a_{1},r-a_{2}).

Call
\mathcal{Y}
the boxes into which

maps

, i.e.

\mathcal{Y}=\left\{(I,J)\in\mathcal{D}^{2}:\left(I-J\right)\cap\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P)\neq\varnothing\right\}.

Noting that

f(X)\subset\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{Y}}B,

we have by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\#X=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{Y}}\sum_{x\in X}1_{f(x)\in B}\leq\#\mathcal{Y}^{\frac{1}{2}}\#\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X^{2}:\left\lvert f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

so substituting our bound on

(3.32)

\delta^{6\epsilon}\#A^{6}\lesssim\#\mathcal{Y}\#\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X^{2}:|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|\lesssim\delta\right\}.

We note that

\#\mathcal{Y}\sim\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}\in D_{\mathrm{pop}}}1_{\left\lvert d_{1}-d_{2}-p\right\rvert\leq\delta}.

Expanding the
p

P

\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in A}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}\in D_{\text{pop}}}1_{\left\lvert a_{2}-a_{1}-p\right\rvert\leq\delta}1_{\left\lvert d_{1}-d_{2}-p\right\rvert\leq\delta}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{2\epsilon}\#A^{2}}{\#D}\cdot\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}\in D_{\mathrm{pop}}}1_{\left\lvert d_{1}-d_{2}-p\right\rvert\leq\delta},

and therefore

\#\mathcal{Y}\lesssim\frac{\#D}{\delta^{2\epsilon}\#A^{2}}\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in A}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}\in D_{\mathrm{pop}}}1_{\left\lvert d_{1}-d_{2}-a_{2}+a_{1}\right\rvert\leq\delta}.

Note that the rightmost sum is

Turning our attention to the remaining term, we write
x_{i}=(r_{i},a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime})
.
we count

We see that
\left\lvert f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta
if and only if

\left\lvert r-a_{1}-(r^{\prime}-a_{1}^{\prime})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta,\qquad\left\lvert r-a_{2}-(r^{\prime}-a_{2}^{\prime})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta,

or rearranging,

\left\lvert r-r^{\prime}-(a_{1}-a_{1}^{\prime})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta,\qquad\left\lvert r-r^{\prime}-(a_{2}-a_{2}^{\prime})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta.

Partitioning the count over values of

,

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{r,r^{\prime}\in R_{A}}\left(\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}1_{\left\lvert r-r^{\prime}-(a-a^{\prime})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{x\in\delta\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}\in R_{A}}1_{\left\lvert r-r^{\prime}-x\right\rvert\leq\delta}\left(\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}1_{\left\lvert x-(a-a^{\prime})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A).

Substituting both of our bounds into (
3.32
), we obtain

\delta^{6\epsilon}\#A^{6}\lesssim\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A)\cdot\frac{\#D}{\delta^{2\epsilon}\#A^{2}}\cdot\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,D_{\text{pop}}).

We use Cauchy-Schwarz to interpolate for
\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,D_{\text{pop}})
, namely we have

\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,D_{\text{pop}})\leq\mathrm{E}_{1,\delta}(A,D_{\text{pop}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,D_{\text{pop}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Noting that

\mathrm{E}_{1,\delta}(A,D_{\text{pop}})=\sum_{x\in\delta\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{d\in D_{\text{pop}}}1_{\left\lvert a-d-x\right\rvert\leq\delta}\lesssim\#A\#D_{\mathrm{pop}},

we obtain

\delta^{6\epsilon}\#A^{6}\lesssim\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A)\cdot\frac{\#D}{\delta^{2\epsilon}\#A^{2}}\cdot\left(\#A\#D_{\mathrm{pop}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,D_{\mathrm{pop}})^{\frac{1}{2}},

and hence by rearranging we obtain the desired statement.
∎

We quickly derive (

The following proposition showcasing the symmetry of

Proposition 4.3
.

For any finite

,

\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,B)\sim\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,-B),

and as a consequence,

\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,B)\gtrsim\frac{\#A^{2}\#B^{2}}{N_{\delta}\left(A+B\right)}.

Proof.

A quick calculation shows

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\delta\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}1_{\left\lvert a-b-z\right\rvert\leq\delta}1_{\left\lvert a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}-z\right\rvert\leq\delta}\sim\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}1_{\left\lvert a-b-a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\right\rvert\leq\delta}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{z\in\delta\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}1_{\left\lvert a+b^{\prime}-z\right\rvert\leq\delta}1_{\left\lvert a^{\prime}+b-z\right\rvert\leq\delta}\sim\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,-B).

For the second part, let
X\subset A-B
be a maximal
\delta
-separated subset.
We see by Cauchy-Schwarz that

\#A\#B\sim\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{(a,b)\in A\times B}1_{\left\lvert a-b-x\right\rvert\leq\delta}\leq\#X^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,B)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and hence by squaring and rearranging

\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,B)\sim\mathrm{E}_{2,\delta}(A,-B)\gtrsim\frac{\#A^{2}\#B^{2}}{N_{\delta}(A+B)}.

∎

To modify our argument to accommodate the sum-set, we use essentially the same idea which is used in the
discrete case.
The issue in passing from the difference-set case to the sum set case

Indeed, we always arrive at something of the form

(a_{1}+a_{2})-(a_{1}+a_{3})=a_{2}-a_{3}.

As such, one

4.3
.
We now map triples
(r_{1},r_{2},a)

,

\mathrm{E}_{7/4,\delta}(R_{1},R_{2})
.
We further refine to an
\epsilon
-uniform subset of

to make use of our energy estimates.

As in the case of the difference-set,

The second notion of popular set is a weaker notion

A quick calculation grants you

From this, we obtain

We choose the rich elements
(r_{1},r_{2})
to be all those whose difference is in our dyadic level.
By the definition of

\Delta\#\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}^{\prime}\leq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}^{\prime}}\#\left(\pi^{-1}(I)\cap(R_{1}\times R_{2})\right)\leq\#\left\{(r_{1},r_{2})\in R_{1}\times R_{2}:r_{1}-r_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P_{\Delta}^{\prime})\right\}.

We then choose all the
a

and
r_{2}+a\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P_{+})

For a fixed pair

by the definition of
R_{1}
,

\#\left\{a\in A:r_{1}+a\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(S_{\text{pop}})\right\}\geq c\cdot\delta^{\epsilon}\#A.

Call the set of all such
a

R_{2}
,

\#\left\{a\in A:r_{2}+a\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(P_{+})\right\}\geq\left(1-\frac{c\delta^{\epsilon}}{2}\right)\#A.

Call the set of all such
a
to be
A_{2}

Using inclusion-exclusion,

\#\left(A_{1}\cap A_{2}\right)=\#A_{1}+\#A_{2}-\#\left(A_{1}\cup A_{2}\right),

and hence

\#\left(A_{1}\cap A_{2}\right)\geq c\delta^{\epsilon}\#A+\#A\left(1-\frac{c\delta^{\epsilon}}{2}\right)-\#A\gtrsim\delta^{\epsilon}\#A.

Taking a union over all such

\#X\gtrsim\delta^{\epsilon}\Delta\#\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}^{\prime}\#A\gtrsim\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\Delta\#\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}\#A.

By a nearly identical argument to that of the difference-set portion,

\#\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X^{2}:\left\lvert f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\right\rvert\lesssim\delta\right\}\lesssim\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A).

Thus, again by Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain

\delta^{O(\epsilon)}\Delta^{2}\#\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}^{2}\#A^{2}\lesssim\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A)\#\mathcal{Y}.

With
\mathcal{Y}
in terms of
P_{\Delta}^{\prime}

term
and then use incidence estimates for the remaining terms.
We see that

Expanding

so eliminating the

We group this as

Combining, we have

\frac{\Delta^{2}\#P_{\Delta}^{2}\#A^{4}}{\#S}\lesssim\delta^{-O(\epsilon)}\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A)\mathrm{E}_{3,\delta}(A,S_{\text{pop}})^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{E}_{\frac{3}{2},\delta}(A,P_{\Delta}^{\prime})^{\frac{2}{3}}.

Recall our energy bound

Interpolating for the
