Planar, rational curves over 𝔽 2 {\mathbb{F}}_{2} , whose only singularity is a double point
János Kollár

Abstract
. We exhibit planar, rational curves of large degree over 𝔽 2 {\mathbb{F}}_{2} that have a unique singular point, which has multiplicity 2. In characteristic 0 such curves exist only for degrees up to 6 6 .



The aim of this note is to construct rational curves

C_{d}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{2}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of degree
d=2^{n}+2
or
d=2^{n}
that have a unique singular point, which has multiplicity 2; see Example
3
.

In characteristic 0 such curves exist only for degrees up to
6
,
see Lemma
4
and Remark
6
.

For
d\geq 8
the curves
C_{d}
cannot be lifted to characteristic 0
in a way that preserves the standard resolution-of-singularities blow-up sequence; see Remark
7
.
This is inconsistent with my understanding of some steps of the program outlined in

[
Ish25
]
; see Remark
7
.
However, these curves do not seem to
shed light on whether the set of
log canonical thresholds depends on the characteristic, which is one of the main topics of
[
Ish25
]
;
see Remark
8
.

A general, planar, rational curve
B_{d}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of degree
d
has

\binom{d-1}{2}
nodes, and it is not easy to obtain examples with few singular points. There seem to be very few curves with only one singular point, which is a cusp.
These appear prominently in the
Abhyankar-Moh-Suzuki theorem describing
\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})

[
Suz74
,
AM75
]
.
Those with a single Puiseux pair are classified in
[
FdBLMHN07
]
.

It is especially restrictive to have only cusps with multiplicity 2

[
MS89
,
GZN00
]
; these can give surprising examples of real polynomials
[
JK11
]
.

In positive characteristic, Artin-Schreier–type polynomials have been used to give non-standard embeddings of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
into
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.
This was most likely observed by Abhyankar;

[
Sat76
]
refers to them as ‘known counterexamples.’
The curves
C_{d}
can be given by Artin-Schreier–type equations as in (
2
.5),
but their properties are easier to see using an explicit parametrization.

Example 1
.

Fix a characteristic
p>0
. Let
q
be a
p
-power, and
g(u)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
a polynomial.
Let
C^{\circ}_{q,g}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
be the the image of the map

\phi_{q,g}:t\mapsto\bigl(t^{q},g(t^{p})+t\bigr)=:(x,y),

and
C_{q,g}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
its closure.

Lemma 2
.

For the curves
C_{q,g}
in Example
1
the following hold.

(2.1)

\phi_{q,g}:{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
is a closed embedding,

(2.2)

C^{\circ}_{q,g}
is a smooth, rational curve,
and

(2.3)

C_{q,g}
has a single point at infinity. If
r:=p\cdot\deg g-q
is positive, it has multiplicity
r
.

Proof. The derivatve of
\phi_{q,g}
is
(0,1)
, so
it is an immersion. Since
t^{q}=x
, it is also purely inseparable,
hence an isomorphism.
The equation of
C^{\circ}_{q,g}
is obtained using

[formula]

y^{q}=g(t^{qp})+t^{q}=g(x^{p})+x.

If
r:=p\cdot\deg g-q
is positive, then
the unique point at infinity is
(0{:}1{:}0)
, and the equation there is

[formula]

z^{r}=g(x^{p})+xz^{q+r-1}.\qed

Example 3
.

Setting
p=2,q=2^{n}
and
g(u)=u^{2^{n-1}+1}
, we get

C_{2^{n},2}:=\bigl(z^{2}y^{2^{n}}=x^{2^{n}+2}+xz^{2^{n}+1}\bigr)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}.

It is a rational curve of degree
2^{n}+2
.
It has a unique singular point, which is a cusp of multiplicity 2.

It is thus of type
A_{m}
, where
m=2^{n}(2^{n}+1)
.
The latter is roughly
(\deg C_{2^{n},2})^{2}
.

Note that for
m
even, there are non-equivalent singularities of type
A_{m}
in characteristic 2 with the same embedded resolution graphs. In the notation of

[
KS85
,
GK90
]
, the normal form of the singularity of
C_{2^{n},2}
is
z^{2}+zx^{r}+x^{m+1}
where
m=2^{n}(2^{n}+1)
and

r=\frac{1}{2}m+2^{n-1}+1
.

Setting
p=2,q=2^{n}
and
g(u)=u^{2^{n-1}-1}
, we get

C_{2^{n}}:=\bigl(y^{2^{n}}=z^{2}+z^{2^{n}-1}\bigr)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}.

It is a rational curve of degree
2^{n}
.
It has a unique singular point of type
A_{m}
, where
m=(2^{n}-1)(2^{n}-2)
.

Lemma 4
.

[
GZN00
]
Let
B\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a reduced, (not necessarily rational) curve of degree
2d
with a singularity of type
A_{m}
. Then

m\leq 3d(d-1)+1
,
which is roughly
\frac{3}{4}(\deg B)^{2}
.

Hint of proof. Let
S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be the double cover ramified along
B
.
It has a surface singularity of type
A_{m}
. Thus the cohomology of the vanishing cycles of the
A_{m}
surface singularity embeds into
H^{2,2}(S_{t},{\mathbb{C}})
of a
smoothing
S_{t}
of
S
. ∎

Remark 5
.

The upper bound in Lemma
4
is not expected to be sharp for
d>6
. The reducible curves

D_{2d}:=\bigl((y-x^{d})^{2}=y^{2d}\bigr)

have a singularity of type
A_{2d^{2}-1}
.
This corresponds to

m=\frac{1}{2}(\deg D_{2d})^{2}-1
.

It is not easy to find curves
B
with an
A_{m}
singularity such that

m>\frac{1}{2}(\deg B)^{2}
.
The largest known examples give asymptotically

m\approx\frac{7}{12}(\deg B)^{2}

[
Ore12
, Sec.5]
.

Remark 6
.

For
d\leq 6

[
Yos79
]
gives degree
d
, rational, plane curves
Y_{d}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
with a single singular point, which has multiplicity 2.
The curve
Y_{6}
has an

A_{19}
singularity; the largest possible.
Simpler equations are given in
[
ABCRCA02
, 5.8]
.

The curve
C_{4,2}
has degree
6
and an
A_{20}
singularity.
It is possible that
Y_{6}
specializes to
C_{4,2}
.
Note that the singularity type changes from
A_{19}
to
A_{20}
, but they both need 10 blow-ups to resolve.
So the embedded resolution of
C_{4,2}
as in (
7
.1) might be liftable to an embedded resolution of
Y_{6}
.

Remark 7

(Connection with
[
Ish25
]
)
.

Let
0\in X\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
be a singularity. Assume for simplicity
that it can be resolved by a sequence of blowing up points

[formula]

(Y_{r},X_{r},p_{r})\to\cdots\to(Y_{1},X_{1},p_{1})\to(Y_{0},X_{0},p_{0})=({\mathbb{A}}^{n},X,0),

where
X_{i}\subset Y_{i}
is the birational transform of
X
, and

p_{i+1}
lies over
p_{i}
. Let
\pi_{i}:Y_{i}\to Y_{0}
denote the composite.

The discrepancies of the exceptional divisors with respect to the ideal sheaf of
X
are determined by
the numbers
\operatorname{mult}_{p_{i}}\pi_{i}^{-1}(p_{0})
and

\operatorname{mult}_{p_{i}}X_{i}
.

Thus if we deform the blow-up sequence (
7
.1)
such that these multiplicities stay constant, then
the discrepancies of the
\pi_{r}
-exceptional divisors are invariant under this deformation.
Call these
discrepancy preserving
deformations of (
7
.1).

[
Ish25
]
notes that every exceptional divisor over
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
can be obtained as a

\pi_{r}
-exceptional divisor for a (somewhat more general)
blow-up sequence as in (
7
.1).

This is especially useful if
0\in X\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
is in characteristic
p
, and the general fiber of such a deformation is in characteristic
0
. It seems that in many cases there are discrepancy preserving liftings from characteristic
p
to characteristic
0
.

However, the curves in Example
3
show that this way of preserving
discrepancies in liftings is not always possible.

To get such examples,
let
C_{d}=\bigl(g(x,y,z)=0\bigr)
be a curve of degree
d
in characteristic
2
that has a single
A_{2r}
singularity. For odd
m\gg 1
consider

X_{g}:=\bigl(g(x,y,z)+x^{m}+y^{m}+z^{m}=0\bigr)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{3}.

This can be resolved by first blowing up the origin, and then
r
-times
the singular point of the curve
C_{d}
.

The resulting blow-up sequence has a discrepancy preserving lifting to
characteristic
0
iff the curve
C_{d}
lifts to a curve
C^{\prime}_{d}
in
characteristic
0
that has an
A_{2r}
or
A_{2r-1}
singularity.

The smallest counterexample is

C_{8}
, which has degree
8
and an
A_{42}
singularity.
A reduced, octic curve
B_{8}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
has at most an

A_{37}
singularity by Lemma
4
.

The next one is
C_{8,2}
, which has degree
10
and an
A_{72}
singularity.
A reduced, decic curve
B_{10}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
has at most an

A_{61}
singularity by Lemma
4
.

Remark 8

(Log canonical thresholds)
.

The program of Ishii aims to understand
log canonical thresholds in positive characteristic

[
Ish25
]
.

The log canonical threshold of the curve

C_{2^{n},2}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{2}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{m+1}
, where
m=2^{2n}+2^{n}
.
For
n\geq 2
there is no curve
B\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
with the same degree and same log canonical threshold as
C_{2^{n},2}
.

However, the
log canonical threshold of the surface
X(g)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{3}

constructed from
C_{2^{n},2}

is
3/(2^{n}+2)
. It is computed by the exceptional divisor of the first blow-up, and a general singular point with the same multiplicity has the same log canonical threshold.
Thus these curves do not seem to give further information about the program in

[
Ish25
]
.

Note,
however, that the above non-liftable examples seem rather exceptional, and possibly their discrepancies can be handled in different ways. The next examples show that liftability may frequently hold.

Example 9

(Quartic surfaces)
.

I first tried to look for non-liftable examples among K3 surfaces.
In characteristic
0
the rank of the Néron-Severi group of a smooth K3 is at most 20,
but in characteristic
p
it is 22 for supersingular ones.

So if
S
is a supersingular K3 surface with Picard number 1
in characteristic
p
, then
there is no lifting
to characteristic
0
preserving
all
the singularity types.

Most supersingular K3 surfaces with Picard number 1 have many singularities, and individually each singularity seems to lift, but there are a few examples where lifting even one singularity is not possible.
I thank Shimada and Schütt for explaining these to me.

Working with the lists of
[
Shi04
]
,
we discuss 4 extremal cases, but none are good enough to be used in Remark
7
.

(
9
.1) There is a degree 4 K3 surface
S_{1}
in characteristic
2
with a
D_{21}
singularity. However,

S_{1}
is not a quartic in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
, but a double cover of the quadric cone. Thus
S_{1}
is naturally an octic surface in
{\mathbb{P}}(1,1,2,4)
. So there is an octic surface in
{\mathbb{P}}(1,1,2,4)

with a
D_{21}
singularity in characteristic
2
, but there is no such in char 0.

It seems likely that if
(s,S)
is any
quartic surface in characteristic
p
with with an ADE singularity at
s
,
then there is a lifting
to characteristic
0
preserving the singularity type at
s
.

(
9
.2) There is a K3 surface
S_{2}
in characteristic
11
with an
A_{21}
singularity, but it has degree
22
.

(
9
.3) There is a quasi-elliptic K3 surface
S_{3}
in characteristic
2
with a
D_{20}
singularity, and a section along which the surface is smooth.
The Weierstrass equation realizes it is a surface in
the projectivization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}+{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(8)+{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(12)
, but there is no similar surface in characteristic
0
.

(
9
.4) There is an elliptic K3 surface
S_{4}
in characteristic
2
with
A_{17}+3A_{1}
singularities, and a section along which the surface is smooth.
As before, there is no lifting that keeps all singularities, but
there is an elliptic K3 surface in characteristic
0
with
A_{17}+A_{1}
singularities.

The curves
C_{2^{n},2}
also lead to supersingular double planes.

10
Supersingular double planes.

Over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
consider the surfaces

[formula]

S_{r}:=\bigl(u^{2}+u(zy^{2r}+x^{2r+1})+xz^{4r+1}+z^{2}y^{4r}+x^{4r+2}=0\bigr)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(1,1,1,2r+1).

In the
y=1
chart, the equation is

[formula]

g:=u^{2}+u(z+x^{2r+1})+xz^{4r+1}+z^{2}+x^{4r+2}=0.

Thus

g_{u}=z+x^{2r+1},\ g_{z}=u+xz^{4r},\ g_{x}=ux^{2r}+z^{4r+1}.

The first 2 define the monomial curve
M_{r}
which is the image of

\phi_{r}:t\mapsto\bigl(t,t^{2r+1},t^{8r^{2}+4r+1}\bigr),

and
g_{x}
vanishes identically along
M_{r}
.
Thus

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[x,z,u]/(g,g_{x},g_{z},g_{u})&\cong&{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[t]/\bigl(g(t,t^{2r+1},t^{8r^{2}+4r+1})\bigr)\\
&\cong&{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[t]/\bigl(t^{8r^{2}+6r+2}(t^{8r^{2}+2r}+1)\bigr).\end{array}

Thus
S_{r}
has an
A_{8r^{2}+6r+1}
singularity at
t=0
.
For the other singularities, write
2r=qr^{\prime}
where
q
is a
2
-power and
r^{\prime}
is odd. Then we have

A_{q-1}
singularities when
t
is a
(4rr^{\prime}+r^{\prime})^{th}
root of unity.
There are no other singular points.

If
r
is a
2
-power,
then the minimal resolution
\bar{S}_{r}\to S_{r}
contains

8r^{2}+6r+1+(4r+1)(2r-1)=16r^{2}+4r
exceptional curves.

Over the line
(z=0)
the equation (
10
.2) becomes

u^{2}+ux^{2r+1}+x^{4r+2}=0
. Setting
v:=ux^{-2r-1}
we get

v^{2}+v+1=0
. If
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are its roots,
then the preimage of
(z=0)
has 2 components

B_{i}:=\bigl(u+\alpha_{i}x^{2r+1}=z=0\bigr).

Thus the Picard number of
\bar{S}_{r}
is at least

16r^{2}+4r+2
.

This equals the second Betti bumber of a double plane ramified along a smooth curve of degree
4r+2
.
Thus the surfaces
\bar{S}_{r}
are supersingular
for every
r=2^{n}
, with Picard number
16r^{2}+4r+2
.

Example 11
.

The surface
S_{1}
is a K3 surface with
A_{15}+5A_{1}
singularities.
Resolving the
A_{15}
singularity, the birational transforms of the
B_{i}
become smooth rational curves, and the dual configuration is

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccccc}&&&&\bar{B}_{1}&&&&\bar{B}_{2}&&&&\\
&&&&|&&&&|&&&&\\
C_{1}&-&C_{2}&-&C_{3}&-&\cdots&-&C_{13}&-&C_{14}&-&C_{15}\end{array}

From this we compute that
(B_{1}\cdot B_{2})=\frac{9}{16}
and

(B_{1}^{2})=(B_{2}^{2})=\frac{7}{16}
.

Contracting all but the curve
C_{8}
gives a supersingular K3 surface
S^{\prime}_{1}
with singularities
2E_{8}+5A_{1}
, and Picard number
1
. The image of
C_{8}
is a rational curve
C^{\prime}_{8}
with 2 cusps at the
E_{8}
-points, and
(C^{\prime}_{8}\cdot C^{\prime}_{8})=2
.
Thus
S^{\prime}_{1}
is a K3 surface of degree 2.
This is the 4th surface on the list of
[
Shi04
]

in characteristic 2.
