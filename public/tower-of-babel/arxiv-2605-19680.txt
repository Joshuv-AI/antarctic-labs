Contractibility of the space of ε \varepsilon -nets in ℝ \mathbb{R}
Ivan N. Mikhailov

Abstract
In this note, we show that the space of all ε \varepsilon -nets in the real line ℝ \mathbb{R} with a natural metric, equipped with either Hausdorff or Gromov–Hausdorff distance, is contractible.



1
Introduction

In
[
3
]
M. Gromov introduced moduli spaces of the class of all metric spaces at finite Gromov–Hausdorff distances from a given metric space. It was mentioned that such moduli spaces are always complete and contractible (
[
3
]
[section
3.11_{\frac{1}{2}_{+}}
]). In
[
1
]
the authors suggested to work with such moduli spaces (they were called
clouds
) in the sense of NBG set theory to avoid arising set-theoretic issues. While the completeness of each cloud was verified in
[
1
]
, the contractibility of each cloud remains an open question for a number of reasons. The main issue here is that a natural homothety-mapping that takes a metric space
(X,d_{X})
into
(X,\lambda d_{X})
for some
\lambda>0
and generates a contraction of a cloud of all bounded metric spaces if
\lambda\to 0
, does not behave so well in case of unbounded metric spaces. Firstly, in
[
1
]
it was shown that there exist metric spaces such that
d_{GH}(X,\lambda X)=\infty
for some
\lambda>0
. The simplest one is a geometric progression
X=\{3^{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
with a natural metric, for which
d_{GH}(X,2X)=\infty
. Secondly, even for clouds that are invariant under multiplication on all positive numbers a homothety-mapping may not be continuous. In
[
5
]
it was shown that
d_{GH}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda\mathbb{Z}^{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all
\lambda>1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In this note we continue the investigation of the geometry of the Gromov–Hausdorff class. In
[
4
]
Hausdorff geodesics were constructed, which are geodesic lines in the Gromov–Hausdorff class joining an arbitrary
\varepsilon
-net in
\mathbb{R}
with
\mathbb{R}
. We show that these natural geodesic lines allow one to construct a contraction of the space of all
\varepsilon
-nets in
\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the Hausdorff metric. We also establish ultrametric inequalities
2
,
5
, which allow us to think of this space as a contractible cone with the vertex in the space
\mathbb{R}
, with the <<Euclidean>> angle at the vertex not exceeding
\frac{\pi}{2}
, which resembles the situation in the cloud of bounded metric spaces (see Theorem
2
).

2
Preliminaries

A metric space
is an arbitrary pair
(X,\,\operatorname{d}_{X})
, where
X
is an arbitrary set,
\operatorname{d}_{X}\colon X\times X\to[0,\,\infty)
is some metric on it, that is, a nonnegative symmetric, positively definite function that satisfies the triangle inequality.

For convenience, if it is clear in which metric space we are working, we denote the distance between points
x
and
y
by
|xy|
. Suppose
X
is a metric space. By
U_{r}(a)=\{x\in X\colon|ax|<r\}
,
B_{r}(a)=\{x\in X\colon|ax|\leq r\}
we denote open and closed balls centered at point
a
of radius
r
in
X
. For an arbitrary subset
A\subset X
of a metric space
X
, let
U_{r}(A)=\cup_{a\in A}U_{r}(a)
be an open
r
-neighborhood of
A
. For non-empty subsets
A\subset X
,
B\subset X
we put
\operatorname{d}(A,\,B)=\inf\bigl\{|ab|:\,a\in A,\,b\in B\bigl\}
.

2.1
Hausdorff and Gromov–Hausdorff distances

Definition 1
.

Let
A
and
B
be non-empty subsets of a metric space.

The Hausdorff distance
between
A
and
B
is the quantity

d_{H}(A,\,B)=\inf\bigl\{r>0\colon A\subset U_{r}(B),\,B\subset U_{r}(A)\bigr\}=\max\left(\sup_{a\in A}\operatorname{d}(a,\,B),\,\sup_{b\in B}\operatorname{d}(b,\,A)\right)\!.

Definition 2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces. The triple
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},Z)
, consisting of a metric space
Z
and its two subsets
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
, isometric to
X
and
Y
respectively, is called
a realization of the pair

(X,Y)
.

Definition 3
.

The Gromov-Hausdorff distance

d_{GH}(X,Y)
between
X
and
Y
is the exact lower bound of the numbers
r\geq 0
for which there exists a realization
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},Z)
of the pair
(X,Y)
such that
d_{H}(X^{\prime},\,Y^{\prime})\leq r
.

Now let
X,\,Y
be non-empty sets.

Definition 4
.

Each
\sigma\subset X\times Y
is called a
relation
between
X
and
Y
.

By
\mathcal{P}_{0}(X,\,Y)
we denote the set of all non-empty relations between
X
and
Y
.

We put

\pi_{X}\colon X\times Y\rightarrow X,\;\pi_{X}(x,\,y)=x,

\pi_{Y}\colon X\times Y\rightarrow Y,\;\pi_{Y}(x,\,y)=y.

Definition 5
.

A relation
R\subset X\times Y
is called a
correspondence
, if restrictions
\pi_{X}|_{R}
and
\pi_{Y}|_{R}
are surjective.

Let
\mathcal{R}(X,\,Y)
be the set of all correspondences between
X
and
Y
.

Definition 6
.

Let
X,\,Y
be metric spaces,
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(X,\,Y)
. The
distortion
of
\sigma
is the quantity

\operatorname{dis}\sigma=\sup\Bigl\{\bigl||xx^{\prime}|-|yy^{\prime}|\bigr|\colon(x,\,y),\,(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})\in\sigma\Bigr\}.

Proposition 1

(
[
2
]
)
.

For arbitrary metric spaces
X
and
Y
, the following equality holds

2d_{GH}(X,\,Y)=\inf\bigl\{\operatorname{dis}\,R\colon R\in\mathcal{R}(X,\,Y)\bigr\}.

For a metric space
(X,d_{X})
, we denote
\operatorname{Net}_{H}(X)
(
\operatorname{Net}_{GH}(X)
) the space of all
\varepsilon
-nets in
X
equipped with the Hausdorff (Gromov–Hausdorff) distance.

2.2
Clouds

Let
\mathcal{VGH}
denote the class of all nonempty metric spaces endowed with the Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

Theorem 1

(
[
2
]
)
.

The Gromov–Hausdorff distance is a generalized pseudometric on
\mathcal{VGH}
that vanishes on every pair of isometric spaces. Namely, the Gromov–Hausdorff distance is symmetric, satisfies the triangle inequality, but in general may be infinite or zero.

The class
\mathcal{GH}_{0}
is obtained from
\mathcal{VGH}
by factoring out zero distances, i.e., by the equivalence relation:
X\sim_{0}Y
if and only if
d_{GH}(X,\,Y)=0
.

Definition 7
.

Consider the equivalence relation
\sim_{1}
on
\mathcal{GH}_{0}
:
X\sim_{1}Y
if and only if
d_{GH}(X,\,Y)<\infty
. The corresponding equivalence classes are called
clouds
.

For an arbitrary metric space
X
, the cloud defined by it will be denoted by
[X]
. Let
\Delta_{1}
denote the metric space consisting of a single point. Thus,
[\Delta_{1}]
is the cloud consisting of the classes of all bounded spaces at zero distance from each other.

Suppose that for some pair of metric spaces
A
and
A^{\prime}
we have
d_{GH}(A,A^{\prime})=0
. Then for any metric space
B
, the equality
d_{GH}(A,B)=d_{GH}(A^{\prime},B)
holds. From this simple observation it follows that any result concerning the Gromov–Hausdorff distance
d_{GH}(A,B)
remains valid when
A
is replaced by
A^{\prime}
with
d_{GH}(A,A^{\prime})=0
. Thus, instead of interpreting the notation
A\in[X]
directly by definition, assuming that
A
is an equivalence class of spaces at zero Gromov–Hausdorff distance from
A
, we will, without loss of generality, consider
A
as a concrete representative of this equivalence class. For example, the notation
X\in[\Delta_{1}]
throughout the paper can be read as ‘‘
X
is a bounded metric space’’.

The following theorem on the structure of the cloud
[\Delta_{1}]
is integral for this work.

Theorem 2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be arbitrary bounded metric spaces. Then

•

The following inequalities hold:

\frac{1}{2}\bigl|\operatorname{diam}X-\operatorname{diam}Y\bigr|\leq d_{GH}(X,Y)\leq\max\bigl\{d_{GH}(X,\Delta_{1}),d_{GH}(Y,\Delta_{1})\bigr\}=\frac{1}{2}\max\bigl\{\operatorname{diam}X,\operatorname{diam}Y\bigr\}.

•

The map
\Phi\colon[\Delta_{1}]\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to[\Delta_{1}]
,
\Phi(X,\lambda)=\lambda X
is continuous and yields a contraction of the cloud
[\Delta_{1}]
as
\lambda\to 0
.

•

The curve
\lambda X
,
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
is a geodesic in the cloud
[\Delta_{1}]
.

Finally, we will need two more theorems. The first one allows the construction of Hausdorff geodesics (see
[

]
for details):

Theorem 3

(

4
]
, Theorem 9.2)
.

Let
X

\mathbb{R}
, then
d_{GH}(X,\mathbb{R})=d_{H}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

And the second one, which shows that a natural homothety-mapping is not a contraction of the cloud
[\mathbb{R}]
:

Theorem 4

(
[
5
]
)
.

Consider the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, equipped with the Euclidean metric. Then, for all
\lambda>1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the inequality holds
d_{GH}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda\mathbb{Z}^{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

3
Main results

3.1
Hausdorff distance

Proposition 2
.

For arbitrary
A,B\subset X
, we have
d_{H}(A,B)\leq\max\big\{d_{H}(A,X),d_{H}(B,X)\big\}
.

Proof.

By Definition
1
, we have

\displaystyle d_{H}(A,B)=\max\big\{\sup_{a\in A}\operatorname{d}(a,B),\sup_{b\in B}\operatorname{d}(b,A)\big\},

\displaystyle d_{H}(A,X)=\max\big\{\sup_{a\in A}\operatorname{d}(a,X),\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{d}(x,A)\big\}=\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{d}(x,A),

\displaystyle d_{H}(B,X)=\max\big\{\sup_{b\in B}\operatorname{d}(b,X),\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{d}(x,B)\big\}=\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{d}(x,B).

Since
\sup_{a\in A}\operatorname{d}(a,B)\leq\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{d}(x,B)
and
\sup_{b\in B}\operatorname{d}(b,A)\leq\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{d}(x,A)
, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

Example 1
.

Consider the spaces
\lambda_{n}\mathbb{Z}
for some sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, tending to
1
with
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then spaces
\lambda_{N}\mathbb{Z}
do not converge to
\mathbb{Z}
with respect to the Hausdorff distance.

Indeed, otherwise it would imply that
\lambda_{n}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow_{GH}\lambda\mathbb{Z}
which contradicts Theorem
4
.

Proposition 3
.

For a finite-dimansional vector space
(V,\|\cdot\|)
, the space
\operatorname{Net}_{H}(V)
is contractible.

Proof.

Define
\Phi\colon\operatorname{Net}(\mathbb{R})\times[0,1]\to\operatorname{Net}(\mathbb{R})
as follows:

\Phi(X,\lambda)=\begin{cases}B_{\lambda/(1-\lambda)}(X)&\lambda\in[0,1),\\
V&\lambda=1.\end{cases}

It suffices to check the continuity of
\Phi
. Denote
f(x)=\frac{x}{1-x}
.

1) Take arbitrary
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=\lambda
, where
\lambda\in[0,1)
. Then
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}f(\lambda_{n})=f(\lambda)
. Note that
d_{H}\bigl(B_{f(\lambda_{n})}(X),B_{f(\lambda)}(X)\bigr)\leq|\lambda_{n}-\lambda|.
It follows that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{f(\lambda_{n})}(X)=B_{f(\lambda)}(X)
.

Since
X
is an
\varepsilon
-net in
V
, there exists
t
such that for all
t^{\prime}>t
, we have
B_{t^{\prime}}(X)=V
. Hence, if
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=1
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{f(\lambda_{n})}(X)=V
.

2) Take arbitrary
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}X_{n}=X
. Note that
d_{H}\bigl(B_{f(\lambda)}(X_{n}),B_{f(\lambda)}(X)\bigr)\leq d_{H}\bigl(X_{n},X)
. Indeed, if
p^{\prime}\in B_{f(\lambda)}(X_{n})
, there exist
p\in X_{n}
and
q\in X
such that
\|p-p^{\prime}\|\leq f(\lambda)
,
\|p-q\|\leq d_{H}(X_{n},X)
. Thus,
\|p^{\prime}-(q+p^{\prime}-p)\|=\|p-q\|\leq d_{H}(X_{n},X)
, and
q+p^{\prime}-p\in B_{f(\lambda)}(X)
, since
\|p-p^{\prime}\|\leq f(\lambda)
. Therefore, since
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{H}(X_{n},X)=0
, we obtain
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{H}\bigl(B_{f(\lambda)}(X_{n}),B_{f(\lambda)}(X)\bigr)=0
, which finishes the proof.
∎

3.2
Gromov–Hausdorff distance

Theorem 5
.

For arbitrary
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
, we have
d_{GH}(A,B)\leq\max\big\{d_{GH}(A,\mathbb{R}),d_{GH}(B,\mathbb{R})\big\}
.

Proof.

Applying theorems
2
and
3
, we obtain

d_{GH}(A,B)\leq d_{H}(A,B)\leq\max\big\{d_{H}(A,\mathbb{R}),d_{H}(B,\mathbb{R})\big\}=\max\big\{d_{GH}(A,\mathbb{R}),d_{GH}(B,\mathbb{R})\big\}.

∎

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
R\in\mathcal{R}(A,B)
, for some subsets
A,B\mathbb{R}
,
\operatorname{dis}R=c
, and
A
is
t
-separated, for some
t>2c
. For each
p\in A
, we choose
p^{\prime}\in R(p)
arbitrarily. Then, for every three distinct points
p,q,r\in\mathbb{R}
, if
p
lies between
q
and
r
, then
p^{\prime}
lies between
q^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Suppose the desired statement fails for
p,q,r\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, on the one hand,

\displaystyle|q^{\prime}r^{\prime}|\geq|qr|-c=|pq|+|pr|-c.

On the other hand, if
p^{\prime}
does not lie between
q^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle|q^{\prime}r^{\prime}|=\bigl||p^{\prime}q^{\prime}|-|p^{\prime}r^{\prime}|\bigr|\leq\max\bigl\{|p^{\prime}q^{\prime}|,|p^{\prime}r^{\prime}|\bigr\}\leq\max\{|pq|,|pr|\}+c\leq.

Thus, we obtain

2c\geq|pq|+|qr|-\max\bigl\{|pq|,|qr|\bigr\}\geq t,

that is a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 6
.

The space
\operatorname{Net}_{GH}(\mathbb{R})
is contractible.

Proof.

Define
\Phi\colon\operatorname{Net}(\mathbb{R})\times[0,1]\to\operatorname{Net}(\mathbb{R})
as follows:

\Phi(X,\lambda)=\begin{cases}B_{\lambda/(1-\lambda)}(X)&\lambda\in[0,1),\\
\mathbb{R}&\lambda=1.\end{cases}

Lemma 2
.

A mapping
\Phi
is continuous.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\Phi
is continuous with respect to each of two variables
X
and
\lambda
. Once again, denot
f(x)=\frac{x}{1-x}
.

1) Take arbitrary
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=\lambda
, where
\lambda\in[0,1)
. Then
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}f(\lambda_{n})=f(\lambda)
. Consider a natural correspondence
R
between
B_{f(\lambda_{n})}(X)
and
B_{f(\lambda)}(X)
which is the union of natural correspondences between segments
[x-f(\lambda_{n}),x+f(\lambda_{n})]
and
[x-f(\lambda),x+f(\lambda)]
. Note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dis}R\leq 2|f(\lambda_{n})-f(\lambda)|=2\Big|\frac{\lambda_{n}-\lambda}{(1-\lambda_{n})(1-\lambda)}\Big|.

Thus,

\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{GH}\Bigl(\Phi(X,\lambda_{n}),\Phi(X,\lambda)\Bigr)=0.

It remains to show that, for a sequence
\lambda_{n}
tending to
1
, we have
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{GH}(\Phi(\lambda_{n},X),\mathbb{R})=0
. Since
X
is an
\varepsilon
-net in
\mathbb{R}
, we have
d_{H}(X,\mathbb{R})<\infty
. Thus, there exists such
t
that, for any
t^{\prime}>t
, we have
B_{t^{\prime}}(X)=\mathbb{R}
, and, hence, the desired statement is true.

2) Take arbitrary
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}X_{n}=X
. Let us show that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi(X_{n},\lambda)=\Phi(X,\lambda)
.

Choose arbitrary
R_{n}\in\mathcal{R}(X,X_{n})
. By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\operatorname{dis}R_{n}\leq\frac{2}{n+100}\lambda
. In particular,
d_{GH}(X_{n},X)\leq\frac{1}{n}
.

Fix some
100
-separated infinite sequence of points
X^{\prime}=\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X
such that
p_{n}<p_{m}\Longleftrightarrow n<m
. Choose
p_{k}^{\prime}\in R_{n}(p_{k})
. By Lemma
1
, the order of points
p_{k}^{\prime}
on the real line is the same (or inverted) as the order of points
p_{k}
on the real line. In the remaining proof we assume that these orders coincide with the usual order (
<
) on the real line.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose
(a,a^{\prime})\in R_{n}
,
(b,b^{\prime})\in R_{n}
satisfy inequalities
a<b
,
a^{\prime}>b^{\prime}
. Then
b-a\leq 2\operatorname{dis}R_{n}
.

Proof.

Choose
p_{k}
such that
p_{k}>b+100
. Then, by definition of distortion,
\big||p_{n}a|-|p_{n}^{\prime}a^{\prime}|\big|\leq\operatorname{dis}R_{n}
,
\big||p_{n}b|-|p_{n}^{\prime}b^{\prime}|\big|\leq\operatorname{dis}R_{n}
. Hence,

0>b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}=|p^{\prime}-b^{\prime}|-|p^{\prime}-a^{\prime}|\geq|p-b|-|p-a|-2\operatorname{dis}R_{n}=b-a-2\operatorname{dis}R_{n}\Longleftrightarrow b-a<2\operatorname{dis}R_{n}.

∎

Now we extend
R_{n}
to a correspondence
R_{n}^{\prime}
between
B_{f(\lambda)}(X)
and
B_{f(\lambda)}(X_{n})
such that
\operatorname{dis}R_{n}^{\prime}\leq 5\operatorname{dis}R_{n}
.

Take arbitrary
a\in B_{f(\lambda)}(x)
for some
x\in X
. Unless
a\in\pi_{X}(R_{n})
, we take arbitrary
x^{\prime}\in R_{n}(x)
and add
(a,x^{\prime}+a-x)
to
R_{n}
.

Let us check the stated inequality.

We consider three cases:

1)
(a,x^{\prime}+a-x)
and
(b,y^{\prime}+b-y)
, where
a\in B_{f(\lambda)}(x)
,
b\in B_{f(\lambda)}(y)
,
(x,x^{\prime}),(y,y^{\prime})\in R_{n}
.

Here we consider two subcases.

Wlog, we assume that
x\leq y
.

Case 1.1
.
x^{\prime}\leq y^{\prime}
. Then

\big||a-b|-|x^{\prime}+a-x-y^{\prime}-b+y|\big|=\big||x+a-x-y-b+y|-|x^{\prime}+a-x-y^{\prime}-b+y|\big|\leq\\
\leq\big||x-y|-|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\big|\leq\operatorname{dis}R_{n}.

Case 1.2
.
x^{\prime}\geq y^{\prime}
. By Lemma
3
, we know that
y-x<2\operatorname{dis}R_{n}<\frac{\lambda}{4}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\big||a-b|-|x^{\prime}+a-x-y^{\prime}-b+y|\big|\leq\big||a-b|-|a-b|\big|+|x-y|+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\leq 2|x-y|+\operatorname{dis}R_{n}\leq 5\operatorname{dis}R_{n}.

2)
(a,x^{\prime}+a-x)
and
(r,r^{\prime})
, where
a\in B_{f(\lambda)}(x)
,
(x,x^{\prime}),(r,r^{\prime})\in R_{n}
.

This case can be considered similarly to the first one.

3)
(r,r^{\prime})
and
(s,s^{\prime})
where
(r,r^{\prime}),(s,s^{\prime})\in R_{n}
.

In this case
\big||r-s|-|r^{\prime}-s^{\prime}|\bigr|\leq\operatorname{dis}R_{n}
, by definition of distortion.
∎

From Lemma
2
, it follows that
\Phi
is the desired retraction of
\operatorname{Net}_{GH}(\mathbb{R})
onto
\mathbb{R}
.
∎
