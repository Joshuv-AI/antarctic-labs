Exploring patients' perspectives and experiences of asthma self-management: A qualitative study
Authors: Saba Alyas, Rabia Hussain, Bayan Faisal Ababneh, Jaya Muneswarao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Patients with asthma can effectively manage their condition and minimize its impact on their everyday life by practicing asthma self-management.Objective: This study aimed to explore knowledge, perceptions, experiences, barriers, and facilitators of asthma self-management among asthma patients in Malaysia.Methods: A qualitative study using a phenomenological approach was conducted from February 2025 to May 2025 among 19 asthma patients aged 18 and above through semi-structured online and face-to-face individual interviews. All interviews were audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, and analysed using inductive thematic analysis through NVivo® software to identify key themes. Later relevant themes were applied deductively to the three self-management tasks (medical, behavioural, and emotional management) described by Corbin and Strauss.Results: To improve interpretation, two overarching themes, (1) impacts of asthma and (2) self-management of asthma, were constructed. Within “impacts of asthma,” one main theme, “living with asthma,” was identified. Under “self-management of asthma,” four main themes were identified: (i) medical management, (ii) behavioural management, (iii) emotional management, and (iv) factors influencing asthma self-management.Conclusion: Asthma patients demonstrated a good understanding of the disease and self-management strategies; gaps remain in the area of self-monitoring. Patients' perceived self-efficacy and beliefs towards traditional methods and asthma medication influenced self-management practices. Asthma counselling seminars and the provision of an asthma action plan can facilitate asthma management. Furthermore, addressing barriers such as passive smoking and workplace difficulties through policies is essential.

Introduction

Asthma is a chronic disease characterized by chronic airway inflammation. Its symptoms include wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, cough, and variable expiratory airflow.1 Globally, asthma affects about 29% of the population2 and it is estimated that it affected about 261 million people and caused 436,000 deaths in 2021.3, 4 Asthma has an early age of onset and, therefore, presents as the most common chronic disease among children and young adults.5

Uncontrolled asthma is associated with a considerable burden of disease, including impaired lung function and exacerbations, high healthcare resource utilization and costs, and impaired health-related quality of life (HRQoL).6 The goal of asthma management is, therefore, to achieve long-term symptom control and risk minimization. This includes few or no asthma symptoms, unaffected physical activity, no disturbed sleep, no exacerbation, stable lung function, and no maintenance for systemic corticosteroids' requirement.1

A promising approach to treating chronic diseases is self-management.7 The patients with chronic illnesses spend most of their time outside the healthcare system, so they must learn how to manage their chronic disease and take responsibility for their own care.8 Self-management is described as the capability of an individual, in association with health care professionals, family, and community, to manage symptoms, treatments, and lifestyle adjustments.9

Corbin and Strauss identified three sets of tasks linked with the work of living with a chronic illness. The tasks included (1) medical management of the condition, which signifies tasks such as taking medication, using an inhaler, and adhering to specific diet; (2) behavioural management, which denotes to keeping, changing, and making new meaningful behaviours or life roles in the context of a specific condition; and (3) emotional management, which needs patients to deal with the emotions such as fear, despair, frustration, and depression that are often experienced by chronically ill individuals.10 Better self-management has been associated with better asthma control and less morbidity.1., 11 Asthma self-management enhances care and reduces the risk of exacerbation.12

In Malaysia, the prevalence of asthma ranges from 8.9 to 13% in children (6–14 years) and 6.3% in adults.13 The Malaysian National Health and Morbidity Survey reported that every year, 20% of adults with asthma visited the emergency department for acute exacerbations, and 10% of these were admitted.14 Furthermore, another Malaysian study reported that 66% of clinic attendees still had poorly controlled asthma.15 Recently, studies related to inhaler use in asthma reported that good asthma control is associated with minimal medical assistance and symptoms.16, 17 The Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA)1 and the Malaysian Clinical Practice Guidelines for asthma18 endorse that all patients should be provided with education and skills to self-manage their asthma effectively. Studies conducted in Malaysia related to self-management focused on various scopes, including the development of self-management skills among individuals, sociocultural influence on asthma management, barriers to implementing self-management in primary care, and the feasibility of a pictorial action plan and asthma application.19, 20, 21., 22., 23. There is a scarcity of data about awareness, practices, perceptions, and the barriers and facilitators related to asthma self-management from the perspective of adult Malaysian asthma patients. Understanding these aspects is necessary as patients with asthma require definite knowledge about their disease and its management to keep asthma under control.24 Reflecting patients' experiences and views about asthma and its treatment helps in effective asthma treatment.25., 26

The study seeks to systematically explore asthma patients' knowledge, perceptions, and experiences, and to delineate the perceived barriers and enablers affecting optimal management outcomes.

Methodology

The consolidated criteria for reporting qualitative research (COREQ) (Appendix A) were applied while reporting this qualitative study.27

Study design

A qualitative phenomenological approach was employed to conduct this study. Phenomenology is a qualitative research approach that seeks to profoundly understand an individual's world of lived experience, which is frequently referred to as their lifeworld.28, 29. Understanding the study participants' life experiences, especially how they perceive, experience, and make sense of the phenomenon being studied, is the main focus of phenomenological research. The purpose of phenomenological research is always to explore each individual's unique interpretation of an experience.30

Study setting, sampling, and recruitment of participants

The study was conducted in Malaysia, and the targeted participants were adult asthma patients. Participants for this study were selected through purposive and snowball sampling techniques,31 according to the following inclusion criteria: Malaysian asthma patients aged 18 and above, diagnosed with asthma at least three months before the study, and able to understand and communicate in the English language. Patients who had any other lung disease, like COPD (whose symptoms overlapped with asthma) were excluded.

The following recruitment strategies were employed to recruit participants from all over Malaysia:1.Research recruitment flyers (Appendix B) were posted on social media platforms (LinkedIn and Facebook).2.Researchers also used existing social networks (e.g., referrals from community pharmacists and other persons) to identify potential asthma patients.3.Recruited participants also referred to the other asthma patients.

The sample size for the qualitative study was estimated based on reaching the saturation point, where no more new information is generated.32

Data collection

A semi-structured interview guide (Appendix C) based on the literature review20, 33, 34., 35., 36, 37., 38., 39, 40., 41 and Corbin and Strauss' three tasks of self-management was developed.10 The interview guide was reviewed by three academic experts from Universiti Sains Malaysia for its content validity, and necessary amendments were made in the interview guide upon the suggestions of the experts. The interview guide was pilot tested with two asthma patients, which were later excluded from the final analyses.

A total of 19 in-depth interviews, 2 (face-to-face) and 17 (online through Webex® Application), were conducted from February 2025 to May 2025. All interviews were conducted by the researcher (SA), a female master's candidate serving as principal investigator, who had received formal training in qualitative interviewing techniques. All interviews were conducted privately with no other individuals present in the vicinity. Each interview lasted from 30 to 45 min. Interviews were audio-recorded with participants' consent. At the end of each interview, the interviewee was offered the opportunity to comment or add anything related to the discussed topics. Additional field notes were also taken by the researcher during the interviews to record the contextual information. Data saturation was achieved after 17 interviews, but additionally, two more interviews were conducted to ensure that no new information emerged.

Data analysis

The data was analysed through reflexive thematic analysis, following the six-step process described by Braun and Clarke.42 Additionally, relevant themes were deductively mapped onto the three self-management tasks described by Corbin and Strauss.10

Researcher (SA) transcribed verbatim the audio-recorded interviews through Microsoft Word® version 2506. The researcher became familiar with the data by reading the transcripts again and again. Next, the transcripts were imported into the NVivo® software version 15, appropriate codes were assigned to the transcribed data by identifying meaningful segments (words, phrases, or sentences), and a codebook was developed. Codes were generated iteratively until no new codes emerged from the data. Codes were then sorted for similarities and differences; codes with similar content were categorized into subthemes. Eventually, related sub-themes were grouped to develop themes. After that, the relevant inductive themes were deductively mapped onto the Corbin and Strauss three self-management tasks.10

Each step of the thematic analysis was reviewed by two other researchers (RH and BA); both reviewed transcripts, codes, and themes and provided feedback on codes, themes, and findings to ensure accuracy and minimize bias, strengthening the study's credibility.

Ethical considerations

This study obtained ethical approval from the Human Research Ethics Committee of the Universiti Sains Malaysia (reference code USM/JEPeM/PP/24080716). All participants provided both written and verbal informed consent before their inclusion in the study, including consent for the audio recording of interviews. Participants were informed about the interviewer's background, the purpose of the study, the voluntary nature of their participation, and their right to withdraw at any time without any consequences. To ensure participant privacy and confidentiality, pseudonyms were assigned, and no identifying information was included in the transcripts or final report. All interviews were audio recorded and stored safely on the principal researcher's computer. The recordings were erased as soon as the data processing was complete.

Reflexivity statement

With expertise in both clinical and pharmacy practice, the research team provided valuable insights into the study exploring asthma self-management among patients; however, reflexivity was essential to prevent bias. Throughout this study, we dedicated ourselves to maintaining transparency and honesty regarding our perspectives and assumptions. No prior relationships were established between participants and researchers before the interviews, which helped to maintain objectivity and reduce potential bias. To ensure a comprehensive and minimalized biased exploration of the topic, we used open-ended questions and employed reflective techniques such as maintaining field notes, audio recording of interviews during data collection, and an inductive approach to analyse data.

Trustworthiness

Our study adhered to the four criteria of trustworthiness in qualitative research: confirmability, dependability, credibility, and transferability.43

Confirmability was achieved through an iterative review of codes, sub-themes, and themes by all researchers. Dependability was ensured by following a consistent data collection process with scripted questions and probes. We also documented each step of the research process to ensure transparency. Credibility was achieved by prolonged engagement of the researcher with the data. Additionally, two researchers (RH and BA) with expertise in qualitative research reviewed transcripts, codebook, and developed themes, hence adding depth to the analysis. Transferability was addressed by providing detailed descriptions of participants and the study context, allowing readers to assess the applicability to similar settings.

Thematic analysis

Themes were derived inductively from thematic analysis, and then relevant themes were applied to three self-management tasks outlined by Corbin and Strauss.10

As shown in Fig. 2, the final data analysis yielded five major themes and 11 subthemes. The results are categorized under two overarching concepts to facilitate reporting: (1) impacts of asthma and (2) self-management of asthma. Under the “impacts of asthma,” one theme, “living with asthma,” was described, while under “self-management of asthma,” the four following themes were defined: (1) medical management, (2) behavioural management, (3) emotional management, and (4) factors influencing asthma self-management.Fig. 2Overarching themes, themes, and subthemes developed from thematic analysis of asthma patients' qualitative interviews.Fig. 2

Impacts of asthma

Theme one: Living with asthma

Subtheme one: Impact on Physical Well-being

Patients (n = 9) often experienced disrupted sleep, such as difficulty falling asleep or nocturnal awakenings due to coughing and breathlessness. Asthma symptoms tend to appear during rainy weather and in cold environments, frequently interrupting individuals' sleep.“The sleeping routine also uh have some changes because when the asthma come uh, I have a frequent awake at night because of the cough and also shortness of breath.”(Female, P8)

“The sleeping routine also uh have some changes because when the asthma come uh, I have a frequent awake at night because of the cough and also shortness of breath.”

Participants (n = 3) reported that asthma affected their eating habits, requiring them to follow restricted diets. They had to avoid certain foods and drinks that could exacerbate their symptoms and practiced portion control to manage their condition.“When I was, when I was like child I loved to eat this food called Mee Bandung I really enjoyed it but when uh when I got my asthma it changed my I can't eat that I cannot eat Mee Bandung and also prawn, just triggered my asthma worsen and worsen also made the allergies, my allergies become worsened and worsened and I need to stay a couple of days in the hospital.”(Male, P14)

Subtheme two: Impact on psychological well-being

Self-management of asthma

Theme two: Medical management

Subtheme one: Personal understanding of disease

All participants showed a good understanding of the nature of the disease. Participants knew that asthma is a condition related to the respiratory system; they mentioned that it is inflammation, obstruction, and blockage of the airway, bronchiole, and lungs that cause difficulty in breathing.“Asthma is the difficulty in breathing due to the constriction of the airways.”(Female, P11)“Asthma is a hyperactive, hypersensitive disease involving airways. It has a particular set of symptoms”(Male, P19)

“Asthma is the difficulty in breathing due to the constriction of the airways.”

Participants shared various causes of asthma that include smoke, dust, pollen, animal fur, allergic rhinitis, extreme temperature, air-conditioner, vigorous activities, and parental smoking.“Causes of asthma could be such as allergens such as pollen, dust, weather changes that can trigger the constriction of the Airways and then making the breathing and the exchange of air more difficult.”(Female, P11)“Oh, maybe environment is one of it and another one is the maybe the temperature and also the food.”(Male, P9)

Theme three: Behavioural management

Subtheme one: Lifestyle adaptations

(a) Environmental trigger control: The majority of the participants (n = 14) adopted different strategies to control the asthma triggering factors in the environment, such as the use of an air purifier to control dust, the use of a dehumidifier to control humidity, and frequent cleaning of the room and house to minimize dust.“I keep my rooms clean before I got my asthma, I don't care about my cleanliness my room but right now the dust triggered my allergies which is asthma, which is my asthma, so I keep everything in my rooms keep the dust away that's the way to do that I couldn't triggered my asthma.”(Male, P14)“Yeah, my mum and dad installed a air purifier and there's one in my room and then I think it helps a lot because during monthly cleaning service I can see there's a lot of dust in air, air purifier air filter.”(Female, P3)

“I keep my rooms clean before I got my asthma, I don't care about my cleanliness my room but right now the dust triggered my allergies which is asthma, which is my asthma, so I keep everything in my rooms keep the dust away that's the way to do that I couldn't triggered my asthma.”

Discussion

The study has explored the asthma patients' knowledge, perceptions, experiences, barriers, and facilitators towards self-management of their condition. Emerging themes (inductive) were identified from data through an iterative process. Relevant themes were deductively applied to three self-management tasks described by Corbin and Strauss as work of living with chronic illness, which includes medical management, behavioural management, and emotional management of disease.10

Under the medical management of the asthma theme, participants demonstrated a good understanding of the disease. The majority of participants were aware that asthma is related to the lungs and airways, and it causes shortness of breath. Regarding asthma treatment, most of them reported inhaler as a treatment option, and some of them knew the pharmacological names of treatment options like bronchodilators and corticosteroids. Having a good understanding of the illness can enhance one's confidence in managing it in practical situations.44 Regarding awareness about asthma symptom control, participants shared different strategies to prevent asthma symptoms and to keep asthma under control, such as doing aerobic exercise like walking, jogging, avoiding asthma triggers, and compliance with asthma medicines. Though participants were aware that medication adherence helps to keep asthma under control but still few participants revealed being non-adherent to their regular medication regimen and using the inhaler only when symptoms flare up.

Several studies confirmed that moderate to high regular exercise and regular physical activity improve asthma control, the quality of life of asthma patients, lung function, and asthma symptoms.45., 46, 47, 48, 49 Improved medication adherence leads to better asthma control and reduces the healthcare costs and future asthma exacerbation risk.50 It was found that a few patients only adhere to medication during symptomatic episodes, but not when they are symptom-free.51

Regarding awareness about self-monitoring of asthma symptoms, the findings highlighted that participants lacked both practice and knowledge about self-monitoring of asthma. They were less aware, particularly about devices like the peak flow meter. Only a few participants (n = 2) monitor their asthma symptoms through self-assessment techniques like counting the number of breaths per minute. Self-monitoring of asthma symptoms by patients helps in early detection of exacerbations and is considered a key step in asthma management.52

Regarding management during an asthma attack, the participant mentioned the use of a blue inhaler, which serves as the reliever inhaler, having a short-acting beta 2-agonist and is used for immediate relief of asthma symptoms. Referring to emergency to get treatment, such as nebulization in case of an asthma attack, was also reported by participants. Inhaled, short-acting selective beta 2-adrenergic agonists (most commonly used salbutamol) remain the mainstay of acute asthma therapy; their main action is to mediate airway smooth muscle relaxation.53 An emergency visit is recommended in case of a serious asthma attack or if symptoms do not subside after at-home medicine use.54

While sharing the experiences of living with asthma, participants described the significant effects of asthma on both physical and mental well-being. Asthma limited the physical activity of participants, as physical exertion beyond certain limits often led to symptom flare-ups and a sense of exhaustion, limiting patients' ability to remain active. Fatigue (defined by patients as a general tiredness and lack of energy) frequently happens in the course of respiratory illnesses as a result of laboured breathing.55, 56 Severe fatigue has been observed in 63% of a general sample of patients with asthma during outpatient consultation.57 Frequent sleep disturbances, due to asthma symptoms, restricted dietary choices, and giving up enjoyable activities such as participation in sports and social hangouts etc., were also highlighted by participants. Nocturnal awakening due to asthma symptoms37 and sleep disturbance, including difficulties in falling and maintaining sleep, as well as early morning awakenings, is a common problem in asthma patients.58., 59, 60 In addition, findings also revealed a substantial psychological burden on participants, including stress and anxiety about always carrying an inhaler and fear of symptom flare-up, especially during physical activity. Similar findings, like activity limitation, avoiding enjoyable activities, and asthma-induced anxiety and fear related to flare-up, were mentioned by studies that existed in the literature.37., 38., 40.

Under the emotional management of the asthma theme, participants described feelings of stress while managing asthma, particularly when they were newly diagnosed with the disease. One of the participants showed concern about whether, at an older age, they would be able to manage it effectively or not. A little bit of anxiety and stress is still experienced by participants, especially when they use an inhaler to subdue the asthma symptoms, and it takes some time to relieve the symptoms. Patients with chronic conditions frequently have to adjust their goals, lifestyle, and employment, and may develop psychiatric disorders, most commonly depression or anxiety.61 The demands of self-management can lead to substantial emotional distress.62 For instance, studies about type 2 diabetes reported that, patients who often have more diabetes severity and more demanding self-management requirements, had increased diabetes distress.63, 64

In order to cope with stress, participants suggested and shared different strategies like communicating with family, watching movies and playing music, meditation, and yoga. Some cognitive strategies, like focusing on other matters of life, thinking positively, and spiritual beliefs that God gives you the courage to pass through different trials, also helped patients to cope with the emotional burden caused by asthma. Therapies like talking therapy and cognitive behavioural therapy were also suggested by participants as stress-relieving therapies. Similar findings, like watching television, listening to music, talking therapy session, and seeking divine help, were also reported by patients suffering from other chronic illnesses as coping strategies to relieve emotional impact.65, 66.

Regarding behavioural management of asthma, findings revealed that participants had made several lifestyle adjustments in order to manage asthma. They incorporated regular exercises and took measures to avoid environmental triggers, like using an air purifier and a mask to control dust. Regarding dietary modification, participants avoided cold-temperature food like cold drinks, ice cream, and iced drinks. While one Chinese participant, by following Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), avoided cold-natured food like oranges for asthma management, as in TCM, there is the concept that food has hot and cold properties, so for asthma management, foods having a cold nature, like oranges, should be avoided. A similar “hot and cold food nature concept” was also reported by a study conducted in Malaysia to understand sociocultural influence on asthma.20 Regarding patients' perceptions towards asthma self-management, the study identified that the majority of participants considered asthma self-management as an important and effective way to keep asthma under control. Participants were confident that they could manage their condition well. It has been demonstrated that self-efficacy, or the perceived confidence to take action to manage chronic illness and to maintain such skills over time, is linked to improved health outcomes.67 Regarding the use of traditional methods for asthma management, few participants (n = 7) of mainly Chinese and Indian ethnicity considered both traditional and modern medicine equally helpful. Most of the participants believed that using modern medication can help them to manage asthma effectively as compared to traditional methods. One participant shared that his mother used to treat his asthma during childhood through traditional methods, but he did not find them effective and quit them. Participants (n = 2) shared that they got suggestions to use vapours from warm baths and crocodile skin powder, but they did not believe in traditional methods and preferred modern medicine. These results suggest that individuals' personal and cultural beliefs shape their self-management behaviour and strategies. It was found that cultural difference impacts the ability to manage, negotiate, and adopt strategies to manage chronic illnesses.68

Participants shared challenges that make their asthma self-management difficult. Challenges were related to patients' personal lives, environment, and healthcare. Most of the environmental challenges include dust, rainy weather, and cigarette smoking of other people at public places like cafés and restaurants. Passive smoking triggers asthma in participants, and it is the biggest challenge patients face. Social stigma was also the most reported barrier; patients felt embarrassed while using an inhaler in front of other people, as participants did not want other people to consider them weak. Participants shared that people would look at them if they use an inhaler, and they don't feel comfortable using an inhaler in public. Participants also shared their workplace-related challenges; the cold environment at the workplace was the most reported challenge. Patients' wrong attitude towards health, like intentionally skipping medicine, forgetting to keep an inhaler while leaving home, lack of self-compliance to treatment, and intentionally doing activities that trigger asthma, such as having pets and smoking. Other less frequently reported barriers included lack of awareness about asthma and its management, long waiting time for an appointment, less discussion time given by HCP to patients, and encountering different HCP at each hospital visit. These reported findings aligned with literature as patients' lack of education about asthma and its management, passive smoking, weather, long waiting times for appointments, and discontinuity of care by similar HCP were barriers related to asthma management reported by published studies.20, 35., 37., 39, 41

Regarding facilitators of asthma self-management, participants shared both current facilitating factors and factors that patients want in the future. Asthma awareness programs among patients and the general public through counselling seminars, social media, and organizational websites can help in enhancing information about asthma and its management. The provision of information by HCP through pamphlets, brochures, and written asthma action plans was also considered as an asthma management facilitator. Social support, including family and colleague support, also facilitates asthma self-management. Both family and colleagues helped patients in their tasks, such as cleaning, reminding them to take medication, and avoiding triggers. Participants' own strategies, like setting reminders for medication intake, using warm drinks and cloths in air-conditioned rooms, and acquiring asthma information from the internet, also facilitate them. Participants also recommended some suggestions that can help them in effective management. These recommendations include implementing a no-smoking policy at public places, building smoking rooms for smokers, and developing an asthma management app having asthma information, symptoms, and medication monitoring features. In existing literature, studies also reported that social support from friends and family helped patients in remembering medication intake and domestic tasks.69, 70, 71, 72. Previously conducted studies also reported on mobile applications and internet use to learn more about asthma, and setting a reminder to take medications as facilitators of asthma management.37., 38., 40., 73

Practical and future implications

The findings recommend an asthma awareness program for both patients and the general public to improve understanding of asthma and its management. Patients should be counselled regarding self-monitoring of asthma symptoms, like the use of monitoring devices and inhaler use techniques. Healthcare professionals should provide asthma-related information through brochures or pamphlets, and a written asthma action plan should be mandatory for each asthma patient. At the workplace, asthma patients should be provided with a clean and isolated workspace where they can control the air conditioning. A no-smoking policy should be implemented at public places like open cafés, as passive smoking triggers asthma. Stress-relieving therapy sessions should be conducted for patients suffering from asthma, as living with a chronic condition impacted patients' mental well-being. Future research should explore the development and effectiveness of tailored strategies to address the barriers recognized and to improve asthma outcomes.

Strengths and limitations

The qualitative design of the study provided an in-depth understanding of participants' knowledge, perceptions, and experiences, catching nuanced facilitators and barriers to asthma self-management. By using thematic analysis, the study mapped inductively emerged relevant themes to Corbin and Strauss's three self-management tasks, which provided a robust structure to organize themes, ensuring that our findings aligned with established self-management tasks. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study that reported data on knowledge, perceptions, facilitators, and barriers towards asthma self-management of Malaysian asthma patients. However, few limitations in this study need to be acknowledged as well. First the small sample size, and purposive select ion of participants limit generalizability of findings to larger population, which is a common constraint in qualitative research. Additionally, inclusion of only English-speaking asthma patients excluded eligible non-English speaker asthma patients.

Despite these limitations, this study provides valuable insights into asthma patients' self-management knowledge, perceptions, experiences, and influencing factors across Corbin and Strauss's three core tasks of self-management (medical, behavioural, and emotional management).

Conclusion

The study findings revealed that participants have a good understanding of asthma and its symptom management but still they have some lacking, like they are not well informed about self-monitoring of asthma symptoms. Educating asthma patients through counselling seminars or training sessions can help in improved practices of asthma self-management. Asthma patients' culture, beliefs and suggestions of closed one shaped their self-management practices. Healthcare provider should have taken into account the patients' practices due to cultural beliefs and guide them properly. Patients lacks proper written asthma action plan, and they find it facilitating if they have one. Healthcare provider should provide individualized written asthma action plan to each patient. Several barriers, like passive smoking, dusty and cold environment at the workplace, and social stigma, were identified. Effective strategies like implementing no-smoking policies at public places and isolated workspaces should be executed. Patients also felt stress and anxiety because of asthma; stress-relieving therapy sessions should be conducted.
