Operational Calculus on Curved Differentials: Optimal N-Complex Bounds and Persistent Homology
Mauricio Angel

Abstract
We establish a canonical normal form for the iterates of a curved differential in curved differential algebras (CDA), proving that d 2 ​ m = ( a ​ d c ) m d^{2m}=(ad_{c})^{m} and d 2 ​ m + 1 = ( a ​ d c ) m ∘ d d^{2m+1}=(ad_{c})^{m}\circ d . This operator calculus clarifies the underlying algebraic structure of CDAs and bypasses the need for complex combinatorics. Using this framework, we provide sharp criteria for curvature constraints to induce N-complex structures. We demonstrate that, while the nilpotency of the curvature element ( c n = 0 c^{n}=0 ) is insufficient to bound the nilpotency of d d to 2 ​ n 2n , it fundamentally guarantees a strict ( 4 ​ n − 2 ) (4n-2) -complex structure. On the applied side, we model curvature as a filtration controller on a genuine square–zero chain complex. This places us under the standard persistence stability framework and yields a Lipschitz control of barcodes with respect to degreewise curvature variation. A reproducible toy example on a four–vertex flag complex illustrates the mechanism.



1
Introduction

Curved differential algebras (CDA) arise as a natural extension of differential graded algebras (DGA), incorporating the notion of curvature into the differential operator. Unlike traditional differentials that strictly satisfy the square-zero condition (
d^{2}=0
), the curvature
c
in a CDA dictates that
d^{2}=[c,-]
. This enrichment of the algebraic structure provides a versatile framework to study phenomena in algebraic topology, deformation theory, and homological algebra where geometric or algebraic obstructions prevent a flat differential.

The foundational theory of curved algebras has been significantly advanced by Keller
[
8
]
, Nicolas
[
11
]
, and Brzeziński
[
2
]
, who established their connections with derived categories and corings. Lazarev
[
9
]
and Căldăraru
[
3
]
further explored their characteristic classes and Hochschild homology, and Pauksztello
[
12
]
characterizes when a differential graded R-S-bimodule induces a full embedding of derived categories. These papers collectively suggest that curved differential graded algebras generalize other algebras by providing a framework for studying algebraic structures in a more general and flexible way.

However, a persistent technical challenge in working with CDAs has been the algebraic complexity of the higher iterates of the differential,
d^{k}
. Naive expansions of
d^{k}
generate nested commutators and derivations that obscure the underlying homological properties.

In this paper, we resolve this obstruction by establishing a canonical normal form for the iterates of a curved differential (Theorem 3). We prove that every iterate
d^{k}
factors uniquely as a power of the inner derivation
ad_{c}
followed by at most one copy of
d
. This structural result drastically simplifies computations and reveals how curvature constraints naturally induce
N
-complex structures (where
d^{N}=0
).A central contribution of this work is the precise identification of the bounds for such
N
-complexes. We demonstrate through a sharp counterexample (Section 4.3) that the mere nilpotency of the curvature element (
c^{n}=0
) does not yield the naive bound
d^{2n}=0
. Rather than the naive
2n
-bound, our normal form combined with degreewise left/right nilpotency yields the universal
(4n-2)
-bound.
The free example with
n=2
shows that this exponent is sharp in that case; for general
n
, optimality remains open.

Finally, to connect with persistent homology, we refrain from curved-to-dg lifts and instead
let curvature drive the filtration on an honest chain complex. Classical stability then applies
verbatim, delivering an interleaving/bottleneck bound controlled by the
\ell^{\infty}
shift of the
filtration (Theorem 10; Corollary 11).

By connecting CDAs to corings
[
2
]
,
N
-complexes
[
1
]
, and persistent homology, this work establishes a new research program at the intersection of homological algebra and topological data science.

2
Conventions and Setting

Throughout we fix a field
k
of characteristic
0
.
All graded components
A^{i}
of our (associative, unital)
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra

A^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{i}
are finite-dimensional
k
-vector spaces.
We consider a
curved dg-algebra

(A^{\bullet},m,d,c)
in the sense that

|d|=1,\qquad|c|=2,\qquad d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}:=[c,-],\qquad d(c)=0,

(1)

where
[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{|x||y|}yx
is the graded commutator and
m
denotes the product.
We use the standard Koszul sign rule.

Inner derivations and degreewise norms.

For each
i
we fix an operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{i}
on
A^{i}
and define

\|\operatorname{ad}_{c}\|_{i\to i+2}:=\sup_{0\neq a\in A^{i}}\frac{\|[c,a]\|_{i+2}}{\|a\|_{i}}.

Unless stated otherwise, norms on
\bigoplus_{i}A^{i}
are taken to be the
\ell^{\infty}
-sum
of the degreewise norms. When we refer to “boundedness of
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
” we mean

\sup_{i}\|\operatorname{ad}_{c}\|_{i\to i+2}<\infty
.

Graded commutator identities.

For a homogeneous
x
we write
\operatorname{ad}_{x}=[x,-]
. We repeatedly use

d(xy)=d(x)\,y+(-1)^{|x|}x\,d(y),\qquad[d,\operatorname{ad}_{x}]=\operatorname{ad}_{d(x)}.

(2)

In particular, from (
1
) we obtain
[d,\operatorname{ad}_{c}]=\operatorname{ad}_{d(c)}=0
,
so
d
and
\operatorname{ad}_{c}

super-commute
as graded derivations of degrees
1
and
2
respectively.

Terminology.

Following Positselski, curved differential graded algebras are often abbreviated as
CDG-algebras
(see
[
13
,
14
]
); our notation
d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}
fits this convention, with
c
playing the role of the curvature element.

3
Iterates of a Curved Differential

We recall from §
2
that a curved dg-algebra is a quadruple

(A^{\bullet},m,d,c)
over a field
k
of characteristic
0
with
|d|=1
,
|c|=2
,

d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}:=[c,-],\qquad d(c)=0,

(3)

and the graded commutator
[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{|x||y|}yx
. We also use the graded Leibniz rule and the identity

[d,\operatorname{ad}_{x}]=\operatorname{ad}_{d(x)}
for homogeneous
x
.

3.1
Structural commutation and a reduction principle

The following observation underlies all computations with iterates of
d
.

Lemma 1

(Super-commutation of
d
and
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
)
.

In any curved dg-algebra
(
(
3
)
)
one has

[d,\operatorname{ad}_{c}]=\operatorname{ad}_{d(c)}=0.

In particular,
d
and
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
commute as endomorphisms of the graded vector space
A^{\bullet}
.

Proof.

The standard identity
[d,\operatorname{ad}_{x}]=\operatorname{ad}_{d(x)}
gives
[d,\operatorname{ad}_{c}]=\operatorname{ad}_{d(c)}
, which vanishes by
d(c)=0
.
∎

The quadratic relation
d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}
together with Lemma
1
implies that

every
word in the letter
d
can be reduced to a canonical normal form. For later use we isolate
the following consequence.

Lemma 2

(Commutation with powers of
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
)
.

For all
m\geq 0
one has
d\,(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}d
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
the operators
d
and
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
commute. Hence so do
d
and
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}
.
∎

3.2
Normal form for
d^{k}

We can now state and prove the main structural result of this section.

Theorem 3

(Normal form for the iterates of
d
)
.

Let
(A^{\bullet},m,d,c)
satisfy (
3
). Then, as endomorphisms of
A^{\bullet}
,

d^{2m}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m},\qquad d^{2m+1}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}\circ d\qquad\text{for all }m\geq 0.

(4)

Equivalently, every iterate
d^{k}
factors uniquely as a power of the inner derivation
\operatorname{ad}_{c}

followed by at most one copy of
d
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
m\geq 0
. For
m=0
the statement reads
d^{0}=\mathrm{id}
and
d^{1}=d
, which is tautological.
For
m=1
we have
d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}
by (
3
) and
d^{3}=d\circ d^{2}=d\circ\operatorname{ad}_{c}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})\circ d

by Lemma
2
.

Assume the claim holds for a fixed
m\geq 1
. Then

d^{2(m+1)}=d^{2}\circ d^{2m}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m+1},

and

d^{2(m+1)+1}=d\circ d^{2(m+1)}=d\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m+1}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m+1}\circ d,

using Lemma
2
. The induction closes.
∎

Remark 1

(No ad hoc binomial sums are needed)
.

The proof only uses the universal relations
d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}
and
[d,\operatorname{ad}_{c}]=0
.
Thus, unlike heuristic expansions that distribute
d
across products and insert formal powers of
c
,
the normal form (
4
) is canonical and independent of additional commutativity assumptions.
All graded signs are governed by the Koszul rule built into the commutator.

3.3
Immediate consequences

The normal form has several direct corollaries that will be used in later sections.

Corollary 4

(Even/odd dichotomy)
.

For any homogeneous
a\in A^{\bullet}
and
m\geq 0
one has

d^{2m}(a)=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}(a),\qquad d^{2m+1}(a)=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}\big(d(a)\big).

In particular,
d^{2m}(1)=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}(1)=0
since
[c,1]=0
.

Corollary 5

(Nilpotency transfer)
.

If
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{n}=0
degreewise for some
n\geq 1
, then
d^{2n}=0
and
(A^{\bullet},d)
is a
2n
-complex.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
3
with
m=n
.
∎

The next criterion is convenient when
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
-nilpotency is verified via the curvature ideal.

Proposition 1

(Nilpotent curvature ideal implies
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
-nilpotency)
.

Let
(c)
denote the two-sided ideal of
A^{\bullet}
generated by
c
. If
(c)^{n}=0
for some
n
, then

(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{n}=0
and hence
d^{2n}=0
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{ad}_{c}(A^{\bullet})\subset(c)
, we have
\operatorname{ad}_{c}^{r}(A^{\bullet})\subset(c)^{r}
for all
r
. If
(c)^{n}=0
then
\operatorname{ad}_{c}^{n}=0
.
Now invoke Corollary
5
.
∎

Remark 2

(On the hypotheses)
.

The identity
[d,\operatorname{ad}_{c}]=0
crucially uses
d(c)=0
. If
d(c)\neq 0
, the normal form (
4
) may fail.
In curved dg-algebras, however,
d(c)=0
is part of the definition (
3
), so the lemma and theorem apply.

3.4
Minimal examples

We record two toy examples illustrating the scope of Theorem
3
.

Example 1

(Central curvature)
.

Suppose
c
is central:
[c,a]=0
for all
a\in A^{\bullet}
. Then
\operatorname{ad}_{c}=0
and
Theorem
3
gives
d^{2}=0
and
d^{2m}=0
for all
m\geq 1
; thus
(A^{\bullet},d)
is an ordinary dg-algebra.

Example 2

(Matrix algebra with inner differential)
.

Let
A^{\bullet}=\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(k)
concentrated in degree
0
and fix
C\in A^{0}
.
Define
d=\operatorname{ad}_{C}
(degree
0\to 0
) and
c=C^{2}\in A^{0}
. Then
d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{C}^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{C^{2}}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}
and
d(c)=\operatorname{ad}_{C}(C^{2})=[C,C^{2}]=0
,
so (
3
) holds. If
C
is nilpotent of index
n
, then
(\operatorname{ad}_{C})^{n}=0
(Engel-type), hence
d^{2n}=0
by
Corollary
5
.

The normal form (
4
) reduces all higher iterates of
d
to iterates of the inner derivation
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
,
followed by at most one copy of
d
. This representation will be the backbone for the
N
-complex bounds in §
4

and for the curved-to-dg construction used in persistence in §
5
.

4
Curvature Constraints and
N
-Complex Bounds

Recall that an
N
-complex is a graded module
(C^{\bullet},d)
with
d^{N}=0
.
Using the normal form from Theorem
3
, we obtain clean
criteria ensuring that
(A^{\bullet},d)
is an
N
-complex.

4.1
From curvature to nilpotency of
d

Corollary 6

(
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
-nilpotency implies an
N
-complex)
.

If
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{n}=0
(degreewise) for some
n\geq 1
, then
d^{2n}=0
, hence
(A^{\bullet},d)

is a
2n
-complex.

Proof.

By Theorem
3
,
d^{2n}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{n}=0
.
∎

The previous condition can be checked via natural algebraic hypotheses on
c
.

Proposition 2

(Nilpotent curvature ideal)
.

Let
(c)
be the two-sided ideal of
A^{\bullet}
generated by
c
. If
(c)^{n}=0
for some
n
,
then
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{n}=0
and, consequently,
(A^{\bullet},d)
is a
2n
-complex.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{ad}_{c}(A^{\bullet})\subset(c)
, we have
\operatorname{ad}_{c}^{r}(A^{\bullet})\subset(c)^{r}
for all
r
.
Thus
(c)^{n}=0
implies
\operatorname{ad}_{c}^{n}=0
. Apply Corollary
6
.
∎

4.2
A sharp degreewise criterion via left/right multiplications

Write
L_{c}
and
R_{c}
for left and right multiplication by
c
on
A^{\bullet}
:

L_{c}(a)=ca
and
R_{c}(a)=ac
. Note that
L_{c}
and
R_{c}
commute and

\operatorname{ad}_{c}=L_{c}-R_{c}
as endomorphisms of the underlying graded vector space.

Lemma 7

(Binomial expansion for
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
)
.

For every
r\geq 0
,

(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{r}=(L_{c}-R_{c})^{r}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{r}{j}\,L_{c}^{\,r-j}\,R_{c}^{\,j}.

Proof.

Since
L_{c}R_{c}=R_{c}L_{c}
, the usual binomial theorem applies to the commuting operators
L_{c}
and
R_{c}
.
∎

Proposition 3

(Left/right nilpotency
\Rightarrow
explicit bound)
.

Assume
L_{c}^{n}=0
and
R_{c}^{n}=0
for some
n\geq 1
. Then
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{2n-1}=0
.
In particular,
d^{4n-2}=0
and
(A^{\bullet},d)
is a
(4n-2)
-complex.

Proof.

By Lemma
7
, any summand of
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{r}
has the form
L_{c}^{\,r-j}R_{c}^{\,j}
.
If
r\geq 2n-1
, then either
j\geq n
or
r-j\geq n
, so
L_{c}^{\,r-j}R_{c}^{\,j}=0
.
Hence
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{2n-1}=0
. The claim for
d
follows from Theorem
3
.
∎

Remark 3

(Hierarchy of hypotheses)
.

The implication
(c)^{n}=0\Rightarrow L_{c}^{n}=R_{c}^{n}=0
holds, hence
Proposition
2
is a direct consequence of
Proposition
3
, which also yields the explicit
(4n-2)
bound for
d
.
By contrast,
c^{n}=0
alone does
not
force
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{n}=0
nor any
2n
-bound for
d

(see Example
3
).

Remark 4

(On
N
-complexes: historical note)
.

The notion of
N
-complex goes back at least to Mayer’s alternative homology
[
10
]

and was revitalized in Kapranov’s
q
-analog of homological algebra
[
7
]
, with broader surveys in
Dubois-Violette
[
6
]
. Our bounds exploit the normal form
d^{2m}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}
and degreewise operator
controls on left/right multiplications by
c
.

4.3
The Optimal
N
-Complex Bound and its Obstruction

We now address the precise relationship between the nilpotency of the curvature element
c
and the induced
N
-complex structure. A naive assumption might be that
c^{n}=0
implies
d^{2n}=0
. As we will show, this is false. However, combining our normal form (Theorem 3) with the binomial expansion of
ad_{c}
(Lemma 7) yields a spectacular and tight universal bound.

Theorem 8

(Optimal Curvature-Induced Complex)
.

Let
(A^{\bullet},m,d,c)
be a curved dg-algebra. If
c^{n}=0
for some
n\geq 1
, then
(A^{\bullet},d)
is a
(4n-2)
-complex (i.e.,
d^{4n-2}=0
). Furthermore, this bound sharp for
n=2
(free example), while optimality for general
n
remains open.

Proof.

Since
c^{n}=0
in the algebra, the left and right multiplication operators satisfy
L_{c}^{n}=0
and
R_{c}^{n}=0
as endomorphisms on
A^{\bullet}
. By Lemma 7,
(ad_{c})^{2n-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{2n-1}{j}L_{c}^{2n-1-j}R_{c}^{j}
. For every term in this sum, either
j\geq n
or
(2n-1-j)\geq n
. Thus, every summand contains either
R_{c}^{n}
or
L_{c}^{n}
, forcing
(ad_{c})^{2n-1}=0
. Applying the normal form from Theorem 3 (
d^{2m}=(ad_{c})^{m}
) with
m=2n-1
, we immediately obtain
d^{4n-2}=0
.
∎

4.4
A counterexample:
c^{n}=0
does not control
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{n}

We show that the nilpotency of
c
alone is insufficient to infer a nilpotency bound for
d
.
Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
and consider the associative algebra

A:=k\langle x,y\rangle/(x^{2}),

graded by
|x|=2
,
|y|=0
. Set
c:=x\in A^{2}
, so
c^{2}=x^{2}=0
.

Lemma 9
.

In
A
one has
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{2}(y)=-2\,xyx\neq 0
.

Proof.

Compute
\operatorname{ad}_{c}(y)=xy-yx
. Then

(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{2}(y)=\operatorname{ad}_{c}(xy-yx)=x(xy-yx)-(xy-yx)x=xxy-xyx-xyx+yxx.

Using
x^{2}=0
the first and last terms vanish, giving
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{2}(y)=-2xyx\neq 0
in
A
.
∎

To realize a curved differential with
d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}
and
d(c)=0
, we adjoin an odd square root
of
c
as follows. Let
\widetilde{A}
be the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra obtained by adjoining
a homogeneous element
\theta
of degree
1
subject to the single relation
\theta^{2}=c

(and extending the product by the graded Leibniz rule). Define

d:=\operatorname{ad}_{\theta}\quad\text{on }\widetilde{A}.

Then
d
is a degree-
1
derivation with

d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{\theta}^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{\theta^{2}}=\operatorname{ad}_{c},\qquad d(c)=[\theta,\theta^{2}]=0,

so
(\widetilde{A},m,d,c)
is a curved dg-algebra in the sense of (
3
).

We now demonstrate that the bound
4n-2
is strictly optimal, and that the mere nilpotency
c^{n}=0
cannot force
(ad_{c})^{n}=0
nor
d^{2n}=0
.

Example 3

(Failure of the
2n
-bound and optimality of
4n-2
)
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic 0 and consider the associative algebra
A:=k\langle x,y\rangle/(x^{2})
graded by
|x|=2,|y|=0
. Set
c:=x\in A^{2}
, so
c^{2}=0
(here
n=2
).

We compute the action of
(ad_{c})^{2}
on
y
:

(ad_{c})^{2}(y)=ad_{c}(xy-yx)=x(xy-yx)-(xy-yx)x=x^{2}y-2xyx+yx^{2}

Using
x^{2}=0
, the first and last terms vanish, leaving
(ad_{c})^{2}(y)=-2xyx\neq 0
. To realize a curved differential, we adjoin an odd element
\theta
of degree 1 subject to
\theta^{2}=c
, and define
d:=ad_{\theta}
. Then
d^{2}=ad_{\theta}^{2}=ad_{c}
and
d(c)=0
, satisfying the CDA axioms.

For
n=2
, the naive bound would predict
d^{2n}=d^{4}=0
. However, by Theorem 3:
d^{4}(y)=(ad_{c})^{2}(y)=-2xyx\neq 0
.

This proves that
c^{n}=0
does not imply
d^{2n}=0
. The true vanishing occurs exactly at the optimal bound provided by Theorem
8
: for
n=2
, the complex is a
(4(2)-2)=6
-complex. Indeed,
(ad_{c})^{3}=L_{c}^{3}-3L_{c}^{2}R_{c}+3L_{c}R_{c}^{2}-R_{c}^{3}=0
since
c^{2}=0
, meaning
d^{6}=0
.

Remark 5

(On the
(4n-2)
bound)
.

Under the degreewise left/right nilpotency
L_{c}^{n}=R_{c}^{n}=0
, Proposition
3

yields
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{2n-1}=0
and therefore
d^{4n-2}=0
. Example
3
shows
that one cannot deduce
any

2n
-bound from
c^{n}=0
alone.

The bounds above will be used in two ways. First, Corollary
6
reduces the
problem of proving
d^{N}=0
to checking
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{N/2}=0
. Second, Proposition
3

provides a practical criterion—verifiable degreewise on left/right multiplications—that implies
the
(4n-2)
bound for
d
. These will feed into the constructions of §
5
.

5
A Minimal Persistence Application

This section provides a reproducible and fully rigorous way to interface curvature with persistence.
Instead of forcing a square-zero property by
curving
the differential, we keep a genuine
chain complex with
\partial^{2}=0
and let the curvature
modify the filtration
.
This places us under the umbrella of the standard stability theorems for persistence
modules over a field, via interleavings or bottleneck distance

[
5
,
4
]
.

5.1
Curvature-controlled filtrations

Let
K
be a finite simplicial complex and
(C_{\bullet}(K;k),\partial)
its chain complex with coefficients
in the base field
k
from §
2
. Fix a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
A^{2}
and a family of
filtration functions

\phi:A^{2}\longrightarrow\{\text{functions }\phi_{c}:\ \mathrm{Simp}(K)\to\mathbb{R}\},\qquad c\longmapsto\phi_{c},

such that there exists a constant
L\geq 0
with

\|\phi_{c}-\phi_{c^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Simp}(K)}|\phi_{c}(\sigma)-\phi_{c^{\prime}}(\sigma)|\ \leq\ L\,\|c-c^{\prime}\|\qquad\text{for all }c,c^{\prime}\in A^{2}.

(5)

We get a
1
-parameter filtration by declaring that a simplex
\sigma
enters at time

t=\phi_{c}(\sigma)
; we denote the resulting filtered chain complex by
(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c}_{\bullet})
.

5.2
Stability theorem

Let
H_{\ast}(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c}_{\bullet})
be the persistence module obtained by applying homology degreewise
to
(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c}_{\bullet})
.

Theorem 10

(Stability under curvature-driven filtration changes)
.

Let
c,c^{\prime}\in A^{2}
and set
\Delta:=\|\phi_{c}-\phi_{c^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}
.
Then the interleaving distance (equivalently, the bottleneck distance of the barcodes) between

H_{\ast}(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c}_{\bullet})
and
H_{\ast}(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c^{\prime}}_{\bullet})
is at most
\Delta
.
In particular, if (
5
) holds, then

d_{I}\!\Big(H_{\ast}(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c}_{\bullet}),\,H_{\ast}(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c^{\prime}}_{\bullet})\Big)\ \leq\ L\,\|c-c^{\prime}\|.

Proof sketch.

A uniform
\infty
-bound on the filtration shift produces an interleaving of the same size,
hence a bottleneck bound
\leq\Delta
; see the classical persistence stability theorem for
filtered chain complexes over a field

[
5
,
4
]
.
The Lipschitz conclusion follows immediately from (
5
).
∎

Corollary 11

(Bar-length robustness)
.

Let
I=[b,d)
be a bar in the barcode of
H_{\ast}(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c}_{\bullet})
.
If
L\,\|c-c^{\prime}\|<\tfrac{1}{2}(d-b)
, then there exists a matching bar

I^{\prime}=[b^{\prime},d^{\prime})
in the barcode of
H_{\ast}(C_{\bullet},F^{\,c^{\prime}}_{\bullet})

with
|b-b^{\prime}|,|d-d^{\prime}|\leq L\,\|c-c^{\prime}\|
.

5.3
Toy example: a flag complex on four vertices

Let
K
be the flag complex on the graph with vertices
V=\{1,2,3,4\}
and edges

E=\{12,23,34,41,13\}
; the unique
2
-simplex is
\tau=[1,2,3]
.
Define
\phi_{c}
by

\phi_{c}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}0,&\sigma\in V,\\
1,&\sigma\in E,\\
2+\ell(c),&\sigma=\tau,\end{cases}

where
\ell:A^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a fixed linear functional (choose
\|\ell\|=1
with respect to the dual norm).
Then
\|\phi_{c}-\phi_{c^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}=|\ell(c)-\ell(c^{\prime})|\leq\|c-c^{\prime}\|
, so
L=1
in (
5
).
In degree
1
, there is a single prominent bar born at time
1
and dying when
\tau
enters,
namely at
t=2+\ell(c)
. By Theorem
10
and
Corollary
11
, changing
c\mapsto c^{\prime}
perturbs this bar by at most

|\ell(c)-\ell(c^{\prime})|
in both birth/death times, hence in its length.

Implementation note.

For numerical experiments, one may pick any norm on
A^{2}
(e.g. an operator norm induced
degreewise) and fix a small family of linear functionals
\ell_{j}
to define several
2
-simplices’
entry times. The constant
L
is then
\max_{j}\|\ell_{j}\|
, and the same stability bound applies
verbatim
[
5
,
4
]
.

6
Conclusions

We established a canonical normal form for the iterates of a curved differential:
for any curved dg-algebra
(A^{\bullet},m,d,c)
with
d^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{c}
and
d(c)=0
,
Theorem
3
shows that
d^{2m}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}
and
d^{2m+1}=(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}d
.
This simple but robust factorization reduces all higher powers of
d
to powers of
the inner derivation
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
and makes the sign bookkeeping transparent.
As immediate consequences, nilpotency of
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
yields nilpotency of
d

(Corollary
6
), while structural hypotheses such as the
nilpotency of the curvature ideal
(c)
or of the left/right multiplications by
c

provide explicit
N
-complex bounds (Propositions
2
and
3
).
The counterexample in §
4.4
illustrates that the mere nilpotency of
c

does not control
d
without additional assumptions, delineating a sharp frontier for feasible bounds.

On the applied side, instead of forcing a curved-to-dg extension, we modeled curvature
as a
filtration controller
: a square–zero chain complex endowed with a
curvature–dependent filtration. This design places us under the umbrella of the
standard persistence stability framework and yields a clean Lipschitz control of
barcodes with respect to degreewise curvature variation
(Theorem
10
and Corollary
11
).
A fully reproducible toy example in §
5.3
illustrates the mechanism
on a small flag complex.

Taken together, these contributions clarify the algebraic mechanics of curved differentials,
pin down verifiable hypotheses guaranteeing
N
-complex structure, and supply a minimal
and principled pipeline to interface curvature with persistent homology.
We expect the normal form, the hierarchy of curvature constraints, and the curved-to-dg extension
to be useful beyond the examples shown here, notably in controlled deformations and quantitative
homological algebra.

7
Open Problems and Future Directions

We conclude with a concise list of questions that naturally extend the present work.

1.

Optimal
N
-bounds under weak hypotheses.

We proved that
L_{c}^{n}=R_{c}^{n}=0
implies
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{2n-1}=0
and hence
d^{4n-2}=0

(Proposition
3
), while
(c)^{n}=0
yields
d^{2n}=0

(Proposition
2
).

Question:
determine optimal (smallest possible) exponents for
d
under
intermediate assumptions between
c^{n}=0
and
(c)^{n}=0
.
In particular, characterize when
c^{n}=0
forces
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{n^{\prime}}=0
degreewise for some
n^{\prime}
,
and identify algebraic obstructions.

2.

Degreewise spectral data for
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
.

The normal form reduces
d^{2m}
to
(\operatorname{ad}_{c})^{m}
.

Question:
develop a degreewise functional calculus for
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
(e.g. Jordan/​Engel-type
decompositions by degree) that yields refined
N
-bounds or filtration-sensitive estimates,
possibly depending on the graded support of
c
.

3.

Stability constants in persistence.

Theorem
10
yields a Lipschitz control where the constant is the
\ell^{\infty}
shift
of the filtration (or an a priori Lipschitz bound on the curvature-to-filtration map).

Question:
derive sharp, computable expressions for these constants in common filtration families
(e.g. sublevel, flag/Rips, degree truncations), and study tightness on explicit parametric examples,
in the spirit of classical stability results
[
5
,
4
]
.

4.

Homological invariants beyond barcodes.

Question:
identify invariants of
(A^{\bullet},d,c)
that are preserved or vary
Lipschitz-continuously under bounded
\operatorname{ad}_{c}
-perturbations, such as torsion exponents,
quantitative Dehn functions, or stability of minimal models, and compare their sensitivity
to that of persistence barcodes.

5.

Controlled deformations and MC solutions.

The Maurer–Cartan viewpoint suggests varying
d
within the curved constraint.

Question:
study solution sets of
d_{\varepsilon}=d+\varepsilon\,\operatorname{ad}_{c}
at small
\varepsilon

and their moduli, seeking KAM-type or Lojasiewicz-type principles that control
the emergence/vanishing of homological features.

6.

Geometric and physical templates (structural, not assertive).

Without claiming classification results, the identities
d_{A}^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{F_{A}}
in gauge theory
and
\nabla^{2}=\operatorname{ad}_{R}
in Riemannian settings offer structural testbeds.

Question:
in discretized or sheaf-theoretic frameworks, isolate regimes where
left/right nilpotency of curvature operators holds (or nearly holds) and quantify the induced

N
-complex bounds; clarify the role of curvature ideals in these contexts.

Outlook.

The techniques developed here—normal form for iterates, curvature-constraint hierarchy,
and the curved-to-dg lift—appear robust and modular. We anticipate applications to
quantitative homological algebra, stability of algebraic invariants under controlled curvature,
and principled bridges to applied topological settings. A companion line of work will address
richer examples and sharper constants, as well as functorial enhancements of the curved-to-dg construction.
