1.0 Introduction
 Thank you for being a U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM)
 user! We hope this guide will help you get started with your ARM field campaign (AFC). It provides
 information useful to all AFC investigators and includes ARM mobile facility (AMF) investigator-
 specific information.

 This guide is primarily for investigators whose campaigns have been approved by ARM. More
 information on the AFC proposal process is available at: https://arm.gov/research/campaigns.

 ARM is a large, complex, distributed organization. Here are a few links to help those unfamiliar with
 ARM:
  • ARM high-level organizational structure
  • ARM acronym list
  • ARM glossary
  • Help request at the bottom of every ARM web page
  • ARM User Program Information.

2.0 Expectations of ARM Principal Investigators
 Prior to submitting an AFC proposal, principal investigators acknowledge and agree to a set of
 Expectations of ARM principal investigators, which may be referenced at any time on the ARM website.
 These expectations include:
  • ARM code of conduct
  • Deliverables you are responsible for, e.g., data submission and final report
  • How to acknowledge ARM.

3.0 ARM Data Requirements
 ARM data requirements are critical to the continuation of the ARM User Facility. AFC investigators must
 respect these requirements to maintain our ability to support you and future investigators. Please be
 mindful to:
  • Provide collaborators with links to the ARM Data Discovery for data download. DO NOT provide
 copies of the data to others. This allows ARM to maintain our reportable metrics to DOE.
  • Use the quality information provided to understand any known limitations or concerns related to data
 sets.
  • Provide data sets from your guest instruments to ARM within the expected timelines.

4.0 Communication with ARM about Your AFC
 You may contact ARM at any time during your campaign through the “Contact Us” link at the top right
 of every ARM webpage. This includes discussing any changes you want to request to the AFC (i.e.,
 website information/links, date changes, delayed or additional activities).

 Figure 1. Contact Us link on every ARM webpage with “Campaigns and Accessing ARM Facilities”

 4.1 AFC Decision Notifications
 Once decisions are made on AFC requests, ARM will send approval or declination notifications by email
 to the lead scientist. AMF approval or declination notifications will be sent by DOE. AMF approval
 notifications will include details of the specific scope that has been approved. Primary contacts will be
 identified in the approval notification.

 4.2 Deliverable Submission Notifications
 ARM will remind you and request your deliverable submissions from the ARM ServiceNow system
 email armcrf@servicenowservices.com. Please add this email to your safe senders list so that
 notifications do not go to your spam/junk mail folders. You will be able to respond to these system emails
 with any questions or updates, including submission of your deliverables.

 4.3 Notify ARM of a Data Quality Issue
 Please contact ARM if you notice any data quality issues during your campaign through the “Contact Us”
 link at the top right of every ARM webpage.

4.4 ARM Field Campaign Acronym
ARM communications will also review the campaign acronym for any sensitivities or copyright
infringements. ARM reserves the right to change your acronym if inappropriate or copyrighted. For help
with acronym creation, see suggested guidance for developing a good ARM acronym.

4.5 ARM Field Campaign Dates, Intensive Operational Periods, and
 Extensions
The proposed AFC start and end dates will be reviewed with the proposal. During the review, the
observatory manager may discuss changes to these dates due to logistical challenges. ARM will confirm
field campaign start and end dates when a campaign is approved. These dates encompass the entire effort.

Intermittent activities or targeted times of scientific interest may be identified as intensive operational
periods (IOPs). IOPs should typically be identified during the AFC proposal process. For large campaigns
(i.e., AMF or aerial campaigns) they may be identified after campaign approval and during science plan
development. During these periods, measurements may be increased or adjusted to better capture the
processes most relevant to the PI’s science goals. IOP activities may include a period when guest
instruments are deployed or a period when ARM observations are enhanced or altered. Examples of IOP
activities could include special radar scan strategies, enhanced coordination of instruments, or site
activities during an aerial campaign.

AFC extensions may be requested at any time until the last month of the campaign. A deliverable
notification will be created in the system for you to provide the required interim report. ARM will review
the extension request after the report is received and appropriate data is in the archive or the data plan is
confirmed.

Please discuss any desired change to the dates with the observatory manager. Changes to campaign or
IOP dates may be requested through the “Contact Us” link at the top right of every ARM webpage.

5.0 Guest Instruments and Data

 5.1 Guest Instruments
 Guest instruments are any non-ARM instruments deployed for an AFC. Each guest instrument MUST be
 approved through the AFC process. Through this process you provided the specific information about the
 instrument(s) and support needs. Note that the person responsible for the guest instrument, if not the
 principal investigator (PI), should be included as a co-investigator on the campaign.

 Please review the guest instrumentation for your approved AFC with your site operations contact to
 ensure everyone is of the same understanding of the upcoming activities. If anything changes related to a
 guest instrument, immediately contact your site operations contact.

 The AFC investigator is responsible for any instrumentation licensing or waivers. ARM will require a
 copy of any licensing or waivers to confirm that the requirements have been met prior to operation of the
 instrument at any ARM site. This applies, but is not limited to, radio frequency authorizations and laser
 transmission authorizations.

 5.2 Adding Instrumentation to an AFC
 Under some circumstances ARM will allow guest instrumentation to be added to approved AFCs, without
 requiring a new AFC. However, any additional instrument still requires an ARM Instrument Support
 Request (ISR) approval prior to consideration. Some examples of instruments that may be allowed to be
 added to approved AFCs include:
  • Support sensors for approved instrumentation when data will only be used for maintenance of the
 guest instrument
  • Instrumentation within the approved proposal scope, such as one used to replace a failed instrument.

 4

The following circumstances require a separate AFC request for additional guest instrumentation to be
operated at a site during an approved AFC:
 • Instrumentation expanding the scope of the AFC
 • Instrumentation other investigators are interested in bringing.

5.3 Guest Instrument Data
Each approved guest instrument requires upload of the associated data within six months of the campaign.
The lead investigator is responsible for insuring that all guest instrument data from the campaign is
submitted to ARM.

The ARM website has more information about submitting data, including guidance on data Digital Object
Identifiers (DOIs).

For data that are available to the public in another archive, ARM will consider linking to that data on the
campaign web page, rather than requiring the investigator to submit the data to ARM. Approval will
depend on ARM’s judgement of the sustainability and public accessibility of the alternative archive.
University archives will generally not be considered.

Once submitted and approved, guest instrument data will be available to the community through ARM
Data Discovery.

5.4 AFC Webpage
ARM provides a dynamic campaign web page displaying information from across the ARM website to
the public about the campaign. The webpage includes the public abstract submitted as part of the proposal
process. Please review the information on the webpage, particularly if it has been a long time since the
proposal was submitted.

Associated guest instrumentation will be visible at the bottom of the page once data are submitted for the
instrument.

Links on the right will include the science plan (if required), any ARM news or media links, campaign
dashboard, and other relevant information, including associated ARM campaigns and data. Investigators
can request the addition of links to any external campaigns/data associated with the ARM campaign. If
changes to the page are needed, you may use the ‘Help’ request at the bottom of any ARM webpage.

5.5 Investigator-Hosted Websites
In addition to the campaign webpage, ARM provides a campaign dashboard to share ARM and PI
quicklooks and reports. If an AFC investigator wants to create or use their own website for the
campaigns, please consider the following:
 • Coordinate with ARM Communications so both sites are enhancing the campaign message.

 5

 • Request a link to the PI web page on the ARM campaign web page and be sure to link the PI web
 page to the ARM campaign web page.
 • Link to ARM Data Discovery for data.
 • Do not display static plots of ARM data on a PI web page. Please see quicklook options; this ensures
 that any plots are up to date in case ARM data are reprocessed.

5.6 Site Access for Work and Tours
Science teams and collaborators, including students, are welcome to visit the site with approved access.
All visits to the site must be requested through the ARM Access Request System Portal and approved
before visitors will be allowed on site. To ensure adequate time to process your request, submit requests
at least 14 days in advance if you are a U.S. citizen and at least 45 days in advance if you are a non-U.S.
citizen. Site-specific information is available as you fill out the request, such as safety information and
restrictions. Details are provided on ARM’s website under the User Program section. If you have any
questions, use the ‘Help’ link at the bottom of every ARM webpage or contact the observatory manager
or delegate.

Note: Site tours are available during the campaign. Please coordinate as early as possible with the
observatory manager or delegate to arrange. ARM Website Information about visiting ARM sites.

5.7 ARM Tools Assisting AFC Investigators
ARM has tools to assist you with ARM data as well as quicklooks for guest instrumentation and some
external data. Please explore the links below and use the ‘Help’ request at the bottom of every ARM
webpage for assistance.

5.7.1 ARM DOI Manager

The new ARM DOI Manager assists in reviewing, reserving, and minting DOIs for ARM data. Features
include:
 • Advanced DOI search-to-search and filter DOIs over multiple fields to find the record
 • Enhanced DOI form to search for authors via the ARM and ORCiD databases, specify related
 identifiers, and manage visibility of DOIs.

5.7.2 Quicklooks

Basic quicklook plots for ARM data are available on ARM Data Discovery, while more advanced
quicklooks are available through the ARM Field Campaign Dashboard that are subsets of plots from the
DQ Plotbrowser. DQ-Zoom is an data visualization tool that can be used to interactively interrogate the
data without having to download it.

 6

 5.7.3 Data Dashboard

 The ARM Data Dashboard provides a platform to create customized dashboard of data, either through the
 use of interactive plots or by pulling in quicklooks from the Data Quality Office. A user can tailor their
 view to what is most relevant to them.

 5.7.4 ARM Data Workbench

 ARM has an alpha release of our data workbench that helps users find data for download, staging data for
 computation, visualizing data, exploring the ARM knowledge base, and data submission.

 5.7.5 Computing Resources

 ARM offers open-source tools and high-performance computing resources to work with ARM data.
 Access to the high-performance computing resources requires a separate High-Performance Computing
 request.

6.0 AMF Investigator-Specific Information

 6.1 Campaign Planning and Deployment
 Once you are notified of an approved AMF deployment, ARM will begin working towards the execution
 of the campaign under the conditions of the DOE approval letter. Regardless of the length of time until
 the campaign starts, it is essential to begin planning and development activities immediately and to have
 strong engagement between the science team and ARM during this planning period to ensure a successful
 campaign.

 The observatory manager, or their delegate, is the main ARM point of contact for the campaign and is
 responsible for engagement and coordination with the lead scientist, host sites, and ARM infrastructure.

 6.2 Kickoff Meeting
 The ARM observatory manager will set up an initial kickoff meeting with DOE, ARM, and the lead
 campaign scientist soon after approval of the campaign. The meeting should include any critical
 co-investigators, such as those responsible for guest instrumentation or data monitoring during the
 campaign. This meeting will cover campaign planning and additional ARM resources available to the
 science team.

 6.3 Lead Scientist Responsibilities
 ARM is responsible for mobile facility site development, installation, and operations and will make all
 final decisions about implementation of the campaign. However, the lead scientist is critical in providing
 scientific guidance and input requested by the ARM observatory manager or ARM leadership to guide
 ARM operations to ensure the success and scientific value of the campaign.

 7

Scientific input may include, but is not limited to, feedback on decisions about site location, instrument
siting and layout, instrument substitution, or instrument operations. Engagement by the science team
ensures science goals are met alongside operational and logistical requirements, (i.e., land agreements,
environmental reviews, instrument licensing, safety). Lead scientist need and ability to support siting
visits should be included in the proposal and any support negotiated prior to approval of the AFC.

The lead scientist is the main point of contact for the science team and is responsible for developing a
public-facing science plan and providing timely responses to ARM requests for information or input for
campaign planning. The lead scientist may develop a steering committee and/or delegate co-investigators
to manage specific aspects of the campaign planning; however, if there is disagreement among the science
team, it is the lead scientist’s responsibility to make decisions on behalf of the science team and
communicate them to ARM.

6.4 Instrumentation and Data Products
ARM will develop an ARM instrument, ARM data products, and guest instrument list for each AMF
campaign, based on the proposal and any conditions in the approval letter. The lead scientist should
review this list regularly to ensure clear understanding of what has been approved. Any additional
requests for guest instrumentation must be submitted through the ARM Field Campaign Process.

Note: AMF campaigns are freely open to the scientific community. Any scientist (not just official
co-investigators) may submit an AFC to deploy guest instruments to an AMF campaign site.

6.5 Campaign Timeline and Beginning Operations
As part of campaign planning, ARM will develop a specific timeline for site development, installation,
data verification, operation, and pack up. ARM will coordinate this timeline with the lead scientist,
subject to ARM’s operational and logistical constraints.

The campaign officially begins on the operational start date. Please be mindful that many internal ARM
activities happen prior to the operational start date including instrument installation, configuration,
review, and calibrations that require the coordination of many people across ARM. There is a period
(called the data verification period) in which instruments may be operating but the measurements are not
suitable for scientific use.

6.6 Science Coordination and Science Plan
The AMF lead scientist has the responsibility to coordinate an effective science team for the campaign
and is also encouraged to do outreach to the broader scientific community to maximize the scientific
impact of the AMF campaign.

The science plan, due nominally 90 days after award, informs ARM as well as the broader scientific
community of the science team plans and intentions toward meeting the scientific campaign goals.

 8

Note: The lead scientists should cross-reference the ARM instrument and data products list prior to
submitting the campaign science plan to ensure accuracy and joint understanding of approved
instrumentation.

Science teams have used the following activities to be successful:
 • Lead and coordinate the science team with routine meetings.
 • Present the campaign at ARM/ASR and other scientific meetings.
 • Coordinate and collaborate with other non-ARM campaigns and institutions.
 • Ensure all co-investigators understand their roles in guest instrument operation, data submission, and
 reporting.

6.7 ARM Campaign Dashboard
ARM provides a dashboard for AMF campaigns to support the science team with custom quicklooks,
ARM data quality plots, external data, and event capture. This platform is highly flexible and
configurable for specific campaign needs. Please review the ARM Field Campaign Dashboard and past
AMF campaigns for ideas. A contact for the campaign dashboard will be provided at the kickoff meeting.
To allow sufficient time for ARM to develop campaign-specific elements for each campaign,
requirements need to be determined in the planning phase. ARM strives to encourage science team
engagement with data and seeks all coordination to avoid duplication and achieve as much as possible
together.

6.8 Campaign Communications
ARM Communications provides information and outreach materials and support for ARM’s mobile,
aerial, and major (typically, level 3 and 4) field campaigns. With lead scientist guidance, ARM
Communications prepares an outreach plan and will work with the PI’s institution and the collaborating
institutions on coordination of possible press releases and kickoff events. The lead scientist or
co-investigator institution will be given the opportunity of first issue of any press releases. Additional
materials, such as backgrounders (single-sheet informational handouts), site posters, and introduction
videos, are prepared after the science plan is made available. Separate communications meetings will be
requested with the PI and the PI’s institutional communicators throughout the campaign to support
amplifying campaign milestones and successes.

Note: ARM does not support the creation of campaign-specific logos; the ARM logo is the preferred logo
and brand for ARM campaigns. However, ARM does develop a campaign sticker image for AMF
campaigns (part of ARM’s sticker series) to be used in outreach efforts. If a PI feels strongly about having
a campaign-specific logo, it is for your own use. The ARM logo will continue to be the preferred logo on
all ARM outreach materials.
