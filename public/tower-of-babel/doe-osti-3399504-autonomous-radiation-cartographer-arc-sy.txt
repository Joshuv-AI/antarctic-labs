1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose of This Training
This training manual is designed to provide end users with a comprehensive knowledge base for
the safe and effective use of the Autonomous Radiation Cartographer (ARC) System. The ARC
is a fully autonomous radiation detection robot based on the Spot Robot platform manufactured
by Boston Dynamics.

1.2 Learning Objectives:
Upon completion of this training, users will be able to:

  Understand the capabilities and limitations of the ARC system
  Safely operate the robot in both manual and autonomous modes
  Perform routine maintenance and basic troubleshooting
  Respond appropriately to emergency situations
  Work safely alongside the robot in operational environments

1.3 Training Importance:
Proper training ensures:

  Safe operation in radiation environments
  Effective system deployment
  Confident troubleshooting of appropriate-level issues
  Maintenance of ARC system
  Compliance with safety and security protocols

2. Vocabulary and Key Terms

Understanding the following terminology is essential for effective communication and system
operation:

Robot: A programmable machine capable of carrying out complex actions automatically or
through remote control.
Motor: Electromechanical actuators that power Spot's legs and enable movement. These motors
are extremely powerful and can cause injury if proper precautions are not followed.
Chassis: The main structural frame of the robot that houses the core components and supports
the payload.
Crash Pad: Protective padding on the robot designed to absorb impact and protect components
during falls or collisions.
Payload: Equipment mounted on top of the robot for specific mission purposes. In the ARC
system, this includes radiation detection equipment and supporting electronics.
Autonomous: Operating independently without direct human control.
Teleoperated: Controlled remotely by a human operator.
Routine: A pre-programmed sequence of movements and actions that the robot executes
autonomously.
LiDAR: Light Detection and Ranging; a sensor technology that uses laser light to measure
distances and create 3D maps of the environment for navigation and obstacle avoidance.
End Effector: Tools or attachments mounted to a robot which interface with external
environment.
Sensor: Devices that detect and measure physical properties of the environment (radiation,
distance, temperature, etc.).
Operating Control Unit (OCU): The device (tablet or laptop) used by operators to control and
monitor the robot.
User Interface (UI)/Graphical User Interface (GUI): The visual display and controls that
allow operators to interact with the robotic system.

3. The Spot Robot Platform
3.1 History
The Boston Dynamics Spot robot was revealed to the public in 2016, representing years of
development in legged robotics. Spot is a commercially available quadruped robot designed for
industrial inspection, data collection, and remote operations in challenging environments.

3.2 Technical Specifications
Weight:

  Base Robot: 55 lb (25 kg)
      With Battery: 76 lb (34.5 kg)
  With Full ARC Payload: 110 lb (50 kg)
  Note that this may vary depending on detector selection
  Payload weight alone (not including arm): 15 lb (6.8 kg)

Performance:

  Runtime: 90 minutes of typical operation while unburdened, 60 minutes with full payload
  Battery recharge time: 60 minutes
  Note: Runtime decreases with heavier payloads and challenging terrain

Protection Rating:

  IP54: Protected against dust ingress and water splashing from any direction

Movement Capabilities:

  Maximum slope: ± 30 degrees
  Maximum step height: 11.8 inches
      Maximum speed: 1.6 m/s (~ 3.5 mph)

3.3 Understanding Spot's Capabilities
Robustness:
Spot is designed to handle challenging terrain, recover from pushes, and maintain stability in
dynamic environments. The robot can:

 Traverse stairs and uneven surfaces
 Recover from slips and minor collisions
 Self-right if it falls
 Navigate around obstacles autonomously
 Use multiple sensors, including depth sensors and cameras, for environmental awareness

4. Safety and Security Protocols
Safety is paramount when working with the ARC system. Multiple hazard categories must be
understood and respected.

4.1 Electrical Safety
Electrical Hazards:

  Payloads contain high voltage connections (up to 2 kV)
  Spot operates at an average power of 400 W

Safety Requirements:

  DO NOT attempt to open Spot's casing or access internal wiring
  Contact your local Electrical Safety Officer (ESO) for any immediate questions or
 concerns on electrical connections
  Do not operate Spot near environments subjected to high voltages that may jump to the
 robot and payload encasement
  Contact the ARC support team for repairs
  Never attempt unauthorized electrical work

Payload Electrical Safety:

  All electrical payloads are ESO approved or NRTL (Nationally Recognized Testing
 Laboratory) listed
  Always use equipment for its intended purpose only
  Follow all manuals and procedures when changing payloads
  Keep payload connections clean and in good condition
  Inspect connections regularly for damage or contamination

Reporting:

  Report any abnormal behavior or faults to the ARC team immediately upon discovery
 during pre-operation checks
  Document electrical anomalies (sparking, unusual sounds, burning smells, etc.)

4.2 Battery Safety

The battery is a high-capacity lithium-ion power source (58.8 V 564 Wh) that requires careful
handling and storage:

Storage Requirements:

  Remove battery when the system is not in use
  Store batteries in case within designated safe locations
  Use provided storage boxes
  Keep away from heat sources and flammable materials

Inspection Protocol:
When removing the battery, inspect for:

  Cracks in the casing
  Debris or foreign objects in terminals
  Swelling or deformation
  Corrosion or contamination
  Damage to connectors

Charging Safety:

  Supervise the charging process when possible
  Turn off charger when battery reaches full charge
  Never run battery to 0% - this can cause permanent damage
  Use only approved chargers
  Charge in well-ventilated areas away from flammable materials

Shore Power Operations:
When operating on external power:

  Always engage motor lockout before connecting shore power
  Never connect/disconnect while motors are active

4.3 Mechanical Safety
Collision Risks:

  Spot in motion can cause serious injury

  Impact can result from the robot's mass or falling weight
  The robot's movement can be sudden and unpredictable

Safe Distance Protocol:

  Maintain a minimum 2-meter safety perimeter during operations
  Never purposefully put Spot into edge cases or unstable scenarios
  Remember: Spot will prioritize self-stabilization over all other commands
  Never touch Spot while it is operating or while motor lockouts are not engaged

Emergency Stop:

  Always know the location of E-stop buttons
  Always engage manual E-stop (motor lockout) before:
  Carrying Spot
  Performing hands-on work
  Changing batteries
  Connecting/disconnecting payloads

4.4 Ergonomic Safety:
Weight Considerations:

  55 lb unburdened
  72.1 lb with battery
  Additional weight with payload additions

Pinch Point Locations:

  Hip joints
  Knee joints
  Battery casing area
  Payload mounting points

Safe Handling Procedures:

  Only move Spot when
  powered off OR
  engaged motor lockout

  Lifting Spot:
  Always use two people
  Lift using the designated hand straps only
  Never lift by legs, joints, or payload
  Maintain proper lifting posture
  Communicate throughout the lift
  Rolling Spot (for battery changes):
  DO NOT use automated “roll-over” program while Spot has a payload
 1. Ensure motor lockout is engaged
 2. Place one foot carefully along Spot's leg as a leverage point
 3. Use straps to lift the opposite end
 4. Be mindful of payload weight and balance
 5. Roll gently to access battery compartment

4.5 Radiation Protection Safety
Contamination Risks:
ARC’s autonomous features prevent it from walking into areas it has not scanned as clean.
However, as with any operation around potential contamination, risk is inherent and all protocols
should be followed.

If Spot Becomes Contaminated / Enters a Contaminated Zone:
 1. Pause program/walking immediately
 2. DO NOT command Spot to sit - this exposes battery and internal components to
 contamination
 3. Keep Spot in standing position
 4. Immediately contact Radiation Protection Program (RP-PROG) for
 decontamination guidance
 5. Establish contamination control boundaries
 6. Follow all site-specific radiological control procedures

General Radiation Safety:

  Spot reduces personnel exposure by performing surveys remotely
  Operators should remain in lowest radiation areas possible
  Use radiation monitoring equipment as required by RP procedures
  Document radiation survey results per site procedures
  Ensure Spot is uncontaminated before exiting Contaminated Areas

  Do not manually walk Spot into areas with potentially removable contamination.

4.6 Operational Safety Guidelines
General Hazard Awareness:

  NEVER touch Spot while it is in operation
  Collision with Spot could cause severe injury: NEVER ATTEMPT TO CATCH OR
 STABILIZE A FALLING SPOT
  Never stand below Spot:
  In stairwells
  On Truck beds
  Anywhere Spot could potentially fall on personnel
  Motors are EXTREMELY POWERFUL
  Spot's priority hierarchy:
 1. Self-stabilization
 2. Obstacle avoidance
 3. Execute operator commands

Protecting Personnel:

  Do not allow untrained individuals to touch or interact with Spot unsupervised
  Establish clear boundaries and signage when operating
  Be mindful of workers who are unaware of operations
  Brief nearby workers before beginning operations
  Assign a dedicated spotter if operating in high-traffic areas

Manual Operations Safety:

  Never rely solely on Spot’s internal obstacle avoidance - assume it could fail at any time
  Alert all nearby personnel that Spot is moving
  Always maintain 2-meter safety perimeter

Autonomous Operations Safety:

  Alert all personnel to clear the operational area
  Verify area is clear before initiating autonomous routine
  Maintain constant vigilance
  Always be prepared to activate E-Stop if a situation becomes dangerous

  Monitor for unexpected obstacles or personnel entering area
  Have contingency plans for system failures

E-Stop Readiness:

  Know locations of all E-stop buttons (physical and software)
  Practice E-stop activation during training
  Understand difference between pause and emergency stop
  Never hesitate to use E-stop if safety is in question

4.8 Security Guidelines
ARC has an 1897 Wireless Security Plan, and it is approved to operate using radio
communications within Limited Areas

Prohibited Areas:

  Cannot operate inside Vault Type Rooms (VTRs)
  Cannot operate in PF4 areas
  CAN operate in any Limited Area outside restricted zones
  Follow all site-specific security requirements

5. Operating Procedures
5.1 Startup Procedure
 1. Notify personnel within the area
 2. Establish 2-meter safety perimeter
 3. Clear startup area around Spot’s starting point
 a. Including survey requirements before operation
 4. Power on Spot and establish connection with the OCU
 5. Follow applicable procedures for operation

5.2 Power and Motor Status Indicators
Power Button:

  Location: Rear of Spot near battery compartment
  Function: Long press (2 seconds) to power on/off

Motor Lockout Button:

  Location: Adjacent to power button
  Status Indicators:
  Flashing Red = Motors are active and can move
  Uncolored = Motors are locked out (safe to handle)
  Always lock out motors before physical interaction with Spot

6. User Interface (UI) Guide
6.1 UI Overview
The ARC User Interface provides comprehensive control and monitoring capabilities for the
system. Familiarization with all interface elements is essential for safe and effective operation.

6.2 Main Tab

Action Rendering and Map:

  This Section contains a 3D rendering of ARC completing its routine in real time as well
 as a map of its surroundings
  View Controls
  Magnifying Glass +: Zooms into the map
  Magnifying Glass -: Zooms out of the map
  Trifold: Changes between 2D and 3D view
  Reset: Refocuses perspective on the Spot robot
  Free Camera/Follow Robot: Toggles map perspective from fixing on the robot
  Theme Selector: Chooses UI theme (either light mode or dark mode)

Count Graph

  Shows the current radiation level from the probe in DPM
  Updates every second

Button Functions:

   E-stop
   Pause
   Start/Stop Scan
   Start Calibration
  Does a background calibration of the probe
6.4 Manual Control Tab

This tab allows operators to teleoperate Spot using the OCU. As this removes protections for
radiation scanning, the manual control should not be used when possible removable
contamination is present. For this reason, the manual control tab is disabled while the
autonomous scanning is running.

Joystick Functions:

  Left stick: Forward/backward and strafing
  Right stick: Turn body

Camera Selection:

   Select Camera to appear on UI screen (Front/Left/Right/Rear)
  Note: Front picture uses a blend of two angled cameras so obstacle locations may
 differ than how they appear on screen
Button Functions:

  Stand
  Sit
  Undock
  Dock
  To dock, after pressing the dock button, select the appropriate dock number as
 seen on the bottom of the dock’s fiducial tag (looks like a QR code)
  E-Stop

6.5 Documentation Tab

The Documentation tab contains a record of reports generated from previous scans. The tab will
also have guides and manuals detailing how to operate ARC and a list of procedures for use of
ARC within different areas. The contact information for members of the ARC team is found
here; if you have any questions, please contact one of us.

7. Maintenance
General Maintenance Guidelines
Keep ARC—both Spot and payloads—stored inside, away from extreme conditions including
heat, cold, and precipitation. Keep in mind that Spot and ARC are tools, not toys. Follow
troubleshooting documentation to fix certain issues, but if problems persist, reach out to the ARC
team for guidance.
