Measurement of degenerate orbital angular momentum qubits by stimulated emission tomography
Authors: Seonghu Jung, Do-Kyeong Ko
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOrbital angular momentum (OAM) of light has acquired the interest of scientists due to its novel properties. The high-dimensional Hilbert space and entanglement that OAM offers make it a valuable DOF for many quantum optical protocols. However, conventional measurement of OAM state, which exploits coincidence count-based quantum state tomography (QST), suffers from low-brightness and subsequent need for data accumulation times. Such problems are a serious obstacle, especially for high-dimensional OAM qudits. In this work, we suggest stimulated emission tomography (SET) as a solution for bright and efficient measurement of OAM entangled photons. We show that SET can successfully extract information of SPDC photons by measuring the spiral bandwidth and reconstructing the density matrix of SPDC photons. The fidelity and linear entropy of the reconstructed density matrix are 0.91± 0.01 and 0.12± 0.01, respectively, while the time required for each projective measurement is 1 s. Our results show the potential of SET as an efficient alternative to conventional QST.Subject terms: Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

In 1992, Allen et al.1 found that light beams with helical wave fronts can carry not only spin angular momentum but also orbital angular momentum (OAM). Twisting photons carrying OAM with topological charge l are described by the helical phase term exp(ilφ), where φ is the transverse azimuthal angle, and these photons carry OAM of lℏ, while l can have any integer values1. Since the topological charge l is theoretically unbound, OAM offers a high-dimensional Hilbert space. Furthermore, these optical vortices have a phase singularity at the center, which gives perfect zero optical intensity. Due to these properties, OAM has emerged as a versatile degree of freedom in both classical and quantum optics2. In the classical regime, novel optical protocols such as OAM-based high-capacity communication3–5, optical tweezers6, imaging7, and interferometry8 have been demonstrated. On the other hand, in the quantum regime, OAM offers feasible generation of high-dimensional entanglement and high-dimensional qudits9. In addition, combined with other DOFs, such as time and polarization, OAM can be exploited to form a hyperentangled state10. By exploiting these advantages, promising quantum optical protocols, such as complete Bell measurement11, variational quantum eigensolver12, quantum key distribution13, and quantum network14 have been demonstrated.

To generate entangled photon pairs required for such protocols, nonlinear optical phenomena such as spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) and spontaneous four-wave mixing are frequently exploited due to their feasibility and robustness. Currently, coincidence count-based quantum state tomography (QST) is commonly used to measure the OAM state of photon pairs generated from such sources. However, the low brightness and probabilistic nature of SPDC requires accumulation of data, which makes the process time-consuming. As the number of independent projective measurements required for full reconstruction of the density matrix scales quadratically with the dimension, the time required for measurement increases proportionally. When it comes to high-dimensional OAM qudits, this can be a serious obstacle for both fundamental experiments and practical implementations. To address these issues, advanced measurement techniques such as compressed sensing15, adaptive tomography16, and biphoton holography17 have been proposed. Among these approaches, we demonstrate stimulated emission tomography (SET) as an efficient alternative18. SET exploits the relation between SPDC and its coherent process, stimulated parametric down-conversion (StimPDC), to extract information of SPDC photons with amplified signals. Due to this amplification, the photons are measured in a larger ensemble, which suppresses the relative statistical fluctuations and enhances signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)18. Consequently, the integration time for the measurement can be decreased while maintaining an SNR equivalent to that of the low-brightness case. By leveraging these advantages, SET provides an effective means to measure the quantum state of SPDC photons, overcoming the limitations of low-brightness and statistical nature of the source. After its first demonstration in polarization qubits19, SET has proved its capability to characterize entangled qubits and hyperentangled qubits20,21.

Furthermore, spatial mode correlation of StimPDC photons has been studied22,23, and recently it was theoretically proved that one can clone the spatial mode of SPDC photons with StimPDC photons24, which makes SET a promising candidate for spatial mode tomography. Despite the effort to characterize OAM entanglement25, spatial mode SET has been partially performed on non-degenerate photon pairs. However, with non-degenerate entangled photons, the potential of quantum optical protocols cannot be fully exploited. Since non-degenerate photon pairs are distinguishable in the frequency domain, it is challenging to utilize the Hong-Ou-Mandel (HOM) interference26 with non-degenerate photon pairs. As a result, many quantum optical protocols that rely on HOM interference have been demonstrated with degenerate photon pairs27,28. Therefore, the thorough measurement of degenerate qubits by SET remains an urgent requirement.

To address this, we demonstrate SET on degenerate OAM entangled photon pairs generated from a standard Gaussian pump. StimPDC photons are generated and detected in Laguerre-Gaussian (LG) modes which have helical wavefronts. Spiral bandwidth is measured and compared to the theoretical expectation for SPDC photons. Our scheme for OAM mode SET and the resultant density matrix is presented. The fidelity and linear entropy of the reconstructed density matrix show that SET is capable of measuring the OAM entangled photons. This work extends the SET to spatial mode tomography and shows the potential of SET for efficient measurement of OAM state of photons.

Physical principle and method

Average photon number of SPDC and StimPDC process

In SPDC, pump photons interact with the vacuum fluctuation in nonlinear materials and are divided into lower energy daughter photon pairs. In StimPDC, on the other hand, the pump photons interact with a seed laser and produce photon pairs in the mode of the seed laser and its conjugate. In the original work by Liscidini and Sipe18, they showed that the average photon number ratio of StimPDC and SPDC is proportional to the average number of photons in the seed laser by calculating the wavefunctions of SPDC and StimPDC states. We can show the same result by describing the situation only with the operators. Experimental setup and methods

We showed that SPDC and StimPDC differ in the state of the seed beam (vacuum state for SPDC and coherent state for StimPDC). We experimentally realize this relation by generating StimPDC photons just by introducing a seed laser in the signal (idler) mode of SPDC photons. Figure 2 shows the schematic diagram of the experimental setup. For the generation of degenerate SPDC photon pairs, a 2 mm type-I noncollinear phase-matched beta barium borate (BBO) crystal is pumped by a single-frequency CW diode laser (TOPTICA TopMode) at 405 nm. The pump laser is prepared in a Gaussian mode with a waist size of 0.44 mm and horizontally polarized to the optical plane. Projective measurement is performed in the idler (signal) arm by a spatial light modulator (SLM) and 4-f system coupled to single mode fiber (SMF)32,33. A bandpass filter (BPF) at 810 nm is placed before the SMF to filter out unwanted noise photons. Coupled photons are detected by a single photon counting module (SPCM), and the electric signals from SPCM are analyzed by a time-correlated single photon counting (TCSPC) module.

Schematic diagram of the experimental setup: SPF, spatial filter; PBS, polarization beam splitter; HWP, half wave plate; SLM, spatial light modulator; CMOS, CMOS camera; SPCM, single photon counting module; TCSPC, time-correlation single photon counting module. Blue solid line, red solid line and red dashed line refer to pump laser, seed laser and StimPDC photons, respectively. Inside the dashed box are the detectors for SPDC photon pair detection.

StimPDC photons are generated by inducing a seed laser in the signal mode of SPDC photons. A vertically polarized CW diode laser (Thorlabs LP808-SA60) with frequency centered at 808 ± 10 nm is used as the seed laser. Phase-only SLMs (Holoeye Pluto 2.1) are deployed for spatial modulation, and the phase holograms for the SLMs are calculated following the method suggested by Davis et al.34. The OAM joint measurement is conducted based on Klyshko’s advanced wave picture35,36. SLM1 determines the condition for the joint measurements, tailoring the spatial mode of the seed laser (in the signal arm) into the desired LG mode. StimPDC photons in the idler arm are generated in the conjugate LG mode, and encoding a specific hologram on SLM2 flattens the helical phase, coupling the projected photons into the SMF. Since we only detect StimPDC photons in one arm, a CMOS camera is placed on the signal arm to check the spatial mode of the seed laser.

Result and discussion

The SPDC coincidence count rate measured with 10 mW Gaussian pump was 100 counts/s. While the power of the seed laser measured at the crystal plane was 368 μW, the power coupled to the SMF was 121 μW. When introducing the seed laser, the scattered seed laser produced extra noise counts larger than the SPDC photon counts. Therefore, to clearly observe the effect of StimPDC and discriminate the StimPDC photons from this background noise, the pump laser was controlled while the seed laser was kept on. Figure 3 illustrates the real-time observation of signal amplification upon introducing the pump laser (red dashed line), where an amplification of more than two orders of magnitude is observed. The count rate of amplified photons was about 5.3 × 104 counts/s and the amplification efficiency was calculated as 4.4 × 103/mW. Theoretically, amplification of 109 is predicted by inducing 1 mW seed laser to BBO pumped by 150 mW pump laser18. The gap between the theoretical value and the measured value may have originated from imperfect alignment and experimental losses. Despite the discrepancy, we achieved an order of magnitude higher amplification efficiency than that of the previous work25 with smaller seed laser power. This result confirms the strong amplification by the stimulated process, which is a prerequisite for measuring the OAM state by SET.

Finally, to confirm that SET is capable of spatial mode tomography, we reconstructed the density matrix in OAM space. Mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) for the OAM qubit with are exploited to perform overcomplete measurement. While the acquisition time for background and SET photons was set to 1 s per projection, it can be further reduced following the detector’s readout configuration. Maximum likelihood estimation38 was implemented to ensure the reconstructed density matrix satisfies physical criteria, being positive semi-definite. In addition, superpositioned states in MUBs showed less efficiency at projective measurements, which made components with brighter than the others. Although this can be mitigated by precise post-processing, to avoid the risk of data manipulation and contamination, we kept post-processing to a minimum.

We have demonstrated SET for OAM entangled qubits generated from a standard Gaussian pump. While the fidelity that we obtained may be less satisfying for the precise characterization of OAM qubits, our scheme requires less time and obtains a higher signal amplitude, while the accuracy can also be improved. Since the seed laser was aligned simply by tracing the SPDC signal photons to demonstrate feasibility, the spatial overlap inside the nonlinear crystal may not have been strictly optimal. Precise alignment of the seed laser may increase the overall performance of SET. Using a pulsed pump will further improve the accuracy. A pulsed pump gives not only a higher signal but also provides the correct and definitive timing of StimPDC photons. This enables the use of Fourier analysis by a computer or a lock-in amplifier, which will significantly mitigate the errors from noise photons.

Conclusions

We demonstrated SET for OAM entangled degenerate qubits. The amplified signals were more than two orders of magnitude larger and the spiral bandwidth of StimPDC photons matched well with the theoretical spiral bandwidth of SPDC photons. Especially for photons with , the deviation from the theoretical value was 0.07± 0.03 for l=-1 and 0.11± 0.03 for l=1. These results confirm that SET can reliably copy the information from the photons and return amplified signals. The fidelity and the linear entropy calculated from the reconstructed density matrix were 0.91± 0.01 and 0.12± 0.01, respectively, while the time required for each projective measurement was only 1 s. This work not only serves as a proof-of-principle but also represents an extension of SET into a new DOF. Even though we have only shown the measurement of qubits, our scheme can be directly used for the measurement of high-dimensional qudits. Furthermore, recent demonstrations of controlling OAM entanglement via pump beam shaping41,42 can be integrated into our scheme by deploying an additional SLM, enabling the generation and efficient detection of customized OAM states. Such system will offer generation and prompt detection of desired OAM qudits. Our results can be applied to various quantum optical protocols for the efficient measurement of low-brightness entangled OAM qudits.
