Effects of sustainable consumption tendency on lunchbox purchase behavioral intention among university students in Gyeongnam: the mediating role of ESG management perception
Authors: Yoo Sun Kwon, Eun Sil Her
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND/OBJECTIVESThe importance of Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) management in the food service industry is increasing as value-based consumption emphasizing the climate crisis and social values expands. This study empirically investigated the structural relationship between university students’ sustainable consumption tendencies and their lunchbox-purchase behavioral intentions through the mediation of the perceived importance of ESG management.SUBJECTS/METHODSAn online survey was conducted among 285 university students in the Gyeongsangnam-do region. The collected data were analyzed using structural equation modeling and bootstrapping techniques to verify the mediating effects.RESULTSHypothesis testing showed that sustainable consumption tendency had a significant positive effect on the perceived importance of ESG management (β = 0.544, P < 0.001). The perceived importance of ESG management was a key determinant directly influencing purchase behavioral intentions (β = 0.482, P < 0.001). Furthermore, mediation analysis confirmed that a tendency toward sustainable consumption had a significant indirect effect on purchase behavioral intentions through the perceived importance of ESG management (β = 0.308).CONCLUSIONThe perceived importance of ESG management may serve as a mechanism linking university students’ sustainable consumption tendency to their lunchbox-purchase behavioral intentions. These findings suggest that lunchbox companies should communicate concrete ESG practices, such as environmental protection, healthy meal offerings, and transparent nutrition and product information, to support healthier and more sustainable food choices.

INTRODUCTION

Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) management has become an important non-financial strategy for corporate sustainability amid the global climate crisis and growing social responsibility [1]. In the food industry, which is closely related to consumer health and safety, ESG management has become a key factor in building brand image and consumer trust [23]. In addition, safety accidents and ethical controversies in food companies have shown that consumers increasingly consider corporate non-financial values in their purchasing decisions [4].

In South Korea, the home meal replacement market, including convenience store lunchbox products, has grown rapidly because of the rise in single-person households and rising food costs [56]. Although lunchbox purchases were previously driven mainly by price and convenience, consumers now also consider nutritional value and ethical aspects of the production process [78]. Among university students in their 20s, the “Meaning Out” trend, in which individuals express their values through consumption, has become prominent, suggesting that sustainable consumption tendencies may influence purchase behavioral intention [910]. Such lifestyle changes provide useful insight into consumer behavior beyond demographic characteristics [111213].

Therefore, lunchbox products should be considered as convenient foods and daily meal options that may influence the dietary quality of university students. Frequent reliance on lunchbox products may be associated with meal regularity, nutritional balance, quality of food choice, and access to healthier meals. Thus, examining university students’ lunchbox purchase behavior has nutritional significance in understanding how young consumers select convenient meals within their daily food environment.

Previous studies have reported that the positive perceptions of corporate ESG activities affect brand attitudes and purchase intentions [141516]. Consumers with a higher sustainable consumption tendency may also be more sensitive to corporate responsibility and ESG-related practices, leading to stronger corporate trust and purchasing support. Despite this, most studies focused on the general food service industry or franchises. Empirical studies linking the tendency toward sustainable consumption with the perceived importance of corporate ESG management among university students, a core consumer group in the lunchbox market, remain limited [1718].

Therefore, this study aimed to identify the effects of university students’ sustainable consumption tendency on their lunchbox purchase behavioral intention and to clarify the mediating role of the perceived importance of ESG management. The following hypotheses were formulated:

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

Study design and subjects

This study used a cross-sectional design, and the participants were selected through convenience sampling. The study population consisted of university students currently enrolled in universities located in selected cities in Gyeongsangnam-do, including Changwon, Gimhae, Yangsan, and Jinju. Although this study did not aim for national representativeness, the participants were recruited from multiple university cities within Gyeongsangnam-do to mitigate the limitations of convenience sampling.

The sample size was calculated using the G*Power 3.1.9.2 program. Based on a statistical power of 0.95, a significance level of 0.05, and an effect size (f2) of 0.30, the minimum required sample size was estimated to be 281 participants. Data were collected from June 30 to October 11, 2025, through an online survey (Google Forms). Two hundred ninety-four questionnaires were collected, and 285 valid responses were used for the final analysis after excluding those with insincere or incomplete answers. The final sample size exceeded the minimum number required for statistical analysis. The Institutional Review Board of Changshin University reviewed and approved the study protocol (Approval No. CSIRB-Y2025013).

Measurement tools and questionnaire composition

The questionnaire was modified and supplemented based on prior research. Before the main survey, a pretest was conducted with 25 university students using the same online Google Forms format to examine the item clarity, wording appropriateness, ease of response, and completion time. Ambiguous expressions were revised based on the pretest results. The survey was structured into 4 distinct domains: demographic characteristics and lunchbox usage status; sustainable consumption tendency; perceived importance of ESG management; and purchase behavioral intention. The demographic characteristics and lunchbox usage status consisted of 8 items: 4 items on demographic information (sex, academic grade, average monthly allowance, and residence type) and 4 items on lunchbox usage patterns (purchase experience, frequency, price range, and information acquisition channels) [178]. Sustainable consumption tendency was measured using 11 items adapted from the literature [91011]. The perceived importance of ESG management in lunchbox companies was measured using 21 items covering environmental protection, social contribution, and ethical management [12131415]. The items were adapted to the context of lunchbox companies while maintaining the conceptual domains used in previous studies. The purchase behavioral intention was assessed using 5 initial items based on previous research [171819]. All items, except for the demographic characteristics, were measured on a 5-point Likert scale.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.3.2 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). First, exploratory factor analysis (EFA) was conducted using principal axis factoring with a Promax rotation to verify the validity of the measurement tools. Items with a communality of less than 0.40 were excluded to derive the final factors. Second, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was performed using the ‘lavaan’ package in R to re-verify and optimize the fit of the measurement model. The reliability and validity of the measurement tools were assessed using EFA, CFA, Cronbach’s α, construct reliability (CR), and average variance extracted (AVE). Third, Pearson correlation analysis was used to examine the linear relationships and directions among the major variables. Fourth, structural equation modeling (SEM) was applied to test the research hypotheses and mediating effects. The statistical significance of the indirect effects was verified by bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples and a 95% confidence interval (CI). P-values < 0.05 were considered significant.

Demographic characteristics and lunchbox usage status

The majority of participants were female (61.4%) compared to males (38.6%), as shown in Table 1. Regarding the academic grade, first-year and fourth-year students accounted for equal proportions at 31.2% each. The most common residence type was living at home (44.2%). Among the various types, convenience store lunchboxes (63.2%) were most preferred by students. The most frequent purchase interval was less than once a week (81.0%). The most common price range for purchased lunchboxes was 4,000–6,000 won (67.7%), followed by 6,000–8,000 won (17.5%). The primary source of information on lunch boxes was social network services (SNS, 73.0%).

Sustainable consumption tendency

EFA was performed to verify the validity of the scale measuring sustainable consumption tendency among university students. The results identified 3 distinct factors: moderate consumption tendency, ethical consumption tendency, and eco-friendly consumption tendency. The Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) value was 0.726, exceeding the recommended threshold of 0.60, and Bartlett’s test was statistically significant (P < 0.001). These results confirmed that the data were suitable for factor analysis, with the identified factors accounting for 66.875% of the total variance. The CFA results further indicated an acceptable model fit, with a comparative fit index (CFI) of 0.932 and a standardized root mean square residual (SRMR) of 0.049 (Table 2). Convergent validity was also established because the CR exceeded 0.7 and the AVE was above 0.5 for all constructs. Reliability analysis revealed Cronbach’s α coefficients from 0.734 to 0.756 for each sub-factor, indicating high internal consistency among the measurement items.

Perceived importance of ESG management in lunchbox companies

EFA showed that the university students’ perceptions of the importance of ESG management in lunchbox companies were structured into 3 factors: environmental protection (E), social contribution (S), and ethical management (G). This 3-factor structure was consistent with the conceptual domains used in previous studies, supporting the content validity of the ESG-related measurement items in the lunchbox company context. The KMO value was significantly high at 0.953, and the cumulative variance explained was 72.775%. CFA further supported the model fit (CFI = 0.905, SRMR = 0.058), and the CR values were excellent, ranging from 0.814 to 0.912 (Table 3). The overall Cronbach’s α was 0.913, indicating the high internal consistency of the ESG-related measurement items.

Hypothesis testing

Testing of direct effects

The research model revealed an acceptable overall fit to the data: χ2 (82) = 277.359, P < 0.001, CFI = 0.944, TLI = 0.892, RMSEA = 0.071, and SRMR = 0.049 (Fig. 1). The path from sustainable consumption tendency to the perceived importance of ESG management was significant (β = 0.544, P < 0.001), suggesting that students with higher sustainable consumption tendencies were more likely to perceive ESG management in lunchbox companies as important. The perceived importance of ESG management also had a significant effect on their purchase behavioral intention (β = 0.482, P < 0.001), and the direct effect of sustainable consumption tendency on their purchase behavioral intention was significant (β = 0.247, P < 0.001). Supplementary SEM analyses showed that, among the sub-factors of sustainable consumption tendency, eco-friendly consumption tendency had the strongest direct effect on their purchase behavioral intention (β = 0.380, P < 0.001), followed by moderate consumption tendency (β = 0.177, P < 0.01) and ethical consumption tendency (β = 0.127, P < 0.05). Among the sub-factors of the perceived importance of ESG management, environmental protection had the strongest direct effect (β = 0.277, P < 0.01), followed by social contribution (β = 0.264, P < 0.001) and ethical management (β = 0.172, P < 0.05).

Testing of indirect effects

The mediating effect was tested at the overall construct level because the primary purpose of this study was to verify the structural pathway from a sustainable consumption tendency to the purchase behavioral intention through the perceived importance of ESG management. The statistical significance of the mediating effect was verified using bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples and a 95% CI (Table 6). The indirect effects of sustainable consumption tendency on the purchase behavioral intention through the perceived importance of ESG management were statistically significant (β = 0.308, 95% CI, 0.209–0.426). In addition, the total indirect effect was significant (β = 0.330, 95% CI, 0.238–0.434). Hence, the perceived importance of ESG management partially mediated the relationship between sustainable consumption tendency and the purchase behavioral intention.

DISCUSSION

This study focused on ESG management, which has emerged as a core driver for the sustainable growth of the food industry, and examined the structural mechanism through which sustainable consumption tendency of university students influences their lunchbox purchase behavioral intentions.

The significant direct effect of sustainable consumption tendency on purchase behavioral intentions suggests that value-based consumption, such as the “Meaning Out” phenomenon, may play an important role in shaping the lunchbox purchase behavior [12]. Although lunchbox consumption has traditionally been driven by utilitarian values such as low price and time efficiency [34], young consumers may now consider broader attributes, including ethical responsibility and sustainability in the production and distribution process [567]. These findings suggest that the sustainable values of consumers may provide a psychological basis for evaluating the importance of corporate ESG management.

The supplementary sub-factor analysis showed that an eco-friendly consumption tendency had the strongest direct effect on the purchase behavioral intention among the dimensions of sustainable consumption tendency. In addition, environmental protection showed the strongest direct effect among the dimensions of the perceived importance of ESG management. Hence, university students’ lunchbox purchase intentions may be influenced more strongly by visible and practical environmental values, such as eco-friendly products, resource conservation, waste reduction, and sustainable ingredients, than by broader ethical or social values.

The partial mediating effects of the perceived importance of ESG management suggest that the sustainable consumption tendency influences the purchase behavioral intention both directly and indirectly through the perceived importance of ESG management. Therefore, corporate ESG practices need to be clearly recognized by consumers for value-oriented attitudes to translate into purchase behavioral intentions [61519]. Therefore, lunchbox companies should communicate their ESG practices in ways that consumers can easily experience in daily purchasing situations [1320].

From a nutritional perspective, lunchbox products should be considered convenient commercial goods and daily meal options that may affect the dietary quality of university students. ESG management in lunchbox companies may help develop a healthier and more sustainable food environment through sustainable ingredients, healthy meal provision, transparent nutrition and product information, and waste reduction. Therefore, nutrition education for university students should incorporate sustainable food choice literacy, helping them to consider nutritional quality and environmental responsibility when selecting convenience foods.

These findings provide practical implications for the lunchbox and convenience store industries. University students pursue both nutritional balance and convenience [212223]. Therefore, companies need to move beyond cost-effectiveness-oriented marketing and integrate ESG values with healthier meal provision, transparent nutrition information, and sustainable product communication. In particular, communicating specific ESG practices through SNS may strengthen brand identification and encourage repeat purchases [1520].

Despite these insights, this study was limited to university students in Gyeongsangnam-do. Thus, caution is needed when generalizing the findings to all Korean university students. Future studies should verify the generalizability of the proposed model using nationwide stratified sampling. In addition, the possibility of social desirability bias cannot be excluded because this study used self-reported survey data. This study did not collect information on the participants’ academic majors or prior exposure to ESG-related coursework, which may have influenced the perceived importance of ESG management. Future research should include these variables as controls.

In conclusion, university students’ sustainable consumption tendency is linked to the lunchbox purchase behavioral intention through the perceived importance of ESG management. These findings suggest that ESG strategies in lunchbox companies should align with the consumers’ value systems while contributing to healthier meal choices, transparent nutrition information, and a more sustainable food environment.
