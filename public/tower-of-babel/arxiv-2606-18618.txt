Abstract
We develop the matched asymptotic framework for the reflection of a planar shock from a rigid wall in the joint limit of weak shock strength and nearly glancing incidence. The outer region is governed by the Rankine–Hugoniot shock polars and the inner region near the triple point by the unsteady transonic small-disturbance ( utsd ) equation. With the incident-shock Mach number M = 1 + λ ​ α 2 M=1+\lambda\alpha^{2} and α \alpha the small glancing/wedge angle, the inner utsd shock-reflection problem ( Hunter & Brio 2000 ; Hunter & Tesdall 2004 ) is controlled by the single dimensionless parameter a = α 2 ​ ( M 2 − 1 ) = 1 2 ​ λ + 𝒪 ⁡ ( α 2 ) , a=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}), so that the limiting inner parameter a 0 = 1 / ( 2 ​ λ ) a_{0}=1/(2\sqrt{\lambda}) is independent of γ \gamma . Hence every leading-order inner quantity depends on ( γ , λ ) (\gamma,\lambda) only through λ \lambda (equivalently a 0 a_{0} ). The conversion from the canonical UTSD trajectory to a physical small angle is controlled by the Mach-number strength scale δ = 2 ​ ( M 2 − 1 ) \delta=\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)} , not by an independent γ \gamma -dependent inner prefactor. The transition between regular reflection and Mach reflection is the detachment value a d = 2 a_{d}=\sqrt{2} , so the Guderley–Mach (von Neumann paradox) regime is a 0 < 2 a_{0}<\sqrt{2} , i.e. λ > 1 8 \lambda>\tfrac{1}{8} . The genuine leading-order inner field is the self-similar utsd solution, of mixed type, whose structure behind the reflected wave is the Guderley sequence of supersonic patches rather than a single fan; the canonical trajectory function g ⁡ ( a 0 ) g(a_{0}) is determined numerically and admits no elementary closed form. The corresponding physical angle satisfies χ phys = 2 ​ ( M 2 − 1 ) ​ g ​ ( a ) + 𝒪 ⁡ ( M 2 − 1 ) \chi_{\rm phys}=\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}\,g(a)+\mathcal{O}(M^{2}-1) , or χ phys = 2 ​ λ ​ α ​ g ​ ( a 0 ) + 𝒪 ⁡ ( α 3 ) \chi_{\rm phys}=2\sqrt{\lambda}\,\alpha\,g(a_{0})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}) in the distinguished scaling. We give a local linearisation and formal adjoint consistency check, and we record the first correction as an implicit solvability coefficient H ⁡ ( a , γ ) H(a;\gamma) rather than as a computed curve. This makes explicit what would be required for a genuine first-order correction about the full Guderley solution. We also derive the self-similar reduction, the sonic parabola, the local adjoint identity, the nonlocal numerical reconstruction, and the utsd self-similar shock polar, whose detachment threshold reproduces a d = 2 a_{d}=\sqrt{2} as an independent check. 1.  Introduction

The reflection of a planar shock wave from a rigid plane wall is a
foundational problem of compressible-flow theory

(
von Neumann 1943
;
Courant & Friedrichs 1948
;
Ben-Dor 2007
)
. For steep incidence the flow
admits a two-shock
regular reflection
(RR); below a critical angle it
transitions to
Mach reflection
(MR), with a Mach stem bridging the
incident and reflected shocks at a triple point
\mathcal{T}
above the wall

(
Henderson 1987
;
Ben-Dor 2007
)
.

For weak shocks the transition occurs in a thin-wedge/weak-shock regime in
which the classical RR/MR criteria fail—the
von Neumann paradox

(
von Neumann 1943
;
Colella & Henderson 1990
)
. Experiments
(
Skews & Ashworth 2005
)
and
high-resolution computations
(
Tesdall & Hunter 2002
;
Vasil’ev & Kraiko 1999
)
show
that the paradox is resolved, following Guderley’s conjecture

(
Guderley 1947
)
, by replacing the sharp triple point with a self-similar
sequence of supersonic patches—a
Guderley–Mach reflection
. The local
flow is governed not by the Rankine–Hugoniot system but by the unsteady
transonic small-disturbance (
utsd
) equation

(
Hunter & Brio 2000
;
Hunter & Tesdall 2004
;
Brio & Hunter 2001
)
, which in canonical form
contains no
\gamma
.

This paper sets the matched asymptotic connection between the outer
shock-polar description and the inner
utsd
solution on a correct footing,
and determines what form the leading-order and first-order results can take.
Our findings are:

1.

The incident-shock strength is
M^{2}-1=2\lambda\alpha^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})
,
and the inner
utsd
problem is controlled, in the limit, by the single,

\gamma
-independent parameter
a_{0}=1/(2\sqrt{\lambda})

(
Sections

2
and
3
).

2.

Every inner quantity therefore depends on
(\gamma,\lambda)
only through

\lambda
; in the Mach-number strength convention the physical angle is
obtained by multiplying the canonical UTSD trajectory by

\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}
(
Sections

3
and
2.4
).

3.

The genuine leading-order inner field is the mixed-type self-similar

utsd
solution with Guderley patch structure; the canonical trajectory
function
g(a_{0})
is determined numerically and has no elementary closed
form (
Sections

3
and
5
).

4.

A local first-order linearisation gives a self-consistent formal adjoint
and defines the first correction implicitly as a solvability coefficient

H(a;\gamma)
for the full linearised Guderley free-boundary problem.
This shows why any numerical value of the correction requires moving
shocks, sonic boundaries, patch interfaces, and domain variations
(
Section

4
).

5.

A pilot finite-volume/nonlocal time-marching solver is included and
benchmarked against Hunter–Tesdall at
a_{0}=0.5
: with the transverse
compression
u>1
admitted, the computed
u=0.5
shock contour matches
the published triple-point abscissa to
0.1\%
(
Section

6
).

6.

The
utsd
self-similar shock polar is obtained in closed form; its
detachment threshold reproduces the literature value
a_{d}=\sqrt{2}
,
bounding the Guderley regime as
\lambda>\tfrac{1}{8}
(
Section

5
).

2.  Outer Expansion and the Control Parameter

2.1.  Geometry, scaling, and convention

We work in the self-similar frame attached to the reflection point, in which
all quantities depend on
(X,Y)=(x/t,y/t)
. Let
\alpha
denote the small
glancing/wedge angle, so that the glancing limit is
\alpha\to 0
, and let
M

be the incident-shock Mach number. The weak-shock, nearly-glancing regime is
the transitional limit
(
Hunter & Tesdall 2004
)

M\to 1,\quad\alpha\to 0,\qquad\lambda\equiv\frac{M-1}{\alpha^{2}}\ \text{fixed},

(1)

so that the shock-strength parameter has the explicit expansion

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle:=M^{2}-1=(1+\lambda\alpha^{2})^{2}-1

\displaystyle=2\lambda\alpha^{2}+\lambda^{2}\alpha^{4}=2\lambda\alpha^{2}\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha^{2}\right).

(2)

Thus
\lambda
measures shock strength relative to the square of the glancing
angle. This single convention is used throughout. In particular, all formulae
below are to be read in the distinguished limit
\alpha\to 0
with
\lambda
fixed;
finite-
\alpha
equalities inherit the displayed
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})
corrections.

2.2.  Incident-shock strength and pressure jump

The incident shock is a shock of strength

\beta=M^{2}-1=2\lambda\alpha^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})
, a purely kinematic quantity independent of

\gamma
. The usual weak normal-shock pressure relation gives

\frac{p_{1}-p_{0}}{p_{0}}=\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\bigl(M^{2}-1\bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2}),

(3)

so that, in the nondimensional pressure variable used by the small-disturbance
scaling,

\frac{p_{1}-p_{0}}{\gamma p_{0}}=\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\bigl(M^{2}-1\bigr)=\frac{4\lambda}{\gamma+1}\,\alpha^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4}).

(4)

By contrast, the canonical UTSD strength is obtained by removing the glancing
scale,

\frac{2(M^{2}-1)}{\alpha^{2}}=4\lambda+2\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2},

(5)

which is independent of
\gamma
. This elementary distinction is the source
of the apparent disappearance of
\gamma
from the inner problem:
\gamma
is
present in the physical pressure jump (
4
), but not in the
canonical UTSD parameterisation (
5
).

2.3.  The single,
\gamma
-free control parameter

The transitional-limit matched asymptotics
(
Hunter & Brio 2000
;
Hunter & Tesdall 2004
)

reduce the reflection to the
utsd
shock-reflection problem
(§
3
), controlled by the single parameter

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}}=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{4\lambda\alpha^{2}+2\lambda^{2}\alpha^{4}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha^{2}\right)^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{4}\alpha^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})\right).

(6)

Thus

\boxed{\ a_{0}:=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}a=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\ }.

(7)

The limiting value
a_{0}
is held fixed in (
1
). The geometry (numerator
\alpha
) and
the strength (denominator
\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}
) enter separately; combining
them, the limiting parameter
a_{0}
is independent of both
\gamma
and
\alpha
.
The map between
\lambda
and the Hunter–Tesdall control parameter is shown
in
Figure

1
, and the collapse of weak-shock pairs onto fixed
a
is
shown in
Figure

2
.

Proposition 2.1

(Single
\gamma
-free inner parameter)
.

The inner self-similar
utsd
reflection problem depends on
(\gamma,\lambda)

only through
\lambda
, equivalently through
a_{0}=1/(2\sqrt{\lambda})
; it does not
depend on
\gamma
. If
g(a)
denotes the canonical UTSD trajectory function,
then the physical small angle is obtained by multiplying by the Mach-number
strength scale
\delta=\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}
. Thus
\gamma
does not enter as
an independent inner parameter or as a separate prefactor in this convention.

2.4.  Physical angular normalisation

The canonical UTSD trajectory function is most cleanly interpreted as a scaled
angular quantity. Define

\delta=\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}.

(8)

The Hunter–Tesdall parameter is
a=\alpha/\delta
, so the physical angular
variable is obtained from the canonical one by multiplication by
\delta
.
Consequently, if
g(a)
denotes the canonical UTSD trajectory function, then

\chi_{\rm phys}=\delta\,g(a)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}).

(9)

Under the distinguished scaling
M=1+\lambda\alpha^{2}
,

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}=\sqrt{4\lambda\alpha^{2}+2\lambda^{2}\alpha^{4}}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\lambda}\,\alpha\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{4}\alpha^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})\right),

(10)

and therefore

\boxed{\chi_{\rm phys}=2\sqrt{\lambda}\,\alpha\,g(a_{0})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),\qquad a_{0}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}.\ }

(11)

Equivalently,

\frac{\chi_{\rm phys}}{\alpha}=\frac{g(a_{0})}{a_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}).

(12)

No additional
\gamma
-dependent prefactor appears in this Mach-number-based
scaling. A
\gamma
-dependent factor arises only if the shock strength is
parameterised by the physical pressure jump rather than by
M^{2}-1
; using
(
4
),

\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}=\sqrt{(\gamma+1)\,{p_{1}-p_{0}\over\gamma p_{0}}},

(13)

so an apparent factor
\sqrt{\gamma+1}
is a change of normalisation, not a
dependence of the canonical UTSD inner problem.

Remark 2.2
.

A tempting normal-Mach-number parametrisation would use the apparent strength

M_{n}^{2}-1=(2\lambda-1)\alpha^{2}

\lambda>\tfrac{1}{2}

\cos^{2}\alpha
into the
strength, double-counting the geometry that the control parameter (

)
keeps separate; it is therefore inconsistent with the parametrisation of

3.  Inner Expansion: The
utsd
Reflection Problem

3.1.  The canonical equation and the reflection problem

The transitional limit yields the canonical
utsd
equation, which contains
no
\gamma
,

u_{t}+\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}u^{2}\Bigr)_{x}+v_{y}=0,\qquad u_{y}=v_{x},

(14)

together with the self-similar shock-reflection initial–boundary value problem

(
Hunter & Tesdall 2004
)

u(x,y,0)=\begin{cases}1,&x<ay,\\
0,&x>ay,\end{cases}\qquad v(x,0,t)=0,\qquad v\to 0\ \text{as}\ x\to+\infty,

(15)

with
a
the parameter (
7
).

3.2.  The self-similar reduction and the sonic line

Under
u=u(\xi,\eta)
,
v=v(\xi,\eta)
with
\xi=x/t
,
\eta=y/t
, the chain rule gives

u_{t}=-\frac{\xi}{t}u_{\xi}-\frac{\eta}{t}u_{\eta},\qquad\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right)_{x}=\frac{1}{t}uu_{\xi},\qquad v_{y}=\frac{1}{t}v_{\eta}.

(16)

Multiplying the first UTSD equation by
t
gives

(u-\xi)\,u_{\xi}-\eta\,u_{\eta}+v_{\eta}=0.

(17)

Similarly,
u_{y}=t^{-1}u_{\eta}
and
v_{x}=t^{-1}v_{\xi}
, so the compatibility
condition becomes

(u-\xi)\,u_{\xi}-\eta\,u_{\eta}+v_{\eta}=0,\qquad u_{\eta}=v_{\xi}.

(18)

The type-change curve follows by introducing a local potential

\Phi(\xi,\eta)
with
u=\Phi_{\xi}
and
v=\Phi_{\eta}
, possible away from
shocks because
u_{\eta}=v_{\xi}
. The principal part of the resulting second-order
self-similar equation is

(u-\xi)\Phi_{\xi\xi}-\eta\Phi_{\xi\eta}+\Phi_{\eta\eta}.

(19)

For a second-order equation
A\Phi_{\xi\xi}+2B\Phi_{\xi\eta}+C\Phi_{\eta\eta}
,
the discriminant is
B^{2}-AC
. Here

B^{2}-AC=\frac{\eta^{2}}{4}-(u-\xi),

(20)

so the sonic parabola is

u=\xi+\frac{1}{4}\eta^{2},

(21)

with (
18
) hyperbolic for
u-\xi<\eta^{2}/4
and elliptic otherwise.
A local model
(u-1)u_{\eta}+v_{\xi}=0
,
u_{\xi}=v_{\eta}
admits a self-similar solution

u=U(s)
,
v=\eta^{1-b}G(s)
,
s=\xi/\eta^{b}
only for
b=1
, with the
centred fan

u^{(0)}(s)=1-\frac{1}{s^{2}},\qquad v^{(0)}(s)=\frac{2}{3s^{3}}+\text{const},\qquad s=\frac{\xi}{\eta},

(22)

a crude local descriptor only.

3.3.  The leading-order field is the Guderley patch structure

Following
Guderley 1947
and the numerical solutions of

Hunter & Brio 2000
;
Tesdall & Hunter 2002
—confirmed by

Tesdall et al. 2006
and
Vasil’ev & Kraiko 1999
and observed
experimentally by
Skews & Ashworth 2005
—the self-similar
utsd
solution
contains a self-similar
sequence of supersonic patches
just behind the
leading triple point, each closed by its own small triple point and fan. Two
consequences are central: the canonical trajectory function
g(a_{0})
is a
numerically determined function of the single inner parameter and has no closed
form; and there is no single smooth leading-order fan for a closed-form first
correction to perturb.

4.  Formal Local First-Order Check: Operator and Adjoint

The previous section rules out a closed-form perturbation of a single smooth
fan: the leading-order inner solution is the numerical Guderley patch cascade.
The calculation below is therefore used only as a local consistency check on
the linearisation and adjoint signs. It should not be read as a Fredholm theory
for the full mixed-type shock-reflection free-boundary problem.

On a smooth patch of a provisional background
u^{(0)},v^{(0)}
, writing

u=u^{(0)}+\alpha\,w+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})
,
v=v^{(0)}+\alpha\,z+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})
gives

(u-1)u_{\eta}\ \longrightarrow\ (u^{(0)}-1)\,\partial_{\eta}w+\bigl(\partial_{\eta}u^{(0)}\bigr)\,w,

(23)

so that the local correction operator has the form

\mathcal{L}(w,z)=\Bigl((u^{(0)}-1)w_{\eta}+u^{(0)}_{\eta}w+z_{\xi},\ \ w_{\xi}-z_{\eta}\Bigr).

(24)

For the actual Guderley solution this operator must be supplemented by the
linearised Rankine–Hugoniot conditions on every shock, the perturbation of
sonic arcs and patch interfaces, and the variation of the self-similar domain.
Those data are numerical and are not replaced here by the local fan
(
22
).

4.1.  Formal adjoint and local null family

The cancellation in the adjoint is useful enough to record explicitly. For
smooth compactly supported perturbations, or equivalently modulo boundary and
shock-interface terms, consider

\displaystyle\langle(p,q),\mathcal{L}(w,z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega}p\bigl[(u^{(0)}-1)w_{\eta}+u^{(0)}_{\eta}w+z_{\xi}\bigr]+q(w_{\xi}-z_{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta.

(25)

Integrating by parts gives the interior contribution

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}w\bigl[-(u^{(0)}-1)p_{\eta}-q_{\xi}\bigr]+z\bigl[-p_{\xi}+q_{\eta}\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta,

(26)

because the term
pu^{(0)}_{\eta}w
cancels the derivative of

(u^{(0)}-1)
arising from
\partial_{\eta}[p(u^{(0)}-1)]
. The discarded
boundary term is

\int_{\partial\Omega}\left[p(u^{(0)}-1)wn_{\eta}+pzn_{\xi}+qwn_{\xi}-qzn_{\eta}\right]ds,

(27)

with additional jump contributions on internal shock or patch interfaces. The
formal differential adjoint is therefore

\mathcal{L}^{*}(p,q)=\Bigl(-(u^{(0)}-1)p_{\eta}-q_{\xi},\ \ -p_{\xi}+q_{\eta}\Bigr).

(28)

On the local fan background
u^{(0)}-1=-1/s^{2}
,
s=\xi/\eta
, the homogeneous
adjoint admits the one-parameter null family

\hat{u}=p=\eta^{\sigma}\,s^{(\sigma+1)/2},\qquad\hat{v}=q=\eta^{\sigma}\,s^{(\sigma-1)/2},\qquad q=p/s,

(29)

with residual identically zero. The exponent is denoted by
\sigma
to avoid
confusion with the Hunter–Tesdall control parameter
a
in (
7
); it
would be fixed by matching and boundary conditions in any complete local
problem.

4.2.  Implicit definition of the first correction

The leading trajectory is determined by the canonical function
g(a)
. The
next correction is a different object. With

\delta=\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)},\qquad a=\frac{\alpha}{\delta},

(30)

the physical trajectory angle has the formal expansion

\chi_{\rm phys}(a,\delta)=\delta\,g(a)+\delta^{2}H(a;\gamma)+o(\delta^{2}),

(31)

where
H
denotes the first correction in the Mach-number strength convention.
We write
H(a;\gamma)
, rather than
h(a)
, because the leading
utsd
problem
is
\gamma
-free but the next Euler/UTSD matching terms may contain outer
thermodynamic normalisations. A
\gamma
-dependence at this order would
therefore be a next-order matching effect, not a leading-order inner dependence.

For a smooth fixed patch, the formal adjoint calculation gives the local
orthogonality condition

\mathcal{S}_{\rm loc}=\iint_{\Omega_{\rm loc}}(F\hat{u}+G\hat{v})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\text{boundary/interface contributions}=0.

(32)

For the full Guderley free-boundary problem this bookkeeping identity should be
read schematically as a solvability condition for the correction amplitude. If

U_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1})
is the first correction and
\mathcal{L}_{a}
is the full
linearisation about the numerical Guderley solution at parameter
a
, then

\mathcal{L}_{a}U_{1}=F_{a}+H(a;\gamma)R_{a}.

(33)

Here
F_{a}
denotes the known forcing from the next-order asymptotic expansion,
while
R_{a}
is the forcing produced by an infinitesimal change in the trajectory
angle. Let
\Psi_{a}
be the corresponding adjoint null vector and let

\mathcal{B}_{a}
denote all boundary and interface contributions from shocks,
sonic arcs, the wall, and Guderley patch interfaces. The solvability condition
is

\left\langle\Psi_{a},F_{a}+H(a;\gamma)R_{a}\right\rangle+\mathcal{B}_{a}\bigl(F_{a}+H(a;\gamma)R_{a}\bigr)=0,

(34)

and hence the first correction is defined implicitly by

\boxed{\ H(a;\gamma)=-\frac{\left\langle\Psi_{a},F_{a}\right\rangle+\mathcal{B}_{a}(F_{a})}{\left\langle\Psi_{a},R_{a}\right\rangle+\mathcal{B}_{a}(R_{a})}.\ }

(35)

Equation (
35
) is the mathematically meaningful way to report the
first correction at this stage. It specifies exactly what must be computed,
without replacing the full linearised free-boundary problem by the local fan
model. The canonical time-marching solver in
Section

6
cannot extract

H
, because it solves only the leading-order
utsd
problem and contains no
independent finite-
\delta
parameter. An empirical estimate would require
full finite-
\delta
Euler simulations, or a next-order
utsd
solver, through

H(a;\gamma)\approx\frac{1}{\delta}\left[\frac{\chi_{\rm phys}(a,\delta)}{\delta}-g(a)\right],\qquad\delta\to 0.

(36)

No numerical value or curve for
H
is claimed here.

5.  Explicit Results

5.1.  The
utsd
self-similar shock polar

For a straight shock with unit normal

n=(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)
and normal speed
s
, the Rankine–Hugoniot condition
for the conservation law
u_{t}+(u^{2}/2)_{x}+v_{y}=0
is

-s[u]+\left[\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right]\cos\phi+[v]\sin\phi=0.

(37)

The compatibility equation
u_{y}-v_{x}=0
gives the distributional jump condition

[u]\sin\phi-[v]\cos\phi=0,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad[v]=[u]\tan\phi.

(38)

Substituting (
38
) into (
37
) and using

[u^{2}]/2=\tfrac{1}{2}(u_{a}+u_{b})[u]
gives

s=\tfrac{1}{2}(u_{a}+u_{b})\cos\phi+\frac{\sin^{2}\phi}{\cos\phi}.

(39)

In self-similar coordinates the normal speed of a straight line through

P=(\xi_{P},\eta_{P})
is
s=\xi_{P}\cos\phi+\eta_{P}\sin\phi
. Thus the UTSD
self-similar shock polar is

[v]=[u]\tan\phi,\qquad\xi_{P}\cos\phi+\eta_{P}\sin\phi=\tfrac{1}{2}(u_{a}+u_{b})\cos\phi+\frac{\sin^{2}\phi}{\cos\phi}.

(40)

5.2.  Regular reflection, detachment, and the window

Normalise the state ahead of the incident shock to
(u_{0},v_{0})=(0,0)
and
the state behind it to
u_{1}=1
. If
t_{i}=\tan\phi_{i}
, the first jump condition
in (
40
) gives
v_{1}=t_{i}
. For the reflected shock, joining state

1
to a wall state
2
with
v_{2}=0
, write
t_{r}=\tan\phi_{r}
. Then

v_{2}-v_{1}=(u_{2}-u_{1})t_{r}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad-t_{i}=(u_{2}-1)t_{r},\qquad u_{2}=1-\frac{t_{i}}{t_{r}}.

(41)

Since regular reflection occurs at the wall, the two shocks meet at the same
point
P=(\xi_{P},0)
. Dividing the second relation in (
40
) by

\cos\phi
gives, for a shock through the wall point,

\xi_{P}=\tfrac{1}{2}(u_{a}+u_{b})+\tan^{2}\phi.

(42)

For the incident shock this yields
\xi_{P}=\tfrac{1}{2}+t_{i}^{2}
. For the reflected
shock it yields
\xi_{P}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+u_{2})+t_{r}^{2}

(44)

It factors exactly as

2t_{r}^{3}+(1-2t_{i}^{2})\,t_{r}-t_{i}=(t_{r}-t_{i})\bigl(2t_{r}^{2}+2t_{i}t_{r}+1\bigr),\qquad t_{i}=\tan\phi_{i}.

(45)

6.  Pilot Numerical Route to the Trajectory Function

The analysis above leaves one genuinely numerical object: the canonical
trajectory function
g(a_{0})
entering the physical angle through
(
49
). A complete computation of
g
requires the full
self-similar mixed-type
Guderley solution. As a reproducibility route, rather than as a converged
replacement for the Hunter–Tesdall calculation, we implemented a minimal
finite-volume time-marching discretisation of the canonical
utsd
problem.
The system is advanced in the form

u_{t}+\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right)_{x}+v_{y}=0,\qquad v_{x}=u_{y},

(50)

with the oblique Riemann data (
15
). Thus the calculation is a
solver for the full leading-order canonical
utsd
system in its
nonlocal time-dependent form: the transverse field
v
is not prescribed or
neglected, but is recovered from the compatibility constraint
v_{x}=u_{y}
at
each stage. It is therefore more than a one-dimensional Burgers calculation.
At the same time, it is not the full Hunter–Tesdall numerical construction: it
does not solve the steady self-similar mixed-type free-boundary problem with
shock fitting, sonic arcs, Guderley patch interfaces, and local adaptive
refinement. It should be read as a benchmarked time-marching route to the
leading-order canonical solution, not as a production-quality self-similar
Guderley solver. At each stage
u_{y}
is computed by centred differences and
the constraint
v_{x}=u_{y}
is inverted by integrating from the right boundary,
using the far-field normalisation
v\to 0
as
x\to+\infty
:

v(x,y,t)=-\int_{x}^{x_{\max}}u_{y}(s,y,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad v(x_{\max},y,t)=0.

(51)

Equivalently, the pilot calculation advances the scalar nonlocal equation

u_{t}+\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right)_{x}+\partial_{y}\left[-\int_{x}^{x_{\max}}u_{y}(s,y,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\right]=0.

(52)

The scalar Burgers flux in
x
is treated by a Rusanov flux, while the nonlocal
transverse contribution is differenced conservatively in
y
. The wall is
imposed through
v(x,0,t)=0
and even reflection of
u
. Crucially,
u
is
not limited to
[0,1]
: the transverse coupling
v_{y}
violates the scalar
maximum-principle intuition, so the flow behind the Mach stem is compressed to

u>1
. Clipping at unity suppresses that compression and with it the Mach
reflection itself.

7.  Discussion

7.1.  The single-parameter,
\gamma
-free structure

The reduction to the single limiting
\gamma
-independent parameter
a_{0}=1/(2\sqrt{\lambda})

(
Proposition

2.1
) is the central organising principle of the inner problem
and is consistent with the parametrisation of
Hunter & Tesdall 2004
. The
two-parameter
(\gamma,\lambda)
space of the outer problem collapses, inside the

utsd
region, to a single
\gamma
-free axis. In the Mach-number strength
normalisation used here, the physical angle is recovered by the factor

\sqrt{2(M^{2}-1)}
in (
9
); a
\gamma
-dependent factor appears
only if the strength is re-expressed using the pressure jump, as in
(
13
). Any expression for a canonical inner quantity
as an independent rational function of
\gamma
and
\lambda
is therefore
inconsistent with the structure of the equations.
Figure

8

illustrates this separation at the level of the weak-shock scaling.

Figure 8:
Diagnostic showing why
\gamma
disappears from the canonical UTSD inner problem.
The physical weak-shock pressure jump scaled by
\alpha^{2}
depends on
\gamma
, whereas
the canonical UTSD strength
2(M^{2}-1)/\alpha^{2}
is
\gamma
-free.

7.2.  The paradox regime and Guderley reflection

The detachment value (
47
) marks the RR/MR transition. For
a<\sqrt{2}

(
\lambda>\tfrac{1}{8}
) the flow is a Guderley–Mach reflection with the
supersonic-patch cascade, and the leading-order triple-point data and any
corrections are properties of the numerical self-similar solution, consistent
with the numerical and experimental record

(
Skews & Ashworth 2005
;
Tesdall & Hunter 2002
;
Vasil’ev & Kraiko 1999
)
. In the narrow band

a_{d}<a<a_{s}
the state behind the reflected shock is subsonic and the precise
transition mechanism is delicate
(
Hunter & Tesdall 2004
)
.

7.3.  Extensions

Real-gas thermodynamics affects the outer relations and the conversion between
pressure jump and Mach-number strength, but the inner problem is unchanged in
canonical form and still depends only on
a
. Wall curvature enters the inner
problem as a forcing term in (
24
). It may shift the numerically
determined
g(a)
and any first-order correction, but it does not produce a
closed-form modification.

8.  Conclusions

We have placed the matched asymptotic treatment of weak shock reflection at
nearly glancing incidence on a consistent footing. A single convention is
fixed; the incident-shock strength is
M^{2}-1=2\lambda\alpha^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})
,
and the inner
utsd
reflection problem is controlled, in the limit, by the
single,
\gamma
-independent parameter
a_{0}=1/(2\sqrt{\lambda})
, in agreement
with
Hunter & Tesdall 2004
. Every canonical inner quantity depends on

(\gamma,\lambda)
only through
\lambda
, and the leading physical trajectory angle is

\chi_{\rm phys}=2\sqrt{\lambda}\,\alpha\,g(a_{0})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),

with
g
a function of the inner parameter alone. No separate
\gamma
-dependent
prefactor appears in this Mach-number-based scaling; such a factor is only a
change of normalisation if pressure jump is used as the strength variable. The
RR/MR transition is the detachment value

a_{d}=\sqrt{2}
, which our independent shock-polar analysis reproduces, bounding
the Guderley–Mach regime as
\lambda>\tfrac{1}{8}
. The genuine leading-order inner
field is the mixed-type self-similar
utsd
solution with Guderley
supersonic-patch structure, whose trajectory angle is determined numerically
and has no elementary closed form. The pilot time-marching calculation in

Section

6
gives a practical route toward computing this function in the
canonical variables, and we benchmark it at
a_{0}=0.5
against the
Hunter–Tesdall triple point
(1.008,0.514)
. The solver advances the full
leading-order canonical
utsd
system, including the nonlocal reconstruction
of
v
from
v_{x}=u_{y}
; it is not merely a Burgers-only approximation. Once the
transverse compression
u>1
behind the Mach stem is admitted, the computed

u=0.5
contour passes through
(\xi,\eta)=(1.007,0.514)
, giving a
0.1\%

match in the contour abscissa over the tested grids. Extracting a single
trajectory angle is more ambiguous on a coarse uniform grid because the curved
Mach stem merges smoothly into the incident shock. We therefore report the
pilot only as a single benchmarked anchor and do not present an automatically
swept
g_{N}(a_{0})
curve; a trustworthy point-by-point
g(a_{0})
requires adaptive
refinement and sub-cell shock fitting. The time-marching solver is also a
leading-order solver only, so it does not determine the first correction
coefficient
H(a;\gamma)
. Instead
Section

4.2
defines
H
implicitly
through the adjoint solvability condition for the full linearised Guderley
free-boundary problem. Evaluating it would require the numerical Guderley
solution together with its linearised shock/interface conditions, or full
finite-
\delta
Euler simulations. Consequently no numerical first-correction
curve, closed-form correction coefficient, critical parameter, or trajectory
table is claimed here.
