A RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN CIRCUMFERENCE AND WEIGHT IN TREES AND ITS BEARING ON BRANCHING ANGLES

By Cecil D. Murray

Published in: The Journal of General Physiology

DOI: 10.1085/jgp.10.5.725

ABSTRACT

Observation reveals a linear relationship between the logarithm of the circumference of a tree, branch, or leaf stem, and the logarithm of the weight of the tree, branch, or leaf. The bearing of this on the angles of branching in trees is discussed.

Accordingly measurements were made, 116 in all, on nine kinds of
trees; namely, aspen, bitternut, hickory, oak, ash, maple, cedar,
hornbeam, and beech. The largest tree measured had a circumference
of 56.4 cm. where cut, and the whole tree weighed 120 kg. The
smallest measurements were made on the stems of leaves,--for
example, circumference of stem = 0.25 cm., weight of leaf = 0.18
gm. All the data thus obtained are included in Fig. 1.
Our procedure was of the simplest character. Whole trees of
varying size, or branches, or leaves, were taken entirely at random
from the vicinity. The only criterion of selection was that the
specimen should not appear to have been recently injured. The
circumference was measured by a tape, encircling the bark, at the
point of section; or, for small specimens, the diameter was measured
by calipers and the circumference subsequently calculated. The
specimen was then weighed on one of three balances according to size.
The season was midsummer, 1926; the place, Grindstone Island, N. Y.
