Exact extremal constructions for the inducibility of blowup graphs
Wanfang Chen and Xizhi Liu

Abstract
For a finite graph H H and a positive integer h h , the h h -blowup H ( h ) H^{(h)} of H H is the graph obtained by replacing each vertex of H H by a set of size h h and each edge by a complete bipartite graph between the corresponding sets. We prove that, for every H H , there exists a constant h ∗ ​ ( H ) h_{*}(H) such that whenever h ≥ h ∗ ​ ( H ) h\geq h_{*}(H) and n n is sufficiently large, every n n -vertex graph maximizing the number of induced copies of H ( h ) H^{(h)} is a blowup of H H . This refines the asymptotic result of Hatami, Hirst and Norine and settles the question posed by Bollobás, Egawa, Harris and Jin in 1995.

Introduction

A fundamental problem in extremal graph theory is to determine the maximum number of induced copies of a given graph
F
among all
n
-vertex graphs. Formally, given two graphs
F
and
G
, let
I(F,G)
denote the number of induced copies of
F
in
G
, that is, the number of subsets
S\subseteq V(G)
of size
v(F)
such that the induced subgraph
G[S]
is isomorphic to
F
. Here
v(F)
denotes the number of vertices of
F
. For every positive integer
n
, put

I(F,n)\coloneqq\max\{I(F,G):v(G)=n\}.

The
inducibility
of
F
is then defined as

i(F)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}{I(F,n)}/{\tbinom{n}{v(F)}}.

The limit exists by the standard averaging argument.

A systematic study of the inducibility problem was initiated by Pippenger–Golumbic
[
16
]
, who established several general properties of inducibility and determined the inducibility of complete bipartite graphs with part sizes differing by at most one. Determining
I(F,n)
, or even
i(F)
, is difficult in general. For example, the inducibility of the path on four vertices remains open; see, for instance, Exoo
[
6
]
and Even-Zohar–Linial
[
5
]
. For small graphs, bounds on the inducibility of graphs on four, five, and six vertices have been obtained in several works, including Exoo
[
6
]
, Hirst
[
11
]
, Even-Zohar–Linial
[
5
]
, Pikhurko–Sliačan–Tyros
[
15
]
, and Bodnár et al.
[
3
]
, some of which employ the computer-assisted flag algebra machinery of Razborov
[
17
]
.

A particularly interesting result of Balogh–Hu–Lidický–Pfender
[
2
]
is the determination of the inducibility of the
5
-cycle
C_{5}
, confirming a special case of an old conjecture of Pippenger–Golumbic
[
16
]
on the inducibility of cycles. This conjecture remains open for longer cycles, and improved upper bounds were obtained by Hefetz–Tyomkyn
[
10
]
and by Král’–Norin–Volec
[
12
]
. For general graphs, results of Yuster
[
19
]
and Fox–Huang–Lee
[
7
]
imply that for almost all graphs
F
, it holds that
i(F)=\frac{v(F)!}{v(F)^{v(F)}-v(F)}
, where the lower-bound construction arises from nested blowups of
F
itself. Extending the results of
[
14
,
13
,
8
]
on the edge-statistic conjecture of Alon–Hefetz–Krivelevich–Tyomkyn
[
1
]
, Ueltzen
[
18
]
recently characterized graphs whose inducibility is bounded away from zero in the large-order regime.

In this work, we focus on the blowup of graphs. If
H
is a graph and
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{V(H)}
, the blowup
H(\mathbf{k})
is obtained by replacing each vertex
x\in V(H)
by an independent set of size
\mathbf{k}(x)
, and by joining two replacement classes completely exactly when the corresponding vertices of
H
are adjacent. If all coordinates of
\mathbf{k}
are equal to
h
, we write
H^{(h)}
and call it the
balanced
h
-blowup
of
H
.

Blowups are natural candidates for extremal constructions in many inducibility problems.
Bollobás, Egawa, Harris and Jin
[
4
]
proved the complete-graph case: if
t>1+\log r
, then, for all sufficiently large
n
, the maximum number of induced copies of
K_{r}^{(t)}
in an
n
-vertex graph is attained by the Turán graph
T_{r}(n)
, and this lower bound on
t
is necessary, since for smaller
t
the extremal construction is no longer a blowup of
K_{r}
. They then asked which base graphs admit this exact blowup conclusion:

Question 1.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

For which graphs
H
does there exist a constant
h
such that, for all sufficiently large
n
, every
n
-vertex graph with the maximum number of induced copies of
H^{(h)}
is a blowup of
H
?

Hatami, Hirst and Norine
[
9
]
proved the corresponding asymptotic theorem for every graph
H
. We briefly recall the language in which their result is stated. A weighted graph
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is a finite graph
X
together with a probability measure
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
on
V(X)
; ordinary finite graphs are viewed with the uniform measure. A map
\phi:V(F)\to V(X)
is a strong homomorphism if it preserves both edges and non-edges. Thus
s(F;X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
is the probability that a random map from
V(F)
to
V(X)
, chosen independently according to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
, realizes the same adjacency pattern as
F
. The use of arbitrary maps rather than injective maps is natural in the asymptotic setting, since collisions have negligible probability in large finite graphs. Finally,
H^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
denotes the graph
H
with vertex weights
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
, and
M(V(H))
is the set of full-support probability measures on
V(H)
; these weights encode the limiting part sizes of blowups of
H
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
H
be a graph. There exists a positive integer
h_{0}
such that for every
h\geq h_{0}
, there exists a distribution
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\in M(V(H))
with

\sup_{X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}s\bigl(H^{(h)};X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\bigr)=s\bigl(H^{(h)};H^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\bigr),

where the supremum is over all finite weighted graphs
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
.

Equivalently, the inducibility of
H^{(h)}
is asymptotically achieved by blowups of
H
. In
[
9
, Question 7.1]
, Hatami, Hirst and Norine returned to the exact finite problem above, asking for the corresponding exact blowup conclusion for induced copies, and indicated how their local lemmas and variational argument should be adapted in order to treat induced embeddings.

The purpose of this note is to supply these finite details. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
H
be a non-empty finite graph. There exists an integer
h_{*}=h_{*}(H)
such that for every
h\geq h_{*}
there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(H,h)
with the following property. If
n\geq n_{0}
and
G
is an
n
-vertex graph maximizing the number of induced copies of
H^{(h)}
, then
G
is a blowup of
H
.

Thus the question of Bollobás, Egawa, Harris and Jin has an affirmative answer for every graph
H
. The theorem is stated for induced copies; in the proof we count labelled induced embeddings, which differ by the fixed factor
|\Aut(H^{(h)})|
and hence have the same extremal graphs.

The proof essentially follows the exact-stability framework of
[
15
, Theorem 5.8]
, with the weighted argument of Hatami, Hirst and Norine supplying the asymptotic input. The finite exact conclusion requires several additional steps. The first is a finite substitute for derivative optimality. With
F\coloneqq H^{(h)}
, if
G
maximizes the number
N_{F}(G)
of labelled induced embeddings, then replacing one vertex of
G
by a clone of another vertex cannot increase
N_{F}(G)
. This gives, in
section

2
, a finite local optimality condition with an error of order
O_{F}(1/n)
.

The second step turns the weighted input of Hatami, Hirst and Norine into a finite stability statement. Their direct results are weighted statements: a continuity estimate, their asymptotic maximum theorem, their global stability lemma, and a rooted stability lemma, recorded below in
sections

2
,
2
and
2
. Writing
R\coloneqq\widetilde{H}
for the twin-free quotient of
H
, so that
H=R(\mathbf{k})
, we combine
section

2
with quotient and rooted adaptations of their weighted machinery to prove
section

3.1
: for every small fixed
\eta>0
, provided first
h
and then
n
are large enough, every exact extremal graph
G
is close to a blowup
B
of
R
on the same vertex set, with all parts of linear size and with
\Delta(G\mathbin{\triangle}B)\leq\eta n
.

The third step is the exact cleaning argument. Put
J\coloneqq G\mathbin{\triangle}B
. Each wrong pair in
J
destroys many type-respecting embeddings present in
B
, while
section

3.2
implies that any embedding created by the error graph must contain a large star in
J
. Since
\Delta(J)\leq\eta n
,
section

3.2
shows that the lost embeddings dominate the created embeddings once
\eta
is chosen sufficiently small and
h
sufficiently large. Hence a non-empty error graph would contradict extremality, so
G=B
. The refinement from the twin-free quotient back to
H
is then immediate from the linear lower bound on the quotient parts.

section

2
fixes notation, records the weighted results of Hatami, Hirst and Norine, proves the quotient form of the rooted result, and proves the finite local-optimality lemma.
section

proves
theorem

1.3

Preliminaries

For a graph
G
, write
d_{G}(x)
for the degree of
x
in
G
and
\Delta(G)
for the maximum degree of
G
.
We identify a graph with its edge set; thus
|G|
denotes the number of edges of
G
.
We write
A_{G}(x,y)
for the
(x,y)
-entry of the adjacency matrix of
G
, with zero diagonal. Thus
A_{G}(x,y)=1
if
xy\in G
and
A_{G}(x,y)=0
otherwise.

For graphs
F
and
G
, let
\Emb(F,G)
be the set of injective maps
\phi:V(F)\to V(G)
such that, for all
x,y\in V(F)
,
xy\in F
if and only if
\phi(x)\phi(y)\in G
.
Put
N_{F}(G)\coloneqq|\Emb(F,G)|
. If
m\coloneqq|V(F)|
and
n\coloneqq|V(G)|\geq m
, set

p_{F}(G)\coloneqq{N_{F}(G)}/{(n)_{m}},

where
(n)_{m}\coloneqq n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)
. Thus
p_{F}(G)
is the probability that a uniform random injective map
V(F)\hookrightarrow V(G)
is an induced embedding.
All embedding counts are labelled. Thus

N_{F}(G)=|\Aut(F)|\,I(F,G).

A map
\phi:V(F)\to V(G)
is a
strong homomorphism
if it preserves both edges and non-edges:

A_{F}(x,y)=A_{G}(\phi(x),\phi(y))\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in V(F).

Thus an induced embedding is precisely an injective strong homomorphism. The only difference is that a strong homomorphism may identify distinct vertices, whereas an induced embedding may not.

Two vertices in
H
are called
twins
if they have the same neighbourhood (and thus are not adjacent). This is an equivalence relation on
V(H)
, and its equivalence classes are independent sets. The
twin-free quotient

\widetilde{H}
is the graph whose vertices are the twin classes of
H
, with two classes adjacent when, equivalently, every pair of vertices between them is adjacent.
Writing
R\coloneqq\widetilde{H}
, for each
x\in V(R)
, let
\mathbf{k}(x)
be the size of the corresponding twin class in
H
. We write

\|\mathbf{k}\|_{1}\coloneqq\sum_{x\in V(R)}\mathbf{k}(x),\qquad\|\mathbf{k}\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\max_{x\in V(R)}\mathbf{k}(x).

Then
H
is the blowup
R(\mathbf{k})
, and hence
H^{(h)}=R(h\mathbf{k})
.
The natural projection
\pi:R(h\mathbf{k})\to R
, which sends each blown-up vertex to the quotient vertex whose part contains it, is called the
type map
.

We shall also use the following elementary fact several times.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
R
is twin-free, then every strong homomorphism
R\to R
is an automorphism.

Proof.

If
\phi(x)=\phi(y)
, then for every
z\in V(R)
,
A_{R}(x,z)=A_{R}(\phi(x),\phi(z))=A_{R}(\phi(y),\phi(z))=A_{R}(y,z)
. Thus
x
and
y
are twins, and so
x=y
. Hence
\phi
is injective and therefore bijective. Since
\phi
preserves adjacency and non-adjacency, it is an automorphism.
∎

We recall the weighted notions of Hatami, Hirst and Norine
[
9
, Section 2.1]
that are used below.
For a finite set
S
, put

\mathcal{P}(S)\coloneqq\Big\{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}:S\to[0,1]\;:\;\sum_{x\in S}{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(x)=1\Big\},\quad\text{and}\quad M(S)\coloneqq\{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\in\mathcal{P}(S):{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(x)>0\text{ for every }x\in S\}.

Thus,
M(S)
denotes the full-support part of the simplex
\mathcal{P}(S)
; the closed simplex
\mathcal{P}(S)
will only be needed when taking closest representatives.

Recall that a
weighted graph

X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is a finite graph
X
together with a probability measure
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\in\mathcal{P}(V(X))
. If no measure is displayed, we use the uniform measure and write it as
u_{X}
.
Throughout, when discussing equivalence and edit distance, weighted graphs are identified with their reduced representatives obtained by deleting all zero-weight vertices.
For a weighted graph
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
, let
s(F;X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
be the probability that a random map
V(F)\to V(X)
, chosen independently according to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
, is a strong homomorphism. We also use a
rooted density
. For
x\in V(X)
, let
T_{x}(F;X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
be the probability that the following experiment gives a strong homomorphism: choose a uniform root
y\in V(F)
, force
y
to be mapped to
x
, and map all other vertices independently according to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
. Then

s(F;X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})=\sum_{x\in V(X)}{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(x)T_{x}(F;X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}).

(1)

Two weighted graphs
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
and
Y^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
are
equivalent
, written
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\sim Y^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
, if there is an isomorphism
\theta:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{Y}
between their twin-free quotients such that, for every quotient vertex
x\in V(\widetilde{X})
, the total
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
-weight of the twin class of
x
equals the total
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
-weight of the twin class of
\theta(x)
. They are
commensurable
if they have the same vertex set and the same probability measure.
A
weighted blowup
of
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
means any weighted graph equivalent to
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
.

The
weighted edit distance
between two weighted graphs
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
and
Y^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
is

\mathrm{edit}(X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}},Y^{\boldsymbol{\nu}})\coloneqq\min\sum_{x,y\in V(W_{1})}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(x){\boldsymbol{\omega}}(y)\left|A_{W_{1}}(x,y)-A_{W_{2}}(x,y)\right|,

where the minimum is over all commensurable weighted graphs
W_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
and
W_{2}^{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
such that
W_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\sim X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
and
W_{2}^{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\sim Y^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
.
The sum is over ordered pairs; this normalization is immaterial for us. Zero-weight vertices may still be introduced temporarily during refinements, but they are then ignored when passing back to the reduced representative.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
9
, Equation (2.1)]
)
.

For every finite graph
F
and weighted graphs
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}},Y^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
,

|s(F;X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})-s(F;Y^{\boldsymbol{\nu}})|\leq|V(F)|^{2}\mathrm{edit}(X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}},Y^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}).

The next proposition records two weighted consequences of the work of Hatami, Hirst and Norine that will be used below: their maximum theorem and their global stability lemma. It is not yet an exact finite statement; the finite adaptation is carried out in
section

3.1
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
9
, Theorem 3.2 and Lemma 5.1]
)
.

Let
H=R(\mathbf{k})
, where
R
is twin-free, and put
F_{h}\coloneqq H^{(h)}=R(h\mathbf{k})
.

(a)

There is
h_{0}
such that, for every
h\geq h_{0}
,

\sup_{X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}s(F_{h};X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})=\max_{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\in M(V(R))}s(F_{h};R^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}),

where the supremum is over all finite weighted graphs
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
.

(b)

For every
\delta>0
there is
h_{1}=h_{1}(\delta)
such that, whenever
h\geq h_{1}
and
s(F_{h};X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})\geq\frac{1}{2}s(F_{h};H)
, we have
\mathrm{edit}(X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}},H)\leq\delta
.

Here
item

2.3(b)
is Hatami, Hirst and Norine’s global stability lemma in its relative-threshold form; the comparison value
\frac{1}{2}s(F_{h};H)
is the threshold appearing in that stability statement.

We shall need one more result of Hatami, Hirst and Norine, namely their rooted stability lemma
[
9
, Lemma 5.3]
. Since their Lemma 5.3 is stated for weighted blowups of the original graph
H
and uses a single parameter to control regularity, the multiplicative loss, and the minimum part weight, we record explicitly the quotient form needed here. The proof is just the parameter conversion from their statement to the two-parameter form below.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
9
, Lemma 5.3]
)
.

Let
H=R(\mathbf{k})
, where
R
is twin-free, and put
F_{h}\coloneqq H^{(h)}=R(h\mathbf{k})
. For every
\rho,\lambda>0
there are
h_{2}=h_{2}(\rho,\lambda)
and
\delta>0
such that the following holds for
h\geq h_{2}
. Suppose
X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
and
B^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
are commensurable weighted graphs,
B^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is equivalent to a weighted blowup
R^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
,
\min_{y\in V(R)}{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(y)\geq\lambda
, and
\mathrm{edit}(X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}},B^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})\leq\delta
. If a positive-weight vertex
x\in V(X)
has no row of
B
within
\rho
in
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
-measure, that is, if

{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\{z:A_{X}(x,z)\neq A_{B}(y,z)\}>\rho\qquad\text{for every }y\in V(B),

then

T_{x}(F_{h};X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})\leq\rho\,T_{x}(F_{h};B^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}).

Proof.

Choose
\varepsilon_{0}
such that
0<\varepsilon_{0}\leq\min\{\rho,\lambda/\|\mathbf{k}\|_{\infty}\}
.
Split the quotient weight
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(y)
among the
\mathbf{k}(y)
vertices of the twin class of
H
lying over
y
; for instance, put

\widehat{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(y)}{\mathbf{k}(y)}\qquad\text{whenever }x\in V(H)\text{ lies over }y\in V(R).

Then every coordinate of
\widehat{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}
is at least
\lambda/\|\mathbf{k}\|_{\infty}
, and hence at least
\varepsilon_{0}
. Thus the minimum vertex weight condition in Hatami, Hirst and Norine’s lemma is satisfied. Since vertices lying over the same quotient vertex are twins, the weighted graph
B^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is also equivalent to the weighted blowup
H^{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}}
.

Before applying their lemma, delete all zero-weight vertices from the common vertex set of the commensurable pair. Since
x
has positive weight, it remains after this deletion. The deletion does not change the edit distance, the equivalence class of
B^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
, the rooted densities at
x
, or any of the row distances from
x
to the remaining rows of
B
.

Hatami, Hirst and Norine’s Lemma 5.3 says, in their notation, that if a vertex is not
\varepsilon_{0}
-regular with respect to a commensurable pair consisting of a weighted graph and a nearby weighted blowup of
H^{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}}
, then its rooted density is smaller by a factor at most
\varepsilon_{0}
. In this reduced commensurable representative, their row distance
d_{1}(x,y)
is exactly

d_{1}(x,y)=\sum_{z\in V(B)}{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(z)\left|A_{X}(x,z)-A_{B}(y,z)\right|={\boldsymbol{\mu}}\{z:A_{X}(x,z)\neq A_{B}(y,z)\}.

Thus their
\varepsilon_{0}
-regularity condition is precisely the existence of some row of
B
within
\varepsilon_{0}
in the above sense. Apply that lemma to the graph
H
with parameter
\varepsilon_{0}
, obtaining
h_{2}
and
\delta
. If
x
has no row of
B
within
\rho
in
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
-measure, then, because
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\rho
, it has no row of
B
within
\varepsilon_{0}
either. In their terminology,
x
is therefore not
\varepsilon_{0}
-regular with respect to the reduced commensurable pair
(X,B,{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
.

In Hatami, Hirst and Norine’s partially labelled notation,
\partial H^{(h)}
denotes the sum of the one-vertex partial labellings of
H^{(h)}
. Thus their rooted density is the sum of the contributions obtained by fixing each possible root at
x
, whereas our
T_{x}
averages over the root. Equivalently, for
W=X,B
,

s(\partial H^{(h)},1\mapsto x;W^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})=|V(F_{h})|\,T_{x}(F_{h};W^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}).

Their lemma gives

s(\partial H^{(h)},1\mapsto x;X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})\leq\varepsilon_{0}\,s(\partial H^{(h)},1\mapsto x;B^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}).

Using the displayed identity for
W=X
and
W=B
, and using
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\rho
, we obtain

T_{x}(F_{h};X^{\boldsymbol{\mu}})\leq\rho\,T_{x}(F_{h};B^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}),

as required.
∎

The following elementary lemma replaces the derivative optimality condition in the weighted argument. It follows by comparing an extremal graph with the graph obtained after cloning one vertex by another.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
F
be a fixed graph, with
m\coloneqq|V(F)|
. There is a constant
C_{F}
such that for every
n\geq 2m
, if
G
is an
n
-vertex graph maximizing
N_{F}(G)
, then

T_{x}(F;G^{u_{G}})\geq p_{F}(G)-\frac{C_{F}}{n}\qquad\text{for all }x\in V(G),

where
u_{G}
is the uniform probability measure on
V(G)
. One may take
C_{F}=4m^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\Emb(F,G)
. For
x\in V(G)
, let
Q_{x}
be the number of pairs
(y,\phi)\in V(F)\times\mathcal{E}
with
\phi(y)=x
, and put

q_{x}\coloneqq\frac{Q_{x}}{m(n-1)_{m-1}}.

Since the maps in
\mathcal{E}
are injective,
Q_{x}
is equivalently the number of embeddings whose image contains
x
, counted with the unique preimage of
x
.
Then
n^{-1}\sum_{x}q_{x}=p_{F}(G)
. Fix
x\in V(G)
. If
q_{x}\geq p_{F}(G)
, there is nothing to prove at this stage. Otherwise choose
x^{\prime}\neq x
with
q_{x^{\prime}}\geq p_{F}(G)
. Form
G^{\prime}
from
G
by replacing
x
with a non-adjacent clone of
x^{\prime}
: delete all edges incident with
x
, join
x
to
z\notin\{x,x^{\prime}\}
exactly when
x^{\prime}
is joined to
z
in
G
, and leave
xx^{\prime}
as a non-edge. Embeddings not using
x
are unchanged, and embeddings using
x
but not
x^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
correspond to embeddings using
x^{\prime}
but not
x
in
G
. The only maps not accounted for are those using both specified vertices, at most
m(m-1)(n-2)_{m-2}
. Since
G
is extremal,

Q_{x}\geq Q_{x^{\prime}}-m(m-1)(n-2)_{m-2},

and hence
q_{x}\geq p_{F}(G)-2m/n
for
n\geq 2m
. Thus, in all cases,

q_{x}\geq p_{F}(G)-\frac{2m}{n}.

Finally, the rooted map defining
T_{x}(F;G^{u_{G}})
is non-injective only if some non-root vertex maps to
x
or two non-root vertices collide. This has probability at most
m^{2}/n
. Therefore

|T_{x}(F;G^{u_{G}})-q_{x}|\leq\frac{m^{2}}{n},

which proves the lemma, after enlarging the constant to
4m^{2}
.
∎

We shall also need to know that a closest weighted quotient blowup has no vanishing part. This is what later lets us round weighted parts to linear-sized finite parts.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

K\coloneqq|J|
.
We compare the embeddings lost when passing from
B
to
G
with the embeddings created by this change. Define

L\coloneqq|\Emb(F,B)\setminus\Emb(F,G)|,\qquad P\coloneqq|\Emb(F,G)\setminus\Emb(F,B)|.

It is enough to prove
L>P
, since then
|\Emb(F,B)|>|\Emb(F,G)|
, equivalently
N_{F}(B)>N_{F}(G)
.

Claim 3.7
.

For all sufficiently large
n
, we have
L\geq\frac{K}{\binom{m}{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{m-2}n^{m-2}
.

Proof.

Fix a wrong pair
xy\in J
. Choose an arbitrary ordering of this unordered pair, and write
x\in B_{u}
and
y\in B_{v}
. If
u\neq v
, choose
a,b\in V(F)
with types
u,v
, respectively; if
u=v
, choose two distinct vertices
a,b
of type
u
. Then

A_{F}(a,b)=A_{B}(x,y)\neq A_{G}(x,y).

Every type-respecting embedding of
F
into
B
with
a\mapsto x
and
b\mapsto y
is therefore destroyed in
G
. If

r_{t}\coloneqq|\{a,b\}\cap\pi^{-1}(t)|\qquad\text{for all }t\in V(R),

then
\sum_{t}r_{t}=2
. After the prescribed images
x,y
have been used, the remaining
h\mathbf{k}(t)-r_{t}
vertices of type
t
in
F
must be injected into the unused vertices of
B_{t}
. Therefore the number of type-respecting embeddings with
a\mapsto x
and
b\mapsto y
is

\prod_{t\in V(R)}(|B_{t}|-r_{t})_{h\mathbf{k}(t)-r_{t}},

which is at least

\prod_{t\in V(R)}\left(\frac{\lambda n}{2}\right)^{h\mathbf{k}(t)-r_{t}}=\left(\frac{\lambda n}{2}\right)^{m-2},

for fixed
h
and all sufficiently large
n
. Summing this bound over all
K
wrong pairs and then dividing by
\binom{m}{2}
gives the claim, because the image of any embedding contains at most
\binom{m}{2}
unordered vertex pairs whose adjacency differs between
B
and
G
.
∎

Claim 3.8
.

For all sufficiently large
n
, we have
P\leq 2\cdot 3^{m}\eta^{\ell-1}Kn^{m-2}
.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in\Emb(F,G)\setminus\Emb(F,B)
. Let
\pi_{B}:V(B)\to V(R)
be the type map of
B
, and set
\theta\coloneqq\pi_{B}\circ\psi
. Since
\psi
is not an embedding into
B
, the map
\theta
is not a strong homomorphism. By
section

3.2
, the image of
\psi
contains a star with at least
\ell
leaves in the error graph
J
.

Therefore
P
is at most the number of ways to specify
\ell
leaves of such a forced star and then place the remaining vertices. Indeed, the center preimage can be chosen in at most
m
ways, the
\ell
leaf preimages in at most
\binom{m-1}{\ell}\leq 2^{m}
ways, the center image as some
x\in V
, the leaf images in at most
d_{J}(x)^{\ell}
ways, and the remaining vertices in at most
n^{m-\ell-1}
ways. Hence

P\leq m2^{m}\sum_{x\in V}d_{J}(x)^{\ell}n^{m-\ell-1}\leq 3^{m}\sum_{x\in V}d_{J}(x)^{\ell}n^{m-\ell-1}.

Using
d_{J}(x)\leq\eta n
and
\sum_{x}d_{J}(x)=2K
, we get
P\leq 2\cdot 3^{m}\eta^{\ell-1}Kn^{m-2}
, as claimed.
∎

Recall that by the choice of
h_{\ref{lem:cleaning}}
, we have
2\cdot 3^{m}\eta^{\ell-1}<\frac{1}{\binom{m}{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{m-2}
.
Combining
sections

3.2
and
3.2
, for all sufficiently large
n
we have

P\leq 2\cdot 3^{m}\eta^{\ell-1}Kn^{m-2}<\frac{K}{\binom{m}{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{m-2}n^{m-2}\leq L.

Thus
N_{F}(B)>N_{F}(G)
.
∎

3.3
Proof of the main theorem

Write
H=R(\mathbf{k})
, where
R\coloneqq\widetilde{H}
is twin-free. If
|V(R)|=1
, then
H=\overline{K}_{q}
for some
q\geq 1
. Choose
h_{*}=2
when
q=1
, and
h_{*}=1
otherwise. Then
qh\geq 2
for every
h\geq h_{*}
. For such
h
and all
n\geq qh
, the unique graph maximizing the number of induced copies of
\overline{K}_{qh}
is the edgeless graph: if a graph has an edge, every
qh
-set containing that edge is not independent, while the edgeless graph makes all
qh
-sets independent. Since the edgeless graph is a blowup of
H
, the theorem holds in this case. Hence assume
|V(R)|\geq 2
.

Let
\lambda_{0}
be given by
section

3.1
. Apply
section

3.2
with
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
, obtaining
\eta>0
and
h_{\ref{lem:cleaning}}
. Apply
section

3.1
with this
\eta
, obtaining
h_{\ref{prop:finite-stability}}
. Set

h_{*}\coloneqq\max\{h_{\ref{lem:cleaning}},h_{\ref{prop:finite-stability}}\}.

Fix
h\geq h_{*}
, put
F\coloneqq H^{(h)}
, and let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph maximizing
N_{F}(G)
, where
n
is sufficiently large. By
section

3.1
, there is a blowup
B
of
R
on
V(G)
such that every part has size at least
\lambda_{0}n
and
\Delta(G\mathbin{\triangle}B)\leq\eta n
. If
G\neq B
, then
section

3.2
gives
N_{F}(B)>N_{F}(G)
, contradicting the extremality of
G
. Therefore
G=B
, so
G
is a blowup of
R
.

Finally, each part of
B
has linear size. For all sufficiently large
n
, the part corresponding to
v\in V(R)
can be split into
\mathbf{k}(v)
non-empty subparts. All vertices inside one quotient part of
B
have identical adjacencies outside the part and no adjacencies inside the part, so this refinement makes
G
a blowup of
H=R(\mathbf{k})
. The theorem follows.
