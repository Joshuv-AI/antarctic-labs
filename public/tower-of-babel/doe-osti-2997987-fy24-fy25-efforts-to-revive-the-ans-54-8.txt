1 Introduction and Background

The U.S. has designed, constructed, and operated several sodium fast reactors (SFRs). To support
these efforts and to document best practices, activities associated with the development of standards
and consensus standards were carried out, however, the vast majority of those SFR standards are
currently in a withdrawn or inactive status. Consensus standards are useful during design and
licensing of reactors, as is evident in the current nuclear power generation fleet. Consensus
standards provide certainty and predictability between stakeholders during licensing and other
regulatory interactions. These standards offer guidance, reflect expert positions, and establish
effective practices for a wide range of topics. Consensus standards are developed by committees
with representation from a broad cross-section of industry, organizations, professional societies,
and government agencies. However, most existing consensus standards applicable to nuclear power
plants were developed specifically for light-water reactor (LWR) designs and may not adequately
address specific advanced non-LWR design topics such as those related to SFRs.

In fiscal year 2023 (FY23), Argonne National Laboratory (Argonne), with support from the U.S.
Department of Energy (DOE), Advanced Reactor Demonstration Program (ARDP) Regulatory
Development, Regulatory Framework Modernization Area, reviewed and made recommendations
[1] pertaining to a series of American Nuclear Society (ANS) standards related to liquid metal fast
breeder reactors (LMFBRs)1. This series of ANS standards is known as the ANS-54 series.

The original development efforts of the ANS-54 series of consensus standards occurred in the 1970s
through the 1980s. These activities included forming working groups with representatives from the
DOE, U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC), national laboratories, and industry to draft a
series of consensus standards to support design, licensing, construction, and operation of LMFBRs.
These efforts were significantly reduced in the 1990s as interest in building new commercial
LMFBRs waned and existing facilities were shutdown. Table 1-1 provides a list of the various
LMFBR consensus standards efforts that were sponsored by ANS along with their status as of the
beginning of fiscal year 2025 (FY25). Not all of these efforts resulted in a consensus standard being
issued for use. Of the 13 standards efforts listed in Table 1-1, only three of the working groups were
able to complete a consensus standard and have it issued for use. An additional five working groups
developed draft consensus standards that were never officially issued or only issued for trial use
and five other working groups efforts did not result in a draft consensus standard being completed.
At the beginning of FY25, only one of the three consensus standards that were issued, ANSI/ANS-
54.1-2020 [2], was currently active. Both ANSI/ANS-54.2-1985 [3] and ANSI/ANS-54.8-19882
[4] were officially withdrawn in the year 2000.

Fiscal year 2024 (FY24) and FY25 efforts described in the remainder of this report focus solely on
efforts to revive the ANS-54.8 standard related to liquid metal fire protection. As in FY23, the
FY24 – FY25 Argonne efforts are supported by the DOE, ARDP Regulatory Development,
Regulatory Framework Modernization Area.

The original purpose of ANS-54.8 as stated in the standard [4] is:

“The purpose of this standard is to establish guidelines and requirements to ensure that the
functional performance of liquid-metal fire detection, alarm, suppression, control, and structural
protection systems are adequate to protect the public health and safety, the plant personnel, and
minimize or limit the economic loss in the event of a sodium/NaK leak.”

One of the recommendations from the FY23 report [1] was to revive ANS-54.8 to support industry
development of appropriate sodium fire protection plans for their designs as well as support
regulatory interactions. It was noted in [1] that the ANS-54.8 standard was acknowledged during
an ANS Research and Advanced Reactor Consensus Committee (RARCC) meeting by an industry
participant as a consensus standard that is critical to SFR stakeholders and should be revived. It
was also noted that ANS-54.8 would have been useful during interactions between the various
organizations that were part of the Versatile Test Reactor (VTR) Project to help define goals,
relevant metrics, and expectations for the VTR sodium fire protection system and strategy. The
report went on to recommend activities where DOE support could assist in the revival of ANS-
54.8. Those activities included:

 3
• National laboratory participation in the ANS-54.8 Working Group to expediate the revival
 process.
• National laboratory interactions with the ANS-54.8 Working Group to provide historical
 sodium fire protection system strategies and designs.
• National laboratory interactions with the ANS-54.8 Working Group on best practices for
 sodium fire analysis methods and national laboratory tools for simulating sodium fire
 progression scenarios.

2 Purpose and Project Objectives

The current multi-year effort seeks to utilize national laboratory support from Argonne to expedite
revival of the ANS-54.8 standard titled “Liquid Metal Fire Protection in LMR Plants.” To achieve
that objective, the following tasks were identified:

Task 1 involves working directly with ANS to revive the ANS-54.8 Working Group. This was to
be accomplished by completing necessary paperwork, submitting that paperwork to ANS for
approval, enlisting potential working group members from industry, the NRC, national laboratories,
and universities, and organizing and leading Working Group activities. The overall purpose of the
Working Group is to develop an updated draft of ANS-54.8 for review and approval by ANS
consensus committees, the ANS Standards Board, and certification from ANSI.

Task 2 focuses on leveraging historical information and expertise at Argonne related to sodium fire
protection systems and strategies. For more than a decade, Argonne has worked on both
international and domestic projects related to sodium fire protection system development and
evaluation. The goal of this task is to utilize that experience to help inform the ANS-54.8 Working
Group on important considerations that may be addressed in an updated draft of ANS-54.8.

Task 3 focuses on leveraging Argonne experience in the development of and use of analysis
methods and tools for the simulation of sodium fire scenarios and evaluation of the effectiveness
of sodium fire protection systems and strategies. For more than a decade, Argonne has recovered,
modified, and utilized several historical sodium fire analysis software tools. The goal of this task
is to utilize that experience to help inform the ANS-54.8 Working Group of best practices for
sodium fire progression analysis that may be addressed in an updated draft of ANS-54.8.

The first required step in reviving the ANS-54.8 Working Group was to complete a Project
Initiation Notification System (PINS) Form. The purpose of the PINS Form is to notify the
American National Standards Institute (ANSI) of the initiation of a standards project intending to
seek ANSI approval as an American National Standard. Information from the PINS Form for
products intending to seek ANSI approval as an American National Standard are submitted to ANSI
to be added to ANSI’s central data bank, which contains information relative to voluntary national
standards and is a key resource in planning and coordination. Along with the PINS form,
background information on the planned project was submitted which is for Standards Committee
purposes only. Part of the required background information was to identify a Working Group Chair
and to identify Working Group Members. The PINS form and background information was
completed by Argonne and submitted to ANS in March of FY24. It should be noted that in the
PINS form, it was recommended to change the title of the ANS-54.8 Standard from “Liquid Metal
Fire Protection in LMR Plants” to “Liquid Metal Fire Protection.” This change reflects the
applicability and relevance of the standard to facilities that are not reactor plants such as liquid
metal test loops.

An RARCC ballot to approve the re-establishment of the ANS-54.8 Working Group based on the
PINS form and background information was opened in March of FY24 and approved by the
RARCC in April of FY24. The ANS-54.8 Working Group would be chaired by Matthew Bucknor
from Argonne, and include members from Aalo Atomics, ARC Clean-Technology Inc., Argonne
National Laboratory, Engineering Planning and Management Inc., the NRC, Oklo Inc., Sandia
National Laboratories, TerraPower, and the University of Wisconsin-Madison.

 6

While the RARCC was considering approval of the re-establishment of the ANS-54.8 Working
Group, Argonne participated in an NRC event titled “NRC: New and Advanced Reactors: Codes
and Standards,” in April of FY24 and presented a summary of the FY23 report [1] along with plans
to revive ANS-54.8. The NRC expressed interest in previous fast reactor codes and standards that
were identified in the FY23 report as well as the current efforts to revive ANS-54.8.

In addition to the NRC event, while the RARCC was considering approval of the re-establishment
of the ANS-54.8 Working Group, Argonne participated in an April FY24 meeting with ANS and
the Advanced Reactor Codes and Standards Collaborative (ARCSC). The purpose of the meeting
was to discuss ARCSC recommendations for ANS standards related to advanced reactors based on
an ARCSC assessment survey. The survey assessment covered 40 ANS standards including ANS-
54.8 and provided recommendations for many of those standards. The ARCSC recommendations
for ANS-54.8 were to reinstate it as-is as soon as possible and then initiate efforts to revise the
standard. The reasons for reinstating ANS-54.8 as soon as possible were due to a lack of alternative
standards for liquid metal fire protection and because industry was utilizing and referencing ANS-
54.8 even in its withdrawn state. Once the RARCC approved re-establishment of the ANS-54.8
Working Group in April of FY24, an ANS-54.8 Working Group ballot was issued to approve
reinstating ANS-54.8 with only minimal updates to references and the foreword material. The
Working Group approved the ballot in May FY24.

To implement ARCSC recommendations, Argonne worked with ANS staff to develop a 2-stage
plan for reinstatement and revision of ANS-54.8.

 • Stage 1: re-issue ANS-54.8 as-is to support current industry activities and engagements with
 only minimal updates to upfront material, references, and dated statements
 • Stage 2: review and revise the standard to include modern practices, RIPB considerations,
 and other relevant information

That plan was then presented to the RARCC at their semi-annual meeting in June FY24. During
that meeting the RARCC unanimously approved the 2-stage plan for ANS-54.8. The ANS
Standards Board approved the 2-stage plan in July FY24.

Argonne organized and held the first ANS-54.8 Working Group meeting in August FY24. Topics
discussed during the meeting included the history of the ANS-54.8 standard, FY24 efforts to revive
the standard, and the planned 2-stage approach to re-issue and revise the standard. It was agreed
upon during the meeting that the Working Group would benefit from Argonne providing
presentations during the next group meeting on sodium fires and historical information related to
sodium fire protection systems and strategies.

Following approval by the ANS Standards Board of the 2-stage plan for reinstatement and revision
of ANS-54.8, Argonne developed a draft Project Implementation Plan (PIP) for ANS-54.8 efforts
which is required by ANS for standards development. The PIP includes background information
for ANS-54.8, a listing of the ANS-54.8 Working Group members along with their company
affiliations, and a preliminary schedule for ANS-54.8 reinstatement and revision efforts. The draft
PIP was completed in September FY24, submitted to ANS for review, and approved by the
RARCC.

General Activities

The ANS-54.8 Working Group Chair (Matthew Bucknor, Argonne) was the featured speaker at an
EPRI Fast Reactor Technology User Group Meeting in July FY25, where he provided a
presentation on sodium fires, sodium fire protection systems, and current relevant DOE-funded
activities including the ANS-54.8 Stage 1 and Stage 2 efforts. The user group meeting included
representatives from fast reactor vendors, owner/operators, and industry supporting organizations.

The ANS-54.8 Working Group Chair (Matthew Bucknor, Argonne) was the featured speaker at an
NRC Office of Nuclear Regulatory Research (RES) Seminar in August FY25 titled: “Sodium Fires
& Sodium Fire Protection Systems: The Basics, Historical Perspectives, and Ongoing Efforts.” His
efforts during this NRC internal seminar were designed to educate NRC staff on topics associated
with sodium fires which are important for SFR license application reviews. He also highlighted
current relevant DOE-funded activities including the ANS-54.8 Stage 1 and Stage 2 efforts.

Stage 1 Activities

For Stage 1, only minor edits to the 1988 version of ANS-54.8 were completed by Argonne in
February FY25. These included:

 • updating the Standard to the modern ANS standards template,
 • updating upfront materials (publisher information and the foreword),
 • reviewing and updating definitions to align with the ANS Standards Committee Glossary of
 Definitions and Terminology,

 8
 • updating text formatting such as bolding the terms “may”, “shall”, and “should”,
 • updating other text, lists, and references to align with modern ANS Standards formatting (the
 references themselves were not updated in Stage 1, but will be updated in Stage 2), and
 • addressing any typos and other errors.

The updated Stage 1 draft was then provided to the ANS-54.8 Working Group for review and
comments. After review and recommendations by the Working Group, additional minor updates
were made by Argonne and the Working Group approved the draft to be sent to ANS consensus
committees/subcommittees for review, comment, and approval.

ANS consensus committee/subcommittee reviews began in March FY25. The reviewers were made
aware of the plans to re-issue and revise ANS-54.8 via the two-stage process. Minor comments
from the committee/subcommittee reviews were addressed by Argonne. Some comments related to
planned Stage 2 revisions and were therefore acknowledged and documented for later use. The
consensus committee/subcommittee review and comment resolution period was concluded in June
FY25. An RARCC ballot to approve the Stage 1 draft was initiated at that time and approved in
July FY25 to be sent to the ANS Standards Manager for review and certification. In August FY25
Argonne worked with ANS technical editing staff to address minor text, grammar, and formatting
issues for the Stage 1 draft. In September FY25, the Stage 1 draft was approved by the ANS
Standards Manager and with ANSI certification it was issued as ANSI/ANS-54.8-2025. The re-
issue of ANS-54.8 marked the end of Stage 1 activities.

Stage 2 Activities

While the main focus of FY25 efforts was on Stage 1 activities to get a version of ANS-54.8 re-
issued for use, initial Stage 2 efforts were completed in parallel to expedite the process of revising
ANS-54.8. A review by the ANS-54.8 Working Group of the 1988 version of ANS-54.8 was
completed in December FY25 to identify sections of the Standard that would be revised. In January
FY25, ANS-54.8 Working Group members from Argonne were tasked with efforts to draft an
updated version of the Standard for Working Group review. These revision efforts were initiated
in March FY25 and will be completed in early fiscal year 2026 (FY26).

Stage 2 activities include steps necessary to revise ANS-54.8 to include updated information
which could include topics such as risk-informed insights, performance-based guidance, leak-
before-break applicability and guidance, and/or information on newly developed liquid metal
fire suppression systems or detection methods. Initial Stage 2 efforts were completed in FY25
and Argonne has already started drafting a revised version of ANS-54.8 for Working Group
review. Efforts to develop the revised Stage 2 draft are anticipated to be completed in October
FY25. Following review, comments, and comment resolution by the Working Group, it will
hold an approval vote for the revised draft of ANS-54.8. Following that, the process to complete
Stage 2 will be similar to that of the Stage 1 process. The revised version of ANS-54.8 will be
provided to ANS consensus committees/subcommittees, such as the RARCC, for review. An
RARCC ballot will be issued for members to vote to approve the revised version of ANS-54.8
with or without comments. The ANS-54.8 Working Group will then work to resolve any
comments from the consensus committees. Following consensus committee comment resolution,
a draft of ANS-54.8 will be provided to the ANS Standards Manager for certification. If the
proposed schedule is achieved, the revised Stage 2 version of ANS-54.8 will be completed and
issued in September FY26.
