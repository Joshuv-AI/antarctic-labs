DT-GV correspondence on the Mukai-Umemura variety
Kiryong Chung, Joonyeong Won

Abstract
. We compute Donaldson–Thomas (DT) invariants and their descendant invariants for the local Calabi–Yau 4 4 –fold over the Mukai–Umemura variety via several localization formulas. Assuming that the genus-one Gopakumar–Vafa (GV) type invariants vanish, our computations verify the predictions of Cao, Maulik, and Toda.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and result

Let
Y
be a Calabi–Yau
4
-fold and

\beta\in H_{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
be a curve class.
Let
\mathbf{M}_{\beta}(Y)(=\mathbf{M})
denote the moduli space of one-dimensional
stable sheaves
F

Y

[F]=\beta
and
\chi(F)=1

Given a class
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}^{4-2i}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
,
the
insertion
is defined by

\tau_{i}(\gamma)=\pi_{\mathbf{M}*}\!\left(\pi_{Y}^{*}\gamma\cup\mathrm{ch}_{i+3}(\mathcal{F})\right),

where
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{Coh}(\mathbf{M}_{\beta}(Y)\times Y)
is the universal sheaf with the
trivial determinant
(i.e.,
\det(R\pi_{\mathbf{M}*}\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{M}}
). The primary (for
i=0
) or descendent (for

) invariants are defined by the integration of the insertion over the virtual class
[\mathbf{M}_{\beta}(Y)]^{\mathrm{vir}}
:

(1)

\langle\tau_{i}(\gamma)\rangle_{\beta}:=\int_{[\mathbf{M}_{\beta}(Y)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\tau_{i}(\gamma).

The integral
\langle\tau_{0}(\gamma)\rangle_{\beta}
is called
\mathrm{DT}
-invariants
(or
primary invariant
).
When
i\geq 1
, we call
\tau_{i}(\gamma)

descendant insertions
, and its integrations over the virtual class are called
descendant invariants
.

The DT theory of Calabi–Yau 4-folds has recently attracted significant attention in enumerative geometry, motivated by its rich geometric structures and deep connections to other curve counting theories. In particular, Cao, Maulik, and Toda proposed a series of conjectures predicting a precise correspondence between DT invariants of Calabi–Yau 4–folds and the Gopakumar–Vafa (GV) type invariants introduced by Klemm and Pandharipande (
[
CMT18
,
CT21
,
KP08
]
). More precisely, it states that

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
CMT18
, Conjecture 0.2]
and
[
CT21
, Conjecture 0.2]
)
.

Let
n_{0,\beta}(-)
(resp.
n_{1,\beta}
) denote genus
0
(resp.
1
) GV-type invariants and
m_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}
be the meeting invariants defined in
[

]
. Under the suitable orientation on the moduli space

, we have

(a)

n_{0,\beta}(\gamma_{0})=\langle\tau_{0}(\gamma_{0})\rangle_{\beta}
for
\gamma_{0}\in\mathrm{H}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

(b)

Furthermore, we have an linear identity:

(2)

\langle\tau_{1}(\gamma_{1})\rangle_{\beta}=\frac{n_{0,\beta}(\gamma_{1}^{2})}{2(\gamma_{1}\cdot\beta)}-\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}\frac{(\gamma_{1}\cdot\beta_{1})(\gamma_{1}\cdot\beta_{2})}{4(\gamma_{1}\cdot\beta)}\,m_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}-\sum_{k\geq 1,\,k|\beta}\frac{\gamma_{1}\cdot\beta}{k}\,n_{1,\beta/k}

with respect to
\gamma_{1}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

In this paper, we study the case where

is the total space of the canonical bundle of the
Mukai-Umemura variety

(i.e. the unique smooth prime Fano threefold of genus

-action). For the defining relations of the meeting invariants

It was previously known that Conjecture

holds for curve classes

Theorem 1.2

(
=

)

For

Some facts in equivariant K-theory

In

,

\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant Euler characteristic

for

and

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem 3.5
[
Tho92
]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with a torus action
T
and let

F,G\in\text{Coh}^{T}(X)
.
Assume that the fixed locus
X^{T}
consists of isolated points.
Then for
i_{p}:\{p\}\hookrightarrow X
, we have

\chi(F,G)=\sum_{p\in X^{T}}\frac{i_{p}^{*}([F])^{\vee}\cdot i_{p}^{*}([G])}{\lambda_{-1}(T_{p}^{*}X)}.

We recall the
(equivariant) self-intersection formula
in the
K
-theory.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
Tho93
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
i\colon C\hookrightarrow X
be an embedding map such that
C
is a locally complete intersection.
Let
p\in C^{T}
be a
T
-fixed point. Then for any
F\in\text{Coh}^{T}(C)
,

i_{p}^{*}\bigl([i_{*}F]\bigr)=[F]_{p}\cdot\lambda_{-1}\!\bigl(N^{*}_{C/X,p}\bigr)\in K_{T}(\mathrm{pt}).

Here
[F]_{p}=[F\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\mathbb{C}_{p}]\in K_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
denotes the derived fiber of
F
at the point
p
.

Remark 2.5
.

In Theorem
2.4
, if
F
is locally free on
C
around the closed point
p
, then
[F]_{p}
coincides with the class
of the
T
-representation on the usual fiber
F|_{p}
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose that
X
is smooth and that the fixed point locus
X^{T}
is isolated.
Then for any
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant locally free sheaf
E
on
X
, we have

\chi(E)=\sum_{p\in X^{T}}\frac{[E]_{p}}{\lambda_{-1}(T_{p}^{*}X)}.

Proof.

Let
F=\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
G=E
. Then by Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
, we get the result.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with a
T
-action, and let

i\colon C\hookrightarrow X
be a smooth
T
-invariant curve.
Then

(5)

\chi(i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C},i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C})=\sum_{i=0}^{\dim X-1}(-1)^{i}\chi(C,\,\wedge^{i}N_{C/X}),

where
\wedge^{0}N_{C/X}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
.

Proof.

Applying

in Theorem

, a direct calculation provides the result by Theorem

3.1.

Let

\mathbf{M}_{\beta}(X)\cong\mathbf{M}_{\pi^{*}\beta}(Y),F\mapsto i_{*}F

for the zero section

\tau_{i}(\gamma)=\pi_{\mathbf{M}*}\left(\pi_{X}^{*}\gamma\cup\{\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{F})\cdot\text{td}(K_{X})^{-1}\}_{i+2}\right)

and thus the integration in (
1

[\mathbf{M}_{\beta}(X)]^{\mathrm{vir}}
(
[
Tho00
, Corollary 3.39]

Proposition 3.1
.

Let us assume that the fixed locus
\mathbf{M}^{T}:=\mathbf{M}_{\beta}(X)^{T}
is isolated. Then for
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}_{T}^{4-2i}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, the integration of the insertion is

(9)

\langle\tau_{i}(\gamma)\rangle_{d}=\sum_{[F]\in\mathbf{M}^{T}}\left(\sum_{p\in\mathrm{supp}(F)^{T}}\frac{\gamma|_{p}\cdot\{\mathrm{ch}^{T}(F|_{p})\cdot\mathrm{td}^{T}(K_{X}|_{p})^{-1}\}_{i+2}}{e^{T}(T_{X}|_{p})}\right)e^{T}\left(\chi(F,F)-1\right).

Proof.

The equivariant-virtual tangent space at
[F]
of
\mathbf{M}:=\mathbf{M}_{\beta}(X)
is

T_{[F]}^{\text{virt}}\mathbf{M}=[\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(F,F)]-[\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(F,F)]=-\chi(F,F)+1

since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\mathrm{Ext}^{3}(F,F)=0
by stability of the stable sheaf
F
(
[
CL14
, Theorem 6.5]
and
[
Tho00
, Corollary 3.39]
). Therefore, by applying the virtual localization formula (Theorem
2.1
