Interplay of bound states in the continuum and Fano–Andreev interference in a hybrid triple quantum dot
Authors: A I González, Pedro A Orellana, Vladimir Juričić
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe investigate bound states in the continuum (BICs) in a hybrid normal–superconducting triple quantum dot system, where the central dot is coupled to two normal leads and the lateral dots are proximity-coupled to superconducting electrodes. Local electron–electron interactions are treated within the Hubbard-I approximation. Finite bias probes Fano–Andreev interference between the direct elastic tunneling path through the central dot and indirect proximity-induced paths involving the lateral dots, revealing an Andreev-type BIC in the symmetric limit and its crossover into a quasi-BIC when lateral-dot detuning mixes the dark and bright sectors. As the bias is swept through the subgap resonances, electron transmission (ET) exhibits sharp antiresonances that evolve into near-zero transmission minima, signaling the transport fingerprints of (quasi-)BICs. We further find a continuous crossover from a Fano–Andreev BIC-supported regime to a Fano–Andreev quasi-BIC regime as the lateral-dot detuning increases. The formation of BICs and quasi-BICs is accompanied by a bias-dependent redistribution of the occupations of the side quantum dots, providing an internal diagnostic directly correlated with the transport signatures of the bound states.Subject terms: Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

Bound states in the continuum (BICs), originally predicted by von Neumann and Wigner1, are localized states embedded in the continuum spectrum. They constitute a paradigmatic interference effect2 and were first observed experimentally by Plotnik et al.3. These states have been reported in acoustic, photonic, and related fields2–5, but their experimental realization in mesoscopic electronic transport remains elusive.

A closely related interference phenomenon in mesoscopic transport is the Fano effect, which arises from the interplay between a discrete level and a continuum channel6,7. It produces asymmetric resonances, antiresonances, or even complete suppression of transport8. This phenomenon has been investigated in T-shaped double quantum dot systems (T-DQD) with metallic leads, both with and without a floating lead. In systems without a floating lead, the Fano effect is observed as an asymmetric line shape in the transmission spectrum, modeled as , where , is the resonance energy, Γ the linewidth, and q the asymmetry parameter8,9. By contrast, an additional floating metallic lead induces decoherence and suppresses the Fano line shape10,11. Superconducting electrodes, however, qualitatively alter this behavior.

In particular, superconducting proximity effects enable coherent electron-hole conversion via Andreev reflection, leading to Andreev bound states (ABS) inside the superconducting gap12–27. When a superconducting lead is additionally coupled to a T-shaped double quantum dot connected to normal leads, the induced particle-hole mixing on the side dot generates ABS that interfere with the direct normal transmission channel, producing Fano-like structures in the electronic transmission between the normal leads28–30. In this case, however, the Fano asymmetry parameter q becomes complex, so the electronic transmission no longer vanishes exactly31,32. Along the same line, Xu and Shi showed that a parallel double-quantum-dot structure with side-coupled superconductors can host true Andreev bound states in the continuum in the symmetric configuration, while a finite superconducting phase difference can drive their evolution into Fano antiresonances33.

In comparison to the double quantum dot (DQD) structure, triple quantum dots provide additional tunability and a richer interference structure due to the extra degree of freedom. In particular, the two lateral dots support symmetric and antisymmetric combinations, which can host quasi-BICs and BICs, thereby offering a highly tunable platform for engineering and controlling such states. In the absence of additional decoherence channels, the antisymmetric combination may fully decouple from the continuum and form a BIC. However, when energy detuning is introduced between the side quantum dots, this state is no longer bound, but instead couples weakly to the continuum and becomes a quasi-BIC. These effects have been investigated in both normal and hybrid nanostructures and have been shown to strongly affect charge, spin, and thermoelectric transport34–44.

It is important to note that many interference-induced states discussed in the context of multiple quantum dot systems, particularly within the Dicke framework, correspond to subradiant states with strongly suppressed but finite coupling to the continuum34,38,39. While such states can exhibit very narrow linewidths and BIC-like features, they do not necessarily fulfill the strict condition of a bound state in the continuum. By contrast, true BIC formation in triple quantum dot systems has been explicitly demonstrated only under conditions of exact destructive interference leading to complete decoupling41. In the present work, we adopt this distinction and refer to finite-linewidth states as quasi-BICs, reserving the term BIC for the symmetry-protected case of exact decoupling.

In parallel, Fano–Andreev interference is well established in hybrid normal–superconducting nanostructures, where transport involves the interplay between direct electron transmission and superconductivity-induced electron–hole conversion processes28–32,42,44,45. Additionally, topological-superconducting hybrid structures involving Majorana bound states have also been shown to exhibit BIC-related spectral features, Fano antiresonances, and transport-suppression effects arising from the coupling between Majorana modes, quantum-dot states, and continuum channels46–48. In contrast to these double-dot or topological-superconducting settings, the present work considers a non-topological triple-dot geometry in which the BIC mechanism is controlled by the symmetry between the two lateral dots and by the gate-tunable detuning parameter η. In such a setting, the combined manifestation of symmetry-protected BIC physics and Fano–Andreev interference in an interacting finite-bias normal–superconducting triple quantum dot remains only partially understood.

In this work, using the nonequilibrium Green’s-function formalism49,50 (see Sect. S1 of the Supplementary Material for details), we show that the detuning parameter provides direct control over the coupling between the BIC-related subgap sector and the transport continuum, thereby driving a continuous crossover from a BIC-supported Fano–Andreev regime to a quasi-BIC regime.

This crossover is reflected simultaneously in transport and spectral observables: the evolution of finite ET antiresonances into near-zero transmission minima, correlated features in the AR response, the broadening of narrow subgap resonances, and a redistribution of the lateral-dot occupations.

Our results thus identify a concrete and experimentally accessible setting in which BIC formation, superconducting proximity, and interaction effects interplay and can be probed on equal footing through hybrid mesoscopic transport.

At the same time, superconducting proximity induces pairing on the lateral dots and generates Andreev bound states (ABS) inside the gap, enabling an indirect Andreev-mediated pathway that interferes with the direct elastic path through . The interference between these channels produces the characteristic Fano–Andreev structure in , with a complex asymmetry parameter q and finite-depth antiresonances. These features arise from the combined action of the symmetry-induced bright/dark decomposition and the Fano–Andreev interference.

Within the superconducting gap, the superconducting self-energy is purely real, so the superconducting electrodes do not provide an additional dissipative channel. As a result, the effective decay rate of the antisymmetric sector vanishes, yielding a bound state in the continuum with a real energy and infinite lifetime with respect to decay into the normal leads. Since this state is dressed by proximity-induced anomalous correlations, it can be interpreted as an Andreev bound state embedded in the continuum.

It is important to emphasize that the BIC originates from symmetry-induced destructive interference within the lateral subsystem, while ET and AR act as complementary probes that reveal its presence through Fano–Andreev interference.

To further clarify the physical mechanism underlying the crossover from the symmetry-protected BIC regime to the quasi-BIC regime, Fig. 3 presents a schematic representation of the bright/dark decomposition of the lateral subsystem and its evolution under finite detuning, in the large-gap and wide-band limits45. In the symmetric case, the antisymmetric sector has zero projection onto the central dot and therefore remains decoupled from the normal continuum, giving rise to Andreev-BICs with purely real energies. When a finite detuning is introduced, the bright and dark sectors become mixed, allowing the formerly dark state to acquire a small but finite linewidth and to evolve into a quasi-BIC.

A useful consistency check is obtained by setting , in which case the superconducting electrodes and reduce to normal leads. In this limit, the proximity-induced anomalous contributions vanish, and the Andreev-related features in the transport, such as the ET antiresonances, disappear. This shows that the observed antiresonances are of genuine Fano–Andreev origin: without superconducting pairing, the indirect amplitude mediated by the lateral subsystem lacks the Andreev-dressed resonant structure required to cancel the direct central-dot contribution. Once superconducting proximity is restored, the indirect transport pathway is qualitatively reshaped and interferes destructively with the background ET contribution, thereby producing the Fano-like conductance dips. Since the Hubbard poles remain inside the superconducting gap, the complex character of the Fano parameter q is not inherited directly from the superconducting density of states, but indirectly from the dissipative normal-lead self-energy transmitted through the lateral-dot sector, which supplies the dissipative phase of the transport channel.

However, to further clarify the relation with previous studies of normal triple quantum dot systems, it is important to note that the limit in the present geometry does not reproduce the configuration studied in Ref.42. In our setup, this limit effectively turns the lateral superconducting electrodes into additional normal reservoirs, while the central dot remains coupled to two normal leads. By contrast, this reference considers a N–TQD–S geometry, where transport is governed exclusively by Andreev reflection. An equivalent configuration would require switching off the lateral superconducting couplings, , and replacing one of the normal leads by a superconducting electrode coupled to the central dot. This regime is not explored in the present work, and therefore the comparison with Ref.42 should be understood at a conceptual level, highlighting the role of interference and superconducting correlations, rather than as a direct quantitative correspondence.

Figures 2c and 2e show that, once a small but finite detuning is introduced, the antiresonances in deepen and eventually approach near-zero values (within numerical resolution) at finite bias , while Fig. 2d and f display the corresponding spectral evolution in the LDOS. Simultaneously, develops pronounced minima, indicating that Andreev transport is suppressed by the same destructive-interference mechanism. Physically, finite detuning mixes the symmetric and antisymmetric sectors, so that the antisymmetric sector acquires an indirect leakage path to the transport continuum through its mixing with the bright sector and, through it, with the central dot. In the presence of superconducting proximity, the ABS on the lateral dots provide additional virtual tunneling paths, allowing this weakly coupled subgap sector to participate actively in transport interference. The coexistence of these channels permits nearly complete destructive interference between direct and indirect processes, leading to a strong suppression of the ET conductance in at specific bias values.

This evolution is also reflected in the LDOS (see Fig. 2d and f), where the lateral-dot peaks broaden progressively with increasing η. This η-dependent broadening indicates that detuning weakens the destructive-interference condition protecting the long-lived states in the nearly symmetric regime. Accordingly, the antisymmetric sector hybridizes more strongly with the symmetric, transport-active sector and, through the central dot, with the normal continuum. Its effective decay rate () therefore increases with η, and the LDOS evolves from an almost δ-like structure to a finite-width resonance. Because the lateral dots are not directly coupled to the normal leads, this broadening should be understood as an indirect leakage process mediated by the central dot. In this sense, the detuning η acts as a symmetry-breaking field in the lateral subspace: in the (+, -) basis it couples the bright and dark sectors, thereby lifting the destructive-interference condition that protects the antisymmetric state in the symmetric configuration. As a consequence, the dark state acquires a finite projection onto the central dot and hence a finite decay rate into the normal continuum, converting the BIC into a quasi-BIC. This mechanism provides a direct physical picture of the detuning-induced crossover and is reflected in the simultaneous broadening of subgap LDOS peaks and the evolution of transport features. In parallel, the AR conductance develops antiresonances at bias values close to, although not exactly coincident with, those of . This shows that the same quasi-BIC that suppresses normal transmission also weakens the coherent electron-hole conversion responsible for Andreev reflection. Both features originate from the same detuning-controlled interference mechanism, although they involve different components of the Green’s function: the normal component for ET and the anomalous one for AR. We therefore identify a continuous crossover from a Fano–Andreev BIC-supported regime to a Fano–Andreev quasi-BIC regime as η increases.

A complementary perspective on the BIC-to-quasi-BIC crossover is provided by the occupations of the lateral dots (see Supplementary Material). In the interacting case, the side-dot occupations also acquire a direct spectral meaning within the Hubbard-I approximation. The local Coulomb interaction generates an additional Hubbard-shifted spectral branch at , whose spectral weight is controlled by the opposite-spin occupation13. Schematically, the local propagator contains contributions with weights and , associated with the branches and , respectively. Therefore, the proximity-induced Andreev-BIC or quasi-BIC features that appear around can be replicated around the Hubbard-shifted energies . This interaction-induced duplication explains why the occupation anomalies are correlated with additional structures in both the differential conductance and the LDOS.

On the other hand, since the occupations are determined by the integrated local spectral weight, they offer an internal probe of the redistribution of spectral weight between the dark and transport-active sectors. In particular, in the symmetric configuration (η =0), the occupations remain nearly independent of bias, reflecting the complete decoupling of the antisymmetric dark sector. For finite detuning, however, pronounced bias-dependent features emerge, signaling the hybridization of this sector with the transport-active bright sector and the redistribution of spectral weight associated with the lifting of the destructive-interference condition. This behavior is fully consistent with the symmetry-breaking mechanism described above and provides an independent signature of the crossover from a BIC to a quasi-BIC regime.

Summary and discussion

In this work, we analyzed quantum transport in an interacting triple quantum dot system coupled to normal and superconducting electrodes, with particular emphasis on the emergence and evolution of BICs driven by interference effects.

Our results highlight the interplay of Coulomb interactions, superconducting proximity, and quantum interference in hybrid nanostructures. They provide a unified interpretation of the BIC mechanism in the interacting triple-dot subsystem and its interplay with the proximity-induced Fano–Andreev effect, and establish these devices as a versatile platform for engineering and controlling BICs in mesoscopic superconducting systems. In particular, we identify a continuous crossover from a Fano–Andreev BIC-supported regime to a Fano–Andreev quasi-BIC regime as the detuning parameter η increases. In the symmetric limit (η =0), the antisymmetric sector is fully decoupled from the transport channel. Because this decoupled sector remains in the subgap window and is induced by superconducting proximity, it can be viewed, in the ideal symmetric limit, as an Andreev bound state in the continuum. With increasing detuning, destructive interference in the elastic-tunneling channel is reinforced, driving the antiresonances towards near-zero values and producing quasi-BIC behavior. Simultaneously, the Andreev differential conductance develops corresponding minima, reflecting a redistribution of spectral weight between elastic and Andreev processes. We further show that, in the interaction-driven regime, the emergence of quasi-BIC and BIC features is intrinsically tied to bias-induced charge redistribution on the lateral dots, rather than being a purely single-particle interference effect.

In conclusion, our results reveal clear and experimentally accessible signatures of the detuning-driven crossover from a Fano–Andreev BIC-supported regime to a Fano–Andreev quasi-BIC regime. These signatures can be directly probed by tunneling spectroscopy, in particular through differential-conductance (dI/dV) measurements and local spectroscopic probes capable of resolving the narrow subgap resonances. Experimentally, the key control parameter is the lateral-dot detuning η, which can be tuned by independent gate voltages applied to the side quantum dots52,53. This gate control provides a direct route to track the progressive evolution from finite ET antiresonances to near-zero transmission minima, together with the accompanying changes in the AR response and in the spectral width of the lateral-dot resonances.

More broadly, our findings show that hybrid triple-quantum-dot devices offer a realistic platform in which interference effects, superconducting proximity, and local Coulomb interactions can be manipulated on equal footing. In this sense, the present setup provides a concrete framework for investigating how long-lived BIC-related subgap states emerge, hybridize, and eventually leak into the transport continuum. We therefore expect our results to be relevant not only for the identification of BIC and quasi-BIC features in mesoscopic superconducting transport, but also for the broader design of hybrid nanostructures in which interference can be engineered and electrically tuned with high precision.
