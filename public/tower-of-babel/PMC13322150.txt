Prediction of Anthracycline Benefit in Hormone Receptor–Positive, Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2–Negative Early-Stage Breast Cancer by the MammaPrint 70-Gene Signature for Patients Enrolled in the FLEX Study

ABSTRACT
PURPOSERandomized trials have not demonstrated clear benefit from anthracyclines for patients with hormone receptor–positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2–negative (HER2−) early breast cancer (EBC). The MammaPrint 70-gene signature, specifically the High-Risk 1 (H1) and High-Risk 2 (H2) subgroups, was evaluated in predicting benefit from anthracycline- and taxane-based chemotherapy (AC-T) compared with taxane/cyclophosphamide (TC) in hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC.MATERIALS AND METHODSThis analysis used prospectively collected real-world data from patients enrolled in the FLEX study with stage I-III hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC, MammaPrint High-Risk, and BluePrint Luminal B tumors who received adjuvant AC-T or TC. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was performed to balance clinical characteristics between treatment groups. The association between the chemotherapy regimen and 3-year invasive disease-free survival (IDFS) was assessed using Kaplan-Meier estimates and Cox proportional hazards models.RESULTSAmong patients with H1 cancers (n = 1,106), 3-year IDFS did not differ between AC-T and TC (95.9% v 95.9%; adjusted hazard ratio [HRs], 1.14 [95% CI, 0.64 to 2.06]; P = .70). By contrast, patients with H2 cancers (n = 153) demonstrated significantly improved 3-year IDFS with AC-T versus TC (100% v 94.8%; adjusted HRs, 0.10 [95% CI, 0.01 to 0.75]; absolute benefit 5.2%; P = .025). A significant treatment-by-MammaPrint interaction demonstrated that AC-T benefit increased with higher genomic risk (adjusted HRs, 0.09 [95% CI, 0.01 to 0.85]; P = .036). Clinical and pathologic variables did not predict anthracycline benefit.CONCLUSIONPatients with MammaPrint H2 tumors had a clinically meaningful benefit from anthracycline-containing chemotherapy, whereas patients with H1 tumors did not derive more benefit from AC-T than from TC despite overlapping high-risk features. These findings support the use of MammaPrint to inform chemotherapy regimen selection and warrant further validation.

INTRODUCTION

Anthracyclines are among the most effective antitumor agents used in the treatment of breast cancer. Meta-analyses from the Early Breast Cancer (EBC) Trialists' Collaborative Group (EBCTCG) have demonstrated that chemotherapy regimens incorporating an anthracycline substantially reduce recurrence risk and improve survival compared with nonanthracycline regimens.1 For patients with hormone receptor–positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2–negative (HER2−) EBC, both anthracycline-containing regimens, such as doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide followed by paclitaxel (AC-T), and nonanthracycline regimens, such as docetaxel/cyclophosphamide (TC), remain standards of care. However, limited data exist to guide identification of patients who benefit from anthracycline-based therapy. Given the uncommon but serious toxicities of anthracyclines including cardiotoxicity and secondary myelodysplastic syndrome or acute myeloid leukemia, careful risk-benefit assessment is required. In current clinical practice, it is generally recommended that patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC receive anthracycline-based adjuvant chemotherapy based on high-risk features including large tumor size, nodal involvement, high grade disease, or younger age.2

Several clinical trials have evaluated whether anthracycline-free chemotherapy regimens are noninferior to anthracycline-containing regimens in clinically high-risk, HER2-negative EBC. In the Anthracyclines in EBC (ABC) Trials, docetaxel/cyclophosphamide (TC) was compared with doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide with a taxane (TaxAC).3 Although the trial did not demonstrate that TC was noninferior to TaxAC overall, among a subgroup of 2,877 patients with clinically high-risk hormone receptor–positive disease, invasive disease-free survival (IDFS) did not differ significantly between TaxAC and TC (hazard ratio [HRs], 1.19 [95% CI, 0.96 to 1.47]; P = .11). Similarly, in the Plan B trial, 2,449 patients with clinically high-risk, HER2-negative breast cancer, with over 80% being hormone receptor–positive, were randomly assigned to TC or epirubicin/cyclophosphamide followed by docetaxel (EC-T).4 Five-year disease-free survival (DFS) was nearly identical in TC (89.9% [95% CI, 88.1 to 91.8]) and EC-T arms (89.6% [95% CI, 87.9 to 91.5]). Consistent with these findings, the phase III MASTER trial with 1,048 patients demonstrated that six cycles of TC were noninferior to epirubicin/cyclophosphamide followed by paclitaxel (EC-P) in patients with operable HER2-negative breast cancer, with similar 5-year DFS rates for TC (85.0%) versus EC-P (85.9%; HRs, 1.05 [90% CI, 0.79 to 1.39]; P = .771).5 The study observed comparable 5-year DFS rates (TC: 85.7%, EC-P: 85.9%) among the ER-positive subgroup of 966 patients (HRs, 0.99 [90% CI, 0.74 to 1.33]; P = .953). Finally, among 2,012 women with early, DNA topoisomerase II-alpha (TOP2A)–normal breast cancer enrolled in the DBCG 07-READ trial, 71% of whom had ER-positive disease and at least one high-risk factor, no significant difference in 5-year DFS was observed between patients treated with EC-T versus TC (HRs, 1.00 [95% CI, 0.78 to 1.28]; P = 1.00).6 Patients in these trials generally had high-risk hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC, yet derived no significant benefit from anthracycline. Diagnostic methods are needed to identify patients who will benefit from neo-/adjuvant chemotherapy for their hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC but can safely forego anthracycline therapy.

The 70-gene expression assay, MammaPrint, determines risk of distant metastasis as UltraLow (UL), Low (LR), High-Risk 1 (H1), or High-Risk 2 (H2) and is widely used to make treatment recommendations in patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC.7 In the Microarray In Negative or Up to Three Nodes Disease May Avoid Chemotherapy (MINDACT) Trial, patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2−, clinically high-risk but MammaPrint UL and LR cancers had excellent outcomes without chemotherapy.7-9 By contrast, data from the ongoing FLEX trial show that patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC with the highest MammaPrint risk (H2) derived significant benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy, with an average of 11% absolute benefit in distant recurrence-free interval.10 Previous studies have also demonstrated that MammaPrint H2 tumors have higher rates of pathologic complete response (pCR) to neoadjuvant anthracycline-based versus taxane-based regimens11 and exhibit greater enrichment of immune-related gene expression compared with H1 tumors.12

In this study, we evaluated the utility of MammaPrint in predicting which patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC are likely to benefit from anthracycline-based therapy. Using prospectively collected data from the FLEX Study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03053193), we compared IDFS among patients with MammaPrint High-Risk tumors treated with adjuvant AC-T or TC based on the H1 versus H2 status of the cancers. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was used to adjust for confounding clinical variables. We hypothesized that patients with H2 cancers would benefit from anthracycline therapy, whereas patients with H1 cancers would not benefit differentially from AC-T over TC, suggesting that it may be safe to omit anthracycline therapy in patients with H1 hormone receptor–positive HER2− cancers.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study Design and Patient Population

FLEX is an ongoing, prospective, observational registry trial that integrates MammaPrint, BluePrint, and whole-transcriptome data with clinical outcomes, providing a real-world evidence platform for evaluating genomic signatures in EBC. Patients 18 years and older with stage I-III invasive EBC whose primary tumors undergo standard-of-care MammaPrint genomic testing, with or without BluePrint molecular subtyping, are eligible to be enrolled. The protocol follows ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki13 and adheres to international Good Clinical Practice guidelines.14 The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Worldwide Clinical Research IRB (protocol code #2017003) and the IRBs at all participating institutions and registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03053193). All participants provided informed consent for study participation, for whole-transcriptome analysis of their cancer, and for collection of their treatment and outcome data. Systemic therapy recommendations for chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, or targeted therapies are made at the discretion of treating physicians.

This study included a subset of FLEX participants diagnosed across 73 US institutions between 2015 and 2022 with hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC classified as MammaPrint High-Risk (January 1, 2025 data cutoff). Analysis was restricted to BluePrint Luminal-B tumors to increase homogeneity. Approximately 15% of MammaPrint High-Risk tumors are genomically basal-type despite hormone receptor–positive HER2− status, with distinct recurrence patterns and therapeutic implications that would confound anthracycline benefit evaluation in a canonical luminal population.15 Eligible patients received adjuvant chemotherapy with either taxane and cyclophosphamide (TC) or anthracycline (doxorubicin) and cyclophosphamide with taxane (AC-T). Patients in the AC-T group received anthracycline plus cyclophosphamide followed by a taxane or received anthracycline, cyclophosphamide, and a taxane concurrently. All patients underwent surgical resection of their primary EBC and postoperative radiation if indicated. Patients who received neoadjuvant therapy were excluded to ensure uniformity in treatment timing, resulting in a total of 1,259 patients included in the current analysis, with a median follow-up of 3.2 years (Table 1, Fig 1). The median follow-up was 2.8 years (IQR, 2.1-3.8) for patients with IDFS events and 3.2 years (IQR, 3.0-4.9) for those without events.

Given the observational, nonrandomized design of the study, IPTW was applied to balance demographic, clinical, and pathologic characteristics and facilitate comparison of outcomes between AC-T and TC treatment groups, thereby mitigating confounding by indication and minimizing bias (Table 1, Fig 1). Propensity scores estimating the probability of receiving treatment were calculated using logistic regression, and the resulting inverse probability weights were applied to each patient. All clinicopathologic variables including age, menopausal status, race, tumor size, lymph node status, and tumor grade were incorporated into the weighting model, retaining the full cohort for analysis. Covariate balance between treatment groups was evaluated using standardized differences; per Austin,17 an absolute standardized difference below 0.125 was considered indicative of well-balanced covariates.

Pathologic and Genomic Evaluation

Hormone receptors (estrogen receptor [ER] and progesterone receptor [PR]) were locally assessed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue by immunohistochemistry (IHC) and defined as positive if ≥1% of tumor cells demonstrated positive nuclear staining based on ASCO/College of American Pathologists (CAP) guidelines.18 HER2 status was determined at local institutions by IHC and/or fluorescence in situ hybridization according to ASCO/CAP guidelines.19 MammaPrint and BluePrint are microarray-based gene expression assays performed on RNA extracted from FFPE tumor tissue with a minimum of 30% tumor cellularity. Testing was conducted at the Agendia, Inc, a CAP- and CLIA-certified laboratory (Irvine, CA) using established protocols.20-22 Briefly, RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA, amplified, labeled, and hybridized onto custom-designed diagnostic microarrays (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA). Test results were generated independent of clinical or pathologic data. MammaPrint classified tumors as UL (+1.000 to +0.356), LR (+0.355 to +0.001), H1 (0 to -0.570), and H2 (-0.571 to -1.000) Risk. BluePrint classified tumors as Basal, HER2, or Luminal-Type, and Luminal type can be classified as Luminal-A or Luminal-B for MammaPrint Low- or High-Risk results, respectively. The current analysis included patients diagnosed with histologic hormone receptor–positive HER2−, MammaPrint H1 and H2, and BluePrint Luminal-B cancers.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics summarize demographic, clinical, and pathologic characteristics including age, race, menopausal status, tumor grade, T size, and LN status. Group comparisons were assessed using chi-squared or Fisher's exact tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables, both before and after IPTW. The primary end point of this study was 3-year IDFS, defined in accordance with STEEP 2.0 criteria,23 as the time from diagnosis to invasive local, regional, or distant recurrence, contralateral invasive breast cancer, second primary nonbreast invasive cancer, or death from any cause. After IPTW, IDFS was estimated using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and treatment groups were compared using log-rank tests. HRs and 95% CIs were calculated using Cox proportional hazards models. The proportional hazards assumption was verified for all covariates using Schoenfeld residuals. Univariate models were unadjusted, whereas multivariate models were adjusted for age, T size, LN status, and tumor grade. The primary hypothesis, prespecified in the statistical analysis plan, was that anthracycline benefit differs across MammaPrint-defined High-Risk subgroups (H1 and H2). To test this unified hypothesis, a treatment-by-MammaPrint interaction term was incorporated into the multivariate Cox model and tested via the likelihood ratio test. The H1 and H2 analyses represent planned, complementary components of this single prespecified hypothesis rather than independent statistical tests. As such, formal adjustment for multiple hypothesis testing was not applied. All tests were two-sided with a significance threshold of P < .05. All analyses were performed using R version 4.5.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, NC).

RESULTS

Patient Population and Clinical-Pathologic Characteristics

Among 1,259 patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2−, MammaPrint High-Risk, and BluePrint Luminal-B EBC, 919 received TC and 340 received AC-T. Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1 for the unweighted study population.

In the TC cohort (n = 919), the mean age was 59 years and 20.5% was pre-/perimenopausal. Most participants were White (74.5%), followed by Black (10.9%) and Latin American/Hispanic (4.6%), with smaller proportions of other racial groups. Most patients treated with TC had T1 (65.8%) or T2 (31.3%), and 79% had LN− and 19% LN+ cancers. Tumor grade distribution was 16.3% G1, 60% G2, and 23.5% G3. Most patients treated with TC received aromatase inhibitors (AI) (61.2%) or a selective ER modulator (SERM; 22.5%; Supplemental Table 1). In the AC-T cohort (n = 340), patients were younger (53 years) compared with the TC cohort and 34.4% was pre-/perimenopausal. The AC-T cohort had a higher representation of Black patients (13.8%). Patients treated with AC-T had most commonly T1 (49.4%) or T2 (44.1%) cancers; 54.7% had LN− disease, and 44.1% had node-positive disease. Patients treated with AC-T were more likely to have grade 2 cancers, 58.2%, rather than G1 (13.8%) and G3 (27.1%). Similarly, most patients in the AC-T group received an AI (61.9% in H1 and 58.8% in H2) or a SERM (21.8% and 21.6%, respectively; Appendix Table A1). After IPTW, baseline characteristics were well-balanced between treatment groups, with all standardized mean differences below 0.125 (Table 1, Appendix Fig A1).

MammaPrint High-Risk subgroups were distributed similarly between both TC and AC-T groups (P = .175). This balance was confirmed within MammaPrint indices for each subgroup. Within the H1 cohort, median MammaPrint indices were -0.199 (IQR, -0.331 to -0.088) for AC-T and -0.186 (IQR, -0.341 to -0.088) for TC. Within the H2 cohort, median indices were -0.715 (IQR, -0.785 to -0.645) for AC-T and -0.659 (IQR, -0.727 to -0.614) for TC. These distributions demonstrate balance of MammaPrint index between treatment arms, with strongly overlapping interquartile ranges, confirming covariate balance and that the H1 and H2 categorization did not introduce treatment-related confounding. Among all clinical factors evaluated, only the distribution of grade was significantly different between MammaPrint risk groups, with G3 tumors more frequent among H2 versus H1 (P < .001; Appendix Table A2).

MammaPrint Risk and Anthracycline Benefit

Among patients with H1 EBC, Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed no significant difference in IDFS between patients treated with AC-T versus TC. At a median follow-up of 3 years postdiagnosis, the IDFS was 95.9% (95% CI, 92.9 to 99.0) for patients with H1 cancers treated with AC-T and 95.9% (95% CI, 94.5 to 97.4) for those treated with TC (P = .60; Fig 2A). The 4-year IDFS rates were 91.7% (95% CI, 87.1 to 95.6) for AC-T and 93.6% (95% CI, 91.5 to 95.7) for TC. Cox proportional hazards analysis confirmed no significant association between the chemotherapy regimen and IDFS in the H1 cohort, with an unadjusted HRs of 1.19 (95% CI, 0.66 to 2.13; P = .60) and an adjusted HRs of 1.14 (95% CI, 0.64 to 2.06; P = .70; Fig 2A, Table 2). Age was significantly associated with IDFS (adjusted HRs, 1.03; 95% CI, 1.00 to 1.05; P = .038), whereas tumor size, LN status, and grade were not (Appendix Table A3).

By contrast, among patients with H2 EBC, treatment with AC-T was associated with significantly improved IDFS compared with TC. At 3 years, the IDFS was 100% (95% CI, 100 to 100) for the AC-T group compared with 94.8% (95% CI, 90.4 to 99.5) for the TC group, with an absolute benefit of 5.2% (P = .023). Most (71%) of the IDFS events in H2 treated with TC occurred in the first 3 years. At 4 years, the IDFS was 99.2% (95% CI, 97.5 to 100) with AC-T and 92.6% (95% CI, 86.7 to 99) with TC (Fig 2B). Cox proportional hazards analysis demonstrated significantly reduced IDFS events with AC-T versus TC, with an unadjusted HRs of 0.09 (95% CI, 0.01 to 0.71; P = .023) and an adjusted HRs of 0.10 (95% CI, 0.01 to 0.75; P = .025; Fig 2B, Table 2). In multivariate analysis, age, tumor size, LN status, and grade were not significantly associated with IDFS in the H2 cohort (Appendix Table A4). A treatment-by-MammaPrint interaction showed that AC-T benefit increased with higher genomic risk (P = .036; Table 2).

DISCUSSION

Recent findings from a meta-analysis from the EBCTCG showed decreased risk of recurrence and breast cancer–related death in patients with high-risk EBC treated with anthracycline- and taxane-based therapy compared with taxane-based therapy alone, regardless of nodal and hormone receptor status.1 There are currently no validated predictive biomarkers that can identify which patients benefit from and can safely avoid anthracyclines. This study shows, in an IPTW cohort of patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− BluePrint Luminal-B EBC prospectively enrolled in the FLEX trial, that MammaPrint H2 identifies patients who have a high risk of recurrence within 3 years with TC and who benefit from AC-T. Conversely, patients with MammaPrint H1 EBC, had an excellent prognosis at 3 years regardless of treatment with TC or AC-T.

The observation of a 5.2% risk of recurrence or death within 3 years of diagnosis in patients with H2 Luminal-B EBC treated with TC, compared with zero risk at 3 years with AC-T, underscores that AC-T is the optimal chemotherapy regimen currently available for these patients. Critically, the interaction test was significant (P = .036), demonstrating that MammaPrint H2 is a validated predictor of anthracycline benefit. Although statistical power in the adjusted analyses within the H2 cohort was limited by sample size, the Kaplan-Meier curves demonstrate an early and sustained separation between treatments with H2, with a significant absolute 5.2% difference in 3-year IDFS, suggesting a potential risk-benefit advantage for AC-T. Importantly, patients with H2 tumors treated with AC-T were approximately 90% less likely to experience an IDFS event compared with TC, a substantial risk reduction that remained significant even after adjusting for standard clinicopathologic prognostic factors. Notably, the majority of IDFS events among H2 patients treated with TC occurred early, suggesting that omission of anthracyclines in this biologically aggressive, chemotherapy-sensitive subgroup will leave some patients vulnerable to early recurrence.

These findings are consistent with previous studies reporting higher pCR rates with anthracycline-containing neoadjuvant chemotherapy among patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− H2 tumors.12,24 MammaPrint H2 has distinct biological and molecular characteristics in hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC. An ISPY-2 study demonstrated that IHC-defined TNBC and hormone receptor–positive HER2− H2 cancers share transcriptomic features characterized by high proliferation, enriched immune infiltration, and low ER-associated transcriptional activity, distinct from hormone receptor–positive HER2− H1 cancers.24 Approximately 15% of hormone receptor–positive HER2−, high-risk tumors are genomically Basal-type, which may account for the heightened immune activity observed in the overall H2 population. An immune deconvolution analysis comparing MammaPrint High-Risk cancers in FLEX (N = 2,916) indicated greater abundance of cells associated with antigen presentation and adaptive immunity within H2 cancers overall compared with H1, within pronounced immune activation in Basal versus Luminal-Type H2 tumors. Together, these data may elucidate why H2 cancers may be preferentially sensitive to anthracyclines and warrants investigation of immunotherapy benefit in patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2−, Basal-Type tumors.

By contrast, patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− MammaPrint H1 cancers had no improvement in IDFS with AC-T compared with TC in the IPTW analysis. Although age was associated with IDFS (P = .038), the regimen was not significantly associated with improved outcomes after adjusting for clinical factors. These results suggest that AC-T may potentially be safely omitted in patients with H1 tumors, regardless of clinical risk features. Importantly, clinicopathologic prognostic factors did not predict for anthracycline benefit. A significant proportion of H1 exhibited high-risk clinical features including T2-T3 (36.5%), LN+ (25.5%), and G3 disease (20.2%), yet patients with H1 cancers had comparable outcomes with AC-T and TC. Conversely, over 50% of patients with H2 tumors had T1 and nearly 60% had LN− disease, yet patients with H2 cancers derived significantly greater benefit from AC-T over TC. Our analyses showed that nodal involvement was not a significant prognostic factor for IDFS, nor was node positivity a significant predictor of anthracycline benefit in either the H1 or H2. This observation underscores that, beyond nodal status being a key prognostic factor, breast cancer biology (H1 v H2) is a primary driver of therapeutic benefit and prognosis. Similar observations of no significant prognostic impact of age, tumor size, and grade on IDFS also underscore that high-risk clinicopathologic features do not predict benefit from AC-T. The patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC in the ABC trials, PLAN B, MASTER, and 07-READ trials did not benefit from the addition of anthracycline to taxane-based therapy, despite having high-risk clinical features.3-6 This is likely because H2 cancers constitute approximately 15% of hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBCs, and any benefit from anthracyclines may be obscured in analyses of populations with predominantly MammaPrint LR and H1 cancers.

These findings suggest a strategy of extending the role of genomic classifiers such as MammaPrint to not only identify patients who need chemotherapy but also to predict regimen benefit, allowing physicians to better individualize treatment recommendations. Similar to this analysis with MammaPrint, a nonrandomized analysis from TAILORx indicated that a higher recurrence score (RS) of ≥ 31 was associated with improved distant recurrence-free interval when patients were treated with AC-T compared with TC, with no benefit from anthracycline observed in the RS 11-30 group. Although this analysis was limited to patients with node-negative disease, these findings further support using genomic assays to guide regimen recommendations.25 We plan to further validate the FLEX Study findings presented here via a retrospective analysis of patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− from the randomized ABC trials, providing long-term data to confirm the differential anthracycline benefit based on MammaPrint H1 versus H2. In addition, future analyses evaluating MammaPrint as a continuous variable may be warranted.

Strengths of this study include the use of a large, prospectively collected registry, genomic stratification to identify genomically high-risk and Luminal-B tumors, and rigorous propensity score matching to mitigate treatment selection bias. Limitations include the observational, nonrandomized design with potential residual confounding despite IPTW and the relatively short median follow-up of 3.2 years for hormone receptor–positive HER2− EBC. CDK4/6 inhibitor treatment data were not captured as most patients were treated before FDA approval of adjuvant abemaciclib in September 2021. In conclusion, this analysis of the FLEX study showed that patients with hormone receptor–positive HER2− MammaPrint H2, genomically Luminal-B EBC had significantly superior IDFS when treated with AC-T versus TC. By contrast, patients with MammaPrint H1 cancers did not benefit from the addition of anthracycline. These observations provide further evidence of the utility of MammaPrint in informing therapy selection in EBC.
