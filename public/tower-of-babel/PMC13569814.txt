Steroidogenesis is associated with bladder cancer progression via reprogramming the metabolic landscape of the tumor immune microenvironment
Authors: Lei Huang, Huachi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBladder cancer represents a common malignant neoplasm of the genitourinary system, featuring an elevated recurrence rate and unfavorable prognosis, whereas the intrinsic mechanisms driving its malignant progression have not yet been fully clarified. In this study, we integrated single-cell transcriptome data from GSE222315 (5 bladder cancer samples and 4 adjacent normal samples) with bulk transcriptome data from the TCGA-BLCA cohort (409 tumor samples and 19 control samples) to investigate the impact of steroid metabolism on bladder cancer progression and the tumor stemness. Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis showed that steroid metabolism pathway genes were up-regulated in cancer cells. Also, the eminent steroid metabolism group exhibited stronger intensity and a greater number of intercellular interactions relative to the cohort characterized by diminished steroid metabolic capacity; additionally, tumor cells with high steroid metabolism showed elevated expression of stemness-related genes and diminished immune activity. Validation using bulk transcriptome data demonstrated that the steroid metabolism pathway was the most significantly enriched metabolic pathway in tumor tissues, and its core upregulated genes were negatively correlated with immune cell infiltration. Built upon differentially expressed genes including ACTC1 and CST1, the developed prognostic model showed superior predictive capability, with AUC scores over 0.77. Meanwhile, genes such as C7, DES, MYH11, and OGN were determined to be associated with unfavorable prognostic profiles in patients. And high-risk patients with poor prognosis outcomes in the survival model highly expressed steroid related genes like DHCR and SQLE. In conclusion, steroid metabolic reprogramming can reshape the TIME and is associated with bladder cancer progression, and reversing aberrant steroid metabolism has the potential to serve as a novel therapeutic regimen for bladder cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s12672-026-05445-8.Keywords: Bladder cancer, Steroid metabolism, Single-cell RNA sequencing, Tumor stemness, Prognostic model

Introduction

Bladder cancer is a malignant tumor ranking among the top in terms of incidence and mortality in the genitourinary system, characterized by the clinical features of high recurrence rate, strong invasiveness and extremely poor prognosis [1]. According to GLOBOCAN 2020 statistics, there were 573,000 newly diagnosed cases of bladder cancer (BC) and 213,000 cancer-related deaths reported globally in that year [2]. Moreover, the high rate of chemotherapy resistance has rendered the prevention and treatment of this disease a formidable challenge [3]. Looking ahead, surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, and hormonal therapy will remain cornerstones of cancer treatment [4]. Meanwhile, targeted therapies, antibody-drug conjugates, immune checkpoint inhibitors, bispecific T-cell engagers, oncolytic virus therapy, and CAR-T therapy will continue to advance and play increasingly important roles [4]. For a long time, research on the mechanisms underlying the malignant progression of bladder cancer has mostly focused on genetic mutations and epigenetic regulation. However, as a core malignant characteristic of tumor cells, metabolic reprogramming has not been fully elucidated in terms of its functions and regulatory networks in bladder cancer [5–7].

Metabolic reprogramming can support cancer cell proliferation/invasion through reshaping the patterns of energy metabolism and material synthesis, while simultaneously mediating the immunosuppressive remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment [8, 9]. Among numerous metabolic pathways, the tumor-regulatory role of steroid metabolism has been verified in breast cancer and other cancer types [10, 11]. For instance, immune cells within the tumor microenvironment (TME) can suppress CD8 + T cell activity via de novo synthesis of steroid hormones, and targeting key steroidogenic enzymes can reverse immunosuppression and inhibit tumor growth [11, 12]. However, in the field of bladder cancer, systematic investigations into the expression profiles, cellular localization, and regulatory effects of steroid metabolism on the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) are still lacking, while its potential to serve as a prognostic biomarker and candidate therapeutic target has not yet been investigated.

To systematically clarify the functional contributions and regulatory mechanisms of steroid metabolism in bladder cancer, we resolved GSE222315 scRNA-seq data(5 bladder cancer samples and 4 adjacent normal samples) and TCGA-BLCA cohort bulk transcriptome data (409 tumor samples and 19 control samples), thereby achieving multi-dimensional insights across single-cell and tissue levels. Our initial single-cell subpopulation annotation and signature scoring identified a marked upregulation of steroid metabolism-related genes (CEL, CYP24A1, DHCR24, and others) in bladder cancer cells, along with significantly higher steroid metabolic pathway activity in tumor cells relative to adjacent normal tissues and other cell subpopulations—key evidence supporting the presence of steroid metabolic reprogramming in bladder cancer. Further cell-cell communication analysis confirmed that the high steroid metabolism group exhibited greater numbers of intercellular interaction events (1534) and stronger interaction intensity than the low group; moreover, the interaction pathways between fibroblasts and tumor cells were more numerous in the high group (88 pathways) relative to the low group (72 pathways), demonstrating that enhanced steroid metabolism facilitates intercellular communication within the tumor microenvironment.

In terms of functional phenotypes, tumor cells with high steroid metabolic activity showed a marked upregulation of stemness-related genes (including MYC, CD44, BMI1), while the activity of immune-associated pathways (NF-κB, T-cell receptor pathway, etc.) was dysregulated, leading to a diminished immune activity phenotype. Analysis of bulk transcriptome data validated that the steroid metabolism pathway ranked as the top-enriched metabolic pathway in bladder cancer. Moreover, its key upregulated genes (HSD17B7, TM7SF2, DHCR7, etc.) showed a significant negative correlation with the infiltration of immune cells, indicating the immune-suppressive function of steroid metabolism within the tumor immune microenvironment [4]. Besides, the prognostic model established on the basis of steroid-related genes (ACTC1, CST1, OTX1, etc.) exhibited outstanding predictive capability with AUC scores over 0.75. Meanwhile, genes such as C7, DES, and MYH11 were found to be closely linked to unfavorable patient prognosis, which validated the prognostic significance of steroid metabolism-related genes. Overall, this study elucidates that steroid metabolic reprogramming is associated with bladder cancer progression and modulates the tumor immune microenvironment and serves as candidate targets for metabolic targeted treatment and prognostic stratification of bladder cancer.

Methods

Data acquisition and quality control

Single-cell transcriptome data were obtained from GSE222315 [13], which included 5 bladder cancer samples (GSM6919778, GSM6919780, GSM6919782, GSM6919784 and GSM6919786) and 4 adjacent normal control samples (GSM6919779, GSM6919781, GSM6919783 and GSM6919785). We excluded cells with fewer than 200 expressed genes, and finally got a total of 76,104 single-cell transcriptome samples. Batch effects were mitigated using Harmony [14], while PCA was utilized for initial dimensionality reduction then clustering of the data. RNA-seq datasets were acquired from the UCSC Xena GDC TCGA Bladder Cancer (BLCA) STAR – Counts database (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/), comprising 409 bladder cancer samples and 19 control samples, and their clinical data were integrated for follow-up analyses. Meanwhile, 10 control and 165 tumor samples from the GSE13507 dataset were used as an external cohort to validate the expression levels of Steroidogenesis homologous genes [15]. Cancer transcriptomic analysis must avoid equating high gene expression with tumorigenic causality and account for biases inherent in bulk sequencing [16, 17].

Cell subpopulation annotation

Cell subpopulations were classified based on the gene expression features of various cell subtypes, and each cell type was matched with specific marker genes: EPCAM for epithelial cells, CD3D/CD3E for CD4 + and CD8 + T cells, GNLY/NKG7 for natural killer cells, MS4A1/CD79A/CD19 for B cells and Plasma cells, CD68/CD1D for macrophages/monocytes, S100A8/S100A9 for neutrophils/myeloid cells, TPSAB1/TPSB2 for mast cells and so on. In addition, we used t-SNE/UMAP for dimensionality reduction and visualization analysis, and separately displayed the highly expressed genes of each subpopulation. The median metabolic score across all cells was − 0.04360693, with a range from − 0.2427112 to 0.6512839. All cells were classified into high-metabolic and low-metabolic subgroups based on this median cutoff.For tumor cells alone, the median score was 0.01769576, ranging from − 0.2104585 to 0.6512839, and tumor cells were similarly divided into high-metabolic and low-metabolic subgroups using the corresponding median value.

Cell-cell communication analysis

Cells were divided into high and low metabolism groups on the basis of their steroid metabolism scores, with cell-cell communication analysis performed individually for each group. We used the createCellChat function to construct CellChat objects, the identifyOverExpressedGenes function to identify differentially expressed genes, and the computeCommunProb function to assess cell-cell communication. For visualization, we applied the ggplot2 package along with the netVisual_circle and netAnalysis_signalingRole_heatmap functions to display the relevant results.

Pathway enrichment analysis and immune-related pathway scoring

The FindAllMarkers function was adopted to identify highly expressed genes across different cell subpopulations, and clusterProfiler was utilized to perform pathway enrichment analysis and corresponding visualization. The AddModuleScore function was utilized to calculate immune-related scores for each cell subpopulation. To assess the activation extent of core immune-associated signaling pathways in distinct cell subpopulations, this study adopted the signature gene sets of six major immune-related pathways. Specifically speaking, the NF-κB signaling pathway comprises genes including IGH, TAB1, and TNFSF13B; the The C-type lectin receptor signaling cascade genes including AKT3, CLEC4M, and IRF9; the T-cell receptor signaling pathway comprises genes including AKT3, RASGRP1, and CDK4; the B-cell receptor signaling pathway includes genes such as AKT3, IGH, and LILRB2; the cell adhesion molecule interaction pathway includes genes such as CDH2, CDH3, and CLDN24; and the NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity pathway includes genes such as KIR2DS2, MICA, and KLRC4-KLRK1.

RNA-seq differential analysis and visualization

The Limma package was used to perform differential expression analysis on the TCGA-BLCA data [18]. Enrichment analysis and visualization were then conducted for the genes identified in the differential analysis. Meanwhile, the pheatmap and ggplot2 packages were employed to visualize the expression profiles of the core gene set involved in the steroid metabolism pathway, which included CEL, EBP, CYP2R1, CYP24A1, CYP27B1, CYP51A1, DHCR7, DHCR24, FDFT1, LBR, LIPA, LSS, NSDHL, HSD17B7, MSMO1, SC5D, SOAT1, SQLE, TM7SF2, and SOAT2 [19].

Immune infiltration analysis

TIMER2.0 [20] is an online analysis platform that integrates multi-omics data of the tumor immune microenvironment. In this study, TIMER2.0 was used to conduct correlation analysis between the expression of target genes (HSD17B7, TM7SF2, CYP27B1, EBP, DHCR24, SQLE, DHCR7) and immune infiltration in bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA). The analysis was performed via the TIMER2.0 web server (available at https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/), where the Gene module and BLCA cancer type were selected to analyze the immune infiltration profiles associated with the seven aforementioned genes.

Survival analysis and prognostic model construction

Relying on steroid-related genes, we built a prognostic model to explore whether these genes have prognostic value for bladder cancer. The stringr and dplyr R packages served to sort through the clinical information of patients, while the survival and survminer packages were employed to carry out survival analysis in BLCA. The My.stepwise.coxph function was used to build the survival model with a stepwise regression model, and the ggforest and ggplot functions were utilized for visualization of the results.

Quantitative PCR

SV‑HUC‑1 and T24 cell lines were used. Total RNA was isolated using the FastPure Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation Kit V2 (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) [21]. cDNA was synthesized with Bimake All‑in‑One cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (Bimake, Houston, USA), and qPCR was performed using Bimake 2× SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix [21]. Relative expression was calculated by the 2 − ΔΔCT method and normalized to GAPDH. The sequences of primers used in this study are shown in Table S1.

Results

Single-cell sequencing analysis reveals significant upregulation of steroid metabolism-related genes in bladder cancer

A total of 9 cell subpopulations were identified via single-cell subpopulation annotation, including T cells, NK cells, B cells, plasma cells, myeloid cells, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, cancer cells, and cycling cells (Fig. 1A). The expression profiles of high expressed genes corresponding to each cell type are depicted in Fig. 1B. Cell composition analysis indicated that tumor samples showed markedly higher proportions of cancer cells and cycling cells than normal adjacent tissue (NAT) samples, whereas the proportions of lymphocytes were notably higher in NAT samples than in tumor samples (Fig. 1C). Analysis of steroid metabolism pathway-related genes revealed that most of these genes were significantly highly expressed in cancer cells, such as CEL, CYP24A1, DHCR24, FDFT1, and MSMO1 (Fig. 1D). Scoring of each cell type using the steroid gene set showed that cancer cells had the highest score for the steroid metabolism pathway (Fig. 1E). Meanwhile, the steroid gene set score within tumor tissues demonstrated a substantially higher abundance than that in NAT samples (Fig. 1F). It can also be seen that the groups with higher steroid metabolism scores are clustered in the tumor and cancer cells (Fig. 1G). Then, all cells were divided into high and low groups based on their steroid metabolism scores, and dimensionality reduction clustering visualization was performed (Fig. 1H). The fraction of cancer cells in the group with high steroid metabolic capacity was substantially higher than that in the group with low steroid metabolic capacity (Fig. 1I). In summary, steroid-related genes exhibited an upregulated expression trend in bladder cancer, suggesting that steroid metabolic reprogramming and enhanced metabolism may exist in bladder cancer.

Enhanced intercellular interactions in the group with high steroid metabolism

For the purpose of further investigating differences in cell-cell interactions within the tumor microenvironment of the high versus low steroid metabolism groups, we performed relevant communication analysis. The results indicated that the high group had more intercellular interactions (1534) than the low group (1339). Meanwhile, both the intensity and probability of intercellular interactions within the high steroid metabolism subgroup demonstrated higher values compared with those in the low subgroup (Fig. 2A-C). It can be observed that the interaction lines in the high group are denser and have higher intensity values, while the interaction network in the low group is relatively sparse—this directly reflects that intercellular interactions in the high group are tighter (Fig. 2D&E). For example, there were 88 interaction pathways between fibroblasts and cancer cells among the high metabolism group, compared with just 72 pathways present in the low metabolism group (Fig. 2D). This suggests that the interactions between fibroblasts and tumor cells are stronger in the high steroid metabolism group, thereby more readily promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), invasion, and metastasis of tumors. Also, vascular growth factors such as EGF and VEGF were more highly upregulated in cancer cells of higher metabolism group (Fig. 2F). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the high steroid metabolism group exhibits enhanced intercellular interactions, with stronger crosstalk between fibroblasts and tumor cells and upregulated vascular growth factors (EGF and VEGF), thus potentially facilitating tumor epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), invasion, and metastasis.

Higher stemness and reduced immune adhesion in tumor cells with high steroid metabolism

On account of the significant enrichment of cancer cells in the high steroid metabolism group, we aimed to investigate the cellular traits of these cells by dividing cancer cells into two subgroups based on varying steroid metabolism levels, followed by analysis and visualization (Fig. 3A). The subgroup with high steroid metabolism exhibited elevated expression of stemness-related genes, including MYC, CD44, BMI1, FN1, SNAI2, and TWIST1 (Fig. 3B). To clarify the functional differences between cancer cell subgroups with distinct steroid metabolism levels, we identified DEGs between the two subgroups and conducted pathway enrichment analysis on these DEGs. The results showed that the high steroid metabolism subgroup was enriched in functional terms related to cell proliferation and intercellular interactions, such as “ribosome assembly”, “cell adhesion junction”, and “cadherin binding” (Fig. 3C). In addition, signaling pathway activity analysis of immune-related gene sets revealed that the activation levels of immune-associated signaling pathways (including NF-κB and T-cell receptor) were lower in the high steroid metabolism subgroup (Fig. 3D). In summary, the tumor cell subgroup with high steroid metabolism shows more prominent features of higher tumor stemness and cell proliferation activity, along with weaker immune activity. These characteristics may indicate poor prognostic progression in patients.

Steroid-related genes are markedly upregulated in tumors and associated with tumor malignant progression pathways in bulk transcriptome data

To investigate the expression level of the Steroid biosynthesis pathway (hsa00100) in a large cohort of bladder cancer (BLCA) samples, we analyzed the expression levels of 20 pathway-related genes across over 400 BLCA samples. Among these genes CYP27B1, SQLE, EBP, DHCR7, DHCR24, MSMO1, and HSD17B7 are up-regulated; Only SOAT2 was down-regulated in tumor cells (Fig. 4A&B). Enrichment analysis of highly expressed genes in BLCA identified several pathways associated with DNA damage repair and ribosomal function, suggesting that these genes may be involved in oncogene amplification and the malignant progression of tumors (Fig. 4C). Enrichment analysis of all differentially expressed genes showed that among all metabolic pathways, the steroid metabolism pathway had the highest enrichment score (Fig. 4D). In conclusion, large-scale tumor transcriptome data further confirmed the existence of steroid metabolic reprogramming in bladder cancer, with significantly elevated metabolic levels in tumor patients.

Upregulated steroid pathway genes in tumors are negatively correlated with immune infiltration levels in the tumor microenvironment

Further immune infiltration analysis was conducted on seven up-regulated steroid-related genes (Fig. 5A&B). These genes also showed a trend of high expression in the external cohort (GSE13507), among which LIPA was downregulated in tumors, whereas four genes, DHCR24, DHCR7, MSMO1 and SQLE, were upregulated in tumors (Fig. 5C). Consistently, qPCR assays verified the upregulated expression of DHCR24 and DHCR7 in bladder cancer cell lines (Fig. 5D). There was a negative correlation between CYP27B1 and B cell infiltration levels, whereas neutrophil and dendritic cell infiltration levels were positively correlated with CYP27B1 (Fig. 5E). No significant association was observed between EBP and immune infiltration in bladder cancer (Fig. 5F). As a key enzyme mediating cholesterol and sex steroid biosynthesis, HSD17B7 demonstrated a negative association with the levels of CD8 + T cell and B cell infiltration (Fig. 5G). CD8 + T cells’ reduced infiltration is closely linked to impaired immune surveillance and poor clinical outcomes in BLCA [22], indicating that HSD17B7 up-regulation may weaken anti-tumor immune responses by limiting CD8 + T and B cell recruitment. DHCR7-a recently identified modulator of BLCA chemoresistance and immunotherapy efficacy [23]-exhibited negative correlations with all immune cell types (Fig. 5H). SQLE showed no obvious correlation with immune infiltration in the bladder cancer tumor microenvironment (Fig. 5I). TM7SF2 showed a notable negative relationship correlating with B cells, CD4 + T cells, neutrophils, CD8 + T cells, and dendritic cells (Fig. 5J), indicating that TM7SF2 has a strong correlation with poor immune cell infiltration in bladder cancer. Meanwhile, DHCR24 was inversely correlated with CD4 + T cell infiltration extent and positively associated with B cell infiltration extent (Fig. 5K). In brief, steroid-related genes are significantly linked to insufficient immune infiltration in bladder cancer.

A prognostic model constructed with steroid-related genes can effectively predict patient prognosis

Based on our preceding findings that steroid metabolism-related genes are closely tied to aggressive bladder cancer (BLCA) traits—including enhanced tumor cell stemness, strengthened intercellular crosstalk, impaired immune infiltration, and activation of malignant progression pathways, we next sought to translate these mechanistic insights into clinically applicable prognostic tools. A survival prognostic model for bladder cancer patients was constructed using multivariate Cox regression analysis based on highly differentially expressed genes, aiming to ensure prognostic prediction performance and better clinical discrimination. The results showed that the prognostic model composed of ACTC1, CST1, OTX1, C7, and PI16 could effectively predict patient outcomes (Fig. 6A&B). The predictive performance of the model was confirmed to be favorable via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, showing AUC values of 0.77 for 1-year survival, 0.82 for 3-year survival, and 0.83 for 5-year survival (Fig. 6C). After Bootstrap correction, the AUC values decreased to 0.63, 0.67 and 0.66, all of which were above 0.5, suggesting that the model possessed prognostic predictive ability (Fig. 6C). All tumor patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups according to their risk scores (Fig. 6D), and patient outcomes showed a uniform distribution across the risk score range (Fig. 6E). Higher expression levels of steroid-related genes were observed in the high-risk group (Fig. 6F). Survival analysis of individual genes revealed that C7, DES, KRT14, ATP1A2, MYH11, OGN, and ACTC1 were associated with poor patient prognosis. SIM2 was associated with favorable patient prognosis, but this correlation was not statistically significant due to overlapping confidence intervals (Fig. 6G&H). To exclude the influence of other factors, known prognostic variables including age, gender, N stage, and metastasis status (M) were adjusted for. The eight aforementioned genes were separately fitted into multivariate Cox models together with these clinical factors to evaluate their independent effects on patient prognosis. The results were highly consistent with those of survival analysis. The mean hazard ratios (HR) of C7, DES, KRT14, ATP1A2, MYH11, OGN, and ACTC1 were 1.14, 1.17, 1.18, 1.04, 1.41, 1.12, and 1.15, respectively, all greater than 1, indicating that these genes serve as risk factors for the tumor (Fig S1 A–F & H). Meanwhile, the mean HR of SIM2 was 0.98, suggesting that it may act as a protective factor (Fig S1G). Meanwhile, in patients with high risk and poorer prognosis, the overall expression levels of genes in the steroid metabolism pathway were higher than those in the low-risk group. Among them, DHCR7, SQLE, LSS, and NSDHL were significantly highly expressed in the high-risk group, while no significant differences were observed in the other genes (Fig S1H). In summary, genes in the steroid-related pathway are associated with patient prognosis and can be an effective cancer’s biomarkers.

Discussion

In this investigation, we identified that genes involved in steroid metabolism (CEL, CYP24A1, DHCR7 and DHCR24) exhibited high expression in bladder cancer cells, and the activity level of the steroid metabolism pathway in tumor cells was higher compared with that in adjacent tissues (NATs) and other cell subpopulations. This finding suggests that bladder cancer exhibits typical features of steroid metabolic reprogramming, which is consistent with the common malignant phenotype of metabolic reprogramming in tumors [24–26]. Previous studies have confirmed that starvation-induced autophagy can mediate glycolytic metabolic reprogramming by upregulating LDHA, thereby promoting the proliferation and invasion of bladder cancer [27–29]. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) can secrete CXCL14, which can also activate the STAT3 signaling pathway to simultaneously enhance DNA repair capacity and glycolysis levels, thereby forming a metabolic vicious cycle that leads to chemoresistance [30, 31]. Recent studies have demonstrated that AK4, CALU, LINC01271, and RUSC1-AS1 as key CD4 + T cell-related genes with distinct expression signatures across diverse human diseases [32]. In the present research, we found that cancer cells with high steroid metabolism exhibited increased expression of stemness-related genes (MYC, CD44 and BMI1), concomitant with marked increases in the number and intensity of intercellular communication events, as well as enrichment of interaction pathways between fibroblasts and tumor cells. These findings suggest that steroid metabolism may provide impetus for bladder cancer invasion and metastasis by enhancing tumor stemness and intercellular coordination.

At the molecular mechanism level, the regulatory role of steroid metabolism in tumor progression has been verified in many cancer types [33]. In triple-negative breast cancer, immune cells such as macrophages and mast cells within the tumor microenvironment can activate the glucocorticoid signaling pathway through de novo synthesis of steroid hormones, thereby inhibiting dendritic cell maturation and CD8 + T cell activity [34–36]. Moreover, inhibitors targeting CYP11A1, a key enzyme in steroid synthesis, can reverse immunosuppression and inhibit tumor growth [37–39]. In this research, we further ascertained that the high steroid metabolism subgroup of bladder cancer exhibited prominently aberrant activity of immune-associated pathways. This finding is consistent with the immunosuppressive properties of steroid hormones—steroid hormones can induce M2 polarization of macrophages and restrain the effector functions of T cells to induce an immunosuppressive microenvironment [40–42], thereby forming a metabolic reprogramming-immunosuppression-malignant progression regulatory axis in bladder cancer. Recently, an increasing number of integrative analyses have aimed to uncover the associations among metabolic reprogramming, immune regulation, and molecular determinants across diverse cancers, emphasizing that integrating multidimensional data can deepen the understanding of cancer biology and optimize personalized therapeutic strategies to improve patient prognosis [43]. Other studies have suggested that in-depth exploration of immune targets and pathways will promote the development of cancer immunoprecision medicine and effectively improve therapeutic outcomes [44]. Together, these reports highlight the complexity of multilayered regulatory networks in cancer. Meanwhile, immunotherapy has gained increasing attention, with research identifying multiple cell subtypes associated with poor prognosis in nasopharyngeal carcinoma and elucidating key immune inhibitory intercellular interactions within the tumor microenvironment, thereby providing important clinical implications for the future treatment of NPC [45].

Furthermore, the high-risk patient cohort displayed increased expression of steroid-related genes and inferior prognostic performance, which validated the independent prognostic value of genes linked to steroid metabolism. Recently, the construction of prognostic models for bladder cancer has mostly focused on fields such as ferroptosis [46] and immunogenic cell death [47, 48]. In contrast, this study correlates the steroid pathway from the metabolic dimension with prognosis. Furthermore, the model established herein integrates both metabolic activity and immune characteristics, conferring more comprehensive predictive efficacy and clinical applicability, and thus providing a novel marker combination for individualized prognostic evaluation of bladder cancer.

The present study is not without limitations. To begin with, all the research data were sourced from public databases. Although the reliability of the results was guaranteed through multi-cohort validation, there is a lack of in situ verification with clinical samples and in vitro/in vivo functional experiments, which prevents direct confirmation of the pro-cancer functions of key steroid metabolism-related genes. Second, the synergistic regulatory mechanisms between steroid metabolism and other metabolic pathways (glycolysis, fatty acid metabolism) were not explored in depth, leading to insufficient resolution of the interactive network of multiple metabolic pathways. Third, the prognostic model only completed bioinformatics validation and has not yet been applied in prospective clinical cohort translation; its clinical practical value needs to be further confirmed. Last but not least, intercellular communication predicted by CellChat was only computational inference rather than functional validation, so the conclusion linking it to EMT and invasion remains preliminary. Future studies can perform perturbation experiments by inhibiting key ligand–receptor pairs and evaluating cell migration and invasion. Also, the correlation between steroid metabolism and stemness gene expression was only observed at the transcriptional level, without confirming a causal relationship. Further studies using drug inhibition or siRNA knockdown combined with stemness assays are needed to verify direct regulation, which will also help validate the druggable potential of steroid metabolic pathways.
