Matsuki duality for loop groups
Tsao-Hsien Chen and Lingfei Yi

Abstract
. We establish versions of Matsuki duality for loop groups. The main result is a bijection between symmetric loop group orbits and real polynomial loop group orbits on the affine Grassmannians or affine flag varieties. Along the way we obtain orbit parametrizations and make connections with vector bundles on real and twistor- ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} and Kottwitz sets .

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a complex connected reductive group.
Let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be a real from of
G
and let
K
be a symmetric subgroup of
G
such that
K_{\mathbb{R}}=G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap K

is a maximal compact subgroup of both
G_{\mathbb{R}}
and
K
.
The celebrated Matsuki duality for
G
is a bijection

K\backslash\mathcal{B}\longleftrightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\mathcal{B}

between

K
-orbits and
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-orbits on the flag variety
\mathcal{B}
of
G
with remarkable properties
[
14
]
.
In this paper we establish a version of Matsuki duality for loop groups.
The
main result is a bijection

K(\!(t)\!)\backslash\mathcal{F}\ell\longleftrightarrow LG_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\mathcal{F}\ell

between

K(\!(t)\!)
-orbits and

LG_{\mathbb{R}}
-orbits on the affine flag variety
\mathcal{F}\ell
with several remarkable properties. Here

K(\!(t)\!)
is the loop group of
K
and
LG_{\mathbb{R}}
is the polynomial loop group of
G_{\mathbb{R}}

consisting of maps that
take the unit circle
S^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
to
G_{\mathbb{R}}\subset G
.
We also establish a version of Matsuki duality for
twisted loop groups orbits
and make connections with vector bundles on the twistor-
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and Kottwitz sets
B(G_{\mathbb{R}})
for
G_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Our approach is entirely combinatorial and
is inspired by
the work
[
16
]
of Nadler on Matsuki duality for affine Grassmannians and
our previous work
[
5
]
on singularities of
K(\!(t)\!)
-orbit closures on
\mathcal{F}\ell
.
The method of proof
treats simultaneously all
the real forms that are in the same pure inner class.
Along the way, we obtain parametrizations for both

K(\!(t)\!)
and
LG_{\mathbb{R}}
-orbits
and the equivariant local systems on them.
In a forthcoming work
[
3
]
, we will provide a
Morse-theoretic interpretation and refinement
of the Matuski duality and use it to
upgrade the Matuski duality to an equivalence of equivariant derived categories, extending the works
[
15
]
and
[
4
]
in the setting of
flag varieties and affine Grassmannians.
Such an equivalence is a key ingredient to relate geometric Langlands for real groups and relative Langlands duality, see
[
6
,
4
]
.

We now describe the paper in more details.

1.1.
Main results

1.1.1.

We fix
\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}
.
Let
F=\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
and
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
be the formal Laurent series and Taylor series rings.
Let

and

be the formal loop group and formal arc group of

Let
G[t,t^{-1}]\subset G(F)

be the polynomial loop group and

G[t]\subset G(\mathcal{O})
,
G[t^{-1}]\subset G(F)

be the polynomial arc group and the negative polynomial arc group.
Let

and we denote by

Let

Let
\eta_{0}
be a conjugation of

such that
G_{\mathbb{R}}=G^{\eta_{0}}

and
\theta_{0}
be a complex Cartan involution on

such that

.
Let
G_{c}=G^{\eta_{0}\circ\theta_{0}}=G^{\theta_{0}\circ\eta_{0}}
be a compact real from of
G

be a maximal torus.
Consider the twisted involution on
G(F)
:

(1)

\theta(\gamma)(t)=\theta_{0}(\gamma(\epsilon t)).

We denote by

the
\theta
fixed points subgroup
of
G(F)

and the
\theta
anti-fixed points set by

G(F)^{\operatorname{inv}\circ\theta}=\{\gamma\in G(F)|\theta(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}\}.

The loop group

acts on
G(F)^{\operatorname{inv}\circ\theta}
by the
\theta
-conjugation action

h\cdot_{\theta}\gamma=h\gamma\theta(h)^{-1}

Consider the twisted involution on
G[t,t^{-1}]

(2)

\eta(\gamma)(t)=\eta_{0}(\gamma(\epsilon\bar{t}^{-1})).

Denote by

the
\eta
fixed point subgroup of
G[t,t^{-1}]

and the
\eta

G[t,t^{-1}]^{\operatorname{inv}\circ\eta}=\{\gamma\in G[t,t^{-1}]|\eta(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}\}.

Then

is stable under
the
\eta
-conjugation action on
G[t,t^{-1}]
by

h\cdot_{\eta}\gamma=g\gamma\eta(\gamma)^{-1}
.
Note that in the un-twisted setting, that is, when
\epsilon=1
, we have

G(F)^{\theta}=K(F)
and
G[t,t^{-1}]^{\eta}=LG_{\mathbb{R}}

consisting of maps

\gamma:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G

that take the unit circle
S^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
to
G_{\mathbb{R}}\subset G
.

3.
Spherical orbits parametrization

In this section we classify spherical orbits on anti-fixed points by twisted conjugation, for
\theta
and
\eta

The Matsuki correspondence will follow from comparing the two classifications.
We also study orbit intersections and the induced correspondence
on the affine Grassmannians.

3.1.
Complex spherical orbits

3.1.1.

By Cartan decomposition, we have

G(F)^{\mathrm{inv}\circ\theta}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in X_{*}(T)^{+}}(G(\mathcal{O})t^{\lambda}G(\mathcal{O}))^{\mathrm{inv}\circ\theta}.

and

We assume

Then we have

(5)

\lambda=-w_{1}^{-1}\theta_{0}(\lambda).

Note that when
\theta_{0}
is quasi-split,

Define parabolic subgroup
P_{\lambda}
,
its Levi

, and its unipotent radical

associated to
\lambda
,
with Lie algebras
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda},\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda},\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda}
.
Let
P_{\lambda}^{-}
be the opposite parabolic,

.
Then
\mathrm{Ad}_{w_{1}^{-1}}\theta_{0}

,

,

For such

3.1.2.

Let subspace
A_{\lambda,0}\subset A_{\lambda}

(7)

A_{\lambda,0}=\{g_{0}\in L_{\lambda}\mid g_{0}=w_{2}\mathrm{Ad}_{w_{1}^{-1}}\theta_{0}(g_{0})^{-1}\epsilon^{\lambda}\}.

3.4.
Matsuki duality for affine Grassmannians

For

,

-orbits and

-orbits

such that then intersection of corresponding orbits

-orbits
on

To this end,

3.4.1.
Image of

It is known that for

However, when

Proposition 25
.

The map

which induces an isomorphism

Proof.

The
G(F)
-orbits
on
G(F)^{\operatorname{inv}\circ\theta}

are in bijection with the group cohomology

where the generator
-1
acts by
\theta
.
Note that there is an identification

H^{1}(\mu_{2},G(F))\cong H^{1}(\operatorname{Gal}(F/F^{\prime}),H_{F^{\prime}}(F))

where
F^{\prime}=k((t^{2}))
and
the right hand side is the Galois cohomology of a

F/F^{\prime}
-form
H_{F^{\prime}}
of

G_{F^{\prime}}
such that
H_{F^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})=G_{\theta}(F)

Since the field

H^{1}(\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\prime}),H_{F^{\prime}}(F_{s}))=0
(where

is a separable closure of

The desired claim follows from the injection

0\to H^{1}(\operatorname{Gal}(F/F^{\prime}),H_{F^{\prime}}(F))\to H^{1}(\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\prime}),H_{F^{\prime}}(F_{s}))
.
∎

3.4.2.
Image of
\tau_{\eta}

Recall the based compact loop group

\Omega G_{c}=\{\gamma(t)\in G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}\mid\gamma(1)=1\}
.

It is well known that the inclusion induces natural homeomorphism

\Omega G_{c}\simeq\mathrm{Gr}
,
so that
G(F)=\Omega G_{c}\cdot G(\mathcal{O})
is a unique factorization.
Clearly
G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}=\Omega G_{c}\cdot G_{c}
.
Denote
G(\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\exp(\mathfrak{p})G(\mathcal{O})_{1}
.
By Cartan decomposition
G=G_{c}\cdot\exp(\mathfrak{p})
,
we have decomposition of space

(17)

G(F)=G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}\cdot G(\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{p}},

i.e. every element of
g\in G(F)

can be written in a unique way as a product

kp
,
k\in G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}
,
p\in G(\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Note that both
G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}

and
G(\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{p}}
are
\theta
-stable.

Lemma 26
.

We have

\tau_{\theta}(G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}})=\tau_{\theta}(G(F))\cap G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}
.

Proof.

The left hand side is obviously contained in the right hand side.
It suffices to show the converse inclusion.
Let
g\in G(F)
be such that

\tau_{\theta}(g)=g\theta(g)^{-1}\in G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}
.
Write
g=kp
,
k\in G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}
,
p\in G(\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Then

kp\theta(p)^{-1}\theta(k)^{-1}=\tau_{\theta}(g),\quad kp=\tau_{\theta}(g)\theta(k)\theta(p).

By uniqueness of the decomposition (
17
),
we obtain

k=\tau_{\theta}(g)\theta(k),\qquad\tau_{\theta}(g)=k\theta(k)^{-1}=\tau_{\theta}(k),

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 27
.

For
\epsilon=-1
, we have

(i)

\tau_{\theta}(G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}})=(G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}})^{\mathrm{inv}\circ\theta}

(ii)

\tau_{\eta}(G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}})=\tau_{\eta}(G[t^{\pm 1}])\cap G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}
.

Proof.

(i):
This follows immediately from
Proposition
25

and Lemma

.

(ii):
We only need to show surjectivity.
This follows from (i):

\tau_{\eta}(G[t^{\pm 1}])\cap G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}\subset(G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\mathrm{inv}\circ\eta})^{\eta_{c}}=(G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}})^{\mathrm{inv}\circ\theta}=\tau_{\theta}(G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}})=\tau_{\eta}(G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta_{c}}).

∎

Proposition 28
.

The map
\tau_{\eta}:G[t^{\pm 1}]\rightarrow G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\mathrm{inv}\circ\eta}

is surjective for
\epsilon=-1
,
which induces an isomorphism

G[t^{\pm 1}]/G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\eta}\simeq G[t^{\pm 1}]^{\mathrm{inv}\circ\eta}

Since

is equivariant with respect to

-action,
it suffices to show

-orbit on

By Theorem

any orbit contains an element fixed by

By Corollary

such an element is contained in

Recall from Theorem

(i):

(iii)

be a pure inner twist of

\epsilon=1
.

defines a

-equivariant bijection

5.4.1.

-twisted conjugation.

[

[

, §13.2]

Proposition 57

\displaystyle\gamma(t)\eta(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=(\lambda(t)g)(\eta(\lambda(t)g))=\lambda(t)g\eta_{0}(\lambda(\epsilon\bar{t}^{-1}))\eta_{0}(g)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{=}}\lambda(t)g\eta_{0}(\lambda(\epsilon\bar{t}^{-1}))g^{-1}\lambda(\epsilon)

Choose an
\eta_{0}
-stable maximal torus

Via conjugation, we can assume
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)^{+}
in (i).
Note that condition (iii) is equivalent to

According to (

Remark 61
.

Note that

G(F)^{\theta}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\!(t^{2})\!)
.
Under the bijection

\tau_{\theta}:\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)/\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\!t^{2})\!)\cong\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)^{\operatorname{inv}\circ\theta}

sending
\gamma\mapsto\gamma\theta(\gamma)^{-1}
,
the elements

\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\
0&t\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\
0&t^{\mu+1}\end{pmatrix}

are mapped into the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
-orbits of

\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&t^{\mu}\\
(-t)^{-\mu}&0\end{pmatrix}

respectively,
where the first matrix is in the same orbit as

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
.
Then the above description of orbits
implies that
the double cosets
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)/\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\!(t^{2})\!)

are represented by

\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\
0&t\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\
0&t^{\mu+1}\end{pmatrix},\mu>0.

In fact, the same computation
shows that
the double cosets
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)/\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\!(t^{2})\!)

for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}

are represented by 1-by-1 blocks of
1,t
together with
2-by-2 blocks

\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\
0&t^{\mu+1}\end{pmatrix}
,
\mu>0
, providing a different argument of
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
.
