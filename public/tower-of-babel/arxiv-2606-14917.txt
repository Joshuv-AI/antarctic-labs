Introduction

The aim of this article is the definition and analysis of a probability-measure-valued diffusion process, whose generator can be identified as the generator of an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck type process.
The process
{(\bm{\mu}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
we suggest as a candidate
has striking similarities with an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process
{(X_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
lives on the
2
-Wasserstein space over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A particularly comparable feature is the second moment, by which we mean the differential equation for the
squared norm of
X_{t}
respectively the second moment of
\bm{\mu}_{t}
.

Since at least 20 years, there has been an extensive discussion on the question of how a canonical diffusion process on the Wasserstein space could be defined (
[
12
,
8
,
5
,
4
,
13
]
), to which there seems to be no ultimate answer.
Thus it is clear, that also the question about a natural notion for an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process needs deep consideration and a careful analysis.

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a volatility matrix

We fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
and a symmetric, strictly positive definite (non-random) matrix
\Sigma={(\Sigma_{k,l})}_{k,l\in\{1,\dots,d\}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
Let
{(X_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued stochastic process satisfying

(1.1)

X_{t}=-\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}\textnormal{d}s+\sqrt{2\Sigma}\,W_{t},\hskip 17.00024ptt\geq 0,

where
{(W_{t})}_{t\geq 0}

denotes a standard Brownian Motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sqrt{2\Sigma}
the symmetric positive matrix with
(\sqrt{2\Sigma})^{2}=2\Sigma
.
Equation (
1.1
) describes a mean-zero Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process whose volatility coefficient is the (constant, non-random) matrix
\sqrt{2\Sigma}
.

The component processes
{\{{(W_{t}^{\Sigma,k})}_{t\geq 0}\}}_{k=1,\dots,d}
of
{(W^{\Sigma}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}:={(\sqrt{\Sigma}W_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
are martingales with quadratic covariation processes

{\big\langle W_{\bullet}^{\Sigma,k},W_{\bullet}^{\Sigma,l}\big\rangle}_{t}=t\Sigma_{k,l},\hskip 17.00024ptt\geq 0,\,k,l\in\{1,\dots,d\}.

The invariant measure of (
1.1
) is the Gaussian measure

\big((2\pi)^{d}\det(\Sigma)\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{\textnormal{T}}\Sigma^{-1}x\big)\textnormal{d}x

with covariance operator
\Sigma
.
For a function
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the multi-dimensional Itô formula yields

(1.2)

\textnormal{d}(\varphi\circ X_{t})=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big[\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{d}\Sigma_{k,l}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}\varphi(X_{s})\Big)-X_{s}^{k}\partial_{k}\varphi(X_{s})\Big]\textnormal{d}s+\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{k}\varphi(X_{s})\textnormal{d}W^{\Sigma,k}_{s}

for
t\geq 0
, where
{\{{(X_{t}^{k})}_{t\geq 0}\}}_{k=1,\dots,d}
denote the component processes of
{(X_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
.
In particular, writing
|x|_{\textnormal{euc}}=\sqrt{x^{\textnormal{T}}x}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and choosing
\varphi(x):=|x|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}
results in

(1.3)

|X_{t}|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}=2\textnormal{tr}(\Sigma)t-2\int_{0}^{t}|X_{s}|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}\textnormal{d}s+2\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}^{\textnormal{T}}\textnormal{d}W^{\Sigma}_{s},\hskip 17.00024ptt\geq 0.

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on the Wasserstein space with volatility operator

We write
\delta_{x}
for the Dirac measure in a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In this article, we define and analyze a diffusion
{(\bm{\mu}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
on the
2
-Wasserstein space
\mathscr{P}_{2}
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
whose drift and martingale parts are suitable generalizations compared to those of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In the drift part of (
1.2
) respectively (
1.3
), i.e.

(1.4)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big[\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{d}\Sigma_{k,l}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}\varphi(x)\Big)-x_{k}\partial_{k}\varphi(x)\Big]{\delta}_{X_{s}}(\textnormal{d}x)\textnormal{d}s,\hskip 8.50012ptt\geq 0,

and

(1.5)

2\textnormal{tr}(\Sigma)t-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}\delta_{X_{s}}(\textnormal{d}x)\textnormal{d}s,\hskip 8.50012ptt\geq 0,

we want to replace integration w.r.t.
{\delta_{X_{s}}}
by integration w.r.t. the time-dependent random measure
{\bm{\mu}_{s}}

which comes from the trajectories of a diffusion in
\mathscr{P}_{2}
. To achieve this, the notion of
\Sigma
has to be adapted.
In the example of the Ornstein–Uhlenbecck process (
1.1
), the parameter
\Sigma
is chosen as any symmetric, strictly positive definite matrix.
For our diffusion
{(\bm{\mu}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathscr{P}_{2}
, the parameter to choose is not a matrix, but an operator
A
of the following type:

•

A
is a self-adjoint, strictly positive definite operator on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some absolutely continuous measure
\lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
.

•

The inverse operator
A^{-1}
has a finite trace.

In the first of these two conditions, the particular choice of
\lambda
is irrelevant (see the discussion in Section
2.2
). The second condition implies that

A^{-1}
is the covariance operator of a centered, non-degenerate Gaussian measure
G
on
T_{\lambda}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
K^{A}={(K^{A}_{k,l})}_{k,l\in\{1,\dots,d\}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
denote the integral kernel of
A^{-1}
, meaning

K^{A}(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}g_{n}(x)g_{n}(y)^{\textnormal{T}}

for the sequence of eigenvalues
{(\alpha_{n})}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(multiplicities included) with a corresponding orthonormal eigenbasis
{(g_{n})}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, we define

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}:T_{\lambda}\ni f

\displaystyle\;\mapsto\;\lambda\circ f^{-1}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}

\displaystyle\text{and}\hskip 17.00024pt\hskip 17.00024pt\vec{\Psi}_{\lambda}:T_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni(f,x)

\displaystyle\;\mapsto\;(\Psi_{\lambda}(f),f(x))\in\mathscr{P}_{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

There is family of coefficient functions
\{\rho^{\mu}_{k,l}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

|

\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2},\,k,l\in\{1,\dots d\}\}
depending only on
A
, such that the drift part of

(1.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\textnormal{d}\bm{\mu}_{t}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\textnormal{d}\bm{\mu}_{0}\hskip 17.00024pt\text{for }t\geq 0,\,\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{k}\partial_{l}\varphi(x)|<\infty,\,k,l\in\{1,\dots,d\},

is given as

(1.7)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big[\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{d}\rho^{\bm{\mu}_{s}}_{k,l}(x)\partial_{k}\partial_{l}\varphi(x)\Big)-x_{k}\partial_{k}\varphi(x)\Big]\bm{\mu}_{s}(\textnormal{d}x)\textnormal{d}s.

The drift part of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\cdot|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}\textnormal{d}\bm{\mu}_{t}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\cdot|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}\textnormal{d}\bm{\mu}_{0}
,
t\geq 0
, is given by

(1.8)

2\textnormal{tr}(A^{-1})t-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}\textnormal{d}\bm{\mu}_{s}(\textnormal{d}x)\textnormal{d}s.

(
1.7
), (
1.8
) are the re-interpretations of (
1.4
), (
1.5
) for a Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on

In fact, the coefficient

in (

(G\times K^{A}_{k,l}(x,x)\lambda(\textnormal{d}x))\circ\vec{\Psi}_{\lambda}^{-1}

w.r.t.
(G\times\lambda)\circ\vec{\Psi}_{\lambda}^{-1}
.

Concerning the quadratic variation of the martingale part, which for (
1.2
) is given as

2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\sqrt{\Sigma}\nabla\varphi(x)\big|^{2}_{\textnormal{euc}}\delta_{X_{s}}(\textnormal{d}x)\textnormal{d}s,\hskip 8.50012ptt\geq 0,

also occurs for the semi-martingale in (

There exists a family of Hilbert spaces

such that

for
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}

The family

(1.10)

\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}{\big\|\eta(\mu,\cdot)\big\|}_{H_{\mu}}^{2}(G\circ\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1})(\textnormal{d}\mu)=\int_{T_{\lambda}}\Big\|A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\eta\big(\Psi_{\lambda}(f),f(\cdot)\big)\Big\|_{T_{\lambda}}^{2}G(\textnormal{d}f),\hskip 8.50012pt\eta\in C_{b}(\mathscr{P}_{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The Dirichlet form and its reference measure

2.1
The Dirichlet form
\mathscr{E}^{G,\lambda}

For
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
we set

T_{\mu}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu;\mathbb{R}^{d}),

the set of
\mu
-equivalence classes of measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu<\infty
.
T_{\mu}
is a separable Hilbert space with scalar product

{\langle f,g\rangle}_{T_{\mu}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\langle f(x),g(x)\rangle}_{\textnormal{euc}}\,\mu(\textnormal{d}x),\hskip 17.00024ptf,g\in T_{\mu}.

\mathscr{P}_{2}
is endowed with the usual (complete and separable)
2
-Wasserstein metric

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\textnormal{Coupl}(\mu,\nu)}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|_{\textnormal{euc}}^{2}\pi(\textnormal{d}x,\textnormal{d}y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the infimum is taken over all couplings
\pi
of
\mu
and
\nu
.

Definition 2.1
.

a) An
intrinsically differentiable
function
u:\mathscr{P}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous map such that

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu;\mathbb{R}^{d})\ni f\mapsto D_{f}u(\mu):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{u\big(\mu\circ(\textnormal{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}+\varepsilon f)^{-1}\big)-u(\mu)}{\varepsilon}

is a bounded linear functional for every
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
. In this case,
the intrinsic derivative of
u
at
\mu
is defined as the unique element
Du(\mu)\in T_{\mu}
such that

D_{f}u(\mu)={\langle Du(\mu),f\rangle}_{T_{\mu}},\hskip 17.00024ptf\in T_{\mu}.

b) The class
C^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
contains all intrinsically differentiable functions
u:\mathscr{P}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\big|u\big(\mu\circ(\textnormal{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}+f_{n})^{-1}\big)-u(\mu)-D_{f_{n}}u(\mu)\big|}{\|f_{n}\|_{T_{\mu}}}=0

for every zero-sequence
{(f_{n})}_{n}\in T_{\mu}
,
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
,
and moreover
Du(\mu)
possesses
\mu
-versions
\widetilde{Du}(\mu)
for
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
such that

\mathscr{P}_{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni(\mu,x)\mapsto\widetilde{Du}(\mu)(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

is continuous.

c) The class
C^{1}_{b}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
contains all elements
u\in C^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
such that

\sup_{(\mu,x)\in\mathscr{P}_{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(\mu)|+|\widetilde{Du}(\mu)(x)|_{\textnormal{euc}}<\infty.

The elements of
\mathscr{P}_{2}
which are absolutely continuous w.r.t. the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure are denoted by
\mathscr{P}_{2}^{\textnormal{ac}}
.
Throughout this text, we fix
\lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{\textnormal{ac}}
and consider the surjective contraction:

\Psi_{\lambda}:T_{\lambda}\to\mathscr{P}_{2},\hskip 17.00024ptf\mapsto\lambda\circ f^{-1}.

Let
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
be a self-adjoint, strictly positive operator on
T_{\lambda}
whose inverse
A^{-1}:T_{\lambda}\to T_{\lambda}
is trace class.
There is a unique centered Gaussian measure
G
on
T_{\lambda}
with covariance

(2.1)

\int_{T_{\lambda}}{\langle f_{1},g\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}{\langle f_{2},g\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}G(\textnormal{d}g)={\big\langle f_{1},A^{-1}f_{2}\big\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}.

the covariance operator of

.
By virtue of Lemma

[
2
, Chap. I]

[

, Chap. I]

The choice of

Lemma 2.1
.

The symmetric, non-negative bilinear form

(2.2)

\mathscr{E}^{G,\lambda}(u,v):=\int_{T_{\lambda}}{\big\langle Du(\lambda\circ f^{-1})\circ f,A^{-1}\big(Du(\lambda\circ f^{-1})\circ f\big)\big\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}G(\textnormal{d}f),\hskip 17.00024ptu,v\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2}),

is a pre-Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{2},G\circ\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1})
, i.e. it has the Markov property and is closable.

Proof.

We denote the
(1,2)
-Sobolev space (see
[
3
, Chap. II.9]
) of the Gaussian measure
G
by
W^{1,2}(T_{\lambda},G)
. Let
{(g_{n})}_{n}
be an eigenbasis
Ag_{n}=\alpha_{n}g_{n}
with eigenvalues
{(\alpha_{n})}_{n}\subset(0,\infty)
. Then,

\int_{T_{\lambda}}{\big\langle\nabla w(f),A^{-1}\nabla w(f)\big\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}G(\textnormal{d}f)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{n}^{-1}\int_{T_{\lambda}}{\langle\nabla w(f),g_{n}\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}^{2}G(\textnormal{d}f)

for

and the operators

,

, are closable in
L^{2}(T_{\lambda},G)
(a consequence of
[
3
, Lem. 9.2.5]
).
So, the minimal closed extension of the symmetric, non-negative bilinear form

(2.3)

(w_{1},w_{2})\;\mapsto\;\int_{T_{\lambda}}{\big\langle\nabla w_{1}(f),A^{-1}\nabla w_{2}(f)\big\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}G(\textnormal{d}f),\hskip 17.00024ptw_{1},w_{2}\in W^{1,2}(T_{\lambda},G),

in

exists. We write
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{W^{1,2}_{A^{-1}}(T_{\lambda},G)}

for the extension of (
2.3
) to the domain of its closure, which is denoted by

As shown in
[

(also see

1

In particular,

and

Since

is immediate by Definition
2.1

,
\chi^{\prime}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for general image structures.
∎

Remark 2.3

The analogue of (

\displaystyle={\big\langle A^{-1}I_{h}^{*}\big(\nabla_{T_{\mu}}(u\circ\Psi_{\mu})\circ I_{h}\big),\nabla(v\circ\Psi_{\lambda})\big\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle={\big\langle A^{-1}I_{h}^{*}\big(\nabla_{T_{\mu}}(u\circ\Psi_{\mu})\circ I_{h}\big),\nabla(v\circ\Psi_{\mu}\circ I_{h})\big\rangle}_{T_{\lambda}}

Remark 2.4
.

G
is chosen as a non-degenerate Gaussian measure on

Remark 2.5
.

a) Let
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\rho_{\lambda}
denote the probability density of
\lambda
w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure.
For an open domain

on which

, we have

(2.6)

\int_{O}\varphi(f(x))\lambda(\textnormal{d}x)=\int_{f(O)}\frac{\varphi(x)\rho_{\lambda}(f^{-1}(x))}{\big|\det(Jf(f^{-1}(x)))\big|}\textnormal{d}x

for measurable

.

b) Let

with
\lambda(\{\det(Jf)=0\})=0
.
The open set
\{\det(Jf)\neq 0\}
can be written as the countable union of open domains
{\{O_{n}\}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on each of which

restricts to a diffeomorphism
(Inverse Function Theorem). If

is measurable and has Lebesgue measure zero, then

\lambda(f^{-1}(N))=\lambda\big(f^{-1}(N)\cap\{\det(Jf)\neq 0\}\big)\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{O_{n}}\bm{1}_{N}(f(x))\lambda(\textnormal{d}x)=0

by (
2.6
). Hence
\Psi_{\lambda}(f)=\lambda\circ f^{-1}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{\textnormal{ac}}
.

c) Under assumption (
2.5
),
\Lambda(\mathscr{P}_{2}^{\textnormal{ac}})=G(\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\mathscr{P}_{2}^{\textnormal{ac}}))=1
follows by b).

We formulate a condition for a Gaussian measure on
T_{\lambda}
to satisfy (

).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be a Gaussian on
T_{\lambda}
with covariance operator
A^{-1}
as in (
2.1
). If

G\big(C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)=1

and there exists
an eigenvector
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
A
with
\lambda(\{\det(Jg)=0\})=0
, then (
2.5
) is satisfied. In particular,

G(\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\mathscr{P}_{2}^{\textnormal{ac}}))=1.

Proof.

Let
g
be as above with
Ag=\alpha g
for some
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and w.l.o.g.
\|g\|_{T_{\lambda}}=1
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{T_{\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{1}_{\{0\}}\big(\det(Jf(x))\big)\lambda(\textnormal{d}x)G(\textnormal{d}f)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{T_{\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{1}_{\{0\}}\big(\det(Jf(x))\big)\lambda(\textnormal{d}x)G(\textnormal{d}f)\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{T_{\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{1}_{\{0\}}\Big(\det\big(Jf(x)-sJg(x)\big)\Big)\lambda(\textnormal{d}x)\exp\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha s^{2}+s\alpha\langle g,f\rangle_{T_{\lambda}}\big)G(\textnormal{d}f)\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\bm{1}_{\{0\}}\Big(\det\big(Jf(x)-sJg(x)\big)\Big)\exp\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha s^{2}+s\alpha\langle g,f\rangle_{T_{\lambda}}\big)\textnormal{d}s\lambda(\textnormal{d}x)G(\textnormal{d}f)

using the Cameron-Martin formula and Fubini.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\det(Jg(x))\neq 0
and
I_{d\times d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
being the identity matrix,

\det\big((Jf(x))+s(Jg(x))\big)=0\hskip 17.00024pt\iff\hskip 17.00024pt\det\big(Jg(x)^{-1}Jf(x)+sI_{d\times d}\big)=0

is true for only finitely many

. Hence, the integral above vanishes.
∎
