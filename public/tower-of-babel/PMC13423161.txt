Effect of transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation on postoperative gastrointestinal dysfunction following laparoscopic urological surgery: a protocol for a randomised, controlled trial
Authors: Linhong Wang, Guanhua Qiu, Yuqi Li, Lifang Chen, Bin Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe incidence of urinary tract tumours is increasing annually, and laparoscopic surgery is currently the predominant surgical method employed in clinical practice. Postoperative gastrointestinal dysfunction (POGD) is one of the most prevalent adverse events associated with patients who receive laparoscopic surgery. Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) may be an effective treatment for POGD. This study intends to evaluate the efficacy and safety of TEAS for POGD following urological laparoscopic surgery.MethodsThis is a prospective, single-centre, randomised controlled trial. A total of 112 urological patients will be randomly assigned to either the TEAS group or the sham TEAS group at a ratio of 1:1. Each group of patients received a total of two treatments, one prior to surgery and one in the post-anaesthesia recovery room (PACU) after surgery, with each treatment lasting 30 min. The intervention group received TEAS treatment at bilateral Neiguan (PC6), Zusanli (ST36) and Ciliao (BL32) acupoints. Once patients achieved an effective sensation of ‘deqi’ at these points, the TEAS intensity was adjusted to the maximum tolerable level and maintained for 30 min. The primary outcome will be the score of intake, feeling nauseated, emesis, physical exam and duration of symptoms (I-FEED) on the day 3 postoperatively. The secondary outcomes will include the time of first postoperative flatus and defecation, 5 days postoperative Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) score and the Quality of Recovery Scale (QoR-15), and preoperative 1-day and postoperative 5-day measurements of platelet/lymphocyte ratio (PLR), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-10 (IL-10), acetylcholine (Ach), norepinephrine (NE) concentration levels, motilin (MTL), and microRNA-10b-5p (miR-10b-5p) concentration levels. The safety of transcutaneous acupoint electrical stimulation will also be evaluated.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the First affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University. The results of the study will be disseminated through a variety of channels, including peer-reviewed open-access publications, scientific conferences and targeted communications with patient organisations, healthcare providers and the broader public.Trial registration numberNCT07133620.Keywords: ANAESTHETICS, Acupuncture, GASTROENTEROLOGY, UROLOGY

Background

In recent years, there has been a gradual increase in the incidence of urinary system tumours.1 Laparoscopic surgery is currently the predominant surgical modality in clinical practice.2 3 Despite its designation as a minimally invasive procedure, a multitude of factors have the potential to influence the recovery of gastrointestinal function postoperatively. These include, but are not limited to, CO2 pneumoperitoneum-induced gastrointestinal ischaemia and reperfusion, inflammatory damage, surgical trauma, preoperative fasting and fluid restriction, and anaesthetic drugs administered during the operation. The consequences of these factors can include delayed recovery of gastrointestinal function, which in turn can significantly impact the early postoperative rehabilitation of patients.

Postoperative gastrointestinal dysfunction (POGD) is an acute pathological physiological change of the gastrointestinal tract that occurs secondary to surgery, trauma, shock and other systemic diseases, mainly characterised by nausea, vomiting, abdominal distension, delayed gas and stool passage, intestinal obstruction, and digestive tract bleeding.4 In severe cases, it can lead to intestinal-origin infections and secondary multiple organ dysfunction. POGD has been observed most frequently in abdominal surgeries, whether intestinal or non-intestinal in nature, with an incidence ranging from 10% to 30%.5 Once POGD occurs, the postoperative recovery time is significantly delayed, especially for elderly patients, prolonging the hospital stay and increasing postoperative complications, greatly increasing the medical burden. To reduce the occurrence of POGD, clinical currently mostly adopt measures such as reducing the prophylactic nasogastric decompression,6 shortening the preoperative fasting and fluid restriction time,7 reducing preoperative intestinal preparation,8 low intra-abdominal pressure, and opiate economy techniques. However, the efficacy of these measures in preventing and treating POGD remains unsatisfactory. There is an urgent need for clinically effective intervention drugs and measures. Non-pharmacological treatment methods that are targeted are gradually receiving attention.

The application of traditional Chinese and Western medicine in the perioperative period has become increasingly widespread, especially acupuncture has gradually been discovered and valued for its potential in treating POGD. Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS), as one of the modified traditional Chinese acupuncture therapies, is a non-invasive acupoint electrical stimulation that applies electrical stimulation pulses to the target acupoints by using electrodes placed on the surface of the acupoints instead of needles, having advantages such as no pain, simple operation, stable efficacy and ease of acceptance by patients.

Recent studies have demonstrated the efficacy of TEAS in postoperative pain and systemic inflammatory responses and the incidence of POGD.9–13 However, there is currently no research that clearly explains the impact of TEAS on the recovery of gastrointestinal function after urological laparoscopic surgery. Therefore, this study intends to evaluate the efficacy and safety of perioperative TEAS for POGD following urological laparoscopic surgery.

Methods

Study design

This prospective, single-centre, randomised controlled trial will be conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University, with a control group and an intervention group. The assessors who will be tasked with evaluating the outcomes will be instructed to maintain blindness with regard to the group assignments. The research ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University (SL-2025KY194-02) granted ethical approval prior to the commencement of participant recruitment. The initial registration of the trial was conducted on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07133620) on 21 August 2025. The study is scheduled to initiate in November 2025 and is expected to conclude in November 2026. The duration of the study for each enrolled patient is 6 days, commencing 1 day prior to surgery and concluding 5 days postoperatively. This period encompasses randomisation, intervention and data collection. The present randomised controlled trial protocol adheres to the 2013 Statement on Intervention Trial Recommendations (SPIRIT). Randomisation and blinding

This study adopts the random number method for random grouping. The specific procedure is as follows: The total 112 subjects who meet the inclusion criteria will be numbered sequentially (from 1 to 112) according to the order of their surgeries. A column of random numbers is generated using the RAND function in Excel software, with each number corresponding to a random value. The numbers are sorted in ascending or descending order based on the random values. The numbers from 1 to 56 are assigned to the sham TEAS group, and those from 57 to 112 are assigned to the acupuncture group. The researchers will fill out the random number cards according to the allocation plan and place them in envelopes for storage. The blind bottom will be properly kept. After the participants are included, the envelopes are opened in sequence according to the enrolment order, and they are grouped based on the random card number in the envelopes. The randomisation process will be conducted by a study administrator who is not directly involved in the study.

Intervention

The study was divided into two groups: the TEAS group and the sham TEAS group. In the TEAS group, the treatment will be carried out by operators who have systematically studied acupuncture. The study will use the QL/T type transcutaneous nerve electrical stimulation device (Sichuan Qianli beoka Medical Technology Co., Ltd., Sichuan, China). The patient will be positioned supine. Prior to the induction of anaesthesia, the practitioner will meticulously disinfect the bilateral Zusanli (ST36),14 15 Neiguan (PC6)16 17 and Ciliao (BL32)18 19 acupoints and apply electrode patches (table 1). The operator will perform TEAS using a sparse-dense waveform at 30 Hz and 50 microseconds, adjusting the current intensity according to the patient’s tolerance until an effective prickling sensation (‘deqi’) is felt.20–23 Once this state is achieved, the stimulation will be maintained for a duration of 30 min. After the procedure, the second stimulation will be continued in the post-anaesthesia recovery room (PACU), with the stimulation method remaining the same as before. Patients in the sham TEAS group underwent the same setup and procedures as the TEAS group, with the exception that they did not receive electrical stimulation. The positions of the acupoints will be based on the naming and location of the acupoints as stipulated in the National Standard of the People’s Republic of China (GB/T 12346-2021) in 2021.24

The induction and intubation of all patients was performed using a protocol that incorporated propofol at a dose of 1.5–2 mg/kg, sufentanil at 0.4 µg/kg and cisphenyl sulfatracurium at 0.2 mg/kg. Prior to the induction of anaesthesia, arterial cannulation was performed for the purpose of continuous blood pressure monitoring. Central venous catheterisation was performed as necessary. Mechanical ventilation was initiated. The maintenance dose of remifentanil (0.1 mg/kg/h), cisphenyl sulfatracurium (0.1 mg/kg/h), sevoflurane and/or propofol was conducted to maintain Bispectral Index (BIS) of 40–60. Postoperative analgesia and antiemetic measures were administered with flurbiprofen axeril 100 mg and ondansetron 4 mg. Continuous vital sign monitoring was maintained throughout the study for all patients. All TEAS procedures were performed by a research nurse who had undergone standardised training, and all surgeries were performed by the same team of senior surgeons.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Sample size

According to the findings of previous studies, the incidence of postoperative gastrointestinal dysfunction was about 45%.25 A 30% reduction in POGD incidence following intervention will be clinically significant. The sample size was estimated using PASS 2023, with α=0.05 and 1-β=0.8, and considering a 20% dropout rate. The total sample size of 112 was calculated, with 56 per group.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome measure

I-FEED score

The intake, feeling nauseated, emesis, physical exam and duration of symptoms (I-FEED) score is a clinical assessment tool used to evaluate postoperative gastrointestinal function, particularly in the context of postoperative care. The I-FEED score is a comprehensive assessment tool that encompasses five domains: food intake, nausea, vomiting, physical examination findings and duration of symptoms. The I-FEED score, a metric of performance, typically ranges from 0 to 2 points. Scores ranging from 3 to 5 are indicative of postoperative gastrointestinal intolerance (POGI), while scores of 6 and above signify POGD.

Laboratory parameters

MTL, miR-10b-5p, PLR, IL-6, IL-10, Ach and NE were measured in blood samples as indicators of gastrointestinal function, inflammatory response, pro-inflammatory cytokine activity and autonomic nervous system function.

Safety assessment

All adverse events (AEs) will be documented by both the patient and the outcome assessor throughout the trial. AEs will be categorised as either treatment-related (eg, burning, itching, pain or skin allergy) or non-treatment-related. A comprehensive data collection process will be implemented to document AEs and SAEs in detail. This will encompass the following information: the subject’s name, the date of onset, the severity of the event, the relationship to TEAS and the ultimate outcome.

Statistics analysis

All analyses will be conducted on a modified intention-to-treat basis, including all patients randomised with available primary outcome data. The analyses will be conducted using SPSS software (version 20.0; IBM SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA). The continuous data will be described as mean (SD) or median (IQR), depending on the distribution of the data. Categorical data will be presented as percentages. The appropriate statistical tests, including independent t-tests, Mann-Whitney U tests, χ² tests and Fisher’s exact tests, will be employed to compare the groups. For primary and secondary endpoints, relative risks and mean or median differences with 95% CIs will be used to analyse between-group differences. Subgroup analyses will be conducted for the primary outcome measures to determine whether there are differences in the efficacy of TEAS across these subgroups. It is anticipated that missing data will be infrequent within the dataset, and there are no plans for the process of imputation. A two-tailed p value less than 0.05 will indicate a statistically significant difference.

Discussion

Postoperative gastrointestinal dysfunction is a prevalent and troublesome symptom among patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery. A variety of measures are routinely employed to address and mitigate the occurrence of postoperative gastrointestinal dysfunction. These measures include prophylactic nasogastric tube placement, the administration of prokinetic agents and opioid-sparing techniques. Despite their frequent implementation, the efficacy of these measures remains suboptimal. Consequently, the identification of a treatment approach that enhances gastrointestinal function without impeding opioid analgesic strategies is imperative for patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery. Previous studies have reported that the use of TEAS has been shown to enhance postoperative gastrointestinal function in patients who have undergone gynaecological surgery.26 27 However, the extant literature has yet to elucidate the effect of TEAS on gastrointestinal recovery following urological laparoscopic surgery. In this trial, a sham control group was used to evaluate the efficacy of TEAS in addressing POGD following urological laparoscopic surgery. The trial employed a rigorous design to ensure the reliability of the randomised controlled trial. The findings of this study will provide stronger evidence regarding the effectiveness of TEAS as a treatment for POGD.

POGD is characterised by symptoms such as nausea and vomiting, abdominal distension, delayed flatus or bowel movements, and even intestinal obstruction. The present trial encompassed the majority of laparoscopic urological procedures, with the exclusion of bladder surgeries due to their intrinsic complexity. The I-FEED score was selected as the primary outcome for this trial. The I-FEED score is a reliable indicator of subjects’ gastrointestinal function and has been selected as the primary outcome measure in numerous clinical trials.4 28–30 In addition, the evaluation of VAS and QoR-15 scores will be conducted to ascertain the impact of TEAS on subjects’ analgesia and recovery quality. Furthermore, we will assess biological markers, including MTL and miR-10b-5p, to ascertain alterations in gastrointestinal function. Cajal interstitial cells (ICCs) function as pacemaker cells within the gastrointestinal tract.31 32 It has been demonstrated that the function of these cells can be reflected by miR-10b-5p.33 Furthermore, the PLR and inflammatory factors will be measured to reflect trends in systemic inflammatory responses.34 35 Finally, the NE and Ach will be measured to explore the influence of autonomic nervous system function.

However, this trial is not without limitations. First, as a single-centre study, the results may not be fully generalisable to other research centres or populations, thereby limiting external validity. Second, due to the inherent characteristics of TEAS, operators cannot be oblivious to the group assignment of subjects. This potential discrepancy may introduce bias and influence outcomes. Third, although subjects were blinded to group assignment, they could not be blinded to treatment due to the nature of the intervention. Patients in the TEAS group exhibited a noticeable response to the acupoint stimulation, while those in the sham TEAS group did not. The potential for placebo effects in the sham TEAS group cannot be discounted. Fourth, this study focuses on the short-term perioperative benefits of TEAS. Future research will validate the long-term efficacy of TEAS through large-scale studies with long-term follow-up.
