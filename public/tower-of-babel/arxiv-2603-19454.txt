Exact and Approximate Convex Reformulation of Linear Stochastic Optimal Control with Chance Constraints
Tanmay Dokania, Yashwanth Kumar Nakka

Abstract
In this paper, we present an equivalent convex optimization formulation for discrete-time stochastic linear systems subject to linear chance constraints, alongside a tight convex relaxation for quadratic chance constraints. By lifting the state vector to encode moment information explicitly, the formulation captures linear chance constraints on states and controls across multiple time steps exactly, without conservatism, yielding strict improvements in both feasibility and optimality. For quadratic chance constraints, we derive convex approximations that are provably less conservative than existing methods. We validate the framework on minimum-snap trajectory generation for a quadrotor, demonstrating that the proposed approach remains feasible at noise levels an order of magnitude beyond the operating range of prior formulations.



I
Introduction

Stochastic Motion Planning refers to the problem of constructing a path from an initial to a final state, subject to the uncertainty from sensors, dynamics, or obstacles, while avoiding them and minimizing a cost function
[
7
,
6
,
2
]
.
Uncertainty leads to a distribution over states, and therefore a notion of ‘tube’ becomes necessary, which is not accounted for by deterministic planners, leading to incorrect safety and quality assessment
[
15
]
. Examples of such systems include spacecrafts encountering interstellar objects
[
13
]
and Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) under turbulent winds
[
11
]
.

A motion planning problem under optimality and chance constraints can be formulated as a stochastic optimal control problem. Optimization-based control of linear stochastic systems
serves as a critical backbone for several nonlinear approaches that iteratively linearize around different points
[
3
,
6
]
, or run multiple in parallel
[
5
]
. In this simplified setting, the problem is convex and can be solved in real time; therefore, a better understanding of this setup can significantly improve algorithms upstream.

Covariance control refers to finding an optimal control law that drives a distribution to another distribution with specified covariance.
Such approaches with a feedback structure can be used to solve the stochastic motion planning problem.
Without chance constraints, the problem simplifies because the mean and covariance optimization can be decoupled; as a result, theoretical results show that feedback laws are optimal for steering distributions
[
4
]
. Additionally, a lossless convex relaxation is used to solve the problem under both multiplicative and additive noise efficiently
[
1
]
.
Decoupling the mean and covariance, however, also decouples the risk-performance tradeoff, as tighter feedback laws navigate a narrow corridor rather than an open space. This can be formulated as a set of chance constraints. In the study of linear chance constraints, feedback laws are assumed optimal and used to formulate an optimization problem that yields nonconvex constraints. They are solved via linearization
[
10
]
or successive convexification
[
9
]
. To the best of the author’s knowledge, there are no theoretical results on the optimality of feedback laws under a chance-constrained setting for the covariance control problem.

We propose a lifted representation method, inspired by
[
6
]
, that captures the distribution of state and control input across different time steps. First, we show that this allows for the exact reformulation of linear chance constraints as second-order cone constraints under a Gaussian distribution, the dynamic constraints remain affine, and the representation of general linear functions involving states and controls from different timesteps remains possible.
Secondly, we develop convex approximations of quadratic chance constraints as linear-matrix and quadratic constraints and compare their approximation tradeoff.
Finally, we show that our approach outperforms other approaches that can represent state and control chance constraints
[
10
,
3
]
in terms of feasibility and optimality, even with an order-of-magnitude larger noise standard deviation.

II
Problem Formulation

This section presents the stochastic optimal control problem with chance constraints. The following stochastic linear time-varying system is considered for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
:

\begin{split}\mathbf{x}_{k+1}&=A_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+B_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}+D_{k}\mathbf{w}_{k},\\
\mathbf{x}_{0}&\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu_{0}^{\star},\Sigma_{0}\right),\qquad\mathbf{w}_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,I_{n_{w}}\right),\end{split}

(1)

here,
N
is the horizon,
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{N},\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{N-1},\{\mathbf{w}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{N-1}
are the state, control and noise random processes over
\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{w}}
respectively,
and
A_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{x}}
,
B_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{u}}
,
D_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{w}}
are the system, control and disturbance matrices respectively.

Assumption 1
.

The initial state
(\mathbf{x}_{0})
and the process noise
(\mathbf{w}_{k})
at each time step are independent Gaussian R. Vs., implying
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\top}\right]=\delta_{ij}I_{n_{w}},\ \forall i,j\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{0}^{\top}\right]=0,\ \forall i\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
.
The control input
(\mathbf{u}_{k})
is an R.V. that can be represented as a linear combination of the independent R.Vs. realized up to time step
k
, i.e.,
\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{k-1}
. This class of causal and affine policies is denoted by
\Pi_{\text{affine}}
.

As the state and control are random processes, chance constraints are required to give probabilistic guarantees. The constraints generally considered are linear chance constraints on state or control at a given time step:

\mathbb{P}\!\left(a^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i}\leq b\right)\geq 1-\epsilon,\quad\mathbb{P}\!\left(\alpha^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{i}\leq b\right)\geq 1-\epsilon,

here,
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},b\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\epsilon>0
measures the confidence of the constraint. We propose the consideration of mixed-multistep linear chance constraints that allow for representing chance constraints across multiple time steps and mixed with control constraints of the following type,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(a_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{0:N}+\alpha_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{0:N-1}\leq b_{i}\right)\geq 1-\epsilon_{i},

(2)

here,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)n_{x}},\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{Nn_{u}},b_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\epsilon_{i}\in[0,0.5)
,
\mathbf{x}_{0:N}=[\mathbf{x}_{0}^{\top},\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\top},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}^{\top}]^{\top}
,
\mathbf{u}_{0:N}=[\mathbf{u}_{0}^{\top},\mathbf{u}_{1}^{\top},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{N-1}^{\top}]^{\top}

\forall\ i\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
.

Remark 1
.

Polytropic chance constraints can be described using multiple linear chance constraints, where the risk associated is allocated using Boole’s and a bilevel optimization problem
[
14
]
.

Representation of state-control-coupled constraints allows us to define a linearized version of power constraints on a motor or friction-circle constraints for tire grip.

We consider centered quadratic chance constraints, which are represented as

\mathbb{P}\!\left((\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{x}_{i}\right])^{\top}Q_{\text{CC}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{x}_{i}\right])\leq 1\right)\geq 1-\epsilon,

(3)

here,
Q_{\text{CC}}\in\mathbb{S}^{++}_{n_{x}},\epsilon\in[0,1]
defines the ellipsoid that the state must be in, with a high confidence measure

In covariance control problems, a constraint on the terminal covariance is imposed with
\Sigma_{f}\in\mathbb{S}^{++}_{n_{x}}
,

\operatorname{var}\!\left(x_{N}\right)\preceq\Sigma_{f}.

(4)

For motion planning problems, waypoints that the robot needs to follow through at specified timesteps
T_{wp}\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
, can be described as soft constraints through the cost function
(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{x}_{k}\right]-\mu_{k}^{\star})^{\top}Q_{\star}(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{x}_{k}\right]-\mu_{k}^{\star})

where
\mu_{k}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
.

Under quadratic cost on the system state and control input, the finite horizon optimal control problem with chance constraints is formulated as:

(
Linear Stochastic Optimal Control
)
.

\begin{aligned} \min_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}}J(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})=\sum_{i\in T_{wp}}(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{x}_{i}\right]-\mu_{i}^{\star})^{\top}Q_{\star}(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{x}_{i}\right]-\mu_{i}^{\star})\\
+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{x}_{k}^{\top}Q_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\top}R_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k})+(\mathbf{x}_{N}-x^{\star})^{\top}Q_{N}(\mathbf{x}_{N}-x^{\star})\right]\end{aligned}

(5)

subject to
\forall\ k\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}

\displaystyle{\eqref{eq:sys_dyn},\eqref{eq:def_lcc_mixed_multi},\eqref{eq:termQ},\eqref{eq:cov_control}}

III-A

Stochastic basis

Under the dynamics (
1
) and
1
, it follows that the state and control remain jointly Gaussian at every time step. Therefore, we propose using scalar Gaussians to capture the correct marginal and conditional distributions, rather than propagating covariances.

As
\mathbf{x}_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu_{0}^{\star},\Sigma_{0}\right)
, it can be represented as
\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mu_{0}^{\star}+\Psi_{0}^{\star}\mathbf{e}^{(0)}
, where
\mathbf{e}^{(0)}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,I_{n_{x}}\right)
and
\Psi_{0}^{\star}\Psi_{0}^{\star\top}=\Sigma_{0}
, thereby, requiring
n_{x}
scalar Gaussians. Additionally, at each time step
k
,
\mathbf{w}_{k}
introduces additional
n_{w}
independent scalar Gaussians. Therefore, we define the stochastic basis recursively as follows:

\mathbf{e}^{(k+1)}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{e}^{(k)}\\
\mathbf{w}_{k}\end{bmatrix},\ \ \forall\ k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}.

(6)

Hence,
\mathbf{e}^{(k)}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,I_{n_{e}(k)}\right)
, where
n_{e}(k):=n_{x}+kn_{w}
.

III-B

State representation

The R.V.
\mathbf{x}_{k}
is represented using two vectors,

\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}},\psi_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}n_{e}(k)}.

The vector
\psi_{k}
is the vectorized form of the matrix
\Psi_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{e}(k)}

\begin{split}\psi_{k}&=\operatorname{vec}\left({\Psi_{k}}\right),\\
\Psi_{k}&=(\operatorname{vec}\left(I_{n_{e}(k)}\right)^{\top}\otimes I_{n_{x}})(I_{n_{e}(k)}\otimes\psi_{k}).\end{split}

(7)

The state at step
k
is represented as:

\mathbf{x}_{k}=\mu_{k}+{\Psi_{k}}\mathbf{e}^{(k)}.

(8)

It follows that:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{x}_{k}\right]=\mu_{k},\ \operatorname{var}\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)=\Psi_{k}\Psi_{k}^{\top}.

(9)

III-C

Control representation

Following
1

k
depends on the basis elements realized up to the time step, i.e.,
\mathbf{e}^{(k)}
. Therefore, it retains the causal property.
Similarly,
\mathbf{u}_{k}

\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},v_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}n_{e}(k)}

The matrix representation

\begin{split}v_{k}&=\operatorname{vec}\left({V_{k}}\right),\\
V_{k}&=(\operatorname{vec}\left(I_{n_{e}(k)}\right)^{\top}\otimes I_{n_{u}})(I_{n_{e}(k)}\otimes v_{k}).\end{split}

(10)

The control at step
k
is represented as:

\mathbf{u}_{k}=\nu_{k}+{V_{k}}\mathbf{e}^{(k)}.

(11)

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathbf{y}
be a scalar R.V. If it is representable in the basis
\mathbf{e}^{(k)}

\mathbf{y}=y_{0}+y\mathbf{e}^{(k)}

y_{0}\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n_{e}(k)}
then,
a chance constraint
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathbf{y}\leq b\right)\geq 1-\epsilon
can be exactly represented as

y_{0}+\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}(1-\epsilon)\|y\|_{I}\leq b,
which is a second-order cone exact reformulation of the chance constraint for
0\leq\epsilon<0.5
.

Proof.

As
\mathbf{y}
is represented as a linear combination of jointly Gaussian R.V.
\mathbf{e}^{(k)}
, it is a Gaussian R.V. Following the linearity of expectation and the fact that
\mathbf{e}^{(k)}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,I_{n_{e}(k)}\right)
, we obtain
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{y}\right]=y_{0},\ \operatorname{var}\!\left(\mathbf{y}\right)=\|y\|^{2}_{2}
. Following the definition of
\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\!\left(y\leq b\right)=\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}\left(\frac{b-y_{0}}{\|y\|_{I}}\right).

(12)

Using monotonicity of
\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}
, we get the above-mentioned constraint, which is a second-order cone for
0\leq\epsilon<0.5
.
∎

III-F

Conservative Approximation of Quadratic Chance Constraint to Covariance Constraint

Let
\mathcal{B}(Q)
denote the ellipsoidal set defined by
Q\in\mathbb{S}^{++}_{n_{x}}
,

\mathcal{B}(Q):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}|x^{\top}Q^{-1}x\leq 1\}.

(19)

Then, using set inclusion, it follows for
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{++}_{n_{x}}
:

\mathcal{B}(Q_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{B}(Q_{2})\iff Q_{1}\preceq Q_{2}.

(20)

Given a Gaussian R.V.
\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
, the
(1-\epsilon)
-confidence ellipsoidal set is
\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{\chi_{n_{x}}^{2}}^{-1}(1-\epsilon)\Sigma)
as defined using
\chi_{n}^{2}
distribution
[
3
]
.
Additionally, if the
(1-\epsilon)
-confidence ellipsoid is contained inside the ellipsoidal set formed by
Q
, then the probability constraint holds. Using (
20
), the following serves as a conservative approximation:

\Phi_{\chi_{n}^{2}}^{-1}(1-\epsilon)\Sigma\preceq Q\implies 1-\epsilon\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{B}(Q)\right).

(21)

Hence, using the above and (
18
) for the terminal state, the following serves as a LMI approximation of the quadratic chance constraint (
3
):

\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{1}{\zeta_{Q}}Q_{\text{CC}}&\Psi_{N}\\
\Psi_{N}^{\top}&I_{n_{e}(N)}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(22)

here,
\zeta_{Q}=\Phi_{\chi_{n}^{2}}^{-1}(1-\epsilon_{N})
.

Lemma 3
.

If
\eta\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\Sigma\right)
and

\operatorname{Tr}({\Sigma})\leq\frac{1}{\left(\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt[n]{1-\epsilon}}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}

(23)

then

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\|\eta\|_{I}\leq 1\right)\geq 1-\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let the singular value decomposition of
\Sigma
be denoted as
\Sigma=U\Lambda U^{\top}
, with
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
being the singular values,
\lambda_{\star}:=\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda)
and the transformed vector
\xi:=U^{\top}\eta
.

Then,
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\Lambda\right)
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
denote a hypercube set defined as

\mathcal{C}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x_{i}|\leq\sqrt{\frac{{\lambda_{i}}}{{\lambda_{\star}}}}\right\}.

Using the separability of the integrals, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(\xi\in\mathcal{C}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{-\sqrt{\lambda_{i}/{\lambda_{\star}}}}^{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}/{\lambda_{\star}}}}\frac{\exp(-x^{2}/2\lambda_{i})}{\sqrt{2\pi\lambda_{i}}}dx\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\star}}}\right)-1\right)^{n}=:1-f_{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda_{\star})

Therefore,
\lambda_{\star}\leq f_{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}(\epsilon)
if and only if
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\xi\in\mathcal{C}\right)\leq 1-\epsilon
, as the function
f_{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda_{\star})
is monotonically increasing for
\lambda_{\star}>0

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x\|_{I}\leq 1\}
denote the unit-sphere. It follows that the hypercube is contained within the sphere, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{S}
, therefore,
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\xi\in\mathcal{C}\right)<\mathbb{P}\!\left(\xi\in\mathcal{S}\right)
.

As
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\eta\in\mathcal{S}\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\xi\in\mathcal{S}\right)
and
\operatorname{Tr}({\Sigma})=\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda)
, the claim follows.
∎

Main Theorem: Deterministic Convex Problem

Using the above results we formulate a convex problem over the decision variables
{\mu,\nu,\psi,v}

Problem 2

(
Lifted Convex Reformulation

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a novel representation method that lifts the state vector to explicitly encode moment information, enabling exact representation of linear chance constraints as second-order cone constraints and less conservative approximate representations of quadratic chance constraints as linear-matrix and quadratic constraints over the decision variables. This representation leads to a deterministic convex formulation of the original stochastic optimal control problem.

We validated the framework on minimum-snap trajectory generation for a quadrotor. In the Circle Arena, the proposed approach achieves cost reductions of up to
35\%
over
[
3
]
and remains feasible at
\sigma=0.85
, while both
[
3
]
and
[
10
]
become infeasible beyond
\sigma\approx 0.06
. In the Funnel Corridor, we observe a cost improvement up to
43\%
under moderate noise and feasibility at
\sigma=0.6
, while both baselines fail beyond
\sigma=0.06
.
Across all tested configurations, the proposed approach returned a feasible solution in every instance. We observe feasibility even with a noise standard deviation an order of magnitude higher than that of existing approaches, as the exact representation yields the true feasible region, thereby improving feasibility and cost optimality.
