Real-Time Prediction Method of Shale Gas Fracturing Construction Pressure Based on MS-1DCNN-TFT Neural Network
Authors: Zhaoyi Liu, Xiangyu Wang, Lingling Han, Hanzhuo Cui, Tao Dou, Ziyang Tong, Yuteng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To realize the accurate and real-time prediction of shale gas fracturing construction pressure, this study proposes a collaborative innovation method for its pressure signal characteristics. An architecture combining multiscale one-dimensional convolutional neural network (MS-1DCNN) and temporal fusion transformer (TFT) is constructed. The former extracts instantaneous jump to long-term trend features synchronously, and the latter models complex timing dependence. To solve the problem of model failure in long-term prediction, a mild online learning strategy combining FIFO buffer and EMA weight update is designed to realize the smooth adaptation of the model to the change of working conditions. In the actual data experiment, the static model of this method (MAE = 3.21 MPa) has been significantly better than the baseline, and online learning has further reduced its MAE to 1.98 MPa (38% increase), effectively suppressing error drift. It provides high reliability algorithm support for intelligent monitoring of shale gas fracturing and has significant application value for achieving cost reduction and efficiency increase.

Introduction

Under the strategic background of the transformation of global energy structure to low carbonization, shale gas is an important clean unconventional natural gas resource, and its efficient development is of great significance for ensuring energy security. − Hydraulic fracturing is the core technology of shale gas development, especially multistage fracturing of horizontal wells, which is the key means to release the productivity of shale reservoirs. , In the process of fracturing construction, the wellhead construction pressure can reflect the formation change, fracture propagation and wellbore operation state in real time, which is the most critical monitoring parameter in the whole operation process. Therefore, accurate and real-time prediction of construction pressure is an important basis for realizing intelligent control and active safety early warning of fracturing. Accurate pressure prediction can not only warn engineering risks such as sand plugging and casing deformation in advance, but also provide a basis for optimizing construction parameters and improving reservoir reconstruction effect, to realize safe and efficient fracturing operation and cost reduction and efficiency increase in a real sense. −

For a long time, fracturing pressure analysis mainly relies on numerical simulation methods based on rock mechanics and fluid dynamics. However, these physical models heavily rely on formation parameters that are difficult to obtain accurately, and the calculations are lengthy, making it difficult to meet the needs of real-time prediction and rapid decision-making in the field. With the development of digital oilfield technology, data-driven methods have provided a new solution to this problem. Early studies used statistical learning models to analyze pressure curve characteristics, but their ability to process nonlinear, high-dimensional time series data is limited. ,

In recent years, deep learning technology has made breakthrough progress in the field of time series prediction and has been widely used in oil and gas engineering. − Recurrent neural networks (RNN) and their variants, such as long short-term memory networks (LSTM) and gated recurrent units (GRU), have been first applied to production and pressure prediction due to their excellent time-series dependency modeling capabilities. For example, Zhang et al. systematically compared the performance of LSTM, BiLSTM and GRU in shale oil fracturing pressure prediction and found that bidirectional structures can capture contextual information more effectively. To further explore the complex nonlinear relationships between multiple variables, attention mechanisms have been introduced, which enable the model to dynamically focus on key features. , On this basis, the Transformer architecture abandons the recurrent structure and relies entirely on the self-attention mechanism to process sequences, showing significant advantages in long-range dependency modeling. The temporal fusion transformer (TFT) proposed by Lim et al. further extends this framework. Through the design of gated residual networks and variable selection networks, it can simultaneously process static covariates, known future inputs and historical time series, and provides a powerful tool for multimodal time series prediction.

Although the above methods have made significant progress, their direct application to the real-time prediction of shale gas fracturing construction pressure still faces two core challenges. First, it is a difficult problem to extract multiscale features of signals. The fracturing pressure signal is a nonstationary mixture, which contains not only the second-order high-frequency pulsation caused by equipment vibration and near-wellbore effect, but also the minute-to-hour macroscopic evolution reflecting the propagation trend of fracture groups. Traditional LSTM or single-scale CNNs are difficult to capture this cross-scale local detail and global pattern at the same time. The second is the generalization and adaptability of the model in a dynamic environment. The strong heterogeneity of shale reservoirs and the switching of different construction stages (such as preacid treatment, temporary plugging steering, and main sanding) lead to significant drift of data distribution over time and space. , A static model trained well on historical data, in the face of new wells or new working conditions, the prediction performance tends to decline rapidly, and the error accumulates over time, which cannot meet the needs of long-term and robust real-time monitoring.

To cope with the challenge of multiscale feature extraction, researchers try to integrate different architectures. Convolutional neural network (CNN) has inherent advantages in extracting local spatial or temporal patterns. Jiang et al. combined CNN with TFT and successfully used it for prediction, which proved the effectiveness of CNN extracting spatial features and TFT modeling temporal dependence fusion. In the field of oil and gas, Gou et al. proposed a hybrid deep learning method, which combines variational mode decomposition (VMD), graph neural network (GNN) and attention mechanism to reduce feature redundancy and capture the spatial correlation between variables, achieving high-precision pressure prediction and risk warning. However, how to design a lightweight and efficient structure to extract end-to-end features specifically for the time-series multiscale characteristics of fracturing pressure signals remains to be further explored.

For the problem of model dynamic adaptability, online learning or continuous learning strategies are key research directions. Simple real-time retraining of the entire model is computationally expensive and prone to “catastrophic forgetting”. Therefore, designing a lightweight adaptive update mechanism that can balance the absorption of new knowledge and the retention of historical knowledge is crucial for engineering practice. −

In view of this, this paper proposes a novel deep learning architecture (MS-1DCNN-TFT) that integrates multiscale one-dimensional convolutional neural network and temporal fusion transformer for real-time prediction of shale gas fracturing pressure. The core innovations of this study are (1) a parallel multiscale one-dimensional convolutional module is designed, which synchronously captures instantaneous jumps, short-term fluctuations and long-term trends in pressure signals through parallel convolutional kernels of different sizes, generating feature representations rich in multiscale information; (2) a mild online learning strategy is introduced, which combines a first-in-first-out (FIFO) sample buffer and an exponential moving average (EMA) parameter update mechanism, enabling the model to use new data collected in real time to make smooth and limited fine-tuning of parameters, thereby continuously adapting to changes in formation response and effectively suppressing error drift in long-term prediction. The proposed method is verified on the actual shale gas fracturing data in the Sichuan Basin. The results show that it is not only significantly superior to the mainstream baseline model in prediction accuracy but also shows excellent long-term operation stability and working condition adaptability through online learning mechanism, which provides a reliable technical means for intelligent real-time monitoring and risk early warning of fracturing construction.

Data Preprocessing

During the high-frequency acquisition of fracturing construction data, factors such as instantaneous sensor failure, communication interruption, and on-site interference often lead to missing points, abnormal jumps, and random noise in the monitoring sequence. These problems can affect the model’s ability to identify key construction features. To ensure the stability and accuracy of the time series forecasting model, this study performed systematic preprocessing on the original data before modeling.

First, a sliding window Z-Score method is applied to eliminate outliers in dynamic variables. For a given window width w, an observation x i is flagged as an outlier if it satisfies 1 |xt−μwσw|>δ where μ w and σ w are the local mean and standard deviation, respectively. Following the Pauta criterion, the threshold δ is empirically set to 3. This optimally distinguishes measurement artifacts from actual fracturing dynamicseffectively suppressing random sensor spikes while preserving essential transient features, such as instantaneous pressure fluctuations during sand addition. Subsequently, missing points are imputed using neighborhood-averaged linear interpolation. A symmetric k-neighborhood is utilized if bidirectional valid data exists; otherwise, a one-sided mean is applied to maintain data continuity and local smoothness.

Second, to eliminate differences in the units and numerical ranges of different monitored variables, all continuous variables were uniformly subjected to max-min normalization 2 x′=x−xminxmax−xmin where x min and x max are the minimum and maximum values of the corresponding variables on the training set, respectively. Continuous features in static covariates also use this normalization method.

To avoid compromising the integrity of the input information, this study did not perform feature selection during the preprocessing stage. Since the temporal fusion TFT architecture used subsequently incorporates a variable selection network (VSN), which can adaptively identify and select key features through learnable weights, prematurely removing variables during preprocessing could lead to information loss and weaken the model’s cross-stage adaptability.

After the above processing, all variables are aligned according to the sampling frequency of 1s, forming a multidimensional time series with regular structure, consistent scale and suppressed noise. Based on the preprocessed time series data, this paper further constructs an MS-1DCNN-TFT fusion model for real-time construction pressure prediction.

Construction Pressure Risk Prediction Model

The real-time prediction model for horizontal well fracturing construction pressure constructed in this paper adopts an end-to-end design. The overall technical route is shown in Figure , which mainly includes the following four parts: local temporal feature extraction based on multiscale one-dimensional convolutional neural network (MS-1DCNN), multivariate construction pressure prediction based on temporal fusion transformer (TFT), construction pressure curve plotting, and a mild online learning strategy. The MS-1DCNN module extracts local features at different scales from time-series data such as construction pressure, discharge rate, and sand concentration, and passes its output as input variables to the TFT module. The TFT module further integrates these timing features, static covariates and future planned pumping volume, captures the time dependence between multiple variables, and outputs the predicted value of construction pressure in the future period. The prediction results are mapped back to the actual physical dimension through the output layer to generate the construction pressure prediction curve. Finally, through the mild online learning strategy, the system can gradually update and adjust the model based on the construction data collected in real time, so as to continuously improve the prediction accuracy, adapt to the change of working conditions, and ensure the stability of long-term operation.

Temporal Feature Extraction Based on MS-1DCNN Module

Convolutional neural networks were originally used for two-dimensional image processing, and spatial features such as edges and textures were extracted through local connections and weight sharing. This idea is also applicable to one-dimensional time series: one-dimensional convolution automatically extracts local patterns and dependencies in the time dimension through a sliding window. However, the traditional one-dimensional convolution uses a single-scale convolution kernel, and the fixed receptive field is difficult to capture the “point” events (such as start–stop, rupture, and instantaneous pump stop), “stage” events (such as pump ball, temporary plugging, sand plugging) and the overall trend in the fracturing construction data at the same time. Therefore, this paper designs a multiscale one-dimensional convolution module (MS-1DCNN), which uses small, medium and large convolution kernels in parallel: small-scale kernels extract instantaneous jumps (point events), mesoscale kernels extract local fluctuations (stage events), and large-scale kernels extract long-term trends, thus effectively improving the multiscale feature learning ability of complex time series signals. Model Online Update

In real-time prediction tasks, model update strategies directly affect the long-term predictive stability and adaptability. If a rolling prediction method is adopted, that is, the predicted value of the previous time step is used as the input of the next time window, although it can achieve complete autoregressive inference, it will lead to error accumulation. Especially in dynamic and noisy scenarios such as fracturing construction, the prediction deviation will gradually increase over time, reducing long-term reliability. Conversely, if a full real-time update is adopted, that is, the model is retrained every time a new sample is received, although it can adapt to changes in data distribution to the greatest extent, the model may overfit the latest data and lose the general rules learned from historical data, thus reducing the generalization ability.

To balance real-time adaptability and model stability, this paper proposes a gentle online learning strategy. This strategy maintains a first-in, first-out (FIFO) buffer B with a fixed capacity of M to continuously store the most recently observed actual samples. When the number of samples in buffer B reaches its capacity M, a round of lightweight model fine-tuning is triggered. During this process, the model is updated K times on the data within buffer B using a specific learning rate to adjust parameters by a limited margin, ensuring the model adapts to the latest operating conditions while maintaining overall stability.

To further control the update magnitude and maintain the overall stability of the model, an exponential moving average method is used to smooth the updated parameters. Specifically, if the model parameters before fine-tuning are denoted as θold and the parameters after fine-tuning as on buffer B as θnew, then the final updated model parameters are 18 θfinal=β·θold+(1−β)·θnew where β is the decay rate, used to adjust the proportion of historical information and new knowledge in the model. This design enables the model to continuously integrate the new models that appear during the construction process without excessively deviating from the existing knowledge, so as to maintain high prediction accuracy and working condition robustness in long-term operations. Results and Discussion

Model Training and Hyperparameter Tuning

To ensure the objectivity and reproducibility of the experimental results, all models were implemented in Python and trained on a workstation (Ubuntu 22.04 LTS, Intel Core i9–13900K CPU, and NVIDIA RTX 5060 GPU), with an average computation time ranging from 30 min to 1 h. The data of this study are derived from the high-frequency fracturing monitoring records of shale gas platforms in Luzhou Block, Sichuan Basin. After preprocessing in Section , the data set contains a total of 60 wells and about 500,000 time series data. In order to avoid future information leakage caused by time series data, this study strictly divides the data into complete construction sections according to time sequence: the first 70% of continuous construction sections are used for model training ; the middle 20% is used for verification and hyperparameter tuning ; the last 10% of the independent construction section is used as the test set. The division strategy ensures that the same construction section is not divided by the cross set, so that the prediction performance of the model in the face of the new operation section can be truly evaluated.

To obtain the optimal hyperparameter configuration, this study employs a Bayesian optimization method to automatically search for the core hyperparameters of the model. This method uses the Gaussian process as a surrogate model for the validation set performance and guides the exploration of the high-dimensional parameter space by estimating the posterior distribution characteristics of the loss function space. Its core iterative process is as follows: starting from random initial sampling points, the probability distribution of the objective function is continuously updated by a Gaussian process. The expected improvement is used as the sampling function to predict the expected improvement of each candidate hyperparameter on the model performance and recommend the next set of sampling points. Then, the model is trained with this set of hyperparameters and evaluated on the validation set. This process is repeated until a preset termination condition is met, and the optimal hyperparameter combination with the lowest mean absolute percentage error on the validation set is finally output. Model Online Learning

The shale gas fracturing operation shows significant nonstationarity, and the formation response characteristics are obviously different in different well sections and even in different construction stages of the same well section. In order to verify the effectiveness of the’ mild online learning strategy’ proposed in Section in dealing with this data distribution drift and maintaining the long-term prediction accuracy of the model, this section conducts systematic experimental research. The successful implementation of the strategy depends on the effective coordination of the two core mechanisms: the FIFO buffer ensures that the model learns based on a limited and continuously updated recent data window, which not only avoids historical data redundancy, but also suppresses instantaneous outliers. The exponential moving average (EMA) weight update mechanism achieves a smooth transition between old and new knowledge and retains the general rules in historical data while absorbing new information.

To quantitatively evaluate the key role of EMA weight smoothing mechanism in this architecture and reveal the potential risk of model updating without this mechanism, a control experiment is designed as follows: First, the EMA strategy is canceled and the model is fully updated by different training epochs. The experimental results are shown in Figure . When the epoch is 1, the model shows the best short-term adaptability. However, with the increase of epochs, the performance of the model is significantly reduced. This trend shows that in the absence of a historical knowledge protection mechanism, full update will lead to overfitting of the model to the latest data distribution, causing “catastrophic forgetting” , confirming that the strategy has limitations in long-term deployment.

Full update loss function.

In contrast, after the introduction of the EMA mechanism, the model update process is effectively optimized. Based on the analysis of the parameter sensitivity heat map shown in Figure , when a large attenuation rate is used with a medium number of epochs, the model achieves the optimal performance, which is about 38% higher than the static model. Conclusion

This paper presents an integrated deep learning framework, coupling MS-1DCNN and TFT, to address the complexities of shale gas fracturing pressure prediction. By synergizing multiscale local feature extraction with global temporal dependence modeling, the proposed architecture achieves comprehensive characterization of both microfluctuations and macro-trends. The inclusion of a mild online learning strategy effectively mitigates the impact of formation heterogeneity, ensuring sustained prediction accuracy throughout the construction process. Validated with field production data, the model demonstrates high robustness and reliability, offering significant engineering value for real-time risk warning and parameter optimization. Future research will focus on integrating physics-informed constraints into the neural network to further enhance the model’s interpretability and reliability under complex geological conditions. Meanwhile, real-time pressure prediction data will be leveraged for risk warning, allowing the model to provide timely alerts and support decision-making in practical applications.
