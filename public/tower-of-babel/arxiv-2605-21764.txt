Quasi-optimal polytopal finite element methods for biharmonic equation Thanks: The author received funding from the European Union’s Horizon 2020 research and innovation programme (project RandomMultiScales, grant agreement No. 865751).
Ngoc Tien Tran

Abstract
. This paper establishes quasi-optimal and lower-order error estimates for weak Galerkin, discontinuous Galerkin, and hybrid-high order finite element methods for the biharmonic equation under minimal regularity assumptions on general polytopal meshes. Furthermore, it is shown that the stabilization is an efficient contribution in a posteriori error estimators.

65N12, 65N15, 65N30

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Quasi-optimal error estimates arise from the Galerkin orthogonality of conforming finite element methods (FEM) in Cea’s lemma
[
9
]
, which is not accessible in nonconforming methods. Instead, we are in the setting of the second Strang lemma
[
2
]
. Bounding the consistency error therein often leads to additional regularity assumptions on the exact solution
[
12
]
.

The medius analysis of
[
18
]
provides, up to data oscillation, quasi-optimal error estimates for classical nonconforming and discontinuous Galerkin (DG) FEM. If smoothing operators are involved in the definition of the discrete right-hand side, then these nonconforming FEM are quasi-optimal without data oscillation
[
26
,
24
]
. Remarkably, quasi-optimality of nonconforming FEM can be precisely characterized in an abstract setting
[
25
]
. An extension of
[
25
]
to hybrid high-order (HHO) methods for elliptic PDE of second and fourth order is provided in
[
14
,
6
,
8
,
21
]
. A crucial ingredient therein is the existence of a continuous right-inverse of the interpolation operator.
This setting is quite restrictive, in particular, it excludes many popular nonconforming methods for the biharmonic problem, where the design of numerical schemes enjoys more flexibility with respect to degrees of freedom.

Another important aspect of the error analysis of
[
24
,
14
,
8
,
21
]
is the efficiency of the stabilization of the discrete solution in the a posteriori error analysis. The latter can be avoided on simplicial meshes via Helmholtz decomposition
[
3
,
10
]
or by pushing the stabilization parameter towards infinity
[
20
]
, but arises naturally as a contribution in error estimators on general polytopal meshes.

This paper is concerned with the a priori error analysis of weak Galerkin (WG), DG, and hybrid high-order (HHO) FEM for the biharmonic problem in two or three space dimensions under minimal regularity assumptions on polytopal meshes.

1.2.
Main results

Given a bounded polyhedral Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the biharmonic problem (with clamped boundary conditions) seeks the solution
u\in V\coloneqq H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
to

(1.1)

\displaystyle a(u,v)=F(v)\quad\text{for any }v\in V.

with
a(u,v)\coloneqq(\Delta u,\Delta v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(\mathrm{D}^{2}u,\mathrm{D}^{2}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
F=(f,\bullet)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\in V^{\prime}
.
Discretizations of (
1.1
) seeks the discrete solution
u_{h}\in V_{h}
with

(1.2)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=F_{h}(v_{h})\quad\text{for any }v_{h}\in V_{h},

where
a_{h}
and
F_{h}\in V_{h}^{\prime}
are suitable approximations of the continuous objects
a
and
F
. We assume that
a_{h}
is coercive w.r.t. some discrete norm
\|\bullet\|_{h}
of
V_{h}
, i.e.,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{h}^{2}\lesssim a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})\quad\text{for any }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Given linear bounded operators
\mathrm{I}_{h}:V\to V_{h}
(interpolation) and
\mathrm{J}_{h}:V_{h}\to V
(smoothing), the error analysis can depart from the split

\displaystyle\|e_{h}\|^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle\lesssim a_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u-u_{h},e_{h})

(1.4)

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u,e_{h})-a(u,\mathrm{J}_{h}e_{h})+F(\mathrm{J}_{h}e_{h})-F_{h}(e_{h}).

with the abbreviation
e_{h}\coloneqq\mathrm{I}_{h}u-u_{h}\in V_{h}
.
The error
F(\mathrm{J}_{h}e_{h})-F_{h}(e_{h})
in data approximation can be bounded using standard techniques and even vanishes if
F_{h}\coloneqq F\circ\mathrm{J}_{h}
. Thus, the difficulty in deriving quasi-optimal error estimates lies in the design of
\mathrm{I}_{h}
and
\mathrm{J}_{h}
so that the consistency error
a_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}u,e_{h})-a(u,\mathrm{J}_{h}e_{h})
is quasi-optimal.

This paper suggests interpolation operators
\mathrm{I}_{h}
based on the Galerkin (also known under the label of elliptic) projection. This is rather non-standard in hybridizable settings, where the
L^{2}
orthogonal projection is employed for interpolation onto cell variables.
While
L^{2}
projections appear to be a canonical choice considering the right-inverse property, Galerkin projections enable quasi-optimality of Lehrenfeld-Schöberl typed stabilizations on polytopal meshes.

Furthermore, we construct smoothing operators that are at least almost consistent with discrete test functions in the sense that the resulting error behaves quasi-optimal. These two ingredients are the driving force behind the analysis of this paper and together with (relatively) standard techniques in medius analysis, we obtain quasi-optimal and lower-order error estimates for the WG method of
[
23
]
, the symmetric and nonsymmetric interior penalty DG methods
[
22
,
16
]
, and the HHO method of
[
13
]
. The results are of the form

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\hat{u}_{h})\|+\|\mathrm{I}_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{h}+|u_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min_{\phi\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\phi)\|+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{M}),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|u-\hat{u}_{h}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{\delta}\big(\min_{\phi\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\phi)\|+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{M})\big)

with a discrete approximation
\hat{u}_{h}
of
u
in the space
P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
of piecewise polynomials w.r.t. some mesh
\mathcal{M}
, data oscillation
\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{M})
, and positive parameter
\delta
depending on the elliptic regularity of the problem.
In particular, the stabilization is an efficient contribution in a posteriori error estimators, i.e.,

|u_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}\lesssim\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\hat{u}_{h})\|+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{M}).

If
F_{h}\coloneqq F\circ\mathrm{J}_{h}
, then the data oscillations in (
1.2
) can be omitted. Note that this right-hand side is also well-defined for any
F\in V^{\prime}
.

We emphasize that (
1.2
) hold without additional regularity assumptions and on general polytopal meshes. Furthermore, it is expected that the analysis of this paper extends beyond the presented numerical schemes.

2.
Smoothing operator

Before the construction of conforming approximations is presented, we provide details on the polytopal mesh and fix the notation of finite element spaces.

2.1.
Polytopal meshes

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite collection of closed polytopes of positive volume with overlap of measure zero that covers
\overline{\Omega}=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}K
.
A face
S
of the mesh
\mathcal{M}
is a closed connected subset of a hyperplane
H_{S}
with positive
(d-1)
-dimensional surface measure such that either (a) there exist
K_{+},K_{-}\in\mathcal{M}
with
S\subset H_{S}\cap K_{+}\cap K_{-}
(interior face) or (b) there exists
K_{+}\in\mathcal{M}
with
S\subset H_{S}\cap K_{+}\cap\partial\Omega
(boundary face).

Let
\Sigma
be a finite collection of faces with overlap of
(d-1)
-dimensional surface measure zero that covers the skeleton
\partial\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\cup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\partial K=\cup_{S\in\Sigma}S
with the split
\Sigma=\Sigma(\Omega)\cup\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
into the set of interior faces
\Sigma(\Omega)
and the set of boundary faces
\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
.
For
K\in\mathcal{M}
,
\Sigma(K)
is the set of all faces of
K
.
The normal vector
\nu_{S}
of an interior face
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
is fixed in its orientation beforehand and set
\nu_{S}\coloneqq\nu|_{S}
for boundary faces
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
.
For
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
,
K_{+}\in\mathcal{M}
(resp.
K_{-}\in\mathcal{M}
) denotes the unique cell with
S\subset\partial K_{+}
(resp.
S\subset\partial K_{-}
) and
\nu_{K_{+}}|_{S}=\nu_{S}
(resp.
\nu_{K_{-}}|_{S}=-\nu_{S}
).
For
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
,
K_{+}\in\mathcal{M}
is the unique cell with
S\subset\partial K_{+}
.
The jump
[v]_{S}
and the average
\{v\}_{S}
of any function
v\in W^{1,1}(\mathrm{int}(T_{+}\cup T_{-}))
along
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
are defined by
[v]_{S}\coloneqq v|_{K_{+}}-v|_{K_{-}}\in L^{1}(S)
and
\{v\}_{S}\coloneqq(v|_{K_{+}}+v|_{K_{-}})/2\in L^{1}(S)
.
If
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
, then
[v]_{S}\coloneqq v|_{S}=v_{K_{+}}|_{S}\eqqcolon\{v\}_{S}
.

For a piecewise Sobolev function
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{M})
, i.e.,
v\in H^{1}(\mathrm{int}(K))
for any
K\in\mathcal{M}
,
[v]_{\Sigma}
and
\{v\}_{\Sigma}
are functions in
L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{M})
defined by
[v]_{\Sigma}|_{S}=[v]_{S}
and
\{v\}_{\Sigma}|_{S}=\{v\}_{S}
for any
S\in\Sigma
.

Differential operators with subscript
\mathrm{pw}
denote the piecewise version without explicit reference to the underlying mesh.

For theoretical purposes, let
\vartheta
denote the mesh regularity parameter of
\mathcal{M}
associated with a matching simplicial submesh
\mathcal{T}
and the set
\mathcal{F}
of faces of
\mathcal{T}
, we refer to
[
12
, Definition 1.38]
for a detailed definition (
\vartheta
is the minimum of the two parameters therein). The constants in inverse estimates and trace inequalities depend on
\vartheta
. Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of faces of
\mathcal{T}
.

2.2.
Finite element spaces

Given a subset
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of diameter
h_{M}
, let
P_{k}(M)
denote the space of polynomials of degree at most
k
;

P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
and
P_{k}(\Sigma)
are
the space of piecewise polynomials of degree at most
k
with respect to the mesh
\mathcal{M}
and the faces
\Sigma
.
The set
P_{k}(\Sigma(\Omega))
contains all functions
v_{\Sigma}\in P_{k}(\Sigma)
that vanish on the boundary, namely,
v_{\Sigma}|_{S}=0
for any
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
.

For any
v\in L^{1}(M)
, we denote the
L^{2}
projection of
v
onto
P_{k}(M)
by
\Pi_{M}^{k}v\in P_{k}(M)
;
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}
and
\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k}
are
L^{2}
orthogonal projections onto
P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
and
P_{k}(\Sigma)
.

The piecewise constant function
h_{\mathcal{M}}\in P_{0}(\mathcal{M})
reads
h_{\mathcal{M}}|_{K}=h_{K}=\mathrm{diam}(K)
and
h_{\max}\coloneqq\max_{K\in\mathcal{M}}h_{K}
is the maximal mesh-size of
\mathcal{M}
. Furthermore, we define the piecewise constant function
\nu_{\Sigma}\in P_{0}(\Sigma)
by
\nu_{\Sigma}|_{S}=\nu_{S}
.

2.3.
Construction of smoothing function

Let
W_{h}\coloneqq P_{\ell}(\mathcal{M})\times P_{m}(\Sigma(\Omega))\times P_{n}(\Sigma(\Omega))
with
\ell,m,n\geq 0
.

Theorem 2.1

(smoothing)
.

Given
w\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
and
(w_{\mathcal{M}},w_{\Sigma},\delta_{\Sigma})\in W_{h}
,
k\geq 2
, there exists a function
v\in V
with the following properties.

(a)

(discrete consistency)
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}v=w_{\mathcal{M}}
,
\Pi_{\Sigma}^{m}v=w_{\Sigma}
,
\Pi_{\Sigma}^{n}\nabla v\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}=\delta_{\Sigma}
.

(b)

(local approximation property) For all
K\in\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle h_{K}^{-4}\|v-w\|_{K}^{2}+h_{K}^{-2}\|\nabla(v-w)\|_{K}^{2}+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}(v-w)\|_{K}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{S\in\Sigma,S\cap K\neq\emptyset}(h_{S}^{-3}\|[w]_{S}\|^{2}_{S}+h_{S}^{-1}\|[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}w]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|^{2}_{S})+h_{K}^{-4}\|\Pi_{K}^{\ell}(w-w_{\mathcal{M}})\|_{K}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\big(h_{S}^{-3}\|\Pi_{S}^{m}(w-w_{\Sigma})\|_{S}^{2}+h_{S}^{-1}\|\Pi_{S}^{n}(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}w\cdot\nu_{S}-\delta_{\Sigma})\|_{S}^{2}\big).

Proof.

Since the two-dimensional case is simpler, the proof below only focuses on the three dimensional case.
In the first step, we average
w
at all degrees of freedom associated with higher-order
C^{1}
conforming finite element functions on the Worsey–Farin splits
[
27
]
of the matching simplicial submesh
\mathcal{T}
.
These degrees of freedom only depend on the evaluation of the function
w
and its first derivative
[
15
,
19
]
.
Standard averaging techniques
[
5
,
15
]
lead to
v_{0}\in V
with

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-4}\|w-v_{0}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{-2}\|\nabla(w-v_{0})\|_{T}^{2}+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}(w-v_{0})\|_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F},F\cap T\neq\emptyset}\big(h_{F}^{-3}\|[w]_{F}\|^{2}_{F}+h_{F}^{-1}\|[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}w]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\|^{2}_{F}\big).

For any
K\in\mathcal{M}
, summing over all
T\in\mathcal{T}
with
T\subset K
and
h_{T}\approx h_{K}
shows

\displaystyle h_{K}^{-4}\|w-v_{0}\|_{K}^{2}+h_{K}^{-2}\|\nabla(w-v_{0})\|_{K}^{2}+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}(w-v_{0})\|_{K}^{2}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\sum_{S\in\Sigma,S\cap K\neq\emptyset}\big(h_{S}^{-3}\|[w]_{S}\|^{2}_{S}+h_{S}^{-1}\|[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}w]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|^{2}_{S}\big),

where we utilize that
w
and
\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}w
only jump across faces
F\in\mathcal{F}
on the skeleton
\partial\mathcal{M}
. To establish (a), we add corrector functions in
C^{1}
conforming finite element spaces, whose degrees of freedom are known from
[
29
]
and can be written in integral form
[
21
, Appendix A.2]
. We prescribe the unique function
v_{1}\in P_{N}(\mathcal{T})\cap V
with
N=\max\{\ell+8,m+9,n+7\}
by setting the degrees of freedom (5)–(7)
in
[
21
, Appendix A.2]
via

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{T}(v_{1}-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}(v_{0}-w_{\mathcal{M}}))p\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{F}(v_{1}-\Pi_{\Sigma}^{m}(v_{0}-w_{\Sigma})|_{F})q\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{F}(\nabla v_{1}\cdot\nu_{F}-\Pi_{\Sigma}^{n}(\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}-\delta_{\Sigma})|_{F})r\,\mathrm{d}s

for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
,
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
,
p\in P_{N-8}(T)
,
q\in P_{N-9}(F)
, and
r\in P_{N-7}(F)
,
while the remaining ones (1)–(4) are set to zero.
Here, the restriction of an
L^{2}
function on the skeleton
\partial\mathcal{M}
to a face
F
with
F\not\subset\partial\mathcal{M}
is defined as zero.
By equivalence of norms in finite dimensional spaces and inverse estimates,

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-4}\|v_{1}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{-2}\|\nabla v_{1}\|_{T}^{2}+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}v_{1}\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim h_{T}^{-4}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}(v_{0}-w_{\mathcal{M}})\|_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}\big(h_{F}^{-3}\|\Pi_{\Sigma}^{m}(v_{0}-w_{\Sigma})|_{F}\|_{F}^{2}+h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{\Sigma}^{n}(\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}-\delta_{\Sigma})|_{F}\|_{F}^{2}\big)

holds for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
. Given
K\in\mathcal{M}
, the sum of this over
T\in\mathcal{T}
,
T\subset K
with
h_{T}\approx h_{K}
implies

\displaystyle h_{K}^{-4}\|v_{1}\|_{K}^{2}+h_{K}^{-2}\|\nabla v_{1}\|_{K}^{2}+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}v_{1}\|_{K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{-4}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}(v_{0}-w_{\mathcal{M}})\|_{K}^{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\big(h_{S}^{-3}\|\Pi_{\Sigma}^{m}(v_{0}-w_{\Sigma})\|_{F}^{2}+h_{S}^{-1}\|\Pi_{\Sigma}^{n}(\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}-\delta_{\Sigma})\|_{S}^{2}\big).

The function
v\coloneqq v_{0}+v_{1}
satisfies (a) by design and (b) from (
2.3
)–(
2.3
) and the triangle inequality.
∎

Remark 2.2

(linearity)
.

. Furthermore,

3.1.
Discrete problem

We consider the ansatz space
V_{h}\coloneqq P_{k}(\mathcal{M})\times P_{k}(\Sigma(\Omega))\times P_{k-1}(\Sigma(\Omega))
with
k\geq 2
.
Given
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
, the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{h}v_{h}\in P_{k-2}(\mathcal{M})
of
v_{h}
is the unique solution to

\displaystyle(\Delta_{h}v_{h},\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\qquad=(v_{\mathcal{M}},\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{S\in\Sigma(\Omega)}\int_{S}\big(\gamma_{\Sigma}[\phi]_{S}-v_{\Sigma}[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

for any
\phi\in P_{k-2}(\mathcal{M})
. The discrete problem seeks the discrete solution
u_{h}=(u_{\mathcal{M}},u_{\Sigma},\beta_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
to (
1.2
) with

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\Delta_{h}u_{h},\Delta_{h}v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\big(h_{S}^{-3}(u_{K}-u_{\Sigma},v_{K}-v_{\Sigma})_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+h_{S}^{-1}(\nabla u_{K}\cdot\nu_{S}-\beta_{\Sigma},\nabla v_{K}\cdot\nu_{S}-\gamma_{\Sigma})_{L^{2}(S)}\big),

\displaystyle F(v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq(f,v_{\mathcal{M}})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for
u_{h}=(u_{\mathcal{M}},u_{\Sigma},\beta_{\Sigma}),v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
.
Here, we use the convention
u_{K}=u_{\mathcal{M}}|_{K}
for any
K\in\mathcal{M}
.
Note that
a_{h}
defines a scalar product in
V_{h}

[
23
]
.
Let
\|\bullet\|_{h}
denote the norm induced by
a_{h}
and
|\bullet|_{\mathrm{s}}\coloneqq\sqrt{\mathrm{s}_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)}
. In particular, (
1.3
) holds with equality.

Lemma 3.1

(equivalence of norms)
.

Any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{h}v_{h}-\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}\|\lesssim|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}.

In particular, the norms
\|\bullet\|_{h}
and
\big(\|\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}\|^{2}+|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\big)^{1/2}
are equivalent.

Proof.

A piecewise integration by parts in (
3.1
)
implies

\displaystyle(\Delta_{h}v_{h}-\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}},\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}(\nu_{K}\cdot\nu_{S})\int_{S}\big((\gamma_{\Sigma}-\nabla v_{K}\cdot\nu_{S})\phi-(v_{\Sigma}-v_{K})\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi\cdot\nu_{S}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

This, the Cauchy inequality, and inverse estimates conclude the assertion. We mention that
\|\bullet\|_{h}
and
\big(\|\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}\|^{2}+|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\big)^{1/2}
are even locally equivalent.
∎

3.2.
Smoothing operator

Based on the construction of smoothing approximations in
Theorem

2.1
, we obtain the subsequent result.

Lemma 3.2

(smoothing operator)
.

There exists a linear bounded operator
\mathrm{J}_{h}:V_{h}\to V
such that any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

(consistent weights)
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}=v_{\mathcal{M}}
,
\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k}\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}=v_{\Sigma}
,
\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k-1}\nabla\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}=\gamma_{\Sigma}
.

(ii)

(approximation property) It holds

\displaystyle\|h_{\mathcal{M}}^{-2}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|+\|h_{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|+\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|\lesssim|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}.

Proof.

Given
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
,
we employ
Theorem

2.1
with
w=v_{\mathcal{M}}
,
w_{\mathcal{M}}=v_{\mathcal{M}}
,
w_{\Sigma}=v_{\Sigma}
,
\delta_{\Sigma}=\gamma_{\Sigma}
,
\ell=m=k
,
n=k-1
to obtain
\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}=v
with (i) and, from
Theorem

2.1
(b),

\displaystyle\|h_{\mathcal{M}}^{-2}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|^{2}+\|h_{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{S\in\Sigma}\big(h_{S}^{-3}\|[v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\|_{S}^{2}+h_{S}^{-1}\|[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|_{S}^{2}\big)+|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}

.

Since
[v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}=[v_{\mathcal{M}}-v_{\Sigma}]_{S}
and
[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}=[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nu_{S}-\gamma_{\Sigma}]_{S}
for any
S\in\Sigma
, this and a triangle inequality conclude (ii) and thus, the boundedness of the operator
\mathrm{J}_{h}
.
∎

The weights in
Lemma

3.2
provide full discrete consistency as follows.

Lemma 3.3

(discrete consistency)
.

Any

The additional term
b_{h}
in DG methods can be controlled as follows.

Lemma 4.7

(quasi-optimal remainder)
.

Any

and

\displaystyle|b_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}v,w_{h})|\lesssim\|w_{h}\|_{h}\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(v-\mathrm{I}_{h}v)\|.

Proof.

The Cauchy inequality in (

) proves

\displaystyle|b_{h}(\mathrm{I}_{h}v,w_{h})|\leq|\mathrm{I}_{h}v|_{\mathrm{s}}\Big(\sum_{S\in\Sigma}h_{S}^{3}\|\{\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\Delta_{h}w_{h}-\theta\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}w_{h}\}_{S}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle+h_{S}\|\{\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}w_{h}-\theta\Delta_{h}w_{h}\}_{S}\|^{2}_{S}

\displaystyle\Big)^{1/2}.

The triangle inequality, the discrete trace inequality, the inverse estimate, the boundedness of
\Delta_{h}
from
Lemma

4.1
, and
Lemma

4.4
conclude the assertion.
∎

Theorem 4.8

5.
Hybrid high-order

This section derives quasi-optimal error estimates for the HHO FEM of
[

]

5.1.
Discrete problem

We consider the ansatz space
V_{h}\coloneqq P_{k}(\mathcal{M})\times P_{k}(\Sigma(\Omega))\times P_{k-2}(\Sigma(\Omega))
with
k\geq 2
. Given
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
, the reconstruction
\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
of
v_{h}
is the unique solution to
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}v_{\mathcal{M}}
and, for any
\phi\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
,

\displaystyle(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}

\displaystyle,\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},\Delta^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(5.1)

\displaystyle-\sum_{S\in\Sigma(\Omega)}\int_{S}\big(v_{\Sigma}[\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi]_{S}-\gamma_{\Sigma}[\partial_{\nu\nu}\phi]_{S}-\partial_{t}v_{\Sigma}[\partial_{\nu t}\phi]_{S}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Here and throughout the remaining parts of this section,
\partial_{\nu}
and
\partial_{t}
denote the normal and tangential derivatives.
The discrete problem seeks the solution
u_{h}=(u_{\mathcal{M}},u_{\Sigma},\beta_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
to (
1.2
) with

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h},\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}h_{S}^{-3}(u_{K}-u_{\Sigma},v_{K}-v_{\Sigma})_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+h_{S}^{-1}(\Pi_{S}^{k-2}(\nabla u_{K}\cdot\nu_{S}-\beta_{\Sigma}),\Pi_{S}^{k-2}(\nabla v_{K}\cdot\nu_{S}-\gamma_{\Sigma}))_{L^{2}(S)},

\displaystyle F_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq(f,v_{\mathcal{M}})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
.
The discrete bilinear form
a_{h}
defines a scalar product in
V_{h}

[
13
]
and, therefore, (
1.3
) holds with equality for the induced norm
\|\bullet\|_{h}\coloneqq\sqrt{a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)}
.
By
|\bullet|_{\mathrm{s}}\coloneqq\sqrt{\mathrm{s}_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)}
, we denote the seminorm induced by
\mathrm{s}_{h}
.

Lemma 5.1

(equivalence of norms)
.

Any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}-v_{\mathcal{M}})\|\lesssim|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}.

In particular, the norms
\|\bullet\|_{h}
and
\big(\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}\|^{2}+|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\big)^{1/2}
are equivalent.

Proof.

The assertion follows from a piecewise integration by parts formula
[
13
, Eq. (19)]
, the discrete trace inequality, and inverse estimates, cf. also
[
13
, Lemma 4.1]
.
∎

5.2.
Smoothing operator

We propose the following smoother based on
Theorem

2.1
for discrete consistency.

Lemma 5.2

(smoothing operator)
.

There exists a linear bounded operator
\mathrm{J}_{h}:V_{h}\to V
such that any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

(consistent weights)
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}=v_{\mathcal{M}}
,
\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k}\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}=v_{\Sigma}
,
\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k-2}\nabla\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}\cdot\nu_{\Sigma}=\gamma_{\Sigma}
.

(ii)

(approximation property) It holds

\displaystyle\|h_{\mathcal{M}}^{-2}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|^{2}+\|h_{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{S\in\Sigma}h_{S}^{-1}\|[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|^{2}_{S}+|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}

.

Proof.

The properties (i)–(ii) follow from
Theorem

2.1
with
w=v_{\mathcal{M}}
,
w_{\mathcal{M}}=v_{\mathcal{M}}
,
w_{\Sigma}=v_{\Sigma}
,
\delta_{\Sigma}=\gamma_{\Sigma}
,
\ell=m=k
, and
n=k-2
. To prove the boundedness of
\mathrm{J}_{h}
, we employ the Poincaré inequality to infer, for any
S\in\Sigma
, that

\displaystyle\|[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\Pi_{S}^{0})[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\Pi_{S}^{0}[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{S}^{2}\|[\partial_{\nu t}v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\|^{2}_{S}+\|\Pi_{S}^{k-2}[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S}\|^{2}_{S}.

The second term on the right hand side is equal to
\|\Pi_{S}^{k-2}[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\nu_{S}-\gamma_{\Sigma}]_{S}\|_{S}^{2}
and is controlled by the stabilization via a triangle inequality. Therefore, we deduce from (ii) and the discrete trace inequality that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|\lesssim\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}v_{\mathcal{M}}\|+|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}.

This and
Lemma

5.1
conclude the assertion.
∎

In contrast to the numerical methods analyzed so far, the weights in
Lemma

5.2
(i) do not allow for full discrete consistency, but rather a weaker version of it.

Lemma 5.3

(discrete consistency)
.

Any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma},\gamma_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
,
w_{h}\in V_{h}
, and
\phi\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
satisfy

\displaystyle|(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}),\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|\lesssim\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\phi-\mathrm{J}_{h}w_{h})\|\big(|v_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|\big).

Proof.

From an integration by parts and the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{h}
in (
5.1
), we deduce that
(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}),\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is equal to

\displaystyle(v_{\mathcal{M}}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h},\Delta^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}

\displaystyle\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\sum_{S\in\Sigma(\Omega)}\int_{S}\big((v_{\Sigma}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})[\partial_{\nu}\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi]_{S}

\displaystyle-(\gamma_{\Sigma}-\partial_{\nu}\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})[\partial_{\nu\nu}\phi]_{S}-\partial_{t}(v_{\Sigma}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})[\partial_{\nu t}\phi]_{S}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

The weights in
Lemma

5.2
(i) show that the first three terms vanish. Hence,

\displaystyle(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}),\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=-\sum_{S\in\Sigma(\Omega)}\int_{S}\partial_{t}(v_{\Sigma}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})[\partial_{\nu t}\phi]_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
[\partial_{\nu t}\mathrm{J}_{h}w_{h}]_{S}
vanishes along any face
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
,
the discrete trace inequality shows

\displaystyle|(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}),\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\phi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|\leq\sum_{S\in\Sigma}\|\partial_{t}(v_{\Sigma}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|_{S}\|[\partial_{\nu t}(\phi-\mathrm{J}_{h}w_{h})]_{S}\|_{S}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\phi-\mathrm{J}_{h}w_{h})\|\Big(\sum_{S\in\Sigma}h_{S}^{-1}\|\partial_{t}(v_{\Sigma}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|_{S}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

.

Inverse estimates, the triangle inequality, and
Lemma

5.2
provide

\displaystyle h_{S}^{-1}\|\partial_{t}(v_{\Sigma}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h})\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{S}^{-3}\|v_{\Sigma}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}\|_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{S}^{-3}\big(\|v_{\Sigma}-\{v_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S}\|_{S}^{2}+\|\{v_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S}-\mathrm{J}_{h}v_{h}\|_{S}^{2}\big).

The combination of the two previously displayed formula with the trace and Poincaré inequality concludes the proof.
∎

5.5.
Conclusions

We derive a priori error estimates for nonconforming polytopal methods with Lehrenfeld-Schöberl typed stabilizations for the biharmonic equation under minimal regularity assumptions. Our analysis has impact beyond a priori error control: As a byproduct, we establish that the stabilization is an efficient contribution in a posteriori error estimators. Furthermore, lower-order error estimates allow for a corresponding error analysis of the eigenvalue problem.
