Fast algorithms for interpolation with clamped L L -splines of order four
O. Kounchev, H. Render, G. Simeonov, Ts. Tsachev

Abstract
Interpolation and smoothing using cubic and generalized splines are fundamental tools in data analysis and statistical modeling. Recently, fast computational algorithms were developed for natural L L -splines of order four, which arise as piecewise solutions to the differential operator L ξ 2 = ( d 2 d ​ t 2 − ξ 2 ) 2 L_{\xi}^{2}=(\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}-\xi^{2})^{2} . In this paper, we extend this mathematical framework to the important case of clamped (or complete) boundary conditions, where the first derivatives at the interval endpoints are prescribed. We explicitly construct the governing linear system for the interpolation problem and mathematically prove that the resulting tridiagonal matrix is strictly row diagonally dominant, thereby guaranteeing its invertibility and the numerical stability of the fast algorithm. The proposed method is implemented in MATLAB. Furthermore, the developed clamped L L -splines provide a foundation for constructing multivariate clamped polysplines, which serve as a promising alternative to Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for solving partial differential equations in Mathematical Physics.



Keywords:

L
-splines, exponential splines, spline interpolation, clamped boundary conditions, fast algorithms, strictly diagonally dominant matrices.

Fast interpolation for natural
L_{\xi}^{2}-
splines - a recap

Suppose that
g\in C^{2}\left[t_{1},t_{n}\right]
is an
L_{\xi}^{2}
-spline. In order to compute the
L_{\xi}^{2}
-spline
g
explicitly one has
to solve for each interval
\left[t_{j},t_{j+1}\right]
with

j=1,....,n-1,
the boundary value problem
L_{\xi}^{2}w=0
with

w\left(t_{j}\right)=g_{j},\ w\left(t_{j+1}\right)=g_{j+1},\text{ and
}L_{\xi}w\left(t_{j}\right)=L_{\xi}g\left(t_{j}\right),\ L_{\xi}w\left(t_{j+1}\right)=L_{\xi}g\left(t_{j+1}\right).

Thus, in order to compute the natural
L_{\xi}^{2}
-spline
g
interpolating
the data
z_{j}=g\left(t_{j}\right)
for
j=1,...,n
, we need the
knowledge of the two vectors

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}^{T}

\displaystyle:=\left(g\left(t_{1}\right),g\left(t_{2}\right),...,g\left(t_{n}\right)\right)

(1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}^{T}

\displaystyle:=\left(L_{\xi}g\left(t_{1}\right),...,L_{\xi}g\left(t_{n}\right)\right).

(2)

In case of a natural interpolating
L_{\xi}^{2}-
spline we know
\mathbf{g}^{T}
and
L_{\xi}g\left(t_{1}\right)=L_{\xi}g\left(t_{n}\right)=0.

For this reason in
[
7
]
the vector

\gamma^{T}:=\left(L_{\xi}g\left(t_{2}\right),L_{\xi}g\left(t_{3}\right),...,L_{\xi}g\left(t_{n-1}\right)\right)

is considered. When dealing with clamped
L_{\xi}^{2}-
splines we know

\mathbf{g}^{T}
and the boundary conditions
g^{\prime}\left(t_{1}\right)=d_{1}
and
g^{\prime}\left(t_{n}\right)=d_{n}.

In
[
7
]
we proved the following:

Proposition 1

Let
t_{1}<....<t_{n}
and
g
an
L_{\xi}^{2}-
spline. Then

g
restricted to the interval
\left(t_{j},t_{j+1}\right)
for

j=1,...,n-1
is equal to

\psi_{j}\left(t\right):=L_{\xi}g\left(t_{j}\right)A_{j}^{\left[1\right]}\left(t\right)+L_{\xi}g\left(t_{j+1}\right)B_{j}^{\left[1\right]}\left(t\right)+g\left(t_{j}\right)A_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right)+g\left(t_{j+1}\right)B_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right)

(3)

where

A_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right)=\frac{\sinh\xi\left(t-t_{j+1}\right)}{\sinh\xi\left(t_{j}-t_{j+1}\right)}\text{ and }B_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right)=\frac{\sinh\xi\left(t-t_{j}\right)}{\sinh\xi\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right)},

(4)

and

A_{j}^{\left[1\right]}\left(t\right)=\frac{\Phi_{\xi}\left(t-t_{j+1}\right)}{\psi_{\xi}\left(t_{j}-t_{j+1}\right)}-A_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right)\frac{\Phi_{\xi}\left(t_{j}-t_{j+1}\right)}{\psi_{\xi}\left(t_{j}-t_{j+1}\right)},

(5)

and

B_{j}^{\left[1\right]}\left(t\right)=\frac{\Phi_{\xi}\left(t-t_{j}\right)}{\psi_{\xi}\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right)}-\frac{\Phi_{\xi}\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right)}{\psi_{\xi}\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right)}B_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right)

(6)

Here we used the notations

\displaystyle\Phi_{\xi}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\xi^{3}}\left(\xi t\cosh\left(\xi t\right)-\sinh\left(\xi t\right)\right)

\displaystyle\psi_{\xi}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\xi}\sinh\xi t=L_{\xi}\Phi_{\xi}\left(t\right).

Theorem 4.1 in
[
7
]
states:

Theorem 2

Let
t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{n}
be real numbers. Assume that
g
is
natural
L_{\xi}^{2}-
spline interpolating the data
g_{1},...,g_{n}
. Then
there exists a
n\times\left(n-2\right)
matrix
Q
and a
\left(n-2\right)\times\left(n-2\right)
matrix
R
such that

Q^{T}\mathbf{g}=R\gamma.

(7)

Here
\mathbf{g}
and
\gamma
denote the vectors defined in (
1
) and
(
2
).

The matrices
R
and
Q
can be explicitly described:

Theorem 3

The matrix
R
is symmetric and tridiagonal and all entries on the diagonal
and the first off-diagonal are positive. These entries are given by

\displaystyle R_{j,j}

\displaystyle=\rho\left(t_{j}-t_{j-1}\right)+\rho\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right)\text{, }

\displaystyle R_{j,j+1}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right).

where
\rho\left(t\right)
is defined by

\rho\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{4\xi}\frac{\sinh\left(2\xi t\right)-2t\xi}{\left(\sinh\xi t\right)^{2}}.

(8)

and
\sigma\left(t\right)

\sigma\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{2\xi}\frac{\xi t\cosh\left(\xi t\right)-\sinh\left(\xi t\right)}{\sinh^{2}\left(\xi t\right).}

(9)

Theorem 4

The elements of the
n\times\left(n-2\right)
matrix
Q
are
given, for
i=1,...,n
and
j=2,3,...,n-1,
by the formulas

q_{j-1,j}=\frac{\xi}{\sinh\xi\left(t_{j}-t_{j-1}\right)},\qquad q_{j+1,j}=\frac{\xi}{\sinh\xi\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right)}

(10)

and

q_{jj}=-\xi\left(\frac{\cosh\xi\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right)}{\sinh\xi\left(t_{j+1}-t_{j}\right)}+\frac{\cosh\xi\left(t_{j}-t_{j-1}\right)}{\sinh\xi\left(t_{j}-t_{j-1}\right)}\right).

The rest of the elements in
Q
are zero.

The arguments can be reversed to construct a natural
L_{\xi}^{2}
-spline
interpolating given data
z=\left(z_{1},...,z_{n}\right)^{T}

now outline:

Step 1

Set
g_{i}=z_{i}
for
i=1,..n,

Step 2

Set
x=Q^{T}\mathbf{g}
and solve
R\gamma=x
.

Then the natural cubic
L_{\xi}^{2}
-spline
g
interpolating the data
z
is
then defined on each interval
\left(t_{j},t_{j+1}\right)
by

\psi_{j}\left(t\right):=\gamma_{j}A_{j}^{\left[1\right]}\left(t\right)+\gamma_{j+1}B_{j}^{\left[1\right]}\left(t\right)+g_{j}A_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right)+g_{j+1}B_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right).

3
The main results

In this section we want to prove analogues of the results in Section
2
for
clamped
L_{\xi}^{2}-
splines. As outlined in the last section, the
L_{\xi}^{2}-
spline
g(\cdot)
coincides on the interval
(t_{j},t_{j+1})
with the
function

\psi_{j}\left(t\right):=L_{\xi}g\left(t_{j}\right)A_{j}^{\left[1\right]}\left(t\right)+L_{\xi}g\left(t_{j+1}\right)B_{j}^{\left[1\right]}\left(t\right)+g\left(t_{j}\right)A_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right)+g\left(t_{j+1}\right)B_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t\right),

(11)

Since in (
11
) the numbers
g(t_{j})=g_{j}
for
j=1,2,\dots,n
are
given, generating the spline
g(\cdot)
reduces to finding the numbers

L_{\xi}g(t_{j})
for
j=1,2,\dots,n
. For ease of notation we denote

\gamma_{j}:=L_{\xi}g(t_{j})\text{ for }j=1,2,\dots,n

and, thus, formula (
11
) transforms to

\psi_{j}(t)=\gamma_{j}A_{j}^{[1]}(t)+\gamma_{j+1}B_{j}^{[1]}(t)+g_{j}A_{j}^{[2]}(t)+g_{j+1}B_{j}^{[2]}(t).

(12)

We next derive a system of
n
linear algebraic equations in
n
unknowns, in
which
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
are the unknowns and
\{g_{j}:=g(t_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}
are given. From the continuity conditions at the knots

t_{2}<t_{3}<\dots<t_{n-1}
of
g^{\prime}
we obtain the equalities

g^{\prime}(t_{j}-0)=g^{\prime}(t_{j}+0)\quad\left(\text{i.e. }\quad\psi_{j-1}^{\prime}(t_{j})=\psi_{j}^{\prime}(t_{j})\right)

for
j=2,3,\dots,n-1
. From the last and from (
12
) we obtain the
following
n-2
equations:

\displaystyle\gamma_{j-1}\frac{d}{dt}A_{j-1}^{[1]}(t_{j})+\gamma_{j}\frac{d}{dt}B_{j-1}^{[1]}(t_{j})+g_{j-1}\frac{d}{dt}A_{j-1}^{[2]}(t_{j})+g_{j}\frac{d}{dt}B_{j-1}^{[2]}(t_{j})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{j}\frac{d}{dt}A_{j}^{[1]}(t_{j})+\gamma_{j+1}\frac{d}{dt}B_{j}^{[1]}(t_{j})+g_{j}\frac{d}{dt}A_{j}^{[2]}(t_{j})+g_{j+1}\frac{d}{dt}B_{j}^{[2]}(t_{j})

for
j=2,3,\dots,n-1
. These equations may be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle\gamma_{j-1}\frac{d}{dt}A_{j-1}^{[1]}(t_{j})+\gamma_{j}\left(\frac{d}{dt}B_{j-1}^{[1]}(t_{j})-\frac{d}{dt}A_{j}^{[1]}(t_{j})\right)-\gamma_{j+1}\frac{d}{dt}B_{j}^{[1]}(t_{j})

\displaystyle=-g_{j-1}\frac{d}{dt}A_{j-1}^{[2]}(t_{j})+g_{j}\left(\frac{d}{dt}A_{j}^{[2]}(t_{j})-\frac{d}{dt}B_{j-1}^{[2]}(t_{j})\right)+g_{j+1}\frac{d}{dt}B_{j}^{[2]}(t_{j})

for
j=2,\dots,n-1
.

The
clamped boundary
conditions
\psi_{1}^{\prime}(t_{1})=g^{\prime}(t_{1})=d_{1}
and
\psi_{n-1}^{\prime}(t_{n})=g^{\prime}(t_{n})=d_{2}
give
two additional equations:

\gamma_{1}\frac{d}{dt}A_{1}^{[1]}(t_{1})+\gamma_{2}\frac{d}{dt}B_{1}^{[1]}(t_{1})=-g_{1}\frac{d}{dt}A_{1}^{[2]}(t_{1})-g_{2}\frac{d}{dt}B_{1}^{[2]}(t_{1})+d_{1},

(13)

and

\gamma_{n-1}\frac{d}{dt}A_{n-1}^{[1]}(t_{n})+\gamma_{n}\frac{d}{dt}B_{n-1}^{[1]}(t_{n})=-g_{n-1}\frac{d}{dt}A_{n-1}^{[2]}(t_{n})-g_{n}\frac{d}{dt}B_{n-1}^{[2]}(t_{n})+d_{2}

(14)

From above three equations we
obtain a linear system of
n
equations in
n
unknowns
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{n}
which we want to write in matrix form of the type

\widetilde{R}\mathbf{\widetilde{\mathbf{\gamma}}}^{T}=\widetilde{Q}\mathbf{g}^{T}+\mathbf{d}^{T}\text{ for }\mathbf{d}=\underset{n}{\underbrace{(d_{1},0,...,0,d_{2}})}

where
\mathbf{\widetilde{\mathbf{\gamma}}}=\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{n}\}
is the vector of the unknowns and
\mathbf{g}=\{g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n}\}
are the given data. For convenience define
as in

[
7
]

\widetilde{R}_{j,j-1}=\frac{d}{dt}A_{j-1}^{[1]}(t_{j}),\quad\widetilde{R}_{j,j}=\frac{d}{dt}B_{j-1}^{[1]}(t_{j})-\frac{d}{dt}A_{j}^{[1]}(t_{j}),\quad\widetilde{R}_{j,j+1}=-\frac{d}{dt}B_{j}^{[1]}(t_{j})

for
j=2,\dots,n-1
. The
n\times n
matrix
\widetilde{R}
is defined by

\widetilde{R}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccccc}\frac{d}{dt}A_{1}^{[1]}(t_{1})&\frac{d}{dt}B_{1}^{[1]}(t_{1})&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
\widetilde{R}_{2,1}&\widetilde{R}_{2,2}&\widetilde{R}_{2,3}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&\widetilde{R}_{n-1,n-2}&\widetilde{R}_{n-1,n-1}&\widetilde{R}_{n-1,n}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&\frac{d}{dt}A_{n-1}^{[1]}(t_{n})&\frac{d}{dt}B_{n-1}^{[1]}(t_{n})\end{array}\right)

Obviously, the structure of the matrix
\widetilde{R}
is tridiagonal. Now we
express the right hand side of the equations by a
n\times n
-matrix

\widetilde{Q}
whose entries are defined by

\widetilde{Q}_{j-1,j}=-\frac{d}{dt}A_{j-1}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t_{j}\right)\text{ and }\widetilde{Q}_{j+1,j}=\frac{d}{dt}B_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t_{j}\right)

and

\widetilde{Q}_{jj}=\frac{d}{dt}A_{j}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t_{j}\right)-\frac{d}{dt}B_{j-1}^{\left[2\right]}\left(t_{j}\right).

Then it is easy to see that

\widetilde{Q}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccccc}-\frac{d}{dt}A_{1}^{[2]}(t_{1})&-\frac{d}{dt}B_{1}^{[2]}(t_{1})&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\widetilde{Q}_{2,1}&\widetilde{Q}_{2,2}&\widetilde{Q}_{2,3}&0&&0\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&\ddots&\widetilde{Q}_{n-1,n-2}&\widetilde{Q}_{n-1,n-1}&\widetilde{Q}_{n-1,n}\\
0&0&\cdots&0&-\frac{d}{dt}A_{n-1}^{[2]}(t_{n})&-\frac{d}{dt}B_{n-1}^{[2]}(t_{n})\end{array}\right)

The following is the main result of this paper:

Theorem 5

The matrix
\tilde{R}
is strictly (row) diagonal dominant, in particular invertible

Proof.
We show that
\widetilde{R}
is strictly diagonal dominant; recall that
a matrix
A=\{a_{i,j}\}_{i=1j=1}^{n\;\;\;\;n}
is called strictly (row)
diagonal dominant if
|a_{i,i}|>\sum_{i\neq j}|a_{i,j}|
for all

i=1,2,\dots,n
), see e.g.
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
. It is
proved in
[
7
]
(see the proof of Theorem

4.2
) that

\frac{d}{dt}B_{1}^{[1]}(t_{1})=-\sigma(t_{2}-t_{1}),\qquad\text{and }\qquad\frac{d}{dt}A_{1}^{[1]}(t_{1})=-\rho(t_{2}-t_{1})

(just before Theorem
4.3
). We also have
\sigma(x)>0
for
x>0
and

\rho(x)>0
for
x>0
. Since in the proof of Theorem
4.4
of

[
7
]
it is established that
\sigma(x)<\frac{1}{2}\rho(x)
for
x>0
, we obtain

\left|\frac{d}{dt}B_{1}^{[1]}(t_{1})\right|=\sigma(t_{2}-t_{1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(t_{2}-t_{1})<\rho(t_{2}-t_{1})=\left|\frac{d}{dt}A_{1}^{[1]}(t_{1})\right|.

Next we deal with the last row of the matrix
R
. Using formula (3.8) from

[
7
]
we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}A_{n-1}^{[1]}(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{\xi}^{\prime}(t_{n}-t_{n})}{\psi_{\xi}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})}-\frac{d}{dt}A_{n-1}^{[2]}(t_{n})\frac{\Phi_{\xi}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})}{\psi_{\xi}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})}=-\frac{\psi_{\xi}^{\prime}(t_{n}-t_{n})}{\psi_{\xi}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})}\frac{\Phi_{\xi}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})}{\psi_{\xi}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Phi_{\xi}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})}{\psi_{\xi}^{2}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})}=-\sigma(t_{n-1}-t_{n})=\sigma(t_{n}-t_{n-1})

(the second equality above follows from the proof of Theorem
4.2
of

[
7
]
). Also, we have the equality
\frac{d}{dt}B_{n-1}^{[1]}(t_{n})=\rho(t_{n}-t_{n-1})
(which follows from

[
7
]
– formula (3.3) and the proof of
Theorem
4.2
). Hence, again

\frac{d}{dt}A_{n-1}^{[1]}(t_{n})=\sigma(t_{n}-t_{n-1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(t_{n}-t_{n-1})<\rho(t_{n}-t_{n-1})=\frac{d}{dt}B_{n-1}^{[1]}(t_{n}).

So, we established the diagonal dominance for the first and for the last rows.
The respective dominance for the rows of
R
with numbers
2,3,\dots,n-1
is
established in
[
7
]
.

The strict (row) diagonal dominance implies that the matrix
R
is invertible,
by Theorem
6.1.10
in
[
5
]
.

Thus we obtain

\widetilde{\gamma}=\left(\widetilde{R}\right)^{-1}\left(\widetilde{Q}\mathbf{g}^{T}+\mathbf{d}^{T}\right)

and we may proceed as at the end of Section 2 for computing the
L-
spline

g\left(t\right)
.

The present
L-
splines may be used to create multivariate clamped polysplines, cf.
[

]
.
The last may be used as an alternative of the PINNs for solving equations of Mathematical
Physics.
