Abstract
This paper tackles the optimization of the point spread function (PSF) of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-borne multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) synthetic aperture radar (SAR) tomography systems. A swarm of UAV-borne synthetic aperture radar systems is deployed to image an area to obtain its height profile. To achieve a high-quality three-dimensional (3D) image of the scene, the point spread function has to exhibit low sidelobes. The heavy computations, required for image generation, are performed on the ground. To this end, the sensor data collected by the unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radars is offloaded in real time via a frequency division multiple access (FDMA) air-to-ground backhaul link. In this work, the unmanned aerial vehicle formation and the power allocated for offloading are jointly optimized for the minimization of the point spread function sidelobe levels. To this end, we propose a novel solution based on the particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm, which meets practical sensing and communication constraints. Our simulation results demonstrate that the proposed solution can significantly improve sidelobe suppression compared to several benchmark schemes.



I
Introduction

The deployment of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) swarms for remote sensing has garnered considerable attention in recent years. This growing interest has facilitated the broader application of unmanned aerial vehicles across various domains, including mapping, traffic monitoring, and climate change analysis. To support these applications, unmanned aerial vehicle platforms are equipped with a range of sensors, including optical cameras, LiDAR systems, and radar instruments. Notably, the integration of synthetic aperture radar (SAR) into unmanned aerial vehicle systems has emerged as a particularly promising option, as it enables the generation of high-resolution imagery of local areas, even under adverse environmental conditions, thereby overcoming the constraints associated with conventional airborne and spaceborne synthetic aperture radar platforms.

An important remote sensing application of unmanned aerial vehicle swarms is three-dimensional (3D) radar imaging.
In particular, tomographic SAR exploits a synthetic aperture in elevation to retrieve the vertical distribution of scatterers
[
13
]
. One key performance metric in tomographic SAR is the point spread function (PSF), which characterizes how a scatterer in elevation is mapped onto the reconstructed tomographic image
[
1
]
. Although the point spread function is well established in the radar literature for Fourier-based tomography, where typically far-field conditions and uniform platform spacing are assumed
[
16
]
, its extension to unmanned aerial vehicle-based systems with arbitrary platform formation has remained largely unexplored. The point spread function determines essential imaging properties, including tomographic resolution, height of ambiguity, and sidelobe levels, all of which directly influence the quality of the resulting image. In particular, the sidelobe levels quantify the extent to which energy from a strong scatterer leaks into adjacent elevation bins. Strong sidelobes can mask weaker scatterers, generate ghost targets, and ultimately degrade the interpretability of complex scenes, such as urban or volumetric environments
[
19
]
. Other relevant metrics for tomographic SAR include the signal-to-noise ratio and coverage
[
6
]
. While tomographic SAR has been extensively studied for spaceborne and airborne platforms
[
16
]
, the optimization of tomographic SAR performance for unmanned aerial vehicle-borne systems has remained unexplored. Furthermore, multiple-input multiple-output tomographic SAR offers several advantages over conventional repeat-pass tomographic SAR
[
16
]
, in which a single synthetic aperture radar platform performs multiple acquisitions of the same scene. Notably, it enables improved discrimination between single-bounce and double-bounce scattering mechanisms
[
6
]
and exhibits enhanced robustness to pronounced sidelobes
[
17
]
. The optimization of unmanned aerial vehicle-based interferometric synthetic aperture radar was considered in
[
7
,
9
,
8
]
. However, these results are not directly applicable here, as interferometric synthetic aperture radar relies on acquisition pairs. Consequently, it cannot recover the scatterer distribution along the elevation dimension and is therefore unable to resolve multiple scatterers within the same resolution cell. In contrast, tomographic SAR is capable of reconstructing the full three-dimensional image by using multiple acquisitions, thereby enabling the separation of scatterers located at different heights but projected onto the same resolution cell
[
15
]
.

In this work, we study a unmanned aerial vehicle-borne multiple-input multiple-output tomographic SAR system, where a swarm of unmanned aerial vehicles, which transmit and receive radar waveforms, offloads the collected radar data to a ground station to generate a three-dimensional image of an area. We investigate the joint optimization of the unmanned aerial vehicle formation and communication power allocation for minimization of the peak sidelobe level (PSL) of the point spread function under communication and sensing constraints. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

•

We jointly optimize the unmanned aerial vehicle formation and communication power allocation for minimization of the peak sidelobe level of the point spread function, while satisfying sensing and communication constraints.

•

We propose a particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm to solve the formulated optimization problem.

•

Our simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach compared to several benchmark schemes, achieving peak sidelobe levels between
-17
dB and
-33
dB.

III
Problem Formulation

In this paper, we aim at minimizing the peak sidelobe level of
\Sigma_{\rm n}(n)
by jointly optimizing the unmanned aerial vehicle formation
\mathcal{Q}=\{\mathbf{q}_{i},\forall i\in\mathcal{I}\}
and communication transmit powers
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathbf{P}_{i},\forall i\in\mathcal{I}\}
, while satisfying communication and sensing quality-of-service constraints. To this end, we formulate the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{P}):\;\min_{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{P}}\hskip 8.53581pt\mathrm{PSL}

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathrm{C1}:\;\Sigma_{\rm n}(0)\geq 1-\epsilon,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C2}:\;\delta_{n}\leq\delta_{n}^{\max},\delta_{r}\leq\delta_{r}^{\max},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C3}:\;z_{\mathrm{min}}\leq z_{i}\leq z_{\mathrm{max}},\forall i\in\mathcal{I},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C4}:\;\theta_{\mathrm{min}}\leq\theta_{i}\leq\theta_{\mathrm{max}},\forall i\in\mathcal{I},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C5}:\;b_{i,j}\geq d_{\mathrm{min}},\forall i,j\in\mathcal{I},i>j,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C6}:\;S_{i}\geq S_{\rm min},\forall i\in\mathcal{I},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C7}:\;\mathrm{SNR}_{i,j}\geq\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{min}},\forall i,j\in\mathcal{I},i\geq j,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C8}:\;0\leq P_{i,k}\leq P_{\mathrm{max}},\forall i\in\mathcal{I},\forall k\in\mathcal{K},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C9}:\;R_{i,k}\geq R_{\mathrm{min}},\forall i\in\mathcal{I},\forall k\in\mathcal{K},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C10}:\;E_{i}\leq E_{\rm max},\forall i\in\mathcal{I}.

Constraint
\mathrm{C1}
enforces that the value of the point spread function at the target location is close to
1
by choosing

, thereby ensuring distortion-free reconstruction of the target. Constraints
\mathrm{C2}
impose a maximum tomographic and slant-range resolutions, denoted by
\delta_{n}^{\max}
and
\delta_{r}^{\max}
, respectively. Constraint
\mathrm{C3}
defines the permissible flight altitude range, bounded by
z_{\mathrm{min}}
and
z_{\mathrm{max}}
. Constraint
\mathrm{C4}
imposes limits on the allowable look angle, specified by
\theta_{\rm min}
and
\theta_{\rm max}
.
Constraint
\mathrm{C5}
guarantees a minimum separation distance

between any pair of unmanned aerial vehicles. Constraint
\mathrm{C6}
enforces a minimum radar swath width
S_{\mathrm{min}}
. Constraint
\mathrm{C7}
ensures that both the monostatic and bi-static signal-to-noise ratios satisfy a minimum threshold
\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{min}}
. Constraint
\mathrm{C8}
limits the maximum communication transmit power to

. Constraint
\mathrm{C9}
ensures that each UAV

, achieves a minimum required communication rate

. Finally, constraint
\mathrm{C10}
restricts the total communication energy consumption of each UAV, defined as

.

is a challenging min-max optimization problem that is difficult to solve. In particular, the point spread function expression is difficult to optimize due to its complex structure (see (
1

2

,
\mathrm{C2}
,
\mathrm{C5}
,

IV
Solution of the Optimization Problem

Given the non-tractability, complexity, and non-convexity of problem
\mathrm{(P)}
, we approach it with stochastic optimization techniques. Motivated by the success of particle swarm optimization methods in handling high-dimensional and complex optimization problems, we propose a modified particle swarm optimization algorithm specifically tailored to solve problem
\mathrm{(P)}
.

IV-A

Particle Swarm Optimization

particle swarm optimization is a population-based stochastic optimization algorithm inspired by the collective behavior of biological swarms (e.g., flocks of birds)
[
4
]
. Thus, a population of size
O\in\mathbb{N}
explores the search space by iteratively updating a set of candidate solutions, also referred to as particles. In iteration
m\in\mathbb{N}
, each particle is represented by
\mathbf{p}^{(m)}=(p^{(m)}[1],\ldots,p^{(m)}[{D}])^{\rm T}\in\mathbb{R}^{{D}}
, where
{D}
is the problem dimension. Each particle is updated using a velocity vector
\mathbf{v}^{(m)}=(v^{(m)}[1],\dots,v^{(m)}[{D}])^{\rm T}\in\mathbb{R}^{{D}}
as follows:

\mathbf{p}^{(m)}=\mathbf{p}^{(m-1)}+\mathbf{v}^{(m-1)},\quad m\geq 2,

(5)

where initial particles
\mathbf{p}^{(1)}
are uniformly distributed in
[\mathbf{p}^{\min},\mathbf{p}^{\max}]
, such that
\mathbf{p}^{\min},\mathbf{p}^{\max}\in\mathbb{R}^{{D}}
define the minimum and maximum boundary area for the particle swarm optimization algorithm
[
4
]
, respectively. The initial velocities are uniformly sampled as
\mathbf{v}^{(1)}\sim\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{0},v_{\text{PSO}}^{\rm max}\mathbf{1})
, where
v_{\text{PSO}}^{\rm max}
is the maximum particle velocity.
Particles are evaluated in each iteration using a predefined fitness function
F
, designed to meet the objective of problem
\mathrm{(P)}
. The particle velocity is updated in each iteration
m
as follows:

\mathbf{v}^{(m)}=w^{(m-1)}\mathbf{v}^{(m-1)}+c_{1}\mathbf{r}_{1}^{(m)}\odot\left(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best}}^{(m-1)}-\mathbf{p}^{(m-1)}\right)+c_{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}^{(m)}\odot\left(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best}}^{(m-1)}-\mathbf{p}^{(m-1)}\right),

(6)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
denote the cognitive and social learning
factors, respectively.
\mathbf{r}_{1}^{(m)}
and

\mathbf{r}_{2}^{(m)}
are vectors whose elements are independently
drawn from a uniform distribution over
[0,1]
,
w^{(m-1)}\in[0,1]
is the linearly
decreasing inertia weight,
\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best}}^{(m-1)}

denotes the particle’s previous best-known position, and

\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best}}^{(m-1)}
represents the global
best-known position
[
4
]
.
In this work, we adopt a reflecting-wall mechanism and set the search boundaries based on constraints
\mathrm{C3}
and
\mathrm{C4}
to ensure their feasibility and to accelerate convergence as described in
[
9
]
. Readers are referred to
[
4
]
for further details about the implementation of the particle swarm optimization algorithm.

\mathbf{P}^{*}_{i}=(\eta_{i,1},\ldots,\eta_{i,K})^{\rm T},\quad\forall i.

(7)

Therefore, the constraints
\rm C8
,
\rm C9
, and
\rm C10
are feasible if and only if the following constraints are met:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{C11:}\ \eta_{i,k}\leq P_{\rm max},&\forall k\in\mathcal{K},\forall i\in\mathcal{I},\\
\mathrm{C12:}\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}\eta_{i,k}\leq\frac{E_{\rm max}}{\delta_{\rm t}},&\forall i\in\mathcal{I},\end{cases}

(8)

In other words, It suffices to check if the values
\eta_{i,k}
satisfy constraints
\rm C11
and
\rm C12
. If the constraints are met,
\eta_{i,k},\forall i,\forall k
is assigned as the solution to problem
(\rm P^{\prime})
. Otherwise, the difference from
P_{\rm max}
is added to the fitness function as shown in (
9
). Based on this formulation,
the particle swarm optimization search space can be reduced such that each particle encodes only the unmanned aerial vehicle positions, i.e.,
\mathcal{Q}
, while the power allocation
\mathcal{P}
is determined separately. In other words, in iteration
m
, a particle is given by
\mathbf{p}^{(m)}=\left(\mathbf{q}^{\mathrm{T}}_{1,m},\ldots,\mathbf{q}^{\mathrm{T}}_{I,m}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2I}
, where
\mathbf{q}_{i,m}
is the candidate across-track position of
U_{i}
. Therefore, for a candidate unmanned aerial vehicle formation selected by
\mathbf{p}^{(m)}
, the power allocation
\mathcal{P}^{*}=\{\mathbf{P}^{*}_{i},\forall i\}
is computed based on (
7
) and used to evaluate
\mathbf{p}^{(m)}
using the following non-parameterized fitness function
[
11
]
:

F(\mathbf{p}^{(m)}|\mathcal{P}^{*})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{PSL}(\mathbf{p}^{(m)}),&\text{if }\mathbf{p}^{(m)}\in\mathcal{F},\\
\mathrm{PSL}_{\rm max}+\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}}g_{l}(\mathbf{p}^{(m)}|\mathcal{P}^{*}),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(9)

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the feasible set,
\mathrm{PSL}_{\rm max}
is the worst peak sidelobe level across the population, and
g_{l},l\in\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}=\left\{1,2,4,5,6,7,11,12\right\}
, quantify constraint violations as follows:

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=[1-\epsilon-\Sigma_{\rm n}(0)]^{+},

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle=[\delta_{r}-\delta_{r}^{\rm max}]^{+}+[\delta_{n}-\delta_{n}^{\rm max}]^{+},

\displaystyle g_{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left([\theta_{\rm min}-\theta_{i}]^{+}+[\theta_{i}-\theta_{\rm max}]^{+}\right),

\displaystyle g_{5}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i>j}[d_{\rm min}-b_{i,j}]^{+},

\displaystyle g_{6}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}[S_{\rm min}-S_{i}]^{+},

\displaystyle g_{7}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{j\leq i}[\mathrm{SNR}_{\rm min}-\mathrm{SNR}_{i,j}]^{+},

\displaystyle g_{11}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{k=1}^{K}[\eta_{i,k}-P_{\rm max}]^{+},

\displaystyle g_{12}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left[\eta_{i,k}-\frac{E_{\rm max}}{\delta_{\rm t}}\right]^{+}.

VI
conclusion

In this work, we investigated a unmanned aerial vehicle-borne multiple-input multiple-output tomographic SAR system to produce three-dimensional image of an area by acquiring
I^{2}
independent observations from different angles. We formulated an optimization problem aimed at minimizing the peak sidelobe level of the point spread function, accounting for the relevant sensing and communication quality-of-service constraints, thereby enhancing the quality of the reconstructed image.. We proposed a modified particle swarm optimization-based approach that jointly optimizes the unmanned aerial vehicle formation as well as the communication power allocation, while fulfilling sensing and communication constraints. Simulation results showed that the proposed scheme significantly improves the peak sidelobe level suppression compared to several benchmark schemes.
