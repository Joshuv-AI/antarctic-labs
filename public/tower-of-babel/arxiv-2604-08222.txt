Free-Energy Minimizing Policies Under Generative Model Ambiguity
Arash Shafiei, Caio César Graciani Rodrigues, Giovanni Russo

Abstract
We present a variational free-energy formulation for distributionally robust decision-making with ambiguity in the generative model. The formulation, related to a broad range of learning and control frameworks, yields a minimax optimal control problem where maximization is over an uncertainty set that represents ambiguities. We prove that computing the optimal policy requires solving a non-convex minimization problem and propose an algorithm with convergence guarantees to find the solution. The effectiveness of our results is illustrated via simulations on a pendulum swing-up problem.



I
Introduction

The minimization of the variational free energy is central to a broad range of decision-making frameworks across learning, control and neuroscience
[
17
,
13
]
. These functionals consist of an expected cost and a term that quantifies the discrepancy from a generative model. Despite the success of these frameworks, free energy minimizing policies can be brittle to ambiguities in the generative model, arising from, e.g., mis-specifications. In this context, a key challenge is guaranteeing robustness of free energy minimizing policies against possibly adversarial uncertainties in the generative model. Motivated by this, we present a distributionally robust variational free-energy formulation for policy computation under generative model ambiguity. The formulation yields an optimal control problem involving a non-convex distributionally robust optimization (DRO) problem and we introduce an algorithm to compute its optimal solution.

Variational Free Energy

The variational free energy, see, e.g.,

\mathcal{F}(p(\mathbf{w}),q(\mathbf{w})):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(p(\mathbf{w})\mid\mid q(\mathbf{w})\right)+\mathbb{E}_{p}\left[l(\mathbf{W})\right],

(1)

where

,
5

Lemma II.2

given
q(\mathbf{w})

\underset{p(\mathbf{w})\in\mathcal{D}}{\min}\mathcal{F}(p(\mathbf{w}),q(\mathbf{w})),

with
\mathcal{F}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined in (

). Then:

•

the problem is convex;

•

the optimal solution is
p^{\ast}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{q(\mathbf{w})\exp\left(-l(\mathbf{w})\right)}{\int q(\mathbf{w})\exp\left(-l(\mathbf{w})\right)d\mathbf{w}}
;

•

the optimal value is

III
Problem statement

Let
\mathbf{X}_{k}\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
be the state and
\mathbf{U}_{k}\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{m}
the control input at time-step
k
. The possibly nonlinear and stochastic dynamics for the system is
p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
and we use the shorthand notation
p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}
to denote this probability. We also let
p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}:=p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)=p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
, where
p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
is a randomized policy. We use
p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}
to denote this policy.

Remark III.1

p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
describes the behavior of the system at time-step
k
given
\mathbf{x}_{k-1}
and
\mathbf{u}_{k}\sim p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
. The joint
p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
captures in probabilistic terms the closed loop interactions. With the control problem, formalized next, we aim to design the agent policy.

The policy is computed by minimizing, in the space of policies, the worst case variational free energy across a set capturing ambiguity on a given
\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}:=\bar{q}_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)\in\mathcal{D}
serving as nominal generative model in our formulation. To formally state the problem, we first give the following:

Definition III.1

let
q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\coloneqq q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\eta_{k}\left(\mathbf{x}_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_{k}\right)
be a positive and bounded function. The ambiguity set associated with
\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\in\mathcal{D}
is

\mathcal{B}_{\eta}\left(\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\right)\coloneqq\left\{q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\in\mathcal{D}:{D}_{\text{KL}}\left(q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\mid\mid\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\right)\right.\\
\leq\eta_{k}\left(\mathbf{x}_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_{k}\right),\left.\support{p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}}\subseteq\support{q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}}\right\}.

(2)

Remark III.2

\mathcal{B}_{\eta}\left(\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\right)
is the set of
q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)\in\mathcal{D}
that: (i) have statistical complexity, i.e., KL-divergence, of at most
\eta_{k}\left(\mathbf{x}_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_{k}\right)
from
\bar{q}_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
; (ii) have a support that covers the support of
p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
.

We are now ready to state our control problem:

Problem III.1

let:

1.

p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\coloneqq p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
;

2.

q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}\coloneqq q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)=q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}
, with
q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\coloneqq q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
and
q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}\coloneqq q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
;

find
p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u),\star}\coloneqq p^{\star}_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
such that

p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u),\star}\in\text{arg }\underset{p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}\in\mathcal{D}}{\min}\ \ \underset{q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\in\mathcal{B}_{\eta}\left(\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\right)}{\max}\ \mathcal{F}\left(p_{{k}\mid{k-1}},q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}\right),

(3)

where:
(i)
\mathcal{F}(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined as in (
1
) with
l(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{u}_{k})=c_{k}^{(x)}\left(\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)+c_{k}^{(u)}\left(\mathbf{u}_{k}\right)
; (ii)
\mathcal{B}_{\eta}\left(\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\right)
is in Definition
III.1
.

Remark III.3

in Problem
III.1
, the loss function in the free energy is a cost, which is possibly non-convex in states/actions. Minimizing the KL divergence term amounts at minimizing the discrepancy between
p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
and the generative model. This model does not need to be a time-series model and can simply capture a desired configuration. In this case, by minimizing the KL divergence, the policy minimizes the error from the desired configuration.

Remark III.4

in Problem
III.1
, the constraint of the inner maximization problem captures the ambiguity around the generative model
q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
, which is not available.

[
32
]
, before introducing the results we briefly relate our Problem

with other decision-making frameworks.

Problem
III.1

And Other Decision-Making Frameworks

The objective of the problem in (
3
) can be written as

{D}_{\text{KL}}\left(p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}\mid\mid\tilde{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}\right)-\log\left(Z(q_{{k}\mid{k-1}})\right),

(4)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}\coloneqq\tilde{q}_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)\exp\left(-c_{k}^{(x)}\left(\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)-c_{k}^{(u)}\left(\mathbf{u}_{k}\right)\right)}{Z(q_{{k}\mid{k-1}})}

(5)

with

\displaystyle Z(q_{{k}\mid{k-1}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{U}}\int_{\mathcal{X}}q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}\exp\left(-c_{k}^{(x)}\left(\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)-c_{k}^{(u)}\left(\mathbf{u}_{k}\right)\right)d\mathbf{x}_{k}d\mathbf{u}_{k}

(6)

being the normalizer. Differently from
[
32
]
the normalizer depends on the decision variables of the optimization problem and therefore it cannot be dropped from the optimization. However, when there is no ambiguity, the ambiguity constraint is relaxed and the second term in (
4
) can be dropped from the resulting minimization problem in the policy space.

Next, we highlight some of the connections drawn in
[
32
]
between the relaxed minimization problem in the policy space and other frameworks; see
[
32
]
for details. In the special case where
q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)
is a maximally diffusive probability and
q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
is uniform, as shown in
[
32
]
, the relaxed problem formulation yields a cost functional that aligns with the maximum diffusion objective in the MaxDiff framework
[
3
]
. Therefore, in this special case, solving Problem
III.1
yields a distributionally robust maximum diffusion policy. This connects our problem statement to the MaxDiff framework and, through it, to MaxEnt. In fact, MaxDiff is known to generalize MaxEnt policies and inherit its desirable robustness properties
[
11
]
. Yet, to compute policies that robustly maximize a reward, MaxEnt must be used with a different/pessimistic objective. Moreover, the ambiguity set over which the policy is robust is not explicit but emerges from the entropy of the optimal policy; explicit bounds for the ambiguity set are only available for discrete variables. In Problem
III.1
, the ambiguity set is instead explicitly defined in the problem formulation, as a control requirement. Finally, when
c_{k}^{(x)}\left(\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)
is a cost-to-go, the objective in Problem
III.1
yields the expected free energy minimized to determine actions in the context of active inference from computational neuroscience
[
13
]
and this relates the formulation to other popular frameworks, such as risk-sensitive control, KL control, linear quadratic Gaussian regulator and control-as-inference.
In this sense, Problem
III.1
offers a generalization of active inference
[
13
]
accounting for ambiguity in the generative model.

IV
Main Results

First, we obtain a reformulation of Problem
III.1
and characterize its key properties. This yields a resolution strategy where, first the free energy is maximized across the ambiguity set and then minimized in the policy space. Solving the maximization problem requires solving an infinite dimensional non-convex problem and tailoring a Frank-Wolfe algorithm to solve this problem. For brevity, results are stated for continuous variables and we give a sketch of the proofs. Complete proofs will be presented elsewhere.

IV-A

Reformulating Problem
III.1

The chain rule for the KL divergence (Lemma
II.1
) implies that the KL divergence term in the cost functional of (
3

{D}_{\text{KL}}\left(p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)\mid\mid q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)\right)+\mathbb{E}_{p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)}\left[{D}_{\text{KL}}\left(p_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)\mid\mid q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}},\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\right)\right)\right]
. Thus, Problem

can be reformulated as

\underset{p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}\in\mathcal{D}}{\min}\left\{{D}_{\text{KL}}\left(p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}\mid\mid q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}\right)+\mathbb{E}_{p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}}\left[l(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{u}_{k})\right]\right.\\
\left.+\underset{q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\in\mathcal{B}_{\eta}\left(\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\right)}{\max}\mathbb{E}_{p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}}\left[{D}_{\text{KL}}\left(p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\mid\mid q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\right)\right]\right\}.

(7)

The outer minimization is over policies
p_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(u)}

q_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}

The next result shows that the feasibility set of the inner maximization is compact.

Lemma IV.1

pick any
\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\in\mathcal{D}
. The ambiguity set
\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)})
is compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Sketch of the Proof:
assume that
\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By definition of the ambiguity set, for any
C>1
,
\int_{\{q^{(x)}_{k\mid k-1}>C\}}q^{(x)}_{k\mid k-1}\leq\frac{\eta_{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})+\|\bar{q}^{(x)}_{k\mid k-1}\|_{\infty}}{\ln C}
, so the family of densities in
\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\bar{q}_{{k}\mid{k-1}}^{(x)})
is uniformly integrable.
By
[
4
, Theorem 4.7.20]
, any uniformly integrable subset of
L^{1}(\Omega)
whose defining constraints are closed is relatively compact in the
L^{1}(\Omega)
weak topology.
Since the KL-divergence constraint and
non-negativity constraint are closed, the ambiguity set is also weakly closed and therefore compact.

VI
Conclusion

We considered the problem of computing optimal policies under a free energy minimization framework that accounts for ambiguity in the generative model. This leads to a distributionally robust minimax formulation of the control problem. We showed that the optimal policy can be computed via a bi-level optimization approach, where the free energy is first maximized within the ambiguity set and then minimized in the policy space.
We then showed that the inner maximization can be recast as a DC program, and we adapted a Frank–Wolfe algorithm, providing convergence guarantees, to tackle the optimization. The effectiveness of the results was illustrated on a pendulum stabilization task. Based on these early results, future work includes: (i) embedding learning in policy computation; (ii) extending our results to include ambiguity on
q_{{k}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{{k}}\mid\mathbf{x}_{{k-1}}\right)
.
