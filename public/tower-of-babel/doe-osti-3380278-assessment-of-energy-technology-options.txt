 Presentation Purpose
The purpose of this presentation is to provide key takeaways of analysis conducted
by the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) and the Pacific Northwest National
Laboratory (PNNL) through the the Energy Technology Innovation Partnership
Project (ETIPP) in collaboration with a community team comprising Sustʻāinable
Molokai, the Molokai Clean Energy Hui, Hoʻāhu Energy Cooperative Molokai, and
Shake Energy.
The analysis is intended to advance three priority energy projects that were
identified through the Community Energy Resilience Action Plan (CERAP), building
from community-led energy planning and further supporting community decision-
making.
This presentation is intended for use by the community team to communicate
results of the analysis to stakeholders and community members. Further detail on
the project is provided in the final report, Assessment of Energy Technology Options
for the Island of Molokai (Rosenlieb et al., forthcoming).

 Task 1: Critical Infrastructure Objective
The objective of this task is to understand distributed
energy resource (DER) sizes for backup energy systems
and on-site generation for six critical facilities to survive an
outage of 14 days in duration. The facilities included in this
analysis are:
1. Kilohana Elementary School
2. Maunaloa Elementary School
3. Molokai High School
4. Kaunakakai Harbor
5. Molokai General Hospital
6. Molokai Airport.
Note: Investment Tax Credit (ITC) of 30% has been considered for these facilities. These analyses
were conducted in March 2025. Molokai High School PV deployment example. Source :
 Created with Google Earth by Jayaraj Rane
Most of the facilities have more than one meter connected to three different rate schedules. The analysis team built rate schedules to replicate these rates to reflect the energy
 consumption charges, demand charge, additional riders, and any fixed charges.

  Task 1: Critical Infrastructure Next Steps
Following are some next steps that the owners/operators and stakeholders can consider:
1. Achieve leadership and stakeholder buy-in with the owners and operators of the facilities.
2. Conduct a feasibility study with more detailed inputs and data (preferably with recorded load data) to validate
 the assumptions and determine accurate sizing.
3. Conduct a detailed static and dynamic study to understand the electrical infrastructure gaps for the facilities.
 The community can work with a developer or an engineering, procurement, construction (EPC) firm to conduct
 the necessary studies using power system modeling tools.
4. Coordinate with the utility company and understand interconnection requirements.
5. Determine if the physical infrastructure is suitable for these plans. A detailed power system study will help
 stakeholders understand what the current electrical infrastructure is and what needs to be upgraded. If rooftop
 deployment is considered, it is important to understand if the existing roof is suitable for the deployment. If a
 ground-mount system is considered, availability of space must be assessed.
6. The Federal Emergency Management Program (FEMP) has developed a Microgrid Procurement Checklist (U.S.
 Department of Energy n.d.) that highlights important stages of a microgrid’s development to help guide
 microgrid developers in their endeavors. The community team can refer to the document as a guideline for
 development of the independent projects for the facilities.
Source: U.S. Department of Energy. n.d. "Microgrid Procurement Checklist." Accessed January 9, 2026. https://www.energy.gov/femp/articles/microgrid-system-project-
development-checklist.

 Task 2: Floating Solar Results
• We estimate the evaporative losses from Kualapuu to
 be between 177 and 219 million gallons (MG) a year.
• Water delivery to end users of the Molokai Irrigation

 Task 2: Floating Solar Next Steps

1. Bathymetry study to understand the depth and shape of the reservoir floor,
 which will impact the position of floats and anchoring and mooring solutions
2. Geotechnical/hydrogeotechnical survey to evaluate geological and soil
 conditions both under and above water. The primary impact is to understand
 anchoring options and locations as different anchor types are adapted for
 fastening to various rock and soil types.
3. Hydrodynamic survey to understand water flows and currents within the
 reservoir, with impacts on location of float and the anchoring and mooring
 system
4. A topographic survey to understand the limitations and options for above-
 ground parts of the system such as cabling and a central inverter, if a central
 inverter is to be used.

  Task 3: Open Loop - Concepts Using
 Kaualapuu Reservoir
• Kualapuu Reservoir is used for open-loop conceptual development.
 ◦ Open-loop—Source of freshwater flowing into upper or lower
 reservoir
 ◦ Closed-loop—Isolated from freshwater sources.
• Advantages
 ◦ Repurpose existing water source
 ◦ Eliminates need to construct one reservoir
 ◦ Centrally located to island power requirements
 ◦ Significant water available for long-duration storage (10+ hours).
• Disadvantages
 ◦ Licensing requirements are more substantial, as one reservoir will be
 located in natural drainage.
 ◦ Kualapuu reservoir is located a significant distance from elevation
 gain, approximately 2 miles, requiring a long penstock. Image from Google Maps

 Task 3: Closed Loop - Prefabricated
 Tank Systems
• Modular System:
 ◦ Include upper and lower reservoir prefabricated tanks
 ◦ Incorporate a modular pump/turbine motor/generator
 ◦ Is suitable for mountainous terrain
 ◦ Requires water source for initial charging
 ◦ Can be rated and sized to suit needs.
• Advantages:
 ◦ Minimizes water use, fully contained, eliminating seepage and
 evaporation
 ◦ Eliminates need for sediment and debris removal
 ◦ Minimizes licensing requirements due to isolation from freshwater.
• Disadvantages:
 ◦ Closed reservoir is more costly.
 ◦ Reservoirs are not visually appealing but could be disguised. Prefabricated schematic

 Task 3: Pumped Storage Hydro Next Steps

1. Preliminary regulatory assessment: Perform a 1- to 2-month assessment to thoroughly define on-island
 regulatory requirements at federal, state, county, and local levels.
2. Energy demand and generation analysis: A 3- to 6-month assessment is suggested to evaluate grid load
 patterns, peak demand, seasonal variability, and energy charging (water pumping) methods to
 determine optimal PSH system sizing while validating Palaau generation can reliably serve as backup.
3. Site assessment and feasibility study: This phase defines the physical layout of all PSH components and
 conducts constructability, logistical, and cost-benefit evaluations to confirm the technical and economic
 feasibility of the proposed system.
4. Civil and hydraulic engineering analysis: Geotechnical, hydrological, and environmental studies—along
 with hydraulic modeling—will confirm suitable powerhouse and penstock locations, ensure structural
 safety, optimize flow performance, and identify cost-effective material choices.
5. Permitting, environmental, and cultural assessments: Over 3–6 months, comprehensive environmental
 and cultural studies will ensure regulatory compliance, protect Molokai’s escosystems and heritage, and
 address key permitting and logistical requirements for project approval.
