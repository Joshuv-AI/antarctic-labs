Back-Action-Evading Measurements and Quantum Non-Demolition Variables via Linear Systems Engineering
Zhiyuan Dong, Weichao Liang, Guofeng Zhang

Abstract
We establish a framework for realizing back-action-evading (BAE) measurements and quantum non-demolition (QND) variables in linear quantum systems. The key condition, a purely imaginary Hamiltonian with a real or imaginary coupling operator, enables BAE measurements of conjugate observables. Symmetric coupling further yields QND variables. For non-compliant systems, coherent feedback can engineer BAE measurements. Crucially, the QND interaction condition simultaneously ensures BAE measurements and promotes the coupling operator to a QND observable. This work provides a unified structural theory for enhancing precision in quantum metrology and sensing.



1
Introduction

In a typical indirect measurement within a quantum system
[
3
,
2
,
22
,
5
]
, a auxiliary quantum system, often termed a probe, is deliberately coupled to the system of interest. Information about the latter is inferred by performing a direct measurement on the probe after their interaction. In this framework, the probe serves as an input channel before the interaction and transforms into an output channel afterwards. A fundamental consequence of this information acquisition process is the introduction of measurement back-action, which inevitably disturbs the dynamics of the system of interest.

A key objective in quantum control and metrology is to identify and protect parts of a quantum system that are inherently immune to such measurement-induced disturbances. If a specific subsystem of a linear quantum system is inaccessible to the probe—meaning it is neither influenced by the input probe nor detectable in the output—it is termed a decoherence-free subsystem (DFS)
[
25
,
17
,
27
,
28
]
. Importantly, a DFS is not an isolated system; it interacts with other parts of the composite system. Its decoherence-free property implies that its state evolves unitarily and remains separable from the rest of the system’s degrees of freedom, provided the system-environment coupling respects a certain symmetry. In the Heisenberg picture, this concept is closely related to the decoherence-free subspaces in the Schrödinger picture
[
9
, Sec. III-C]
, which play a crucial role in protecting quantum information in quantum computation
[
19
]
.

While a DFS represents a structural immunity where certain system degrees of freedom are statically inaccessible to the probe, a more dynamical form of back-action evasion can be engineered at the level of input-output relationships. Specifically, a quantum back-action evading (BAE) measurement is achieved when a particular observable of the output probe, denoted
\boldsymbol{y}_{q}
, is insensitive to a specific conjugate observable of the input probe, denoted
\boldsymbol{u}_{p}
. In the language of linear systems and the Kalman canonical form, this condition corresponds to a zero transfer function from the input
\boldsymbol{u}_{p}
to the output
\boldsymbol{y}_{q}
. This frequency-domain characterization is consistent with the standard linear systems approach to quantum measurement theory, where transfer functions have been rigorously established as a valid tool for describing BAE conditions
[
24
, Fig. 2]
. In continuous-wave BAE experiments, the quantity of interest is typically the measured quadrature of the output signal
[
15
]
; a zero transfer function in the frequency domain guarantees that the back-action noise from input
\boldsymbol{u}_{p}
is evaded across all relevant frequencies. Quantum BAE measurements are therefore of paramount importance in quantum sensing and metrology
[
23
,
1
]
, as they enable precision beyond the standard quantum limit by selectively rendering the measurement immune to specific fluctuations of the input field.

The concept of back-action evasion focuses on the input-output relation—evading back-action from a specific input channel. A related but distinct paradigm concerns the system observable itself: the notion of quantum non-demolition (QND) measurements
[
6
]
. According to the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, the precision of simultaneous measurements of canonically conjugate observables (e.g., position and momentum, or amplitude and phase) is fundamentally limited. Consequently, an indirect measurement of an observable
\mathcal{O}
generally introduces back-action onto its conjugate counterpart. However, if the Heisenberg-picture evolution of
\mathcal{O}
is a function only of itself and is independent of its conjugate observables, then the measurement of
\mathcal{O}
does not perturb its own future evolution. This defines a QND measurement: an observable
\mathcal{O}
is a QND variable if it can be measured repeatedly, with the measurement back-action confined entirely to its conjugate variables, leaving the measured observable’s future trajectory unaffected
[
20
,
23
,
24
,
27
,
8
]
. While back-action evasion ensures that a specific output is free from a specific input noise, QND measurement ensures that the observable of interest is free from the measurement back-action generated by its own measurement.

This paper presents a systematic framework for realizing BAE measurements and identifying QND variables using linear systems engineering. By leveraging the structural properties of linear quantum systems, we unify the concepts of BAE and QND within a common state-space representation, providing both a theoretical foundation and practical design criteria for back-action evasion in continuous-mode measurement scenarios.

2
Preliminaries

The time evolution of a linear quantum system is governed by a unitary operator
\mathbf{U}(t,t_{0})
, which satisfies the following quantum stochastic differential equation (QSDE)

d\mathbf{U}(t,t_{0})=\left[\left(-\imath\mathbf{H}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{L}\right)dt-\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}Sd\mathbf{B}_{\rm in}(t)+d\mathbf{B}_{\rm in}^{\dagger}(t)\mathbf{L}+{\rm Tr}\left[(S-I)d\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\top}(t)\right]\right]\mathbf{U}(t,t_{0}),

(5)

where
\mathbf{U}(t_{0},t_{0})=I
.
S
denotes the scattering matrix,
\mathbf{L}
is the coupling operator with
\mathbf{L}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}&C_{+}\end{array}\right]\breve{\mathbf{a}}
, and
\mathbf{H}
is the system Hamiltonian
\mathbf{H}=\frac{1}{2}\breve{\mathbf{a}}^{\dagger}\Omega\breve{\mathbf{a}}
, where
\Omega=\Delta(\Omega_{-},\Omega_{+})
. The integrated input annihilation, creation, and gauge processes are

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\rm in}(t)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mathbf{b}_{\rm in}(s)ds,~~\mathbf{B}_{\rm in}^{\ast}(t)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mathbf{b}_{\rm in}^{\ast}(s)ds,~~\mathbf{\Lambda}(t)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mathbf{b}_{\rm in}^{\ast}(s)\mathbf{b}_{\rm in}(s)ds,

(6)

respectively, and satisfy the following commutation relation

\left[\mathbf{b}_{j}(t),\mathbf{b}^{\ast}_{k}(s)\right]=\delta_{jk}\delta(t-s).

In the Heisenberg picture, denote a system operator
\mathbf{X}(t)=\mathbf{U}^{\ast}(t,t_{0})(\mathbf{X}\otimes I_{\rm field})\mathbf{U}(t,t_{0})
, and its dynamical evolution can be described as

\displaystyle d\mathbf{X}(t)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{X}(t))dt+[\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}(t),\mathbf{X}(t)]Sd\mathbf{B}_{\rm in}(t)

(7)

\displaystyle+d\mathbf{B}_{\rm in}^{\dagger}(t)S^{\dagger}[\mathbf{X}(t),\mathbf{L}(t)]+{\rm Tr}\left[(S^{\dagger}\mathbf{X}(t)S-\mathbf{X}(t))d\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\top}(t)\right],

where the superoperator

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{X}(t))=-\imath[\mathbf{X}(t),\mathbf{H}(t)]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}(t)[\mathbf{X}(t),\mathbf{L}(t)]+\frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}(t),\mathbf{X}(t)]\mathbf{L}(t).

(8)

The input-output relation is given by

d\mathbf{B}_{\rm out}(t)=\mathbf{L}(t)dt+Sd\mathbf{B}_{\rm in}(t).

(9)

In the Schrödinger picture, the conditioned density operator
\rho_{c}
is governed by the following quantum stochastic master equation (QSME)

d\rho_{c}(t)=\mathcal{L}^{\star}(\rho_{c}(t))dt+\{\mathbf{L}^{\top}\rho_{c}(t)+\rho_{c}(t)\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}-{\rm Tr}[\rho_{c}(t)(\mathbf{L}^{\top}+\mathbf{L}^{\dagger})]\rho_{c}(t)\}d\nu(t),

(10)

where the superoperator
\mathcal{L}^{\star}(\rho_{c}(t))
is defined by

\mathcal{L}^{\star}(\rho_{c}(t))=-\imath[\mathbf{H},\rho_{c}(t)]+\mathbf{L}^{\top}\rho_{c}(t)\mathbf{L}^{\#}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{L}\rho_{c}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{c}(t)\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{L},

(11)

and the innovation process

d\nu(t)=d\mathbf{Q}_{\rm out}(t)-{\rm Tr}[\rho_{c}(t)(\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\#})]dt,

(12)

where
d\nu(t)d\nu^{\top}(t)=dt
, and
\mathbf{Q}_{\rm out}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathbf{B}_{\rm out}(t)+\mathbf{B}_{\rm out}^{\#}(t)\right)
being assumed to be observed continuously.

4
QND interaction

A QND interaction enables the repeated measurement of a quantum observable without perturbing its value or its eigenstate distribution. This is achieved by designing the system–probe coupling such that the measurement operator commutes with the system Hamiltonian. For example, consider a set of mutually commuting
self-adjoint
coupling operators
\mathbf{L}=[\mathbf{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{L}_{m}]^{\top}
, which can be used as measurement operators for a continuous-time projection measurement process.
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
is assumed in the so-called QND interaction
[
16
, Sec. 4.2.4]
, and thus the time evolution of the measurement operator
\mathbf{L}
can be calculated by Eq. (
7
) (
S=I
)

d\mathbf{L}_{j}=(-\imath[\mathbf{L}_{j},\mathbf{H}]+\frac{1}{2}([\mathbf{L}^{\dagger},\mathbf{L}_{j}]\mathbf{L})dt+[\mathbf{L}^{\dagger},\mathbf{L}_{j}]d\mathbf{B}_{\rm in}=0,

(42)

which means that the observable
\mathbf{L}_{j}
remains as its initial value and is independent of the measurement process. Moreover the transfer matrix is the identity matrix, therefore quantum BAE measurements are realized.

Remark 4.1
.

According to Eq. (
42
),
\mathbf{L}
is set of mutually commuting observables, then they are QND variables. Moreover, as the transfer function is an identity matrix, therefore, quantum BAE measurements are realized. Thus, if
\mathbf{L}
is a set of mutually commuting observables and
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
, then QND and BAE are realized simultaneously.

Belavkin quantum filtering equation, which is a classical stochastic differential equation, is given by

\displaystyle d\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j})=\pi_{t}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{L}_{j}))dt+[\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j}\mathbf{L}^{\top}+\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{L}_{j})-\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}^{\top}+\mathbf{L}^{\dagger})\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j})]d\nu(t),

(43)

where
\nu(t)
is Wiener process
[
5
, Sec. 6.1]
and the superoperator

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{L}_{j})=-\imath[\mathbf{L}_{j},\mathbf{H}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}[\mathbf{L}_{j},\mathbf{L}]+\frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{L}^{\dagger},\mathbf{L}_{j}]\mathbf{L}=0.

(44)

Here, we assume the initial conditional state has the finite first and second moments,
\pi_{0}(\mathbf{L}_{j})=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\rm S}(0)\mathbf{L}_{j})
, where
\rho_{\rm S}(0)
is the initial system density state. Thus, we have
d\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j})=[\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j}\mathbf{L}^{\top}+\mathbf{L}^{\dagger}\mathbf{L}_{j})-\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j})\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}^{\top}+\mathbf{L}^{\dagger})]d\nu(t)
. Taking expectation with respect to the Wiener process
\nu(t)
, yields that
\mathbb{E}[d\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j})]=0
.
As a result,

\mathbb{E}[\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j})]=\mathbb{E}[\pi_{0}(\mathbf{L}_{j})]={\rm Tr}(\rho_{S}(0)\mathbf{L}_{j}).

(45)

Moreover,
\mathbb{E}[\pi_{t}(\mathbf{L}_{j}^{2})]=\mathbb{E}[\pi_{0}(\mathbf{L}_{j}^{2})]={\rm Tr}(\rho_{S}(0)\mathbf{L}_{j}^{2})
.

Remark 4.2
.

According to Eq. (
45
), under the QND interaction, measurements have no back action on the estimation of
\mathbf{L}_{j}
.

In the following SISO case, we go to the Schrödinger picture.

Apply the spectral decomposition to the observable
\mathbf{L}
, which can be written as

\mathbf{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}l_{j}P_{l_{j}},

(46)

where
l_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,k
, are the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{L}
,
P_{l_{j}}
denotes the corresponding orthogonal projection operator, and resolves the identity
\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{l_{j}}=I
. Define the probability

P_{j}(t)={\rm Tr}[\rho_{c}(t)P_{l_{j}}].

(47)

Differentiate both sides of (
47
), we have

dP_{j}(t)={\rm Tr}[d\rho_{c}(t)P_{l_{j}}].

(48)

Assuming the system Hamiltonian
\mathbf{H}=0
and inserting (
10
) into (
48
), implies that

dP_{j}(t)=2\left(l_{j}-\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{k}l_{j^{\prime}}P_{j^{\prime}}(t)\right)P_{j}d\nu(t).

(49)

Take expectation to (
49
), we have
d\mathbb{E}(P_{j}(t))/dt\equiv 0
, which indicates that
P_{j}(t)
is a martingale and thus

\mathbb{E}(P_{j}(t))\equiv\mathbb{E}(P_{j}(0))={\rm Tr}[\rho_{c}(0)P_{l_{j}}].

(50)

Consequently, the conditioned system density operator
\rho_{c}(t)
after measurement converges to a projection
P_{l_{j}}
with
constant
probability
P_{j}(0)
. If the initial density operator
\rho(0)
is exactly one of eigenspaces of
L
, then
\rho(0)
is a steady state and remains unchanged during the measurement process. Similar discussions on the QND interaction measurement in the Schrödinger picture can be found in
[
4
]
,
[
14
, Thm. 5.1]
,
[
7
, Lemma 1]
.

Remark 4.3
.

Let

\mathbf{H}=\sum_{j}h_{j}P_{l_{j}},\ \ \rho_{c}(t)=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}(t)P_{l_{j}}.

(51)

Then

[\mathbf{H},\rho_{c}(t)]=\sum_{j}h_{j}\alpha_{j}(t)P_{l_{j}}.

(52)

Thus, in general, even
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
,
[\mathbf{H},\rho_{c}(t)]
may not be zero, thus Eq. (
49
) may not hold. For the passive case, it seems OK as you can rotate away
\mathbf{H}
. But in general it is unclear.

4.1
The SISO case

In this subsection, we firstly investigate a more general case,
[\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{H}]=0
or
[\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{H}]=0
in the single-input-single-output (SISO) quantum systems, which can be used to achieve the BAE measurements.

Since the input channel
m=1
, it can be easily verified that
C_{-}C_{+}^{\top}=C_{+}C_{-}^{\top}
. In this case,
[\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{H}]=0
yields

d(\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{L}^{\ast}[\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{L}]+[\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{L}]\mathbf{L}\right)dt+d\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\ast}[\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{L}]+[\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{L}]d\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{in}}.

(53)

As
\mathbf{L}=C_{-}\mathbf{a}+C_{+}\mathbf{a}^{\#}
, and

[C_{-}^{\#}\mathbf{a}^{\#},C_{-}\mathbf{a}]=-\sum_{j}|C_{-,j}|^{2},~~~~~[C_{+}^{\#}\mathbf{a},C_{+}\mathbf{a}^{\#}]=\sum_{k}|C_{+,k}|^{2},

(54)

we have

d\left(\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast}\right)=-\frac{g}{2}\left(\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast}\right)dt-\sqrt{2}gd\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{in}},

(55)

where

g\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(|C_{-,j}|^{2}-|C_{+,j}|^{2}),~~\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathbf{B}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{in}}\right),

(56)

and

(\mathbf{L+L^{\ast}})(t)=e^{-gt/2}(\mathbf{L+L^{\ast}})(0)-\sqrt{2}g\int_{0}^{t}e^{-g(t-\tau)/2}d\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{in}}(\tau),

(57)

\mathbf{q}_{\rm out}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbf{L+L^{\ast}})(t)+\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}}(t).

(58)

By Eq. (
55
),

\mathbf{q}_{\rm out}[s]=[1-g(s+\frac{g}{2})^{-1}]\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}}[s]=\frac{s-g/2}{s+g/2}\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}}[s].

(59)

Thus, the BAE measurement of
\mathbf{q}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{p}_{\rm in}
is realized.

On the other hand,
[\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{H}]=0
implies that

\displaystyle d(\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{L}^{\ast})=-\frac{g}{2}(\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{L}^{\ast})dt-\imath\sqrt{2}gd\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{in}},

(60)

where
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{-\imath}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{in}}-\mathbf{B}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{in}}\right)
. Solving Eq. (
60
), yields that

(\mathbf{L-L^{\ast}})(t)=e^{-gt/2}(\mathbf{L-L^{\ast}})(0)-\imath\sqrt{2}g\int_{0}^{t}e^{-g(t-\tau)/2}d\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{in}}(\tau).

(61)

We have

\mathbf{p}_{\rm out}(t)=\frac{-\imath}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbf{L-L^{\ast}})(t)+\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}}(t),

(62)

and thus

\mathbf{p}_{\rm out}[s]=[1-g(s+\frac{g}{2})^{-1}]\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}}[s]=\frac{s-g/2}{s+g/2}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}}[s],

(63)

which realizes the BAE measurement of
\mathbf{p}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{q}_{\rm in}
.

Consequently, quantum BAE measurement in the SISO case can be realized if
[\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{H}]=0
or
[\imath(\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{L}^{\ast}),\mathbf{H}]=0
.

Remark 4.4
.

If
\sum_{j}|C_{-,j}|^{2}=\sum_{k}|C_{+,k}|^{2}
, then
g=0
in (
56
), which yields that
d(\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast})=0
and the coupling operator
(\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast})(t)=(\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast})(0)=\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast}
can be detected via the input-output relation (
58
). Thus, this kind of interaction does not change the physical property of the coupling operator
\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast}
,
which is called a QND interaction in
[
16
, Sec. 4.2.4]
. On the other hand, by Eq. (
58
),
\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast}
is observable, and thus it is a QND variable. Similar results are also applied to the combined Hermitian coupling operator
\imath(\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{L}^{\ast})(t)\equiv\imath(\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{L}^{\ast})
, in which the QND interaction can be observed by Eq. (
60
) and the input-output relation (
62
). Indeed,
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{L}^{\ast}
assumed in
[
16
, Sec. 4.2.4]
is a more special case, which realizes the condition
g=0
in Eq. (
56
). In that special case,
[\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\ast},\mathbf{H}]=0
reduces to
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
.

Remark 4.5
.

In the SISO case, let
\mathbf{L}
be an observable and
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
, then
\mathbf{L}
is a QND variable and BAE measurement is also realized. If
\mathbf{L}
is not an observable, by Eq. (
59
) BAE measurements can still be implemented. However conditions like
\sum_{j}|C_{-,j}|^{2}=\sum_{k}|C_{+,k}|^{2}

In the following subsections, we focus on the multi-input-multi-output (MIMO) quantum systems and discuss the properties of

4.2
The MIMO case in the annihilation-creation form

In the annihilation-creation operator form,
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{L}^{\#}
is equivalent to
C_{-}=C_{+}^{\#}
. In particular, if both
C_{-}
and
C_{+}
are real, then
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{L}^{\#}
is equivalent to
C_{-}=C_{+}
.

Remark 4.6
.

It is obvious that
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
implies that
[\mathbf{L}^{\#},\mathbf{H}]=0
, and thus
[\mathbf{L}\pm\mathbf{L}^{\#},\mathbf{H}]=0
. However, either
[\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{\#},\mathbf{H}]=0
or
[\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{L}^{\#},\mathbf{H}]=0
alone does not necessarily lead to
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
.

The following lemma will be helpful to derive the equations given in Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
26
]

Let
\mathbf{X}
,
\mathbf{Y}
, and
\mathbf{Z}
be vectors of operators with dimension
l
,
m
, and
n
, respectively. Let
M\in\mathbf{C}^{m\times n}
, and the commutators
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\in\mathbf{C}
where
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
are arbitrary elements of the vectors
\mathbf{X}
,
\mathbf{Y}
and
\mathbf{Z}
. Then

\left[\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}^{\top}M\mathbf{Z}\right]=\left[\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\right]M\mathbf{Z}+\left[\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\right]M^{\top}\mathbf{Y}.

(64)

Lemma 4.2
.

[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
if and only if

\displaystyle C_{-}\Omega_{-}=C_{+}\Omega_{+}^{\dagger},

(65)

\displaystyle C_{-}\Omega_{+}=C_{+}\Omega_{-}^{\top},

or equivalently,

\mathcal{C}\Omega=2\Delta(C_{-},0)\Omega.

(66)

Proof.

Inserting
\mathbf{L}=C_{-}\mathbf{a}+C_{+}\mathbf{a}^{\#}
and
\mathbf{H}=\frac{1}{2}\breve{\mathbf{a}}^{\dagger}\Omega\breve{\mathbf{a}}
into the commutator
\left[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}\right]
, by Lemma
4.1
we have

\displaystyle=(C_{-}\Omega_{-}-C_{+}\Omega_{+}^{\dagger})\mathbf{a}+(C_{-}\Omega_{+}-C_{+}\Omega_{-}^{\top})\mathbf{a}^{\#},

(67)

\displaystyle[\mathbf{L}^{\#},\mathbf{H}]

\displaystyle=(C_{+}^{\#}\Omega_{-}-C_{-}^{\#}\Omega_{+}^{\dagger})\mathbf{a}+(C_{+}^{\#}\Omega_{+}-C_{-}^{\#}\Omega_{-}^{\top})\mathbf{a}^{\#}.

Thus,
\left[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}\right]=0
if and only if (
65
) holds, and is equivalent to (
66
) due to the Hermitian property of
\Omega
.

1.

C_{+}=\mathbf{0}

and

As in

Moreover,
\Sigma[s]
can be further calculated as

\Sigma[s]=\frac{1}{2s}\mathcal{C}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{-\imath}{s})^{n}(J_{n}\Omega)^{n}\mathcal{C}^{\flat},

(69)

where
(I+A)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}A^{n}
has been used in the derivation. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}J_{n}\Omega=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}\Omega_{-}&C_{-}\Omega_{+}\\
C_{-}^{\#}\Omega_{+}^{\#}&-C_{-}^{\#}\Omega_{-}^{\#}\end{array}\right]=\mathbf{0},

(70)

and
\Sigma[s]=\frac{1}{2s}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{\flat}
. Consequently, the transfer function reduces to

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}[s]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(sI-\frac{1}{2}C_{-}C_{-}^{\dagger})(sI+\frac{1}{2}C_{-}C_{-}^{\dagger})^{-1}&0\\
0&(sI-\frac{1}{2}C_{-}^{\#}C_{-}^{\top})(sI+\frac{1}{2}C_{-}^{\#}C_{-}^{\top})^{-1}\end{array}\right],

(71)

which is block diagonal and BAE measurements are realized.

2.

C_{-}=\mathbf{0}
, which means that
\mathcal{C}\Omega=\mathbf{0}
by Eq. (
66
). In this case, we have
C_{+}\Omega_{+}^{\dagger}=0
and
C_{+}\Omega_{-}^{\top}=0
. Similarly, it can be also verified that
\mathcal{C}J_{n}\Omega=\mathbf{0}
. And the block diagonal transfer function

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}[s]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(sI+\frac{1}{2}C_{+}C_{+}^{\dagger})(sI-\frac{1}{2}C_{+}C_{+}^{\dagger})^{-1}&0\\
0&(sI+\frac{1}{2}C_{+}^{\#}C_{+}^{\top})(sI-\frac{1}{2}C_{+}^{\#}C_{+}^{\top})^{-1}\end{array}\right],

(72)

which realizes BAE measurements.

3.

\Omega_{+}=\mathbf{0}
. In this case,
C_{-}\Omega_{-}=\mathbf{0}
and
C_{+}\Omega_{-}^{\top}=\mathbf{0}
, which implies that
C_{+}\Omega_{-}^{\#}=0
and
\mathcal{C}\Omega=\mathbf{0}
. It can be calculated that

\mathcal{C}J_{n}\Omega=\mathbf{0}
and
\Sigma[s]=\frac{1}{2s}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{\flat}
. However, the transfer function
\mathbb{G}[s]=(sI-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{\flat})(sI+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{\flat})^{-1}
, which is not block diagonal. Let
C_{-}C_{+}^{\top}=C_{+}C_{-}^{\top}
, i.e.,
C_{-}C_{+}^{\top}
is symmetric, then the transfer function

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}[s]=\mathrm{diag}\bigg\{

\displaystyle(sI-\frac{1}{2}(C_{-}C_{-}^{\dagger}-C_{+}C_{+}^{\dagger}))(sI+\frac{1}{2}(C_{-}C_{-}^{\dagger}-C_{+}C_{+}^{\dagger}))^{-1},

(73)

\displaystyle(sI+\frac{1}{2}(C_{+}^{\#}C_{+}^{\top}-C_{-}^{\#}C_{-}^{\top}))(sI-\frac{1}{2}(C_{+}^{\#}C_{+}^{\top}-C_{-}^{\#}C_{-}^{\top}))^{-1}\bigg\},

is block diagonal and BAE measurements are realized.

4.

\Omega_{-}=\mathbf{0}
, which means
\mathcal{C}\Omega=\mathbf{0}
by Eq. (
66
). Similar to the case of
\Omega_{+}=\mathbf{0}
, the condition of
C_{-}C_{+}^{\top}
is symmetric implies that the transfer function
\mathbb{G}[s]
is block diagonal and BAE measurements are feasible.

Remark 4.7
.

The condition
C_{-}C_{+}^{\top}
being symmetric holds if
C_{-}=\pm C_{+}
(
\mathbf{q}
or
\mathbf{p}
coupling) or the quantum system is with the SISO case (see Subsection
4.1
for details).

Remark 4.8
.

In the above four special cases with
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
, we all have
\mathcal{C}\Omega=\mathbf{0}
, which means that the transfer function
\mathbb{G}[s]
does not depend on
\Omega
. This may not be true in general even when
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
.

4.3
The MIMO case in the quadrature form

In this section, we investigate linear quantum systems in the quadrature form, whose properties can be proved to be consistent with the related results in the annihilation-creation form given before.

Denote

\Lambda=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}&C_{+}\end{array}\right]V_{n}^{\dagger}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda_{q}&\Lambda_{p}\end{array}\right].

(74)

In the quadrature representation, the coupling operator
\mathbf{L}
and Hamiltonian
\mathbf{H}
can be described by

\mathbf{L}=\Lambda\mathbf{x},~~~~\mathbf{H}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbb{H}\mathbf{x}.

(75)

The condition
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
is equivalent to

\Lambda\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=0,

(76)

where Lemma
4.1
has been used in the derivation.

Similarly,
[\mathbf{L}^{\#},\mathbf{H}]=0
is equivalent to

\Lambda^{\#}\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=0,

(77)

In what follows we look at four special cases.

1.

C_{+}=0
, then we have
\Lambda_{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}C_{-}
and
\Lambda_{p}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\imath C_{-}
. In this case

\mathbb{C}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}+C_{-}^{\#}&-C_{-}+C_{-}^{\#}\\
C_{-}-C_{-}^{\#}&C_{-}+C_{-}^{\#}\end{array}\right],~~\mathbb{C}^{\sharp}=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}^{\dagger}+C_{-}^{\top}&-C_{-}^{\dagger}+C_{-}^{\top}\\
C_{-}^{\dagger}-C_{-}^{\top}&C_{-}^{\dagger}+C_{-}^{\top}\end{array}\right].

(78)

Since

\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}+\Omega_{+})&\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-}-\Omega_{+})\\
-\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-}+\Omega_{+})&\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}-\Omega_{+})\\
\end{array}\right],

(79)

we have

\Lambda\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}C_{-}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\imath C_{-}\end{array}\right]\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=\mathbf{0},

(80)

which yields

C_{-}\Omega_{-}=0,~~~~C_{-}\Omega_{+}=0.

(81)

It can be observed that (
81
) is as the same as the results given before, and in this case
\mathbb{G}[s]
is block diagonal, which means that BAE measurements are realized.

2.

C_{-}=0
, then we have
\Lambda_{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}C_{+}
and
\Lambda_{p}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\imath C_{+}
. In this case

\mathbb{C}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{+}+C_{+}^{\#}&C_{+}-C_{+}^{\#}\\
C_{+}-C_{+}^{\#}&-C_{+}-C_{+}^{\#}\end{array}\right],~~\mathbb{C}^{\sharp}=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-C_{+}^{\dagger}-C_{+}^{\top}&C_{+}^{\dagger}-C_{+}^{\top}\\
C_{+}^{\dagger}-C_{+}^{\top}&C_{+}^{\dagger}+C_{+}^{\top}\end{array}\right].

(82)

Since

\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}+\Omega_{+})&\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-}-\Omega_{+})\\
-\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-}+\Omega_{+})&\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}-\Omega_{+})\\
\end{array}\right],

(83)

we have

\Lambda\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}C_{+}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\imath C_{+}\end{array}\right]\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=\mathbf{0},

(84)

which yields

C_{+}\Omega_{-}^{\top}=0,~~~~C_{+}\Omega_{+}^{\dagger}=0.

(85)

It can be observed that (
85
) is as the same as the results given before, and in this case
\mathbb{G}[s]
is block diagonal, which means that BAE measurements are realized.

3.

\Omega_{+}=0
, then we have

\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-})&\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-})\\
-\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-})&\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-})\end{array}\right].

(86)

Since
\Lambda\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=0
, we have

C_{-}\Omega_{-}=0,~~~~C_{+}\Omega_{-}^{\top}=0.

(87)

It can be observed that (
87
) is as the same as the results given before. However, in this case
C_{-}C_{+}^{\top}=C_{+}C_{-}^{\top}
is needed to satisfy the condition of
\mathbb{G}[s]
is block diagonal, which means that BAE measurements are realized.

4.

\Omega_{-}=0
, then we have

\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{+})&-\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{+})\\
-\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{+})&-\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{+})\end{array}\right].

(88)

Since
\Lambda\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}=0
, we have

C_{-}\Omega_{+}=0,~~~~C_{+}\Omega_{+}^{\#}=0.

(89)

It can be observed that (
89
) is as the same as the results given before. However, in this case
C_{-}C_{+}^{\top}=C_{+}C_{-}^{\top}
is also needed to satisfy the condition of
\mathbb{G}[s]
is block diagonal, which means that BAE measurements are realized.

4.4
The MIMO case in the Kalman canonical form

In the case of Kalman canonical form, the condition
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
is equivalent to

\Gamma\bar{\mathbb{J}}_{n}\tilde{\mathbb{H}}=0.

(90)

Thus, by
[
28
, Lemma 3.2]
we have

\Gamma_{co}\bar{\mathbb{J}}_{n}\tilde{\mathbb{H}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\Gamma\\
\Gamma^{\#}\end{array}\right]\bar{\mathbb{J}}_{n}\tilde{\mathbb{H}}=0,

(91)

which yields

C_{h}A_{h}^{22}+C_{co}\mathbb{J}_{n_{1}}A_{12}^{\top}+\frac{1}{2}C_{co}B_{co}\mathbb{J}_{m}B_{h}^{\top}=0,

(92)

and

C_{co}A_{co}=\frac{1}{2}C_{co}B_{co}C_{co}.

(93)

The following theorem presents the realization of BAE measurements by
the linear quantum system in the Kalman canonical form.

\Gamma_{co}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Gamma_{co,q}&\Gamma_{co,p}\\
\Gamma_{co,q}^{\#}&\Gamma_{co,p}^{\#}\end{array}\right]

(94)

C_{co}=V_{m}\Gamma_{co}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}C_{co,q}\\
C_{co,p}\end{array}\right]=\sqrt{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Re}(\Gamma_{co,q})&{\rm Re}(\Gamma_{co,p})\\
{\rm Im}(\Gamma_{co,q})&{\rm Im}(\Gamma_{co,p})\end{array}\right]

(95)

Theorem 4.1
.

The linear quantum system in the Kalman canonical form under the condition of
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
realizes the BAE measurements of
\mathbf{q}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{p}_{\rm in}
, i.e., the transfer function
\Xi_{\mathbf{p}_{\rm in}\rightarrow\mathbf{q}_{\rm out}}(s)\equiv 0

if and only if

C_{co,q}B_{co,p}=0,

(96)

or equivalently,

{\rm Re}(\Gamma_{co,q}){\rm Re}(\Gamma_{co,p}^{\top})={\rm Re}(\Gamma_{co,p}){\rm Re}(\Gamma_{co,q}^{\top}).

(97)

Meanwhile, the linear quantum system in the Kalman canonical form under the condition of
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
realizes the BAE measurements of
\mathbf{p}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{q}_{\rm in}
, i.e., the transfer function
\Xi_{\mathbf{q}_{\rm in}\rightarrow\mathbf{p}_{\rm out}}(s)\equiv 0
if and only if

C_{co,p}B_{co,q}=0,

(98)

or equivalently,

{\rm Im}(\Gamma_{co,q}){\rm Im}(\Gamma_{co,p}^{\top})={\rm Im}(\Gamma_{co,p}){\rm Im}(\Gamma_{co,q}^{\top}).

(99)

Furthermore, the realization of both the two cases of BAE measurements imply that
{\rm Re}(\Gamma_{co,q}\Gamma_{co,p}^{\top})
is symmetric.

Proof.

Partition the system matrices
C_{co}
and
B_{co}
as

C_{co}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}C_{co,q}\\
C_{co,p}\end{array}\right],~~~~B_{co}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{co,q}&B_{co,p}\end{array}\right],

(100)

respectively. Under the condition
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
, by (
93
) we have

\displaystyle C_{co}A_{co}^{k}B_{co}=\frac{1}{2^{k}}(C_{co}B_{co})^{k+1}.

(101)

Thus, if
C_{co,q}B_{co,p}=0
, then
C_{co}A_{co}^{k}B_{co}
is block lower triangular, which implies that
C_{co,q}A_{co}^{k}B_{co,p}=0
. By
[
28
, Theorem 4.1]
, the quantum Kalman canonical form realizes the BAE measurements of
\mathbf{q}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{p}_{\rm in}
. On the other hand, if the BAE measurements of
\mathbf{q}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{p}_{\rm in}
is realized, then by
[
28
, Theorem 4.1]

C_{co,q}A_{co}^{k}B_{co,p}=0,~~k=0,1,\ldots,

(102)

which implies that
C_{co,q}B_{co,p}=0
. The proof of the BAE measurements of
\mathbf{p}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{q}_{\rm in}
follows in a similar way.
∎

Example 4.1
.

In
[
8
, Fig. 1(A)]
, it can be calculated that

C_{co}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}C_{co,q}\\
C_{co,p}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{\kappa}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\kappa}\end{array}\right],~~B_{co}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{co,q}&B_{co,p}\end{array}\right]=-\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{\kappa}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\kappa}\end{array}\right],

(103)

which imply that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}C_{co,q}B_{co,p}=0,\\
C_{co,p}B_{co,q}=0.\end{array}\right.

(104)

Thus, both the BAE measurements of
\mathbf{q}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{p}_{\rm in}
and
\mathbf{p}_{\rm out}
with respect to
\mathbf{q}_{\rm in}
are realized in this experiment.

Remark 4.9
.

According to
[
28
, Lemma 3.2]
, if
H
is of the form of
[
28
, Eq. (12)]
and
\Gamma
is of the form of
[
28
, Eq. (13)]
, then the linear system is of the Kalman canonical form. If
\Gamma_{h}\neq 0
, then the variables
p_{h}
exist, which means there are QND variables.

4.5
Realization of QND variables under QND interaction

QND variables correspond to the uncontrollable and observable subsystems. In this subsection, we investigate sufficient conditions for the realization of a QND observable under QND interaction, i.e.,
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
.

Since
\Lambda=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}+C_{+}&\imath(C_{-}-C_{+})\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda_{q}&\Lambda_{p}\end{array}\right]
, and

\mathbb{B}=-\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger}-C_{+}^{\dagger})&-\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger}-C_{+}^{\dagger})\\
\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger}+C_{+}^{\dagger})&\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger}+C_{+}^{\dagger})\end{array}\right]=-\sqrt{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{p}^{\dagger})&-\mathrm{Re}(\Lambda_{p}^{\dagger})\\
\mathrm{Im}(\Lambda_{q}^{\dagger})&\mathrm{Re}(\Lambda_{q}^{\dagger})\end{array}\right],

(105)

we need
\Lambda_{q}=0
or
\Lambda_{p}=0
to realize QND variables.

1.

\Lambda_{q}=0
, i.e.,
C_{-}=-C_{+}
. In this case,
C_{-}-C_{+}=2C_{-}=-\sqrt{2}\imath\Lambda_{p}
, and
\Lambda_{p}=\sqrt{2}\imath C_{-}
. We have

\mathbb{C}=2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})\\
0&\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})\end{array}\right],~~~~\mathbb{B}=2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})&\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\\
0&0\end{array}\right],

(106)

and

\mathbb{C}^{\sharp}\mathbb{C}=4\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})-\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})\\
0&0\end{array}\right].

(107)

If further
\Omega_{-}=-\Omega_{+}
, then
\Omega_{-}-\Omega_{+}=2\Omega_{-}
, and

\mathbb{A}=2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-})\\
0&\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-})\end{array}\right]-2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})-\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})\\
0&0\end{array}\right].

(108)

Actually,
C_{-}=-C_{+}
(
\mathbf{p}
coupling) and
\Omega_{-}=-\Omega_{+}
imply that
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle=2\left[\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-})+\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})+\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})\right]\mathbf{p}-2\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}}+2\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}},

(109)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-})\mathbf{p},

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}}.

Thus,
\mathbf{p}
is a QND variable if the subsystem
(\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}),0,-\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}))
or
(\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}),0,\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}))
is observable. Moreover, BAE measurement is realized from
\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}}
to
\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{out}}
as well as from
\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}}
to
\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{out}}
.

2.

\Lambda_{p}=0
, i.e.,
C_{-}=C_{+}
. In this case,
C_{-}+C_{+}=2C_{-}=\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{q}
, and
\Lambda_{q}=\sqrt{2}C_{-}
. We have

\mathbb{C}=2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})&0\\
\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})&0\end{array}\right],~~~~\mathbb{B}=-2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})&\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\end{array}\right],

(110)

and

\mathbb{C}^{\sharp}\mathbb{C}=4\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})+\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})&0\end{array}\right].

(111)

If further
\Omega_{-}=\Omega_{+}
, then
\Omega_{-}+\Omega_{+}=2\Omega_{-}
, and

\mathbb{A}=2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-})&0\\
-\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-})&0\end{array}\right]-2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})+\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})&0\end{array}\right].

(112)

Actually,
C_{-}=C_{+}
(
\mathbf{q}
coupling) and
\Omega_{-}=\Omega_{+}
also imply that
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-})\mathbf{q},

(113)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=-2\left[\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-})+\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})+\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})\right]\mathbf{q}-2\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}}-2\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}^{\dagger})\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Re}(C_{-})\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Im}(C_{-})\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}}.

Thus,
\mathbf{q}
is a QND variable if the subsystem
(\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}),0,\mathrm{Im}(C_{-}))
or
(\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}),0,\mathrm{Re}(C_{-}))
is observable. Moreover, BAE measurement is realized from
\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{in}}
to
\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{out}}
as well as from
\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{in}}
to
\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{out}}
.

Finally, if
C_{+}=0
and
C_{-}
is real, then

\mathbb{B}=-\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}^{\dagger}&0\\
0&C_{-}^{\dagger}\end{array}\right],\ \ \mathbb{C}=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}ll@{}}C_{-}&\\
&C_{-}\end{array}\right],

(114)

and

\mathbb{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}+\Omega_{+})&\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-}-\Omega_{+})\\
-\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-}+\Omega_{+})&\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-}-\Omega_{+})\\
\end{array}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}^{\dagger}C_{-}&0\\
0&C_{-}^{\dagger}C_{-}\end{array}\right].

(115)

Furthermore, if
\Omega_{-}=\Omega_{+}
, then

\mathbb{A}=2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{Im}(\Omega_{-})&0\\
-\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{-})&0\end{array}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{-}^{\dagger}C_{-}&0\\
0&C_{-}^{\dagger}C_{-}\end{array}\right].

(116)

Thus, BAE measurement can be realized. However, there is no QND variables.

5
Conclusion

This paper establishes a comprehensive framework for realizing BAE measurements and QND variables in linear quantum systems. We demonstrate that bilateral BAE measurements of conjugate observables can be achieved when the system Hamiltonian is purely imaginary and the coupling operator is real or purely imaginary. Furthermore, specific coupling configurations (e.g.,
C_{-}=C_{+}
or
C_{-}=-C_{+}
) yield QND variables for position or momentum, respectively. The analysis extends to systems where direct BAE realization conditions are not met, showing that coherent feedback control can engineer an effective Hamiltonian to enable BAE measurements. Crucially, under QND interaction condition
[\mathbf{L},\mathbf{H}]=0
, the coupling operator itself becomes a QND observable, allowing for continuous, non-perturbative monitoring. These results, formulated in both annihilation-creation and quadrature operator representations and clarified through the Kalman canonical form, provide a systematic design methodology for high-precision quantum metrology, with direct applications in quantum sensing, gravitational wave detection, and quantum information processing. Future work may explore nonlinear extensions and non-Markovian environments to further advance quantum measurement and control capabilities.
