Quality of life with nivolumab and AVD first‐line treatment of Hodgkin lymphoma in the Phase II GHSG NIVAHL trial

Christian Peter Jaworek, Ina Bühnen, Peter Herhaus, Julia Meissner, Karolin Trautmann‐Grill, Teresa Halbsguth, Andrea Kerkhoff, Valdete Schaub, Michael Fuchs, Bastian von Tresckow, Peter Borchmann, Karolin Behringer, Paul Jan Bröckelmann

First‐line treatment of classic Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) is undergoing a shift towards early incorporation of targeted agents. The anti‐PD1‐based regimen nivolumab plus doxorubicin, vinblastine and dacarbazine (N‐AVD) is now approved for patients with advanced‐stage disease and also considered a treatment option for intermediate‐stage HL., , Because cure rates are high and many HL patients are young, longitudinal quality of life (QoL), fatigue, and return to work are central to shared decision‐making. To this end, the QoL impact of anti‐PD1‐based first‐line treatment remains poorly described.,

To address this knowledge gap, we herein report the preplanned longitudinal QoL analysis of the randomized Phase II GHSG NIVAHL trial (NCT03004833). In NIVAHL, 109 patients aged 18–60 years with intermediate‐stage HL received cumulatively four cycles of N‐AVD (concomitant, Group A; or sequential, Group B), each followed by 30 Gy involved‐site radiotherapy (IS‐RT)., Patients providing separate informed consent completed the validated EORTC QLQ‐C30 and QLQ‐FA12 questionnaires at enrollment, after systemic treatment, after IS‐RT, and at 1‐ and 3‐year follow‐up (FU)., Functional, symptom, and global health status (GHS) scores were age‐ and sex‐standardized to German general population reference values, with zero indicating no difference from the reference population. As previously established, fatigue scores of 30 or higher were considered clinically relevant. Differences from the reference population and changes over time were considered statistically significant if the corresponding 95% CI excluded zero. Time to recovery from fatigue (TTR‐F; interval from end of treatment [EOT] to the first assessment with a fatigue score below 30) and time to return to work (TTR‐W; interval from EOT to the first documented return to work or education among patients reporting employment or education before diagnosis) were analyzed using time‐to‐event methods, with multivariable Cox models adjusted for age and sex. In an exploratory analysis, the impact of immune‐related hypothyroidism (irHypothyroidism), the most frequent chronic immune‐related adverse event (irAE), on QoL domains was assessed. Analyses were performed in SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Of 109 patients, 98 (90%) were evaluable for the present analysis, contributing 397 questionnaires (median 4 per patient) with high completion (QLQ‐C30 93%, QLQ‐FA12 97%) and declining availability over time (Figures and ). The median age was 27 years (range 18–60), 60% were female, 74% had an ECOG of 0, and 77% presented with Stage IIA disease. As expected in this trial for patients with intermediate‐stage HL, at least one of the GHSG risk factors was present in all patients, with ≥3 nodal areas most common (Table ). Baseline life situation, including educational level and employment, is detailed in Table . Patients with and without an evaluable late assessment were largely comparable in age, sex, stage, educational level and treatment arm and had virtually identical FU, although those contributing 3‐year data reported lower baseline fatigue (Table ).

Compared to the age‐ and sex‐matched German general population, HL patients in NIVAHL reported impaired GHS already at baseline relative to reference values (mean difference −26, 95% CI −30 to −21) and worsened further on treatment (−35, 95% CI −40 to −30; Figure ). Baseline symptom burden was highest for fatigue (+22), appetite loss (+17), financial impact (+15), sleep disturbance (+13), and dyspnea (+10), whereas functioning impairment was most pronounced for emotional (−23) and role functioning (−15). On treatment, fatigue (+39) and most symptom and functioning scales deteriorated, including nausea and vomiting despite mandatory dexamethasone‐containing prophylaxis (+22), with role functioning showing the steepest on‐treatment decline (−41).

After EOT, however, most domains improved substantially compared to baseline and on‐treatment status. Importantly, most domains such as for example GHS (−14 at 1 year; −8 at 3 years) or fatigue (+10 at 1 year; −3 at 3 years; Figures , and ; unadjusted scores in Tables ) approximated reference values during FU. In Cox regression, baseline scores were consistently associated with the corresponding 1‐ and 3‐year scores, whereas effects of age and sex were small and domain‐specific (Tables ).

A more detailed analysis of fatigue was conducted from the QLQ‐FA12 measurements. Physical fatigue showed the highest burden and the most pronounced on‐treatment deterioration before improvement occurred during FU. Cognitive fatigue increased transiently and returned to baseline, while emotional fatigue improved steadily (Figure , Table ). In an exploratory comparison, GHS and fatigue trajectories did not differ markedly between the concomitant and sequential treatment groups (Figure , Table ). Median TTR‐F (n = 79) was 12.8 months (95% CI 7.3–20.3) and median TTR‐W (n = 54, among patients employed or in education before diagnosis) was 3.8 months (1.4–8.0), with most patients recovering from severe fatigue and being able to return to work or education (Figure ). In Cox regression analyses, higher baseline fatigue was associated with delayed fatigue recovery, and TTR‐W tended to be shorter in men, whereas neither endpoint differed between treatment arms (Table ).

Treatment‐related irHypothyroidism developed in 21 of 98 patients (21%). On‐treatment impairment of QoL and symptoms, particularly fatigue, sleep disturbance and appetite loss, appeared more pronounced in affected patients compared to those without irHypothyroidism. Overall, no consistent inter‐group differences were apparent at 1 and 3 years, and the low numbers at 3 year FU (n = 4 with irHypothyroidism vs. 23 without) preclude firm conclusions. This comparison was exploratory and hypothesis‐generating (Figure , Tables and ).

To our knowledge, this is the first comprehensive longitudinal characterization of patient‐reported QoL domains, including fatigue, other symptoms, functioning and return to work with anti‐PD1‐based first‐line HL treatment. The analysis benefits from high patient participation, relatively high longitudinal completeness and standardization to national age‐ and sex‐matched reference values, extending prior GHSG longitudinal QoL data from chemotherapy‐based regimens, to the anti‐PD1 setting. The on‐treatment increase of fatigue parallels a preliminary signal of higher fatigue with N‐AVD than with brentuximab vedotin plus AVD in the Phase III SWOG S1826 trial,, suggesting a possible class effect of anti‐PD1 antibodies in the HL first‐line population. Of note, S1826 differs from NIVAHL in enrolling adolescents and adults aged 12 and older with advanced‐stage (III–IV) disease treated with six cycles and virtually no consolidative radiotherapy, whereas NIVAHL enrolled patients aged 18–60 years with intermediate‐stage disease. This observation warrants further exploration and ideally confirmation in larger series, since it may justify early fatigue‐directed intervention such as exercise, cognitive behavioral therapy and psychosocial support. Notably, disease control in NIVAHL was near‐universal (92% complete remission, 3‐year progression‐free survival 99% and overall survival 100%,, so the observed QoL trajectories reflect outcomes under effective first‐line therapy rather than being confounded by treatment failure. Reassuringly, QoL improved during FU with reference values approximated for most domains, consistent with patterns observed after other highly effective contemporary regimens., , These data enable more granular patient counseling and support survivorship‐focused care rather than the assumption of permanent impairment. Because deterioration peaks on or at the EOT, this window appears key for proactive symptom monitoring and symptom‐oriented interventions. Limitations of the present analysis include the absence of a non‐anti‐PD1 control arm, possible under‐reporting of less frequent irAE, and attrition at later FU. As patients contributing 3‐year data reported lower baseline fatigue, missingness cannot be assumed to be at random and late estimates may be somewhat optimistic. The ongoing GHSG INDIE trial (NCT04837859), with collection of longitudinal PRO data including PRO‐CTCAE and a more detailed irAE assessment as well as the pending more detailed QoL analysis from the S1826 trial will hopefully address some of the remaining uncertainties in the future.

Collectively, the data and findings reporting herein help counsel patients on the expected QoL trajectories with transient worsening of QoL incl. fatigue, before meaningful recovery beyond baseline status during FU.
