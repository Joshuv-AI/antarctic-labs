RECQL correlates with immune infiltration and serves as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic predictor in gastric cancer
Authors: Tana Lin, Lu Wang, Xin Wang, Haiqing Hu, Ying Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecQ-like helicase (RECQL), a member of the RecQ-like DNA helicase family, plays a crucial role in maintaining genomic stability. However, its relevance in gastric cancer (GC) has not been fully investigated. This study aimed to explore the clinical significance, biological functions, and potential role of RECQL in the tumor immune microenvironment of GC through comprehensive bioinformatics analyses and in vitro experiments.MethodsWeighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), differential expression analysis, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression were performed using public datasets [The Cancer Genome Atlas Stomach Adenocarcinoma (TCGA-STAD), GSE150290] to identify key genes associated with GC progression. Subsequently, key pathways were identified through functional enrichment analysis, while immune infiltration and spatial transcriptomic analyses were conducted to characterize RECQL expression and its association with the tumor immune microenvironment. Finally, the effects of RECQL knockdown on the biological function of GC cells were assessed through Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8), colony formation, scratch, and terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assays.ResultsRECQL was significantly upregulated in GC tissues and correlated with advanced clinical stage and poor prognosis. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed a strong association between high RECQL expression and DNA repair pathway. Immune infiltration analysis indicated significant enrichment of M2 macrophages in the high-RECQL group, along with upregulation of immune checkpoint molecules including PDCD1, CTLA4, and CD274. Spatial transcriptomics further demonstrated co-localization of RECQL with myeloid cell-enriched regions in tumor parenchymal areas. Furthermore, in vitro experimental results indicated that RECQL was highly expressed in GC cell lines, and its knockdown effectively inhibited the viability, proliferation, and migration capabilities of HGC-27 cells, while enhancing their apoptosis.ConclusionsRECQL serves as a promising biomarker and potential therapeutic target in GC.Keywords: RecQ-like helicase (RECQL), gastric cancer (GC), DNA repair, tumor immune microenvironment, bioinformatics

Highlight box

Key findings

• RecQ-like helicase (RECQL) is significantly overexpressed in gastric cancer (GC).

• RECQL expression correlates with disease progression and poor survival.

• High RECQL expression is associated with M2 macrophage infiltration.

What is known and what is new?

• Dysregulation of DNA helicases is often associated with tumorigenesis in various cancers.

• This study identifies RECQL as a novel driver and prognostic marker specifically for GC. For the first time, spatial transcriptomics reveals the co-localization of RECQL with M2 macrophages in the tumor parenchyma.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• RECQL testing could be integrated into the pathological evaluation of GC to identify high-risk patients.

• RECQL represents a potential therapeutic target; developing small-molecule inhibitors of RECQL may sensitize GC cells to treatment or overcome immunotherapy resistance.

• Future clinical trials should investigate whether RECQL expression levels can predict patient response to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, potentially guiding more personalized immunotherapy.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is one of the most common and lethal malignancies of the digestive tract worldwide (1). According to the latest Global Cancer Observatory (GLOBOCAN) estimates, GC accounts for more than 968,000 new cases and nearly 660,000 deaths annually, ranking fifth globally for both incidence and mortality (2). The burden is particularly pronounced in East Asia—including China, Japan, and South Korea—where incidence and mortality have persistently remained high, posing a major public-health challenge (3). Clinically, early-stage GC is frequently asymptomatic, and a substantial proportion of patients presents with advanced disease at diagnosis, constraining therapeutic options and limiting survival gains (4).

Current standards of care encompass surgical resection, cytotoxic chemotherapy, molecularly targeted agents, and immunotherapy (5). Surgery is the preferred approach for early disease, yet eligibility is restricted and postoperative recurrence is common (6). Conventional chemotherapy can extend survival but is hampered by substantial toxicity and the emergence of resistance (7). Targeted therapies—such as HER2 and VEGFR inhibitors—benefit selected subpopulations, though response rates and durability remain modest (8). Immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., PD-1/PD-L1 antibodies) have opened new avenues for advanced GC, but clinical benefit is variable and often transient (9). Collectively, these limitations underscore an urgent need to elucidate the molecular drivers of GC and to discover tractable therapeutic targets.

Recent advances in high-throughput sequencing have markedly accelerated progress in this field. In particular, single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptomic (ST) offer transformative means to dissect the complex tissue architecture of GC. scRNA-seq resolves transcriptional programs at single-cell resolution across malignant, immune, and stromal compartments, revealing functional diversification within cancer cell populations and patterns of immune infiltration that pinpoint genes and pathways linked to tumor progression and therapeutic response (10). Complementarily, ST preserves native tissue architecture while mapping gene expression in situ, enabling precise visualization of the spatial relationships and interaction networks among tumor, immune, and stromal cells within the microenvironment (11).

This study utilizes bioinformatics methods to screen and identify key genes closely associated with the occurrence and development of GC. By analyzing the molecular characteristics of GC, it reveals potential related genes and their spatial distribution and interactions within the tumor microenvironment (TME). These findings provide important insights for identifying new diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets, thus laying a more reliable molecular foundation for precision treatment of GC. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD and MDAR reporting checklists (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0411/rc).

Methods

Data sources

Publicly available transcriptomes and clinical annotations were aggregated from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). From TCGA, RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) profiles for 385 GC tumors and 35 normal tissues with matched clinical metadata were obtained for the stomach adenocarcinoma cohort [The Cancer Genome Atlas Stomach Adenocarcinoma (TCGA-STAD); https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/]. From the GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), the following datasets were included: GSE150290 (scRNA-seq of paired tumor and adjacent tissues from 23 GC patients), GSE29272 (gene-expression profiles for 134 normal and 134 tumor samples), GSE66229 (gene-expression profiles for 100 normal and 300 tumor samples), GSE15459 (tumor expression with clinical annotations for 192 cases), and GSE251950 (ST from 10 GC specimens).

Clinical sample collection

This study was conducted at the Inner Mongolia Hospital of Peking University Cancer Hospital. A total of 15 patients with primary GC were enrolled. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) histological diagnosis of gastric adenocarcinoma; (II) scheduled for radical gastrectomy; and (III) provision of written informed consent. The exclusion criteria were: (I) receipt of preoperative neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy; (II) presence of other concurrent malignancies; and (III) severe systemic organ dysfunction. Patient demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table S1. Paired tumor and adjacent normal tissues were collected from all patients. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Inner Mongolia Hospital of Peking University Cancer Hospital (approval No. WZ202529). All participants provided written informed consent.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

Genes with expression above the cohort median were analyzed using the WGCNA R package (v1.73) (12). Pairwise Pearson correlations were computed and an appropriate soft-thresholding power (β) was selected to approximate scale-free topology. Adjacency matrices were constructed via the one-step method and transformed to topological overlap matrices (TOM). Hierarchical clustering yielded gene dendrograms; module detection and module eigengenes were used to evaluate gene significance and module–trait relationships, thereby identifying key modules. Gene lists from modules of interest were extracted for downstream analyses.

Differential expression analysis

Tumor versus normal contrasts were assessed with limma (v3.62.2) (13). Multiple testing was controlled using the false discovery rate (FDR); differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined by |log2 fold-change| >1 and FDR <0.05.

scRNA-seq analysis

For GSE150290, single-cell data were processed with Seurat (v5.1.0). Cells were filtered if mitochondrial gene fraction >10%, zero-count gene fraction >95%, or if the number of detected features <200 or >6,000. Data were normalized using NormalizeData (method = “LogNormalize”), followed by principal component analysis (PCA). Batch effects were mitigated with Harmony. Neighborhood graphs and clusters were computed with FindNeighbors and FindClusters; cell types were annotated using canonical markers. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) embeddings were generated with RunUMAP. Cluster-level differential expression was performed using FindMarkers with min.pct =0.1, logfc.threshold >0.25, and adjusted P (adj. P) <0.05. For the tumor-versus-normal comparison, differential expression was assessed across all captured cells without stratification by cell type, with the aim of characterizing the overall expression distribution of candidate molecules within the TME at single-cell resolution.

Functional enrichment analyses

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment of DEGs sets were performed with clusterProfiler (v4.8.2) (14); terms with adj. P<0.05 were considered significant. GO term networks were visualized using Cytoscape (v3.10.2) with ClueGO (P<1.0×10−4; kappa score >0.7). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) contrasted high- vs. low-expression groups defined by the median expression of the target gene; KEGG pathways with adj. P<0.05 were deemed significant. To further characterize RecQ-like helicase (RECQL)-associated functional pathways, a separate KEGG enrichment analysis was conducted on DEGs identified by quartile-based stratification, with the high-expression group defined as log2[transcripts per million (TPM) + 1] >4.26 and the low-expression group as log2(TPM + 1) <3.11.

Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression

Patients were stratified by survival status (alive vs. deceased), and an expression matrix of candidate genes was constructed. LASSO logistic regression was performed using glmnet (v4.1-9; family = “binomial”), with ten-fold cross-validation to identify the optimal penalty (λ). Genes exhibiting non-zero coefficients at λ_min were retained as signature features.

Immune-related analyses

Relative abundances of 22 immune cell types were inferred with CIBERSORT (15). The effectiveness of immunotherapy was evaluated using Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/). ESTIMATE (v1.0.13) yielded Immune, Stromal, and ESTIMATE scores.

ST analysis

For GSE251950, data were normalized with Seurat using SCTransform and reduced via PCA. Cell-type deconvolution of spatial spots was performed with spacexr (v2.2.1) employing the RCTD framework, using the GSE150290 single-cell dataset as the reference. Spatial maps and overlays were generated in Seurat.

Drug-sensitivity prediction

Sample-specific half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) for multiple anticancer agents were inferred using oncoPredict (v1.2) (16), trained on the GDSC2 reference panel. Predicted IC50 values were compared across groups to estimate differential drug response.

Molecular docking

The small-molecule ligand was retrieved from PubChem (CID: 9933475) and the target protein model from AlphaFold (ID: AF-P46063-F1-v4). Ligand and receptor were preprocessed in Discovery Studio 4.5. Docking was performed via CB-DOCK2 using the AutoDock Vina scoring function. Two- and three-dimensional interaction maps of the resulting complex were rendered in Discovery Studio 4.5.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations

MD simulations were performed using GROMACS (v2024.4) with the CHARMM36 force field and TIP3P explicit solvent model. Taking the molecular docking results as the initial structure, energy minimization was performed first. The system was then equilibrated using the NVT ensemble (constant volume and temperature) and the NPT ensemble (constant pressure and temperature), after which MD simulations were carried out for 10 ns at room temperature and pressure. Structural dynamics were assessed through the calculation of root mean square deviation (RMSD), root mean square fluctuation (RMSF), radius of gyration (Rg), and hydrogen-bond occupancy, and the corresponding trajectory profiles were analyzed to monitor system equilibration and stability.

Cell culture and transfection

The GES-1 cells (JY128, Jinyuan Biotech) were cultured in 90% DMEM medium (PM150210, Procell, Wuhan, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, CF-01P-02, Cell-box, Changsha, China) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (P/S, P1400, Procell). The MKN-45 cells (JY033, Jinyuan Biotech, Shanghai, China) were cultured in 90% RPMI-1640 medium (PM150110, Procell) with 10% FBS and 1% P/S. The HGC-27 cells (JY078, Jinyuan Biotech) were cultured in 90% RPMI-1640 medium with 10% FBS and 1% P/S. The AGS cells (ml095988, Mlbio, Shanghai, China) were cultured in 90% F12K medium (PM150910, Procell) with 10% FBS and 1% P/S. All cells were cultured at 37 ℃ in a humidified environment with 5% CO2, and passaged when cell confluence reached 80–90%. All cell-based assays were performed in three independent biological replicates.

For RECQL overexpression in MKN-45 cells, transfection was carried out using Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) with pcDNA3.1 negative control (OE-NC) and pcDNA3.1-RECQL (OE-RECQL) plasmids.

Using the transfection kit (GoldenTran-R, GoldenTran Biotechnology, Changchun, China), HGC-27 cells and MKN-45 cells were transfected with either negative control small interfering RNA (siRNA) (si-NC) or siRNAs targeting RECQL (si-RECQL-1/2/3) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The siRNA sequences are as follows: si-NC, 5'-UAAAUCAGCUUUAACUCGGAG-3'; si-RECQL-1, 5'-UUAUUCUGAAUAUAUCUCCAA-3'; si-RECQL-2, 5'-UACCAUUTCAGCAUGAACCCA-3'; and si-RECQL-3, 5'-UAACUGGUAACAUAAGCUCUU-3'.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted using TriQuick reagent (R1100, Solarbio, Beijing, China), and complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized by reverse transcription using the SureScriptTM First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (QP056T, GeneCopeia, Rockville, MD, USA). qPCR was performed using 2× SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (None ROX) (G3320-05, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) on a real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR machine. GAPDH was used as the internal control, and the relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Data represent the mean of three biological replicates, each analyzed in technical triplicate. The primer sequences are as follows: GAPDH: forward primer (F), 5'-CAGGAGCGAGACCCCACTAA-3'; reverse primer (R), 5'-ATCACGCCACAGCTTTCCAG-3'. RECQL: forward primer (F), 5'-TGGGAATTCATGCAGGTGCT-3'; reverse primer (R), 5'-GCCACAACACCTGCTACTCT-3'.

Western blotting

Total protein was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (BN25012-100mL, Biorigin, Beijing, China) and quantified using the BCA protein assay kit (SL201, UtiBody, Tianjin, China). The protein was incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with the following primary antibodies: anti-RECQL (ab172607, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) and anti-β-actin (ab8226, Abcam). After washing, the membrane was incubated with goat anti-rabbit immunoglobulin G-horseradish peroxidase (IgG-HRP) secondary antibody (BN20601, Biorigin) at room temperature for 1.5 hours. Western blot analysis was conducted using three independent protein lysates to ensure reproducibility. Protein bands were visualized using the enhanced chemiluminescence detection kit (BN16009-100mL, Biorigin) and imaged with the ChemiScope6100 imaging system. Band intensity was quantified using ImageJ software.

Cell viability assay

si-NC or si-RECQL cells (2×103 cells/well) were seeded into a 96-well plate. Cell viability at 0, 24, 48, and 72 hours was quantitatively analyzed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay kit (C0037, Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and the absorbance at 450 nm was recorded using the Infinite F50 microplate reader (Tecan, Männedorf, Switzerland).

Colony formation assay

After transfection with si-NC or si-RECQL, HGC-27 cells were seeded into a 6-well plate at a density of 600 cells/well and cultured in a 37 ℃, 5% CO2 incubator for 2 weeks. Once the number of colonies reached over 20 per well, cells were washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (P0099, Beyotime) at room temperature for 30 minutes, and then stained with 0.1% crystal violet (G1062, Solarbio) for 1 hour. The culture plate was air-dried at room temperature, photographed, and colony formation was analyzed.

Wound healing assay

After culturing the si-NC or si-RECQL transfected HGC-27 cells to approximately 90% confluence, a straight-line scratch was made on the monolayer using a sterile 200 µL pipette tip. The cells were washed once with PBS and then cultured in serum-free medium. Images were captured at 0, 24, and 48 hours to assess the wound healing process.

Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) staining assay

Cell apoptosis was detected using the TUNEL assay kit (C1086, Beyotime). The cells were fixed with immunostaining fixative (P0098, Beyotime), permeabilized with immunostaining permeabilization solution (P0097, Beyotime), treated with TUNEL reaction mixture, and then counterstained with DAPI. Apoptosis was observed under a fluorescence microscope.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using R (v4.4.1). Differences between groups were assessed with the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and a P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

For experimental data, the Student’s t-test was used for comparisons between two groups, while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied for comparisons among three or more groups. Data are shown as mean ± standard deviation, with P<0.05 considered significant.

Results

Identification of key candidate genes in GC

Using the TCGA-STAD cohort, a weighted gene co-expression network was first constructed to identify modules associated with GC. The network exhibited scale-free topology at a soft-thresholding power of β=4 (fit index R2>0.85), while preserving gene connectivity (Figure 1A). A total of seven co-expression modules were identified, among which the turquoise module showed the strongest correlation with the GC phenotype (r=0.57, P=1.6×10−37) (Figure 1B,1C). This module contained 1,295 GC-associated genes (GC-AGs) (Figure 1D; table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0411-1.xlsx). Furthermore, differential expression analysis between tumor and normal tissues in the TCGA-STAD dataset identified 3,368 DEGs (TCGA-DEGs), comprising 2,863 upregulated and 505 downregulated genes (Figure 1E; table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0411-2.xlsx).

In the single-cell dataset GSE150290, after stringent quality control and dimensionality reduction, cells were clustered into 15 subpopulations, which were annotated into eight major cell types: B cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, T cells, endothelial cells, myeloid cells, neuroendocrine cells, and mast cells (Figure 2A,2B). Comparison between tumor and normal groups yielded 1,151 single-cell DEGs (SC-DEGs), including 815 upregulated and 336 downregulated genes (Figure 2C; table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0411-3.xlsx). Intersection analysis of GC-AGs with upregulated genes from both TCGA-DEGs-Up and SC-DEGs-Up identified 37 key candidate genes (Figure 2D).

GO enrichment analysis of the 37 key candidate genes revealed significant overrepresentation in 214 biological processes (BP), including regulation of response to wounding, regulation of wound healing, and wound healing; 36 cellular components (CC), such as apicolateral plasma membrane, collagen-containing extracellular matrix, and lateral plasma membrane; and 47 molecular functions (MF), including chondroitin sulfate binding, chemokine activity, and phosphatidylinositol phosphate binding (Figure 2E; table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0411-4.xlsx). In addition, KEGG pathway analysis indicated enrichment in eight signaling pathways, notably cornified envelope formation, complement and coagulation cascades, and the IL-17 signaling pathway (Figure 2F; table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0411-4.xlsx).

Identification and expression profiling of RECQL

To identify genes most predictive of clinical outcomes in GC, LASSO logistic regression was performed using patient survival status (alive vs. deceased) as the binary response variable. The analysis yielded 12 feature genes with independent prognostic significance (Figure 3A,3B). Among these, RECQL—a member of the RecQ helicase family—encodes a key enzyme involved in DNA unwinding and replication repair, playing a central role in maintaining genomic stability (17). Previous studies have demonstrated that RECQL is overexpressed in multiple malignancies and contributes to tumor proliferation, DNA damage repair, and chemoresistance (18). However, its biological role in GC remains poorly characterized. Accordingly, RECQL was selected for in-depth investigation. Across three independent cohorts—TCGA-STAD, GSE29272, and GSE66229—RECQL expression was consistently and significantly elevated in tumor tissues compared with normal counterparts (Figure 3C-3E).

Moreover, RECQL expression was markedly elevated in deceased patients compared with survivors (Figure 4D).

In the scRNA-seq dataset GSE150290, RECQL expression was likewise significantly higher in tumor samples relative to adjacent normal tissues (Figure 4E,4F). Cell-type-specific analysis revealed that RECQL exhibits significantly differential expression in epithelial cells, T cells, and fibroblasts (Figure 4G).

Functional enrichment analysis of RECQL high- and low-expression groups

To elucidate the potential biological functions of RECQL in GC, TCGA-STAD samples were stratified into high-expression [log2(TPM + 1) >4.26] and low-expression [log2(TPM + 1) <3.11] groups based on RECQL expression quartiles. Differential expression analysis was performed between the two groups, and KEGG pathway enrichment was conducted for genes significantly upregulated in the high-expression group. A total of 128 significantly enriched pathways were identified, primarily involving the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, extracellular matrix (ECM)-receptor interaction, and proteoglycans in cancer (Figure 5A; table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0411-5.xlsx).

(B) GSEA based on differential expression between RECQL high- and low-expression groups. (C) ClueGO-based Gene Ontology enrichment analysis of RECQL-associated genes. Further GSEA based on differential expression between high- and low-RECQL groups (dichotomized by median RECQL expression) identified 42 significantly enriched pathways in the high-expression subgroup (Figure 5B; table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0411-5.xlsx). These pathways were predominantly associated with cell-cycle regulation and genomic integrity, including E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, DNA repair and Wnt/β-catenin signaling.

Subsequent GO enrichment analysis using ClueGO identified 332 significantly enriched terms, with the top ten clusters primarily associated with cell division, chromosome segregation, DNA replication, and cell-cycle progression (Figure 5C; table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0411-6.xlsx).

Correlation between RECQL expression and immune cell infiltration

In the TCGA-STAD cohort, immune cell composition was estimated using the CIBERSORT algorithm. Significant differences in immune infiltration were observed between RECQL high- and low-expression groups (Figure 6A). Compared with the low-expression group, the high-expression group exhibited markedly elevated infiltration of M2 macrophages, resting natural killer (NK) cells, and activated CD4+ memory T cells, all of which showed strong positive correlations with RECQL expression (Figure 6B,6C). Conversely, activated NK cells and regulatory T cells (Tregs) displayed reduced infiltration in the RECQL high-expression group and were negatively correlated with RECQL expression (Figure 6B,6C). Analysis based on the ESTIMATE algorithm further indicated that the StromalScore, ImmuneScore, and composite ESTIMATEScore were all significantly higher in the RECQL high-expression group (Figure 6D).

Differential expression analysis of immune checkpoint-related genes revealed consistent upregulation of CD274, CD80, CD86, CTLA4, PDCD1, and PDCD1LG2 in the RECQL high-expression group, with each gene showing strong positive correlations with RECQL levels (Figure 7A,7B). In addition, TIDE analysis showed that the high-expression group exhibited significantly higher TIDE, Exclusion, interferon gamma (IFNG), and Merck18 scores compared with the low-expression group (Figure 7C-7F).

ST analysis based on the GSE251950 dataset revealed the spatial distribution pattern of RECQL and its colocalization with myeloid cells in GC tissue (Figure 7G). RECQL expression was predominantly enriched in the tumor parenchyma, whereas myeloid cells mainly accumulated in the stromal regions and partially within tumor nests. Notably, areas with high RECQL expression showed substantial spatial overlap with myeloid cell-enriched regions, suggesting that RECQL may play a potential role in regulating myeloid cell recruitment within the TME.

Analysis of drug sensitivity and molecular binding stability of RECQL

To evaluate the clinical diagnostic and prognostic value of RECQL in GC, ROC curve analysis was conducted using the TCGA-STAD and GSE29272 datasets. The area under the curve (AUC) reached 0.78 in TCGA-STAD and 0.71 in GSE29272 (Figure 8A,8B). Subsequent survival analyses in the TCGA-STAD and GSE15459 cohorts demonstrated that patients with high RECQL expression had significantly poorer overall survival (OS) compared with those in the low-expression group (Figure 8C,8D). These findings suggest that RECQL possesses potential diagnostic utility and high RECQL expression is closely associated with poor prognosis in GC.

To explore the potential role of RECQL in drug responsiveness, the OncoPredict algorithm was applied to estimate the IC50 of multiple anticancer agents in the TCGA-STAD cohort. Correlation analyses were then performed between RECQL expression levels and predicted IC50 values. Significant differences were observed between the high- and low-expression groups across several compounds, including cediranib_1922, UMI-77_1939, Ozasertib_1096, ZM447439_1050, and GSK2606414_1618. Notably, the expression of RECQL exhibited a strong negative correlation with the IC50 values of these drugs (Figure 8E,8F). Among these, cediranib showed the most pronounced correlation, implying that RECQL may serve as a potential novel binding target of cediranib.

To further validate the interaction between RECQL and cediranib, molecular docking and MD simulations were performed. Docking analysis revealed that cediranib binds stably within the active pocket of RECQL, comprising residues SER120, GLN124, GLN147, VAL150, ASP379, and ARG407, with a calculated binding energy of −8.7 kcal/mol, and forms two stable hydrogen bonds (Figure 8G-8I). During a 10-ns MD simulation, the RECQL-cediranib complex exhibited pronounced structural stability. The backbone RMSD stabilized after approximately 3 ns, maintaining an average of 0.26 nm with fluctuations below 0.1 nm (Figure 8J). The short-range Lennard-Jones (LJ-SR) interaction energy remained stable at around −250 kJ/mol, indicating persistent van der Waals and hydrophobic interactions throughout the simulation (Figure 8K). RMSF analysis confirmed minimal residue-level movement, suggesting that the overall protein conformation remained compact without significant relaxation or unfolding (Figure 8L). The Rg was consistently maintained between 2.62 and 2.67 nm, showing no notable expansion or collapse of the protein structure (Figure 8M). Moreover, hydrogen-bond dynamics analysis indicated that 1–3 hydrogen bonds were preserved during the trajectory, underscoring their critical role in maintaining complex stability (Figure 8N).

Knockdown of RECQL affects the cellular functions of GC cells

Based on the transcriptional analysis of RECQL expression, we further validated the expression of RECQL in clinical and cell samples. Both qRT-PCR and western blot analyses showed that, compared to adjacent normal tissues, RECQL was significantly upregulated in GC tissues (Figure 9A,9B). Similarly, in the cell model, we measured the expression levels of RECQL in normal gastric mucosal epithelial cells (GES-1) and three GC cell lines (MKN-45, HGC-27, and AGS). The qRT-PCR and western blot results confirmed that RECQL was significantly overexpressed in GC cells, with the highest expression observed in HGC-27 cells (Figure 9C,9D).

To investigate the biological function of RECQL in GC, three siRNAs were designed to silence its expression. All three siRNAs effectively reduced RECQL expression and significantly suppressed HGC-27 cell viability (Figure 10A-10C), with si-RECQL-1 demonstrating the highest knockdown efficiency; it was therefore selected for subsequent functional experiments. Treatment with si-RECQL-1 markedly reduced the proliferation and migration capacities of HGC-27 cells and significantly promoted cell apoptosis (Figure 10D-10F). To assess the cross-cell-line consistency of these findings, the knockdown experiment was repeated in MKN-45 cells, where si-RECQL-1 again exhibited the highest knockdown efficiency at both the mRNA and protein levels (Figure S1A,S1B). CCK-8 assays further confirmed that RECQL knockdown significantly reduced MKN-45 cell viability (Figure S1C), recapitulating the phenotype observed in HGC-27 cells and indicating that the regulatory role of RECQL in GC cell viability is not cell-line-specific.

Discussion

RECQL, an ATP-dependent DNA helicase belonging to the RecQ helicase family, plays a pivotal role in DNA replication, recombination, and repair, and is regarded as an essential guardian of genomic stability (19). Previous studies have demonstrated that RECQL is aberrantly overexpressed in various malignancies and is closely associated with tumor aggressiveness, proliferative capacity, and poor clinical outcomes, including in hematologic malignancies, multiple myeloma, and gliomas (18,20). However, the expression characteristics and functional implications of RECQL in GC remain poorly elucidated, particularly with respect to its involvement in the tumor immune microenvironment. The results of this study indicate that high expression of RECQL is associated with poor prognosis. RECQL is highly expressed in both clinical GC tissues and GC cell lines, and knocking down RECQL effectively suppresses the malignant phenotype of HGC-27 cells.

The GSEA analysis in this study revealed that high expression of RECQL is closely associated with DNA repair pathway. Previous research has shown that GC cells often exhibit persistent activation of DNA repair mechanisms to cope with elevated replication stress and DNA damage (21). However, when the DNA repair machinery becomes dysregulated or excessively activated, the accuracy of repair may decline, leading to accumulation of genetic mutations and chromosomal instability, thereby promoting uncontrolled cell proliferation and malignant transformation (22). RECQL has been found to be highly expressed in rapidly proliferating cells. Acute depletion of RECQL by RNA interference significantly increases the sensitivity of cells to DNA damage, with the mechanism being the impairment of DNA double-strand break repair function (23). This suggests that RECQL is a key component in the DNA repair pathway. Notably, RecQL4, an important member of the RecQ helicase family, becomes overactivated to enhance the DNA repair capacity of tumor cells, enabling rapid repair of radio-/chemotherapy-induced DNA breaks and thereby maintaining genomic stability while facilitating immune evasion and resistance to apoptosis (24). Likewise, the RecQ helicase BLM facilitates the repair of therapy-associated DNA damage, thereby increasing tumor cell survival and augmenting drug resistance (25). Taken together, these findings suggest that elevated RECQL expression in GC may foster malignant progression and therapeutic resistance by enhancing DNA repair activity. Nevertheless, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying these effects require further experimental validation.

CIBERSORT deconvolution revealed a significantly higher proportion of M2 macrophages in the RECQL high-expression group, concordant with prior work by Wang et al. showing a positive correlation between RECQL levels and macrophage infiltration (20). M2 macrophages are a subtype of macrophages and constitute a principal immunosuppressive subset in GC (26,27); they accelerate disease progression by promoting angiogenesis, stromal remodeling, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and facilitate dissemination through regulation of peritoneal metastasis and secretion of extracellular vesicles (28). In addition, M2 macrophages secrete anti-inflammatory cytokines—including IL-10, IL-13, and IL-4—that dampen effector T-cell function and foster a tumor-permissive milieu (29). These observations suggest that elevated RECQL may contribute to an immunosuppressive TME by promoting M2 macrophage recruitment and polarization. Consistent with this, ST indicated spatial colocalization between RECQL-high regions in the tumor parenchyma and myeloid-cell-enriched areas, supporting a role for RECQL in myeloid recruitment and microenvironmental remodeling.

RECQL high-expression samples also exhibited marked upregulation of immune checkpoint molecules—PDCD1, CTLA4, and CD274—together with elevated TIDE scores. High expression of checkpoint molecules and high TIDE scores typically indicate T-cell exhaustion, an immunosuppressive state linked to poorer prognosis and limited efficacy of immunotherapy (30,31). Taken together, these data suggest that high RECQL expression may drive immune evasion by coordinating M2 macrophages polarization and checkpoint upregulation, thereby reinforcing an immunosuppressive TME that weakens antitumor immunity and may limit immunotherapeutic efficacy.

Drug-sensitivity analyses indicated that RECQL expression may modulate GC responsiveness to multiple anticancer agents. RECQL levels showed a significant negative correlation with the predicted IC50 of several drugs, with the strongest association observed for cediranib. Cediranib is a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor that chiefly suppresses VEGFR signaling to inhibit angiogenesis and has demonstrated antitumor activity across solid tumors (32). Beyond canonical anti-angiogenic effects, cediranib can also perturb cell-cycle progression and inhibit DNA-repair pathways, thereby restraining tumor growth (33,34). This aligns with the present GSEA findings, in which high RECQL expression—consistent with a DNA-repair–linked profile—was associated with activation of repair programs. A plausible interpretation is that cediranib may interfere with RECQL-mediated DNA repair, increasing tumor sensitivity to genotoxic stress; definitive mechanisms remain to be established. Supporting a direct interaction, molecular docking and MD simulations indicated that cediranib engages a putative active pocket on RECQL via hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic contacts, forming a stable complex. These data suggest that RECQL may serve as a potential target of cediranib.

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged when interpreting the findings. First, the clinical validation cohort is relatively limited in size (n=15), primarily attributable to the stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria applied and the logistical requirements of paired fresh tissue collection. This cohort was designed to serve as exploratory validation, providing preliminary confirmation of bioinformatically identified candidates at the protein and mRNA levels in real patient tissues; the core conclusions of this study are principally supported by systematic analyses across multiple independent public database cohorts. Nevertheless, the limited sample size constrains statistical power and the generalizability of the findings to some extent, and future studies in larger, multicenter, prospective cohorts are warranted to further validate the conclusions of the present study.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that RECQL is highly expressed in GC, and its high expression is closely associated with poor prognosis, enhanced DNA repair activity, and an immunosuppressive TME. Knockdown of RECQL significantly reduced the proliferation and migration abilities of HGC27 cells while enhancing apoptosis. Furthermore, it is suggested that sunitinib may directly interact with RECQL, highlighting a potential new therapeutic mechanism. Taken together, these findings suggest that RECQL could be a potential biomarker and therapeutic target in GC.
