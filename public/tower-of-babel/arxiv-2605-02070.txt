Sharp regret–Hellinger bounds for Gaussian empirical Bayes via polynomial approximation
Jiafeng Chen and Yihong Wu

Abstract
A central problem in the theory of empirical Bayes is to control the regret (excess risk) of a learned Bayes rule by the Hellinger distance between the estimated and true marginal densities. In the normal means model, the classical result of Jiang and Zhang [ 14 ] achieves this only after regularizing the Bayes rule and incurs an extraneous cubic logarithmic factor through a delicate recursive argument. This paper introduces a new technique, based on polynomial approximation and Bernstein-type inequalities for weighted L 2 L_{2} norms, that bounds the unregularized regret directly. The method is conceptually simpler and yields sharper, sometimes optimal, regret bounds. For compactly supported priors, we prove the sharp bound that the regret is at most O ⁡ ( ϵ 2 ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ ) log ⁡ log ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ ) ) O(\epsilon^{2}\frac{\log(1/\epsilon)}{\log\log(1/\epsilon)}) , where ϵ \epsilon is the Hellinger distance between the marginal densities. The same method also extends to priors with exponential tails. Conversely, we show that regularization is genuinely necessary for heavy-tailed priors under only bounded moment assumptions. As a statistical consequence, we obtain improved regret bounds for the nonparametric maximum likelihood estimator.

Introduction

Empirical Bayes (EB) is a powerful framework for large-scale statistical inference that applies Bayesian methods with a data-driven prior, thereby adapting to the latent structure in the data
[

A standard setting for EB is the normal means model.
Consider
\theta\sim G

is independent of
\theta

(1)

This estimator minimizes the mean-squared error (MSE) among all estimators of
\theta

\min_{\widehat{\theta}(\cdot)}\mathbb{E}_{G}[(\theta-\widehat{\theta}(Y))^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{G}[(\theta-m_{G}(Y))^{2}]
.

In the context of empirical Bayes,

When

\mathsf{Regret}(H\|G):=\mathbb{E}_{G}[(m_{H}(Y)-m_{G}(Y))^{2}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{f_{G}^{\prime}}{f_{G}}-\frac{f_{H}^{\prime}}{f_{H}}\right)^{2}f_{G}dy,

This quantity is also known as

In practice—and especially in the

Widely used approaches for learning the prior include maximum likelihood and minimum distance, both of which aim at approximating the true data-generating distribution

We present a new technique for bounding regret in terms of Hellinger

Compared to the recursive argument of Jiang and Zhang, the proposed method is conceptually simpler

The major contributions of this paper are as follows.

•

For priors with compact support, we show that

Proof ideas.

As discussed earlier, controlling regret in terms of Hellinger distance entails bounding a derivative in terms of the original function.
Such a bound is clearly impossible in general, but it becomes possible for certain classes
of regular functions, most notably
polynomials
. This crucial insight is gained
through interactions with GPT-5.4 using OpenAI Codex. It motivates the following
program:

1.

Identify a target function
g
and a weight function
w
such that
the Hellinger distance
d_{\rm H}^{2}(f_{G},f_{H})
is largely determined by

\|g\|_{L_{2}(w)}^{2}:=\int g^{2}wdy
and the regret
\mathsf{Regret}(H\|G)
by

\|g^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(w)}^{2}
.

2.

Show that the weight
w
satisfies a
Bernstein-type inequality
:
for any degree-
k
polynomial
p
,

\|p^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(w)}\leq a_{k}\|p\|_{L_{2}(w)}

(8)

3.

Quantify the approximation error: find a degree-
k
polynomial
p
so that
\|g-p\|_{L_{2}(w)}
and

\|g^{\prime}-p^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(w)}
is bounded in terms of
k
.

Putting these ingredients together and optimizing the degree yield a regret–Hellinger inequality.

There are multiple ways to execute this program. To fix ideas, let us focus on the class of compactly supported priors. One may choose

g=\frac{f_{G}-f_{H}}{f_{G}+f_{H}}
and
w=(f_{G}+f_{H})/2=f_{(G+H)/2}*\varphi
,
in order to capitalize on the classical Bernstein inequality for the standard Gaussian density
\varphi
(an immediate consequence of properties of Hermite polynomials; see Remark
1
):

\|p^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(\varphi)}\leq\sqrt{k}\|p\|_{L_{2}(\varphi)}

(9)

and hence (by linearity) for any Gaussian mixture. In addition,
the best degree-
k
polynomial approximation error of this choice of
g
is at most
\mathop{\rm exp}(-\Omega(\sqrt{k}))
.
Choosing
k=\Theta((\log\frac{1}{\epsilon})^{2})
leads to
\mathsf{Regret}(H\|G)=O(\epsilon^{2}(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon})^{2})
, which already improves over the state of the art of
[
14
]
and does so without regularization.

To obtain the sharp inequality, we instead choose

g=\frac{f_{G}-f_{H}}{\varphi},\quad w=\frac{\varphi^{2}}{(f_{G}+f_{H})/2}.

This
g
admits a substantially better polynomial approximation error of
\mathop{\rm exp}(-\Omega(k\log k))
. In addition, we prove a Bernstein-type inequality
(
8
) for the non-standard weight
w
with
a_{k}=O(\sqrt{k})
.
Setting

k=\Theta(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}/\log\log\frac{1}{\epsilon})

yields the desired (
7
).
We note that there is a sizable literature on Bernstein-type inequalities for weighted
norms (see, e.g.,
[
20
,
22
]
); however these results
require additional assumptions that appear difficult to verify in our setting. Instead, we
present self-contained proofs using a linear algebraic argument, as the inequality (
8
) boils down to bounding the operator norm of the matrix representing the differentiation operator on the space of polynomials.

Main results

2.1
New Regret–Hellinger inequality

Theorem 1
.

Let
G,H
be supported on
[-M,M]
.
There exists a constant
C=C(M)
such that

\mathsf{Regret}(H\|G)\leq C\epsilon^{2}\frac{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{\log\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}

where
\epsilon^{2}=d_{\rm H}^{2}(f_{G},f_{H})
.

The following result shows that the regret upper bound in Theorem
1
is sharp up to constants:

Theorem 2
.

There exists a sequence
(G_{m},H_{m})
all supported on
[-1,1]
, such that

\epsilon_{m}^{2}\equiv d_{\rm H}^{2}(f_{G_{m}},f_{H_{m}})\to 0
and

\mathsf{Regret}(H_{m}\|G_{m})\geq c_{0}\epsilon_{m}^{2}\frac{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon_{m}}}{\log\log\frac{1}{\epsilon_{m}}}

where
c_{0}
is an absolute constant.

We now extend Theorem
1
to priors with exponential tails.
For
\alpha,\sigma>0
, define

{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}(\sigma)=\{\text{Law}(U):\mathbb{E}[\mathop{\rm exp}((|U|/\sigma)^{\alpha}]\leq 2\}.

(10)

In the special case of
\alpha=2
, this is the class of subgaussian distributions with
proxy variance proportional to
\sigma^{2}
; for
\alpha=1
, it is the subexponential
class.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G,H\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}(\sigma)
.
There exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,\sigma)
such that

\mathsf{Regret}(H\|G)\leq\begin{cases}C\epsilon^{2}\left(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{2/\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}&\alpha\leq 2\\
C\epsilon^{2}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}&\alpha>2\end{cases}

where
\epsilon^{2}=d_{\rm H}^{2}(f_{G},f_{H})
.

For heavy-tailed priors—for instance, those satisfying only a bounded
p
th-moment
condition—we show in Appendix
C
that regret can be polynomially larger than
the squared Hellinger distance. Hence the regularization of
[
14
]
is not
merely a proof device but is in fact necessary in order to obtain a regret nearly linear
in
d_{\rm H}^{2}
.

Next we discuss implications of these

State-of-the-art regret bound:

By (

) and (

Appendix C
Counterexamples for moment class

Recall that Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
bound the regret proportionally to the squared Hellinger distance up to logarithmic factors for priors with compact support or exponential tails.
In this appendix, we show that such
nearly linear regret bound fails for moment class.

Proposition 14
.

Fix
p>0
. There exists
(G_{m},H_{m})
such that
\epsilon_{m}^{2}=H^{2}(f_{G_{m}},f_{H_{m}})\to 0
and

\mathsf{Regret}(H_{m}\|G_{m})\geq c\epsilon_{m}^{2-2/p}

for some constant
c
.

Proof.

For
b>0
, let

H=\delta_{0},\qquad G=(1-\eta)\delta_{0}+\eta\delta_{b},\qquad\eta=b^{-p}

both with
p
th moment at most 1.

Note that
f_{H}(y)=\phi(y)
, and
f_{G}(y)=(1-\eta)\phi(y)+\eta\phi(y-b)
.
By data processing inequality,

H^{2}(f_{G},f_{H})\leq H^{2}(\text{Bern}(\delta),\text{Bern}(0))\leq 2\eta.

To lower bound the regret,
note that the Bayes estimator under
H
is
m_{H}(y)=0
so

\mathsf{Regret}(H\|G)=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[U|Y]^{2}]=\mathrm{Var}(U)-\mathbb{E}[(U-\mathbb{E}[U|Y])^{2}],

where
U\sim G\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}Z\sim N(0,1)
, and
Y=U+Z
.
Write
U=bW
with
W\sim\text{Bern}(\eta)
.
Then
\mathrm{Var}(U)=\eta(1-\eta)b^{2}
. To bound the conditional variance from above, consider a suboptimal estimator
\widehat{U}=b\widehat{W}
and
\widehat{W}={1\{{Y\geq b/2}\}}
.
Then

\mathbb{P}[W\neq\widehat{W}]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[|Z|\geq b/2\right]\leq\mathop{\rm exp}(-b^{2}/8)
and hence

\mathbb{E}[(U-\mathbb{E}[U|Y])^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[(U-\widehat{U})^{2}]\leq b^{2}\mathop{\rm exp}(-b^{2}/8)

In all,

\mathsf{Regret}(H\|G)\geq b^{2}(\eta(1-\eta)-\mathop{\rm exp}(-b^{2}/8)).

∎
