ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectivesThis study describes the human leukocyte antigen (HLA) Class II allele frequencies in patients with anti-CNTN1+ and anti-Caspr1+ autoimmune nodopathy (AN).MethodsForty-four AN patients and 50 seronegative CIDP patients from 19 different European hospitals were included in the study. Thirty AN patients had anti-contactin 1 (CNTN1) antibodies, 11 anti–contactin-associated protein 1 (Caspr1) antibodies, and 3 had antibodies against both proteins. HLA-DRB1 was genotyped at the 4-digit allele levels, and the percentage of individuals carrying each allele was compared with that of the general population, obtained from the Allele frequencies database.ResultsHLA-DRB1*11 alleles appeared in higher proportions in anti-CNTN1+ patients than in seronegative CIDP patients and in the general population (46.7% vs 18% vs 28.4%), with an odds ratio of 3.99 (CI = 1.44 to 11.03, p = 0.01) and 2.2 (CI = 1.07 to 4.53, p = 0.04), respectively. HLA-DRB1*03:01 alleles appeared in significantly higher proportions in anti-Caspr1+ patients than in CIDP patients and in the general population (64.3% vs 22% vs 24.2%), with an odds ratio of 6.38 (CI = 1.77 to 22.99, p = 0.007) and 5.64 (CI = 1.876 to 16.96, p = 0.002), respectively. In the anti-Caspr1+ group, we included 3 patients presenting with antibodies against both CNTN1 and Caspr1 proteins in the acute phase, in which the anti-CNTN1 antibodies disappeared in the chronic phase.DiscussionHLA-DRB1*11 alleles are associated with the detection of anti-CNTN1 antibodies in AN patients, and HLA-DRB1*03:01 alleles associate with anti-Caspr1 antibodies. In addition, our study suggests that antiparanodal antibodies targeting both Caspr1 and CNTN1 are present in a small number of patients with AN. These data reinforce the idea that these patients represent specific subsets with clinical features and risk factors that differ from seronegative CIDP patients and from other AN patients. However, further studies should address the functional relevance of these associations and their pathophysiologic implications.

Introduction

Autoimmune nodopathies (AN) are peripheral nerve disorders associated with antibodies targeting the node of Ranvier, such as neurofascin 155 (NF155),1,2 contactin 1 (CNTN1),3,4 contactin-associated protein 1 (Caspr1),5,6 or pan-neurofascin (pan-NF).7,8 They account for 5–10% of patients fulfilling the diagnostic criteria of chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP).9 They are now considered a differential diagnostic category due to their specific clinical, pathologic, and response to treatment features.10

Patients with anti-CNTN1 antibodies display a rapidly progressive sensorimotor neuropathy with predominant distal weakness and sensory ataxia and, frequently, poor response to IV immunoglobulins (IVIg).3,11 Anti-Caspr1 phenotype is characterized by an acute or subacute onset with sensorimotor involvement that includes early axonal motor damage, sensory ataxia, pain, facial weakness, ophthalmoparesis, or respiratory failure.12 Recently, double-positive antibodies targeting both Caspr1 and CNTN1 proteins separately were reported in 2 patients with acute-onset neuropathies.13

Anti-CNTN1 and anti-Caspr1 antibodies are predominantly IgG4, an immunoglobulin subclass unable to activate complement and with reduced Fcγ-receptor mediated activation of inflammatory cells, although in the acute phase of the disease, these antibodies may also be IgG3.9,13 The etiological factors that drive the production of these autoantibodies are unknown, and genetic risk factors associated to their appearance have not been reported.

Previous studies confirmed a strong association between anti-NF155 AN and the allele HLA-DRB1*15 from the human leukocyte antigen (HLA) class II.2,14 Of the genes that predispose to systemic or organ-specific autoimmune diseases, HLA is the most relevant group. The HLA class II locus on chromosome 6 consists of a set of polymorphic genes which haplotypes products are involved in out important for antigen presentation. For B cells HLA class II-mediated antigen presentation along with other co-stimulatory signals promotes class switching, memory formation and associated with antibody production, and HLA class II alleles strongly associate with several IgG4-mediated diseases.15-19 However, the relationship between HLA genotypes and anti-CNTN1 and anti-Caspr1 AN has not been studied.

This study describes the HLA class II DRB allele frequencies in anti-CNTN1 and anti-Caspr1 positive patients, to assess whether there is any genetic susceptibility factor that could drive the development of the disease.

Methods

Patients and Samples

Patients testing positive for anti-CNTN1 or CASPR1 antibodies identified in routine clinical testing from which DNA was available were included over the 2015–2024 period. Forty-four autoimmune nodopathy patients and 50 seronegative CIDP patients10 from 19 different European hospitals were included in the study. Thirty AN patients had anti-CNTN1 antibodies, 11 anti-Caspr1 antibodies and 3 had antibodies against both proteins (CNTN1 and Caspr1). Serum and DNA samples were obtained, processed and frozen at −20°C until needed.

Anti-CNTN1 and Anti-Caspr1 Antibody Detection

Antibodies against CNTN1 were detected by a cell-based assay (CBA) with human CNTN1 transfected HEK293, and antibodies against Caspr1 were detected by ELISA and confirmed with CBA, as described elsewhere.4,6

In patients with antibodies against both CNTN1 and Caspr1, IgG subclasses and pretreatment and post-treatment antibody titers were investigated by ELISA as previously described.6 Clinical data of these patients were retrospectively collected.

HLA Genotyping

Genomic DNA from peripheral blood of the 44 AN patients included in the study was extracted following standard protocols. HLA-DRB1 genotypes were determined at the 4-digit allele levels using DNA sequence analysis following manufacturers' instructions for HLA-DRB1 (SBT Excellerator GenDx HLA-DRB1, GenDx, Utrecht, The Netherlands). HLA-DRB1 genotypes of the 50 seronegative CIDP patients were extracted from a previous study.14

In addition, the whole spectrum of class I and class II HLA alleles of the 3 patients with antibodies against both CNTN1 and CASPR1 was genotyped using the NGSgo kits (GenDX) following manufacturers' instructions.

The percentage of individuals carrying each allele (carrier frequency) was calculated and compared with the carrier frequency in the general population, which were obtained from the Allele frequencies database.20 We chose the reference populations according to geographical proximity to the participant Institutions and availability of data of the alleles of interest. To control for the different HLA frequencies in each local population from where the samples were obtained, the allele frequencies were weighted according to the country of origin of the positive AN patients included in the study.

In Silico HLA-Peptide Binding and CNTN1 or CASPR1 Antigenicity Predictions

In silico studies were performed to predict HLA-peptide binding and CNTN1 or CASPR1 antigenicity, and to analyze overlap between presented peptides and antigenic regions.

To predict peptides binding with higher affinity to those HLA alleles associated with anti-CNTN1 or anti-CASPR1 antibodies, we used the IEDB NetMHCIIpan (ver. 4.1)21 and ProPred22 tools. In addition, the Kolaskar and Tongaonkar antigenicity scale was used to predict CNTN1 and CASPR1 antigenic targets.23

For that purpose, the human CNTN1 and CASPR1 protein sequences obtained from UniProt (accessions number Q12860 and P78357) were submitted to each server.

Statistical Analyses

Fisher exact test, odds ratio, and confidence interval calculations were performed. Adjusted p values for contingency table analysis were calculated using post hoc Bonferroni corrections. Statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism v8.0 package.

Standard Protocol Approvals, Registrations, and Patient Consents

This study was conducted according to a protocol approved by the Ethics Committee of the Hospital de la Santa Creu i Sant Pau (code IIBSP-NAI-2022-88). All patients gave written informed consent to participate in the study.

Data Availability

Anonymized data not published within this article will be made available by request from any qualified investigator.

Results

Association of Anti-CNTN1 Antibodies With HLA-DRB1*11

We included 30 anti-CNTN1 AN patients in the study. Mean age was 68 years, patients were predominantly men (76.7%) and 34.6% of them had at least one concomitant autoimmune disease.

The proportion of patients carrying HLA-DRB1*11 alleles, including HLA-DRB1*11:01, HLA-DRB1*11:02, HLA-DRB1*11:03, and HLA-DRB1*11:04, was higher in anti-CNTN1+ patients than in seronegative CIDP patients (46.7% vs 18%), with an odds ratio of 3.99 (CI = 1.44 to 11.03, p = 0.01). When comparing the proportion of patients carrying HLA-DRB1*11 alleles in anti-CNTN1+ patients with the general population, the differences were also statistically significant (46,7% vs 28.4%; OR = 2.2, CI = 1.07 to 4.53, p = 0.04).

Moreover, although only 6 of 30 anti-CNTN1+ patients carried HLA-DRB1*14 alleles (HLA-DRB1*14:01 and HLA-DRB1*14:04), the proportion of carriers of these alleles in anti-CNTN1+ patients was higher than in seronegative CIDP patients (20% vs 2%; OR = 12.25; CI = 1.39 to 107.6; p = 0.01) and normal population (20% vs 5.7%; OR = 4.11; CI = 1.66 to 10.14; p = 0.068).

Association of Anti-Caspr1 Antibodies With HLA-DRB1*03:01

We included 11 anti-Caspr1 positive and 3 double anti-Caspr1 and anti-CNTN1 positive AN patients (with antibodies against both proteins) in the study. Patients with anti-Caspr1+ AN had a median age at onset of 67 years, were predominantly female (54.5%) and 33.3% of them had at least one concomitant autoimmune disease. The 3 patients with anti-Caspr1 and anti-CNTN1+ AN were men, had a median age of 71 years and did not have any associated autoimmune condition.

HLA-DRB1*03:01 alleles were present in 6 anti-Caspr1+ patients (54,5%), 3 double anti-Caspr1 and anti-CNTN1+ patients (100%) and 11 CIDP patients (22%). In the double anti-Caspr1/CNTN1-positive patient P44, the anti-CNTN1 antibodies disappeared in the chronic phase, with only the anti-Caspr1 antibodies remaining; in patient P42 the anti-CNTN1 antibodies remained at very low titers; and in patient P43 both antibodies (anti-Caspr1 and anti-CNTN1) disappeared completely. We included these patients in the anti-Caspr1 AN group. Overall, HLA-DRB1*03:01 alleles appeared in significantly higher proportions in anti-Caspr1+ patients than in seronegative CIDP patients (64.3 vs 22%; OR = 6.38, CI = 1.77 to 22.99, p = 0.0068). When comparing the presence of HLA-DRB1*03:01 alleles in anti-Caspr1+ patients with the general population, the differences were also statistically significant (64.3 vs 24.2%; OR = 5.64, CI = 1.876 to 16.96, p = 0.0018).

In Silico Analyses

Specific CNTN1 peptides displayed a high predicted affinity to DRB1*11 alleles according to HLA-peptide affinity prediction tools.

These experiments showed a significant overlap between the ProPred and IEDB prediction methods. Moreover, we observed that the 4 DRB1*11 alleles studied (11:01 to 11:04) were predicted to present similar sets of peptides, suggesting functional similarity. The CNTN1 peptides predicted to be more antigenic by the Kolaskar and Tongaonkar score significantly overlapped with those predicted to be presented with higher affinities by DRB1*11 according to the HLA-peptide affinity prediction tools.

By contrast, in silico studies performed to predict HLA-DRB1*3 peptide binding and CASPR1 antigenicity did not show any overlap between presented peptides and antigenic regions.

All these results are shown in eAppendix 1 and eAppendix 2.

Patients With Antibodies Against Caspr1 and CNTN1

One recent study reported the coexistence of antibodies against Caspr1 and CNTN1 in 2 patients with acute-onset neuropathies.13 Based on these observations, we investigated the coexistence of these 2 antibodies in available samples from the patients included in this study. We detected 3 double positive patients for CNTN1 and for Caspr1, all 3 patients had an aggressive and rapid-onset neuropathy. Clinical data from these 3 patients are summarized in eAppendix 3.

As presented in table 3, in 2 patients (P42, P43), IgG4 was the predominant subclass but also IgG3 antibodies were detected, while in the remaining patient, antibodies were mainly of the IgG3 subclass. These differences could be explained by the time of sample extraction, since in the third patient (P44) the onset was acute and the diagnosis and initiation of treatment with steroids and rituximab were rapid. In patient P44, only anti-Caspr1 antibodies were detected in the post-treatment sample, whereas no antibodies were detected in the sample taken after plasmapheresis in patient P43. In patient P42, the anti-CNTN1 titles decreased much more dramatically than anti-Caspr1 titles (which showed only one dilution change in the ELISA).

Discussion

Our study describes the association between the presence of anti-Caspr1 antibodies in AN patients and the HLA-DRB1*03:01 alleles (present in 64.3% of cases) and between anti-CNTN1 AN and the HLA-DRB1*11 alleles (present in 47% of cases), compared with the reference populations.

This is the second study describing the correlation of AN patients with a genetic risk factor. In a previous study, we found a strong association between patients with autoantibodies against the NF155 protein and HLA-DRB1*15 alleles.2,14 In the case of anti-CNTN1+ and anti-Caspr1+ AN, the association that we describe is not as strong as in anti-NF155+ AN15, suggesting that HLA does not play such an important role in the development of these disease variants. Thus, the presence of HLA-DRB1*11 or HLA-DRB1*03:01 might increase the risk of having anti-CNTN1 or anti-Caspr1 antibodies (respectively) synergistically with other risk factors.24,25 HLA-associated increased risk of autoimmunity against CNTN1 and/or Caspr1 may also explain recurrence of autoimmune nodopathy with target antigen switch after years of serologic remission in rare cases.13,26

Interestingly, previous studies examining HLA associations in European CIDP cohorts have reported an increased frequency of HLA-DRB1*11 in a Greek cohort27 and of HLA-DRB1*03 in an Italian cohort.28 By contrast, other studies found no association between CIDP and any HLA alelle.29 Moreover, we observed significant differences in DRB1*11 (in the case of CNTN1+patients) and DRB1*3 (in the case of CASPR1+ patients) allele frequencies compared with our seronegative CIDP cohort. These findings support that the presence of these specific autoantibodies defines a distinct immunogenetic subgroup within the broader spectrum of CIDP, reinforcing the notion of heterogeneity in this diseases.

Regarding patients with antibodies against both CNTN1 and Caspr1 proteins, one recent study reported the coexistence of antibodies against these 2 proteins, in 2 patients with acute-onset neuropathies.13 In the first patient, anti-CNTN1 and anti-Caspr1 antibodies were of the IgG2 subclass and were negative during follow-up. The second patient had anti-CNTN1 and anti-Caspr1 IgG3 antibodies that evolved to anti-Caspr1 IgG4 antibodies during follow-up, while anti-CNTN1 antibodies became negative. Thus, we investigated the coexistence of these 2 antibodies in the patients included in the study and detected 3 double positive patients for CNTN1 and Caspr1.

Our observations confirm that anti-paranodal antibodies targeting both Caspr1 and CNTN1 epitopes are present in a small number of patients with a clinical picture of CIDP. These patients present in the most initial phase antibodies against both epitopes, CNTN1 and Caspr1, but in the follow-up (post-treatment) the antibodies recognize only Caspr1. Interestingly, these 3 patients shared the same HLA (HLA-DRB1*03:01 allele, that in our study was associated with anti-Caspr1 AN susceptibility). Response to treatment in these 3 patients was indistinguishable to the rest of the cohort, including poor response to IVIg and response was excellent in the patient that was treated with rituximab, despite having IgG3 predominant antibodies.30 In summary, we can differentiate 3 populations of patients with antibodies to the CNTN1/Caspr1 complex: (1) patients with antibodies anti-CNTN1 that are positive by CNTN1-containing CBA and by CNTN1 ELISA; (2) patients with anti-Caspr1 antibodies that are positive by Caspr1/CNTN1 CBA and by Caspr1 ELISA but negative for CNTN1 CBA and ELISA; and (3) patients with antibodies targeting both Caspr1 and CNTN1 epitopes, that are positive by CNTN1 and Caspr1 ELISA and by Caspr1/CNTN1 and CNTN1 CBA. This last group behaves in the follow-up like the anti-Caspr1 patients and shares the same HLA susceptibility allele.

In silico data showed that DRB1*11 alleles can present peptides derived from the CNTN1 protein with strong predicted affinity and that the CNTN1 peptides predicted to be more antigenic are located at the beginning of the protein, in a region that does not correspond to any specific domain. Although the exact epitopes recognized by patients' antibodies are unknown yet, Labasque et al.31 suggested that the immunoreactivity was directed to the Ig domains of CNTN1. Regarding in silico analyses of Caspr1, we could not find any overlap between presented peptides by DRB1*3 and antigenic regions within the Caspr1 protein. However, since not all anti-CNTN1 patients shared the DRB1*11 allele and the predicted sequences were not necessarily the ones targeted by the autoantibodies, pathogenicity of the in silico detected peptides cannot be inferred directly from these data and proper in vitro studies challenging T cells with these peptides should be developed.

The main limitation of this work is its sample size. This limitation reduces the statistical power to detect true associations and results in wide confidence intervals for the estimated odds ratios, reflecting a degree of statistical fragility. Anti-CNTN1+ and anti-Caspr1+ AN patients are extremely rare, and this is the reason why many centers were needed to recruit the study population. This fact conditioned the second main limitation of the study, that is the heterogeneity of the population, including patients from 12 different countries. HLA allele frequencies differ significantly across populations, and there are no reports that include HLA frequencies from mixed European populations. To overcome this point, we calculated a weighted average allele frequency of the reference populations at the country level using the number of anti-CNTN1+ or anti-Caspr1+ patients from every country included in the study as the weighting factor. Considering this, we cannot rule out that these allele frequencies are influenced by local enrichment of specific alleles in the population where the participating centers are based. Another limitation is that our study is limited to DRB1 alleles. Considering our previous experience with anti-NF155 AN we decided to focus on DRB1 alleles only, which may have limited the ability to find associations with alleles outside the DRB1 locus. Related with this, the rarity of these disorders and the enormous diversity of potential HLA alleles (even when restricting only to DRB1) were determinant in the loss of statistical significance with the adjusted p values when clear percentual differences are seen in specific DRB1 alleles. Multiple comparisons correction in HLA research is a frequent problem that has been previously reported in the literature. The magnitude of the differences we found in our study suggest that the associations are relevant despite the loss of statistical power in some comparisons.

In conclusion, HLA-DRB1*11 alleles are associated with the appearance of anti-CNTN1 antibodies in AN patients, and HLA-DRB1*03:01 alleles associate with anti-Caspr1 antibodies. These data reinforce the idea that these patients represent specific subsets of patients with clinical features and risk factors that differ from seronegative CIDP patients and from other AN patients. However, further studies with larger cohorts are needed to confirm these results and to address the functional relevance of these associations and their pathophysiologic implications.
