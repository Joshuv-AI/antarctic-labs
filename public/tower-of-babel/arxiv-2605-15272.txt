A general proof of integer Rényi QNEC
Tanay Kibe, and Pratik Roy

Abstract
The Rényi quantum null energy condition conjectures that the second null shape variation of the sandwiched Rényi divergence (SRD) of an excited state relative to the vacuum is non-negative in local Poincaré-invariant quantum field theory, giving a one-parameter generalization of the quantum null energy condition (QNEC). We prove Rényi QNEC for all integer Rényi parameters n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 for von Neumann algebras carrying a half-sided modular inclusion structure. The only assumption on the excited state is finiteness of its SRD relative to the vacuum. Concretely, for any σ \sigma -finite von Neumann algebra with such an inclusion, we prove log-convexity, under the associated null-translation semigroup, of the Kosaki L n L^{n} norm of any normal positive functional with finite L n L^{n} norm.

Introduction

QNEC was first conjectured as a non-gravitational limit of the quantum focusing conjecture in semi-classical gravity
Bousso:2015mna
, and has since been proven in free QFTs
Bousso:2015wca
;
Malik:2019dpg
, holographic QFTs with AdS duals
Koeller:2015qmn
, and general local Poincaré-invariant QFTs
Balakrishnan:2017bjg
. Moreover, mathematically rigorous proofs of the QNEC have been provided using von Neumann algebraic techniques
Ceyhan:2018zfg
;
Hollands:2025glm
.

A rather elegant, albeit non-rigorous, information theoretic argument for the QNEC was presented in
Wall:2017blw
by Wall. The argument exploits the fact that local Poincaré-invariant QFTs obey a global energy condition, namely the averaged null energy condition (ANEC), which has been proven under various conditions
Klinkhammer:1991ki
;
Kelly:2014mra
;
Faulkner:2016mzt
;
Hartman:2016lgu
;
Kravchuk:2018htv
. Schematically, the ANEC is:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\braket{\Psi,T_{vv}\Psi}dv\geq 0\,,

(1)

where
\Psi
is any state of the QFT, the integral is along a null geodesic with affine parameter
v
, and
T_{vv}
is the energy-momentum tensor.
Wall argues that whenever a theory has a global energy condition, the knowledge of all information in a given region places a lower bound on the energy in the complementary region. The argument relies on an estimate for the minimum energy in the complementary region given all information in a region, the invariance of this estimate under local unitaries, and the fact that the estimate must satisfy the data processing inequality for quantum channels. This reasoning leads to the QNEC, which in a
1+1
-dimensional QFT takes the form

\braket{\Psi,T_{vv}(v_{0})\Psi}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}S_{\rm vN}^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{v<v_{0}})\,,

(2)

where
S_{\rm vN}
is the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{v<v_{0}}
of the state
{\Psi}
in the
{v<v_{0}}
region, and the prime indicates a derivative in
v
.

The von Neumann entanglement entropy and the one-point function of the energy-momentum tensor are ultraviolet (UV) divergent in QFT, so neither side of (
2
) is individually well-defined; only their difference is finite.
Ceyhan and Faulkner
Ceyhan:2018zfg
, and later Hollands and Longo
Hollands:2025glm
, make Wall’s argument mathematically rigorous for arbitrary QFTs that admit the structure of half-sided modular inclusions (HSMI)
Borchers:1991xk
;
Wiesbrock:1992mg
;
Borchers:1995zg
;
Borchers1996
;
Araki:2005we
,
assuming that the state
\Psi
has finite averaged null energy. Relative entropy in QFT is UV-finite, and
Ceyhan:2018zfg
rigorously prove the QNEC as the non-negativity of the second null derivative of the relative entropy:

\partial^{2}_{v}S(v)\geq 0\,,\quad S(v)\coloneqq D(\Psi\|\Omega;M_{v})\,,

(3)

where
D(\Psi\|\Omega;M_{v})
is the relative entropy of the excited state
\Psi
with respect to the QFT vacuum
\Omega
, for a one-parameter family of local algebras
M_{v}
, with
M_{w}\subset M_{v}
if
w>v
.

Our interest in QNEC stems, among other things, from the fact that it is not merely a formal consistency condition on QFTs, and can lead to non-trivial quantum thermodynamic bounds on quantities such as entropy production in dynamical processes. These bounds have been found in quenched holographic QFTs
Kibe:2021qjy
;
Banerjee:2022dgv
;
Kibe:2024icu
, and general
1+1
-dimensional conformal field theories
Kibe:2025cqc
(see also
Mezei:2019sla
). QNEC has also been applied to prove renormalization group irreversibility theorems in the presence of defects
Casini:2023kyj
. It is therefore interesting to explore generalisations of QNEC and investigate the bounds that they can reveal. Second null derivatives of generalised relative entropies are a natural place to seek more general QNECs. From Wall’s arguments
Wall:2017blw
, it is also natural to restrict to generalised relative entropies that satisfy the data processing inequality (DPI), and are invariant under local unitaries. In this paper we shall focus on one such quantity, the sandwiched Rényi divergence (SRD)
Muller-Lennert:2013liu
;
Wilde:2013bdg
, which is a one-parameter generalization of the relative entropy.

1.1
Sandwiched Rényi divergence and Rényi QNEC

In finite-dimensional systems, the SRD between two density matrices
\rho,\sigma
is
Muller-Lennert:2013liu

{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{\Tr\left[\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}\right]}{\Tr(\rho)}\,.

(4)

For normalized density matrices, the SRD is a measure of distinguishability between the two states. As we will review, the SRD can be defined for general QFTs in the von Neumann algebraic setting
Berta:2016vnw
;
Jencova:2016tqz
;
Jencova:2017txf
, and has been shown to satisfy the properties that are expected of a distinguishability measure for normalized states: DPI for
\alpha\in[1/2,1)\cup(1,\infty)
and invariance under local unitaries. In the
\alpha\to 1
limit, the SRD becomes the standard relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
,

\lim_{\alpha\to 1}{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=D(\rho\|\sigma)\,.

(5)

Lashkari
Lashkari:2018nsl
conjectured the Rényi QNEC (RQNEC): second null shape variations of SRD in a Poincaré-invariant QFT are non-negative. Schematically:

\partial^{2}S_{\alpha}(v)\geq 0,\quad S_{\alpha}(v)=D_{\alpha}(\Psi\|\Omega;M_{v})\,,

(6)

where
D_{\alpha}(\Psi\|\Omega,M_{v})
is the SRD defined for the one-parameter family of von Neumann algebras
M_{v}
,
\Psi
is any excited state, and
\Omega
is the QFT vacuum. For
\alpha>1
, RQNEC has been proven in free field theories, and counter-examples have been constructed for
\alpha<1

Moosa:2020jwt
;
Roy:2022yzm
.

Main theorem

Our main result, Theorem
5.5
, is a proof of the RQNEC for integer
\alpha\geq 2
in general Poincaré-invariant QFTs that possess the structure of half sided modular inclusions (HSMI), with the single additional hypothesis that the SRD of the excited state relative to the QFT vacuum is finite.
Stated differently, with integer
\alpha\geq 2
, for Kosaki
L^{\alpha}
spaces constructed with a reference normal faithful state on any (
\sigma
-finite) von Neumann algebra, our main result Theorem
5.4
is the log-convexity under HSMI of the Kosaki
L^{\alpha}
norm of any normal positive functional in the predual that has finite
L^{\alpha}
norm. These terms are all defined in Sections
2
and
3
. A key point of our proof is that no differentiability or generator-domain assumptions are imposed on the excited state. The finiteness of SRD (equivalently
L^{\alpha}
norms) is the minimal condition under which the RQNEC (equivalently log-convexity) is well defined.

Below, we sketch the broad logic of our proof. The rest of this paper makes this logical chain rigorous. This sketch is presented using the vectors
\Psi,\Omega\in\mathcal{H}
, but the proof itself only depends on the normal functionals

on the algebra and not on a particular choice of their vector representative. For the reader’s convenience, we also provide a detailed summary of the proof in Appendix

To prove RQNEC for a given integer
n\geq 2
, the only condition we impose on the excited state is:

Assumption:

D_{n}(\Psi\|\Omega;M_{A})<\infty

(9)

for the null cut algebra
M_{A}
.

The proof proceeds by first showing that the SRD
D_{n}(\Psi\|\Omega;M_{c})
of some excited state
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}
in the one-parameter family of algebras
M_{c}
is equal to the SRD
D_{n}(\Psi_{c}\|\Omega;M_{A})
of a one-parameter family of states
\Psi_{c}\coloneqq U_{c}\Psi
on the fixed algebra
M_{A}
. Hollands and Longo use this reformulation for the relative entropy in their proof of QNEC
Hollands:2025glm
.

We then show that the null translations,
U_{c}=e^{-icP}
, can be lifted to a one-parameter contractive semigroup on Haagerup’s
L^{n}
spaces for integer
n\geq 2
. Thus, schematically,

D_{n}(\Psi\|\Omega;M_{c})=D_{n}(\Psi_{c}\|\Omega;M_{A})=\frac{1}{n-1}\log\frac{\tr(x_{\psi_{c}}^{n})}{\tr(h_{\psi})}=\frac{1}{n-1}\log\frac{\tr((V^{(n)}_{c}x_{\psi})^{n})}{\tr(h_{\psi})}\,,

(10)

where
V^{(n)}_{c}
is the one-parameter semigroup action on
L^{n}
. If
G_{n}
is the generator of the semigroup
V^{(n)}_{c}
, then with the necessary regularity assumption on the excited state we establish the following null derivatives:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{c}(\tr(x_{\psi_{c}}^{n}))=n\tr((G_{n}x_{\psi_{c}})x_{\psi_{c}}^{n-1})\,,\\
&\partial_{c}^{2}(\tr(x_{\psi_{c}}^{n}))=n\tr((G_{n}^{2}x_{\psi_{c}})x_{\psi_{c}}^{n-1})+n\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\tr((G_{n}x_{\psi_{c}})x_{\psi_{c}}^{j}(G_{n}x_{\psi_{c}})x_{\psi_{c}}^{n-2-j})\,.\end{aligned}

(11)

Under the same regularity assumption, we prove a cyclic Ward identity on a core of
G_{n}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta_{n}^{k}\tr\bigl(y_{1}\ldots y_{k}\,(G_{n}y_{k+1})\,y_{k+2}\ldots y_{n}\bigr)=0

(12)

where
\zeta_{n}=e^{-2\pi i/n}
. This is a key algebraic input in our proof, and follows from a covariance relation between the null translations and the modular automorphisms, along with the invariance of the vacuum under null translations. The restriction in our proof that
n\geq 2
is an integer enters through this identity.

Combining the derivatives with the Ward identity, and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in Haagerup’s
L^{2}
Hilbert space yields

\partial_{c}^{2}D_{n}(\Psi_{c}\|\Omega;M_{A})\geq 0,

(13)

establishing that
D_{n}(\Psi_{c}\|\Omega;M_{A})
is a convex function of
c\in[0,\infty)
. Finally, we remove the regularity assumption by showing that the
x_{\psi_{c}}
associated to a general
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}
can be approximated by an appropriately convergent sequence
x^{(m)}_{\psi_{c}}
that satisfies the regularity assumption. This establishes that the SRD for these approximants is a convex function of
c\in[0,\infty)
. Passing to the limit then proves convexity, i.e., the RQNEC, for general excited states
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}
that satisfy Assumption
9
.

For the special case of
n=2
, so that
L^{2}
is a Hilbert space, we provide a different simpler proof directly on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of the QFT.

3
Half-sided modular inclusions and Rényi QNEC

The null variations appearing in the statement of QNEC and RQNEC can be rigorously defined for algebras carrying a half-sided modular inclusion (HSMI) structure. In this section we recall the HSMI structure, collect the properties needed in later sections, and reformulate RQNEC in terms of a fixed algebra and a one-parameter family of null-deformed states, following
Hollands:2025glm
. Our conventions for modular operators and relative modular operators are the same as
Hiai:2021qfv
.

3.1
Half-sided modular inclusions and null translations

Motivation from null-cuts

Consider a local Poincaré-invariant QFT living on
d
-dimensional Minkowski space, with metric

ds^{2}=-dx^{+}\,dx^{-}+d\vec{y}^{2}\,,

(41)

where
(x^{+},x^{-},\vec{y})
are null coordinates and
\vec{y}
denotes the
d-2
coordinates transverse to the lightcone. The Rindler wedge
R
is the region
\{x^{+}>0,x^{-}<0\}
, and we denote the algebra of bounded operators associated with
R
by
M_{R}
. The regions
N_{C}=\{x^{-}=0,x^{+}>C(\vec{y})\}
, where
C(\vec{y})
is a continuous function of the transverse coordinates along the entangling surface, are called null cuts. We consider the von Neumann algebra,
M_{C}=M_{N_{C}^{\prime\prime}}
, of bounded operators localised in the causal completion
N_{C}^{\prime\prime}
.
The Rindler wedge corresponds to
M_{0}=M_{R}
. The algebras are illustrated in Fig.
1
.

Denote by
\Omega\in\mathcal{H}
the vacuum vector in the QFT, which is cyclic and separating for all the local algebras. For the Rindler cut, the modular unitary group
\Delta_{\Omega;R}^{is}
implements Lorentz boosts that fix the entangling surface
x^{-}=0
. Define the modular Hamiltonian associated with a cut
A
by
K_{A}=-\log\Delta_{\Omega;A}
. Then, we have
Casini:2017roe

K_{A}=2\pi\int_{x^{-}=0}(x^{+}-A(\vec{y}))T_{++}\,.

(42)

The modular Hamiltonians associated with two null cuts
A(\vec{y}),B(\vec{y})
have the commutation relation

[K_{A},K_{B}]=2\pi i(K_{A}-K_{B})=(2\pi)^{2}iP\,,

(43)

where
P
is the self-adjoint and positive semi-definite null translation generator acting on Rindler horizons as translations by
B(\vec{y})-A(\vec{y})
. For null cuts,
P
is the averaged null energy (ANE) operator

P=\int_{x^{-}=0}(B(\vec{y})-A(\vec{y}))T_{++}\,.

(44)

Given two null cuts
A,B
, with
B(\vec{y})\geq A(\vec{y})
, define the one-parameter family of cuts

A_{b}=A+b(B-A).

We introduce the notation

M_{b}\coloneqq M_{A_{b}},

and the algebras associated with the null cuts
A
and
B
are denoted by
M_{A},M_{B}
, respectively. In cases of interest, these cuts are expected to satisfy the properties of half-sided modular inclusions (HSMI)
Borchers:1991xk
;
Wiesbrock:1992mg
;
Borchers:1995zg
;
Borchers1996
;
Araki:2005we
.

This preceding null-cut discussion is intended to motivate the notation and terminology, and to provide a physical backdrop for our setup. In particular, the stress-tensor expressions for
K_{A},P
and the commutator
[K_{A},K_{B}]
should only be read as formal QFT motivation.

Abstract HSMI framework

From this point onward, we work entirely in the following abstract operator-algebraic setting: a pair of von Neumann algebras
M_{B}\subset M_{A}
with a common cyclic separating vector
\Omega
, or equivalently a common faithful normal state
\omega
, satisfying the half-sided modular inclusion condition in Definition
3.1
. All subsequent results depend only on this HSMI structure and on the consequences collected in Theorem
3.2
.

Definition 3.1

(Half-sided modular inclusion)
.

Let
M_{B}\subset M_{A}
. This inclusion is a half-sided modular inclusion if there is a faithful normal state
\omega
, common to both algebras, such that:

\Delta_{\omega;A}^{-is}M_{B}\Delta_{\omega;A}^{is}\subset M_{B}\qquad s\geq 0\,.

(45)

Theorem 3.2

(Null translations)
.

For a HSMI
M_{B}\subset M_{A}
with common faithful normal state
\omega
, there is a family of unitary operators, which we refer to as null translations,

U_{b}\coloneqq e^{-ibP},\quad b\in\mathbb{R}\,,

such that

P\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\overline{\left(\ln\Delta_{\omega;B}-\ln\Delta_{\omega;A}\right)}

is self-adjoint and positive semi-definite
Borchers:1991xk
, where the bar denotes the closure.
Furthermore, the following are true
Borchers:1991xk
;
Wiesbrock:1992mg
;
Borchers1996
;
Araki:2005we
;
Borchers:1995zg
.

1.

\omega\circ{\rm Ad}(U_{-b})=\omega
for all
b\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

The null translations
U_{b}
generate the one-parameter family of sub-algebras:

U_{-b}M_{A}U_{b}=M_{b}\,,

with
M_{c}\subset M_{b}
if
c\geq b
. Moreover
M_{{1}}=M_{B}
. This family of algebras has
\omega
as a faithful normal state, and
M_{b}\subset M_{A}
is a HSMI relative to
\omega
. The translations apply for all real
b
and also apply for the commutants, which satisfy:

M_{b}^{\prime}\subset M_{c}^{\prime},\quad b\leq c\,.

3.

The modular automorphism of the algebra
M_{A}
acts as

\Delta_{\omega;A}^{is}M_{b}\Delta_{\omega;A}^{-is}=M_{be^{-2\pi s}}\,,

and similarly for the commutant.

4.

The modular operators satisfy the Borchers covariance relation with the null translations

\Delta_{\omega;A}^{is}U_{b}\Delta_{\omega;A}^{-is}=U_{e^{-2\pi s}b}\,.

Furthermore, the following properties hold:

\displaystyle J_{\omega;A}U_{b}J_{\omega;A}=U_{-b}

\displaystyle\Delta^{is}_{\omega;A_{b_{1}}}\Delta_{\omega;A_{b_{2}}}^{-is}=U_{(b_{1}-b_{2})(e^{-2\pi s}-1)},

where
J_{\omega;A}
is the modular conjugation with respect to
\omega
.

5.

is strongly continuous and can be analytically continued to complex
b
, where it is bounded by
1
and strongly continuous for
{\rm Im}\,b\leq 0
.

4.4
A Ward graph core of the semigroup generator

We recall the definition of the resolvent of a semigroup generator. See Definition
A.17
and Appendix
A.2
for more details.

Remark 4.10

(Resolvent of
G_{n}
)
.

Denote by
\rho(G_{n})
the resolvent set of
G_{n}
, defined as:

\rho(G_{n})\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda-G_{n}\text{ has a bounded inverse}\}\,.

For
\lambda\in\rho(G_{n})
, denote the resolvent by

R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}\coloneqq(\lambda-G_{n})^{-1}\,.

By the Hille–Yosida theorem (
engel.nagel:00:one-parameter
and Theorem
A.18
),
G_{n}
is a closed densely defined operator with
(0,\infty)\subset\rho(G_{n})
, and
\|R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}\|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}
. Moreover, the resolvent admits the Laplace representation:

R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}V_{t}^{(n)}\,dt\,.

We now combine the resolvent of
G_{n}
with the null and modular smoothings to prove that
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
is dense in
\Dom(G_{n})
in the graph norm.

Proposition 4.11

(Ward graph core)
.

The subspace
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
is a graph core for
G_{n}
. Equivalently, for every
y\in\Dom(G_{n})
, there exists a sequence
y_{m}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
such that

y_{m}\longrightarrow y,\qquad G_{n}y_{m}\longrightarrow G_{n}y\qquad\text{in }L^{n}(M).

Proof.

The proof has two stages. First, we use the resolvent of
G_{n}
to show that
j_{n}(M)\cap\Dom(G_{n})
is a graph core. Second, we approximate each resolvent-core vector by an element of
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
using null and modular-Gaussian smoothing.

The symmetric bounded core
j_{n}(M)
is norm dense in
L^{n}(M)
.
For
a\in M
define the ultraweak integral

a_{\lambda}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}T_{t}(a)\,dt\in M\,.

For every normal functional
\varphi\in M_{*}
,
t\mapsto e^{-\lambda t}\varphi(T_{t}(a))
is integrable. Hence the ultraweak integral exists by norm-dual boundedness.

With
R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}
as defined in Remark
4.10
, we claim that

R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(a)=j_{n}(a_{\lambda})\,,

(100)

so that
R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}
maps
j_{n}(M)
into itself. This follows from Lemma
4.3
, with
\mathcal{S}=(0,\infty)
,
d\nu(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\,dt
, and
a_{t}=T_{t}(a)
.
Thus
R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(M)\subset j_{n}(M)\cap\Dom(G_{n})
for
\lambda\in(0,\infty)\subset\rho(G_{n})
, and
j_{n}(M)\cap\Dom(G_{n})
is a graph core by the resolvent-core criterion
7
7

7

The resolvent-core criterion
(
engel.nagel:00:one-parameter
, Exercise II.1.15(2))
is:
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathrm{Dom}(G)
is a graph core for
G\,\Longleftrightarrow\,(\lambda-G)\mathcal{C}
is dense in
X
for some
\lambda\in\rho(G)
, where
G
is an operator on the ambient Banach space
X\supset\Dom(G)
.
. This follows straightforwardly from the fact that
(\lambda-G_{n})R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(M)=j_{n}(M)
is dense in
L^{n}(M)
.

It remains to approximate the resolvent-core vectors
R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(a)
by vectors in
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
.
Consider

f_{m}(t)=g(mt)k(t/m)e^{-\lambda t}\,,

with
g,k\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that
g
vanishes in a neighbourhood of
0
,
g(u)=1
for
u\geq 1
, and
k(u)=1
for
u\in[0,1]
,
k(u)=0
for
u\geq 2
. Then,
f_{m}\in C_{c,0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
and
f_{m}\to e^{-\lambda t}
in
L^{1}([0,\infty))
.
Therefore,

N_{f_{m}}j_{n}(a)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{m}(t)V_{t}^{(n)}j_{n}(a)dt\longrightarrow R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(a)\,,

since

\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(f_{m}(t)-e^{-\lambda t}\bigr)V_{t}^{(n)}j_{n}(a)\,dt\right\|_{n}\leq\|f_{m}-e^{-\lambda\,\cdot\,}\|_{L^{1}}\|j_{n}(a)\|_{n}\to 0\,.

The generator part is

G_{n}N_{f_{m}}j_{n}(a)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{m}^{\prime}(t)V_{t}^{(n)}j_{n}(a)dt=A_{m}+B_{m}+C_{m}\,,

where

\displaystyle A_{m}=

\displaystyle\ -\int_{0}^{\infty}mg^{\prime}(mt)k(t/m)e^{-\lambda t}V_{t}^{(n)}j_{n}(a)dt\longrightarrow-j_{n}(a),

\displaystyle B_{m}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(mt)k^{\prime}(t/m)e^{-\lambda t}V_{t}^{(n)}j_{n}(a)dt\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle C_{m}=

\displaystyle\ \lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}g(mt)k(t/m)e^{-\lambda t}V_{t}^{(n)}j_{n}(a)dt\longrightarrow\lambda R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(a)\,.

Combining,

G_{n}N_{f_{m}}j_{n}(a)\longrightarrow-j_{n}(a)+\lambda R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(a)=G_{n}R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(a)\,.

So,
N_{f_{m}}j_{n}(a)\to R_{\lambda}^{G_{n}}j_{n}(a)
in graph norm.

For each fixed
m
, Lemma
4.2
gives

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}N_{f_{m}}j_{n}(a)\longrightarrow N_{f_{m}}j_{n}(a)

in the graph norm as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.
Moreover, by Lemma
4.4

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}N_{f_{m}}j_{n}(a)=j_{n}(a_{f_{m},\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}\,.

Choosing a diagonal sequence in

and
\varepsilon
gives a graph-norm approximation of each resolvent-core vector by elements of
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
. Combining this with the resolvent-core approximation proves that
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
is a graph core for
G_{n}
.
∎

4.5
The cyclic
n
-point Ward identity

With
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
established as the graph core for
G_{n}
, we now state and prove the cyclic Ward identity.

For
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in L^{n}(M)
define the cyclic
n
-linear form

\Lambda_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}):=\tr(y_{1}y_{2}\cdots y_{n})\,.

(101)

By Hölder’s inequality,

|\Lambda_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})|\leq\prod_{k=1}^{n}\|y_{k}\|_{n}\,.

(102)

Proposition 4.12

(Core cyclic Ward identity)
.

For
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in M^{W}\subset\Dom(\delta)
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta_{n}^{k}\Lambda_{n}\bigl(j_{n}(a_{1}),\ldots,j_{n}(a_{k}),j_{n}(\delta a_{k+1}),j_{n}(a_{k+2}),\ldots,j_{n}(a_{n})\bigr)=0\,,

(103)

where
\zeta_{n}=e^{-2\pi i/n}
.
Equivalently, for
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta_{n}^{k}\Lambda_{n}\bigl(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},G_{n}y_{k+1},y_{k+2},\ldots,y_{n}\bigr)=0\,.

(104)

Proof.

For entire analytic
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
, the KMS placement of the symmetric
L^{n}
insertions gives

\Lambda_{n}(j_{n}(a_{1}),\ldots,j_{n}(a_{n}))=\omega\bigl(a_{1}\sigma_{-i/n}(a_{2})\sigma_{-2i/n}(a_{3})\cdots\sigma_{-(n-1)i/n}(a_{n})\bigr)\,.

Since
M^{W}
consists of modular-entire elements, this applies to all
a_{i}\in M^{W}
.

Let

B:=a_{1}\sigma_{-i/n}(a_{2})\sigma_{-2i/n}(a_{3})\cdots\sigma_{-(n-1)i/n}(a_{n})\,.

By Proposition
4.9
, the algebras
M^{W}
and
\sigma_{z}(M^{W})
are contained in
\Dom(\delta)
, and by Lemma
4.6

\delta
is a derivation on
\Dom(\delta)
. Hence
B\in\Dom(\delta)
. Since
\omega\circ T_{t}=\omega
, the scalar

\omega(T_{t}(B))

is independent of
t\geq 0
. Taking a right derivative
t=0
in operator norm (in the sense of (
84
)), we get

0=\omega(\delta B).

Using the derivation rule,

\displaystyle 0={}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega\bigl(a_{1}\sigma_{-i/n}(a_{2})\cdots\delta(\sigma_{-ki/n}(a_{k+1}))\cdots\sigma_{-(n-1)i/n}(a_{n})\bigr)\,.

For
k=0
, the differentiated factor is
\delta a_{1}
. For
k\geq 1
, Borchers covariance gives

\delta(\sigma_{-ki/n}(a))=e^{-2\pi ik/n}\sigma_{-ki/n}(\delta a)=\zeta_{n}^{k}\sigma_{-ki/n}(\delta a)\,.

Substituting this into the previous expression and translating back through the KMS placement formula gives the first displayed Ward identity. The equivalent form follows from

G_{n}j_{n}(a)=j_{n}(\delta a),\qquad a\in M^{W}\,.\qed

Proposition 4.13

(Ward identity on the full generator domain)
.

For all
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\Dom(G_{n})
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta_{n}^{k}\Lambda_{n}\bigl(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},G_{n}y_{k+1},y_{k+2},\ldots,y_{n}\bigr)=0\,.

(105)

Proof.

Equip
\Dom(G_{n})
with the graph norm

\|y\|_{G_{n}}:=\|y\|_{n}+\|G_{n}y\|_{n}\,.

For each
k
, Hölder’s inequality gives

\bigl|\Lambda_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k},G_{n}y_{k+1},y_{k+2},\ldots,y_{n})\bigr|\leq\|G_{n}y_{k+1}\|_{n}\prod_{j\neq k+1}\|y_{j}\|_{n}\,.

Thus the left-hand side of the claimed identity is continuous on
\Dom(G_{n})^{n}
with respect to the product graph norm. Since
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{W}
is a graph core by Proposition
4.11
, the identity extends from Proposition
4.12
to all of
\Dom(G_{n})^{n}
.
∎

Remark 4.14

(Replica geometry and modular monodromy)
.

The cyclic Ward identity in Proposition
4.13
admits a transparent replica-geometric interpretation that makes contact with the replica-trick computation of Rényi entropies in QFT
Calabrese:2004eu
. The KMS placement formula expresses the integer cyclic trace
\Lambda_{n}(j_{n}(a_{1}),\ldots,j_{n}(a_{n}))
as a single vacuum expectation value with the
n
insertions placed at imaginary modular times
z_{k}=-ik/n
for
k=0,\ldots,n-1
. These insertion points are evenly spaced around the modular thermal circle. In this picture,
Q_{n}=\tr(x^{n})
is an algebraic analogue of the replica partition function on the
n
-fold cover. The phases
\zeta_{n}^{k}=e^{-2\pi ik/n}
in the Ward identity then admit an interpretation as
modular monodromies
. This parallels the monodromy structure of twist operators in two-dimensional CFT, where the replica geometry produces analogous rotation phases
Calabrese:2004eu
.

5
General proof of integer Rényi QNEC

In this section we use the cyclic Ward identity to prove the integer RQNEC. We first prove log-convexity of
Q_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)
, defined in Lemma
5.2
, for positive initial data in
\Dom(G_{n}^{2})
, then relax this regularity assumption using Yosida regularization, obtaining the integer RQNEC for general functionals with finite SRD. We also provide a slightly different proof for the
\alpha=2
case, which has a straightforward interpretation in terms of the averaged null energy operator
P
.

5.1
Null-translated sandwiched Rényi density

Definition 5.1

(Sandwiched Rényi density)
.

Let
\psi\in M_{*}^{+}
be non-zero and assume

Q_{n}(\psi\|\omega;M)<\infty\,.

(106)

By the Haagerup–Kosaki characterization of SRD (Definition
2.20
and Lemma
2.21
), there exists a unique element
x_{0}\in L^{n}(M)_{+}
, which we call the sandwiched Rényi density,
such that

h_{\psi}=h^{\eta_{n}}x_{0}h^{\eta_{n}}\,,\quad\text{ and }\quad Q_{n}(\psi\|\omega;M)=\tr(x_{0}^{n})\,.

(107)

For
c\geq 0
, define

x_{c}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=V_{c}^{(n)}x_{0}\,.

(108)

Since
V_{c}^{(n)}
is positive and contractive,

x_{c}\in L^{n}(M)_{+}\,,\qquad\|x_{c}\|_{n}\leq\|x_{0}\|_{n}\,.

(109)

We highlight that in what follows, although we suppress it in the notation,
x_{0}
and
x_{c}
are both defined in terms of a given
\psi\in M_{*}^{+}
with
Q_{n}(\psi\|\omega;M)<\infty
.

We now show that null translations and the sandwiching operation are compatible.

Lemma 5.2

(Null translation of sandwiched Rényi density)
.

Consider a non-zero
\psi\in M_{*}^{+}
such that
Q_{n}(\psi\|\omega;M)<\infty
.
Let

\psi_{c}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\psi\circ T_{c}

(110)

be the null-translated functional on the fixed algebra
M
. Then

h_{\psi_{c}}=h^{\eta_{n}}x_{c}h^{\eta_{n}},\qquad x_{c}=V_{c}^{(n)}x_{0}\,.

(111)

With
Q_{n}(c)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=Q_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)=\tr(x_{c}^{n})
, we also have

0<Q_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)<\infty\qquad\,\forall\,c\geq 0.

(112)

Proof.

For
a\in M
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tr\bigl(x_{c}j_{q_{n}}(a)\bigr)&=\tr\bigl(V_{c}^{(n)}x_{0}\,j_{q_{n}}(a)\bigr)=\tr\bigl(x_{0}V_{c}^{(q_{n})}j_{q_{n}}(a)\bigr)=\tr\bigl(x_{0}j_{q_{n}}(T_{c}(a))\bigr)\\
&=\tr\bigl(h_{\psi}T_{c}(a)\bigr)=\psi(T_{c}(a))=\psi_{c}(a)\,.\end{aligned}

On the other hand, by Hölder multiplication and cyclicity,

\tr\bigl(x_{c}j_{q_{n}}(a)\bigr)=\tr\bigl(x_{c}h^{\eta_{n}}ah^{\eta_{n}}\bigr)=\tr\bigl(h^{\eta_{n}}x_{c}h^{\eta_{n}}a\bigr)\,.

Thus,
h^{\eta_{n}}x_{c}h^{\eta_{n}}
represents the normal positive functional
\psi_{c}
. Since the map
M_{*}\ni\varphi\mapsto h_{\varphi}\in L^{1}(M)
is injective,

h_{\psi_{c}}=h^{\eta_{n}}x_{c}h^{\eta_{n}}\,.

The formula for
Q_{n}(c)
follows from the uniqueness of the integer SRD density. Finiteness follows from
x_{c}\in L^{n}(M)_{+}
. If
Q_{n}(c)=0
, then

\|x_{c}\|_{n}=0
, hence
x_{c}=0
. Therefore
h_{\psi_{c}}=h^{\eta_{n}}x_{c}h^{\eta_{n}}=0
,
so
\psi_{c}=0
by the injectivity of
M_{*}\ni\phi\mapsto h_{\phi}\in L^{1}(M)
.
But

\psi_{c}(1)=\psi(T_{c}(1))=\psi(1)>0,

a contradiction. Thus
Q_{n}(c)>0
.
∎

5.2
Log-convexity for smooth semigroup orbits

Under a regularity assumption on
x_{0}
, the Ward identity (
105
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the
L^{2}(M)
Hilbert space lead to a straightforward proof of RQNEC (equivalently log-convexity of

) for integer values of the Rényi index

. The regularity restriction is removed in Sec.

.

Proposition 5.3

(Smooth-orbit log-convexity)
.

Let
\psi,x_{0},x_{c},Q_{n}(c)
be as in Lemma
5.2
, so that
0<Q_{n}(c)<\infty
for
c\geq 0
. Assume further that

x_{0}\in L^{n}(M)_{+}\cap\Dom(G_{n}^{2}).

Then
Q_{n}(c)
is log-convex in
c
on
[0,\infty)
:

Q_{n}((1-\theta)c_{0}+\theta c_{1})\leq Q_{n}(c_{0})^{1-\theta}Q_{n}(c_{1})^{\theta}

(113)

for all
c_{0},c_{1}\geq 0
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.

Proof.

Since
x_{0}\in\Dom(G_{n}^{2})
,

x_{c}\in\Dom(G_{n}^{2}),\qquad\frac{d}{dc}x_{c}=G_{n}x_{c},\qquad\frac{d^{2}}{dc^{2}}x_{c}=G_{n}^{2}x_{c}\,.

Fix
c>0
and write

x\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=x_{c},\qquad u\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=G_{n}x_{c},\qquad v\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=G_{n}^{2}x_{c}\,.

(114)

Because
x_{c}=x_{c}^{*}
for all
c
and the involution is an isometry on
L^{n}(M)
, differentiation in
L^{n}(M)
gives
u=u^{*},v=v^{*}
.

The product map

L^{n}(M)\times\cdots\times L^{n}(M)\longrightarrow L^{1}(M)

with
n
factors is continuous by Hölder’s inequality. Since
c\mapsto x_{c}
is twice continuously differentiable in
L^{n}(M)
by assumption, the finite Leibniz rule gives

Q_{n}^{\prime}(c)=nC,\quad C\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tr(ux^{n-1})\,.

(115)

The second derivative is

Q_{n}^{\prime\prime}(c)=n\left(A+\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}B_{j}\right)\,,

(116)

where

A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tr(vx^{n-1}),\quad B_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tr(ux^{j}ux^{n-2-j}),\qquad j=0,\ldots,n-2\,.

(117)

Each

is non-negative. To see this, define

Y_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=x^{j/2}ux^{(n-2-j)/2}\in L^{2}(M)\,.

(118)

Hölder multiplication gives

, and cyclicity gives

\|Y_{j}\|_{2}^{2}=\tr(Y_{j}^{*}Y_{j})=\tr\bigl(x^{(n-2-j)/2}ux^{j}ux^{(n-2-j)/2}\bigr)=B_{j}\geq 0\,.

Moreover,

B_{j}=B_{n-2-j}\,.

This follows from cyclicity and self-adjointness:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} B_{j}&=\tr(ux^{j}ux^{n-2-j})=\tr\bigl((ux^{j}ux^{n-2-j})^{*}\bigr)=\tr(x^{n-2-j}ux^{j}u)=\tr(ux^{n-2-j}ux^{j})=B_{n-2-j}\,.\end{aligned}

We now apply the cyclic Ward identity from Proposition
4.13
to

(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})=(u,x,x,\ldots,x)\,.

This is allowed because
x\in\Dom(G_{n}^{2})
by assumption, and
u=G_{n}x\in\Dom(G_{n})
. The
k=0
term is

\Lambda_{n}(G_{n}u,x,\ldots,x)=\tr(vx^{n-1})=A\,.

For
k=j+1
, where
j=0,\ldots,n-2
, the term is

\zeta_{n}^{j+1}\tr(ux^{j}ux^{n-2-j})=\zeta_{n}^{j+1}B_{j}\,.

Thus, the Ward identity (
105
) gives

0=A+\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\zeta_{n}^{j+1}B_{j}\,.

Substituting this into (
116
) yields

\frac{Q_{n}^{\prime\prime}(c)}{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\bigl(1-\zeta_{n}^{j+1}\bigr)B_{j}\,.

(119)

Using
B_{j}=B_{n-2-j}
, one can write this as

\frac{Q_{n}^{\prime\prime}(c)}{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}w_{j}B_{j},\qquad w_{j}=2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(j+1)}{n}\right),

(120)

with

w_{j}\geq 0,\qquad\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}w_{j}=n.

Let

Z\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=x^{n/2}\in L^{2}(M)\,.

Then for
j=0,\ldots,n-2
, with
Y_{j}
defined in (
118
), we have

\|Y_{j}\|_{2}^{2}=B_{j},\qquad\|Z\|_{2}^{2}=Q_{n}(c)\,.

Furthermore, by cyclicity,

\langle Y_{j},Z\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}=\tr(Y_{j}^{*}Z)=\tr\bigl(x^{(n-2-j)/2}ux^{j/2}x^{n/2}\bigr)=C\,.

where
C
was defined in (
115
).
Cauchy-Schwarz in
L^{2}(M)
gives

|C|^{2}\leq Q_{n}(c)B_{j},\qquad j=0,\ldots,n-2\,.

(121)

Also,
C
is real: using
u=u^{*}
,
x=x^{*}
, and cyclicity,

\overline{C}=\tr\bigl((ux^{n-1})^{*}\bigr)=\tr(x^{n-1}u)=\tr(ux^{n-1})=C\,.

Thus
|C|^{2}=C^{2}
.

Then, using (
120
), (
121
), and the fact that
C
is real,

Q_{n}(c)Q_{n}^{\prime\prime}(c)=nQ_{n}(c)\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}w_{j}B_{j}\geq n\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}w_{j}C^{2}=n^{2}C^{2}=Q_{n}^{\prime}(c)^{2}\,.

Since
Q_{n}(c)>0
,

(\log Q_{n})^{\prime\prime}(c)=\frac{Q_{n}(c)Q_{n}^{\prime\prime}(c)-Q_{n}^{\prime}(c)^{2}}{Q_{n}(c)^{2}}\geq 0\,.

(122)

Hence
\log Q_{n}
is convex on
(0,\infty)
, which is equivalent to the claimed log-convexity inequality for
c\in(0,\infty)
.

Since
c\mapsto x_{c}
is strongly continuous in
L^{n}(M)
, and since the product map
L^{n}(M)^{n}\to L^{1}(M)
is continuous by Hölder,
Q_{n}(c)=\tr(x_{c}^{n})
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
. Since
Q_{n}(c)>0
,
\log Q_{n}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
. Convexity on
(0,\infty)
then extends to convexity on
[0,\infty)
by taking limits from the right.
∎

5.3
Integer Rényi QNEC for general functionals

We now relax the assumption
x_{0}\in\Dom(G_{n}^{2})
in Proposition
5.3
and prove log convexity for arbitrary
x_{0}\in L^{n}(M)_{+}
. This is done by approximating
x_{0}
by a sequence in
L^{n}(M)_{+}\cap\Dom(G_{n}^{2})
, so that Proposition
5.3
applies as we take the limit.

Theorem 5.4

(Log-convexity of
Q_{n}(c)
for general functionals)
.

Let
\psi,x_{0},x_{c},Q_{n}(c)
be as in Lemma
5.2
, so that
0<Q_{n}(c)<\infty
for
c\geq 0
. Then
Q_{n}
is log-convex on
[0,\infty)
:

Q_{n}((1-\theta)c_{0}+\theta c_{1})\leq Q_{n}(c_{0})^{1-\theta}Q_{n}(c_{1})^{\theta}

(123)

for all
c_{0},c_{1}\geq 0
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.

Proof.

Define the Yosida approximants (see Appendix
A.2
)

x_{0}^{(m)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=R_{m}^{2}x_{0}\,,

with
R_{m}
the Yosida resolvent of
G_{n}
.
Then

x_{0}^{(m)}\in L^{n}(M)_{+}\cap\Dom(G_{n}^{2}),\qquad x_{0}^{(m)}\to x_{0}\quad\text{in }L^{n}(M).

Also
x_{0}^{(m)}\neq 0
. Since
x_{0}^{(m)}\to x_{0}
in
L^{n}(M)
, after discarding finitely many terms we may assume
x_{0}^{(m)}\neq 0
for all
m
.

For
c\geq 0
, set

x_{c}^{(m)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=V_{c}^{(n)}x_{0}^{(m)}\,.

Then
x_{c}^{(m)}\in L^{n}(M)_{+}\cap\Dom(G_{n}^{2})
, and Proposition
5.3
applies to

Q_{n,m}(c)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tr\bigl((x_{c}^{(m)})^{n}\bigr)\,.

Thus each
Q_{n,m}
is positive and log-convex:

Q_{n,m}((1-\theta)c_{0}+\theta c_{1})\leq Q_{n,m}(c_{0})^{1-\theta}Q_{n,m}(c_{1})^{\theta}

for all
c_{0},c_{1}\geq 0
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.

For each fixed
c\geq 0
,

x_{c}^{(m)}=V_{c}^{(n)}R_{m}^{2}x_{0}=R_{m}^{2}V_{c}^{(n)}x_{0}\longrightarrow V_{c}^{(n)}x_{0}=x_{c}\qquad\text{in }L^{n}(M).

The map
c\to x_{c}
is continuous in
L^{n}
norm.
By norm-continuity of the positive power map
L^{n}(M)_{+}\ni x\mapsto x^{n}\in L^{1}(M)_{+}
(see Lemma
A.23
),

(x_{c}^{(m)})^{n}\longrightarrow x_{c}^{n}\qquad\text{in }L^{1}(M).

Hence, for every
c\geq 0
,

Q_{n,m}(c)\longrightarrow Q_{n}(c)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tr(x_{c}^{n}).

The limit
Q_{n}(c)
is positive by Lemma
5.2
.
Passing to the limit in the log-convexity inequality for
Q_{n,m}
gives

Q_{n}((1-\theta)c_{0}+\theta c_{1})\leq Q_{n}(c_{0})^{1-\theta}Q_{n}(c_{1})^{\theta}\,.

Thus
Q_{n}
is log-convex on
[0,\infty)
.
∎

We now state the final result.

Theorem 5.5

(Integer Rényi QNEC)
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Assume the HSMI structure from Theorem
3.2
and the associated Haagerup–Kosaki
L^{p}
extension framework from Sec.
4
. Let
\psi\in M_{*}^{+}
be non-zero and assume

Q_{n}(\psi\|\omega;M)<\infty\,.

For
c\geq 0
, define

\psi_{c}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\psi\circ T_{c}\,.

Then

0<Q_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)<\infty

for all
c\geq 0
, and the function

c\longmapsto S_{n}(c)=D_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)=D_{n}(\psi\|\omega;M_{c})

is convex on
[0,\infty)
. Equivalently,

S_{n}((1-\theta)c_{0}+\theta c_{1})\leq(1-\theta)S_{n}(c_{0})+\theta S_{n}(c_{1})

for all
c_{0},c_{1}\geq 0
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. Consequently,

\partial_{c}^{2}S_{n}(c)\geq 0

in the sense of distributions on
(0,\infty)
, and pointwise wherever the classical second derivative exists.

Proof.

By the finite-
Q_{n}
assumption, there exists a unique

x_{0}\in L^{n}(M)_{+}

with

h_{\psi}=h^{\eta_{n}}x_{0}h^{\eta_{n}},\qquad Q_{n}(\psi\|\omega;M)=\tr(x_{0}^{n})\,.

For
c\geq 0
, Lemma
5.2
gives

h_{\psi_{c}}=h^{\eta_{n}}x_{c}h^{\eta_{n}},\qquad x_{c}=V_{c}^{(n)}x_{0}\,.

and

0<Q_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)=\tr(x_{c}^{n})<\infty\,.

By the Yosida regularization argument in the proof of Theorem
5.4
, the function

Q_{n}(c)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=Q_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)=\tr(x_{c}^{n})

is log-convex on
[0,\infty)
:

Q_{n}((1-\theta)c_{0}+\theta c_{1})\leq Q_{n}(c_{0})^{1-\theta}Q_{n}(c_{1})^{\theta}\,.

Taking logarithms gives convexity of
\log Q_{n}(c)
. Since

D_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)=\frac{1}{n-1}\log\left(\frac{Q_{n}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)}{\psi_{c}(1)}\right)=\frac{1}{n-1}\log\left(\frac{Q_{n}(c)}{\psi(1)}\right)\,,

where
\psi_{c}(1)=\psi(T_{c}(1))=\psi(1)
is independent of
c
, convexity of
D_{n}
follows. Convexity implies non-negativity of the second derivative in the distributional sense, and the pointwise statement holds at every point where the classical second derivative exists.
∎

5.4
Rényi QNEC for
n=2

We have proven RQNEC for all integers
n\geq 2
. In this section we present a slightly different proof of RQNEC for
n=2
that is easier to interpret directly on
\mathcal{H}
.
The Rényi QNEC for
n=2
is special since
L^{2}(M)
is a Hilbert space. Using the isometric equivalence of standard forms, we can identify the action of
V_{c}^{(2)}
on
L^{2}(M)
with left multiplication by
e^{-cP}
on the vector representative
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
of the
L^{2}(M)
sandwiched density
x_{0}
. This lends itself to a more transparent physical interpretation of the SRD and RQNEC in terms of the averaged null energy operator/null translation generator
P
.

In this subsection we work with Haagerup’s
L^{2}(M)
space and the pairing
\braket{\cdot,\cdot}_{L^{2}(M)}
refers to the inner product on
L^{2}(M)
defined as

\braket{a,b}\coloneqq\tr(a^{*}b),\quad a,b\in L^{2}(M).

Recall the
L^{2}(M)
semigroup
V_{c}^{(2)}
defined in Sec.
4.1
. The following proposition provides an explicit characterization of
V_{c}^{(2)}
in terms of the null translation generator
P
.

Lemma 5.6

(
L^{2}
-implementation of the null flow)
.

Define
W:L^{2}(M)\to\mathcal{H}
on the dense subspace
Mh^{1/2}
by

W(ah^{1/2})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=a\Omega,\qquad a\in M\,.

Then
W
extends to a unitary. Let

\widehat{P}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=W^{-1}PW,\qquad\widehat{V}_{c}^{(2)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=e^{-c\widehat{P}},\qquad c\geq 0\,.

For every
a\in M^{\mathrm{an}}
,

\widehat{V}_{c}^{(2)}\big(h^{1/4}ah^{1/4}\big)=h^{1/4}T_{c}(a)h^{1/4}\,,

which allows the identification

V_{c}^{(2)}=\widehat{V}_{c}^{(2)}.

In particular,
(V_{c}^{(2)})_{c\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup of self-adjoint contractions on

L^{2}(M)
.

Proof.

For
a\in M
,

\|ah^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}=\tr(h^{1/2}a^{*}ah^{1/2})=\omega(a^{*}a)=\|a\Omega\|^{2}\,.

Since
Mh^{1/2}
is dense in
L^{2}(M)
and
M\Omega
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
,
W

extends to a unitary. Moreover,
W
intertwines the left action of
M
on
L^{2}(M)
with
the given representation of
M
on
\mathcal{H}
. By uniqueness of the standard form
Haagerup1975
,

W\Delta_{L^{2}(M)}^{it}=\Delta_{\Omega;M}^{it}W,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

where
\Delta_{L^{2}(M)}
denotes the modular operator of the Haagerup standard form defined in Theorem
2.13
. Hence, for

a\in M^{\mathrm{an}}
,

W\big(h^{1/4}ah^{1/4}\big)=W\big(\Delta_{L^{2}(M)}^{1/4}(ah^{1/2})\big)=\Delta_{\Omega;M}^{1/4}a\Omega\,.

Now let
a\in M^{\mathrm{an}}
. Using
T_{c}(a)\Omega=U_{-c}a\Omega
, we get

W\big(h^{1/4}T_{c}(a)h^{1/4}\big)=\Delta_{\Omega;M}^{1/4}U_{-c}a\Omega\,.

By the imaginary-time continuation of the Borchers covariance relation (Theorem
3.2
(4)),

\Delta_{\Omega;M}^{1/4}U_{-c}a\Omega=U_{-ic}\Delta_{\Omega;M}^{1/4}a\Omega=e^{-cP}\Delta_{\Omega;M}^{1/4}a\Omega\,.

Therefore

W\big(h^{1/4}T_{c}(a)h^{1/4}\big)=e^{-cP}W\big(h^{1/4}ah^{1/4}\big)=W\widehat{V}_{c}^{(2)}\big(h^{1/4}ah^{1/4}\big)\,.

Since
W
is unitary, this proves the claimed action formula on the dense core
h^{1/4}M^{\text{an}}h^{1/4}
of
L^{2}(M)
. The equality extends by boundedness to all of
L^{2}(M)
. The remaining statements follow
from the spectral calculus for the positive self-adjoint operator
P
.
∎

Proposition 5.7

(
n=2
SRD as expectation value)
.

Let
\psi,x_{0},x_{c},Q_{2}(c)
be as in Lemma
5.2
with
n=2
, so that
0<Q_{2}(c)<\infty
for
c\geq 0
.
Then

x_{c}=V_{c}^{(2)}x_{0}=W^{-1}e^{-cP}Wx_{0}\,.

If
\xi_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=Wx_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, then

Q_{2}(c)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=Q_{2}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)=\|x_{c}\|_{2}^{2}=\|e^{-cP}\xi_{0}\|^{2}=\langle\xi_{0},e^{-2cP}\xi_{0}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.6
,

V_{c}^{(2)}=W^{-1}e^{-cP}W.

Therefore, with
\xi_{0}=Wx_{0}
,

x_{c}=W^{-1}e^{-cP}\xi_{0},

and

Q_{2}(c)=\|x_{c}\|_{2}^{2}=\|e^{-cP}\xi_{0}\|^{2}=\langle\xi_{0},e^{-2cP}\xi_{0}\rangle.

The formula for
Q_{2}(c)
is immediate.
∎

Thus, we have translated the null-flowed SRD to the logarithm of the expectation value of
e^{-2cP}
in the
\mathcal{H}
representative
\xi_{0}
of the
L^{2}(M)
sandwiched density
x_{0}
, where
P
is the averaged null energy operator for the null cut algebras.
RQNEC for
n=2
now follows simply from spectral calculus.

Theorem 5.8

(Rényi QNEC at
n=2
)
.

Assume
Q_{2}(\psi\|\omega;M)<\infty
. Then the function

S_{2}(c)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=D_{2}(\psi_{c}\|\omega;M)

is
C^{\infty}
and convex on
(0,\infty)
. Equivalently,

S_{2}^{\prime\prime}(c)\geq 0,\qquad c>0\,.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.7
,

Q_{2}(c)=\langle\xi_{0},e^{-2cP}\xi_{0}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad\xi_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=Wx_{0}\in\mathcal{H}\,.

Let
E_{P}
be the spectral measure of
P
, and define a finite positive measure on

[0,\infty)
by

\mu(B)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\|E_{P}(B)\xi_{0}\|^{2}\,.

Then

Q_{2}(c)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-2c\lambda}\,d\mu(\lambda)\,.

For
c>0
, differentiation under the integral gives

Q_{2}^{\prime}(c)=-2\int\lambda e^{-2c\lambda}\,d\mu(\lambda),\qquad Q_{2}^{\prime\prime}(c)=4\int\lambda^{2}e^{-2c\lambda}\,d\mu(\lambda)\,.

Set
d\nu_{c}(\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=e^{-2c\lambda}\,d\mu(\lambda)
. By Cauchy-Schwarz in

L^{2}([0,\infty),d\nu_{c})
,

\big(Q_{2}^{\prime}(c)\big)^{2}=4\Big(\int\lambda\,d\nu_{c}\Big)^{2}\leq 4\Big(\int 1\,d\nu_{c}\Big)\Big(\int\lambda^{2}\,d\nu_{c}\Big)=Q_{2}(c)Q_{2}^{\prime\prime}(c)\,.

Since
Q_{2}(c)>0
, it follows that

\frac{d^{2}}{dc^{2}}\log Q_{2}(c)=\frac{Q_{2}(c)Q_{2}^{\prime\prime}(c)-Q_{2}^{\prime}(c)^{2}}{Q_{2}(c)^{2}}\geq 0\,.

This proves the convexity of
S_{2}(c)
.

For normalized states,
S_{2}(c)=\log Q_{2}(c)
; in general one has

S_{2}(c)=\log Q_{2}(c)-\log\psi(1)
, so the additive constant is irrelevant for convexity.
∎

6
Discussion and future directions

We have proven the integer RQNEC (Conjecture
3.6
) for arbitrary von Neumann algebras carrying a half-sided modular inclusion structure, under the single additional hypothesis that the SRD of the excited state with respect to the vacuum is finite on the larger algebra
M_{A}
.
In particular, no form-domain assumption
\Psi\in\Dom(P^{1/2})
is required, in contrast to the proofs of QNEC
Ceyhan:2018zfg
;
Hollands:2025glm
.
Whether QNEC itself can be proven without such a form-domain assumption remains an interesting open problem.

An immediate target for future work is to prove RQNEC for general real Rényi parameter
\alpha>1
. We expect that the proof technique used here can be adapted to prove this statement, and we hope to present the details in a future publication.

In the remainder of this section, we discuss several directions for future research.

RQNEC for semigroups

The integer Rényi QNEC proved here is a log-convexity statement for the semigroup
V_{c}^{(n)}
on
L^{n}(M)
: writing
Q_{n}(c)=\|x_{c}\|_{n}^{n}
for the propagated density
x_{c}=V_{c}^{(n)}x_{0}
, our result is the log-convexity inequality

Q_{n}((1-\theta)c_{0}+\theta c_{1})\leq Q_{n}(c_{0})^{1-\theta}Q_{n}(c_{1})^{\theta}\,.

This is a noncommutative functional inequality for the semigroup
V_{c}^{(n)}
, in the spirit of the log-Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities developed in
olkiewicz1999hypercontractivity
;
junge2015noncommutative
;
carlen2017gradient
and others.
Hollands and Longo
Hollands:2025glm
noted the connection between QNEC-type inequalities and noncommutative entropy production under semigroup actions, in the sense of
Spohn:1978pfz
;
Wirth:2025goz
.
It would be interesting to investigate whether QNEC and RQNEC-type inequalities exist for semigroups arising from other geometric flows in quantum field theory. Similar proof techniques to those in the present work may apply.

Further generalizations of Rényi QNEC

A family of divergences known as optimized quantum
f
-divergences has been studied for finite-dimensional systems
Wilde:2017okz
. These divergences satisfy the data processing inequality, and the SRD is one example of this family. It would be interesting to study these divergences in the von Neumann algebra setting for general QFTs, and prove convexity statements similar to RQNEC.
It is also natural to conjecture the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi QNEC for the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi divergence
Audenaert:2015npv
, a two-parameter generalization of the relative entropy. It has been formulated in terms of Haagerup
L^{p}
spaces
Kato:2023aro
;
Kato:2023hlj
;
Hiai:2024qve
, and investigating its log-convexity under null translations will also be interesting.

Rényi quantum focusing

Recently,
Chandrasekaran:2026pnc
proved the quantum focusing conjecture of
Bousso:2015mna
at leading non-trivial order in perturbative quantum gravity on Killing horizon backgrounds, building on the proof of QNEC
Ceyhan:2018zfg
. For a one-parameter family of horizon cuts labelled by affine parameter
u
, the quantum expansion is defined as the derivative of the generalized entropy,

\Theta(u)\coloneqq\partial_{u}\bar{S}_{\text{gen}}(u;\hat{\psi})\,,

(124)

evaluated on a dressed state
\ket{\hat{\psi}}=\int dx\,f(x)\ket{\Psi}\ket{x}
that pairs an excited bulk state
\ket{\Psi}
with eigenstates
\ket{x}
of the area operator. The quantum focusing conjecture asserts that
\Theta
is non-increasing in
u
,

\partial_{u}\Theta(u)\leq 0\,.

(125)

Equivalently, it is the statement that the generalized entropy is concave.

Following
Chandrasekaran:2022eqq
, the authors of
Chandrasekaran:2026pnc
show that up to perturbative corrections in
G_{N}
,

\bar{S}_{\text{gen}}(u;\hat{\psi})\approx-S_{\text{rel}}(u)+\text{const.},\qquad S_{\text{rel}}(u)=-\langle\hat{\psi},\log\Delta_{\hat{\psi},\hat{\Omega};\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{u}}\hat{\psi}\rangle\,,

(126)

where
\hat{\Omega}
is the cyclic and separating Hartle–Hawking state and
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{u}
is the crossed product of the bulk QFT algebra by its vacuum modular automorphism group. The algebras
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{u}
form a gravitational half-sided modular inclusion. The proof of
Chandrasekaran:2026pnc
then follows
Ceyhan:2018zfg
to derive a variational expression for
\Theta
and deduce its monotonicity from convexity of relative entropy.

Haagerup’s
L^{p}
spaces appear to be the natural language to use in perturbative quantum gravity, since, after accounting for gravitational constraints, the algebras encountered there are crossed products. It would be interesting to investigate whether the SRD and our proof of RQNEC can be used to establish an analogous Rényi quantum focusing theorem. We define the Rényi quantum expansion as

\Theta_{\alpha}(u)=-\partial_{u}D_{\alpha}(\hat{\psi}\|\hat{\Omega};\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{u})\,,

(127)

and conjecture that the Rényi quantum expansion is non-increasing in
u

\partial_{u}\Theta_{\alpha}(u)\leq 0,\quad\forall\alpha>1\,.

(128)

Several alternative definitions are possible, reflecting the various Rényi generalizations of relative entropy, such as the Petz-Rényi divergence. Our choice of the SRD is well-motivated from the perspective that the Rényi quantum expansion (and Rényi focusing) should have a well-defined limit as
G_{N}\to 0
.

Appendix A
Analysis on Banach spaces

This appendix provides the definitions of various standard concepts in functional analysis that are relevant for our work. We also prove some results needed in the main text.

A.1
Topologies and integrals

For a Banach space
X
, we denote its dual by
X^{*}
, and denote the duality pairing by
\phi(x)=\langle\phi,x\rangle
for
x\in X,\phi\in X^{*}
.

Definition A.1

(Weak and weak-
*
topologies)
.

The weak topology on
X
, denoted
\sigma(X,X^{*})
, is defined to be the weakest topology on
X
such that all elements of
X^{*}
are continuous. The weak-
*
topology on
X^{*}
is then the topology
\sigma(X^{*},X)
.

Definition A.2

(Operator topologies)
.

Let
T_{n}
be a sequence of linear operators on a Banach space
X
. The statement that the sequence
T_{n}
converges to an operator
T
on
X
can have the following meanings, with
x\in X
denoting a vector in the Banach space.

1.

If
\|T_{n}-T\|=\sup_{\|x\|\leq 1}\|(T_{n}-T)x\|\to 0
, we say that
T_{n}\to T
in norm topology. This is also referred to as the uniform topology.

2.

If
T_{n}x\to Tx
for all
x\in X
, we say
T_{n}\to T
in the strong operator topology.

3.

If
T_{n}x\to Tx
for all
x\in X
in the
\sigma(X,X^{*})
topology, i.e., if
\phi(T_{n}x)\to\phi(Tx)
for all
\phi\in X^{*}
, we say that
T_{n}\to T
in the weak operator topology.

4.

Consider the Banach space
X=B(\mathcal{H})
, the set of bounded operators on a Hilbert space. The strong-
*
operator topology is defined by convergence in the semi-norms
\|T\|_{\xi}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\|T\xi\|+\|T^{*}\xi\|
. We say that
T_{n}\to T
iff
\|(T_{n}-T)\xi\|+\|(T_{n}^{*}-T^{*})\xi\|\to 0
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
.

Definition A.3

(Ultraweak integral)
.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
a\in M
, define the ultraweak integral

x_{f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{uw}}f(t)\sigma_{t}^{\omega}(a)\,dt

to be the unique element of
M
satisfying

Lemma A.8

We provide a concise summary of the relevant aspects of one-parameter semigroups on Banach spaces. We refer the reader to

Definition A.10

(Strongly continuous semigroup)
.

A one-parameter family

of bounded linear operators on a Banach space

T(0)=I,\qquad\text{and}\qquad T(t+s)=T(t)T(s)\quad\,\forall\,t,s\geq 0,

(135)

and the map
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni t\mapsto T(t)\in B(X)

is continuous w.r.t. the strong operator topology on
X
.
Here
B(X)
is the Banach algebra of all bounded linear operators on
X
with the operator norm
\|\cdot\|
. Define the orbit maps of the semigroup to be
\xi_{x}:t\mapsto T(t)x\in X
.

Proposition A.11
.

For a semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach space
X
, the following are equivalent.

1.

(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is strongly continuous.

2.

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}T(t)x=x
for all
x\in X
.

3.

There exist
\delta>0,M\geq 1
, and a dense subset
D\subset X
such that

(a)

\|T(t)\|\leq M
for all
t\in[0,\delta]
,

(b)

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}T(t)x=x
for all
x\in D
.

Strongly continuous semigroups are uniformly bounded on each compact interval.

Definition A.12

(Strongly continuous contractive semigroup)
.

A strongly continuous semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is said to be bounded if there exists

such that
\|T(t)\|\leq M
for all

. A bounded semigroup is called contractive if

.

Definition A.13

(Generator of semigroup)
.

The generator

(137)

The domain

, a linear subspace of

.

Theorem A.14
.

The generator of a strongly continuous semigroup is a closed densely defined linear operator that determines the semigroup uniquely.

Definition A.15

(Operator graph and graph norm)
.

The graph of a linear operator

(138)

(Core of an operator)

A subspace

of

Definition A.17

For a (not necessarily densely defined) closed linear operator
