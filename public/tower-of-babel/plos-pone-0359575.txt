On the stability of equilibria in degenerate periodic Hamiltonian systems

Nina Xue

ABSTRACT
This paper addresses the problem of Lyapunov stability for equilibrium solutions in periodic Hamiltonian systems under degenerate conditions. The degeneracy occurs in resonant cases when the leading coefficient function in the normal form possesses only zeros of high multiplicity, rendering standard criteria inapplicable. By employing Lie normal form theory, we establish two general stability criteria that resolve the stability problem for nearly all such degenerate scenarios. Our results explicitly demonstrate the decisive role of higher-order terms and provide easily verifiable conditions that generalize several known results in the literature.

The Hamiltonian H ( x , y , t ) is taken to be real analytic in the spatial variables ( x , y ) near (0,0), continuous and 2 π -periodic in t , and satisfies the equilibrium conditions H ( 0 , 0 , t ) = H x ( 0 , 0 , t ) = H y ( 0 , 0 , t ) = 0 . Over the past two decades, the study of the stability of equilibrium solutions in Hamiltonian systems has garnered widespread interest ([ 1 – 4 ]). Understanding the stability of equilibrium positions in Hamiltonian systems is crucial for characterizing their qualitative dynamics. Despite its importance, determining Lyapunov stability for periodic Hamiltonian systems such as system (2) remains an unresolved challenge in many cases. Analytical methods for determining stability are limited to specific circumstances. First, if the linearization has a characteristic exponent with a non-zero real part, the equilibrium is unstable. This leaves the case where the linearized system is stable, with purely imaginary characteristic exponents ± i ω , ( ω ≠ 0 ) , as the subject of inquiry. Here, the nonlinear part of the system becomes crucial for resolving the stability question. For the general elliptic case, (that is, the classical situation in which the characteristic exponents are purely imaginary and the Hamiltonian, after suitable normalization, depends on the action variable in a non-degenerate manner, i.e., with a non-vanishing derivative with respect to the action at the origin), seminal results on stability under certain nondegeneracy conditions were established by Arnold ([ 5 ]) and Moser ([ 6 ]). Their work has spawned a substantial body of subsequent literature on the stability of the trivial solution. Meanwhile, for time-periodic Lagrangian equations, Ortega ([ 7 – 10 ]) developed the third-order approximation method, a technique later extended by other researchers to establish stability for systems like the forced pendulum and singular equations, (by “singular equations” we mean second-order Newtonian periodic differential equations x ″ + g ( x ) = f ( t ) , where the nonlinear restoring termw g ( x ) exhibits a blow-up singularity at some finite spatial value of x ), one can refer to [ 11 – 13 ] and [ 14 ]. A common limitation of these cited results, however, is their inapplicability to the de g enerate case central to this paper. By employing a linear 2 π -periodic symplectic transformation, the quadratic Hamiltonian H 2 can be cast in the form H 2 ( x , y ) = ω 2 ( x 2 + y 2 ) . Then, by the Moser twist theorem (see [ 6 ]), the existence of any non-vanishing coefficient A 2 j ≠ 0 (for some j ≥ 2 ) implies the Lyapunov stability of the origin in system (2). Although it has been shown [ 3 ] that the Hamiltonian (3) can be analytically linearized to H 2 = ω r ensuring stability when ω is Diophantine (a number ω is called Diophantine if there exist constants γ > 0 and τ ≥ 1 such that | q ω − p | ≥ γ | q | − τ for all integers p , q with q ≠ 0 ). and all nonlinear coefficients A 2 j vanish, this conclusion does not extend to non-Diophantine irrational frequencies, whose stability remains unresolved. There exists an extensive literature addressing the stability problem in the resonant case where ω is rational (see [ 1 , 2 , 15 – 20 ]). A typical approach begins with the derivation of the Lie normal form to a certain order. This is followed by the application of either Chetaev’s/Lyapunov’s theorems to demonstrate instability, or the Moser twist theorem to verify stability. Within this framework, Cabral and Meyer [ 16 ] established a general stability criterion. Our analysis also concerns the resonant case, namely when ω = p k is rational. We restrict our attention to the degenerate situation where A 4 = ⋯ = A 2 l = 0 , since otherwise stability is immediate. In this setting, the Hamiltonian (3) transforms into the structure described in Theorem A. For an odd-order resonance, k = 2 n + 1, the Hamiltonian (3) reduces to H = B r 2 n + 1 2 cos ( 2 n + 1 ) φ + H ~ ( r , φ , t ) , this Hamiltonian is analytic, and H ~ = O ( r n + 1 ) is periodic in t . Theorem A thus guarantees Lyapunov instabili t y for B ≠ 0 . Having addressed the odd-order case, we turn to the situation where B = 0 or the resonance order is even ( k = 2 n + 2). Here, the Hamiltonian (3) takes the form H = r n + 1 ( C + D cos 2 ( n + 1 ) φ ) + H ~ ( r , φ , t ) . The analytic Hamiltonian features a remainder H ~ = O ( r n + 3 2 ) that is periodic in t . Under the condition | C | > | D |, Theorem A guarantees t he Lyapunov stability of the origin. Conversely, instability occurs when | C | < | D |. However, Theorem A offers no conclusion when | C | = | D |, as all zeros of the function C + D cos 2 ( n + 1 ) φ are of multiplicity two. This is the precise case that falls outside the scope of the standard theory and will be investigated in this work. We further extend our analysis to the general degenerate case, where all zeros of ψ ( φ ) in Theorem A possess multiplicities greater than one. This scenario, in which Theorem A is no longer applicable, is commonly referred to in the literature as the degenerate case. Hence, to resolve the stability problem in the degenerate case, it becomes essential to incorporate terms in the Hamiltonian K ( r , φ , t ) of order higher than r n . Several results exist for stability analysis in degenerate settings. To our knowledge, the pioneering work is due to Markeyev [ 18 , 19 ], who derived sufficient conditions for both stability and instability in the case of fourth-order resonance. His analysis applied to both periodic one-degree-of-freedom and autonomous two-degree-of-freedom Hamiltonian systems, employing the Moser twist theorem and Lyapunov’s method, respectively. Following this line of research, Mansilla and Vidal [ 17 ] further examined even-order resonances, and Bardin [ 15 ] recently developed general criteria for degeneracy using Lie normal forms. The core method unifying these results is the derivation of stability conditions from a normal form truncated at an appropriate order. For the class of degenerate periodic Hamiltonian systems with one degree of freedom in which the normalized action-dependent part vanishes identically and the leading angular coefficient possesses only zeros of finite multiplicity, Theorems 1 and 2 in the Main results section provide a complete stability classification except for the measure-zero set of parameters where φ 0 and φ 1 share common zeros. Moreover, our approach offers distinct advantages over existing ones. Compared to [ 17 ] and [ 18 ], we provide stability criteria applicable to resonances of arbitrary order. Furthermore, unlike the methodology in [ 15 ], our resulting criteria are straightforward to verify and circumvent the need for computing the intricate main part required in their analysis. To substantiate the claimed novelty, we present the following formal comparison. Proposition 1. Whenever the hypotheses of Bardin (2015) or Mansilla and Vidal (2013) are satisfied, the hypotheses of our Theorems 1 and 2 are also satisfied. The converse is not true. The proof of this proposition follows by observing that the conditions in [ 15 ] and [ 17 ] require a non-degenerate twist at the lowest non-vanishing order, whereas our criteria allow for zeros of arbitrary finite multiplicity. A representative example is given in Example below, which falls outside the scope of Bardin (2015) and Mansilla and Vidal (2013) but is covered by our Theorem 1. The principal difficulty encountered in this work is the complete vanishing of the action-dependent part of the normalized Hamiltonian, which prevents the direct application of standard twist theorems. To overcome this, we developed a two-step normalization procedure combined with a carefully chosen canonical transformation that restores a twist-like structure at a higher order. The difficulties related to handling zeros of arbitrary (even or odd) multiplicity and the need for distinct instability proofs are addressed by analyzing the sign of the higher-order angular coefficient ψ 1 ( φ ) near each zero of the leading coefficient ψ 0 ( φ ) . Main results Overview of the strategy Before stating the main theorems, we outline the four main steps of our approach: Derive the Birkhoff–Gustavson normal form and introduce action-angle variables. Identify the degeneracy in the action-dependent part (i.e., the vanishing of all coefficients A 2 j ). Stability criteria We begin by establishing two general stability criteria applicable to Hamiltonian of the form H ( r , φ , t ) = r α ψ 0 ( φ ) + r α + γ ψ 1 ( φ ) + O ( r α + γ + 1 2 ) , (7) where α = m 2 , m ≥ 3 ; γ = n 2 , n ≥ 1 . This paper focuses on the stability problem for the origin of the Hamiltonian (7) under degenerate conditions, specifically when the leading coefficient function ψ 0 ( φ ) possesses only zeros of multiplicity greater than one. We first present a criterion for the case where all zeros of ψ 0 ( φ ) are of even multiplicity. Theorem 1. Consider the Hamiltonian system φ ˙ = ∂ H ∂ r ( r , φ , t ) , r ˙ = − ∂ H ∂ φ ( r , φ , t ) (8) where the Hamiltonian function is given by (7). Assume that all zeros of the function ψ 0 ( φ ) have even multiplicity. Then the following stability properties hold for the equilibrium r = 0. (A) (Stability Criterion) If, for every zero φ 0 of ψ 0 ( φ ) with multiplicity m > 1, the condition ψ 0 ( m ) ( φ 0 ) ψ 1 ( φ 0 ) > 0 , is satisfied, then the equilibrium r = 0 is Lyapunov stable. (B) (Instability Criterion) If ψ 1 ( φ ) ≠ 0 at every zero of ψ 0 ( φ ) , and there exists at least one zero φ 0 of ψ 0 ( φ ) with multiplicity m > 1 such that ψ 0 ( m ) ( φ 0 ) ψ 1 ( φ 0 ) < 0 , then the equilibrium r = 0 is Lyapunov unstable. We make two observations regarding this theorem. When ψ 0 ( φ ) and ψ 1 ( φ ) share common zeros, the sign of the combined term ψ 0 ( φ ) + r γ ψ 1 ( φ ) near such zeros is not determined by the leading order alone, and our current theorems do not cover this case. Examples 1 Consider the case of fourth-order resonance investigated in [ 18 ], our criteria are directly applicable to this scenario, providing explicit conditions for both stability and instability. In this particular case, the Hamiltonian function simplifies to the following H = ( 1 − cos 4 φ ) r 2 + ( s + k ( 1 − cos 4 φ ) ) r 3 + O ( r 4 ) . [18] showed that stability of the equilibrium r = 0 depends on the sign of s , for s = 1 it is stable, whereas for s = − 1 it is unstable. However, applying Theorem 1 to this setting, we identify the functions ψ 0 ( φ ) and ψ 1 ( φ ) as ψ 0 ( φ ) = 1 − cos 4 φ , ψ 1 ( φ ) = s + k ( 1 − cos 4 φ ) . It is clear that ψ 0 ( φ ) vanishes at φ j = j π 2 , j ∈ ℤ , and each zero is of multiplicity two. Hence, for the case s = 1, we have d 2 ψ 0 d φ 2 ( φ j ) ψ 1 ( φ j ) > 0 , it then follows from (A) of Theorem 1 that the equilibrium is stable. Conversely, if s = − 1 , d 2 ψ 0 d φ 2 ( φ j ) ψ 1 ( φ j ) < 0 holds, according to the (B) of Theorem 1, the equilibrium is unstable. Examples 2 Consider the Hamiltonian H ( r , θ , t ) = ω r + r 2 ( cos 2 θ − 1 2 ) 2 + r 3 Φ 1 ( θ , t ) + r 4 Φ 2 ( r , θ , t ) , where ψ 0 ( θ ) = ( cos 2 θ − 1 2 ) 2 has zeros of multiplicity two. This example satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 1 but does not satisfy the conditions of Cabral and Meyer ([ 16 ]) (which requires simple zeros) or Bardin ([ 15 ]) (which requires multiplicity one). Therefore, it falls outside the scope of previously published results and illustrates the broader applicability of our stability criteria. Conclusion In this paper, we have established two general stability criteria (Theorems 1 and 2) for equilibrium solutions in degenerate periodic Hamiltonian systems with one degree of freedom. For the class of degenerate systems in which the normalized action-dependent part vanishes identically and the leading angular coefficient possesses only zeros of finite multiplicity, our theorems provide a complete stability classification except for the measure-zero set of parameters where ψ 0 and ψ 1 share common zeros. The criteria are applicable to resonances of arbitrary order and provide easily verifiable conditions, thereby eliminating the need for the intricate computations required in earlier analyses. The main limitations of our results are: (i) the case where ψ 0 and ψ 1 share common zeros remains open, and (ii) the analysis is restricted to one degree of freedom. Potential future research directions include: (1) extension to more degrees of freedom; (2) treatment of the common-zero case via blow-up or degenerate KAM theory; (3) numerical verification of the stability criteria on concrete physical models; and (4) application to celestial-mechanics problems with degenerate resonances.
