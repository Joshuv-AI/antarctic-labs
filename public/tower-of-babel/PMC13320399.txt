Interview dataset: Encouraging the development of industrial symbiosis networks in Slovenia – transition to the circular economy
Authors: Erika Džajić Uršič
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This article provides a dataset collected through semi-structured interviews with stakeholders involved in industrial symbiosis and circular economy practices in Slovenia. The dataset includes anonymized transcripts in Slovene and English translations, offering insights into sustainability practices, collaboration mechanisms, and stakeholder trust in the context of the Slovenian transition to a circular economy. All interviews were conducted in Slovene, converted into note-based transcripts, anonymized, and translated into English, resulting in 50 transcripts. The article also presents a structured coding scheme developed from the interview material, including main categories, subthemes, operational definitions, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and illustrative quotations. The coding scheme enhances the dataset's reuse potential by supporting thematic analysis, comparative research, and methodological training in qualitative coding. The Zenodo record contains 54 relevant files across versions v3 and v4: 25 anonymized Slovene transcripts, 25 English translations, 2 interview protocols, 1 README file, and 1 structured codebook file in Excel format. The dataset provides qualitative insights into organizational structures, sustainability goals, collaboration mechanisms, regulatory frameworks, and relational trust dynamics in symbiotic networks. It is openly available for reuse in thematic analysis, discourse studies, comparative policy research, and training in qualitative research methods.

Background

The motivation for compiling this dataset is to provide a structured, qualitative account of industrial symbiosis and circular economy practices in Slovenia. This dataset was generated as part of the research project Encouraging the development of Industrial Symbiosis Networks in Slovenia – transition to the circular economy (J7–50,186), which aimed to investigate how stakeholders across diverse sectors in Slovenia engage with circular economy principles and industrial symbiosis. The dataset primarily comprises anonymized semi-structured interview transcripts with key stakeholders, providing insights into organizational structures, sustainability practices, and collaborative mechanisms within industrial symbiosis networks.

Methodologically, the dataset is rooted in qualitative research, following a semi-structured interview approach and utilizing snowball sampling. The interviews were conducted across various regions of Slovenia to ensure a diverse range of perspectives. The interviews were documented through detailed notes, converted into standardized note-based transcripts, anonymized, and translated into English to ensure accessibility for both local and international researchers. This data article complements the original research by enabling secondary analysis and cross-country comparisons, adding value to the findings by providing open access to detailed interview data and thematic analysis.

Data Description

Version 3 of the dataset contains 53 files: 25 anonymized Slovene transcripts, 25 English translations of the transcripts, one Slovene interview protocol, one English interview protocol, and one README file. Version 4 adds a single structured codebook file in Excel format, bringing the total to 54 relevant files. The codebook provides a condensed overview of ten analytical categories and 35 themes, together with operational definitions, inclusion and exclusion criteria, illustrative quotations, frequency counts, and organization-level coding details. The files are organized with consistent identifiers ranging from 01 to 25, allowing users to match each Slovene transcript to its English translation. Each transcript follows a semi-structured protocol and covers organizational structure, sustainability perspectives, regulatory challenges, collaboration mechanisms, stakeholder trust, and perceived opportunities and barriers to industrial symbiosis. The transcripts range in length from 8 to 15 pages and are stored in .docx format.

The interviews were conducted between September 2023 and June 2025 across multiple Slovenian regions, predominantly in Southeast Slovenia, using a snowball sampling approach. All interviews were documented with detailed notes and converted into standardized note-based transcripts, as participants declined audio recording due to sensitive business information. The interviews reflect a wide diversity of organizational contexts, including public research institutes, manufacturing SMEs, consultancies, and project-based organizations, non-profit institutes, functional enterprises, micro-enterprises, small family businesses, multinational subsidiaries in the automotive sector, and renewable energy companies [2]. This variety ensures representation of multiple stakeholder groups across Slovenia’s business, academic, non-profit, and policy environments.

In addition to the transcripts, the dataset includes two versions of the interview protocol, available in Slovene and English, which were used to guide semi-structured conversations. The protocol covers general organizational structures, sustainability goals, cooperation frameworks, legislative aspects, resource flows, and trust relations, ensuring comparability across cases. The leading researcher and data expert were responsible for collecting, processing, and preparing the data. The dataset is openly shared under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC-BY 4.0) license and is fully anonymized to protect participants’ privacy. It is structured into Slovene and English transcripts, two protocol documents, and one ReadMe file, with clear identifiers for each interview (01–25) consistently applied across languages. Three dataset versions were released: the initial set of Slovene transcripts (v1.0, August 1, 2025), a subsequent version with English translations (v2.0, August 20, 2025), and the combined bilingual dataset with an added ReadMe file (v3.0, August 20, 2025). File formats are standardized (.docx for transcripts and protocols; .txt for documentation) to facilitate reuse in qualitative analysis software. The dataset is openly accessible via Zenodo [3]. Experimental Design, Materials, and Methods

Population and recruitment

Data generation followed a five-step qualitative workflow: participant recruitment, semi-structured interviewing, note-based transcription, anonymization and translation, and thematic coding. The workflow was designed to preserve participant confidentiality while providing reusable qualitative data for secondary analysis. The coding process resulted in a structured codebook that links interview themes with operational definitions, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and illustrative quotations. The dataset is based on 25 semi-structured interviews conducted individually (interviewer-interviewee) between September 2023 and June 2025. Participants were recruited using a snowball sampling strategy, ensuring representation across relevant stakeholder groups, in line with our qualitative case study approach [4]. No participants were paid or rewarded in any way for their participation in the study. The population included representatives from all types of organizations, including NGOs, businesses, public institutions, local associations, and academia.

Recruitment began with initial contacts in Southeast Slovenia, and gradually expanded to other regions as participants referred additional stakeholders embedded in relevant networks. This approach was particularly suitable for accessing organizations and individuals involved in sensitive or less visible collaborations, and recruitment continued until sufficient thematic saturation was achieved across stakeholder groups.

Case selection and participant sampling

Cases were selected to reflect the diversity of Slovenia’s industrial symbiosis landscape, including organizations of different sizes, ownership (public/private/family-owned), and sectoral affiliation (manufacturing, energy, waste management, consulting, cultural institutions, and education). Individual experts and sole proprietors were also included to capture micro-level perspectives. The sample was designed to include both established actors in sustainability networks and emerging participants, allowing comparison of practices, competencies, and institutional conditions across contexts. Participation was voluntary, and recruitment continued until sufficient thematic saturation was reached across stakeholder groups.

Setup of interviews

Depending on the interviewee's availability, all interviews were done in Slovene, either in person at their home, at a public institution, or virtually. Because participants declined audio recording due to the sensitivity of business-related information, the interviews were documented through detailed written notes taken during and immediately after each interview. These notes were then converted into standardized note-based transcripts in Microsoft Word. The process was guided by an interview protocol that was created in Slovene and translated into English. The interview protocol consisted of 8 open-ended questions (with 2–3 sub-questions) organized into three thematic sections. The first section addressed the general potential of industrial symbiosis networks in Slovenia, including organizational structure, willingness to participate in symbiotic cooperation, sustainability goals, local cooperation, and regulatory requirements. The second section focused on the institutional context, including legislation on waste recycling and the reuse of secondary products, as well as factors influencing the implementation of sustainable projects. The third section addressed relationships between companies, local communities, and institutions, with particular attention to knowledge exchange, stakeholder trust, and the implementation of sustainable measures aligned with the Sustainable Development Goals. Participants were given the freedom to go into greater detail on particular subjects significant to their organization, thanks to the design.

Ethical considerations

The research followed ethical principles of informed consent before the interview started, anonymity, and confidentiality. All participants were informed about the study’s objectives, the anonymization process, and their right to withdraw at any time. No personal identifiers, company names, or sensitive business data were retained in the transcripts. Instead, anonymization markers were applied (e.g., “Organization X” or “Stakeholder Y”). This ensured compliance with ethical standards while preserving the material's analytic value. The dataset is published under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License (CC-BY 4.0) and fully anonymized to protect participants’ privacy.

Data generation and analysis

The dataset was generated through semi-structured interviews with stakeholders. The interview protocol was developed to guide conversations around sustainability goals, collaboration practices, regulatory frameworks, and stakeholder trust [5,6], while leaving space for participants to elaborate on issues most relevant to their own contexts. The interviews were analyzed and transcribed according to the qualitative methodology.

The initial interviews and transcriptions were verified by colleagues. The coding was done by the project leader. Instead, detailed notes were taken during the interviews and entered into Microsoft Word for transcription and into Microsoft Excel for thematic analysis to identify recurring themes related to organizational structures, sustainability goals, and collaboration dynamics. To eliminate organizational and personal identifiers, all transcripts were anonymized. Bilingual researchers who were conversant in the terms of sustainability and industrial symbiosis then translated the Slovene transcripts into English, cross-referencing the translations to guarantee accuracy. The analysis followed a framework approach to thematic analysis [4]. Researchers first familiarized themselves with the transcripts and conducted open coding to identify recurring concepts. We grouped these initial codes into categories that revealed common themes across the interviews. These categories included organizational context, perceptions of industrial symbiosis, sustainability practices, regulatory environment, community and location, barriers to cooperation, drivers of cooperation, human capital, collaboration dynamics, and opportunities for change, as shown in the Zenodo repository as the v4 file “Unified_Codebook_with_Org.xlsx”) [3]. Within each category, more specific themes were created. For instance, cost savings and competitiveness were combined as economic drivers, while mistrust and information gaps were combined as barriers to cooperation. The process was iterative and reflexive, with categories and themes refined through repeated review of transcripts until thematic saturation was reached.

Each of the 10 main categories and their associated 35 themes was systematically coded across all 25 interviews, resulting in 1,247 coded segments. Theme occurrence, defined as the number of interviews in which each theme appeared at least once, ranged from 6 to 25, with an average of 19.8 per theme. This provides a semi-quantitative overview of the relative salience of themes across the dataset. The full structured codebook, including operational definitions, inclusion and exclusion criteria, illustrative quotations, frequency counts, and organization-level coding details, is available in the Zenodo repository as the v4 file “Unified_Codebook_with_Org.xlsx” [3]. The frequency counts show that relational and cooperation-related themes, including lack of trust, informal relations, soft skills, and trust as a precondition for cooperation, appeared across all 25 interviews, while themes such as practical irrelevance and systemic incentives were more context-specific. Overall, the distribution of codes supports the interpretation that thematic saturation was reached across the dataset. A more detailed explanation of the codebook categories and themes follows. Limitations

This dataset should be interpreted with several limitations in mind. First, snowball sampling may introduce selection bias because participants were recruited in part through existing professional networks. Second, the absence of audio recordings means that transcripts are based on detailed interview notes rather than verbatim recordings. Third, translation from Slovene into English may have reduced some linguistic nuance, although bilingual researchers cross-checked the translations. Finally, the dataset is context-specific to Slovenia and should therefore be used carefully in direct cross-country generalizations.

Ethics Statement

The authors confirm that the current work complies with ethical guidelines for publication in Data in Brief. The research involved human subjects through semi-structured interviews, and informed consent was obtained verbally from all participants prior to the interviews. As the interviews contained sensitive business information, no audio or video recordings were made to respect participants' privacy. Instead, detailed notes were taken during the interviews, and all data were anonymized to protect participants' identities and confidentiality. The study was conducted in accordance with ethical principles, ensuring that participants were fully informed of their rights and that participation was voluntary.
