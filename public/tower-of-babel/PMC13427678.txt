ECS-Net: An adaptive feature learning semantic segmentation network for accurate concrete porosity prediction
Authors: Tiantian Liang, Dunxin Gu, Zerun Guo, Yongyang Li, Yang Liu, Henan Hu, Mao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryPorosity prediction is important for concrete engineering safety and quality, but reliable computed tomography (CT) image segmentation remains challenging because of evolving internal pore structures, tiny pores, low grayscale contrast, and intricate morphologies. This study develops efficient concrete segmentation network (ECS-Net), an encoder-decoder semantic segmentation network equipped with three dedicated core modules for adaptive pore feature extraction from complex CT backgrounds. Its multiscale encoder integrates global structural information with local tiny-pore details, while the complexity-aware decoder adaptively improves segmentation at fuzzy boundaries and in low-contrast regions. Experiments on a self-built concrete CT dataset, Crack500, and aggregate datasets show that ECS-Net achieves improved segmentation performance and generalization ability compared with mainstream models. Three-dimensional reconstruction experiments using a limited number of specimens further confirm its stable performance in porosity prediction. These findings suggest that ECS-Net can support nondestructive detection of internal damage in concrete, structural integrity diagnosis, and long-term performance evaluation of in-service concrete structures.

Introduction

As a composite material widely used in construction,1,2 concrete exhibits significant variations in its physicomechanical properties owing to the considerable density differences among its constituents. The internal pore structure directly influences concrete’s macroscopic mechanical properties, ultimately determining its service life and structural reliability.3,4 In recent years, with the development of emerging materials such as 3D-printed ultra-high-performance concrete,5,6 the accurate prediction of pore characteristics in concrete has become particularly important. Therefore, porosity, a key characteristic of concrete, plays a critical role in accurately assessing structural health. Thus, a systematic investigation of the pore structure and an accurate prediction of porosity are imperative for a deeper understanding of concrete’s damage mechanisms.7 Relevant studies further indicate that pore-structure evolution is closely correlated with crack propagation and damage progression in concrete, which further highlights the significance of accurate pore characterization for revealing concrete deterioration mechanisms.8 Computed tomography (CT) generates images with varying grayscale values based on the differential absorption of X-rays by materials, clearly revealing the microstructure of concrete. Many researchers have extracted the morphological characteristics of pores from CT images to analyze and quantify internal damage.9,10 However, the internal pores in concrete exhibit complex distributions at varying scales. In studies on damage mechanisms based on CT images, the failure to accurately segment these features can lead to the omission of critical details, thereby impairing porosity prediction accuracy and hindering the interpretation of crack initiation and damage evolution mechanisms.11,12 Therefore, it is essaforementionedential to employ advanced image-processing and segmentation techniques to accurately identify pores in CT images and predict porosity, thereby providing critical support for diagnosis of structural integrity and performance evaluation of existing concrete structures.

Traditional machine-learning methods have been widely applied to extract microstructural features from concrete CT images.13,14 Among these, threshold segmentation is a predominant method owing to its operational simplicity and efficacy in segmenting targets with distinct grayscale differences. For instance, Fang et al.15 proposed a probabilistic statistical method for determining threshold values, segmenting CT images into aggregates, hardened cement, and hole-crack areas, to develop a stochastic numerical model that simulates the microscopic constituents and characteristics of the aggregate-mortar interface. To effectively extract regions with similar grayscale values or blurred boundaries, Hu et al.16 developed an image radius-based adaptive threshold segmentation algorithm that can accurately distinguish aggregates, air voids, and asphalt mastic during high-temperature damage analysis. Considering that using threshold segmentation alone could lead to the misclassification of objects with similar grayscale values, Liu et al.17 built upon this work by combining threshold segmentation with K-means clustering to partition images into multiple clusters based on the global distribution of pixel grayscale. To address complex pore boundaries in porous asphalt CT images, Wang et al.18 used the Otsu thresholding algorithm and introduced an image-dodging algorithm to correct mis-segmented boundaries, thereby accurately identifying and subclassifying pores into interconnected, semi-interconnected, and closed. Nevertheless, the data-processing speed and accuracy of these methods still require improvement. In addition to conventional threshold-based segmentation methods, researches on gray-value segmentation have also advanced in the characterization of concrete pores and damage evolution. Huang et al. segmented and reconstructed the three-dimensional (3D) mesoscale structure of concrete using in-situ CT images, and verified the significant influence of pore structure on microscopic failure analysis and damage evolution.19 Furthermore, CT-image-based mesoscale models have been employed to analyze the dynamic fracture behavior of concrete under impact loading based on gray-value segmentation. The results revealed a close correlation between pore distribution characteristics and multiscale mechanical response patterns.20 The aforementioned studies achieved strong physical interpretability for image segmentation and pore-related analysis under limited labeled data, and ensured the practical applicability of the proposed method when combined with mechanical correlation analysis. Despite the numerous machine-learning methods reported for segmenting concrete CT images, their application often entails laborious parameter tuning for different samples,21,22,23 which is time-consuming and offers limited accuracy, thereby failing to meet the porosity-prediction requirements under complex data conditions. Nevertheless, CT-based studies have advanced continuously in the field of microstructural characterization, evolving from simple phase segmentation to the quantitative analysis of void morphology, connectivity, pore-throat structure, and damage evolution. For instance, Shakib and Mayah24 summarized quantitative characterization methods for pore size, crack development, and phase distribution at the 3D scale. Meanwhile, for reinforced concrete, high-resolution 3D CT has been integrated with complementary material characterization techniques to investigate the coupled evolution of voids, corrosion products and cracks, further demonstrating the importance of reliable pore characterization.25

In recent years, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have provided a novel approach for processing and analyzing concrete images.26,27 Trained on large volumes of labeled data, CNN-based methods can learn complex image patterns, enabling the automatic extraction of discriminative features and pixel-level segmentation of target regions. This approach mitigates errors resulting from manual parameter tuning while enhancing segmentation accuracy and robustness. These advantages make CNN-based methods a mainstream approach in structural health monitoring research, particularly for segmenting concrete images.28,29,30 Among these, UNet-based methods have been widely adopted. For instance, Li et al.31 employed UNet for automatic crack extraction from real-time CT images under uniaxial compression. To enhance segmentation accuracy in images with brightness anomalies, Yu et al.32 incorporated residual modules and a hybrid loss function into UNet to create R-UNet, which offers faster convergence and a lower loss value. To reduce training parameters and enhance accuracy, Liu et al.33 developed a lightweight network, called light-weight and modified VGG-Unet, which integrates two attention modules and an improved VGG-11 backbone into the UNet architecture and demonstrated robustness in segmenting both micro-scale pore structures from scanning electron microscopy-backscattered electron images and meso-scale pores from X-ray CT images. Furthermore, Li et al.34 adopted U-Net3+ to effectively capture and quantify intricate internal pore structures, providing an intuitive methodology for investigating pore evolution under complex service conditions. In addition to the UNet series, other improved network architectures have been applied for concrete CT image analysis. Hong et al.35 proposed an improved DeepLabV3+ network that uses Xception as the backbone and incorporates an additional 3 × 3 convolutional layer during upsampling to enhance boundary details for identifying fiber regions. Zhao et al.36 adopted an improved faster R-CNN framework to detect pores and cracks by integrating a feature pyramid network and region of interest (ROI) alignment into ResNet backbones, which enhanced multiscale feature representation at the cost of slower detection speed. Yu et al.37 trained a mask R-CNN model to distinguish between aggregate and paste phases in pervious concrete, whereas Tian et al.38 improved it by incorporating ROI align, supporting high-precision three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction and investigations of defect propagation under real-time loading conditions. Meanwhile, generative models and Transformer architectures have been introduced into this field. Generative adversarial networks have been trained on limited CT data to synthesize micro-structural images for the numerical simulation of crack propagation.39 Moreover, a sinogram Transformer model has been proposed to tackle imaging challenges arising from sparse projection data, allowing efficient tracking of crack evolution.40 Nevertheless, the reconstruction accuracy and robustness of this approach are limited by insufficient prior information or significant projection noise.

Accurate segmentation of pore structures is the foundational step for one of its most critical applications: predicting concrete porosity—a factor widely recognized as decisive for mechanical and durability performance in concrete engineering research.41,42 Many studies have established the theoretical and empirical link between porosity and concrete performance. For instance, Peng et al.43 and Wang et al.44 developed porosity-based mechanical and mesoscale models, respectively, demonstrating the influence of porosity on crack initiation and tensile behavior. Other researchers have further confirmed the decisive role of pore structure in compressive strength evolution under varying environmental conditions.45,46 Yu et al.47 found compressive strength to be more sensitive to porosity than to permeability, whereas Wijekoon et al.48 showed that using porosity as an input feature significantly boosted the predictive accuracy (R2 from 0.78 to 0.92) of neural-network models. This underscores the importance of precise porosity prediction in concrete engineering research. However, prevailing predictive approaches, such as the extreme gradient boosting method,49 face a significant limitation: they often depend on prior knowledge of mixture proportions or manual feature engineering rather than deriving porosity directly and autonomously from image data. This limitation is particularly acute for image-based methods. Although you look only once version 11 network has been employed to detect porosity,50 a fundamental challenge persists: existing segmentation networks remain insufficiently adaptive to the non-uniform distribution and diverse morphologies of pores in concrete images. In addition to the previous studies, advanced CT imaging techniques have been adopted to explore the relationships among pores, crack initiation, and damage propagation. These findings reveal that pore- and void-related features significantly affect crack initiation patterns and propagation paths under different cover thicknesses and corrosion conditions.51,52 The aforementioned theories and methodologies have laid a solid theoretical foundation for relevant investigations. However, research that directly integrates image-based pore segmentation with quantitative porosity prediction remains relatively limited.

In summary, traditional methods such as gray-value segmentation require less data and low computational cost, and facilitate mechanical correlation analysis, but the capability to accurately characterize complex pores needs to be improved. In addition, deep-learning-based methods have been increasingly applied for concrete-image identification. However, current CNNs still face difficulties. These challenges stem from the small size and intricate morphology of internal pores, the presence of dark impurities that resemble defects in grayscale, and the blurred boundaries of pores in CT images. Current methods encounter the following issues: first, local detail is prone to loss during feature extraction, complicating the accurate segmentation of pores. Second, global and local features remain insufficiently integrated, adversely affecting the accurate modeling of multiscale pore structures. Finally, in complex regions, the grayscale features of dark impurities closely resemble those of actual defects, leading to mis-segmentation. These issues collectively pose significant challenges for concrete-porosity prediction. Therefore, this paper proposes an encoder-decoder semantic segmentation network with adaptive feature learning capabilities, named the efficient concrete segmentation network (ECS-Net), which is used to segment pores in concrete CT images to support more reliable porosity prediction. Different from the aforementioned conventional methods, ECS-Net relies on supervised training data and consumes more computing resources but exhibits distinct advantages in fine boundary reconstruction and pore morphology quantification, and thus presents satisfactory novelty in CT-based quantitative pore analysis. Meanwhile, ECS-Net is not a simple module addition based on existing models but a task-oriented architectural design specifically for concrete CT image segmentation. Its innovation is as follows.

First, in Transformer-assisted segmentation architectures, Transformers tend to focus more on global contextual trends, while their ability to capture fine-grained local details remains inadequate. In contrast, ECS-Net incorporates the proposed residual-like group convolution (RLGConv) into its network architecture. This module fuses grouped convolution with a residual branch integrated with downsampling, thereby strengthening the network’s representational capacity for localized details, particularly small and irregularly shaped continuous pores. Second, traditional multiscale architectures mainly rely on parallel feature extraction and straightforward feature concatenation, which are still insufficient in addressing the inherent conflict between global and local feature representations. To overcome this limitation, ECS-Net integrates a novelly designed multi-port feature integration and optimization module (MPFIOM). This module adopts a triple-branch feature extraction structure with hierarchical interactive fusion, thereby enabling bidirectional feature enhancement in which local features guide global understanding and global context regularizes local details, while significantly boosting the modeling capability for multiscale pore structures. Furthermore, conventional attention-based approaches primarily introduce spatial or channel reweighting to enhance feature representation, yet their ability to characterize regions with low contrast and blurred boundaries needs further improvement. Accordingly, ECS-Net develops a complexity-aware adaptive optimizer (CAAO) to address interference from dark substances and insufficient segmentation accuracy in complex regions. By calculating regional complexity metrics from feature maps, this module provides adaptive guidance for dynamically adjusting deformation parameters within its internal convolutions, thus automatically refining the feature-extraction procedure and substantially improving segmentation accuracy for structurally complex areas. It is emphasized that the three sophisticatedly designed components in the proposed ECS-Net do not work independently, but complement each other, forming an integrated structure that can effectively balance global context and fine-grained local details. Aiming at the issues of small and irregularly shaped continuous pores, multiscale pore structures, and the difficulty in distinguishing dark impurities from real pores, these three components perform their respective targeted functions while mutually boosting the performance of other modules. Consequently, the proposed ECS-Net achieves favorable segmentation accuracy and generalization for concrete image segmentation under complex backgrounds, further laying a solid foundation for improving the accuracy of porosity prediction.

Results

Network architecture

The explanation of the core modules of the encoder-decoder structure with the RLGConv, MPFIOM, and CAAO. In this structure, the RLGConv module uses grouped convolution to perform initial characterization of the input features and forms a residual-like structure with the downsampling operation. The obtained features are finally fed into the spatial attention (SA) block for in-depth processing. MPFIOM, which aims to achieve the efficient fusion of global and local features, consists of two stages: the information processing stage and the information fusion stage. The former stage is primarily composed of local information processing, global information processing, and prior feature processing, while the core component of the latter stage is the residual structure. CAAO leverages multisource complexity evaluation to compute multiple complexity metrics and employs dynamic parameter generation to produce evaluation scores, thereby achieving adaptive adjustment of the internal parameters of AK Conv.

This study proposes an adaptive feature learning network based on an encoder-decoder architecture, termed ECS-Net, designed for the comprehensive and precise segmentation of pores from concrete CT images to enable accurate porosity prediction. Its architecture is shown in Figure 1.Figure 1Architecture of ours (ECS-Net)

Architecture of ours (ECS-Net)

The encoder first processes the input image using two consecutive 3 × 3 convolutions for preliminary feature extraction, effectively capturing fundamental textural information. Subsequently, following the down-sampling operation, another two consecutive 3 × 3 convolutions are performed, and the resulting feature maps are fed into a triple-branch architecture for deeper information processing. The local-information-extraction branch comprises four custom-designed RLGConv modules, which enhance the network’s sensitivity and representational capacity for localized details. Simultaneously, a Swin-Transformer module constitutes the global-information-extraction branch, enabling the effective capture of long-range dependency features and enhancing the recognition of complex backgrounds and large-scale structures. To effectively integrate these local and global features, the MPFIOM is designed to systematically fuse and enhance the extracted information, ensuring a balanced and complementary multiscale feature representation. In the decoder, the CAAO further enhances segmentation accuracy. This optimizer integrates and refines features from both the encoder’s MPFIOM and the previous decoder stage’s deconvolution operations. It adaptively evaluates feature complexity and dynamically adjusts the internal parameters of its convolutional kernels. This mechanism significantly reduces the probability of mis-segmenting low-intensity small material regions while maintaining computational efficiency, thereby achieving a balance between precision and inference speed. Finally, the standard convolution in the decoder’s output stage is replaced with a dual convolution to refine feature-map processing. Experimental setup

Data acquisition and processing

The dataset used in this study comprised CT images of cement concrete specimens, which were prepared using conventional fabrication methods and acquired independently. Part of the slice images of the specimens are presented in Figure 3. To obtain high-resolution internal structural information, a Brucker Skyscan 1172 micro-CT scanner operating at a scanning resolution of 5.87 μm was used.Figure 3Representative examples from the self-developed concrete CT image dataset used for training and evaluation

Representative examples from the self-developed concrete CT image dataset used for training and evaluation

The CT dataset adopted in this study was derived from seven concrete specimens (3 specimens were employed to model training and testing, the remaining 4 specimens were used for 3D reconstruction), which were fabricated with a typical C40 concrete mixture. All specimens were cured under standard curing conditions and scanned at a curing age of 28 days. The geometric dimensions of each specimen were Φ2 cm × 1 cm. Additionally, the scanned regions contained defect-containing areas. Among them, the first two contained a total of 1,200 CT slices for model training, while the third one included 600 CT slices for testing. Data augmentation strategies, including random rotation and flipping, were only applied to the training set, expanding the training samples to 2,400 images to improve the generalization capability of the model. Due to the strong spatial correlation between adjacent CT slices from the same specimen, a specimen-level data splitting strategy was adopted to avoid potential data leakage caused by slice-level random partitioning. Specifically, all slices collected from an identical specimen were entirely assigned to either the training set or the test set, thereby ensuring the reliability of experimental results.

The annotation process was completed using the open-source tool LabelMe, in which pore regions were manually delineated via polygon-based annotations. The generated JSON annotation files were subsequently converted into pixel-level binary masks for model training and evaluation. For regions of low-contrast pores with blurred boundaries, the annotation results were determined through joint consultation by three experts in the field of concrete materials. In addition, all annotated samples were carefully reviewed to guarantee the consistency and reliability of annotations.

Evaluation metrics

To quantitatively evaluate the performance of the proposed method in segmenting small and irregularly shaped continuous pores, three widely adopted metrics were selected: the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC), intersection over union (IoU), and average surface distance (ASD). The DSC measures the overlap between the segmentation result and the ground truth, reflecting overall segmentation accuracy. The IoU quantifies the ratio of the intersection to the union of the predicted and true regions, serving as a key indicator of segmentation precision. Given the importance of pore-boundary morphology for subsequent analysis, ASD was employed to measure the average distance between the predicted and actual boundaries, thereby assessing the model’s edge-localization capability. Among the three primary evaluation metrics, higher IoU and DSC values indicate superior segmentation performance, while a lower ASD value (measured in pixels, 1 pixel corresponds to 0.01 mm in practice) reflects more accurate boundary localization.

It is worth noting that DSC and IoU mainly reflect the quality of regional overlap, while ASD focuses more on boundary accuracy, making these three metrics mutually complementary. Higher DSC and IoU values generally correspond to lower ASD values, demonstrating an overall consistency between regional segmentation accuracy and boundary localization accuracy. However, in certain cases, if a model can adequately cover target regions yet suffers from boundary offset, relatively high DSC and IoU may be obtained together with an unsatisfactorily large ASD. Thus, this study adopts the three metrics for performance evaluation, to comprehensively reveal the advantages of ours (ECS-Net) in regional overlap measurement, boundary reconstruction, and pore morphology preservation.

DSC is calculated as follows:(Equation 1)DSC=2×TP2×TP+FP+FN,where TP (true positive) denotes the number of correctly predicted positive features, FP (false positive) represents the number of negative features incorrectly predicted as positive, and FN (false negative) indicates the number of positive features incorrectly predicted as negative.

IoU is calculated as(Equation 2)IoU=|P∩GT|P∩GT,where P denotes the predicted region and GT represents the ground truth labeled region.

Finally, ASD is calculated as(Equation 3)ASD=1|S|+|G|[∑s∈Sd(s,G)+∑g∈Gd(g,S)](Equation 4)d(x,Y)=miny∈Y‖x−y‖2,where S denotes the set of predicted boundary points, G represents the set of ground truth boundary points, and d(x,Y) is the minimum Euclidean distance from point x to set Y.

Because higher IoU and DSC and lower ASD values indicate better performance, this study adopted IoU, DSC, and ASD as performance metrics to facilitate analysis. Consequently, higher values for all three metrics corresponded to improved overall performance.

Loss function

Considering the inherent characteristics of concrete CT images, including sparse pore distribution, severe foreground-background imbalance, and blurred object boundaries, this study employs a BCE+Dice loss function. BCE provides stable supervision at the pixel level and facilitates stable convergence during model training. By contrast, Dice loss optimizes segmentation from the perspective of regional overlap and is highly adaptive to small-scale foreground targets and class imbalance issues. The integration of the two loss functions simultaneously balances local classification accuracy and overall regional consistency, making it well suited for the pore segmentation task in this work. The binary cross-entropy loss is calculated as(Equation 5)LCE=−[y·log(p)+(1−y)·log(1−p)],where y∈(0,1) is the true label and p∈[0,1] is the predicted probability of the positive class.

The DSC loss is calculated as(Equation 6)LDICE=1−2∑ipiti+smooth∑ipi+∑iti+smooth,where pi is the flattened vector of predicted values, ti is the flattened vector of ground-truth labels, and “smooth” is a term used to prevent division by zero. The hybrid loss function is calculated as(Equation 7)Lmixed=α·LCE+(1−α)·LDICE,where α∈[0,1] is a weighting coefficient that balances the contribution of the two loss components. In this study, α was set to 0.5 to ensure an approximately equal contribution of the two loss terms during training.

To verify the rationality of the selected loss function for pore segmentation tasks with severe class imbalance, comparative experiments were conducted based on ours (ECS-Net) using BCE + Dice loss, Focal loss, and Tversky loss functions, respectively. As shown in Figure 4A, all three loss functions exhibited an overall downward trend during the training process. This result indicated that under the three loss functions, ours (ECS-Net) could achieve effective optimization. Specifically, the BCE + Dice loss function demonstrated a rapid decrease in the early training stage and reached a relatively stable state at an earlier epoch. Its subsequent convergence process was smooth, with the final loss value reaching 0.03395. Similarly, focal loss also exhibited a stable optimization trend, achieving a final loss value of 0.04244. This suggested that focal loss was effective in addressing class imbalance issues, yet its overall convergence performance was slightly inferior to that of BCE + Dice loss. In contrast, although Tversky loss decreased rapidly in the early stage of training, its convergence speed slowed down in the later phase, resulting in a relatively high final loss value of 0.05913. On this basis, the three metrics in Figure 4B further demonstrate the superiority of the BCE + Dice loss. It achieves the optimal overall test performance, with an IoU of 0.897, a DSC of 0.956, and an ASD of 0.618. Compared with the second-best loss function, focal loss (IoU = 0.867, DSC = 0.929, ASD = 0.672), the BCE + Dice loss increased the IoU and DSC by 0.030 and 0.027, respectively, while reducing the ASD by 0.054. By contrast, Tversky loss yields the poorest overall performance, with an IoU of merely 0.850, a DSC of merely 0.919, and the highest ASD of 0.872. Collectively, these results demonstrated that the proposed BCE + Dice loss function was more suitable for this task compared to both focal loss and Tversky loss.

As shown in Figure 5A, ours (ECS-Net) achieved the highest precision across all metrics throughout the training process. Although its ASD value experienced a brief stagnation around epoch 20, it subsequently resumed rapid convergence. Among the competitor networks, SegDecNet++ achieved the second-best performance. However, its ability to capture fine spatial details in complex environments might be affected. UNet-based networks, such as CSAUNet and UNet++, showed relatively unsatisfactory performance, possibly because they relied on skip connections for detail recovery. Porosity prediction results and analysis

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed ECS-Net in predicting concrete porosity, this section adopted a 3D reconstruction method to obtain both the overall porosity and layer-by-layer surface porosity output by ECS-Net. Furthermore, the model predictions were compared against two types of reference results: the experimental results from mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP), and the 3D reconstruction results of expert-annotated concrete CT images.

3D reconstruction results and analysis

This study utilized CT scans from 4 custom-made concrete specimens, each containing 600 tomographic layers, to predict porosity and explore practical applications. All pretrained networks were directly applied to segment these CT image datasets without modifying their architectures or parameters. Following segmentation, 3D volume reconstruction and visualization were performed using Avizo software, and the resulting models were used for quantitative porosity analysis.

First, the binary slice sequences predicted by the network were imported into Avizo software in the original order, and voxel calibration was completed according to the CT scanning parameters. The scanning resolution adopted in this study was 5.87 μm. All slices were continuously exported and reconstructed with isotropic voxels. Thus, the 3D voxel size was set to 5.87 μm × 5.87 μm × 5.87 μm. Subsequently, the segmentation results were processed in Avizo following the workflow: volume edit → interactive thresholding → generate surface → AND image → label analysis.

The total porosity in this study was calculated via the volume fraction, i.e.,(Equation 8)Ptotal=VporeVROI,where Vpore represents the total pore volume and VROI denotes the total volume of the region of interest.

The layer-by-layer surface porosity was calculated using the area fraction of each slice, i.e.,(Equation 9)Pi=Apore,iAROI,i,where Apore,i denotes the pore area of the i-th slice, and AROI,i represents the total area of the corresponding region of interest.

In summary, the 3D reconstruction results generated by ours (ECS-Net) provide a solid foundation for higher-precision and more convenient concrete porosity prediction. This holds great potential for concrete engineering research, serving as a reference for identifying local damage, predicting material service life, and formulating maintenance strategies.

In conclusion, we proposed a novel encoder-decoder segmentation network with adaptive feature learning capabilities, termed ECS-Net, to address the challenge of accurately identifying small, irregular, and low-contrast pores in concrete CT images. By integrating the RLGConv module for fine-grained feature extraction, the MPFIOM module for multiscale representation, and a complexity-adaptive adjustment mechanism in the decoder, the model yielded improved segmentation precision for structurally complex regions. Comprehensive evaluations against mainstream approaches confirmed its superior performance in terms of IoU, DSC, and ASD. Specifically, ECS-Net outperformed the second-best SegDecNet++ by 0.7% and 0.9% in IoU and DSC, respectively, and reduced ASD values by 0.047. Moreover, ECS-Net surpassed the baseline UNet by 8.3% and 4.6% in IoU and DSC, respectively, and reduced ASD by 0.401. Using different random seeds, ECS-Net achieved mean ± SD values of 0.9624 ± 0.0028 in IoU and 0.9524 ± 0.0014 in DSC, along with 0.2750 ± 0.0035 in ASD, showing optimal metric performance among all competing models. In generalization tests on the Crack500 dataset, ECS-Net outperformed SegDecNet++ by 0.7% in both IoU and DSC, and surpassed UNet by 8.5% and 10.4% in IoU and DSC, respectively. Meanwhile, ECS-Net reduced ASD by 0.028 and 0.722, compared with SegDecNet++ and UNet, respectively. Extensive experiments on the custom aggregate dataset demonstrated that ECS-Net achieved optimal performance across all evaluation metrics. Its IoU, DSC, and ASD reached 0.914, 0.922, and 0.233, respectively, which comprehensively outperform all comparative networks.

Furthermore, 3D reconstruction experiments based on the segmentation outputs of different networks validated the effectiveness of ECS-Net in concrete porosity prediction. For four specimens, zoomed-in local detail experiments indicated that ECS-Net achieved the optimal results in three key indicators, including the mean local IoU, mean local pore connectivity preservation rate, and mean small pore detection rate, verifying its prominent advantages in pore connectivity, boundary integrity, and small pore capture. Using the prediction error between network-predicted porosity and MIP-based reference values as the evaluation metric, ECS-Net reduced the average porosity prediction error by 3.55% and 44.65% compared with SegDecNet++ and UNet, respectively. It should be noted that the porosity derived from CT images is not completely consistent with that measured by MIP. MIP can characterize connected pores over a wide pore-size range, including nanoscale gel pores and fine capillary pores, whereas the micro-CT images used in this study were limited by the scanning resolution of 5.87 μm and mainly captured pores larger than this scale. Therefore, they cannot effectively identify the smaller-scale concrete pore structures that can be detected by MIP. Consequently, the porosity obtained using accurate image segmentation algorithms is generally lower than the MIP measurement results. The overall porosity prediction results also support this viewpoint. After excluding the influence of abnormally high porosity caused by mis-segmentation in comparison algorithms, the porosity values obtained by the proposed ECS-Net were lower than the MIP reference value. In future work, higher-resolution scanning methods will be adopted to improve the identification accuracy of small pores, thereby making the porosity results obtained from algorithm-based segmentation closer to the MIP reference value. For layer-by-layer surface porosity prediction, based on the reference values derived from 3D reconstruction of expert-labeled CT images, ECS-Net also exhibited significant accuracy advantages over other state-of-the-art networks. These results solidly demonstrated its accuracy in both pore segmentation and porosity prediction.

Overall, the findings of this work sufficiently indicated that, although the incorporation of these advanced components led to a slight increase in both computational cost and inference time, the proposed method offered a reliable and automated approach for pore structure analysis in concrete CT imaging. Further studies may adopt high-resolution imaging techniques to break through the resolution bottleneck and improve the recognition capability of tiny pores in 3D-printed ultra-high-performance concrete. In addition, future work may explore time-series CT data to capture pore evolution and adopt lightweight strategies to enhance computational efficiency, they apply and test the model to more challenging images, such as those with various concrete mixtures. In future research, different concrete mixtures will be further investigated, including concrete incorporating various mineral admixtures, concrete with different strength grades, and concrete with different air contents. The adaptability of the model to various concrete mixtures will be further optimized, with the aim of promoting its application in practical engineering projects, thereby further improving the practicality of the model.

Discussion

To address the limited segmentation capability for small, irregularly shaped pores with blurred boundaries in concrete CT images, a challenging issue that leads to inaccurate porosity prediction, this study proposed a novel encoder-decoder semantic segmentation architecture. The proposed network with adaptive feature learning capabilities enhances the extraction of fine-grained features by introducing the RLGConv module and optimizes multiscale feature representation using the MPFIOM module. Additionally, a CAAO in the decoder dynamically adjusts convolutional parameters based on regional feature complexity, thereby improving segmentation accuracy in structurally complex areas.

Comparative experiments against several mainstream models, including UNet, UNet++, DeepLabV3+, CrackWNet, CSAUNet, and SegDecNet++, demonstrated that the proposed method consistently outperforms them across key metrics (IoU, Dice, and ASD), achieving higher segmentation quality and more refined boundary delineation. Generalization experiments on a public crack dataset further validated its cross-dataset adaptability. Validation experiments based on 3D reconstructions confirmed the model’s high accuracy in porosity prediction. The prediction error for ECS-Net was minimal when compared to reference MIP measurements, indicating that the method provides more structurally faithful quantitative results. Moreover, the layer-by-layer surface-porosity predictions were smooth and coherent, demonstrating strong interlayer consistency and detail preservation. The network’s high sensitivity to specimen edge information also enables the analysis of surface spalling, offering valuable insights into local structural damage and degradation trends.

It should be noted that integrating these advanced modules significantly enhances the segmentation accuracy in concrete pores. Nevertheless, in the comparative and generalization experiments, the proposed ECS-Net increases computational time by 3.2% and 3.8%, respectively, compared with the second-best performing SegDecNet++. Within the experimental scope of this study, such increases in computational complexity and inference time are marginal, but may pose a limitation in more complex, resource-constrained scenarios. In recent years, the pore structure characterization and damage mechanism analysis of 3D-printed ultra-high-performance concrete (3DP-UHPC) have gradually become a prominent research hotspot. Existing studies have demonstrated that the layered deposition forming process of 3DP-UHPC induces distinct interlayer characteristics, anisotropic pore architecture, and printing-path-induced mesoscopic heterogeneity. Meanwhile, the coupling effect among pores, fibers, and interfaces further governs its fracture behavior and mechanical anisotropy. From the perspective of structural design, the RLGConv and MPFIOM proposed in this work exhibit promising adaptability to such complex pore structures in terms of local detail capture and global-local feature fusion. Nevertheless, ECS-Net is also constrained by the upper limit of imaging resolution when the pore scale is lower than the spatial resolution of CT images. In addition, the high-resolution and large-scale slice data of 3DP-UHPC may inevitably increase the computational cost and GPU memory burden of the model during the inference phase. Accordingly, ECS-Net possesses considerable application potential in the research of 3DP-UHPC. However, its adaptability to interlayer heterogeneous structures, ultrafine pores and large-scale high-resolution datasets still requires comprehensive verification.

Overall, this study substantially enhances the segmentation accuracy of diverse pore structures in concrete CT images, offering an efficient and automated solution for porosity prediction, and thus may hold broader prospects in mechanical correlation analysis. Future work may incorporate time-series scanning data to analyze the evolution of pore structures and model material degradation, while further improving computational efficiency to strengthen its practical applicability in real-world engineering scenarios.

Limitations of the study

Although the proposed ECS-Net achieved promising performance in concrete CT image segmentation and porosity prediction, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the dataset used in this study was self-developed and relatively limited in terms of specimen number, concrete mixture type, curing condition, and scanning resolution. Therefore, further validation on larger and more diverse datasets is required to confirm the generalizability of the proposed method. Second, although ECS-Net showed strong capability in extracting small pores and blurred boundaries, a small number of extremely fine pores may still be missed, and slight boundary deviations may occur in regions with very low contrast, irregular morphology, or locations close to specimen edges. Third, the porosity prediction experiments were conducted on a limited number of specimens, and the relationship between CT-based reconstruction results and experimental measurements still requires broader verification. Finally, ECS-Net introduces additional computational complexity compared with simpler segmentation models, which may limit its direct application in real-time or resource-constrained scenarios. Future work will focus on expanding the dataset, improving the recognition of extremely small and low-contrast pores, and further validating the method under different concrete materials and imaging conditions.

Method details

Methodology

The proposed ECS-Net in this paper mainly consists of three core modules. The design rationale and implementation details of RLGConv, MPFIOM, and CAAO are elaborated in the following subsections, with their detailed architectures illustrated in Figure 2.

RLGConv design

The small scale and nonuniform distribution of pore areas in concrete CT images pose a significant challenge, necessitating a model with enhanced local detail perception to accurately extract micro-pore features. In standard convolution, each input channel interacts with all convolutional kernels, which can introduce inter-channel interference. Therefore, the RLGConv module, illustrated in Figure 2A, is proposed to improve the network’s efficiency and accuracy in extracting features of micro-pores and irregularly shaped continuous pores. This module comprises two primary components: a residual-like structure and SA block. In the residual-like architecture, the left branch employs a specialized convolutional layer to enhance semantic representation in the channel dimension, whereas the right branch incorporates a downsampling strategy to improve spatial localization.

Specifically, the left branch is constructed using a “1 × 1 convolution for dimensionality reduction followed by a 3 × 3 grouped convolution for feature processing” scheme. The grouped convolution mitigates interference from irrelevant channels, thereby enhancing the network’s focus on local patterns. Each convolution is followed by batch normalization and a rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation function to enhance nonlinear representational capacity. The processed multigroup feature maps are then concatenated and passed through a 1 × 1 convolution to increase dimensionality, thereby preserving effective information. This design enables the feature maps from the branch to undergo comprehensive multiscale processing, significantly enhancing their semantic representation strength. As shown in the right branch of Figure 2A, the input features first undergo channel adjustment via a 1 × 1 convolution to achieve spatial dimension matching before being added to the output of the main branch.

Furthermore, to enable the model to capture spatial information within the CT images, the feature maps are processed by the SA block, as shown in Equation 8:(Equation 10)Ms(F)=σ(f3×3([AvgPool(F);MaxPool(F)]))=σ(f3×3[Favgs;Fmaxs])where Ms(F)∈RH,W represents the weight matrix of the spatial information; Favgs∈R1×H×W and Fmaxs∈R1×H×W represent the average- and max-pooled feature maps, respectively; σ is the Sigmoid activation function; and f3 × 3 represents a 3 × 3 convolution.

The SA block fuses features by integrating both pooling operations to generate a spatial response. This response is then passed through a 3 × 3 convolution and Sigmoid function to produce a spatial attention weight matrix, Ms(F). This weight matrix is subsequently applied to the original feature maps, guiding the network to focus on salient spatial regions and enhancing its sensitivity to small and irregularly shaped pores.

The local feature extraction branch in the encoder (Figure 1) employs multiple stacked RLGConv modules to hierarchically process the input feature maps, significantly enhancing the network’s ability to capture fine-grained local details.

Principle of MPFIOM

Pore structures in concrete CT images typically exhibit complex morphological characteristics. Features such as multiscale distribution require the network to have a strong capability for capturing global contextual relationships, whereas attributes such as irregular shapes and low-contrast boundaries demand robust local detail-processing. However, the encoder structures of most existing networks struggle to accommodate both requirements simultaneously. Therefore, our encoder incorporates a three-branch design: RLGConv, Swin Transformer, and global-local feature fusion (GLFF). The GLFF branch integrates global and local features to enhance the characterization of complex pores, with its core mechanism being the proposed MPFIOM, as shown in Figure 2B.

This framework comprises two main stages: information processing and fusion. During the information-processing stage, MPFIOM takes three inputs: the local feature Fl from the RLGConv branch, global feature Fg from the Swin Transformer branch, and the prior feature (fused feature Fx from the previous MPFIOM). First, MPFIOM projects both Fl and Fg to a unified intermediate dimension using separate 1 × 1 convolutional layers. If Fx from the previous layer is available, it is downsampled and concatenated with Fl and Fg. The concatenated features then undergo normalization and a nonlinear transformation to form the fused feature Ff. A SAM and CAM are also incorporated to enhance the module’s focus on salient regions. For the local feature branch, SAM uses the concatenated outputs of max and average pooling, processed through a normalized 7 × 7 convolution, to generate an SA map that guides the model to focus on critical structures within local regions. For the global feature branch, CAM applies two successive 1 × 1 convolutions to both the max- and average-pooled features to derive channel weights via a Sigmoid function.

During the information-fusion stage, the SAM-enhanced local features Fl, CAM-enhanced global features Fg, and fused features Fx are concatenated along the channel dimension. The combined features are then passed through a normalization layer, final residual structure, dimension-increasing 1 × 1 convolution, and dimension-decreasing 1 × 1 convolution, ultimately yielding the fused feature representation Ff. These convolutional layers effectively enhance the model’s representational capabilities. Finally, an element-wise addition is performed between Ff and the previously fused feature Fx to produce the module’s output Fx+1, effectively integrating and nonlinearly enhancing multisource features.

By leveraging this collaborative multibranch fusion strategy, the encoder successfully incorporates both local and global features, providing high-quality representations for the subsequent decoder and enhancing extraction for regions with complex pores.

Architecture of CAAO

In concrete CT images, pores exhibit grayscale values highly similar to those of dark impurities, and irregularly shaped continuous pores display complex edge morphologies. Deformable convolution techniques, which use offset learning to improve geometric adaptability, have been applied to segment complex structures. However, the lack of semantic guidance in their deformation can still lead to mis-segmentation and boundary blurring. To address this, we propose the CAAO, an optimizer that integrates an adaptive feature-extraction mechanism by combining the semantic entropy of feature maps with the statistical characteristics of images. Based on a dynamic analysis of encoder and decoder features, it generates adaptive parameters that directly regulate its internal convolutional deformation, enabling precise modeling of complex regions and suppressing mis-segmentation. As illustrated in Figure 2C, the CAAO comprises three key components: multisource complexity evaluation, dynamic parameter generation, and a convolution operation.

In the dynamic parameter generation stage, the concatenated feature map I is integrated with these metrics to generate a spatially adaptive complexity weight map. The core workflow involves three stages: feature compression, recalibration, and scoring. Feature compression, achieved through the spatial reconstruction unit (SRU) of the squeeze-and-calibration convolution (ScConv), preserves critical features while suppressing redundant information in both spatial and channel dimensions. Recalibration, handled by the channel reconstruction unit (CRU), then enhances the semantic representation of these key features through cross-channel interaction. After this processing, the recalibrated features are compressed to a 1 × 1 resolution via adaptive average pooling and passed through a Sigmoid function to produce a scalar score s∈[0,1], which is used to directly adjust the number of kernels k in the alterable kernel convolution (AKConv) as follows:(Equation 16)k=k0+(km−k0)swhere k0 and km represent the minimum and maximum numbers of kernels in AKConv, respectively, and s represents the generated score.

In the convolution operation stage, the score s adjusts the internal parameters of AKConv. Subsequently, the decoder feature map Fdc from the preceding stage is concatenated with the encoder feature map Fe and passed through the adjusted AKConv, yielding the module output Fo. By dynamically adjusting the number of convolutional kernels based on input-feature complexity, the CAAO enhances feature representation in high-complexity regions while reducing redundant computation in low-complexity areas. This adaptive mechanism enhances segmentation accuracy and boundary integrity, leading to a more precise reconstruction of pores by the decoder.

Implementation details

Experimental settings. To ensure the fairness of comparison, all methods were evaluated on the same dataset with consistent configurations for model training and performance testing. The specific experimental settings are as follows: the input image size was set to 2000 × 2000 pixels, and the batch size was 1. In the loss function validation, the number of training epochs was 200, while 400 training epochs were adopted for all other experiments. The model was implemented based on the PyTorch deep learning framework and optimized using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1 × 10−4. During the training process, a cosine annealing scheduler was employed to adjust the learning rate. Input images were normalized to the range of [0, 1]. To guarantee the reproducibility of the experiments, a fixed random seed of 42 was used in all trials. All experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel Core i9-14900 K CPU. Moreover, the random seeds adopted for repeated experiment in comparative experiments and ablation experiments were set as seed = 42, seed = 123, and seed = 999, respectively.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Descriptive statistical analysis was performed on the IoU, DSC, and ASD values obtained from repeated experiments using different random seeds to evaluate the stability of ECS-Net, using Python 3.10 for numerical calculations.

Sample size: n = 3 independent training runs with different random seeds (42, 123, and 999).

Data representation: Results are reported as mean and standard deviation (Std) (Tables 4, 5, and 7).

ECS-Net achieved mean ASD, DSC, and IoU values of 0.2750, 0.9624, and 0.9524, respectively, with corresponding Std values of 0.0035, 0.0028, and 0.0014 across three independent runs. These results indicate that ECS-Net maintained stable segmentation performance under different random initializations.
