Clinical and radiological outcomes of unilateral biportal endoscopy and percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy for highly downward-migrated lumbar disc herniation: a retrospective study
Authors: Haifeng Liu, Siping Zhang, Kuixian Zhang, Jiangtao Zhou, Chui Qian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo compare the clinical efficacy and safety of unilateral biportal endoscopy (UBE) versus percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy (PEID) in the treatment of highly downward-migrated lumbar disc herniation (HDM-LDH).MethodA retrospective study included 61 patients with HDM-LDH, comprising 27 in the UBE group and 34 in the PEID group. All patients followed up for 12 months. Compare differences in general information between the two groups. By analysing and comparing perioperative indicators, MacNab excellent/good rate, complication, and clinical and radiographic outcomes between the two groups.ResultsThere were no significant differences between the two groups in general information (P > 0.05). The UBE group had a shorter operation time (OT), greater intraoperative blood loss (IBL) and longer postoperative hospital stay (PHS) than the PEID group (P < 0.05). There was no significant difference of MacNab and complications between the two groups (P > 0.05). The visual analogue scale (VAS) - Back at 3 days postoperative in the UBE group was higher than PEID group (P < 0.05). There were no significant differences between the two groups in VAS-Leg and Oswestry disability index scores (P>0.05). There were no significant differences between the two groups in intervertebral disc height (P>0.05). The cross-sectional area of the spinal canal (CASC) and cross-sectional area of the dural sac (CADS) in the UBE group were more significant than PEID group postoperative, and the cross-sectional area of the paraspinal muscles (CAPM) in the UBE group decreased more than PEID group postoperative (P < 0.05). The UBE group had a lower residual area of nucleus pulposus (RANP), a higher area of laminectomy (AL) and a lower preservation rate of facet joints (PRFJ) compared to the PEID group (P < 0.05).ConclusionUBE yields higher AL, lower PRFJ, larger IBL and longer PHS compared with PEID. It also leads to more severe short-term postoperative low back pain and paraspinal muscle injury. Nevertheless, UBE possesses better surgical efficiency and shorter OT. It can better restore CASC and CADS, and reduce RANP simultaneously. In clinical practice, the surgical approach for HDM-LDH should be selected according to surgeon experience and individualized treatment strategies.Keywords: Highly migrated lumbar disc herniation, Unilateral biportal endoscopy, Percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy, Minimally invasive

Introduction

Low back pain (LBP) is one of the most common clinical symptoms, with approximately 70% of adults experiencing it in their lives. Lumbar disc herniation (LDH) is the primary cause of LBP [1–3]. Although most patients achieve satisfactory outcomes with conservative treatment, for patients with LDH who have ineffective conservative treatment and are complicated with neurological dysfunction, surgy alone can definitively relieve neural compression and prevent the progression of pain and functional impairment [4]. With the continuous innovation of minimally invasive spinal techniques, percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy (PEID) has become one of the mainstream procedures for treating LDH due to its application advantages such as minimal trauma, rapid recovery, and fewer complications [5, 6]. Unilateral biportal endoscopy (UBE) was first reported in the 1990s and has gained rapid development in recent years owing to its advantages [7]. The dual-channel system enables the UBE to operate with flexibility, safety and efficiency, effectively performing spinal canal decompression and intervertebral disc discectomy. It frequently employs an interlaminar approach [8]. Highly migrated lumbar disc herniation (HM-LDH) represents the most severe type of migratory LDH. The degenerated nucleus pulposus frequently protrudes posteriorly into the spinal canal, migrating either superiorly or inferiorly, often with lateral deviation. This leads to severe LBP and radiating pain in the lower limbs, usually requiring surgery for effective symptom relief [9]. PEID was previously primarily employed for patients presenting with difficulties in puncture and limited manoeuvrability via the intervertebral foramen approach. However, with the evolution of spinal endoscopy techniques, the application advantages of PEID have continually expanded, leading to its gradual adoption in the treatment of HM-LDH [10]. Previous studies have suggested that UBE primarily demonstrates significant advantages in the treatment of lumbar spinal stenosis. However, with improvements in endoscopic instruments, UBE has also achieved favourable outcomes in the treatment of HM-LDH [11]. In HM-LDH, highly downward-migrated lumbar disc herniation (HDM-LDH) exhibits the highest incidence rate (56%) and presents significant surgical challenges, often necessitating laminoplasty. Concurrently, the operational difficulty and risk of nerve injury are markedly elevated [9]. Therefore, this study aims to compare the differences between UBE and PEID in treating HDM-LDH across multiple dimensions, including perioperative indicators, clinical and radiological outcomes. This will enable an objective and effective evaluation of the clinical efficacy and safety of UBE versus PEID, thereby providing rational recommendations for selecting surgical approaches for HDM-LDH in clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Sample size

A priori analysis was conducted using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.7) to determine the minimum sample size required for the independent t - test. Based on the existing raw data from the pre - experiment, the effect size d = 0.83 (calcul fromated the data mean and standard deviation), the significance level α = 0.05, the test power (1 - β) = 0.80, and a two - sided test was performed. The analysis results showed that at least 22 cases were needed in the UBE group and at least 28 cases were needed in the PEID group, with a total sample size of 50 cases, to ensure sufficient statistical power to detect the expected effect differences. To avoid sample loss or insufficient data quality in the actual study, we additionally increased the sample size by 11 cases. Finally, 61 patients with HDM - LDH were included to ensure the reliability of the research results.

Study design

A retrospective analysis of 61 patients with HDM-LDH admitted between January and December 2024 revealed 25 males and 36 females, with a mean age of 45.39 ± 8.81 years. Classification of LDH according to the six-grade system proposed by Ahn et al. [12]: A bisecting line is drawn between the inferior margin of the pedicle and the superior/inferior margin of the intervertebral disc. Nucleus pulposus displacement not reaching this line is classified as low migration; displacement exceeding this line but not reaching the inferior margin of the pedicle is classified as high migration; and displacement exceeding the inferior margin of the pedicle is classified as very-high migration (Fig. 1). In this study, LDH with high migration and very-high migration in the Ahn classification were classified as HDM-LDH.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: ① Diagnosed with LDH based on clinical symptoms, signs, and radiological outcomes; ② MRI demonstrating nucleus pulposus protrusion with downward migration; ③ Graded as high or very high migration according to the Ahn classification: the nucleus pulposus has displaced at least beyond the midline between the lower margin of the pedicle and the upper/lower margin of the intervertebral disc; ④ Single-level LDH; ⑤ Failure of conservative treatment for at least 6 weeks. Exclusion criteria: ① Coexisting lumbar spondylolisthesis or segmental lumbar instability; ② Recurrent LDH; ③ History of previous lumbar surgery; ④ LDH secondary to tumours, infections, etc.; ⑤ Coexisting other lumbar disorders such as ankylosing spondylitis or lumbar fractures; ⑥ Inability to undergo surgery due to systemic conditions (cardiovascular, respiratory, etc.) or psychiatric disorders; ⑦ Incomplete follow-up data or follow-up duration < 1 year. Patients were divided into two groups based on their surgical procedure. This study obtained approval from the ethics committee of Wenzhou Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University before commencement and adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided informed consent before enrolment.

Surgical techniques

PEID

PEID is performed under continuous epidural anaesthesia. Taking the L3/4 intervertebral disc as an example, the patient is placed in the prone position following anaesthesia. Under C-arm fluoroscopy, the L3/4 interlaminar space was identified and marked on the skin. The surgical site was then disinfected and draped. Insert the needle along the paraspinal approach adjacent to the posterior midline spinous process, then place the working channel along the needle. After confirming the working channel position is satisfactory under C-arm fluoroscopy, switch on and connect the imaging system. Under endoscopic monitoring, the inferior margin of the L3 lamina and the superior margin of the L4 lamina were resected using an visual trephine or electric grinding drill to widen the laminae interval. Under endoscopic guidance, after excising part of the ligamentum flavum with a bite-off forceps, the spinal canal is entered to expose the dural sac and ipsilateral nerve root. The degenerative nucleus pulposus compressing the dural sac and ipsilateral nerve root is removed, followed by caudal exploration and excision of any downwardly migrated nucleus pulposus. Should nerve root compression be present on the contralateral side, continue exploration and removal of the downwardly migrated nucleus pulposus on that side. Under endoscopic guidance, after re-examining the L4 nerve root to confirm no residual nucleus pulposus was present, local haemostasis was achieved and dexamethasone injection was administered to reduce nerve root oedema. Under the microscope, nerve root activity and dura mater pulsation were found to be normal, with no active haemorrhage. Withdraw the working channel, suture the incision, and cover with sterile local dressing. As shown in Fig. 2.

UBE

UBE is performed under general anaesthesia. Taking the L5/S1 intervertebral disc as an example, the patient is placed in the prone position following anaesthesia. Under C-arm fluoroscopy, after confirming the interlaminar space, markings are made approximately 1.5 cm lateral to the L5/S1 spinous process at the upper and lower margins of the lamina. After disinfection and draping, make two transverse incisions approximately 0.8 cm in length, dissecting through the skin, subcutaneous tissue, and deep fascia. Under fluoroscopic guidance, insert the positioning needle such that it is centred at the margins of the superior and inferior laminae. Using graduated cannulas, dilate two channels to establish an observation channel at the inferior margin of the superior lamina and a working channel at the superior margin of the inferior lamina. Open and connect the imaging system. Employ an electrocautery device to clear the muscular tissue from the laminae surface, thereby exposing the superior and inferior laminae. Using rongeurs, closely follow the edges of the upper and lower vertebral laminae to remove bone material from the lower margin of the L5 lamina and the upper margin of the S1 lamina. After locating the ligamentum flavum, it was dissected using a curette. Under direct visualisation, the ligament was excised with a bite-off forceps, exposing the underlying dura mater. Using a neuropeeler, retract the dura mater and nerve roots medially to facilitate removal of the downwardly migrated nucleus pulposus using a nucleus pulposus forceps. Expose the shoulder and axillary parts of the nerve root, explore caudally for the migrated nucleus pulposus tissue downward and remove it. Should nerve root compression be present on the contralateral side, continue exploration and remove the herniated nucleus pulposus that has migrated to that side. Upon re-examination, no significant compression or residual nucleus pulposus was identified around the dura mater and S1 nerve root. Following thorough local haemostasis, dexamethasone injection was administered to alleviate nerve root oedema. Endoscopic examination revealed good nerve root mobility and dural pulsation, with no active haemorrhage. Exit the working channel, leaving the drainage tube in place at the caudal incision for drainage and securing it. After suturing the cranial incision, apply sterile dressing. As shown in Fig. 3.

Perioperative management

All patients receive preoperative education, during which they are informed of the purpose of the procedure, the surgical plan, and relevant precautions, and their informed consent is obtained. Administer intravenous antibiotics 30 min preoperatively and within 24 h postoperatively to prevent infection. administer 10 mg of sodium hydrogencitrate dexamethasone solution intravenously to reduce nerve root oedema and alleviate symptoms; at the same time, prescribe oral non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs to relieve pain. The patient commenced ankle pump exercises within 6 h postoperatively, combined with intermittent pneumatic compression therapy, to promote deep venous circulation in the lower limbs and prevent the development of deep vein thrombosis. Within 24 h postoperatively, guide the patient to walk with the lumbar brace in place, gradually increasing the range of activity. When the drainage volume is less than 30 ml/day, the drainage tube is removed in the UBE group; in the PEID group, no drainage tube is placed during the procedure. A follow-up lumbar CT scan and MRI should be performed within 1 week postoperatively to assess the outcome of intervertebral disc discectomy and spinal canal decompression.

Observation indicators

Collect general data on two groups of patients, including gender, age, body mass index (BMI), surgical segment, surgical approach, Michigan State University (MSU) grading, degree of migration, and area of herniated nucleus pulposus (AHNP). The perioperative indicators between the two groups were compared in terms of operation time (OT), intraperioperative blood loss (IBL), intraoperative fluoroscopy times (IFT), and postoperative hospital stay (PHS). comparing the excellent/good rates of MacNab and the differences in complications. Comparing the differences in clinical efficacy between the two groups using visual analogue scale (VAS) and Oswestry disability index (ODI) scores. All radiological outcomes were independently measured and obtained by two radiologists, and the average of the two was taken as the final outcome. The differences between the two groups’ radiological outcomes were compared using intervertebral disc height (IDH), cross-sectional area of the spinal canal (CASC), cross-sectional area of the dural sac (CADS), cross-sectional area of the paraspinal muscles (CAPM), residual area of nucleus pulposus (RANP), area of laminectomy (AL), and preservation rate of facet joints (PRFJ). IDH= (anterior height of the intervertebral disc + posterior height of the intervertebral disc) /2 [13]. The CASC, CADS, CAPM, and the AHNP were all measured on the same plane of the transverse lumbar MRI (T2-weighted image) [13]. AL (coronal CT) = postoperative lamina gap area - peoperative lamina gap area [14]. PRFJ (cross-sectional CT) = postoperative articular process joint diameter / preoperative articular process joint diameter × 100% [15].

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0. Normally distributed quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (‾x ± s). Comparisons between two groups were conducted using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed quantitative data are represented by the median and interquartile range M (P25, P75). Comparisons between two groups were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Count data are expressed as percentages (%). Comparisons between groups were performed using the chi-square (χ²) test. To eliminate the confounding effect of AL on postoperative CASC and CADS, analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was adopted to adjust for AL and compare intergroup differences in these two indicators between the UBE and PEID groups. All adjusted outcomes were expressed as adjusted mean ± standard error. P < 0.05 indicates a significant difference.

Discussion

PEID was introduced by Ruetten et al. [16] in 2006, demonstrating superior clinical efficacy and safety compared to open lumbar discectomy [17]. PEID involves a small incision, minimal soft tissue damage, and reduced blood loss, facilitating rapid postoperative recovery and shorter hospital stays [18]. Unlike PEID, UBE offers two channels, permitting the coordinated use of conventional surgical instruments alongside specialised tools. This facilitates intraoperative manoeuvring, thereby ensuring effective decompression and achieving a seamless integration of traditional open surgery with endoscopic techniques [19]. Compared to conventional open lumbar discectomy, UBE causes less damage to paraspinal muscles and soft tissues, involves reduced bone resection and blood loss, and facilitates the maintenance of spinal stability and rapid postoperative recovery. Consequently, it offers superior clinical efficacy and safety [20].

Although the clinical significance of the two groups of OTs in this study was not significant, the statistical differences between them were still significantly correlated with the differences in the surgical procedures themselves. Previous studies [21] have demonstrated that the OT for UBE is significantly longer than that for PEID, and this duration correlates markedly with procedural characteristics such as establishing the UBE pathway, laminectomy, and achieving adequate haemostasis. However, the duration of surgery is often significantly correlated with the type of LDH. The more complex the LDH type, the more the relative advantages of UBE versus PEID in terms of OT will shift [15]. In HDM-LDH, the downwardly migrated of the nucleus pulposus is often more severe, even reaching the lower intervertebral space. Consequently, greater lamina resection and occasionally partial facet removal may be required in selected HDM-LDH cases to ensure complete nucleus pulposus removal; the extent should be individualised rather than treated as a routine requirement. The UBE’s dual-channel system permits the use of multiple surgical instruments, enabling more efficient laminoplasty that significantly enhance surgical efficiency and reduce OT [22]. The PEID uses a coaxial endoscopic system with a relatively small working channel, offering limited choices of instruments. It may also be restricted in operation due to coaxiality, resulting in low efficiency of laminectomy and increased OT. Zuo et al. [23] found that although UBE required a longer time to establish the pathway, the decompression time was shorter than PEID. Unlike other types of LDH, HDM-LDH surgery primarily involves laminectomy, nucleus pulposus removal, and spinal canal decompression, which account for the majority of the procedure time. Consequently, UBE may significantly reduce OT due to its high efficiency. Moreover, the duration required to establish the channel has a significant correlation with the learning curve. Most studies indicate that the learning curve for PEID is steeper, with a markedly increased risk of intraoperative nerve injury. However, the learning curve for UBE aligns more closely with surgeons’ operating habits, with proficiency typically attained after approximately 15 cases, this further enhances surgical efficiency and safety while shortening OT [24]. Similar to the present study, the majority of research [25] consistently found that IBL during UBE was greater than PEID, a finding associated with numerous factors. Firstly, although both procedures employ an interlaminar approach, UBE often necessitates partial dissection of the paraspinal muscles to artificially create a working space. In contrast, PEID requires only targeted puncture to directly access the herniated intervertebral disc, without the need for excessive paraspinal muscle dissection. Secondly, owing to the use of a dual-channel system, UBE requires the creation of two incisions, whereas PEID necessitates only one incision, thereby causing less damage to the soft tissue and muscles. For HDM-LDH, partial laminectomy is frequently performed to widen the laminar window, although the extent is technique - and case - dependent. UBE typically necessitates more extensive lamina dissection to completely remove the migrated intervertebral disc, whereas PEID requires less lamina resection due to its single-channel and coaxial features [15]. Consequently, owing to the particularities of the surgical approach and HDM-LDH, IBL during UBE is often greater than that during PEID. Consistent with previous studies [21], postoperative placement of a drainage tube is often required following UBE to facilitate the removal of residual blood and exudate from the surgical site, thereby reducing the incidence of complications such as epidural haematoma. Typically, the drainage tube can only be removed once the daily drainage volume falls below 30 ml, consequently resulting in a significantly longer PHS compared to PEID. However, there was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of MacNab scores and complications, indicating comparable surgical excellence/good rates. UBE does not lead to an increased incidence of complications due to prolonged postoperative drainage and prolonged PHS, consistent with the findings of the meta-analysis by Zhang et al. [22].

The VAS score is one of the key indicators for assessing improvements in patients’ symptoms postoperative. We found that the VAS-Back score at 3 days post-operative was significantly higher in the UBE group than in the PEID group; the difference between the two groups was statistically significant, but the magnitude of the difference was small and did not appear to have clinicianical significance, which may be related to our small sample size. The statistical difference between the two groups may be related to the surgical technique. UBE usually requires the creation of two channels and necessitates more extensive soft tissue dissection than PEID, with relatively greater paraspinal muscle injury [26]. Concurrently, the area of lamina resection during UBE was greater than that during PEID, and the preservation of facet joints was markedly lower than in PEID. These factors were both significantly correlated with postoperative pain, consequently resulting in a higher VAS-Back score in the UBE group compared to the PEID group in the early postoperative period [21]. However, the disparity between the two gradually diminished during subsequent follow-up, suggesting that although early postoperative LBP was higher in the UBE group than in the PEID group, there was no significant difference between them over time. Furthermore, the differences in VAS-Leg and ODI scores at various postoperative stages were not significant between the two groups, indicating that both approaches achieve favourable clinical efficacy.

CASC, CADS and RANP are key indicators reflecting the thoroughness of nucleus pulposus removal and spinal canal decompression. In this study, we observed that the postoperative CASC and CADS in the UBE group were significantly higher than those in the PEID group, while the RANP score was significantly lower than in the PEID group. This suggests that UBE achieves more thorough nucleus pulposus removal and spinal canal decompression, consistent with findings from several recent studies [22, 27]. Through its dual-channel operating system, UBE enhances the viewing angles and manoeuvring space for both the endoscopy and surgical instruments. This provides a broader surgical field of view and greater operational flexibility, reducing blind spots to achieve more comprehensive decompression. Consequently, it significantly lowers the risk of RANP while markedly improving CASC and CADS [28]. PEID employs a single-channel operating system, resulting in a relatively constrained operating space with certain blind spots. Constraints imposed by bony structures lead to significantly higher RANP compared to UBE, and yield less improvements in CASC and CADS than UBE. This may also increase the recurrence rate of LDH in long-term outcomes [21]. He et al. [29] observed that the recurrence rate of UBE was lower than that of PEID, which may be significantly correlated with higher CASC and CADS and lower RANP in UBE. The wider operative field associated with the conventional UBE technique used here facilitated thorough nucleus pulposus removal; however, this should not be interpreted as a general requirement of UBE itself. This approach may offer higher efficacy and lower risk of nerve injury, particularly in cases of HDM-LDH [15]. The paraspinal muscles constitute a vital component in achieving spinal stability, movement, and support. It has a significant correlation with the spinal degenerative diseases, making them a key research focus for delaying spinal degeneration and promoting recovery [30]. In the early postoperative period, a lower CAPM value indicates more severe paraspinal muscle injury and the more notable LBP becomes [31]. The risk of paraspinal muscle atrophy and fatty degeneration following lumbar surgery is significantly elevated, with MRI serving as a crucial indicator for assessing paraspinal muscle injury [32]. Similar to the findings of Wang et al. [33], we also observed that UBE resulted in significantly greater paraspinal muscle damage than PEID. UBE achieves a seamless integration of open and minimally invasive techniques. However, precisely because of this, UBE necessitates the creation of two channels and involves more extensive dissection of the paraspinal muscles, leading to greater damage to these muscles and surrounding soft tissues. This trade-off is a consequence of the conventional UBE technique used here; modified UBE approaches that minimise soft-tissue and bony disruption are under active development [34]. In some HDM-LDH cases, partial resection of the lamina - and rarely the facet joints - may be required to access and remove the downwardly migrated nucleus pulposus; this should be regarded as a case - specific consideration rather than a procedural necessity. Although PEID involves a smaller AL and higher PRFJ, it also exhibits higher rates of RANP and less improvements in CASC and CADS compared to UBE. This is significantly correlated with insufficient AL, limited operative space due to single-channel instrumentation, and the formation of blind spots in the surgical field [35]. In this series, higher AL and lower PRFJ accompanied a more complete decompression; this association reflects the technique used and should not be interpreted as a causal requirement - bone preservation and adequate decompression are not mutually exclusive goals. Although AL during UBE is higher and PRFJ is lower than those during PEID, potentially increasing the incidence of lumbar instability, relevant studies suggest that lumbar instability only significantly increases when both the lamina and facet joints are removed simultaneously. Preserving the isthmus can prevent the occurrence of lumbar instability [8, 36]. Some studies have suggested that a PRFJ of less than 75% is associated with iatrogenic instability. Given that the PRFJ for UBE in this study was 66.8%, this may be related to our measurement method and the small sample size. In measuring the PRFJ, we selected the CT scan of the lumbar cross-section with the largest area of bone resection; this reflected only the extent of resection at the largest plane and did not measure the total volume of bone resection, which may have led to an underestimation of the PRFJ. However, the decline in PRFJ remains a matter of concern; although we conducted a 12-month postoperative follow-up, it is still unclear whether delayed instability or degeneration of adjacent segments will occur. It is therefore crucial to identify a method that minimises the resection of the lamina or facet joints. As research has progressed, Lee et al. by altering the surgical approach, utilising novel curved surgical instruments, or employing the ligamentum flavum turndown technique, have avoided the resection of bony structures or minimised the extent of bone resection to the greatest possible extent. This has significantly reduced damage to the posterior stabilising structures of the spine, minimised the incidence of iatrogenic spinal instability, and preserved normal spinal biomechanical properties [37, 38]. Although the modified procedure described by Lee et al. is currently only used for lumbar spinal stenosis and for both foraminal and extraforaminal LDH, applying this concept to future research on HDM-LDH is of great significance. The aim is to reduce the extent of laminectomy or facet joint resection through modifications to the surgical technique, approach or instruments, thereby preserving the normal physiological structure and biomechanical stability of the spine.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, this is a single-centre retrospective analysis conducted without blinding, which has led to a degree of selection bias and confounding factors, thereby limiting the generalisability of the results to other centres employing different surgical procedures or with surgeons of varying levels of experience. Secondly, the small sample size included in this study reduces statistical power and increases the risk of bias, which may have imposed certain limitations on the study results—particularly regarding the OT and VAS-Back scores at three days post-operation—resulting in findings that, whilst statistically significant, lack clinical significance. Thirdly, in our measurements of AL and PRFJ, we typically select the cross-sectional CT slice showing the greatest extent of bone resection, rather than measuring the total volume of bone resection; this results in measurement bias, which may in turn undermine the reliability of the study findings. Finally, the follow-up period remains relatively brief, preventing a thorough comparison of differences between the two procedures in terms of long-term recurrence rates and changes in lumbar spinal stability. Therefore, although this study has yielded preliminary conclusions regarding HDM-LDH therapy, multicentre, large-sample prospective studies or clinical trials with extended follow-up periods remain necessary to validate the reliability of the present findings.

Conclusion

Both UBE and PEID are effective procedures for treating HDM-LDH. Compared to PEID in this series, conventional UBE is associated with higher AL and a lower PRFJ, and greater IBL, longer PHS, and more significant short-term LBP postoperative and paraspinal muscle injury, the conventional UBE technique achieved efficient decompression with favourable CASC and CADS restoration, though at the cost of greater bone resection; whether equivalent decompression can be achieved with less bone loss remains to be established. Therefore, the choice of the HDM-LDH procedure should still be based on the surgeon’s experience and in accordance with an individualised treatment strategy.
