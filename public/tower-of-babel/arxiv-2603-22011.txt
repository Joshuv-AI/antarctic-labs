Asymptotically Ideal Hierarchical Secret Sharing Based on CRT for Integer Ring
Jian Ding

Abstract




Abstract
: In Shamir’s secret sharing scheme, all participants possess equal privileges. However, in many practical scenarios, it is often necessary to assign different levels of authority to different participants. To address this requirement, Hierarchical Secret Sharing (HSS) schemes were developed, which partitioned all participants into multiple subsets and assigned a distinct privilege level to each. Existing Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT)-based HSS schemes benefit from flexible share sizes, but either exhibit security flaws or have an information rate less than
\frac{1}{2}
. In this work, we propose a disjunctive HSS scheme and a conjunctive HSS scheme by using the CRT for integer ring and one-way functions. Both schemes are asymptotically ideal and are proven to be secure.

Keywords
: disjunctive hierarchical secret sharing, conjunctive hierarchical secret sharing, asymptotically ideal secret sharing, Chinese Remainder Theorem

1
Introduction

Secret Sharing (SS)
[
1
,
2
]
is a method for sharing a secret among a group of participants. It typically consists of two phases: the share generation phase and the secret reconstruction phase. In the share generation phase, a dealer divides the secret into multiple shares and securely distributes each share to its designated participant. In the secret reconstruction phase, any authorized subset can reconstruct the secret by gathering their shares together, while any unauthorized subset cannot. The collection of all authorized subsets is known as the
access structure
. An SS scheme is termed
perfect
if any given unauthorized subset gains no information about the secret. An SS scheme is said to be
ideal
if it is perfect and has an information rate 1. It is referred to as
asymptotically ideal
if it is asymptotically perfect and its information rate approaches 1 as the secret size increases.

The
(t,n)
-threshold scheme is an SS scheme such that any subset of at least
t
participants is authorized, while any subset with
(t-1)
or fewer participants is unauthorized. In a
(t,n)
-threshold scheme, all participants possess identical privileges, but in many real-world scenarios, it is often necessary to assign differing privilege levels to different participants. To address this need, Simmons
[
3
]
introduced the hierarchical threshold access structure in 1988, which partitions the participants into multiple disjoint subsets and assigns a distinct privilege level to each. More precisely, let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of all participants, which are divided into
m
disjoint subsets
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{m}
. Denote by
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{m}
be a sequence of thresholds such that
1\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{m}
. By using geometric methods, Simmons
[
3
]
constructed an SS scheme with access structure

\Gamma_{1}=\{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}:\exists\ell\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}~such~that~|\mathcal{A}\cap(\bigcup_{w=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{w})|\geq t_{\ell}\},

which is called a Disjunctive Hierarchical Secret Sharing (DHSS) scheme. However, this scheme is not ideal and requires the dealer to verify the non-singularity of many matrices. Tassa
[
4
]
introduced the Conjunctive Hierarchical Secret Sharing (CHSS) scheme, which is an SS scheme with access structure

\Gamma_{2}=\{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}:|\mathcal{A}\cap(\bigcup_{w=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{w})|\geq t_{\ell}~\mathrm{for~all}~\ell\in[m]\}.

Moreover, an ideal CHSS scheme and an ideal DHSS scheme were proposed by using Birkhoff interpolation, and the dealers were required to check many matrices. Chen et al.
[
5
]
constructed ideal CHSS and DHSS schemes based on polymatroids, and the dealers were required exponential time to find a specific parameters via special algorithms.

In order to reduce the dealer’s computational complexity from exponential time to polynomial time, Yuan et al.
[
6
]
constructed a CHSS scheme by using linear homogeneous recurrence relations and one-way functions, which is not ideal due to the use of one-way functions, and many values were published. The Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) provides the flexibility to assign shares of varying sizes to different participants. Harn et al.
[
7
]
constructed a DHSS scheme based on the CRT for integer ring, which was insecure pointed by Ersoy et al.
[
8
]
. Furthermore, Ersoy et al.
[
8
]
proposed a new DHSS scheme and a new CHSS scheme by using the CRT for integer ring and hash functions. Their information rates were smaller than
\frac{1}{2}
. Tiplea et al.
[
9
]
proposed an asymptotically ideal DHSS scheme and an asymptotically ideal CHSS scheme by using the CRT for integer ring, achieving information rates approaching 1. Recently, Yang et al.
[
10
]
constructed an ideal DHSS scheme by using the CRT for polynomial rings. However, all these CRT-based ideal or asymptotically ideal DHSS schemes and CHSS schemes
[
9
,
10
]
have been shown to be insecure in our other work.

2.1
Relevant mathematical tools

Lemma 1

(CRT for integer ring,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}
be positive integers that are pairwise coprime. Given any integers
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}
and a system of congruences

\left\{\begin{aligned} x&\equiv x_{1}\pmod{m_{1}},\\
x&\equiv x_{2}\pmod{m_{2}},\\
&~\vdots\\
x&\equiv x_{n}\pmod{m_{n}},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

it holds that

x\equiv\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}M_{i}x_{i}\pmod{M},

where
M=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
,
M_{i}=\frac{M}{m_{i}}
, and
\lambda_{i}\equiv M_{i}^{-1}\pmod{m_{i}}
.
If the integer
x
satisfies
0\leq x<M
, the solution is unique, denoted by

x=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}M_{i}x_{i}\pmod{M}.

Definition 1

(
k
-compact sequences of co-primes
[
9
]
)
.

We assume
L=\{m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}
is sequences of co-primes, i.e
\gcd(m_{i},m_{j})=1,0\leq i<j\leq n,i\neq j
. The sequence
L
is called
(k,\theta)
-compact if it satisfies

(i)

m_{0}<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{n}
.

(ii)

km_{0}<m_{i}<km_{0}+m_{0}^{\theta}
, where
k\geq 1
,
\theta\in(0,1)
are real numbers,
i\in[n]
.

L
is classified as
k
-compact if it fulfills the
(k,\theta)
-compactness criterion for some
\theta\in(0,1)
.

Remark 1
.

A sequences of co-primes
L=\{m_{0},m_{1}^{1},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}^{1},\ldots,m_{1}^{m},\ldots,m_{n_{m}}^{m}\}
is
1
-compact if and only if all sub-sequences
L_{\ell}=\{m_{0},m_{1}^{1},\ldots,m_{n_{1}}^{1},\ldots,m_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,m_{n_{\ell}}^{\ell}\}
is
1
-compact, where
\ell\in[m]

Remark 2
.

k
-compact sequences of co-primes can be efficiently generated; see Section
5
of scheme
[
9
]
for the specific algorithm.

2.2
Secret sharing

Definition 2

(Secret sharing scheme)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{n}\}
be a set of
n
participants. A secret sharing scheme consists of a share generation phase and a secret reconstruction phase, as follows.

•

Share Generation Phase: We assume
\mathcal{S}
is the secret space. For any secret
s\in\mathcal{S}
, the dealer distribute shares to participants using a share distribution algorithm

\mathsf{SHARE}\colon\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{R}\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}\times\dotsb\times\mathcal{S}_{n},

where
\mathcal{R}
is a set of random symbol set, and
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is the share space of the participant
P_{i}
.

•

Secret Reconstruction Phase. Participants in any authorized set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
can use their shares to reconstruct the secret through the secret reconstruction algorithm

\mathsf{RECON}\colon\prod_{P_{i}\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{S}_{i}\mapsto\mathcal{S}.

Conversely, any unauthorized set cannot reconstruct the secret.

Denote by
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
[N_{1},N_{2}]=\{N_{1},N_{1}+1,\ldots,N_{2}\}
, where
n,N_{1},N_{2}
be positive integers such that
N_{1}<N_{2}
. In this work, the index
i
conventionally denotes the
i
-th participant
P_{i}
. Consequently, the set
[n]
represents the complete participant set
\mathcal{P}
. A secret sharing scheme is perfect security if any unauthorized subset gains no information about the secret.

Definition 3

(Information rate,
[
12
]
)
.

The information rate quantifies the efficiency of a secret sharing scheme, defined as:

\rho=\frac{\log_{2}|\mathcal{S}|}{\max_{i\in[n]}{\log_{2}|\mathcal{S}_{i}|}},

where
|\mathcal{S}|
is the number of elements in the secret space, and
|\mathcal{S}_{i}|
is the number of elements in the share space of the
i
-th participant.

The information rate
\rho
of any perfect secret sharing scheme is bounded above by
1
(
\rho\leq 1
). If a perfect secret sharing scheme has information rate one, it is said to be ideal.

We now introduce essential Shannon entropy. For discrete random variables
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}
,
\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{X})
signifies the Shannon entropy of
\mathbf{X}
. The conditional entropy of
\mathbf{X}
given
\mathbf{Y}
is denoted
\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{X}|\mathbf{Y})
.

Definition 4

(Asymptotically ideal hierarchical secret sharing scheme,
[
13
]
)
.

For a hierarchical secret sharing (HSS) scheme with the secret space
\mathcal{S}
and share spaces
\mathcal{S}_{i},i\in[n]
, it is said to be asymptotically ideal if the following asymptotic perfectness and asymptotic maximum information rate are satisfied.

•

Asymptotic perfectness. For all
\epsilon_{1}>0
, there is a positive integer
\sigma_{1}
such that for all
\mathcal{B}\notin\Gamma
and
|\mathcal{S}|>\sigma_{1}
, the loss entropy

\Delta(|\mathcal{S}|)=\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{S})-\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{S}|\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{B}})\leq\epsilon_{1},

where
\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{S})\neq 0
, and
\mathbf{S},\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{B}}
are random variables corresponding to the secret and the knowledge of
\mathcal{B}
, respectively.

•

Asymptotic maximum information rate. For all
\epsilon_{2}>0
, there is a positive integer
\sigma_{2}
such that for all
\mathcal{B}\notin\Gamma
and
|\mathcal{S}|>\sigma_{2}
, it holds that

\frac{\max_{i\in[n]}{\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{S}_{i})}}{\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{S})}\leq 1+\epsilon_{2}.

The
(t,n)
-threshold secret sharing scheme
[
15
]
by Asmuth and Bloom, based on the Chinese Remainder Theorem, ranks among the most widely implemented schemes.

Definition 5

(Asmuth-Bloom’s secret sharing scheme,
[
15
]
)
.

Let
m_{0}
be a prime. For a secret
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{0}}
. Asmuth-Bloom’s secret sharing scheme is a
(t,n)
-threshold scheme with the following two phases.

•

Share Generation Phase: The dealer chooses an increasing sequence of pairwise co-prime integers
m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{n}
satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

\deg(m_{0},m_{i})=1,i\in[n]

(ii)

m_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}m_{i}<\prod_{i=1}^{t}m_{i}
(The Asmuth-Bloom constraint)

The dealer then chooses a integer
\alpha
such that
0<y=s+\alpha m_{0}<\prod_{i=1}^{t}m_{i}
.
The dealer computes
s_{i}=y\pmod{m_{i}},i\in[n]
, and distributes each share
s_{i}
to the
i
-th participant
P_{i}
.

•

Secret Reconstruction Phase: Given any
\mathcal{A}\subseteq[n]
of cardinality
|\mathcal{A}|\geq t
, participants from
\mathcal{A}
reconstruct the secret
s
with their shares
s_{i}=y\pmod{m_{i}},i\in\mathcal{A}
. Since
y<\prod_{i=1}^{t}m_{i}<\prod_{i\in\mathcal{A}}m_{i}
, based on Lemma
1
, they can compute
y=s+\alpha m_{0}
and
s=y\mod m_{0}

The Asmuth-Bloom constraint ensures that any set of fewer than
t
participants cannot recover the secret. However, this scheme is only asymptotically perfect (as detailed in
[
13
]
), due to its reliance on the Chinese Remainder Theorem. Furthermore, its information rate is strictly less than
1
. To address this limitation, we introduce
k
-compact sequences of co-primes.

Remark 3
.

When
m_{0}
is sufficiently large, the
k
-compact co-prime sequence
L
satisfies the Asmuth-Bloom constraint. Let
L=\{m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}
be a
k
-compact sequences of co-primes. First, we known
(km_{0})^{t}>m_{0}(km_{0}+m_{0}^{\theta})^{t-1}
for
m_{0}
is sufficiently large. Second, for
i\in[n]
,
km_{0}<m_{i}<km_{0}+m_{0}^{\theta}
. Finally,
m_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}m_{i}<m_{0}(km_{0}+m_{0}^{\theta})^{t-1}<(km_{0})^{t}<\prod_{i=1}^{t}m_{i}
. Therefore, the sequence
L
satisfies the Asmuth-Bloom constraint.

Definition 6

(Disjunctive hierarchical secret sharing scheme,
[
3
]
)
.

The set
\mathcal{P}
can be divided into
m
disjoint subsets
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{m}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{P}=\cup_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathcal{P}_{\ell},~and~\mathcal{P}_{\ell_{1}}\cap\mathcal{P}_{\ell_{2}}=\varnothing~for~any~1\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}\leq m.

A Disjunctive Hierarchical Secret Sharing (DHSS) scheme associated with a strictly increasing threshold sequence
1\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{m}\leq n
satisfies the following properties:

•

Correctness. Any authorized set
\mathcal{A}\in\Gamma
can reconstruct the secret,
\Gamma
is the access structure defined as:

\Gamma=\{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}:\exists\ell\in[m]~such~that~|\mathcal{A}\cap(\bigcup_{w=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{w})|\geq t_{\ell}\}.

•

Privacy. Any given
\mathcal{B}\notin\Gamma
does not get any information about the secret.

A DHSS scheme attains ideal status if and only if it is perfect and information rate achieves one(
\rho=1
).

Definition 7

(Conjunctive hierarchical secret sharing scheme,
[
4
]
)
.

The setting is the same as that of the DHSS Definition
6
. Only the access structure
\Gamma
is different, as defined below:

\Gamma=\{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}:|\mathcal{A}\cap(\bigcup_{w=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{w})|\geq t_{\ell}~for~\forall\ell\in[m]\}.

3
A novel asymptotically ideal DHSS scheme

In this section, we construct a hierarchical secret sharing scheme for disjunctive access structures. The construction is based on the Chinese Remainder Theorem over the ring of integers and one-way functions. Our scheme is given in subsection
3.1
, and we analyze its security in subsection
3.2
.

3.1
Our scheme 1

Consider a set
\mathcal{P}
of
n
participants partitioned into
m
disjoint subsets
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2},\ldots,

\mathcal{P}_{m}
. Let
n_{\ell}=|\mathcal{P}_{\ell}|
and
N_{\ell}=\sum_{w=1}^{\ell}n_{w}
for
\ell\in[m]
. For
\ell\in[m]
,
t_{\ell}
define as the threshold for the subsets
\bigcup_{w=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{w}
. The threshold sequence
satisfies
1\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{m}
and
t_{\ell}\leq N_{\ell}
. Our scheme consists of two phases: the share generation phase and the secret reconstruction phase.

Share Generation Phase
.

•

The dealer first generates a publicly co-prime sequence,
L=\{m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}
. The dealer assign each
m_{i}
to a participant
i
,
i\in[n]
. Let
L_{\ell}=\{m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{N_{\ell}}\}
,
\ell\in[m]
denotes the sub-sequence of
L
such that the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

m_{0}
is a big prime and
\gcd(m_{i},m_{j})=1
,
0\leq i<j\leq n
.

(ii)

m_{0}<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{N_{\ell}}
,
\ell\in[m]
.

(iii)

km_{0}<m_{i}<km_{0}+m_{0}^{\theta}
, where
k\geq 1
,
\theta\in(0,1)
are real numbers,
i\in[N_{\ell}]
and
\ell\in[m]
.

•

For any given secret
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{0}}
, the dealer selects random integers
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{i}}
,
i\in[N_{m-1}]
.

The dealer selects integers
\alpha_{\ell}
,
\ell\in[m]
such that

0\leq y_{\ell}=s+\alpha_{\ell}m_{0}<m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{t_{\ell}}

The dealer sends each share
s_{i}
to the
i
-th participant, where

s_{i}=\begin{cases}c_{i},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mathrm{if}~i\in[N_{m-1}],\\
y_{m}\pmod{m_{i}},\mathrm{if}~i\in[N_{m-1}+1,N_{m}].\\
\end{cases}

•

The dealer selects
m
publicly known distinct one-way functions
h_{1}(\cdot),h_{2}(\cdot),\ldots,h_{m}(\cdot)
. These functions
h_{m}(\cdot):Z_{m_{i}}\times Z_{m}\longrightarrow Z_{m_{i}}
for
i\in[n],\ell\in[m]
. For any
i\in[N_{m-1}]
, there is an integer
\ell_{1}\in[m-1]
such that
i\in\mathcal{P}_{\ell_{1}}
. For all
\ell\in[\ell_{1},m]
, the dealer computes and publishes

w_{i}^{(\ell)}=(y_{\ell}-h_{\ell}(s_{i},\ell))\pmod{m_{i}}.

Secret Reconstruction Phase
. For any
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
such that
\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}=\mathcal{A}\cap(\cup_{w=1}^{\ell}P_{w}),\\
|\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}|\geq t_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in[m]
, participants of
\mathcal{A}
first compute

s_{i}^{(\ell)}=\begin{cases}h_{\ell}(s_{i},\ell)+w_{i}^{(\ell)},~\mathrm{if}~\ell\in[m-1],i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\subseteq[N_{\ell}],\\
h_{m}(s_{i},m)+w_{i}^{(m)},~\mathrm{if}~\ell=m,i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\cap[N_{m-1}],\\
s_{i},~\mathrm{if}~\ell=m,i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\cap[N_{m-1}+1,N_{m}].\\
\end{cases}

Then, they based on CRT for the ring of integers compute

y_{\ell}=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}\lambda_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}M_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}s_{i}^{(\ell)}\pmod{M_{\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}},

and reconstruct the secret
s=y_{\ell}\pmod{m_{0}}
, where
M_{\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}=\prod\limits_{i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}m_{i}
,
M_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}=M_{\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}/m_{i}
,
\lambda_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}\equiv M_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}^{-1}\pmod{m_{i}}
.

3.2
Security analysis of our scheme

We will prove the correctness, asymptotic perfectness and asymptotic maximum information rate of our scheme in this subsection.

Theorem 1

(Correctness)
.

Any authorized subset

\mathcal{A}\in\{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}:\exists\ell\in[m]~such~that~|\mathcal{A}\cap(\bigcup_{w=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{w})|\geq t_{\ell}\}.

can reconstruct the secret.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{|\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}|}\}
such that
\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}=\mathcal{A}\cap(\cup_{w=1}^{\ell}P_{w}),|\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}|\geq t_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in[m]
.
We will prove that the all participants in
\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}
can reconstruct the secret.

For
i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\cap[N_{m-1}]
, each participant needs to compute the required values by using the public one-way function
h_{\ell}(\cdot)
, the public values
m_{i}
,
w_{i}^{(\ell)}=(y_{\ell}-h_{\ell}(s_{i},\ell))\pmod{m_{i}}
, and their shares
s_{i}
. For
i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\cap[N_{m-1}+1,N_{m}]
, the participant’s operation is straightforward, requiring only the provision of their share. Specifically, participants of
\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}
compute

s_{i}^{(\ell)}=\begin{cases}h_{\ell}(s_{i},\ell)+w_{i}^{(\ell)},~\mathrm{if}~\ell\in[m-1],i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\subseteq[N_{\ell}],\\
h_{m}(s_{i},m)+w_{i}^{(m)},~\mathrm{if}~\ell=m,i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\cap[N_{m-1}],\\
s_{i},~\mathrm{if}~\ell=m,i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\cap[N_{m-1}+1,N_{m}].\\
\end{cases}

And for
i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}
, get the system of congruences

y_{\ell}\equiv s_{i}^{(\ell)}\pmod{m_{i}}.

Since
y_{\ell}
satisfies

y_{\ell}<\prod\limits_{i=1}^{t_{\ell}}m_{i}\leq\prod\limits_{i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}m_{i},

where
|\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}|\geq t_{\ell}
.
Based on the CRT for integer ring in Lemma
1
, it holds a unique solution that

y_{\ell}\equiv\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}\lambda_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}M_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}s_{i}^{(\ell)}\pmod{M_{\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}},

where
M_{\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}=\prod\limits_{i\in\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}m_{i}
,
M_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}=M_{\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}/m_{i}
and
\lambda_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}\equiv M_{i,\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}}^{-1}(x)\pmod{m_{i}}
. And they can compute the secret
s=y_{\ell}\pmod{m_{0}}
.
∎

Now we will prove our scheme is
asymptotic perfectness
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{P}
be an unauthorized subset. Therefore,

\mathcal{B}\notin\{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}:\exists\ell\in[m]~such~that~|\mathcal{A}\cap(\bigcup_{w=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{w})|\geq t_{\ell}\},

such that,

|\mathcal{B}\cap(\cup_{w=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{w})|<t_{\ell}~\mathrm{for~all}~\ell\in[m].

Participants in
\mathcal{B}
attempt to reconstruct the secret using their shares, one-way functions and all available public information. In other words, for any given secret
s\in\mathcal{S}
