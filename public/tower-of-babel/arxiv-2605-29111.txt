1.
Main Results

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the collection of all
1
-bounded, completely multiplicative functions. For
f\in\mathcal{F}
we define

L_{f}(x):=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{n},\quad x\geq 1.

Given
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
and
z\geq 1
we denote by

\mathbb{D}(f,g;z):=\left(\sum_{p\leq z}\frac{1-\text{Re}(f(p)\overline{g(p)})}{p}\right)^{1/2},

the
pretentious distance
between
f
and
g
up to
z
.

In
[
2
]
, Goldmakher conjectured the following bound, relating the size of the logarithmic partial sums of
f
to its pretentious distance from the constant function
1
.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
2
]
, Conj. 2.6)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Then for any
1\leq y\leq x
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p|n\Rightarrow p\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll 1+(\log y)e^{-\mathbb{D}(f,1;y)^{2}}.

Morally, Goldmakher’s conjecture asserts that the only way for
L_{f}(x)
to be “large” (or even
unbounded
with
x
) is if
f
“pretends” to be the constant function
1
, i.e.,
\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}
does not grow too fast with
x
. This is in contrast to the situation for ordinary (unweighted) partial sums

\tilde{M}_{f}(x):=\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n),

for which it is known that
\tilde{M}_{f}(x)\gg 1
whenever
f\in\mathcal{F}
“pretends” to be
n^{it}
for
some
fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Estimates of the kind given by Conjecture
1.1
(especially in the case
y=x
) are sought after in the literature on multiplicative functions, as obtaining quantitative bounds on the size of
L_{f}(x)
is made easier if all that is required is to know how close
f
is to
1
(with respect to pretentious distance), rather than to
n^{it}
with some uniformity in a range of
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

A significant, well-known example of this arises from the work of Hall and Tenenbaum
[
8
]
and Hall
[
9
]
(see also the earlier works of Elliott
[
1
]
and Halász
[
5
]
). They proved several results concerning the best possible constant
\kappa=\kappa_{f}>0
that can be taken so that

\left|\tilde{M}_{f}(x)\right|\ll e^{-\kappa\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}}.

The example
f(n)=n^{it}
with
t\neq 0
shows that
\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\kappa_{f}=0
(as in this case,
\mathbb{D}(n^{it},1;x)^{2}\rightarrow\infty
by virtue of
\zeta(1+it)\neq 0
); however when the infimum is taken over

\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}:=\{f\in\mathcal{F}:\ f(p)\in\Omega\text{ for all primes }p\},

for certain convex sets
\Omega\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ |z|\leq 1\}
containing
0
it is possible for

\kappa_{\Omega}:=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\kappa_{f}>0.

As a well-known special case, when
\Omega=[-1,1]
, i.e., when restricting to
1
-bounded, real-valued functions, one has
\kappa_{[-1,1]}=0.32867...
(the value of
-\cos\phi_{0}
, where
\phi_{0}
is the unique root of the equation
\sin\phi_{0}-\phi_{0}\cos\phi_{0}=\pi/2
). Rather definitive results were obtained in far more generality in
[
9
]
. In many cases, however, one can also show that
\kappa_{\Omega}<1
.

In the case of logarithmic partial sums, the situation is rather different. When
f\in\mathcal{F}
is real-valued, for example, it is a simple consequence of (
4
) below and the non-negativity of the multiplicative function
1\ast f
that

L_{f}(x)\ll 1+(\log x)e^{-\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}},

thus confirming Conjecture
1.1
for the subcollection
\mathcal{F}_{[-1,1]}
(see also
[
10
, Cor. 2.7]
, where the secondary term
1
is refined to
(\log x)^{2/\pi-1}
).

When
f
is not real-valued the situation is less clear. Since
L_{n^{it}}(x)
is well-approximated by
\zeta(1+1/\log x-it)
, which is rather small compared to
\log x
when
t\neq 0
is fixed and
x\rightarrow\infty
, one does not expect the distance from
f
to
n^{it}
for
|t|\geq 1
, say, to influence the bounds on
L_{f}(x)
if this is unbounded. Indeed, as a special case of
[
11
, Thm. 1.4]
, Lamzouri and the author showed that for any
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
2\leq y\leq x
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p|n\Rightarrow p\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll 1+(\log y)e^{-\min_{|t|\leq 1}\mathbb{D}(f,n^{it};y)^{2}},

thus giving a weak variant of Conjecture
1.1
.

As far as bounds given only in terms of
\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}
, a first result in this direction was obtained by Granville and Soundararajan, as a key ingredient in their breakthrough work on improvements to the Pólya-Vinogradov inequality for odd order characters
[
4
]
. They showed that for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
we have the weaker inequality

(1)

L_{f}(x)\ll 1+(\log x)e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}}.

The improvements in
[
2
]
on the odd order character sum problem motivate the speculation of Conjecture
1.1
that
1/2
can be improved to
1
in (
1
).

In
[
3
, Prop. 1.2]
, Granville and the author showed that

(2)

L_{f}(x)\ll(\log\log x)\left(1+(\log x)e^{-\lambda\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}}\right),

where
\lambda=0.8221...
(the solution to an explicit integral equation), and that moreover
\lambda
is best possible in that for any large scale
x
there exists a function
f=f_{x}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

(3)

L_{f}(x)\asymp(\log x)\exp\left(-\lambda\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}\right).

The latter results already suggest that the estimate in Conjecture
1.1
(with exponent
1
in place of
\lambda
) is not correct. However, the estimate (
2
) is, in many cases, weaker than Goldmakher’s conjecture (for example, whenever
|L_{f}(x)|
is bounded). Also, the example furnishing the optimality of
\lambda
in (
3
) satisfies
L_{f}(x)\ll 1
, and is therefore not a counterexample to Conjecture
1.1
.

The purpose of this note is to definitively disprove Goldmakher’s conjecture.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|L_{f}(x)|}{1+(\log x)e^{-\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}}}=\infty.

Thus, Conjecture
1.1
is false.

Key to the construction of the counterexample given in Theorem
1.2
is the following finite scale version.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
C>0
. Then there is
x_{0}(C)
such that for every
x\geq x_{0}(C)
there is a function
f=f_{x}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

L_{f}(x)\geq C\left(1+(\log x)e^{-\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}}\right).

1.1.
Proof Idea

In the sequel, for any arithmetic function
h
and
x\geq 1
we define

M_{h}(x):=\sum_{n\leq x}h(n),\quad\tilde{M}_{h}(x):=\frac{1}{x}M_{h}(x).

In light of the elementary estimate

(4)

L_{f}(x)=\tilde{M}_{1\ast f}(x)+O(1),

as well as Mertens’ theorem, we may obtain the following consequence of Goldmakher’s conjecture, in the case
y=x
; it is this statement that we shall disprove in the sequel.

Conjecture 1.4

(Consequence of Conjecture
1.1
)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}
and set
g:=1\ast f
. Then for any
x\geq 1
,

(5)

\tilde{M}_{g}(x)\ll 1+\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\text{Re}(f(p))}{p}\right).

Here and elsewhere, for
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
with
\text{Re}(s)>1
we write

L(s,f):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{f(n)}{n^{s}}.

Now, according to Perron’s formula the LHS of (
5
) depends on the sizes of the peaks of the function

\frac{L(\sigma+it,f)\zeta(\sigma+it)}{\sigma+it}x^{\sigma+it},\quad\sigma=1+1/\log x,\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

We already expect any such peaks to occur for
|t|\leq 1
, where
|\zeta(\sigma+it)|
is largest. Since

|L(\sigma,f)|\asymp\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{Re(f(p))}{p}\right)

(as in (
8
) below), Conjecture
1.4
implicitly suggests that the Perron integral will be dominated by a peak at
t=0
.
Our counterexample emerges from investigating whether other possible peaks can occur. In particular, to prove Theorem
1.3
, given a large scale
x
we select a suitable
\xi=\xi(x)\in[-1,1]
(in fact,
\xi\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
) and construct a function
f=f_{x}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

(6)

|L(\sigma+i\xi,f)\zeta(\sigma+i\xi)|>(\sigma-1)^{-c}|L(\sigma,f)\zeta(\sigma)|,

for some absolute constant
c>0
. The additional factor
(\sigma-1)^{-c}
is crucial for us in establishing
\sigma+i\xi
as a dominant peak in the Perron integral representing
\tilde{M}_{1\ast f}(x)
(or more precisely in an
L^{2}
-integral related to Bessel’s inequality for the Fourier transform of
\tilde{M}_{1\ast f}(e^{v})e^{-(\sigma-1)v}
; see Section
4
below).

We deduce Theorem
1.2
by patching together in a compatible way the counterexamples produced by the proof of Theorem
1.3
, at a suitably chosen infinite increasing sequence of scales; see Section
5
for the details.

2.
Construction of a counterexample

Let
x
be a large scale and set
\sigma:=1+1/\log x
. As in the discussion of the previous section, our goal is to choose
\xi=\xi(x)\in[-1,1]
and
f=f_{x}\in\mathcal{F}
such that an estimate of the shape (
6
) holds. As a first step we investigate sufficient conditions required to ensure the validity of the more modest inequality

(7)

|L(\sigma+i\xi,f)\zeta(\sigma+i\xi)|>|L(\sigma,f)\zeta(\sigma)|.

Let us assume that such a pair
(\xi,f)
exists, in order to determine what properties it must satisfy.

As a standard consequence of Mertens’ theorem, for any
h\in\mathcal{F}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(8)

|L(\sigma+it,h)|\asymp\exp\left(\sum_{p}\frac{\text{Re}(h(p)p^{-it})}{p^{\sigma}}\right)\asymp\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\text{Re}(h(p)p^{-it})}{p}\right).

Applying this with
h\in\{1,f\}
and
t\in\{0,\xi\}
, then taking logarithms of both sides, we obtain from (
7
) that

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\text{Re}(f(p)(p^{-i\xi}-1))}{p}>\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\text{Re}(1-p^{-i\xi})}{p}+O(1),

or equivalently, that

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\text{Re}((1-p^{-i\xi})(1+f(p)))}{p}+O(1)<0.

Using

\text{Re}(z\overline{w})=\text{Re}(z)\text{Re}(w)+\text{Im}(z)\text{Im}(w)

we may thus obtain (
7
) by ensuring that for
most
primes
p
,

(9)

\text{Im}(f(p))\sin(\xi\log p)>(1-\cos(\xi\log p))(1+\text{Re}(f(p))),

and that the weaker
non-strict
inequality holds for
1
1

1

We remark that whenever
f\in\mathcal{F}
the RHS is non-negative, and so such a strict inequality is not necessarily possible for all
p
, e.g. if
\sin(\xi\log p)=0
for some
p
.

all

p
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1/2)
to be small, and
\xi\in[-1,1]
. In the sequel, to simplify notation let us write
2

2

In the sequel we denote the set of primes by
\mathbb{P}
.

\sigma_{p}:=\sin(\xi\log p),\quad\gamma_{p}:=\cos(\xi\log p),\quad p\in\mathbb{P}.

Define the functions
R=R_{\xi,\delta}
and
I=I_{\xi,\delta}
on primes via

(10)

R(p):=\begin{cases}(1-\delta)\gamma_{p}&\text{ if }\gamma_{p}\geq 0,\\
(1+\delta)\gamma_{p}&\text{ if }0>\gamma_{p}>-\frac{1}{1+\delta},\\
\gamma_{p}&\text{ otherwise};\end{cases}\quad I(p):=\text{sign}(\sigma_{p})(1-R(p)^{2})^{1/2}

(note that
I(p)
is well-defined since it is easily seen that
|R(p)|\leq 1
for all

f=f_{\xi,\delta}

f(p):=R(p)+iI(p),\quad p\in\mathbb{P}.

Proposition 2.1
.

With the above notation, the function
f=f_{\xi,\delta}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

We have
|f(p)|=1
for all primes
p
, thus
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

(2)

For every
p
we have

(11)

\text{Re}((1-p^{-i\xi})(1+f(p)))\leq 0,

with equality if and only if

\gamma_{p}\in\{0,1\}\cup[-1,-1/(1+\delta)].

Proof.

The first item is obvious, since
|f(p)|^{2}=R(p)^{2}+I(p)^{2}=1
for all
p
.

We next prove the second item. As in (
9
), we may rewrite (
11
) as

(12)

I(p)\sigma_{p}\geq(1-\gamma_{p})(1+R(p)).

We note that the LHS is
|\sigma_{p}|(1-R(p)^{2})^{1/2}\geq 0
, and as
|\gamma_{p}|,|R(p)|\leq 1
the RHS is also patently non-negative. Therefore, it suffices to verify the equivalent inequality

(13)

I(p)^{2}\sigma_{p}^{2}\geq(1-\gamma_{p})^{2}(1+R(p))^{2}.

If
\sigma_{p}=0
then either
\gamma_{p}=1
or
\gamma_{p}=-1
. Clearly both sides are
0
in the former case, while in the latter case the same is true because then
R(p)=\gamma_{p}=-1
. Thus, (
13
) must hold whenever
\sigma_{p}=0
.
Let us assume henceforth that
\sigma_{p}\neq 0
(and thus
R(p)\neq-1
as well).
Now, we clearly have

\frac{I(p)^{2}}{(1+R(p))^{2}}=\frac{1-R(p)^{2}}{(1+R(p))^{2}}=\frac{1-R(p)}{1+R(p)},

and similarly

\frac{(1-\gamma_{p})^{2}}{\sigma_{p}^{2}}=\frac{(1-\gamma_{p})^{2}}{1-\gamma_{p}^{2}}=\frac{1-\gamma_{p}}{1+\gamma_{p}}.

Therefore, (
13
) is equivalent to

\frac{1-R(p)}{1+R(p)}\geq\frac{1-\gamma_{p}}{1+\gamma_{p}}.

or indeed, equivalently

R(p)\leq\gamma_{p}.

By construction, we have
R(p)=(1-\delta)\gamma_{p}<\gamma_{p}
for
\gamma_{p}>0
, and
R_{p}=0=\gamma_{p}
when
\gamma_{p}=0
; also,
R(p)=(1+\delta)\gamma_{p}<\gamma_{p}
when
\gamma_{p}\in(-1/(1+\delta),0)
, and
R(p)=\gamma_{p}
for
\gamma_{p}\in[-1,-1/(1+\delta)]
. Thus we have
R(p)<\gamma_{p}
for all
p
satisfying
\gamma_{p}\notin[-1,-1/(1+\delta))\cup\{0\}
, and
R(p)\leq\gamma_{p}
for all
p
.

We have thus shown that (
13
) holds for all
p
, and furthermore it holds with a
strict
inequality
unless
either
\sigma_{p}=0
(in which case
\gamma_{p}\in\{-1,1\}
) or
\gamma_{p}\in[-1,-1/(1+\delta)]\cup\{0\}
. Conversely, we have seen that equality holds with
\gamma_{p}=1
, and if
\gamma_{p}\in[-1,-1/(1+\delta)]\cup\{0\}
then
R(p)=\gamma_{p}
, and thus

I(p)^{2}\sigma_{p}^{2}=(1-\gamma_{p}^{2})\sigma_{p}^{2}=(1-\gamma_{p}^{2})^{2}=(1-\gamma_{p})^{2}(1+\gamma_{p})^{2}=(1-\gamma_{p})^{2}(1+R(p))^{2},

so equality holds in this case. This completes the proof of item (2).
∎

In this section we find sufficient conditions on

The purpose of this subsection is to prove the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1/2)
,

In particular, we have

Lemma 3.2

Let

.

Fix

.
When

The sum over

Proposition 3.4
.

There are absolute constants
c,\delta_{0}>0
such that the following holds. Let
x
be large and let
\sigma=1+1/\log x
. Let
\xi=(\log x)^{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
and let
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
. Then the function
f=f_{\xi,\delta}
constructed above satisfies

|L(\sigma+i\xi,f)\zeta(\sigma+i\xi)|\gg(\sigma-1)^{-c\delta}|L(\sigma,f)|\zeta(\sigma).

Proof.

We shall consider

\Delta_{f}(p):=\text{Re}\left((1+f(p))(p^{-i\xi}-1)\right)=I(p)\sigma_{p}-(1-\gamma_{p})(1+R(p)),\quad p\leq x.

By Mertens’ theorem, we have (as in the derivation of (
12
))

\displaystyle\left|\frac{L(\sigma+i\xi,f)\zeta(\sigma+i\xi)}{L(\sigma,f)\zeta(\sigma)}\right|\asymp\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\text{Re}(f(p)p^{-i\xi}+p^{-i\xi}-f(p)-1)}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\text{Re}((1+f(p))(1-p^{-i\xi}))}{p}\right)

(20)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\Delta_{f}(p)}{p}\right).

By Proposition
2.1
(2), we have
\Delta_{f}(p)\geq 0
for all
p
.
Thus, in the notation of the proof of Proposition
3.1
(taking
y=x
), it suffices to consider only
p\in S_{2}
.

Fix
p\in S_{2}
for the moment. Using
\Delta_{f}(p)\geq 0
, we see that

I(p)\sigma_{p}+(1+R(p))(1-\gamma_{p})=2I(p)\sigma_{p}-\Delta_{f}(p)\leq 2I(p)\sigma_{p}\leq 2.

It follows that

(21)

\displaystyle 2\Delta_{f}(p)\geq I(p)^{2}\sigma_{p}^{2}-(1+R(p))^{2}(1-\gamma_{p})^{2}.

As
p\in S_{2}
we have
R(p)=(1-\delta)\gamma_{p}
, thus

I(p)^{2}\sigma_{p}^{2}=\sigma_{p}^{2}(1-R(p)^{2})=\sigma_{p}^{2}(\sigma_{p}^{2}+2\delta\gamma_{p}^{2})+O(\delta^{2}).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle(1+R(p))^{2}(1-\gamma_{p})^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta\gamma_{p}-(1-\delta)\gamma_{p}^{2})^{2}=1+(1-\delta)^{2}\gamma_{p}^{4}+2(\delta\gamma_{p}^{3}-\delta\gamma_{p}-(1-\delta)\gamma_{p}^{2})+O(\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=(1-2\gamma_{p}^{2}+\gamma_{p}^{4})+2\delta\left(\gamma_{p}^{2}(1-\gamma_{p}^{2})-\gamma_{p}(1-\gamma_{p}^{2})\right)+O(\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{p}^{4}+2\delta\gamma_{p}^{2}\sigma_{p}^{2}-2\delta\gamma_{p}\sigma_{p}^{2}+O(\delta^{2}).

Combining these two estimates with (
21
), we deduce that for each
p\in S_{2}
,

\Delta_{f}(p)\geq\max\{0,\delta\gamma_{p}\sigma_{p}^{2}+O(\delta^{2})\}.

We may restrict further to the set

S^{\prime}:=\{e^{1/|\xi|}<p\leq x:\ \gamma_{p}\in[\tfrac{1}{10},\tfrac{\sqrt{99}}{10}]\}\subseteq S_{2},

say, on which we have
\min\{\gamma_{p},|\sigma_{p}|\}\geq\tfrac{1}{10}
. Since the set

\{u\in[-\pi,\pi]:\ \cos u\in[\tfrac{1}{10},\tfrac{\sqrt{99}}{100}]\}

is an interval whose measure
c_{0}
is a positive absolute constant (and whose indicator function is thus of variation bounded by an absolute constant), it follows from Lemma
3.2
that

\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}}\frac{\Delta_{f}(p)}{p}\geq\left(\frac{\delta}{1000}+O(\delta^{2})\right)\frac{c_{0}}{2\pi}\log(|\xi|\log x)+O(1)\geq\frac{c_{0}(1-\alpha)\delta}{7000}\left(1+O(\delta)\right)\log\log x+O(1).

Now, there must exist some
\delta_{0}
such that if
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
then the bracketed term
1+O(\delta)\geq 1/2
. Since
\alpha<1/2
by assumption, choosing
c:=c_{0}/28000
then yields

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\Delta_{f}(p)}{p}\geq\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}}\frac{\Delta_{f}(p)}{p}\geq c\delta\log\log x+O(1)=c\delta\log\left(\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\right)+O(1).

Inserting this into the RHS of (
20
) completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

In this section we shall prove Theorem

Lemma 4.1

Let

. Then for any

Proof.

Since
|1\ast f(n)|\leq d(n)

and the triangle inequality yield

as claimed.
∎

The following result is standard.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
x\geq 3
, let
\sigma=1+1/\log x
. Then for any

,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|L(\sigma+it,1\ast f)|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+t^{2}}dt\asymp\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{M}_{1\ast f}(e^{v})|^{2}e^{-2(\sigma-1)v}dv.

Proof.

For

we define the function

G(v):=\tilde{M}_{1\ast f}(e^{v})e^{-(\sigma-1)v}=M_{1\ast f}(e^{v})e^{-\sigma v}.

Clearly,
G(v)=0
for
v<0
, and by Lemma
4.1
,

|G(v)|\ll ve^{-(\sigma-1)v},

so that
G\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It is then readily verified that
G
has Fourier transform

\hat{G}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(v)e^{-itv}dv=\frac{L(\sigma+it,1\ast f)}{\sigma+it},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

The claim now follows by Plancherel’s theorem.
∎
