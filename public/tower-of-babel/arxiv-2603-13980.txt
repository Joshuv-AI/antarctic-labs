Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the decay behaviors of three imaginarity-related metrics, specifically the l 1 l_{1} -norm-based imaginarity measure, imaginarity robustness, and imaginarity relative entropy, for arbitrary single-qubit pure initial states under three typical quantum channels: dephasing, generalized amplitude damping, and phase-amplitude damping. Furthermore, we extend our analysis to higher-dimensional systems by examining the decay trends of the aforementioned imaginarity metrics for several key two-qubit states in two-qubit channels. We also generalize the concept of the maximal imaginary state (originally defined for single qubits in the resource theory of imaginarity) to separable two-qubit states. In addition, we extend the definitions of imaginary and de-imaginary powers for single-qubit channels to two-qubit channels acting on separable two-qubit states. Finally, we compute the imaginary and de-imaginary powers for several common two-qubit channels.

Introduction

Quantum information technology has rapidly developed in recent years. As a cutting-edge technology grounded in quantum mechanics, it integrates multiple disciplines and has spurred increasing research on the control and manipulation of microscopic quantum systems. The Quantum Resource Theory (QRT) provides a rigorous mathematical framework for quantifying and manipulating quantum mechanical resources — including entanglement

, superposition

In the traditional quantum mechanics framework, complex numbers play an important role in any canonical form
[

[

[

[
3

proposed the resource theory of imaginarity and introduced the concept of imaginarity measure. Focusing on two specific imaginarity measures, geometric imaginarity and robustness of imaginarity, Wu et al.
[
4
]
[

]
quantified quantum imaginarity and provided an operational interpretation for the quantification of imaginarity in state conversion problems. Quantifying and analyzing imaginarity is crucial for advancing quantum information technology, because the same quantum state often yields different quantitative results when evaluated using different methods under varying physical contexts. Xue et al.
[
6
]
further explored the quantification of quantum state imaginarity by introducing two new measures: the weight and relative entropy of imaginarity, while also investigating the decay of imaginarity when a single-qubit quantum state passes through a quantum channel. Recently, Chen et al.
[
7
]
proposed imaginarity measures based on the
l_{1}
norm and convex functions, and studied the changes in the order of single-qubit quantum states after transmission through a quantum channel.

As the imaginary part of a quantum state density matrix appears only in its offdiagonal elements, the quantum resource theory of imaginarity is closely associated with the quantum resource theory of coherence
[

]
[

[

[
12

. As transmission channels for quantum information, quantum channels play a pivotal role in fields such as quantum computing and communication. They can be employed to transmit quantum keys
[
13
]
and realize quantum teleportation
[
14
]
, among other applications. Notably, the imaginarity of a quantum state is often altered when it passes through a quantum channel. Therefore, studying the effect of quantum channels on quantum imaginarity is of great theoretical and practical significance. Exploring the role of imaginarity in quantum channels could facilitate the development of secure quantum communication systems.

In this study, we first investigate the decay trends of three imaginarity measures: the
l_{1}
-norm-based imaginarity measure, imaginarity robustness, and imaginarity relative entropy, for arbitrary single-qubit pure states under three typical quantum channels: the dephasing channel, generalized amplitude damping channel, and phase-amplitude damping channel. We then extend this analysis to higher-dimensional systems by examining the decay trends of these imaginarity measures for key two-qubit states (e.g., two-qubit entangled states and dual-rail two-qubit states) under specified two-qubit channels. Furthermore, we generalize the concept of the maximal imaginary state (originally defined for single qubits in the resource theory of imaginarity) to separable two-qubit states, thereby defining the maximal imaginary state for this class of states. By analogy with the definitions of imaginary power and de-imaginary power for single-qubit channels, we propose corresponding definitions for two-qubit channels acting on separable states, that characterize the ability of such channels to generate and destroy imaginarity in separable states. Finally, we compute the imaginary power and de-imaginary power for several classes of two-qubit channels and explore their correlations with the channel parameters.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Framework of the Resource Theory of Imaginarity

We review the fundamentals of resource theory of imaginarity with reference to Refs.
[
3
]
[
4
]
[
5
]
. Based on the basic assumptions of quantum mechanics: any isolated physical system is associated with a complex inner product vector space (i.e., a Hilbert space) called the state space of the system, and the system is completely described by a state vector, which is a unit vector in the system’s state space. Now, we consider a quantum system associated with a
d
-dimensional complex Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
, and
\{\ket{j}\}_{j=0}^{d-1}
is the orthonormal basis of
\mathscr{H}
. Let
\rho
be the density operator in Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
. The set of all density operators on Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
is denoted by
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H})
.
In the resource theory of imaginarity, free states are real states. When the density operator
\rho
is a real matrix on a given basis
\{\ket{j}\}_{j=0}^{d-1}
, we call
\rho
is the real state (free state). The set of all real states is defined as

\mathscr{R}=\{\rho\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H}):\bra{i}\rho\ket{j}\in\mathbb{R},\;i,j=0,1,\dots,d-1\}.

From the above definition, it follows that
\rho\in\mathscr{R}
if and only if
\rho
is a real symmetric matrix, that is,
\rho=\rho^{T}
.

Free operations in resource theory of imaginarity are real operations. If the Kraus operators
\{K_{m}\}
of a quantum operation
\Lambda
(i.e.,
\Lambda(\cdot)=\sum_{m}K_{m}\cdot K_{m}^{\dagger}
) satisfy
\sum_{m}K_{m}^{\dagger}K_{m}=I
and
\bra{i}K_{m}\ket{j}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
m,i,j
, then
\Lambda
is called a real operation (free operation). This definition is natural, implying that any real operation acting on a real state does not produce an imaginary state. The existence of the operator-sum representation is guaranteed by the following theorem
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Existence of Operator-Sum Representation) All quantum operations
\varepsilon
on a Hilbert space of dimension
d
can be generated by an operator-sum representation with
d^{2}
elements,

\varepsilon(\rho)\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}E_{k}\rho E_{k}^{\dagger},

where
1\leq M\leq d^{2}
.

There is a very important state in the theory of imaginarity called the maximal imaginary state:

\ket{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}+i\ket{1}),

that can be converted to any quantum state of any dimension through real operations. The state
\ket{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}-i\ket{1})
is also a maximal imaginary state
[
3
]
.

In the framework of the theory of imaginarity, an imaginarity measure
\mathscr{F}:\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H})\to[0,+\infty)
must satisfy the following conditions (1) and (2)
[
3
]
:

(1) Non-negativity:
\mathscr{F}(\rho)\geq 0
, and
\mathscr{F}(\rho)=0
if and only if
\rho\in\mathscr{R}
;

(2) Monotonicity: Let
\Lambda
be a real operation. Then
\mathscr{F}(\Lambda(\rho))\leq\mathscr{F}(\rho)
.

In addition, other properties can be discussed:

(3) Strong monotonicity:
\mathscr{F}(\rho)\geq\sum_{j}p_{j}\mathscr{F}(\rho_{j})
, where
p_{j}=\mathrm{Tr}[K_{j}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}]
,
\rho_{j}=K_{j}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}/p_{j}
, and
K_{j}
are real Kraus operators;

(4) Convexity:
\sum_{j}p_{j}\mathscr{F}(\rho_{j})\geq\mathscr{F}(\sum_{j}p_{j}\rho_{j})
;

(5) Block additivity:
\mathscr{F}(p\rho_{1}\oplus(1-p)\rho_{2})=p\mathscr{F}(\rho_{1})+(1-p)\mathscr{F}(\rho_{2})
.

Here, we introduce several well-defined imaging methods.

(I) The
l_{1}
-norm-based imaginarity measure
[
7
]
can be expressed as

\mathscr{F}_{l_{1}}(\rho)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathscr{R}}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{l_{1}}=\sum_{i\neq j}|\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{ij})|,

where
\rho
is any quantum state,
\sigma
is the real state, and
\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{ij})
is the imaginary part of the matrix element
\rho_{ij}
.

(II) In quantum resource theories, robustness is used to measure the ability of a quantum resource to maintain its properties under external disturbances, which is of great significance for quantum information processing. The robustness of imaginarity
[
4
]
is defined as:

\mathscr{F}_{R}(\rho)=\min_{\tau}\left\{s\geq 0:\frac{\rho+s\tau}{1+s}\in\mathscr{R},\tau\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H})\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\rho^{T}\|_{1},

where
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H})
denotes the set of density operators in Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
,
T
denotes the transpose, and
\|A\|_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}
is the trace norm.

It is worth noting that the robustness of all resource theories is closely related to the success rate of channel discrimination tasks.

(III) The weight-based imaginarity measure
[
16
]
is defined as

\mathscr{F}_{w}(\rho)=\min_{\sigma,\tau}\left\{s\geq 0:\rho=(1-s)\sigma+s\tau,\sigma\in\mathscr{R},\tau\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{H})\right\}.

In convex resource theory, the weight-based quantifier is closely related to tasks that involve exclusion.

(IV) Based on quantum relative entropy, we define the relative entropy of imaginarity for a quantum state
\rho
as
[
6
]
:

\mathscr{F}_{r}(\rho)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathscr{R}}S(\rho\|\sigma)=S(\mathrm{Re}(\rho))-S(\rho),

where
S(\rho\|\sigma)=-S(\rho)-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log{\sigma})=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log{\rho})-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log{\sigma})
is the quantum relative entropy,
S(\rho)=-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log{\rho})
is the von Neumann entropy, and
\mathrm{Re}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}(\rho+\rho^{T})
.

3.
Decay of Imaginarity of Quantum States After Passing Through Different Quantum Channels

Similar to quantum entanglement and coherence, the quantum imaginarity is destroyed as the quantum state evolves. In this section, we explore the changes in different imaginarity measures of a pure state after it passes through a quantum noise channel.

Here, we use the decay of an imaginarity measure to quantify the effect of a quantum noise channel. Let
\varepsilon
be a quantum channel. Imaginarity decay is defined as the difference between the initial imaginarity and the imaginarity after passing through a quantum noise channel:

(1)

\displaystyle\Delta I=\mathscr{F}(\rho)-\mathscr{F}(\varepsilon(\rho)),

where
\rho
is a pure state and
\mathscr{F}
is an imaginarity measure.

3.1.
Single-Qubit Case

For any pure state
\ket{\psi}
, there exists a real orthogonal matrix
O
such that

(2)

\displaystyle O\ket{\psi}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\left|\left\langle\psi^{\ast}|\psi\right\rangle\right|}{2}}\ket{0}+i\sqrt{\frac{1-\left|\left\langle\psi^{\ast}|\psi\right\rangle\right|}{2}}\ket{1},

which keeps the imaginarity unchanged
[
4
]
, where
\ket{\psi^{\ast}}
represents the complex conjugate of
\ket{\psi}
.

We can consider the following general form of the quantum state:

\ket{\gamma}=\sqrt{\frac{1+|\left\langle\psi^{\ast}|\psi\right\rangle|}{2}}\ket{0}+i\sqrt{\frac{1-|\left\langle\psi^{\ast}|\psi\right\rangle|}{2}}\ket{1}=\sqrt{\frac{1+A}{2}}\ket{0}+i\sqrt{\frac{1-A}{2}}\ket{1},

where
A=|\left\langle\psi^{\ast}|\psi\right\rangle|
. The density matrix corresponding to
\ket{\gamma}
is

\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1+A}{2}&-\frac{\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i\\
\frac{\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i&\frac{1-A}{2}\end{bmatrix}.

We consider the dephasing (
D
), generalized amplitude damping (
GAD
)
[
15
]
and phase-amplitude damping (
PAD
) channels given by the following Kraus operators:

\displaystyle E_{0}^{D}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{1-\frac{p}{2}}&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-\frac{p}{2}}\end{bmatrix},E_{1}^{D}=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{\frac{p}{2}}&0\\
0&-\sqrt{\frac{p}{2}}\end{bmatrix};

\displaystyle E_{0}^{GAD}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{p_{1}}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-p_{2}}\end{bmatrix},E_{1}^{GAD}=\sqrt{p_{1}}\begin{bmatrix}0&\sqrt{p_{2}}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle E_{2}^{GAD}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-p_{1}}\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{1-p_{2}}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},E_{3}^{GAD}=\sqrt{1-p_{1}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\sqrt{p_{2}}&0\end{bmatrix};

\displaystyle E_{0}^{PAD}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-p_{1}-p_{2}}\end{bmatrix},E_{1}^{PAD}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\sqrt{p_{2}}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},E_{2}^{PAD}=\sqrt{p_{1}}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}.

Under the
D
,
GAD
,
PAD
channels, the pure state
\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma}
is transformed
into, respectively,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{D}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1+A}{2}&-\frac{(1-p)\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i\\
\frac{(1-p)\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i&\frac{1-A}{2}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{GAD}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1+p_{2}(2p_{1}-1)+A(1-p_{2})}{2}&-\frac{\sqrt{(1-p_{2})(1-A^{2})}}{2}i\\
\frac{\sqrt{(1-p_{2})(1-A^{2})}}{2}i&\frac{1-p_{2}(2p_{1}-1)-A(1-p_{2})}{2}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{PAD}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1+A+p_{2}(1-A)}{2}&-\frac{\sqrt{(1-p_{1}-p_{2})(1-A^{2})}}{2}i\\
\frac{\sqrt{(1-p_{1}-p_{2})(1-A^{2})}}{2}i&\frac{(1-p_{2})(1-A)}{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Note: When
p_{1}=1
, the generalized amplitude damping channel degenerates to the standard amplitude damping channel. In the phase-amplitude damping channel,
p_{1}
is the probability parameter of phase-damping, which represents the probability of phase loss,
p_{2}
is the parameter of amplitude damping, representing the probability of decay from the excited state to the ground state. When
p_{1}=0
, the phase-amplitude damping channel degenerates into an amplitude-damping channel, and when
p_{2}=0
, it degenerates into a phase-damping channel.

Through accurate algebraic manipulations, we obtain the following expressions for the decay of
\mathscr{F}_{l_{1}}
,
\mathscr{F}_{R}
and
\mathscr{F}_{r}
in these channels:

\displaystyle\Delta I_{l_{1}}^{D}=\Delta I_{R}^{D}=p\sqrt{1-A^{2}},

\displaystyle\Delta I_{l_{1}}^{GAD}=\Delta I_{R}^{GAD}=(1-\sqrt{1-p_{2}})\sqrt{1-A^{2}},

\displaystyle\Delta I_{l_{1}}^{PAD}=\Delta I_{R}^{PAD}=(1-\sqrt{1-p_{1}-p_{2}})\sqrt{1-A^{2}};

\displaystyle\Delta I_{r}^{D}=

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{r}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})-\mathscr{F}_{r}(\varepsilon_{D}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1+\sqrt{1-(1-A^{2})(1-(p-1)^{2})}}{2}\log{[1+\sqrt{1-(1-A^{2})(1-(p-1)^{2})}]}

\displaystyle-\frac{1-\sqrt{1-(1-A^{2})(1-(p-1)^{2})}}{2}\log{[1-\sqrt{1-(1-A^{2})(1-(p-1)^{2})}]}+1,

\displaystyle\Delta I_{r}^{GAD}=

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{r}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})-\mathscr{F}_{r}(\varepsilon_{GAD}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1+A}{2}\log{(1+A)}-\frac{1-A}{2}\log{(1-A)}-\frac{1+\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\log{[1+\sqrt{\alpha}]}-\frac{1-\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\log{[1-\sqrt{\alpha}]}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-p_{2}+2p_{1}p_{2}+(1-p_{2})A}{2}\log{[1-p_{2}+2p_{1}p_{2}+(1-p_{2})A]}

\displaystyle+\frac{1+p_{2}-2p_{1}p_{2}-(1-p_{2})A}{2}\log{[1+p_{2}-2p_{1}p_{2}-(1-p_{2})A]}+1,

\displaystyle\Delta I_{r}^{PAD}=

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{r}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})-\mathscr{F}_{r}(\varepsilon_{PAD}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1+A}{2}\log{(1+A)}-\frac{1-A}{2}\log{(1-A)}+\frac{(1-p_{2})(1-A)}{2}\log{[(1-p_{2})(1-A)]}

\displaystyle-\frac{1+\sqrt{\beta}}{2}\log{[1+\sqrt{\beta}]}-\frac{1-\sqrt{\beta}}{2}\log{[1-\sqrt{\beta}]}

\displaystyle+\frac{1+p_{2}+(1-p_{2})A}{2}\log{[1+p_{2}+(1-p_{2})A]}+1,

where
\alpha
=(p_{2}{-}2p_{1}p_{2}{-}(1{-}p_{2})A)^{2}{+}(1{-}p_{2})(1{-}A^{2})
,

3.2.
Two-Qubit Case

Here we discuss the decay of imaginarity for different measures of two-qubit states after passing through a quantum channel.

3.2.1.
Decay of Imaginarity of the Pure State
\ket{\gamma}=\alpha\ket{00}+\beta\ket{11}
under a Two-Qubit Bit-Flip Channel

For the pure state
\ket{\gamma}=\alpha\ket{00}+\beta\ket{11}
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
are complex numbers satisfying
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
, the state
\ket{\gamma}
is an entangled state when both
\alpha
and
\beta
are non-zero. We can write the pure state
\ket{\gamma}=\alpha\ket{00}+\beta\ket{11}
in the following form:

\ket{\gamma}=\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{00}+e^{i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{11},

where
\theta\in[0,\pi]
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi]
. Its corresponding density matrix is

\rho=\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}1+\cos\theta&0&0&e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\\
e^{i\phi}\sin\theta&0&0&1-\cos\theta\end{smallmatrix}\end{bmatrix}.

For the pure state
\ket{\psi}=\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{0}+e^{i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{1}
, there exists an orthogonal matrix
O
such that

O\ket{\psi}=\sqrt{\frac{1+A}{2}}\ket{0}+i\sqrt{\frac{1-A}{2}}\ket{1},

where
A=|\left\langle\psi^{\ast}|\psi\right\rangle|

[
4
]
. Let the orthogonal matrix be
O=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
. Then

O\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}O^{T}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1+\cos\theta&e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta\\
e^{i\phi}\sin\theta&1-\cos\theta\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1+A}{2}&-\frac{\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i\\
\frac{\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i&\frac{1-A}{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, for the
\rho
above, there exists an orthogonal matrix
O^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}a&0&0&b\\
0&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&0\\
c&0&0&d\end{smallmatrix}\end{bmatrix}

O^{\prime}\rho O^{\prime T}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}\frac{1+A}{2}&0&0&-\frac{\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\\
\frac{\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i&0&0&\frac{1-A}{2}\end{smallmatrix}\end{bmatrix}.

Because any imaginarity measure is invariant under real orthogonal transformations, that is,
\mathscr{F}(O\rho O^{T})

=\mathscr{F}(\rho)
, we only need to consider the following quantum state:

\ket{\gamma}=\sqrt{\frac{1+A}{2}}\ket{00}+i\sqrt{\frac{1-A}{2}}\ket{11}.

The Kraus operators for the two-qubit flip channel are presented in Section 4. Therefore, the entangled state
\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma}
after passing through the two-qubit bit-flip channel is:

\varepsilon_{BF}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}\frac{1-\alpha+(p_{1}+p_{2}-1)A}{2}&0&0&\frac{(1-p_{1}-p_{2})\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i\\
0&\frac{\alpha+(p_{1}-p_{2})A}{2}&\frac{(p_{2}-p_{1})\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i&0\\
0&\frac{(p_{1}-p_{2})\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i&\frac{\alpha+(p_{2}-p_{1})A}{2}&0\\
-\frac{(1-p_{1}-p_{2})\sqrt{1-A^{2}}}{2}i&0&0&\frac{1-\alpha-(p_{1}+p_{2}-1)A}{2}\end{matrix}\end{bmatrix},

where
\alpha=p_{1}+p_{2}-2p_{1}p_{2}
.

The decay of
\mathscr{F}_{l_{1}}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})
and
\mathscr{F}_{R}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})
after passing through the two-qubit bit-flip channel is:

\Delta I_{l_{1}}=\Delta I_{R}=(1-|2p_{1}-1|-|2p_{2}-1|)\sqrt{1-A^{2}}.

And the decay for
\mathscr{F}_{r}(\ket{\gamma}\bra{\gamma})

4.
Imaginary Power and De-imaginary Power of Quantum Channels

Quantum channels can generate and destroy imaginarity. Because we have well-defined imaginarity measures to quantify the imaginarity of quantum states, we define the imaginary power and de-imaginary power of two-qubit channels with respect to separable states, based on appropriate imaginarity measures, to evaluate these two abilities of quantum channels acting on separable states.

First, we define a separable state as in

Definition 4.1
.

For a composite quantum system consisting of two subsystems
A
and
B
, with corresponding Hilbert spaces
\mathscr{H}_{A}
and
\mathscr{H}_{B}
, the Hilbert space for the entire composite system is
\mathscr{H}_{A}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{B}
. If state
\rho
in the composite system can be expressed as

\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{A}^{i}\otimes\rho_{B}^{i},

where
p_{i}\geq 0
,
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
, and
\rho_{A}^{i}
and
\rho_{B}^{i}
are the states in subsystems
A
and
B
, respectively, and
\rho
is a separable state.

Now we consider a two-qubit system. The general form of a pure state in this system is:

\ket{\varphi}=a\ket{00}+b\ket{01}+c\ket{10}+d\ket{11},

where
\{\ket{00},\ket{01},\ket{10},\ket{11}\}
are the computational basis states, and
a,b,c,d
are complex numbers satisfying
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|c|^{2}+|d|^{2}=1
.

According to the definition of the maximal imaginary state
[
3
]
, for any quantum state
\rho
, there exists a real operation
\Lambda
such that

\rho=\Lambda(\ket{+}\bra{+}),

where
\ket{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}+i\ket{1})
.

Thus, we can see that a separate state in a two-qubit system has the following form:

\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Lambda_{A}^{i}(\ket{+}\bra{+})\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i}(\ket{+}\bra{+}),

where
p_{i}\geq 0
,
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
, and
\Lambda_{A}^{i}
and
\Lambda_{B}^{i}
are the real operations. Further, the Kraus operators for the real operations
\Lambda_{A}^{i}
and
\Lambda_{B}^{i}
are
\{K_{m}^{A_{i}}\}
and
\{K_{n}^{B_{i}}\}
respectively, that is,
\Lambda_{i}^{A}(\cdot)={\textstyle\sum_{m}K_{m}^{A_{i}}\cdot(K_{m}^{A_{i}})^{\dagger}}
and
\Lambda_{i}^{B}(\cdot)={\textstyle\sum_{n}K_{n}^{B_{i}}\cdot(K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger}}
, where
{\textstyle\sum_{m}(K_{m}^{A_{i}})^{\dagger}K_{m}^{A_{i}}}=I
,
{\textstyle\sum_{n}(K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger}K_{n}^{B_{i}}}=I
. The tensor products of these two real operations are as follows.

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{A}^{i}\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i})(\rho_{A}^{i}\otimes\rho_{B}^{i})=(\Lambda_{A}^{i}\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i})(\rho_{A}^{i}\otimes\rho_{B}^{i})=\left({\textstyle\sum_{m}K_{m}^{A_{i}}\rho_{A}^{i}(K_{m}^{A_{i}})^{\dagger}}\right)\left({\textstyle\sum_{n}K_{n}^{B_{i}}\rho_{B}^{i}(K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger}}\right)

Utilize the properties of the Kronecker product of matrices:

(AB)\otimes(CD)=(A\otimes C)(B\otimes D)

then

\left({\textstyle\sum_{m}K_{m}^{A_{i}}\rho_{A}^{i}(K_{m}^{A_{i}})^{\dagger}}\right)\left({\textstyle\sum_{n}K_{n}^{B_{i}}\rho_{B}^{i}(K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger}}\right)=\sum_{m,n}(K_{m}^{A_{i}}\otimes K_{n}^{B_{i}})(\rho_{A}^{i}\otimes\rho_{B}^{i})(K_{m}^{A_{i}}\otimes K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger}

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}(K_{m}^{A_{i}}\otimes K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger}(K_{m}^{A_{i}}\otimes K_{n}^{B_{i}})=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}((K_{m}^{A_{i}})^{\dagger}\otimes(K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger})(K_{m}^{A_{i}}\otimes K_{n}^{B_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}((K_{m}^{A_{i}})^{\dagger}K_{m}^{A_{i}})\otimes((K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger}K_{n}^{B_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sum_{m}(K_{m}^{A_{i}})^{\dagger}K_{m}^{A_{i}})\otimes(\sum_{n}(K_{n}^{B_{i}})^{\dagger}K_{n}^{B_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\otimes I=I

So,
\left\{K_{m}^{A_{i}}\otimes K_{n}^{B_{i}}\right\}
are the Kraus operators of
\Lambda_{A}^{i}\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i}
, and it is easy to see that
\Lambda_{A}^{i}\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i}
is also a real operation. Therefore, we arrive at the following conclusions.

Theorem 4.1
.

All separable states in a two-qubit system have the following form:

\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}(\Lambda_{A}^{i}\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i})(\ket{+}\bra{+}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+}).

If
\rho
is a pure state, then
\rho
can be obtained from
(\ket{+}\bra{+})\otimes(\ket{+}\bra{+})
through a real operation, i.e.,

\rho=(\Lambda_{A}\otimes\Lambda_{B})(\ket{+}\bra{+}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+}),

where
\Lambda_{A}\otimes\Lambda_{B}
is a real operation.

Next, we consider the imaginarity measure of
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}(\Lambda_{A}^{i}\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i})(\ket{+}\bra{+}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+})
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}(\Lambda_{A}^{i}\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i})(\ket{+}\bra{+}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathscr{F}\left((\Lambda_{A}^{i}\otimes\Lambda_{B}^{i})(\ket{+}\bra{+}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathscr{F}(\ket{+}\bra{+}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+})=\mathscr{F}(\ket{+}\bra{+}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+}).

Therefore,
\mathscr{F}(\ket{+}\bra{+}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+})
is an upper bound on the imaginarity measure for all separable states in a two-qubit system. Thus, we consider the state
(\ket{+}\bra{+})\otimes(\ket{+}\bra{+})
as the maximal imaginary state among the separable states in a two-qubit system. Similarly, the state
(\ket{-}\bra{-})\otimes(\ket{-}\bra{-})
is also a maximal imaginary state.

Here, we focus on the ability of the two-qubit channels to generate and destroy imaginarity in separable states. By analogy with the definitions in Reference
[
26
]
, we now present the definitions of imaginary power and de-imaginary power for separable states, which are required to quantify this ability.

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose
\varepsilon
is a quantum channel. Its imaginary power with respect to separate states is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{K}(\varepsilon)\equiv\max_{\rho\in\mathscr{R}}\{\mathscr{F}_{K}(\varepsilon(\rho))-\mathscr{F}_{K}(\rho)\}=\max_{\rho\in\mathscr{R}}\mathscr{F}_{K}(\varepsilon(\rho)),

where
\mathscr{F}_{K}
represents any imaginarity measure,
\mathscr{R}
is the set of real states among separable states, and the second equality uses the fact that the imaginarity measure of a real state is zero.

Definition 4.3
.

Suppose
\varepsilon
is a quantum channel. Its de-imaginary power with respect to separate states is defined as

\mathcal{D}_{K}(\varepsilon)\equiv\max_{\rho\in\mathscr{M}}\{\mathscr{F}_{K}(\rho)-\mathscr{F}_{K}(\varepsilon(\rho))\},

where
\mathscr{F}_{K}
represents any imaginarity measure, and
\mathscr{M}
is the set of maximal imaginary states among the separable states.

From the above definition, it can be observed that the imaginary power represents the maximum amount of imaginarity generated when the channel acts on a real state among the separable states, and the de-imaginary power represents the ability of the channel to maximally reduce the imaginarity of the maximal imaginary state among the separable states.

5.
Conclusion

This study conducts a hierarchical study on single-qubit and two-qubit systems, and investigates the decay trends of three imaginarity measures (the
l_{1}
-norm-based measure, robustness of imaginarity, and relative entropy of imaginarity) for arbitrary pure states under three typical quantum channels (dephasing, generalized amplitude damping, and phase-amplitude damping channels). For the two-qubit level, we analyze the decay laws of the aforementioned imaginarity measures for important states, such as entangled states and dual-rail states under the given two-qubit channels. Dual-rail encoding has strong error detection and correction capabilities. It can effectively convert hardware errors (such as photon loss, and decoherence, etc.) into detectable erasure errors, thereby simplifying the quantum error correction process and improving the reliability and fault tolerance of quantum computers. In addition, dual-rail qubits are resistant to environmental interference and noise, allowing them to maintain the coherence of quantum states for a longer time, thereby reducing the information loss and errors caused by qubit decoherence. We then have generalized the concept and definition by extending the concept of the maximal imaginary state for single qubits to separable two-qubit states and defining the maximal imaginary state for the latter, referencing single-qubit channels to define the imaginary power and de-imaginary power of two-qubit channels for separable states, which characterize the channels’ ability to generate and destroy the imaginarity of separable states. Finally, we calculate the imaginary and de-imaginary powers of various two-qubit channels and explore their correlations with channel parameters.
