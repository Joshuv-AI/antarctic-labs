The threshold for the asymmetric vertex-Ramsey property in randomly perturbed graphs
Asier Calbet, Victor Falgas-Ravry, Joseph Hyde

Abstract
For r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 and graphs H 1 , … , H r , G H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},G , we say that G G is ( H 1 , … , H r ) (H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}) vertex-Ramsey, or ( H 1 , … , H r ) v (H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v} -Ramsey, if whenever we colour the vertices of G G with colours from the set [ r ] = { 1 , 2 , … , r } [r]=\{1,2,\ldots,r\} there exists j ∈ [ r ] j\in[r] such that some copy of H j H_{j} in G G is monochromatic in colour j j . Given any fixed collection of graphs H 1 , … , H r H_{1},\ldots,H_{r} , Łuczak, Ruciński and Voigt [ 22 ] and Kreuter [ 13 ] determined in the 1990s the threshold edge probability p p at which the binomial random graph G ⁡ ( n , p ) G(n,p) becomes ( H 1 , … , H r ) v (H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v} -Ramsey. More recently, Das, Morris and Treglown [ 6 ] investigated the vertex-Ramsey property in the randomly perturbed setting. When r = 2 r=2 they determined the number of random edges one must add to a dense graph to ensure that with probability 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) 1-o(1) the resulting graph is ( H 1 , H 2 ) v (H_{1},H_{2})_{v} -Ramsey whenever one of H 1 H_{1} or H 2 H_{2} is a clique. They posed the problem of extending their results to all pairs of graphs ( H 1 , H 2 ) (H_{1},H_{2}) . In this paper we resolve a more general form of their problem and determine for any r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 and r r -tuple of graphs ( H 1 , … , H r ) (H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}) the number of random edges one must add to a dense graph to ensure that with probability 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) 1-o(1) the resulting graph is ( H 1 , … , H r ) v (H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v} -Ramsey.

Introduction

Given

(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})

G\rightarrow(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v}

G\rightarrow(H,r)_{v}
and say ‘
G

(H,r)_{v}
-Ramsey’.

In the classical setting, the vertex-Ramsey problem is fairly uninteresting compared with its edge counterpart.
Indeed, denoting by

if and only if

. More generally for dense but non-complete graphs, writing

for the number of edges in a graph

, we have the following. We say a graph is non-empty is it contains at least one edge.

Observation 1.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
be non-empty graphs.
There exists a graph
G^{\star}
with

e(G^{\star})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\chi(H_{j})-1)}\right)\left(\binom{v(G^{\star})}{2}+\frac{v(G^{\star})}{2}\right)

such that

On the other hand, for any fixed

n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that any graph

satisfies
G\rightarrow(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v}

Proof.

For the first part, consider a complete balanced
\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\chi(H_{j})-1)
-partite graph

. This graph has

e(G^{\star})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\chi(H_{j})-1)}\right)\frac{v(G^{\star})^{2}}{2}

containing no monochromatic copy of
H_{j}
in colour

The second part of the observation follows from the famous Erdős–Stone–Simonovits
[
8
,
9
]

be the complete
\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\chi(H_{j})-1)\right)

Now for any

[
8
,
9

such that for any graph

-Ramsey.

In this vein, Łuczak, Ruciński and Voigt
[

initiated the study of vertex-Ramsey properties of random graphs.
The model of random graphs considered here is the Erdős-Rényi binomial random graph model

. To state Łuczak, Ruciński and Voigt’s main result, we must first introduce a key graph parameter.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a graph

m_{1}(H):=\max\left\{\frac{e(J)}{v(J)-1}:J\subseteq H,v(J)\geq 2\right\},

setting

Theorem 1.3

(Łuczak, Ruciński and Voigt

)
.

For

We refer to the statements in the cases

When

Observe that

Hence, we require

Kreuter
[

To state Kreuter’s result, we must introduce another graph parameter, refining the notion of 1-density.

Definition 1.4

(Kreuter threshold)
.

Given any two graphs
H_{1}
and

,

The following facts, which can be proved by elementary algebra, illustrate the relationship between 1-density and mixed 1-density.

Fact 1.5
.

For all pairs of graphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
with
m_{1}(H_{1})\geq m_{1}(H_{2})
,
the mixed

-density of

and

lies between the 1-densities of the corresponding graphs:
m_{1}(H_{1})\geq m_{1}(H_{2},H_{1})\geq m_{1}(H_{2})
. Furthermore,
m_{1}(H_{1})>m_{1}(H_{2},H_{1})>m_{1}(H_{2})
if and only if
m_{1}(H_{1})>m_{1}(H_{2})
, and if for some graph

we have
m_{1}(H_{2})=m_{1}(H_{3})\leq m_{1}(H_{1})
then
m_{1}(H_{2},H_{1})=m_{1}(H_{3},H_{1})
.

Theorem 1.6

(Kreuter
[
13
]
)
.

For
r\geq 2
and graphs
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
such that
m_{1}(H_{1})\geq\cdots\geq m_{1}(H_{r})

the following holds:
there exist constants

such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[G(n,p)\to(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v}]=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if}\ p>Cn^{-\frac{1}{m_{1}(H_{2},H_{1})}}\\

In this paper, we shall obtain an analogue of Kreuter’s result for
randomly perturbed graphs
, which were introduced by Bohman, Frieze and Martin
[
2
]
in 2003. In this setting, one has a (somewhat) dense graph which is perturbed by the addition of a given number of random edges. This can be viewed as a model interpolating between the random graph
G(n,p)
on the one hand and the (somewhat) dense or structured graphs usually studied in extremal graph theory. Randomly perturbed graphs have received extensive attention due to the often delicate interplay between extremal and probabilitic considerations required for their study, and constitute a highly active area of research in combinatorics — there are now over 138 citations of the original paper of Bohman, Frieze and Martin — with a particular focus on finding spanning structures in randomly perturbed graphs.

Our goal is to investigate threshold functions for (multicolour, asymmetric) vertex-Ramsey properties in randomly perturbed graphs. Given a graph
G
, we define its
density

d(G)
to be
d(G):=e(G)/\binom{v(G)}{2}
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
d\in[0,1]
and
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
be graphs. We say a function
p=p(n):\ \mathbb{N}\rightarrow[0,1]
is a

perturbed vertex-Ramsey threshold function for
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
at density
d
if:

(i)

there exists an infinite increasing sequence of positive integers
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and an associated sequence of
n_{k}
-vertex graphs
(G_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, each of density at least
d
, such that if
q(n)=o(p(n))
,
then

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[G_{n_{k}}\cup G(n_{k},q(n_{k}))\rightarrow(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v}\right]=0

(ii)

for any
q(n)=\omega(p(n))
and any sequence
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
n
-vertex graphs, each of density at least
d
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[G_{n}\cup G(n,q(n))\rightarrow(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v}\right]=1.

We write
p(n;H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},d)
as a placeholder for any instance
p(n)
of a perturbed vertex-Ramsey threshold function for
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
at density
d
. Further if every graph on sufficiently many vertices of density at least
d
is
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})_{v}
-Ramsey (and thus (i) above cannot be satisfied), we define
p(n;H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},d):=0
. Hence from Observation
1.1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we have
p(n;H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},d):=0
when
d=(1-1/({\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\chi(H_{j})-1)})+\varepsilon)
.
If
H_{1}=\cdots=H_{r}=H
for some graph

, we write
p(n;H,r,d)
in place of
p(n;H_{1},\ldots,H_{r},d)
. Since being
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})
vertex-Ramsey is a non-trivial monotone property, it follows from the Bollobás–Thomason threshold theorem
[
3
]
that perturbed vertex-Ramsey threshold functions exist for all values of
d

.

Remark 1.8
.

In some very specific cases the vertex-Ramsey problem in the randomly perturbed setting may reduce to the same problem in the random setting. Indeed, as observed in

Let

Remark 1.10
.

For

and

Theorem 1.11

(Krivelevich, Sudakov and Tetali
[

]
)

Let

to be the unique integer satisfying

Let

Building on Theorem
1.11
, Das, Morris and Treglown initiated the study of vertex-Ramsey properties of randomly perturbed graphs, resolving the problem in the two-colour case

Definition 1.12
.

Given an ordered
r
-tuple of graphs
\mathbf{H}=(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{r})
, let
\sigma
be any permutation such that
m_{1}(H_{\sigma(1)})\geq m_{1}(H_{\sigma(2)})\geq\ldots\geq m_{1}(H_{\sigma(r)})
. We then define

\beta(\mathbf{H})=\beta(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{r}):=m_{1}\left(H_{\sigma(2)},H_{\sigma(1)}\right).

Further, given an
r
-tuple of families of graphs
\mathbf{\mathcal{H}}=(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{r})
, we define

\beta(\mathbf{\mathcal{H}}):=\min_{H_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{1},\ \ldots,\ H_{r}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}}\beta\left(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{r}\right).

Note that
\beta(\mathbf{H})
is well defined as, by Fact
1.5
, the value of

is independent of the choice of
\sigma
. Indeed, let
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
be permutations such that
\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}
,
m_{1}(H_{\sigma(1)})\geq m_{1}(H_{\sigma(2)})\geq\ldots\geq m_{1}(H_{\sigma(r)})
and
m_{1}(H_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)})\geq m_{1}(H_{\sigma^{\prime}(2)})\geq\ldots\geq m_{1}(H_{\sigma^{\prime}(r)})
. If
m_{1}(H_{\sigma(1)})=m_{1}(H_{\sigma(2)})
, then by Fact

we have
m_{1}(H_{\sigma(2)},H_{\sigma(1)})=m_{1}(H_{\sigma^{\prime}(2)},H_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)})\ (=m_{1}(H_{\sigma(1)}))
. Further, if
m_{1}(H_{\sigma(1)})>m_{1}(H_{\sigma(2)})=m_{1}(H_{\sigma(3)})
, then
\sigma(1)=\sigma^{\prime}(1)
and
m_{1}(H_{\sigma(2)})=m_{1}(H_{\sigma(3)})=m_{1}(H_{\sigma^{\prime}(2)})=m_{1}(H_{\sigma^{\prime}(3)})

m_{1}(H_{\sigma^{\prime}(2)},H_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)})=m_{1}(H_{\sigma(2)},H_{\sigma(1)})
.

The critical parameter governing the

-Ramsey property in the randomly perturbed setting is then the following.

Definition 1.13
.

For

where the first maximum is taken over all ordered

-tuples
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})

of non-negative integers summing to at most

Contributions of this paper

In their concluding remarks, Das, Morris and Treglown
[
6

.

Let
H

\mathcal{F}(H;k)=(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{k})

k

there is some

.

We note that a similar notion appears in the concluding remarks of

]
under the name ‘

[

.
Next, given an ordered

\mathbf{H}=(H_{1},\ldots,H_{r})
for some

, and an integer
k\geq 2
, we set

\displaystyle\beta(\mathbf{H};k):=\underset{\mathcal{F}(H_{1};k),\ldots,\mathcal{F}(H_{r};k)}{\max}\ \underset{i\in[k]}{\min}\ \beta\left(\mathcal{F}(H_{1};k)_{i},\ldots,\mathcal{F}(H_{r};k)_{i}\right),

(1)

where the maximum is taken over all ordered
r
-tuples
(\mathcal{F}(H_{1};k),\ldots,\mathcal{F}(H_{r};k))
of

-,

Remark 1.16
.

The quantity
\beta
is computable in principle. Indeed,
there are at most
2^{k\cdot{2}^{|V(H)|}}
many
k
-partite certificates of
H
-freeness so the maximum in (
1
) is taken over a finite set, and the Kreuter threshold inherent in the parameter
\beta\big(\mathcal{F}(H_{1};k)_{i},\ldots,\mathcal{F}(H_{r};k)_{i}\big)
is itself a maximum over finitely many pairs of finite graphs of a maximum over their finitely many subgraphs. However in practice computing

is not a tractable task unless the graphs

are of small order
or have a very simple structure, such as being cliques.

Remark 1.17
.

While there are superficial differences in the way we have presented these parameters, it is easily checked that

Theorem 1.18
.

Let
\mathbf{H}=(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots H_{r})
be an
r

be fixed with
\frac{k-2}{k-1}<d\leq\frac{k-1}{k}

\displaystyle p(n;\mathbf{H},d)=n^{-1/\beta(\mathbf{H};k)}.

Before proving Theorem
1.18

Theorem 1.19

Definition 2.2

Given

As is well known, the random-like distribution of edges in regular pairs allows them to mimic complete bipartite graphs with respect to containing (sparse) bipartite subgraphs.

Lemma 2.3

Let

. For any

-regular pair

in

(i)

|\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})\in A^{\ell}:|\cap_{i}N(x_{i})\cap B|\leq(d-\varepsilon)^{\ell}|B|\}|\leq\ell\varepsilon|A|^{\ell}.

(ii)

If

, and

and

are such that

and

-regular pair of density

Property (i) essentially says that small sets of vertices typically have many common neighbours in regular pairs.
Property (ii) relates how sufficiently large subgraphs of dense regular pairs remain both regular and dense. In our arguments, we will use the following simple corollary of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma
[
19
]

and Turán’s theorem

]

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper we have focused on vertex-Ramsey properties of randomly perturbed graphs.
The study of edge-Ramsey properties of randomly perturbed graphs is ongoing.
For graphs
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
, we say a graph
G
is
(H_{1},H_{2})_{e}
-Ramsey if every red/blue edge colouring of the edges of
G
yields a monochromatic copy of
H_{1}
in red or a monochromatic copy of
H_{2}
in blue.
The study of edge-Ramsey properties of randomly perturbed graphs was initiated by Krivelevich, Sudakov and Tetali
[
14
]
who determined for all
t\geq 3
the threshold at which adding a copy of
G(n,p)
to a dense graph results in a graph that is
(K_{3},K_{t})_{e}
-Ramsey.
This was followed by work of Das and Treglown
[
7
]
who almost completely answered a question of
Krivelevich, Sudakov and Tetali
[
14
]
by generalising their result to cover all pairs of cliques except
(K_{4},K_{t})
, for
t\geq 5
,
with the result for
(K_{4},K_{4})
following from bounds given by Das and Treglown
[
7
]
and a construction of Powierski
[
18
]
.
Das and Treglown
[
7
]
also addressed the corresponding problems for pairs of cycles fully and for pairs of cycles and cliques in almost all cases.

All these results were obtained before the recent resolution of the Kohayakawa-Kreuter
[
11
]
conjecture on the edge-Ramsey properties of
G(n,p)
by Mousset, Nenadov and Samotij
[
17
]
(for the 1-statement),
Kuperwasser, Widgerson and Samotij
[
15
]
,
Bowtell, Hancock
[
4
]
and the third author, and Christoph, Martinsson, Steiner and Wigderson
[
5
]
(for the 0-statement).The confirmation of this conjecture paves the way for building on the results of
[
7
,
14
,
18
]
and determining the threshold for edge-Ramsey properties in randomly perturbed random graphs in full generality.
The very recent work of Yancey
[
21
]
on a generalisation of the Kohayakawa-Kreuter conjecture to families of graphs via a graph partitioning conjecture of Kuperwasser, Widgerson and Samotij
[
15
]
may also prove useful to this endeavour.

Finally, we should mention work of Kuperwasser and Samotij
[
16
]
on a list version of the Kohayakawa–Kreuter conjecture and of Aigner-Horev, Danon, Hefetz and Letzter
[
1
]
on an anti-Ramsey problem in randomly perturbed graphs, which provide potentially interesting directions to extend the work in this paper. In addition, one could consider hypergraph versions of our problem; however, we currently lack an adequate extremal theory of hypergraphs to extend our methods, and this presents us with an obstacle which it would likely be necessary to overcome first before one can study vertex-Ramsey problems in randomly perturbed hypergraphs.
