Executive Summary
The U.S. Research Impact Alliance (USRIA) was created with a vision to leverage successful models that
accelerate technology development and commercialization, and support startup businesses based upon
federal research investments. Building upon lessons learned and feedback from cohort teams, the USRIA
programs have morphed into customized solutions that offer services which provide the most impact to
startup businesses. Based upon discussions with small and startup businesses, entrepreneurs, and
technology developers, a variety of resources have been developed to support each of these target
audiences. USRIA created program differentiators to offer one-on-one engagement and direct
communication with cohort teams, highly relevant conversations with targeted introductions, and
minimal “class” time (only used when an issue is applicable to all cohort members).

USRIA’s Commercialization Connection Challenge (C3) program offers tailored, hands-on guidance to
tech startups focusing on themes that include energy storage, industrial technologies, clean chemicals,
coal-to-high-value products, and hydrogen. In its initial two cohorts, our C3 Program supported thirteen
startups with results that include:

 ● Cohort company applications for more than $100 million in funding.
 ● More than 125 one-on-one meetings with industry advisors.
 ● Introductions to dozens of investors and strategic partners.
 ● Advising of startups through the process of forming nondisclosure and joint development
 agreements with multinational partners.
 ● Creation of multiple startups based on SBIR-funded technologies.
 ● Recruitment of a senior growth officer for a startup team.

Toward the end of the EPIC program, USRIA expanded its advisory programming to create an Expert-in-
Residence (EIR) Corps program to begin addressing the talent gap in formation-stage startups by
recruiting and subsidizing the work of experienced startup team members to partner with inventors of
promising energy technologies while they are still at the university or national lab. By matchmaking
seasoned industry executives, board advisors, and mentors with technical founders, EIR-Corps enhances
the potential for commercialization success from the initial point of startup creation. The EIR-Corps
model is based on the thesis that placing a heavy emphasis on the startup team and technology
curation, coupled with proactive recruitment of startup team members with specific industry
experience, will yield a significantly higher commercialization success rate.

Project Activities
USRIA’s C3 Program launched in early 2022 with the theory that pairing highly relevant industry advisors
(SMEs) with technical startup teams (often located in rural areas and/or without access to specialized
talent and capital networks) in a one-on-one coaching setting would yield successful long-term results.
Two cohorts later, this advisory model yielded positive results and showed the need for even earlier
engagement from industry experts. USRIA therefore began to think more deeply about how best to
embed a deep level of industry & business acumen into startup teams earlier in the formation process
and began developing an EIR-Corps Program that could serve as a precursor to the C3 advisory program
to enable a higher success rate for lab-based innovations that might otherwise stall.

Lessons Learned
  ● EIR-Corps Model: Lessons learned from the C3 Program included identifying a “valley of death”
 when it comes to finding talent to create startup entities at the founder stage, as the typical
 faculty-founder model has many pitfalls. Historically, efforts to commercialize research
 innovations have not placed sufficient importance on one of the most difficult tasks - finding
 talented professionals with the industry knowledge and network required to accelerate the
 successful deployment of new integrated, hard-tech energy technologies. University tech
 transfer offices (TTOs) often encourage faculty members to initiate spin-offs on a part-time
 basis, most of which do not have access to business talent or relevant industry networks. Due to
 competing priorities, however, this approach often places the burden of commercial and
 technical leadership on ill-prepared students whose commitment disappears upon graduation
 when the need for steady income becomes acute. Alternatively, the faculty member attempts
 to juggle university responsibilities concurrent to startup launch, which rarely results in success.
 Similar challenges exist in national lab TTOs. In essence, USRIA’s EIR -Corps model veers from
 the "train-the-scientist" approach and recruits business leadership (the “EIR,” serving as interim
 CEO or strategy leader) and facilitates their collaboration with the technical team, while also
 surrounding the team with senior industry advisors (“SMEs”) to increase the success rates of
 startups.
  ● To address this, USRIA is deploying a new program titled “EIR-Corps” focused on finding the
 right fractional employees with the right skillset for these early-stage companies. Securing a key
 team is critical in company growth trajectory.

 ● Advisor Network: Creating an expert advisor network afforded USRIA the opportunity to tap into
 technical resources that are applicable to cohort companies. Relevant industry advisors to work
 1:1 with cohorts have proven to be unique value-add to teams.
 ● Consortium Building: During the initial launch of the C3 Accelerator, USRIA leveraged existing
 consortiums by tapping into established relationships and infrastructure, as well as participating
 in the formation of new consortiums.
 ● Training: More mature companies seem to have more experience yet understanding customer
 discovery vs. pitch is key tenet to growth and success of a startup. Startups would benefit from
 more training and resources regarding fundraising. Cohort group training should be used only
 when the topic applies to all companies at that given time.
 ● University Tech Transfer Support: There is a clear need for team building services for early-stage
 startups/late-stage university technologies. University TTOs also benefit from having
 experienced industry experts evaluate their technology portfolios to identify the most relevant
 university IP for industry.
 ● Awareness of DOE-funded Programs: USRIA’s internal knowledge and understanding of the
 ENERGYWERX Voucher opportunities was communicated to cohort teams via monthly
 newsletters, meetings, and in a funding opportunity tracking tool. The promotion of these
 opportunities led to several teams receiving support.
