On the model theory of the Free Factor Complex of rank 2
Zahra Mohammadi Khangheshlaghi & Katrin Tent

Abstract
. We begin the investigation of the free factor complex of a free group of finite rank. For the case of rank 2 2 we axiomatize its theory and show that it is ω \omega -stable with prime model 𝒜 ​ ℱ 2 \mathcal{AF}_{2} .



1.
Introduction

The free factor complex
\mathcal{AF}_{n}
is a simplicial complex naturally associated to the free group
\mathcal{F}_{n}
of rank
n
. Its vertices are the proper free factors of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and edges denote containment. The cliques in this graph form simplices, making this graph a simplicial complex of rank
n-1
very similar in flavor to the spherical Tits building associated to a projective space. Free factor complexes were originally introduced by Hatcher and Vogtmann
[
2
]
, who showed that (as in the case of spherical buildings arising from projective spaces), the free factor complex of a free group of rank
n
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres of
dimension
n-2
.
Recently, Bestvina and Bridson
[
1
]
extended this analogy to the fundamental theorem of projective geometry by showing that for
n\geq 3
we have
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathcal{AF}_{n})\cong\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathcal{F}_{n})
. In the case of rank
2
, all proper free factors have rank
1
and so there is no containment relation. In this case, we study the graph of the complement relation between the free factors, i.e. we put an edge between free factors if their free product is the free group. It was shown in
[
1
]
that this complement relation is definable in the free factor complex of higher rank. Thus, necessarily, in order to study the model theory of
\mathcal{AF}_{n}
for
n\geq 3
we have to start with the model theory
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
with respect to the complement relation and this is what we do in this note.

2.
The Free Factor Complex of rank 2

Definition 2.1
.

A subgroup
H\leq\mathcal{F}_{2}
is called a
free factor
of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
if there exists a subgroup
K\leq\mathcal{F}_{2}
such that

\mathcal{F}_{2}=H*K,

where
*
denotes the free product of groups. In this case,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is the free product of
H
and
K
, and both
H
and
K
are themselves free groups.

Remark 2.2
.

An element
g\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
is called
primitive
if and only if there exists a basis
\{x,y\}
of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
such that
g=\langle x\rangle
if and only if
\langle g\rangle
is a nontrivial proper free factor of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Thus, the nontrivial proper free factors of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are precisely the infinite cyclic subgroups generated by primitive elements.

Definition 2.3
.

The free factor complex
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
is the simplicial graph
whose vertices are the nontrivial proper free factors of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
and two distinct
vertices
x,y\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
are connected by an edge
E(x,y)
if they generate
\mathcal{F}_{2}
as a free product.

Notation 2.4
.

To simplify notation we will mostly write
x\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
instead of
\langle x\rangle\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
for a primitive element
x\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

The following are some easy, but useful observations:

Remark 2.5
.

Edges are invariant under
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathcal{F}_{2})
,
Aut(\mathcal{F}_{2})
acts transitively on the edges of this graph and the stabilizer of an edge
(x,y)
in
Aut(\mathcal{F}_{2})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Note that if
E(x,y)
holds, then
x
and
y
are not conjugate.

We have the following characterization of edges in
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
:

Remark 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{2}=\langle a,b\rangle
. Then
E(b,x)
holds for
x\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}

if and only if
x=b^{m}a^{\delta}b^{k}
for some
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\delta\in\{\pm 1\}
.

We will be using the quantifier-free diagram of specific subgraphs
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
in our axiomatization as the main building blocks, see Section
10
. These subgraphs are inductively defined as follows:

Definition 2.7
.

Let
e=\{x,y\}
be an edge in
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
.
There exist exactly four vertices, called
sticks
in
[
1
]
, which are adjacent to both of
x
and
y
, namely the vertices generated by
xy,xy^{-1},x^{-1}y
and
x^{-1}y^{-1}
, respectively. We call the graph consisting of these six vertices the
1
-extension
\mathrm{Ext}_{1}(e)
of
e
and put
\mathrm{Ext}_{0}(e)=e
.

Inductively, the
k
-extension
\mathrm{Ext}_{k}(e)
of
e
is
\mathrm{Ext}_{k-1}(e)

together with the
1
-extension of every edge in the
k-1
-extension.
Then

\mathcal{AF}_{2}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Ext}_{k}(e).

If
A=\mathrm{Ext}_{k}(e)
for some edge
e
, we call
A
a block of level
k
.

The Farey graph
\mathcal{OF}_{2}
is the quotient of
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
under the conjugation action of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
We briefly recall its inductive definition:

Definition 2.8
.

(see e.g.
[
3
]
) The Farey graph
FG
is the planar graph defined inductively as follows: let
FG_{0}
be the graph consisting of two triangles sharing one edge.
If
FG_{i}
has already been defined,
FG_{i+1}
is the graph obtained by adjoining a triangle to every boundary edge of
FG_{i}
. Finally
FG=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}FG_{i}
.

Note that in contrast to the Farey graph, the extensions
\mathrm{Ext}_{k}(e)
for
k\geq 2
are not free: some of the new vertices added in the 1-extension of
\mathrm{Ext}_{k}(e)
have valency strictly larger than two, i.e. there are edges to vertices in
\mathrm{Ext}_{k-1}(e)
and these additional edges are controlled by Remark
2.6
, see Section
10
.

Remark 2.9
.

(1)

Clearly, for any edge
e=E(x,y)
we have
\mathrm{acl}(\{x,y\})=\mathcal{AF}_{2}
and in fact
\mathcal{AF}_{2}\subset\mathrm{dcl}(Exp_{1}(e))
.

(2)

The initial edge of a block of each level is unique. This can be seen inductively by removing all edges that are not contained in exactly four triangles.

(3)

Any two blocks of level
k
are isomorphic under an automorphism of
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
.

3.
An additional graph structure on
\mathcal{AF}_{2}

Until further notice we will be working in the language of graphs
L_{0}=\{E\}
.

Notation 3.1
.

For
x,y\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
we write
D_{k}(x,y)
if
k
is minimal such that there is a path of length
k
from
x
to
y
and

D_{k}(x)=\{y\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}\colon D_{k}(x,y)\}.

We will be expanding the language by
L_{0}
-definable relations and start with conjugates.
We use Remark
2.6
to describe the set of conjugates of a vertex in
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
.
To this end we define an additional graph structure on
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
:

Definition 3.2
.

For
x,y\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
we put an edge
C(x,y)
and say that
x
is a 1-conjugate of
y
if there is a basis
\{x,z\}
for
\mathcal{F}_{2}
such that
y=x^{z}
.

To show that this edge relation
C
is definable in
L
we first observe:

Lemma 3.3
.

If
x,y,z\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
are distinct conjugates, then

|D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(y)\cap D_{1}(z)|\leq 1.

Proof.

Suppose towards a contradiction that we have distinct conjugates
x,y,z\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
and

t_{1}\neq t_{2}\in D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(y)\cap D_{1}(z)
.
By Remark
2.6
we may assume that
x=a,t_{1}=b
. Then by Remark
2.6

we have
y=\langle a^{b^{m}}\rangle
, and

z=\langle a^{b^{k}}\rangle
for some
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Again using Remark
2.6
we obtain from
E(a,t_{2})
that there are
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
t_{2}=\langle a^{m_{1}}ba^{m_{2}}\rangle
and from
E(t_{2},y)
we see that
y=a^{t_{2}^{l}}=a^{b^{m}}
. This implies
t_{2}=a^{m_{1}}b
and
m=l
.

Applying the same argument to
z
we now see that
z=a^{t_{2}^{m}}=a^{b^{m}}=y
, contradicting our assmption.
∎

We can now show that the relation
C
is definable.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

x
is a 1-conjugate of
y
;

(2)

D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(y)
is infinite;

(3)

|D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(y)|\geq 5
;

If any of the above conditions hold, we call the set

\ell{(x,y)}:=D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(x^{\prime})

the
line
determined by
x
and
y
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: Let
y
be a 1-conjugate of
x
with respect to
t
, so we can assume that
y=txt^{-1}
. Then
t\in D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(y)
and for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\langle tx^{k}\rangle\in D_{1}(\langle x\rangle)\cap D_{1}(txt^{-1})
, so this set is infinite.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: This is clear.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: Let
x,y\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
be such that
|D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(y)|\geq 5
.
We first claim that
x
and
y
are conjugate.
Suppose not and let
\pi
be the quotient map from
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
to
\mathcal{OF}_{2}
taking
every vertex to its conjugacy class.
If
x
and
y
are not conjugate,
\pi(x)\neq\pi(y)
.
Since
\mathcal{OF}_{2}
is isomorphic to the Farey graph and two vertices
in the Farey graph have at most two neighbors in common, we have

|D_{1}(\pi(x))\cap D_{1}(\pi(y))|\leq 2
.
By the pigeonhole principle
there exist
A\subset D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(y)
such that
|A|\geq 3

and
|\pi(A)|=1
.
Thus
A
consists of conjugates having at least two common neighbours,
contradicting Lemma
3.3
.
Hence,
x
and
y
are conjugate.

Now let
t\neq t^{\prime}\in D_{1}(x)\cap D_{1}(y)
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
x=b
and
t=a
,
so by Remark
2.6
we have
y=a^{-k}ba^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
t^{\prime}\in D_{1}(b)
, by Remark
2.6
we have

t^{\prime}=\langle b^{k_{1}}ab^{k_{2}}\rangle
for some
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, not both zero,
and since
t^{\prime}\in D_{1}(y)
we also have

t^{\prime}=y^{m_{1}}ay^{m_{2}}=(a^{-k}ba^{k})^{m_{1}}a(a^{-k}ba^{k})^{m_{1}}
for some
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Since the reduced word for
t^{\prime}
begins or ends in the letter
b^{\pm 1}
, it follows that
k=0

and hence
y=b^{a}
as claimed.

∎

We need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{2}=\langle a,b\rangle,g\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Then
C(b^{g},y)
holds if and only if
y=b^{a^{\delta}b^{k}g}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\delta\in\{\pm 1\}
.

Proof.

First consider the case
g=1
. If
C(b,y)
holds, then by Remark
2.6
we know that
y=b^{x}
where
x=b^{m}ab^{k}
for some
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
and hence
y=b^{ab^{k}}
. The converse is clear.

By conjugating the previous argument we now see that if
C(b^{g},y)
holds, then
y=(b^{g})^{x}
where
x
is of the form
x=(b^{g})^{m}a^{g}(b^{g})^{k}
for some
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
y=(b^{g})^{a^{g}(b^{g})^{k}}=b^{ab^{k}g}
as claimed. Again the converse is clear.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

(1)

Aut(\mathcal{AF}_{2})
acts transitively on
C
-edges.

(2)

\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathcal{AF}_{2})
acts transitively on triples
(x,y,z)
with
C(x,y)
and
z\in\ell(x,y)
.

Proof.

For the first part consider bases
\{a,b\}
and
\{c,d\}
for
\mathcal{F}_{2}
and
C
-edges
C(b,b^{a^{\delta_{1}}b^{m}})
and
C(d,d^{c^{\delta_{2}}d^{k}}),k,m\in\mathbb{Z},\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}
. If
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}
let
\sigma\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathcal{F}_{2})
with
\sigma(a,b)=(c,d)
. Otherwise let
\sigma(a,b)=(c^{-1},d)
. Let
\kappa
be conjugation by
d^{k-m}
. Then
\sigma(b,b^{a^{\delta_{1}}b^{m}})=(d,d^{c^{\delta_{2}}d^{m}})
and hence the composition of
\sigma
with
\kappa
takes
(b,b^{a^{\delta_{1}}b^{m}})
to
(d,d^{c^{\delta_{2}}d^{m}})
.

Part (2): By part (1) it is enough to show that the stabilizer of
x=b
and
y=\langle aba^{-1}\rangle
acts transitively on
\ell(x,y)=\{ab^{k}\,\mid\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Let
z=ab^{k}\in\ell(x,y)
and note that
\{b,ba\}
and
\{b,b^{k}a\}
are bases. Let
\sigma\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathcal{F}_{2})
be such that
\sigma(b,ba)=(b,b^{k}a)
. Then

\sigma(a)=b^{k-1}a
and hence
\sigma
fixes
b
and
b^{a}
and takes
ba
to
b^{k}a
as required.
∎

For future reference we also note the following:

Remark 3.7
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathcal{F}_{2})
be such that
\sigma(a,b)=(b^{k-1}a,b)
as above. Then

\sigma
fixes

and hence

for any

.

Notation 3.8
.

we call

.
the

-length of

.

We call each

between two occurrences of

a

-tile (note that

-tiles can be empty). Thus, if

has

-length

k+1
-many

-tiles, including the ones before the first and after the last occurrence of

.

For
x,y\in\mathcal{AF}_{2}
we say that
x
is a
k
-conjugate of

(and write

) if

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose
x\in\mathcal{F}_{2}=\langle a,b\rangle
is conjugate to
b
, i.e.
x=b^{g}
where we write
g
in reduced form as

g=a^{k_{1}}b^{m_{1}}\ldots a^{k_{s}}b^{m_{s}},k_{i},m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq s

Then there is a unique

-path from

to
\langle b\rangle
of length

.
Hence the conjugacy class of a vertex in
\mathcal{AF}_{2}
is a tree under the edge relation

.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on

.
First suppose
k=1
, so
g=a^{\delta}b^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\delta\in\{{\pm 1}\}
. Then

holds by Lemma
3.5
.

Now suppose we have proved the claim for

and suppose

has

-length

. Write

where the

-length of

is
k
and
\delta\in\{{\pm 1}\}
. By induction assumption there is a

-path of length

to

and
by Lemma

Definition 3.14
.

We call parallel lines

Lemma 3.15
.

Every line
\ell
has exactly four neighbours. More precisely,
\ell(b,b^{ab^{k}})
is a neighbour of
\ell=\ell(b,b^{a})

if and only if

.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.6
and Lemma

it suffices to prove the second statement.

By Corollary
3.6
and Remark

the stabilizer of

Note that if
