Abstract




Abstract

A graph
G
is called
k
-factor-critical if after deleting any
k
vertices the remaining subgraph still has a perfect matching. Fan and Lin [Adv. in Appl. Math. 174 (2026) 103019] posed an adjacency spectral condition for a graph with minimum degree to be
k
-factor-critical. A graph
G
is fractional
k
-factor-critical if after deleting any
k
vertices the remaining subgraph still has a fractional perfect matching. Clearly, the fractional
k
-factor-criticality of a graph is a necessary property for a graph to be
k
-factor-critical. Jia, Fan and Liu [Discrete Appl. Math. 386 (2026) 255-263] proposed a tight sufficient condition in terms of the spectral radius for a graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality to be
k
-factor-critical. A natural question arises: can we derive analogous sufficient conditions by incorporating the minimum degree parameter of graphs?

We first establish a lower bound on the size to ensure that a
(k+1)
-connected graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality is
k
-factor-critical, where
k
is a positive integer with
k\geq 1
. Moreover, we provide a sufficient condition in terms of the spectral radius for a
(k+1)
-connected graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality to be
k
-factor-critical. Our results generalize the result of
Jia, Fan and Liu to
(k+1)
-connected graphs. Furthermore, our spectral conditions apply to a broader family of connected graphs compared with the results of Fan and Lin, as well as Jia et al.

Keywords
: Spectral radius; Size;
k
-factor-critical; minimum degree; fractional
k
-factor-criticality.

Introduction

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite, undirected and simple graph, where
V(G)
is the vertex set and
E(G)
is the edge set. We denote the
order
and
size
of
G
by
|V(G)|
and
|E(G)|
, respectively. Let
\delta(G)
be the
minimum degree
(or simply
\delta
) of
G
.
For a vertex subset
S
of
G
, we denote by
G-S
and
G[S]
the subgraph of
G
obtained from
G
by deleting the vertices in
S
together with their incident edges and the subgraph of
G
induced by
S
, respectively. The number of components and the number of odd components of
G
are denoted by
c(G)
and
o(G)
, respectively. The set of isolated vertices of
G
is denoted by
i(G)
. Let
K_{n}
denote the
complete graph
of order
n
.
For two vertex-disjoint graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, we use
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
to denote the
disjoint union
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
.
The
join

G_{1}\vee G_{2}
is the graph obtained from
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
by adding all possible edges between
V(G_{1})
and
V(G_{2})
. A graph
G
of order
n
is called
k
-connected
if
n>k
and
G-X
is connected for every set
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|<k
, where
k
is a positive integer.

For a simple graph
G
of order
n
, its
adjacency matrix

A(G)=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
is a symmetric matrix with
a_{ij}=1
if and only if vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are adjacent, and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise. The
spectral radius

\rho(G)
of
G
is the largest eigenvalue of
A(G)
.

A
matching
in a graph
G
is a subset
M
of
E(G)
such that no vertex of
G
is incident with more than one edge in
M
. A
perfect matching
in
G
is a matching such that every vertex of
G
is incident with precisely one edge in it. The problem of determining when a general graph has a perfect matching is a classic topic in graph theory. In 2005, Brouwer and Haemers
[
1
]
provided several spectral sufficient conditions for the existence of a perfect matching in a graph. Motivated by their works, Fan et al.
[
6
]
gave a spectral radius condition for the existence of a perfect matching in a balanced bipartite graph with minimum degree
\delta
. Since then, many researchers have been interested in finding sufficient conditions to guarantee the existence of a perfect matching in a graph by using various graph invariants, see
[
4
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

To enhance the understanding of graph structures with perfect matchings, Gallai
[
8
]
and Lovász
[
11
]
introduced the concepts of factor-critical and bicritical graphs, respectively. A graph
G
is defined as
factor-critical
(resp.
bicritical
) if the removal of any vertex
v
(resp. two vertices
u,v
) results in a graph
G-v
(resp.
G-u-v
) that has a perfect matching. Motivated by the similarities and interesting properties of these two concepts, Favaron
[
7
]
and Yu
[
17
]
independently introduced the concept of
k
-factor-critical graphs: a graph
G
of order
n
is
k
-factor-critical
(where
0\leq k<n
) if removing any
k
vertices leaves a graph with a perfect matching. The case
k=0
is trivial and will not be discussed further in this article. Clearly, if
G
is a
k
-factor-critical graph with
n
vertices, then
k
and
n
have the same parity. Zhang and Fan
[
18
]
provided sufficient conditions for a graph to be
k
-factor-critical in terms of the edge number and the spectral radius. Fan and Lin
[
4
]
further derived an adjacency spectral condition for a connected graph with minimum degree to be
k
-factor-critical. Motivated by these results, Zheng et al.
[
19
]
established three sufficient conditions based on the size, the signless Laplacian spectral radius and the distance spectral radius of a connected graph with minimum degree to be
k
-factor-critical. Recently, Wang et al.
[
12
]
gave two sufficient conditions in terms of the size and the spectral radius for
k
-factor-criticality of
t
-connected graphs. More results on the relationships between the spectral radius and spanning subgraphs can be found in
[
2
,
5
,
10
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

A
fractional perfect matching
of a graph
G
is a function
h
giving each edge a number in
[0,1]
such that
\sum_{e\in E_{G}(v)}h(e)=1
for each
v\in V(G)
, where
E_{G}(v)
is the set of edges incident to
v
. A graph
G
is
fractional
k
-factor-critical
if after deleting any
k
vertices the remaining subgraph still has a fractional perfect matching.
Obviously, if
G
is
k
-factor-critical, then it is necessarily fractional
k
-factor-critical, whereas the converse is not universally valid.
Naturally, the following problem was raised.

Problem 1.1
.

What is a tight spectral radius condition which guarantees a graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality to be
k
-factor-critical?

Concerning Problem 1.1, Jia, Fan and Liu
[
9
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality of order
n\geq\max\{k+56,~23k+20\}
, where
n\equiv k\pmod{2}
and
k\geq 1
is an integer. If

\rho(G)\geq\rho(K_{k}\vee(K_{n-k-3}\cup K_{3})),

then
G
is
k
-factor-critical unless
G\cong K_{k}\vee(K_{n-k-3}\cup K_{3})
.

Motivated by the above result, our first main result gives
a lower bound based on the size to ensure that a
(k+1)
-connected graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality is
k
-factor-critical.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a
(k+1)
-connected graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality of order
n\geq\max\{6\delta-5k+8,~\frac{\delta^{2}}{6}-(\frac{k}{3}-\frac{3}{2})\delta+\frac{k^{2}}{6}-\frac{k}{2}+4\}
, where
\delta
is the minimum degree of
G
,
k\geq 1
is an integer and
n\equiv k\pmod{2}
. If

|E(G)|\geq|E(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1}))|,

then
G
is
k
-factor-critical unless
G\cong K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})
.

Our second main result provides a sufficient condition in terms of the spectral radius for a
(k+1)
-connected graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality to be
k
-factor-critical.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a
(k+1)
-connected graph with fractional
k
-factor-criticality of order
n\geq\max\{8\delta-5k+20,~\delta(\delta-k-1)^{2}-2\delta-1\}
, where
\delta
is the minimum degree of
G
,
k\geq 1
is an integer and
n\equiv k\pmod{2}
. If

\rho(G)\geq\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})),

then
G
is
k
-factor-critical unless
G\cong K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})
.

One can check that
\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})<\rho(K_{k}\vee(K_{n-k-3}\cup K_{3}))
for
\delta\geq k+1
and
n\geq 2\delta-k+6
.

Indeed, Fan and Lin
[
4
]
posed an adjacency spectral condition for a connected graph with minimum degree to be
k
-factor-critical.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
4
]
)
.

Suppose that
G
is a connected graph of order
n\geq\max\{8\delta-5k+4,\,\delta(\delta-k)^{2}+\delta-1\}
with minimum degree
\delta\geq k
, where
n\equiv k\pmod{2}
and
k\geq 1
. If

\rho(G)\geq\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-1}\cup(\delta-k+1)K_{1})),

then
G
is
k
-factor-critical unless
G\cong K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-1}\cup(\delta-k+1)K_{1})
.

We can also check that
\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})<\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-1}\cup(\delta-k+1)K_{1}))
for
\delta\geq k+1
and
n\geq 2\delta-k+6
.

For clarity, the exceptional graph in Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
is shown in Figure 1.

Figure 1
:
K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})
.

In the proof of Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.4, we assume that
G
is
(k+1)
-connected, which leaves the question open of whether these results still hold for graphs with exactly connectivity
k
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, some lemmas used in this paper are presented. In Section 3, we present the proof of Theorem 1.3. In Section 4, we present the proof of Theorem 1.4.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we first present two fundamental characterizations that provide necessary and sufficient conditions for a graph to be
k
-factor-critical and fractional
k
-factor-critical, respectively.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
and
k\geq 1
be an integer. Then
G
is
k
-factor-critical if and only if

n\equiv k\pmod{2}
and
o(G-S)\leq|S|-k
for any
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|\geq k
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
and
1\leq k\leq n-2
be an integer. Then
G
is fractional
k
-factor-critical if and only if

i(G-S)\leq|S|-k
for any
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|\geq k
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
n=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}+s
with
s\geq 1
. If
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}\geq 1
,
n_{2}\geq 3
and
n_{1}<n-s-t-1
, then

\rho(K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup K_{n_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n_{t}}))<\rho(K_{s}\vee(K_{n-s-t-1}\cup K_{3}\cup(t-2)K_{1})).

According to Lemma 2.3, we give the following critical lemma which generalizes the above lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
n=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}+s
with
s\geq 1
. If
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}\geq p\geq 1
,
n_{2}\geq 3
and
n_{1}<n-s-(t-2)p-3
, then

\rho(K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup K_{n_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n_{t}}))<\rho(K_{s}\vee(K_{n-s-(t-2)p-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(t-2)K_{p})).

Proof.

Let
G=K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup K_{n_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n_{t}})
and
G^{\prime}=K_{s}\vee(K_{n-s-(t-2)p-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(t-2)K_{p})
. The vertex set of
G
can be partitioned as
V(G)=V(K_{s})\cup V(K_{n_{1}})\cup V(K_{n_{2}})\cup\cdots\cup V(K_{n_{t}})
, where
V(K_{s})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{s}\}
and
V(K_{n_{i}})=\{u_{i1},u_{i2},\ldots,u_{in_{i}}\}
for
1\leq i\leq t
. Let

\displaystyle E_{1}=

\displaystyle\{u_{ik}u_{il}\mid 3\leq i\leq t,\ 1\leq k\leq n_{i}-p,\ n_{i}-p+1\leq l\leq n_{i}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{u_{2k}u_{2l}\mid 1\leq k\leq n_{2}-3,\ n_{2}-2\leq l\leq n_{2}\},

\displaystyle E_{2}=

\displaystyle\{u_{1k}u_{ij}\mid 1\leq k\leq n_{1},\ 3\leq i\leq t,\ 1\leq j\leq n_{i}-p\}

\displaystyle\cup\{u_{1k}u_{2j}\mid 1\leq k\leq n_{1},\ 1\leq j\leq n_{2}-3\},

\displaystyle E_{3}=

\displaystyle\{u_{ij}u_{kl}\mid 3\leq i\leq t-1,\ i+1\leq k\leq t;\ 1\leq j\leq n_{i}-p,\ 1\leq l\leq n_{k}-p\}

\displaystyle\cup\{u_{2j}u_{kl}\mid\ 3\leq k\leq t,\ 1\leq j\leq n_{2}-3,\ 1\leq l\leq n_{k}-p\}.

It is straightforward to check that
G+E_{2}+E_{3}-E_{1}\cong G^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
be the Perron vector of
A(G)
corresponding to
\rho(G)
, and let
x_{i}
denote the entry of
\mathbf{x}
corresponding to the vertex
v_{i}\in V(G)
. By symmetry, let
x_{r}=y
for all
r\in V(K_{s})
and
x_{r}=x_{i}
for all
r\in V(K_{n_{i}})
, where
1\leq i\leq t
. Then, from
A(G)\mathbf{x}=\rho(G)\mathbf{x}
, we get

\rho(G)x_{1}=sy+(n_{1}-1)x_{1},~\rho(G)x_{j}=sy+(n_{j}-1)x_{j}~(j=2,3,\ldots,t).

Then, we have

[\rho(G)-(n_{1}-1)]x_{1}=[\rho(G)-(n_{j}-1)]x_{j},~2\leq j\leq t.

Note that
K_{n_{1}}
and
K_{n_{j}}
are two proper subgraphs of
G
, we have
\rho(G)>\rho(K_{n_{1}})=n_{1}-1
and
\rho(G)>\rho(K_{n_{j}})=n_{j}-1
. Together with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}\geq p\geq 1
, we obtain
x_{1}\geq x_{j}
for
2\leq j\leq t
.
According to the Rayleigh quotient, we have

\displaystyle\rho(G^{\prime})-\rho(G)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{x}^{T}(A(G^{\prime})-A(G))\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=2\Bigl[n_{1}(n_{2}-3)x_{1}x_{2}+\sum_{j=3}^{t}n_{1}(n_{j}-p)x_{1}x_{j}-3(n_{2}-3)x_{2}^{2}-\sum_{j=3}^{t}p(n_{j}-p)x_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=3}^{t}(n_{j}-p)(n_{2}-3)x_{2}x_{j}+\sum_{i=3}^{t-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{t}(n_{i}-p)(n_{j}-p)x_{i}x_{j}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=2\Bigl[(n_{2}-3)(n_{1}x_{1}-3x_{2})x_{2}+\sum_{j=3}^{t}(n_{j}-p)(n_{1}x_{1}-px_{j})x_{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=3}^{t}(n_{j}-p)(n_{2}-3)x_{2}x_{j}+\sum_{i=3}^{t-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{t}(n_{i}-p)(n_{j}-p)x_{i}x_{j}\Bigr]

\displaystyle>0,

since
x_{1}\geq x_{j}\ (2\leq j\leq t)
,
n_{j}\geq p\geq 1\ (3\leq j\leq t)
and
3\leq n_{2}\leq n_{1}<n-s-(t-2)p-3
.

Thus, the result follows.
\square

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n\geq 8\delta-5k+20
, where
k\geq 1
and
\delta\geq k+2
. Then

\rho(K_{k+1}\vee(K_{n-\delta-4}\cup K_{3}\cup K_{\delta-k}))<\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})).

Proof.

Let
G=K_{k+1}\vee(K_{n-\delta-4}\cup K_{3}\cup K_{\delta-k})
. Then the vertex set of
G
can be partitioned as
V(G)=V(K_{k+1})\cup V(K_{n-\delta-4})\cup V(K_{3})\cup V(K_{\delta-k})
, where
V(K_{k+1})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k+1}\}
,
V(K_{n-\delta-4})=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n-\delta-4}\}
,
V(K_{3})=\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\}
and
V(K_{\delta-k})=\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{\delta-k}\}
. Suppose that

E_{1}=\{u_{i}w_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq\delta-k-1,\ 1\leq j\leq\delta-k\}\cup\{u_{i}z_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq\delta-k-1,\ 1\leq j\leq 3\}

and

E_{2}=\{w_{i}w_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq\delta-k-1,\ i+1\leq j\leq\delta-k\}.

Let
G^{\prime}=G+E_{1}-E_{2}
. Clearly,
G^{\prime}\cong K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})
.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be the Perron vector of
A(G)
corresponding to
\rho(G)
. By symmetry,
\mathbf{x}
takes the same value (say
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
,
x_{3}
and
x_{4}
) on the vertices of
V(K_{k+1}),V(K_{n-\delta-4}),V(K_{3})~and~V(K_{\delta-k})
, respectively. Then, by
A(G)\mathbf{x}=\rho(G)\mathbf{x}
, we have

\begin{cases}\rho x_{2}=(k+1)x_{1}+(n-\delta-5)x_{2},\\
\rho x_{3}=(k+1)x_{1}+2x_{3},\\
\rho x_{4}=(k+1)x_{1}+(\delta-k-1)x_{4},\end{cases}

which leads to

(\rho-\delta+k+1)(x_{2}-x_{4})=(n-2\delta+k-4)x_{2}>0,

because
n\geq 8\delta-5k+20
,
k\geq 1
and
\delta\geq k+2
. Note that
K_{n-\delta+k-3}
is a proper subgraph of
G
, then
\rho(G)>n-\delta+k-4>\delta-k-1
. Therefore,

x_{2}>x_{4},

and hence

\displaystyle\rho(G^{\prime})-\rho(G)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{x}^{T}(A(G^{\prime})-A(G))\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\delta-k}\sum_{i=1}^{\delta-k-1}x_{u_{i}}x_{w_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\delta-k-1}x_{u_{i}}x_{z_{j}}-\sum_{i=1}^{\delta-k-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\delta-k}x_{w_{i}}x_{w_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=2[(\delta-k)(\delta-k-1)x_{2}x_{4}+3(\delta-k-1)x_{2}x_{3}-\frac{(\delta-k)(\delta-k-1)}{2}x_{4}^{2}]

\displaystyle=2[x_{4}(\delta-k)(\delta-k-1)(x_{2}-\frac{x_{4}}{2})+3(\delta-k-1)x_{2}x_{3}]

\displaystyle>0.

The result follows.
\square

Above, we compare the spectral radius of the two kinds of extremal graphs. Now we compare their edge numbers.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}+s
with
s\geq 1
. If
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{t}\geq p\geq 1
,
n_{2}\geq 3
and
n_{1}<n-s-(t-2)p-3
, then

|E(K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup K_{n_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n_{t}}))|<|E(K_{s}\vee(K_{n-s-(t-2)p-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(t-2)K_{p}))|.

Proof.

Let
G=K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup K_{n_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n_{t}})
and
G^{\prime}=K_{s}\vee(K_{n-s-(t-2)p-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(t-2)K_{p})
. The vertex set of
G
can be partitioned as
V(G)=V(K_{s})\cup V(K_{n_{1}})\cup V(K_{n_{2}})\cup\cdots\cup V(K_{n_{t}})
, where
V(K_{s})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{s}\}
and
V(K_{n_{i}})=\{u_{i1},u_{i2},\ldots,u_{in_{i}}\}
for
1\leq i\leq t
. Let

\displaystyle E_{1}=

\displaystyle\{u_{ik}u_{il}\mid 3\leq i\leq t,\ 1\leq k\leq n_{i}-p,\ n_{i}-p+1\leq l\leq n_{i}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{u_{2k}u_{2l}\mid 1\leq k\leq n_{2}-3,\ n_{2}-2\leq l\leq n_{2}\},

\displaystyle E_{2}=

\displaystyle\{u_{1k}u_{ij}\mid 1\leq k\leq n_{1},\ 3\leq i\leq t,\ 1\leq j\leq n_{i}-p\}

\displaystyle\cup\{u_{1k}u_{2j}\mid 1\leq k\leq n_{1},\ 1\leq j\leq n_{2}-3\},

\displaystyle E_{3}=

\displaystyle\{u_{ij}u_{kl}\mid 3\leq i\leq t-1,\ i+1\leq k\leq t;\ 1\leq j\leq n_{i}-p,\ 1\leq l\leq n_{k}-p\}

\displaystyle\cup\{u_{2j}u_{kl}\mid\ 3\leq k\leq t,\ 1\leq j\leq n_{2}-3,\ 1\leq l\leq n_{k}-p\}.

It is straightforward to check that
G+E_{2}+E_{3}-E_{1}\cong G^{\prime}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle|E(G^{\prime})|-|E(G)|

\displaystyle=n_{1}(n_{2}-3)+\sum_{j=3}^{t}n_{1}(n_{j}-p)-3(n_{2}-3)-\sum_{j=3}^{t}p(n_{j}-p)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=3}^{t}(n_{j}-p)(n_{2}-3)+\sum_{i=3}^{t-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{t}(n_{i}-p)(n_{j}-p)

\displaystyle=(n_{2}-3)(n_{1}-3)+\sum_{j=3}^{t}(n_{j}-p)(n_{1}-p)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=3}^{t}(n_{j}-p)(n_{2}-3)+\sum_{i=3}^{t-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{t}(n_{i}-p)(n_{j}-p)

\displaystyle>0.

due to

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-\delta)x_{1}y_{3}}{\rho(\rho^{\prime}-2)}\left[(2n-2\delta-3s+3k-7)\rho^{\prime}-\rho\rho^{\prime}+\rho(n-2\delta+k-9)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-2(2n-2\delta-3s+3k-7)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{s(s-\delta)x_{1}y_{3}}{\rho(\rho^{\prime}-2)}\left[\rho^{\prime}(3n-4\delta-3s+4k-16-\rho)-2(2n-2\delta-3s+3k-7)\right]\quad(\text{since }\rho\geq\rho^{\prime})

\displaystyle>\frac{s(s-\delta)x_{1}y_{3}}{\rho(\rho^{\prime}-2)}\left[\rho^{\prime}(3n-4\delta-\frac{3(n+k-6)}{2}+4k-16-(n-1))-2(2n-2\delta-3s+3k-7)\right]

\displaystyle\quad(\text{since }\rho<n-1~and~s\leq\dfrac{n+k-6}{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-\delta)x_{1}y_{3}}{\rho(\rho^{\prime}-2)}\left[(\dfrac{n}{2}-4\delta+\dfrac{5}{2}k-6)\rho^{\prime}-2(2n-2\delta-3s+3k-7)\right]

\displaystyle>\frac{s(s-\delta)x_{1}y_{3}}{\rho(\rho^{\prime}-2)}\left[4(n-\delta+k-4)-2(2n-2\delta-3(\delta+1)+3k-7)\right]

\displaystyle\quad(\text{since }n\geq 8\delta-5k+20~and~\rho^{\prime}>n-\delta+k-4)

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-\delta)x_{1}y_{3}}{\rho(\rho^{\prime}-2)}(6\delta-2k+4)

\displaystyle>0\quad(\text{since }s\geq\delta+1\geq k+2),

a contradiction. Therefore, we have
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
, and it follows from (1) and (2) that

\rho(G)\leq\rho(G^{\prime})\leq\rho(G^{\prime\prime})<\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})).

Case 2.

s=\delta
.

By Lemma 2.4, we have

\rho(G^{\prime})\leq\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})),

with equality holding if and only if
G^{\prime}\cong K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})
. Combining this with (1), we conclude that

\rho(G)\leq\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})),

where the equality holds if and only if
G\cong K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})
. By Lemma 2.8, we have
K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})
is not
k
-factor-critical. Thus the result follows.

Case 3.

k+1\leq s\leq\delta-1
.

Let
G^{*}=K_{s}\vee(K_{n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(s-k)K_{\delta+1-s})
. Recall that
G
is a spanning subgraph of
G^{\prime}=K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup K_{n_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n_{s-k+2}})
, where
n_{i}\geq 3
,
n_{j}\geq 1
for
1\leq i\leq 2
,
3\leq j\leq s-k+2
,
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s-k+2}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{s-k+2}n_{i}=n-s
. Clearly,
n_{s-k+2}\geq\delta+1-s
because the minimum degree of
G^{\prime}
is at least
\delta
. By Lemma 2.4, we have

\rho(G^{\prime})\leq\rho(G^{*}),

where the equality holds if and only if
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},\dots,n_{s}-k+2)=(n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3,3,\delta+1-s,\dots,\delta+1-s)
.

If
s=k+1
, then
G^{*}=K_{k+1}\vee(K_{n-\delta-4}\cup K_{3}\cup K_{\delta-k})
. Combining Lemma 2.5, (1) and (5), we get

\rho(G)\leq\rho(G^{\prime})\leq\rho(G^{*})<\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})).

Thus, we consider
s\geq k+2
in the following. Assume that
\rho(G^{*})=\rho^{*}\geq n-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3
. Let
x
be the Perron vector of
A(G^{*})
. By symmetry,
x
takes the same values
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
, and
x_{4}
on the vertices of
V(K_{s})
,
V(K_{n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3})
,
V(K_{3})
and
V(K_{\delta+1-s})
, respectively. Then from
A(G^{*})x=\rho^{*}x
we get

\begin{cases}\rho^{*}x_{1}=(s-1)x_{1}+(n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3)x_{2}+3x_{3}+(s-k)(\delta+1-s)x_{4},\\
\rho^{*}x_{2}=sx_{1}+(n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-4)x_{2},\\
\rho^{*}x_{3}=sx_{1}+2x_{3},\\
\rho^{*}x_{4}=sx_{1}+(\delta-s)x_{4}.\end{cases}

Combining with the above, we yield that

\begin{cases}x_{2}=\dfrac{sx_{1}}{\rho^{*}-(n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-4)},\\[10.00002pt]
x_{3}=\dfrac{sx_{1}}{\rho^{*}-2},\\[10.00002pt]
x_{4}=\dfrac{sx_{1}}{\rho^{*}-\delta+s}.\end{cases}

Note that
n\geq\delta(\delta-k-1)^{2}-2\delta-1
,
s\geq k+2
and
\delta\geq s+1
. Then
\rho^{*}\geq n-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3>\delta+1
. Combining this with (6), we have

\displaystyle\rho^{*}+1

\displaystyle=s+(n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3)\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}+\frac{3x_{3}}{x_{1}}+(s-k)(\delta+1-s)\frac{x_{4}}{x_{1}}

\displaystyle=s+\frac{s(n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3)}{\rho^{*}-(n-s-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-4)}+\frac{3s}{\rho^{*}-2}+\frac{s(\delta+1-s)(s-k)}{\rho^{*}-\delta+s}

\displaystyle<s+\frac{s(n-s)}{s+1}\quad(\text{since }\rho^{*}\geq n-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3>\delta+1)

\displaystyle=n-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3-\frac{n-s-((s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3)(s+1)}{s+1}

\displaystyle\leq n-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3-\frac{\delta((\delta-k-1)^{2}-3)-((s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3)(s+1)}{s+1}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\delta-s-1}{s+1}\quad(\text{since }n\geq\delta(\delta-k-1)^{2}-2\delta-1)

\displaystyle\leq n-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3\quad(\text{since }\delta\geq s+1\text{ and }s\geq k+2)

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{*},

which is impossible. Therefore,

\displaystyle\rho^{*}

\displaystyle<n-(s-k)(\delta+1-s)-3

\displaystyle=n-\delta+k-4-((s-k-1)(\delta-s)-1)

\displaystyle\leq n-\delta+k-4\quad(\text{since }\delta\geq s+1\text{ and }s\geq k+2).

Since
K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})
contains
K_{n-\delta+k-3}
as a proper subgraph, we have
\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1}))>n-\delta+k-4
. Combining this with (1) and (5), we may conclude that

\rho(G)\leq\rho(G^{\prime})\leq\rho(G^{*})<\rho(K_{\delta}\vee(K_{n-2\delta+k-3}\cup K_{3}\cup(\delta-k)K_{1})).

This completes the proof.

\square
