 Release of evaluated minor U average prompt fission neutron
 multiplicities for ENDF/B-IX.0

 D. Neudecker, A.E. Lovell, N.A. Kleedtke, W.N. Fritsch, I. Stetcu, J. Alwin
 Los Alamos National Laboratory, Los Alamos, NM
 March 2026

 Abstract
 This report documents the release of evaluated 232–234,236 U prompt fission neutron multiplic-
 ities, ν p , for ENDF/B-IX.0. For each isotope, one evaluation out of four is chosen. Each chosen
 evaluation is based on experimental ν p and model values from the CGMF code. The chosen evalua-
 tions were those that agree best to differential experimental ν p and show the expected smoothness
 in the evaluated data. These evaluated data are then validated with respect to values of the
 effective neutron multiplication factor, keff , of ICSBEP assemblies. Several hundred assemblies
 that contain U isotopes were studied; namely assemblies containing mainly 233 U, intermediate,
 low or highly enriched uranium as core fuel. The studied assemblies span thermal to fast energy
 ranges. The chosen evaluated 232,234,236 U ν p have very little impact on the simulated keff values;
 the changes in simulated keff values with these new data are not larger than 12 pcm. The new
 evaluated 233 U ν p has a significant impact on 233 U assemblies: The mean bias in keff U233 of metal
 fast ICSBEP assemblies changed from -61 pcm in ENDF/B-VIII.1 to 158 pcm requiring correction
 via future changes in cross sections. The bias in U233-COMP-THERM and U233-SOL-THERM
 assemblies increased by about 60–70 pcm, while a 50 pcm decrease in bias was observed for the
 few U233-SOL-INTER assemblies.

1 Introduction
Here, we document which evaluation of minor uranium average prompt fission neutron multiplicities,
ν p , will be released to the “Cross Section Evaluation Working Group” (CSEWG) for the next U.S.
nuclear data library, ENDF/B-IX.0, and the reasoning behind this choice.
 This work started from an evaluation of 232–236 U ν p that was a Nuclear Criticality Safety Program
(NCSP) milestone for FY25 [1] that required: “Attempt a consistent nu-bar evaluation supported by
a model code to provide better nu-bar data for minor U-isotopes”. In addition to that, there are two
more related milestones. Namely: “In FY26, LANL will make improvements on the consistent nu-
bar evaluation, finalize minor U nu-bar evaluations, report covariances, format and validate nu-bars.
Develop consistent PFNS evaluation supported by a model code to better provide evaluated PFNS for
minor U isotopes in FY27-FY28.”
 This report documents the works that answers the FY26 milestone: It is shown in Section 2 which
evaluated 232–236 U ν p was chosen to be released to CSEWG for ENDF/B-IX.0 beta testing. The
related ENDF-6 formatted ν p mean values and covariances are provided in appendices A–D. Finally,
Section 3 shows validation results of the chosen evaluated ν p with respect to the effective neutron
multiplication factor of several ICSBEP [2] critical assemblies. A summary and conclusions are given
in Section 4.

2 Selection of evaluated data
This work started from an evaluation of 232–236 U ν p [1]. This evaluation included modeling with
the fission-event generator CGMF [3] that is consistent across the range of isotopes and a detailed
uncertainty quantification of all accepted experimental data. One major stumbling block was that
the evaluations shown in Ref. [1] differed systematically from part of the experimental ν p . New

 1

evaluations were performed including more advanced model parameterizations, but still this issue
persisted as can be seen in Figs. 3–6 for the evaluations titled “Eval. CGMF across isotope”. For
instance, this evaluation pulls far away from experimental 233,236 U ν p undermining our trust in the
evaluated parameters we obtained.
 It was suspected that the too strong model constraints across the chain of isotopes and/or incorrect
model systematics were pulling the evaluated data away from experimental ν p . Therefore, evaluations
were undertaken based on only experimental ν p as well as on experimental ν p and model values for
each isotope separately.
 In Figs. 2–5, four evaluations are shown:

  A. An evaluation obtained via generalized least squares that uses only experimental data (“Eval.
 exp. only” in Figs. 2–5).
  B. An evaluation obtained via Kalman filter fits of ARIADNE [4] using a CGMF prior (the prior
 parameter file was called “UIso CGMF optBaseline pBaseline.txt”) and the same experimental
 data as in bullet A. This fit was undertaken such that model parameters were fit simultaneously
 to experimental 232–234 U and 232–237 U ν p at once. The resulting evaluated model parameters
 were then used as input for CGMF to give evaluated ν p . The corresponding evaluated data are
 called in Figs. 2–5 “Eval. CGMF across isotope”). This fit leverages model correlations and
 experimental data across isotopes to inform the evaluation. However, due to the model stiffness,
 it is more difficult for the Kalman filter in ARIADNE to get close to experimental ν p of specific
 isotopes.
  C. The same prior as bullet B was fit except that model parameters of one isotope were fit at a
 time. So, we do not inform the fit for 232 U ν p by experimental 234 U ν p . The corresponding
 evaluated data are called in Figs. 2–5 “Eval. Kalman”. Contrary to bullet B, these are evaluated
 ν p directly coming out of Kalman which are often not quite as smooth as when we use Kalman-
 evaluated parameters as input for CGMF. By fitting experimental ν p of one isotope at a time,
 one has more flexibility to get evaluated data closer to the individual experimental ν p . However,
 one looses the model consistency across the chain of isotopes.
  D. The same as bullet C except that the model parameters coming out of the Kalman fit of bullet C
 were used as input to the code CGMF [3] to produce evaluated ν p values that are termed “Eval.
 CGMF” in Figs. 2–5.

For the purpose of clarity, the evaluations are named according to the legend entries in Figs. 2–5
henceforth.

 232
2.1 U
Choice: The “Eval. CGMF across isotope” evaluation was chosen as it is based on other
experimental data. With that choice, we implicitly state that we don’t trust Jaffey, 1970
data.
 There is only one experimental data point by Jaffey et al. [5] used for the evaluation. W. Fritsch,
who did the experimental uncertainty quantification [6], quoted Jaffey’s article [5] that stated that the
detector drifted significantly during the measurement. The authors did take counts from measurements
where little drift was observed, but it does call the experimental value into question.
 The experimental value is very far from ENDF/B-VIII.1. ENDF/B-VIII.1 was adopted from a
JENDL-4.0 [7] evaluation. Their documentation is puzzling because it states that they adopt Jaffey,
1970 data at thermal but then give a (reasonable) value of 2.5 n/f based on parameter systematics
rather than 3.1 n/f. The JENDL-4.0 paper does not give more details. So, the JENDL-4.0 evaluators
seemed to not have fully trusted the thermal value measured by Jaffey, 1970.
 Also, if one believe Jaffey’s ν p value, then 232 U ν p would be by a factor of more than 0.7 n/f larger
than any other U isotope shown here at thermal. There is no clear physics reason why 232 U ν p at
thermal would be so different than any other U isotope shown here. Finally, if we forward-propagate
the parameters related to the evaluation “Eval. Kalman” (they agree best to Jaffey’s data in Fig. 2)
with CGMF, the resulting average total kinetic energy is significantly lower than it is for 233,234,236 U,
as shown in Fig. 1. It is also significantly lower than the available experimental data by Ramaswami

experimental ν p for 233,234 U. However, one might want to consider that the related 233 U
ν p “Eval. CGMF across isotope” does not agree well to 233 U experimental data.

 233
2.2 U
Choice: We chose a smoothed version of the “Eval. Kalman” evaluation as it is closest
to the Neutron Data Standards at thermal and other experimental data.
 There are many trustworthy experimental 233 U ν p . Also, the thermal 233 U ν t (the average total
fission neutron multiplicity) is a “Thermal Constant” of the Neutron Data Standard [8]. Its cur-
rent value is 2.487 n/f. When one subtracts the delayed fission neutron multiplicity of thermal from
ENDF/B-VIII.1 (.0067), one obtain 2.4803 n/f. The “Eval. Kalman” is closest to this value with
a value of 2.486 n/f (larger by 0.0057 n/f at thermal than Standard.), while the “Eval. Exp. only”
is slightly higher with 2.493 n/f and “Eval. CGMF” is slightly smaller at thermal with 2.4732 n/f
(smaller by 0.0071 n/f at thermal than Standard).
 The “Eval. CGMF across isotope” is systematically low—a trend that stays true compared to
experimental data across all energies. Hence, the latter evaluation is rejected. “Eval. CGMF” is
smoother than “Eval. Kalman” at 3 MeV but does not follow data as closely from 3.5–10 MeV.
 Reasoning for replacing ENDF/B-VIII.1: ENDF/B-VIII.1 is systematically lower than
experimental ν p from 3–4.5 MeV and 1–2.5 MeV. This difference might stem from in-
tentional tweaking of evaluated nuclear data to have good agreement when using 233 U
nuclear data for simulating ICSBEP critical assembly benchmarks [2]. However, that
disagreement is a concern and might mask changes necessary in other nuclear data (e.g.,
cross sections or prompt fission neutron spectra) to get good agreement in benchmarks
with the new 233 U LANL file. Therefore, we propose to remove these ν p tweaks with this
new evaluations to allow subsequent changes in cross sections and spectra.

2.3 U
Choice: We chose the “Eval. CGMF across isotope” evaluation as it is also based on
other experimental data (primarily 233 U ν p ) and is smooth.
 There is only one experimental data set by Mather et al. [9]. These data have high statistical
uncertainty but are well described and measured. There are no obvious issues in those data [6].
 The “Eval. CGMF across isotope” in Fig. 4 trends low like all other evaluations obtained from
these systematics but agrees with three out of four data points. The “Eval. exp. only” and “Eval.
Kalman” show unphysical structure that rule them out. “Eval. CGMF” is systematically lower than
the “Eval. Kalman” it is based on which is unstable. Based on the instabilities observed in “Eval.
Kalman”, we choose “Eval. CGMF across isotope”.
 Reasoning for replacing ENDF/B-VIII.1: The ENDF/B-VIII.1 ν p (from a 2003 eval-
uation by Maslov) evaluation grid is very sparse with only 7 energy points (3 data points
between thermal and 5 MeV) that won’t capture all physics. One could question the
new evaluation as the related 233 U ν p evaluation is systematically low compared to ex-
perimental data.

2.4 U
Choice: We chose a smoothed version of the “Eval. Kalman” evaluation as it is close
to experimental data and is smoother than the evaluation “Eval. exp. only”. There are
only two experimental data sets by two groups that worked together [10, 11]. Both data sets are well
documented and are trustworthy given their documentation [6]. The “Eval. CGMF across isotope”
and “Eval. CGMF” evaluated data trend systematically low compared to the experimental data in
Fig. 5 and are rejected. Also, Fig. 6 shows that the related 235 U ν p evaluation (“Eval. CGMF across
isotope”) that is tied through common evaluated parameters to the “Eval. CGMF across isotope”
236
 U ν p is far from the 235 U experimental data which is very well measured.
 Reasoning for replacing ENDF/B-VIII.1: The ENDF/B-VIII.1 ν p and our current
proposed evaluation are close. So, in principle one could leave the old evaluation, but an
update after 20+ years might be good.

3 Performance in simulations of keff of ICBSEP critical assem-
 blies
Below, validation of the chosen 232–234,236 U ν p with effective neutron multiplication factors, keff , of
selected ICSBEP critical assemblies [2] is shown. To this end, the new ν values were cut into ENDF/B-
VIII.1 files. As of March 2026, the NNDC gitlab file 232,234,236 U have not changed from the ENDF/B-
VIII.1 release and can be directly replaced. 233 U ν p will be released as part of the LANL ENDF/B-IX.0
file.
 The following assumptions were made during the file assembly process:
 • The new 232,234,236 U ν p were added in the entire energy range and linearly extrapolated from
 20 to 30 MeV and from thermal down to 1.0−11 MeV. We chose to include these evaluations
 for 232,234,236 U into the files from thermal to 30 MeV, because (a) there is no RRR (resolved
 resonance range) evaluation possible for any of these ν p due to lack of experimental RRR ν p ,
 and (b) thermal is well-described by GLS/ CGMF.
 • The new 233 U ν p was added from 0.5 to 20 MeV. At thermal, the Neutron Data Standards
 value was added in for the average prompt neutron multiplicity by reducing it for the delayed
 component, because the Standard observable is actually the average total neutron multiplicity.

ENDF/B-VIII.1 provided for 232,233,234,236 U total, delayed and prompt multiplicities with different
grids and number of datapoints for all three observables. Delayed was carried over unchanged from
ENDF/B-VIII.1. Therefore, total was replaced to be the sum of delayed and prompt.

All these files were incorporated into ENDF/B-VIII.1 in ENDF-6 format and then processed into ACE
format with NJOY [12]. The keff values shown in Table 1 were calculated otherwise with ENDF/B-
VIII.1 using MCNP-6.2 [13].
 In Table 1, several hundreds of assemblies were studied that contain U isotopes, namely containing
mainly 233 U (starting with “U233”), intermediate (“IEU”), low (“LEU”) and highly enriched (“HEU”)
uranium as core fuel. The studied assemblies span thermal to fast energy ranges. We studied so many
assemblies as we were changing ν p of several U isotopes at once, and wanted to make sure we understand
the changes they inflict on a large space of U assemblies.
 The chosen evaluated 232,234,236 U ν p have very little impact on the simulated keff values. The
changes in simulated keff values with these new data are not larger than 12 pcm for any of the studied
assemblies. Hence, these new evaluated 232,234,236 U ν p could be accepted for ENDF/B-IX.0 without
causing any major impact on the simulation of ICSBEP uranium critical assemblies.
 However, the new evaluated 233 U ν p has a significant impact on 233 U assemblies as can be seen
both in Tables 1–2 and Figs. 7–10. The mean bias in keff U233 metal fast ICSBEP assemblies changed
from -61 pcm in ENDF/B-VIII.1 to 158 pcm (see also bottom of Fig. 7). This clearly points to the fact
that we need to counter-balance this change via future changes in cross sections. Right now, a new
233
 U cross section evaluation is ongoing at LANL and new prompt fission neutron spectra (PFNS)
are expected from the Chi-Nu team that will factor into future evaluations. Such updated nuclear
data will change keff as well. Hence, judgment on whether these new evaluated 233 U ν p should be
accepted by the community or not, should be passed once all pieces (cross sections, PFNS and ν p ) can
be studied at the same time.
 The bias in U233-COMP-THERM and U233-SOL-THERM (Figs. 7 top and 9) assemblies increased
by about 60–70 pcm. On the other had a 50 pcm decrease in bias was observed for the few U233-
SOL-INTER assemblies (Fig. 10). While such a change in bias is well within the experimental keff
uncertainties, it is noteworthy that changing the thermal 233 U ν p to be in line with the Neutron Data
Standard, led to a decrease in keff experiment-simulation agreement. As Neutron Data Standards
evaluations are well supported by accurately measured experimental data of many connected isotopes,
this could point to issues in other thermal cross sections or PFNS.

4 Summary and conclusions
This report documents the work that successfully answered a FY26 NCSP milestone. The milestone
required: “234,236 U: In FY26, LANL will make improvements on the consistent nu-bar evaluation, fi-
nalize minor U nu-bar evaluations, report covariances, format and validate nu-bars.” At the same time,
this report also documents the release of evaluated 232–234,236 U prompt fission neutron multiplicities,
ν p , to the CSEWG community for potential inclusion in ENDF/B-IX.0.
 As a first step, we undertook several 232–234,236 U ν p evaluations. Some of these evaluations included
model systematics across varying isotope ranges or were based exclusively on experimental data. From
these evaluations we chose the ones that agreed best to differential experimental data while maintaining
the smoothness expected from ν p . The chosen evaluations were all based on experimental ν p and model
values from the fission event generator CGMF.
 These evaluated data were validated with respect to values of the effective neutron multiplication
factor, keff , of ICSBEP uranium assemblies spanning the thermal to the fast energy ranges. Assemblies
were included that had as their core materials 233 U, highly, intermediate and low enriched uranium.
The impact of the new 232,234,236 U ν p is well within the Monte Carlo statistics uncertainties for most
assemblies. The changes in the simulated keff values were not larger than 12 pcm for any of the new

 However, larger changes have been observed, when the new evaluated 233 U ν p were used for sim-
ulating 233 U assemblies. The mean bias in keff U233 metal fast ICSBEP assemblies changed from
-61 pcm in ENDF/B-VIII.1 to 158 pcm. The bias in U233-COMP-THERM and U233-SOL-THERM
assemblies increased by about 60–70 pcm, while a 50 pcm decrease in bias was observed for the few
U233-SOL-INTER assemblies.
 This significant increase in bias in U233-MET-FAST assemblies requires to be counter-balanced
via changes in cross sections in order for it to be adopted by CSEWG for future ENDF/B libraries. In
previous years, LANL undertook new evaluations of 233 U cross sections in response to newly measured
capture to fission ratio data with DANCE [14] that could help in reducing that bias. In addition to
that, the Chi-Nu project recently measured 233 U prompt fission neutron spectra (PFNS). A new PFNS
evaluation could also potentially help to reduce the observed bias.
 The evaluated 232,234,236 U ν p and their covariance will be directly submitted to the CSEWG col-
laboration. The 233 U ν p will be submitted as part of the new 233 U LANL file once all cross section
and PFNS updates have been included and tested.
 We will further analyze the average total kinetic energy predicted by CGMF using model parameters
fitted to experimental 232–236 U ν p . This analysis of the parameterization of the average total kinetic
will serve as input for a FY27-28 NCSP milestone to evaluate PFNS for minor U isotopes. There
are no experimental data for 232,234,236,237 U PFNS in EXFOR [15]. Experimental 233 U PFNS are
only available for incident neutron energies of up to 0.5 MeV [16]. Hence, Chi-Nu 233 U PFNS will be
pivotal for the 233 U PFNS evaluations. For all other minor U isotope PFNS, we will have to rely on
model calculations that in turn depends on parameter systematics fit to other observables (such as
ν p ) across a chain of isotopes. This report shows that a good parameterization for the average total
kinetic energy that holds up across the chain of isotopes will need additional work.
