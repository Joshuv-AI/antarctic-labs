1.
Introduction

Tropical (max-plus) mathematics replaces the classical operations of addition and multiplication by

a\oplus b:=\max\{a,b\},\qquad a\otimes b:=a+b,

typically on the set
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
(with additive identity
\varepsilon=-\infty
and multiplicative identity
0
).
This “max–plus” algebra provides an effective linear-algebraic language for problems that are nonlinear over fields but become linear over
\mathbb{T}
, with applications ranging from discrete-event systems and scheduling to optimization and dynamical systems
(see, for example,
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
1
]
).
In this setting,
n\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{T}
form a semiring under tropical matrix addition (entrywise
\oplus
) and tropical matrix multiplication (using
\oplus
and
\otimes
),
and the resulting “linear” theory is tightly intertwined with weighted directed graphs:
to a matrix
A=(a_{ij})
one associates a digraph with an edge
i\to j
whenever
a_{ij}\neq\varepsilon
.
Many structural properties of tropical matrices admit graph-theoretic interpretations, and spectral notions are governed by directed cycles and their weights
[
2
,
3
]
.

A basic limitation of
\mathbb{T}
is that
\oplus
is idempotent, so “ties” (multiple maximizers) are collapsed, and there are no additive inverses.
Supertropical algebra, initiated by Izhakian and collaborators, enriches tropical algebra by adjoining
ghost
elements that record such degeneracies and restore a more robust algebraic structure for polynomials, matrices, and linear algebra
[
8
,
9
,
7
]
.
Motivated by these developments, in this paper we work with a concrete supertropical extension
\mathbb{T}[i]
obtained by adjoining a central symbol
i
with
i^{2}=0
and distinguishing a ghost (“zero”) ideal
\Phi\subset\mathbb{T}[i]
(defined precisely in §2).
This framework is well-suited for formulating matrix-theoretic notions such as nilpotency and for exploiting the close connection between tropical products and directed walks in graphs.

The purpose of the present note is to prove an analogue of
Lie’s theorem
in this supertropical setting.
Recall that, over an algebraically closed field, Lie’s classical theorem asserts that every finite-dimensional solvable Lie algebra of matrices can be simultaneously upper triangularized; in particular, a nilpotent Lie algebra of matrices is simultaneously strictly upper triangular in a suitable basis
[
6
]
.
In tropical and supertropical algebra one cannot form the usual commutator
AB-BA
, so one must choose a substitute for the Lie bracket.
Here we consider the bilinear bracket on
M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
given by

[A,B]\;:=\;A\otimes B\;\oplus\;B\otimes A,

and we call a
\mathbb{T}[i]
-linear subspace
G\subseteq M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
a (supertropical) Lie algebra if it is closed under this operation.
We focus on the nilpotent case, defined via the lower central series in the natural way.

Our main result shows that nilpotent supertropical Lie algebras admit a simultaneous strict upper-triangular form after a permutation change of coordinates (see Theorem
3.3
). More precisely, we prove that if
G\subseteq M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
is nilpotent, then there exists a permutation matrix
P
such that

P^{-1}\otimes G\otimes P\;\subseteq\;\mathcal{U}_{n},

where
\mathcal{U}_{n}
denotes the set of strictly upper triangular matrices (equivalently, every element of
G
becomes strictly upper triangular after relabeling the basis).
This can be viewed as a tropical/supertropical counterpart of the strict upper-triangularization conclusion for nilpotent Lie algebras in the classical theory
[
6
]
. As a corollary, the same conclusion holds for nilpotent Lie algebras of max–plus matrices over
\mathbb{T}
(Corollary
3.4
).

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1

(Max–plus semifield)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
with

a\oplus b=\max\{a,b\},\qquad a\otimes b=a+b

(where
+
is the usual addition on
\mathbb{R}
). The additive identity is
\varepsilon=-\infty
and
the multiplicative identity is 0.

Definition 2.2
.

The super-tropical extension of
\mathbb{T}
is defined as follows. Adjoin a central symbol
i
satisfying
i^{2}=0
. Set

\mathbb{T}[i]=\{\,a+ib:\ a,b\in\mathbb{T}\,\},

where the display “
a+ib
” denotes pairing (not tropical addition).
For
x=a+ib
and
y=c+id
, define

x\oplus y=(a\oplus c)+i(b\oplus d),

x\otimes y=\bigl((a\otimes c)\oplus(b\otimes d)\bigr)+i\bigl((b\otimes c)\oplus(a\otimes d)\bigr).

The additive zero is
\varepsilon=\varepsilon+i\varepsilon
.

Observation 2.3
.

If
x,y\in\mathbb{T}[i]
and
x\otimes y=\varepsilon
, then
x=\varepsilon
or
y=\varepsilon
.
In particular, if
x\neq\varepsilon
and
y\neq\varepsilon
, then
x\otimes y\neq\varepsilon
.

Definition 2.4
.

Dfine the set
\Phi=\{\,a+ia:\ a\in\mathbb{T}\,\}\subset\mathbb{T}[i]
. We regard
\Phi
as the “zero” set. Note that
\varepsilon=\varepsilon+i\varepsilon\in\Phi
.

Observation 2.5
.

We have the following facts for the above mentioned zero set.

(a)

If
x\in\Phi
and
y\in\mathbb{T}[i]
, then
x\otimes y\in\Phi
and
y\otimes x\in\Phi
. Moreover, if
x,y\in\Phi
, then
x\oplus y\in\Phi
.

(b)

If
x,y\in\mathbb{T}[i]
and
x\otimes y=\varepsilon
, then
x=\varepsilon
or
y=\varepsilon
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
. We say
A
is nilpotent, if
A^{k}=\mathcal{E}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathcal{E}
denotes the all-
\varepsilon
‘zero’ matrix.

Definition 2.7
.

For
A=(a_{ij})\in M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
, the directed graph
G_{A}
has vertex set

V=\{1,\dots,n\}
and a directed edge
i\to j
iff
a_{ij}\neq\varepsilon
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
. Then
A
is nilpotent if and only if
G_{A}
is a directed acyclic graph (DAG), including no self-loops. Further, spectrum of
A
is
\{\varepsilon\}
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
A^{k}=\mathcal{E}
for some
k
. If
G_{A}
had a directed cycle

v_{0}\to v_{1}\to\cdots\to v_{m-1}\to v_{0}
, then for every
r\geq 1
there is a directed walk
of length
mr
from
v_{0}
to itself obtained by repeating the cycle
r
times.
In the tropical matrix product, the
(v_{0},v_{0})
-entry of
A^{mr}
is an
\oplus
-sum of

\otimes
-products along all directed walks of length
mr
; in particular it contains the
product of the
mr
edge-labels along the repeated cycle.
Since each cycle edge corresponds to a non-
\varepsilon
entry of
A
, Observation
2.3

implies that this product is non-
\varepsilon
, hence
(A^{mr})_{v_{0}v_{0}}\neq\varepsilon
.
This contradicts
A^{k}=\mathcal{E}
. Thus
G_{A}
is acyclic.

(
\Leftarrow
) Conversely, assume
G_{A}
is a DAG. Since
G_{A}
is finite, it has a maximal
directed path length
L
. Then there is no directed walk of length
L+1
.
But
(A^{L+1})_{pq}
is precisely the
\oplus
-sum of
\otimes
-weights of all directed walks
of length
L+1
from
p
to
q
; hence
(A^{L+1})_{pq}=\varepsilon
for all
p,q
.
Therefore
A^{L+1}=\mathcal{E}
, so
A
is nilpotent.
∎

Definition 2.9
.

[
4
]

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite directed graph, with
|V|=n
and edge set
E
. A
topological ordering

\preceq
of
G
is a bijection
\pi:V\rightarrow\{1,\dots,n\}
(an ordering of the vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
)
such that for every edge
v_{i}\to v_{j}
in
E
, with distinct vertices
v_{i},v_{j}
, we have
\pi(v_{i})<\pi(v_{j})
(each pair of edge points
moves forward in the ordering).

Proposition 2.10
.

[(Theorem
14-4
of
[
4
]
)]
A finite directed graph admits a topological ordering if and only if it is acyclic. In that case, there exists a bijection

\ell:V\to\{1,\dots,n\}
such that every edge
u\to v
satisfies
\ell(u)<\ell(v)
.

Definition 2.11
.

For
A,B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
, define the Lie bracket as
[A,B]=AB\oplus BA
. A linear subspace
\mathcal{G}\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
is called a Lie algebra, if for every
A,B\in\mathcal{G}
,
[A,B]\in\mathcal{G}
. Further, a linear subspace
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{G}
is called an ideal, if for any
X\in\mathcal{G}
, and
A\in\mathcal{I}
,
[X,A]\in\mathcal{I}
.

Definition 2.12

(Lower central series and nilpotent Lie algebra)
.

Define

D^{0}(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{G},\qquad D^{k}(\mathcal{G})=[\,\mathcal{G},\ D^{k-1}(\mathcal{G})\,]\quad(k\geq 1).

We say
\mathcal{G}
is
nilpotent
if
D^{m}(\mathcal{G})=\{\mathcal{E}\}
for some
m
.

Remark:
In the super-tropical Lie algebra framework as well, nilpotency implies solvability. By letting the derived series
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=\mathcal{G}
,
\mathcal{G}^{(k+1)}=[\mathcal{G}^{(k)},\mathcal{G}^{(k)}]
, the bracket monotonicity (if
U_{1}\subseteq U_{2}
and
V_{1}\subseteq V_{2}
are
\mathbb{T}[i]
-linear subspaces, then
[U_{1},V_{1}]\ \subseteq\ [U_{2},V_{2}]
) gives

\mathcal{G}^{(k)}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{k}(\mathcal{G})
for all
k
. Hence if
\mathcal{D}_{m}(\mathcal{G})=\{\mathcal{E}\}
, then
\mathcal{G}^{(m)}=\{\mathcal{E}\}
.
For context, the same implication holds in the classical theory of Lie algebras and groups.

Example 2.13
.

(a)

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
be nilpotent. Then the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{T}[i]_{0}[A]
is a nilpotent algebra.

(b)

Define
\mathcal{U}_{n}\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
to be the class of strictly upper triangular matrices. Then
\mathcal{U}_{n}
is a nilpotent algebra.

3.
Lie’s Theorem for Supertropical Algebra

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
be a nilpotent Lie algebra.
Then every
A\in\mathcal{G}
is a nilpotent matrix.

Proof.

Fix
A\in\mathcal{G}
. Note that

[A,A]=A\otimes A\oplus A\otimes A=A^{2},

since
\oplus
is idempotent. Hence
A^{2}\in D^{1}(\mathcal{G})
.

Inductively, assume
A^{k}\in D^{k-1}(\mathcal{G})
. Then

[A,A^{k}]=A\otimes A^{k}\oplus A^{k}\otimes A=A^{k+1}\oplus A^{k+1}=A^{k+1},

so
A^{k+1}\in D^{k}(\mathcal{G})
.

If
D^{m}(\mathcal{G})=\{\mathcal{E}\}
, then
A^{m+1}\in D^{m}(\mathcal{G})
forces
A^{m+1}=\mathcal{E}
,
hence
A
is nilpotent.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
be a nilpotent Lie algebra, and let
A,B\in\mathcal{G}
.
Then there do not exist vertices
v\neq w
such that there is a directed path from
v
to
w

in
G_{A}
and a directed path from
w
to
v
in
G_{B}
.

Proof.

Assume, for contradiction, that such
v,w
exist. Let
l
be the length of a directed path
from
v
to
w
in
G_{A}
and
m
the length of a directed path from
w
to
v
in
G_{B}
.
Then
(A^{l})_{vw}\neq\varepsilon
and
(B^{m})_{wv}\neq\varepsilon
by the path-expansion property of tropical
matrix products and Observation
2.3
.

Consider
C=[A^{l},B^{m}]=A^{l}\otimes B^{m}\oplus B^{m}\otimes A^{l}
.
In the
(v,v)
-entry of
A^{l}\otimes B^{m}
, one term is

(A^{l})_{vw}\otimes(B^{m})_{wv}\neq\varepsilon,

again by Observation
2.3
. Hence
C_{vv}\neq\varepsilon
.
Therefore
G_{C}
has a self-loop at
v
, so
C
is not nilpotent.
But
A^{l},B^{m}\in\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}
is closed under brackets, hence
C\in\mathcal{G}
,
contradicting Lemma
3.1
. Thus no such pair
v,w
exists.
∎

As a result of above Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
, we can define a partial order in any nilpotent algebra
\mathcal{G}
. We say
A\geq B
, if every path in
B
is a sub-path in
A
. Equivalently (since paths of length
1
are the edges),
E(G_{B})\subseteq E(G_{A})
. This guarantees a dominant element in
\mathcal{G}
.

Theorem 3.3

(Lie’s Theorem for supertropical algebra)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq M_{n}(\mathbb{T}[i])
be a nilpotent Lie algebra.
Then there exists a permutation matrix
P
such that

P^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{G}\otimes P\ \subseteq\ \mathcal{U}_{n}.

Equivalently,
\mathcal{G}
is simultaneously conjugate to a Lie subalgebra of strictly upper
triangular matrices.

Proof.

First let us construct the dominant element in
\mathcal{G}
. For each ordered pair
(i,j)
with
i\neq j
such that there exists some
A\in\mathcal{G}
with

a_{ij}\neq\varepsilon
, choose one witness matrix
A^{(i,j)}\in\mathcal{G}
having that entry non-
\varepsilon
.
Define

C=\bigoplus_{(i,j)}A^{(i,j)}\in\mathcal{G},

since
\mathcal{G}
is closed under
\oplus
.
By construction,

c_{ij}\neq\varepsilon\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\,A\in\mathcal{G}\ \text{with}\ a_{ij}\neq\varepsilon.

Hence every edge that appears in some
G_{A}
(
A\in\mathcal{G}
) appears in
G_{C}
, i.e.

E(G_{A})\subseteq E(G_{C})\qquad\text{for all }A\in\mathcal{G}.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is nilpotent, Lemma
3.1
implies
C
is nilpotent.
Therefore,
G_{C}
is a DAG by Theorem
2.8
.
By Proposition
2.10
, there exists a labeling
\ell:\{1,\dots,n\}\to\{1,\dots,n\}

such that each edge
u\to v
of
G_{C}
satisfies
\ell(u)<\ell(v)
.
Let
P
be the permutation matrix implementing this relabeling.

Fix
A\in\mathcal{G}
. If
p\to q
is an edge of
G_{A}
, then
p\to q
is an edge of
G_{C}
, hence
\ell(p)<\ell(q)
. Thus, in the relabeled matrix
P^{-1}\otimes A\otimes P
, every non-
\varepsilon
entry lies strictly
above the diagonal, i.e.
P^{-1}\otimes A\otimes P\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
.
Since
A
was arbitrary,
P^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{G}\otimes P\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{n}
.
∎

Corollary 3.4

(Lie’s Theorem for max-plus algebra)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{T})
be a nilpotent Lie algebra, then there is a labeling such that
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{U}_{n}
. In other words, there exists a
P\in Gl_{n}(\mathbb{T})
, such that
P^{-1}\mathcal{G}P\subset\mathcal{U}_{n}
.

The proof follows the same argument as the proof of the above Theorem
3.3
.
