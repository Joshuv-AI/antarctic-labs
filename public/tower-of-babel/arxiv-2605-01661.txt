Higher-derivative 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 and 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 supersymmetric Maxwell-Chern-Simons theories at one loop in superspace
F. S. Gama

Abstract
We define a higher-derivative generalization of Maxwell-Chern-Simons theory in 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 and 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 superspaces. In particular, the chosen higher-derivative operator is a polynomial function of the d’Alembertian of arbitrary degree, and it is introduced exclusively in the gauge sector. The main goal is to explicitly compute the one-loop quantum corrections to the superfield effective potential for these theories. This is carried out by means of background field quantization in a higher-derivative R ξ R_{\xi} gauge. The effective potential is obtained in closed form and expressed in terms of the roots of polynomial functions.



I
Introduction

The Maxwell-Chern-Simons (MCS) theory is an extension of three-dimensional Maxwell electrodynamics obtained through the inclusion of a Chern-Simons term. This gives rise to a new mechanism for generating mass for the electromagnetic field while preserving gauge symmetry. This is fundamentally different from the Higgs mechanism, since the mass emerges from a term of purely topological origin
Siegel
;
Schonfeld
;
JT
. In addition to this property, the study of MCS theory is also motivated by its role as a useful theoretical laboratory for the investigation of dualities. Indeed, shortly after the formulation of the MCS theory, it was shown in
TPN
;
DJ
that the self-dual and MCS models are dual to each other, that is, they provide distinct mathematical descriptions of the same physical content. Since then, several studies of this duality have been carried out involving different extensions of the MCS theory, such as noncommutative
noncom
, Lorentz-violating
Lorentzviol
, and supersymmetric ones
SUSY-dual
.

At the same time, the study of higher-derivative (HD) field theories is a recurrent and relevant theme in physics. The main reason is that these types of theories perform better than standard ones with regard to classical and quantum divergences
newtonsing
;
QG
. Indeed, this improved ultraviolet behavior also allows the use of higher covariant derivatives to regularize standard field theories
regularization
. Additionally, HD theories arise naturally in different contexts, such as semiclassical gravity theories
Shapiro
, string models
string
, and the effective field theory approach
EFT
. As for the MCS theory, there is a considerable body of literature on extensions of this theory involving higher derivatives
HDMCS
. For example, issues related to stability and Ostrogradsky ghosts were analyzed in
PDS
for an HD version of the MCS theory obtained by adding the standard Maxwell term to a HD Chern-Simons term. This theory was also studied in
ANPRS
, where the authors investigated the properties of microcausality and perturbative unitarity up to one-loop order, while in
MP
a Hamiltonian analysis of the theory was carried out in order to construct the gauge generator and identify the independent gauge symmetry.

In
GNP
and
GGNPS
, we formulated generic three-dimensional HD gauge theories in
\mathcal{N}=1
and
\mathcal{N}=2
superspaces, respectively, which, for appropriate choices of the HD operators, reduce to supersymmetric generalizations of the HD MCS theory. For these generic theories, we explicitly calculated the one-loop superfield effective potential (EP), due to its importance as a theoretical instrument for studying spontaneous symmetry breaking induced by radiative corrections
breaking
, the restoration of symmetry at high temperatures
restoration
, and the decay of the false vacuum
falsevacuum
. However, a major limitation of Ref.
GNP
is that we did not find a final expression for the EP that applies to an HD MCS theory, while Ref.
GGNPS
also has a limitation because, although it applies to an HD MCS theory, the final EP was obtained in an integral representation rather than in closed form in terms of elementary or standard special functions. Due to these limitations, our goals in the present work are (i) to determine the one-loop superfield EP for an HD MCS theory in
\mathcal{N}=1
superspace, thereby filling this gap in the literature, and (ii) to obtain, for the first time, the one-loop superfield EP in closed form and expressed in terms of the roots of polynomial functions for an HD MCS theory in
\mathcal{N}=2
superspace. To achieve these goals, we perform the calculations in an HD
R_{\xi}
gauge and obtain the EP by evaluating the functional traces directly. This methodology is completely different from the one used in
GNP
;
GGNPS
, where the calculations were performed in an HD Fermi-Feynman gauge and the EP was obtained by evaluating one-loop supergraphs.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II

\mathcal{N}=1

III

\mathcal{N}=2
superspace and compute its one-loop superfield EP. Finally, in Sec.
IV

\mathcal{N}=1

In the

Siegel
;

S_{\mathcal{N}=1}=\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\int d^{5}z\left[W^{\alpha}f(\Box)W_{\alpha}+mA^{\alpha}f(\Box)W_{\alpha}\right]+\int d^{5}z\bar{\Phi}\nabla^{2}\Phi,

In the background field approach

;

(2)

Therefore, upon substituting Eqs. (

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}=e^{i\omega}\Phi;\ \ \bar{\Phi}^{\prime}=e^{-i\omega}\bar{\Phi};\ \ \phi^{\prime}=e^{i\omega}\phi;\ \ \bar{\phi}^{\prime}=e^{-i\omega}\bar{\phi};\ \ a_{\alpha}^{\prime}=a_{\alpha};

For the computation of the one-loop correction to the EP, it is sufficient to expand (

\begin{split}S^{(2)}_{1}=&\int d^{5}z\bigg\{\frac{1}{4g^{2}}a^{\alpha}\left[\left(\Box f(\Box)-2g^{2}\left|\Phi\right|^{2}\right){\delta_{\alpha}}^{\beta}+f(\Box)i{\partial_{\alpha}}^{\beta}D^{2}+mf(\Box)D^{\beta}D_{\alpha}\right]a_{\beta}\\

For our purposes, it is convenient to choose the following parametrization for the complex matter superfields:

\Phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\Phi_{1}+i\Phi_{2}\right)\ \ ;\ \ \phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\phi_{1}+i\phi_{2}\right),

where
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}

&+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{n}D^{2}\phi_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{nm}\Phi_{n}a^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\phi_{m}\bigg\},\end{split}

(9)

where

is the Levi-Civita symbol.
Moreover, the mass parameter

M_{A}^{2}:=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\Phi_{n}\Phi_{n}=g^{2}\left|\Phi\right|^{2}.

(10)

It is also convenient to eliminate from (

9

This gauge-fixing function is invariant under global background transformations and, therefore, so is

\begin{split}S_{GF1}=&\int d^{5}z\bigg[\frac{1}{4g^{2}}a^{\alpha}\Box f(\Box)a_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{4g^{2}}a^{\alpha}f(\Box)i{\partial_{\alpha}}^{\beta}D^{2}a_{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{n}\varepsilon_{nm}a^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\phi_{m}\\

where

Now, since the gauge-fixing function (

\begin{split}S^{(2)}_{1}&+S_{GF1}+S_{FP1}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{5}zd^{5}z^{\prime}\Bigg[a^{\alpha}(z){\left(\mathcal{H}_{a}\right)_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\delta^{5}(z,z^{\prime})a_{\beta}(z^{\prime})\\

(17)

where the Hessians are given by

\displaystyle{\left(\mathcal{H}_{a}\right)_{\alpha}}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g^{2}}\left[\left(\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{A}\right){\delta_{\alpha}}^{\beta}+\frac{m}{2}f(\Box)D^{\beta}D_{\alpha}\right];

(18)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{H}_{\phi}\right)_{nm}

\displaystyle=\left[\delta_{nm}-\frac{M^{2}_{nm}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right]D^{2};

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{FP1}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{M^{2}_{A}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)\left(-i\sigma_{2}\right)D^{2}.

Note that, as a consequence of the gauge choice (

The Hessians given above are essential for determining the one-loop correction to the effective action, since the calculation ultimately reduces to computing the trace of the logarithm of the Hessians above. Indeed, by integrating out the quantum superfields, it can be shown that the one-loop Euclidean effective action is given by

\Gamma^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=1}=\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln{\left(\mathcal{H}_{a}\right)_{\alpha}}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(\mathcal{H}_{\phi}\right)_{nm}+\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{FP1}.

(21)

The second and third traces can be disregarded for the following reason. Let

\left(\mathcal{C}_{\phi}\right)_{nm}=\delta_{nm}f(\Box)D^{2}\ \ ;\ \ \mathcal{C}_{FP1}=i\sigma_{2}f(\Box)D^{2}

(22)

be operators. Since they are independent of the background superfields, adding
-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(\mathcal{C}_{\phi}\right)_{nm}
or

to (
21
) does not modify the result, because these quantities are integrals of a constant function, which vanish due to the properties of the Berezin integral. Thus, we may add them to (
21
) and use Eqs. (
19
), (
20

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(\mathcal{C}_{\phi}\mathcal{H}_{\phi}\right)_{nm}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(\delta_{nm}\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{nm}\right);

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(\mathcal{C}_{FP1}\mathcal{H}_{FP1}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(\left(\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{A}\right)I_{2\times 2}\right).

(24)

Even though these are traces of operators that depend on the background superfields, we can drop them because the operators contain no spinor covariant derivatives, which implies that the traces in Eqs. (
23

) vanish.

Therefore, only the first trace in (

\Gamma^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=1}=\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\frac{1}{g^{2}}\left[\left(\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{A}\right){\delta_{\alpha}}^{\beta}+\frac{m}{2}f(\Box)D^{\beta}D_{\alpha}\right].

(25)

Note that, if
m=0
, this one-loop correction vanishes. This means that the Chern-Simons term in (

We can rewrite Eq. (

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left[\frac{1}{g^{2}}\left(\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{A}\right){\delta_{\alpha}}^{\gamma}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left[{\delta_{\gamma}}^{\beta}+\frac{m}{2}\frac{f(\Box)}{\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{A}}D^{\beta}D_{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{5}z\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{p}\arctan\left(\frac{mpf(-p^{2})}{p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{A}}\right),

(26)

where the last equality follows from Eq. (16) of
GNP2
. Since this result was obtained under the assumption that the background matter superfields are constant, it follows immediately that the one-loop EP is given by

K^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=1}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{p}\arctan\left(\frac{mpf(-p^{2})}{p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{A}}\right).

(27)

The last step consists of evaluating the Euclidean Feynman integral. We can handle the integral in (
27
) by defining the vector field

V_{\mu}(p):=\frac{p_{\mu}}{p}\arctan\left(\frac{mpf(-p^{2})}{p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{A}}\right)

(28)

and using the fact that, in dimensional regularization, surface terms can be disregarded:

\mu^{2\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\partial V_{\mu}(p)}{\partial p_{\mu}}=0.

(29)

After some algebraic manipulations, this identity leads to

\begin{split}\mu^{2\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{1}{p}\arctan&\left(\frac{mpf(-p^{2})}{p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{A}}\right)=\\
&-\frac{\mu^{2\varepsilon}}{d-1}\int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{d}{dp}\arctan\left(\frac{mpf(-p^{2})}{p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{A}}\right).\end{split}

(30)

Substituting this expression into (

K^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=1}=-\frac{m\mu^{2\varepsilon}}{2(d-1)}\int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{N(s)}{D(s)},

(31)

where
s:=p^{2}
and

\displaystyle N(p^{2})

\displaystyle:=p^{2}f^{2}(-p^{2})-M^{2}_{A}\left[f(-p^{2})+p\frac{d}{dp}f(-p^{2})\right];

(32)

\displaystyle D(p^{2})

\displaystyle:=\left[p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{A}\right]^{2}+m^{2}p^{2}f^{2}(-p^{2}).

(33)

Suppose that the coefficients of the polynomial
f(\Box)
are chosen in such a way that

has distinct roots

has degree lower than that of

\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{N(s_{j})}{D^{\prime}(s_{j})}\frac{1}{s-s_{j}}.

(34)

Plugging this expression into (

higher-derivative Maxwell-Chern-Simons theory (

) is given by

K^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=1}=\frac{m}{16\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\sqrt{-s_{j}}\,N(s_{j})}{D^{\prime}(s_{j})}.

(35)

This result makes explicit how the functional structure of the one-loop correction is governed by the degrees of freedom of the HD theory. In fact, the higher the degree of the polynomial
f(\Box)
in (

), the larger the number of extra propagating degrees of freedom, including, in general, Ostrogradsky ghosts. At the same time, as the degree of
f(\Box)
increases, new roots

appear, and the one-loop correction acquires additional contributions of the form
\sqrt{-s_{j}}\,N(s_{j})/D^{\prime}(s_{j})
.

As an application of our general result, let us consider, for the sake of simplicity, a theory without higher derivatives, that is,
f(\Box)=1
. This assumption, in conjunction with Eqs. (

where we have used (

and these roots are negative real numbers. Finally, the one-loop potential follows from (

\left.K_{\mathcal{N}=1}^{(1)}\right|_{f(\Box)=1}=\frac{m}{16\pi}\left[\frac{\sqrt{-s_{+}}\left(s_{+}-g^{2}|\Phi|^{2}\right)}{s_{+}-s_{-}}+\frac{\sqrt{-s_{-}}\left(s_{-}-g^{2}|\Phi|^{2}\right)}{s_{-}-s_{+}}\right],

(38)

where
s_{+}
and
s_{-}
are given in (
37
).

Of course, we could also consider a HD operator, for example,

. However, in this case, the polynomial

has degree four, and its roots are very cumbersome to write out explicitly in full. That is why we have left this example out of the present discussion.

(45)

where we have introduced the following matrix notation:

\bm{\bar{\Phi}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{\Phi}_{+}&\bar{\Phi}_{-}\end{array}\right);\ \ \ \bm{\Phi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\Phi_{+}\\
\Phi_{-}\end{array}\right);\ \ \ \bm{\bar{\phi}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{\phi}_{+}&\bar{\phi}_{-}\end{array}\right);\ \ \ \bm{\phi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{+}\\
\phi_{-}\end{array}\right).

(46)

Moreover,
\sigma_{3}
denotes a Pauli matrix, and the mass parameter
M_{v}^{2}
is defined by

M_{v}^{2}:=4g^{2}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\bm{\Phi}.

(47)

Similarly to what we have done in the previous section, we now remove the mixed terms
v\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\sigma_{3}\bm{\phi}
and
v\bm{\bar{\phi}}\sigma_{3}\bm{\Phi}
from (
45
) by adding to it the following HD
R_{\xi}
gauge-fixing term:

S_{GF2}=-\frac{1}{4g^{2}}\int d^{7}z\bar{F}f(\Box)F,

(48)

where the appropriate gauge-fixing function is defined as

F:=\bar{D}^{2}\left(v+4g^{2}\frac{1}{\Box f(\Box)}\bm{\bar{\phi}}\sigma_{3}\bm{\Phi}\right).

(49)

This choice fixes the quantum gauge symmetry while preserving background invariance. Moreover, it cancels the unwanted mixed terms, as can be seen by substituting (
49
) into (
48
) and expanding the product:

S_{GF2}=-\int d^{7}z\left[\frac{1}{4g^{2}}(D^{2}v)f(\Box)\bar{D}^{2}v+v\bm{\bar{\phi}}\sigma_{3}\bm{\Phi}+v\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\sigma_{3}\bm{\phi}+\bm{\bar{\phi}}\bm{M}^{2}\frac{1}{\Box f(\Box)}\bm{\phi}\right],

(50)

where we have introduced the background-superfield-dependent mass matrix

\bm{M^{2}}:=4g^{2}\sigma_{3}\bm{\Phi}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\sigma_{3}=4g^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left|\Phi_{+}\right|^{2}&-\Phi_{+}\bar{\Phi}_{-}\\
-\Phi_{-}\bar{\Phi}_{+}&\left|\Phi_{-}\right|^{2}\end{array}\right).

(51)

Now, since
M^{2}_{v}
depends on the background matter superfields and
F
changes under infinitesimal quantum gauge transformations as

\delta_{\Lambda}F=i\bar{D}^{2}\left[\left(1-\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)\bar{\Lambda}-4g^{2}\frac{1}{\Box f(\Box)}\left(\bar{\Lambda}\bm{\bar{\phi}}\right)\bm{\Phi}\right],

(52)

it is clear that the ghosts interact with the background superfields through
M^{2}_{v}
at the one-loop level. Thus, it is necessary to add to (
45
) the ghost action

\displaystyle S_{FP2}

\displaystyle=\left.\left(i\int d^{5}zc^{\prime}\delta_{\Lambda}F+i\int d^{5}\bar{z}\bar{c}^{\prime}\delta_{\Lambda}\bar{F}\right)\right|_{\Lambda\to c,\ \bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{c}}

(53)

\displaystyle=\int d^{7}z\left[-c^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)\bar{c}+\bar{c}^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)c\right],

(54)

where we have omitted the cubic contributions, since they are irrelevant for one-loop calculations.

Finally, we can now collect the quadratic terms in the quantum superfields arising from (
45
), (
50
), and (
54
) into a single functional expression:

\begin{split}S^{(2)}_{2}+S_{GF2}+S_{FP2}^{(2)}=&\frac{1}{2}\int d^{7}z\bigg[\frac{1}{8g^{2}}v\left(-\Box f(\Box)+M_{v}^{2}+mf(\Box)\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right)v\\
&+\bm{\bar{\phi}}\left(\bm{1}-\frac{\bm{M^{2}}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)\bm{\phi}-c^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)\bar{c}\\
&+\bar{c}^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)c\bigg].\end{split}

(55)

From this result, we infer that the Hessians corresponding to each sector are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4g^{2}}\left(-\Box f(\Box)+M_{v}^{2}+mf(\Box)\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right);

(56)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{0}&\displaystyle\left(\bm{1}-\frac{\bm{M^{2T}}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)\bar{D}^{2}\\
\displaystyle\left(\bm{1}-\frac{\bm{M^{2}}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)D^{2}&\bm{0}\end{array}\right);

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{FP2}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{0}&-\sigma_{1}\bar{D}^{2}\\
\sigma_{1}D^{2}&\bm{0}\end{array}\right).

In the same manner as in the
\mathcal{N}=1
case, the Hessian corresponding to the matter sector (
III
) is modified by the HD operator
f(\Box)
, even though we have not introduced it into the matter sector of (
39
).

With the Hessians in hand, the next step is to obtain the one-loop contribution to the Euclidean effective action, which can be calculated from

\Gamma^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=2}=-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{v}-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{FP2}.

(61)

The last two functional traces on the right-hand side of (
61
) are identical to those found in
Meu
. Thus, we can use Eq. (33) of
Meu
to obtain

-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{FP2}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}_{+}\ln\left(\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{v}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}_{-}\ln\left(\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{v}\right),

(62)

where
\text{Tr}_{+}
and
\text{Tr}_{-}
denote the trace operations restricted to the chiral and antichiral subspaces, respectively.

The trace over the chiral subspace is evaluated by extracting the background-independent factor
\text{Tr}_{+}\ln\left(\Box f(\Box)\right)
from the trace and subsequently discarding it, so that

\begin{split}\text{Tr}_{+}\ln\left(\Box f(\Box)-M^{2}_{v}\right)&=\text{Tr}_{+}\ln\left(1-\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{\Box f(\Box)}\right)\\
&=-\int d^{7}z\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ln\left(1+\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{p^{2}f(-p^{2})}\right),\end{split}

(63)

where the details of the calculation have been omitted, since it proceeds in a way very similar to that presented in
BMS
. Additionally, the trace over the antichiral subspace is evaluated in an analogous way, yielding the same result as in (
63
). Therefore, by substituting (
63
) into (
62
), we obtain

-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{FP2}=-\int d^{7}z\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ln\left(1+\frac{M^{2}_{v}}{p^{2}f(-p^{2})}\right).

(64)

Now we are left with the calculation of the first trace in (
61
), which is explicitly given by

-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{v}=-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left[\frac{1}{4g^{2}}\left(-\Box f(\Box)+M^{2}_{v}+mf(\Box)\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right)\right].

(65)

Note that, if we disregard the Chern-Simons term by taking
m=0
, this contribution vanishes due to the absence of spinor covariant derivatives (this also occurs in the
\mathcal{N}=1
case [see Eq. (
25
)]). Indeed, the absence of spinor covariant derivatives in
\textrm{Tr}\ln\left[\frac{1}{4g^{2}}\left(-\Box f(\Box)+M^{2}_{v}\right)\right]
allows us to extract this term from the trace and subsequently discard it, so that we are left with

-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{v}=-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(1+\frac{mf(\Box)}{-\Box f(\Box)+M^{2}_{v}}\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right).

(66)

In order to evaluate this trace, we need to use the following identity:

\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(1+A(\Box)\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right)=\int d^{7}z\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ln\left(1+p^{2}A^{2}(-p^{2})\right).

(67)

To prove it, we first use the definition of the trace of differential operators acting on superfields together with the series expansion of the logarithm. Thus,

\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(1+A(\Box)\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right)=\int d^{7}zd^{7}z^{\prime}\delta^{7}(z^{\prime},z)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\frac{A^{n}(\Box)}{n}\left(\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right)^{n}\delta^{7}(z,z^{\prime}).

(68)

Since

\displaystyle\left(\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=-\Box^{\frac{n}{2}-1}D^{\alpha}\bar{D}^{2}D_{\alpha},\ \ \text{if $n$ is even};

(69)

\displaystyle\left(\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\Box^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha},\ \ \text{if $n$ is odd},

(70)

and

\delta^{4}(\theta^{\prime},\theta)\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\delta^{7}(z,z^{\prime})=0\ \ ;\ \ \delta^{4}(\theta^{\prime},\theta)D^{\alpha}\bar{D}^{2}D_{\alpha}\delta^{7}(z,z^{\prime})=2\delta^{7}(z,z^{\prime}),

(71)

only terms with even powers survive, and the expansion (
68
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(1+A(\Box)\bar{D}^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\int d^{7}zd^{7}z^{\prime}\delta^{3}(x^{\prime},x)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{A^{2\ell}(\Box)}{\ell}\Box^{\ell-1}\delta^{7}(z,z^{\prime})

(72)

\displaystyle=-\int d^{7}zd^{3}x^{\prime}\delta^{3}(x^{\prime},x)\frac{1}{\Box}\ln\left(1-\Box A^{2}(\Box)\right)\delta^{3}(x,x^{\prime}),

(73)

which, in momentum space, corresponds to (
67
), thereby completing the proof.

Using the identity (
67
) in (
66
), we obtain, after a straightforward algebraic manipulation,

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\mathcal{H}_{v}&=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{7}z\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ln\left[\left(p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{v}\right)^{2}+m^{2}p^{2}f^{2}(-p^{2})\right]\\
&+\int d^{7}z\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ln\left(p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{v}\right).\end{split}

(74)

Notice that, on the right-hand side, the second integral is essentially the integral in (
64
), but with the opposite sign. Thus, by substituting the contributions (
64
) and (
74
) into (
61
) and canceling the integrals, we arrive at

\Gamma^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{7}z\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ln\left[\left(p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{v}\right)^{2}+m^{2}p^{2}f^{2}(-p^{2})\right].

(75)

Since this result was obtained under the assumption (
41
), it follows immediately that the one-loop Kähler EP is given by

K^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{s}\ln A(s),

(76)

where
s:=p^{2}
and

A(p^{2}):=\left(p^{2}f(-p^{2})+M^{2}_{v}\right)^{2}+m^{2}p^{2}f^{2}(-p^{2}).

(77)

Fortunately, the Euclidean Feynman integral (
76
) is easier to evaluate than (
27
) because of the product rule for logarithms. Indeed, let us first suppose that
A(s)
has roots
s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n}
, not necessarily distinct. Since
A(s)
is a nonconstant polynomial, it follows from the fundamental theorem of algebra that
A(s)
admits a unique factorization (up to the ordering of the factors) of the form
Axler
:

A(s)=c\prod_{j=1}^{n}(s-s_{j}),

(78)

where
c
is a constant. Now, by substituting the factorization above into (
76
) and using the product rule for logarithms, we obtain a sum of Feynman integrals that can be evaluated exactly by means of dimensional regularization. Therefore, the one-loop Kähler effective potential for the
\mathcal{N}=2
higher-derivative Maxwell-Chern-Simons theory (
39
) is given by

K^{(1)}_{\mathcal{N}=2}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sqrt{-s_{j}}.

(79)

As in the
\mathcal{N}=1
theory, this one-loop correction is highly sensitive to the number of degrees of freedom in the HD theory (
39
), which, in turn, depends on the degree of the polynomial
f(\Box)
. Indeed, the number of roots of the polynomial
A(s)
is directly proportional to the degree of the polynomial
f(\Box)
, that is, as the degree of
f(\Box)
increases, the number of contributions of the form
\sqrt{-s_{j}}
to the one-loop correction (
79
) also increases.

Let us conclude this section by considering a simple application of our general result. Suppose a theory without higher derivatives, that is,
f(\Box)=1
. It follows from (
77
) that

A(s)=\left(s+4g^{2}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\bm{\Phi}\right)^{2}+m^{2}s=(s-s_{+})(s-s_{-}),

(80)

where we have used (
47
). The roots of
A(s)
are negative real numbers and are given by

s_{\pm}=-\frac{1}{2}\left[m^{2}+8g^{2}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\bm{\Phi}\pm m^{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{16g^{2}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\bm{\Phi}}{m^{2}}}\right].

(81)

Finally, by substituting (
81
) into (
79
), we arrive at the following one-loop correction:

\begin{split}\left.K_{\mathcal{N}=2}^{(1)}\right|_{f(\Box)=1}&=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left[m^{2}+8g^{2}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\bm{\Phi}+m^{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{16g^{2}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\bm{\Phi}}{m^{2}}}\right]}\\
&-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left[m^{2}+8g^{2}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\bm{\Phi}-m^{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{16g^{2}\bm{\bar{\Phi}}\bm{\Phi}}{m^{2}}}\right]}.\end{split}

(82)

Similarly to the result found in the previous section, we could also choose
f(\Box)=1+\frac{\Box}{\Lambda^{2}}
as an application of our general result, but this choice of HD operator would lead to a polynomial
A(s)
of degree four, and its roots would be highly cumbersome to write explicitly. For this reason, we did not include such an example in the present discussion.

IV
Conclusions

We have defined two HD extensions of the MCS theory in
\mathcal{N}=1
and
\mathcal{N}=2
superspaces. This was achieved by introducing, only in the gauge sector, an HD operator chosen to be a polynomial function of the d’Alembertian of arbitrary degree. For these theories, we calculated the one-loop corrections to the superfield EP. In contrast to our previous studies
GNP
;
GGNPS
, all quantum calculations were performed in an HD
R_{\xi}
gauge, and the EPs were obtained by evaluating the functional traces directly. The gauge choice is rather relevant because the EP generally depends on the gauge-fixing procedure, although the values of the EP at its extrema are gauge independent
gauge-dependence
.

The two main results of this paper are the one-loop corrections given in (
35
) and (
79
) for the superfield EPs corresponding to the
\mathcal{N}=1
and
\mathcal{N}=2
versions of the HD MCS theories defined in (
1
) and (
39
), respectively. The importance of these results is twofold. First, from a technical point of view, they provide closed analytical expressions for the EPs in terms of the roots of polynomial functions, which, in previous treatments
GNP
;
GGNPS
, were either not obtained for the MCS case or were left in an integral representation. Second, from a physical point of view, the results show how the one-loop corrections are directly controlled by the degrees of freedom associated with the combined Maxwell, Chern-Simons, and higher-derivative terms, where the latter include, in general, Ostrogradsky ghosts. Since the EP is a useful tool for studying the vacuum properties of a quantum field theory, our results indicate that the extra degrees of freedom generated by the HD operator, including possible Ostrogradsky-type ghosts, may affect the quantum-corrected ground-state configuration.

The most natural next step in this work would be to calculate the two-loop corrections to the superfield EPs of the HD MCS theories studied here. However, when compared to supersymmetric quantum electrodynamics with higher derivatives investigated in
Meu
, the calculations in the present case would be more technically intricate due to the more complicated structure of the propagators for the gauge superfields, which would be obtained by inverting the Hessians (
18
) or (
56
), depending on the theory under consideration. These propagators would lead to a larger number of two-loop supergraphs that would need to be computed than the number of supergraphs considered in
Meu
. For this reason, we plan to address this problem in future work, and the present contribution should be regarded as a first step in this direction.
