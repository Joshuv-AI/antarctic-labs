Peakon solutions and analytical properties for the Camassa-Holm type equations with quadratic nonlinearities
Yonghong Chen, Zhijun Qiao, Mingxuan Zhu ∗

Abstract
In this paper, we derive the multi-peakon dynamical system of a class of Camassa-Holm-type equations with quadratic nonlinearities. We also consider the analytical properties for the Cauchy problem. Firstly, we establish local well-posedness of solutions in Besov spaces and then provide the blow-up criteria. Subsequently, we impose appropriate sufficient conditions on the initial data to guaranty that the corresponding solution either exists globally or blows up in a finite time. Finally, we prove the ill-posedness in the Besov space B 2 , ∞ 3 / 2 B_{2,\infty}^{3/2} by utilizing the non-traveling wave solutions.



1
Introduction

A class of fundamental equations in the study of nonlinear waves is characterized by the following form:

u_{t}+P(u,u_{x})+(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}Q(u,u_{x})+\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}R(u,u_{x})=0,

(1)

where
P,Q,
and
R
are bivariate polynomials of degree
n
, and
(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}
denotes the convolution with the Helmholtz kernel
G(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Representative examples include:

•

Camassa-Holm equation:

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}\right)=0

(2)

•

Degasperis–Procesi equation:

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{2}u^{2}\right)=0

(3)

•

Novikov equation:

u_{t}+u^{2}u_{x}+\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left(u^{3}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}^{2}\right)+(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{3}\right)=0

(4)

•

Fokas-Olver-Rosenau-Qiao equation:

u_{t}+u^{2}u_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{3}+\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{3}u^{3}+uu_{x}^{2}\right)+(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{3}\right)=0

(5)

A common feature of these models is their admission of peakon solutions. Formally, any function of the form

u(x,t)=p(t)e^{-|x-q(t)|}

can be regarded as a candidate peakon. To the best of our knowledge, waves similar to peakons first appeared in the context of the Fornberg–Whitham equation
[
17
]
:

u_{t}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}+\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}u=0.

(6)

This equation admits the traveling wave solution

u(x,t)=\frac{8}{9}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\,|x-\frac{4}{3}t|}.

(7)

The Fornberg–Whitham equation also exhibits the phenomenon of wave breaking
[
21
]
, in a manner similar to the Camassa–Holm equation. However, in contrast to Camassa–Holm type equations, which possess a whole family of peakon solutions, the solution (
7
) is essentially the unique peakon of the Fornberg–Whitham equation. This distinction suggests, at least heuristically, that the richness of peakon structures may be closely related to the integrability of the underlying equation.

The Camassa–Holm (CH) equation
[
2
]
was derived by Camassa and Holm in the study of shallow water wave regimes. They established its physical relevance, gave the Lax pair to guaranty the integrability of the equation, and showed that the CH equation admits peakon solutions, the most breakthrough achievement in soliton theory. Later in 1995 Fokas
[
18
]
generalized the CH model to a large class of physically important integrable equations.
Based on those foundational results, Qiao
[
30
]
further developed the Camassa–Holm hierarchy, investigated its integrable extensions of dimensions
N
and constructed algebro-geometric solutions on a symplectic submanifold.

In particular, the Camassa–Holm equation is renowned for exhibiting fascinating phenomena: wave breaking, blow-up, and peakon stability. These phenomena play crucial roles in both the physical interpretation and the mathematical structure of the equation.

1.

Wave Breaking and Blow-up
:
One of the features of the Camassa–Holm equation is its ability to model wave breaking,
where the solution remains bounded while its slope becomes unbounded in finite time.
As described in
[
2
]
, a smooth initial condition can break down into a series of peakons
by developing vertical slopes at each inflection point with negative gradient, from which derivative discontinuities emerge. This provides a concrete dynamical mechanism for the formation of singularities.
Constantin and Escher
[
6
,
7
]
later gave a rigorous justification of this phenomenon,
showing that such wave breaking occurs even when the energy remains finite. From a mathematical perspective, wave breaking represents a particular form of finite-time blow-up.
More generally, the Camassa–Holm equation admits blow-up solutions even for smooth initial data,
indicating that singularity formation is an intrinsic feature of the equation.

2.

Peakon Stability
:
Another crucial aspect of the Camassa–Holm equation is the stability of peakon solutions.
Constantin and Strauss
[
10
]
investigated the orbital stability of these localized waves,
showing that the peakons persist under small perturbations. Their results highlight the robustness of peakons as
physically relevant structures in nonlinear shallow water wave dynamics. In
[
31
]
, Qiao and
Zhang analyze the peaked and smooth soliton solutions of the Camassa–Holm equation and investigate their dynamical
behavior. This dynamical approach might be applied to Solitary and self-similar solutions of two-component system of nonlinear Schrödinger equations
[
25
]
.

Similarly, the Degasperis–Procesi equation, which also admits peakon solutions, was derived using the method of asymptotic
integrability by Degasperis and Procesi
[
13
]
. As an integrable system, it possesses a Lax
pair, a bi-Hamiltonian structure, and an infinite hierarchy of symmetries and conservation laws

[
14
]
.

The Degasperis–Procesi equation also exhibits wave-breaking phenomena, though the conditions triggering this behavior differ
slightly from those of the Camassa–Holm equation. Liu and Yin
[
28
]
demonstrated that wave breaking occurs
under specific sign conditions imposed on the initial data. Concerning the blow-up phenomenon, Escher, Liu, and
Yin
[
16
]
established the precise blow-up rate and characterized the blow-up set for strong
solutions to the Degasperis–Procesi equation across a wide range of initial data. Additionally, Liu and Yin
[
28
]
proved
that the first blow-up in finite time for the Degasperis–Procesi equation manifests itself as wave breaking, with the potential emergence
of shock waves thereafter. In particular, the blow-up rate for the breaking waves in the Camassa–Holm equation is

-2

[
9
]
, while for the Degasperis–Procesi equation, it is
-1

[
16
]
. Regarding
peakon stability in the Degasperis–Procesi equation, this property was established by Lin and Liu in
[
27
]
.

The Holm-Staley
b
-family of equations is a one-parameter generalization of the CH and Degasperis–Procesi equations, introduced
by Holm and Staley
[
23
]
to describe nonlinear shallow water wave dynamics with varying
nonlinear dispersion properties. This family is given by

m_{t}+m_{x}u+bmu_{x}=0,\quad m=u-u_{xx},

(8)

where
b
is a real parameter.

In particular, for
b=2
, the equation reduces to the Camassa–Holm equation, while for
b=3
, it corresponds to the DP
equation. The Holm-Staley equation
b
-family exhibits rich mathematical structures, including a non-canonical
Hamiltonian formulation, and also admits peakon solutions
[
15
]
.

The purpose of this paper is twofold. First, we show how to derive the
N
-peakon dynamical system from Camassa–Holm-type equations with quadratic nonlinearity. Second, we consider the Cauchy problem for this class of equations, investigating local and global well-posedness, wave breaking, and ill-posedness.

2
Camassa–Holm Type Equations with Quadratic Nonlinearities

In this section, we derive the dynamical system of
N
-peakon in a nonlocal formulation. The convolution-based computations developed by Lundmark in
[
29
]
are adapted here to a general class of Camassa–Holm-type equations with quadratic nonlinearity:

u_{t}+\lambda_{1}u^{2}+\lambda_{2}uu_{x}+G\ast(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2})+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2})\right)=0,

(9)

where
G(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}
is the Helmholtz kernel and
\ast
denotes the convolution with respect to the spatial variable.

As Constantin and Strauss pointed out, the nonlocal formulation provides a natural framework in which peakon solutions of the Camassa–Holm equation can be rigorously interpreted
[
10
]
. We extend this point of view to the general class of equations (
9
) and introduce the following definition.

Definition 1

(N-peakon solution)
.

A function of the form

u(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}(t)\,e^{-|x-q_{i}(t)|}

(10)

is called an
N
-peakon solution of equation (
9
) on
\mathbb{R}\times[0,T)
, for some
T>0
, if it satisfies (
9
) in the weak sense.

Throughout this paper, we introduce the following shorthand notation. We set

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}=e^{-|x-q_{\ell}|},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{i,j}=e^{-|q_{i}-q_{j}|},

(11)

and

\sigma_{\ell}=\operatorname{sgn}(x-q_{\ell}),\qquad\sigma_{ij}=\operatorname{sgn}(q_{i}-q_{j}),

(12)

where
q_{\ell}=q_{\ell}(t)
for all
\ell
, depending only on the time variable
t
.

To derive peakon solutions, a key step is to evaluate convolutions with the Helmholtz kernel
G
.
Remarkably, the convolution of
G
with expressions of the form
\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}
remains algebraically closed within the class generated by the above notation.
More precisely, the result can be expressed in terms of
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
,
\sigma_{\ell}
,
\mathcal{E}_{i,j}
, and
\sigma_{ij}
.

A direct computation yields the following identities.

Lemma 1
.

\displaystyle G\ast\Big(\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(-\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{j})+\frac{2\sigma_{ij}}{3}(\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}-\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}-\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{j})

(13)

\displaystyle G\ast\Big(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(-\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{j})-\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{3}(\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}-\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}-\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{j})

In particular,

G\ast(\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2})=\frac{1}{3}(-\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}+2\mathcal{E}_{i}).

(14)

Theorem 1
.

If an equation of the form (
9
) satisfies the parameter conditions

\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{1},\quad\lambda_{4}=2\lambda_{1},\quad\lambda_{5}=\frac{3}{2}\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{6},

then it admits an
N
-peakon solution, where the time evolution of the amplitude
p_{i}
and positions
q_{i}
is governed by the dynamical system:

\displaystyle p_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{2\lambda_{5}-\lambda_{6}}{3}\sigma_{ij}-\lambda_{1}\right)p_{i}p_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i,j},

(15)

\displaystyle q_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\lambda_{5}+\lambda_{6}}{3}\right)p_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i,j}.

Proof.

By the
N
-peakon ansatz and Lemma
1
, we obtain:

\displaystyle G\ast\Big(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{i}p_{j}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}}{3}\Big(-\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{(2\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4})\sigma_{ij}}{3}\Big(\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}-\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}-\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{j}\Big)\Bigg].

Taking derivative yields:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}G\ast\Big(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{i}p_{j}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{5}+\lambda_{6}}{3}\Big(\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}+\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}-\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{i}-\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\sigma_{j}\mathcal{E}_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{(2\lambda_{5}-\lambda_{6})\sigma_{ij}}{3}\Big(-\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{j}\Big)\Bigg].

Since
i
and
j
are dummy indices, we can use symmetry to rewrite the combined expression under the double summation
\sum_{i,j}
as:

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{1}-\frac{\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}}{3}\right)\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}+2\left(\frac{\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}}{3}-\frac{(2\lambda_{5}-\lambda_{6})\sigma_{ij}}{3}\right)\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\mathcal{E}_{i}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\left(-\frac{(2\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4})\sigma_{ij}}{3}+\frac{2\lambda_{5}+2\lambda_{6}-3\lambda_{2}}{6}\right)\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}+2\left(\frac{(2\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4})\sigma_{ij}}{3}-\frac{\lambda_{5}+\lambda_{6}}{3}\right)\mathcal{E}_{i,j}\sigma_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}.

Under the assumed parameter conditions,

\lambda_{1}-\frac{\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}}{3}=-\frac{2\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}{3}=\frac{2\lambda_{5}+2\lambda_{6}-3\lambda_{2}}{6}=0,

the peakon dynamical system is derived by:

\displaystyle p_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{(2\lambda_{5}-\lambda_{6})\sigma_{ij}}{3}-\frac{\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}}{3}\right)p_{i}p_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i,j},

\displaystyle q_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\lambda_{5}+\lambda_{6}}{3}-\frac{(2\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4})\sigma_{ij}}{3}\right)p_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i,j}.

∎

We now show that Theorem
1
encompasses the standard peakon constructions for a broad class of Camassa–Holm type equations, including the Camassa–Holm equation, the Degasperis–Procesi equation, and the Holm–Staley equation.

Corollary 1
.

Camassa–Holm equation (
2
) admits the following two-peakon dynamical system:

\begin{cases}p_{1}^{\prime}=p_{1}p_{2}\sigma_{12}\mathcal{E}_{1,2},\\
p_{2}^{\prime}=-p_{1}p_{2}\sigma_{12}\mathcal{E}_{1,2},\\
q_{1}^{\prime}=p_{1}+p_{2}\mathcal{E}_{1,2},\\
q_{2}^{\prime}=p_{2}+p_{1}\mathcal{E}_{1,2},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{E}_{1,2}=e^{-|q_{1}-q_{2}|}
and
\sigma_{12}=\operatorname{sgn}(q_{1}-q_{2})
.

Corollary 2
.

Equation

u_{t}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+uu_{x}+G\ast\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)\right)=0

(16)

admits the following two-peakon dynamical system

\left\{\begin{aligned} p_{1}^{\prime}&=-p_{1}^{2}-p_{1}p_{2}\mathcal{E}_{1,2},\\
p_{2}^{\prime}&=-p_{2}^{2}-p_{1}p_{2}\mathcal{E}_{1,2},\\
q_{1}^{\prime}&=p_{1}+p_{2}\mathcal{E}_{1,2},\\
q_{2}^{\prime}&=p_{2}+p_{1}\mathcal{E}_{1,2}.\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 1
.

Equation (
16
) arises from the two-component integrable system proposed by Xia and Qiao
[
32
]
. Consequently, it possesses a Lax pair corresponding to its local form, given by

U=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-1&\lambda m\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad V=-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{-2}&-\lambda^{-1}(u-u_{x})+\lambda mu\\
0&-\lambda^{-2}\end{pmatrix}.

3
Peakon Solutions

In this section, we apply Theorem
1
to obtain explicit peakon solutions and study their properties.

Theorem 2
.

Equation (
9
) has single peakon traveling-wave solutions if and only if

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=0,\\
&\lambda_{2}-\frac{2\lambda_{5}}{3}-\frac{2\lambda_{6}}{3}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

If equation (
9
) admits a single peakon solution
p(t)e^{-|x-q(t)|}
, the corresponding dynamical system must
satisfy system (
15
). To ensure the existence of a traveling wave peakon solution of the
form
u(x,t)=u(x-ct)
, the velocity
q^{\prime}(t)
must remain constant, which implies

q^{\prime}=c=\frac{2(\lambda_{5}+\lambda_{6})}{3}p.

As a result,
p(t)
must be constant. Since
p^{2}\neq 0
for a nontrivial solution, it follows that
\frac{\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}}{3}=0
.
Furthermore, substituting the single peakon ansatz into equation (
9
) yields the following
constraints:

\lambda_{1}-\frac{\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}}{3}=\frac{2\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}{3}=\frac{2\lambda_{5}+2\lambda_{6}-3\lambda_{2}}{6}=0.

By Theorem
1
, equation (
9
) possesses a single peakon traveling-wave
solution if and only if:

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=0,\quad\lambda_{2}-\frac{2\lambda_{5}}{3}-\frac{2\lambda_{6}}{3}=0.

∎

Remark 2
.

Indeed, if
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=0
, then equation (
9
) reduces to a conservation form. Therefore, the expression

\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}u^{2}+G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2})

can be interpreted as the associated flux.

Example 1
.

Let

u(x,t)=p(t)\mathrm{e}^{-|x-q(t)|},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

If the coefficients in (
9
) satisfy the following relations:

\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{1},\quad\lambda_{4}=2\lambda_{1},\quad\lambda_{5}=\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{6},

then equation (
9
) holds if and only if

\left\{\begin{aligned} p^{\prime}&=-2\lambda_{1}p^{2},\\
q^{\prime}&=\lambda_{2}p.\end{aligned}\right.

(17)

Furthermore, the solution
u(x,t)
takes the following form:

•

(Traveling wave solution)
If
\lambda_{1}=0
, then

u(x,t)=c\,\mathrm{e}^{-|x-\lambda_{2}ct|},

where
c
is an arbitrary constant.

•

(Non-traveling wave solution)
If
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\lambda_{1}t-A}\,\exp\left(-\left|x-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2\lambda_{1}}\ln|2\lambda_{1}t-A|-B\right|\right),

(18)

defined on any connected domain where
2\lambda_{1}t-A\neq 0
,
where
A
and
B
are arbitrary constants.

Although we have derived the explicit
N
-peakon ODE system, numerical methods are recommended to solve peakon solutions, since symbolic computations can be highly time-consuming. We now consider a particular Cauchy problem associated with equation (
16
), namely

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+uu_{x}+G\ast\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)\right)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>t_{0},\\
u(x,t_{0})=\xi_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-|x-\eta_{1}|}+\xi_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-|x-\eta_{2}|},\quad\eta_{1}<\eta_{2},\end{array}\right.

(19)

for some
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. After a tedious computation, the time-shifted solution to the system (
19
) is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{p}_{1}(s)&=(C_{1}+C_{2})\frac{\mu_{1}\alpha s^{-\beta}+\mu_{2}\beta s^{-\alpha}}{\mu_{1}s^{\alpha}+\mu_{2}s^{\beta}},\\
\tilde{p}_{2}(s)&=\frac{C_{2}}{s},\\
\tilde{q}_{1}(s)&=\ln\left(\frac{C_{1}}{C_{1}+C_{2}}\frac{\mu_{1}s^{\alpha}+\mu_{2}s^{\beta}}{\mu_{1}\alpha s^{-\beta}+\mu_{2}\beta s^{-\alpha}}\right),\\
\tilde{q}_{2}(s)&=\ln(s),\end{aligned}\right.

where, with
t=\frac{s-C_{3}}{C_{1}+C_{2}}
, the time-shifted variables satisfy

\tilde{p}_{i}(s)=p_{i}(t),\quad\tilde{q}_{i}(s)=q_{i}(t),\quad i=1,2.

Hence, a family of two-peakon solutions can be expressed as

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=(C_{1}+C_{2})\,\frac{\mu_{1}\alpha s^{-\beta}+\mu_{2}\beta s^{-\alpha}}{\mu_{1}s^{\alpha}+\mu_{2}s^{\beta}}\exp\left(-\left|x-\ln\left(\frac{C_{1}}{C_{1}+C_{2}}\frac{\mu_{1}s^{\alpha}+\mu_{2}s^{\beta}}{\mu_{1}\alpha s^{-\beta}+\mu_{2}\beta s^{-\alpha}}\right)\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{2}}{s}\,\exp(-|x-\ln s|),

where
s=(C_{1}+C_{2})t+C_{3}
,
\alpha=\frac{C_{1}}{C_{1}+C_{2}}
and
\beta=\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}+C_{2}}
,
and

4
Analytical properties for the Cauchy problem

In this section, we consider the Cauchy problem corresponding to equation (
9
), which is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}+\lambda_{1}u^{2}+\lambda_{2}uu_{x}+G\ast(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2})+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2})\right)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0,\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

(20)

Applying the Helmholtz operator
1-\partial_{x}^{2}
, we can rewrite equation (

Now, we present the precise blow-up scenario for the Cauchy problem (

Theorem 5
.

Let
u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s>\frac{3}{2}
and let

be the existence time of the solution

to the Cauchy problem (

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M,\quad t\in[0,T),

then the

-norm of

does not blow up on
[0,T)

Proof.

Let
\Lambda=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{\tfrac{1}{2}}
. Applying
\Lambda^{s}
on (
20
), multiplying by

\Lambda^{s}u
and integrating with respect to
x
over
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(u^{2})\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x-\lambda_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(uu_{x})\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x

(25)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(G\ast(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2}))\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x-\lambda_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(\partial_{x}\left(G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2})\right))\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then, using the Hölder inequality, the Young inequality, the Kato-Ponce inequalities (
22

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(u^{2})\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq c\|\Lambda^{s}(u^{2})\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq c\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(uu_{x})\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\lambda_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\Lambda^{s}(uu_{x})-u\Lambda^{s}u_{x}\right)\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\lambda_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\Lambda^{s}u_{x}\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\|[\Lambda^{s},u]u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}+\left|\lambda_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}(\Lambda^{s}u)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(G\ast(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2}))\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

(28)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s-1}(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2})\Lambda^{s-1}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq c(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}})\|u\|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(\partial_{x}(G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2})))\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

(29)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s-1}(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2})\Lambda^{s}u\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq c(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}})\|u\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Combining (

)-(

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq c(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

By Gronwall’s inequality and the condition (
24

\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq\mathrm{e}^{cMt}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2},\quad t\in[0,T).

This completes the proof of Theorem
5
.
∎

4.2
Global existence

In this subsection, we establish sufficient conditions on the initial data to ensure the global existence of the solution.

Lemma 4
.

If the coefficients in (
20
) satisfy the following relations:

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}=0,\quad 2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}=0,\quad\lambda_{2}=2\lambda_{6},

then, equation (
20
) has the conservation law

\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}}=\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}}.

Proof.

Multiplying (
20
) by
m
and integrating with respect to
x
over
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda_{1}u^{2}m+\lambda_{2}uu_{x}m+G\ast(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2})m+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2})\right)mdx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda_{1}u^{2}m+\lambda_{2}uu_{x}m+(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2})u+\partial_{x}(\lambda_{5}u^{2}+\lambda_{6}u_{x}^{2})udx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})u^{3}+(2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4})uu_{x}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{5}}{3}\partial_{x}(u^{3})+\left(\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}-\lambda_{6}\right)u_{x}^{3}dx

\displaystyle=0.

This completes the proof of Lemma

.
∎

Before going to our main result, for
\lambda_{2}\neq 0
, we introduce the characteristics
q(x,t)
associated to the equation (
20
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}q_{t}(x,t)=\lambda_{2}u(q(x,t),t),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad t\in[0,T),\\
q(x,0)=x.\end{array}\right.

(32)

where

is the life-span of the solution.
Taking the derivative (

x

\frac{dq_{t}}{dx}=q_{tx}=\lambda_{2}u_{x}(q,t)q_{x},\qquad t\in(0,T).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q_{x}=\exp\left(\int^{t}_{0}\lambda_{2}u_{x}(q,s)ds\right),\qquad 0<t<T,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
q_{x}(x,0)=1,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

which is always positive before the blow-up time. Therefore, the function

is an increasing diffeomorphism of the line before it blows up.

Theorem 7
.

If the coefficients in (
20
) satisfy the following relations:

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}=0,\quad 2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}=0,\quad\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{6}=0.

(34)

Suppose that

,

, then, the corresponding solution

to
the Cauchy problem (
20

u_{0}
as the initial datum exists globally.

Proof.

Under the relations (
34

-norm of solution

is conserved.
By inequality
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}

and blow-up scenario (Theorem

T>0
, there exists a number

, such that
\|u_{x}(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M(T)
.

Under the relations (
34
), equation (

) reduces to

u_{t}+\lambda_{1}u^{2}+G\ast(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2})+\partial_{x}\left(G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2})\right)=0.

Differentiating the above equation with respect to
x
, we have

u_{xt}+2\lambda_{1}uu_{x}+\partial_{x}G\ast(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2})+G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2})-\lambda_{5}u^{2}=0.

By fundamental ODE methods, for any

u_{x}(x,T)=e^{\int_{0}^{T}-2\lambda_{1}udt}\left(u_{0x}(x)-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\partial_{x}G\ast(\lambda_{3}u^{2}+\lambda_{4}u_{x}^{2})+G\ast(\lambda_{5}u^{2})-\lambda_{5}u^{2}\right)e^{\int_{0}^{s}2\lambda_{1}u(x,\tau)d\tau}ds\right).

\displaystyle|u_{x}(x,T)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{|\lambda_{1}|\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}T}\left(u_{0x}(x)+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{3}|+\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{4}|+|\lambda_{5}|\right)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}e^{|\lambda_{1}|\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}s}ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{|\lambda_{1}|\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}T}\left(u_{0x}(x)+\left(\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{3}|+\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{4}|+|\lambda_{5}|\right)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}e^{|\lambda_{1}|\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}T}T\right).

This means
\|u_{x}(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M(T)
.

This completes the proof of Theorem

.
∎

.

If the coefficients in (

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{3},\quad\lambda_{4}=2\lambda_{1},\quad\lambda_{5}=\frac{3}{2}\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{6},\quad\lambda_{2}\neq 0.

Suppose that

,

\displaystyle m_{0}(x)\geq(\not\equiv)0,\quad\lambda_{1}\geq\left|\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{5}{2}\lambda_{2}-2\lambda_{6}\right)\right|;

\displaystyle\mathrm{or}\quad

to

Proof.

Under the relations (

Proof.

It follows that

5
Conclusions

We have derived peakon solutions for a class of Camassa–Holm-type equations with quadratic nonlinearities, which indicates that the convolutional approach is feasible and may be extended to higher-order nonlinear equations. In particular, it is expected to be applicable to more general models, such as the generalized modified Camassa–Holm equation (gmCH)
[
19
]
, for which only existence results are currently available. Non-traveling
wave solutions play a pivotal role in investigating the ill-posedness of Camassa-Holm-type equations with quadratic nonlinearities. Nevertheless, owing to several critical factors—most notably the absence of conserved quantities—the stability analysis
[
20
]
of non-traveling solitray wave solutions remains highly challenging in three dimensions. Compared with our previous work
[
33
]
, the wave breaking results established in the present paper have been substantially enhanced.
Some symbiotic bright solitary wave solutions
[
26
]
might be caught for typical nonlinear dynamical systems, however, additional restrictions are still imposed on the coefficients. Due to intrinsic structural limitations of the equation itself, addressing the wave breaking problem under more general coefficient conditions entails significantly greater technical difficulties.
