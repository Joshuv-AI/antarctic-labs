A cocktail of chemical reaction networks and mathematical epidemiology tools for positive ODE stability problems
Florin Avram, Rim Adenane, Andrei-Dan Halanay

Abstract
We continue recent attempts to put together concepts and results of chemical reaction networks theory (CRNT) and mathematical epidemiology (ME), for solving problems of stability of positive ODEs. We provide first an elegant chemical reaction networks-flavored generalization of the most cited result in ME, the next generation matrix (NGM) theorem. We review next the “symbolic-numeric" approach of Vassena and Stadler, which tackles bifurcation problems by viewing the characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian at fixed points as a formal polynomial in the “symbolic reactivities", and identifies its coefficients as “Child Selection minors of the stoichiometric matrix". We also review two applications of this approach using the Mathematica package Epid-CRN at https://github.com/florinav/EpidCRNmodels , which implements tools from both chemical reaction networks and mathematical epidemiology.

A bird’s eye view of positive ODEs

Population dynamics, ecology, mathematical epidemiology (ME), virology, rumour spreading, social networks, chemical reaction networks theory (CRNT), are a few of the biological interaction networks (BIN) subfields, which study all positive dynamical systems, and have similar preoccupations: the existence and multiplicity of equilibria, their local and global stability, the occurrence of bifurcations, persistence, permanence, extinction, etc.

Definition 1

(Positive / non-negative ODE)
.

An ODE is called
positive

Rantzer 2015
or
non-negative

Haddad and Chellaboina 2011

if the non-negative orthant

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}\geq 0,\;i=1,\dots,n\}

is forward invariant under the flow.

A polynomial or fractional positive ODE may always be represented as:

\dot{x}=\Gamma v(x,\kappa),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0},\ \kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{>0},

(1)

where
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{n,m}
is called the stoichiometric matrix and
v(\cdot,\kappa)
is called the vector of rates. The rank of the representation is
\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma)
.

In CRN,

\Gamma
is further decomposed as

\Gamma_{kj}=\tilde{s}_{k}^{j}-s_{k}^{j},

(2)

where
s_{k}^{j},\tilde{s}_{k}^{j}\geq 0
are called reactant and product coefficients, respectively. Thus,
\Gamma=\beta-\alpha,
where both the reactant and product matrices contain only non-negative elements.

Our new results, package and open problems come from the following circle of ideas:

1.

2.

The

correspond often to

.

3.

The “infected variables" at the disease free equilibrium (DFE) are the union of all minimal siphons, and the associated face is the intersection of all invariant boundary faces.

4.

The result of
Diekmann et al. 1990
;
Van den Driessche and Watmough 2002
;
Van den Driessche and Watmough 2008

J_{xy}=0
for polynomial mass-action fields

Theorem 1

(NGM theorem for forward invariant faces/siphons)
.

(generalizing
Diekmann et al. 1990
;
Van den Driessche and Watmough 2002
;
Van den Driessche and Watmough 2008
,
Johnston and Avram 2025
)
Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and let

\dot{x}=f_{x}(x,y),\qquad\dot{y}=f_{y}(x,y)

be a polynomial positive (equivalently, mass-action representable) vector field.

Assume that the face

is
invariant
, i.e.

f_{x}(0,y)\equiv 0\quad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}

(3)

(equivalently,
x
is a siphon).
Then:

1.

The mixed Jacobian block
J_{xy}:=D_{y}f_{x}(0,y)
vanishes identically on the face
F_{x}
:

D_{y}f_{x}(0,y)=0\quad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0},

and at any point
(0,y^{\ast})
on the face, the Jacobian has block lower-triangular form

J(0,y^{\ast})=\begin{pmatrix}J_{x}&0\\
*&J_{y}\end{pmatrix}.

2.

A fixed point on the face
F_{x}
is stable iff the Jacobians
J_{x},J_{y}
at that point are both stable.

3.

If furthermore
J_{x}=F-V
, where
(F,V)
is a
regular splitting
(i.e.
F,V^{-1}
are non-negative),
then a fixed point on the face
F_{x}
is stable if

\rho(FV^{-1})<1
, and unstable if

\rho(FV^{-1})>1

Varga 1962
.

Proof.

Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and write the
i
th component of

as a polynomial

(f_{x})_{i}(x,y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{m},\;\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\alpha,\beta}\,x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},\qquad x^{\alpha}:=\prod_{p=1}^{m}x_{p}^{\alpha_{p}},\quad y^{\beta}:=\prod_{q=1}^{n}y_{q}^{\beta_{q}},

with real coefficients

and only finitely many nonzero terms.
Evaluating at
x=0
yields

(f_{x})_{i}(0,y)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{0,\beta}\,y^{\beta},

because every monomial with
\alpha\neq 0
vanishes when

.

By the invariance hypothesis (
3
),
(f_{x})_{i}(0,y)\equiv 0
as a polynomial in
y
on the set

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Since a real polynomial that vanishes on a set with nonempty interior must be the zero
polynomial, it follows that
c_{0,\beta}=0
for all
\beta
. Hence every nonzero monomial in
(f_{x})_{i}
has

\alpha\neq 0
, i.e. it contains at least one factor
x_{p}
. Equivalently, there exists a polynomial

such that

(f_{x})_{i}(x,y)=\sum_{p=1}^{m}x_{p}\,h_{i,p}(x,y),

and in particular
(f_{x})_{i}(x,y)
is divisible by each

that appears in every monomial.

Now fix
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Differentiating termwise gives

\frac{\partial(f_{x})_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}(x,y)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha,\beta}\,\beta_{j}\,x^{\alpha}y^{\beta-e_{j}},

where

is the
j
th standard basis vector of

and terms with
\beta_{j}=0
contribute

.
Since every term in the sum has
\alpha\neq 0
, each monomial still contains a factor

\frac{\partial(f_{x})_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}(0,y)=0\qquad\text{for all }y.

As

and

were arbitrary, this proves

Finally, the Jacobian at
(0,y^{\ast})
splits into blocks

J(0,y^{\ast})=\begin{pmatrix}D_{x}f_{x}(0,y^{\ast})&D_{y}f_{x}(0,y^{\ast})\\
D_{x}f_{y}(0,y^{\ast})&D_{y}f_{y}(0,y^{\ast})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{x}&0\\
*&J_{y}\end{pmatrix},

which is block lower-triangular, as claimed.
∎

Compliments to Routh-Hurwitz analysis: the Child Selections Cauchy Binet expansion
Vassena 2019
;
Vassena and Stadler 2024
;
Vassena 2023
;
Blokhuis et al. 2025
;
Golnik et al. 2025
and the Banaji inheritance theorems
Banaji 2018
;
Banaji and Boros 2022
;
Gutierrez et al. 2023
;
Banaji et al. 2025

Since the eigenvalues are rarely available in symbolic ME models, the Routh-Hurwitz necessary and sufficient conditions for stability use instead the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial. Still, the totality of the Routh-Hurwitz conditions leads to complicated computations which are typically beyond the power of nowadays computers, already in dimension 4 (especially due to the Hurwitz determinants). The limitations of Routh-Hurwitz make particularly attractive the idea of
“Detailed-Routh-Hurwitz"
analysis (DRH), namely of extracting information from partial summaries of the Jacobian matrix, like for example its
determinant.

This ties well to Ivanova’s idea
Ivanova and Tarnopolskii 1979

of establishing results like existence of Hopf bifurcations, chaos, etc first for smaller sub-networks, and then use recipes which
guarantee inheritance to larger networks. Note this quest exists also in the general dynamical systems literature under the name of “lifting bifurcations from invariant subspaces to the full system"
Soares 2018
.
This idea is facilitated by using a
symbolic Jacobian
with functional increasing rates, and decomposes its coefficients via the Cauchy-Binet formula (CB) into negative and positive summands (each summand being the determinant of a minor of the stoichiometric matrix); subsequently, special attention is paid to
the
negative
summands, which
“oppose the Routh-Hurwitz criterion".

The relevant minors, the “Child Selections"
Vassena 2023
;
Vassena and Stadler 2024
are square sub-matrices of the stoichiometric matrix, rearranged to have on the diagonal a reaction which produces the corresponding species, which translates into a negative sign for autocatalytic reactions (this reordering relates them to the theory of Perron-Frobenius for Metzler matrices
with nonnegative off-diagonal entries).

Conveniently, the signs of the determinants/summands may be obtained via symbolic decomposition of the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of the
symbolic Jacobian
into positive and negative terms
Vassena 2023
;
Vassena and Stadler 2024
;
Golnik et al. 2025
.
Amazingly, this approach yields existence of bifurcation results under any
rich kinetics parameters
(which include Michaelis-Menten, Hill, etc); however, for mass-action models, further work
Vassena 2025
, or application of inheritance extension criteria
Banaji 2018
;
Banaji and Boros 2022
;
Gutierrez et al. 2023
;
Banaji et al. 2025
;
Boros and Rost 2026
, is required.

The idea of using inheritance ideas in ME was already illustrated in particular examples in
Vassena et al. 2024
and Boros and Rost
Boros and Rost 2026
; below, we provide first a tutorial on how possible network reductions are obtained automatically via the BiRNe method, and then illustrate on the SIRWS example how these may suggest
rigorous reductions for mass-action models.

From critical fragments to child selections: a historical overview

The idea of explaining oscillations and multistationarity in chemical reaction
networks by isolating small destabilizing substructures has evolved gradually,
with increasing levels of formalization. The following paragraph reviews that evolution,
clarifying terminology and attribution.

1.

The critical fragments of Ivanova, and Mincheva–Roussel.

The earliest formulation of the idea is due to Ivanova, followed by Mincheva and Roussel

Ivanova and Tarnopolskii 1979
;
Mincheva and Roussel 2007
;
Mincheva and Roussel 2007b
.
Working directly at the level of the Jacobian matrix, they introduced the notion
of a
critical fragment
: a minimal subnetwork whose contribution to the
Jacobian is responsible for a loss of stability; the surrounding network may be stabilizing, but cannot compensate for the
instability generated by the fragment.

2.

Jacobian determinant expansions and matchings

Subsequent work, notably by Mincheva, Banaji, Craciun
Mincheva and Craciun 2008
;
Banaji and Craciun 2010
, and collaborators, clarified
the algebraic structure underlying Jacobian minors and determinants.
Using Cauchy–Binet expansions, these works showed that determinants of Jacobian
sub-matrices can be written as sums indexed by matchings between species and
reactions.

3.

Vassena–Stadler: child selections

Vassena and Stadler introduced
child selections

Vassena 2023
;
Vassena and Stadler 2024
as explicit combinatorial
objects indexing the terms appearing in Jacobian determinant expansions.
A child selection is a bijection between a set of species and a set of reactions
in which they appear as reactants.

This formalization represents a conceptual shift: destabilizing mechanisms are
no longer detected indirectly via Jacobian minors, but directly through
well-defined combinatorial structures.
In particular, the sign of the determinant associated with a child selection
can be read off from the stoichiometric submatrix it defines.

4.

Blokhuis et al. 2025
introduce the notion of an
oscillatory core
, combining:
(i) a destabilizing UPF, and
(ii) a larger child selection whose associated Jacobian block is Hurwitz-stable
and contains the UPF as a principal substructure.

5.

The BiRNe framework
(Golnik, Gatter, Stadler, Vassena)
Golnik et al. 2025
builds on the child
selection formalism and provides an algorithmic procedure to enumerate such
structures.

Remark 2
.

Inheritance results for reaction networks explain how oscillatory
mechanisms identified in small cores persist under biologically motivated model
extensions.
In this sense, inheritance theory complements the critical fragment / child
selection program by transferring local bifurcations from analytically tractable
cores to larger networks.

Remark 3

(Child selections encode the permutation data in Cauchy–Binet)

Remark 5
.

-child-selection and let

Lemma 3

(Instability mechanisms for unstable cores)
.

Let

be an unstable core of size
k
.

1.

positive eigenvalue.

2.

Proof.

The parity restrictions follow directly from Lemma 2.
Minimality excludes the possibility of multiple unstable real eigenvalues or multiple unstable

Recall now the core ideas of the method of
Symbolic Hunt
of bifurcations for positive equilibria
Vassena 2023
:

1.

The Jacobian matrix
Jac
of (
1
) is made symbolic,

Jac:=\Gamma\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}(x)}{\partial x}=G(\mathbf{v}^{\prime}),

(9)

with ‘free’ symbols
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{jk}
which replace
\partial r_{j}/\partial x_{k}
(by the monotonicity assumption on rates, all symbols are strictly positive at a positive equilibrium).

Definition 7

(symbolic Jacobian)
.

We refer to the Jacobian matrix in which all the partial derivatives have been replaced by symbols as
symbolic Jacobian
.

Any equilibria bifurcation requires a choice of such symbols
\mathbf{v^{\prime}}
so that the symbolic Jacobian
G(\mathbf{v^{\prime}})
is non-hyperbolic, i.e., possesses eigenvalues with zero real part.

2.

The existence of a positive equilibrium of (
1
) necessarily requires the existence of a positive right kernel vector
equilibrium flux

\bar{\mathbf{v}}>0
of the stoichiometric matrix
\Gamma
, i.e.,

\Gamma\bar{\mathbf{v}}=0.

(10)

For the Capasso-Ruan-Wang model, the flux constraints at an endemic equilibrium
(\bar{s},\bar{i},\bar{r})>0
are:

\begin{cases}\Lambda=\mu_{s}\bar{s}+\mu_{i}\bar{i}+\mu_{r}\bar{r};\\
f(\bar{s},\bar{i})=h(\bar{i})+\mu_{i}\bar{i};\\
(\gamma_{r}+\mu_{r})\bar{r}=\gamma_{s}\bar{s}+h(\bar{i}).\end{cases}

(11)

Note that (
11
) together with
h(i)>0
imply that the existence of an endemic equilibrium requires

\gamma_{r}+\mu_{r}>0.

(12)

3.

Assuming the existence of an equilibrium flux vector
\bar{\mathbf{v}}
and of a choice of symbols
\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}
such that
G(\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime})
is non-hyperbolic, for any given parametric rate
r_{j}(x,p)
one needs to find
\bar{x}>0
and parameter values
\bar{p}
such that

\begin{cases}r_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{p})=\bar{v}_{j}\\
\partial r_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{p})/\partial x_{k}=\bar{v}^{\prime}_{jk}.\end{cases}

(13)

The common sense program above culminated in the introduction of “
parameter-rich kinetics
" in
Vassena and Stadler 2024
. These are defined by conditions which ensure, in the monotone case, the existence of positive solutions to (
13
) for any choice of
\bar{x}>0
, and which include Michaelis–Menten and Hill, but not mass action. For systems that include reaction rates that are in mass action form, we can still use the approach both to exclude bifurcations or as a first guidance to find a bifurcation. The full solution of (
13
) must be however pursued case-by-case.

Using this approach, we have proved in
Vassena et al. 2024
that a very common network pattern in ME implies that the symbolic Jacobian admits purely imaginary eigenvalues.

Theorem 2

(symbolic Jacobians of ME models admit always purely imaginary eigenvalues)

S

I

\begin{cases}\dot{s}=-r_{1}(s,i)+...\\

For systems that are parameter-rich and admit a positive endemic equilibrium, Theorem

A particular case

Theorem 4

(nonlinearity of T(i) is necessary for bifurcation in the Monod-Haldane class)
.

If

f[s,i]=\beta si\frac{1}{1+bi^{n}},\quad n\geq 1,

(18)

(which includes in particular Michaelis-Menten for

),
then for an equilibrium bifurcation to occur, the nonlinearity of

Proof.

Firstly note that

f^{\prime}_{i}(s,i)=\frac{\beta s(1-b(n-1)i^{n})}{(1+bi^{n})^{2}}\leq\frac{\beta s}{(1+bi^{n})^{2}}=\frac{\beta si}{(1+bi^{n})^{2}i}=\frac{f[s,i]}{(1+bi^{n})i}\leq\frac{f[s,i]}{i}.

(19)

Assume that

is linear, i.e.

. Fix an equilibrium

G_{ii}(\bar{s},\bar{i})=f^{\prime}_{i}(\bar{s},\bar{i})-\gamma_{i}-\mu_{i}\leq\frac{f(\bar{s},\bar{i})}{\bar{i}}-\gamma_{i}-\mu_{i}=\frac{f(\bar{s},\bar{i})-\gamma_{i}\bar{i}-\mu_{i}\bar{i}}{\bar{i}}=0
