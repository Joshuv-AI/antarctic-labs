Alternative posology for brolucizumab: a 6-week loading regimen is as effective as a 4-week regimen for the treatment of nAMD
Authors: Zufar Mulyukov, Ufuk Olgac, Jeffrey D Kearns, Chang Liu, Etienne Pigeolet, Georges Weissgerber
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundExtending the dosing interval with the anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) brolucizumab in the loading phase may reduce treatment burden while maintaining functional and anatomical outcomes for patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration.MethodsWe integrated population pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) and quantitative system pharmacology (QSP) modelling approaches to evaluate the effect of brolucizumab 6 mg on central subfield thickness (CSFT) and best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) using an alternative reduced loading regimen (two or three administrations every 6 weeks (q6w)) followed by individualised every 12 weeks/every 8 weeks (q12w/q8w) maintenance. The simulation results were compared with the CSFT and BCVA outcomes in the phase 3 HAWK and HARRIER studies, in which patients received the approved loading regimen of 3 monthly doses.ResultsFor both loading regimens, simulations of the QSP model showed almost complete VEGF inhibition during the loading phase and free VEGF recovery commenced 6‒7 weeks following the last loading dose, returning to baseline levels after 12‒13 weeks.The population PK/PD simulations showed that week 48 and week 36–48 mean BCVA and CSFT changes from baseline using the alternative loading regimen and individualised maintenance treatment were similar to HAWK and HARRIER study measures. Simulated disease activity assessments indicate that only 20% of patients needed third q6w loading dose before switching to q12w/q8w maintenance treatment.ConclusionsThis integrated modelling approach predicted that reducing the loading regimen of brolucizumab 6 mg treatment to two or three doses every 6 weeks is expected to result in similar efficacy outcomes, resulting in regulatory approval of this alternative dosing regimen in several countries.Keywords: Vision, Neovascularisation, Macular Degeneration

Introduction

Patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) often experience considerable burden associated with repeated anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) injections and frequent follow-up visits to the clinic, which may result in low treatment adherence, poor visual outcomes and ultimately, vision loss.1 2 According to the current nAMD treatment standards, individualised treatment schedules that tailor anti-VEGF intravitreal (IVT) injection frequencies to the re-occurrence of active disease help to reduce treatment burden while maintaining the optimal visual acuity for patients.3 4

Brolucizumab 6 mg, an anti-VEGF therapy, was approved for the treatment of nAMD based on data from the pivotal phase 3 HAWK and HARRIER clinical trials,5 6 in which all patients received a loading regimen of three doses every 4 weeks (q4w) followed by an every 12 weeks (q12w) or 8 weeks (q8w) individualised maintenance regimen in the brolucizumab 3 mg and 6 mg arms and fixed q8w maintenance regimen in the aflibercept 2 mg arm. In these studies, brolucizumab was associated with a faster and more effective resolution of the retinal fluid than aflibercept with comparable visual outcomes (ie, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), primary outcome assessed at week 48), which suggests that the intensity of the loading regimen could be reduced. Brolucizumab 6 mg also demonstrated greater reductions from baseline versus aflibercept 2 mg in central subfield thickness (CSFT), which is an important indicator of disease activity (DA) reduction, throughout the 96 weeks of the study, starting from first injection.5 6 Additionally, in the KESTREL and KITE phase 3 studies for the diabetic macular oedema indication, brolucizumab dosed at a q6w interval in the loading phase resulted in similar efficacy through week 96 compared with aflibercept dosed at a q4w interval in the loading phase.7 8

In the evolving clinical trial landscape, the model-informed drug discovery and development approach that integrates mathematical modelling and simulation has been introduced to address regulatory and other relevant questions for optimising drug development processes and improving decision-making efficiency.9 The objective of the current modelling and simulation analysis was to investigate the effects of reduced loading with brolucizumab 6 mg on BCVA gain, CSFT reduction and retinal VEGF suppression. To this end, a population non-linear mixed-effects pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) model was developed to simulate BCVA gain and CSFT reduction and the results for reduced loading were compared with the observed results of the brolucizumab 6 mg treatment arms in the HAWK and HARRIER trials (3×q4w loading doses followed by individualised q12w/q8w maintenance). To complement the analysis from a mechanistic point of view, a quantitative system pharmacology (QSP) model was used to predict VEGF suppression in the retina for the reduced loading regimen. The results of this integrated modelling and simulation analysis have led to an European Union label update10 for brolucizumab, which includes an individualised alternative to the approved fixed loading regimen (ie, 3×q4w loading doses) in which a total of 2 or 3 brolucizumab 6 mg loading doses may now be administered every 6 weeks (q6w). At the time of writing, the alternative loading regimen is also approved in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Egypt, Hong Kong, India, Malaysia, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Switzerland, Thailand and the UK.

Methods

Clinical data

Data from patients with nAMD treated with ranibizumab, brolucizumab or aflibercept in the phase I/II SEE (N=194),11 phase II OSPREY (N=89)12 and the phase III HAWK (N=1078) and HARRIER (N=739)5 6 studies online supplemental etable 1, were included in this modelling analysis. All patients included in these studies were treatment naïve. The study designs and inclusion/exclusion criteria for the four brolucizumab studies included herein are published elsewhere.5 6 11 12

QSP model

The QSP model was used to predict retinal VEGF inhibition for an alternative brolucizumab loading regimen. The application of this model to brolucizumab considered the rapid distribution of a single chain variable fragment antibody in the eye owing to its small size (hydrodynamic radius) and inhibition of VEGF (formation of complexes of brolucizumab and VEGF). A detailed model description has been published previously, including tables of reactions, species and parameters as well as executable code in MATLAB SimBiology (The Mathworks).13 The QSP model is a mechanistic model consisting of three interconnected compartments (aqueous, vitreous and retinal). Following an IVT injection, the model simulates the distribution of drug within and in between these compartments, as well as the clearance out of the eye via the retinal and aqueous compartments, and the dynamics of VEGF production and inhibition by drug. IVT injection of a VEGF antagonist (eg, brolucizumab) at specified doses and times can be simulated with the QSP model. VEGF is produced in the retinal compartment and is modelled as a zero-order reaction in which new VEGF appears in the retina (ie, VEGF synthesis). VEGF is present in the eye in a dimeric form consisting of two VEGF monomers and can thus form complexes with one or two VEGF antagonists. VEGF distribution between the compartments and clearance out of the eye is modelled similar to the VEGF antagonists. The QSP model was used to simulate and to compare the originally approved brolucizumab 3×q4w loading regimen with an alternative 2×q6w loading followed by an q12w or q8w maintenance phase. Executable MATLAB SimBiology scripts for these simulations as well as model description, equations and parameters are provided in the online supplemental material.

Population PK/PD model

A non-linear, mixed effects modelling approach was used to jointly model the anti-VEGF effect on CSFT and BCVA change from baseline (figure 1). A one-compartment model was used to describe the vitreous PK of anti-VEGF drugs, in which brolucizumab vitreal elimination half-life (T1/2) was fixed to 4.3 days obtained from a separately developed population PK analysis.10 CSFT was modelled as a sum of time-independent normal thickness (Rnorm) and disease-induced thickness Rfluid(t) that can be reduced by anti-VEGF treatment. A logistic growth model with added drug effect was used to describe the effect of anti-VEGF on Rfluid(t) whereas BCVA improvement was governed by CSFT reduction after delay using an effect compartment. Interindividual variability included for normal CSFT Rnorm, baseline disease-related CSFT Rfluid, drug effect on CSFT, CSFT growth rate, CSFT reduction effect on BCVA and CSFT reduction effect delay on BCVA (online supplemental appendix). This model was used for clinical trial simulations of individualised treatment regimen based on DA.

Simulations for DA assessment

In the HAWK and HARRIER studies after the loading phase, brolucizumab-treated eyes were intended to receive injections q12w but were adjusted to q8w if DA was detected during any of the pre-specified DA assessment visits. The general guidance and criteria for DA assessment were provided in the study protocols in terms of BCVA, CSFT or anatomical features worsening, the latter two being evaluated by optical coherence tomography (OCT). The decision to adjust to 8-week treatment intervals was at the investigator’s discretion.

Instead of the flexible definition of DA in the studies, an algorithmically quantifiable DA criterion using modelled endpoints was needed for DA simulations. As the current standard of anti-VEGF treatment individualisation is largely guided by OCT,14 the DA assessment and treatment interval individualisation were approximated using the simulated dynamics of CSFT which is the best available proxy to quantify retinal fluid in OCT images. For clinical trial simulations CSFT value above 340 µm11 or CSFT increase from Week 12 (maximum loading effect visit in the fixed 3×q4w loading regimen) above 75 µm15 was defined as DA presence leading to switch individual patients to q8w treatment. This DA simulation algorithm replicated HAWK and HARRIER efficacy data well in terms of CSFT reduction, BCVA improvement and per cent of patients on q12w at week 48. These thresholds have been used in clinical trials to trigger anti-VEGF retreatment and are therefore considered clinically meaningful. A sensitivity analysis was also performed to check that the model was able to detect sub-optimal anti-VEGF treatment. A regimen consisting of 2×q8w loading doses followed by q12w maintenance treatment without individualisation was simulated and the results were compared with the observed data for 3×q4w loading regimen followed by an individualised q12w/q8w maintenance regimen in the 6 mg arms of both studies. It visually demonstrated that the model was able to show even moderate undertreatment in these patients (results not shown).

Simulation of a new alternative regimen for brolucizumab 6 mg dose (two/three q6w loading doses and q12w/q8w maintenance)

An in silico treatment algorithm was developed to simulate the reduced loading regimen of interest—a 2×q6w or 3×q6w loading followed by individualised q12w/q8w maintenance in the brolucizumab 6 mg treatment arms of the HAWK and HARRIER studies (figure 2). At week 12, 6 weeks after the second loading injection, a DA assessment was simulated: if DA was present, the patient received an injection and was treated q8w thereafter; if DA was absent, the patient was scheduled for a week 18 visit, 12 weeks from the last loading dose, and DA was assessed again. At this week 18 visit, the patient was treated independently of DA, but if DA was present, then the patient was switched to q8w, otherwise the patient was treated q12w with DA assessment at the end of each 12-week treatment interval. At any subsequent positive DA assessments, patients were switched to q8w treatment. The HAWK and HARRIER study populations, defined by baseline BCVA, CSFT and age, were simulated based on distribution of inter-individual variabilities of population PK/PD model. The studies were simulated 20 times with this regimen to estimate standard errors of the means of simulated values for BCVA change from baseline, CSFT change from baseline and percent of patients on q12w treatment interval at week 48.

Results

VEGF inhibition simulations

Simulations of the QSP model were performed to assess the predicted level of retinal VEGF inhibition in response to the two loading dose regimens (3×q4w and 2×q6w). To account for the population variability in baseline VEGF levels, simulations were performed with low (3.7 fmol/day), mean (17.5 fmol/day) and high (31.3 fmol/day) values for the VEGF synthesis parameter, as previously described.13 The QSP simulations demonstrated that for the loading phase (figure 3A), both regimens provide almost complete VEGF inhibition (near zero retinal VEGF concentrations), with recovery of free VEGF beginning approximately 6–7 weeks after the last dose. A complete recovery to baseline VEGF levels was predicted to occur at approximately 12–13 weeks after the last dose of each loading regimen, which is consistent with the ocular half-life of the drug (ie, clearance of the drug from the eye) of 4.3 days estimated from separate population PK analysis.10 No apparent difference in VEGF inhibition was observed in the maintenance phase following either loading regimen (figure 3B).

Population PK/PD model evaluation

The PK/PD model was fit to CSFT and BCVA data from four clinical studies in patients with nAMD (HAWK and HARRIER, SEE and OSPREY) online supplemental efigure 1 The goodness-of-fit plots for CSFT and BCVA showed good predictive performance of the model (figure 4 for the 6 mg arms, online supplemental efigure 2 for all studies and arms, online supplemental material). In general, the model captured central tendencies and variability of CSFT data well. For BCVA, the median values were well captured; however, predicted variability of BCVA change from baseline was higher in some treatment arms due to high variability in the modelled data (HAWK and OSPREY studies vs HARRIER study, for 6 mg brolucizumab). Analysis of goodness-of-fit plots and results of visual predictive checks indicate that the model is suitable for treatment-naïve nAMD population simulations.

Simulated versus observed mean BCVA and CSFT change from baseline during the study

After 2×q6w or 3×q6w loading doses and individualised q12w/q8w treatment in the maintenance phase, simulated mean BCVA change from baseline was similar to the observed data at week 48 and over the week 36‒48 interval in the HAWK and HARRIER studies (figure 5A). Simulated BCVA values were numerically lower than the observed values in HAWK and numerically higher in HARRIER; however, these differences are not considered clinically relevant. At week 48 and for the week 36–48 interval, there were no significant differences between simulated and observed BCVA changes from baseline.

Similarly, simulated CSFT change from baseline after 2×q6w or 3×q6w loading doses and individualised q12w/q8w treatment in the maintenance phase was similar to the observed HAWK and HARRIER study data at week 48 and over the week 36‒48 interval (figure 5B). Simulated CSFT values are numerically lower than the observed values in HAWK and numerically higher in HARRIER. At week 48 and for the week 36–48 interval, there were no significant differences between simulated and observed CSFT changes from baseline.

Percentage of patients remaining on q12w regimen at the end of first year treatment

After the alternative q6w loading regimen, the percentages of patients remaining on q12w treatment interval at the end of the simulated studies (week 48) in the brolucizumab 6 mg HAWK and HARRIER populations were 51.0% and 50.4%, respectively, which is slightly lower than the reported 55.6% in HAWK and 51.0% in HARRIER study.3 The alternative loading may therefore lead to a small (<5%) reduction of percentage of patients on q12w maintenance treatment interval compared with 3 monthly loading doses.

Patients requiring a third q6w loading dose at week 12

The percentage (SE) of simulated patients who required a third loading dose at week 12 was 20.4% (1.0%). Therefore, for approximately 80% of the treatment-naïve nAMD patients, loading with two q6w doses of brolucizumab 6 mg is expected to be sufficient before switching to the q12w/q8w maintenance period. Patients with high baseline CSFT are more likely to need a third loading dose; for example, among patients with baseline CSFT above 500 µm, 41% require a third loading dose compared with overall 20.4%.

Discussion

The QSP model13 used for simulating the intraocular drug PK and VEGF inhibition for alternative dosing regimen was developed by adapting and expanding on a series of previously published models16,19 that elucidate the relationship between intravitreal administration of anti-VEGF biologics, intraocular VEGF and clearance mechanisms in the eye. In summary, the QSP model simulations reveal that a 2×q6w loading regimen achieves similar (near-complete) retinal VEGF inhibition as a 3×q4w regimen, regardless of baseline VEGF levels. Second, there is no apparent difference in drug PK or VEGF inhibition in the maintenance period following either loading dose regimen (3×q4w or 2×q6w). Third, the extent of free retinal VEGF recovery with q8w and q12w maintenance dosing is more apparent under conditions of high VEGF synthesis. The second and third observations are expected, as the model simulations assume that VEGF synthesis is constant during the treatment period and is not modulated by drug activity. Together, QSP posology simulations suggest that reduced dose intensity within the loading period may still maintain robust VEGF inhibition and that patient factors such as intraocular VEGF levels may influence the ability to maintain VEGF inhibition with prolonged dosing intervals in the maintenance period.

The developed population PK/PD model of CSFT and BCVA performed suitably in capturing response to the treatment and the differences of effects for modelled drugs and doses. The between-subject BCVA variability was slightly overestimated for the brolucizumab 6 mg dose in HARRIER, which is due to the observed larger variability in the HAWK and OSPREY studies compared with HARRIER. However, between-study differences in BCVA were not modelled intentionally to simulate brolucizumab effect and its variability with a different dosing regimen in the general nAMD population. All parameters were well estimated by this model, and therefore, it was considered fit for purpose for comparing treatment differences between the current and alternative loading regimens in nAMD population.

Of note, numerical differences between simulated and observed data were not systematically in one direction in the HAWK and HARRIER studies.5 6 The differences between simulated and observed BCVA improvements were at a maximum of 0.8 letters, and for CSFT decreases were less than 12 µm. These small numerical differences could be attributable to expected between-study variations of the observed results, and therefore, not considered clinically relevant. Since brolucizumab 6 mg simulations are in good agreement with the observed data for both efficacy metrics in the HAWK and HARRIER studies, we consider these simulations to be an adequate representation of treatment effect in the treated population.

Results of this modelling and simulation analysis confirmed that reducing brolucizumab 6 mg treatment during the loading phase from 3×q4w doses to 2× or 3×q6w doses is expected to lead to similar efficacy outcomes at the cost of a <5% reduction of patients on q12w maintenance treatment at the end of the first year of treatment. Notably, for the majority of the patient population (approximately 80%), two q6w loading doses were sufficient to switch to maintenance treatment. From a clinical perspective, loading with two brolucizumab 6 mg injections 6 weeks apart, followed by a DA assessment visit another 6 weeks after the second loading dose and subsequent treatment at q12w or q8w could be an appropriate treatment scenario. Furthermore, the risk of undertreatment was minimised as the alternative dosing regimen10 recommends DA assessment at week 12 and retreatment, if necessary.

Limitations of the QSP model include that the model only considers the relationship between intraocular drug PK and target engagement (VEGF inhibition) and does not consider efficacy endpoints such as modulation of visual acuity. The recovery of VEGF levels may not translate to a simultaneous loss of efficacy which may in fact occur sometime later. However, the QSP model is complementing the population PK/PD model for assessment of loading regimens by providing a quantitative insight at the mechanism of action. While it is expected that there is a time delay between recovery of disease-associated retinal VEGF levels and loss of efficacy, this relationship is not fully understood. As such, the loss of retinal VEGF inhibition observed in the QSP model is likely a conservative estimate for the durability of drug effect.

The population PK/PD model has potential limitations also. First, the between-subject variability in BCVA change from baseline was not perfectly reproduced by the model, leading to its predicted 10th and 90th percentiles being slightly wider than the observed ones. This makes the model unsuitable for predicting extreme responders eg, proportion of patients with >15 letters change from baseline. However, an accurate capture of mean BCVA response (maximum 1–2 letter-deviation across the 2 studies), given the large between-subject variability of approximately 25 letters (~ −5 to ~+20 letters for the 10th to 90th percentiles) indicates that this model is fit for the comparison of the average treatment difference between the various treatment regimens.

Second, the presence of DA was approximated using the CSFT variable, which is only part of the information commonly used for DA assessment, with information such as the presence of intraretinal haemorrhage not accounted for. The thresholds for CSFT and CSFT change from Week 12 to approximate DA presence in the simulations were selected to match the proportions of subjects on q12w intervals at week 48. However, since the model closely reproduced the efficacy results of the observed data, the simulated DA assessments appear to adequately represent individual treatment needs of this population.

In conclusion, data from the modelling and simulation analysis confirm that reducing brolucizumab 6 mg treatment during the loading phase from 3×q4w doses to 2×q6w or 3×q6w doses is expected to lead to similar efficacy outcomes. Hence, a less intense loading regimen could potentially contribute to a reduced treatment burden for patients via fewer loading dose injections and less frequent follow-up visits to the clinic. This, in turn, could potentially improve patient treatment adherence through individualised treatment schedules tailored to DA leading to improved visual outcomes and ultimately reduced vision loss. The population PK/PD model was a subject of rigorous regulatory scrutiny before reduced loading posology was approved based only on clinical trial simulations. Though the data was not presented in this article, simulations of fixed two and three q6w loading doses, simulations of all patients maintained on q8w and q12w, simulations and assessment of statistical significance of difference between simulated and observed mean BCVA and CSFT over weeks 4–12, 4–16, 36–48 and week 48 were reviewed, as well as assessment of differences between simulations and observation in 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of the data. Impact of various CSFT-based DA definitions was also assessed. The analysis presented here is an example of the Model-Informed Drug Development approach which was proven to present sufficiently strong evidence to support its application for subsequent regulatory filings.
