On Lexicographic Product and Multi-Word-Representability
Benny George Kenkireth, Gopalan Sajith, Sreyas Sasidharan

Abstract
We investigate the relationship between the lexicographic product of graphs and their multi-word-representation number. Although the lexicographic product of two word-representable graphs need not itself be word-representable, a precise characterization has not previously been established. We provide a complete characterization, showing that for word-representable graphs G 1 G_{1} and G 2 G_{2} , the lexicographic product G 1 ∘ G 2 G_{1}\circ G_{2} is word-representable if and only if G 2 G_{2} is a comparability graph. For lexicographic powers, we prove that G [ k ] G^{[k]} is word-representable if and only if G G is a comparability graph. The multi-word-representation number μ \mu for lexicographic powers and products satisfies the following bounds. If G G is a non-comparability graph, then μ ⁡ ( G [ k ] ) ≤ k \mu(G^{[k]})\leq k , whereas if G G is the union of two comparability graphs, then μ ⁡ ( G [ k ] ) = 2 \mu(G^{[k]})=2 . More generally, for graphs G 1 G_{1} and G 2 G_{2} with μ ⁡ ( G 1 ) = k 1 \mu(G_{1})=k_{1} and μ ⁡ ( G 2 ) = k 2 \mu(G_{2})=k_{2} , the lexicographic product H = G 1 ∘ G 2 H=G_{1}\circ G_{2} satisfies the upper bound μ ⁡ ( H ) ≤ k 1 + k 2 \mu(H)\leq k_{1}+k_{2} . This bound is tight, with equality μ ⁡ ( H ) = k 1 \mu(H)=k_{1} , when k 1 ≥ k 2 k_{1}\geq k_{2} and G 2 G_{2} is the union of k 1 k_{1} comparability graphs. Moreover, if G 1 G_{1} and G 2 G_{2} are minimal non-word-representable graphs, then μ ⁡ ( G 1 ∘ G 2 ) ≤ 3 \mu(G_{1}\circ G_{2})\leq 3 . Finally, we study the function τ ⁡ ( n ) \tau(n) , which measures the size of the largest word-representable induced subgraph guaranteed in every n n -vertex graph. By constructing extremal graphs via lexicographic powers, we establish a sublinear upper bound, showing that τ ⁡ ( n ) ≤ n 0.86 \tau(n)\leq n^{0.86} for sufficiently large n n .



1
Introduction

The notion of word-representable graphs was introduced by Kitaev and Seif in
[
7
]
and has since attracted considerable attention (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
2
,
8
]
). A graph
G=(V,E)
is
word-representable
if there exists a word
w
over the alphabet
V
such that, for any distinct vertices
x,y\in V
,
(x,y)\in E
if and only if the occurrences of
x
and
y
alternate in
w
. In this case, the word
w
is said to
represent
the graph
G
.

Word-representable graphs are of particular interest in graph theory, as they include several well-known graph classes such as
3
-colorable graphs, subcubic graphs, and comparability graphs
[
5
]
. Moreover, they admit a characterization in terms of
semi-transitive orientations
: a graph is word-representable if and only if it admits a semi-transitive orientation
[
5
]
. An orientation of a graph is
semi-transitive
if it is acyclic and contains no shortcuts; that is, there is no directed path
v_{1}\to\cdots\to v_{k}
together with an edge
v_{1}\to v_{k}
such that some edge
v_{i}\to v_{r}
with
1\leq i<r\leq k
is missing (see Section
2
for a formal definition). Comparability graphs, which admit a transitive orientation, form a natural subclass of word-representable graphs.

The concept of
multi-word-representability
was introduced by Kenkireth and Malhotra in
[
3
]
as a natural extension of word-representability. A graph
G
is said to be
k
-multi-word-representable
if it can be expressed as the union of at most
k
word-representable graphs, where the union is taken over both vertex and edge sets. Equivalently,
G
is
k
-multi-word-representable if there exists a collection of at most
k
words such that each word represents a subgraph of
G
, and the union of these subgraphs is
G
. The
multi-word-representation number
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\mu(G)
, is the smallest integer
k
such that
G
is
k
-multi-word-representable. Clearly, any word-representable graph has
\mu(G)=1
.

The function
\tau(n)
is an extremal graph-theoretic function that measures the size of the largest word-representable induced subgraph guaranteed to exist in every graph on
n
vertices. This function was introduced and studied in
[
4
]
, where its values were computed for
n\leq 9
and connections to multi-word-representability were also discussed. In this paper, we establish a sublinear upper bound on
\tau(n)
. This result indicates that, although there are connections between
\tau(n)
and multi-word-representability as noted in
[
4
]
, in general, extracting maximum-size word-representable induced subgraphs provides limited effectiveness, since the size of such subgraphs grows sublinearly with
n
.

The
lexicographic product
of two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, denoted by
G_{1}\circ G_{2}
, is obtained by replacing each vertex of
G_{1}
with a copy of
G_{2}
and connecting vertices between these copies according to the adjacency relations in
G_{1}
(see Section
2
for a formal definition). The
k
-th
lexicographic power
of a graph
G
, denoted by
G^{[k]}
, is the iterated lexicographic product of
G
with itself
k
times.

It has been observed that the lexicographic product of two word-representable graphs is not necessarily word-representable; an explicit counterexample was given in
[
1
]
. However, a complete characterization of when the lexicographic product is word-representable has not yet been established. In this paper, we provide such a characterization and further explore the relationship between the lexicographic product of graphs and their multi-word-representation number.

Our Contributions.

This paper presents the following results.

1.

We provide a complete characterization of the word-representability of lexicographic products. Specifically, if
G_{1}
is a non-empty word-representable graph and
G_{2}
is any graph, then the lexicographic product
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
is word-representable if and only if
G_{2}
is a comparability graph. Moreover, if
G_{2}
is not a comparability graph, then
\mu(H)=2
.

2.

We analyze the behavior of comparability under lexicographic products. If both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are comparability graphs, then
G_{1}\circ G_{2}
is a comparability graph. Conversely, if
G_{1}
is not a comparability graph while
G_{2}
is, then
G_{1}\circ G_{2}
is not a comparability graph.

3.

We extend the characterization to lexicographic powers. For a non-empty word-representable graph
G
and an integer
k\geq 2
, the lexicographic power
G^{[k]}
is word-representable if and only if
G
is a comparability graph.

4.

We establish bounds on the multi-word-representation number of lexicographic powers. If
G
is a non-comparability word-representable graph, then
\mu(G^{[k]})\leq k
for all
k\geq 2
. Furthermore, if
G
is the union of two comparability graphs, then
\mu(G^{[k]})=2
for all
k\geq 2
, and
G^{[k]}
itself remains a union of two comparability graphs.

5.

We derive general bounds for lexicographic products. For graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
with
\mu(G_{1})=k_{1}
and
\mu(G_{2})=k_{2}
, the product
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
satisfies
\mu(H)\leq k_{1}+k_{2}
. Moreover, if
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
and
G_{2}
is the union of
k_{1}
comparability graphs, then
\mu(H)=k_{1}
. Additionally, if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are minimal non-word-representable graphs, then
\mu(G_{1}\circ G_{2})\leq 3
.

6.

Finally, we establish extremal bounds for the function
\tau(n)
. In particular, for every integer
k\geq 1
, we show that
\tau(8^{k})\leq 6^{k}
, which implies the sublinear bound
\tau(n)\leq n^{0.86}
for sufficiently large
n
.

2
Preliminaries

All graphs considered in this paper are simple and undirected. For a graph
G
, we denote its vertex set by
V(G)
and its edge set by
E(G)
. For
A\subseteq V(G)
, the induced subgraph on
A
is denoted by
G[A]
.

Definition 1

A class of graphs
\mathcal{X}
is
hereditary
if for every
G\in\mathcal{X}
, all induced subgraphs of
G
also belong to
\mathcal{X}
.

Examples of hereditary classes include planar graphs, comparability graphs, and word-representable graphs
[
5
]
. Consequently, if a graph contains a non-comparability induced subgraph, it is non-comparability; similarly, if it contains a non-word-representable induced subgraph, it is non-word-representable.

Definition 2

A graph
G
is
word-representable
if there exists a word
w
over the alphabet
V(G)
such that, for every distinct pair of vertices
x,y\in V(G)
,
(x,y)\in E(G)
if and only if
x
and
y
alternate in
w
. The word
w
is called a
word-representant
of
G
.

Definition 3

A graph
G
is a
minimal non-word-representable graph
if
G
is not word-representable, but all its proper induced subgraphs are word-representable.

Definition 4

A graph
G
is
semi-transitive
if it admits an acyclic orientation such that for every directed path

v_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow v_{k}
, either

v_{1}\not\rightarrow v_{k}
, or

v_{i}\rightarrow v_{j}
exists for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
. If
v_{1}\rightarrow v_{k}
is present while some

v_{i}\rightarrow v_{j}
with
i<j
is missing, this forms a
shortcut

[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.1

A graph
G
is word-representable if and only if it admits a semi-transitive orientation.
[
5
]

Definition 5

A graph
G
is a
comparability graph
if its edges can be oriented transitively:
a\rightarrow b
and
b\rightarrow c
imply
a\rightarrow c
. Otherwise,
G
is a non-comparability graph.

Comparability graphs form a natural subclass of word-representable graphs. Indeed, any transitive orientation is, by definition, semi-transitive.

Theorem 2.2

[
6
]

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices, and let
x\in V(G)
be a vertex of degree
n-1
(that is,
x
is adjacent to all other vertices in
G
).
Let
H
be the induced subgraph of
G
with
V(H)=V(G)\setminus\{x\}
.
Then,
G
is word-representable if and only if
H
is a comparability graph.

Definition 6

For graphs
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
, their union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}G_{i}
is the graph
G
with
V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(G_{i})
and
E(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}E(G_{i})
.

Definition 7

The
multi-word-representation number
of a graph
G
, denoted
\mu(G)
, is the smallest positive integer
k
such that
G
can be represented as the union of
k
word-representable graphs.

Theorem 2.3

Let
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}
be word-representable graphs with disjoint vertex sets. Then their union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}
is word-representable
[
3
]
.

Definition 8

Let
G
be a graph and
S\subseteq V(G)
.

•

The set
S
is
representable
if the induced subgraph
G[S]
is word-representable.

•

A representable set
S
is
maximum
if it has the largest cardinality among all representable subsets of
V(G)
.

•

Let
\eta(G)
denote the size of a maximum representable set of
G
. This is well-defined since all maximum representable sets have the same size.

Definition 9

For a positive integer
n
, the function
\tau(n)
is defined by

\tau(n)=\min\{\eta(G)\mid G\text{ is a graph on }n\text{ vertices}\}.

Definition 10

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs. The
lexicographic product

H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
is defined as follows:

•

The vertex set of
H
is
V(H)=V(G_{1})\times V(G_{2})
.

•

Two vertices
(u,v)
and
(x,y)
in
H
are adjacent in
H
if and only if

1.

\{u,x\}\in E(G_{1})
, or

2.

u=x
and
\{v,y\}\in E(G_{2})
.

•

Alternatively, the lexicographic product can be visualized as follows: each vertex
v_{i}\in V(G_{1})
is replaced by a set of vertices

V_{i}=\{(v_{i},u)\mid u\in V(G_{2})\}\subseteq V(H),

which we call a
supervertex
. The subgraph of
H
induced by
V_{i}
is isomorphic to
G_{2}
.

•

For each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G_{1})
, every vertex in
V_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex in
V_{j}
.

•

If
v_{i}v_{j}\notin E(G_{1})
, then there are no edges between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
.

Definition 11

Let
G
be a graph and
k
a positive integer. The
k
-th lexicographic power
of
G
, denoted
G^{[k]}
, is defined recursively as

G^{[1]}=G,\quad G^{[k]}=G^{[k-1]}\circ G\text{ for }k\geq 2,

where
\circ
denotes the lexicographic product.

Remark:
Since the lexicographic product is associative,
G^{[k]}
is well-defined. Equivalently, one may write
G^{[k]}=G\circ G^{[k-1]}
.

The lexicographic product and its power are standard notions. The following definitions are introduced to simplify the presentation and proofs of the subsequent results.

Definition 12

Let
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
be the lexicographic product of graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, and let
G_{1}^{\prime}
be a subgraph of
G_{1}
.
The
lexicographic map of
G_{1}^{\prime}
in
H
, denoted
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1}^{\prime})
, is the subgraph of
H
defined as follows:

1.

No edges within any supervertex
V_{i}
are included.

2.

For each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G_{1}^{\prime})
, all edges between the corresponding supervertices
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
are included.

Here, each supervertex is
V_{i}=\{(v_{i},u):u\in V(G_{2})\}
.

Definition 13

Let
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
, and let
G_{1}^{\prime}
be a word-representable subgraph of
G_{1}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1}^{\prime})
denote its lexicographic map in
H
.
A
special subgraph of
H
is defined for any such
G_{1}^{\prime}
. It is any subgraph obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1}^{\prime})
by adding edges within each supervertex

so that the induced subgraph on
V_{i}
is a comparability graph (possibly containing isolated vertices).

Observation:
If
G_{1}^{\prime}=G_{1}
and each supervertex

induces a copy of
G_{2}
, the resulting graph coincides with

itself.

For a special subgraph

, its
underlying lexicographic map
is the spanning subgraph obtained by deleting all edges within the supervertices.

3
Results

We first establish four intermediate theorems based on the new definitions, which are then used to prove the main results.

Theorem 3.1

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs, and let
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
be their lexicographic product.
Then, for any word-representable subgraph
G_{1}^{\prime}
of
G_{1}
, the lexicographic map
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1}^{\prime})
is word-representable.

Proof

Let
G_{1}^{\prime}
be an arbitrary word-representable subgraph of
G_{1}
, and let
H^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1}^{\prime})
denote its lexicographic map in
H
.
We show that
H^{\prime}
is word-representable by constructing a semi-transitive orientation. Consider a semi-transitive orientation of
G_{1}^{\prime}
.
We extend this orientation to
H^{\prime}
as follows. For any pair of supervertices
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, the edges between them in
H^{\prime}
correspond to the edge
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E(G_{1}^{\prime})
.
If
v_{i}\rightarrow v_{j}
in
G_{1}^{\prime}
, we orient every edge between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
from each vertex of
V_{i}
to each vertex of
V_{j}
.
Conversely, if
v_{j}\rightarrow v_{i}
, all edges between
V_{j}
and
V_{i}
are oriented from
V_{j}
to
V_{i}
. Since all edges between supervertices are oriented uniformly, any directed path in
H^{\prime}
projects to a directed path in
G_{1}^{\prime}
. Therefore, any directed cycle or violation of semi-transitivity in
H^{\prime}
would induce a corresponding directed cycle or semi-transitivity violation in
G_{1}^{\prime}
, which is impossible.
Hence, the induced orientation of
H^{\prime}
is semi-transitive, and it follows that
H^{\prime}
is word-representable.
∎

Theorem 3.2

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs, and let
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
be their lexicographic product.
For any subgraph
G_{1}^{\prime}
of
G_{1}
,
G_{1}^{\prime}
is a comparability graph if and only if the lexicographic map
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1}^{\prime})
is a comparability graph.

Proof

Let
G_{1}^{\prime}
be an arbitrary subgraph of
G_{1}
, and let
H^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1}^{\prime})
denote its lexicographic map in
H
.

(\Rightarrow)
Assume that
G_{1}^{\prime}
is a comparability graph. We show that
H^{\prime}
is also a comparability graph by constructing a transitive orientation. Consider a transitive orientation of
G_{1}^{\prime}
. We extend this orientation to
H^{\prime}
exactly as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
: for any two supervertices
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, if
v_{i}\to v_{j}
in
G_{1}^{\prime}
, then every edge between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
is oriented from
V_{i}
to
V_{j}
;
otherwise, if
v_{j}\to v_{i}
, all edges between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
are oriented from
V_{j}
to
V_{i}
. By this uniform orientation, any directed path in
H^{\prime}
projects onto a directed path in
G_{1}^{\prime}
. Consequently, the transitivity of the orientation in
G_{1}^{\prime}
ensures that the orientation of
H^{\prime}
is transitive. Therefore,
H^{\prime}
is a comparability graph.

(\Leftarrow)
Assume that
G_{1}^{\prime}
is not a comparability graph. Select one vertex from each supervertex in
H^{\prime}
, and consider the subgraph induced by these vertices. Since
G_{1}^{\prime}
is not a comparability graph, this induced subgraph is also non-comparability. Because any graph containing a non-comparability induced subgraph is itself not a comparability graph, it follows that
H^{\prime}
is not a comparability graph.
∎

Theorem 3.3

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs, and let
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
.
Then, any special subgraph of
H
is word-representable.

Proof

Let
H_{s}
be an arbitrary special subgraph of
H
.
Partition the edges of
H_{s}
into two types: edges between distinct supervertices are colored
red
, and edges within each supervertex are colored
green
.

To construct a semi-transitive orientation of
H_{s}
, we proceed as follows.
First, consider the red edges. These edges form the lexicographic map of a word-representable subgraph of
G_{1}
.
By Theorem
3.1
, they admit a semi-transitive orientation.
Explicitly, for any two supervertices
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, if the corresponding edge in
G_{1}^{\prime}
is oriented
v_{i}\rightarrow v_{j}
, then all red edges between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
are oriented from vertices of
V_{i}
to vertices of
V_{j}
.

Next, consider the green edges. Within each supervertex
V_{i}
, the green edges induce a comparability graph, and we assign a transitive orientation to these edges.
We now show that the resulting orientation is acyclic and semi-transitive.

Acyclicity.
Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a directed cycle in
H_{s}
, and let
C
be a cycle of minimum length.
Since the orientation of the red edges is semi-transitive by Theorem
3.1
, the red edges form an acyclic orientation.
Moreover, within each supervertex, the green edges induce a transitive orientation of a comparability graph, and hence they are acyclic.
Therefore,
C
must contain both red and green edges.
Consider a green edge
g_{1}\rightarrow g_{2}
in
C
that immediately follows a red edge along the cycle.
If the next edge in
C
is another green edge
g_{2}\rightarrow g_{3}
, then all three vertices
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}
lie within the same supervertex.
By transitivity of the green orientation, the edge
g_{1}\rightarrow g_{3}
exists, allowing
g_{2}
to be bypassed and producing a shorter cycle, contradicting the minimality of
C
.
Hence, in a minimal cycle, no green edge is immediately followed by another green edge.

It follows that each green edge in
C
must be followed by a red edge connecting to a different supervertex.
Let
g_{1}\rightarrow g_{2}
be such a green edge, followed by a red edge
g_{2}\rightarrow y
, where
y
lies in a different supervertex.
By the uniform orientation of red edges between supervertices, the edge
g_{1}\rightarrow y
also exists, allowing
g_{2}
to be bypassed.
Repeating this argument for all green edges in
C
, any minimal cycle would consist solely of red edges, which is impossible since the red edges form the lexicographic map of a word-representable subgraph and hence are acyclic.
Therefore, the orientation of
H_{s}
contains no directed cycles.

Semi-transitivity.
Suppose, for contradiction, that the orientation of
H_{s}
is not semi-transitive.
Since the orientation is acyclic, any violation must occur along a directed path.
Let
P
be a directed path in
H_{s}
that violates semi-transitivity.
By reasoning analogous to the acyclicity case,
P
must contain both red and green edges.
Let
g_{1}\rightarrow g_{2}
denote the first green edge along
P
.
We consider two possibilities for the continuation of
P
after
g_{2}
:

1.

g_{2}
is the terminal vertex of
P
;

2.

g_{2}
is followed by another vertex in
P
.

Case 1:

g_{2}
is the terminal vertex of
P
.

Suppose that both
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
belong to the supervertex
V_{i}
.
Assume, for contradiction, that there exists a vertex
v
appearing in
P
before
g_{1}
that also belongs to
V_{i}
.
Let
c
denote the predecessor of
g_{1}
on
P
.
Since
g_{1}\rightarrow g_{2}
is the first green edge, the edge
c\rightarrow g_{1}
must be red, so
c
lies in a supervertex distinct from
V_{i}
.

By the uniform orientation of red edges,
c\rightarrow g_{1}
implies that every vertex of the supervertex containing
c
has a directed edge to every vertex of
V_{i}
, including
v
.
Now consider the subpath of
P
from
v
to
c
.
Together with
c\rightarrow v
, this forms a directed cycle, contradicting acyclicity.
Hence no vertex of
V_{i}
appears in
P
before
g_{1}
.

Consequently, all vertices of
P
preceding
g_{1}
lie in supervertices distinct from
V_{i}
, and
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
have identical red adjacencies to all vertices of
P
outside
V_{i}
.
If
P
violates semi-transitivity, any missing edge involving
g_{2}
also involves
g_{1}
.
Therefore,
g_{2}
can be removed from
P
, producing a shorter path
P^{\prime}
that still violates semi-transitivity.
Since
g_{1}\rightarrow g_{2}
was the only green edge,
P^{\prime}
consists entirely of red edges, which contradicts Theorem
3.1
.
This completes Case 1.

Case 2:

g_{2}
is followed by another vertex in
P
.

Let
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}
be the maximal sequence of consecutive vertices of
P
within the same supervertex
V_{i}
connected by green edges.
By transitivity of the green orientation,
g_{1}\rightarrow g_{k}
.

As in Case 1, no vertex of
P
preceding
g_{1}
belongs to
V_{i}
.
Consequently, the vertices
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
have identical red adjacencies to all vertices of
P
outside
V_{i}
.
In particular, if the next vertex after
g_{k}
along
P
is
r
in a different supervertex, then the red edge
g_{k}\rightarrow r
implies
g_{1}\rightarrow r
by uniform orientation of red edges.
Hence, any potential semi-transitivity violation involving a vertex among
g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}
also occurs with
g_{1}
as one of the endpoints.

Therefore, the vertices
g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}
can be removed from
P
without eliminating any semi-transitivity violations.
Repeating this procedure eliminates all green edges from
P
, yielding a path consisting entirely of red edges, which contradicts Theorem
3.1
.
This completes Case 2 and the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs, and let
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
denote their lexicographic product.
A special subgraph
H_{s}
of
H
is a comparability graph if and only if its underlying lexicographic map
H^{\prime}
is a comparability graph.

Proof

Let
H^{\prime}
denote the underlying lexicographic map of
H_{s}
.

Suppose first that
H^{\prime}
is a comparability graph. We show that
H_{s}
is also a comparability graph by constructing a transitive orientation of its edges. Orient the edges of
H^{\prime}
according to a transitive orientation. By Theorem
3.2
, there exists a transitive orientation in which all edges between distinct supervertices are oriented uniformly. We adopt this orientation for the edges of
H^{\prime}
. Next, consider the edges within each supervertex of
H_{s}
. By the definition of a special subgraph, these edges induce comparability graphs and therefore admit transitive orientations. At this point, all edges of
H_{s}
are oriented. We now verify transitivity. Partition the edges of
H_{s}
into two types: edges connecting distinct supervertices (red edges) and edges within each supervertex (green edges). Assume, for contradiction, that the orientation is not transitive. Then there exists a directed path of length two,
P=u\rightarrow v\rightarrow x
, such that
u\rightarrow x
is missing. The edges
\{u,v\}
and
\{v,x\}
cannot both be red, nor both green, so one must be red and the other green.

Case 1:

u\rightarrow v
is red and
v\rightarrow x
is green. Then
v
and
x
belong to the same supervertex. By uniformity of the red edge orientation,
u
must be connected to
x
, yielding a contradiction.

Case 2:

u\rightarrow v
is green and
v\rightarrow x
is red. Then
u
and
v
belong to the same supervertex. Again, uniformity of the red edge orientation implies that
u
must be connected to
x
, producing a contradiction.
In both cases, the assumption of non-transitivity fails. Hence, the orientation of
H_{s}
is transitive, and
H_{s}
is a comparability graph.

Now, suppose that
H^{\prime}
is not a comparability graph. Selecting one vertex from each supervertex of
H_{s}^{\prime}
induces a subgraph isomorphic to
H^{\prime}
. Since
H^{\prime}
is not a comparability graph, this induced subgraph is also not a comparability graph. Therefore,
H_{s}
cannot be a comparability graph, as it contains a non-comparability induced subgraph.

∎

In

, it was shown that the lexicographic product of two word-representable graphs need not be word-representable, as demonstrated by a specific example.
However, a complete characterization of when the lexicographic product of two word-representable graphs is itself word-representable has not been established.
The following theorem provides such a characterization, precisely identifying the conditions under which the lexicographic product is word-representable.

Theorem 3.5

Let
G_{1}
be a non-empty word-representable graph and
G_{2}
a word-representable graph.
Let
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
be their lexicographic product.
Then
H
is word-representable if and only if
G_{2}
is a comparability graph.
Moreover, if
G_{2}
is not a comparability graph, then
\mu(H)=2
.

Proof

(
\Rightarrow
) Assume that
G_{2}
is a comparability graph.
Consider the lexicographic map
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1})
of
G_{1}
in
H
.
Since
G_{1}
is word-representable,
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1})
is word-representable by Theorem
3.1
.
In
H
, each supervertex
V_{i}
induces a subgraph isomorphic to
G_{2}
, which is a comparability graph.
Thus,
H
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1})
by adding edges within each supervertex so that each induces a comparability graph.
By definition,
H
is a special subgraph of itself, and Theorem
3.3
implies that
H
is word-representable.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume that
G_{2}
is not a comparability graph.
Since
G_{1}
is non-empty, there exist adjacent vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in V(G_{1})
corresponding to supervertices
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
in
H
.
By the definition of the lexicographic product, every vertex in
V_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex in
V_{j}
.

Consider the induced subgraph of
H
on
V_{i}\cup\{u\}
, where
u\in V_{j}
.
The subgraph induced by
V_{i}
is isomorphic to
G_{2}
, which is not a comparability graph.
Since
u
is adjacent to all vertices of
V_{i}
, Theorem
2.2
implies that this induced subgraph is not word-representable.
Hence,
H
contains a non-word-representable induced subgraph, and therefore
H
is not word-representable. In particular,
\mu(H)\geq 2
.

To show that
\mu(H)=2
, observe that
H
can be expressed as the union of two word-representable subgraphs:

1.

The lexicographic map
\mathcal{L}_{H}(G_{1})
, which is word-representable by Theorem
3.1
.

2.

The vertex-disjoint union of the subgraphs induced by the supervertices
V_{i}
. Each of these is isomorphic to
G_{2}
and hence word-representable, and their disjoint union is word-representable by Theorem
2.3
.

Since their union is

, it follows that
\mu(H)=2
.

∎

Theorem 3.6

Let

be a non-empty word-representable graph, and let

is word-representable.

. For any

Theorem 3.7

Let

be a non-comparability word-representable graph, and let

be an integer. Then the

.

is a union of two comparability graphs, then

We prove that

G^{[2]}=G\circ G

\mu(G^{[2]})\geq 2

. To this end, we construct two subgraphs
A
and
B
of

such that both are word-representable and

A
be the subgraph corresponding to the lexicographic map of
G
in

. Let

be the subgraph consisting of the union of vertex-disjoint copies of

. Clearly,

. By Theorem

, the graph

is word-representable. Furthermore, since

.

Now assume that the statement holds for some

.

edges). By the induction hypothesis, each copy of

in

, this graph is word-representable.

Now suppose that
G
is the union of two comparability graphs.

Theorem 3.8

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs with
\mu(G_{1})=k_{1}
and
\mu(G_{2})=k_{2}
, and let

. Then
\mu(H)\leq k_{1}+k_{2}
.

Moreover, if
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
and
G_{2}
is the union of
k_{1}
comparability graphs, then
\mu(H)=k_{1}
.

Proof

We partition the edges of
H=G_{1}\circ G_{2}
into
red
edges (between distinct supervertices) and
green
edges (within supervertices). Since
\mu(G_{1})=k_{1}
, we write

\mu(G_{2})=k_{2}

in

. By Theorem

, each such graph is word-representable, and together these

G_{2}
. For each

k_{2}

\mu(H)\leq k_{1}+k_{2}
.

Now suppose that

and that

, where each

is a comparability graph. For each

A_{i}
in
H
, and denote it by

. Within each supervertex of

.

.

such subgraphs, whose union is

.

.

As
\log_{8}6<1
, Corollary
1
yields a sublinear upper bound for the function
\tau(n)
.

4
Concluding Remarks

We provided a complete characterization of when the lexicographic product of two word-representable graphs is word-representable, and extended this characterization to lexicographic powers. We also investigated how the multi-word-representation numbers of two graphs relate to the multi-word-representation number of their lexicographic product or power.

Additionally, we established a sublinear upper bound for the extremal function
\tau(n)
, showing that, although
\tau(n)
is related to multi-word-representability
[
4
]
, in general, extracting maximum-size word-representable induced subgraphs has limited effectiveness, as their size grows sublinearly with
n
.
