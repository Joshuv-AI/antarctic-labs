Efficacy and Safety of a Topical Skincare Regimen with Tripeptide/Hexapeptide following Aesthetic Procedures: Analysis of Two Randomized, Split-Face Trials in Asian Subjects
Authors: Zhenfeng Liu, Zhen Yi, Peng Wang, Li Wan, Li Ye, Yijing Chen, Yiping Zhong, Mengting Qiu, Linshan Wu, Li Fan, Xiaoyan Fu, Alan D. Widgerow, Yuanmeng Yang, Céline Blanché, Bin Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionPre- and postprocedure skin care is crucial for patients undergoing energy-based device (EBD) treatments. A serum containing a proprietary blend of peptides to support extracellular matrix remodeling (TriHex Technology®) was developed to aid skin preparation and recovery following aesthetic procedures. This analysis evaluated the skin recovery effects and safety of this serum in Asian subjects following fractional CO2 laser (FCL) and radiofrequency microneedling (RFMN).MethodsTwo prospective, randomized, evaluator-blind, split-face studies were conducted. In the FCL study (n = 26), subjects applied a reference moisturizer on the entire face and the serum on an assigned hemi-face for 2 weeks preprocedure. Postprocedure, the serum and moisturizer were applied to opposite hemi-faces for 3 days, followed by the preprocedure regimen until day 14. In the RFMN study (n = 25), serum and moisturizer were applied to opposite hemi-faces immediately postprocedure for 3 days, followed by serum on the assigned hemi-face and moisturizer on the entire face until day 14. Blinded clinical evaluations, self-assessments, and skin measurements were performed on days 0, 1, 3, 7, and 14.ResultsAll 26 subjects (18 female, 8 male) completed the FCL study and 25 (24 female, 1 male) completed the RFMN study. Overall, the serum-treated side showed superior or comparable recovery versus control across multiple parameters. The primary endpoint of investigator-assessed erythema showed significant within-side improvement on both sides in both studies, while between-side differences did not reach statistical significance. Among secondary endpoints, the serum-treated side showed significantly greater recovery that withstood multiplicity correction for transepidermal water loss (TEWL) at day 3, and edema, VISIA redness, and tactile roughness at day 14 in the FCL study, while crusting at day 3 showed numerical improvement. In the RFMN study, the serum-treated side showed significantly greater edema reduction at day 7 that withstood correction; numerical improvements were observed for pain and heat sensation at day 1 and for erythema and crusting at day 7. On day 14, significantly more subjects reported better recovery on the serum-treated side (FCL, 81.8%; RFMN, 100%; p < 0.05). Safety was favorable with few mild adverse events.ConclusionsIn these two exploratory studies, the topical serum containing TriHex Technology® demonstrated superior or comparable recovery to a basic moisturizer across multiple parameters following FCL and RFMN in Asian subjects, with the most pronounced benefits in the early postprocedure period. These findings suggest that the serum may be a useful periprocedural adjunct, although further controlled studies are warranted.Trial registrationChictr.org.cn identifiers: ChiCTR2400089516 and ChiCTR2400089424.

Introduction

The process of skin aging arises from a combination of intrinsic factors, such as genetics and hormonal shifts, and extrinsic factors, including ultraviolet (UV) radiation and environmental pollutants. This aging progression manifests as reduced collagen content, thinning of the skin, increased dryness, and the formation of wrinkles [1–3]. These manifestations can be mitigated through a range of approaches, such as aesthetic procedures, targeted skincare regimens, and other clinical interventions [4–8]. Energy-based devices (EBDs), such as fractional CO2 lasers (FCL) and radiofrequency microneedling (RFMN), are widely used for skin rejuvenation. These procedures work on the principle of controlled thermal injury to stimulate the skin’s natural wound-healing response, promoting dermal remodeling, neocollagenesis, and improved skin texture [9, 10]. FCL creates microscopic ablation zones extending into the dermis, which stimulates both dermal and epidermal regeneration while preserving the majority of the surrounding tissue, promoting collagen synthesis, reducing erythema, and improving skin texture and tone. However, RFMN delivers radiofrequency energy via fine needles to generate targeted heat in the dermis, stimulating the body’s natural wound-healing response, thereby resulting in increased collagen and elastin production [11–13]. Histopathological studies have demonstrated increased collagen, enhanced skin thickness, reduced elastosis, and restored skin barrier function following treatment.

Despite their efficacy, the thermal mechanism of EBDs invariably induces a local wound-healing process accompanied by transient adverse effects such as erythema, edema, crusting, pain, and compromised skin barrier integrity [10, 13, 14]. In Asian skin, the risk of postinflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) following thermal injury is heightened, and prolonged inflammation and delayed barrier recovery are recognized contributing factors [15]. Therefore, optimal periprocedural skincare—particularly regimens that support barrier repair and mitigate inflammation—is crucial to support recovery, minimize downtime, enhance patient comfort, and potentially improve treatment outcomes [14, 15].

TriHex Technology® is a proprietary complex containing the signal peptides palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl hexapeptide-12. Palmitoyl tripeptide-1 (GHK) has been shown to upregulate matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP-2), facilitating the clearance of damaged extracellular matrix (ECM) protein fragments while stimulating collagen synthesis. Palmitoyl hexapeptide-12 (VGVAPG) specifically promotes the production of tropoelastin, the soluble precursor of elastin. Together, these peptides are proposed to support ECM remodeling through a coordinated “remove–rebuild–replenish” mechanism, clearing senescent matrix components and promoting de novo synthesis of collagen and elastin [16, 17]. While its benefits have been documented in conjunction with laser procedures in previous studies [18–20], data, specifically in Asian populations, following common EBD procedures remain limited. Asian skin, with its tendency for PIH, may have distinct recovery needs.

This analysis aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a facial serum containing this tripeptide/hexapeptide complex on skin recovery following FCL and RFMN procedures in Asian subjects.

Methods

Materials

The investigational product was a facial serum (Regenerating Skin Nectar, ALASTIN Skincare, Inc.) containing TriHex Technology® (palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl hexapeptide-12), arnica montana flower extract, glycine soja (soybean) oil, panthenyl triacetate, and other botanicals in a silicone-based, water-free formulation. The comparator was a basic moisturizer (Daily Hydrating Lotion, Cetaphil) containing glycerin, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium PCA, and other humectants. Exact concentrations of individual ingredients are proprietary and cannot be disclosed. The subjects were provided with auxiliary products, including a facial cleanser and a sunscreen.

Facial fractional CO2 laser procedures were performed with the Lumenis® UltraPulse device (Deep FX mode; energy 15–20 mJ; density 5%; pulse duration 100–150 μs; spot size 0.12 mm; single pass). Facial RFMN procedures were performed with the INTRAcel system (Jeisys®; insulated microneedles; depth 0.8–2.0 mm; radiofrequency [RF] energy level 4–6; pulse duration 100/50/40 ms for levels 4/5/6; two passes).

Subjects

Subjects were included in the study if they met the following main inclusion criteria, among other more general criteria: aged between 18 and 70 years, Fitzpatrick skin type II–IV, and planning to undergo FCL and RFMN procedures for aesthetic purposes. Main exclusion criteria for the study encompassed: significant facial asymmetry; noncosmetic procedure purposes; the presence of active systemic infections or skin conditions that could potentially disrupt skin recovery; specific previous and concomitant medications/treatments without a designated washout period (e.g., topical retinoids and corticosteroids required a minimum 2-week washout; systemic retinoids, 6 months; chemical peels and energy-based facial treatments, 3–6 months); pregnancy, breastfeeding, or the planning of pregnancy during the study period, among others.

Informed consent was provided by all subjects in writing before being enrolled in the study. The procedures were conducted in compliance with the approved protocol following ethical clearance.

Study Design

Both studies were prospective, randomized, evaluator-blind, split-face comparative trials conducted at a single center in China.

For both studies, hemi-face allocation was determined by a computer-generated random sequence (SAS 9.4) prepared by an independent statistician before study initiation. Allocation was concealed by revealing the assigned side to site staff only at the time of product dispensing. Subjects received products in containers labeled “left” and “right” according to the assignment. The randomization schedule was withheld from the blinded evaluator and site staff until database lock.

To maintain evaluator blinding, all clinical grading was performed by a single board-certified dermatologist who was blinded to the hemi-face allocation and was not involved in randomization, product dispensing, or any subject communication regarding product use. Because the investigational serum and the comparator moisturizer differed in texture and packaging, it was not feasible to blind subjects to treatment allocation.

FCL study: After screening, subjects applied a basic moisturizer (Daily Hydrating Lotion, Cetaphil) to the entire face and the investigational serum (Regenerating Skin Nectar with TriHex Technology®, ALASTIN Skincare, Inc.) to a randomly assigned hemi-face twice daily for 2 weeks preprocedure. The facial cleanser was used twice daily, in the morning and evening, and sunscreen was to be applied once daily in the morning. On day 0, a full-face FCL treatment was performed using the Lumenis® device. Postprocedure, subjects applied the serum and moisturizer to opposite hemi-faces separately (≤ 6 times daily) for 3 days, to provide intensive hydration and active peptide delivery during the acute phase of epidermal barrier disruption. From day 4 to day 14, they reverted to the preprocedure regimen (serum on assigned hemi-face, moisturizer on full face). The hemi-face that received only the comparator served as the control side, while the other hemi-face was the treatment side.

RFMN study: On the day of the RFMN procedure, assessments and instrumental measurements were conducted before and after the procedure. On day 0, after preprocedure assessments, subjects underwent a full-face RFMN treatment using the Jeisys® INTRAcel device. Immediately postprocedure and for the subsequent 3 days, they applied the investigational serum and the comparator moisturizer to randomly assigned opposite hemi-faces as needed (≤ 6 times daily), to provide intensive hydration and active peptide delivery during the acute phase of epidermal barrier disruption. From day 4 to day 14, subjects applied the investigational serum to the designated hemi-face twice daily and the moisturizer to the entire face twice daily. The hemi-face that received only the comparator served as the control side, while the other hemi-face was the treatment side.

Auxiliary products (cleanser and sunscreen) were provided in both studies with standardized instructions regarding their postprocedure use (e.g., no cleansing for 24 h, restricted use of facial cleanser and sunscreen for 7 days). From day 8 to day 14 postprocedure, subjects were allowed to resume their use as per the preprocedure instructions. Subjects were advised to minimize sun exposure during the study period.

Compliance Monitoring and Prevention of Cross-Contamination

Subject compliance was monitored through daily diaries in which participants recorded the timing of each product application. Diaries were reviewed by study staff at each follow-up visit. In addition, subjects were instructed to record a short video of each product application throughout the study and submit it to the study physician for review. The videos were used to verify compliance with the application frequency, correct hemi-face product allocation, hand-washing before and after application, and separate application for each side of the face. To further minimize cross-contamination risk, the investigational serum and comparator were supplied in containers labeled “left” and “right” according to the randomization assignment.

Assessments

Assessments were conducted at similar time points for both studies: baseline (preprocedure), immediately postprocedure (day 0), and on days 1, 3, 7, and 14 postprocedure. A blinded evaluator employed a 5-point scale (0 = none to 4 = severe) to assess the healing process, including erythema, edema, crusting, and exudation, at the procedure visit (both pre- and postprocedure), and all subsequent follow-up visits. Additionally, the evaluator used a 10-point scale (0 = none to 9 = severe) to evaluate skin aesthetic appearance, encompassing tactile roughness, skin dullness, and PIH (only the FCL study) during the screening visit, procedure visit (preprocedure), and the final visit. Changes in scores from day 0 postprocedure were calculated to assess healing, while changes from day 0 preprocedure were calculated to evaluate the aesthetic appearance of facial skin.

Subjects were instructed to self-grade various dermatological symptoms, including redness, swelling, crusting, pain, heat, and dryness, using a 5-point scale (0 = none to 4 = severe) at the procedure visit (both pre- and postprocedure) and during all follow-up visits. Changes in scores from day 0 postprocedure were calculated to assess healing.

The investigator-assessed scales and subject self-assessment scales were ordinal tools with descriptive anchors for each grade specified in the study protocol; none have undergone formal psychometric validation for inter- or intrarater reliability. To minimize variability, all investigator assessments were performed by the same blinded evaluator, eliminating inter-rater variability.

The VISIA CR Gen 5 system (Canfield, USA) was used at all visits to capture cross-polarized photographs and analyze skin redness. Additionally, the AquaFlux® 200 probe (Biox, UK) was utilized to measure skin water barrier at all visits and assess barrier repair.

Questionnaires were used to gather subject feedback regarding product experience. Subject tolerability was assessed through a subject’s diary after the initial use of the investigational product. Adverse events (AEs) were closely monitored throughout the duration of the study.

Statistical Analysis

For both studies, continuous variables were compared between each hemi-face using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank test, according to the characteristics of the data distribution. Categorical variables were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test or Pearson’s chi-squared test, on the basis of data distribution characteristics. Statistical tests were performed as two-tailed with a significance level of 0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., USA).

The primary efficacy endpoint was change from day 0 in investigator-assessed erythema at day 7 (FCL study) and day 3 (RFMN study), as specified in the respective study protocols. For secondary endpoints, Bonferroni correction was applied to adjust for multiplicity across the postprocedure time points at which each endpoint was assessed (days 1, 3, 7, and 14). The number of comparisons (k) was defined as the number of time points at which that endpoint was evaluated. Adjusted p-values were calculated as p_adj = min(p_raw × k, 1). Cohen’s d effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for all between-side comparisons that reached nominal significance, computed as the mean paired difference divided by its standard deviation.

This report follows the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) 2010 guidelines for randomized controlled trials, adapted as appropriate for the split-face design.

Ethics Compliance

Both studies were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. Each study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Dermatology Hospital of Southern Medical University. All subjects provided written informed consent before enrollment, including consent for the use of facial photographs in the publication.

Between-side differences for this endpoint did not reach statistical significance at either time point. Product Experience Questionnaire

In both studies, subjects strongly agreed that the serum was more effective than the basic moisturizer in relieving postprocedural discomfort and aiding skin recovery.

Safety Results

The safety data from both studies indicated favorable tolerability profiles for the investigational products.

In the FCL study, four subjects reported a total of six AEs. The majority (five AEs) were mild, with four considered possibly unrelated to the products and one with an unevaluable relationship. One moderate AE was also reported and deemed possibly unrelated. No further tolerability concerns were noted.

In the RFMN study, subject-graded parameters (redness, swelling, etc.) showed identical mean scores for both the investigational product and comparator. These symptoms were attributed primarily to the procedure itself rather than the topical products. Only one mild, possibly unrelated AE was reported. Overall, both products demonstrated good tolerability.

Discussion

Composed of collagen, elastin, laminin, fibronectin, and hyaluronic acid, the ECM forms a sophisticated three-dimensional (3D) network that not only provides mechanical support but also actively orchestrates cellular behavior through signal transduction. Its dysregulation—characterized by collagen fragmentation, elastin degradation, and loss of viscoelasticity—compromises tissue integrity systemically, extending far beyond cutaneous aging to influence organismal senescence, a systemic impact that has elevated ECM change to the status of a critical hallmark of aging [21]. This has spurred interest in strategies that support endogenous ECM renewal.

Energy-based devices such as FCL and RFMN achieve rejuvenation by creating controlled micro-injuries that stimulate dermal remodeling and ECM renewal. However, this process inherently triggers inflammation, including erythema, edema, and pain, necessitating effective periprocedural skincare to support recovery [22–24].

Bioactive peptides, especially signal peptides, have been widely used in topical formulations for noninvasive ECM renewal, functioning by activating collagen and elastin synthesis endogenously. The early postprocedure benefits observed with the serum are consistent with the proposed “remove–rebuild–replenish” mechanism of TriHex Technology®, which targets ECM remodeling during the initial wound-healing phase [16, 17].

It should be noted that the two studies employed different protocols reflecting the distinct clinical requirements of FCL and RFMN procedures. Their results are therefore presented and discussed separately and should not be directly compared; rather, they provide complementary evidence on the use of the serum across different procedural contexts.

In the present studies, Asian subjects receiving FCL or RFMN treatments applied a serum containing this technology periprocedurally. The primary endpoint showed significant within-side erythema improvement on both sides, while between-side differences did not reach statistical significance. Secondary endpoints, which focused on more detailed healing parameters, revealed several between-side differences favoring the serum-treated side. In the FCL study, TEWL at day 3 showed significantly greater barrier recovery that withstood multiplicity correction, while crusting at the same time point showed numerical improvement but did not retain significance after correction. At day 14, edema, VISIA redness, and tactile roughness all showed significantly greater recovery that withstood multiplicity correction. In the RFMN study, pain and heat sensation at day 1 showed numerical improvement but did not retain significance after correction. Significantly greater edema reduction at day 7 withstood multiplicity correction, while numerical improvements were observed for erythema and crusting at the same time point. Instrumental measurements in the RFMN study (VISIA redness and TEWL) showed significant recovery on both sides but limited between-side differences, likely reflecting the milder thermal injury of RFMN compared with FCL. Subject satisfaction was high, with 81.8% (FCL) and 100% (RFMN) reporting superior recovery on the serum-treated side (p < 0.05). The pattern of early and sustained benefits across both studies supports the proposed mechanism of ECM modulation during initial wound healing.

The serum is a silicone-based formulation containing anti-inflammatory botanicals but fewer traditional humectants than conventional moisturizers. The results observed against a basic moisturizer suggest that ECM-targeted activity may contribute to postprocedure recovery. Published literature suggests optimal outcomes may combine such active serums with robust hydration [16, 17], aligning with our study design where serum was ultimately used alongside moisturizer after the initial 3-day period.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. These were exploratory studies with modest sample sizes and no formal sample size calculation, which may have limited the power to detect differences in certain secondary endpoints. Subjects could not be blinded because the two products differed in texture and packaging, which may have introduced expectation bias for subject-reported outcomes such as pain, heat sensation, and dryness. The split-face design carries an inherent risk of cross-contamination; although mitigating measures were implemented, residual risk cannot be entirely excluded. In addition, the clinical grading scales have not been formally validated, and the two studies employed different protocols, so their results should be viewed as complementary rather than directly comparable.

Conclusions

In these two exploratory studies, the topical serum containing TriHex Technology® demonstrated superior or comparable recovery to a basic moisturizer across multiple parameters following FCL and RFMN in Asian subjects, with the most pronounced benefits in the early postprocedure period. These findings suggest the serum may be a useful periprocedural adjunct, although further controlled studies are warranted.
