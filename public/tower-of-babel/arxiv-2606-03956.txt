Operator spreading in random circuits with orthogonal or symplectic symmetry
Zhiyang Tan (谭志阳), Piet W. Brouwer

Abstract
We investigate operator spreading in random quantum circuits with gates drawn from orthogonal-invariant or symplectic-invariant ensembles, revealing several key distinctions from the well-studied unitary-invariant case. We find that the ensemble-averaged Pauli-string weights relax to a ternary-valued structure, instead of the binary structure of unitary-invariant circuits. For orthogonal- or symplectic-invariant circuits, the domain wall separating trivial and scrambled regions has a finite width even for Haar-random gates, whereas domain walls are sharp for Haar-distributed random unitary circuits. We further find a fundamental dichotomy between random circuits with two-qubit gates from the two disconnected components of the orthogonal group: While the butterfly velocity for the special orthogonal ensemble lies between zero and the Haar value, the negative-determinant sector exhibits a non-zero lower bound for any gate distribution. Moreover, for qudit size q = 2 q=2 , the butterfly velocity can exceed that of the Haar-random ensemble.

We also find that the probability distribution of generalized Pauli operators approaches a “ternary” form, in which it depends not only on whether or not the generalized Pauli operator equals the identity operator, but also on whether it is symmetric or antisymmetric.

Operator spreading in random unitary circuits

The non-local correlations that an initially local operator

Its support is the set of sites

(4)

The time evolution of an operator

\bigotimes_{\text{even }x}\mathcal{U}_{x-1,x},&\text{if }t\text{ is odd},\end{cases}

(7)

Here,

unitary matrix acting on the qudits at sites

and

We take the two-qudit gate operators

from a probability distribution, which satisfies the invariance condition

(9)

The gate operator

may itself also be subject to additional symmetries,

have an additional symmetry constraint in the next Section.

The time evolution of the Pauli-string coefficients

follows from that of the operator

\displaystyle\gamma_{p}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a}W_{ap}(t,t-1)\gamma_{a}(t-1)\,

\displaystyle W_{ap}(t,t-1)=\frac{1}{q^{L}}\mbox{tr}\,U^{\dagger}(t,t-1)O_{a}U(t,t-1)O_{p}^{\dagger}.

For an evolution matrix of the form (
7

W_{ap}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\prod_{\textrm{$x$ even}}{\cal W}_{ap;x,x+1}&\mbox{$t$ even},\\
\prod_{\textrm{$x$ even}}{\cal W}_{ap;x-1,x}&\mbox{$t$ odd},\end{array}\right.

(11)

where the factors

do not depend on the full Pauli strings

,

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{q^{2}}\mbox{tr}\,{\cal U}^{\dagger}_{x,y}(\sigma_{a_{x}}\otimes\sigma_{a_{y}}){\cal U}_{x,y}(\sigma_{p_{x}}^{\dagger}\otimes\sigma_{p_{y}}^{\dagger}).

The coefficients

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{x},p_{y}}{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}{\cal W}_{bp;x,y}^{*}=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{a_{x},b_{x}}\delta_{a_{y},b_{y}},

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{x},a_{y}}{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}{\cal W}_{aq;x,y}^{*}=

III
orthogonal and symplectic-invariant ensembles

The invariance condition (
1
), with
{\cal V}_{x,y}
orthogonal or symplectic, can be seen as a purely mathematical condition on the pair-qudit gate operation. We here consider symmetry conditions for the evolution of single qudits and of qudit pairs, for which such a symmetry restriction on the matrix
{\cal V}_{x,y}

appears naturally. We consider qudits with
q
degrees of freedom and gate operations that arise from the operation of a Hamiltonian for a finite period of time, whereby the randomness appears because these Hamiltonians differ from time step to time step.

The restriction to invariance with orthogonal or symplectic basis transformations appears naturally if there exists an antiunitary involution

\psi_{x}\to Z_{x}\psi_{x}^{*}

(16)

on the
q
-dimensional single-qudit spinors, where
Z_{x}
is a
q\times q
unitary matrix with
Z_{x}Z_{x}^{*}=\pm 1
, and the Hamiltonian that is applied to a single qudit or a pair of qudits is symmetric or antisymmetric under the involution. Specifically, we consider involutions corresponding to time-reversal symmetry and particle-hole symmetry. For time-reversal symmetry, the qudit Hamiltonian is symmetric under the involution, whereby
Z_{x}
is symmetric if the qudit has integer spin and antisymmetric if the qudit spin is half-integer. In the language of the tenfold-way classification
Altland and Zirnbauer 1997
;
Schnyder et al. 2008
;
Kitaev 2009
, these two cases correspond to the Cartan classes AI and AII, respectively. For particle-hole symmetry, the qudit Hamiltonian is antisymmetric under the involution. The cases of symmetric and antisymmetric
Z_{x}
then correspond to Cartan classes D and C, respectively.
For integer spin, we may choose the qudit basis such that

Z_{x}=\openone.

(17)

For half-integer spin,
q
must be even, and we can choose the qudit basis such that

Z_{x}=\sigma_{2}\otimes\openone_{q/2}.

(18)

For a single-qudit gate operation described by the Hamiltonian
H_{x}
, one has the constraint

H_{x}=Z_{x}H_{x}^{*}Z_{x}^{\dagger}\ \ \mbox{or}\ \ H_{x}=-Z_{x}H_{x}^{*}Z_{x}^{\dagger}

(19)

for the case of time-reversal symmetry or particle-hole symmetry, respectively. Correspondingly, for the special choice of basis corresponding to Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
), the single-qudit gate operations are unitary-symmetric, unitary-antisymmetric, orthogonal, and symplectic for Cartan classes AI, AII, D, and C, respectively. Whereas these are well-defined symmetry properties for the evolution matrix of a single time step, the properties of being unitary symmetric or unitary antisymmetric are not preserved if multiple time steps are taken and one ends up with evolution matrices that are simply unitary, without further symmetry constraints.

The generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{p_{x}}
form a basis for operators on the qudit
x
. If the qudit dimension
q=2^{m}
, we can take tensor products of the standard
2\times 2
Pauli matrices for the generalized Pauli matrices. In this case, the generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{p_{x}}
can be chosen such that they have a well-defined parity
s_{p_{x}}\in\{1,-1\}
under the involution (
16
),

\sigma_{p_{x}}=s_{p_{x}}Z_{x}\sigma_{p_{x}}^{\rm T}Z_{x}^{\dagger}.

(20)

For general
q
, a generalization of Eq. (
20
) exists, which will be discussed in Sec.
VI
.

The invariance property (
1
) refers to two-qudit gates. The two-qudit Hamiltonian
H_{x,x+1}
satisfies a constraint analogous to Eq. (
19
), but with

\Omega_{x,x+1}=Z_{x}\otimes Z_{x+1}

(21)

instead of
Z_{x}
. The corresponding two-qudit gate operator
{\cal U}_{x,x+1}
then satisfies the symmetry relation

{\cal U}_{x,x+1}=\Omega_{x,x+1}{\cal U}_{x,x+1}^{\rm T}\Omega_{x,x+1}^{\dagger}

(22)

for time-reversal symmetry and

{\cal U}_{x,x+1}=\Omega_{x,x+1}{\cal U}_{x,x+1}^{*}\Omega_{x,x+1}^{\dagger}

(23)

for particle-hole symmetry. Since
\Omega_{x,x+1}
is symmetric if
Z_{x}
and
Z_{x+1}
are both symmetric as well as if they are both antisymmetric, the corresponding evolution operator
{\cal U}_{x,x+1}
is unitary-symmetric or orthogonal (in the sense described below Eq. (
9
)),
regardless of whether the spin of the individual qudits is half-integer or integer. This is to be expected, since the spin of two neighboring qudits is always an integer, regardless of whether the individual spins are of half-integer or integer type. Evolution operators of unitary-self-dual or symplectic type appear if the qudits in the circuit are alternatingly of half-integer and integer spin,
e.g
, if the
Z_{x}
are symmetric for even
x
and antisymmetric for odd
x
, so that
\Omega_{x,x+1}=Z_{x}\otimes Z_{x+1}
is an antisymmetric matrix.

If the two-qudit gate evolution matrix
{\cal U}_{x,x+1}
is orthogonal or symplectic, the transition factors
{\cal W}_{ap;x,x+1}
of Eq. (
11
) are parity preserving for
q=2^{m}
,
i.e.
,

{\cal W}_{ap;x,x+1}=0\ \ \mbox{if}s_{p_{x}}s_{p_{x+1}}s_{a_{x}}s_{a_{x+1}}=-1.

(24)

A generalization to arbitrary
q
will be discussed in Sec.
VI
.

IV
Time evolution of correlation function

The Pauli-string coefficient
\gamma_{p}(t)
contains the full evolution information of the operator
O
under unitary evolution. We will be interested in the correlation function

\rho_{pq}(t)=\langle\gamma_{p}(t)\gamma^{*}_{q}(t)\rangle

(25)

with respect to the ensemble of two-qudit gates. The diagonal elements
\rho_{pp}
may be interpreted as a (classical) probability distribution on the set of Pauli strings
p

Nahum et al. 2018
. With the help of Eq. (
10
),
\rho_{pq}(t)
may be expressed in terms of
\rho_{pq}(t-1)
and the covariance
\langle W_{ap}W_{bp}^{*}\rangle
,

\rho_{pq}(t)=\sum_{a,b}\langle W_{ap}W^{*}_{bq}\rangle\rho_{ab}(t-1).

(26)

Using the properties (
13
)–(
15
), one verifies that the evolution equation (
26
) admits the maximally random steady-state solution

\rho^{\infty}_{pq}(t)=q^{-2L}\prod_{x}\delta_{p_{x},q_{x}}

(27)

as well as the trivial solution

\rho^{0}_{pq}(t)=\prod_{x}\delta_{p_{x},0}\delta_{q_{x},0}.

(28)

The butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
corresponds to the speed at which a domain wall between these two solutions propagates through the qudit array. For definiteness, we here will consider a domain wall separating a maximally random region to the left (smaller
x
) from a trivial region to the right (larger
x
). Such a domain wall propagates to the right (in the positive
x
direction).

For circuits with orthogonal or symplectic gates, the parity-conservation condition (
24
) implies that the total parity of a Pauli string is preserved under time evolution. In principle, this allows for two maximally random steady-state solutions, each with well-defined global parity. A global parity constraint has no effect on how the steady-state solution looks locally. Hence, for the problem of operator spreading, it is sufficient to consider a domain wall between steady-state solutions that locally have the forms of Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
).

For a Haar-random unitary circuit, the expectation value

\langle W_{ap}W_{bq}^{*}\rangle=0\ \mbox{if $a\neq b$ or $p\neq q$}.

(29)

Moreover,
\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle
only depends on whether the indices
a
and
p
are trivial or nontrivial. This means that the correlation function
\rho_{pq}(t)
acquires a “binary” form after the first time step, which is such that the only nonzero elements are the diagonal elements
\rho_{pp}
, whereby
\rho_{pp}
depends only on whether
p_{x}
is trivial or nontrivial for each
x
.
For a generic unitary-invariant circuit, Eq. (
29
) no longer holds, but there is a weaker constraint
Tan and Brouwer 2025
,

\langle W_{ap}W_{bq}^{*}\rangle=0\ \mbox{if $a\neq b$ and $p=q$ or if
$p\neq q$ and $a=b$}.

(30)

The constraint (
30
) is sufficient to show that the evolution of the diagonal elements
\rho_{pp}
can be solved separately from that of the off-diagonal elements,
i.e.
,
to find diagonal elements
\rho_{pp}(t)
it is sufficient to know the diagonal elements
\rho_{aa}(t-1)
at the preceding times,

\displaystyle\rho_{pp}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a}\ \rho_{aa}(t-1)\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle.

(31)

In Ref.
Tan and Brouwer 2025
, we showed that the diagonal elements
\rho_{pp}
exponentially relax to the binary form after a finite relaxation time.

In an orthogonal-invariant or symplectic-invariant circuit, Eq. (
30
) holds only for a basis of generalized Pauli matrices that have well-defined parity
s_{p_{x}}
, see Eq. (
20
). Such a basis exists for qudit size
q=2^{m}
, but not for other qudit sizes.
We therefore first consider the case
q=2^{m}
and postpone the discussion of general
q
to Sec.
VI
.

The transition probabilities

in the stochastic growth model (

\displaystyle\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\prod_{x\,{\rm even}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle|{\cal W}_{ap;x,x+1}|^{2}\rangle&\mbox{for $t$ even},\\

In App.

\displaystyle\langle|{\cal W}_{ap;x,y}|^{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{a,0}\delta_{p,0}+(1-\delta_{a,0})(1-\delta_{p,0})

V.2
Projected ternary strings

Since the stochastic growth process defined by Eqs. (
31
)–(
V.1
) refers to the basis of qudit operators — the generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{p_{x}}
—, it has
q^{2}
degrees of freedom per qudit. This obstructs an efficient simulation of the stochastic growth process for a large qudit dimension
q
. In Ref.
Tan and Brouwer 2025
we showed that for a unitary-invariant circuit, by appropriate summation the growth process may be simplified to a growth process with only two degrees of freedom per qudit, specifying whether the index
p_{x}
is trivial (
p_{x}=0
) or nontrivial (
p_{x}\neq 0
). Hereto, we defined the
binary Pauli string

\bar{p}
associated with
p
as the string
[\bar{p}_{0},\bar{p}_{1},\ldots,\bar{p}_{L-1}]
, where
\bar{p}_{x}=0
if
p_{x}=0
and
\bar{p}_{x}=1
if
p_{x}\neq 0
. Writing
p\to\bar{p}
to denote the binary string
\bar{p}
corresponding to the Pauli string
p
, the “projected binary-string distribution”
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
corresponding to
\rho_{pp}
is then defined as

\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}=\sum_{p\to\bar{p}}\rho_{pp},

(36)

i.e.
,
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
is the sum of all diagonal elements
\rho_{pp}
with the same binary Pauli string
\bar{p}
.
The evolution equation (
26
) closes when applied to the projected binary-string distribution,
i.e.
,
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t)
depends only on
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t-1)

Tan and Brouwer 2025
. Since the projected binary-string distribution has only two degrees of freedom per qubit, its stochastic evolution equation can be easily simulated classically, independent of the actual qudit dimension

. Moreover, it is a good starting point for analytical approximation schemes.

A similar simplification is possible for an orthogonal-invariant or symplectic-invariant circuit, but with three degrees of freedom per qudit instead of two.
In order to achieve this simplification, we consider a projection of the classical probabilities
\rho_{p}(t)
onto a “ternary” probability string
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
, where the string index
\bar{p}
is a list of ternaries
[\bar{p}_{0},\bar{p}_{1},\ldots,\bar{p}_{L-1}]
with
\bar{p}_{x}\in\{-1,0,1\}
, where
\bar{p}_{x}=0
if
\sigma_{p_{x}}=\openone
and
\bar{p}_{x}=s_{p_{x}}
if
\sigma_{p_{x}}\neq\openone
. (The parity
s_{p_{x}}
is defined in Eq. (
20
).)
In analogy to Eq. (
36
), we write
p\to\bar{p}
to denote the ternary string
\bar{p}
corresponding to the Pauli string
p
and again define the
projected ternary-string distribution

\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
corresponding to
\rho_{pp}
using Eq. (
36
).

In App.
C
we show that there is a closed Markovian evolution equation for the projected ternary distribution function
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
,

\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t)=\sum_{\bar{a}}\bar{\rho}_{\bar{a}}(t-1)T_{\bar{a}\bar{p}},

(37)

with transition probabilities
T_{\bar{a}\bar{p}}
that are products of pair-transition probabilities,

T_{\bar{a}\bar{p}}=\prod_{x\,{\rm even}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T_{\bar{a}_{x}\bar{a}_{x+1};\bar{p}_{x},\bar{p}_{x+1}}&\mbox{for $t$ even},\\
T_{\bar{a}_{x-1}\bar{a}_{x};\bar{p}_{x-1}\bar{p}_{x}}&\mbox{for $t$ odd}.\end{array}\right.

(38)

We refer to App.
C
for explicit expressions.

The projected binary– or ternary–string distributions corresponding to the maximally random steady state (
27
) factorize sitewise,

\bar{\rho}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}}(t)=\prod_{x}\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}},

(39)

with

\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}}=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\!\left[\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},0}+(q^{2}-1)\,\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},1}\right]

(40)

for the unitary case. For orthogonal- or symplectic-invariant random circuits, the maximally random steady state also has the form (
39
), but with

\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}}=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\!\left[\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},0}+\frac{q-1}{2}\!\left((q+2)\,\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},1}+q\,\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},-1}\right)\right],

(41)

for qudits with integer spin (
i.e.
, qudits that have an involution of the form (
16
) with symmetrix
Z_{x}
) and

\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}}=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\!\left[\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},0}+\frac{q+1}{2}\!\left((q-2)\,\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},1}+q\,\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},-1}\right)\right],

(42)

for qudits with half-integer spin (
i.e.
, qudits that have an involution with antisymmetric
Z_{x}
). We recall that orthogonal-invariant two-gate distributions appear if all qudits have the same spin, whereas symplectic-invariant two-gate distributions appear if the qudits alternate between integer and half-integer spin between even and odd
x
. The projected ternary-string distribution corresponding to the trivial solution (
28

\bar{\rho}^{0}_{\bar{p}}(t)=\prod_{x}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},0}

(43)

for all cases.

V.3
Right-propagating
n
-point density

The next steps in the analysis of operator spreading in orthogonal-invariant or symplectic-invariant random circuits closely follow that of the analysis of operator spreading in unitary-invariant circuits in Ref.
Tan and Brouwer 2025
. We say that the ternary string
\bar{p}
“ends at
x
” if
x
is the rightmost qudit position with
\bar{p}_{x}\neq 0
. (This implies that
\bar{p}_{y}=0
for all
y>x
.) Following Ref.
Nahum et al. 2018
, we define the “right-propagating density”
\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{a}_{0})
as the fraction of ternary Pauli strings ending at
x=t+\Delta x
, with the last nontrivial element equal to
\bar{a}_{0}\in\{-1,1\}
,

\bar{\rho}^{(0)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{a}_{0})=\sum_{\textrm{$\bar{p}$ ends at $t+\Delta x$}}\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t)\delta_{\bar{p}_{t+\Delta x},\bar{a}_{0}}.

(44)

(Note that the argument
\Delta x
is measured with respect to the ballistic propagation at unit velocity.) We also define the “right-propagating
n
-point density”

\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{0})=\,\sum_{\textrm{$p$ ends at $t+\Delta x$}}\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t)\,\delta_{\bar{p}_{t+\Delta x},\bar{a}_{0}}\delta_{\bar{p}_{t+\Delta x-1},\bar{a}_{1}}\ldots\delta_{\bar{p}_{t+\Delta x-n},\bar{a}_{n}},

(45)

which is the fraction of ternary Pauli strings that end at
t+\Delta x
and that have the sequence
\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{0}
leading up to the end of the string at
\Delta x
.

From the stochastic evolution equation for the full binary probability density
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}(t)
, we may deduce evolution equations for the right-propagating density and for the right-propagating
n
-point densities. These are

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{0})=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n}}T_{\bar{a}_{n}\bar{a}_{n-1};\bar{p}_{n}\bar{p}_{n-1}}\ldots T_{\bar{a}_{2},\bar{a}_{1};\bar{p}_{2},\bar{p}_{1}}T_{\bar{a}_{0}0;\bar{p}_{0}0}\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x+1;t-1;\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{0})

(46)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\sum_{\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n+1}}T_{\bar{a}_{n+1}\bar{a}_{n};\bar{p}_{n}\bar{p}_{n-1}}\ldots T_{\bar{a}_{3},\bar{a}_{2};\bar{p}_{2},\bar{p}_{1}}T_{\bar{a}_{1}\bar{a}_{0};\bar{p}_{0}0}\bar{\rho}^{(n+1)}(\Delta x+2;t-1;\bar{a}_{n+1},\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{0})

if
n
and
\Delta x
are both even. The summation variables
\bar{a}_{j}\in\{-1,0,1\}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
;
\bar{a}_{0}
takes the values
-1
and
1
only. In the same manner, we find

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{0})=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n}}\sum_{\bar{p}_{n+1}}T_{\bar{a}_{n}\bar{a}_{n-1};\bar{p}_{n+1}\bar{p}_{n}}\ldots T_{\bar{a}_{2},\bar{a}_{1};\bar{p}_{3},\bar{p}_{2}}T_{\bar{a}_{0}0;\bar{p}_{1}\bar{p}_{0}}\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t-1;\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{0})

(47)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\sum_{\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n+1}}\sum_{\bar{p}_{n+1}}T_{\bar{a}_{n+1}\bar{a}_{n};\bar{p}_{n+1}\bar{p}_{n}}\ldots T_{\bar{a}_{3},\bar{a}_{2};\bar{p}_{3},\bar{p}_{2}}T_{\bar{a}_{1}\bar{a}_{0};\bar{p}_{1}\bar{p}_{0}}\bar{\rho}^{(n+1)}(\Delta x+1;t-1;\bar{a}_{n+1},\bar{a}_{n},\ldots,\bar{a}_{0}),

if
n
is even and
\Delta x
is odd. The summation variables
\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n}
take the same values as for the case that
n
and
\Delta
are both even;
\bar{p}_{n+1}
takes the values
-1
,
0
, and
1
.
To avoid spurious even-odd effects, we will not consider the evolution equations for the right-propagating
n
-point density with
n
odd.

V.4
Mapping to a drift-diffusion process

The evolution equations (
46
) and (
47
) for the right-propagating
n
-point density both involve the
(n+1)
-point density. To obtain a closed set of equations, we follow Ref.
Tan and Brouwer 2025
and truncate the evolution equations (
46
) and (
47
) at sufficiently high order
n
by replacing the
(n+1)
-point density by its maximally random approximation,

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(n+1)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n+1},\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{0})\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{n+1}},

(48)

where
\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}}
was defined in Eqs. (
40
)–(
42
). The long-time solution of the truncated evolution equation is of the form
Tan and Brouwer 2025

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t;\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{0})=

\displaystyle\,R_{1}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t)

(49)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}\times V_{1}^{(n)}((-1)^{\Delta x};\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{0}),

where
V_{1}^{(n)}(\sigma;\bar{p}_{n},\ldots,\bar{p}_{0})
is independent of
\Delta x
and
t
(up to the parity of
\Delta x
) and
R^{(n)}(\Delta x;t)
satisfies a drift-diffusion equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)=

\displaystyle\,(1-v_{\rm B}^{(n)})\partial_{\Delta x}R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+\frac{{\cal D}^{(n)}}{2}\partial_{\Delta x}^{2}R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t).

(50)

Explicit expressions for the drift velocity
v_{\rm B}^{(n)}
and the diffusion constant
{\cal D}^{(n)}
in terms of the ternary-string transition matrices
T_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}
of Eq. (
70
) are given in App.
D
. We verify that the results for
v_{\rm B}
and
{\cal D}
converge with respect to the truncation order
n
.

V.5
Approach to a binary or ternary distribution

In Subsec.
V.2
we showed that a closed evolution equation can be obtained if instead of the full
\rho_{pp}
we consider a “projected ternary” weight
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
that is obtained from
\rho_{pp}
by summing over all Pauli strings
p
with the same binary or ternary string
\bar{p}
. We say that the full correlation function
\rho_{pq}
is of “binary form” or “ternary form” if it is diagonal and all Pauli strings
p
that map to the same binary or ternary string
\bar{p}
have the same probability
\rho_{pp}
. In Ref.
Tan and Brouwer 2025
, we showed that
\rho_{pp}
exponentially relaxes towards such a binary form for the unitary-invariant case and calculated the corresponding relaxation time. In fact, the exponential relaxation to the binary form not only applies to the diagonal elements
\rho_{pp}
, but the full correlation function
\rho_{pq}
, including the off-diagonal elements.

In analogy to the unitary-invariant case, the full correlation function
\rho_{pq}
of an orthogonal-invariant or symplectic-invariant random circuit relaxes towards a diagonal distribution of ternary form. In such a distribution, all Pauli strings
p
that have the same ternary string
\bar{p}
have the same probability. Explicitly, we define the operator
\mathbb{B}
, which maps
\rho_{pq}
to its associated ternary distribution, as

\mathbb{B}\rho_{pq}=\delta_{pq}\left(\prod_{x}B_{\bar{p}_{x}}\right)\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}},

(51)

where
\bar{p}
is the ternary string corresponding to
p
,
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
the projected ternary weight corresponding to
\rho_{pp}
. The factor
B_{{\bar{p}}_{x}}
gives the uniform probability to find the generalized Pauli operator
p_{x}
given the ternary label
\bar{p}_{x}
. In the orthogonal-invariant case, it is

B_{\bar{p}_{x}}=\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},0}+\frac{2}{(q-1)(q+2)}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},1}+\frac{2}{q(q-1)}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},-1}.

(52)

For the symplectic-invariant case, the projection operator depends on whether
x
is even or odd. For even
x
,
B_{\bar{p}_{x}}
is identical to Eq. (
52
); however, for odd
x
, one has

B_{\bar{p}_{x}}=\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},0}+\frac{2}{(q+1)(q-2)}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},1}+\frac{2}{q(q+1)}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},-1}.

(53)

For the specific case
q=2
, the definition of the ternary projector
B
given in Eq. (
53
) is inapplicable. Instead, the operator at odd
x
is given by:

B_{\bar{p}_{x}}=\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},0}+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},-1}.

(54)

In App.
E
we show that the two-norm
||\rho_{pq}-\mathbb{B}\rho_{pq}||_{2}\to 0
in the long-time limit.

VI
General qubit dimension
q

For an arbitrary qubit dimension
q
it is not possible to find a set of generalized Pauli matrices that satisfy the orthonormality condition (
3
) and that have a well-defined parity under transposition or duality. As a consequence, it is not possible to construct a stochastic growth process that involves the diagonal elements
\rho_{pp}
of the correlation function only.
In both the orthogonal-invariant and the symplectic-invariant case, however, the Weyl-Heisenberg choice for the generalized Pauli matrices (see App.
A
) has the property that
Z_{x}\sigma_{p}^{\rm T}Z_{x}^{\dagger}
is again a generalized Pauli matrix, up to a phase factor. For each generalized Pauli matrix
\sigma_{p_{x}}
we therefore define the “transpose index”
p_{x}^{\rm T}
and phase
\phi_{p_{x}}
via the relation

Z_{x}\sigma_{p_{x}}^{\rm T}Z_{x}^{\dagger}=\sigma_{p_{x}^{\rm T}}e^{-i\phi_{p_{x}}},

(55)

if
Z_{x}
is symmetric.
For the Weyl-Heisenberg generalized Pauli matrices
\sigma_{p_{x}}
, the index
p_{x}
is represented by a pair of integers
p_{x}=(p_{x}^{\prime},p_{x}^{\prime\prime})
with
p_{x}^{\prime}
,
p_{x}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, see App.
A
, and the phase shift
\phi_{p_{x}}
is given by

\phi_{p_{x}}=\frac{2\pi}{q}p^{\prime}_{x}p^{\prime\prime}_{x}.

(56)

Analogously, for anti-symmetric
Z_{x}
, we define the “dual index”
p_{x}^{\rm R}
and corresponding phase via the relation

Z_{x}\sigma_{p_{x}}^{\rm T}Z_{x}^{\dagger}=\sigma_{p_{x}^{\rm R}}e^{-i\phi_{p_{x}}},

(57)

where
\phi_{p_{x}}=2\pi p^{\prime}_{x}p^{\prime\prime}_{x}/2+\pi(1-

\left.\delta_{p^{\prime\prime\prime}_{x},0}\right)
.

For generalized Pauli matrices with this property, we may generalize the definition (
36
) of the projected ternary weight
\rho_{\bar{p}}
as

\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}=\sum_{a,b}\rho_{ab}\prod_{x}\chi_{\bar{p}_{x};a_{x},b_{x}},

(58)

where

\displaystyle\chi_{\bar{p}_{x};a_{x},b_{x}}=

\displaystyle\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta_{a_{x},0}\delta_{b_{x},0},&\mbox{if $\bar{p}_{x}=0$},\\
\frac{1}{2}(1-\delta_{a_{x},0})\\
\mbox{}\times(\delta_{a_{x},b_{x}}+\bar{p}_{x}\delta_{a_{x},b_{x}^{\rm T}}e^{-i\phi_{a_{x}}})&\mbox{else}\end{array}\right.

where
\bar{p}_{x}
continues to take values in
\{-1,0,1\}
.
In the case of symplectic circuits, for even
x
, the function
\chi_{\bar{p}_{x};a_{x},b_{x}}
remains identical to Eq. (
VI
); however, for odd
x
, it takes the form,

\displaystyle\chi_{\bar{p}_{x};a_{x},b_{x}}=

\displaystyle\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta_{a_{x},0}\delta_{b_{x},0},&\mbox{if $\bar{p}_{x}=0$},\\
\frac{1}{2}(1-\delta_{a_{x},0})\\
\mbox{}\times(\delta_{a_{x},b_{x}}+\bar{p}_{x}\delta_{a_{x},b_{x}^{\rm R}}e^{-i\phi_{a_{x}}})&\mbox{else}\end{array}\right.

For
q=2
,
\bar{p}_{x}
retains the same interpretation as defined in Subsec.
V.2
, and the projected ternary weight function constructed in Eq. (
58
) reduces to Eq. (
36
).

In App.
C
we show that there is a well-defined stochastic growth model for the projected ternary weight
\rho_{\bar{p}}
, which has the same structure as the stochastic growth process for the case
q=2^{m}
considered previously.
As in the unitary-invariant case, under time evolution, the correlation function
\rho_{pq}
relaxes to a “ternary-form” distribution, which is a distribution of the form

\rho_{ab}=\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}\prod_{x}\frac{\chi_{\bar{p}_{x};a_{x},b_{x}}^{*}}{q^{2}\bar{r}^{\infty}_{\bar{p}_{x}}},

(65)

with
\bar{\rho}_{\bar{p}}
the ternary distribution corresponding to
\rho_{ab}
. For more details, we refer to App.
E
.

VIII
Discussion and Conclusion

In this article, we extend the results of Ref.
Tan and Brouwer 2025
from circuits composed of unitary‐invariant two‐qudit gates to those composed of orthogonal‐ or symplectic‐invariant gates. As in the unitary case, the late‐time operator‐spreading dynamics map onto a classical stochastic growth model whose long‐time limit obeys drift–diffusion equations, characterized by a butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
and a diffusion constant
\mathcal{D}
. We derive explicit expressions for
v_{\rm B}
and
\mathcal{D}
in both the orthogonal‐ and symplectic‐invariant ensembles.

There are four key differences from the random circuits with unitary invariance:
i)
In the unitary‐invariant case, averaging directly yields a closed Markov process on the space of Pauli strings; with orthogonal or symplectic symmetry, one must first project onto the invariant ternary subspace (in which a summation over degrees of freedom with the same symmetry has taken place) before obtaining a description in terms of a classical Markovian stochastic process for a generic dimension
q
.
ii) Instead of approaching a binary (trivial vs. nontrivial) distribution, the system relaxes to a three‐valued (“trivial,” “even,” “odd”) distribution. As in the unitary-invariant case, the decay time only depends on the parameters of the local two-qudit gate distribution.
iii) Haar‐unitary circuits produce a perfectly sharp front between trivial and maximally mixed regions; orthogonal/symplectic constraints broaden this front, even for Haar‐distributed two-qudit gates.
iv) The trivial limit does not necessarily determine the lower bound of the butterfly velocity, nor does the Haar measure necessarily establish its upper bound in orthogonally invariant circuits. Our results reveal a qualitative difference in operator spreading between special orthogonal ensembles and orthogonal ensembles with negative determinant for even qudit dimension
q
. Most notably, while the butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
in the special orthogonal case is bounded between the values corresponding to the trivial circuit and the Haar measure, the orthogonal ensemble with negative determinant exhibits a strictly positive lower bound for
v_{\rm B}
. Furthermore, for
q=2
, it is possible to have a butterfly velocity
v_{\rm B}
that exceeds the value for the Haar-distributed case. This result demonstrates that the determinant structure of gate ensembles plays a crucial role in determining how fast quantum information can spread, even overriding the typical upper bound set by Haar randomness.

We illustrate our results using two representative examples: the Haar–orthogonal circuit and the Brownian special orthogonal circuit, which interpolates between the trivial and Haar limits. In both cases, the domain-wall width remains finite. From the fast convergence of our calculations of the butterfly velocity with the approximation order
n
, we estimate that the domain-wall width
n_{\rm DW}\sim 4
. Numerical simulations of the corresponding classical stochastic growth model—without assuming a maximally random distribution far from the end of the Pauli string—show excellent agreement with our analytical predictions. Furthermore, we find that the results in the large-
q
limit coincide with those of the unitary case.

We identify three distinct temporal regimes for random quantum circuits with orthogonal- or symplectic-invariant gate distributions:

Short‐time binary/ternary relaxation:
At early times, independent of the system size, the weight
\rho_{pp}(t)
relaxes to a form that depends solely on whether each local generalized Pauli operator is trivial, even, or odd.

Intermediate thermalization:
A localized Pauli string spreads, with a broadened domain wall separating high‐entropy and trivial regions, propagating at velocity
v_{\rm B}
. The crossover time for a site at position
x
scales as
n_{\rm DW}/v_{\rm B}
.

Long‐time saturation:
At times of order
L/v_{\rm B}
(for system size
L
), the distribution has extended across the entire system, completing the scrambling process
Xu and Swingle 2024
;
Xu and Swingle 2019
;
Styliaris et al. 2021
.

In this work, we have focused on the effects of discrete symmetries on operator spreading. In the literature, there has also been interest in random quantum circuits with continuous symmetries
Rakovszky et al. 2018
;
Khemani et al. 2018
, such as
{\rm U}(1)
and
{\rm SU}(2)
symmetries. We leave the incorporation of continuous symmetries in our analysis for future investigation.

Appendix D
Drift-diffusion equation

To derive the drift-diffusion equation (
V.4
), we follow Ref.
Tan and Brouwer 2025
and combine
\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(x;t)
and
\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(x+1,t)
into a two-component spinor,

\displaystyle R^{(n)}(\Delta x;t)=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x;t)\\
\bar{\rho}^{(n)}(\Delta x+1;t)\end{pmatrix},

(145)

where now
\Delta x
is always even.
The evolution equations (
46
)–(
47
) with the truncation prescription (
V.4
), may then be represented as

\displaystyle R^{(n)}(\Delta x;t)=

\displaystyle\,D^{(n)}R^{(n)}(\Delta x;t-1)

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}+D^{\prime(n)}R^{(n)}(\Delta x+2;t-1),

(146)

where
D^{(n)}
and
D^{\prime(n)}
are
(4\times 3^{n})\times(4\times 3^{n})
matrices.
The transition matrices
D^{(n)}
and
D^{\prime(n)}
satisfy the normalization rule

\sum_{i}(D^{(n)}_{ij}+D^{\prime(n)}_{ij})=1

(147)

for each
j=1,\ldots,4\times 3^{n}
. We denote the left-eigenvectors, right-eigenvectors, and eigenvalues of
D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}
by
\tilde{V}_{j}^{(n)}
,
V_{j}^{(n)}
, and
d_{j}^{(n)}
, respectively. Equation (
147
) ensures that the largest eigenvalue
d_{1}^{(n)}=1
, with left-eigenvector
\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n)}=(1,1,\ldots,1)^{\rm T}
. In the two-component spinor notation of Eq. (
145
), the right-eigenvectors
V_{1}^{(n)}
are written

V_{1}^{(n)}=\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}^{(n)}(1)\\
V_{1}^{(n)}(-1)\end{pmatrix}.

(148)

The right-eigenvector
V_{1}^{(n)}
also appears in Eq. (
49
) of the main text, where we write its dependence on the ternary labels
\bar{p}_{0}
, …,
\bar{p}_{n}
explicitly.

In the long-time limit, the solution of Eq. (
146
) is of the form

R^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)=R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)V_{1}^{(n)},

(149)

where
R_{{\rm}1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
satisfies the drift-diffusion equation (
V.4
), with

v_{\rm B}^{(n)}=1-2d^{\prime(n)}_{11},

(150)

the butterfly velocity and

{\cal D}^{(n)}=4d^{\prime(n)}_{11}[1-d^{\prime(n)}_{11}]+8\sum_{j\neq 1}\frac{d^{\prime(n)}_{1j}d^{\prime(n)}_{j1}}{1-d_{j}^{(n)}},

(151)

the diffusion constant governing the diffusive spreading of the front. (Recall that
\Delta x
is measured with respect to a ballistic propagation at unit velocity so that the drift velocity measured with respect to
\Delta x
is
v_{\rm B}-1
.) In Eqs. (
150
) and (
151
) we abbreviated

d_{ij}^{\prime(n)}=\tilde{V}_{i}^{(n){\rm T}}D^{\prime(n)}V_{j}^{(n)}.

(152)

In Eq. (
149
), the function
R_{1}^{(n)}(\Delta x,t)
describes the propagation of the ends of the Pauli strings, whereas the right-eigenvector
V_{1}^{(n)}
contains information on the structure of the Pauli strings immediately behind the end.

The drift-diffusion equation can also be derived for the case that a complete basis of left- and right-eigenvectors of
D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}
does not exist. We refer to Ref.
Tan and Brouwer 2025
for details.

In the case of symplectic-invariant circuits, a closed drift-diffusion equation can only be derived by considering two consecutive time steps as a single unit. We denote the truncated projected transition matrix for even time steps as
D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}
, and for odd time steps as
\widetilde{D}^{(n)}+\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)}
. This coarse-graining yields a drift-diffusion equation identical in form to Eq. (
V.4
), but with a modified butterfly velocity and diffusion constant given by

v_{B}^{(n)}=1-\tilde{d}^{\prime(n)}_{11}

(153)

where

\tilde{d}^{\prime(n)}_{11}=\tilde{V}^{(n){\rm T}}_{1}\left[\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}(\widetilde{D}^{(n)}+\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)})\right]V^{(n)}_{1}.

(154)

The diffusion constant is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(n)}=

\displaystyle 2\tilde{d}^{\prime(n)}_{11}(1-\tilde{d}^{\prime(n)}_{11})

\displaystyle+4\tilde{V}^{(n){\rm T}}_{1}\left[\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}(\widetilde{D}^{(n)}+\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)})\right]\widetilde{G}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\times\left[(D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)})\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)}+D^{\prime(n)}(\widetilde{D}^{(n)}+\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)})\right]

\displaystyle\times V^{(n)}_{1}+4\tilde{V}^{(n){\rm T}}_{1}D^{\prime(n)}\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)}V^{(n)}_{1}

(155)

with

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}^{(n)}=

\displaystyle\,\left[\openone-(D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)})(\widetilde{D}^{(n)}+\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)})\right.

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}\ \ \ \ \left.+V_{1}^{(n)}\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n){\rm T}}\right]^{-1}-V_{1}^{(n)}\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n){\rm T}}.

(156)

Here,
\tilde{V}_{1}^{(n)}
and
V_{1}^{(n)}
are the left and right eigenvectors, respectively, of the two-step propagator
(D^{(n)}+D^{\prime(n)})(\widetilde{D}^{(n)}+\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)})
corresponding to the eigenvalue
1
. One can verify that the butterfly velocity and diffusion constant recover the original forms in Eqs. (
150
) and (
151
) when
D^{(n)}=\widetilde{D}^{(n)}
and
D^{\prime(n)}=\widetilde{D}^{\prime(n)}
.

Appendix E
Convergence to the ternary form

We first give an outline of the arguments that show that the two-norm
||\rho_{pq}-\mathbb{B}\rho_{pq}||_{2}\to 0
in the long-time limit. In this appendix, we use the notation

\mathbb{W}_{ab,pq}=W_{ap}W_{bq}^{*}.

(157)

Both
\mathbb{W}
and its ensemble average
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
are operators on the density matrix
\rho_{pq}
.

The ternary-state projector
\mathbb{B}
is defined in Eq. (
52
) for qudit size
q=2^{m}
. For general qudit dimension
q
the operator
\mathbb{B}
is defined as

\begin{split}B_{p_{x},q_{x};\bar{p}_{x}}={}&\frac{2(1-\delta_{p_{x},0})\chi^{*}_{+1}(p_{x},q_{x})\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},+1}}{q^{2}+q-2}\\
&+\frac{2(1-\delta_{p_{x},0})\chi^{*}_{-1}(p_{x},q_{x})\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},-1}}{q^{2}-q}\\
&+\delta_{p_{x},q_{x}}\delta_{p_{x},0}\delta_{\bar{p}_{x},0},\end{split}

(158)

with
\chi_{\bar{p};a,b}
defined in Eqs. (
VI
) and (
VI
) for qudits with integer and half-integer spin, respectively.
If
q=2^{m}
, we can choose the tensor products of Pauli matrices as the operator basis, in which case Eq. (
158
) simplifies to Eq. (
52
).

Using the fact that the ternary-state projector
\mathbb{B}
and the evolution operator
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
for
\rho_{pq}
commute,

\mathbb{B}\rho_{pq}(t)=\sum_{a,b}\langle\mathbb{W}_{ab,pq}\rangle\mathbb{B}\rho_{ab}(t-1),

(159)

we find that for a succession of two time steps the two-norm

\left\|\rho(t)-\mathbb{B}\rho(t)\right\|_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{pq}\left|\rho_{pq}(t)-\mathbb{B}\rho_{pq}(t)\right|^{2}},

(160)

satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\left\|\rho(t+2)-\mathbb{B}\rho(t+2)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{\infty}\,\left\|\rho(t)-B\rho(t)\right\|_{2},

(161)

where
\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{\infty}
denotes the operator norm (i.e., the largest singular value) of the averaged transition probability matrix over two layers
\sum_{m,n}\langle\mathbb{W}_{2;ab,mn}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1;mn,pq}\rangle
,
i.e.
, the largest singular value, after exclusion of the ternary Pauli-string weights for which
\rho_{pq}-\mathbb{B}\rho_{pq}=0
. Per qudit pair, there are three Pauli-string weights with this property, corresponding to the trivial one and maximally random even-parity and odd-parity distributions. The largest singular value of the transition matrix
\langle\mathbb{W}_{ab,pq}\rangle=\langle W_{ap}W_{bq}^{*}\rangle
for a pair of neighboring qudits is
1
. Its multiplicity is generically two, corresponding to the trivial state and the maximally random nontrivial state. Exceptions are the case of the trivial circuit, for which all singular values of the transition matrix are
1
, and the case where the transition matrices are orthogonal or symplectic. In the latter cases, the multiplicity is three for
q>2
, because there are separate maximally random even-parity and odd-parity states, and four for
q=2
in the orthogonal case, as discussed further below.

For the operator norm
\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{\infty}
we find the bound

\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime 2}_{\infty}\leq\lambda^{2}_{W}

(162)

with

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}_{W}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{2}\!\left[\sigma_{2}^{2}+(1-\sigma_{2}^{2})\,\|\mathbb{P}_{1}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}\right]

\displaystyle+(1-\sigma_{2}^{2})[\chi+(1-\chi)\sigma_{2}^{2}]\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}.

(163)

Here
\mathbb{P}_{j}
is the projector onto the eigenvalue-1 eigenspace of
({\langle\mathbb{W}^{\dagger}_{j}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{j}\rangle})^{1/2}
,
j=1,2
,
\sigma_{2}\in[0,1)
the largest singular value of the transition matrix
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
other than
1
, and
\chi:=\|\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}
.
The parameter
\chi
characterizes the largest singular value of the product
\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle
in a Haar-distributed circuit, evaluated after projecting out vectors satisfying
\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho=0
.
For Haar-distributed ensembles, the second largest singular value vanishes,
\sigma_{2}=0
, which leads to

\lambda_{W}^{2}=\chi\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|_{2}^{\prime 2}.

(164)

For qudit dimension
q>2
, the quantity
\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|_{2}^{\prime 2}
vanishes, implying
\lambda_{W}=0
, in agreement with the behavior of fully unitary circuits.
In contrast, for
q=2
, if the local gate belongs to the
{\rm SO}(4)
or
{\rm SO}^{-}(4)
ensemble, one finds
\|P_{2}\|_{2}^{\prime 2}=1
.
This is due to the presence of an additional singular vector, beyond the ternary states, associated with the largest singular value of the transition matrix
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
.
Consequently, in this case,

\lambda_{W}^{2}=\chi.

(165)

The parameter
\chi
is strictly smaller than 1 and is independent of the specific properties of the probability distribution of the gate ensemble.
Its precise value can be obtained numerically.
Since all contributing parameters are strictly less than one, it follows that
\lambda_{W}<1
.
Moreover, since these parameters are independent of the system size,
\lambda_{W}
remains strictly smaller than
1
as the system size increases.

We define the decay time for convergence to a ternary distribution,
\tau_{\rm t}
, as

\tau_{\rm t}=\frac{-1}{\ln\lambda_{W}}.

(166)

For Haar-distributed ensembles other than
{\rm SO}(4)
and
{\rm SO}^{-}(4)
, the convergence to the ternary distribution occurs already after the first time step.

E.1
Detailed derivation

Before giving the full derivation, we first outline the logical structure. We denote the ensemble of two-qudit gate operators by
{\cal E}
and the transition matrix corresponding to the two-qudit operator
U
by
W(U)
.

1. We show that the largest singular value of the ensemble average
\langle W\rangle
is
1
;

2. We show that if
\langle W\rangle\rho=c\,\rho
with
|c|=1
, then
W(U)\rho=c\,\rho
for all
U\in\mathcal{E}
;

3. We show that if the ensemble is unitary-invariant and
\langle W\rangle\rho_{i\mu}=c_{i}\,\rho_{i\mu}
, then
W(V)\rho_{i\mu}=\rho_{i\mu}
for all
q^{2}\times q^{2}
unitary matrices
V
;

4. We identify the solutions in point 3 as the steady states;

5. We derive the inequality (
E
);

6. We give an upper bound for
\|\langle W_{2}W_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{\infty}
.

We begin by bounding the singular values of the averaged two-qudit gate operator
\langle\mathbb{W}_{ab;pq}\rangle=\langle{\cal W}_{ap}{\cal W}_{bq}^{*}\rangle
. The positive semi-definiteness of the variance of
W
implies that for any normalized two-qudit state
\|\widetilde{\rho}\|=1
, one has the inequality

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}^{\dagger}\left<\left(W-\langle W\rangle\right)\left(W^{\dagger}-\langle W^{\dagger}\rangle\right)\right>\widetilde{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\widetilde{\rho}^{\dagger}\left(\left<WW^{\dagger}\right>-\langle W\rangle\langle W^{\dagger}\rangle\right)\widetilde{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,1-\widetilde{\rho}^{\dagger}\langle W\rangle\langle W^{\dagger}\rangle\widetilde{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,0.

(167)

Here
\langle\ldots\rangle
denotes the ensemble average over
\mathcal{E}
, and we have used
\langle W\rangle^{\dagger}=\langle W^{\dagger}\rangle
, together with the unitarity of
W_{ab;pq}
, which is guaranteed by

\displaystyle(WW^{\dagger})_{ab;a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{pq}W_{ab;pq}W^{*}_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime};pq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{q^{8}}\sum_{pq}\tr U^{\dagger}O_{a}UO_{p}^{\dagger}\tr U^{\dagger}O_{b}^{\dagger}UO_{q}

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}\times\tr U^{\dagger}O^{\dagger}_{a^{\prime}}UO_{p}\tr U^{\dagger}O_{b^{\prime}}UO_{q}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{aa^{\prime}}\delta_{bb^{\prime}}.

(168)

It follows that

1\geq\max_{\|\widetilde{\rho}\|_{2}=1}\left(\widetilde{\rho}^{\dagger}\langle W\rangle\langle W^{\dagger}\rangle\widetilde{\rho}\right)=\sigma^{2}_{\max}=\|W\|_{2}^{2},

(169)

so that the largest singular value of
\langle W\rangle
is bounded from above by
1
.

If
\rho
is a “steady state”, which satisfies
\langle W\rangle\rho=c\rho
with
|c|=1
, we can obtain that

\left<W\right>^{\dagger}\rho=c^{*}\rho.

(170)

This follows from the observation that

\displaystyle||\left<W\right>^{\dagger}\rho-c^{*}\rho||^{2}=

\displaystyle\,||\left<W\right>^{\dagger}\rho||^{2}-||\rho||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,||\left<W\right>^{\dagger}||^{2}_{2}||\rho||^{2}-||\rho||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,0,

(171)

where we have used
||\left<W\right>^{\dagger}||^{2}_{2}=||\left<W\right>||^{2}_{2}\leq 1
, and non-negativity of the norm. It follows that
\rho
satisfying
\langle W\rangle\rho=c\rho
with
|c|=1
also satisfy

\left<W\right>\left<W\right>^{\dagger}\rho=\left<W\right>^{\dagger}\left<W\right>\rho=\rho.

(172)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\|W\rho-c\rho\|^{2}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\left\langle\rho^{\dagger}W^{\dagger}W\rho+\rho^{\dagger}\rho-2\,\operatorname{Re}\left(\rho^{\dagger}W\rho\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2\rho^{\dagger}\rho-2\,\operatorname{Re}\left(c^{*}\rho^{\dagger}\left\langle W\right\rangle\rho\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,0

(173)

if
\langle W\rangle\rho=c\rho
with
|c|=1
. Since the squared norm is non-negative for any
W
, Eq. (
E.1
) implies that
W\rho=c\rho
must hold for all
W(U)
, without taking the ensemble average. Therefore,
\rho
is a simultaneous eigenvector with eigenvalue
c
for the entire ensemble of
W
.

One may argue that the steady states
\rho
would depend on the structure of the ensemble. We now demonstrate that the two steady states identified earlier are the only vectors satisfying this condition if the ensemble is unitary invariant.

E.2
Unitary invariant ensembles

We can vectorize the generalized Pauli operators
O_{a}
, so that
W_{ab;pq}
can be written in terms of the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product,

W_{ab;pq}=\left<ab^{*}\right|U^{*}\otimes U\otimes U\otimes U^{*}\left|pq^{*}\right>,

(174)

or, in matrix notation,

W(U)=U^{*}\otimes U\otimes U\otimes U^{*}.

(175)

Here, we have used the identities
Macedo and Oliveira 2013

\tr(A^{\dagger}B)=\left<\mathrm{vec}(A)|\mathrm{vec}(B)\right>,

(176)

\left<\mathrm{vec}(ABC)\right|=C^{T}\otimes A\,\left|\mathrm{vec}(B)\right>

(177)

and we defined

\displaystyle\left<a\right|

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{vec}(O^{\dagger}_{a}))^{\dagger},

\displaystyle\left<b^{*}\right|

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{vec}(O^{\dagger}_{b}))^{\rm T},

\displaystyle\left|p\right>

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{vec}(O^{\dagger}_{p})),

\displaystyle\left|q^{*}\right>

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{vec}^{*}(O^{\dagger}_{q})).

(178)

Due to the invariance of the ensemble-averaged operator
\langle W\rangle
under unitary transformations
V
from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
, we find

\displaystyle\langle W\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\langle W(VUV^{\dagger})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,W(V)\langle W(U)\rangle W^{\dagger}(V)

(179)

for all
q^{2}\times q^{2}
unitary matrices
V
.

Now, suppose
\{\rho_{i\mu}\}
is an orthonormal basis set of eigenvectors of
\langle W\rangle
with eigenvalue
c_{i}
with
|c_{i}|=1
,

\langle W\rangle\rho_{i\mu}=c_{i}\,\rho_{i\mu}.

(180)

Then

\displaystyle W(V)\,\langle W\rangle\,W^{\dagger}(V)\,W(V)\rho_{i\mu}

\displaystyle=\langle W\rangle\,W(V)\rho_{i\mu}

\displaystyle=c_{i}\,W(V)\rho_{i\mu}

(181)

for all
q^{2}\times q^{2}
unitary matrices
V
, so that
W(V)\rho_{i\mu}
is also an eigenvector of
\langle W\rangle
at the same eigenvalue
c_{i}
. Hence, for an arbitrary unitary matrix
V
,
W(V)\rho_{i\mu}
can be expressed as a linear combination of the basis vectors
\rho_{i\mu}
,

\displaystyle W(V)\rho_{i\mu}=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\nu}C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V)\,\rho_{i\nu},

(182)

where the coefficients
C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V)
satisfy the orthonormality constraint

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu}C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V)\,C^{i*}_{\mu\nu}(V)=

\displaystyle\,1.

(183)

The matrix
C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V)
further satisfies

C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V^{\dagger})=C^{i*}_{\nu\mu}(V),

(184)

\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}C^{i}_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}(V)\,C^{i}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(U)=C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(UV),

(185)

where we have used
W(UV)=W(U)W(V)
.

We first consider the case that the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
of two-qudit operators
U
forms a group, and relax this assumption later. Since
\mathcal{E}
is invariant under unitary conjugation,
VUV^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{E}
for all unitary matrices
V
, the group formed by
\mathcal{E}
is a normal subgroup of the full unitary group
{\rm U}(q^{2})

Dummit and Foote 2003
. If
\mathcal{E}
is a group, we have

W(U^{2})\rho_{i\mu}=W^{2}(U)\rho_{i\mu}=c_{i}^{\,2}\,\rho_{i\mu}=c_{i}\,\rho_{i\mu},

(186)

where we have used that
W(U)\rho_{i\mu}=c_{i}\rho_{i\mu}
for all
U\in\mathcal{E}
if
\langle W\rangle\rho_{i\mu}=c_{i}\rho_{i\mu}
if
|c_{i}|=1
. It follows that
c_{i}=1
.

Any
q^{2}\times q^{2}
unitary matrix
V
can be written as

V=(\det V)^{1/q^{2}}\,V^{\prime},

(187)

where
V^{\prime}\in{\rm SU}(q^{2})
. Moreover, any special unitary matrix can be expressed as a commutator
Fraleigh 2003
;
Sepanski 2007
,

V^{\prime}=ABA^{-1}B^{-1},

(188)

with
A,B\in{\rm U}(q^{2})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V)=

\displaystyle\,C^{i}_{\mu\nu}\!\left((\det V)^{1/q^{2}}V^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}C^{i}_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}(V^{\prime})\,C^{i}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}\!\left((\det V)^{1/q^{2}}\openone\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V^{\prime}),

(189)

where we used
C^{i}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}((\det V)^{1/q^{2}}\openone)=C^{i}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(\openone)=\delta_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}
. We also note that

W(V)=W(V^{\prime}),

(190)

because
V
and
V^{\prime}
only differ by a factor, see Eq. (
187
), and
V
contains as equally many factors
U
and
U^{*}
, see Eq. (
174
).

Likewise, any matrix
U_{i}\in\mathcal{E}
can be written as
U_{i}=(\det U_{i})^{1/q^{2}}U^{\prime}_{i}
with
U^{\prime}_{i}\in{\rm SU}(q^{2})
. If
\mathcal{E}
is a group, then the set
\{U^{\prime}_{i}\}
also forms a group. Indeed, if
U_{i}U_{j}=U_{k}
, then

\displaystyle U_{i}U_{j}=

\displaystyle\,(\det(U_{i}U_{j}))^{1/q^{2}}\,U^{\prime}_{i}U^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(\det U_{k})^{1/q^{2}}\,U^{\prime}_{k},

(191)

so that

U^{\prime}_{i}U^{\prime}_{j}=U^{\prime}_{k}.

(192)

Since
\openone\in\mathcal{E}
, it follows that
\openone\in\{U^{\prime}_{i}\}
. Associativity holds automatically. Hence
\{U^{\prime}_{i}\}
is a group whenever
\mathcal{E}
is. It is then a normal subgroup of
{\rm SU}(q^{2})
.

We now make use of the fact that there are only two kinds of normal subgroups of
{\rm SU}(q^{2})

Dummit and Foote 2003
: (i) subgroups of its center
\{e^{2\pi ik/q^{2}}\openone\,|\,k=0,1,\dots,q^{2}-1\}
; and (ii) the entire group
{\rm SU}(q^{2})
. The first case yields
W(U)
behaving like
W(\openone)
, corresponding to the trivial circuit and producing no convergence to the binary or ternary state; thus, only the second case is nontrivial. Therefore, we must consider the case that

W(V)\rho_{i\mu}=\rho_{i\mu}

(193)

for all
q^{2}\times q^{2}
unitary matrices
V
, if
\langle W\rangle\rho_{i\mu}=c_{i}\rho_{i\mu}
with
|c_{i}|=1
for the ensemble average
\langle W\rangle
over all
U\in{\cal E}
.

If the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
does not form a group, we can reach the same conclusion as follows:
For any
U\in\mathcal{E}
with
U=(\det U)^{1/q^{2}}U^{\prime}
, define

\mathcal{S}=\{\,VU^{\prime\,k}V^{\dagger}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z},\;V\in{\rm U}(q^{2})\,\}.

(194)

Let
G
be the set of all finite products of elements of
\mathcal{S}
. Then
G
is a group. Indeed, if
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
with
g_{1}=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots
and
g_{2}=m_{1}m_{2}\cdots
for
s_{i},m_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
, then

g_{1}g_{2}=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots m_{1}m_{2}\cdots,

(195)

which is again a finite product of elements of
\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, for
A=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{m}\in G
,

A^{-1}=s_{m}^{-1}\cdots s_{2}^{-1}s_{1}^{-1}\in G.

(196)

Since
\openone\in\mathcal{S}
, we also have
\openone\in G
. Associativity holds for unitary matrices. Hence
G
is a (normal) subgroup of
{\rm SU}(q^{2})
. By the same reasoning as above,
G
is either central or all of
{\rm SU}(q^{2})
. For a nontrivial circuit, one must be in the noncentral case,
i.e.
,
G={\rm SU}(q^{2})
.

Furthermore, for any
A=VU^{\prime k}V^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{S}
, one has

\displaystyle W(VU^{\prime\,k}V^{\dagger})\rho_{i\mu}=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\nu}C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(VU^{\prime\,k}V^{\dagger})\,\rho_{i\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,c_{i}^{\,k}\,\rho_{i\mu},

(197)

so
C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(A)
is a scalar (proportional to
\delta_{\mu\nu}
) on the subspace spanned by the
\{\rho_{i\mu}\}
. For any
M\in G
, write
M=A_{1}A_{2}\ldots
with
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
\ldots\in\mathcal{S}
. Then

\displaystyle W(M)\rho_{i\mu}=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\nu}C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(M)\,\rho_{i\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,W(A_{1}A_{2}\ldots)\rho_{i\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,W(A_{1})\,W(A_{2})\,\ldots\,\rho_{i\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,c_{i}(M)\,\rho_{i\mu},

(198)

and thus
C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(M)
is a scalar for every
M\in G
.

Now we can prove that the constant
c_{i}(V^{\prime})=1
for all
V^{\prime}\in{\rm SU}(q^{2})
if
|c_{i}(V^{\prime})|=1
. Here, we notice that

\displaystyle c_{i}(V^{\prime})c_{i}(U^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\,c_{i}(V^{\prime}U^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,c_{i}(U^{\prime}V^{\prime}),

(199)

which means that
c_{i}(V^{\prime})
is a one‐dimensional character of the connected group
{\rm SU}(q^{2})
.
Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle c_{i}(V^{\prime})

\displaystyle=c_{i}(A)c_{i}(B)c_{i}(A^{-1})c_{i}(B^{-1})

\displaystyle=1,

(200)

where we wrote the special unitary matrix
V^{\prime}
as a commutator, see Eq. (
188
).
For the same reason we discussed before, we can conclude
C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V)=\delta_{\mu\nu}
for all
V\in\mathcal{U}(q^{2})
.

The finding that
W(V)\rho_{i\mu}=\rho_{i\mu}
for all
V\in\mathcal{U}(q^{2})
if
\rho_{i\mu}
satisfies
\langle W\rangle\rho_{i\mu}=c_{i}\rho_{i\mu}
implies that
\rho
transforms under the trivial representation of the group action defined by
W(V)
. Consequently, the dimension of the subspace spanned by such invariant states
\rho
is determined by the multiplicity of the trivial representation in the decomposition of
W(V)
. To analyze this, we write the representation
W(V)
as

W(V)=T^{*}(V)\otimes T(V),\quad\text{where }T(V)=V\otimes V^{*}.

(201)

Under the adjoint action
A\mapsto VAV^{\dagger}
, the representation
T(V)
acting on the space of linear operators
L(\mathcal{V})
decomposes as
Fulton and Harris 2004

T(V)=T_{0}\oplus T_{\mathrm{adj}},

(202)

where
T_{0}
is the trivial representation (spanned by the identity matrix), and
T_{\mathrm{adj}}
corresponds to the adjoint subspace (traceless operators).
Therefore, the full representation
W(V)
decomposes as

\displaystyle(T^{*}_{0}\oplus T^{*}_{\mathrm{adj}})\otimes(T_{0}\oplus T_{\mathrm{adj}})=(T^{*}_{0}\otimes T_{0})\oplus(T^{*}_{0}\otimes T_{\mathrm{adj}})

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \oplus(T^{*}_{\mathrm{adj}}\otimes T_{0})\oplus(T^{*}_{\mathrm{adj}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{adj}}).

(203)

Since only tensor products of a representation with its complex conjugate contain the trivial representation, the subrepresentations
T^{*}_{0}\otimes T_{0}
and
T^{*}_{\mathrm{adj}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{adj}}
each contain the trivial representation
Fulton and Harris 2004
;
Hall 2003
. Hence, the dimension of the invariant subspace (i.e., the multiplicity of eigenvalue 1) is two, corresponding exactly to the steady states identified in Sec.
IV
. Therefore, we can conclude that if
\left<W\right>\rho=c\rho
with
|c|=1
, then
\rho
must be a superposition of the maximum random state and the trivial state. Both of these states obviously have eigenvalue
c=1
.

E.3
Orthogonal invariant ensembles

A parallel argument applies to orthogonal-invariant ensembles. Unlike the unitary case, the orthogonal group is not connected, so there are two disjoint components: the special orthogonal group
{\rm SO}(q^{2})
and the determinant-
-1
component
{\rm SO}^{-}(q^{2})

Hall 2003
. For any matrix
V^{\prime\prime}
in
{\rm SO}^{-}(q^{2})
, we can write

V^{\prime\prime}=RV^{\prime},

(204)

where
R=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,\ldots)
is a reflection and
V^{\prime}\in{\rm SO}(q^{2})
.

As in the unitary case, if the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
is a group, then
C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(U)=\delta_{\mu\nu}
for all
U\in\mathcal{E}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{E}
is closed under conjugation:
VUV^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{E}
for all
V\in{\rm O}(q^{2})
. If
\mathcal{E}
is not a group, the conclusion must be adjusted slightly. Let
\{U_{i}\}
denote the elements of
\mathcal{E}
and define

\mathcal{S}=\{\,VU_{i}^{\,k}V^{\dagger}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z},\ U_{i}\in\mathcal{E},\ V\in{\rm O}(q^{2})\,\}.

(205)

Let
G
be the set of all finite products of elements of
\mathcal{S}
. The same closure, inverse, identity, and associativity arguments as before show that
G
is a group. For any
A\in G
we then have
C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(A)=c_{i}(A)\,\delta_{\mu\nu}
(a scalar on the
\{\rho_{i\mu}\}
subspace).
Furthermore,
G\subset U(q^{2})
is a subgroup that is preserved under orthogonal conjugation, i.e., normalized by
{\rm O}(q^{2})
.
In the
W(V)
-representation, central phases act trivially. Up to this trivial central action, there are exactly three nontrivial
{\rm O}(q^{2})
-invariant possibilities for the image of
G
: the special orthogonal group
{\rm SO}(q^{2})
, the full orthogonal group
{\rm O}(q^{2})
, and the image of
{\rm SU}(q)
, which is isomorphic to the projective unitary group
{\rm PU}(q)

Grove 2002
;
Dummit and Foote 2003
.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{E}
consists only of elements whose action on
W
lies in
{\rm O}(q^{2})
(equivalently, in
{\rm U}(1)\cdot{\rm O}(q^{2})
), then
G
is a normal subgroup of
{\rm O}(q^{2})

Grove 2002
;
Dummit and Foote 2003
; for
q\geq 2
, the only nontrivial normal subgroups of
{\rm O}(q^{2})
(besides its center) are
{\rm SO}(q^{2})
and
{\rm O}(q^{2})
.
If
\mathcal{E}
contains an element outside
{\rm O}(q^{2})
(or outside
U(1)\cdot{\rm O}(q^{2})
), then
G
has the same image on
W
as
U(q)
(equivalently, as
{\rm SU}(q)
), which is isomorphic to
{\rm PU}(q)
.
In particular, when
G={\rm SO}(q^{2})
, every element of
G
is a commutator
Fraleigh 2003
;
Sepanski 2007
, which implies

C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V^{\prime})=\delta_{\mu\nu}

(206)

for all
V^{\prime}\in G
.
If
G={\rm O}(q^{2})
, then for
V^{\prime\prime}=RV^{\prime}
with
V^{\prime}\in{\rm SO}(q^{2})
we obtain

C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(V^{\prime\prime})=C^{i}_{\mu\nu}(R)=c_{i}(R),

(207)

and since

c_{i}(R)\,c_{i}(R)=c_{i}(\openone)=1,

(208)

it follows that
c_{i}(R)=\pm 1
.

For the full group
{\rm SO}(q^{2})
, the representation of
W(V^{\prime})
decomposes as
Fulton and Harris 2004
;
Hall 2003

\displaystyle(T_{0}\otimes T_{0})\oplus(T_{\mathrm{sym}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{sym}})\oplus(T_{\mathrm{ant}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{ant}})

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \mbox{}\oplus(T_{0}\otimes T_{\mathrm{sym}})\oplus(T_{\mathrm{sym}}\otimes T_{0})\oplus(T_{0}\otimes T_{\mathrm{ant}})

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \mbox{}\oplus(T_{\mathrm{ant}}\otimes T_{0})\oplus(T_{\mathrm{sym}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{ant}})\oplus(T_{\mathrm{ant}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{sym}}).

Only tensor products of a representation with its complex conjugate contain the trivial representation; since
T_{0}
,
T_{\mathrm{sym}}
, and
T_{\mathrm{ant}}
are self-conjugate, the subrepresentations
T_{0}\otimes T_{0}
,
T_{\mathrm{sym}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{sym}}
, and
T_{\mathrm{ant}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{ant}}
each contain a trivial component. Therefore, when
q>2
, there exist three independent eigenvectors with

W(V^{\prime})\,\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}

for all
V^{\prime}\in{\rm SO}(q^{2})
,
namely

\rho_{1}=\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd},\qquad\rho_{2}=\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc},\qquad\rho_{3}=\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}.

Explicitly,

\displaystyle W(V^{\prime})_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}d^{\prime};abcd}\,\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}

\displaystyle=\delta_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}\delta_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}},

\displaystyle W(V^{\prime})_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}d^{\prime};abcd}\,\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}

\displaystyle=\delta_{a^{\prime}d^{\prime}}\delta_{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}},

\displaystyle W(V^{\prime})_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}d^{\prime};abcd}\,\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}

\displaystyle=\delta_{a^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\delta_{b^{\prime}d^{\prime}}.

(209)

These three vectors are common eigenvectors of
W(V)
with eigenvalue
1
for all
V\in{\rm O}(q^{2})
and they satisfy

W(R)\,\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}.

(210)

Hence
\langle W\rangle\,\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
when
q>2
. These invariant eigenstates
\rho_{i}
coincide with those identified in Section
IV
.

For
q=2
, there is an additional common eigenvector of
W(V^{\prime})
for
{\rm SO}(4)
with eigenvalue
1
, namely
\rho_{4}=\epsilon_{abcd}
. This eigenvector does not correspond to the binary or ternary sectors and is not stable under the even–odd structure. Moreover,

W(R)\,\rho_{4}=-\rho_{4}.

(211)

If the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
lies entirely within one of the components
{\rm SO}(4)
or
{\rm SO}^{-}(4)
, then there are four independent eigenvectors of
\langle W\rangle
with
|c|=1
. Specifically, if
\mathcal{E}\subset{\rm SO}(4)
, then
\langle W\rangle\,\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}
for
i=1,2,3,4
; if
\mathcal{E}\subset{\rm SO}^{-}(4)
, then
\langle W\rangle\,\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
and
\langle W\rangle\,\rho_{4}=-\rho_{4}
. However, if
\mathcal{E}
contains elements from both components
{\rm SO}(4)
and
{\rm SO}^{-}(4)
, then only
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}
remain eigenvectors of
\langle W\rangle
with
|c|=1
. (Here we used the fact that if
\langle W\rangle\,\rho_{i}=c_{i}\rho_{i}
, then
W(V)\rho_{i}=c_{i}\rho_{i}
for all
V\in\mathcal{E}
.)

If
\mathcal{E}
contains elements not in
U(1).{\rm O}(q^{2})
, we reach the same conclusion as the unitary case.

E.4
Symplectic invariant ensembles

For the symplectic-invariant ensemble, one proceeds almost the same as the orthogonal case, with a few small differences. The constructed group
G
is invariant under symplectic transformations. In the
W(V)
representation, up to the trivial action, there are only two nontrivial possibilities of the image of G:
{\rm Sp}(q^{2}/2)
, the projective unitary group
{\rm PU}(q^{2})

Grove 2002
;
Dummit and Foote 2003
.

If the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
only contains elements in the symplectic group, then there are three independent eigenvalue-1 eigenvectors of
W(V)
,

\rho_{1}=\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd},\qquad\rho_{2}=\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc},\qquad\rho_{3}=Z_{ac}Z_{bd},

where the involution matrix
Z
is defined in Eq. (
18
).
Unlike the orthogonal case, there does not exist a fourth independent eigenvalue-1 eigenvector for
q=2
, as
{\rm Sp}(2)
is not a real matrix group, but a subgroup of
{\rm SU}(4)

Hall 2003
.

If the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
contains elements outside

, then there exist only two independent eigenvalue-1 eigenvector of
W(V)
,
namely,
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
.

E.5
Relaxation time

One can verify that
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
commutes with
\mathbb{B}
, by using the Weingarten calculus result in App.
B
, which also guarantees the validation of the projection method.

As an example, consider the term
C_{16}=B_{16}\,\tr\!\left(\Sigma_{a}\Sigma_{p}^{\dagger}\Sigma_{b}^{*}\Sigma_{q}^{T}\right)
and investigate its action on a density matrix
\rho_{pq}
of ternary form. The nonzero elements of such density matrices have
p=q
or
p=q^{T}
, see Eq. (
VI
).
If
p=q
, then

q^{-2}\,\delta_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime},\,2p^{\prime}}\,\delta_{a^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime\prime}}\,e^{\,i(a^{\prime}-p^{\prime})(p^{\prime\prime}-a^{\prime\prime})}\,B_{16}

is obtained. Summing over all
p=(p_{x},p_{x+1})
where both indices are non-identity (
p_{x},p_{x+1}\neq 0
) yields

\xi(a_{x},b_{x})\xi(a_{x+1},b_{x+1})B_{16}

with
\xi(a,b)=\delta_{a,b}-q^{-1}\delta_{a,\,b^{T}}e^{-i\phi_{a}}
.
If, instead,
p=q^{T}
, we multiply by
e^{-i\phi_{p}}
and sum over all non-identity indices
p_{x},p_{x+1}\neq 0
, we obtain

q^{-2}\,\delta_{a,\,b^{T}}e^{-i\phi_{a}}B_{16}\,\bigl[(1-\delta_{a_{x},0})(1-\delta_{a_{x+1},0})\\
-(q^{2}-1)(1-\delta_{a_{x},0})\delta_{a_{x+1},0}-(q^{2}-1)\delta_{a_{x},0}(1-\delta_{a_{x+1},0})\bigr].

Both contributions depend only on
\delta_{a,b}
,
\delta_{a,\,b^{T}}e^{-i\phi_{a}}
, and on whether the indices
a_{x}
and
a_{x+1}
are zero or non-zero. Hence, they exhibit a clear ternary structure. Repeating the same analysis for the remaining terms shows that each term likewise assumes a ternary form. Because
a
and
p
play symmetric roles, summing over
a
and
b
with a fixed ternary pattern induces a ternary pattern on
p
and
q
. Consequently, the pair transition probability matrix
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
commutes with the ternary projection operator
\mathbb{B}
. As a result, once the Pauli strings are in the ternary or binary form, then they will be in the ternary or binary form forever.

One can also verify that
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
is normal based on the Weingarten calculus results in App.
B
. Then it follows that
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
and
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle^{\dagger}
share eigenvectors. Consequently, the eigenspace of
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle^{\dagger}
corresponding to the maximum singular value coincides with the eigenspace of
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
with eigenvalue
c
satisfying
|c|=1
(as established above). Therefore, the invariant subspace of
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
is precisely the top-singular-value eigenspace of
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle^{\dagger}
.

Since we have proved that the locally averaged
\mathbb{W}
commutes with the ternary projection operator
\mathbb{B}
, the bound of (
160
) over two time steps follows. It remains to construct an upper bound for the norm
\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{2}
.
Hereto, we may restrict to vectors with
\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho\neq 0
. We recall that the primed norm
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}_{2}
is the largest singular value of the matrix evaluated after excluding vectors with
\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho=0
.
We write
\sigma_{2}\in[0,1)
for the largest singular value of the local transition matrix
\langle\mathbb{W}\rangle
smaller than
1
.
An upper bound for
\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{2}
follows from submultiplicativity,

\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{2}\leq\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{2}\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime}_{2}.

(212)

In the unitary case, since the only eigenvalue-1 states are the binary steady states,
||\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle||^{\prime}_{2}
and
||\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle||^{\prime}_{2}
are strictly less than
1
. Hence, the product can be bounded as

|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle|^{\prime}_{2}\,|\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle|^{\prime}_{2}\leq\sigma_{2}^{2}.

(213)

For the orthogonal-invariant ensemble with
q\geq 3
and for the symplectic-invariant ensemble, the same conclusion as in the unitary case holds. Since
0\leq\sigma_{2}<1
, it follows that in the long-time limit, the state
\rho_{p}
approaches the binary/ternary form
\mathbb{B}\rho_{p}
.

For the special case
q=2
in the orthogonal-invariant ensemble,
\mathbb{W}
possesses additional eigenvalue-1 eigenstates, as discussed above. In this case, showing that
\rho(t)
acquires a ternary form for large
t
requires extra care, because Eq. (
213
) does not apply. We define
\mathbb{P}_{j}
as the projector onto the eigenvalue-1 eigenspace of
\langle\mathbb{W}_{j}\rangle
,
j=1,2
, and set
\mathbb{Q}_{j}=\openone-\mathbb{P}_{j}
. For any
\rho
, we also define
\alpha=\|\mathbb{P}_{1}(\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho)\|
.
For
q=2
, we bound
\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime 2}_{2}
directly:

\displaystyle\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime 2}_{2}=

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|\rho\|^{2}=1\\
\rho-B\rho\neq 0\end{subarray}}\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\rho\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{2}\!\left[\sigma_{2}^{2}+(1-\sigma_{2}^{2})\,\|P_{1}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}\right]

(214)

\displaystyle+(1-\sigma_{2}^{2})\!\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|\rho\|^{2}=1\\
\rho-B\rho\neq 0\end{subarray}}\|P^{\prime}_{1}\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\rho\|^{2}.

Here
\|\mathbb{P}_{1}\|^{\prime}_{2}=\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime}_{2}=0
if the only eigenvalue-1 vectors in
\mathbb{P}_{1}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}
are ternary states (so the projectors have no support on the
(\openone-\mathbb{B})
sector); otherwise
\|\mathbb{P}_{1}\|^{\prime}_{2}=\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime}_{2}=1
.

The eigenspace of
\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle
decomposes as
\mathbb{P}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{1}
. Given
|x\rangle=a\,\hat{x}_{1}+b\,\hat{x}_{1}^{\prime}
, where
\hat{x}_{1}
and
\hat{x}_{1}^{\prime}
are normalized vectors in the
\mathbb{P}_{1}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{1}
sectors, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\langle x|\mathbb{P}_{2}|x\rangle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\mathrm{Ran}(\mathbb{P}_{2})\\
\|\phi\|=1\;\&\;\phi-\mathbb{B}\phi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\big|\langle\phi|x\rangle\big|^{2}\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2},

(215)

with the constraint
(\openone-\mathbb{B})|x\rangle\neq 0
. Since vectors orthogonal to
|x\rangle
do not contribute to
|\langle\phi|x\rangle|^{2}
, the maximum is attained when
|x\rangle
and
\phi
lie in the same plane,
\phi=\cos\theta\,\hat{x}_{1}+\sin\theta\,\hat{x}_{1}^{\prime}
. Assuming

\|\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\mathrm{Ran}(\mathbb{P}_{2})\\
\|\phi\|=1\;\&\;\phi-\mathbb{B}\phi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\langle\phi|\mathbb{P}_{1}|\phi\rangle\leq\chi,

(216)

we obtain

\langle x|\mathbb{P}_{2}|x\rangle=\max_{\theta}\big(a\cos\theta+b\sin\theta\big)^{2}\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}

(217)

subject to
\cos^{2}\!\theta\leq\chi
. The maximum occurs either on the boundary or at an interior extremum, hence

\langle x|\mathbb{P}_{2}|x\rangle=\begin{cases}(a^{2}+b^{2})\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2},&\text{if }\dfrac{a^{2}}{a^{2}+b^{2}}\leq\chi,\\[6.0pt]
(a\sqrt{\chi}+b\sqrt{1-\chi})^{2}\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2},&\text{if }\dfrac{a^{2}}{a^{2}+b^{2}}>\chi.\end{cases}

(218)

Consequently, we obtain the inequalities

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle(\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\begin{cases}(\alpha^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}(1-\alpha^{2}))\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2},&\text{if }\eta\leq\chi,\\[4.30554pt]
\Xi^{2}\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2},&\text{if }\eta>\chi\end{cases}

(219)

with

\displaystyle\Xi=

\displaystyle\,\alpha\sqrt{\chi}+\sigma_{2}\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\sqrt{1-\chi},

(220)

\displaystyle\eta=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\|\mathbb{P}_{1}(\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho)\|^{2}}{\|\mathbb{P}_{1}(\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho)\|^{2}+\|\mathbb{Q}_{1}\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle(\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho)\|^{2}}.

(221)

If there exists a vector
\rho
such that
\eta\leq\chi
, then this holds if and only if

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|\mathbb{P}_{1}(\rho-B\rho)\|=\alpha\end{subarray}}\|\mathbb{Q}_{2}\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle(\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho)\|^{2}\geq\frac{1-\chi}{\chi}\,\alpha^{2}.

(222)

It follows that

\alpha^{2}\leq\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}\chi}{1-\chi+\sigma_{2}^{2}\chi}.

(223)

Furthermore, if
\alpha^{2}\geq\dfrac{\sigma_{2}^{2}\chi}{1-\chi+\sigma_{2}^{2}\chi}
, then

\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle(\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho)\|^{2}\leq\max\Big(\Xi^{2}\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}\Big).

(224)

The maximum of
\Xi
is attained at the value
\alpha^{2}
given by

\alpha^{2}=\frac{\chi}{\chi+\sigma_{2}^{2}(1-\chi)}>\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}\chi}{1-\chi+\sigma_{2}^{2}\chi}.

(225)

Hence, the inequality simplifies to

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle(\rho-\mathbb{B}\rho)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{multlined}\max\bigg\{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{1-\chi+\sigma_{2}^{2}\chi}\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2},\\
\big(\chi+(1-\chi)\sigma_{2}^{2}\big)\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}\bigg\}\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\chi+(1-\chi)\sigma_{2}^{2}\big)\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}.

(228)

Combining these results, we arrive at the final bound on the norm:

\displaystyle\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime 2}_{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{2}\left[\sigma_{2}^{2}+(1-\sigma_{2}^{2})\,\|\mathbb{P}_{1}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}\right]

(229)

\displaystyle+(1-\sigma_{2}^{2})\big(\chi+(1-\chi)\sigma_{2}^{2}\big)\,\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime 2}_{2}.

The parameter
\chi
can be calculated numerically and is found to be less than one. This ensures that
\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime 2}_{2}<1
, a result that is independent of the system size. While
\|\mathbb{P}_{1}\|^{\prime}_{2}=\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime}_{2}=1
for any
\chi>0
, both norms vanish in the limit
\chi=0
. Consequently, at
\chi=0
, the vanishing of
\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\|^{\prime}_{2}
simplifies the bound to
\|\langle\mathbb{W}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbb{W}_{1}\rangle\|^{\prime 2}_{2}\leq\sigma_{2}^{4}
, thereby recovering the unitary limit.

Appendix F
Haar orthogonal circuit for general qudit size
q

For a Haar orthogonal circuit of a general qudit size
q
, the steady state is defined in terms of the function

\rho^{\infty}_{p,s_{p}}(t)=\sum_{q}\gamma_{p}(t)\gamma^{*}_{q}(t)\prod_{x}\chi_{s_{p_{x}}}(p_{x},q_{x}),

(230)

where each
s_{p_{x}}\in\{+1,-1\}
and

VI
). The function

\displaystyle\rho_{p,s_{p}}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a,s_{a}}\ \rho_{a,s_{a}}(t-1)\langle|W_{ap,s_{a}s_{p}}|^{2}\rangle.

(231)

The transition probabilities
\langle|W_{ap}|^{2}\rangle
in this stochastic model factorize into pair contributions from the two-qudit gates, with the specific layer of gates depending on the parity of the timestep
t

\displaystyle\langle|W_{ap,s_{a}s_{p}}|^{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\prod_{x\,{\rm even}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle|{\cal W}_{ap,s_{a}s_{p};x,x+1}|^{2}\rangle&\mbox{for $t$ even},\\
\langle|{\cal W}_{ap,s_{a}s_{p};x-1,x}|^{2}\rangle&\mbox{for $t$ odd}.\end{array}\right.

The pair-transition probability
\langle|W_{ap;s_{a}s_{p};x,y}|^{2}\rangle
for the Haar orthogonal circuit at a generic dimension
q
can be expressed in the form of Eq. (
34
). For the Haar-random case, the coefficients are
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathcal{P}_{3}=0
, and

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\frac{\eta_{s_{p}}(p)}{q^{2}-1}\times\begin{cases}\frac{2}{q^{2}+2}&\mbox{if $s_{p_{x}}s_{p_{x+1}}=s_{a_{x}}s_{a_{x+1}}=1$},\\
\frac{2}{q^{2}}&\mbox{if $s_{p_{x}}s_{p_{x+1}}=s_{a_{x}}s_{a_{x+1}}=-1$},\\
0&\mbox{if $s_{p_{x}}s_{p_{x+1}}\neq s_{a_{x}}s_{a_{x+1}}$},\end{cases}

(234)

where the function
\eta_{s_{p}}(p)
is defined as

\eta_{s_{p}}(p)=\frac{(1+s_{p_{x}}\Delta(p_{x}))(1+s_{p_{x+1}}\Delta(p_{x+1}))}{4},

(235)

with

\delta_{p^{\prime}_{x},0}+\delta_{p^{\prime}_{x},\frac{q}{2}}(-1)^{p^{\prime\prime}_{x}},&q\text{ even}.\end{cases}
