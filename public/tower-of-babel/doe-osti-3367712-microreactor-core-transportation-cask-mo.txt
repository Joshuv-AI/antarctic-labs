 ABSTRACT
 Criticality safety analyses are completed on transportation casks used for microreactor whole
core shipment to provide examples of models and analyses to industry, regulators, and nuclear
community at large to be used in verification and validation analyses of similar applications. The
microreactors considered are based on a Gas-Cooled Microreactor (GCMR) and a Heat-Pipe
Microreactor (HPMR), both utilize HALEU fuel in the form of TRISO particles and various other
design options considered in industry microreactor designs. Variant design options of GCMR and
HPMR were also investigated to provide a wider application range for each technology. Criticality
safety analyses for the GCMR and HPMR packages were performed using the CSAS6 sequence of
SCALE 6.3.2 with the ENDF/B-VII.1-based continuous energy neutron libraries. Different
scenarios were investigated, including normal operation and water flooded conditions to represent
nominal and hypothetical accident scenarios. Sensitivity and similarity analyses are also performed
using the TSUNAMI sequence of SCALE 6.3.2, and the similarity analysis uses all the experiments
from the ICSBEP Handbook with High Enriched Uranium (HEU), Intermediate and mixed
Enriched Uranium (IEU) and Low Enriched Uranium (LEU) systems. Many ICSBEP experiments
were found marginally similar, with similarity index (ck) values greater than 0.8, to the GCMR and
HPMR cask models, especially in flooded conditions that may be more constraining due to reduced
reactivity margins. Some experiments using TRISO fuel particles and graphite moderator,
including the recent THETA and Deimos experiments from LANL, together with some IRPhEP
experiments, were also used for the similarity analyses. These TRISO-fueled and graphite
moderated experiments behaved similarly to the GCMR models, with maximum ck value greater
than 0.9, but they are less similar to the HPMR models. This observation indicates that additional
critical experiments might be needed to further validate the criticality safety models, especially for
heat-pipe based microreactor transport packages and for non-flooded transportation configurations.

1. Introduction
 The Department of Energy (DOE) / Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) Criticality Safety
for Commercial-Scale HALEU Fuel Cycle and Transportation (DNCSH) project aims to support
the NRC in providing data for criticality safety validation of reactor designs. This is part of a larger
initiative from the DOE to secure domestic supply of High-Assay Low Enriched Uranium
(HALEU) fuel for deployment of advanced nuclear reactors [1]. This report focuses on criticality
safety analyses applied to transportation of HALEU fueled microreactors.
 The advancement of microreactor technology has highlighted the need for specialized fuel
transportation packaging, as existing packages are not certified to transport the full range of fuel
configurations considered in these advanced reactors. Most microreactors concepts rely on a form
of HALEU fuel, and some even consider transportation of fully loaded fresh reactor, while currently
there aren’t any packages approved for reactor shipment.
 The previous study [2] provided an example of criticality safety analysis performed on a
microreactor assembly transportation package that is developed and analyzed based on a modified
ES-3100 [3] transportation cask. The current work builds from the previous study, performing
criticality safety analysis for microreactor whole-core transportation packages, with both a Gas-
Cooled Microreactor (GCMR) [4] and a Heat-Pipe Microreactor (HPMR) [5] selected for the
analysis.
 Criticality safety assessments were conducted using Standardized Computer Analyses for
Licensing Evaluation (SCALE) version 6.3.2 [6] to analyze the criticality margin for the
transportation packages under various operational conditions. Tools such as CSAS6 with KENO-
VI are used for neutron multiplication factor (keff) calculations, while TSUNAMI-3D/IP was
employed for sensitivity and similarity analyses, utilizing ENDF/B-VII.1 nuclear data libraries. The
study evaluated several configurations representing different transport conditions, including normal
conditions of transport (NCT) under dry scenario, and hypothetical accident conditions (HAC) with
water flooded. Parametric studies assessed design variations to provide conclusions that are
applicable to a wider range of microreactor concepts.
 The sensitivity data files (SDF) of the experiments from the International Criticality Safety
Benchmark Evaluation Project (ICSBEP) Handbook [7], including several which were originally
absent but were recently calculated by research staff at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL),
were selected for the similarity analysis to identify the validation gaps and quantify uncertainties
and biases of the nuclear data and calculation methods [8]. The recently developed new criticality
experiments, Deimos [9] and THETA [10], by Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) and
partially funded by the DNCSH program, are also used for the similarity analysis. A few TRISO-
fueled and graphite moderated experiments from International Handbook of Evaluated Reactor
Physics Benchmark Experiments (IRPhEP handbook) [11], for which the sensitivity data files
(SDF) were recently generated or updated by ORNL staffs, are also selected for the similarity
analysis.

2. Model Description
  In this analysis, the main calculation tool is SCALE 6.3.2 [6]. The Criticality Safety Analysis
Sequences (CSAS6) with KENO-VI, were utilized for keff calculations. The SCALE sensitivity and
uncertainty analysis sequences, known as the Tools for Sensitivity and Uncertainty Analysis
Methodology Implementation (TSUNAMI), are used for sensitivity and similarity analysis.
TSUNAMI-3D with KENO-VI was utilized to calculate the sensitivity data files (SDFs), while
TSUNAMI-IP (TSUNAMI Indices and Parameters) was used with the generated SDFs for
similarity analysis. For both CSAS6 and TSUNAMI-3D sequences and KENO-IV calculations, the
ENDF/B-VII.1-based continuous energy neutron libraries are used.

2.1 GCMR Core Model
 The GCMR model was developed at ANL for the DOE Nuclear Energy Advanced Modeling
and Simulation (NEAMS) Program as a modeling experiment that gathers several expected
modeling challenges encountered by the U.S. microreactor industry. This core concept has been
modeled in detail and is publicly available on the Virtual Test Bed (VTB) [12]. The GCMR
assembly uses a graphite structure, TRi-structural ISOtropic (TRISO) fuel blocks with U-235
enriched at 19.95 wt.% HALEU fuel, YH₂ moderator pins with FeCrAl envelope, and axial/radial
BeO reflector regions. The fuel blocks are formed by TRISO fuel particles, with 40% packing
fraction, dispersed in graphite matrix. In this work, additional modeling specificities were
implemented into the GCMR models such as burnable poison blocks, helium coolant channels and
central control and shutdown rods. This horizontal gas-cooled microreactor system provides a
thermal power of 20 MW with an approximate lifespan of 9.5 years. Its power conversion cycle
utilizes a Brayton cycle, circulating high-temperature (650°C-850°C) and high-pressure (7 MPa)
helium coolant. Surrounding the core are twelve control drums positioned in the radial reflector
encircling the core. Control (shutdown) rods, containing 96 wt.%-enriched B4C, are inserted into
holes within the middle core assemblies, to increase the subcriticality margin during transportation.
The radial and axial views of GCMR core are shown in Figure 2-1. It is a relatively compact core,
measuring 2.42 m in diameter and 2.40 m in length.
 This project is leveraging existing GCMR whole-core neutronic models [12] using Monte Carlo
code Serpent [13], and this model was manually converted to CSAS6/KENO-VI model, but with
all the compositions set at room temperature (293 K) for criticality-safety analysis of reactor in
transportation conditions. For the case with TRISO particles modeled explicitly, SCALE 6.3.2 has
the capability to generate geometry model with random fuel (or absorber) particles, but only the
cases with packing fractions less than 20% could be achieved due to the limit of stochastic
algorithm. Therefore, in this analysis, the fuel and absorber particles are filled in cubic array
preserving the packing fraction. Using the same modeling of TRISO particles and ENDF/B-VII.1
neutron data library in Serpent, the values of keff calculated by Serpent and CSAS6 for the GCMR
core, with control drums and control rods fully withdrawn, are compared in Table 2-1, and it could

2.2 HPMR Core Model
 The Heat-Pipe Microreactor (HPMR) model [5] is also developed under the NEAMS program
as a modeling exercise to gather some of the most pressing modeling challenges faced by the micro-
reactor industry: a) the use of heat pipe technologies to remove the nuclear heat; b) the use of
TRISO fuel to enable operations at very high temperatures; c) the use of rotating control rod drums
in the radial reflectors. This core concept has been modeled in detail and is publicly available on
the VTB [14].
 This core has a rated power of 2 MWth and its layout is shown in Figure 2-3. A traditional TRISO
fuel with 19.95 wt.% High-Assay Low-Enriched Uranium (HALEU) in UCO form was adopted in
a hexagonal graphite matrix with a 40% packing fraction. The core length is set to 160 cm with 20
cm upper and lower axial reflectors made of beryllium metal. Thirty fuel assemblies are surrounded
by one ring of beryllium hexagonal reflector and 12 control drums. The HPMR core has a 60-degree
symmetry, and one sixth of the core geometry is shown in Figure 2-3 [12].

 10

 Figure 2-3. One sixth geometry of the HPMR core

 To achieve an optimum level of moderation, yttrium-hydride (YH2) pins are employed in
addition to the graphite structure component, as YH2 provides more efficient neutron slowing-down
capability, enabling the design of a more compact core. The yttrium-hydride is clad in a stainless
steel envelope, with a helium gap between the YH 2 and envelope and then another helium gap
between the envelope and the graphite monolith.
 This concept employs heat pipes with a stainless-steel envelope and potassium working fluid.
The heat pipe is separated from the graphite monolith by a helium gap. The heat pipe is divided
into three radial regions: the working fluid vapor, the working fluid liquid plus wick, and the
envelope.
 The control system of the core includes 12 control drums located in the radial reflector that are
capable of bringing the core to cold shutdown throughout the operation of the reactor. For
redundancy purposes, a shutdown rod is located in the central location.
 Similar to the GCMR case, one leverages the HPMR whole-core neutronic models using Monte
Carlo code Serpent [13] from the VTB, and this model was manually converted to CSAS6/KENO-
VI model, with the TRISO particles filled in cubic array preserving the packing fraction, and with
all the compositions at room temperature (293 K). Using the same modeling of TRISO particles
and ENDF/B-VII.1 neutron data library in Serpent, the values of keff calculated by Serpent and
CSAS6 for the GCMR core are compared in Table 2-2. Radial configurations of both cases with
control drums rotated in and out are shown in Figure 2-4. The keff calculated by CSAS6 agrees well
with that of Serpent.

3. Results for the GCMR Cask Model
 Criticality safety analysis was performed with SCALE/CSAS6 on the GCMR cask model. For
the original design of the GCMR core, UCO-type fuel TRISO particles with packing fraction (PF)
between 20% and 40% were considered in order to investigate the impact on similarity results. An
additional configuration of the core using UN (Uranium Nitride)-type TRISO particles is also
analyzed as a variant design option.
 For each design option (reference and variant), minor changes to the original design (burnable
absorbent concentration, etc.) are implemented to target initial keff around 1.05. The investigation
of several GCMR variants aims to extend similarity analysis for wider range of microreactor design
options.

3.1 Criticality Safety Eigenvalue Calculations
3.1.1 Evaluation of reference GCMR design option
 For transportation of a microreactor, it is unrealistic to pack multiple casks into the same
shipment. Therefore, only the single package model is evaluated in this analysis. Based on
guidelinse from the NRC [16], the single package calculations must consider a model of the single
containment vessel fully reflected by water (a 30-cm-thick region of full-density water is
recommended). However, for the GCMR cask model, due to the existence of built-in pressure
vessel and BeO reflector, the water reflected boundary external to the cask would have little impact
on reactivity when the internal face of the cask is dry (as further shown in the following analysis).
 Five configurations described in Table 3-1 correspond to different water intrusion and control
drums/shutdown rod positions:
• Configuration 1 would show the excess reactivity of the package under dry scenario with all
 reactivity control systems extracted.
• Configuration 2 corresponds to the normal condition of transport (NCT)
• Configuration 3 corresponds a case with fully water flooded
• Configuration 4 corresponds a case with partially water flooded (the coolant channel would be
 dry under this configuration)
• Configuration 5 corresponds a case with fully water flooded, with part (25%) of absorber lost
 for all the control drums and shutdown rods.

 The configurations of GCMR core with control drums/shutdown rods fully in and out are shown
in Figure 3-1. For the design of GCMR utilized in this study, each fuel assembly has a central
control hole for shutdown rod, which is different from the original design where only the fuel

 15

 assemblies in middle core have central control holes. In this study the shutdown rod of GCMR is
 made of 96 wt.%-enriched B4C which is the same as the absorber material of control drum.

3.2 Sensitivity and Similarity Analysis
 The objective of the similarity analysis is to identify validation gaps and quantify uncertainties
and biases in nuclear data and methods by comparing applications to similar benchmark critical
experiments which have been analyzed, reviewed, and validated by nuclear criticality safety
experts. A similarity index, named ck, is calculated by correlating calculated keff sensitivities for a
given application and a reference benchmark, yielding a value less than or equal to one. If two
compared systems are very similar, ck > 0.9, then the application is assumed to behave like the
benchmark and have similar uncertainties and biases, helping analysts and regulators to assess the
safe operation of the application. The sensitivity calculation is a required preliminary step to
generate a sensitivity data file (SDF) which would be used to compute similarity coefficients in the
following calculations.

3.2.1 Sensitivity analysis
 First TSUNAMI-3D calculations were performed for the five configurations, shown in Table
3-1, for the reference design, to generate the SDFs. For the generated sensitivity data files, the
energy dependent (region integrated) sensitivity coefficients for neutron cross section of U-235
fission and C-graphite total are plotted in Figure 3-3 and Figure 3-4, with the sensitivity coefficients
tallied into the SCALE 252-energy-group structure1. The energy-integrated sensitivity coefficients
for each configuration are also shown in the legend.
 In Figure 3-3, the water flooded cases (configurations 3 and 5) display larger sensitivity
coefficients in the thermal energy range for the U-235 fission cross section. This phenomenon is
expected because of the softer neutron spectrum for the water flooded cases. It could also be seen
that configurations 3 and 5 have similar sensitivity coefficients, both for energy integrated values
and for the ones with 252-energy-group, due to the similarity of neutron spectrum of these two
configurations. The dry scenario configuration 2 also behaves similar to the partially flooded
configuration 4.

3.2.2 Similarity analysis

3.2.2.1     ICSBEP
 With generated SDFs for both reference and variant design options, the GCMR is examined
against reference SDFs of critical experiments using TSUNAMI-IP. The generated sensitivity
profiles for each configuration under different design options, in conjunction with the ENDF/B-
VII.1 56-group covariance library included in SCALE 6.3.2, were used in TSUNAMI-IP to produce
estimated nuclear data–induced uncertainties [18]. A correlation coefficient called similarity index,
ck was used to determine the similarity of the applications and experiments by evaluating the data-
induced uncertainties. The value ck is one of several integral similarity indices considered valuable
for validation, and it provides a rigorous quantitative approach to selecting experiments. If c k is
equal to or greater than 0.90, it indicates a high degree of similarity between the application and

 24

experiment. If ck is between 0.80 and 0.90, although not ideal, the application and experiment could
still be considered marginally similar.
 The neutron spectrum plays an important role in determining the similarity between two
systems. The neutron spectra of GCMR reference design and PWR are compared in Figure 3-5,
with neutron flux tallied using the SCALE 252-energy-group structure. It could be seen that
compared with PWR neutron spectrum, the GCMR dry scenarios (configuration 1 and 2) have
smaller neutron flux in the thermal range but apparently larger neutron flux in the resonance range.
The neutron spectrum of GCMR configuration 5 (water flooded) is apparently over-moderated,
compared with that of PWR. In this report the similarity analysis heavily replies on the experiments
from the ICSBEP Handbook, many of which are water moderated system, and the neutron spectra
impact would be shown in the following analysis.

3.2.2.2 THETA and Deimos
 Recently, new criticality experiments THETA and Deimos were developed at LANL. Both the
Deimos and THETA experiments are nuclear critical experiments using TRISO fuel particles with
HALEU fuels with reflector and moderator materials representative of those proposed in some of
advanced reactor technologies such as graphite, beryllium, yttrium hydride, Hypatia, 316 stainless
steel, and polyethylene [9] [10]. Ten experiment configurations, with five for THETA and five for
Deimos, can be evaluated using their corresponding SDF files. The sensitivity data files for these
experiments were originally generated in 2025 based on the MCNP6 results using ENDF/B-VIII.1
data library, while possibly due to the different treatment of graphite isotopes between ENDF/B-
VIII.1 (C-12 and C-13 separate) and ENDF/B-VII.1 (C-12 and C-13 combined together), the
similarity analysis didn’t give satisfying results. In 2026, the SDF files were updated by ORNL
staff using ENDF/B-VII.1 data library, and the similarity analysis for the GCMR cask models was
re-performed with the ck values shown in Table 3-8 and Table 3-9. The number of similar cases
among the ten experiment configurations of Deimos and THETA relative to the GCMR
configurations, is also shown in Figure 3-11 (with ck > 0.80) and Figure 3-12 (with ck > 0.90).
 The THETA and Deimos experiments are marginally similar (with ck > 0.80) to most of the
GCMR design options and configurations, and they are highly similar (with ck > 0.90) to some of
the water flooded configurations of most GCMR design options. The GCMR variant 6 design
option (without burnable absorber) is the least similar case as the Deimos and THETA experiments,
because the fuel content (TRISO PF and fuel pin radius) is reduced significantly to preserve the
excess reactivity.
 The TRISO fuel particles packing fraction is ~60% in the THETA and Deimos experiments. The
sensitivity coefficient of U-235 fission cross section for the THETA configuration 3 is plotted in
Figure 3-6 and is reasonably close to those of GCMR reference design. When comparing the ck
values among the GCMR dry configurations (configurations 1 & 2) with different TRISO packing
fraction (reference design, variant 1, 2 and 9), it could be seen variant 1 tends to have the largest ck
values although the TRISO PF is only 20%, while variant 9, in which the TRISO PF is 60%, tends
to have the smallest ck values. It should be noted that in this analysis, when the TRISO PF is
changed, other design parameters, e.g. the content of burnable absorber, are also adjusted to
preserve the excess reactivity of GCMR. This could partially explain that the variant 1 design with
20% TRISO PF gets larger ck values than the variant 9 design with 60% TRISO PF, because
combination of design parameters (fuel TRISO PF, burnable absorber content, others) happens to
perform similar as the THETA and Deimos experiments.

4. Results for the HPMR Cask Models
 Criticality safety analysis was performed with SCALE/CSAS6 on the HPMR cask model. The
original HPMR design uses the same type of UCO TRISO particle as the GCMR, but no burnable
absorbers were included. The fuel TRISO particles packing fraction (PF) is 40%. Several variant
design options, e.g. using the different monolith material, were also analyzed to evaluate a wide
range of microreactor options in our similarity analysis with ICSBEP experiments. For each design
option (reference and variant), a few selected configurations were selected, and similarity analyses
were completed with SCALE/TSUNAMI.

4.1 Criticality Safety Eigenvalue Calculations
4.1.1 Evaluation of reference HPMR design option
 Only the single package model is evaluated for HPMR in this analysis, and the compact cask
design without Kaolite filling shown in Figure 2-5, is considered as the reference design option.
The thickness of built-in reactor vessel of HPMR is assumed to be only 2.0 cm, which is much
smaller than the thickness of pressure-vessel of GCMR. To comply with the NRC guideline which
requires at least 30 cm of water reflector outside the cask, more than 50 cm of water is added outside
the cask of HPMR even for the dry scenario, to give a conservative keff.
 The reference HPMR design has an empty central location, reserved for shutdown rod
(assembly) to increase the shutdown reactivity margin. Tentatively a shutdown rod assembly,
consisting of 37 absorber pins made of B4C with natural boron, was introduced. Similar to the
GCMR cask analysis, five configurations of HPMR cask listed in Table 4-1 were analyzed,
corresponding to different water intrusion and control drums/shutdown rod positions:
• Configuration 1 would show the excess reactivity of the package under dry scenario with all
 reactivity systems extracted.
• Configuration 2 corresponds to the normal condition of transportation
• Configuration 3 corresponds to a case fully flooded with water
• Configuration 4 corresponds to a case partially flooded with water. (The heat-pipes would not
 be flooded under this configuration)
• Configuration 5 corresponds to a case with partial water flooding, with part (25%) of absorber
 lost. (The heat-pipes would be dry under this configuration)

4.1.2 Evaluation of variant HPMR design options
 In this section, the following variant design options of HPMR were analyzed for criticality safety
analysis:
• Variant 1 uses a large cask with Kaolite filling, and the configuration is shown in Figure 2-6,
 while the HPMR core is the same as the reference design.
• Variant 2 uses a compact cask as the reference design, while the central hole is filled with a
 graphite block and the shutdown rods are inserted into the radial reflector region. The fuel
 TRISO particles packing fraction is reduced slightly to 37%, to preserve the excess reactivity
 of the reference design.
• Variants 3, 4, 5 and 6 use different monolith materials, and the fuel TRISO particles packing
 fractions are reduced (if applicable) accordingly to preserve the excess reactivity of the
 reference design.
• Variant 7 uses heat-pipe with sodium working fluid instead of potassium.
 The change of design parameters of these six HPMR variant designs, compared with the
reference design, are shown in Table 4-3. The same type of UCO TRISO particles is used for all
the reference and variant design options.
 For each variant design option model, three configurations defined in Table 4-1 are investigated,
and the values of keff are shown in Table 4-4. For the dry scenario Configuration 1 in which all the
control drums and shutdown rods are fully out, the variant design options have about the same keff
as the reference design, except for variant 5 and 6 (those display additional shutdown margins).
 For variant 1 design, the keff values are statistically unchanged from the reference design. Due
to the existence of radial reflector, the enlarged cask with Kaolite filling has very little impact on
keff .
 For variant 2 design, due to filling the central hole with graphite block, the fuel particle packing
fraction was decreased from 40% to 37% to preserve the excess reactivity. Each reflector assembly
has four shutdown rods inserted, as shown in Figure 4-6, and the shutdown rod is made of 96%-
enriched B4C which is the same as the absorber material of the control drums. Compared to
reference design, the variant 2 design would make the radial neutron flux and power profiles center-
tilted, and the reflector-located control drums, even combined with shutdown rods, couldn’t give
sufficient reactivity margin. As a result, configuration 5 is supercritical with keff > 1.0.
 For variants 3 to 6 testing different monolith materials other than reactor-grade graphite (density
~ 1.8 g/cm3), only the case using graphite with theoretical density 2.2 g/cm3 (variant 3) and the case
using BeO (variant 4) could increase reactivity, and the fuel particle packing fraction needs to be
decreased accordingly. For the cases using MgO (variant 5) and SiC (variant 6) as monolith
material, the keff values decreases. Since in this study the maximum fuel particles packing fraction
is 40%, the variant 5 and 6 design options would have much smaller excess reactivity compared
with the reference design.

 47

 For variant 7 design, sodium heat-pipes were used instead of the potassium ones used in all the
other design options. All the other design parameters are consistent with the reference design, and
the keff values increase by ~ 1,200 pcm compared with the reference design.
 It should be mentioned that these variant design options of HPMR cask models are simply
introduced as a parameterization study, and they need further revision to become practical, which
is outside the scope of this study.

4.2.2.1    ICSBEP
 Using the SDFs generated by TSUNAMI-3D for reference and variant design options, the
HPMR cask is examined against reference SDFs of critical experiments using TSUNAMI-IP, in
conjunction with the ENDF/B-VII.1 56-group covariance library included in SCALE 6.3.2.
 The generated SDFs for the HPMR cask models, were first examined against the critical
experiments included in the ICSBEP handbook. All the reference SDFs available from the ICSBEP
Handbook using HEU, IEU and LEU, as well as the recently generated ones for ‘IEU-COMP-
THERM-008’ and ‘IEU-COMP-THERM-010’, are used as references to examine the generated
sensitivity data files.
  For the experiments included in the ICSBEP Handbook, almost all the cases similar with the
HPMR cask models are under category ‘LEU-COMP-THERM’. For the experiments under other
categories, only one experiment ‘IEU-COMP-THERM-009’ is marginally similar as the HPMR
cask models, with ck greater than 0.80. It should be mentioned that the experiment ‘IEU-COMP-
THERM-010’, for which the SDF file was recently calculated by ORNL staff, is not similar as the
HPMR cask models yielding a maximum ck ~ 0.6, although it had been found to be similar with
many of the GCMR cask models (as discussed in Section 3.2.2).

fully flooded configuration (configuration 3 of reference design), and the maximum ck ~ 0.68. This

cases under the category ‘LEU-SOL-THERM’.

5. Conclusions
 Criticality safety analyses were completed on transportation casks for whole core microreactors
to provide example models and results for industry, regulators, and end-users to reproduce this type
of study on their verification and validation of microreactor transportation and storage. The
microreactors considered in this study are based on the GCMR and HPMR models developed by
ANL under the NEAMS program, those utilize HALEU fuel in the form of TRISO particles and
various design options considered in some of the proposed designs that are either actively being
developed or at various stages of certification.
 Criticality safety analyses for the GCMR and HPMR transportation packages were performed
using the CSAS6 sequence of SCALE 6.3.2 with the ENDF/B-VII.1 based continuous energy
neutron libraries. Initial code-to-code comparison was completed with Serpent to verify the
developed SCALE models. Different scenarios, e.g. dry scenarios for normal operation, water
flooded cask losing part of absorber, were analyzed to verify that the packages with reference
GCMR and HPMR designs always display adequate shutdown margins.
 Sensitivity and similarity analyses were also performed using the TSUNAMI sequence of
SCALE 6.3.2, and the similarity analysis uses all the experiments from ICSBEP Handbook with
high, intermediate and low enriched uranium systems, together with the THETA and Deimos
experiment models supported by the DNCSH program, and selected IRPhEP experiments with
TRISO particle-type fuel and graphite moderator.
 For GCMR models, only the HTR-10 experiment (TRISO-fueled graphite moderated system,
with SDF recently calculated), among the ICSBEP handbook experiments, behaves similar (ck
values greater than 0.8) to the normal conditions (dry) model, while the water flooded GCMR
models have many similar experiments within the ICSBEP handbook. The Deimos and THETA
experiments sponsored by the DNCSH program primarily focus on HALEU TRISO fuel for
advanced reactor designs. Those were found marginally similar (ck>0.8) to most of the GCMR
models, and highly similar cases (ck>0.9) could also be found for some of the water flooded GCMR
models. Four selected IRPhEP experiments with TRISO fuel particles and graphite moderator are
also used for the similarity analysis, and these IRPhEP experiments also behave similarly to the
investigated GCMR models: the water flooded GCMR models could find high similar cases
(ck>0.9) among the selected IRPhEP experiments. For all the investigated GCMR models, the only
high similar cases (ck>0.9) are from the experiments using TRISO-fueled graphite moderated
technology.
 For HPMR models, both cases for normal conditions of transport and for water flooded scenarios
could be paired with many similar experiments from the ICSBEP handbook, while none of them
are using similar TRISO-fueled graphite moderated technology. For the Deimos and THETA
experiments, and the selected IRPhEP experiments, they were found to be less similar to the
investigated GCMR models, which may be explained by the heat-pipe technology used in the
HPMR design. Additional critical experiments might be needed to further validate the heat-pipe
based microreactors transport packages considered in this report.
