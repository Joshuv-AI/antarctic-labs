Occupational exposure to 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) and associated oxidative and genomic biomarkers among soybean farmers: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Camila Baldin, Sheila de Castro Cardoso Toniasso, Ana Carolina Lazzari, Nelson David Suarez Uribe, Vittoria Calvi Sampaio, Raquel Boff da Costa, Patrícia Gabriela Riedel, Maria Carlota Borba Brum, Débora Silva Costa, Norma Possa Marroni, Elizângela Schemitt, Sandielly Rebeca Benitez Fonseca, Juliana Silva, Ana Letícia Garcia, Eliane Dallegrave, Osmar Damian Prestes, Marisa Demarco, Renato Zanella, Luciana Grazziotin Rossato Grando, Cícero de Campos Baldin, Dvora Joveleviths
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) is a herbicide widely used for weed control in soybean cultivation. This study aimed to investigate hepatic and genetic damage biomarkers in farmers occupationally exposed to 2,4-D, widely used in soybean cultivation in southern Brazil (Rio Grande do Sul), in addition to estimate urinary 2,4-D concentrations as an indicator of recent exposure.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted, including 54 occupationally exposed farmers and 51 non-exposed controls (organic farmers).ResultsUrinary 2,4-D was detected in 88.5% of the exposed group versus 25% of the controls (p < 0.001). Creatine kinase (CK) was significantly elevated in the exposed group (p < 0.05), suggesting possible muscle injury, while AST and ALT (classical liver enzymes) did not differ between groups. Oxidative stress markers showed a clear pattern of redox imbalance, with increased TBARS (p < 0.001), increased CAT activity (p < 0.001), and reduced SOD activity (p < 0.001). Telomere length was significantly shorter in the exposed group (p = 0.001). Use of personal protective equipment (PPE) was reported to be inadequate.ConclusionOccupational exposure to 2,4-D is associated with systemic oxidative stress and telomere shortening, even in the absence of transaminase elevation. This points to early hepatocellular vulnerability mediated by oxidative mechanisms rather than overt cytolysis. The study emphasizes the need for continuous monitoring of populations chronically exposed to chlorophenoxy herbicides.Keywords: 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid; Biomonitoring; Occupational exposure; Oxidative stress; Telomere length

Introduction

Brazil ranks among the world's leading agricultural producers and exporters, with commodities such as soybean, sugarcane, and maize playing a central role in the national economy1, 2 The high productivity of these crops relies heavily on chemical inputs, particularly herbicides. Among them, glyphosate and 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) are extensively applied, especially in soybean cultivation.2 Although these compounds contribute substantially to crop yield, increasing attention has been directed toward their potential adverse effects on environmental integrity and human health.3

2,4-D is a selective systemic herbicide widely employed for the control of broadleaf weeds. Its relatively low cost, broad-spectrum efficacy, and agronomic selectivity have supported its global dissemination.4 However, widespread and often indiscriminate application has resulted in environmental dispersion, affecting both aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems.5, 6 Furthermore, environmental degradation processes may generate metabolites with enhanced toxicity, thereby amplifying ecological risks and potential impacts on biodiversity.7

Human exposure to 2,4-D occurs through environmental contamination and occupational activities. The general population may be exposed through contaminated water and food, whereas agricultural workers are particularly vulnerable due to direct handling and field application.8 Epidemiological evidence has associated 2,4-D exposure with an increased risk of non-Hodgkin lymphoma,9 endocrine disruption,10 and neurodegenerative disorders such as Parkinson's disease.11

Experimental investigations have demonstrated hepatotoxic effects of 2,4-D in different biological models, including rodents and fish.12, 13, 14 These toxic effects are closely linked to oxidative imbalance, as the compound promotes the excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and free radicals. Alterations in antioxidant defense systems have been reported, including reduced activity of catalase (CAT), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase (GPx), along with increased lipid peroxidation of cellular membranes.15, 16, 17 Such findings suggest that oxidative stress plays a central role in 2,4-D–induced toxicity.

Assessment of genotoxicity represents a critical approach for evaluating cellular damage resulting from chemical exposure. The micronucleus (MN) assay in exfoliated buccal cells is a well-established biomarker for detecting chromosomal instability and DNA damage. In addition, telomere length has emerged as a complementary biomarker in genotoxic biomonitoring studies.18, 19 Both critically shortened and excessively elongated telomeres have been associated with increased cancer susceptibility, including lung and kidney malignancies. Shortened telomeres are also frequently observed in several other cancers, such as bladder, head and neck, ovarian, gastric, skin, osteosarcoma, and esophageal tumors.20 These findings reinforce the importance of genetic damage monitoring in populations exposed to potentially hazardous agents.

Following ingestion, inhalation, or dermal absorption, 2,4-D is rapidly absorbed and predominantly eliminated in urine in its unmetabolized form. Its biological half-life ranges from 10 to 33 h, with an average of approximately 17.7 h.21 Despite its extensive use in Brazilian agriculture, data on human biomonitoring of 2,4-D remain scarce.

Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate hepatic and genetic damage biomarkers in farmers occupationally exposed to 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid, a herbicide widely used in soybean cultivation in southern Brazil (Rio Grande do Sul).

Materials and methods

Study population

A cross-sectional study was conducted in July 2023, during the period of 2,4-D application in soybean crops. The exposed group comprised 54 soybean farmers from the Midwestern Plateau region of Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil, who were actively involved in pesticide application. The control group included 51 agricultural workers from the metropolitan region of Porto Alegre, Rio Grande do Sul, engaged exclusively in certified organic farming.

Inclusion criteria for the exposed group were: (i) age ≥ 18 years and (ii) occupational use of 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) for at least five years. For the control group, eligibility criteria were: (i) age ≥ 18 years; (ii) a minimum of five years working in organic agriculture; and (iii) certification or documented compliance with organic production standards accredited by official regulatory agencies. Participants with a current diagnosis of malignant neoplasm were excluded.

The study protocol was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre (approval number: 68042722.4.0000.5327). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Data collection and clinical assessment

Data were obtained through a structured questionnaire administered by the research team, including sociodemographic characteristics, educational level, occupational history, work practices, use of personal protective equipment (PPE), duration of 2,4-D exposure, self-reported health conditions, and current medication use. Alcohol consumption was assessed using the Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (AUDIT).

All participants underwent anthropometric evaluation and physical examination. Rapid serological tests for hepatitis B and C were performed. Peripheral blood samples were collected for analyses of hematology, biochemistry, oxidative stress, genotoxicity, and butyrylcholinesterase (BChE) activity. Samples were collected in the field, centrifuged onsite, and serum aliquots were transported under refrigerated conditions to Porto Alegre for laboratory processing.

Biochemical and hematological analyses

Colorimetric methods determined serum levels of urea and creatinine. Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) were measured using enzymatic assays, and alkaline phosphatase was assessed by a kinetic colorimetric method (p-NPP – DGKC). Complete blood count and platelet analysis were performed using automated light absorbance, impedance, and flow cytometry techniques. Butyrylcholinesterase (BChE) activity was determined by a kinetic enzymatic method.

Oxidative stress parameters

Lipid peroxidation was quantified in serum by measuring thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS), according to Buege and Aust (1978),22 and results were expressed as nmol/mg protein. Total protein concentration was determined using the Bradford method (1976).23 Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity was evaluated following the method of Misra and Fridovich (1972),24 with results expressed as U SOD/min/mg protein. Catalase (CAT) activity was determined according to Boveris and Chance (1973),25 and expressed as pmol/min/mg protein.

Genotoxicity assessment

Genotoxic damage was assessed using the Buccal Micronucleus Cytome (BMCyt) assay. Biomarkers of DNA damage, including micronuclei (MN), nuclear buds (NBUDs), broken eggs, and binucleated cells (BIN), were classified according to Thomas et al. (2009).26 A total of 2000 cells per participant were evaluated under light microscopy. Cells presenting MN or NBUDs were considered positive for genotoxic damage.

Telomere length measurement

Relative telomere length (TL) was determined by quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) following the method described by Cawthon (2002),27 with modifications proposed by Kahl et al. (2018).28 Genomic DNA was extracted using the PureLink™ Genomic DNA Isolation Kit (Invitrogen, USA) and quantified using a NanoDrop™ Lite spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific™). Only samples with OD260/OD280 ratios between 1.7 and 2.0 were included. DNA was diluted to a working concentration of 5 ng/μL.

A standard curve was generated using serial dilutions of pooled genomic DNA, which also served as reference DNA in all experiments. Amplification specificity was verified by melting curve analysis. Assay efficiency was considered acceptable between 90% and 110%, with R2 values between 0.98 and 1.00.

Samples were analyzed in triplicate. Reactions presenting a coefficient of variation >5% were reassessed and excluded if technical error was confirmed. Only samples with a standard deviation of Ct < 0.5 among triplicates were included in the final analysis.

Telomeric and single-copy gene (36B4) amplification was performed using SYBR™ Green PowerUp Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, USA) in a final reaction volume of 20 μL containing 20 ng genomic DNA and 0.2 μmol/L of each primer. Primer sequences were as follows:

Telomere forward: 5′-GGTTTTTGAGGGTGAGGGTGAGGGTGAGGGTGAGGGT-3′

•Telomere reverse: 5′-CCCGACTATCCCTATCCCTATCCCTATCCCTATCCCTA-3′

Telomere reverse: 5′-CCCGACTATCCCTATCCCTATCCCTATCCCTATCCCTA-3′

•36B4 forward: 5′-CAGCAAGTGGGAAGGTGTAATCC-3′

36B4 forward: 5′-CAGCAAGTGGGAAGGTGTAATCC-3′

•36B4 reverse: 5′-CCCATTCTATCATCAACGGGTACAA-3′

qPCR cycling conditions consisted of an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 15 s and 60 °C for 1 min. Telomere length was calculated by converting the T/S ratio into base pairs (bp) using the equation AX + B, where X represents the T/S ratio and A (slope) and B (intercept) were derived from the standard curve, as described by Dhillon et al. (2017).29

Negative controls, pooled genomic DNA, and the lymphoblastic T-leukemia cell line 1301 (positive control) were included in each plate. Amplification was performed using the StepOnePlus™ Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA).

Determination of urinary 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D).

Urine samples were collected in polypropylene tubes and stored at −80 °C until analysis. Quantification of 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) was performed using a modified QuEChERS (Quick, Easy, Cheap, Effective, Rugged, and Safe) extraction protocol.30

Briefly, 10 mL of urine (previously centrifuged at 6000 rpm for 3 min and adjusted to pH 5) were extracted with 2 mL of acidified acetonitrile. Phase separation was achieved by addition of magnesium sulfate and sodium chloride. A dispersive solid-phase extraction (d-SPE) cleanup step was subsequently performed by mixing an aliquot of the supernatant with magnesium sulfate, primary secondary amine (PSA), and C18 sorbent. The purified extract was diluted (1:4, v/v) with ultrapure water, filtered, and subjected to ultrahigh-performance liquid chromatography coupled with tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC–MS/MS) analysis.

Chromatographic determination was carried out using a Xevo® TQ-XS UHPLC–MS/MS system (Waters, USA) equipped with an Acquity UPLC® HSS T3 analytical column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm particle size). The mobile phase consisted of (A) water and (B) acetonitrile: methanol (1:1, v/v), both containing 0.1% (v/v) formic acid and 5 mmol/L ammonium formate. Elution was performed at a constant flow rate of 0.5 mL/min, with an injection volume of 5 μL and a total run time of 19 min.

The method limit of detection (LOD) was 0.030 μg/L, and the limit of quantification (LOQ) was 0.100 μg/L. Urinary creatinine was measured in a separate aliquot of each sample to allow for concentration adjustment.

Statistical analysis

Sample size estimation was performed using WINPEPI software, based on the expected differences between group means for quantitative variables, assuming a statistical power of 80% and a two-sided alpha level of 0.05.

Continuous variables were assessed for distributional assumptions and described as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data or median and interquartile range (IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were summarized as absolute frequencies and percentages.

Between-group comparisons for continuous variables were performed using the independent Student's t-test when normality assumptions were met. The Mann–Whitney U test was applied for non-parametric data. Differences in proportions were evaluated using the Pearson chi-square test.

Associations between urinary 2,4-D concentrations and numerical variables were examined using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient.

To control for potential confounding factors, including age and use of antilipidemic medications, generalized linear models (GLM) were constructed. When appropriate, Bonferroni correction was applied for multiple comparisons. Poisson regression analysis was performed for count-dependent outcomes.

A two-tailed p-value ≤0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software (version 28.0).

Demographic and occupational characteristics

All control participants were from the metropolitan region of Porto Alegre. Among exposed individuals, 42.6% were from Passo Fundo, 20.4% from Marau, and 37.0% from Casca. Body mass index (BMI) did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0.570).

PPE use was reported by 69.8% of exposed workers and 22.7% of controls (p < 0.001). Nevertheless, PPE adherence among exposed individuals was considered inadequate, since approximately 30% reported not using protective equipment during pesticide handling.

Approximately 39.6% of exposed workers reported occupational pesticide exposure for ≥30 years. The median duration of recent 2,4-D exposure was 20 days.

The cultivated area was significantly larger in the exposed group (p < 0.001), reflecting large-scale soybean production.

Lifestyle and clinical variables

Risky alcohol consumption (≥2 doses at least 2–3 times per week or ≥ 4 times per week) was observed in 9.8% of controls and 3.8% of exposed participants, with no statistically significant difference (p = 0.265).

Regarding medication use, 60% of controls and 42.6% of exposed individuals reported no regular medication use. However, antilipidemic drug use was significantly more frequent in the exposed group (13% vs. 0%, p = 0.013).

The prevalence of hepatitis B was 6.4% in controls and 7.4% in exposed individuals, exceeding the regional average for Rio Grande do Sul, although without a significant difference between groups (p = 1.000). No cases of hepatitis C were detected.

Biochemical and hematological findings

Creatine kinase (CK) levels were significantly elevated in the exposed group (P < 0.001) (See Fig. 1). Even after adjustment for confounders, exposure remained associated with elevated CK levels, corresponding to a 128% higher prevalence of elevated CK among exposed individuals. AST and ALT (classical liver enzymes) did not differ between groups.

Butyrylcholinesterase (BChE) activity differed significantly between groups (p = 0.026), with higher values observed in exposed participants.

Urinary 2,4-D concentrations

Urinary 2,4-D concentrations were significantly higher in the exposed group compared to controls, both before and after creatinine adjustment (p < 0.001) (Table 2). Detectable levels (≥LOD, 0.03 μg/L) were found in 88.5% of exposed individuals and 25.0% of controls. Concentrations ≥LOQ (0.10 μg/L) were observed in 65.4% of exposed participants and 12.5% of controls.

Genotoxicity parameters

Genotoxicity analysis revealed significant differences between groups. The exposed group presented lower frequencies of binucleated cells (p = 0.025) and micronuclei (p < 0.001). In addition, relative telomere length was significantly shorter in exposed individuals compared to controls (p = 0.001) (See Fig. 4). Discussion

The sample of farmers exposed to 2,4-D was predominantly male, similar to the populations described by Alexander et al.31 and Bernieri.32 The exposed group was recruited from regions of southern Brazil characterized by intensive soybean cultivation, where pesticide use is widespread.

Regarding potential risk factors, BMI did not significantly differ between the exposed and control groups and was comparable to values reported for the Brazilian population in 2022.33 The prevalence of hypertension among organic farmers was similar to that reported for the Brazilian population in 2023 (27.9%), whereas a lower prevalence was observed among exposed individuals.34 Educational attainment differed between groups, with controls presenting higher levels of education, possibly reflecting their proximity to urban centers and educational institutions. These socioeconomic differences should be considered when interpreting lifestyle-related variables and potential confounding factors.

Alcohol consumption was assessed using the AUDIT questionnaire, one of the most widely used tools for evaluating alcohol use patterns.35 Hazardous alcohol use, according to ICD-11 definitions, has been associated with increased risk of chronic diseases, including liver-related conditions.35 Although alcohol consumption represents a potential confounder in studies involving oxidative stress and hepatic biomarkers, no clear differences between groups appeared sufficient to explain the observed findings.

Approximately 30% of exposed individuals reported not using personal protective equipment (PPE), despite recommendations for universal PPE use during pesticide application. Since dermal absorption, inhalation, and ingestion represent important exposure routes for 2,4-D,36 inadequate PPE use may contribute to increased occupational exposure. The persistence of biomarker alterations even among PPE users suggests that current protective practices may be insufficient or inconsistently applied, reinforcing the importance of preventive occupational health strategies.

An inverse association was observed between time since last exposure and urinary 2,4-D concentration (r = −0.37, p < 0.001), suggesting that more recent exposure is associated with higher urinary levels of 2,4-D, consistent with the relatively short biological half-life of this herbicide, which averages approximately 17.7 h.21 A positive trend was also observed between cultivated area and creatinine-adjusted 2,4-D concentration, indicating potentially greater exposure among individuals managing larger planting areas, although this association did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.089). Notably, nearly 40% of exposed participants reported 30 years or more of occupational pesticide exposure, with exposure often beginning in youth. This prolonged and early-life occupational exposure may contribute to cumulative biological effects and could partly explain the observed oxidative and telomeric alterations, potentially reflecting chronic exposure-related processes.

Higher CK levels were observed among exposed workers compared with controls. Elevated CK has been reported in cases of chlorophenoxy herbicide poisoning and may reflect musculoskeletal stress or tissue injury rather than liver dysfunction.37 Because CK is primarily considered a marker of muscle injury, the observed increase should be interpreted cautiously and may warrant further investigation as a biomarker associated with occupational agricultural exposure.

BChE levels were significantly higher in exposed individuals than in controls. Since 2,4-D does not inhibit cholinesterase activity, unlike organophosphate or carbamate pesticides,38 this finding differs from the expected pattern associated with cholinesterase-inhibiting compounds. However, agricultural workers are commonly exposed to multiple pesticide classes, and the elevated BChE levels observed may reflect co-exposure to other pesticides not assessed in this study. These findings should therefore be regarded as exploratory, and interpreted with caution.

Classical liver biomarkers (AST and ALT) did not significantly differ between exposed and control groups. Therefore, the present findings do not provide direct evidence of hepatocellular injury. However, altered oxidative stress biomarkers may reflect systemic biological responses associated with occupational pesticide exposure. Additional longitudinal studies incorporating repeated exposure measurements and complementary hepatic assessments are needed to clarify potential long-term implications.

To our knowledge, this study represents the first human biomonitoring investigation measuring urinary 2,4-D concentrations among Brazilian agricultural workers. Detectable urinary 2,4-D was detected in 88.5% of exposed individuals, a proportion higher than that reported by Alexander et al.31 and comparable to findings reported by Arbuckle et al.39

The detection of urinary 2,4-D in 25% of controls may reflect environmental contamination, pesticide drift, or indirect exposure, as previously reported in non-occupational populations.40 Such findings emphasize the widespread environmental presence of this herbicide beyond directly exposed workers.

The expected increase in 2,4-D use over the coming years raises concerns regarding population exposure and reinforces the need for continued biomonitoring efforts.41

Curiously, the control group presented higher frequencies of both micronucleated and binucleated cells. In the BMCyt assay, binucleated cells are considered indicators of cytokinesis and proliferative activity, and not direct genotoxicity biomarkers.26 Therefore, differences in the frequency of binucleated cells between the groups may reflect variations in epithelial turnover, cell proliferation, or cytotoxic responses. These findings should be interpreted with caution, especially considering the cross-sectional design of the study and the possibility that the dynamics of chronic exposure may influence the persistence or elimination of damaged cells.

Unexpectedly, micronucleus frequency was lower among exposed individuals than among controls. Similar variability has been reported in healthy Brazilian populations,42 suggesting that geographic, genetic, demographic, or lifestyle-related factors may contribute to these findings. Because urinary 2,4-D reflects recent exposure due to its relatively short biological half-life, transient genotoxic effects may not have been captured at the time of sampling.

The absence of micronuclei in some exposed individuals may indicate effective DNA repair mechanisms, interindividual variability, or limitations associated with exposure timing and biomarker detection. However, shorter telomere length was observed among exposed workers.

Telomere shortening may reflect cumulative biological stress or accelerated cellular aging associated with occupational exposure.20, 43 Since telomere length is influenced by multiple factors, including age, lifestyle, and chronic stress, these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Nevertheless, shortened telomeres have been associated with increased risk of age-related diseases and cancer.44

Experimental studies have demonstrated that exposure to 2,4-D may alter antioxidant defense systems and promote oxidative imbalance in dose-dependent models.12, 15, 16, 45 In the present study, exposed workers exhibited increased lipid peroxidation (TBARS), increased CAT activity, and reduced SOD activity, a pattern compatible with redox imbalance. Increased CAT activity may represent an adaptive response to elevated oxidative stress, whereas reduced SOD activity may indicate impairment of antioxidant defense mechanisms.46, 47, 48

Similar oxidative patterns have been seen in experimental in vivo studies with toxicant exposure. Increased lipid peroxidation and altered antioxidant enzyme activities were accompanied by inflammatory responses and organ pathology.49 Gunduz et al. showed that sodium fluoride exposure caused oxidative and inflammatory damage in rats. Antioxidant treatment reduced this damage, supporting links between oxidative imbalance and tissue injury.50 Samuel et al. found vincristine-induced organ pathology in Wistar rats was also linked to oxidative imbalance, with changes in antioxidant systems and lipid peroxidation.51 These studies support the idea that persistent oxidative imbalance may lead to cellular dysfunction after chronic toxicant exposure.

The combined profile of increased TBARS, increased CAT activity, and decreased SOD activity suggests oxidative imbalance among exposed workers.49 The concordance between experimentally reported oxidative mechanisms and the oxidative biomarker profile observed in the present study supports the biological plausibility of these findings. However, because agricultural workers are potentially exposed to multiple pesticides and the exposure assessment was based on a single urinary measurement, attribution of these oxidative alterations specifically to 2,4-D should be made cautiously.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, urinary 2,4-D was measured at a single time point and therefore primarily reflects recent exposure rather than cumulative chronic burden due to the relatively short biological half-life of the compound. Second, co-exposure to other pesticides commonly used in soybean cultivation was not quantified. Third, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference between occupational exposure and observed biomarkers. Finally, factors potentially influencing telomere length and oxidative stress, including age and lifestyle variables, should be considered in future analysis.

Future studies by our research group incorporating microbiota analyses, nutritional factors, and transient liver elastography may contribute to a broader understanding of chronic health outcomes associated with occupational pesticide exposure.

Repeated biomonitoring measurements combined with longitudinal designs may provide more robust evidence regarding chronic exposure patterns, seasonal variations, PPE effectiveness, and long-term biological effects associated with occupational pesticide exposure.

Conclusions

This study represents, to our knowledge, the first human biomonitoring investigation of urinary 2,4-D exposure among Brazilian soybean farmers and identified significantly higher urinary concentrations among occupationally exposed workers compared with controls.

Occupational exposure was associated with altered oxidative stress biomarkers, characterized by increased TBARS and CAT activity, reduced SOD activity, and shorter telomere length. These findings suggest biological alterations compatible with oxidative imbalance and genomic stress among exposed individuals.

The concordance between experimentally described oxidative mechanisms and the oxidative biomarker profile observed in the present study supports the biological plausibility of these findings. However, no significant differences were observed in classical liver enzymes (AST and ALT), indicating that the present results do not provide direct evidence of hepatocellular injury. Therefore, interpretations regarding hepatotoxicity should be made cautiously.

Because of the cross-sectional design, single-point assessment of urinary exposure, and potential co-exposure to multiple pesticides, causal relationships cannot be established, and findings should not be attributed exclusively to 2,4-D exposure.

Future longitudinal studies with repeated biomonitoring measurements, broader pesticide exposure assessment, and complementary clinical evaluations are needed to better characterize chronic health implications associated with occupational pesticide exposure.

These findings reinforce the importance of occupational health surveillance, biomonitoring programs, preventive strategies, and improved protective practices to reduce pesticide exposure among agricultural workers.
