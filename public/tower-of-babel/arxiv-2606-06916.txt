The

This formulation of the

•

The case

) equation, which is the most well-known member of this family. It was originally derived as a model for the unidirectional propagation of nonlinear dispersive long waves

•

For

KdV
(

\sigma=-1

•

Another important variant is the

[

The Cauchy problem for

30

. On the real line, Kenig-Ponce-Vega developed a systematic contraction-principle theory for

On the torus, the special integrable structure of

[

For the periodic monomial

. Bao-Wu obtained global well-posedness for periodic defocusing generalized

For equations with general analytic nonlinearity

The distinction between conditional and unconditional uniqueness is important for the present work. Many low-regularity well-posedness arguments construct solutions in auxiliary Bourgain-type resolution spaces. Such a theory gives solutions in
C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{s}
, but uniqueness may initially be known only inside the smaller resolution class. By contrast, an unconditional theorem asserts uniqueness directly in the natural class
C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{s}
, without imposing membership in an auxiliary space. This notion for dispersive PDEs was first introduced by Kato in the context of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
[
29
]
. See also
[
2
,
40
,
42
]
.

Our result goes in the opposite direction. We prove an unconditional nonuniqueness statement in a weak singular class: the zero solution and the solution constructed in Theorem
1.4
have the same initial data, and no auxiliary resolution space condition is imposed to distinguish them. Nonuniqueness occurs below the Lebesgue threshold at which the nonlinearity is classically meaningful. Indeed, if
u\in C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{k}
, then
u^{k}\in C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}
and the term
\partial_{x}(u^{k})
is an ordinary distribution. Below
C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{k}
, however, the product
u^{k}
need not be integrable and one must specify what it means to solve the equation. The weak singular formulation below does this through an absolute Fourier summability condition on the nonzero modes of
u^{k}
.

In the
KdV
case,
k=2
, the result is sharp in the Lebesgue scale: Theorem
1.4
gives nonuniqueness in every
C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{2-\epsilon}
, while at the
L^{2}
endpoint
[
2
]
shows unconditional uniqueness. For general
k\geq 3
, the construction reaches the Sobolev endpoint-minus scale

\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}-\epsilon}

which is the Sobolev counterpart of the Lebesgue threshold
L^{k}_{x}
.

1.2.
Overview of the Scheme

To achieve this nearly optimal Sobolev regularity (at least in the case of
KdV
) for the unconditional nonuniqueness, we employ a convex integration scheme with intermittent building blocks which are nearly fully intermittent. Intermittency has played a key role in many convex integration constructions; see for instance

[

,
[

,
[

and references therein. The point of this choice is so that the perturbations at level
q+1
, which we denote
w_{q+1}
, have order-one endpoint size, so that
w_{q+1}^{k}
can cancel the old error, while their subendpoint Lebesgue and Sobolev norms are summable. More precisely, the intermittency parameters
\epsilon_{q+1}=2^{-(q+1)}
tend to zero, so the perturbations approach the fully intermittent scaling

\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L^{p}_{x}}\sim\lambda_{q+1}^{\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p}},\qquad p<k,

up to a vanishing loss. At the same time, the frequency support of
w_{q+1}
is constructed to be nearly the natural frequency scale
\lambda_{q+1}^{1+\epsilon_{q+1}}
, which gives

\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}H^{\alpha}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{(1+\epsilon_{q+1})\alpha}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{2})}.

Hence the Sobolev increments are summable for every

\alpha<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}

and so the construction reaches the endpoint-minus Sobolev scale corresponding to the Lebesgue threshold
L^{k}_{x}
, while still keeping enough endpoint strength to cancel the error. In the
KdV

k=2
, this means that the nonuniqueness occurs below
L^{2}_{x}
, the endpoint where unconditional uniqueness is known.

The main new contribution introduced in this paper is to propose a very natural interpretation of the nonlinearity as an element of
C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}_{x}^{-s}
for some

as an element of

if the formal Fourier expansion is absolutely summable in this norm. Examining the expression this leads to, it is natural then to measure the errors generated by the scheme in what we refer to as

counterpart of Sobolev spaces. Sobolev norms had previously been used as the norm to drive the errors to

[

[

We mention here the result of Christ

Related open problems

[

[

cannot succeed in this dispersive setting. For the purposes of this heuristic discussion, let us assume that
w_{q+1}
is frequency localized to scale
\lambda_{q+1}\gg 1
. In these standard intermittent convex integration schemes one is required to estimate this term,
\partial_{xx}w_{q+1}
either in an
L^{p}
norm or a
C^{\alpha}
norm
[
10
]
.
Let us assume we work in the
L^{1}
setting, with parameters given by

\lambda_{q}=a^{b^{q}},\qquad a,b>1,\qquad\delta_{q}=\lambda_{q}^{-k\beta},\quad\beta>0.

Suppose moreover that the inductive hypotheses furnish the bounds

\|E_{q}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\delta_{q+1},\qquad\|w_{q}\|_{C^{0}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\delta_{q}^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{q}^{\frac{1}{k}-1}

Where
E_{q}
is the "error" that one wishes to drive to zero in the convex integration scheme. From the intermittent scaling and the frequency localization of
w_{q+1}
one has

\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{\alpha}\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{2}}

and thus we would have the solution
u
lies in
C_{t}^{0}H^{\alpha}_{x}
for
\alpha<\beta+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}
. This surpasses the regularity achieved in our scheme (see Theorem
1.4
) so long as
\beta>0
, but as we will demonstrate, this is impossible. To see this, we estimate the "dissipative error term" as

\|\partial_{xx}w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{2}\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{2}\,\delta_{q+1}^{\frac{1}{k}}\,\lambda_{q+1}^{\frac{1}{k}-1}.

For the inductive scheme to close, it is necessary that

\lambda_{q+1}^{2}\,\delta_{q+1}^{\frac{1}{k}}\,\lambda_{q+1}^{\frac{1}{k}-1}\lesssim\delta_{q+2}.

However, a comparison of the exponents shows that this requirement imposes the condition

\beta\leq\frac{k+1}{k(1-bk)},

which is strictly negative since
bk>k\geq 2
. This contradicts the assumption
\beta>0
, and hence the convex integration scheme cannot be closed under the given parameter regime.

Our scheme is successful so long as the nonlinearity
u^{k}\not\in C_{t}^{0}L^{1}_{x}
. If one wishes to push this scheme beyond this threshold, this would require one to use a "standard scheme" which as we’ve illustrated above cannot succeed. This leads one to conjecture that this natural threshold identified by the failure of the convex integration scheme is in fact the threshold for unconditional nonuniqueness vs. unconditional uniqueness. We formulate this conjecture below. More precisely, our considerations and the fact the result is known to be sharp for
KdV
indicate the possibility that the correct unconditional local well-posedness threshold for
k
-
gKdV
may be
s\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}

Conjecture 1.1
.

The
k
-generalized Korteweg-de Vries equation (
1.1
),
k\geq 3
, is unconditionally locally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for all
s\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}

Remark 1.6
.

We remark here that
k
-
gKdV
always has the following conserved quantities for sufficiently regular solutions:

Mass:

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(t,x)\,dx

Momentum:

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx

Energy:

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{k+1}\left(u(t,x)\right)^{k+1}\right)\,dx.

KdV

, the solutions we construct will lie in

, and thus the momentum is well-defined. However, this will not be a conserved quantity, as otherwise it would force all solutions which have identically zero initial data to remain identically zero for all time. Finally, for all

, the solutions we construct will lie in
C_{t}^{0}L^{1}_{x}
, and hence the mass is a well-defined quantity, and indeed it is also a conserved quantity. We prove this in Lemma
7.1
in the

. This explains why we insist on the solutions we construct having zero spatial mean for all time, it is in fact forced by the way we define a weak singular solution with identically zero intial data.

1.5.
Outline

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we set up the analytic
framework used in the iteration. This includes the Littlewood-Paley notation,
the weighted Wiener spaces in which the error is measured, and the intermittent
profiles which are used to generate large
k
-th powers while remaining small
in subcritical norms. We also record the elementary multiplier and convolution
estimates needed later in the proof.

Section
3
introduces the relaxed equation and describes one step of the convex integration scheme. Starting from a relaxed solution
(u_{q},E_{q})
, we add an intermittent perturbation
w_{q+1}
and decompose the new error into its oscillation, Nash, dispersion, and temporal parts.

Section
4
states the inductive proposition. This is the quantitative core of
the paper: it records the support, convergence, error, and absolute-summability
bounds propagated by the iteration.

Section
5
proves the inductive proposition. We construct the increment
w_{q+1}
and estimate the four errors produced by the iteration. We then verify the absolute Fourier summability of the nonlinear products and pass to the limit to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Section
6
contains further consequences and variants. We first discuss the stationary equation, where the endpoint
L^{k}
class is rigid but the
subendpoint classes remain flexible. We then explain why the same construction
also applies to the generalized Benjamin-Ono and to other translation-invariant
dispersive variants for which the linear term contributes only a controllable
dispersion error.

The
appendix
collects compatibility properties of the weak singular formulation.
First, we prove that weak singular solutions conserve their spatial mean. We
then compare the absolute Fourier summability condition with two more standard
ways of interpreting rough nonlinearities: Littlewood-Paley paraproduct
summation and Christ’s cutoff formulation. Finally, we show that whenever the
regularity is high enough to make the ordinary product
u^{k}
belong to
L^{1}
, the weak singular formulation implies the classical distributional weak formulation.

\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{j}\cap\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{j^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for all

, where
\varphi_{j}(\cdot)=\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)
. We define the projection of a function

on its

\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(f)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t,x)\,dx,

and the projection on the

\mathbb{P}_{2^{j},x}(f)(t,x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{f}(t,\xi)\varphi_{j}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\xi x}\,.

We also define the frequency cutoff by

\mathbb{P}_{\leq 2^{j},x}(f)=\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(f)+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\mathbb{P}_{2^{k},x}(f),

the projection onto high frequencies by
\mathbb{P}_{>2^{j},x}(f)=(\operatorname{Id}-\mathbb{P}_{\leq 2^{j},x})(f)
and the projection off the mean by
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0,x}f:=(\operatorname{Id}-\mathbb{P}_{=0,x})f
.
Denote by
m_{\leq 2^{j}}
the Fourier multiplier of
\mathbb{P}_{\leq 2^{j},x}
. Since
\varphi\geq 0
, the above definition gives

0\leq m_{\leq 2^{j}}(\xi)\leq 1

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.1.
Weighted Wiener Spaces

We next introduce the weighted Wiener spaces, which are Fourier-side
\ell^{1}
spaces with polynomial
weights. The main advantage of working with Wiener-type norms is that they
allow us to estimate oscillatory errors directly by summing their Fourier
coefficients, and in particular, negative weights are well suited for measuring
high-frequency errors.

Definition 2.6

(
(Homogeneous Weighted Wiener Space)
)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
we define the homogeneous Wiener space to be

\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=\left\{f:\sum_{\xi\not=0}|\xi|^{s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|<\infty\right\}.

Define

\|f\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{s}}=\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|.

It is clear
\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\cong\ell^{1}\bigl(\{\xi\neq 0\},|\xi|^{s}\bigr)
modulo constants, is a Banach Space.

To encode the time dependence, we propose the alternative space as follows.

Definition 2.7

(
Spacetime Homogeneous Weighted Wiener Space
)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, define the spacetime homogeneous weighted Wiener space as

\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{s}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})=\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}):\sum_{\xi\not=0}|\xi|^{s}\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}<\infty\right\}

and define

\|u\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{s}}=\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{s}\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}.

It is clear
\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{s}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})\cong\ell^{1}(\{\xi\neq 0\},|\xi|^{s};C^{0}([0,1]))
modulo
C^{0}([0,1])
-valued spatial constants is a Banach Space.

Remark 2.8
.

Note the spaces
\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{s}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})
and
C_{t}^{0}\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x}^{s}=C^{0}([0,1];\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{s}(\mathbb{T}))
equipped with norm

\|u\|_{C_{t}^{0}\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x}^{s}}=\underset{t\in[0,1]}{\sup}\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{s}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|

are different in nature. It is obvious that one has the continuous embedding
\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{s}\hookrightarrow C_{t}^{0}\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x}^{s}
. Note that the inclusion is strict.

Definition 2.9

(
Inhomogeneous Weighted Wiener Spaces
)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
we define the inhomogeneous Wiener space to be

\mathbb{A}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=\left\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}):\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|<\infty\right\}.

where

\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

Define

\|f\|_{\mathbb{A}^{s}}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|

Similarly, define the spacetime version as

\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})=\left\{u\in C_{t}^{0}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}):\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}<\infty\right\}

and define

\|u\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

As above,
\mathbb{A}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
is isometrically isomorphic to the
weighted sequence space

\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z},\langle\xi\rangle^{s})
, and

\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})
is isometrically isomorphic to

\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z},\langle\xi\rangle^{s};C^{0}([0,1]))
. Hence both spaces are
Banach spaces.

Lemma 2.10

(
Kato-Ponce-type product estimate for weighted Wiener Spaces
)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
with
\beta
having zero mean. Then for
s\geq 0
we have

\|\alpha\beta\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}}\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb{A}^{s}}\|\beta\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}}.

Moreover, for

\alpha\in\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}),\qquad\beta\in\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}),

and assume that
\beta(t,\cdot)
has zero spatial mean for every
t\in[0,1]
. Then

\|\alpha\beta\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}\lesssim_{s}\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}}\|\beta\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we show for spacetime version.
Since
\beta(t,\cdot)
has zero spatial mean, we have

\widehat{\beta}(t,0)=0\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,1].

Therefore, for every
\xi\neq 0
,

\widehat{\alpha\beta}(t,\xi)=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\alpha}(t,\xi-\eta)\widehat{\beta}(t,\eta)=\sum_{\eta\neq 0}\widehat{\alpha}(t,\xi-\eta)\widehat{\beta}(t,\eta).

Hence

\displaystyle\|\alpha\beta\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{-s}\left\|\widehat{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,\xi)\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{-s}\left\|\sum_{\eta\neq 0}\widehat{\alpha}(\cdot,\xi-\eta)\widehat{\beta}(\cdot,\eta)\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\sum_{\eta\neq 0}|\xi|^{-s}\|\widehat{\alpha}(\cdot,\xi-\eta)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\|\widehat{\beta}(\cdot,\eta)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}.

For
s\geq 0
and
\xi,\eta\neq 0
, we use

|\xi|^{-s}\lesssim_{s}|\eta|^{-s}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{s}.

Indeed,

|\eta|\leq|\xi|+|\xi-\eta|\leq|\xi|(1+|\xi-\eta|),

because
|\xi|\geq 1
. Therefore

|\xi|^{-s}\leq|\eta|^{-s}(1+|\xi-\eta|)^{s}\lesssim_{s}|\eta|^{-s}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{s}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|\alpha\beta\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\sum_{\eta\neq 0}|\eta|^{-s}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{s}\|\widehat{\alpha}(\cdot,\xi-\eta)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\|\widehat{\beta}(\cdot,\eta)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\neq 0}|\eta|^{-s}\|\widehat{\beta}(\cdot,\eta)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{s}\|\widehat{\alpha}(\cdot,\xi-\eta)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}.

For fixed
\eta
, setting
m=\xi-\eta
, we have

\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{s}\|\widehat{\alpha}(\cdot,\xi-\eta)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle m\rangle^{s}\|\widehat{\alpha}(\cdot,m)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}=\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\alpha\beta\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}}\sum_{\eta\neq 0}|\eta|^{-s}\|\widehat{\beta}(\cdot,\eta)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}}\|\beta\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}.

∎

2.2.
Intermittent Slabs

As mentioned previously, intermittency plays a crucial role in the construction. Roughly speaking, an intermittent function is one which is highly concentrated in space and whose
L^{p}
norms obey radically different estimates. For the same reasons discussed in
[
24
]
and
[
44
]
, we utilize a generic intermittent building block which we do not require to be a stationary solution of any PDE. However, unlike these schemes, one must utilize the maximum amount of intermittency in order to achieve the desired Sobolev estimates; this is more reminiscent of what is presented in
[
25
]
. The reason for this is clear: The intermittent slabs constructed in Lemma
2.11
will be
L^{k}
-normalized, and so for
k\geq 3
, to achieve the maximum
L^{2}
-based decay one must saturate Bernstein’s inequality. In the language of Lemma
2.11
, this means sending
\epsilon\to 0
. This provides "room" for a positive amount of fractional derivatives to be taken and maintain decay. However, when
k=2
, the intermittent slab is
L^{2}
normalized, so therefore any amount of fractional anti-derivatives will give decay. For this reason, in the
KdV
case, one may instead choose to utilize a vanishingly small amount of intermittency in order to achieve in some sense the ideal amount of Besov regularity allowed by the scheme. We will not pursue this here, but the scheme utilized in
[
44
]
can easily be adapted to the time dependent
KdV
setting.

Lemma 2.11

(
L^{k}
normalized intermittent slabs
)
.

Fix
k\geq 2
. Let
\lambda
be a large power of
2
and take
0<\epsilon<1
such that
\lambda^{\epsilon}
is also an integer. Then there exist smooth
\rho_{k,\lambda,\epsilon}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{k,\lambda,\epsilon}=0
and
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{k,\lambda,\epsilon}^{k}=1
;

(2)

\|\rho_{k,\lambda,\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\lambda^{(1-\epsilon)\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}
;

(3)

\rho_{k,\lambda,\epsilon}
is
\frac{\mathbb{T}}{\lambda^{\epsilon}}
-periodic.

Proof.

For fixed
k\geq 2
, choose a nonzero real-valued
\psi_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1/2,1/2)
with

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{k}=0.

Set

\widetilde{\psi}_{k}(x):=\psi_{k}(-x),\qquad\phi:=\psi_{k}\ast\widetilde{\psi}_{k}.

Then
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1,1)
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi=0,\qquad\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=|\widehat{\psi_{k}}(\xi)|^{2}\geq 0.

Since
\widehat{\phi}\geq 0
and
\phi\not\equiv 0
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{k}>0.

We normalize

\phi_{k}:=\frac{\phi}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{k}\right)^{1/k}}

Thus

\phi_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1,1),\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{k}=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{k}^{k}=1,\qquad\widehat{\phi_{k}}\geq 0.

Then define

(2.1)

\rho_{k,\lambda,\epsilon}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{(1-\epsilon)\frac{1}{k}}\phi_{k}(\lambda x+\lambda^{1-\epsilon}n)

Using the number of active Fourier coefficients is
\#\{n\mid|\lambda x+\lambda^{1-\epsilon}n|\lesssim 1\}\sim\lambda^{\epsilon}
, and support size
\lambda^{-1}
, one has

\displaystyle\|\rho_{k,\lambda,\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}\cdot\lambda^{(1-\epsilon)\frac{p}{k}}\cdot\lambda^{-1}

\displaystyle\|\rho_{k,\lambda,\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{(1-\epsilon)(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p})}

∎

3.
Convex Integration Scheme

We now describe the convex integration mechanism. The starting point is the relaxed version of (
1.1
),

(3.1)

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{xxx}u+\sigma\partial_{x}\left(u^{k}\right)=\partial_{x}E,

where
E:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is the error. The goal of the iteration is to construct smooth pairs
(u_{q},E_{q})
solving (
3.1
), while driving
E_{q}\to 0
in a weighted Wiener norm with a negative regularity index and keeping
u_{q}
convergent in the desired subcritical Sobolev spaces.

Assume that
(u_{q},E_{q})
is already a smooth solution of (
3.1
). Define

(3.2)

u_{q+1}=u_{q}+w_{q+1},

where
w_{q+1}
is a highly oscillatory intermittent perturbation with zero mean. This is chosen so that the mean of its
k^{th}
power cancels the old error,
E_{q}
, possibly with some remainder terms which are small in the weighted Wiener spaces.

More precisely, expanding the relaxed equation for
u_{q+1}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{x}E_{q+1}&=\partial_{t}u_{q+1}+\partial_{xxx}u_{q+1}+\sigma\partial_{x}\left(u_{q+1}^{k}\right)\\
&=\partial_{t}w_{q+1}+\partial_{x}\left(E_{q}+\sigma w_{q+1}^{k}\right)+\partial_{xxx}w_{q+1}+\sigma\partial_{x}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{n}w_{q+1}^{n}u_{q}^{k-n}\right)\end{split}

Since
w_{q+1}
has zero spatial mean, let
\omega_{q+1}
denote its mean-free antiderivative

\partial_{x}\omega_{q+1}=w_{q+1}.

We then define

(3.3)

E_{O}=E_{q}+\sigma w_{q+1}^{k},

(3.4)

E_{N}=\sigma\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{n}w_{q+1}^{n}u_{q}^{k-n}

(3.5)

E_{D}=\partial_{xx}w_{q+1}

and

(3.6)

E_{T}=\partial_{t}\omega_{q+1}.

Thus

(3.7)

E_{q+1}=E_{O}+E_{N}+E_{D}+E_{T}.

We refer to these four terms as the oscillation error, Nash error, dispersion error and temporal error, respectively.

The oscillation error is the main cancellation. We will use that

\sigma\mathbb{P}_{=0}(w_{q+1}^{k})=-E_{q}+R_{q+1}

where
R_{q+1}
is some remainder term which can be made arbitrarily small in
\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}_{x,t}
for some
s>0
independent of
q
by choosing the frequency of
w_{q+1}
large enough. Thus

E_{O}=R_{q+1}+\sigma\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(w_{q+1}^{k}).

and finally using that the minimum frequency of
\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(w_{q+1}^{k})
is very large, we will be able to show this term can be made arbitrarily small in
\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}_{x,t}
.

The remaining errors are perturbative. The Nash error contains lower powers of
w_{q+1}
, and is small because the intermittent slab is small in every
L^{p}
with
p<k
. The dispersion error is controlled in the negative weighted Wiener norm by the
C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}
norm of
w_{q+1}
, which is small. The temporal error is controlled by taking a mean-free antiderivative of
w_{q+1}
, which gains a factor of frequency, and we design
w_{q+1}
such that the loss from the time derivative is controlled by the
C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}
norm.

The induction below makes these estimates quantitative. It also keeps track of the absolute Fourier summability of the nonlinear products, which is needed in order to pass to the limit and obtain a weak singular solution in the sense of Definition
1.3
.

&=\left([a_{q}(t,x)]^{k}+Q_{1}(a_{q},\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}/2})\right)\left(\rho_{q+1}^{k}+Q_{2}(\rho_{q+1},\lambda^{1+\epsilon_{q+1}}_{q+1})\right)h_{q+1}^{k}(t)\end{split}

where

Q_{1}(a_{q},\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}/2})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}[a_{q}(t,x)]^{k-i}\left[\mathbb{P}_{>\mu_{q+1}^{1/2}}(a_{q}(t,x))\right]^{i}

and

Q_{2}(\rho_{q+1},\lambda_{q+1}^{1+\epsilon_{q+1}})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}[\rho_{q+1}(x)]^{k-i}\left[\mathbb{P}_{>\nu_{q+1}}(\rho_{q+1}(x))\right]^{i}.

We further rewrite

w_{q+1}^{k}=a^{k}_{q}(t,x)\rho_{q+1}^{k}(x)h_{q+1}^{k}(t)+\mathsf{R}_{q+1}

where

\mathsf{R}_{q+1}=[\rho_{q+1}^{k}Q_{1}+a_{q}^{k}Q_{2}+Q_{1}Q_{2}]h_{q+1}^{k}.

Using
a_{q}(t,x):=(A_{q+1}-\sigma E_{q}(t,x))^{1/k}
so that

\sigma a_{q}^{k}=\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q}

One may write

\displaystyle E_{q}+\sigma w_{q+1}^{k}

\displaystyle=E_{q}+(\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q})\rho_{q+1}^{k}(x)h_{q+1}^{k}(t)+\sigma\mathsf{R}_{q+1}

Now decompose

\displaystyle\rho_{q+1}^{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{=0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k})+\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k})

Using the normalization as in Lemma
2.11

\mathbb{P}_{=0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{q+1}^{k}dx=1

and that
h_{q+1}\equiv 1
on
\mathrm{supp}_{t}E_{q}
, one obtain

E_{q}h_{q+1}^{k}=E_{q}

Therefore

\displaystyle E_{q}+(\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q})\rho_{q+1}^{k}(x)h_{q+1}^{k}(t)

\displaystyle=E_{q}+(\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q})(1+\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k}))h_{q+1}^{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma A_{q+1}h_{q+1}^{k}+(\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q})\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k})h_{q+1}^{k}

The term
\sigma A_{q+1}h_{q+1}^{k}
is independent of
x
so it vanishes under the
{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}
norm.

Consequently

\|E_{O}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}\leq\|(\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q})\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k})h_{q+1}^{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}+\|\mathsf{R}_{q+1}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

We first analyze the principal oscillatory term. Via Lemma
2.10

\displaystyle\|(\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q})\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k})h_{q+1}^{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q})h_{q+1}^{k}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}}\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k})\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}}

Since
\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q}
depends only on
\lambda_{q+1}
-independent quantities, one obtains

\|(\sigma A_{q+1}-E_{q})h_{q+1}^{k}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{s}}\lesssim_{q}1

Now in view of
(
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k}(x))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\neq 0}\hat{g}_{k}(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}n)e^{2\pi i\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}nx}

for some
g_{k}
as in Lemma
2.12
. Now in the weighted Wiener norm one obtains

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k}(x))\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\neq 0}|\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}n|^{-s}|\hat{g}_{k}(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}n)|=\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}\sum_{n\neq 0}|n|^{-s}|\hat{g}_{k}(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}n)|

Since
\hat{g}_{k}
is Schwartz, then

|\hat{g}_{k}(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}n)|\lesssim 1

Thus taking
s>1
so that
\sum_{n\neq 0}|n|^{-s}
converges,
we have

(5.11)

\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(\rho_{q+1}^{k}(x))\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}<2^{-(q+1)-200-8}

for any fixed
s>1
, by choosing
\lambda_{q+1}
sufficiently large.

Now it suffices to bound
\mathsf{R}_{q+1}
. Since the torus
\mathbb{T}
has finite measure, for any
s>1

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{R}_{q+1}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathsf{R}_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\rho_{q+1}^{k}Q_{1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}+\|a_{q}^{k}Q_{2}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}+\|Q_{1}Q_{2}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}

It suffices to estimate the high-frequency tails in view of

\displaystyle\|Q_{1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|a_{q}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}^{k-i}\|\mathbb{P}_{>\mu_{q+1}^{1/2}}(a_{q})\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}^{i}\lesssim\|\mathbb{P}_{>\mu_{q+1}^{1/2}}(a_{q})\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\|Q_{2}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\rho_{q+1}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{k-i}\|\mathbb{P}_{>\nu_{q+1}}(\rho_{q+1})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{i}\lesssim\|\rho_{q+1}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{k-1}\|\mathbb{P}_{>\nu_{q+1}}(\rho_{q+1})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\frac{k-1}{k}}\|\mathbb{P}_{>\nu_{q+1}}(\rho_{q+1})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

For the tail frequencies, using Lemma
2.13
for every integer
M\geq 1

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{>\mu_{q+1}^{1/2}}(a_{q})\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M}\lambda_{q+1}^{-M\epsilon_{q+1}/2}\|a_{q}\|_{C_{t}^{0}C_{x}^{M}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{-M\epsilon_{q+1}/2}

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{>\nu_{q+1}}(\rho_{q+1})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M}\lambda_{q+1}^{\frac{1-\epsilon_{q+1}}{k}-M\epsilon_{q+1}}

Now

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{R}_{q+1}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{1-\epsilon_{q+1}-\frac{M\epsilon_{q+1}}{2}}+\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})-M\epsilon_{q+1}}+\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})-\frac{3}{2}M\epsilon_{q+1}}

We may choose
M=M_{q+1}
sufficiently large so that all the three exponents are negative. After this choice of
M
, we may choose
\lambda_{q+1}
sufficiently large so that the total remainder is as small as required at stage
q+1
. Combining both estimates yields the desired estimate

(5.12)

\|E_{O}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}<2^{-(q+1)-200-2}.

Temporal Error

Recall

E_{T}=\partial_{t}\omega_{q+1}

where
\omega_{q+1}
is the unique mean-free antiderivative of
w_{q+1}
, i.e.

\partial_{x}\omega_{q+1}=w_{q+1},\qquad\hat{\omega}_{q+1}(t,0)=0

And so

\hat{E}_{T}(t,\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi i\xi}\partial_{t}\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\xi).

Thus

(5.13)

\displaystyle\|E_{T}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{-s}\|\hat{E}_{T}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\lesssim\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{-s-1}\|\partial_{t}\hat{w}_{q+1}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

Now we have

\|\partial_{t}\hat{w}_{q+1}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\leq\|\partial_{t}w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}},

and so from (
5.13
) we obtain

\|E_{T}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}\sum_{|\xi|\gtrsim\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}}|\xi|^{-s-1}.

For
s>0
the series converges, thus

(5.14)

\|E_{T}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}

It remains to estimate
\partial_{t}w_{q+1}
in
C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}
. Differentiating in time gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{q+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\leq\mu_{q+1}}(\partial_{t}a_{q})\,\mathbb{P}_{\leq\nu_{q+1}}(\rho_{q+1})\,h_{q+1}+\mathbb{P}_{\leq\mu_{q+1}^{1/2}}(a_{q})\,\mathbb{P}_{\leq\nu_{q+1}}(\rho_{q+1})\,h_{q+1}^{\prime}.

Using Lemma
2.3
, Hölder’s inequality, and the condition
0\leq h_{q+1}\leq 1
, we obtain

\|\partial_{t}w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim\Bigl(\|\partial_{t}a_{q}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}+\|a_{q}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|h_{q+1}^{\prime}\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\Bigr)\|\rho_{q+1}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}.

Now
\|\partial_{t}a_{q}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}
and
\|a_{q}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{\infty}}
are
\lambda_{q+1}
-independent constants and so we may estimate them by
1
. By construction for the fixed parameter

we have

\|h_{q+1}^{\prime}\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\lesssim\lambda^{\beta}_{q+1}.

Finally, Lemma
2.11
gives

\|\rho_{q+1}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{1}{k}-1\right)}=\lambda_{q+1}^{-\frac{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(k-1)}{k}}.

Thus

\|\partial_{t}w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim_{q}(1+\lambda^{\beta}_{q+1})\lambda_{q+1}^{-\frac{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(k-1)}{k}}\lesssim_{q}\lambda_{q+1}^{\beta-\frac{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(k-1)}{k}}.

Therefore substituting into (
5.14
) yields

\displaystyle\|E_{T}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{\beta-\frac{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(k-1)}{k}}

Since
\epsilon_{q+1}=2^{-(q+1)}
and
\beta<\frac{1}{16}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)
, one may choose
\lambda_{q+1}
large enough so that

(5.15)

\|E_{T}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}<2^{-(q+1)-200-2}.

Finally combining (
5.9
), (
5.10
), (
5.12
), and (
5.15
) we deduce

\|E_{q+1}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}<2^{-(q+1)-200}

which closes the induction.

First from (
5.2
) we have the Taylor expansion

\mathbb{P}_{\leq\mu_{q+1}^{1/2}}\left(a_{q}(t,x)\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/k}{n}A_{q+1}^{1/k-n}\sigma^{n}\mathbb{P}_{\leq\mu_{q+1}^{1/2}}\left(E_{q}^{n}\right)

and from Lemma

\mathbb{P}_{\leq\nu_{q+1}}\left(\rho_{q+1}\right)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{1}{k}-1\right)}\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi\right)m_{\leq\mu_{q+1}}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi x}r

Lemma 5.3
.

We have that

\displaystyle AF(w_{q+1},j)\lesssim_{q,j,s}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-1}\|E_{q}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{\mathbb{A}}}^{-s}_{x,t}}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-1}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-2}.

\begin{split}&\|\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\eta_{1})\cdots\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\eta_{j})\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\\

(5.17)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\eta_{1}+\ldots+\eta_{j}\not=0}\sum_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{1}{k}-1\right)}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{1}\right)\cdots\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{j}\right)\right|A_{q+1}^{j/k}\\

\begin{split}|\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\ldots+\eta_{j}|&=\left|\eta_{1}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{1}+\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\left(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\ldots+\xi_{j}\right)\right|\\
&\geq\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\left|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}\right|-|\eta_{1}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{1}|\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\left|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}\right|\\
&\simeq\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\left|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}\right|\end{split}

Hence again applying the same convolution estimate we have

(5.20)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\eta_{1}+\ldots+\eta_{j}\not=0}\sum_{\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}\not=0}\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{1}{k}-1\right)}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{1}\right)\cdots\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{j}\right)\right|A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-1}\\
&\times\left\|\hat{E}_{q}(t,\eta_{1}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{1})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\delta(\eta_{2}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{2})\cdots\delta(\eta_{j}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{j})|\eta_{1}+\ldots+\eta_{j}|^{-s}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}\not=0}\sum_{\eta_{1}\not=\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{1}}\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{1}{k}-1\right)-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-1}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{1}\right)\cdots\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{j}\right)\right|\\
&\times\left\|\hat{E}_{q}(t,\eta_{1}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{1})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|^{-s}\\
&\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{j}{k}-1\right)-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-1}\left(\sum_{\mu\not=0}\left\|\hat{E}_{q}(t,\mu)\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\right)\\
&\lesssim\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{j}{k}-1\right)-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-1}\end{split}

Case 4:

n_{1}+n_{2}+\ldots+n_{j}\geq 2
.
Since
h_{q+1}\equiv 1
on
\operatorname{supp}_{t}E_{q}
, we have

E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}=E_{q}^{n_{i}}\qquad\text{whenever }n_{i}\geq 1,

while for
n_{i}=0
,

\left(E_{q}^{0}h_{q+1}\right)^{\wedge}(t,\mu)=\delta_{\mu,0}h_{q+1}(t).

Define

C_{q}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\hat{E}_{q}(t,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}=\|E_{q}\|_{\mathbb{A}^{0}_{x,t}}.

This quantity is finite due to the compact frequency support of
E_{q}
discussed earlier and is clearly independent of
q+1
. Now using the algebra property of
\mathbb{A}^{0}_{x,t}
, we have

\|E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}<\|E_{q}^{n_{i}}\|_{\mathbb{A}^{0}_{x,t}}\lesssim C_{q}^{n_{i}}

for every
n_{i}\geq 0
.

Fix
n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}\geq 0
with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}\geq 2
. We split according to whether
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}=0
, or not.

If
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}=0
, then

\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}\right).

After the change of variables
\mu_{i}=\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}\neq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|\left(E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}\right)^{\wedge}\left(t,\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}\right)\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|(E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1})^{\wedge}(t,\mu_{i})\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\lesssim_{q}C_{q}^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}.

Therefore, using the same convolution estimate as in Case 2,

(5.21)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}=0}\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\\
&\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{i}\right)\right|C_{q}^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}\\
&\lesssim_{q,j,s}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}\lambda_{q+1}^{(j-1)(1-\epsilon_{q+1})}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}C_{q}^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}\\
&=\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}}\left(\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\right)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}.\end{split}

Now suppose
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}\neq 0
. Then, on the support of the frequency cutoff factors appearing in
\hat{w}_{q+1}
, each summand vanishes unless
\left|\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}\right|\lesssim\mu_{q+1}^{1/2}
for

. Hence,

(5.22)

|\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}|\geq\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}|-\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}|\xi+\cdots+\xi_{j}|

provided
\lambda_{q+1}
is sufficiently large. Therefore

|\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}|^{-s}\lesssim_{s}\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}|^{-s}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\|(E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1})^{\wedge}(t,\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i})\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{j,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}|^{-s}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{\eta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|(E^{n_{i}}_{q}h_{q+1})^{\wedge}(t,\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i})\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q,j,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}|^{-s}C^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}_{q}.

Using the same convolution estimate as in Case 1, we obtain

(5.23)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}\neq 0}\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\\
&\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{i}\right)\right|\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}|^{-s}C_{q}^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}\\
&\lesssim_{q,j,s}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)-s\epsilon_{q+1}}\lambda_{q+1}^{(j-1)(1-\epsilon_{q+1})}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}C_{q}^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}\\
&=\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}}\left(\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\right)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}\\
&\leq\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}}\left(\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\right)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}.\end{split}

Combining the two subcases, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}\neq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\sum_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{i}\right)\right|\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|\left(E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}\right)^{\wedge}\left(t,\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}\right)\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q,j,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\left(\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\right)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}.

Choosing
A_{q+1}
sufficiently large so that

(5.24)

\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\leq\frac{1}{2},

the absolute convergence of the binomial series gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\left(\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\right)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}\lesssim_{j,k}\left(\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\right)^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}\neq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\sum_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{i}\right)\right|\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|\left(E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}\right)^{\wedge}\left(t,\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}\right)\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q,j,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-2}.

Combining Cases 1-4, we obtain

\displaystyle AF(w_{q+1},j)\lesssim_{q,j,s}

\displaystyle\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}^{j/k}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-1}\|E_{q}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}_{x}C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-1}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-2}.

Since
A_{q+1}\geq 1
,
\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}\leq 1
, and

\|E_{q}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{0}}
is a
\lambda_{q+1}
-independent constant, we conclude

AF(w_{q+1},j)\lesssim_{q,j,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{j/k}.

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
1\leq j\leq k
, we have

ZF(w_{q+1},j)\lesssim_{q,j}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{j/k}.

Proof.

Recall that

ZF(w_{q+1},j)=\sum_{\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}=0}\left\|\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\eta_{1})\cdots\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\eta_{j})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}.

Using the same expansion as in the previous lemma, we have

\displaystyle ZF(w_{q+1},j)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}=0}\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}

\displaystyle\times\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{i}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|\left(E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}\right)^{\wedge}\left(t,\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}\right)\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}.

We first record the stage-
q
bound used below. Since
h_{q+1}\equiv 1
on

\operatorname{supp}_{t}E_{q}
, we have

E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}=E_{q}^{n_{i}}\qquad\text{whenever }n_{i}\geq 1,

while for
n_{i}=0
,

\left(E_{q}^{0}h_{q+1}\right)^{\wedge}(t,\mu)=\delta(\mu)h_{q+1}(t).

Recall that we had chosen

C_{q}=\|E_{q}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x,t}^{0}}.

and thus

\|E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}\|_{{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{0}}\lesssim C_{q}^{n_{i}}

for every
n_{i}\geq 0
, with an implicit constant independent of
n_{i}
.

Fix
n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}\geq 0
. For fixed
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{j}
, make the change of variables

\mu_{i}=\eta_{i}-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}\xi_{i}.

The constraint
\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}=0
becomes

\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j})+\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{j}=0.

Therefore the contribution of this fixed choice of
n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|\left(E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}\right)^{\wedge}(t,\mu_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j})+\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{j}=0\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{i}\right)\right|.

We claim that, uniformly in
\Xi\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j}=\Xi}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{i}\right)\right|\lesssim_{j}\lambda_{q+1}^{(j-1)(1-\epsilon_{q+1})}.

Indeed, writing

\xi_{j}=\Xi-\xi_{1}-\cdots-\xi_{j-1},

we get for
\lambda_{q+1}
chosen large enough

(5.25)

\begin{split}&\lambda_{q+1}^{(j-1)(\epsilon_{q+1}-1)}\sum_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{j-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{i}\right)\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\Xi-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{1}-\cdots-\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}-1}\xi_{j-1}\right)\right|\\
&\lesssim_{j}\sup_{Y\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{j-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}|\hat{\phi}_{k}(y_{i})|\left|\hat{\phi}_{k}\left(Y-y_{1}-\cdots-y_{j-1}\right)\right|\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{j-1}\\
&\lesssim_{j}\|\hat{\phi}_{k}\|_{L^{1}}^{j-1}\|\hat{\phi}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{j}1.\end{split}

Multiplying by
\lambda_{q+1}^{(j-1)(1-\epsilon_{q+1})}
gives the claim.

Now fix
\mu_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\mu_{j}
. The constraint

\lambda_{q+1}^{\epsilon_{q+1}}(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{j})+\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{j}=0

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\\

Therefore the contribution of this fixed choice of
n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\lambda_{q+1}^{j(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}\lambda_{q+1}^{(j-1)(1-\epsilon_{q+1})}A_{q+1}^{\frac{j}{k}-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{j}\|E_{q}^{n_{i}}h_{q+1}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{x}^{0}C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q,j}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{j/k}\left(\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\right)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}.

Summing over
n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle ZF(w_{q+1},j)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q,j}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{j/k}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\left|\binom{1/k}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{1/k}{n_{j}}\right|\left(\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\right)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}.

Recall from (
5.24
) we had chosen
A_{q+1}
large enough so that

\frac{C_{q}}{A_{q+1}}\leq\frac{1}{2},

and hence the binomial series converges absolutely. Therefore

ZF(w_{q+1},j)\lesssim_{q,j}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{j}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{j/k}.

∎

We also record the exact coefficient of the

-term which appears in the pure

We now return to (
5.16
). Fix

. We split the mixed sum according to whether

\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}=0

or not.

First suppose

\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}\neq 0.

Using Petree’s inequality

with

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0\\
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{i})\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}\\
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\xi_{m+1},\ldots,\xi_{k}}\left\|\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\left\langle\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\right\rangle^{s}

\displaystyle=AF(w_{q+1},m)\sum_{\xi_{m+1},\ldots,\xi_{k}}\left\|\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\left\langle\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\right\rangle^{s}.

is a fixed smooth stage-

, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0\\
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{i})\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}\lesssim_{q,m,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{m}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{m/k}.

Since the full output frequency satisfies

we must have

\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0.

Therefore

|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}=|\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0\\
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}=0\end{subarray}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{i})\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}=0}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\left\|\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}

\displaystyle=ZF(w_{q+1},m)\sum_{\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\left\|\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}.

Again, the final sum is bounded by a stage-
q
constant. Therefore, by Lemma
5.4
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0\\
\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}=0\end{subarray}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{i})\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q,m,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{m}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{m/k}.

Combining the two subcases, for each
1\leq m\leq k-1
,

(5.28)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\hat{w}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{i})\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\hat{u}_{q}(t,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q,m,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{m}{k}-1)}A_{q+1}^{m/k}.

Since
m\leq k-1
, the exponent

(1-\epsilon_{q+1})\left(\frac{m}{k}-1\right)

is strictly negative. Thus, after
A_{q+1}
has been fixed, we choose
\lambda_{q+1}
sufficiently large so that the total mixed contribution in (
5.16
) is bounded by
2^{-q-103}
.

It remains to estimate the pure
w_{q+1}
contribution. For this term, we use the first estimate in Lemma
5.3
with
j=k
, keeping the
E_{q}
-coefficient from (
5.26
) explicit. This gives

\displaystyle AF(w_{q+1},k)\leq

\displaystyle K_{q+1}\|E_{q}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle+C_{q,k,s}\left(\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}+\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}+A_{q+1}^{-1}\right).

By the inductive hypothesis for the error, and (
5.27
),

K_{q+1}\|E_{q}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}\leq 2^{-q-200}.

Now we choose
A_{q+1}
sufficiently large so that

(5.29)

\frac{C_{q,k,s}}{A_{q+1}}\leq 2^{-q-104}.

After
A_{q+1}
is fixed, choose
\lambda_{q+1}
sufficiently large so that

(5.30)

C_{q,k,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}A_{q+1}+C_{q,k,s}\lambda_{q+1}^{-s\epsilon_{q+1}}\leq 2^{-q-104}.

Hence

AF(w_{q+1},k)\leq 2^{-q-103}.

Combining these estimates in (
5.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\left\|\hat{u}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{1})\cdots\hat{u}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{k})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}

\displaystyle\leq C-2^{-q-100}+2^{-q-103}+2^{-q-103}.

Since

2^{-q-103}+2^{-q-103}=2^{-q-102}<2^{-q-101}=2^{-q-100}-2^{-(q+1)-100},

we conclude

\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\left\|\hat{u}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{1})\cdots\hat{u}_{q+1}(t,\xi_{k})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}<C-2^{-(q+1)-100}.

This proves Item
5
at level
q+1
.

6.
Further Results

In this section we briefly indicate some other PDEs where the methods we have presented to this point will apply
mutatis mutandis
.

6.1.
Stationary
gKdV

In
[
44
]
the the third author considered the stationary
KdV
equation and proved both a flexibility and rigidity theorem. Our methods in this paper improve on this result in two regards. First, we may replace the paraproduct notion for defining the nonlinearity which was employed in
[
44
]
by a stationary analogue of Definition
1.2
. We will in fact prove the notion for defining the nonlinearity in Definition
1.2
is stronger than this "paraproduct notion" in Lemma
7.3
in the
appendix
. Second, and more obviously, we are able to treat stationary
gKdV
instead of being confined to
KdV
.

Theorem 6.1

(
Rigidity for stationary
gKdV
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer and let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a\neq 0
.
Suppose that
u\in L^{k}(\mathbb{T})
is real-valued and is a weak solution of the
stationary generalized
KdV
equation

a\,u^{(3)}+b\,(u^{k})^{\prime}=0

in the sense that

-a\int_{\mathbb{T}}u\,\psi^{(3)}\,dx-b\int_{\mathbb{T}}u^{k}\,\psi^{\prime}\,dx=0

for every
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. Then
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proof.

Since
u\in L^{k}(\mathbb{T})
, we have
u^{k}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, so the weak formulation is
an ordinary distributional identity. It says precisely that

a\,u^{(3)}+b\,(u^{k})^{\prime}=0

in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
, or equivalently

\bigl(a\,u^{\prime\prime}+b\,u^{k}\bigr)^{\prime}=0.

Hence

(6.1)

a\,u^{\prime\prime}+b\,u^{k}=C

in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
for some constant
C\in\mathbb{R}
.

Since
u^{k}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, (
6.1
) gives

u^{\prime\prime}=\frac{C-bu^{k}}{a}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}).

Together with
u\in L^{k}(\mathbb{T})\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, standard one-dimensional
periodic elliptic regularity yields

u\in W^{2,1}(\mathbb{T}).

Therefore, by the Sobolev embedding
W^{2,1}(\mathbb{T})\subset C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}).

Consequently
u^{k}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
. Returning to
(
6.1
), we obtain

u^{\prime\prime}=\frac{C-bu^{k}}{a}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}),

and hence
u\in C^{3}(\mathbb{T})
.

Now bootstrap. If
u\in C^{m}(\mathbb{T})
, then
u^{k}\in C^{m}(\mathbb{T})
, so
(
6.1
) gives
u^{\prime\prime}\in C^{m}(\mathbb{T})
, and therefore

u\in C^{m+2}(\mathbb{T})
. Starting from
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, this induction gives
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
∎

Theorem 6.2

(
Flexibility for stationary
gKdV
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer, and let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with

ab\neq 0.

For every
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists a real-valued distribution
u
on

\mathbb{T}
such that

u\in L^{k-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})\setminus L^{k}(\mathbb{T}),

the product
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})
is well-defined by the absolute
summability criterion of Definition
1.2
, with the obvious modifications for the stationary setting, as an element of

\dot{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{T})
for all sufficiently large
s
, and
u
solves

a\,u^{(3)}+b\,(u^{k})^{\prime}=0

in the weak singular sense

-a\langle u,\psi^{(3)}\rangle-b\left\langle\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k}),\psi^{\prime}\right\rangle=0

for every
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. Moreover, when
k\geq 3
, the construction
may be arranged so that

u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T}).

Proof.

The proof is an obvious modification of the arguments presented.
∎

6.2.
The generalized Benjamin-Ono equation

The same convex integration scheme also applies to the generalized
Benjamin-Ono (
gBO
) equation in the formulation used by Molinet-Tanaka in
[
40
]
. Namely, if
\mathcal{H}
denotes the periodic Hilbert transform,

\widehat{\mathcal{H}f}(\xi)=-i\,{\rm sgn}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi),\qquad{\rm sgn}(0)=0,

then the
k
-generalized Benjamin-Ono equation (
k
-
gBO
) is

(6.2)

\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{H}\partial_{xx}u+\sigma\partial_{x}(u^{k})=0.

Equivalently, this is the case
L_{2}=-\mathcal{H}\partial_{xx}
and

f(u)=\sigma u^{k}
in the generalized dispersive framework of
[
40
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is insensitive to this replacement of the
linear dispersive operator. Indeed, in the relaxed equation for
gBO
one writes

\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{H}\partial_{xx}u+\sigma\partial_{x}(u^{k})=\partial_{x}E.

The initial relaxed solution is defined with this linear operator in place of

\partial_{xxx}
. After that harmless change, the inductive step is the same as in the proof Theorem
1.4
. After setting
u_{q+1}=u_{q}+w_{q+1}
, the only change in the error decomposition is the dispersion error. For
gKdV
we used

\partial_{xxx}w_{q+1}=\partial_{x}\bigl(\partial_{xx}w_{q+1}\bigr),\qquad E_{D}=\partial_{xx}w_{q+1}.

For
gBO
we instead have

-\mathcal{H}\partial_{xx}w_{q+1}=\partial_{x}\bigl(-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}w_{q+1}\bigr),

since
\mathcal{H}
commutes with
\partial_{x}
. Thus the dispersion error is
replaced by

E_{D}^{\rm BO}:=-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}w_{q+1}.

Moreover, the estimate of the dispersion error is easier in the
gBO
case. The
Hilbert transform has Fourier multiplier of modulus at most one, and therefore
is bounded on the Fourier absolute spaces used in the scheme:

\|\mathcal{H}f\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|E_{D}^{\rm BO}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle=\|-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}w_{q+1}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{1-s}\|\hat{w}_{q+1}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}.

Using

\|\hat{w}_{q+1}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}\leq\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}},

we obtain, for
s>2
,

\displaystyle\|E_{D}^{\rm BO}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}\sum_{\xi\not=0}|\xi|^{1-s}\lesssim\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}.

By Lemma
5.2
,

\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim_{q}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}.

Therefore

\|E_{D}^{\rm BO}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}\lesssim_{q}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)}.

Choosing
\lambda_{q+1}
sufficiently large at each stage makes this error as small as required. This is strictly better than the corresponding
gKdV
dispersion estimate, where
\partial_{xxx}
produces the error
\partial_{xx}w_{q+1}
and hence one extra power of frequency.

Consequently the same construction gives nonunique weak singular solutions to (
6.2
). In particular, there exists a nontrivial solution to (
6.2
), in the corresponding weak singular sense, with zero initial data, such that

u\in\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{k-\epsilon}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})

When
k\geq 3
, the same Sobolev refinement as in Theorem
1.4

also gives

u\in\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}-\epsilon}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}).

Here the weak formulation is obtained from
Definition
1.3
by replacing the test-function term

\partial_{xxx}\phi
with
-\mathcal{H}\partial_{xx}\phi
.

6.3.
Other dispersive variants

The construction is not tied to the precise Airy dispersion

\partial_{xxx}
or the dispersion term appearing in
gBO
. The same argument applies to any translation-invariant
linear dispersive operator
\mathcal{L}
whose symbol can be divided by the
spatial derivative with at most polynomial loss.

More precisely, suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a skew-adjoint Fourier
multiplier of the form

\widehat{\mathcal{L}f}(\xi)=i\ell(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi),

where
\ell:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
is odd,
\ell(0)=0
, and for some

r\geq 0
,

\left|\frac{\ell(\xi)}{\xi}\right|\lesssim|\xi|^{r},\qquad\xi\neq 0.

Equivalently, on nonzero frequencies,

\mathcal{L}=\partial_{x}\mathcal{M},

where
\mathcal{M}
is a real Fourier multiplier of order at most
r
. Then the convex integration scheme for

\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u+\sigma\partial_{x}(u^{k})=0

is unchanged except for the dispersion error.

Indeed, the relaxed equation becomes

\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u+\sigma\partial_{x}(u^{k})=\partial_{x}E.

After setting
u_{q+1}=u_{q}+w_{q+1}
, the oscillation, Nash, and temporal
errors are exactly the same as in the proof of Theorem
1.4
. The only
replacement is

E_{D}=\mathcal{M}w_{q+1}

instead of
E_{D}=\partial_{xx}w_{q+1}
. We obtain, for
s>r+1
,

\displaystyle\|E_{D}\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x,t}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\xi\neq 0}|\xi|^{-s}\left|\frac{\ell(\xi)}{\xi}\right|\|\hat{w}_{q+1}(\cdot,\xi)\|_{C_{t}^{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}\sum_{\xi\not=0}|\xi|^{r-s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}.

Since

\|w_{q+1}\|_{C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim_{q}\lambda_{q+1}^{(1-\epsilon_{q+1})(\frac{1}{k}-1)},

Choosing
\lambda_{q+1}
sufficiently large at each stage makes this error as small as required. Thus, the induction closes exactly as before.

This includes the generalized dispersive class considered by Molinet-Tanaka

[
40
]
, where the linear operator
L_{\alpha+1}
has Fourier
symbol
-ip_{\alpha+1}(\xi)
with

p_{\alpha+1}^{\prime}(\xi)\sim\xi^{\alpha},\qquad p_{\alpha+1}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\sim\xi^{\alpha-1},\qquad 1\leq\alpha\leq 2.

In that case

p_{\alpha+1}(\xi)\sim|\xi|^{\alpha}\xi,

so after division by
\partial_{x}
the dispersion error has order

r=\alpha\leq 2
. Therefore the estimate above is no worse than the
gKdV

dispersion estimate. The endpoint
\alpha=2
gives
gKdV
, while the endpoint

\alpha=1
gives generalized Benjamin-Ono from Subsection
6.2
.
The same observation also covers mixed constant-coefficient dispersions such as

\partial_{t}u+\bigl(c_{3}\partial_{xxx}-c_{2}\mathcal{H}\partial_{xx}+c_{1}\partial_{x}\bigr)u+\sigma\partial_{x}(u^{k})=0,

and, with a larger choice of
s
, higher-order dispersive models such as

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{5}u+\sigma\partial_{x}(u^{k})=0.

In every case, the nonlinear part of the construction is unchanged; only the
order of the dispersion error changes.

We first record the elementary fact that weak singular solutions conserve their
spatial mean.

Lemma 7.1

(Conservation of the spatial mean)
.

Let
u\in C_{t}^{0}L_{x}^{1}
be a weak singular solution of

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{xxx}u+\sigma\partial_{x}(u^{k})=0

on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}
in the sense of Definition
1.3
,
with initial datum
u_{0}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{0}(x)\,dx\qquad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].

Equivalently,

\hat{u}(t,0)=\hat{u}_{0}(0)\qquad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].

Proof.

Recall

\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(t,x)\,dx=\langle u(t),1\rangle_{H^{s},H^{-s}}.

Since
u\in C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{s}
and
1\in H^{-s}(\mathbb{T})
, the function

\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)
belongs to
C^{0}([0,1])
.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,1))
, and use the spatially constant test
function

\phi(t,x):=\chi(t).

Then

\partial_{xxx}\phi=0,\qquad\partial_{x}\phi=0.

Therefore the dispersive term and the nonlinear term vanish in the weak
formulation. Hence Definition
1.3
gives

\int_{0}^{1}\langle u(t),\chi^{\prime}(t)\rangle_{H^{s},H^{-s}}\,dt=-\langle u_{0},\chi(0)\rangle_{H^{s},H^{-s}}.

Since
\chi(t)
is spatially constant, this is exactly

\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(t)\chi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=-\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(0)\chi(0).

On the other hand,

\int_{0}^{1}\chi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=-\chi(0),

because
\chi
vanishes near
t=1
. Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(t)-\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(0)\right)\chi^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(t)\chi^{\prime}(t)\,dt-\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(0)\int_{0}^{1}\chi^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(0)\chi(0)-\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(0)(-\chi(0))

\displaystyle=0.

Thus the distributional derivative of
\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(t)-\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(0)
vanishes on
(0,1)
.
Hence
\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(t)-\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(0)
is constant on
(0,1)
. Since
\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)
is continuous, the constant is zero, and hence

\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(t)=\mathbb{P}_{=0,x}(u)(0)\qquad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].

This proves the claim.
∎

.

In the special case of Theorem
1.4
, the initial data is

u_{0}=0
. Therefore Lemma
7.1
gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(t,x)\,dx=0\qquad\text{for every }t\in[0,1]

for the solution we construct.

Next we show absolute Fourier summability implies the paraproduct summability criterion utilized in
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
24
]
, and
[
25
]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
.
Assume

\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}|\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})|\cdots|\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k})||\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{\alpha}<\infty.

Assume moreover that the Littlewood-Paley resolution reconstructs every
input frequency which contributes to the above sum. Equivalently, either each

f_{i}
is mean-zero and the homogeneous resolution satisfies

\sum_{j}\varphi_{j}(\xi)=1\qquad\text{for every }\xi\neq 0,

or the resolution includes the zero mode and satisfies

\sum_{j}\varphi_{j}(\xi)=1\qquad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then the paraproduct expansion

\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr)

converges absolutely in the homogeneous weighted Wiener space

\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})=\left\{g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}):\|g\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}}:=\sum_{\eta\neq 0}|\eta|^{\alpha}|\hat{g}(\eta)|<\infty\right\}.

In particular it converges absolutely in
\dot{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
. Moreover its
sum agrees with the absolutely defined Fourier product

\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(f_{1}\cdots f_{k}):=\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})\cdots\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k})e^{2\pi i(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k})x}.

Proof.

Recall from Definition
2.1
,
\varphi_{j}
denotes the Fourier multiplier of
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}
. We use the finite overlap
property

\sum_{j}|\varphi_{j}(\xi)|\leq 2

for every frequency
\xi
covered by the resolution.

Fix
J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})
. For
\eta\neq 0
, the
\eta
-Fourier coefficient
of

\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr)

is

\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}=\eta}\varphi_{j_{1}}(\xi_{1})\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})\cdots\varphi_{j_{k}}(\xi_{k})\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k}).

Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr)\right\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}|\varphi_{j_{1}}(\xi_{1})|\cdots|\varphi_{j_{k}}(\xi_{k})||\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})|\cdots|\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k})||\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{\alpha}.

Summing over
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}
and applying Tonelli’s theorem gives

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr)\right\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}|\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})|\cdots|\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k})||\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{\alpha}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{j_{\ell}}|\varphi_{j_{\ell}}(\xi_{\ell})|\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2^{k}\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}|\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})|\cdots|\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k})||\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{\alpha}<\infty.

Thus the paraproduct series converges absolutely in
\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}
.

Since

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr)\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\eta\neq 0}|\eta|^{2\alpha}\left|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr)^{\wedge}(\eta)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\eta\neq 0}|\eta|^{\alpha}\left|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr)^{\wedge}(\eta)\right|

\displaystyle=\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr)\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}},

the same series also converges absolutely in
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
.

It remains to identify the limit. Let
I_{N}
be an increasing sequence of
finite index sets exhausting the Littlewood-Paley resolution, and define

S_{N}:=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\in I_{N}}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{1}}}f_{1}\cdots\mathbb{P}_{2^{j_{k}}}f_{k}\Bigr).

For
\eta\neq 0
,

\hat{S}_{N}(\eta)=\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}=\eta}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{j_{\ell}\in I_{N}}\varphi_{j_{\ell}}(\xi_{\ell})\right)\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})\cdots\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k}).

For every contributing input frequency, the reconstruction assumption gives

\sum_{j}\varphi_{j}(\xi)=1.

Also,

\left|\sum_{j\in I_{N}}\varphi_{j}(\xi)\right|\leq\sum_{j}|\varphi_{j}(\xi)|\leq 2.

Therefore, by dominated convergence and the absolute summability assumption,

\hat{S}_{N}(\eta)\to\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}=\eta}\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})\cdots\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k})

for every
\eta\neq 0
. Moreover, the same majorant gives convergence in

\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}
. Indeed,

\|S_{N}-\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(f_{1}\cdots f_{k})\|_{\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}}\to 0

by dominated convergence applied to the nonnegative summable majorant

2^{k}|\hat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})|\cdots|\hat{f}_{k}(\xi_{k})||\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{\alpha}.

Hence the paraproduct sum agrees with the absolutely defined Fourier product.
∎

We now compare our absolute Fourier product with Christ’s cutoff formulation. We use Christ’s terminology from
[
14
, Definitions 2.1-2.3]
: a Fourier cutoff sequence is a sequence of finite-support Fourier multipliers
P_{N}
whose multipliers are uniformly bounded and converge pointwise to
1
; a nonlinear expression exists in the cutoff sense if the corresponding regularized nonlinearities converge in distributions for every such sequence.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
u\in C_{t}^{0}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})
, let
k\geq 2
, and let
s>0
. Assume

\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\left\|\hat{u}(\cdot,\xi_{1})\cdots\hat{u}(\cdot,\xi_{k})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}<\infty.

Define
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})
by

\left(\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})\right)^{\wedge}(t,\eta)=\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}=\eta}\hat{u}(t,\xi_{1})\cdots\hat{u}(t,\xi_{k}),\qquad\eta\neq 0,

and set its zero Fourier mode equal to
0
. Then

\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})\in C_{t}^{0}\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}_{x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}^{-s}_{x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}).

Moreover, for every Fourier cutoff sequence
P_{N}
in the sense of
[
14
, Definition 2.1]
,

\partial_{x}\bigl((P_{N}u)^{k}\bigr)\longrightarrow\partial_{x}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})\qquad\text{in }C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}^{-s-1}_{x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}).

Consequently,
\partial_{x}(u^{k})
exists in Christ’s cutoff sense and equals

\partial_{x}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})

in the sense of distributions. In particular, if

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{xxx}u+\sigma\partial_{x}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})=0

in
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{0}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})
, then
u
is a solution in Christ’s extended sense with nonlinear term given by
\partial_{x}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})
.

Proof.

The absolute summability assumption gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})\|_{C_{t}^{0}\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\left\|\hat{u}(\cdot,\xi_{1})\cdots\hat{u}(\cdot,\xi_{k})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}<\infty.

The embedding into
C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}^{-s}_{x}
follows from
\ell^{1}\hookrightarrow\ell^{2}
.
Let
P_{N}
be a Fourier cutoff sequence in Christ’s sense with multiplier
m_{N}
. For
\eta\neq 0
,

\left(\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}((P_{N}u)^{k})\right)^{\wedge}(t,\eta)=\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}=\eta}\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{N}(\xi_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\hat{u}(t,\xi_{i}).

Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}((P_{N}u)^{k})-\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}\dot{\mathbb{A}}_{x}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}\neq 0}\left|\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{N}(\xi_{i})-1\right|\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{k}\hat{u}(\cdot,\xi_{i})\right\|_{C_{t}^{0}}|\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{k}|^{-s}.

For each fixed tuple
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k})
, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{N}(\xi_{i})\to 1.

Since the multipliers
m_{N}
are uniformly bounded, the summand is dominated by a constant multiple of the absolutely summable series above. Thus dominated convergence gives

\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}((P_{N}u)^{k})\to\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})\qquad\text{in }C_{t}^{0}\dot{\mathbb{A}}^{-s}_{x}\subset C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}^{-s}_{x}.

Finally,
\partial_{x}
annihilates the zero spatial mode, so

\partial_{x}((P_{N}u)^{k})=\partial_{x}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}((P_{N}u)^{k}).

Applying

\partial_{x}:C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}^{-s}\to C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}_{x}^{-s-1}

to the convergence above gives

\partial_{x}((P_{N}u)^{k})\to\partial_{x}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(u^{k})\qquad\text{in }C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}_{x}^{-s-1}.

This is precisely the existence of the nonlinearity in Christ’s cutoff sense, and the final assertion follows from
[
14
, Definition 2.3]
.
∎
