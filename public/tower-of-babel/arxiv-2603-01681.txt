A Monotone Operator Approach to Separable Mean-Field Games with Mixed Boundary Conditions
AbdulRahman M. Alharbi, Diogo Gomes

Abstract
We study a class of local, first-order, stationary mean-field games (MFGs) on bounded domains with nonstandard mixed boundary conditions: prescribed inflow on Γ N \Gamma_{N} and a relaxed Signorini-type exit condition on Γ D \Gamma_{D} (complementarity between exit flux and boundary value). For separable Hamiltonians, we overcome the lack of coercivity and the boundary complementarity constraints by introducing a monotone operator on a convex domain, augmented with an auxiliary nonnegative boundary variable h h encoding exit flux. To address a constant-shift degeneracy in the value function u u (the transport equation depends only on D ​ u Du ), we employ a quotient-space formulation that restores coercivity. Using the Browder–Minty theorem, we prove existence for a penalized operator A ϵ A_{\epsilon} on a convex domain and pass to the limit as ϵ → 0 + \epsilon\to 0^{+} . We obtain weak solutions ( m , u , h ) (m,u,h) solving the associated variational inequality, with m ∈ L β + 1 ​ ( Ω ) m\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega) , u ∈ W 1 , γ ​ ( Ω ) u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega) , and h h in the dual trace space on Γ D \Gamma_{D} .

Introduction

Mean Field Games (MFGs) provide a rigorous framework for analyzing strategic interactions in systems comprising a continuum of small players.
Independently introduced by Huang, Malhamé, and Caines
[
23
,
24
]
and by Lasry and Lions
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, the theory characterizes equilibria via coupled systems of partial differential equations: a Hamilton–Jacobi (HJ) equation governing individual optimal control, and a transport equation governing the population distribution. These models have been applied in crowd dynamics
[
2
,
34
,
21
]
, economics
[
1
]
, and finance
[
12
,
5
]
.

Variational techniques apply to first-order separable MFG systems
[
11
,
10
]
. Monotone operator methods extend this framework to low-regularity regimes: the monotonicity of the coupling corresponds to convexity of the associated functional, yielding existence, uniqueness, and stability
[
16
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
19
,
18
]
. This structure admits monotone-operator discretizations

[
6
,
22
,
32
]
. More recently, displacement-type or “hidden” monotonicity has been identified for non-separable Hamiltonians and master equations, extending the theory beyond classical separable settings
[
20
,
31
,
8
,
9
]
.
We establish an existence theory for local, stationary first-order separable MFGs subject to mixed inflow/Signorini boundary conditions. Our approach centers on an augmented monotone-operator formulation on a convex cone; when paired with a quotient-space reduction to restore coercivity in
u
, this framework provides a systematic operator-theoretic alternative to the variational methods developed in
[
3
]
.

1.1
The Model Problem

We analyze the operator associated with the following separable MFG system.

MFG System 1

(
The Separable MFG):

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open, bounded, and connected domain with a
C^{1}
boundary, denoted by
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
.
Throughout,
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal on
\Gamma
, and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
denotes the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
We assume that
\Gamma
is partitioned into two relatively open,
C^{1}

(d-1)
-dimensional manifolds: a Dirichlet boundary
\Gamma_{D}
and a Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}
such that

\Gamma=\overline{\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}},\quad\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset,\quad\text{and }\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial\Gamma_{D}\cap\partial\Gamma_{N})=0,

and assume that
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
are both nonempty (hence of positive
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-measure).
Additionally, let

H:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R},\quad g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R},\quad\text{and }\enskip j:\Gamma_{N}\to[0,\infty)

be given functions such that the map
p\mapsto H(x,p)
is convex,
g
is strictly increasing, and
j\not\equiv 0
. The MFG we study is given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}H(x,Du)=g(m)&\text{in }\Omega,\\
-\Div(m\,D_{p}H(x,Du))=0&\text{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

subject to the boundary conditions

(1.2)

\begin{cases}m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu=j(x)&\text{on }\Gamma_{N},\\
m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu\leq 0,\quad u(x)\leq 0&\text{on }\Gamma_{D},\\
u\,m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu=0&\text{on }\Gamma_{D}.\end{cases}

We call (
1.1
)
separable
since the coupling enters additively:
\mathcal{H}(x,p,m)=H(x,p)-g(m)
. For the boundary conditions, the population flux is
J=-mD_{p}H(x,Du)
.
On
\Gamma_{N}
, the condition
mD_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu=j
prescribes an inflow of magnitude
j
(since
J\cdot\nu=-j<0
).
On
\Gamma_{D}
, setting
h:=J\cdot\nu=-m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu
, the conditions take the Signorini form:
u\leq 0
,
h\geq 0
, and
uh=0
. This complementarity structure, reminiscent of the thin obstacle problem
[
33
]
and the boundary obstacle problem
[
7
]
, means the natural weak formulation is a variational inequality rather than a classical boundary-value problem.

The foundation for studying MFGs with such conditions was established in
[
3
]
, where a monotone MFG operator akin to that in
[
16
]
was derived. However, the boundary complementarity forces the natural operator domain to be non-convex, which prevents the application of Browder–Minty-type existence theory. We overcome this by introducing an augmented operator with an auxiliary boundary flux variable, proving existence on a convex set, and recovering coercivity via a quotient-space reduction.
While
[
3
]
derives the operator structure and establishes existence via a variational approach, here we obtain existence (and the
\epsilon\to 0
limit) by convexifying the operator domain through the auxiliary boundary flux variable.

1.2
Methodology and Operators

We construct a MFG operator on a convex domain that naturally incorporates the boundary conditions. We achieve this by introducing an auxiliary variable
h\geq 0
on
\Gamma_{D}
encoding the exit flux
h=J\cdot\nu
.
This allows us to encode the Signorini complementarity through a variational inequality on a convex cone, while keeping the operator domain convex.

Let
\gamma>1
be a key growth exponent (see Section
2
), and let
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma/(\gamma-1)
be its Hölder conjugate. We consider triplets
(m,u,h)
in a product of Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces. We define the limit operator
A_{0}
and a penalized version
A_{\epsilon}
(for
\epsilon>0
) as follows:

(1.3)

A_{0}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}-H(x,Du)+g(m)\\
-\Div\big(m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\big)+\big(m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma}-j\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{N}}+h\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}\big)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
-u\,\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\end{bmatrix},

and

(1.4)

A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}-H(x,Du)+g(m)\\
-\Div\big(m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\big)+\big(m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma}-j\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{N}}+h\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}\big)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
\big(-u+\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}\,h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}\big)\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\end{bmatrix},

where
\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{S}
denotes the characteristic function of a set
S
.
These expressions are formal; the rigorous definitions via duality and trace-space pairings are given in Section
3
.

In earlier work (e.g.
[
16
,
14
,
18
]
) the loss of coercivity in MFG operators was typically handled by adding a penalization term. In the present setting, the obstruction is linked to the mixed boundary complementarity on
\Gamma_{D}
and the constant-shift invariance of the transport equation;
since
the HJ and transport equations depend only on
Du
and the boundary terms depend only on fluxes, the shift
u\mapsto u+c
is invisible once
\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j
. So, coercivity must be restored on
W^{1,\gamma}/\mathbb{R}
.
Accordingly, we employ a boundary-driven regularization and a reduction strategy:

1.

We define the operator on a convex cone of nonnegative densities and fluxes.

2.

The boundary-only penalty (the
\epsilon
-term in (
1.4
)) restores coercivity in
(m,h)
for fixed
u
, allowing us to solve for
(m_{u},h_{u})
explicitly.

3.

Substituting these back into the system produces a reduced monotone operator on the quotient space
W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, which removes the constant-shift degeneracy and restores coercivity in
u
.

Our existence proof relies on applying the Browder–Minty theorem to this reduced operator, followed by passing to the limit as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

1.3
Main Results

To state our results precisely, we introduce the functional setting.
The assumptions governing the exponents
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are stated in Assumption
D.1
, while structural conditions on
H
and
g
are given in Assumptions
S
.

Let
\mathcal{X}:=L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
, and let
\mathcal{X}^{+}\subset\mathcal{X}
denote the convex cone of triplets with nonnegative density and boundary flux:

\mathcal{X}^{+}:=\left\{(m,u,h)\in\mathcal{X}:m\geq 0\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\text{ and }h\geq 0\text{ a.e. on }\Gamma_{D}\right\}.

Our first main result establishes the existence of solutions for the penalized operator.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
and
S
hold, and let
\epsilon>0
. Then, there exists a triplet
(m_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon},h_{\epsilon})\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
such that

\left\langle A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m_{\epsilon}\\
u_{\epsilon}\\
h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m_{\epsilon}\\
v-u_{\epsilon}\\
k-h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0

for all
(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
.

Our second result establishes the existence of a solution to the variational inequality associated with the limit operator
A_{0}
. In this limit, the boundary flux
h
relaxes to a distribution in the dual trace space
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
.
Here we identify
k\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
with its canonical embedding into
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
when forming
k-h
, so that the duality pairing is well-defined.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
and
S
hold. Then, there exists a triplet

(m,u,h)\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)\times W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})

such that
m\geq 0
,
h
is a nonnegative distribution, and

\left\langle A_{0}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m\\
v-u\\
k-h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}^{+}.

Theorem
1.2
is obtained by solving the penalized variational inequalities for
A_{\epsilon}
using Theorem

1.1
and passing to the limit as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce our notation and assumptions, and review preliminary results including the Browder–Minty theorem and auxiliary estimates. In Section
3
, we motivate the penalized MFG operator via a variational principle and rigorously define its functional framework. In Section
4
, we establish the fundamental properties of the operator and detail its lack of global coercivity. In Section
5
, we address this failure of coercivity via a quotient-space formulation and establish Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in Section
6
, we pass to the vanishing penalty limit to establish Theorem
1.2
.

Notation, Assumptions, and Preliminary Results

In this section, we establish the functional and technical framework necessary for the analysis of the Mean-Field Game operator. We begin by defining the notation and conventions used throughout the paper. Next, we specify the exponent regime and structural conditions on
H
and
g
—requirements that ensure the operator
A_{\epsilon}
is well-defined, monotone, and coercive enough to satisfy the hypotheses of the Browder–Minty theorem. These conditions, specifically the polynomial growth assumptions, are standard in the variational MFG literature (see, e.g.,
[
11
]
). Finally, we establish several auxiliary bounds on the Hamiltonian and its gradient that are essential for the continuity and convergence arguments in the subsequent sections.

2.1
Notation

Before outlining the main assumptions, we discuss our notation and conventions throughout the paper.

•

Differentiation:
Any differential operator without a subscript, such as
Du
or
\Div F
, denotes differentiation with respect to the spatial variable
x
. Differentiation with respect to any other variable is explicitly indicated (e.g.,
D_{p}H(x,p)
).

•

Constants:
An inequality involving a constant
C>0
is understood to hold for some sufficiently large
C
, independent of all free variables; dependencies on parameters are indicated by subscripts (e.g.,
C_{\delta}
). The value of
C
may change from line to line.

•

Positive/Negative Parts:
For any quantity

, we denote
q_{+}:=\max\{q,0\}
and
q_{-}:=\max\{-q,0\}
.

•

Conjugates:
We denote the Hölder conjugate of

q^{\prime}:=q/(q-1)
.

•

Extended Inverse:

Throughout,
g^{-1}
denotes the
extended inverse
introduced in Definition

(not the usual inverse on the range of

).

•

Surface Measure:

On the boundary

{\,\,\rm d}s
for the surface measure

; thus

\int_{\Gamma}f\,{\,\,\rm d}s:=\int_{\Gamma}f\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
.
For measurable

, we also write
|E|:=\int_{E}1\,{\,\,\rm d}s=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(E)
.

•

Traces:

For

interchangeably for its trace on

for the restriction to

•

Boundary integrals and normal traces:

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, boundary integrals

are understood using the trace
Tu
.
When we write
F\cdot\nu
for a vector field

, we mean the

in
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
(when it is well-defined, e.g. if
F\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

•

Duality:

The pairing between a Banach space

and its dual

is denoted

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
for the trace-space duality pairing.

•

Convergence:
We write

(resp.

2.2
Assumptions

The assumptions detailed here address the problem data and the separable structure of the Hamiltonian, ensuring the well-posedness of MFG System
1
and the underlying control problem.

2.2.1
The problem data

We begin with our assumptions on the basic parameters and data. We denote these assumptions by
D
(for “data”).

Assumptions D
:

D.1

Our assumptions are formulated in terms of two key exponents:

(2.1)

\alpha>1\,\text{ and }\,\beta>0.

To simplify our presentation, we additionally introduce an auxiliary exponent:

(2.2)

\gamma:=\dfrac{\beta+1}{\beta}\alpha.

Note that
\gamma>\alpha>1
since
\beta>0
and
\alpha>1
. This choice balances the growth of the coupling
g(m)
and the Hamiltonian
H(Du)
in the energy estimates.

D.2

The incoming flow
j\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{N})
is nonnegative and not identically zero; that is,

j\geq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad j\not\equiv 0.

We recall that
\Gamma_{N}\subset\partial\Omega
denotes the (Neumann/inflow) boundary portion where the incoming flux is prescribed. The integrability condition in the preceding assumption is required for the weak formulation of the transport equation, ensuring the boundary source term is well-defined.

2.2.2
The Hamiltonian

The following assumptions, denoted by
\mathbf{S}
(for “separable”), govern the regularity and growth of the Hamiltonian
H
and the coupling term
g
. These conditions are essential for the well-posedness of MFG System
1
and distinguish our framework from that of a companion paper on nonseparable MFGs
[
4
]
.

Assumptions S
:

S.0

a
.

The coupling term
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

b
.

The Hamiltonian
H:\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable in
x
and
H(x,\cdot)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for almost every
x\in\Omega
.

This regularity guarantees that the terms in the Hamilton-Jacobi and transport equations are well-defined.

S.1

The coupling term
g
is strictly increasing.

This assumption represents players’ aversion to congestion
and ensures that
g^{-1}
exists (see Definition
2.9
).

S.2

The function
H
is convex in
p
.

This convexity is used twice: to (i) obtain monotonicity of the
H
-terms and (ii) control
mD_{p}H
via growth/coercivity estimates.

S.3

There exists a constant
C>1
such that, for all
m\in[0,\infty)

C^{-1}m^{\beta}-C\leq g(m)\leq Cm^{\beta}+C.

The lower bound establishes the coercivity of the coupling term, while the upper bound controls its growth rate. Both properties are essential for the continuity and coercivity arguments used in the analysis of the MFG operator.

S.4

There is a constant
C>1
such that

a
.

|D_{p}H(x,p)|\leq C|p|^{\alpha-1}+C
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

b
.

H(x,0)\leq C
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.

This assumption provides an upper bound on the growth of the Hamiltonian and its derivative (see Remark
2.1
). This control is essential for two reasons: it guarantees that the underlying optimal control problem is well-defined, and it is a key ingredient in the proof of continuity for the MFG operator.

Remark 2.1
.

Due to the fundamental theorem of calculus, for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, Assumption
S.4
gives the following bound on the Hamiltonian

H(x,p)\leq H(x,0)+\int_{0}^{1}|D_{p}H(x,tp)||p|{\,\,\rm d}t\leq C|p|^{\alpha}+C.

The following coercivity assumption is stated in three forms; Proposition
2.2
shows they are equivalent under Assumptions
S.2
and
S.4
.

S.5

There exists a constant
C>1
such that, for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one of
the following conditions holds.

a
.

H(x,p)\geq C^{-1}|p|^{\alpha}-C
.

b
.

D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot p\geq C^{-1}|p|^{\alpha}-{C}
and
H(x,p)\geq-C
.

c
.

-H(x,p)+D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot p\geq C^{-1}|p|^{\alpha}-C
and
H(x,p)\geq-C
.

Forms
S.5a
and
S.5b
establish growth bounds for the operator analysis.

Proposition 2.2
.

Under Assumptions
S.2
and
S.4
, the three forms of
S.5
are equivalent.

Proof
.

We show that
\text{\ref{assume:S.5.a}}\Rightarrow\text{\ref{assume:S.5.b}}\Rightarrow\text{\ref{assume:S.5.c}}\Rightarrow\text{\ref{assume:S.5.a}}
.

(a
\Rightarrow
b)
. By the convexity of
H
in
p
(
S.2
), we have that

H(x,0)\geq H(x,p)+D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot(0-p).

Rearranging gives

D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot p\geq H(x,p)-H(x,0).

Using
S.5a
for the lower bound on
H(x,p)
and
S.4
.b for the upper bound on
H(x,0)
, we get

D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot p\geq(C^{-1}|p|^{\alpha}-C)-C=C^{-1}|p|^{\alpha}-2C.

After adjusting the constant
C
, this gives the first inequality in
S.5b
. The second,
H(x,p)\geq-C
, is an immediate consequence of
S.5a
.

(b
\Rightarrow
c)
.
Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
. By convexity (
S.2
),

H(x,p)-H(x,\sigma p)\leq(1-\sigma)D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot p.

We rearrange this to get

-H(x,p)+D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot p\geq\sigma D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot p-H(x,\sigma p).

To the right-hand side of this inequality,
we apply the lower bound from
S.5b
to the first term and the upper bound from Remark
2.1
(derived from
S.4
) to the second:

-H(x,p)+D_{p}H(x,p)\cdot p\geq\sigma(C^{-1}|p|^{\alpha}-C)-(C|\sigma p|^{\alpha}+C)=(\sigma C^{-1}-C\sigma^{\alpha})|p|^{\alpha}-C(\sigma+1).

We can choose
\sigma>0
small enough such that the coefficient
(\sigma C^{-1}-C\sigma^{\alpha})
is positive. Adjusting the constants then yields the required inequality in
S.5c
. The condition
H(x,p)\geq-C
is given directly by
S.5b
.

(c
\Rightarrow
a)
. We write
H(x,p)=H(x,p/2)+\int_{1/2}^{1}D_{p}H(x,tp)\cdot p{\,\,\rm d}t
. Rearranging the integrand and applying

gives

\displaystyle H(x,p)

\displaystyle=H(x,p/2)+\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{1}{t}\left(D_{p}H(x,tp)\cdot(tp)-H(x,tp)\right){\,\,\rm d}t+\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{1}{t}H(x,tp){\,\,\rm d}t

\displaystyle\geq-C+\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{1}{t}\big(C^{-1}|tp|^{\alpha}-C\big){\,\,\rm d}t+\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{1}{t}(-C){\,\,\rm d}t

\displaystyle\geq-C-2C\log(2)+C^{-1}|p|^{\alpha}\int_{1/2}^{1}t^{\alpha-1}{\,\,\rm d}t=C^{-1}\frac{1-2^{-\alpha}}{\alpha}|p|^{\alpha}-C^{\prime}.

Adjusting constants gives
S.5a
.
∎

The convexity of
H
in
p
(Assumption
S.2
) and its superlinear growth (Assumption
S.5
) ensure that the Legendre–Fenchel transform is well-defined. This allows us to introduce the Lagrangian
L(x,v)
as the convex conjugate of
H(x,\cdot)
:

L(x,v)=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[-p\cdot v-H(x,p)\right].

Under these conditions,
L(x,v)
is a proper, lower semi-continuous, and convex function that is also coercive in
v
. Furthermore, the involution property of the conjugate holds, dually recovering the Hamiltonian:

H(x,p)=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[-v\cdot p-L(x,v)\right].

Form
S.5c
of Assumption
S.5
corresponds to the coercivity of the Lagrangian
L(x,v^{*})=-H+p\cdot D_{p}H
.

Example.

A prototype Hamiltonian for which the assumptions hold is

H(x,p)=(|p|^{2}+1)^{\alpha/2}+1,

with the following coupling term

g(m)=(m^{2}+1)^{\beta/2}.

Here
g(0)=1
, so
g^{-1}(z)=0
for
z<1
.
For
\alpha
and
\beta
as in Assumption
D.1
,
these functions satisfy the growth, convexity, and coercivity conditions detailed in Assumptions
S
.

2.3
Monotone Operators and the Browder–Minty Theorem

Following
[
26
]
,
we recall the Browder–Minty theorem for variational inequalities. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a reflexive Banach space and let
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
be its topological dual, with the duality pairing denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. We begin by introducing the primary notions needed to apply the theorem: monotonicity, coercivity, and continuity on finite-dimensional subspaces.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty, closed, and convex set. A mapping
A:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is
monotone
if

\langle Au-Av,\;u-v\rangle\;\geq\;0\quad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathcal{K}.

It is
strictly monotone
if the above inequality is strict for
u\neq v
; that is,

\langle Au-Av,\;u-v\rangle\;=\;0\;\;\implies\;\;u=v.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty, closed, and convex set. A mapping
A:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is
continuous on finite-dimensional subspaces
if, for every finite-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{X}
, the restriction

A\bigl|_{\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{M}}:\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{M}\;\to\;\mathcal{X}^{\prime}

is weakly continuous; that is, whenever
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{M}
(equivalently in
\mathcal{M}
),
we have
Au_{n}\rightharpoonup Au
in
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
(with respect to the weak topology
\sigma(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{X})
).

Remark 2.5
.

A mapping
A:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is
hemicontinuous
if for all
u,v\in\mathcal{K}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}
, the function

f(\theta)=\langle A((1-\theta)u+\theta v),\varphi\rangle

is continuous for
\theta\in[0,1]
.
For monotone mappings, hemicontinuity is equivalent to continuity on finite-dimensional subspaces in the sense of Definition
2.4
; see, e.g.,
[
25
]
.

We recall the Browder–Minty theorem.

Lemma 2.6

(​​
[
26
]
, Theorem 1.7)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty, closed, bounded, and convex subset, and let
A:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}

be monotone and continuous on finite-dimensional subspaces.
Then, there exists
u\in\mathcal{K}
such that

\langle Au,v-u\rangle\geq 0\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{K}.

To extend the Browder–Minty theorem to an unbounded domain
\mathcal{K}
, the theorem requires an additional coercivity assumption. We define this notion as follows.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be nonempty, closed, and convex. A mapping
A:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is
coercive
if there is
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}
for which

\frac{\langle Au-A\varphi,u-\varphi\rangle}{\|u-\varphi\|_{\mathcal{X}}}\to+\infty\quad\text{as }\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}\to+\infty,u\in\mathcal{K}.

This leads to the following extension of the Browder–Minty theorem.

Lemma 2.8

(​​
[
26
]
, Corollary 1.8)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty, closed, and convex set. Suppose
A:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is coercive, monotone, and continuous on finite-dimensional subspaces. Then, there exists
u\in\mathcal{K}
such that

\langle Au,v-u\rangle\geq 0\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{K}.

If
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{X}
is a linear space, then
\langle Au,v-u\rangle\geq 0
for all
v\in\mathcal{X}
is equivalent to
Au=0
in
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.14

Suppose that Assumptions

and

–

and

Proof
.

From Assumptions

–
S.5

The convexity of the mapping

Since

Lemma 2.15

Suppose that Assumptions

and
S.4

.

From Assumption

3
The MFG Operator

In this section, we construct the operator
A_{\epsilon}
associated with MFG System
1
. We first motivate its structure by formally deriving it from a penalized variational principle. Subsequently, we establish the functional framework and rigorously define the operator via a duality pairing on the product space
\mathcal{X}
, which allows us to incorporate the mixed boundary conditions.

We recall that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded, open domain with a
C^{1}
boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
. The boundary is partitioned as in MFG System
1
into two disjoint subsets
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
, with
\Gamma=\overline{\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}}
and
\Gamma_{D},\Gamma_{N}\neq\emptyset
. Throughout this section, we work under Assumptions
D
and
S
.

3.1
Variational Derivation and Interpretation

In this subsection, we derive the penalized MFG operator
A_{\epsilon}
from a variational principle and link it to the strong formulation of System
1
. This approach synthesizes the variational formulation for mixed boundary conditions developed in
[
3
]
with the duality techniques found in
[
11
]
.

The Penalized Functional.

Let
g^{-1}
be the extended inverse of
g
(Definition
2.9
) and let
G:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be its primitive. We begin with the unpenalized baseline problem: minimizing
\int_{\Omega}G(H(x,Du)){\,\,\rm d}x-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju{\,\,\rm d}s
subject to
u\leq 0
on
\Gamma_{D}
.

To explicitly incorporate the density
m
, we utilize the Fenchel conjugate
G^{*}(m)=\sup_{z}\{mz-G(z)\}
. For
m\geq 0
, this satisfies

G^{*}(m)=mg(m)-G(g(m)),

allowing us to write
G(H)=\sup_{m\geq 0}\{mH-G^{*}(m)\}
.
Similarly, to enforce the boundary inequality constraint
u\leq 0
on
\Gamma_{D}
, we introduce the penalty term
\frac{1}{\gamma\epsilon^{\gamma}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}u_{+}^{\gamma}{\,\,\rm d}s
.
Using convex duality on
L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
, we express this penalty as a maximization over the boundary flux
h
:

\frac{1}{\gamma\epsilon^{\gamma}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}u_{+}^{\gamma}{\,\,\rm d}s=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})\\
h\geq 0\end{subarray}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}\left(hu-\frac{\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}}{\gamma^{\prime}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}}\right){\,\,\rm d}s.

Substituting these relations into the baseline problem
leads to the following minimax problem on the convex cone of nonnegative densities and fluxes (which we will rigorously define as
\mathcal{X}^{+}
in the next subsection):

(3.1)

\inf_{u}\sup_{h\geq 0,m\geq 0}\kern 5.0pt\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(m,u,h),

where

(3.2)

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(m,u,h):=\int_{\Omega}\big(mH(x,Du)-mg(m)&+G(g(m))\big){\,\,\rm d}x\\
&-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}\left(hu-\frac{\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}}{\gamma^{\prime}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}}\right){\,\,\rm d}s.\end{split}

First Variations and Physical Consistency.

The functional
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}
is convex in
u
and concave in
(m,h)
and the operator
A_{\epsilon}
corresponds to the skew-symmetric gradient-like map of this saddle-point problem:

(m,u,h)\mapsto\left(-\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{I}}}{\delta m},\,\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{I}}}{\delta u},\,-\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{I}}}{\delta h}\right).

We note that while the Gâteaux derivatives below are computed formally for smooth triplets, the resulting identities extend to
\mathcal{X}^{+}
via the growth bounds in Assumptions
S
and the dominated convergence theorem (see
[
3
]
). This saddle-point gradient map is the monotone operator studied in Subsection
4.1
.

I.

Variation in
u
:
For any
v\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
, the stationarity condition
\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{I}}}{\delta u}=0
yields

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega}mD_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot Dv{\,\,\rm d}x-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}jv{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}hv{\,\,\rm d}s=0.

Formally, this corresponds to the conservation law
-\Div(mD_{p}H(x,Du))=0
with boundary fluxes
mD_{p}H\cdot\nu=j
on
\Gamma_{N}
and
-mD_{p}H\cdot\nu=h
on
\Gamma_{D}
.

II.

Variation in
m
:
For any
\mu\geq 0
, the optimality condition
\langle-\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{I}}}{\delta m},\mu-m\rangle\geq 0
yields

(3.4)

\int_{\Omega}(\mu-m)(-H(x,Du)+g(m)){\,\,\rm d}x\geq 0.

This encodes the full complementarity system:

m\geq 0,\qquad-H(x,Du)+g(m)\geq 0,\qquad m\bigl(-H(x,Du)+g(m)\bigr)=0\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

This is equivalent to the explicit inversion
m=g^{-1}(H(x,Du))
.

III.

Variation in
h
:
For any
k\geq 0
, the optimality condition
\langle-\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{I}}}{\delta h},k-h\rangle\geq 0
yields

(3.5)

\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(k-h)(-u+\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}){\,\,\rm d}s\geq 0.

This implies the penalized Signorini conditions:

h\geq 0,\quad\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}\geq u,\quad\text{and}\quad h(\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}-u)=0.

Solving for
h
, we obtain
h=\epsilon^{-\gamma}u_{+}^{\gamma-1}
, equivalently,

(3.6)

\epsilon^{\gamma}\,h=u_{+}^{\gamma-1}.

Introducing
h
as an independent variable circumvents the difficulty inherent in the nonlinearity of the flux
J:=-mD_{p}H
by providing a linear variable in the boundary integral.
Moreover, using the boundary flux relation
h=-mD_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu
on
\Gamma_{D}
(formally for smooth triplets), (
3.6
) yields the boundary identity:

(3.7)

-u\,mD_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu=\epsilon^{-\gamma}\,u_{+}^{\gamma}\qquad\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}.

The Variational Inequality.

Combining these conditions, any saddle point
(m,u,h)
of (
3.1
) satisfies the variational inequality:

(3.8)

\left\langle A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m\\
v-u\\
k-h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0\qquad\text{for all }(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}^{+}.

Formally, (
3.8
) recovers System
1
: the
u
-variation yields
-\Div(mD_{p}H(x,Du))=0
in
\Omega
with fluxes
mD_{p}H\cdot\nu=j
on
\Gamma_{N}
and
-mD_{p}H\cdot\nu=h
on
\Gamma_{D}
; the
m
-variation yields
m=g^{-1}(H(x,Du))
a.e. in
\Omega
; and the
h
-variation yields the penalized Signorini condition on
\Gamma_{D}
, so that if
u<0
on
\Gamma_{D}
then
h=0
and the outflow vanishes.

Remark 3.1

(Regularization and Domain)
.

The parameter
\epsilon>0
acts as a boundary regularization, introducing the term
h^{\gamma^{\prime}}
which ensures coercivity in
h
. Recovering the original hard constraint
u\leq 0
requires the limit
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, using estimates derived from (
3.7
).
Furthermore, while the penalized operator
A_{\epsilon}
requires
h\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
to define
h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}
, the limit operator
A_{0}
admits
h
in the strictly larger dual trace space
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
, consistent with interpreting the limit boundary flux as a distribution.

3.2
Functional Framework

We first establish the Banach spaces and duality pairings necessary for the weak formulation. Let the exponents
\beta
and
\gamma
be given as in Assumption
D.1
, and let
\gamma^{\prime}
denote the Hölder conjugate of
\gamma
. We define the product space

\mathcal{X}:=L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D}).

The topological dual of
\mathcal{X}
can be identified with

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=L^{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}}(\Omega)\times W^{-1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D}),

where
W^{-1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega)
denotes the dual space
(W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega))^{\prime}
.

The Operator Domain.

The domain of our operator is the convex cone of nonnegative densities and boundary fluxes:

(3.9)

\mathcal{X}^{+}:=\big\{(m,u,h)\in\mathcal{X}:m\geq 0\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\text{ and }h\geq 0\text{ a.e. on }\Gamma_{D}\big\}.

Trace Spaces and Duality.

While the
L^{p}
components of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
are standard, the handling of the divergence terms requires precise properties of the Sobolev dual. Let
T:W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)\to W^{1-\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma}(\Gamma)
denote the continuous trace operator, and let
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
denote the dual of the trace space. We rely on the continuous embedding (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
):

(3.10)

L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow W^{-1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega).

This embedding identifies a triplet
\mathbf{\Xi}=(f,F,\mathfrak{f})
in the product space with a functional acting on
\varphi\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
via the pairing:

\langle\mathbf{\Xi},\varphi\rangle_{W^{-1,\gamma^{\prime}},W^{1,\gamma}}=\int_{\Omega}f\,\varphi{\,\,\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}F\cdot D\varphi{\,\,\rm d}x+\big\langle\mathfrak{f},T\varphi\big\rangle_{\Gamma}.

Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
denotes the duality pairing between
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
and the trace space; when
\mathfrak{f}\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
, this reduces to the standard boundary integral
\int_{\Gamma}\mathfrak{f}\,T\varphi{\,\,\rm d}s
.

In the context of the operator
A_{\epsilon}(m,u,h)
, the transport component corresponds to the triplet

\bigl(0,\;mD_{p}H(\cdot,Du),\;-j\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{N}}+h\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}\bigr)

under the embedding (
3.10
). For the penalized operator
A_{\epsilon}
, the boundary terms lie in
L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
. However, for the limit operator
A_{0}
, the boundary flux
h
belongs to the wider space
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
, requiring the boundary terms to be interpreted strictly via the trace duality.

3.3
Definition and Well-Posedness

We define the MFG operator
A_{\epsilon}:\mathcal{X}^{+}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
associated with System
1
. While formally
A_{\epsilon}
corresponds to the vector-valued operator

(3.11)

A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}-H(x,Du)+g(m)\\
-\Div\big(m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\big)+\big(m\,D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot\nu\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma}-j\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{N}}+h\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}\big)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
\big(-u+\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}\big)\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\end{bmatrix},

we define it rigorously via a duality pairing to ensure boundedness in
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.
In particular, the transport component (the second line in (
3.11
)) is encoded through its action on
v\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
by

v\longmapsto\int_{\Omega}mD_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot Dv\,{\,\,\rm d}x\;-\;\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,v\,{\,\,\rm d}s\;+\;\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h\,v\,{\,\,\rm d}s,

which agrees with the formal expression in (
3.11
) whenever
mD_{p}H(x,Du)
admits a normal trace on
\Gamma
. Hence no pointwise normal trace of
mD_{p}H(x,Du)
is required to define
A_{\epsilon}
at this stage.

Definition 3.2

(The Operator
A_{\epsilon}
)
.

For any
(m,u,h)\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
and test triplet
(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}
, we define the action of
A_{\epsilon}
by:

(3.12)

\begin{split}\left\langle A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle&:=\int_{\Omega}\bigl[-H(x,Du)+g(m)\bigr]\mu{\,\,\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}mD_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot Dv{\,\,\rm d}x\\
&\quad-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}jv{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}hv{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(-u+\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1})k{\,\,\rm d}s.\end{split}

In this expression,
v
and
u
are understood in the sense of traces on
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{D}
, respectively.

We now verify that this pairing defines a bounded mapping.

Proposition 3.3
.

Under Assumptions
S
and
D
, the operator
A_{\epsilon}:\mathcal{X}^{+}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, defined by (
3.12
), is well-defined for any
\epsilon>0
.

Proof
.

To establish that
A_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{X}^{+})\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, it suffices to demonstrate that each component of the pairing defines a functional in the appropriate space. Specifically, we show:

\begin{array}[]{rlcrl}-H(\cdot,Du)+g(m)&\in L^{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}}(\Omega),&&mD_{p}H(\cdot,Du)&\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\[5.0pt]
-u|_{\Gamma_{D}}+\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}&\in L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D}),&\text{ and }&-j\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{N}}+h\,\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}&\in W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma).\end{array}

We establish these bounds as follows:

1.

Hamiltonian and Coupling:
Assumption
S.4
(specifically Remark
2.1
) implies the bound
|H(x,p)|\leq C(|p|^{\alpha}+1)
. Since
\alpha\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}=\gamma
, we have
H(\cdot,Du)\in L^{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}}(\Omega)
. Similarly, Assumption
S.3
implies
g(m)\in L^{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}}(\Omega)
. Thus, the sum lies in
L^{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}}(\Omega)
.

2.

Transport Flux:
Assumption
S.4
yields
|D_{p}H(x,p)|\leq C(|p|^{\alpha-1}+1)
; hence,

|D_{p}H(x,Du)|^{\gamma^{\prime}}\leq C(|Du|^{(\alpha-1)\gamma^{\prime}}+1).

Applying Young’s inequality to the mixed term with conjugate exponents

\lambda=\frac{\beta+1}{\gamma^{\prime}},\qquad\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{\gamma-1}{\alpha-1}\quad\left(\text{so that }\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}=1\right),

and using
m^{\gamma^{\prime}}\leq 1+m^{\beta+1}
(since
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\beta+1
when
\alpha>1
), yields

m^{\gamma^{\prime}}|D_{p}H(x,Du)|^{\gamma^{\prime}}\leq Cm^{\beta+1}+C(|Du|^{\gamma}+1).

Thus,
mD_{p}H(x,Du)\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

3.

Boundary Terms:
Since
j\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{N})
and
h\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
, the boundary flux
-j\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{N}}+h\scalebox{1.18}{$\chi$}_{\Gamma_{D}}
lies in
L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)\subset W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
. Finally, the trace theorem ensures
u|_{\Gamma_{D}}\in L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
, and
h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}\in L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
, so the penalty term is well-defined.

This confirms that the pairing defines a map from
\mathcal{X}^{+}
to
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.
∎

4
On the Properties of the MFG Operator

In this section, we investigate the fundamental properties of the penalized MFG operator

A_{\epsilon}
. To simplify the presentation, throughout this and the following section, we fix
\epsilon=1
and write

(4.1)

A:=A_{1},

noting that all statements and proofs extend naturally to arbitrary
\epsilon>0
. We begin by establishing the monotonicity and hemicontinuity of the full operator
A
. Then, we introduce a restricted auxiliary operator
A_{u}
to prove that the system exhibits partial coercivity with respect to
m
and
h
. Finally, we demonstrate that
A
lacks global coercivity due to a constant-shift degeneracy in the transport variable
u
. This failure prevents the direct application of the Browder–Minty theorem on
\mathcal{X}^{+}
, motivating the quotient-space formulation introduced in Section
5
.

4.1
The monotonicity of the operator
A

In this subsection, we establish the defining property of the monotone operator
A
, namely, its monotonicity.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
S
and
D
hold. Then, the operator
A:\mathcal{X}^{+}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, defined by (
4.1
), is monotone.

Proof
.

Let
(m,u,h),(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
be arbitrary. To establish the monotonicity of
A
, we need to show that

\varpi:=\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix}-A\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0.

By rearranging the terms of the pairing, we obtain

\displaystyle\varpi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[g(m)-g(\mu)](m-\mu){\,\,\rm d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}-k^{\gamma^{\prime}-1})(h-k){\,\,\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}m\left[H(x,Dv)-H(x,Du)-D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot(Dv-Du)\right]{\,\,\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\mu\left[H(x,Du)-H(x,Dv)-D_{p}H(x,Dv)\cdot(Du-Dv)\right]{\,\,\rm d}x.

The first two integrals are nonnegative because the maps
\mu\mapsto g(\mu)
and
h\mapsto h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}
are increasing. The last two integrals are nonnegative due to the convexity of the map
p\mapsto H(x,p)
and the non-negativity of
m
and
\mu
. Therefore,
A
is monotone.
∎

Remark 4.2

(On strict monotonicity)
.

Note that the operator
A
is monotone, but not strictly monotone. The terms in the monotonicity calculation involving
H
are guaranteed to be non-negative by convexity, but they can be zero even if
Du\neq Dv
(for instance, if
H
is linear in a region).
Even if
H
is strictly convex, strict monotonicity in
u
holds only
modulo constants
(and can still fail in the vanishing-density regime).
This lack of strict monotonicity in the
u
component is a key feature of the problem. In contrast, the auxiliary operator
A_{u}
in Proposition
4.4
is strictly monotone in
(m,h)
due to the strict increase of
g
and the term
h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}
.

4.2
The continuity of the operator
A

The hemicontinuity of the monotone operator
A
is a necessary condition for the application of the Browder–Minty theorem.
In view of the discussion in Subsection
2.3
, it is enough to verify continuity along convex combinations.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose Assumptions
S
and
D
hold, and let
A:\mathcal{X}^{+}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
be the operator defined by (
4.1
). Let
(m_{0},u_{0},h_{0}),(m_{1},u_{1},h_{1})\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
be fixed, and define the convex combination

(m_{\vartheta},u_{\vartheta},h_{\vartheta}):=\vartheta(m_{1},u_{1},h_{1})+(1-\vartheta)(m_{0},u_{0},h_{0})

for
\vartheta\in[0,1]
. Then, for every
(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}
, the mapping

\vartheta\mapsto\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m_{\vartheta}\\
u_{\vartheta}\\
h_{\vartheta}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle

is continuous on
[0,1]
.

Proof
.

Fix
(m_{0},u_{0},h_{0}),(m_{1},u_{1},h_{1})\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
and define the convex combination

(m_{\vartheta},u_{\vartheta},h_{\vartheta}):=\vartheta(m_{1},u_{1},h_{1})+(1-\vartheta)(m_{0},u_{0},h_{0}),

for
\vartheta\in[0,1]
. For an arbitrary
(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}
, we consider the function

\begin{split}f(\vartheta):=\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m_{\vartheta}\\
u_{\vartheta}\\
h_{\vartheta}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Expanding this expression using the definition of
A
, we obtain

\displaystyle f(\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[-H(x,Du_{\vartheta})+g(m_{\vartheta})]\mu{\,\,\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}m_{\vartheta}D_{p}H(x,Du_{\vartheta})\cdot Dv{\,\,\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}jv{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}[h_{\vartheta}v-u_{\vartheta}k]{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h_{\vartheta}^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}k{\,\,\rm d}s.

Our goal is to prove that
f
is continuous using the dominated convergence theorem.

From Lemma
2.14
and Young’s inequality, with

p_{\vartheta}:=Du_{\vartheta}
and

p_{i}:=Du_{i}
for
i=0,1
, we obtain

\begin{split}|-H(x,p_{\vartheta})+g(m_{\vartheta})||\mu|&\leq C\left[|p_{1}|^{\alpha}+m_{1}^{\beta}+|p_{0}|^{\alpha}+m_{0}^{\beta}+1\right]|\mu|\\
&\leq C\left[|p_{1}|^{\gamma}+m_{1}^{\beta+1}+|p_{0}|^{\gamma}+m_{0}^{\beta+1}+|\mu|^{\beta+1}+1\right],\end{split}

where we used the exponent relations in (
2.1
) and Young’s inequality to bound the products
|p|^{\alpha}|\mu|
(with the conjugate exponents
\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}
and
\beta+1
) and
m^{\beta}|\mu|
(with the exponents
\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}
and
\beta+1
).

Moreover, from Lemma
2.15
and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}|m_{\vartheta}D_{p}H(x,Du_{\vartheta})\cdot Dv|&\leq|m_{\vartheta}D_{p}H(x,p_{\vartheta})||Dv|\\
&\leq C\left[|p_{0}|^{\gamma-1}+m_{0}^{(\beta+1)/\gamma^{\prime}}+|p_{1}|^{\gamma-1}+m_{1}^{(\beta+1)/\gamma^{\prime}}+1\right]|Dv|\\
&\leq C\left[|p_{0}|^{\gamma}+m_{0}^{\beta+1}+|p_{1}|^{\gamma}+m_{1}^{\beta+1}+|Dv|^{\gamma}+1\right].\end{split}

Similarly, Young’s inequality and the fact that
\vartheta\leq 1
give us

h_{\vartheta}^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}|k|\leq C(h_{1}^{\gamma^{\prime}}+h_{0}^{\gamma^{\prime}}+|k|^{\gamma^{\prime}}).

Hence, all integrands are dominated by an
L^{1}(\Omega)
or
L^{1}(\Gamma_{D})
function independent of
\theta
,
and pointwise convergence follows from continuity of
H
and
D_{p}H
in
p
.
Finally, the term
\int_{\Gamma_{D}}[h_{\vartheta}v-u_{\vartheta}k]{\,\,\rm d}s
is continuous in
\vartheta
by linearity, and the term
-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}jv{\,\,\rm d}s
is constant. Thus,
by the pointwise convergence of the remaining integrands as
\vartheta\to\vartheta_{0}
and the established dominating functions, the dominated convergence theorem yields

\lim_{\vartheta\to\vartheta_{0}}f(\vartheta)=f(\vartheta_{0}).

This completes the proof.
∎

4.3
The Restricted Auxiliary Operator
A_{u}
and Partial Coercivity

This subsection establishes the coercivity of the operator
A
in
m
and
h
(see Definition
2.7
).
As usual, we identify
L^{(\beta+1)/\beta}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
with the dual of
L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
.
For each
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
, we define the restricted auxiliary operator

A_{u}:L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})\rightarrow L^{(\beta+1)/\beta}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D}),

via the pairing

(4.2)

\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
k\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle:=\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
0\\
k\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle.

Because
A_{u}
is a restriction of the full operator
A
evaluated at a fixed
u
, it naturally inherits the monotonicity and hemicontinuity of
A
. Furthermore, due to the strictly increasing nature of the coupling
g
and the boundary penalty term,
A_{u}
is strictly monotone.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
S
and
D
hold, and let
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
be fixed.
Then, the operator
A_{u}:L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})\to L^{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
, defined by (
4.2
), is strictly monotone on the cone of nonnegative pairs
(m,h)
.

Proof
.

Let
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
be fixed, and let
(m,h),(\mu,k)\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
be distinct pairs of nonnegative functions. To establish the strict monotonicity of
A_{u}
, we need to show that

\displaystyle\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}-A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
k\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
k\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[g(m)-g(\mu)](m-\mu){\,\,\rm d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}-k^{\gamma^{\prime}-1})(h-k){\,\,\rm d}s>0.

Since
g
and
z\mapsto z^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}
are strictly increasing, the integrands are strictly positive wherever the functions differ. Since
(m,h)\not\equiv(\mu,k)
, at least one integral is strictly positive, implying the inequality is strict.
∎

Finally, the following proposition establishes the coercivity of
A_{u}
, which is essential for solving the sub-problem in Section
5
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose that Assumptions
S
and
D
hold, and let
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
be fixed. Moreover,
let
A_{u}:L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})\to L^{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
be the operator defined by (
4.2
). Let
(m_{0},h_{0}),(m_{1},h_{1})\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
be fixed pairs of nonnegative functions, and define the convex combination

(m_{\vartheta},h_{\vartheta}):=\vartheta(m_{1},h_{1})+(1-\vartheta)(m_{0},h_{0})

for
\vartheta\in[0,1]
. Then, for every
(\mu,k)\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
, the mapping

\vartheta\mapsto\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m_{\vartheta}\\
h_{\vartheta}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
k\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle

is continuous on
[0,1]
.

Proof
.

Since
A_{u}
is a restriction of
A
(with
u
fixed), the result follows immediately from Proposition
4.3
.
∎

The following result establishes the coercivity of
A_{u}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
and
S
hold.
For a fixed
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
, let
A_{u}
be the operator defined by (
4.2
). Then, for
m\geq 0
and
h\geq 0
, we have

\frac{1}{\|m\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}+\|h\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}}\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}-A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\longrightarrow\infty

as
\|m\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}+\|h\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}\to\infty
.

Remark 4.7

(On the assumptions in Proposition
4.6
)
.

Proposition
4.6
is stated under the standing hypotheses (in particular, Assumptions
S
, together with the exponent specification in Assumption
D.1
) so that

A_{u}:L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})\;\longrightarrow\;L^{(\beta+1)/\beta}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})

is a well-defined operator. However, the
coercivity estimate itself
uses only the superlinearity of the coupling and the boundary penalty.
More precisely, we see from the proof below that once the pairing (
4.2
) is meaningful, the proof of coercivity relies only on Assumption
S.3
and the fact that
\beta>0
(hence
\beta+1>1
) and
\gamma^{\prime}>1
.
In particular, no structural assumption on
H
and no assumption on the inflow
j
is used in this coercivity argument.
Moreover, the constants in the coercivity bound are independent of the fixed
u
, since the terms involving
H(x,Du)
and
u|_{\Gamma_{D}}
cancel in the difference

A_{u}(m,h)-A_{u}(0,0)
.

Proof
.

Fix
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
, and consider arbitrary nonnegative functions
m\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)
and
h\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
.
From Assumption
S.3
,

m(g(m)-g(0))\geq cm^{\beta+1}-C(m+1)
.
Accordingly,
we obtain

(4.3)

\begin{split}\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}-A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle&=\int_{\Omega}m\bigl(g(m)-g(0)\bigr){\,\,\rm d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}}{\,\,\rm d}s\\
&\geq C^{-1}\|m\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}^{\beta+1}-C(\|m\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}+1)+\|h\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}^{\gamma^{\prime}}.\end{split}

for some
C>1
, independent of
m
and
h
.
Thus

\begin{split}\frac{\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}-A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle}{\|m\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}+\|h\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}}\to\infty,\end{split}

as
\|m\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}+\|h\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}\to\infty
, concluding the proof.
∎

4.4
Constant-Shift Degeneracy and the Failure of Coercivity

In this subsection, we demonstrate that the operator
A
fails to be coercive with respect to the variable
u
. This failure prevents the direct application of the Browder–Minty theorem.
We identify two specific cases where coercivity breaks down. To this end, we fix
m
and
h
and introduce the auxiliary operator

A_{m,h}:W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)\to W^{-1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega),

which we define via the restriction of the pairing as follows:

(4.4)

\left\langle A_{m,h}u\,,\,v\right\rangle:=\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}0\\
v\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle.

The vanishing density case:

Under the condition
m\equiv 0
, we observe that the interaction term in the pairing drops out, leaving

\langle A_{0,h}u,u\rangle=-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}hu{\,\,\rm d}s.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and the trace inequality, we obtain

\left|\langle A_{0,h}u,u\rangle\right|=\left|-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}hu{\,\,\rm d}s\right|\leq C\big(\|j\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{N})}+\|h\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}\big)\|u\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
\gamma
and
\Omega
. This linear upper bound implies that the ratio
\langle A_{0,h}u,u\rangle/\|u\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}
remains bounded, which violates the coercivity condition (Definition
2.7
).

The flux compatibility case:

When the boundary fluxes balance, i.e.,
\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h{\,\,\rm d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s
.
This case is significant because this identity is a necessary condition for the existence of solutions (see Subsection
5.2
).
In this setting, the duality pairing is invariant under simultaneous constant shifts. Since
D(u+c_{1})=Du
and
D(v+c_{2})=Dv
, the interior integral term remains unchanged.
Consequently, for any constants
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
A_{m,h}
satisfies

\langle A_{m,h}(u+c_{1}),v+c_{2}\rangle=\langle A_{m,h}u,v\rangle-c_{2}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h{\,\,\rm d}s\right)=\langle A_{m,h}u,v\rangle.

Specifically, setting
v=u
and
c_{1}=c_{2}=c
, we obtain

\left\langle A_{m,h}(u+c),u+c\right\rangle=\left\langle A_{m,h}u,u\right\rangle.

This invariance under constant shifts allows
c\to\infty
, driving
\|u+c\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\to\infty
while
\langle A_{m,h}(u+c),u+c\rangle
remains unchanged.
Thus,
A_{m,h}
cannot be coercive. Hence, the operator
A
fails to be coercive as well. In Section
5
, we resolve this lack of coercivity by reformulating the problem on a quotient space.

5
Existence of Solutions

In this section, we establish the existence of solutions to the penalized variational inequality (
3.8
).
As in the previous section, we work
in the normalized case
\epsilon=1
(so
A=A_{1}
). The passage to the limit
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, which yields solutions to the original MFG system, is addressed in Section
6
.
The primary obstruction to a direct application of the Browder–Minty theorem is the operator’s lack of coercivity in the variable
u
, stemming from the system’s invariance to constant shifts (see Subsection
4.4
). We circumvent this issue by first solving for the pair
(m_{u},h_{u})
for each fixed
u
, thereby isolating the coercivity failure. We then restore coercivity by reformulating the problem on a quotient space.

The proof proceeds in the following steps.

i.

Establish the existence and uniqueness of
(m_{u},h_{u})
for each fixed
u
.

ii.

Modify the operator’s domain to circumvent the coercivity failure in
u
.

iii.

Redefine the operator on the new domain, incorporating the solutions
(m_{u},h_{u})
.

iv.

Prove the coercivity of the modified operator and establish the existence of solutions.

5.1
Solvability of the Auxiliary Problem for
(m,h)

In Subsection
4.3
, we established a partial coercivity result for the operator
A
and introduced the auxiliary operator
A_{u}
, defined for fixed
u
through the restricted pairing (
4.2
).
In this subsection, we fix the function
u
and exploit this partial coercivity to establish the existence of solutions for the variational inequality:

(5.1)

\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
h\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m\\
k-h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall\,(\mu,k)\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})\text{ with }\mu,k\geq 0.

The solution we obtain is unique and denoted by
(m_{u},h_{u})\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
, as shown below.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
S
and
D
hold, and let
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
be fixed.
Let
\mathcal{K}:=\{(m,h)\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D}):m\geq 0,\;h\geq 0\}
.
Then, there exists a unique pair
(m_{u},h_{u})\in\mathcal{K}
that solves the variational inequality (
5.1
).

Proof
.

Due to Proposition
4.6
, Proposition
4.4
, and Corollary
4.5
, the operator
A_{u}
is coercive, strictly monotone, and hemicontinuous. Therefore, we apply Lemma
2.8
with the nonempty, closed, and convex set
\mathcal{K}
to show the existence of a pair
(m_{u},h_{u})\in\mathcal{K}
that solves (
5.1
).

To prove uniqueness, let
(m_{u},h_{u})
and
(\bar{m}_{u},\bar{h}_{u})
be two solutions. The variational inequality implies

\displaystyle\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m_{u}\\
h_{u}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\bar{m}_{u}-m_{u}\\
\bar{h}_{u}-h_{u}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{m}_{u}\\
\bar{h}_{u}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}m_{u}-\bar{m}_{u}\\
h_{u}-\bar{h}_{u}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Combining these inequalities yields

\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m_{u}\\
h_{u}\end{bmatrix}-A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{m}_{u}\\
\bar{h}_{u}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}m_{u}-\bar{m}_{u}\\
h_{u}-\bar{h}_{u}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\leq 0.

Since
A_{u}
is strictly monotone (Proposition
4.4
), the equality must hold, which implies
(m_{u},h_{u})=(\bar{m}_{u},\bar{h}_{u})
.
∎

Theorem
5.1
guarantees the existence and uniqueness of the pair
(m_{u},h_{u})
, which satisfies the variational inequality (
5.1
). While this establishes solvability, we can gain further insight by considering specific test functions in the inequality. This leads to the following Corollary that establishes pointwise characterizations of
m_{u}
and
h_{u}
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
S
and
D
hold. Let
(m_{u},h_{u})\in\mathcal{K}
be the unique solution provided by Theorem
5.1
to the inequality (
5.1
). Then, the following characterizations hold:

i.

For almost every
x\in\Omega
,

-H(x,Du(x))+g(m_{u}(x))\geq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad m_{u}(x)\bigl(-H(x,Du(x))+g(m_{u}(x))\bigr)=0.

Consequently, for almost every
x\in\Omega
,

m_{u}(x)=g^{-1}(H(x,Du(x))),

where
g^{-1}
is the extended inverse of
g
(see Definition
2.9
).

ii.

For almost every
x\in\Gamma_{D}
,

-u(x)+h_{u}(x)^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}\geq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad h_{u}(x)(-u(x)+h_{u}(x)^{\gamma^{\prime}-1})=0.

Consequently, for almost every
x\in\Gamma_{D}
,

h_{u}(x)=u_{+}^{\gamma-1}(x).

Remark 5.3
.

In the case of arbitrary
\epsilon
, the formula for
m
is the same. However, we have that

(5.2)

h_{u}=\frac{u_{+}^{\gamma-1}}{\epsilon^{\gamma}}.

Proof of Corollary
5.2
.
.

Let
(m_{u},h_{u})\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
be the solution provided by Theorem
5.1
. We prove each statement separately.

i.

Consider arbitrary test functions
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi\geq 0
and
\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Define

\mu=\left(1+\phi\right)m_{u}+\varphi\quad\text{ and }\quad k=h_{u}.

Because
(m_{u},h_{u})
solve the variational inequality (
5.1
), we obtain

\begin{split}0\leq\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m_{u}\\
h_{u}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}\phi\,m_{u}+\varphi\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}(-H(x,Du)+g(m_{u}))\left(\phi\,m_{u}+\varphi\right){\,\,\rm d}x.\end{split}

Because
\varphi
is arbitrary with
\varphi\geq 0
, taking
\phi\equiv 0
yields
-H(x,Du)+g(m_{u})\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Then, taking
\varphi\equiv 0
and
\phi=\pm\psi
with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
gives

\int_{\Omega}(-H(x,Du)+g(m_{u}))\,\psi\,m_{u}\,{\,\,\rm d}x=0
. Hence,
m_{u}(-H(x,Du)+g(m_{u}))=0
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Taking into account that either
m_{u}=0
or
m_{u}>0
, using the definition of the extended inverse, we have

-

If
m_{u}=0
, then
H(x,Du)\leq g(0)
. Therefore,
g^{-1}(H(x,Du))=0
.

-

If
m_{u}>0
, then
H(x,Du)=g(m_{u})
. Therefore,
g^{-1}(H(x,Du))=m_{u}
.

ii.

Similar to the above, consider arbitrary test functions
\varphi,\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Gamma_{D})

such that
\varphi\geq 0
and
\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Define

k=\left(1+\phi\right)h_{u}+\varphi,\quad\text{and set }\mu=m_{u}.

Because
(m_{u},h_{u})
solve the variational inequality (
5.1
), we obtain

\begin{split}0\leq\left\langle A_{u}\begin{bmatrix}m_{u}\\
h_{u}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\phi\,h_{u}+\varphi\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(-u+h_{u}^{\gamma^{\prime}-1})(\phi\,h_{u}+\varphi){\,\,\rm d}s.\end{split}

Because
\varphi
is arbitrary with
\varphi\geq 0
, taking
\phi\equiv 0
yields
-u+h_{u}^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}\geq 0
a.e. on
\Gamma_{D}
.
Then, taking
\varphi\equiv 0
and
\phi=\pm\psi
with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
gives

\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(-u+h_{u}^{\gamma^{\prime}-1})\,\psi\,h_{u}\,{\,\,\rm d}s=0
. Hence,
h_{u}(-u+h_{u}^{\gamma^{\prime}-1})=0
a.e. on
\Gamma_{D}
. Moreover,
taking into account that either
h_{u}=0
or
h_{u}>0
, we have

-

If
h_{u}=0
, then
0\geq u
. Therefore,

h_{u}=0=u_{+}^{\gamma-1}.

-

If
h_{u}>0
, then
h_{u}^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}=u
. Therefore,

h_{u}=u_{+}^{\gamma-1}.

Thus, in both cases, we conclude that

h_{u}=u_{+}^{\gamma-1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.2
The modified domain

The failure of coercivity identified in Subsection
4.4
prevents a direct application of the Browder–Minty theorem on
\mathcal{X}^{+}
. This failure is structurally linked to the operator’s insensitivity to additive constants in
u
when boundary fluxes are balanced. To address this, we define the operator on the quotient space of functions modulo constants.
Specifically, we define the quotient space

(5.3)

\mathcal{Q}={\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\{u=c\colon c\in\mathbb{R}\}}$}}{\raisebox{4.07pt}{$\textstyle{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\{u=c\colon c\in\mathbb{R}\}}$}}{\raisebox{3.12001pt}{$\scriptstyle{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\scriptstyle{\{u=c\colon c\in\mathbb{R}\}}$}}{\raisebox{2.3663pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\{u=c\colon c\in\mathbb{R}\}}$}}},

where each equivalence class
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{Q}
is represented by an element
u\in\bar{u}
.
The quotient space
\mathcal{Q}

is reflexive and admits a norm equivalent to
\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}}
;
we establish these properties in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

The quotient space
\mathcal{Q}
defined in (
5.3
) has the following properties:

i.

The space
\mathcal{Q}
is reflexive, and its dual space consists precisely of functionals that annihilate constants, i.e.,

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\cong\{v\in W^{-1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega)\colon\langle v,u\rangle=0\text{ for all }u\equiv c,\,c\in\mathbb{R}\}.

ii.

The seminorm

(5.4)

\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}:=\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}

defines a norm on
\mathcal{Q}
equivalent to its quotient norm.

Proof
.

i.
The reflexivity of
\mathcal{Q}
follows from classical functional analysis results (see
[
30
]
, Corollary 1.11.18 and Theorem 1.10.16).

ii.
The quotient norm of
\mathcal{Q}
is

\|\bar{u}\|_{in,\mathcal{Q}}:=\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|u-c\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}=\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|u-c\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}.

Setting

(5.5)

c=\avg_{\Omega}u:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(x){\,\,\rm d}x,

and using the Poincaré inequality, we obtain

(5.6)

\begin{split}\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bar{u}\|_{in,\mathcal{Q}}\leq\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+\|u-\avg_{\Omega}u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}&\leq C\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)},\end{split}

where
C>1
depends only on
\gamma
and the domain
\Omega
. This shows that
\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}:=\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}
is an equivalent norm.
∎

Before we specify the domain modification that affects the function
h
, we first establish that solutions belong to a specific subset of the function space, allowing us to restrict the operator’s domain without excluding relevant solutions.
More precisely, consider a triplet
(m,u,h)
that solves (
3.8
). Fix
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
v\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
, and test (
3.8
) with
(\mu,k)=(m,h)
and
v_{\rm test}=u+v+c
. Then

0\leq\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0\\
v+c\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle=\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0\\
v\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle+c\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h\,{\,\,\rm d}s\right).

Dividing by
|c|
and letting
|c|\to\infty
, we conclude that

0\leq\pm\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h{\,\,\rm d}s\right),

which is only possible if

(5.7)

\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h\,{\,\,\rm d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,{\,\,\rm d}s.

This compatibility condition aligns with the optimization problem (
3.1
). It is also consistent with Lemma
5.4
, which imposes that the image of the modified monotone operator belongs to the set of annihilators of constant functions.
In other words, solutions to the variational inequality must satisfy the compatibility condition
(
5.7
).
Accordingly, a natural domain to look for a solution is

(5.8)

\mathcal{Y}:=\Big\{(m,\bar{u},h)\in L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{Q}\times L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D}):m\geq 0,\;h\geq 0,\;\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h{\,\,\rm d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s\Big\}.

We note that
\mathcal{Y}
is convex (since
\mathcal{Q}
is a linear space and the constraints are affine/positivity).
Since this modification changes the domain’s structure, the operator must be redefined accordingly; we address this in the next subsection.

5.3
The modified operator

Defining an operator on a quotient space typically requires a set-valued formulation. A key observation is that the image of the operator lies in the annihilator of constant functions (Lemma
5.4
). By combining this property with the solution
(m_{u},h_{u})
from Subsection
5.1
, we construct a
single-valued operator that circumvents these difficulties.

We begin our analysis by selecting a canonical representative for each equivalence class
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{Q}
.
The choice of representative is governed by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
hold, and let
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a unique
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(5.9)

\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u+\kappa)_{+}^{\gamma-1}{\,\,\rm d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s.

Proof
.

First, note that the following function is well-defined

f(\varsigma):=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u+\varsigma)_{+}^{\gamma-1}{\,\,\rm d}s,

because
u\in L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
due to the trace theorem. Moreover,
f
is (strictly) increasing since for any
\kappa_{1}<\kappa_{2}
such that
f(\kappa_{1})>0
, we have

f(\kappa_{1})=\int_{\Gamma_{D}\cap\{u+\kappa_{1}>0\}}(u+\kappa_{1})^{\gamma-1}{\,\,\rm d}s<\int_{\Gamma_{D}\cap\{u+\kappa_{2}>0\}}(u+\kappa_{2})^{\gamma-1}{\,\,\rm d}s=f(\kappa_{2}).

Therefore,
there is at most one value

\kappa
for which (
5.9
) holds.

To establish the existence of
\kappa
, we first note that Assumption
D.2
implies
\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s>0
. Thus, it suffices to show that

(0,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb{R}).

By the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{\varsigma\to-\infty}f(\varsigma)=0.

Furthermore,
since
|\Gamma_{D}|>0
and
u\in L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
,
Chebyshev’s
inequality implies that

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{|u|>\varsigma/2\})\to 0
, so
f(\varsigma)\geq(\varsigma/2)^{\gamma-1}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{|u|\leq\varsigma/2\})\to\infty
.
Accordingly,
we have

\lim_{\varsigma\to\infty}f(\varsigma)=+\infty.

Moreover, for any
M>0
and all
\varsigma\in[-M,M]
, we have

(u+\varsigma)_{+}^{\gamma-1}\leq(|u|+M)^{\gamma-1}\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{D}),

since
u\in L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
by the trace theorem. Therefore,
f
is continuous by the dominated convergence theorem.
Consequently, by the intermediate value theorem,
f(\varsigma)
attains all values in
(0,\infty)
; hence, there exists a solution
\kappa
to (
5.9
).
∎

This lemma allows us to assign a unique representative
u^{*}=u+\kappa
to each
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{Q}
, where
\kappa
is the unique constant satisfying

(5.10)

\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h_{u^{*}}{\,\,\rm d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u+\kappa)_{+}^{\gamma-1}{\,\,\rm d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s.

Lastly, we note that by Corollary
5.2
, the density
m_{u}
depends only on
Du
. Since
u^{*}=u+\kappa
differs from
u
only by a constant, we have
Du^{*}=Du
, and consequently
m_{u^{*}}=m_{u}
. Thus, the modification of the domain affects only the boundary flux
h_{u^{*}}
, while the interior transport density remains invariant.
This justifies defining the modified operator
\bar{B}:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
such that for any
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in\mathcal{Q}
,

(5.11)

\langle\bar{B}\bar{u},\bar{v}\rangle:=\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m_{u}\\
u^{*}\\
h_{u^{*}}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}0\\
v\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle,

where
v
is an arbitrary representative of
\bar{v}
, and
(m_{u},u^{*},h_{u^{*}})
is the compatible triplet derived from
u
.

The operator
\bar{B}
is well-defined. The compatible triplet depends only on the class
\bar{u}
.
Note that
m_{u}=m_{u^{*}}
so either can be used in the definition of
\bar{B}
.
The compatibility condition (
5.10
) guarantees that the pairing is independent of the representative
v
. Indeed, for any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left\langle A\begin{bmatrix}m_{u}\\
u^{*}\\
h_{u^{*}}\end{bmatrix}\,,\,\begin{bmatrix}0\\
c\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle=c\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h_{u^{*}}{\,\,\rm d}s\right)=0.

5.4
The key properties of the modified operator

In this subsection, we verify the monotonicity, hemicontinuity, and coercivity properties of
\bar{B}
. We begin with the following proposition on the monotonicity of
\bar{B}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
and
S
hold. Then, the operator
\bar{B}
defined by (
5.11
) is monotone.

Proof
.

Let
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in\mathcal{Q}
be arbitrary. Let
u
and
v
denote arbitrary representatives, and let
u^{*}
and
v^{*}
denote their compatible representatives according to (
5.10
).
By the definition of
\bar{B}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\bar{B}\bar{u}-\bar{B}\bar{v},\bar{u}-\bar{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(m_{u}D_{p}H(x,Du)-m_{v}D_{p}H(x,Dv))\cdot D(u-v){\,\,\rm d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{v^{*}})(u-v){\,\,\rm d}s.

For the first term, recall that
m_{u}=g^{-1}(H(x,Du))
. Let
G
be the primitive of
g^{-1}
such that
G(0)=0
. Since
g^{-1}
is non-decreasing,
G
is convex and non-decreasing. Since
H
is convex, the composition
\Phi(p):=G(H(x,p))
is convex.
Observing that
m_{u}D_{p}H(x,Du)=D_{p}\Phi(Du)
, the monotonicity of the gradient of the convex function
\Phi
implies that the first integral is non-negative.
For the boundary term, let
c=(u-u^{*})-(v-v^{*})
. This is a constant. By the compatibility condition (
5.10
), we have
\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{v^{*}}){\,\,\rm d}s=0
, so

\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{v^{*}})(u-v){\,\,\rm d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{v^{*}})(u^{*}-v^{*}){\,\,\rm d}s+c\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{v^{*}}){\,\,\rm d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{v^{*}})(u^{*}-v^{*}){\,\,\rm d}s.

Since
h_{w}=w_{+}^{\gamma-1}
is non-decreasing in
w
, the remaining integral is non-negative.
∎

The proof of hemicontinuity of the operator
\bar{B}
requires us to establish the following lemma on the continuity of the compatibility constant
\kappa
, arising from Lemma
5.5
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
hold.
Let
\bar{u}_{0},\bar{u}_{1}\in\mathcal{Q}
be arbitrary, fix representatives
u_{0}\in\bar{u}_{0}
and
u_{1}\in\bar{u}_{1}
, and set
u_{\vartheta}:=(1-\vartheta)u_{0}+\vartheta u_{1}
. Define
\bar{u}_{\vartheta}=(1-\vartheta)\bar{u}_{0}+\vartheta\bar{u}_{1}
for every
\vartheta\in[0,1]
.
Furthermore, let

u^{*}_{\vartheta}\quad\text{ and }\quad\kappa_{\vartheta}:=u_{\vartheta}^{*}-u_{\vartheta}

respectively denote the compatible representative and the corresponding compatibility constant arising from Lemma
5.5
.
Then,

\vartheta\mapsto u_{\vartheta}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\vartheta\mapsto\kappa_{\vartheta}

are continuous for all
\vartheta\in[0,1]
.

Proof
.

Fix
J:=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,{\,\,\rm d}s>0
. Define

\Phi(\varsigma,\vartheta):=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u_{\vartheta}+\varsigma)_{+}^{\gamma-1}\,{\,\,\rm d}s,\qquad(\varsigma,\vartheta)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1].

For each fixed
\varsigma
, the map
\vartheta\mapsto\Phi(\varsigma,\vartheta)
is continuous by the dominated convergence theorem, since
u_{\vartheta}(x)=(1-\vartheta)u_{0}(x)+\vartheta u_{1}(x)
converges pointwise and

(u_{\vartheta}+\varsigma)_{+}^{\gamma-1}\leq C\bigl(|u_{0}|+|u_{1}|+|\varsigma|\bigr)^{\gamma-1}\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{D})
.
For each fixed
\vartheta
, the map
\varsigma\mapsto\Phi(\varsigma,\vartheta)
is continuous and nondecreasing, with

\lim_{\varsigma\to-\infty}\Phi(\varsigma,\vartheta)=0
and
\lim_{\varsigma\to+\infty}\Phi(\varsigma,\vartheta)=+\infty
; hence, by Lemma
5.5
, there exists a unique
\kappa_{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Phi(\kappa_{\vartheta},\vartheta)=J
.

To prove continuity of
\vartheta\mapsto\kappa_{\vartheta}
, fix
\vartheta_{0}\in[0,1]
.
Choose
M>0
such that
\Phi(-M,\vartheta_{0})<J<\Phi(M,\vartheta_{0})
. By continuity of
\vartheta\mapsto\Phi(\pm M,\vartheta)
, the same inequalities hold for all
\vartheta
sufficiently close to
\vartheta_{0}
; hence
\kappa_{\vartheta}\in[-M,M]
locally.
If
\vartheta_{n}\to\vartheta_{0}
, extract a subsequence (not relabeled) with
\kappa_{\vartheta_{n}}\to\kappa_{*}\in[-M,M]
. Then, by continuity of
\Phi
,

J=\Phi(\kappa_{\vartheta_{n}},\vartheta_{n})\to\Phi(\kappa_{*},\vartheta_{0}).

By uniqueness at
\vartheta_{0}
,
\kappa_{*}=\kappa_{\vartheta_{0}}
. Therefore
\kappa_{\vartheta_{n}}\to\kappa_{\vartheta_{0}}
, and
\vartheta\mapsto\kappa_{\vartheta}
is continuous. Finally,
u_{\vartheta}^{*}=u_{\vartheta}+\kappa_{\vartheta}
is continuous as a sum of continuous maps.
∎

The following result is a direct corollary of Lemma
5.7
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
hold. Furthermore, let
\bar{u}_{0},\bar{u}_{1}\in\mathcal{Q}
be arbitrary, and let
\kappa_{\vartheta}
denote the compatibility constant defined in Lemma

. Then, there exists a constant

, independent of

, such that

|\kappa_{\vartheta}|\leq C.

Proof
.

Due to Lemma

,

is a continuous map on a compact set; hence, it is bounded.
∎

With the continuity of the compatibility constant established, we now prove the hemicontinuity of the operator
\bar{B}
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
and
S
hold. Then, the operator
\bar{B}
is hemicontinuous. More specifically, let
\bar{u}_{0},\bar{u}_{1},\bar{v}\in\mathcal{Q}
be fixed. Then, the map

\vartheta\mapsto\left\langle\bar{B}[(1-\vartheta)\bar{u}_{0}+\vartheta\bar{u}_{1}]\,,\,\bar{v}\right\rangle

is continuous for all
\vartheta\in[0,1]
.

Proof
.

Let
\bar{u}_{0},\bar{u}_{1}\in\mathcal{Q}
and define

\bar{u}_{\vartheta}=(1-\vartheta)\bar{u}_{0}+\vartheta\bar{u}_{1}\quad\text{for }\vartheta\in[0,1],

and let
u^{*}_{\vartheta}
and
\kappa_{\vartheta}:=u_{\vartheta}^{*}-u_{\vartheta}
be defined as before.
Fix
\bar{v}\in\mathcal{Q}
, and let
v\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
be an arbitrary representative of
\bar{v}
.
Define the map

\varpi(\vartheta):=\left\langle\bar{B}\bar{u}_{\vartheta}\,,\,\bar{v}\right\rangle.

Due to Corollary
5.2
and Lemma
2.16
, we have

\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}r>{\displaystyle}r>{\displaystyle}l}m_{u_{\vartheta}}\left|D_{p}H(x,Du_{\vartheta}(x))\right|=&G^{\prime}(H(x,Du_{\vartheta}(x)))\left|D_{p}H(x,Du_{\vartheta})\right|\par\\[2.5pt]
&\leq C\left(|Du_{1}|^{\gamma-1}+|Du_{0}|^{\gamma-1}+1\right)&\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega)\end{array}

for all
\vartheta\in[0,1]
. In addition, Corollary
5.8
and some basic inequalities give us that

\begin{split}h_{u_{\vartheta}^{*}}&=(u_{\vartheta}+\kappa_{\vartheta})_{+}^{\gamma-1}\leq C((u_{0})_{+}^{\gamma-1}+(u_{1})_{+}^{\gamma-1}+1)\end{split}

for some
C>1
, independent of
\vartheta
.
Expanding the definition of
\bar{B}
, we have

\varpi(\vartheta)=\int_{\Omega}m_{u_{\vartheta}}D_{p}H(x,Du_{\vartheta})\cdot Dv{\,\,\rm d}x-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}jv{\,\,\rm d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h_{u_{\vartheta}^{*}}v{\,\,\rm d}s.

Pointwise continuity (a.e. in
\Omega
and on
\Gamma_{D}
) in
\vartheta
follows from the continuity of
p\mapsto H(x,p)
,
p\mapsto D_{p}H(x,p)
, and of
g^{-1}
, together with Lemma
5.7
.
The uniform bounds above provide integrable majorants, so the dominated convergence theorem yields the continuity of
\varpi
on
[0,1]
.
∎

The last result in this subsection is the coercivity of the operator
\bar{B}
, which we establish in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.10
.

Suppose that Assumptions
D
and
S
hold. Then,

\frac{1}{\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}\langle\bar{B}\bar{u}-\bar{B}\bar{0},\bar{u}\rangle\to\infty\quad\text{ as }\quad\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}\to\infty.

Proof
.

First, note that

m_{0}=g^{-1}(H(x,0)),\quad 0^{*}=\left(\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{D}|}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s\right)^{\gamma^{\prime}-1},\enskip\text{ and }\enskip h_{0^{*}}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{D}|}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j{\,\,\rm d}s.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\langle\bar{B}\bar{u}-\bar{B}\bar{0},\bar{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(m_{u}D_{p}H(x,Du)-m_{0}D_{p}H(x,0)\right)\cdot Du{\,\,\rm d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{0^{*}})u{\,\,\rm d}s.

Due to Corollary
2.13
and Assumption
S.5
, we have the lower bounds

m_{u}=g^{-1}(H(x,Du))\geq C^{-1}|Du|^{\alpha/\beta}-C,\qquad D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot Du\geq C^{-1}|Du|^{\alpha}-C.

Consequently,

m_{u}D_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot Du\geq\frac{1}{C}|Du|^{\gamma}-C,\qquad\gamma=\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}.

Moreover, since
m_{0}D_{p}H(x,0)\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, Hölder and Young yield

\left|\int_{\Omega}m_{0}D_{p}H(x,0)\cdot Du\,{\,\,\rm d}x\right|\leq\|m_{0}D_{p}H(x,0)\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2C}\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}+C.

Since
\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h_{u^{*}}\,ds=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,ds=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h_{0^{*}}\,ds
,
we have
\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{0^{*}})\,ds=0
. Hence

\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{0^{*}})u\,ds=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(h_{u^{*}}-h_{0^{*}})(u^{*}-0^{*})\,ds.

Because
h_{w}=w_{+}^{\gamma-1}
and
s\mapsto s_{+}^{\gamma-1}
is monotone,
the integrand is nonnegative a.e., and therefore the integral is
\geq 0
.

Combining these estimates and using
\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}=\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}
, we obtain

\langle\bar{B}\bar{u}-\bar{B}\bar{0},\bar{u}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{C}\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\gamma}-C.

We conclude the proof by dividing both sides by
\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}
, and taking the limit
\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}\to\infty
.
∎

6
Passage to the Limit and Recovery of the MFG System

In this section, we establish the existence of solutions to MFG System
1
by passing to the limit
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
in the family of solutions to the penalized variational inequalities associated with
A_{\epsilon}
. This requires uniform a priori estimates as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
. In what follows,
(m_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon},h_{\epsilon})
denotes a solution (whose existence is ensured by Theorem
5.13
) to the penalized variational inequality

(6.1)

\left\langle A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m_{\epsilon}\\
u_{\epsilon}\\
h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m_{\epsilon}\\
v-u_{\epsilon}\\
k-h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0\qquad\text{for all }(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}^{+}.

As in the proof of Corollary
5.2
,
testing (
6.1
) with
(\mu,v,k)=(\mu,u_{\epsilon},h_{\epsilon})
and varying
\mu\geq 0
,
and with
(\mu,v,k)=(m_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon},k)
and varying
k\geq 0
, yields

m_{\epsilon}=g^{-1}(H(x,Du_{\epsilon}))\quad\text{a.e.\ in }\Omega,\qquad h_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-\gamma}(u_{\epsilon})_{+}^{\gamma-1}\quad\text{a.e.\ on }\Gamma_{D}.

Remark 6.1
.

Testing (
6.1
) with
(\mu,v,k)=(2m_{\epsilon},2u_{\epsilon},2h_{\epsilon})
and with
(\mu,v,k)=(0,0,0)
, we obtain

\left\langle A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m_{\epsilon}\\
u_{\epsilon}\\
h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}m_{\epsilon}\\
u_{\epsilon}\\
h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle=0.

We recall two Poincaré-type inequalities with boundary terms. First, recall that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Gamma_{D})>0
.
Then for any
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\Omega
,
\Gamma_{D}
, and
\gamma
, such that

(6.2)

\|u\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})}\right).

The second version of the Poincaré inequality anchored by the inflow is stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose Assumption
D.2
holds and that

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Gamma_{N})>0
. Let
J_{\mathrm{in}}:=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,{\,\,\rm d}s
.
Then there exists
C>0
, depending only on
\Omega
,
\gamma
,
J_{\mathrm{in}}
, and
\|j\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{N})}
, such that for every
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C\Bigl(\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,u{\,\,\rm d}s\Big|\Bigr).

Proof
.

By Assumption
D.2
and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Gamma_{N})>0
, since
j\not\equiv 0
and
j\geq 0
, we have
J_{\mathrm{in}}>0
.
To prove the inequality in the lemma, we argue by contradiction. Suppose the claim fails. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
u_{n}\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
such that

\|u_{n}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\|Du_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+\left|\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,u_{n}{\,\,\rm d}s\right|\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Let
c_{n}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u_{n}{\,\,\rm d}x
. By the standard Poincaré inequality on
\Omega
,

\|u_{n}-c_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\|Du_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

and, hence,
u_{n}-c_{n}\to 0
in
W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
(since also
\|D(u_{n}-c_{n})\|_{L^{\gamma}}=\|Du_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}}\to 0
).
By the trace theorem, it follows that

\|u_{n}-c_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{N})}\to 0.

Recalling that
J>0
, we have

|c_{n}|\,J_{\mathrm{in}}=\left|\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,c_{n}{\,\,\rm d}s\right|\leq\left|\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,u_{n}{\,\,\rm d}s\right|+\left|\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,(u_{n}-c_{n}){\,\,\rm d}s\right|\leq\left|\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,u_{n}{\,\,\rm d}s\right|+\|j\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{N})}\|u_{n}-c_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{N})}\to 0,

so
c_{n}\to 0
. Therefore,

\|u_{n}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{n}-c_{n}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}+\|c_{n}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}=\|u_{n}-c_{n}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}+\|c_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\to 0,

which contradicts
\|u_{n}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}=1
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Our first result is a general estimate that connects the integral information on the Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}
to the integral information on the Dirichlet boundary
\Gamma_{D}
and the interior of
\Omega
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose Assumptions
D
and
S
hold. Let
\delta>0
and let
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
. Then, there is a constant
C_{\delta}>1
, independent of
u
, such that

\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju{\,\,\rm d}s\leq\delta\,\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}+\|u_{+}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})}+C_{\delta}.

Proof
.

Since
j\geq 0
, we have

\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju{\,\,\rm d}s\leq\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju_{+}{\,\,\rm d}s\leq\|j\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{N})}\,\|u_{+}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{N})}

by Hölder’s inequality.
By the Trace Theorem and the Poincaré inequality (
6.2
) applied to
u_{+}\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
,

(6.3)

\|u_{+}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{N})}\leq C\|u_{+}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C\Bigl(\|Du_{+}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+\|u_{+}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})}\Bigr).

Using
\|Du_{+}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}
and Young’s inequality, for any
\delta>0
we obtain

\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju{\,\,\rm d}s\leq\delta\,\|Du\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}+\|u_{+}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})}^{\gamma}+C_{\delta},

for a constant
C_{\delta}>1
independent of
u
.
∎

Next, we establish the following estimate, which gives a bound on the growth of the solution as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Under Assumptions
D
and
S
, let
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
. Let
(m_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon},h_{\epsilon})\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
be a solution to (
6.1
).
Then, there is a constant
C>0
, independent of
\epsilon
, such that

\epsilon^{-\gamma}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u_{\epsilon})_{+}^{\gamma}{\,\,\rm d}s+\|Du_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}\leq C.

Proof
.

To simplify the notation,
(m,u,h)=(m_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon},h_{\epsilon})
. By Remark
6.1
and the definition of the pairing (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\langle A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}m\\
u\\
h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle

(6.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}m\bigl(g(m)-H(x,Du)\bigr){\,\,\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}mD_{p}H(x,Du)\cdot Du{\,\,\rm d}x-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}ju{\,\,\rm d}s+\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}}{\,\,\rm d}s.

Note that the boundary terms

and
\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(-u)h\,{\,\,\rm d}s

\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}h^{\gamma^{\prime}}\,{\,\,\rm d}s

5.2

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose Assumptions
S
and

Finally, we combine the preceding results to establish the existence theorem for MFG System

from the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, and let
(m_{\epsilon},u_{\epsilon},h_{\epsilon})\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
be a solution of (
6.1
).

Step 1: Uniform bounds and weak compactness.

From Proposition
6.4
, we have

(6.8)

\|Du_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}+\epsilon^{-\gamma}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u_{\epsilon})_{+}^{\gamma}{\,\,\rm d}s\leq C.

In particular,
\|Du_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C
.
Moreover,
\|(u_{\epsilon})_{+}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})}^{\gamma}\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma}\to 0
.

Using Corollary
2.13
and Corollary
5.2
, we have
m_{\epsilon}=g^{-1}(H(x,Du_{\epsilon}))
and therefore

\|m_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}\leq C.

Moreover, by Assumption
S.4
and the same Young-inequality argument used in Proposition
3.3
(transport-flux estimate), we have

(6.9)

\|m_{\epsilon}D_{p}H(x,Du_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\gamma^{\prime}}\leq C\Bigl(\|m_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}^{\beta+1}+\|Du_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}+1\Bigr).

In particular, combining (
6.8
) with
\|m_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)}\leq C
, we obtain

\|m_{\epsilon}D_{p}H(x,Du_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C.

By Lemma
6.5
and the normal-trace estimate (see
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.8),

\|h_{\epsilon}\|_{W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}\leq C\|m_{\epsilon}D_{p}H(x,Du_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C.

Finally, by Lemma
6.2
we have

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C\Bigl(\|Du_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,u_{\epsilon}{\,\,\rm d}s\Big|\Bigr).

Using (
6.5
), Hölder’s inequality, and (
6.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{N}}j\,u_{\epsilon}{\,\,\rm d}s\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}m_{\epsilon}D_{p}H(x,Du_{\epsilon})\cdot Du_{\epsilon}{\,\,\rm d}x+\epsilon^{-\gamma}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u_{\epsilon})_{+}^{\gamma}{\,\,\rm d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|m_{\epsilon}D_{p}H(x,Du_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|Du_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+C\leq C.

Therefore,

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C.

Weak compactness in
W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
implies that there exists
u\in W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
and a subsequence such that

u_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
.
By continuity of the trace operator,
u_{\epsilon}|_{\Gamma}\rightharpoonup u|_{\Gamma}
in
W^{1-\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma}(\Gamma)
(and hence in
L^{\gamma}(\Gamma)
), in particular on
\Gamma_{N}
and
\Gamma_{D}
.
Since the functional
w\mapsto\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(w_{+})^{\gamma}\,{\,\,\rm d}s
is convex and weakly lower semicontinuous on
L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
,

\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u_{+})^{\gamma}\,{\,\,\rm d}s\leq\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}((u_{\epsilon})_{+})^{\gamma}\,{\,\,\rm d}s=0.

Hence,
u\leq 0
a.e. on
\Gamma_{D}
.

To summarize, the preceding bounds imply there exists a subsequence (not relabeled) and
(m,u,h)
such that

m_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup m\ \text{ in }L^{\beta+1}(\Omega),\qquad u_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup u\ \text{ in }W^{1,\gamma}(\Omega),\qquad h_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup h\ \text{ in }W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D}).

Since
m_{\epsilon}\geq 0
and
m_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup m
in
L^{\beta+1}(\Omega)
, we have
m\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Similarly,
since
h_{\epsilon}\geq 0
and
h_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup h
in
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
,
we have
\langle h,\phi\rangle\geq 0
for all
\phi\geq 0
by weak convergence. Thus,
h\geq 0
in the sense of distributions on

\Gamma_{D}
.

Step 2: Passage to the limit (Minty formulation).

Fix
(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}^{+}
. By monotonicity of
A_{\epsilon}
,

\left\langle A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix}-A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}m_{\epsilon}\\
u_{\epsilon}\\
h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m_{\epsilon}\\
v-u_{\epsilon}\\
k-h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0.

Combining this with (
6.1
) yields

\left\langle A_{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m_{\epsilon}\\
v-u_{\epsilon}\\
k-h_{\epsilon}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0.

Expanding the pairing,
letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, and using weak convergence,
we obtain

\left\langle A_{0}\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m\\
v-u\\
k-h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}k^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}(k-h_{\epsilon}){\,\,\rm d}s\geq 0.

Here boundary terms involving
h_{\epsilon}
are interpreted via the duality pairing

W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})\times W^{1-\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
,
and we use
h_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup h
in
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
.
The last term
vanishes in the limit. Indeed, recalling that
h_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-\gamma}(u_{\epsilon})_{+}^{\gamma-1}
, we estimate the
L^{\gamma^{\prime}}
norm of
h_{\epsilon}
:

\|h_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}^{\gamma^{\prime}}=\int_{\Gamma_{D}}\epsilon^{-\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}(u_{\epsilon})_{+}^{(\gamma-1)\gamma^{\prime}}{\,\,\rm d}s=\epsilon^{-\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u_{\epsilon})_{+}^{\gamma}{\,\,\rm d}s,

where we used that
(\gamma-1)\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma
. Using the uniform bound from Proposition
6.4
, we have
\int_{\Gamma_{D}}(u_{\epsilon})_{+}^{\gamma}{\,\,\rm d}s\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma}
. Thus,

\|h_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}\leq\left(C\epsilon^{-\gamma\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma}\right)^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}=C^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}\epsilon^{-\gamma+\gamma/\gamma^{\prime}}=C^{\prime}\epsilon^{-1}.

We can now bound the penalty term using Hölder’s inequality. Since
k\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
, we have
k^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}\in L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
, and

\left|\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}k^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}h_{\epsilon}{\,\,\rm d}s\right|\leq\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}\|k^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})}\|h_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}\epsilon^{-1}=C^{\prime\prime}\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}-1}.

Since
\gamma>1
implies
\gamma^{\prime}>1
, the exponent
\gamma^{\prime}-1
is positive, and the term converges to
0
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
. Since
\epsilon^{\gamma^{\prime}}\int_{\Gamma_{D}}k^{\gamma^{\prime}}{\,\,\rm d}s
clearly vanishes, the entire limit term is zero.

Hence,

\left\langle A_{0}\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
v\\
k\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mu-m\\
v-u\\
k-h\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}^{+},

where we identify
k\in L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
with an element of
W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
via the natural embedding, so that
k-h\in W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
is well-defined and paired against traces in
W^{1-\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma}(\Gamma_{D})
.

Step 3: Conclusion.

Since
A_{0}
is monotone and hemicontinuous (by the same argument as for
A_{\epsilon}
) on the convex cone of admissible triples (allowing
h\in W^{-1+\frac{1}{\gamma},\gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
),
the Minty characterization of variational inequality solutions implies that the limit triplet
(m,u,h)
satisfies

\langle A_{0}(m,u,h),(\mu-m,v-u,k-h)\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall(\mu,v,k)\in\mathcal{X}^{+},

which is exactly the statement of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

7
Conclusion

We proved the existence of weak solutions for separable first-order MFGs with mixed boundary conditions via a monotone operator formulation.
To this end, we introduced an auxiliary boundary variable
h
to encode the exit flux, enabling the formulation of the MFG system as a variational inequality on a convex domain. Then, we identified the failure of coercivity in the
u
-variable and resolved it through a quotient-space formulation that eliminates the constant-shift degeneracy. Finally, we established the existence of weak solutions by applying the Browder–Minty theorem to a family of penalized operators and passing to the limit.

The separable structure
\mathcal{H}(x,p,m)=H(x,p)-g(m)
was essential to our approach: the strict monotonicity of
g
allowed us to define the density explicitly as
m=g^{-1}(H(x,Du))
, which in turn enabled the reduction to the quotient-space operator
\bar{B}
.

For non-separable Hamiltonians
\mathcal{H}(x,p,m)
, one may still define the density implicitly via the relation
\mathcal{H}(x,Du,m)=0
, yielding
m=m(x,Du)
under appropriate monotonicity conditions on
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
m
. However, this implicit formulation requires a substantially different structure for the assumptions—in particular, joint conditions on the growth and monotonicity of
\mathcal{H}
in both
p
and
m
—and leads to different analytical challenges in establishing the requisite bounds. These are addressed in a forthcoming companion paper.
