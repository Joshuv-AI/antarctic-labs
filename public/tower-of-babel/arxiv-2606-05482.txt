Introduction

In quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) methods, the accuracy of numerical integration hinges on how uniformly sample points cover the domain, in the most basic case the unit cube
[0,1)^{d}
. Evenly distributed point sets and sequences minimize clustering and gaps, producing more balanced coverage than pseudorandom points and thereby reducing the integration error. This is usually formalized by a Koksma-Hlawka-type inequality, which bounds the error by the product of a measure for the variation of the integrand (in the classical case, the variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause) and a suitable corresponding notion of discrepancy (in the classical case, the star-discrepancy), which measures the irregularity of distribution of a point set. Introductions to the QMC method are, e.g., provided by the books
[
12
,
7
,
11
,
26
,
33
]
or the survey
[
8
]
. Nowadays, there are various notions of (geometric) discrepancies, which in many cases appear in “integration-discrepancy dualities” as the worst-case errors of QMC rules in corresponding function spaces (see
[
31
, Chapter 9]
or
[
30
, Section 4]
). In the present paper we consider the problem of numerical integration of periodic functions. Naturally, in this case, the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy is our preferred quality criterion for the underlying sample points. This kind of discrepancy has received increasing attention during the last decade, see, e.g.,
[
18
,
20
,
21
,
25
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
36
]
.

The periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy is a quantitative measure for the irregularity of distribution of point sets and sequences modulo 1 which uses periodic rectangles as test sets. These are defined as follows. For
x,y\in[0,1]
put

B(x,y)\coloneqq\begin{cases}[x,y)&\text{if $x\leq y$},\\
[0,y)\cup[x,1)&\text{if $x>y$,}\end{cases}

and for
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
and
{\boldsymbol{y}}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})
in
[0,1]^{d}
we set
B({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\coloneqq B(x_{1},y_{1})\times\dots\times B(x_{d},y_{d})
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{d,N}=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0},\dots,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{N-1}\}
be an
N
-point set in
[0,1)^{d}
(which will be identified with the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
in the following). Then the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy of
\mathcal{P}_{d,N}
is defined as

L_{2,N}^{\mathrm{per}}(\mathcal{P}_{d,N})\coloneqq\left(\int_{[0,1]^{2d}}\left|\frac{\#(\mathcal{P}_{d,N}\cap B({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}))}{N}-\lambda(B({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}))\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\right)^{1/2},

where
\#(\cdot)
denotes the number of points in a set and
\lambda
the Lebesgue (uniform) measure of a set. For an infinite sequence
\mathcal{S}_{d}=({\boldsymbol{x}}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
let
\mathcal{S}_{d,N}
denote the set
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{N-1}\}
consisting of the initial
N
elements of the sequence and define

L_{2,N}^{\mathrm{per}}(\mathcal{S}_{d})\coloneqq L_{2,N}^{\mathrm{per}}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N}).

The periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy can be expressed in terms of exponential sums. For
\mathcal{P}_{d,N}
as above we have

L_{2,N}^{{\rm per}}(\mathcal{P}_{d,N})=\left(\frac{1}{3^{d}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{h}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\}}\frac{1}{r({\boldsymbol{h}})^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}{\boldsymbol{h}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}_{n})\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(1)

where
\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}
, where “
\cdot
” denotes the usual Euclidean inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and where for
{\boldsymbol{h}}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
we set

r({\boldsymbol{h}})\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d}r(h_{j})\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ r(h_{j})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if $h_{j}=0$},\\
\frac{2\pi|h_{j}|}{\sqrt{6}}&\mbox{ if $h_{j}\not=0$.}\end{array}\right.

For a proof of this relation, see
[
27
, Theorem 1]
.

Equation (
1
) shows that the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy is—up to a multiplicative factor depending on
d
—asymptotically equivalent to the diaphony. Diaphony is another quantitative measure for the irregularity of distribution which has been introduced by Zinterhof
[
45
]
in 1976 (see also
[
12
]
). For a finite set
\mathcal{P}_{d,N}
as above the diaphony is defined as

F_{N}(\mathcal{P}_{d,N})\coloneqq\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{h}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\}}\frac{1}{\rho({\boldsymbol{h}})^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}{\boldsymbol{h}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}_{n})\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
\rho({\boldsymbol{h}})\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d}\max(1,|h_{j}|)
for
{\boldsymbol{h}}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. For an infinite sequence
\mathcal{S}_{d}
the diaphony
F_{N}(\mathcal{S}_{d})
is the diaphony of the initial
N
elements of
\mathcal{S}_{d}
, i.e.,

F_{N}(\mathcal{S}_{d})\coloneqq F_{N}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N})\quad\mbox{for all }N\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We express the fact that periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy and diaphony are asymptotically equivalent up to a factor depending only on
d
in the form

L_{2,N}^{{\rm per}}(\mathcal{P}_{d,N})\asymp_{d}F_{N}(\mathcal{P}_{d,N}).

As a consequence, the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy can be understood as a geometrical interpretation of the diaphony, which is of analytic nature. From this point of view, the results on periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy presented in this paper also directly apply to the diaphony and vice versa.

The periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy/diaphony is also closely related to the worst-case error of QMC rules in suitable spaces of smooth periodic functions over
[0,1]^{d}
, see equation (
6
) in Section
2
. This is a major motivation for the study of the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy/diaphony and will be discussed in more detail in Section
2
.

We pause briefly to explain important notation that is used throughout this work. For functions
f,g:D\subseteq\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
g\geq 0
the notation
f(N)\lesssim g(N)
means that there exists some
C>0
such that
f(N)\leq Cg(N)
for all
N\in D
. If we want to stress that
C
depends on some parameters, say
a,b
, then this is indicated by writing
f(N)\lesssim_{a,b}g(N)
. If in addition also
f\geq 0
and if we have
f(N)\lesssim g(N)
and
g(N)\lesssim f(N)
, then we write
f(N)\asymp g(N)
and similarly
f(N)\asymp_{a,b}g(N)
if we want to emphasize the dependence on additional parameters
a,b
, etc.

The periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy/diaphony of
finite
point sets has been studied in multiple papers, for example in
[
18
,
20
,
19
,
25
]
. In the present work we are interested in the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy/diaphony of
infinite
sequences (in dimension
d
). It is known that for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a quantity
c_{d}>0
such that for any sequence
\mathcal{S}_{d}
in
[0,1)^{d}
we have

L_{2,N}^{{\rm per}}(\mathcal{S}_{d})\geq c_{d}\,\frac{(\log N)^{d/2}}{N}\qquad\mbox{for infinitely many }N\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}.

(2)

This has been shown first in the context of diaphony by Proĭnov
[
37
]
(this work is only available in Bulgarian). A discussion of Proĭnov’s work can be found in
[
23
]
. Later, a direct proof in terms of the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy has been provided in
[
24
]
.

On the other hand, for every
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists an infinite sequence
\mathcal{S}_{d}
in
[0,1)^{d}
such that

L_{2,N}^{{\rm per}}(\mathcal{S}_{d})\lesssim_{d}\,\frac{(\log N)^{d/2}}{N}\qquad\mbox{for all }N\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}.

(3)

Combining the two equations (
2
) and (
3
) shows that

(\log N)^{d/2}/N
is the exact asymptotic order of magnitude in
N
of the optimal periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy/diaphony for infinite sequences in dimension
d
.

One-dimensional infinite sequences whose periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy satisfies the bound (
3
) are given in, e.g.,
[
3
,
16
,
35
,
38
,
39
]
. These constructions comprise the well-known van der Corput sequence as shown by Proĭnov and Grozdanov
[
39
]
.

For arbitrary
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
the upper bound (
3
) is obtained by the means of order-5 digital sequences over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, as shown in
[
36
]
. The construction of an order-5 digital sequence is based on an ordinary digital sequence in the sense of Niederreiter
[
32
]
in dimension
d^{\prime}=5d
via a procedure called interlacing (see Section
3
). This is a major disadvantage of these sequences and makes the practical application of the existing result infeasible in higher dimensions. A remedy would be to reduce the order parameter, which can be seen as a complexity reduction in the construction of the sequences and, therefore, in the whole method. In fact, already in
[
36
]
it is conjectured that the optimal order (
3
) of periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy/diaphony of infinite sequences in dimension
d
is achieved by means of order-2 (instead of order-5) digital sequences. In this way, the dimension of the underlying ordinary digital sequences is reduced from
d^{\prime}=5d
to
d^{\prime}=2d
, which represents a significant improvement in terms of practicality.

In this paper, we shall establish our result in a much broader framework and study QMC integration of multivariate periodic functions by means of infinite sequences. A QMC rule approximates the integral of a function
f:\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
using only a finite set of sample nodes
{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
\#{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}=N
, by means of

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}).

The quality of this approximation for functions
f
in a Banach space
F\subseteq\{f:\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\}
of test functions with norm
\|\cdot\|_{F}
depends on the set of sample nodes and can be quantified via the worst-case error

{\rm wce}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N},F)\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in F\\
\|f\|_{F}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})\right|.

The underlying function space setting for our analysis is based on Besov spaces with dominating mixed smoothness,
S_{p,q}^{r}B(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, with smoothness parameter
r\in(1/p,2)
. The necessary definitions will be given in Section
2
. In our main result, which is Theorem
12
in Section
4
, we show that QMC rules based on infinite order-2 digital sequences achieve a convergence rate of the worst-case error of order
N^{-\min(r,1)}(\log N)^{(d-1)(1-1/q)}
if
r\not=1
and
N^{-1}(\log N)^{d(1-1/q)}
if
r=1
(this case comprises the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy/diaphony as a special case; see Corollary
14
, also in Section
4
) for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
. The fundamentals of digital sequences necessary for this article are explained in Section
3
. Our result can be seen as the “infinite sequence variant” of the result for finite point sets in
[
19
]
. Rules based on infinite sequences have the advantage that they are extensible in the number of integration nodes
N
. This means that in order to increase
N
in a QMC rule, one only needs to evaluate the integrand at the additional nodes, and there is no need to discard previous function evaluations. QMC rules that are extensible in
N
in this sense are sometimes referred to as rules of “open type”; see, e.g.,
[
8
, Section 2.3]
.

2
Besov spaces and the Faber basis

In this section we collect basic definitions and results from
[
19
]
regarding Besov spaces and the Faber basis. The definitions require extensive explanations; we try to minimize the necessary effort while ensuring proper understanding.

For the analysis we will use function spaces based on integrability and regularity assumptions on the set
F
of test functions. Concretely, for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
consider the Besov space
S^{r}_{p,q}B(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of dominating mixed smoothness
r
. These spaces and their respective norms
\|\cdot\|_{S_{p,q}^{r}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}
can be defined via dyadic decompositions of the Fourier spectrum or via decay conditions on moduli of continuity, see
[
19
]
for details. Relevantly for us, in the range
r\in(1/p,2)
functions from these spaces fulfill a decay condition when represented in a certain basis. We present this characterization in greater detail. In what follows, let
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\coloneqq\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}
be the nonnegative integers and
{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}\coloneqq\{-1\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

Consider the hat function

v(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1-|2x-1|&\mbox{if }x\in[0,1],\\
0&\text{else,}\end{cases}

for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The Faber basis (also known as hierarchical basis) is given by

v_{-1,0}(x)\coloneqq 1\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad v_{j,k}(x)\coloneqq v(2^{j}x-k)

for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
k\in{\mathbb{D}}_{j}\coloneqq\{0,1,\dots,2^{j}-1\}
, where for notational convenience we set
{\mathbb{D}}_{-1}\coloneqq\{0\}
.

We also require second-order differences. For univariate functions
f:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
for
x\in{\mathbb{T}}
and
h\in[0,1]
, put

\Delta_{h}(f,x)\coloneqq f(x)-2f(x+h)+f(x+2h).

(4)

It was shown in
[
15
]
that every continuous function
f:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
(recalling that we identify
{\mathbb{T}}
with
[0,1)
) can be represented pointwise as

f(x)=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{D}}_{j}}d_{j,k}(f)v_{j,k}(x)

with coefficients
d_{j,k}(f)
given by
d_{-1,0}(f)\coloneqq f(0)
, and for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},k\in{\mathbb{D}}_{j}
via second differences

d_{j,k}(f)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{2^{-j-1}}\left(f,\frac{k}{2^{j}}\right).

In the case of
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
for general
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
one may proceed via tensorization. Define for
{\boldsymbol{j}}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}
and

{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{D}}_{j_{i}}
the tensor Faber basis

v_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{d}v_{j_{i},k_{i}}(x_{i}).

Furthermore, define for
f:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
,
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[d]
, and
{\boldsymbol{h}}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})\in[0,1]^{d}
the
\mathfrak{v}
-mixed difference operator
\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\mathfrak{v}}
as

\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\mathfrak{v}}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in\mathfrak{v}}\Delta_{h_{i},i}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\emptyset}\coloneqq{\rm id},

where
{\rm id}(f)\coloneqq f
and
\Delta_{h_{i},i}
is the univariate second-order difference operator (
4
), applied to the
i
-th coordinate of
f
with the other variables unchanged. Then, for continuous functions
f:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
one obtains

f({\boldsymbol{x}})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}}d_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(f)v_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})

pointwise, where (see
[
19
, Equation (3.6)]
), for
{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}
and
{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}
,

d_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(f)=\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{|\mathfrak{v}({\boldsymbol{j}})|}\Delta^{\mathfrak{v}({\boldsymbol{j}})}_{(2^{-j_{1}-1},\ldots,2^{-j_{d}-1})}\left(f,\left(\frac{k_{1}}{2^{(j_{1})_{+}}},\ldots,\frac{k_{d}}{2^{(j_{d})_{+}}}\right)\right),

with
\mathfrak{v}({\boldsymbol{j}})\coloneqq\{i:j_{i}\not=-1\}
and, for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle(a)_{+}\coloneqq\max(a,0).

(5)

The main tool in
[
19
]
is the following result, characterizing the Besov spaces
S_{p,q}^{r}B(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of regularity
r<2
in terms of the decay of the coefficients
d_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(f)
. For its statement in Proposition
1
, define the auxiliary norm

\|f\|_{s_{p,q}^{r}}\coloneqq\left[\sum_{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}}2^{|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}(r-1/p)q}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}}|d_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(f)|^{p}\right)^{q/p}\right]^{1/q}

with the usual modifications if
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
. Here, we write
|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}\coloneqq|j_{1}|+\dots+|j_{d}|
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and let
r\in(1/p,2)
. There is a constant
c_{p,q,r,d}>0
depending only on
p,q,r
, and
d
, such that

\|f\|_{S_{p,q}^{r}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\geq c_{p,q,r,d}\|f\|_{s_{p,q}^{r}}

for all
f\in S_{p,q}^{r}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
. If in addition
r<1+1/p
, there is a constant
C_{p,q,r,d}>0
depending only on
p,q,r
, and
d
, such that

\|f\|_{S_{p,q}^{r}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq C_{p,q,r,d}\|f\|_{s_{p,q}^{r}}

for all
f\in S_{p,q}^{r}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
.

A proof can be found in
[
19
, Proposition 3.4 and 3.5]
.

Remark 2
.

The condition
1/p<r
is required to have the continuous embedding
S_{p,q}^{r}B(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{T}^{d})
into the space of continuous functions. The upper bound
r<2
comes from the intrinsic regularity of the Faber functions
v_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}
with kinks at dyadic rationals. A similar decay behavior for the endpoint case
r=2
in the Sobolev setting was recently given in
[
22
]
(based on
[
2
]
) and characterizations via other basis functions were considered in
[
40
]
.

In the case of
p=q=2
one has
S_{2,2}^{r}B(\mathbb{T}^{d})=H_{\text{mix}}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with the usual Sobolev space of integrability
2
and dominating mixed smoothness
r>1/2
(also called Korobov space in the literature). For integral smoothness parameters
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
these Sobolev spaces are given by functions with weak partial derivatives of order up to
r
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, see
[
13
]
. For general
r>1/2
they can be characterized via decay conditions on Fourier coefficients, see e.g.
[
4
]
. The space
H_{\text{mix}}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
has the structure of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
[
1
]
, which significantly facilitates the analysis of the QMC rule (see e.g.
[
4
,
11
]
). It is well known that

\displaystyle{\rm wce}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N},H_{\text{mix}}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\asymp_{d}L_{2,N}^{{\rm per}}(\mathcal{P}_{d,N})

(6)

with implied factors only depending on
d
, see, e.g.,
[
18
,
20
]
. We thus see how bounds on the worst-case error
{\rm wce}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N},S_{p,q}^{r}B(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
can be used to study the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy of point sets or sequences.

The worst-case error
{\rm wce}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N},S_{p,q}^{r}B(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
may now be bounded in terms of the specific errors of the tensor Faber functions

c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N})\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}}v_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}v_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.

(7)

The proof of Lemma
3
below is a simple application of Proposition
1
and Hölder’s inequality; see
[
19
, Proof of Theorem 5.3]
for details, and in particular
[
19
, Equation (5.8)]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in(1/p,2)
. Also let
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
be the Hölder conjugates of
p
and
q
, respectively given by
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
and
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
. Then for all
N
-point sets
{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}
in
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
it holds that

\displaystyle{\rm wce}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N},S_{p,q}^{r}B(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\lesssim_{p,q,r,d}\left[\sum_{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}}2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}(r-1/p)q^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}}|c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N})|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{q^{\prime}/p^{\prime}}\right]^{1/q^{\prime}}

with an implied factor only depending on
p,q,r
, and
d
, and the usual adaptions if
p=1
or
q=1
.

It remains to discuss the relation of the errors
c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N})
of the Faber basis functions to the Haar coefficients of the local discrepancy function, which are an important analytic tool in the study of digital nets and sequences. The local discrepancy function
D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}}:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
of an
N
-point set
{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}
in
[0,1)^{d}
is given by

D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\coloneqq\frac{\#({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}\cap[\mathbf{0},{\boldsymbol{x}}))}{N}-\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\qquad\mbox{for }{\boldsymbol{x}}\in[0,1]^{d},

where
[\mathbf{0},{\boldsymbol{x}})\coloneqq[0,x_{1})\times\cdots\times[0,x_{d})
.

Let

\displaystyle I_{j,k}\coloneqq\left[\frac{k}{2^{j}},\frac{k+1}{2^{j}}\right)

(8)

be the dyadic interval for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},k\in{\mathbb{D}}_{j}
. Observe that
I_{j,k}=I_{k+1,2k}\cup I_{k+1,2k+1}
disjointly partitions the interval
I_{j,k}
into a left half
I_{j+1,2k}
and a right half
I_{k+1,2k+1}
. For
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}
and
k\in{\mathbb{D}}_{j}
define the Haar basis
h_{j,k}:[0,1)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
by
h_{-1,0}(x)\coloneqq 1
and, if
j\not=-1
, by

h_{j,k}(x)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1&\mbox{if }x\in I_{j+1,2k},\\
-1&\mbox{if }x\in I_{j+1,2k+1},\\
0&\mbox{else}.\end{array}\right.

Also, define its tensor product analogue

h_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{j_{i},k_{i}}(x_{i})\qquad\mbox{for }{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d},\,{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\boldsymbol{j}},\,{\boldsymbol{x}}\in[0,1)^{d}.

The Haar coefficients of the local discrepancy function are defined as

\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}})\coloneqq\int_{[0,1)^{d}}D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}}({\boldsymbol{x}})h_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\,\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.

The next lemma, which is
[
19
, Lemma 5.2]
, shows that the Haar coefficients
\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}})
can be used to determine the errors
c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N})
of the Faber basis functions from (
7
). Here we write, like in (
5
),
|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{+}\coloneqq(j_{1})_{+}+\cdots+(j_{d})_{+}
.

Lemma 4
.

For an
N
-point set
{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}
in
[0,1)^{d}
we have:

(i)

If
{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
and
{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}
, then

\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}})=(-1)^{d}2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}}c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}({\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}).

(ii)

If
{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
and
{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}
, then

\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{d,N}})=(-1)^{s}2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{+}}c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(\mathcal{P}),

where
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}_{d,N}
denotes the set of points obtained from the projection of the elements of
\mathcal{P}_{d,N}
onto those
s
coordinates
x_{i}
where
j_{i}\not=-1
. Furthermore,
\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}})
is the Haar coefficient with respect to the
s
-variate function
D_{\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}}
.

3
Digital nets and sequences

The concepts of digital nets and sequences over finite fields were introduced by Niederreiter
[
32
]
in 1987. Detailed introductions to this topic can be found in the books
[
11
,
26
,
33
]
. In the present work, we restrict ourselves to the binary case. Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}
be the finite field of order 2, identified with the set
\{0,1\}
equipped with arithmetic operations modulo 2. In the following, let
[d]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,d\}
, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

The digital construction scheme.

We begin with the definition of digital nets according to Niederreiter, which we present in a slightly more general form here. For
n,q,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
q\geq n
let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{q\times n}
be
q\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. For
k\in\{0,\ldots,2^{n}-1\}
with binary expansion
k=k_{0}+k_{1}2+\cdots+k_{n-1}2^{n-1}
, where
k_{j}\in\{0,1\}
, we define the binary digit vector
\vec{k}=(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1})^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
(the symbol
\top
means the transpose of a vector or a matrix; hence
\vec{k}
is a column-vector). Then compute

C_{j}\vec{k}=:(x_{j,k,1},x_{j,k,2},\ldots,x_{j,k,q})^{\top}\quad\mbox{for }j\in[d],

(9)

where the matrix vector product is evaluated over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We interpret the entries
x_{j,k,i}
of the resulting vector in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{q}
in (
9
) as binary digits in
\{0,1\}
, and put

x_{j,k}\coloneqq\frac{x_{j,k,1}}{2}+\frac{x_{j,k,2}}{2^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{x_{j,k,q}}{2^{q}}\in[0,1)

and
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\coloneqq(x_{1,k},\ldots,x_{d,k})
, which is a point in
[0,1)^{d}
. The point set
\mathcal{P}_{d,2^{n}}=\{\boldsymbol{x}_{0},\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_{2^{n}-1}\}
constructed this way is called a digital net (over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
) with generating matrices
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
. A digital net
\mathcal{P}_{d,2^{n}}
consists of
2^{n}
points in
[0,1)^{d}
.

A variant of digital nets are so-called digitally shifted digital nets (see
[
11
, Section 4.4.4]
). Here, one additionally selects a so-called digital shift vector for each component
j\in[d]
of the form
\vec{\sigma}_{j}=(\sigma_{j,1},\sigma_{j,2},\ldots)^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}
(for our purposes it suffices to restrict ourselves to the case where only a finite number of components differ from zero) and replaces (
9
) by

{C_{j}\vec{k}\choose\vec{0}}+\vec{\sigma}_{j}=:(x_{j,k,1},x_{j,k,2},x_{j,k,3},\ldots,)^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}\quad\mbox{for }j\in[d],

where
{C_{j}\vec{k}\choose\vec{0}}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}
is the
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{q}
-vector
C_{j}\vec{k}
concatenated with consecutive zeros, and puts

x_{j,k}=\frac{x_{j,k,1}}{2}+\frac{x_{j,k,2}}{2^{2}}+\frac{x_{j,k,3}}{2^{3}}+\cdots\in[0,1).

The main objects of our study are digital sequences, which are infinite versions of digital nets. Let now
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
be
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. For
C_{j}=(c_{j,k,\ell})_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
it is assumed that for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a
K(\ell)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
c_{j,k,\ell}=0
for all
k>K(\ell)
. For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with binary expansion
k=k_{0}+k_{1}2+\cdots+k_{m-1}2^{m-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define the infinite binary digit vector
\vec{k}=(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{m-1},0,0,\ldots)^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Then compute, over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
,

C_{j}\vec{k}=:(x_{j,k,1},x_{j,k,2},\ldots)^{\top}\quad\mbox{for }j\in[d]

and put, similarly to before,

x_{j,k}=\frac{x_{j,k,1}}{2}+\frac{x_{j,k,2}}{2^{2}}+\cdots,

and
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}=(x_{1,k},\ldots,x_{d,k})\in[0,1)^{d}
. A sequence
\mathcal{S}_{d}=(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
constructed this way is called a digital sequence (over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
) with generating matrices
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
. Since
c_{j,k,\ell}=0
for all
k
large enough, the numbers
x_{j,k}
are always dyadic rationals, i.e., have a finite dyadic expansion, which belong to
[0,1)
.

Higher order nets and sequences.

The choice of the respective generating matrices is crucial for the distribution quality of digital nets and sequences. The following definitions put some restrictions on
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
with the aim to quantify the quality of equidistribution of the digital net or sequence.

Definition 5
.

Let
n,q,\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
q\geq\alpha n
and let
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that
0\leq t\leq\alpha n
. Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{q\times n}
. Denote the
i
-th row vector of the matrix
C_{j}
by
\vec{c}_{j,i}
. The
\vec{c}_{j,i}
are row vectors of length
n
with components in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. If for all integers
1\leq i_{j,\nu_{j}}<\cdots<i_{j,1}\leq q
with

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l=1}^{\min(\nu_{j},\alpha)}i_{j,l}\leq\alpha n-t,

the vectors

\vec{c}_{1,i_{1,\nu_{1}}},\ldots,\vec{c}_{1,i_{1,1}},\ldots,\vec{c}_{d,i_{d,\nu_{d}}},\ldots,\vec{c}_{d,i_{d,1}}

are linearly independent
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then the digital net with generating matrices

C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
is called an order-
\alpha
digital
(t,n,d)
-net over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. The parameter
t
is referred to as the quality parameter.

Furthermore, we consider digital sequences whose initial segments are order-
\alpha
digital
(t,n,d)
-nets over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Definition 6
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
and let
C_{j,\alpha n\times n}
denote the left upper
\alpha n\times n
submatrix of
C_{j}
for
j\in[d]
. If for all
n>t/\alpha
the matrices
C_{1,\alpha n\times n},\ldots,C_{d,\alpha n\times n}
generate an order-
\alpha
digital
(t,n,d)
-net over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then the digital sequence with generating matrices
C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}
is called an order-
\alpha
digital
(t,d)
-sequence over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Order-1 (i.e.,
\alpha=1
) in the above definitions refers to the classical definition of digital
(t,n,d)
-nets and
(t,d)
-sequences by Niederreiter
[
32
]
(see also
[
33
]
and the introductory books
[
11
,
26
]
). The extension to higher order
\alpha\geq 1
was introduced by Dick
[
4
,
5
]
. For a geometrical interpretation of higher order digital nets and sequences we refer to
[
33
,
11
,
26
]
for the classical case
\alpha=1
and
[
6
]
for general
\alpha
. Roughly speaking, the definitions imply that special intervals or unions of intervals of prescribed volume contain the correct share of points with respect to a perfect uniform distribution.

Definition
5
shows that if
\mathcal{P}_{d,2^{n}}
is an order-
\alpha
digital
(t,n,d)
-net, then for any
t\leq t^{\prime}\leq\alpha n
,
\mathcal{P}_{d,2^{n}}
is also an order-
\alpha
digital
(t^{\prime},n,d)
-net. An analogous result also applies to higher order digital sequences.

We point out that a digital net can be an order-
\alpha
digital
(t,n,d)
-net over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and at the same time an order-
\alpha^{\prime}
digital
(t^{\prime},n,d)
-net over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
for
\alpha^{\prime}\not=\alpha
. The quality parameter
t
may depend on
\alpha
(i.e.,
t=t(\alpha)
). The same is true for digital sequences. In particular,
[
5
, Theorem 4.10]
implies that an order-
\alpha
digital
(t,n,d)
-net is an order-
\alpha^{\prime}
digital
(t^{\prime},n,d)
-net for all
1\leq\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
with

t^{\prime}=\lceil t\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha\rceil\leq t.

The same result applies to order
\alpha
digital
(t,d)
-sequences which are also order
\alpha^{\prime}
digital
(t^{\prime},d)
-sequences for
1\leq\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
and
t^{\prime}
as above. In other words,
t(\alpha^{\prime})=\lceil t(\alpha)\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha\rceil
for all
1\leq\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
. For more details, see
[
4
,
5
]
or
[
11
, Chapter 15]
.

Explicit constructions of order-2 digital sequences.

Explicit constructions of order-
\alpha
digital nets and sequences have been provided by Dick
[
4
,
5
]
. For our purposes, it suffices to restrict ourselves to the case
\alpha=2
.

In order to construct a sequence in dimension
d
we start with
2d
(in general,
\alpha d
) generating matrices
C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d}
of a digital sequence over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let
\vec{c}_{j,k}
denote the
k
-th row of
C_{j}
. We now define matrices
E_{1},\ldots,E_{d}
, where the
k
-th row of
E_{j}
is denoted by
\vec{e}_{j,k}
, in the following way. For all
j\in[d]
,
u\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and
v\in\{1,2\}
, set

\vec{e}_{j,2u+v}=\vec{c}_{2(j-1)+v,u+1}.

We illustrate the construction for
d=1
. In this case we have

C_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vec{c}_{1,1}\\
\vec{c}_{1,2}\\
\vdots\end{array}\right),\ C_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vec{c}_{2,1}\\
\vec{c}_{2,2}\\
\vdots\end{array}\right)\ \Rightarrow\ E_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vec{c}_{1,1}\\
\vec{c}_{2,1}\\
\vec{c}_{1,2}\\
\vec{c}_{2,2}\\
\vdots\end{array}\right).

This procedure is called interlacing (with interlacing factor
\alpha=2
).

Remark 7
.

Recall that for generating matrices of digital sequences we demand
c_{j,k,\ell}=0
for all
k>K(\ell)
. Let
E_{j}=(e_{j,k,\ell})_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then the construction yields
e_{j,k,\ell}=0
for all
k>2K(\ell)
.

From
[
4
, Theorems 4.11 and 4.12]
we obtain the following result.

Proposition 8
.

If
C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}}
generate an order-1 digital
(t^{\prime},2d)
-sequence over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then
E_{1},\ldots,E_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}}
generate an order-2 digital
(t,d)
-sequence over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with
t=2t^{\prime}+d
.

Examples of explicit constructions of suitable generating matrices of order-1 sequences over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
were obtained by Sobol’
[
41
]
, Niederreiter
[
32
,
33
]
, Niederreiter and Xing
[
34
]
, and others. An overview is presented in
[
11
, Chapter 8]
. Any of these constructions is sufficient for our purpose; however, for completeness, we briefly describe a special case of a construction of generating matrices by Tezuka
[
44
]
, which is a generalization of Sobol’s
[
41
]
and Niederreiter’s
[
32
]
constructions, respectively.

We explain how to construct the entries
c_{j,k,\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
of the generating matrices
C_{j}=(c_{j,k,\ell})_{k,\ell\geq 1}
for
j\in[d^{\prime}]
(for our purpose,
d^{\prime}=2d
). To this end, for
j\in[d^{\prime}]
, choose a polynomial
p_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]
as the
j
-th irreducible polynomial in a list of irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. The list is assumed to be sorted in increasing order according to the degrees
e_{\ell}=\deg(p_{\ell})
of the polynomials
p_{\ell}
, i.e.,
e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\cdots\leq e_{d^{\prime}}
(the ordering of polynomials with the same degree is irrelevant).

Let
j\in[d^{\prime}]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Take
i-1
and
z
to be the main term and the remainder when we divide
k-1
by
e_{j}
, respectively, so that
k-1=(i-1)e_{j}+z
, with
z\in\{0,\ldots,e_{j}-1\}
. Now consider the Laurent series expansion

\frac{x^{e_{j}-z-1}}{p_{j}(x)^{i}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}a_{\ell}(i,j,z)x^{-\ell}.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we set

c_{j,k,\ell}=a_{\ell}(i,j,z).

Every digital sequence with generating matrices
C_{j}=(c_{j,k,\ell})_{k,\ell\geq 1}
for
j\in[d^{\prime}]
obtained this way is a special instance of a Sobol’ sequence, which in turn is a special instance of a so-called generalized Niederreiter sequence (see
[
44
, Equation (3)]
). Note that in the construction above we always have
c_{j,k,\ell}=0
for
k>\ell
. The quality parameter of these sequences is known to be
t^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{d^{\prime}}(e_{j}-1)
, see
[
33
, Chapter 4.5]
for the case of Niederreiter sequences.

Remark 9
.

Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d}
be
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
-matrices constructed according to Tezuka’s method as described above. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{d}
be obtained from an order-2 interlacing of
C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d}
. Then we have
e_{j,k,\ell}=0
for all
k>2\ell
, where
e_{j,k,\ell}
is the entry in row
k
and column
\ell
of the matrix
E_{j}
. The matrices
E_{1},\ldots,E_{d}
generate an order-2 digital
(t,d)
-sequence over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with
t=2\sum_{j=1}^{2d}(e_{j}-1)+d
.

Haar coefficients of the local discrepancy of order-2 nets and sequences.

Here we collect bounds on the Haar coefficients of the local discrepancy function of (digitally shifted) order-2 nets and sequences. By Lemma
4
these bounds may be used to obtain bounds for the corresponding errors of the tensor Faber functions (
7
).

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{d,2^{n}}
be a digitally shifted order-2 digital
(t,n,d)
-net over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let
{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}
with
|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}+t/2\leq n
and
{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{D}_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}
. Then

|\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{\mathcal{P}_{d,2^{n}}})|\lesssim 2^{-2n+t}(2n-t-2|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1})^{d-1}.

Proof.

The lemma is a slight generalization of
[
28
, Lemma 5.9]
. The result was originally proved for order-
2
digital
(t,n,d)
-nets. The extension to digitally shifted order
2
digital
(t,n,d)
-nets follows by almost exactly the same arguments as the proof of
[
28
, Lemma 5.9]
. The statement in the present form can also be found in
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

The next lemma gives a bound for order-2 digital sequences. The dyadic boxes
I_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{d}I_{j_{i},k_{i}}
appearing below are given with
I_{j,k}
as in (
8
). In the following, let
{\rm ld}\,N\coloneqq\log_{2}N
denote the logarithm to the base
2
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{d}
be an order-2 digital
(t,d)
-sequence over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with generating matrices
E_{i}=(e_{i,k,\ell})_{k,\ell\geq 1}
for which
e_{i,k,\ell}=0
for all
k>2\ell
and for all
i\in[d]
, and let
\mathcal{S}_{d,N}
be the point set consisting of the first
N
elements of
\mathcal{S}_{d}
.

1.

Let
|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}+t/2\geq\lfloor{\rm ld}\,N\rfloor
and set
{\mathbb{A}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}\coloneqq\{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}:I_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{d,N}\neq\emptyset\}
. Then

|\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{\mathcal{S}_{d,N}})|\lesssim\begin{cases}2^{t/2}\,N^{-1}2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}}&\text{if }{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{A}}_{\boldsymbol{j}},\\
2^{-2|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}}&\text{if }{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}\setminus{\mathbb{A}}_{\boldsymbol{j}},\end{cases}

where the second case occurs for
\#({\mathbb{D}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}\setminus{\mathbb{A}}_{\boldsymbol{j}})\geq 2^{|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}}-N
indices
{\boldsymbol{k}}
.

2.

On the other hand, if
|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}+t/2<\lfloor{\rm ld}\,N\rfloor
, so that
n_{\gamma}\leq|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}+t/2<n_{\gamma+1}
for some
\gamma\in\{0,1,\dots,u-1\}
, where
N=2^{n_{u}}+\cdots+2^{n_{1}}
with
n_{u}>\cdots>n_{1}\geq 0
and also setting
n_{0}\coloneqq 0
, then

|\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{\mathcal{S}_{d,N}})|\lesssim 2^{t}\,N^{-1}\left(2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}}+(2n_{\gamma+1}-t-2|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1})^{d-1}2^{-n_{\gamma+1}}\right).

Proof.

This is
[
10
, Equations (13), (14), and (16)]
. The statement in the present form can also be found in
[
9
, Lemma 4.5]
.
∎

4
Upper bound for order-2 digital sequences

For order-
2
digital
(t,m,d)
-nets
\mathcal{P}_{d,2^{m}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
we know from
[
19
, Theorem 5.3]
that for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in(1/p,2)
we have

{\rm wce}(\mathcal{P}_{d,2^{m}},S^{r}_{p,q}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\lesssim_{p,q,r,d}2^{rt/2}\frac{(\log N)^{(d-1)(1-1/q)}}{N^{r}},\qquad\mbox{where}\ N=2^{m}.

For an infinite sequence we then have the following result, which constitutes the main result of this work.

Theorem 12
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{d}
be an order-2 digital
(t,d)
-sequence over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with generating matrices
E_{i}=(e_{i,k,\ell})_{k,\ell\geq 1}
for which
e_{i,k,\ell}=0
for all
k>2\ell
and for all
i\in[d]
. For
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
let
\mathcal{S}_{d,N}
denote the point set consisting of the
N
initial terms of
\mathcal{S}_{d}
. Then for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
1/p<r<2
we have for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
,

{\rm wce}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N},S^{r}_{p,q}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\lesssim_{p,q,r,d}2^{(r+1-1/p)t/2}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\log N)^{(d-1)(1-1/q)}N^{-\min(r,1)}&\mbox{if }r\not=1,\\[5.0pt]
(\log N)^{d(1-1/q)}N^{-1}&\mbox{if }r=1.\end{array}\right.

The proof of this result will be given in Section
5
. But first, the result should be classified in comparison to lower bounds and a corollary regarding our initial problem measure, the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy, should be formulated.

Remark 13
.

It is known that for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and any
r>1/p
we have for every
N
-element set
{\mathcal{P}}_{d,N}
in
[0,1)^{d}
,

{\rm wce}(\mathcal{P}_{d,N},S^{r}_{p,q}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\gtrsim\frac{(\log N)^{(d-1)(1-1/q)}}{N^{r}}.

This lower bound even holds for general cubature rules, not only for QMC rules, see
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
or
[
43
]
for the case of Sobolev spaces; the result is also stated in
[
19
, Theorem 5.5]
. This shows that the order of magnitude in
N
in Theorem
12
is best possible if
r\in(1/p,1)
, which is non-empty for
p>1
. For
r\geq 1
the upper bound in Theorem
12
is, apart from the logarithm factors, of order
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
. Considering infinite sequences, also this order of magnitude cannot be improved for all
N
and not even for infinitely many
N
in an arithmetic progression. Indeed, the following result, based on an argument by Sobol’
[
42
]
, was stated in
[
17
, Proposition 1]
. If for some
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, any
M
-point QMC rule has a positive worst-case error, which is bounded below away from zero, i.e.,

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{P}_{d,M}\subseteq[0,1)^{d}\\
\#\mathcal{P}_{d,M}=M\end{subarray}}{\rm wce}(\mathcal{P}_{d,M},S^{r}_{p,q}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\geq c_{M}>0,

where
c_{M}
is allowed to depend on
M
, then the sequence

({\rm wce}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N_{0}+nM},S^{r}_{p,q}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d})))_{n\geq 0}

cannot converge to zero faster than
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
, with
N
of the form
N=N_{0}+nM
, for any fixed
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is easy to see that the required assumption is true for any
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
in the space
S^{r}_{p,q}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
. Thus, the order of magnitude in Theorem
12
is also optimal in the case
r\in[1,2)
(up to logarithmic factors). For
r=1
and
p=q=2
the obtained order of magnitude is optimal even with regard to logarithmic factors (see Corollary
14
below).

By (
6
), if in Theorem
12
the parameters
p,q
are set to 2 and
r
to 1, then we obtain the desired best possible estimate for the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy/diaphony of order-2 digital sequences.

Corollary 14
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{d}
be an order-2 digital
(t,d)
-sequence over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with generating matrices
E_{i}=(e_{i,k,\ell})_{k,\ell\geq 1}
for which
e_{i,k,\ell}=0
for all
k>2\ell
and for all
i\in[d]
. Then we have

L_{2,N}^{{\rm per}}(\mathcal{S}_{d})\lesssim_{d}2^{t}\ \frac{(\log N)^{d/2}}{N}\qquad\mbox{for all }N\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\},

and this is best possible according to Proĭnov’s lower bound (
2
).

Remark 15
.

It is well known that order-2 digital sequences exhibit the best possible order for the usual star (anchored)
L_{2}
-discrepancy and even for the star
L_{p}
-discrepancy for any
p\in(1,\infty)
; see
[
10
]
. The same holds true for the extreme (unanchored)
L_{p}
-discrepancy. In this case, the upper bound results from the aforementioned bound for the star
L_{p}
-discrepancy from
[
10
]
together with the fact that the extreme
L_{p}
-discrepancy is dominated—up to a multiplicative factor that depends only on
p
and
d
—by the star
L_{p}
-discrepancy (see
[
25
, Corollary 5]
), and the lower bound from
[
24
, Theorem 1]
. Furthermore, order-2 digital sequences exhibit optimal rates for star discrepancy with respect to the exponential Orlicz norm,
the BMO semi-norm, as well as the Sobolev, Besov, and Triebel-Lizorkin norms with dominating mixed smoothness, see
[
9
, Theorems 2.1, 2.2, 3.1 and 3.2]
. The present Corollary
14
guarantees the same behavior for the periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy. This is further evidence of the universally excellent distribution properties of Dick’s order-2 digital sequences with respect to various discrepancies and figures of merit.

Lemma 19

For

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}\atop|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}+t/2\geq\lfloor{\rm ld}\,N\rfloor}2^{-\alpha|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}}

The following lemma is similar to

Lemma 20
.

Let
A\geq 1/2
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

. Then

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(A+k)^{s}}{b^{k}}\lesssim A^{s}

with an implicit constant independent of

.

Proof.

Since

it holds
that

for all

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(A+k)^{s}}{b^{k}}\leq\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+1)^{s}}{b^{k}}\right)A^{s}.

The infinite sum converges to a finite value, proving the claim.

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1,1}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1,2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}\atop|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}+t/2<\lfloor{\rm ld}\,N\rfloor}2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}(r-1-1/p)q^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}}|\mu_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(D_{\mathcal{S}_{d,N}})|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{q^{\prime}/p^{\prime}}

For any

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1,2}

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma=0}^{u-1}\frac{1}{2^{q^{\prime}n_{\gamma+1}}}\sum_{n_{\gamma}\leq|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}+t/2<n_{\gamma+1}}2^{|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}(2-r)q^{\prime}}(2n_{\gamma+1}-t-2|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1})^{q^{\prime}(d-1)}

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma=0}^{u-1}\frac{1}{2^{q^{\prime}n_{\gamma+1}}}\sum_{n_{\gamma}\leq|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}+t/2<n_{\gamma+1}}2^{|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}(2-r)q^{\prime}}(2n_{\gamma+1}-t-2|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1})^{q^{\prime}(d-1)}

\displaystyle~~\lesssim~~2^{-(2-r)q^{\prime}t/2}(\log N)^{d-1}\sum_{\gamma=0}^{u-1}2^{(1-r)q^{\prime}n_{\gamma+1}}

\displaystyle~~\lesssim~~2^{-(2-r)q^{\prime}t/2}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\log N)^{d-1}N^{(1-r)q^{\prime}}&\mbox{if }r<1,\\
(\log N)^{d}&\mbox{if }r=1,\\
(\log N)^{d-1}&\mbox{if }r>1.\end{array}\right.

Altogether, we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\max(1,r)q^{\prime}t/2}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\log N)^{d-1}N^{(1-r)q^{\prime}}&\mbox{if }r<1,\\
(\log N)^{d}&\mbox{if }r=1,\\
(\log N)^{d-1}&\mbox{if }r>1.\end{array}\right.

Adding
\Sigma_{1,1}
and
\Sigma_{1,2}
, noting that

r\leq r+1-1/p
, we obtain

\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma_{1,1}+\Sigma_{1,2}\lesssim 2^{q^{\prime}(r+1-1/p)t/2}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(\log N)^{d-1}}{N^{rq^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r<1,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{(\log N)^{d}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r=1,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{(\log N)^{d-1}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r>1.\end{array}\right.

(13)

Finally, we have to consider the partial sum

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}}2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}(r-1/p)q^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}}|c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N})|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{q^{\prime}/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subsetneq[d]}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{u}}}2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}(r-1/p)q^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}}|c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N})|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{q^{\prime}/p^{\prime}}

where for
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]
we put
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{u}}\coloneqq\{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{-1}^{d}:j_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\mbox{ if }i\in\mathfrak{u},\mbox{ and }j_{i}=-1\mbox{ if }i\in[d]\setminus\mathfrak{u}\}
. Note that the projection of an order-2 digital
(t,d)
-sequence over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
onto coordinates in
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[d]
is an order-2 digital
(t,|\mathfrak{u}|)
-sequence over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. Hence, using Lemma
4
and (
13
) with
d
replaced by
|\mathfrak{u}|
, we obtain

\sum_{{\boldsymbol{j}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{u}}}2^{-|{\boldsymbol{j}}|_{1}(r-1/p)q^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}}|c_{{\boldsymbol{j}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N})|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{q^{\prime}/p^{\prime}}\lesssim 2^{q^{\prime}(r+1-1/p)t/2}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(\log N)^{|\mathfrak{u}|-1}}{N^{rq^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r<1,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{(\log N)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r=1,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{(\log N)^{|\mathfrak{u}|-1}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r>1.\end{array}\right.

Note that for
\mathfrak{u}=\emptyset
we obtain
c_{(-1,\ldots,-1),(0,\ldots,0)}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N})=0
. Hence, for
r\geq 1/p
,

\Sigma_{2}\lesssim 2^{q^{\prime}(r+1-1/p)t/2}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(\log N)^{d-2}}{N^{rq^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r<1,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{(\log N)^{d-1}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r=1,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{(\log N)^{d-2}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r>1.\end{array}\right.

Finally,

{\rm wce}(\mathcal{S}_{d,N},S^{r}_{p,q}B({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))^{q^{\prime}}=\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}\lesssim 2^{q^{\prime}(r+1-1/p)t/2}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(\log N)^{d-1}}{N^{rq^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r<1,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{(\log N)^{d}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r=1,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{(\log N)^{d-1}}{N^{q^{\prime}}}&\mbox{if }r>1,\end{array}\right.

and the result is obtained by taking the
q^{\prime}
-th root, recalling that
1/q^{\prime}=1-1/q
.
∎
