The Pardon Algebra for zero-cycles
Kai Behrend

Abstract
Recently, John Pardon proved the MNOP conjecture by introducing a new mathematical gadget, which we call the Pardon homology algebra of 1-cycles in 3-folds. We work out an analogous construction for 0 0 -cycles in d d -folds. This gives a new point of view on enumerative problems involving point-counting, such as, for example, the degree zero MNOP conjecture.



Introduction

This is an exploration of John Pardon’s theory
[
9
]
, in a much simpler case: instead of considering 1-cycles, we consider 0-cycles. Pardon used his theory to prove the MNOP Gromov-Witten-Donaldson-Thomas correspondence. We arrive at a new point of view on point enumeration problems.

Summary of main result.

Fix

The

Outline.

If
X
is an algebraic
d
-fold, the space of
0
-cycles of degree
n
is the symmetric product which we denote by
\overline{Z}_{n}X
. In order to avoid dealing with singular varieties, we prefer working with the quotient stack
Z_{n}X=X^{n}/\Sigma_{n}
, of which
\overline{Z}_{n}X
is the coarse moduli space. In terms of (co-)homology with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-coefficients, this makes essentially no difference. The moduli problem solved by
Z_{n}X
is that of morphisms
D\to S\times X
, where
D\to S
is finite étale of degree
n
. Thus, we can treat the theory of
0
-cycles also as a theory of maps. When passing from a fixed target variety
X
to a family
X\to S
, we consider diagrams
D\to X\to S
, where
X\to S
is a family of
d
-folds and
D\to S
is finite étale. We denote the relative space of maps of degree
n
by
Z_{n}X=X^{n}_{S}/\Sigma_{n}
.

For the theory to be compatible with passing from
Z_{n}X
to its coarse moduli space
\overline{Z}_{n}X
, we need to make sure
Z_{n}X
has finite stabilizers. This requires
X\to S
to be separated.
On the other hand, dropping the separatedness assumption on our target families
X\to S
gives rise to a perfectly consistent theory, which we develop in the first part of this paper. It is simpler than the separated theory, deformation to the normal bundle is allowed, and multiple points can be separated into sums of simple points. The tautological Pardon algebra gets smaller, and is now equal to
{\mathbb{Q}}[q_{1\lambda}]
.

In the second part, we briefly introduce the dual of the Pardon homology algebra, the Pardon cohomology algebra. It contains the enumerative theories against which classes in
H_{\ast}
can be evaluated to get enumerative invariants.

The third part contains the main results announced above. As an illustration, we finish with a proof of the degree zero MNOP conjecture. (Our proof apparently works for proper Deligne-Mumford stacks, and it does not use relative Donaldson-Thomas theory.)

Many problems involving point counts in
d
-folds can be formulated (and solved) using these results. We expect that the calculations this gives rise to are essentially equivalent to well-known approaches to these kinds of problems, as developed for example by Ellingsrud-Göttsche-Lehn
[
3
]
.

All geometric arguments are purely algebraic. This distinguishes this work from Pardon’s for 1-cycles, which is essentially analytic. Therefore, what we describe here should work for an algebraic (co)-homology theory such as Chow-Witt (co)homology theory, which is homotopy invariant, and should give values in the Witt-ring. This will be explored elsewhere.

Definition 1.10

The

Here, the colimit is taken over the category of families of

Thus, given any family

Grading

We note that

is graded by the degree

H_{\ast}(ZX)=H_{\ast}(\coprod_{n\geq 0}Z_{n}X)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H_{\ast}(Z_{n}X)

survives the colimit. Of course,

has a second grading, the homological grading, so

is, in fact, bigraded. When referring to the latter, we will always use the term ‘homological grading’, to distinguish it from the former.

Multiplication

We define multiplication in
H_{\ast}
on representatives as follows. Let
\alpha\in H_{\ast}(ZX)
, for a family

, and
\beta\in H_{\ast}(ZY)
for a family

. Then

is associated with the disjoint union family

over

. In fact,

is the external product of

, via the isomorphism observed in Remark

This operation is compatible with the relations defining

It is straightforward to prove that this multiplication is bigraded, associative, and graded commutative with respect to the homological grading. A unit is provided by the empty family

Graded Hopf algebra

Proposition 1.11

Pardon homology
H_{\ast}
is a bigraded bialgebra. It is graded commutative and cocommutative with respect to the homological grading.

Proof.

As an example of the various properties, let us check that the coproduct is multiplicative.
Our claim is that

\Delta:H_{\ast}\longrightarrow H_{\ast}\otimes H_{\ast}

respects multiplication.
Thus, let
\alpha\in H_{\ast}(ZX)
and
\beta\in H_{\ast}(ZY)
, for families
X\to S
and
Y\to T
. We claim

\Delta(\alpha\cdot\beta)=\Delta(\alpha)\cdot\Delta(\beta)\,.

Applying the cartesian diagram from Remark
1.9
, we see that

+^{!}(\alpha\cdot\beta)=(+^{!}\alpha)\times(+^{!}\beta)\,.

Mapping to
ZX\times ZX\times ZY\times ZY=ZX\times ZY\times ZX\times ZY
, get

\Delta_{\ast}(+^{!}(\alpha\cdot\beta))=\Delta_{\ast}(+^{!}\alpha)\times\Delta_{\ast}(+^{!}\beta)

Applying the Alexander-Whitney map
F
, we get

F(\Delta_{\ast}+^{!}(\alpha\cdot\beta))=F(\Delta_{\ast}+^{!}\alpha)\times F(\Delta_{\ast}+^{!}\beta)\,,

which proves the claim.

1.4
Vertical classes

There are two fundamental ways to construct classes in
H_{\ast}
. Thus, we obtain horizontal and vertical classes.

If

is a proper family, then
\pi:Z_{n}X\to S
is a proper morphism, smooth of relative dimension

. Hence Gysin pullback defines a map

\pi^{!}:H_{\ast}(S)\to H_{\ast+2nd}(Z_{n}X)
, and so every class

gives rise to a class

, which defines a class in
H_{\ast+2nd}

S=\ast
, we get the fundamental class
[Z_{n}X]\in H_{2nd}(Z_{n}X)
. We call classes of the type
\pi^{!}\alpha

. We may sometimes use the notation
(Z_{n}X)^{!}\alpha=\pi^{!}\alpha
for such a class.

If
X\to S
is a proper family, then for every point

, the fibre

of

over
s
gives rise to a class
[Z_{n}X_{s}]
in Pardon homology. If

is connected, then all these vertical classes are equivalent in the Pardon homology algebra. (This uses the fact that

, for connected

In particular,
[Z_{n}X]
and

(for proper

Proposition 1.15

(Product of vertical classes)

Proposition 1.16

(coproduct of vertical classes)

Let

be a proper family, and

\displaystyle\longrightarrow H_{\ast}

1.5
Horizontal classes

If we have a relative cycle family
D\to X
of degree
n
in a (non-proper!) family
X\to S
, it induces a section
\Delta:S\to Z^{n}X
of
\pi:Z_{n}X\to S
. Via
\Delta_{\ast}:H_{\ast}(S)\to H_{\ast}(Z_{n}X)
,
we get, for every
\alpha\in H_{\ast}(S)
, an element
\Delta_{\ast}\alpha
in
H_{\ast}
, which we will denote by
\eta(D/X/S,\alpha)
.
Such classes are

.

Two horizontal cycles
\eta(D/X/S,\alpha)
and
\eta(E/Y/T,\beta)
are equal in Pardon homology if there exists a family
M\to U
, and a relative cycle
F\to M\to U
, and morphisms
s:S\to U
and
t:T\to U
, such that
D\to X\to S
is the pullback of
F\to M\to U
via
s
, and
E\to Y\to T
is the pullback of
F\to M\to U
via
t
, and
s_{\ast}\alpha=t_{\ast}\beta
in
H_{\ast}(U)
.

Remark 1.17

(Local nature of horizontal classes)
If we have a relative cycle family
D\to X\to S
, and
U
is an open subset of
X
through which
D
factors, then for every
\alpha\in H_{\ast}(S)
, we have
\eta(D/X/S,\alpha)=\eta(D/U/S,\alpha)
.

Proof.

Consider the relative cycle family
D\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to(X\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus Z\times\{0\})\to S\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
, where

is the closed complement of
U
in
X
. The pullback of this cycle family via
(\id,0):S\to S\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
is
D\to U\to S
, and the pullback via
(\id,1):S\to S\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
is
D\to X\to S
. Moreover,
(\id,0)_{\ast}\alpha=(\id,1)_{\ast}\alpha
in
H_{\ast}(S\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1})
by homotopy invariance of homology.

\Box

Proposition 1.18

(Product of horizontal cycles)

The product of horizontal cycles

Let us denote by

and

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z(X_{T}\amalg Y_{S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ZX\times ZY}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times T\hbox to0.0pt{\,.\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{T}\amalg\gamma_{S}}

1.6
Pardon’s trick.

For most intents we are interested in vertical cycles, but horizontal cycles are easier to manipulate, especially because the assumption of properness on the family is dropped. A key feature of the Pardon homology algebra is that it allows the conversion of vertical cycles into horizontal cycles. This goes via passing to the universal situation.

In our simple situation, where cycle spaces are smooth, this trick is almost a tautology.

Let us suppose given a homology class
\alpha\in H_{\ast}(S)
, a proper family
X\to S
with induced cycle space
\pi:Z_{n}X\to S
, and the induced vertical class
\pi^{!}\alpha\in H_{\ast}(Z_{n}X)
.
We consider the universal cycle family in the family
X\to S
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X\times_{S}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X\times_{S}Z_{n}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X\hbox to0.0pt{\,.\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}

Its classifying map
\Delta
is the diagonal.
We get the horizontal class

\Delta_{\ast}\pi^{!}\alpha\in H_{\ast}(Z_{n}X\times_{S}Z_{n}X\to Z_{n}X)\,.

The claim is that

\pi^{!}\alpha\sim\Delta_{\ast}\pi^{!}\alpha\,,

in the Pardon ring
H_{\ast}
. This can be seen by considering the pullback diagram of families, and associated diagram of cycle spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X\times_{S}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X\times_{S}Z_{n}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}

In fact, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ast}\pi^{!}\alpha\sim

\displaystyle p_{\ast}\Delta_{\ast}\pi^{!}\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\id_{\ast}\pi^{!}\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{!}\alpha\,.

For example, (for proper
X\to\ast
) the (vertical) fundamental class
[Z_{n}X]
is equal to the (horizontal) class of the diagonal in
H_{\ast}(Z_{n}X\times Z_{n}X)
.

1.7
Tautological subalgebra

We introduce some natural elements of the Pardon Hopf algebra. They will be horizontal classes. They will be indexed by
d\times n
-matrices of integers. For later purposes, only the

case will be important, as the corresponding classes suffice to multiplicatively generate the tautological subalgebra.

Suppose given
n
vector bundles of rank

over

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus E_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}

of non-negative integers and specialize further to

where

Of particular importance will be the case

is a matrix with one column, which we will denote by a column vector

. So the tautological class

is associated to the family

Remark 1.21

We have the obvious relation

q_{N}=q_{M}

if

is obtained from

by permuting the columns.

Proposition 1.22

For the product of the tautological classes

and

, we have

q_{N}\cdot q_{M}=q_{N\amalg M}\,,

where we write

for a matrix obtained by taking all columns from both

and

and forming a new matrix. (This follows from Proposition

.)

For the coproduct of the tautological class

, we have

\Delta(q_{N})=\sum_{N=P\amalg Q}q_{P}\otimes q_{Q}\,,

where the sum is over all ways to split the set of columns of

into two subsets.

It follows that the

-vector space spanned by the

, for all

, and all

-matrices

of non-negative integers, is a Hopf subalgebra of

.

Proof.

Let us prove the formula for the coproduct. We need some more notation. Let us write
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
for the product of all

, where

. Let us write

for the indexing set of the columns of

. For a subset

, let us write

and

The Künneth decomposition of the diagonal of

So by Proposition

, we have

\displaystyle\Delta(q_{N})=

\displaystyle\Delta(\eta(\delta_{J}/E_{J}/{\mathbb{P}}^{N},[{\mathbb{P}}^{N}]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=J_{1}\amalg J_{2}}\sum_{N=N_{1}+N_{2}}\eta(\delta_{J_{1}}/E_{J}/{\mathbb{P}}^{N},[{\mathbb{P}}^{N_{1}}])\otimes\eta(\delta_{J_{2}}/E_{J}/{\mathbb{P}}^{N},[{\mathbb{P}}^{N_{2}}])

\displaystyle\sum_{J=J_{1}\amalg J_{2}}\sum_{N=N_{1}+N_{2}}\eta(\delta_{J_{1}}/E_{J_{1}}/{\mathbb{P}}^{N},[{\mathbb{P}}^{N_{1}}])\otimes\eta(\delta_{J_{2}}/E_{J_{2}}/{\mathbb{P}}^{N},[{\mathbb{P}}^{N_{2}}])\,,

Definition 1.23

We call the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector subspace of
H_{\ast}
generated by the

the

Pardon-Hopf algebra (of finite maps to
d
-folds).

Remark 1.24

This is, indeed, a Hopf algebra, it is bigraded, although all homological degrees are even, so this Hopf algebra is commutative and cocommutative without the ‘graded’ qualifier. It will turn out that the space of primitives is equal to the subspace of cycle degree 1.

Note that the
d\times 1
-matrices with non-negative integer entries form a multiplicative generating set for the tautological algebra. Let us write
d\times 1
-matrices as column vectors
\vec{n}
, and so
q_{\vec{n}}
for these multiplicative generators. There is exactly one

matrix, namely the empty one. The associated tautological horizontal class is
1\in H_{\ast}
, the multiplicative unit. Taking this into account, we see that the multiplicative generators

are primitive (this is anyway clear, because degree one elements are automatically primitive).

Proposition 1.25

The
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
P
of primitive elements in the tautological algebra is spanned by the

, where
\vec{n}
runs over the column vectors of size

, with non-negative integer entries. The symmetric group on
d

acts on these generators by permuting the entries of

. The space

. We can canonically identify

(or the graded dual of the cohomology of

, where

There cannot be any primitives which are not in the span of the

. But this subalgebra is the entire tautological algebra.

gives an isomorphic family of maps, and hence an equal element in

. It remains to show that the

are

Let

, and

.

Denote

, and the bundle projection by

. So

We have proved that

We see that all vertical classes are tautological.

In fact, the tautological algebra is equal to the polynomial ring

, because it is graded and

is non-zero, as can be seen from the enumerative theory (see below), which is

, for all

Example 1.28

We define the

2
Enumerative theories

Definition 2.1

The
cohomology Pardon algebra
is defined as

H^{\ast}=\mathop{{\lim\limits_{\textstyle\longleftarrow}}}\limits_{X/S}H^{\ast}(ZX)\,,

the connecting maps being cohomology pullback. Following Pardon, we call elements of
H^{\ast}
,
enumerative theories
.

Thus,
an enumerative theory

, associates to every family of
d
-folds
X\to S
, and every integer
n
, a cohomology class

e(n,X/S)\in H^{\ast}(Z_{n}X)\,.

The only condition is that this class is natural with respect to pullbacks of families.

Enumerative theories are formal sums:
e(X/S)=\sum_{n\geq 0}e(n,X/S)
. If
e(n,X/S)
is the only non-zero term in this sum,
e(X/S)
is
homogeneous
of degree
n
.

Example 2.2

(Chern classes of tangent bundle)

Let
n
be a non-negative integer, and
P
be a symmetric function. We define a homogeneous enumerative theory of degree
n
by

e_{P}(n,X/S)=P(T_{Z_{n}X/S})\in H^{\ast}(Z_{n}X)\,.

(We substitute the Chern roots of the relative tangent bundle of
Z_{n}X\to S
into the symmetric function
P
.)
This defines an enumerative theory, because relative tangent bundles pull back under pullback of families, and their cycle spaces, and Chern classes are natural, as well.

Joining all the
e_{P}(n)
together, we obtain
e_{P}=\sum_{n}e_{P}(n)
.

Example 2.3

(Powers of total Chern class and Euler class)

Define

c^{k}(X/S)=c(T_{ZX/S})^{k}\in H^{\ast}(ZX)\,.

Here,
c
denotes the total Chern class.
Write

e^{k}(X/S)=e(T_{ZX/S})\in H^{\ast}(ZX)\,,

where
e
denote the Euler class.

Both
c^{k}
and
e^{k}
are enumerative theories.

Example 2.4

Let
d=1
, so that

has a coarse moduli space

, which is smooth over

.
Then

an enumerative theory, because the formation of the coarse moduli space does not commute with pullbacks. For it to commute with pullbacks, we would need our cycle stacks to have finite stabilizers, which would require the families to be separated.

Pairing with homology.

There is a natural pairing

H^{\ast}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}H_{\ast}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}\,,

induced by the pairing

H^{\ast}(Z_{n}X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}H_{\ast}(Z_{n}X)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}\,,

for every family
X/S
.
Via this pairing,
H^{\ast}
is the dual of
H_{\ast}
as a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space.
We denote this pairing by

.

Example 2.5

The coproduct of symmetric functions and the induced inhomogeneous enumerative theories are compatible with the product in

\langle e_{\Delta P},\alpha\otimes\beta\rangle=\langle e_{P},\alpha\cdot\beta\rangle\,.

Thus, we obtain a coalgebra morphism from the coalgebra

Example 2.6

For proper

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle c^{k},[Z_{n}X]\rangle T^{n}=(e^{T})^{\langle c(T_{X})^{k},[X]\rangle}

Product.

As
H^{\ast}
is the dual of
H_{\ast}
, the coproduct on
H_{\ast}
induces a product on
H^{\ast}
. Explicitly, if

and

are enumerative theories, their product

is defined by

\langle c\cdot e,\alpha\rangle=\langle c\otimes e,\Delta(\alpha)\rangle\,.

Another way to say this is

The multiplication is graded with respect to both degrees. It is graded commutative with respect to cohomological degree. Thus

is a bigraded

-algebra.
Cohomology is not a Hopf algebra, as it lacks the coproduct, although there is a formal coproduct.

2.1
Multiplicative theories

Definition 2.8

Multiplicative theories are those theories
e
, which satisfy

\langle e,\alpha\cdot\beta\rangle=\langle e,\alpha\rangle\langle e,\beta\rangle\,,

for elements
\alpha,\beta\in H_{\ast}
. Equivalently, for any two families
X\to S
and
Y\to T
we have

e((X_{T}\amalg Y_{S})/(S\times T))=e(X/S)\times e(Y/T)\,.

Remark 2.9

In terms of the formal coproduct on
H^{\ast}
this is equivalent to

\Delta(e)=e\hat{\otimes}e\,,

or that
e
is group-like, with respect to the formal Hopf algebra structure on theories.

Multiplicative theories are never homogeneous (except the trivial one). The degree
0
part of any multiplicative theory is 1.

Multiplicative theories are determined by their values on primitive homology classes.

Remark 2.10

Multiplicative theories form a commutative group under multiplication of theories. This follows from the fact that the comultiplication on
H_{\ast}
is multiplicative.

Example 2.11

The theories
c^{k}
and
e^{k}
are multiplicative.

Example 2.12

Let
P
be a multiplicative generating function, or a group-like element of
\hat{\Lambda}
, the formal completion of the Hopf algebra of symmetric functions. This means that

P(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}P(x_{i})\,,

for a power series
P(x)=1+t_{1}x+t_{2}x^{2}+\ldots
.
Equivalently,

P=\exp\sum_{n\geq 1}{a_{n}p_{n}}\,,

where the
p_{n}
are the power sum symmetric functions (which are primitive elements in the Hopf algebra of symmetric functions). Substituting Chern roots for the variables
x_{i}
, the induced characteristic class is multiplicative, i.e.,
P(E_{1}\oplus E_{2})=P(E_{1})P(E_{2})
.

Multiplicative characteristic classes

define multiplicative enumerative theories by substituting the Chern roots of
T_{ZX/S}
into

. We denote the multiplicative theory defined by
P
by

, see
2.2
and
2.5
.

e_{P}(X/S)=P(T_{ZX/S})\,.

The theory

is defined by
P(x)=(1+x)^{k}
.

2.2
Primitive theories

A theory
e\in H^{\ast}
is
primitive
if for any two
\alpha,\beta\in H_{\ast}

\langle e,\alpha\cdot\beta\rangle=\epsilon(\alpha)\langle e,\beta\rangle+\epsilon(\beta)\langle e,\alpha\rangle\,,

where
\epsilon:H_{\ast}\to{\mathbb{Q}}
is the counit (
3
).

Another way to say this is that for any two families
X\to S
, and
Y\to T
, we have

e((X_{T}\amalg Y_{S})/(S\times T))=1\times e(Y/T)+e(X/S)\times 1\,.

Remark 2.13

We can think of primitive theories as connected theories.

Primitive theories are homogeneous: a theory is primitive if and only if its homogeneous components are primitive.

Proposition 2.14

There is a bijection between primitive and multiplicative theories given by exponential and logarithm. For a primitive theory
a=\sum_{n>0}a_{n}
, we obtain a multiplicative theory

\exp(a)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}a^{n}

And for a multiplicative theory
e=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}e_{n}
we obtain a primitive theory by

\log(e)=\log(1+\sum_{n\geq 1}e_{n})=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 1}e_{n}\Big)^{k}

Under this correspondence, addition of primitive theories corresponds to multiplication of multiplicative theories.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
H^{\ast}
is complete with respect to the grading by cycle degree.

\Box

Example 2.15

\displaystyle\langle e,\alpha_{0}\beta_{1}\rangle+\langle e,\alpha_{1}\beta_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(\alpha_{0})\langle e,\beta_{1}\rangle+\epsilon(\beta_{0})\langle e,\alpha_{1}\rangle

essentially by the fact that

is connected.

Thus, for any degree 1 theory

A

is a smooth

morphism of Deligne-Mumford stacks
X\to S
. The notion of family of cycles is the same as in Section

, namely maps
D\to X
, such that

As we restrict to separated families, the moduli stack

has finite stabilizers, and hence has a good moduli space

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{Z}_{n}X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{Z}_{n}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}

This fact follows from the universality of moduli spaces in characteristic zero. Of course,
\overline{Z}_{n}X\to S
is nothing but the (relative)

-th symmetric power of

.

Let us remark that we work with relative moduli spaces, meaning that the morphism

becomes the good moduli space whenever it is pulled back to a scheme

. The stack
\overline{Z}_{n}X

.

We can interpret sections of the coarse space

.

Definition 3.1

We define

to be the colimit of the

Remark 3.2

As

As

Proposition 3.21

For a proper curve
X\to\ast
, we can express the vertical classes
[Z_{n}X]=[\overline{Z}_{n}X]\in H_{\ast}^{{\mbox{\tiny sep}},{\mbox{\tiny taut}}}
in terms of the primitive classes
p_{{\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}n\\
m\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)}}
, which we will write as

, by the formula

\sum_{n}[Z_{n}X]T^{n}=\Big(\exp\sum_{n}p_{nn}T^{n}\Big)^{\chi(X)}\,.

Unfortunately, we were not able to find such a simple formula in terms of the tautological classes
q_{nm}
.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\overline{e}=e(T_{\overline{Z}})
. The Euler class
e=e(T_{Z})
is less useful here,
\langle e,p_{nn}\rangle=0
, unless
n=1
.

We introduce formal variables
T
, which will track the degree, and
U
which will track half the homological degree.
We pair the equation

\sum_{n,m}p_{nm}T^{n}U^{m}=\log\sum_{n,m}q_{nm}T^{n}U^{m}

with
\overline{e}
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m}\langle\overline{e},p_{nm}\rangle T^{n}U^{m}=

\displaystyle\log\sum_{n,m}\langle\overline{e},q_{nm}\rangle T^{n}U^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\sum_{n,m}\delta_{nm}T^{n}U^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\frac{1}{1-TU}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n}(TU)^{n}\,.

This works because
\overline{e}
is multiplicative.
We conclude that
\langle\overline{e},p_{nm}\rangle=0
, if
n\not=m
, and

\langle\overline{e},p_{nn}\rangle=\frac{1}{n}
.

We consider a proper curve
X\to\ast
. Since
\sum_{n}[Z_{n}X]T^{n}U^{n}
is group-like, its logarithm is primitive, and therefore a linear combination of the
p_{n,m}T^{n}U^{m}
.

\log\sum_{n}[Z_{n}X](TU)^{n}=\sum_{n,m}a_{nm}p_{nm}T^{n}U^{m}\,.

Projecting into total degree zero, we get the equation

\log\sum_{n}[Z_{n}X](TU)^{n}=\sum_{n}a_{nn}p_{nn}(TU)^{n}\,.

Let us pair this equation with
\overline{e}
:

\log\sum_{n}\langle\overline{e},[Z_{n}X]\rangle(TU)^{n}=\sum_{n}a_{nn}\langle\overline{e},p_{nn}\rangle(TU)^{n}\,,

or

\log\Big(\frac{1}{1-TU}\Big)^{\chi(X)}=\sum_{n}\frac{a_{nn}}{n}(TU)^{n}\,,

which implies that
a_{nn}=\chi(X)
, for all
n
. We conclude that

\log\sum_{n}[Z_{n}X](TU)^{n}=\chi(X)\sum_{n}p_{nn}(TU)^{n}\,,

or,

\sum_{n}[Z_{n}X](TU)^{n}=\Big(\exp\sum_{n}p_{nn}(TU)^{n}\Big)^{\chi(X)}\,,

which expresses the vertical classes
[Z_{n}X]
in terms of the primitive horizontal classes
p_{nn}
.

\Box

) has no poles, i.e., it is an element of
{\mathbb{Q}}[\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}][[T]]
. Therefore, the following makes sense:

\log\sum_{n\geq 0}\langle e,[Z_{n}X]\rangle T^{n}=\int_{[X]}\gamma\log\sum_{n,\vec{m}}\langle e,q_{n\vec{m}}\rangle\gamma^{\vec{m}}\Big(\frac{T}{\gamma}\Big)^{n}\,.

It expresses the
\langle e,[Z_{n}X]\rangle
directly in terms of the Chern numbers of
X
and the equivariant enumerative invariants
\langle e,q_{n\vec{m}}\rangle
.

This formula shows how to convert the generating function for the equivariant invariants
\langle e,q_{n\vec{m}}\rangle
into the generating function for the proper invariants
\langle e,[Z_{n}X]\rangle
.

Proof.

The expression under consideration is equal to

\sum_{n,\vec{x}}\langle e,p_{n,\vec{x}+n-1}\rangle\gamma^{\vec{x}}T^{n}\,.

This follows directly from the definitional relationship between the
p_{n\vec{m}}
and the
q_{n\vec{m}}
. This expression potentially extends over
\vec{x}
with negative entries, but they can all be expressed in terms of those with
\vec{x}\geq 0
. As
e
is an enumerative theory, all relations among the
q_{n\vec{m}}
also hold among the
\langle e,q_{n\vec{m}}\rangle
. Therefore, any terms with negative entries in an
\vec{x}
can be removed.

\Box

Remark 3.27

In fact, we have the formula even before evaluating against a multiplicative theory:

\log\sum_{n\geq 0}[Z_{n}X]T^{n}=\int_{[X]}\gamma\log\sum_{n,\vec{m}}q_{n\vec{m}}\gamma^{\vec{m}}\Big(\frac{T}{\gamma}\Big)^{n}\,.

It is a fact that the expression under the integral sign is a power series in
H_{\ast}[\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}][[T]]
, but we don’t know how to write it explicitly as such. So this formula is a bit implicit. Once we evaluate against a theory
e
, we can usually see the expansion in non-negative powers of
\gamma
, which allows us to get explicit formulas.

3.5
An example

Let us continue Example
3.7
. Consider the threefold
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
, and its Hilbert scheme
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
. The torus
T={{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}^{3}
acts on
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
in the standard way. We get an induced action on
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
. The virtual fundamental class of
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
descends to an equivariant virtual class
[\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}/T\to BT]\in H^{0}(\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}/T\to BT)
.
Recall that
H^{\ast}(BT)=R(T)={\mathbb{Q}}[\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}]={\mathbb{Q}}[\gamma]
is the symmetric algebra on the universal Chern roots, and that all bivariant groups
H^{\ast}[X/T\to BT]
are
{\mathbb{Q}}[\gamma]
-modules. Let us denote by
{\mathbb{Q}}(\gamma)
the quotient field of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{Q}}[\gamma]
.

Denote by
F_{n}
the fixed locus (the set of fixed points) of the action of
T
on
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
. Let
i:F_{n}\to\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
be the inclusion morphism. By the equivariant localization theorem, we have

[\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}/T\to BT]=i_{\ast}\frac{1}{e(N_{F/H})}\,,

once we tensor
H^{\ast}(\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}\to BT)
(over
{\mathbb{Q}}[\gamma]
) with
{\mathbb{Q}}(\gamma)
. Here
e(N_{F/H})
is the Euler class of the virtual normal bundle to the inclusion
i:F_{n}\to\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
. This Euler class is contained in
H^{\ast}(F_{n}\times BT)=\bigoplus_{x\in F_{n}}{\mathbb{Q}}[\gamma]
. Once tensored with
{\mathbb{Q}}(\gamma)
, it is an element of
\bigoplus_{x\in F_{n}}{\mathbb{Q}}(\gamma)
, and is an invertible element of degree
0
there.

Let
E={\mathscr{O}}(1,0,0)+{\mathscr{O}}(0,1,0)+{\mathscr{O}}(0,0,1)
be the tautological rank 3 bundle on
{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}={\mathbb{P}}^{m_{1}}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m_{2}}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m_{3}}
. There is a canonical morphism
{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}\to BT
, such that
E
is the pullback of the universal split rank 3 bundle. The relative Hilbert scheme of
E
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}
fits into a cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{Z}_{n}E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}/T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{Z}_{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}/T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BT}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Chasing through this diagram, we see that

\displaystyle\langle f_{\ast}e^{\rm vir}(\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits),q_{n,\vec{m}}\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{\ast}[\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}E\to{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}],0_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{\ast}[\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}\to BT],0_{\ast}\pi_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\rangle

Using notation as in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{n}/T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}/T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}/T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{Z}_{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}/T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

we continue the calculation:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{\ast}i_{\ast}\frac{1}{e(N_{F/H})},0_{\ast}\pi_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\ast}j_{\ast}\frac{1}{e(N_{F/H})},0_{\ast}\pi_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle j_{\ast}\frac{1}{e(N_{F/H})},0_{\ast}\pi_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\frac{e(T_{Z_{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}})}{e(N_{F/H})},\pi_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\rangle

So evaluating the Hilbert enumerative theory on the tautological homology class
q_{n\vec{m}}
amounts to evaluating the equivariant Donaldson-Thomas invariant
\frac{1}{e(N_{F/H})}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\gamma)
, multiplied by
e(T_{Z_{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}})
to raise it into degree
6n
, on the homology class
\pi_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\in H_{6n}(BT)
.

In
[
8
]
it is proved that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{n}}{e(N_{F_{n}/H})}={M(-T)}^{-\frac{(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})(\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3})(\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{1})}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}}}

Substituting
T
with
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}T
, we deduce

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}T)^{n}}{e(N_{F_{n}/H^{n}})}={M(-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}T)}^{-\frac{(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})(\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3})(\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{1})}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}}}

We have
e(T_{Z_{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}})=(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})^{n}
, and so

\displaystyle\sum_{n,\vec{m}}\langle f_{\ast}e^{\rm vir}(\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits),q_{n,\vec{m}}\rangle T^{n}U^{\vec{m}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,\vec{m}}\langle\frac{e(T_{Z_{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{3}})}{e(N_{F/H})},\pi_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\rangle T^{n}U^{\vec{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{m}}\langle{M(-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}T)}^{-\frac{(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})(\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3})(\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{1})}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}}},\pi_{\ast}[{\mathbb{P}}^{\vec{m}}]\rangle U^{\vec{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{M(-U_{1}U_{2}U_{3}T)}^{-\frac{(U_{1}+U_{2})(U_{2}+U_{3})(U_{3}+U_{1})}{U_{1}U_{2}U_{3}}}

This gives also

We rewrite this as:

\sum_{n,\vec{x}}\langle e,p_{n,\vec{x}+n-1}\rangle T^{n}U^{\vec{x}}=-(U_{1}+U_{2})(U_{2}+U_{3})(U_{3}+U_{1})\log M(-T)

Now we substitute
U_{i}
with
\gamma_{i}
and pair with
[X]

\sum_{n,\vec{x}}\langle e,p_{n,\vec{x}+n-1}\rangle\langle\gamma^{\vec{x}},[X]\rangle T^{n}=-\langle(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})(\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3})(\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{1}),[X]\rangle\log M(-T)

Now applying Theorem

. In the Calabi-Yau case (
