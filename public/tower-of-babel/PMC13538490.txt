Quantum-inspired hybrid optimization framework for energy-efficient clustering and routing in wireless sensor networks
Authors: Ayush Mahanta, Rohan Gupta, Rachit Manchanda, Ankush Mehta, Abhijit Bhowmik, Nagaraj Ashok
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes QIHOR-WSN, a Quantum-Inspired Hybrid Optimization Framework that jointly optimizes clustering and routing in Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) to address the dual challenge of energy depletion and network lifetime in large-scale, heterogeneous deployments. In resource-constrained WSNs with unevenly distributed nodes and limited battery capacity, existing methods treat clustering and routing as separate problems, yielding suboptimal global performance and insufficient adaptability to dynamic network conditions. The proposed framework integrates Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization with a Genetic Algorithm to achieve a balanced exploration–exploitation trade-off and superior convergence characteristics. An innovative multi-objective fitness criterion is developed by collectively addressing residual energy, node density, communication distance, and network stability to facilitate energy-conscious cluster-head selection and the most energy-efficient routing paths. The framework presents a density-load-aware radio energy model that extends the classical first-order radio model to account for spatial heterogeneity and communication interference. A complete algorithmic implementation is provided, accompanied by a theoretical complexity analysis establishing O(T·(N2 + P·N)) computational cost and O(P·N) memory requirements. Comprehensive simulations over 1,000 rounds on 100-node random deployments demonstrate that QIHOR-WSN consistently outperforms all five baseline protocols. Against classical protocols (LEACH, DEEC), network lifetime improvements reach 41–120% (FND metric); against the strongest hybrid baseline (Hybrid PSO-GA), QIHOR-WSN achieves 21.5% longer FND, 10.2% lower total energy consumption at round 1,000, 27.9% extended high-PDR operation, and 22.6% lower end-to-end delay—all statistically significant over 30 independent simulation runs (CV ≤ 5.6%). These gains confirm the robustness, scalability, and practical suitability of QIHOR-WSN for next-generation WSN applications including industrial IoT, environmental monitoring, and smart city infrastructure.Keywords: Wireless sensor networks, Quantum-inspired optimization, Quantum particle swarm optimization, Genetic algorithm, Hybrid metaheuristic, Clustering, Routing, Energy efficiency, Network lifetime, Internet of things, Load balancing, Multi-objective optimizationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

WSNs have emerged as one of the most critical enabling technologies, finding deployment across a wide range of real-world applications, such as environmental monitoring and precision agriculture, industrial automation, structural health monitoring and smart healthcare systems1–4. A WSN typically consists of a large number of spatially distributed sensor nodes—each equipped with sensing, processing, and wireless communication capability—that collaborate to collect and relay data to a central base station (BS). The rapid growth of Internet of Things (IoT) systems has only served to accelerate the rate of deployment of large-scale WSN deployments where long-lasting, energy-autonomous service is a key requirement to long-term sustainability of networks5,6. Moreover, since battery resources are finite and in most deployments non-replaceable, energy efficiency is the paramount design constraint of WSN architectures7. Communication operations—particularly data transmission—are far more energy-intensive than sensing or local computation, and thus constitute the primary driver of energy depletion and curtailed network lifetime8,9. Without an effective mechanism for distributing energy consumption across the network, energy hotspots form around cluster heads and base stations, ultimately leading to premature node failure and network partitioning. Clustering-based hierarchical solutions have therefore been widely adopted to reduce energy consumption and extend network lifetime. In a clustered architecture, sensor nodes are organized into groups, each governed by a Cluster Head (CH) that aggregates and forwards data to the base station. Adaptive CH selection has been shown to improve energy efficiency in protocols like LEACH8, SEP11, HEED10, and DEEC12, but the protocols are based upon probabilistic or heuristic adapted approaches, which do not consider spatial heterogeneity, imbalance in communication loads and dynamic changes in state of nodes that are observed in real-world examples13. Concurrently, routing optimization is equally critical to overall WSN performance. Position-sensitive routing tries to reduce the overall transmission expenses, and distribute the load evenly and uniformly, extending the lifetime of the network14. Despite these advances, the predominant approach in the existing literature treats clustering and routing as separate optimization sub-problems, solved sequentially and independently15. This decoupling introduces inherent inefficiency: even a globally optimal routing plan cannot compensate for a poor CH assignment, and vice versa, resulting in unnecessary energy waste and accelerated node failure. Only a joint formulation that simultaneously solves both sub-problems within a unified framework can achieve global optimality. A number of metaheuristic-based optimization algorithms have been applied to the NP-hard joint clustering and routing problem in WSNs including Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)17, Genetic Algorithms (GA)18, Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)19, Gray Wolf Optimizer (GWO)20, and Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO)21. Such algorithms are competitive and provide global search alternatives22 with drawbacks like premature convergence, parameter selection sensitivity, and exploration–exploitation imbalance in particular in high dimension, multi objective optimization of WSNs. Quantum-inspired optimization algorithms are a more recent paradigm that borrows mathematical formalisms from quantum mechanics—such as probabilistic amplitude representation and the delta-potential-well position model—to design classical metaheuristic algorithms with improved exploration characteristics28. These algorithms run entirely on classical hardware; the term “quantum-inspired” refers to conceptual analogies rather than actual quantum computation. In Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization (QPSO)28, a delta-potential-well model represents particle position as a probability distribution, enabling broader search space exploration than classical velocity-position PSO. QPSO/genetic operator combinations have been shown to improve convergence in complex optimization landscapes29,30. Based on these results, the present paper introduces the Quantum-inspired Hybrid Optimization Framework for WSNs (QIHOR-WSN), a novel architecture in which clustering and routing are jointly formulated as a single unified optimization problem, solved using a hybrid strategy combining Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization (QPSO) for probabilistic exploration with Genetic Algorithm (GA) operators for local refinement. The model further introduces a density-load-sensitive radio energy model that provides more realistic and accurate network energy dynamics by incorporating spatial node density, communication load, and network stability—attributes absent from classical first-order energy models.

Motivation and problem context

The rationale behind QIHOR-WSN is based on three main observations of the literature analysis. First, more traditional clustering algorithms (LEACH, DEEC and HEED) will save energy by using localization heuristics, but do not cover the entire network, resulting in spatially imbalanced energy loss. Second, the current metaheuristic frameworks are used to optimize clustering or routing separately; a joint optimization problem has an entirely different and more complex solution landscape that will require sophisticated algorithmic principles. Third, quantum-inspired schemes, though proven to be useful in search space exploration, have not been applied before to the unified joint clustering and routing optimization in heterogeneous WSNs, which is a novel and unexplored research area.

Research scope and application context

The proposed QIHOR-WSN architecture is aimed at the context of static or quasi-static WSN deployments—the most common deployment model in the context of such applications as environmental monitoring, agricultural field sensing, smart building infrastructure, and industrial process control—where network lifetime and energy efficiency have been the key drivers of its design. It is configured to handle interconnection conditions that are defined by: (i) the non-uniform energy distributions of nodes, moved by time-dependent depletion; (ii) spatially non-homogeneous node locations; and (iii) time-varying loads of communication by bursty or periodic sensing activities. The framework will be protocol-agnostic with regards to physical-layer and MAC-layer technology and it is analyzed under the popular first-order radio energy model together with the addition of density and load factors. This guarantees wide applicability in practice WSN hardware platforms16.

Related work

This section presents a structured review of the relevant literature, organized into four thematic categories: namely: classical clustering protocols, metaheuristic optimization techniques, hybrid optimization methods, and quantum-inspired methods. Each category is described by a survey table and then critically analyzed to define certain limitations and gaps in research that will drive the proposed work.

Classical clustering protocols

The basis of the effort in WSN energy management lies in hierarchical clustering, in which the network gets divided into clusters to minimize long distance transmissions. LEACH8, pioneered by Heinzelman et al., in 2002, is the classic protocol in this genre. LEACH uses a round-based, stochastic rotation scheme of CH such that each node randomly selects itself as a CH with a desired fraction p. Although the simplicity and distributed working of LEACH is an excellent feature, the random selection of CH disregards the residual energy, spatial location, and the density of nodes, causing uneven energy exhaustion and energy hotspot in compact or peripherally situated areas. SEP11 generalized LEACH to networks with heterogeneous nodes assigning weighted CH election probability depending on the initial node energy ratios and DEEC12 went a step further to include residual energy in the CH election process, which provided longer network lifetimes. HEED10 used node energy and node degree to select CH which enhanced load distribution. PEGASIS9 came up with a chain-based data aggregation scheme which also shortened transmission distances by allowing node-to-node forwarding. These incremental enhancements notwithstanding, none of the classical protocols provides the global optimization needed to simultaneously achieve energy efficiency, load balancing, and routing optimization and are unable to adapt to the dynamically changing energy state of long-period deployments.

As shown in Table 1, classical clustering protocols achieve energy savings primarily through restructured communication patterns rather than through optimization of CH selection parameters. The major commonality of all five protocols is that none of them attempt to account for global network state knowledge in the selection of CHs. In particular, none of these protocols accounts for spatial density variation within clusters, communication load imbalance, or dynamic routing path quality—structural gaps that motivate joint CH-selection and routing optimization frameworks.

Metaheuristic optimization techniques

Given the NP-hard character of optimal clustering and routing problems, researchers brought metaheuristic optimization algorithms to WSN design. PSO17 solves the clustering problem with rapid adaptation through encoding the selection of CHs as particle positions and optimization of optimal configurations with swarm intelligence. GA18 is able to maintain diversity to a greater extent using cross operators and mutation operators, whereas it would be greater with multi-modal fitness landscapes. ACO19 builds on the use of a pheromone-based path construction and in this regard is more effective in advancing routing. This is mirrored by more recent nature-based algorithms such as GWO20 or HHO21 which have been applied to several WSN clustering and routing sub-problems with competitive results. Critical analysis reveals that each of these algorithms exhibits characteristic pathologies in high-dimensional WSN optimization: PSO suffers from velocity saturation and premature stagnation in local optima; GA incurs high computational cost per generation; ACO is sensitive to pheromone evaporation parameters and scales poorly; and GWO and HHO lack theoretical guarantees for multi-objective solution quality. Importantly, none of these approaches addresses clustering and routing as a joint problem—each optimizes only one sub-problem in isolation.

Table 2 validates the fact that although metaheuristic algorithms build a great optimization strength than the classical algorithms, they always lack in extending the optimization framework that will undertake both clustering and routing instances. The table, further, indicates that none of the existing single- algorithm strategies offer a good trade-off between exploration (diversity maintenance) and exploitation (velocity of convergence) in high-dimensional WSN optimization issues.

Hybrid optimization frameworks

To address the shortcoming of individual metaheuristic algorithms, hybrid models consisting of two or more algorithms have been suggested. It uses PSO-GA hybrids23, which use diversity-maintaining crossover operators on PSO, taking advantage of its speed of convergence in exploration; and better results in joint-clustering-routing tasks. Fuzzy-PSOs24 uses fuzzy logic in adaptive CH selection thresholds and GA-ACOs strategies/combos25 use the effectiveness of combining both the GA and the ACO in the optimization of multi-hop routing paths. More recent hybrids that incorporate machine learning26,27 use predictive models to infer load-optimal routing, but have large training costs and need labelled training.

With such improvements, hybrid frameworks come with new issues: higher complexity in the algorithms, and lacks coordination across hyperparameters among component algorithms, and are heavily scaled to large-scale networks. Moreover, all of the available hybrid models include no quantum-inspired search algorithms, which is why a huge gap between the quality that can be achieved during optimization and the state-of-the-art results remains.

Quantum-inspired optimization techniques

Quantum-inspired optimization is paradigm shifting in the algorithm design of evolutionary and swarm intelligence. QPSO28, proposed by Sun et al., is an update of the velocity-position update of classical PSO, replacing the deterministic position with a probability distribution in position space, in which each particle occupies a probability distribution over position space. This essentially increases the effective searches radius, and removes the parameter tuning based on the velocity, resulting in better global search ability. Quantum Genetic Algorithms (QGA)29 map the genes onto quantum probability amplitudes, allowing superposition of a large number of possible solutions, and enhancing the number of possible solutions originally available dramatically. More recent attempts to apply quantum-inspired algorithms to WSN clustering30 and routing31,32 have been shown to have far more impressive performance benefits, however, these are attempts to apply quantum algorithms to single sub-problems without a thorough approach to integrating clustering and routing into a single solution.

Recent machine learning and AI-driven approaches

The latest published amount of literature (2023–2026) is characterized by an increasing desire to include machine learning and deep reinforcement learning (DRL) in the WSN optimization34–38. Reward-signal-based policy learning using reinforcement learning-based frameworks34 allows adapting the selection of CH and routing, and deep neural network methods35 forecast the optimal routing paths based on traffic history. These algorithms demonstrate scalability in very dynamic environments but need a large amount of training data, the initial policy learning energies are high, and the algorithms do not have a theoretical converge continuum to the space of WSN energy depletion dynamics. More recently, Romanowski51 proposed K-NN-PEGASIS, a data-driven machine learning framework that applies a weighted k-nearest neighbours algorithm for intelligent leader selection in PEGASIS-based WSNs. By processing a normalised feature vector comprising residual energy, distance to the base station, node degree, and historical reliability, K-NN-PEGASIS adaptively selects optimal leaders in each round. Simulations on networks up to 1000 nodes demonstrated up to 47.3% improvement in network lifetime and 52.8% reduction in energy dissipation, while also showing passive resilience against routing attacks—reducing selection of malicious leaders by 96%51. Additionally, Ahmad et al.52 provide a comprehensive overview of ML algorithms for WSN security, demonstrating that ML can simultaneously address energy efficiency and security constraints, which is directly relevant to the deployment context of QIHOR-WSN. In a related contribution, Juwaied53 proposed EMO-PEGASIS, a dual-phase ML protocol combining K-means clustering with K-NN chain formation to jointly optimise energy consumption and transmission delay in PEGASIS-based WSNs, demonstrating that multi-objective ML-driven approaches yield superior trade-offs compared to single-objective classical protocols.

Recent energy-efficient hybrid algorithms for WSNs

Recent literature has introduced a variety of bio-inspired and hybrid clustering models that advance energy efficiency in WSNs. Lonkar and Karmore46 proposed BCEWN, a hybrid bioinspired clustering model for energy-aware wireless networks that demonstrated significant improvements in cluster head selection through combining multiple bio-inspired heuristics with adaptive threshold mechanisms. The work identified that purely probabilistic CH selection fails to account for dynamic energy depletion patterns, leading to premature node failures in heterogeneous deployments. Lonkar et al.47 conducted an empirical study on optimal hybrid energy-saving cluster head selection, evaluating combinations of swarm intelligence algorithms for WSN cluster head election and concluded that hybrid approaches consistently outperform single-algorithm baselines in terms of network lifetime and load balancing. Lonkar and Karmore48 performed a statistical evaluation of power-aware routing protocols for wireless networks, providing a rigorous comparative framework showing that energy-aware routing must be coupled with adaptive clustering to yield maximum network lifetime extension. More recently, Lonkar and Karmore49 proposed EBBSMWN, an energy-aware blockchain-based bioinspired security model for wireless networks that integrates trust management with energy-efficient cluster formation, revealing that security constraints further complicate the clustering-routing trade-off in practical deployments. Lonkar et al.50 presented a comprehensive review of hybrid clustering and routing protocols targeting the hotspot problem in wireless sensor networks, systematically identifying that existing protocols fail to simultaneously resolve load imbalance, spatial heterogeneity, and routing inefficiency—precisely the research gaps addressed by the proposed QIHOR-WSN framework. These recent contributions underscore the growing recognition that energy efficiency in WSNs requires joint consideration of clustering, routing, and network stability, validating the integrated approach adopted in this work.

Research gap analysis and framework motivation

The systematic review presented in Tables 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, together with the recent hybrid energy-efficient approaches surveyed in Sect. “Recent energy-efficient hybrid algorithms for WSNs”46–50, reveals five critical research gaps that collectively motivate the proposed QIHOR-WSN framework. First, no existing method provides a unified mathematical formulation of the joint clustering-routing optimization problem that explicitly incorporates spatial density heterogeneity, communication load imbalance, and node stability—three variables essential to accurate energy modeling in heterogeneous WSN deployments. Second, although quantum-inspired methods have demonstrated advantages in search-space exploration, no prior work has applied them to a combined clustering-routing optimization of WSNs. Third, existing hybrid frameworks lack a theoretically grounded density-aware energy model, limiting their ability to predict and prevent energy hotspots. Fourth, current single-objective or lexicographically prioritized WSN optimization formulations do not fully capture the multi-objective nature of the problem, which simultaneously involves energy, lifetime, throughput, and delay. Fifth, there is no existing framework that will offer a thorough analysis of complexity-performance trade-offs that will define the limits of feasibility of the proposed approach in practice. QIHOR-WSN directly fills in all the five gaps with: (i) density, load-sensitive energy model, (ii) quantum-inspired probabilistic solution encoding in a combined optimization engine, (iii) the QPSO-GA hybrid optimal objective model, (iv) the five-term multi-objective fitness function, and (v) full complexity modeling. This has resulted in a complete solution, making QIHOR-WSN an original contribution at the quantum-inspired computing and optimization of WSN. It is important to note that, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, no prior work has simultaneously addressed all five of these gaps within a single unified framework. Existing quantum-inspired methods (e.g.,30–32) address clustering or routing in isolation; existing hybrid frameworks (e.g.,23–27) improve optimization quality but operate on decoupled sub-problems; and existing energy models (e.g.,8,12) do not incorporate spatial density and communication load as first-class modeling variables. QIHOR-WSN is therefore not an incremental extension of any single prior work but a new architectural approach whose original scientific contribution lies in the principled integration of these previously disjoint research strands.

System model and mathematical formulations

The following part describes a full mathematical system model that supports QIHOR-WSN framework. This model is formulated to model the critical physical processes that control the dynamics of energy in WSN with enhanced accuracy compared to classical models, but with the benefit that it is computationally amenable. The main novelty is the clear inclusion of spatial node density, communication load, and network stability in the energy consumption model and formulation of the optimization problem.

Network deployment and communication architecture

The hierarchical communication architecture of the proposed framework operates in two stages per round: intra-cluster and an inter-cluster routing stage. In the intra-cluster step, every non-CH node forwards the sensed data to their designated cluster head, via a single-hop point-to-point path. At the inter-cluster routing stage, the corresponding CH handles the received data and forwards it to the BS through the optimized multi-hop based on the routing optimization module. The change in density in the deployment region is evident, whereby greater densities in the centrally located areas and lesser densities in the fringes. Such spatial heterogeneity is a direct inspiration of the density-conscious method of modeling that will be used in this work: nodes in high-density areas will have a higher level of interference and load on communication, which will accelerate energy depletion compared to isolated nodes.

Density-load-aware radio energy model

Proposed QIHOR-WSN framework

This section shows the full architectural design and mathematical formulation of the proposed QIHOR-WSN. The model aims to jointly optimize the number of cluster heads, routing paths in order to realize an integrated quantum-inspired hybrid optimization system. The system is made up of three tightly-integrated modules: (i) the system parameter extraction module, (ii) the quantum-inspired optimization engine containing QPSO and GA modules, and (iii) the routing decision module.

Algorithm overview and execution flow

The QIHOR-WSN algorithm executes in two nested loops: an outer per-round loop that governs one complete sensing and communication cycle, and an inner per-iteration loop that executes the QPSO-GA optimization engine within each round. In each round t, the outer loop: (1) updates node state vectors based on energy consumption from the previous round, (2) invokes the optimization engine to determine optimal CH assignments and routing paths for round t, (3) executes intra-cluster data collection and aggregation, and (4) executes inter-cluster multi-hop forwarding along the optimized routing paths.

Computational complexity analysis

The step-by-step computational complexity of QIHOR-WSN is discussed. Density estimation (Eq. 2) involves O(N2) pairwise computations of distances per round. There is O(P × N) population initialization needed from time to time. Using the optimization iteration, QPSO update scales O(P), CA crossover scales O(P × N) (applying operators to P solutions (length N)) and fitness scales O(P N) and routing scales O(N × k) ≈ O(N) where k is the average node degree. The total sum of all the terms multiplied by T rounds is:

Over R simulation rounds: O(R × (N2 + T × P × N)). This value is quite large at R = 1000, T = 100, P = 30, N = 100, and is estimated to be about 3.1 × 10⁸ elementary operations, or about 0.8–1.2 s per simulation on typical hardware. Storage of particles has a space complexity of O(P × N), and the density matrix has a space complexity of O(N2), making O(N2) the most important space complexities.

Parameter sensitivity analysis

Another practical consideration is how QIHOR-WSN decays with important parameters. The value of P is supported by a grid search of the QPSO population size P ∈ {10, 20, 30, 50} shows a decline in returns after P = 30 and an improvement of performance after 8.2 and 2.1 when P = 10 to P = 20 and P = 30 to P = 50. It can be seen in the contraction factor range [0.4–0.6, 0.9–1.1] that anything below [0.450, 0.6] results in poor performance since either the range is too small to explore or to exploit, respectively. The range [0.7, 0.9] is in the range of relatively robust crossover rate of the GA, whereas the mutation rates exceeding 0.1 interfere with the quality of solutions. These weight vectors {w1-w5} = [0.35, 0.20, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10] are confirmed with exhaustive comparison with 20 alternative weight configurations as shown to be highly balanced in terms of performance. The over-weight configurations that gain longer FND but at the expense of reduced throughput (w1 > 0.5) and the over-weight configurations with intra-cluster transmission efficiency increased (w2 > 0.4), but reduced load balance. The weights picked are the Pareto-optimal of the five-metric evaluation framework.

Ablation study: contribution of quantum-inspired encoding

To isolate the contribution of the quantum-inspired probabilistic encoding from the other novel components of QIHOR-WSN (the density-load-aware energy model and the five-term fitness function), a fully controlled ablation study was conducted. QIHOR-WSN was re-executed with standard hard binary (0/1) cluster-head encoding—replacing the qubit-inspired probability amplitude representation of Eq. 11—while retaining the identical density-load-aware energy model (Eqs. 4–9) and five-term fitness function (Eq. 17). This variant, denoted QIHOR-Binary, was evaluated over 30 independent simulation runs under the same experimental conditions described in Sect. “Simulation environment and experimental setup”. The Hybrid PSO-GA baseline23 already uses the same two optimization algorithms (PSO exploration + GA refinement) as QIHOR-WSN, but uses standard binary/continuous encoding rather than the qubit-inspired probability amplitude encoding of Eq. 11. The comparison between Hybrid PSO-GA and QIHOR-WSN, holding all other parameters equal, therefore specifically isolates the effect of the encoding scheme. As shown in Table 10 and Fig. 18, QIHOR-WSN achieves F* = 0.147 versus Hybrid PSO-GA’s F* = 0.163 (a 9.8% improvement in final objective value) and converges in 38 iterations versus 52 (26.9% faster). The controlled ablation results, presented in Table 12, confirm that the quantum-inspired encoding is the primary driver of the performance advantage. QIHOR-Binary achieves FND = 375 rounds, LND = 1,138 rounds, total energy @ R = 1000 of 49.8 J, final fitness F* = 0.157, and convergence in 45 iterations—all of which are statistically inferior to QIHOR-WSN (FND = 412, LND = 1,247, energy = 47.3 J, F* = 0.147, convergence = 38 iterations; p < 0.01, Wilcoxon rank-sum test). This performance gap of 8.9% in FND and 18.4% in convergence speed is directly attributable to the smoother gradient landscape provided by continuous amplitude updates (Eq. 13) versus discrete binary crossover operations, confirming that the quantum-inspired encoding contributes independently and meaningfully to the optimization quality of QIHOR-WSN beyond the shared density-load model and fitness function.

Discussion: critical analysis and practical implications

In this section, the proposed QIHOR-WSN framework is critically analyzed with balanced considerations as it will address the strengths of the framework, limitations, trade-offs of the framework, and practical considerations in deploying the framework.

Key strengths and novel contributions

The strongest feature of QIHOR-WSN is the systematic combination of three components which are considered separately in current methods: (i) a physics-inspired energy model that accurately represents density- and load-dependent energy consumption; (ii) a quantum-inspired probabilistic optimization engine that avoids the pathologies of classical metaheuristics (premature convergence in PSO, slow convergence in GA); and (iii) a joint clustering-routing optimization formulation that exploits the structural interdependency between CH selection and multi-hop path quality. The interaction among these three elements produces performance gains that exceed the sum of the individual components, as evidenced by the ablation-style comparison of PSO-only, GA-only, Hybrid PSO-GA, and QIHOR-WSN results. The density-load-aware energy model (Eqs. 4–8) constitutes an independent, transferable contribution: it can serve as a drop-in replacement for the standard first-order model in any WSN simulation or optimization study requiring realistic energy modeling in heterogeneous deployments, and its three calibration parameters (α, β, γ) can be tuned empirically to specific hardware platforms. Critically, the novelty of QIHOR-WSN lies not in combining any two existing algorithms, but in the co-design of a mathematically grounded energy model, a unified optimization problem formulation, and a quantum-inspired search strategy that together address a problem space—joint heterogeneous WSN clustering and routing under density and load constraints—that no prior framework has treated in an integrated manner. This positioning distinguishes QIHOR-WSN from incremental extensions of single-algorithm frameworks and from existing hybrid approaches that optimize clustering and routing as decoupled sub-problems.

Limitations and boundary conditions

There are three main limitations of QIHOR-WSN examined. First, the O(N2) complexity of density estimation can be used to restrict to networks with about 200 or 300 nodes without algorithmic extensions. To scale up to very large networks (N > 1,000) density approximation methods based on distributed density computing, like spatial grid partitioning or a k-nearest-neighbor density estimator, would be necessary to simplify the calculations to O(N log N) while still making an approximation accurate enough. Second, the model assumes that the nodes are deployed in a static manner. Although this assumption applies to most of the practice WSN application cases (environmental monitoring, infrastructure sensing, industrial IoT), it rules out the mobile WSN and vehicular network application scenarios in which the positions of nodes vary between rounds. The growth to mobile deployments would necessitate position prediction schemes and mobile awareness routing cost models. Third, the weight vector w1-w5 has to undergo calibration when applied in certain situations. Although the values obtained in grid search can be highly effective in all the configurations tested, when using the production system, it is advisable to calibrate it with specific values of deployment. A mechanism of automated weight adaptation by a monitoring of online performance would also contribute to increased practical deployability of the framework.

Trade-off analysis

The primary trade-off of QIHOR-WSN lies between computational cost (O(N2 + T × P × N))45 and solution optimality. In the case of N 200, this trade-off greatly leans towards QIHOR-WSN: the computational cost (about 3.5 s per 1500 round simulation) is insignificant when used in the real network setting, where CH election and routing optimization is done at the network gateway, and not at individual sensor nodes. Further on, in the case of N over 200, cost of the density estimation will start playing dominance role and scaled-back version with spatial partitioning is expected to be useful. A key design decision is the exploration–exploitation trade-off which is controlled by adaptive contraction factor β(t) (Eq. 14). The linear high-to-low bias in schedule βmax = 1.0 to βmin = 0.5 gives an intermediate transition that would best fit the networks of 100 nodes with T = 100 iterations. On smaller network or smaller iterations it can be desired to use a more aggressive schedule (higher βmin) in order to achieve faster convergence sacrificing some solution quality. On networks that are larger and more complex, a slower schedule (smaller βmin, or nonlinear decay) can be useful to allow exploration of longer durations to time.

Sensitivity to deployment geometry: field size and sink placement

All primary experiments use a 100 × 100 m2 sensing field with the base station fixed at (50, 100) m—a boundary sink configuration commonly adopted in WSN benchmarks. Real deployments vary considerably in field area, aspect ratio, and sink placement. To assess QIHOR-WSN’s generalizability, three supplementary sensitivity conditions were examined: (i) a larger field (200 × 200 m2, N = 100), representing sparse deployment; (ii) a corner-placed sink at (100, 100) m, creating asymmetric routing cost gradients; and (iii) a centre-placed sink at (50, 50) m, favouring balanced multi-hop routing. In the sparse field, QIHOR-WSN’s relative FND advantage over Hybrid PSO-GA increased to approximately 24% (compared to 21.5% in the 100 × 100 m2 field), as the density-aware model provides greater benefit when spatial heterogeneity is more pronounced and inter-node distances are larger. The corner-sink configuration reduced the absolute FND values for all protocols (as some nodes face long-distance routing paths), but QIHOR-WSN maintained a 19–22% relative advantage due to the routing cost function’s load-imbalance penalty term (Eq. 10, w₄) redistributing traffic away from corner-proximal nodes. The centre-sink configuration produced more uniform energy depletion across all protocols, with QIHOR-WSN’s relative advantage reduced to approximately 17% (still statistically significant). These results suggest that QIHOR-WSN’s advantages are robust to moderate variations in deployment geometry, though the absolute magnitude of improvement varies with the degree of spatial heterogeneity and routing asymmetry in the deployment scenario.

Security considerations in WSN deployments

Common attack vectors in WSNs

While the primary focus of QIHOR-WSN is energy efficiency and network lifetime, practical WSN deployments face significant security threats that interact directly with routing and clustering decisions. A comprehensive treatment is beyond the scope of this paper; however, acknowledging the major attack categories is important for assessing real-world applicability. Sinkhole attacks occur when a malicious node advertises artificially high-quality routing paths to attract and drop or manipulate traffic, directly undermining the routing decisions made by frameworks such as QIHOR-WSN. Selective forwarding attacks involve compromised nodes forwarding only a subset of traffic, degrading PDR in ways indistinguishable from legitimate energy depletion. Wormhole attacks create tunnels between distant nodes, creating false route quality illusions and disrupting multi-hop path optimization. Sybil attacks involve a single malicious node presenting multiple identities, inflating its weight in cluster head selection algorithms and potentially biasing the density and stability metrics (Eqs. 1–3) that QIHOR-WSN relies upon for CH election. Hello flood attacks exploit the broadcasting of CH announcement messages to overwhelm non-CH nodes. Byzantine attacks involve compromised cluster heads colluding to degrade network performance. Romanowski51 demonstrated that a K-NN-based leader selection approach provides passive resilience against routing attacks, reducing the selection of malicious leaders by 96% in simulated adversarial scenarios, suggesting that machine learning-driven node selection provides a natural defence layer against Sybil and wormhole-style manipulations.

Security implications for QIHOR-WSN

The QIHOR-WSN framework does not currently incorporate explicit security mechanisms. The stability index Si (Eq. 3) provides a partial natural defence by down-weighting nodes with erratic CH participation history, which may indirectly penalize compromised nodes exhibiting inconsistent behaviour. The node density estimation (Eq. 2) could also be extended to incorporate anomaly detection for Sybil-like inconsistencies (where a single physical location appears to host multiple identities). Future work should investigate integrating trust-based node scoring into the multi-objective fitness function (e.g., adding a trustworthiness term w₆ × Tᵢ to Eq. 17), building on approaches such as the blockchain-based bioinspired security model of Lonkar and Karmore49 and the ML-driven intrusion detection framework of Ahmad et al.52. The gateway-centric architecture of QIHOR-WSN (Sect. “Deployment scenarios and practical applicability”) is advantageous for security: centralising the optimization computation at the gateway means that security policy enforcement, anomaly detection, and trust management can be applied without constraining the energy budget of individual sensor nodes.

Conclusion and future research directions

This paper has introduced QIHOR-WSN, a Quantum-Inspired Hybrid Optimization Framework for energy-efficient clustering and routing in Wireless Sensor Networks. The framework contributes three major innovations: (i) a density-load-aware radio energy model that extends the classical first-order model with spatial heterogeneity and communication load correction factors; (ii) a quantum-inspired probabilistic solution encoding combined with a QPSO-GA hybrid optimization engine that achieves a balanced exploration–exploitation trade-off; and (iii) a five-term multi-objective fitness function that jointly optimizes energy efficiency, load balance, spatial density, stability, and intra-cluster communication cost. Comprehensive simulation results over 30 independent network realizations demonstrate that QIHOR-WSN statistically outperforms all five baseline protocols across all evaluated metrics: 21.5% improvement in FND (first node death), 20.9% in LND (network lifetime), 10.2% reduction in total energy consumption, 27.9% extension of high-PDR operation, and 22.6% reduction in end-to-end delay—all gains statistically significant (Wilcoxon p < 0.01, CV ≤ 5.6%). These advances affirm the value of quantum-inspired diversity enhancement and joint clustering-routing formulation for WSN energy management. Theoretical complexity analysis establishes a time complexity of O(N2 + T × P × N) and space complexity of O(N2), confirming practical feasibility for networks of up to approximately 200 nodes on standard hardware. For larger deployments, approximation strategies for density computation can reduce the bottleneck term to O(N log N).
