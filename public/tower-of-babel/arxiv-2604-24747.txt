A determinant identity for the sum of contour integral matrices

Abstract

1
Introduction

Very recently, a determinant identity was derived in
[
BLL26
]
, which plays a crucial role in analyzing the energy function of the periodic totally asymmetric simple exclusion process with a general initial condition. The identity is as follows, modulo a simple conjugation.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
BLL26
]
)
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
be analytic functions on a neighborhood
\Omega_{0}
of
0
satisfying
f_{i}(0)=1
for all
i
. Let
\Gamma
be a simple contour contained in the region
\{u\in\mathbb{C}:|u+1|<1\}\setminus\{u:|u|<\epsilon\}
for some positive constant
\epsilon
, and let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
be bounded continuous functions on
\Gamma
. Suppose there exists a function
H(v,u)
on
\Omega_{0}\times\Gamma
that is analytic at
v=0
and satisfies

\oint_{0}\frac{f_{i}(v)}{v^{i}}H(v,u)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}=g_{i}(u),\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n.

(1.1)

Then,

\det\left[\oint_{0}v^{i-j-1}f_{j}(v)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}+\int_{\Gamma}u^{i-1}g_{j}(u)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}=\det(\mathrm{I}+K),

(1.2)

where
K
is an integral kernel acting on
L^{2}(\Gamma,\mathrm{d}u/2\pi\mathrm{i})
given by

K(u_{1},u_{2})=\oint_{0}\frac{u_{1}^{n}H(v,u_{2})}{v^{n}(u_{1}-v)}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}.

(1.3)

The main goal of this short note is to provide a generalization of the above identity. We remark that this generalization arises from a different problem involving the study of a general geodesic in the directed last passage percolation
[
LT26
]
. In our opinion, it is of independent interest; thus, we present it in this separate note.

Below, we state our result.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be a neighborhood of
0
, and let
\Gamma
be any simple contour on the complex plane. Let
p_{i}
and
f_{i}
be analytic functions in
\Omega_{0}
satisfying
f_{i}(0)=p_{i}(0)=1
, and
q_{i}
and
g_{i}
be square integrable continuous functions on
(\Gamma,|\mathrm{d}u|)
, for all
i
, i.e.,
\int_{\Gamma}|q_{i}(u)|^{2}|\mathrm{d}u|<\infty
and
\int_{\Gamma}|g_{i}(u)|^{2}|\mathrm{d}u|<\infty
for all
i
. Suppose there exists a function
H(v,u)
on
\Omega_{0}\times\Gamma
that is analytic at
v=0
and satisfies

\oint_{0}\frac{f_{i}(v)}{v^{i}}H(v,u)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}=g_{i}(u),\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n.

(1.4)

Then we have

\det\left[\oint_{0}v^{i-j-1}p_{i}(v)f_{j}(v)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}+\int_{\Gamma}q_{i}(u)g_{j}(u)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}=\det(\mathrm{I}+K),

(1.5)

where
K
is an integral kernel acting on
L^{2}(\Gamma,\mathrm{d}u/2\pi\mathrm{i})
given by

K(u_{1},u_{2})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}q_{\ell}(u_{1})\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{\ell}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{1},u_{2})}{\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(v_{i}-v_{i+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}v_{i}P_{i}(v_{i})\right]},

(1.6)

where
P_{1}(v):=p_{1}(v)
, and
P_{i}(v):=p_{i}(v)/p_{i-1}(v)
for
2\leq i\leq n
. The integration contours in the above equation are nested small circles around the origin such that
|v_{1}|>|v_{2}|>\cdots
.

Remark 1.3
.

A special case of these two propositions gives an identity between a Toeplitz determinant and a Fredholm determinant. However, it does not seem to have connections to other known identities, such as the well known Borodin-Okounkov identity
[
BO00
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

The kernel
K
can be simplified when we choose simple functions for
p_{i}
and
q_{i}
. In particular, if
p_{i}(v)=1
for all
i
, we can evaluate all integrals except the one for
v_{1}
. The kernel then reduces to a single integral of a function involving a sum, which can be further simplified if the
q_{i}
’s are simple functions. See Section
3
for more discussions.

Remark 1.5
.

There are infinitely many functions
H
that satisfy (
1.4
) and the analyticity assumption at
v=0
, for any given sequences of functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
. In the case when
f_{i}(v)=(v+1)^{y_{i}+i}
and
g_{i}(u)=-u^{-i}(u+1)^{y_{i}+i}
, where
y_{1}>y_{2}>\cdots>y_{n}
are integers, this
H(v,u)
function appears in the totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP) and is called the characteristic function of the initial condition
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
. It has a nice hitting expectation representation in terms of a geometric random walk, which allows one to study the asymptotics of the multipoint distribution of TASEP with a general initial condition
[
LL25
,
BLL26
]
.

In Section
2
, we verify that
K
is a trace class operator; therefore,
\det(\mathrm{I}+K)
in the above proposition is well defined. In Section
3
, we show that the kernel can be simplified when
p_{i}=1
for all
i
. In particular, under the assumptions of Proposition
1.1
, it reduces to (
1.3
). Finally, in Section
4
, we prove Proposition
1.2
.

2
Verification of a trace class operator

Note that
K
is a finite rank operator. It suffices to show that the following function is square integrable

L_{\ell}(u):=\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{\ell}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{1},u)}{\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(v_{i}-v_{i+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}v_{i}P_{i}(v_{i})\right]}

(2.1)

where the integration contours are the same as in Proposition
1.2
. We shrink the
v_{1}
contour so that it lies inside all the
v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell}
contours. This results in two terms: one with the same expression but with a modified
v_{1}
contour, and a second term consisting of
\ell-1
integrals with the
v_{1}
integral evaluated at the pole
v_{1}=v_{2}
. For the second term, we then shrink the
v_{2}
contour and repeat this procedure. This yields

L_{\ell}(u)=L_{\ell}^{(1)}(u)+L_{\ell}^{(2)}(u)+\cdots+L_{\ell}^{(\ell)}(u).

(2.2)

The functions
L_{\ell}^{(i)}
are given by

L_{\ell}^{(i)}(u)=\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{\ell}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{i},u)}{\left[\prod_{j=i}^{\ell-1}(v_{j}-v_{j+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{j=i+1}^{\ell}v_{j}P_{j}(v_{j})\right]\cdot v_{i}^{i}p_{i}(v_{i})},

(2.3)

where contour for
v_{i}
is the smallest, and the other contours remain in order:
|v_{i}|<|v_{\ell}|<|v_{\ell-1}|<\cdots<|v_{i+1}|
. We claim that each
L_{\ell}^{(i)}
is square integrable. In fact, when
i=\ell
, we have

L_{\ell}^{(\ell)}(u)=\oint\frac{H(v,u)}{v^{\ell}p_{\ell}(v)}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}c_{\ell,j}\oint_{0}\frac{f_{j}(v)}{v^{j}}H(v,u)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}c_{\ell,j}g_{j}(u),

(2.4)

where
c_{\ell,j}
are the coefficients in the series expansion

\frac{1}{v^{\ell}p_{\ell}(v)}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}c_{\ell,j}\frac{f_{j}(v)}{v^{j}}+\text{a function of $v$ analytic at $0$}.

(2.5)

Note such an expansion exists since
p_{\ell}
and
f_{j}
are all analytic at the origin, and
p_{j}(0)=f_{j}(0)=1
for each
j
.

Recall that
g_{j}
is square integrable on
\Gamma
. Thus (
2.4
) implies that
L^{(\ell)}(u)
is square integrable.

The case when
i<\ell
can be proved similarly. We write
1/(v_{i}-v_{i+1})
as the sum of a geometric series. This gives

\begin{split}L_{\ell}^{(i)}(u)&=\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{\ell}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{i},u)}{\left[\prod_{j=i+1}^{\ell-1}(v_{j}-v_{j+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{j=i+1}^{\ell}v_{j}P_{j}(v_{j})\right]\cdot v_{i}^{i}p_{i}(v_{i})}\cdot\left(-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{v_{i}^{k}}{v_{i+1}^{k+1}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\left[\text{constant depending on $k$}\right]\cdot\left[\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{i},u)}{v_{i}^{i-k}p_{i}(v_{i})}\right],\end{split}

(2.6)

where we dropped the terms for
k\geq i
due to the analyticity of the integrand at
v_{i}=0
. For each
k
, the function of
u
in the summand is square integrable by the same argument used for
L_{\ell}^{(\ell)}
. Hence,
L_{\ell}^{(i)}
is also square integrable. This completes the proof.

3
Simplification of the kernel when
p_{i}=1
for all
i

When
p_{i}(v)=1
for all
i
, we have
P_{i}(v)=1
. The
v_{i}
integrals in the kernel can be simplified

\begin{split}\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{\ell}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{1},u_{2})}{\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(v_{i}-v_{i+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}v_{i}\right]}&=\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{1},u_{2})}{v_{1}^{\ell}}\end{split}

(3.1)

by evaluating the
v_{\ell},\ldots,v_{2}
integrals consecutively (and deforming the contours to infinity). Then the kernel becomes

K(u_{1},u_{2})=\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}q_{\ell}(u_{1})v^{-\ell}\right]\cdot H(v,u_{2}).

(3.2)

The summation in the above integrand can be simplified in many cases, such as when
q_{\ell}
is of the following form

q_{\ell}(u)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}(u)\ell^{i}\right)\cdot(q(u))^{\ell},\quad\text{for all }1\leq\ell\leq n,

(3.3)

where
d\geq 1
, and
c_{i}(u)
and
q(u)
are given functions. In such a case, one can use the known formula for the expression
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ell^{i}z^{\ell}
.

In particular, under the assumptions in Proposition
1.1
, we have
q_{\ell}(u)=u^{\ell-1}
, and the kernel becomes

\begin{split}K(u_{1},u_{2})&=\oint\frac{H(v,u_{2})}{u_{1}-v}\left(\frac{u_{1}^{n}}{v^{n}}-1\right)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\\
&=\oint\frac{H(v,u_{2})}{u_{1}-v}\frac{u_{1}^{n}}{v^{n}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}-\oint\frac{H(v,u_{2})}{u_{1}-v}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Note that
u_{1}\neq v
for sufficiently small
v
, due to the assumption that
\Gamma
is at least distance
\epsilon>0
away from the origin. The second integral vanishes since the integrand is analytic at
v=0
. This leaves the first term, which matches (
1.3
).

4
Proof of Proposition
1.2

The starting point of the proof of Proposition
1.2
is the following lemma, which provides a formula for the inverse of an upper-triangular matrix whose entries are defined via contour integrals.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p_{i}
be analytic at
0
with
p_{i}(0)\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Define an
n\times n
matrix
S
by

S(i,j):=\oint_{0}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\,v^{i-j-1}p_{i}(v),\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

(4.1)

Then we have

S^{-1}=W,

(4.2)

where
W
is an
n\times n
matrix whose entries are given by

W(i,j)=\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{v_{1}^{i-1}}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

(4.3)

Here
P_{1}(v):=p_{1}(v)
, and
P_{i}(v):=p_{i}(v)/p_{i-1}(v)
for
2\leq i\leq n
, and the integration contours in the above equation are nested small circles around the origin such that
|v_{1}|>|v_{2}|>\cdots
. These notations are the same as in Proposition
1.2
.

Proof.

For convenience, we introduce
2n
nested small circles
\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{n},\ldots,\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{1},\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{n}
that are centered at the origin and ordered from the outermost to the innermost.

Assume that the functions
h_{i}(v)
,
1\leq i\leq n
, are analytic in the region between
(1+\epsilon)\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{n}
and
(1-\epsilon)\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{n}
. We have the following simple identity for the multiple contour integrals

\begin{split}&\oint_{\mathrm{C}_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint_{\mathrm{C}_{j}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}h_{\ell}(v_{\ell})}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}=j}\oint_{\mathrm{C}_{i_{1}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{1}}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint_{\mathrm{C}_{i_{k}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{k}}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\prod_{a=i_{\ell-1}+1}^{i_{\ell}}h_{a}(v_{i_{\ell}})}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(v_{i_{\ell}}-v_{i_{\ell+1}})},\end{split}

(4.4)

where we set
i_{0}=0
. The proof of this identity is as follows. We deform the contours
\mathrm{C}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{in}}
to
\mathrm{C}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{out}}
for
\ell=1,\ldots,j
subsequently. Note that the
v_{\ell}
-integral produces two contributions: one from the residue of the integrand at
v_{\ell}=v_{\ell-1}
, and the other from the integral along the deformed contour
\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{\ell}
. Collecting and organizing these terms, we obtain (
4.4
).

In the expression for
W(i,j)
in (
4.3
), we could assume that
v_{\ell}\in\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\ell}
for each
\ell
. Applying the identity (
4.4
), we obtain

W(i,j)=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}=j}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i_{\ell}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{\ell}}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{v^{i-1}_{i_{1}}}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(v_{i_{\ell}}-v_{i_{\ell+1}})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\prod_{a=i_{\ell-1}+1}^{i_{\ell}}v_{i_{\ell}}P_{a}(v_{i_{\ell}})\right]}

(4.5)

with
i_{0}=0
.
We also choose the specific contour
\mathrm{C}_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}
for
v
in
S(i,j)
and have

S(i,j)=\oint_{\mathrm{C}_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}v^{i-j-1}p_{i}(v).

(4.6)

We use the above expressions to evaluate

(SW)(i,j)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}S(i,m)\,W(m,j).

Note that the index
m
only appears in
v^{i-m-1}
and
v_{i_{1}}^{m-1}
and the sum over
m
of the product of these two terms becomes

\sum_{m=1}^{n}v^{i-m-1}v_{i_{1}}^{m-1}=\frac{v^{i-1}}{v-v_{i_{1}}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{v_{i_{1}}^{n}}{v^{n}}\right).

(4.7)

Inserting this summation, we obtain

\begin{split}&SW(i,j)\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}=j}\oint_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i_{\ell}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{\ell}}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{v^{i-1}p_{i}(v)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(v_{i_{\ell}}-v_{i_{\ell+1}})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\prod_{a=i_{\ell-1}+1}^{i_{\ell}}v_{i_{\ell}}P_{a}(v_{i_{\ell}})\right]}\frac{1}{v-v_{i_{1}}}\\
&-\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}=j}\oint_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i_{\ell}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{\ell}}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{v^{i-1}p_{i}(v)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(v_{i_{\ell}}-v_{i_{\ell+1}})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\prod_{a=i_{\ell-1}+1}^{i_{\ell}}v_{i_{\ell}}P_{a}(v_{i_{\ell}})\right]}\frac{1}{v-v_{i_{1}}}\frac{v^{n}_{i_{1}}}{v^{n}}.\end{split}

The first term vanishes since the integrand is analytic at
v=0
. Thus,
(SW)(i,j)
equals to

\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}=j}\oint_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i_{\ell}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{\ell}}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{v^{i-1}p_{i}(v)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(v_{i_{\ell}}-v_{i_{\ell+1}})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\prod_{a=i_{\ell-1}+1}^{i_{\ell}}v_{i_{\ell}}P_{a}(v_{i_{\ell}})\right]}\frac{1}{v_{i_{1}}-v}\frac{v^{n}_{i_{1}}}{v^{n}}.

(4.8)

Now we evaluate the
v_{i_{1}}
-integral. Due to the
v_{i_{1}}^{n}
factor, it is analytic at
v_{i_{1}}=0
and hence only the residue at
v_{i_{1}}=v
survives (note that
v_{i_{1}}-v_{i_{2}}
in the denominator does contribute to any pole in this evaluation since
v_{i_{2}}
lies outside
\mathrm{C}_{i_{1}}^{\mathrm{out}}
). We rewrite
v
as
v_{i_{1}}
and deform the contour back to
\mathrm{C}_{i_{1}}^{\mathrm{out}}
. This gives

\begin{split}(SW)(i,j)&=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}=j}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i_{\ell}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{\ell}}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{v_{i_{1}}^{i-1}p_{i}(v_{i_{1}})}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(v_{i_{\ell}}-v_{i_{\ell+1}})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\prod_{a=i_{\ell-1}+1}^{i_{\ell}}v_{i_{\ell}}P_{a}(v_{i_{\ell}})\right]}\\
&=\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\ell}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{\ell}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{v^{i-1}_{1}p_{i}(v_{1})}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]},\end{split}

(4.9)

where, in the second equality, we applied (
4.4
).

It remains to show the right hand side of (
4.9
) equals to
\delta_{i}(j)
. We take residues successively at
v_{1}=v_{2}
,
v_{2}=v_{3}
, and so on. If
i\geq j
, we take all residues starting from
v_{1}=v_{2}
and ending with
v_{j-1}=v_{j}
. Using
\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}P_{\ell}(v)=p_{j}(v)
, we find that

(SW)(i,j)=\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{j}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{v_{j}^{\,i-1}p_{i}(v_{j})}{v_{j}^{j}p_{j}(v_{j})}=\delta_{i}(j),\quad\text{for }i\geq j.

If
i<j
, we take residues starting from
v_{1}=v_{2}
and ending with
v_{i-1}=v_{i}
. Noting that
\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}P_{\ell}(v)=p_{i}(v)
, we obtain

\begin{split}(SW)(i,j)&=\left(\prod_{\ell=i}^{j}\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\ell}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{\ell}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\right)\frac{v_{i}^{\,i-1}p_{i}(v_{i})}{\left[\prod_{\ell=i}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[v_{i}^{i}\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}P_{\ell}(v_{i})\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}\\
&=\left(\prod_{\ell=i}^{j}\int_{\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\ell}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{\ell}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\right)\frac{v_{i}^{-1}}{\left[\prod_{\ell=i}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}.\end{split}

The resulting integrand is of order
O(v_{i}^{-2})
. Therefore, we can deform the outermost contour
\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}
to infinity, which implies that
(SW)(i,j)=0
for all
i<j
. Hence, the lemma follows.

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.
Denote by
M
the matrix inside the determinant on the left-hand side of (
1.5
). Write
M(i,j)=A(i,j)+B(i,j)
, where

A(i,j):=\oint_{0}v^{i-j-1}p_{i}(v)f_{j}(v)\,\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}},\quad B(i,j):=\int_{\Gamma}q_{i}(u)g_{j}(u)\,\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}.

(4.10)

Note that
A
is upper triangular with
1
’s on the diagonal. Thus,

\det(M)=\det(\mathrm{I}+BA^{-1}).

(4.11)

Define an
n\times n
matrix
R
by

R(i,j):=\oint_{0}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\,v^{i-j-1}f_{j}(v),\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

(4.12)

Note that
R
is also upper triangular with
1
’s on the diagonal. Now we define a family of polynomials
\{\alpha_{i}(v)\}_{i=1}^{n}
with degree less than
n
as follows:

\alpha_{i}(v)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}R^{-1}(i,k)v^{k-1},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

(4.13)

where
R^{-1}=(R^{-1}(i,j))_{i,j=1}^{n}
. By definition, it follows that

\oint_{0}\alpha_{i}(v)\frac{f_{j}(v)}{v^{j}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}R^{-1}(i,k)R(k,j)=\delta_{i}(j),\quad\text{for }1\leq i,j\leq n.

(4.14)

Recall the
n\times n
matrix
S
defined in (
4.1
), and let
R>r>0
. For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we have

\begin{split}(SR)(i,j)&=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\oint_{|v_{1}|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}v_{1}^{i-m-1}p_{i}(v_{1})\oint_{|v_{2}|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{2}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}v_{2}^{m-j-1}f_{j}(v_{2})\\
&=\oint_{|v_{1}|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\oint_{|v_{2}|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{2}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}v_{1}^{i-1}p_{i}(v_{1})v_{2}^{-j}f_{j}(v_{2})\frac{1}{v_{1}-v_{2}}\left(1-\frac{v_{2}^{n}}{v_{1}^{n}}\right).\end{split}

Since
\frac{v_{1}^{i-1}p_{i}(v_{1})}{v_{1}-v_{2}}
is analytic at
v_{1}=0
, we find that

\begin{split}(SR)(i,j)&=\oint_{|v_{1}|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\oint_{|v_{2}|=R}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{2}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}v_{1}^{i-1}p_{i}(v_{1})v_{2}^{-j}f_{j}(v_{2})\frac{1}{v_{2}-v_{1}}\frac{v_{2}^{n}}{v_{1}^{n}}\\
&=\oint_{|v_{1}|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}v_{1}^{i-j-1}p_{i}(v_{1})f_{j}(v_{1})\\
&=A(i,j).\end{split}

Now Lemma
4.1
implies that
A^{-1}=R^{-1}W
, where
W
is an
n\times n
matrix defined in (
4.3
). Hence, for
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we have

A^{-1}(i,j)=\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{\alpha_{i}(v_{1})}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]},

(4.15)

where we used the definition of
\alpha_{i}
in (
4.13
). The contours here are small circles around the origin with decreasing radii
|v_{1}|>|v_{2}|>\cdots>|v_{j}|
. Now we combine it with the definition of the matrix
B
, and obtain

\begin{split}(BA^{-1})(i,j)=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{q_{i}(u)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}\sum_{m=1}^{n}g_{m}(u)\alpha_{m}(v_{1}).\end{split}

(4.16)

Recall in the assumption of Proposition
1.2
,
H(v,u)
is analytic at
v=0
. We also recall (
4.13
).
\alpha_{i}(v)=v^{i-1}+
higher order terms since both
R
and
R^{-1}
are upper triangular with
1
’s on the diagonal. Thus,
H(v,u)
has the following series expansion near
v=0

H(v,u)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}c_{m}(u)\alpha_{m}(v)+J(v,u),

(4.17)

where
J(v,u)
as a function of
v
is of at least
O(v^{n})
near the origin. The relation (
1.4
) implies

\oint_{0}H(v,u)\frac{f_{i}(v)}{v^{i}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}=g_{i}(u).

(4.18)

Inserting (
4.17
) in this formula and applying the orthogonal relation (
4.14
), we obtain
c_{m}(u)=g_{m}(u)
and

H(v,u)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}g_{m}(u)\alpha_{m}(v)+J(v,u).

(4.19)

Now, from (
4.16
), (
4.19
), we have

\begin{split}(BA)^{-1}(i,j)&=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{q_{i}(u)H(v_{1},u)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}\\
&\quad-\int_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{q_{i}(u)J(v_{1},u)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}.\end{split}

(4.20)

Now we claim that the second term on the right hand side of (
4.20
) vanishes. In fact, this follows from the analyticity of
v_{1}^{-n}J(v_{1},u)
near
v_{1}=0
. If we evaluate the
v_{1}
integral, the only contribution comes from the residue at
v_{1}=v_{2}
. Then we evaluate the
v_{2}
integral, due to the factor
J(v_{2},u)
, only the residue at
v_{2}=v_{3}
survives. We repeat this procedure and obtain

\int_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{q_{i}(u)J(v_{1},u)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{q_{i}(u)J(v_{j},u)}{v_{j}^{j}p_{j}(v_{j})}=0,

(4.21)

since the integrand is analytic at
v_{j}=0
.

Now we have

\begin{split}(BA)^{-1}(i,j)&=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{q_{i}(u)H(v_{1},u)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}\\
&=\int_{\Gamma}L_{1}(i,u)L_{2}(u,j)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n,\end{split}

where

L_{1}(i,u)=q_{i}(u),

and

L_{2}(u,j)=\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{j}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{1},u)}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}.

Denote by
K=L_{2}L_{1}
, the product operator with the following kernel,

\begin{split}K(u_{1},u_{2})&=\sum_{m=1}^{N}L_{2}(u_{1},m)L_{1}(m,u_{2})\\
&=\sum_{m=1}^{N}q_{m}(u_{2})\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{1}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots\oint\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{m}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{H(v_{1},u_{1})}{\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell+1})\right]\cdot\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}v_{\ell}P_{\ell}(v_{\ell})\right]}.\end{split}

Proposition
1.2
follows from the identity
\det(\mathrm{I}+L_{1}L_{2})=\det(\mathrm{I}+L_{2}L_{1})
and the fact that the Fredholm determinant is invariant under transposition of the kernel.
