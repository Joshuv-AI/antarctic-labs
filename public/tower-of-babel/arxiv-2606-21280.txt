Parabolic induction for modular finite 𝑾 W -algebras Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 17B10, 17B20, 17B45, 17B50.
Simon M. Goodwin, Lewis Topley and Matthew Westaway

Abstract
. We study the modules of minimal dimension for reduced enveloping algebras of Lie algebras of reductive algebraic groups using the theory of modular finite W W -algebras. First of all we consider the case where the p p -character lies in a unique sheet, and demonstrate that in classical cases and in most exceptional cases all minimal modules are parabolically induced from a Levi subalgebra and a rigid p p -character. Secondly we consider the minimal modules which are invariant under twisting by the component group, showing that in classical cases and in most exceptional cases these are also parabolically induced from a Levi subalgebra and a rigid p p -character.



1.
Introduction

Fix an algebraically closed field
{\mathbbm{k}}
of characteristic
p>0
. Let
G
be a connected, simply-connected simple group over
{\mathbbm{k}}
with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{Lie}G
. Suppose that
p
is good for the root system of
G
and that
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a non-degenerate
G
-invariant bilinear form. These assumptions are often referred to as the standard hypotheses
[
Ja1
, §6.3]
.

1.1.
Sheets and minimal modules

The enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}})
is finite over the
p
-centre
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
and the prime spectrum of
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
naturally identifies with
({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{(1)}
, the Frobenius twist of the coadjoint
G
-module. Using a version of Schur’s lemma we see that every irreducible representation of
U({\mathfrak{g}})
factors through one of the central reductions over
({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{(1)}
. The associated element
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is called the
p
-character of the representation, and the central reductions are known as reduced enveloping algebras and denoted
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

It is well-known that the dimensions of irreducible representations are controlled by the
p
-character: Premet’s theorem asserts that whenever
G
satisfies the standard hypotheses of
[
Ja1
, §6.3]
every
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module has dimension divisible by
p^{d_{\chi}}
where
d_{\chi}:=\frac{1}{2}\dim G\cdot\chi
, see
[
Pr1
]
. Meanwhile, work of Premet and the second author
[
PT2
]
shows that modules of dimension
p^{d_{\chi}}
exist for every
p
-character, confirming a long-standing conjecture of Humphreys. We refer to
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules of dimension
p^{d_{\chi}}
as
minimal modules
. By Premet’s theorem, minimal modules are necessarily simple and, as we shall explain, they play a distinguished role amongst all simple
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules.

Premet showed that
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
is Morita equivalent to a restricted finite
W
-algebra
[
Pr2
]
, and the minimal
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules are equivalent to one dimensional representations for the
W
-algebra. This relationship allows many tools to be deployed in their study which are unavailable for arbitrary simple modules. One dimensional representations of complex finite
W
-algebras have been studied for many years, and it is an important general objective to extend these methods and results to positive characteristics. For example, the long-running project to show the existence of such one dimensional modules over
{\mathbb{C}}
(see
[
Lo1
,
Pr5
]
and the references therein) was extended to positive characteristics in
[
PT2
]
. The relationship between one dimensional modules and quantizations of nilpotent orbits (and their affinisations) over
{\mathbb{C}}
is now understood, by the work of Losev and collaborators
[
Lo2
,
Lo4
,
LMM
]
. This has recently been extended to positive characteristics, for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, by Ambrosio and the second and third author
[
ATW
]
.

The current paper makes a significant advancement in this program of research by firstly constructing a parabolic induction functor for finite
W
-algebras, analogous to Losev’s construction over
{\mathbb{C}}
in
[
Lo3
]
, and then utilising this functor to extend most of the main results of
[
PT1
]
to the modular setting. In particular, we study minimal
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules for general
G
, satisfying one of two hypotheses: (1) that the
p
-character lies on a unique sheet or (2) that the module is
G^{\chi}
-stable. We expect this work will play a decisive role in the classification of minimal modules, which is a central open problem in the field. When
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, this classification has been carried out by the first and second authors in
[
GT2
]
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{[m]}
be the union of orbits of dimension
m
. A sheet of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is defined as an irreducible component of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{[m]}
for some
m
. Sheets are classified using the theory of induction of coadjoint orbits and decomposition classes
[
LS
,
PS1
]
, and the reader may refer to our Section
3.4
for a brief survey. We recall that the decomposition classes of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
are the equivalence classes under the relation

\chi\sim\psi\Leftrightarrow g\cdot\chi_{\rm n}=\psi_{\rm n}\text{ and }gG^{\chi_{\rm s}}g^{-1}=G^{\psi_{\rm s}}\text{ for some }g\in G,

where
\chi=\chi_{\rm s}+\chi_{\rm n}
is the Jordan decomposition and
G^{\chi}
denotes the coadjoint stabliser of
\chi
.

Sheets and decomposition classes are locally closed subvarieties of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and every sheet is stratified by classes. We write
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
for the class of
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, and we say that
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
is rigid if it is dense in a sheet. Similarly we say that
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is
rigid
if
\mathcal{D}_{\chi}
is rigid. If
\chi
is nilpotent then this corresponds to the
G
-orbit of
\chi
being rigid i.e. it cannot be induced from a proper Levi subalgebra under Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction.

We are interested in whether minimal modules can be parabolically induced. An affirmative answer in type
{\sf A}
is given in
[
GT2
]
(there, every element lies in a unique sheet); our main result is the following generalisation of that result to other types.

Excluding the orbits in Table

We conclude this subsection by noting that in light of the above comparison with

G

1.2.
Sketch of methodology

Let
e\in{\mathfrak{g}}
nilpotent. To
({\mathfrak{g}},e)
we can attach two algebras: the
finite
W
-algebra

U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
and the
extended finite
W
-algebra

\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. Under Jantzen’s standard hypotheses
[
Ja1
, §6.3]
, both these algebras were defined and studied thoroughly in
[
GT1
]
, although they had both appeared in earlier work of Premet in large characteristics
[
Pr4
]
. We recall the construction in Section
4.2
, generalising the approach of
[
GT1
]
by showing that large families of graded nilpotent Lie subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{g}}
are algebraic (cf. Lemma
4.1
).

Both
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
and
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
admit actions by the reductive part
C^{e}
of the centraliser
G^{e}
, and their categories of modules are equivalent to certain full subcategories of
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules, see
[
GT1
, §9.2]
. We refer to these equivalences collectively as
Skryabin’s equivalence
, after
[
Pr2
, Appendix]
. Our first milestone shows that the corresponding functor is
C^{e}
-equivariant on simple modules, see Section
5.1
. This result is fairly natural; surprisingly, the proof is slightly non-trivial.

The main technical tool of this paper is the construction of a
parabolic induction functor
for modular finite
W
-algebras. Our results on this topic are inspired by Losev
[
Lo3
]
, however, our methods are very different.

Suppose that the orbit of
e\in\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})
is Lusztig–Spaltenstein induced from a nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}
in a Levi subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a parabolic subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{p}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
such that there exists
e_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
with
e-e_{0}\in\mathfrak{n}
. We say that a
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
-module is
semi-restricted
if the
p
-support is contained in
\chi_{0}+\kappa_{0}\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
, where
\kappa_{0}:=\kappa|_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}:{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\to{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{*}
is a
G_{0}
-equivariant isomorphism.
We construct a functor
\operatorname{Ind}_{({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})}^{({\mathfrak{g}},e)}
from semi-restricted
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
-modules to
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules, and show (Theorem
5.8
)
that it enjoys the following properties.

(1)

It is dimension preserving.

(2)

The effect on
p
-characters can be described explicitly.

(3)

Restricting to one dimensional representations gives a finite morphism of representation schemes.

(4)

Any subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq C_{0}^{e_{0}}\cap C^{e}
, where
C_{0}^{e_{0}}
is the reductive part of
G_{0}^{e_{0}}
, acts on the variety of semi-restricted one dimensional representations of
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
in such a way that the finite morphism from (3) is
\Gamma
-equivariant.

Note that the functor does depend on our choice of parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
, although we usually omit this from the notation.

To proceed with the description of the proof, we need a little more notation. If
X\subseteq({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{(1)}
is Zariski closed then there is a corresponding ideal
I_{X}\subseteq Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathbbm{k}}[({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{(1)}]
. Generalising the notation for reduced enveloping algebras, we write
U_{X}({\mathfrak{g}})=U({\mathfrak{g}})/I_{X}U({\mathfrak{g}})
. In Section
4.4
we explain that
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
admits a
p
-centre with a natural inclusion
\operatorname{Spec}\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\subseteq({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{(1)}
. Thus if
X\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
then we can also use the notation
\widehat{U}_{X}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
; we refer the reader to Section
4.6
for more detail. For example, the category of semi-restricted
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
-modules is
\widehat{U}_{\chi_{0}+\kappa_{0}\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})\!\operatorname{-mod}
.

The parabolic induction functor is constructed as a composition of functors, as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\widehat{U}_{\chi_{0}+\kappa_{0}\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})\!\operatorname{-mod}&\overset{\eqref{e:skryabin1}}{\to}&U_{\chi_{0}+\kappa_{0}\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})\!\operatorname{-mod}&\overset{\eqref{e:classicalinductionfunctor}}{\to}&U_{\chi+\kappa\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})}({\mathfrak{g}})\!\operatorname{-mod}\\
&\overset{\eqref{e:skryabin2}}{\to}&\widehat{U}_{\chi+\kappa\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}&\overset{\eqref{e:pullbackfunctor}}{\to}&U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}\end{array}

The first and third functors are induced by Skryabin’s equivalence. Under these functors, with the relevant restriction on
p
-support, the minimal modules of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
and
U({\mathfrak{g}})
correspond to one dimensional modules over
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
and
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
, respectively. The second functor appearing in (
1.2
) is parabolic induction for reduced enveloping algebras, while the fourth is simply pull-back through the natural homomorphism
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\to\widehat{U}_{\chi+\kappa\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. Using these facts, we can deduce Theorem
1.1
(1) by showing for particular
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
that the following map is surjective

(1.4)

\displaystyle\{\mbox{one dimensional semi-restricted }\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})\mbox{-modules}\}\to\{\mbox{one dimensional }U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\mbox{-modules}\}.

Indeed, when
e
lies in a unique sheet we show there exists a Levi subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and a rigid nilpotent
p
-character
\chi_{0}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{*}
(inducing to
\chi
) such that the variety of one dimensional
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules is an affine space whose dimension is precisely the dimension of the variety of one dimensional semi-restricted
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
-modules. This ensures that the finite morphism (
1.4
) is surjective.

Using Property (4) of the parabolic induction functor, listed above, a similar argument proves Theorem
1.1
(2). For this, we must choose (when possible) the Levi subgroup
G_{0}
with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
to have the property that there exists a finite group
\Gamma\subseteq G_{0}\cap C^{e}
whose order is not divisible by
p
and for which
\Gamma
-stability of one dimensional representations coincides with
G^{\chi}
-stability, i.e.
{\mathbbm{k}}\Gamma\!\operatorname{-mod}
is semisimple and
\Gamma\subseteq G^{\chi}
gives rise to a surjection
\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow G^{\chi}/(G^{\chi})^{\circ}
. Such restriction means that we may not always assume
\chi_{0}
is rigid, but we can construct
\chi_{0}
lying on a unique sheet of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
, and thus apply Theorem
1.1
(1). In this case we show that the variety of
\Gamma
-stable one dimensional
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules is an affine space of the desired dimension.

1.3.
Structure of the paper

In Section
2
we fix some general notation and gather some elementary facts from commutative algebra.

In Section
3
we survey several theories which are fundamental to our constructions: associated cocharacters, minimal modules, Lusztig–Spaltenstein induction, decomposition classes and sheets. We also explain how to generalise the main theorem to arbitrary
p
-characters for standard reductive groups.

In Section
4
we recap the theory of modular finite
W
-algebras, mostly following
[
GT1
]
. We recall the notions of
p
-centres and the PBW theorems and recall the Poisson structure on the transverse slice. We recap the properties of reduced finite
W
-algebras, which are obtained by specialising the
p
-character, similar to the reduced enveloping algebra mentioned above. Finally we obtain general results on the abelian quotients of the finite
W
-algebra, using methods inspired by
[
Pr4
]
.

In Section
5
we start by recapping Skryabin’s equivalence and showing that it is
C^{e}
-equivariant on simple modules. We then construct our parabolic induction functor and spend the rest of that section proving Properties (1)–(4) from Section
1.2
(Theorem
5.8
).

In Section
6
we focus on
G=\mathrm{SO}_{N}
or
\mathrm{Sp}_{N}
. The majority of the work involves constructing a special choice of (Lusztig–Spaltenstein) induction datum. Using detailed information on the structure of centralisers in classical Lie algebras from
[
PT1
]
we can bound the dimensions of varieties of one dimensional representations of the finite
W
-algebras (see Theorem
6.5
). We complete the proof of the main theorem for classical cases in Section
6.5
.

Finally, in Section
7
we consider the case when
G
is of exceptional type. The majority of work in this section is in computing a certain value
c_{\pi}(e)
associated to
e
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
; the main theorem in the exceptional cases is then proved in Sections
7.3
and
7.4
, using similar arguments to the classical case.

2.
Preliminaries

We fix
{\mathbbm{k}}
once and for all to be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. As the paper progresses we will place further restrictions on
p
.

All vector spaces, algebras, varieties and Lie algebras will be defined over
{\mathbbm{k}}
. For a finitely generated commutative
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra
A
we write
\operatorname{Spec}A
for the affine scheme of prime ideals. If we refer to a scheme as a variety then we are implicitly only interested in the topological subspace of closed points.

If
A
is a
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra we write
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
for the category of finitely generated
A
-modules.

2.1.
Twists

When a group acts by automorphisms on a group or an algebra, the modules of the latter can be twisted by the action of the former. In this paper twisting will manifest itself in two rather different ways: the Frobenius twist of an affine scheme and the twist of a representation by automorphisms. We summarise these notions of twist now, fixing the relevant notation.

The Frobenius map on
{\mathbbm{k}}
is the bijective ring homomorphism
{\mathbbm{k}}\to{\mathbbm{k}}
given by
a\mapsto a^{p}
. The Frobenius twist of an affine
{\mathbbm{k}}
-scheme
X
is the
{\mathbbm{k}}
-scheme
X^{(1)}
which is equal to
X
as a scheme but with the
{\mathbbm{k}}
-linear structure on
{\mathbbm{k}}[X]
twisted by the inverse of the Frobenius on
{\mathbbm{k}}
. The resulting
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra is denoted
{\mathbbm{k}}[X]^{(1)}
; we may similarly apply this process to any
{\mathbbm{k}}
-vector space
V
, and we denote the resulting
{\mathbbm{k}}
-vector space by
V^{(1)}
. If
X
is a reduced scheme then
X^{(1)}
may be naturally identified with
\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbbm{k}}[X]^{p}
.

Since we may view any
{\mathbbm{k}}
-vector space as a
{\mathbbm{k}}
-scheme, this might first appear to lead to some ambiguity. Fortunately, these constructions are compatible in the obvious sense.

If
A
is an algebra,
V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
and
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)
then we may define the
g
-twisted
A
-module
{}^{g}V
which is equal to
V
as a vector space, with twisted action
a\cdot_{g}v:=(g^{-1}\cdot a)\cdot v
for
a\in A
,
v\in V
. In this way,
\operatorname{Aut}(A)
may be viewed as a group of autoequivalences of
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
. If
G\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(A)
then we say that
V
is
G
-stable if it is fixed by these autoequivalences, in other words, if
{}^{g}V\cong V
for every
g\in G
.

We record here a general fact to be used a few times: if
B\subseteq A
are associative
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebras,
V\in B\!\operatorname{-mod}
, and
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)
then there is a natural isomorphism
{}^{g}(A\otimes_{B}V)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}A\otimes_{g(B)}{}^{g}V
given by
u\otimes v\mapsto(g\cdot u)\otimes v
for
u\in A
,
v\in V
. In particular, if
g
stabilises
B
then
{}^{g}(A\otimes_{B}V)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}A\otimes_{B}{}^{g}V
.

2.2.
Abelian quotients

Let
A
be a finitely generated
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra and
\Gamma
a group of automorphisms of
A
. We write
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
for the maximal abelian quotient of
A
, obtained by factoring
A
by the ideal generated by
[a,b]
with
a,b\in A
. The action of
\Gamma
descends to
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
and we write
A^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
for the coinvariant algebra, obtained by factoring
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
by the ideal generated by
a-\gamma\cdot a
with
a\in A
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Now let
Z\subseteq A
be a central subalgebra and assume that
A
is finitely-generated as a
Z
-module; this implies that
Z
is a finitely-generated noetherian
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra and that
A
is noetherian (see
[
BGl
, §III.1]
). If
I\subseteq Z
is any ideal then we can consider the algebra
A^{\operatorname{ab}}\otimes_{Z}Z/I\cong(A\otimes_{Z}Z/I)^{\operatorname{ab}}
. With
Z\subseteq A
as above and
\chi\in\operatorname{Spec}Z
, we write
A_{\chi}
for the corresponding central reduction; in this notation, the previous isomorphism becomes
(A^{\operatorname{ab}})_{\chi}\cong(A_{\chi})^{\operatorname{ab}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following hold:

(i)

The closed points of
\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\operatorname{ab}})
parametrise the one dimensional representations of
A
.

(ii)

The closed points of
\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma})
parametrise those one dimensional representations of
A
which are
\Gamma
-stable.

(iii)

If
K=\operatorname{Ker}(Z\to A^{\operatorname{ab}})
then

\operatorname{Spec}(Z/K)=\{\chi\in\operatorname{Max}Z\mid A_{\chi}\text{ admits a one dimensional }A\text{-module}\}.

(iv)

If
K_{\Gamma}=\operatorname{Ker}(Z\to A^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma})
then

\operatorname{Spec}(Z/K_{\Gamma})=\{\chi\in\operatorname{Max}Z\mid A_{\chi}\text{ admits a }\Gamma\text{-stable one dimensional }A\text{-module}\}.

Proof.

Both (i) and (ii) follow from the Nullstellensatz, so we turn to (iii).

Let
I_{\chi}\in\operatorname{Spec}(Z)
be a maximal ideal such that
A_{\chi}
admits a one dimensional representation, so
(A_{\chi})^{\operatorname{ab}}\neq 0
. The composition
\phi:Z\to A\to A_{\chi}\to(A_{\chi})^{\operatorname{ab}}
is therefore non-zero as it preserves the identity, and
I_{\chi}\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
by construction. Since
I_{\chi}
is maximal, we thus have
I_{\chi}=\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
. Furthermore,
\phi
coincides with the composition
Z\to A\to A^{\operatorname{ab}}\to(A^{\operatorname{ab}})_{\chi}\cong(A_{\chi})^{\operatorname{ab}}
and thus
K\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)=I_{\chi}
; hence
I_{\chi}\in\operatorname{Spec}(Z/K)
.

Conversely, suppose
I_{\chi}
is a maximal ideal of
Z
containing
K
; then
I_{\chi}/K
is a maximal ideal of
Z/K
, which we may view as a subalgebra of
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
. It suffices to show that
(I_{\chi}/K)A^{\operatorname{ab}}
is a proper ideal of
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
, since then
(A_{\chi})^{\operatorname{ab}}\cong A^{\operatorname{ab}}/(I_{\chi}/K)A^{\operatorname{ab}}\neq 0
. Since
A
is a finitely-generated
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra which is finitely-generated as a
Z
-module, it is immediate that
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
is a finitely-generated
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra which is finitely-generated as a
Z/K
-module. We may thus apply the lying-over theorem
[
BGl
, Proposition III.1.1(2)]
to conclude that
(I_{\chi}/K)A^{\operatorname{ab}}
is contained in a prime ideal
P
of
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
such that
P\cap Z/K=I_{\chi}/K
. Since
I_{\chi}/K\neq Z/K
we must have
P\neq A^{\operatorname{ab}}
and thus
(I_{\chi}/K)A^{\operatorname{ab}}
must be a proper ideal of
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
as required.

This proves (iii); the argument for (iv) is essentially the same.
∎

2.3.
A condition for finiteness

We record a general result about finite extensions of commutative
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebras, which we use in Section
5.4
to prove the finiteness of a certain morphism of schemes. The following is a slight generalisation of
[
Kr
, p. 144]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
A={\mathbbm{k}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
is positively graded with each
x_{i}
homogeneous. If
f_{1},...,f_{m}\in A
are homogeneous and
\sqrt{(f_{1},...,f_{m})}=A_{>0}=\bigoplus_{i>0}A_{i}
then
A
is finite over
{\mathbbm{k}}[f_{1},...,f_{m}]
.

Proof.

Write
I=(f_{1},...,f_{m})
,
d=\max_{i}\deg x_{i}
and
d_{i}=\deg f_{i}
. For
1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq n
the monomial
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}
has degree
\leq kd
and it follows that for
r\geq kd
we have
A_{r}\subseteq A_{>0}^{k}
. The assumption
\sqrt{I}=A_{>0}
implies that
A_{>0}^{N}\subseteq I
for some
N>0
. Combining these facts,
A_{r}\subseteq I
for all
r\geq Nd
. Since the
f_{i}
are homogeneous it follows that
A_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{r-d_{i}}f_{i}
for all such
r
and, by induction, we see that
A
is generated by
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{Nd-1}A_{i}
as a
{\mathbbm{k}}[f_{1},...,f_{m}]
-module.
∎

3.
Reductive groups and Lie algebras

The following notation and hypotheses will be used throughout the current section:

•

G
is a reductive algebraic
{\mathbbm{k}}
-group, satisfying the standard hypotheses
[
Ja1
, §6.3]
; we call
G
a
standard reductive group
.

•

{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
and
U({\mathfrak{g}})
is the universal enveloping algebra.

•

A choice of non-degenerate
G
-invariant bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is denoted
\kappa:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathbbm{k}}
. We often abuse notation and also write
\kappa:{\mathfrak{g}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
for the corresponding
G
-module isomorphism
x\mapsto\kappa(x,-)
.

•

The natural
G
-equivariant restricted structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is denoted
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
.

•

\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})
is the set of nilpotent elements of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, i.e. those satisfying
x^{[p]^{r}}=0
for
r\gg 0
.

•

If
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
or
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
then the (co)adjoint stabiliser in
G
is denoted
G^{x}
, the (co)adjoint stabiliser in
{\mathfrak{g}}
is denoted
{\mathfrak{g}}^{x}
, and the (co)adjoint orbit in
{\mathfrak{g}}
or
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is denoted
G\cdot x
.

The standard hypotheses imply several useful properties for Lie algebras, which we record here for future reference.

Remark 3.1
.

(1)

If
G
is standard reductive then the (co)adjoint stabilisers are smooth: for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
(resp.
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
) we have
{\mathfrak{g}}^{x}=\operatorname{Lie}(G^{x})

[
Ja3
, §2.9]
.

(2)

By
[
PS1
, §2.1]
, if
G
is a standard reductive group then
{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{Lie}G
is isomorphic to

\mathfrak{z}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}
where
\mathfrak{z}
is a subalgebra of the centre of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and each
{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
is a Lie algebra of one of the following types:

•

\mathfrak{gl}_{k}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
p\mid k
; or

•

the Lie algebra of a simply-connected, simple algebraic group for which
p
is very good.

The stabilisers in
G
of semisimple elements of
{\mathfrak{g}}
are Levi subgroups of
G
. All Levi subgroups of
G
are standard reductive.

(3)

If
G
is simple and standard reductive then every isogeny from
G
is separable, i.e. every finite homomorphism of algebraic groups from
G
induces an isomorphism of Lie algebras (this follows from
[
Le
, Proposition 2.4.4]
since
\mathrm{SL}_{kp}
is not standard reductive).

3.1.
The associated cocharacter and the component group

The following facts are taken from
[
Ja3
, §5]
. Given
e\in\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})
there exists a cocharacter
\gamma_{e}:{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}\to G
such that:

(i)

\gamma_{e}(t)\cdot e=t^{2}e
for all
t\in{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}
;

(ii)

e
is distinguished in a Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}=\operatorname{Lie}(L)
such that
\gamma_{e}:{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}\to[L,L]
.

Any two cocharacters satisfying (i) and (ii) are
(G^{e})^{\circ}
-conjugate. We refer to
\gamma_{e}
as
an associated cocharacter for
e
.

Note that
\gamma_{e}
gives rise to a grading
{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathfrak{g}}(i)
where

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}(i):=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\mid\gamma_{e}(t)\cdot x=t^{i}x\text{ for }t\in{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}\}

which we call the
associated grading
. One of the key properties of the associated grading is:

(iii)

{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}\subseteq\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}{\mathfrak{g}}(i)
.

When a grading satisfies property (iii) and
e\in{\mathfrak{g}}(2)
we say that it is a
good grading for
e
. We direct the reader to
[
GT1
, §3]
and the references therein for the background theory of good gradings.

The centraliser of
\gamma_{e}({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})
in
G^{e}
is denoted
C^{e}
, and is referred to as
the reductive part of the centraliser
. Thanks to
[
Ja3
, §5.10]
it is a reductive group, and
G^{e}
is the semi-direct product of
C^{e}
and its unipotent radical, which is connected. As a consequence

(3.2)

\displaystyle G^{e}/(G^{e})^{\circ}\cong C^{e}/(C^{e})^{\circ}.

3.2.
The
p
-centre, coadjoint orbits and the dimensions of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules

The
p
-centre of the universal enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}})
is the central subalgebra
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
generated by the elements
x^{p}-x^{[p]}
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. The
{\mathbbm{k}}
-linear map
{\mathfrak{g}}^{(1)}\to Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
defined by
x\mapsto x^{p}-x^{[p]}
extends to a
G
-equivariant algebra isomorphism
{\mathbbm{k}}[({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{(1)}]\xrightarrow{\sim}Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

Since we may identify
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
with
({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{(1)}
, each
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
corresponds to a unique maximal ideal
I_{\chi}
of
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
. The corresponding quotient
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})=U({\mathfrak{g}})/I_{\chi}U({\mathfrak{g}})
is known as a reduced enveloping algebra. More generally, if
X\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is any closed subvariety with defining ideal
I_{X}\subseteq Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
, then we denote the corresponding quotient by
U_{X}({\mathfrak{g}})=U({\mathfrak{g}})/I_{X}U({\mathfrak{g}})
.

Note that if
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
is a vector subspace then

U_{\chi+\mathfrak{m}^{\perp}}({\mathfrak{g}})=U({\mathfrak{g}})/U({\mathfrak{g}})\{x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)^{p}\mid x\in\mathfrak{m}\},

where
\mathfrak{m}^{\perp}=\{\eta\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}:\eta|_{\mathfrak{m}}=\chi|_{\mathfrak{m}}\}
.

The following is a direct application of Premet’s theorem
[
Pr1
]
that all
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules have dimension divisible by
p^{d_{\chi}}
(where
d_{\chi}:=\frac{1}{2}\dim G\cdot\chi
) and Premet-Topley’s theorem
[
PT2
]
that
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
actually admits a module of dimension
p^{d_{\chi}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
G
is a standard reductive group over
{\mathbbm{k}}
of characteristic
p>0
, then the following are equivalent for each
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
:

(1)

U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})\text{ admits a module of dimension }p^{k}
;

(2)

\dim(G\cdot\chi)\leq 2k
.

3.3.
Lusztig–Spaltenstein induction

In this section we assume that the reader is familiar with the basic theory of parabolic and Levi subgroups of algebraic groups over fields of positive characteristic, and their Lie algebras (see
[
Bo
, §14]
, for example). Recall that any parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
has a (non-unique) Levi decomposition
P=G_{0}R
into a reductive group
G_{0}
and a unipotent group
R
which is the unipotent radical of
P
. We call
G_{0}
a Levi subgroup, and note that Levi subgroups of standard reductive groups are also standard reductive. This induces a corresponding decomposition
\mathfrak{p}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{r}
. For any semisimple element
x_{\rm s}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
, the centraliser
G^{x_{\rm s}}
is a Levi subgroup of
G
and its Lie algebra is the centraliser
{\mathfrak{g}}^{x_{\rm s}}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Given a Levi subgroup
G_{0}
with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
we may consider the adjoint
G_{0}
-action on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and thus the nilpotent orbits
\mathcal{O}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
. For
g\in G
,
g\cdot{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}:=\operatorname{Ad}(g)({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
is a Levi subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\operatorname{Ad}(g)(\mathcal{O}_{0})
(written as
g\cdot\mathcal{O}_{0}
) is a nilpotent
gG_{0}g^{-1}
-orbit in
g\cdot{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
. Thus
G
acts on the set of pairs
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})
where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
is a Levi subalgebra and
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is a nilpotent orbit in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
.

We now discuss Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction, first introduced in
[
LS
]
for conjugacy classes in algebraic groups, and studied in the setting of the present paper in
[
PS1
]
. Suppose that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Levi subalgebra and
\mathcal{O}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
is a nilpotent orbit. Choose a parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
admitting
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
as a Levi factor and write
\mathfrak{r}=\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{p})
so that
\mathfrak{p}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{r}
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r}
is an irreducible subvariety of
\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})
there is a unique nilpotent
G
-orbit intersecting
\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r}
densely. More remarkably, this nilpotent orbit only depends upon the
G
-orbit of
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})
, not upon the choice of parabolic
\mathfrak{p}
admitting Levi factor
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
. This nilpotent orbit is denoted
\operatorname{Ind}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
and the operation
\operatorname{Ind}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}^{{\mathfrak{g}}}:\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})/G_{0}\to\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})/G
is known as
Lusztig–Spaltenstein induction
.

We denote by
{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})
the set of pairs
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})
where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
is a proper Levi subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is a nilpotent orbit in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
, and we denote by
{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})/G
the set of
G
-orbits of such pairs. We consider Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction to be a map
\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})/G
which factors through the obvious map
\operatorname{Ind}_{G}^{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})/G\to\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})/G
.

When
\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})/G
is in the image of
\operatorname{Ind}_{G}^{\mathfrak{g}}
(or equivalently
\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}
), we say that
\mathcal{O}
is
properly induced
from each
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\in(\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathfrak{g}})^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
and we refer to such
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})
as an
induction datum for
\mathcal{O}
. We denote
{\mathrm{LD}}(\mathcal{O})=(\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathfrak{g}})^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
, the set of induction data for
\mathcal{O}
, and also write
{\mathrm{LD}}(\mathcal{O})/G=(\operatorname{Ind}_{G}^{\mathfrak{g}})^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
, the set of
G
-orbits of induction data for
\mathcal{O}
.

If an orbit is not properly induced then it is
rigid
. If
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is rigid in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
we call
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})
a
rigid induction datum for
\mathcal{O}
. We write
{\mathrm{RLD}}(\mathcal{O}):=\{({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\in{\mathrm{LD}}(\mathcal{O})\mid\mathcal{O}_{0}\mbox{ rigid in }{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\}
and we set

{\mathrm{RLD}}({\mathfrak{g}}):=\bigcup_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})/G}{\mathrm{RLD}}(\mathcal{O}).

If we write
G_{\mathbb{C}}
for the reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{C}}
with the same root datum as
G
and write
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}=\operatorname{Lie}(G_{\mathbb{C}})
, then in good characteristic there is a canonical bijection
\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})/G\simeq\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})/G_{\mathbb{C}}
, coming from the fact that both are indexed by the Bala-Carter labelling
[
Ja3
, §4]
. Since Levi subgroups of standard reductive groups remain standard reductive, the well-known classification of Levi subgroups also gives a canonical bijection
{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})/G\simeq{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})/G_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Induction of orbits enjoys a number of nice properties, including preservation of dimension of stabilisers
[
PS1
, Theorem 2.8]
. In particular, given
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\in{\mathrm{LD}}(\mathcal{O})
there is equality

(3.9)

\displaystyle\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{e_{0}}=\dim{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}

for all
e_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
and
e\in\mathcal{O}
.

For what follows, fix
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\in{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
is a Levi factor of
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{Lie}(P)
. Denote
\mathfrak{r}=\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{p})
, so that
\mathfrak{p}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{r}
, and let
\mathfrak{r}_{-}
be the opposite nilradical so that
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{r}_{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{r}
. Let
\mathcal{O}=\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})
, and fix
e\in\mathcal{O}\cap(\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r})
.

We record here a useful lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Maintain the notation from before the statement of the lemma. Then:

(i)

(G^{e})^{\circ}\subseteq P
; and

(ii)

if
g\in G
and
g\cdot e\in\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r}
then there exists
z\in G^{e}
such that
zg\in P
.
\hfill\qed

Proof.

Part (i) is
[
Ja3
, Proposition 13.17(c)]
, and part (ii) follows easily from the fact that
g\cdot e\in\mathcal{O}\cap(\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r})=P\cdot e
.
∎

Corollary 3.4

(cf. Lemma 6.1.3 of
[
Lo3
]
)
.

Maintain the notation from Lemma
3.3
(ii). Let
\gamma_{e}:{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}\to G
be an associated cocharacter for
e
. Then, after replacing
G_{0}
with a
P
-conjugate if necessary,
\gamma_{e}({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})\subseteq G_{0}
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\gamma_{e}({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})\subseteq P
. For
t\in{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}
we can choose
z_{t}\in G^{e}
such that
z_{t}\gamma_{e}(t)\in P
, by Lemma
3.3
. By part (i) of that lemma
(G^{e})^{\circ}\subseteq P
and so
\{\gamma_{e}(t)P\mid t\in{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}\}
is a finite, connected subset of the partial flag variety
G/P
. Thus
\gamma_{e}(t)P=P
for all
t
, and
\gamma_{e}({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})\subseteq P
by
[
Bo
, Theorem 11.16]
.

Now, since
\gamma_{e}({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})
is a torus it is linearly reductive and thus by
[
Ja3
, Lemma 11.24]
lies inside a Levi subgroup of
P
. By replacing
G_{0}
with a
P
-conjugate if necessary, we may assume
\gamma_{e}({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})\subseteq G_{0}
. ∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that the property
e\in\mathcal{O}\cap(\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r})
is preserved when we replace
G_{0}
with a
P
-conjugate, since
\mathcal{O}\cap(\mathcal{O}_{0}+\mathfrak{r})=P\cdot e
.

We observe finally that
\gamma_{e}({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})\subseteq G_{0}
implies that
\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(0)
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathfrak{g}}(i)
is the grading associated to
\gamma_{e}
. Therefore, we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0}),\bigoplus_{i\leq-1}{\mathfrak{g}}(i))=0.

3.4.
Decomposition classes and sheets

The sheets of
{\mathfrak{g}}
are the irreducible components of the varieties
{\mathfrak{g}}_{[m]}:=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\mid\dim(G\cdot x)=m\}
. The standard hypotheses
[
Ja1
, §6.3]
which we have adopted ensure that
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
are isomorphic as
G
-modules, and so our remarks here apply equally well to the sheets of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. Under the standard hypotheses, sheets are classified using the theory of decomposition classes and induced orbits. We recall here the basics of this theory, largely following
[
PS1
, §2]
.

Given
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\in{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
e_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
, we define the decomposition class

\mathcal{D}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0}):=G\cdot(e_{0}+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})_{\operatorname{reg}}),

where
\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})_{\operatorname{reg}}=\{x\in\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})\mid{\mathfrak{g}}^{x}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\}
. Write
{\mathrm{Dec}}({\mathfrak{g}})
for the set of decomposition classes of
{\mathfrak{g}}

rather than our choice of

.

There is a natural surjective map

\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})/G,\qquad x\mapsto({\mathfrak{g}}^{x_{\rm s}},G^{x_{\rm s}}\cdot x_{\rm n}).

The decomposition class of

In particular,
\mathcal{D}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
is the equivalence class containing
\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})_{\operatorname{reg}}+e_{0}

There are finitely many

contains a unique dense decomposition class

\mathcal{S}=\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\operatorname{reg}}:=\{x\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}\mid\dim(G\cdot x)=\dim(G\cdot y)\mbox{ for all }y\in\mathcal{D}\}.

Part (iii) of the next result explains the classification of sheets.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\in{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})

(i)

\mathcal{N}({\mathfrak{g}})\cap\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\operatorname{reg}}

\operatorname{Ind}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})

(ii)

\dim\mathcal{D}=\dim\operatorname{Ind}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{O}_{0})+\dim\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})

The sheets of
{\mathfrak{g}}

runs over the decomposition classes associated to

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\operatorname{reg}}
if and only if there exists

Parts (i), (ii), and (iii) are Proposition 2.5 and Theorem 2.8 of

Proof.

3.5.
Extending the main theorem to arbitrary
p
-characters for standard reductive groups

In this section, we explain that Theorem
1.1
generalises to standard reductive groups and arbitrary
p
-characters, with appropriate modifications. Before doing so, we articulate what we mean by parabolic induction in this paper. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a parabolic subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{p}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{r}
, and let
\chi_{0}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{*}
. For
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
with
\chi(\mathfrak{r})=0
and
\chi|_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}=\chi_{0}
, we define the parabolic induction functor associated to
\mathfrak{p}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}U_{\chi_{0}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})\!\operatorname{-mod}&\longrightarrow&U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})\!\operatorname{-mod};\\
V&\longmapsto&U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p})}V,\end{array}

where we view
V
as a
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p})
-module via the surjection
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p})\twoheadrightarrow U_{\chi_{0}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
. For
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, we say that a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module is
parabolically induced
if it lies in the essential image of a parabolic induction functor for some proper parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
and some Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{p}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{r}
with
\chi(\mathfrak{r})=0
.

We recall that we call an element
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
rigid if the decomposition class of
\chi
is dense in a sheet. Equivalently
\chi=\chi_{\rm s}+\chi_{\rm n}
and
\chi_{\rm n}|_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}}}
is rigid nilpotent.

The following result explains how to generalise Theorem
1.1
to standard reductive groups and arbitrary
p
-characters.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that Theorem
1.1
holds. Let
G
be a standard reductive group and
\chi=\chi_{\rm s}+\chi_{\rm n}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
be the Jordan decomposition. Let
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}}=\mathfrak{z}\oplus\bigoplus_{i}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}})_{i}
be the decomposition described in Remark
3.1
and suppose that for all
i
the projection of
G^{\chi_{\rm s}}\cdot\chi_{\rm n}
to
({\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}})_{i}
does not appear in Tables
1
or
2
. Then:

(1)

If
\chi
lies in a unique sheet then every minimal
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module is parabolically induced from a minimal module for a reduced enveloping algebra with
p
-character.

(2)

Every
G^{\chi}
-stable minimal
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module is parabolically induced from a minimal module for a reduced enveloping algebra with rigid
p
-character.

Proof.

The proof is in five steps. Step 1 reduces to the case where
G
is simple or
G=\mathrm{GL}_{pk}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Steps 2 through 4 then reduce to the case of a nilpotent
p
-character when
G
is a simple group. Step 5 concludes the argument for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{kp}
.

Step 1: Reduction to the case
G
simple or
G=\mathrm{GL}_{pk}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
G
be standard reductive. By Remark
3.1
there is a group
G^{\prime}=Z\times G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{r}
such that
\operatorname{Lie}(G^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{Lie}(G)
, where
Z
is a central torus and the factors
G_{i}
are either isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{pk}
for some
k
, or are simply-connected, simple algebraic groups. Since the actions of both
G
and
G^{\prime}
factor through
\operatorname{Ad}(G)
it follows that a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module is
G^{\chi}
-stable if and only if it is
(G^{\prime})^{\chi}
-stable.

Write
\chi=\chi_{\mathfrak{z}}+\chi_{1}+\cdots+\chi_{r}
for the decomposition of
\chi
along the decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{z}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
described in Remark
3.1
. It is not hard to see the following:

(1)

Every minimal
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module is parabolically induced from a rigid
p
-character if and only if every minimal
U_{\chi_{i}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{i})
-module is parabolically induced from a rigid
p
-character for
i=1,...,r
.

(2)

\chi
lies in a unique sheet of
{\mathfrak{g}}
if and only if
\chi_{i}
lies in a unique sheet of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}^{*}
for
i=1,...,r

3.7
.

(3)

Decomposing a minimal module
M_{0}\in U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})\!\operatorname{-mod}
as
M_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{r}
, where
M_{i}
is a minimal
U_{\chi_{i}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{i})
-module, we have that
M_{0}
is
G_{0}^{\chi}
-stable if and only if
M_{i}
is
G_{i}^{\chi_{i}}
-stable for
i=1,...,r
.

This completes Step 1.

Step 2: If the current theorem holds for nilpotent
p
-characters in simple groups then it holds for nilpotent
p
-characters for Levi subgroups of simple groups.

The proof of this step is almost identical to Step 1 and so we omit the details.

Step 3: Reduction of Part (1) of the theorem to the nilpotent case.

Now let
G
be a simple group, let
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
be arbitrary, and suppose that conclusion (1) of the current theorem holds whenever
\chi
is nilpotent. We will show that the same conclusion holds for arbitrary
\chi
. Thanks to Remark
3.1
(3) we can suppose that
G
is simply-connected. Write
\chi=\chi_{\rm s}+\chi_{\rm n}
for the Jordan decomposition, and note that
G_{0}:=G^{\chi_{\rm s}}
is a Levi subgroup of
G
.

Let
M
be a minimal
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module and
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
a parabolic subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{p}_{0}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
such that
\chi(\mathfrak{u})=0
. Since parabolic induction

is an equivalence of categories by
[

, there exists a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
-module
M_{0}
such that
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p}_{0})}M_{0}\cong M
; by comparing dimensions, we see that
M_{0}
is a minimal
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
-module. Let
E
be a one dimensional
U_{-\chi_{\rm s}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
-module, which exists since
\chi_{\rm s}([{\mathfrak{g}}_{0},{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}])=0

is a minimal
U_{\chi_{\rm n}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
-module and

If
\chi
lies on a unique sheet of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
then
\chi_{\rm n}

{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{*}

3.7

of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and a Levi factor

The first of these claims follows from the last observation of Section

, setting

B=U(\mathfrak{p})
and
V=M_{0}
, along with the fact that
G^{\chi}=(G^{\chi_{\rm s}})^{\chi}
. The second claim follows as
G^{\chi_{\rm s}}
is a connected reductive group which acts regularly on the following finite set (see
[
St
, Theorem 3.14]
a proof of connectedness):

\Lambda_{\chi_{\rm s}}=\{\lambda:{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}}/[{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}}]\to{\mathbbm{k}}\mid\lambda(x)^{p}-\lambda(x^{[p]})=(-\chi_{\rm s}(x))^{p}\mbox{ for all }x\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}}/[{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi_{\rm s}}]\};

it thus acts trivially on
\Lambda_{\chi_{\rm s}}
and hence twisting by
(G^{\chi_{\rm s}})^{\chi_{\rm n}}
fixes the isoclass of
E
.

Now we repeat the arguments which led to the isomorphism (
3.15
) to conclude that Theorem
1.1
(2) can be reduced to the nilpotent case. This concludes the proof.

Step 5: The case
G=\mathrm{GL}_{kp}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

The arguments in Steps 2, 3 and 4 apply equally in this case, and so we may reduce to
\chi
nilpotent. The result then follows from
[
GT2
, Corollary 1.2]
.∎

Remark 3.10
.

When proving Theorem
1.1
it is sufficient to pick a representative for each orbit. If
\chi^{\prime}=g\cdot\chi
for some
g\in G
then it is easy to show that twisting by
g
induces a dimension preserving equivalence between
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})\!\operatorname{-mod}
and
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})\!\operatorname{-mod}
, which preserves the property of being parabolically induced, as well as sending
G^{\chi}
-stable modules to
G^{\chi^{\prime}}
-stable modules.

4.1.
Graded nilpotent Lie algebras and their algebraic groups

Before we begin to discuss finite
W
-algebras we demonstrate that a certain family of nilpotent Lie subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{g}}
are algebraic, which allows us to simplify the definition of the modular finite
W
-algebra given in
[
GT1
]
.

Let
\gamma:{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}\to G
be a cocharacter, and let
{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathfrak{g}}(i)
be the grading defined in (
3.1
). Fix
d>1
and let
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(1-d)
be a vector subspace. Write
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{k}}=\mathfrak{k}+\bigoplus_{i\leq-d}{\mathfrak{g}}(i).

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists an algebraic group
N_{\mathfrak{k}}\subseteq G
such that
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{k}}=\operatorname{Lie}N_{\mathfrak{k}}
.

Proof.

Let
T\subseteq G
be a maximal torus containing
\lambda({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})
, let
\Phi
be the associated root system, and let
\{e_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
the corresponding root vectors. Set
\Phi_{i}=\{\alpha\in\Phi\mid e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{g}}(i)\}
so that
\Phi=\bigsqcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Phi_{i}
. Now for all
\alpha\in\bigsqcup_{i\leq-1}\Phi_{i}
there is a one parameter subgroup
u_{\alpha}:{\mathbbm{k}}\to G
such that
d_{1}u_{\alpha}=e_{\alpha}
.

Suppose
\Phi_{1-d}=\{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}\}
and abbreviate
e_{j}:=e_{\alpha_{j}}
for
j=1,...,m
. Pick a basis
\{x_{1},...,x_{k}\}
for
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(1-d)
and write
x_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i,j}e_{j}
. Now we define curves
v_{i}:{\mathbbm{k}}\to G
for
i=1,...,k
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle v_{i}(t)=u_{1}(\lambda_{i,1}t)u_{2}(\lambda_{i,2}t)\cdots u_{m}(\lambda_{i,m}t).

We let
N_{0}
denote the subgroup of
G
generated by the root subgroups
u_{\alpha}({\mathbbm{k}})
with
\alpha\in\Phi_{\leq-d}
.
We claim that the smooth algebraic group
N_{\mathfrak{k}}:=\langle N_{0},v_{i}({\mathbbm{k}})\mid i=1,...,k\rangle
(see
[
Mi
, Proposition 2.51]
) is the desired subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{k}}
.

The following three facts can be verified using
[
Bo
, §3.2]
and the formulas in
[
GT1
, (2.2)]
, along with the chain rule.

(1)

d_{1}v_{i}=x_{i}
for
i=1,...,k
;

(2)

each
\{u_{\alpha}({\mathbbm{k}})\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{1-d}\}
normalises
N_{0}
;

(3)

[v_{i}(s),v_{j}(t)]\in N_{0}
for
1\leq i,j\leq k
and
s,t\in{\mathbbm{k}}
.

As a consequence of (1) we have
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{k}}\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}(N_{\mathfrak{k}})
whilst (2) and (3) together imply that
\dim\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{k}}=\dim N_{\mathfrak{k}}=\dim\operatorname{Lie}(N_{\mathfrak{k}})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Modular finite
W
-algebras

Restricted versions of modular finite
W
-algebra were introduced by Premet in
[
Pr2
]
. In his subsequent works
[
Pr4
,
Pr6
]
he made use of the unrestricted versions along with their central reductions, over fields of large positive characteristics. Finally, the theory was mapped out under the standard hypotheses by two of the current authors
[
GT1
]
. Using Lemma
4.1
we are able to streamline the definition slightly, as we now explain.

The assignment
\Psi:(x,y)\mapsto\chi([x,y])
defines a symplectic form on
{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
. We pick any isotropic subspace
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
such that
\chi(\mathfrak{l}^{[p]})=0
, and denote the annihilator of
\mathfrak{l}
with respect to this form by
\mathfrak{l}^{\perp}
. Note that a Lagrangian choice of
\mathfrak{l}
is always possible, as noted in
[
Pr2
, §2.6]
.

We define

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\bigoplus_{i<-1}{\mathfrak{g}}(i)

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}=\mathfrak{l}^{\perp}\oplus\bigoplus_{i<-1}{\mathfrak{g}}(i).

As noted in
[
GT1
, (4.2)]
the dimensions of these Lie algebras satisfy

(4.3)

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}+\dim\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}=\dim(G\cdot e)=2d_{\chi}.

Thanks to Lemma
4.1
, there is a unipotent algebraic group
N_{\mathfrak{l}}\subseteq G
such that
\operatorname{Lie}(N_{\mathfrak{l}})=\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}
. Write
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l},\chi}=\{x-\chi(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}\}\subseteq U({\mathfrak{g}})
and consider the
generalised Gelfand–Graev module

Q_{\mathfrak{l}}=U({\mathfrak{g}})/U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l},\chi}
. The adjoint actions of both
N_{\mathfrak{l}}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}
on
U({\mathfrak{g}})
preserve
U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l},\chi}
(see
[
GT1
, Lemma 4.1]
) and so descend to
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}
. A short calculation shows that
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
inherits an algebra structure from
U({\mathfrak{g}})
with
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}
embedded as a subalgebra
[
GT1
, Lemma 4.2]
. Furthermore there are natural inclusions of algebras

(4.4)

\displaystyle Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}\hookrightarrow Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{g}}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}})^{\operatorname{op}}.

To explain the second inclusion we note that, for
q+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l},\chi}\in Q_{\mathfrak{l}}
and
u+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l},\chi}\in Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
, the following right action is well-defined

(4.5)

\displaystyle(q+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l},\chi})\cdot(u+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l},\chi})=qu+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l},\chi}.

The
finite
W
-algebra
and the
extended finite
W
-algebra
are the invariant subspaces

(4.6)

\displaystyle U({\mathfrak{g}},e):=Q_{0}^{N_{0}}

and

\displaystyle\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e):=Q_{0}^{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}.

In previous work
[
Pr4
,
GT1
]
this notation was always used for the invariant algebras
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}
corresponding to a Lagrangian
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
. Thanks to
[
GT1
, Proposition 6.1]
we have an isomorphism
Q_{0}^{N_{0}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}
, and so the algebra
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}
does not depend on
\mathfrak{l}
. By contrast
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}

does
depend on the choice of isotropic space, and the precise relationship between
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
Q_{0}^{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}
is explained by the PBW theorem (Theorem
4.3
).

The advantage of studying
Q_{0}^{N_{0}}
and
Q_{0}^{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}
is that these algebras carry natural actions of
C^{e}
by automorphisms, and this additional structure is vital to the present work.

4.3.
Slices and sheets

We continue with a fixed isotropic space
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}
be the set of
\eta\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
vanishing on
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}
and let
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
be any graded complement to
[{\mathfrak{g}},e]
. Since
{\mathbbm{k}}\Gamma
is semisimple and
\Gamma\subseteq C^{e}
we may (and shall) choose
\mathfrak{v}
to be
\Gamma
-stable. We set
\check{\mathfrak{v}}=\kappa(\mathfrak{v})\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
.

Define the
good transverse slice to
G\cdot\chi
to be
\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
. This good slice admits a contracting
{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}
-action given by

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mu(t)\cdot\eta:=t^{2}\gamma_{e}(t^{-1})\cdot\eta

for
t\in{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}
, with unique fixed point
\chi
. One important feature is that the dimension of the
G
-orbit of
\mu(t)\cdot\eta
does not depend on
t\in{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}
, and so
\dim(G\cdot\eta)\geq\dim(G\cdot\chi)
for all
\eta\in\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
. It follows that
\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
is transversal to every orbit which it intersects.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a decomposition class of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
such that
\chi\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\operatorname{reg}}
, write
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\in{\mathrm{LD}}({\mathfrak{g}})
for a corresponding induction datum, and set
\mathfrak{z}^{*}:=\kappa(\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0}))\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. The following hold:

(i)

The coadjoint action map restricts to an isomorphism

N_{\mathfrak{l}}\times(\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}.

(ii)

\dim\big((\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}})\cap\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\operatorname{reg}}\big)=\dim\mathfrak{z}^{*}
.

(iii)

Suppose that there exists a parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
containing
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
as a Levi factor such that
e\in\mathcal{O}_{0}+\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{p})
. Then
\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\operatorname{reg}}
.

Proof.

Part (i) is
[
GT1
, Lemma 5.1]
. Part (ii) follows from Lemma
3.6
(ii) along with the fact that
\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
is transversal to
G\cdot\chi
at
\chi
.

We now prove part (iii). The assumption
e\in\mathcal{O}_{0}+\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{p})
implies that
e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})\subseteq e_{0}+\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{p})+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})}
for some
e_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
, using the description of
\overline{\mathcal{D}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})}
from
[
PS1
, Theorem 2.3]
. Applying
\kappa
gives
\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}
. Using part (i) and the transversality of
\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
again, it follows that the dimension of the
G
-orbit of
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}
cannot be strictly less that
\dim G\cdot\chi
. We conclude that
\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\operatorname{reg}}
which completes the proof.
∎

4.4.
The
p
-centre and the PBW theorem for finite
W
-algebras

The
p
-centre
\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
of the extended finite
W
-algebra

\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
is the image of the natural map
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})\to\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. Applying the proof of
[
GT1
, Lemma 7.6]
verbatim, we see that the kernel of this map is generated by
\{x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)^{p}\mid x\in\mathfrak{m}_{0}\}
, and so the identification
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathbbm{k}}[({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})^{(1)}]
descends to an identification
\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)={\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})^{(1)}]
.

Both
N_{0}
and
C^{e}
act on
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
by automorphisms preserving
\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
, and the identification
\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)={\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})^{(1)}]
is
N_{0}
-equivariant and
C^{e}
-equivariant. Furthermore,
C^{e}
normalises
N_{0}
and thus also acts on
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)=\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{N_{0}}
by automorphisms. The
p
-centre
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
of the finite
W
-algebra

U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
is the invariant subalgebra
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)=\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{N_{0}}
and by Lemma
4.2
(i) we identify
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)={\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})^{(1)}]^{N_{0}}
with
{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}})^{(1)}]
; this identification is also
C^{e}
-equivariant. Observe furthermore that both
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
and
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
are finite over their
p
-centres.

If
V
is a
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-module annihilated by the maximal ideal of
\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
corresponding to
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
then we say
V
has
p
-character
\eta
, and use similar terminology for
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules. For an arbitrary isotropic space
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
, the
p
-centres
Z_{p}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}})
and
Z_{p}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}})
are defined analogously to
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
and
\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
, and we have identifications
Z_{p}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}})={\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}]^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}={\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}})^{(1)}]
and
Z_{p}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}})={\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}]
.

The Kazhdan filtration
U({\mathfrak{g}})=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\sf F}_{i}U({\mathfrak{g}})
is defined by placing
{\mathfrak{g}}(i)
in degree
i+2
. The associated graded algebra
\operatorname{gr}U({\mathfrak{g}})
is isomorphic to
S({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathbbm{k}}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]
(see
[
GT1
, §5.2]
for example).
The filtration induced on
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}
is concentrated in non-negative degrees, with
{\sf F}_{0}Q_{\mathfrak{l}}={\mathbbm{k}}
, and
\operatorname{gr}Q_{\mathfrak{l}}
identifies with
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]
. This leads to Kazhdan filtrations on both
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
and
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
with
C^{e}
acting by filtered automorphisms.

The following is the PBW theorem for the (extended) finite
W
-algebra with an arbitrary choice of isotropic space.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
be isotropic.

(i)

There are natural isomorphisms

(4.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}];

(4.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}]^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}}{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}].

(ii)

Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}\cong Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}\otimes_{Z_{p}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}})}Z_{p}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}})
.

(iii)

The map
Q_{0}\twoheadrightarrow Q_{\mathfrak{l}}
induces

(4.10)

\displaystyle U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}};

(4.11)

\displaystyle\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}.

In particular,
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}
is independent of
\mathfrak{l}
up to isomorphism.

(iv)

There is a
\Gamma
-equivariant embedding
\Theta:{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}\hookrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
such that
\Theta({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})
generates
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
.

Proof.

The isomorphism (
4.8
) is
[
GT1
, Theorem 5.2]
and Lemma
4.2
(i).

The first isomorphism in (
4.9
) follows from the more precise claim that the inclusion
\operatorname{gr}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}})\subseteq(\operatorname{gr}Q_{\mathfrak{l}})^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
is an equality. The second isomorphism in (
4.9
) follows from the claim that
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}]^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
is generated by the subalgebras
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}]={\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]^{p}
, and that the natural map
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}]^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}}{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}]\to{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
is an isomorphism. Both of these claims follow from
[
GT1
, §5.4]
(see also
[
GT3
, Lemma 2.1]
).

To prove (ii) from (i) we only need to show that both
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}]^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}]
can be lifted to filtered subalgebras of
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
. Thanks to (
4.8
) these algebras are lifted by
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{N_{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
Z_{p}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}})
respectively.

The isomorphism (
4.10
) is explained in
[
GT1
, §6]
, whilst the surjection (
4.11
) follows directly from (ii) along with the identification
Z_{p}(Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}})={\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp})^{(1)}]
.

It remains to prove (iv). Recall that
\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
is
\Gamma
-stable by construction. The identification
\operatorname{gr}(Q_{0}^{N_{0}})\cong{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}]
is
\Gamma
-equivariant and the invariant bilinear form
\kappa
allows us to identify
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}]=S({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})
. In summary,
\operatorname{gr}U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\cong S({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})
as algebras with
\Gamma
-action. Writing
S({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})_{i}
for the
i
th graded piece with respect to the Kazhdan filtration, we have for each
i\geq 0
an exact sequence
{\sf F}_{i-1}U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\hookrightarrow{\sf F}_{i}U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\twoheadrightarrow S({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})_{i}
of
\Gamma
-modules. Using the semisimplicity of
{\mathbbm{k}}\Gamma
once more we see that these sequences all split. It follows that the embedding
{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}\hookrightarrow S({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})
may be lifted to an embedding
{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}\hookrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
of
\Gamma
-modules such that the image generates
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. This completes the proof of (iv).
∎

4.5.
The Poisson structure on the slice

Some of our arguments rely on some elementary Poisson geometry and we gather together the necessary details here. If
X
is any smooth affine Poisson variety then the Hamiltonian vector fields give rise to a space of tangent vectors at each
x\in X
. The
rank
of
X
at
x
is defined to be the dimension of this space.

The identification
{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})/\!/N_{0}]={\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}]
from Lemma
4.2
(i) allows us to equip
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}]
with a Poisson bracket by Poisson reduction, as follows. The preimage of
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}
under
{\mathbbm{k}}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]\to{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]
is contained in the Poisson idealiser of the kernel. Therefore if we write
J_{0}=(\mathfrak{m}_{0,\chi})\subseteq{\mathbbm{k}}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]
for the defining ideal of
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]
then for
f_{1}+J_{0},f_{2}+J_{0}\in{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}
the bracket

\{f_{1}+J_{0},f_{2}+J_{0}\}=\{f_{1},f_{2}\}+J_{0}\in{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}.

is well-defined. Furthermore (
4.8
) implies that
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
is a filtered quantization of this Poisson structure.

Let
f\in{\mathbbm{k}}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]
be in the preimage of
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}
and
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
, so that
N_{0}\cdot\eta\in(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})/\!/N_{0}
. Then the corresponding Hamiltonian tangent vector at
N_{0}\cdot\eta
lies in
T_{N_{0}\cdot\eta}\big(G\cdot\eta\cap(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})/\!/N_{0}\big)
. Using Lemma
4.2
(i) and the transversality of
\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
it follows that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}_{\eta}(\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}})\leq\dim G\cdot\eta-\dim G\cdot\chi.

In fact, one can prove equality but we wont need this in what follows.

4.6.
Reduced finite
W
-algebras

We identify
\operatorname{Spec}\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
with
(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})^{(1)}
. On the level of sets of closed points we can also identify
(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})^{(1)}=\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
, and, since this will cause no confusion, we allow ourselves to do this henceforth.
If
X\subseteq\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
is a closed subscheme with defining ideal
I\subseteq\widehat{Z}_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
, then we write
\widehat{U}_{X}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
for the
p
-central reduction
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)/I\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. If
X=\{\eta\}
is a point we simply write
\widehat{U}_{\eta}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
for this algebra and call it a
reduced finite
W
-algebra with
p
-character
\eta
.

We use similar notation
U_{X}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
for a
p
-central reduction of
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
whenever
X\subseteq\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}/\!/N_{0}=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}
is a closed subvariety. If
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
then, writing
\pi:\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}\to\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}/\!/N_{0}
for the projection, Theorem
4.3
(ii) states that the map
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\to\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
induces an isomorphism

(4.13)

\displaystyle U_{\pi(\eta)}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\widehat{U}_{\eta}({\mathfrak{g}},e).

The significance of these algebras comes from
Premet’s equivalence
, which states that for every
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle U_{\eta}({\mathfrak{g}})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Mat}_{p^{d_{\chi}}}\widehat{U}_{\eta}({\mathfrak{g}},e).

Remark 4.4
.

Although Premet’s equivalence was originally proven for
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}
where
\mathfrak{l}
is Lagrangian
[
Pr2
, §2]
, this slightly more general version can be deduced by observing that for any
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
there exists some Lagrangian
\mathfrak{l}
with
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}
.

4.7.
Abelian quotients of
W
-algebras

Recall that
\Gamma\subseteq C^{e}
is a finite subgroup such that
p
does not divide
|\Gamma|
. The maximal abelian quotient
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
of
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
is the quotient algebra obtained by factoring out the ideal
([u,v]\mid u,v\in\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e))
(cf. Section
2.2
). The action of
C^{e}
on
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
descends to an action on
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and we consider the coinvariant algebra
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
obtained from
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
by factoring out the ideal
(u-\gamma\cdot u\mid\gamma\in\Gamma,u\in\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}})
.
Similarly we consider the abelian quotient
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and the coinvariant algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
. If
X\subseteq\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
and
Y\subseteq(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})/\!/N_{0}
are closed subvarieties then we use the notation

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{X}({\mathfrak{g}},e):=\operatorname{Spec}\widehat{U}_{X}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{Y}({\mathfrak{g}},e):=\operatorname{Spec}U_{Y}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}

and make the abbreviations
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)=\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
and
\mathcal{E}({\mathfrak{g}},e)=\mathcal{E}_{\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}/\!/N_{0}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. As we observed in Section
2.2
the points of
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{X}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
parametrise the one dimensional
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules admitting a
p
-character in
X
, and similar for
\mathcal{E}_{Y}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. By the same token, the points of
\operatorname{Spec}\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
and
\operatorname{Spec}U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
parametrise the
\Gamma
-invariant one dimensional
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules and
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules, respectively. As reduced schemes we have equality

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\Gamma}=\operatorname{Spec}\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\Gamma}=\operatorname{Spec}U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}
be a
\Gamma
-stable complement to
[{\mathfrak{g}}^{e},{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}]
.

(i)

There is a
\Gamma
-equivariant surjective homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{c})\twoheadrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
;

(ii)

There is a surjective homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{c}^{\Gamma})\twoheadrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
(iv), we see that
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is a commutative algebra with an action of
\Gamma
by automorphisms, generated by a
\Gamma
-submodule
\Theta({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}
. By the universal property of
S({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})
we have
\Gamma
-equivariant mapping
S({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})\twoheadrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
. We claim that the composition
S(\mathfrak{c})\hookrightarrow S({\mathfrak{g}}^{e})\twoheadrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is surjective. To see this we note that the ordered monomials in
\Theta(\mathfrak{c})
together with
([u,v]\mid u,v\in U({\mathfrak{g}},e))
span
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. This last claim can be proven using the same argument as
[
PT1
, Lemma 16]
, in view of
[
GT1
, Remark 7.5]
.

Observe that
\mathfrak{c}_{-1}:=\{c-\gamma\cdot c\mid c\in\mathfrak{c},\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is a
\Gamma
-submodule of
\mathfrak{c}
which contains nonzero elements of every submodule which carries non-trivial
\Gamma
-action. Since
p\nmid\left|\Gamma\right|
, we can conclude that
\mathfrak{c}_{-1}
is a
\Gamma
-stable complement to
\mathfrak{c}^{\Gamma}
in
\mathfrak{c}
, contained in
(f-\gamma\cdot f\mid\gamma\in\Gamma,f\in S(\mathfrak{c}))
. Taking
\Gamma
-coinvariants is a right exact functor and so the map
S(\mathfrak{c})\twoheadrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
from (i) descends to a map
S(\mathfrak{c}^{\Gamma})\cong S(\mathfrak{c})/(\mathfrak{c}_{-1})\twoheadrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
as required.
∎

We proceed to provide lower bounds on the Krull dimensions of
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
and
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
in terms of sheets, similar to
[
Pr4
, Theorem 1.2]
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{1},...,\mathcal{S}_{t}
be the sheets of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
containing
\chi
, and make the notation
\{({\mathfrak{g}}_{i},\mathcal{O}_{i})\mid i=1,...,t\}
for the corresponding (
G
-orbits of) rigid induction data (cf. Lemma
3.6
). Consider the varieties

(4.15)

\displaystyle\chi+Y:=(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{i}

and

\displaystyle\chi+X=(\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}})\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{i}.

Let
\pi:\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}\to(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})/\!/N_{0}
be the quotient map. Using Lemma
4.2
(i) we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle\pi(\chi+Y)=\chi+X.

Lemma 4.6
.

The following hold:

(1)

\chi+Y=\{\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}\mid\dim G\cdot\eta\leq\dim G\cdot\chi\}
.

(2)

\chi+Y
is a closed subvariety of
\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
.

(3)

\chi+X
is a closed subvariety of
\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
.

Proof.

Certainly the orbits
G\cdot\eta
with
\eta\in\chi+Y
have dimension
\leq\dim G\cdot\chi
. In view of Lemma
3.6
(iii) the reverse inclusion follows from the general assertion: a decomposition class intersects
\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
if and only if its closure contains
\chi
(see
[
To
, Proposition 7.4]
, for example). This proves (1).

The collection of
\eta\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
such that
\dim G\cdot\eta\leq\dim G\cdot\chi
is closed in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, and so its intersection with
\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
is closed in
\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
, whence (2). Now (3) follows from (2) and Lemma
4.2
(i).
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

(Cf.
[
Pr4
, §3]
)

The homomorphism
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\to U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
induces a finite, surjective,
\Gamma
-equivariant morphism
\mathcal{E}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\to\chi+X
hence

\dim\mathcal{E}({\mathfrak{g}},e)=\dim(\chi+X)=\max_{1\leq i\leq t}\dim\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{i}).

Proof.

Write
\phi:Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\to U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}
for the
\Gamma
-equivariant homomorphism. Since finite morphisms of algebraic varieties are closed, the image of the map
\phi^{*}:\mathcal{E}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\to\operatorname{Spec}Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)=(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})^{(1)}/\!/N_{0}
is a closed subvariety, and the set
\operatorname{im}\phi^{*}
consists of those maximal ideals of
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
for which the corresponding quotient admits a one dimensional representation (cf. Lemma
2.1
).

Thanks to (
4.14
),
\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\phi^{*})
consists of those
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
such that
U_{\eta}({\mathfrak{g}})
admits a module of dimension
p^{d_{\chi}}
. Applying Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
4.6
(1) we deduce
\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\phi^{*})=\chi+Y
.

Combining the remarks of the previous paragraph with (
4.16
) we have
\operatorname{im}\phi^{*}=\chi+X.
Since
\phi^{*}
is finite we have
\dim\mathcal{E}({\mathfrak{g}},e)=\dim(\chi+X)
. It follows from Lemma
4.2
(ii) that
\dim(\chi+X)=\max_{i}\dim\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{i})
, which completes the proof of the current proposition.
∎

Let
A
be an affine Poisson algebra. If we choose a system of generators
a_{1},...,a_{m}
of
A
and form the matrix
\pi_{A}=(\{a_{i},a_{j}\})_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
then the rank of
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
at a point
x
coincides with the rank of
\pi_{A}
modulo the maximal ideal of
x
(see
[
LPV
, 1.2.3]
for example). Write
A^{\operatorname{ab}}
for the
maximal Poisson abelian quotient
which is obtained by factoring out the ideal
(\{f_{1},f_{2}\}\mid f_{1},f_{2}\in A)
generated by Poisson brackets. By the Leibniz rule, this ideal is generated by the entries of
\pi_{A}
. It follows that
\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\operatorname{ab}})
can be characterised as the points of
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
where the rank is zero, in the sense of Section
4.5
.

Proposition 4.8
.

There is an embedding of algebraic varieties
(\chi+X)^{\Gamma}\hookrightarrow(\mathfrak{c}^{\Gamma})^{*}
.

Proof.

Combining (
4.12
) with our remarks prior to Proposition
4.7
, we see that the rank of the Poisson structure at a point
\eta\in\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}
is less than or equal to
r(\eta):=\dim G\cdot\eta-\dim G\cdot\chi
. By Lemma
4.6
we see that
\chi+X
consists of points such that
r(\eta)=0
. Therefore
\chi+X\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}]^{\operatorname{ab}})
. By Lemma
4.6
(3) this is a closed embedding of affine varieties, and it follows that
{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}]^{\operatorname{ab}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+X]
.

Applying an argument analogous to part (i) of Proposition
4.5
we see that there is a
\Gamma
-equivariant homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{c})\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbbm{k}}[\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}]^{\operatorname{ab}}
.

Combining these remarks and taking
\Gamma
-coinvariants we have a surjective homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{c}^{\Gamma})\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+X)^{\Gamma}]
which proves the claim.
∎

5.
The parabolic induction functor

We retain the notation and hypotheses of Sections
3
and
4
.

5.1.
Equivariance of Skryabin’s equivalence

Throughout this section,
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
will denote a Lagrangian subspace. Skryabin’s equivalence is a Morita equivalence between
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
and a certain central quotient of
U({\mathfrak{g}})
. It can be viewed as a global version of (
4.14
). Using Theorem
4.3
(ii) we obtain similar equivalences when the isotropic subspace of
{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
is zero.

Proposition 5.1
.

There is a
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
-equivariant isomorphism
U_{\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}}({\mathfrak{g}})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Mat}_{p^{d_{\chi}}}\widehat{U}_{\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}}({\mathfrak{g}},e).

Proof.

This follows straight from Theorem
4.3
(ii) along with
[
GT1
, Theorem 9.2(iv)]
; note that the proof there does not require
\mathfrak{l}
to be compatible with
T
in the sense of §2.3 of
op. cit.
, and it works verbatim in the current situation.
∎

Combining (
4.5
) and (
4.11
) we see that
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}
is a
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-bimodule. This bimodule gives rise to
Skryabin’s functor

\displaystyle s_{\mathfrak{l}}:\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle U({\mathfrak{g}})\!\operatorname{-mod};

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle Q_{\mathfrak{l}}\otimes_{\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)}V.

In fact one can check that the category equivalence which results from Proposition
5.1
is realised by lifting
\widehat{U}_{\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules to
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-modules and then applying
s_{\mathfrak{l}}
.

The reductive group
C^{e}
acts on the algebras
U({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
and thus, as in Section
2.1
, acts on the categories
U({\mathfrak{g}})\!\operatorname{-mod}
and
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}
by twisting.

Theorem 5.2
.

The following hold:

(i)

\dim s_{\mathfrak{l}}(V)=p^{d_{\chi}}\dim V
for all finite-dimensional
V\in\widehat{U}_{\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}
.

(ii)

Let
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{\perp}
and let
V
be a simple
\widehat{U}_{\eta}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-module. Then
{}^{g}s_{\mathfrak{l}}(V)\cong s_{\mathfrak{l}}({}^{g}V)
for all
g\in C^{e}
.

Remark 5.3
.

Suppose that in Theorem
5.2
(ii) we had only assumed
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}
. If there were to exist
g\in C^{e}
such that
\eta\notin\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{g^{-1}\cdot\mathfrak{l}}
(and thus
g\eta\notin\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}
) then we would have
s_{\mathfrak{l}}(V)\neq 0
(and so
{}^{g}s_{\mathfrak{l}}(V)\neq 0
) but
s_{\mathfrak{l}}({}^{g}V)=0
. Assuming
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{\perp}
avoids this problem.

The dimension claim follows from the fact that
s_{\mathfrak{l}}
induces the equivalence of Proposition
5.1
. The proof of the second claim will occupy the rest of the current section. Our method is to show that
s_{\mathfrak{l}}
occurs as a quotient of a
C^{e}
-equivariant functor
s_{0}
and that
s_{0}
splits into a direct sum of copies of
s_{\mathfrak{l}}
when applied to simple modules.

Lemma 5.4
.

The following hold:

(i)

Q_{0}
is a
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-bimodule and
Q_{0}\twoheadrightarrow Q_{\mathfrak{l}}
is a bimodule homomorphism;

(ii)

The functor
s_{0}:=Q_{0}\otimes_{\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)}(-):\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}\to U({\mathfrak{g}})\!\operatorname{-mod}
is
C^{e}
-equivariant;

(iii)

For finite-dimensional
V\in\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}
we have
\dim s_{0}(V)=p^{\dim\mathfrak{n}_{0}}\dim V
.

Proof.

It is an easy exercise to see that the right
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-action on
Q_{0}
given by
(q+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{0,\chi})(u+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{0,\chi})=qu+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{0,\chi}
is well-defined and (i) follows immediately because the right
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-action on
Q_{\mathfrak{l}}
is defined by the same formula (see (
4.5
)).

We have observed that the
C^{e}
-action on
U({\mathfrak{g}})
descends to both
Q_{0}
and
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
. If
g\in C^{e}
and
V\in\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}
then as in Section
2.1
an isomorphism
{}^{g}(Q_{0}\otimes_{\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)}V)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}Q_{0}\otimes_{\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)}{}^{g}V
is given by
q\otimes v\mapsto(g\cdot q)\otimes v
, which proves (ii). Finally Theorem
4.3
(i) and (ii) imply that
Q_{0}
is a free right
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-module of rank
p^{r}
where
r=\dim{\mathfrak{g}}-\dim\mathfrak{m}_{0}-\dim{\mathfrak{g}}^{e}
(cf.
[
GT1
, Theorem 9.2(i)]
). The latter equals
\dim\mathfrak{n}_{0}
by (
4.3
), which proves part (iii).
∎

Following
[
BGK
]
we define
\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}}
to be the
neutral copy of
{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)
. It is a symplectic vector space equipped with a linear isomorphism
{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)\to\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}};x\mapsto x_{\operatorname{ne}}
. The symplectic form
\Psi_{\operatorname{ne}}
is defined
\Psi_{\operatorname{ne}}(x_{\operatorname{ne}},y_{\operatorname{ne}}):=\Psi(x,y)=\kappa(e,[x,y])
, so that
{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)\to\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}}
is a symplectomorphism. We abuse notation by writing
x\mapsto x_{\operatorname{ne}}
for the map
{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}}
obtained by projecting via the grading.

The Weyl algebra associated to
(\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}},\Psi_{\operatorname{ne}})
will be denoted
U(\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}})
.
We consider the algebra
U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}):=U({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes U(\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}})
. Our next objective is to construct a functor
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\!\operatorname{-mod}\to U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})\!\operatorname{-mod}
. The subspace

\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{0,\chi}:=\{x-x_{\operatorname{ne}}-\chi(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{n}_{0}\}\subseteq U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})

allows us to define the left
U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
-module
\widetilde{Q}:=U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})/U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{0,\chi}
. We identify
U({\mathfrak{g}})
with the subalgebra
U({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes 1\subseteq U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
, and we note that
U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
satisfies the following PBW theorem: there is a standard filtration placing
{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}}
in degree one and
\operatorname{gr}U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})\cong S({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}})
.

Lemma 5.5
.

The following hold:

(i)

The map
U({\mathfrak{g}})\to U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
gives an embedding
U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{0,\chi}\subseteq U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{0,\chi}
.

(ii)

\widetilde{Q}
is a
U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
-
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-bimodule with right
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-action

(5.1)

\displaystyle(q+U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{0,\chi})(u+U({\mathfrak{g}})\mathfrak{m}_{0,\chi}):=qu+U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{0,\chi}.

(iii)

The following map induced by part (i) is an isomorphism of
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-bimodules

(5.2)

\displaystyle Q_{0}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\widetilde{Q}.

Proof.

The inclusion
U({\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
restricts to
\mathfrak{m}_{0,\chi}\hookrightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{0,\chi}
, which proves (i). Checking that the action (
5.1
) is well-defined is a short calculation which we leave to the reader, and it clearly commutes with the left
U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
-action.

Finally the map (
5.2
) is an isomorphism on the level of vector spaces, using (i) and the PBW theorems for
U({\mathfrak{g}})
and
U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
. This isomorphism intertwines the right
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-actions, which may be seen by comparing the first line of the proof of Lemma
5.4
with (
5.1
).
∎

We define the
p
-centre of
U(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})
to be the central subalgebra
Z_{p}(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}):=Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes Z(\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}})
. Using an argument almost identical to
[
GT1
, Lemma 7.6]
we see that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}_{Z_{p}(\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}})}(\widetilde{Q})=(x^{p}-x^{[p]}-x_{\operatorname{ne}}^{p}-\chi(x)^{p}\mid x\in\mathfrak{n}_{0}).

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
V
be a simple
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-module with
p
-character
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}
. Then

s_{0}(V)\cong s_{\mathfrak{l}}(V)^{\oplus p^{\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)}}.

Proof.

If
V
is a
\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
-module with
p
-character
\eta
then
\widetilde{Q}\otimes_{\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)}V
has
p
-character
\eta
, and in particular,
\{x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\eta(x)^{p}\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)\}
lies in the annihilator. From (
5.3
) it follows that
\{x^{p}-x^{[p]}-x_{\operatorname{ne}}^{p}-\chi(x)^{p}\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)\}
annihilates
\widetilde{Q}\otimes_{\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)}V
. Combining, we see that
\{x_{\operatorname{ne}}^{p}-(\eta-\chi)(x)^{p}\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)\}
also annihilates.

Since
U(\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}})
is Azumaya over the
p
-centre, we see that
\widetilde{Q}\otimes_{\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)}V
descends to a module over

U({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes U(\mathfrak{k}_{\operatorname{ne}})/(x_{\operatorname{ne}}^{p}-(\eta-\chi)(x)^{p}\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(-1))\cong U({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes\operatorname{Mat}_{p^{\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)}}({\mathbbm{k}})\cong\operatorname{Mat}_{p^{\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)}}U({\mathfrak{g}}).

By (
5.2
) we conclude that
Q_{0}\otimes_{\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)}V\cong W^{\oplus p^{\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(-1)}}
for some
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-module
W
. Using (
4.3
) we calculate

5.3.
Classical parabolic induction as a finite morphism

In this section we show that (
5.2
) is a finite morphism between two representation schemes which parametrise minimal modules. First we need a lemma.

Lemma 5.9
.

(i)

For
\eta\in\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}
the algebra
\widehat{U}_{\eta}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
admits a one dimensional module.

(ii)

For
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}
the algebra
\widehat{U}_{\eta}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
admits a one dimensional module.

(iii)

\dim\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})=\dim\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)=\dim\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
.

Proof.

First we prove (i) and (ii). Combining Lemma
3.2
with (
4.14
) it will suffice to show that
\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{\eta}=\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{\chi_{0}}
for
\eta\in\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}
and that
\dim{\mathfrak{g}}^{\eta}=\dim{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi}
for
\eta\in\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}
. The first of these assertions follows from the definition of
\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}
. The second follows from Lemma
4.2
(iii), making use of the hypotheses
\chi|_{\mathfrak{r}}=0
and
\chi|_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}=\chi_{0}
.

Now we move on to prove (iii). The natural map
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})={\mathbbm{k}}[({\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{*})^{(1)}]\to\widehat{U}_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})^{\operatorname{ab}}
is finite because
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
is finite over the
p
-centre and it certainly factors through the quotient
{\mathbbm{k}}[({\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{*})^{(1)}]\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*})^{(1)}]
. By Lemma
2.1
the kernel of the map
{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*})^{(1)}]\to\widehat{U}_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})^{\operatorname{ab}}
lies inside the intersection of the maximal ideals
I_{\eta}\subseteq{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*})^{(1)}]
such that the
p
-character
\eta
supports a one dimensional
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})
-module. By (i) we deduce that
{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*})^{(1)}]\hookrightarrow\widehat{U}_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})^{\operatorname{ab}}
and so there is a finite, dominant morphism
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})\to(\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*})^{(1)}
, which confirms
\dim\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})=\dim\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
. The proof of
\dim\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)=\dim\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
is identical using (ii) in place of (i).
∎

When

and
A

{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra we write

for the set of isomorphism classes of
d
-dimensional representations of

. By Lemma
5.7
the functor (
5.2
) restricts to a map

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\operatorname{Rep}_{p^{d_{\chi_{0}}}}U_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{Rep}_{p^{d_{\chi}}}U_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}).\end{array}

Proposition 5.10
.

\operatorname{Rep}_{p^{d_{\chi_{0}}}}U_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
and
\operatorname{Rep}_{p^{d_{\chi}}}U_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}})
each carry the structure of an affine algebraic variety, and (

) is a finite morphism.

Proof.

For the duration of the proof we use the notation
D_{\chi}=p^{d_{\chi}}
and
D_{\chi_{0}}=p^{d_{\chi_{0}}}
.

(i)
\operatorname{Rep}_{D_{\chi_{0}}}U_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
as a variety
. Choose a basis
x_{1},...,x_{m}
for
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and let
I
denote the kernel of the resulting map
{\mathbbm{k}}\langle x_{1},...,x_{m}\rangle\twoheadrightarrow U_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
. Let
X
denote the affine variety consisting of tuples
(A_{1},...,A_{m})\in\operatorname{Mat}_{D_{\chi_{0}}}({\mathbbm{k}})^{m}
such that
F(A_{1},...,A_{m})=0
for all

. The group
H_{0}=\mathrm{GL}_{D_{\chi_{0}}}({\mathbbm{k}})
acts on

parametrise the semisimple representations of dimension
D_{\chi_{0}}
(see
[

, Theorem 4.1]
for example). Since

we know that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{\chi_{0}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{\eta}
for all

and it follows from Proposition

that every finite dimensional

. In particular,
\operatorname{Rep}_{D_{\chi_{0}}}U_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})

.

(ii)
\operatorname{Rep}_{D_{\chi}}U_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}})
as a variety.
The description of the variety structure on
\operatorname{Rep}_{D_{\chi}}U_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is very similar to part (i), however we provide some details for convenience, and to fix notation. Let
x_{1},...,x_{n}
be a basis for
{\mathfrak{g}}
, for some

Y
denote the subvariety of

. Let
H=\mathrm{GL}_{D_{\chi}}({\mathbbm{k}})
act on
Y
by simultaneous conjugation, and consider

. Thanks to Lemma
4.2
(iii) we see that
\dim{\mathfrak{g}}^{\chi}=\dim{\mathfrak{g}}^{\eta}
for all

and thus
\operatorname{Rep}_{D_{\chi}}U_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}})
consists of simple modules, leading us to identify
\operatorname{Rep}_{D_{\chi}}U_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}})=Y/\!/H
.

(iii)
Parabolic induction is a morphism of varieties.
If
V
is a fixed representation of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
corresponding to a tuple

then the entries of matrices

. In other words parabolic induction defines a morphism

(iv)

By Proposition

. The

of an induced module

\widehat{U}_{\chi_{0}+\mathfrak{z}_{0}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},e_{0})^{\operatorname{ab}}
{\mathbbm{k}}[(\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*})^{(1)}]
\widehat{U}_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}

5.4.
The pullback functor as a finite morphism

Here we show that the functor (
5.11
) induces a finite morphism on the varieties of one dimensional representations.
In particular, the homomorphism
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)\hookrightarrow\widehat{U}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\to\widehat{U}_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)
induces a map via pullback

(5.20)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}},e)\to\mathcal{E}({\mathfrak{g}},e).

By Lemma
4.2
we have a map

(5.21)

\displaystyle\chi+\mathfrak{z}^{*}\hookrightarrow\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}\twoheadrightarrow(\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})/\!/N_{0}=\chi+\check{\mathfrak{v}}.

Lemma 5.11
.

The composition (
5.21
) is a finite morphism.

Proof.

We prove the equivalent statement in
{\mathfrak{g}}
: the map
\rho:e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})\to(e+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp})/\!/N_{0}
is a finite morphism, where we slightly abuse notation by writing
\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\mid\kappa(\mathfrak{m}_{0},x)=0\}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
for the duration of the proof.
Equivalently we show that the algebra homomorphism
\rho^{*}:{\mathbbm{k}}[e+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}\to{\mathbbm{k}}[e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})]
is finite. Observe that both
{\mathbbm{k}}[e+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}
and
{\mathbbm{k}}[e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})]
are graded via the contracting
{\mathbbm{k}}^{\times}
-action on
\chi+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}
, and that
\rho^{*}
is a graded homomorphism. Let
f_{1},...,f_{n}\in{\mathbbm{k}}[e+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}
be a collection of homogeneous generators of
{\mathbbm{k}}[e+\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\perp}]^{N_{0}}
. By definition, their vanishing locus is precisely
\{e\}
.

By Lemma
4.2
(i) for
z\in\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
there is a unique pair
(g,v)\in N_{0}\times\mathfrak{v}
such that
g\cdot(e+v)=e+z
. With these choices we have
\rho^{*}(f_{i})(e+z)=f_{i}(e+v)
and it follows that the vanishing locus of the ideal
(\rho^{*}(f_{i})\mid i=1,...,n)
in
e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
is
(N_{0}\cdot e)\cap(e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0}))
.

We claim that an element
e+z\in e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
is nilpotent if and only if
z=0
. To see this, we recall from the set-up in Section
5.2
that
e=e_{0}+r
for
r\in\mathfrak{r}
. Let
T\subseteq G_{0}
be a maximal torus and choose a system of positive roots for the action of
T
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
e_{0}
and
\mathfrak{r}
both lie in the subspace spanned by positive roots. Now
e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
lies inside the corresponding Borel subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and it follows that
e
is the only nilpotent element, as claimed.

Combining the previous two paragraphs we see that the vanishing locus of the ideal
(\rho^{*}(f_{i})\mid i=1,...,n)
in
e+\mathfrak{z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{0})
is
\{e\}
. Now the ideal
I
generated by
\{\rho^{*}(f_{i})\mid i=1,...,n\}
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
2.2
which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.12
.

The map (
5.20
) is a

-characters the map coincides with (

Proof.

The claim about

-characters follows from (
4.13
).
We have the following commutative diagram

Remark 5.15

Fix

By Section

6.1.
Nilpotent orbits and partitions

The classification of nilpotent

-orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}
is best stated in terms of partitions. Every nilpotent

-orbit in

is labelled by some

corresponding to the Jordan normal form, and an orbit

intersects

if and only if:

(i)

the odd parts of
\lambda
occur with even multiplicity (when
\varepsilon=-1
);

(ii)

the even parts of
\lambda
occur with even multiplicity (when
\varepsilon=1
).

Furthermore the intersection is a single

-orbit unless

and every part of

is even, in which case the intersection splits into two

-orbits, which are labelled I and I​I

. We use the notation

for the partitions of

For

•

\lambda_{i-1}\neq\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}\neq\lambda_{i+2}
; and

•

\varepsilon(-1)^{\lambda_{i}}=\varepsilon(-1)^{\lambda_{i+1}}=-1.

Here we use the convention that
\lambda_{0}=\infty
and
\lambda_{n+1}=0
. The set of all 2-steps is denoted

. When

is even, we say that a partition

Fix

6.5.

By Section

For part (1), we begin by fixing some notation; for this part of the proof, we do not set things up as in Section

Since

. We also have

To prove (2) we first suppose

implies that

and every part of

In the following we use the notation

Suppose that

Remark 6.6
.

We conclude the section by mentioning the natural question of understanding the fibres of the parabolic induction map on minimal modules. In
[
GT2
]
the results for
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
imply that minimal modules can be parabolically induced, and what is more, that two induced modules are isomorphic if and only if the original modules are conjugate by a shifted action of the normaliser of the Levi subalgebra. This is analogous to the linkage principle for parabolically induced representations, similar to
[
Ja1
, §11]
. It seems plausible that this generalises to other types, in which case the main theorem of the current paper would subtend a classification of the minimal

-modules considered in the present article.

is a principal ideal domain, we may put

in Smith Normal Form. In other words, there exist invertible matrices
S,T\in M_{n}(R)
such that
SAT
is diagonal; say
SAT=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
for
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in R
. This also implies that
{\bm{S}},{\bm{T}}\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\overline{S},\overline{T}\in M_{n}({\mathbbm{k}})
are invertible and that
{\bm{S}}{\bm{A}}{\bm{T}}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
and
\overline{S}\overline{A}\overline{T}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1}\otimes 1,\ldots,\lambda_{n}\otimes 1)
. Thus

\operatorname{rank}({\bm{A}})=\operatorname{rank}({\bm{S}}{\bm{A}}{\bm{T}})=\#\{i\mid\lambda_{i}\neq 0\}\geq\#\{i\mid\lambda_{i}\otimes 1\neq 0\}=\operatorname{rank}(\overline{S}\overline{A}\overline{T})=\operatorname{rank}(\overline{A}).

∎

Putting this all together, we get the following result.

Proposition 7.8
.

c_{\pi}({\bm{e}})\geq c_{\pi}(e)
.

Proof.

Set

D=\frac{1}{\left|\pi\right|}\sum_{g\in\pi}A_{g}\in M_{c}(R_{e}).

Then by Proposition
7.6
and Lemma
7.7
we have

c_{\pi}({\bm{e}})=\operatorname{rank}({\bm{\psi}})=\operatorname{rank}([{\bm{\psi}}]_{{\bm{\mathcal{B}}}_{\bm{\mathfrak{c}}}})=\operatorname{rank}({\bm{D}})\geq\operatorname{rank}(\overline{D})=\operatorname{rank}([\psi]_{\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{c}}})=\operatorname{rank}(\psi)=c_{\pi}(e).

∎

7.4.
Even orbits

Given a nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
, the weighted Dynkin diagram of
\mathcal{O}
is a labelling of the Dynkin diagram of
G
by the numbers
0
,
1
and
2
. The construction of this labelling over
{\mathbb{C}}
can be found in
[
CM
, §3.5]
; over
{\mathbbm{k}}
, one may either construct it directly using associated cocharacters (as in
[
LT
, §3]
) or from the bijection between nilpotent orbits over
{\mathbbm{k}}
and over
{\mathbb{C}}
(see
[
Pr3
, §2]
). Both approaches yield the same labelling, since both constructions involve the existence of
\hat{e}\in\mathcal{O}
and a (unique) associated cocharacter
\gamma_{\hat{e}}
for
\hat{e}
with the property that

(7.1)

\gamma_{\hat{e}}(t)\cdot e_{\pm\alpha}=t^{\pm r_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\alpha\in\Delta\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\gamma_{\hat{e}}(t)\cdot h=h\quad\mbox{for all}\quad h\in\mathfrak{t}

where
r_{\alpha}\in\{0,1,2\}
is the label of the node on the weighted Dynkin diagram corresponding to the simple root
\alpha
.

A nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}
is called
even
if all the labels on its weighted Dynkin diagram are even (so
0
or
2
); for (a representative
e
of) such an orbit, we define

d(e)=d(\mathcal{O})=\mbox{\# 2s on the weighted Dynkin diagram of }\mathcal{O}.

For even orbits, the following result is known.

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
be an even nilpotent orbit with weighted Dynkin diagram
\Omega
, and let
\hat{e}\in\mathcal{O}
have the property that an associated cocharacter
\gamma_{\hat{e}}
for
\hat{e}
satisfies (
7.1
). Let
\Omega_{0}
be the subdiagram of
\Omega
consisting of only those nodes with label
0
, and let
\hat{G}_{0}
be the standard Levi subgroup of
G
corresponding to the simple roots in
\Omega_{0}
. Write
\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0}=\operatorname{Lie}(\hat{G}_{0})
and
\hat{e}_{0}=0\in\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0},\hat{e}_{0})
is a rigid induction datum for
\mathcal{O}
.

(2)

\dim(\mathfrak{z}(\hat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0}))=d(\mathcal{O})
.

(3)

There exists a parabolic subgroup
\hat{P}
of
G
with
\hat{G}_{0}
as a Levi factor such that
G^{\hat{e}}\subseteq\hat{P}
.

(4)

\hat{C}^{\hat{e}}\subseteq\hat{G}_{0}
and
\gamma_{\hat{e}}({\mathbbm{k}}^{\times})\subseteq\hat{G}_{0}
.

(5)

\hat{e}
lies in the Lie algebra of the unipotent radical of
\hat{P}
.

Proof.

Part (1) is
[
CM
, Theorem 7.1.6]
(which holds over
{\mathbbm{k}}
as in Section
3.3
), while part (2) is clear from the construction. For parts (3) and (4), note that
\hat{G}_{0}
is precisely the centraliser in
G
of
\gamma_{\hat{e}}
. Then part (3) is
[
Pr3
, Proposition 2.3(ii)]
and part (4) follows immediately from the fact that
\hat{C}^{\hat{e}}:=G^{\hat{e}}\cap\hat{G}_{0}
. Finally, part (5) follows from the construction of
\hat{e}
in
[
Pr3
]
and the description of the Lie algebra of the unipotent radical of
P
.
∎

We have used the notation

Remark 7.13

We let

Corollary 7.15

The Krull dimension of

Proof.

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem

Recall that in this section,

and
C^{e}
acts on

. With
\Gamma
as above, we may then consider the fixed point space

Lemma 7.16
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
is an even nilpotent orbit with representative
e
as in
[
LT
, Table 2]
. Then
c_{\Gamma}(e)=d(e)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.8
(ii) there exists a surjective homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{c}^{\Gamma})\twoheadrightarrow U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
; thus,
c_{\Gamma}(e)
, which is the Krull dimension of

, is at least the Krull dimension of
U({\mathfrak{g}},e)^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\Gamma}
. Corollary
7.15
then yields that
c_{\Gamma}(e)\geq d(e)
.

On the other hand, the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathfrak{c}
passes (surjectively) through the action of
\pi
on
\mathfrak{c}
. In particular,
c_{\pi}(e)=c_{\Gamma}(e)
. Furthermore, note that
c_{\pi}({\bm{e}})=d({\bm{e}})
by
[

, Proposition 13]
. Since the weighted Dynkin diagram is independent of good characteristic, we also know
d({\bm{e}})=d(e)
.

Combining these results with Proposition
7.8
thus yields

d(e)=d({\bm{e}})=c_{\pi}({\bm{e}})\geq c_{\pi}(e)=c_{\Gamma}(e)\geq d(e),

which gives the result.
∎
