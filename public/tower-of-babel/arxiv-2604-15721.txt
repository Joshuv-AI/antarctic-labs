Remarks on Lifespan and Continuation Criteria of Two Dimensional Incompressible Fluid Models
Anping Pan

Abstract
In this paper, we study the lifespan and continuation criteria of several two-dimensional incompressible fluid models. Motivated by a novel energy-vorticity formulation, combining linear transport estimate and a bootstrap argument, we are able to show several longtime existence results of two dimensional fluid models in close-to-Euler regime. A byproduct of our approach is a new conditional BKM type result for the inhomogeneous Euler equation.



1
Introduction

The motion of incompressible fluids is an ubiquitous phenomenon in the real world and is a central theme in modern PDE research. The incompressible Euler equation, describing the motion of incompressible fluid flow in a vessel, admits both fruitful geometric structure and delicate mathematical challenges. The global well-posedness issue in
3
-d boundaryless domain (
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or
\mathbb{T}^{3}
) with smooth initial data remains open and is closely related to the Clay millionaire problem. Recently, many significant progresses have been made in finite-time singularity formation of Euler equation, either from initial data with limited regularity, or in domain with boundaries. We refer the readers to
[
16
]
[
7
]
[
8
]
[
22
]
for more details on this topic.

Despite the difficulty of understanding the global well-posedness issue in
3
-d, the classical Yudovich theory
[
28
]
proved that the incompressible Euler equation in
2
-d is globally well-posed as long as the initial vorticity
\omega_{0}=\nabla\times u_{0}=\partial_{1}u_{0}^{2}-\partial_{2}u_{0}^{1}
is bounded. Regarding partial regularity results in
3
-d, in the seminal work
[
6
]
, the authors established a continuation/blow up criterion (known as BKM criteria) for incompressible Euler in
3
-d, using the
L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\infty}
-norm of vorticity
\omega
. More precisely, solution of incompressible Euler equation can be continued past time
T
, if

\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\omega(t)\rVert_{\infty}dt<\infty.

(1)

From a geometric and variational viewpoint,the Euler equation and many other incompressible fluid PDEs, are Hamiltonian equations falling in the category of Euler-Poincaré equations on the dual Lie algebra of the infinite dimensional Lie group of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms on some manifold
D
, and can be derived from various action principles. A non-exhaustive list of such equations includes the ideal Boussinesq equation, Magneto-Hydrodynamic equation (MHD in short), Inhomogeneous Euler equation (IIE in short), Euler-
\alpha
equation, generalized SQG equation, Hall MHD and many more. We refer the interested readers to
[
2
]
[
1
]
[
18
]
[
19
]
[
24
]
for further discussions in this direction.

Similar to the Lagrangian-Hamiltonian correspondence in classical mechanics, incompressible fluid PDEs can also be interpreted as Hamiltonian systems associated with various energy functionals (Hamiltonians), thus are fit into variational framework from Hamiltonian viewpoint. Specifically in case of dimension
2
, such energies usually admit realization as functionals of a velocity field
u
and an active scalar
\rho
transported by
u
. Let
D=\mathbb{T}^{2}
, typical examples include:

•

(i) The Boussinesq equation with energy given by “kinetic plus gravitational potential”:

E_{\text{Bou}}(u,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\frac{1}{2}\lvert u\rvert^{2}-\rho x_{2})dx.

•

(ii) The MHD equation with energy given by “kinetic plus magnetic”:

E_{\text{MHD}}(u,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}(\lvert u\rvert^{2}+\lvert\nabla\rho|^{2})dx.

(2)

In a more familiar form, the magnetic field and current read
B=\nabla^{\perp}\rho
and
J=\Delta\rho
respectively.

•

(iii) The inhomogeneous incompressible Euler equation with variable density kinetic energy:

E_{\text{IIE}}(u,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\rho dx.

(3)

All of the above equations will be reduced to the incompressible Euler equation when
\rho=1
. Meanwhile, the global well-posedness of the above three equations is not known in
2
-d. Indeed, in contrast to the incompressible Euler equation, there are recent breakthroughs in finite time singularity formation of the Boussinesq equation in
2
-d, and we refer the interested readers to
[
14
]
[
15
]
.

The aim of this work is to establish estimates of the lifespan of the above three equations in close-to-Euler regime using an elementary approach, which is motivated by a unified geometric formulation of incompressible fluids and is Lagrangian in nature. Such a topic has been studied in some recent works, we refer the interested readers to
[
9
]
[
11
]
[
3
]
. However, our approach is different from all existing works: in
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
the authors applied tools from paradifferential calculus to obtain the lifespan estimates in endpoint Besov spaces, in
[
3
]
the authors used energy estimate and Grönwall, which are Eulerian in nature. Meanwhile, our approach is to reduce the analysis to a perturbed system of differential inequalities using
Lagrangian
representation of linear transport equations.

We formally state our first main theorem in this manuscript:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Informal) Let
p>2
, assume that
\omega_{0}\in W^{1,p},\theta_{0}\in W^{2,p}
and
\rho_{0}=1+\delta\theta_{0}
. Taking
E=E_{\text{Bou}}
,
E=E_{\text{IIE}}
or
E_{\text{MHD}}
respectively in the equation (
7
), then for sufficiently small
\delta
, the lifespan
T
of the solution
(\omega,\rho)
of (
7
) admits a triple-log lower bound:

T\geq C_{1}\log C_{2}\log C_{3}\log C_{4}\delta^{-1}\quad\text{ for some }C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}>0

(4)

The choice of the
W^{1,p}
regularity class is crucial, as we require control of
\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}
to close the bootstrap argument. This necessity arises because our estimates for the linear transport equation do not benefit from the cancellation mechanisms inherent in nonlinear fluid PDEs (where high Sobolev norms are typically controlled by
\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}
via Kato-Ponce commutator estimates). Whether such cancellation mechanisms can be recovered within a purely Lagrangian framework, to the author’s knowledge, remains an intriguing question.

The advantages of our Lagrangian approach are two folds: First, it stems from estimates of linear transport equation and standard bootstrap argument, which is clean and elementary. Second, it leads to the first result in continuation of the inhomogeneous Euler equation using just
L^{\infty}
norm of vorticity, as a generalization of the Beale-Kato-Majda criteria.

We briefly recall some recent results in the continuation criteria of IIE. In
[
4
]
, the authors showed that the blow up of IIE can be controlled by
L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\infty}
norm of
\nabla u
(which has four components in
2
-d) and in a very recent work
[
17
]
, a geometric criteria controlling blow up using
L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\infty}
norm of
\partial_{v}u
is obtained, where
v=\nabla^{\perp}\rho
. By elementary Lagrangian estimates and bootstrap argument, we are able to derive a new quantitative BKM-type criteria for the inhomogeneous Euler equation based on our geometric formulation, using
L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\infty}
norm of a single scalar quantity
\omega=\nabla\times(\rho u)
.

The organization of the manuscript is as follows: We first introduce a novel energy-vorticity formulation of these hydrodynamic models, which is a variant of the Clebsch formulation of hydrodynamics. Then we combine the classical propagation of Sobolev regularity of transport equation with a simple bootstrap argument to obtain estimates of lifespan of Boussinesq, MHD and IIE in perturbative regime. Lastly, we establish a quantitative BKM criteria for IIE using our vorticity formulation.

Some Preliminaries

2.1
Conventions and Notations

We will consider our fluid with velocity field
u
and active density
\rho
in the physical domain
D=\mathbb{T}^{2}
. The functional setting we will work with is the space
W^{k,p}(D)
, where:

W^{k,p}(D)=\{f:\lVert f\rVert_{k,p}<+\infty\},\quad\lVert f\rVert_{k,p}=\sum_{\lvert j\rvert\leq k}\lVert D^{j}f\rVert_{p},

here
j
is a multi-index. We will assume
p>2
to ensure
W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}
, thanks to the Sobolev embedding theorem. Moreover, under the same assumption,
W^{1,p}
is an algebra.

We will denote by
X
the flow map associated with vector field
u\in W^{2,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which is assumed to be divergence-free throughout this paper. Lipschitz regularity and divergence-free property of
u
guaranty that
X
is a well-defined curve of area-preserving diffeomorphisms. We denote by
A
the back-to-label map:
A(t,x)=X_{t}^{-1}(x)
.

We will always assume our initial density
\rho_{0}
satisfies

0<C_{\rho}^{-1}\leq\rho_{0}(x)\leq C_{\rho},\quad\text{for all }x\in D,

for some given
C_{\rho}>1
. Letter
C
is used for positive constants depending on the initial vorticity
\omega_{0}
and
C_{\rho}
unless specifically mentioned, and may change from line to line. We say two numbers
A,B>0
satisfies the relation
A\lesssim B
if
A\leq CB
. Two quantities
A,B
are equivalent, denoted by
A\approx B
, if there exists numerical constants
\alpha>1
such that

\alpha^{-1}A\leq B\leq\alpha A.

We will abuse the notation a little bit that the same numbers like
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
will be repeatedly used in different contexts and different sections. Of course, they will not be related and will hopefully not cause any ambiguity.

2.2
The Energy-Vorticity Formulation

We recall the following Clebsch action functional for hydrodynamics (See
[
18
]
[
19
]
):

S(u,\rho,\chi)=\int_{0}^{T}E(u,\rho)-\langle\chi,\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho\rangle_{2}dt.

Here,
u
is the divergence-free fluid velocity,
\rho
is a scalar density and
\chi
is a scalar Lagrange multiplier enforcing
\rho
to be transported by
u
.

Computing the first variation of the functional, we obtain the following system of critical point equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{u}E=\chi\nabla\rho+\nabla q,\\
&\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\chi+u\cdot\nabla\chi=-\partial_{\rho}E.\end{aligned}\right.

(5)

It is not hard to check that choose
E=E_{\text{Bou}}
,
E=E_{\text{IIE}}
and
E=E_{\text{MHD}}
respectively, we will recover the Clebsch variable representation of corresponding fluid PDEs.

Now, we define the vorticity
\omega=\nabla\times\partial_{u}E=\{\rho,\chi\}
. Combined with the transport equations:

\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\quad\partial_{t}\chi+\nabla\cdot(u\chi)=-\partial_{\rho}E.

In Lagrangian coordinate, thanks to Duhamel’s principle, we have the following formulae representing the solutions

\rho=\rho_{0}\circ A_{t},\quad\chi=\bigg(\chi_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\rho}E(X_{\tau})d\tau\bigg)\circ A_{t}.

Hence, in
2
-d, the following Lagrangian formulae for vorticity holds:

\omega=\nabla^{\perp}\rho\cdot\nabla\chi=[(\nabla X\nabla^{\perp}\rho_{0})\cdot(\nabla^{*}X)^{-1}(\nabla\chi_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{*}X_{\tau}\nabla\partial_{\rho}E(X_{\tau})d\tau)]\circ A_{t}

=\bigg(\omega_{0}-\nabla^{\perp}\rho_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla(\partial_{\rho}E\circ X_{\tau})d\tau\bigg)\circ A_{t}

=\bigg(\omega_{0}-\nabla^{\perp}\rho_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{*}X_{\tau}\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\circ X_{\tau}d\tau\bigg)\circ A_{t},

(6)

which solves the following transport equation:

\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=-\{\rho,\partial_{\rho}E\}.

Hence, we are ready to convert the equations (
5
) of
(u,\rho,\chi)
into
(\omega,u,\rho)
as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\{\partial_{\rho}E,\rho\},\\
&\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\\
&\nabla\times\partial_{u}E=\omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(7)

The above system (
7
) will be named as the energy-vorticity system. The recovery of
u
from
\omega
via the third equation is the standard Biot-Savart law when the kinetic energy part of
E
is
\rho
-independent. For inhomogeneous fluid with kinetic energy density depending on
\rho
, we will use weighted Biot-Savart operator introduced in
[
25
]
.

2.3
Transport Lemmas and Inequalities

We collect some useful analytical results in this subsection. We first recall the following standard transport lemma which will be repeatedly used. For a more general version of propagation of Sobolev regularity in transport equation, we refer the readers to
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in W^{2,p}(D)
, assume
X
is the flow map associated with vector field
u\in W^{2,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then we have:

\lVert\nabla(f\circ X_{t})\rVert_{r}\leq\lVert\nabla X_{t}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{r},\quad\lVert f\circ X_{t}\rVert_{1,r}\leq\lVert f\rVert+\lVert\nabla X_{t}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{r}\quad\forall 1\leq r\leq\infty.

(8)

Moreover, there exists constant
C
such that the following exponential bound holds true:

\lVert\nabla X_{t}\rVert_{\infty}\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}d\tau\bigg),\quad\lVert\nabla X_{t}\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{1,p}d\tau\bigg),

(9)

and the above estimates (
8
) (
9
) also hold true if we replace
X_{t}
with
A_{t}=X_{t}^{-1}
.

Thanks to (
6
), we are now ready to prove the following Lagrangian Duhamel estimate, which is not restricted to the perturbative regime:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p>2
, let
\rho_{0}\in W^{2,p}
,
\omega_{0}\in W^{1,p}
. Moreover, we denote
\lVert\rho_{0}-1\rVert_{2,p}=\delta
and define

M=\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}d\tau\bigg),\quad N=\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{1,p}d\tau\bigg),

and

K_{p}=\lVert\nabla^{2}\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{p},\quad K_{0}=\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{\infty}.

Where
C
is chosen such that
\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\leq M
,
\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{1,p}\leq N
, which is legitimate due to lemma
2.1
. Then,
\omega
satisfies the following apriori
W^{1,p}
estimate:

\lVert\omega(t,\cdot)\rVert_{{1,p}}\lesssim 1+M+\delta M\int_{0}^{t}(M+N)K_{0}+M^{2}K_{p}d\tau;

(10)

\lVert\rho(t,\cdot)\rVert_{{2,p}}\lesssim 1+\delta(M+N+M^{2}).

(11)

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

The proof of (
11
) is standard and we omit it here. To demonstrate (
10
) In view of (
6
), straightforward calculation yields:

\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}=\lVert\omega\rVert_{p}+\lVert\nabla\omega\rVert_{p}

\leq\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert_{p}+\lVert\nabla\rho_{0}\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{p}d\tau;

+\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\omega_{0}\rVert_{p}+\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho_{0}\rVert_{p}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{\infty}d\tau;

+\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\rho_{0}\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla^{2}A\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{\infty}d\tau+\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\rho_{0}\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla^{2}\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{p}d\tau.

Using the chord-arc bound (
9
), inhomogeneity condition
\lVert\rho_{0}-1\rVert_{2,p}=\delta
, Sobolev embedding
W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}
and propagation of Sobolev regularity of transport equation (
8
), we conclude

\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim 1+\delta\int_{0}^{t}MK_{p}d\tau+M+\delta M\int_{0}^{t}MK_{0}+\delta M\int_{0}^{t}NK_{0}d\tau+\delta M\int_{0}^{t}M^{2}K_{p}

\lesssim 1+M+\delta M\int_{0}^{t}(M+N)K_{0}+M^{2}K_{p}d\tau.

∎

We will also frequently use the following logarithmic extrapolation inequality. The proof can be found in
[
23
]
and
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
d=2,3
and
p>d
, let
u\in W^{2,p}
be a divergence-free vector field and denote by
\omega=\nabla\times u
the vorticity of
u
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following inequality holds:

\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\leq C[1+\log(2+\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p})]\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}.

(12)

3
Lifespan Estimates of Hydrodynamic Models

In this section, we will derive lower bound estimates of lifespan of the three hydrodynamic models in
2
-d, in nearly homogeneous regime.

In the rest of this section, we will consider respectively the Boussinesq, MHD and IIE in perturbative regime, the initial data
\rho_{0}
is a perturbation of
1
in appropriate sense.

3.1
Boussinesq Case

We now establish the long-time existence of perturbative Boussinesq equation in
2
-d. More precisely, we fix
p>2
and define
\delta:=\lVert\rho_{0}-1\rVert_{2,p}
which measures the inhomogeneity of initial data. We will study the Boussinesq equations in case that
\delta
is suitably small.

The main theorem of this section is:

Theorem 3.1
.

There exist constants
\delta_{0}
and
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}>0
depending on the initial data
\omega_{0}
such that for any small positive number
\delta<\delta_{0}
, the energy norm
\mathcal{E}(t):=\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{2,p}
remains bounded on the interval
[0,T_{\delta}]
, where

T_{\delta}=C_{1}\log C_{2}\log C_{3}\log C_{4}\delta^{-1}.

We will use an elementary Lagrangian approach to prove the result. The idea is to establish apriori estimates of the
W^{1,\infty}
and
W^{2,p}
norm of the flow map
X
of
u
, which leads to a system of perturbed differential inequalities. We then find the lower bound of the lifespan based on a bootstrap argument. Let us state an abstract lemma (cf.
[
26
]
) which we will use throughout the rest of this section.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
I
be an interval of time. Suppose we have one hypothesis
\mathscr{H}(t)
and one outcome
\mathscr{O}(t)
defined for each
t\in I
, then
\mathscr{O}(t)
holds true for all
t\in I
provided the validity of the following statements:

(I)

Suppose
\mathscr{H}(t)
is true at
t\in I
, then
\mathscr{O}(t)
is true.

(II)

Suppose
\mathscr{O}(t)
holds for
t\in I
, then there exists a neighborhood
I_{t}\owns t
such that
\mathscr{H}(t^{\prime})
is true for all
t^{\prime}\in I_{t}
.

(III)

There exists a
t_{*}\in I
such that
\mathscr{H}(t_{*})
is true.

(IV)

Assume
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of time converging to
\tilde{t}
such that
\mathscr{O}(t_{j})
is true for all
j
, then
\mathscr{O}(\tilde{t})
is true.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Let
I_{\mathscr{O}}\subset I
be the interval where
\mathscr{O}(t)
holds true. Condition (IV) guarantees that
I_{\mathscr{O}}
is closed, (I) and (III) guarantees that
I_{\mathscr{O}}
is nonempty, while (I) and (II) guarantees that
I_{\mathscr{O}}
is open. Hence, we conclude
I_{\mathscr{O}}=I
.

∎

We now present the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We divide the proof into several steps.

•

Step 1.
We first establish a system of differential inequalities given by the apriori estimates. Notice that in the Boussinesq case, we have

E(u,\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}-\langle\rho,x_{2}\rangle,\quad\nabla\partial_{\rho}E=-e_{2},\nabla^{2}\partial_{\rho}E=0.

Hence, we obtain
K_{0}=1
,
K_{p}=0
in this case, and by Lemma
2.2
we have the following:

\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\leq C+CM+CM\delta\int_{0}^{t}(M+N)K_{0}d\tau.

Using the Calderon-Zygmund estimate
\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{1,p}\leq C\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}
, we conclude:

\dot{N}\leq CN(1+M+M\delta Y),\quad Y=\int_{0}^{t}(M+N)d\tau.

Meanwhile, by Lemma
2.3
, we have

\dot{M}\leq CM(1+\log(2+\frac{\dot{N}}{N}))\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}.

Plus, we have the following
L^{\infty}
bound for
\omega
:

\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}\leq C+C\delta Z,\quad Z=\int_{0}^{t}Md\tau.

Hence, we end up with the following perturbed system of differential inequalities:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{M}\leq CM[1+\log(2+\frac{\dot{N}}{N})](1+\delta Z),\\
&\dot{N}\leq CN(1+M+M\delta Y),\\
&\dot{Y}=M+N,\quad\dot{Z}=M,\quad Y_{0}=Z_{0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(13)

Clearly, the above system of differential inequality with memory holds for generic
\delta
. Moreover, if we assume that
\delta=0
, we will obtain the double exponential growth of
M
and triple exponential growth of
N
as a consequence.

•

Step 2.
We now establish our bootstrap procedure towards the proof. We will
WLOG
fix a number
C>2
in the system (
13
).
We propose the following bootstrap assumption
\mathscr{H}(t)
: We say
\mathscr{H}(t)
holds, if

\delta Z(t)\leq 1,\quad\delta Y(t)\leq 1

(14)

Since
M,N>0
, the above assumption reduces to
\delta Y(t)\leq 1
.

Now, thanks to the increasing property of
Y
, we conclude that
\mathscr{H}(t)
holds true for all
t\in[0,T_{*}]
, provided
\mathscr{H}(T_{*})
holds true. Therefore,
(M,N)
satisfy a relaxed system of inequality on
[0,T]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{M}\leq 2CM(1+\log(2+\frac{\dot{N}}{N}))\\
&\dot{N}\leq CN(1+2M)\end{aligned}\right.

Using monotonicity of the
\log
function, the inequality of
M
implies the following:

\dot{M}\leq 2CM(1+\log 2C+\log(1+M))\leq C_{*}(1+M)[1+\log(1+M)],\quad C_{*}:=2C(1+\log 2C).

Which, after standard computation, yields for all
t\in[0,T_{*}]

\frac{d}{dt}\log(1+M)\leq C_{*}[1+\log(1+M)]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M\leq e^{-1}\exp(2e^{C_{*}t})-1.

Now, plugging the above bound into the differential inequality of
N
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\log N\leq 2Ce^{-1}\exp(2e^{C_{*}t})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N\leq\exp(\tilde{C}\exp(2e^{C_{*}t})),\quad\tilde{C}=2Ce^{-1}>1.

Which is chosen as our
\mathscr{O}(t)
: We say
\mathscr{O}(t)
holds, if

M(t)\leq e^{-1}\exp(2e^{C_{*}t})-1,\quad N(t)\leq\exp(\tilde{C}\exp(2e^{C_{*}t})).

(15)

We already showed that
\mathscr{H}(t)
implies
\mathscr{O}(t)
. Meanwhile, by absolute continuity of
Y
and
Y(0)=0
, the condition (III) is readily verified. The closedness condition (IV) is obvious and it suffices to verify (II). To this end, with the above bounds of
M
and
N
, we are ready to conclude:

Z(T_{*})\leq e^{-1}\exp(2e^{C_{*}T_{*}}),\quad Y(T_{*})\leq(e^{-1}+1)\exp(\tilde{C}\exp(2e^{C_{*}T_{*}})).

Step 3.
We now complete the bootstrap procedure. Notice that in our above analysis, we do not have any smallness assumption on
\delta
, and it will play a role here to make sure
[0,T_{\delta}]
is non-empty.

We now fix:

\delta_{0}=\frac{99e}{100(1+e)}\exp(-3Ce)

For
T_{*}\leq T_{\delta}=C_{1}\log C_{2}\log C_{3}\log(C_{4}\delta^{-1})
with

C_{1}=C_{*}^{-1},\quad C_{2}=\frac{1}{2},\quad C_{3}=\tilde{C}^{-1},\quad C_{4}=\frac{99e}{100(1+e)}

We have the following:

T_{\delta_{0}}=C_{*}^{-1}\log\frac{1}{2}\log{\frac{e}{2C}\log C_{4}\delta_{0}^{-1}}=C_{*}^{-1}\log(1+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{3}{2})>0,

Hence, fix any
\delta<\delta_{0}
,
T_{*}\leq T_{\delta}
implies :

\delta Y(T_{*})\leq\delta(1+e^{-1})\exp(\tilde{C}\exp(2e^{C_{*}T_{*}}))\leq\delta(1+e^{-1})C_{4}\delta_{0}^{-1}=\frac{99}{100}<1.

Which, thanks to the fact that
Y
is increasing and continuous, yields that
\delta Y(t)\leq 1
holds true on a neighborhood of
T_{*}
.

Finally, we conclude that

\mathscr{O}(t)\quad\textit{holds true }\quad\forall t\in[0,C_{*}^{-1}\log\frac{1}{2}\log\tilde{C}^{-1}\log\frac{99e}{100(1+e)\delta}]=:I_{\delta}.

Moreover, we have:

M(t)\leq\tilde{C}^{-1}e^{-1}\log\frac{99e}{100(1+e)\delta},\quad N(t)\leq\frac{99e}{100(1+e)\delta}.

Hence, for all
t\in I_{\delta}
, we have

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C+CM+CM\delta Y+C\delta(M+M^{2}+N)\lesssim 1+\log\frac{1}{\delta}<\infty.

Therefore, we conclude that the lifespan of the solution has a triple logarithmic lower bound as desired.
∎

3.2
The Two-Dimensional IIE

Now we study the lifespan of inhomogeneous Euler equation in near-Euler regime. For the convenience of representation, we rephrase our setting as:

\rho_{0}=1+\delta\theta_{0},\quad\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{2,p}=1.

By the transport nature of
\rho
, we have

\rho(t,x)-1=\theta(t,x):=\theta_{0}(X_{t}^{-1}(x)),\quad\dot{X}=u(X).

Plugging
E=E_{\text{IIE}}
in (
7
), we derive the energy-vorticity formulation of IIE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\{\frac{1}{2}\lvert u\rvert^{2},\rho\};\\
&\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0;\\
&u=\nabla^{\perp}\mathbf{L}_{\rho}^{-1}\omega,\quad\mathbf{L_{\rho}}=-\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla).\end{aligned}\right.

(16)

We recall that IIE is known to be locally well-posed in Hölder and Hilbert Sobolev spaces (see
[
27
]
), and we refer the readers to
[
12
]
[
13
]

[
11
]
for various well-posedness results in
L^{p}
-based Sobolev spaces and endpoint Besov spaces.

Let the stream function
\psi
be such that
u=\nabla^{\perp}\psi
, we have:

\Delta\psi=\omega-\delta\nabla\cdot(\theta\nabla\psi).

(17)

We now establish several estimates which will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 3.3
.

We define positive increasing functions
M(t),N(t)\geq 1
as follows:

M=\exp\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}d\tau,\quad N=\exp\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{1,p}d\tau.

Then, there is a numerical constant
C
, such that for any fixed
\delta<1/(2C)
and a corresponding time
T_{\delta}>0
, such that for all
t\in[0,T_{\delta}]
the following estimates hold:

\lVert u\rVert_{2,p}\leq\frac{CN\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}}{1-\delta CN}

(18)

\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{1-C\delta}(\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}+\delta M\lVert u\rVert_{\infty})\big[1+\log(2+\frac{C\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}}{1-\delta CN})\big].

(19)

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

We first consider the equation (
17
). Applying the Biot-Savart operator
\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}
to the equation we get:

u=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega-\delta\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\nabla\times(\theta u)

Let
k
be a generic positive integer, then combining boundedness of Biot-Savart operator from
W^{k-1,p}
to
W^{k,p}
, boundedness of Leray projector on
W^{k,p}
and algebra structure of
W^{k,p}
, there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that

\lVert u\rVert_{k,p}=\lVert\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega-\delta\mathbf{P}(\theta u)\rVert_{k,p}\leq C_{1}\lVert\omega\rVert_{k-1,p}+C_{1}\delta\lVert\theta\rVert_{k,p}\lVert u\rVert_{k,p}

Hence, in case that
C_{1}\delta\lVert\theta\rVert_{k,p}<1
, we have

\lVert u\rVert_{k,p}\leq\frac{C_{1}\lVert\omega\rVert_{k-1,p}}{1-C_{1}\delta\lVert\theta\rVert_{k,p}},\quad k=1,2

(20)

On the other hand, by classic Cauchy Lipschitz theory of transport equation, we have for
C_{0}=\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\nabla\theta_{0}\rVert_{\infty}
:

\lVert\nabla\times u\rVert_{\infty}=\lVert\Delta\psi\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}+\delta\lVert\nabla\times(\theta u)\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}+\delta C_{0}\lVert\nabla\times u\rVert_{\infty}+\delta\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\theta\rVert_{\infty}

\leq\frac{C_{0}}{1-C_{0}\delta}(\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}+\delta M\lVert u\rVert_{\infty})

As long as
\delta<C_{0}^{-1}
. Using Sobolev embedding
W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}
in
2
-d and
\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{2,p}=1
, we can choose
C_{0}
as the numerical constant in the above Sobolev embedding, which we still denote by
C_{0}
.

Meanwhile, the logarithmic Sobolev inequality on borderline space yields:

\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\times u\rVert_{\infty}[1+\log(2+\lVert\nabla\times u\rVert_{1,p})]\leq C_{2}\lVert\nabla\times u\rVert_{\infty}[1+\log(2+\lVert u\rVert_{2,p})]

\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1-C_{0}\delta}(\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}+\delta M\lVert u\rVert_{\infty})[1+\log(\lVert u\rVert_{2,p}+2)].

Hence, we have

\frac{\dot{M}}{M}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1-C_{0}\delta}(\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}+\delta M\lVert u\rVert_{\infty})(1+\log(2+\frac{\dot{N}}{N})).

On the other hand, by (
18
) we have:

\frac{\dot{N}}{N}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{1-\delta C_{1}N}\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}.

(21)

Plugging (
21
) back to (
19
), the desired result follows from choosing
C
and
T_{\delta}
as

C=\max\{C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}\},\quad T_{\delta}=\min\big\{t\geq 0:M(t)\geq\frac{3}{4C\delta},N(t)\geq\frac{3}{4C\delta}\big\},

which is legitimate as
M,N
are continuous increasing and
M(0)=N(0)=1
.
∎

We now state and prove the first theorem concerning lifespan of IIE in perturbative regime. We will make use of the controlling constant
C
appeared above.

Theorem 3.4
.

There exist positive constants
\delta_{0}
and
C_{1}^{*},C_{2}^{*},C_{3}^{*},C_{4}^{*}
, possibly depending on the data
\omega_{0}
, such that for any
\delta<\delta_{0}
, the energy norm
\mathcal{E}(t):=\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{2,p}
remains bounded on the interval
[0,T_{\delta}^{*}]
, where

T_{\delta}^{*}=C_{1}^{*}\log C_{2}^{*}\log C_{3}^{*}\log C_{4}^{*}\delta^{-1}.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

We divide the proof into several steps.

(I)

We start with necessary estimates to establish the system of differential inequality, like (
13
). Consider the Duhamel term:

\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau,X_{\tau})d\tau=\nabla^{\perp}\rho_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{*}X_{\tau}(\nabla^{*}uu)(X_{\tau})d\tau,

which admits the following estimates holding for generic
\delta
:

\bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau,X_{\tau})d\tau\bigg\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim\delta\int_{0}^{\infty}\lVert\nabla X_{\tau}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}d\tau+\delta\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla^{2}X_{\tau}\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}

+\delta\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\lVert\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}d\tau.

Hence, we have:

\bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau,X_{\tau})d\tau\circ A_{t}\bigg\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim M\delta(\int_{0}^{t}(\dot{M}+N\frac{\dot{M}}{M})\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{d}{dt}M^{2}\frac{\dot{N}}{N}d\tau).

Therefore, Duhamel’s formula yields the following estimate:

\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim 1+M+M\delta(\int_{0}^{t}(\dot{M}+N\frac{\dot{M}}{M})\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}+M\dot{M}\frac{\dot{N}}{N}d\tau),

and

\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim 1+M+\delta M(\int_{0}^{t}(\dot{M}+N\frac{\dot{M}}{M})\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}+M\dot{M}\frac{\dot{N}}{N}d\tau)+\delta N.

(22)

So, for any time
t\geq 0
and any
\delta>0
, we have the
\mathbf{unconditional}
estimate:

\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim 1+M+\delta MQ,\quad Q=\int_{0}^{t}(\dot{M}+N\frac{\dot{M}}{M})\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}+<\dot{M}\frac{\dot{N}}{N}d\tau.

(23)

By lemma
3.3
, we obtain that for
t\in[0,T_{\delta}]
and
\delta<\delta_{0}\leq(2C)^{-1}
:

\frac{\dot{N}}{N}\lesssim\frac{1}{1-\delta CN}\bigg(1+M+M\delta(\int_{0}^{t}(\dot{M}+N\frac{\dot{M}}{M})\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}+M\dot{M}\frac{\dot{N}}{N}d\tau)\bigg).

Moreover, taking
k=1
in (
20
), we have

\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim\frac{\lVert\omega\rVert_{p}}{1-C\delta\lVert\theta\rVert_{1,p}}\leq\frac{C\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}}{1-C\delta M}.

By noticing that the bounding constant in (
23
) is some
C^{\prime}=C(\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert_{1,p},\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{2,p})
, abusing the notation a little bit, we conclude that the following system of differential inequalities hold on
[0,T_{\delta}]
for some
C=C(\omega_{0},\theta_{0})
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\dot{M}}\leq CM\big(\frac{1}{1-C\delta}\cdot\frac{\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}}{1-C\delta M}\big)[1+\log(2+\frac{\dot{N}}{N})];\\
&\dot{N}\leq\frac{CN}{1-\delta CN}\big(1+M+\delta MQ\big);\\
&\dot{Q}=(\dot{M}+N\frac{\dot{M}}{M})\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}+M\dot{M}\frac{\dot{N}}{N},\quad Q(0)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(24)

Moreover, as long as
C\delta M(t)<1
, we have the following estimate for
\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}
:

\lVert\omega(t,\cdot)\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\nabla\rho_{0}\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla^{*}X\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}d\tau

\leq C+C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\dot{M}\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}}{1-C\delta M}d\tau.

(25)

(II)

Now we propose our bootstrap scheme. We say
\mathscr{H}(t)
is satisfied if

C\delta M\leq 1/2,\quad C\delta N\leq 1/2,\quad\delta Q\leq 1.

(26)

Now we verify the four conditions in Lemma
3.2
. Assuming that
\mathscr{H}(T_{*})
holds, then clearly
T_{*}\leq T_{\delta}
and the estimates (
18
) and (
19
) hold. Since
M,N,Q
are all increasing, we conclude that
\mathscr{H}(t)
holds for all
0\leq t\leq T_{*}
.

Using the fact that
M\geq 1
, system (
24
) then implies that on
[0,T_{*}]
,
(M,N,Q)
satisfies the following system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\dot{M}}\leq 4CM\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}(1+\log(2+\frac{\dot{N}}{N}));\\
&\dot{N}\leq 2CN\big(1+2M);\\
&\dot{Q}\leq 2{C\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}}(\dot{M}+N\dot{M}/M)+M\dot{M}\dot{N}/N.\end{aligned}\right.

(27)

Moreover, for
t\in[0,T_{*}]
, (
25
) implies that

\lVert\omega(t,\cdot)\rVert_{\infty}\leq C+2C\delta\int_{0}^{t}{\dot{M}\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}}d\tau.

By the integral version of Grönwall inequality, we have:

\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2C+4C^{2}\delta\int_{0}^{t}\dot{M}(\tau)\exp 2(C\delta(M(t)-M(\tau))d\tau\leq 2C\exp(2C\delta M(t))\leq 2C{e}.

Combining with (
27
), simple algebraic manipulation implies that for
t\in[0,T_{*}]
:

\frac{d}{dt}\log(1+M)\leq 4C(1+2C{e})[1+\log(2+4C)+\log(1+M)],

which yields the following bound:

\frac{d}{dt}\log(1+M)\leq C_{1}(1+\log(1+M)),\quad C_{1}:=4C(1+2C{e})[1+\log(2+4C)].

Grönwall inequality then yields:

M(t)\leq\exp(tC_{1}e^{C_{1}t})-1\leq\exp(\exp(2C_{1}t))-1,

and moreover:

N(t)\leq\exp\bigg(4C\exp(e^{2C_{1}t})\bigg)=\exp(C_{2}\exp(e^{2C_{1}t})),\quad C_{2}=4C.

Hence, thanks to the bound
\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2Ce
, we compute:

Q(t)\leq\int_{0}^{t}2{C\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}}(\dot{M}+N\dot{M}/M)+M\dot{M}\dot{N}/Nd\tau

\leq 4C^{2}e\exp(e^{2C_{1}t})+4C^{2}e\exp(C_{2}\exp(e^{2C_{1}t}))\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\dot{M}}{M}d\tau+4C\exp(2e^{2C_{1}t})\int_{0}^{t}\dot{M}d\tau

\leq C_{3}\exp(2C_{2}\exp(e^{2C_{1}t})),\quad C_{3}=8eC^{2}+4C.

We now introduce the following outcome
\mathscr{O}(t)
: We say
\mathscr{O}(t)
holds if

M(t)\leq\exp(e^{2C_{1}t})-1,\quad N(t)\leq\exp(C_{2}\exp(e^{2C_{1}t})),\quad Q(t)\leq C_{3}\exp(2C_{2}\exp(e^{2C_{1}t})).

Clearly, we have by our definition that
\mathscr{H}(t)
implies
\mathscr{O}(t)
. Since
M,N,Q
are all absolutely continuous with
M(0)=N(0)=1
,
Q(0)=0
, there exists sufficiently small
t
such that
\mathscr{H}(t)
holds. On the other hand, the closedness condition trivially holds for
\mathscr{O}(t)
.

(III)

We now close the bootstrap, which amounts to show (II) in lemma
3.2
. To this end, we choose

C_{1}^{*}=(2C_{1})^{-1},\quad C_{2}^{*}=1,\quad C_{3}^{*}=(2C_{2})^{-1},\quad C_{4}^{*}=\frac{9}{10C_{3}}.

Now, we assume
\mathscr{O}(T_{*})
holds and we observe for
T_{*}\in[0,T_{\delta}^{*}]
:

C\delta N\leq C\delta\sqrt{\frac{9}{10C_{3}\delta}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{9}{160eC}}<\frac{1}{4e}<1/2,\quad\delta Q\leq\frac{9}{10}<1.

Moreover, our assumption
\delta_{0}<(2C)^{-1}<(2e)^{-1}
guarantees that
-\delta\log\delta
is increasing, hence:

C\delta M(t)\leq C\delta(2C_{2})^{-1}\log\frac{9}{10C_{3}\delta}\leq\frac{9C}{20C_{2}C_{3}}\leq\frac{1}{8C_{3}}<1/2.

Finally, we fix

\delta_{0}=\frac{9\exp(-8Ce^{2})}{80(2eC^{2}+C)}.

It remains to check that
T_{\delta_{0}}^{*}>0
, which follows from

\log C_{2}^{*}\log C_{3}^{*}\log C_{4}^{*}\delta_{0}^{-1}=\log\frac{1}{2}\log[(8C)^{-1}\log 2+e^{2}]>\log\frac{1}{2}\log e^{2}=0.

Therefore, together with continuity of
M,N,Q
, we conclude
\mathscr{H}(t)
holds on a neighborhood of
T_{*}
. Thus the bootstrap loop is proven to be closed on
[0,T_{\delta}^{*}]
, and the energy norm (
22
) admits the following bound:

\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim 1+M+\delta MQ+\delta N\leq C+CM\leq C+CC_{3}\log\frac{C_{4}}{\delta}.

Hence, we conclude that lifespan of IIE admits a triple-log lower bound.

∎

3.3
MHD Case

We now move on to show the long-time existence of MHD equation in
2
-d, when the magnetic field
B
is near
0
. Here, we fix
p>2
and define the inhomogeneity measurement to be
\delta:=\lVert\rho_{0}-1\rVert_{3,p}
. But we will have to require one more degree of regularity of
\rho_{0}
compared to threshold regularity to guarantee the local wellposedness of
2
-d MHD, i.e.
\rho_{0}\in W^{4,p}
, which is crucial in our proof and matches the results obtained in
[
9
]
.

Consider the following vorticity formulation of MHD:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\{\rho,\Delta\rho\},\\
&\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\\
&u=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(28)

It is known that if we switch to the vorticity-current variable
(\omega,J)
,
J=\Delta\rho
, we can rewrite the above system as the following symmetric form:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\{\rho,J\},\\
&\partial_{t}J+u\cdot\nabla J=\{\rho,\omega\}+\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J),\\
&u=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega,\quad J=\Delta\rho.\end{aligned}\right.

(29)

Here,
\mathscr{Q}
is a bilinear
0
-th order operator:

\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)=2\mathsf{Tr}(\nabla\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega\cdot\nabla\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}J).

We now introduce the Elsässer variable, which diagonalizes the system: Let
\xi=\omega+J
,
\eta=\omega-J
. Then, the original equation becomes:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\xi+(u-\nabla^{\perp}\rho)\cdot\nabla\xi=\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J),\\
&\partial_{t}\eta+(u+\nabla^{\perp}\rho)\cdot\nabla\eta=-\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J).\end{aligned}\right.

(30)

Apply standard Calderon-Zygmund estimates to
\mathscr{Q}
yields the following:

\lVert\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)\rVert_{1,p}=2\lVert\mathsf{Tr}(\nabla\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega\cdot\nabla\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}J)\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lVert J\rVert_{1,p},

\lVert\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\lVert\omega\rVert_{2,p}\lVert J\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}\lVert J\rVert_{2,p}.

Change the variable into Lagrangian coordinates, we define

\tilde{\xi}:=\xi\circ X_{t},\quad\tilde{\eta}:=\eta\circ X_{t},

(31)

here
X
is the flow map of
u
, and we let
A
be the back to label map.

The Elsässer system has the advantage that it allows simple linear transport estimate to close in Sobolev spaces, with smoother driven velocity field and forcing term admitting the same level regularity as transported quantities. However, it buries the smallness of
\rho
,
B
and
J
in the close-to-Euler regime. Hence, we are forced to single out the magnetic field equation independently and handle the loss of derivative in vector transport. This is the key technical difficulty. The idea, similar to the treatment in
[
9
]
, is to go to higher norm
\lVert u\rVert_{3,p}+\lVert\rho\rVert_{4,p}
, use commutator estimate to get a nuanced control of growth of
B
based on the higher norm. Then, we can obtain a perturbed system of differential inequalities as Boussinesq and IIE to run a bootstrap argument.

We set up several notations here. Define:

M:=\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}d\tau\bigg),\quad Y:=\lVert\xi\rVert_{1,p}+\lVert\eta\rVert_{1,p},\quad Z:=\lVert\xi\rVert_{2,p}+\lVert\eta\rVert_{2,p}.

We also observe that for any
k\geq 1
, the following equivalence between norms hold:

\lVert\xi\rVert_{k,p}+\lVert\eta\rVert_{k,p}\approx\lVert\omega\rVert_{k,p}+\lVert J\rVert_{k,p}\approx\lVert u\rVert_{k+1,p}+\lVert B\rVert_{k+1,p}.

(32)

Hence, we have
\lVert u\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim Y
and

\lVert\xi_{0}\rVert_{k,p},\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{k,p}\lesssim\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert_{k,p}+\lVert J_{0}\rVert_{k,p}\lesssim\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert_{k,p}+\lVert\rho_{0}\rVert_{4,p}\quad\text{for }k=1,2.

Therefore, we may harmlessly represent
A\leq C(\lVert\xi_{0}\rVert_{2,p},\lVert\eta_{0}\rVert_{2,p})B
as
A\lesssim B
, provided
C
is increasing and
\lVert\rho_{0}\rVert_{4,p}
is assumed to be bounded by some given numerical constant.

We now carry out the following lemma, which provides several
a priori
estimates that we will need later.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
\rho_{0}\in W^{4,p}
satisfies the conditions
\lVert\rho_{0}-1\rVert_{3,p}\leq\delta<1
and
\lVert\rho_{0}\rVert_{4,p}\leq 100
, let
u_{0}\in W^{3,p}
, then the following estimates hold:

(I)

The magnetic field
B=\nabla^{\perp}\rho
satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}+\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{\infty}\lVert u\rVert_{3,p}.

(33)

As a consequence, we have

\lVert B(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\delta M+\delta M\int_{0}^{t}Z(\tau)d\tau

(34)

(II)

The Lagrangian variables
\tilde{\xi}
,
\tilde{\eta}
defined in (
31
) satisfies the following estimate:

\lVert\tilde{\xi}\rVert_{1,p},\lVert\tilde{\eta}\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim 1+\exp(Ct\delta)+\exp(2Ct\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lVert J\rVert_{1,p}d\tau.

(35)

As a consequence, we have:

Y\lesssim 1+M+\exp(Ct\delta)M+\delta M^{2}\exp(2Ct\delta)\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}Z(\tau)d\tau\bigg)d\tau.

(36)

(III)

We have the following higher norm estimate:

\lVert\tilde{\xi}\rVert_{2,p},\lVert\tilde{\eta}\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg)+\int_{0}^{t}\exp\bigg(C\int_{\tau}^{t}Y(r)dr\bigg)Y(\tau)Z(\tau)d\tau.

(37)

Which implies:

Z\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}Y(r)d\tau\bigg).

(38)

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

We first remark that the proof works as long as
\lVert\rho_{0}\rVert_{4,p}
is bounded, here we set a bound to remove the dependence of constants
C
in
\lVert\rho_{0}\rVert_{4,p}
. However, although
u_{0},B_{0}\in W^{3,p}
the smallness assumption is really at the level of local wellposedness regularity class of MHD.

(I) We estimate
\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}=\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{2,p}
. Straightforward calculation implies:

\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\lVert D^{j}\rho\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{D}\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{p}dx.

Therefore:

\frac{d}{dt}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}^{p}=p\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}^{p-1}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{D}\frac{d}{dt}(\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{2})^{p/2}dx

=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{D}p\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{p-2}D^{j}\rho D^{j}\partial_{t}\rho dx

=-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{D}p\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{p-2}D^{j}\rho D^{j}(u\cdot\nabla\rho)dx

=-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{D}p\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{p-2}D^{j}\rho[D^{j},u\cdot\nabla]\rho dx-\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{D}p\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{p-2}D^{j}\rho(u\cdot\nabla D^{j}\rho)dx}_{(*)}.

Notice that:

p\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{p-2}D^{j}\rho(u\cdot\nabla D^{j}\rho)=u\cdot\nabla(\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{p}),

and the last term
(*)
vanishes due to divergence-free property of
u
. Moreover, by Kato-Ponce commutator estimate [cf.
[
20
]
[
21
]
]:

\lVert[D^{j},u\cdot\nabla]\rho\rVert_{p}\lesssim\lVert D^{j}u\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla^{3}\rho\rVert_{p}.

(39)

Therefore, by Hölder inequality, we obtain

\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}^{p-1}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{D}\bigg\lvert\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{p-2}D^{j}\rho[D^{j},u\cdot\nabla]\rho\bigg\rvert dx

\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{3}\bigg(\int_{D}\lvert D^{j}\rho\rvert^{(p-1)\cdot q}dx\bigg)^{1/q}\lVert[D^{j},u\cdot\nabla]\rho\rVert_{p}

\lesssim\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}^{p/q}(\lVert u\rVert_{3,p}\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla^{3}\rho\rVert_{p})

where
q
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Hence, dividing both sides by
\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}^{p/q}
and using
p/q=p-1
, we conclude:

\frac{d}{dt}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}+\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{\infty}\lVert u\rVert_{3,p}.

Therefore, we established (
33
).

Now, rewrite (
33
) in terms of
M
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\frac{\dot{M}}{M}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}+\delta MZ,

where we use (
8
). Now, we define the new variable
\mathcal{B}:=M^{-1}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}
and rewrite the above inequality as follows:

\dot{\mathcal{B}}\lesssim\delta Z,\quad\mathcal{B}_{0}=M^{-1}(0)\lVert B_{0}\rVert_{2,p}\leq\delta.

Integrating the inequality, we conclude:

\mathcal{B}(t)\lesssim\lVert B_{0}\rVert_{2,p}+\delta\int_{0}^{t}Z(\tau)d\tau\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lVert B(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\delta M\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}Z(\tau)d\tau\bigg).

(II) We notice by Duhamel’s principle that
\tilde{\xi}
,
\tilde{\eta}
are respectively transported by the flows
X_{\mp}
, defined as follows:

\dot{X}_{\mp}=\mp B_{0}(X_{\mp}),\quad B_{0}=\nabla^{\perp}\rho_{0}.

Denote by
A_{\mp}
the corresponding back-to-label map, by chord-arc estimate we have:

\lVert\nabla A_{\mp}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla B_{0}\rVert_{\infty}d\tau\bigg)\leq\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla\rho_{0}\rVert_{2,p}d\tau\bigg)\leq\exp(Ct\delta).

Now, Duhamel’s principle yields:

\tilde{\xi}(t,x)=\bigg[\tilde{\xi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)(X_{-}(\tau,\cdot))d\tau\bigg]\circ A_{-}(t,x);

\tilde{\eta}(t,x)=\bigg[\tilde{\xi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)(X_{+}(\tau,\cdot))d\tau\bigg]\circ A_{+}(t,x).

Standard computation yields:

\lVert\tilde{\xi}\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{0}\rVert_{p}+\lVert\nabla A_{-}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{0}\rVert_{p}+\lVert\nabla A_{-}\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla X_{-}(\tau)\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\mathscr{Q}\rVert_{p}d\tau

\lesssim 1+\exp(Ct\delta)+\exp(2Ct\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lVert J\rVert_{1,p}d\tau.

Similar estimates yield the same bound:

\lVert\tilde{\eta}\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim 1+\exp(Ct\delta)+\exp(2Ct\delta)\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lVert J\rVert_{1,p}d\tau,

therefore (
35
) holds true.

By Sobolev estimates of rearrangements (
8
), we have

\lVert\xi\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim 1+\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\tilde{\xi}\rVert_{1,p},\quad\lVert\eta\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim 1+\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\tilde{\eta}\rVert_{1,p},

which, together with the estimate
\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim Y
and (
34
), demonstrates (
36
).

(III) We now track
\xi
,
\eta
along Elsässer characteristics: Define
\Phi_{\pm}(r,t)
as the two-parameter flow map associated with div-free vector field
u{\pm}\nabla^{\perp}\rho
:

\partial_{t}\Phi_{\pm}^{r,t}(a)=(u\pm\nabla^{\perp}\rho)(t,\Phi_{\pm}^{r,t}(a)),\quad\Phi_{\pm}^{r,r}(a)=a.

The following semigroup property holds:

\Phi_{\pm}^{r,s}\circ\Phi_{\pm}^{s,t}=\Phi_{\pm}^{r,t},\quad\forall 0\leq r\leq s\leq t.

We also need to make use of the spatial inverse of
\Phi_{\pm}^{r,t}
. Let us denote

\Psi_{\pm}^{t,s}=(\Phi_{\pm}^{s,t})^{-1}.

Hence we have

\Phi_{\pm}^{r,s}=\Phi_{\pm}^{r,t}\circ\Psi_{\pm}^{t,s}.

Then, we notice:

\xi_{t}=\xi_{0}\circ\Psi_{-}^{t,0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)\circ\Psi_{-}^{t,\tau}d\tau,\quad\eta:=\eta_{0}\circ\Psi_{+}^{t,0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)\circ\Psi_{+}^{t,\tau}d\tau.

(40)

Now, we use the Sobolev rearrangement estimate: For general function
f\in W^{2,p}
:

\lVert f\circ\Psi_{-}^{t,s}\rVert_{2,p}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{p}+\lVert\nabla\Psi_{-}^{t,s}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{p}+R_{2},

where
R_{2}
is the second-order differentiated term, reads:

R_{2}=\lVert\nabla(\nabla^{*}\Psi_{-}^{t,s}\nabla f\circ\Psi_{-}^{t,s})\rVert_{p}\lesssim\lVert\nabla^{2}\Psi_{-}^{t,s}\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\nabla\Psi_{-}^{t,s}\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\lVert\nabla^{2}f\rVert_{p}

\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\nabla(u-B)\rVert_{1,p}d\tau\bigg)\lVert f\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{s}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg)\lVert f\rVert_{2,p}.

Combining with the chord-arc bound (
9
):

\lVert\nabla\Psi_{-}^{t,s}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\exp\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\nabla(u-B)\rVert_{\infty}d\tau\bigg)\leq\exp\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\nabla(u-B)\rVert_{1,p}d\tau\bigg)\leq\exp\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg),

where we use the Sobolev embedding
L^{\infty}\hookrightarrow W^{1,p}
, we conclude:

\lVert f\circ\Psi_{-}^{t,s}\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{s}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg)\lVert f\rVert_{2,p}.

(41)

It is easy to see that the estimate of
\eta
follows the same line as
\xi
. Hence, it suffices to estimate
\lVert\xi\rVert_{2,p}
. We notice that

\lVert\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\lVert\omega\rVert_{2,p}\lVert J\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}\lVert J\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim YZ.

Then, using (
40
), we have

\lVert\xi(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2,p}\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg)+\int_{0}^{t}\exp\bigg(C\int_{\tau}^{t}Y(r)dr\bigg)Y(\tau)Z(\tau)d\tau.

Similar estimate also holds for
\eta
, following the same line of argument. Summing up the inequalities, by definition of
Z
, we obtain:

Z(t)\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg)+\int_{0}^{t}\exp\bigg(C\int_{\tau}^{t}Y(r)dr\bigg)Y(\tau)Z(\tau)d\tau.

(42)

Now we define

\mathcal{Z}(t):=\exp\bigg(-C\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg)Z(t).

Then the inequality (
42
) can be reformulated in terms of
\mathcal{Z}
:

\mathcal{Z}(t)\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)\mathcal{Z}(\tau)d\tau.

Thanks to the Grönwall inequality, we conclude:

\mathcal{Z}(t)\lesssim\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z(t)\lesssim\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg).

Therefore, the desired estimate (
38
) follows.

∎

Now, combining the extrapolation inequality (
2.3
) with the above estimates, we have:

\frac{\dot{M}}{M}=\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\infty}\leq(1+\log(2+\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}))\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}

\lesssim[1+\log(2+\lVert\xi\rVert_{1,p}+\lVert\eta\rVert_{1,p})](\lVert\xi\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\eta\rVert_{\infty})

\lesssim[1+\log(2+\lVert\xi\rVert_{1,p}+\lVert\eta\rVert_{1,p})](\lVert\xi\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\eta\rVert_{\infty}).

Using the transport structure of
\xi
and Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
, we have:

\lVert\xi\rVert_{\infty}=\lVert\tilde{\xi}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert\xi_{0}\rVert_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\mathscr{Q}(\omega,J)\rVert_{\infty}d\tau

\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lVert J\rVert_{1,p}d\tau.

(43)

And the same estimates hold for
\eta
following the same argument. Therefore, we conclude the following system of inequality holds for
M
,
Y
and
Z
for short time, with sufficiently large
C
depending on
\omega_{0}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{M}\leq CM(1+\log(1+Y))(1+Q),\\
&Y\leq C+CM+C\exp(Ct\delta)M+CM\exp(2Ct\delta)Q,\\
&Z\leq C\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg),\\
&\dot{Q}=\lVert u\rVert_{2,p}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}\leq C\delta MY\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}Z(\tau)d\tau\bigg),\quad Q(0)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(44)

Now we state our main theorem in this section.

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
\lVert\rho_{0}\lVert_{4,p}\leq 100
and
\lVert\rho_{0}-1\rVert_{3,p}<\delta
, then there exist constants
\delta_{0}
and
C_{1}^{\prime},C_{2}^{\prime},C_{3}^{\prime},C_{4}^{\prime}>0
depending on
\omega_{0}
, such that as long as
\delta<\delta_{0}
, the energy norm
Z
stays bounded on
[0,T_{\delta}]
. Where

T_{\delta}:=C_{1}^{\prime}\log C_{2}^{\prime}\log C_{3}^{\prime}\log C_{4}^{\prime}\delta^{-1}.

(45)

Proof of theorem
3.6
.

We use a similar bootstrap argument to show the desired result. Our bootstrap assumption
\mathscr{H}(t)
is said to be satisfied if

Q(t)\leq 1,\quad C\delta t\leq 1.

Since
Q(0)=0
and
\dot{Q}>0
, for any
\delta
fixed we can define

T_{*}=\sup\{t:Q(t)\leq 1,C\delta t\leq 1\}.

Then
\mathscr{H}(t)
is satisfied for all
t\in[0,T_{*}]
. Now on
[0,T_{*}]
, the system (
44
) of differential inequalities becomes:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{M}\leq 2CM(1+\log(1+Y)),\\
&Y\leq C+C(e^{2}+e+1)M,\\
&Z\leq C\exp\bigg(C\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)d\tau\bigg),\\
&\dot{Q}=\lVert u\rVert_{2,p}\lVert B\rVert_{2,p}\leq C\delta MY\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}Z(\tau)d\tau\bigg),\quad Q(0)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(46)

Define
\mathfrak{M}(t):=1+\log(1+M(t))
, following a similar argument as in the proof of Boussinesq and IIE case, we find that for
C_{1}:=6C^{2}
:

\dot{\mathfrak{M}}(t)\leq C_{1}(1+\mathfrak{M}(t))\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathfrak{M}(t)\leq 2e^{C_{1}t}-1.

As a consequence, we obtain

M(t)\leq\exp(2e^{C_{1}t})-1,\quad Y(t)\leq 2Ce^{2}\exp(2e^{C_{1}t}).

Now, double exponential growth of
Y
implies the triple exponential growth of
Z
. More precisely:

Z(t)\leq C\exp\bigg(2C^{2}e^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\exp(2e^{C_{1}r})dr\bigg)\leq C\exp(C_{2}\exp(2e^{C_{1}t})),\quad C_{2}:=\frac{C^{2}e^{2}}{C_{1}}=e^{2}/6.

Now, we define the bootstrap outcome
\mathscr{O}(t)
to be:

M(t)\leq\exp(2e^{C_{1}t}),\quad Y(t)\leq 2Ce^{2}\exp(2e^{C_{1}t}),\quad Z(t)\leq C\exp(C_{2}\exp(2e^{C_{1}t})).

We have already demonstrate that
\mathscr{H}(t)
implies
\mathscr{O}(t)
. It suffices to show the feedback loop closes for some chosen
\{C_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{4}
and corresponding
T_{\delta}
.

To this end, we let

C_{1}^{\prime}=C_{1}^{-1},\quad C_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{4},\quad C_{3}^{\prime}=C_{2}^{-1},\quad C_{4}^{\prime}=\frac{99}{100C_{4}}.

Assume now
\mathscr{O}(T_{*})
holds for some
T_{*}\leq T_{\delta_{0}}
, notice first that
Q
satisfies the following triple exponential estimate:

Q(t)\leq 2C^{2}e^{2}\delta\int_{0}^{t}\exp(4e^{C_{1}\tau})\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{\tau}C\exp(C_{2}\exp(2e^{C_{1}r}))dr\bigg)d\tau

\leq 2C^{2}e^{2}\delta\int_{0}^{t}\exp(4e^{C_{1}\tau})C_{3}\exp(C_{2}\exp(4e^{C_{1}\tau})))d\tau

\leq 2C^{2}\delta e^{2}C_{3}(4C_{1}C_{2})^{-1}\exp(C_{2}\exp(4e^{C_{1}t})):=C_{4}\delta\exp(C_{2}\exp(4e^{C_{1}t})).

Here:

C_{3}=\frac{e}{2e^{3}C_{1}C_{2}},\quad C_{4}=2C^{2}e^{2}C_{3}(4C_{1}C_{2})^{-1}=\frac{C^{2}}{4C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{2}}=\frac{1}{4C^{2}e^{2}}.

Now for all
0\leq t\leq T_{*}\leq T_{\delta_{0}}
, increasing property of the exponential yields:

Q(t)\leq Q(T_{\delta_{0}})=1,\quad C\delta_{0}t\leq CC_{1}^{-1}\delta_{0}\log\frac{1}{4}\log C_{2}^{-1}\log\frac{1}{C_{4}^{\prime}\delta_{0}}:=f(\delta_{0}).

It suffices to find
\delta_{0}
small enough such that
f^{\prime}(\delta)>0
for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
and
f(\delta_{0})\leq 1
, so the desired bound holds for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
and the proof is thus completed. To this end, we let

\alpha(\delta):=\log(C_{4}^{\prime}\delta^{-1}),\quad\beta(\delta):=\log(C_{2}^{-1}\alpha(\delta)),\quad\gamma(\delta):=\log(\beta(\delta)/4).

Then we have:

\alpha^{\prime}(\delta)=-\delta^{-1},\quad\beta^{\prime}(\delta)=-\frac{1}{\delta\alpha(\delta)},\quad\gamma^{\prime}(\delta)=-\frac{1}{\delta\alpha(\delta)\beta(\delta)},\quad f(\delta)=CC_{1}^{-1}\delta\gamma(\delta).

Hence, we obtain

f^{\prime}(\delta)=CC_{1}^{-1}\bigg(\gamma(\delta)-\frac{1}{\alpha(\delta)\beta(\delta)}\bigg).

So it suffices to let
\gamma\geq 2
and
\alpha\beta\geq 1
. Hence, we may choose
\delta_{0}=C_{4}^{\prime}\exp(-C_{2}e^{4e^{2}})
, which gives

\alpha(\delta_{0})=C_{2}e^{4e^{2}}\geq 1,\quad\beta(\delta_{0})=4e^{2}\geq 1,\quad\gamma(\delta_{0})=2.

Finally, we complete the proof by computing

f(\delta_{0})=CC_{1}^{-1}\delta_{0}\gamma(\delta_{0})=2CC_{1}^{-1}C_{4}^{\prime}\exp(-C_{2}e^{4e^{2}})=\frac{99}{1200C^{3}e^{2}}\exp(-C_{2}e^{4e^{2}})<1.

∎

4
A Quantitative Continuation Criteria of Inhomogeneous Euler

As suggested by (
27
), it is possible to control the energy norm using
\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}
only. Here, we present a quantitative continuation criteria of IIE based on the scalar quantity
\omega
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
2<p<\infty
, let
(\omega_{0},\rho_{0})\in W^{1,p}\times W^{2,p}
. Let
\delta
measuring inhomogeneity of the initial data be defined as:

\delta:=\lVert\rho_{0}-1\rVert_{W^{2,p}}.

Assume
(\omega,\rho)
is a solution of (
16
) on
[0,T_{*})
. Then, there exist constants
\delta_{0}
and
\{C_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{4}
such that if
\delta<\delta_{0}
, and the following condition holds:

\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\omega(t,\cdot)\rVert_{\infty}dt\leq C_{1}^{*}\log C_{2}^{*}\log C_{3}^{*}\log\frac{1}{C_{4}^{*}\delta}

(47)

then the solution can be continued past time
T
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We follow a similar bootstrapping argument. We first propose the same bootstrap assumption
\mathscr{H}
defined as in (
26
) and we have accordingly the differential inequality (
24
) holding for all
t\in[0,T_{*}]
, provided
\mathscr{H}(T_{*})
holds.

Hence, similar computation yields:

\frac{\dot{M}}{1+M}\leq C_{1}\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}+C_{1}\lVert\omega\rVert_{\infty}\log(1+M),\quad C_{1}=4C(1+\log(4C+2)).

As a matter of fact of Grönwall inequality, we have

M(t)\leq\exp\bigg\{\bigg[C_{1}\log 2\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\omega(\tau,\cdot)\rVert_{\infty}d\tau\bigg]\exp\bigg(C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\omega(\tau,\cdot)\rVert_{\infty}d\tau\bigg)\bigg\}-1.

Now we set

U(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\omega(\tau,\cdot)\rVert_{\infty}d\tau,

and we relax the
M
-bound to

M(t)\leq\exp[\exp(2C_{1}U(t))]-1,

N(t)\leq\exp[4C(1+M)]\leq\exp[C_{2}\exp e^{(2C_{1}U(t))}],\quad C_{2}=4C.

Moreover,
\mathscr{H}(T_{*})
implies
\lVert\omega(t,\cdot)\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2Ce
holds for all
t\in[0,T_{*}]
. Hence, we conclude the following upper bound for
Q
:

Q(t)\leq C_{3}\exp[2C_{2}\exp\exp(2C_{1}U(t))],\quad C_{3}=8C^{2}e+4C.

Now we define
\mathscr{O}(t)
as the following

M(t)\leq\exp[\exp(2C_{1}U(t))]-1,\quad N(t)\leq\exp[C_{2}\exp e^{(2C_{1}U(t))}].

and

Q(t)\leq C_{3}\exp[2C_{2}\exp\exp(2C_{1}U(t))].

We now choose our numerical constants. Fix

C_{1}^{*}=(2C_{1})^{-1},\quad C_{2}^{*}=1,\quad C_{3}^{*}=(2C_{2})^{-1},\quad C_{4}^{*}=\frac{9}{10C_{3}}.

Our quantitative assumption (
47
) guarantees that

U(t)\leq\frac{1}{2C_{1}}\log\log\frac{1}{2C_{2}}\log\frac{9}{10C_{3}\delta}.

Which implies the following:

\delta Q(t)\leq\frac{9}{10},\quad\delta CN(t)\leq C\delta\sqrt{\frac{9}{10C_{3}\delta}}\leq\frac{1}{4e}<\frac{1}{2},

and

C\delta M(t)\leq\frac{C\delta}{2C_{2}}\log\frac{9}{10C_{3}\delta}\leq\frac{1}{8C_{3}}<\frac{1}{2}.

It suffices to choose
\delta_{0}
small enough such that

g(\delta)=\frac{C\delta}{2C_{2}}\log\frac{9}{10C_{3}\delta}=-\frac{C\delta\log\delta}{2C_{2}}+\frac{C\delta}{2C_{2}}\log\frac{9}{10C_{3}}

is increasing on
(0,\delta_{0})
. It suffices to let

\delta_{0}<\frac{9}{10C_{3}e}.

Thus the feedback loop closes and the proof is completed, as we have the same estimate for
\mathcal{E}
as in Theorem
3.4
.
∎

5
Discussions of the Three-Dimensional Case

Our approach will unfortunately not work in
3
-dimensional case due to the vortex streching effect, we sketch the attempt here to see how the approach fails. In
3
-d, we have the following version of energy-vorticity formulation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega-\omega\cdot\nabla u=\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\times\nabla\rho,\\
&\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\\
&\omega=\nabla\times\partial_{u}E.\end{aligned}\right.

(48)

Similar Lagrangian-Duhamel formula yields:

\omega=(X_{t})_{\sharp}\bigg(\omega_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(A_{\tau})_{\sharp}(\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\times\nabla\rho)d\tau\bigg)

Therefore, we have the following Lagrangian estimates:

\lVert\omega\rVert_{p}\leq\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\bigg(\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert_{p}+C\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{p}d\tau\bigg)

\leq CM+CM\int_{0}^{t}M^{2}(\tau)\lVert\nabla\rho_{0}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{p}d\tau,

and

\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim\lVert\nabla^{2}X\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\lVert A\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert\nabla^{2}X\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{\infty}d\tau

+\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}(\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla^{2}A\rVert_{p}+\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty})d\tau

+\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla X\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\nabla^{2}\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{p}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{\infty}d\tau.

So taking into account the estimate of
\rho
, with perturbative initial data
\rho_{0}=1+\delta\theta_{0}
we have

\lVert\omega\rVert_{1,p}\lesssim MN+M^{2}+\delta MN\int_{0}^{t}M^{2}\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{\infty}d\tau+\delta M\int_{0}^{t}M(\lVert\nabla\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{\infty}MN+M^{2}\lVert\nabla^{2}\partial_{\rho}E\rVert_{p})d\tau.

In Boussinesq case we have:

\dot{N}\lesssim MN^{2}+M^{2}N+\delta M^{2}N\int_{0}^{t}M^{2}d\tau+\delta MN\int_{0}^{t}M^{2}Nd\tau,

which already exhibit a severe issue due to the quadratic term
N^{2}
. The logarithmic inequality is safe since powers in log become constant multipliers, which still guaranty
M
to grow at most double exponentially, but the presence of
N^{2}
in non-perturbative part of the above inequality destroys the triple exponential growth and bootstrap argument in
2
-d.

On the other hand, one should not expect any asymptotic global existence type result in
3
-d, since the global existence of incompressible Euler in dimension
3
already fails. But one may try to develop an asymptotic lifespan comparison result, phrased as follows: Assume
3
-d incompressible Euler equation with initial data
u(0,\cdot)=u_{E}
exists for time
T
, then study the lifespan
T_{\delta}
of various Euler-tracer coupled models with initial data
(u_{0},\rho_{0})=(u_{E},1+\delta\theta_{0})
for small
\delta
. We leave this question for future study.
