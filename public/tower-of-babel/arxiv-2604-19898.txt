Abstract
. For an integer n ⩾ 1 n\geqslant 1 , consider the n n -th metallic number ϕ n = n + n 2 + 4 2 \phi_{n}=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2} (e.g. ϕ 1 \phi_{1} is the golden number) and denote by [ ϕ n ] q [\phi_{n}]_{q} its q q -deformation in the sense of S. Morier-Genoud & V. Ovsienko. This is an algebraic continued fraction which admits an expansion into a power series [ ϕ n ] q = ∑ l = 0 + ∞ κ l ​ ( ϕ n ) ​ q l [\phi_{n}]_{q}=\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})q^{l} around q = 0 q=0 , with integral coefficients. By using techniques from analytic combinatorics, we establish several properties of the sequence ( κ l ​ ( ϕ n ) ) l ⩾ 0 (\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n}))_{l\geqslant 0} of Taylor coefficients: characterisation by recurrences or by differential equations, closed-form expressions when n = 1 , 2 , 3 n=1,2,3 , and asymptotics. We also present some remarkable identities induced by the action of the modular group PSL ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}) and address, mainly through computer experimentations, the question of the logarithmic behaviour of the sequence ( κ l ​ ( ϕ n ) ) l ⩾ 0 (\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n}))_{l\geqslant 0} . By doing so, we would be pleased to bring the interest of combinatoricians to the newly discovered world of q q -numbers.

2.1.
A short introduction to
q
-numbers

Our article is devoted to properties of
q
-deformation
of particular real numbers. Let us define precisely what kind of deformation is meant here and give a brief introduction to the subject.

Quantisation of integers is a very classical topic which goes back to Euler and Gauss: any integer
n\geqslant 0
can be deformed as a polynomial in one variable
q

(2.1)

[n]_{q}:=1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{n-1}

such that
[n]_{1}=n
. Equivalently, one can define
[n]_{q}
as the unique solution of the recurrence

(2.2)

[n+1]_{q}=q[n]_{q}+1,\qquad[0]_{q}=0.

Using (
2.2
) backwards, one can easily guess a similar formula for the
q
-deformation of negative integers
n<0
:

(2.3)

[n]_{q}:=-q^{-1}-q^{-2}-\cdots-q^{-n}

and this gives a polynomial in
q^{-1}
. Note that expressions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) can be unified under the form

[n]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

if
q
differs from
1
.
A considerable amount of related objects have been based on these definitions, e.g.
q
-factorials,
q
-binomials,
q
-hypergeometric functions,
q
-calculus, etc. used in combinatorics, number theory, fractals, operator theory, mathematical physics… But until recently we missed a really satisfactory extension to more general numbers (reals, or even just rationals).

Several equivalent models are available to define Morier-Genoud & Ovsienko’s
q
-real numbers
. One of them involves continued fractions and is probably the simplest to state.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Keeping notation given in the introduction, we consider its regular continued fraction expansion (finite if and only if
x\in\mathbb{Q}
)

x\;=\;a_{0}+\frac{1}{a_{1}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{1}{a_{2}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{1}{a_{3}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\mathord{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$\cdots$}}

where the

’s are integers, positive for
i\geqslant 1
. The
q
-deformation
or
q
-analogue
of

is the following algebraic continued fraction:

(2.4)

[x]_{q}:=[a_{0}]_{q}+\frac{q^{a_{0}}}{[a_{1}]_{q^{-1}}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{q^{-a_{1}}}{[a_{2}]_{q}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{q^{a_{2}}}{[a_{3}]_{q^{-1}}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{q^{-a_{3}}}{[a_{4}]_{q}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\mathord{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$\cdots$}}

where
[n]_{q}
stands for the
q

), and

is the same expression with reciprocal parameter.
When infinite, the right-hand side in (

of formal Laurent series in

-rationals
. If
\frac{r}{s}\in\mathbb{Q}
, its deformation
[\frac{r}{s}]_{q}
takes the form of a fractional function
\frac{\mathcal{R}(q)}{\mathcal{S}(q)}
, where the polynomials
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{S}
have positive integer coefficients and depend both on
r
and

[
MGO20
]
. A great success of the theory is that
q
-rationals enjoy several combinatorial models. For instance, they can be produced inductively from a weighted version of the Farey graph, exactly as rational numbers are produced from the usual Farey graph
[
MGO20
]
. Also, polynomials
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{S}
describe rank generating functions of certain partially ordered sets
[
MGO20
]
and enumerate perfect matchings inside weighted snake graphs
[
Ovs25
]
as well as points in Schubert cells in Grassmannians over a finite field
[
Ove23
]
.

On the other hand,
q
-irrationals are obtained as Laurent series by a limit process and, except in the case of the
q
-golden number
[\phi_{1}]_{q}
which is, as explained in the introduction, a signed version of secondary structures, giving them an enumerative interpretation is a challenging problem.

3)

Modular equivariance and other group symmetries
. We have, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle[x+1]_{q}

\displaystyle=q[x]_{q}+1,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{1}{x}\right]_{q}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{q[x]_{q}}.

These two crucial properties can be interpreted as commutation of the quantification map
[\,\cdot\,]_{q}
with the action on
\mathbb{Z}(\!(q)\!)\cup\{\infty\}
of a group of linear-fractional transformations which is isomorphic to the modular group
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

[
MGO22
,
LMG21
]
. Moreover,
[\,\cdot\,]_{q}
is the unique
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
-equivariant quantification which fixes
0
(
[
OP25
]
, §2.2). Such an equivariance is at the heart of the construction and plays a crucial role in the proof of numerous properties of these
q
-numbers. Also, it can be understood as equivariance with respect to the Burau representation of
the braid group
B_{3}

[
BBL23
,
MGOV24
]
.

Actually, the bigger group
PGL(2,\mathbb{Z})
also acts on
q
-numbers by symmetry and leads to the equivalent two formulas
[
Jou25b
]

(2.8)

[-x]_{q}=\frac{-[x]_{q}+1-q^{-1}}{(q-1)[x]_{q}+1},\qquad\left[\frac{1}{x}\right]_{q}=\frac{(q-1)[x]_{q}+1}{q[x]_{q}+1-q}.

4)

Specialization
. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
[x]_{q}\to x
when
q\to 1
,
0<q<1
(
[
Eti25
]
, Proposition 5.2). In particular, the quantification map is injective.

5)

Radius of convergence
. Let
x
be a positive real number. The Laurent expansion (
2.5
) of
[x]_{q}
is then a power series expansion, i.e.
l_{0}\geqslant 0
in (
2.5
), and
[x]_{q}
can be considered as an analytical function of the complex variable
q
in some open disk centered at
0
. Since it has integer coefficients, its radius of convergence
R(x)
is
\leqslant 1
if
x\notin\mathbb{Z}
(and
R(x)=+\infty
, obviously, if
x\in\mathbb{Z}
), and it is conjectured that
R(x)\geqslant\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\simeq 0,38
. In fact, this conjectural minimum is exactly the radius of convergence of the
q
-golden number
[\phi_{1}]_{q}=\bigl[\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\bigr]_{q}
, see
[
LMGOV24
]
. The conjecture has been verified for all
q
-metallic numbers
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
(see
[
Ren22
,
Ren23
]
) and for a large class of
q
-deformed positive quadratic irrationals (see
[
Lec24
]
, §4.4).
At present, the most general known result is that

for all
x>0
; even better,
q\mapsto[x]_{q}
defines a holomorphic function in some region containing
D(0,2-\sqrt{3})\cup(\frac{\sqrt{5}-3}{2},1)
: see
[
Eti25
]
, where are given many interesting analytical properties of

-numbers.

6)

q
-deformations of quadratic irrationals

[
LMG21
]
. When

is a quadratic irrational number (

integers,

),

is itself of the form

[x]_{q}=\frac{R(q)\pm\sqrt{P(q)}}{S(q)}

with polynomials

and

of a quadratic irrational number

[

]
§VII).

2.2.

2)

One other amazing property of

-metallic numbers concerns their

. Fix

\Delta_{j}^{(s)}([\phi_{n}]_{q}):=\det(\kappa_{a+b+s}(\phi_{n}))_{a,b=0}^{j-1},\quad s,j\geqslant 0.

3.
Recurrences and differential equations

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer. Our work starts with recurrences that hold for the sequence
(\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n}))
of power series coefficients of the
q
-metallic number
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
, see (
2.16
). For short, we will set

\kappa_{l}:=\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n}),\quad l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0},

throughout this section, since
n
will be fixed.

First of all, the quadratic functional equation (
2.10
) easily imply a recursive way of computing coefficients
\kappa_{l}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Coefficients
\kappa_{l}=\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})
of the power series representing
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
are determined by the following formulas:

(i)

if
n=1
:

\begin{cases}\kappa_{0}=1,\quad\kappa_{1}=0,\\
\kappa_{l}=\kappa_{l-1}+\kappa_{l-2}-\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{l-1-i}&for all $l\geqslant 2$.\end{cases}

(ii)

if
n=2
:

\begin{cases}\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{1}=1,\quad\kappa_{2}=0,\\
\kappa_{l}=2\kappa_{l-1}+\kappa_{l-3}-\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{l-1-i}&for all $l\geqslant 3$.\end{cases}

(iii)

if
n\geqslant 3
:

\begin{cases}\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{n-1}=1,\quad\kappa_{n}=0,\\
\kappa_{l}=2\kappa_{l-1}+\kappa_{l-2}+\cdots+\kappa_{l-n+1}+\kappa_{l-n-1}-\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{l-1-i}&for all $l\geqslant n+1$.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let us give the proof in the generic case
n\geqslant 3
(others are simpler). In this situation, the polynomial
R_{n}
in (
2.11
) can be written as

R_{n}(q)=-1+2q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{n-1}+q^{n+1},

hence the functional equation (
2.10
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{l\geqslant 1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{l-1-i}\right)q^{l}

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\kappa_{l}q^{l}+2\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\kappa_{l}q^{l+1}+\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\kappa_{l}q^{l+2}+\cdots

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\kappa_{l}q^{l+n-1}+\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\kappa_{l}q^{l+n+1}.

By identifying coefficients of the power series we get the recurrence formula in (iii). Initial conditions are provided by (
2.17
).
∎

In the previous result, because of the convolutive nature of the recursion, the calculation of a coefficient
\kappa_{l}
needs to know the values of all previous coefficients
\kappa_{i}
for
0\leqslant i\leqslant l-1
, which is awkward. We now show that these coefficients satisfy a much shorter recurrence.

Indeed, all
q
-metallic numbers
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
are algebraic power series, and this implies that the sequences
(\kappa_{l})=(\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n}))
of their Taylor coefficients at
q=0
are
P
-recursive
, which means that these sequences satisfy a homogeneous linear recurrence of finite degree with polynomial coefficients (see e.g.
[
Sta24
]
, Section 6.4). We are able to make these recurrences completely explicit:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n
be any positive integer. The sequence
(\kappa_{l})=(\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n}))
is
P
-recursive of order
2n+2
. More precisely, it satisfies the following recurrence relation:

(i)

if
n=1
, then for all
l\geqslant 4
,

(l+1)\kappa_{l}+(2l-1)\kappa_{l-1}-(l-2)\kappa_{l-2}+(2l-7)\kappa_{l-3}+(l-5)\kappa_{l-4}=0.

(ii)

if
n=2
, then for all
l\geqslant 6
,

(l+1)\kappa_{l}+4(l-2)\kappa_{l-2}-(2l-7)\kappa_{l-3}+4(l-5)\kappa_{l-4}+(l-8)\kappa_{l-6}=0.

(iii)

if
n\geqslant 3
, then for all
l\geqslant 2n+2
,

\displaystyle 2(l+1)\kappa_{l}+4(l-2)\kappa_{l-2}+\sum_{j=3}^{n-1}(j-1)(2l-3j+2)\kappa_{l-j}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(n+1)(2l-3n+2)\kappa_{l-n}+(n-4)(2l-3n-1)\kappa_{l-n-1}+(n+1)(2l-3n-4)\kappa_{l-n-2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=n+3}^{2n-1}(2n-j+1)(2l-3j+2)\kappa_{l-j}+4(l-3n+1)\kappa_{l-2n}+2(l-3n-2)\kappa_{l-2n-2}=0.

(When
n=3
, in last formula the two sum symbols must be replaced by zero.)

Again, we only prove the generic case

2q\,\Phi_{n}(q)-R_{n}(q)=\sqrt{P_{n}(q)}

where

is a polynomial of degree

and

the palindromic polynomial given by

P_{n}(q)=(1-q+q^{2})\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}jq^{j}+(n+2)q^{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)q^{n+j}+q^{2n}\right).

We expand this expression and get

\begin{split}P_{n}(q)&=1+2q^{2}+\sum_{j=3}^{n-1}(j-1)q^{j}+(n+1)q^{n}+(n-4)q^{n+1}+(n+1)q^{n+2}\\
&\hskip 50.00008pt+\sum_{j=n+3}^{2n-1}(2n-j+1)q^{j}+2q^{2n}+q^{2n+2}.\end{split}

This formula holds for any

, with convention

Now, we follow the method explained in Example 6.4.7 of

If we expand functions

(for clarity, we omit indication of the dependance of

\sum_{j=0}^{2n+2}(4n+4+2k-3j)\,p_{j}\,f_{k+2n+2-j}=0.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+1)(n+4+2k)f_{n+2+k}+(n-4)(n+1+2k)f_{n+1+k}+(n+1)(n-2+2k)f_{n+k}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=n+3}^{2n-1}(2n-j+1)(4n+4+2k-3j)f_{2n+2+k-j}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle+(n+1)(2l-3n+2)f_{l-n+1}+(n-4)(2l-3n-1)f_{l-n}+(n+1)(2l-3n-4)f_{l-n-1}

Proposition 3.4

Let

1
; namely, it is characterised by the differential equation:

4qP_{n}\Phi_{n}^{\prime}+(4P_{n}-2qP^{\prime}_{n})\Phi_{n}+R_{n}P^{\prime}_{n}-2P_{n}R^{\prime}_{n}=0,\qquad\Phi_{n}(0)=1,

where polynomials
P_{n}
and
R_{n}
were defined in (
2.11
) and (
2.13
). Equivalently, coefficients of this differential equation can be expressed only with the polynomial
R_{n}
:

q(R_{n}^{2}+4q)\Phi^{\prime}_{n}+(R_{n}^{2}-qR_{n}R^{\prime}_{n}+2q)\Phi_{n}+R_{n}-2qR^{\prime}_{n}=0.

Proof.

By (
2.12
) one has

\sqrt{P_{n}}=2q\,\Phi_{n}-R_{n}.

Hence,

2\Phi_{n}+2q\,\Phi^{\prime}_{n}-R^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{P^{\prime}_{n}}{2\sqrt{P_{n}}}=\frac{P^{\prime}_{n}(2q\,\Phi_{n}-R_{n})}{2P_{n}}

and this yields the first formula. The second one follows from the fact that
P_{n}=R_{n}^{2}+4q
.
∎

Example 3.5
.

The generating function
\Phi_{1}
of the
q
-golden ratio satisfies the following differential equation:

q(1-q+q^{2})(1+3q+q^{2})\Phi^{\prime}_{1}+(1+q)(1-q^{3})\Phi_{1}=1+q+3q^{2}.

Remark 3.6
.

It is clear that the results of this section can be borrowed similarly for the
q
-deformation of any quadratic irrational, provided that the (quadratic) generating function is explicitly known.

4.
Closed formulas for the coefficients

In
3.2
we gave ‘short’ recurrences that can be used to compute the Taylor coefficients
\kappa_{l}=\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})
of
q
-metallic numbers.
In this section we do better, but only for
n=1,2,3
; that is, we produce closed-formed expressions for the coefficients of the
q
-deformed gold, silver and bronze ratios. As we shall see the situation seems too complicated to hope for a usable formula in general.

In the simplest case
n=1
, an explicit expression for the coefficients follows immediately from (
1.7
) and (
1.10
):

Proposition 4.1
.

Coefficients of the power series of the
q
-golden number
[\phi_{1}]_{q}=\Bigl[\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\Bigr]_{q}
admit the following explicit expression:

\kappa_{0}(\phi_{1})=1,\quad\kappa_{1}(\phi_{1})=0,

and, for
l\geqslant 2
,

(4.1)

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{1})=(-1)^{l}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor}\frac{1}{l-k}\binom{l-k}{k}\binom{l-k}{k-1}=(-1)^{l}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor}N(l-k,k).

Here,
N(j,k)
denotes the Narayana number defined in (
1.11
).

Before dealing with case
n=2
, let us introduce some notation. We begin with multinomial coefficients: if
j
is a nonnegative integer, and if
j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{m}
are nonnegative integers such that
j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{m}=j
then we set

\binom{j}{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{m}}=\frac{j!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\cdots j_{m}!}.

It is often useful to understand a multinomial coefficient as a product of binomial coefficients:

\binom{j}{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{m}}=\binom{j}{j_{1}}\binom{j-j_{1}}{j_{2}}\binom{j-j_{1}-j_{2}}{j_{3}}\cdots\binom{j-j_{1}-j_{2}-\cdots-j_{m-1}}{j_{m}}.

We will also handle
ordinary Bell polynomials
, equally called
De Moivre polynomials
in
[
O’S22
]
. These are defined as follows: let

f(q)=\sum_{l\geqslant 1}f_{l}q^{l}

be a formal power series without constant term and with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
. For a nonnegative integer
k
, one defines the sequence of complex numbers
(A_{l,k}(f))_{l\geqslant 1}
by the expansion of the
k
-th power of
f(q)
:

f(q)^{k}=\sum_{l\geqslant 1}A_{l,k}(f)q^{l}.

If
l<k
, then
A_{l,k}(f)=0
, else
A_{l,k}(f)
has an expression involving multinomial coefficients

(4.2)

A_{l,k}(f)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{m}\geqslant 0\\
j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{m}=k\\
1j_{1}+2j_{2}+\cdots+mj_{m}=l\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{m}}f_{1}^{j_{1}}f_{2}^{j_{2}}\cdots f_{m}^{j_{m}}

which actually shows that
A_{l,k}(f)
can be viewed as a polynomial in, at most, the coefficients
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m}
with
m\leqslant l-k+1
.

Now, we go back to our subject. We shall first prove the following result:

Theorem 4.2
.

Coefficients of the
q
-silver ratio
[\phi_{2}]_{q}=[1+\sqrt{2}]_{q}
admit the following explicit expression:

\kappa_{0}(\phi_{2})=\kappa_{1}(\phi_{2})=1,\quad\kappa_{2}(\phi_{2})=\kappa_{3}(\phi_{2})=0,

and, for
l\geqslant 4
,

(4.3)

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})=\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{l-1}{3}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{l-2}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j-1}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{j+l-1}}{j}2^{3j-k-l+1}\binom{j}{k,k+1,l-2-2j-k,3j-k-l+1}.

Here, and thereafter,
\lfloor x\rfloor
(resp.
\lceil x\rceil
) denotes the greatest integer
\leqslant x
(resp. the least integer
\geqslant x
).

Remark 4.3
.

Alternatively,
\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})
can be written in terms of binomial coefficients:

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})=\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{l-1}{3}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{l-2}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j-1}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{j+l-1}}{j}\,2^{3j-k-l+1}\binom{j}{j-k}\binom{j-k}{j-2k-1}\binom{j-2k-1}{3j-k-l+1}

and this makes easier the comparison with expression (
4.1
) of the
q
-golden number coefficients. However, in the latter, no multinomial coefficient appears, and as concerns (
4.3
) a similarity is maybe to be found with
Motzkin numbers:

M_{l}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}\frac{1}{k+1}\binom{l}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}\frac{1}{l+1}\binom{l+1}{k,k+1,l-2k}.

Also, we notice that, unlike for the
q
-golden ratio, coefficients
\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})
are not alternating with the parity of
l
, and that their absolute value is not an increasing sequence.

Proof.

The first four coefficients are easily obtained from the expression (
2.20
) of the function
\Phi_{2}
.
From now on, we suppose that
l\geqslant 4
. We proceed as in the articles
[
BR23
]
and
[
BFR24
]
which deal with the case of the generating function
A
of RNA secondary structures.

Define the function

F(q)=\frac{\Phi_{2}(q)-1-q}{q^{3}}

which, from the point of view of series (
2.21
), represents a shifted variant of the generating function of the
q
-Silver number. This trick helps us to get a functional equation of the form required by Lagrange inversion formula (see e.g. §A.6 in
[
FS09
]
): indeed, using (
2.19
) we see that
F
satisfies equation

F(q)=q\,\phi(F),

where

\phi(u)=1+q(q-2)u-q^{3}u^{2}.

Since
\phi(0)=1
,
we are in the adequate situation to apply Lagrange inversion formula, except that
\phi
should
not
depend on the variable
q
. To get round this problem we introduce a new parameter
t
(to be replaced by
q
later) and define the function

\phi_{t}(u)=1+t(t-2)u-t^{3}u^{2},

whose coefficients are independant of
q
. Then we consider the function
F_{t}(q)
satisfying the corresponding functional equation

F_{t}(q)=q\,\phi_{t}(F_{t}(q)).

Since
F_{q}(q)=F(q)
, we will have, for all
l\geqslant 4
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})

\displaystyle=[q^{l}]\Phi_{2}(q)

\displaystyle=[q^{l-3}]F(q)

\displaystyle=[q^{l-3}]\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\left\{[q^{j}]F_{t}(q)\right\}_{t=q}\cdot q^{j}

\displaystyle=[q^{l-3}]\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\left\{[q^{j}]F_{t}(q)\right\}_{t=q}\cdot q^{j}.

Last equality is due to
F(0)=0
. (We use the standard notation
[x^{l}]T(x)
for the coefficient of
x^{l}
in the power series expansion of
T
about
x=0
.)
Now, Lagrange inversion formula can be applied to
F_{t}(q)
and yields:

(4.4)

[q^{j}]F_{t}(q)=\frac{1}{j}[u^{j-1}]\left((1+t(t-2)u-t^{3}u^{2})^{j}\right).

To compute the right-hand side of (
4.4
) one can use the multinomial theorem, but we found more convenient and a bit shorter to use ordinary Bell polynomials. Indeed, thanks to formula (3.2) in
[
O’S22
]
, we have:

[q^{j}]F_{t}(q)=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\binom{j}{k}A_{j-1,k}(\phi_{t}-1),

where
A_{j-1,k}
was defined in (
4.2
). Thus,

\displaystyle A_{j-1,k}(\phi_{t}-1)

\displaystyle=A_{j-1,k}(t(t-2),-t^{3},0,0\ldots)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0\\
\alpha+2\beta=j-1\\
\alpha+\beta=k\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{\alpha,\beta}\left(t(t-2)\right)^{\alpha}(-t^{3})^{\beta}

and there is only one term in this sum, for

\alpha=2k-j+1\quad\text{and}\quad\beta=j-1-k,

which reads

\displaystyle A_{j-1,k}(\phi_{t}-1)

\displaystyle=\binom{k}{j-1-k}\left(t(t-2)\right)^{2k-j+1}(-t^{3})^{j-1-k}

\displaystyle=\binom{k}{j-1-k}\sum_{l=0}^{2k-j+1}(-1)^{k-l}\binom{2k-j+1}{l}2^{2k-j+1-l}t^{2j-k-2+l}.

Summing up calculations, we have obtained

\displaystyle\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})

\displaystyle=[q^{l-3}]\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{j}\binom{j}{k}\binom{k}{j-1-k}\sum_{l=0}^{2k-j+1}(-1)^{k-l}\binom{2k-j+1}{l}2^{2k-j+1-l}q^{3j-k-2+l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}(-1)^{k-i}\frac{1}{j}\binom{j}{k}\binom{k}{j-1-k}\binom{2k-j+1}{i}2^{2k-j+1-i},

where, in the last sum, parameters
i,j,k
are subject to the following conditions and relations:

(i)

j\geqslant 1
;

(ii)

0\leqslant k\leqslant j-1
;

(iii)

j-1-k\leqslant k
, hence
k\geqslant{\frac{j-1}{2}}
;

(iv)

0\leqslant i\leqslant 2k-j+1
;

(v)

3j-k-2+i=l-3
, i.e.
3j=l-1+k-i
or
i=l-1-3j+k
.

From (ii), (iii) and (iv) we infer
k-i\leqslant j-1
and
k-i\geqslant k-(2k-j+1)\geqslant 0
, so that we get, by (v)

l-1\leqslant 3j\leqslant l+j-2.

Thus,

\displaystyle\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{l-1}{3}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{l-2}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{k=\lceil\frac{j-1}{2}\rceil}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{3j+1-l}}{j}\binom{j}{k}\binom{k}{j-1-k}\binom{2k-j+1}{l-1-3j+k}2^{2j+k+2-l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{l-1}{3}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{l-2}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{k=\lceil\frac{j-1}{2}\rceil}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+l-1}}{j}\frac{j!\,2^{2j+k+2-l}}{(j-k)!(j-k-1)!(l-1-3j+k)!(2j+k-l+2)!}

By changing variable
k\leftarrow j-1-k
we finally get (
4.3
).
∎

Lastly, we skip to the case of the
q
-bronze number and state:

Theorem 4.4
.

Coefficients of the
q
-bronze ratio
[\phi_{3}]_{q}=\left[\frac{3+\sqrt{13}}{2}\right]_{q}
admit the following explicit expression:

\kappa_{0}(\phi_{3})=\kappa_{1}(\phi_{3})=\kappa_{2}(\phi_{3})=1,\quad\kappa_{3}(\phi_{3})=\kappa_{4}(\phi_{3})=0,

and, for
l\geqslant 5
,

(4.5)

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{3})=\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{l-2}{4}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{l-4}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j-1}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{i=0}^{4j-k-l+2}\frac{(-1)^{l+i}}{j}\,2^{4j-k-2i-l+2}\\
\times\binom{j}{k,k+1,i,l-3-3j-k+i,4j-k-2i-l+2}.

Note that
4.3
also applies to coefficients
\kappa_{l}(\phi_{3})
obtained here.

Proof.

We mimic the proof of
4.2
and thus let ourselves off giving as much detail. The first five coefficients are obtained by expanding (
2.23
). Now, suppose that
l\geqslant 5
and consider the shifted generating function

F(q)=\frac{\Phi_{3}(q)-1-q-q^{2}}{q^{5}}.

Because of (
2.22
),
F
satisfies the functional equation

F(q)=q\,\phi(F),

where

\phi(u)=1+q(q^{2}-2q-1)u-q^{5}u^{2}.

Therefore we introduce the auxiliary function

\phi_{t}(u)=1+t(t^{2}-2t-1)u-t^{5}u^{2}

so that
F(q)=F_{q}(q)
if
F_{t}(q)
denotes the solution of the functional equation
F_{t}(q)=q\,\phi_{t}(F_{t}(q))
. We will then have

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{3})=[q^{l-5}]\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\left\{[q^{j}]F_{t}(q)\right\}_{t=q}\cdot q^{j}.

By Lagrange inversion and formula (3.2) in
[
O’S22
]
, we obtain, exactly as in the proof of
4.2
:

(4.6)

\displaystyle[q^{j}]F_{t}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}[u^{j-1}]\left((1+t(t^{2}-2t-1)u-t^{5}u^{2})^{j}\right)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\binom{j}{k}A_{j-1,k}(\phi_{t}-1)

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\binom{j}{k}\binom{k}{j-1-k}\left(t(t^{2}-2t-1)\right)^{2k-j+1}(-t^{5})^{j-1-k}.

But

(t^{2}-2t-1)^{2k-j+1}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0\\
\alpha+\beta\leqslant 2k-j+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j+1+\alpha}\,2^{\beta}\binom{2k-j+1}{\alpha,\beta,2k-j+1-\alpha-\beta}t^{2\alpha+\beta}

hence

\displaystyle\kappa_{l}(\phi_{3})

\displaystyle=[q^{l-5}]\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\binom{j}{k}\binom{k}{j-1-k}q^{2k-j+1}(-q^{5})^{j-1-k}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0\\
\alpha+\beta\leqslant 2k-j+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j+1+\alpha}\,2^{\beta}\binom{2k-j+1}{\alpha,\beta,2k-j+1-\alpha-\beta}q^{2\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k,\alpha,\beta}\frac{1}{j}\binom{j}{k}\binom{k}{j-1-k}(-1)^{k+\alpha}\,2^{\beta}\binom{2k-j+1}{\alpha,\beta,2k-j+1-\alpha-\beta},

where, in the last sum, parameters are subject to the following conditions and relations:

(i)

j\geqslant 1
;

(ii)

0\leqslant k\leqslant j-1
;

(iii)

j-1-k\leqslant k
, hence
k\geqslant{\frac{j-1}{2}}
;

(iv)

\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0
;

(v)

\alpha+\beta\leqslant 2k-j+1
;

(vi)

5(j-1-k)+2k-j+1+2\alpha+\beta=l-5
, i.e.
5j=l-1+3k-(2\alpha+\beta)
or
\beta=l-1-5j+3k-2\alpha
.

Conditions (iv) and (v) imply
0\leqslant 2\alpha+\beta\leqslant 4k-2j+2
, so that (vi) gives

l-2-j\leqslant 5j\leqslant l-4+3j

and then
\frac{l-2}{4}\leqslant j\leqslant\frac{l-4}{2}
. Also, (v) and (vi) imply

l-1-5j+3k-\alpha\leqslant 2k-j+1

i.e.
\alpha\geqslant l-2-4j+k
. Finally, we end up with

\displaystyle\kappa_{l}(\phi_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{l-2}{4}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{l-4}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{k=\lceil\frac{j-1}{2}\rceil}^{j-1}\sum_{\alpha=l-2-4j+k}^{2k-j+1}\frac{(-1)^{k+\alpha}}{j}\,2^{l-1-5j+3k-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\binom{j}{k}\binom{k}{j-1-k}\binom{2k-j+1}{\alpha,l-1-5j+3k-2\alpha,4j-k+\alpha-l+2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{l-2}{4}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{l-4}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{k=\lceil\frac{j-1}{2}\rceil}^{j-1}\sum_{\alpha=l-2-4j+k}^{2k-j+1}\frac{(-1)^{k+\alpha}}{j}\,2^{l-1-5j+3k-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{j!}{(j-k)!(j-k-1)!\alpha!(l-1-5j+3k-2\alpha)!(4j-k+\alpha-l+2)!}

Setting
i=\alpha-(l-2-4j+k)
and then letting
k\leftarrow j-1-k
gives (
4.5
).
∎

.

We have checked with a computer program that expressions given in

and

match coefficients of the power series given in (

) and (

.

5.
Asymptotics

Let
n
be a positive integer. Because explicit expressions for the coefficients
\kappa_{l}=\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})
of the power series (
2.16
) representing the
q
-metallic numbers
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
are missing, or rather complicated when they exist (see
Section

4
), it is worth looking at their asymptotics. Although these will be also complicated, we are able to give the general behaviour of the coefficients, as well as more precise formulas for
n=1,2,3
.

The link between asymptotics of a sequence of numbers and singularities of its generating function is well-known.
Darboux-Pólya’s method
(see e.g.
[
Odl96
]
§11.2) works quite well in our situation but we prefer to apply here the
principles of singularity analysis
developed by Ph. Flajolet and A.M. Odlyzko in the paper
[
FO90
]
and nicely presented in the book
[
FS09
]
, which will serve us as a guide for this section.

In any case, what is needed for asymptotics is to determine the
dominant singularities
of the function
\Phi_{n}(q)
, i.e. singularities which are lying on the boundary of the disk of convergence.
Recall from
Section

2.2
that

(5.1)

\Phi_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{2q}\left(R_{n}(q)+\sqrt{(1-q+q^{2})Q_{n}(q)}\right)

where
R_{n}
and
Q_{n}
are the polynomials given by

R_{n}(q)=q[n]_{q}+(q^{n}+1)(q-1)

and

Q_{n}(q)=\begin{cases}1+3q+q^{2}&if $n=1$,\\
1+q+4q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}&if $n=2$,\\
1+q+2q^{2}+\cdots(n-1)q^{n-1}+(n+2)q^{n}\\
\quad+(n-1)q^{n+1}+(n-2)q^{n+2}+\cdots 2q^{n-2}+q^{2n-1}+q^{2n}&if $n\geqslant 3$.\end{cases}

It follows that
\Phi_{n}(q)
is analytic in some open disc
D(0,\rho_{n})
and has at least one algebraic singularity on the circle
|q|=\rho_{n}
, due to the square root factor. We know that the roots
\frac{1\pm i\sqrt{3}}{2}
of
1-q+q^{2}
lie on the unit cercle, but it seems very difficult to determine the roots of
Q_{n}(q)
, except for small values of
n
(see below). However,
Q_{n}
is palindromic, and any root
\lambda
comes with another (possibly equal) root
1/\lambda
, so that it must have at least one root of modulus
\leqslant 1
. In other words,
\rho_{n}\leqslant 1
, a fact which follows also from the integrality of the power series coefficients of
\Phi_{n}
. On the other hand,
\rho_{n}>0
since
0
is not a root of
Q_{n}
, but of course, we already know a much better lower bound, see (
2.18
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Dominant singularities and radii of convergence of
\Phi_{n}(q)
for
n=1,2,3
.

1)

Case of the
q
-golden ratio:

(5.2)

q_{1}=\frac{-3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\simeq-0,381966

is the only dominant singularity of
\Phi_{1}(q)
and the radius of convergence is

\rho_{1}=|q_{1}|=\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\simeq 0,381966.

2)

Case of the
q
-silver ratio: let

(5.3)

q_{2}=\frac{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7}-1}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\sqrt{8\sqrt{2}-11}-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{8\sqrt{2}+11}\right)\simeq-0.109976-0.519497\,\mathrm{i}.

Then
q_{2}
and its complex conjugate
\overline{q_{2}}
are the two dominant singularities of
\Phi_{2}(q)
and the radius of convergence is

\rho_{2}=|q_{2}|=\frac{1+\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{2\sqrt{2}-1}}{2}\simeq 0.53101.

3)

Case of the
q
-bronze ratio: let

z=\frac{1}{3}\left(-1+\frac{2^{2/3}(1-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{3})}{3(23-3\sqrt{57})^{1/3}}+\frac{(23-3\sqrt{57})^{1/3}(1+\mathrm{i}\sqrt{3})}{3\cdot 2^{2/3}}\right)

and

(5.4)

q_{3}=\frac{z-\sqrt{z^{2}-4}}{2}\simeq 0.148582-0.578415\,\mathrm{i}.

Then
q_{3}
and its complex conjugate
\overline{q_{3}}
are the two dominant singularities of
\Phi_{3}(q)
and the radius of convergence is

\rho_{3}=|q_{3}|\simeq 0.597194.

Note that
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
were first calculated in
[
LMGOV24
]
. On the other hand, approximate values of
\rho_{n}
were given in
[
Ren22
]
for
1\leqslant n\leqslant 48
, where they illustrate the fact that
\rho_{n}\geqslant\rho_{1}
for any
n
(which is one of the main results of that paper).

Proof.

The case of the
q
-golden ratio is immediate, because the roots of
Q_{1}(q)=1+3q+q^{2}
are

q_{1}=\frac{-3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\simeq-0,381966\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{q_{1}}=-\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\simeq-2,618034.

Next, we consider the case of the
q
-silver ratio. The palindromic polynomial
Q_{2}(q)
factorises as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{2}(q)

\displaystyle=1+q+4q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\biggl(\biggl(q+\frac{1}{q}\biggr)^{2}+q+\frac{1}{q}+2\biggr)

\displaystyle=\biggl(q^{2}+\frac{1-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7}}{2}q+1\biggr)\biggl(q^{2}+\frac{1+\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7}}{2}q+1\biggr)

(5.5)

\displaystyle=(q-q_{2})\biggl(q-\frac{1}{q_{2}}\biggr)(q-\overline{q_{2}})\biggl(q-\frac{1}{\overline{q_{2}}}\biggr),

with

q_{2}=\frac{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7}-1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2},\qquad\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7}-1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2},

where

(5.6)

\delta:=\sqrt{-\frac{11+\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7}}{2}}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\sqrt{8\sqrt{2}-11}-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{8\sqrt{2}+11}\right).

Since

|q_{2}|=\frac{1+\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{2\sqrt{2}-1}}{2},\qquad|1/q_{2}|=\frac{1+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{2\sqrt{2}-1}}{2},

we get that
q_{2}
and its conjugate are the two dominant singularities of
\Phi_{2}(q)
and that
\rho_{2}=|q_{2}|
.

As concerns the
q
-bronze number, calculations are more tricky. We have

Q_{3}(q)=1+q+2q^{2}+5q^{3}+2q^{4}+q^{5}+q^{6}=q^{3}\,\widetilde{Q}_{3}\Bigr(q+\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)

with
\widetilde{Q}_{3}(y):=y^{3}+y^{2}-y+3
. This polynomial has three roots:
z
, its complex conjugate
\overline{z}
and the real number
t
, where

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(-1+\frac{2^{2/3}(1-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{3})}{3(23-3\sqrt{57})^{1/3}}+\frac{(23-3\sqrt{57})^{1/3}(1+\mathrm{i}\sqrt{3})}{3\cdot 2^{2/3}}\right)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\left(1+\frac{2^{5/3}}{(23-3\sqrt{57})^{1/3}}+\Bigl(2(23-3\sqrt{57})\Bigr)^{1/3}\right)

so that

Q_{3}(q)=(q^{2}-zq+1)(q^{2}-\overline{z}q+1)(q^{2}-tq+1).

Thus, if we put

q_{3}=\frac{z-\sqrt{z^{2}-4}}{2},\qquad r=\frac{t-\sqrt{t^{2}-4}}{2}

we conclude that the 6 roots of
Q_{3}(q)
are

\displaystyle q_{3}

\displaystyle\simeq 0.148582-0.578415\,\mathrm{i}

\displaystyle 1/q_{3}

\displaystyle\simeq 0.416616+1.621843\,\mathrm{i}

\displaystyle\overline{q_{3}}

\displaystyle\simeq 0.148582+0.578415\,\mathrm{i}

\displaystyle 1/\overline{q_{3}}

\displaystyle\simeq 0.416616-1.621843\,\mathrm{i}

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\simeq-1.432139

\displaystyle 1/r

\displaystyle\simeq-0.698256.

Hence
q_{3}
and
\overline{q_{3}}
are the dominant roots and
\rho_{3}=|q_{3}|\simeq 0.597194
.
∎

We come to the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.2
.

1)

Generic estimates.
Let
n
be any positive integer. Denote by
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{r}
all the roots of
Q_{n}
which are dominant, i.e. whose modulus
|\zeta_{j}|
is the smallest, equal to
\rho_{n}
, and define the complex numbers

(5.7)

\gamma_{j}:=\frac{1}{2\zeta_{j}}\sqrt{(1-\zeta_{j}+\zeta_{j}^{2})\,\zeta_{j}\,\frac{Q_{n}(q)}{\zeta_{j}-q}\biggl|_{q=\zeta_{j}}\biggr.}\qquad(j=1,\ldots,r).

Then the coefficients
\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})=[q^{l}]\Phi_{n}(q)
of the power series (
2.16
) representing
\Phi_{n}(q)
about
q=0
satisfy the asymptotics

(5.8)

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{-\gamma_{j}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\zeta_{j}^{-l}\,l^{-3/2}+\mathrm{O}(\zeta_{j}^{-l}l^{-5/2})\right).

2)

Case of
q
-golden ratio.
If
n=1
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{l}(\phi_{1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l}\frac{5^{1/4}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\phi_{1}^{2l}\,l^{-3/2}+\mathrm{O}(\phi_{1}^{2l}l^{-5/2})

(5.9)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l}\frac{5^{1/4}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\biggl(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\biggr)^{l}\,l^{-3/2}+\mathrm{O}\bigl((\tfrac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2})^{l}l^{-5/2}\bigr).

3)

Case of
q
-silver ratio.
If
n=2
, we have

(5.10)

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})=\frac{-1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\re\left(\sqrt{(35-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7})\delta+32\,\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7}}\cdot q_{2}^{-l-1}\right)\,l^{-3/2}+\mathrm{O}(q_{2}^{-l}l^{-5/2}),

where
q_{2}
is defined by (
5.3
) and
\delta
by (
5.6
).

4)

Case of
q
-bronze ratio.
If
n=3
, we have

(5.11)

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{3})=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\re\bigl(\gamma_{1}q_{3}^{-l}\bigr)\,l^{-3/2}+\mathrm{O}(q_{3}^{-l}l^{-5/2}),

where
q_{3}
is defined by (
5.4
) and
\gamma_{1}
by (
5.7
); approximately,
\gamma_{1}\simeq 0.244816+1.134722\,\mathrm{i}
.

Proof.

Let us start with the generic estimates.
For each
j=1,\ldots,r
, one can rewrite expression (
5.1
) of
\Phi_{n}(q)
as follows:

\Phi_{n}(q)=\frac{R_{n}(q)}{2q}+\frac{1}{2q}\sqrt{\Bigl(1-\frac{q}{\zeta_{j}}\Bigr)(1-q+q^{2})\zeta_{j}\frac{Q_{n}(q)}{\zeta_{j}-q}}.

Consequently, the Puiseux expansion of
\Phi_{n}(q)
near
\zeta_{j}
starts like this:

\Phi_{n}(q)=\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}\Bigl(1-\frac{q}{\zeta_{j}}\Bigr)+\mathrm{O}\biggl(\Bigl(1-\frac{q}{\zeta_{j}}\Bigr)^{2}\biggr)+\sqrt{1-\frac{q}{\zeta_{j}}}\biggl(\gamma_{j}+\mathrm{O}\Bigl(1-\frac{q}{\zeta_{j}}\Bigr)\biggr)

where
\gamma_{j}
is defined by (
5.7
) and
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}
are some constants depending on
R_{n}
and
\zeta_{j}
. According to the principles of singularity analysis (see §VI.4 in
[
FS09
]
), only the singular part of this expression, namely,

\gamma_{j}\sqrt{1-\frac{q}{\zeta_{j}}}+\mathrm{O}\biggl(\Bigl(1-\frac{q}{\zeta_{j}}\Bigr)^{3/2}\biggr)

contributes non-trivially to the estimates of the Taylor coefficients of
\Phi_{n}(q)
, with the term

\frac{-\gamma_{j}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\zeta_{j}^{-l}\,l^{-3/2}+\mathrm{O}(\zeta_{j}^{-l}l^{-5/2}).

Summing the contributions of all singularities
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{r}
we obtain (
5.8
). Note that this estimate does not depend at all on the polynomial
R_{n}
.

Now, we apply the results of
5.1
to give more precise information for small values of
n
. In the case of the
q
-golden ratio (
n=1
), the sole dominant root
\zeta_{1}
is the number
q_{1}
given by (
5.2
) and one gets easily

\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{2q_{1}}\sqrt{(1-q_{1}+q_{1}^{2})\,q_{1}\,\Bigl(\frac{1}{q_{1}}-q_{1}\Bigr)}=-5^{1/4}.

Since

we obtain (

).

In the case
n=2

\zeta_{1}=q_{2}
and

, with

given by (
5.3
), hence

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})=\frac{-1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\gamma_{1}\zeta_{1}^{-l}+\gamma_{2}\zeta_{2}^{-l}\right)+\mathrm{O}(\zeta_{1}^{-l}l^{-5/2})=\frac{-1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\gamma_{1}q_{2}^{-l}+\gamma_{2}\overline{q_{2}}^{-l}\right)+\mathrm{O}(q_{2}^{-l}l^{-5/2}).

Because of expression (
5.5
) it is easily seen that
\gamma_{2}=\overline{\gamma_{1}}
, so that

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{2})=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\re(\gamma_{1}q_{2}^{-l})+\mathrm{O}(q_{2}^{-l}l^{-5/2}).

A lengthy calculation (shortened by the use of a computer) gives

\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\,q_{2}}\sqrt{(35-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7})\delta+32\,\mathrm{i}\sqrt{7}}

where
\delta
as in (
5.6
) and this yields (
5.10
).

We finish with case

(
q
-bronze ratio). According to
5.1
there are again two dominant singularities
\zeta_{1}=q_{3}
and
\zeta_{2}=\overline{q_{3}}
, with

given by (
5.4

, we obtain
the estimate (
5.11
) for
\kappa_{l}(\phi_{3})
. The explicit expression of
\gamma_{1}
is too complicated to be written here.
∎

6.
Remarkable identities

As explained in
Section

2.1
,
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
-equivariance of the quantification map, as reflected by (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), is at the heart of the theory of
q
-numbers. To begin this section we investigate particular consequences that concern
q
-metallic numbers
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
, namely remarkable relations existing between the
q
-deformations of
\phi_{n}
,
-\phi_{n}
,
1/\phi_{n}
and
-1/\phi_{n}
, and between their Laurent coefficients at
q=0
.

Of course, modular equivariance rule (
2.7
) already gives

(6.1)

\left[-\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}\right]_{q}=-\frac{1}{q[\phi_{n}]_{q}}

and if we use also (
2.8
), that is, equivariance with respect to the bigger group
PGL(2,\mathbb{Z})
, we get in addition

[-\phi_{n}]_{q}=\frac{-[\phi_{n}]_{q}+1-q^{-1}}{(q-1)[\phi_{n}]_{q}+1},\qquad\left[\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}\right]_{q}=\frac{(q-1)[\phi_{n}]_{q}+1}{q[\phi_{n}]_{q}+1-q}.

All these formulas involve series reversions and products, and using them would yield quite complicated relations between the Laurent coefficients of the corresponding
q
-numbers. On the contrary, the following result reveals the existence of much simpler relations.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
n
be any positive integer.
We have:

(6.2)

\displaystyle[\phi_{n}]_{q}

\displaystyle=q^{n}\left[\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}\right]_{q}+[n]_{q},

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}\right]_{q}

\displaystyle=q^{n}[-\phi_{n}]_{q}+[n]_{q},

(6.4)

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}\right]_{q}

\displaystyle=[n]_{q}+\frac{(q^{n}+1)(q-1)}{q}-[\phi_{n}]_{q}

(6.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2q}\left(R_{n}(q)-\sqrt{P_{n}(q)}\right),

(6.6)

\displaystyle[\phi_{n}]_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{n}+1)(q-1)}{q}-q^{n}[-\phi_{n}]_{q}.

Proof.

First, we note that (
2.6
) immediately generalises as follows:

[x+k]_{q}=q^{k}[x]_{q}+[k]_{q}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}).

From this and from the fundamental relation of metallic numbers

\phi_{n}=n+\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}

we deduce both (
6.2
) and (
6.3
). Secondly, the functional equation (
2.10
) reads

(6.7)

[\phi_{n}]_{q}-\frac{R_{n}(q)}{q}=\frac{1}{q[\phi_{n}]_{q}}.

Using relation (
6.1
) and expression (
2.11
) of
R_{n}(q)
, we obtain (
6.4
). Then, (
6.5
) comes from the expression (
2.12
). Finally, (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) yield (
6.6
).
∎

Recall that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
[x]_{q}=\sum_{j\geqslant j_{0}}\kappa_{j}(x)q^{j}
for the Laurent series expansion (about zero) of its
q
-deformation.

Corollary 6.2
.

The Laurent coefficients of the
q
-deformations of
-\phi_{n},\dfrac{1}{\phi_{n}}
and
\dfrac{-1}{\phi_{n}}
are all expressible by those of
\phi_{n}
, namely:

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}\right]_{q}

\displaystyle=q^{n}+\sum_{j=n+1}^{+\infty}\kappa_{j+n}(\phi_{n})q^{j}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}\right]_{q}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{q}+1-q^{n-1}+q^{n}-q^{2n}-\sum_{j=2n+1}^{+\infty}\kappa_{j}(\phi_{n})q^{j}

\displaystyle[-\phi_{n}]_{q}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}(q)-\left[\frac{1}{\phi_{n}}\right]_{q}=\alpha_{n}(q)-q^{n}-\sum_{j=n+1}^{+\infty}\kappa_{j+n}(\phi_{n})q^{j}

where

\alpha_{n}(q)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{q^{2}}-\frac{1}{q}+1&if $n=1$,\\
-\frac{1}{q^{3}}-\frac{2}{q}+1&if $n=2$,\\
-\frac{1}{q^{n+1}}-\frac{1}{q^{n-1}}-\frac{1}{q^{n-2}}-\cdots-\frac{1}{q^{2}}-\frac{2}{q}+1&if $n\geqslant 3$.\end{cases}

Proof.

All formulas follow at once from the previous proposition, when replacing
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
by its Taylor series expansion (
2.17
).
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Conjunction of the power series expansion (
2.17
) with the functional equation (
6.7
) of
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
provides a similar link between the coefficients of
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
and those of its multiplicative inverse, namely:

\frac{1}{[\phi_{n}]_{q}}=1-q+q^{n}-q^{n+1}+q^{2n+1}+\sum_{j=2n+2}^{+\infty}\kappa_{j-1}(\phi_{n})q^{j}.

Example 6.4
.

The relations given in
6.2
and
6.3
can be checked on the golden ratio
\phi_{1}
:

\displaystyle[\phi_{1}]_{q}

\displaystyle\qquad+978q^{12}-2283q^{13}+5373q^{14}-12735q^{15}+30372q^{16}-72832q^{17}+\cdots

\displaystyle[-\phi_{1}]_{q}

as well as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[\phi_{1}]_{q}}

Remark 6.5
.

We observe that
-1/\phi_{n}=\frac{n-\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}
is the algebraic conjugate of
\phi_{n}=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}
and that (
2.12
) and (
6.5
) show that
[-1/\phi_{n}]_{q}
is still the conjugate of
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
. Actually, this is a consequence of a general fact explained in
[
LMG21
]
: if
x=\frac{r+\sqrt{p}}{s}
is a quadratic irrational number such that
[x]_{q}=\frac{R(q)+\sqrt{P(q)}}{S(q)}
(see (
2.9
)), then the
q
-deformation of the conjugate number
\tilde{x}=\frac{r-\sqrt{p}}{s}
is

[\tilde{x}]_{q}=\frac{R(q)-\sqrt{P(q)}}{S(q)}
.

In some cases, this yields automatically the identity
\kappa_{j}(x)=-\kappa_{j}(\tilde{x})
for
j
large enough. Indeed, if for instance
S(q)
is a monomial, then the Laurent series expansion of
[x]_{q}
and
[\tilde{x}]_{q}
at
q=0
will be essentially determined by the infinite expansion of the part
\pm\sqrt{P(q)}
in the numerator. This is the case, e.g., for

\displaystyle[\sqrt{7}]_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1-q+q^{4}+q^{5}+\sqrt{1+2q+q^{2}+4q^{3}+6q^{4}+6q^{6}+4q^{7}+q^{8}+2q^{9}+q^{10}}}{2q^{3}}

\displaystyle=1+q+q^{3}-q^{4}+2q^{5}-3q^{6}+4q^{7}-6q^{8}+8q^{9}-9q^{10}+9q^{11}-5q^{12}

\displaystyle\qquad-9q^{13}+40q^{14}-101q^{15}+215q^{16}-411q^{17}+724q^{18}-1195q^{19}+\cdots

whose coefficients
\kappa_{l}(\sqrt{7})
are the opposites of the ones of its conjugate starting from
j=3
:

\displaystyle[-\sqrt{7}]_{q}

\displaystyle=-q^{-3}-q^{-2}-1+q^{2}-q^{3}+q^{4}-2q^{5}+3q^{6}-4q^{7}+6q^{8}-8q^{9}+9q^{10}-9q^{11}

\displaystyle\qquad+5q^{12}+9q^{13}-40q^{14}+101q^{15}-215q^{16}+411q^{17}-724q^{18}+1195q^{19}+\cdots

On the contrary, if
S(q)
is more complicated, it will contribute in a non-trivial way to the total Laurent expansion of
[x]_{q}
and
[\tilde{x}]_{q}
.
For instance, the coefficients of

\displaystyle[1/\sqrt{7}]_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1-q+q^{4}+q^{5}+\sqrt{1+2q+q^{2}+4q^{3}+6q^{4}+6q^{6}+4q^{7}+q^{8}+2q^{9}+q^{10}}}{2q+4q^{2}+2q^{3}+4q^{4}+2q^{5}}

\displaystyle=q^{2}-q^{3}+q^{4}-2q^{5}+3q^{6}-3q^{7}+3q^{8}-3q^{9}+8q^{11}-22q^{12}

\displaystyle\qquad+48q^{13}-95q^{14}+169q^{15}-277q^{16}+426q^{17}-603q^{18}+754q^{19}-756q^{20}+\cdots

are not related to opposites of coefficients of its conjugate

\displaystyle[-1/\sqrt{7}]_{q}

\displaystyle=-q^{-1}+1-q+2q^{2}-4q^{3}+8q^{4}-16q^{5}+31q^{6}-60q^{7}+116q^{8}-222q^{9}

\displaystyle\qquad+423q^{10}-804q^{11}+1522q^{12}-2873q^{13}+5414q^{14}-10186q^{15}

\displaystyle\qquad+19142q^{16}-35952q^{17}+67505q^{18}-126745q^{19}+238023q^{20}+\cdots

These examples show also that there is no simple link between
\kappa_{j}(x)
and
\kappa_{j}(\pm 1/x)
, in general, even for a quadratic irrational
x
. The case of metallic numbers seems really special.

We end this paragraph by highlighting another kind of symmetry we have found for the
q
-metallic ratios.

Proposition 6.6
.

For any integer
n\geqslant 1
we have

(6.9)

q^{n}\,\Phi_{n}\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)=(1-q)(1+q^{n})+q\,\Phi_{n}(q).

In particular, the
q
-golden ratio satisfies the following equation:

\Phi_{1}\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)-\frac{1}{q}=\Phi_{1}(q)-q.

Relation (
6.9
) should be taken rather formally, because we do not know what are the domains of the analytic functions involved here and how they intersect. In particular, no implication for coefficients can be infered, since the right-hand side of (
6.9
) expands as a power series in an open disc
0<|q|<\rho_{n}
while the left-hand side expands as a Laurent series in the region
|q|>\rho_{n}
.

Proof.

Recall from
Section

2.2
that

\Phi_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{2q}\left(R_{n}(q)+\sqrt{P_{n}(q)}\right)

where
R_{n}(q)=q[n]_{q}+(q^{n}+1)(q-1)
and
P_{n}(q)
is a palindrome of degree
2n+2
. We compute easily

R_{n}\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{q^{n+1}}(R_{n}(q)+2(1+q^{n})(1+q)),

hence

\Phi_{n}\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)=\frac{q}{2}\biggl(\frac{R_{n}(q)+2(1+q^{n})(1+q)+\sqrt{P_{n}(q)}}{q^{n+1}}\biggr)

and (
6.9
) follows.
∎

7.
Logarithmic behaviour

Recall that a sequence
(x_{l})_{l\geqslant l_{0}}
of real numbers is called
log-convex
if

(7.1)

x_{l}^{2}\leqslant x_{l-1}\,x_{l+1}

for all
l\geqslant l_{0}
, and
log-concave
if

(7.2)

x_{l}^{2}\geqslant x_{l-1}\,x_{l+1}

for all
l\geqslant l_{0}
. It seems that log-convexity and log-concavity properties are studied exclusively for sequences of
non-negative
numbers (despite our efforts, we were not able to find a more general example in the literature). In such a case, a sequence
(x_{l})
is log-convex (respectively log-concave) if and only if the sequence
(\log x_{l})
is convex (respectively concave), and this explains the vocabulary. In the sequel, we deal with numbers that can be negative, hence with no natural logarithm, but we keep the same vocabulary.

Also, when the
x_{l}
are positive numbers, the log-convexity (respectively log-concavity) of the sequence
(x_{l})
means that the sequence of consecutive ratios
x_{l+1}/x_{l}
is monotonically increasing (respectively decreasing), but this is not true anymore without the assumption of positivity.

However, we can make the following basic observations, which are straightforward.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
(x_{l})
a sequence of non-negative numbers, and let
y_{l}=(-1)^{l}x_{l}
.

1)

The sequence
(y_{l})
is log-convex (respectively log-concave) if and only if the sequence
(x_{l})
is log-convex (respectively log-concave).

2)

If the numbers
y_{l}
are nonzero, the sequence
(y_{l})
is log-convex (respectively log-concave) if and only if the sequence of consecutive ratios
y_{l+1}/y_{l}
is monotonically decreasing (respectively increasing). That is, the characterisation is reversed when compared with the case of the sequence of positive numbers
(x_{l})
.

Both facts can be used to deal with the sequence
(\kappa_{l}(\phi_{1}))
of the coefficients of the
q
-golden ratio
[\phi_{1}]_{q}
. Indeed, recall from the introduction that these numbers are related to the the RNA secondary structure sequence
(a_{l})
the formula

\kappa_{l}(\phi_{1})=(-1)^{l}a_{l-1}\quad(l\geqslant 2).

Since it is known that
(a_{l})
is a log-convex sequence (
[
DSV04
]
, Theorem 2.6), we immediately get:

Proposition 7.2
.

The sequence
(\kappa_{l}(\phi_{1}))_{l\geqslant 6}
is log-convex.

As concerns the log-convexity of the coefficients of
[\phi_{n}]_{q}
for
n\geqslant 2
we are unable to state a result or even to give a precise conjecture: computer experimentations suggest that, for
2\leqslant n\leqslant 100
, the sequence of coefficients
\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})
is
log-concave
(starting from some rank
l_{0}
depending on
n
),
with a unique exception for the case
n=19
for which the sequence seems neither log-convex nor log-concave. To be precise, if
2\leqslant n\leqslant 100
and
n\not=19
, relation (
7.2
) is checked for all
l_{0}\leqslant l\leqslant 5000
, while if
n=19
, both (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) occur, at least when
l\leqslant 10000
. This exceptional case is a very curious phenomenon for which we have no explanation so far, except that the sequence could still be log-concave starting from some
l>10000
.
Also, apart from case
n=1
, which amounts to consider the sequence
(a_{l})
, absolute values of the coefficients do not seem to satisfy log-convexity or log-concavity properties.

We can explain our difficulties to study cases
n\geqslant 2
by the fact, already mentioned, that the combinatorial world essentially deals with non-negative numbers, and that we miss available techniques for situations without this assumption.
