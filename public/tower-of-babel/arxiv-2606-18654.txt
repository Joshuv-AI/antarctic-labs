Rigidity of infinite exchangeable sequences with Gaussian marginals
Maximillian Newman

Abstract
We study infinite exchangeable sequences with Gaussian one-dimensional marginals. We formulate the conjecture that joint Gaussianity of a single pair of coordinates forces the entire sequence to be a Gaussian process. Although this conjecture remains open, we prove that joint Gaussianity of the first four coordinates is sufficient. We also establish the corresponding two-point criterion under the additional assumption that the directing measure is almost surely infinitely divisible.

Introduction

For a sequence of observations
X=\left(X_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
, exchangeability is a natural relaxation of the notion of being independent and identically distributed (iid) as it preserves the marginal structure of the sequence. This relaxation allows one to still recover many of the properties of iid sequences by preserving some of the geometric and combinatorial structures of iid sequences, e.g. the classical works of Aldous
1
, Hoover
6
, and Kallenberg
7
, as well as recent works
3
;
4
;
9
. The most comprehensive survey for such rigidity results, to the best of our knowledge, remains
8
. These results are often strengthened versions of the classical work of de Finetti
5
.

Consider an infinite sequence
X=\left(X_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
of random variables on a probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
invariant under the action of finite permutations of
\mathbb{N}
, i.e. for which

\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\left(X_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,X_{\sigma(n)}\right)

for any permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and any natural number
n
. De Finetti
5
established that there exists a unique random probability measure
\eta
such that the sequence
X
is independent and identically distributed conditional on
\eta
.

It is well known
(
2
, p. 30)
that when
X
is a Gaussian process if and only if
\eta
admits a representation

\eta=\delta_{M}*\mu,

(1)

where
M
is a Gaussian random variable and
\mu
is the law of a Gaussian random variable. If
X
is not a Gaussian process, even with Gaussian marginals, this need not hold in general, as shown in the following example.

Example 1.1
.

Let
\epsilon_{i}
be an iid sequence of random variables, independent of a centered Gaussian random variable
Z
, for which

\mathbb{P}\left(\epsilon_{1}=1\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\epsilon_{1}=-1\right)=\frac{1}{2}.

Then
X_{i}:=\epsilon_{i}Z
yields an infinite exchangeable sequence of random variables for which the representation of Equation (
1
) fails to hold. Indeed, by construction we have

\eta=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{-Z}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{Z}.

(2)

Note that exchangeability fixes the covariance structure of
X
. We elect to normalize
X
so that
\rm{Var}(X_{1})=1
and
\rm{Cov}(X_{1},X_{2})=\rho
for some
\rho\in[0,1]
. For any
r
-tuple
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
the covariance matrix
\Sigma^{(r)}=\left(\Sigma_{i,j}^{(r)}\right)_{i,j=1}^{r}
is defined by

\Sigma_{i,j}^{(r)}=\begin{cases}1&,\quad\text{ if }i=j\\
\rho&,\quad\text{ if }i\neq j\end{cases}.

As the covariance structure is fixed by exchangeability and
X
need not be a Gaussian process if each
X_{i}
is marginally Gaussian, is there a counterexample if we make the stronger assumption that
(X_{1},X_{2})
is jointly Gaussian? We indeed conjecture there is no such counterexample.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
X=\left(X_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an infinite exchangeable sequence of random variables. If
(X_{1},X_{2})
is a centered multivariate Gaussian, then
X
is a Gaussian process.

We make partial progress in this paper towards this 2-point conjecture.

If
X
were a finite sequence, this would surely not be the case, as can be seen by the following example.

Example 1.3
.

For any finite
K
, there exists a collection of exchangeable, centered, marginally Gaussian random variables
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{K}
such that each pair
(X_{i},X_{j})
(for distinct
i
and
j
) is a multivariate Gaussian with covariance matrix
I_{2\times 2}
but for which no triple is Gaussian.

Indeed, let
p
denote the probability density function of a standard Gaussian random variable. Define a density
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{K}
by

f(x)=\left(1+\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{K-2}\sum_{j=i+1}^{K-1}\sum_{k=j+1}^{K}\sin(x_{i}x_{j}x_{k})\right)\prod_{i=1}^{K}p(x_{i})

for
\epsilon<\binom{K}{3}^{-1}
. Integrating out all but two of the marginals then yields a pairwise Gaussian kernel as
\sin
is odd and
p
is even. Yet no triple is Gaussian as integrating out all the other variables keeps the
\sin
perturbation.
f
then is the density of such a desired tuple.

While we are unable to prove Conjecture
1.2
, we are able to prove a rigidity result under a
4
-point assumption. The significance of this result is that a finite amount of Gaussian information forces the entire de Finetti directing measure to have the form of Equation (
1
). In this sense, the theorem rules out infinite exchangeable analogues of the finite-dimensional perturbations in Example
1.3
once four coordinates are assumed to be jointly Gaussian.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X=\left(X_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an infinite exchangeable sequence of random variables for which
(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})
is a centered multivariate Gaussian with covariance structure
\Sigma^{(4)}
. Then
X
is a Gaussian process.

Equivalently, the directing measure
\eta
must almost surely admit the representation in Equation (
1
). The proof is given in Section
2
.

We are also able to prove the two-point conjecture under an additional structural assumption on the directing measure, namely almost sure infinite divisibility. The Lévy-Khintchine representation guaranteed by infinite divisibility gives a non-negativity property for the fourth cumulant, which provides enough rigidity to recover the Gaussian de Finetti representation of Equation (
1
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X=\left(X_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an infinite exchangeable sequence of random variables with directing measure
\eta
that is almost surely infinitely divisible. If
(X_{1},X_{2})
is a centered multivariate Gaussian with covariance structure
\Sigma^{(2)}
, then
X
is a Gaussian process.

Theorem
1.5
is proven in Section
3
. The pairwise Gaussian assumption in Theorem
1.5
cannot be replaced by one-dimensional Gaussianity alone, even when the directing measure is almost surely infinitely divisible. The following example shows that randomizing the conditional variance of an otherwise Gaussian directing measure preserves the Gaussian marginal but destroys joint Gaussianity.

Example 1.6
.

Let
V
be a non-constant random variable taking values in
(0,1)
. Conditional on
V
, we let
M
, conditional on
V
, be a centered normal random variable with variance
1-V
. We define the almost surely infinitely divisible directing measure

\eta=\delta_{M}*\gamma_{V}.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
be independent and identically distributed with law
\eta
. Then each
X_{i}
is marginally a standard Gaussian, and yet
(X_{1},X_{2})
is not jointly Gaussian unless
V
is deterministic. Indeed, if it were we would have that
X_{1}-X_{2}
is Gaussian. In particular, we would have that its fourth moment satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{1}-X_{2})^{4}\right]=12\mathbb{E}\left[V^{2}\right]=3(2\mathbb{E}\left[V\right])^{2},

which fails because
V
is non-constant.

The remaining difficulty in Conjecture
1.2
is to upgrade information about averaged two-point marginals into an almost sure structural statement about the directing measure. The two-point Gaussian assumption imposes identities only after averaging over the random measure
\eta
that constrain
\eta
, but it is not obvious to the author how these restrictions imply the conjecture. The proof of Theorem
1.4
overcomes this by using four-point Gaussianity to control the second-order fluctuations of the relevant averaged identities, forcing an almost sure multiplicative relation for the moment generating function of the directing measure. The proof of Theorem
1.5
overcomes the same obstruction by leveraging the additional structure given by the Lévy-Khintchine representation of infinitely divisible measures. In the unrestricted two-point problem, neither of these additional mechanisms is available, and this is the source of the remaining gap.

We fix

\eta

\eta

\mathbb{R}

We now introduce some notation. Fix
\rho\in[0,1]

M_{\eta}
and the log moment generating function
K_{\eta}
by

M_{\eta}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tx}d\eta(x)\quad\text{ and }\quad K_{\eta}(t):=\log M_{\eta}(t),

respectively, whenever
M_{\eta}(t)<\infty
. We define a tilt
g_{t}(\eta)
of the moment generating function by

g_{t}(\eta):=\exp\left(K_{\eta}(t)-\frac{1+\rho}{2}t^{2}\right).

(3)

The law of a centered Gaussian random variable with variance
\rho
we denote by
\gamma_{\rho}
, and we set
\gamma=\gamma_{1}
.
We define the defect
q_{a,b}

q_{a,b}:=g_{a}g_{b}-e^{2\rho ab}g_{a+b}.

(4)

Observe that if
\eta=\delta_{N}*\gamma_{1-\rho}

will be identically zero for any

almost surely. It is this relationship to the representation in Equation (

The proof proceeds by expressing Gaussianity of finite-dimensional marginals as a rigidity statement for the tilted law of the directing measure. Lemma
2.1
first shows that exponential moments of finite tuples of

are encoded by the log moment generating function
K_{\eta}
of the directing measure. Lemma
2.3
translates the Gaussian assumption on
(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})
into explicit mixed moment identities for the tilted quantities
g_{t}
. The key step is Lemma
2.4
; using the four-point Gaussian assumption, we compute the

norm of the multiplicative defect

from Equation (

then converts this identity into additivity of

\mathbb{Q}
and then on all of
\mathbb{R}
by continuity. This then forces the desired representation of Equation (

The following lemma demonstrates that the log moment generating function characterizes the full joint distribution of any finite tuple from
X
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
n\geq 1
suppose that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies
M_{\eta}(a_{j})<\infty
almost surely for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{\eta}(a_{i})\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right)\right].

Proof.

Conditional on
\eta
, the variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent and identically distributed with common law
\eta
. Therefore

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right)\,\mid\,\eta\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{a_{i}x}d\eta(x)=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{\eta}(a_{i})\right).

Taking expectations then gives the result.
∎

We now verify that
K_{\eta}
and
M_{\eta}
are almost surely finite, and that
K_{\eta}
is continuous.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have that
M_{\eta}(t)
and
K_{\eta}(t)
are almost surely finite. Moreover,
K_{\eta}
is almost surely continuous.

Proof.

Since
X_{1}
is a standard Gaussian random variable, for every fixed

we have

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{tX_{1}}\right]=e^{t^{2}/2}<\infty.

By de Finetti’s theorem,

\mathbb{E}\left[M_{\eta}(t)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tx}\,d\eta(x)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[e^{tX_{1}}\mid\eta\right]\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{tX_{1}}\right]<\infty.

Since
M_{\eta}(t)
is nonnegative, this implies that
M_{\eta}(t)<\infty
almost surely for each fixed

.

We now upgrade this fixed-

statement to a simultaneous statement for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\mathbb{Q}
is countable, there is an event
E
of probability one such that

M_{\eta}(q)<\infty\qquad\text{for every }q\in\mathbb{Q}

on
E
. Fix
\omega\in E
and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Choose rational numbers
q_{-}<t<q_{+}
. Write

t=\theta q_{-}+(1-\theta)q_{+}

for some

. By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle M_{\eta}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tx}\,d\eta(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{q_{-}x}\right)^{\theta}\left(e^{q_{+}x}\right)^{1-\theta}\,d\eta(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{q_{-}x}\,d\eta(x)\right)^{\theta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{q_{+}x}\,d\eta(x)\right)^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle=M_{\eta}(q_{-})^{\theta}M_{\eta}(q_{+})^{1-\theta}<\infty.

Therefore, on

,
M_{\eta}(t)<\infty
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Since
e^{tx}>0
and
\eta
is a probability measure, we also have

M_{\eta}(t)>0\qquad\text{for every }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence
K_{\eta}(t)=\log M_{\eta}(t)
is finite for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
on the same event
E
.

Finally, the same Hölder inequality shows that

is convex. Indeed, for

K_{\eta}\left(\theta s+(1-\theta)t\right)\leq\theta K_{\eta}(s)+(1-\theta)K_{\eta}(t).

Thus, on
E
,
K_{\eta}
is a finite convex function on all of
\mathbb{R}
. Every finite convex function on
\mathbb{R}
is continuous. Therefore
K_{\eta}
is almost surely continuous.
∎

Combining Equations (

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume for any fixed
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
that
q_{a,b}=0
almost surely.
Then there exists a real-valued random variable
N=N(\eta)
such that with probability one

K_{\eta}(t)=Nt+\frac{1-\rho}{2}t^{2}\quad\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

(12)

Moreover,

\eta=\delta_{N}*\gamma_{1-\rho}

almost surely.

Proof.

We first claim that there is an event
E
of probability one such that for every
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
that

K_{\eta}(a+b)=K_{\eta}(a)+K_{\eta}(b)+(1-\rho)ab.

Indeed, if
q_{a,b}=0
, we have that

g_{a}g_{b}=e^{2\rho ab}g_{a+b}\quad\text{ a.s.}

Substituting the definition of

\exp\left(K_{\eta}(a)-\frac{1+\rho}{2}a^{2}\right)\exp\left(K_{\eta}(b)-\frac{1+\rho}{2}b^{2}\right)=e^{2\rho ab}\exp\left(K_{\eta}(a+b)-\frac{1+\rho}{2}(a+b)^{2}\right).

Taking logarithms and rearranging then gives

K_{\eta}(a+b)=K_{\eta}(a)+K_{\eta}(b)+(1-\rho)ab.

As this holds almost surely for any fixed

we obtain that with probability one that it holds for all
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
by countability of

.

We proceed now to prove Equation (

Then for every

Therefore

. Note that

L_{\eta}(t)=tL_{\eta}(1)\quad\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Defining

3
Proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.5
. The proof exploits the extra rigidity imposed by the Lévy-Khintchine representation theorem. Without infinite divisibility, the two-point Gaussian assumption only constrains certain averaged conditional moments of
\eta
, leaving room for cancellations between fluctuations of the conditional variance and higher order conditional cumulants. Lemma
3.1
rules out this cancellation mechanism; the fourth cumulant of
\eta
is non-negative and vanishes only when
\eta
is Gaussian. We apply this to
X_{1}-X_{2}
. Conditional on
\eta
, Lemma
3.2
expresses the fourth moment of
X_{1}-X_{2}
in terms of the conditional variance of
\eta
and the fourth cumulant of
\eta
. Unconditionally, however,
X_{1}-X_{2}
is Gaussian by the two-point assumption. Comparing these two fourth-moment calculations then forces the conditional variance and cumulants to be constant, which yields the result after a short calculation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda
be an infinitely divisible probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
with finite fourth moment. Let
\kappa_{4}(\lambda)
denote its fourth cumulant. Then
\kappa_{4}(\lambda)\geq 0
. Moreover,
\kappa_{4}(\lambda)=0
if and only if
\lambda
is a (possibly degenerate) Gaussian law.

Proof.

Since
\lambda
is infinitely divisible, its characteristic function
\hat{\lambda}
has Lévy-Khintchine representation

\hat{\lambda}(t)=\exp\left(iat-\frac{b}{2}t^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{itx}-1-itx\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}\right)d\nu(x)\right),

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\geq 0
, and
\nu
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\nu(\{0\})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1\wedge x^{2}\right)d\nu(x)<\infty.

Since
\lambda
has a finite fourth moment we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{4}d\nu(x)<\infty.

Moreover, differentiating the logarithm of the characteristic function four times at the origin yields

\kappa_{4}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{4}d\nu(x).

Hence
\kappa_{4}(\lambda)\geq 0
.

If
\kappa_{4}(\lambda)=0
, then
\nu
is the zero measure. In particular,
\hat{\lambda}
simplifies to

\hat{\lambda}(t)=\exp\left(iat-\frac{b}{2}t^{2}\right),

which is the characteristic function of a Gaussian random variable with mean
a
and variance
b
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\lambda
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
with finite fourth moment. Let
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
be independent with common law
\lambda
. Let
v(\lambda)
and
\kappa_{4}(\lambda)
be the variance and fourth cumulant of
\lambda
, respectively. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[(Y_{1}-Y_{2})^{4}\right]=12v(\lambda)^{2}+2\kappa_{4}(\lambda).

Proof.

Cumulants add under convolution, and the fourth cumulant is unchanged by multiplication by
-1
. Therefore the second and fourth cumulants of
Y_{1}-Y_{2}
, denoted by
\kappa_{2}(Y_{1}-Y_{2})
and
\kappa_{4}(Y_{1}-Y_{2})
, are
2v(\lambda)
and
2\kappa_{4}(\lambda)
, respectively. Since
Y_{1}-Y_{2}
is centered, its fourth moment is related to its cumulants by

\mathbb{E}\left[(Y_{1}-Y_{2})^{4}\right]=\kappa_{4}(Y_{1}-Y_{2})+3\kappa_{2}(Y_{1}-Y_{2})^{2}.

The result then follows by substitution.
∎

Proof.

We proceed by demonstrating that we may write the directing measure
\eta
as

\delta_{M}*\gamma_{1-\rho},

for

\rho

We first observe that, because
X_{1}
is a standard normal, we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}^{4}\right]=3.

By de Finetti’s Theorem, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{4}d\eta(x)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}^{4}\right]=3<\infty,

so
\eta
almost surely has finite fourth moment.

Define
M
,
V
, and
K
to be the conditional mean, variance, and fourth cumulant, i.e.

M:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}xd\eta(x),\quad V:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x-M)^{2}d\eta(x),\quad\text{ and }\quad K:=\kappa_{4}(\eta).

By Lemma
3.1
we have that
K\geq 0
almost surely. As
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are conditionally independent with common law
\eta
, Lemma
3.2
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{1}-X_{2})^{4}\mid\eta\right]=12V^{2}+2K.

Taking expectations yields

\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{1}-X_{2})^{4}\right]=12\mathbb{E}\left[V^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[K\right].

(14)

As
(X_{1},X_{2})
is a centered multivariate Gaussian with covariance structure
\Sigma^{(2)}
, we have that
X_{1}-X_{2}
is a centered Gaussian with variance
2(1-\rho)
. In particular, Equation (
14
) then yields

12(1-\rho)^{2}=12\mathbb{E}\left[V^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[K\right]\geq 12(1-\rho)^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\left[K\right]

by Jensen’s inequality.
We therefore have that
K\equiv 0
almost surely and
V
almost surely equal to
1-\rho
.

As
K
is almost surely equal to zero and
\eta
is almost surely infinitely divisible, we have that
\eta
is almost surely a Gaussian law. In particular,
\eta
is a Gaussian law with mean
M
and variance
1-\rho
conditional on
M
.

We now check that
M
is indeed Gaussian distributed. To this end, we observe, using the Lévy-Khintchine representation, that

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itX_{1}}\mid\eta\right]=\exp\left(itM-\frac{1}{2}(1-\rho)t^{2}\right).

Taking expectations and using the fact that
X_{1}
is marginally a standard normal random variable we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{itM}\right]=e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}t^{2}},

proving the claim. Therefore
X
is a Gaussian process.
