Coexact 1 1 -form spectral gaps of hyperbolic rational homology spheres
Francesco Lin, Michael Lipnowski

Abstract
. We discuss a construction of families of hyperbolic rational homology spheres with coexact 1 1 -form spectral gap uniformly bounded below which is well-suited for explicit computations. Using this, we provide several disjoint intervals containing a limit point of such spectral gaps, the rightmost of which is [ 0.8196 , 0.8277 ] [0.8196,0.8277] . Furthermore, we also exhibit a family of arithmetic examples, answering a question of Abdurrahman-Adve-Giri-Lowe-Zung.



Introduction

The coexact
1
-form spectrum of hyperbolic three-manifolds has recently sparked much interest. It relates closely to a conjecture of Bergeron and Venkatesh growth of torsion homology in congruence covers
[

\lambda_{1}^{*}

coexact
1
-form spectral gap
. This gap behaves qualitatively very differently than the more classically studied spectral gap on functions
\lambda_{1}

L^{2}
-spectra on hyperbolic space

are
\lambda_{1}^{*}(\mathbb{H}^{3})=0
and
\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{3})=1
respectively. Geometrically,

[
Cha84
]

can be bounded below in terms of suitable stable isoperimetric ratios
[

Rud23

[
AAG+24
]

[

]
. In particular, they construct infinite families with a uniform lower bound on

and study in detail their attendant geometric and topological properties.

In this paper, we introduce an alternate construction of infinite families of hyperbolic rational homology spheres with
\lambda_{1}^{*}
uniformly bounded below. Roughly speaking, our construction is based on a fixed mapping torus
M_{\varphi}
(of a diffeomorphism
\varphi

with dihedral symmetry. The manifolds we consider are obtained from these cyclic covers by taking the quotient by a fixed-point-free involution.

This construction lends itself well to

\lambda_{1}^{*}
converges, and for which we can provide an explicit arbitrarily small interval containing the limit. The example for which the limit is the largest among those we found leads to the following.

Theorem 1
.

There exists an infinite family
\{Y_{n}\}
of hyperbolic rational homology spheres such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1}^{*}(Y_{n})\in[0.8196,0.8277]

Relation to Floer theory

The authors’ original interest in explicitly understanding the coexact

-form spectral gap of hyperbolic rational homology spheres in

Conjecture

There exists infinitely many hyperbolic rational homology spheres with

It is not clear whether one can hope for a positive solution to the conjecture by a sequence in a fixed commesurability class. While there are several ways to generalize the construction we present - in particular, the starting point does not need to be a fibered manifold - explicitly understanding

1.
Mapping tori and special involutions

Let
\Sigma
be a closed orientable surface of genus at least two, and consider an orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphism

M_{\varphi}={\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$(t,x)\sim(t+1,\varphi(x))$}\right.}.

This manifold fibers over the circle by sending

Fix an involution

(1)

I\circ\varphi\circ I^{-1}=\varphi^{-1}

is a rational homology sphere. We will refer to

as a

of the mapping torus.

By a celebrated theorem of Thurston’s (see for example

admits a hyperbolic metric. Furthermore, by Geometrization,

admits a hyperbolic metric for which

is a fixed-point-free isometry (see again

]

For a general diffeomorphism
\varphi
satisfying our assumptions, the plots of the family of functions
\{J_{\psi}\}_{\psi\in U(1)}
will not have peaks as nicely localized as the one in Figure
3

\Delta_{\psi}
has distinct but nearby eigenvalues. Heuristically speaking, by Weyl’s law one expects the square roots of low-lying eigenvalues
\sqrt{\lambda^{*}}
to be spaced roughly proportionally to
\varepsilon:=\mathrm{vol}(M_{\varphi})^{-1}
apart from each other. To distinguish them using the trace formula requires using a test function
H
for which the Fourier transform
\widehat{H}
is
\varepsilon

H
being at least
{\varepsilon}^{-1}
-diffuse. Alas, to calculate the geometric side of the trace formula at such a diffuse scale
R={\varepsilon}^{-1}
requires, by the Prime Geodesic Theorem
[
Cha84
]
, computing lengths of roughly
e^{2R}/2R
many prime geodesics, an exponentially difficult task.
