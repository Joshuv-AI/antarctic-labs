Perturbed saddle-point problems in 𝐋 p \mathbf{L}^{p} with non-regular loads
Abeer F. AlSohaim 1,2, Thomas Führer 3, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier 1,4, Segundo Villa-Fuentes 5

Abstract
. In this work, we develop the discrete solvability analysis for perturbed saddle-point problems in Banach spaces with forcing terms regularised by means of a projector constructed using the adjoint of a weighted Clément quasi-interpolation. We take as driving example the linearised Poisson–Boltzmann (an advection-diffusion-reaction problem) in mixed form. We use perturbation arguments on the continuous and discrete levels and then derive a priori estimates that remain valid when the load that appears on the right-hand side of the ”second” equation is in H − 1 \mathrm{H}^{-1} . Further, we show a supercloseness result and analyse convergence of an adequate adaptation of Stenberg postprocessing for mixed advection equations with non-regular data. We provide numerical results that illustrate the convergence of the proposed scheme.



1.
Introduction

We are interested in the mixed form of reaction-diffusion equations with singular sources. The main motivation comes from electrochemical flows, in models describing the distribution of electric potential in the direction normal to charged surfaces (see, for instance,
[
10
]
). In this case, the continuous problem does not possess the usual regularity for elliptic equations. Moreover, while the discrete problem can be defined, the error in the usual energy norms lacks sense, and the typical error estimates need to be modified accordingly.

Relevant references regarding finite element discretisation include the works for primal formulations in
[
29
,
8
,
25
,
16
,
15
]
(see also
[
19
]
for HHO schemes), as well as saddle-point problems such as Stokes, Darcy, elasticity, and coupled flow-transport treated in, e.g.,

[
4
,
1
,
5
]
.
More broadly, one way of designing well-defined methods for problems with loads in
\mathrm{H}^{-1}
is to construct quasi-interpolation operators on piecewise polynomial spaces (see, e.g.,

[
21
,
28
]
).

Here, and because of the structure of the model equations, the singular forcing appears as the right-hand side of the constraint equation in the saddle-point problem. Problems of this type have been recently addressed in
[
22
]
for the case of mixed Poisson, and also in
[
20
]
for first-order least-squares formulations. To handle the singular force, following
[
22
]
we replace it by a regularised version using a linear and bounded projection operator.
While
[
22
]
considers projections onto piecewise constants only, here, we extend their construction to piecewise polynomials.
Such a map is constructed based on the adjoint of Clément quasi-interpolators having second-order approximation properties from a piecewise projection on constants. Moreover, to analyse the resulting mixed formulation in terms of the pseudo-potential flux and the potential, we rely on the theory developed in
[
13
]
, together with the Banach–Nečas–Babuška theorem (cf.
[
17
]
). This framework allows us, by means of a global inf–sup condition combined with a smallness assumption on the advecting velocity field, to prove the well-posedness of the problem at the continuous level. For the finite element approximation, we use Raviart–Thomas spaces and, exploiting their inf–sup stability, we can appeal to the discrete perturbed saddle-point theory from
[
13
]
.
Solutions to the regularised scheme are shown to satisfy a perturbed quasi-optimally result in Banach spaces where the perturbation includes the approximation error of the advection field and the best-approximation of a lower-order term.

We also consider accuracy enhancement by a Stenberg-type postprocessing
[
30
,
11
]
, whose analysis in the present context requires a reformulation of the typical Aubin–Nitsche duality arguments including additional consistency terms which we control with data smallness assumptions and properties of the proposed smoother. The postprocessing technique requires higher regularity to achieve optimal convergence
[
30
]
.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the present paper is the first work addressing the analysis of low-regularity forcing terms for advection-diffusion-reaction in the non-Hilbertian setting and in the presence of a lower-diagonal block perturbation.

The contents of the paper have been organised as follows. Section
2
presents the strong form of the modified Poisson–Boltzmann equations including a load regularisation that turns the continuous problem depending on the mesh. We derive a weak formulation in Banach spaces, and establish the well-posedness using Banach–Nečas–Babuška theorem, data smallness assumptions, and the theory of perturbed saddle-point problems. In Section
3
we construct a mixed finite element scheme using conforming Raviart–Thomas elements and show discrete inf-sup conditions by means of a quasi-interpolator. We prove the unique solvability of the discrete problem following a similar structure to the continuous case. Next, Section
4
briefly addresses Céa estimates, and we derive precise a priori error estimates with respect to the original solution of the linearised Poisson–Boltzmann equation. We also present a postprocess that allows us to achieve higher order approximation of the double layer potential (the primal unknown), and show its properties in Section
5
. Section
6
has a collection of numerical results that highlight the convergence properties and suitability of the proposed formulation. Finally, in Section
7
we close with a brief discussion and comment on ongoing extensions of this work.

2.
Continuous setting

Let us consider a Lipschitz and simply connected bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3
with boundary
\partial\Omega=\overline{\Gamma_{D}}\cup\overline{\Gamma_{N}}
disjointly split into two parts where different types of boundary conditions will be considered (we assume that both sub-boundaries have positive measure), and denote by
\boldsymbol{n}
the outward unit normal vector on the boundary.

We recall the standard notation for Lebesgue spaces
\mathrm{L}^{t}(\Omega)
,
t\in(1,+\infty)
,
with norm
\|\bullet\|_{0,t;\Omega}
, and for Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega)
,
s\geq 0
, endowed
with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{s,\Omega}
and seminorm
|\bullet|_{s,\Omega}
. In
particular,
{\mathrm{H}}^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
stands for the space of traces
of functions of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
{\mathrm{H}}^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)

denotes its dual (similarly for subsets of the boundary). We recall from
[
23
, Section 2.4.2]
the definition of the space
\mathrm{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})=\{\eta\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D}):E_{00}(\eta)\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\}
supplied with the norm

\|\bullet\|_{1/2,00,\Gamma_{D}}:=\|E_{00}(\bullet)\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega},

where

E_{00}:\mathrm{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D})\to\mathrm{H}^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
denotes the extension-by-zero operator

E_{00}(\eta)=\begin{cases}\eta&\text{on $\Gamma_{D}$},\\
0&\text{on $\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{D}$},\end{cases}\qquad\forall\eta\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D}).

Furthermore, the restriction of
\psi\in\mathrm{H}^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
to
\Gamma_{D}
, defined as

\langle\psi|_{\Gamma_{D}},\eta\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}=\langle\psi,E_{00}(\eta)\rangle_{\partial\Omega}\quad\forall\eta\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D}),

belongs to the dual space, denoted as
[\mathrm{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})]^{\prime}=:\mathrm{H}^{-1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})
.
Its norm is

(2.1)

\|\psi|_{\Gamma_{D}}\|_{-1/2,00,\Gamma_{D}}=\sup_{0\neq\eta\in\mathrm{H}_{00}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D})}\frac{\langle\psi,E_{00}(\eta)\rangle_{\partial\Omega}}{\|E_{00}(\eta)\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}}.

Finally, for any pair of normed spaces
(X,\|\bullet\|_{X})
and

(Y,\|\bullet\|_{Y})
, we provide the product space
X\times Y
with the
natural norm
\|(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}:=\|x\|_{X}+\|y\|_{Y}

for all
(x,y)\in X\times Y
.

For a Dirichlet datum
\psi_{D}\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})
, the motivating problem under consideration is the nonlinear Poisson–Boltzmann equation
[
10
]
,

(2.2a)

\displaystyle\kappa\psi-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\varepsilon\nabla\psi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi)

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.2b)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\psi_{D}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{D},

(2.2c)

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla\psi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi)\cdot\boldsymbol{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{N},

including a fixed arbitrary advecting velocity field
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)
. This particular regularity comes from the specific functional structure of the Navier–Stokes equations in mixed form from
[
14
]
, aimed at coupling with the Poisson–Boltzmann equation as in
[
3
]
.
The permittivity coefficient
\varepsilon
is heterogeneous and bounded away from zero, the reaction function
\kappa
depends nonlinearly on the potential and it is monotone and unbounded, and the right-hand side source
g
is a sum of Dirac measures concentrated at points, and line sources (when
d=2
).
However, and as a preliminary step, we consider that
\kappa\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
is heterogeneous but independent of
\psi
, and that
g\in\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\Omega):=(\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime}
.

In the work at hand we are therefore interested in a mixed formulation of the linearised Poisson–Boltzmann equation in terms of the
double layer potential

\psi
and the
pseudo potential flux

\boldsymbol{\zeta}
. This gives

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\zeta}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\nabla\psi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.3b)

\displaystyle\kappa\psi-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.3c)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\psi_{D}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{D},

(2.3d)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{N}.

2.1.
Analysis of the primal formulation

The weak formulation of (
2.2a
) reads as follows: Find
\psi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\kappa\psi\varphi+\varepsilon\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\varphi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi\cdot\nabla\varphi)&=\int_{\Omega}g\varphi\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega),\quad\psi|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\psi_{D}.\end{split}

Throughout, we make the standing assumption that

\varepsilon\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
0<\underline{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon\leq\overline{\varepsilon}:=\|\varepsilon\|_{0,\infty;\Omega}
,
\kappa\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\kappa\geq 0
(a.e. in
\Omega
) and that
the norm of
\boldsymbol{u}
is sufficiently small to guarantee existence and uniqueness of solutions, i.e.,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{0,4;\Omega}<\frac{\underline{\varepsilon}}{C_{\mathrm{Sob}}{(1+c_{P}^{2})^{1/2}}},

where
c_{P}>0
denotes Poincaré’s constant and
C_{\mathrm{Sob}}>0
denotes the continuity constant of the Sobolev embedding
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{4}(\Omega)
, i.e., the smallest constants such that

\displaystyle{\|\varphi\|_{0,2;\Omega}\leq c_{P}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,2;\Omega},}\quad\|\varphi\|_{0,4;\Omega}\leq C_{\mathrm{Sob}}{\|\varphi\|_{1,2;\Omega}}

holds for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
.
We stress that assumption (
2.5
) implies that there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\leq\alpha\frac{\underline{\varepsilon}}{C_{\mathrm{Sob}}{(1+c_{P}^{2})^{1/2}}}.

This observation is used in the proof of several results below.
In the following, we use notation
\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)=(\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega))^{\prime}
.
And note that any
g\in\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\Omega)
satisfies

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)}\frac{\langle g,v\rangle}{\|v\|_{1,2;\Omega}}\leq\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\langle g,v\rangle}{\|v\|_{1,2;\Omega}}=:\|g\|_{\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\Omega)}

with
\langle g,v\rangle
denoting the duality pairing on
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Using the Sobolev embedding we note that, for
\varphi\in\mathrm{L}^{4/3}(\Omega)
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}=\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)}\frac{\langle\varphi,v\rangle}{\|v\|_{1,2;\Omega}}\leq\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)}\frac{\|\varphi\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\|v\|_{0,4;\Omega}}{\|v\|_{1,2;\Omega}}\leq C_{\mathrm{Sob}}\|\varphi\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
g\in\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\Omega)
and
\psi_{D}\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})
, Problem (
2.2
) admits a unique solution
\psi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{1,2;\Omega}\leq C(\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}}+\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}).

Proof.

By
[
27
, Theorem 4.10]
it remains to show that the bilinear form corresponding to the variational formulation (
2.4
) is coercive and bounded.
We further note that
[
27
]
states the bound with
\|g\|_{\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\Omega)}
on the right-hand side. This can be improved to the present bound by following the very same arguments in the proof which are therefore omitted.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
be given, and note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\kappa\psi\varphi+\varepsilon\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\varphi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi\cdot\nabla\varphi|\leq C(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{0,4;\Omega})\|\psi\|_{1,2;\Omega}\|\varphi\|_{1,2;\Omega},

where
C>0
is some generic constant.
Further, by our assumption on the norm of
\boldsymbol{u}
, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}|\boldsymbol{u}\psi\cdot\nabla\psi|\geq-\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\psi\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\nabla\psi\|_{0,2;\Omega}\geq-\alpha\underline{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\psi\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}.

Then, using
\kappa\geq 0
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\kappa\psi^{2}+\varepsilon|\nabla\psi|^{2}-\boldsymbol{u}\psi\cdot\nabla\psi)\geq(1-\alpha)\underline{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\psi\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\kappa\psi^{2}\geq(1-\alpha)\underline{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\psi\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2},

thus concluding the proof.
∎

We remark that the assumption
\kappa\geq 0
can be replaced by assuming that
\|\kappa\|_{0,2;\Omega}
is sufficiently small.

2.2.
Notation and approximation spaces for mixed formulations

Before we define and analyse the a regularised problem we need to introduce some spaces and notation.
In view of the functional structure of the weak formulation associated with (
2.10
) (dictated by the integrability of each term, in particular the one coming from the last term on the right-hand side of the constitutive equation (
2.10a
)), we consider the following spaces for the pseudo potential flux and double layer potential, respectively

\mathbf{H}:=\mathbf{H}_{N}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{4/3};\Omega)=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega):\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathrm{L}^{4/3}(\Omega)\ \text{and}\ \boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{N}\},\quad\mathrm{Q}:=\mathrm{L}^{4}(\Omega).

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
denote a family of non-degenerate triangular / tetrahedral meshes on
\Omega
and denote by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
the set of all facets (edges in 2D) in the mesh.
By
h_{K}
we denote the diameter of the element
K
and by
h_{F}
we denote the length/area of the facet
F
. As usual, by
h
we denote the maximum of the diameters of elements in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. For all meshes we assume that they are sufficiently regular (there exists a uniform positive constant
\eta_{1}
such that each element
K
is star-shaped with respect to a ball of radius greater than
\eta_{1}h_{K}
. It is also assumed that there exists
\eta_{2}>0
such that for each element and every facet
F\in\partial K
, we have that
h_{F}\geq\eta_{2}h_{K}
, see, e.g.,
[
17
]
).

By
\mathrm{P}_{k}(K)
we will denote the scalar space of degree up to
k
, defined locally on
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. In addition, we denote by
\mathbf{RT}_{k}(K):=[\mathrm{P}_{k}(K)]^{d}\oplus\mathrm{P}_{k}(K)\,\boldsymbol{x}
the local Raviart–Thomas space and by
\mathbf{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
its global counterpart.

Spaces
\mathbf{H}_{h}
and
\mathrm{Q}_{h}
are suitable subspaces of the functional spaces
\mathbf{H}
and
\mathrm{Q}
, respectively.
For the analysis of the discretised mixed problem below, we require kernel characterisation and discrete inf-sup conditions and so we can simply take, for example, Raviart–Thomas elements

\mathbf{H}_{h}=\mathbf{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap\mathbf{H}=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{N}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{4/3};\Omega):\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbf{RT}_{k}(K),\ \forall\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},\quad\mathrm{Q}_{h}=\mathrm{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

Note that
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{H}_{h})\subset\mathrm{Q}_{h}
which is a property that will be used in the analysis.
In particular, we even have that
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{H}_{h})=\mathrm{Q}_{h}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{P}^{k}_{h}\colon\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{Q}_{h}\subset\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
orthogonal projection.

2.3.
The perturbed regularised primal problem

In order to deal with the singular force
g\in\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\Omega)
we replace it by a regularised one,
\mathcal{Q}_{h}g
, where

\mathcal{Q}_{h}\colon\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{Q}_{h}
denotes a bounded linear operator with the properties

(2.7)

\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{2}=\mathcal{Q}_{h},\quad\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathcal{Q}_{h}}<\infty.

Additionally,
\boldsymbol{u}
is replaced by an approximation
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{4}(\Omega)
.
This yields an auxiliary problem which in its (primal) weak form reads as follows: Find
\psi^{\star}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\kappa\psi^{\star}\varphi+\varepsilon\nabla\psi^{\star}\cdot\nabla\varphi-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\psi^{\star}\cdot\nabla\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}_{h}g\varphi\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega),\qquad\psi^{\star}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\psi_{D}.

Existence and uniqueness of solutions follows as in Proposition
2.1
assuming that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}<\frac{\underline{\varepsilon}}{C_{\mathrm{Sob}}{(1+c_{P}^{2})^{1/2}}}.

Note that if
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\to\boldsymbol{u}
in the sense of
\mathrm{L}^{4}(\Omega)
when
h\to 0
, it follows that there exists a sufficiently small
h_{0}
such that (
2.9
) is satisfied for
h\leq h_{0}
provided that assumption (
2.5
) holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\psi
and
\psi^{\star}
denote the unique solutions of Problems (
2.4
) and (
2.8
), respectively.
Then, there exist constants
C_{1}>0
,
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{2}(\min_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}}),

where
\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\varepsilon\nabla\psi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi
, and, with
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}=\varepsilon\nabla\psi^{\star}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\psi^{\star}
,

\displaystyle\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{2}(\min_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}}).

Proof.

Let us start by first considering the perturbation in
\boldsymbol{u}
. Let
\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
denote the unique solution to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\kappa\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\varphi+\varepsilon\nabla\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\cdot\nabla\varphi-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\cdot\nabla\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g\varphi\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega),\qquad\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\psi_{D}.

Set
\psi_{d}=\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\kappa\psi_{d}^{2}+\varepsilon|\nabla\psi_{d}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u}\psi-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\widetilde{\psi}^{\star})\cdot\nabla\psi_{d}=\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\psi\cdot\nabla\psi_{d}+\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\psi_{d}\cdot\nabla\psi_{d}.

Noting that
\|(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\psi\cdot\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,1;\Omega}\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\psi\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,2;\Omega}
as well as

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\psi_{d}\cdot\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,1;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\psi_{d}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Using the continuity of the Sobolev embedding
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\subset\mathrm{L}^{4}(\Omega)
and assumption (
2.9
) further yields

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\psi_{d}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\leq{\alpha}\underline{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,2;\Omega},

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Together with
\|\psi\|_{0,4;\Omega}\lesssim\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}}+\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}
(Proposition
2.1
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\kappa\psi_{d}^{2}+\varepsilon|\nabla\psi_{d}|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}}+\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\alpha}\underline{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-1}/2C^{2}(\|u_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}}+\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)})^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\delta/2\|\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}+{\alpha}\underline{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}

where in the last estimate we have employed Young’s inequality with paramater
\delta>0
.
Subtracting the last two terms on the right-hand side and using that
\kappa\geq 0
, it follows that

\displaystyle(1-\alpha-\delta/2)\|\nabla\psi_{d}\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}\leq\delta^{-1}/2C^{2}(\|u_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}}+\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)})^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}^{2}.

Choosing
\delta
sufficiently small we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{d}\|_{1,2;\Omega}\lesssim(\|u_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}}+\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}.

It remains to estimate
\|\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}
.
Note that by continuous dependence on data, which can be shown as in Proposition
2.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}\lesssim\|g-\mathcal{Q}_{h}g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}.

Noting that
g=-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}+\kappa\psi^{\star}
, where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}=\varepsilon\nabla\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}
,
we have that
(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})g=-(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}+(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})\kappa\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}
.
We stress that
(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=0
for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}
, which follows from the projection property of
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
and the fact that
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathbf{H}_{h})\subset\mathrm{Q}_{h}
, hence, for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)}\frac{\langle{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})},v\rangle}{\|v\|_{1,2;\Omega}}=\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)}\frac{-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\cdot\nabla v}{\|v\|_{1,2;\Omega}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Here, we have used that
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|_{\Gamma_{N}}=0
for
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}
and the definition of the conormal derivative, which formally reads
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|_{\Gamma_{N}}=0
.
Further, by the projection and boundedness property of
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
we can assert that for any
\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})(\kappa\widetilde{\psi}^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\|\kappa\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}}.

Combining the latter estimates with the triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g-\mathcal{Q}_{h}g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\|\kappa\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\|\kappa(\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\|\kappa(\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star})\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+{\|\kappa\|_{0,2;\Omega}\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}.}

Employing the very same arguments as before we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\psi\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}.

Combining this estimate with the previous ones concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that if
\kappa\in\mathrm{L}^{4}(\Omega)
, then,
\kappa\psi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. Therefore, duality arguments and the approximation properties of
\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k}
show that

\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}\lesssim h\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi)\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

If, in addition,
\kappa
is piecewise constant, then
(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi)|_{K}=\kappa|_{K}(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})\psi|_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and therefore

\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi)\|_{0,2;\Omega}\lesssim\min_{v_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\psi-v_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}\leq\min_{v_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\psi-v_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}.

2.4.
Mixed formulation

Let us now rewrite a regularised counterpart of the strong mixed form (
2.3
):

(2.10a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\nabla\psi^{\star}-{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\psi^{\star}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.10b)

\displaystyle\kappa\psi^{\star}-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{h}g

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.10c)

\displaystyle\psi^{\star}

\displaystyle=\psi_{D}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{D},

(2.10d)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{N}.

We remark that the flux and potential
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})
depend upon discretisation—through the dependence on the discrete map
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
and on the discrete advection velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
—but they are not necessarily discrete.

Using appropriate integration by parts formulas in Banach spaces, we obtain a perturbed saddle-point problem: for given
\kappa\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{Q}_{h}g\in\mathrm{L}^{4/3}(\Omega)
, and
\psi_{D}\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})
, find
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}
such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle+b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\psi^{\star})+d_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\psi^{\star})

\displaystyle=F(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H},

\displaystyle b(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\varphi)

\displaystyle-c(\psi^{\star},\varphi)

\displaystyle=G(\varphi)

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Q},

where (again for a fixed
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
) the bilinear forms
a:\mathbf{H}\times\mathbf{H}\to\mathbb{R}
,
b:\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
,
c:\mathrm{Q}\times\mathrm{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
,
d_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}:\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
, and the linear functionals
F:\mathbf{H}\to\mathbb{R}
and
G:\mathrm{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined as

(2.12)

\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}\,,\quad b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\,,\quad c(\psi^{\star},\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\kappa\,\psi^{\star}\,\varphi\,,\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle d_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}\,\varphi\,,\quad F(\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\langle\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{n},\psi_{D}\rangle\,,\quad G(\varphi):=-\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}_{h}g\,\varphi\,,\end{array}

where
\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle
stands for the duality pairing between
\mathrm{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})
and
\mathrm{H}^{-1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})
.

Equivalently, by defining the bilinear forms
A,A_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}:(\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q})\times(\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

(2.13a)

\displaystyle A((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi))

\displaystyle:=a(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi})+b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\psi^{\star})+b(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\varphi)-c(\psi^{\star},\varphi),\quad

and

(2.13b)

\displaystyle A_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi))

\displaystyle:=A((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi))+d_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\psi^{\star}),

for all
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}
, we can rewrite (
2.11
) as

\text{find }(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}\text{ such that}

(2.14)

A_{{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi))=F(\boldsymbol{\xi})+G(\varphi)\qquad\forall(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}.

The well-posedness analysis follows from
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
, along with the Banach–Nečas–Babuška Theorem
[
17
, Theorem 2.6]
. In what follows, we set the necessary conditions on the bilinear forms to apply the aforementioned theorems. We start by proving the boundedness of the bilinear forms and functionals, which follows directly from the Cauchy–Schwarz and Hölder inequalities:

(2.15)

\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\left|a(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|\leq\|a\|\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{\mathbf{H}}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}\,,\quad\left|b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\right|\leq\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\,,\quad\left|c(\psi^{\star},\varphi)\right|\leq\|c\|\|\psi^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Q}},\\[8.61108pt]
\left|d_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\right|\leq\|d\|\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\,,\quad\left|F(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|\leq\|F\|\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}\,,\quad\left|G(\varphi)\right|\leq\|G\|\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Q}}.\end{array}

Here, the bounding constants are

\|a\|:=\frac{1}{\underline{\varepsilon}},\;\|c\|:=\|\kappa\|_{{0,2;\Omega}},\;\|d\|:=\frac{1}{\underline{\varepsilon}}\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\|_{0,4;\Omega},\;\|F\|:=C_{T}\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}},\;\|G\|:=\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}g\|_{0,4/3;\Omega},

where
C_{T}
denotes the constant from the trace inequality.

The following result establishes the inf-sup condition for the bilinear form
b(\bullet,\bullet)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists a positive constant
\beta
such that

(2.16)

\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}}\dfrac{b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)}{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\geq\beta\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Q}.

Proof.

For a given
\varphi\in\mathrm{Q}
, we set
\widehat{\varphi}:=|\varphi|^{2}\varphi
. Then, the following holds (cf.
[
24
, Lemma 2.2]
):

(2.17)

\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathrm{L}^{4/3}(\Omega){\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\widehat{\varphi}=\|\varphi\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

Now, let
z\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
be the unique solution (by the Lax–Milgram lemma) of the auxiliary problem

(2.18)

-\Delta z=\widehat{\varphi}\quad\text{in }\Omega\,,\qquad z=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\,,\qquad\nabla z\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{N}.

Using Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding and Poincaré’s inequality we obtain the bound

(2.19)

\|\nabla z\|_{0,2;\Omega}\leq C_{\mathrm{Sob}}(1+c_{P}^{2})^{1/2}\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}.

Then, we define
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}:=-\nabla z\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, and observe that (see (
2.18
))

\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}=-\Delta z=\widehat{\varphi}
in
\Omega
, with
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|_{\Gamma_{N}}=0
. Hence,
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\mathbf{H}
, and combined with (
2.19
), this implies

\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\|_{\mathbf{H}}\leq\left({C_{\mathrm{Sob}}(1+c_{P}^{2})^{1/2}}+1\right)\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}.

Finally, using the definition of
b(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf. (
2.12
)), the second part of (
2.17
), and the last estimate, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}}\dfrac{b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)}{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\geq\dfrac{b(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}},\varphi)}{\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\geq\dfrac{\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}}{({C_{\mathrm{Sob}}(1+c_{P}^{2})^{1/2}}+1)\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}}\geq\beta\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Q}},

with
\beta:=\left({C_{\mathrm{Sob}}(1+c_{P}^{2})^{1/2}}+1\right)^{-1}
. ∎

Now, let
\mathbf{B}:\mathbf{H}\rightarrow\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{B}^{t}:\mathrm{Q}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
be the bounded linear operators induced by
b(\bullet,\bullet)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{\xi})(\varphi)

\displaystyle:=b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H},\,\,\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Q}

\displaystyle{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\mathbf{B}^{t}(\varphi)(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle:=b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Q},\,\,\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H},

we have the following kernel characterisations

(2.20a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{ker}(\mathbf{B})=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}:b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)=0\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Q}\}=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}:\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}=0\quad\mathrm{in}\ \Omega\},

(2.20b)

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{ker}(\mathbf{B}^{t})=\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Q}:b(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)=0\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H}\}=\{0\}.

On the other hand, it is clear that the bilinear forms
a(\bullet,\bullet)
and
c(\bullet,\bullet)
are symmetric. In addition, from the definition of
\mathbf{V}
and the boundedness of the permittivity, it readily follows that

(2.21)

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\xi})\geq\alpha_{a}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{V}\,,\qquad c(\varphi,\varphi)\geq 0\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Q},

with
\alpha_{a}:=\dfrac{1}{{\overline{\varepsilon}}}
.
The non-negativity of the bilinear form
c(\bullet,\bullet)
follows from the assumption
\kappa\geq 0
.

From the bounds of the involved bilinear forms (
2.15
), the inf-sup condition of the bilinear form
b(\bullet,\bullet)
in Lemma
2.4
, the characterisation of the kernel in (
2.20
), the ellipticity of
a(\bullet,\bullet)
on the kernel of
\mathbf{B}
(
2.21
), and using that
c(\bullet,\bullet)
is symmetric and positive semidefinite, we have that the hypotheses of
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
are satisfied for
A(\bullet,\bullet)
, implying that there exists a constant
\alpha_{A}>0
such that

(2.22)

\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\dfrac{{A}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi))}{\|(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}}\geq\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\quad\forall(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}},

with
\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}:=\dfrac{1}{2\tilde{c}}
, where
\tilde{c}:=\max\{c_{3},c_{4}\}
, and

(2.23)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{{\alpha_{a}}}+\frac{1}{\beta}\left(1+\frac{\|a\|}{{\alpha_{a}}}\right)+\frac{\|c\|}{\beta^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\|a\|}{{\alpha_{a}}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(1+\frac{\|a\|}{{\alpha_{a}}}\right)\left\{1+\frac{\|a\|}{\beta}+\frac{\|a\|^{2}\|c\|}{\beta^{3}}\left(1+\frac{\|a\|}{{\alpha_{a}}}\right)\right\},

\displaystyle c_{3}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{{\alpha_{a}}}+\frac{1}{\beta}\left(1+\frac{\|a\|}{{\alpha_{a}}}\right)\left\{1+\left(1+\frac{\|a\|}{\beta}\right)\left(2\|c\|\max\{c_{1},c_{2}\}\right)^{1/2}\right\},

\displaystyle c_{4}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(1+\frac{\|a\|}{{\alpha_{a}}}\right)\left(1+\frac{\|a\|}{\beta}\right)\left\{1+\left(2\|c\|\max\{c_{1},c_{2}\}\right)^{1/2}\right\}.

Moreover, using the bound of the bilinear form
d_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf. (
2.15
)), the definition of the bilinear forms
A(\bullet,\bullet)
and
A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf. (
2.13a
) and (
2.13b
)), and the global inf-sup condition (
2.22
), we can readily obtain

(2.24)

\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\dfrac{A_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi))}{\|(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}}\geq\left\{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}-\|a\|\,\|{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\right\}\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\quad\forall(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}.

Then, assuming that (see also Remark
2.6
below)

(2.25)

\|{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\leq\frac{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}{2\|a\|},

a global inf-sup stability is established for
A_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}
:

(2.26)

\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\dfrac{A_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi))}{\|(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}}\geq\frac{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}{2}\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\quad\forall(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}.

Similarly, by the symmetry of
A(\bullet,\bullet)
and under the same assumption on
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
(
2.25
), we obtain

(2.27)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{A_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi))}{\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}}\geq\frac{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}{2}\|(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\quad\forall(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varphi)\in{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}.

In this way, we are now able to establish the well-posedness of (
2.11
) (equivalently (
2.14
)).

Theorem 2.5
.

Given
{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\in\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)
satisfying (
2.25
), there exists a unique
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}
solution to (
2.11
), and there holds

(2.28)

\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{\mathbf{H}}+\|\psi^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\leq\frac{2}{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}\max\{1,C_{T}\}\left\{\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}}+\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}g\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\right\}.

Proof.

Since (
2.26
) and (
2.27
) guarantee that
A_{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}}(\bullet,\bullet)
satisfies the hypotheses of the BNB Theorem
[
17
, Theorem 2.6]
, the proof becomes a direct application of this theorem. Hence, the estimate (
2.28
) follows from (
2.14
) together with the boundedness of
F(\bullet)
and
G(\bullet)
(cf. (
2.15
)).
∎

Remark 2.6
.

We note here that the mixed formulation (
2.10
) requires
\|u_{h}\|<\frac{\underline{\varepsilon}}{4C^{*}}
, whereas the primal problem (
2.2
) only assumes
\|u_{h}\|<\frac{\underline{\varepsilon}}{C}
. The constant
C
depends on
\Omega
, as well as the Sobolev and Poincaré constants, while
C^{*}
additionally depends on
\kappa
, the permittivity bounds, and the inf–sup constant
\beta
. Hence, the mixed smallness condition (
2.25
) is more restrictive.

3.
Discrete problem

Let us denote by
\mathbf{M}_{h}
a finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)
(for example,
[\mathrm{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})]^{d}
), and consider for this section that the given vector field
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{M}_{h}
.

Following the usual approach, we obtain the Galerkin discretisation of (
2.10a
)-(
2.10b
) as a perturbed saddle-point problem that looks for
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})

\displaystyle+b(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\psi_{h})+d_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=F(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h},

\displaystyle b(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle-c(\psi_{h},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=G(\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle\forall\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h},

In order to analyse the discrete solvability of (
3.1
) we use the abstract discrete theory from
[
13
, Theorem 3.5]
.

We remark that, for our particular choice of Raviart–Thomas elements (and similarly for other finite elements such as the Brezzi–Douglas–Marini (BDM) elements), the following property holds. Since we will later need a more explicit characterisation of the kernel of the discrete divergence operator, denoted by
\mathbf{V}_{h}
, this property will play a key role

(3.2)

\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathbf{H}_{h}\subseteq\mathrm{Q}_{h}.

Let us recall some preliminary results. First, we consider the quasi-interpolation operator defined in
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
,

\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}}:\mathbf{H}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}_{h},

which satisfies the following properties:

•

For all
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}
, we have

(3.3a)

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}.

•

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, there exists
C_{\mathsf{q}}
, independent of
h
, such that

(3.3b)

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}}\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{0,p;\Omega}\leq C_{\mathsf{q}}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{0,p;\Omega},

for all
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)
.

•

The following commuting property (cf.
[
12
, Section 5.3]
) holds

(3.4)

\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}}\boldsymbol{\xi})={\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\,\boldsymbol{\xi})\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H},

where
\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k}:\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathrm{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is is the usual orthogonal projector
with respect to the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
-inner product, that is, given
w\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{P}^{k}_{h}(w)
is the unique element in
\mathrm{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
satisfying

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{P}^{k}_{h}(w)\,q_{h}\,=\,\int_{\Omega}w\,q_{h}\qquad\forall\,q_{h}\in\mathrm{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\,.

The following result can be found in

[
24
, Eq. (4.4)]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
-orthogonal projector onto the piecewise polynomial space
\mathrm{Q}_{h}
is bounded as a mapping from
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

In order to analyse the discrete solvability of (
3.1
) we use
[
13
, Theorem 3.5]
. We begin by considering
\mathbf{B}_{h}:\mathbf{H}_{h}\to\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{t}:\mathrm{Q}_{h}\to\mathbf{H}_{h}^{\prime}
to be the discrete bounded linear operators induced by
b(\bullet,\bullet)
, that is

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{h}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})(\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle:=b(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h},\;\forall\,\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h},

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{h}^{t}(\varphi_{h})(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})

\displaystyle:=b(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle\forall\,\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h},\;\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}.

Using the property (
3.2
) and proceeding similarly as in the continuous case, we have that the following discrete kernel characterisations hold

(3.5a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\ker(\mathbf{B}_{h})=\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}:b(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})=0,\quad\forall\,\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}\right\}\equiv\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}:\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}=0\right\},

(3.5b)

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\ker(\mathbf{B}_{h}^{t})=\left\{\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}:b(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})=0,\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\right\}\equiv\{0\},

thus
\mathbf{V}_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{V}
, and from (
2.21
), we obtain

(3.6)

a(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})\geq\alpha_{a}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},\qquad c(\varphi_{h},\varphi_{h})\geq 0\quad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h},

with
\alpha_{a}
the same constant as in (
2.21
).

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a positive constant
\beta_{d}>0
, independent of h, such that

(3.7)

\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}}\dfrac{b(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})}{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\geq\beta_{d}\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\qquad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}.

Proof.

We first invoke the continuous inf-sup condition (cf. Lemma
2.4
) where for a given
\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}\subseteq\mathrm{Q}
, we have
\bar{\varphi}_{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{4/3}(\Omega)
such that there exists
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbf{H},
with
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}=\bar{\varphi}_{h}
and

(3.8)

\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\|\bar{\varphi}_{h}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}.

Next, we take
\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}
to be the quasi-interpolation of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
(i.e.
\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}}\boldsymbol{\xi}=\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}
). Then, using (
3.3b
) and the definition of the
\mathbf{H}
-norm, we obtain

(3.9)

\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{0;\Omega}\leq C_{\mathsf{q}}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{0;\Omega}\leq C_{\mathsf{q}}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}.

On the other hand, by applying the commuting property of
\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}}
(cf. (
3.4
), for
p=4
), we have

(3.10)

\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}}\boldsymbol{\xi}=\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}),

which together with (
3.9
), (
3.8
), the continuity of
\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k}
(with
C_{\mathcal{P}}>0
, independent of
h
), the definition of the
\mathbf{H}
-norm, and (
3.8
), gives

(3.11)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}=\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{{0,2;\Omega}}+\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}\leq\frac{C_{\mathsf{q}}}{\beta}\|\bar{\varphi}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}+\|\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}\\
\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C_{\mathsf{q}}}{\beta}\|\bar{\varphi}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}+C_{\mathcal{P}}\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}\leq\frac{C_{\mathsf{q}}}{\beta}\|\bar{\varphi}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}+C_{\mathcal{P}}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathbf{H}}\leq\left(\frac{C_{\mathsf{q}}+C_{\mathcal{P}}}{\beta}\right)\|\bar{\varphi}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}.\end{array}

Finally, according to the definition of
b(\bullet,\bullet)
and (
3.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{b(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})}{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{b(\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h},\varphi_{h})}{\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\,=\,\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{h}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}}{\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\,=\,\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{h}\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi})}{\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\,=\,\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{h}\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k}(\bar{\varphi}_{h})}{\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}

\displaystyle=\,\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{h}\bar{\varphi}_{h}}{\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\,\geq\,\frac{\beta}{C_{\mathsf{q}}+C_{\mathcal{P}}}\frac{\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\|\bar{\varphi}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}}{\|\bar{\varphi}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}}\,=\,\frac{\beta}{C_{\mathsf{q}}+C_{\mathcal{P}}}\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{Q}},

with
\beta_{d}:=\dfrac{\beta}{C_{\mathsf{q}}+C_{\mathcal{P}}}
.
∎

Consequently, by applying
[
13
, Theorem 3.5]
directly, we obtain that the discrete global inf–sup condition for
A(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf. (
2.13a
)) holds with
\alpha_{\mathcal{A},d}>0
, where the constant depends only on
{\alpha},\,\beta_{d},\,\|a\|
, and
\|c\|
(cf.(
2.23
)). As a result, we have the following discrete analogue of (
2.26
), that is

(3.12)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}\\
(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{A_{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h}))}{\|(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}}\geq\left\{\alpha_{\mathcal{A},d}-\|a\|\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\right\}\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}},

for all
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
.
And under the following discrete version of the data assumption

(3.13)

\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\leq\frac{\alpha_{\mathcal{A},d}}{2\|a\|},

we obtain

(3.14)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{A_{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h}))}{\|(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}}\geq\frac{\alpha_{A,d}}{2}\,\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\qquad\forall(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}.

As a consequence of the above bound, we are now in a position to establish the solvability of (
3.1
) (equivalently, the discrete counterpart of (
2.14
)).

Theorem 3.3
.

Given
{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\in\mathbf{M}_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)
satisfying
\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\leq\frac{\alpha_{\mathcal{A},d}}{2\|a\|}
, there exists a unique
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
solution to (
3.1
), and there holds

(3.15)

\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}+\|\psi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\leq\frac{2}{\alpha_{\mathcal{A},d}}\max\{1,C_{T}\}\left\{\|\psi_{D}\|_{\frac{1}{2},00;\Gamma_{D}}+\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}g\|_{0,\frac{4}{3};\Omega}\right\}.

Proof.

The proof follows from (
3.14
) and from the fact that, in finite-dimensional linear problems, surjectivity and injectivity are equivalent. In addition, (
3.15
) is obtained by using arguments similar to those in Theorem
2.5
.
∎

4.
Error estimates

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star}),(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})
be the unique solutions of (
2.11
) and (
3.1
), respectively. Then, and assuming that (
3.13
) holds, there exists
\hat{C}>0
, independent of
h
, such that

(4.1)

\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}+\|\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\displaystyle\leq\hat{C}\left\{\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\mathbf{H}_{h})+\mathrm{dist}(\psi^{\star},\mathrm{Q}_{h})\right\}.

Proof.

The proof follows the same approach as
[
6
, Theorem 4.7]
. For a given
(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h},\hat{\varphi}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
, we decompose the errors as

(4.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}}:=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}}+\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}}

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h})+(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}),

\displaystyle e_{\psi^{\star}}:=\eta_{\psi^{\star}}+\chi_{\psi^{\star}}

\displaystyle=(\psi^{\star}-\hat{\varphi}_{h})+(\hat{\varphi}_{h}-\psi_{h}).

Next, we apply the Galerkin orthogonality property to
A_{\boldsymbol{u}}(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf. (
2.13b
) and (
2.14
))
and to its corresponding discrete formulation and substitute the decomposition of
\psi^{\star}
from (
4.2
), to obtain

(4.3)

A\bigl((\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},e_{\psi^{\star}}),(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\bigr)+d_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h};e_{\psi^{\star}})=0.

Then, using the definition of
A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\bullet,\bullet)
in the above equality, together with (
4.2
), we get

A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\bigl((\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\chi_{\psi^{\star}}),(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\bigr)=-A\bigl((\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\eta_{\psi^{\star}}),(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\bigr)-d_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\eta_{\psi^{\star}}).

Moreover, by expanding
A(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf.(
2.13a
)), we have for all

(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}

A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\bigl((\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\chi_{\psi^{\star}}),(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\bigr)=-a(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h})-b(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\varphi_{h})-b(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\eta_{\psi^{\star}})-c(\eta_{\psi^{\star}},\varphi_{h})-d_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\eta_{\psi^{\star}}).

We now bound the right-hand side using the continuity of

a(\bullet,\bullet)
,
b(\bullet,\bullet)
,
c(\bullet,\bullet)
, and
d_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf. (
2.15
)),
the smallness assumption on
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
(
3.13
), this gives

|A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\bigl((\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\chi_{\psi^{\star}}),(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\bigr)|\leq\max\!\left\{1,\|a\|,\|c\|,\tfrac{\alpha_{\mathcal{A},d}}{2}\right\}\,\bigl\|(\eta_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\eta_{\psi^{\star}})\bigr\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\,\bigl\|(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})\bigr\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}.

For the left-hand side, we take the supremum over
(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\varphi_{h})
, and apply the discrete inf-sup condition of
A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf. (
3.14
)), which gives

(4.4)

\|(\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\chi_{\psi^{\star}})\|\leq\tfrac{2}{\alpha_{\mathcal{A},d}}\max\!\left\{1,\|a\|,\|c\|,\tfrac{\alpha_{\mathcal{A},d}}{2}\right\}\|(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\eta_{\psi^{\star}})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}.

Finally, combining (
4.2
), the triangle inequality, and (
4.4
), we obtain

\bigl\|(\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},e_{\psi^{\star}})\bigr\|\lesssim\bigl\|(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}},\eta_{\psi^{\star}})\bigr\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}},

and since
(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h},\hat{\varphi}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
is arbitrary, the result follows.
∎

We are in a position to state our main result. It is a perturbed quasi-optimality result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
g\in\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}(\Omega)
and
\psi_{D}\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D})
be given.
Suppose that
\psi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
solves (
2.2
) and set
\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\nabla\psi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\psi)
solves (
2.3
).
If
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
is the solution of (
3.1
),
then,

\displaystyle\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\min_{(v_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}(\|\psi-v_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega})

(4.5)

\displaystyle\qquad+(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+{\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}}.

Proof.

Let
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}
denote the unique solution of (
2.11
). From Theorem
4.1
it follows that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{H}}+\|\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(1-\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}})\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{\mathbf{H}}+\|\psi^{\star}-\varphi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

for all
\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}
.
Since
\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}}
is a projection, using the properties (
3.3a
)–(
3.4
) we obtain for any
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}
that

\displaystyle\|(1-\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}})\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{\mathbf{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}_{\mathsf{q}})(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi^{\star}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}g)\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

\displaystyle=\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi^{\star})\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi)\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa(\psi-\psi^{\star}))\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

Using Lemma
3.1
we stress that for any
\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi)\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi-\chi_{h})\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\lesssim\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega},

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})(\kappa\psi-\kappa\psi^{\star})\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\kappa(\psi-\psi^{\star})\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\lesssim\|\kappa\|_{0,2;\Omega}\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,4;\Omega}.

Combining all of the estimates above, together with the triangle inequality, shows that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi-\varphi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+{\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,4;\Omega},

for all
(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
and
\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}
.
Finally, the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega},

and an application of Lemma
2.2
and the estimate (
2.6
) conclude the proof.
∎

5.
Stenberg-type postprocessing

In this section we provide a postprocessing technique that delivers an approximate solution to
\psi^{\star}
with asymptotic better accuracy than the approximate solution
\psi_{h}
. We follow

[
22
, Section 4]
for the low-regular forcing case (see also, e.g.,
[
11
]
for postprocessing of mixed formulations in
\mathrm{L}^{p}
spaces).
The analytical results rely on duality arguments and regularity properties of solutions to the dual problem.
For the latter we assume a regularity shift. In order to utilise the additional regularity we require an additional non-standard approximation property for the dual of the projection operator
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
.
In subsection
5.1
we show how to construct such an operator.
Then, we present duality results, the adapted Stenberg postprocessing scheme, and accuracy enhancement results in Subsection
5.2
.

5.1.
Construction of
\mathcal{Q}_{h}

We first recall from
[
22
,
21
]
the construction of the weighted Clément quasi-interpolator
J_{h}:\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{P}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}({\Omega})
.
Let
V_{0}
denote the set of interior vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and let
V_{N}
denote the set of vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
on the boundary
\Gamma\setminus\overline{\Gamma_{D}}
.
We assume that each
z\in V_{N}
has a neighbouring interior node
z_{0}\in V_{0}
in the sense that there exists
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
with
z,z_{0}\in V_{K}
where
V_{K}
denotes the set of vertices of
K
.

Given the nodal basis
\{\eta_{z}\}_{z\in V_{0}\cup V_{N}}
of
\mathrm{P}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}({\Omega})
, i.e.,
\eta_{z}(z^{\prime})=\delta_{z,z^{\prime}}
for all
z,z^{\prime}\in{V_{0}\cup V_{N}}
, the quasi-interpolator
J_{h}
is defined by

v\mapsto J_{h}(v)=\sum_{z\in V_{0}}(\int_{\Omega}v\psi_{z})\eta_{z},\quad\text{for }v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega).

For an interior vertex
z\in V_{0}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{z}
denote the patch of all elements sharing the node
z\in V_{0}
.
For a boundary vertex
z\in V_{N}
, we set
\mathcal{T}_{z}=\mathcal{T}_{z_{0}}
.
Further,
\{\psi_{z}\}_{z\in V_{0}\cup V_{N}}

is a collection of weight functions given by

\psi_{z}|_{K}=\begin{cases}\tfrac{\alpha_{z,K}}{|K|}&K\in{\mathcal{T}_{z}},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For each
z\in V_{0}\cup V_{N}
,
the real values
(\alpha_{z,K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{z}}
are chosen such that
\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{z}}\alpha_{z,K}\,s_{K}=z
,
\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{z}}\alpha_{z,K}=1
, where
s_{K}
denotes the centre of mass of
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Due to the simple geometric observation that not all centre of mass are colinear resp. coplanar, it follows that there exist
(\alpha_{z,K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{z}}
satisfying the above relations, though they might not be unique, see
[
22
, Remark 10]
.
If
z\in V_{0}
then we can even choose all
\alpha_{z,K}
,
K\in\mathcal{T}_{z}
to be non-negative and
\|\psi_{z}\|_{0,\infty;\Omega}\simeq\tfrac{1}{|\Omega_{z}|}
, see
[
22
]
.
If
z\in V_{N}
, then some coefficients might be negative whilst others are positive. Anyway, we can always choose them such that
\|\psi_{z}\|_{0,\infty;\Omega}\simeq\tfrac{1}{|\Omega_{z}|}
holds true as well.
Note that, by the above definition, we have
\psi_{z}\in\mathrm{P}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

We also denote by

\eta_{K}=\Pi_{z\in V_{K}}\eta_{z}
an element bubble function.
Further, let
\eta_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,N:=\dim(\mathrm{Q}_{h})
denote a basis of of
\mathrm{Q}_{h}=\mathrm{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

and define the bubble function space

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{k}^{b}(K)=\{\eta_{K}\eta_{j}\,|\,j=1,\dots,N\}\subset\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(K).

Let
\chi_{K,j}\in\mathrm{P}_{k}^{b}(K)
(
j=1,\dots,N
) denote a dual basis in the sense that

\displaystyle\int_{K}\chi_{K,j}\eta_{k}=\delta_{jk},\quad j,k=1,\dots,N.

Further, we define the bounded map

B_{h}v=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{K}(v\eta_{j})\chi_{K,j}.

With these ingredients, the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
is defined as follows

\mathcal{Q}_{h}=J_{h}^{\prime}+(1-J_{h})^{\prime}B_{h}^{\prime}:{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}\to\mathrm{Q}_{h}=\mathrm{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\subset{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}.

We now prove some properties of the map
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
. The arguments closely follow the proofs of
[
22
, Theorem 11 and 12]
and
[
21
, Theorem 18]
and we therefore only focus on essential differences.

Lemma 5.1

(Further properties of
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
)
.

In addition to the properties (
2.7
), the map
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime})\phi\|_{0,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|\nabla\phi\|_{0,2;\Omega}\quad\forall\phi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime})\phi\|_{1,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}\|\phi\|_{1+s,2;\Omega}\quad\forall\phi\in\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega),\ s\in[0,1].

Proof.

First, let us verify the projection property.
By construction we have that
\int_{K}B_{h}(1-J_{h})v\eta_{j}=\int_{K}(1-J_{h})v\eta_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,N
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}_{h}\phi v=\int_{\Omega}\phi(J_{h}v+B_{h}(1-J_{h})v)=\int_{\Omega}\phi v\quad\forall\phi\in\mathrm{Q}_{h},v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega).

That is,
\mathcal{Q}_{h}\phi=\phi
for all
\phi\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}
.

Second, we prove boundedness of
\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime}
and therefore
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
as an operator from
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
to
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
To do so, note that by standard scaling arguments one proves that
\|B_{h}v\|_{0,2;K}\lesssim\|v\|_{0,2;K}
for all
v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(K)
.
Then, with the arguments from
[
22
, Theorem 11]
it follows that

\displaystyle\|J_{h}v\|_{0,2;K}\lesssim\|v\|_{0,2;\Omega_{K}}

where
\Omega_{K}\subset\Omega
is the domain associated to the patch of
K
if
K
does not contain vertices from
V_{N}
or the domain associated to the second-order patch of
K
if
K
contains vertices of
V_{N}
. Combining the latter estimates we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime}v\|_{0,2;K}\lesssim\|v\|_{0,2;\Omega_{K}}\quad\text{and further}\quad\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime}v\|_{0,2;\Omega}\lesssim\|v\|_{0,2;\Omega}\quad\forall v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega).

Third, we stress that by construction
J_{h}
preserves affine functions (on patches). Therefore, see
[
22
, Theorem 11]
,
this implies that

\displaystyle\|(1-J_{h})v\|_{0,2;K}+h_{K}\|\nabla(1-J_{h})v\|_{0,2;K}\lesssim h_{K}\|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Omega_{K}},\quad\forall v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega).

as well as

\displaystyle\|(1-J_{h})v\|_{0,2;K}+h_{K}\|\nabla(1-J_{h})v\|_{0,2;K}\lesssim h_{K}^{2}\|D^{2}v\|_{0,2;\Omega_{K}},\quad\forall v\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega).

Together with an inverse inequality we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\nabla Q_{h}^{\prime}v\|_{0,2;K}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla J_{h}v\|_{0,2;K}+\|\nabla B_{h}(1-J_{h})v\|_{0,2;K}\lesssim\|\nabla J_{h}v\|_{0,2;K}+h_{K}^{-1}\|B_{h}(1-J_{h})v\|_{0,2;K}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{0,2;\Omega_{K}}+h_{K}^{-1}\|(1-J_{h})v\|_{0,2;K}\lesssim\|v\|_{0,2;\Omega_{K}}\quad\forall v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega).

We conclude that
\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
is bounded which implies the boundedness of
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
in the dual norm.

Finally, to see the stated approximation properties we stress that
\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime}
preserves, such as
J_{h}
, affine functions on patches. Therefore, with the very same arguments to show the approximation properties for
J_{h}
can be used to show the ones for
\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime}
.
The estimate in the non-integer Sobolev spaces follows by interpolation, which in turn finishes the proof.
∎

Alternative construction of
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
for
k>0
.

For higher polynomial orders (
k>0
) one can choose
J_{h}
to be, e.g., the Scott–Zhang quasi-interpolator into
\mathrm{P}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
in the definitions above.

5.2.
Adjoint problem and accuracy enhancement

Let us first develop Aubin–Nitsche-type estimates.
To that end, consider the adjoint problem

(5.1a)

\displaystyle\kappa\phi-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\varepsilon\nabla\phi-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla\phi

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(5.1b)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{D},

(5.1c)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\nabla\phi\cdot\boldsymbol{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{N}.

We make the following standing assumption throughout this section:
Suppose that
\kappa
,
\varepsilon
,
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
, and
\Omega
are such that there exists
0<s_{\Omega}\leq 1
,
C_{\Omega}>0
such that for any
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the unique weak solution of (
5.1
) satisfies

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\|\varepsilon\nabla\phi\|_{s_{\Omega},2;\Omega}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{1+s_{\Omega},2;\Omega}\leq C_{\Omega}\|f\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\psi
and
\psi^{\star}
denote the unique solutions of Problems (
2.4
) and (
2.8
), respectively.
Then, there exist constants
C_{1}>0
,
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{2}h^{s_{\Omega}}\biggl(\min_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}\biggr)

where
\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\varepsilon\nabla\psi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi
.

Proof.

We write
\psi-\psi^{\star}=(\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star})+(\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star})
where
\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}
is defined as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
Recall that in the proof we have shown that

\displaystyle\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}\lesssim(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}.

By the Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality, the term
\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}
can be estimated by the same upper bound.

Consider the solution
\phi\in\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}(\Omega)
of the adjoint problem (
5.1
) with
f=\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star}
. Then, integrating by parts twice, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star})(-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\varepsilon\nabla\phi)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla\phi+\kappa\phi)=\langle(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})g,\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})g,(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime})\phi\rangle\leq\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})g\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{-1}(\Omega)}\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime})\phi\|_{1,2;\Omega}.

Here, we have used the projection property
(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})=(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})
.
The last term is further estimated using the approximation property of
\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{\prime}
and the elliptic regularity assumption (
5.2
) leading to

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}\lesssim\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})g\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{-1}(\Omega)}h^{s_{\Omega}}\|\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Putting all estimates together we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}\leq\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+h^{s_{\Omega}}\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})g\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{-1}(\Omega)}.

Estimating
\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})g\|_{\mathrm{H}_{D}^{-1}(\Omega)}
as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
gives

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{Q}_{h})g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\kappa\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\kappa(\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\kappa(\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star})\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}
,
\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}
, and with
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}=\varepsilon\nabla\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}
.
Finally,
\|\kappa(\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star})\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\leq\|\kappa\|_{0,2;\Omega}\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\lesssim\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}
.
The proof is concluded by noting that as in Lemma
2.2

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}^{\star}\|_{1,2;\Omega}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

and
h^{s_{\Omega}}\lesssim 1
.
∎

Additionally to the assumption on the regularity shift for the adjoint problem (
5.1
) we suppose that the solution to the adjoint problem enjoys the approximation estimate

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})(\kappa\phi-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla\phi)\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\leq Ch^{s_{\Omega}}\|\phi\|_{1+s_{\Omega},2;\Omega}.

To see that the latter estimate is likely to hold, consider for simplicity that
\kappa
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
are piecewise constants. Then,

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})(\kappa\phi-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla\phi)\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\leq\|\kappa\|_{0,2;\Omega}\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})\phi\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})\nabla\phi\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Employing the regularity shift assumption for the adjoint problem and a Sobolev embedding we conclude that

\displaystyle\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})\phi\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})\nabla\phi\|_{0,2;\Omega}\lesssim h^{s_{\Omega}}(\|\phi\|_{s_{\Omega},4;\Omega}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{s_{\Omega},2;\Omega})\lesssim h^{s_{\Omega}}\|\phi\|_{1+s_{\Omega},2;\Omega}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\psi^{\star}
denote the unique solution of Problem (
2.8
) and let

(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\phi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
denote the discrete solution of (
3.1
).
Then, there exists constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0}(\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s_{\Omega}}\Big(\min_{(v_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}(\|\psi-v_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega})

\displaystyle\qquad+(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\Big),

where
\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\varepsilon\nabla\psi-\boldsymbol{u}\psi
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{H}_{D}^{1}(\Omega)
denote the unique solution of (
5.1
) with

f:=\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0}(\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}).

Set
\boldsymbol{r}=-\varepsilon\nabla\phi
and note that
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathrm{L}^{4/3}(\Omega)
, hence,
\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathbf{H}
.
We have that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h})f=\int_{\Omega}(\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h})(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{r}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}+\kappa\phi)

\displaystyle=A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}),(\boldsymbol{r},\phi)),

where in the last identity we used that
\varepsilon\nabla\phi+\boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{0}
and, therefore,
\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})(\varepsilon\nabla\phi+\boldsymbol{r})=0
.
Employing Galerkin orthogonality we further obtain with continuity of
A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}(\cdot,\cdot)
that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=A_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}((\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}),(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{h},\phi-\phi_{h}))

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\|(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{h},\phi-\phi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}

for all
(\boldsymbol{r}_{h},\phi_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
. For the remainder of the proof we consider
\boldsymbol{r}_{h}=\Pi^{\mathsf{RT}}\boldsymbol{r}
and
\phi_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0}\phi
.
First, observe that

\displaystyle\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min_{(v_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}(\|\psi-v_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega})

\displaystyle\quad+(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega},

which has already be shown in the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

It remains to estimate
\|(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{h},\phi-\phi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}
.
Clearly, by assumption (
5.2
) and the Sobolev embedding,

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\lesssim h^{s_{\Omega}}\|\phi\|_{1+s_{\Omega},2;\Omega}\lesssim h^{s_{\Omega}}\|f\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Note that

\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{h})={(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{r}={(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})}(f+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla\phi-\kappa\phi)={(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla\phi-\kappa\phi),

with the latter identity following from the fact that
f
is piecewise constant.
Employing assumption (
5.3
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{h})\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

\displaystyle=\|{(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{k})}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla\phi-\kappa\phi)\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla\phi-\kappa\phi)\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\lesssim h^{s_{\Omega}}\|f\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Putting the last two estimates together we infer that

\displaystyle\|(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{h},\phi-\phi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\lesssim h^{s_{\Omega}}\|f\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Overall, we see that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{0,2;\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}\|(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{h},\phi-\phi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathrm{Q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s_{\Omega}}\|f\|_{0,2;\Omega}\Big(\min_{(v_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}(\|\psi-v_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\Big),

which gives the stipulated estimate.
∎

Let us now consider a discrete auxiliary local problem on each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
(which arises from the pseudo potential flux constitutive equation) similarly to the classical Stenberg postprocess
[
30
]
, but adapted to our case with advecting velocity. We define
\psi^{\sharp}_{h}(K)\in\mathrm{P}_{k+1}(K)
by

(5.4)

\begin{split}\int_{K}\varepsilon\nabla\psi^{\sharp}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}&=\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\int_{K}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h})\psi_{h}\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\mathrm{P}_{k+1}(K),\\
\Pi_{K}^{0}\psi^{\sharp}_{h}&=\Pi_{K}^{0}\psi_{h}.\end{split}

Theorem 5.4
.

Under the aforegoing assumptions,

\displaystyle\|\psi-\psi^{\sharp}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle+Ch^{s_{\Omega}}\Big(\min_{{(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},v_{h})}\in\mathbf{H}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}(\|\psi-v_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega})

\displaystyle\qquad+(\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{D}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{D}\|_{1/2,00;\Gamma_{D}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\min_{\chi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|\kappa\psi-\chi_{h}\|_{0,4/3;\Omega}\Big)

\displaystyle+Ch\min_{v_{h}\in\mathrm{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\nabla(\psi-v_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\|\psi-\psi^{\sharp}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}\leq\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0}(\psi^{\star}-\psi^{\sharp}_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})(\psi^{\star}-\psi^{\sharp}_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}.

The first term on the right-hand side is estimated with Lemma
5.2
. For the second term
we recall that
\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0}\psi^{\sharp}_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0}\psi_{h}
by construction of the postprocessed solution. Then, we employ Lemma
5.3
.
For the last term, note that
\|(1-\mathcal{P}_{h}^{0})(\psi^{\star}-\psi^{\sharp}_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}\lesssim h\|\nabla(\psi^{\star}-\psi^{\sharp}_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}
.
Some standard arguments then show that with
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}=\varepsilon\nabla\psi^{\star}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\psi^{\star}

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\psi^{\star}-\psi^{\sharp}_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}\lesssim\min_{v_{h}\in\mathrm{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\nabla(\psi^{\star}-v_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}.

A triangle inequality and uniform boundedness of
\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}
further yields

\displaystyle\min_{v_{h}\in\mathrm{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\nabla(\psi^{\star}-v_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\|\psi^{\star}-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min_{v_{h}\in\mathrm{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\nabla(\psi-v_{h})\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\star}\|_{0,2;\Omega}+\|\psi-\psi^{\star}\|_{0,4;\Omega}.

The last two terms are estimated by Lemma
2.2
.

Combining all the results and noting that
h\lesssim h^{s_{\Omega}}
concludes the proof.
∎

Note that if
s_{\Omega}=1
, and
(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\psi)
is sufficiently smooth, as well as
\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+2})
, then,

(5.5)

\|\psi-\psi^{\sharp}_{h}\|_{0,2;\Omega}=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+2}),

by the last theorem.

In this test we examine the behaviour of the postprocess without the regularisation. For that we follow
[
20
, Section 6.3]
and use the domain
\Omega=(-1,1)^{2}

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
a projection of

\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathrm{ex}}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}x)\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}y)\\
-\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}x)\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}y)\end{pmatrix},

and the manufactured potential

\psi_{\mathrm{ex}}(x,y)=x|x|^{65/128}(1-x^{2})(1-y^{2}),

with the now space-dependent coefficients

\varepsilon(x,y)=\exp(-xy),\quad\kappa(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}+\sin(xy)^{2}.

With these functions the load is such
g_{\mathrm{ex}}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
but
g_{\mathrm{ex}}\notin\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega)
for
s\geq\frac{1}{128}

g_{\mathrm{ex}}
directly without
\mathcal{Q}_{h}

\mathcal{Q}_{h}

O(h)

\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
norm of the flux and for the
\mathrm{L}^{4}(\Omega)
norm of the potential, whereas for the postprocessed solution we obtain approximately
O(h^{5/3})
and
O(h^{2})
for the methods without and with regularisation
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
, respectively. The regularised method is then appropriate to use even if there is no need to regularise the load.

Example 3.

Finally, we address the advection diffusion problem with a line Dirac delta function as the source term, which can be relevant not only in the Poisson–Boltzmann context but also in mono-phasic flow in porous media and tissue perfusion
[
26
,
7
]
. We consider the polygonal domain enclosed by the points
(0,0)
,
(1,0)
,
(1.25,0.75)
,
(0.5,1.25)
,
(-0.25,0.75)
and consider that there is a line fracture
\gamma
joining the points
(0.4,0.25)
and
(0.6,0.85)
. We construct a sequence of non-structured triangulations that are conforming with the line fracture, and that are slightly agglomerated near the line. We use a smooth advecting velocity going from left to right with higher magnitude near the domain centre:

\eta(x,y)=U_{0}\sigma\tanh\bigl(\frac{y-\frac{1}{2}}{\sigma}\bigr)(1+\theta\sin(2\pi x)),\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{u}(x,y)=\mathrm{rot}\,\eta(x,y),

and set the parameters
U_{0}=\in\{0.25,0.0025\}
,
\sigma=0.08
,
\theta=0.12
and the remaining model coefficients are
\varepsilon=10^{-3}
and
\kappa=1
. We use pure Dirichlet boundary conditions setting
\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
. The source term is
\delta_{\gamma}
and in this case its regularity is
\mathrm{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}(\Omega)
for
\epsilon>0
.

Since we do not know the closed-form solution, we use a numerical convergence rate computing errors between approximate solutions and a reference numerical solution (obtained on a finer mesh). The convergence history for this test is reported in Table
6.3
. The error decay seems to correspond to the behaviour in Example 2, thanks to the conformity and smoothness of the mesh closer to the line fracture. We show snapshots of the approximate solutions for two advecting intensities
U_{0}
, as well as an example of the unstructured mesh. The top panels in Figure
6.3
illustrate how the higher velocity is able to advect the higher potential produced by the source
\delta_{\gamma}
whereas the second row shows flux and potential that remain concentrated near
\gamma
.

7.
Conclusion

We have developed a mixed finite element method for the linearised Poisson–Boltzmann equations assuming an advective velocity in
\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)
. One of the challenges is to derive error estimates when the load is in
\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\Omega)
, and this is tackled using a quasi-interpolation operator, here conveniently redefined for
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)
spaces and mixed boundary conditions. The continuous and discrete problems were shown to be well-posed and we established quasi-optimality and convergence rates. A postprocess of the double layer potential allows us to get superconvergence.

For us, a natural extension to this work is to perform the full coupling analysis with the Navier–Stokes equations (see, for example,
[
9
,
14
,
12
,
6
]
), and to incorporate the unbounded nonlinearity in the reaction function
\kappa
as done for the primal coupling in
[
3
]
. This requires to add box constraints in the double layer potential space, and to use a fixed-point structure to handle a perturbed saddle-point problem when the nonlinearity is in the lower-diagonal block. We will also address a posteriori error estimates as in
[
7
]
, and other types of singular forcing terms including Dirac point sources following
[
25
,
15
]
.
