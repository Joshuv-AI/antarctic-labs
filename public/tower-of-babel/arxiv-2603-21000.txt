Motivic Cohomology and K-groups of varieties over higher local fields
Rahul Gupta, Amalendu Krishna, Jitendra Rathore

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14C25, 14F22; Secondary 14F30, 19D45

Abstract.

For quasi-projective varieties over a higher local field
k_{N}
, we prove that its
K
-groups, above a suitable degree, are divisible-by-finite.
We also prove the finiteness of the prime-to-
p
torsion subgroup of certain higher Chow groups for smooth projective varieties over such fields, where
p
denotes the final residue characteristic of
k_{N}
. As an application, we show that the kernel of the tame reciprocity map is uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible. A key ingredient in achieving these results is the finiteness of étale cohomology groups over such fields.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The algebraic
K
-groups of an algebraic variety
X
are important invariants attached to
X
and they encode deep arithmetic and geometric information about
X
. One of the central themes in algebraic
K
-theory is to understand these groups for nice classes of algebraic varieties. These groups are very difficult to compute, even for fields. It was a remarkable achievement when Quillen
[
41
]
computed all
K
-groups of finite fields.

One of the deep conjectures in the subject involves the Bass conjecture and the Parshin conjecture. The former predicts that for a smooth projective variety
X
over a finite field, the algebraic
K
-groups
K_{i}(X)
are finitely generated for all
i\geq 0
, while the latter predicts that
K_{i}(X)
are torsion for
i>0
. Over several decades, many formulations in terms of motivic cohomology, as well as their relations to other known conjectures, have been established
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
28
]
. These conjecture are far from being resolved, except in dimensions at most one (
[
52
, Chapter 6, Theorem 6.1]
), but there are some partial results in higher dimension (see
[
44
]
,
[
28
]
).

Passing from finite fields to local fields, in contrast, the
K
-groups are not necessarily finitely generated. In
[
47
]
, Tate showed that
K_{2}(F)
is the direct sum of a finite group and a divisible
group, where
F
is a non-archimedean local field. This was later appropriately extended to higher algebraic
K
-groups for non-archimedean local fields (see
[
52
, Chapter VI, Section 7]
). In the archimedian setting, the structure of
K
-groups for the archimedean local fields
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
has also been established (see
[
52
, Chapter VI, Theorem 1.6, Theorem 3.1]
). For smooth varieties over
{\mathbb{C}}
,
Pedrini-Weibel
[
38
]
,
[
39
]
proved that algebraic
K
-groups of a smooth variety
X
over
{\mathbb{C}}
, above the dimension of
X
, are divisible-by-finite.
In other words, they proved that
K_{n}(X)
is the direct sum of a finite group and a divisible group when
n>{\rm dim}(X)
.

In this article, we study the
K
-groups of quasi-projective varieties over finite fields, non-archimedean local fields, and more generally, higher local fields (in the sense of Kato), and establish analogous divisible-by-finite results. The aforementioned results of Pedrini and Weibel are among the primary motivations for our work. We now state our main results.

1.2.
Algebraic
K
-groups for quasi-projective varieties over
N
-local fields

Recall that for
N\geq 0
, an
N
-local field is defined inductively so that it is
a complete discrete valued field
k_{N}
such that its residue field
k_{N-1}
is an
(N-1)
-local field, where
0
-local field is a finite field.
In particular,
k_{N}
is an
N
-local field if and only if there exists an
(N+1)
-tuple
(k_{N},k_{N-1},\dots,k_{1},k_{0})
of fields such that each
k_{i}
is a complete discrete valuation field,
k_{i-1}
is the residue field of
k_{i}
, and
k_{0}
is a finite field.
We let
p>0
denote the characteristic of
k_{0}
.
Our first main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a connected quasi-projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over
k_{N}
. Then for any integer
m\geq d+N+1
, we have

K_{m}(X)\cong F\oplus D
,

where
F
is a finite group and
D
is a uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible group. In particular,
K_{m}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
(except
p
-torsion) is finite. Moreover, if
{\rm char}(k_{N})={\rm char}(k_{0})
, then
D
is a uniquely divisible group and
K_{m}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
is finite.

In the case where
X
is a smooth projective variety over a finite field, we get following corollary, which shows the equivalence of Bass and Parshin’s Conjectures in higher degrees.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over a finite field
k
. For any integer
m\geq d+1
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

K_{m}(X)
is a finite group of order not divisible by
p
.

(2)

K_{m}(X)
is finitely generated.

(3)

K_{m}(X)
is torsion.

Moreover, if
K_{d}(X)
is torsion, then
K_{d}(X)
is finite.

For smooth projective varieties over a finite field
k_{0}
(i.e., the case
N=0
), the group
D
in Theorem
1.1
is conjecturally zero. This stronger version of the theorem was established for
d=0
by Quillen
[
41
]
and for
d=1
by Harder and Quillen (see
[
52
, Chapter VI, Theorem 6.1]
). In higher dimensions,
K
-groups are known to be torsion for certain classes of surfaces and threefolds (see
[
24
]
,
[
44
, Théorème 6]
, and
[
28
, Corollaire 2.1]
). Explicit examples of such smooth projective surfaces include abelian surfaces, Kummer surfaces, and supersingular
K3
surfaces
[
24
, Proposition A.5]
. Hence by Theorem
1.1
, one can conclude that
D
is zero for these classes of varieties when
m
is greater than the dimension of variety.
When
N=1
, the case
d=0
is well-understood (see
[
47
]
,
[
36
]
and
[
52
, Chapter VI, Theorem 7.3]
). Furthermore, known results in class field theory for smooth projective varieties over local fields have partially aided the study of
K
-groups
[
3
]
,
[
42
]
,
[
11
]
through an understanding of
SK_{1}(X)
. By contrast, very little is known when
N\geq 2
(see
[
15
]
).

We now state analogue results for étale
K
-groups of smooth projective varieties over finite and local fields, which were obtained as a consequence of proving similar results for algebraic
K
-groups. For
i\geq 0
, the étale
K
-group
K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{i}(X)
is defined as the
i^{th}
homotopy
group of the spectrum
\widehat{\mathbf{K}}^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{X}
, which is defined in

[
12
, Definition 14.3]
(also see
[
17
]
). For
v\geq 1
and
i\geq 0
, the
i^{th}
étale
K
-group with coefficients

\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}
, denoted by
K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v})
, is defined
as the
i^{th}
homotopy group of the spectrum

\widehat{\mathbf{K}}^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{X}\wedge\mathcal{M}(l^{v})
, where

\mathcal{M}(l^{v})
denotes the mod
l^{v}
Moore spectrum
(see
[
12
, Definition 4.3]
), where
l
is a prime invertible on
X
. We have the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over a field
k
.

(1)

If
k
is finite, then for any integer
m\geq 1
, we have

K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)\cong F\oplus D
,

where
F
is finite and
D
is a uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible group.

(2)

If
k
is a non-archimedian local field and
X
admits a good reduction, then for any integer
m\geq 2
, we have

K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)\cong F^{\prime}\oplus D^{\prime}
,

where
F^{\prime}
is a finite group and
D^{\prime}
is a uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible group.

One of the key results used to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
is to establish the finiteness results for the étale cohomology of quasi-projective varieties over higher local fields. These results are themselves of independent importance, with a wide range of applications. We now state our results for the étale cohomology of quasi-projective varieties.

1.3.
Finiteness of étale cohomology groups over
N
-local fields

The finiteness of the étale cohomology groups with divisible coefficients has been a central problem (for example, see
[
10
, Section 2.1]
,

[
30
]
and
[
25
, Section 7]
). We generalize these results to quasi-projective varieties over higher local fields. We proved the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over an
N
-local field
k_{N}
. Then there exists an integer
M
such that

\lvert H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/m(n))\rvert\leq M

for all integers
m
such that
(m,p)=1
if any of the following holds.

(1)

i\nin[2n-d-N,n+d+2]
;

(2)

n\nin[0,d+N]
.

For
N=0
, the theorem follows from
[
30
, Theorem 2]
and for
N=1
,
the part
(2)
of the theorem is known in the case when
X
is projective by
[
25
, Corollary 7.11]
.

1.4.
Bloch’s Higher Chow groups

We now study the finiteness of torsion subgroup in higher Chow groups for smooth projective varieties over finite, local, or more generally higher local fields. Bass conjectured that algebraic
K
-groups of smooth projective varieties over finite fields are finitely generated. A more stronger conjecture predicts that
higher Chow groups of such varieties are also finitely generated abelian groups.
A weaker form of the above conjecture is as follows.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over a finite field
k
. Then
{\rm CH}^{i}(X,j)_{{\rm tor}}
is finite for all
i,j\geq 0
.

First, note that if
i>d+j
, then
{\rm CH}^{i}(X,j)=0
by dimension argument. So only interesting range is
i\leq d+j
. Not much is known about this conjecture in general except two cases
j=0
,
i=d
and
j=1,i=d+1
. In first case when
j=0
, the groups
{\rm CH}^{d}(X,0)={\rm CH}^{d}(X)\cong{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
are finitely generated as shown by Kato-Saito using unramified class field theory (see
[
31
]
). In the second case,
{\rm CH}^{d+1}(X,1)
is shown to be finite by Akthar (see
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
). Here, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over a finite field
k
. Then
{\rm CH}^{i}(X,j)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite for
i\geq d+1
and
j\geq 0
.

We will now discuss some results for varieties over non-archimedian local fields. We denote by
k
a local field with the residue characteristic
p>0
. Conjecture
1.5
is no longer true for smooth projective varieties over
k
. In
[
2
]
, Asakura and Saito construct an example of smooth projective curves
C
over
p
-adic fields such that
{\rm CH}^{2}(C,1)\{p^{\prime}\}
is infinite. Furthermore, it is shown in loc. cit. that there are examples of smooth projective surfaces
X
over
p
-adic fields having good reduction for which
{\rm CH}^{2}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is not finite
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
. In
[
34
, Proposition 1.4]
, Lüders proved that for a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over a local field having a good reduction, the groups
{}_{n}{\rm CH}^{d+j}(X,j)
are finite if
j\geq 1
and
p\nmid n
. We generalize this result and prove the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over a local field
k
that admits a good reduction. Then the
{\rm CH}^{i}(X,j)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite if
j\geq 1
and either
i\leq j+2
or
i\geq d+1
.

Note that Theorem
1.7
is a generalization of
[
34
, Proposition 1.4]
in two aspects: first, the above theorem establishes that the
p^{\prime}
-torsion subgroup of
\text{CH}^{i}(X,j)
is finite; second, the result covers a broader range of indices for
i
and
j
. We also generalized this to smooth varieties over
N
-local fields and proved the following.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over
k_{N}
, where
N\geq 2
. Then
{\rm CH}^{i}(X,j)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite if
j\geq d+N
and either
i\leq j+2
or
i\geq d+N+1
.

See
[
27
]
for recent work on the groups
{\rm CH}^{i}(X,j)
, when
i=d+N+1
and
j=N+1
, for smooth projective varieties
X
over
k_{N}
.

1.5.
Application to Class field theory

Finally, we give an application of these results to tame class field theory for smooth varieties over local fields. The tame reciprocity map
\rho^{t}_{X}:C^{t}(X)\rightarrow\pi^{ab,t}_{1}(X)
for a smooth variety
X
over a non-archimedian local field is well studied in
[
25
]
. It is shown in loc. cit. that the kernel of the map
\rho^{t}_{X}
is
p^{\prime}
-divisible whenever
X
has a smooth compactification
\overline{X}
, which has a good reduction. Here, we strengthen this result by showing that it is, in fact, uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible. The analogous result for
\overline{X}
over a
p
-adic field was shown in
[
46
, p. 185]
. We have the following:

Theorem 1.9
.

The kernel of the tame reciprocity map
\rho^{t}_{X}:C^{t}(X)\rightarrow\pi^{ab,t}_{1}(X)
is uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible.

1.6.
Outline of proofs

We briefly outline our proofs. The main ingredient to prove the theorems mentioned above is to prove the uniform finiteness of étale cohomology groups, i.e., to prove Theorem
1.4
. We achieve this in Sections
2
,
3
(for projective varieties) and
4
(for quasi-projective varieties). The main input here is similar uniform boundedness results of
Colliot-Thélène-Sansuc-Soulé
[
10
, Section 2.1]
and Kahn
[
30
]
over finite fields.
For a smooth variety
X
over a local field that admits a semi-stable reduction, using the above results, Gabber purity theorem and an induction, we prove the uniform finiteness for the étale cohomology groups of a semi-stable model of
X
with support in the special fiber (see Theorem
2.9
).

When
X
is also projective, we appeal to Gabber rigidity theorems and localization long exact sequence to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.4
for such varieties (see Theorem
2.10
). We then use alteration theorems of de Jong and Gabber
and the flatification theorems of Raynaud-Gruson to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.4
for all projective varieties over local fields (see Theorem
2.11
). In Section
3
, we prove similar results for projective varieties over higher local fields (see Proposition
3.5
, Theorem
3.6
, and Theorem
3.7
for the corresponding statements). To prove Theorem
1.4
for a smooth quasi-projective schemes over an
N
-local field, the main idea is to use localization theorem, purity theorem and to approximate a singular scheme by regular schemes from inside (see Section
4
for details).

In Section
5
, using Theorem
1.4
and a spectral sequence, we obtain divisible-by-finite type results for étale
K
-groups (see Theorem
5.1
). The first part of Theorem
1.1
is then obtained in Section
6
using Quillen-Lichtenbaum Conjecture and results about the étale
K
-groups obtained in the previous section. To complete the proof of the theorem, we show that in a suitable range,
K
-groups of a smooth variety over a positive characteristic
N
-local field are
p
-divisible (see Lemma
6.5
). Over perfect fields, this result is known by Geisser and Levine
[
22
]
.
We then discuss the various results (from previous sections) over finite fields and local fields in Section
7
and obtain Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.2
.

One of the main ingredients for proving Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.7
is to use Voevodsky comparison result to relate the motivic cohomology groups and the higher Chow groups. In Section
8
, we then use the results on
ét
ale realization map proved in
[
25
]
and
Theorem
1.4
, to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.9
, we compare the tame reciprocity map with the
ét
ale realization map from compactly supported motivic cohomology to compactly supported
ét
ale cohomology groups with finite invertible coefficient. We already know that the kernel of this
ét
ale realization map is
p^{\prime}
-divisible by our results from
[
25
]
. It therefore suffices to show that the kernel does not have any prime-to-
p
torsion. This is achieved in Section
9
.

2.
Finiteness in étale cohomology over finite and local fields

Throughout the section, we denote by
k
any non-archimedian local field. We write
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)=\oplus_{l\neq p}H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{l}/\mathbb{Z}_{l}(n))
, where
l
varies over all primes except
p
, the residue characteristic of
k
. In this section, we prove the following main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
d
over a local field
k
. Then the group
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)
is finite if any one of the following holds.

(1)

i\nin[2n-d-1,n+d+1]
;

(2)

n\nin[0,d+1]
.

We have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over a local field
k
. Then there exists an integer
M
such that

\lvert H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/m(n))\rvert\leq M

for all integers
m
such that
(m,p)=1
if any of the following holds.

(1)

i\nin[2n-d-1,n+d+2];

(2)

n\nin[0,d+1]
.

Proof.

For any integer
m
such that
(m,p)=1
. We have the exact triangle

{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m(n)\to({{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\prime}(n)\xrightarrow{m}({{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\prime}(n),

where
({{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\prime}={\underset{\ell\neq p}{\bigoplus}}{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
and we have the following long exact sequence,

(2.1)

\cdots\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i-1}(X,n)\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/m(n))\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)\rightarrow\cdots

Taking the limit over integers
m
such that
(m,p)=1
, the required result now follows from Theorem
2.1
.
∎

We now prove Theorem
2.1
and will achieve this in several steps. We first prove it when
X
is smooth projective having semi-stable model and later reduce general case to the above case via alteration. We begin with a recollection of known finiteness result for étale cohomology groups of varieties over finite fields.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety of dimension
d
over a finite field. Then
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)

is finite if any of the following holds.

(1)

i\nin[n,2n+1]
.

(2)

i\nin[n,n+d+1]
.

(3)

n\nin[0,d]
.

(4)

i\nin[2n,2n+1]
and
X
is projective.

Proof.

This follows from combining
[
30
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2]
and
[
10
, Theorem 2]
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over a finite field. Then the group
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)
is finite if
i\nin[2n,n+d+1]
or
n>d
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
30
, Theorem 3(b)]
.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

Recall that a reduced Noetherian scheme
Y
of pure dimension
d
with
irreducible components
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{r}\}
is called a normal crossing scheme
if for every nonempty subset subset
J\subset\{1,2,...,r\}
, the scheme theoretic
intersection

is a regular scheme
which is either empty or of pure dimension

. It is clear from this
definition that if
Y
is a normal crossing scheme, then

Y_{i}\bigcap\ (\cup_{j\neq i}Y_{j})
is also a normal crossing scheme (of smaller
dimension than that of

).

\square

Definition 2.6
.

We let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a connected Noetherian regular scheme with a flat and projective

{\mathbb{F}}\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to S
of relative dimension

, where

We let

denote the generic

{\mathcal{X}}_{s}
the (scheme-theoretic) closed fiber of
{\mathbb{F}}
. We shall assume that

Y:=({\mathcal{X}}_{s})_{\rm red}
is a simple normal crossing divisor on
{\mathcal{X}}
. A morphism

satisfying these properties will be called a semi-stable model for the
k
-scheme

. Moreover, if

is a smooth morphism, we then say

.
We let

\iota\colon Y\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{X}}

3.
Finiteness in étale cohomology over higher local fields

Let
N
be any non-negative integer.
Recall that an
N
-local field is defined inductively so that it is complete discrete valued field
k_{N}
such that its residue field
k_{N-1}
is an
(N-1)
-local field, where
0
-local field is a finite field.
In particular,
k_{N}
is an
N
-local field if and only if there exists an
(N+1)
-tuple
(k_{N},k_{N-1},\dots,k_{1},k_{0})
of fields such that each
k_{i}
is a complete discrete valuation field,
k_{i-1}
is the residue field of
k_{i}
, and
k_{0}
is a finite field.
We let
p>0
denote the characteristic of
k_{0}
.
We call these fields as higher local fields. With this definition, finite fields are
0
-local fields and local fields are
1
-local fields. These fields were introduced by K. Kato. In this section, we generalize the finiteness result proved in the previous section over local fields to
N
-local fields for any
N\geq 1
. For any quasi-projective
k_{N}
-variety
X
, we write
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)=\oplus_{l\neq p}H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{l}/\mathbb{Z}_{l}(n))
, where
l
varies over all primes except
p
, the characteristic of the finite field
k_{0}
. The main result of this section is the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over an
N
-local field
k_{N}
. Then the group
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)
is finite if any one of the following holds.

(1)

i\nin[2n-d-N,n+d+1]
;

(2)

n\nin[0,d+N]
.

Using this, one can deduce the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
d
over
k_{N}
. Then there exists an integer
M
such that

\lvert H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(n))\rvert\leq M

for all
v\geq 0
and
l\neq p
if any of the following holds.

(1)

i\nin[2n-d-N,n+d+2];

(2)

n\nin[0,d+N]
.

Proof.

For any integer
m
such that
(m,p)=1
. We have the exact triangle

{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m(n)\to({{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\prime}(n)\xrightarrow{m}({{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\prime}(n),

where
({{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\prime}={\underset{\ell\neq p}{\bigoplus}}{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
and we have the following long exact sequence,

(3.1)

...\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i-1}(X,n)\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/m(n))\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)\rightarrow...

Taking the limit over integers
m
such that
(m,p)=1
, the required result now follows from Theorem
3.1
.
∎

We prove the theorem in several steps. We begin with the finiteness result for
{\rm Spec\,}(k_{N})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
X={\rm Spec\,}(k_{N})
, then
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}({\rm Spec\,}(k_{N}),n)

is finite if any of the following holds.

(1)

i\nin[2n-N,n+1]
.

(2)

n\nin[0,N]
.

Proof.

First note that when
N=0
, this follows from Theorem
2.3
. In this case, the result says that
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}({\rm Spec\,}(k_{0}),n)
is finite either

or

. So we assume that
N\geq 1
, and prove this result by induction on
N
. As
X={\rm Spec\,}(k_{N})
, we can choose
\mathcal{X}={\rm Spec\,}(\mathcal{O}_{{k}_{N}})
and
A={\rm Spec\,}(k_{N-1})
. Assume that
N\geq 1
and the results holds for
N-1
. Using the long exact sequence, we have

(3.2)

...\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(\mathcal{X},n)\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,n)\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}},A}^{i+1}(\mathcal{X},n)\rightarrow..,

it suffices to show the finiteness of
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(\mathcal{X},n)
and
H_{et,A}^{i+1}(\mathcal{X},n)
in the given range of
i
and
n
. Now by
[
37
, Chapter VI, Corollary 2.7]
, we have
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(\mathcal{X},n)\cong H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(A,n)

and

5.
Étale
K
-groups over higher local fields

In this section, we study the étale
K
-groups for varieties and prove certain structure of étale
K
-groups for quasi-projective varieties over higher local fields. We use this structure to prove analogous results for algebraic
K
-groups in the later part of the paper.

Étale
K
-theory is closely related to algebraic
K
-theory via the
Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjectures. One of the advantages of étale

K
-theory over algebraic
K
-theory is the existence of an
Atiyah–Hirzebruch–type spectral sequence (
[
12
, Proposition 5.2]
) whose
E_{2}^{p,q}
-terms are
given by étale cohomology and which converges to étale
K
-groups.
In this section, we exploit this spectral sequence to compute étale

K
-groups of varieties over higher local fields, using the known results
on the étale cohomology of such varieties established in the previous
sections.

Let
k
be a field and let
l
be a prime invertible in
k
such that

\mathrm{cd}_{l}(k)<\infty
. Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme of finite type
over
k
. The étale
K
-theory spectrum of
X
, denoted by

\widehat{\mathbf{K}}^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{X}
, is defined in

[
12
, Definition 14.3]
(also see
[
17
]
). The étale
K
-theory
groups
K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{i}(X)
are defined, for
i\geq 0
, as the
i^{th}
homotopy
group of the spectrum
\widehat{\mathbf{K}}^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{X}
. For
v\geq 1
, the étale
K
-theory groups with coefficients

\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}
, denoted by
K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v})
, are defined
for
i\geq 0
as the
i^{th}
homotopy group of the spectrum

\widehat{\mathbf{K}}^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{X}\wedge\mathcal{M}(l^{v})
, where

\mathcal{M}(l^{v})
denotes the mod
l^{v}
Moore spectrum
(see
[
12
, Definition 4.3]
). The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be connected quasi-projective variety of dimension
d
over
k_{N}
. Then for any integer
m\geq d+N+1
, we have

K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)\cong F\oplus D
,

where
F
is a finite and
D
is uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible group.

We first recall a well-known spectral sequence, which we use to achieve the main results of this section.

Theorem 5.2
.

[
12
, Proposition 5.2]

Let
X
be a connected Noetherian scheme over
{\mathbb{Z}}/l
of finite
\mathbb{Z}/l
-cohomological dimension. Then there is a natural strongly convergent, fourth quadrant spectral sequence

E^{p,-q}_{2}=H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{p}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(q/2))\Rightarrow K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{q-p}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}),

where
\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(\frac{q}{2})
is
0
unless
q
is a non-negative even integer.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
X
be connected quasi-projective variety of dimension
d
over
k_{N}
. Then for any given integer
m\geq d+N+1
, there exists an integer
M
(depending upon
m
) such that we have

\lvert K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v})\rvert\leq M

for all primes
l\neq p
and all
v\geq 1
.

Proof.

By applying Proposition
5.2
, we have

E^{p,-q}_{2}=H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{p}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(q/2))\Rightarrow K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{q-p}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}).

It is enough to show that there exists an integer
M
such that
\lvert E^{p,-q}_{2}\rvert\leq M
whenever
q-p\geq d+N+1
, for all primes
l\neq p
and every
v\geq 1
. Indeed, the condition
q-p\geq d+N+1
is equivalent to
p\leq q-d-N-1
. This implies that
p\nin[q-d-N,\frac{q}{2}+d+2]
, and hence by Theorem
1.4
, we obtain

\lvert H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{p}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(q/2))\rvert\leq M

for every prime
l\neq p
and every
v\geq 1
. Fix any
q
such that
q-p\geq d+N+1
. Then we have
\lvert E^{p,-q}_{2}\rvert\leq M
, and consequently,

\lvert E^{p,-q}_{r}\rvert\leq M
for all
r\geq 2
.

In particular, we have
\lvert E^{p,-q}_{\infty}\rvert\leq M
. Therefore, for any integer
m
such that
m\geq d+N+1
with
q-p=m
, we have
\lvert E^{p,-q}_{\infty}\rvert\leq M
. This implies that

\lvert K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v})\rvert\leq M

for every prime
l\neq p
and every
v\geq 1
. This completes the proof.
∎

We denote by
\mathbb{L}
the set of natural numbers that are not divisible by
p
, where
p
is the characteristic of

.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
X
be connected quasi-projective variety of dimension
d
over
k_{N}
. Then for any given integer
m\geq d+N+1
, there exists an integer
M
(depending upon
m
) such that

\lvert K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)\rvert\leq M

for all
n\in\mathbb{L}
.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{L}
be any integer, and we write
n=l^{v_{1}}_{1}l^{v_{2}}_{2}...l^{v_{s}}_{s}
for some integer
s\geq 1
, where
l_{1},l_{2},...,l_{s}
are distinct primes such that
p\neq l_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,2,...,s\}
. We let
E^{p,-q}_{2}(n):=H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{p}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n(q/2))
. Then we have

(5.1)

E^{p,-q}_{2}(n)=E^{p,-q}_{2}(l^{v_{1}}_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus E^{p,-q}_{2}(l^{v_{s}}_{s}),\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}

(5.2)

K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)=K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v_{1}}_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v_{s}}_{s}).

for any integer
m
. By Theorem
1.4
(also see Proposition
5.3
), there exists an integer
M
such that

\lvert E^{p,-q}_{2}(n)\rvert\leq M
, whenever
q-p\geq d+N+1
.

This implies that

\lvert E^{p,-q}_{2}(n)\rvert=\lvert E^{p,-q}_{2}(l^{v_{1}}_{1})\rvert\cdots\lvert E^{p,-q}_{2}(l^{v_{s}}_{s})\rvert\leq M
, whenever
q-p\geq d+N+1
.

Now by mimicking the similar arguments as in the proof of Proposition
5.3
, we have

\lvert K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)\rvert=\lvert K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v_{1}}_{1})\rvert\cdots\lvert K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{v_{s}}_{s})\rvert\leq M
,

whenever
q-p\geq d+N+1
with
q-p=m
. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
X
be a connected quasi-projective variety of dimension
d
over
k_{N}
. Then for any given integer
m\geq d+N+1
, there exists an integer

(depending upon

) such that

\lvert K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)/n\rvert\leq M
and
\lvert_{n}K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m-1}(X)\rvert\leq M
.

for all

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{L}
be any integer. The corollary follows from Proposition
5.4
and the short exact sequence

(5.3)

0\rightarrow K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)/n\rightarrow K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)\rightarrow_{n}K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m-1}(X)\rightarrow 0.

∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
X
be as in Corollary
5.5
. Then for
m\geq d+N
, the
p^{\prime}
-torsion subgroup of
K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)
is finite i.e.,
K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite.

Proof.

For

, Corollary
5.5
yields that
\lvert_{n}K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)\rvert\leq M
for all

. Taking the direct limit over all integers in

, we get the desired result.
∎

Recall that we say an abelian group

is
\mathbb{L}
-divisible if

for all

. This is equivalent to saying that

is
p^{\prime}
-divisible group. We now recall the following result from group theory, which is well known to experts.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

be an abelian group. Suppose that
there exists an integer

such that
|{A}/{m}|\leq M
for all

. Then
A\cong F\bigoplus D
, where

is finite group of
order lying in
\mathbb{L}
and

is
\mathbb{L}
-divisible.

Proof.

The proof is an immediate consequence of
[
25
, Corollary 11.4]
, upon taking
\mathbb{M}
to be the set of all primes different from

in their argument.
∎

We now begin the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Proof.

Let
m\geq N+d+1
be any given integer. We first claim that

K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)\cong F\oplus D
,

where
F
is a finite and
D
is
p^{\prime}
-divisible group. By Lemma
5.7
, this claim is equivalent to showing that there exists an integer
M
such that
\lvert K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)/n\rvert\leq M
for all
n\in\mathbb{L}
. But this follows from Corollary
5.5
. This completes the proof of the claim. It now remains to show that
D
is uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible, which is equivalent to show
D\{p^{\prime}\}=0
. First note that the group is
D\{p^{\prime}\}
is
p^{\prime}
-divisible as
D
is
p^{\prime}
-divisible. By applying Corollary
5.6
, we get that
K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite, which in particular implies that
D\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite. The group
D\{p^{\prime}\}
is therefore finite

-torsion and
p^{\prime}
-divisible group, and hence
D\{p^{\prime}\}=0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
. ∎

-groups over higher local fields

In this section, we prove one of the main results of this paper, which describe the structure of algebraic
K
-groups of quasi-projective varieties over higher local fields. An analogous structure of

-groups for smooth varieties over archimedian local fields such as complex numbers was proved by Weibel-Pedrini
[
38
]
,
[
39
]
. We first describe the structure of algebraic

-groups of quasi-projective varieties over
N
-local fields. We then specialize to smooth varieties over finite fields, where we prove a stronger result that also allows us to relate the Bass conjecture and the Parshin conjecture in higher degrees.

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety over a field
k
. The algebraic

-groups of
X
were defined by D. Quillen. We denote by
K_{i}(X)
the
i^{th}

K
-group of

in the sense of Quillen
[
40
]
; by definition,

is the

homotopy group of the
K
-theory spectrum
\mathbf{K}(X)
. Similarly, one can define the
K
-groups with finite coefficient say
\mathbb{Z}/n
for any integer

, denoted as

(see
[
52
, IV, Definition 2.4]
).

We denote by

a
N
-local field and we let
p
denote the characteristic of of the associated finite field
k_{0}
. We describe the structure of algebraic
K
-groups of quasi-projective varieties in the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a connected quasi-projective variety of dimension

over
k_{N}
. Then for any integer

, we have

K_{m}(X)\cong F\oplus D
,

where
F
is a finite and
D
is a uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible group. In particular,
K_{m}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite. Moreover, if
{\rm char}(k_{N})={\rm char}(k_{0})
, then

is a uniquely divisible group and
K_{m}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
is finite.

One of the key results we need to prove the above theorem is the theorem of Rost, Voevodsky
[
51
]
, Weibel which was initially a conjecture known as the Quillen-Lichtenbaum Conjecture. Later the result was shown for arbitrary quasi-projective varieties by Clausen-Mathew (see
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
). This result allow us to use results of étale

Theorem 6.2
.

(
[
51
]
,
[

]
)
Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety over
k
and let

be a positive integer invertible on

. Then for any integer

K_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)\rightarrow K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
X
be a connected quasi-projective variety of dimension

over

. Then for any given integer

, there exists an integer

(depending upon

\lvert K_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)\rvert\leq M

\forall\hskip 5.69054ptn\in\mathbb{L}

(2)

\lvert K_{m}(X)/n\rvert\leq M

\forall\hskip 5.69054pt

n\in\mathbb{L}

(3)

\lvert_{n}K_{m-1}(X)\rvert\leq M

\forall\hskip 5.69054pt

n\in\mathbb{L}

Proof.

The parts
(2)
and
(3)
follow from
(1)
by using the following short exact sequence (see
[

, Page. 306]
).

(6.1)

0\rightarrow K_{m}(X)/n\rightarrow K_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)\rightarrow{}_{n}K_{m-1}(X)\rightarrow 0.

It now remains to show

. By Theorem
6.2
, we have an isomorphism

K_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)\cong K^{{\text{\'{e}t}}}_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/n)

for

, and for all

. Hence it suffices to show that the similar claim for étale

-groups in the given range. This follows from Proposition

, which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let

be as in Proposition

. Then for

-torsion subgroup of

Proof.

For

, Proposition

\lvert_{n}K_{m}(X)\rvert\leq M
for all

We prove with the following lemma before turning to the proof of Theorem
6.1
. The following lemma is motivated from a similar result of Geisser and Levine

[

, Last claim of Theorem 8.4]
over perfect fields.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let

be a smooth variety of dimension

over

of characteristics

. Then
K_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/p)=0
if

.

Proof.

We have the following convergent spectral sequence (see
[
22
, Page 465]
).

E^{i,j}_{2}=H^{i}(X_{\rm Zar},(\mathcal{K}/p)_{-j})\implies K_{-i-j}(X,Z/p),

where
(\mathcal{K}/p)_{-j}
is the Zariski sheaf associated to the presheaf
(U\mapsto K_{-j}(U,{\mathbb{Z}}/p))
. It therefore suffices to show that the groups
H^{i}(X_{\rm Zar},(\mathcal{K}/p)_{-j})
are zero when
-i-j\geq d+N+1
. Since these groups vanish for

, it suffices to show that the sheaf
(\mathcal{K}/p)_{-j}
vanish when

-j\geq d+N+1
. Since the Gersten resolution of the sheaf

is universally exact (see
[

]
), it suffices to show that

for
-j\geq d+N+1
, where

is a generic point of

. To prove this, we first note that
K_{-j}(k(\eta))/p\cong K^{M}_{-j}(k(\eta))/p=\Omega^{-j}_{k(\eta),{\rm log}}\hookrightarrow\Omega^{-j}_{k(\eta)}
. We finally observe that
\Omega^{-j}_{k(\eta)}=0
for

because

. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We now prove Theorem

.

Proof.

Let

be any given integer. We first claim that

K_{m}(X)\cong F\oplus D
,

where

is uniquely

-divisible, which is equivalent to show

. First note that the group

is
p^{\prime}
-divisible. By applying Corollary
6.4
, we get that
K_{m}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite, which in particular implies that
D\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite. The group
D\{p^{\prime}\}
is therefore a finite
p^{\prime}
-torsion and
p^{\prime}
-divisible group, and hence
D\{p^{\prime}\}=0
. This completes the proof of the first part of Theorem
6.1
. We now assume that

is an equi-characteristic

{\rm char}(k_{N})={\rm char}(k_{0})
.
By Lemma
6.5
, we have

K_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/p)=0
for
m\geq d+N+1
. We use the short exact sequence

(6.2)

0\rightarrow K_{m}(X)/p\rightarrow K_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/p)\rightarrow_{p}K_{m-1}(X)\rightarrow 0,

to conclude that

is

7.2.

Throughout this subsection, we let

Conjecture 7.6

. The case

Theorem 7.8
.

Let

be a smooth projective variety of dimension

over a finite field

. Then for any

, we have

K_{m}(X)\cong F\oplus D,

where

is a finite group and

is a uniquely divisible group. Moreover, if

, then

is finite. In particular,

.

Proof.

Let

be a given integer. By Theorem
6.1
(for

), we know that

(7.3)

K_{m}(X)\cong F\oplus D,

where
F
is a finite group and
D
is a
p^{\prime}
-divisible group. Therefore it suffices to show that

is uniquely
p
-divisible group. Note that

for

(see
[

, Theorem 8.4]
). We now use the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow K_{m}(X)/p\rightarrow K_{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}/p)\rightarrow_{p}K_{m-1}(X)\rightarrow 0,

to conclude that

is
p
-divisible whenever

and has no
p
-torsion when

. Since the order of

is not divisible by

(see Lemma

),

is also uniquely
p
-divisible. This implies that

is uniquely
p
-divisible whenever

has no

-torsion if

. ∎

Remark 7.9
.

Bass and Parshin conjectures predicts that the group

in Theorem

vanishes.

\square

One of the nice consequences of the above theorem is the following corollary, which shows the equivalence of Bass and Parshin’s conjectures in higher degrees.

Let

be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over a finite field

. For any integer

(1)

is finite group of order not divisible by

(2)

K_{m}(X)
is finitely generated.

(3)

K_{m}(X)
is torsion.

By Theorem

where

and

is a uniquely divisible group if

k
having good reduction. Then étale realization maps
\epsilon_{\overline{X},m}^{2d,d+1}:H_{M}^{2d}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{2d}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))
is an isomorphism, where
m\in\mathbb{L}
be any integer.

Proof.

This map is known to be surjective (see
[
25
, Lemma 13.2]
). So it is enough to show this map is injective. Let’s denote by
\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m})
the Zariski sheaf associated to the presheaf
U\rightarrow H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{i}(X,\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m})
for every open
U\subset X
. We have the following distinguished triangle in the derived category of complexes of Zariski sheaves (see
[
46
, Page 190]
).

(9.1)

\tau_{\leq d+1}\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1)\rightarrow R\epsilon_{\ast}\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m}\rightarrow R^{d+2}\epsilon_{\ast}\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m}[-d-2].

This yields a long exact sequence of cohomology groups.

(9.2)

H_{zar}^{i-d-3}(\overline{X},\mathcal{H}^{d+2}(\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m}))\rightarrow H^{i}_{zar}(\overline{X},\tau_{\leq d+1}\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rightarrow H^{i}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rightarrow H_{zar}^{i-d-2}(\overline{X},\mathcal{H}^{d+2}(\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m}))

By Bloch-Ogus
[
5
]
, we know the following complex (
9.3
) can be used to compute the Zariski cohomology of the sheaf
\mathcal{H}^{d+2}(\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m})
.

(9.3)

\bigoplus_{x\in\overline{X}_{(d)}}H^{d+2}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(k(x),\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in\overline{X}_{(d-1)}}H^{d+1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(k(x),\mu^{\otimes d}_{m})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in\overline{X}_{(0)}}H^{2}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(k(x),\mu_{m}).

We denote the above complex (
9.3
) by
C_{d+2,d+1}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m)
. Therefore, we get that

(9.4)

H_{zar}^{i}(\overline{X},\mathcal{H}^{d+2}(\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m}))\cong H_{d-i}(C_{d+2,d+1}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m)).

Since the complex
C_{d+2,d+1}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m)
is known to be acyclic in positive degrees (see
[
33
, Theorem 0.4]
), we have
H_{zar}^{i}(\overline{X},\mathcal{H}^{d+2}(\mu^{\otimes d+1}_{m}))=0
whenever
i\neq d
. It follows from sequence (
9.2
) that the map
H^{2d}_{zar}(\overline{X},\tau_{\leq d+1}\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rightarrow H^{2d}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))
is an isomorphism. We are therefore reduced to show that the natural map

(9.5)

H^{2d}_{M}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\to H^{2d}_{zar}(\overline{X},\tau_{\leq d+1}\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))

is injective.
To see this, we consider the following commutative diagram, where the top horizontal arrow is the same as the map in (
9.5
).

(9.6)

\textstyle{H^{2d}_{M}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{2d}_{zar}(\overline{X},\tau_{\leq d+1}\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{2d}_{M}(\overline{X}^{\heartsuit},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{2d}_{zar}(\overline{X}^{\heartsuit},\tau_{\leq d+1}\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1)).}

The left vertical arrow is an isomorphism by
[
25
, Proposition 8.2]
and the bottom horizontal arrow is isomorphism by

[
35
, Proposition 13.9]
and
[
21
, Theorem 1.6]
. It follows that the top horizontal arrow is injective, which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 9.2
.

The group
{\rm CH}^{d+1}(\overline{X},1)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite.

Proof.

By (
8.8
), we have

(9.7)

{\rm CH}^{d+1}(X,1)\{p^{\prime}\}\cong H_{M}^{2d+1}(X,\mathbb{Z}(d+1))\{p^{\prime}\}.

Using the short exact sequence (
8.10
) with
i=d+1
and
j=1
, it suffices to show that the group
H_{M}^{2d}(X,d+1)
is finite. We now apply Lemma
9.1
to reduce the problem to showing that
H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{2d}(X,d+1)
is finite. Indeed, Lemma
9.1
provides an isomorphism
H_{M}^{2d}(X,d+1)\cong H_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}^{2d}(X,d+1)
. The latter group is finite by Corollary
2.8
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 9.3
.

The group
C^{t}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite.

Proof.

Note that
C^{t}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
injects into
C^{t}(X)[\frac{1}{p}]
. So it is enough to show that
C^{t}(X)[\frac{1}{p}]\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite. By
[
25
, Prop 8.15]
, we know that

(9.8)

H_{c,M}^{2d+1}(X,\mathbb{Z}[1/p](d+1))\cong C^{t}(X)[\frac{1}{p}].

We have exact triangle of complexes,

(9.9)

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}]\xrightarrow{m}\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}]/m\rightarrow 0.

As
p\nmid m
,
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}]/m\cong\mathbb{Z}/m
. We therefore have

(9.10)

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}]\xrightarrow{m}\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/m\rightarrow 0.

Using (
9.10
), we get an exact sequence of cohomology groups,

(9.11)

0\to H_{c,M}^{2d}(X,\mathbb{Z}[1/p](d+1))/m\rightarrow H_{c,M}^{2d}(X,\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rightarrow_{m}H_{c,M}^{2d+1}(X,\mathbb{Z}[1/p](d+1))\to 0.

Therefore, it is enough to show that

(9.12)

\lvert H_{c,M}^{2d}(X,\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rvert\leq M

for all
m\in\mathbb{L}
and for some integer
M\geq 0
. Now using the long exact sequence,

(9.13)

...\rightarrow H_{M}^{2d-1}(\overline{X}\setminus X,\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rightarrow H_{c,M}^{2d}(X,\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rightarrow H_{M}^{2d}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rightarrow,

we are reduced to show the existence of two integers
M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
such that the following two conditions hold.

(1)

\lvert H_{M}^{2d-1}(\overline{X}\setminus X,\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rvert\leq M^{\prime}
,

(2)

\lvert H_{M}^{2d}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/m(d+1))\rvert\leq M^{\prime\prime}
.

The result for
\overline{X}\setminus X
follows from
[
25
, Corollary 10.11]
and for
\overline{X}
, it follows from combining Lemma
9.1
and Proposition
7.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section, i.e., the kernel of tame reciprocity map
\rho^{t}_{X}
is uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible.

Theorem 9.4
.

The kernel of the tame reciprocity map
\rho^{t}_{X}:C^{t}(X)\rightarrow\pi^{ab,t}_{1}(X)
is uniquely
p^{\prime}
-divisible.

Proof.

Note that
{\rm Ker}(\rho_{X}^{t})
is
p^{\prime}
-divisible by
[
25
, Theorem 13.3]
. It is therefore enough to show that
{\rm Ker}(\rho_{X}^{t})\{p^{\prime}\}=0
. Note that the group
{\rm Ker}(\rho_{X}^{t})\{p^{\prime}\}
is
p^{\prime}
-divisible because the group
{\rm Ker}(\rho_{X}^{t})
itself is
p^{\prime}
-divisible. Therefore, it suffices to show that
{\rm Ker}(\rho_{X}^{t})\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite, but this follows from Corollary
9.3
. This completes the proof. ∎
