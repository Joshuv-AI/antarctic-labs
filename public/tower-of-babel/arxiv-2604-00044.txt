Cherenkov plasmons emission by primordial neutrinos
Maxim Dvornikov

Abstract
We study the emission of Cherenkov plasmons by the gas of neutrinos with nonzero temperature and chemical potential. The background plasma, consisting of charged leptons, is taken to be nonrelativistic. The energy emission rate is obtained for longitudinal plasmons. To get the neutrino emissivity we average quantum field theory matrix element over the distribution functions of incoming and outgoing particles. Our results are applied for the description of the cooling down of a neutrino cluster formed in the early universe. Such clusters can exist owing to the neutrino interaction with a hypothetical light scalar boson. Using particular cluster parameters, we demonstrate that the proposed cooling mechanism is efficient for some clusters. We find the temperature range where the proposed cooling channel is valid. Some useful calculations of the polarization tensor, as well as the plasmon form factors and their dispersion relations are also provided.



1
Introduction

A great fraction of the mass in our universe was established in numerous
astronomical observations to consist of dark matter. The essence of
dark matter is unclear. We can just say that it is nonbaryonic
[
1
, pp. 159–173]
.
Multiple candidates are considered to play the role of dark matter.
First, we mention weakly interacting massive particles
[
2
]
.
Nowadays, light pseudoscalar particles, called axions and axion like
particles (ALPs), are assumed to be the most plausible candidates
for dark matter constituents
[
3
]
. Nevertheless, besides
the claims in Ref.
[
4
]
, no signals for dark matter particles
are detected in laboratory experiments (see, e.g., Ref.
[
5
]
).

Previously, massive neutrinos were thought to contribute to dark matter
[
6
]
.
However, later, it was understood (see, e.g., Ref.
[
1
, pp. 190–191]
)
that this kind of neutrinos cannot be cold dark matter since they
are relativistic. Nevertheless, if we assume that light massive neutrinos
can form a condensate around galaxies, these particles do not diffuse
and can contribute to dark matter to some extent. It is clear that
the formation of a neutrino condensate implies an attractive interaction
beyond the standard model. The mediator of this interaction can be
a hypothetical light scalar particle weakly coupled to neutrinos.
Thus, such a scalar boson can be a competitor to axions and ALPs for
a dark matter constituent.

The formation of neutrino clusters owing to a scalar boson interaction
was first considered in Ref
[
7
]
. Then, the formation of
neutrino clusters was studied in Ref.
[
8
]
, where multiple
masses of a scalar boson and coupling constants to neutrinos have
been considered. Besides the formation of a cluster, the interaction
with a scalar particle makes the neutrino condensate to be superfluid
[
9
]
.
The neutrino superfluidity owing to the scalar boson interaction was
studied in Refs.
[
10
,
11
]
. The implication of the neutrino
condensation to the dark matter problem was considered in Ref.
[
12
]
.
Recently, the formation of dark matter halos in the presence of the
neutrino condensate was studied in Ref.
[
13
]
.

When a cluster appears owing to an instability, the neutrino gas is
compressed and its temperature increases. Thus, a cluster can decay
because of the thermal motion of neutrinos. Moreover, if one expects
the superfluid neutrino condensate in such a cluster, additional thermal
fluctuations can destroy the supefluidity. The problem of the cluster
cooling was raised in Ref.
[
8
]
. However, the mechanisms
to carry away thermal energy from a cluster, proposed in Ref.
[
8
]
,
are not efficient.

In Refs.
[
11
,
14
]
, we put forward the alternative cooling
mechanism based on the Cherenkov plasmons emission. We assumed that
a neutrino cluster is formed in the early universe. Then, accounting
for the parameters of a cluster from numerical simulations, we estimated
the cooling time. Requesting that the cooling time is less than the
universe age, we obtained the range of temperatures for the cluster
formation.

It is known that Cherenkov emission is allowed for charged particles
whereas a neutrino is a neutral fermion. Nevertheless, it can acquire
the induced electric charge while propagating in background medium
[
15
]
.
Cherenkov radiation was shown in Ref.
[
16
]
to exist
even for standard model massless neutrinos. This kind of the radiation
by supernova neutrinos was studied in Ref.
[
17
]
.

Despite we demonstrated in Refs.
[
11
,
14
]
that a neutrino
cluster can potentially cool down by Cherenkov plasmons emiussion,
the consideration of the cooling process in Refs.
[
11
,
14
]

had some shortcomings. The description of the energy emission in Ref.
[
11
]

was quite estimatory. In Ref.
[
14
]
, we adopted the ultrarelativistic
plasma model. However, the temperature interval for the cluster formation
was extrapolated to the nonrelativistic range. The aim of the present
work is the detailed study of the cluster cooling in case of the nonrelativistic
background plasma which is more interesting from the point of view
of phenomenological applications.

This work is organized in the following way. First, in Sec.
2
,
we derive the matrix element for the Cherenkov radiation and the emissivity
of the neutrino gas. Then, in Sec.
3
, we apply our
results for the description of the particular cluster cooling. Finally,
we conclude in Sec.
4
. Some useful expressions from
the finite temperature field theory are provided in Appendix
A
.
The properties of plasmons in background medium are described in Appendix
B
.
We calculate the form factors of plasmons in Appendix
C
.
The dispersion relations for plasmons in nonrelativistic medium are
established in Appendix
D
.

2
Cherenkov emission by a neutrino gas

In this section, we derive the emissivity of the neutrino gas with
the nonzero temperature and chemical potential.

The matrix element for the process
\nu\to\nu+\gamma
, with the Feynman
diagram being depicted in Fig.
1
, reads

\mathcal{M}=\frac{eG_{\mathrm{F}}}{\sqrt{2}}\bar{u}_{2}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma^{5})u_{1}\Pi^{\mu\nu}e_{\nu}(q),

(2.1)

where
u_{1,2}=u(p_{1,2})
are the bispinors of the incoming and
the outgoing neutrinos having the four momenta
p_{1,2}^{\mu}=(E_{1,2},\mathbf{p}_{1,2})
,

\gamma^{\mu}=(\gamma^{0},\bm{\gamma})
and
\gamma^{5}=\mathrm{i}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}

are the Dirac matrices,
e_{\mu}(q)
is
the plasmon polarization vector with
q=p_{1}-p_{2}
,
e
is the
elementary charge,
G_{\mathrm{F}}=1.17\times 10^{-5}\,\text{GeV}^{-2}

is the Fermi constant, and

\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q)=-\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mathrm{tr}\left[(\not p+m)\gamma^{\mu}(c_{\mathrm{V}}-c_{\mathrm{A}}\gamma^{5})(\not p-\not q+m)\gamma^{\nu}\right]}{[p^{2}-m^{2}][(p-q)^{2}-m^{2}]},

(2.2)

is the generalized polarization tensor of a plasmon. The image for

\Pi^{\mu\nu}
can be obtained from Fig.
1
by
the truncation of the external lines. In Eq. (
2.2
),

c_{\mathrm{V,A}}
are the vector and axial vector coupling constants,
and
m
is the mass of a charged lepton
l
counterpart to
\nu
.

Figure 1:
The Feynman diagram for the neutrino Cherenkov emission
\nu\to\nu+\gamma

which results in the matrix element in Eq. (
2.1
).

The Cherenkov emission is forbidden in vacuum, where the photon dispersion
is
q^{2}=0
. However, in matter, where
q^{2}\neq 0
for a plasmon,
this process is allowed
[
16
]
. In this situation, we
have to take into account the nontrivial plasmon dispersion and evaluate
\Pi^{\mu\nu}

in Eq. (
2.2
) in matter with the nonzero temperature
and chemical potential. We calculate
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
using the imaginary
time perturbation theory. Some basic integrals are computed in Appendix
A
.
We calculate the plasmon form factors, based on
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
, in
Appendix
C
.

Squaring the matrix element in Eq. (
2.1
), one gets
that

|\mathcal{M}|^{2}=\frac{e^{2}G_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}{2}\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{1}\gamma_{\alpha}(1-\gamma^{5})\rho_{2}\gamma_{\beta}(1-\gamma^{5})]\Pi^{*\alpha\mu}e_{\mu}^{*}(q)\Pi^{\beta\nu}e_{\nu}(q),

(2.3)

where
\rho_{1,2}=u_{1,2}\bar{u}_{1,2}=(\gamma_{\lambda}p_{1,2}^{\lambda})(1+\gamma^{5})/2

are the spin density matrices of ultrarelativistic neutrinos
[
18
, p. 115]
.
We should sum
|\mathcal{M}|^{2}
in Eq. (
2.3
) over the
polarizations
\lambda
of a plasmon. Since we consider the nonchiral
medium, we have one longitudinal,
\lambda=\mathrm{L}
, and two transverse
plasmons,
\lambda=s=1,2
, with the degenerate dispersion relation;
cf. Appendix
B
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}|\mathcal{M}|^{2}=

\displaystyle 4e^{2}G_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}[p_{1}^{\mu}p_{2}^{\nu}+p_{2}^{\mu}p_{1}^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}(p_{1}p_{2})]\left(\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}Q_{\mu\nu}-\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}R_{\mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=4e^{2}G_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\bigg\{\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}(\omega,k)\left(1-\frac{\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\left[2E_{1}(E_{1}-\omega)+\frac{\omega^{2}-k^{2}}{2}\right]_{\mathrm{long}}

\displaystyle+\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}(\omega,k)\left(1-\frac{\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\left[2E_{1}(E_{1}-\omega)+\frac{\omega^{2}+k^{2}}{2}\right]_{\mathrm{trans}}\bigg\},

(2.4)

where the subscripts mean that one should account for either longitudinal
or transverse dispersion relations which are given in Eqs. (
D.4
)
and (
D.10
). To derive Eq. (
2
) we
represent the plasmon momentum as
q^{\mu}=(\omega,\mathbf{k})
.
In Eq. (
2
), we use the conservation laws,

E_{1}=E_{2}+\omega,\quad\mathbf{p}_{1}=\mathbf{p}_{2}+\mathbf{k},

(2.5)

with both neutrinos and the plasmon being on mass shell. Moreover,
in Eq. (
2
), we take into account the properties of
the projections operators,
Q_{\mu\nu}
and
R_{\mu\nu}
(defined
in Eq. (
B.2
)), which are given in Eq. (
B.4
).

Based on Eq. (
2.5
), one gets that the Cherenkov emission
is possible if
k>\omega
. Using Eq. (
D.4
),
we obtain that, for longitudinal plasmons, this condition is fulfilled
when
\omega>\omega_{p}\sqrt{1+3T/m}
, where
\omega_{p}
is the
plasma frequency. On the contrary, based on Eq. (
D.10
),
we get that the condition
k>\omega
is never satisfied for transverse
plasmons. Analogous result was obtained in Ref.
[
17
]
for
an ultrarelativistic plasma. Therefore, we take into account only
the longitudinal plasmons contribution in Eq. (
2
).

Suppose that the neutrino gas occupies the volume
V
. The emissivity
of the gas by Cherenkov plasmons, i.e. the energy emitted per unit
time, reads

\displaystyle\dot{E}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{V\mathrm{d}^{3}p_{1}}{2E_{1}(2\pi)^{3}E_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p_{2}}{2E_{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{2\omega(2\pi)^{3}}\omega(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(p_{2}+q-p_{1})

\displaystyle\times\frac{\omega}{k}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{\mathrm{d}k}\sum_{\lambda}|\mathcal{M}|^{2}f_{1}(1-2f_{2}),

(2.6)

where
f_{1,2}=\left\{\exp[(E_{1,2}-\mu_{\nu})/T_{\nu}]+1\right\}^{-1}

are the distribution functions of incoming and outgoing neutrinos,
corresponding to the temperature
T_{\nu}
and the chemical potential

\mu_{\nu}
. Since we apply our results for the cluster cooling,
the neutrino temperature inside a cluster is not equal to that of
charged leptons,
T_{\nu}\equiv T_{\mathrm{clust}}\neq T
.

We take that neutrinos are ultrarelativistic. Nevertheless, we account
for their small but nonzero masses. Thus, two polarization degrees
stay in the Pauli blocking factor for outgoing neutrinos in Eq. (
2.6
).
We also average over the spin states for incoming neutrinos. The matrix
element, summed over the plasmon polarizations (in fact we have only
one polarization for a longitudinal plasmon), is given in Eq. (
2
).
Finally, we mention that, in Eq. (
2.6
), we account
for the electric charge renormalization in Eq. (
B.13
).

The integration over the momentum of an outgoing neutrino is made
with help of the momentum conservation
\delta
-function
[
17
]
.
Then, we integrate over the angle between
\mathbf{p}_{1}
and
\mathbf{k}

using the remaining energy conservation
\delta
-function
[
17
]
.
Changing the integration variable
k\to\omega
(see, Appendix
B
),
one obtains the range of the
\omega
variation,
\omega_{\mathrm{min}}<\omega<\omega_{\mathrm{max}}
.
The maximal value
\omega_{\mathrm{max}}
is the solution of the
equation

k(\omega)+\omega=2E_{1},

(2.7)

where
k(\omega)
is the dispersion relation for longitudinal plasmons
in Eq. (
D.4
). The minimal value
\omega_{\mathrm{min}}

is again given by Eq. (
D.4
) at
k=\omega
. It
is interesting to mention that the condition
\omega<\omega_{\mathrm{max}}
,
with
\omega_{\mathrm{max}}
given by Eq. (
2.7
),
guarantees that the matrix element in Eq. (
2
) is
positive.

Using the fact that
\omega^{2}-k^{2}=\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}(\omega,k)

for longitudinal plasmons, we rewrite Eq. (
2.6
) in
the form,

\displaystyle\dot{E}=

\displaystyle\frac{VG_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}c_{\mathrm{V}}^{2}}{8\pi^{3}e^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}E_{1}\int_{\omega_{\mathrm{min}}}^{\omega_{\mathrm{max}}}\omega^{5}\mathrm{d}\omega f(E_{1})\left[1-2f(|E_{1}-\omega|)\right]\left(1-\frac{\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\times\left[2E_{1}(E_{1}-\omega)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\right)\right],

(2.8)

where
c_{\mathrm{V}}=\tfrac{1}{2}+2\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}

and
c_{\mathrm{A}}=\tfrac{1}{2}
for the gas of electron neutrinos.
If
\nu=\nu_{\mu,\tau}
, one has that
c_{\mathrm{V}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}+2\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}

and
c_{\mathrm{A}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}
. Here,
\theta_{\mathrm{W}}

is the Weinberg angle, with
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}=0.23
. The
integral in Eq. (
2.8
) is to be evaluated numerically.

3
Neutrino cluster cooling

In this section, we apply the results of Sec.
2

to estimate the cooling rate of a neutrino cluster.

We assume that a neutrino cluster is formed in the early universe
owing to a fluctuation. The temperature of background plasma is

T
at that time. Since the neutrino gas becomes denser in a cluster,
its temperature is higher than
T
:
T_{\mathrm{clust}}>T
. To find
the relation between
T_{\mathrm{clust}}
and
T
, we should consider
a particular cluster.

The numerical simulations of the cluster structure were carried out
in Ref.
[
11
]
for different parameters of the neutrino gas
and scalar particles. The samples of these simulations are present

2
. We consider in details one of the clusters

2

potential
\mu_{\nu}^{(\mathrm{now})}=0.6m_{\nu}
, the radius
R_{\mathrm{now}}\approx 5m_{s}^{-1}

and the maximal Fermi momentum
p_{\mathrm{F}}^{(\mathrm{max})}\approx 0.6m_{\nu}
.
These cluster parameters correspond to the present time universe.
We take that the neutrino mass is
m_{\nu}=0.1\,\text{eV}
.

The distribution of the Fermi momentum inside neutrino clusters. In

and

to
\mu_{\nu}^{(\mathrm{now})}=0.6m_{\nu}

line corresponds to
\mu_{\nu}^{(\mathrm{now})}=0.15m_{\nu}
,

. All cluster

.

Since neutrinos are nonrelativistic and degenerate in the present
universe, the neutrino density inside a cluster now has the value

n_{\mathrm{clust}}^{(\mathrm{now})}=2\int_{0}^{p_{\mathrm{F}}^{(\mathrm{max})}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}=9.5\times 10^{8}\,\text{cm}^{-3}.

(3.1)

Here we consider the upper bound for the neutrino density taking
p_{\mathrm{F}}=p_{\mathrm{F}}^{(\mathrm{max})}=6\times 10^{-2}\,\text{eV}

in the whole cluster. The density of cosmic neutrinos in the present
universe is
n^{(\mathrm{now})}=56\,\text{cm}^{-3}
. We assume that
a cluster evolves in time, with its size being affected only by the
universe expansion. Thus, one has the following relation between
n_{\mathrm{clust}}^{(\mathrm{now})}

and
n^{(\mathrm{now})}
, as well as
n_{\mathrm{clust}}
and
n

which are the densities of neutrinos inside a cluster and of background
neutrinos at the time of the cluster formation:

\frac{n_{\mathrm{clust}}}{n}=\frac{n_{\mathrm{clust}}^{(\mathrm{now})}}{n^{(\mathrm{now})}}=1.7\times 10^{7}.

(3.2)

We assume that the cluster formation happens adiabatically, with the
heat capacity ratio
\gamma=5/3
. Thus, one gets the relation between
T_{\mathrm{clust}}

and
T
,

\frac{T_{\mathrm{clust}}}{T}=\left(\frac{n_{\mathrm{clust}}}{n}\right)^{\gamma-1}=6.6\times 10^{4},

(3.3)

where we use Eq. (
3.2
).

Since the neutrino distribution functions in Eq. (
2.8
)
correspond to
T_{\mathrm{clust}}
it is convenient to normalize
the integration variables
E_{1}
and
\omega
to
T_{\mathrm{clust}}
,
with
x=E_{1}/T_{\mathrm{clust}}
and
y=\omega/T_{\mathrm{clust}}

being dimensionless parameters. Finally, we rewrite Eq. (
2.8
)
in the form,

\displaystyle\dot{E}=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{3}G_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}c_{\mathrm{V}}^{2}T_{\mathrm{clust}}^{9}}{24\pi^{3}\alpha}I,

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{e^{x-\xi}+1}\int_{y_{1}}^{y_{2}}y^{5}\mathrm{d}y\frac{e^{|x-y|-\xi}-1}{e^{|x-y|-\xi}+1}\left(1-\frac{y^{2}}{y_{k}^{2}}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\times\left[2x(x-y)+\frac{1}{2}\left(y^{2}-y_{k}^{2}\right)\right],

(3.4)

where
y_{k}=y_{k}(y)
obeys the relation
y^{4}=b^{2}(y^{2}+ay_{k}^{2})

(see Eq. (
D.4
)),
y_{1}=b\sqrt{1+a}
,
y_{2}=y_{2}(x)

is the maximal real root of the equation
y_{k}+y=2x
(see Eq. (
2.7
)),

\xi=\mu_{\nu}/T_{\mathrm{clust}}
,
a=3T/m
,
b=\omega_{p}/T_{\mathrm{clust}}
,
and
\alpha=\tfrac{e^{2}}{4\pi}=7.3\times 10^{-3}
is the fine structure
constant. We remind that we deal with a nonrelativistic primordial
plasma. Thus, we consider the range of temperatures
T\lesssim m
.

The dimensionless parameter
b
is temperature dependent,
b=\omega_{p}/T_{\mathrm{clust}}\propto\sqrt{T}
.
Using Eq. (
3.3
), we write down that

b(T)=2.5\times 10^{-12}\left(\frac{T}{\text{keV}}\right)^{1/2}.

(3.5)

To obtain Eq. (
3.5
) we take that the electron density
at the cluster formation reads
n_{e}(T)=n_{e}^{(\mathrm{now})}\frac{T^{3}}{T_{\mathrm{CMB}}^{3}}
,
where the present time electron density
n_{e}^{(\mathrm{now})}=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{baryon}}}{m_{p}}=2.5\times 10^{-7}\,\text{cm}^{-3}
,
the present time density of baryonic matter
\rho_{\mathrm{baryon}}\approx 4.2\times 10^{-31}\,\text{g}\cdot\text{cm}^{-3}
,
which is about
(4-5)\,\%
of the critical density
\rho_{c}\approx 9.4\times 10^{-30}\,\text{g}\cdot\text{cm}^{-3}
,

T_{\mathrm{CMB}}=2.7\,\text{K}
is the temperature of the cosmic
microwave background radiation, and
m_{p}
is the proton mass. Here,
we assume that, roughly, the present time universe consists of the
electroneutral hydrogen plasma.

In Eq. (
3.4
), we assume that plasmons carry away
the energy from the entire cluster. However, Cherenkov plasmons are
unstable. It results from the nonzero imaginary part of
\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}

in Eq. (
D.7
). Hence, if emitted plasmon decays inside
a cluster, its energy is returned back to medium. We have to estimate
the propagation length
L
of a plasmon. The total cooling rate of
a cluster depends whether
L
is greater or smaller than the cluster
radius
R
. We obtained in Ref.
[
14
]
that
L\ll R
for the
ultrarelativistic plasma. In that situation, we supposed that a cluster
cools down layer-by-layer. It significantly increased the cooling
time. Now, we revisit this issue in case of the nonrelativistic plasma.

We estimate
L
as
L=v_{\mathrm{ph}}t
, where
v_{\mathrm{ph}}=\mathrm{d}\omega/\mathrm{d}k

is the plasmon phase velocity,
t\approx 1/\delta\omega
is the typical
time of the plasmon propagation, and
\delta\omega
is the imaginary
part of the frequency given in Eq. (
D.8
). Therefore,
the ratio
\rho=R/L
reads

\rho=\sqrt{\pi}R\left(\frac{m}{2T}\right)^{3/2}\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{\mathrm{d}\omega}\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{k^{5}}(k^{2}-\omega^{2})^{2}e^{-\frac{m\omega^{2}}{2Tk^{2}}},

(3.6)

where

\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{\mathrm{d}\omega}=\frac{m}{3T}\frac{\omega}{k}\left(2\frac{\omega^{2}}{\omega_{p}^{2}}-1\right),

(3.7)

is calculated based on Eq. (
D.4
). Note that, at

\omega=\omega_{\mathrm{min}}
,
k=\omega
and
\rho\to 0
. Thus,

L\gg R
.

Then, using Eq. (
D.4
), rewrite
\rho
in Eq. (
3.6
)
in the form,

\rho=\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{3/2}\frac{R\omega}{a^{3/2}}\frac{(2\chi^{2}-1)(\chi^{2}-1-a)^{2}}{\chi^{2}(\chi^{2}-1)^{3}}\exp\left[-\frac{3}{2(\chi^{2}-1)}\right],

(3.8)

where
\chi=\omega/\omega_{p}
and
a=3T/m\ll 1
. We estimate the
typical plasmon energy as
\omega\sim T_{\mathrm{clust}}
. Thus,

\chi=T_{\mathrm{clust}}/\omega_{p}=b^{-1}\gg 1
, where
b
is given
in Eq. (
3.5
). Therefore, we get the limiting expression
for
\rho
in Eq. (
3.8
),

\rho\to 2\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{3/2}RT_{\mathrm{clust}}\frac{b^{2}}{a^{3/2}}=4.5\times 10^{-14}\left(\frac{T}{\text{keV}}\right)^{-1/2},

(3.9)

where we use Eqs. (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), as well
as take that
R_{\mathrm{now}}=5m_{s}^{-1}
and
m_{s}=10^{-4}\,\text{eV}
.
Note that the cluster radius at the time of its formation,
R
, is
related to
R_{\mathrm{now}}
by
R=R_{\mathrm{now}}T_{\mathrm{CMB}}/T
.
Even if
T\sim\text{a few keV}
, one can see that
\rho\ll 1
in
Eq. (
3.9
). Hence,
R\ll L
and the approximation implied
in Eq. (
3.4
) is valid.

To lower the cluster temperature
T_{\mathrm{clust}}
to the value

T
outside, the cooling time should be less than the universe age

H^{-1}
, where
H=T^{2}/M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{*}
is the Hubble parameter,

M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{*}=M_{\mathrm{Pl}}/1.66\sqrt{g_{*}}
,
M_{\mathrm{Pl}}=1.2\times 10^{19}\,\text{GeV}

is the Planck mass, and
g_{*}
is the number of the relativistic
degrees of freedom. Since
\dot{E}
in Eq. (
3.4
)
is the emissivity of the entire cluster, the cooling time can be estimated
as
t_{\mathrm{cool}}\sim E_{\mathrm{clust}}/\dot{E}
, where
E_{\mathrm{clust}}=N_{\nu}\bar{E}_{\nu}

is the energy of all neutrinos in the cluster at the moment of its
formation,
N_{\nu}=n_{\mathrm{clust}}V
is the number of neutrinos
inside the cluster and
\bar{E}_{\nu}\sim T_{\mathrm{clust}}
is
the mean neutrino energy in the cluster.

Thus, we should demonstrate that the parameter

\Xi=\frac{N_{\nu}T_{\mathrm{clust}}H}{\dot{E}}=\frac{1.1\times 10^{-22}}{I(T)}\left(\frac{T}{\text{keV}}\right)^{-3},

(3.10)

is less than one. In Eq. (
3.10
), we account for Eq. (
3.3
),
take that
g_{*}\approx 10
at
T<1\,\text{MeV}

[

The simulation of the neutrino clusters structure in Ref.

.

4
Conclusion

We have studied the emission of Cherenkov plasmons by primordial neutrinos
which form the gas with a nonzero temperature and a chemical potential.
Despite a neutrino is an electrically neutral particle, one can consider
its induced electric charge in medium owing to the loop effects
[
15
]
.
It is this charge which is responsible for the neutrino Cherenkov
radiation.

Previously, Cherenkov radiation of neutrinos was discussed in the
context of an individual particle moving through medium
[
16
,
17
]
.
In our work, we considered a gas of neutrinos emitting plasmons. Thus,
we had to average the matrix element over the incoming and outgoing
neutrinos having the Fermi-Dirac distribution functions.

Our calculations implied the generalized polarization tensor of a
plasmon and the plasmon form factors in medium. We have provided their
computation in Appendices
A
-
C

using the imaginary time perturbation theory. We have decided to provide
these computations, firstly, for the convenience of a reader. Secondly,
as a rule, the plasmon polarization tensor is calculated in the Hard Thermal Loops (HTL) limit (see, e.g., Ref.
[
23
, pp. 118–124]
)
where one looses the chemical potential contribution. In our work,
we consider the situation of nonrelativistic plasma when the density
of charged leptons, or their chemical potential, is important.

In Sec.
2
, we have derived the general expression
for the neutrino gas emissivity. It was found that only longitudinal
plasmons contribute to the matrix element. Previously, this fact was
mentioned in Ref.
[
17
]
in case of the ultraretivistic plasma.
We have also established the range for the integration over the plasmon
frequencies.

Then, in Sec.
3
, we have considered the application
of our results for the cooling a neutrino cluster formed in the early
universe. This kind of neutrino clusters were described, first, in
Ref.
[
8
]
. Neutrinos are held together inside a cluster
by the exchange of hypothetical light scalar particles. However, when
a cluster is formed, the neutrino gas is compressed and heats up.
Hence, a cluster can be destroyed by thermal effects. The cooling
mechanisms put forward in Ref.
[
8
]
turned out to be inefficient.

In Ref.
[
11
]
, we proposed the mechanism for the cluster
cooling based on the emission of Cherenkov plasmons by neutrinos in
a cluster. However, the consideration of this process was quite estimatory
in Ref.
[
11
]
. The description of the cluster cooling by
the mechanism in question was refined in Ref.
[
14
]
. We used
the model of the ultrerelativistic plasma Ref.
[
14
]
. However,
the temperature interval where the cooling is efficient, obtained
in Ref.
[
14
]
, spanned both relativistic and nonrelativistic
ranges.

In the present work, we have reconsidered the cluster cooling in case
when plasma is nonrelativistic,
T<m
. This situation is of the main
importance since neutrinos are sure to decouple from the primordial
plasma at that epoch. We remind that the neutrino decoupling happens
at
\sim(2-3)\,\text{MeV}

[
19
, pp. 22–23]
. Therefore
a cluster is not destroyed by thermal effects associated with neutrinos.

Using particular cluster parameters, obtained in Ref.
[
11
]
,
we have found that the Cherenkov plasmon emission is, indeed, efficient
to cool down some clusters since the cooling rate is higher than the
universe expansion. It happens for clusters with greater radii. We
have found that the proposed cooling mechanism is valid if a cluster
is formed at
T\gtrsim 220\,\text{keV}
. The neutrino decoupling temperature
is within this range. We have also found that the nonzero chemical
potential of neutrinos does not contribute significantly to the cluster
cooling rate.

In our work, we studied the case of electron neutrinos interacting
with a hydrogen plasma. In principle, a cluster can consist of other
flavor neutrinos. However, the proposed cooling mechanism is not efficient
for
\nu_{\mu}
and
\nu_{\tau}
. Indeed, the vector coupling constant,
which
\dot{E}
in Eq. (
2.8
) is proportional to, is
much smaller for other flavor neutrinos,
c_{\mathrm{V}}^{(\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau})}\ll c_{\mathrm{V}}^{(\nu_{e})}
.

Appendix B
Plasmon properties in matter

In this Appendix, we list the properties of a plasmon in background
matter. Here, we rely on Ref.
[
20
]
.

The plasmon characteristics depend on the polarization tensor in matter

\Pi_{\mu\nu}
, which can be represented in the following form:

\Pi_{\mu\nu}=\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}Q_{\mu\nu}+\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}R_{\mu\nu}+\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}P_{\mu\nu},

(B.1)

where
\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}
is the longitudinal form factor,
\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}

is the transverse form factor, and
\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}
is the parity
violating one. As usual, we denote the plasmon momentum as
q^{\mu}=(\omega,\mathbf{k})
.
Moreover, we choose the frame where the background matter is at rest
having the four velocity
v^{\mu}=(1,\mathbf{0})
. Then, the projection
operators in Eq. (
B.1
) have the form,

\displaystyle Q_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{q^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(v_{\mu}-\omega\frac{q_{\mu}}{q^{2}}\right)\left(v_{\nu}-\omega\frac{q_{\nu}}{q^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q^{2}}-Q_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle P_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{k}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}q^{\alpha}v^{\beta},

(B.2)

where
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
is the antisymmetric tensor
having
\varepsilon^{0123}=+1
.

It is instructive to write down the components of the projection operators
in Eq. (
B.2
),

\displaystyle Q_{00}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k^{2}}{q^{2}},\quad Q_{0i}=\frac{\omega k_{i}}{q^{2}},\quad Q_{ij}=-\frac{\omega^{2}}{q^{2}}\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}},

\displaystyle R_{00}

\displaystyle=0,\quad R_{0i}=0,\quad R_{ij}=-\delta_{ij}+\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle P_{00}

\displaystyle=P_{0i}=0,\quad P_{ij}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{k}\varepsilon_{ijk}k_{k}.

(B.3)

Moreover, the projection operators obey the properties,

\displaystyle Q^{2}

\displaystyle=Q,\quad R^{2}=R,\quad QR=0,

\displaystyle P^{2}

\displaystyle=R,\quad PQ=0,\quad RP=PR=P,

(B.4)

which are represented in the symbolic form.

The dressed plasmon propagator in matter has the form,

D_{\mu\nu}=\frac{Q_{\mu\nu}}{q^{2}-\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{R_{\mu\nu}+P_{\mu\nu}}{q^{2}-\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}-\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{R_{\mu\nu}-P_{\mu\nu}}{q^{2}-\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}+\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}}+\dotsc,

(B.5)

where the omit the term
\propto q_{\mu}q_{\nu}
. Based on Eq. (
B.5
),
we get the dispersion relation for longitudinal plasmons,
q^{2}=\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}
,
and for the transverse ones,
q^{2}=\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}\pm\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}
.

We choose the polarization vector for a longitudinal plasmon as

e_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{2}-\omega^{2}}}\left(k,\frac{\mathbf{k}\omega}{k}\right).

(B.6)

The definition in Eq. (
B.6
) is adapted to the case when

k>\omega

[
16
]
. In this situation, for transverse
plasmons, we define the polarization vectors,

e_{\mu}^{(\pm)}=\frac{1}{2}(e_{1\mu}\pm ie_{2\mu}),

(B.7)

where
e_{1,2}^{\mu}=(0,\mathbf{e}_{1,2})
and
(\mathbf{e}_{1}\times\mathbf{e}_{2})=\hat{\mathbf{k}}
.
Using Eq. (
B.3
), one gets that

\displaystyle e_{\mathrm{L}\mu}e_{\mathrm{L}\nu}

\displaystyle=Q_{\mu\nu},

(B.8)

\displaystyle e_{\mu}^{(\pm)*}e_{\nu}^{(\pm)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(R_{\mu\nu}\mp P_{\mu\nu}),

(B.9)

which are used to deal with the plasmon polarizations in Eq. (
2
).

If matter is nonchiral, then
\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}=0
. Thus, the relation
in Eq. (
B.8
) still holds true for longitudinal plasmons.
For transverse ones, we can use
e_{1,2}^{\mu}
as the polarization
vectors. Instead of Eq. (
B.9
), we have

\sum_{s=1,2}e_{s,\mu}^{*}e_{s,\nu}=-R_{\mu\nu},

(B.10)

to sum over the plasmon polarizations.

We also provide the relations between the plasmon form factors and
the components of
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
. Based on Eqs. (
B.1
)-(
B.3
),
we obtain that

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\Pi_{00},

(B.11)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Pi_{\mu}^{\mu}-\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}\right).

(B.12)

The expression for
\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}
is proportional to an antisymmetric
part of
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
if there is any.

Finally, we mention that the elementary electric charge
e
, which
is the coupling constant between electromagnetic and electron-positron
fields, should be renormalized in background matter. Thus, we should
replace

e\to e\sqrt{Z},\quad Z=\frac{\omega}{k}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{\mathrm{d}k}.

(B.13)

As a rule, the matrix element of a process, involving a plasmon,
\mathcal{M}\propto e
.
Moreover, one typically integrates
|\mathcal{M}|^{2}
over the plasmon
phase volume
\mathrm{d}^{3}k=k^{2}\mathrm{d}k\mathrm{d}\Omega
;
cf. Eq. (
2.6
). Therefore, the renormalization procedure
in Eq. (
B.13
) is equivalent to the change of the integration
variable,
Z\mathrm{d}k=\omega\mathrm{d}\omega/k
.

\Pi_{\mu\nu}
.
Indeed, the Maxwell plasma is not a chiral medium. Thus,
\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}=0
.
Nevertheless, we provide its computation since, formally, it contributes
to the plasmon emission by neutrinos since
c_{\mathrm{A}}\neq 0

in Eq. (
2.2
). One can
show that the term
\Pi^{0i}\propto c_{\mathrm{A}}
vanishes. Thus,
one has to compute only
\Pi^{ij}\propto c_{\mathrm{A}}
.

Using Eq. (
2.2
), one
gets that

\displaystyle\Pi^{ij}=

\displaystyle 4\mathrm{i}c_{\mathrm{A}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\varepsilon^{ijk0}p_{k}(p-k)_{0}+\varepsilon^{ij0k}p_{0}(p-k)_{k}}{[p^{2}-m^{2}][(p-k)^{2}-m^{2}]}

\displaystyle=-4\mathrm{i}c_{\mathrm{A}}\varepsilon_{ijk}\left(S^{\prime}_{1}[p_{k}]-S_{1}[p_{k}-k_{k}]\right),

(C.9)

where
S_{1}
and
S^{\prime}_{1}
are given in Eq. (
A.3
).
Analogously to the computation of
\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}
and
\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}
,
we represent Eq. (
C
) in the form,

\displaystyle\Pi^{ij}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}c_{\mathrm{A}}}{\pi^{2}\mathbf{q}^{2}}\varepsilon_{ijk}q_{k}\mathrm{Re}\bigg[q_{0}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{p^{2}}{E}\mathrm{d}p(n-\bar{n})

\displaystyle\times\left[1+\frac{q_{0}^{2}-\mathbf{q}^{2}}{4p|\mathbf{q}|}\left(\frac{2E}{q_{0}}-1\right)\right]\ln\left(\frac{q_{0}^{2}-\mathbf{q}^{2}-2q_{0}E+2p|\mathbf{q}|}{q_{0}^{2}-\mathbf{q}^{2}-2q_{0}E-2p|\mathbf{q}|}\right)\bigg].

(C.10)

Using the approximation Ref.
[
24
, p. 210]
, we rewrite Eq. (
C
)
as

\Pi_{ij}=\mathrm{i}\frac{c_{\mathrm{A}}}{\pi^{2}}\frac{\omega}{k^{2}}\varepsilon_{ijk}k_{k}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{p^{2}}{E}\mathrm{d}p(n-\bar{n})\ln\left(\frac{\omega-kv}{\omega+kv}\right).

(C.11)

We stress that one cannot relate
\Pi_{ij}
in Eq. (
C.11
)
to
\Pi_{\mathrm{P}}
in Eq. (
B.1
).

Appendix D
Dispersion relations of plasmons

In this Appendix, using the results of Appendix
C
,
we derive the dispersion relations for plasmons and evaluate their
damping. Since we deal with the plasmons dispersion, we should put

c_{\mathrm{V}}=e^{2}
and
c_{\mathrm{A}}=0
.

First, we analyze longitudinal plasmons. We show in Appendix
B

that the dispersion relation results from
\omega^{2}-k^{2}=\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}
.
Using Eq. (
C.5
), one gets that the dispersion relation
reads

k^{2}=-\frac{4\alpha}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{p^{2}}{E}\mathrm{d}p(n+\bar{n})\left[1+\frac{\omega^{2}-k^{2}}{\omega^{2}-k^{2}v^{2}}+\frac{\omega}{kv}\ln\frac{\omega-kv}{\omega+kv}\right],

(D.1)

where
\alpha=e^{2}/4\pi=7.3\times 10^{-3}
is the fine structure
constant.

To analyze the integral expression in Eq. (
D.1
)
we restrict ourselves to the nonrelativistic plasma. The properly
normalized electron distribution function has the form,

n(p)=\frac{n_{e}}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{mT}\right)^{3/2}e^{-\frac{p^{2}}{2mT}},

(D.2)

where
n_{e}
is the mean electron density. Moreover, we put
\bar{n}=0

since positron distribution is exponentially suppressed, as well as
we take that
p=mv
and
E=m
. Considering small
v
, one gets
the expression in square brackets in the integrand of Eq. (
D.1
),

\left[\dots\right]\approx-\frac{k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\left[1-v^{2}\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)+v^{4}\frac{2}{5}\frac{k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right].

(D.3)

Using Eq. (
D.3
) and integrating over
v
, we rewrite
Eq. (
D.1
) as

\omega^{2}=\omega_{p}^{2}\left(1+\frac{3T}{m}\frac{k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right),

(D.4)

where
\omega_{p}=\sqrt{4\pi\alpha n_{e}/m}
is the plasma frequency.
In deriving Eq. (
D.4
), we keep only the leading
terms in the small parameter
T/m
. Note that Eq. (
D.4
)
coincides with the known result, e.g., in Refs.
[
24
, p. 213]

and
[
25
, p. 134]
.

We notice that, while integrating over the interval
0<v<\infty
,
one encounters a pole at
v=\omega/k<1
if
k>\omega
. One resolves
this issue by assuming that the frequency acquires a small imaginary
part. This phenomenon is called the Landau damping
[
25
, pp. 124–127]
.
Nevertheless, one cannot directly use the Cauchy’s residue theorem
to compute the integral in this case. Indeed, one has to close the
integration contour either in the upper or in the lower half-planes. However,
the distribution function in Eq. (
D.2
) is singular
at
p=mv\to\pm\mathrm{i}\infty
.

Nonetheless, let us evaluate the imaginary part of
\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}

in case
\omega\to\omega+\mathrm{i}\delta\omega
.
Using Eq. (
D.2
), we rewrite Eq. (
C.5
)
in the form,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}=

\displaystyle\frac{4\alpha m^{2}}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\bigg[-\frac{\omega^{2}-k^{2}}{2xk^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nv^{2}\mathrm{d}v\left(\frac{1}{v-x}-\frac{1}{v+x}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\omega}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}nv\mathrm{d}v\int_{\dots}^{x}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{x^{\prime}-v}-\frac{1}{x^{\prime}+v}\right)\bigg]_{x=\omega/k}.

(D.5)

Then, we change
x\to x+\mathrm{i}0
and account for a ‘semiresidue’
of the integrand in Eq. (
D
) with help of the relation,

\frac{1}{v-x-\mathrm{i}0}=\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{v-x}+\mathrm{i}\pi\delta(v-x),

(D.6)

where
\mathcal{P}
stays for the principle value of the integral.

We decompose the longitudinal form factor into the real and imaginary
parts,
\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}=\mathrm{Re}(\Pi{}_{\mathrm{L}})+\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}(\Pi{}_{\mathrm{L}})
.
Using Eqs. (
D
) and (
D.6
), we obtain

\mathrm{Im}(\Pi{}_{\mathrm{L}})
in the form,

\mathrm{Im}(\Pi{}_{\mathrm{L}})=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{m}{T}\right)^{3/2}\omega_{p}^{2}\frac{\omega}{k^{5}}(k^{2}-\omega^{2})^{2}e^{-\frac{m\omega^{2}}{2Tk^{2}}}.

(D.7)

If
\omega
acquires a small imaginary part, the plasmon four momentum
reads
q^{2}\approx\omega^{2}-k^{2}+2\mathrm{i}\omega\delta\omega
.
Note that the real part of
\Pi_{\mathrm{L}}
, which is given in
Eq. (
C.5
), obeys
\omega^{2}-k^{2}=\mathrm{Re}(\Pi{}_{\mathrm{L}})
.
Therefore, based on Eq. (
D.7
), we obtain the following
expression for
\delta\omega
:

\delta\omega=\frac{\mathrm{Im}(\Pi{}_{\mathrm{L}})}{2\omega}=\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{m}{2T}\right)^{3/2}\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{k^{5}}(k^{2}-\omega^{2})^{2}e^{-\frac{m\omega^{2}}{2Tk^{2}}}.

(D.8)

It should be noted that
\delta\omega>0
in Eq. (
D.8
)
as predicted in Ref.
[
25
, p. 122]
.

Now, we provide the basic results for the transverse plasmons dispersion
relation. Using Eqs. (
C.8
) and (
D.2
),
one obtains that

\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}\approx\omega_{p}^{2}\left(1+\frac{T}{m}\frac{k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right),

(D.9)

where we keep only the leading contributions in the small parameter

T/m
. Based on the result of Appendix
C
and
Eq. (
D.9
), we derive the dispersion relation for
transverse plasmons as

\omega^{2}=k^{2}+\omega_{p}^{2}\left(1+\frac{T}{m}\frac{k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right),

(D.10)

which coincides with the result of Ref.
[
24
, p. 213]
. The
Landau damping was shown in Ref.
[
25
, pp. 130–131]
to
be suppressed for transverse plasmons.
