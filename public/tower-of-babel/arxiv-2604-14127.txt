Lagrangian correspondences for moduli spaces of Higgs bundles and holomorphic connections
Panagiotis Dimakis, Đinh Quý Dương, Shengjing Xu

Abstract
On a compact connected Riemann surface C C of genus at least 2 2 , we construct Lagrangian correspondences between moduli spaces of rank- n n Higgs bundles (respectively, holomorphic connections) and the Hilbert schemes of points on T ∗ ​ C T^{\ast}C (respectively, the twisted cotangent bundles of C C ). Central to these constructions are Higgs bundles (respectively, holomorphic connections) which are transversal to line subbundles of the underlying bundles: these naturally induce divisors on C C together with auxiliary parameters, namely lifts to divisors on spectral curves for Higgs bundles and residue parameters of apparent singularities for holomorphic connections. We discuss the evidence showing that the Dolbeault geometric Langlands correspondence is generically realized by these Lagrangian correspondences; we expect that the de Rham geometric Langlands correspondence can be realized by their quantization, following Drinfeld’s construction of Hecke eigensheaves. We also discuss the relations of our constructions to various topics, including reductions of Kapustin-Witten equations, the conformal limit, separation of variables and degenerate fields in conformal field theories.

On the spectral side: skyscraper sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathcal{M}_{dR}(\check{G}))
– the Langlands dual of these skyscraper sheaves are Hecke eigensheaves
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
whose singular supports are in the nilpotent cone and microlocal supports are generically line bundles on Hitchin fibers;

•

On the automorphic side: the Whittaker sheaf
\mathrm{Whit}_{G}
, which is dual to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{dR}(\check{G})}
and has the Hitchin section
\mdmathbb{L}_{H}(\varnothing)
as microlocal support, and its Hecke transforms, which ought to have
\mdmathbb{L}_{H}(D)
as microlocal supports upon appropriate restriction.

Such an intertwining of bases is the signature that makes
Separation of Variables
techniques powerful in the theories of systems of PDE in general and of integrable systems in particular.
Our Lagrangian correspondences in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, which we conjecture to realize the (classical) Dolbeault GLC, appear to be the
classical changes of variables
compatible with the spectral decomposition
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}\mapstochar\rightarrow\sigma^{[d]}\mapstochar\rightarrow\sigma
in the de Rham GLC.

2.1
Moduli of Higgs bundles

From this point forward we fix
C
to be a smooth projective curve over
\mdmathbb{C}
of genus
g\geq 2
. For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, let
\mathcal{H}^{ss}(n,\ell)
be the space of semistable Higgs bundles of rank
n
and degree
\ell
on
C
, parametrizing pairs
(E,\phi)
where
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
n
and degree
d
on
C
whose holomorphic structure we denote by
\bar{\partial}_{E}
, and
\phi\in H^{0}\!\left(C,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes K_{C}\right)
is a Higgs field satisfying
\bar{\partial}_{E}\phi=0
. The gauge group
\mathrm{GL}(E)
acts on such pairs

g\cdot(\bar{\partial}_{E},\phi):=(g^{-1}\circ\bar{\partial}_{E}\circ g,g^{-1}\circ\phi\circ g).

We define the moduli space of Higgs bundles as
\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell):=\mathcal{H}^{ss}(n,\ell)/\mathrm{GL}(E)
. Each point of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)
corresponds to the unique polystable representative in its
S
–equivalence class, and
\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)
is a quasi-projective variety of complex dimension
\dim_{\mdmathbb{C}}\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)=2n^{2}(g-1)+2
. The structure of the
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
moduli space is analogous, the only difference being that we impose the conditions
\det(E)=\mathcal{O}
and
\Tr(\phi)=0
.

Holomorphic symplectic form.

The stable locus of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)
can be equipped with a canonical holomorphic symplectic form
H
. A tangent vector at
(E,\phi)\in\mathcal{H}^{ss}(n,\ell)
is given by a pair

(\alpha,\beta)\in{}^{0,1}\!\left(C,\operatorname{End}(E)\right)\oplus{}^{1,0}\!\left(C,\operatorname{End}(E)\right)

satisfying the linearized Higgs field equation

\bar{\partial}_{E}\beta+[\alpha,\phi]=0.

For two such tangent vectors
(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
and
(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})
,

{}_{H}\bigl((\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}),(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})\bigr):=\int_{C}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\alpha_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2}\wedge\beta_{1}\right)

defines a closed, nondegenerate
(2,0)
–form on
\mathcal{H}^{ss}(n,d)
which descends to a symplectic form on the stable locus.

Hitchin fibration.

For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, the
Hitchin fibration
is the map

h:\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow B:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}\!\left(C,K_{C}^{i}\right),

(2.1)

sending
(E,\phi)
to the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of
\phi
; we call
B
the
Hitchin base
. Let
\pi:\operatorname{Tot}(K_{C})\rightarrow C
be the projection and let
\lambda\in H^{0}(\operatorname{Tot}(K_{C}),\pi^{\ast}K_{C})
be the tautological section. Writing
h(E,\phi)=b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
, the characteristic polynomial defines a section

\det\!\left(\lambda\cdot\mathrm{Id}-\pi^{\ast}\phi\right)=\lambda^{n}+\pi^{\ast}b_{1}\,\lambda^{n-1}+\cdots+\pi^{\ast}b_{n}\in H^{0}\!\left(\operatorname{Tot}(K_{C}),\pi^{\ast}K_{C}^{\otimes n}\right),

whose vanishing locus is the
spectral curve

C_{b}\subset\operatorname{Tot}(K_{C})
. The map
h
endows
\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)
with the structure of a completely integrable system. For a generic
b\in B
,
C_{b}
is smooth and the fiber
h^{-1}(b)
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C_{b})
with
d=\ell+n(n-1)(g-1)
. Let
B_{\mathrm{reg}}\subset B
be the locus of
b
for which
C_{b}
is smooth, and set
\mathcal{M}_{H,\mathrm{reg}}(n,\ell):=h^{-1}(B_{\mathrm{reg}})
.

Spectral correspondence.

Over
B_{\mathrm{reg}}
, a point of
\mathcal{M}_{H,\mathrm{reg}}(n,\ell)
corresponds to spectral data
(\widetilde{C},\mathcal{L})
, where
\widetilde{C}=C_{b}
is a smooth spectral curve with covering map
\pi:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
, and
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(\widetilde{C})
is a line bundle such that

(E,\phi)\ \cong\ \bigl(\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{L},\ \pi_{\ast}(\lambda:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\otimes\pi^{\ast}K_{C})\bigr).

Conversely, given
(E,\phi)
with smooth spectral curve
\widetilde{C}
, the spectral line bundle
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{(E,\phi)}
can be recovered as

\mathcal{L}_{(E,\phi)}\coloneqq\operatorname{coker}\!\left(\pi^{\ast}(\phi)-\lambda:\pi^{\ast}\!\left(E\otimes K_{C}^{-1}\right)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\pi^{\ast}E\right),

where
\lambda
acts by multiplication on
\pi^{\ast}E
along
\widetilde{C}
.

2.2
Moduli of holomorphic connections

For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, we consider holomorphic connections
(E,\nabla)
on
C
, where
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
n
and

\nabla:E\rightarrow E\otimes K_{C}

is a holomorphic connection, i.e. a
\mdmathbb{C}
–linear map satisfying
\nabla(fs)=f\nabla(s)+(\partial f)\otimes s
. A theorem of Atiyah–Weil asserts that
E
admits a holomorphic connection if and only if every indecomposable summand of
E
has degree
0
. This in particular forces
\deg(E)=0
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{dR}^{ss}(n)
denote the space of semistable pairs
(E,\nabla)
which further satisfy the compatibility condition
[\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla]=0
. Again, the gauge group acts on this space and we define the de Rham moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n):=\mathcal{H}_{dR}^{ss}(n)/\mathrm{GL}(E)
. It is a normal quasi-projective variety of complex dimension
\dim_{\mdmathbb{C}}\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n)=2n^{2}(g-1)+2
. Equivalently, it may be viewed as the moduli space of semisimple flat
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
–connections on
C
by sending
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla)\rightarrow\bar{\partial}_{E}+\nabla
.

Irreducible locus and tangent space.

We write
\mathcal{M}_{dR}^{\mathrm{irr}}(n)\subset\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n)
for the open locus of
irreducible
connections, i.e. those connections for which
E
has no proper nonzero
\nabla
–invariant holomorphic subbundles (equivalently, the monodromy representation of
\pi_{1}(C)
is irreducible). On
\mathcal{M}_{dR}^{\mathrm{irr}}(n)
the moduli space is smooth, and the tangent space at
(E,\nabla)
is canonically identified with

T_{(E,\nabla)}\mathcal{M}_{dR}^{\mathrm{irr}}(n)\ \cong\ \mdmathbb{H}^{1}\!\left(C,\ \mathcal{E}nd(E)\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{ad}(\nabla)\ }\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}\right),

where
\operatorname{ad}(\nabla)(u)=[\nabla,u]
.

Atiyah–Bott holomorphic symplectic form.

On
\mathcal{M}_{dR}^{\mathrm{irr}}(n)
there is a canonical holomorphic symplectic form
AB
. It is induced by the trace pairing
\operatorname{tr}:\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes\mathcal{E}nd(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}
via cup product and Serre duality, giving a bilinear pairing

{}_{\mathrm{AB}}:\ \mdmathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{(E,\nabla)})\otimes\mdmathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{(E,\nabla)})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mdmathbb{H}^{2}(C,K_{C})\cong\mdmathbb{C},

where
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{(E,\nabla)}
denotes the above two-term deformation complex. Equivalently, given a semisimple complex flat connection
\nabla=d+A
on a fixed smooth bundle and two tangent vectors
\delta_{1}A
and
\delta_{2}A
, we define the symplectic form

{}_{\mathrm{AB}}(\delta_{1}A,\delta_{2}A)=\int_{C}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\delta_{1}A\wedge\delta_{2}A\right).

This form descends to a non-degenerate symplectic form on
\mathcal{H}_{dR}^{s}(n)/\mathrm{GL}(E)
, namely on the stable locus.

The
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
case is treated similarly, after imposing
\det(E)\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}
and
\Tr(\nabla)=0
(equivalently, passing to
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
–connections with fixed determinant).

2.3
The Hodge moduli space

We now introduce the Hodge moduli space, which interpolates between the moduli space of Higgs bundles and the de Rham moduli space.

For the group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, the Hodge moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
parametrizes triples

(\lambda,E,\nabla_{\lambda}),\qquad\lambda\in\mdmathbb C,

up to gauge equivalence, where
E
is a rank-
n
holomorphic vector bundle on
C
, and
\nabla_{\lambda}
is a
\lambda
-connection on
E
, namely a
\mdmathbb C
-linear map

\nabla_{\lambda}:E\rightarrow E\otimes K_{C},\text{ satisfying }\nabla_{\lambda}(fs)=\lambda\,\partial f\otimes s+f\,\nabla_{\lambda}(s)

satisfying the compatibility condition
[\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda}]=0
.
When
\lambda=0
, the operator
\nabla_{\lambda}
is simply a Higgs field, while when
\lambda=1
, it is an ordinary holomorphic connection. More generally, for every
\lambda\neq 0
, rescaling by
\lambda^{-1}
identifies the fiber over
\lambda
with the de Rham moduli space. In this way,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(n)
fits into a natural family

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mdmathbb{C},

whose fiber over
0
is
\mathcal{M}_{H}
, and whose fibers over
\lambda\neq 0
are naturally identified with
\mathcal{M}_{dR}
.

For the group
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
, one defines
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\mathrm{SL}_{n})
similarly, by imposing the natural extra conditions.

There is a natural
\mdmathbb C^{\ast}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}
, given by scaling the parameter and the
\lambda
-connection:

t\cdot[(\lambda,E,\nabla_{\lambda})]=[(t\lambda,E,t\nabla_{\lambda})],\qquad t\in\mdmathbb C^{\ast}.

On the central fiber
\lambda=0
, this reduces to the usual scaling action on the Higgs fields.

By the nonabelian Hodge correspondence, the smooth locus of the Higgs/de Rham moduli space carries a hyperkähler structure. The corresponding twistor family is obtained by gluing the Hodge moduli spaces along the nonzero fibers, and the parameter
\lambda
may be viewed as the coordinate along the twistor line. Thus
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}
should be regarded as the holomorphic family underlying the twistor space of the nonabelian Hodge moduli space.

2.4
Hilbert schemes of points on a surface

Let
S
be a quasi-projective complex surface. For an integer
d\geq 1
, the Hilbert scheme of
d
points on
S
is the moduli space

\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S)

parametrizing
0
–dimensional subschemes
Z\subset S
of length
d
, equivalently ideal sheaves
I_{Z}\subset\mathcal{O}_{S}
with

\dim_{\mdmathbb{C}}H^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}/I_{Z})=d
.
If
S
is smooth, then
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S)
is smooth of complex dimension
2d
.

Hilbert–Chow morphism.

There is a natural morphism (the Hilbert–Chow map)

\rho:\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(S),

sending a subscheme
Z
to the associated effective
0
–cycle
\sum m_{i}p_{i}
. It is an isomorphism over the open locus of reduced subschemes
\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(S)\setminus\Delta
(i.e.
d
distinct points) and provides a resolution of singularities of the symmetric product.

Holomorphic symplectic structure.

Assume that
S
carries a holomorphic symplectic form, i.e. a non-degenerate holomorphic
2
–form

\omega_{S}\in H^{0}(S,{}_{S}^{2})
.
Then
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S)
admits a canonical holomorphic symplectic form
\omega_{S}^{[d]}
, uniquely characterized by the property that on the open locus of reduced subschemes

\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S)^{\mathrm{red}}\cong(S^{d}\setminus\Delta)/\mathfrak{S}_{d},

the form
\omega_{S}^{[d]}
agrees with the
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
–invariant
2
–form induced from
\omega_{S}
on each factor of
S^{d}
. In particular,
\omega_{S}^{[d]}
is closed and nondegenerate, hence holomorphic symplectic.

S=T^{\ast}C
and twisted cotangent bundles.

In this paper the symplectic surfaces we deal with are either the cotangent bundle of the curve
C
or a twisted version of it.

The cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}C
carries its tautological
1
–form
\theta
and the canonical holomorphic symplectic form

\omega_{T^{\ast}C}=\mathrm{d}\theta\in H^{0}(T^{\ast}C,{}_{T^{\ast}C}^{2}),

so
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(T^{\ast}C)
inherits a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega_{T^{\ast}C}^{[d]}
.

More generally, let
\mathcal{S}\rightarrow C
be a
twisted cotangent bundle
. By this we mean a holomorphic torsor under
T^{\ast}C
, namely an affine bundle locally isomorphic to
T^{\ast}C
, with transition functions given by translations by holomorphic
1

\omega_{T^{\ast}C}
, the local identifications glue to a global holomorphic symplectic form

\omega_{\mathcal{S}}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{S},{}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}),

and hence
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(\mathcal{S})
inherits a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}^{[d]}
.

(or

) opers, following

Definition 2.1.

An
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
-oper on a smooth curve

A
\mathrm{PSL}_{n}
-oper is the projectivization of an
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
-oper, which is denoted by
\mdmathbb P(E,\nabla,\{E_{i}\})
.

Remark 2.2.

The oper conditions admit a concrete local form.
Choose a coordinate
z
and a local frame adapted to the filtration.
Then the isomorphism conditions on the successive quotients allow one to normalize the connection matrix into the shape

\nabla=\partial_{z}+\begin{pmatrix}*&*&\cdots&*\\
1&*&\cdots&*\\
0&1&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&*\\
0&\cdots&0&1&*\end{pmatrix}.

Equivalently, if

is the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{b}
, then after gauge transformation by its unipotent radical

one may write

\nabla=\partial_{z}+p_{-1}+v(z),\qquad p_{-1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}E_{i+1,i},\qquad v(z)\in\mathfrak{b}.

(2.2)

The fixed subdiagonal term
p_{-1}
is precisely the local expression of the oper condition: it records that the induced maps

E_{i}/E_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\sim}(E_{i+1}/E_{i})\otimes K_{C}

are everywhere nonzero, hence isomorphisms.

Remark 2.3.

More generally, let

up to gauge transformation by

In this paper, however, we only need the cases

For simplicity, one specializes to

or

\mathfrak{n}

3
Lagrangian subvarieties and Lagrangian correspondences for Hitchin moduli spaces

In this section, we formulate and prove our main results concerning the moduli spaces of stable Higgs bundles. As introduced in
Section

1.1
, the main objects of our consideration are triples of the form
(L\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E,\phi)
, where
(E,\phi)
is a Higgs bundle and
L
is a line subbundle of
E
.
After introducing the sections
s_{i}(\phi)\in\Gamma(C,K_{C}^{n\choose 2}L^{-n}\det(E))
induced by such triples, we discuss how generic triples of such form are Hecke transforms of Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section. We then prove the Lagrangian correspondence results in
Theorem

1.2
and as a corollary construct Lagrangians
\mdmathbb{L}_{H}(D)
in the moduli spaces of Higgs bundles by fixing divisors
D
on
C
.

3.1
Higgs bundles with line subbundles

Given a triple
(i:L\hookrightarrow E,\phi)
with
(E,\phi)\in\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)
, for each
j\geq 0
consider the morphism

\phi^{j}\circ i:L\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{\phi}E\otimes K_{C}\xrightarrow{\phi\otimes\mathbbm{1}}\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\otimes K_{C}^{j}.

Define an
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-linear morphism

s_{i}(\phi)=i\wedge(\phi\circ i)\wedge\cdots\wedge(\phi^{n-1}\circ i):L^{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\det(E)K_{C}^{\binom{n}{2}},

(3.1a)

by

\sigma_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{n}\longmapsto\det\bigl(i(\sigma_{1}),\phi\circ i(\sigma_{2}),\phi^{2}\circ i(\sigma_{3}),\dots,\phi^{n-1}\circ i(\sigma_{n})\bigr),

(3.1b)

for local sections
\sigma_{k}\in\Gamma(U,L)
. If
s_{i}(\phi)\not\equiv 0
, we define

D_{i}(\phi):=\operatorname{div}\bigl(s_{i}(\phi)\bigr).

By the spectral correspondence,
(E,\phi)
determines spectral data
(\widetilde{C},\mathcal{L})
. In the rest of this section we always assume that
\widetilde{C}
is smooth, and write
\pi:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
. Consider the composition

f:\pi^{*}L\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}i}\pi^{*}E\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}.

(3.2)

If
f\not\equiv 0
, we define an effective divisor
\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)
on
\widetilde{C}
(possibly empty) by the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\pi^{*}L\xrightarrow{\,f\,}\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}|_{\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)}\rightarrow 0,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\mathcal{L}\cong\pi^{*}L\bigl(\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)\bigr).

(3.3)

Lemma 3.1.

Given a triple
(i:L\hookrightarrow E,\phi)
with
(E,\phi)\in\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)
, assume that the corresponding spectral curve
\widetilde{C}
is smooth. Then the composition
f:\pi^{*}L\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
is never identically zero. Equivalently,
s_{i}(\phi)\not\equiv 0
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for any
p\in C
outside the branch locus of
\pi
, the restriction
f|_{\pi^{-1}(p)}
is nonzero. Write
\pi^{-1}(p)=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
, and let
\lambda_{p_{i}}
be the eigenvalue of
\phi|_{p}
corresponding to
p_{i}
. Since
p
is not a branch point, we have a decomposition

E|_{p}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mdmathbb Ce_{i},

where
e_{i}
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
\lambda_{p_{i}}
. Suppose by contradiction that
f|_{\pi^{-1}(p)}=0
. Then for each
i
, the image of
L|_{p}
in
\mathcal{L}|_{p_{i}}
vanishes, equivalently

L|_{p}\subset\operatorname{Im}\bigl((\phi-\lambda_{p_{i}}\mathrm{id})|_{p}\bigr)=\bigoplus_{j\neq i}\mdmathbb Ce_{j}\subset E|_{p}.

Since this holds for every
i
, we get

L|_{p}\subset\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\bigoplus_{j\neq i}\mdmathbb Ce_{j}=0,

a contradiction because
L|_{p}
is one-dimensional.
∎

Proposition 3.2.

Given a triple
(i:L\hookrightarrow E,\phi)
with
(E,\phi)\in\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\ell)
, assume that the corresponding spectral curve
\widetilde{C}
is smooth. Then

\pi_{\ast}\bigl(\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)\bigr)=D_{i}(\phi).

Conversely, the embedding
i:L\hookrightarrow E
can be recovered from
\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)
.

Proof.

By
Section

3.1
, (
3.3
) always holds. Pushing forward along
\pi:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
, we obtain

0\rightarrow\pi_{*}\pi^{*}L\xrightarrow{\,\pi_{*}f\,}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\pi_{*}\bigl(\mathcal{L}|_{\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)}\bigr)\rightarrow 0.

By the projection formula and the spectral correspondence,

\pi_{*}\pi^{*}L\cong L\otimes\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}}\cong L\otimes\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n-1}K_{C}^{-j},\qquad\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}\cong E.

Under these identifications,
\pi_{*}f
is precisely
\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi^{j}\circ i
. Hence
\det(\pi_{*}f)=s_{i}(\phi)
, so its vanishing divisor is
D_{i}(\phi)
. On the other hand, the cokernel is the torsion sheaf
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}|_{\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)})
, whose associated divisor on
C
is
\pi_{\ast}(\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi))
. Therefore

\pi_{\ast}\bigl(\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)\bigr)=D_{i}(\phi).

Finally,
\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi)
determines the inclusion

\pi^{*}L\cong\mathcal{L}(-\widetilde{D}_{i}(\phi))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}.

Pushing forward gives
\pi_{*}f:\pi_{*}\pi^{*}L\rightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}=E
, and restricting
\pi_{*}f
to the first direct summand
L\subset\pi_{*}\pi^{*}L
recovers the original embedding
i:L\hookrightarrow E
.
∎

Given a triple
(i:L\hookrightarrow E,\phi)
, together with the divisor

, recall that for each

the morphism

can be viewed as

\phi^{j}\circ i:LK_{C}^{-j}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E.

is a half-dimensional subspace of

.
Hence, it suffices to show that

is isotropic.

For

such that

and is away from the ramification divisor

of spectral cover, one considers a two-parameter deformation

(\widetilde{D}(t_{1},t_{2}),\widetilde{C}(t_{1},t_{2}),\mathcal{L}(t_{1},t_{2}))\in\mdmathbb{L}_{H}(L,d),

with

sufficiently small so that
\widetilde{D}(t_{1},t_{2})
remains disjoint from

.

To compute (
3.22
), we need to describe the local coordinates
z_{i}^{j}(\lambda;t_{1},t_{2})
of the deformed spectral curve and the transition functions
f_{i}^{j}(\lambda;t_{1},t_{2})
of
\mathcal{L}(t_{1},t_{2})
. Set

D(t_{1},t_{2}):=\pi\left(\widetilde{D}(t_{1},t_{2})\right)=\sum_{i}p_{i}(t_{1},t_{2}),\quad\lambda(p_{i}(t_{1},t_{2}))=u_{i}(t_{1},t_{2})

with
u_{i}(0,0)=0
, where
(V_{i},\lambda)
is an open chart over
p_{i}(0,0)
. Let
z_{i}
denote the fiber coordinate on
\mathrm{Tot}(K_{C})
over
V_{i}
. The spectral curve
\widetilde{C}(t_{1},t_{2})
in a neighborhood of
\pi^{-1}(V_{i})
is defined by the polynomial:

E(\lambda,z_{i};t_{1},t_{2})=0,

with each branch over
V_{i}
corresponds to a root

z_{i}^{j}(\lambda;t_{1},t_{2}),

for
j=1,\dots,n
.
For simplicity, we assume that
\widetilde{D}(t_{1},t_{2})
is reduced and lies on the branch corresponding to

. The general case follows from the same computation.

By (
3.3
),

\mathcal{L}(t_{1},t_{2})\cong\pi_{t_{1},t_{2}}^{*}(L)(\widetilde{D}(t_{1},t_{2})),

where
\pi_{t_{1},t_{2}}:\widetilde{C}(t_{1},t_{2})\rightarrow C

For simplicity, we omit the contribution from
\pi_{t_{1},t_{2}}^{*}(L)
. Thus the transition functions of
\mathcal{L}(t_{1},t_{2})
from
\pi^{-1}(U)
to

\displaystyle f_{i}^{1}(\lambda;t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=E(\lambda,z_{i}^{1}(u_{i}(t_{1},t_{2});t_{1},t_{2});t_{1},t_{2}),

\displaystyle f_{i}^{j}(\lambda;t_{1},t_{2})

Differentiating the polynomial equation gives

\displaystyle\left.\partial_{t_{k}}z_{i}^{1}(\lambda)\right|_{(t_{1},t_{2})=(0,0)}=-\frac{\left(\partial_{t_{k}}E\right)(\lambda,z_{i}^{1}(\lambda))}{\left(\partial_{z}E\right)(\lambda,z_{i}^{1}(\lambda))},\text{ for }k=1,2.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial_{t_{k}}f_{i}^{1}}{f_{i}^{1}}\right|_{(t_{1},t_{2})=(0,0)}

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial_{t_{k}}f_{i}^{j}}{f_{i}^{j}}\right|_{(t_{1},t_{2})=(0,0)}

\displaystyle=0,\text{ for }j\neq 1,k=1,2.

Substituting into (
3.22
), we compute

\displaystyle{}_{H}\left((\widetilde{C},\mathcal{L})_{t_{1}},(\widetilde{C},\mathcal{L})_{t_{2}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\operatorname{Res}_{p_{i}}\left(\partial_{t_{1}}z_{i}^{1}\cdot\frac{\partial_{t_{2}}f_{i}^{1}}{f_{i}^{1}}-\partial_{t_{2}}z_{i}^{1}\cdot\frac{\partial_{t_{1}}f_{i}^{1}}{f_{i}^{1}}\right)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\operatorname{Res}_{\lambda=0}\left[\frac{\left(\partial_{z}E\right)(\lambda,z_{i}^{1}(0))\cdot\partial_{\lambda}z_{i}^{1}(0)}{E(\lambda,z_{i}^{1}(0))}\left(\partial_{t_{1}}z_{i}^{1}(0)\cdot\partial_{t_{2}}u_{i}(0,0)-\partial_{t_{2}}z_{i}^{1}(0)\cdot\partial_{t_{1}}u_{i}(0,0)\right)\right]d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}-\left(\partial_{t_{1}}z_{i}^{1}(0)\cdot\partial_{t_{2}}u_{i}(0,0)-\partial_{t_{2}}z_{i}^{1}(0)\cdot\partial_{t_{1}}u_{i}(0,0)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega^{[d]}\left(\widetilde{D}_{t_{1}},\widetilde{D}_{t_{2}}\right).

Here, the third equality uses the asymptotic behavior

-\frac{(\partial_{z}E)(\lambda,z_{i}^{1}(0))\cdot\partial_{\lambda}z_{i}^{1}(0)}{E(\lambda,z_{i}^{1}(0))}=\frac{1}{\lambda}+\mathrm{O}(1),\quad\text{as }\lambda\rightarrow 0,

which follows from the local expression

; the last equality follows from the fact that a point in

corresponds to coordinates

, and the canonical symplectic form on

over

is

∎

4
Lagrangian subvarieties and Lagrangian correspondences for de Rham moduli spaces

In this section, we formulate and prove our main results for the de Rham moduli space. As introduced in
Section

1.1
, the main objects of our consideration are triples of the form
(L\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E,\nabla)
, where
(E,\nabla)
are pairs of holomorphic bundles with holomorphic connections and
L
is a line subbundle of
E
.
We start by introducing the sections
s_{i}(\nabla)
of the line bundle
K_{C}^{n\choose 2}L^{-n}\det(E)
induced by such triples and showing how they give rise to a filtration of
E
. We also make the critical observation that for generic divisor
D
the forgetful assignment
(L\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E,\nabla)\mapstochar\rightarrow(E,\nabla)
with
D_{i}(\nabla)=D
fixed is generically finite.
We continue by identifying triples with a subset of opers with regular singularities. We use this identification to show that
\mdmathbb L_{dR}(D)
are non-empty and of the right dimension. We finish the section with the proof of the Lagrangian correspondence for
M_{dR}
.

4.1
Holomorphic connections with line subbundles

Given a triple
(i:L\hookrightarrow E,\nabla)
, where
(E,\nabla)\in\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n)
or
\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n,\mathcal{O}_{C})
,
we shall define (cf.
Section

4.1
) a map

s_{i}(\nabla):L^{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\det(E)K_{C}^{\binom{n}{2}},

(4.1a)

which serves as the counterpart to
s_{i}(\phi)
defined in the Higgs side (
3.1
).

We begin by considering a flat frame of
\nabla
, which yields a basis
\{\mathbf{e_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{e_{n}}\}
of the local system
E^{\nabla}:=\ker\nabla
. Let
U\subseteq C
be a local chart with holomorphic coordinate
z
, and let
s\in\Gamma(U,L)
be a nowhere vanishing local section. Via the inclusion
i:L\hookrightarrow E
, we have
i(s)\in\Gamma(U,E)
, which is expressed in terms of the local flat frame as

i(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(z)\mathbf{e_{j}},

for
a_{j}(z)\in\mathcal{O}_{U}
. For any
f_{i}(z)\in\mathcal{O}_{U}
, we define the morphism
s_{i}(\nabla):L^{n}\rightarrow\det(E)K_{C}^{\binom{n}{2}}
locally by

\displaystyle\begin{split}s_{i}(\nabla)\left(f_{1}(z)s\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}(z)s\right):=\ &\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{1}a_{1}&\left(f_{2}a_{1}\right)^{\prime}&\cdots&\left(f_{n}a_{1}\right)^{(n-1)}\\
f_{1}a_{2}&\left(f_{2}a_{2}\right)^{\prime}&\cdots&\left(f_{n}a_{2}\right)^{(n-1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
f_{1}a_{n}&\left(f_{2}a_{n}\right)^{\prime}&\cdots&\left(f_{n}a_{n}\right)^{(n-1)}\end{array}\right)\otimes(\mathrm{d}z)^{\binom{n}{2}}\\
=\ &\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(z)\right)\cdot\operatorname{Wr}(a_{1}(z),\dots,a_{n}(z))\otimes(\mathrm{d}z)^{\binom{n}{2}},\end{split}

(4.1b)

where
\operatorname{Wr}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
denotes the Wronskian determinant.

Lemma 4.1.

s_{i}(\nabla)
defines a global section of the line bundle
L^{-n}\det(E)K_{C}^{\binom{n}{2}}
.

Proof.

The claim follows immediately from (
4.1b
):
changes of the local flat frames
(\mathbf{e}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
,
the local coordinate
z

and the section
s\in\Gamma(U,E)

induce a multiplicative factor in
s_{i}(\nabla)
,
compatible with the scaling of sections of

L^{-n}\det(E)K_{C}^{\binom{n}{2}}
.
∎

Remark 4.2.

(a) For a
\lambda
-connection
(E,\nabla_{\lambda})
and a line subbundle
L\hookrightarrow E
, one can analogously define the section
s_{i}(\nabla_{\lambda})
of
L^{-n}\det(E)K_{C}^{\binom{n}{2}}
.

(b) For
1\leq k\leq n
,
even though
\nabla^{k}\circ i
does not define a bundle morphism
as
\phi^{k}\circ i
does,
their “wedge product”

i\wedge(\nabla\circ i)\wedge\cdots\wedge(\nabla^{n-1}\circ i)

is well-defined and gives the morphism
s_{i}(\nabla)
.

Lemma 4.3.

If
(E,\nabla)
is irreducible (i.e. admits no proper nonzero
\nabla
-invariant subbundle), then for any
i:L\hookrightarrow E
, the morphism
s_{i}(\nabla)\not\equiv 0
.

Proof.

We argue by contrapositive. Suppose there exists
i:L\hookrightarrow E
with
s_{i}(\nabla)\equiv 0
.
Consider the dual holomorphic connection
(E^{\vee},\nabla^{\vee})
.
It is clear that
sections of the rank-
(n-1)
subbundle
\ker(L)\subset E^{\vee}
which are also flat w.r.t.
\nabla^{\vee}

define a subbundle which is
\nabla^{\vee}
-invariant.
To this end, we will show that such
\nabla^{\vee}
-invariant subbundle exists. Note that locally
s_{i}(\nabla)\equiv 0
implies
\operatorname{Wr}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\equiv 0

(cf. (
4.1b
)).
This means that the functions

a_{1}(z),\dots,a_{n}(z)
are
\mdmathbb{C}
-linearly dependent. If we denote
\mathrm{rank}_{\mdmathbb C}\{a_{1}(z),\dots,a_{n}(z)\}=k
,
it follows that there are
\mdmathbb{C}
-linearly independent constant sections
c_{j}\in\Gamma(U,\ker(\nabla^{\vee}))
,

j=1,\dots,c_{n-k}
such that the pairing
\left(c_{j},i(s)\right)
vanishes identically on
U
.
These sections
c_{j}
precisely generate the
\nabla^{\vee}
-invariant bundle we seek.
So
(E^{\vee},\nabla^{\vee})
and hence
(E,\nabla)
are reducible.
∎

In the rest of this paper, we will assume that
(E,\nabla)
is irreducible,
i.e. we work in the smooth part of the de Rham moduli space.
For a triple

(i:L\hookrightarrow E,\nabla)
, denote by
D_{i}(\nabla)

the divisor defined by the vanishing locus of
s_{i}(\nabla)
.
Fixing
D
implies that

L^{-1}=K_{C}^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\otimes\mathcal{O}(D)^{1/n},

up to the choice of
n
–th root bundle.

Filtrations

Given a triple
(i\colon L\hookrightarrow E,\nabla)
, the connection
\nabla

endows
E
with a left
\mathcal{D}_{C}
–module structure, where

\mathcal{D}_{C}
denotes the sheaf of differential operators on
C
.
Composing the action map with the inclusion
i
yields a natural morphism

\mathcal{D}_{C}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}L\rightarrow E.

For each
i\geq 1
, define the subsheaf

\mathcal{F}_{i}\;:=\;\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\mathcal{D}_{C}^{\leq i-1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}L\rightarrow E\right),

(4.2)

where
\mathcal{D}_{C}^{\leq i-1}
denotes differential operators of order

\leq i-1
on
C
.
This yields an increasing filtration of subsheaves, which are compatible with
\nabla
,

0=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{n}\subset E.

(4.3)

We next relate the subsheaves
\mathcal{F}_{i}
to jet bundles.

Proposition 4.4.

There is a canonical isomorphism

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\vee}\;\cong\;J^{i-1}(L^{-1}).

Proof.

Locally on an open set
U
with coordinate
z
and a nowhere
vanishing section
s\in\Gamma(U,L)
, the
\mathcal{D}_{C}
–module structure
induced by
\nabla
gives

\mathcal{F}_{i}|_{U}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}\bigl\{s,\ \nabla_{\partial_{z}}s,\ \ldots,\ (\nabla_{\partial_{z}})^{\,i-1}s\bigr\}.

In particular,
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is locally free of rank
i
. This implies that the morphism
\mathcal{\mathcal{}}D_{C}^{\leq i-1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}L\rightarrow E
is sheaf theoretically injective.

On the other hand, for any
\mathcal{O}_{C}
–modules
F_{1},F_{2}
, differential
operators of order
\leq i-1
satisfy the identification

\operatorname{Diff}_{C}^{\leq i-1}(F_{1},F_{2})\;\cong\;\operatorname{Hom}\!\bigl(J^{i-1}(F_{1}),F_{2}\bigr).

Applying this with
F_{1}=L^{-1}
and
F_{2}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
, taking the global section, we obtain canonical isomorphisms

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{D}_{C}^{\leq i-1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}L=\operatorname{Diff}_{C}^{\leq i-1}(L^{-1},\mathcal{O}_{C})\;\cong\;\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(J^{i-1}(L^{-1}),\mathcal{O}_{C}\bigr)=\bigl(J^{i-1}(L^{-1})\bigr)^{\vee}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

By construction, the filtration (
4.3
)
satisfies the Griffiths transversality condition

\nabla\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset\mathcal{F}_{i+1}\otimes K_{C}.

Furthermore, one has the induced
\mathcal{O}_{C}
–isomorphism,

\overline{\nabla}:\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\overset{\cong}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathcal{F}_{i+1}/\mathcal{F}_{i}\otimes K_{C}.

(4.4)

In the rest of this paper, for simplicity, we will focus on the case where

D_{i}(\nabla)
is reduced.
In this case, the following lemma states that
except for
\mathcal{F}_{n}
which is a lower Hecke transform of
E
,
the sheaves
\mathcal{F}_{i}
with
i\leq n-1
are subbundles of
E
.

Proposition 4.5.

Given a triple
(i:L\hookrightarrow E,\nabla)
,
with
D=D_{i}(\nabla)
a reduced divisor, each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
with
i\leq n-1
is a rank-
i
subbundle
E_{i}\subset E
,
and (
4.3
)
induces a filtration of subbundles for
E
,

\displaystyle 0\subset i(L)=E_{1}\subset E_{2}\subset\dots\subset E_{n-1}\subset E.

(4.5)

The connection
\nabla
preserves the filtration (
4.5
) and thus is gauge equivalent to the form

\nabla=\mathrm{d}+\begin{pmatrix}&0&\ast&\dots&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
&1&0&\dots&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
&0&1&\dots&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
&\vdots&\vdots&\dots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
&0&0&\dots&0&\ast&\ast\\
&0&0&\dots&1&\ast&\ast\\
&0&0&\dots&0&s_{i}(\nabla)&\ast\end{pmatrix}.

(4.6)

Furthermore,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is a lower Hecke transform of
E

at the subspaces
E_{n-1}\mid_{p}<E\mid_{p}
for each
p<D
,
and fits into the s.e.s.

\displaystyle 0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow LK_{C}^{-(n-1)}(D)\mid_{D}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

(4.7)

where
LK_{C}^{-(n-1)}(D)\mid_{D}
is the skyscraper sheaf.

Proof.

For each
p<D
, choose a local coordinate
z
, such that
p
corresponds to
z=0
.
For simplicity, consider the inclusion
\mathcal{F}_{i}\hookrightarrow E
as sheaves,
and let
I(p)
be the smallest index
i
such that

\dim\mathcal{F}_{i}\mid_{p}<i
.
We claim that
I(p)=n
, for all
p<D
.

By (
4.1b
), definition of
D_{i}(\nabla)

and the fact that
D
is reduced, the Wronskian

\operatorname{Wr}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

has a simple zero at
z=0
.
By way of contradiction, assume
I(p)=i<n
. By (
4.2
), there exist constants
c_{k}
such that

\bm{a}^{(i-1)}(0)=\sum_{k=0}^{i-2}c_{k}\bm{a}^{(k)}(0).

Replacing the
i
-th column of the Wronskian matrix by

\bm{a}^{(i-1)}(z)\;\longmapsto\;\bm{a}^{(i-1)}(z)-\sum_{k=0}^{i-2}c_{k}\,\bm{a}^{(k)}(z)=z\,\mathbf{v}(z),

for some column vector
\mathbf{v}(z)
, we obtain

\operatorname{Wr}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})(z)=z^{\,n-i}\,\det\!\left(\bm{a}(z),\dots,\bm{a}^{(i-2)}(z),\bm{v}(z),\bm{v}^{\prime}(z),\dots,\bm{v}^{(n-i-1)}(z)\right).

This shows that

\operatorname{Wr}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

vanishes to order at least

at

,
contradicting the fact that the Wronskian has a simple zero there.
Therefore,

, which implies that each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a subbundle
E_{i}\subset E
, for
i<n
.

Since at

, the sheaf inclusion

\mathcal{F}_{n}\hookrightarrow E

evaluates to

,
we have
\mathcal{F}_{n}
as the result of consecutive
lower Hecke transforms of
E
at
E_{n-1}\mid_{p}<E\mid_{p}

for each

.
It follows from (

) that

\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\cong LK_{C}^{-(i-1)},\qquad\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

The following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces LK_{C}^{-(n-1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces LK_{C}^{-(n-1)}(D)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

is commutative.
This gives the s.e.s. (

).
∎

4.2
Opers with apparent singularities

In this section, we introduce the subvarieties
\mdmathbb L_{dR}(L,D)
(resp.
\mdmathbb L_{dR}(D)
) of the de Rham moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n)
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n,\mathcal{O}_{C})
). Later, we will show (
Theorem

4.21
) that these are the de Rham counterparts of the Lagrangian subvarieties
\mdmathbb L_{H}(L,D)
(resp.
\mdmathbb L_{H}(D)
) on the Higgs side introduced in
Section

3.3
.

In the
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
case, we identify the moduli of triple
\widetilde{\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}}(L,D)
with the moduli of opers with prescribed coweight

\mathrm{Op}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{n},D}(C)_{\bm{\omega}_{n-1}^{\vee}},

and show that such triples can be further identified with a differential operator with vanishing principle symbol at
D
. We do not treat the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
case since it is completely analogous.

Definition 4.6.

•

Given a triple
(L\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E,\nabla)
such that
s_{i}(\nabla)
does not identically vanish,
we say that the divisor
D_{i}(\nabla)
is the divisor of
apparent singularities
associated to the triple.

•

Let
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.
Given a line bundle
L
and an effective divisor
D
of degree

\deg(D)=n(n-1)(g-1)-n\deg(L)
,
let
\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(L,D)
be the subvariety of
\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n)
given by

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(L,D)=\left\{(E,\nabla)\in\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n)\mid(E,\nabla)\text{ irreducible},\exists i:L\hookrightarrow E,\text{ }D_{i}(\nabla)=D\right\}.

(4.8)

•

Let
G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}
.
Given an effective divisor
D
and a line bundle
L
such that

L^{n}\simeq K_{C}^{n\choose 2}(-D)
,
let
\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(L,D)
be the subvariety
of
\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n,\mathcal{O}_{C})
given by (

).
Let
\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(D)
be the union of all such subvarieties, i.e.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(D)&=\bigcup_{L^{n}\simeq K_{C}^{n\choose 2}(-D)}\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(L,D)\\
&=\left\{(E,\nabla)\in\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n,\mathcal{O}_{C})\mid(E,\nabla)\text{ irreducible},\exists i:L\hookrightarrow E,D_{i}(\nabla)=D\right\}.\end{split}

Proposition 4.7.

Fix a line bundle
L
satisfying
L^{\otimes n}\simeq K_{C}^{\binom{n}{2}}(-D)
. Then there is a natural isomorphism

\widetilde{\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}}(L,D):=\bigl\{([i]\colon L\hookrightarrow E,\nabla)\mid D_{i}(\nabla)=D\bigr\}\;\cong\;\mathrm{Op}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{n},D}(C)_{\bm{\omega}_{n-1}^{\vee}}.

Proof.

By

, each triple

Conversely, let

Identifying

1\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mdmathbb G_{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{PGL}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 1.

The obstruction to lifting the
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-bundle

(together with its

The generic oper conditions imply canonical isomorphisms

Proposition 4.8.

Fix a line bundle
L
such that

L^{\otimes n}\simeq K_{C}^{\otimes\binom{n}{2}}(-D).

Consider the natural forgetful morphism

\pi(L,D)\colon\widetilde{\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}}(L,D)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(L,D),\qquad([i]\colon L\hookrightarrow E,\nabla)\longmapsto(E,\nabla),

then for generic
D\in\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(C)
, the morphism
\pi(L,D)
is generically finite.

Proof.

Recall the construction of Hodge moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}=\{(\lambda,E,\nabla_{\lambda})\}/\!\sim,

where
\nabla_{\lambda}
is a
\lambda
-connection. Consider the moduli space of
\lambda
-connections together with line subbundles,

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Hod}}=\bigl\{(\lambda,[i]\colon L\hookrightarrow E,E,\nabla_{\lambda})\bigr\},

where the embedding
[i]\colon L\hookrightarrow E
is taken up to scaling.

Now define

X=\Bigl\{(\lambda,[i],E,\nabla_{\lambda},D)\;\Big|\;\operatorname{div}\bigl(s_{i}(\nabla_{\lambda})\bigr)=D\Bigr\}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Hod}}\times\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(C).

By construction,
X
is an algebraic family. Let

F\colon X\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}\times\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(C)

be the natural forgetful map
and let

Y:=F(X)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}\times\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(C)

be its image.

For each
(\lambda,D)\in\mdmathbb{C}\times\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(C)
, one has

F_{(\lambda,D)}\colon X_{(\lambda,D)}=\widetilde{\mdmathbb{L}_{\lambda}}(L,D)=\bigl\{([i]\colon L\hookrightarrow E,\nabla_{\lambda})\mid D_{i}(\nabla_{\lambda})=D\bigr\}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow Y_{(\lambda,D)}=\mdmathbb{L}_{\lambda}(D).

In particular, by
Section

4.2

X_{(1,D)}=\operatorname{Op}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{n},D}(C)_{\bm{\omega}_{n-1}^{\vee}},\qquad Y_{(1,D)}=\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(L,D),\qquad F_{(1,D)}=\pi(L,D).

On the Higgs side, namely at
\lambda=0
, the forgetful morphism

F_{(0,D)}\colon\widetilde{\mdmathbb{L}_{0}}(L,D)\rightarrow\mdmathbb{L}_{0}(L,D)

is finite and generically one-to-one for generic
D
.

Let

Z:=\bigl\{y\in Y\mid\dim F^{-1}(y)\geq 1\bigr\}.

Then the closure of

is a closed subvariety of positive codimension in

. Therefore, for generic

and
\lambda
sufficiently small, the morphism

is generically finite.

Finally, for

\lambda

for one nonzero

. It follows that for generic

\pi(L,D)

is generically finite, which proves the statement.
∎

Associated differential operators

Given a triple
(i:L\hookrightarrow E,\nabla)

with
D_{i}(\nabla)=D
and
(E,\nabla)
irreducible, we
define an associated differential operator as follows.

Consider the dual connection
(E^{\vee},\nabla^{\vee})
with a local system
{({E}^{\nabla})}^{\vee}:=\ker(\nabla^{\vee})

and a nonzero injective morphism
i^{\vee}:{({E}^{\nabla})}^{\vee}\rightarrow L^{-1}
.
The injectivity follows from the irreducibility.
Following the same construction as in
Section

2.5
(which was also considered in
[
55
,
43
,
70
,
68
]
),
one can define a global differential operator of degree
n
,

\mathbf{D}:L^{-1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L^{-(n+1)}\otimes K_{C}^{\binom{n+1}{2}}\cong L^{-1}\otimes K_{C}^{n}(D),

\mathbf{D}y=\det\begin{pmatrix}i^{\vee}(\mathbf{v}_{1})&\cdots&i^{\vee}(\mathbf{v}_{n})&y\\

\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\

Here

is a local frame for

i_{1}
and
i_{2}
.

Proposition 4.9.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between
(L\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E,\nabla)
and the differential operator

\mathbf{D}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{C}^{n}\bigl(L^{-1},\,L^{-1}K_{C}^{n}(D)\bigr)^{\circ}.

Here
\operatorname{Diff}^{n}_{C}\bigl(L^{-1},\,L^{-1}K_{C}^{n}(D)\bigr)^{\circ}
denotes the space of order-
n

L^{-1}\rightarrow L^{-1}K_{C}^{n}(D)

whose principal symbol
\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{D})
has divisor equal to
D
.

Proof.

By (
4.11b
), the principal symbol of
\mathbf{D}
is locally given by

\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{D})=\det\begin{pmatrix}i^{\vee}(\mathbf{v}_{1})&\cdots&i^{\vee}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\\
i^{\vee}(\mathbf{v}_{1})^{\prime}&\cdots&i^{\vee}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{\prime}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
i^{\vee}(\mathbf{v}_{1})^{(n-1)}&\cdots&i^{\vee}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{(n-1)}\end{pmatrix}\otimes(\mathrm{d}z)^{\binom{n}{2}}.

(4.12)

Choosing local frames
(\mathbf{e}_{i})
of

and using (

) for

gives

To show the correspondence, it remains to recover the triple from

be the sheaf of local solutions; it is a local system with a natural evaluation map

∎

[

over

∎

Remark 4.17.

Lemma 4.19.

Adopt the notation above. Fix a holomorphic
\mathrm{PSL}_{n}
–oper

\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{reg}}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{C}^{n}\bigl(K_{C}^{-\frac{n-1}{2}},\,K_{C}^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\bigr)

admits a solution, and the number of solutions is finite.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, we assume that (
4.26
) has no solution.
Fix
r>0
, consider

\bm{v}=r\bm{\tilde{v}}=(r\tilde{v}_{1},...,r\tilde{v}_{d}),

with
\|\tilde{v}\|=1

Consider a one-parameter family of algebraic equations

P_{t}(\bm{v}):=\bm{v}^{n}+t\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\bm{v}^{n-i-1}f_{i}(\bm{v};b).

For any
r>0
, define the maps

F_{1}(\bm{\tilde{v}};r):=\frac{P_{1}(r\bm{\tilde{v}})}{|P_{1}(r\bm{\tilde{v}})|}:S^{2d-1}\rightarrow S^{2d-1},\quad|\tilde{v}|=1.

(4.34)

Note that
F_{1}(\cdot;r)
are well defined continuous maps depending continuously on the parameter

. Taking
r\rightarrow 0
, we see that
F_{1}(\cdot;r)
are homotopic to the constant map
F_{1}(\cdot;0)
sending
S^{2d-1}
to the point
\frac{P(0)}{|P(0)|}
.

On the other hand, since
f_{i}(\bm{v})
are polynomial in
\bm{v}
of degree

have no root on the sphere of radius

F_{t}(\bm{\tilde{v}};r):=\frac{P_{t}(r\bm{\tilde{v}})}{|P_{t}(r\bm{\tilde{v}})|}:S^{2d-1}\rightarrow S^{2d-1}

are well defined continuous maps depending continuously on

in (

5
Examples and applications

The aim of this last section is to provide explicit examples and applications of the theory developed and relate it to previous work. We hope that this section nicely complements the abstract and general nature of the rest of the paper. In the first subsection, by choosing appropriate degrees for the divisors, we construct Darboux coordinates on the Hitchin and de Rham moduli spaces. In the second subsection we relate our holomorphic Lagrangian subvarieties to the
\mdmathbb C^{\star}
-action on the Hodge moduli space and the conformal limit. In the final subsection, we give a detailed description of our Lagrangian subvarieties and Lagrangian correspondences in the rank
2
cases.

5.1
Darboux coordinates on moduli spaces

Recall that the dimensions of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\Lambda)
and
\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n,\mathcal{O}_{C})
are equal to
2(n^{2}-1)(g-1)
.

Proposition 5.1.

(a) Let
\Lambda
be a line bundle on
C
and
d\equiv\ell=\deg(\Lambda)\pmod{n}
.
Then
\mdmathbb{L}_{H}(d)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\Lambda)
is dominant if and only if
d\geq(n^{2}-1)(g-1)
.

(b) Let
d\equiv 0\pmod{n}
.
Then
\mdmathbb{L}_{dR}(d)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{dR}(n,\mathcal{O}_{C})
is dominant if and only if
d\geq(n^{2}-1)(g-1)
.

Proof idea.

This follows from
[
70
]
. One can alternatively prove the statement via a direct Riemann-Roch calculation to show that a generic bundle
E\in\mathrm{Bun}_{n,\Lambda}
admits a line subbundle
L
such that
\deg(C,K_{C}^{n\choose 2}L^{-n}\Lambda)\geq(n^{2}-1)(g-1)
.
∎

Corollary 5.2.

(a) For
\ell+g-1\equiv 0\pmod{n}
,
\deg(\Lambda)=\ell
, the Lagrangian correspondence
\mdmathbb{L}_{H}((n^{2}-1)(g-1))
provides Darboux coordinates on an open dense subset of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\Lambda)
.

(b) For
g\equiv 1\pmod{n}
, apparent singularities and their residue parameters provide Darboux coordinates on an open dense subset of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(n,\mathcal{O}_{C})
.

5.2
Conformal limit and Białynicki-Birula stratification

When the degree of the divisor is small enough the Lagrangians
\mdmathbb L_{H}(L,D)
and
\mdmathbb L_{dR}(L,D)
interact nicely with the conformal limit construction for Higgs bundles
[
14
]
. Below, we briefly explain this interaction.

Given a stable Higgs bundle
[(\bar{\partial}_{E},\phi)]
denote its
\mdmathbb C^{\star}
-limit by
[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})]:=\lim\limits_{\xi\rightarrow 0}[(\bar{\partial}_{E},\xi\phi)]
. Given such a fixed point we define

W(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0}):=\{[(\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})]\in\mathcal{M}_{Hod}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \lim\limits_{\xi\rightarrow 0}[(\xi\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E},\xi\nabla_{\lambda})]=[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})]\}.

(5.1)

Then it holds that
W(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})\cap\pi^{-1}(0)=:W^{0}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})
and
W(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})\cap\pi^{-1}(1)=:W^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})
are holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds of the Dolbeaut and De Rham moduli space respectively. Assume that the fixed point
[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})]
is of type
(1,...,1)
so that

\begin{split}E\cong&L_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{n}\\
\phi_{0}=&\begin{pmatrix}0&\dots&\dots&0\\
\Phi_{1}&0&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\dots&\Phi_{n-1}&0\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(5.2)

with
\Phi_{i}\in H^{0}(C;L_{i}^{-1}L_{i+1}K)
. Denote
d_{i}=\deg L_{i}
and
D_{i}
the vanishing set of
\Phi_{i}
. The divisor
D
we have studied throughout the paper is given by the weighted sum
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(n-i)D_{i}
. We can also associate to the fixed point the divisor
\mathfrak{D}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}D_{i}
which is used in
[
38
]
. Under the above assumption it can be inferred from
[
14
]
that

\mdmathbb L_{H}(L_{1},D)=W^{0}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})\penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \mdmathbb L_{dR}(L_{1},D)=W^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0}).

(5.3)

In the next proposition, given a
\mdmathbb C^{\star}
-fixed point
[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})]
we give bounds on the possible degree of the divisors
D
and
\mathfrak{D}
.

Proposition 5.3.

(a) Consider a stable Hodge bundle
[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
, and let
D
and
\mathfrak{D}
be divisors on
C
defined by
[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
as above. If
\deg E\equiv\ell\pmod{n}
then necessarily

\begin{split}\deg(D)&\leq n(n-1)(g-1)-(n-\ell),\\
\deg(\mathfrak{D})&\leq 2(n-1)(g-1)-1-\delta_{0,\ell}.\end{split}

There exist divisors which realize equality in both inequalities.

(b) Let
D
be a reduced effective divisor of degree
d\equiv\ell\pmod{n}
.
Then there exists a stable Hodge bundle
[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
of the form (
5.2
) with

\mdmathbb L_{H}(L_{1},D)=W^{0}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})

if and only if

\deg(D)\leq n(g-1)-(n-\ell).

(c) Let
D
be a reduced effective divisor of degree
d\equiv 0\pmod{n}
.
Then there exists a stable Hodge bundle
[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
of the form (
5.2
) with

\mdmathbb L_{dR}(L_{1},D)=W^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\phi_{0})

if and only if

\deg(D)\leq n(g-2).

Proof.

The total degree of the divisor
D
is given by

\begin{split}\deg(D)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(n-i)\deg(D_{i})=&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(n-i)(\deg K+\deg L_{i+1}-\deg L_{i})\\
=&n(n-1)(g-1)+d_{1}+...+d_{n}-nd_{1},\end{split}

(5.4)

and the total degree of
\mathfrak{D}
is given by

\begin{split}\deg(\mathfrak{D})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\deg(D_{i})=&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(\deg K+\deg L_{i+1}-\deg L_{i})\\
=&2(n-1)(g-1)+d_{n}-d_{1}.\end{split}

(5.5)

The
\phi
-invariant subbundles of
E
are precisely
E_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=k}^{n}L_{i}
and by stability we get the following inequalities:

\frac{d_{k}+...+d_{n}}{n-k+1}<\frac{d_{1}+...+d_{n}}{n}.

(5.6)

In particular, the inequality (
5.6
) for
k=2
gives the restriction
d_{1}+...+d_{n}<nd_{1}
. Since all the
d_{i}
are integers and
d_{1}+...+d_{n}\equiv\ell\pmod{n}
, it holds that
d_{1}+...+d_{n}-nd_{1}\leq-(n-\ell)
and therefore
\deg(D)\leq n(n-1)(g-1)-(n-\ell)
. Equality can be obtained by choosing for example
d_{1}=d_{2}=...=d_{\ell}=d
and
d_{\ell+1}=...=d_{n}=d-1
.

Regarding the divisor
\mathfrak{D}
, the inequality (
5.6
) for
k=n
gives
nd_{n}<d_{1}+...+d_{n}
. Combining with
d_{1}+...+d_{n}<nd_{1}
we get that
d_{n}-d_{1}\leq-1
. If
d_{1}+...+d_{n}\neq 0\mod n
then equality can be obtained by choosing
d_{1}=d_{2}=...=d_{k}=d
and
d_{k+1}=...=d_{n}=d-1
. If
d_{1}+...+d_{n}=0\mod n
then
d_{n}-d_{1}\leq-2
with equality obtained by choosing
d_{1}=d+1
,
d_{2}=...=d_{n-1}=d
and
d_{n}=d_{1}
.

Finally, suppose that
D
is reduced. This forces all
\Phi_{i}
to be constant sections of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
for
i\neq n-1
so that

L_{i+1}=L_{i}K^{-1}\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ i\leq n-2\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ L_{n}=L_{n-1}K^{-1}(D).

This implies that
d_{i+1}=d_{i}-(2g-2)
for
i\leq n-2
and
d_{n}=d_{n-1}-(2g-2)+d
where
d=\deg(D)
. The only constraint on
D
comes from the stability constraints (
5.6
). The most restrictive constraint is the one for
k=n
which gives

d_{n}<d_{n}+(n-1)(g-1)-\frac{(n-1)d}{n}

which implies that
d\leq n(g-1)-1
. Finally, since
d_{1}+...+d_{n}\equiv\ell\pmod{n}
and
d_{1}+...+d_{n}=nd_{n}+n(n-1)(g-1)-(n-1)d=d\mod n
we must have
d\leq n(g-1)-(n-\ell)
.
This proves both parts (b) and (c) of the proposition.
∎

Remark 5.4.

When the divisor
\mathfrak{D}
is reduced, one can recover
D
from
\mathfrak{D}
and vice versa. Neither inequality in

is a sufficient condition in general.

A surprising fact is that there exists a natural map from the Dolbeaut to the De Rham space which identifies these Lagrangians biholomorphically. In order to define it, let

and let
h_{R}
denote the harmonic metric associated to
[(\bar{\partial}_{E},R\Phi)]
