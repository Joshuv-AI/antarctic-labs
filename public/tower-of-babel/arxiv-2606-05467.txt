The Cascade Log: Reference-Stable Windowing over Tiered Append Sequences
Faruk Alpay, Levent Sarıoğlu

Abstract
A long-running, append-mostly sequence of records—edits in an interactive session, events in an event-sourced store, revisions in a version-controlled working set—cannot be kept fully materialised, so it is layered into tiers: a hot working set over progressively colder strata into which aged records are folded and summarised. Records nonetheless carry stable handles that are referenced across tier boundaries, and resolving a handle must stay correct as its target migrates downward. We show that the obvious tiered design silently violates this requirement at exactly the tier boundaries: under a realistic access mixture a quarter of cross-window references resolve to a stale, wrong, or absent record, and after sustained folding more than ninety-eight percent of the history becomes unaddressable. The Cascade Log removes these failures. A single persistent coalescing interval map over handles is the sole authority on each handle’s live version; folding coalesces a contiguous run into one interval node in a single split–merge splice. Our central result is a tight characterisation of this structure by its fragmentation A A —the number of index pieces, namely live handles plus maximal same-digest runs. The map uses Θ ⁡ ( A ) \Theta(A) space, resolves a point in O ⁡ ( log ⁡ A ) O(\log A) , reports a k k -handle range in O ⁡ ( log ⁡ A + k ) O(\log A+k) , and updates in O ⁡ ( ( a / B + s ) ​ log ⁡ A ) O((a/B+s)\log A) over a a appends and s s supersedes; matching Ω ⁡ ( A ) \Omega(A) space and Ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ A + k ) \Omega(\log A+k) query lower bounds hold for any reference-stable, ordered, bounded-working-set structure, and an adversary forces A = Θ ⁡ ( s ) A=\Theta(s) . The index is thus instance-optimal— o ⁡ ( n ) o(n) when appends dominate, Θ ⁡ ( s ) \Theta(s) under adversarial edits, and then no structure does better. Resolution is total, snapshot-consistent, and anomaly-free; the working set is independent of history length; and the design transfers to external memory at O ⁡ ( log β ⁡ A ) O(\log_{\beta}A) I/Os. A reference implementation and a reproducible study to 10 6 10^{6} records, including adversarial and distribution-free workloads and a copy-on-write ordered-map baseline, trace A A directly and confirm the bounds.

Introduction

Many systems are organised around a single growing sequence of immutable
records that is appended to far more often than it is read in full: the edit
history of an interactive session, the event log of an event-sourced
application, the revision stream of a version-controlled working set, the chunk
sequence of an incremental indexer, the tuple stream of a continuous analytic
query. The sequence quickly outgrows fast memory, so it is
tiered
. A
bounded
hot
stratum holds the recent records fully materialised; aged
records are
folded
in contiguous blocks into colder strata, where a block
is replaced by a compact
digest
that summarises it. Tiering is what keeps
the materialised working set bounded as the history grows without limit, and it
is the same idea that underlies log-structured storage
[
19
]
.

The records are not independent. Each carries a stable
handle
, and
records reference earlier handles: an edit refers to the region it revises, an
event to the entity it mutates, a derived chunk to its source. A consumer
resolves a handle to obtain the record’s current version, and assembles a
bounded
window
of related records around a query. The difficulty is that
references cross tier boundaries. A handle minted while its target was hot is
followed later, after the target has been folded into a cold digest; a handle is
edited after the block holding it was already summarised; a window is assembled
while a fold is in flight. At each of these boundaries the obvious tiered design
fails in a way that is easy to miss because every individual tier is internally
consistent—the defect lives only in the transition between tiers.

We make the failure precise as three
cross-tier reference anomalies
.
A
dangling
resolution returns nothing for a handle that was appended and
never deleted, because folding reclaimed its addressing entry. A
stale

resolution returns a superseded version, because no tier holds the authoritative
one. A
snapshot-skew
window mixes records read before and after a fold and
is therefore not a consistent view of any single instant.
Section

6

quantifies them: with a hot stratum of a few hundred records over a history of
tens of thousands, a tiered log leaves under two percent of the history
addressable and mis-resolves roughly a quarter of references drawn from a
realistic access mixture.

This paper presents the Cascade Log, a tiered append structure for which
resolution is provably free of all three anomalies while the working set stays
bounded. Three ingredients combine:

•

a
versioned interval index
(
Section

3.1
)—one persistent search
tree, shared across snapshots by path copying
[
13
,
23
]
—which spans every
stratum and is the single authority on where each handle’s current version
lives, so resolution never depends on the order in which tiers are probed;

•

a
coalescing interval map
(
Section

3.2
)—folding a contiguous
run collapses it to a single interval node in one
O(\log n)
splice, so
compaction is amortised
O(1)
per appended record and the index is
sublinear, while a folded handle still resolves through its digest; and

•

snapshot tokens
(
Section

3.3
)—immutable roots that serve
reads as of any past instant, making windows consistent by construction.

Windows are chosen (
Section

3.5
) by diffusing relevance from the query over
the reference graph with a personalised random walk
[
9
,
14
]

and then solving a standard budgeted maximum-coverage instance over the
candidates
[
18
,
16
]
. The selection
layer applies known algorithms; our concern is the structure beneath it that
keeps the candidates resolvable, snapshot-consistent, and cheap to gather.

Model

Records, handles, versions.

The structure stores a sequence of records appended over time. Appending a
record mints a fresh handle from a monotonically increasing counter; the
k
-th
appended record has handle
k
. A record may be
superseded
by a later
version of the same handle, modelling an in-place edit, so a handle denotes a
logical record whose content evolves through versions
0,1,2,\dots
Each handle

h
carries a set of out-references
R(h)
to earlier handles; the directed graph

G=(V,E)
with
E=\{(h,h^{\prime})\,:\,h^{\prime}\in R(h)\}
is the
reference graph
.

Strata and folding.

Records occupy a sequence of strata
T_{0},T_{1},\dots
with the hot stratum
T_{0}

holding the recent records fully materialised. When the number of materialised
records exceeds a capacity
C
, the oldest are demoted; once a block of
B

demoted records accumulates it is
folded
into a
digest
, a
fixed-footprint object that summarises the block. A cost
c(h)>0
(think bytes or
tokens) is charged for each materialised version; a digest costs
O(1)

irrespective of
B
.

Operations.

A structure supports
\textsc{Append}(p,c,R)\to h
,
\textsc{Supersede}(h,p,c)\to v
,

\textsc{Resolve}(h,\tau)
returning the version of
h

\tau
(or the
current instant), and
\textsc{Window}(q,\beta)
returning a budgeted set of records
relevant to a query handle
q
within cost budget
\beta
.

Persistence model.

Updates (
Append
,
Supersede
, fold) apply only to the newest version and
produce a linear sequence of versions
D_{0},D_{1},\dots
; we index them by an
integer
epoch
, the count of updates applied. The structure is therefore

partially persistent
in the sense of
13
:
any past epoch is read-only and queryable, but the history does not branch—there
is no operation that forks an alternate timeline. A
snapshot token
names
an epoch;
\textsc{Resolve}(h,\tau)
with a token reads version
D_{\tau}
. We make the
retention policy explicit because it governs both correctness and space:

•

Ephemeral mode
retains only the current epoch. Past epochs are not
queryable, and a version superseded by a later update becomes unreachable and
is reclaimed; the space bound (
Theorem

4.7
) is stated for this mode.

•

Retained mode
keeps a caller-chosen set of tokens; the epochs they
name remain queryable, and the path-copied state they reach is kept alive and

counted
in the space bound. Reclamation is by reachability from the
current root and the retained tokens (reference counting suffices, as the
version graph is acyclic).

A digest is reachable, hence retained, exactly while some live or retained epoch
maps a handle to it; once every handle of a digest has been superseded and no
retained token predates those edits, the digest is reclaimed.
“Resolve against any past instant” thus means any
retained
epoch, not an
arbitrary one—no claim is made about epochs whose tokens were not kept.

Correct resolution.

Let
\mathrm{ver}^{\star}_{\tau}(h)
and
\mathrm{pay}^{\star}_{\tau}(h)
be the true
version and payload of
h
at instant
\tau
. A summarised resolution returns

\big(h,\ \mathrm{ver}^{\star}_{\tau}(h),\ \sigma(\mathrm{pay}^{\star}_{\tau}(h))\big)
,
where
\sigma
is the digest’s summary map; it is

always and

exactly when
\sigma
is the identity. A resolution

\rho=\textsc{Resolve}(h,\tau)
is

when
h
exists at
\tau
and
\rho

reports the handle
h

\mathrm{ver}^{\star}_{\tau}(h)

anomaly classification below concern identity and version only, a guarantee strictly
weaker than payload-faithful resolution, which holds iff
\sigma=\mathrm{id}
.
We classify an incorrect resolution as one of the following
cross-tier
anomalies
.

Definition 2.1

(Cross-tier anomalies)
.

A resolution of an existing handle
h
at instant
\tau
is

if it returns nothing;

stale
if it returns a version
v<\mathrm{ver}^{\star}_{\tau}(h)

defect appears in the transition between layers”: each is triggered only by a

Theorem 4.1

(Totality)

For every handle

We show the invariant that the live root contains an entry for every appended

Theorem 4.2

(Snapshot consistency)
.

Fix a retained token naming epoch
\tau

returns the value prescribed by the canonical sequential execution truncated
after its first
\tau

resolved against one token is a consistent snapshot of epoch
\tau

Proof.

A token is an immutable root produced by path copying: each update derives a new
root from the previous one without mutating any existing
node
[
13
]
. Hence the root at epoch
\tau
is,
node for node, the tree obtained by applying exactly the first
\tau

the linear history (
Section

2
); reading any set of handles against it
returns the state after update
\tau
and no later update. A window built from one
token therefore cannot mix pre- and post-fold states.
∎

Remark 4.3

(Concurrent readers)
.

The structure as specified is sequential, but its immutability gives the
single-writer, many-reader model for free, and that model is exactly the read path
of multiversion concurrency control
[
6
]
: a writer serialises
updates and publishes each new root with one atomic pointer store, while readers
load the pointer once and traverse the immutable tree it names. Readers never block,
never coordinate, and each observes a consistent snapshot; every operation
linearises at its publish (writer) or load (reader), so the object is linearizable
in the sense of
15
. This is the regime the motivating
applications read in—snapshot-isolation queries and time-travel reads run against
exactly such immutable versions—and the working set keeps a writer’s update local.

5
Implementation

The reference implementation is a self-contained Python package. The
vii
is a
path-copying treap (standard library only); the Cascade Log adds the strata,
folding, digests, and windows. Four comparison structures share the operation
interface.
Flat
keeps every version materialised in one table.

NaiveTier
keeps a bounded hot table over a cold tier that is folded by
discarding all per-handle state;
ForwardTier
adds a never-reclaimed
per-handle version counter, which restores version authority but not
addressability. The progression
NaiveTier
\to

\to
Cascade Log
isolates the two mechanisms—version authority and post-fold addressability.

HashSpine
is the strong baseline: it is the Cascade Log stripped of
persistence and order, a single hash map that is the sole version authority and
retains the same constant-size spine through folding. It is therefore anomaly-free
and bounded in working set, and resolves in expected
O(1)
, matching the
Cascade Log on every current-state metric; what it cannot do is read a past epoch
(it keeps no version history) or report an ordered key range across strata in

O(\log n+k)
(a hash imposes no order). It isolates precisely what the

persistent, ordered
index buys. An independent oracle tracks the exact
current and historical version of every handle and classifies each resolution by
Definition
2.1
. All workloads and measurements are seeded and reproduce
bit-for-bit; the package, the result files, and the figure scripts accompany the
paper as ancillary files.

Summary.

Among designs that bound the working set,
HashSpine
and the Cascade Log are
the anomaly-free ones; the tiered baselines that are not lose almost the entire
history. Against the hash spine the Cascade Log gives up a constant factor in
resolution latency and, in return, coalesces its index—to
o(n)
nodes on
append-dominated streams, and a smaller constant times
n
under sustained edits,
in both regimes below the hash spine’s
n
—folds in amortised constant index work
per appended record, and serves snapshot-consistent past-epoch reads and ordered
cross-tier ranges that a hash cannot.
Theorem

4.5
shows the per-handle version
both retain cannot be lossily compressed, and Proposition
4.6
that the
ordered range the Cascade Log adds is provably beyond a hash.

Related work

Persistence.

Path copying and node copying make pointer structures persistent at logarithmic
or constant amortised overhead
[
13
]
, and
persistent search trees underlie classical multiversion
queries
[
23
]
. The
vii
uses persistence not for geometric
queries but to make each fold and edit produce an immutable instant that windows
can be read against; the snapshot consistency of
Theorem

4.2
follows from
persistence rather than from locking, in the linearizable
sense of
15
.

Tiered and write-optimised storage.

Folding aged blocks into summaries is the organising idea of the log-structured
merge-tree
[
19
]
, where membership filters
[
7
]
avoid
fruitless probes into cold levels and amortised analysis bounds the merge
cost
[
27
,
5
]
. These designs optimise
throughput for opaque keys; the Cascade Log adds what they do not track—a stable
per-record handle that must keep resolving across the very compaction that moves
its target.

External memory.

The motivating setting is tiered storage, and
Theorem

4.12
carries the design into
the I/O model
[
1
]
: an
(a,b)
-tree
realisation
[
2
,
12
]
resolves and folds in

O(\log_{\beta}n)
I/Os and reports a range in
O(\log_{\beta}n+k/\beta)
,
bounded below by the dictionary update–query trade-off
of
10
that the log-structured merge-tree also navigates.
Our measurements are RAM-resident (
Section

6
); a paginated implementation
and a head-to-head against tuned LSM and B-tree stores are the systems counterpart
this paper does not attempt.

Multiversion and temporal indexing.

Serving a read as of a past instant is the business of multiversion concurrency
control
[
6
]
and of versioned and temporal indexes—the
asymptotically optimal multiversion B-tree
[
4
]
and the access
methods surveyed by
22
—which answer time-travel queries
at scale and concurrently. These index opaque keys and keep every version; the
Cascade Log differs in the two ways that define its niche. It bounds the materialised
working set by
lossily summarising
aged records (
Definition

2.1
), trading
payload fidelity for a footprint independent of history length, where a
multiversion B-tree retains every version in full; and it guarantees

reference integrity
, a stable handle resolving to the right version across the
compaction that summarises its target, which the versioned-index literature does not
pose because its keys neither migrate nor summarise. The two are complementary: a
multiversion B-tree is one concurrent, external-memory realisation of the persistent
ordered map the Cascade Log needs (
Theorem

4.12
), and the Cascade Log’s own
contribution is the coalescing fold and the cross-tier guarantees layered on it.

Layered search.

Searching ordered layers in one logarithmic pass is the province of fractional
cascading
[
11
]
and of probabilistic layered
lists
[
21
]
; B-trees give the external-memory
counterpart
[
12
]
. The Cascade Log’s cross-tier reporting reaches
the same
O(\log n+k)
through a single multiversion index, and any of
these techniques may realise it over per-stratum indexes instead.

Ranking and selection.

Diffusing relevance by a personalised random walk is personalised
PageRank
[
9
,
14
]
; choosing a budgeted subset that
maximises a submodular coverage is budgeted maximum
coverage
[
16
]
, whose greedy inherits the classical

1-1/e
analysis for submodular maximisation
[
18
]
.
The window of
Section

3.5
composes the two over the reference graph and
recovers prefix-by-rank as the modular special case.

Sequence buffers and diffs.

Editor buffers that keep a long mutable sequence in balanced
pieces
[
8
]
and difference algorithms that compute
edits between versions
[
17
]
address the per-record content that a
Cascade Log record may hold; they are orthogonal to, and composable with, the
cross-tier addressing studied here.

8
Conclusion

A tiered append sequence keeps its working set bounded, but the obvious way to do
so discards the per-record state that lets handles resolve across tier
boundaries, and the result fails exactly where the tiers meet. The Cascade Log
shows that the two goals are compatible at a price that is provably small: a
persistent interval map as the single authority on every handle, folding that
coalesces a run into one node in a single splice—so compaction is amortised
constant per appended record and the index is sublinear—and digests that keep
each handle’s version, the information a lower bound shows is irreducible. The
result is resolution that is total, snapshot-consistent, and anomaly-free at
logarithmic cost, with a working set independent of history length; the budgeted
window is a standard maximum-coverage instance layered on top. The reference
implementation is single-threaded and pays the constant factor of a pure-language
persistent tree; a concurrent, de-amortised, external-memory realisation
(
Section

7
), and digests that compress versions succinctly, are natural
next steps.
