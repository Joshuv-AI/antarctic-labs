Association of glycolysis-related protein expression with prognosis in Canine Pulmonary Adenocarcinoma
Authors: Ami AOYAMA, Taiki KATO, Masanao ICHIMATA, Kosuke KOBAYASHI, Masashi SAKURAI, Yumiko KAGAWA, Hiroto TOYODA, Ryuzo KATAYAMA, Kei HARADA, Eri FUKAZAWA, Fukiko MATSUYAMA, Tetsuya KOBAYASHI, Takuya MIZUNO, Masaya IGASE
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Canine pulmonary adenocarcinoma (CPAC) is a rare, aggressive tumor with no established prognostic molecular biomarkers. The expression of monocarboxylate transporter 4 (MCT4) and glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1), key glycolysis-related proteins, is induced by hypoxia and is generally associated with poor prognosis in human pulmonary adenocarcinoma; however, their clinical significance in dogs remains unclear. This study investigated the expression of MCT4 and GLUT1 in 61 CPAC cases. Immunohistochemistry revealed significantly higher MCT4 and GLUT1 expression in tumors compared with normal lung tissues. High expression of both proteins was associated with lymph node metastasis. Kaplan–Meier analysis showed that high MCT4 expression was associated with prolonged overall survival, while GLUT1 showed no prognostic impact. However, MCT4 was not retained as an independent prognostic factor in multivariable Cox proportional hazards analysis. These results suggest that MCT4 and GLUT1 are upregulated in CPAC and may have distinct biological and clinical significance compared with human pulmonary adenocarcinoma.

INTRODUCTION

Tumor metabolism depends on glycolysis, with glucose uptake mediated by glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1) and adenosine triphosphate generated through the glycolytic pathway [17]. Lactate, a glycolysis byproduct, is exported via monocarboxylate transporter 4 (MCT4), acidifying the tumor microenvironment [3]. This metabolic reprogramming is regulated by hypoxia-inducible factors, particularly hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF1α), which is activated under hypoxia and overexpressed in various malignancies. HIF1α promotes angiogenesis, invasion, metastasis, augmented glucose metabolism, and adaptation to acidic conditions, with high expression associated with increased metastatic potential and poor prognosis [23].

Glycolysis-related transporters MCT4 and GLUT1 are upregulated in several human cancers, including lung [19, 20, 22], breast [2], and hepatocellular carcinoma [1], and their elevated expressions are correlated with tumor progression and poor prognosis.

Primary lung cancer is one of the most common and deadly cancers in humans, with adenocarcinoma as the predominant subtype, and advances in molecular targeted therapies have deepened the understanding of its pathogenesis. In dogs, primary lung cancer is rare, but adenocarcinoma is the most frequent form and shares histological features with human lung adenocarcinoma [15]. Canine pulmonary adenocarcinoma (CPAC) primarily affects older dogs, often remains subclinical in the early stage, and typically progresses to chronic respiratory signs like cough. Surgery is the primary treatment, with adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy used selectively [12, 13, 15].

Previous studies in small-breed dogs with pulmonary adenocarcinoma identified tumor size, surgical margin status, and Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2) mutation as predictors of tumor progression and survival. [5, 6] Although many dogs achieve long-term survival after surgery, some have poor outcomes, underscoring the need for reliable prognostic biomarkers. In human lung adenocarcinoma, high MCT4 and GLUT1 expression is associated with increased malignancy, metastasis, and worse prognosis [19, 20]. However, their expression and prognostic relevance have not yet been studied in CPAC.

Although immunohistochemical analyses of clinical specimens can clarify the prognostic relevance of glycolysis-related markers, they provide limited insight into their functional roles within tumor cells. Therefore, we first performed in vitro experiments using CPAC cell lines cultured under normoxic and hypoxic conditions to characterize hypoxia-associated regulation and the biological significance of key glycolysis-related transporters, including MCT4 and GLUT1. We then evaluated MCT4 and GLUT1 expression by immunohistochemistry in clinical CPAC specimens and examined their associations with clinicopathologic variables and outcome, to determine their potential utility as prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Cell culture

The CPAC-derived cell line CPACd-p [9] was cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin–streptomycin, and 55 µM 2-mercaptoethanol, and maintained at 37°C in 5% CO2. Hypoxic conditions (1% O2, 5% CO2, 37°C) and normoxic conditions (21% O2, 5% CO2, 37°C) were applied a multi-gas incubator (SA-30D, Astec, Fukuoka, Japan), as previously described [7].

Protein extraction and Western blotting

The in vitro hypoxia experiments in this study were designed to evaluate the time-dependent induction of hypoxia-responsive proteins, including MCT4, GLUT1, and HIF1α. CPACd-p cells were seeded in 9.2-cm2 petri dishes at a density of 5 × 105 cells and incubated overnight under normoxia. After replacing the culture supernatant, cells were cultured for 0, 3, 6, and 24 hr under normoxic or hypoxic conditions. Cells were then harvested and lysed with NP40-based buffer (1% NP40, 10 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.5), 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, protease inhibitor cocktails (Nacalai Tesque, Kyoto, Japan), 1 mM Na3VO4, and 50 mM NaF), to extract total protein, which was separated by 10% acrylamide gel electrophoresis (SuperSep Ace, FujiFilm, Tokyo, Japan). Proteins were transferred to a PVDF membrane (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany) and blocked for 1 hr at room temperature (RT) in Tris-buffered saline with 0.05% Tween 20 and 5% skimmed milk. The membrane was incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies diluted in TBS-T (Tris-buffered saline with 0.05% Tween-20) containing 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA): mouse anti-human HIF1α (H1α67, Novus Biologicals, Centennial, CO, USA) [7], rabbit anti-human MCT4 (Novus Biologicals) [7], rabbit anti-human GLUT1 (Abcam, Cambridge, UK) [16].The membrane was then incubated for 1 hr at RT with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)–conjugated secondary antibodies: anti-rabbit IgG (Jackson ImmunoResearch, West Baltimore Pike, West Grove, PA, USA) or HRP-conjugated anti-mouse IgG (BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA). Mouse anti-β-actin monoclonal antibody served as a loading control. Finally, specific proteins were visualized using ECL reagent (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA) and subsequently detected with AMERSHAM Image Quant 800 (Global Life Sciences Technologies Japan, Tokyo, Japan).

Specimens

Normal lung tissues (n=6) were obtained from our laboratory’s archival collection of healthy canine samples or from the Laboratory of Veterinary Pathology at Yamaguchi University. CPAC tumor tissues (n=72) were retrieved from our institutional archive and included dogs from previous retrospective cohort studies [5, 6]. Cases comprised dogs seen at the Japan Small Animal Cancer Center between May 2005 and November 2021 with histologically confirmed lung adenocarcinoma. Eleven dogs lacking tumor tissue or censored from progression-free survival (PFS) analysis were excluded. As no new animal experiments were conducted, an ethics committee approval was not required.

Immunohistochemistry

Paraffin-embedded tissue sections (4 µm thick) were placed on MAS-coated glass slides (Matsunami glass kogyo, Osaka, Japan). After deparaffinization and alcohol rehydration, antigens were retrieved by autoclaving in Dako Target Retrieval Solution pH9 (Agilent Technologies Japan, Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) for 20 min at 121°C. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% H2O2 in methanol for 30 min at RT, followed by rinsing in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Subsequently, the sections were blocked with 5% BSA and 5% skimmed milk in PBS for 30 min at RT. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4°C with rabbit anti-MCT4 polyclonal antibody (Novus Biologicals), rabbit anti-human GLUT1 polyclonal antibody (Abcam), or rabbit IgG isotype control (DA1E, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA) diluted in 2% BSA in PBS. The sections were treated with Histofine Simple Stain MAX PO (Nichirei Corp., Tokyo, Japan) for 30 min at RT as the secondary antibody. After the PBS washes, color was developed using diaminobenzidine (Nacalai Tesque) to produce a brown precipitate, followed by nuclear counterstaining with Mayer’s hematoxylin. The sections were mounted using an aqueous mounting medium. Five random fields per slide were selected, and cases were considered positive if membranous staining of MCT4 or GLUT1 was detected. Quantitative analysis of MCT4- and GLUT1-positive areas was performed using ImageJ software, as previously reported [8]. The scale unit was converted from pixels to micrometers (µm), RGB channels were separated, and blue channel intensity was subtracted from the red channel intensity. A threshold was set to define the positive staining area for quantification. A fixed threshold was set for MCT4 and GLUT1 in each case. The positive area was measured in five randomly selected fields, and the mean value was calculated to represent the expression score for each tissue sample. MCT4 and GLUT1 expression scores were analyzed as continuous variables and dichotomized at the median for association analyses (low, <median; high, ≥median).

Statistical analysis

PFS was defined as the time from surgery to first documented disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first, and overall survival (OS) as the period from surgery to death from any cause. PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with group differences assessed by the log-rank test. Potential risk factors for MCT4 and GLUT1 expression, including age, body weight, clinical signs, vascular invasion, lymph node status, tumor location, surgical procedure and margin, histological grade and pattern based on the primary pulmonary carcinomas (PPCs) grading system [14], tumor size, and adjuvant chemotherapy, were evaluated. The canine lung carcinoma stage classification (CLCSC) staging system was used in this study [10]. Dogs without lymph node sampling were classified as N0 according to previous reports [5, 6]. Associations between MCT4 or GLUT1 expression and each variable were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum or Kruskal–Wallis test, as appropriate, and reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR). A multivariable Cox proportional hazards analysis was conducted to assess whether MCT4 and GLUT1 were independent prognostic factors. To minimize overfitting, a parsimonious model with a limited number of variables was employed. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05. Analyses were conducted using JMP version Pro 18.0.2 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). All the data analyzed in this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study cohort

Archived normal lung tissue was obtained from six dogs, including five Beagles and one Akita. Of 72 CPAC cases [5, 6], 61 dogs were eligible for analysis after excluding 10 dogs censored for PFS due to being alive at the time of analysis or lost to follow-up, and one without tumor tissue. The cohort included 4 intact males, 26 neutered males, 1 intact female, and 30 spayed females, with a median age of 12 years (IQR, 9–13 years) and median weight of 5.8 kg (IQR, 4.9–9.6 kg). Twenty-four breeds were represented, including Toy Poodle (n=9), Miniature Dachshund (n=7), Chihuahua (n=6), Shih Tzu (n=5), and mixed breed dogs (n=5), with the remaining 29 dogs each belonging to other breeds (≤4 per breed).

Histopathology showed complete tumor resection in 52 dogs (85.2%). Tumor grades based on the PPCs grading systems were grade 1 in 39 dogs (63.9%), grade 2 in 20 (32.8%), and grade 3 in 2 (3.3%), with vascular invasion in 10 dogs (16.4%). Using the CLCSC staging system, 15 dogs were stage 1, 30 dogs as stage 2, and 16 dogs as stage 3, with no cases of stage 4 disease. Postoperative chemotherapy was administered to 21 dogs (34.4%), mainly carboplatin monotherapy (n=20), and 1 dog received a combination of carboplatin and doxorubicin. Median PFS was 326 days (95% confidence interval [CI], 358–680 days), and median OS was 506 days (95% CI, 484–811 days). The PFS events included documented disease progression and death without documented progression. Documented progression events were heterogeneous and included intrapulmonary progression (n=19), pleural effusion (n=11), lymph node metastasis (n=2), bone involvement (n=4), and other distant metastatic lesions (n=4). Death without documented progression was observed in 28 dogs. Seven dogs showed more than one type of documented progression event.

DISCUSSION

In this study, hypoxia induced the upregulation of glycolysis-related proteins, including MCT4 and GLUT1, in CPACd-p cells. This aligns with prior studies of human lung adenocarcinoma (A549) and canine melanoma cell lines, where hypoxic conditions similarly increased glycolysis-related proteins and promoted anaerobic glycolysis [7, 20].

Immunohistochemistry revealed significantly higher MCT4 and GLUT1 expressions in CPAC tissues compared with normal lung tissues. Moreover, both proteins were elevated in tumors with lymph node metastasis, with MCT4 significantly elevated in N1 cases, suggesting a link to tumor aggressiveness. In human lung adenocarcinoma, MCT4 and GLUT1 are overexpressed and linked to lymph node metastasis [20, 24]. High MCT4 promotes tumor invasion by exporting lactate, acidifying the microenvironment, and facilitating epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) [11, 18]. In dogs, however, the association between MCT4 and metastasis remains unexplored, warranting further study. In addition, the heterogeneous staining pattern of MCT4 observed in this study, particularly the presence of focal expression within tumor regions, may reflect intratumoral hypoxia or hypoxic gradients. MCT4 expression has been reported to increase with distance from blood vessels and to be prominent in perinecrotic regions, supporting its association with localized hypoxic conditions. Therefore, focal MCT4 expression in CPAC may represent an adaptive response to severe hypoxia within the tumor microenvironment. Future studies incorporating co-localization analyses with other hypoxia-related markers, such as HIF1α, are warranted to further clarify the relationship between spatial hypoxia, metabolic adaptation, treatment response, and prognosis.

This study revealed that high MCT4 expression was significantly associated with prolonged OS, indicating a favorable prognosis. Stratification by lymph node status demonstrated that MCT4-high tumors had better outcomes regardless of nodal status, suggesting MCT4 may be an independent prognostic factor. Although MCT4 and GLUT1 expression levels were positively associated with lymph node metastasis, high MCT4 expression was also associated with prolonged OS in the univariable survival analysis. This apparent discrepancy may reflect the complex and context-dependent role of MCT4 in tumor biology. MCT4 may contribute to tumor aggressiveness through lactate export and adaptation to hypoxic microenvironments; however, its expression may also represent a hypoxia-adaptive phenotype with distinct prognostic implications in CPAC. Importantly, the association between high MCT4 expression and prolonged survival was not retained in the multivariable Cox proportional hazards model after adjustment for age and clinical stage. These findings indicate that MCT4 is not an independent prognostic factor in this cohort, but rather may reflect underlying clinicopathological or biological features, including tumor progression status and hypoxia-related adaptation. Therefore, the clinical significance of MCT4 expression should be interpreted with caution.

Consistent findings were obtained when MCT4 and GLUT1 were analyzed as either continuous or dichotomized variables, supporting the robustness of the overall analytical approach. Nevertheless, validation in larger, multi-institutional cohorts is required. In addition, evaluation of MCT4 alone may be insufficient to fully characterize the metabolic phenotype of CPAC. Future studies should include comparative analyses of MCT4 expression between primary tumors and metastatic lesions, as well as integrative assessment of additional hypoxia- and glycolysis-related markers, such as monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) and lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA) [21]. Functional studies are also warranted to clarify the biological role of MCT4 in canine pulmonary adenocarcinoma.

In addition, the small number of metastatic cases (n=12) may have biased results. In human lung adenocarcinoma, prognosis is strongly influenced by tumor stage and the use of targeted therapies, and high glycolysis-related protein expression generally correlates with tumor aggressiveness [20]. By contrast, in dogs, tumor size and nodal involvement are the key prognostic factors, with treatment primarily surgical and limited systemic therapy evidence [12]. This suggests MCT4 may have a distinct biological and clinical role in CPAC compared with human pulmonary adenocarcinoma. Notably, in human small intestinal neuroendocrine tumors, MCT4 expression is a favorable prognostic factor, possibly due to hypoxia-driven HIF1α signaling promoting Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF)-mediated angiogenesis [4]. A similar mechanism might underlie the favorable prognosis of MCT4-high CPAC, although a direct link to VEGF in canine tumors remains unconfirmed.

This study has several limitations. First, this study focused on the time-dependent induction of hypoxia-responsive proteins under in vitro hypoxic conditions and did not include functional analyses such as cell morphology, proliferation, or invasion. Further studies are warranted to investigate the functional impact of hypoxia-induced proteins, including MCT4, on tumor cell behavior. Second, immunohistochemical quantification of MCT4 and GLUT1 was based on thresholding by a single evaluator, introducing potential bias. In addition, the tissue expression of HIF1α was not evaluated due to the insufficient stainability of commercial anti-HIF1α antibodies. Third, the study was conducted at a single institution with a relatively small sample size, limiting statistical power. Fourth, cases without lymph node resection were classified as N0, which may have affected the results. Reasons for non-resection included clinician choice, intraoperative failure to locate lymph nodes, or unknown factors. Fifth, progression events in CPAC are heterogeneous. While recurrence-free survival (RFS) is commonly used in surgically treated cohorts, it is limited to recurrence events and may not fully capture the spectrum of clinically relevant progression events. Therefore, RFS could provide less comprehensive information than PFS in this context. Finally, the clinical impact of the observed difference should be interpreted cautiously in this retrospective cohort and requires validation in independent datasets.

In conclusion, hypoxia promotes glycolysis in CPAC cells through HIF1α-mediated upregulation of MCT4 and GLUT1. High MCT4 expression was associated with a favorable prognosis, whereas GLUT1 showed no clear survival association. These findings underscore the need for further research on the functional and clinical significance of MCT4 and GLUT1 in CPAC.
