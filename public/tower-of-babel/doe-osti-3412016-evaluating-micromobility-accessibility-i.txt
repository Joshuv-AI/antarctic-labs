  E2C Technical Assistance Opportunities

In-Depth Partnerships ~three years
Multiyear partnership made up of teams (local governments, community-based organizations, and electric utilities) that work
alongside national laboratory staff to apply robust modeling and analysis tools and simulate hardware in conditions that match
communities' unique contexts to evaluate potential scenarios and strategies before full technology deployment.
Supports nine communities currently.

Peer-Learning Cohorts ~six months
Six months of regular convenings where cohorts of up to 15 participants exchange strategies and best practices, learn in a
collaborative environment, and workshop plans and strategies to overcome challenges relating to a similar energy
topic. Eligibility varies by cohort topic.
Supports about 100 communities per year.

Expert Match ~three months
Short-term, no-cost technical assistance for communities seeking to answer near-term energy questions. Eligible entities include
local governments, utilities, community-based organizations, schools, and nonprofits.
Supports about 200 communities per year. Applications are accepted all year on a rolling basis.

 MEP Metric

The MEP metric is a unitless accessibility-based performance metric that evaluates
how efficiently a transportation system enables access to essential destinations relative
to travel time, cost, and energy consumption.
Mobility energy productivity
includes access to many
types of opportunities (food,
healthcare, education,
recreation, retail) and access
to jobs.
A MEP score can combine
these multiple factors into one
number or a subset of these
dimensions. MEP Metric Overview
MEP is a unitless metric, where a higher score for a region indicates better
accessibility.                                                            National Laboratory of the Rockies | 8

 Study Area

Study area: Greeley, Colorado, urban area
boundary.
The city boundary was divided into 0.25- ×
0.25-kilometer grid cells, resulting in 1,250
pixels.
For each pixel, raw MEP scores are
calculated by accounting for the
reachability of various opportunity types by
micromobility, discounted by time, cost,
and energy factors.
Pixel-level raw MEP scores are then
aggregated using population weights to
obtain the city-wide score.

Level of Traffic Stress

 Level of Traffic Stress

Level of traffic stress (LTS) quantifies the discomfort riders experience when
traveling by micromobility modes or bicycles near traffic.
There are four LTS levels:
LTS 1 = Low traffic stress and suitable for all cyclists, including children.
LTS 2 = Little traffic stress but requires more attention, especially for
children.
LTS 3 = Moderate traffic stress—suitable for confident cyclists.
LTS 4 = High traffic stress.

Appendix: Mobility Energy
Productivity and Economic
Productivity Relationships

 Rationale

When people can access jobs, healthcare, and services more easily, their
productivity increases—enhancing local and regional economic growth.
How can we measure that economic productivity gain (or loss) resulting from
change in accessibility?
Accessibility isn’t just a mobility issue—it’s a foundation for economic productivity
and business efficiency.
Imagine an individual living in a location who is trying to access a job. Because of
limited walkable infrastructure, they face delays or additional costs. This not only
reduces their productivity but also impacts overall business output.
 Goal: Quantify how a $1 infrastructure investment can translate to
 measurable gains in economic productivity.

                                                                    National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   34

 Accessibility Impacts on Economic Growth

Accessibility has a considerable impact on a region’s economic
growth:
• Poor-quality transport infrastructure leads to higher travel times, costs,
  and uncertainties, which indirectly affect the productivity of a region
  because transportation costs are directly related to sales prices (Ozbay
  et al. 2003).
• Reduced transport cost and time increase the real incomes of society
  and the competitiveness of a region (Jubiz-Diaz et al. 2021).
• Accessibility significantly affects the employment growth and total
  earnings growth of a region.
