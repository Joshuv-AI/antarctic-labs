Meta Algebras and Special Functions: the Racah Case
Nicolas Crampé, Quentin Labriet, Lucia Morey, Satoshi Tsujimoto, Luc Vinet, Alexei Zhedanov

Abstract
. Finite families of biorthogonal rational functions and orthogonal polynomials of Racah-type are studied within a unified algebraic framework based on the meta Racah algebra and its finite-dimensional representations. These functions are identified as overlap coefficients between eigensolutions of generalized and standard eigenvalue problems posited on the representation space. The approach naturally yields their orthogonality relations and bispectral properties.



1.
Introduction

Orthogonal polynomial families belonging to the Askey scheme
[
20
]
possess a dual spectral structure: they obey a three-term recurrence relation and simultaneously satisfy a differential or difference equation, both interpretable as eigenvalue problems in either the variable or degree picture, for suitable linear operators. These two operators generate the so called Askey–Wilson algebra, or one of its limiting or specialized forms
[
31
]
. From this perspective, the orthogonal polynomials are realized as overlap coefficients between the eigenbases of the corresponding two algebra generators within a given irreducible representation. In the case of finite-dimensional representations, this set of operators constitutes a Leonard pair
[
24
]
, a structure that is in one-to-one correspondence with the terminating families in the Askey scheme.

This work advances a long-term research program devoted to a unified algebraic treatment of biorthogonal rational functions. An algebraic interpretation

In the present paper, we turn to the Racah case and investigate terminating

2.
The Meta Racah Algebra

The starting point for the algebraic interpretation of the biorthogonal rational functions of Racah type is the meta Racah algebra defined below. Throughout this paper, we use the notations

[A,B]=AB-BA
and
\{A,B\}=AB+BA
for the commutator and the anti-commutator, respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

The meta Racah algebra
m{\mathfrak{R}}
is the associative algebra with unit
I
and generators
X
,
V
,
Z

obeying the defining relations

(2.1)

\displaystyle[Z,X]

\displaystyle=Z^{2}+X\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle[X,V]

\displaystyle=\{V,Z\}+2\zeta X+2\zeta^{2}Z+\xi I\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle[V,Z]

\displaystyle=V+2X+2\zeta Z+\eta I\,,

with
\eta
,
\zeta
and
\xi
central elements.

The meta Racah algebra contains a Casimir element given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle C=2ZVZ+\{X,V\}+2\zeta\{X,Z\}+2X^{2}+2\zeta^{2}Z^{2}+2\eta X+V+2\xi Z\,,

which commutes with the generators
X
,
Z
and
V
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let us define the following generators:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\overline{Z}=Z-\frac{\zeta}{2}\,,\quad\overline{X}=X+\zeta Z-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}\,,\quad\overline{V}=V,

which satisfy the following commutation relations

(2.6)

\displaystyle[\overline{Z},\overline{X}]

\displaystyle=\overline{Z}^{2}+\overline{X},

(2.7)

\displaystyle[\overline{X},\overline{V}]

\displaystyle=\{\overline{V},\overline{Z}\}+\overline{\xi}I,

(2.8)

\displaystyle[\overline{V},\overline{Z}]

\displaystyle=\overline{V}+2\overline{X}+\overline{\eta}I,

where
\overline{\xi}=\xi-\eta\zeta
and
\overline{\eta}=\eta+\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{2}
.
The above presentation of the meta Racah algebra is equivalent to that given in Definition
2.1
and provides a more convenient framework for establishing connections with other algebras, as illustrated in the following paragraphs. We emphasize, however, that the presentation given in Definition
2.1
is the one that is most relevant for the study of special functions. Although this presentation of the meta Racah algebra is equivalent to that given in Definition
2.1
,
\zeta
disappears from the equations, and for this reason one cannot properly recover all the parameters of the special functions.

Next we discuss important connections between the meta Racah algebra and some related algebras.

Hahn algebra.

Let us remark that
\overline{X}
can be expressed in terms of
\overline{V}
and
\overline{Z}
using relation (
2.8
). Substituting this expression, one obtains an equivalent set of defining relations for the meta Racah algebra involving only
\overline{V}
and
\overline{Z}
:

(2.9)

\displaystyle[[\overline{V},\overline{Z}],\overline{V}]=2\{\overline{V},\overline{Z}\}+2\overline{\xi}I\,,

(2.10)

\displaystyle[\overline{Z},[\overline{V},\overline{Z}]]=2\overline{Z}^{2}-\overline{V}-\overline{\eta}I\,.

These relations coincide with the defining relations of the Hahn algebra. Indeed, upon identifying
K_{1}=-2\overline{Z}
and
K_{2}=-\overline{V}
, one recovers the definition of the Hahn algebra given in equation (1.3) of
[
8
]
. Hence, the Hahn algebra through its connection with the meta Racah algebra will lead to interpretation of rational functions of
Racah
type.

Meta Hahn algebra.

The meta Hahn algebra

was introduced and studied in
[
29
]

m{\mathfrak{R}}
. To this end, we define new generators by

(2.11)

\displaystyle\overline{Z}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{Z}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\,,\qquad\overline{X}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{X}-\frac{1}{4\epsilon^{2}}\,,\qquad\overline{V}=\widetilde{V}\,,\qquad\overline{\xi}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{\xi}\,,\qquad\overline{\eta}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{\eta}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\,.

These new generators satisfy the modified relations

(2.12)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{Z},\widetilde{X}]=\widetilde{Z}^{2}+\widetilde{Z}+\epsilon\widetilde{X}\,,

(2.13)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{X},\widetilde{V}]=\{\widetilde{V},\widetilde{Z}\}+\widetilde{V}+\widetilde{\xi}I\,,

(2.14)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{V},\widetilde{Z}]=\epsilon\widetilde{V}+2\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{\eta}I\,.

In the limit

q

Meta

The meta

as the natural algebra encoding the bispectral properties of the

-Racah rational functions. This algebra is generated by three elements

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{Z}},{\mathcal{X}}]_{q}={\mathfrak{f}}_{1}{\mathcal{Z}}+{\mathfrak{f}}_{2}{\mathcal{X}}\,,

(2.16)

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{V}}]_{q}={\mathfrak{f}}_{1}{\mathcal{V}}+{\mathfrak{f}}_{6}{\mathcal{Z}}+{\mathfrak{f}}_{7}I\,,

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{Z}}]_{q}={\mathfrak{f}}_{2}{\mathcal{V}}+{\mathfrak{f}}_{4}{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathfrak{f}}_{5}I\,,

where

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}_{6}=\frac{b}{a}(1+q)\,,\quad{\mathfrak{f}}_{7}=\frac{qb}{a^{2}}(1+q)-\frac{q}{a}(bed+eb+bd+1)\;.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}=(1-q)^{2}\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}-\frac{{\mathfrak{f}}_{1}{\mathfrak{f}}_{2}{\mathfrak{f}}_{4}}{\omega^{2}}I,\quad{\mathcal{Z}}=(1-q)\omega\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}}-\omega+{\mathfrak{f}}_{2},

2.6

Racah algebra.

The subalgebra of the meta Racah algebra generated by

(with
\rho
a free parameter),

and its Casimir element

, defined by (

), is the Racah algebra

(2.21)

\displaystyle[V,[W,V]]=2\{W,V\}+2V^{2}+2(\eta+\zeta(\zeta-\rho))V+2(\rho\xi+\zeta(\zeta\eta-\xi-\eta\rho))I\,,

(2.22)

\displaystyle[[W,V],W]=2\{W,V\}+2W^{2}+2(\eta+\zeta(\zeta-\rho))W+(1-\rho^{2})V-C+\rho(\xi-\rho\eta)I\,.

The Racah algebra encodes the bispectrality of the eponymous polynomials: we show in the following that the overlap coefficients between the basis diagonalizing

Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

The subalgebra of the meta Racah algebra generated by
X
and
Z
subject only to (

) is isomorphic to a Borel subalgebra of the Lie algebra

(see
[

which is the defining relation of the Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.
Another way to say it is that it is isomorphic to the enveloping algebra of the non-abelian two-dimensional solvable Lie algebra.

Two-diagonal Representation

Let
N

{\mathscr{V}}_{N}

\mid{v}\,\rangle

\mid{w}\,\rangle

The representations of the Hahn algebra (

\displaystyle\overline{\xi}=(\overline{\beta}+1)(\overline{\beta}-N)(N-2\overline{\alpha}),\quad\overline{\eta}=\frac{1}{2}\big((2\overline{\alpha}-N)^{2}+(\overline{\beta}-N)^{2}+\overline{\beta}(\overline{\beta}+2)\big).

(3.10)

\displaystyle Z^{\top}\mid{n}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=(n-\alpha)\mid{n}\,\rangle+\mid{n-1}\,\rangle\,,

(3.11)

\displaystyle V^{\top}\mid{n}\,\rangle

\displaystyle X^{\top}\mid{n}\,\rangle

(V,Z)
is the linear combination

(3.13)

\displaystyle H=h_{0}I+h_{1}Z+h_{2}V+h_{3}ZV+h_{4}VZ\,,

for

and appears in the study of quantum integrable systems

(V,Z)

.

The most general algebraic Heun operator associated to the Leonard pair
(V,Z)

(3.14)

for

For simplicity, let us denote the bidiagonal representations (

\displaystyle Z\mid{n}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}\mid{n}\,\rangle+\mid{n+1}\,\rangle,

\displaystyle V\mid{n}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}\mid{n}\,\rangle+\gamma_{n}\mid{n-1}\,\rangle,

and consider the algebraic Heun operator

defined in (

)
The action of

\displaystyle H\mid{n}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=(h_{1}+h_{3}\beta_{n}+h_{4}\beta_{n+1})\mid{n+1}\,\rangle+(h_{0}+h_{1}\alpha_{n}+h_{2}\beta_{n}+h_{3}\beta_{n}\alpha_{n}+h_{3}\gamma_{n}+h_{4}\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}+h_{4}\gamma_{n+1})\mid{n}\,\rangle

4.
(Generalized) Eigenbases

Finite-dimensional representations of the meta Racah algebra have been obtained in the previous section.
In order to study biorthogonal rational functions some GEVP appears naturally. Hence it is important to consider GEVP bases associated with the elements
X
and
Z
.
We also consider EVP bases associated with the elements
X+\rho Z
and
V
. In the forthcoming sections, the orthogonal polynomials and bispectral rational functions will then be identified explicitly as overlap coefficients between different (generalized) eigenbases, and their properties are derived using the actions of the generators in these various bases. We provide these bases below and additionally, we include the EVP associated with

More precisely, the different (generalized) eigenbases useful in this paper are:

\displaystyle\mid{z_{n}}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-N)_{N-n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}(n-N)_{N-\ell}\mid{\ell}\,\rangle,

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mid{z^{*}_{n}}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(-n)_{n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}(-1)^{\ell+n}(-n)_{\ell}\mid{\ell}\,\rangle.

Proof.

Here is a sketch of the proof.

(i) Consider to begin, the eigenvectors

of

in the

(iii) Similarly, the eigenvectors

(4.31)

\displaystyle(\mu_{N-\ell}-\mu_{n})\langle\,{N-\ell}\mid{e_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle+(N-\ell)(\ell+1)(2N-\ell-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta-1)\langle\,{N-\ell-1}\mid{e_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle=0\,,

(4.32)

6.
Racah Polynomials

It is well-known that the Racah polynomials can be interpreted as overlap functions between basis vectors for representation spaces of the Racah algebra. Here, in spirit of previous works
[
29
]
,
[
2
]
on the (
q
-)Hahn case, we explain how the Racah polynomials can be recovered and characterized in a straightforward manner using the bidiagonal representation of the meta Racah algebra. This approach provides a unified framework for the study of both orthogonal polynomials and bispectral rational functions.
Recall that the Racah polynomials are defined in terms of the generalized hypergeometric functions as

(6.1)

\displaystyle R_{i}(x;\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},\hat{\gamma},N)={}_{4}F_{3}\left({{-i,\;i+\hat{\alpha}+\hat{\beta}+1,\;-x,\;x+\hat{\gamma}-N}\atop{\hat{\alpha}+1,\;\hat{\beta}+\hat{\gamma}+1,\;-N}}\;\Bigg|\;1\right)\,,\qquad i=0,\ldots,N.

As noted previously, the matrices
V
and
X+\rho Z
satisfy the Racah algebra and, as a consequence
[
13
,
14
]
, the overlap coefficients between the bases
\mid{e_{n}}\,\rangle
and
\mid{f_{n}}\,\rangle
diagonalizing respectively
V
and
X+\rho Z
are proportional to the Racah polynomials. We denote these overlap coefficients by

(6.2)

S_{m}(n)=\langle\,{f_{n}^{*}}\mid{e_{m}}\,\rangle,\qquad\tilde{S}_{m}(n)=\langle\,{f_{n}}\mid{e_{m}^{*}}\,\rangle.

6.1.
Identification

We start by identifying precisely the Racah polynomials as overlap coeﬃcients between EVP
bases in the representation of the meta Racah algebra.

Proposition 4
.

The functions
S_{m}(n)=\langle\,{f_{n}^{*}}\mid{e_{m}}\,\rangle
and
\tilde{S}_{m}(n)=\langle\,{f_{n}}\mid{e_{m}^{*}}\,\rangle
are both expressible in terms of Racah polynomials as follows

(6.3)

\displaystyle S_{m}(n)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\hat{\alpha}+1)_{n}(-N,\hat{\beta}+\hat{\gamma}+1)_{m}}{n!(n-N+\hat{\gamma})_{n}(m+\hat{\alpha}+\hat{\beta}+1)_{m}}R_{m}(n;\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},\hat{\gamma},N),

(6.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{m}(n

\displaystyle)=\frac{(-1)^{N}(-N,\hat{\gamma}-\hat{\alpha}-N,-\hat{\beta}-N)_{N-m}(\hat{\alpha}+1)_{m}(-\hat{\gamma}-\hat{\beta}-n)_{n}}{(-N-m-\hat{\alpha}-\hat{\beta}-1)_{N-m}(n-N,-\hat{\gamma}-n)_{N-n}(\hat{\gamma}-\hat{\alpha}-N,-N-\hat{\beta})_{n}}R_{m}(n;\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},\hat{\gamma},N),

with

(6.5)

\hat{\alpha}=-\beta-\rho-1,\quad\hat{\beta}=-\beta+\rho-2\zeta-1,\quad\hat{\gamma}=N-2\alpha-\rho.

Proof.

Given the expressions (
4.18
) and (
4.21
) for
\mid{e_{n}}\,\rangle
and
\mid{f_{n}^{\ast}}\,\rangle
respectively, using
\langle\,{\ell}\mid{k}\,\rangle=\delta_{\ell,l}
and simple transformations, one straightforwardly finds

(6.6)

\displaystyle S_{m}(n)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(-\beta-\rho)_{n}(-N,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{m}}{n!(n-2\alpha-\rho)_{n}(m-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{m}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}\dfrac{(-n,-m,n-2\alpha-\rho,m-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{\ell}}{\ell!(-N,-\beta-\rho,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{\ell}},

which in view of definition (
6.1
) is readily seen to yield (
6.3
) under the identification (
6.5
). The
derivation of formula (
6.4
) requires a little more eﬀort. Given the expressions of (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) of
\mid{e_{n}^{\ast}}\,\rangle
and
\mid{f_{n}}\,\rangle
respectively, one finds

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{m}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-N,N+m-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{N-m}(\beta+\rho-N+1)_{N-n}}{(2\beta+2\zeta-N-m+1)_{N-m}(n-N,2\alpha+\rho-N-n)_{N-n}}

(6.7)

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left({{m-N,-N-m+2\beta+2\zeta+1,n-N,-N+2\alpha+\rho-n}\atop{-N,2\alpha+\beta+2\zeta-2N+1,\beta+\rho-N+1}}\;\Bigg|\;1\right).

The hypergeometric function in (
6.7
) needs to be transformed to identify the Racah polynomials. The following formula for terminating balanced
\,\mbox{}_{4}F_{3}
-series will be used
[
10
]

(6.8)

{}_{4}F_{3}\left({{-n,a,b,c}\atop{d,e,f}}\;\Bigg|\;1\right)=\frac{(e-a,f-a)_{n}}{(e,f)_{n}}{}_{4}F_{3}\left({{-n,a,d-b,d-c}\atop{d,a+1-n-e,a+1-n-f}}\;\Bigg|\;1\right)\,.

where
n
is a non-negative integer and
1-n+a+b+c=d+e+f
.
Applying twice (
6.8
) with
a=-N-m+2\beta+2\zeta+1,\,b=n-N,c=-N+2\alpha+\rho-n,d=-N,e=2\alpha+\beta+2\zeta-2N+1
and

, then with

and

\displaystyle{}_{4}F_{3}\left({{m-N,-N-m+2\beta+1,n-N,-N+2\alpha+\rho-n}\atop{-N,2\alpha+\beta+1-2N,-N+\beta+\rho+1}}\;\Bigg|\;1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{N+m}(-N,m+2\alpha-\beta-N,\rho+m-\beta-2\zeta)_{N-m}(\beta+\rho-N+1)_{N-n}}{(2\beta+2\zeta-N-m+1,\beta+\rho-N+1)_{N-m}(n-N,2\alpha+\rho-N-n)_{N-n}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(2\alpha+2\zeta+\beta-N-n+1,\beta+\rho-n+1)_{n}}{(\beta-2\alpha+1,\beta-\rho+2\zeta-N+1)_{n}}{}_{4}F_{3}\left({{-m,m-2\beta-2\zeta-1,-n,n-2\alpha-\rho}\atop{-N,-\beta-\rho,N-2\alpha-2\zeta-\beta}}\;\Bigg|\;1\right)\,.

Some simplifications can now be performed. One notes that

\displaystyle\frac{(\beta+\rho-N+1)_{N-n}(\beta+\rho-n+1)_{n}}{(\beta+\rho-N+1)_{N-m}}

(6.13)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{m}(n)

\displaystyle\times\frac{(2\alpha+2\zeta+\beta-N-n+1)_{n}}{(\beta-2\alpha+1,\beta-\rho+2\zeta-N+1)_{n}}{}_{4}F_{3}\left({{-m,m-2\beta-2\zeta-1,-n,n-2\alpha-\rho}\atop{-N,-\beta-\rho,N-2\alpha-2\zeta-\beta}}\;\Bigg|\;1\right)\,.

which in view of definition (

Using the explicit expressions (

\langle e_{m}\mid{z_{k}^{*}}\,\rangle=\frac{(-N,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{m}}{k!(m-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{m}}R^{(dH)}_{k}(m;\boldsymbol{\rho}).

when

\langle z_{k}\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle=(-1)^{k}\frac{(\alpha-\beta-n)_{n}(-\alpha)_{k}(-n)_{k}}{n!(-\alpha)_{n+1}(\alpha-\beta-n)_{k}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left({{-n+k,-\alpha+k}\atop{-n+k+\alpha-\beta}}\;\Bigg|\;1\right)=\frac{(-\alpha)_{k}(2\alpha-\beta-n)_{n-k}}{(-\alpha)_{n+1}(n-k)!},

where we used Chu–Vandermonde’s identity for the last equality. The result is then obtained from (

∎

(7.21)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}(j)=\frac{(-N,1-\alpha+\beta,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{j}(2\alpha-\beta-N,\alpha+\beta-N+2\zeta)_{N-j}}{j!(-\alpha,-2\beta-2\zeta)_{N}},

(7.22)

\displaystyle h_{n}^{*}=\frac{(1,1-2\alpha+\beta,1-\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{n}}{(-N,1-\alpha+\beta,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{n}},

(7.23)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}^{*}(j)=\frac{(-N)_{j}}{j!}\frac{(2j-1-2\beta-2\zeta)}{(j-1-2\beta-2\zeta)_{N+1}}\frac{(1-2\alpha+\beta,1-\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{N}}{(-\alpha)_{N}}.

As a result of (

), (
4.12
), (
4.13
), (
4.14
), the orthogonality relations between

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\widetilde{U}_{k}(n)U_{m}(n)=\delta_{k,m},

(7.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{N}\widetilde{U}_{m}(k)U_{m}(n)=\delta_{k,n}.

Hence substituting the expressions (

) and (

and

in terms of

respectevely, one gets

(7.41)

\displaystyle X-2Z-I=X\big|_{\alpha\to\alpha-1,\beta\to\beta-2,\zeta\to\zeta+2}\,,\quad Z+I=Z\big|_{\alpha\to\alpha-1,\beta\to\beta-2,\zeta\to\zeta+2}\,.

The last two relations imply

(7.42)

\displaystyle(X^{\top}-\lambda_{n}Z^{\top})\,\big|_{\alpha\to\alpha-1,\beta\to\beta-2,\zeta\to\zeta+2}\ Z^{\top}\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle=0.

Therefore,
Z^{\top}\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle
is proportional to the vectors that are solutions of the GEVP (
4.2
) when the parameters are shifted according to
\alpha\to\alpha-1,\beta\to\beta-2,\zeta\to\zeta+2
. The proportionality factor is computed by comparing the coefficients of
\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle
and
Z^{\top}\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle
in front of
\mid{n}\,\rangle
. One obtains:

(7.43)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-\alpha+1}Z^{\top}\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle=\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle\big|_{\alpha\to\alpha-1,\beta\to\beta-2,\zeta\to\zeta+2}.

Moreover the action of
V
is not affected by these shifts and, by consequence, its eigenvectors
\mid{e_{n}}\,\rangle
are unchanged. One deduces that

(7.44)

\displaystyle\langle\,{e_{m}}|Z^{\top}\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle=(n-\alpha+1)U_{m}(n;\alpha-1,\beta-2,\zeta+2).

Using the actions of
Z^{\top}
on
\mid{d_{n}^{*}}\,\rangle
computed in subsection
5.3
, one deduces that

(7.45)

\displaystyle(n-\alpha+1)U_{m}(n;\alpha-1,\beta-2,\zeta+2)=(n-\alpha)U_{m}(n;\alpha,\beta,\zeta)+\dfrac{n-2\alpha+\beta}{n-\alpha}U_{m}(n-1;\alpha,\beta,\zeta).

Inserting the expression (
7.4
) of
U_{m}(n)
in terms of
\mathcal{U}_{m}(n)
, one proves the result of the proposition.
∎

) can be obtained by setting

.

8.
Differential relization of
m{\mathfrak{R}}

This section presents an explicit differential realization of the meta Racah algebra.
Consider the following differential operators (we use the same symbols for the abstract generators
and their realizations):

(8.1)

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=x(1-x)\frac{d}{dx}+(Nx-\alpha)I,

(8.2)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=x(1-x)\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\left(2x(\beta+\zeta)+N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta\right)\frac{d}{dx}-(\beta+\zeta)(\beta+\zeta+1)I,

(8.3)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=-x^{2}(1-x)\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-x((N+\beta-1)x-2\alpha+1)\frac{d}{dx}+(N\beta x-\alpha^{2})I.

A direct computation shows that these operators realize the defining relations (
2.1
)–(
2.3
) of the meta Racah algebra, with the parameters
\xi
and
\eta
given by (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). As already observed in
[
15
,
21
]
, the operators
Z
and
V
generate a Hahn algebra (see Section
2
). We take these operators as acting on
{\mathscr{V}}_{N}
, the space of polynomials of degree at most
N
, and consider the monomial basis
g_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}(-N)_{n}x^{n}
,
n=0,\ldots,N.
The action of the operators on this basis reads

(8.4)

\displaystyle{Z}g_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(n-\alpha)g_{n}(x)+g_{n+1}(x)\,,\qquad{Z}g_{N}(x)=(N-\alpha)g_{N}(x)\,,

(8.5)

\displaystyle{V}g_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(n-\beta-\zeta-1)(\beta+\zeta-n)g_{n}(x)+n(N+1-n)(n-1-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta+N)g_{n-1}(x)\,,

(8.6)

\displaystyle{X}g_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=-(n-{\alpha})^{2}g_{n}(x)-(n-{\beta})g_{n+1}(x)\,,\qquad Vg_{N}(x)=-(N-\alpha)^{2}g_{N}(x)\,.

Comparing with the definitions (
3.5
)-(
3.7
) of the abstract representation, we conclude that the functions
g_{n}
model the vectors
\mid{n}\,\rangle
of the standard basis.

We realize the bilinear pairing
\langle f,g\rangle
by the contour integral

(8.7)

\langle f,g\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}f(x)g(x)dx\,,

where
\Gamma
is the circle
|x|<a<1
. Notice that this is not an inner product on
{\mathscr{V}}_{N}
but this pairing identifies
({\mathscr{V}}_{N})^{*}
with the vector space generated by
\,\{\,x^{-n-1}:n=0,\ldots,N\,\}
.
Defining
g_{n}^{*}(x)=(-1)^{n}x^{-n-1}/(-N)_{n}
for
n=0,\ldots,N
one finds

\langle g_{m}^{*},g_{n}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}\,.
Thus the functions
g_{n}^{*}
realize the vectors
\langle\,{n}\,|
in this identification. We use this pairing to compute the transpose operators for
Z,\ X,\ V
and one can then check that on the basis
g_{n}^{*}
they are given by the formulas
\eqref{eq:ZT-standard}-\eqref{eq:XT-standard}
which read

(8.8)

\displaystyle Z^{\top}g_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=(n-\alpha)g_{n}^{*}(x)+g_{n-1}^{*}(x)\,,\quad Z^{\top}g_{0}^{*}(x)=(-\alpha)g_{0}^{*}(x)\,,

(8.9)

\displaystyle V^{\top}g_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=(n-\beta-\zeta-1)(\beta+\zeta-n)g_{n}^{*}(x)+(n+1)(N-n)(n-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta+N)g_{n+1}^{*}(x)\,,

(8.10)

\displaystyle X^{\top}g_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=-(n-\alpha)^{2}g_{n}^{*}(x)-(n-1-\beta)g_{n-1}^{*}(x)\,,\quad X^{\top}g_{0}^{*}(x)=-\alpha^{2}g_{0}^{*}(x)\,.

Integration by parts yields the following differential expressions for
Z^{\top}
,
V^{\top}
, and
X^{\top}

(8.11)

\displaystyle Z^{\top}

\displaystyle=-x(1-x)\frac{d}{dx}+(x(N+2)-\alpha-1)I,

(8.12)

\displaystyle V^{\top}

\displaystyle=x(1-x)\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-(2x(\beta+\zeta+2)+N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta-2)\frac{d}{dx}-(\beta+\zeta+1)(\beta+\zeta+2)I\,,

(8.13)

\displaystyle X^{\top}

\displaystyle=-x^{2}(1-x)\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+x\left(\left(N+\beta+5\right)x-2\alpha-3\right)\frac{d}{dx}+((\beta+2)(N+2)x-(\alpha+1)^{2})I.

Remark 8.1
.

Notice that these differential operators coincide with the action on the basis
g_{n}^{*}
except for the action of
V^{\top}
on
g_{N}^{*}
and the actions of
X^{\top},\ Z^{\top}
on
g_{0}^{*}
. This is solved as follows. The differential realization for
X,\ Z,\ V
can be seen to act on the space of Laurent series, and the subspace
{\mathscr{V}}_{N}
is then an irreducible subrepresentation. This implies that the transpose operators will act on a quotient of the space of Laurent series which will be isomorphic to
({\mathscr{V}}_{N})^{*}
. In other words one has to impose
g_{N+1}^{*}=g_{-1}^{*}=0
.

Given the above realization, explicit models for the bases
d,d^{*},e,e^{*},f,f^{*},z
and
z^{*}
can be constructed. These bases can be constructed either by solving the associated differential equations or by using the results of Section
4
.

We begin by observing that the operator
V
coincides with the Jacobi differential operator on the interval
(0,1)
. Consequently, in this model the basis
e
is expressed in terms of Jacobi polynomials with parameters
a=N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta-1
and
b=2\alpha-\beta-N-1
:

\displaystyle e_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-N,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{n}}{(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{n}}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}-n,n-2\beta-2\zeta-1\\
N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,x\right)

(8.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{n!(-N)_{n}}{(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{n}}J_{n}^{(N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta-1,2\alpha-\beta-N-1)}(x)\,.

Recall the definition of the Jacobi polynomials on
(0,1):

(8.17)

J_{n}^{(a,b)}(x)=\frac{(a+1)_{n}}{n!}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}-n,n+a+b+1\\
a+1\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,x\right)\,.

The bases
d
,
f
and
z
are given by

(8.18)

\displaystyle d_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}(-N)_{n}x^{n}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left({{n-N,\alpha-\beta}\atop{-\alpha+n+1}}\;\Bigg|\;x\right)\,,

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}(-N)_{n}x^{n}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}n-N,n-\beta-\rho\\
2n-2\alpha-\rho+1\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,x\right)\,,

(8.21)

\displaystyle z_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}(-N)_{n}x^{n}\left(1-x\right)^{N-n}\,.

The action of the operator
Z
on
d_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle Zd_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(n-\alpha)(-1)^{n}(-N)_{n}x^{n}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}-N+n,\alpha-\beta-1\\
-\alpha+n\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,x\right)\,.

The dual bases on the span of
\{\,x^{-n-1}:n=0,\ldots,N\,\}
are given by

(8.22)

\displaystyle d_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{-n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(-N)_{n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\frac{(\beta-\alpha+1,1+N-n)_{\ell}}{\ell!(\alpha-n)_{\ell+1}}x^{\ell}\,.

(8.23)

\displaystyle e_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{-n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(-N)_{n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-n}\frac{(n+1,N+n-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{\ell}}{\ell!(2n-2\beta-2\zeta)_{\ell}}\frac{1}{x^{\ell}}\,.

(8.24)

\displaystyle f_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{-n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(-N)_{n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\frac{(\beta+\rho+1-n,1+N-n)_{\ell}}{\ell!(1+2\alpha+\rho-2n)_{\ell}}x^{\ell}\,,

(8.25)

\displaystyle z^{*}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{-n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(-N)_{n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{l}(1+N-n)_{l}}{l!}x^{\ell}\,.

Remark 8.2
.

In the spirit of Remark
8.1
these eigenvectors coincide with the following function on the quotient space of Laurent series

\displaystyle d_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(-N)_{n}(n-\alpha)}x^{-n-1}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\beta-\alpha+1,1+N-n\\
\alpha-n+1\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,x\right)\,,

\displaystyle e_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(-N)_{n}}x^{-n-1}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}n+1,N+n-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta\\
2n-2\beta-2\zeta\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,\frac{1}{x}\right)\,,

\displaystyle f_{n}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(-N)_{n}}x^{-n-1}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\beta+\rho+1-n,1+N-n\\
1+2\alpha+\rho-2n\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,x\right)\,,

(8.32)

\displaystyle z^{*}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(-N)_{n}}x^{-n-1}(1-x)^{n-1-N}\,.

An important remark for our purpose is that if
f\equiv g
then
\langle f,h\rangle=\langle g,h\rangle
for all
h\in{\mathscr{V}}_{N}
. This will be used in the proof of the following Proposition.

The following proposition can be obtained as a consequence of the orthogonality relations of Section
4
but we recover them here using residue computations.

Proposition 10
.

The following orthogonality relations hold:

(8.33)

\displaystyle\langle f_{m}^{*},f_{n}\rangle=\langle f_{n},f_{m}^{*}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}\,,

(8.34)

\displaystyle\langle e_{m}^{*},e_{n}\rangle=\langle e_{n},e_{m}^{*}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}\,,

(8.35)

\displaystyle\langle z_{m}^{*},z_{n}\rangle=\langle z_{n},z_{m}^{*}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}\,,

(8.36)

\displaystyle\langle d_{m}^{*},Zd_{n}\rangle=\langle d_{m},Z^{\top}d_{n}^{*}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}\,.

Proof.

We begin by establishing the relation
\langle f_{m}^{*},f_{n}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}
. To this end, introduce the function

\displaystyle h_{m,n}(x)=\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}1+\rho+\beta-m,1+N-m\\
1+2\alpha+\rho-2m\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,x\right)\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}n-N,n-\beta-\rho\\
2n-2\alpha-\rho+1\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,x\right)\,.

By definition of
f
and
f^{*}
and by Cauchy theorem we have

(8.41)

\displaystyle\langle f_{m}^{*},f_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{m}^{*}\epsilon_{n}}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{h_{m,n}(x)}{x^{m-n+1}}dx=\begin{cases}1\,\,\,\hskip 54.06006ptm=n\\
(-1)^{n+m}\frac{(-N)_{n}}{(-N)_{m}}\frac{h^{(m-n)}_{m,n}(0)}{(m-n)!}\qquad m>n\\
0\hskip 73.97733ptm<n\end{cases}

It therefore remains to show that
h^{(s)}_{n+s,n}(0)=0
for all
s>0
. Using Leibniz’s rule together with the identity
(a)_{s}=(-1)^{k}(a)_{s-k}(1-a-s)_{k}

and applying the Chu–Vandermonde identity, we compute

(8.42)

\displaystyle h^{(s)}_{n+s,n}(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{s}\binom{s}{k}\frac{(1+\rho+\beta-n-s)_{s-k}(1+N-n-s)_{s-k}(n-N)_{k}(n-\beta-\rho)_{k}}{(1-2n-2s+2\alpha+\rho)_{s-k}(2n-2\alpha-\rho+1)_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\rho+\beta-n-s)_{s}(1+N-n-s)_{s}}{(1-2n-2s-2\alpha+\rho)_{s}}\sum_{k=0}^{s}\frac{(-s,2n+2\alpha-\rho+s)_{k}}{(2n-2\alpha-\rho+1)_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\rho+\beta-n-s)_{s}(1+N-n-s)_{s}}{(1-2n-2s-2\alpha+\rho)_{s}}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}-s,2n-2\alpha-\rho+s\\
2n-2\alpha-\rho+1\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\rho+\beta-n-s)_{s}(1+N-n-s)_{s}}{(1-2n-2s-2\alpha+\rho)_{s}}\frac{(1-s)_{s}}{(2n-2\alpha-\rho+1)_{s}}=0\,.

The identities
\langle z_{m}^{*},z_{n}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}
and
\langle d_{m}^{*},Zd_{n}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}
follow by an analogous argument and are therefore omitted.
The Laurent expansion around
x=0
of the product
e_{m}^{*}(x)e_{n}(x)
is

(8.45)

e_{m}^{*}(x)e_{n}(x)=\frac{(-N,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{n}(-1)^{m}}{(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{n}(-N)_{m}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}B_{m,j}\,x^{k-m-1-j}\,,

with

(8.46)

A_{n,k}=\frac{(-n)_{k}(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{k}}{(N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{k}\,k!}\,,\qquad B_{m,j}=\frac{(m+1)_{j}(N+m-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{j}}{(2m-2\beta-2\zeta)_{j}\,j!}\,.

Thus, the only terms that contribute to the integral are those satisfying
k-m-1-j=-1
,
i.e.
,
k=m+j.

If
m>n
, then
m+j>n
for all
j\geq 0
, and hence the condition

k\leq n
cannot be satisfied.
Therefore no term of order
x^{-1}
appears in the Laurent expansion, and
\langle e_{n}^{*},e_{m}\rangle=0
if
m>n
. In the case
m<n
, the condition
k=m+j\leq n
allows
0\leq j\leq n-m,
.
The coefficient of
x^{-1}
is then given by

(8.47)

\displaystyle\frac{(-N,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{n}(-1)^{m}}{(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{n}(-N)_{m}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-m}\frac{(-n)_{m+j}(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{m+j}}{(N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{m+j}(m+j)!}\,\frac{(m+1)_{j}(N+m-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{j}}{(2m-2\beta-2\zeta)_{j}\,j!}.

Using the identities
(a)_{m+j}=(a)_{n}\,(a+m)_{j},

(k+m)!=(m+1)_{k}m!
, and the Chu-Vandermonde identity,
the sum becomes

\displaystyle\frac{(-N,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{n}(-1)^{m}}{(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{n}(-N)_{m}}\frac{(-n)_{m}(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{m}}{(N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{m}m!}\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}m-n,n+m-2\beta-2\zeta-1\\
2m-2\beta-2\zeta\end{array}\!\!\Bigg|\,1\right)

(8.50)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-N,N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{n}(-1)^{m}}{(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{n}(-N)_{m}}\frac{(-n)_{m}(n-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{m}}{(N-2\alpha-\beta-2\zeta)_{m}m!}\frac{(1-n+m)_{n-m}}{(2m-2\beta-2\zeta)_{n-m}}=0\,,

and we conclude
\langle e_{n}^{*},e_{m}\rangle=0
if
m<n.

In the case
n=m
, the only term contributing to the term of order
x^{-1}
is
j=0
,
k=m
, and the coefficient of
x^{-1}
is equal to
1.
We conclude
\langle e_{m}^{*},e_{n}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}
.
∎

.

The Racah polynomials
S_{m}(n)=\langle f_{n}^{*},e_{m}\rangle
, the rational functions of Racah type

admit the following contour integral representations:

\displaystyle S_{m}(n)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2\pi i}\frac{m!(-N)_{m}}{(-N)_{n}(m-2\beta-2\zeta-1)_{m}}\oint_{\Gamma}x^{-n-1}J_{m}^{(a,b)}(x)\,\mbox{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}1+\beta+\rho-n,1+N-n\\

9.
Conclusion

The program of extending the Askey scheme to biorthogonal rational functions through the introduction of meta algebras and their representations, initiated in
[
29
]
with the Hahn case, and continued in
[
2
]
with the
q
-Hahn case, was pursued in the present paper by dealing with the Racah case. Following the general approach of
[
29
]
, the meta Racah algebra with three generators
X,V,Z

was defined abstractly, and a representation on a finite-dimensional vector space where all three generators act in a bidiagonal fashion was obtained. This bidiagonal representation allowed us to explicitly solve the relevant generalized and ordinary eigenvalue problems involving the operators

X,V,Z
or their transpositions. The representations of the meta Racah algebra in these eigenbases were characterized. The overlap coefficients between solutions of different ordinary eigenvalue problems were identified with Racah polynomials, while those involving generalized eigenvalue problems gave rise to Racah type rational functions. Their orthogonality, biorthogonality, and bispectral properties were shown to follow directly from the algebraic relations and the representation theory of the meta Racah algebra.
It was found that the operators
V
and
Z
form the Hahn algebra, and that

X
is an algebraic Heun operator associated with the Leonard pair
(V,Z).

Furthermore, the construction developed was seen to relate to the Leonard trios formalism. All this extends therefore the correspondence between Leonard pairs and the terminating families in the Askey scheme to one that includes biorthogonal rational functions.

These results open several directions for further research. A natural next step is the study of infinite dimensional representations of meta algebras, which are expected to provide an algebraic framework for infinite families of orthogonal polynomials and biorthogonal rational functions. Another promising direction concerns multivariate generalizations, where meta algebras may offer a systematic approach to the construction of multivariate bispectral rational functions. Finally, it would be particularly interesting to apply the meta algebraic approach to the Bannai–Ito scheme
[
13
,
14
,
26
,
12
,
11
,
7
,
27
]
. Given the role of reflection operators and symmetries in this setting, suitable meta extensions of the corresponding algebras could provide new algebraic interpretation of the Bannai–Ito polynomials and their rational analogues, further illuminating their position within a broader bispectral scope.

Further directions include the study of meta algebras associated with Wilson rational functions and elliptic biorthogonal rational functions; in particular, an open problem is to clarify the relation between meta algebras of Askey–Wilson type and elliptic Sklyanin algebras, given that the most general explicitly known elliptic biorthogonal rational functions are related to elliptic quadratic Sklyanin algebras, as observed by Rains and Rosengren
[
23
]
.
