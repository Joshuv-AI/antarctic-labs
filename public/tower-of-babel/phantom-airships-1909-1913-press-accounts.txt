The Phantom Airship Waves of 1909-1913: Contemporary Press Accounts (Various newspapers, 1913)
Public domain. Sourced from the Library of Congress, Chronicling America: Historic American Newspapers (https://www.loc.gov/newspapers/). Every account below is a verbatim transcription of a newspaper article published in 1909 or 1913, and therefore in the public domain in the United States. A documentary compilation of the "phantom airship" scares that preceded the First World War: the 1909 wave over Britain, American mirage and aviator explanations, and the 1913 Zeppelin incident at Luneville that shows how seriously governments took mysterious aircraft.
Hosted by the Tower of Babel library, antarctic-labs.com.

COMPILER'S NOTE. In the spring of 1909, Britain was swept by reports of a phantom airship -- a "flying Dutchman" seen over the eastern counties and the North Sea, culminating in the famous alleged landing on Caerphilly Mountain in Wales. American papers carried the story by cable while reporting their own domestic scares. By 1913, with Europe arming, every strange light was suspected to be a German dirigible; when a real Zeppelin came down at Luneville, France, the French Army seized it. The accounts are reproduced as printed, with light cleanup only.
