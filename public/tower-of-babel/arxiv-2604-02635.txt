From Liouville equation to universal quantum control: A study of generating ultra highly squeezed states
Zhu-yao Jin, J. Q. You, Jun Jing

Abstract
Within a unified framework, we reveal that the seemingly disparate control approaches for classical and quantum continuous-variable systems are interconnected via differential manifolds of the ancillary representations. For classical systems, the ancillary representation is defined by the time-dependent ancillary canonical variables resulting from a symplectic transformation over the original canonical variables. Under the conditions of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation, the ancillary canonical variables act as dynamical invariants to guide the system nonadiabatically through the entire phase space. The second quantization of the Liouville equation for the canonical variables leads to the Heisenberg equation for the relevant ancillary operators, which is found to be a sufficient condition to yield nonadiabatic passages towards arbitrary target states in both Hermitian and non-Hermitian systems and constrained exact solutions of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation. Using the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian rigorously derived from the Lindblad master equation, our theory is exemplified by the generation of single-mode squeezed states with a squeezing level of 29.3 29.3 dB and double-mode squeezed states with 20.5 20.5 dB, respectively.

In this work, we develop an operational framework from a kinematic respect to unify the control dynamics of both classical systems and (closed and open) quantum continuous-variable systems under time-dependent Hamiltonian. Our theory is fundamentally built upon the dialogue between the generating function on the differential manifold of ancillary canonical variables and the gauge potential for ancillary operators. In particular, the nonadiabatic dynamics of classical systems is formulated in terms of time-dependent canonical variables by a simplectic transformation over the original ones, which is determined by the generating function. The transformed Hamiltonian satisfies the Hamilton-Jacobi equation
3
;
46
if and only if the ancillary variables satisfy the Liouville equation for the original Hamiltonian. In this case, the ancillary variables become dynamical invariants governing the system trajectory. The Liouville equation can be mapped to the Heisenberg equation for time-dependent ancillary bosonic operators up to a gauge field on the standard second quantization
23
. The ancillary operators are related to the original bosonic operators through the same symplectic matrix as its classical counterpart, establishing a manifold structure captured by the gauge potential. In both Hermitian and non-Hermitian systems, we prove that the Heisenberg equation for the ancillary operators constitutes a sufficient condition for both constrained exact solutions to the time-dependent Schrödinger equation and system controllability. As a paradigmatic example of running our universal framework, we demonstrate how to generate both single-mode and two-mode squeezed states of much higher squeezing levels than the existing protocols, in which we employ the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian rigourously consistent with the Lindblad master equation
58
;
75

Universal framework for classical control.—
Consider a general system composed of

Main results.—
Upon the second quantization, i.e., the translation of the classical Hamiltonian to its quantum counterpart
H(\vec{a},\vec{a}^{*},t)\mapsto\hat{H}(t)
, the Poisson bracket to the commutator
\{A,B\}_{\vec{a},\vec{a}^{*}}\mapsto[A,B]
, and the ancillary canonical variables to the time-dependent ancillary bosonic operators
u_{k}(\vec{a},\vec{a}^{*},t)\mapsto\hat{\mu}_{k}(t)
and
u_{k}^{*}(\vec{a},\vec{a}^{*},t)\mapsto\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)
, the Liouville equation (
6
) can become the Heisenberg equation:

\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{k}(t)}{\partial t}=-i\left[\hat{H}(t),\mathcal{J}_{k}(t)\right],

(13)

where the dynamical invariants
\mathcal{J}_{k}(t)=\tilde{\mu}_{k}(t)
and
\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)
,
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
, are attained by a gauge transformation of the ancillary operators,

\hat{\mu}_{k}(t)\mapsto\tilde{\mu}_{k}(t)\equiv e^{if_{k}(t)}\hat{\mu}_{k}(t),\quad\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)\mapsto\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t),

(14)

with
f_{k}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}_{kk}(s)ds
the gauge-invariant global phase. Equation (
13
) constitutes a sufficient condition for demonstrating the system dynamics, under which
\hat{\mu}_{k}(0)
and
\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(0)
evolve as (see SM
D1
for details)

\hat{\mu}_{k}(0)\rightarrow e^{-if_{k}(t)}\hat{\mu}_{k}(t),\quad\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(0)\rightarrow e^{if_{k}(t)}\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t).

(15)

They also provide constrained exact solutions to the time-dependent Schrödinger equation (
7
) when the system is initially prepared by
\hat{\mu}_{k}(0)
or
\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(0)
.

Non-Hermitian case.—
The Heisenberg equation (
13
) also applies to the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian, i.e.,
\hat{H}(t)\neq\hat{H}^{\dagger}(t)

13
, the system dynamics can be described by two sets of time-dependent Schrödinger equations as

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi(t)\rangle=\hat{H}(t)|\psi(t)\rangle,\quad i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\phi(t)\rangle=\hat{H}^{\dagger}(t)|\phi(t)\rangle

(16)

with
|\psi(t)\rangle
and
\langle\phi(t)|
describe the pure-state solutions in the ket and bra spaces, respectively. When focusing on the ket space and if
\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)
satisfies Eq. (
13
), then the nonunitary dynamics of the system can be described by

\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(0)\rightarrow e^{if_{k}(t)}\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t).

(17)

In contrast to the second component of Eq. (
15
), here the global phase
f_{k}(t)
is generally a complex number, whose imaginary part indicates the probability nonconservation in open quantum systems. We can, however, always have a real
f_{k}(\tau)
at the target moment

E
for details).

Conclusion.—
We establish a unified and compact framework that is readily usable in the control of both classical and quantum continuous-variable systems under time-dependent Hamiltonian. It is rooted in the differential manifolds of the time-dependent ancillary canonical variables or operators. Our universal quantum control appears as a natural consequence of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation. For classical systems, the time-dependent ancillary variables, which is obtained by the generating function for canonical transformation, serve as dynamical invariants for controlling the expansion and transport of the system trajectories in the phase space. As the dynamical invariants for quantum systems, the time-dependent ancillary operators are obtained by the same simplectic transformation as in classical systems on the original operators. Then the unitary transformation from the time-dependent ancillary operators to the stationary ones induces a general gauge potential that encodes the information of differential structure. The second quantization offers deeper insight by mapping the Liouville equation to the Heisenberg equation, which is proved to be a sufficient condition to for the time-dependent ancillary operators obtain both nonadiabatic passage toward the target modes and constrained solutions to the time-dependent Schrödinger equation. As a nontrivial application, our theory is used to generate ultra high-level single-mode and two-mode squeezed states through nonunitary preparation.

\hat{H}_{I}(t)=ig(t)\sum_{N=k}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{N-k}\mathcal{N}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\left[|n+j,N-n-k\rangle\langle n,N-n|-|n,N-n\rangle\langle n+j,N-n-k|\right],

(D30)

where the coefficients
\mathcal{N}_{n}\equiv\sqrt{(n+j)!/n!}
and
\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\equiv\sqrt{(N-n)!/(N-n-k)!}
, and
|n,m\rangle=|n\rangle_{1}\otimes|m\rangle_{2}
with
|n\rangle_{1}
and
|m\rangle_{2}
denoting the Fock states of modes
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
, respectively.

The dynamical invariant of this system can be assumed in the form

\hat{\mu}(t)=\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\cos\theta_{n,m}(t)|n-j,m\rangle\langle n,m|-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{n,m}(t)|n,m-k\rangle\langle n,m|

(D31)

with the time-dependent parameters
\theta_{n,m}(t)
and
\tilde{\theta}_{n,m}(t)
. Substitute the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{I}(t)
in Eq. (
D29
) or (
D30
) and
\hat{\mu}(t)
in Eq. (
D31
) into the Heisenberg equation (
13
) or (
D18
), one can obtain the constraints

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{\theta}}_{n+j,N-n}\cos\tilde{\theta}_{n+j,N-n}=-g(t)\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\left(\mathcal{N}_{n}\cos\theta_{n+j,N-n}-\mathcal{N}_{n+j}\cos\theta_{n+2j,N-n-k}\right),\quad N\geq k,\quad 0\leq n\leq N-k,

(D32)

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{\theta}}_{n,N-n}\cos\tilde{\theta}_{n,N-n}=g(t)\mathcal{N}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\cos\theta_{n+j,N-n-k},\quad N\geq n+k,\quad 0\leq n\leq j-1,

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{n+j,N-n}\sin\theta_{n+j,N-n}=-g(t)\mathcal{N}_{n}\left(\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{n+j,N-n}-\mathcal{N}_{N+k,n}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{n,N-n+k}\right),\quad N\geq k,\quad 0\leq n\leq N-k,

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{n+j,N-n-k}\sin\theta_{n+j,N-n-k}=g(t)\mathcal{N}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{n,N-n},\quad N\geq n+k,\quad n\geq 0,

with
\mathcal{N}_{n}\cos\theta_{n+2j,N-n-k}=\mathcal{N}_{n+j}\cos\theta_{n+j,N-n}
and
\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{n,N-n+k}=\mathcal{N}_{N+k,n}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{n+j,N-n}
. In other words, for the system dynamics governed by
\hat{H}_{I}(t)
in Eq. (
D29
) or (

D31

D32
).

As an example, one can consider the three-photon parametric down-conversion governed by the Hamiltonian (
D30

and
m=2

(D33)

\mathcal{N}_{n}=\sqrt{(n+1)!/n!}
and
\mathcal{N}_{N,n}=\sqrt{(N-n)!/(N-n-2)!}

D31

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{\theta}}_{n+1,N-n}\cos\tilde{\theta}_{n+1,N-n}=-g(t)\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\left(\mathcal{N}_{n}\cos\theta_{n+1,N-n}-\mathcal{N}_{n+1}\cos\theta_{n+2,N-n-2}\right),\quad N\geq 2,\quad 0\leq n\leq N-2,

(D35)

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{\theta}}_{n,N-n}\cos\tilde{\theta}_{n,N-n}=g(t)\mathcal{N}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\cos\theta_{n+1,N-n-2},\quad N\geq n+2,\quad n=0,

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{n+1,N-n}\sin\theta_{n+1,N-n}=-g(t)\mathcal{N}_{n}\left(\mathcal{N}_{N,n}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{n+1,N-n}-\mathcal{N}_{N+2,n}\sin\tilde{\theta}_{n,N-n+2}\right),\quad N\geq 2,\quad 0\leq n\leq N-2,

E
Universal quantum control with time-dependent non-Hermitian Hamiltonian

This section demonstrates that when only
\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)
but not
\tilde{\mu}_{k}(t)
in Eq. (
14

13
) with the time-dependent non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t)\neq\hat{H}^{\dagger}(t)
, it suffices to demonstrate the system dynamics in the ket space described by Eq. (

).

Under the biorthogonal assumption
13
, the dynamics of non-Hermitian systems can be described by two sets of time-dependent Schrödinger equations as

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi(t)\rangle=\hat{H}(t)|\psi(t)\rangle,\quad i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\phi(t)\rangle=\hat{H}^{\dagger}(t)|\phi(t)\rangle

(E1)

with the pure-state solutions
|\psi(t)\rangle
and
\langle\phi(t)|
in the ket and bra spaces, respectively. The time-dependent non-Hermitian quadratic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t)
can be written in the general form,

\hat{H}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\vec{a}^{\dagger}&\vec{a}\end{pmatrix}H^{a}(t)\begin{pmatrix}\vec{a}^{T}\\
(\vec{a}^{\dagger})^{T}\end{pmatrix},

(E2)

where the
2N\times 2N
coefficient matrix
H^{a}(t)
is not Hermitian, i.e.,
H^{a}(t)\neq[H^{a}(t)]^{\dagger}
. The non-Hermitian Hamiltonian can reduce to the pseudo-Hermitian Hamiltonian when the spectrum is real, or when
|\psi(t)\rangle
and
|\phi(t)\rangle
share the same complex eigenvalues
62
.

By Eq. (
9
), the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian (
E2
) can be expressed in the time-dependent ancillary representation as

\displaystyle\hat{H}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}^{\dagger}&\vec{\mu}\end{pmatrix}(\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}(t))^{-1}H^{a}(t)\mathcal{M}^{-1}(t)\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}^{T}\\
(\vec{\mu}^{\dagger})^{T}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}^{\dagger}&\vec{\mu}\end{pmatrix}H^{\mu}(t)\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}^{T}\\
(\vec{\mu}^{\dagger})^{T}\end{pmatrix},

(E3)

with the
2N\times 2N
rotated coefficient matrix
H^{\mu}(t)
. Also, in the rotating frame with respect to
\mathcal{V}(t)
defined in Eq. (
11
), the transformed Hamiltonian can be expressed

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm rot}(t)=\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{H}(t)\mathcal{V}(t)-i\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}(t)\frac{\partial\mathcal{V}(t)}{\partial t}

(E4)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}_{0}^{\dagger}&\vec{\mu}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\left[H^{\mu}(t)-\mathcal{A}(t)\right]\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}_{0}^{T}\\
(\vec{\mu}_{0}^{\dagger})^{T}\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}_{0}^{\dagger}&\vec{\mu}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{H}(t)\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}_{0}^{T}\\
(\vec{\mu}_{0}^{\dagger})^{T}\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathcal{H}(t)
is a
2N\times 2N
coefficient matrix in the representation of stationary ancillary operators. Without loss of generality, we consider the bosonic modes coupled by the squeezing interaction. Thus the Hamiltonian in Eqs. (
E3
) and (
E4
) take the same form as those in Eqs. (
D1
) and (
D11
) for the Hermitian case. However, here the coefficient matrices are non-Hermitian, i.e.,
H^{\mu}(t)\neq[H^{\mu}(t)]^{\dagger}
and
\mathcal{H}(t)\neq\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}(t)
. Consequently, the ancillary operator and its Hermitian conjugate cannot satisfy the Heisenberg equation (
13
) at the same time.

In the following, we prove that the Heisenberg equation (
13
) for
\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)
with
\hat{H}(t)
(
E2

N+k
th row and
k
th column of the coefficient matrix
\mathcal{H}(t)
in Eq. (
D12

.

Necessary condition.—
If all the off-diagonal elements of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm rot}(t)
in Eq. (
E4
) in the
N+k
th row and

th column are zero, then we have

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm rot}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\dot{f}_{j}(t)\hat{\mu}_{j}^{\dagger}(0)\hat{\mu}_{j}(0)+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\sum_{m\geq j+1}^{N}\mathcal{H}_{j,N+m}(t)\hat{\mu}_{j}^{\dagger}(0)\hat{\mu}_{m}^{\dagger}(0)

(E5)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\neq k,j=1}^{N-1}\sum_{m\neq k,m\geq j+1}^{N}\mathcal{H}_{N+m,j}(t)\hat{\mu}_{m}(0)\hat{\mu}_{j}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}_{0}^{\dagger}&\vec{\mu}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{H}(t)\begin{pmatrix}\vec{\mu}_{0}^{T}\\
(\vec{\mu}_{0}^{\dagger})^{T}\end{pmatrix},

where the coefficient matrix
\mathcal{H}(t)
can still be divided into four
N\times N

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}(t)&=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc|ccccccc}\dot{f}_{1}&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&0&0&\mathcal{H}_{1,N+2}&\cdots&\mathcal{H}_{1,N+3}&\cdots&\mathcal{H}_{1,2N-1}&\mathcal{H}_{1,2N}\\
0&\dot{f}_{2}&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&0&0&0&\cdots&\mathcal{H}_{2,N+3}&\cdots&\mathcal{H}_{2,2N-1}&\mathcal{H}_{2,2N}\\

The commutation relation between

Sufficient condition.—
Consider the Heisenberg equation (

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(t)\frac{\partial\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(0)}{\partial t}\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}(t)

F
Derivation of non-Hermitian squeezing Hamiltonian

This section presents detailed derivations of the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian for squeezing in the single-mode and double-mode systems in Eqs. (
19
) and (
26
), respectively, from the Lindblad master equation. The quantum jump terms are fully retained throughout the derivations.

F1
Single mode case

The dynamics of an open single-mode bosonic system under a two-photon driving field can be described by the master equation as
17

\frac{d}{dt}\rho=-i[\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t),\rho]+\gamma\mathcal{L}[\hat{a}_{1}]\rho,

(F1)

where the coherent Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t)=\omega\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{1}+[\Omega(t)\exp{(i\varphi)}(\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger})^{2}+{\rm H.c.}]
provides the eigenenergy of the bosonic mode and the driving intensity of the driving field. The Lindblad superoperator
\mathcal{L}[\hat{a}_{1}]\rho\equiv\hat{a}_{1}\rho\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}-\{\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{1},\rho\}/2
describes the system dissipation with a damping rate
\gamma
.

Under Eq. (
F1
), the Schrödinger-picture operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
is connected to the Heisenberg-picture operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(t)
by

{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\dot{\rho}(t)\right]={\rm Tr}\left[\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\left(-i[\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t),\rho]+\gamma\mathcal{L}[\hat{a}_{1}]\rho\right)\right]={\rm Tr}\left[\left(i[\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t),\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}]+\gamma\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1}]\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\right)\rho\right]={\rm Tr}\left[\dot{\hat{\mathcal{O}}}_{H}(t)\rho(0)\right],

(F2)

with the Hermitian conjugate superoperator
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1}]\mathcal{O}_{S}\equiv\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathcal{O}_{S}\hat{a}_{1}-\{\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{S}\}/2
. The second equivalence of Eq. (
F2
) has used the cyclic property of the density trace. Accordingly, the dynamics of
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(t)
can be described by the adjoint Lindblad master equation as

\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(t)=i[\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t),\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}]+\gamma\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1}]\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}.

(F3)

Using Eq. (
F3
), the dynamics of the modes
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{a}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\omega\hat{a}_{1}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\hat{a}_{1}-i2\Omega(t)e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger},

(F4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}(t)

\displaystyle=i\omega\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}+i2\Omega(t)e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}_{1},

which is equivalent to that governed by the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian in Eq. (
19
).

F2
Two modes case

Consider an open two-mode bosonic system coupled to two individual environments, the system dynamics can be described by the master equation as
17

\frac{d}{dt}\rho=-i[\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t),\rho]+\gamma_{1}\mathcal{L}[\hat{a}_{1}]\rho+\gamma_{2}\mathcal{L}[\hat{a}_{2}]\rho,

(F5)

where the coherent Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t)=\omega_{1}\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{1}+\omega_{2}\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{2}+[g(t)\exp(i\varphi)\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}+{\rm H.c.}]
represents the eigenenergies of the two modes and the squeezing interaction between them. The Lindblad superoperators are defined as
\mathcal{L}[\hat{o}]\rho=\hat{o}\rho\hat{o}^{\dagger}-\{\hat{o}^{\dagger}\hat{o},\rho\}/2
,
\hat{o}=\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}
. They are associated with the local damping rates
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
.

Similar to Eq. (
F2
), the Schrödinger-picture operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
governed by Eq. (
F5
) is connected to the Heisenberg-picture operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(t)
by

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Tr}\left[\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\dot{\rho}(t)\right]={\rm Tr}\left[\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\left(-i[\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t),\rho]+\gamma_{1}\mathcal{L}[\hat{a}_{1}]\rho+\gamma_{2}\mathcal{L}[\hat{a}_{2}]\rho\right)\right]

(F6)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left[\left(i[\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t),\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}]+\gamma_{1}\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1}]\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}+\gamma_{2}\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2}]\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\right)\rho\right]={\rm Tr}\left[\dot{\hat{\mathcal{O}}}_{H}(t)\rho(0)\right].

Accordingly, the time derivative of
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(t)
can be expressed by the adjoint Lindblad master equation as

\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(t)=i[\hat{H}_{\rm coh}(t),\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}]+\gamma_{1}\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1}]\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}+\gamma_{2}\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2}]\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}.

(F7)

Using Eq. (
F7
), the dynamics about the modes
\hat{a}_{1}
and
\hat{a}_{2}
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{a}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\omega_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\hat{a}_{1}-ige^{i\varphi}\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger},

(F8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{a}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\omega_{2}\hat{a}_{2}-\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\hat{a}_{2}-ige^{i\varphi}\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}.

They are the same as the Heisenberg equations governed by the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian in Eq. (
26
).

Full symplectic matrix for two-modes system and a brief recipe for general ancillary operators

This section first introduces the full symplectic matrix used to construct the ancillary operators for two-mode bosonic systems with squeezing interactions, and then provides a brief recipe for constructing the ancillary operators in a general
N
-mode bosonic system with squeezing interactions.

For the two-mode bosonic system governed by the squeezing Hamiltonian (
26
), we construct the time-dependent ancillary operators by a
4\times 4
symplectic matrix to follow Eq. (
9
):

\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mu}_{1}(t)\\
\hat{\mu}_{2}(t)\\
\hat{\mu}_{1}^{\dagger}(t)\\
\hat{\mu}_{2}^{\dagger}(t)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\theta(t)&0&0&-\sinh\theta(t)e^{-i\alpha(t)}\\
0&\cosh\theta(t)&-\sinh\theta(t)e^{-i\alpha(t)}&0\\
0&-\sinh\theta(t)e^{i\alpha(t)}&\cosh\theta(t)&0\\
-\sinh\theta(t)e^{i\alpha(t)}&0&0&\cosh\theta(t)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{a}_{1}\\
\hat{a}_{2}\\
\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\\
\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}.

(G1)

Due to the symmetric structure of the symplectic matrix, Eq. (
G1
) can be reduced to
[\hat{\mu}_{1}(t),\hat{\mu}_{2}^{\dagger}(t)]^{T}=\mathcal{M}(t)(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger})^{T}
with
\mathcal{M}(t)
given in Eq. (
20
) as employed in the main text.

In the following, we outline a brief recipe for constructing the ancillary operators for
N
-mode bosonic systems with squeezing interactions described by the Hamiltonian (
D1
). The construction order is suggested as (1)
\{\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\}\rightarrow\{\hat{\mu}_{1}(t),\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger}(t)\}
,
\{\hat{b}_{1}(t),\hat{a}_{3}^{\dagger}\}\rightarrow\{\hat{\mu}_{2}(t),\hat{b}_{2}^{\dagger}(t)\}
,
\{\hat{b}_{2}(t),\hat{a}_{4}^{\dagger}\}\rightarrow\{\hat{\mu}_{3}(t),\hat{b}_{3}^{\dagger}(t)\}
,
\cdots
,
\{\hat{b}_{N-3}(t),\hat{a}_{N-1}^{\dagger}\}\rightarrow\{\hat{\mu}_{N-2}(t),\hat{b}_{N-2}^{\dagger}(t)\}
and (2)
\{\hat{b}_{N-2}(t),\hat{a}_{N}^{\dagger}\}\rightarrow\{\hat{\mu}_{N-1}(t),\hat{\mu}_{N}^{\dagger}(t)\}
. It is straightforward to verify that the time-dependent ancillary and bright operators satisfy the canonical commutation relations as
[\hat{\mu}_{j}(t),\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)]=\delta_{jk}
and
[\hat{b}_{j}(t),\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)]=\delta_{jk}
, while they commute as
[\hat{\mu}_{j}(t),\hat{b}_{k}(t)]=0
.

Using a SU(1,1)-like rotating matrix, the ancillary and bright operators in Step (1) can be formulated as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{\mu}_{k}(t)\\
\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cosh\theta_{k}(t)&-\sinh\theta_{k}(t)e^{-i\alpha_{k}(t)}\\
-\sinh\theta_{k}(t)e^{i\alpha_{k}(t)}&\cosh\theta_{k}(t)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{b}_{k-1}(t)\\
\hat{a}_{k+1}^{\dagger}\end{array}\right),

(G2)

where
k=1,2,\cdots,N-2
and
\hat{b}_{0}(t)\equiv\hat{a}_{1}
. Step (2) can be formulated as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{\mu}_{N-1}(t)\\
\hat{\mu}_{N}^{\dagger}(t)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cosh\theta_{N-1}(t)&-\sinh\theta_{N-1}(t)e^{-i\alpha_{N-1}(t)}\\
-\sinh\theta_{N-1}(t)e^{i\alpha_{N-1}(t)}&\cosh\theta_{N-1}(t)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{b}_{N-2}(t)\\
\hat{a}_{N}^{\dagger}\end{array}\right).

(G3)

Here the time-dependent parameters
\theta_{k}(t)
and
\alpha_{k}(t)
, with
k=1,2,\cdots,N-1
, are relative to the squeezing strength and phase, respectively. The ancillary operators defined in Eqs. (
G2
) and (
G3

\vec{\mu}_{t}^{T}=\mathcal{M}(t)\vec{a}^{T},\quad\vec{\mu}_{t}\equiv\left[\hat{\mu}_{1}(t),\hat{\mu}_{2}(t),\cdots,\hat{\mu}_{N-1}(t),\hat{\mu}_{N}^{\dagger}(t)\right],\quad\vec{a}\equiv\left[\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger},\hat{a}_{3}^{\dagger},\cdots,\hat{a}_{N}^{\dagger}\right],

(G4)

where the
N\times N
unitary transformation matrix
\mathcal{M}(t)
admits a general representation of the form

\mathcal{M}(t)=\left[\vec{M}_{1}(t),\vec{M}_{2}(t),\cdots,\vec{M}_{N}(t)\right]^{T}

(G5)

with the row vectors of
N
dimensionality

\displaystyle\vec{M}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\cosh\theta_{1}(t),-\sinh\theta_{1}(t)e^{-i\alpha_{1}(t)},0,\cdots,0\right),

(G6)

\displaystyle\vec{M}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\cosh\theta_{k}(t)\vec{b}_{k-1}(t),-\sinh\theta_{k}(t)e^{-i\alpha_{k}(t)},0,\cdots,0\right],

\displaystyle\vec{M}_{N-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\cosh\theta_{N-1}(t)\vec{b}_{N-2}(t),-\sinh\theta_{N-1}(t)e^{-i\alpha_{N-1}(t)}\right],

\displaystyle\vec{M}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=\vec{b}_{N-1}(t),

where
k
runs from
2

N-2

is a row vector of
k+1

\vec{b}_{k}(t)\equiv\left[-\sinh\theta_{k}(t)e^{i\alpha_{k}(t)}\vec{b}_{k-1}(t),\cosh\theta_{k}(t)\right]

(G7)

where
1\leq k\leq N-1
and
\vec{b}_{0}(t)\equiv 1
. Using Eq. (
G7

G2

and
\hat{b}_{N-1}^{\dagger}(t)\equiv\hat{\mu}_{N}(t)
, e.g.,
\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger}(t)=-\sinh\theta_{1}(t)e^{i\alpha_{1}(t)}\hat{a}_{1}+\cosh\theta_{1}(t)\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}
