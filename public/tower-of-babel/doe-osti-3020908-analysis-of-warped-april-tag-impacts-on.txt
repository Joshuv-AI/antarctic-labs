 ABSTRACT

This report evaluates the impact of geometric deformation on an April Tag, particularly when warped due
to attachment on a curved surface, on its detectability and pose estimation performance. A comparative
analysis was conducted using a flat April Tag as a control under identical experimental conditions, which
involved recording video sequences with varying viewing angles. For detectability, the warped tag
exhibited consistent detection failures at viewing angles beyond 40° and complete failures beyond 60°,
whereas the flat tag maintained reliable detection across all angles. For pose estimation, measured by pose
jitter (variation in rotation and translation), differences between the warped and flat tags were minimal
and statistically insignificant, indicating robust performance even for the deformed tag. These findings
suggest that while geometric warping reduces an April Tag’s detectability, its pose estimation accuracy
remains relatively unaffected under the tested conditions.

 1. Introduction

April Tag [1], a type of visual fiducial marker, are widely used in robotics, augmented reality, and
computer vision applications for their high reliability in detection and pose estimation. They work by
encoding unique binary patterns that can be identified and localized to determine the marker's position
and orientation relative to the camera. These capabilities make April Tags essential for tasks such as robot
navigation, object tracking, and interactive augmented reality.

The robustness of April Tags strongly depends on their physical and visual integrity. Our interest in this
report is to evaluate how geometric deformations, such as warping when the tag is attached to curved
surfaces, affect its detectability and pose estimation performance. Warping alters the geometric structure
of the tag, potentially distorting its characteristic features and causing inaccuracies in detection and
position estimation. Understanding the extent of these effects is critical for deploying April Tags in real-
world applications where perfectly flat surfaces may not always be available.

This report investigates the impact of physical deformation on the detectability and pose estimation
stability of an April Tag. The study compares a warped April Tag—created by attaching it to a curved
speaker surface—with a flat April Tag mounted on a planar surface. A controlled experiment was
conducted to assess the detectability of the tags across varying viewing angles, as well as to analyze the
pose estimation stability using pose jitter as a metric. The findings shed light on the limitations of April
Tag performance under physical deformation and provide guidance for their application in challenging
real-world scenarios.

 2. Experiment

2.1 Experiment Setup

The experiment was designed to compare the performance of a warped April Tag and a flat April Tag
under identical conditions. Two key setups were used:

  Warped April Tag: The tag was physically deformed by attaching it to the curved surface of a
 speaker. This introduced geometric distortion due to the curved surface.

  Flat April Tag: The tag was mounted on the flat surface of a monitor placed adjacent to the
 speaker.

The tags were observed under two camera movement sequences:

  Sequence 1: The camera moved from one side of both tags to the front and then to the opposite
 side, simulating a dynamic change in the viewing angle. Figures 1(a) and 1(b) shows the starting
 and central frames of this sequence.

  Sequence 2: The camera moved laterally from right to left, maintaining a consistent viewing
 angle. Figures 1(c) and 1(d) show the beginning and ending frames of this sequence.

This experimental setup allowed for a systematic comparison of the detectability and pose estimation
performance of the two tags under varying visual and geometric conditions.

2.2 Detectability Experiment

To test detectability, the number of frames where each tag was successfully detected and identified by the
detection algorithm provided by April Robotics was recorded. Detection success rate was monitored as
the viewing angle changed throughout both sequences, focusing particularly on how detection failures
correlated with viewing angles. The viewing angle of an AprilTag was estimated based on the tag's pose
relative to the camera. However, when a detection failure occurred, it was impossible to directly
determine the viewing angle of the undetected tag.

To overcome this issue, the relative pose between two AprilTags was determined by averaging their
relative poses during frames where both tags were successfully detected. Leveraging this relative pose,
when a tag failed to be detected, its pose was estimated using the pose of the other tag combined with the
relative pose between the two tags. This approach, however, could not be applied if both tags were
undetected simultaneously. Nevertheless, during our experiments, at least one tag was detected in every
frame.

 2

Finally, for each range of viewing angles, the detection success rate was calculated as the ratio of
successful detections to the total number of frames within that range.

2.3 Pose Estimation Experiment

Pose estimation is performed by detecting the tag corners and applying a Perspective-n-Point (PnP)
algorithm [2] to compute the tag’s position and orientation relative to the camera. Figure 2 shows an
example of an estimated pose of the two tags with respect to the camera.
The stability of the estimated pose was evaluated for both tags using the pose jitter metric. Pose jitter was
defined as the deviation of the estimated pose in terms of rotation (degrees) and translation (millimeters)
relative to a smoothed pose. Pose jitter arises due to two primary factors:
 1. Image Corner Detection Error: Inherent inaccuracies in detecting the tag’s corners in the image
 space, which can lead to small deviations in the estimated pose. These errors occur regardless of
 tag surface shape but are amplified by distortions.

 2. Warped Surface Geometry: For the warped tag, the curvature introduces additional 3D
 distortion of the tag’s corner positions, which can further contribute to pose estimation
 uncertainty.

To quantify pose jitter for a video sequence, the estimated pose for each frame was first smoothed using a
smoothing algorithm – Savitzky-Golay filter [3] [4] – to remove high-frequency noise. The jitter was then
quantified by calculating the deviation of the estimated pose from the smoothed pose, calculated
separately for rotation (in degrees) and translation (in meters). This deviation serves as a measure of how
stable or noisy the estimated pose is for each tag. The jitter was measured separately for both sequences to
account for the differences in motion patterns and viewing angles.

 3. Result

3.1 Detectability Result

The results of the detectability experiment are summarized as follows:

  Flat Tag: The flat April Tag was detected reliably across all frames in Sequence 1, even at steep
 viewing angles up to 80°. No detection failures were observed during the test. Figure 3 shows the
 histogram of successful detections (in orange) relative to the viewing angle, confirming consistent
 performance for the flat tag.

  Warped Tag: The warped April Tag exhibited significant detectability issues. Detection failures
 began to occur at viewing angles exceeding 40°. At angles beyond 60°, the tag consistently failed
 to be detected. Figure 3 displays detection failures for the warped tag (in blue), with increasing
 instances of failure as the viewing angle increases.

These findings indicate that geometric deformation disrupts the visual integrity of the warped tag,
reducing its detectability, especially at steep viewing angles. In contrast, the flat tag maintained consistent
performance throughout.

 4. Conclusion

This study explored the effects of geometric warping on the detectability and pose estimation
performance of an April Tag. The results highlight the following key findings:

 1. Detectability Impact: The warped tag experienced significant detectability issues, failing to be
 identified at viewing angles beyond 40° and consistently failing beyond 60°. The flat tag
 demonstrated robust detectability across all viewing angles.

 2. Pose Estimation Resilience: Despite its reduced detectability, the warped tag exhibited
 comparable pose estimation performance to that of the flat tag. Differences in pose jitter for both
 rotation and translation were minimal and unlikely to impact practical applications.

These findings suggest that while geometric deformation significantly hampers tag detection reliability, it
has limited effect on pose estimation accuracy. This indicates that April Tags retain their effectiveness for
pose estimation under minor geometric distortions, provided they are detectable within the given
application's constraints.
