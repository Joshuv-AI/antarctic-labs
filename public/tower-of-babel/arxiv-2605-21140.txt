Optimization of Secret Key Rate for BB84 under Collective Rotation Noise
Wajiha Masood, Dr. Afshan Irshad

Abstract
Practical quantum key distribution (QKD) systems operate under noise, but security of most protocols have been analyzed under ideal noiseless scenarios. In this work, we investigated security performance of BB84 protocol under effect of collective rotation noise. Using theoretical quantum information frameworks, we analyzed key security parameters including quantum bit error rate (QBER), mutual information and secret key rate (SKR). Security of protocol is studied under various eavesdropping scenarios based on intercept and resend attacks. Our results show that collective rotation noise has a significant impact on the information shared between the two parties. Particularly, we extended prior treatments by suggesting a noise engineering strategy where we identified a non-zero noise range where information accessed by Eve is minimized while corresponding SKR degradation remains relatively small. This analysis provide insights into robustness of BB84 protocol under realistic noisy channels and may contribute towards development of more resilient QKD systems.



I
INTRODUCTION

Development of quantum technologies pose an existential threat to classical cryptography. Quantum communication has emerged as a promising alternative by leveraging quantum mechanical principles. Among quantum communication protocols, QKD enables two parties, commonly referred to as Alice and Bob to establish a secure cryptographic key. BB84 is most widely studied protocol and was proposed by Bennett and Brassard in 1984
[
2
]
.

Although BB84 is provably secure under ideal conditions, practical quantum communication operate in noisy environments that affect transmitted quantum states[
[
11
]
. Environmental imperfections may arise from imperfect photon sources
[
3
]
, detector limitations
[
8
]
, information leakage
[
10
]
, and decoherence
[
13
]
. Such effects lead to increase in QBER and may provide an opportunity for Eve to extract information during communication process. In particular, collective noise channels are crucial as they model physical processes where all qubits experience identical disturbance during propagation. Collective rotation noise is highly relevant to optical fiber communication as well as polarization-based quantum systems.

Despite extensive studies, impact of collective rotational noise on secret key rate(SKR) of QKD protocols has not been studied yet.
In this paper, we investigate the impact of collective rotation noise on security parameters of BB84 protocol. We analyze the behavior of QBER, mutual information, and SKR under intercept and resend attack in presence of collective rotation noise. Furthermore, we examine if specific noise regime can reduce Eve’s information while preserving an acceptable key rate. The results provide an insight into interplay between noise and security in realistic QKD channels.

II
Preliminaries

II-A

BB84 Protocol

BB84 is a type of QKD protocol that belongs to prepare and measure (P&M) category. Like every other QKD protocol, BB84 leverages non-cloning theorem and measurement property of quantum mechanics to establish secure key. It combines one-time pad encryption method with quantum key distribution method
[
2
]
. It employs four quantum states encoded in two Pauli eigenbases, i.e., Z basis (
\ket{0}
,
\ket{1}
) and X basis (
\ket{+}
,
\ket{-}
).

BB84 is structured such that Alice selects a random bit sequence consisting of 0’s and 1’s and prepares a quantum state in any Pauli bases. She sends this state to Bob through an authenticated quantum channel. Bob measures the received bit by choosing a random basis. Alice and Bob perform basis reconciliation. Only those bits are retained for which bases coincide.

To monitor Eve, Alice and Bob perform parameter estimation by publicly disclosing a subset of their sifted bits and bases, which are immediately discarded after QBER estimation. If QBER is within tolerable range i.e 11%, protocol is continued, otherwise it is aborted. Alice and Bob might modify the bits of their shared key to enhance the distribution process
[
5
]
. Traditional error correction techniques, like Cascade
[
4
]
or Low-Density Parity-Check codes
[
6
]
, are used to minimize differences in Alice and Bob keys. To reduce the information leakage to Eve, privacy amplification i.e. another hash function
[
1
]
is applied to reduce bit string size for key generation.

Let’s assume Alice prepared quantum states from sequence
=[1,0,0,1,1,1,0]
using a random bases so that the final states sent to Bob are
S=[+,0,0,-,1,1,+]
over quantum channel. Bob randomly chooses the bases to measure bits that Alice sent and obtains results
S=[1,0,0,1,1,1,1]
. In sifting phase, Alice shares her bases, and Bob discards his unmatched bases, so resulting key becomes [1,0,1,1]. Ideally Alice and Bob should have same keys but eavesdropping or noise changes their keys that is modified using error correction code and privacy amplification.

II-B

Collective rotation noise

Noise impacts qubits in such a way that fluctuations in quantum channel either by environmental conditions or by imperfect calibrations leads to distortion of qubits. Collective noise includes all factors that affects all the qubits equally or in a same manner
[
7
]
. As this noise has collective effect on all qubits, these are globally rotated by a same angle on Bloch Sphere
[
7
]
. Such noise arises in optical channels like fiber optic cable or a special environmental condition where qubits are under effect of some perturbation. The state of qubit
\ket{\Psi^{\prime}}
under rotation noise is transformed as:

\ket{\Psi^{\prime}}=R(\theta)\ket{\Psi},

(1)

where R(
\theta
) is rotation angle for angle
\theta
and
\ket{\Psi}
is the initial qubit state, and
\ket{\Psi^{\prime}}
is the state after rotation. For collective rotation noise, unitary
transformation is written as:

U(\theta)=\cos(\theta)I+i\sin(\theta)\sigma_{z}.

(2)

Here I is identity matrix and
\sigma_{z}
is Pauli matrix, which can also be represented as:

U=\left(\begin{matrix}cos\theta&sin\theta\\
-sin\theta&cos\theta\\
\end{matrix}\right).

(3)

It evolves from density matrix
\rho^{\prime}
written in compact form using ensemble decomposition as:

\rho^{\prime}=U(\theta)\rho U^{\dagger}(\theta).

(4)

Hence, mixed states are written in terms of density matrices:

\rho=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\ket{\Psi_{i}}\bra{\Psi_{i}},

(5)

here
\rho_{i}
are classical mixing probabilities satisfying
\sum_{i}\rho_{i}=1
, and
\ket{\Psi_{i}}
are the pure states comprising the mixture.
The diagonal entries in such matrix represent probability of attaining original state while the off-diagonal entries contribute to bit flip.

V
CONCLUSIONS

In this work, we analyzed security parameters of the BB84 protocol in the presence of collective rotation noise, and observed the impact of noise on these parameters particularly key rate under three scenarios: (i) collective rotation noise present in the channel with no eavesdropper, (ii) an intercept-and-resend eavesdropper active across the entire Alice to Bob channel also subject to noise, and (iii) an eavesdropper whose devices are placed near Alice’s laboratory, so that only the Eve to Bob segment experiences channel noise. By deriving expressions for QBER, mutual information and secret key rate as functions of rotation angle
(\theta)
, we found non-trivial minimum in Eve’s mutual information. At
\theta\approx 0.13
radians Eve extract
20\%
less information than in zero noise or maximum noise cases, while key rate remains meaningfully positive.

This finding motivates a noise engineering approach to QKD systems that is integration of an optimal value of noise in experimental setup of a QKD protocol for enhanced security. Future work will extend this security analysis to other eavesdropping strategies, finite key effects as well as experimental validation in free space and fiber based QKD testbeds.
