GENERAL

Yugoslav-Cuban Relations: Belgrade's conclusion on 30
July of its first permanent trade agreement with Cuba is a
further indication of continuing Yugoslav support for the
Castro regime. Yugoslavia will export industrial goods and
will .receive sugar, leather, agricultural products, and
minerals of unspecified amounts. A scientific and technical
cooperation agreement was also concluded.

Yugoslavia has consistently supported Castro,but it has
sought to prevent this support from affecting its relations
with the United States. On 15 July, Foreign Ministry spokes-
man Kunc implied a censure of US policy toward Cuba, but Yugo-
Slav officials subsequently sought to soften their position
in private talks with American diplomats. In a move to im-
prove the milieu for talks with US Under Secretary of State
Dillon, conducted in Belgrade from 17 to 20 July, Kunc an-
nounced the postponement of the Cuban trade delegation's
visit, scheduled to begin on 13 July. The delegation arrived
two weeks later.

Belgrade's concern over its relations with the US is based
on its need for continued American assistance--particularly
financial aid in carrying out a sizable economic and currency
reform. Yugoslav papers were quick to acclaim both the trans-
fer of the Cuban charges against the US from the forum of the
United Nations to the Organization of American States and Am-
bassador Lodge's statement in the UN that the US harbors no

aggressive intentions toward Cuba.
