As a generalization of biderivation,

This notion also generalizes the notion of

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the Lie algebras we study in this paper,
the notions of
\delta
-derivation and
\delta
-biderivation,
and some basic results.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Witt algebra
is a complex Lie algebra
\mathcal{W}
with a basis

\left\{L_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

subjecting to the commutation relation

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}\quad\text{ for any }m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

The
Virasoro algebra

\mathcal{V}
is the universal central extension of
\mathcal{W}
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{V}
has a basis

\left\{L_{n},C_{0}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

with commutation relation

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)C_{0}\delta_{m+n,0}\quad\text{ for any }m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

The
W
-algebras are tensor products of the Witt algebra and its modules of intermediate series.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, the
W
-algebra

W(a,b)
is a Lie algebra with a
\mathbb{C}
-basis
\{L_{n},I_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
satisfying Lie brackets

[L_{i},L_{j}]=(i-j)L_{i+j},\quad[L_{i},I_{j}]=-(a+j+bi)I_{i+j},\quad[I_{i},I_{j}]=0\quad\text{for }i,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since
W(a,b)\cong W(a+k,b)
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we always assume
0\leq a<1
in this paper.
Note that
W(a,b)
is perfect if and only if
(a,b)\neq(0,1)
.

The universal central extension
\widetilde{W}(a,b)
of
W(a,b)
was determined in
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

The algebra
\widetilde{W}(a,b)
satisfies the following Lie brackets

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}C_{0}\delta_{m+n,0},

\displaystyle[L_{m},I_{n}]=-(a+n+bm)I_{m+n}+\delta_{m+n,0}\delta_{a,0}\left((m^{2}+m)C_{1}^{0}\delta_{b,0}+(mC_{1}^{1}+C_{2}^{1})\delta_{b,1}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}C_{1}^{-1}\delta_{b,-1}\right),

\displaystyle[I_{m},I_{n}]=mC_{2}^{0}\delta_{m+n,0}\delta_{a,0}\delta_{b,0}+(2m+1)C_{2}^{\frac{1}{2},0}\delta_{m+n+1,0}\delta_{a,\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{b,0},

where
C_{0},C_{1}^{0},C_{1}^{1},C_{2}^{1},C_{1}^{-1},C_{2}^{0},C_{2}^{\frac{1}{2},0}
are central elements.

Denote by
\mathfrak{C}
the center of
\widetilde{W}(a,b)
.
Note that
\widetilde{W}(a,b)
is perfect unless
(a,b)=(0,1)
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra.
A linear map
f:\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}
is called a
\delta
-derivation
if

\delta[f(x),y]+\delta[x,f(y)]=f([x,y])\quad\text{ for any }x,y\in\mathcal{L}.

Clearly, when
\delta=1
, the notion of
\delta
-derivation is the ordinary derivation.

Denote by
\mathfrak{Der}_{\delta}(\mathcal{L})
the space of
\delta
-derivations of
\mathcal{L}
for a fixed
\delta
.
For simplicity we write
\mathfrak{Der}(\mathcal{L})=\mathfrak{Der}_{1}(\mathcal{L})
.
All
\delta
-derivations of the Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}
and the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
were determined in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
12
]
)
.

(1)
\mathfrak{Der}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\mathrm{ad}L_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
.

(2)
\mathcal{D}er_{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\varphi_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
, where
\varphi_{j}(L_{i})=L_{i+j}
.

(3)
\mathfrak{Der}_{\delta}(\mathcal{W})=0
if
\delta\neq\frac{1}{2},1
.

Now we recall the notion of
\delta
-biderivation from
[
25
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra.
A bilinear map
f:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}
is called a
\delta
-biderivation
of
\mathcal{L}
if

\displaystyle\delta[x,f(y,z)]+\delta[f(x,z),y]=f([x,y],z),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\delta[f(x,y),z]+\delta[y,f(x,z)]=f(x,[y,z])

(2.2)

for any
x,y,z\in\mathcal{L}
.
When
\delta=1
, we simply call
f
a
biderivation
.
A
\delta
-biderivation of
\mathcal{L}
is called
central
if
f(x,y)\in Z(\mathcal{L})
the center of
\mathcal{L}
,
called
symmetric
if
f(x,y)=f(y,x)
,
and called
skew-symmetric
if
f(x,y)=-f(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{L}
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)
the space of
\delta
-biderivations of
\mathcal{L}
for a fixed
\delta
.
Let
f
be a
\delta
-biderivation of
\mathcal{L}
, then for any
x\in\mathcal{L}
,
the linear maps
f(x,\cdot)
and
f(\cdot,x)
are
\delta
-derivations of
\mathcal{L}
.

3.
Basic properties of
\delta
-biderivations

In this section we give some basic properties of
\delta
-biderivations over an arbitrary Lie algebra.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra with center
Z(\mathcal{L})
, and
f
a
\delta
-biderivation of
\mathcal{L}
.

(1) If
\delta\neq 0
, then
f(\alpha,y)=f(y,\alpha)\in Z(\mathcal{L})
for any
\alpha\in Z(\mathcal{L}),y\in\mathcal{L}
.

(2)
f(\alpha,y)=f(y,\alpha)=0
for any
\alpha\in Z(\mathcal{L}),y\in[\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}]
.

(3) If
\mathcal{L}
is perfect, then
f(\alpha,y)=f(y,\alpha)=0
for any
\alpha\in Z(\mathcal{L}),y\in\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

(1) For any
x\in\mathcal{L}
,
\phi_{x}=f(x,\cdot)
is a
\delta
-derivation of
\mathcal{L}
.
Then for any
y\in\mathcal{L}
, we have

0=\phi_{x}([\alpha,y])=\delta[\phi_{x}(\alpha),y]+\delta[\alpha,\phi_{x}(y)]=\delta[\phi_{x}(\alpha),y],

which gives
f(x,\alpha)=\phi_{x}(\alpha)\in Z(\mathcal{L})
if
\delta\neq 0
.
Similarly, one gets
f(\alpha,x)\in Z(\mathcal{L})
.

(2) Since
f
is bilinear, we may assume
y=[z,z^{\prime}]
for some
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}.

Then we have

f(y,\alpha)=f([z,z^{\prime}],\alpha)=\delta[z,f(z^{\prime},\alpha)]+\delta[f(z,\alpha),z^{\prime}]=0.

Similarly, one gets
f(\alpha,y)=0
. (3) follows from (2).
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Any central
\delta
-biderivation of a perfect Lie algebra is a zero map.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a perfect Lie algebra and
f
a central
\delta
-biderivation of
\mathcal{L}
.
For any
x,y\in\mathcal{L}
, write
y=[z,z^{\prime}]
for some
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}.

By (
2.2
), we have

f(x,y)=f(x,[z,z^{\prime}])=\delta[z,f(x,z^{\prime})]+\delta[f(x,z^{\prime}),z]=0
.
So
f=0
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
G
is an abelian group and
\mathcal{L}
is a finitely generated
G
-graded Lie algebra
such that
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{L}_{g}
.
Then
\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)
is also
G
-graded,

\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)_{g},

where
\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)_{g}=\{\phi\in\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)\mid\phi(x,y)\in\mathcal{L}_{g+a+b}\text{ for any }a,b\in G,x\in\mathcal{L}_{a},y\in\mathcal{L}_{b}\}
.
We call elements in
\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)_{g}
are of degree
g
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma 2.1 in
[
14
]
, and we omit the details.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\phi
be a
\delta
-biderivation of a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
with center
Z(\mathcal{L})
.
Define

\overline{\phi}(\overline{x},\overline{y})=\overline{\phi(x,y)}=\phi(x,y)+Z(\mathcal{L}),

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{L}
, where
\overline{x}=x+Z(\mathcal{L})\in\mathcal{L}/Z(\mathcal{L})
.
Then
\overline{\phi}
is a
\delta
-biderivation of
\mathcal{L}/Z(\mathcal{L})
.

Proof.

The verification is easy by using Proposition
3.1
,
and we omit the details.
∎

4.
\delta
-biderivations of the Witt and Virasoro algebras

In this section we determine all
\delta
-biderivations of the Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}

and the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
in two subsections.

4.1.
\delta
-biderivations of the Witt algebra

Clearly the bilinear map
\pi:\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
defined by

\pi(L_{i},L_{j})=[L_{i},L_{j}]\quad\text{ for all }i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

is a (1-)biderivation, and for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the bilinear map
\theta_{n}:\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
defined by

\theta_{n}(L_{i},L_{j})=L_{n+i+j}\quad\text{ for all }i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

is a
\frac{1}{2}
-biderivation.

Theorem 4.1
.

The space of all
\delta
-biderivations of the Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}
is

\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{W}\right)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}\,\pi&\text{ if }\delta=1;\\
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\theta_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}&\text{ if }\delta=\frac{1}{2};\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
f
be a
\delta
-biderivation of
\mathcal{W}
.
Then for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the linear map
f(L_{i},\cdot)
and
f(\cdot,L_{i})
are
\delta
-derivations of
\mathcal{W}
.
Moreover, we may assume
f\in\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{W}\right)_{n}=\{g\in\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{W}\right)\mid g(L_{i},L_{j})\in\mathbb{C}L_{n+i+j}\text{ for any }i,j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by Proposition
3.3
.

If
\delta\neq\frac{1}{2},1
, then by Theorem
2.5
(3) we have
f(L_{i},\cdot)=0
,
i.e.,
f(L_{i},L_{j})=0
for any
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
f=0
.

If
\delta=1
, then for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
the linear map
f(L_{i},\cdot)
is a derivation of degree
n+i
, hence
f(L_{i},\cdot)=\lambda\mathrm{ad}L_{n+i}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
by Theorem
2.5
(1).
So we have
f(L_{i},L_{j})=\lambda[L_{n+i},L_{j}]=\lambda(n+i-j)L_{n+i+j}
.
Similarly, since
f(\cdot,L_{j})
is a derivation of degree
n+j
, we have

f(L_{i},L_{j})=\mu(n-i+j)L_{n+i+j}
for some
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
. So

\lambda(n+i-j)=\mu(n-i+j)\quad\text{ for any }i,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let
j=n+i
and we get
n\mu=0
.
If
n\neq 0
, then
\mu=0
, and further we have
\lambda=0
. So
f=0
.
If
n=0
, then it is clear that
\mu=-\lambda
and hence
f=\lambda\pi
.
This proves that
\mathcal{BD}^{[1]}(\mathcal{W})=\mathbb{C}\,\pi.

If
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
, then for any
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
we have by Theorem
2.5
(2) that

f(L_{i},\cdot)=a_{i}\varphi_{n+i},\quad f(\cdot,L_{j})=b_{j}\varphi_{n+j},

where
a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Hence
a_{i}L_{n+i+j}=f(L_{i},L_{j})=b_{j}L_{n+i+j}
,
which further implies that
a_{i}=b_{j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
So
f
is a multiple of
\theta_{n}
.
This proves the case
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

4.2.
\delta
-biderivations of the Virasoro algebra

In this subsection we determine all
\delta
-biderivations of the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
.
It is easy to check that the bilinear map
\widetilde{\pi}:\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}
defined by

\widetilde{\pi}(L_{i},L_{j})=[L_{i},L_{j}]=(i-j)L_{i+j}+\frac{1}{12}(i^{3}-i)C_{0}\delta_{i+j,0}\quad\text{ for all }i,j\in\mathbb{Z},

is a (1-)biderivation of degree
0
.

Theorem 4.2
.

The space of all
\delta
-biderivations of the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
is

\mathfrak{BD}^{[\delta]}\left(\mathcal{V}\right)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}\,\widetilde{\pi}&\text{ if }\delta=1;\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
f
be a
\delta
-biderivation of
\mathcal{V}
and
\overline{f}
the
\delta
-biderivation of the quotient algebra
\mathcal{V}/\mathbb{C}C_{0}
defined by Proposition
3.4
.
Since
\mathcal{V}
is perfect, we have
f(C_{0},\mathcal{V})=f(\mathcal{V},C_{0})=0
by Proposition
3.1
(3).
Note that
\mathcal{V}/\mathbb{C}C_{0}\cong\mathcal{W}
.

If
\delta\neq\frac{1}{2},1
, then
\overline{f}=0
by Theorem
4.1
,
i.e.,
f
is a central
\delta
-biderivation of
\mathcal{V}
.
So
f=0
by Proposition
3.2
.

If
\delta=1
, then
\overline{f}=\lambda\,\pi
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
by Theorem
4.1
.
So
f_{1}=f-\lambda\widetilde{\pi}
is a central biderivation of
\mathcal{V}
.
Then
f_{1}=0
by Proposition
3.2
, and hence
f=\lambda\,\widetilde{\pi}
.

For the case
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
, we may further assume that
f
is of degree
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by Proposition
3.3
.
Hence
\overline{f}=a_{n}\theta_{n}
for some
a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
by Theorem
4.1
.
So for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
we may write

f(L_{i},L_{j})=a_{n}L_{n+i+j}+C_{i,j}C_{0}\quad\text{for some }C_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}.

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, apply
x=L_{i},y=L_{-i},z=L_{-n}
to (
2.1
) and we get

a_{n}(i^{2}-1)-24C_{0,-n}=0,

which implies
a_{n}=0
.
So
f
is a central
\frac{1}{2}
-biderivation of
\mathcal{V}
.
Hence
f=0
by Proposition
3.2
.
∎

In this section we determine all
\delta

All

Proposition 5.1
.

(1) The space of (1-)derivations of

is

\mathfrak{Der}(W(a,b))=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Inn}(W(a,b))\oplus\mathbb{C}D_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}D_{2}^{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}D_{3}&\text{if }(a,b)=(0,0),\\

\mathrm{Inn}(W(a,b))\oplus\mathbb{C}D_{1}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the derivations

6.1.
Commuting linear maps

A linear map
\varphi
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called
commuting
if

[\varphi(x),x]=0\quad\text{ for any }x\in\mathfrak{g}.

This condition is equivalent to

[\varphi(x),y]=[x,\varphi(y)]\quad\text{ for any }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Clearly, the identity map
\mathrm{id}
is a commuting linear map of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The space of all commuting linear maps of
\mathcal{L}
is

\mathfrak{clm}(\mathcal{L})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\mathrm{id},\Theta_{1}^{(0,-1)}\right\}&\text{if }\mathcal{L}=W(0,-1);\\
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\mathrm{id},\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{(0,-1)}\right\}&\text{if }\mathcal{L}=\widetilde{W}(0,-1);\\
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\mathrm{id}\right\}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the linear maps
\Theta_{1}^{(0,-1)}
and
\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{(0,-1)}
are defined by

\displaystyle\Theta_{1}^{(0,-1)}(L_{i})=I_{i},\quad\Theta_{1}^{(0,-1)}(I_{i})=0;

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{(0,-1)}(L_{i})=I_{i},\quad\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{(0,-1)}(I_{i})=0,\quad\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{(0,-1)}(C_{0})=\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{(0,-1)}(C_{1}^{-1})=0.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a commuting linear map of
\mathcal{L}
,
the bilinear map
f_{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{L}
defined by

f_{\varphi}(x,y)=[\varphi(x),y]=[x,\varphi(y)]\quad\text{for any }x,y\in\mathcal{L},

(6.1)

is a skew-symmetric biderivation of
\mathcal{L}
.
So
\{f_{\varphi}\mid\varphi\in\mathfrak{clm}(\mathcal{L})\}
is a subspace of the space
\mathcal{SSBD}(\mathcal{L})
of skew-symmetric biderivations of
\mathcal{L}
,
which has dimension 2 if
\mathcal{L}=W(0,-1)
or
\widetilde{W}(0,-1)
,
and dimension 1 otherwise, by Theorem
4.1
, Theorem
4.2
, Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
5.3
.

It is easy to check that
\Theta_{1}^{(0,-1)}
and
\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{(0,-1)}
are commuting linear maps of
W(0,-1)
and
\widetilde{W}(0,-1)
respectively.
So the skew-symmetric biderivations constructed through (
6.1
) using all linear combinations of
\mathrm{id},\Theta_{1}^{(0,-1)}
and
\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{(0,-1)}
exhaust
\mathcal{SSBD}(\mathcal{L})
in each case respectively.
This proves the theorem.
∎

6.2.
Commutative post-Lie algebra

In this subsection we determine all commutative post-Lie algebra structures on the algebra
\mathcal{L}
. Let us first recall the definition of commutative post-Lie algebra.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra.
A
commutative post-Lie algebra structure
on
\mathfrak{g}

is a bilinear product
\circ
satisfying that

x\circ y=y\circ x,\quad[x,y]\circ z=x\circ(y\circ z)-y\circ(x\circ z),\quad x\circ[y,z]=[x\circ y,z]+[y,x\circ z]

for any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The commutative post-Lie algebra structure
\circ
on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
is called
trivial
if
x\circ y=0
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.
We also call
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\circ)
a
commutative post-Lie algebra
.

It is easy to check that the bilinear map
f(x,y)=x\circ y
on
\mathfrak{g}
is a symmetric biderivation.
This observation enables us to classify all commutative post-Lie algebra structures on the algebra
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Any commutative post-Lie algebra structures on the algebra
\mathcal{L}
is trivial unless
\mathcal{L}=\widetilde{W}(0,1)
.
Any nontrivial commutative post-Lie algebra structures on the algebra
\widetilde{W}(0,1)
is of the form
\Lambda_{a,b,c},a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
, defined by

\Lambda_{a,b,c}(I_{0}+x,I_{0}+y)=aC_{0}+bC_{1}^{1}+cC_{2}^{1}\quad\text{for any }x,y\in\widetilde{W}(0,1)^{\prime}.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{SBD}(\mathcal{L})
the space of symmetric biderivations of
\mathcal{L}
.
By Theorem
4.1
, Theorem
4.2
, Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
5.3
we have

\mathcal{SBD}(\mathcal{L})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\Psi_{n}^{(a,0)}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}&\text{ if }\mathcal{L}=W(a,0);\\
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\Psi_{n}^{(a,1)}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}&\text{ if }\mathcal{L}=W(a,1);\\
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}\right\}&\text{ if }\mathcal{L}=\widetilde{W}(0,1);\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
f
be the symmetric biderivation of
\mathcal{L}
defined by

f(x,y)=x\circ y\quad\text{for any }x,y\in\mathcal{L}.

So any commutative post-Lie algebra structures on
\mathcal{L}
is trivial if
\mathcal{L}\neq W(a,0),W(a,1)
, or
\widetilde{W}(0,1)
.

If
\mathcal{L}=W(a,0)
,
then we may write

for some
\mu_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
,

p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
p\geq q
.
Therefore, we have by Theorem
5.2
(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=q}^{p}\mu_{n}I_{n}

\displaystyle=f(L_{1},L_{-1})=f([L_{1},L_{0}],L_{-1})=[L_{1},L_{0}]\circ L_{-1}=L_{1}\circ(L_{0}\circ L_{-1})-L_{0}\circ(L_{1}\circ L_{-1})

\displaystyle=f\left(L_{1},f(L_{0},L_{-1})\right)-f\left(L_{0},f(L_{1},L_{-1})\right)=0,

which implies all
\mu_{n}=0
. So
f=0
.
Hence any commutative post-Lie algebra structures on
W(a,0)
is trivial.

If
\mathcal{L}=W(a,1)
,
then we may write
f=\sum_{n=q}^{p}\mu_{n}\Psi_{n}^{(a,1)}
for some
\mu_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
,

p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
p\geq q

Let

be such that

5.2
(2)

\sum_{n=q}^{p}\mu_{n}(a+n+k+1)I_{n+k}=f(L_{1},L_{k})=[L_{1},L_{0}]\circ L_{k}=f\left(L_{1},f(L_{0},L_{k})\right)-f\left(L_{0},f(L_{1},L_{k})\right)=0,

which implies all
\mu_{n}=0
. So
f=0
.
Hence any commutative post-Lie algebra structures on
W(a,1)
is trivial.

If
\mathcal{L}=\widetilde{W}(0,1)
,
then we have
f(x,y)\in\mathfrak{C}
for any
x,y\in\widetilde{W}(0,1)
,
and one may easily check that for any
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
,
\Lambda_{a,b,c}

actually defines a commutative post-Lie algebra structure on
\widetilde{W}(0,1)
.
This proves the theorem.
∎

6.3.

Definition 6.4

A

\mathcal{P}

\circ

\delta z\circ[x,y]=[z\circ x,y]+[x,z\circ y]\quad\text{ for any }x,y,z\in\mathcal{P}.

\delta
-Poisson algebra structure

\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}
a transposed
\delta
-Poisson algebra.
A transposed
\delta
-Poisson algebra structure
\circ
on
\mathfrak{g}
is called
trivial
if
x\circ y=0
for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\circ,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a transposed
\delta
-Poisson algebra.
It is easy to check that the bilinear map
f(x,y)=x\circ y
on
\mathfrak{g}
is a symmetric
\frac{1}{\delta}
-biderivation.
This fact enables us to classify all transposed
\delta
-Poisson algebra structures on the algebra
\mathcal{L}

.

(1) Any transposed
2

-Poisson algebra structure on

is trivial.

(2) Any transposed
