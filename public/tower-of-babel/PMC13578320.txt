Epidemiological and genetic characteristics of a sapovirus GI.6 acute gastroenteritis outbreak in Jiangxi, China
Authors: Qian Wang, Jun Zhou, Shiwen Liu, Yanni Zhang, Hailong Liu, Xiaoqing Liu, Yong Shi, Fang Xiao, Yangbowen Wu, Dajin Xiao, Yuan xie, Gang Xu, Jiabo Du, Shuting Wei, Jianxiong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo clarify the etiological and genetic evolutionary characteristics of an acute gastroenteritis cluster outbreak caused by sapovirus in Nanchang, Jiangxi, China. A total of 10 anal swab samples were collected from student and teacher cases during a gastroenteritis outbreak at a primary school in Nanchang, Jiangxi Province, in November 2024. Real-time fluorescent RT-PCR was used for routine gene detection of diarrhea viruses. For the sapovirus nucleic acid-positive samples, traditional RT-PCR was used for amplification, sequence determination, splicing, and genetic relationship analysis. We also conducted whole-genome sequencing on sapovirus to analyze the amino acid variations. This outbreak was caused by contact transmission. Real-time fluorescent reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction detection confirmed that 5 samples were positive for sapovirus nucleic acid, and the capsid protein gene VP1 fragments were successfully amplified. Genetic relationship analysis inferred from the phylogenetic tree revealed that the molecular type of sapovirus was the GI.6 genotype, with 100% homology among the 5 sequences. The nucleotide homology between the sequences in this study and the sapovirus GI.6 reference sequence ranged from 97.07% to 99.12%, and the amino acid homology ranged from 96.49% to 97.37%. Amino acid variation site analysis revealed that there were 4 amino acid site substitutions between them. Compared with VP1 ,the closest distance of nucleotide homology was 99.12% from Zhejiang, China, in 2023 (OR351089). Through amino acid comparison of the whole-genome sequence, we found that the similarity of sapovirus in Jiangxi Province to other reference strains in ORF1 ranged from 99.21% to 99.25%, and the similarity in ORF2 ranged from 86.8% to 88%. The causative agent of this outbreak was identified as sapovirus GI.6, a genotype rarely reported in Jiangxi. In the future, further research on the molecular epidemiology of sapovirus with more gene sites that may mutate should be strengthened.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57428-y.Keywords: Sapovirus, Acute gastroenteritis, Outbreak, Molecular epidemiologySubject terms: Diseases, Genetics, Microbiology, Molecular biology

Introduction

Sapovirus (SaV) belongs to the genus Sapovirus in the family Caliciviridae and is one of the important pathogens that cause human acute gastroenteritis (AGE)1. SaV is a single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus with a genome of approximately 7.1–7.6 kb, and is divided into 2–3 open reading frames (ORFs). ORF1 encodes six non-structural proteins, NSP1–NSP7, and one major capsid structural protein VP1. ORF2 encodes the smaller structural protein VP2. The function of ORF3 remains unclear2. According to the nucleotide sequence differences of the capsid protein VP1 encoded by ORF1, SaV can be divided into 20 genotypes (GI-GXX), among which GI, GII, GIV, and GV can infect humanss3. Human SaV is mainly transmitted through the fecal-oral route. Its clinical symptoms include dysphoria, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, and abdominal colic. According to sentinel surveillance data at both the national and Jiangxi provincial levels, sporadic cases occur throughout the year, with peak incidence observed during winter and spring seasons. It is characterized by diverse transmission routes, a low infectious dose, and general susceptibility of the population. Gastroenteritis induced by SaV is usually milder than that caused by NoV and is relatively rare in Jiangxi Province. However, with the widespread use of the rotavirus vaccine, outbreaks of acute gastroenteritis (AGE) caused by SaV in kindergartens, nursing homes, and schools are increasingly becoming a major public health issue4. In November 2024, a clustered outbreak of acute gastroenteritis occurred in a primary school in Nanchang, Jiangxi. This study conducted pathogen identification for this outbreak, as well as sequencing, typing, and genetic evolution analysis, aiming to understand the evolutionary characteristics of SaV and provide more effective strategies and means for the prevention and control of viral diarrhea.

Methods

Sample collection

According to the definitions of suspected cases, clinically diagnosed cases, and confirmed cases in the “National Surveillance Program for Viral Diarrhea (2021 Edition)”, a suspected case is defined as any teacher, student, or staff member at the Yuquandao Campus of Nanchang Normal University Affiliated Primary School who has presented with gastrointestinal symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and diarrhea since November 5, 2024. A clinically diagnosed case refers to a suspected case that meets the criteria of having ≥ 3 bowel movements with a change in stool consistency (watery stools) within 24 h and/or ≥ 2 episodes of vomiting within 24 h, without other etiological diagnoses, and having an epidemiological association with a confirmed case. A confirmed case is a suspected or clinically diagnosed case with a positive nucleic acid test result for SaV in fecal, anal swab, or vomitus samples. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in Qingshanhu District conducted a case search by reviewing the school’s morning check records and records of absences due to illness, and interviewing school administrators and class teachers. On November 12, 2024, a total of 10 anal swab samples were collected from suspected cases on-site and sent to the district CDC laboratory for testing.

Sample processing and viral nucleic acid extraction

After vortexing the collected anal swab samples at 2500 rpm/min for 2 min, let them stand for 5 min for later use. A fully automatic nucleic acid extractor (Strean SP96, Guangzhou Daan Gene) and its matching nucleic acid extraction or purification kit (catalog no. DA0602) were used to extract viral nucleic acid according to the kit instructions. The extracted nucleic acid was stored at 4 °C for short-term storage and at -80 °C for long-term storage.All SaV specimen manipulation was performed in BSL-2 biosafety laboratory following standard biosafety operation specifications, researchers wore disposable protective clothing, gloves and masks during nucleic acid extraction, all residual samples and waste were inactivated and disposed of in accordance with infectious waste management rules.

Real-time fluorescent RT-PCR (real-time RT-PCR)

Viral nucleic acids were extracted and used as templates for real-time fluorescent reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) detection. Two commercial triple real-time fluorescent PCR kits were applied: Group A rotavirus/adenovirus/astrovirus nucleic acid detection kit (Beijing Applied Biological Technologies Co., Ltd., Cat. No. A7133YH-50T) and norovirus GI/norovirus GII/sapovirus nucleic acid detection kit (Beijing Applied Biological Technologies Co., Ltd., Cat. No. A7123YH-50T). The total reaction volume was set to 25 µL. Amplification parameters and result interpretation were performed in strict accordance with the manufacturer instructions.

Amplification and sequencing of the SaV capsid region (VP1)

For the samples positive for SaV by real-time RT-PCR, the primer pair SLV5317 and SLV5749 was used to amplify the SaV VP1 gene [11]. SLV5317: 5’-CTC GCC ACC TAC RAW GCB TGG TT-3’, SLV5749: 5’-CGG RCY TCA AAV STA CCB CCC CA-3’. The reactions were performed using the One-Step RT-PCR Kit (QIAGEN, Germany; Cat. No. 210212) on an Eppendorf thermal cycler. The reaction system was as follows: 8 µL of 5xRT-PCR Buffer, 1.6 µL of dNTP mixture (10 mM), 1.6 µL of RT-PCR Enzyme mixture,0.8 µL of SLV5317 (30 µM),0.8 µL of SLV5749 (30 µM),12 µL of DEPC-treated H₂O, and 8 µL of RNA template, for a total volume of 40 µL. The PCR conditions were as follows: reverse transcription at 50 °C for 30 min; initial denaturation at 95 °C for 15 min; 40 cycles of denaturation at 94 °C for 30 s, annealing at 50 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 1 min, and a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min; 4 °C indefinitely. The expected PCR product size was 434 bp. The PCR products were sent to Sangon Biotechnology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. for sequencing.

Sequence splicing and molecular genetic evolution analysis of SaV VP1

The SeqMan software was used to organize and splice the sequences. At the same time, the sequences of the VP1 region of SaV GI~GVII types were downloaded from GenBank. Using the ClustalW method in the MEGA 12.0 software, the sequences that had been sequenced and spliced were compared with the nucleotide sequences of the VP1 region of SaV downloaded from the GenBank database.The neighbor-joining algorithm and maximum composite likelihood model with 1000 bootstraps method was used to construct a phylogenetic tree. NCBI BLAST was used to analyze the nucleotide and amino acid sequence homology, and Megalin software was used to analyze the average genetic distance of the sequences and the amino acid variation sites.

SaV whole genome sequencing and analysis of variant sites

The Sapovirus Whole Genome Capture Kit (BAIYITECH Lot: BK.SAV024) was used to ampilify SaV whole genome. The sample number is 2024FXYQ153. Library preparation was carried out with the Rapid Barcode Sequencing Kit (Nanopore Lot: SQK-RBK114). Pretreatment reactions before sequencing was accomplished using Sequencing chip initiation kit (Nanopore Lot: EXP-FLP004), and the sequencing reaction is accomplished using MinION/ GridION flow cell sequencing chip (Nanopore Lot: FLO-MIN114). Raw sequencing data were assembled using the Pathogenic Microorganisms Bioinformatics Analysis Platform version 5.2 (BAIYITECH Co.,Ltd, Hangzhou, China).We use Megalin to analysis the variant sites between 2024FXYQ153 and the reference sequences of SaV GI.6 whole genomes uploaded from NCBI in recent years.

Ethical statement

All experimental procedures involving human clinical specimens were performed in accordance with relevant national ethical guidelines and regulations.

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Jiangxi Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention with file number JJS-1.1/2024.12.31.(Although the sample collection occurred on 12 November 2024,the sequencing and analysis of the sample genes was a retrospective study. The sample detection work was carried out in Jan 2025, which was after the ethical document was approved.)

Written informed consents were obtained from all enrolled patients or their legal guardians before sample collection or detection.

Results

Basic situation of the outbreak

The primary school involved in the outbreak consists of 6 grades and 64 classes, with a total of 2,890 students and 201 teaching and administrative staff. As of 15:00 on November 20, 2024, a total of 10 suspected cases were identified, all of whom were teachers and students from Class 10, Grade 5. The male-to-female ratio of the cases was 2.33:1, including 9 student cases and 1 teacher case. Based on epidemiological investigation and laboratory testing results, 5 cases were confirmed, and another 5 were clinically diagnosed. The predominant clinical manifestations among the 10 cases included vomiting, nausea, abdominal pain, and diarrhea, while 2 patients presented with dysphoria. See Table 1 for details. The earliest onset time was 11:00 on November 8, and the latest was 09:00 on November 12. The onset peak was concentrated between 00:00 on November 11 and 12:00 on November 12.

Outbreak control measures and effects

The affected class was closed for 4 days from November 12 to 15. Affected teachers and students were required to undergo 72 h of isolation after recovery before returning to school. Sick personnel were strictly barred from attending classes. Epidemiological investigations and health education were carried out for all cases. Guidance was provided to the school to conduct terminal disinfection. The whole school implemented rigorous cleaning and disinfection protocols to prevent cross-contamination, and indoor ventilation was enhanced.

The school was also instructed to strengthen food and drinking water safety management. Campus health education and publicity were intensified, and regular communication was maintained with students’ families to prevent parental panic. Follow-up management was arranged for students in the suspended class. Large-scale gatherings were suspended and public classrooms were closed during the outbreak.

The school was required to promptly report any teachers or students with vomiting or diarrhea symptoms to the district Center for Disease Control and Prevention, and submit a daily outbreak progress report to the Qingshan District Disease Control Center before 15:00 each day. Following the strict implementation of these control measures, no new cases, severe cases or deaths occurred. The outbreak was well contained, ensuring public health and social stability.

Real-time RT-PCR detection results

Of the 10 samples, all of which were anal swabs collected from the 10 suspected cases, 5 tested positive for SaV, yielding a positive rate of 50% (5/10), while the remaining samples were all negative.

Sequencing and analysis of the SaV VP1

All five Real-time RT-PCR-positive samples showed successful amplification, and the amplified products were consistent with the expected fragment size. A total of 45 global reference sequences covering the VP1 region of typical and representative sapovirus (SaV) genotypes were retrieved for this study. All reference SaV genotype sequences were downloaded from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI).

Genetic relatedness analysis was performed using the five sequences obtained in this outbreak, and a phylogenetic tree was constructed accordingly. Phylogenetic analysis indicated that the SaV strains identified in this outbreak belonged to genotype GI.6, and shared the closest genetic homology with a Zhejiang, China isolate from 2023 (accession number: OR351089).

The sequences determined in this study (2024FXYQ153-2024FXYQ157) have been submitted to NCBI, with GenBank accession numbers PX480016-PX480020. The phylogenetic tree is presented in Fig. 1.

Discussions

SaV and NoV are important pathogens causing acute gastroenteritis, especially common in children5,6.This study investigated an outbreak of acute gastroenteritis within a school.After epidemiological investigation and laboratory tests, the possibility of food poisoning has been ruled out.It was initially inferred to be a SaV infection transmitted through contact.It should be noted that this study has certain limitations: the sample was limited to teachers and students from a specific class, the sample quantity was relatively small, and there were no control samples from other classes in the same school, other schools, or the community.Therefore, although SaV GI.6 was clearly detected in this outbreak, it should be treated with caution before being identified as the sole pathogen of this outbreak.The results of this study provide important clues for tracing the origin, and their universality needs to be verified through expanding the sampling scope in the future.

The samples from this outbreak were subjected to molecular typing, and it was determined to be of the GI.6 genotype of SaV.Since SaV was first discovered in 1976 through electron microscopy of human diarrhea samples7,SaV has shown genetic diversity. Among them, the GI type is dominant globally, while GI.1 and GI.2 are the most commonly reported genotypes8–14.In contrast, the GI.6 genotype is relatively rare. However, recent monitoring data shows that the GI.6 genotype has gradually emerged in the outbreaks in different regions of China in recent years.For instance, there were SaV GI.6 outbreaks relevanted reports in Shenyang in 20222,Beijing from 2019 to 202315,and Zhejiang in 202316.Especially during the SaV outbreak in Zhejiang Province from 2021 to 2023,the GI.6 strain accounted for as high as 57.14%16.Moreover, this genotype was also detected in sporadic cases in Shandong and other places in China17,as well as in environmental sewage samples in Africa18 and Italy19. This suggests that the SaV GI.6 may have formed a low-level transmission on a large scale, and its epidemiological significance deserves continuous attention.

In our study, the nucleotide sequence homology of the capsid region genes of the 5 detected GI.6 strains in this study was 100%,suggesting that they belong to the same transmission chain. Further analysis revealed that the partial VP1 regions of these strains have nucleotide homology ranging from 97.07% to 99.12% and amino acid homology ranging from 96.49% to 97.37% compared to the reference sequence.VP1 is a key region for virus genotyping and antigenicity of the SaV1,20. The variant sites in the VP1 region discovered through fragment sequencing in this study have not been reported to be directly related to pathogenicity. Therefore, a more in-depth analysis of the entire genome mutation spectrum revealed potential changes in other regions of the virus.In this study, we identified 19 amino acid variation sites in the proteins encoded in the ORF1, including 15 variation site in non-structural protein (NS1:5, NS2:2, NS3:3, NS6-7:5) and 4 variation site in structural protein VP1.There are 3 protease catalytic sites and a part of the 7 protease active sites in NS6-721. However, in the sequences obtained in this study, no mutations were found in these protease catalytic sites and active sites.The antigenic diversity and immunogenicity of SaVs were primarily produced in four hypervariable regions, respectively are HRV1 (2017–2033),HRV2 (2060–2075), HRV3(2098–2112), HRV4(2126–2142)20.In this study, Compared with the reference sequence, the amino acid mutation at the S2137G site was found in HRV4, and there may be a potential risk of changes in viral infectivity.The amino acid variation sites in the NS1, NS2, NS3 and VP2 regions have not been reported in the literature yet and require further investigation.Notably, although the classification of the SaV mainly relies on the VP1 region1,this study found that there were also many variations in the NS1 region.Although functions of the non-structural proteins NS1/2 and NS4 remain elusive, recent studies suggest that mutations in their coding sequences may influence tissue tropism, virulence, and epidemiological fitness22.Therefore, the existing molecular epidemiological studies based on the VP1 region (or the entire genome) may have overlooked the crucial role of non-structural protein variations in the evolution of the SARS virus and the formation of epidemic characteristics. The rare variations discovered in this study may be associated with certain characteristics of the current epidemic. Additionally, in the VP2 region encoded by ORF2, we identified 22 variation sites. We found that ompared to the VP1 region, the variations in VP2 are more abundant, which is consistent with some literature reports17,23. The specific function of VP2 is not yet fully clear24, and the potential impact of its high variability on virus assembly and stability is worthy of further exploration.

This investigation confirmed a contact-transmitted cluster outbreak caused by the SA virus strain GI.6. Although the GI.6 strain was not the predominant circulating strain in history, multiple point detections both domestically and internationally, as well as the occurrence of this outbreak, indicate that it has become one of the significant prevalent genotypes and requires enhanced monitoring. This study not only reports on a single outbreak, but more importantly, through the analysis of the complete genome of the virus strain, it reveals significant variations not only in the classic VP1 region but also in the non-structural protein region (such as NS1). This suggests that future molecular monitoring and research on the SaV should expand from the single VP1 typing to the whole genome level to better understand the variation patterns, evolutionary dynamics, and their correlations with clinical manifestations and transmission ability of the virus. Clarifying the functions of these variant sites will be crucial for revealing the pathogenic and transmission mechanisms of the SaV and assessing its potential public health risks.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this outbreak of viral gastroenteritis in a primary school was caused by SaV genotype GI.6 and was most likely transmitted through close contact. Phylogenetic and homology analyses revealed the genetic characteristics and variations of the VP1 gene and genome-wide amino acid mutations of SaV GI.6 strains in Jiangxi Province.This study represents the first laboratory-confirmed report of an acute gastroenteritis outbreak associated with SaV GI.6 in Jiangxi, providing new data on the genetic diversity of local SaV strains. These findings highlight the need for enhanced monitoring of SaV GI.6 in local surveillance.Future studies with expanded sample sizes will be necessary to further characterize the molecular epidemiology, genotype distribution, and evolutionary patterns of SaV in Jiangxi Province, thereby supporting evidence-based prevention and control of childhood acute gastroenteritis.
