Invariants of Sequential Circuits and Generalized Non-Abelian Statistics Preprint: RIKEN-iTHEMS-Report-26
Shintaro Sato, Yoshimasa Hidaka, Ryohei Kobayashi

Abstract
Non-invertible symmetries in quantum many-body systems generally give rise to sequential unitary circuits that move symmetry defects. In this paper, we investigate invariants defined by sequences of such circuits, which move non-invertible defects and generate a Berry phase evaluated on quantum states with defects. We show that this Berry phase generally defines an invariant under local deformations, provided that the sequential circuits preserve the locality of those deformations. This invariant also rules out a short-range-entangled state that preserves the non-invertible symmetry, thereby signaling the ’t Hooft anomaly of a non-invertible symmetry purely in terms of unitary operators acting on a state. We then apply this framework to loop excitations in three spatial dimensions and identify a new loop excitation in the (3+1)D 𝔻 4 \mathbb{D}_{4} topological order, which we dub a non-Abelian fermionic loop. Using the invariant of sequential circuits, we characterize the statistics of non-Abelian fermionic loops. In addition, we find a new (3+1)D mixed topological order with a single non-Abelian fermionic loop, whose long-range entanglement is protected by an invariant of sequential circuits.

Unlike invertible global symmetries, which are represented by locality-preserving unitary operators, non-invertible symmetries are not generically captured by a single unitary acting on the whole Hilbert space.

II
Invariant of sequential circuits

We formulate an invariant of sequential circuits associated with non-invertible symmetry, under a set of physically motivated assumptions that the sequential unitaries are required to satisfy.
We consider a tensor-product Hilbert space in a generic spatial dimension
d
. In the presence of a non-invertible symmetry, one can generally define a defect state
\ket{\mathcal{D}}
with an inserted defect network
\mathcal{D}
. Our goal is to construct a Berry phase from a family of defect states.

We begin by fixing a finite set of defect-network configurations
\{\mathcal{D}\}
in
d
-dimensional space. Each defect network
\mathcal{D}
is associated with a quantum state
\ket{\mathcal{D}}
carrying the corresponding defect insertion. For each pair
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
, we further fix a defect-“movement” operator
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
, defined by

V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\ket{\mathcal D}=e^{i\theta(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})}\ket{\mathcal D'}
(see Fig.
1
).
Here,
\Sigma
denotes the support of the operator, and
a
labels a simple object in the symmetry (generally a higher fusion) category, representing a generator of the non-invertible symmetry. We assume that each movement operator
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
is implemented by a sequential unitary circuit. The maximal geometrical range of each local circuit in the sequential unitary is denoted by
\epsilon
.

We then consider a Berry phase associated with a sequence of defect-movement operators acting on a fixed defect state:

\displaystyle\bra{\mathcal{D}_1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}V(\Sigma_{j},a_{j};\mathcal{D}_{j},\mathcal{D}_{j+1})\right)\ket{\mathcal{D}_1}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(i\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\theta(\Sigma_{j},a_{j};\mathcal{D}_{j},\mathcal{D}_{j+1})\right)

(1)

Not every such sequence defines an invariant under local deformations of the operators and states. To formulate and establish invariance under admissible deformations, we impose several conditions on the movement operators and on the allowed sequences of their composition:

•

Fixed operator support.

For a given pair of defect configurations
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
together with a label of the movement operator
a
, the support
\Sigma
of the operator
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
is uniquely fixed. Namely, if two operators
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}),V(\Sigma^{\prime},a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
appear in the sequence (
1
), then
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}
. Therefore, we sometimes write
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
as
V(a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
by omitting
\Sigma
.

•

Local uniqueness of circuits.

Consider a point
p
of
\Sigma
. Then, we require that the sequential circuit
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
can be expressed in the form of

\displaystyle V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})=U^{\prime}U_{r,p}U^{\prime\prime}

(2)

where
U_{r,p}
is a circuit supported on a ball
D_{r,p}
of
p
with radius
r
, and the supports of
U^{\prime}
and
U^{\prime\prime}
are away from
p
(at a distance
r-\epsilon
). Here,
r
is taken much larger than
\epsilon
, while much smaller than the size of
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
.
Further, consider movement operators
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
,
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{3},\mathcal{D}_{4})
, where the restrictions of the defect configurations are identical in the
r
-neighborhood of
p
:
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})|_{D_{r,p}}=(\mathcal{D}_{3},\mathcal{D}_{4})|_{D_{r,p}}
. Then, we require that the circuit
U_{r,p}
in the
r
-neighborhood of
p
can be taken to be identical:

\displaystyle U_{r,p}(a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=U_{r,p}(a;\mathcal{D}_{3},\mathcal{D}_{4})~.

(3)

In other words, the local circuit
U_{r,p}
depends on defect configurations only through those in the vicinity of
p
:
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})|_{D_{r,p}}
.

•

Inverse of sequential circuits.

For any pair of defect configurations
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
and an operator label
a
, we require

\displaystyle V(\Sigma,a^{*};\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\mathcal{D})=V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})^{\dagger}~,

(4)

where
a^{*}
is a dual of
a

1
1

1

Here we assumed that the left and right dual of the symmetry fusion
d
-category are identical. That generally requires additional structure to the symmetry category, e.g., being pivotal.
, hence
\theta(\Sigma,a^{*};\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})+\theta(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\mathcal{D})=0
mod
2\pi
.

•

Defect creation operator.

Each state is obtained by acting a defect creation operator on the canonical state
\ket{0}
with an empty defect configuration:

\displaystyle\ket{\mathcal{D}}=V(\mathcal{D})\ket{0}~.

(5)

The operator
V(\mathcal{D})
is not required to be unitary.

Figure 1:
A sequential circuit
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
is supported at a region
\Sigma
. The corresponding symmetry generator is labeled by
a
(generally an object of a symmetry category), and transforms a defect network.

The Berry phase is expressed as a sum of phase factors

\displaystyle\Theta=\sum_{a,\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\epsilon(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\theta(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})~,

(6)

with
\epsilon(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then, it turns out that the necessary and sufficient condition for
\Theta
to define an invariant is expressed as a set of linear constraints satisfied by the integral coefficients
\epsilon
:

1.

By redefining each state by a phase
\ket{\mathcal{D}}\to e^{i\phi(\mathcal{D})}\ket{\mathcal{D}}
, each phase factor is shifted as
\theta(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\to\theta(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})+\phi(\mathcal{D})-\phi(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
. Invariance under these shifts corresponds to the linear constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{a,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\epsilon(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})-\sum_{a,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\epsilon(\Sigma,a^{*};\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\mathcal{D})=0~.

(7)

2.

We can redefine each operator by a phase
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\to e^{i\phi(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})}V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
. The redefinitions have to be consistent with the constraints (
3
), (
4
); for instance, when
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}=\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}_{3},\mathcal{D}_{4}}=\Sigma
and
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})|_{\partial\Sigma}=(\mathcal{D}_{3},\mathcal{D}_{4})|_{\partial\Sigma}
, the phase shift must be identical:
\phi(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=\phi(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{3},\mathcal{D}_{4})
. The invariance under such phase redefinitions corresponds to the linear constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}\\
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})|_{\partial\Sigma}=(\mathcal{D}_{1}|,\mathcal{D}_{2}|)\end{subarray}}\left(\epsilon(a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})-\epsilon(a^{*};\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{1})\right)=0~,

(8)

where we omitted
\Sigma
from the expression since it is uniquely determined by
(a,\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
, and the sum means that we sum over defects with fixed configurations
(\mathcal{D}_{1}|,\mathcal{D}_{2}|)
at
\partial\Sigma
.

3.

We also require the Berry phase to be invariant under admissible local deformations of a movement operator
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
. Such deformations may occur near the boundary
\partial\Sigma
and are associated with possible redefinitions of defect states along the defect network. Unlike the case of invertible symmetries, however, local deformations of sequential circuits involve an additional subtlety.
To see this, consider a sequential-circuit representation of
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
of the form
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=\cdots U^{\prime}U_{\epsilon,p}U^{\prime\prime}\cdots
, where
U_{\epsilon,p}
is a local circuit supported at
p
near the boundary
\partial\Sigma
. A local deformation at a point
p\in\partial\Sigma
is implemented by modifying the local circuit
U_{\epsilon,p}
as
U_{\epsilon,p}\to U^{\prime}_{\epsilon,p}=V_{\epsilon,p}U_{\epsilon,p}
, where
V_{\epsilon,p}
is a local circuit supported in a region of size
\epsilon
. However, this seemingly local modification can induce a nonlocal change in the Berry phase, because a sequential circuit does not generally preserve operator locality; the conjugation action of
U^{\prime}
or
U^{\prime\prime}
on
U_{\epsilon,p}
can become nonlocal. The induced redefinition of
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
may hence become nonlocal, so invariance of the Berry phase is not expected in general.

For this reason, we restrict to local deformations for which the locality of
V_{\epsilon,p}
is preserved under the action of the symmetry (movement) operators.
As an example, when the non-invertible symmetry is the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Kramers-Wannier duality, we consider deformations that preserve the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, since the duality preserves the locality of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-even operators.

In Appendix
A
, we show that invariance under such admissible local deformations is equivalent to a set of linear constraints on the integer coefficients
{\epsilon}
that define the Berry phase,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},\\
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})|_{D_{r,p}}=(\mathcal{D}_{1}|,\mathcal{D}_{2}|)\end{subarray}}(\epsilon(a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})-\epsilon(a^{*};\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{1}))=0~,

(9)

where we omitted
\Sigma
from the expression since it is uniquely determined by
(a,\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
, and the sum means that we sum over defects with fixed configurations
(\mathcal{D}_{1}|,\mathcal{D}_{2}|)
at the
r
-neighborhood of
p
.

III
Berry phase as an ’t Hooft anomaly

Here, we show that the generalized statistics invariant forbids a short-range-entangled (SRE) state preserving the non-invertible symmetry.
We demonstrate it by showing that the generalized statistics must have a trivial phase on an SRE state. This argument generalizes the discussions in Refs.
55
;
49
to non-invertible defects with sequential circuits.

Assume that the symmetric ground state
|\Psi\rangle
is SRE.
Then there exists a finite-depth unitary circuit
W
and a product state
|0\rangle
such that

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=W|0\rangle~,

(10)

with the defect state
\ket{\mathcal{D}}
given by
\ket{\mathcal{D}}=V(\mathcal{D})\ket{\Psi}
.

Conjugating defect states and movement operators by
W
, we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=W^{\dagger}V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})W~,

(11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle:=W^{\dagger}V(\mathcal{D})W~,

(12)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle

\displaystyle:=W^{\dagger}|\mathcal{D}\rangle=\widetilde{V}(\mathcal{D})\ket{0}~.

(13)

Note that
\widetilde{V}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
is again a sequential unitary circuit.
Then any Berry phase of a closed sequence of movement operators is unchanged:

\displaystyle\bra{\mathcal D_1}\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}V(\Sigma_{j},a_{j};\mathcal{D}_{j},\mathcal{D}_{j+1})\Bigr)\ket{\mathcal D_1}

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{\widetilde{\mathcal D}_1}\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\widetilde{V}(\Sigma_{j},a_{j};\mathcal{D}_{j},\mathcal{D}_{j+1})\Bigr)\ket{\widetilde{\mathcal D}_1}~.

(15)

Therefore, to analyze generalized-statistics invariants on SRE states, it suffices to work with the product reference state
|0\rangle
and the conjugated operators
\widetilde{V}
.

III.1
Factorization of defects in SRE states

We show that, making a suitable topological-deformability assumption for defect creation operators
V(\mathcal{D})
, the above defect state
|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle
created on a product state forms a trivial product state away from the defect network. Concretely, we show that
|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle
has the form of

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle=\ket{\psi}_{A}\otimes\ket{0}_{A^{c}}~,

(16)

where
A
is an
\epsilon
-neighborhood of the defects
\mathcal{D}
, and
A^{c}
is its complement, meaning that the defect state forms a product state away from the defects.

To see this, we first consider the case where in
d
spatial dimensions, the movement operators
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}),V(\Sigma^{\prime},a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
have dimension
<d
, i.e., non-invertible higher-form symmetry. The case with the 0-form symmetry is discussed later.

We assume that the symmetry action is topological on defect states
\ket{\mathcal{D}}
: each movement operator
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
can be topologically deformed so that its support
\Sigma
is deformed into
\Sigma^{\prime}
with the same boundary
\partial\Sigma=\partial\Sigma^{\prime}
, and the actions of
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}),V(\Sigma^{\prime},a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
on the defect states are identical as long as the deformation does not cross defects;
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\ket{\mathcal{D}}=V(\Sigma^{\prime},a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\ket{\mathcal{D}}
. We impose such a topological condition on the defect creation operators
V(\Sigma;\mathcal{D})
as well.

Let
\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
denote the geometric support of the defect network.
Let
A
be an
\epsilon
-neighborhood (a “thickening”) of
\mathcal{D}
.
We write the Hilbert space as
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A^{c}}
.

Recall that defect states are obtained by acting with a defect-creation operator:

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle=\widetilde{V}(\Sigma;\mathcal{D})\,|0\rangle~,

(17)

where we write the support of the operator
\widetilde{V}(\mathcal{D})
as
\Sigma
, such that the region
A
is supported along the boundary of
\Sigma
.
Assuming that the symmetry operator is topological on the SRE state, there is another choice of symmetry operator
\widetilde{V}(\Sigma^{\prime};\mathcal{D})
supported at
\Sigma^{\prime}
, such that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle=\widetilde{V}(\Sigma^{\prime};\mathcal{D})\,|0\rangle~,\quad\Sigma\cap\Sigma^{\prime}\subset A~.

(18)

Now, take a complement region of
A
in the space and denote it by
A^{c}
. We write the projector onto the trivial state
\ket{0}
within
A^{c}
as
\Pi^{A^{c}}
. This projector is written in terms of projectors in the small regions,

\displaystyle\Pi^{A^{c}}=\prod_{L_{j}\in A^{c}}\Pi^{L_{j}}~,

(19)

where each region
L_{j}\subset A^{c}
is small enough so that
\Pi^{L_{j}}
commutes with either
\widetilde{V}(\Sigma;\mathcal{D})
or
\widetilde{V}(\Sigma^{\prime};\mathcal{D})
. Then, by using the expression
|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle=\widetilde{V}(\Sigma;\mathcal{D})\,|0\rangle=\widetilde{V}(\Sigma^{\prime};\mathcal{D})\,|0\rangle
, we get

\displaystyle\Pi^{A^{c}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle=|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle~,

(20)

implying that the defect state
|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle
has the form of
\ket{\psi}_{A}\otimes\ket{0}_{A^{c}}
, showing that the state factorizes into the state along the defect network and the trivial product state at the complement.

For the case of 0-form symmetry, when the space is a sphere
S^{d}
and
A
is a neighborhood of a
(d-1)
-sphere (an equator), one can assume the existence of
V(\Sigma^{\prime};\mathcal{D})
such that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a complement of
\Sigma
in the space, and
|{\mathcal{D}}\rangle=V(\Sigma;\mathcal{D})\,|0\rangle=V(\Sigma^{\prime};\mathcal{D})\,|0\rangle
is satisfied. Then the above argument from Eq. (
18
) is valid for 0-form symmetry as well.

III.2
Generalized statistics is trivial on factorized defect states

We now show that, assuming the form of the defect state (

III.2.1
When defects are points

We first consider the case when the defects are 0d point objects; the defect state
\ket{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}
has a local 0d state along the defect network,

\displaystyle\ket{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}=\ket{\psi}_{\mathcal{D}}\otimes\ket{0}~,

(21)

where
\ket{\psi}_{\mathcal{D}}
is regarded as a union of 0d local states localized at each defect.

For each 1d line operator support
\Sigma
of the sequential circuit
\widetilde{V}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
, we consider a partition of a line that separates
\Sigma
into small fractions
\{L_{r^{\prime}}\}

We decompose each sequential circuit into small circuits as

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})=\prod_{L_{r^{\prime}}}U_{L_{r^{\prime}}}~,

(22)

where we take an ordered product of non-commuting circuits
U_{L_{r^{\prime}}}
. Starting with a defect configuration
\mathcal{D},
the above sequential circuit transforms
\mathcal{D}
into
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
through intermediate defect configurations,
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\cdots\to\mathcal{D}_{n}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}

\ket{\psi}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{j}}

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{j}}=\bigotimes_{p}\ket{\psi}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{j}|_{p}}~,

(23)

where each state
\ket{\psi}_{{\mathcal{D}_{j}|}_{p}}
is supported at a point
p
where a defect is located.

Now, suppose we apply a circuit
U_{L_{r^{\prime}}}
to the state
\ket{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_j}
and evolve it into
\ket{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{j+1}}

Each circuit

acts on a pair of points at the boundary

by

\displaystyle U_{L_{r^{\prime}}}[\ket{\psi}_{\mathcal{D}_{j}\cap L_{r^{\prime}}}\otimes\ket{0}]=

(24)

\displaystyle\exp(i\theta(L_{r'}, a; \mathcal{D}_j|_{L_{r'}},\mathcal{D}_{j+1}|_{L_{r'}}))[\ket{\psi}_{\mathcal{D}_{j+1}\cap L_{r^{\prime}}}\otimes\ket{0}]~,

where

denotes the trivial product state of

that complements the defect state at the ends.
Here, once we fix the choice of each point state

,

acts by the phase
\theta(L_{r^{\prime}},a;\mathcal{D}_{j}|_{L_{r^{\prime}}},\mathcal{D}_{j+1}|_{L_{r^{\prime}}})

.

Due to the condition (
2
), a sequential circuit
\widetilde{V}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
can be decomposed into a form

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})=U^{\prime}U_{r}(a,(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})|_{D_{r}})U^{\prime\prime}~,

IV.3.2
Action of the local unitary on the defect states

Now we show that the above local unitary deforms the defect configurations. Let us consider the defect states
\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma}^{b}}
and
\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma\cup\hat\sigma^\prime}^{b}}
. Assume that these defect states do not include the other flux excitations. Since the flux excitation violates the flatness condition, the Hermitian operators act on these states as

\displaystyle{M}^{b}_{e,e^{\prime}}\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma}^{b}}

\displaystyle={B}_{f_{e,e^{\prime}}^{*}}\left({X}^{A}_{e^{\prime}}+{X}^{B}_{e^{\prime}}\right)\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma}^{b}}=\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma\cup\hat\sigma^\prime}^{b}},

(72)

\displaystyle{M}^{b}_{e,e^{\prime}}\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma\cup\hat\sigma^\prime}^{b}}

\displaystyle={B}_{\bar{f}_{e,e^{\prime}}}\left({X}^{A}_{e^{\prime}}+{X}^{B}_{e^{\prime}}\right)\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma\cup\hat\sigma^\prime}^{b}}=\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma}^{b}}.

\displaystyle\left({M}^{b}_{e,e^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma}^{b}}

\displaystyle=\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma}^{b}},

(74)

\displaystyle\left({M}^{b}_{e,e^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma\cup\sigma}^{b}}

\displaystyle=\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma\cup\sigma}^{b}}.

(75)

As a result, we obtain the expected actions of the local unitary on the defect states as

\displaystyle U^{b}_{e,e^{\prime}}\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma}^{b}}

\displaystyle=-i\left(\cos{\frac{\pi}{2}}+i\sin{\frac{\pi}{2}}M^{b}_{e,e^{\prime}}\right)\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma}^{b}}=\ket{\mathcal{D}_{\hat\Sigma\cup\hat\sigma^\prime}^{b}}.

(76)

IV.3.3
Construction of the sequential circuit

We then define

by assigning a strict ordering to the edges

as

\displaystyle V^{b}(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{\Sigma}_{1})=\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}{U}^{b}_{e_{k},e_{k+1}},

where the product is path-ordered to represent a step-by-step expansion of the surface defect as follows.
Let

be the unit open surface expanded by the

and

\hat{\Sigma}_{1}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N-1}\hat{\sigma}_{k}

for bosonic magnetic fluxes, together with the fermionic ones

in Eq. (

V
(3+1)D mixed topological order with a non-Abelian fermionic loop

In this section, we construct a new intrinsically mixed topological order in (3+1)D whose long-range entanglement is protected by non-Abelian fermionic loop statistics of the strong symmetry.

V.1
Review: (3+1)D mixed topological order with a single fermionic loop

Let us first review an intrinsically mixed topological order in (3+1)D with a single fermionic loop

We consider a cubic lattice in three spatial dimensions with a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
qudit residing on each edge

A convenient stabilizer Hamiltonian realizing the (3+1)D

(99)

Here
\widetilde{A}_{v}
and

are the standard
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
toric-code vertex and plaquette terms:

is a product

\widetilde{Z}_{e}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
gauge theory.

Within the stabilizer subspace of
\widetilde{X}_{e}^{2}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
1-form symmetry generated by the magnetic surface

(100)

where the product is over edges

of the primal lattice intersected (cut) by
\hat{\Sigma}

Meanwhile, one may define a distinct membrane operator that generates an emergent
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
1-form symmetry

\widetilde{S}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(\hat{\Sigma})\coloneq\prod_{e\subset\hat{\Sigma}}\widetilde{S}_{e},

built from local edge operators

We start with two copies of the imTO with a fermionic loop,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=\mathcal{N}\rho_{0}^{A}\otimes\mathcal{N}\rho_{0}^{B}\otimes\ket{0}\bra{0}^{C}~,

(105)

where the operator

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
symmetry swapping

\displaystyle G_{v}^{C}=A_{v}^{C}\text{SWAP}_{N(v)}^{{(AB)}}\text{SWAP}_{E(v)}^{{(AB)}}\text{SWAP}_{U(v)}^{{(AB)}}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

corresponds to gauging the weak
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

Let us consider the mixed state
\rho=\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\rho_{1}
with the maximal decoherence
p=1/2
. Then the decohered state
\rho
has a strong symmetry generated by a non-Abelian fermionic loop operator (

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{C}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

VI
Discussions

We close by highlighting several future directions. Although sequential-circuit invariants provide a transparent diagnostic for an important subclass of anomalies, they do not yet complete the definition of non-invertible ’t Hooft anomalies. While it is expected that the Berry phase invariants would capture a wide class of ’t Hooft anomalies including those corresponding to non-Abelian braiding invariants
44
;
40
;
47
or Frobenius-Schur indicators
79
;
21
;
37
, it is unlikely that e.g., the anomaly of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Kramers-Wannier duality is characterized by the Berry phase invariant.
It would be valuable to formulate a complete lattice anomaly theory for non-invertible symmetries, and to clarify which anomalies are detectable purely from sequential circuits.

Our construction of non-Abelian fermionic loops should generalize to higher-dimensional anyonic excitations. For example, consider a (4+1)D theory with symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{(1)}_{9}
, where the
\mathbb{Z}^{(1)}_{9}
1-form symmetry carries anomaly
\frac{2\pi}{9}B_{2}B_{2}B_{2}
with
B_{2}
the 2-form
{\mathbb{Z}}_{9}
background, which corresponds to nontrivial anyonic membrane statistics
29
.
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
then acts by the automorphism
n\mapsto 4n\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 9)
, which leaves the anomaly invariant. Gauging the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
symmetry then yields a non-Abelian anyonic membrane from the resulting coset 1-form symmetry. Exploring such extended operators and their non-Abelian statistics is an interesting direction for future work.

Appendix A
Invariance of Berry phases under local deformations

In this appendix, we establish the invariance of Berry phase invariants under possible local deformations. As described in the main text, the Berry phase is generally expressed as a sum of phase factors
\theta(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle\Theta=\sum_{a,\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\epsilon(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\theta(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})~,

(108)

with
\epsilon(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then, the necessary and sufficient condition for
\Theta
to define an invariant is expressed as a set of linear constraints satisfied by the integer coefficients
\epsilon
. Here we focus on the condition arising from the local deformations of the movement operators presented in Eq. (
9
) in the main text.

We require invariance under possible local deformations of a movement operator
V(\Sigma,\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
near its boundary. As described in the main text, we only consider the local deformations such that the locality of the deformation is preserved by the conjugation action of movement operators.

In general, we consider the deformation in the form of

\displaystyle V^{\prime}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})=O_{p}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1}|_{D_{r,p}},\mathcal{D}_{2}|_{D_{r,p}})V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})~,

(109)

with some local operator
O_{p}
supported at
\epsilon
-neighborhood of the point
p
within the boundary
\partial\Sigma
, such that the locality of
O_{p}
is preserved by generic movement operators
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}),V(\mathcal{D})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}(O_{p}):=V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})^{\dagger}O_{p}V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})~,\quad\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{D}}(O_{p}):=V(\mathcal{D})^{\dagger}O_{p}V(\mathcal{D})~,

(110)

is again a local operator at
p
.

Here,
O_{p}
can depend on the configurations of defects through those in the
r
-neighborhood of
p
. All movement operators
V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
with generic configurations
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
supported at
\Sigma
is modified by
O_{p}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}|_{D_{r,p}},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}|_{D_{r,p}})
; one can see that such modification is consistent with the constraint (
3
).

Accordingly, the states possibly get modified by a local unitary,

\displaystyle\ket{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}=U_{p}(\mathcal{D}|_{D_{r,p}})\ket{\mathcal{D}}~,

(111)

which can again depend on
\mathcal{D}
through its configuration in the
r
-neighborhood of
p

Let us study how the Berry phase

is shifted by these redefinitions.
The Berry phase
\theta^{\prime}
after the redefinition is given via

\displaystyle U_{2,p}^{\dagger}O_{p}V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})U_{1,p}\ket{\mathcal{D}_1}=e^{i\theta^{\prime}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})}\ket{\mathcal{D}_2}~,

(112)

where we wrote
U_{p}(\mathcal{D}_{i}|_{p})
as
U_{i,p}
.
By acting

on both sides of the above equation, we get

{}^{[\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}]}(U_{2,p}^{\dagger}O_{p})U_{1,p}\ket{\mathcal{D}_1}=e^{i\theta^{\prime}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})-i\theta(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})}\ket{\mathcal{D}_1}~,

(113)

where
{}^{[\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}]}O:=V(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})^{\dagger}OV(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
denotes conjugation action of
V

Recalling that
\ket{\mathcal{D}}=V(\mathcal{D})\ket{0}
we get

\displaystyle\bra{0}^{[\mathcal{D}_{1}]}(^{[\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}]}(U_{2,p}^{\dagger}O_{p})U_{1,p})\ket{0}=e^{i\theta^{\prime}(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})-i\theta(\Sigma,a;\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})}~,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},\\
