Odd-exponent blockers and a Mordell–Weil construction of Euler bricks
René Peschmann

Abstract
. A body cuboid is a rectangular parallelepiped whose three edges and three face diagonals are simultaneously integer; if its space diagonal is also integer, it is a perfect cuboid , the existence of which is open since the eighteenth century. We make two contributions to the study of body cuboids parametrised by two coprime Pythagorean pairs ( a , b ) (a,b) and ( m , n ) (m,n) in Euclid form ( Master-Hits ). The first contribution is a verified exponent-one blocker phenomenon . For every verified Master-Hit, the space-diagonal norm f 1 := ( W 1 ​ U 2 ) 2 + ( U 1 ​ V 2 ) 2 f_{1}:=(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2} admits a prime divisor ℓ \ell which appears to exponent exactly one and is coprime to a fixed list of 29 29 canonical expressions in the parameters. This is strictly stronger than the mere existence of an odd-exponent prime divisor: a prime of exponent 3 3 , 5 5 , … would already obstruct f 1 f_{1} from being a square, but would carry an additional square factor; the observed obstruction is always primitive. We verify the phenomenon without exception on the 151,575 151{,}575 Master-Hits of our database for which f 1 f_{1} has been fully factorised. We also show that two natural variants both fail: the largest outside-parameter prime need not be a blocker (three explicit counterexamples), and the smallest outside-parameter blocker need not have exponent 1 1 (several hundred explicit counterexamples). The second contribution uses the elliptic fibration of the Master-Hit variety over the ( m , n ) (m,n) -plane. For each fixed coprime pair ( m , n ) (m,n) , the Master-Hit equation defines a genus-one quartic H m , n H_{m,n} , from which an algorithmic quartic-to-Weierstrass normalisation produces an elliptic model E m , n E_{m,n} together with a rational function τ \tau on E m , n E_{m,n} that returns the value of t 2 t^{2} . Our generator enumerates bounded Mordell–Weil combinations on E m , n ​ ( ℚ ) E_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q}) , lifts precisely those points satisfying the quadratic condition τ ⁡ ( P ) ∈ ℚ > 0 □ \tau(P)\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{\square} back to admissible Euclid pairs ( a , b ) (a,b) , and certifies each result by exact integer arithmetic. Starting from a base of 61,829 61{,}829 primitive Master-Hits collected from classical exhaustive search, Rathbun’s catalogue, and the Saunderson generator, this produces 1,222,841 1{,}222{,}841 further Master-Hits over 411 411 fibres. None of the resulting 1,284,670 1{,}284{,}670 Master-Hits is a perfect cuboid (a finite, exact integer square check). All fully factorised records satisfy the exponent-one blocker phenomenon.

Two contributions.

This paper takes a structural-and-computational stance on body

Setup and notation

We use the Euclid parametrisation in master-tuple form, introduced

Notation.

We write
\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{\square}:=\{\,q\in\mathbb{Q}:q>0\text{ and }q=r^{2}\text{ for some }r\in\mathbb{Q}\,\}

for the set of positive rational squares; the algorithmic lifting

Let

and
(m,n)
be coprime pairs of positive integers with

a>b>0,\quad m>n>0,\quad a-b\text{ odd},\quad m-n\text{ odd}.

Write

U_{1}=a^{2}-b^{2},\quad V_{1}=2ab,\quad W_{1}=a^{2}+b^{2},

U_{2}=m^{2}-n^{2},\quad V_{2}=2mn,\quad W_{2}=m^{2}+n^{2}.

Then
(U_{i},V_{i},W_{i})
is a primitive Pythagorean triple with

U_{i}^{2}+V_{i}^{2}=W_{i}^{2}
. Note that
U_{i}
is odd (since

and

are odd,
U_{i}=(a-b)(a+b)
resp.
(m-n)(m+n)

product of two odd numbers in one factor and an even-difference
sum in the other),
V_{i}
is even (the explicit factor
2
), and

W_{i}
is odd. The associated body cuboid candidate has edges

(2)

x=U_{1}U_{2},\quad y=V_{1}U_{2},\quad z=U_{1}V_{2}.

Two of the three face diagonals are automatically integer:

\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}=W_{1}U_{2}
and
\sqrt{x^{2}+z^{2}}=U_{1}W_{2}
. The third

\sqrt{y^{2}+z^{2}}
is integer iff

(3)

M(a,b,m,n):=(V_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}

is a perfect square in
\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A tuple
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}
satisfying the coprimality and parity

is called a

. We write

for the set of Master-Hits.

The space-diagonal condition is governed by

A Master-Hit gives a

.

Remark 2.2
.

The integer
f_{1}
is the Gaussian norm of

\zeta:=W_{1}U_{2}+i\,U_{1}V_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
. Note that
W_{1}U_{2}
is odd
(both
W_{1}
and
U_{2}
are odd) and
U_{1}V_{2}
is even (since

2\mid V_{2}
); consequently
f_{1}=(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}
is
odd, and the prime
\ell=2
never divides
f_{1}
. Since

f_{1}=N(\zeta)
has
v_{\ell}(f_{1})=v_{\pi}(\zeta)+v_{\bar{\pi}}(\zeta)

for every split prime
\ell=\pi\bar{\pi}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
in
\mathbb{Z}[i]

we have

iff for every such
\ell
the two Gaussian

2
,

v_{\pi}(\zeta)\;\equiv\;v_{\bar{\pi}}(\zeta)\pmod{2}.

Primes
\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}
are inert in
\mathbb{Z}[i]
and automatically
appear with even exponent in any norm.

Remark 2.3

(Scope of the parametrisation)
.

Equation (
2
) produces a body cuboid from each Master-Hit.
We do not claim the converse, i.e. that every primitive body cuboid
arises from a Master-Hit. The class of body cuboids covered here is
the one constructible from two coprime Pythagorean pairs in the
standard Euclid form; this includes all infinite families known to
the author.

A rational prime

The basic obstruction is the equivalence

Conjecture

4.
Odd-exponent blocker conjecture and computational verification

4.1.
The conjecture and its computational verification

We state the central conjecture together with its main empirical
content as a computational theorem. We do not claim a proof of the
conjecture.

Conjecture 4.1

(Universal exponent-one blocker)
.

For every Master-Hit
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathcal{M}
there exists a prime

\ell\mid f_{1}
such that

(i)

v_{\ell}(f_{1})=1
, i.e.
\ell
divides
f_{1}
exactly once, and

(ii)

\gcd(\ell,N)=1
for every
N\in\mathcal{P}(a,b,m,n)
.

The principal computational result of this paper is the verification
of Conjecture
4.1
on a large finite dataset.

Verification 4.2

(Exponent-one blocker on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fact}}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fact}}
denote the set of Master-Hits in
our database for which
f_{1}
has been completely factorised, with
the stored factorisations re-verified by exact integer
multiplication. Then
|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fact}}|=151{,}575
,
and Conjecture
4.1
holds for every

(a,b,m,n)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fact}}
.

Proof.

The verification is entirely computational: for every record we
load the factorisation
f_{1}=\prod_{i}\ell_{i}^{e_{i}}
from the
database, identify the primes
\ell_{i}
with
e_{i}=1
, and check
that at least one such
\ell_{i}
is coprime to every element of

\mathcal{P}(a,b,m,n)
. The check is reproduced by the script

paper2/analysis/theorem_check.py
; see
Section
8
.
∎

4.2.
Why the exponent-one condition matters

A blocker is any prime divisor of
f_{1}
occurring to odd exponent,
hence primes of exponent
3,5,\ldots
would already obstruct

f_{1}
from being a square. The exponent-one condition is therefore
strictly stronger than the obstruction needed.

Remark 4.3

(Exponent one is not cosmetic)
.

The empirical phenomenon observed here is sharper than “
f_{1}
has
some odd-exponent prime divisor”. After removing all primes
supported by the canonical parameter list
\mathcal{P}(a,b,m,n)
,
the obstruction can in every verified case be chosen
primitive
,
i.e. with
v_{\ell}(f_{1})=1
. A blocker of exponent
3
, for
example, would factor as
\ell\cdot\ell^{2}
, contributing a genuine
asymmetric Gaussian prime
\ell
alongside a square companion

\ell^{2}
; the exponent-one condition removes the square companion.
The phenomenon thus says that
f_{1}
fails to be a square in the
most elementary possible way at a generic split prime, even in
large-parameter families where small prime values do appear.

In the language of the Gaussian factorisation

\zeta=W_{1}U_{2}+i\,U_{1}V_{2}
(Remark
4.11
),
Conjecture
4.1
asserts the existence of a Gaussian
prime
\pi
with
\pi\bar{\pi}=\ell
such that

v_{\pi}(\zeta)=1
,
v_{\bar{\pi}}(\zeta)=0
, and
\ell
coprime
to
\mathcal{P}
.

4.3.
Counterexamples to two natural variants

The exponent-one condition cannot be replaced by either “the

largest
outside-
\mathcal{P}
prime is a blocker” (a
strengthening) or “the
smallest
outside-
\mathcal{P}

blocker has exponent one” (an apparent intermediate strengthening
of the existence claim). Both fail empirically.

Remark 4.4

(Failure of the largest-prime strengthening)
.

The strict strengthening that requires the
largest
prime
divisor of
f_{1}
outside
\mathcal{P}
to be a blocker is false:
three explicit counterexamples are listed in
Appendix
B
. The exponent-one
blocker required by Conjecture
4.1
exists in each of
these hits, but is smaller in magnitude than some symmetric-exponent
prime.

Remark 4.5

(Failure of the smallest-blocker strengthening)
.

A different strengthening would assert that the
smallest

outside-
\mathcal{P}
blocker (the smallest prime
\ell\mid f_{1}

with
v_{\ell}(f_{1})
odd and
\ell
coprime to
\mathcal{P}
) has
exponent exactly
1
. This too fails: among the

151{,}575
records of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fact}}
, exactly

242
have a smallest outside-
\mathcal{P}
blocker of exponent
\geq 3
.
For instance, hit
200
with
(a,b,m,n)=(55,48,44,9)
has

13^{3}
as its smallest outside-
\mathcal{P}
prime of odd
exponent. In each of these
242
records, an exponent-one blocker
required by Conjecture
4.1
still exists, but is a

larger
prime than the smallest odd-exponent prime outside

\mathcal{P}
.

Remark 4.6

(Removing the exponent-one condition)
.

Replacing condition (i) of Conjecture
4.1
by the
weaker “
v_{\ell}(f_{1})
is odd” yields a conjecture that is
consistent with the data (since exponent
1
is a special case of
odd exponent), but does not isolate the same clean Gaussian-prime
obstruction. We do not adopt this weaker formulation; the
exponent-one statement is the sharper empirical phenomenon.

The basis form follows trivially from Conjecture
4.1
.

Conjecture 4.7

(Basis form)
.

Every Master-Hit has at least one blocker.

Corollary 4.8

(Conditional)
.

Assume Conjecture
4.7
. Then no Master-Hit is a perfect
cuboid; equivalently,
f_{1}\neq\square
for every
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

f_{1}=\square
is equivalent to
\mathrm{Blk}(a,b,m,n)=\emptyset
.
Conjecture
4.7
says

\mathrm{Blk}(a,b,m,n)\neq\emptyset
, contradicting
f_{1}=\square
.
∎

Verification 4.9

(Non-existence on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{all}}
)
.

For every Master-Hit
(a,b,m,n)
in our database

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{all}}
(
|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{all}}|=1{,}284{,}670
),
the integer
x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}
is not a perfect square. That is, no
Master-Hit in
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{all}}
is a perfect cuboid.

Proof.

For every record, integer
\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}
is computed by

math.isqrt
and squared back; equality is rejected in every
case. Reproduced quickly by

paper2/analysis/perfect_check.py

on all
1{,}284{,}670
records.
∎

4.4.
Gaussian anatomy of
f_{1}

The blocker set is most naturally read off the Gaussian factorisation
of

\zeta:=W_{1}U_{2}+i\,U_{1}V_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[i],

since
f_{1}=N(\zeta)=\zeta\bar{\zeta}
(Remark
2.2
).
For an odd rational prime
p
we set

\alpha_{p}:=v_{p}(W_{1}U_{2}),\qquad\beta_{p}:=v_{p}(U_{1}V_{2}),

the
p
-adic valuations of the two real summands of
f_{1}
.

Proposition 4.10

(
p
-adic asymmetry)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathcal{M}
. Then

(i)

if
\alpha_{p}\neq\beta_{p}
, then

v_{p}(f_{1})=2\min(\alpha_{p},\beta_{p})
;

(ii)

if
\alpha_{p}=\beta_{p}
, then
v_{p}(f_{1})\geq 2\alpha_{p}
.

Proof.

Standard
p
-adic estimate: writing
X=W_{1}U_{2}
,
Y=U_{1}V_{2}
,

f_{1}=X^{2}+Y^{2}
. If
v_{p}(X^{2})\neq v_{p}(Y^{2})
, then

v_{p}(X^{2}+Y^{2})=\min(v_{p}(X^{2}),v_{p}(Y^{2}))=2\min(\alpha_{p},\beta_{p})
;
this proves (i). If
v_{p}(X^{2})=v_{p}(Y^{2})=2\alpha_{p}
, the leading
term may cancel, so only the lower bound
v_{p}(f_{1})\geq 2\alpha_{p}

holds.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

A Gaussian prime
\pi
above a split prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}

contributes a blocker iff its exponents

v_{\pi}(\zeta),v_{\bar{\pi}}(\zeta)
are unequal modulo
2

after

descent to
\mathbb{Z}
. Equivalently, the prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
is a
blocker iff
\zeta
is
asymmetric
at
p
in
\mathbb{Z}[i]
. Inert
primes
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and the ramified
p=2
contribute only
even exponents to
f_{1}
and never block.

The Gaussian anatomy suggests an empirical exclusion principle for
the smallest split prime,
p=5
.

Remark 4.12

(
5
does not appear as an exponent-one blocker)
.

On the dataset
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fact}}

(Verification
4.2
), no Master-Hit satisfies

v_{5}(f_{1})=1
: every record with
5\mid f_{1}
has

v_{5}(f_{1})\geq 2
. This is a finite computational observation, not a
proof. A clean modular derivation from the Master condition

M=\square
together with
\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(m,n)=1
would
require a finer use of the Gaussian factorisation of
\zeta
than
what a naive case analysis modulo
5
delivers (the equation

\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\equiv 0\pmod{5}
has nontrivial isotropic
solutions, e.g.
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,2)
, so a direct argument is
not available). We do not pursue the question further; the
observation is included only because
5
is the smallest split prime
and the empirical exclusion is striking, and is not used elsewhere
in the paper.

4.5.
Canonical decomposition and Conjecture E1

A more powerful structural observation is that
f_{1}
admits a

canonical squareful/squarefree decomposition
that captures
all blockers in a single step.

Lemma 4.13

(Canonical decomposition)
.

Let
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathcal{M}
and set

g_{0}:=\gcd(W_{1}U_{2},\ U_{1}V_{2}),\qquad\xi:=\frac{W_{1}U_{2}}{g_{0}},\qquad\eta:=\frac{U_{1}V_{2}}{g_{0}}.

Then
\gcd(\xi,\eta)=1
and

(10)

f_{1}(a,b,m,n)\;=\;g_{0}^{2}\,(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}).

Proof.

Since
f_{1}=(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}
and
g_{0}\mid W_{1}U_{2}
,

g_{0}\mid U_{1}V_{2}
, we have
g_{0}^{2}\mid f_{1}
. Dividing through by

g_{0}^{2}
gives
f_{1}/g_{0}^{2}=\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}
with

\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\gcd(\xi,\eta)=1
by definition of
g_{0}
.
∎

The decomposition (
10
) is the cleanest reformulation
of the blocker condition for Master-Hits that we know.

Conjecture 4.14

(Conjecture E1)
.

For every Master-Hit
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathcal{M}
, the integer

\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}
obtained from Lemma
4.13
is not a
perfect square. Equivalently:
(\xi,\eta)
are never the legs of a
Pythagorean triple.

Proposition 4.15
.

Conjecture
4.14
implies Conjecture
4.7
(and hence
Corollary
4.8
).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.13
,
f_{1}=g_{0}^{2}(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})
, hence
for every prime
p

(11)

v_{p}(f_{1})\;=\;2\,v_{p}(g_{0})\;+\;v_{p}(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}),

so
v_{p}(f_{1})
has the same parity as
v_{p}(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})
. If

\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}\neq\square
, there is at least one prime
p
for
which
v_{p}(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})
is odd. Since
\gcd(\xi,\eta)=1
, every
such
p
is necessarily
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
(sums of two coprime
squares cannot be divisible to an odd power by
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}

or by
p=2
), and by (
11
) also
v_{p}(f_{1})
is
odd. Hence
p
is a blocker of the Master-Hit, and

\mathrm{Blk}(a,b,m,n)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

The reduction is striking: the entire blocker phenomenon collapses to
the question whether the coprime pair
(\xi,\eta)
of explicit
polynomial expressions in
a,b,m,n
can ever be a Pythagorean
leg pair.

Computational determination of the exponent-one blockers

For Master-Hits in which every blocker has

-valuation exactly

(by far the dominant case in our database; cf. §

),
Lemma
4.13
reduces blocker identification to an

itself. Let
\mathrm{rf}=\prod_{\ell\in\mathrm{Blk}(a,b,m,n)}\ell

denote the squarefree product of the blocker primes. If every blocker
has valuation

in

, then

is a perfect

gives a closed form for it.

Proposition 4.18

(
g_{0}
exposes the blockers)
.

Let
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathcal{M}
have only blockers of valuation
1
, and
write
f_{1}=\mathrm{rf}\cdot h^{2}
with

. Then there is an

with

(12)

.

By Lemma

(Disjointness of the

\displaystyle A-C

\displaystyle=2bn,

\displaystyle B+D

\displaystyle=2an,

4.6.
Proof strategy for Conjecture
4.1

Conjecture
4.1
combines an
existence
statement
(a blocker exists, Conjecture
4.7
) with two further
requirements: that some blocker lie outside
\mathcal{P}
, and that
its
f_{1}
-valuation be exactly
1
. We outline an approach for each.

Existence of a blocker.

By Lemma
4.13
and Proposition
4.15
,
the existence of a blocker is equivalent to Conjecture
4.14
:
the coprime pair

\xi=\frac{W_{1}U_{2}}{\gcd(W_{1}U_{2},U_{1}V_{2})},\qquad\eta=\frac{U_{1}V_{2}}{\gcd(W_{1}U_{2},U_{1}V_{2})}

is never a Pythagorean leg pair. The natural approach is to combine
the master condition
M=\square
(which couples
a,b,m,n
via the
quartic
H_{m,n}
) with the hypothetical pythagoreity of

(\xi,\eta)
and derive a contradiction with the coprimality
conditions
\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(m,n)=1
. Concretely,

\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}=w^{2}
together
with
M=\square
would yield two simultaneous Gaussian-square
identities for
\zeta:=W_{1}U_{2}+i\,U_{1}V_{2}
and

z^{\prime}:=V_{1}U_{2}+i\,U_{1}V_{2}
, and we expect a height/discriminant
estimate on
E_{m,n}
to rule them out. The argument is delicate
because the discriminant of
E_{m,n}
is explicit but not
factor-controllable for general
(m,n)
; this is where the proof
currently stalls.

Existence of an exponent-one blocker outside
\mathcal{P}
.

Granted that some blocker exists, Conjecture
4.1
adds two
requirements that must hold
simultaneously
for a single witness
prime
\ell
: namely (i)
v_{\ell}(f_{1})=1
and (ii)
\ell
is coprime

5.
The Mordell–Weil generator

We describe an algorithm that takes a coprime pair
(m,n)
of
positive integers with
m>n>0
and
m-n
odd, together with a
positive scalar bound
K
, and produces a finite list of Master-Hits
with that
(m,n)
. The algorithm performs three steps. We label it

and refer to it as the

Mordell–Weil generator
.

Step 1.

Quartic to Weierstrass.
Compute
U_{2}=m^{2}-n^{2}
and

V_{2}=2mn
. Form the quartic
H_{m,n}
of (
6
).
Apply PARI’s
ellfromeqn
to the polynomial

At^{4}+Bt^{2}+C-s^{2}
to obtain Weierstrass coefficients

a_{2},a_{4},a_{6}
and the rational map
\phi_{m,n}
of
Section
3.1
. The resulting curve is denoted

E_{m,n}
.

Step 2.

Mordell–Weil generators.
Attempt to compute generators

P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}
of
E_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})
modulo torsion using Sage’s

E.gens()
with second descent at limit
15
. If

E.gens()
fails to terminate within a fixed time, fall back
to a
database lift
: for every Master-Hit
(a,b)
over

(m,n)
already in the database, lift
(a,b)
to a point of

E_{m,n}(\mathbb{Q})
via (
8
), and use the resulting (possibly
non-basis) collection of points as
P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}
.

Step 3.

Scalar enumeration and certification.
Let

T_{1},\ldots,T_{t}
enumerate the rational torsion of
E_{m,n}
. For
each
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})\in\{-K,\ldots,K\}^{r}\setminus\{0\}
and
each
T_{j}
, form
R:=c_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}P_{r}+T_{j}
in

. Compute
\tau(R)
via (
9
); if undefined
(i.e.
R\in\{O\}\cup\mathcal{T}_{\tau}

\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}

or zero, discard

2.1

(a,b,m,n)
which

Theorem 5.1

(Correctness of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{MW}}
)
.

Every tuple output by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{MW}}
is a Master-Hit.

Proof.

The output is the set of
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}
for which all the
checks of Step 3 pass. The check

is
exactly the Master-Hit defining condition (Definition
2.1
),
and the parity, positivity, and coprimality conditions are the rest
of the definition. The lift via Lemma
3.1

guarantees that the

so produced indeed corresponds to a

proof: every output is verified by exact integer arithmetic against
the Master-Hit defining condition.

6.1.
The semi-scaled
(g_{+},g_{-})
-fibration and single-blocker hits

The family tags of Table
4
are tied to closed-form
generators with finite parameter bounds, so a Master-Hit produced by
the same algebraic mechanism as Saunderson but outside the
generator’s reach receives no Saunderson family tag. A coarser invariant
that ignores the bound captures the algebraic mechanism directly:
for
(a,b,m,n)\in\mathcal{M}
define

g_{+}\;:=\;\gcd(am+bn,\;an+bm),\qquad g_{-}\;:=\;\gcd\bigl(|am-bn|,\;|an-bm|\bigr).

Lemma 6.1

(Structural identity for
(g_{+},g_{-})
)
.

For every Master-Hit
(a,b,m,n)
:

g_{+}\cdot g_{-}\;=\;\gcd(a^{2}-b^{2},\;m^{2}-n^{2}),\qquad\gcd(g_{+},\,g_{-})\;=\;1,\qquad g_{\pm}^{2}\;\bigm|\;f_{1}.

Proof.

Set
A=am+bn
,
B=an+bm
,
C=am-bn
,
D=an-bm
, so

g_{+}=\gcd(A,B)
and
g_{-}=\gcd(|C|,|D|)
, and write

g:=\gcd(a^{2}-b^{2},\,m^{2}-n^{2})=\gcd(U_{1},U_{2})
.

Step 1:
g_{\pm}\mid g
.

The four linear combinations

mA-nB=aU_{2},\quad nA-mB=-bU_{2},\quad aA-bB=mU_{1},\quad bA-aB=-nU_{1}

show
g_{+}\mid aU_{2},\,bU_{2},\,mU_{1},\,nU_{1}
.
Since
\gcd(a,b)=1
, the first two yield
g_{+}\mid U_{2}
;
since
\gcd(m,n)=1
, the last two yield
g_{+}\mid U_{1}
, hence

g_{+}\mid g
. The analogous combinations for
C,D

(
mC-nD=aU_{2}
,
nC-mD=bU_{2}
,
aC+bD=mU_{1}
,

bC+aD=nU_{1}
) give
g_{-}\mid g
.

Step 2:
\gcd(g_{+},g_{-})=1
.

Since
a-b
and
m-n
are odd by Definition
2.1
,
exactly one of each pair
\{A,B\}
and
\{C,D\}
is even and the
other odd, so both
g_{+}
and
g_{-}
are odd. Suppose an odd prime

p
divided both. Then
p\mid A,B,C,D
, so

p\mid A+C=2am
,
p\mid B+D=2an
,
p\mid A-C=2bn
,

p\mid B-D=2bm
. As
p
is odd,
p
divides
am,an,bm,bn
.
Combining
\gcd(m,n)=1
with
p\mid am
and
p\mid an
gives

p\mid a
; combining
\gcd(m,n)=1
with
p\mid bm
and
p\mid bn

gives
p\mid b
, contradicting
\gcd(a,b)=1
.

Step 3:
g_{+}g_{-}=g
(the converse direction).

Step 1 gives
g_{+}g_{-}\mid g\cdot g=g^{2}
, but combined with

\gcd(g_{+},g_{-})=1
from Step 2 it actually gives
g_{+}g_{-}\mid g
.
For equality we show that every odd prime power
p^{e}\mid g
divides
exactly one of
g_{+},g_{-}
. Pick odd prime
p
with
p^{e}\,\|\,g
.
Since
\gcd(a,b)=1
, the entire
p
-part of

U_{1}=(a-b)(a+b)
sits in exactly one of the factors, so

a\equiv\varepsilon b\pmod{p^{e}}
with
\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}
;
analogously
m\equiv\delta n\pmod{p^{e}}
with
\delta\in\{+1,-1\}
.
Reducing mod
p^{e}
:

•

If
\varepsilon=\delta
:

C=am-bn\equiv\delta bn-bn=(\varepsilon\delta-1)bn\equiv 0

and
D=an-bm\equiv 0
, hence
p^{e}\mid g_{-}
.

•

If
\varepsilon=-\delta
:

A=am+bn\equiv-\delta bn+bn=(-\varepsilon\delta+1)bn\equiv 0

and
B=an+bm\equiv 0
, hence
p^{e}\mid g_{+}
.

Since
g
is odd (both
U_{1},U_{2}
are products of two odd numbers),
multiplying over all primes
p\mid g
gives
g\mid g_{+}g_{-}
.
Combined with
g_{+}g_{-}\mid g
this yields
g_{+}g_{-}=g
.

Step 4:
g_{\pm}^{2}\mid f_{1}
.

From Step 1,
g_{\pm}\mid U_{1}
and
g_{\pm}\mid U_{2}
. Hence

g_{\pm}\mid W_{1}U_{2}
and
g_{\pm}\mid U_{1}V_{2}
, so

g_{\pm}^{2}\mid(W_{1}U_{2})^{2}+(U_{1}V_{2})^{2}=f_{1}
.
∎

We call
(g_{+},g_{-})
the
semi-scaled fibration coordinates
.
The pair partitions Master-Hits into four disjoint strata:

(1,1)
,
(r,1)
with
r>1
,
(1,r)
with
r>1
, and “both
>\!1
”.
We say a hit is
strictly semi-scaled
if

\min(g_{+},g_{-})\;=\;1\quad\text{and}\quad\max(g_{+},g_{-})\;>\;1,

i.e. it lies in
\{(r,1):r>1\}\cup\{(1,r):r>1\}
.
The Saunderson generator produces strictly semi-scaled hits by
construction; the
(1,1)
- and “both
>\!1
”-strata are excluded.

Empirically, every single-blocker hit is strictly semi-scaled. The
converse fails: many strictly semi-scaled hits carry several
blockers. We therefore conjecture only the forward implication.

Conjecture 6.2

(Single-blocker hits are strictly semi-scaled)
.

Every primitive Master-Hit
(a,b,m,n)
with exactly one
exponent-one blocker satisfies

\min(g_{+},\,g_{-})\;=\;1\quad\text{and}\quad\max(g_{+},\,g_{-})\;>\;1.

(Single-blocker verification of Conjecture
6.2
)
.

On
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fact}}
, all

97
of them lie in the stratum
(r,1)
with
r>1

and
175
in the stratum
(1,r)
with
r>1
; none is in the

(1,1)
- or “both
>\!1
”-strata. We make no converse claim:
the strictly semi-scaled stratum also contains many multi-blocker
records.

Verification.

For each single-blocker record we computed
(g_{+},g_{-})
from

(a,b,m,n)
and verified that
\min(g_{+},g_{-})=1
and

\max(g_{+},g_{-})>1

such
records. The reproducibility script is

.

For every record in

quantities satisfy the elementary identities

\displaystyle x=U_{1}U_{2},\quad y=V_{1}U_{2},\quad z=U_{1}V_{2},\qquad g_{\mathrm{scale}}=\gcd(x,y,z),

\displaystyle(x_{\mathrm{prim}},y_{\mathrm{prim}},z_{\mathrm{prim}})=(x,y,z)/g_{\mathrm{scale}},
