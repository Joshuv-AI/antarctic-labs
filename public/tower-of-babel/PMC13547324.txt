An algorithm for optimizing psychological regulation strategies for college students based on image recognition and reinforcement learning
Authors: Panpan He, Shouyang Yu, Jingjing Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Intelligent and adaptable psychological management systems are needed due to the growing academic pressure and emotional problems experienced by college students. Traditional methods, relying on self-reported questionnaires and manual counselling, are often subjective and lack real-time flexibility. The research aims to develop an algorithm for optimizing psychological regulation strategies using image recognition and reinforcement learning, incorporating a Weighted Butterfly-Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (WB-TwinD3PG) Model. Emotional and psychological data are obtained from a publicly available dataset containing around 10,000 records with more than 20 features representing diverse psychological states. Two pre-processing methods are applied: face detection and alignment to separate and normalize facial regions, and illumination normalization to minimize lighting variations. For feature extraction, Local Binary Patterns (LBP) are employed to extract discriminative texture-based features indicative of emotional states, such as stress, anxiety, neutrality, and relaxation. The reinforcement learning module engages WB-TwinD3PG to dynamically optimize psychological regulation strategies. This advanced algorithm develops an exploration-exploitation balance, accelerates convergence, and increases policy stability by integrating WB-inspired optimization with TwinD3PG. The agent selects personalized interventions, including relaxation exercises, cognitive prompts, and motivational feedback, based on emotional state feedback. The combination of WB-TwinD3PG enables adaptive, robust, and personalized mental health support for college students. According to experimental data, the suggested model outperformed traditional techniques in terms of recall (0.921), F1-score (0.927), precision (0.934), and accuracy (0.913). Overall, this research provides a practical framework that combines image-based emotion analysis with advanced reinforcement learning for psychological well-being management.

Introduction

The overall pressure of college life is becoming a global issue owing to the growing academic pressure, social pressure, and the rapid changes in life. Some of the emotional issues that college students experience include stress and anxiety, mental instability, and adjustment issues in unfamiliar surroundings. These emotional issues may lead to major damage to the psychological health and academic success of college students when unattended1. The traditional psychological assessment practices in colleges are based on self-reporting, routine therapy, and manual observation. These are useful methods that are subjective, rare, and incapable of providing individualized emotional support at the moment2,3.

Current developments in artificial intelligence (AI) and computer vision have made it possible to monitor emotions continuously and objectively. A non-intrusive method of identifying emotional states, including stress, anxiety, neutrality, and relaxation, is facial expression analysis4,5. Image-based emotion identification systems can offer prompt insights into a student’s psychological state by examining small changes in facial texture and micro-expressions. Nevertheless, most of the current research on emotion identification stops at emotion detection and concentrates solely on classification accuracy, failing to connect the identified emotional states to adaptive psychological therapies6,7. A remote emergency mental medicine system that makes use of a psychiatric state prediction model is described in the invention. It consists of an acquisition unit that gathers personal information and real-time mental health symptoms, a prediction unit that uses this information to predict the psychiatric state, and a transmission unit that transmits the anticipated psychotic mental state8,9. Under these circumstances, difficult emotions could be felt by college students for a long time, and negative emotional states like aloneness, concern, depression, and fear can surface. There might be more dire repercussions if it fails to give notice and make the necessary adjustments quickly10. Rapid economic growth has brought about significant changes in society, and these changes have been accompanied by several pressures, including those related to life, work, development, and education. Half of society attends college. College students encounter social, graduation, academic, and life challenges as the leading members of the next generation of society11.

In ever-changing decision-making environments, in which optimal approaches need to be discovered by interacting with the system and receiving feedback, reinforcement learning (RL) has shown considerable potential. Reinforcement learning can be utilized by personalizing the strategies of psychological regulation by identifying the best approach to a particular individual with the aid of intervention, e.g., relaxation strategies, motivational stimuli, or cognitive reappraisal tasks12,13. However, challenges of unreliable learning, slow convergence, and Q-value estimation are likely to arise with the traditional reinforcement learning algorithms and thus are ineffective in more sophisticated applications of these algorithms (such as psychological regulation)14. While progress has been made in facial expression recognition and reinforcement learning, little is known about their combination for adaptive psychological regulation and the impact of emotion recognition accuracy on intervention outcomes. Emotional states of college students vary and are difficult to understand through traditional approaches. The challenge is approached as a multi-stage decision-making process where emotional states are detected through facial expressions and adaptive psychological interventions are selected, and a reinforcement learning approach is used to learn strategies to enhance psychological well-being.

To overcome these challenges, the research suggests a smart and adaptable psychological regulatory system combining a steady reinforcement learning algorithm and real-time face emotion recognition. Face detection, alignment, and illumination normalization of face images are done to guarantee desired feature extraction using both controlled and natural campus setups. This is followed by the extraction of discriminative facial texture attributes associated with emotional faces using Local Binary Patterns (LBP). The identified emotional states create the basis of a reinforcement learning agent that dynamically chooses personalized psychological interventions as environmental feedback.

The WB-TwinD3PG method is introduced to enhance the stability of learning and the efficiency of exploration. The stability advantage of Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3), including the use of two critic networks and delayed actor updates, is integrated with the global search ability of Weighted Butterfly Optimization in the hybrid model. The result is a more robust policy learning method that reduces bias in overestimation and balances exploration and exploitation. The model finally learns the optimal intervention strategies that promote emotional stability and reduce negative psychological experiences from continuous interaction with the input of emotional states.

Research objective and key contributions

The research seeks to combine cutting-edge RL technology with face expression detection to create an intelligent, dynamic system for psychological regulation among college students. Image analysis is utilized to monitor the emotional state in real time, while the WB-TwinD3PG algorithm is utilized to optimize customized intervention strategies. The goals endeavor were set as to promote emotional well-being, to aid students in the management of their stressors, and to supply evidence-based mental health intervention.

Recent literature points to the roles of AI, deep learning and reinforcement learning in emotional identification and adaptive therapies, based on increasing mental health concerns among college students. Studies on various positive effects including those of exercise, meditation and emotion regulation were found to have small sample sizes and are typically performed under strict control or with subjective measures.

The study15 explored the potential of AI-assisted psychotherapy in the context of the alarming rise in college students’ mental distress. Using a dataset from public resources, descriptive statistical analysis was employed to examine symptoms of fear, depression, knowledge use, social support, life quality etc. The results indicated that many emotional distress conditions occur frequently and over use of technology correlates with poor psychological symptoms. For adaptive treatments, a customized AI framework had been set out. However, generalizability and real-time emotional accuracy are constrained using self-reported measures and secondary data. Considering the increased interest in adaptive education16, investigated emotion-aware learning powered by AI. Using cameras, microphones, and biometric sensors, the data of 500 students was collected. Deep reinforcement learning was then used for pre-processing and modelling. Through 10-fold cross-validation, the system demonstrated a considerable improvement in emotion recognition correctness, real-time flexibility, and learning customization. However, scalability and real-world applicability across many educational contexts could be limited by reliance on sensor-based data, possible privacy issues, and controlled-environment testing.

Research17 suggested a dual-branch deep learning model that combines active and stable facial appearance analysis to address the requirement for quick, objective mental health evaluation in higher education. While Mobile Vision Transformer (MobileViT) gathers static peak-frame data and fuses them through cross-attention, a sliding-window Transformer with optical flow records motion characteristics. The model producedfast response time, high F1 and AUC, and 94.5% accuracy. The system depends on both laboratory testing and facial recognition data which decreases its ability to handle different real-world conditions. Facial expression recognition enables people to comprehend human emotions which applies to fields such as communication analysis and psychiatric evaluation. A deep learning (DL) system for recognition of four emotions: angry, sad, happy, and neutral18 has been developed. The system combines Faster Region-based Convolutional Neural Network (R-CNN) technology with mini-Extreme Inception (Xception) technology. The results of the comparison test indicate that mini-Xception outperforms R-CNN. The model requires improvement because it relies on controlled datasets and only handles basic emotional responses which results in difficulties with advanced emotional responses and unpredictable situations.

Based on Situational Learning Theory, the study19 looked at how AI-assisted learning affected undergraduate students’ academic uncertainty, motivation, and emotion control. A experimental setup including seventy-eight Chinese pupils was carried out. Data privacy, ethics, and context-dependent variations continue to be the most difficult parts of a big project rollout. AI group analyses revealed less unclear emotions, more control, and greater motivation among students. However, its generalizability was constrained by the small number of subjects, data from just one institution, and somewhat brief intervention length. Rising rates of mental health issues among college students have called for continual, data-driven evaluation not sufficiently covered by standard questionnaire scales20. AI and machine learning techniques utilizing multimodal data such as behavioral characteristics, physiological signals and social interactions have been developed in order to imitate fluctuating psychological conditions. Adaptive and real-time intervention strategies customized to individual needs were developed through computational analyses. The results indicated high customizability potential and accuracy in detecting. However, data privacy, ethics and context specific variability remain the biggest challenges of large-scale implementation of the system (Table 1).

Research gaps

The basic assumptions used here is that the facial expression is indeed the correct representation of the underlying emotion, which may not be true, and then applied on the small-scale, single class room data. The hardware limitations and influences of environment limit the scalability and the accuracy. It’s also heavily context-and language-dependent, thus might generate biased or wrongly interpreted conclusions. Besides, there is a lack of multi-modal validation that the detected signs of stress or emotion do truly represent the psychological status of students. Also, both approaches cannot adapt in real time. This WB-TwinD3PG algorithm integrates adaptive learning-based therapy with real time facial emotion recognition, which in contrast to static detection-only models can offer higher reliability, better scalability and more beneficial psychological assistance in closed-loop by means of continuous emotion tracking, adaptively evolving strategies, and dynamically responsive control.

Image-based emotion recognition

Image-based emotion detection, a computer vision method, automatically identifies human emotional states by examining facial images. To ensure an accurate depiction of facial expressions, the procedure usually entails face identification, alignment, illumination normalization, and feature extraction. Then, using machine learning or deep learning models, texture or deep characteristics are categorized into distinct emotional categories, such as tension, anxiety, neutrality, or relaxation. This technology functions as a crucial perception module in adaptive human-centered intelligent systems and permits non-intrusive affect monitoring (Fig. 2).

Data acquisition

The student psychological regulation dataset is a tabular dataset sourced from Kaggle (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ziya07/student-emotional-and-psychological-state-dataset/data) that aims to facilitate studies in psychological and mental health analytics, emotion recognition, educational data mining, and smart intervention systems for college students. It generates realistic student behavior, academic and emotional data for exploring machine learning models for stress, anxiety, and psychological state detection and improvement, and reinforcement learning models for adaptive psychological strategy optimization. The dataset includes 10,000 student records and 25 features representing student demographic, academic, lifestyle, physiological, psychological, and intervention-related information. It can be used to train machine learning models for prediction, classification, clustering, recommendation, and reinforcement learning-based adaptive strategies for mental health, for applications including stress and anxiety prediction, student well-being, adaptive personalized interventions, emotion-aware learning systems, and data visualization. It is divided into 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% testing to learn a model and assess its performance.

Image pre-processing and feature extraction

Image pre-processing refers to standardization of facial image by locating, aligning and normalizing faces and extracting only relevant face regions and removing irrelevant noisy features by means of standardizing light, background or image scale which help to guarantee high quality and consistency input data required by accurate and reliable emotion recognition models.

Face detection and alignment

To make the process of facial feature extraction easy and accurate, Face recognition and alignment become a significant pre-processing step. First, robust face identification algorithms are used to accurately and reliably detect facial regions regardless of pose even with changes in posture, expression and occlusion in all images. Then, key geometrical correspondences are derived between samples by mapping significant landmarks on the faces such as nose, eyes, and mouth corners. Finally, the intra-subject variability is reduced by normalizing the location, pose, and size of faces, transforming them to a standard canonical coordinate system using alignment algorithms. The normalization can improve the precision of emotion recognition as well as performance of the learning based psychological control mechanisms since further feature extraction processes, such as texture or expression analysis are able to operate in geographically coherent regions.

Feature extraction using local binary patterns (LBP)

LBP is a texture feature extraction technique that describes facial expressions using local intensity information around pixels. LBP is selected due to its simplicity, efficiency, illumination invariance, and effectiveness in capturing local texture variations in the face, which are suitable for discriminating subtle emotional states in facial analysis. It compares the intensity of each pixel with its surrounding pixels, and assigns binary values based on whether the neighbor’s intensity is greater or less than the central pixel, resulting in a concise texture descriptor. LBP is invariant to illumination conditions and captures fine-scale patterns like edges, wrinkles, and facial muscles in areas such as the mouth, eyes, and eyebrows, and can be used to distinguish emotional states, including stress, anxiety, neutral, and relaxation. Reinforcement learning for strategy optimization

The LBP features are integrated with psychological and behavioural identifiers to define a state that is used in the RL model for intervention. An ideal psychological intervention strategy that decreases negative emotional states and encourages stable or pleasant states is learned through RL. The student-system interaction is depicted as a sequential decision-making process, identical to control systems. The reinforcement learning agent is implemented in a virtual environment where each record is a state, which consists of emotional, psychological, behavioral, and physiological features. Time sequences are created from emotional development, psychological state transitions, and intervention results, allowing for controlled learning and human-like responses.

WB-twinD3PG algorithm integration

WB-TwinD3PG combines TwinD3PG with WB to select personalized psychological interventions. TD3 leverages the actor–critic architecture for an action-value policy that maps the emotional feature representation to continuous intervention actions, and twin critic networks to estimate long-term intervention quality, avoiding overestimation and enhancing stability. Target policy smoothing and delayed policy updates accelerate convergence in the presence of noisy and nonlinear emotional feature dynamics, while experience replay allows for learning from diverse emotional feature transitions. WB-TwinD3PG presents a novel hybrid RL-optimization approach that is different from traditional RL-metaheuristic hybrid methods in that it embeds the WB optimization within the action space of the TD3 actor. In contrast to other hybrid approaches that rely on metaheuristics for external hyperparameter tuning or offline policy analysis, the proposed approach introduces action-level perturbations and subsequent refinement, thus facilitating exploration of decision space rather than parameter space. The WB optimization module provides population-based perturbations around the actor’s actions to generate intervention candidates that are evaluated by reward. A weighted combination of exploration and exploitation facilitates broad search during early training phases, followed by fine-tuning in later phases. The perturbations are exclusively applied to the actor, ensuring TD3 stability while increasing exploration diversity, convergence speed, and adaptability of psychological interventions. The integrated design thus improves exploration diversity, convergence, and robust, adaptive psychological intervention policies that are suitable for dynamic mental states.

Twin delayed deep deterministic policy (TwinD3PG)

Because of their manual or rule-based character, traditional psychological therapies have a limited capacity to adjust to shifting emotional dynamics. By contrast, RL enables continuous and personalized control based on moment-to-moment emotional input. However, typical value-based RL methods such Q-learning often struggle with Q-value overestimation, therefore compromising stable policy learning. The proposed framework overcomes this by ensuring consistent and trustworthy learning in tasks requiring continuous psychological regulation by employing the Twin-Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) algorithm as the primary element of the WB-TwinD3PG model.

TD3PG is the reinforcement learning algorithm to learn optimal psychological intervention strategies based on emotional states. TD3PG builds on deterministic policy gradient algorithms by using two critic networks to minimize value overestimation, allowing for more reliable learning of the effectiveness of interventions. Actor network delay in updates ensures the critics are better converged before the policy is updated, enhancing stability in emotionally rich settings. Target policy smoothing also smooths out the impact of minor changes in action choices, ensuring greater stability in intervention decisions under emotional uncertainty. TD3PG maps the current emotional status of students to the best psychological interventions to select actions that avoid stress and anxiety and maintain stable emotional states.

Deep neural networks are used by the off-policy actor-critic algorithm TD3 to choose the best course of action. In the research, the actor network generates a continuous psychological intervention strategy (e.g., relaxation intensity, motivational prompt level) based on the emotional state vector obtained from facial expression analysis. In terms of long-term emotional improvement, the critic networks assess the quality (Q-value) of the chosen intervention. Two opponents lessen Q-value overestimation in the twin-critic actor-critic paradigm depicted in Fig. 4. Critics provide step-by-step updates, the actor learns through policy gradients, and stability is enhanced by delayed actor updates with target critics.

Weighted butterfly (WB) optimization

WB is a population-based global optimization approach that is integrated with the TD3 algorithm to improve policy learning for intervention selection. WB mimics butterfly foraging, with candidate solutions as different policy perturbations in the action space. It enhances exploration by producing multiple policy perturbation candidates and assessing them based on the reward feedback from the emotional state transition. The weighting mechanism strikes a balance between exploration and exploitation by promoting diversity in search in the beginning, and convergence to high-reward solutions in the end. WB avoids premature convergence, enhances stability, and works with TD3 to help the actor learn more effective and resilient intervention strategies. Existing algorithms in facial expression identification29] and [30 are often evaluated using small, controlled data, and this prevents both scalability and robustness in real-world academic contexts. Facial expressions are not always accurate indicators of internal psychological conditions; nor do they always respond to changes in the light, nor are they resistant to differences in individual expressions. The psychological stress detection methods under text may introduce some bias and misconception as they depend greatly on the quality of language, context, and the desire of the person to reveal their emotions. Neither of the two approaches has real-time flexibility, multimodal validation, and closed-loop systems to provide individualized psychological treatment, and both are mainly focused on the establishment of emotional or stress states. The proposed WB-TwinD3PG model can be used to achieve continuous emotion tracking and psychological regulation via image-based perception and reinforcement learning. It also provides credible, and personalized intervention alternatives through a consistent closed-loop optimization procedure.

The WB-TwinD3PG system performs real-time operation through its ability to process continuous video streams, which include facial frame extraction, LBP feature extraction, subsequent construction of emotional psychological states for immediate intervention generation through its TD3-based actor network, while the system maintains low inference latency, high resistance to various pose lighting, and occlusion changes during continuous psychological monitoring in campus settings.

Conclusion

The research suggested a smart and adaptable system of psychological regulation that had evolved reinforcement learning and visual image detection of emotion to improve the mental well-being of college students. The suggested framework was effective in integrating emotional recognition and individualized intervention decision-making by integrating the proposed WB-TwinD3PG algorithm, and effective face features extraction using Local binary patterns. The emotion recognition module was able to identify the main states of psychology, such as stress, anxiety, neutrality, and relaxation, in other environmental conditions. The learning agent of reinforcement utilized such emotional insights as dynamic feedback to maximize the psychological regulating strategies.

The TwinD3PG framework was much better at exploration, policy stability, and convergence rate with the Weighted Butterfly mechanism. The proposed model outperformed the conventional approach in every measure of evaluation, such as precision (0.934), accuracy (0.913), F1-score (0.927), and recall (0.921). These findings demonstrate the efficiency of hybrid optimization of tasks associated with the complexity and sensitivity of psychological intervention.

Further, the system is not based on fixed or general support mechanisms, and it can instead be adjusted to the needs of individual students through the closed-loop interaction between monitoring of the emotional state and strategy choice. Consequently, the system has been especially adapted to scalable and real-time mental health applications in learning institutions. Finally, the paper offers a practical, state-of-the-art solution to smart psychological support, emphasizing the opportunities of pairing computer vision with reinforcement learning to develop data-driven, responsive mental health support systems of the intelligent campuses of the future.

Limitation and future scope

Although effective, challenges include facial expressions that may not always reflect actual psychological states, being sensitive to lighting and occlusions, relying on sufficient interaction data for personalization, data bias, discrete emotion model simplification, noisy facial/physiological signals, short-term reward design, and practical deployment issues (privacy, ethics, consent, and lack of generalization to varied scenarios). Future research can explore the use of real-world multimodal data (audio, physiological, behavioral), continuous psychological state modeling, long-term reward design, lightweight, privacy-preserving, and real-time deployable intervention systems.
