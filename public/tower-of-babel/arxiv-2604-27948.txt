Rational characteristic classes of bundles with fibre a product of spheres
Jan McGarry-Furriol

Abstract
We prove the existence of many non-trivial characteristic classes of smooth oriented bundles with fibre a product S n × S n \mathrm{S}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{n} of odd-dimensional spheres. We do so by proving injectivity of the map from the ring of rational characteristic classes of oriented fibrations with fibre S n × S n \mathrm{S}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{n} ; the latter is proven by Berglund–Zeman to be isomorphic to the group cohomology of the symmetric powers of the standard representation of a certain finite-index subgroup Γ \Gamma of SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) . These characteristic classes of smooth bundles are not generalised Miller–Morita–Mumford classes, and they exist in arbitrarily large cohomological degrees. Inspired by an example given by Morita, we provide a collection of smooth oriented S n × S n \mathrm{S}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{n} -bundles, indexed by cyclic subgroups of Γ \Gamma , which detect any given non-zero characteristic class of such fibrations.

We present results regarding rational characteristic classes of numerable fibre bundles with fibre a product
\mathrm{S}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{n}

A rational characteristic class of smooth oriented

The set of isomorphism classes of smooth oriented

(1)

-fibrations.

]

(3)

]

]

Fibrations, bundles, and classifying spaces

We present the classification of oriented fibrations using the formalism of
[
May75
]
.
By a space we mean a compactly generated weakly Hausdorff space with the homotopy type of a CW complex.

Let
F
be a finite CW complex together with a Poincaré duality space structure of dimension
m
.
That is, an integral homology class
[F]
of degree

\H^{*}(F;\mathbb{Z})\xlongrightarrow{}\H_{m-*}(F;\mathbb{Z})

is an isomorphism.
Such a class
[F]
is called an orientation on
F
.

A fibration with fibre

F
-fibration, is a (Hurewicz) fibration

such that for every point

there exists a homotopy equivalence

to the fibre over
b
.

Definition 2.1
.

An oriented

-fibration is an
F
-fibration
E\xrightarrow{}B
together with a Poincaré duality space structure on

for every

, such that, for every path in
B
, the induced map between the respective fibres preserves the orientation.

Given an

-fibration over

for the local system that takes a point of

m
th homology of the fibre over
b

We call it the monodromy local system.
An orientation on the fibration, with fibre of dimension

, provides an isomorphism between

and the constant functor
\H_{m}(F;\mathbb{Z})
.

A fibre homotopy equivalence between two oriented

and

is a
a map

of over

Let

be the geometric bar construction of

A map to another such triple

We omit an entry on a triple when it is the terminal object.
All actions in this paper are left actions, unless specified otherwise.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

The set of equivalence classes of oriented
F
-fibrations over

Rational dg Lie algebra models for spaces

We present the geometric realisation functors that we use from appropriate subcategories of differential graded Lie algebras (
\mathsf{dgl}
), and commutative differential graded algebras (
\mathsf{cdga}
), all over the rational numbers.
These functors take values in the category of simplicial sets.
We may compose them with the geometric realisation functor
|-|
to the category of compactly generated weakly Hausdorff spaces.

An action of a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a dg Lie algebra
L
is a dg Lie algebra homomorphism

\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\xlongrightarrow{}\Der(L)

to the dg Lie algebra of derivations of
L
.
The semidirect product of
\mathfrak{g}
and
L
with respect to
\rho
is the dg Lie algebra
L\rtimes\mathfrak{g}
with underlying chain complex
L\oplus\mathfrak{g}
, and the bracket given by

\displaystyle[(\theta,x),(\delta,y)]=([\theta,\delta],[x,y]+\rho(\theta)(x)-{(-1)}^{|y||\delta|}\rho(\delta)(y)).

The action is nilpotent if for every degree
m
there is a natural number
k
such that the
k
th composition
\rho{(\mathfrak{g}_{0})}^{\circ k}(L_{m})
is zero; here,
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is the degree zero part of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent if its adjoint action on itself is nilpotent.
That is, if its lower central series terminates at a finite stage in each degree.
Given a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with differential
d
, we denote by
\mathfrak{g}\langle{0}\rangle
the dg Lie subalgebra

\mathfrak{g}\langle{0}\rangle_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathfrak{g}&\text{if $k>0$},\\
\mathrm{Z}_{0}(\mathfrak{g})&\text{if $k=0$},\\
0&\text{if $k<0$},\end{array}\right.

where
\mathrm{Z}_{0}(\mathfrak{g})
are the
0
-cycles.
We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is connected if
\mathfrak{g}\langle{0}\rangle=\mathfrak{g}
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra, we denote by
\exp\mathfrak{g}
the nilpotent group with the same underlying set as
\mathfrak{g}
, and with the group operation given by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula.
Following
[
Ber20
, §3.2]
, given a connected nilpotent dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we define a simplicial nilpotent group by

\exp_{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}=\exp\mathrm{Z}_{0}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{\bullet})

where
\Omega_{\bullet}
is the simplicial cdga that in degree
n
consists of polynomial differential forms on the standard
n
-simplex.
We obtain a nilpotent topological group
|\exp_{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}|
by applying geometric realisation, because
|-|
preserves finite products.

For the remainder of this section, we let
\mathfrak{g}
and
L
be connected nilpotent dg Lie algebras.
A nilpotent action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
L
induces a simplicial group action of
\exp_{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}
on the simplicial dg Lie algebra

by taking the exponential series of a derivation
\theta

e^{\theta}(x)=x+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\rho{(\theta)}^{\circ n}(x).

Thus, it acts on
\mathrm{B}\exp_{\bullet}(L)
.

Similarly, a nilpotent action of
\mathfrak{g}
on a cdga
A
by derivations induces an action of
\exp_{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}
on the realisation of

Group cohomology

In this section, we provide the necessary preliminaries on group cohomology.
We refer to
[
Bro82
]
for more details.
Note that the group cohomology of the discrete group
\Gamma
agrees with the singular cohomology of
\mathrm{B}\Gamma
.

Let

be a commutative ring.
Our convention is that modules over the group ring
R[\Gamma]
will be left modules. We denote by
\mathsf{Mod}_{R[\Gamma]}
their category.
Given an
R[\Gamma]
-module

, consider the

-modules

V^{\Gamma}=\{v\in V\mid\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\ \gamma\cdot v=v\}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptV_{\Gamma}=V/\langle{\gamma\cdot v-v\mid\gamma\in\Gamma,\ v\in V}\rangle

of its invariants and coinvariants, respectively.
Here, the brackets
\langle{-}\rangle
denote the submodule generated by a set.
These constructions are functorial.
For each natural number

, the

th cohomology and homology functors of
\Gamma
over

are defined as the

th derived functors

\H^{k}(\Gamma;-),\ \H_{k}(\Gamma;-)\colon\mathsf{Mod}_{R[\Gamma]}\xlongrightarrow{}\mathsf{Mod}_{R}

of the invariants and coinvariants functors, respectively.

In particular,
\H^{0}(\Gamma;V)=V^{\Gamma}
and
\H_{0}(\Gamma;V)=V_{\Gamma}
.
The first cohomology group can be described as follows.
Recall that a derivation for a left
R[\Gamma]
-module

is a function

such that
d(g\cdot h)=d(g)+g\cdot d(h)
for every

.
There is an isomorphism

with the quotient of the
R
-module of derivations by the submodule of derivations of the form
d(g)=v-g\cdot v
for some
v\in V
.

Given a subgroup
G\leq\Gamma
, there are natural transformations

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}^{\Gamma}_{G}\colon

\displaystyle\H^{k}(\Gamma;-)\xlongrightarrow{}\H^{k}(G;-),

\displaystyle\operatorname{cores}^{\Gamma}_{G}\colon

\displaystyle\H_{k}(G;-)\xlongrightarrow{}\H_{k}(\Gamma;-),

as explained in
[
Bro82
,
\nopp
III.8]
.
Group cohomology is functorial in pairs of a group homomorphism
\varphi\colon G^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}G
, and a map of
R[G^{\prime}]
-modules
V^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\varphi^{*}V
.

Remark 4.1
.

A pair of elements
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
v\in V
such that
\gamma\cdot v=v
yields a homology class

under the cap product

\cap\colon\H^{k}(G;V)\otimes_{R}\H_{1}(G;R)\xlongrightarrow{}\H_{1-k}(G;V)

(4)

for

, and

the subgroup generated by

Following

Lemma 10.6
.

For each natural number
k
there is a unique derivation

{E}_{2k}\colon\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\xlongrightarrow{}\mathbb{Q}[e_{x},e_{y}]

such that
{E}_{2k}(\alpha)=e_{x}^{2k}-e_{y}^{2k}
and
{E}_{2k}(\beta)=0
.

Proof.

There is a unique extension of
f={E}_{2k}
to a derivation on the free group on
\alpha
and
\beta
. To extend it to
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
it is enough to check that
f(\alpha^{4})=f(1)
and
f(\alpha^{2})=f(\beta^{3})
; indeed, see
[
Bro82
, 90]
. Using the derivation condition we rewrite these equations as

\displaystyle(1+\alpha+\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{3})f(\alpha)=0

(32)

\displaystyle(1+\alpha)f(\alpha)=(1+\beta+\beta^{2})f(\beta).

(33)

Note that
\alpha^{2}=-\mathrm{id}
acts trivially on homogeneous polynomials of even degree.
Therefore, the left-hand side of (
32
) takes the form

2(1+\alpha)f(\alpha)=2(e_{x}^{2k}-e_{y}^{2k}+e_{y}^{2k}-e_{x}^{2k})=0.

We defined
f(\beta)=0
, so both equations (
32
) and (
33
) are satisfied.
∎

Lemma 10.7
.

The class
{E}_{2k}
is non-zero when restricted along a parabolic subgroup.

Proof.

It is enough to check that it is non-zero when restricted a single parabolic subgroup, because up to finite-index they are all conjugate in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and the coefficients are rational.
We calculate the pullback of
{E}_{2k}
under the homomorphism

\H^{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Q}[e_{x},e_{y}])\xlongrightarrow{}\Hom(P,\mathbb{Q}[e_{x}])

given by
\operatorname{res}_{P}
followed by the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{x}\colon\mathbb{Q}[e_{x},e_{y}]\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{Q}[e_{x}]
, where
\mathbb{Q}[e_{x}]
is given the trivial action.
The class
{E}_{2k}
is sent to the homomorphism
\mathrm{pr}_{x}\circ{E}_{2k}\circ\operatorname{res}_{P}
, where
{E}_{2k}
now denotes a derivation representative.
Observe that
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=\beta^{-1}\alpha
.
Then we compute

\displaystyle{E}_{2k}(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix})

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}\cdot{E}_{2k}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=(\beta^{-1}e_{x})^{2k}-(\beta^{-1}e_{y})^{2k}

\displaystyle=e_{y}^{2k}-(e_{y}-e_{x})^{2k}

using the derivation property.
This element is sent to
-e_{x}^{2k}
by
\mathrm{pr}_{x}
.
∎
