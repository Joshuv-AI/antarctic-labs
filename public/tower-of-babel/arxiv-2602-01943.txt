Universal scaling of finite-temperature quantum adiabaticity in driven many-body systems
Li-Ying Chou, Jyong-Hao Chen

Abstract
Establishing quantitative adiabaticity criteria at finite temperature remains substantially less developed than in the pure-state setting, despite the fact that realistic quantum systems are never at absolute zero. Here we derive rigorous bounds on the Hilbert–Schmidt fidelity between mixed states by combining a mixed-state quantum speed limit with mixed-state fidelity susceptibility within the Liouville space formulation of quantum mechanics. Applied to protocols that drive an initial Gibbs state toward a quasi-Gibbs target, these bounds yield an explicit threshold driving rate for the onset of nonadiabaticity. For a broad class of local Hamiltonians in gapped phases, we show that, in the thermodynamic limit, the threshold factorizes into two factors: a system-size contribution that recovers the zero-temperature scaling and a universal temperature-dependent factor. The latter is exponentially close to unity at low temperature, whereas at high temperature it increases linearly with temperature. We verify the predicted scaling in several spin- 1 / 2 1/2 chains by obtaining closed-form expressions for the threshold driving rate. Our results provide practical and largely model-independent criteria for finite-temperature adiabaticity in closed many-body systems.

(Temperature scaling of

Consider a broad class of local Hamiltonians in gapped phases, i.e., Hamiltonians that can be written as sums of local operators and possess a nonzero spectral gap above the ground state.

\displaystyle\text{High-temperature regime:}\quad f(\beta)\simeq c^{\,}_{2}/\beta.

The high-temperature scaling form (

These universal scaling forms [Eq. (
16
)] are consistent with the following physical picture.
In the infinite-temperature limit
\beta\to 0
, the initial Gibbs state (
7
) approaches the maximally mixed state, which commutes with any Hamiltonian.
Therefore, under unitary evolution the dynamical state remains equal to the initial one,
\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{\lambda}=\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}
for all
\lambda
.
At the same time, the quasi-Gibbs state (
8
) also tends to the maximally mixed state, so that
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)=\mathcal{C}(\lambda)=1
as
\beta\to 0
.
Thus, in the infinite-temperature limit, both the adiabatic fidelity
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
and the thermal-state overlap
\mathcal{C}(\lambda)
saturate their maximal value, independent of the driving rate, and the threshold
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
can be taken arbitrarily large.
For small but finite
\beta
, deviations of the Boltzmann weights from their infinite-temperature values are of order
\beta
, so one expects the maximal admissible driving rate to scale inversely with this parameter, consistent with the high-temperature behavior
f(\beta)\propto\beta^{-1}
in Eq. (
16b
).
In other words, at high temperature the quasi-Gibbs target state becomes nearly maximally mixed and is therefore much less sensitive to the details of the driving.

In the opposite, zero-temperature limit
\beta\to\infty
, the initial Gibbs state (
7
) and the quasi-Gibbs state (
8
) reduce to the initial and instantaneous ground states, respectively.
Accordingly, the finite-temperature threshold driving rate must reproduce the zero-temperature value, implying
\lim_{\beta\to\infty}f(\beta)=1
.
In a gapped phase with excitation gap
\Delta
, contributions of excited states to thermodynamic quantities at low but nonzero temperature are suppressed by Boltzmann factors
e^{-\beta\Delta}
, so deviations of
\delta V
and
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
from their ground-state values are likewise exponentially small in
\beta\Delta
.
It is therefore natural to expect the leading deviation of
f(\beta)
from unity at low but nonzero temperature to be proportional to
e^{-\beta\Delta}
, consistent with Eq. (
16a
).

Experimental relevance.—
In our formulation, the target state
\hat{\sigma}^{\,}_{\lambda}
is the quasi-Gibbs state (
8
).
Estimating the adiabatic fidelity (
9
) therefore reduces to measuring

\Tr(\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{\lambda}\hat{\sigma}^{\,}_{\lambda})
and
\Tr[(\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{\lambda})^{2}]
, since

\Tr[(\hat{\sigma}^{\,}_{\lambda})^{2}]
is fixed by the initial Boltzmann weights
p^{\,}_{n}
.
Moreover, using

\Tr(\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{\lambda}\hat{\sigma}^{\,}_{\lambda})=\sum_{n}p^{\,}_{n}\,\langle E^{\,}_{n}(\lambda)|\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{\lambda}|E^{\,}_{n}(\lambda)\rangle
,
it suffices to access populations in the instantaneous eigenbasis.
The purity
\Tr[(\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{\lambda})^{2}]
can be obtained via two-copy interference or SWAP-type protocols
26
;
22
;
49
, or via randomized measurements
27
;
16
, without full state tomography.

Operationally, one may fix a target tolerance (e.g.,
\mathcal{F}\geq e^{-1}
) and, for each initial temperature, scan the ramp rate
\Gamma
to identify the largest value that still meets the criterion; this defines
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
.
Dividing out the size-dependent factor
\Gamma^{\,}_{N}
then tests the predicted temperature dependence through a data collapse of
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}/\Gamma^{\,}_{N}
versus
\beta
.
Systematic deviations from the closed-system scaling
f(\beta)
provide a quantitative diagnostic of nonunitary effects, such as residual heating and decoherence.

Summary and outlook.—
In summary, we developed a Liouville space framework that combines a mixed-state quantum speed limit with fidelity susceptibility to derive explicit bounds on the adiabatic fidelity.
For protocols that start from a Gibbs state and drive toward a quasi-Gibbs target, these bounds yield a threshold driving rate
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
beyond which adiabatic following fails.
For local Hamiltonians in gapped phases, we found that
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
factorizes into the familiar zero-temperature system-size scaling and a universal temperature-dependent factor.
The latter is exponentially close to unity at low temperature and grows linearly with temperature at high temperature.
We validated this scaling in representative spin-
1/2
chains,
where
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
can be obtained in closed form.
Overall, our results provide a broadly applicable quantitative criterion for finite-temperature adiabaticity in closed many-body systems.
A natural next step is to extend the present framework to open systems governed by Lindblad dynamics.
Such an extension would provide a rigorous tool for assessing adiabaticity in the presence of decoherence, with applications to adiabatic preparation of both ground and thermal states
36
;
75
in quantum simulators and quantum annealers.

S1
Derivation of the mixed-state quantum speed limit [Eq. (
2
)]

In this section, we derive the mixed-state quantum speed limit inequality [Eq. (
2
)] for closed, unitary dynamics.
We parametrize the evolution by a driving coordinate
\lambda=\lambda(t)
and assume that the Hamiltonian depends on time only through
\lambda(t)
, i.e.,
\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda(t)}
.

S1.1
Liouville space setup

We consider unitary dynamics generated by a Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\Gamma\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{\lambda}=[\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda},\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{\lambda}],\qquad\Gamma:=\partial^{\,}_{t}\lambda.

(S1)

Vectorizing operators as
\left|A\right)
with Hilbert–Schmidt inner product

\left(A\middle|B\right)=\Tr(\hat{A}^\dagger\hat{B})
, Eq. (
S1
) becomes

\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}\frac{1}{\Gamma}\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\,\left|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\right),\qquad\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\bullet\right)=\left|[\hat{H}_{\lambda},\bullet]\right),

where the superoperator
\hat{\hat{L}}_{\lambda}
is Hermitian,

\hat{\hat{L}}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}=\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}
.

For later convenience we introduce normalized Liouville space vectors

\left|\Psi^{\,}_{\lambda}\right):=\frac{\left|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)}{\|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}},\qquad\left|\Psi^{\,}_{0}\right):=\frac{\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)}{\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}},

where
\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}=\Tr(\hat\rho_0^2)
.
Note that for closed systems the purity is conserved,

\Tr(\hat{\rho}^{2}_\lambda)=\Tr(\hat{\rho}^{2}_0)
, hence
\|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}=\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}
.

S1.2
Hilbert–Schmidt angle and its rate of change

Define the Hilbert–Schmidt fidelity amplitude
M(\lambda)
and Hilbert–Schmidt angle
\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}
by

M(\lambda):=\left(\Psi_{\lambda}\middle|\Psi_{0}\right)=\frac{\Tr(\hat\rho^{\,}_\lambda\hat\rho_0)}{\Tr(\hat\rho_0^2)}\in[0,1],\qquad\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}:=\arccos M(\lambda)\in[0,\pi/2],

so that
M(\lambda)=\cos\Theta_{\lambda}
.
Differentiating
M(\lambda)
with respect to
\lambda
yields

\displaystyle\partial^{\,}_{\lambda}{M}=\left(\partial_{\lambda}\Psi_{\lambda}\middle|\Psi_{0}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}\frac{1}{\Gamma}\,\frac{1}{\Tr(\hat\rho_0^2)}\,\left(\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\right|\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right).

(S2)

Introduce the rank-one superprojector onto the initial Hilbert–Schmidt state and its complement,

\displaystyle\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}:=\left|\Psi_{0}\right)\left(\Psi_{0}\right|,\qquad\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}:=\openone-\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}},

(S3)

so that
\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}=\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}
,
\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}^{2}=\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}
, and

\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}=0
.
Decompose
\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)
in Eq. (
S2
) as

\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)=\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)+\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right).

The
\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}
-term vanishes because

\left(\Psi_{0}\right|\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)=\frac{1}{\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}}\left(\rho^{\,}_{0}\right|\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)=\frac{1}{\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}}\Tr\!\left(\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}[\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda},\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}]\right)=0,

by cyclicity of the trace.
Hence, Eq. (
S2
) implies

|\partial^{\,}_{\lambda}{M}|=\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\frac{1}{\Tr(\hat\rho_0^2)}\left|\left(\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\right|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)\right|.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle|\partial^{\,}_{\lambda}M|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\frac{1}{\Tr(\hat\rho_0^2)}\|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}^{\,}\left|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\;\|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}^{\,}\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.

(S4)

The two norms evaluate to

\|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}^{\,}\left|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}\,\sin\Theta_{\lambda},\qquad\|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}^{\,}\hat{\hat{L}}^{\,}_{\lambda}\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda},\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}.

Substituting into Eq. (
S4
) yields

|\partial^{\,}_{\lambda}M|\leq\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\,\frac{1}{\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}}\,\sin\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}\;\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda},\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}.

Since
M(\lambda)=\cos\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}
, we have

\partial^{\,}_{\lambda}{M}=-\sin\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}\,\partial^{\,}_{\lambda}\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}
.
Canceling
\sin\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}
gives

\left|\partial^{\,}_{\lambda}\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\frac{1}{\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}}\,\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda},\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}.

Integrating in
\lambda
and using the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}-\underbrace{\Theta^{\,}_{0}}^{\,}_{=0}=\left|\int^{\lambda}_{0}\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\prime}\,\partial^{\,}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\lambda}_{0}\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\prime}\,\bigl|\partial^{\,}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\int_{0}^{\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\prime}}{|\partial^{\,}_{t}\lambda^{\prime}|}\;\frac{1}{\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}}\,\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda^{\prime}},\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{\,}.

(S5)

Finally, the commutator norm can be written explicitly as

\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda},\hat{\rho}_{0}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}=2\left[\Tr(\hat\rho_0^2\hat H_\lambda^2)-\Tr(\hat\rho_0\hat H^{\,}_\lambda\hat\rho_0\hat H_\lambda)\right],

so Eq. (
S5
) is equivalent to Eq. (
2
) in the main text (with
\hbar\equiv 1
).

S2
Derivation of mixed-state fidelity bounds [Eq. (
3
)]

This section derives Eq. (
3
) of the main text.
The derivation is inspired by Ref.
19
.
Let
\left|\Psi^{\,}_{0}\right)
,
\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right)
, and
\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)
be
normalized
vectors
in the Liouville (Hilbert-Schmidt) space introduced in Sec.
S1
.
We define the Hilbert–Schmidt fidelity

F[\Phi_{2},\Phi_{1}]:=\bigl|\left(\Phi_{2}\middle|\Phi_{1}\right)\bigr|^{2}\in[0,1].

Our goal is to bound
F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi_{\lambda}]
in terms of

F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]
and the Hilbert–Schmidt angle
\Theta_{\lambda}
defined by

\cos\Theta_{\lambda}:=\bigl|\left(\Psi^{\,}_{0}\middle|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)\bigr|=\sqrt{F[\Psi^{\,}_{0},\Psi_{\lambda}]},\qquad\Theta_{\lambda}\in[0,\pi/2].

Introduce the rank-one superprojector onto
\left|\Psi^{\,}_{0}\right)
and its orthogonal complement as in
Eq. (
S3
).
By orthogonal decomposition,

\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)=\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)+\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right),\qquad\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right)=\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right)+\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right).

It follows that

F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{\lambda}]=\Bigl|\left(\Phi_{\lambda}\right|\bigl(\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}+\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\bigr)\left|\Psi^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)\Bigr|^{2}=\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}} \HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}+\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}+2\,\mathrm{Re}\Big[\left(\Phi_{\lambda}\right|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)\,\left(\Psi_{\lambda}\right|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right)\Big].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{F[\Phi_\lambda,\Psi_{\lambda}] - F[\Phi_\lambda,\Psi^{\,}_{0}]}

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}} \HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}+\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}} \HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}+2\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left[\left(\Phi_{\lambda}\right|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)\,\left(\Psi_{\lambda}\right|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right)\right]-F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg|\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}} \HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}+\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}} \HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}-F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]\Bigg|+2\Bigg|\mathrm{Re}\!\left[\left(\Phi_{\lambda}\right|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)\,\left(\Psi_{\lambda}\right|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right)\right]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg|\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}} \HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}+\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}} \HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}-F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]\Bigg|+2\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}}}\,\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Psi_{\lambda}} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}} \HSket{\Phi_\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{-\sin^2\Theta_\lambda\,F[\Phi_\lambda,\Psi^{\,}_{0}] + \mathcal{D}_{\rm un}(\lambda)}+2\cos\Theta_{\lambda}\sqrt{F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{\rm un}(\lambda)}.

(S6)

Here, we used the triangle inequality to obtain the first inequality, the bound

\mathrm{Re}(z)\leq|z|
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
to obtain the second inequality, and the identities

\|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)\|=\cos\Theta_{\lambda},\qquad\|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right)\|=\sqrt{F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]},\qquad\mathcal{D}_{\rm un}(\lambda):=\absolutevalue{\HSbra{\Phi_\lambda} \hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}} \HSket{\Psi_{\lambda}} }^{2}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{\rm un}(\lambda)}\leq\|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\left|\Psi_{\lambda}\right)\|\,\|\hat{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right)\|=\sin\Theta_{\lambda}\,\sqrt{1-F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]},

and hence Eq. (
S6
) implies

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{F[\Phi_\lambda,\Psi_{\lambda}] - F[\Phi_\lambda,\Psi^{\,}_{0}]}\leq\sin^{2}\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}\,\absolutevalue{1-2F[\Phi_\lambda,\Psi^{\,}_{0}]}+\sin\!\left(2\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)\sqrt{F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]}\,\sqrt{1-F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]}.

(S7)

Maximizing the right-hand side of Eq. (
S7
) with respect to
F[\Phi_{\lambda},\Psi^{\,}_{0}]

yields a simpler, though looser bound,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{F[\Phi_\lambda,\Psi_{\lambda}] - F[\Phi_\lambda,\Psi^{\,}_{0}]}\leq\sin\Theta_{\lambda}.

(S8)

Finally, Eq. (
3
) of the main text follows by making the identifications

\left|\Psi^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)\equiv\frac{\left|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)}{\|\rho^{\,}_{\lambda}\|},\qquad\left|\Phi^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)\equiv\frac{\left|\sigma^{\,}_{\lambda}\right)}{\|\sigma^{\,}_{\lambda}\|},\qquad\left|\Psi^{\,}_{0}\right)\equiv\frac{\left|\rho^{\,}_{0}\right)}{\|\rho^{\,}_{0}\|}

in Eqs. (
S7
) and (
S8
), and then applying the
mixed-state quantum speed limit inequality (
2
) to bound
\Theta^{\,}_{\lambda}
.

In this section, we provide a proof of Theorem

To this end, we evaluate both

[Eq. (

and

[Eq. (

S3.1
Useful expressions for
\delta V
and
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}

S3.1.1
Useful expression for
\delta V

We begin by deriving a convenient expression for
\delta V
(
6

\displaystyle\delta V

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{2\,I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}\!\left(\hat{\tilde{\rho}}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}\right)},\qquad I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}\!\left(\hat{\tilde{\rho}}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}\right):=\Tr\!\left[\hat{\tilde{\rho}}^{\,}_{0}\,\hat{V}^{2}\right]-\Tr\!\left[\left(\hat{\tilde{\rho}}^{1/2}_{0}\hat{V}\right)^{2}\right].

(S9)

When
\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(\beta)
is a Gibbs state at inverse temperature
\beta

(
7
), the order-
2

\hat{\tilde{\rho}}^{\,}_{0}
reads

\hat{\tilde{\rho}}^{\,}_{0}:=\frac{\hat{\rho}_{0}^{\,2}}{\Tr[\hat{\rho}_{0}^{\,2}]}=\frac{e^{-2\beta\hat{H}_{0}}}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}=\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta).

Therefore, Eq. (
S9
) becomes

I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}\!\left(\hat{\tilde{\rho}}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}\right)=\Tr\!\left[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\,\hat{V}^{2}\right]-\Tr\!\left[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\,\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\right]=:I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta),

where

\hat{V}(\beta):=e^{\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}}\hat{V}e^{-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}}

is the imaginary-time Heisenberg operator at time
\beta
.
Introducing the shorthand for a thermal average at inverse temperature

\langle\cdots\rangle^{\,}_{2\beta}:=\Tr[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\cdots],

we can write
\delta V
(
S9

\displaystyle\boxed{\delta V=\sqrt{2\,I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)},\qquad I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)=\langle\hat{V}^{2}\rangle^{\,}_{2\beta}-\langle\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\rangle^{\,}_{2\beta}.}

(S10)

S3.1.2
Useful expression for
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}

We now turn to the fidelity susceptibility
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
(
11
),

\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}\ln\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda^{2}}\Big|^{\,}_{\lambda=0},

where the thermal-state overlap
\mathcal{C}(\lambda)
is defined in
Eq. (
12
).
Since we consider a unitary evolution, the purity is conserved,

\Tr[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}]=\Tr[\hat{\sigma}^{\,}_{\lambda}(\beta)^{2}]
,
and hence, the denominators in Eq. (
12
) are independent of
\lambda
.
Therefore, in computing
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
we only need

\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}=-2\,\frac{\partial^{2}\ln S(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda^{2}}\Big|_{\lambda=0},\qquad S(\lambda):=\Tr[\hat{\rho}_{0}(\beta)\hat{\sigma}_{\lambda}(\beta)].

Working out the derivatives gives

\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}=-2\left[\frac{S^{\prime\prime}(0)}{S(0)}-\left(\frac{S^{\prime}(0)}{S(0)}\right)^{2}\right].

Write the Boltzmann weights
p^{\,}_{n}:=e^{-\beta E_{n}^{(0)}}
and
Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)=\sum_{n}p^{\,}_{n}
.
Upon using the spectral decomposition

\hat{\rho}_{0}(\beta)=\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)}\sum_{m}p^{\,}_{m}\,|E_{m}^{(0)}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{m}|,\qquad\hat{\sigma}_{\lambda}(\beta)=\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)}\sum_{n}p^{\,}_{n}\,\outerproduct{E_n(\lambda)}{E_n(\lambda)},

we obtain

\displaystyle S(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}}\sum_{n}p_{n}^{2}\,|\langle E_{n}^{(0)}|E_{n}(\lambda)\rangle|^{2}+\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}p^{\,}_{m}p^{\,}_{n}\,|\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|E_{n}(\lambda)\rangle|^{2}.

(S11)

For
\hat{H}_{\lambda}=\hat{H}_{0}+\lambda\hat{V}
, standard (non-degenerate) perturbation theory yields

|E_{n}(\lambda)\rangle=|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle+\lambda\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle}{E_{n}^{(0)}-E_{m}^{(0)}}\,|E_{m}^{(0)}\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}).

For
m\neq n
, the overlap expands as

|\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|E_{n}(\lambda)\rangle|^{2}=\lambda^{2}\frac{|\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle|^{2}}{(E_{n}^{(0)}-E_{m}^{(0)})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{3})\qquad(m\neq n),

while normalization implies

|\langle E_{n}^{(0)}|E_{n}(\lambda)\rangle|^{2}=1-\lambda^{2}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{|\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle|^{2}}{(E_{n}^{(0)}-E_{m}^{(0)})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{3}).

Substituting into Eq. (
S11
) and reorganizing the sums gives

S(\lambda)=S(0)-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}(p^{\,}_{m}-p^{\,}_{n})^{2}\frac{|\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle|^{2}}{(E_{m}^{(0)}-E_{n}^{(0)})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{3}),

with

S(0)=\Tr(\hat{\rho}_0^2)=\frac{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}}.

(S12)

It follows that
S^{\prime}(0)=0
and

S^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}(p^{\,}_{m}-p^{\,}_{n})^{2}\frac{|\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle|^{2}}{(E_{m}^{(0)}-E_{n}^{(0)})^{2}}.

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\boxed{\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{2}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}\left(e^{-\beta E_{m}^{(0)}}-e^{-\beta E_{n}^{(0)}}\right)^{2}\frac{|\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle|^{2}}{(E_{m}^{(0)}-E_{n}^{(0)})^{2}},}

(S13)

where
Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)=\sum_{m}e^{-2\beta E_{m}^{(0)}}
.

S3.2
Low-temperature regime

S3.2.1
\delta V
in the low-temperature regime

We begin by evaluating the standard deviation
\delta V
(
S10
)
in the low-temperature regime.
For
\beta\Delta\gg 1
, it is sufficient to retain the ground state (n=0) and the lowest excited state that
couples to it under
\hat{V}
.
Throughout, we choose
|E^{(0)}_{1}\rangle
such that

V^{\,}_{10}:=\langle E^{(0)}_{1}|\hat{V}|E^{(0)}_{0}\rangle\neq 0
and

\Delta:=E^{(0)}_{1}-E^{(0)}_{0}=\min_{n>0:\,\langle E^{(0)}_{n}|\hat{V}|E^{(0)}_{0}\rangle\neq 0}\bigl(E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{0}\bigr)>0.

(S14)

In this approximation, the Gibbs state at inverse temperature
2\beta
reads

\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\simeq\frac{e^{-2\beta E^{(0)}_{0}}}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\left(|E^{(0)}_{0}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{0}|+e^{-2\beta\Delta}\,|E^{(0)}_{1}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{1}|\right),\qquad Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\simeq e^{-2\beta E^{(0)}_{0}}\left(1+e^{-2\beta\Delta}\right).

Expanding to first order in
e^{-2\beta\Delta}
yields

\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\approx\left(1-e^{-2\beta\Delta}\right)|E^{(0)}_{0}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{0}|+e^{-2\beta\Delta}|E^{(0)}_{1}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{1}|,

(S15)

which reduces to the ground-state projector as
\beta\to\infty
.

Term 1:
\langle\hat{V}^{2}\rangle_{2\beta}
.

Using Eq. (
S15
),

\langle\hat{V}^{2}\rangle^{\,}_{2\beta}=\Tr[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\hat{V}^{2}]\approx(1-e^{-2\beta\Delta})(V^{2})^{\,}_{00}+e^{-2\beta\Delta}(V^{2})^{\,}_{11},

(S16)

where
(V^{2})_{mn}:=\langle E^{(0)}_{m}|\hat{V}^{2}|E^{(0)}_{n}\rangle
.

Imaginary-time Heisenberg evolution.

The imaginary-time Heisenberg operator is

\hat{V}(\beta):=e^{\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}}\hat{V}e^{-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}}=\sum_{m,n}e^{\beta(E_{m}^{(0)}-E_{n}^{(0)})}V_{mn}\,|E_{m}^{(0)}\rangle\langle E_{n}^{(0)}|.

Retaining the two lowest levels gives

\hat{V}(\beta)\approx V^{\,}_{00}|E^{(0)}_{0}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{0}|+e^{-\beta\Delta}V^{\,}_{01}|E^{(0)}_{0}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{1}|+e^{\beta\Delta}V^{\,}_{10}|E^{(0)}_{1}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{0}|+V^{\,}_{11}|E^{(0)}_{1}\rangle\langle E^{(0)}_{1}|,

(S17)

where
V_{mn}:=\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle
.

Term 2:
\langle\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\rangle_{2\beta}
.

Using Eqs. (
S15
) and (
S17
), and keeping terms up to
order
e^{-2\beta\Delta}
, we obtain

\langle\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\rangle^{\,}_{2\beta}=\Tr[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}]\approx(V^{\,}_{00})^{2}+2e^{-\beta\Delta}|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}+e^{-2\beta\Delta}\Bigl[(V_{11})^{2}-(V_{00})^{2}\Bigr].

(S18)

Low-
T
expansion of
I_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)
and
\delta V
.

Recalling
I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)=\langle\hat{V}^{2}\rangle_{2\beta}-\langle\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\rangle_{2\beta}
[Eq. (
S10
)],
Eqs. (
S16
) and (
S18
) give

I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)\approx\Bigl[(V^{2})^{\,}_{00}-(V^{\,}_{00})^{2}\Bigr]-2e^{-\beta\Delta}|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\beta\Delta}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\delta V=\sqrt{2I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)}

\displaystyle=\delta V^{(0)}\left(1-2e^{-\beta\Delta}\frac{|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}}{(\delta V^{(0)})^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\beta\Delta}),

(S19)

where

\delta V^{(0)}:=\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{(V^{2})^{\,}_{00}-(V^{\,}_{00})^{2}}.

Finally, we show in Sec.
S3.4.1
that

|V^{\,}_{10}|=|\langle E^{(0)}_{1}|\hat{V}|E^{(0)}_{0}\rangle|
scales as
\sqrt{N}
.
Since
\delta V^{(0)}
also scales as
\sqrt{N}
, the ratio

|V^{\,}_{10}|/\delta V^{(0)}
in Eq. (
S19
)
is independent of
N
in the thermodynamic limit (
N\to\infty
).

S3.2.2
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
in the low-temperature regime

We now evaluate the fidelity susceptibility
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}

(
S13
) in the low-temperature regime.
To simplify notation, define

\Delta_{m}:=E_{m}^{(0)}-E_{0}^{(0)}\geq 0,\qquad A_{mn}:=\frac{\bigl|\bra{E_m^{(0)}}\hat{V}\ket{E_n^{(0)}}\bigr|^{2}}{\bigl(E_{m}^{(0)}-E_{n}^{(0)}\bigr)^{2}}.

We again fix the label
m=1
such that
V_{10}\neq 0
and

\Delta:=\Delta_{1}=\min_{n>0:\,V_{n0}\neq 0}\Delta_{n}>0.

(S20)

Factoring out
E_{0}^{(0)}
from
Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)
gives

Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)=e^{-2\beta E_{0}^{(0)}}\Bigl(1+\sum_{k>0}e^{-2\beta\Delta_{k}}\Bigr)=:e^{-2\beta E_{0}^{(0)}}\,\tilde{Z}(2\beta),\qquad\tilde{Z}(2\beta)=1+\sum_{k>0}e^{-2\beta\Delta_{k}}.

Likewise,

\bigl(e^{-\beta E_{m}^{(0)}}-e^{-\beta E_{n}^{(0)}}\bigr)^{2}=e^{-2\beta E_{0}^{(0)}}\bigl(e^{-\beta\Delta_{m}}-e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}}\bigr)^{2}.

Therefore, Eq. (
S13
) becomes

\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}(\beta)=\frac{2}{\tilde{Z}(2\beta)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}\bigl(e^{-\beta\Delta_{m}}-e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}}\bigr)^{2}\,A_{mn}.

(S21)

Step 1: Split the sum into ground–excited and excited–excited sectors.

Write

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}=\sum_{n>0}\Bigl[(m,n)=(0,n)+(n,0)\Bigr]\;+\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n>0\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}.

Since
\Delta_{0}=0
, the ground–excited sector yields

\sum_{n>0}\Bigl[(1-e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}})^{2}A_{0n}+(e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}}-1)^{2}A_{n0}\Bigr]=2\sum_{n>0}(1-e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}})^{2}A_{0n},

where
A_{n0}=A_{0n}
.
Expanding,

(1-e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}})^{2}=1-2e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}}+e^{-2\beta\Delta_{n}}.

The excited–excited sector is

S_{\rm ex}(\beta):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n>0\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}\bigl(e^{-\beta\Delta_{m}}-e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}}\bigr)^{2}A_{mn}.

(S22)

Since

(e^{-\beta\Delta_{m}}-e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}})^{2}\leq e^{-2\beta\Delta_{m}}+e^{-2\beta\Delta_{n}}+2e^{-\beta(\Delta_{m}+\Delta_{n})},

one has
S_{\rm ex}(\beta)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\beta\Delta})
.

Putting these into Eq. (
S21
) gives

\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}(\beta)=\frac{2}{\tilde{Z}(2\beta)}\left[2\sum_{n>0}\Bigl(1-2e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}}+e^{-2\beta\Delta_{n}}\Bigr)A_{0n}+S_{\rm ex}(\beta)\right].

(S23)

Step 2: Expand the normalization
1/\tilde{Z}(2\beta)
.

Since
\tilde{Z}(2\beta)=1+\sum_{k>0}e^{-2\beta\Delta_{k}}
,

\frac{1}{\tilde{Z}(2\beta)}=1-\sum_{k>0}e^{-2\beta\Delta_{k}}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-4\beta\Delta}).

(S24)

Step 3: Collect terms order by order.

Define the zero-temperature fidelity susceptibility

\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}:=\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}(\beta\to\infty)=4\sum_{n>0}A_{0n}=4\sum_{n>0}\frac{\bigl|\bra{E_n^{(0)}}\hat{V}\ket{E_0^{(0)}}\bigr|^{2}}{\Delta_{n}^{2}}.

(S25)

Using Eqs. (
S23
) and (
S24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}(\beta)=\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}-8\sum_{n>0}e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}}A_{0n}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\beta\Delta}).

(S26)

(At order
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\beta\Delta})
, the contributions come from the
e^{-2\beta\Delta_{n}}
term in
(1-e^{-\beta\Delta_{n}})^{2}
,
the correction from
1/\tilde{Z}(2\beta)
, and the excited–excited sector
S_{\rm ex}(\beta)
.)

Factorizing
\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}
.

Keeping only the leading Boltzmann correction in Eq. (
S26
) and factorizing
\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}
yields

\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}(\beta)=\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}\left[1-8e^{-\beta\Delta}\,\frac{1}{\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}}\frac{|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{O}\!\left(e^{-\beta\Delta_{2}},\,e^{-2\beta\Delta}\right),

(S27)

where
\Delta_{2}
denotes the next smallest excitation gap among states with nonzero ground-state coupling (namely, those with
V_{n0}\neq 0
).

We show in Sec.
S3.4.2
that
|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}/\Delta^{2}\asymp N
.
Since
\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}\asymp N
in a gapped phase, the ratio

(1/\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}})\,(|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}/\Delta^{2})

is asymptotically independent of
N
as
N\to\infty
.

S3.2.3
Threshold driving rate
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
in the low-temperature regime

We now compute the threshold driving rate
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
(
10
)
by substituting the low-temperature expansions of
\delta V
(
S19
)
and
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
(
S27
):

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}=\Gamma^{\,}_{N}\frac{1-2e^{-\beta\Delta}\left(\frac{|V^{\,}_{10}|}{\delta V^{(0)}}\right)^{2}}{1-8e^{-\beta\Delta}\frac{1}{\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}}\frac{|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}}\equiv\Gamma^{\,}_{N}\,f^{\,}_{\mathrm{low}\text{-}T}(\beta)+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\beta\Delta}),

where expanding the ratio to first order in
e^{-\beta\Delta}
gives

\displaystyle f^{\,}_{\mathrm{low}\text{-}T}(\beta)=1+2e^{-\beta\Delta}\underbrace{\left[-\left(\frac{|V^{\,}_{10}|}{\delta V^{(0)}}\right)^{2}+\frac{4}{\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}}\frac{|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\right]}_{=:W}.

(S28)

In the thermodynamic limit,
f^{\,}_{\mathrm{low}\text{-}T}(\beta)
is independent of
N

because both ratios
|V_{10}|/\delta V^{(0)}
and
|V_{10}|^{2}/(\chi_{F}^{(0)}\Delta^{2})
are
N
-independent.

Bounds on
W
.

We next show that
W\geq 0
and
W\leq 1
.
From Eq. (
S28
),

W=|V_{10}|^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{(\delta V^{(0)})^{2}}+\frac{4}{\chi_{F}^{(0)}\,\Delta^{2}}\right),\qquad V_{n0}:=\bra{E_n^{(0)}}\hat{V}\ket{E_0^{(0)}}.

Introduce the dimensionless ratios

a:=\frac{|V_{10}|^{2}}{(\delta V^{(0)})^{2}},\qquad b:=\frac{|V_{10}|^{2}}{\chi_{F}^{(0)}\,\Delta^{2}},

so that

W=-a+4b.

(S29)

Now note that
\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}
defined in Eq. (
S25
) implies two simple spectral inequalities.
First,

\chi_{F}^{(0)}=4\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{|V_{n0}|^{2}}{\Delta_{n}^{2}}\geq 4\frac{|V_{10}|^{2}}{\Delta^{2}},

which implies

b\leq\frac{1}{4}.

(S30)

Second, since
\Delta\leq\Delta_{n}
for every
n>0
with
V_{n0}\neq 0
,

\chi_{F}^{(0)}=4\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{|V_{n0}|^{2}}{\Delta_{n}^{2}}\leq 4\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}|V_{n0}|^{2}=2\frac{(\delta V^{(0)})^{2}}{\Delta^{2}},

where we used
\sum_{n\neq 0}|V_{n0}|^{2}=(\delta V^{(0)})^{2}/2
.
This implies

a\leq 2b.

(S31)

Combining Eqs. (
S30
) and (
S31
), and using
a\geq 0
, we obtain

0\leq a\leq 2b\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Together with Eq. (
S29
), this yields the universal bound

0\leq W\leq 1.

(S32)

In our convention,
V_{10}\neq 0
by construction, and hence,
W>0
.
The upper end
W\simeq 1
is approached when
b\simeq 1/4
(so that
\chi_{F}^{(0)}
is dominated by the
n=1
contribution)
while
a\ll 1
(so that
(\delta V^{(0)})^{2}
is dominated by matrix elements to higher excited states).

We emphasize that
W
is generally model dependent, even though it always obeys the universal bound (
S32
).
Indeed, its value is controlled by how the spectral weight of
\hat{V}
is distributed across excitations:

a=\frac{|V_{10}|^{2}}{2\sum_{n\neq 0}|V_{n0}|^{2}},\qquad b=\frac{|V_{10}|^{2}/\Delta^{2}}{4\sum_{n\neq 0}|V_{n0}|^{2}/\Delta_{n}^{2}}.

(S33)

Finally, applying Eq. (
S32
) to Eq. (
S28
) gives the low-temperature form stated in
Theorem
1
, with

c^{\,}_{1}=2W=2|V_{10}|^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{(\delta V^{(0)})^{2}}+\frac{4}{\chi_{F}^{(0)}\,\Delta^{2}}\right).

S3.3
High-temperature regime

S3.3.1
\delta V
in the high-temperature regime

Without loss of generality, we assume
\Tr[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}\big]=0

(this can always be achieved by shifting
\hat{H}_{0}\mapsto\hat{H}_{0}-\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}\big]\mathbb{I}/d
, which does not affect the dynamics)
and that the Hilbert-space dimension scales as
d\asymp 2^{N}
.

At infinite temperature, the state is maximally mixed (
\hat{\rho}=\mathbb{I}/d
), which is invariant under unitary time evolution;
hence, all normalized overlaps are trivially unity.
In the high-temperature regime, the appropriate small parameter is
\beta
compared to microscopic local energy scales
(for example,
\beta\|\hat{h}_{i}\|\ll 1
for the local terms in
\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{i}\hat{h}_{i}
).
We therefore expand the Boltzmann weight to second order in
\beta
. The initial Gibbs state
(
7
) is

\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(\beta)\approx\frac{\mathbb{I}-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\hat{H}^{2}_{0}}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)}.

The partition function
Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)
is fixed by
\Tr[\hat{\rho}_{0}(\beta)]=1
, hence

\displaystyle Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\Tr\!\left[\mathbb{I}-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\hat{H}^{2}_{0}\right]=d+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\Tr[\hat{H}^{2}_{0}\big].

(S34)

Therefore,

\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(\beta)\approx\frac{1}{d}\left[\mathbb{I}-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\left(\hat{H}^{2}_{0}-\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\hat{H}^{2}_{0}\big]\,\mathbb{I}\right)\right].

Likewise, the initial state at inverse temperature
2\beta
is

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{d}\left[\mathbb{I}-2\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}+2\beta^{2}\left(\hat{H}^{2}_{0}-\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\hat{H}^{2}_{0}\big]\,\mathbb{I}\right)\right].

(S35)

Term 1:
\langle\hat{V}^{2}\rangle_{2\beta}
.

Using Eq. (
S35
),

\displaystyle\langle\hat{V}^{2}\rangle^{\,}_{2\beta}=\Tr[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\hat{V}^{2}]\approx\frac{1}{d}\Biggl[\Tr[\hat{V}^{2}\big]-2\beta\,\Tr[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}\hat{V}^{2}]+2\beta^{2}\left(\Tr[\hat{H}^{2}_{0}\hat{V}^{2}]-\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\hat{H}^{2}_{0}\big]\Tr[\hat{V}^{2}]\right)\Biggr].

(S36)

Imaginary-time Heisenberg evolution.

Using

e^{\pm\beta\hat{H}_{0}}=\mathbb{I}\pm\beta\hat{H}_{0}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\hat{H}_{0}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}),

we obtain, up to
\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2})
,

\displaystyle\hat{V}(\beta)

\displaystyle=e^{\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\hat{V}e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}=\hat{V}+\beta\bigl[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}\bigr]+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\bigl[\hat{H}_{0},\bigl[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}\bigr]\bigr]+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}),

(S37)

where

[\hat{H}_{0},[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]]=\hat{H}_{0}^{2}\hat{V}-2\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{V}\hat{H}_{0}^{2}.

Term 2:
\langle\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\rangle_{2\beta}
.

Using Eq. (
S37
), we expand

\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}=\hat{V}^{2}+\beta\,[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]\,\hat{V}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\,[\hat{H}_{0},[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]]\,\hat{V}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\rangle_{2\beta}

\displaystyle:=\Tr[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\hat{V}^{2}\big]-\frac{2\beta}{d}\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}^{2}]+\frac{\beta}{d}\Tr\!\left([\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]\hat{V}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\beta^{2}}{d}\left(\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}^{2}\hat{V}^{2}]-\frac{\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}^{2}]\Tr[\hat{V}^{2}\big]}{d}\right)-\frac{2\beta^{2}}{d}\Tr\!\left(\hat{H}_{0}[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]\hat{V}\right)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2d}\Tr\!\left([\hat{H}_{0},[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]]\hat{V}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}).

(S38)

Now simplify the trace terms using cyclicity:

\Tr\!\left([\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]\hat{V}\right)=\Tr(\hat H_0\hat V^2)-\Tr(\hat V\hat H_0\hat V)=0,\quad\Tr\!\left(\hat{H}_{0}[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]\hat{V}\right)=\Tr(\hat H_0^2\hat V^2)-\Tr(\hat H_0\hat V\hat H_0\hat V),

\Tr\!\left([\hat{H}_{0},[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]]\hat{V}\right)=2\Tr(\hat H_0^2\hat V^2)-2\Tr(\hat H_0\hat V\hat H_0\hat V).

Substituting these identities into Eq. (
S38
) yields

\langle\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\rangle_{2\beta}\approx\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\hat{V}^{2}\big]-\frac{2\beta}{d}\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}^{2}]+\frac{\beta^{2}}{d}\left(\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}^{2}\hat{V}^{2}]+\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}]-\frac{2\,\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}^{2}]\Tr[\hat{V}^{2}\big]}{d}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}).

High-temperature expansion of
I_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)
and
\delta V
.

Taking the difference,

I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)=\langle\hat{V}^{2}\rangle_{2\beta}-\langle\hat{V}(\beta)\hat{V}\rangle_{2\beta}\approx\frac{\beta^{2}}{d}\left(\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}^{2}\hat{V}^{2}]-\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}]\right)+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}).

Upon using the identity (for Hermitian
\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}
and
\hat{V}
)

\|[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\Tr\!\left([\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]^{\dagger}[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{V}]\right)=2\left(\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}^{2}\hat{V}^{2}]-\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}\hat{H}_{0}\hat{V}]\right),

we obtain the compact form

\displaystyle I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\beta^{2}}{2d}\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}),\qquad\delta V=\sqrt{2I^{\,}_{\mathrm{WY}}(\beta)}\approx\beta\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{\,}_{\mathrm{HS}}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2}).

(S39)

In Sec.
S3.4.3
, we show that

\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{\,}_{\mathrm{HS}}\asymp\sqrt{Nd}
,
which implies
\delta V\asymp\beta\sqrt{N}
as
\beta\to 0
in the thermodynamic limit.

S3.3.2
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
in the high-temperature regime

We now evaluate the fidelity susceptibility
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}

(
S13
) in the high-temperature regime.
Expanding the Boltzmann weights to second order in
\beta
gives

e^{-\beta E^{(0)}_{n}}=1-\beta E^{(0)}_{n}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\left(E^{(0)}_{n}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}),

hence

\displaystyle\bigl(e^{-\beta E_{m}^{(0)}}-e^{-\beta E_{n}^{(0)}}\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\left(E^{(0)}_{m}-E^{(0)}_{n}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}).

(S40)

Moreover,

\displaystyle Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)=\Tr[e^{-2\beta\hat{H}_{0}}]=d+2\beta^{2}\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}^{2}]+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}),\qquad(\Tr[\hat{H}_{0}\big]=0).

(S41)

Substituting Eqs. (
S40
) and (
S41
) into
Eq. (
S13
) yields

\displaystyle\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\beta^{2}}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}|V^{\,}_{mn}|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3})=\frac{2\beta^{2}}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}|V^{\,}_{mn}|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{3}).

(S42)

In Sec.
S3.4.4
, we show that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}|V^{\,}_{mn}|^{2}\asymp Nd
for an extensive local
\hat{V}
,
which implies
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}(\beta)\asymp\beta^{2}N
as
\beta\to 0
.

S3.3.3
Threshold driving rate
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
in the high-temperature regime

We finally evaluate the threshold driving rate
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}

(
10
)
using
\delta V
(
S39
)
and
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
(
S42
):

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta\,d^{-1/2}\,\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{\,}_{2}}{(2\beta^{2}/d)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}|V^{\,}_{mn}|^{2}}\,\alpha=\Gamma^{\,}_{N}\,\frac{1}{\beta}\,\frac{\left(d^{-1/2}\,\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{\,}_{2}\right)/\delta V^{(0)}}{\left((2/d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}|V^{\,}_{mn}|^{2}\right)/\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}}\equiv\Gamma^{\,}_{N}\,f^{\,}_{\mathrm{hi}\text{-}T}(\beta),

where

f^{\,}_{\mathrm{hi}\text{-}T}(\beta)=\frac{c^{\,}_{2}}{\beta},\qquad c^{\,}_{2}:=\frac{\left(d^{-1/2}\,\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{\,}_{2}\right)/\delta V^{(0)}}{\left((2/d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}|V^{\,}_{mn}|^{2}\right)/\chi^{(0)}_{\mathrm{F}}}.

This establishes the high-temperature scaling form in Theorem
1
, with a model-dependent coefficient
c^{\,}_{2}>0
.

S3.4
System-size scaling of key quantities

In this subsection, we derive the
N
-scaling of several quantities that appear above.
Though the derivation is quite general, one may imagine a system of
N
spin-
1/2
degrees of freedom, so that the Hilbert-space dimension is
d=2^{N}
.
We focus on the interpolating Hamiltonian

\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda}=\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}+\lambda\hat{V}.

Unless stated otherwise, we assume a standard many-body setting in which

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{h}^{\,}_{i},\qquad\hat{V}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{v}^{\,}_{i},

(S43)

with each
\hat{h}^{\,}_{i}
and
\hat{v}^{\,}_{i}
acting on
\mathcal{O}(1)
sites (finite range) and having an
\mathcal{O}(1)
operator norm.
We further assume that we are away from criticality so that low-energy gaps remain
\mathcal{O}(1)
,
and that no symmetry/selection-rule obstruction forces the relevant matrix elements to vanish.
Without loss of generality, we may take
\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{h}^{\,}_{i}]=\Tr[\hat{v}^{\,}_{i}]=0
for all
i
(by shifting each local term by a multiple of the identity).

Note that, for translation-invariant systems away from criticality,
the extensivity of
(\delta V)^{2}
[Eq. (
S10
)] and

\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
[Eq. (
S13
)] can be established rigorously
via exponential clustering of correlations
41
;
61
;
62
, which in turn follows from the Lieb–Robinson bound
56
.
Here, we instead give a more elementary, intuitive argument.

S3.4.1
Scaling of
|V^{\,}_{10}|
in the low-temperature regime

We consider
|V^{\,}_{10}|=|\langle E_{1}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{0}^{(0)}\rangle|
,
which enters the low-temperature expansion of
\delta V
[Eq. (
S19
)].
At low temperature, a convenient physical picture is that the lowest excited state that couples to the ground state
under
\hat{V}
is a delocalized single-quasiparticle superposition of local excitations:

|E_{1}^{(0)}\rangle\simeq\ket{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{\mathrm{i}kj}\ket{j},

where
\ket{j}
is localized near site
j
and
k
is a crystal momentum.
Assuming that
\hat{v}^{\,}_{j}
creates
\ket{j}
from the ground state with an
\mathcal{O}(1)
amplitude
a

(namely,
\hat{v}^{\,}_{j}|E_{0}^{(0)}\rangle\simeq a\ket{j}
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\langle E_{1}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{0}^{(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\bra{k}\hat{V}|E_{0}^{(0)}\rangle=\sum^{N}_{j=1}\bra{k}\hat{v}_{j}|E_{0}^{(0)}\rangle\simeq a\sum^{N}_{j=1}\innerproduct{k}{j}=a\sum^{N}_{j=1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum^{N}_{\ell=1}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\ell}\innerproduct{\ell}{j}\right)=a\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum^{N}_{j=1}e^{-\mathrm{i}kj}.

The remaining factor is a standard geometric lattice sum. For a periodic chain, the crystal momentum is quantized, and one has the exact identity (see, e.g., Ref.
5
):

\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\mathrm{i}kj}=N\,\delta^{\,}_{k,0}.

Since we have chosen
|E_{1}^{(0)}\rangle
such that
V^{\,}_{10}\neq 0
, the corresponding lattice sum does not cancel and therefore contributes a factor proportional to
N
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\langle E_{1}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{0}^{(0)}\rangle\right|\asymp\sqrt{N}.

(S44)

Remark.

A crude upper bound is
|V^{\,}_{10}|\leq\|\hat{V}\|\asymp N
.
The estimate (
S44
) is the anticipated scaling in generic gapped phases when the lowest relevant
excitation is a delocalized quasiparticle and the local creation amplitude
a
is
\mathcal{O}(1)
.

S3.4.2
Scaling of
|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}/\Delta^{2}
in the low-temperature regime

The ratio
|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}/\Delta^{2}
, which appears in the low-temperature expansion of

\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
[Eq. (
S27
)],
can be estimated using Eq. (
S44
) together with
\Delta=\mathcal{O}(1)
in a gapped phase:

|V^{\,}_{10}|^{2}/\Delta^{2}\asymp N.

S3.4.3
Scaling of
\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{\,}_{\mathrm{HS}}
in the high-temperature regime

The Hilbert–Schmidt norm
\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}^{\,}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
appears in the high-temperature expansion of
\delta V

[Eq. (
S39
)].
To estimate its
N
-scaling, we use the locality assumptions in Eq. (
S43
).
Write the commutator as

[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}^{\,}]=\sum_{i,j}[\hat{h}^{\,}_{i},\hat{v}^{\,}_{j}].

If
\hat{h}^{\,}_{i}
and
\hat{v}^{\,}_{j}
have disjoint supports, then they commute and
[\hat{h}^{\,}_{i},\hat{v}^{\,}_{j}]=0
.
Because the terms are finite range, for each
i
only
\mathcal{O}(1)
values of
j
overlap, so there are only
\mathcal{O}(N)
nonzero local
commutators in the sum. Denote these nonzero terms by
\{\hat{c}^{\,}_{a}\}_{a=1}^{\mathcal{O}(N)}
, where each
\hat{c}^{\,}_{a}
is supported on

\mathcal{O}(1)
sites and has
\mathcal{O}(1)
norm.
Then,

\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}^{\,}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}=\sum_{a,b}\Tr\!\left((\hat{c}^{\,}_{a})^{\dagger}\hat{c}^{\,}_{b}\right).

By locality and tracelessness of the building blocks,
\Tr((\hat{c}^{\,}_{a})^{\dagger}\hat{c}^{\,}_{b})
vanishes unless the supports
of
\hat{c}^{\,}_{a}
and
\hat{c}^{\,}_{b}
overlap to form an
\mathcal{O}(1)
-sized connected cluster; hence only
\mathcal{O}(N)
pairs
(a,b)
contribute.
For each such nonzero pair, the operator
(\hat{c}^{\,}_{a})^{\dagger}\hat{c}^{\,}_{b}
acts nontrivially on only
\mathcal{O}(1)
sites, so tracing over the
remaining
N-\mathcal{O}(1)
inactive sites produces an overall multiplicative factor

\Tr(\mathbb{I})=2^{\,N-\mathcal{O}(1)}\propto d.

Therefore,

\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}^{\,}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\asymp Nd,
so that

\|[\hat{H}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}^{\,}]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\asymp\sqrt{Nd}.

S3.4.4
Scaling of
\sum_{m\neq n}|V^{\,}_{mn}|^{2}
in the high-temperature regime

Consider

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}|V_{mn}|^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{V}^{\,2}]-\sum_{n}|V_{nn}|^{2},

which enters the high-temperature expansion of
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
[Eq. (
S42
)].
We first estimate the basis-independent quantity
\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{V}^{\,2}]=\|\hat{V}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}
.
Write
\hat{V}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{v}^{\,}_{i}
as a sum of local terms with
\mathcal{O}(1)
norm, and shift each local term by the identity so that
\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{v}^{\,}_{i}]=0
.
Expanding
\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{V}^{\,2}]
then yields a sum over pairs
(i,j)
.
If
\hat{v}^{\,}_{i}
and
\hat{v}^{\,}_{j}
have disjoint supports, the trace factorizes and vanishes because the local terms are traceless.
Hence, only
\mathcal{O}(N)
pairs
(i,j)
with overlapping supports contribute.
Moreover, each nonzero contribution acts nontrivially on only
\mathcal{O}(1)
sites, so tracing over the remaining
N-\mathcal{O}(1)
inactive sites produces an overall factor proportional to
d=2^{N}
.
Therefore, generically,

\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{V}^{\,2}]\asymp Nd.

Next, the diagonal contribution satisfies the general bound

0\leq\sum_{n}|V^{\,}_{nn}|^{2}\leq\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{V}^{\,2}],

and therefore the off-diagonal sum is also bounded above by
\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{V}^{\,2}]
.
For a generic local drive (i.e., excluding symmetry-enforced selection rules or fine-tuned cases where
\hat{V}
is (block-)diagonal in the
\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}
eigenbasis), we do not expect the diagonal part
\sum_{n}|V_{nn}|^{2}
alone to account for essentially all of
\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{V}^{\,2}]=\sum_{m,n}|V_{mn}|^{2}
.
Under this genericity assumption, the off-diagonal contribution remains extensive, so

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}|V_{mn}|^{2}\asymp Nd.

S4
Transverse-field Ising chain and quantum XY chain: exact results via the transfer-matrix method

We consider driven spin chains with periodic boundary conditions, whose Hamiltonian takes the form

\hat{H}^{\,}_{\lambda}=\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}+\lambda(t)\,\hat{V},

where the unperturbed Hamiltonian is of Ising type,

\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}=-J\sum^{N}_{j=1}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1},

with
J>0
. The control parameter
\lambda(t)
is time-dependent and monotonic, with
\lambda(0)=0
.

We study two representative choices of the driving term
\hat{V}
.
For the transverse-field Ising chain (TFIC), we take

\hat{V}^{\,}_{\mathrm{TFIC}}=-J\sum^{N}_{j=1}X^{\,}_{j}

with

\lambda(t)=\frac{h(t)}{J},

where
h(t)
is the time-dependent transverse field, and we assume
h(0)=0
.
For the quantum XY chain (QXYC), we take

\hat{V}^{\,}_{\mathrm{QXYC}}=-J\sum^{N}_{j=1}\left(X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{j+1}-Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}\right)

with

\lambda(t)=\frac{1+\gamma(t)}{2},

where
\gamma(t)
is the time-dependent anisotropy parameter, and we assume
\gamma(0)=-1
.

We assume that the system is initially in a thermal state at inverse temperature
\beta
,

\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(\beta)=\frac{e^{-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}}}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)}

with

Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)\equiv\Tr\!e^{-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}}.

The Boltzmann weight can be factorized as

e^{-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0}}=e^{\beta J\sum^{N}_{j=1}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}=\prod^{N}_{j=1}e^{\beta JZ^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}.

For later convenience, we evaluate
Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)
.
Since all
Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}
commute,
\hat{H}_{0}
is diagonal in the
Z
-product basis

|\mathbf{s}\rangle=|s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}\rangle
, where
s^{\,}_{j}=\pm 1
are the eigenvalues of
Z^{\,}_{j}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)=\Tr\!\left(e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0}}\right)=\sum_{\{s^{\,}_{j}=\pm 1\}}\exp\!\Big(\beta J\sum_{j=1}^{N}s^{\,}_{j}s_{j+1}\Big).

Introduce the transfer matrix
T\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
with entries

\displaystyle T^{\,}_{s,s^{\prime}}=\exp(\beta J\, s s')=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\beta J}&e^{-\beta J}\\
e^{-\beta J}&e^{\beta J}\end{pmatrix},

so that
Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)=\Tr(T^N)
.
The eigenvalues of
T
are

\lambda_{\pm}=e^{\beta J}\pm e^{-\beta J},

i.e.

\lambda_{+}=2\cosh(\beta J)

and

\lambda_{-}=2\sinh(\beta J).

Therefore,

\displaystyle Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)=\lambda_{+}^{N}+\lambda_{-}^{N}=(2\cosh(\beta J))^{N}+(2\sinh(\beta J))^{N}=2^{N}\Big(\cosh^{N}(\beta J)+\sinh^{N}(\beta J)\Big).

(S45)

With these preliminaries, we now evaluate both
\delta V
[Eq. (
6
)]
and
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
[Eq. (
11
)] for the two models at arbitrary temperature.

S4.1
Evaluation of
\delta V

Our first goal is to compute
\delta V
[Eq. (
6
)] for both models:

\displaystyle\delta V=\frac{\|[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{\,}_{2}}{\|\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}\|^{\,}_{2}}=\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{\frac{\Tr\!\left(\hat{\rho}^{2}_{0}\hat{V}^{2}\right)-\Tr\!\left[\left(\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}\hat{V}\right)^{2}\right]}{\Tr\!\left(\hat{\rho}^{2}_{0}\right)}}\,.

(S46)

It is convenient to rewrite
\delta V
in terms of unnormalized Boltzmann weights.
Define
K\equiv\beta J
and use

\hat{\rho}_{0}=\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)}e^{K\sum_{j}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}
. One finds

\displaystyle\Tr(\hat{\rho}_0^2)

\displaystyle=\frac{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}},

(S47)

\displaystyle\Tr(\hat{\rho}_0^2\hat V^2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}}\Tr\!\left(e^{2K\sum_{j}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}\hat{V}^{2}\right),

(S48)

\displaystyle\Tr\!\left[(\hat{\rho}_{0}\hat{V})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)^{2}}\Tr\!\left(e^{K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\hat{V}\,e^{K\sum_{j}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}\hat{V}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(\delta V\right)^{2}=\frac{2}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\Bigg[\Tr\!\left(e^{2K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\hat{V}^{2}\right)-\Tr\!\left(e^{K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\hat{V}\,e^{K\sum_{j}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}\hat{V}\right)\Bigg].

(S49)

S4.1.1
Evaluation of
\delta V
in TFIC

For the TFIC drive,

\hat{V}^{\,}_{\rm TFIC}=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{\,}_{j}.

Step 1: Compute

\Tr(e^{2K\sum^{\,}_{i} Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\hat V^2)
.
Expanding
\hat{V}^{2}_{\rm TFIC}
gives

\hat{V}^{2}_{\rm TFIC}=J^{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{k}.

Since
e^{2K\sum Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}
is diagonal in the
Z
-basis,
only “on-site” terms of
X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{k}
contribute to the trace.
For
j\neq k
,
X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{k}
flips two distinct spins and has vanishing diagonal elements,
while for
j=k
,
(X^{\,}_{j})^{2}=\mathbb{I}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\Tr\!\left(e^{2K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}^{2}\right)=J^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Tr\!\left(e^{2K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\mathbb{I}\right)=NJ^{2}Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta).

(S50)

Step 2: Compute

\Tr(e^{K\sum_{i} Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\hat V\,e^{K\sum_{j} Z_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}\hat V)
.
Similarly,

\displaystyle A:=\Tr\!\left(e^{K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}\,e^{K\sum_{j}Z_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}\right)

\displaystyle=J^{2}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}\Tr\!\left(e^{K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}X^{\,}_{k}\,e^{K\sum_{j}Z_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}X^{\,}_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=J^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Tr\!\left(e^{K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}X^{\,}_{k}\,e^{K\sum_{j}Z_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}X^{\,}_{k}\right),

where we have used the fact that, in the
Z
-basis, the trace is nonzero only for
k=\ell
.
By translational invariance, each term has the same value, thus

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\,NJ^{2}\Tr\!\left(e^{K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}X^{\,}_{1}\,e^{K\sum_{j}Z_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}X^{\,}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,NJ^{2}\Tr\!\left[\left(e^{KZ_{1}\left(Z_{2}+Z^{\,}_{N}\right)}X^{\,}_{1}\,e^{KZ_{1}\left(Z_{2}+Z^{\,}_{N}\right)}X^{\,}_{1}\right)\left(e^{2K\sum^{N-1}_{i=2}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\right)\right].

To proceed, recall that
|s^{\,}_{1}\rangle
(with
s^{\,}_{1}=\pm 1
) is the eigenstate of
Z^{\,}_{1}
with eigenvalue
s^{\,}_{1}
,
and
X^{\,}_{1}|s^{\,}_{1}\rangle=|-s^{\,}_{1}\rangle
.
We use this basis to perform the trace over the first spin:

\displaystyle\Tr^{\,}_{1}\left(e^{KZ_{1}\left(Z_{2}+Z^{\,}_{N}\right)}X^{\,}_{1}\,e^{KZ_{1}\left(Z_{2}+Z^{\,}_{N}\right)}X^{\,}_{1}\right)=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{\,}_{s^{\,}_{1}=\pm 1}\langle s^{\,}_{1}|\left(e^{KZ_{1}\left(Z_{2}+Z^{\,}_{N}\right)}X^{\,}_{1}\,e^{KZ_{1}\left(Z_{2}+Z^{\,}_{N}\right)}X^{\,}_{1}\right)|s^{\,}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum^{\,}_{s^{\,}_{1}=\pm 1}e^{Ks^{\,}_{1}\left(Z_{2}+Z^{\,}_{N}\right)}e^{-Ks^{\,}_{1}\left(Z_{2}+Z^{\,}_{N}\right)}=2.

Therefore, we obtain

A=2NJ^{2}\Tr^{\,}_{2,3,\cdots,N}\left(e^{2K\sum^{N-1}_{i=2}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\right)=2NJ^{2}Q^{\,}_{N-1}(2\beta),

where

\displaystyle Q^{\,}_{N-1}(2\beta)\equiv\sum^{\,}_{s^{\,}_{2},\cdots,s^{\,}_{N}=\pm 1}\exp\!\Big(2K\sum^{N-1}_{i=2}s^{\,}_{i}s^{\,}_{i+1}\Big).

(S51)

One recognizes that
Q^{\,}_{N-1}(2\beta)
is the partition function of the open Ising chain with
N-1
spins at inverse temperature
2\beta
.
Its value can be obtained using a recursive method (see, e.g., Ref.
66
):

Q^{\,}_{N-1}(2\beta)=2\left[2\cosh(2K)\right]^{N-2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle A=\Tr\!\left(e^{K\sum_{i}Z^{\,}_{i}Z^{\,}_{i+1}}\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}\,e^{K\sum_{j}Z_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}}\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}\right)=2NJ^{2}Q^{\,}_{N-1}(2\beta)=4NJ^{2}\left[2\cosh(2K)\right]^{N-2}.

(S52)

Step 3: Assemble
(\delta V)^{2}
.

Plugging (
S50
) and (
S52
) into (
S49
) yields

\displaystyle(\delta V^{\,}_{\rm TFIC})^{2}=2NJ^{2}\left(1-2\frac{Q^{\,}_{N-1}(2\beta)}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\right).

Using
Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)=(2\cosh(2\beta J))^{N}+(2\sinh(2\beta J))^{N}
[Eq. (
S45
) with
\beta\to 2\beta
], we obtain the closed form

\displaystyle\boxed{\delta V^{\,}_{\rm TFIC}=\sqrt{2N}J\tanh(2\beta J)\,\left(\frac{1+\tanh^{N-2}(2\beta J)}{1+\tanh^{N}(2\beta J)}\right)^{1/2}.}

(S53)

In particular, for fixed finite
\beta
and large
N
(so that
\tanh^{N}(2\beta J)\to 0
),

\displaystyle\delta V_{\rm TFIC}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\sqrt{2N}\,J\,\tanh(2\beta J).

(S54)

S4.1.2
Evaluation of
\delta V
in QXYC

We now consider the quantum XY drive,

\hat{V}^{\,}_{\rm QXYC}=\hat{V}^{\,}_{x}+\hat{V}^{\,}_{z},

with

\hat{V}_{x}=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{j+1},

and

\hat{V}_{z}=+J\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}.

Since
\hat{V}^{\,}_{z}
is diagonal in the
Z
-basis, it commutes with
\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\delta V^{\,}_{\mathrm{QXYC}}=\frac{\|[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}^{\,}_{\mathrm{QXYC}}]\|^{\,}_{2}}{\|\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}\|^{\,}_{2}}=\frac{\|[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}^{\,}_{x}]\|^{\,}_{2}}{\|\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}\|^{\,}_{2}}\equiv\delta V^{\,}_{x}.

Now repeat the TFIC steps with the bond-flip operators

\hat{B}^{\,}_{j}\equiv X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{j+1}
, so that
\hat{V}^{\,}_{x}=-J\sum_{j}\hat{B}^{\,}_{j}
.
Exactly as before, only identical bonds contribute to both traces in Eq. (
S49
).
The analog of
Q^{\,}_{N-1}(2\beta)
becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(2\beta)\equiv\sum_{\{s^{\,}_{j}=\pm 1\}}\exp\!\Big(K\sum_{j=1}^{N}s^{\,}_{j}s_{j+1}\Big)\,\exp\!\Big(K\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{s}^{\,}_{j}\tilde{s}_{j+1}\Big),

(S55)

where
\{\tilde{s}^{\,}_{j}\}
differs from
\{s^{\,}_{j}\}
by flipping two adjacent spins, e.g.,

\tilde{s}^{\,}_{1}=-s^{\,}_{1}
,
\tilde{s}^{\,}_{2}=-s^{\,}_{2}
, and
\tilde{s}^{\,}_{j}=s^{\,}_{j}
for
j\geq 3
.
A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}s^{\,}_{j}s_{j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{s}^{\,}_{j}\tilde{s}_{j+1}=2s^{\,}_{1}s^{\,}_{2}+2\sum_{j=3}^{N-1}s^{\,}_{j}s_{j+1},

i.e., the two bonds connecting the flipped dimer to the rest cancel, while all remaining bonds are doubled to
2K
.
Hence, the sum factorizes, giving

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(2\beta)=\sum_{s^{\,}_{1},s^{\,}_{2}=\pm 1}e^{2Ks^{\,}_{1}s^{\,}_{2}}\sum_{s^{\,}_{3},\ldots,s^{\,}_{N}=\pm 1}\exp\!\Big(2K\sum_{j=3}^{N-1}s^{\,}_{j}s_{j+1}\Big)=\big(4\cosh(2K)\big)\times 2\big(2\cosh(2K)\big)^{N-3}=4\big(2\cosh(2K)\big)^{N-2}.

Consequently, one obtains the same
\delta V
as in the TFIC case [Eq. (
S53
)]:

\delta V^{\,}_{\rm QXYC}=\delta V^{\,}_{\rm TFIC}.

S4.2
Evaluation of
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}

Next, we compute the fidelity susceptibility
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
using Eq. (
S13
) for both
the TFIC and the QXYC models:

\displaystyle\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{2}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}(p^{\,}_{m}-p^{\,}_{n})^{2}\frac{|\langle E_{m}^{(0)}|\hat{V}|E_{n}^{(0)}\rangle|^{2}}{(E_{m}^{(0)}-E_{n}^{(0)})^{2}},\qquad p^{\,}_{n}:=e^{-\beta E_{n}^{(0)}}.

(S56)

Here and throughout this section, we exclude degenerate pairs with
E_{m}^{(0)}=E_{n}^{(0)}
(i.e., the
\Delta E=0
channels),
consistent with using the non-degenerate perturbative form of
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
.

S4.2.1
Evaluation of
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
in TFIC

For the TFIC drive given by

\hat{H}_{0}=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}
and

\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{\,}_{j}

with

(J>0),

an eigenbasis of
\hat{H}_{0}
is the
Z
-product basis

\ket{\mathbf s}:=\ket{s_1,\dots,s_N}
with
s^{\,}_{j}=\pm 1
,
and eigenenergies

E(\mathbf{s}):=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}s^{\,}_{j}s_{j+1}.

The operator
X^{\,}_{j}
flips spin
j
. Denote by
\mathbf{s}^{(j)}
the configuration obtained from
\mathbf{s}

by flipping
s^{\,}_{j}\to-s^{\,}_{j}
.
Then, the operator
\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}
connects
|\mathbf{s}\rangle
only to the configuration
|\mathbf{s}^{(j)}\rangle
obtained by flipping
j
, with matrix element

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{s}|\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}|\mathbf{s}^{(j)}\rangle=-J,\qquad\langle\mathbf{s}|\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}|\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\rangle=0\quad\text{if }\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{s}^{(j)}\ \forall j.

Hence, the sum in (
S56
) reduces to single-spin-flip pairs.

The energy difference associated with flipping spin
j
is

\Delta E_{j}(\mathbf{s})\equiv E(\mathbf{s})-E(\mathbf{s}^{(j)})=-2J\,s^{\,}_{j}(s_{j-1}+s_{j+1}).

Therefore,
\Delta E_{j}=-4Js^{\,}_{j}s^{\,}_{j+1}
when
s_{j-1}=s_{j+1}
, while
\Delta E_{j}=0
when
s_{j-1}=-s_{j+1}
.
The latter contribution is excluded in the sum in Eq. (
S56
).
Hence, we may restrict to the case
s_{j-1}=s_{j+1}
.

Using
|\langle\mathbf{s}|\hat{V}_{\rm TFIC}|\mathbf{s}^{(j)}\rangle|^{2}=J^{2}
and
(\Delta E_{j})^{2}=(4J)^{2}
on the contributing configurations,
and writing the sum over
(m,n)
as a sum over
(\mathbf{s},j)
,
Eq. (
S56
) becomes

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm TFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\sum_{\mathbf{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}I^{\,}_{j}(\mathbf{s})\big(p(\mathbf{s})-p(\mathbf{s}^{(j)})\big)^{2}\frac{J^{2}}{16J^{2}},

where
I^{\,}_{j}(\mathbf{s})
denotes the indicator function:

\displaystyle I^{\,}_{j}(\mathbf{s}):=\frac{1+s_{j-1}s_{j+1}}{2}=\begin{cases}1,&s_{j-1}=s_{j+1},\\
0,&s_{j-1}=-s_{j+1}.\end{cases}

Now use

\big(p(\mathbf{s})-p(\mathbf{s}^{(j)})\big)^{2}=p(\mathbf{s})^{2}\Big(1-e^{-\beta(E(\mathbf{s}^{(j)})-E(\mathbf{s}))}\Big)^{2}=e^{-2\beta E(\mathbf{s})}\Big(1-e^{+\beta\Delta E_{j}(\mathbf{s})}\Big)^{2},

so that the entire sum becomes an expectation value in the
2\beta
thermal average:

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm TFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\sum_{\mathbf{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}I^{\,}_{j}(\mathbf{s})e^{-2\beta E(\mathbf{s})}\Big(1-e^{+\beta\Delta E_{j}(\mathbf{s})}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big\langle I^{\,}_{j}(\mathbf{s})\big(1-e^{+\beta\Delta E_{j}(\mathbf{s})}\big)^{2}\Big\rangle_{2\beta},\qquad\langle\cdots\rangle_{2\beta}:=\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\sum_{\mathbf{s}}e^{-2\beta E(\mathbf{s})}(\cdots).

Due to translation invariance, we may replace
\sum^{N}_{j=1}
by
N
times a representative site:

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm TFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{N}{8}\Big\langle I^{\,}_{1}(\mathbf{s})\Big(1-e^{-4\beta J\,s^{\,}_{1}s_{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big\rangle_{2\beta}.

(S57)

We evaluate Eq. (
S57
) using the transfer-matrix method.
Let
K^{\,}_{2}\equiv 2\beta J
. Then

\displaystyle Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)=\sum_{\mathbf{s}}e^{K^{\,}_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}s^{\,}_{i}s^{\,}_{i+1}}=\Tr(T^N)=\lambda^{N}_{+}+\lambda^{N}_{-},\qquad T^{\,}_{s,s^{\prime}}:=e^{K^{\,}_{2}ss^{\prime}}.

The eigenvalues of the transfer matrix
T
are

\lambda^{\,}_{+}=2\cosh K^{\,}_{2}

and

\lambda^{\,}_{-}=2\sinh K^{\,}_{2}.

Define

\displaystyle S^{\,}_{1}:=\sum_{\mathbf{s}}e^{K^{\,}_{2}\sum_{i}s_{i}s_{i+1}}I^{\,}_{1}(\mathbf{s})\Big(1-e^{-2K^{\,}_{2}\,s^{\,}_{1}s_{2}}\Big)^{2},

so that Eq. (
S57
) can be written as

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm TFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{NS^{\,}_{1}}{8\,Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}.

(S58)

We are left with evaluating
S^{\,}_{1}
using the transfer-matrix method.

Writing
e^{K^{\,}_{2}\sum_{i}s_{i}s_{i+1}}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}T_{s_{i},s_{i+1}}
and noting that

I^{\,}_{1}(\mathbf{s})=1
enforces
s_{N}=s_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{1}=\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm 1}T_{s_{1},s_{2}}\,(T^{N-2})_{s_{2},s_{2}}\,T_{s_{2},s_{1}}\,\Big(1-e^{-2K^{\,}_{2}s_{1}s_{2}}\Big)^{2}.

The prefactor simplifies as

\displaystyle T_{s_{1},s_{2}}T_{s_{2},s_{1}}\Big(1-e^{-2K^{\,}_{2}s_{1}s_{2}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=e^{2K^{\,}_{2}s_{1}s_{2}}\Big(1-e^{-2K^{\,}_{2}s_{1}s_{2}}\Big)^{2}=\Big(e^{K^{\,}_{2}s_{1}s_{2}}-e^{-K^{\,}_{2}s_{1}s_{2}}\Big)^{2}=4\sinh^{2}K^{\,}_{2},

which is independent of
s_{1},s_{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle S^{\,}_{1}

\displaystyle=4\sinh^{2}K^{\,}_{2}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}(T^{N-2})_{s_{2},s_{2}}=8\sinh^{2}K^{\,}_{2}\;\Tr(T^{N-2})=8\sinh^{2}K^{\,}_{2}\;\big(\lambda_{+}^{N-2}+\lambda_{-}^{N-2}\big).

(S59)

Substituting (
S59
) into (
S58
) gives

\chi^{\rm TFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}=N\sinh^{2}K^{\,}_{2}\,\frac{\lambda_{+}^{N-2}+\lambda_{-}^{N-2}}{\lambda_{+}^{N}+\lambda_{-}^{N}}.

Using
\lambda_{+}=2\cosh K^{\,}_{2}
,
\lambda_{-}=2\sinh K^{\,}_{2}
, and
\lambda_{-}/\lambda_{+}=\tanh K^{\,}_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\boxed{\chi^{\rm TFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{N}{4}\,\tanh^{2}(2\beta J)\,\frac{1+\tanh^{N-2}(2\beta J)}{1+\tanh^{N}(2\beta J)}.}

(S60)

S4.2.2
Evaluation of
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
in QXYC

For the QXYC drive,

\hat{V}^{\,}_{\rm QXYC}=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{j+1}-Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}\Big)\equiv\hat{V}^{\,}_{x}+\hat{V}^{\,}_{z},

with

\hat{V}^{\,}_{x}=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{j+1}

and

\hat{V}^{\,}_{z}=+J\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}.

In Eq. (
S56
), only off-diagonal matrix elements
\langle m|\hat{V}|n\rangle
with
m\neq n
contribute.
Since
\hat{V}_{z}
is diagonal in the
Z
-basis, it does not contribute. Hence,
\chi^{\rm QXYC}_{\mathrm{F}}=\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}[\hat{V}_{x}]
.

Now
X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{j+1}
flips the adjacent spins
j
and
j+1
, mapping
\mathbf{s}\mapsto\mathbf{s}^{(j,j+1)}
,
with matrix element
\langle\mathbf{s}|\hat{V}_{x}|\mathbf{s}^{(j,j+1)}\rangle=-J
.
The corresponding energy difference under
\hat{H}_{0}
is

\Delta E_{j,j+1}(\mathbf{s}):=E(\mathbf{s})-E(\mathbf{s}^{(j,j+1)})=-2J\Big(s_{j-1}s^{\,}_{j}+s_{j+1}s_{j+2}\Big),

so
\Delta E_{j,j+1}\in\{0,\pm 4J\}
.
As above, the
\Delta E_{j,j+1}(\mathbf{s})=0
sector is excluded in the sum in Eq. (
S56
), so we restrict to the
\Delta E=\pm 4J
channels.
The computation proceeds exactly as in the TFIC case, with the only change that the relevant indicator is now expressed in terms of the bond variables

b_{j}:=s^{\,}_{j}s_{j+1}
(note that
E(\mathbf{s})=-J\sum_{j}b_{j}
).
In these variables,
X^{\,}_{j}X^{\,}_{j+1}
flips precisely two bonds,
b_{j-1}\to-b_{j-1}
and
b_{j+1}\to-b_{j+1}
,
while leaving the rest unchanged. This leads to the same transfer-matrix evaluation as above, and yields the same closed form:

\chi^{\rm QXYC}_{\mathrm{F}}=\chi^{\rm TFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}.

S4.3
Threshold driving rate
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}

Substituting Eqs. (
S53
) and (
S60
) into Eq. (
10
), we obtain for both the TFIC and QXYC

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\sqrt{2}J}{\sqrt{N}}\,\coth(2\beta J)\left(\frac{1+\tanh^{N}(2\beta J)}{1+\tanh^{N-2}(2\beta J)}\right)^{1/2}\alpha\equiv\Gamma^{\,}_{N}f^{\,}_{N}(\beta),

with

\Gamma^{\,}_{N}\equiv\frac{4\sqrt{2}J}{\sqrt{N}}\alpha,\qquad f^{\,}_{N}(\beta)\equiv\coth(2\beta J)\left(\frac{1+\tanh^{N}(2\beta J)}{1+\tanh^{N-2}(2\beta J)}\right)^{1/2}.

For any fixed finite
\beta>0
,
\tanh(2\beta J)<1
and thus

f(\beta):=\lim_{N\to\infty}f^{\,}_{N}(\beta)=\coth(2\beta J),\qquad f(\beta)=\begin{cases}1+2e^{-4\beta J}+\cdots,&\beta\to\infty,\\[2.0pt]
(2\beta J)^{-1}+\cdots,&\beta\to 0.\end{cases}

It is instructive to reverse the order of the thermodynamic limit and the low-temperature limit.
Taking the extreme-temperature limits at fixed
N
gives instead

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to\infty}f^{\,}_{N}(\beta)

\displaystyle=1+e^{-4\beta J}+\left(N-\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{-8\beta J}+\left(N-\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{-12\beta J}-\left(\frac{1}{3}N^{3}-\frac{3}{2}N^{2}+\frac{7}{6}N-\frac{3}{8}\right)e^{-16\beta J}+\mathcal{O}\!\big(e^{-20\beta J}\big),

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to 0}f^{\,}_{N}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\beta J}+\frac{2\beta J}{3}-\frac{(2\beta J)^{3}}{45}+\mathcal{O}\!\big((\beta J)^{5}\big),

showing that the thermodynamic and low-temperature limits do not commute.

S5
Mixed-field Ising chain: exact results via the transfer-matrix method

We consider the mixed-field Ising chain (MFIC) with periodic boundary conditions.
The Hamiltonian is written as
\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}+\lambda(t)\hat{V}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}
, where the
unperturbed part contains a longitudinal field in addition to the Ising coupling:

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\,}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}

\displaystyle=\sum^{N}_{j=1}\Big(-JZ^{\,}_{j}Z^{\,}_{j+1}+BZ^{\,}_{j}\Big),\qquad\hat{V}^{\,}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}=-J\sum^{N}_{j=1}X^{\,}_{j},\qquad\lambda(t)=\frac{h(t)}{J},

with
h(0)=0
.

We assume an initial thermal state at inverse temperature
\beta
,

\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}(\beta)=\frac{1}{Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)}e^{-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}

with

Z^{\,}_{0}(\beta)=\Tr\!e^{-\beta\hat{H}^{\,}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}.

Since
\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}
is diagonal in the
Z
-product basis, all traces reduce to classical Ising sums
and can be evaluated via the transfer-matrix method. Throughout this section, it is convenient to work at inverse temperature
2\beta
.
Define

\displaystyle K:=2\beta J,\qquad H:=2\beta B.

Introduce the symmetric
2\times 2
transfer matrix

\displaystyle T^{(B)}_{s,s^{\prime}}(2\beta)=\exp\!\left(K\,ss^{\prime}-\frac{H}{2}(s+s^{\prime})\right),\qquad s,s^{\prime}=\pm 1.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle T^{(B)}(2\beta)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{K-H}&e^{-K}\\
e^{-K}&e^{K+H}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{2\beta(J-B)}&e^{-2\beta J}\\
e^{-2\beta J}&e^{2\beta(J+B)}\end{pmatrix}.

The partition function at inverse temperature
2\beta
is

Z_{0}(2\beta)=\Tr\!\big[(T^{(B)})^{N}\big]=\Lambda_{+}^{N}+\Lambda_{-}^{N},

with eigenvalues

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\pm}=e^{K}\cosh H\pm\sqrt{e^{2K}\sinh^{2}H+e^{-2K}}.

(S61)

We will use the following two-eigenvalue trace identity later:
Let
T
be a
2\times 2
matrix with eigenvalues
\Lambda_{\pm}
with
\Lambda_{+}\neq\Lambda_{-}
. Then, for any
2\times 2
matrix
M
and any integer
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Tr(T^n M)=a^{\,}_{+}(M)\Lambda_{+}^{n}+a^{\,}_{-}(M)\Lambda_{-}^{n},\qquad a^{\,}_{+}(M):=\frac{\Tr(TM)-\Lambda_{-}\,\Tr(M)}{\Lambda_{+}-\Lambda_{-}},\qquad a^{\,}_{-}(M):=\frac{\Lambda_{+}\,\Tr(M)-\Tr(TM)}{\Lambda_{+}-\Lambda_{-}}.

(S62)

S5.1
Evaluation of
\delta V
in MFIC

We compute
\delta V
[Eq. (
6
)] in the form

\displaystyle\delta V=\frac{\|[\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0},\hat{V}]\|^{\,}_{2}}{\|\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}\|^{\,}_{2}}=\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{\frac{\Tr\!\left(\hat{\rho}^{2}_{0}\hat{V}^{2}\right)-\Tr\!\left[\left(\hat{\rho}^{\,}_{0}\hat{V}\right)^{2}\right]}{\Tr\!\left(\hat{\rho}^{2}_{0}\right)}}\,.

As in the TFIC computation [recall Eq. (
S49
)], it is convenient to rewrite
\delta V

in terms of unnormalized weights:

\displaystyle(\delta V)^{2}=\frac{2}{Z^{\,}_{0}(2\beta)}\Bigg[\Tr\!\left(e^{-2\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat{V}^{2}\right)-\Tr\!\left(e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat{V}\,e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat{V}\right)\Bigg],

(S63)

where
Z_{0}(2\beta)=\Tr(e^{-2\beta\hat H_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}})
.

Step 1: Compute
\Tr(e^{-2\beta\hat H_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat V^2)
.

With
\hat{V}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}
and
(X^{\,}_{j})^{2}=\mathbb{I}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}^{2}=J^{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}X_{j}X_{k}=NJ^{2}\mathbb{I}+J^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N}X_{j}X_{k}.

Since
e^{-2\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}
is diagonal in the
Z
-basis, only
j=k
contributes to the trace,
giving

\displaystyle\Tr\!\left(e^{-2\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat{V}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}^{2}\right)=NJ^{2}\,Z_{0}(2\beta).

(S64)

Step 2: Compute
\Tr(e^{-\beta\hat H_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat V\,e^{-\beta\hat H_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat V)
.

Using the same
Z
-basis argument (net spin flips must return to the original configuration),
only identical sites contribute:

\displaystyle A^{(B)}:=\Tr\!\left(e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat{V}\,e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}\hat{V}\right)=J^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Tr\!\left(e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}X_{k}\,e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}X_{k}\right).

In the
Z
-product basis
\ket{\mathbf s}=\ket{s_1,\dots,s_N}
,
X_{k}
flips
s_{k}
, and one finds

\langle\mathbf{s}|e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}X_{k}e^{-\beta\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}}X_{k}|\mathbf{s}\rangle=e^{-\beta E(\mathbf{s})}e^{-\beta E(\mathbf{s}^{(k)})},

where
E(\mathbf{s})=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}s_{j+1}+B\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}
.
By translation invariance it suffices to take
k=1
.
A direct cancellation shows that the bonds
(N,1)
and
(1,2)
and the field term
Bs_{1}
drop out in

E(\mathbf{s})+E(\mathbf{s}^{(1)})
, yielding

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{s})+E(\mathbf{s}^{(1)})=-2J\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}s_{j}s_{j+1}+2B\sum_{j=2}^{N}s_{j}.

Hence,
s_{1}
completely decouples and produces an overall factor
2
, so that

\displaystyle A^{(B)}=2NJ^{2}\,Q^{(B)}_{N-1}(2\beta),

(S65)

where
Q^{(B)}_{N-1}(2\beta)
is the partition function of an
open
Ising chain of length
N-1

at inverse temperature
2\beta
with longitudinal field
B
:

\displaystyle Q^{(B)}_{N-1}(2\beta):=\sum_{s_{2},\dots,s_{N}=\pm 1}\exp\!\left(K\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}s_{j}s_{j+1}-H\sum_{j=2}^{N}s_{j}\right),\qquad(K\equiv 2\beta J,\ H\equiv 2\beta B).

Introduce the boundary vector

\mathbf{u}:=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-H/2},e^{+H/2}\end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}},

and use the same transfer matrix at inverse temperature
2\beta
, i.e.
T^{(B)}\equiv T^{(B)}(2\beta)
.
Then

\displaystyle Q^{(B)}_{N-1}(2\beta)={\mathbf{u}}^{\mathsf{T}}\,(T^{(B)})^{N-2}\,{\mathbf{u}}=\Tr\!\big[(T^{(B)})^{N-2}U^{(B)}\big],\qquad U^{(B)}:={\mathbf{u}}{\mathbf{u}}^{\mathsf{T}}.

Applying Eq. (
S62
) with
T=T^{(B)}
,
M=U^{(B)}
, and
n=N-2
yields

\displaystyle Q^{(B)}_{N-1}(2\beta)

\displaystyle=c_{+}^{(B)}\Lambda_{+}^{N-2}+c_{-}^{(B)}\Lambda_{-}^{N-2},\qquad c_{\pm}^{(B)}:=a_{\pm}(U^{(B)}),

where
\Lambda^{\,}_{\pm}
are given by Eq. (
S61
), and

\displaystyle\Tr(U^{(B)})

\displaystyle={\mathbf{u}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{u}}=2\cosh H,\qquad\Tr(T^{(B)}U^{(B)})={\mathbf{u}}^{\mathsf{T}}T^{(B)}{\mathbf{u}}=2e^{K}\cosh(2H)+2e^{-K},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{+}-\Lambda_{-}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{e^{2K}\sinh^{2}H+e^{-2K}}.

Step 3: Assemble
\delta V
.

Substituting Eqs. (
S64
) and (
S65
) into
Eq. (
S63
) gives

\displaystyle\boxed{\delta V_{\mathrm{MFIC}}=\sqrt{2N}J\left(1-\frac{2Q^{(B)}_{N-1}(2\beta)}{Z_{0}(2\beta)}\right)^{1/2}.}

(S66)

In particular, for fixed finite
\beta
and large
N
[so that
(\Lambda^{\,}_{-}/\Lambda^{\,}_{+})^{N}\to 0
],
Eq. (
S66
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\delta V_{\rm MFIC}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\sqrt{2N}\,J\,\left(1-\frac{2c^{(B)}_{+}}{\Lambda^{2}_{+}}\right)^{1/2},

(S67)

with

\displaystyle c^{(B)}_{+}=\frac{e^{K}\sinh^{2}H+e^{-K}}{\sqrt{e^{2K}\sinh^{2}H+e^{-2K}}}+\cosh H,\qquad\Lambda^{\,}_{+}=e^{K}\cosh H+\sqrt{e^{2K}\sinh^{2}H+e^{-2K}}.

Setting
B=0
reduces
c^{(B)}_{+}
to
2
and
\Lambda^{\,}_{+}
to
2\cosh K
,
and hence reproduces the TFIC result

\delta V_{\rm TFIC}\to\sqrt{2N}\,J\,\tanh(2\beta J)
[Eq. (
S54
)].

S5.2
Evaluation of
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
in MFIC

We next compute the fidelity susceptibility
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
using Eq. (
S56
)
with
\hat{H}_{0}=\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{MFIC}}
and
\hat{V}=\hat{V}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}
.
Throughout this subsection we assume
B\neq 0,\pm 2J
, so that
B-J(s_{j-1}+s_{j+1})\neq 0
for all local configurations.

In the
Z
-product basis
\ket{\mathbf s}=\ket{s_1,\dots,s_N}
(
s_{j}=\pm 1
), the unperturbed energies are

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{s})=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}s_{j+1}+B\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j},\qquad(s_{N+1}\equiv s_{1}),

and
\hat{V}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}=-J\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}
flips a single spin:

\langle\mathbf{s}|\hat{V}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}|\mathbf{s}^{(j)}\rangle=-J
, where
\mathbf{s}^{(j)}
is obtained
from
\mathbf{s}
by
s_{j}\to-s_{j}
.
The corresponding energy difference is

\Delta E_{j}(\mathbf{s}):=E(\mathbf{s})-E(\mathbf{s}^{(j)})=2s_{j}\Big(B-J(s_{j-1}+s_{j+1})\Big).

Starting from Eq. (
S56
) and using

|\langle\mathbf{s}|\hat{V}_{\mathrm{MFIC}}|\mathbf{s}^{(j)}\rangle|^{2}=J^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm MFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2J^{2}}{Z_{0}(2\beta)}\sum_{\mathbf{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\big(p(\mathbf{s})-p(\mathbf{s}^{(j)})\big)^{2}}{\big(\Delta E_{j}(\mathbf{s})\big)^{2}},\qquad p(\mathbf{s}):=e^{-\beta E(\mathbf{s})}.

Using

\big(p(\mathbf{s})-p(\mathbf{s}^{(j)})\big)^{2}=e^{-2\beta E(\mathbf{s})}\big(1-e^{+\beta\Delta E_{j}(\mathbf{s})}\big)^{2}

and
(\Delta E_{j})^{2}=4\big(B-J(s_{j-1}+s_{j+1})\big)^{2}
, we can rewrite
\chi^{\,}_{\mathrm{F}}
as an expectation value
in the
2\beta
thermal average:

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm MFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{NJ^{2}}{2}\,\Bigg\langle\frac{\big(1-e^{+\beta\Delta E_{1}(\mathbf{s})}\big)^{2}}{\big(B-J(s_{N}+s_{2})\big)^{2}}\Bigg\rangle_{2\beta},\quad\langle\cdots\rangle_{2\beta}:=\frac{1}{Z_{0}(2\beta)}\sum_{\mathbf{s}}\exp\!\Big(K\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}s_{j+1}-H\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}\Big)\,(\cdots).

(S68)

Here, we used translation invariance to replace
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
by
N
times
j=1
, and

\beta\Delta E_{1}(\mathbf{s})=s_{1}\big(H-K(s_{N}+s_{2})\big)
.

Transfer-matrix evaluation.

Define

\displaystyle S^{(B)}_{1}:=\sum_{\mathbf{s}}\exp\!\Big(K\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}s_{j+1}-H\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}\Big)\,\frac{\big(1-e^{s_{1}(H-K(s_{N}+s_{2}))}\big)^{2}}{\big(B-J(s_{N}+s_{2})\big)^{2}},

so that Eq. (
S68
) becomes

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm MFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{NJ^{2}}{2}\,\frac{S^{(B)}_{1}}{Z_{0}(2\beta)}.

(S69)

We can write

\displaystyle S^{(B)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c=\pm 1}T^{(B)}_{a,b}\,T^{(B)}_{b,c}\,(T^{(B)})^{N-2}_{c,a}\;\frac{\big(1-e^{b(H-K(a+c))}\big)^{2}}{\big(B-J(a+c)\big)^{2}},

(S70)

where
a=s^{\,}_{N}
,
b=s^{\,}_{1}
, and
c=s^{\,}_{2}
.
Introduce the
2\times 2
matrix
\mathcal{M}^{(B)}
with entries

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(B)}_{a,c}:=\sum_{b=\pm 1}T^{(B)}_{a,b}\,T^{(B)}_{b,c}\;\frac{\big(1-e^{b(H-K(a+c))}\big)^{2}}{\big(B-J(a+c)\big)^{2}},\qquad a,c=\pm 1.

(S71)

Since
T^{(B)}
is symmetric, Eq. (
S70
) becomes

S^{(B)}_{1}=\Tr\!\Big((T^{(B)})^{N-2}\mathcal{M}^{(B)}\Big).

Explicit form of
\mathcal{M}^{(B)}
.

Evaluating the
b
-sum in Eq. (
S71
) gives the symmetric matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(B)}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{M}^{(B)}_{+,+}&\mathcal{M}^{(B)}_{+,-}\\
\mathcal{M}^{(B)}_{+,-}&\mathcal{M}^{(B)}_{-,-}\end{pmatrix},

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(B)}_{+,+}

\displaystyle=\frac{8e^{-H}\,\sinh^{2}\!\big(\frac{H-2K}{2}\big)}{(B-2J)^{2}}=\frac{8e^{-2\beta B}\,\sinh^{2}\!\big(\beta(B-2J)\big)}{(B-2J)^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(B)}_{-,-}

\displaystyle=\frac{8e^{+H}\,\sinh^{2}\!\big(\frac{H+2K}{2}\big)}{(B+2J)^{2}}=\frac{8e^{+2\beta B}\,\sinh^{2}\!\big(\beta(B+2J)\big)}{(B+2J)^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(B)}_{+,-}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\sinh^{2}\!\big(\frac{H}{2}\big)}{B^{2}}=\frac{8\sinh^{2}\!\big(\beta B\big)}{B^{2}}.

Closed form.

Using Eq. (
S62
) with
T=T^{(B)}
,
M=\mathcal{M}^{(B)}
, and
n=N-2
, we obtain

\displaystyle S^{(B)}_{1}=d_{+}^{(B)}\Lambda_{+}^{N-2}+d_{-}^{(B)}\Lambda_{-}^{N-2},\qquad d_{\pm}^{(B)}:=a_{\pm}(\mathcal{M}^{(B)}).

Explicitly,

\displaystyle\Tr(\mathcal{M}^{(B)})

\displaystyle=8e^{-H}\frac{\sinh^{2}\!\big(\frac{H-2K}{2}\big)}{(B-2J)^{2}}+8e^{+H}\frac{\sinh^{2}\!\big(\frac{H+2K}{2}\big)}{(B+2J)^{2}},

\displaystyle\Tr\!\big(T^{(B)}\mathcal{M}^{(B)}\big)

\displaystyle=8e^{K-2H}\frac{\sinh^{2}\!\big(\frac{H-2K}{2}\big)}{(B-2J)^{2}}+8e^{K+2H}\frac{\sinh^{2}\!\big(\frac{H+2K}{2}\big)}{(B+2J)^{2}}+16e^{-K}\frac{\sinh^{2}\!\big(\frac{H}{2}\big)}{B^{2}},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{+}-\Lambda_{-}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{e^{2K}\sinh^{2}H+e^{-2K}}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\boxed{\chi^{\rm MFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{NJ^{2}}{2}\,\frac{d_{+}^{(B)}\Lambda_{+}^{N-2}+d_{-}^{(B)}\Lambda_{-}^{N-2}}{\Lambda_{+}^{N}+\Lambda_{-}^{N}}.}

(S73)

In the thermodynamic limit at fixed
\beta
(so that
(\Lambda_{-}/\Lambda_{+})^{N}\to 0
), Eq. (
S73
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm MFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\frac{NJ^{2}}{2}\,\frac{d_{+}^{(B)}}{\Lambda_{+}^{2}},

where
d_{+}^{(B)}=a_{+}(\mathcal{M}^{(B)})
and
\Lambda_{\pm}
are given by Eq. (
S61
).

S5.3
Threshold driving rate
\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th}}
in MFIC

Using the definition of the threshold driving rate [Eq. (
10
)]
together with Eq. (
S66
) for
\delta V_{\mathrm{MFIC}}
and
Eq. (
S73
) for
\chi^{\rm MFIC}_{\mathrm{F}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th,MFIC}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\alpha}{\sqrt{N}}\,\frac{Z_{0}(2\beta)}{J\,S^{(B)}_{1}}\,\left(1-\frac{2Q^{(B)}_{N-1}(2\beta)}{Z_{0}(2\beta)}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\alpha}{\sqrt{N}}\,\frac{\Lambda_{+}^{N}+\Lambda_{-}^{N}}{J\big(d_{+}^{(B)}\Lambda_{+}^{N-2}+d_{-}^{(B)}\Lambda_{-}^{N-2}\big)}\,\left(1-\frac{2\big(c_{+}^{(B)}\Lambda_{+}^{N-2}+c_{-}^{(B)}\Lambda_{-}^{N-2}\big)}{\Lambda_{+}^{N}+\Lambda_{-}^{N}}\right)^{1/2}.

Taking
\beta\to\infty
, the dominant single-spin-flip gap is
2J+|B|
, and

S^{(B)}_{1}/Z_{0}(2\beta)\to(2J+|B|)^{-2}
while
Q^{(B)}_{N-1}(2\beta)/Z_{0}(2\beta)\to 0
.
Hence the zero-temperature threshold rate is

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\,}_{N}:=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th,MFIC}}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\alpha}{\sqrt{N}}\,\frac{(2J+|B|)^{2}}{J}.

We normalize the temperature-dependent threshold rate by

\displaystyle f^{\,}_{N}(\beta):=\frac{\Gamma^{\,}_{\mathrm{th,MFIC}}}{\Gamma^{\,}_{N}}=\frac{\Lambda_{+}^{N}+\Lambda_{-}^{N}}{(2J+|B|)^{2}\big(d_{+}^{(B)}\Lambda_{+}^{N-2}+d_{-}^{(B)}\Lambda_{-}^{N-2}\big)}\,\left(1-\frac{2\big(c_{+}^{(B)}\Lambda_{+}^{N-2}+c_{-}^{(B)}\Lambda_{-}^{N-2}\big)}{\Lambda_{+}^{N}+\Lambda_{-}^{N}}\right)^{1/2}.

By construction,
f^{\,}_{N}(\beta)\to 1
as
\beta\to\infty
.

In the thermodynamic limit at fixed
\beta
,

\displaystyle f(\beta):=\lim_{N\to\infty}f^{\,}_{N}(\beta)=\frac{\Lambda_{+}^{2}}{(2J+|B|)^{2}\,d_{+}^{(B)}}\,\left(1-\frac{2c_{+}^{(B)}}{\Lambda_{+}^{2}}\right)^{1/2},

(S74)

where
c_{+}^{(B)}
is the thermodynamic-limit coefficient appearing in Eq. (
S67
).
We observe that
f(\beta)
[Eq. (
S74
)] is not guaranteed to be monotonic in
\beta
.
Expanding
f(\beta)
in the low- and high-temperature regimes, one finds

\displaystyle f(\beta)\simeq\begin{cases}1+e^{-2\beta(2J+|B|)},&\beta\to\infty,\\[4.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2+(B/J)^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\,\bigl(2+|B|/J\bigr)^{2}}\;\frac{1}{\beta J},&\beta\to 0,\end{cases}

which matches the asymptotic temperature dependence stated in
Theorem
1
, with

c^{\,}_{1}=1,\qquad\Delta=2(2J+|B|),\qquad c^{\,}_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{2+(B/J)^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\,\bigl(2+|B|/J\bigr)^{2}}\;\frac{1}{J}.
