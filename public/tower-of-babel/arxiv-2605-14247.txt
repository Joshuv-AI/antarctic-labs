Monomial bases and canonical bases for quantum affine algebras
Toshiaki Shoji and Zhiping Zhou

Abstract
. We construct a monomial basis of a quantum affine algebra of simply-laced type, associated to the PBW basis of Beck-Nakajima. We show that there exists a simple algorithm of computing the canonical basis in terms of the monomial basis. We discuss the relations of the canonical basis obtained from this PBW basis with Lusztig’s canonical basis constructed by using the geometry of quivers.



Introduction

Let
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be the negative half of a quantum affine algebra
\mathbf{U}_{q}

associated to the Cartan datum
X=(I,(\ ,\ ))
.
In
[
BN
]
, Beck and Nakajima constructed a PBW basis
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
associated to
a doubly infinite sequence
\mathbf{h}=(\dots,i_{-1},i_{0},i_{1},\dots)
of vertices in
I
,
by using braid group actions
T_{i}
.
They defined the
canonical basis
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by making use of
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the affine Lie algebra associated to
X
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
the corresponding
Lie algebra of finite type.

\mathfrak{g}
has a realization as a central extension of the
loop algebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, as
\mathfrak{g}=L\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}c\oplus\mathbb{C}d
.
The sequence
\mathbf{h}
is defined by using the loop algebra structure of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
the basis
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
has a good connection with Kashiwara’s theory of
extremal weight modules (
[
Ka2
]
).

More generally, consider the quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
associated to the Cartan datum
X

of Kac-Moody type, and let
\vec{Q}
be a quiver related to
X
.
In
[
L3
]
,
[
L5
]
, Lusztig gave a categorification of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

in terms of the geometry of
\vec{Q}
.
For simplicity, assume that
X
is simply-laced. For each representation space
\mathbf{V}
of
\vec{Q}
,
he constructed a certain category
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V}}
consisting of semisimple complexes on
\mathbf{V}

by using the theory of perverse sheaves on
\mathbf{V}
,
and showed that the direct sum of the Grothendieck group
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{V}}
of this category,
for all non-isomorphic
\mathbf{V}
, has a structure of an algebra
which is isomorphic to (the integral form of)

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
. Then he defined the canonical basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
as
the set of simple perverse sheaves in the category
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V}}
for all
\mathbf{V}
, under this
isomorphism.
We denote this set of simple perverse sheaves in
\bigsqcup_{\mathbf{V}}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V}}
as
\mathcal{B}
.

By the general theory of perverse sheaves, a simple perverse sheaf can be
expressed as an intersection cohomology complex.
It is an interesting problem to describe a simple perverse sheaf contained in

\mathcal{B}
as an explicit form of intersection cohomology complex. In the case where

X
is simply-laced of affine type, this was first achieved
by Lusztig
[
L4
]
for the McKay quiver (a special type of the orientation),
and was generalized by Li-Lin
[
LL
]
to the affine quiver of any orientation.
These results heavily depend on the representation theory of quivers.

Monomial bases of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
was originally constructed by Lusztig
[
L2
]

in the case where
X
is simply-laced of finite type, by using the representation
theory of quivers. But in his paper, the terminology “monomial bases” does not
appear, since the monomial basis is just used as a step for constructing the canonical
basis.
In
[
SZ3
]
, Lusztig’s monomial basis was generalized
to the case of non-symmetric finite type, by applying the folding theory of quantum groups
(
[
SZ1
]
).
In particular, it is shown in
[
SZ3
]
that there is a simple algorithm of
computing the transition matrix between canonical basis and PBW basis, by making use
of the monomial basis.

We return to the case where
X
is of affine type, and assume that
X
is simply-laced.
It is known that
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is realized as a subalgebra of the Ringel-Hall algebra

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{q}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
associated to the quiver
\vec{Q}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite
field of
q
-elements (here we consider
q
as a generic parameter).
Under this situation,
the braid group action on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
corresponds to the action of reflection functors on
the representations of quivers. In
[
XXZ
]
, Xiao, Xu and Zhao constructed
the PBW basis
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}^{\prime}}
in
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{q}
associated to the doubly infinite sequence
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
, and
then defined a monomial basis. (Their result was generalized in
[
XX
]
to the
non simply-laced case.) By making use of this monomial basis, they constructed
a certain basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
(they call it a bar-invariant basis), and showed that this basis
coincides with Lusztig’s canonical basis
\mathcal{B}
.
Here the doubly infinite sequence
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
is defined by using an affine Coxeter element

C=s_{i_{0}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
of the Weyl group
W
with
I=\{i_{0},\dots,i_{n}\}
,
and the sequence
i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{n}
is extended to
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
in a periodic way.
We require that the order in
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
is chosen so that
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
is adapted (see 5.3 for the definition).
This is a crucial condition for applying the representation theory of quivers.
From the construction, those PBW basis
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
and
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}^{\prime}}
have similar theoretical
structures. But
\mathbf{h}
and
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
have no relations. Note that
\mathbf{h}
is not adapted
(see the example in 1.9),
and the representation theory of quivers cannot be applied directly to
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
.

In this paper, we concentrate on the PBW basis
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
and the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
.
Assume that
X
is simply-laced affine type.
We construct a monomial basis associated to
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
.
The construction of monomial basis by
[
L2
]
(finite case) and by
[
XXZ
]
(affine case)
depends on the representation theory of quivers. Instead, in our construction, we apply
Lusztig’s geometric construction of canonical basis
\mathcal{B}
.
Once the monomial basis is constructed, the algorithm of computing the canonical basis
in terms of monomial basis can be extended to the affine case.
It is interesting that this algorithm is quite
similar to the algorithm of computing generalized Green functions in the theory of
character sheaves (
[
L1
]
).

Finally, we discuss the relationship between the basis
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
and the basis
\mathcal{B}
.
By using the parametrization of
\mathcal{B}
based on the representation theory of quivers
due to
[
LL
]
,
we give a description of
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
in terms of the intersection cohomology complexes.

1.
Beck-Nakajima’s canonical bases

1.1.

In this section, we review the results of Beck-Nakajima
[
BN
]
on the
costruction of the PBW basis and the canonical basis of quantum affine algebras.
Let
X=(I,(\ ,\ ))
be a Cartan datum, where
(\ ,\ )
is a symmetric bilinear form
on a finite dimensional vector space
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{i}
with the basis

\{\alpha_{i}\mid\in I\}
such that
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying the property

•

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for any
i\in I
,

•

\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for any
i\neq j\in I
.

The Cartan datum
X
is said to be simply-laced if
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})\in\{0,-1\}
for any
i\neq j
,
and
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})=2
for any
i\in I
.
Set
a_{ij}=2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})/(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})
for any
i,j\in I
. The Cartan matrix is
defined by
A=(a_{ij})
. If
X
is simply-laced, then
A
is symmetric.
In the affine case,
X
is called untwisted (resp. twisted) if
X
is simply-laced
(resp. not simply-laced).
Let
Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the root lattice, and set

Q_{+}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{i}
,
Q_{-}=-Q_{+}
.

In the rest of this paper, we assume that
X
is affine, untwisted type.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the affine Kac-Moody algebra corresponding to the vertex set

I=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
of finite type
corresponding to
I_{0}=I-\{0\}
.
Let
\varDelta
be the affine root system for
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\varDelta_{0}
the root system for
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.
Let
\varDelta^{+}
(resp.
\varDelta_{0}^{+}
) be the set of positive roots in
\varDelta
(resp. in
\varDelta_{0}^{+}
).
We also denote by
\Pi=\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}
the set of simple roots in
\varDelta^{+}
,
and
\Pi_{0}=\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I_{0}\}
the set of simple roots in
\varDelta_{0}^{+}
.
Let
\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+}
(resp.
\varDelta^{\operatorname{im},+}
) the set of positive real roots
(resp. positive imaginary roots).
Then we have
\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+}=\varDelta_{>}^{\operatorname{re},+}\sqcup\varDelta_{<}^{\operatorname{re},+}
, and

\varDelta^{\operatorname{im},+}=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\delta
, where
\delta
is the minimal imaginary root.
The real positive roots
\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+}_{>}
and
\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+}_{<}
are given by

(1.1.1)

\displaystyle\varDelta_{>}^{\operatorname{re},+}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha+m\delta\mid\alpha\in\varDelta_{0}^{+},m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\},

\displaystyle\varDelta_{<}^{\operatorname{re},+}

\displaystyle=\{-\alpha+m\delta\mid\alpha\in\varDelta_{0}^{+},m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}.

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by simple reflections
\{s_{i}\mid i\in I\}
,
and
W_{0}
the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
generated by
\{s_{i}\mid i\in I_{0}\}
.

1.2.

Let
q
be an indeterminate. For an integer
n
, a positive integer
m
, set

[n]=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad[m]^{!}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}[i],\qquad[0]^{!}=1.

Let
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be the negative half of the quantum enveloping algebra
\mathbf{U}_{q}=\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

associated to
\mathfrak{g}
. Hence
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is the associative algebra over
\mathbb{Q}(q)

with generators
f_{i}(i\in I)
subject to the fundamental relations

(1.2.1)

\sum_{k=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{k}f_{i}^{(k)}f_{j}f_{i}^{(1-a_{ij}-k)}=0

for any
i\neq j\in I
, where
f_{i}^{(n)}=f_{i}^{n}/[n]^{!}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\mathbf{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
be the Laurent polynomial ring over
\mathbb{Z}
, and let

{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be Lusztig’s integral form of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, namely,
the
\mathbf{A}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
generated by
f_{i}^{(n)}
for
i\in I

and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

The bar involution is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra automorphism
-
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

defined by
\overline{q}=q^{-1},\overline{f_{i}}=f_{i}
for
i\in I
.
Also we define an anti-involution
*
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
as an anti-algebra automorphism
over
\mathbb{Q}(q)
by
f_{i}^{*}=f_{i}
for any
i\in I
.

1.3.

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
has a weight space decomposition
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}=\bigoplus_{\nu\in Q_{-}}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\nu}
,
where
(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\nu}
is a subspace of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
spanned by
f_{i_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}

such that
\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{k}}=-\nu
.

x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is said to be homogeneous with weight
\nu
if
x\in(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\nu}
.
We define a multiplication on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})(x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x^{\prime}_{2})=q^{-(\operatorname{wt}x_{2},\operatorname{wt}x_{1}^{\prime})}x_{1}x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime},

where
x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime}
are homogeneous elements in
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
Then
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
turns out to be an associative algebra with respect to
this twisted product. One can define a homomorphism
r:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

by
r(f_{i})=f_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes f_{i}
for each
i\in I
.
There exists a unique bilinear form
(\ ,\ )
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
satisfying the following properties;

(1,1)=1
and

\displaystyle(f_{i},f_{j})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}(1-q^{2})^{-1},

\displaystyle(x,y^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=(r(x),y^{\prime}\otimes y^{\prime\prime}),

\displaystyle(x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},y)

\displaystyle=(x^{\prime}\otimes x^{\prime\prime},r(y)),

where the bilinear form on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is defined by

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}^{\prime})=(x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime})(x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime})
.
Thus defined bilinear form is symmetric, and non-degenerate. The bilinear
form
(\ ,\ )
is called the inner product of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

The inner product satisfies the property

(1.3.1)

((\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\nu},(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\nu^{\prime}})=0\quad\text{ if }\quad\nu\neq\nu^{\prime}.

A basis
B
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is called
an almost orthonormal basis if it satisfies the property, for
x,y\in B
,

(1.3.2)

(x,y)\in\begin{cases}1+(q\mathbb{Z}[[q]]\cap\mathbb{Q}(q))&\quad\text{ if }x=y,\\
q\mathbb{Z}[[q]]\cap\mathbb{Q}(q)&\quad\text{ if }x\neq y.\end{cases}

1.4.

A doubly infinite sequence

(\dots i_{-1},i_{0},i_{1},\dots)
is a sequence in
I

satisfying the property that
w=s_{i_{p}}s_{i_{p+1}}\cdots s_{i_{q}}

is a reduced expression of
w\in W
for any
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
p<q
.

In
[
BN
]
, Beck-Nakajima constructed a PBW basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

associated to a special choice of a doubly infinite sequence
\mathbf{h}
.
The sequence
\mathbf{h}
is given as follows.
Let
Q_{0}
(resp.
P_{0}
) be the root lattice (resp. the weight lattice)
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. We have
W\simeq W_{0}\ltimes Q_{0}
, and we define an extended Weyl group

\widetilde{W}
by
\widetilde{W}=W_{0}\ltimes P_{0}
. Then
W
is a normal subgroup of
\widetilde{W}
, and

\widetilde{W}/W\simeq\mathcal{T}
, where
\mathcal{T}
is a subgroup of the group of diagram automorphisms
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
.
\mathcal{T}
is a finite group, and

\widetilde{W}\simeq\mathcal{T}\ltimes W
. For
\omega\in P_{0}
, we denote by
t_{\omega}

the corresponding element in
\widetilde{W}
.

Let
\omega_{i}
(
i\in I_{0}
) be the fundamental weights in
P_{0}
.
Then
\sum_{i\in I_{0}}\omega_{i}=\rho
, where
\rho
is a half sum of all the positive roots in
\varDelta^{+}_{0}
.
Now
t_{\rho}
is written as
t_{\rho}=w\tau
, where
w\in W,\tau\in\mathcal{T}
.
We fix a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{N}}\in W
.
Let
(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})
be the corresponding sequence in
I
, and extend it to
the infinite sequence

(1.4.1)

\mathbf{h}=(\dots,i_{-1},i_{0},i_{1},\dots)

by the condition
i_{k+N}=\tau(i_{k})
. This is the sequence constructed
in
[
BN
, 3.1]
, and satisfies the condition on reduced expressions.

We define
\beta_{k}\in\varDelta^{+}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
by

(1.4.2)

\beta_{k}=\begin{cases}s_{i_{0}}s_{i_{-1}}\cdots s_{i_{k+1}}(\alpha_{i_{k}})&\quad\text{ if }k\leq 0,\\
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k-1}}(\alpha_{i_{k}})&\quad\text{ if }k>0.\end{cases}

Then as remarked in
[
BN
, 3.1]
,
\beta_{k}
are all distinct, and

(1.4.3)

\varDelta_{>}^{\operatorname{re},+}=\{\beta_{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}\},\qquad\varDelta_{<}^{\operatorname{re},+}=\{\beta_{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}.

For any
i\in I
, let
T_{i}:\mathbf{U}_{q}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}
be the braid group action.
For
k\in\mathbb{Z},c\in\mathbb{N}
, define a root vector
F_{\beta_{k}}^{(c)}\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by

(1.4.4)

F_{\beta_{k}}^{(c)}=\begin{cases}T_{i_{0}}T_{i_{-1}}\cdots T_{i_{k+1}}(f_{i_{k}}^{(c)}),&\quad\text{ if }k\leq 0,\\
T_{i_{1}}^{-1}T_{i_{2}}^{-1}\cdots T_{i_{k-1}}^{-1}(f_{i_{k}}^{(c)})&\quad\text{ if }k>0.\end{cases}

Let
\mathbf{c}_{+}=(c_{0},c_{-1},\dots)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
,

\mathbf{c}_{-}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be functions which are almost
everywhere 0.
We define
L(\mathbf{c}_{+}),L(\mathbf{c}_{-})
by

(1.4.5)

\displaystyle L(\mathbf{c}_{+})

\displaystyle=F_{\beta_{0}}^{(c_{0})}F_{\beta_{-1}}^{(c_{-1})}F_{\beta_{-2}}^{(c_{-2})}\cdots,

\displaystyle L(\mathbf{c}_{-})

\displaystyle=\cdots F_{\beta_{3}}^{(c_{3})}F_{\beta_{2}}^{(c_{2})}F_{\beta_{1}}^{(c_{1})}.

1.5.

Next we define root vectors for imaginary roots.
For
i\in I_{0},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, set

(1.5.1)

\widetilde{\psi}_{i,k}=F_{k\delta-\alpha_{i}}f_{i}-q^{2}f_{i}F_{k\delta-\alpha_{i}}.

Note that since
k\delta-\alpha_{i}\in\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+}_{<}
, the root vector
F_{k\delta-\alpha_{i}}

is defined.
\widetilde{\psi}_{i,k}
is a homogeneous element of weight
-k\delta
.

It is known that
\widetilde{\psi}_{i,k}
(
i\in I_{0},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
) are mutually
commuting.

For each
i\in I_{0},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we define
\widetilde{P}_{i,k}\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

by the following recursive identity.

(1.5.2)

\widetilde{P}_{i,k}=\frac{1}{[k]}\sum_{s=1}^{k}q^{s-k}\widetilde{\psi}_{i,s}\widetilde{P}_{i,k-s}.

\widetilde{P}_{i,k}
is a homogeneous element of weight
-k\delta
.

In the discussion below, we borrow some notions from the theory of symmetric functions.
See Macdonald’s book
[
M
]
for details.

A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
of an integer
m
is
a sequence of integers
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 0
such that

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=m
.
m=|\lambda|
is called the size of
\lambda
.
We denote by
\mathscr{P}_{m}
the set of partitions of size
m
, and set

\mathscr{P}=\bigsqcup_{m\geq 1}\mathscr{P}_{m}
, the set of partitions of any size.

For a fixed
i\in I_{0}
, we regard
\widetilde{P}_{i,k}
as a complete symmetric function

h_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
of
m
,
we define a Schur function
S_{i,\lambda}
by making use of the determinant
formula

(1.5.3)

S_{i,\lambda}=\det(\widetilde{P}_{i,\lambda_{j}-j+k})_{1\leq j,k\leq r}.

Thus
S_{i,\lambda}
is a homogeneous element of weight
-m\delta
.
Note that in
[
BN
]
, Schur functions are defined by regarding
\widetilde{P}_{i,k}

as elementary symmetric functions
e_{k}
. In that case, their Schur function
coincides with our
S_{i,\lambda^{\prime}}
, where
\lambda^{\prime}
is the dual partition of
\lambda
.

For each
i\in I_{0}
, we choose a partition
\lambda^{(i)}
, and let

\mathbf{c}_{0}=(\lambda^{(i)})_{i\in I_{0}}
be an
I_{0}
-tuple of partitions. We define

S_{\mathbf{c}_{0}}
by

(1.5.4)

S_{\mathbf{c}_{0}}=\prod_{i\in I_{0}}S_{i,\lambda^{(i)}}.

We denote by
\mathscr{C}
the set of triples
(\mathbf{c}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{0},\mathbf{c}_{-})
, where

\mathbf{c}_{+}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}},\mathbf{c}_{-}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
are functions almost
everywhere 0, and
\mathbf{c}_{0}
is an
I_{0}
-tuple of partitions.
For each
\mathbf{c}\in\mathscr{C}
, we define
L(\mathbf{c})
by

(1.5.5)

L(\mathbf{c})=L(\mathbf{c}_{+})\cdot S_{\mathbf{c}_{0}}\cdot L(\mathbf{c}_{-}).

1.6.

We define a partial order
\prec_{0}
on the set
\mathscr{C}
by
letting
\mathbf{c}\prec_{0}\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
if and only if

(1.6.1)

\mathbf{c}_{+}\leq\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{+}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{c}_{-}\leq\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{-}\quad\text{ and one of these is strict,}

where both
\leq
are the lexicographic order from left to right for

\mathbf{c}_{+}=(c_{0},c_{-1},\dots)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
and for

\mathbf{c}_{-}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
For example,
\mathbf{c}_{+}<\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{+}
if there exists
k<0
such that

c_{0}=c^{\prime}_{0},\dots,c_{k+1}=c^{\prime}_{k+1}
and that
c_{k}<c^{\prime}_{k}
.

Note that the
\mathbf{c}_{0}
part gives no contribution on this order
\prec_{0}
.
For example,
(0,\mathbf{c}_{0},0)\prec_{0}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{+},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{0},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{-})
means that
one of
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{+},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{-}
is non-zero.

The following result was proved in
[
BN
, Thm. 3.13]
.

Theorem 1.7
.

For a fixed

2.1.

We follow the notation in Section 1.
Recall that
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{L(\mathbf{c})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathscr{C}\}
is the PBW-basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
,
and
\prec_{0}
is the partial order of
\mathscr{C}
defined in 1.6.
In order to define a monomial basis, we need to consider a refinement of the
partial order
\prec_{0}
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be the set of partitions. We define a partial order (called the dominance order)
on
\mathscr{P}
as follows. For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots),\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots)\in\mathscr{P}
,

\lambda\leq\mu
if and only if
|\lambda|=|\mu|
and

(2.1.1)

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{i}\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\geq 1.

In Section 1, the partial order
\prec_{0}
on
\mathscr{C}
is defined only
by the condition for
\mathbf{c}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{-}
in
\mathbf{c}=(\mathbf{c}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{0},\mathbf{c}_{-})\in\mathscr{C}
.
Here we define a partial order on the set
\mathscr{C}_{0}
of
I_{0}
-tuple of partitions

\mathbf{c}_{0}=(\lambda^{(i)})_{i\in I_{0}}
.
For
\mathbf{c}_{0}=(\lambda^{(i)})_{i\in I_{0}}
, and
\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime}=(\mu^{(i)})_{i\in I_{0}}
,
we say that
\mathbf{c}_{0}<\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime}
if
\lambda^{(i)}\leq\mu^{(i)}
for each
i\in I_{0}
,
and the strict inequality holds for at least one
i
.
Then we define a partial order on
\mathscr{C}
by the condition, for

\mathbf{c}=(\mathbf{c}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{0},\mathbf{c}_{-}),\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}_{-}^{\prime})\in\mathscr{C}
,
that
\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
if and only if

(2.1.2)

\mathbf{c}_{+}\leq\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{+},\quad\mathbf{c}_{0}\leq\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime},\quad\mathbf{c}_{-}\leq\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{-},\quad\text{ and one of them is strict. }

generators,

for any sequence

3.1.

Here we review some general results obtained by Lusztig (
[
L5
]

Let
W
be a Weyl group. For
w\in W
, let
w=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{k}}

be a reduced expression. We consider a subspace of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
spanned by

(3.1.1)

f_{i_{1}}^{(c_{1})}T_{i_{1}}(f_{i_{2}}^{(c_{2})})\cdots(T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\cdots T_{i_{k-1}})(f_{i_{k}}^{(c_{k})}).

This element is a product of root vectors
F_{\beta}
such that

of

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(w)

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(w)
.
It is known that
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(w)
is closed under the multiplication, hence

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(w)

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

We consider an infinite sequence
i_{0},i_{-1},i_{-2},\dots

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(+)

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(w)

Similarly, for an infinite sequence
i_{1},i_{2},\dots

(3.1.2)

\cdots T^{-1}_{i_{1}}T^{-1}_{i_{2}}(f_{i_{3}}^{(c_{3})})T^{-1}_{i_{1}}(f_{i_{2}}^{(c_{2})})f^{(c_{1})}_{i_{1}}.

We denote by
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(-)
the subspace spanned by those elements. This also gives
a subalgebra of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

We define

\mathbf{U}^{-}_{q}(0)=\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\cap\biggl(T_{i_{0}}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})\cap T_{i_{0}}T_{i_{-1}}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})\cap\cdots\biggr)\cap\biggl(T_{i_{1}}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})\cap T_{i_{1}}^{-1}T_{i_{2}}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})\cap\cdots\biggr).

For
\mathbf{c}_{+}=(c_{0},c_{-1},\dots)\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}}
,
an element

is defined by using (3.1.1).
Similarly, for
\mathbf{c}_{-}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
,

is defined by using (3.1.2).

[
L5

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

Define

\displaystyle(L(\mathbf{c}_{+})xL(\mathbf{c}_{-}),L(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{+})x^{\prime}L(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{-}))

For any sequence

\mathbb{N}

For a given

5.
Canonical bases and representations of quivers

5.1.

Let
\vec{Q}=(I,H)
be the quiver defined as in 4.1.
In this section, we discuss the relations of the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}

with Lusztig’s basis
\mathcal{B}
, via the representation theory of quivers.
Recall that
I=\{i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\}
is the total order on
I
defined in
4.11, and the orientation
H
of the quiver satisfies the condition as in (4.11.1), namely,

(5.1.1)
i_{k^{\prime}}\to i_{k}
implies that
k^{\prime}>k
.

Note that in this case,
\vec{Q}
has no oriented cycles.
The vertex
i\in I
is called a sink (resp. a source) if there does not exist

i\to j
(resp.
j\to i
) in
H
.
For any
i\in I
, let
\sigma_{i}\vec{Q}=(I,H^{\prime})
, where
H^{\prime}
is defined as follows.
If
j
is joined to
i
in
H
, then reverse the orientation to (or from)
i
in
H^{\prime}
.
For
j\to j^{\prime}
with
j\neq i,j^{\prime}\neq i
, the orientation is left stable.
Then one can show

Lemma 5.2
.

For
r=0,\dots,n
,
i_{r}
is a sink of
\sigma_{i_{r-1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{0}}\vec{Q}
.
Moreover,

(5.2.1)

\sigma_{i_{n}}\sigma_{i_{n-1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{0}}\vec{Q}=\vec{Q}.

Proof.

First note that
i_{0}
is a sink of
\vec{Q}
. In fact, if
i_{k}
is joined to
i_{0}

with
k>0
, thus
i_{k}\to i_{0}
by (5.1.1). Hence
i_{0}
is a sink of
\vec{Q}
.
Let
\sigma_{i_{r-1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{0}}\vec{Q}=(I,H_{r})
. By induction on
r
,
it is easy to see that the set of arrows
H_{r}
is given by

(5.2.2)

\begin{cases}i_{k}\to i_{r}\quad\text{ if }k<r,\\
i_{r}\leftarrow i_{k}\quad\text{ if }r<k,\\
i_{k^{\prime}}\to i_{k}\quad\text{ if $k^{\prime}<r<k$, }\\
i_{k^{\prime}}\leftarrow i_{k}\quad\text{ if $k^{\prime}<k<r$ or if $r<k^{\prime}<k$. }\end{cases}

Thus
i_{r}
is a sink of
(I,H_{r})
. (5.2.1) is also clear from (5.2.2).
∎

5.3.

We review the representation theory of quivers following
Li and Lin
[
LL
]
. Note that in
[
LL
, 2.2]
, the order of
I
is chosen
so that it satisfies the condition “
i_{r}
is a sink in
\sigma_{i_{r-1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{0}}\vec{Q}

for
r=0,\dots,n
”. By Lemma 5.2, our total order
I=\{i_{0},\dots,i_{n}\}

satisfies this condition, and so the results of
[
LL
]
can be applied freely to our quiver

\vec{Q}
.

More generally, we consider a doubly infinite sequence
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}=(\dots,i_{-1},i_{0},i_{1},\dots)

of
I
satisfying the property on reduced expressions as in 1.4.
The sequence is called adapted if for any
s\leq 1
,

i_{s-1}
is a sink in
\sigma_{i_{s}}\sigma_{i_{s+1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{0}}\vec{Q}

and if for any
t\geq 0
,
i_{t+1}
is a source in

\sigma_{i_{t}}\sigma_{i_{t-1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{1}}\vec{Q}
.
Such a doubly infinite sequence
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
always exists for a given acyclic quiver
(see, e.g.,
[
XXZ
, 5.2]
).
In
[
XXZ
]
, Xiao, Xu and Zhao constructed a monomial basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
based on
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
,
and by making use of it, defined a basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
(a bar-invariant basis, in their
terminology) in an algebraic way, and showed that it coincides with
Lusztig’s canonical bases
\gamma^{-1}(\mathcal{B})
.
However, our sequence
\mathbf{h}
defined in 1.4 does not give an
adapted sequence (see the example in 1.9).
So their theory cannot be applied directly to our situation.

5.4.

A representation of a quiver
\vec{Q}
over
\mathbf{k}
is a pair
(\mathbf{V},x)
where

x\in E_{\mathbf{V}}
. A morphism
f:(\mathbf{V},x)\to(\mathbf{W},y)
is a collection of linear maps

f_{i}:V_{i}\to W_{i}
for
i\in I
such that
f_{h^{\prime}}x_{h}=y_{h}f_{h^{\prime\prime}}
.
The representations of quiver define an abelian category, which is denoted by

\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})
.

If
i
is a sink in
\vec{Q}
, a reflection functor

\Phi^{+}_{i}:\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})\to\operatorname{Rep}(\sigma_{i}\vec{Q})

can be defined as in
[
LL
, 2.2]
.
By Lemma 5.2, one can define the Coxeter functor
\tau:\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})\to\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})
by

(5.4.1)

\tau=\Phi^{+}_{i_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi^{+}_{i_{0}}.

Similarly, if
i
is a source in
\vec{Q}
, a reflection functor

\Phi^{-}_{i}:\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})\to\operatorname{Rep}(\sigma_{i}\vec{Q})

can be defined.
Then as in Lemma 5.2, one can show that

i_{n}
is a source of
\vec{Q}
, and
i_{r}
is a source of

\sigma_{i_{r+1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{n}}(\vec{Q})
for
r=n,n-1,\dots,0
.
Hence the Coxeter functor
\tau^{-}:\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})\to\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})
is defined as

(5.4.2)

\tau^{-}=\Phi^{-}_{i_{0}}\circ\Phi^{-}_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi^{-}_{i_{n}}.

5.5.

An indecomposable representation
M
in
\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})
is called preprojective
if
\tau^{i}M=0
for
i\gg 0
, preinjective if
\tau^{-i}M=0
for
i\gg 0
, and
regular if
\tau^{i}M\neq 0
for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Note that
M
is projective if and only if
\tau M=0
, and that
M
is injective
if and only if
\tau^{-}M=0
.
More generally, a decomposable representation
N
is called
preprojective, regular or preinjective if all its indecomposable summands are so,
and we denote by
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{I}
the full subcategories of
\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q})
whose objects
are preprojective, regular, preinjective.

It is known that the categories
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{I}
are exact, and stable under extensions.
The category
\mathbb{R}
is abelian, and stable under extensions.
For
P\in\mathbb{P},I\in\mathbb{I},R\in\mathbb{R}

(5.5.1)

5.6.

The following result concerning the structure of the category of regular modules
is due to Ringel
[
R
]
(see also
[
S
, Sec. 2]
).
A regular representation is called simple if it is simple as an object of
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
R
be a regular representation. Then there exists
p\geq 1
such that
\tau^{p}R\simeq R
.
The smallest integer
p
is called the period of
R
.
A simple regular module is called homogeneous regular if it has the period 1,
and is called non-homogeneous if the period
p>1

If

is a regular simple module of period

\dim(R\oplus\tau R\oplus\cdots\oplus\tau^{p-1}R)=\delta.

In particular, all homogeneous regular simple module
R
has
\dim R=\delta
.

The set of regular simple modules are classified as follows.
There is a natural bijection
R_{z}\leftrightarrow z
between the set of homogeneous regular
simple modules and points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}-D
, where
D
is a finite set consisting of
d
points.
There are exactly
d

\tau
-orbits
\mathscr{O}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{O}_{d}
of non-homogeneous regular simple
modules of period
p_{1},\dots,p_{d}
.
Those integers
p_{1},\dots,p_{d}
are explicitly computed for each case

A_{n}^{(1)},D_{n}^{(1)}
and
E_{n}^{(1)}
for

(see
[
S
, Thm. 2.24]
).

Let
R
be a regular simple module. The tube associated to
R

is the set of indecomposable modules whose subquotients consist of regular simple
modules appearing in the
\tau
-orbit of
R
. We denote by
\mathbb{R}^{\prime}

the subcategory of
\mathbb{R}
whose indecomposable modules are tubes of homogeneous
regular simple modules. We also denote by
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{d}
, the subcategory of

\mathbb{R}
whose indecomposable modules are tubes of non-homogeneous regular
simple modules contained in the
\tau
-orbits
\mathscr{O}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{O}_{d}
.

If
R
is a simple regular module of period

generated by

is equivalent to the category

\operatorname{Rep}(\vec{Q}_{p-1})
,
where
\vec{Q}_{p-1}
is a cyclic quiver of rank

.
The set of nilpotent representations of

is parametrized by

A multi-partition

is called aperiodic if partitions

By the above category equivalence,

corresponds to a nilpotent representation
in
\vec{Q}_{p-1}

L_{\mu,\delta}\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{l}}\operatorname{IC}(U_{l\delta},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}})^{\oplus K_{\lambda,\mu}}\oplus\mathbb{T}.

5.17.

We consider similar objects obtained from the monomial basis.
Recall the construction of monomial basis corresponding to imaginary roots.
For
i\in I_{0}
and
c\in\mathbb{N}
,
set
\beta=\delta-\alpha_{i}\in\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+}_{<}
, and decompose
c\beta
as

c\beta=\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{j}\alpha_{i_{j}}
. Then by (2.9.4), the monomial
m(i,c)
is defined
by

m(i,c)=f_{i_{n}}^{d_{n}}\cdots f_{i_{0}}^{d_{0}}\cdot f_{i}^{(c)}.

For a partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{m})
of
l
, define a monomial

m(i,\mu)
by

m(i,\mu)=m(i,\mu_{1})m(i,\mu_{2})\cdots m(i,\mu_{m}).

Correspondingly, the semisimple complex
M_{i,c},M_{i,\mu}
on
E_{\mathbf{V}}

can be defined.
Let
b(i,\mu)
be the canonical basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

corresponding to the monomial basis
m(i,\mu)
.
We denote by
A_{i,\mu}
the simple perverse sheaf on
E_{\mathbf{V}}
corresponding to

b(i,\mu)
, which is a direct summand of
M_{i,\mu}
.

Lemma 5.18
.

Assume that
\dim\mathbf{V}=l\delta
.

(i)

If
i\neq i_{0}
,
M_{i,l}
has a proper support in
E_{\mathbf{V}}
.

(ii)

If
i=i_{0}
,
\operatorname{\!\langle\!}M_{i_{0},l}\operatorname{\!\rangle\!}
coincides with
\operatorname{\!\langle\!}L_{l\delta}\operatorname{\!\rangle\!}
in the Grothendieck group

\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{V}}
, up to scalar.

Proof.

Put
\beta=\delta-\alpha_{i}
, and write
l\beta=\sum_{k=0}^{n}d_{k}\alpha_{i_{k}}
.
Then
M_{i,l}
can be written as

(M_{d_{n}\alpha_{i_{n}}}\star\cdots\star M_{d_{0}\alpha_{i_{0}}})\star M_{l\alpha_{i}}.

If
i\neq i_{0}
, we can move
M_{l\alpha_{i}}
from right to left so that

\alpha_{i}
reaches the appropriate place in the order,
\alpha_{i_{n}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{0}}
. Then
as in the discussion in the proof of Proposition 4.17, we encounter the step where
the support becomes strictly smaller than before. Hence (i) holds.

We show (ii). From the construction,
L_{l\delta}
is the constant sheaf
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
on
E_{\mathbf{V}}
,
up to shift, and
L_{l\delta}
is a simple perverse sheaf on
E_{\mathbf{V}}
.
Now assume that
i=i_{0}
. Then in the Grothendieck group
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{V}}
,
M_{i_{0},l}

coincides with the following element, up to some scalar in
\mathbf{A}
,

L=M_{d_{n}\alpha_{i_{n}}}\star\cdots\star M_{d_{1}\alpha_{i_{1}}}\star M_{d^{\prime}_{0}\alpha_{i_{0}}}.

Now
L\simeq L_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
with
\boldsymbol{\nu}=(d_{n}\alpha_{i_{n}},\dots,d_{1}\alpha_{i_{1}},d_{0}^{\prime}\alpha_{i_{0}})
,
and by our assumption on the order,
i_{0},\dots,i_{n}
,
L_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
gives a constant
sheaf
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
, up to shift, and gives a simple perverse sheaf on
E_{\mathbf{V}}
.
It follows that both of
L_{l\delta}
and
M_{i_{0},l}
give a simple perverse sheaf
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[d]
.
Thus (ii) holds.
∎

Proposition 5.19
.

Assume that
\dim\mathbf{V}=l\delta
.
Under the notation of 5.16, 5.17, for each
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{l}
, we have

A_{i_{0},\mu}\simeq\operatorname{IC}(U_{l\delta},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\mu}}).

Proof.

By (2.11.5),
m(i,\mu)
is written as

m(i,\mu)\equiv S_{i,\mu}+\sum_{\lambda>\mu}K_{\lambda,\mu}S_{i,\lambda}\mod\mathcal{Z}_{0},

where
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
is a sum of
L(\mathbf{c})
such that
\mathbf{c}\succ_{0}0
and
that
\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c})=l\delta
. Since
S_{i,\mu}
is a PBW basis, we have

S_{i,\mu}\equiv b(i,\mu)\mod\mathcal{Z}_{0}
. By applying this to the case
i=i_{0}
,

m(i_{0},\mu)\equiv b(i_{0},\mu)+\sum_{\lambda>\mu}K_{\lambda,\mu}b(i_{0},\lambda)\mod\mathcal{Z}_{0}.

Passing to the semisimple complexes on
E_{\mathbf{V}}
, we have

(5.19.1)

M_{i_{0},\mu}=A_{i_{0},\mu}\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda>\mu}A_{i_{0},\lambda}^{\oplus K_{\lambda,\mu}}\oplus\mathbb{T},

where
\mathbb{T}
is a direct sum of simple perverse sheaves whose supports are proper subsets
of
E_{\mathbf{V}}
.
Note that if
L(\mathbf{c})
belongs to
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
, the simple perverse sheaf
A_{\mathbf{c}}
corresponding
to
b(\mathbf{c})
has the property whose support is a proper subset of
E_{\mathbf{V}}
. Hence
the condition modulo
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
is replaced by the complex
\mathbb{T}
.

On the other hand, (5.16.1) can be rewritten as

(5.19.2)

L_{\mu,\delta}=\operatorname{IC}(U_{l\delta},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\mu}})\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda>\mu}\operatorname{IC}(U_{l\delta},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}})^{\oplus K_{\lambda,\mu}}\oplus\mathbb{T}^{\prime}.

We now compare (5.19.1) and (5.19.2).
By Lemma 5.18 (ii),
\operatorname{\!\langle\!}M_{i_{0},l}\operatorname{\!\rangle\!}=\operatorname{\!\langle\!}L_{l\delta}\operatorname{\!\rangle\!}
, up to scalar, for any
l
.
Since
M_{i_{0},\mu}=M_{i_{0},\mu_{1}}\star\cdots\star M_{i_{0},\mu_{r}}
, and

L_{\mu,\delta}=L_{\mu_{1}\delta}\star\cdots\star L_{\mu_{r}\delta}
,
we have
\operatorname{\!\langle\!}M_{i_{0},\mu}\operatorname{\!\rangle\!}=\operatorname{\!\langle\!}L_{\mu,\delta}\operatorname{\!\rangle\!}
, up to scalar.
Then the proposition holds by the backward induction on the dominance order
\lambda>\mu
.
∎

5.20.

We compare the parametrization of the basis
\mathcal{B}
in terms of
Theorem 5.12, and in terms of
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
via the isomorphism
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\simeq\mathcal{K}
.
Take
\nu\in Q_{+}
. We fix a data
\mathscr{D}=(Y,l,l^{\prime},\lambda)
, where

Y
is a set of pairs
(\beta,c)
(
\beta\in\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+},c\in\mathbb{N}
) such that

\nu=\sum_{(\beta,c)\in Y}c\beta+(l+l^{\prime})\delta
, and
\lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{l}
.
For each data
(Y,l,l^{\prime},\lambda)
, one can construct
\mathbf{c}=(\mathbf{c}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{0},\mathbf{c}_{-})\in\mathscr{C}

as follows. Let
Y_{+}
(resp.
Y_{-}
) be the set of pairs
(\beta,c)

such that
\beta\in\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+}_{>}
(resp.
\beta\in\varDelta^{\operatorname{re},+}_{<}
). Then we have

Y=Y_{+}\sqcup Y_{-}
. Arranging
\beta
in
Y_{+}
or
Y_{-}
along the order for
\mathbf{h}
,
we obtain
\mathbf{c}_{+}=(c_{0},c_{-1},\dots),\mathbf{c}_{-}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
.
Let
\lambda=\lambda^{(i_{0})}
, and choose any
\lambda^{(i)}\in\mathscr{P}_{l_{i}}
for each
i\neq i_{0}

such that
\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}l_{i}=l^{\prime}
.
We set
\mathbf{c}_{0}=(\lambda^{(i)})_{i\in I_{0}}
.
In this way,
\mathbf{c}\in\mathscr{C}
such that
\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c})=\nu
is determined, and we obtain
the simple perverse sheaf
A_{\mathbf{c}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V}}
, where
\dim\mathbf{V}=\nu
.

Next let
Y_{\mathbb{P}}
(resp.
Y_{\mathbb{I}}
) be the set of
(\beta,c)
such that
\partial(\beta)<0

(resp.
\partial(\beta)>0
), and
define a preprojective module
P
(resp. preinjective module
I
) by

\displaystyle P=\bigoplus_{(\beta,c)\in Y_{\mathbb{P}}}(P_{\beta})^{\oplus c},\qquad I=\bigoplus_{(\beta,c)\in Y_{\mathbb{I}}}(I_{\beta})^{\oplus c},

where
P_{\beta}
(resp.
I_{\beta}
) is an indecomposable preprojective
(resp. an indecomposable preinjective) module determined by
\beta
.
Furthermore, Let
Y_{\mathbb{R}}
be the set of
(\beta,c)
such that
\partial(\beta)=0
,
and we define a non-homogeneous regular module
N
by

N=\bigoplus_{(\beta,c)\in Y_{\mathbb{R}}}(N_{\beta})^{\oplus c},

where
N_{\beta}
is an indecomposable non-homogeneous regular module corresponding to
\beta
.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}}
be the irreducible local system on
U_{l\delta}
.
Let
N^{\prime}
be any non-homogeneous regular module such that
\dim N^{\prime}=l^{\prime}\delta
.
Then
N\oplus N^{\prime}
is decomposed as
N\oplus N^{\prime}=N_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus N_{d}

such that
N_{i}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{i}
. Thus we can define a stratum data

\mathcal{A}=(P,l,N_{1},\dots,N_{d},I)
. Here we choose
N
and
N^{\prime}
so that
\mathcal{A}
is
aperiodic. We obtain the simple perverse sheaf

\operatorname{IC}(S_{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}})\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V}}
.
Now the above
A_{\mathbf{c}}
and
\operatorname{IC}(S_{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}})
give the parametrization of

\mathcal{B}_{\nu}
,
which corresponds to the set of canonical basis with weight
-\nu
.

The following result gives a geometric description of the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}

in terms of the set
\mathcal{B}
of simple perverse sheaves.

Theorem 5.21
.

Let
\mathbf{c}=(\mathbf{c}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{0},\mathbf{c}_{-})\in\mathscr{C}
with
\mathbf{c}_{0}=(\lambda^{(i)})_{i\in I_{0}}
.
Assume that
\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c})=\nu
.
Set
\lambda=\lambda^{(i_{0})}\in\mathscr{P}_{l}
.

(i)

If
l\geq 1
, then
A_{\mathbf{c}}=\operatorname{IC}(S_{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}})

for a certain stratum data
S_{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}
.

(ii)

If
l=0
, then
A_{\mathbf{c}}=\operatorname{IC}(\mathcal{O},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is the
G_{\mathbf{V}}
-orbit
of some
x\in E_{\mathbf{V}}
, with
\dim E_{\mathbf{V}}=\nu
.

(iii)

In particular, if
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}_{+}
,
\mathbf{c}_{-}
, or
\mathbf{c}_{0}
with
\mathbf{c}_{0}=(\lambda^{(i)})_{i\neq i_{0}}
,
then
A_{\mathbf{c}}=\operatorname{IC}(\mathcal{O},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
.

Proof.

(iii) will follow from (i) and (ii).
We prove (i) and (ii) by induction on
|\nu|
for the weight
\nu=\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c})
.
Take
\mathbf{c}=(\mathbf{c}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{0},\mathbf{c}_{-})\in\mathscr{C}
with
\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c})=\nu
.
Let
\mathbf{V}
be the
I
-graded vector space such that
\dim E_{\mathbf{V}}=\nu
.
We assume that
\mathbf{c}
belongs to the data
\mathscr{D}=(Y,l,l^{\prime},\lambda)
.
We further assume, by backward induction on
l
,
that the theorem holds for
l_{1}>l
.

We show (i). Thus assume that
l\geq 1
.
Write
\mathbf{c}_{0}=\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime}\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime\prime}
, where
\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime}=\lambda^{(i_{0})}
and

\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime\prime}=(\lambda^{(i)})_{i\neq i_{0}}
.
Then
M_{\mathbf{c}}
can be written as

(5.21.1)

M_{\mathbf{c}}=M_{\mathbf{c}_{+}}\star M_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{0}}\star M_{\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime\prime}}\star M_{\mathbf{c}_{-}}.

By induction, and by (iii), we may assume that
A_{\mathbf{d}}
is of the form

\operatorname{IC}(\mathcal{O},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
for
\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{c}_{+},\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{c}_{-}
, where
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{+},\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{O}_{-}
are
the corresponding orbits.
Moreover, by Proposition 5.19,
A_{\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime}}=A_{i_{0},\lambda}=\operatorname{IC}(U_{l\delta},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}})
.
Let
\mathbf{V}_{+},\mathbf{V}_{l\delta},\mathbf{V}_{l^{\prime}\delta},\mathbf{V}_{-}
be the I-graded vector spaces such that

\dim\mathbf{V}_{+}=\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c}_{+}),\dim\mathbf{V}_{l\delta}=l\delta,\dim\mathbf{V}_{l^{\prime}\delta}=l^{\prime}\delta,\dim\mathbf{V}_{-}=\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c}_{-})
.
and let

E_{\mathbf{V}_{+}},E_{\mathbf{V}_{l\delta}},E_{\mathbf{V}_{l^{\prime}\delta}},E_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}
the corresponding representation
spaces.
Consider the diagram

\begin{CD}E_{\mathbf{V}_{+}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{l\delta}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{l^{\prime}\delta}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}@<{p_{1}}<{}<E^{\prime}@>{p_{2}}>{}>E^{\prime\prime}@>{p_{3}}>{}>E_{\mathbf{V}}\end{CD}

Let
Z=\operatorname{Im}p_{3}
. Then
Z
is a closed subset of
E_{\mathbf{V}}
.
Let
K=A_{\mathbf{c}_{+}}\boxtimes A_{\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime}}\boxtimes A_{\mathbf{c}_{0}^{\prime\prime}}\boxtimes A_{\mathbf{c}_{-}}
,
the semisimple complex on
E_{\mathbf{V}_{+}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{l\delta}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{l^{\prime}\delta}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}
.
Then we have
(p_{3})_{!}(p_{2})_{\flat}p_{1}^{*}K=A_{\mathbf{c}}\oplus\mathbb{T}
, where
\mathbb{T}
is a direct sum
of the (shift) of simple perverse sheaves whose support is a proper subset of

Z
. Now by Theorem 5.12,
A_{\mathbf{c}}
can be written as

A_{\mathbf{c}}=\operatorname{IC}(U,\mathcal{L})
, where
U
is an open dense subset of
Z
, and
\mathcal{L}

is a simple local system on
U
.
Then we have a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\mathcal{O}_{+}\times U_{l\delta}\times\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}\times\mathcal{O}_{-}@<{q_{1}}<{}<U^{\prime}@>{q_{2}}>{}>p_{2}(U^{\prime})@>{q_{3}}>{}>Z\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
E_{\mathbf{V}_{+}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{l\delta}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{l^{\prime}\delta}}\times E_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}@<{p_{1}}<{}<E^{\prime}@>{p_{2}}>{}>E^{\prime\prime}@>{p_{3}}>{}>E_{\mathbf{V}},\end{CD}

where
U^{\prime}=p_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{+}\times U_{l\delta}\times\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}\times\mathcal{O}_{-})
.
The vertical maps are natural inclusions, and

q_{2}
is a principal bundle.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{K}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}}\otimes\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}\otimes\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}

be the local system on
\mathcal{O}_{+}\times U_{l\delta}\times\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}\times\mathcal{O}_{-}

obtained from
K
by the restriction. Then
(q_{2})_{\flat}q_{1}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{K}

coincides with the restriction of
(p_{2})_{\flat}p_{1}^{*}K
on
p_{2}(U^{\prime})
.
Set
U^{\prime\prime}=p_{2}(U^{\prime})\cap p_{3}^{-1}(U)
.
Since
U
is open dense in
Z
,
U^{\prime\prime}
is open dense in
p_{2}(U^{\prime})
.
Since
(p_{3})_{!}(p_{2})_{\flat}p_{1}^{*}K
contains
A_{c}
as a direct summand,
we must have
\dim U^{\prime\prime}=\dim Z
. Thus for
x\in U\cap p_{3}(U^{\prime\prime})
, the fibre

p_{3}^{-1}(x)
has dimension 0.

In general, it may happen that
p_{3}^{-1}(x)
has more than two elements.
In that case, there is a possibility that a certain covering occurs
in the restriction of
p_{3}
on
U^{\prime\prime}
. Then the local system
\mathcal{L}
on
U
is
associated to a Galois group bigger than
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
. By Theorem 5.12, this
Galois group coincides with
\mathfrak{S}_{l_{1}}
with
l_{1}>l
. Thus, by induction,

\operatorname{IC}(U,\mathcal{L})
coincides with
A_{\mathbf{d}}
, where
\mathbf{d}=(\mathbf{d}_{+},\mathbf{d}_{0},\mathbf{d}_{-})

with
\mathbf{d}_{0}=(\mu^{(i)})_{i\in I_{0}}
such that
|\mu^{(i_{0})}|=l_{1}
.
It follows that
A_{\mathbf{d}}\neq A_{\mathbf{c}}
,
and this case does not occur.

Thus for
x\in U\cap p_{3}(U^{\prime\prime})
, the fibre has a single element, and

q_{3}
gives an isomorphism
U^{\prime\prime}\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,p_{3}(U^{\prime\prime})\cap U
(note that
p_{3}(U^{\prime\prime})

is an open subset of
U
).
Hence
\mathcal{L}
is isomorphic to the local system on
U^{\prime\prime}
obtained as the restriction
of the local system
(p_{2})_{\flat}p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{K}
to
U^{\prime\prime}
, which is a local system
associated to the Galois group
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
, and its irreducible representation
\chi_{\lambda}
.
It follows, by Theorem 5.12,
that
A_{\mathbf{c}}
coincides with
\operatorname{IC}(S_{\mathcal{A}}^{0},\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{\lambda}})
for
a certain stratum datum
S_{\mathcal{A}}
. This proves (i).

The proof of (ii) is completely similar.
The theorem is proved.
