EPTAS for Hard Graph Cut Problems for Dense Graphs
Kaisei Deguchi, Ken-ichi Kawarabayashi, Hiroaki Mori

Abstract
Everywhere- δ \delta -dense graphs are defined as graphs on n n vertices in which every vertex has degree at least δ ​ n \delta n for some constant δ > 0 \delta>0 . Approximation schemes are vital for handling NP-hard optimization problems, but for many graph cut problems, existing PTAS algorithms often suffer from running times of n f ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) n^{f(1/\varepsilon)} . In this paper, we bring PTASs down to EPTASs for several fundamental minimization problems on everywhere- Ω ⁡ ( 1 ) \Omega(1) -dense graphs. Specifically, we present the first Efficient Polynomial-Time Approximation Scheme (EPTAS), running in time f ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) ​ n O ⁡ ( 1 ) f(1/\varepsilon)n^{O(1)} , for the ConstrainedMinCut problem under a global constraint on vertex weights, a problem that captures BalancedSeparator and SmallSetExpansion . Moreover, we give the first EPTASs for MinQuotientCut and ProductSparsestCut on everywhere- δ \delta -dense graphs with integer-valued dense vertex weights; these problems generalize the four well-known problems UniformSparsestCut , EdgeExpansion , Conductance , and NormalizedCut . Our main technical contribution is an EPTAS for ConstrainedMinCut , based on the weak regularity lemma and sampling and estimation techniques. We then obtain EPTASs for MinQuotientCut and ProductSparsestCut via a unified reduction that invokes this algorithm as a subroutine. In contrast, previous works giving PTASs for these problems on everywhere- δ \delta -dense graphs typically rely on powerful tools such as the Lasserre hierarchy or specific integer programming techniques [ 2 , 15 ] , which we avoid.



1
Introduction

1.1
Approximation Schemes

It is known that many optimization problems cannot be solved in polynomial time, under the assumption that P
\neq
NP.
Approximation algorithms are among the most important approaches for handling such hard problems.
To be more precise, approximation algorithms are algorithms that, in polynomial time, produce a solution that is guaranteed to be close to the optimal solution within a particular factor.

In this paper, we are particularly interested in approximation schemes.
Let
f
be a function that depends only on
\varepsilon
.
A
polynomial-time approximation scheme (PTAS)
is a deterministic algorithm that takes an instance of an optimization problem and a parameter
\varepsilon>0
and, in
n^{f(1/\varepsilon)}
time, produces a solution that is within a factor of
1+\varepsilon
of the optimal solution for minimization problems (or within a factor of
1-\varepsilon
for maximization problems).
A PTAS allows us to obtain a solution arbitrarily close to optimal by choosing a sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, although the running time may increase rapidly as
\varepsilon
decreases.
To overcome this difficulty, let us consider an
efficient polynomial-time approximation scheme (EPTAS)
, which is a PTAS with running time
f(1/\varepsilon)n^{O(1)}
, allowing us to obtain a solution arbitrarily close to optimal more efficiently than a PTAS.
The randomized analogue of an EPTAS is called an EPRAS, i.e., an algorithm that runs in time
f(1/\varepsilon)n^{O(1)}
and that returns a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate solution for minimization problems (or a
(1-\varepsilon)
-approximate solution for maximization problems) with constant probability.

Unfortunately, many NP-hard problems do not even admit a PTAS unless P
=
NP
[
3
]
. However, this result does not refute the existence of a PTAS for some restricted classes of instances.
For example, Arora, Karger, and Karpinski
[
2
]
presented the first PTAS in
n^{O(1/\varepsilon^{2})}
time for several NP-hard problems for dense graphs.
Indeed, there has been extensive work on PTASs for maximization problems for dense graphs
[
7
,
10
,
14
,
17
,
23
,
28
]
.
In addition, Goldreich, Goldwasser, and Ron presented an EPRAS for
MaxCut
and additive-error approximation algorithms for some cut problems by applying property testing techniques
[
14
]
.
As stated above, our focus is on NP-hard problems for dense graphs.

1.2
Minimization Problems for Dense Graphs

To the best of our knowledge, there are few known approximation results for NP-hard minimization problems on dense graphs. Indeed, the density required for PTASs for minimization problems is, in a sense, often stronger than that for maximization problems.
One reason is that maximization problems on dense graphs also have large optimal solution values, which allow large additive error (i.e., small relative error), thereby yielding a PTAS or even an EPTAS.
On the other hand, since density alone in the minimization setting does not, in general, ensure a large optimal solution value, even when the given graph instances are dense, it is difficult to apply techniques from maximization problems to minimization problems directly.
This difference makes it substantially more challenging to design approximation algorithms that achieve small relative error for minimization problems.

Nevertheless, some PTASs for minimization problems for dense graphs are known.
For example, several PTASs for CSPs on dense instances have been developed:
Bazgan, Fernandez de la Vega, and Karpinski gave PTASs for specific classes of minimum CSPs
[
6
,
5
]
,
Karpinski and Schudy gave EPRASs for minimum CSPs on specific (
fragile
or
rigid
, i.e., if we independently round or guess the assignments of vertices, the objective value only shifts locally) classes of dense instances
[
16
]
, and
Giotis and Guruswami gave a PTAS for correlation clustering with a fixed number of clusters
[
13
]
.
Guruswami and Sinop
[
15
]
presented a unified approximation scheme for optimization problems that are formulated as specific quadratic integer programs with positive semidefinite objectives and linear constraints.
Specifically, they gave a
\tfrac{1+\varepsilon}{\min\{1,\lambda_{r}\}}
-approximation algorithm in
n^{O(r/\varepsilon^{2})}
time for several graph cut problems, including
UniformSparsestCut
,
EdgeExpansion
,
BalancedSeparator
,
Conductance
,
NormalizedCut
, and
SmallSetExpansion
(for the precise definitions of these problems, see below) by using the Lasserre hierarchy, where
\lambda_{r}
is the
r
-th smallest eigenvalue of the normalized Laplacian of the graph.
Their running time
n^{O(r/\varepsilon^{2})}
depends on the
O(r/\varepsilon^{2})
rounds of the Lasserre hierarchy.
If the graph has the property known as low threshold rank, then these algorithms imply PTASs for these problems.

One of the most powerful tools for designing approximation algorithms for dense graphs is the regularity lemma.
Szemerédi’s regularity lemma is a fundamental result in graph theory that states that every sufficiently large graph can be approximated by a union of a bounded number of random-like bipartite graphs called regular pairs.
Building on this perspective, Frieze and Kannan
[
10
]
presented an algorithmic version of the regularity lemma and gave a constant-time approximation scheme.
Subsequently, many researchers have improved the error bounds of the partition
[
25
]
, and a deterministic version of the regularity lemma was presented
[
8
]
.

In this paper, we focus on graph cut problems, including those with global constraints and those minimizing cut ratios.
We obtain an EPTAS for
ConstrainedMinCut
on the so-called everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs by using the weak regularity lemma
[
10
,
8
]
and sampling and estimation techniques, while avoiding heavier tools such as the Lasserre hierarchy used in previous PTAS results.
Moreover, we obtain EPTASs for
MinQuotientCut
and
ProductSparsestCut
through a unified reduction from the EPTAS for
ConstrainedMinCut
.

More details are given below.

1.3
Graph Cut Problems

For two sets
S,T
, let
\mathrm{E}(S,T)
denote the set of edges with one endpoint in
S
and the other in
T
, i.e.,
\mathrm{E}(S,T)=\{(u,v)\in E\mid u\in S,v\in T\}
.
Note that an edge with both endpoints in
S\cap T
is counted twice.
For
S\subseteq V
, let
\overline{S}:=V\setminus S
.
In particular, we call
\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})
the cut induced by
S
, and
|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|
the cut size of
S
.
Let
d_{v}
be the degree of vertex
v\in V
.
We define the volume of
S\subseteq V
as
\mathrm{vol}(S):=\sum_{v\in S}d_{v}
.
For
S\subseteq V
and a function
c
on
V
, let
c(S)
denote
\sum_{v\in S}c_{v}
.

We now define several graph cut problems.

Problem 1.1

(
BalancedSeparator
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
, a parameter
t\ (1\leq t\leq n/2)
, and a constant
\zeta\ (\zeta>0)
,
find a cut
S
such that
t(1-\zeta)\leq|S|\leq t(1+\zeta)
and
|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|
is minimized.

When
t=n/2
and
\zeta=0
, the problem coincides with the
MinBisection
problem, although this case is outside the scope of the above definition.

Problem 1.2

(
UniformSparsestCut
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
, find a cut
S
such that the
sparsity for uniform demand
\Phi(S)=\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|}{|S||\overline{S}|}
is minimized.
We define
\Phi(G):=\min_{S\subseteq V}\Phi(S)
.

Problem 1.3

(
EdgeExpansion
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
, find a cut
S
such that the
edge expansion
h(S)=\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|}{\min(|S|,|\overline{S}|)}
is minimized.
We define
h(G):=\min_{S\subseteq V}h(S)
.

Problem 1.4

(
SmallSetExpansion
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
, a parameter
\rho\ (1\leq\rho\leq\mathrm{vol}(V)/2)
, and a constant
\zeta\ (\zeta>0)
, find a cut
S
such that
\rho(1-\zeta)\leq\mathrm{vol}(S)\leq\rho(1+\zeta)
and
|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|
is minimized.

Problem 1.5

(
Conductance
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
, find a cut
S
such that the
conductance
\phi(S)=\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|}{\min(\mathrm{vol}(S),\mathrm{vol}(\overline{S}))}
is minimized.
We define
\phi(G):=\min_{S\subseteq V}\phi(S)
.

Problem 1.6

(
NormalizedCut
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
, find a cut
S
such that the
normalized cut
\mathrm{NC}(S)=\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|}{\mathrm{vol}(S)\mathrm{vol}(\overline{S})}
is minimized.
We define
\mathrm{NC}(G):=\min_{S\subseteq V}\mathrm{NC}(S)
.

Problem 1.7

(
ConstrainedMinCut
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
with an integer vertex-weight function
c:V\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
c_{v}\leq n
for every
v\in V
,
a parameter
\rho\ (1\leq\rho\leq c(V)/2)
, and a constant
\zeta\ (\zeta>0)
,
find a cut
S
such that
\rho(1-\zeta)\leq c(S)\leq\rho(1+\zeta)
and
|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|
is minimized.
We refer to the constraint
\rho(1-\zeta)\leq c(S)\leq\rho(1+\zeta)
as the cost constraint.

Problem 1.8

(
MinQuotientCut
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
with an integer vertex-weight function
c:V\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
c_{v}\leq n
for every
v\in V
,
find a cut
S
such that the
quotient cut
q(S)=\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|}{\min(c(S),c(\overline{S}))}
is minimized.
We define
q(G):=\min_{S\subseteq V}q(S)
.

Problem 1.9

(
ProductSparsestCut
)
.

Given an unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
with an integer vertex-weight function
c:V\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
c_{v}\leq n
for every
v\in V
,
find a cut
S
such that the
sparsity for product demand
\Phi^{\times}(S)=\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|}{c(S)c(\overline{S})}
is minimized.
We define
\Phi^{\times}(G):=\min_{S\subseteq V}\Phi^{\times}(S)
.

The
SmallSetExpansion
problem
[
26
]
plays an essential role in complexity theory and hardness of approximation.
Under the Small Set Expansion Hypothesis (SSEH), strong inapproximability results are known for many problems
[
4
,
11
,
21
]
.
Moreover, Raghavendra and Steurer
[
26
]
showed that SSEH implies the Unique Games Conjecture (UGC), and UGC yields tight (often optimal) inapproximability results for a wide range of problems
[
18
,
19
,
27
]
.

1.4
Our Contributions

Below, we present our main results. We use
\tilde{O}(\cdot)
to hide polylogarithmic factors.

Definition 1.10

(everywhere-
\delta
-dense graph)
.

An unweighted graph
G=(V,E)
is everywhere-
\delta
-dense if each vertex has degree at least
\delta n
.

Definition 1.11

(dense instance of
ConstrainedMinCut
)
.

A
ConstrainedMinCut
instance
\mathcal{I}=(G,c,\rho,\zeta)
is called dense if
G
is an everywhere-
\Omega(1)
-dense graph and there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that
c_{0}n\leq c_{v}
for every vertex
v\in V
.

Throughout this paper, we treat the density parameters
\delta
and
c_{0}
as fixed positive constants.
Accordingly, the hidden constants may depend on
\delta
and
c_{0}
.
We also treat the interval parameter
\zeta
as a constant; however, since the EPTAS for
ConstrainedMinCut
is used as a subroutine in later reductions, we make its dependence on
\zeta
explicit whenever it is relevant.

Algorithmic Improvements:

Our main result is an EPTAS for
ConstrainedMinCut
, which yields EPTASs for several problems on everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs, either by direct implication or by a short unified reduction.
This improves upon prior PTAS results, whose running times are of the form
n^{f(1/\varepsilon)}
.

Table
1
summarizes prior results and our contributions.
The heavy
2^{\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon^{c})}
dependence in the running time stems from our use of the deterministic weak regularity lemma
[
8
]
, which yields a decomposition of width
O(\varepsilon^{-16})
.
If we allow randomization, we may instead use the randomized weak regularity lemma
[
10
]
with width
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
, thereby improving the exponent of
1/\varepsilon
in the
2^{\tilde{O}(\cdot)}
term (with high probability).

Theorem 1.12

(Main)
.

There exists an EPTAS for the

ConstrainedMinCut
problem
on dense instances.

In particular, by setting
\rho=tn
and
c_{v}=n
for every vertex
v\in V
, we may take
c_{0}=1
, and the cost constraint coincides with the cardinality constraint in the instance
BalancedSeparator
(G,t,\zeta)
.
Hence, Theorem
1.12
yields an EPTAS for
BalancedSeparator
(G,t,\zeta)
.
Similarly, by setting
c_{v}=d_{v}
for every vertex
v\in V
, the cost constraint coincides with the volume constraint in
SmallSetExpansion
;
moreover, since
G
is everywhere-
\delta
-dense, we may take
c_{0}=\delta
.
Thus, Theorem
1.12
also yields an EPTAS for
SmallSetExpansion
and hence the following corollary holds.

Corollary 1.13
.

There exist EPTASs for the problems

BalancedSeparator
and
SmallSetExpansion

on everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs, and their running times are
\varepsilon^{-O(1)}n^{O(1)}+2^{\tilde{O}(1/(\zeta^{16}\varepsilon^{32}))}
.

Moreover, dense instances of
MinQuotientCut
and
ProductSparsestCut
admit a short reduction to
ConstrainedMinCut
.

(dense instance of
MinQuotientCut
and

)
.

A

\mathcal{I}=(G,c)
is called dense if

c_{0}>0
such that
c_{0}n\leq c_{v}
for every vertex
v\in V
.

Therefore, the EPTAS for

yields EPTASs for dense instances of

and

.

Theorem 1.15
.

There exist EPTASs for the problems

MinQuotientCut
and

on dense instances, and their running times are
\varepsilon^{-O(1)}n^{O(1)}+2^{\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon^{48})}
.

EdgeExpansion
and
Conductance
(resp.
UniformSparsestCut
and
NormalizedCut
) are special cases of
MinQuotientCut
(resp.
ProductSparsestCut
).
Hence, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.16
.

There exist EPTASs for the problems

UniformSparsestCut
,
EdgeExpansion
,
Conductance
, and
NormalizedCut

on everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs, and their running times are
\varepsilon^{-O(1)}n^{O(1)}+2^{\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon^{48})}
.

Previous works giving PTASs for these problems on everywhere-
\delta

[

]
or specific integer programming techniques
[

.
As a consequence, the resulting running time is of the form

, which yields only PTASs but not EPTASs.
In contrast, using the weak regularity lemma as the main tool

Hardness:

Unlike problems such as
MaxCut
, for
ConstrainedMinCut
, the assumption that the input graph is everywhere-
\delta
-dense cannot be weakened to the condition
|E|\geq\Omega(n^{2})
, often used to define so-called dense graphs.
To see this, we first observe that by an argument similar to that in
[
2
]
, if there exists a PTAS for
BalancedSeparator
on dense graphs, then there exists a PTAS for
BalancedSeparator
on general graphs, which clearly contradicts the known hardness result.

Given any (not necessarily dense) instance of
BalancedSeparator

(G=(V,E),t(=\Theta(n)),\zeta(=\Theta(1)))
with
n
vertices,
we construct an instance
G^{\prime}
for
BalancedSeparator
with
|E(G^{\prime})|\geq\Omega(|V(G^{\prime})|^{2})
and show that we can recover a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate solution to the original
BalancedSeparator
instance from a nearly optimal solution to the constructed instance.

We add a clique
K
with
t(1+\zeta)+n
vertices to
G
, and let
G^{\prime}
denote the resulting graph, which is dense.
We show that if we run the PTAS on
(G^{\prime},t,\zeta)
, we can recover a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate solution to the original
BalancedSeparator
instance.
Let
S
be a feasible solution to the constructed instance, where
X=S\cap V
and
Y=S\cap K
.

Now, our goal is to show that we can recover a cut
T
from
S
such that
|\mathrm{E}_{G}(T,\overline{T})|\leq|\mathrm{E}_{G^{\prime}}(S,\overline{S})|
holds.
The cut size of
S
is

|\mathrm{E}_{G^{\prime}}(S,\overline{S})|=|\mathrm{E}_{G}(X,\overline{X})|+|Y|(|K|-|Y|).

When
X
is a feasible solution to the original instance, the condition
|\mathrm{E}_{G}(T,\overline{T})|\leq|\mathrm{E}_{G^{\prime}}(S,\overline{S})|
holds.

When
|X|<t(1-\zeta)
holds, we construct a feasible cut
T=X\cup A
where
A\subseteq V\setminus X
is an arbitrary vertex set with
|X\cup A|=t(1-\zeta)
.
By the feasibility of
S
,
|A|
is at most
|Y|
.
Since
|K|-|Y|=t(1+\zeta)+n-|Y|\geq n
holds,

|\mathrm{E}_{G}(T,\overline{T})|\leq|\mathrm{E}_{G}(X,\overline{X})|+|A|n\leq|\mathrm{E}_{G}(X,\overline{X})|+|Y|n\leq|\mathrm{E}_{G}(X,\overline{X})|+|Y|(|K|-|Y|)=|\mathrm{E}_{G^{\prime}}(S,\overline{S})|
.
The above argument shows that every feasible solution
S
of
G^{\prime}
can be converted
into a feasible solution
T
of
G
such that

|\mathrm{E}_{G}(T,\overline{T})|\leq|\mathrm{E}_{G^{\prime}}(S,\overline{S})|.

Hence
\mathrm{OPT}_{G}\leq\mathrm{OPT}_{G^{\prime}}
.
Conversely, every feasible solution of
G
remains feasible for
G^{\prime}
when we take no vertices from
K
.
Therefore,
\mathrm{OPT}_{G^{\prime}}\leq\mathrm{OPT}_{G}
, and hence
\mathrm{OPT}_{G}=\mathrm{OPT}_{G^{\prime}}
holds.

When we run the PTAS for the constructed instance and obtain a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate solution
S
, we can construct
T
such that
|\mathrm{E}_{G}(T,\overline{T})|\leq|\mathrm{E}_{G^{\prime}}(S,\overline{S})|\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}_{G^{\prime}}=(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}_{G}
holds.
Therefore, if there exists a PTAS for
BalancedSeparator
on dense graphs, it yields a PTAS for
BalancedSeparator
on general graphs.

Thus, it is unlikely that there exist PTASs for either
BalancedSeparator
or
ConstrainedMinCut
on dense graphs.

Comparison with prior PTASs:

Our algorithm builds on the same sampling and estimation paradigm as prior PTASs for dense graph problems.
Most prior PTASs for dense CSPs
[
6
,
5
,
13
]
treat unconstrained problems, and a few
[
16
]
handle hard
local
constraints, such as those in the
MultiwayCut
problem.
On the other hand,
BalancedSeparator
and
SmallSetExpansion
require global interval cardinality or volume constraints, and hence these problems involve non-fragile global constraints.
Therefore, these previous works do not provide PTASs for problems that require non-fragile global constraints.
We give more details in the next subsection.

The most relevant prior work is the PTAS for
BalancedSeparator
on dense graphs due to Arora, Karger, and Karpinski
[
2
]
, which runs in
n^{O(1/\varepsilon^{3})}
time and is therefore not an EPTAS.
Our results improve upon this in two directions: we obtain an EPTAS running in
f(1/\varepsilon)n^{O(1)}
time, and we extend the framework to the more general
ConstrainedMinCut
problem, which captures both
BalancedSeparator
and
SmallSetExpansion
.
A naive sampling and estimation framework does not produce solutions satisfying the global constraint, and we introduce an additional rebalancing step that adjusts the cut to satisfy the constraint without significantly increasing the cut size.

1.5
Technical Contributions

As mentioned above, we leverage weak regularity and specialized sampling and rebalancing procedures for cost-constrained problems.
To this end, technically speaking, our main tool is to generalize the EPRAS for the
MinBisection
problem in everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs with small optimal value
[
2
]
to an EPRAS for the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem in the same setting.
For the
MinBisection
problem, there is a strong structural characterization of the optimal solution, and this characterization is used to design the EPRAS.

Lemma 1.17

(Lemma 5.1 in
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(L,R)
be an optimal cut for the
MinBisection
problem.
Then at least one of
L
and
R
has the property that every vertex on this side has more than half of its neighbors on the same side.

Unfortunately, such a strong structural characterization does not extend to the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem because of the cost constraint.
Indeed, this problem requires an interval cost constraint (this is also the case for
BalancedSeparator
and
SmallSetExpansion
).
If, for example, we apply the independent sampling framework in
[
16
]
to the
MinBisection
problem, we may miss the exact bisection threshold by
O(\varepsilon n)
vertices.
We may blindly try to “fix” the resulting cut by greedily moving
O(\varepsilon n)
vertices back across the cut to balance the bisection.
But then we may cut up to
O(\varepsilon\delta n^{2})
new edges.
However, when the “Small OPT” case happens (
\mathrm{OPT}\ll n^{2}
), an error of
O(\varepsilon\delta n^{2})
completely violates the
(1+\varepsilon)
relative approximation.
This is exactly why our Algorithm
3
(the exact knapsack DP) is more than an incremental contribution.
By using an exact DP to carefully resolve the uncertain set
X
to meet the cost constraint without incurring the
O(\varepsilon\delta n^{2})
greedy penalty, we can successfully overcome this difficulty.

In the end, we show that our sampling procedure correctly classifies vertices with high probability, and that the rebalancing procedure can adjust the cut cost to satisfy the cost constraint without increasing the cut size too much.
This is a key lemma, which enables us to obtain an EPRAS for the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem on everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs when the optimal value is small.
We then derandomize the two randomized steps in the algorithms for
ConstrainedMinCut
.

By using the EPTAS for
ConstrainedMinCut
, we obtain EPTASs for
MinQuotientCut
and
ProductSparsestCut
for dense instances in a unified manner.

In Section

-dense graphs, and then we derandomize the randomized steps of the algorithm.
In Section

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

In this section, we provide some notation used in this paper.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected unweighted graph with
n
vertices.
We denote by
N(v)
the set of neighbors of vertex
v
.

\mathbbm{1}[P]
is the indicator function that equals 1 if the predicate
P
is true and 0 otherwise.

2.2
Weak Regularity Lemma

We first introduce notation and then state the Frieze-Kannan Weak Regularity Lemma
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Frobenius Norm)
.

For an
n\times n
matrix
A
, its Frobenius norm
\|A\|_{F}
is defined as

\|A\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{i,j}^{2}}.

Definition 2.2

(Cut Norm)
.

For an
n\times n
matrix
A
, its cut norm
\|A\|_{C}
is defined as

\|A\|_{C}=\max_{S,T\subseteq[n]}\left|\sum_{i\in S,j\in T}A_{i,j}\right|.

Definition 2.3

(Cut Decomposition)
.

A cut decomposition of an
n\times n
matrix
A
is a decomposition of the form

A=d_{1}\chi_{S_{1}}\chi_{T_{1}}^{T}+d_{2}\chi_{S_{2}}\chi_{T_{2}}^{T}+\cdots+d_{w}\chi_{S_{w}}\chi_{T_{w}}^{T}+W

where for each
t
,
S_{t},T_{t}\subseteq[n]
,
d_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\chi_{S_{t}}
is the indicator vector of set
S_{t}
, and
W
is an
n\times n
matrix called the error matrix.
We call
w
the width of the cut decomposition,
\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{w}d_{t}^{2}}
the coefficient length, and
\|W\|_{C}
the error of the cut decomposition.

We are now ready to present the main lemma.

Theorem 2.4

(Frieze-Kannan Weak Regularity Lemma
[
10
]
)
.

Suppose
A
is an
n\times n
matrix and
\varepsilon,\gamma\in(0,1)
.
There exists an algorithm that finds a cut decomposition of
A
of width
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
, coefficient length at most
\sqrt{27}\|A\|_{F}/n
, and error at most
\varepsilon n\|A\|_{F}
in time
2^{\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})}/\gamma^{2}
with probability at least
1-\gamma
.

Remark 2.5
.

For the adjacency matrix of an unweighted graph, we have
\|A\|_{F}\leq n
. Hence, the coefficient length is at most
\sqrt{27}
and the error is at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
.

3
EPTAS for ConstrainedMinCut

Throughout this section, we work with dense instances of
ConstrainedMinCut
.
Since
d_{v}\leq n
and
c_{0}n\leq c_{v}
,
d_{v}\leq\tfrac{1}{c_{0}}c_{v}
holds.

Our first step is to show that when the parameter of the problem is unbalanced, we can find a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate solution for the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem.

We may assume
\varepsilon\leq 1
without loss of generality.
For any
\zeta>0
, if
\rho(1+\zeta)\leq c_{0}\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\delta^{2}n^{2}
, then
c_{0}|S|\delta n\leq c_{0}\mathrm{vol}(S)\leq c(S)\leq\rho(1+\zeta)\leq c_{0}\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\delta^{2}n^{2}
.
Thus, we have
|S|\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\delta n
, and hence
|S|^{2}\leq|S|\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\delta n\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathrm{vol}(S)
.
Under this condition, we can conclude the following inequalities hold:

(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2})\mathrm{vol}(S)\leq\mathrm{vol}(S)-|S|^{2}\leq|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|\leq\mathrm{vol}(S).

Since
\tfrac{1}{1-\varepsilon/2}\leq 1+\varepsilon
for
\varepsilon\leq 1
, this gives
|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|\leq\mathrm{vol}(S)\leq(1+\varepsilon)|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|
for any feasible
S
.
Therefore, for the cut
S^{\prime}
that minimizes
\mathrm{vol}(S)
subject to
c(S)\in[\rho(1-\zeta),\rho(1+\zeta)]
, we have

|\mathrm{E}(S^{\prime},\overline{S^{\prime}})|\leq\mathrm{vol}(S^{\prime})\leq\mathrm{vol}(S^{*})\leq(1+\varepsilon)|\mathrm{E}(S^{*},\overline{S^{*}})|,

where
S^{*}
is the optimal cut for
ConstrainedMinCut
. Hence we obtain a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate solution.
This can be done by simple knapsack dynamic programming in
O(n^{3})
time.

Below, we focus on the case where the input parameters are balanced.
Let
\rho^{\prime}:=\rho/c(V)
be the normalized parameter, and we may assume that
\rho^{\prime}>\tfrac{c_{0}\varepsilon\delta^{2}}{2(1+\zeta)}
, since
c(V)\leq n^{2}
.

In
[
9
]
, Frieze and Kannan presented an EPRAS for the
BalancedSeparator
problem in the large-optimal-value case on everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs by using the algorithmic regularity lemma.
Combining the result in
[
2
]
for the small-optimal-value case, they obtained an EPRAS for the
BalancedSeparator
problem for everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs.
In this section, we present an EPRAS for the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem on everywhere-
\delta
-dense graphs by extending their technique.
We denote the optimal cut by
(L_{\mathrm{OPT}},R_{\mathrm{OPT}})
and its size by
\mathrm{OPT}:=|\mathrm{E}(L_{\mathrm{OPT}},R_{\mathrm{OPT}})|
. We divide the algorithm into two cases, based on the size of
\mathrm{OPT}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists an EPRAS for the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem on dense instances, and its running time is
n^{O(1)}+2^{\tilde{O}(1/(\zeta^{2}\varepsilon^{4}))}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

There exists an EPTAS for the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem on dense instances, and its running time is
\varepsilon^{-O(1)}n^{O(1)}+2^{\tilde{O}(1/(\zeta^{16}\varepsilon^{32}))}
.

The difference in runtime between Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
is derived from the width of the cut decomposition.

The most important difference from the
BalancedSeparator
problem is the existence of a cost constraint
c(S)\in[\rho(1-\zeta),\rho(1+\zeta)]
.

This applies to both the large-optimal-value and small-optimal-value cases of the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem.
We use the parameter
\alpha
, which will be determined in the proof of the small-optimal-value case.

3.1
Large OPT Case for ConstrainedMinCut Problem

If
n\leq N(1/\varepsilon)
, the problem can be solved exactly by brute force in
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
time.
Thus, in what follows, we assume that
n
is sufficiently large.

We apply the weak regularity lemma to handle the case in which the optimal cut size
\mathrm{OPT}\geq\alpha n^{2}
for the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem (we refer to this as the “Large OPT” case).

Our main result for this case is as follows:

Theorem 3.3
.

For dense instances of
ConstrainedMinCut
with optimal cut size
\mathrm{OPT}\geq\alpha n^{2}
,
we can find a cut
\mathrm{E}(L,\overline{L})
such that
c(L)\in[\rho(1-\zeta),\rho(1+\zeta)]
and its cut size is at most
(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}
in time
2^{\tilde{O}(1/(\alpha\varepsilon)^{2})}+n^{O(1)}
with probability
9/10
.

Frieze and Kannan
[
10
]
applied the weak regularity lemma to obtain additive-error approximation algorithms for many graph problems, including
MaxCut
and
UniformSparsestCut
.
In this subsection, building on their framework, we impose a cost constraint to derive an algorithm for the
ConstrainedMinCut
problem and prove Theorem
3.3
.

The algorithm consists of the following four steps:

1.

Compute a cut decomposition.

2.

Check feasibility via an LP.

3.

Round down the fractional solution.

4.

Move vertices to satisfy the cost constraint.

Following the algorithm above, we first obtain a cut decomposition with constant width and small error, according to the given precision parameter
\varepsilon
, by applying the weak regularity lemma (Theorem
2.4
) to the adjacency matrix of the given dense graph.
Then, for each part of the cut decomposition, we discretize its possible contribution to the cut size. For each assignment, we check the feasibility of the assignment by solving a linear program.
Finally, we round the fractional solution of the LP to obtain a feasible cut satisfying the cost constraint.
If we take the best candidate among all possible assignments, we can obtain a cut of size at most
(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}
.
Since the optimal cut size is large, the additive errors introduced by the weak regularity lemma and by the discretization are small compared to
\mathrm{OPT}
.

By Theorem
2.4
, we can obtain a cut decomposition
(d_{t},S_{t},T_{t})_{t=1,\ldots,w}
of the adjacency matrix
A
with error at most
\tfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{10}n^{2}
, width
w=O(\varepsilon_{0}^{-2})
and coefficient length at most
\sqrt{27}
in time
2^{\tilde{O}(\varepsilon_{0}^{-2})}
with probability
9/10
(we fix error parameter
\gamma
in Theorem
2.4
to be
1/10
).

First, we split
V
into at most
2^{2w}
parts
\mathcal{P}
.
Recall that
S_{t},T_{t}\subseteq V
.
We can construct vectors
x_{v}=(\mathbbm{1}[v\in S_{1}],\mathbbm{1}[v\in T_{1}],\ldots,\mathbbm{1}[v\in S_{w}],\mathbbm{1}[v\in T_{w}])
for each vertex
v\in V
.
For each
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{2w}

P_{\sigma}=\{v\in V\mid x_{v}=\sigma\}
. Then
\mathcal{P}
forms a partition of
V
.
In the cut decomposition
(d_{t},S_{t},T_{t})
, the contribution of each term to the cut
\mathrm{E}(L,\overline{L})
is expressed by
d_{t}f_{t}g_{t}
where
f_{t}=|L\cap S_{t}|
and
g_{t}=|\overline{L}\cap T_{t}|
.
Discretizing
f_{t}
and
g_{t}
into
\bar{f}_{t}:=\lfloor\tfrac{f_{t}}{\nu}\rfloor\nu
and
\bar{g}_{t}:=\lfloor\tfrac{g_{t}}{\nu}\rfloor\nu
respectively, we can approximate
\sum_{t=1}^{w}d_{t}f_{t}g_{t}

\sum_{t=1}^{w}d_{t}\bar{f}_{t}\bar{g}_{t}
with error at most
3n\nu\sum_{t=1}^{w}|d_{t}|
where
\nu=\tfrac{\varepsilon_{0}n}{70\sqrt{27}w}
(after appropriate scaling).
Now,
\bar{f}_{t}
and
\bar{g}_{t}
only take discrete values in
\nu K:=\{0,\nu,2\nu,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{\nu}\rceil\nu\}
, so we can enumerate all
O(1/\varepsilon_{0}^{3})
values for each
\bar{f}_{t}
and
\bar{g}_{t}
.

We further split each of these
2^{2w}
parts into
\lceil\frac{n}{\kappa}\rceil
parts based on their cost
c_{v}
.
We fix a parameter
\kappa=c_{0}\tfrac{\min\{\varepsilon_{0},\delta\}n}{30}
.
We define
V_{m}=\{v\in V\mid c_{v}\in[\kappa m,\kappa(m+1))\}
for
m=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{\kappa}\rceil-1
.
Note that
V_{m}
forms a partition of
V
and
\{P\cap V_{m}\mid P\in\mathcal{P},m=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{\kappa}\rceil-1\}
is also a partition of
V
.
Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{P\cap V_{m}\mid P\in\mathcal{P},m=0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{\kappa}\rceil-1\}
.
Thus, each part
P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is characterized by
S_{t}
and
T_{t}
for
(t=1,\ldots,w)
and its cost
\Delta_{P}:=\kappa m
.
The number of parts in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is at most
2^{2w}\lceil\frac{n}{\kappa}\rceil=2^{2w}\lceil\tfrac{30}{c_{0}\min\{\varepsilon_{0},\delta\}}\rceil
, which does not depend on
n
.

For each
(\bar{f}_{1},\ldots,\bar{f}_{w},\bar{g}_{1},\ldots,\bar{g}_{w})\in\nu K^{2w}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq{x_{P}}

\displaystyle\leq|P|\quad(P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq{\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime},P\subseteq S_{t}}x_{P}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{f}_{t}+\nu\quad(t=1,2,\ldots,w)

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq{\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime},P\subseteq T_{t}}(|P|-x_{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{g}_{t}+\nu\quad(t=1,2,\ldots,w)

\displaystyle\rho(1-\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}(\Delta_{P}+\kappa)x_{P},\quad\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Delta_{P}x_{P}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(1+\zeta),

\displaystyle x_{P}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}\quad(P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime})

is an integer variable representing the number of vertices in part
P
assigned to cut
\mathrm{E}(L,\overline{L})
.
For any cut
\mathrm{E}(L,\overline{L})
corresponding to
\bar{f}_{t}=\lfloor\frac{|L\cap S_{t}|}{\nu}\rfloor\nu
and
\bar{g}_{t}=\lfloor\frac{|\overline{L}\cap T_{t}|}{\nu}\rfloor\nu
for all
t=1,\ldots,w
, there exists a feasible solution to this integer problem.
In other words, there exist parameters
(\bar{f}_{1},\ldots,\bar{f}_{w},\bar{g}_{1},\ldots,\bar{g}_{w})
such that the optimal cut
\mathrm{E}(L_{\mathrm{OPT}},\overline{L_{\mathrm{OPT}}})
corresponds to them.

To obtain an integer solution for this problem, we consider the LP relaxation of this problem and let
x_{P}
be a feasible solution.
We round
x_{P}
to
y_{P}=\lfloor x_{P}\rfloor
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.
Once we obtain
y_{P}
, we can construct the corresponding cut
\mathrm{E}(L^{*},\overline{L^{*}})
.
We show that the cut
\mathrm{E}(L^{*},\overline{L^{*}})
corresponding to the rounded solution
y_{P}
does not violate the cost constraint too much, and its cut size is close to the value computed from
\bar{f}_{t}
and
\bar{g}_{t}
.

Since
x_{P}
is a feasible solution to the LP relaxation, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\rho(1-\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}(\Delta_{P}+\kappa)x_{P}

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Delta_{P}x_{P}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(1+\zeta).

Let
z_{P}:=x_{P}-\lfloor x_{P}\rfloor
for each
P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.
We have the upper and lower bounds on the cost of the rounded solution as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}(\Delta_{P}+\kappa)y_{P}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}(\Delta_{P}+\kappa)(x_{P}-z_{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\left(\Delta_{P}x_{P}+\kappa x_{P}-z_{P}(\Delta_{P}+\kappa)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\rho(1+\zeta)+\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\kappa x_{P}\qquad(\because\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Delta_{P}x_{P}\leq\rho(1+\zeta))

\displaystyle\leq\rho(1+\zeta)+\kappa n.\qquad(\because\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}x_{P}\leq n)

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Delta_{P}y_{P}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\left((\Delta_{P}+\kappa)(x_{P}-z_{P})-\kappa\lfloor x_{P}\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\left((\Delta_{P}+\kappa)x_{P}-z_{P}(\Delta_{P}+\kappa)-\kappa\lfloor x_{P}\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\geq\rho(1-\zeta)-\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\left((\Delta_{P}+\kappa)+\kappa\lfloor x_{P}\rfloor\right)\qquad(\because 0\leq z_{P}\leq 1)

\displaystyle\geq\rho(1-\zeta)-|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|n-\kappa|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|-\kappa n\qquad(\because\Delta_{P}\leq n\text{ and }\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\lfloor x_{P}\rfloor\leq n)

\displaystyle\geq\rho(1-\zeta)-3\kappa n.\qquad(\because|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|<n,|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|<\kappa\text{ holds for sufficiently large }n)

Since
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\Delta_{P}y_{P}\leq c(L^{*})\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}(\Delta_{P}+\kappa)y_{P}
holds, by rounding
x_{P}
down to
y_{P}
, we can ensure that
\rho(1-\zeta)-3\kappa n\leq c(L^{*})\leq\rho(1+\zeta)+\kappa n
.

We now verify that the cut size induced by
y_{P}
is close to the value computed from
\bar{f}_{t}
and
\bar{g}_{t}
.
Since
x_{P}-y_{P}\leq 1
for each
P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
,
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime},P\subseteq S_{t}}\left(x_{P}-y_{P}\right)
is at most
|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|=2^{2w}\lceil\tfrac{n}{\kappa}\rceil
.
With
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime},P\subseteq S_{t}}x_{P}\geq\bar{f}_{t}
, we can ensure that
\bar{f}_{t}-|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime},P\subseteq S_{t}}y_{P}\leq\bar{f}_{t}+\nu
.
We have a similar bound for
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime},P\subseteq T_{t}}(|P|-y_{P})
.
Thus, we obtain the following bounds:

\displaystyle||S_{t}\cap L^{*}|-\bar{f}_{t}|\leq\nu+2^{2w}\lceil\tfrac{n}{\kappa}\rceil\leq 2\nu

\displaystyle||T_{t}\cap\overline{L^{*}}|-\bar{g}_{t}|\leq\nu+2^{2w}\lceil\tfrac{n}{\kappa}\rceil\leq 2\nu

for sufficiently large
n
.

These bounds imply:

\displaystyle|\sum_{t=1}^{w}d_{t}|S_{t}\cap L^{*}||T_{t}\cap\overline{L^{*}}|-\sum_{t=1}^{w}d_{t}\bar{f}_{t}\bar{g}_{t}|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{t=1}^{w}d_{t}\bigl((|S_{t}\cap L^{*}|-\bar{f}_{t})|T_{t}\cap\overline{L^{*}}|+\bar{f}_{t}(|T_{t}\cap\overline{L^{*}}|-\bar{g}_{t})\bigr)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{w}|d_{t}|(2\nu n+2\nu n)

\displaystyle\leq 4n\nu\sum_{t=1}^{w}|d_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{27}n\nu w.

Thus, the total error in the cut size
|\mathrm{E}(L^{*},\overline{L^{*}})|
from discretizing
f_{t}
and
g_{t}
and rounding is at most
7\sqrt{27}n\nu w\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon_{0}n^{2}}{10}
.

Finally, we can adjust the rounded solution
y_{P}
to satisfy the cost constraint by moving vertices.
Since
\kappa n\leq c_{0}\tfrac{\min\{\varepsilon_{0},\delta\}}{30}n^{2}
and
\rho(1+\zeta)-\rho(1-\zeta)=2\rho\zeta=\Omega(n^{2})
, we can adjust the cut to satisfy the cost constraint as follows:
We move vertices one by one in an arbitrary order until the cost constraint is satisfied, and let
\hat{L}
be the final cut after moving vertices.
Since the cost of each vertex is at most
n
, and the length of the constraint interval is
\Omega(n^{2})
, we can always find a feasible solution by this procedure.
For example, if
c(L^{*})\leq\rho(1-\zeta)
holds, then for the final moved vertex
v
and moved set
S
, we have:

c(L^{*})+c(S)<\rho(1-\zeta)\leq c(L^{*})+c(S)+c_{v}

Since
\rho(1-\zeta)-3\kappa n\leq c(L^{*})
holds, we have
c(S)+c_{v}<3\kappa n+n
and
\hat{L}=L^{*}\cup S\cup\{v\}
is a feasible solution satisfying the cost constraint.
The increase in the cut size by moving one vertex is bounded by its degree, which is at most
\tfrac{1}{c_{0}}c_{v}
.
More generally, moving
T\subseteq L^{*}
to
\overline{L^{*}}
or moving
T\subseteq\overline{L^{*}}
to
L^{*}
changes the cut size by at most
\tfrac{1}{c_{0}}c(T)
.
Hence, the total increase in the cut size by the moving procedure is at most
\tfrac{1}{c_{0}}(3\kappa n+n)
.
Thus, the total error for the cut size is at most
\tfrac{1}{c_{0}}(3\kappa n+n)+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{0}n^{2}}{10}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon_{0}n^{2}}{10}+\tfrac{1}{c_{0}}n+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{0}n^{2}}{10}\leq\tfrac{1}{c_{0}}n+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}n^{2}}{5}
.
The cut size of the final solution is at most
\mathrm{OPT}+\tfrac{1}{c_{0}}n+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}n^{2}}{5}+\tfrac{2\varepsilon_{0}}{10}n^{2}<\mathrm{OPT}+\varepsilon_{0}n^{2}
for sufficiently large
n
(
\tfrac{2\varepsilon_{0}}{10}n^{2}
is the error from the weak regularity lemma).

If we set
\varepsilon_{0}=\alpha\varepsilon
and run the above algorithm, the best cut among all
\bar{f}_{t}
and
\bar{g}_{t}
has size at most
\mathrm{OPT}+\varepsilon_{0}n^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}
with probability
9/10
.
Repeating this process
O(\log{(1/\eta)})
times and taking the best cut among them, the success probability increases to

Proof of Theorem
3.4
:

First of all, let
S=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k\log n})
be the sampled sequence.
We split the occurrences of
S
into two subsequences
S_{L}
and
S_{R}
.
A partition
(S_{L},S_{R})
of the occurrences of
S
is called consistent if, for all
i,j
with
s_{i}=s_{j}
, the two occurrences
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
are assigned to the same side.

Throughout this subsection, for a vertex set
U\subseteq V
and a sampled sequence or subsequence
T
, the quantity
|U\cap T|
denotes the number of occurrences in
T
whose vertex belongs to
U
.
In particular, if the same vertex appears multiple times in
T
, it is counted with its multiplicity.

Let
\hat{p}_{L}(v)=\frac{|N(v)\cap S_{L}|}{|N(v)\cap S|}
and
p_{L}(v)=\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|}{d_{v}}
for each vertex
v\in V
(similarly, we define
\hat{p}_{R}(v):=\tfrac{|N(v)\cap S_{R}|}{|N(v)\cap S|}
and
p_{R}(v):=\tfrac{|N(v)\cap R_{\mathrm{OPT}}|}{d_{v}}
).
The estimators
\hat{p}_{L}(v)
and
\hat{p}_{R}(v)
are therefore computed using the multiplicities of sampled neighbors in
S
.
We show that, when the consistent partition
(S_{L},S_{R})
agrees with the optimal cut
(L_{\mathrm{OPT}},R_{\mathrm{OPT}})
on the sampled vertices, with high probability,
\hat{p}_{L}(v)
is close to
p_{L}(v)
for all
v\in V
.

We show the following key lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

If we take
k\geq\max(\frac{2}{a^{2}\delta},\frac{4}{\delta^{2}})
for a constant
a>0
, then
|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\leq a
holds with high probability (over the choice of
S
) for all
v\in V
.

Proof.

First, we consider the conditional probability
\Pr[|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a\mid|N(v)\cap S|=m]
.
We define random variables
X_{i},Y_{i}\ (i=1,\ldots,m)
as follows:

Y_{i}\text{ is sampled uniformly at random from }N(v)

X_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{$Y_{i}\in L_{\mathrm{OPT}}$}\\
0&\text{$Y_{i}\notin L_{\mathrm{OPT}}$}.\end{cases}

Conditional on
|N(v)\cap S|=m
, the
m
occurrences of
S
that lie in
N(v)
have the same distribution as independent samples
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
drawn uniformly from
N(v)
with replacement.
Note that these occurrences form a multiset.

Specifically, we have
\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=\frac{1}{d_{v}}\sum_{u\in V}\mathbbm{1}[u\in N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}]=\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|}{d_{v}}=p_{L}(v)
.
By Hoeffding’s inequality, we have:

\Pr[|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a\mid|N(v)\cap S|=m]\leq 2\exp(-2ma^{2}).

We denote
I=[(\frac{d_{v}}{n}-u)|S|,(\frac{d_{v}}{n}+u)|S|]
.
Then we have:

\displaystyle\Pr[|\frac{|N(v)\cap S|}{|S|}-\frac{d_{v}}{n}|\geq u]

\displaystyle=\Pr[|N(v)\cap S|\notin I]

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp(-2|S|u^{2})

\displaystyle=2n^{-\frac{k\delta^{2}}{2}}.

We set
u=\frac{\delta}{2}
and
I=[\frac{k\delta\log{n}}{2},(1+\frac{\delta}{2})k\log{n}]
. Then we have:

\displaystyle\max_{m\in I}\Pr[|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a\mid|N(v)\cap S|=m]

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp(-k\delta a^{2}\log{n})

\displaystyle=2n^{-k\delta a^{2}}.

\displaystyle\Pr[|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in I}\Pr[|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a\mid|N(v)\cap S|=m]\Pr[|N(v)\cap S|=m]

\displaystyle+\sum_{m\notin I}\Pr[|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a\mid|N(v)\cap S|=m]\Pr[|N(v)\cap S|=m]

\displaystyle\leq\max_{m\in I}\Pr[|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a\mid|N(v)\cap S|=m]+\Pr[|N(v)\cap S|\notin I]

\displaystyle\leq 2n^{-k\delta a^{2}}+2n^{-\frac{k\delta^{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4n^{-\min(k\delta a^{2},\frac{k\delta^{2}}{2})}.

By union bound, we have:

\displaystyle\Pr[^{\exists}v\in V,|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V}\Pr[|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\geq a]

\displaystyle\leq 4n^{1-\min(k\delta a^{2},\frac{k\delta^{2}}{2})}.

Thus, if we set
k\geq\max(\frac{2}{a^{2}\delta},\frac{4}{\delta^{2}})
,
|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)|\leq a
holds with high probability for all
v\in V
.
∎

In what follows, we assume that the above lemma holds.

Let
L_{0}=L\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}
,
R_{0}=R\cap R_{\mathrm{OPT}}
,
U_{L}=L\cap R_{\mathrm{OPT}}
, and
U_{R}=R\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}
.
We obtain the following lemma concerning the volumes of
U_{L}
and
U_{R}
, respectively.

Claim 3.6
.

\mathrm{vol}(U_{L}),\mathrm{vol}(U_{R})\leq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{1/2+a}
and
|U_{L}|,|U_{R}|\leq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{(1/2+a)\delta n}
hold.

Proof.

If
v\in U_{L}
, then
p_{L}(v)\geq\frac{1}{2}+a
holds.

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in U_{L}}|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in U_{L}}(\frac{1}{2}+a)d_{v}

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}+a)\mathrm{vol}(U_{L}).

Thus, we have
\mathrm{vol}(U_{L})\leq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{1/2+a}
.

\mathrm{vol}(U_{R})
can be bounded in the same way.
∎

This claim implies that
L,R
are close to
L_{\mathrm{OPT}},R_{\mathrm{OPT}}
, respectively.
In addition, we can bound the volume of
X
in a similar manner.
We denote
X_{L}=X\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}
and
X_{R}=X\cap R_{\mathrm{OPT}}
.

Claim 3.7
.

\mathrm{vol}(X)\leq\frac{2\mathrm{OPT}}{1/2-3a}
and
|X|\leq\frac{2\mathrm{OPT}}{(1/2-3a)\delta n}
hold.

Proof.

If
v\in X_{L}
, then
\hat{p}_{L}(v)\leq\frac{1}{2}+2a
, and hence
p_{L}(v)\leq\frac{1}{2}+3a
by the key lemma. Therefore
p_{R}(v)\geq\frac{1}{2}-3a
.
Similarly, if
v\in X_{R}
, then
p_{L}(v)\geq\frac{1}{2}-3a
.
Thus, every vertex in
X
contributes at least
(\frac{1}{2}-3a)d_{v}
to the optimal cut, counted from its own side.

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{OPT}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in X_{L}}|N(v)\cap R_{\mathrm{OPT}}|+\sum_{v\in X_{R}}|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in X_{L}}(\frac{1}{2}-3a)d_{v}+\sum_{v\in X_{R}}(\frac{1}{2}-3a)d_{v}

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{1}{2}-3a)\mathrm{vol}(X).

Thus, we have
\mathrm{vol}(X)\leq\frac{2\mathrm{OPT}}{1/2-3a}
and
|X|\leq\frac{2\mathrm{OPT}}{(1/2-3a)\delta n}
.
∎

Intuitively, the vertices in
X
make a large contribution to the optimal cut.
Since the optimal cut is bounded by
\alpha n^{2}
, their total volume and size cannot be too large.

We now show that, in the first case of Algorithm
3
, where
c(L)+c(X)\geq C_{M}
, we can obtain a cut of size at most
(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
c(L)+c(X)\geq C_{M}
,
we can find sets
S\subseteq L
and
T\subseteq X
in polynomial time such that

|\mathrm{E}(S,L)|-|\mathrm{E}(S,R)|+|\mathrm{E}(T,R)|+|\mathrm{E}(X\setminus T,L)|\leq|\mathrm{E}(U_{L},L)|-|\mathrm{E}(U_{L},R)|+|\mathrm{E}(X_{L},R)|+|\mathrm{E}(X_{R},L)|

holds and the adjusted cut
((L\setminus S)\cup T,(R\cup S)\cup(X\setminus T))
is a feasible solution for the original
ConstrainedMinCut
instance.

Proof.

Consider the following auxiliary optimization problem:

\begin{gathered}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\text{minimize}}\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$\displaystyle\sum_{u\in S}V_{u}+\sum_{u\in T}V^{\prime}_{u}$}\\
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\text{s.t.}}\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$S\subseteq L,T\subseteq X$}\\
\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$\sum_{u\in S}d_{u}\leq\frac{\alpha n^{2}}{1/2+a}$}\\
\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$c(L)-c(S)+c(T)\leq C_{M}$}\end{gathered}

where
V_{u}=|N(u)\cap L|-|N(u)\cap R|
for
u\in L
and
V^{\prime}_{u}=|N(u)\cap R|-|N(u)\cap L|
for
u\in X
.

We claim that
(S,T)=(U_{L},X_{L})
is feasible for this problem.
By Claim
3.6
, we have

\sum_{u\in U_{L}}d_{u}=\mathrm{vol}(U_{L})\leq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{1/2+a}\leq\frac{\alpha n^{2}}{1/2+a}.

Moreover,

c(L)-c(U_{L})+c(X_{L})=c(L_{0})+c(X_{L})=c(L_{\mathrm{OPT}})-c(U_{R})\leq c(L_{\mathrm{OPT}})\leq C_{M}.

Thus
(U_{L},X_{L})
is feasible for the auxiliary problem.

We emphasize that the lower cost constraint is intentionally not imposed in this auxiliary problem.
Indeed, the natural solution
(S,T)=(U_{L},X_{L})
satisfies the upper constraint, but it may violate the lower constraint, since
c(L)-c(U_{L})+c(X_{L})=c(L_{0})+c(X_{L})=c(L_{\mathrm{OPT}})-c(U_{R})
.
Thus imposing the lower bound would prevent us from comparing the optimum with the feasible pair
(U_{L},X_{L})
.
Instead, we show that every feasible solution of the auxiliary problem automatically satisfies the lower bound.

Let
S^{*},T^{*}
be the optimal solution for this problem.
Since

\displaystyle|\mathrm{E}(T,R)|+|\mathrm{E}(X\setminus T,L)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in T}|N(u)\cap R|+\sum_{u\in X\setminus T}|N(u)\cap L|

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in X}|N(u)\cap L|+\sum_{u\in T}V^{\prime}_{u}

holds, we have

\displaystyle|\mathrm{E}(S^{*},L)|-|\mathrm{E}(S^{*},R)|

\displaystyle+|\mathrm{E}(T^{*},R)|+|\mathrm{E}(X\setminus T^{*},L)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathrm{E}(U_{L},L)|-|\mathrm{E}(U_{L},R)|+|\mathrm{E}(X_{L},R)|+|\mathrm{E}(X\setminus X_{L},L)|.

Here, we used the fact that
X=X_{L}\sqcup X_{R}
.

Let
(S,T)
be any feasible solution of the auxiliary problem.
Since

\sum_{u\in S}d_{u}\leq\frac{\alpha n^{2}}{1/2+a}

and every vertex has degree at least
\delta n
, we have

|S|\leq\frac{\alpha n}{\delta(1/2+a)},\qquad c(S)\leq\frac{\alpha n^{2}}{\delta(1/2+a)}.

Moreover, by Claim
3.7
,

|X|\leq\frac{2\mathrm{OPT}}{(1/2-3a)\delta n}\leq\frac{2\alpha n}{(1/2-3a)\delta},

and hence

c(X)\leq\frac{2\alpha n^{2}}{(1/2-3a)\delta}.

Using the assumption
c(L)+c(X)\geq C_{M}
, we obtain

\displaystyle c(L)-c(S)+c(T)

\displaystyle\geq c(L)-c(S)

\displaystyle\geq c(L)+c(X)-c(S)-c(X)

\displaystyle\geq C_{M}-\frac{\alpha n^{2}}{\delta(1/2+a)}-\frac{2\alpha n^{2}}{\delta(1/2-3a)}.

By the choice
\alpha\leq\tfrac{6\rho^{\prime}\zeta c_{0}\delta}{35}
, we have

\frac{\alpha n^{2}}{\delta(1/2+a)}+\frac{2\alpha n^{2}}{\delta(1/2-3a)}\leq 2\rho^{\prime}\zeta c_{0}n^{2}\leq C_{M}-C_{m},

where the last inequality follows from
c(V)\geq c_{0}n^{2}
.
Therefore,
c(L)-c(S)+c(T)\geq C_{m}
.
The upper bound
c(L)-c(S)+c(T)\leq C_{M}
is imposed explicitly in the auxiliary problem.
Hence every feasible solution of the auxiliary problem yields a feasible cut
((L\setminus S)\cup T,(R\cup S)\cup(X\setminus T))
for the original
ConstrainedMinCut
instance.

Finally, we show that the above problem can be solved in polynomial time by dynamic programming.
First, we solve the following two knapsack problems separately:

\begin{gathered}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\text{minimize}}\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$\displaystyle\sum_{u\in S}V_{u}$}\\
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\text{s.t.}}\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$S\subseteq L$}\\
\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$c(S)=C$}\\
\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$\sum_{u\in S}d_{u}\leq\tfrac{\alpha n^{2}}{1/2+a}$}\end{gathered}

\begin{gathered}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\text{minimize}}\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$\displaystyle\sum_{u\in T}V^{\prime}_{u}$}\\
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\text{s.t.}}\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$T\subseteq X$}\\
\makebox[345.0pt][c]{$c(T)=C^{\prime}$}\end{gathered}

Now, we compute these optimal solutions
\min\{\sum_{u\in S}V_{u}\mid S\subseteq L,c(S)=C,\sum_{u\in S}d_{u}\leq\tfrac{\alpha n^{2}}{1/2+a}\}
and
\min\{\sum_{u\in T}V^{\prime}_{u}\mid T\subseteq X,c(T)=C^{\prime}\}
for all
C=0,\ldots,c(L)
and
C^{\prime}=0,\ldots,c(X)
.
The first minimization is solved by dynamic programming over both the cost and the degree.
Next, we combine the two solutions to obtain the optimal solution to the auxiliary problem.

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}C=0,\ldots,c(L)\\
C^{\prime}=0,\ldots,c(X)\\
c(L)-C+C^{\prime}\leq C_{M}\end{subarray}}\left\{\min\{\sum_{u\in S}V_{u}\mid S\subseteq L,c(S)=C,\sum_{u\in S}d_{u}\leq\tfrac{\alpha n^{2}}{1/2+a}\}+\min\{\sum_{u\in T}V^{\prime}_{u}\mid T\subseteq X,c(T)=C^{\prime}\}\right\}

Since
c(L),c(X),\mathrm{vol}(L)\leq n^{2}
, the dynamic programming procedures and the final minimization run in polynomial time.

The dynamic program returns a pair attaining the above minimum, which is precisely an optimal solution to the auxiliary problem.
Therefore, by comparing it with the feasible solution
(U_{L},X_{L})
, we obtain the desired inequality.
Moreover, by the feasibility argument above, the adjusted cut
((L\setminus S^{*})\cup T^{*},(R\cup S^{*})\cup(X\setminus T^{*}))
satisfies the original cost constraint.
This completes the proof.
∎

We can derandomize the above algorithm.
We use the randomized algorithm in two places:

1.

Fox et al. showed that there is a deterministic algorithm to obtain a partition of

Theorem 3.10

(Theorem 1.3 in

]
)

Suppose

We can derandomize the large-optimal-value case by replacing the randomized weak regularity lemma in Theorem

The increased width of the cut decomposition yields a running time of

Next, we derandomize the sampling step.
Gillman showed a Chernoff bound for a random walk on a weighted graph in terms of the eigenvalue gap

Theorem 3.11

(Theorem 2.1 in
[

]
)
.

Let

be a weighted graph with

For

Let

Let

Then, for all

\Pr\left[\left|\frac{t_{S}}{t}-\pi(S)\right|\geq u\right]\leq 2(1+u\lambda/10)\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right\|_{2}e^{-\frac{\lambda u^{2}t}{20}}.

To apply Theorem
3.11

-regular expander on exactly

Many explicit constructions of such expander families are known
[

Fix an even constant

By Alon’s construction of explicit expanders of every degree and size

For sufficiently small fixed

For derandomization, we enumerate all walks of length

Note that

Since

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\left|\frac{|N(v)\cap S|}{|S|}-\frac{d_{v}}{n}\right|\geq u\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+u\lambda/10)\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right\|_{2}e^{-\frac{\lambda u^{2}t}{20}},

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\left|\frac{|N(v)\cap S_{L}|}{|S|}-\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|}{n}\right|\geq u\right]

\displaystyle\left|\hat{p}_{L}(v)-p_{L}(v)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{|N(v)\cap S_{L}|/|S|}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}-\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n}{d_{v}/n}\right|

\displaystyle=\bigg\lvert\frac{|N(v)\cap S_{L}|/|S|}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}-\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}-\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n}{d_{v}/n}\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{|N(v)\cap S_{L}|/|S|}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}-\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}-\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n}{d_{v}/n}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{||N(v)\cap S_{L}|/|S|-|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n|}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{|N(v)\cap L_{\mathrm{OPT}}|/n}{d_{v}/n}\cdot{\frac{||N(v)\cap S|/|S|-d_{v}/n|}{|N(v)\cap S|/|S|}}

satisfies
\min\{c(S^{*}),c(\overline{S^{*}})\}\leq c(V)/2
, we may assume without loss of generality that
c(S^{*})\leq c(V)/2
.
Therefore, for the largest index
k
, we replace
\rho_{k}
with the clamped value
\rho_{k}^{\prime}:=\tfrac{c(V)}{2(1+\zeta)}
so that the cost interval at index
k
becomes
[\rho_{k}^{\prime}(1-\zeta),c(V)/2]
.
This clamp does not create a gap in the coverage of
[\rho_{0}(1-\zeta),c(V)/2]
because
\rho_{k}^{\prime}(1-\zeta)=\tfrac{c(V)(1-\zeta)}{2(1+\zeta)}\leq\rho_{k}(1-\zeta)
holds by
\rho_{k}(1+\zeta)\geq c(V)/2
.
Since
\zeta
is unchanged, the runtime of the subroutine is not worsened.

Consequently, there always exists
j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
such that
c(S^{*})\in[\rho_{j}(1-\zeta),\rho_{j}(1+\zeta)]
and
\rho_{j}(1+\zeta)\leq c(V)/2
, which justifies the bound
\min\{c(S^{*}),c(\overline{S^{*}})\}\leq\rho_{j}(1+\zeta)
in the analysis below.

We run
ConstrainedMinCut
(G,c,\rho_{j},\zeta,\varepsilon/3)
with
\rho_{j},\zeta
from Lemma
4.2
(with the clamp at
j=k
in Remark
4.3
) for
j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
, and denote the output cut by
S_{j}
.
We take the best cut among them, which is shown to be a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate solution for
MinQuotientCut
as follows:

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon/3)q(S^{*})

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon/3)\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S^{*},\overline{S^{*}})|}{\min\{c(S^{*}),c(\overline{S^{*}})\}}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{0\leq j\leq k}\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S_{j},\overline{S_{j}})|}{(1+\zeta)\rho_{j}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\zeta}{1+\zeta}\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S_{j^{*}},\overline{S_{j^{*}}})|}{c(S_{j^{*}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon/3}q(S_{j^{*}}).

Here,
j^{*}
is an index of the best cut among
S_{j}
.
The first inequality follows by running the EPTAS for
ConstrainedMinCut
with parameters
\rho_{j},\zeta
for an index
j
such that
c(S^{*})\in[\rho_{j}(1-\zeta),\rho_{j}(1+\zeta)]
with precision parameter
\varepsilon/3
.
Its running time is
\varepsilon^{-O(1)}n^{O(1)}+2^{\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon^{48})}
, since we run the EPTAS for
ConstrainedMinCut

\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon)
times, each with
\zeta=\Theta(\varepsilon)
and precision parameter
\varepsilon/3
.

4.2
ProductSparsestCut

4.2.1
When the Optimal Cut Is Unbalanced

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be an everywhere-
\delta
-dense graph.
For all cuts
\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})
with
|S|\leq\min\{\varepsilon\delta n,\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}c_{0}n\}
, the sparsity
\Phi^{\times}(S)
can be approximated by
\tfrac{\mathrm{vol}(S)}{c(V)c(S)}
within
1\pm\varepsilon
multiplicative error and
\tfrac{\mathrm{vol}(S)}{c(V)c(S)}
can be minimized in
O(n)
time.

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S)}{c(V)c(S)}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S)-|S|^{2}}{c(S)(c(V)-c(S))}\leq\Phi^{\times}(S)=\frac{|\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})|}{c(S)c(\overline{S})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S)}{c(S)c(V)(1-c(S)/c(V))}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S)}{c(S)c(V)}(1+\varepsilon).

Thus, for all cuts
\mathrm{E}(S,\overline{S})
with
|S|\leq\min\{\varepsilon\delta n,\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}c_{0}n\}
, we have

(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S)}{c(V)c(S)}\leq\Phi^{\times}(S)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S)}{c(V)c(S)}.

∎

As in the previous section,
\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S)}{c(V)c(S)}
can be minimized in
O(n)
time.

4.2.2
When the Optimal Cut Is Balanced

Lemma 4.5
.

For a given
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a constant
\zeta
and a sequence of
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\ldots
such that the following hold:

•

\rho_{0}(1-\zeta)\leq\min\{\varepsilon\delta c_{0}n^{2},\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}c_{0}^{2}n^{2}\}
.

•

\rho_{i+1}(1-\zeta)=\rho_{i}(1+\zeta)
.

•

The minimum index
k
such that
\rho_{k}(1+\zeta)\geq c(V)/2
is at most
O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right)
.

•

For
{}^{\forall}i\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
,
\tfrac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta}\tfrac{c(V)-\rho_{i}(1-\zeta)}{c(V)-\rho_{i}(1+\zeta)}\leq 1+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We fix
\zeta=\tfrac{\varepsilon/2}{2+3\varepsilon/2}
and define
\rho_{i}
as
\left(\tfrac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta}\right)^{i}\tfrac{\min\{\varepsilon\delta c_{0}n^{2},\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}c_{0}^{2}n^{2}\}}{1-\zeta}
.

\rho_{0}(1-\zeta)=\min\{\varepsilon\delta c_{0}n^{2},\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}c_{0}^{2}n^{2}\}
and
\rho_{i+1}(1-\zeta)=\rho_{i}(1+\zeta)
hold by definition.
The minimum index
k
that satisfies
\rho_{k}(1+\zeta)\geq c(V)/2
is obtained as follows:

\rho_{k}(1+\zeta)=(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon})^{k+1}\cdot\min\{\varepsilon\delta c_{0}n^{2},\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}c_{0}^{2}n^{2}\}\geq n^{2}\geq c(V)/2

(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon})^{k+1}\geq\frac{2}{\varepsilon c_{0}\min\{\delta,c_{0}\}}

k+1\geq\frac{\log{\frac{2}{\varepsilon c_{0}\min\{\delta,c_{0}\}}}}{\log{(1+\varepsilon/(2+\varepsilon))}}=O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right).

Finally, we prove the last condition.
We can assume
\rho_{i}(1-\zeta)<c(V)/2
for all
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
because
\rho_{k-1}(1+\zeta)=\rho_{k}(1-\zeta)<c(V)/2
holds by the definition of
k
.
The last condition is equivalent to the following:

2\zeta c(V)+\varepsilon\rho_{i}(1-\zeta^{2})\leq\varepsilon(1-\zeta)c(V).

Since
\rho_{i}(1-\zeta)<c(V)/2
, we have

\varepsilon\rho_{i}(1-\zeta^{2})=\varepsilon\rho_{i}(1-\zeta)(1+\zeta)<\frac{\varepsilon(1+\zeta)}{2}c(V).

Thus, it suffices to show

2\zeta c(V)+\frac{\varepsilon(1+\zeta)}{2}c(V)\leq\varepsilon(1-\zeta)c(V).

This holds by the choice

\zeta=\frac{\varepsilon/2}{2+3\varepsilon/2}.

This completes the proof.
∎
