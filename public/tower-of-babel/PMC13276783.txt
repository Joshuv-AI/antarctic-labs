Inhibition of FGFR signaling attenuates phosphaturia and early kidney injury in sickle cell disease mice
Authors: Marja M Hurley, Yanlin Wang, Liping Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe examined whether short term treatment with the pan-FGF Receptor (FGFR) inhibitor BGJ398 (Infigratinib) modulated impaired FGFR downstream signaling, phosphate wasting and markers of kidney damage in sickle cell disease (SCD) mice. Healthy control (Ctrl) and SCD mice were subcutaneously injected with BGJ398 for 24 h. In serum both FGF23 intact and carboxy-terminal-fragment FGF23 were significantly higher in SCD and were not decreased with BGJ398. In SCD mice, serum phosphate level was significantly decreased, and urine phosphate/creatinine ratio were significantly higher and BGJ398 significantly reduced urine phosphate wasting. Immunohistochemistry analysis of kidneys revealed that phosphate transporters NPT2a and PIT-2 proteins were significantly decreased in SCD kidneys and were significantly improved by BGJ398. Signaling molecules that mediate decreased NPT2a, and PIT-2 expression were significantly increased and were significantly reduced by BGJ398. Markers of SCD kidney injury such as glomeruli sclerosis, tubular-damage-marker NGAL, early kidney-damage-marker KIM1, inflammatory M1 iNOS+ macrophages, and cytokines tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFa) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) were significantly increased. BGJ398 did not reduce glomerular sclerosis or NGAL, but significantly reduced iNOS+ macrophages, KIM-1, TNFa, and IL-6 in SCD kidneys. BGJ398 also significantly decreased kidney osteopontin and its receptor integrin αVβ3, that are associated with inflammation. In summary impaired FGFR downstream signaling mediated phosphaturia in SCD that could be modulated by BGJ398. We further suggest that osteopontin and aberrant integrin signaling contributes to kidney inflammation that can be partially rescued by BGJ398.Keywords: Sickle cell disease mice, FGF receptor inhibitor, Phosphaturia, Kidney inflammation

Introduction

Sickle cell disease (SCD) is an inherited autosomal recessive hematologic disorder that impacts about 100,000 Americans and millions of individuals globally [1]. In SCD subjects the abnormal form of hemoglobin S (HbS), caused by valine replacing glutamic acid in the β-globin gene, polymerizes under conditions such dehydration or acidosis causing red blood cell (RBC) sickling, resulting in impaired RBC function and longevity culminating in anemia [1]. A leading complication of vaso-occlusion is hypoxia resulting in ischemic tissues and organs, including the kidney [1,2] and previous studies have shown that damage to the kidney, results in acute and chronic kidney disease (CKD) with initial phosphate wasting and later hyperphosphatemia [2,3].

Fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23) plays a central role in maintaining phosphate (Pi) homeostasis [4]. It has been reported that FGF23 is as an early marker of impaired phosphate homeostasis in the early stages of renal disease prior to evidence of increased parathyroid hormone (PTH) and phosphate [5,6]. Of relevance to SCD, recent reports by Batte et al. [3] showed that FGF23 is a potential biomarker for bone mineralization disorders and renal disease in SCD patients admitted to the hospital.

Of importance, FGF receptors (FGFRs), co-receptor alpha klotho (αKlotho), required for FGF23 downstream signaling [[7], [8], [9], [10]], mediates phosphate excretion in the kidney via activation of the mitogen-activated kinases (MAPK) and serum-glucocorticoid-regulated Kinase 1 (SGK1) pathways [8], to modulate the sodium-phosphate cotransporter NaPi-2a (NPT2a), as well as the sodium phosphate co-transporter PIT2/Slc20a2 [9]. Since we previously reported that pan-FGF receptor (FGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor BGJ398 (Infigratinib) rescued FGF23 mediated phosphate wasting in a FGF2 isoform transgenic mouse model [10] mouse model, in this study, we determined whether phosphate wasting in the kidneys of SCD mice could be reduced by FGFR blockade using BGJ398 that modulates several downstream signaling pathways [11].

Of relevance to the present study, osteopontin (OPN), a phosphorylated glycoprotein that has the ability to inhibit bone mineral deposition, was reported along with FGF23 as a potential biomarker of SCD kidney disease [3]. OPN, a member of the small integrin-binding ligand N-linked glycoprotein (SIBLING) family, is a principal sialoprotein of bone [12] that is also expressed in multiple tissues, including the kidney, where it can participate in inflammatory processes [13]. Since OPN can be associated with kidney disease in SCD [3], we examined whether SCD kidney pathologic changes was associated with increased OPN. In addition, Since OPN activates various signaling pathways including integrins to mediate kidney injury [14,15], we examined whether BGJ398 could affect OPN, kidney injury and inflammatory cells in Townes SCD mice [[16], [17], [18]] that phenocopy the clinical manifestation of human SCD [19].

This novel study shows that phosphaturia and abnormal OPN accumulation in kidney of humanized SCD mice demonstrating signs of kidney inflammation, is accompanied by aberrant FGFR signaling, down-regulation of the kidney Pi transporters NPT2a, PiT2, and increased inflammatory macrophages and cytokines that could be significantly modulated by FGFR inhibitor BGJ398 that may be translationally relevant.

Materials and methods

Animals

Male and female Townes sickle cell disease (SCD) mice and their healthy littermate controls, all on a mixed C57BL/6 and 129 backgrounds, were acquired from The Jackson Laboratory (Stock #013071, Maine, USA). These genetically modified mice carry human α- and β-globin genes inserted into the mouse locus, enabling the breeding of healthy control and SCD offspring from heterozygous sickle cell trait (SCT) parents.

All animals were maintained at the Center for Comparative Medicine at UConn Health. Since chronic kidney disease (CKD) commonly arises with age in SCD patients and is associated with elevated phosphate levels in blood, we used 6-month-old Ctrl and SCD mice. Mice were maintained on Envigo Teklad 2918 diet, which contains 1% calcium, 0.7% total phosphate, and 1.5 IU/g of vitamin D3. A total of 34 male mice (18 Ctrl, 29.8–42.9 g; and 16 SCD, 29.5–35.8 g) were randomly assigned to receive either vehicle (3.5 mM HCl, 5% DMSO) or BGJ398 treatment, with 8–9 mice per genotype and treatment group. A parallel cohort of female mice (n = 6/group) was studied independently using the same protocol. Randomization was performed using the RAND () function in Microsoft Excel. Group sizes were based on pilot data. Prior to initiating treatment, mice were group-housed by genotype to reduce variability from fecal exchange between genotypes.

All animal procedures were approved by the UConn Health Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee with reference number AP-201028.01, approved on 12/19/2023. The ethical guidelines were followed. All studies were conducted in compliance with institutional and ARRIVE guidelines, as well as the NIH (National Research Council) Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

In this study, we first conducted the treatment and all the immunohistochemistry staining and analyses in male mice. Once these experiments were completed, we then initiated the treatment procedures in female mice. Thus, the staining and treatments in male and female cohorts were performed at different times. All methods and protocols applied to female mice were identical, but the timeline was staggered to ensure appropriate treatment intervals.

Administration of FGFR inhibitor BGJ398

At 6 months of age, both control and SCD mice received a single subcutaneous injection of either vehicle (3.5 mM HCl in 5% DMSO) or BGJ398 at a dose of 2 mg/kg body weight. This dosing protocol was selected based on prior findings demonstrating its effectiveness in reducing phosphaturia in a murine model of hypophosphatemia [10]. Following injection, mice were placed individually in metabolic cages (Sugiyama-Gen, Tokyo, Japan) for a 24-h period to collect urine samples. After the 24-h period, urine samples were collected, and mice were euthanized using CO2. The right kidney was excised, fixed in formalin, and processed for paraffin embedding for subsequent histological analysis.

Biochemical analyses

Following euthanasia, blood samples were harvested via cardiac puncture to obtain serum. Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels were examined by the Urea Nitrogen Liqui-UV assay (StanBio Laboratory). Serum concentrations of ferritin, erythropoietin (EPO), α-Klotho, intact and C-terminal fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23), 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D], and 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D [1,25(OH)2D] and parathyroid hormone (PTH) were quantified using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits in accordance with each manufacturer's protocol [20]. Creatinine levels in both serum and urine were measured using the QuantiChrom assay kit (BioAssay Systems). Phosphate levels in serum and urine were assessed using the Phosphorus Liqui-UV assay (StanBio Laboratory). Urinary albumin was quantified using a mouse-specific ELISA kit (Crystal Chem). Detailed information regarding ELISA kit sources is provided in Supplemental Table 1.

Histological and immunohistochemical analysis of kidney tissue

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded kidney samples were sectioned at 2 μm thickness and stained using the Periodic Acid–Schiff (PAS) method, following the manufacturer's instructions (Poly Scientific R&D Corp., Bay Shore, NY). For immunohistochemistry (IHC), 5 μm paraffin sections were used. After deparaffinization and rehydration, endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched using 3% hydrogen peroxide for 15 min. Antigen retrieval was performed by heating the sections to 95°C in 10 mM sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for 10 min. Sections were then blocked with 10% serum at room temperature for 30 min, followed by incubation with primary antibodies for 1 h. After washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), sections were incubated with biotinylated secondary antibodies for 1 h. Section staining was detected with a DAB Peroxidase Substrate Kit (Vector Laboratories, Burlingame, CA), counterstained with Harris hematoxylin, dehydrated, and cover slipped. Microscopic images were captured using a Nikon microscope (Nikon Corp., Tokyo, Japan) at the corticomedullary junction. DAB staining was quantified in Fiji/ImageJ using color deconvolution. Positive staining was assessed by measuring area fraction and mean gray value, and the integrated DAB signal (arbitrary units, AU) was calculated as Area Fraction × Mean Gray Value. All quantification was performed by an investigator blinded to the experimental groups. Antibody sources are provided in Supplemental Table 1. Corresponding low-magnification IHC images are available in the Supplemental Figures. Negative control sections processed without primary antibody were included for all IHC experiments to assess non-specific staining and negative control (NC) images are shown in the Supplemental Figures.

Statistical analysis

Results are expressed as mean ± standard error (SE). Outliers exceeding two standard deviations from the group mean (mean ± 2SD) were excluded from analysis. Statistical comparisons were conducted using two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post hoc test for multiple group comparisons. Analyses were performed using SPSS software, with significance set at p < 0.05. Bar graphs displaying individual data points along with group means and SE were generated using GraphPad Prism version 10.

There was a significant increase in serum ferritin in SCD-Vehicle treated male (Table 1) and female (Table 2) mice versus Ctrl-Vehicle that was not reduced by 24 h BGJ398 treatment and there was not a significant genotype × treatment interaction. We also measured serum erythropoietin (EPO) whose expression in the kidney increases during hypoxia and anemia [21]. EPO was significantly higher in SCD-Vehicle treated male (Table 1) and female (Table 2) mice compared with Ctrl-Vehicle. BGJ398 significantly reduced EPO and there was no significant interaction between genotype and treatment.

Serum FGF23, alpha klotho, PTH, 25(OH)Vit D, 1,25(OH)2Vit D in SCD versus Ctrl mice

Since FGF receptor and klotho mediates FGF23 signaling in kidney to maintain phosphate homeostasis, we measured serum intact FGF23 (iFGF23), total including carboxy-terminal fragments of FGF23 (cFGF23), soluble alpha klotho, parathyroid hormone (PTH), 25(OH) VitD and 1,25(OH)2D in Control and SCD mice treated with Vehicle or BGJ398. As shown in male (Table 1) and female (Table 2) mice, serum iFGF23, total and cFGF23, alpha klotho, and 25(OH) Vit D were significantly increased in SCD-Vehicle group compared to Ctrl-Vehicle group. BGJ398 treatment did not significantly reduce serum iFGF23, total and cFGF23, alpha klotho, and 25(OH) Vit D. Serum PTH was significantly lower in SCD-Vehicle group versus Ctrl-Vehicle group, and was not significantly affected by BGJ398 treatment. Serum 1,25(OH)2D was significantly higher in SCD-Vehicle treated male and female mice relative to Ctrl and was further significantly increased with BGJ398 treatment.

Serum and urine biomarkers of renal function in SCD versus Ctrl mice treated with BGJ398

Since FGF23 regulates phosphate homeostasis via FGFR, and both serum iFGF23 and cFGF23 were significantly increased in SCD-Vehicle treated mice of both sexes, we examined the effect of BGJ398 on serum and urine phosphate as well as other markers of renal function. Serum Pi was significantly decreased in SCD-Vehicle-treated male mice, and was not affected by BGJ398 treatment (Fig. 1A). Urine phosphate/urine creatinine ratio was significantly increased in SCD-Vehicle treated male mice, and was significantly reduced by BGJ398 (Fig. 1B). Serum Pi was similar among groups in female mice (Fig. 1C). However, urine phosphate/urine creatinine ratio was significantly higher in SCD-Vehicle female mice and was not decreased in SCD by BGJ398 treatment (Fig. 1D).

There was a significant increase in BUN in SCD-Vehicle treated male mice that was not modulated by BGJ398 (Table 1). BUN was also significantly higher in SCD-Vehicle females but in contrast to males, was significantly decreased by BGJ398 (Table 2). In both male (Table 1) and female (Table 2) mice, serum creatinine was significantly decreased in SCD-Vehicle treated group consistent with reduced muscle mass observed in SCD but was not modulated by BGJ398. To assess for proteinuria, urine albumin was measured. As shown in male (Fig. 1E) and female (Fig. 1F), urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR) was not higher in SCD mice, BGJ398 treatment increased uACR in female SCD mice.

Expression of sodium-phosphate transporters NPT2a and PIT2, in kidneys of SCD versus Ctrl mice

We examined whether the expression of the key phosphate transporter NPT2a responsible for phosphate reabsorption in kidneys was altered in SCD kidneys. IHC of kidneys of Ctrl and SCD male (Fig. 1G) and female (Fig. 1H) mice (Supplemental Fig. 1 for lower magnification of both sexes), revealed decreased NPT2a in SCD-Vehicle kidneys. Quantitation shows that significantly reduced NPT2a in SCD-vehicle was substantially increased in SCD-BGJ398 treated male (Fig. 1I) and female (Fig. 1J) mice.

We further assessed the expression of PIT2/Slc20a2 that is also important in Pi-dependent signaling (9). As shown in kidney of male (Fig. 1K) and female (Fig. 1L) (Supplemental Fig. 1 for lower magnification of both sexes), there was decreased staining of PIT2 in proximal tubules of SCD-IgG mice. Quantitation shows significantly increased PIT2 staining in SCD-BGJ398 treated male (Fig. 1M) and female (Fig. 1N) mice.

IRS scoring of pFGFR1 was significantly higher in SCD-Vehicle and was significantly decreased by BGJ398 male (Fig. 2C) and female (Fig. 2D). Membrane-bound αKlotho in the kidney plays a crucial role in FGF23/FGFR-mediated regulation of phosphate homeostasis [23]. IHC staining of kidneys from Ctrl and SCD male and female mice for αKlotho are shown in Fig. 2E and F (Supplemental Fig. 2 for lower magnification of both males and females). Quantitation shows substantially increased αKlotho in tubules of SCD-Vehicle treated kidneys that was significantly reduced by BGJ398 in male (Fig. 2G) and female (Fig. 2H).

Effect of FGFR inhibition on kidney osteopontin expression and osteopontin signaling pathways in kidney of SCD mice

It is known that OPN is increased in SCD (3) and OPN can also be increased in kidney disease associated with inflammation (13), therefore we determined the expression level of OPN in SCD kidneys and the effect of BGJ398. OPN staining was increased in male SCD-Vehicle kidneys versus Ctrl-Vehicle (Fig. 4A) and female (Fig. 4B) (Supplemental Fig. 4 for lower magnification of both sexes) that was significantly decreased by BGJ398 in male (Fig. 4C) and female (Fig. 4D). Since pathologic effects of OPN was reported to be mediated via interaction with receptors, integrin αVβ3 and CD44 to induce multiple downstream signaling molecules [14], we examined integrin αVβ3 and CD44 expression in the kidneys. Immunostaining and quantitation of kidney of male (Fig. 4E and F) and female (Fig. 4G and H) (Supplemental Fig. 4 for lower magnification of both sexes) showed that integrin αVβ3 level was significantly higher in SCD-Vehicle group versus Ctrl-Vehicle. BGJ398 treatment significantly decreased integrin αVβ3 expression in SCD kidney of both sexes. CD44 expression was also significantly increased in SCD-Vehicle kidneys compared with Ctrl-Vehicle but was not reduced by BGJ398 treatment in male (Fig. 4I and J) or female (Fig. 4K and L) SCD mice (Supplemental Fig. 4 for lower magnification of both sexes).

Staining for NGAL, a kidney tubular injury marker [3] shows increased staining in renal proximal and distal tubules of male SCD-Vehicle versus Ctrl-Vehicle (Fig. 5C) and female (Fig. 5D) (Supplemental Fig. 5 for lower magnification of both males and females). Quantitation showed significantly increased NGAL staining in male SCD-Vehicle (Fig. 5E) and female (Fig. 5F) that was not reduced by BGJ398. However, staining and quantitation for early kidney injury molecule 1 (KIM1), revealed a significant increase in KIM1 in SCD-Vehicle kidneys versus Ctrl-Vehicle that was significantly reduced in the kidneys of BGJ398 treated SCD male (Fig. 5G and H) and female (Fig. 5I and J) mice (Supplemental Fig. 5 for lower magnification of both sexes).

We observed evidence of inflammation in SCD kidneys, thus, to determine the type of inflammatory cells, kidney sections were stained for macrophage and lymphocyte markers observed in SCD kidney injury and we assessed whether BGJ398 affected their expression. F4/80, a pan macrophage marker, showed increased staining in SCD-Vehicle treated males (Fig. 6A) and females (Fig. 6B) (Supplemental Fig. 6 for lower magnification of both sexes). Quantitation showed increased F4/80-positive macrophages that was not significantly reduced by BGJ398 in male (Fig. 6C) or female (Fig. 6D). However, iNOS staining and quantitation for pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages showed considerable increase in M1 macrophages that was significantly decreased by BGJ398 in male (Fig. 6E and F) and female (Fig. 6G and H) (Supplemental Fig. 6 for lower magnification of both sexes). Discussion

To investigate the role of aberrant FGFR signaling and OPN in SCD in phosphaturia and kidney abnormalities in SCD, we used the Townes SCD mice that phenocopy human SCD [16]. We measured serum ferritin a marker of iron overload in SCD subjects [25,26] that can also be increased in inflammatory states [24,27]. Serum ferritin was significantly higher in SCD mice but was not reduced with short term BGJ398 treatment. We also measured serum erythropoietin (EPO) since EPO, along with iron deficiency and inflammation, was reported to increase FGF23 production and metabolism [28]. We observed that there was a significant increase in EPO in serum of SCD mice, which is an expected finding in states of anemia to facilitate increased red blood cell production in the bone marrow. Although FGFR inhibition significantly reduced EPO expression in serum, there was no significant reduction in either the significantly elevated serum iFGF23 or cFGF23 suggesting that EPO elevation did not contribute to the increased FGF23 levels in SCD mice.

With age, SCD results in progressive end-organ damage including the kidneys [1]. We evaluated renal function in SCD mice and found a significant increase in BUN in SCD-Vehicle treated male and female mice versus Ctrl mice that was not altered with BGJ398 treatment in SCD male mice but was significantly decreased with BGJ398 in SCD females. The reason for the sex difference in response to BGJ398 is unclear. Chronic Kidney disease is also associated with significantly increased serum creatinine. However, creatinine was not increased but rather was significantly decreased in both male and female SCD-Vehicle treated mice and most likely reflects skeletal muscle wasting often observed in SCD [1]. Interestingly, urine albumin was not increased in SCD mice suggesting that these SCD mice do not develop podocyte injury. Thus, the increased BUN in the SCD-Vehicle mice may be due to mild dehydration. However, this study demonstrates early onset kidney dysfunction in SCD mice because we detected phosphaturia in these mice that was alleviated by BGJ398.

Phosphaturia in SCD-vehicle group was associated with significantly decreased expression of phosphate transporters NPT2A and PIT2 necessary for phosphate reabsorption supporting their role in kidney phosphate reabsorption since the urine phosphate excretion was significantly increased. The observation that BG398 significantly increased NPT2A and PIT2 protein expression in the SCD kidneys are novel and significant findings.

Phosphate homeostasis in kidneys reflects FGF23/FGFR/αklotho modulation of multiple downstream signaling pathways [26,[29], [30], [31], [32]]. Therefore, we determined the effect of BGJ398 FGFR blockade on FGF23/FGFR/klotho and downstream signaling in kidneys from Ctrl versus SCD mice. As previously noted, our data shows that BGJ398 did not alter iFGF23 or cFGF23 expression in serum. However, the substantially increased immunostaining for activated FGFR1 and αklotho in renal proximal and distal tubules of Vehicle-treated SCD mice which was significantly reduced by BGJ398, is consistent with their important roles as co-receptors for FGF23-induced activation of several downstream pathways in the kidney. Our observations support earlier reports showing that FGFR/αKlotho-driven activation of MAPK/SGK1 signaling plays a key role in the regulation of NPT2a-mediated phosphate reabsorption in the kidney [31,33,34], which was blocked by BGJ398 inhibition of FGFR.

Phosphaturia, was accompanied by structural evidence of SCD kidney damage as evidenced by increased inflammatory macrophages and lymphocytes, sclerotic glomeruli and the damaged tubules. Short term BGJ398 treatment did not alleviate the increased tubular and glomerular abnormalities detected in SCD kidneys. The limited effect may be attributable to the brief 24-h FGFR inhibitor treatment in SCD mice, which constitutes a limitation of the study. However, we detected a significant decrease in early kidney damage marker KIM1 as well as inflammatory M1 macrophages and inflammatory proteins TNFa and IL-6. These findings suggest that FGFR inhibition exerts rapid anti-inflammatory effects that may precede detectable structural improvement in SCD mice.

Importantly, this study focused on the acute effects of a single dose of BGJ398 to delineate FGFR1-dependent mechanisms while minimizing confounding factors such as compensatory adaptations or systemic toxicity that may arise with prolonged treatment. A key limitation, therefore, is the lack of chronic dosing data. It remains unclear whether the beneficial effects observed here would be sustained, attenuated, or further enhanced with long-term FGFR inhibition. Ongoing studies in our laboratory are addressing this question using repeated dosing regimens to evaluate durability of response and safety.

Besides hypoxia and elevated iron deposition, which can induce inflammation in SCD kidneys [1], as reported, FGF receptors can activate JAKs, which phosphorylates STATs activate the promoters of inflammatory cytokines in the nucleus [11]. Thus, our observation that the expression of pAKT, pJAK3, and pSTAT3 was significantly increased in SCD-Vehicle treated kidneys is significant. Previous studies supported a role for JAK3 signaling in FGF23-depenedent regulation of phosphate homeostasis [35]. However as reported independent of FGF23, FGF receptors are also known to activate AKT, JAK/STAT signaling [36]. Interestingly BGJ398 not only reduced significantly increased kidney expression of inflammatory cytokines IL6 and TNFα, but also significantly reduced pJAK3 and pSTAT3 that were significantly increased in SCD kidney.

The abnormal SCD kidney morphology observed in our study could be due in part to increased OPN which is essential in multiple biological processes and has been linked to the development of diverse kidney diseases where increased OPN has been reported in tubules and glomeruli of damaged kidneys associated with activated macrophages and T-Cells [15]. Our study detected a significant increase in OPN accumulation in the SCD-vehicle treated kidney associated with increased integrin αVβ3 through which OPN is known to signal, and BGJ398 significantly reduced OPN accumulation as well as integrin αVβ3 expression in the SCD kidneys. Thus, the ability of BGJ398 to reduce inflammatory cells in SCD kidney may be due to reduce OPN expression and integrin signaling in SCD BGJ398 treated kidney.

In addition to OPN, FGF23 promotes anemia and regulates inflammatory responses [28]. Its expression is tightly regulated at both transcriptional and translational levels by iron, cytokines, oxidative stress, and inflammation [37]. Thus, although higher level of FGF23 is associated with inflammation, in this study, treatment with FGF receptor via which FGF23 signals did not modulate FGF23 but effectively blocked FGF23 multiple downstream signaling pathways thereby significantly reducing some components of inflammation in the SCD kidneys.

In summary, short-term FGF receptor blockade rescued impaired multiple downstream pathways important in FGF23 signaling in SCD kidneys. It also significantly reduced OPN and partially rescued the abnormal kidney injury markers in SCD male and female mice by significantly reducing inflammatory macrophages and inflammatory cytokines. While structural kidney changes were not reversed within the short treatment window, early injury markers were significantly improved. Furthermore, multiple overlapping signaling pathways contribute to phosphate wasting and abnormal kidney morphology in SCD mice. These novel findings suggest that blocking aberrant FGFR signaling including increased integrin αVβ3 expression in SCD kidney with BGJ398 might provide a novel option for preventing early kidney pathology in SCD mice (Fig. 8). Future studies evaluating chronic FGFR inhibition will be essential to determine the long-term efficacy, durability, and safety of this approach.

Schematic illustration of signaling pathways involved in BGJ398-mediated improvement in dysregulated phosphate metabolism in the kidneys of SCD mice. In SCD, anemia stimulates the kidneys to secrete erythropoietin (EPO), which travels to bone and triggers fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23) production. High level of FGF23 activate FGFR1/αKlotho signaling in the kidneys, leading to phosphaturia. Increased FGFR signaling in the kidneys also promotes inflammation. Inhibition of FGFR signaling with BGJ398 alleviates phosphate wasting and improves renal phosphate handling in SCD mice.
