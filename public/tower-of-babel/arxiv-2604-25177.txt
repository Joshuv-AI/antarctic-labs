1.
Introduction

A common question in number theory is this: given an arithmetic function
f
and an integer
a\neq 0
, what is the largest
\delta>0
for which
Q\leq X^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
implies that

\sum_{q\sim Q}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\sim X\\
n\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\sim X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\right|\ll\frac{X}{\log^{A}X}

for some (or possibly for every)
A>0
?

Often - such as in the case of the primes - such questions can be expressed in terms of convolutions. For
m\sim M
and
n\sim N
with
MN=X
, define
\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{n}
to be sequences of complex numbers. We then wish to find a
\delta
such that for
Q\leq X^{1/2+\delta}
,

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\left|\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\sim N,m\sim M\\
mn\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}-\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\sim N,m\sim M\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}\right|\ll\frac{X}{\log^{A}X}.

The cases where
\delta<0
follow from the generalized version of the Bombieri-Vinogradov Theorem. Cases where
\delta>0
, however, are often much more difficult. For instance, in the case of the primes, this is the Elliot-Halberstam conjecture, and thus at present there is no fixed
\delta>0
for which (
1
) can be shown to hold if
\alpha_{m}=\mu(m)
and
\beta_{n}=\log n
.

In order for it to be possible for (
1
) to hold, it is generally required that at least one of the two functions is equidistributed for small moduli. We call such equidistribution the
Siegel-Walfisz condition
, defined as follows:

Definition 1
.

An arithmetic function
\beta_{n}
is defined to be Siegel-Walfisz if there exists
an integer
k>0
such that for any fixed
A>0
, uniformly in
x\geq 2
,
q>|a|\geq 1
,
r\geq 1
, and
(a,q)=1
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq 2x\\
n\equiv a\pmod{q}\\
(n,r)=1\end{subarray}}\beta_{n}=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq 2x\\
(n,qr)=1\end{subarray}}\beta_{n}+O\left(x(\log x)^{-A}\tau_{k}(r)\right).

Here,
\tau_{k}
denotes the
k
-fold divisor function.

In 2018, Fouvry and Radziwill showed that (
1
) holds for small positive values of
\delta>0
as long as
N
is fairly small relative to
M
. In particular, Corollary 1.1 of
[
FR
]
was the following:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
k>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be given. Let

\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{n}
be two sequences of complex numbers such that

|\alpha_{m}|\leq\tau_{k}(m)\quad\text{and}\quad|\beta_{n}|\leq\tau_{k}(n)

for every
m,n>1
. Suppose that
\beta_{n}
satisfies the Siegel-Walfisz condition.
Then for every
A>0
, uniformly for
M,N\geq 2
with

\frac{MN}{2}\leq X\leq 4MN,

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\leq q\leq 2Q\\
(q,a)=1\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<mn\leq 2X\\
mn\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<mn\leq 2X\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}\right|\ll X(\log X)^{-A},

provided that one of the following three conditions holds:

(i)

\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon})\leq N\leq Q^{-11/12}X^{17/36-\varepsilon}

and
1\leq|a|\leq X/12
;

(ii)

\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon})\leq N\leq X^{7/90-\varepsilon}
,

Q\leq X^{53/105-\varepsilon}
,
and
1\leq|a|\leq X/12
;

(iii)

\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon})\leq N\leq X^{101/630-\varepsilon}
,

Q\leq X^{53/105-\varepsilon}
,
and
1\leq|a|\leq(X/4)^{\varepsilon/1000}
.

A key step in this result involves a bound for trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions discovered by Bettin and Chandee
[
BC
]
. Define

\mathcal{B}(M,N,A)=\mathop{\sum\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\sim A,m\sim M,n\sim N\\
(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}\nu_{a}e\left(\vartheta\frac{a\overline{m}}{n}\right),

where
\vartheta
is a nonzero integer.

In
[
BC
]
, the authors then prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(M,N,A)\ll

\displaystyle||\alpha||||\beta||||\nu||\left(1+\frac{|\vartheta|A}{MN}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left((AMN)^{\frac{7}{20}+\varepsilon}(M+N)^{\frac{1}{4}}+(AMN)^{\frac{3}{8}+\varepsilon}(AN+AM)^{\frac{1}{8}}\right).

2.
New Ideas

In this paper, we alter the Bettin-Chandee result to make it slightly more helpful for the Fouvry-Radziwill result.

We recall that the
[
FR
]
work considers functions
g(m)
and
h(n)
with
m\sim M
and
n\sim N
where
MN
is roughy of size
X
but
\exp((\log X)^{\theta})\leq N\leq X^{1/72-\epsilon}
(i.e.
N
is much smaller than
M
). When the authors of
[
FR
]
then apply the result of
[
BC
]
, they have a sum of the form

(2)

\displaystyle\mathop{\sum\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\sim A,b\sim Q,q\sim Q\\
(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}f_{1}(b)f_{2}(q)f_{3}(a)e\left(\vartheta\frac{a\overline{b}}{Rq}\right)

for a parameter
R\leq 2N
. Translating their work into the language of Bettin and Chandee, they then define their function
\alpha_{n}
to be such that
n
lies on intervals of the form
[RQ,2RQ]
with the caveat that
\alpha_{n}=0
if
R\nmid n
. This allows them to apply Theorem
1.2
, but at the slight cost of having to replace
Q
with
NQ
in the bound.

However, the original proof of Bettin and Chandee (and the preceding work of Duke, Friedlander, and Iwaniec) already has a useful mechanism that one could exploit find a better bound for something like (
2
). In
[
BC
]
, the authors define

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(M,N,A)=\sum_{m\sim M}\left|\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\sim A,n\sim N\\
(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}\beta_{n}\nu_{a}e\left(\vartheta\frac{a\overline{m}}{n}\right)\right|^{2}

and then introduce the so-called ”complementary divisor”
b
, considering

\mathcal{C}_{b}(M,N,A;\beta,\nu)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\left|\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{A},n\in\mathcal{N}\\
(mb,n)=1\\
n\mbox{ }square-free\end{subarray}}\beta_{n}\nu_{a}e\left(\theta\frac{a\overline{m}}{nb}\right)\right|^{2}.

The authors then relate
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{b}
back to
\mathcal{B}
, bounding the latter via estimates on
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{b}
as well as Cauchy-Schwarz.

If one were to consider instead

\sum_{m\sim M}\left|\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\sim A,n\sim N\\
(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}\beta_{n}\nu_{a}e\left(\vartheta\frac{a\overline{m}}{Rn}\right)\right|^{2}

and

\sum_{m\sim M}\left|\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\sim A,n\sim N\\
(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}\beta_{n}\nu_{a}e\left(\vartheta\frac{a\overline{m}}{bRn}\right)\right|^{2},

the sum now already has a built-in complementary divisor in
R
, and hence one could use the same process the authors used to bound
\mathcal{C}_{b}
to find bounds for these expressions as well. Applying this process and Cauchy-Schwarz, we can then give a bound for

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(M,N,A;R):=\mathop{\sum\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{A},m\in\mathcal{M},n\in\mathcal{N}\\
(m,nR)=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}\nu_{a}e\left(\theta\frac{a\overline{m}}{nR}\right)

in the same way as before. This is the mechanism in the Bettin-Chandee proof that we use to find improvements on the Fouvry-Radziwill bound.

In particular, we will prove the following version of Bettin and Chandee’s result:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M\ll N^{2}
and
R\ll M^{A}
for some large
A
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(M,N,A;R)\ll

\displaystyle M^{\epsilon}||\alpha||||\nu||||\beta||(AMN)^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(1+\frac{|\vartheta|A}{MN}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{8}}}+\frac{R^{\frac{1}{8}}N^{\frac{1}{8}}}{M^{\frac{1}{4}}}+\frac{M^{\frac{1}{10}}}{R^{\frac{3}{20}}A^{\frac{1}{20}}N^{\frac{3}{20}}}+\frac{N^{\frac{3}{20}}}{A^{\frac{3}{20}}M^{\frac{1}{5}}}+\frac{N^{\frac{3}{8}}}{M^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

When
R=1
, this is (7.2) of
[
BC
]
. However, it is clear that when
R
is much larger than 1, this theorem will yield a smaller bound than would simply substituting
NR
for
N
in Theorem
1.2
.

Armed with this theorem, we record the following improvement of Corollary
1.1
:

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
k>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be given. Let

\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{n}
be
k
-fold divisor-bounded sequences of complex numbers as in Corollary
1.1
with
\beta_{n}
satisfying the Siegel-Walfisz condition. Then for every
A>0
, uniformly for
M,N\geq 2
with

\frac{MN}{2}\leq X\leq 4MN,

we have

(4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\leq q\leq 2Q\\
(q,a)=1\end{subarray}}\left|\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<mn\leq 2X\\
mn\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<mn\leq 2X\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}\right|\ll_{A}x(\log X)^{-A},

provided that one of the following three conditions holds:

(i)

\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon})\leq N\leq Q^{-33/28}X^{17/28-\varepsilon}

and
1\leq|a|\leq X/12
;

(ii)

\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon})\leq N\leq X^{7/90-\varepsilon}
,

Q\leq X^{45/89-\varepsilon}
,
and
1\leq|a|\leq X/12
;

(iii)

\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon})\leq N\leq X^{101/630-\varepsilon}
,

Q\leq X^{45/89-\varepsilon}
,
and
1\leq|a|\leq(X/4)^{\varepsilon/1000}
.

Note that in the extremal case where
N
is very small, the bound in (i) gives
Q\leq X^{1/2+1/66-\epsilon}
, which is the same as in
[
FR
]
. This is of course because the savings are entirely dependent on the size of
N
, and thus if
N
is very small we have no appreciable savings. On the other hand, if
Q=X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
then (i) of our corollary gives
N\leq X^{1/56-\epsilon^{\prime}}
for some
\epsilon^{\prime}
depending on
\epsilon
, which is an improvement on the
N\leq X^{1/72-\epsilon^{\prime}}
from Corollary
1.1
.

In their paper, Fouvry and Radziwill state Corollary
1.1
as the consequence of an estimate involving Linnik’s dispersion. Define

E(\alpha,\beta,M,N,q,a):=\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\sim M\\
n\sim N\\
mn\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\sim M\\
n\sim N\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n},

and define

\Delta(\alpha,\beta,M,N,Q,a):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\\
(q,a)=1\end{subarray}}\left|E(\alpha,\beta,M,N,q,a)\right|.

For the contribution of the small moduli to
\Delta(\alpha,\beta,M,N,Q,a)
, we have

E^{\ast}(\beta,N,Q):=\sum_{\delta}\sum_{v\sim Q/\delta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{\prime}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\delta)\\
(\delta^{\prime},\delta)=1\end{subarray}}\left|E^{\ast}(\beta,N,\delta,\delta^{\prime};v)\right|^{2}.

Note that if
\beta
is Siegel-Walfisz and satisfies

|\beta_{n}|\leq\tau_{k}(n)
for some
k>0
, and if
N>Q^{\varepsilon}
, then

E^{\ast}(\beta,N,Q)\ll_{A}N^{2}Q(\log N)^{-A}.

In this paper, we will prove the following:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
k
,
\varepsilon
,
\alpha_{m}
, and
\beta_{n}
be as in the previous corollary.
Let
k\geq 1
be an integer and let
\varepsilon>0
be given.

M>Q(MN)^{\varepsilon},\qquad M>N>D^{10}.

Then, for all integers

, we have

\displaystyle|\Delta(\alpha,\beta,M,N,Q,a)|

\displaystyle\ll||\alpha||\left(MQ^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{*}(\beta,N,Q)+(\log X)^{\kappa}N^{2}Q+(\log X)^{\kappa}N^{2}D^{-\frac{1}{2}}M+D^{C}X^{\epsilon}\left(Q^{\frac{15}{8}}N^{\frac{11}{4}}+M^{\frac{3}{20}}Q^{\frac{33}{20}}N^{\frac{51}{20}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In
[
FR
]
, the last term is

D^{C}X^{\epsilon}\left(Q^{\frac{15}{8}}N^{\frac{23}{8}}+M^{\frac{3}{20}}Q^{\frac{33}{20}}N^{\frac{59}{20}}\right).

Hence we have a savings of

in the first term and
N^{\frac{2}{5}}
in the second.

We note that Theorem

also gives an improvement of
[

Let

and
