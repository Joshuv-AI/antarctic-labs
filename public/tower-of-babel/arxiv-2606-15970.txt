Introduction

1.1
Determinantal complexity and the quadratic frontier

The
determinantal complexity

\mathrm{dc}(f)
of a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
is the least
m
such that

f(x)\;=\;\det M(x),\qquad M(x)=A_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}A_{i},\quad A_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}.

Valiant’s program
[
Val79
]
makes the growth of
\mathrm{dc}(\mathrm{per}_{n})
the central question of algebraic complexity (in this paragraph only,
n
is the size of the permanent matrix):
\mathrm{dc}(\mathrm{per}_{n})=n^{\omega(\log n)}
separates
\mathrm{VP}_{ws}
from
\mathrm{VNP}
. The known lower bounds tell a striking story of saturation at the quadratic scale. Mignon and Ressayre
[
MR04
]
proved
\mathrm{dc}(\mathrm{per}_{n})\geq n^{2}/2
via the rank of the Hessian; Cai, Chen and Li
[
CCL10
]
extended this to all characteristics
\neq 2
; Landsberg, Manivel and Ressayre
[
LMR13
]
recovered quadratic-type bounds from the geometry of dual varieties, and Alper, Bogart and Velasco
[
ABV17
]
obtained linear bounds from the codimension of the singular locus, in each case with an analysis suggesting — but not proving — that the technique in question cannot go further. On the upper side, Grenet’s construction
[
Gre11
]
gives
\mathrm{dc}(\mathrm{per}_{n})\leq 2^{n}-1
. The gap between
n^{2}/2
and
2^{n}
has not moved in two decades.

The companion paper
[
She26a
]
introduces a
polar method
: a determinantal representation of size
m
of
f
induces a corank-one kernel incidence in
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
, the top polar degree
\delta_{\mathrm{top}}
of the hypersurface
X=V(f)
is read off as a count of solutions of that incidence, and a multihomogeneous Bézout estimate converts the count into the lower bound

\mathrm{dc}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{N}\Bigr)\;\geq\;\Bigl(\frac{1}{4e}-o(1)\Bigr)N^{2},

(1)

together with a stronger border variant. (The companion papers write this bound with
n
for the number of variables; in this paper
n:=N-1
is reserved throughout for the dimension of the ambient projective space, so we restate the bound in
N
and refer to
d=N
as the
power-sum diagonal
.) The bound (
1
) again sits at the quadratic scale. The purpose of the present paper is to explain why, with proofs rather than heuristics: we show that the quadratic scale is not an artifact of the specific invariant (
\delta_{\mathrm{top}}
), the specific family (the Fermat), or the specific estimate (Bézout), but is the
exact reach
of the entire class of methods that read characteristic-cycle data of
V(f)
through kernel incidences of the representation — in the precise sense fixed by Definitions
6.1
and
6.5
: invariants of characteristic-cycle type, injected into solution counts of the kernel incidence, with the counts bounded by intersection theory. Remark
6.14
records exactly what falls outside this sense.

Such “limits of the method” theorems have precedent and value. The dual-variety analysis of
[
LMR13
]
and the singular-locus analysis of
[
ABV17
]
each identify a wall for one invariant. The theorem proved here subsumes both (Section
7
): the dimension of the dual variety and the codimension of the singular locus are coarse shadows of the full characteristic cycle, and the wall is the same wall, now established for the finest invariant of the conormal type and for every reading mechanism of the kernel-incidence type in the sense of Definition
6.5
, with matching constants from both sides.

1.2
The two halves of the argument

The no-go theorem couples two quantitative statements that are of independent interest.

The reading cost is exact (Fork B, Section
4
).
Let
M(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}A_{i}
be a generic
N
-dimensional linear space of
m\times m
matrices, and let

Z_{1}=\{(x,v,u)\in\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}\;:\;M(x)v=0,\ u^{\mathsf{T}}M(x)=0\}

be the corank-one kernel incidence. The first structural observation (Lemma
3.3
) is that
Z_{1}

is
the conormal variety of the determinant hypersurface: the assignment
(x,v,u)\mapsto(x,[u^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bullet}v])
identifies
Z_{1}
birationally with
\mathrm{Con}(V(\det M))
, because the differential of the determinant at a corank-one matrix is the rank-one form
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{adj}(M)\,dM)\propto u^{\mathsf{T}}(dM)v
. The reading therefore counts honest conormal geometry. We compute its class exactly. The system
u^{\mathsf{T}}M(x)=0
on
\{M(x)v=0\}
carries one syzygy (
u^{\mathsf{T}}(Mv)\equiv 0
), so
Z_{1}
is a degeneracy locus of excess one; the correct class is the top Chern class of an explicit kernel bundle, and the resulting polar count

T(N,m)\;=\;\bigl[h^{N-1}a^{m-1}b^{m-1}\bigr]\ \frac{(h+a)^{m}(h+b)^{m}(a+b)^{N-2}}{h+a+b}

admits a double-binomial closed form (Theorem
4.2
). Three classical anchors confirm it on the nose:
T(3,m)=m(m-1)
and
T(4,m)=m(m-1)^{2}
are the dual degrees of the (smooth) generic determinantal plane curve and surface, and
T(5,5)=220
is the dual degree of the generic determinantal quintic threefold, equal to the smooth value
5\cdot 4^{3}=320
minus the Teissier drop
\mu+\mu^{(1)}=2
at each of its
50
ordinary nodes (a Thom–Porteous count) (Proposition
4.3
). The Bézout estimate of the companion papers is then compared exactly: the count-level ratio is
T/B_{\mathrm{bez}}\to C_{N}=\frac{N-1}{2N-3}\to\frac{1}{2}
(exactly
\tfrac{2}{3}
for every
m
at
N=3
), while the per-root cost — the only quantity the lower-bound pipeline ever uses — is unchanged:
T(N,m)^{1/(N-1)}=(4e+o(1))\,m/N
(Theorem
4.7
). Finally, a Bertini argument shows the generic determinantal hypersurface
attains

T(N,m)
as an honest transverse count (Proposition
4.4
), so the reading cost is pinned from both sides: no refinement of the intersection theory can lower it by more than a constant that the
(N{-}1)
-st root annihilates.

The invariants are capped (Fork A, Section
5
).
One might hope to escape the quadratic wall by reading a
finer
invariant than
\delta_{\mathrm{top}}
: the full multidegree profile of the characteristic cycle
\mathrm{CC}(\mathbf{1}_{X})
, stratum by stratum, distinguishes (for example) the three-nodal from the two-cuspidal plane curve of the same degree and the same
\delta_{\mathrm{top}}
, and one might engineer singular families whose stratum data grows super-quadratically after root extraction. We prove this is impossible:
fineness was never the binding constraint; magnitude is.
For an arbitrary degree-
d
hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
,

m_{S}\,\delta_{i}(\mathrm{Con}\bar{S})\;\leq\;8(d-1)^{N-1}+8N\qquad\text{for every stratum $S$ and every multidegree slot $i$}

(Theorem
5.7
), with no hypothesis whatsoever on the singularities. The proof chain is short and each link is independently checkable. (1)
Envelope:
the global Milnor fibre
F=\{f=1\}
is smooth affine for any homogeneous
f
and is a
d
-sheeted cover of
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\setminus X
; Morse theory with a generic linear pencil and Fulton’s refined Bézout theorem give
|\chi(X\cap L_{k})|\leq 2(d-1)^{k}+2k+2
for generic linear sections of every dimension
k
, uniformly over all singularity structures (Proposition
5.4
). (2)
Transform:
the multidegrees of
\mathrm{CC}(\varphi)
are recovered from the generic-slice Euler characteristics by the signed second difference

\Delta_{i}(\varphi)=(-1)^{i}\bigl(\chi_{n-i}-2\chi_{n-i-1}+\chi_{n-i-2}\bigr),\qquad n=N-1,

an operator of
\ell^{1}
-row-mass exactly
4
, independent of
N
(Theorem
5.5
); the exponential coefficient mass one might fear from Chern-class conversions is an artifact of routing through the CSM basis and disappears when the slice data talks to the multidegrees directly. (3)
Positivity:
a reduced hypersurface is a local complete intersection, so
\mathbb{Q}_{X}[\dim X]
is perverse and Kashiwara positivity makes
(-1)^{\dim X}\mathrm{CC}(\mathbf{1}_{X})
effective — no large component can hide inside small totals by cancellation. The same ceiling shape holds for the nearby- and vanishing-cycle variants
\mathrm{CC}(\varphi_{f})
and for Milnor classes, where exactly one constant, in the deeply non-isolated non-reduced case, rests on a Lê-number envelope of Massey flagged in Remark
5.9
; this affects no exponent anywhere.

1.3
Main theorems

Write
n=N-1
for the dimension of the ambient projective space,
d=\deg f
, and let
X=V(f)\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Theorem A

(Exact reading cost; Theorems
4.1
,
4.2
, Propositions
4.3
,
4.4
)
.

For generic
M
, the incidence
Z_{1}
is the conormal variety of
V(\det M)
, its class is

[Z_{1}]=(h+a)^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}a^{k}(h+b)^{m-1-k}
,
and the polar count
T(N,m)=\int_{Z_{1}}(a+b)^{N-2}
has the closed forms of Theorem
4.2
. The generic determinantal hypersurface attains
T(N,m)
as a transverse, reduced count.

Theorem B

(Per-root exactness; Theorem
4.7
)
.

At fixed
N
,
\ T(N,m)/B_{\mathrm{bez}}(N,m)\to\frac{N-1}{2N-3}
as
m\to\infty
; for
N\to\infty
,
m/N\to\infty
,

T(N,m)^{1/(N-1)}\;=\;(4e+o(1))\,\frac{m}{N}\;=\;(1+o(1))\,B_{\mathrm{bez}}(N,m)^{1/(N-1)}.

Bézout was loose by an explicit constant and tight in the exponent.

Theorem C

(Invariant ceiling; Theorem
5.7
)
.

Let
f
be any nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
in
N
variables and
X=V(f)_{\mathrm{red}}
. Write
\mathrm{CC}(\mathbf{1}_{X})=(-1)^{\dim X}\sum_{S}m_{S}[\mathrm{Con}\bar{S}]
with
m_{S}\geq 0
. Then for every
S
and every multidegree slot
i
,

m_{S}\,\delta_{i}(\mathrm{Con}\bar{S})\;\leq\;8(d-1)^{N-1}+8N,

hence
\bigl(m_{S}\,\delta_{i}\bigr)^{1/(N-1)}\leq(d-1)\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)
uniformly in
d
. The analogous ceiling holds for
\mathrm{CC}(\varphi_{f})
, Chern–Mather, CSM and Milnor classes, with the single conditional constant of Remark
5.9
.

Theorem D

(No-go; Theorem
6.11
)
.

Every lower bound on
\mathrm{dc}(f)
derivable by reading a characteristic-cycle-type invariant of
V(f)
(Definition
6.1
) through a kernel-corank incidence with intersection-theoretic extraction (Definition
6.5
) satisfies

\text{derivable bound}\;\leq\;e^{O(1)}\cdot d\,N.

Corank one is unconditional; corank
\geq 2
is established under Hypothesis
4.10
. On the diagonal
d=N
this is
O(N^{2})=O(d^{2})
; combined with (
1
), the exact reach of the method is
\Theta(N^{2})
, with constant in
[\tfrac{1}{4e},\,e^{O(1)}]
.

The slogan form of Theorem D:
the topology of a degree-
d
hypersurface is exponentially bounded by the degree of its equation, uniformly in its singularities, and the
(N{-}1)
-st root that every kernel-incidence reading must take converts that exponential into
d
per root.
A finer invariant carries more information but not more magnitude; the smooth Fermat is, within
e^{O(N)}
and hence exactly at the per-root level, the extremal family for the entire engine.

1.4
Relation to known barriers

Section
7
carries out the comparisons in detail; the summary is as follows. The top polar degree
\delta_{\mathrm{top}}
, the invariant of the companion papers, is strictly coarser than the full profile
\{m_{S}\delta_{i}\}
— plane curves with three nodes versus two cusps witness this — yet Theorem C shows the refinement buys no magnitude. The dual-variety dimension of
[
LMR13
]
is the index of the top nonvanishing main-term slot, a coarsening of the profile; their quadratic cap and ours are the same wall seen from two sides. The codimension of the singular locus of
[
ABV17
]
is the codimension of the largest stratum carrying mass, again a coarse shadow. Shifted-partial and other rank-based circuit measures are genuinely incomparable with conormal data (neither determines the other), so they sit outside the scope of Theorem D — but they are known to cap at the near-quadratic scale for determinantal questions independently.

1.5
What remains open

Two exits are left genuinely open by Theorem D (they are formalized in Remark
6.14
), and the proof shows precisely why. First,
scheme-theoretic conormal data
: the Hilbert function and Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of the dual variety
X^{\vee}
, as a scheme, are strictly finer than any cycle-class datum, and no mechanism currently bounds them in terms of
m
in either direction. Second,
non-conormal reading mechanisms
: any way of extracting an inequality from a determinantal representation that does not factor through intersection numbers of conormal-type cycles is untouched. Within the conormal geometry itself, the proof closes the last interior door: the only candidate slack was the gap between the Bézout estimate and the true class, and Theorem B shows that gap to be a factor of
2
at the count level and
1+o(1)
per root.

2
Conventions and background

2.1
Conormal varieties and multidegrees

We work over
\mathbb{C}
. Fix
N\geq 3
and write
n=N-1
,
\mathbb{P}^{n}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
, with dual space
\check{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
parametrizing hyperplanes. Let
h\in A^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
and
\check{h}\in A^{1}(\check{\mathbb{P}}^{n})
denote the hyperplane classes; on a product we keep the same letters for their pullbacks.

For an irreducible closed subvariety
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the
conormal variety
is

\mathrm{Con}Z\;=\;\overline{\{(z,H)\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\check{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\;:\;z\in Z_{\mathrm{sm}},\ T_{z}Z\subseteq H\}},

an irreducible variety of dimension
n-1
for every
Z
(it is Lagrangian for the natural contact structure). The
dual variety

Z^{\vee}\subseteq\check{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is the image of the second projection. In characteristic zero, biduality holds: the involution
(z,H)\mapsto(H,z)
carries
\mathrm{Con}Z
to
\mathrm{Con}Z^{\vee}

[
GKZ94
,
Tev05
]
.

For an
(n-1)
-dimensional cycle
C
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\check{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, its
multidegrees
are

\delta_{i}(C)\;=\;\int_{C}h^{i}\,\check{h}^{\,n-1-i},\qquad i=0,\dots,n-1.

When
C=\mathrm{Con}X
for a hypersurface
X
, these are the classical
polar degrees
(ranks) of
X

[
Pie78
]
:
\delta_{n-1}(\mathrm{Con}X)=\deg X
, and
\delta_{0}(\mathrm{Con}X)=\deg X^{\vee}
whenever
X^{\vee}
is a hypersurface. For
X
smooth of degree
d
one has
\delta_{i}(\mathrm{Con}X)=d(d-1)^{n-1-i}
. We write
\delta_{\mathrm{top}}:=\delta_{0}
. Multidegrees of effective cycles are nonnegative, since
h
and
\check{h}
are globally generated.

2.2
Constructible functions and characteristic cycles

A constructible function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a finite sum
\sum_{W}c_{W}\mathbf{1}_{W}
,
c_{W}\in\mathbb{Z}
, over closed subvarieties. For a constructible
\varphi
and a constructible subset
A
we write
\int_{A}\varphi\,d\chi
for the Euler-characteristic integral. For
0\leq k\leq n
define the
generic slice values

\chi_{k}(\varphi)\;:=\;\int_{L_{k}}\varphi\,d\chi,\qquad L_{k}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}\ \text{a generic linear subspace of dimension }k,

which is well defined by genericity (Kleiman transversality); we set
\chi_{-1}:=0
and
\chi_{-2}:=0
. For
\varphi=\mathbf{1}_{X}
,
\chi_{k}(\varphi)=\chi(X\cap L_{k})
.

MacPherson’s local Euler obstruction
\mathrm{Eu}_{W}

[
Mac74
]
is the constructible function supported on
W
with
\mathrm{Eu}_{W}\equiv 1
on
W_{\mathrm{sm}}
; the functions
\{\mathrm{Eu}_{W}\}
form a basis of the group of constructible functions. The
characteristic cycle
is the unique linear map
\varphi\mapsto\mathrm{CC}(\varphi)
from constructible functions to Lagrangian cycles in
T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n}
normalized by

\mathrm{CC}(\mathrm{Eu}_{W})\;=\;(-1)^{\dim W}\,[\,\overline{T^{*}_{W}\mathbb{P}^{n}}\,],

where
\overline{T^{*}_{W}\mathbb{P}^{n}}
is the closure of the conormal bundle of
W_{\mathrm{sm}}
; under projectivization,
[\overline{T^{*}_{W}\mathbb{P}^{n}}]
corresponds to
[\mathrm{Con}W]
for
W\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and to the zero section for
W=\mathbb{P}^{n}
. This is the normalization under which
\mathrm{CC}
of a constructible complex equals
\mathrm{CC}
of its stalkwise Euler characteristic and Kashiwara–Dubson index theory applies
[
Kas73
,
BDK81
,
KS90
]
. Writing

\mathrm{CC}(\varphi)\;=\;n_{0}(\varphi)\,[\text{zero section}]\;+\;\sum_{W\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{n}}n_{W}(\varphi)\,[\mathrm{Con}W],

we package the proper part into the
multidegree vector

\Delta_{i}(\varphi)\;:=\;\sum_{W\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{n}}n_{W}(\varphi)\,\delta_{i}(\mathrm{Con}W),\qquad i=0,\dots,n-1.

Two standard facts are used repeatedly. First, the
index theorem
: Euler characteristics of constructible complexes are computed by intersection of characteristic cycles
[
Kas73
,
BDK81
,
Dub84
,
KS90
]
; in the projective-slice form we need, for generic
L_{k}
the value
\chi_{k}(\varphi)
is a universal
\mathbb{Z}
-linear functional of
\bigl(n_{0}(\varphi),\Delta_{0}(\varphi),\dots,\Delta_{n-1}(\varphi)\bigr)
, with coefficients depending only on
(n,k,i)
— this is the topological Radon / projective duality transform of Ernström and Matsui–Takeuchi
[
Ern94
,
MT07
]
. Second,
Kashiwara positivity
: if
F^{\bullet}
is a perverse sheaf,
\mathrm{CC}(F^{\bullet})
is an effective Lagrangian cycle
[
Kas73
,
KS90
,
Gin86
]
. We use it through the following standard consequence: a reduced hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of pure dimension
n-1
is a local complete intersection, hence
\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n-1]
is perverse
[
Dim04
]
, its stalkwise Euler characteristic is
(-1)^{n-1}\mathbf{1}_{X}
, and therefore

(-1)^{n-1}\,\mathrm{CC}(\mathbf{1}_{X})\;=\;\sum_{S}m_{S}\,[\mathrm{Con}\bar{S}],\qquad m_{S}\geq 0,

(2)

a nonnegative combination over the closures of the strata of a Whitney stratification of
X
, with
m_{X}=1
on the open stratum. No zero-section term appears since
\mathbf{1}_{X}
vanishes at a generic point.

2.3
Nearby cycles and Milnor data

For a nonzero homogeneous
f
of degree
d
, the hypersurface

carries the constructible function

\nu_{f}(x)\;:=\;\chi\bigl(F_{f,x}\bigr),\qquad F_{f,x}\ \text{the Milnor fibre at $x$ of a local equation of $f$},

g(x)=0

g

\nu_{f}(x)=e

\psi_{f}(\mathbb{Q}[n])

\mathbb{Z}
-combinations of the data

attached to
\mathrm{CC}(\mathbf{1}_{X})
,
\mathrm{CC}(\mathrm{Eu}_{X})
and
\mathrm{CC}(\nu_{f})
, with coefficient masses at most
2^{O(N)}

[

,
Alu18
]
; Section

makes this the definition of the invariant class covered by the no-go theorem.

3
Determinantal representations and the kernel incidence

3.1
Determinantal complexity

For
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
,
\mathrm{dc}(f)
is the least
m
with
f=\det(A_{0}+\sum_{i}x_{i}A_{i})
,
A_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
. For homogeneous
f
of degree
d
, a
homogeneous
representation
f=\det M(x)
with
M(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}A_{i}
linear forces
m=d
, since
\det M
is homogeneous of degree
m
: the homogeneous model carries no optimization over the size, and we attach no separate complexity measure to it. All of the size slack
m\geq d
lives in the affine-linear model, and it is recovered inside the homogeneous model geometrically: by Remark
3.1
below, a size-
m
affine-linear representation of
f_{0}
is the same thing as a size-
m
homogeneous linear representation of
x_{0}^{\,m-d}f_{0}
in
N+1
variables. Accordingly, the kernel-incidence geometry of this section and the next is run at arbitrary size
m
on the homogeneous form
x_{0}^{\,m-d}f_{0}
— never on
f_{0}
itself — and the multiplicity
x_{0}^{\,m-d}
is dealt with once and for all in Lemma
6.9
.

Remark 3.1

(Affine-linear representations homogenize)
.

If
f
is homogeneous of degree

and

with

, then

G(x_{0},x)\;:=\;\det\Bigl(x_{0}A_{0}+\sum_{i}x_{i}A_{i}\Bigr)\;=\;\sum_{j}x_{0}^{\,m-j}f_{j}(x)\;=\;x_{0}^{\,m-d}f(x),

since the degree-
j
part
f_{j}
of

vanishes for

. Thus every size-
m
affine-linear representation of

is a size-
m
homogeneous representation, in
N+1
variables, of

, whose reduced zero locus

has degree at most

. All results below are stated for the homogeneous model; by this remark they apply to affine-linear representations at the cost

,

, which is absorbed by every constant in the paper. The ceiling of Section
5
is insensitive to the multiplicity

for the
\mathbf{1}_{X}
-type invariants, and Section
6
explains why the multiplicity carries no usable information for the
\nu
-type invariants either.

The kernel incidence

Fix

with

We concentrate on

Lemma 3.2

(Genericity package)
.

Assume

,

, and let

be generic. Then:

1.

X_{M}
is reduced and irreducible, and

, which has pure codimension

in

.

3.

Z_{1}
is reduced and irreducible, and is smooth away from the preimage of

.

Proof sketch.

Consider the universal objects over the full matrix space
\mathbb{P}^{m^{2}-1}

of codimension

Lemma 3.3

(
Z_{1}
is the conormal variety of the determinant)
.

Let
M
be generic,
N\geq 3
,
m\geq 2
. The rational map

\psi:Z_{1}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\times\check{\mathbb{P}}^{N-1},\qquad(x,v,u)\;\longmapsto\;\bigl(x,\ [\,u^{\mathsf{T}}A_{1}v:\cdots:u^{\mathsf{T}}A_{N}v\,]\bigr)

is defined away from the preimage of
\Sigma_{2}
, and it maps

birationally onto
\mathrm{Con}(X_{M})
. Moreover
\psi^{*}\check{h}=a+b
in

(restricted from the product), so that

\delta_{i}(\mathrm{Con}X_{M})\;=\;\int_{Z_{1}}h^{i}\,(a+b)^{\,N-2-i},\qquad i=0,\dots,N-2.

In the universal case

this recovers the classical statement that the dual of the determinant hypersurface is the Segre variety
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}

[
GKZ94
,
Tev05
]
.

Proof.

Jacobi’s formula gives
d(\det M)=\mathrm{tr}\bigl(\mathrm{adj}(M)\,dM\bigr)
. At a corank-one matrix

with kernel line
\mathbb{C}v
and cokernel line
\mathbb{C}u^{\mathsf{T}}
, the adjugate is rank one with image
\mathbb{C}v
and kernel
u^{\perp}
:
\mathrm{adj}(M(x))=\kappa\,vu^{\mathsf{T}}
for some
\kappa\neq 0
. Hence
\partial_{i}\det M(x)=\kappa\,u^{\mathsf{T}}A_{i}v
, i.e. the gradient of

at

is proportional to
(u^{\mathsf{T}}A_{i}v)_{i=1}^{N}
. By Lemma

(1),
X_{M}
is reduced with singular locus
\Sigma_{2}
, so on

the gradient is nonzero and spans the conormal line of
X_{M}
at

. Therefore
\psi
is a morphism on

, lands in
\mathrm{Con}X_{M}
, and is bijective over the smooth locus (the conormal point determines

, and

determines
(v,u)
by Lemma

(2)). Both

and
\mathrm{Con}X_{M}
are irreducible of dimension

, so
\psi
is birational onto
\mathrm{Con}X_{M}
. Finally, for a linear form

on
\check{\mathbb{P}}^{N-1}
,

is bilinear in
(u,v)
, a section of
\mathcal{O}(0,1,1)
; hence
\psi^{*}\check{h}=a+b
. The multidegree formula follows by the projection formula, using that

is birational and that the multidegrees of

can be computed on any birational model on which the two polarizations are pulled back. The degenerate locus has dimension
\leq N-3
and cannot affect intersection numbers against
N-2
divisor classes; this is made precise in Proposition

.
∎

Remark 3.4

(What a reading is allowed to see)
.

Lemma

is the structural reason the no-go theorem has its scope: counting solutions of the corank-one system, against any multihomogeneous conditions in

. Whatever invariant of the input

. Corank-

(conditional on Hypothesis

) addresses whether they are cheaper per root, and none of the corank-one results below depend on it.

Throughout this section
M

4.1
The class of the incidence

Theorem 4.1

(Class of
Z_{1}
)
.

In

,

[Z_{1}]\;=\;(h+a)^{m}\cdot c_{m-1}(K),\qquad c_{m-1}(K)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}\,a^{k}\,(h+b)^{\,m-1-k},

where

is the rank-
(m-1)
kernel bundle of the contraction with the tautological

Proof.

The
m
entries of
M(x)v
are bilinear in
(x,v)
, i.e. a section of
E_{1}:=\mathcal{O}(h+a)^{\oplus m}
on
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}_{v}
. For generic

its zero locus

fibres over

with the projectivized kernels as fibres, generically points (Lemma
3.2
(2)), so

: this is the expected codimension
m
in the
(N+m-3)
-dimensional product, the section is regular, and
[Y]=c_{m}(E_{1})=(h+a)^{m}
.

Now work on
Y\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}_{u}\subset P
. The
m
entries of
u^{\mathsf{T}}M(x)
form a section
s
of
\mathcal{O}(h+b)^{\oplus m}
. The coordinates
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
of the
v
-factor define a surjection of bundles
\mathcal{O}(h+b)^{\oplus m}\to\mathcal{O}(h+a+b)
,
(\phi_{j})\mapsto\sum_{j}\phi_{j}v_{j}
(surjective since

K
of rank
m-1
. On

the section
s
takes values in
K
: pointwise,
\sum_{j}(u^{\mathsf{T}}M)_{j}v_{j}=u^{\mathsf{T}}(Mv)=0
. Its zero locus is exactly

, of the expected codimension

in

(Lemma
3.2
(2):
\dim Z_{1}=N-2=\dim(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}_{u})-(m-1)
), and for generic

. Hence

[Z_{1}]=[Y\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}_{u}]\cdot c_{m-1}(K)=(h+a)^{m}\,c_{m-1}(K).

It remains to evaluate

. From
0\to K\to\mathcal{O}(h+b)^{\oplus m}\to\mathcal{O}(h+a+b)\to 0
,

c(K)\;=\;\frac{(1+h+b)^{m}}{1+h+a+b}.

Write
s=h+b
,

as independent formal variables. In

one has the telescoping identity

\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}a^{k}s^{\,m-1-k}\Bigr)(s+a)\;=\;s^{m}-(-a)^{m},

(3)

while the degree-
(m-1)

is

\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{m}{m-1-j}s^{m-1-j}t^{j}\Bigr)\,t\;=\;s^{m}-\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}s^{m-j}(-t)^{j}\;=\;s^{m}-(s-t)^{m}\;=\;s^{m}-(-a)^{m}.

Both candidate expressions for

multiply to the same polynomial under the non-zero-divisor

, and therefore agree after mapping to

∎

4.2
The polar count

By Lemma

the top polar degree of the generic determinantal hypersurface is read off

by intersecting with

Theorem 4.2

(Closed forms)
.

With
[\,\cdot\,]
denoting coefficient extraction in
\mathbb{Z}[h,a,b]
:

\displaystyle T(N,m)

\displaystyle=\bigl[h^{N-1}a^{m-1}b^{m-1}\bigr]\,(h+a)^{m}\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}a^{k}(h+b)^{m-1-k}\Bigr)(a+b)^{N-2}

(4)

\displaystyle=\bigl[h^{N-1}a^{m-1}b^{m-1}\bigr]\,\frac{(h+a)^{m}(h+b)^{m}(a+b)^{N-2}}{h+a+b}

(5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\binom{N-2}{j}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k+j+1}\binom{m-1-k}{\,N-2-j-k\,}

(6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\binom{N-2}{j}\sum_{l=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-l}\binom{m}{l}\binom{m+j-l}{\,N-1-l\,}.

(7)

T(N,m)
is a polynomial in
m
of degree

with leading coefficient
\binom{2N-4}{N-2}/(N-1)!
.

Proof.

(
4
) is Theorem
4.1
together with the standard fact that an intersection number on
P=\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
is the coefficient of

. For (
5
), interpret the fraction by expanding

at

, which makes the coefficient extraction well defined on homogeneous rational functions. By (
3
),
(h+a)^{m}\,c_{m-1}(K)\,(a+b)^{N-2}
differs from the fraction in (

) by

, every term of whose expansion has
a
-degree at least

and hence contributes nothing to the coefficient of

. So (
5
) agrees with (
4
). For (
6
): expand the three factors of (
4
),

(h+a)^{m}=\sum_{\alpha}\binom{m}{\alpha}h^{m-\alpha}a^{\alpha},\quad c_{m-1}(K)=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}a^{k}\sum_{\beta}\binom{m-1-k}{\beta}h^{m-1-k-\beta}b^{\beta},\quad(a+b)^{N-2}=\sum_{j}\binom{N-2}{j}a^{j}b^{N-2-j},

and match exponents:
a
forces
\alpha=m-1-k-j
,
b
forces
\beta=m-N+1+j
, and then the

. The coefficient is
\binom{m}{m-1-k-j}\binom{m-1-k}{m-N+1+j}=\binom{m}{k+j+1}\binom{m-1-k}{N-2-j-k}
, proving (
6
). For (
7
), fix
j
and write the inner sum of (
6
) as a residue: with
\binom{m-1-k}{r-k}=[z^{r-k}](1+z)^{m-1-k}
,

\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k+j+1}\binom{m-1-k}{r-k}=[z^{r}](1+z)^{m-1}\sum_{k}\binom{m}{k+j+1}w^{k},\qquad w:=\frac{-z}{1+z},

\sum_{k}\binom{m}{k+j+1}w^{k}=w^{-(j+1)}\bigl((1+w)^{m}-\sum_{l\leq j}\binom{m}{l}w^{l}\bigr)

\text{inner}_{j}=(-1)^{j+1}[z^{N-1}](1+z)^{j}+\sum_{l=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-l}\binom{m}{l}\,[z^{N-1-l}](1+z)^{m+j-l},

and the first term vanishes since

(after a generic change of the

-frame), has pure expected dimension
N-2
and class
(h+a)^{m}(h+b)^{m-1}
; it contains
Z_{1}
, and the residual cycle
Z^{\prime}
is effective. Intersecting with
(a+b)^{N-2}
and using nonnegativity of multidegrees of effective cycles gives the inequality. The
N=3
evaluation is the two-term coefficient extraction recorded in the statement.
∎

4.6
Asymptotics: tight per root, loose by one half

The asymptotics rest on one more closed form, with all terms nonnegative; it is the form in which the count is easiest to bound from below, and it removes every alternating-sum issue from the estimates.

Lemma 4.6

(A positive form of the count)
.

For all
N\geq 3
and
m\geq 2
,

T(N,m)\;=\;\sum_{i=\lceil N/2\rceil}^{\min(m,\,N-1)}\binom{m}{i}\binom{i-1}{N-1-i}\binom{2i-2}{i-1},

(8)

a sum with all terms nonnegative. In particular
T(N,m)\geq\binom{m}{N-1}\binom{2N-4}{N-2}
whenever
m\geq N-1
(the term
i=N-1
).

Proof.

In
\mathbb{Z}[h,a,b]
one has
(h+a)(h+b)=h(h+a+b)+ab
, hence

(h+a)^{m}(h+b)^{m}\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}\,h^{i}\,(h+a+b)^{i}\,(ab)^{m-i}.

Dividing by
h+a+b
in the expansion at
h=\infty
used in (
5
),

\frac{(h+a)^{m}(h+b)^{m}}{h+a+b}\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}\binom{m}{i}\,h^{i}\,(h+a+b)^{i-1}(ab)^{m-i}\;+\;\frac{(ab)^{m}}{h+a+b},

and the expansion of the last term contains only negative powers of
h
, hence contributes nothing to the coefficient of
h^{N-1}
. In the
i
-th summand, multiplied by
(a+b)^{N-2}
, the factor
(ab)^{m-i}
forces the extraction of
a^{i-1}b^{i-1}
from
h^{i}(h+a+b)^{i-1}(a+b)^{N-2}
: choosing
h^{N-1-i}
from
(h+a+b)^{i-1}
, in
\binom{i-1}{N-1-i}
ways, leaves
(a+b)^{(i-1)-(N-1-i)}\cdot(a+b)^{N-2}=(a+b)^{2i-2}
, whose
a^{i-1}b^{i-1}
-coefficient is
\binom{2i-2}{i-1}
. Terms with
i<\lceil N/2\rceil
or
i>N-1
vanish through
\binom{i-1}{N-1-i}
. (Hand checks: at
N=3
the single term
i=2
gives
2\binom{m}{2}=m(m-1)
; at
N=4
,
i=2,3
give
2\binom{m}{2}+6\binom{m}{3}=m(m-1)^{2}
; at
(5,5)
,
i=3,4
give
120+100=220
; and
T(5,2)=0
since the range is empty. The identity was additionally machine-checked against (
7
) by exact integer arithmetic on the grid
3\leq N\leq 12
,
2\leq m\leq 6N
.)
∎

Theorem 4.7

(Asymptotics)
.

Fix
N\geq 3
. As polynomials in
m
,

T(N,m)=\frac{\binom{2N-4}{N-2}}{(N-1)!}\,m^{N-1}\bigl(1+O_{N}(1/m)\bigr),\qquad B_{\mathrm{bez}}(N,m)=\frac{\binom{2N-3}{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\,m^{N-1}\bigl(1+O_{N}(1/m)\bigr),

so that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{T(N,m)}{B_{\mathrm{bez}}(N,m)}\;=\;C_{N}\;:=\;\frac{\binom{2N-4}{N-2}}{\binom{2N-3}{N-1}}\;=\;\frac{N-1}{2N-3}\;\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\;\frac{1}{2}.

Moreover, in any regime with
N\to\infty
and
m/N\to\infty
,

T(N,m)^{1/(N-1)}\;=\;(4e+o(1))\,\frac{m}{N}\;=\;\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\,B_{\mathrm{bez}}(N,m)^{1/(N-1)},

with
o(1)
terms that depend only on
N
and on
m/N
and tend to
0
as both tend to infinity, at any joint rate.

Proof.

The leading coefficient of
T
is Theorem
4.2
; for
B_{\mathrm{bez}}
, the same computation gives
(N-1)!\cdot[\text{lead}]=\sum_{j}\binom{N-2}{j}\binom{N-1}{j+1}=\binom{2N-3}{N-2}=\binom{2N-3}{N-1}
by Vandermonde. The ratio identity
\binom{2N-3}{N-1}=\frac{2N-3}{N-1}\binom{2N-4}{N-2}
is Pascal plus the absorption identity; since
T
and
B_{\mathrm{bez}}
are polynomials in
m
of degree
N-1
with these positive leading coefficients, the fixed-
N
ratio limit follows.

For the per-root law we give a two-sided bound with explicit error factors; no estimate is ever applied to an alternating sum.
Upper bound.
By Proposition
4.5
,
T\leq B_{\mathrm{bez}}
, and every term of
B_{\mathrm{bez}}=\sum_{j}\binom{N-2}{j}\binom{m}{j+1}\binom{m-1}{N-2-j}
is nonnegative, so
\binom{m}{r}\leq m^{r}/r!
applies termwise:

B_{\mathrm{bez}}(N,m)\;\leq\;m^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{\binom{N-2}{j}}{(j+1)!\,(N-2-j)!}\;=\;\frac{\binom{2N-3}{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\,m^{N-1},

the last equality being the Vandermonde evaluation above. Hence, using
\binom{2N-3}{N-1}\leq 2^{2N-3}
,
(N-1)!\geq((N-1)/e)^{N-1}
, and
2^{(2N-3)/(N-1)}\leq 4
, for
every

N\geq 3
and every
m\geq 2
,

T(N,m)^{1/(N-1)}\;\leq\;B_{\mathrm{bez}}(N,m)^{1/(N-1)}\;\leq\;\frac{4e\,m}{N-1}\;=\;\Bigl(4e+\tfrac{4e}{N-1}\Bigr)\frac{m}{N}.

Lower bound.
Keep the single term
i=N-1
of Lemma
4.6
: for
m\geq N-1
, using
\binom{2K}{K}\geq 4^{K}/(2K+1)
at
K=N-2
,

T(N,m)\;\geq\;\binom{m}{N-1}\binom{2N-4}{N-2}\;\geq\;\frac{(m-N+2)^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\cdot\frac{4^{N-2}}{2N-3}.

Taking
(N-1)
-st roots and using Stirling’s upper bound
(N-1)!\leq e\sqrt{N-1}\,\bigl((N-1)/e\bigr)^{N-1}
,

T(N,m)^{1/(N-1)}\;\geq\;\Bigl(1-\frac{N}{m}\Bigr)\,\frac{4e\,m}{N-1}\cdot\Bigl(4(2N-3)\,e\sqrt{N-1}\Bigr)^{-1/(N-1)}\;\geq\;\bigl(4e-o(1)\bigr)\,\frac{m}{N},

where the first error factor depends only on
m/N
and the second only on
N
, each tending to
1
in the stated regime. Since
B_{\mathrm{bez}}\geq T
, the same lower bound holds for
B_{\mathrm{bez}}
, and combining the four inequalities gives the displayed per-root chain with the claimed uniformity.
∎

4.7
Corank monotonicity

The corank-one floor used in Section
6
is unconditional. For corank
c\geq 2
we record the natural monotonicity statement, conditional on one explicitly displayed hypothesis whose verification is Schubert-calculus bookkeeping that we have not carried out.

Hypothesis 4.10

(Effective specialization)
.

For each
c\geq 2
, each slot, and each size
m
, there is a flat degeneration of the slot-resolved corank-
c
extraction conditions of Definition
6.5
— specializing the
c
-planes to contain a fixed corank-one pair
(v,u)
— under which the limit of the solution class decomposes as the class of a slot-matched corank-one system of size
m-c+1

plus an effective cycle
; equivalently, the Schubert correction terms produced by the specialization pair nonnegatively against the globally generated condition classes.

Proposition 4.11

(Higher corank is not cheaper; conditional on Hypothesis (S))
.

Assume Hypothesis
4.10
. Let
c\geq 2
and let
B
be any valid intersection-theoretic upper bound for the number of solutions of the corank-
c
reading at size
m
, against any complementary-dimension product of the tautological globally generated classes on
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}\times\mathrm{Gr}(c,m)\times\mathrm{Gr}(c,m)
. Then in the regime
N\to\infty
,
m/N\to\infty
,

B^{1/(N-1)}\;\geq\;(4e-o(1))\,\frac{m}{N}.

Discussion.

Two ingredients, the second of which is exactly where Hypothesis (S) enters. (i)
Positivity
: by Fulton–Pragacz
[
FP98
,
Pra88
]
, the class of the corank-
c
kernel incidence (a degeneracy locus with kernel and cokernel flags) is a nonnegative combination of Schubert monomials in the tautological classes; intersection numbers of effective cycles against globally generated classes are nonnegative and monotone under adding effective terms. (ii)
Specialization
: degenerating the corank-
c
configuration so that the
c
-planes contain a fixed corank-one pair
(v,u)
exhibits the corank-one count
T(N,m-c+1)
as a lower bound for the corank-
c
count read against the matching conditions,
provided
the correction terms produced by the specialization carry the effective sign — which is precisely Hypothesis
4.10
, and is what would have to be written out. Granting it, the per-root cost of
T(N,m-c+1)
is
(4e-o(1))\,m/N
for
c=O(m/\log m)
by Theorem
4.7
, while for larger
c
the dimension count alone already forces a super-quadratic number of equations and the bound is vacuous.
∎

Flag.
Hypothesis
4.10
is the sign half of step (ii): Fulton–Pragacz positivity gives effectivity of the corank-
c
classes themselves, but the decomposition of the
specialized
class into a corank-one part plus corrections must additionally be shown effective, not merely Schubert-expressible, before the lower bound transfers. Until that is written out, Proposition
4.11
is conditional. Nothing else in the paper depends on it except the corank-
c\geq 2
clause of Theorem
6.11
; the corank-one no-go is unconditional on this point.

5
The invariant ceiling

In this section
f
is an arbitrary nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
in
N
variables,
X=V(f)_{\mathrm{red}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n=N-1
. No hypothesis is placed on
\mathrm{Sing}X
:
X
may be reducible, non-normal, with singularities in arbitrary codimension. The degree

5.1
The Euler-characteristic envelope

Lemma 5.1

(Global Milnor fibre as a cover)
.

Let
g
be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree
d\geq 1
in
k+1
variables,
C=V(g)\subset\mathbb{P}^{k}
,
U=\mathbb{P}^{k}\setminus C
, and
F=\{g=1\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{k+1}
. Then
F
is a smooth affine variety of dimension
k
, the map
\pi:F\to U
,
x\mapsto[x]
, is a
d
-sheeted covering, and
\chi(F)=d\,\chi(U)
.

Proof.

Smoothness: by Euler’s relation
\sum_{i}x_{i}\partial_{i}g=dg
, on
F
we have
x\cdot\nabla g=d\neq 0
, so
\nabla g\neq 0
. The fibre of
\pi
over
[x]
with
g(x)\neq 0
is
\{tx:\,t^{d}g(x)=1\}
, which has exactly
d
points; local triviality is standard (the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action). Euler characteristics of complex algebraic varieties are multiplicative in finite coverings.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Critical points via refined Bézout)
.

With
g,F
as in Lemma
5.1
and
d\geq 2
: for a generic linear function
\ell
on
\mathbb{C}^{k+1}
and generic linear functions
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k-j}
and generic
t_{1},\dots,t_{k-j}\in\mathbb{C}
, the restriction of
\ell
to the smooth
j
-dimensional affine variety

F_{j}:=F\cap\{\ell_{1}=t_{1},\dots,\ell_{k-j}=t_{k-j}\}

is a Morse function with at most
d(d-1)^{\,j}
critical points, for every
0\leq j\leq k
.

Proof.

That
\ell|_{F_{j}}
is Morse with finitely many critical points for generic data is standard (Sard applied to the relevant gradient/covector maps). Choose linear coordinates in which
\mathrm{span}(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k-j},\ell)
is spanned by the first
k-j+1
coordinate functions. By Lagrange,
x\in F_{j}
is critical for
\ell|_{F_{j}}
iff
\nabla g(x)\in\mathrm{span}(e_{1},\dots,e_{k-j+1})
, i.e.

g(x)=1,\qquad\ell_{s}(x)=t_{s}\ (s\leq k-j),\qquad\partial_{i}g(x)=0\ \ (k-j+2\leq i\leq k+1),

a system of
(1)+(k-j)+(j)
equations of degrees
d,1,\dots,1,d-1,\dots,d-1
. (Solutions with
\nabla g=0
cannot occur: Euler’s relation forces
g=0\neq 1
.) Every critical point is an isolated solution of this system. Homogenize with
x_{0}
(replacing
g=1
by
g=x_{0}^{d}
, degrees unchanged): an isolated affine solution remains an isolated point of the projective intersection in
\mathbb{P}^{k+1}
, since a positive-dimensional projective component through it would meet the affine chart in positive dimension near the point. By Fulton’s refined Bézout theorem
[
Ful98
, Ex. 12.3.1]
, the number of isolated points of an intersection of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{k+1}
is at most the product of the degrees, even in the presence of positive-dimensional (excess) components. The product is
d\cdot 1^{\,k-j}\cdot(d-1)^{\,j}
.
∎

Hypothesis 5.3

(Affine Lefschetz attaching for generic pencils)
.

Let
F\subset\mathbb{C}^{M}
be a smooth closed affine variety of pure dimension
k
and let
\ell:\mathbb{C}^{M}\to\mathbb{C}
be a generic linear function. Then
\ell|_{F}
has finitely many critical points, no atypical values arise from infinity, and for generic
t
the variety
F
has the homotopy type of
F\cap\{\ell=t\}
with one
k
-cell attached per critical point of
\ell|_{F}
; in particular

\chi(F)\;=\;\chi\bigl(F\cap\{\ell=t\}\bigr)\;+\;(-1)^{k}\,\#\mathrm{crit}(\ell|_{F}).

Proposition 5.4

(Envelope)
.

Let
f
be nonzero homogeneous of degree
d\geq 2
in
N
variables,
X=V(f)\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
(as a set). Then for every
0\leq k\leq n
and generic linear
L_{k}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of dimension
k
,

\bigl|\chi(X\cap L_{k})\bigr|\;\leq\;E(d,k)\;:=\;2(d-1)^{k}+2k+2.

Proof.

A generic
L_{k}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{k}
on which
f
restricts to a nonzero degree-
d
form
g
, with
X\cap L_{k}=V(g)
, so it suffices to bound
|\chi(V(g))|
in
\mathbb{P}^{k}
. Let
F,U
be as in Lemma
5.1
. By Hypothesis
5.3
— the affine Lefschetz theorem of Hamm–Lê for generic pencils on smooth affine varieties
[
HL73
,
Dim92
]
— for generic
\ell
and generic
t
,
F
is obtained from
F\cap\{\ell=t\}
, up to homotopy, by attaching one
k
-cell per critical point of
\ell|_{F}
; hence

\chi(F)=\chi(F\cap\{\ell=t\})+(-1)^{k}\,\#\mathrm{crit}(\ell|_{F})
,
and inductively, slicing down to dimension
0
and applying Lemma
5.2
at each level,

|\chi(F)|\;\leq\;\sum_{j=0}^{k}d(d-1)^{\,j}.

For
d\geq 3
the geometric sum is at most
d(d-1)^{k}\frac{d-1}{d-2}\leq 2d(d-1)^{k}
, so
|\chi(U)|=|\chi(F)|/d\leq 2(d-1)^{k}
and
|\chi(X\cap L_{k})|\leq\chi(\mathbb{P}^{k})+|\chi(U)|\leq(k+1)+2(d-1)^{k}\leq E(d,k)
. For
d=2
the sum is
2(k+1)
, giving
|\chi(U)|\leq k+1
and
|\chi(X\cap L_{k})|\leq 2k+2\leq E(2,k)
.
∎

Flag.
Status of Hypothesis
5.3
. This is the classical affine Lefschetz attaching statement; the content requiring verification is the at-infinity genericity — that a
generic
linear pencil on a smooth affine variety has no atypical values coming from infinity, so the attaching is governed by the finite critical points alone. The author must pin the precise statement against
[
HL73
]
and the exposition in
[
Dim92
, Ch. 1, §6 and Ch. 5]
before this flag is removed; until then, Proposition

— and with it every constant in the ceiling and in Theorem

— should be read as conditional on (HL). Two mitigations. First, the dependence is on a textbook statement, not on new mathematics; no exponent depends on the precise form of the genericity. Second, there is an independent classical route to the same envelope shape: the Euler characteristic of the complement

equals the alternating sum of the projective degrees of the gradient map of

by the same refined-Bézout argument as Lemma

5.2
The transform: slice data to multidegrees, mass four

Theorem 5.5

(Second-difference transform)
.

Let
\varphi
be a constructible function on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with characteristic cycle
\mathrm{CC}(\varphi)=n_{0}[\mathrm{zero\ section}]+\sum_{W}n_{W}[\mathrm{Con}W]
and multidegree vector
\Delta_{i}(\varphi)=\sum_{W}n_{W}\delta_{i}(\mathrm{Con}W)
as in Section
2.2
. Then, with
\chi_{k}=\chi_{k}(\varphi)
and
\chi_{-1}=\chi_{-2}=0
,

\Delta_{i}(\varphi)\;=\;(-1)^{i}\bigl(\chi_{n-i}-2\chi_{n-i-1}+\chi_{n-i-2}\bigr),\qquad i=0,\dots,n-1,\qquad\text{and}\qquad n_{0}(\varphi)=(-1)^{n}\chi_{0}(\varphi).

In particular the transform from slice data to any single multidegree slot has
\ell^{1}
-mass at most
4
(exactly
4
for
i\leq n-2
), independently of
n
.

Proof.

Universality.
By the microlocal index theorem
[
Kas73
,
BDK81
,
Dub84
,
KS90
]
in its projective Radon-transform form
[
Ern94
,
MT07
]
, for generic
L_{k}
the value
\chi_{k}(\varphi)
is computed by the intersection pairing of
\mathrm{CC}(\varphi)
with the (co)normal data of
L_{k}
; transversality for generic
L_{k}
(Kleiman) makes the pairing of each fixed component
[\mathrm{Con}W]
(resp. the zero section) with the
L_{k}
-data depend only on
(n,k)
and the class of
\mathrm{Con}W
in
A_{n-1}(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\check{\mathbb{P}}^{n})
, i.e. only on the multidegrees
\delta_{i}(\mathrm{Con}W)
. Hence there is a matrix
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)}
, depending only on
n
, with

\bigl(\chi_{0}(\varphi),\dots,\chi_{n}(\varphi)\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}=A\cdot\bigl(n_{0}(\varphi),\Delta_{0}(\varphi),\dots,\Delta_{n-1}(\varphi)\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}\quad\text{for all constructible }\varphi.

Evaluation on a basis.
Take
\varphi_{m}=\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}
for linear subspaces
\mathbb{P}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
m=0,\dots,n
. These are smooth, so
\mathrm{CC}(\varphi_{m})=(-1)^{m}[\mathrm{Con}\mathbb{P}^{m}]
, and
\mathrm{Con}\mathbb{P}^{m}\cong\mathbb{P}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-m-1}
has
\delta_{i}=\mathbb{1}_{\{i=m\}}
; thus the coefficient vector of
\varphi_{m}
is
(-1)^{m}e_{m}
(reading
e_{n}
as the zero-section slot). On the other side,
\chi_{k}(\varphi_{m})=\chi(\mathbb{P}^{m+k-n})=\max(0,\,m+k-n+1)
, a ramp in
k
with kink at
k=n-m-1
. The matrix
R=[\chi_{k}(\varphi_{m})]
is unimodular (triangular with respect to
m+k
, with units on the antidiagonal
m+k=n
), so
A=R\cdot D^{-1}
with
D=\mathrm{diag}((-1)^{m})
is invertible over
\mathbb{Z}
, and the coefficient vector of any
\varphi
is the
unique
universal linear image of its slice vector.

Closed form.
It therefore suffices to verify the displayed formulas on the basis. The second difference of the ramp
r_{k}=\max(0,m+k-n+1)
, evaluated as
r_{k+1}-2r_{k}+r_{k-1}
, equals
1
at the kink
k=n-m-1
and
0
elsewhere; the formula’s
i
-th entry reads the second difference centred at
k=n-i-1
, multiplied by
(-1)^{i}
, hence equals
(-1)^{m}
exactly at
i=m
and
0
otherwise: this is
(-1)^{m}e_{m}
, as required. For the zero-section slot,
\chi_{0}(\varphi_{m})=\mathbb{1}_{\{m=n\}}
and
n_{0}(\varphi_{m})=(-1)^{n}\mathbb{1}_{\{m=n\}}
, verifying
n_{0}=(-1)^{n}\chi_{0}
. The mass count is immediate from the coefficients
(1,-2,1)
.
∎

5.3
The ceiling

Theorem 5.7

(Invariant ceiling)
.

Let

The same statement holds verbatim with

7.4

Shifted partial derivatives and the related flattening ranks are

7.5
What would evade the theorem

Either exit of Remark
