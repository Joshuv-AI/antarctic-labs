Tripartite Entanglement as a Probe of Neutrino Mass Hierarchy, CP Violation, and Non-Standard Interactions
Hridya Harish Nambiar, Bipin Singh Koranga

Abstract
We investigate global tripartite quantum entanglement in three-flavor neutrino oscillations as a tool for probing the neutrino mass hierarchy and CP violation. Using the linear entropy formalism, we compute the global entanglement E global E_{\rm global} for an initial electron neutrino state as a function of L / E L/E , comparing Normal Ordering (NO) and Inverted Ordering (IO) across CP phases δ CP ∈ { 0 ∘ , 90 ∘ , 120 ∘ , 180 ∘ } \delta_{\rm CP}\in\{0^{\circ},90^{\circ},120^{\circ},180^{\circ}\} , in vacuum and in constant-density matter ( ρ = 2.8 \rho=2.8 g/cm 3 , L = 1300 L=1300 km). We define the hierarchy sensitivity diagnostic Δ ​ S = E global NO − E global IO \Delta S=E_{\rm global}^{{\rm NO}}-E_{\rm global}^{{\rm IO}} and show that MSW matter effects amplify | Δ ​ S | max |\Delta S|_{\rm max} by roughly a factor of two relative to vacuum, with peak sensitivity at L / E ≈ 655 L/E\approx 655 km/GeV ( E ≈ 2 E\approx 2 GeV at the DUNE baseline). For antineutrinos the diagnostic is near-perfectly antisymmetric to the neutrino case at the MSW resonance, with deviations directly encoding δ CP \delta_{\rm CP} . The combination Δ ​ S + = Δ ​ S ν + Δ ​ S ν ¯ \Delta S_{+}=\Delta S_{\nu}+\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}} and Δ ​ S − = Δ ​ S ν − Δ ​ S ν ¯ \Delta S_{-}=\Delta S_{\nu}-\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}} separates the matter hierarchy signal from the CP asymmetry signal in the tripartite entanglement. For non-standard interactions (NSI) parameterized by ε e ​ e \varepsilon_{ee} , we find | Δ ​ S | max ≈ 0.113 + 0.105 ​ ε e ​ e |\Delta S|_{\rm max}\approx 0.113+0.105\,\varepsilon_{ee} , a linear and CP-phase-independent relation. The optimal L / E L/E for hierarchy discrimination is stable at ≈ 655 \approx 655 km/GeV for all | ε e ​ e | ≤ 0.2 |\varepsilon_{ee}|\leq 0.2 , providing a robust, NSI-independent energy recommendation for DUNE. Keywords : Neutrino oscillations; tripartite entanglement; linear entropy; CP violation; mass hierarchy; MSW effect; non-standard interactions; DUNE.



1
Introduction

The question of how quantum information is encoded in
traveling particles has driven a productive intersection between
quantum information theory and neutrino physics over the past
two decades
[
9
,
10
,
13
,
Abdolalizadeh2024
]
. Neutrinos are
particularly well-suited for such studies. They travel
macroscopic distances without losing quantum coherence,
interact almost not at all with the environment,
and their flavor oscillations are a pure consequence of quantum
mechanical superposition of mass eigenstates.

The three-flavor neutrino system can be modeled as a three-qubit
system, one qubit per flavor mode, making it a natural setting for
multipartite entanglement studies. Previous work has examined
bipartite entanglement using concurrence and von Neumann
entropy
[
9
,
Blasone2014a
,
Aydiner2023
,
5
]
,
and tripartite entanglement via complementarity
relations
[
11
,
12
]
.
However, none of these studies have simultaneously addressed
CP violation, mass hierarchy discrimination, matter effects,
antineutrinos, and non-standard interactions (NSI)
within a single tripartite entanglement framework.

We close this gap. Using the global linear entropy as our
entanglement measure, we introduce the hierarchy sensitivity
diagnostic
\Delta S
and study how it responds to each of the
above effects. We also propose its neutrino-antineutrino
decomposition as a tool to independently isolate the
CP-asymmetry signal and the matter-hierarchy signal.
All results are benchmarked against the DUNE
experiment
[
23
]
at
L=1300
km.

2
Theoretical Framework

2.1
Oscillation Probabilities

Flavor eigenstates
(\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau})
are related to mass
eigenstates
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})
via the PMNS
matrix
[
1
,
2
]
:

U=\begin{pmatrix}c_{12}c_{13}&s_{12}c_{13}&s_{13}e^{-i\delta_{\rm CP}}\\
-s_{12}c_{23}-c_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta_{\rm CP}}&c_{12}c_{23}-s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta_{\rm CP}}&s_{23}c_{13}\\
s_{12}s_{23}-c_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta_{\rm CP}}&-c_{12}s_{23}-s_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta_{\rm CP}}&c_{23}c_{13}\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
c_{ij}=\cos\theta_{ij}
,
s_{ij}=\sin\theta_{ij}
.
The vacuum transition probability from
\nu_{e}
to
\nu_{\beta}
is

\displaystyle P(\nu_{e}\to\nu_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\delta_{e\beta}-4\sum_{i>j}\mathrm{Re}\bigl[U_{ei}U^{*}_{\beta i}U^{*}_{ej}U_{\beta j}\bigr]\sin^{2}\!\!\left(\frac{1.27\,\Delta m^{2}_{ij}\,L}{E}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{i>j}\mathrm{Im}\bigl[U_{ei}U^{*}_{\beta i}U^{*}_{ej}U_{\beta j}\bigr]\sin\!\!\left(\frac{2.54\,\Delta m^{2}_{ij}\,L}{E}\right),

(2)

with
\Delta m_{ij}^{2}
(eV
2
),
L
(km),
E
(GeV)
[
8
]
.
The second term is the CP-odd contribution.
It vanishes for
\delta_{\rm CP}=0^{\circ}
or
180^{\circ}

and is largest at
\delta_{\rm CP}=90^{\circ}
.

2.2
Matter Hamiltonian and NSI

Propagation through matter adds a charged-current potential
to the Hamiltonian in the flavor basis
(MSW effect
[
6
,
7
]
):

H_{\rm matter}=H_{\rm vac}+\mathrm{diag}\bigl(A(1+\varepsilon_{ee}),\ 0,\ 0\bigr),

(3)

where
H_{\rm vac}=(2E)^{-1}U\,\mathrm{diag}(0,\Delta m^{2}_{21},\Delta m^{2}_{31})U^{\dagger}

and the MSW potential is

A\ (\mathrm{eV}^{2})=7.63\times 10^{-5}\times\rho\ (\mathrm{g/cm}^{3})\times Y_{e}\times E\ (\mathrm{GeV}).

(4)

We use
\rho=2.8
g/cm
3
and
Y_{e}=0.5
appropriate
for the DUNE Earth-crust baseline.
The parameter
\varepsilon_{ee}
in Eq. (
3
)
parameterizes diagonal NSI between electron neutrinos and
matter
[
22
]
, with current bounds

|\varepsilon_{ee}|\lesssim 0.3
–
0.5

[
Esteban2018
]
.
Setting
\varepsilon_{ee}=0
recovers standard MSW.

Since
A\propto E
, the matter Hamiltonian is energy-dependent
and there is no closed analytic probability formula in the
three-flavor case with
\delta_{\rm CP}\neq 0

[
Freund2001
]
.
At each
L/E
point we compute
E=L/(L/E)
with
L=1300
km,
numerically diagonalize
H_{\rm matter}
, and obtain the
full evolution operator
\mathcal{U}(L)=Ve^{-i\Lambda L}V^{\dagger}
.
Transition probabilities are then
P_{\alpha\beta}=|[\mathcal{U}]_{\beta\alpha}|^{2}
.
For antineutrinos,
A\to-A
and
U\to U^{*}

(equivalent to
\delta_{\rm CP}\to-\delta_{\rm CP}
),
so whichever ordering is resonance-enhanced for neutrinos
is suppressed for antineutrinos, and vice versa.

2.3
Entanglement Measure and Hierarchy Diagnostic

We treat the three flavor modes as qubits.
The initial state
|\nu_{e}\rangle=|100\rangle
evolves into
a superposition of all three flavor states as the neutrino propagates.
The linear entropy of flavor qubit
\alpha
is

S_{\alpha}=2P_{e\alpha}(1-P_{e\alpha})
,
ranging from 0 (no entanglement) to
1/2
(maximum entanglement).
The
global tripartite entanglement

is
[
11
]
:

E_{\rm global}=\frac{1}{3}\Bigl[2P_{ee}(1-P_{ee})+2P_{e\mu}(1-P_{e\mu})+2P_{e\tau}(1-P_{e\tau})\Bigr].

(5)

This is symmetric over all three flavor subsystems and vanishes
at
L=0
. It is maximized when
P_{e\alpha}=1/3
for all
\alpha

(maximum flavor-democratic mixing).

To quantify how well the two mass orderings can be told apart
through entanglement alone, we define the

hierarchy sensitivity diagnostic
:

\Delta S(L/E)=E_{\rm global}^{{\rm NO}}(L/E)-E_{\rm global}^{{\rm IO}}(L/E).

(6)

When
\Delta S>0
, NO produces more entanglement at that
L/E
.
When
\Delta S<0
, IO does. Zeros of
\Delta S
are blind spots.
The peak value of
|\Delta S|
indicates the best
L/E

at which a measurement of
E_{\rm global}
can distinguish the two orderings.

For the combined neutrino-antineutrino system, we define:

\Delta S_{+}=\Delta S_{\nu}+\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}},\qquad\Delta S_{-}=\Delta S_{\nu}-\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}}.

(7)

Since the matter effect is nearly antisymmetric between
neutrinos and antineutrinos,

\Delta S_{+}
largely cancels the matter-driven asymmetry
and exposes the CP signal,
while
\Delta S_{-}
largely cancels the CP contribution
and exposes the pure matter hierarchy signal.

4.1
Matter vs Vacuum Comparison

One of the results we have obtained is the elevated first peak
of the NO curve in matter at low
L/E
.
This is the MSW resonance.
The resonance condition for the dominant atmospheric-scale
oscillation is approximately

A\approx\Delta m^{2}_{31}\cos 2\theta_{13}/(2E)
,
satisfied for neutrinos in ordinary matter only when

\Delta m^{2}_{31}>0

Near the resonance energy (
E_{\rm res}\approx 10

corresponding to low
L/E
at DUNE’s 1300 km baseline),
the effective mixing angle
\theta_{13}^{\rm eff}
is
resonantly driven toward
45^{\circ}
,
greatly amplifying the
\nu_{e}\to\nu_{\tau}

The result is that the NO entanglement receives a strong
boost at low
L/E
in matter that has no equivalent for IO,
this happens because IO cannot satify the resonance condition for neutrinos.

Looking at the IO comparison in Figure

the vacuum and matter curves are almost indistinguishable.
The small differences at intermediate
L/E

off-resonance matter corrections, which are much weaker
than the resonance enhancement.
This asymmetric response — large matter effect for NO,
negligible for IO — is precisely why matter is so valuable
for hierarchy determination, and it is what our
diagnostic
\Delta S
is designed to capture.

The vacuum
\Delta S

\approx 0.06

The roughly twofold amplification is a direct consequence
of the MSW resonance lifting
E_{\rm global}^{{\rm NO}}
while

E_{\rm global}^{{\rm IO}}

causing the difference to approximately double at this
L/E

The optimal point aligns with DUNE.

The peak sensitivity at
L/E\approx 655

E_{\rm opt}=\frac{1300\ \rm km}{655\ \rm km/GeV}\approx 2\ \mathrm{GeV}

DUNE’s beam peaks at
\approx 2.5

The oscillatory structure of
\Delta S
is a quantum beating pattern.

\Delta S
oscillates and changes sign throughout the
L/E
range.
The oscillatory structure reflects interference between NO and IO frequencies.
The NO and IO entanglement curves oscillate with slightly
different effective frequencies because the sign of
\Delta m^{2}_{31}

shifts the oscillation phase differently in each case.
Their point-by-point difference
\Delta S
therefore exhibits
a modulated, oscillating envelope.
Matter sharpens this beating by making the effective NO and IO
oscillation frequencies more distinct near the resonance,
which is why the matter
\Delta S
has larger amplitude swings
than the vacuum
\Delta S
.
The zero-crossings of
\Delta S
mark the blind spots where
the two orderings produce identical entanglement
and measurement cannot distinguish them.
The peaks are the sweet spots.

\Delta S
in matter.

\delta_{\rm CP}
.

Interestingly,
\delta_{\rm CP}=180^{\circ}

This is because the CP-even term

0^{\circ}
or
90^{\circ}

This is a genuine matter-CP interference effect
and is worth highlighting: it means the choice of
\delta_{\rm CP}

Antineutrino Results and the
\Delta S_{+}
/
\Delta S_{-}
Decomposition

Figure
6
shows
\Delta S
for neutrinos (red solid)
and antineutrinos (blue dashed) in matter at
\delta_{\rm CP}=90^{\circ}
,
and Figure
7
shows the
\Delta S_{+}
/
\Delta S_{-}
decomposition.

The antineutrino
\Delta S
at low
L/E
is nearly a
mirror image of the neutrino
\Delta S
— it dips to

\approx-0.113
where the neutrino peaks at
\approx+0.113
.
This antisymmetry is the clearest possible signal of
the MSW resonance flip.
For antineutrinos
A\to-A
, so the resonance condition

A\approx\Delta m^{2}_{31}\cos 2\theta_{13}
is now satisfied by IO
(since
\Delta m^{2}_{31}<0
for IO and we need
A<0
).
IO is resonance-enhanced for antineutrinos exactly as NO is
for neutrinos.
The result is
E_{\rm global}^{{\rm IO}}>E_{\rm global}^{{\rm NO}}
for antineutrinos,
so
\Delta S
is negative — the hierarchy ordering of the
entanglement has flipped.
The amplitude is the same because the underlying resonance
physics is the same; only the sign flips.

The anti symmetry is not mathematically exact or accurate.
If it were, the neutrino and antineutrino cases would be
related by a pure
{\rm NO}\leftrightarrow{\rm IO}
swap and the
curves would be exactly
\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}}=-\Delta S_{\nu}
.
The small but visible deviations from this at low
L/E

are the fingerprint of
\delta_{\rm CP}\neq 0
.
When
\delta_{\rm CP}=0^{\circ}
the PMNS matrix is real and the
symmetry
U\to U^{*}=U
holds exactly,
giving a perfectly antisymmetric pair.
For
\delta_{\rm CP}=90^{\circ}
, the conjugation
U\to U^{*}
effectively
sends
\delta_{\rm CP}\to-\delta_{\rm CP}
, introducing a CP-driven asymmetry
in the oscillation probabilities that breaks the exact mirror
relation between
\Delta S_{\nu}
and
\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}}
.
The magnitude of this breaking is a direct measure of
\delta_{\rm CP}
.

At intermediate
L/E
(roughly 5000–25000 km/GeV) the
neutrino and antineutrino
\Delta S
curves oscillate with
different phases and amplitudes,
reflecting the combined effect of the opposite matter potential
and the conjugated CP phase.
Neither curve is a simple rescaling of the other in this range.

\Delta S

.

The
\Delta S_{+}
/
\Delta S_{-}
decomposition in Figure

extracts these two signals cleanly.

\Delta S_{+}=\Delta S_{\nu}+\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}}
(matter signal, blue) is nearly zero
at low
L/E
.
This is expected: the neutrino matter effect (lifting NO)
and the antineutrino matter effect (lifting IO)
are nearly equal and opposite, so they cancel in the sum.
The fact that
\Delta S_{+}
is not exactly zero at low
L/E

confirms the CP-driven breaking of the exact antisymmetry.

\Delta S_{+}
grows and becomes non-negligible at intermediate
L/E
,
reaching amplitudes of
\approx 0.025
–
0.035
.
This is the region where the imperfect cancellation between
the neutrino and antineutrino matter effects becomes visible,
and where
\Delta S_{+}
provides information about both the hierarchy
and
\delta_{\rm CP}
simultaneously.

\Delta S_{-}=\Delta S_{\nu}-\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}}
(CP asymmetry, orange dashed)
is dominant throughout. At low
L/E
, since

\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}}\approx-\Delta S_{\nu}
, we have
\Delta S_{-}\approx 2\Delta S_{\nu}
,
which explains why the orange curve has amplitude

\approx 2\times
the individual neutrino
\Delta S
.

\Delta S_{-}
is the quantity most sensitive to the hierarchy
at the MSW resonance peak, and it is large wherever
the neutrino
\Delta S
is large.

The key experimental implication is that
\Delta S_{-}
and
\Delta S_{+}

have complementary
L/E
dependences.
A DUNE measurement at
E\approx 2
GeV (low
L/E
)
primarily probes the CP asymmetry through
\Delta S_{-}
.
Measurements at higher
L/E
(lower energies) provide
access to the pure matter hierarchy signal through
\Delta S_{+}
.
Running DUNE in both neutrino and antineutrino modes and
forming these combinations would allow the two effects
to be disentangled within the tripartite entanglement framework.

Amplitude scaling with NSI.

Positive

enhances the effective matter

V_{ee}=A(1+\varepsilon_{ee})
, deepening the
MSW resonance asymmetry between NO and IO.
At
\varepsilon_{ee}=+0.5

At
\varepsilon_{ee}=-0.5

The response is clearly asymmetric: doubling the potential
approximately doubles the peak
\Delta S

while halving it reduces the peak by a factor of
\approx 4
.
This asymmetry arises from the nonlinearity of the resonance
condition — positive NSI sharpens the resonance while
negative NSI broadens and eventually eliminates it.

Linear scaling of
|\Delta S|_{\rm max}
.

The right panel of Figure

shows that

|\Delta S|_{\rm max}
increases linearly with
\varepsilon_{ee}
:

|\Delta S|_{\rm max}\approx 0.113+0.105\,\varepsilon_{ee}.

(9)

The slope is
\approx 0.105
per unit
\varepsilon_{ee}
,
meaning every increase of
\Delta\varepsilon_{ee}=0.1

boosts the maximum hierarchy sensitivity by approximately
0.0105
.
More remarkably, all three CP phases (
0^{\circ}
,
90^{\circ}
,
180^{\circ}
)
fall on exactly the same line.
The NSI-driven enhancement of
|\Delta S|_{\rm max}
is
completely independent of the CP-violating phase.

if DUNE measures
\varepsilon_{ee}\neq 0
through
dedicated NSI analyses,
the shift in the maximum entanglement-based hierarchy
sensitivity is given by
0.105\,\varepsilon_{ee}
,
regardless of the simultaneously measured value of
\delta_{\rm CP}
.

7
Conclusions

We have studied global tripartite quantum entanglement in
three-flavor neutrino oscillations as a probe of the
mass hierarchy and CP violation,
extending previous work to include the CP-phase dependence,
systematic NO/IO comparison, matter effects,
antineutrinos, and non-standard interactions simultaneously.

Our main findings are:

1.

In vacuum, the global entanglement
E_{\rm global}
rises from zero,
forms a broad plateau at
\approx 0.43
–
0.45
,
and falls at large
L/E
.
The NO/IO splitting follows
|\sin\delta_{\rm CP}|
,
vanishing at
\delta_{\rm CP}=0^{\circ}
and
180^{\circ}
and
reaching its maximum at
\delta_{\rm CP}=90^{\circ}
.

2.

Matter effects (MSW) resonantly amplify the NO entanglement
while leaving IO nearly unchanged.
The hierarchy sensitivity diagnostic
\Delta S
peaks at

|\Delta S|_{\rm max}\approx 0.113
at
L/E\approx 655
km/GeV in matter,
roughly twice the vacuum value.
This optimal point corresponds to
E\approx 2
GeV at the
DUNE baseline, directly within DUNE’s operating energy range.

3.

For antineutrinos,
\Delta S
is near-perfectly antisymmetric
to the neutrino case at the MSW resonance.
The deviation from exact antisymmetry encodes
\delta_{\rm CP}

and grows with its magnitude.

4.

The decomposition
\Delta S_{+}=\Delta S_{\nu}+\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}}
and

\Delta S_{-}=\Delta S_{\nu}-\Delta S_{\bar{\nu}}
separates the matter hierarchy
signal from the CP asymmetry signal in the tripartite entanglement,
with complementary
L/E
sensitivity ranges.

5.

NSI modifies
|\Delta S|_{\rm max}
linearly:

|\Delta S|_{\rm max}\approx 0.113+0.105\,\varepsilon_{ee}
,
independently of
\delta_{\rm CP}
.
The optimal
L/E
for hierarchy discrimination is stable
at
\approx 655
km/GeV for all
|\varepsilon_{ee}|\leq 0.2
.
