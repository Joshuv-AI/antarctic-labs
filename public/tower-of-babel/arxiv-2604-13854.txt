A proof of p p -adic Gross–Zagier theorem via BDP formula
Kâzım Büyükboduk, Peter Neamti

Abstract
. This paper provides a new proof of the p p -adic Gross–Zagier formula for the p p -adic L L -function associated with the base change of a normalised cuspidal eigen-newform f f of weight k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 (and families of such) to an imaginary quadratic field K K . Our results encompass both the classical p p -ordinary cases and non-ordinary scenarios, including new cases where k > 2 k>2 and ord p ​ ( a p ​ ( f ) ) > 0 \mathrm{ord}_{p}(a_{p}(f))>0 . Unlike the traditional approach of comparing geometric and analytic kernels, we employ a “wall-crossing” strategy centred on the BDP formula and the theory of Beilinson–Flach elements.

K(p^{\infty}):=\varinjlim K(p^{n}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{?}^{\circ}:=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{?}/K),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{q}}:=\operatorname{Gal}(K^{(\mathfrak{q})}_{\infty}/K),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\circ}:=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K).

Let

Let

\Gamma^{\circ}=\Gamma^{+}\times\Gamma^{-}

where

(resp.

(resp.
\Gamma^{\circ}_{\textup{ac}}

[
10
]
, we fix topological generators

for

. Moreover we require that
\gamma_{+}\mapsto\gamma_{\mathfrak{q}}^{p^{h_{p}}/2}
, and
\gamma_{-}\mapsto\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1/2}

For

-adically complete

-algebra (most commonly for us, the ring of integers of some finite extension of

\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}(\Gamma^{\circ}_{?}):=\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma^{\circ}_{?}\rrbracket.

Unless there is danger of confusion, we will often drop the

from the notation and just write

. If

is some finite extension of

, let us also set

\mathcal{H}_{E}:=\{f(X)\in E[[X]]:f(X)\text{ converges on ann}(0,1)\}

where

and define, for

If

The canonical map
\Gamma^{+}\times\Gamma^{-}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma^{\circ}
gives the isomorphism

(2.6)

\Lambda(\Gamma^{+}){\,\widehat{\otimes}\,}\Lambda(\Gamma^{-})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda(\Gamma^{\circ}).

For

or

and hence gives the isomorphism

\Lambda(\Gamma^{\circ})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda(\Gamma^{\circ}_{\star}){\,\widehat{\otimes}\,}\Lambda(\Gamma^{\circ}_{\bullet}).

By our choices of generators, we see that the isomorphism obtained by composing (
2.6

\begin{cases}\gamma_{+}\mapsto\gamma_{\rm cyc},&\gamma_{-}\mapsto\gamma_{\rm ac},\ \ \ \ \text{if }(\star,\bullet)=(\rm cyc,\rm ac)\\
\gamma_{+}\mapsto(\gamma_{\rm cyc},\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p^{h_{p}}/2}),&\gamma_{-}\mapsto\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1/2},\ \ \text{if }(\star,\bullet)=(\rm cyc,\mathfrak{p}).\end{cases}

For

\Gamma_{?}\cong\Gamma^{\circ}_{?}\times\Delta_{?},

where

is a finite abelian group, and hence we have the decomposition

(2.8)

\Lambda(\Gamma_{?})\cong\bigoplus_{\psi\in\widehat{\Delta}_{?}}\Lambda_{\psi}(\Gamma^{\circ}_{?}):=\bigoplus_{\psi\in\widehat{\Delta}_{?}}\Lambda(\Gamma_{?}^{\circ})\cdot e_{\psi},

where
\widehat{\Delta}_{?}:=\mathrm{Hom}(\Delta_{?},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
and

is the idempotent associated to
\psi

In what follows, it will be understood that

\Delta_{?}
whenever it appears in the expression

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose

2.3.1.
Weight space

Let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers of a finite extension
E
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(which we shall enlarge as our purposes require). Let us denote by

the tautological injection, where
\Lambda_{\rm wt}:=\Lambda(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})
. We define the universal weight character
\bbchi
as the composite map
G_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{\chi_{\textup{cyc}}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\hookrightarrow\Lambda_{\rm wt}^{\times},
where
\chi_{\textup{cyc}}
is the cyclotomic character giving the Galois action on the
p
-power roots of unity.

We regard integers as elements of the weight space

via

\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{{\rm wt}},\mathcal{O})=\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{O})\,,\qquad n\mapsto(\nu_{n}\colon[x]\mapsto x^{n}).

We call a ring homomorphism

an arithmetic specialisation of weight

if
\nu
agrees with

on a finite-index subgroup of
[\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}]
.

For an integer

, we define

as the component determined by the weight

.

2.4.
Hida families

We let

and nebentype character

of weight

We let

2.4.1.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of places of

that contains all those that divide

, as well as the archimedean prime. By the fundamental work of Hida, there exists a unique Galois representation

attached to

(cf. §

below). We denote by

the Ohta lattice. More precisely,

corresponds to

in the notation of
[
31

, which realises the Galois representation

in the étale cohomology groups of a tower of modular curves.

2.4.2.

Let us consider the following conditions on

.

Irr
)

The residual

-representation

Dist

We remark that (a) ensures that any

[

2.5.
Coleman families

Our approach to the proof of
p
-adic Gross–Zagier theorems applies equally well also for eigenforms of positive slope, at the expense of working with non-ordinary components (i.e. Coleman families) of the eigencurve. In this subsection, we briefly review the basic definitions and properties of such families. We remark that, if one restricts a Hida family
h
as above to a wide open disc
U_{\textup{\bf h}}
as in §
2.4.4
, the discussion in §
2.5
recovers that in §
2.4
away from the neighbourhoods of non-étale points on the eigencurve.

2.5.1.

For each
r=p^{v}<p^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}
with
v=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(e)
for some

, we let
B(r)\subset\mathcal{W}
denote the closed affinoid disc about
k
of radius

. Let

\mathscr{A}(r)\simeq E\left\langle\left\langle\frac{X-k}{e}\right\rangle\right\rangle:=\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}\left(\frac{X-k}{e}\right)^{n}\in E\left[\left[\frac{X-k}{e}\right]\right]:\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}c_{n}=0\right\}\subset\Lambda_{k,r}[1/p]

denote the Noetherian ring of
E
-valued analytic functions on
B(r)
, where
\Lambda_{(k,r)}:=\mathcal{O}_{L}\left[\left[\frac{X-k}{e}\right]\right]
.

2.5.2.

Let

be a newform of weight

and level

. Assume either that
\gcd(N^{\circ},p)=1
, or else
p||N^{\circ}
and
p\nmid{\rm cond}(\varepsilon)
. Let
h^{\lambda}=h
be its

-stabilisation with
U_{p}
-eigenvalue

(if

is divisible by

, then

and

). Suppose also that

is non-

-critical in the sense of Coleman (cf.
[
2
]
, Definition 2.12).

For a sufficiently small number

as above, there exists a unique Coleman family
h
(up to Galois conjugation) over the affinoid disc
B(r_{0})
of fixed slope
v(\lambda):=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(\lambda)
. Let us fix henceforth
r<r_{0}
as above, and put
\Lambda_{\textup{\bf h}}:=\Lambda_{(k,r)}[1/p]
. We may then consider
h
as a family over the wide open disc
U_{\textup{\bf h}}:={\rm Spm}(\Lambda_{\textup{\bf h}})
. That means,
h
admits a formal

\textup{\bf h}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{a}_{n}(\textup{\bf h})q^{n}\,\in\,\Lambda_{\textup{\bf h}}[[q]]\,.

Let
\bbalpha:=\mathbb{a}_{p}(\textup{\bf h})\in\Lambda_{\textup{\bf h}}
denote the eigenvalue with which

acts on

. We define the tame level of the Coleman family as
N_{\textup{\bf h}}:=N^{\circ}/\gcd(N^{\circ},p)
and its tame nebentype
\varepsilon_{\textup{\bf h}}:=\varepsilon
(which has conductor dividing

).

2.5.3.

We let
B(r)_{\rm cl}:=\mathbb{N}\cap B(r)
denote the set of classical points in the affinoid disc

. An element
\kappa\in B(r)_{\rm cl}\subset U_{\textup{\bf h}}(E)={\rm Hom}(\Lambda_{\textup{\bf h}},E)
is called a classical specialisation, and the corresponding ring homomorphism

is denoted by

. For such

, let us fix a generator

of

. We then have

\textup{\bf h}(\kappa):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{a}_{n}(\textup{\bf h})_{|_{\kappa}}\,q^{n}\in S_{\kappa+2}(\Gamma_{1}(N_{\textup{\bf h}})\cap\Gamma_{0}(p))\,.

We call
\textup{\bf h}(\kappa)
the specialization of

of weight

, one can ensure for
k\neq\kappa\in B^{\circ}(r)_{\rm cl}
that the

\textup{\bf h}(\kappa)
is
p
-old and it arises as the
p
-stabilisation of a newform
\textup{\bf h}(\kappa)^{\circ}\in S_{\kappa}(\Gamma_{1}(N_{\textup{\bf h}}))
with

-eigenvalue
\bbalpha(\kappa)=\mathbb{a}_{p}(\textup{\bf h})_{|_{\kappa}}
.

2.5.4.

As explained in
[

, there exists a free
\Lambda_{\textup{\bf h}}
-module

of rank

is denoted by

in op. cit., whereas our

As in §

, let us denote by
\textup{\bf h}^{c}:=\textup{\bf h}\otimes\varepsilon_{\textup{\bf h}}^{-1}

(

2.5.6.
Self-dual families of Galois representations

In the situation of §

In other words,

3.
Selmer complexes and height pairings

In this section, we review the general theory of Selmer complexes (due to Nekovář and Pottharst; cf.
[
47
,
6
]
), following closely
[
5
, §1.2]
.

Let
F
be an algebraic number field and let
S_{p}
(resp.,
S_{\infty}
) denote the set of primes of
F
above
p
(resp., archimedean places of
F
). Let us fix a finite set of places
S
of
F
containing
S_{p}
. We denote by
G_{F,S}
the Galois group of the maximal algebraic extension of
F
unramified outside
S\cup S_{\infty}
. Let
\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Spm}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}})
be an affinoid over
E
. We fix a continuous
G_{F,S}
-representation
V
with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
. In application,
\mathfrak{X}
will be chosen as
\mathscr{A}(r)
as in §
2.5.1
. When
\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Spm}(E)
is a singleton, we fix a
G_{F,S}
-stable
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-lattice
T\subset V
.

3.1.
Unramified local conditions

Let
\lambda\in S\setminus S_{p}
be a prime. Let
I_{\lambda}\subset G_{F_{\lambda}}
denote the inertia subgroup and
{\rm Fr}_{\lambda}\in G_{F_{\lambda}}/I_{\lambda}
the arithmetic Frobenius. The definition of Selmer complexes will require the following versions of unramified local conditions:

3.1.1.

Nekovář’s unramified local conditions are determined in terms of the complex

C_{\textup{ur}}^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\lambda}},V)=\left[V^{I_{\lambda}}\xrightarrow{{\rm Fr}_{\lambda}-1}V^{I_{\lambda}}\right],

where the terms are concentrated in degrees
0
and
1
. When
\mathcal{O}_{X}=E
, we similarly define
C_{\textup{ur}}^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\lambda}},T)
. We let
\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\textup{ur}}(F_{\lambda},V)
(resp.
\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\textup{ur}}(F_{\lambda},T)
when
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}=E
) denote the
corresponding complex in the derived category
\mathscr{D}_{\textrm{ft}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}})
(resp. in
\mathscr{D}_{\textrm{ft}}(\mathcal{O}_{E})
if
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}=E
). These complexes are equipped with a natural map

g_{\lambda}\,:\,C_{\textup{ur}}^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\lambda}},M)\longrightarrow C^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\lambda}},M)\,,\qquad M=V,\,T\,.

We then have

\mathbf{R}^{0}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\textup{ur}}(F_{\lambda},M)\simeq H^{0}(F_{\lambda},M),\qquad\mathbf{R}^{1}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\textup{ur}}(F_{\lambda},M)\simeq\ker\left(H^{1}(F_{\lambda},M)\rightarrow H^{1}(I_{\lambda},M)\right).

On the level of cohomology, the map
g_{\lambda}
induces the isomorphism
\mathbf{R}^{0}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\textup{ur}}(F_{\lambda},M)\simeq H^{0}(F_{\lambda},M)
and the natural inclusion
\mathbf{R}^{1}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\textup{ur}}(F_{\lambda},M)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\lambda},M)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Nekovář also defines Greenberg local conditions for a Galois representation
T
over a complete local Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra.
\triangle

3.1.2.

When
\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Spm}(E)
is a singleton, these are supplemented by the Bloch–Kato conditions:

H^{1}_{\rm f}(F_{\lambda},M):=\begin{cases}\mathbf{R}^{1}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\textup{ur}}(F_{\lambda},V),&\qquad\textrm{if $M=V$},\\
\mathrm{im}\left(H^{1}_{\rm f}(F_{\lambda},V)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\lambda},V/T)\right),&\qquad\textrm{if $M=V/T$},\\
\textrm{$\ker\left(H^{1}(F_{\lambda},T)\longrightarrow\dfrac{H^{1}(F_{\lambda},V)}{H^{1}_{\rm f}(F_{\lambda},V)}\right)$},&\qquad\textrm{if $M=T$}.\end{cases}

We remark that
H^{1}_{\rm f}(F_{\lambda},M)
need not agree with
\mathbf{R}^{1}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\textup{ur}}(F_{\lambda},M)
for
M=T
,
V/T
: the difference is accounted by Tamagawa numbers.

3.2.
Greenberg local conditions

We introduce local conditions at primes
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}
that we shall work with.

3.2.1.
Triangulations

We let
C^{\bullet}_{\varphi,\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig},\mathfrak{X}}(V))
denote the Fontaine–Herr complex of the
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module
\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig},\mathfrak{X}}(V_{|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}})
(over the relative Robba ring
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{X}}
) associated to the
G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-representation
V
, given as in
[
6
, §2.4]
. We note for the convenience of the reader that
K
in op. cit. coincides with our
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
and Benois writes
\gamma_{K}\in\gamma_{\rm cyc}
in place of our
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Recall from
[
6
, §2.5]
that there exists a complex
K^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{p}}(V)
of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-modules
together with quasi-isomorphisms

(3.1)

C^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}},V)\xrightarrow{\xi_{\mathfrak{p}}}K^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{p}}(V)\xleftarrow{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}C^{\bullet}_{\varphi,\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig},\mathfrak{X}}(V))\,.

Let
\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig},\mathfrak{X}}(V_{|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}})
be a saturated
(\varphi,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}})
-submodule. Such a choice of
\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is called a triangulation, and it defines the local condition

g_{\mathfrak{p}}\,:\,C^{\bullet}_{\varphi,\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\,\,\iota_{\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\,\,}}{{\longrightarrow}}C^{\bullet}_{\varphi,\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig},\mathfrak{X}}(V))\xrightarrow{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}K^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{p}}(V).

On the level of cohomology,
g_{\mathfrak{p}}
induces natural maps

H^{i}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}})\longrightarrow H^{i}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig},\mathfrak{X}}(V_{|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}}))\simeq H^{i}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},V)\,.

3.2.2.
Étale triangulations

An important special case is the scenario when the
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module
\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig},\mathfrak{X}}(Z_{\mathfrak{p}})
for some
G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-submodule
Z_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset V_{|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}}
. These then recover the classical Greenberg local conditions, which are defined by the natural map

g_{\mathfrak{p}}\,:\,C^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}},Z_{\mathfrak{p}})\longrightarrow C^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}},V)\,.

On the level of cohomology,
g_{\mathfrak{p}}
induces a natural morphism

H^{i}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},Z_{\mathfrak{p}})\longrightarrow H^{i}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},V)\,.

When
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}=E
and
T
is as in §
3.1.1
, one may equally consider Greenberg local conditions on
T
associated with a
G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-submodule
Z_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset T
. Moreover, in this situation (as in the case of unramified local conditions), one may (and we will) define Greenberg local conditions for a Galois representation
T
with coefficients in a complete local Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra.

3.2.3.
Bloch–Kato conditions at
S_{p}
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Spm}(E)
. Following Bloch and Kato, we put

H^{1}_{\rm f}({\mathbb{Q}_{p}},M):=\begin{cases}\ker\left(H^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},V)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\mathbf{B}_{\textrm{cris}})\right),&\quad\textrm{if $M=V$},\\
\mathrm{im}\left(H^{1}_{\rm f}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},V)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},V/T)\right),&\quad\textrm{if $M=V/T$},\\
\textrm{$\ker\left(H^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},T)\longrightarrow\dfrac{H^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},V)}{H^{1}_{\rm f}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},V)}\right)$},&\quad\textrm{if $M=T$}.\end{cases}

3.3.
Selmer complexes

In this paper, we will work with Selmer complexes with coefficients in
V
, defined by both étale (in the
p
-ordinary setting) and non-étale (in the non-
p
-ordinary case) Greenberg local conditions. We revisit their general constructions following
[
6
]
.

3.3.1.

As above, let
V
be a
G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-representation over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
. We write
\mathbf{R\Gamma}(V)
(resp.,
\mathbf{R\Gamma}(F_{\lambda},V)
) for the complexes of continuous cochains of
G_{F,S}
(resp. of
G_{F_{\lambda}}
) with coefficients in

. We let

\textup{res}_{S}:=(\textup{res}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in S}\,:\,C^{\bullet}(G_{F,S},V)\xrightarrow{(\textup{res}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in S}}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in S}C^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\lambda}},V)

denote the restriction map. For any
\lambda\in S\setminus S_{p}
, let us put
K^{\bullet}_{\lambda}(V)=C^{\bullet}(G_{F_{\lambda}},V)
and define
K^{\bullet}(V):=\underset{\lambda\in S}{\bigoplus}K^{\bullet}_{\lambda}(V)
. For
\lambda\in S
, we let
f_{\lambda}
denote the map

f_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}\textup{res}_{\lambda}&\text{if $\lambda\in S\setminus S_{p}$},\\
\xi_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ\textup{res}_{\mathfrak{p}}&\text{if $\lambda=\mathfrak{p}\mid p,$}\end{cases}

where
\xi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the map that appeared in (
3.1
). We then have the following morphism of complexes:

f_{S}=(f_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in S}\,:\,C^{\bullet}(G_{F,S},V)\longrightarrow K^{\bullet}(V)\,.

3.3.2.

Given triangulations
\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig},\mathfrak{X}}(V_{|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}})
(étale or non-étale) for each
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}
, we put
\mathbf{D}=\{\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\}_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}}
. We associate to
\mathbf{D}
the collection
\left\{U_{\lambda}^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D}),g_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in S}
of local conditions given as follows:

U_{\lambda}^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D}):=\begin{cases}C^{\bullet}_{\textrm{ur}}(G_{F_{\lambda}},V),&\quad\lambda\in S\setminus S_{p}\,,\\
C^{\bullet}_{\varphi,\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}),&\lambda=\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}.\end{cases}

Let us put
U^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D}):=\oplus_{\lambda\in S}U^{\bullet}_{\lambda}(V,\mathbf{D})
and
g_{S}:\sum_{\lambda\in S}g_{\lambda}
.

The Selmer complex associated to local conditions
\left(U^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D}),g_{S}\right)
is defined as the mapping cone

S^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D}):=\mathrm{cone}\left[C^{\bullet}(G_{F,S},V)\oplus U^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D})\xrightarrow{f_{S}-g_{S}}K^{\bullet}(V)\right][-1].

We denote by
\mathbf{R\Gamma}(V,\mathbf{D})=[S^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D})]
the corresponding object in the category of bounded perfect complexes of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-modules (see
[
6
, §3]
for details). We write
H^{i}(V,\mathbf{D})
for the cohomology of
S^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D})
. Note that
H^{i}(V,\mathbf{D})=0
for
i\geqslant 4
.

3.3.3.

In the setting of §
3.3.2
, we have a distinguished triangle

K^{\bullet}(V)\longrightarrow S^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D})[1]\longrightarrow\bigl(C^{\bullet}(G_{F,S},V)\oplus U^{\bullet}(V,\mathbf{D})\bigr)[1]\longrightarrow K^{\bullet}(V)[1]\,,

which induces the long exact sequence (cf.
[
47
]
, §0.8.0)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H^{0}(F,V)\longrightarrow&\underset{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}}{\bigoplus}H^{0}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbf{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-})\longrightarrow H^{1}(V,\mathbf{D})\\
&\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{F,S},V)\longrightarrow\underset{\lambda\in S\setminus S_{p}}{\bigoplus}\frac{H^{1}(F_{\lambda},V)}{H^{1}_{\rm f}(F_{\lambda},V)}\oplus\underset{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}}{\bigoplus}H^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\mathfrak{p}})\,,\end{aligned}

where

Theorem 3.2

(Rubin’s formula)
