Wavefront sets for genuine representations of GL \rm GL -covers of Kazhdan–Patterson or Savin types
Fan Gao, Runze Wang, Jiandi Zou

Abstract
. First, we consider general Brylinski–Deligne covers of the p p -adic general linear groups, and discuss the theory of Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives. We also recall the Zelevinsky-type classification of the irreducible genuine spectrum for the Kazhdan–Patterson and Savin covers. Following this, for these two special families of covers, we determine the wavefront sets of their irreducible genuine representations, expressed in terms of the iterated degrees of the highest Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives. Finally, for Kazhdan–Patterson covers, we reinterpret this result on the wavefront set using a version of the local Langlands correspondence and the covering Barbasch–Vogan duality.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbbm{Q}_{p}
. (In fact, we can also take
F
to be a local field of sufficiently large characteristic
p
.) We consider the general linear group
{\bf G}_{r}:={\rm GL}_{r}
and its
F
-points
G_{r}={\bf G}_{r}(F)
. The theory of Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives, as developed in
[
2
,
3
,
41
]
, has been an indispensable tool in understanding irreducible admissible representations of
G_{r}
. Coupled with the Zelevsinky classification of the irreducible spectrum
{\rm Irr}(G_{r})
in terms of the multisegments, the theory provides many insights on questions regarding the structure of induced representations, the branching problem, the degenerate Whittaker models, etc., to name a few; see
[
30
,
6
,
20
,
18
]
and references therein.

For any
\pi\in{\rm Irr}(G_{r})
, one important representation-theoretic invariant associated with
\pi
is its wavefront set
{\rm WF}(\pi)
. It gives the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of
\pi
, which measures the “size” of
\pi
in an asymptotic way, see for instance
[
36
,
11
]
. The intimate relation between the theory of Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives and that of Whittaker models enables one to determine
{\rm WF}(\pi)
in terms of the multisegment structure
\mathfrak{m}(\pi)
underlying
\pi
(i.e., the Zelevinsky classification of
\pi
), and one has

(1.1)

{\rm WF}(\pi)=\left\{\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}(\pi)}\right\},

where
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}(\pi)}
is a partition of
r
arising from the (iterated) Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives of
\pi
. On the other hand, in view of the local Langlands correspondence for
G_{r}
, one has

(1.2)

{\rm WF}(\pi)=\left\{d_{BV,G_{r}}(\mathcal{O}(\phi_{{\rm AZ}(\pi)}))\right\}.

Here,
{\rm AZ}(\pi)
is the Aubert–Zelevinsky dual of
\pi
,
\phi_{{\rm AZ}(\pi)}
is its L-parameter, and
d_{BV,G_{r}}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p}^{\top}
is the Barbasch–Vogan duality (just the transpose map for
G_{r}
). The equalities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) were proved in
[
34
]
. In fact, for general linear algebraic group
G
, there has been an abundance of literature regarding
{\rm WF}(\pi)
for
\pi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
, see for example
[
34
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
24
,
25
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we escalate to consider degree-
n
central covering group
\overline{G_{r}}
of
G_{r}
, which sits in a short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{G_{r}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{r}}

and arises from the Brylinski–Deligne (BD) framework
[
4
]
. We call
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
an irreducible (
\epsilon
-)genuine representation if
\mu_{n}
acts via a fixed embedding
\epsilon:\mu_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
. We are motivated from (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) above, and the goal is to establish similar equalities for
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
. Note that this is not a task to be accomplished mutatis mutandis. Indeed, it is well-known that genuine representations of
\overline{G_{r}}
tend to possess larger wavefront set and thus bigger Gelfand–Kirillov dimension. This implies in particular that the left-hand sides of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) tend to be bigger for genuine
\pi
than its linear counterpart.

We elaborate on the issues involved and also state our main result below.

1.2.
Main result

First, the basic mechanism and principles underlying the Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives can be applied to any BD covering group
\overline{G_{r}}
. However, for a general BD covering group of
\overline{G_{r}}
, the potential non-commutativity of blocks of a covering Levi subgroup posits technical issues with such an application. Thus, starting from Section
4
we restrict to the Savin (S) covers and the Kazhdan–Patterson (KP) covers of
G_{r}
. For Savin covers of
G_{r}
, the blocks of a covering Levi subgroup do commute from its structure de facto; for KP covers, one can utilize the metaplectic tensor product construction (see
[
32
,
37
,
27
]
). In fact, we consider certain extended metaplectic tensor product in this paper, which seems to be more convenient and robust in order to adapt the Bernstein–Zelevinsky theory for the Savin covers or Kazhdan–Patterson covers.

For
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
, in order to compute
{\rm WF}(\pi)
by determining
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}(\pi)}
as in the linear case for
G_{r}
, it is certainly a prerequisite to have a Zelevinsky-type classification for
{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
. Such a classification is given in the earlier work of Kaplan–Lapid–Zou
[
27
]
for Kazhdan–Patterson covers. It was already noted in loc. cit. that the classification holds for Savin covers as well. We mention in passing that for general BD cover of
G_{r}
, a Zelevinsky-type construction is not sufficient to describe the whole of
{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
. It is expected that there exist essential extra difficulties besides the non-commutativity issue. For instance, the parabolic induction of a unitary cuspidal representation might not be irreducible anymore for general BD cover of
G_{r}
. In any case, this entails a second reason why we restrict to the Savin covers and Kazhdan–Patterson covers, for which one has
\pi=Z(\mathfrak{m})
with
\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{m}(\pi)
a multisegment for
\overline{G_{r}}
.

For
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
, we write
\pi^{(k)}
for the highest Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivative of
\pi
, for some
k\in\mathbbm{N}_{\geqslant 1}
. Then we obtain iteratively a partition
\lambda_{\pi}:=(k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{s})
arising from the iterated highest BZ derivatives, see §
5
for a detailed description. For Savin covers or Kazhdan–Patterson covers, one has
\pi=Z(\mathfrak{m})
as mentioned above, and it follows from Theorem
5.1
that
\lambda_{\pi}
can be computed from
\mathfrak{m}
in an explicit way; thus we write
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\lambda_{\pi}
. This also gives us our main result of the paper, which is an extension of (
1.1
):

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
6.2
)
.

Let
\overline{G_{r}}
be an
n
-fold cover of
G_{r}
of Savin type or Kazhdan–Patterson type. Assume
p\nmid n
. Then for every
Z(\mathfrak{m})\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
, one has

{\rm WF}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))=\left\{\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\right\}.

In particular, this shows that for Savin or Kazhdan–Patterson covers, if
n_{\alpha}\geqslant r
, then every
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
is generic, i.e., it possesses Whittaker models. Here
n_{\alpha}=n
for KP-covers and
n_{\alpha}=n/\gcd(n,2)
for S-covers. We also note that the equality in Theorem
1.1
has been verified for Iwahori-spherical representations with positive Satake parameter, for arbitrary BD cover
\overline{G_{r}}
, see
[
16
]
.

Clearly, an extension of (
1.2
) to covering
\overline{G_{r}}
is contingent on its local Langlands correspondence, which has not been established in general. Moreover, one needs to replace
d_{BV,G_{r}}
by the covering Barbasch–Vogan duality
d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}
, defined and studied in
[
15
]
.
In the last part of this paper, we try to reconcile Theorem
1.1
with a form generalizing (
1.2
) to covers, using
d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}
.
For this purpose, we further confine to Kazhdan–Patterson cover, and in this case we can define the L-parameter
\tilde{\phi}_{\pi}
of any
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
by utilizing the metaplectic correspondence between a KP-cover
\overline{G_{r}}
and
G_{r}
established for discrete series in
[
13
]
(see also
[
42
]
), and the Zelevinsky classification for
\overline{G_{r}}
as in
[
27
]
. Granted with these, we show in Corollary
6.4
that

{\rm WF}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))=\left\{d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})}))\right\},

where
L(\mathfrak{m})
is just the Aubert–Zelevinsky dual of
Z(\mathfrak{m})
. This is the sought generalization of (
1.2
) to
\overline{G_{r}}
mentioned above, and is also an improvement of
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
.

2.
Extended metaplectic tensor product and Whittaker models

2.1.
Covers of
G_{r}

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field with discrete valuation
|\cdot|_{F}
and residual characteristic
p
. Starting from §
6
, we further require that
F
is of characteristic 0. We fix a positive integer
n
, such that the subgroup of
n
-th roots of unity in
F^{\times}
, denoted by
\mu_{n}
, is of cardinality
n
. Starting from §
4
, we assume that we are in the tame case, meaning that
n
is coprime to
p
.

2.1.1.
Finite central covers

By an
n
-fold cover of an
\ell
-group
G
, we mean a central extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}

of
G
by
\mu_{n}
as topological groups. Let
{\bf p}:\overline{G}\rightarrow G
be the natural projection. A section
{\bf s}:G\rightarrow\overline{G}
is a continuous map such that
{\bf p}\circ{\bf s}={\rm id}
.

All representations in this article are smooth complex representations. We fix a faithful character
\epsilon:\mu_{n}\rightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
. For an
n
-fold cover
\overline{H}
of an
\ell
-group
H
, we consider (
\epsilon
-)genuine representations of
\overline{H}
, meaning that those representations with the
\mu_{n}
-action given by the multiplication by
\epsilon
. For an
\epsilon
-genuine representation
\rho
of
\overline{H}
, its contragredient
\rho^{\vee}
is an
\epsilon^{-1}
-genuine representation.

The commutator
\overline{G}\times\overline{G}\rightarrow\overline{G}
factors through
G\times G
, and we denote by

[\cdot,\cdot]:G\times G\rightarrow\overline{G}

the resulting map. In particular, given two commuting elements
h_{1},h_{2}
in
G
, we have
[h_{1},h_{2}]={\bf s}(h_{1}){\bf s}(h_{2}){\bf s}(h_{1})^{-1}{\bf s}(h_{2})^{-1}\in\mu_{n}
, where
{\bf s}
is any section of
G
.

A splitting of a closed subgroup
H
of
G
is a section as well as a group homomorphism
{\bf s}:H\rightarrow\overline{H}
. Once we fix a (sometimes, canonical) choice of
{\bf s}
, we identify
H
with the image
{\bf s}(H)
as a subgroup of
\overline{H}
. One typical case is that when
\gcd(p,n)=1
, there exists a unique splitting of a pro-
p
-subgroup
H
, thus we may canonically regard
H
as a subgroup of
\overline{G}
. Another typical example is that for a reductive group
G
, there exists a unique section
{\bf s}
on the set of unipotent elements of
G
such that it is invariant up to
G
-conjugacy and its restriction to any unipotent subgroup
N
of
G
is a splitting. Using
{\bf s}
, we canonically identify any unipotent subgroup
N
of
G
as a subgroup of
\overline{G}
.

2.1.2.
Finite central covers of
G_{r}

For a positive integer
r
, the group
G_{r}={\rm GL}_{r}(F)
is an
\ell
-group. Denote
\nu:=\left|\det(\cdot)\right|_{F}:G_{r}\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
.

By an
n
-fold Brylinski–Deligne cover, we mean a central extension studied in
[
4
]
associated with a certain pair
(D,\eta)
. See also
[
40
,
14
]
for more references. We write BD-cover for short.
For
G_{r}
, its
n
-fold BD covers are essentially parametrized by a Weyl-invariant bilinear form
B_{Q}:Y\times Y\to\mathbbm{Z}
, where
Y
is the cocharacter lattice of
G_{r}
with a standard basis
\{e_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant r\}
.
The Weyl-invariance enforces that
B_{Q}
is determined by
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbbm{Z}
such that

B_{Q}(e_{i},e_{j})=\begin{cases}2\mathbf{a}&i=j,\\
\mathbf{b}&i\neq j.\end{cases}

We have
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=2{\bf a}-{\bf b}
for any coroot
\alpha^{\vee}
, and write

n_{\alpha}:=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,Q(\alpha^{\vee}))}.

A Kazhdan–Patterson cover is a BD-cover of
G_{r}
such that
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=-1
and a Savin cover is a BD-cover of
G_{r}
associated with the pair
(\textbf{a}=-1,\textbf{b}=0)
. We write KP-cover or S-cover for short. It is clear that a KP-cover is determined by the number
{\bf a}\in\mathbbm{Z}
, which corresponds to the notation
c
used in
[
28
]
. For a KP-cover, we write

(2.1)

d_{r}:=\gcd(n,2r{\bf a}-r+1).

We note that the BD-cover
\overline{G_{r}}
associated with
{\bf a},{\bf b}
has the following heredity property. If we take
G_{r^{\prime}},r^{\prime}\leqslant r
and view it as a subgroup of
G_{r}
in the natural way (i.e., as a block of a standard Levi subgroup of
G_{r}
), then the cover
\overline{G_{r^{\prime}}}
obtained as the restriction from
\overline{G_{r}}
is also associated with
{\bf a},{\bf b}
. By convention, we have
\overline{G_{0}}:=\mu_{n}
. For a closed subgroup
H\subseteq G_{r}
, we write

H^{(n)}:=\left\{h\in H:\ \det(h)\in F^{\times n}\right\}\subseteq H

for the subgroup consisting of elements whose determinants are always a power of
n
.

2.2.
Metaplectic tensor product

Let
M=G_{r_{1}}\times\dots\times G_{r_{k}}
be a Levi subgroup of

, where
r=r_{1}+\dots+r_{k}

Let

be a subgroup of

, such that each

is a closed subgroup of

. We call

[H_{i},H_{j}]=\{1\}
for any

. For example,

is block compatible in the following two cases:

(i)

, the determinant of every element in

is an

;

(ii)

\gcd(n,p)=1
, and for every

is in

Let

be a genuine representation of

as a representation of

\Xi=\{(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{k})\in\mu_{n}\times\dots\times\mu_{n}\mid\zeta_{1}\dots\zeta_{k}=1\}.

If

is block compatible, then we have the isomorphism

\overline{H}\simeq\overline{H_{1}}\times\dots\times\overline{H_{k}}/\Xi,

and in this case
\rho_{1}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}
gives rise to a genuine representation of
\overline{H}
, and every irreducible genuine representation of
\overline{H}
is of this form.

2.2.1.
Tensor product for S-covers

If
\overline{G_{r}}
is an S-cover, then any subgroup

as above is block compatible. This is because the parameter
{\bf b}=0
for Savin covers, and thus
[G_{r_{i}},G_{r_{j}}]=1
for

. Thus we may always take the tensor product of genuine representions of
\overline{H_{i}}
and get a representation of
\overline{H}
. In particular, if each representation of
\overline{H_{i}}
is of finite length (resp. irreducible), so is the tensor product for
\overline{H}
.

2.3.
Extended metaplectic tensor product

Motivated by the Kazhdan–Patterson case, especially Proposition
2.1
, we consider the following extended metaplectic tensor product (EMTP for short), which works for general BD-covers.
By definition, the EMTP is just the map

\displaystyle\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}:

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Rep}(\overline{M_{1}})\times\dots\times{\rm Rep}(\overline{M_{k}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{M}),\

\displaystyle(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})\mapsto\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}:={\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M}}(\rho_{1}\lvert_{\overline{M_{1}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}\lvert_{\overline{M_{k}^{(n)}}}).

When
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k}
are irreducible, the right-hand side is a direct sum of finitely many irreducible representations, which in particular contains the original MTP for S-cover and KP-covers (
cf.

2.4
later on). When

{\rm Ind}_{\overline{M^{(n)}}}^{\overline{M}}(\rho\lvert_{\overline{M^{(n)}}}).

Later we will also need to work in a more general setting. Thus, let
H_{i}
be a closed subgroup of

for each

H_{i}/H_{i}^{(n)}\simeq M_{i}/M_{i}^{(n)}\simeq F^{\times}/F^{\times n}
. For

, write

and
H_{\beta}^{[n]}:=H_{1}^{(n)}\times\dots\times H_{k}^{(n)}
. We define similarly the EMTP by

\displaystyle\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}:

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Rep}(\overline{H_{1}})\times\dots\times{\rm Rep}(\overline{H_{k}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{H}),\

\displaystyle(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})\mapsto\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}:={\rm Ind}_{\overline{H_{\beta}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{H}}(\rho_{1}\lvert_{\overline{H_{1}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}\lvert_{\overline{H_{k}^{(n)}}}).

The following lemma is a special case of the result in
[
17
]
*Section 2, when we take
G,H
(in the notation there) to be
\overline{M}
and
\overline{M_{\beta}^{(n)}}
respectively.

Lemma 2.3
.

(1)

Given an irreducible representation
\pi
of
\overline{M}
, its restriction to
\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}
decomposes into a finite sum of irreducible representations. These irreducible representations of
\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}
are
M
-conjugate to each other.

(2)

Given an irreducible representation
\tau
of
\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}
, its induction to
\overline{M}
decomposes into a finite sum of irreducible representations. These representations are equivalent to each other up to twisting by a character of
M/M_{\beta}^{[n]}
.

(3)

The restriction and induction from (1) and (2) induce a bijection between
M
-conjugacy classes of irreducible representations
\tau
of
\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}
and
X^{*}(M/M_{\beta}^{[n]})
-classes of irreducible representations
\pi
of
\overline{M}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\rho_{i}\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{M_{i}})
for
i=1,\dots,k
.

(1)

The representation
\rho_{1}\lvert_{\overline{M_{1}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}\lvert_{\overline{M_{k}^{(n)}}}
is a direct sum of irreducible representations of the form
\tau:=\tau_{1}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\tau_{k}
of the same multiplicity, where each
\tau_{i}
is an irreducible constituent of
\rho_{i}\lvert_{\overline{M_{i}^{(n)}}}
,
i=1,\dots,k
.

(2)

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
\overline{M}
whose restriction to
\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}
intersects
\rho_{1}\lvert_{\overline{M_{1}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}\lvert_{\overline{M_{k}^{(n)}}}
. Then the EMTP
\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}
is a finite direct sum of representations of the form
\pi\cdot\chi
, where
\chi\in X^{*}(M/M_{\beta}^{[n]})
.

Proof.

Statement (1) is clear. Also we remark that these
\tau
are
M
-conjugate to each other, since for each
i
these
\tau_{i}
are
M_{i}
-conjugate to each other and
M_{\beta}^{[n]}
is block compatible. Using (1), we see that
\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}
is a direct sum of the induction
{\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M}}(\tau)
. Using
Lemma
2.3
, the irreducible constituents occurring in
\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}
are all of the form
\pi\cdot\chi
, which proves (2).

∎

Remark 2.5
.

One disadvantage of the EMTP construction is that as a multifunctor it is not associative, meaning that for
\rho_{i}\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{M_{i}})
,
i=1,2,3
, in general we may have

(\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{2})\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{3}\not\simeq\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{2}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{3}\not\simeq\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}(\rho_{2}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{3}).

Still, the EMTP construction possesses the property of “associativity up to multiplicity” for finite length representations, as follows.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\rho_{i}\in{\rm Rep}_{\epsilon}(\overline{M_{i}})
for
i=1,\dots,k
.

(1)

Let
T(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
be an “iterated” EMTP of
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k}
defined by adding certain brackets on the original tensor
\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}
. Then for every
\chi_{i}\in X^{*}(M_{i}/M_{i}^{(n)})
and thus
\chi:=\chi_{1}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\chi_{k}\in X^{*}(M/M_{\beta}^{[n]})
, we have
T(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})\cdot\chi\simeq T(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
.

(2)

Assume furthermore that each
\rho_{i}
is of finite length. Then for two different iterated EMTP
T_{1}(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
and
T_{2}(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
, there exist positive integers
n_{1},n_{2}
such that
n_{1}\cdot T_{1}(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})\simeq n_{2}\cdot T_{2}(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
.

Proof.

Statement (1) follows from the definition of the EMTP. More precisely, given
\pi_{i}\in{\rm Rep}_{\epsilon}(\overline{M_{i}})
and
\chi_{i}\in X^{*}(M_{i})
for each
i=1,\dots,k
and
\chi=\chi_{1}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\chi_{k}\in X^{*}(M)
, we have

\chi\cdot{\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M}}(\pi_{1}\lvert_{\overline{M_{1}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\pi_{k}\lvert_{\overline{M_{k}^{(n)}}})\simeq{\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M}}(\chi\lvert_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}\cdot(\pi_{1}\lvert_{\overline{M_{1}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\pi_{k}\lvert_{\overline{M_{k}^{(n)}}})).

Furthermore, if we know that
(\chi_{i}\cdot\pi_{i})\lvert_{\overline{M_{i}^{(n)}}}\simeq\pi_{i}\lvert_{\overline{M_{i}^{(n)}}}
for each
i
, we get

\chi\cdot{\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M}}(\pi_{1}\lvert_{\overline{M_{1}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\pi_{k}\lvert_{\overline{M_{k}^{(n)}}})\simeq{\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M}}(\pi_{1}\lvert_{\overline{M_{1}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\pi_{k}\lvert_{\overline{M_{k}^{(n)}}}).

Iterating this property, we get
T(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})\chi\simeq T(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
for any
\chi=\chi_{1}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\chi_{k}\in X^{*}(M/M_{\beta}^{[n]})
.

For Statement (2), we first consider adding one bracket to
\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}
. For
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k
, define
\beta^{\prime}=(r_{i},r_{i+1},\dots,r_{j})
and

M^{j}_{i}=M_{i}\times\dots\times M_{j},\quad(M^{j}_{i})^{[n]}_{\beta^{\prime}}=M^{(n)}_{i}\times\dots\times M^{(n)}_{j}.

We clearly have the inclusions
(M^{j}_{i})^{[n]}_{\beta^{\prime}}\subseteq(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}\subseteq M_{i}^{j}
of groups. Also define

\pi_{i}^{j}=(\text{Ind}^{\overline{M^{j}_{i}}}_{\overline{(M^{j}_{i})_{\beta^{\prime}}^{[n]}}}\rho_{i}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{i}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{j}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{j}}})|_{\overline{(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}}},\quad\tau_{i}^{j}=\text{Ind}^{\overline{(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}}}_{\overline{(M^{j}_{i})^{[n]}_{\beta^{\prime}}}}(\rho_{i}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{i}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{j}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{j}}}).

Using the Mackey formula, we have

(2.3)

\pi_{i}^{j}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\in M^{j}_{i}/(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}}(\tau_{i}^{j})^{m}.

By definition we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}(\rho_{i}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{j})\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\text{Ind}^{\overline{M}}_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{1}\times\dots\times(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}\times\dots\times M^{(n)}_{k}}}(\rho_{1}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{1}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\pi_{i}^{j}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{k}}}).

Since
\overline{M^{j}_{i}}
commutes with
\overline{M^{(n)}_{l}}
for
l<i
or
l>j
as subgroups of
\overline{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Ind}^{\overline{M^{(n)}_{1}\times\dots\times(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}\times\dots\times M^{(n)}_{k}}}_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{1}\times\dots\times(M^{j}_{i})_{\beta^{\prime}}^{[n]}\times\dots\times M^{(n)}_{k}}}(\rho_{1}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{1}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{k}}})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\rho_{1}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{1}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes(\text{Ind}^{\overline{(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}}}_{\overline{(M^{j}_{i})^{[n]}_{\beta^{\prime}}}}(\rho_{i}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{i}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{j}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{j}}}))\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{k}}}

and thus

(2.5)

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}\simeq\text{Ind}^{\overline{M}}_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{1}\times\dots\times(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}\times\dots\times M^{(n)}_{k}}}(\rho_{1}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{1}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\tau_{i}^{j}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k}|_{\overline{M^{(n)}_{k}}}).

Also, we may plug (
2.3
) into (
2.4
) and “pull” the conjugation by
m\in M^{j}_{i}/(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}
out of the tensor product for the same reason. Comparing this with (
2.5
), we get

\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}(\rho_{i}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{j})\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}\simeq|M^{j}_{i}/(M^{j}_{i})^{(n)}|\cdot\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{i}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{j}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}.

In general, Statement (2) follows from an iterated application of the above special case.

∎

.

Let
M=M_{1}\times\dots\times M_{k}

(\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k})^{w}\simeq\rho_{\varsigma(1)}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{\varsigma(k)}.

Let
\beta=(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
and
\beta^{\prime}=(r_{\varsigma(1)},\dots,r_{\varsigma(k)})

2.4.
Whittaker and semi-Whittaker models

We briefly discuss the Whittaker model and semi-Whittaker model for a BD-cover
\overline{G_{r}}
and its Levi subgroup
\overline{M}
. We fix a non-trivial additive character
\psi_{F}:F\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
.

Let
U_{r}\subset G_{r}
be the unipotent group of upper triangular matrices and let

\psi_{r}:U_{r}\rightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times},\ (v_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant r}\mapsto\psi_{F}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}v_{i,i+1}\Big)

be the standard generic character of
U_{r}
associated with
\psi_{F}
. Such a pair
(U_{r},\psi_{r})
, with
U_{r}
viewed as a subgroup of
\overline{G_{r}}
via its canonical splitting, is called the standard Whittaker pair of
\overline{G_{r}}
.

Furthermore, we define a Whittaker pair of
\overline{M}
as follows. If
\overline{M}
is block-diagonal and thus a standard Levi subgroup, then
(M\cap U_{r},\psi_{r}\lvert_{M\cap U_{r}})
is called the standard Whittaker pair of
\overline{M}
. In general, a Whittaker pair of
\overline{M}
is a
G
-conjugation to the standard Whittaker pair of some block-diagonal Levi subgroup.

Given a Whittaker pair
(U_{M},\psi_{M})
of
\overline{M}
and a smooth representation
\pi_{M}
of
\overline{M}
, we define the associated Whittaker space

{\rm Wh}(\pi_{M})={\rm Hom}_{U_{M}}(\pi_{M}\lvert_{U_{M}},\psi_{M}).

It is well-known that the functor

{\rm Wh}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{M})\rightarrow\{\mathbbm{C}\ \text{vector spaces}\}

is exact.
Moreover, we know that
\dim{\rm Wh}(\pi_{M})<\infty
for every irreducible
\pi_{M}
, see
[
35
]
*Theorem 2. However, unlike in the linear reductive group case, such dimension is in general not bounded above by one for genuine irreducible representations, i.e., multiplicity-one property fails for covers.

We discuss the semi-Whittaker model of representations of
\overline{G_{r}}
associated with a composition
\lambda=(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
of
r
, following the terminology in
[
5
]
. Consider the character

\psi_{\lambda}:U_{r}\rightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times},\ (v_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant r}\mapsto\psi_{F}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}-1}v_{\sum_{s=1}^{i-1}r_{s}+j,\sum_{s=1}^{i-1}r_{s}+j+1}\Big).

Then
(U_{r},\psi_{\lambda})
is called the standard semi-Whittaker pair of
\overline{G_{r}}
associated with
\lambda
.
It is known that the set of
G_{r}
-conjugacy classes of semi-Whittaker pairs are in bijection with the set of partitions of
r
, or equivalently, the set of nilpotent orbits of
G_{r}
. In any case, we define the
\lambda
-semi-Whittaker space of a smooth representation
\pi
of
\overline{G}
to be

{\rm Wh}_{\lambda}(\pi):={\rm Hom}_{U_{r}}(\pi\lvert_{U_{r}},\psi_{\lambda}).

Using the Frobenius reciprocity, we have

(2.6)

{\rm Wh}_{\lambda}(\pi)\simeq{\rm Hom}_{U_{r}\cap M_{\lambda}}(J_{U_{\lambda}}(\pi),\psi\lvert_{U_{r}\cap M_{\lambda}}),

where

•

M_{\lambda}=G_{r_{1}}\times\dots\times G_{r_{k}}
is the block-diagonal standard Levi subgroup of
G_{r}
,

•

U_{\lambda}
is the standard unipotent radical of the block upper triangular parabolic subgroup of
G_{r}
with Levi factor
M_{\lambda}
,

•

(U_{r}\cap M_{\lambda},\psi_{r}\lvert_{U_{r}\cap M_{\lambda}})
is a generic Whittaker pair of
\overline{M_{\lambda}}
,

•

J_{U_{\lambda}}(\pi)
denotes the Jacquet module of
\pi
as a smooth representation of
\overline{M_{\lambda}}
.

Thus, we have

{\rm Wh}_{\lambda}(\pi)\simeq{\rm Wh}(J_{U_{\lambda}}(\pi))

as vector spaces, which is finite dimensional if
\pi
is of finite length.

Finally, we discuss the Whittaker model of an EMTP of irreducible representations. Let
r=r_{1}+\dots+r_{k}
. For
\rho_{i}\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r_{i}}})
, taking the EMTP and using the Mackey formula, we have

\dim{\rm Wh}((\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}))=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\dim({\rm Wh}({\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{r_{i}}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{r_{i}}}}\rho_{i}))

Let
m
(resp.
m_{i}
) be the total multiplicity of
\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}
(resp.
{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{r_{i}}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{r_{i}}}}\rho_{i}
) when written as a direct sum of irreducible representations. Using the fact that those irreducible summands have the same Whittaker dimension (since they only differ by a character twist by Corollary
2.4
), we have

(2.7)

m\cdot\dim{\rm Wh}(\rho)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}\cdot\dim({\rm Wh}(\rho_{i})),

where
\rho
is any irreducible summand of
\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}
.

In this section, we discuss the Bernstein–Zelevinsky theory for a BD-cover

3.1.
Extended Bernstein-Zelevinsky product

Let
\overline{G}
be an
n
-fold cover of a reductive group
G
over
F
. Consider a group
P=MN
with
M,N
being subgroups of
G
,
M\cap N=\{1\}
and
M

N
. We assume

is a unipotent subgroup of
G
, identified as a subgroup of
\overline{G}
, then
\overline{P}=\overline{M}N
is a subgroup of
\overline{G}
satisfying the above conditions as well. Let
\psi
be a character of
N

M
, then as in
[
3
]
*§1.8, we define the normalized non-compact and compact induction functors and restriction functor

I_{N,\psi},i_{N,\psi}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{M})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{G}),\quad J_{N,\psi}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{G})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{M}).

In particular, in the case
P
is a parabolic subgroup of
G
with
N

\psi=\mathbbm{1}

i_{\overline{P}}^{\overline{G}}=i_{N,\mathbbm{1}}\text{ and }J_{N}=J_{N,\mathbbm{1}}

for the related parabolic induction and Jacquet module functor.

From now on we focus on the case
G=G_{r}
. Let
\overline{G_{r}}
be a BD-cover of
G_{r}
. For
r=r_{1}+\dots+r_{k}
and
\beta=(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})

M=G_{r_{1}}\times\dots\times G_{r_{k}}
and let

M

N

\displaystyle\overline{\times_{i=1}^{k}}:=i_{\overline{P}}^{\overline{G_{r}}}\circ\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}:

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Rep}(\overline{G_{r_{1}}})\times\dots\times{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r_{k}}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r}}),\

\displaystyle(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})\mapsto\rho_{1}\overline{\times}\dots\overline{\times}\rho_{k}:=i_{\overline{P}}^{\overline{G_{r}}}(\overline{\boxtimes}_{i}\rho_{i}).

It maps finite length representations to finite length representations.

We emphasize that an associative law up to multiplicity is satisfied.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
r=r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}
and
\rho_{i}\in{\rm Rep}_{\epsilon}^{\rm fl}(\overline{G_{r_{i}}})
, there exist positive integers
m_{123},m_{12},m_{23}
such that

m_{123}\cdot(\rho_{1}\overline{\times}\rho_{2}\overline{\times}\rho_{3})\simeq m_{12}\cdot((\rho_{1}\overline{\times}\rho_{2})\overline{\times}\rho_{3})\simeq m_{23}\cdot(\rho_{1}\overline{\times}(\rho_{2}\overline{\times}\rho_{3})).

Proof.

First, using Proposition
2.6
.(2) we can find
m_{123},m_{12},m_{23}
such that

m_{123}\cdot(\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{2}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{3})\simeq m_{12}\cdot((\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{2})\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{3})\simeq m_{23}\cdot(\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}(\rho_{2}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{3})).

Let
P=P_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}
be the standard parabolic subgroup whose Levi subgroup is
M_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}\simeq G_{r_{1}}\times G_{r_{2}}\times G_{r_{3}}
. Taking the parabolic induction
i_{\overline{P}}^{\overline{G}}
,
we need to show that

i_{\overline{P}}^{\overline{G}}((\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{2})\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{3})\simeq(\rho_{1}\overline{\times}\rho_{2})\overline{\times}\rho_{3}\quad\text{and}\quad i_{\overline{P}}^{\overline{G}}(\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}(\rho_{2}\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{3}))\simeq\rho_{1}\overline{\times}(\rho_{2}\overline{\times}\rho_{3}).

This follows from a direct calculation using associativity of induction functor and the geometric lemma.

∎

We specialize to the case where
\overline{G_{r}}
is either a KP-cover or an S-cover and each
\rho_{i}

is genuine and of finite length. In the KP-cover case, we further assume that for each
i
every irreducible subquotient of
\rho_{i}
has genuine central character
\omega_{i}
of
Z(\overline{G_{r_{i}}})
. Moreover, we assume that
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k})
is a sequence of characters compatible with a genuine character
\omega
of

. Then we define the usual Bernstein–Zelevinsky product

\displaystyle(\rho_{1}\times\dots\times\rho_{k})_{\omega}:=i_{\overline{P}}^{\overline{G_{r}}}((\rho_{1}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\rho_{k})_{\omega})

as in
[
27
]
*§5.2, Remark 7.6.
Note that in the S-cover case, the representation
(\rho_{1}\times\dots\times\rho_{k})_{\omega}
is the usual Bernstein–Zelevinsky product and the notation

is only symbolic.

The proposition below follows from the above discussion and Corollary
2.4
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\overline{G_{r}}
is either a KP-cover or an S-cover and each
\rho_{i}

is a direct summand of the extended Bernstein–Zelevinsky product

.

3.2.
Cuspidal support and
n
-equivalence classes

For any irreducible representation
\pi
of
\overline{G}
, there exist a Levi subgroup

of

of
\overline{M}
such that
\pi

is unique up to

of

Write

\rho

\overline{M}
, using Lemma

and Corollary

, there exist genuine irreducible representations
\rho_{i}
of each
G_{r_{i}}
, such that
\rho
is an irreducible summand of
\rho_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\dots\overline{\boxtimes}\rho_{k}
. By considering the matrix coefficient, the representation
\rho
is cuspidal if and only if each
\rho_{i}
is cuspidal. On the one hand, for each
i
the
X^{*}(G_{r_{i}}/G_{r_{i}}^{(n)})
-orbit of
\rho_{i}
is uniquely determined by
\rho
. On the other hand, replacing
\rho_{i}
by another representation in the same
X^{*}(G_{r_{i}}/G_{r_{i}}^{(n)})
-orbit does not change the EMTP. Finally, an irreducible representation
\rho^{\prime}
occurs as a summand in the above EMTP if and only if it lies in the
X^{*}(M/M_{\beta}^{[n]})
-orbit of
\rho
with
\beta=(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
.

For any irreducible representation
\rho
of
\overline{M}
, we denote by
[\rho]
the
X^{*}(M/M_{\beta}^{[n]})
-orbit of
\rho
(which in particular includes the

n
-equivalence class
of
\rho
. If moreover
\rho
is cuspidal, then
[\rho]
is called an
n
-cuspidal equivalence class
. As a result, the above discussion shows that the set of genuine
n
-cuspidal equivalence classes
[\rho]
of
\overline{M}
corresponds bijectively to the set of sequences

([\rho_{1}],\dots,[\rho_{k}]),\quad\quad\rho_{i}\in{\rm Cusp}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r_{i}}}),\ r_{1}+\dots+r_{k}=r.

Using Lemma
2.7
, the set of
G
-conjugacy classes of all possible pairs
(\overline{M},[\rho])
corresponds bijectively to the set of multisets (meaning that there is no specific order for
[\rho_{1}],\dots,[\rho_{k}]
)

[\rho_{1}]+\dots+[\rho_{k}],\quad\rho_{i}\in{\rm Cusp}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r_{i}}}),\ r_{1}+\dots+r_{k}=r.

For a genuine irreducible representation
\pi
of
\overline{G_{r}}
, we call the related multiset
[\rho_{1}]+\dots+[\rho_{k}]
the
n
-cuspidal support
of
\pi
, if
\pi
occurs as a subquotient in

.

Remark 3.3
.

For
M=G_{r}
and an irreducible representation
\rho
of
\overline{M}
, the

-equivalence class
[\rho]
is exactly the same as the so-called weak equivalence class of

in
[

]
*Definition 4.2 (the latter is only defined for representations of KP-covers). However, for general Levi subgroup

, these two concepts are different. Also, our

-cuspidal support is exactly the weak cuspidal support omitting the central character
\omega
in
[

]
*§5.4.

3.3.
Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives

Now we discuss the theory of Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives. Let
P_{r}
be the mirabolic subgroup of
G_{r}
(i.e. elements in
G_{r}
with last row being
(0,\dots,0,1)
) and
V_{r}\subseteq P_{r}
the unipotent radical of
P_{r}
. Define the character
\theta_{r}
of
V_{r}
by
\theta_{r}((v_{ij}))=\psi_{F}(v_{r-1,r})
. Then
P_{r}
normalizes
\theta_{r}
. Define functors

\Psi^{-}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-1}}),\qquad\Psi^{+}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-1}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}})

\Phi^{-}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r-1}}),\qquad\Phi^{+}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r-1}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}})

\Psi_{n}^{-}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-1}^{(n)}}),\qquad\Psi_{n}^{+}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-1}^{(n)}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}})

\Phi_{n}^{-}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r-1}^{(n)}}),\qquad\Phi_{n}^{+}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r-1}^{(n)}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}})

by

\Psi^{-},\Psi_{n}^{-}:=J_{V_{r},\mathbbm{1}},\quad\Phi^{-},\Phi_{n}^{-}:=J_{V_{r},\theta_{r}},\quad\Psi^{+},\Psi_{n}^{+}:=i_{V_{r},\mathbbm{1}},\quad\Phi^{+},\Phi_{n}^{+}:=i_{V_{r},\theta_{r}},

where
V_{r}
is viewed as a subgroup of
\overline{G_{r}}
via the canonical splitting. The definition here is identical with that in
[
3
]
*§3.2. Note that all the elements in
N_{r}
are of determinant 1, thus the related functors
\Psi_{n}^{-},\Psi_{n}^{+},\Phi_{n}^{-},\Phi_{n}^{+}
are well-defined. All the results in
[
3
]
*Section 3 work here without essential change.

Let us recall the notion of Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivative. Consider
\tau\in{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}})
(resp.
{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}})
). Define

\tau^{(k)}:=\Psi^{-}\circ(\Phi^{-})^{k-1}(\tau)\in{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-k}})\text{ (resp. ${\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}})$)},

called the
k
-th derivative of
\tau
. Then,
\tau
is glued from the representations
(\Phi^{+})^{k-1}\circ\Psi^{+}(\tau^{(k)})
(resp.
(\Phi_{n}^{+})^{k-1}\circ\Psi_{n}^{+}(\tau^{(k)})
),
k=1,2,\dots,r
. Also, the representation is generic if and only if
\tau^{(r)}\neq 0
and the dimension of
\tau^{(r)}
(as a representation of
\mu_{n}
) is exactly the Whittaker dimension of
\tau
. For
\pi\in{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r}})
(resp.
{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r}^{(n)}})
), we define
\pi^{(k)}=(\pi\lvert_{\overline{P_{r}}})^{(k)}
(resp.
\pi^{(k)}=(\pi\lvert_{\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}}})^{(k)}
). In this way, we get a functor

(\cdot)^{(k)}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-k}}).

In particular,
\pi^{(r)}\simeq{\rm Hom}_{U_{r}}(\pi,\psi_{r})
is the Whittaker space of
\pi
. If
\pi
is of finite length, then
\pi^{(r)}
is finite dimensional.

We also define the “partial version” of the above functors. For a fixed
r_{0}\geqslant 1
and each
r\geqslant 1
, consider the embedding
\iota_{1}:V_{r}\hookrightarrow G_{r}\times G_{r_{0}},\ x\mapsto(x,1)
given by the natural injection on the first coordinate. For any closed subgroup
H
of
G_{r_{0}}
, we define

\,{}^{1}\Psi^{-}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}\times H})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-1}\times H}),\qquad\,^{1}\Psi^{+}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-1}\times H})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}\times H})

\,{}^{1}\Phi^{-}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}\times H})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r-1}\times H}),\qquad\,^{1}\Phi^{+}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r-1}\times H}),\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}\times H})

\,{}^{1}\Psi_{n}^{-}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}\times H})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-1}^{(n)}\times H}),\qquad\,^{1}\Psi_{n}^{+}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-1}^{(n)}\times H})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}\times H})

\,{}^{1}\Phi_{n}^{-}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}\times H})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r-1}^{(n)}\times H}),\qquad\,^{1}\Phi_{n}^{+}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r-1}^{(n)}\times H})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{P_{r}^{(n)}\times H})

by

\,{}^{1}\Psi^{-},\,^{1}\Psi_{n}^{-}:=J_{V_{r},\mathbbm{1}},\ \,^{1}\Phi^{-},\,^{1}\Phi_{n}^{-}:=J_{V_{r},\theta_{r}},\ \,^{1}\Psi^{+},\,^{1}\Psi_{n}^{+}:=i_{V_{r},\mathbbm{1}},\ \,^{1}\Phi^{+},\,^{1}\Phi_{n}^{+}:=i_{V_{r},\theta_{r}}

with
V_{r}
being identified with a subgroup of
\overline{G_{r}\times G_{r_{0}}}
via the composite of the embedding
\iota_{1}
and the canonical splitting. Similarly, for the embedding
\iota_{2}:V_{r}\hookrightarrow G_{r_{0}}\times G_{r},\ x\mapsto(1,x)
given by the natural injection on the second coordinate, in parallel we define functors

\,{}^{2}\Psi^{-},\,^{2}\Psi_{n}^{-},\,^{2}\Phi^{-},\,^{2}\Phi_{n}^{-},\,^{2}\Psi^{+},\,^{2}\Psi_{n}^{+},\,^{2}\Phi^{+},\,^{2}\Phi_{n}^{+}.

Let
M_{r-k,k}=G_{r-k}\times G_{k}
and consider
M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}=G_{r-k}^{(n)}\times G_{k}^{(n)}
. Define the partial derivative functor (with respect to the second factor)

\partial^{s}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{M_{r-k,k}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{M_{r-k,k-s}})\ (\text{resp.}\ {\rm Rep}(\overline{M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{M_{r-k,k-s}^{[n]}})

by first restricting to
\overline{G_{r-k}\times P_{k}}
(resp.
\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}\times P_{k}^{(n)}}
), and then composing the functor
\,{}^{2}\Psi^{-}\circ(\,^{2}\Phi^{-})^{s-1}
.

The relation between the derivative and the partial derivative is as follows. Let
N_{r-k,k}
be the standard unipotent radical of

, then we get

(3.1)

\partial^{k}\circ J_{N_{r-k,k}}=(\cdot)^{(k)}:{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r}})\rightarrow{\rm Rep}(\overline{G_{r-k}}).

The following result is immediate.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

is zero unless

, and in this case

is a finite direct sum of the character

of

Recall that for an

and a character

of

-pairing of

is a bilinear form

that is

-equivariant (
[

]
*Definition 3.6). Such a bilinear form corresponds to an element in

. The form

is called

with respect to

(resp.

(resp.

(

3.4.
Calculating derivatives in a special case

Now we consider a special case of
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
of a BD-cover
\overline{G_{r}}
, where we assume that the Jacquet module
J_{N_{r-k,k}}(\pi)
is either 0 or irreducible for a given
k
. In the former case, we have
\pi^{(k)}=0
. In the latter case, there is an irreducible genuine representation
\tau=\tau_{1}\boxtimes\tau_{2}
of
\overline{M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}}=\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}\times G_{k}^{(n)}}
, unique up to
M_{r-k,k}
-conjugacy, such that
J_{N_{r-k,k}}(\pi)
is an irreducible summand of
{\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M_{r-k,k}}}(\tau)
. Using the geometric lemma, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{k}({\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M_{r-k,k}}}(\tau))

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{g\in M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}\backslash M_{r-k,k}/G_{r-k}\times\{1\}}{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{r-k}}}(\partial^{k}((\tau_{1}\boxtimes\tau_{2})^{g}))

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{g_{2}\in G_{k}/G_{k}^{(n)}}\dim(\mathrm{Wh}(\tau_{2}^{g_{2}}))\cdot{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{r-k}}}(\tau_{1})

\displaystyle\simeq\dim(\mathrm{Wh}({\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{k}}}\tau_{2})))\cdot{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{r-k}}}(\tau_{1})

Write
m
,
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
for the total multiplicity of
{\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M_{r-k,k}}}(\tau)
,
{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{r-k}}}(\tau_{1})
and
{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{k}}}(\tau_{2})
respectively, when decomposing the representation into direct sum of irreducible representations. Using Lemma
2.3
, we see that the irreducible representations occurring in the direct sum are of the same Whittaker dimension. Let
\pi_{1}
be an irreducible summand of
{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{r-k}}}(\tau_{1})
and
\pi_{2}
be that of
{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{k}}}(\tau_{2})
, we get

\partial^{k}({\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M_{r-k,k}}}(\tau))=m_{2}\cdot\dim(\mathrm{Wh}(\pi_{2}))\cdot{\rm Ind}_{\overline{G_{r-k}^{(n)}}}^{\overline{G_{r-k}}}(\tau_{1}).

On the other hand, we have

\partial^{k}({\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{r-k,k}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M_{r-k,k}}}(\tau))=\bigoplus_{\chi\in X^{*}(M_{r-k,k}/M_{r-k,k}^{[n]})}m_{\chi}\cdot\partial^{k}(J_{N_{r-k,k}}(\pi)\cdot\chi),

where
\sum_{\chi\in X^{*}(M_{r-k,k}/M_{r-k,k}^{[n]})}m_{\chi}=m
. Write
\chi=\chi_{1}\boxtimes\chi_{2}
, we have

\partial^{k}(J_{N_{r-k,k}}(\pi)\cdot\chi)=\partial^{k}(J_{N_{r-k,k}}(\pi))\cdot\chi_{1}.

Using the above facts, we have that

\partial^{k}(J_{N_{r-k,k}}(\pi))\simeq\bigoplus_{\chi_{1}\in X^{*}(G_{r-k}/G_{r-k}^{(n)})}m_{\chi_{1}}^{\prime}\cdot(\pi_{1}\chi_{1})

for some
m_{\chi_{1}}^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{N}
such that
m\cdot\sum_{\chi_{1}\in X^{*}(G_{r-k}/G_{r-k}^{(n)})}m_{\chi_{1}}^{\prime}=m_{1}m_{2}\dim(\mathrm{Wh}(\pi_{2}))
. Thus we have shown the following:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
. Assume that the Jacquet module
J_{N_{r-k,k}}(\pi)
is an irreducible constituent of
\pi_{1}\overline{\boxtimes}\pi_{2}
with
\pi_{1}\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r-k}})
and
\pi_{2}\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{k}})
. Then
\pi^{(k)}
is a direct sum of irreducible representations of the form

\pi_{1}\chi_{1},\quad\chi_{1}\in X^{*}(G_{r-k}/G_{r-k}^{(n)}),

where the total multiplicity is
m_{1}m_{2}\dim(\mathrm{Wh}(\pi_{2}))/m
.

If
\overline{G_{r}}
is a KP-cover, then
m_{1}m_{2}/m
is a number depending only on groups but independent of representations. More precisely, assuming
\gcd(p,n)=1
for simplicity and using the formula in
[
42
]
*Proposition 2.3, we have

m_{1}=m_{2}=n^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad m=n^{4}d_{r}/d_{r-k}d_{k}

and

(3.2)

m_{1}m_{2}/m=d_{k}d_{r-k}/d_{r}.

4.1.

First, for

Remark 4.1

For a KP-cover, an alternative way of characterizing

are integers. In our convention,

*§7.1. More precisely, two segments

6.
Wavefront sets and local Langlands correspondence

6.1.
Wavefront set of
Z(\mathfrak{m})

We first briefly recall the notion of wavefront sets for genuine representations of a BD-cover
\overline{G}_{r}
. Let
\mathcal{N}(G_{r})
denote the partially-ordered set of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}={\rm Lie}(G_{r})
under the conjugation action of
G_{r}
. Here the partial order is given by the closure ordering in the usual topology of
\mathfrak{g}
induced from that of
F
.

Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be a finite-length genuine representation of
\overline{G_{r}}
. It gives a character distribution
\chi_{\pi}
in a neighborhood of
0
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, there exists a compact open subset
S_{\pi}
of
0
such that for every smooth function
f
with compact support in
S_{\pi}
, one has (see
[
23
,
21
,
31
]
)

(6.1)

\chi_{\pi}(f)=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}(G_{r})}{c_{\mathcal{O},\psi_{F}}(\pi)}\cdot\int\hat{f}_{\psi_{F}}\ \mu_{\mathcal{O}}.

Here
\mu_{\mathcal{O}}
is a certain Haar measure on
\mathcal{O}
properly normalized, and
\hat{f}_{\psi_{F}}
is the Fourier transform of
f
with respect to the Cartan–Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
and the non-trivial character

\psi_{F}:F\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}.

One has
c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi):=c_{\mathcal{O},\psi_{F}}(\pi)\in\mathbbm{C}
.
We have used implicitly in (
6.1
) an exponent map
{\rm exp}:L\to\overline{G}_{r}
defined for a sufficiently small open set
L\subset\mathfrak{g}
containing
0
; it is used to “pull-back” the character distribution of
\pi
defined on
\overline{G}_{r}
to be on (a small neighborhood of 0 in)
\mathfrak{g}
, see
[
31
]
*§4.3.

Denote

\mathcal{N}(\pi)=\left\{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}(G_{r}):\ c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)\neq 0\right\},

and let

{\rm WF}(\pi)\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\pi)

be the subset consisting of all maximal elements in
\mathcal{N}(\pi)
.
Here,
{\rm WF}(\pi)
is called the wavefront set of
\pi
. For
G_{r}
(i.e., in the
n=1
case) and irreducible
\pi
, the set
{\rm WF}(\pi)
is singleton from the work of Mœglin–Waldspurger
[
34
]
, and it can be computed from the mulltisegment
\mathfrak{m}
underlying
\pi=Z(\mathfrak{m})
. The goal of this section is to extend such results on
G_{r}
to KP-covers or Savin covers.

For this purpose, we briefly recall the notion of degenerate Whittaker models and their relation with wavefront set, see
[
34
,
18
]
for details.
Let
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{p}_{\mathcal{O}}
be a partition of
r
, which is associated with a nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}
of
G_{r}
.
First, the nilpotent orbit
\mathfrak{p}
corresponds to a
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, and this gives rise to a nilpotent subgroup
U_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq G_{r}
which surjects naturally to a Heisenberg group
H_{\mathfrak{p}}
as in

U_{\mathfrak{p}}\twoheadrightarrow H_{\mathfrak{p}},

both of which depend on
\mathfrak{p}
. Moreover, the orbit
\mathfrak{p}
also gives the center
C_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
H_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Using
\psi_{F}
(and the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
), one has a natural non-trivial character
\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}:C_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
.
Let
\omega_{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}
be the irreducible representation of
H_{\mathfrak{p}}
with central character
\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}
. By pull-back, we have a representation
\omega_{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}
of
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
. For a finite-length genuine representation
\pi
of
\overline{G_{r}}
, the degenerate Whittaker model of
\pi
is then by definition

{\rm Wh}(\pi;\mathfrak{p}):={\rm Hom}_{\overline{G}_{r}}(\pi,{\rm Ind}_{\mu_{n}\times U_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\overline{G}_{r}}\epsilon\boxtimes\omega_{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*})={\rm Hom}_{\mu_{n}\times U_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\pi,\epsilon\boxtimes\omega_{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}).

We also define

\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(\pi):=\max\left\{\mathcal{O}:\ {\rm Wh}(\pi;\mathfrak{p}_{\mathcal{O}})\neq 0\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(G_{r}).

It is shown in
[
34
,
39
,
35
]
that the equality

(6.2)

{\rm WF}(\pi)=\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(\pi)

holds; moreover, with the involved measures properly normalized, for every
\mathcal{O}
in the above (equal) sets, one has
c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)=\dim_{\mathbbm{C}}{\rm Wh}(\pi;\mathfrak{p}_{\mathcal{O}})
.
Furthermore, by
[
18
, Theorem A]
, we have an injection

(6.3)

{\rm Wh}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)\simeq{\rm Wh}(J_{U_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\pi))\hookrightarrow{\rm Wh}(\pi;\mathfrak{p}),

where
the left-hand side represents the
\mathfrak{p}
-semi-Whittaker model of
\pi
. By
[
35
]
*Theorem 2, the embedding (
6.3
) becomes an isomorphism for those orbits

that lie in the wavefront set of
\pi
. As a result, if we similarly define

\mathcal{N}_{\rm{sem}}(\pi):=\max\left\{\mathcal{O}:\ {\rm Wh}_{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathcal{O}}}(\pi)\neq 0\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(G_{r}),

then we have

(6.4)

\mathcal{N}_{\rm{sem}}(\pi)=\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(\pi)={\rm WF}(\pi).

Before stating the main theorem, we need the following lemma for general BD-covers. Write

\mathfrak{p}:=(\mathfrak{p}_{1},...,\mathfrak{p}_{k})
and view it as an orbit for
M_{\beta}
.
For any
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{M_{\beta}})
and every character
\chi
of
M_{\beta}
, it is easy to see

{\rm Wh}(\pi\cdot\chi;\mathfrak{p})\simeq{\rm Wh}(\pi;\mathfrak{p}).

Applying this to any irreducible constituent
\pi
of
\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}\pi_{i}
, it follows from Corollary
2.4
(2) that

(6.5)

{\rm Wh}(\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}\pi_{i};\mathfrak{p})\simeq c_{1}\cdot{\rm Wh}(\pi;\mathfrak{p})

as vector spaces, for some
c_{1}\in\mathbbm{N}_{\geqslant 1}
. On the other hand, if we write

\pi_{\flat}:=\pi_{1}|_{\overline{G_{r_{1}}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes...\boxtimes\pi_{k}|_{\overline{G_{r_{k}}^{(n)}}}

as a genuine representation of
\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}:=\overline{\prod_{i=1}^{k}G_{r_{i}}^{(n)}}
, then by definition
\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}\pi_{i}={\rm Ind}_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}^{\overline{M_{\beta}}}\pi_{\flat}
.
We get by Mackey theory that

(\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}\pi_{i})|_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}}=\bigoplus_{s\in M_{\beta}/M_{\beta}^{[n]}}{}^{s}\pi_{\flat}.

We can write
s=(s_{1},s_{2},...,s_{i},...,s_{k})
with
s_{i}\in G_{r_{i}}/G_{r_{i}}^{(n)}
. Then

{}^{s}\pi_{\flat}:=({}^{s_{1}}\pi_{1})|_{\overline{G_{r_{i}}^{(n)}}}\boxtimes...\boxtimes({}^{s_{k}}\pi_{k})|_{\overline{G_{r_{k}}^{(n)}}}.

We have

{\rm Wh}(({}^{s_{i}}\pi_{i})|_{\overline{G_{r_{i}}^{(n)}}};\mathfrak{p}_{i})={\rm Wh}({}^{s_{i}}\pi_{i};\mathfrak{p}_{i})\simeq{\rm Wh}(\pi_{i};\mathfrak{p}_{i}).

This gives

{\rm Wh}({}^{s}\pi_{\flat};\mathfrak{p})\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\rm Wh}(\pi_{i};\mathfrak{p}_{i})

and hence

{\rm Wh}(\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}\pi_{i};\mathfrak{p})={\rm Wh}((\overline{\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}}\pi_{i})|_{\overline{M_{\beta}^{[n]}}};\mathfrak{p})\simeq\bigoplus_{s\in M_{\beta}/M_{\beta}^{[n]}}{\rm Wh}({}^{s}\pi_{\flat};\mathfrak{p})\simeq\left|M_{\beta}/M_{\beta}^{[n]}\right|\cdot\prod_{i}{\rm Wh}(\pi_{i};\mathfrak{p}_{i}).

This coupled with (
6.5
) shows that
\mathfrak{p}=(\mathfrak{p}_{i})_{i}
supports the degenerate Whittaker model of
\pi
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
supports degenerate Whittaker model of
\pi_{i}
for every
i
.

∎

Now we may state and prove the main theorem.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\overline{G_{r}}
be an
n
-fold Kazhdan–Patterson cover or a Savin cover. Assume
p\nmid n
. Let
Z(\mathfrak{m})\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
be associated with a multisegment
\mathfrak{m}
and let
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
be the partition given as in (
5.1
). Then

(6.6)

{\rm WF}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))=\left\{\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\right\}.

In particular, the wavefront set of
Z(\mathfrak{m})
is a singleton set.

Proof.

In view of the equality (
6.2
), it suffices to show

(6.7)

\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))=\left\{\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\right\}.

Note it follows from
Corollary
5.2
that
{\rm Wh}(J_{U_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}}}(Z(\mathfrak{m})))\neq 0
. Applying this and (
6.3
) to
\mathfrak{p}:=\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\pi:=Z(\mathfrak{m})
, we get
{\rm Wh}(Z(\mathfrak{m});\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})\neq 0
, i.e.,
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
supports degenerate Whittaker model of
Z(\mathfrak{m})
.

To prove (
6.7
), it now suffices to show that
\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))\leqslant\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Furthermore, we will show that

(6.8)

{\rm WF}(\Pi(\mathfrak{m}))=\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(\Pi(\mathfrak{m}))=\left\{\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\right\},

which implies
{\rm WF}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))=\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))\leqslant\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Now, by a result of Mœglin–Waldspurger
[
34
]
*II.1, we have

(6.9)

{\rm WF}(\Pi(\mathfrak{m}))={\rm Ind}_{M_{\beta}}^{G_{r}}{\rm WF}(\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}Z(\Delta_{i})),

where
\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}Z(\Delta_{i})
is the metaplectic tensor product of
Z(\Delta_{i})\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r_{i}}})
.
Using Lemma
6.1
for
\pi_{i}=Z(\Delta_{i})
, we get

\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}Z(\Delta_{i}))=\prod_{i}\mathcal{N}_{\rm{deg}}(Z(\Delta_{i})),

or equivalently

{\rm WF}(\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}Z(\Delta_{i}))=\prod_{i}{\rm WF}(Z(\Delta_{i})).

To proceed for (
6.8
), for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, we consider
c_{i}\geqslant 0,0\leqslant d_{i}<n(\rho_{i})
such that

l_{i}=c_{i}\cdot n(\rho_{i})+d_{i}.

By Corollary
4.7
, the partition
((r_{i}^{0}\cdot n(\rho_{i}))^{c_{i}},r_{i}^{0}d_{i})
corresponds to the largest nilpotent orbit that contributes to a semi-Whittaker model of
Z(\Delta_{i})
. Thus using (
6.4
) we have

{\rm WF}(Z(\Delta_{i}))=((r_{i}^{0}\cdot n(\rho_{i}))^{c_{i}},r_{i}^{0}d_{i}).

It can be checked easily that this partition is exactly
\lambda_{\Delta_{i}}
, i.e., the equality (
6.6
) holds for the single segment
\mathfrak{m}=\Delta_{1}
case.
From the definition of
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
it is easy to see that

\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{\Delta_{i}}={\rm Ind}_{M_{\beta}}^{G_{r}}(\lambda_{\Delta_{1}},...,\lambda_{\Delta_{k}}),

where the right hand side means the induction of orbits. In view of (
6.9
), this gives (
6.8
) as desired. The proof is completed.
∎

6.2.
Relation with local Langlands correspondence

In this subsection, for Kazhdan–Patterson covers we reformulate Theorem
6.2
in the framework of
[
15
]
using a version of the hypothetical local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for such
\overline{G_{r}}
, and also the covering Barbasch–Vogan duality.

Let
\overline{G_{r}}
be a Kazhdan–Patterson cover or a Savin cover. Let
\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}
be the complex dual group of
\overline{G_{r}}
. One has

\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}\simeq\begin{cases}\left\{(g,\gamma)\in G_{r}(\mathbbm{C})\times G_{1}(\mathbbm{C}):\det(g)=\gamma^{d_{r}}\right\}&\text{ for KP-covers},\\
G_{r}(\mathbbm{C})&\text{ for S-covers}.\end{cases}

Clearly we have a natural group homomorphism

\eta:\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}\longrightarrow G_{r}(\mathbbm{C}),

which is the identity for S-covers and is the projection on the first coordinate for KP-covers.

For every partition
\mathfrak{p}^{\vee}=(p_{1},...,p_{k})
of
r
, viewed as a nilpotent orbit of
\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}
, we define

d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p}^{\vee})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{s}(p_{i};n_{\alpha})

with the partition
\mathfrak{s}(p_{i};n_{\alpha})
given by

(6.10)

\mathfrak{s}(p_{i};n_{\alpha}):=(n_{\alpha}^{a_{i}},b_{i})=(\underbrace{n_{\alpha},n_{\alpha},...,n_{\alpha}}_{a_{i}},b_{i}),

where
p_{i}=a_{i}\cdot n_{\alpha}+b_{i},0\leqslant b_{i}<n_{\alpha}
. Here, the summation of partition is the usual one, for example,
(5,4,2,2)+(6,3)+(5,2,2)=(16,9,4,2)
. We view
d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{p}^{\vee})
as a nilpotent orbit for
{\bf G}_{r}(F^{\rm alg})
. This gives the covering Barbasch–Vogan duality for
\overline{G}_{r}
:

d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}:\mathcal{N}(\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}({\bf G}_{r}(F^{\rm alg})).

Note that
n_{\alpha}=n
for KP-covers, and
n_{\alpha}=n/\gcd(n,2)
for S-covers.

For
G_{r}
, we know the map
\mathcal{N}(G_{r})\rightarrow\mathcal{N}({\bf G}_{r}(F^{\rm alg}))
given by
\mathcal{O}\mapsto\mathcal{O}\otimes F^{\rm alg}
is a bijection and thus we identify the two sets.
From the prior work
[
15
,
16
]
, it is expected that one has

{\rm WF}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))=\left\{d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}(\phi_{{\rm AZ}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))}))\right\},

where
{\rm AZ}
is the Aubert–Zelevinsky involution discussed as in
[
15
]
*§2.3, and
\phi_{\pi}
is the hypothetical L-parameter associated with
\pi
.

To show that Theorem
6.2
is compatible with the above speculation, in the remaining of this subsection, we consider Kazhdan–Patterson covers exclusively and utilize a version of LLC that arises from the metaplectic correspondence for discrete series given by Flicker, Kazhdan and Patterson
[
28
,
29
,
13
]
. Indeed, from loc. cit. we see that every genuine supercuspidal representation
\rho\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
can be lifted to a certain discrete series
{\rm MC}(\rho)\in{\rm Irr}(G_{r})
. Then it is natural to consider

\phi_{\rho}:=\phi_{{\rm MC}(\rho)}:\text{W}_{F}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})\longrightarrow G_{r}(\mathbbm{C}).

Let
Z(\mathfrak{m})\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
be associated with
\mathfrak{m}=(\Delta_{1},...,\Delta_{k}),\Delta_{i}=[0,l_{i}-1]_{\rho_{i}}
and
\rho_{i}
being a supercuspidal representation. Let
L(\mathfrak{m})
be the unique irreducible quotient associated with
\mathfrak{m}
as in
[
27
]
. Then we know
Z(\mathfrak{m})={\rm AZ}(L(\mathfrak{m}))
, which follows from the same argument as in
[
33
, Proposition A.7]
. Also, the metaplectic lifting of
\rho_{i}
is of the form

{\rm MC}(\rho_{i})=L(\Delta_{i}^{\sharp}),\text{ with }\Delta_{i}^{\sharp}=[0,l(\rho_{i})-1]_{\rho_{i}^{\sharp}},

with
\rho_{i}^{\sharp}\in{\rm Cusp}(G_{r_{i}^{\sharp}}),r_{i}^{\sharp}:=r_{i}^{0}/l(\rho_{i})
.

For each
i
, let
\phi_{\rho_{i}^{\sharp}}
be the L-parameter of
\rho_{i}^{\sharp}
. We call the homomorphism

\phi_{L(\mathfrak{m})}:W_{F}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})\longrightarrow G_{r}(\mathbbm{C})

given by

\phi_{L(\mathfrak{m})}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\phi_{\rho_{i}^{\sharp}}\left|\cdot\right|^{\frac{l_{i}l(\rho_{i})-1}{2}}\boxtimes S_{l_{i}\cdot l(\rho_{i})}

the L-parameter of
L(\mathfrak{m})
. A caveat is that it is not valued in
\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}
by definition. We will show that the parameter
\phi_{L(\mathfrak{m})}
defined above can be lifted through
\eta
naturally and thus is valued in
\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}
.

First, consider the torus
{\bf T}_{z}\subseteq Z({\bf G}_{r})
associated with the lattice
c_{n,r}\cdot e_{c}
, where
c_{n,r}:=n/d_{r}
and
e_{c}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}
. It gives the the group
T_{z}
of
F
-points, and we know

Z(\overline{G_{r}})\simeq\overline{T_{z}}.

Let
\omega:Z(\overline{G_{r}})\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
be a genuine character.
By choosing a distinguished genuine character
\chi_{\psi}:Z(\overline{T})\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
as in
[
14
, §7]
, viewed as a character of
Z(\overline{G_{r}})
by restriction, we get a linear character

\omega^{\psi}:=\omega/\chi_{\psi}:T_{z}\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}.

Consider
L(\mathfrak{m})\in{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G_{r}})
, which has a central character
\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}
and thus gives

\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}:T_{z}\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}.

The local Langlands correspondence for
T_{z}
gives the L-parameter

\phi_{\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}}:W_{F}\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}

of
\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}
, which is explicitly given by
\phi_{\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}}(a)=\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}((c_{n,r}\cdot e_{c})\otimes{\rm rec}(a))
, where
{\rm rec}:W_{F}\rightarrow F^{\times}
is the Artin reciprocity for
F
.
Now we consider the map

\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})}:W_{F}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})\longrightarrow G_{r}(\mathbbm{C})\times G_{1}(\mathbbm{C})

given by

\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})}(a,g):=(\phi_{L(\mathfrak{m})}(a,g),\phi_{\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}}(a)).

Lemma 6.3
.

The parameter
\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})}
is valued in
\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}
.

Proof.

To save notation, we identify
a
with
{\rm rec}(a)
, and also write
a^{y}:=y\otimes a
for
y\in Y_{G_{r}}
and
a
. Write
\left|a\right|=\left|{\rm rec}(a)\right|=q^{-v_{a}}
for some
v_{a}\in\mathbbm{Z}
.

In view of the definition of
\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}
for KP covers, it suffices to show that

(6.11)

\det(\phi_{L(\mathfrak{m})}(a,g))=(\phi_{\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}}(a))^{d_{r}}.

By the definition of
\phi_{L(\mathfrak{m})}
, the left-hand side of (
6.11
) is equal to

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\det(\phi_{\rho_{i}^{\sharp}}(a))^{l_{i}\cdot l(\rho_{i})}\cdot q^{-v_{a}r_{i}^{0}l_{i}\cdot\frac{l_{i}l(\rho_{i})-1}{2}}.

To compute the right-hand side, we first recall that there is a natural section
{\bf s}:T\to\overline{T}
, which is actually a splitting when restricted to
(nY_{G_{r}})\otimes F^{\times}\subseteq T
. Also, one property of
\chi_{\psi}
(see
[
14
, §7]
) is that
\chi_{\psi}({\bf s}(a^{ny}))=1
for all
y\in Y_{G_{r}}
and
a
. This gives that

(\phi_{\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}}(a))^{d_{r}}=\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\psi}(a^{ne_{c}})=(\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}/\chi_{\psi})(a^{ne_{c}})=\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}({\bf s}(a^{ne_{c}})).

For every
i
, let
e_{c,i}\in Y_{G_{r_{i}^{0}}}
be the natural generator of the cocharacter lattice of
Z(G_{r_{i}^{0}})
. Since
L(\mathfrak{m})
is a constituent of the induced representation

\bigtimes_{i,j}\rho_{i}\left|\cdot\right|^{j/n(\rho_{i})}

with
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
and
0\leqslant j\leqslant l_{i}-1
, we see that

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}({\bf s}(a^{ne_{c}}))=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=0}^{l_{i}-1}\omega_{\rho_{i}}({\bf s}(a^{ne_{c,i}}))\cdot\left|{\bf s}(a^{ne_{c,i}})\right|^{j/n(\rho_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\omega_{\rho_{i}}({\bf s}(a^{ne_{c,i}}))^{l_{i}}\cdot q^{-v_{a}r_{i}^{0}l(\rho_{i})\cdot\frac{l_{i}(l_{i}-1)}{2}},

where
\omega_{\rho_{i}}
means the central character of
\rho_{i}
. However, the property of metaplectic correspondence shows that (see
[
13
, Pg. 95-96]
)

\omega_{\rho_{i}}({\bf s}(a^{ne_{c,i}}))=\omega_{{\rm MC}(\rho_{i})}(a^{e_{c,i}}),

where
{\rm MC}(\rho_{i})=L([\rho_{i}^{\sharp},\rho_{i}^{\sharp}\left|\cdot\right|,...,\rho_{i}^{\sharp}\left|\cdot\right|^{l(\rho_{i})-1}])\in{\rm Irr}(G_{r_{i}^{0}})
. Also, it is easy to see that

\omega_{{\rm MC}(\rho_{i})}(a^{e_{c,i}})=\det(\phi_{\rho_{i}^{\sharp}})^{l(\rho_{i})}\cdot q^{-v_{a}r_{i}^{\sharp}\cdot\frac{l(\rho_{i})(l(\rho_{i})-1)}{2}}.

Now, using the above three displayed equalities, one can simplify
\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}({\bf s}(a^{ne_{c}}))
and show that (
6.11
) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

Thus we have a well-defined map
\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})}:\text{W}_{F}\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})\to\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}
, which is called the L-parameter of
L(\mathfrak{m})
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Keep notation as above. We have

{\rm WF}(Z(\mathfrak{m}))=\left\{d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})}))\right\}.

Proof.

We have

\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})})=((l_{1}\cdot l(\rho_{1}))^{r_{1}^{\sharp}},...,(l_{i}\cdot l(\rho_{i}))^{r_{i}^{\sharp}},...,(l_{k}\cdot l(\rho_{k}))^{r_{k}^{\sharp}}).

Now a direct computation shows that
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}=d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})}))
. Indeed, if we write

l_{i}l(\rho_{i})=n\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}

with
\alpha_{i}\geqslant 0
and
0\leqslant\beta_{i}<n
. We get
\alpha_{i}\geqslant 1
if and only if
l_{i}l(\rho_{i})\geqslant n
, equivalently,
l_{i}\geqslant n(\rho_{i})
.
Then the leading numeral of
d_{BV,G_{r}}^{(n)}(\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\phi}_{L(\mathfrak{m})}))
is equal to

\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}^{\sharp}\cdot\min(n,l_{i}l(\rho_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}^{\sharp}\cdot l(\rho_{i})\cdot\min(n(\rho_{i}),l_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}^{0}\cdot\min(n(\rho_{i}),l_{i}),

which is exactly
k_{1}
. The other numerals in
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
can be checked in a similar way.
∎

6.3.
Some remarks

For a partition
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{s})
, we write

{\rm H}(\mathfrak{p}):=s\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm W}(\mathfrak{p}):={\rm H}(\mathfrak{p}^{\top})=\max\left\{p_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant s\right\}.

We can view
{\rm H}(\mathfrak{p})
and
{\rm W}(\mathfrak{p})
as the height and width (and thus the “shape”) of the Young diagram associated with
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
\pi\in{\rm Irr}(G_{r})
and consider the L-parameter
\phi_{{\rm AZ}(\pi)}
of
{\rm AZ}(\pi)
.
We get

(6.12)

{\rm H}(\mathcal{O}(\phi_{{\rm AZ}(\pi)}))=\max\left\{k:\ \pi^{(k)}\neq 0\right\}.

On the other hand, if we assume that
\pi
can always be “lifted” to a genuine representation
\pi_{n}
of KP-cover or S-cover, for every
n
; then from the above disucssion, we expect that

(6.13)

{\rm W}(\mathcal{O}(\phi_{{\rm AZ}(\pi)}))=\min\left\{n:\pi_{n}\text{ is generic}\right\}.

The relations (
6.12
) and (
6.13
) above can be viewed as conservation-alike, and they hold for Iwahori-spherical representations
\pi
, whenever the parameter
\phi_{{\rm AZ}(\pi)}:\text{WD}_{F}\to G_{r}(\mathbbm{C})
can be lifted through the natural homomorphism
\eta:\overline{G_{r}}^{\vee}\to G_{r}(\mathbbm{C})
for a KP-cover or S-cover
\overline{G_{r}}
.
