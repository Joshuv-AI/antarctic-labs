Collocation-based Robust Physics Informed Neural Networks for time-dependent simulations of pollution propagation under thermal inversion conditions on Spitsbergen Journal: Computers and Mathematics with Applications
Leszek Siwik, Maciej Sikora, Natalia Leszczyńska, Tomasz Maciej Ciesielski, Eirik Valseth, Manuela Bastidas Olivares, Marcin Łoś, Tomasz Służalec, Jacek Leszczyński, Maciej Paszyński

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a Physics-Informed Neural Network framework for time-dependent simulations of pollution propagation originating from moving emission sources. We formulate a robust variational framework for the time-dependent advection-diffusion problem and establish the boundedness and inf-sup stability of the corresponding discrete weak formulation. Based on this mathematical foundation, we construct a robust loss function that is directly related to the true approximation error, defined as the difference between the neural network approximation and the (unknown) exact solution. Additionally, a collocation-based strategy is introduced to speed up neural network training. As a case study, we investigate pollution propagation caused by snowmobile traffic in Longyearbyen, Spitsbergen, supported by detailed in-field measurements collected using dedicated sensors. The proposed framework is applied to analyze the effects of thermal inversion on pollutant accumulation. Our results demonstrate that thermal inversion traps dense and humid air masses near the ground, significantly enhancing particulate matter (PM) concentration and worsening local air quality.

Neural networks estimating the pollution propagation from snowmobiles

To model the propagation of air pollution, we consider the following advection-diffusion equation:
Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be an open set and
I=(0,T)
with
T>0
. We seek the pollution propagation scalar field
\Omega\times I\ni(\mathbf{x},t)\rightarrow u(\mathbf{x},t)\in{\cal R}
such that:

\displaystyle{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot(\epsilon\nabla u)&+{\bf b}\cdot\nabla u=f(\mathbf{x},t),&\mbox{in}&\;\Omega\times I,\\
u&=0,&\mbox{on}&\;\partial\Omega\times I,\\
u(\mathbf{x},0)&=u_{0},&\mbox{on}&\;\Omega\times\{0\}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.}

(1)

In (
1
),
\epsilon
is the diffusion coefficient,
\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{x},t)
the advection field (modeling the air movement), and
u_{0}
is a given initial concentration pf pollution.

2.1
Physics Informed Neural Networks

To numerically solve (
1
), we first employ PINNs
[
32
,
7
,
8
]
, to approximate the solution of partial differential equations (PDEs) by neural networks. It assumes that neural network input is the point in the space-time domain
(\mathbf{x},t)
and the output from the neural network is the concentration of the pollution at point
\mathbf{x}
at time moment
t

u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t)

It minimizes the PDE residual:

\displaystyle{\cal RES}_{PDE}(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t))

\displaystyle=\left(\right.\frac{\partial u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t)}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot(\epsilon\nabla u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t))

\displaystyle\left.+{\bf b}\cdot\nabla u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t)-f(\mathbf{x},t)\right),

(2)

\displaystyle\mbox{for}\;(\mathbf{x},t)\in\Omega\times I,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{PDE}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\mathbf{x}_{i},t_{i})}\left({\cal RES}_{PDE}(u(\mathbf{x}_{i},t_{i}))\right)^{2},

together with the initial condition residual:

\displaystyle{\cal RES}_{Init}(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},0))

\displaystyle=\left(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},0)-u_{0}(\mathbf{x})\right),\;\mbox{for}\;\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,

(3)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{Init}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\mathbf{x}_{i},0)}\left({\cal RES}_{Init}(u(\mathbf{x}_{i},0))\right)^{2},

and the boundary condition residual:

\displaystyle{\cal RES}_{BC}(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t))

\displaystyle=\left(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t)-0\right),\;\mbox{for}\;(\mathbf{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times I,

(4)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{BC}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\mathbf{x}_{i},t_{i})}\left({\cal RES}_{BC}(u(\mathbf{x}_{i},t_{i}))\right)^{2}.

Note that the residual loss function is based on the strong residual of the PDE and is computed by selecting a set of collocation points
(\mathbf{x},t)\in\Omega\times I
. Additionally, note that the boundary loss uses selected collocation points located on the boundary
(\mathbf{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times I
, and the initial loss uses the collocation points selected from the spatial domain
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
at the initial time.

2.2
Variational Physics Informed Neural Networks

In this section, we present a transformation of the strong-form residuals of PINN into the weak-form residual of Variational Physics Informed Neural Networks
[
21
]
. For this purpose, we consider the space-time domain
\Omega\times I
and perform a shift with respect to the initial boundary condition.
We define the shift
u_{shift}
such that:

\displaystyle u(\mathbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=w(\mathbf{x},t)+u_{shift}(\mathbf{x},t),

(5)

\displaystyle u_{shift}(\mathbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{1}{T}t)u_{0}(\mathbf{x}).

where
w(\mathbf{x},0)=0
fulfills the zero initial boundary condition, thus, we seek a solution of:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}-

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\epsilon\nabla w)+{\bf b}\cdot\nabla w=

\displaystyle\underbrace{f+\frac{\partial u_{shift}}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot(\epsilon\nabla u_{shift})+{\bf b}\cdot\nabla u_{shift}}_{\mathbf{F}},

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\mbox{in}\;\Omega\times I

(6)

In the following, we overwrite the notation and replace
w
with
u
.
We define
V=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega\times I)
and select a family of test functions
v\in V
. We multiply the strong formulation (
6
) and integrate it by parts:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t},v\right)_{\Omega\times I}+\left(\epsilon\nabla u,\nabla v\right)_{\Omega\times I}

\displaystyle+\left({\bf b}\cdot\nabla u,v\right)_{\Omega\times I}+(\mathbf{F},v)_{\Omega\times I},

\displaystyle\forall v\in V.

(7)

During the derivation of the weak formulation, we integrated by parts and employed the fact that test functions are zero on the boundary to cancel out the boundary integral term.

Since we have eliminated the non-zero initial boundary conditions, we can now introduce a single loss based on the weak residual and test with the functions that are zero on the initial boundary.

\displaystyle{\cal RES}_{PDE-weak}(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t),v(\mathbf{x},t))

\displaystyle=

(8)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial u_{\theta}}{\partial t},v\right)_{\Omega\times I}+\left(\epsilon\nabla u_{\theta},\nabla v\right)_{\Omega\times I}

\displaystyle+\left({\bf b}\cdot\nabla u_{\theta},v\right)_{\Omega\times I}+(\mathbf{F},v)_{\Omega\times I}

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{PDE-weak}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v(\mathbf{x},t)}\left({\cal RES}_{PDE-weak}(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t),v(\mathbf{x},t))\right)^{2}.

Note that this loss function is based on the weak-form residual, and it is computed by selecting a set of test functions
v\in V
. Also, having this weak residual, we do not need to minimize the initial and boundary condition residuals, since they are already included in the weak residual.

2.3
Robust Variational Physics Informed Neural Networks

As has been shown in
[
34
]
, the VPINN loss is not robust and is not directly related to the true error.
Thus, to control the convergence of the training, we introduce the Robust Variational Physics Informed Neural Network
[
34
]
method with the following robust loss function:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{PDE-robust}

\displaystyle={\cal RES}_{PDE-weak}^{T}(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t),v(\mathbf{x},t)){\cal G}_{vpinn}^{-1}{\cal RES}_{PDE-weak}(u_{\theta}(\mathbf{x},t),v(\mathbf{x},t)),

(9)

where
{\cal G}_{vpinn}^{-1}
is the inverse of the Gram matrix
{\cal G}_{vpinn}
. The Gram matrix is selected using the inner product norm of the test space
V
. In our case, we select:

\displaystyle{{\cal G}_{vpinn}}_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\left(v_{m},v_{n}\right)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega\times I)}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{m}(\mathbf{x},t)\cdot\nabla v_{n}(\mathbf{x},t)d\mathbf{x}dt,

(10)

where
v_{m},v_{n}\in V
is the selected set of test functions. Thus, the dimension of the Gram matrix
{\cal G}_{vpinn}
is equal to the dimension of the test space.

2.4
Collocation-based Robust Variational Physics Informed Neural Networks

The last step in our derivation is to replace the Robust VPINN with the Collocation-based PINN
[
46
]
. This way, we replace the continuous description of the RVPINN and the expensive three-dimensional integration with a discrete domain constructed over the discrete set of collocation points. We also replace the gradients of the weak formulation by discrete finite-difference gradients computed over the collocation points.
To do that, we first define the set of points:

\Omega_{h}\times I_{h}:=\{(ih_{x},jh_{y},kh_{t})\in(0,1)^{2}\times(0,T):0\leq i\leq N_{x},0\leq j\leq N_{y},0\leq j\leq N_{t}\},

(11)

where
h_{x}=1/N_{x},h_{y}=1/N_{y},h_{t}=T/N_{t}
. We consider:

D_{h}:=\{u:\Omega_{h}\times I_{h}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\}\cong\mathbb{R}^{(N_{x}+1)(N_{y}+1)(N_{t}+1)},

(12)

and equip it with the following discrete inner product and induced norm:

(u,v)_{h}:=h_{x}h_{y}h_{t}\sum_{p\in\Omega_{h}\times I_{h}}u(p)v(p),\quad\|u\|_{h}^{2}:=(u,u)_{h},\qquad u,v\in D_{h}.

(13)

Now, we follow the notation convention below for simplicity:

u_{i,j,k}:=u(ih_{x},jh_{y},kh_{t}),\qquad 0\leq i\leq N_{x},0\leq j\leq N_{y},0\leq k\leq N_{t},

(14)

We also introduce a space that considers a zero-Dirichlet boundary condition at the initial state:

D_{0,h}=\{u\in D_{h}:u_{i,j,0}=0,0\leq i\leq N_{x},0\leq j\leq N_{y}\}.

(15)

We introduce a canonical orthonormal basis for
D_{h}
given by a set of functions
\delta_{i,j,k}:\Omega_{h}\times I_{h}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq i\leq N_{x},0\leq j\leq N_{y},0\leq k\leq N_{t},
that behave as Kronecker deltas over
\Omega_{h}\times I_{h}

\delta_{i,j,k}(\mathbf{x},t)=\displaystyle{\left\{\begin{aligned} &1\quad\textrm{ if }\mathbf{x}=x_{i,j,k},\\
&0\quad\textrm{ if }\mathbf{x}\neq x_{i,j,k}.\end{aligned}\right.}

(16)

We introduce finite difference gradient operations, given by:

\displaystyle\nabla_{+}u_{i,j,k}:=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{x+}u_{i,j,k},\nabla_{y+}u_{i,j,k},\nabla_{t+}u_{i,j,k}\right):=

(17)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u_{i+1,j,k}-u_{i,j,k}}{h_{x}},\frac{u_{i,j+1,k}-u_{i,j,k}}{h_{y}},\frac{u_{i,j,k+1}-u_{i,j,k}}{h_{t}},\right),

(18)

\displaystyle\nabla_{-}u_{i,j,k}:=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{x-}u_{i,j,k},\nabla_{y-}u_{i,j,k},\nabla_{t-}u_{i,j,k}\right):=

(19)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u_{i,,k}-u_{i-1,j,k}}{h_{x}},\frac{u_{i,j,k}-u_{i,j-1,k}}{h_{y}},\frac{u_{i,j,k}-u_{i,j,k-1}}{h_{t}}\right),

(20)

for
0\leq i\pm 1,j\pm 1\leq N
. As a result, we can define the following discrete inner product according to these gradient values:

\displaystyle(u,v)_{\nabla,h}

\displaystyle:=(\nabla_{x+}u,\nabla_{x+}v)_{h_{x}}+(\nabla_{y+}u,\nabla_{y+}v)_{h_{y}}+(\nabla_{t+}u,\nabla_{t+}v)_{h_{t}}

(21)

\displaystyle\phantom{:}=(\nabla_{x-}u,\nabla_{x-}v)_{h_{x}}+(\nabla_{y-}u,\nabla_{y-}v)_{h_{y}},(\nabla_{t-}u,\nabla_{t-}v)_{h_{t}},

(22)

with the corresponding induced norm:

k\|u\|_{\nabla,h}^{2}:=(u,u)_{\nabla,h}=\|\nabla_{x+}u\|_{h_{x}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{y+}u\|_{h_{y}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{t+}u\|_{h_{t}}^{2}.

(23)

We now rewrite the weak formulation into the discrete weak form. We start from the discrete strong form:

\nabla_{t+}u+\beta_{x}\nabla_{x+}u+\beta_{y}\nabla_{y+}u-\epsilon\Delta_{h}u=f,

(24)

where
f,\beta_{x},\beta_{y}\in D_{0,h}
are given source and coefficient functions, and
\Delta_{h}
is the discrete Laplacian defined pointwise as:

\Delta_{h}u_{i,j,k}:=\frac{u_{i+1,j,k}-2u_{i,j,k}+u_{i-1,j,k}}{{h_{x}}^{2}}+\frac{u_{i,j+1,k}-2u_{i,j,k}+u_{i,j-1,k}}{{h_{y}}^{2}}+\frac{u_{i,j,k+1}-2u_{i,j,k}+u_{i,j,k-1}}{{h_{t}}^{2}}.

(25)

Testing with Kronecker Deltas:

v\in D_{0,h}

\displaystyle(\nabla_{t+}u+\beta_{x}\nabla_{x+}u+\beta_{y}\nabla_{y+}u-\epsilon\Delta_{h}u,v)_{h}=(f,v)_{h},

(26)

We perform discrete integration by parts on the term with
the Laplacian, to obtain the following discrete weak variational reformulation: find
u\in D_{0,h}
such that:

\displaystyle\overbrace{(\nabla_{t+}+\beta_{x}\nabla_{x+}u+\beta_{y}\nabla_{y+}u,v)_{h}+\epsilon(\nabla_{+}u,\nabla_{+}v)_{h}}^{b(u,v)}=\overbrace{(f,v)_{h}}^{l(v)},\qquad\forall v\in D_{0,h},

(27)

where
b
and

are the corresponding discrete bilinear and linear forms over

Following

\text{RES}(u)=\{b(u,r(u)_{i,j})-l(r(u)_{i,j})\}_{0<i<N_{x},0<j<N_{y}0<k<N_{t}}

(28)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{crvpinn}=\text{RES}(u)^{T}\;\mathbf{G}^{-1}\;\text{RES}(u).

(29)

where

We will now construct the Gram matrix employing the Kronecker delta test functions as:

3
Error Analysis and Residual Minimization

In this section, we provide the detailed mathematical justification for minimizing the weak-form residual norm. We establish that the norm of the Riesz representative of the residual is spectrally equivalent to the true error norm for the Space-Time Convection-Diffusion equation.

Functional Spaces and Problem Setting.
Let
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
be the space-time domain. We define the trial space
\mathbb{U}
and test space
\mathbb{V}
as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\mid u(\cdot,0)=0\right\},

(31)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}

\displaystyle=L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)).

(32)

We equip these spaces with the norms
\|u\|_{\mathbb{U}}:=(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2})^{1/2}
and
\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}:=\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Q)}
. The variational problem is to find
u^{*}\in\mathbb{U}
such that
B(u^{*},v)=l(v)
for all
v\in\mathbb{V}
, where the bilinear form
B
and linear functional
l
are defined by:

B(u,v)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}v+\epsilon\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u)v\right)d\mathbf{x}\,dt,\quad l(v)=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}fv\,d\mathbf{x}\,dt.

(33)

Riesz Representation of the Residual.
Let
u_{\theta}\in\mathbb{U}
be an approximate solution. The weak residual functional
\mathcal{R}(u_{\theta})\in\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
is defined by
\langle\mathcal{R}(u_{\theta}),v\rangle=B(u_{\theta},v)-l(v)
. By the Riesz Representation Theorem, there exists a unique
r_{\theta}\in\mathbb{V}
such that
(r_{\theta},v)_{\mathbb{V}}=B(u_{\theta},v)-l(v)
for all
v\in\mathbb{V}
. Since
B(u^{*},v)=l(v)
, this implies the fundamental error identity:

(r_{\theta},v)_{\mathbb{V}}=B(u_{\theta}-u^{*},v)=B(e,v),\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{V},

(34)

where
e=u_{\theta}-u^{*}
is the approximation error.

Boundedness and Stability.
To relate
\|r_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}}
to
\|e\|_{\mathbb{U}}
, we prove that
B
is continuous and coercive (inf-sup stable).

1.

The
Continuity
follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Specifically, we bound each term of the bilinear form defined on the space-time domain
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
:

B(u,v)=\underbrace{\int_{Q}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}v\,d\mathbf{x}dt}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{Q}\epsilon\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,d\mathbf{x}dt}_{I_{2}}+\underbrace{\int_{Q}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u)v\,d\mathbf{x}dt}_{I_{3}}.

(35)

We bound each term separately, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Poincaré inequality
\|v\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq C_{P}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Q)}=C_{P}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}
.

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t},v\right)_{L^{2}(Q)}\right|\leq\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq C_{P}\|u\|_{\mathbb{U}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(36)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle=\left|\epsilon(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{L^{2}(Q)}\right|\leq\epsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq\epsilon\|u\|_{\mathbb{U}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(37)

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle=\left|(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u,v)_{L^{2}(Q)}\right|\leq\|\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq C_{b}C_{P}\|u\|_{\mathbb{U}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

(38)

where
C_{b}=\|\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}
is the maximum magnitude of the velocity field. So, we obtain:

|B(u,v)|\leq(C_{P}+\epsilon+C_{b}C_{P})\|u\|_{\mathbb{U}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

(39)

Setting
M=C_{P}(1+C_{b})+\epsilon
, the continuity condition is satisfied.

2.

The
Inf-Sup Stability
requires showing
\sup_{v\neq 0}\frac{B(u,v)}{\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}}\geq\gamma\|u\|_{\mathbb{U}}
. Choosing
v=u
(valid since
\mathbb{U}\subset\mathbb{V}
) and utilizing the properties
u(0)=0
and
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}=0
, we obtain the energy estimate:

B(u,u)=\underbrace{\int_{Q}\partial_{t}u\cdot u}_{\geq 0}+\epsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\underbrace{\int_{Q}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u)u}_{0}\geq\epsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}.

(40)

This controls the spatial part of the norm. To control the time derivative, we observe that
\langle\partial_{t}u,v\rangle=B(u,v)-\langle\text{spatial terms},v\rangle
. By triangle inequality and the continuity of spatial terms,
\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\mathbb{V}^{\prime}}\lesssim\sup_{v}\frac{B(u,v)}{\|v\|}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(H^{1})}
. Combining these yields the stability condition
\|u\|_{\mathbb{U}}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\sup_{v}\frac{B(u,v)}{\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}}
.

Then, we get:

\|r_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}=(r_{\theta},r_{\theta})_{\mathbb{V}}=B(e,r_{\theta})\leq M\|e\|_{\mathbb{U}}\|r_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}}\implies\|r_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq M\|e\|_{\mathbb{U}}.

(41)

For the
Lower Bound
, we use the Inf-Sup stability of
B
. There exists a
v\in\mathbb{V}
such that
\gamma\|e\|_{\mathbb{U}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq B(e,v)
. Substituting
B(e,v)=(r_{\theta},v)_{\mathbb{V}}
and applying Cauchy-Schwarz:

\gamma\|e\|_{\mathbb{U}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq(r_{\theta},v)_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\|r_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}}\implies\|e\|_{\mathbb{U}}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\|r_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

(42)

In conclusion, the derived bounds
\frac{1}{M}\|r_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\|u_{\theta}-u^{*}\|_{\mathbb{U}}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\|r_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}}
confirm that the Riesz residual norm is an equivalent metric to the true error.

We let
\{v_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}\subset\mathbb{V}=L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
be a linearly independent set of space-time test functions and let:

\mathbb{V}_{M}:=\text{span}\{v_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}.

(43)

Then, given an approximate solution
u_{\theta}\in\mathbb{U}
, we define the orthogonal projection of the continuous Riesz residual
r(u_{\theta})\in\mathbb{V}
onto the finite-dimensional subspace
\mathbb{V}_{M}
, denoted
r_{M}(u_{\theta})
, as the unique element in
\mathbb{V}_{M}
satisfying:

(r_{M}(u_{\theta}),v_{m})_{\mathbb{V}}=B(u_{\theta},v_{m})-l(v_{m}),\quad 1\leq m\leq M.

(44)

Recalling our specific space-time formulation, the right-hand side corresponds exactly to the weak residual value for the
m
-th test function:

B(u_{\theta},v_{m})-l(v_{m})=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial u_{\theta}}{\partial t}v_{m}+\epsilon\nabla u_{\theta}\cdot\nabla v_{m}+(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u_{\theta})v_{m}+fv_{m}\right)d\mathbf{x}\,dt.

(45)

Thus, the norm of the projected residual is given by:

\|r_{M}(u_{\theta})\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}=\mathbf{r}(u_{\theta})^{T}\mathbf{G}^{-1}\mathbf{r}(u_{\theta})=:\|\mathbf{r}(u_{\theta})\|_{\mathbf{G}^{-1}}^{2},

(46)

where:

1.

\mathbf{r}(u_{\theta})=[B(u_{\theta},v_{m})-l(v_{m})]_{m=1}^{M}
is the vector of weak residuals.

2.

\mathbf{G}=[(v_{m},v_{n})_{\mathbb{V}}]_{mn}
is the space-time Gram matrix with entries defined by the test space inner product:

\mathbf{G}_{mn}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{m}(\mathbf{x},t)\cdot\nabla v_{n}(\mathbf{x},t)\,d\mathbf{x}\,dt.

(47)

As a result, we end up with a discretized residual minimization scheme given by:

\min_{u_{\theta}\in\mathbb{U}}\|\mathbf{r}(u_{\theta})\|_{\mathbf{G}^{-1}}^{2}.

(48)

In the CRVPINN method, we test with Kronecker deltas and consider a discrete domain of points.
However, it is possible to implement the CRVPINN method with a very simple modification of the PINN code
and still benefit from the better convergence provided by the robust loss function.
Namely, if we integrate back to the strong form multiplied by the Kronecker Deltas:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{t+}u+\beta_{x}\nabla_{x+}u+\beta_{y}\nabla_{y+}u-\epsilon\Delta_{h}u,\delta_{i,j,k})_{h}=(f,\delta_{i,j,k})_{h},

(49)

and:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{t+}u,\delta_{i,j,k})_{h}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{t+}u_{i,j,k},

(50)

\displaystyle(\beta_{x}\nabla_{x+}u,\delta_{i,j,k})_{h}=

\displaystyle\beta_{x}\nabla_{x+}u_{i,j,k},

\displaystyle(\beta_{y}\nabla_{y+}u,\delta_{i,j,k})_{h}=

\displaystyle\beta_{y}\nabla_{y+}u_{i,j,k},

\displaystyle-(\epsilon\Delta_{h}u,\delta_{i,j,k})_{h}=

\displaystyle-\epsilon\Delta_{h}u_{i,j,k},

\displaystyle(f,\delta_{i,j,k})_{h}=

\displaystyle f_{i,j,k}.

We recover the original PINN formulation of the residual estimated at the collocation points, thus:

\text{RES}(u)_{i,j,k}=\nabla_{t+}u_{i,j,k}+\beta_{x}\nabla_{x+}u_{i,j,k}+\beta_{y}\nabla_{y+}u_{i,j,k}-\epsilon\Delta_{h}u_{i,j,k}-f_{i,j,k},

(51)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{crvpinn}=\text{RES}(u)^{T}\;\mathbf{G}^{-1}\;\text{RES}(u).

(52)

where:

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{i,j,k;l,m,n}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{x}h_{y}h_{t}}\displaystyle{\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phantom{-}\,\,6\ \quad\textrm{ for }(i,j,k)=(l,m,n)\\

Conclusions

In the paper, we introduced the Physics Informed Neural Network code for time-dependent simulations of pollution propagation from moving objects. We introduced the robust loss function for time-dependent advection-diffusion problems.
We also introduced the collocation method to speed-up the training process.
As an interesting case study, we focused on simulations of pollution propagation from snowmobiles in the Spitzbergen area.
We also described detailed measurements of pollution generated by snowmobiles at Spitsbergen using the Airly sensors.
We focused on the time-dependent modeling of the thermal inversion with the advection-diffusion model.
Starting from the PINN formulation, we augmented the model into the Robust VPINN
to obtain the robust loss function for the time-dependent advection-diffusion problem.
We formulate, for the first time, the time-dependent RVPINN with the collocation method, introducing the Collocation-based RVPINN method (CRVPINN).
The CRVPINN method has been applied to analyze the thermal inversion effects on the accumulation of pollution generated by snowmobiles.
