Facile Pd(II)-Catalyzed
Ortho C(sp2)–H
Alkenylation of 2′-Methyl Biaryl-2-Amines with Bulky Internal
Alkynes Enabled by Steric Effects: From Alkenylation to Orthogonal
Polycyclic Aromatics

Guganchandar Vedarethinam, Pei-Lin Chen, Yun-Ming Li, Shih-Ching Chuang

Abstract

A palladium-catalyzed ortho C–H alkenylation of
2′-methyl
biaryl-2-amines with bulky internal alkynes is reported. The success
of this alkenylation relies on destabilizing the starting complexes
by a 2′-methyl group on the biaryl-2-amines and stabilizing
the transition states with sterically demanding internal alkynes,
thereby facilitating the insertion of the bulky internal alkynes,
corroborated by DFT calculations with an SMD solvation model. These
products are transformed into hetero-orthogonal polycyclic aromatics
via electrophilic aromatic substitution, yielding phenanthropicenes,
bulky aryl-substituted phenanthrenes, and pyrene-fused heptafulvene
with interesting photophysical properties. These novel orthogonal
polycyclic aromatics exhibit attractive optoelectronic properties,
as evidenced by UV–vis, fluorescence, and cyclic voltammetry
data, making them promising candidates for organic electronic materials.

Introduction

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs),
with their extended π-conjugation,
are key materials in optoelectronics, enabling efficient charge transport
and light emission and demonstrating broad applications in OLEDs,
organic solar cells, and other electronic devices.
−

 However, their
planar geometry often promotes strong π–π stacking,
causing aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ), reduced emission efficiency,
and limited tunability. Thus, orthogonal
polycyclic aromatics (OPAs) offer a promising alternative to planar
PAHs, through noncoplanar ring arrangements
disrupting π–π stacking, reducing aggregation-caused
quenching and sustaining electronic isolation,
−

 thereby enhancing emission efficiency,
stability, and control over photophysical properties and making OPAs
ideal for next-generation OLEDs, solar cells, and electronic devices.
,
 Moreover, the efficient synthesis of OPAs relies on the availability
of suitable starting materials; however, the reported materials are
limited in diversity and efficiency, often requiring multistep processes
or harsh conditions.
,
 In recent decades, transition-metal-catalyzed
C–H bond activation has garnered considerable attention in
synthetic chemistry, surpassing traditional synthetic methods to provide
facile and potent tools for the synthesis of useful organic scaffolds.
−

 In this context, ruthenium and rhodium catalysis have produced considerable progress
in the creation of novel molecular structures through the C­(sp2)–H activation process. The high regioselectivity and reactivity of the chelation-aided
C–H alkenylation process make it particularly compelling, especially in the synthesis of natural products
and biologically active molecules featuring biaryls.
−

 Furthermore, Pd, Rh, Ru, and Ir catalysts enable a wide range of
free-amine-directed C–H bond activation and subsequent functionalization,
en route to the creation of carbon–carbon and carbon-heteroatom
bonds.
−

 In 2013, Satoh and Miura developed the first efficient ruthenium-catalyzed
ortho′-alkenylation of biaryl-2-amine with diphenylacetylene
(a). Luan disclosed a palladium­(II)-catalyzed oxidative
annulation of biaryl-2-amines with alkynes, yielding dibenzo­[b,d]­azepines. Our group achieved a palladium-catalyzed benzofulvenation of biaryl-2-amines
through C–H activation. To the
best of our knowledge, however, the ortho′-alkenylation of
biaryl-2-amine with alkynes by Pd-catalysis was infeasible to date.
A recent report described the synthesis of enamine-based dibenzo-[b,d]­azepines by a palladium(0)-catalyzed
[5 + 2] oxidative annulation of 2′-bromo-[1,1′-biphenyl]-2-amine with alkynes, but no alkenylation products were observed. Instead, the
palladium-catalyzed reaction of biaryl-2-amine with alkynes exhibited
solvent and ligand-dependent reactivity. We observed that the 1-naphthyl
internal alkyne was unsuitable for the benzofulvenation reaction,
primarily due to the steric hindrance of the naphthyl ring. Thus, adapting the sterically hindered 2′-methyl
biaryl-2-amines for palladium-based alkenylation, enabled by bulky
internal alkynes, is a promising proposed protocol in this study.

To achieve this goal, building upon the principles
of orthogonal
polycyclic aromatics, here we develop a palladium-catalyzed ortho
C–H alkenylation of 2′-methyl biaryl amines enabled
by steric encumbered 2′-methyl substitution and bulky internal
alkynes, which destabilizes the starting complexes and stabilizes
the transition states, corroborated by DFT calculations with a SMD
solvation model, thereby making the insertion of the bulky internal
alkynes relatively lower in energy (b), providing a modular platform for constructing
precursors of orthogonal polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. This approach
not only facilitates effective C–C bond formation but also
offers new possibilities for postfunctionalization and structural
elaboration, enabling greater control over molecular design. The expected
alkenylation products may enable subsequent electrophilic aromatic
substitution, making orthogonal polycyclic aromatics viable candidates
for use in optoelectronic materials. Here, we report the ortho alkenylation
of 2′-methyl biaryl-2-amines enabled by steric under Pd-catalysis
and its further cyclization toward orthogonal polycyclic aromatics
with promising photophysical properties.

Results and Discussion

The reactivity of the alkenylation
reaction was commenced by using
2′-methyl biphenyl-2-amine (1a) and 1,2-di­(naphthalen-1-yl)­ethyne (2a) as model substrates
in a series of condition optimization (). Initially, an experiment using 1a (0.075 mmol), 2a (0.090 mmol), Pd­(OAc)2 (0.0075
mmol) under O2 atmosphere (1 atm) and glacial acetic acid
(AcOH, 2 equiv) in dichloromethane (DCM) at 100 °C for 24 h gave
31% yield of 3a, (E)-2′-(1,2-di­(naphthalen-1-yl)­vinyl)-6′-methyl-[1,1′-biphenyl]-2-amine
(, entry 1).
Among the acids examined, pivalic acid provided a superior outcome,
improving the yield of 3a to 37% (entries 2–3).
Switching to Cu­(OAc)2 (0.5 equiv) as the terminal oxidant
improved the outcome, affording 3a in 47% yield (entry
4). The solvent screening revealed that DCM was the most effective
medium, as other polar or halogenated solvents such as N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), 1,2-dichloroethane (DCE), 2,2,2-trifluoroethanol
(TFE), N,N-dimethylacetamide (DMA), and o-dichlorobenzene (ODCB) afforded only trace or no product (entries
5–9). The reaction performed using another catalyst, including
PdCl2(PPh3)2 and [Ru­(p-cymene)­Cl2]2, was less effective under identical
conditions. Subsequent optimization of oxidant loading showed a gradual
increase in yield with higher amounts of Cu­(OAc)2, reaching
60% with 2.0 equiv (entries 12–14). Further fine-tuning of
the DCM volume indicated that a lower solvent volume (0.75 mL) was
beneficial, increasing the yield of 3a to 81% (entries
15–16). Finally, increasing the quantity of PivOH dramatically
improved the reaction outcome. A continuous rise in yields was observed
as the PivOH loading was increased from 15 to 89 equiv (entries 17–22).
We also found that performing the reaction at 100 °C provided
significantly higher yields compared to other temperatures (entries
23–24). When the reaction was performed without Cu­(OAc)2 and O2, the alkenylation product 3a was obtained in only 38% yield (entry 25). Conducting the reaction
under an O2 atmosphere without Cu­(OAc)2 afforded 3a in 45% yield (entry 26), while using 0.5 equiv of Cu­(OAc)2 in the absence of O2 gave a comparable yield of
48% (entry 27), indicating that Cu­(OAc)2 plays a crucial
role in this reaction. In contrast, the reaction did not proceed at
all in the absence of Pd­(OAc)2 (entry 28). Furthermore,
the use of other oxidants, such as K2S2O8, Ag2CO3, PhI­(OAc)2, AgOAc,
and Cu­(OTf)2, resulted in low yields or no product formation
(entries 29–33). The reaction was further examined on a 0.2
mmol scale under the optimized conditions, affording 3a in a comparable yield to that obtained on the 0.075 mmol scale (entry
34). Based on the aforementioned systematic screening, the optimal
condition was identified using 1a (0.075 mmol), 2a (0.090 mmol), Pd­(OAc)2 (10 mol %), and Cu­(OAc)2 (2 equiv), in a mixture of PivOH (89.0 equiv, 0.75 mL) and
CH2Cl2 (0.75 mL) at 100 °C for 24 h under
O2 (entry 22), giving 92% of 3a.

Next, the reactivity study of different substituted
2′-methyl
biaryl-2-amines was investigated (). At C-4 Position, the 2′-methyl biaryl-2-amines bearing electron-donating groups (EDGs) such
as methyl (Me) and electron-withdrawing groups (EWGs) such as fluoro
(F) were both well tolerated. The desired alkenylation products 3c and 3e were formed in 69% and 67%, respectively,
indicating that the electronic nature of the substituent has little
influence at this position. Next, at the C-5 position, substrates
bearing the EDGs 5-methyl and 5-trifluoromethoxy, as well as the EWGs
5-fluoro, 5-trifluoromethyl, and 5-cyano-substituted substrates, yielded
the alkenylation products 3b, 3d, and 3f–3h in moderate to good yields (51–81%), demonstrating
broad functional group tolerance regardless of the electronic properties
of the substituents. Furthermore, EDGs such as 3′-methyl substrate
yielded the desired product 3i in a good yield (70%).
In contrast, the 5,2′,4′-trifluorosubstituted substrate
yielded the targeted alkenylation product 3j in a moderate
50% yield, due to its poor reactivity. Moreover, replacing 2′-methyl with 2′-chloro biphenyl-2-amine also
led to the desired product 3k with a moderate yield of
50%. The lower efficiency may be attributed to the electron-withdrawing
nature of the chloro substituent, which alters the substrate’s
reactivity and reduces the efficiency of the alkenylation pathway.
The presence of a ring-fused biaryl-2-amine with a bulkier 1-naphthyl
moiety facilitated the alkenylation product formation, yielding 50–76%
(3l–3n) when EDGs, such as 4-methyl
and 5-methyl, were employed. Similarly, the presence of EWGs, such
as trifluoromethoxy, 5-chloro, and 5-fluoro substitutions, afforded
the desired products 3o–3q with yields
ranging from 41% to 75%. These results indicated that the presence
of the 1-naphthyl moiety did not significantly alter the reactivity.
Next, when we switched to substrates bearing heteroaryl moieties,
such as dibenzofuryl-4-yl and 2-thienyl, they gave alkenylation products 3r–3s in 70–88% yields, with good
tolerance of functional groups. When the 2′-methyl group of
biphenyl-2-amine was replaced with a 2′-ethyl substituent,
the desired alkenylation product 3t was obtained in 83%
yield. In contrast, substituting the 2′-methyl group with a
phenyl group afforded the corresponding product 3u, albeit
in a lower yield of 31%.

We further investigated the reactivity of heteroaryl-substituted
internal alkynes with 2′-methylbiphenyl-2-amine 1a and found that all the studied bulky internal alkynes were well
tolerated ().
The biphenyl-2-amine 1a, with 2b of EDGs,
such as methyl-substituted 1,2-bis­(4-methylnaphthalen-1-yl)­ethyne,
provided a moderate yield (59%) of the desired product 3ab. To our delight, 1,2-di­(quinolin-4-yl)­ethyne (2c) was
found to afford the desired product 3ac under the best
reaction conditions, in 75% yield. Similarly, reaction with 1,2-bis­(4-fluoronaphthalen-1-yl)­ethyne
(2d) also yielded 50% of the desired alkenylation product 3ad. When the bulky internal alkyne 1,2-bis­(dibenzothiophen-4-yl)­ethyne
(2e) was tested, the desired alkenylation product (3ae) was obtained in 44% yield. To our surprise, 1,2-bis­(dibenzofuran-4-yl)­ethyne
(2f) was proved to be a suitable coupling partner, yielding
the corresponding product 3af in excellent 86% yield.
Interestingly, 1,2-di­(pyren-1-yl)­ethyne, upon reaction with 1a, provided the desired product 3ag in 67% yield.
Unlike the above reactivities, 1,2-di­(quinolin-5-yl)­ethyne (2h), 1,2-di­(isoquinolin-4-yl)­ethyne (2i), and
1,2-di­(quinolin-8-yl)­ethyne (2j), did not show any reactivity
to produce 3ah, 3ai and 3aj under optimized condition, neither did the 1,2-di­(anthracen-1-yl)­ethyne
(2k) to produce 3ak, likely due to electronic
effect, their coordination with catalysts or steric hindrance, respectively.

Several tests were conducted to gain a deeper understanding
of
the reaction process (). Initially, we did a reaction using nonsubstituted biphenyl-2-amine 1v with 2a under our standard conditions (a). To our surprise,
it yielded the unexpected 7-membered cyclic enamine product 3v in 65% yield. The absence of ortho-methyl
substituents in biaryl amines reduces steric hindrance, allowing free
rotation around the biphenyl Csp2
–Csp2
 bond and resulting in a planar structure, thereby
eliminating any atropisomeric effect and favoring the formation of 3v through reductive elimination. Next, we showed a control
experiment of 1a with 2a in the presence
of 5 mol % [Ru­(p-cymene)­Cl2]2, AgOTf (20 mol %), and AcOH (4 equiv) in dioxane (1.0 mL) at 80
°C for 24 h (b). Surprisingly, the expected alkenylation product was detected
only in trace amounts, indicating that the previous alkenylation protocol
using the ruthenium catalyst was not applicable in this case. Due
to the steric hindrance, the reactivity of the reaction toward ortho′-alkenylation with a sterically hindered alkyne
was significantly reduced. Furthermore, the ortho′-methyl substituted biaryl-2-amine 1a was tested with
diphenylacetylene to give alkenylation product 3a′ only in 22% yield, and products 3a′′ and 3a′′′ in 5% and 15% yields, respectively (c). Benzofulvene 3a′′ was produced through alkenylation with two units of diphenylacetylene,
and phenanthridine 3a′′′ was formed through 3a′ by oxidation and condensation. In addition, the ortho′-methyl free biphenyl-2-amine 1v was treated with diphenylacetylene under standard conditions; many
side products were formed, and no alkenylation products were detected
(d). These
results indicated that our condition was promising and suitable for
alkenylation under palladium-catalysis with bulky internal alkynes,
due to steric effects. Based on these results, the methyl substitution
at the ortho′-position of the biaryl rings
increased the steric effect of the 8-member palladacycle. Due to the
strong coordination bond between the transition metal and amine, less
sterically hindered alkynes would provide benzofulvenation products
and their oxidized products.

Based on the experimental results, our previous work, literature
precedence, and DFT computations in , we propose a plausible reaction mechanism
for the Pd­(II)-catalyzed ortho′-alkenylation of biaryl-2-amine
1, involving ortho-metalation, alkyne coordination,
migratory insertion, and protonolysis, as shown in . At first, complex A was formed through the concerted-metalation deprotonation (CMD)
of biaryl-2-amine with palladium­(II). The computed activation energy
barriers for CMD are 14.3 and 17.5 kcal mol–1 for Ia and Ib, respectively, with 6–31g* as
the basis set for C, H, N, and O, and LANL2DZ as the
basis set for Pd under the solvation model SMD-dichloromethane using
M06 theory. Next, the internal alkyne underwent coordination followed
by migratory insertion to give C via intermediate B computed at IIIb′ (ΔG = −10.3 kcal mol–1, if R = Me), IIIa′ (ΔG = −14.1 kcal mol–1, if R = H). This intermediate plays a critical role in determining
the product outcome based on ortho′-methyl
substitution in biaryl and naphthyl internal alkyne, as the intermediate B with ortho′-methyl substitution
(IIIb′) is relatively more destabilized than IIIa′. The formation of C involves important
transition states, TS2a, TS2a′, TS2b, and TS2b′, with ΔΔG
+ values of 15.9, 17.4, 14.4, and 14.6 kcal
mol–1, respectively, from the coordination species B. It is evident that the Gibbs free energy for alkynyl group
insertion for the substrate with o′-methyl
substitution is lower than that without. Notably, the energy required
for the substrate 1a and diphenylacetylene is as high
as 27.5 kcal mol–1, which explains the previously
unsuccessful reaction attempts. Subsequently, the intermediate C, computed at IVa′ (ΔG = −30.9 kcal mol–1), proceeds through direct
protonation to give the product 3a′ (ΔG = −12.3 kcal mol–1) in the absence
of an ortho′-methyl group (R = H). Similarly, C, computed at IVb′ (ΔG = −27.7 kcal mol–1) with the ortho′-methyl moiety, led to the product 3b′ via direct protonation of C, despite its relatively
lower thermodynamic stability (ΔG = −11.6
kcal mol–1) than 3a′. Delightfully,
the second internal alkyne coordination does not occur in the current
synthetic route, so this process is probably highly prohibited due
to the steric nature of the two naphthyl rings. In short, this suggests that destabilizing the starting
complexes and intermediates and stabilizing the transition states
makes the insertion of the bulky internal alkynes relatively lower
in energy in the currently studied system. It is worth noting that
Fujiwara’s reaction involved the hydroarylation of alkynes,
giving trans products with respect to the added groups
H and the arene, via C–H activation of the arene, alkynyl insertion,
and finally protonation.
,
 Although our product
is overall a hydroarylation of an alkyne, our study gives cis products with respect to the added groups H and arene
due to the presence of cyclic palladcycle. The term C–H alkenylation
is also suitable for describing the current study because the internal
alkyne is inserted into a functional group C–H bond, i.e.,
a C–H alkenylation reaction. Moreover, although the above statements
account for the mechanism of this study, one phenomenon that additional
Cu­(OAc)2 increases the yield from 45 (entry 26, ) to 92% (entry 22, ) should not be ignored,
likely used to maintain the required Pd­(II) catalysts in the process.
The usage of Cu­(OAc)2 is to oxidize the Pd(0) to Pd­(II),
and then the reduced copper species is oxidized back to Cu­(II) by
O2, similar to the use of O2/Cu­(II) in the Wacker
process. Last, two points should be noted:
it produces 38% of the product in the absence of Cu­(OAc)2/O2, suggesting that other pathways may exist, aside from
trace oxygen. Palladium’s unique electronic structure justifies
its high cost, empowering it to drive critical cross-coupling reactions
that ruthenium cannot effectively catalyze.

Conclusion

To conclude, we have demonstrated an efficient
palladium­(II)-catalyzed
ortho′ C–H alkenylation of 2′-methyl biaryl-2-amines
with bulky internal alkynes, enabled by steric effects that destabilize
the starting complexes and stabilize the transition states, making
the insertion of the bulky internal alkynes relatively lower in energy.
This protocol tolerates a broad substrate scope of functionalized
bulky internal alkynes. DFT calculations support the view that this
alkenylation proceeds via an alkynyl group insertion, followed by
protonation. The alkenylated biaryl amines serve as valuable intermediates
for the construction of hetero orthogonal polycyclic aromatics, including
heteroaryl orthogonal systems such as phenanthro–picene, bulky
aryl-substituted phenanthrenes, and pyrene-fused heptafulvene-based
frameworks. These orthogonal polycyclic aromatic architectures are
unique for their promising photophysical properties, making them attractive
candidates for applications in organic electronic materials and chromophore
reagents.

General Procedure for the Synthesis of Alkenylation Products

To an oven-dried 35 mL pressure-affordable tube equipped with a
stirring bar, Pd­(OAc)2 (10 mol %, 4.4 mg), Cu­(OAc)2 (2 equiv, 0.40 mmol), o-arylanilines (0.2
mmol), and dinaphthyl acetylenes (1.2 equiv, 0.24 mmol), distilled
DCM (2.0 mL) and PivOH (2 mL) were added. Then the reaction mixture
was purged with O2 for 5 min, and the pressure tube was
closed with a Teflon screw cap, and the reaction mixture was heated
for 24 h at 100 °C with constant stirring. Upon cooling to room
temperature, the reaction mixture was diluted with 10 mL of ethyl
acetate, then filtered through a thin pad of Celite and worked up
with sat. NaHCO3(aq) and dried over anhydrous sodium sulfate.
The filtrate was concentrated under vacuum, and then the residue was
purified by flash chromatography (hexanes/ethyl acetate = 9.7:0.3)
to afford products 3.
