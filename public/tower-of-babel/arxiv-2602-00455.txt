Global regularity of the multi-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system with large initial data
Xiangdi Huang, Weili Meng, Xueyao Zhang a. State Key Laboratory of Mathematical Sciences, Academy of MathematicsSystems Science,Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China;b. School of MathematicsStatistics, Yulin University, Yulin 719000, China;

Abstract
In this paper, we establish the global existence of strong solutions to the multi-dimensional compressible Navier–Stokes–Korteweg system with arbitrarily large initial data on the torus 𝕋 N \mathbb{T}^{N} , provided the adiabatic exponent satisfies γ ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) \gamma\in[1,\infty) when N = 2 N=2 or γ ∈ [ 1 , 7 3 ) \gamma\in[1,\frac{7}{3}) when N = 3 N=3 . This system was derived by Dunn and Serrin [Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 88(2):95–133, 1985] and is widely used to model capillarity in compressible fluids. Via an original modified Nash-Moser type iteration, we establish a critical estimate linking the effective velocity u + ∇ log ⁡ ϱ u+\nabla\log\varrho and ϱ − 1 \varrho^{-1} , namely ‖ u + ∇ log ⁡ ϱ ‖ L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 N × ( 0 , T ) ) ≤ C ​ log ⁡ ( e + ‖ ϱ − 1 ‖ L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 N × ( 0 , T ) ) ) , \left\|u+\nabla\log\varrho\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N}\times(0,T))}\leq C\sqrt{\log(e+\left\|\varrho^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N}\times(0,T))})}, which plays a crucial role in deriving the positive lower bound of the density. To our knowledge, this can be viewed as the first existence result of global strong solutions for the compressible fluid dynamics equations with physical significance in general three-dimensional domains with arbitrarily large initial data. Keywords: compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system; quantum Navier-Stokes system; density-dependent viscosity; global strong solutions; large initial data. Introduction

In order to provide a continuum mechanical model for capillarity within fluids, in 1901, Korteweg
[
29
]
formulated a constitutive equation for the Cauchy stress tensor which includes terms depending on density gradients. Dunn and Serrin
[
18
]
further modified the system of compressible fluids based on the Korteweg theory of capillarity. The compressible Navier–Stokes–Korteweg (NSK) system with density-dependent viscosity coefficients takes the form:

\begin{cases}\varrho_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\varrho u)=0,\\
(\varrho u)_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\varrho u\otimes u)+\nabla P(\varrho)-\operatorname{div}\big(2\mu(\varrho)\mathbb{D}u\big)-\nabla\big(\lambda(\varrho)\operatorname{div}u\big)={\rm div}\mathbb{K},\end{cases}

(1)

where
\varrho(x,t)
,
u(x,t)
and
P(\varrho)=\varrho^{\gamma}
are the density,
velocity, and pressure of the fluid, respectively, and
\mathbb{D}u=\frac{{\nabla u+{\nabla u}^{T}}}{2}
. The viscosities
\mu(\varrho)
and
\lambda(\varrho)
satisfy

\mu(\varrho)\geq 0,\ \mu(\varrho)+N\lambda(\varrho)\geq 0,

where
N
denotes the spatial dimension. The capillary term is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\Big(\varrho{\rm div}(\kappa(\varrho)\nabla\varrho)-\frac{\varrho\kappa^{\prime}(\varrho)-\kappa(\varrho)}{2}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\Big)\mathbb{I}-\kappa(\varrho)\nabla\varrho\otimes\nabla\varrho,

where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix. Note that when
\kappa(\varrho)=0
, the system
(
1
) is reduced to the compressible Navier–Stokes system.

In this paper, we require the coefficients in the system (
1
) to satisfy

\displaystyle\mu(\varrho)=\nu_{0}\varrho,\quad\lambda(\varrho)=0,\quad\kappa(\varrho)=\frac{\kappa_{0}^{2}}{\varrho},

(2)

where the positive constants
\nu_{0}
and
\kappa_{0}
satisfy

\displaystyle\nu_{0}=\kappa_{0}>0.

(3)

Without loss of generality, we always assume that
\nu_{0}=\kappa_{0}=1
.

When the viscosity and capillarity coefficients in system (
1
) satisfy condition (
2
), the compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system reduces to the quantum Navier-Stokes system. For a more detailed derivation of the quantum Navier–Stokes system, we refer to
[
7
,
28
]
. Similar systems also play a central role in quantum fluid dynamics. A prominent example is the inviscid case (
\nu_{0}=0
), which reduces to the well-known quantum hydrodynamics model for superfluids
[
33
]
. These models are employed in the study of quantum semiconductors
[
19
]
, weakly interacting Bose gases
[
21
]
, and Bohmian trajectories
[
39
]
. In recent decades, these models have attracted considerable attention, driven in part by advances in nanotechnology. For the one-dimensional quantum Navier–Stokes system, Jüngel
[
26
]
established the global existence of smooth solutions with strictly positive particle densities on
\mathbb{R}
for arbitrarily large initial data, under the assumption
\nu_{0}=\kappa_{0}
. Subsequently, Chen and Zhao
[
15
]
extended this result to the case
\nu_{0}\geq\kappa_{0}
. For the high-dimensional quantum Navier–Stokes system, Jüngel
[
27
]
obtained the global weak solutions to the system with
\nu_{0}<\kappa_{0}
on the torus for large initial data, by employing the special test function
\rho\phi

with
\phi
smooth and compactly supported. By the construction of a regular approximating system, Antonel and Spirito
[
3
,
4
]
proved the global existence of finite energy
weak solutions on the two- and three-dimensional torus for large initial data with
\nu_{0}>\kappa_{0}
and other technical restrictions imposed on
\nu_{0}
and
\kappa_{0}
, where the vacuum region is included in the weak formulations. Recently, Lacroix-Violet and Vasseur
[
31
]
proved the existence of the renormalized
solution which implies that the above technical restriction in
[
3
]
can indeed be removed, and the semi-classical limit
of the solution is studied. The global existence of finite energy weak solutions in the
whole space with non trivial far-field condition is proved by Antonelli, Hientzsch and Spirito
[
2
]
. Some results about global weak solution of the quantum Navier-Stokes equations with damping term are considered in Vasseur and Yu
[
38
]
and Lü, Zhang and Zhong
[
34
]
and the references therein.
Although the study of weak solutions to the high‑dimensional quantum Navier–Stokes system is well developed, the existence of global strong solutions for large initial data in higher dimensions remains open. This is precisely the objective of the present paper.

In what follows, we shall focus on some closely related works about the well-posedness of the broader Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system. The investigation of Navier-Stokes equations incorporating a capillarity term has garnered significant
attention in recent research. Serrin
[
35
]
reconsidered the Korteweg theory and showed the existence of steady profile
connecting two different phases. Slemrod
[
36
]
considered the existence of
travelling wave solutions connecting two different phases. In two or three space dimensions, for initial data away from vacuum, Hattori and Li
[
22
]
proved the local existence of strong solution of the Cauchy problem for the compressible fluid models of Korteweg type in Sobolev space. Later, Kotschote
[
30
]
considered the initial-boundary-value problem and proved the local existence of the strong solution. In subsequent research, there have been many mathematical results on the global existence of compressible fluid models of Korteweg type.

In studies of the one-dimensional space case, Tsyganov
[
37
]
proved the global existence and asymptotic behaviors of weak solutions in bounded domain with large data away from vacuum. For the system (
1
) with density-dependent viscosity and capillary coefficients, Germain and LeFloch
[
20
]
established the global existence of finite energy weak solutions of the Cauchy problem as well as their convergence toward the entropy solutions to the Euler system. Burtea and Haspot
[
8
]
studied the capillarity vanishing limit scenarios. Specifically, when the viscosity
\mu(\varrho)=\varrho^{\alpha}
and the capillarity coefficient
\kappa(\varrho)=\varrho^{\beta}
satisfy some given conditions, Antonelli, Bresch and Spirito
[
1
]
obtained the global weak solutions of periodic problem with large data.
For existence results concerning global strong solutions, we refer to
[
13
,
12
,
14
,
9
,
17
]
.

However, relatively few results are available for high-dimensional problems. Hattori and Li
[
23
]
extended the local results obtained in
[
22
]

and established the global existence of solutions for the Korteweg system with the small and non-vacuum initial data of the Cauchy problem, where the viscosity and capillary coefficients are constant. When the viscosity and capillarity coefficients depend on the density, in a functional setting as close as possible to the physical energy spaces, Danchin and Desjardins
[
16
]
proved the global existence of suitably smooth solutions which are close to a stable non-vacuum equilibrium. Bresch, Desjardins, and Lin
[
5
]
proved both the
L^{1}
stability and the global existence of weak solutions for Korteweg-type fluids in a periodic box or strip domain, allowing for vacuum. In their analysis, a novel a priori entropy estimate was established, yielding higher regularity for the density. Bresch and Desjardins
[
6
]
considered the two dimensional viscous shallow water model with a capillary term allowing vacuum, and obtained the global existence of weak solutions which converge to the strong solution of the viscous quasi-geostrophic equation with free surface term. Under a condition of smallness on the initial data, Haspot
[
24
]
investigated
the existence of local and global strong solutions with some different types of density-dependent viscosity and capillarity coefficients.

In sharp contrast to the one-dimensional case, the global existence of strong solutions to the multi‑dimensional Navier–Stokes–Korteweg system is currently known only under smallness conditions on the initial data. In fact, the problem of global existence for arbitrarily large data remains largely open in higher dimensions. As a first step toward this goal, we establish the global existence of strong solutions to system (
1
)–(
3
) on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}
with large initial data away from vacuum.

More precisely, we study system (
1
)–(
3
) with prescribed initial data
(\varrho_{0},u_{0})
, which are periodic with period
1
in each space direction. We require that

\displaystyle\varrho(x,0)=\varrho_{0}(x),\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{N},

(4)

where
\mathbb{T}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N}/\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with
N=2,3
. With the choice
\kappa(\varrho)=\frac{1}{\varrho}
from (
2
) and (
3
), the Korteweg tensor takes the following form:

\displaystyle{\rm div}\mathbb{K}={\rm div}(\varrho\nabla\nabla\log\varrho).

When the density is positive, we define the effective velocity, introduced by Jüngel
[
27
]
, as

\displaystyle v=u+\nabla\log\varrho.

This transformation converts system (
1
)–(
3
) into the following parabolic system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\varrho_{t}+\div(\varrho v)-\Delta\varrho=0,\\
\varrho v_{t}+\varrho(v-\nabla\log\varrho)\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P=\div(\varrho\nabla v).\end{array}\right.

(5)

Accordingly, the initial effective velocity
v_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle v_{0}=u_{0}+\nabla\log\varrho_{0}.

(6)

A key structural feature of (
5
)
2
relies on a precise relation between the coefficients in (
2
), that is,
\nu_{0}=\kappa_{0}
.

Before stating our main results, we introduce the following notations and conventions throughout this paper:

\displaystyle\int fdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}fdx,\quad\int_{Q_{T}}fdxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int fdxdt,\quad Q_{T}=\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T].

For
1\leq s\leq\infty
, we denote the standard Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces as follows:

\displaystyle L^{s}=L^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),\quad W^{k,s}=W^{k,s}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),\quad H^{s}=W^{s,2}.

For
1\leq a,s\leq\infty
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
T>0
, we denote

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{a}_{T}L^{s}}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{a}(0,T;L^{s})},\quad\left\|f\right\|_{L^{a}_{T}W^{k,s}}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{a}(0,T;W^{k,s})}.

The constants
C
appearing in the proofs of Sections 2–4 may vary from line to line and generally depend on quantities such as certain norms of the initial data or the system parameters stated in the corresponding proposition or lemma.

We now state the main result on the global existence of strong solutions to problem (
1
)–(
4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N=2
or
N=3
. Assume that
\gamma
satisfies

\displaystyle N=2,\quad\gamma\in[1,\infty);

(7)

\displaystyle N=3,\quad\gamma\in\Big[1,\frac{7}{3}\Big),

(8)

and that the initial data
(\varrho_{0},u_{0})
satisfy

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\varrho_{0}}\leq\varrho_{0}\leq\overline{\varrho_{0}},\quad\varrho_{0}\in H^{3},\quad u_{0}\in H^{2},

(9)

where
\underline{\varrho_{0}}
and
\overline{\varrho_{0}}
are positive constants. Then the problem (
1
)–(
4
) admits a unique global strong solution
(\varrho,u)
satisfying for any
0<T<\infty
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(C(T))^{-1}\leq\varrho(x,t)\leq C(T),\\
\varrho\in C([0,T];H^{3})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}),\ \varrho_{t}\in C([0,T];H^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}),\\
u\in C([0,T];H^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}),\ u_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}),\end{array}\right.

where the constant
C(T)>0
depends on the initial data and
T
.

Remark 1.1
.

Haspot
[
25
]
studied the existence of strong solutions to the Cauchy problem for the isothermal case
(\gamma=1)
of the same compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system (
1
)–(
3
). In his paper, he introduced a series of highly original and important ideas and techniques. Indeed, there is a crucial estimate in Haspot’s article that requires the estimate for
\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p+2}}
independent of
p
, which plays a key role in deriving the positive lower bound of the density. Unfortunately, following his approach, we are unable to obtain such a uniform estimate—even for the isothermal case of
[
25
]
discussed in his paper. In fact, the estimate for
\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p+2}}
necessarily depends on
p
. This dependence prevents us from deriving an upper bound for the effective velocity by passing to the limit
p\to\infty
, as was done in
[
25
]
, ultimately leading to the inability to obtain a positive lower bound estimate for the density. To overcome this difficulty, we prove another novel critical control relation between
v
and
\varrho^{-1}
, i.e.,
\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C\sqrt{\log(e+\left\|\varrho^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})})}
. Based on these new observations, we are able to prove the results for the global strong solutions with large initial data for the isothermal and non-isothermal cases. One can refer to Proposition
3.1
for the details. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
can be regarded as presenting the first existence result of global strong solutions for the compressible fluid dynamics equations with physical significance, applicable to the three-dimensional general domains and large initial data. In contrast, for incompressible fluids, the only known existence result of global strong solutions for three-dimensional general domains with large initial data dates back to Cao and Titi
[
10
]
in 2007.

Remark 1.2
.

Another crucial argument is that we can establish the upper bound of the density to the general adiabatic exponent
\gamma
in the three-dimensional case. One can refer to Proposition
2.4
for the details.

Remark 1.3
.

We primarily focus on the case
\gamma>1
and
N=3
. For the isothermal case with
\gamma=1
, its proof can be carried out almost similarly to the non-isothermal case line by line. For the case
N=2
, we give a brief discussion in the final section.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
mainly consists of three steps. In the first step, we obtain an upper bound for the density via the mass equation, which is primarily achieved through the high integrability of the effective velocity and De Giorgi iteration. In the second step, we establish a precise control relation between the effective velocity and the lower bound of the density, as stated in Remark
1.1
. This estimate, in turn, yields a positive lower bound for the density itself. To this end, we devise an original modified Nash–Moser iteration scheme to estimate the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the effective velocity and that of
\varrho^{-1}
step by step. Notably, the method developed here can be adapted to enhance the regularity of solutions for a broad class of parabolic systems, from finite
L^{p}
regularity to
L^{\infty}
bounds. In the third step, based on the estimates for the upper and lower bounds of the density, we obtain the higher-order regularity of both the density and the effective velocity, which ensures that the strong solution exists globally.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 establishes the upper bound for the density, while Section 3 provides the positive lower bound for the density. Section 4 is devoted to higher-order derivative estimates for the density and the effective velocity. Finally, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed in Section 5.

2
Upper bound for the density (3D)

Owing to the better parabolic structure of (
5
) compared with the original system (
1
), we focus on (
5
). Standard theory yields that system (
5
)–(
6
) admits a unique local strong solution
(\varrho,v)
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T^{*})
with maximal existence time
T^{*}>0
, and this solution satisfies
\varrho>0
on its interval of existence. To prove that the solution is global, we argue by contradiction. Suppose that
T^{*}<\infty
. Then, for any
T
satisfying
0<T<T^{*}
, the solution is well-defined on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T]
.

We begin with the standard energy estimate. Denote

\displaystyle E_{0}=\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho_{0}^{\gamma}+2|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}|^{2}\right)dx.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\gamma
and
E_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\left(\varrho|u|^{2}+\varrho^{\gamma}+|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(13)

For brevity we omit the proof of Proposition
2.1
; details can be found in Proposition 3.1 of
[
40
]
. The standard approach then gives the following
L^{2}
-estimate for
v
.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\gamma
and
E_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\varrho|v|^{2}dx+\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla\varrho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}|dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(14)

The following Proposition
2.3
shows the higher integrability of the effective velocity, which is crucial for obtaining an upper bound on the density via De Giorgi iteration. For any
\gamma\in(1,\tfrac{7}{3})
we choose
q\in(1,4)
depending only on
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\gamma<1+\frac{4}{q+2}.

(15)

Proposition 2.3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0},
and
\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\|_{L^{q+2}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v\right\|_{L^{q+2}}\leq C.

(16)

Proof.

Multiplying equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{2}
by
|v|^{q}v
and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, we use integration by parts and Young’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\varrho|v|^{q+2}dx+\int\varrho|v|^{q}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+q\int\varrho|v|^{q}|\nabla|v||^{2}dx\\
&=\int\varrho^{\gamma}{\rm div}(|v|^{q}v)dx\\
&=\int\varrho^{\gamma}|v|^{q}\div vdx+q\int\varrho^{\gamma}|v|^{q-1}v\cdot\nabla|v|dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\varrho|v|^{q}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\frac{q}{2}\int\varrho|v|^{q}|\nabla|v||^{2}dx+C\int\varrho^{2\gamma-1}|v|^{q}dx,\end{split}

where we have used the simple fact
|{\rm div}v|\leq\sqrt{3}|\nabla v|
. Hölder’s inequality implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int\varrho|v|^{q+2}dx&\leq C\int\varrho^{2\gamma-1}|v|^{q}dx\\
&\leq C\left(\int\varrho|v|^{q+2}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\left(\int\varrho^{q\gamma+2\gamma-q-1}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}.\end{split}

(17)

To handle the right-hand side of the above inequality, we denote

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{q\gamma+2\gamma-q+4}{2},\quad\xi=\frac{4-q}{q\gamma+2\gamma-q+4}.

Using (
13
) and (
14
), we obtain from the standard Sobolev embedding and Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\varrho\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{2}{q+2}\zeta}L^{\zeta}}&\leq C\left\|\varrho\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{3}}^{\xi}\left\|\varrho\right\|_{L^{\gamma}_{T}L^{3\gamma}}^{1-\xi}\\
&=C\left\|\sqrt{\varrho}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{6}}^{2\xi}\left\|\varrho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{6}}^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(1-\xi)}\\
&\leq C\left\|\sqrt{\varrho}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}^{2\xi}\left\|\varrho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}H^{1}}^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(1-\xi)}\\
&\leq C.\end{split}

(18)

Therefore, it follows from (
17
) and (
15
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v\right\|_{L^{q+2}}^{2}&\leq C\left(\int\varrho^{q\gamma+2\gamma-q-1}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\\
&=C\left(\int\varrho^{\zeta}\varrho^{\frac{q\gamma+2\gamma-q-6}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\\
&\leq C\left(\int(\varrho^{\zeta}+1)dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\\
&\leq C\left(\int\varrho^{\zeta}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}+C.\end{split}

Integrating the above inequality over time and using (
18
), we obtain (
16
). This completes the proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Next we use the De Giorgi iteration to obtain an upper bound on the density. This approach originates from De Giorgi’s method for improving the regularity of solutions to elliptic equations, which was later extended to parabolic equations by Ladyzhenskaya et al. in
[
32
]
, and has subsequently been elegantly applied by Haspot in
[
25
]
. More recently, Yu and Wu
[
40
]
used it to establish an upper bound for the density in the two-dimensional Cauchy problem. Here, we adapt and extend this technique to establish the corresponding upper bound estimate in the three-dimensional case.

For simplicity, we introduce the following notations:

\displaystyle\varrho^{(k)}:=\max\{\varrho-k,0\},\text{ for }k\geq 2,

\displaystyle|\varrho^{(k)}|_{Q_{t}}:=\left\|\varrho^{(k)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla\varrho^{(k)}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\left|\varrho(x,t)\geq k\right.\},

\displaystyle\mu(k):=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\Gamma_{k}(t))^{\frac{q}{q+2}}dt,

where
\Gamma_{k}(t)
denotes the upper level set of the density and
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma_{k}(t))
stands for the Lebesgue measure of
\Gamma_{k}(t)
.
Now we are ready to derive the space-time estimate of
\varrho^{(k)}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0}
, and
\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\|_{L^{q+2}}
such that for any
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle|\varrho^{(k)}|_{Q_{T}}\leq Ck\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}(k).

(19)

Proof.

For any
k\geq 2
, equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho-k)+{\rm div}(\varrho v)-\Delta(\varrho-k)=0.

Multiplying the above equation by
\varrho^{(k)}
, integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, and using integration by parts and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int|\varrho^{(k)}|^{2}dx+\int|\nabla\varrho^{(k)}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int\nabla\varrho^{(k)}\cdot\varrho vdx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla\varrho^{(k)}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{k}(t)}\varrho^{2}|v|^{2}dx.

Integrating this inequality in time yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\varrho^{(k)}|_{Q_{T}}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{k}(t)}\varrho^{2}|v|^{2}dxdt.\end{split}

(20)

Next, we estimate the right-hand side of the above inequality. From (
16
), the definition of
\mu(k)
, Hölder’s inequality, and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{k}(t)}\varrho^{2}|v|^{2}dxdt\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\varrho v^{q+2}\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\left\|\varrho\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q+2}{q}}(\Gamma_{k}(t))}^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}dt\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\left\|\varrho^{(k)}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q+2}{q}}(\Gamma_{k}(t))}^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}+\left\|k\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q+2}{q}}(\Gamma_{k}(t))}^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}\Big)dt\\
&\leq C\left\|\varrho^{(k)}\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}L^{\frac{2q+2}{q}}(\Gamma_{k}(t))}^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}+Ck^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}\mu(k)\\
&\leq C\left\|\varrho^{(k)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}(\Gamma_{k}(t))}^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}\frac{2q-2}{5q+4}}\left\|\varrho^{(k)}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{6}(\Gamma_{k}(t))}^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}\frac{3q+6}{5q+4}}\left\|\chi_{\Gamma_{k}(t)}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{\frac{q+2}{q}}}^{\frac{2q+1}{5q+4}}+Ck^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}\mu(k)\\
&\leq C|\varrho^{(k)}|_{Q_{T}}^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}\mu(k)^{\frac{2q+1}{5q+4}}+Ck^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}\mu(k)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}|\varrho^{(k)}|_{Q_{T}}^{2}+C\mu(k)^{\frac{2q^{2}+5q+2}{5q+4}}+Ck^{\frac{2q+2}{q+2}}\mu(k)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}|\varrho^{(k)}|_{Q_{T}}^{2}+Ck^{2}\mu(k),\end{split}

(21)

where
\chi_{\Gamma_{k}(t)}
is the indicator function of
\Gamma_{k}(t)
. Combining (
21
) with (
20
) yields (
19
), which completes the proof.
∎

We recall the following lemma from Ladyzhenskaya et al.
[
32
]
(Lemma 5.6, p.95); see also
[
25
]
(Lemma 5.2) or
[
40
]
(Lemma 2.2).

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\Phi_{n}
,
n=0,1,2,\dots
, is a non-negative sequence satisfying

\displaystyle\Phi_{n+1}\leq cb^{n}\Phi_{n}^{1+\eta},

for some positive constants
c,\eta
, and
b>1
. If, in addition,

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}\leq\theta=c^{-1/\eta}b^{-1/\eta^{2}},

then we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}\leq\theta b^{-n/\eta}\text{ and }\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{n}=0.

With the above lemmas at hand, we can now establish an upper bound for the density.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0},\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\|_{L^{q+2}},
and
\left\|\varrho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\varrho\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

(22)

Proof.

We denote that

\displaystyle\hat{k}_{0}=\max\{2,\left\|\varrho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\},\quad k_{n}=M_{T^{*}}\hat{k}_{0}(2-2^{-n}),

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
M_{T^{*}}>1
is a sufficiently large constant to be determined, depending only on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0},\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\|_{L^{q+2}},
and
\|\varrho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\left\|\varrho^{(k_{n})}\right\|_{L^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}_{T}L^{\frac{10q+8}{3q}}}\leq C\left\|\varrho^{(k_{n})}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{\frac{2q-2}{5q+4}}\left\|\varrho^{(k_{n})}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{6}}^{\frac{3q+6}{5q+4}}\leq C|\varrho^{(k_{n})}|_{Q_{T}}.

(23)

Using Chebyshev’s inequality, (
23
), together with (
19
), one has

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu(k_{n+1})&=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\varrho-k_{n}\geq k_{n+1}-k_{n})^{\frac{q}{q+2}}dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{(k_{n+1}-k_{n})^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int|\varrho^{(k_{n})}|^{\frac{10q+8}{3q}}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}dt\\
&=\frac{1}{(k_{n+1}-k_{n})^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}}\left\|\varrho^{(k_{n})}\right\|_{L^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}_{T}L^{\frac{10q+8}{3q}}}^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}\\
&\leq\frac{C}{(k_{n+1}-k_{n})^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}}|\varrho^{(k_{n})}|_{Q_{T}}^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}\\
&\leq\frac{Ck_{n}^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}}{(k_{n+1}-k_{n})^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}}}\mu(k_{n})^{\frac{5q+4}{3q+6}}\\
&\leq C2^{\frac{10q+8}{3q+6}n}\mu(k_{n})^{\frac{5q+4}{3q+6}}\\
&\leq C16^{n}\mu(k_{n})^{\frac{5q+4}{3q+6}}.\end{split}

(24)

Denoting by
C_{0}
the constant
C
appearing in the last inequality of (
24
), we obtain the relation

\displaystyle\mu(k_{n+1})\leq C_{0}16^{n}\mu(k_{n})^{1+\frac{2q-2}{3q+6}},\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(25)

Employing
(
13
)
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu(k_{0})&=\mu(M_{T^{*}}\hat{k}_{0})=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}\bigl(\Gamma_{M_{T^{*}}\hat{k}_{0}}(t)\bigr)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\,dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int\frac{\varrho}{M_{T^{*}}\hat{k}_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}dt\\
&\leq CM_{T^{*}}^{-\frac{q}{q+2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int\varrho\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\\
&\leq CM_{T^{*}}^{-\frac{q}{q+2}}.\end{split}

(26)

Denoting the constant
C
appearing in the last line of (
26
) by
C_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu(k_{0})\leq C_{1}M_{T^{*}}^{-\frac{q}{q+2}}.

(27)

To apply Lemma
2.2
to the sequence
\{\mu(k_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we choose
M_{T^{*}}
sufficiently large so that

\displaystyle C_{1}M_{T^{*}}^{-\frac{q}{q+2}}\leq C_{0}^{-\frac{3q+6}{2q-2}}16^{-(\frac{3q+6}{2q-2})^{2}}.

(28)

Therefore, combining (
25
), (
27
), and (
28
), an application of Lemma
2.2
yields

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mu(k_{n})=0.

This limit forces
\mu(2M_{T^{*}}\hat{k}_{0})=0
, which in turn implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\varrho\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2M_{T^{*}}\hat{k}_{0}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
2.4
.
∎

3
Positive lower bound for the density (3D)

In this section we prove that the density has a positive lower bound on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(0,T^{*})
, which is the most important and challenging part of ensuring that the local strong solution exists globally in time.

We begin with Lemma
3.1
, which describes how the
L^{p}
-integrability of the effective velocity grows with respect to
p
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the assumption that
p>2
, there exists a constant
\hat{C}\geq 1
depending on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0},\left\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q+2}},\left\|\varrho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
but independent of
p
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v\right\|_{L^{p+2}}\leq\hat{C}\sqrt{p+2}.

(29)

Remark 3.1
.

Indeed, the estimate of
\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p+2}}
depends on
p
, and as
p\to\infty
this
p
-dependent bound tends to infinity. This dependence prevents us from using the method of Haspot
[
25
]
to obtain a positive lower bound on the density.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

For any
p>2
, multiplying equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{2}
by
|v|^{p}v
and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, we use integration by parts and Young’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx+\int\varrho|v|^{p}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+p\int\varrho|v|^{p}|\nabla|v||^{2}dx\\
&=\int\varrho^{\gamma}{\rm div}(|v|^{p}v)dx\\
&=\int\varrho^{\gamma}|v|^{p}\div vdx+p\int\varrho^{\gamma}|v|^{p-1}v\cdot\nabla|v|dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\varrho|v|^{p}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\frac{p}{2}\int\varrho|v|^{p}|\nabla|v||^{2}dx+C(p+1)\int\varrho^{2\gamma-1}|v|^{p}dx.\end{split}

(30)

Therefore, by (
22
) and (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx&\leq C(p+1)\int\varrho^{2\gamma-1}|v|^{p}dx\\
&\leq C(p+1)\int\varrho|v|^{p}dx\\
&\leq C(p+1)\left(\int\varrho|v|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\\
&\leq C(p+1)\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v\right\|_{L^{p+2}}^{\frac{(p-2)(p+2)}{p}},\end{split}

which shows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v\right\|_{L^{p+2}}^{\frac{2(p+2)}{p}}\leq C(p+1).

Integrating the above inequality with respect to time, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\varrho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v\right\|_{L^{p+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|\varrho_{0}^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{p+2}}^{\frac{2(p+2)}{p}}+C(p+1)\right)^{\frac{p}{2(p+2)}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\varrho_{0}^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{p+2}}+C(p+1)^{\frac{p}{2(p+2)}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\varrho_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{p}{p+2}}+C\sqrt{p+1}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{p+2}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

The following proposition is key to obtaining the positive lower bound on the density. It establishes a control relation between the effective velocity and the lower bound of the density. We introduce the following notation:

\displaystyle\tau:=\varrho^{-1},\quad\Theta_{T}:=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+e^{\frac{25}{9}}.

(31)

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{v}\geq 1
depending on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0},\left\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q+2}}
,
\left\|\varrho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{v}\sqrt{\log\Theta_{T}}.

(32)

Proof.

For any
p>0
, multiplying equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{2}
by
|v|^{p}v
, integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, and using integration by parts, we obtain from (
30
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int\varrho|v|^{p}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\frac{p}{2}\int\varrho|v|^{p}|\nabla|v||^{2}dx\\
&\leq C(p+1)\int\varrho^{2\gamma-1}|v|^{p}dx\\
&\leq C(p+1)\int\varrho|v|^{p}dx\\
&\leq C(p+1)\left(\int\varrho|v|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\\
&\leq C(p+1)\left(\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}},\end{split}

where we have used (
22
) and (
14
). Therefore, integrating the above inequality with respect to time and using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{1}{p+2}\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|v|^{p}|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt+\frac{p}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|v|^{p}|\nabla|v||^{2}dxdt\\
&\leq C(p+1)\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}dt+\frac{2}{p+2}\int\varrho_{0}|v_{0}|^{p+2}dx\\
&\leq C(p+1)\left(\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|v|^{p+2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+\frac{2}{p+2}\int\varrho_{0}|v_{0}|^{p+2}dx.\end{split}

(33)

Next, we establish a reverse Hölder’s inequality. From (
22
), (
33
), Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|v|^{\frac{5}{3}(p+2)}dxdt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int\varrho^{\frac{3}{2}}|v|^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int|v|^{3(p+2)}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}dt\\
&\leq C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla|v|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}|^{2}dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}|v|^{p+2}dxdt\right)\\
&\leq C\Theta_{T}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\varrho|v|^{p+2}dx+\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|\nabla|v|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{5}{3}}\\
&\leq C\Theta_{T}\left(C(p+2)^{2}\left(\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|v|^{p+2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+C\int\varrho_{0}|v_{0}|^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{5}{3}}\\
&\leq C\Theta_{T}(p+2)^{\frac{10}{3}}\left(\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|v|^{p+2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{5}{3}}+C\Theta_{T}(p+2)^{\frac{10}{3}}+C\Theta_{T}\left(\int\varrho_{0}|v_{0}|^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{5}{3}}.\end{split}

(34)

A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\left\|\varrho_{0}^{\frac{1}{p+2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{p+2}}\leq\left\|\varrho_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\leq\left\|\varrho_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(35)

Denote the constant
C
in the last inequality of (
34
) by
C_{2}
, and assume without loss of generality that
C_{2}\geq 1
. For simplicity, we introduce the following notation. For any integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k>l
, where
l\geq 3
is a positive integer to be determined later, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}&r=\frac{5}{3},\quad p_{k}+2=r^{k},\quad C_{3}=\left\|\varrho_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+1,\\
&\Xi(k)=\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|v|^{r^{k}}dxdt.\end{split}

Substituting
p
with
p_{k}
in
(
34
)
, we use (
35
) to get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Xi(k+1)\leq C_{2}\Theta_{T}(r^{\frac{10}{3}k}\Xi(k)^{r}+r^{\frac{10}{3}k}+C_{3}^{r^{k+1}}).\end{split}

Let
\tilde{\Xi}(k)=\max\{\Xi(k),C_{3}^{r^{k}}\}
. Then a direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\Xi}(k+1)&=\max\{\Xi(k+1),C_{3}^{r^{k+1}}\}\\
&\leq\max\{C_{2}\Theta_{T}r^{\frac{10}{3}k}\Xi(k)^{r}+C_{2}\Theta_{T}r^{\frac{10}{3}k}+C_{2}\Theta_{T}C_{3}^{r^{k+1}},C_{3}^{r^{k+1}}\}\\
&\leq\max\{C_{2}\Theta_{T}r^{\frac{10}{3}k}\tilde{\Xi}(k)^{r}+C_{2}\Theta_{T}r^{\frac{10}{3}k}\tilde{\Xi}(k)^{r}+C_{2}\Theta_{T}r^{\frac{10}{3}k}\tilde{\Xi}(k)^{r},\tilde{\Xi}(k)^{r}\}\\
&\leq 3C_{2}\Theta_{T}r^{\frac{10}{3}k}\tilde{\Xi}(k)^{r}.\end{split}

(36)

Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\Xi}(k+1)^{\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}}&\leq(3C_{2}\Theta_{T})^{\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\frac{k}{r^{k+1}}}\tilde{\Xi}(k)^{\frac{1}{r^{k}}}\\
&\leq(3C_{2}\Theta_{T})^{\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{r^{k}}}r^{\frac{10}{3}(\frac{k}{r^{k+1}}+\frac{k-1}{r^{k}})}\tilde{\Xi}(k-1)^{\frac{1}{r^{k-1}}}\\
&\leq\dots\\
&\leq(3C_{2}\Theta_{T})^{\sum_{i=l+1}^{k+1}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=l+1}^{k+1}(i-1)r^{-i}}\tilde{\Xi}(l)^{\frac{1}{r^{l}}}.\end{split}

(37)

Finally, we will choose an appropriate
l
as the starting point of the iteration and proceed by iteration using the reverse Hölder’s inequality
(
37
)
. We choose
l
as follows:

\displaystyle l=\bigl\lfloor\log_{r}(\log\Theta_{T})+1\bigr\rfloor,

where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function. From
(
37
)
and
(
29
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\Xi}(k+1)^{\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}}&\leq(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}\Theta_{T}^{\sum_{i=l+1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}\tilde{\Xi}(l)^{\frac{1}{r^{l}}}\\
&=(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}\Theta_{T}^{\frac{1}{r-1}r^{-l}}\max\{\Xi(l)^{\frac{1}{r^{l}}},C_{3}\}\\
&\leq(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}\Theta_{T}^{\frac{1}{r-1}r^{-{\log_{r}(\log\Theta_{T})}}}\max\{T^{\frac{1}{r^{l}}}\hat{C}r^{\frac{l}{2}},C_{3}\}\\
&\leq(1+T^{*})\hat{C}C_{3}(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}\Theta_{T}^{\frac{1}{r-1}r^{-{\log_{r}(\log\Theta_{T})}}}r^{\frac{l}{2}}\\
&\leq(1+T^{*})\hat{C}C_{3}(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}\Theta_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{\log\Theta_{T}}}r^{\frac{\log_{r}(\log\Theta_{T})+1}{2}}\\
&\leq r^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+T^{*})\hat{C}C_{3}(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}\Theta_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{\log\Theta_{T}}}(\log\Theta_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq r^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+T^{*})\hat{C}C_{3}(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}e^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\log\Theta_{T}}.\end{split}

Combined with the definition of
\tilde{\Xi}(\cdot)
, it shows that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
with
k>l
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Q_{T}}\varrho|v|^{r^{k+1}}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}}\leq r^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+T^{*})\hat{C}C_{3}(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}e^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\log\Theta_{T}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Q_{T}}|v|^{r^{k+1}}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}}\leq\Theta_{T}^{\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+T^{*})\hat{C}C_{3}(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}e^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\log\Theta_{T}}.

Letting
k\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq r^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+T^{*})\hat{C}C_{3}(3C_{2})^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r^{-i}}r^{\frac{10}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(i-1)r^{-i}}e^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\log\Theta_{T}}.

Therefore, the proof of Proposition
3.1
is complete.
∎

We are now in a position to prove that the density has a positive lower bound.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0},\left\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q+2}}
,
\left\|\varrho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
\left\|\tau_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

(38)

Proof.

Step 1.
We claim that there exists a
T_{0}>0
sufficiently small, depending on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0},\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\|_{L^{q+2}},\|\varrho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},
and
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
; and a constant
C>0
sufficiently large, depending on
T^{*},q,\gamma,E_{0},\|\varrho_{0}^{1/(q+2)}v_{0}\|_{L^{q+2}},\|\varrho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
\|\tau_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, such that

\displaystyle\Theta_{T_{0}}\leq C.

(39)

From
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
, we know that
\tau
satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tau-\Delta\tau+2\tau^{-1}|\nabla\tau|^{2}+v\cdot\nabla\tau-\tau{\rm div}v=0.

(40)

For any
p>0
, multiplying equation
(
40
)
by
\tau^{p+1}
, integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, and using integration by parts and Young’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\tau^{p+2}dx+(p+3)\int\tau^{p}|\nabla\tau|^{2}dx\\
&\leq(p+3)\int|v||\nabla\tau|\tau^{p+1}dx\\
&\leq\frac{p+3}{2}\int\tau^{p}|\nabla\tau|^{2}dx+(p+2)\int|v|^{2}\tau^{p+2}dx\\
&\leq\frac{p+3}{2}\int\tau^{p}|\nabla\tau|^{2}dx+(p+2)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{2}\int\tau^{p+2}dx.\end{split}

(41)

Integrating the above inequality with respect to time yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{1}{p+2}\int\tau^{p+2}dx+\frac{p+3}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\tau^{p}|\nabla\tau|^{2}dxdt\\
&\leq 2(p+2)\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\tau^{p+2}dxdt+\frac{2}{p+2}\int\tau_{0}^{p+2}dx.\end{split}

(42)

Next, we establish a reverse Hölder’s inequality. Using
(
42
)
and the Sobolev embedding, one has

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{Q_{T}}\tau^{\frac{5}{3}(p+2)}dxdt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int\tau^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int\tau^{3(p+2)}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}dt\\
&\leq C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\int\tau^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla\tau^{\frac{p+2}{2}}|^{2}dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}\tau^{p+2}dxdt\right)\\

(69)

(

(

)

(
