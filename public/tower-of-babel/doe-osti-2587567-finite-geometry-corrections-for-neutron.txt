In this report, we describe the corrections necessary for neutron scattering cross section measurements
in experiments which use finite-geometry scattering samples. A new Geant4 based framework was
developed to calculate these corrections which has been benchmarked against a well-measured test
case. This framework also calculates the outgoing neutron energy dependence of the correction factors,
which is crucial when resolving individual final states in the scatterer is not possible.

I. Introduction
 A campaign of experiments studying neutron elastic and inelastic scattering on a 238U sample
was recently completed. These experiments took place at the Triangle Universities Nuclear Laboratory
(TUNL) on the campus of Duke University and employed monoenergetic neutron beams of 2, 3, 4, and 5
MeV. Details on the experimental setup and data analysis are given in a previous Technical Report [1].
Here, we describe the procedure to correct the experimental data for finite sample size effects.

 Figure 1 shows the geometry of the neutron source and scattering sample used in these
measurements. The path of a primary neutron shown in the figure, indicated by the black line, highlights
the two main finite-geometry effects which must be accounted for: the angular spread of the incident
neutrons impinging on the sample, and the possibility that a neutron can scatter more than once inside
the sample. The angles indicated in the figure, explained below, are those needed to calculate correction
factors which account for these effects.

II. GEANT4 Simulation
 A new GEANT4-based [3] framework has been developed to calculate the correction factors 𝐶MS
and 𝐶G . This simulation can generate neutrons from two production reactions: T(p,n)3He and D(d,n)3He.
For the (p,n) reaction, the proton bombarding energy can be between 2.5 and 6.5 MeV. For the (d,n)
reaction, deuteron bombarding energies between 1.0 and 8.0 MeV can be simulated. A simple isotropic
neutron source of arbitrary energy is also possible. The geometry of the neutron source can be
configured as either a point source or extended over a cylindrical volume as depicted in Fig. 1. The
scattering sample is also a cylinder, and the geometry and material properties of the sample can be
configured with an input file. The distance between the source and sample can also be adjusted.

 For the results presented in this work, the Geant4 Nuclear Data Library (G4NDL) version 4.7.1
was used in the simulations. This library employs the JEFF-3.3 evaluation [4] for neutron interaction cross
sections. The simulation begins with a primary neutron generated by the chosen production reaction at
the source location. This primary neutron is then propagated through time and space by the GEANT4
kernel. For each simulated step, the following information is recorded:

 Process

 Volume

        𝑟⃑ = (𝑥, 𝑦, 𝑧)

        𝑝⃑ = (𝑝𝑥 , 𝑝𝑦 , 𝑝𝑧 )

Process is a number which specifies what physical process created the step. The possible physical
processes for neutrons are: elastic scattering (111), inelastic scattering (121), neutron capture (131), and
neutron-induced fission (141). Additionally, a pure “tracking” process (91) is provided by GEANT4 which
simply propagates the neutron through the simulation geometry without a discreet physical interaction.
Volume specifies what physical volume the step occurred in, for example inside the scattering sample. The
vectors 𝑟⃑ and 𝑝⃑ are the position and momentum of the neutron, respectively. The kinetic energy 𝑇 of the
neutron at each point can be calculated as

                                           𝑇 = √𝑝2 + 𝑚𝑛2 − 𝑚𝑛

where 𝑚𝑛 is the neutron mass.

 The collection of these steps is one neutron history. If any secondary neutrons are created during
the simulated event, their histories are recorded as well. Most relevantly, inelastic scattering processes
such as (n,n’), (n,2n), etc., as well as neutron-induced fission can create secondary neutrons. The collection
of neutron histories after each simulated event is written to an output file. The various angular
distributions needed to calculate the finite-geometry correction factors can be extracted from these
histories.

III. Simulation Benchmark
 The validity of the simulation was tested by reproducing the correction factors derived in the work
of Velkley et. al. [5] which investigated the elastic scattering of 8.97 MeV neutrons on a cylindrical 59Co
sample. In this work, the neutrons were generated by bombarding a 5.75 MeV deuteron beam on a
deuterium gas cell inside which a D(d,n) reaction took place; this is the geometry depicted in Fig. 1.

 In the benchmark simulation, a 59Co sample 2.54 cm in height and 1.905 cm in diameter was
employed, and the neutron source was placed 6.26 cm away from the sample axis (see Ref. [5] for all exact
details of the source-sample geometry). Most critically, this geometry implies the incident angle 𝜃N varies
between 0° and 14.5°. With this geometry, the histories of 1.6⋅107 elastically scattered neutrons were
recorded. For a direct comparison to the analysis of Ref [5], any neutrons which inelastically scattered
were excluded from the recorded histories.

 Figure 2 shows the 𝜎(𝜃𝐷 ) and 𝜎(𝜃𝐼 ) distributions generated by the benchmark simulation. These
two distributions are different due to the incident angular spread (they are identical for 𝜃N = 0). Note that
𝜎(𝜃𝐷 ) is effectively the input cross section which is being distorted by finite-size effects. The uncorrected
distributions 𝜎̅(𝜃), 𝜎̅s (𝜃), and 𝜎̅m (𝜃) are shown in Figure 3. Using the distributions shown in Figures 2 and
3, the correction factors 𝐶MS and 𝐶G can be calculated; these are shown in Figure 4 as a function of the
measured angle θ.

IV. Energy-Dependent Corrections
 When the low-lying states of the scatterer are well separated in energy (roughly 1 MeV apart), as
is the case for light and medium mass nuclei, resolving the individual final states populated by the reaction
is possible using neutron detection. This is why it was appropriate to derive the correction factors for the
benchmark case discussed in Sect. III only for elastic scattering, which leaves the scatterer in its ground
state. In heavier nuclei such as 238U and other actinides, excited states are closer together which makes
resolving individual final states infeasible with neutron detection. Further, the density of states rapidly
increases with excitation energy in these nuclei, forming a continuum. This feature of heavy nuclei results
in a continuous outgoing neutron energy distribution for a given scattering angle.

 To accurately measure the outgoing neutron energy spectrum, the energy dependence of the
finite-geometry correction factors must be accounted for [6]. A correction procedure analogous to what
was described previously is used to derive the energy dependence. The cross-section distributions shown
in Sect. III are replaced by neutron kinetic energy distributions which are evaluated at a fixed angle.
Additionally, no distinction is made between elastic and inelastic scattering. In this way, the correction
factors relevant for the 238U neutron scattering campaign were derived with the new framework.

 The geometry parameters of the 238U measurement are listed in Table I. Figures 6 and 7 show the
simulated kinetic energy distributions needed to derive the energy-dependent correction factors for 5
MeV neutrons incident of the 238U sample. These distributions show the neutron yield 𝑌 as function of
the neutron kinetic energy 𝑇, evaluated at a constant angle. Figures 6 and 7 are analogous to Figures 2
and 3. Figure 8 shows the correction factors derived from the simulated energy distributions. The
attenuation factor is not included in Figure 8.

similar for the different angles; the correction is constant at roughly 0.83 until 1.5 MeV, then decreases
smoothly to 0.65 at 0 MeV.

V. Summary and Outlook
 A new GEANT4-based simulation framework has been developed to calculate the finite-
geometry correction factors needed in neutron scattering measurements. The new framework was
benchmarked against the work of Velkley et. al. [5], which studied 8.97 MeV neutrons elastically
scattered from a 59Co sample. The new framework can account for the outgoing neutron energy
dependence of the correction factors, which is critical for measurements on heavy nuclei such as 238U
and other actinides. The energy dependent correction factors have been derived for all the neutron
energy settings from the recently-completed 238U campaign (2, 3, 4, and 5 MeV) which will enable a
more accurate measurement of the outgoing neutron spectra.
