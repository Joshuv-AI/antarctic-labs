Bounded Treewidth and Complete Monotonicity for Scott–Sokal Spanning-Tree Polynomials Journal: Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B
Domingos S. P. Salazar

Abstract
Scott and Sokal asked for a structural description of the finite graphs G G for which inverse powers T G − β T_{G}^{-\beta} of the spanning-tree polynomial are completely monotone. We prove the following bounded-treewidth criterion. If G G is a finite connected simple graph with tw ⁡ ( G ) ≤ k \operatorname{tw}(G)\leq k , then T G − β T_{G}^{-\beta} is completely monotone for every β > k − 1 2 . \beta>\frac{k-1}{2}. Consequently, every partial 3 3 -tree is covered throughout the first Scott–Sokal open interval 1 < β < 3 / 2 1<\beta<3/2 , including finite Apollonian networks, K 5 − e K_{5}-e , and the four-spoke wheel W 4 W_{4} . The proof combines the real Riesz/Wishart integral for determinants, star–mesh elimination of simplicial vertices, and a Gaussian Laplace kernel for the degree- d d star. These ingredients produce a Euclidean Laplace representation invariant stable under perfect elimination orderings; general bounded-treewidth graphs follow by chordal completion and monotone deletion of completion edges. The ordinary Riesz threshold also marks the boundary of the method: in the interval 1 < β < 3 / 2 1<\beta<3/2 , genuine treewidth- 4 4 cores would require a new endpoint or boundary-Riesz ingredient.

Formalism

2.1
Complete monotonicity

We use the standard multivariate version of complete monotonicity and its Laplace-transform interpretation. Background may be found in Widder’s treatment of the Bernstein theorem and in the semigroup formulation of Berg, Christensen and Ressel
Widder 1941
;
Berg et al. 1984
.

Definition 2.1

(Complete monotonicity)
.

Let
n\geq 1
. For a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, write
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
. A function
f\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty)^{n})
is
completely monotone
if, for every such
\alpha
,

(-1)^{|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}f(x)\geq 0,\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{n}.

(2.1)

The following elementary form of the Bernstein–Hausdorff–Widder principle is the only direction used in the proofs
(
Widder 1941
, Chapter IV)
.

Proposition 2.2

(Positive Laplace kernels)
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,\infty)^{n}
such that

f(x)=\int_{[0,\infty)^{n}}e^{-x\cdot t}\,d\mu(t)

(2.2)

is finite for every
x\in(0,\infty)^{n}
. Then
f
is completely monotone.

Proof.

For each multi-index
\alpha
and each compact subcone
K\Subset(0,\infty)^{n}
, the integrand

t^{\alpha}e^{-x\cdot t}

(2.3)

is dominated locally by finite linear combinations of Laplace kernels at slightly smaller positive arguments. After truncating by
t_{i}\leq R
, monotone or dominated convergence justifies passage to the limit. Thus

(-1)^{|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{[0,\infty)^{n}}t^{\alpha}e^{-x\cdot t}\,d\mu(t)\geq 0.

(2.4)

∎

Lemma 2.3

(Closure properties)
.

Let
f
and
g
be completely monotone functions on positive orthants.

1.

If
f
and
g
are functions of disjoint variable blocks, then
fg
is completely monotone in the union of the variables.

2.

More generally, if
f(x)=\int e^{-x\cdot s}\,d\mu(s)
and
g(x)=\int e^{-x\cdot t}\,d\nu(t)
are positive Laplace transforms in the same variables, then
fg
is a positive Laplace transform, namely the transform of the convolution of
\mu
and
\nu
.

3.

If
f_{y}(x)
is completely monotone for each
y
and
F(x)=\int f_{y}(x)\,d\nu(y)
is finite for a positive measure
\nu
, then
F
is completely monotone.

Proof.

The first two assertions follow either by differentiating products or by multiplying the corresponding Laplace kernels. The third follows from
Proposition

2.2
when the
f_{y}
are represented by positive Laplace kernels. Equivalently, one may integrate the inequalities

(-1)^{|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}f_{y}\geq 0

against
\nu
after truncation and apply monotone convergence.
∎

2.2
Spanning-tree polynomials and Scott–Sokal graph classes

Our graph notation is standard. Unless explicitly noted otherwise, graphs are finite, connected, and simple when treewidth is discussed. A spanning tree is a connected acyclic spanning subgraph, and the weighted generating function below is the usual spanning-tree polynomial
Bollobas 1998
;
Bapat 2010
;
Sokal 2005
.

Definition 2.4

(Spanning-tree polynomial)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected graph. Its spanning-tree polynomial is

T_{G}(x)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}(G)}\prod_{e\in T}x_{e},\qquad x=(x_{e})_{e\in E}.

(2.5)

Here
\mathcal{T}(G)
denotes the set of spanning trees of
G
.

Throughout the paper the variables
x_{e}
are taken in the positive edge cone
(0,\infty)^{E}
. We often call them conductances, following the standard electrical-network convention used for weighted graph Laplacians and star–mesh transformations
Doyle and Snell 1984
;
Curtis et al. 1998
.

Definition 2.5

(The Scott–Sokal class)
.

For
\beta>0
, let
\mathcal{G}_{\beta}
denote the class of finite connected graphs
G
such that
T_{G}^{-\beta}
is completely monotone on
(0,\infty)^{E}
.

Proposition 2.6

(Matrix-tree determinant form)
.

Fix a root vertex
r\in V
. For each edge
e=ij
, let
b_{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\setminus\{r\}}
be the signed incidence vector obtained from
e_{i}-e_{j}
after deleting the root coordinate. Then

T_{G}(x)=\det\left(\sum_{e\in E}x_{e}b_{e}b_{e}^{T}\right).

(2.6)

Proof.

This is Kirchhoff’s matrix-tree theorem in weighted form
Kirchhoff 1847
;
Bapat 2010
. The weighted graph Laplacian is

L_{G}(x)=\sum_{e\in E}x_{e}\widetilde{b}_{e}\widetilde{b}_{e}^{T},

(2.7)

where
\widetilde{b}_{e}
is any oriented incidence vector of
e
in
\mathbb{R}^{V}
. Deleting the row and column indexed by
r
gives the reduced Laplacian

L_{G}^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{e\in E}x_{e}b_{e}b_{e}^{T}.

(2.8)

Kirchhoff’s matrix-tree theorem gives
\det L_{G}^{(r)}(x)=T_{G}(x)
.
∎

3
Riesz and star–mesh tools

3.1
The real Riesz/Wishart determinant kernel

Let
\Pi_{m}
denote the cone of positive-definite real symmetric
m\times m
matrices. We use the standard real symmetric-cone normalization of the Riesz/Wishart integral
Faraut and Koranyi 1994
;
Muirhead 1982
. The multivariate gamma function is

\Gamma_{m}(\alpha)=\int_{\Pi_{m}}e^{-\operatorname{tr}Y}\det(Y)^{\alpha-(m+1)/2}\,dY,

(3.1)

finite precisely for
\alpha>(m-1)/2
; explicitly,

\Gamma_{m}(\alpha)=\pi^{m(m-1)/4}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma\left(\alpha-\frac{j-1}{2}\right).

(3.2)

Lemma 3.1

(Riesz/Wishart integral)
.

Let
M\in\Pi_{m}
and let
\beta>(m-1)/2
. Then

\det(M)^{-\beta}=\Gamma_{m}(\beta)^{-1}\int_{\Pi_{m}}e^{-\operatorname{tr}(MY)}\det(Y)^{\beta-(m+1)/2}\,dY.

(3.3)

Proof.

Apply the change of variables
Y=M^{-1/2}ZM^{-1/2}
in the defining integral for
\Gamma_{m}(\beta)
. Then

\operatorname{tr}(MY)=\operatorname{tr}Z,\qquad\det Y=\det(M)^{-1}\det Z.

(3.4)

The Lebesgue measure on
\operatorname{Sym}(m,\mathbb{R})
transforms as

dY=\det(M)^{-(m+1)/2}\,dZ.

(3.5)

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{m}}e^{-\operatorname{tr}(MY)}\det(Y)^{\beta-(m+1)/2}\,dY

\displaystyle=\det(M)^{-\beta}\int_{\Pi_{m}}e^{-\operatorname{tr}Z}\det(Z)^{\beta-(m+1)/2}\,dZ

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{m}(\beta)\det(M)^{-\beta}.

(3.7)

Dividing by
\Gamma_{m}(\beta)
proves the identity.
∎

Corollary 3.2

(Determinantal complete monotonicity)
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
be positive-semidefinite real symmetric
m\times m
matrices and suppose
M(x)=\sum_{i}x_{i}A_{i}
is positive definite for all
x\in(0,\infty)^{n}
. If
\beta>(m-1)/2
, then

x\longmapsto\det M(x)^{-\beta}

(3.8)

is completely monotone on
(0,\infty)^{n}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.1
,

\det M(x)^{-\beta}=\Gamma_{m}(\beta)^{-1}\int_{\Pi_{m}}\exp\left(-\sum_{i}x_{i}\operatorname{tr}(A_{i}Y)\right)\det(Y)^{\beta-(m+1)/2}\,dY.

(3.9)

Since
A_{i},Y
are positive semidefinite,
\operatorname{tr}(A_{i}Y)\geq 0
. This is a positive Laplace representation in the variables
x_{i}
, and
Proposition

2.2
applies.
∎

3.2
Star–mesh elimination

The next lemma is the spanning-tree-polynomial form of the familiar star–mesh, or
Y
–
\Delta
, elimination from electrical network theory
Doyle and Snell 1984
;
Curtis et al. 1998
. We state it for a degree-
d
eliminated vertex because the bounded-treewidth proof iterates this operation along chordal elimination orderings.

Lemma 3.3

(Star–mesh elimination)
.

Let
H=(V,E_{H})
be a connected network and let
S\subseteq V
have size
d\geq 1
. Form
G
by adjoining a new vertex
v
adjacent exactly to the vertices of
S
, with conductances
u_{i}>0
for
i\in S
. Put

U=\sum_{i\in S}u_{i},\qquad p_{ij}(u)=\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{U}\quad(i<j,\ i,j\in S).

(3.10)

Let
x+p(u)
denote the edge-weight vector of the network obtained from
H
by adding
p_{ij}(u)
to the conductance between
i
and
j
for every distinct
i,j\in S
, creating that terminal edge if it was absent, and leaving all other edge weights unchanged. Then

T_{G}(x,u)=U\,T_{H}(x+p(u)).

(3.11)

Proof.

Choose a root vertex different from
v
, and put
v
last in the reduced weighted Laplacian of
G
. The reduced Laplacian has block form

\begin{pmatrix}L_{H}(x)+D&-w\\
-w^{T}&U\end{pmatrix},

(3.12)

where
D
and
w
encode the conductances incident to
v
. Taking the Schur complement of the scalar block
U

Horn and Johnson 2013
gives

\det L_{G}=U\det\left(L_{H}(x)+D-\frac{ww^{T}}{U}\right).

(3.13)

The matrix
D-ww^{T}/U
is exactly the reduced Laplacian contribution of the complete graph on
S
, with edge conductances
u_{i}u_{j}/U
. These conductances are added to the terminal network, either increasing existing terminal edges or creating absent ones. The matrix-tree theorem gives the asserted identity.
∎

For a three-edge star with conductances
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
, we continue to write

p(u)=\left(\frac{u_{1}u_{2}}{U},\frac{u_{1}u_{3}}{U},\frac{u_{2}u_{3}}{U}\right),\qquad U=u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3},

(3.14)

with coordinates indexed by
12,13,23
.

3.3
The Gaussian star kernel

Lemma 3.4

(Gaussian star kernel)
.

Let
d\geq 1
, let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}
be points in a real Euclidean space, and put

r_{ij}=\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2},\qquad U=\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{i},\qquad u_{i}>0.

(3.15)

If

\beta>\frac{d-1}{2},

(3.16)

then

K(u)=U^{-\beta}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{U}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}u_{i}u_{j}r_{ij}\right)

(3.17)

is a positive Laplace transform in
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})
. More precisely, after embedding the original Euclidean space into a larger Euclidean space if necessary, let
B
be any
(d-1)
-dimensional affine subspace containing
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}
. Then

\displaystyle K(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{-(d-1)/2}}{\Gamma(\beta-(d-1)/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B}t^{\beta-(d-1)/2-1}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}\bigl(t+\|z-\xi_{i}\|^{2}\bigr)\right)\,dz\,dt.

For
d=1
,
B=\{\xi_{1}\}
and the empty pair sum is zero. In particular, for
d=3
the kernel used in the parallel star-lift proof is completely monotone for every
\beta>1
.

Proof.

The weighted variance identity gives

\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}\|z-\xi_{i}\|^{2}=U\left\|z-\frac{\sum_{i}u_{i}\xi_{i}}{U}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{U}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}u_{i}u_{j}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}.

(3.19)

The weighted barycenter belongs to
B
. Therefore

\int_{B}\exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}\|z-\xi_{i}\|^{2}\right)\,dz=\left(\frac{\pi}{U}\right)^{(d-1)/2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{U}\sum_{i<j}u_{i}u_{j}r_{ij}\right).

(3.20)

Set
\alpha=\beta-(d-1)/2>0
. The gamma identity gives

U^{-\alpha}=\Gamma(\alpha)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-tU}\,dt.

(3.21)

Multiplying the two identities gives the displayed representation. Since all coefficients
t+\|z-\xi_{i}\|^{2}
are nonnegative, this is a positive Laplace representation in the variables
u_{i}
.
∎

4
The bounded-treewidth theorem

The proof uses a stronger invariant than complete monotonicity. Its Laplace coordinates are squared Euclidean distances between graph vertices.

Definition 4.1

(Euclidean Laplace representation)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected graph and let
\beta>0
. We say that
G
has a Euclidean Laplace representation at exponent
\beta
, abbreviated
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
, if there exist an integer
N\geq 0
and a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{V}
such that, for every
x\in(0,\infty)^{E}
,

T_{G}(x)^{-\beta}=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{V}}\exp\left(-\sum_{ij\in E}x_{ij}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}\right)\,d\mu(\xi),

(4.1)

with the integral finite for every positive
x
.

Lemma 4.2

(
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
implies complete monotonicity)
.

If
G
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
, then
T_{G}^{-\beta}
is completely monotone on
(0,\infty)^{E}
.

Proof.

The displayed formula is a positive Laplace representation in the edge variables, with Laplace coordinates

t_{ij}(\xi)=\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}\geq 0.

(4.2)

The result follows from
Proposition

2.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Complete graphs have Euclidean Laplace representations)
.

For
m\geq 1
, the complete graph
K_{m+1}
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
whenever

\beta>\frac{m-1}{2}.

(4.3)

Equivalently,
K_{r}
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
whenever
\beta>(r-2)/2
.

Proof.

Root
K_{m+1}
at vertex
0
, set
c_{0}=0
, and set
c_{i}=e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. For an edge
ij
, put
b_{ij}=c_{i}-c_{j}
. By
Proposition

2.6
,

T_{K_{m+1}}(x)=\det\left(\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq m}x_{ij}b_{ij}b_{ij}^{T}\right).

(4.4)

For
Y\in\Pi_{m}
, choose the positive square root
Y^{1/2}
and define

\xi_{i}(Y)=Y^{1/2}c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(4.5)

Then

b_{ij}^{T}Yb_{ij}=\|\xi_{i}(Y)-\xi_{j}(Y)\|^{2}.

(4.6)

Inserting the matrix-tree determinant into
Lemma

3.1
gives

\displaystyle T_{K_{m+1}}(x)^{-\beta}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{m}(\beta)^{-1}\int_{\Pi_{m}}\exp\left(-\sum_{i<j}x_{ij}\|\xi_{i}(Y)-\xi_{j}(Y)\|^{2}\right)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\times\det(Y)^{\beta-(m+1)/2}\,dY.

This is an
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
representation, after pushing the positive Riesz measure forward by
Y\mapsto(\xi_{i}(Y))_{i=0}^{m}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Simplicial extension preserves
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
)
.

Let
H=(V,E_{H})
be a connected graph having
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
. Let
S\subseteq V
be a clique of size
d\geq 1
, and let
G
be obtained from
H
by adjoining a new vertex
v
adjacent exactly to the vertices of
S
. If

\beta>\frac{d-1}{2},

(4.8)

then
G
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
.

Proof.

Let
u_{i}=x_{vi}>0
for
i\in S
, and put
U=\sum_{i\in S}u_{i}
. By
Lemma

3.3
,

T_{G}(x,u)=U\,T_{H}(x+p(u)).

(4.9)

Since
H
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
, there are
N
and a positive measure
\mu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{V}
such that

T_{H}(y)^{-\beta}=\int\exp\left(-\sum_{ij\in E_{H}}y_{ij}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}\right)\,d\mu(\xi).

(4.10)

Substituting
y=x+p(u)
gives

\displaystyle T_{G}(x,u)^{-\beta}

\displaystyle=\int\exp\left(-\sum_{ij\in E_{H}}x_{ij}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}\right)

(4.11)

\displaystyle\times U^{-\beta}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{U}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i,j\in S\end{subarray}}u_{i}u_{j}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}\right)\,d\mu(\xi).

For each fixed
\xi
, the second line is the Gaussian star kernel of
Lemma

3.4
.

It remains only to record the measure-theoretic construction of the new configuration measure. Put
N_{0}=\max\{N,d-1\}
and embed
\mathbb{R}^{N}
isometrically into
\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
. For each configuration
\xi
, let
a(\xi)=d^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\xi_{i}
. Apply Gram–Schmidt, with a fixed deterministic tie-breaking rule using the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
, to obtain measurably an orthonormal
(d-1)
-frame
e_{1}(\xi),\ldots,e_{d-1}(\xi)
whose span contains the vectors
\xi_{i}-a(\xi)
,
i\in S
. The deterministic tie-breaking is included only to make this a Borel choice of frame, so that the push-forward measure below is unambiguous. Thus

z_{\xi}(s)=a(\xi)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d-1}s_{\ell}e_{\ell}(\xi),\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},

(4.12)

parametrizes a
(d-1)
-dimensional affine subspace containing the clique points.

Define a map to configurations in
(\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}+1})^{V\cup\{v\}}
by

\xi^{\prime}_{w}=(\xi_{w},0)\quad(w\in V),\qquad\xi^{\prime}_{v}=(z_{\xi}(s),\sqrt{t}).

(4.13)

Then, for
i\in S
,

\|\xi^{\prime}_{v}-\xi^{\prime}_{i}\|^{2}=t+\|z_{\xi}(s)-\xi_{i}\|^{2},

(4.14)

and old edge distances are unchanged. Let
\nu
be the push-forward of

d\mu(\xi)\,\frac{\pi^{-(d-1)/2}}{\Gamma(\beta-(d-1)/2)}t^{\beta-(d-1)/2-1}\,dt\,ds.

(4.15)

All integrands are nonnegative, so Tonelli’s theorem applies. Substituting the Gaussian star-kernel formula gives

T_{G}(x,u)^{-\beta}=\int\exp\left(-\sum_{ij\in E_{H}}x_{ij}\|\xi^{\prime}_{i}-\xi^{\prime}_{j}\|^{2}-\sum_{i\in S}u_{i}\|\xi^{\prime}_{v}-\xi^{\prime}_{i}\|^{2}\right)\,d\nu(\xi^{\prime}).

(4.16)

This is precisely an
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
representation for
G
.
∎

Proposition 4.5

(Chordal bounded-clique graphs)
.

Let
H
be a connected chordal graph with clique number
\omega(H)\leq k+1
. Then
H
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
for every

\beta>\frac{k-1}{2}.

(4.17)

Proof.

A chordal graph has a perfect elimination ordering
Fulkerson and Gross 1965
. Equivalently, it may be built by starting with a clique
K_{r}
,
1\leq r\leq k+1
, and repeatedly adding a new vertex adjacent to a clique
S
in the graph already built. At each extension step,
|S|\leq k
, because the new vertex together with
S
is a clique of the final graph.

If
r=1
, the base graph has spanning-tree polynomial
1
. If
r\geq 2
, write
r=m+1
. Since
m\leq k
, the assumption
\beta>(k-1)/2
implies
\beta>(m-1)/2
, so
Lemma

4.3
gives
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
for the base clique. At each subsequent extension,
|S|=d\leq k
, so
\beta>(k-1)/2\geq(d-1)/2
, and
Proposition

4.4
preserves
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
. Induction over the construction proves the proposition.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(Deleting edges preserves
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
)
.

Let
\widehat{G}=(V,\widehat{E})
have
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
, and let
G=(V,E)
be a connected spanning subgraph with
E\subseteq\widehat{E}
. Then
G
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
.

Proof.

Write
F=\widehat{E}\setminus E
. Let
x=(x_{e})_{e\in E}
, and give every edge in
F
conductance
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\widehat{G}
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
,

T_{\widehat{G}}(x,\varepsilon\mathbf{1})^{-\beta}=\int\exp\left(-\sum_{e\in E}x_{e}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}-\varepsilon\sum_{f\in F}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}\right)\,d\mu(\xi).

(4.18)

As
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

T_{\widehat{G}}(x,\varepsilon\mathbf{1})\longrightarrow T_{G}(x),

(4.19)

because the spanning trees using completion edges receive a factor tending to zero, while the spanning trees using only
E
are exactly the spanning trees of
G
. Since
G
is connected,
T_{G}(x)>0
. On the integral side the integrand increases pointwise to

\exp\left(-\sum_{e\in E}x_{e}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}\|^{2}\right).

(4.20)

Monotone convergence gives the desired
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
representation for
G
.
∎

Theorem 4.7

(Main theorem: bounded treewidth)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected simple graph. If
\operatorname{tw}(G)\leq k
, then for every
\beta>0
satisfying

\beta>\frac{k-1}{2},

(4.21)

the function
T_{G}^{-\beta}
is completely monotone on
(0,\infty)^{E}
.

Proof.

By the chordal-completion characterization of treewidth
Bodlaender 1998
, there is a chordal graph
\widehat{G}=(V,\widehat{E})
on the same vertex set such that
E\subseteq\widehat{E}
and
\omega(\widehat{G})\leq k+1
. By
Proposition

4.5
,
\widehat{G}
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
for every
\beta>(k-1)/2
. By
Lemma

4.6
,
G
has
\mathrm{ELR}_{\beta}
. Finally,
Lemma

4.2
implies that
T_{G}^{-\beta}
is completely monotone.
∎

. Hence every partial

-tree belongs to

Corollary 4.9

(Parallel star-lifts over triangle and

cores)
.

Let

be obtained from a core

of size

or

by adjoining any finite set of pairwise nonadjacent vertices, each adjacent to exactly three vertices of

Remark 4.10

(Boundary of the method)
.

The proof is tied to the ordinary real Riesz threshold. A clique of size
k+1
produces a reduced determinant of size

, and the ordinary positive Riesz density requires

\mathcal{G}_{\beta}
in this interval, nor does it imply that every

Application: finite Apollonian networks

Finite Apollonian networks give a useful infinite application class from statistical-physics graph geometry. Andrade, Herrmann, Andrade, and da Silva introduced them as scale-free, small-world, Euclidean graphs motivated by physical and infrastructure networks
Andrade et al. 2005

A finite Apollonian network is obtained from a plane triangle by repeatedly selecting a triangular face, inserting a new vertex into that face, and joining the new vertex to the three boundary vertices of the face. These graphs are planar
3
-trees, also called stacked triangulations

In determinant form, if

The point is a positive fractional Kirchhoff determinant theory on Apollonian geometry, not a claim about the different Ising, Potts, percolation, sandpile, resistor-network, or quantum-walk models and observables studied on the same graphs.

8
Consequences, scope, and limitations

Theorem

4.7
gives the structural threshold

\operatorname{tw}(G)\leq k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_{G}^{-\beta}\text{ is completely monotone for }\beta>\frac{k-1}{2}.

(8.1)

For the first open Scott–Sokal interval
1<\beta<3/2
, this proves every partial
3
-tree, including finite Apollonian networks and the test graphs
K_{5}-e
and
W_{4}
. These examples are therefore not isolated positive cases; they are members of a full treewidth class.

The theorem does not classify
\mathcal{G}_{\beta}
in this interval, nor does it imply that all
K_{5}
-minor-free graphs lie in
\mathcal{G}_{\beta}
. Its natural boundary is the ordinary Riesz threshold: a clique of size
k+1
requires
\beta>(k-1)/2
in the positive-density argument. Thus the present method reaches treewidth
3
in the first open interval, but a genuine treewidth-
4
core would require a new boundary-Riesz representation or a different positivity mechanism. The endpoint
\beta=1
for partial
3
-trees is likewise outside the present open-density theorem.
