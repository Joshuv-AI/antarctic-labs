Comment on “Bioavailability and Growth Performance of Zinc Sulfate, Zinc Glycinate, Zinc Amino Acid Hydrate, and Zinc Methionine Hydroxy Analog in Male Broilers”
Authors: Madhuri Tribhuvan, Shashikant N Phatke
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis correspondence discusses additional considerations regarding the evaluation of zinc source efficacy in broilers. We highlight the limitations of relying on tibia zinc concentration as a functional endpoint, the impact of analyzed-versus-formulated zinc discrepancies, potential phytase-related effects on mineral utilization, and the need for broader commercial validation.Keywords: Zinc bioavailability, Broiler nutrition, Trace minerals, Zinc glycinate, Growth performance

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the study by Mortada et al. evaluating the relative bioavailability and performance effects of several zinc sources in broiler chickens (Mortada et al., 2026). The combination of a depletion–repletion model and a commercial floor-pen trial provides a valuable framework for assessing trace mineral efficacy. However, several additional considerations may improve interpretation of the findings.

The study primarily relied on tibia zinc concentration and body weight gain as indicators of zinc utilization. Although tibia zinc is a sensitive marker of mineral exposure, it does not necessarily reflect functional skeletal outcomes. Increased zinc deposition within bone tissue may correspond to improvements in bone strength, structural integrity, or resistance to locomotor disorders (Qin et al., 2026). The absence of biomechanical or histomorphometric bone assessments makes it difficult to determine whether the higher relative bioavailability values observed for organic zinc sources translate into biologically meaningful benefits for broiler health and productivity.

Another consideration involves the variability between formulated and analyzed dietary zinc concentrations. Several treatment diets exhibited notable deviations from target zinc levels. Because slope-ratio and regression-based relative bioavailability calculations depend on accurate nutrient exposure, these discrepancies may have influenced the estimation of comparative zinc source efficacy (Careli et al., 2025). Reanalysis using analyzed zinc concentrations as the primary exposure variable could provide a more robust assessment of relative bioavailability.

The practical feeding trial incorporated phytase in all experimental diets. While this approach reflects commercial feeding practices, phytase substantially alters zinc release from phytate complexes and may modify mineral utilization. Consequently, the similar growth responses observed between zinc sulfate and organic zinc sources could partially reflect phytase-mediated improvements in zinc availability. Inclusion of a phytase-free reference treatment would have enabled a clearer understanding of whether source-specific differences persist across varying digestive environments.

Finally, the study focused exclusively on male Ross 708 broilers maintained under controlled experimental conditions. Genetic background, sex, stocking density, and environmental stressors can influence trace mineral requirements and utilization (Loh et al., 2024). Validation of these findings under commercial production settings would strengthen external applicability and help define optimal zinc supplementation strategies for modern poultry systems.

The authors are to be commended for addressing an important topic in poultry nutrition. These considerations may help refine the interpretation of zinc bioavailability data and guide future investigations aimed at improving mineral nutrition in broiler production.
