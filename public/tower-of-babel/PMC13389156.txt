Prevalence and associated risk factors of pneumonia following surgery for geriatric osteoporotic fracture: A nationwide cross-sectional study of China
Authors: Jingru Chen, Fei Chen, Qingqing Su, Mengqi Shao, Youting Wang, Yuan Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Postoperative pneumonia (POP) is a common and severe complication following surgery for geriatric osteoporotic fracture (OPF), which warrants close attention as a major global public health issue. With the growth of global life expectancy, the number of patients who suffer OPF is expected to increase dramatically. However, national data on the incidence of POP and its associated risk factors remain scarce. This study aims to investigate the incidence and independent risk factors of POP among elderly patients with OPF in China via a large-scale cross-sectional survey. From September to November 2023, elderly patients who underwent OPF surgery were recruited for this cross-sectional study via stratified random sampling and a self-designed questionnaire from 594 hospitals in China. Patients’ data, including demographic characteristic, medical history and postoperative information, were analyzed with descriptive statistics, and intergroup comparisons were conducted between patients with and without POP. One-to-one propensity score matching (PSM) was applied to baseline clinical datawith a caliper value of 0.01. Independent associated factors related to POP for geriatric OPF were identified by using univariate analysis and multifactorial logistic regression. P < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference.A total of 12,496 participants were ultimately included. Among all the patients we investigated, the overall incidence of pneumonia following geriatric OPF surgery was 2.42%. Multifactorial logistic regression analysis showed that history of fracture (OR [odds ratio] 4.483, 95% CI [confidence interval] 2.824–7.117); history of surgery (OR 2.260, 95% CI 1.457–3.507); history of trauma (OR 2.866, 95% CI 1.461–5.625); pre-operative self-care ability (OR 3.254, 95% CI 2.177–4.864); aid of crutches/walkers/wheelchairs (OR 1.692, 95% CI 1.149–2.490); combined chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) (OR 4.634, 95% CI 1.889–11.369); length of stay (LOS) (OR 1.044, 95% CI 1.016–1.073) were independent risks factors predicting pneumonia for geriatric OPF surgery during hospitalization. Our study identified a non-negligible incidence of POP in elderly patients with OPF. For older adults with a history of fracture, surgery or trauma, comorbid COPD, poor preoperative self-care ability, dependence on walking aids, and longer hospital stays, clinicians should implement evidence-based pneumonia prevention protocols to optimize postoperative recovery.

Introduction

With the acceleration of population aging, osteoporotic fracture (OPF) has been labelled as a global public health challenge due to severe morbidity, long-term disability, and elevated mortality among the geriatric patients1–3. According to The Asian Federation of Osteoporosis Societies, the number of OPF (e.g., hip fractures) in Asia will rise 2.28-fold from 112 million in 2018 to 256 million in 20504. In addition, epidemiological evidence has shown that the one-year mortality rate following OPF is as high as 30% and half of survivors suffer permanent loss of functional independence5,6. Complications following OPF surgery could further adversely influence patients’ recovery and even be life-threatening. It has been reported that postoperative pneumonia (POP) mediated the largest proportion of mortality7. Therefore, understanding the prevalence of POP after OPF surgery, identifying associated factors and early intervention of pneumonia serve as an essential method to avoid further damage.

Some researchers pointed out that the incidence of pneumonia following OPF surgery ranged from 4.9% to 17%8–12. Various modifiable and non-modifiable associated factors including advanced age13, gender11,14, COPD14, hypoalbuminemia9,15, general anesthesia16 have been confirmed in the earlier studies. However, there still have some limitations. In the first place, the exact association between POP and OPF surgery has not been detected among Chinese individuals; Secondly, some previous studies were limited to small-scale surveys in one region or one hospital or relatively insufficient clinical variables to include, which could not provide a comprehensive and systematic understanding of the incidence of pneumonia and the factors affecting it after OPF surgery in China. Consequently, there is an urgent need to carry out a nationwide large-sample survey across the whole of China.

We clearly state that although previous studies have identified multiple risk factors for POP, there is still a lack of large-scale epidemiological data on postoperative POP in China nationwide. This study aims to fill this gap and provide evidence-based guidance for the prevention of postoperative pneumonia in elderly patients undergoing OPF in China. In this study, we conducted a nationwide cross-sectional study of 594 hospitals in 31 provinces, municipalities and autonomous regions in China, aiming to identify the incidence and explore associated risk factors of POP for geriatric OPF surgery.

Materials and methods

Study design and settings

This is a nationwide cross-sectional study enrolling elderly patients with OPF between September 2023 and November 2023. Data collection was conducted in 31 provinces, municipalities and autonomous regions in China (excluding the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, Macao Special Administrative Region, and Taiwan Region of China). A total of 594 hospitals covering diverse types were included in this survey, with hospital types including tertiary and secondary hospitals, general and specialty hospitals, and public and private hospitals. This study focused on patient-level and system-level risk factors for POP from a public health and nursing perspective. Detailed surgical techniques (e.g., intramedullary nailing vs. arthroplasty, spinal vs. hip surgery) were not systematically collected due to the large-scale cross-sectional design and variability in documentation standards across participating hospitals.

Participants

Stratified random sampling was adopted, with provinces, cities and districts set as sampling strata. Eligible OPF inpatients from the Department of Orthopedics at each participating hospital were screened according to uniform inclusion and exclusion criteria. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients diagnosed with OPF via X-ray, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) if necessary, with bone mineral density measured by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) showing a T-score ≤ −2.5, and aged ≥ 60 years; - (2) having received surgery treatment, currently stabilized and not yet discharged; (3) capable of understanding instructions provided by researchers; (4) informed consent and pledged to engage in this research endeavor; (5) POP was diagnosed by pathogenic bacteria detection (such as sputum culture, blood culture, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid culture, etc.), combined with imaging (such as chest X-ray or CT showing pulmonary inflammation) and clinical manifestations (fever, cough, purulent sputum, etc.). Exclusion criteria were patients who were unable to complete the questionnaire with the following situations: (1) reading and listening difficulties or cognitive disorders; (2) comorbid psychological diseases such as severe anxiety or depression; (3) comorbid osteoarticular diseases or other bone-related conditions; (4) the subject tested positive for Corona Virus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) upon investigation.

Bias

To guarantee the integrity of data collection and minimize bias, the Orthopedic Nursing Professional Committee of the Chinese Nursing Association was responsible for the organization, implementation, and quality control during the throughout the survey. Before the survey started, online unified training was delivered step by step in the order of project general director - provincial research director - research hospital director, requiring all supervisors at every level to be familiar with the content of the investigation and to understand the specific steps and precautions for filling out the questionnaire.

Data collection and variables

An electronic questionnaire was distributed to the patients 1 to 2 days prior to hospital discharge. The questionnaire could only be submitted when all the questions had been complted. At the end of the whole survey, the data were imported into Microsoft Excel 365 (office) software, and raw data was independently screened by two researchers. Questionnaires that took fewer than 2 minutes to finish or contained obvious logical contradictions in the data were excluded.

The questionnaire was designed by the researchers and included basic information of patients (hospital admission number, gender, age, education level, occupation, admitting diagnosis, etc.), daily lifestyle (such as whether they smoke or drink alcohol), previous medical history (types of underlying diseases and previous illness, etc.) and post-surgical conditions (complications, rehabilitation, use of assistive devices). Height and weight were measured in centimeters and kilograms, and body mass index (BMI) is expressed as weight/height squared. Clinicians diagnosed postoperative pneumonia based on patients’ symptoms and signs, laboratory findings (routine blood tests, sputum culture), and chest imaging (chest X-ray or CT when necessary. History of fracture was defined as any self-reported previous fracture (excluding the index OPF leading to the current hospitalization). We did not further differentiate fracture types by energy mechanism, anatomical site, or whether surgical treatment was received, due to the large-scale cross-sectional design and the potential for recall bias in such detailed information. The questionnaire focused on patient-level demographic characteristics, lifestyle factors, medical history, and postoperative conditions. Regarding respiratory comorbidities, only COPD was systematically recorded as a binary variable (Yes/No). Other active lung diseases (e.g., asthma, lung cancer, interstitial lung disease, bronchiectasis, or active tuberculosis) were not specifically queried in the original instrument, as the survey was designed primarily from a nursing and public health perspective to capture modifiable and system-level risk factors across a broad range of hospitals with varying documentation capacities.

In addition to clinical variables, we collected sociodemographic data including the number of children and education level. In the Chinese geriatric care context, the number of children serves as a proxy for family support availability, which is a critical determinant of postoperative rehabilitation adherence, early mobilization, and caregiver-assisted pulmonary hygiene. Education level was included as an indicator of health literacy, which has been associated with the ability to comprehend postoperative instructions and participate in pneumonia prevention strategies such as deep breathing exercises. While we acknowledge that these variables do not replace the clinical importance of detailed pulmonary disease history, they reflect the patient-level and system-level risk factors that this nationwide survey was designed to evaluate from a public health and nursing perspective.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted in SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to determine whether the continuous data were normally distributed. Normally distributed continuous variables were analyzed by independent samples t-test and expressed as mean ± standard deviation\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\left(X\pm\:S\right)$$\end{document}; non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median [M (Q1, Q3)], and differences between groups were determined by the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables (age group, gender, etc.) were described by frequency and percentage (%), and differences between groups were judged by the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. P < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference.

To minimize confounding effects due to differences between groups, propensity score matching (PSM) was used in a 1:1 ratio based on patients’ propensity score by the logistic regression model. The caliper value is set to 0.01 and the matching variables include patients’ basic information on age and gender, which have been confirmed to be the associated factors in the former studies17,18.

Variables with significant differences in univariate analysis (P < 0.05) were included in multifactorial logistic regression to determine independent predictors of POP in elderly OPF patients. The strength of the correlation was described using OR (odds ratio) and 95% CI (95% confidence interval). The Hosmer-Lemeshow test was used to assess the goodness of fit of the logistic regression model, P > 0.05 is an acceptable level at which the model’s estimates fit the data, indicating that the scoring model works well.

Ethics approval

The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Chinese PLA General Hospital (No: S2023-621-01). Written informed consent was obtained from all respondents before the investigation. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Results of the investigation

A total of 14,349 questionnaires were distributed in our study, and 12,496 valid questionnaires were finally collected, with an effective response rate of 87.09%. The age range of these subjects was 60–106 years. Among them, 3,284 (26.28%) were male, and 9,212 (73.72%) were female. Discussion

POP is one of the most devastating complications in the population among OPF patients, for the reason that patients’ respiratory infections increase hospitalization costs, consumption of healthcare resources, and even lead to higher mortality rates19,20. However, national survey data on POP in OPF patients is seldom reported.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the largest cross-sectional investigation of POP after OPF surgery in China to date, owing to this study enrolled tens of thousands of OPF patients, covering all provinces in China mainland, including Tibet, Xinjiang and other remote areas, and explored independent associated factors for the occurrence of POP following OPF surgery.

Our investigation reveals that the overall incidence of POP after OPF among the elderly was 2.42% (302/12,496 cases), which is slightly lower than the rates reported in previous studies. For instance, a prospective research of 1,495 elderly patients undergoing surgery for intertrochanteric fractures showed the prevalence of POP was 3.5%21. A meta-analysis comprising 35 articles found the incidence of POP in hip fracture patients was 8.9%22. Tian et al.23 carried out a retrospective study in a tertiary referral center in China from 2006 to 2020, denoting a rate of 5.8%. Another cohort study conducted in Denmark found that 5.3% of patients developed pneumonia within 30 days of fracture surgery24. After the participants were classified by different regions, the results showed that the incidence of POP was the lowest in eastern China (1.42%, 51/3,599 cases) and the highest in southern China (3.76%, 34/905 cases). Therefore, We speculate that the relatively low POP incidence observed in our study can be attributed to the large sample size and nationwide multi-center design.

More importantly, this may be closely related to the current situation of medical resource allocation in China. Eastern China and northern China have a high level of economic development, especially the municipalities directly under the central government represented by Shanghai and Beijing. The economic level directly affects the input and allocation of medical resources25. Developed regions have more financial support and a favorable external environment, which can attract and maintain more medical resources, including high-quality medical facilities, professionals, and advanced medical technology, showing the quality and concentration of medical resources26. Therefore, the incidence of POP in elderly patients with OPF is significantly lower in these regions. However, in the northwest and southwest of China, the allocation and accessibility of medical resources encounter significant challenges. The western region of the country is comparatively deficient in educational infrastructure, which may hinder the development and retention of medical personnel, resulting in a dearth of skilled healthcare talent. Additionally, the remoteness of certain areas elevates the costs and complexities associated with providing medical services, thereby constraining the efficient allocation and application of healthcare assets27. Consequently, the postoperative care for patients who have undergone OPF procedures in these regions tends to be less comprehensive, making it challenging to achieve a standard of care equivalent to that found in more developed areas.

An additional noteworthy finding from our investigation is the observed higher incidence of POP among patients with OPF in public and general hospitals, a topic that has not been adequately addressed by prior research scholars. A comprehensive analysis of management practices across 1,183 hospitals in 7 distinct countries revealed that managers in public hospitals generally exhibit inferior performance compared to their counterparts in private hospitals, which may support our results28. Simultaneously, public and general hospitals tend to manage more severe and complex cases, often facing bed shortages and rapid patient turnover. In contrast, private hospitals typically possess greater financial resources and are more likely to implement stringent infection control measures. They also provide more personalized and comprehensive postoperative care as well as rehabilitation services for OPF patients, which can contribute to a reduction in the incidence of POP. Moreover, the specialized hospitals typically concentrate on specific disease types and treatments, which may result in enhanced experience and expertise in the management of OPF, encompassing advanced equipment and highly specialized medical personnel29. Such focused clinical resources enable stricter infection prevention standards and standardized postoperative nursing workflows, thereby effectively reducing the occurrence of POP.

In this study, OPF patients with a previous history of fracture, those with a second fracture, are four times more likely to develop POP after surgery than patients without a history of fracture (OR 4.483; 95% CI 2.824–7.117; P < 0.0001). This result is consistent with those reported in the literature so far. Van et al.30 prospectively collected data through the electronic medical record system and compared the incidence of postoperative complications between patients with primary and secondary fractures, and found that POP occurred more frequently in the secondary fracture group (4/71 vs. 1/71). The declined bone strength, disruption in bone architecture, and a reduction in bone mineral density observed in osteoporosis, combined with the poor physiological function commonly seen in aged individuals with OPF, may contribute to this phenomenon. Furthermore, the stimulation of the fracture end elicits severe pain and diminishes patient mobility, ultimately leading to suboptimal rehabilitation outcomes and a bleak prognosis. The former studies have also confirmed that the patients sustaining a second fracture have more postoperative comorbidities and/or other harms due to decreased physical function than those suffering a fracture31.

Furthermore, our analysis has identified a history of surgery as an additional independent contributor to the development of POP among hospitalized OPF patients. A consensus among Chinese medical experts endorses the notion that patients exhibiting postoperative infections following fracture surgery typically present a distinct surgical history32. This is likely due to the potential for long-term impacts on patients, such as the risks of respiratory infections, lung infections, or pulmonary insufficiency, which may persist following surgical procedures33. Besides, it also depends on factors such as the type of surgery, the patient’s health status, and post-surgical care measures.

COPD induces structural and functional alterations in the lung and thoracic cavity of patients, leading to reduced thoracic compliance, impaired ventilation, and ventilation-perfusion mismatch, ultimately resulting in irreversible pulmonary injury34, which has been proven to be a significant associated factor for the development of POP in our study. Yakubek et al.35 queried The American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS NSQIP) and prospectively collected 64,796 patients’ data. Their results revealed that patients with COPD were more likely to develop postoperative complications, including pneumonia (OR 4.24, 95% CI 2.90–6.20, P < 0.001). Similarly, reasonable evidence summarized by 10 original articles suggested that the retention of airway secretions due to COPD may result in postoperative pulmonary complications among patients with OPF (OR 3.76, 95% CI 2.07–6.81)18. The problem of mitigating the impact of COPD history on patient rehabilitation has been addressed by researchers, who have highlighted that inhaled nebulized agents like ipratropium bromide can induce relaxation of the airway smooth muscle and decrease sputum production during and after surgery, thereby diminishing the risk of POP36. Other interventions to decrease the risk of POP in the perioperative period include using preoperative risk scoring tools to assess the degree of risk, followed by appropriate analgesia administration, provision of health education, and early mobilization37.

We also found the rate of POP in OPF patients with a history of trauma was 1.866 times greater compared to those without such a history (OR 2.866, 95% CI 1.461–5.625, P = 0.002). The potential causes could be with the increase of basic diseases in the elderly, the tolerance to various stimuli decreases. After suffering from trauma, especially chest injury, it will lead to (1) partial alveolar collapse, insufficient pulmonary ventilation function provides conditions for bacterial growth and increases the risk of POP infection38; (2) Diaphragmatic limitation affects normal respiratory function, leading to accumulation of gases and secretions in the alveoli, providing a suitable environment for bacterial growth; (3) After trauma, the cough reflex is affected and the ability to remove foreign bodies and secretions in the airway is weakened, resulting in the retention of bacteria and sputum in the airway, and eventually induce pneumonia39; (4) Stress after trauma can lead to fluid imbalance and dehydration, cause the reduction of water in the bronchus, and make the airway mucus thick and difficult to effectively remove40,41.

Functional independence of the elderly is considered to be an indicator evaluating their basic activities of daily living. Our study has demonstrated that the low pre-operative self-care ability and aid of crutches/walkers/wheelchairs postoperatively may contribute to the occurrence of POP. The primary objective of surgical intervention for OPF is to facilitate patients’ complete restoration to their pre-fracture physical condition. Postoperative mobility impairment not only adversely impacts the quality of life among elderly individuals but also hampers their rehabilitation process, thereby leading to postoperative complications and prolonged hospitalization duration. Regrettably, the current state of postoperative rehabilitation for OPF patients remains unsatisfactory.

Based on the statistics, 31%-46% of hip fracture patients continued to exhibit impairments in mobility, self-care, and activities of daily living at the 4-month follow-up42. Another observational study conducted by Lim et al. revealed that 60% of patients achieved independent ambulation at the 6-month postoperative mark, while 66.7% of patients who had already attained self-sufficient mobilization before surgery were able to regain their pre-fracture walking ability43. Furthermore, a critical review of the long-term disability outcomes following hip fracture reported that between 40% and 60% of patients accomplished a level of physical performance and ability to perform instrumental activities of daily living that was comparable to their pre-fracture state. Additionally, 40%-70% regained their independence in basic activities. Among individuals who were able to live completely on their own prior to the fracture, only 20%-60% required assistance for various tasks 1 to 2 years post-fracture44.

Therefore, active and effective postoperative rehabilitation training such as plyometrics, joint mobility exercises, gait, balance, and mobility practice play a key role in minimizing complications, restoring muscle strength and coordination, and achieving optimal joint conditions. Ståhl et al.45 implemented a quasi-experimental study among those aged 80 years or older who had hip fracture surgery, patients in both groups received standard preoperative and postoperative care therapy as conventionally used at the clinic while the physical therapy group accepted additional intensified treatments. These interventions involve encouraging patients to transition from lying in bed to sitting on the edge of the bed and to stand or walk as immediately as possible after surgery (ideally on postoperative day 1) with at least four stands during the day. Apart from this, they were also instructed to take a minimum of four sets of ten deep breathing exercises each per day. Post-intervention results showed a significantly lower incidence of POP in the physiotherapy group.

LOS is widely recognized as a critical predictor of the development of POP among hospitalized patients, which not only increases mortality rates but also adversely affects both clinical and financial outcomes in public health settings46. However, it must be acknowledged that there is a bidirectional relationship between LOS and pneumonia: on the one hand, postoperative pneumonia as a complication can prolong the hospital stay; on the other hand, the prolonged LOS itself may increase the risk of pneumonia through mechanisms such as increased in-hospital exposure time, muscle atrophy, and decreased respiratory function. This study is of a cross-sectional design and cannot determine the temporal sequence. Therefore, the OR value of LOS (1.044) should be interpreted as a correlation rather than causation. This result may reflect that pneumonia leads to the prolongation of hospital stay, rather than the mere increase in the length of hospital stay itself increasing the risk of pneumonia. In clinical practice, measures such as early mobilization and optimization of respiratory function should be taken simultaneously to shorten the LOS and prevent pneumonia, breaking this vicious cycle. Numerous scholars have conducted extensive research on the correlation between LOS and POP, revealing a complex relationship influenced by several contributing factors. Firstly, the intricacies associated with orthopedic surgery can result in prolonged operative times and increased anesthetic exposure, both established associated factors for POP, particularly in elderly patients with multiple comorbidities47. Furthermore, patients experiencing complications or delayed recovery may necessitate extended LOS during which they are at an elevated risk for infections such as POP. Prolonged hospital stays can lead to deterioration in overall physical condition including muscle wasting and weakness, which may compromise respiratory function. This decline heightens the likelihood of respiratory complications, including POP, particularly among individuals with pre-existing pulmonary conditions. In conclusion, while POP extends LOS, it is important to note that longer LOS concurrently elevates the risk of developing POP. Preventive measures such as early mobilization and optimization of respiratory function are essential strategies to mitigate the incidence of POP in orthopedic patient.

Limitations

There are several limitations to our study. First, this cross-sectional investigation does not capture the longitudinal trajectory or fluctuations in the prevalence of POP among elderly patients with OPF. Second, recall bias may be present, as respondents might inaccurately remember or report their past behaviors or circumstances. Third, despite implementing standardized training and instruction, cultural and linguistic disparities may influence the comprehension and feedback of a subset among respondents, given that the survey encompasses diverse regions nationwide. Fourth, a more detailed classification and analysis of the fractured parts was not carried out. Fifth, this study did not collect detailed information on active lung diseases other than COPD, such as asthma, lung cancer, and interstitial lung disease, or tuberculosis and surgical procedures (e.g., type of internal fixation, arthroplasty versus intramedullary nailing, spinal versus hip surgery). Different surgical techniques may influence postoperative mobility, respiratory function, and the duration of bed rest, thereby potentially affecting the incidence of POP. Future large-scale prospective studies should incorporate standardized documentation of surgical details to further clarify the relationship between surgical approaches and POP in this population.

Conclusions

People aged over 60 years are at high risk of OPF and the predicted increase in the number of the elderly (e.g., in China from 254 million in 2019 to 402 million in 2040) is expected to result in a proportional increase in the number of fragility fractures in the next decades. Clinicians should closely monitor perioperative respiratory function to prevent POP and improve postoperative recovery, as well as lower the socioeconomic burden on patients. High-risk groups include elderly OPF patients with a prior history of fracture, surgery or trauma, comorbid COPD, poor preoperative self-care ability, postoperative reliance on walking aids, and prolonged LOS. Although our study did not collect surgical details, it is plausible that patients undergoing different surgical procedures (e.g., hip replacement versus spinal fixation) may have varying degrees of early postoperative mobility, which could influence the risk of POP. Future studies should incorporate surgical variables to explore these potential differences.
