TFAP2A enhances radioresistance of non-small cell lung cancer by transcriptionally activating BRCA1
Authors: Jiachun Ma, Fei Wang, Jingxin Zhang, Chen Tian, Shunshun Bao, Jupeng Yuan, Jinming Yu, Xiao Zhang, Dawei Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionRadioresistance remains a major obstacle in the treatment of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Transcription factor AP-2α (TFAP2A) has been implicated in tumor progression, but its role in NSCLC radioresistance and translational therapeutic relevance remain unclear.MethodsBioinformatics analyses of TCGA and GEO datasets were used to identify transcription factors associated with NSCLC radioresponse. TFAP2A expression was evaluated in NSCLC tissues by immunohistochemistry. The functional role of TFAP2A was investigated using gain- and loss-of-function experiments in NSCLC cells, xenograft models, and patient-derived NSCLC organoids. Mechanistic studies included RNA sequencing, DNA damage repair assays, dual-luciferase reporter assays, chromatin immunoprecipitation, BRCA1 rescue experiments, and extended DNA damage response pathway validation. Structure-based virtual screening, surface plasmon resonance analysis, clonogenic survival assays, and xenograft experiments were performed to assess TFAP2A-targeted radiosensitization.ResultsTFAP2A was overexpressed in NSCLC tissues and was associated with poor prognosis and reduced radiotherapy response. TFAP2A knockdown enhanced radiosensitivity by reducing post-irradiation proliferative recovery, increasing apoptosis, inducing G2/M accumulation, and impairing DNA damage repair, whereas TFAP2A overexpression promoted radioresistance. TFAP2A directly bound to the BRCA1 promoter and transcriptionally activated BRCA1. BRCA1 restoration partially rescued the radiosensitizing effects and G2/M accumulation induced by TFAP2A knockdown. Extended validation of representative DNA damage response pathways and additional RNA-seq-derived repair candidates further supported BRCA1 as a principal downstream effector in this model. Patient-derived NSCLC organoids confirmed that TFAP2A knockdown enhanced radiosensitivity in a clinically derived ex vivo model. Pharmacological validation identified Dephospho-CoA as a candidate TFAP2A-binding compound that enhanced radiotherapy response in clonogenic assays and xenograft models.ConclusionsTFAP2A promotes NSCLC radioresistance by transcriptionally activating BRCA1 and enhancing DNA damage repair. The TFAP2A/BRCA1 axis may serve as a predictive biomarker and therapeutic target for overcoming radioresistance, and TFAP2A-targeted radiosensitization warrants further preclinical development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13402-026-01267-6.Keywords: NSCLC, Radioresistance, TFAP2A, BRCA1, DNA damage repair

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for approximately 85% of cases [1]. Despite advances in treatment modalities such as surgery, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and radiotherapy (RT), the 5-year survival rate for NSCLC patients remains dismal, particularly for those diagnosed at advanced stages [2–4]. Radiotherapy plays a pivotal role in the management of NSCLC, offering both curative and palliative benefits [5–7]. However, the development of radioresistance significantly limits its efficacy, leading to treatment failure and disease progression [8–10]. The mechanisms underlying radioresistance are multifaceted, involving enhanced DNA damage repair, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and cancer stem cell properties [11–13]. Among these, aberrant activation of DNA repair pathways, particularly homologous recombination (HR), has emerged as a critical contributor to radioresistance [14].

Transcription factors are key regulators of gene expression and have been implicated in tumor progression and therapy resistance [15–19]. Transcription factor AP-2α (TFAP2A), a member of the AP-2 family, is known for its dual roles in embryogenesis and tumorigenesis [20, 21]. Although previous studies have identified TFAP2A as a transcription factor involved in regulating the proliferation of NSCLC and promoting esophageal cancer cell metastasis and radioresistance via activating EGFR [19, 22], whether TFAP2A participates in DNA damage repair and its potential association with radiotherapy resistance in NSCLC remains unreported.

Breast cancer susceptibility gene 1 (BRCA1) is a well-established mediator of HR-mediated DNA repair [23, 24], and its overexpression has been linked to chemoresistance and radioresistance in multiple cancers [24–26]. In NSCLC, elevated BRCA1 expression correlates with poor response to platinum-based chemotherapy and radiotherapy [25]. However, the upstream regulatory mechanisms controlling BRCA1 expression in NSCLC, particularly in the context of radiotherapy, remain unclear. Although BRCA1 has been clearly demonstrated to play a critical role in radioresistance in NSCLC [27], the precise mechanisms regulating BRCA1 expression following radiation remain elusive.

In this study, we integrated bioinformatics analyses with clinical and experimental validation to elucidate the role of TFAP2A in NSCLC radioresistance. We identified TFAP2A as a potential biomarker for radioresistance and demonstrated its ability to transcriptionally activate BRCA1, thereby enhancing DNA repair and conferring radiotherapy resistance. Our findings reveal a novel TFAP2A/BRCA1 axis as a therapeutic target to overcome radioresistance in NSCLC and improve patient outcomes.

Results

TFAP2A is a potential biomarker for radioresistance in NSCLC

To identify transcription factors associated with radiotherapy resistance in NSCLC, we analyzed differential expression patterns across multiple datasets, including TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-LUSC, GSE30219, and GSE31210. Venn diagram analysis revealed 18 common differentially expressed transcription factors (Fig. 1A). Among these, GEPIA2 survival analysis identified four transcription factors (TFAP2A, CSRNP1, E2F8, and E2F7) significantly correlated with poor prognosis in NSCLC patients (Fig. 1A-B). Further analysis of radiotherapy-treated NSCLC patients in TCGA datasets demonstrated that only TFAP2A expression was significantly associated with worse overall survival (OS) (Fig. 1C, P < 0.05).

Analysis of GEPIA2 dataset revealed that TFAP2A expression was significantly elevated in both LUAD and LUSC compared to normal lung tissues (Fig. 1D). High TFAP2A expression was associated with reduced OS for NSCLC patients in the TCGA dataset (Fig. 1E, P < 0.05). Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining of 24 paired NSCLC and adjacent normal tissues confirmed higher TFAP2A expression in tumor tissues (Fig. 1F, P < 0.05). Among 66 NSCLC patients who underwent radiotherapy (Table 1), patients with low TFAP2A expression exhibited higher radiotherapy response rates (36.4% vs. 21.2%, P = 0.001) and better progression-free survival (PFS) (Fig. 1G, P = 0.039). Multivariate analysis confirmed that high TFAP2A expression independently predicted poor radiotherapy response and shorter PFS (Tables 2 and 3). These findings suggest that TFAP2A may play a critical role in NSCLC radiotherapy resistance.

TFAP2A Modulates Radiosensitivity of NSCLC Cells In Vitro and In Vivo

To explore the functional role of TFAP2A in radioresistance, we generated stable TFAP2A-knockdown NSCLC cell lines (H1299, H1975) (Fig. 2A-B). Colony formation assays revealed that TFAP2A depletion significantly sensitized NSCLC cells to irradiation (Fig. 2C-D). Additionally, CCK-8 assays showed that TFAP2A knockdown reduced post-irradiation cell viability and proliferative recovery (Fig. 2E-F). Flow cytometry analysis further indicated that TFAP2A silencing increased radiation-induced apoptosis (Fig. 2G-H, P < 0.001) and G2/M phase arrest (Fig. 2I-J, P < 0.05). In vivo, TFAP2A knockdown enhanced the tumor response to irradiation in H1299 xenografts, as evidenced by reduced tumor growth and decreased Ki67 staining after radiotherapy compared with control tumors (Fig. 2K-M, P < 0.05).

In contrast, we established TFAP2A-overexpressing NSCLC cell lines (A549, PC9) (Fig. 3A-B). As evidenced by increased colony formation after irradiation, TFAP2A overexpression enhanced radioresistance in NSCLC cells (Fig. 3C-D). CCK-8 assays confirmed that elevated TFAP2A expression promoted post-radiotherapy cell viability (Fig. 3E-F). Flow cytometry analysis showed that TFAP2A overexpression reduced radiation-induced apoptosis (Fig. 3G-H) and mitigated G2/M phase arrest (Fig. 3I-J). Conversely, TFAP2A overexpression attenuated the response of A549 xenografts to irradiation, resulting in greater post-radiotherapy tumor growth and higher Ki67 staining compared with vector controls (Fig. 3K-M, P < 0.05). Consistently, TUNEL staining of xenograft tumors showed that TFAP2A knockdown enhanced, whereas TFAP2A overexpression reduced, radiation-induced apoptosis, further supporting the role of TFAP2A in regulating in vivo radiosensitivity (Figure S1A-B).

To examine whether irradiation regulates TFAP2A expression or cellular localization, we performed time-course Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses after irradiation. TFAP2A protein expression remained largely unchanged (Figure S2A-B), and TFAP2A was predominantly localized in the nucleus without obvious irradiation-induced redistribution (Figure S2C-D), suggesting that TFAP2A-mediated radioresistance depends on its baseline nuclear transcriptional activity rather than acute radiation-induced upregulation or subcellular redistribution.

To further strengthen the translational relevance of these findings, we established patient-derived NSCLC organoids and evaluated their response to irradiation after TFAP2A knockdown (Figure S3A-B). Compared with shNC controls, TFAP2A-knockdown organoids showed reduced viability after irradiation, indicating enhanced radiosensitivity in a clinically derived ex vivo model (Figure S3C).

TFAP2A enhances DNA damage repair in NSCLC cells

To determine whether TFAP2A affects the repair of radiation-induced DNA damage, we examined γH2AX dynamics after irradiation. Immunofluorescence staining showed that γH2AX foci were rapidly induced at 0.5 h in both control and TFAP2A-knockdown cells. However, at 24 h, TFAP2A-knockdown H1299 and H1975 cells retained more γH2AX foci than control cells, indicating delayed DNA damage resolution (Fig. 4A-B, P < 0.001). Consistently, Western blot analysis confirmed that γH2AX protein levels remained elevated at 24 h after TFAP2A knockdown (Fig. 4C-D).

Neutral comet assays further showed that TFAP2A-knockdown H1299 and H1975 cells exhibited increased tail moment at 24 h after irradiation, supporting impaired DNA damage repair after TFAP2A depletion (Fig. 4E-F, P < 0.001). Conversely, TFAP2A overexpression in A549 and PC9 cells promoted γH2AX clearance after irradiation, as shown by reduced γH2AX foci and decreased γH2AX protein levels at 24 h compared with vector controls (Fig. 4G-H). Together, these findings suggest that TFAP2A enhances the repair of radiation-induced DNA damage, thereby contributing to NSCLC radioresistance in this model.

TFAP2A Transcriptionally Activates BRCA1 to Induce Radioresistance

GSEA of RNA-seq data and TCGA datasets demonstrated that TFAP2A expression was significantly enriched in DNA damage repair-related pathways, suggesting its potential functional role in DNA repair regulation (Fig. 5A). Further intersection analysis of downstream targets identified BRCA1, a critical DNA repair gene, as a key transcriptional target of TFAP2A (Fig. 5B). Importantly, clinical correlation analysis in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) tissues revealed a strong positive association between TFAP2A and BRCA1 expression (Fig. 5C, Pearson’s R = 0.53, P < 0.001), a finding that was further corroborated by independent validation in TCGA datasets (Fig. 5D, R = 0.52, P < 0.001).

At the mechanistic level, luciferase reporter assays and ChIP experiments provided direct evidence that TFAP2A binds to the specific DNA motif GCCCCAGGC, located at positions − 787 to -779 relative to the BRCA1 transcriptional start site, thereby activating BRCA1 transcription (Fig. 5E-F). To functionally validate this regulatory relationship, rescue experiments were performed, demonstrating that ectopic overexpression of BRCA1 effectively counteracted the radiosensitizing effects induced by TFAP2A knockdown, restoring cellular radioresistance (Fig. 5G-H, Table S3). No significant changes were detected in the ATM/ATR, DNA-PKcs, or PARP pathways, nor in the other RNA-seq-derived DNA repair candidates, further supporting BRCA1 as the principal downstream effector in our model (Figure S4A-C). Additional cell-cycle analysis in the context of BRCA1 rescue showed that BRCA1 restoration partially alleviated TFAP2A knockdown-induced G2/M accumulation after irradiation, thereby linking BRCA1-mediated DNA damage repair to downstream cell-cycle recovery (Figure S4D). To further explore the translational feasibility of TFAP2A-targeted radiosensitization, we performed structure-based virtual screening and pharmacological validation. Candidate TFAP2A-binding compounds were first identified through virtual screening based on docking score ranking, binding-pose plausibility, predicted TFAP2A–compound interactions, structural diversity, and compound availability (Figure S5A). Molecular docking analysis predicted the binding modes and interaction patterns of representative candidate compounds within the TFAP2A protein pocket (Figure S5B). The 20 virtual-screening-derived compounds were then subjected to SPR-based binding validation using purified human TFAP2A protein. Six compounds with relatively high SPR responses were selected for downstream functional assessment (Figure S5C-D). Among these candidates, Dephospho-CoA showed the strongest radiosensitizing effect in clonogenic survival assays when combined with irradiation, producing the most pronounced reduction in survival fraction (Figure S5E). In vivo, Dephospho-CoA enhanced the antitumor efficacy of radiotherapy in xenograft models, whereas Dephospho-CoA alone showed limited tumor-suppressive activity (Figure S5F). These findings provide proof-of-concept evidence that pharmacological targeting of TFAP2A may enhance radiotherapy response in NSCLC.

Clinical implications of TFAP2A and BRCA1 expression

Patients with NSCLC exhibiting high BRCA1 expression demonstrated significantly reduced radiotherapy response rates compared to those with low BRCA1 expression (39.4% vs. 18.2%, respectively; P < 0.001) (Fig. 6A). The combination of TFAP2A and BRCA1 expression levels showed superior predictive accuracy for radiotherapy response (AUC = 0.770) when compared with either biomarker alone (Fig. 6B). Furthermore, elevated BRCA1 expression was associated with significantly worse progression-free survival (PFS) in NSCLC patients (Fig. 6C). The combined evaluation of TFAP2A and BRCA1 expression provided enhanced predictive value for disease progression (AUC = 0.727) relative to individual marker assessment (Fig. 6D). Notably, in patients receiving combined radiotherapy and immunotherapy, high TFAP2A/BRCA1 expression predicted inferior outcomes (Figure S6A-B, P < 0.05).

Discussion

Radiotherapy remains a cornerstone in the treatment of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), yet the development of radioresistance significantly limits its efficacy [8, 28, 29]. Our study elucidates the pivotal role of the transcription factor TFAP2A in promoting radioresistance in NSCLC by transcriptionally activating BRCA1, thereby enhancing DNA damage repair (DDR). These findings not only deepen our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying radioresistance but also highlight TFAP2A and BRCA1 as potential therapeutic targets to improve radiotherapy outcomes in NSCLC patients.

Our bioinformatics analyses identified TFAP2A as a critical transcription factor associated with poor prognosis and radioresistance in NSCLC. Clinical data revealed that high TFAP2A expression correlated with reduced radiotherapy response rates and shorter progression-free survival (PFS), consistent with its oncogenic role in other cancers [30–34]. Functional experiments demonstrated that TFAP2A knockdown sensitized NSCLC cells to radiation by impairing clonogenic survival, increasing apoptosis, and exacerbating G2/M arrest. Conversely, TFAP2A overexpression conferred radioresistance, underscoring its direct involvement in modulating radiotherapy sensitivity. These findings should be interpreted in the context of irradiation, indicating that TFAP2A mainly modulates post-irradiation recovery and radiosensitivity rather than basal proliferation alone. These results align with previous studies implicating TFAP2A in tumor progression through mechanisms such as EMT and promoting EGFR transcription [19, 22, 35, 36], but our work is the first to link TFAP2A to radioresistance in NSCLC. TFAP2A may exert context-dependent functions across cancer types. Our findings are consistent with previous reports linking TFAP2A to tumor progression and therapy resistance, and further identify BRCA1-mediated DNA damage repair as a mechanism of TFAP2A-driven radioresistance in NSCLC. Although total TFAP2A expression was not markedly altered after irradiation, radiation stress may still regulate TFAP2A transcriptional activity through post-translational modifications, altered localization, chromatin binding, or upstream signaling pathways. Time-course Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses showed that TFAP2A protein expression and nuclear localization were not markedly altered after irradiation, supporting its role as a baseline nuclear determinant of radioresistance rather than an acutely radiation-induced or relocalized factor. Importantly, patient-derived NSCLC organoid experiments further confirmed that TFAP2A knockdown enhanced radiosensitivity in a clinically relevant ex vivo model, supporting the translational relevance of TFAP2A-mediated radioresistance beyond conventional cell-line systems.

A key finding of this study is the identification of BRCA1 as a downstream effector of TFAP2A. BRCA1, a well-established player in homologous recombination (HR)-mediated DDR [24, 37–40], was transcriptionally activated by TFAP2A through direct binding to the BRCA1 promoter. This interaction was validated by luciferase reporter and ChIP assays, pinpointing a specific binding motif (GCCCCAGGC) essential for TFAP2A-dependent BRCA1 upregulation. Rescue experiments confirmed that BRCA1 overexpression reversed the radiosensitizing effects of TFAP2A knockdown, establishing a linear TFAP2A-BRCA1 axis in radioresistance. These results are supported by clinical evidence showing co-upregulation of TFAP2A and BRCA1 in NSCLC tissues, with high BRCA1 expression similarly predicting poor radiotherapy response and survival. Notably, the combined assessment of TFAP2A and BRCA1 expression improved the predictive accuracy for radiotherapy outcomes, suggesting their potential as dual biomarkers. These data support BRCA1 as the most robust downstream mediator identified in this NSCLC radioresistance model, although additional context-dependent functions of TFAP2A cannot be excluded. Mechanistically, this rescue effect suggests that TFAP2A-dependent BRCA1 activation not only facilitates DNA damage repair but also contributes to the recovery of cell-cycle progression after irradiation, thereby supporting sustained clonogenic survival.

The TFAP2A-BRCA1 axis offers a promising target to overcome radioresistance. Pharmacological inhibition of TFAP2A or BRCA1 could sensitize tumors to radiation, particularly in patients with high baseline expression. Intriguingly, our subgroup analysis revealed that TFAP2A/BRCA1 levels also influenced the efficacy of combined radiotherapy and immunotherapy, with high expression correlating with inferior PFS. This aligns with emerging evidence linking DDR defects to immune checkpoint inhibitor responses [41–43], possibly due to increased neoantigen load from impaired DNA repair. Thus, TFAP2A/BRCA1 status might guide personalized strategies integrating radiotherapy, DDR inhibitors (e.g., PARP inhibitors), and immunotherapy. To strengthen the translational relevance of this study, we further identified Dephospho-CoA as a candidate TFAP2A-targeting radiosensitizer through pharmacological validation. Its ability to enhance radiotherapy response in both clonogenic assays and xenograft models provides preclinical proof-of-concept for TFAP2A-targeted radiosensitization, although further mechanistic, pharmacokinetic, and safety evaluations are still required before clinical translation.

While our study provides robust preclinical evidence, clinical validation in larger cohorts is needed to confirm the prognostic and predictive value of TFAP2A/BRCA1. The mechanistic details of how TFAP2A-BRCA1 signaling intersects with other DDR pathways (e.g., NHEJ) or immune modulation remain to be explored. Additionally, developing TFAP2A-specific inhibitors will be crucial for translational applications.

In summary, we unveil TFAP2A as a novel regulator of NSCLC radioresistance that operates through transcriptional control of BRCA1. Targeting this axis could enhance radiotherapy efficacy and improve survival in NSCLC patients, particularly those with TFAP2A/BRCA1-driven resistance. Further research should focus on translating these findings into clinical interventions, including biomarker-driven trials and combination therapies.

Materials and methods

Clinical samples and data collection

We obtained 66 pretreatment biopsy specimens from NSCLC patients who underwent radiotherapy at Shandong Cancer Hospital between December 2022 and February 2023. Inclusion criteria were: (1) pathologically confirmed NSCLC; (2) no other primary tumors; (3) no prior antitumor treatments (chemotherapy or immunotherapy) before radiotherapy; and (4) negative driver gene mutations. Additionally, we collected 24 paired NSCLC and adjacent normal lung tissues from surgical specimens (I-II stage NSCLC, AJCC 8th edition). All samples were obtained with informed consent and approved by the Ethics Committee of Shandong First Medical University Affiliated Cancer Hospital (No. SDTHEC2024006060).

Bioinformatics analysis

Transcriptomic data from TCGA-LUAD (568 samples), TCGA-LUSC (545 samples), GSE30219 (307 samples), and GSE31210 (246 samples) were analyzed using R packages “DESeq2” and “limma” to identify differentially expressed transcription factors (|log2FC|>1–2, p < 0.05). Venn diagrams identified common differentially expressed transcription factors. Survival analyses were performed using GEPIA2 (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/) and R package “survminer”. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted using “clusterProfiler”. TFAP2A-BRCA1 correlation was analyzed in GEPIA2 (Spearman’s correlation). TFAP2A binding sites on BRCA1 promoter were predicted using PROMO (http://alggen.lsi.upc.es) and JASPAR (https://jaspar.genereg.net) databases.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissues were stained using anti-TFAP2A (ab108311, Abcam) and anti-BRCA1 (22362-1-AP, Proteintech) antibodies. Slides were scanned (Zeiss) and analyzed using Fiji software. TFAP2A expression was categorized as high/low based on median integrated optical density (IOD = 6.1%). Radiotherapy response was evaluated per RECIST 1.1 criteria.

Cell culture and irradiation

NSCLC cell lines (H1299, H1975, A549, PC9) and HEK293T cells were obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank. Cells were cultured in RPMI-1640/DMEM with 10% FBS and irradiated using an RS2000 X-ray irradiator (160 kV, 25 mA). Patient-derived NSCLC organoids were established from fresh surgical tumor specimens and cultured in a three-dimensional matrix system. For functional validation, organoids were infected with lentivirus carrying shTFAP2A or shNC, followed by irradiation. Organoid viability after radiotherapy was assessed and normalized to the corresponding non-irradiated control.

Plasmid construction and viral transduction

TFAP2A knockdown (shRNA in pLKO.1-Puro) and overexpression (pLV3-CMV-MCS-HA-EF1a-Puro) plasmids were constructed by Wuhan Miaoling Biotechnology. Lentiviruses were packaged in HEK293T cells using psPAX2 and PMD2.G. Stable cell lines were selected with 2 µg/mL puromycin.

Functional assays

The clonogenic survival assay involved irradiating cells (0–8 Gy), culturing them for 10–14 days, followed by fixation and crystal violet staining, with survival fractions calculated using the single-hit multi-target model. The CCK-8 assay measured cell viability at 450 nm post-irradiation. Flow cytometry was used to assess apoptosis (Annexin V-FITC/PI staining) and cell cycle distribution (PI/RNase A staining). Consistently, BRCA1 restoration partially alleviated TFAP2A knockdown-induced G2/M accumulation after irradiation, supporting a link between BRCA1-mediated DNA damage repair and post-irradiation cell-cycle recovery. Additionally, DNA damage assays included immunofluorescence detection of γH2AX foci and quantification of DNA damage via the neutral comet assay.

Structure-based virtual screening and SPR validation

Structure-based virtual screening was performed against the predicted TFAP2A binding pocket to identify candidate TFAP2A-binding compounds. Candidate compounds were first ranked according to their docking scores, with more favorable docking scores indicating stronger predicted binding affinity. The top-ranked compounds were further evaluated by visual inspection of binding-pose plausibility, predicted non-covalent interactions with residues within the TFAP2A binding pocket, structural diversity, and compound availability for experimental validation. Based on these criteria, 20 candidate compounds were selected for subsequent SPR-based binding validation using purified human TFAP2A protein.

For SPR screening, the binding responses of the 20 virtual-screening-derived compounds to TFAP2A were measured at a single concentration. Compounds showing relatively high binding responses above the predefined screening threshold were prioritized for downstream functional validation. Six compounds were selected for clonogenic survival assays combined with graded irradiation to evaluate their radiosensitizing effects.

Molecular mechanism studies

RNA-seq was conducted in TFAP2A-knockdown vs. control cells (Novogene). For qRT-PCR, total RNA was extracted (Trizol), reverse transcribed (HiScript II Q RT SuperMix), and amplified (ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix; primers in Supplementary Table 1). Western blotting involved SDS-PAGE separation, PVDF membrane transfer, and probing with antibodies (TFAP2A, BRCA1, γH2AX, GAPDH). In the luciferase assay, BRCA1 promoter fragments (cloned into pGL3-Basic) were co-transfected with TFAP2A plasmid into HEK293T cells, and activity was measured (Dual-Luciferase Reporter System). ChIP used crosslinked A549 chromatin immunoprecipitated with anti-TFAP2A, followed by qPCR of BRCA1 promoter regions.

In vivo studies

Female BALB/c nude mice (6–8 weeks) were subcutaneously injected with A549 cells (2 × 10^6 cells/mouse) mixed with Matrigel (1:1). When tumors reached ~ 100 mm³, mice were randomized into irradiation (8 Gy) or control groups (n = 4/group). Tumor volumes were measured every 3 days. All procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care Committee (No. SDTHEC2024003059).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 24.0, GraphPad Prism 8.0, or R 4.4.0. Survival curves were compared by log-rank test. Correlations were assessed by Spearman’s test. Two-group comparisons used Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test. P < 0.05 was considered significant.
