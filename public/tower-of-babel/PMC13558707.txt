Effects of anchor characteristics and product attributes on consumer purchase decisions in live streaming sales: evidence from an eye-tracking study
Authors: Ma Jun, Zhou Xiaohong, Yi Ding, Wei Lyu, Wen-Gang Zhang, Chao Shen, Bin-Xiu Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs live streaming sales have become a significant model in e-commerce, a critical scientific question arises: how should the characteristics of anchor combinations be matched with product attributes to enhance consumers’ purchase intention? Based on a 5 × 2 × 2 experimental design, this study investigates the interactive effects of anchor combination mode (single male, single female, male pair, female pair, mixed-gender pair), product type (search-based vs. experience-based), and the gender orientation of the target audience (male-oriented vs. female-oriented) on consumers’ purchase intention. The study recruited 95 participants and employed a multi-dimensional quantitative approach combining eye-tracking technology with self-report scales. Metrics such as average pupil diameter, fixation duration, and total number of fixations were used to capture consumers’ cognitive load and visual attention distribution during the decision-making process. Data analysis utilized repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA), supplemented by post-hoc tests and simple effect analysis to reveal interactions and specific differences among variables. The results indicate a dual-pathway mechanism in live streaming consumption decisions: a Bottom-Up Pathway for processing product information and a Top-Down Pathway for social evaluation of the anchor. These two pathways show functional dissociation between eye-tracking metrics and behavioral intentions. This research provides empirical evidence and practical guidance for brands to precisely configure anchor combinations based on product attributes and to employ a “mixed-gender pairing” strategy to mitigate the risks associated with gender mismatch.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55624-4.Keywords: Live-streaming e-commerce, Anchor gender, Product gender, Purchase intention, Eye-tracking, Dual-path processing modelSubject terms: Business and management, Business and management, Neuroscience, Psychology, Psychology

Introduction

With the advancement of internet technology, live-streaming e-commerce offers consumers a more vivid shopping experience through immersive scenario construction and real-time interaction mechanisms1. It boasts high conversion rates and effective marketing outcomes2. The “immersive scenarios + real-time interaction” model of live-streaming commerce has become a new growth driver for e-commerce platforms and content platforms3. The user base of live-streaming e-commerce has reached nearly 600 million, with its penetration rate surging from 4.9% in 2019 to 37.8% in 2024, making it the largest incremental source in the consumer market. Its unique interactivity and immediacy have attracted a vast number of consumers. By the end of 2024, the number of active anchors in China’s live-streaming e-commerce industry had exceeded 5 million, and it is projected to grow to 8 million by 2025, with an expected scale of 12 million by 2030.In live-streaming e-commerce, live streaming serves as the method, while e-commerce forms the foundation for its operation4. In the field of live-streaming e-commerce, the gender of the anchor, as a fundamental social cue, has often been studied in conjunction with product types (such as search-based vs. experience-based goods), forming a classic research paradigm focused on the interaction between anchor gender (male/female) and product categories5. However, this simplistic binary classification fails to capture the diversity of anchor combinations in real live-streaming scenarios and their impact on consumers’ purchase intentions.

As the live streaming commerce model shifts from “solo operations” to “team-based collaborations”, the impact of the number of anchors and their compositional forms on marketing effectiveness has become increasingly prominent. Yet, academic research on this phenomenon still lags behind industry practice.

Specifically, there is a lack of systematic classification and exploration in current scholarship regarding the composite structure of anchor quantity and gender—namely, the five distinct gender configurations ranging from individuals (Single Male, Single Female) to pairs (Male-Male, Female-Female, and Male-Female). More importantly, few studies have conducted an in-depth analysis of the three-way interaction mechanism between these anchor gender configurations and multi-dimensional product attributes (Search vs. Experience goods, and Male-oriented vs. Female-oriented products). As live streaming rooms transition from a mono-gender perspective to multi-gender interactions, the question of how products with different target audience orientations can be optimally matched with specific anchor configurations remains an unexplored “black box.”

The classification of product types has been relatively simplistic6. Most studies have focused narrowly on the functional categorization of products (search-based vs. experience-based goods)7. Existing research has shown that product type (such as search-based versus experience-based) moderates the effect of anchor gender—male anchors perform better than female anchors when selling experience-based products, while female anchors excel in promoting search-based products8. However, these studies have largely overlooked the core dimension of Product Gender, failing to adequately uncover the matching mechanism between anchor gender and Product Gender9. Based on social identity theory10, social gender role theory11, and symbolic consumption theory12, this study introduces Product Gender as a key dimension, explicitly distinguishing products into male-oriented products and female-oriented products. This aims to further explore the interactive effects of Product Gender and anchor gender combinations on consumers’ purchase intentions.

When the product’s target gender aligns with the consumer’s own gender (or perceived gender), the product essentially becomes “a tool for the consumer’s gender expression, a badge of group identity, and an aid for role practice13.” The gender attributes of products stem from gender stereotypes, which are inherent concepts combining stereotypical beliefs with gender roles, wherein all individuals within a group are ascribed the characteristics of that group14. Men and women are perceived to possess different traits and psychological characteristics due to their gender. Certain products are frequently associated with distinct gender stereotypes within the market. This gender-based association serves as a framework for consumers to align brand attributes with their gender identity, facilitating the selection of suitable products through a process of attribute matching15. Consumers tend to exhibit positive reactions such as “high acceptance, strong purchase intention, high loyalty, and active display.” The intensity of these reactions depends on factors such as the individual’s identification with gender norms, the clarity of their gender identity, and the consumption context16.

To further uncover consumers’ cognitive processing mechanisms while watching live streams, this study introduces eye-tracking technology as a critical supplement to traditional behavioral and attitudinal scales17. The “Eye-Mind Hypothesis” proposes a close relationship between eye movements and cognitive processing in the brain. When viewing objects or scenes, people’s eye movements can reflect underlying cognitive and comprehension processes18. Eye-tracking technology serves as an effective tool for objectively collecting and analyzing consumers’ visual behavioral data. This technology is widely used in research to study visual attention and underlying cognitive processes toward specific stimuli. By capturing metrics such as fixation point distribution, gaze duration, and changes in pupil diameter, we will objectively quantify consumers’ visual attention preferences and cognitive load levels in response to different anchor combinations and product attributes19. From the perspective of neural mechanisms, applying physiological indicators can reveal consumers’ cognitive processing and quantify visual perception20. When a consumer fixates on a product, a series of neural activities occur in the brain, including perception of the product, memory retrieval, and emotional processing21. These neural activities collectively influence the consumer’s decision-making process. Analyzing eye-tracking behavioral data—such as gaze duration and changes in pupil diameter—through eye-tracking technology can help unveil the “black box” of consumer behavioral decision-making22.

Building upon the above, to address the existing research gaps, this study aims to break through the traditional “anchor gender * product type” binary framework and establish a more comprehensive and integrated research model. This research will introduce the number and gender composition of anchors as a key independent variable, while comprehensively examining the moderating effects of product type (search-based vs. experience-based) and Product Gender (male-oriented vs. female-oriented). The goal is to delve deeply into the complex interactive mechanisms among Anchor Gender, product type, and Product Gender. To this end, this study designs a multi-factor experiment using a 5 (Anchor Gender: single male, single female, two males, two females, mixed male and female) × 2 (product type: search-based/experience-based) × 2 (Product Gender: male/female-oriented) framework. This design is intended to systematically investigate the interaction effects among anchor composition characteristics, product functional attributes, and product gender attributes.

Method

Participants

The sample size was calculated using G*Power 3.1 software23. G*Power requires the dependent variable to be unique and continuous, and each within-subjects factor to have two or more levels for sample size calculation. In this experiment, this study adopted a three-factor fully within-subjects experimental design: 5 (anchor gender: single male (M), single female (F), male–male (MM), female–female (FF), male–female (FM)) × 2 (product type: search product (PT-S) vs. experience product (PT-E)) × 2 (product target gender: male-targeted products(PG-M) vs. female-targeted products (PG-F)). The dependent variables consisted of various eye-tracking physiological metrics and self-assessment measures. Each dependent variable was unique and continuous, and each factor possessed two or more levels. A priori analysis using G*Power indicated that a minimum of 70 participants was required to detect effects in a multiple repeated measures ANOVA. The recommended statistical power (β) was set at 95%, and the probability of error (α) was selected as 0.05.

A total of 95 datasets underwent cleaning and screening. Eight samples with low sampling rates or severe data loss were removed, resulting in 95 valid self-assessment datasets and 87 valid eye-tracking datasets. The sample exhibited high homogeneity in terms of age (21.1 ± 4.9 years) and occupation (100% were full-time students). All participants were right-handed, had normal or corrected-to-normal vision, and had not consumed alcohol, caffeine, or other stimulants on the day of the experiment. To ensure higher reliability of the experiment, participants were allowed a five-minute relaxation period before starting to achieve their daily baseline state. All participants provided signed informed consent forms and received a gift valued at 50 RMB upon completion of the experiment. This experiment was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Institute of Neuroscience and Cognitive Psychology of AHPU (AHPU- SEM-2023-003). All experiments were performed in accordance with relevant named guidelines and regulations.

Stimuli

To ensure theoretical clarity, this study operationalizes three key constructs as follows:

Anchor Gender Combination refers to the gender composition of live streamers, operationalized at five levels: single male (M), single female (F), male–male (MM), female–female (FF), and male–female (FM). This classification captures both the number and gender configuration of anchors in live streaming rooms, reflecting the shift from solo to team-based broadcasting in industry practice.

The selection of experimental stimuli was grounded in established product attribute definitions and empirical market evidence to ensure scientific rigor and representativeness. For functional classification, electric shavers and hair removal devices were selected as search-based products, as their core performance metrics, such as motor speed or pulse light intensity, can be objectively verified through technical specifications. Conversely, gaming keyboards and lipsticks were categorized as experience-based products, as their value is primarily derived from subjective sensory experiences, such as tactile feedback and shade compatibility, consistent with the classification framework proposed by Kotler25.

Regarding the gender orientation of the products, market data and consumption trends provide strong justification for the current selection. Electric shavers are defined as male-oriented due to their direct correspondence to male physiological needs. While gaming keyboards have seen a rise in female users, market research, such as the 2023 China E-sports Industry Research Report, indicates that the core consumer base for high-performance mechanical peripherals remains predominately male, a trend further evidenced by user demographics on digital review platforms. In contrast, lipsticks and hair removal devices are classified as female-oriented; market sales data from major e-commerce platforms like Tmall and JD.com reveal that over 85% of purchasers and search users for these personal care categories are female, maintaining a strong gender-specific market positioning.

To further mitigate potential subjective bias in product classification, a pilot study was conducted prior to the formal experiment. Twenty independent participants, consisting of an equal number of males and females, were recruited to evaluate the gender orientation of the four selected products using a 7-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly female-oriented) to 7 (strongly male-oriented). The results indicated that electric shavers (M = 6.85) and gaming keyboards (M = 6.15) were perceived as significantly male-oriented, while lipsticks (M = 1.25) and hair removal devices (M = 1.45) were perceived as significantly female-oriented. These empirical findings robustly validate the rationality of the stimuli classification and provide a solid foundation for the subsequent eye-tracking analysis.

The stimulus materials used in the experiment were sourced from real online live-streaming screenshots. To effectively control for potential confounding variables, this study standardized the original materials through the following process: during editing, external contextual information such as the live-stream room name and real-time viewer count was removed, retaining only the anchor’s image, the physical product, and the product explanation. Furthermore, all video backgrounds were uniformly set to a solid color (see Fig. 1 Panel 1.1) to eliminate interference from background complexity on the experimental results.

To control for potential confounding effects of anchor-specific characteristics—particularly facial attractiveness, which has been identified as a critical visual cue influencing consumers’ visual attention and purchase intentions in live streaming e-commerce26—a rigorous stimulus selection and standardization process was implemented. According to dual systems theory, observable user features in online environments function as important heuristic cues that shape consumer decision-making, with an anchor’s facial attractiveness serving as the first visual cue encountered in a livestreaming room27. Similarly, research employing eye-tracking methodology has demonstrated that highly attractive anchors induce longer fixation duration and greater fixation count, thereby affecting consumers’ visual attention allocation. Therefore, controlling for attractiveness across experimental conditions is essential to isolate the effects of anchor gender combination.

All anchor images were sourced from real live-streaming platforms and underwent a multi-step screening process. First, three research assistants independently evaluated candidate images based on the following criteria: (1) facial expression maintained as neutral or slight smile (to avoid emotional display effects, as research has shown that both positive and negative emotional expressions can negatively impact sales in live streaming contexts28; (2) attire standardized as business casual or everyday wear without prominent brand logos or extreme stylistic features; (3) posture oriented toward the camera in a half-body shot; (4) estimated age range between 25 and 35 years. Images that did not meet these criteria were excluded.

Furthermore, to ensure that any observed effects are attributable to anchor gender combination rather than differential facial attractiveness across conditions, the selected anchors were matched on perceived attractiveness. Following established practices in dual-anchor live streaming research that emphasize the importance of controlling for streamer-related confounders29, we ensured that the five anchor configurations (M, F, MM, FF, FM) did not systematically differ in terms of the average attractiveness levels of the individuals depicted. This matching procedure helps rule out alternative explanations based on physical appearance heuristics and enhances the internal validity of the experimental design.

Additionally, all images were standardized in terms of size, resolution, and composition—with anchors and products positioned consistently across stimuli—to eliminate variation in low-level visual features that might drive attention independently of the manipulated variables. This comprehensive control protocol ensures that the experimental manipulations of anchor gender combination, product type, and product gender orientation are not confounded by extraneous anchor characteristics.

This study involves three independent variables: Anchor Gender (with five levels: single male, single female, two males, two females, and mixed male and female), product type (categorized as search-based or experience-based based on consumer information acquisition methods), and Product Gender (categorized as male-oriented or female-oriented based on the product’s target consumer group).In the specific operationalization, representative products were selected based on attribute definitions30. For electric shavers and epilators, the core functions are primarily evaluated through searching and comparing objective, verifiable parameters. Conversely, mechanical keyboards and lipsticks usually require consumers to use them before they can better determine whether they are suitable for them, which aligns with the classification method of search-based and experience-based products described in “Marketing Management” co-authored by Philip Kotler and Kevin Keller25. The term ‘severe data loss’ referred to trials where sampling rate fell below 80% for more than 50% of the trial duration or where complete signal loss exceeded 1000ms. Task design and procedure

In this study, the dependent variables were measured using a combination of self-report scales and objective physiological indicators, aiming to comprehensively assess consumer responses from both the level of subjective intention and subconscious reactions.

Subjective measurement employed the self-report method. After viewing each set of stimulus materials, participants were required to immediately complete a single-item test regarding purchase intention, Purchase Intention was based on a question from Spangenberg et al. rated on a 7-point Likert scale from very unlikely to very likely: “If you were going to purchase this type of product, how likely would you be to purchase this particular product”31. Single-item measures are appropriate in experimental settings where repeated measurements across multiple trials could induce fatigue32, and have been widely used in eye-tracking studies with repeated-measures designs20. The single-item approach also minimizes the time between stimulus exposure and response, capturing immediate purchase intention without contamination from intervening questions. This was done to capture their immediate, subjective inclination in purchase decision-making.

Objective measurement was achieved through eye-tracking technology33. The study utilized a Tobii Pro Spectrum eye-tracker (Tobii, Sweden) to record participants’ eye movement data during the viewing of stimulus materials, with a sampling rate of 600 Hz. Three eye-tracking measures (average pupil diameter, fixation duration, and total fixation points) assessed cognitive processing mechanisms34.

Results

The analysis aimed to investigate the effects of Anchor Gender, Product Type, and Product Gender on both subjective purchase intention and objective eye-tracking metrics. To provide a clear and structured account of the findings, this section is organized as follows: Sect. 3.1 reports the effects on the primary behavioral outcome, purchase intention, breaking down the main effects and critical two-way interactions. Section 3.2 presents the results from the eye-tracking data, which reveal the underlying cognitive processes, specifically analyzing average pupil diameter, average fixation duration, and total number of fixations.

Purchase intention

For the analysis of consumer purchase intention, a 5 (Anchor Gender: M, F, MM, FF, FM) × 2 (Product Type: Search vs. Experience) × 2 (Product Gender: Male- vs. Female-oriented) repeated-measures ANOVA was conducted on subjective Likert scale ratings. The main effects of Anchor Gender and Product Gender were followed by post-hoc multiple comparisons, with p-values adjusted according to the Bonferroni (p < .05) method for multiple contrasts. The significant interactions, including Anchor Gender × Product Gender and Product Type × Product Gender, were followed by simple effects analyses to evaluate specific mean differences across experimental conditions.

Main effect of anchor gender on purchase intention

A multifactorial repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted in SPSS35 to examine the effects of Anchor Gender, product type, and Product Gender on purchase intention. The data analysis results are presented in Table 2. The results indicate: The main effect of Anchor Gender on purchase intention was significant (F = 5.140, p < .001, ηp2 = 0.052). Product Gender had a significant impact on purchase intention (F = 23.063, p < .001, ηp2 = 0.197). Based on the results of the ANOVA, post hoc tests and simple effects analysis were conducted. As the Anchor Gender factor had five levels, pairwise comparisons were performed using Bonferroni post hoc tests. The results regarding the effect of Anchor Gender on purchase intention indicated that FM had the highest purchase intention, and its data distribution was the most concentrated, suggesting the most stable effect. Interaction effect between anchor gender and product gender

To further investigate the significant interaction effect between anchor gender and product gender (F(3.432, 1) = 102.430, p < .001, ηp2 = 0.521), a simple effects analysis was conducted with Bonferroni correction applied to adjust the significance level. The analysis revealed that when Anchor Gender was at M, the effect of product gender on the dependent variable was significant (p < .001). Specifically, the score for the dependent variable was significantly higher for male-oriented products (PG-M; M = 4.411) than for female-oriented products (PG-F; M = 3.089), with a mean difference of 1.321. When Anchor Gender was at F, the effect of Product Gender remained significant (p< .001) but in the opposite direction: the score for male-oriented products (M = 3.558) was significantly lower than that for female-oriented products (M = 4.384), with a mean difference of − 0.826.

When anchor gender was at MM, the effect of product gender was highly significant (p < .001). The score for PG-M (M = 4.805) was significantly higher than that for PG-F (M = 3.011), yielding a mean difference of 1.795, which represented the largest observed difference across all levels of anchor gender. At FF, the effect of product gender was again significant (p < .001), showing a reversed pattern: the score for PG-M (M = 3.442) was significantly lower than that for PG-F(M = 4.574), with a mean difference of − 1.132.

Notably, when Anchor Gender was FM, the effect of product gender on the dependent variable was not significant (p=.182). Specifically, no statistically significant difference was found between the scores for male-oriented products (M = 4.100) and female-oriented products (M = 3.979), with a mean difference of only 0.121. Interaction effect between product type and product gender

A simple effects analysis of the PT × PG interaction revealed a significant crossover pattern. Under the PG-M condition, purchase intention for PT-E (M = 4.152, SE = 0.080) was significantly higher than for PT-S (M = 3.975, SE = 0.084), with a difference of − 0.177 (p = .011). Eye-tracking metrics

The main effects of product type and product gender were followed by post-hoc multiple comparisons using the Bonferroni (p < .05) correction. The interactions, specifically the product type*product gender interaction observed across multiple eye-tracking indicators, were followed by simple effects analysis to quantify differences in subconscious cognitive load and visual attention preferences.

The relationship between eye-tracking metrics and purchase intention

To directly test whether eye-tracking metrics predict consumers’ purchase intention, a linear mixed model was conducted with purchase intention as the dependent variable, average pupil diameter, average fixation duration, and total number of fixations as fixed-effect predictors, and participant as a random intercept. The results revealed that average fixation duration significantly predicted purchase intention, F(1,1735) = 4.132, p=.042, indicating that longer fixations were associated with higher purchase intention. However, average pupil diameter (F (1,1735) = 2.348, p=.126) and total number of fixations (F (1,1735) = 0.022, p=.881) did not show significant predictive effects.

These findings provide partial support for the hypothesized functional dissociation. While fixation duration—a metric closely tied to interest and attention—may directly translate into behavioral intention, pupil diameter and fixation count appear to reflect distinct cognitive processes (cognitive load, exploratory search) that are not directly coupled with final purchase decisions. This nuanced pattern aligns with the dual-pathway model, suggesting that different eye-tracking indices capture different stages or types of information processing.

Discussion

This study substantiates a dual-pathway framework for consumer decision-making in live-stream shopping, wherein final purchase intentions arise from the integrated outputs of two distinct cognitive processes. The findings reveal a top-down cognitive process informed by anchor-related social identity cues and a bottom-up cognitive process driven by inherent product attributes. Crucially, the divergence between behavioral intentions and eye-tracking metrics underscores a functional dissociation between these processes, offering a nuanced mechanistic account of how consumers navigate the live-stream environment.

The strategic, top-down pathway: conscious evaluation via social identity

At the behavioral level, Anchor Gender exhibited a significant main effect on purchase intention, establishing it as a primary cue for conscious evaluation. This finding aligns with dual-systems theory as applied to live-streaming commerce. Specifically, He and Jin36 argue that anchor characteristics are processed reflectively to form trust and intention. Our results extend this by delineating a hierarchy of effectiveness: when the number of anchors increases, both same gender and opposite gender anchors can significantly increase the purchase intention; the mixed-gender anchor pair (FM) achieved the highest purchase intention, while a single male anchor (M) was the least persuasive. This synergy from multi-anchor setups is consistent with the work of Korsakul37, who found that configurations involving multiple anchors can build more complex trust, enhancing credibility beyond a single source. Crucially, our research identifies a boundary condition: the mixed-gender pair effectively neutralized the penalty typically incurred by a singular anchor-product gender mismatch. The neutralizing effect of mixed-gender anchor pairs can be explained by social consensus theory38. When consumers observe both male and female anchors endorsing a product, they perceive cross-gender agreement, which signals broader social acceptance and reduces the salience of gender mismatch. This social harmony cue overrides the identity-dissociation process typically triggered by single-gender mismatches, thereby broadening the product’s appeal across gender groups. This outcome empirically supports the proposition by Fu39. that presentations challenging rigid gender stereotypes can mitigate bias in digital endorsements, likely by projecting a socially harmonious, cross-gender consensus that broadens appeal.

The powerful interaction between Anchor Gender and Product Gender epitomizes the top-down cognitive process. This interaction is a direct manifestation of social identity processes in consumption. As articulated in foundational work by Hogg40, individuals favor in-group symbols to affirm group membership. Here, the anchor serves as a pivotal social mirror; congruence between the anchor’s gender and the product’s target audience validates consumer identity, fostering in-group trust and enhancing persuasion. This mechanism is directly corroborated by the findings of Chen et al.41 on gender-identity congruity in live streaming. Conversely, a mismatch may trigger the identity-dissociation process discussed by Dunn et al.42, whereby consumers psychologically distance themselves from undesired identities reflected by the anchor, thereby diminishing purchase likelihood. This top-down modulation is further corroborated by the linear mixed model results, which showed that anchor-driven behavioral intentions were not uniformly predicted by early eye movements; only fixation duration—among the three eye-tracking metrics—exhibited a significant relationship with purchase intention.

The automatic, bottom-up pathway: early processing driven by product attributes

Eye-tracking data delineate a separate bottom-up cognitive process where initial visual and cognitive engagement is predominantly anchored to Product Type and Product Gender. The complete absence of significant effects from Anchor Gender on any oculomotor metric is telling, indicating that social-categorical anchor cues are not prioritized in early, bottom-up attention.

The patterns in eye movements are informed by established psychophysiological principles and reveal how product attributes shape pre-conscious processing:

First, average pupil diameter—a sensitive index of cognitive load and affective arousal—was shaped by the Product Type*Product Gender interaction. This aligns with the neurobehavioral framework of Grujic et al.43, which indicates that pupil dilation may be associated with attention and cognitive load, as well as emotional valence and imagination arousal. Specifically, when view search-oriented products targeted at males (Electric Shaver), their pupil diameter increases, which may reflect the higher cognitive load they experience while processing technical parameters. Conversely, when view experience-oriented products targeted at females (lipsticks), their pupil dilation occurs, potentially due to higher emotional engagement and arousal evoked by imagining usage scenarios. This is consistent with the findings of Ursu et al.44,which demonstrated that processing objective, specification-dense information imposes measurable cognitive load, which can be reflected in pupil size.

Secondly, there is a significant difference in average fixation duration across product types, with search products exhibiting significantly longer fixation durations than experience products. This phenomenon is commonly observed in consumer eye-tracking research. For example, studies such as Tang et al.20 indicate that complex or information-rich stimuli demand longer processing time per fixation for comprehension.

Third, the total number of fixations is also significantly influenced by the main effect of product type and its interaction with the gender of the audience. Overall, experiential products elicited a higher total number of fixations, suggesting that consumers engaged in more active and divergent visual exploration of these products. Moreover, the interaction effect further indicates that, when faced with specific product combinations (Search-based products targeted at female), consumers may repeatedly switch their visual attention among multiple information points, reflecting a more complex decision-making trade-off process. When evaluating subjective qualities, consumers engage in active integration of multiple aspects (design, implied usage) through iterative visual switching between information points—a behavior that reflects a complex trade-off analysis. This pattern is consistent with the active information sampling reported by Imai45. and parallels the findings of Ji46. These bottom-up effects, however, did not uniformly translate into purchase intention. While fixation duration—a metric closely tied to attentional engagement—did show a significant predictive relationship with purchase intention, pupil diameter and fixation count did not, underscoring the selective nature of the link between early visual processing and final decision-making.

Synthesis: a sequential dual-pathway model

The critical dissociation—where Anchor Gender drives the final behavioral judgment but leaves no trace on early eye-tracking metrics—provides the strongest evidence for the proposed framework. It demonstrates that the social symbolism of the anchor is not processed in the initial wave of automatic attention. Instead, as has been indicated by Scott G in their research on complex scene perception47, social and self-relevant information is often evaluated in a later, top-down stage, after more basic perceptual and semantic features are encoded.

Our data confirm this sequence in live-stream commerce: First, the automatic, bottom-up pathway (indexed by eye movements) is engaged, where product attributes establish a context-dependent cognitive-emotional foundation. The nature of this foundation varies (technical cognition for male-search, emotional simulation for female-experience). Subsequently, the strategic, top-down pathway consciously applies the “anchor-audience gender match” heuristic, derived from social identity principles, to modulate this pre-existing foundation and arrive at the final purchase judgment. This model coherently explains why anchor effects are powerful yet dissociated from early attention, and how product attributes set the stage upon which social identity rules operate.

The linear mixed model analysis further illuminated this dissociation. Average fixation duration significantly predicted purchase intention (F(1,1735) = 4.132, p = .042), suggesting that longer visual engagement with the product or anchor may reflect heightened interest, which in turn contributes to behavioral intention. This finding aligns with research showing that fixation duration is a reliable indicator of attention and involvement. In contrast, average pupil diameter and total number of fixations showed no predictive power (p>.05), indicating that other dimensions of eye movement—such as cognitive load (indexed by pupil size) and exploratory search (indexed by fixation count)—are not directly coupled with the final decision.

Thus, while certain aspects of visual attention (sustained fixation) may feed into the top-down evaluation pathway, other perceptual processes operate independently, reflecting the automatic encoding of product attributes. This pattern supports a partially integrated dual-pathway model: bottom-up processing establishes a foundation of attention and cognitive engagement, but the ultimate purchase intention is predominantly shaped by a reflective, identity-based evaluation of the anchor. The functional dissociation between different eye-tracking metrics highlights the importance of using multiple physiological indicators to disentangle distinct cognitive processes in consumer research.

Conclusions

This study investigates the interactive influence of anchor combination characteristics and product attributes within the context of live-streaming e-commerce, a topic of critical importance for enhancing marketing efficiency in an increasingly digital marketplace. The significance of this research lies in its challenge to and extension of the traditional anchor gender-product type interaction paradigm. By shifting the research perspective from individual anchor characteristics to the interpersonal structure of anchor teams and the social gender attributes of products, this study provides a more comprehensive theoretical framework. This expansion not only aligns with the diversity of real-world scenarios but also offers a new lens to resolve inconsistent findings in prior literature by identifying product gender as a key moderating variable.

Results show that the conventional male-experience and female-search match is more complex than previously concluded. Upon introducing Product Gender, the main effect of product type becomes non-significant, revealing a more complex interaction pattern. A key finding is that mixed-gender anchor pairs demonstrate unique advantages when promoting products aimed at cross-gender audiences; they effectively neutralize the risk of gender mismatch by creating a scenario of social harmony and cross-gender consensus. Furthermore, the proposed dual-path model clarifies the differential roles of social identity cues and product attribute cues in the bottom-up and top-down cognitive processes. The partial dissociation observed between eye-tracking metrics and purchase intention, with fixation duration uniquely predicting behavioral outcomes, further refines this framework by highlighting the selective contribution of sustained attention to final decisions.

For practitioners in live-streaming e-commerce, this study offers clear and actionable optimization strategies. In terms of anchor selection, the primary principle should be matching the anchor’s gender with the product’s target Product Gender to most effectively enhance trust and purchase intention. When the target audience is gender-mixed or a perfect match is unattainable, prioritizing mixed-gender anchor pairs can minimize mismatch risks and attract a broader audience. Regarding content design, search-based products should prioritize clear, structured information to assist in complex decision-making, whereas experience-based products should focus on creating emotional atmospheres and rich usage scenarios. Additionally, companies are encouraged to use physiological metrics like eye-tracking to gain deeper cognitive insights that go beyond simple sales data.

Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations that point toward future research directions. First, the experimental sample consisted entirely of university students; while this ensured internal validity, future research should validate these findings across populations with more diverse ages, occupations, and income levels to improve generalizability. Second, the use of static screenshots as experimental stimuli, while necessary to isolate key variables and ensure internal validity, inevitably simplifies the dynamic nature of real live-streaming environments In authentic live-streaming commerce, multiple dynamic elements—such as the anchor’s speech, tone, and facial expressions, as well as real-time comments and interactions among viewers—continuously shape consumers’ purchasing decisions By presenting participants with static images, our study could not capture these real-time interactive cues, which may reduce the ecological validity of our findings. Future research should address this limitation by employing dynamic stimuli, such as video clips or actual live-streaming recordings, to better approximate the immersive and interactive nature of real-world live-streaming experiences. Finally, while eye-tracking effectively captures visual attention, the interpretation of emotional valence remains ambiguous. Future studies could integrate technologies such as electroencephalography (EEG) or facial expression analysis to more precisely disentangle cognitive and emotional components in a dynamic live-streaming environment.
