Intratumoral Fibrosis is a Potential Biomarker in Patients with Hepatocellular Carcinoma Undergoing Liver Resection
Authors: Qi-Yuan Deng, Tian-Cheng Wang, Kun Ye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntratumoral fibrosis (ITF) has been demonstrated to influence the prognosis of various fibrotic solid tumors. Thus, this study aimed to validate its role as a potential prognostic biomarker in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) undergoing liver resection.MethodsThis investigation enrolled 367 adult patients diagnosed with HCC who underwent liver resection. The patients were classified into high- (score ≥ 2) and low-ITF (score < 2) groups based on a semiquantitative Sirius red staining scale (0–3). IHC staining for CD34 was performed on resected samples to determine microvascular density (MVD) and to investigate the association between ITF and tumor vascularization. Cox proportional hazards regression with backward stepwise selection was employed to identify independent predictors for early HCC recurrence. Moreover, to mitigate the impact of confounding factors, propensity score matching (PSM) was conducted between patients in the high- and low-ITF groups. Lastly, the Kaplan-Meier method was utilized to assess and compare the disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) between the two cohorts.ResultsThe study categorized patients into high-ITF and low-ITF groups. Backward stepwise Cox regression analysis revealed that liver cirrhosis (P<0.001), serum monocyte count (P<0.001), AFP >200 ng/mL (P<0.001), substantial intratumoral necrosis (P<0.001), presence of intratumoral artery (P<0.001), nodule-in-nodule architecture (P<0.001), and high-ITF (P<0.001) were significantly associated with early HCC recurrence. Furthermore, the results suggested that DFS and OS were significantly shorter in the high-ITF group compared to the low-ITF group, both before and after PSM (P<0.05). Finally, compared to low-ITF HCCs, high-ITF HCCs were more frequently associated with high MVD counts (54.0, IQR: 43.0–67.5 vs. 45.0, IQR: 30.0–58.0, P<0.001).ConclusionITF holds promise as a prognostic biomarker for early recurrence and prognosis in patients with HCC who underwent liver resection, with practical applicability for developing personalized treatment strategies for HCC.

Introduction

Liver cancer is the sixth leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally, with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) constituting over 80% of primary liver cancer cases.1 Liver resection (LRE) is extensively acknowledged as the principal curative approach for HCC, as corroborated by multiple guidelines.2,3 In accordance with the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging system, Western clinical guidelines advocate for liver resection in patients with HCC at very early to early stages, whereas Asian clinical guidelines restrict its application to patients with intermediate-stage HCC.4–7 Overall, patients with HCC undergoing liver resection demonstrate heterogeneous outcomes, with five-year survival rates ranging between 35% and 70% and five-year recurrence rates between 50% and 70%.8–11 Among recurrent cases, 61% and 83% manifest within two years, a phenomenon commonly referred to as early recurrence.12 The observed variations can be ascribed to tumor burden, liver function, and performance status.13,14 To enhance risk stratification and prognostic accuracy, it is essential to identify effective prognostic biomarkers. Recent evidence has highlighted the importance of various molecular and immunological indicators in HCC progression. For instance, the miR-3130-5p/FDX1 axis and the expression of Vav1 have been identified as key drivers of tumor growth and poor prognosis.15,16 Furthermore, integrative analyses of hub genes and the evolving landscape of immunotherapy continue to provide vital insights into the complex microenvironmental factors influencing patient outcomes.17,18

In the context of cancer progression, the substantial impact of intratumoral fibrosis (ITF) on the mechanisms of priming, proliferation, angiogenesis, and migration is well-established,19 and ITF has been described to significantly affect the prognosis of various solid tumors, including renal cell carcinoma, breast cancer, and colorectal cancer.20–22 ITF emerges as a pathological outcome of chronic wound healing processes initiated by persistent cancer-induced tissue damage within tumors.20 Notably, it is mediated by a diverse population of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) within the tumor microenvironment.23 These fibroblasts are activated through the TGF-β/SMAD signaling pathway, subsequently enhancing matrix stiffening via cross-linking enzymes, thereby facilitating collagen cross-linking and the formation of a rigid extracellular matrix (ECM).24,25 The densely packed ECM within tumors acts as a physical barrier, hindering the infiltration of immune cells and promoting the growth of vascular endothelial cells, ultimately culminating in tumor immunosuppression and angiogenesis.26–30 Although HCC is generally characterized by low levels of fibrosis, the presence of fibrosis within HCC is still associated with malignant features.31 Therefore, ITF in HCC is anticipated to serve as an effective prognostic indicator for recurrence following liver resection.

Given the uncertain prognostic implications of preoperative ITF in patients with HCC undergoing liver resection, this study aimed to evaluate the prognostic utility of ITF within this specific patient cohort and determine its suitability as an optimal biomarker for HCC prognosis.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

This study aimed to assess the prognostic significance of ITF in patients with HCC undergoing liver resection by analyzing 367 cases from the Second Xiangya Hospital (October 2013-March 2017). The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) preserved liver function; (2) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0; and (3) absence of macrovascular invasion or extrahepatic metastasis. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) absence of baseline CT imaging within 1 month prior to LRE; (2) poor CT image quality that hindered interpretation; (3) absence of baseline laboratory data within 1 week before LRE; (4) absence of pathological slides for histological staining; and (5) loss to follow-up. The flowchart of the study population is illustrated in Figure 1. This retrospective study was approved by the institutional review boards of the Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University and performed in strict accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Clinical Data Collection

Clinical data, including age, sex, Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage, liver cirrhosis status, and etiology of the underlying liver disease, were collected. Likewise, laboratory data, encompassing neutrophil, lymphocyte, monocyte, and platelet counts, as well as serum albumin, total bilirubin levels, alanine aminotransferase levels, aspartate aminotransferase levels, international normalized ratio (INR), creatinine levels, and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels, were collected. Patients were initially observed for 2–3 months following LRE and subsequently at least every 6 months.

CT Acquisition and Imaging Analysis

CT images were independently reviewed by two board-certified radiologists with 12 and 16 years of experience in abdominal imaging who were aware of the HCC diagnosis but blinded to patients’ clinical, laboratory, and histological data. The following imaging features of the largest tumor in each patient were recorded and qualitatively assessed: (a) Arterial Phase Hyperenhancement (APHE); (b) Absence of arterial phase enhancement; (c) Presence of satellite nodules; (d) Enhancing capsule; (e) Corona enhancement; (f) Delayed central enhancement; (g) Intratumoral hemorrhage; (h) Fat in mass; (i) Intratumoral artery; (j) Rim APHE; (k) Substantial intratumoral necrosis; (l) Dilated Intratumoral artery; (m) Mosaic architecture; (n) Nodule-in-nodule architecture; (o) Portal venous phase enhancement; (p) Delayed phase enhancement; (q) Washout during the portal venous phase, and (r) Washout during the equilibrium phase. Imaging features were defined according to the 2018 version of the Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS). Disagreement between the radiologists on imaging feature assessment was resolved by consensus. LI-RADS categories were also assigned.

Histological Examination

Two liver pathologists with 11 and 10 years of experience in liver pathology independently performed histological analyses and reached a consensus in cases of disagreement. HCC was diagnosed according to the 2019 WHO classification. The ITF grade was evaluated via Sirius red staining on a semiquantitative scoring scale from 0–3, corresponding to absent, mild, moderate, or severe, respectively; a score of ≥2 indicated high-grade ITF. Additionally, IHC staining for CD34 was performed on resected samples to determine microvascular density (MVD).

Early Recurrence Evaluation and Follow-Up

Early HCC recurrence was defined as the emergence of new lesions within two years following liver resection. To monitor for early recurrence, contrast-enhanced CT or MRI and serum AFP measurements were routinely conducted. Follow-up assessments were performed via telephone interviews (March 2024) or during the most recent hospital visit if telephone contact was not feasible. The primary clinical outcome, Disease-free survival (DFS), was defined as the interval from the date of diagnosis to tumor recurrence. The secondary outcome, overall survival (OS), was defined as the interval between the date of diagnosis to the date of the last follow-up or death from any cause. DFS and OS were calculated both before and after one-to-one propensity score matching (PSM).

Statistical Analysis

Categorical variables were assessed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Normally distributed continuous variables were reported as mean and SD and compared using independent t-tests, whilst non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range [IQR]) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U-test. Data integrity was strictly monitored, and patients with missing key variables were excluded during the recruitment phase; thus, the final dataset of 367 patients contained no missing values, and no imputation methods were applied.

Interreader agreement for the qualitative assessment of CT imaging features between the two radiologists was evaluated using Cohen’s kappa statistics. The level of agreement was interpreted based on kappa values as follows: 0.01–0.20, slight; 0.21–0.40, fair; 0.41–0.60, moderate; 0.61–0.80, substantial; and 0.81–1.00, almost perfect.

Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed to minimize the impact of potential selection bias. The propensity score was estimated using a multivariable logistic regression model, incorporating variables that demonstrated baseline imbalances (P<0.05) between the groups, specifically: liver cirrhosis, INR, portal hypertension, splenomegaly, APHE, intratumoral artery, dilated intratumoral artery and washout at equilibrium phase. Patients were matched at a 1:1 ratio using the nearest-neighbor algorithm without replacement, with a caliper width of 0.2 times the standard deviation of the logit of the propensity score. The balance of covariates post-matching was rigorously assessed using standardized mean differences (SMD) and P-values, with an SMD<0.25 and P>0.05 considered to indicate an acceptable balance. Following matching, early recurrence rates were compared using the χ2 test, while DFS and OS were analyzed via the Log rank test and visualized using Kaplan-Meier curves. These comparisons were conducted in both the pre-matching and post-matching cohorts.

Potential predictors were first screened using univariable Cox proportional hazards regression. Variables with a P-value <0.05 in the univariable analysis were subsequently entered into a multivariable Cox model, where a backward stepwise selection process was performed using the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) to identify the most independent predictors. The discriminatory capacity of this combined model for predicting early HCC recurrence was assessed using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis at 2 years. The 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the Area Under the Curve (AUC) were estimated using the iid representation method. Furthermore, statistical differences in discriminatory efficacy between the combined model and individual predictive markers were verified using the time-dependent AUC comparison test based on inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW).

Statistical analyses were performed using the R software (version 4.0.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing; http://www.R-project.org) and SPSS (version 26.0; IBM), with a two-sided value of P<0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

The prevalence of HBV infection in the study cohort was 74.9%, with BCLC stage A accounting for the majority at 91.3%. Potential Predictive Factors for Early HCC Recurrence

Early HCC recurrence was operationally defined as tumor reappearance within two years after liver resection. Among the study population, 198 individuals (54.0%) experienced recurrence within two years, whereas 169 (46.0%) remained recurrence-free. In the univariable Cox model, 18 variables demonstrated statistically significant associations with early HCC recurrence, with p-values ranging from <0.001 to 0.048 (Table 2). A backward stepwise multivariable Cox model, incorporating all 18 significant variables identified in the univariable analysis (P<0.05 threshold), identified seven independent predictors of early HCC recurrence, namely liver cirrhosis (HR: 1.93, 95% CI: 1.43–2.62, P<0.001), serum monocyte count (HR: 4.92, 95% CI: 2.03–11.91, P<0.001), AFP >200 ng/mL (HR: 1.62, 95% CI: 1.22–2.16, P<0.001), substantial intratumoral necrosis (HR: 3.18, 95% CI: 1.87–5.42, P<0.001), presence of intratumoral artery (HR: 1.80, 95% CI: 1.33–2.44, P<0.001), nodule-in-nodule architecture (HR: 2.39, 95% CI: 1.14–4.99, P=0.021), and ITF (HR: 2.73, 95% CI: 2.03–3.68, P <0.001). Impact of Low and High ITF on DFS and OS

To mitigate potential selection bias caused by confounding variables, this study employed PSM with a 1:1 nearest-neighbor algorithm using a caliper width of 0.2 standard deviations. This process successfully generated 106 matched patient pairs with balanced baseline characteristics (all covariates P>0.05 and SMD<0.25; Table 1). Survival analysis demonstrated that in the pre-matching cohort, the high-ITF group had a significantly shorter median DFS compared to the low-ITF group (9.60 months [95% CI: 7.92–14.63] vs. not reached [NR; 95% CI: 19.63-NR]; P<0.001; Figure 6A). This significant difference persisted after matching, with a median DFS of 9.96 months (95% CI: 8.25–15.62) in the high-ITF group compared to NR (95% CI: 17.72-NR) in the low-ITF group (P<0.001; Figure 6B). Regarding overall survival, the pre-PSM median OS was significantly shorter in the high-ITF group (29.20 months [95% CI: 20.80–41.00] vs. NR [95% CI: 81.80-NR]; P<0.001; Figure 6C), and this disparity remained statistically significant post-matching (29.20 months [95% CI: 21.90–41.00] vs. NR [95% CI: 28.90-NR]; P=0.023; Figure 6D). Discussion

In our study, we evaluated the prognostic significance of ITF in patients with HCC undergoing LRE. Our comprehensive findings indicate that ITF is significantly correlated with unfavorable clinical outcomes and may serve as a critical histological parameter for risk stratification.

The impact of ITF on cancer is context-dependent, exhibiting both inhibitory and supportive effects.32 During early oncogenesis, the baseline stromal response can act as a host defense mechanism. Fibroblasts and pathways like Hedgehog signaling induce a restraining form of stromal hyperplasia that mechanically limits cancer cell invasion and maintains tissue integrity. However, as the disease progresses, tumor cells “educate” these normal stromal cells to adopt a cancer-supportive phenotype, characterized by the accumulation of CAFs. Through this stromal reprogramming, fibrosis facilitates tumor progression via mechanisms such as ECM stiffening.32 Consistent with this tumor-promoting role in established tumors, our survival analysis demonstrated that elevated ITF scores were significantly correlated with reduced DFS and OS. Notably, this prognostic link stayed strong before and after PSM, clinically confirming that ITF is a reliable sign of poor prognosis.

In addition to its independent prognostic value, we assessed the clinical utility of ITF for risk stratification. Our results showed that combining ITF with standard clinicopathological and imaging features improved the prediction of early recurrence. Traditional staging systems rely primarily on tumor size and nodule number. Consequently, they often miss the biological variations within the tumor microenvironment.33,34 As a quantitative marker of stromal aggressiveness, ITF provides this missing biological context. The improved accuracy of our combined model highlights the practical value of routine ITF evaluation. This approach could help clinicians better identify high-risk patients who require closer postoperative monitoring or adjuvant therapies.

The detrimental clinical impact of ITF is primarily driven by the aberrant accumulation of collagen produced by CAFs.19 Elevated ITF often reflects the activation of WNT/β-catenin and TGF-β signaling pathways, which mediate extensive ECM remodeling.31 This dense collagen matrix exerts several pro-tumorigenic effects. Physically, it establishes a barrier that hinders immune cell infiltration and suppresses cytotoxic activity.26,35 Mechanically, increased collagen cross-linking stiffens the ECM, activating integrin-mediated focal adhesions and triggering tumor cell invasion.36 Concurrently, this mechanical stress can activate YAP/TAZ signaling to upregulate immune checkpoints, further fostering an immunosuppressive microenvironment.37,38

Crucially, this fibrotic remodeling is intimately linked to intratumoral vascularization. Specifically, our evaluation revealed that MVD was significantly higher in the high-ITF group compared to the low-ITF group, indicating intensified tumor angiogenesis. This clinical finding clearly supports the concept that ITF serves as a primary driver of neovascularization. Biologically, the ITF supports new vessel formation and enhances pro-angiogenic pathways, including HIF-1α stabilization and VEGFA expression, to promote endothelial proliferation.39–42

More importantly, this ITF-driven vascular network provides a direct histological explanation for the early recurrence and reduced survival rates observed in our cohort. Tumor-induced blood vessels within fibrotic areas are inherently abnormal and highly permeable.43,44 Leaky blood vessels and aligned collagen fibers form channels that aid tumor cells in entering the bloodstream and spreading.45,46 Furthermore, this abnormal angiogenesis promotes an invasive, immunosuppressive environment by triggering epithelial-mesenchymal transition and increasing inhibitory checkpoints on endothelial cells.47–49 Therefore, our findings indicate that ITF not only coexists with tumor vessels but also actively contributes to creating a dysfunctional vascular network, leading to early metastasis and poor clinical outcomes in HCC.

Nevertheless, several limitations of this study merit acknowledgment. Firstly, the small sample size and retrospective nature of this cohort may have introduced selection bias and compromised the generalizability of the findings. Validation in prospective multi-center cohorts is essential to validate the clinical utility of ITF for risk stratification. Secondly, the mechanistic link between ITF and vascularization remains elusive. Future studies should examine ITF-induced vascular remodeling, particularly the heterogeneity of CAF distribution, underlying signaling pathways, and associated immune microenvironment alterations. Lastly, this study was conducted in a region where hepatitis B is endemic, whereas hepatitis B is not the primary cause of HCC in Europe or America.

In summary, the findings of this study position ITF as an independent risk factor for early tumor recurrence and poor prognosis in HCC patients undergoing LRE. Thus, incorporating ITF as a biomarker may be practical in the clinical setting, enabling the development of more evidence-based and personalized treatment strategies.
