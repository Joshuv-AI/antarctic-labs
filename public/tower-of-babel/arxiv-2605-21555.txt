1.
Introduction

The study of truncated Toeplitz operators (TTOs in short) has its origins in the seminal paper of Sarason (
[
20
]
, 2007), who introduced a systematic framework for these operators and developed many of their fundamental properties. Given an inner function
\theta
, the corresponding model space
K_{\theta}=H^{2}\ominus\theta H^{2}
serves as the natural domain for these operators, where
H^{2}
denotes the Hardy space over the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, and they are defined as the compression of the Laurent operators acting on the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
to the model space
K_{\theta}
.
Sarason’s work stimulated considerable interest in the operator-theoretic community, leading to rapid developments in comprehending the algebraic and spectral properties of TTOs. A central theme in Sarason’s work is characterizing when TTOs belong to specific operator classes. Cima and Garcia
[
6
]
studied the structure of these operators in connection with complex symmetric operators, demonstrating that every TTO is complex symmetric with respect to a canonical conjugation on
K_{\theta}
. They also investigated their properties on the finite dimensional model spaces
[
7
]
. Later, several authors investigated these operators. We mention a few relevant works:
[
2
,
1
,
13
,
22
]
including the recent survey
[
5
]
and the references therein. Sedlock
[
21
]
subsequently established a complete characterization of idempotent TTOs while investigating the problem of when the product of two truncated Toeplitz operators is a truncated Toeplitz operator. More precisely, he introduced the so-called Sedlock classes which form maximal abelian subalgebras within the collection of bounded TTOs and provide a powerful framework for studying algebraic properties of TTOs. Alongside TTOs, the dual setting corresponding to orthogonal complements of model spaces gave rise to dual truncated Toeplitz operators (DTTOs in short). Ding and Sang
[
10
]
introduced these operators and established their basic structural properties along with the zero product problem of two DTTOs. These operators are the compression of the Laurent operators acting on the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
to the orthogonal complement of the model space, that is,
K_{\theta}^{\perp}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\ominus K_{\theta}
.
Later development includes the Brown-Halmos type theorems for and factorization results for DTTOs, characterizations and structural theory, essential commutation and related spectral problems (see
[
14
,
16
,
19
]
).

Among the various operator classes arising in Hilbert space operator theory, partial isometries play a crucial role. They naturally appear in the study of shift operators, polar decompositions, operator models, and
C^{*}
-algebras. Consequently, determining when a truncated or dual truncated Toeplitz operator is a partial isometry becomes an interesting and natural problem. The concrete structure of partially isometric Toeplitz operators were given in the classical Brown and Douglas
[
4
]
classification of partially isometric Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}
. Later, Deepak, Pradhan, and Sarkar
[
9
]
established the Brown-Douglas result for partially isometric Toeplitz operators over the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Recently, Debnath, Pradhan, and Sarkar
[
8
]
characterized the partially isometric truncated Toeplitz operatoprs with inner symbols on model spaces over the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. But the characterization of partial isometric truncated Toeplitz operators for any bounded symbols is still not known. In this paper, we aim to characterize partially isometric dual truncated Toeplitz operators for some specific symbols which will surely enlarge the earlier classes. More precisely, we give characterization results for truncated and dual truncated Toeplitz operators with symbols of the form
\bar{u}v
, where
u
and
v
are inner functions such that
v
divides
\theta
.

The structure of the rest of the paper is as follows: In Section
2
, we give some preliminary notations and definitions. In Section
3
, we provide the characterization of dual truncated Toeplitz operators (and truncated Toeplitz operators) for some specific symbols. Section
4
focuses on some interesting facts about the sets of extremal vectors for the non-zero partially isometric truncated and dual truncated Toeplitz operators, which are of independent interest. We conclude with some remarks in Section 5.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the space of all bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
. For
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, let
\mathcal{N}(T)
and
\mathcal{R}(T)
denote the null space and range space of
T
, respectively. An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be a partial isometry if
\|Th\|=\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{N}(T)^{\perp}
. The following characterization of a partial isometry are well-known to the literature (see
[
11
, Proposition 4.38]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

T
is a partial isometry.

(2)

TT^{*}T=T
.

(3)

T^{*}TT^{*}=T^{*}
.

(4)

TT^{*}
is a projection.

(5)

T^{*}T
is a projection.

(6)

T^{*}
is a partial isometry.

Let
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{T}
be the open unit disc and the unit circle in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, respectively. Let
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
be the space of all square integrable functions with respect to normalised Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
denote the Banach algebra of all essentially bounded functions on
\mathbb{T}
. The
Hardy-Hilbert space
, denoted by

, consists of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
with square summable Taylor coefficients at the origin. Let
H^{\infty}
denote the space of all bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
.
We often identify
H^{2}
as a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, and write
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})=H^{2}\oplus{H^{2}}^{\perp}
. For any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, the Laurent operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, denoted by
M_{\varphi}
, is defined as

M_{\varphi}(f)=\varphi f\quad(f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})),

and the
Toeplitz operator

T_{\varphi}:{H}^{2}\rightarrow{H}^{2}
with symbol
\varphi
is defined as

T_{\varphi}f=P(\varphi f)\quad(f\in{H}^{2}),

where
P
is the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
onto
{H}^{2}{}
. The
Hankel operator

H_{\varphi}:{H}^{2}\rightarrow{H}^{2}{}^{\perp}
is defined by

H_{\varphi}f=(I-P)(\varphi f)\quad(f\in{H}^{2}).

Given a non-constant inner function
\theta
, the
model space

K_{\theta}
is defined as
K_{\theta}={H}^{2}\ominus\theta{H}^{2}
and clearly, it is
T_{z}^{*}
-invariant. For any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, the
truncated Toeplitz operator
, first introduced by Sarason
[
20
]
, is defined by

A_{\varphi}f=P_{\theta}(\varphi f),\quad(f\in K_{\theta}),

where
P_{\theta}
is the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
onto
K_{\theta}
. It is easy to see that
\|A_{\varphi}\|\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
. If
\varphi(z)=z
, then
A_{\varphi}
is called the
compressed shift
, denoted by
S_{\theta}
. For any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
the
dual truncated Toeplitz operator
, denoted by
D_{\varphi}
, is defined on
K_{\theta}^{\perp}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\ominus K_{\theta}
as

{D_{\varphi}}f=Q_{\theta}(\varphi f)\quad(f\in K_{\theta}^{\perp}),

where
Q_{\theta}
is the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
onto
K_{\theta}^{\perp}
.
Clearly,

Q_{\theta}=I-P_{\theta}=I-P+M_{\theta}PM_{\overline{\theta}}.

In the special case
\varphi(z)=z
,
D_{\varphi}
is referred to as
dual of the compressed shift
S_{\theta}
, denoted as
D_{\theta}
. Also, for
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, the
truncated Hankel operator

B_{\varphi}:K_{\theta}\rightarrow K_{\theta}^{\perp}
and the
dual truncated Hankel operator

C_{\varphi}:K_{\theta}^{\perp}\rightarrow K_{\theta}
are defined as

B_{\varphi}f=Q_{\theta}(\varphi f),\quad(f\in K_{\theta})

and

C_{\varphi}f=P_{\theta}(\varphi f),\quad(f\in K_{\theta}^{\perp}),

respectively. It is a routine check that
A_{\varphi}^{*}=A_{\bar{\varphi}}
,
D_{\varphi}^{*}=D_{\bar{\varphi}}
, and
B_{\varphi}^{*}=C_{\bar{\varphi}}
. Thus, the multiplication operator
M_{\varphi}
has the following block matrix representation with respect to the decomposition
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})=K_{\theta}\oplus K_{\theta}^{\perp}
:

M_{\varphi}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{\varphi}&C_{\varphi}\\
B_{\varphi}&D_{\varphi}\end{bmatrix}.

Now for
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, the relation
M_{\varphi}M_{\psi}=M_{\varphi\psi}
yields the following identities (see
[
10
]
):

(2.1)

\displaystyle A_{\varphi\psi}-A_{\varphi}A_{\psi}

\displaystyle=B_{\bar{\varphi}}^{*}B_{\psi}

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{\varphi\psi}-D_{\varphi}B_{\psi}

\displaystyle=B_{\varphi}A_{\psi}

(2.3)

\displaystyle D_{\varphi\psi}-D_{\varphi}D_{\psi}

\displaystyle=B_{\varphi}B_{\bar{\psi}}^{*}.

A
conjugation
on a complex Hilbert space is an antilinear isometric involution. In what follows, the operator
C:L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
defined by

Cf=\theta\overline{zf},\quad(f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))

is a conjugation which bijectively maps
\theta{H}^{2}
to
{H}^{2}{}^{\perp}
and
K_{\theta}
to itself
[
12
]
. We will write
\tilde{f}
for
Cf
for the sake of brevity. Note that the TTOs and DTTOs are
C
-symmetric, that is

A_{\varphi}^{*}=CA_{\varphi}C\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\varphi}^{*}=CD_{\varphi}C.

In the following theorem, we recall some basic properties of
A_{\varphi},D_{\varphi},
and
B_{\varphi}
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
10
]
,
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. The following statements hold:

(1)

D_{\varphi}
is a bounded operator and
\|D_{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
.

(2)

A_{\varphi}=0
if and only if
\varphi=\theta{H}^{2}+\overline{\theta{H}^{2}}
.

(3)

D_{\varphi}
is compact if and only if
\varphi=0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.

(4)

B_{\varphi}=0
if and only if
\varphi
is constant.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, the Sedlock class
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}^{\theta}
is the set of such bounded TTOs which have a symbol of the form
\varphi+\overline{\alpha S_{\theta}\tilde{\varphi}}+c
, where
\varphi\in K_{\theta}
and

. While
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}^{\theta}
is the set of TTOs with co-analytic symbols. The set of all TTOs on

forms a weakly closed subspace of

that we will denote by

. We also use the following results related to the product of TTOs given by Sedlock in
[

to prove our main results.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

such that
A_{\varphi}A_{\psi}\in\mathscr{T}_{\theta}
. If one of the operators in the product is in

Let

and

3.
Partially isometric dual truncated Toeplitz operators

In this section we obtain the characterization of partially isometric DTTO (hence TTO)
for the symbol of the form
\bar{u}v
, where
u,v
are inner functions and
v
divides

\theta
.

Before proceeding to the characterization result, we first show the equivalent relation of TTO, DTTO and truncated Hankel operator.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
with
|\varphi|=1
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry.

(2)

B_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry.

(3)

D_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry.

Proof.

Suppose that
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
with
|\varphi|=1
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Now the relations in (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) give

\displaystyle I_{K_{\theta}}-A_{\varphi}^{*}A_{\varphi}=A_{\bar{\varphi}\varphi}-A_{\bar{\varphi}}A_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=B_{\varphi}^{*}B_{\varphi}

\displaystyle I_{K_{\theta}^{\perp}}-D_{\varphi}D_{\varphi}^{*}=D_{\varphi\bar{\varphi}}-D_{\varphi}D_{\bar{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=B_{\varphi}B_{\varphi}^{*}.

It now follows from Lemma
2.1
that
A_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry if and only it
B_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry if and only if
D_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry.

The partially isometric TTOs with inner symbols were recently characterized by Debnath, Pradhan and Sarkar in
[
8
]
. Indeed, they proved the following result:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u
and
\theta
be non-constant inner functions in
H^{\infty}
. Then
A_{u}
on
K_{\theta}
is a partial isometry if and only if
u
divides
\theta
or
\theta
divides
u
.

However, the following theorem provides a new proof of this characterization by employing dual truncated Toeplitz operators. Our approach is more algebraic in nature and uses the structural properties of DTTOs. It also reveals an interesting interplay between TTOs and DTTOs.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\theta
be a non-constant inner function and
D_{u}
be the dual TTO on
K_{\theta}^{\perp}
corresponding to the inner function
u
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

D_{u}
is a partial isometry.

(2)

u
divides
\theta
or
\theta
divides
u
.

Proof.

Note that
K_{\theta}^{\perp}=\theta H^{2}\oplus{H^{2}}^{\perp}
. Now for any
h\in H^{2}

we have

\displaystyle D_{u}^{*}D_{u}D_{u}^{*}(\theta h)

\displaystyle=D_{u}^{*}D_{u}(M_{\theta}PM_{\bar{\theta}}(\bar{u}\theta h)+(I-P)(\bar{u}\theta h))

\displaystyle=D_{u}^{*}D_{u}(M_{\theta}P(\bar{u}h)+(I-P)(\bar{u}\theta h)

\displaystyle=D_{u}^{*}D_{u}(\theta T_{u}^{*}h+\bar{u}\theta h-P(\bar{u}\theta h))

\displaystyle=D_{u}^{*}(M_{\theta}PM_{\bar{\theta}}(u\theta T_{u}^{*}h+\theta h-uP(\bar{u}\theta h))+(I-P)(u\theta T_{u}^{*}h+\theta h-uP(\bar{u}\theta h))

\displaystyle=D_{u}^{*}(M_{\theta}P(uT_{u}^{*}h+h-u\bar{\theta}P(\bar{u}\theta h)))

\displaystyle=D_{u}^{*}(u\theta T_{u}^{*}h+\theta h-\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h)

\displaystyle=M_{\theta}PM_{\bar{\theta}}(\theta T_{u}^{*}h+\bar{u}\theta h-\bar{u}\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h)+(I-P)(\theta T_{u}^{*}h+\bar{u}\theta h-\bar{u}\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h)

\displaystyle=\theta T_{u}^{*}h+\theta T_{u}^{*}h-\theta T_{u}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h+(I-P)(\bar{u}\theta h-\bar{u}\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h)

\displaystyle=\theta T_{u}^{*}h+(I-P)(\bar{u}\theta h-\bar{u}\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h).

Also, for
g\in H^{2}
we have

D_{u}^{*}D_{u}D_{u}^{*}(\overline{zg})=D_{u}^{*}D_{u}Q_{\theta}(\overline{zug})=D_{u}^{*}D_{u}(\overline{zug})=D_{u}^{*}Q_{\theta}(\overline{zg})=D_{u}^{*}(\overline{zg}).

Thus,
D_{u}
is a partial isometry if and only if
D_{u}^{*}D_{u}D_{u}^{*}f=D_{u}^{*}f
for all
f\in K_{\theta}^{\perp}
if and only if
D_{u}^{*}D_{u}D_{u}^{*}(\theta h)=D_{u}^{*}(\theta h)
for all
h\in H^{2}
.
That is,

\theta T_{u}^{*}h+(I-P)(\bar{u}\theta h-\bar{u}\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h)=\theta T_{u}^{*}h+(I-P)(\bar{u}\theta h)\quad(h\in H^{2}).

Therefore, for all
h\in H^{2}

\displaystyle(I-P)(\bar{u}\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\bar{u}\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h

\displaystyle=P(\bar{u}\theta T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h)

\displaystyle T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h

\displaystyle=u\bar{\theta}T_{\bar{u}\theta}T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h

\displaystyle T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h

\displaystyle=T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}h=(T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta})^{2}h.

Hence
T_{\bar{u}\theta}^{*}T_{\bar{u}\theta}
is a projection operator on
H^{2}
. Consequently, by Lemma
2.1
,
T_{\bar{u}\theta}
is a partial isometry on
H^{2}
. Therefore, it follows from Brown-Douglas
[
4
]
characterization of partially isometric Toeplitz operators that either
\bar{u}\theta
or
\bar{\theta}u
is inner function. That is, either
u|\theta
or
\theta|u
.
This completes the proof.

Then considering the corresponding TTO in (
3.2
) we get
\bar{\beta}A_{\psi}=0
. Consequently,
\beta=0
. Now from the equation (
3.1
), we get
u=w+\theta^{2}h_{2}
. Again considering the corresponding TTO, we have
A_{u}=A_{w}
. Since
w|\theta
,
A_{w}
is a partial isometry and hence
A_{u}
is so. Also
A_{u}
is non-zero, thus
u|\theta
.

Conversely, if
\theta^{2}|u-c
for some constant
c
, then
A_{\bar{u}\theta}=0
and hence a partial isometry. If
u|\theta
, then
\bar{u}\theta
is inner and also divides

\theta
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.3
,
D_{\bar{u}\theta}
is a partial isometry.
This completes the proof.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
A_{\bar{u}\theta}
is a non-zero partial isometry, then
\bar{u}\theta
is either analytic or co-analytic.

Recall that if
u
and
v
are inner functions, then
K_{u}\cap K_{v}=\{0\}
if and only if
gcd(u,v)=1
(cf.
[
12
]
). Thus we get the following corollary:

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\theta
and
u
be inner functions such that
gcd(u,\theta)=1
. Then
D_{\bar{u}\theta}
is a partial isometry if and only if
A_{\bar{u}\theta}=0
.

Next we will characterize partially isometric DTTO with symbol
\bar{u}v
, where
v
divides
\theta
. Note that while considering the symbols of the form
\bar{u}v
, where
u
and
v
are arbitrary inner functions, we can assume that
gcd(u,v)=1
until it does not hamper any given condition.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
u,v
and
\theta
be inner functions with
gcd(u,v)=1
such that
v
divides

\theta
. If
A_{\bar{u}v}=0
, then either
\theta=cv
where
c
is a unimodular constant or
v
is a unimodular constant.

Proof.

Note that
A_{\bar{u}v}=0
if and only if there exists
h_{1},h_{2}\in H^{2}

such that
\bar{u}v=\theta h_{1}+\overline{\theta h_{2}}
. Thus

(3.3)

\displaystyle\bar{u}=\bar{v}\theta h_{1}+\overline{v\theta h_{2}},

which implies that
\bar{v}\theta h_{1}\in\overline{H^{2}}
. But since
\bar{v}\theta
is inner,
\bar{v}\theta h_{1}
must be constant, and hence
h_{1}
is constant. Now if
h_{1}

is non-zero, then
\theta=cv
, where
c
is a unimodular constant. If
h_{1}=0
, then the equation
(\ref{div})
implies that
u=v\theta h_{2}
, i.e.,
v
divides
u
. Since
gcd(u,v)=1
,
v
must be a unimodular constant.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\theta
be a non-constant inner function and
\varphi=\bar{u}v
not co-analytic, where
u
and
v
are inner functions such that
gcd(u,v)=1
. Suppose
v
is non-constant and divides
\theta
non-trivially, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

D_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry.

(2)

u
is a unimodular constant.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2):

Assume that
D_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry, where
\varphi=\bar{u}v
and also
v
divides
\theta
. Consider the corresponding truncated Toeplitz operator
A_{\varphi}
on the model space
K_{\theta}
. Then
A_{\varphi}
is also a partial isometry by Lemma
3.1
. If
A_{\bar{u}v}=0
, then by Lemma
3.9
and given assumption,
v
is a unimodular constant. In that case
\varphi=\bar{u}
which is co-analytic.

On the other hand, if
A_{\bar{u}v}\neq 0
, then there exists
0\neq f\in\mathcal{N}(A_{\bar{u}v})^{\perp}
such that
\|A_{\bar{u}v}f\|=\|f\|
.
This yields
\bar{u}vf\in K_{\theta}
. Therefore,

P(\bar{u}v\bar{\theta}f)=T_{\theta}^{*}(\bar{u}vf)=0.

Thus

A_{\bar{u}v\bar{\theta}}f=P_{\theta}(\bar{u}v\bar{\theta}f)=P_{\theta}P(\bar{u}v\bar{\theta}f)=0,

and hence
\mathcal{N}(A_{\bar{u}v})^{\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(A_{\bar{u}v\bar{\theta}})
. Consequently,

\mathcal{R}(A_{u\bar{v}\theta})\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{R}(A_{u\bar{v}\theta})}=\mathcal{N}(A_{\bar{u}v\bar{\theta}})^{\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{N}(A_{\bar{u}v}),

which implies

A_{\varphi}A_{u\bar{v}\theta}=A_{\bar{u}v}A_{u\bar{v}\theta}=0.

Now
A_{u\bar{v}\theta}
is of type
0
. Then, by Lemma
2.3
, either
A_{u\bar{v}\theta}=cI
for some constant
c
or
A_{\varphi}
is of type
0
. If
A_{u\bar{v}\theta}=cI
, clearly
A_{\varphi}=0
which is not the case.

Also, if
A_{u\bar{v}\theta}=0
, then it is easy to check that
v
divides
u
but since
gcd(u,v)
is constant,
v
must be constant. Thus
A_{\varphi}
is of type
0
. It follows that there exists
\psi\in H^{\infty}
such that
A_{\varphi}=A_{\psi}
and
\|\psi\|=\|A_{\varphi}\|=1
. Again
A_{\varphi}
is a non-zero partial isometry, so is
A_{\psi}
. Then
\psi|\theta
. Consequently, there exist
h_{1},h_{2}\in{H}^{2}
such that

\bar{u}v=\psi+\theta h_{1}+\overline{\theta h_{2}}.

Therefore,

u\bar{v}\theta=\bar{\psi}{\theta}+\overline{h_{1}}+\theta^{2}h_{2},

which implies
h_{1}=0
. Then

A_{u\bar{v}\theta}=A_{\bar{\psi}{\theta}}.

Note that
\theta=(\bar{\psi}{\theta})\psi
. Thus,
A_{\bar{\psi}{\theta}}
is a partial isometry and so is
A_{u\bar{v}\theta}
. Hence, by Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
,
u\bar{v}\theta|\theta
, i.e.,
\theta=u\bar{v}\theta w
for some inner function
w
.
It follows that
v=uw
and hence
u|v
. Now
gcd(u,v)=1
infers that u is a unimodular constant.

The converse part follows trivially from Theorem
3.3
.

4.
Extremal vectors

In this section, we will examine the structure of initial spaces of partially isometric TTOs and DTTOs. We will be needed the following definition for truncated Hankel operator given in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
f\in K_{\theta}
,

B_{\varphi}f=H_{\varphi}H_{\bar{\theta}}^{*}H_{\bar{\theta}}f+M_{\theta}H_{\bar{\varphi}}^{*}H_{\bar{\theta}}f.

We will start with the initial space of
A_{\bar{u}v}
on
K_{\theta}
, where
u,v
are inner functions.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
u
and
v
be non-constant inner functions such that
A_{\bar{u}v}
is a non-zero partial isometry on
K_{\theta}
. Then

\mathcal{N}(A_{\bar{u}v})^{\perp}=\{f\in K_{\theta}\cap u\,\overline{gcd(u,v)}{H}^{2}:vf\in K_{u\theta}\}.

Proof.

Note that for
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, if
A_{\varphi}
is a partial isometry, then

\mathcal{N}(A_{\varphi})^{\perp}=\mathcal{N}(B_{\varphi})
. So, we will find the structure of
\mathcal{N}(B_{\bar{u}v})
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{N}(B_{\bar{u}v})
. Then

H_{\bar{u}v}H_{\bar{\theta}}^{*}H_{\bar{\theta}}f+M_{\theta}H_{u\bar{v}}^{*}H_{\bar{\theta}}f=B_{\bar{u}v}f=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle H_{\bar{u}v}H_{\bar{\theta}}^{*}H_{\bar{\theta}}f+M_{\theta}H_{u\bar{v}}^{*}H_{\bar{\theta}}f=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\,\,

\displaystyle H_{\bar{u}v}H_{\bar{\theta}}^{*}H_{\bar{\theta}}f=0\text{ and }M_{\theta}H_{u\bar{v}}^{*}H_{\bar{\theta}}f=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\,\,

\displaystyle H_{\bar{u}v}(I-T_{\theta}T_{\theta}^{*})f\text{ and }(T_{\bar{u}v\bar{\theta}}-T_{\bar{u}v}T_{\theta}^{*})f=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\,\,

\displaystyle H_{\bar{u}v}f=0\text{ and }T_{\bar{u}v\bar{\theta}}f=0,

where the last implication holds as
f\in K_{\theta}
. Let
gcd(u,v)=w
with
u=ww_{1}
and
v=ww_{2}
. Then
\bar{u}v=\bar{w_{1}}w_{2}
and
gcd(w_{1},w_{2})=1
. Now
H_{\bar{u}v}f=0
implies
H_{\bar{w_{1}}w_{2}}f=0
. Thus

f\in w_{1}{H}^{2}=uw{H}^{2}.

This completes the proof.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
u
and
v
be non-constant inner functions with
gcd(u,v)=1
such that
A_{\bar{u}v}
is a non-zero partial isometry on
K_{\theta}
. Then

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(A_{\bar{u}v})^{\perp}=\{f\in K_{\theta}\cap u{H}^{2}:vf\in K_{u\theta}\}.

Remark 4.4
.

For
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, define

\mathcal{E}_{T}=\{h\in\mathcal{H}:\|Th\|=\|T\|\|h\|\},

that is,
\mathcal{E}_{T}
is the set of all such points on which
T
attains its norm, called the set of extremal vectors. From the above proof, for a non-zero partially isometric
TTO
A_{\bar{u}v}
on
K_{\theta}
,

\mathcal{E}_{A_{\bar{u}v}}=\{f\in K_{\theta}\cap u\overline{gcd(u,v)}{H}^{2}:vf\in K_{u\theta}\}.

Next we will discuss about the initial space of
D_{\bar{u}v}
whenever
A_{\bar{u}v}
is a non-zero partial isometry. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
gcd(u,v)=1
.
Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(D_{\bar{u}v})^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\{f\in K_{\theta}^{\perp}:\|D_{\bar{u}v}f\|=\|f\|\}

\displaystyle=\{f\in K_{\theta}^{\perp}:\|Q_{\theta}(\bar{u}vf)\|=\|f\|\}

\displaystyle=\{f\in K_{\theta}^{\perp}:\bar{u}vf\in K_{\theta}^{\perp}\}.

Since
K_{\theta}^{\perp}=\theta{H}^{2}\oplus{H}^{2}{}^{\perp}
, we will define the following sets:

\mathcal{E}_{+}=\{\theta h\in\theta{H}^{2}:\|D_{\bar{u}v}(\theta h)\|=\|\theta h\|\},

and

\mathcal{E}_{-}=\{\overline{zg}\in{H}^{2}{}^{\perp}:\|D_{\bar{u}v}(\overline{zg})\|=\|\overline{zg}\|\}.

It is easy to verify that
\theta u{H}^{2}\subset\mathcal{E}_{+}
and
\overline{zv{H}^{2}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{-}
. Thus

\theta u{H}^{2}\oplus\overline{zv{H}^{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}v}}.

We claim that if
A_{\bar{u}v}
is a non-zero partial isometry (so is
D_{\bar{u}v}
), then
\mathcal{E}_{+}
and
\mathcal{E}_{-}
are subspaces and also

\mathcal{E}_{+}=\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}v}}\cap\theta{H}^{2}=\theta u{H}^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{E}_{-}=\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}v}}\cap{H}^{2}{}^{\perp}=\overline{zv{H}^{2}}.

First observe that if
A_{\bar{u}v}
is non-zero partial isometry, then (
4.1
) implies that
K_{\theta}\cap u{H}^{2}\neq\{0\}
and hence
\mathcal{N}(T_{\bar{\theta}u})\neq\{0\}
(see
[
23
]
). By Coburn’s alternative,
\mathcal{N}(T_{\bar{u}\theta})=\{0\}
, that is,
K_{u}\cap\theta{H}^{2}=\{0\}
. Now assume that
\theta h\in\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}v}}\cap\theta{H}^{2}=\mathcal{E}_{+}
. Then
\|D_{\bar{u}v}(\theta h)\|=\|\theta h\|
which implies
\bar{u}v\theta h\in K_{\theta}^{\perp}
. Thus there exist
h_{1},h_{2}\in{H}^{2}
such that

\bar{u}v\theta h=\theta h_{1}+\overline{zh_{2}}.

Therefore,

(4.2)

\displaystyle v\theta h=u\theta h_{1}+u\overline{zh_{2}}.

Since
v\theta h,u\theta h_{1}\in{H}^{2}
, so does
u\overline{zh_{2}}
. Hence,
u\overline{zh_{2}}\in K_{u}\cap\theta{H}^{2}=\{0\}
. Consequently,
h_{2}=0
and from (
4.2
),

\bar{u}vh=h_{1}\in{H}^{2}.

Let
h=h^{i}h^{o}
is the inner-outer factorization of
h\in{H}^{2}
. Thus,

\bar{u}vh^{i}h^{o}\in{H}^{2}
, and hence, by Lemma
3.4
,
\bar{u}vh^{i}
is inner, that is,
u
divides
vh^{i}
. Now
gcd(u,v)=1
yields
u
divides
h^{i}
. Thus we get
h\in u{H}^{2}
. It proves the first claim.

For the second one, assume that
\overline{zg}\in\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}v}}\cap{H}^{2}{}^{\perp}
, then
\|D_{\bar{u}v}{(\overline{zg})}\|=\|\overline{zg}\|
. Since
D_{\bar{u}v}
is
C
-symmetric, we have

\displaystyle\|CD_{\bar{u}v}{(\overline{zg})}\|=\|C(\overline{zg})\|

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\|D_{\bar{v}u}C(\overline{zg})\|=\|C(\overline{zg})\|

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\|D_{\bar{v}u}{(\theta g)}\|=\|\theta g\|.

Since
D_{\bar{u}v}
is a partial isometry, so is
D_{\bar{v}u}
. Hence by above argument,
g\in v{H}^{2}
. This proves our claim.

We have shown that
\mathcal{E}_{+}=\theta uH^{2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{-}=\overline{zvh_{2}}
and

\mathcal{E}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{-}\subseteq\mathcal{E}.

We will prove the equality in some specific cases.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
A_{v}
be a non-zero partial isometry on
K_{\theta}
for some inner function
v
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{D_{v}}=\mathcal{E}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{-}
.

Proof.

Since
A_{v}
be a non-zero partial isometry, so is
D_{v}
and hence, by
3.3
, either
v|\theta
or
\theta|v
. If
\theta|v
, then
A_{v}=0
which is not the case. Thus,
v|\theta
, that is,
\bar{v}\theta
is inner. One can easily check that

\mathcal{E}_{+}=\theta{H}^{2}\quad\text{ and }\mathcal{E}_{-}=\overline{zv{H}^{2}}.

It is now enough to prove that

\mathcal{E}_{D_{v}}\subseteq\theta{H}^{2}\oplus\overline{zv{H}^{2}}.

Let
\theta h_{1}\oplus\overline{zh_{2}}\in\mathcal{E}_{D_{v}}
. Then there exist
g_{1},g_{2}\in{H}^{2}
such that

\displaystyle v(\theta h_{1}+\overline{zh_{2}})=\theta g_{1}+\overline{zg_{2}},

and hence

\displaystyle\theta h_{1}+\overline{zh_{2}}=\bar{v}\theta g_{1}+\overline{zvg_{2}}.

Since
h_{1},\bar{v}\theta g_{1}\in{H}^{2}
, we get
h_{2}=vg_{2}
. This completes the proof.

Remark 4.6
.

Under the assumptions of the previous theorem, using the fact that
D_{u}
is a
C
-symmetric operator, we get

\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}}}=\theta u{H}^{2}\oplus{H}^{2}{}^{\perp}.

Note that from Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
4.5
, we get the following result.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
u,v,\theta
be inner functions such that
\text{gcd}(u,v)=1
and
v
divides
\theta
. If
A_{\bar{u}v}
is a non-zero partial isometry, then
\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}v}}=\mathcal{E}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{-}
.

The following theorem is a more stronger result and it is true for any symbol of type

\bar{u}v
, where
u
and
v
are inner functions.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
A_{\bar{u}v}
be a non-zero partial isometry such that
\text{gcd}(u,v)=1
and
v
divides
\theta
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}v}}=\mathcal{E}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{-}
if and only if either
u
or
v
is a unimodular constant.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{E}_{D_{\bar{u}v}}=\mathcal{E}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{-}
. Then

\mathcal{N}(D_{\bar{u}v})^{\perp}=\theta u{H}^{2}\oplus\overline{zv{H}^{2}}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{N}(D_{\bar{u}v})=\theta K_{u}\oplus\overline{zK_{v}}.

Let
g\in K_{u}
and
h\in K_{v}
, then
\theta g,\overline{zh}\in\mathcal{N}(D_{\bar{u}v})
.
It follows that
\theta h=C(\overline{zh})\in\mathcal{N}(D_{\bar{v}u})
. Note that
\theta g\in\mathcal{N}(D_{\bar{u}v})
which implies
D_{\bar{u}v}(\theta g)=0
. Thus

\bar{u}v\theta g\in K_{\theta}\implies\ D_{\bar{u}v}(\theta g)=0.

Therefore,
\bar{u}v\theta g\in K_{\theta}
and hence

T_{u}^{*}T_{v}g=T_{\theta}^{*}T_{u}^{*}T_{v}T_{\theta}g=0.

On the similar lines, we can prove that
\theta h=C(\overline{zh})\in\mathcal{N}(D_{\bar{v}u})
which implies that
T_{v}^{*}T_{u}h=0
. But by Coburn’s alternative, either
T_{u}^{*}T_{v}
or
T_{v}^{*}T_{u}
is injective. Consequently, either
K_{u}=\{0\}
or
K_{v}=\{0\}
.

The converse part follows from Theorem
4.5
and Remark
4.6
.

5.
Concluding remarks

With the help of the given structure of initial spaces and set of extremal vectors, we may try to characterize partial isometric TTO and DTTO for any general symbol of the form
\bar{u}v
, where
u
and
v
are inner functions.
The results obtained in this paper provide a partial solution to the problem of characterizing partially isometric truncated Toeplitz operators and dual truncated Toeplitz operators associated with
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
-symbols. More precisely, we solved the problem for symbols of the form
\bar{u}v
, where
v
divides
\theta
.

An important remaining question is whether a complete characterization can be obtained for arbitrary symbols in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
(see
[
8
]
). In particular, the unimodular case appears to be especially significant. Since partially isometric TTO or DTTO with unimodular symbol can be expressed in the form
\bar{u}v
, where
u
and
v
are inner functions (see Lemma
3.5
). The problem essentially reduces to understanding the general
\bar{u}v
-symbol case without divisibility restrictions. The question remains open and seems to require new ideas beyond the methods developed here and will be carried out in the future project.
