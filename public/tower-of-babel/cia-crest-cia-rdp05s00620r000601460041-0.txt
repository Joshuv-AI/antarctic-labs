Trying to pick the right adjectives to describe those
who will be attending the American Academy of Achieve-
ment’s 17 th annual “gathering of the greats’’ in Owensboro,
. Ky., June 22-24 probably would send the most literate
communicator scrambling to the Thesaurus for help.

An “astounding array of extradordinary ‘exemplars of
excellence’ from the nation’s great fields of endeavor, and
abroad, will share a unique weekend in Owensboro,” it was
formally announced this weekend.

About 40 guests will be honored — 30 new recipients
and past honorees.

Among the new recipents, 24 were named today. They
include:

Robert 0. Anderson is board chairman and chief
stockholder of the $8-billion Atlantic Richfield Co. of Los
Angeles. His power and wealth are incredible. As a rancher
he has accumulated over one million acres of land; his
holdings are almost equal in size to the state of Delaware.

He’s described as an “an oil tycoon, land baron, mul-
timillionaire cattleman, newspaper owner, think-tank
sponsor.” Known as the “King of the Sunbelt,” Anderson is
New Mexico’s most famous citizen.

The credentials of Dr. Andrew V. Schally might not be
as flashy as Robert 0. Anderson’s, but they are no less
noteworthy. As senior medical investigator of the Veterans
Administration, and professor of medicine at Tulane
University, New Orleans, Dr. Schally is a co-recipient ol
the Lasker Award for basic medical research, the highest
honor bestowed in American medicine.

He also was a co-winner of the 1977 Nobel Prize in
recognition of research which helped to unravel the hor-

monal mysteries of the hypothalamus — the body’s master
gland that sits at the bottom of the brain. The studies may
explain how the mind affects physical and mental well-
being through hormones. ^

What kind of introduction does Carol Burnett need to
the American audience? She’s just one of the greatest stars
of comedy in the history of television, that’s all.

The Beverly Hills actress recently decided against
continuing with The Carol Burnett Show, which has been a
television staple for over a decade. Another highlight of her
career came when she starred in “Once Upon A Mattress”
on Broadway. After the run, superstardom was almost
certain.

The nation’s chief prosecutor and legal adviser to the
president is Griffin B. Bell, Attorney General of the United
States. He has spearheaded the Investigation into alleged
Korean influence buying in Congress, checked possible FBI
wrongdoings and monitoried the activities of the CIA.

He has been saluted by Time for “advancing a bold
idea ... (to) send the biggest anti-trust cases to Congress as
legislative matters rather than taking them to court.” He
was recently voted one of America’s most influential men
inlaw.

The name Kaiser Is famous in North American devel-
opment. And Edgar F. Kaiser Jr., president and chief
executive officer of Canada’s largest metallurgical coal
manufacturer — Kaiser Resources of Vancouver — is “the

Described as a maverick workaholic, Kaiser traveled
over 500,000 miles last year turning his company from one
