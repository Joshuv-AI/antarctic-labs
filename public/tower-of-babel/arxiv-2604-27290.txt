Boundedness of solutions in feedback systems with antithetic controllers
Moh Kamalul Wafi, Arthur C. B. de Oliveira, Eduardo D. Sontag

Abstract
This paper studies whether solutions of a class of nonlinear feedback systems remain bounded over time. The systems we consider arise naturally in synthetic biology, where the antithetic feedback controller regulates a biological process through a delayed feedback loop. Our main result is that every trajectory of such a system is bounded. The key insight is simple: if the regulated state grows too large for too long, the feedback loop will eventually respond and push it back down. More precisely, we show that whenever the state exceeds a threshold and remains there long enough, the feedback signal becomes strong enough to force the state to decrease. We then show that once this happens, the feedback remains strong enough to keep the state from growing unbounded. The proof works directly with differential inequalities and does not require constructing a Lyapunov function, making the mechanism transparent and easy to interpret. The boundedness result can be understood as a time-domain small-gain effect, where the delayed feedback ultimately counteracts any persistent growth in the system.

This paper was motivated by the study of the following four-dimensional nonlinear dynamical system, which represents the closed-loop interconnection of an “antithetic controller” (described by the variables

Since its original formulation by Briat, Gupta, and Khammash, the antithetic controller has attracted sustained attention in both theoretical and experimental systems biology.
It is well known from linear systems theory, as well as from several classes of nonlinear systems, that integral feedback, or more generally the presence of an “internal model” of admissible exogenous signals, is essential for robust regulation. This principle also plays a central role in biological processes at both the cellular and organismal levels

It is easy to show that the closed-loop system (

The key feature of the system is that the damping term

Rather than relying on Lyapunov functions, the argument works directly with
differential inequalities and comparison principles, tracing lower bounds
stage by stage through the cascade. The boundedness mechanism admits a
natural interpretation as a time-domain small-gain effect, in which forward
propagation through the cascade is eventually counteracted by delayed
negative feedback, offering clear insight into how the feedback delay
influences the overall system behavior.

Understanding boundedness and stability properties of nonlinear feedback systems is a fundamental problem in control theory. In many applications, the feedback mechanism that regulates a system does not act instantaneously, but is mediated through intermediate dynamics that introduce delays and attenuation. As a result, classical Lyapunov-based approaches may be
difficult to apply directly, especially when the feedback enters the system in a nonlinear or multiplicative form. We believe that the techniques introduced here may be helpful in the analysis of many such systems.

The vector field

x_{1}(t)<L+\alpha_{1}T_{0}

for all

\dot{x}_{1}(t_{0})\geq 0

Since

,

∎

Proposition 1
.

For the system presented in (

Proposition 2
.

There exist constants

x_{2}
,
x_{3}
, and

Since

\dot{x}_{2}(t)\leq\alpha_{3}M_{1}-\alpha_{4}x_{2}(t).

By the comparison principle,
x_{2}(t)\leq z(t)
for all

. Since
z(t)
remains between

and its equilibrium

(7)

is bounded on
[0,\infty)
.

Next, using the bound

\dot{x}_{3}(t)\leq\alpha_{5}M_{2}-\alpha_{6}x_{3}(t).

Technical results

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}>0
and
\theta,\gamma,\alpha_{1}>0
. For each
L>0
, consider the fixed-point equation

\tau(L)=\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}+\frac{\theta}{\gamma(L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L))}.

(11)

Then, the unique positive solution
\tau^{*}=\tau(L)
exists and
\tau(L)
satisfies:

(i)

\tau(L)>\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}
for all
L>0
;

(ii)

\tau(L)
is strictly decreasing in
L
;

(iii)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\tau(L)=\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}
.

Proof.

Setting
\psi_{1}:=\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}
and
\psi_{2}:=\theta/\gamma
,
the fixed-point equation becomes
\tau(L)=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}/(L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L))
. Equivalently
(\tau(L)-\psi_{1})(L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L))=\psi_{2}
or

q(\tau(L)):=\alpha_{1}\tau(L)^{2}+(L-\alpha_{1}\psi_{1})\tau(L)-(\psi_{1}L+\psi_{2})=0
,
which has exactly one positive root

\tau^{*}=\frac{\alpha_{1}\psi_{1}-L+\sqrt{(L+\alpha_{1}\psi_{1})^{2}+4\alpha_{1}\psi_{2}}}{2\alpha_{1}}.

(12)

Hence
\tau^{*}=\tau(L)
is well defined. From (
12
), it is clear that
\tau(L)>\psi_{1}
for every
L>0
, which proves
(i)
. Moreover, differentiating
q(\tau(L))
with respect to
L
yields

2\alpha_{1}\tau(L)\tau(L)^{\prime}+\tau(L)+(L-\alpha_{1}\psi_{1})\tau(L)^{\prime}-\psi_{1}=0,\qquad\rightarrow\qquad\tau(L)^{\prime}=\frac{\psi_{1}-\tau(L)}{2\alpha_{1}\tau(L)+L-\alpha_{1}\psi_{1}}.

Since
\tau(L)>\psi_{1}
, it follows that
\tau(L)^{\prime}<0
. Therefore
\tau(L)
is strictly decreasing, proving
(ii)
. Furthermore, using the expansion

\sqrt{(L+\alpha_{1}\psi_{1})^{2}+4\alpha_{1}\psi_{2}}=L+\alpha_{1}\psi_{1}+\frac{2\alpha_{1}\psi_{2}}{L+\alpha_{1}\psi_{1}}+o\!\left(\frac{1}{L}\right),\qquad L\to\infty,

and substituting this into the expression for
\tau(L)
gives, as
L\to\infty
,
\lim_{L\to\infty}\tau(L)=\psi_{1}
, which proves point
(iii)
.

Thus, for large excursion levels
L
, the waiting time is asymptotically determined by the first two cascade stages
\psi_{1}
, while the contribution of the final stage becomes negligible.
∎

Proposition 4
.

Let
\tau(L):(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be defined as in (
11
). Define
\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))=KL/[8(L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L))]
with
K\coloneqq(\alpha_{3}\alpha_{5}\alpha_{7})/(\alpha_{4}\alpha_{6}\alpha_{8})
.
Then
\ell_{4}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is strictly increasing with
\lim_{L\to\infty}\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))=K/8.

Proof.

From the definition of
\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))
, it suffices to study the function

\psi(L):=\frac{L}{L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L)},

since
\ell_{4}(L)=\frac{K}{8}\psi(L)
. Differentiating, we obtain

\psi(L)^{\prime}=\frac{(L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L))-L(1+\alpha_{1}\tau(L)^{\prime})}{(L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L))^{2}}=\frac{\alpha_{1}\bigl(\tau(L)-L\tau(L)^{\prime}\bigr)}{(L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L))^{2}}.

By Proposition
3
, one has
\tau(L)>0
and
\tau(L)^{\prime}<0
for all
L>0
. Hence

\tau(L)-L\tau(L)^{\prime}>0,
and therefore
\psi(L)^{\prime}>0
for all
L>0
. Thus
\psi(L)
, and hence
\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))
, is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
.

Moreover, Proposition
3
gives that
\tau(L)
converges to a finite limit as
L\to\infty
. Therefore,

\lim_{L\to\infty}\psi(L)=1,
and hence
\lim_{L\to\infty}\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))=K/8.

∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))=KL/[8(L+\alpha_{1}\tau(L))]
with
K\coloneqq(\alpha_{3}\alpha_{5}\alpha_{7})/(\alpha_{4}\alpha_{6}\alpha_{8})
. Then the map
L\mapsto L\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
and satisfies
\lim_{L\to\infty}L\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))=\infty.

Consequently, for every
a>0
, there exists a unique
L^{*}>0
such that
L^{*}\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L^{*}))=a.

Proof.

By Proposition
4
,
\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))>0,\forall L>0
and
\ell_{4}
is strictly increasing so that
L\mapsto L\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
.
Moreover, Proposition
4
gives
\lim_{L\to\infty}\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))=K/8

and it follows that
\lim_{L\to\infty}L\ell_{4}(L,\tau(L))=\infty.

Since

there exists a unique

such that

L^{*}\ell_{4}(L^{*})=a.

Taking

∎

Lemma 4

(First hitting-time)
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and differentiable, and let

Q\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that the set
\{t\geq 0:f(t)=Q\}
is nonempty and define
t^{\ast}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:f(t)=Q\}.
Therefore,

1.

If
f(0)<Q
, then
f(t)\leq Q
for all
t\in[0,t^{\ast}],
and
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})\geq 0.

Moreover, if
f
crosses
Q
transversally from below at
t^{\ast}
, i.e.,
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})\neq 0
, then
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})>0
.

2.

If
f(0)>Q
, then
f(t)\geq Q
for all
t\in[0,t^{\ast}],
and
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})\leq 0.

Moreover, if
f
crosses
Q
transversally from above at
t^{\ast}
, i.e.,
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})\neq 0
, then
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})<0.

Proof.

We prove the case
f(0)<Q
; the case
f(0)>Q
is analogous with the inequalities reversed.
Since
t^{\ast}
is the first time at which
f
reaches the level
Q
and

f(0)<Q
, we have
f(t)<Q
for all
t\in[0,t^{\ast})
and
f(t^{\ast})=Q.

Hence
f(t)\leq Q
for all
t\in[0,t^{\ast}]
.

For
h<0
sufficiently small, we have
t^{\ast}+h<t^{\ast}
, and so
f(t^{\ast}+h)<Q=f(t^{\ast}).

Therefore,
\frac{1}{h}[f(t^{\ast}+h)-f(t^{\ast})]\geq 0
. Passing to the limit as
h\to 0^{-}
gives
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})\geq 0.

If, in addition,
f
crosses
Q
transversally from below at
t^{\ast}
, then

\dot{f}(t^{\ast})\neq 0
. Since
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})\geq 0
, it follows that
\dot{f}(t^{\ast})>0.

∎

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied boundedness of a class of nonlinear feedback systems motivated by antithetic control. We first introduced a simple time-domain idea: if a state becomes large and stays large for some time, then the feedback will eventually become strong enough to push it down. This gives a direct way to guarantee boundedness without using Lyapunov functions.
We then applied this idea to the four-dimensional antithetic feedback system. By following how the signal propagates through the cascade, we showed that a large value of
x_{1}
leads, after a delay, to a sufficiently large value of
x_{4}
. This makes the term
x_{1}x_{4}
dominate and forces
x_{1}
to decrease. We also showed that once this happens, the effect persists, which prevents
x_{1}
from growing without bound. As a result, all states of the system are bounded.
The proofs rely only on differential inequalities and comparison arguments, which makes the mechanism easy to understand. The result can also be viewed as a time-domain small-gain effect: growth in the forward direction is eventually balanced by delayed feedback.
Our technique generalizes to more general stable positive linear systems
\dot{z}=Az+bu
,
y=c^{\top}z
(instead of just second order systems defined by
(x_{2},x_{3})
, with
u=x_{1}
and using
y
instead if
x_{3}
in the
x_{4}
equation); details will be included in a follow-up paper.
Other possible extensions include systems with time-varying parameters, disturbances, or more general intermediate dynamics, as well as studying convergence properties in addition to boundedness.
