Iwasawa invariants of Bertolini–Darmon Theta Elements
Abhishek, Jishnu Ray, Pronay Kumar Karmakar

Abstract
. In this article we study the Iwasawa invariants of Bertolini–Darmon theta elements in the anticyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of an imaginary quadratic field K K for weight two modular forms f ∈ S 2 ​ ( Γ 0 ​ ( N ) ) f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N)) . We cover both the cases of ordinary and non-ordinary reduction at a prime p p . Our results extend the known results of Pollack–Weston and Leonard–Lei in the cyclotomic setting.

2.
Iwasawa invariants in finite-level group algebras

In this section, we recall the definitions and some properties of the Iwasawa invariants of elements of finite-level group algebras, which will be used in later sections.

Let
K_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
with Galois group
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and
K_{n}
be the subextension of
K_{\infty}
with Galois group
G_{n}
of order
p^{n}
.
Let us fix a finite integrally closed extension
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and set
\Lambda_{n}=\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
. Let
\Lambda=\varprojlim\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
denote the Iwasawa algebra. Note that
\Lambda\cong\mathcal{O}[[T]]
via the map
\gamma\mapsto 1+T
, where
\gamma
is a topological generated of
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
\varpi
be a fined uniformizer of
cO
and for any integer
n\geq 1
, define
\omega_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(1+T)^{p^{n}}-1\in\mathcal{O}[[T]]
.

For any
P\in\Lambda
, write
P=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{j}T^{j}
. The Iwasawa invariants of
P
is defined as follows:

\mu(P)=\min_{j}\ord_{p}(b_{j}),

\lambda(P)=\min\{j\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ord_{p}(b_{j})=\mu(P)\}.

It is to be noted that the above definitions are independent of the choice of isomorphism
\Lambda\cong\mathcal{O}[[T]]
.

We now define the Iwasawa invariants in the finite-level group algebras
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
. Suppose
\theta\in\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
such that
\theta=\sum_{\sigma\in G_{n}}c_{\sigma}\sigma
, we then define

\mu(\theta)=\min_{\sigma\in G_{n}}\ord_{p}(c_{\sigma}).

To define the
\lambda
-invariants , let us first fix a uniformizer
\pi
of
\mathcal{O}
, and set
\theta^{\prime}=\pi^{-\mu(\theta)}\theta
. Let
\bar{\theta^{\prime}}
denote the non-zero image of
\theta^{\prime}
under the natural map
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}[G_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{F}
is the residue field of
\mathcal{O}
.

Since all ideals of
\mathbb{F}[G_{n}]
are of the form
I_{n}^{j}
, where
I_{n}
is the augmentation ideal. We then define

\lambda(\theta)=\ord_{I_{n}}\bar{\theta^{\prime}}=\max\{j\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\bar{\theta^{\prime}}\in I_{n}^{j}\}.

We now state and prove some lemmas following the work of
10
,
9
and
3
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
f,g\in\Lambda_{n}
we have

(1)

\mu(fg)\geq\mu(f)+\mu(g)
,

(2)

\lambda(fg)=\lambda(f)+\lambda(g)
if
\mu(fg)=0
.

Proof.

(1)

By the definition of the
\mu
-invariant we have,
f\in p^{\mu(f)}\Lambda_{n}
and
g\in p^{\mu(g)}\Lambda_{n}
. This implies
fg\in p^{\mu(f)+\mu(g)}\Lambda_{n}
. This proves the first part.

(2)

It is clear that
\mu(f)=\mu(g)=0
, since
\mu(fg)=0
. Therefore the
\lambda
-invariants of
f,g
and
fg
are defined. Now we have

\lambda(fg)=\ord_{I_{n}}(\overline{fg})=\ord_{I_{n}}(\bar{f})+\ord_{I_{n}}(\bar{g})=\lambda(f)+\lambda(g).

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For
f\in\Lambda_{n-1}
and
g\in\Lambda_{n}
we have

(1)

\Image(\xi_{n})={\xi}_{n}\Lambda_{n}
,

(2)

\pi_{n}^{n-1}(\xi_{n}(f))=p\cdot f
,

(3)

\xi_{n}(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(g))={\xi}_{n}\cdot g
.

Proof.

See
(
10
, Lemma. 4.6)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

We have
\mu(\xi_{n})=0
and
\lambda(\xi_{n})=p^{n}-p^{n-1}
.

Proof.

Let us suppose that
\gamma
be the generator of
G_{n}
. Then
I_{n}
is generated by
\gamma-1
. Therefore we have

\xi_{n}=\sum_{\sigma^{p}=1}\sigma=\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\gamma^{ap^{n-1}}=\gamma^{p^{n}}-1/\gamma^{p^{n-1}}-1\equiv(\gamma-1)^{p^{n}-p^{n-1}}\mod p.

This shows
\mu(\xi_{n})=0
and
\lambda(\xi_{n})=p^{n}-p^{n-1}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
P\in\Lambda
and let
P_{n}
denote the image of
P
in
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
. Then for
n\gg 0
, we have
\mu(P)=\mu(P_{n})
and
\lambda(P)=\lambda(P_{n})
.

Proof.

See
(
9
, Lemma. 3.1)
.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
P\in\Lambda
and
Q\in\mathcal{O}[T]
, and suppose that
P\equiv Q\mod{(\varpi,\omega_{n})}
. If
\deg(Q)<p^{n}
,
\lambda(P)<p^{n}
for
n\geq 0
, and
\mu(P)=0
, then the Iwasawa invariants of
P
and
Q
agrees.

Proof.

See
3
.

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\theta\in\mathcal{O}[G_{n-1}]
and
g\in\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
we have

(1)

\mu(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(g))\geq\mu(g)
,

(2)

\mu(\xi_{n}(\theta))=\mu(\theta)
,

(3)

\lambda(\xi_{n}(\theta))=p^{n}-p^{n-1}+\lambda(\theta)
.

Proof.

The first part is clear from the definition. For the second part suppose
\theta=p^{\mu(\theta)}\theta^{\prime}
with
\mu(\theta^{\prime})=0
. This implies
\xi_{n}(\theta)=p^{\mu(\theta)}\xi_{n}(\theta^{\prime})
. Thus
\mu(\xi_{n}(\theta))=\mu(\theta)
if
\mu(\xi_{n}(\theta^{\prime}))=0
. This is reduced to the case where
\mu(\theta)=0
. Now let us choose
g\in\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
such that
\pi_{n}^{n-1}(g)=\theta
. It is to be noted that
\mu(g)=0
by
(1)
. Thus we have

\xi_{n}(\theta)=\xi_{n}(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(g))=\xi_{n}\cdot g

by lemma
2.2
. So we have

\mu(\xi_{n}(\theta))=\mu(\xi_{n}\cdot g)=\mu(g)=0=\mu(\theta)
.
For the proof of
(3)
, we may assume that
\mu(\theta)=0
. Then let us choose
g\in\Lambda_{n}
lifting
\theta
and thus we have
\lambda(\xi_{n}(\theta))=\lambda(\xi_{n}\cdot g)=\lambda(g)+\lambda(\xi_{n})
, by
(2)
of Lemma
2.1
. This implies

\lambda(g)+\lambda(\xi_{n})=p^{n}-p^{n-1}+\lambda(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(\theta))=p^{n}-p^{n-1}+\lambda(\theta).

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
.

(1)

If
\mu(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(\theta))=0
, then
\mu(\theta)=0
.

(2)

If
\mu(\theta)=\mu(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(\theta))
, then
\lambda(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(\theta))=\lambda(\theta)
.

Proof.

Part
(1)
follows from the fact that
\mu(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(\theta))\geq\mu(\theta)
. For
(2)
we know
\theta\mod p\in I_{n}^{a}
if and only if
\pi_{n}^{n-1}(\theta)\mod p\in I_{n-1}^{a}
, since these augmentation ideals are principal. This implies
\lambda(\pi_{n}^{n-1}(\theta))=\lambda(\theta)
since the
\mu
-invariants of both these elements are same.

∎

3.
Bertolini–Darmon Theta elements

In this section we recall the definition of Bertolini–Darmon theta elements as defined in
1
and
4
. We follow the notation and setup of
2
.

Let
p\geq 5
be a fixed prime and
K
be an imaginary quadratic number field. Let
N
be a positive integer coprime to
p
, write
N=N^{+}N^{-}
where
N^{+}
(resp.
N^{-}
) is divisible only by primes that are split (resp. are inert) in
K
. Throughout this section we assume that
N^{-}
is square-free and is a product of an odd number of primes.

Let
B
be the definite quaternion algebra of discriminant
N^{-}
. Let
R
be an Eichler
\mathbb{Z}[1/p]
-order of level
N^{+}
in

and

We denote by

\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T})
be the set of vertices and
\vec{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{T})
denotes the ordered edges of
\mathcal{T}
. Set
\mathbf{\Gamma}=R^{\times}/\mathbb{Z}[1/p]^{\times}
and let

be a ring. Recall that the

-valued weight two modular form on

is a

-valued function

We denote by

the space of

-valued weight two modular forms on
\mathcal{T}/\mathbf{\Gamma}
. The following Proposition gives the correspondence between modular forms in

and

as a cosequence of Jacquet–Langlands correspondence, see
(
1
, Proposition 1.3)
and
(

, Theorem 2.2, Proposition 2.3)

.

Let

be a newform, then there exists

such that

and
h
have the same Hecke eigenvalues at all primes
\ell\nmid N
. This form

is unique up to multiplication by a non-zero scalar.

We now fix a newform

of level

and weight
2
. Using the above proposition, we can identify

, which is not divisible by
\varpi
. Let

be the completion of

at

. Let

be the ring of integers of

.

Fix an embedding
\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}K\longrightarrow B
such that
\Psi(K)\cap R=\Psi(\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/p]^{\times})

and any vertex or edge

of
\mathcal{T}
. Set
\widetilde{G}_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Pi_{\infty}/v_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, where

is a fundamental unit of
K
. There is natural filtartion of
\Pi_{\infty}
as given in
(
4

,

\cdots\subset U_{n}\subset\cdots\subset U_{1}\subset U_{0}\subset\Pi_{\infty},

We note that, if

is choosen as in

be the natural projection and

f
is defined by

f_{\alpha}(z)=f(z)-\beta\cdot f(pz).

The Bertolini-Darmon theta element of
f_{\alpha}
over
K_{n}
is characterized by the following relation

\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{f,n}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\xi_{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{f,n-1}).

By the three-term relation (
3
) we have

\pi_{n+1}^{n}(\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n+1})=\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n}.

This tells us that the theta elements of
f_{\alpha}
satisfy the norm compatibility relation.
Then the anticyclotomic
p
-adic
L
-function of
f
is defined by taking projective limit;

L_{p}(f/K_{\infty})=\varprojlim_{n}(\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n}.\iota(\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n}))\in\Lambda,

where
\iota
be the involution on
\Lambda_{n}
.

4.2.
Iwasawa invariants in the
p
-ordinary case.

Let
f
continue to be a
p
-ordinary eigenform on
\Gamma=\Gal(K_{\infty}/K)
. By Lemma
2.6
, we have

(4)

2\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n})=\mu(L_{p}(f/K_{\infty}))\quad\text{and}\quad 2\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n})=\lambda(L_{p}(f/K_{\infty})).

Now we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
L_{p}(f/K_{\infty})
is non-zero and
\mu(L_{p}(f/K_{\infty}))=0
. Then for
n\gg 0
, we have

(1)

\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=0
,

(2)

2\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=\lambda(L_{p}(f/K_{\infty})
.

Proof.

The proof is based on
(
9
, Prop. 3.7)
. We know

(5)

\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{f,n}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\xi_{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{f,n-1}).

Since by assumption
\mu(L_{p}(f/K_{\infty}))=0
and Lemma
2.4
, we have
\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n})=0
for
n\gg 0
. Since
\alpha
is a
p
-adic unit, it follows from (
5
) that
\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=0
, for sufficiently large
n
.

By lemma
2.6
we have,

\lambda(\xi_{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{f,n-1})\geq p^{n}-p^{n-1},

thus for large enough
n
we have,

\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n})<\lambda(\xi_{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{f,n-1}).

Therefore, we obtain

\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f_{\alpha},n}).

This shows that

2\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=\lambda(L_{p}(f/K_{\infty})).

Now we know
\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})\neq\lambda(\xi_{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{f,n-1})
. Which implies that the reduction of these two elements are not equal. Hence we have
\mu(\pi_{n+1}^{n}(\mathcal{L}_{f,n+1}))=0
, by
3
. This implies
\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f,n+1})=0
. This shows that
\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=0
for
n\gg 0
. This completes the proof.

Remark 4.2
.

The hypotheses in Theorem
4.1
are true under certain assumptions (cf.
(
8
, Theorem 4.1)
).

∎

5.1.
Construction Of Sprung-type matrices

For any integer
n\geq 0
, we write
\omega_{n}=\gamma^{p^{n}}-1\in\Lambda
and we denote the ring
\Lambda/(\omega_{n})
by
\Lambda_{n}
. Note that
\Lambda_{n}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
via the natural projection map. For
n\geq 1
, let
\Phi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n-1}}
be the
p^{n}
-th cyclotomic polynomial in the variable
\gamma
. For
n\geq 0
, define the polynomials

\omega_{n}^{+}=\prod_{1\leq j\leq n\text{ even}}\Phi_{j}(\gamma),\quad\omega_{n}^{-}=\prod_{1\leq j\leq n\text{ odd}}\Phi_{j}(\gamma).

For
n\geq 1
, define
M_{f,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=B_{f}^{-m-1}C_{f,n}\cdot\cdot\cdot C_{f,1}
, where
C_{f,n}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{p}(f)&1\\
-\Phi_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\Lambda)
and
B_{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{p}(f)&1\\
-p&0\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\Lambda)

Note that
C_{f,n+1}\equiv B_{f}\mod\omega_{n}
, as we know
\Phi_{n}\equiv p\mod\omega_{n}
. This implies that the sequence
\{M_{f,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a compatible system of matrices and converges to a matrix
M_{f,\log}\in M_{2}(\mathcal{H}(\Gamma))
.

Consider the
\Lambda
-morphism

H_{f,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Lambda_{n}^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{n}^{2},

defined by

H_{f,n}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}=C_{f,n}\cdot\cdot\cdot C_{f,1}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}.\\

Moreover, there is a natural isomorphism induced by the projections
\Lambda\longrightarrow\Lambda_{n}
,

\Lambda^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varprojlim_{n}\Lambda^{2}/\ker(H_{f,n}).

Using the above isomorphism, we have the following lemma from
2

Lemma 5.1
.

(
2
, Theorem 3.5)

There exist unique
\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp},\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\flat}\in\Lambda
such that for
n\geq 1
we have

H_{f,n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp}\\
\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\flat}\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{f,n}\\
-\xi_{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{f,n-1}\end{pmatrix}\mod\omega_{n},

where
H_{f,n}
is the logarithm matrix defined above as in
(
2
, Definition 3.3)
.

For simplicity, we first consider the case when
a_{p}=0
. the case
a_{p}\neq 0
will be dealt in a similar fashion with minor modification later in this section. In this case we first compute the matrices
H_{f,n}
for
n\geq 1
as follows:

(6)

H_{f,n}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{n/2}\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{-}_{n}&0\\
0&\omega^{+}_{n}\end{pmatrix}&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\
(-1)^{(n-1)/2}\begin{pmatrix}0&\omega^{+}_{n}\\
-\omega^{-}_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}&\text{if }n\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

This observation gives us the following explicit description of
\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp}
and
\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\flat}
in terms of
\mathcal{L}_{f,n}
and
\xi_{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{f,n-1}
. Thus we have

(7)

\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{f,n}\\
-\xi_{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{f,n-1}\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{cases}(-1)^{n/2}\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{-}_{n}&0\\
0&\omega^{+}_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp}\\
\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\flat}\end{pmatrix}\mod\omega_{n}&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\
(-1)^{(n-1)/2}\begin{pmatrix}0&\omega^{+}_{n}\\
-\omega^{-}_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp}\\
\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\flat}\end{pmatrix}\mod\omega_{n}&\text{if }n\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

From the above observation, we deduce the following Lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\mathcal{L}_{f,n}\equiv\begin{cases}(-1)^{n/2}\omega^{-}_{n}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp}\mod\omega_{n}&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\
(-1)^{(n-1)/2}\omega^{+}_{n}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\flat}\mod\omega_{n}&\text{if }n\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is clear from (
7
).
∎

Recall the definition of

5.2.
Iwasawa invariants in the non-ordinary case

To prove our main results in the non-ordinary case, we first prove the following propositions.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a new form with
a_{p}(f)=0
. Assume that
\mu(L_{p}^{\sharp}(f,K_{\infty}))=\mu(L_{p}^{\flat}(f,K_{\infty}))\neq\infty

Let
n_{0}^{-}
(resp.,
n_{0}^{+}
) be the least odd (resp., even) integer such that
\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\ast})<p^{n}-q_{n}
for
\ast\in\{\sharp,\flat\}

n\gg 0
(resp.,
n\gg 0
) depending on
n_{0}^{+}
and
n_{0}^{-}

\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\ast})\text{ and }\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\ast})+q_{n},

where
\ast=\sharp
if
n
is even and
\ast=\flat
if
n
is odd.

Proof.

Suppose

is an even integer. Then from

and lemma

\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=\mu(\omega^{-}_{n}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp})=\mu(\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp})

(see lemma

5.3
, we get that the

\omega^{-}_{n}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp}

\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f,n})=\lambda(\omega^{-}_{n}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp})=q_{n}+\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\sharp})
