Abstract
This letter derives the noncoherent (NC) maximum likelihood (ML) detection rule for LoRa signals under Rician multi-path fading channel. The proposed NC-ML detection only requires the channel statistic, not the actual instantaneous channel state information (CSI), which eliminates the overhead associated with channel estimation. Simulation results show that despite the low-complexity, the proposed detection scheme significantly improves the performance of LoRa detection over multipath channel. Notably, in time-invariant channel, the NC-ML receiver can achieve an equivalently good performance as compared to existing coherent schemes, and even surpasses them when Doppler shift is present, while not relying on the channel estimation nor reference signal extracted from the preamble.



I
Introduction

Chirp spread spectrum (CSS) modulation, also known as
the long range (LoRa) modulation is one of the key standards for IoT communications. The constant envelope and linearly-varying-frequency waveform of LoRa signal make LoRa robust against non-linear distortion from power amplifiers and Doppler shift. More importantly, LoRa is also well-known for its power efficiency, which can facilitate long-range, low power communication
[
1
]
. These advantages have made LoRa a suitable candidate for many IoT applications.

Despite originally designed for long range, stationary IoT communication applications, recently, LoRa has also found its applications in more unfavorable propagation environments with multipath fading channels
[
2
]
. In
[
3
,
4
]
, the theoretical performance of LoRa modulation over multi-path fading channel is derived, which shows that conventional LoRa demodulation scheme has a very poor performance in multipath fading channel. This highlights the importance of designing proper detection schemes that are specialized for multipath fading.

In
[
5
]
, a frequency domain equalization method for binary orthogonal keying CSS signal is studied. Two types of equalization techniques are investigated: zero forcing (ZF) and minimum mean squared error (MMSE), in which the MMSE outperforms the ZF at the cost of a higher complexity.
In
[
6
]
, a time-delay-estimation-like (TDEL) method to detect LoRa signal over multi-path fading is proposed. Based on the fact that different received LoRa chirps at the receiver are just a circularly shifted version of one another, the authors proposed to perform cyclic correlation between the received signal with a reference signal extracted from the preamble of LoRa packets. Based on the position of the highest correlation, the transmitted signal can be recovered. In
[
7
]
, a matched filter approach was proposed to detect LoRa signals over multipath channel, where the interference caused by multipath fading can be combined constructively similar to a RAKE receiver structure. The reported results show superior performance as compared to existing coherent LoRa detection designed for multipath fading scenarios. In
[
9
]
, a block interleaved chirp spreading LoRa modulation scheme is proposed, which can reduce the probability that the interfering peaks from non-direct paths surpass the peak corresponding to the direct path. Simulation results show that the performance of the proposed scheme grows as the spreading factor (SF) increases.

The aforementioned LoRa detection schemes for LoRa in multipath channel are mostly coherent, which require channel state information (CSI) acquisition, which are often estimated from LoRa packets’ preamble
[
7
]
. The only exception is the method in
[
6
]
, which does not explicitly requires channel estimation, but still needs the use of the preamble as a reference signal for data detection. Not only does the CSI requirement result in a complexity overhead, but it also makes the detector susceptible to errors caused by the mismatch between the estimated and the true instantaneous CSI. The reason is that a LoRa packet has a maximum payload from
51
(SF12) to
222
bytes (SF7)
[
1
]
, and thus, the channel is unlikely to stay constant in such significantly long airtime in the presence of Doppler effect. For example, a LoRa packet with 500-kHz bandwidth, 222-byte payload and 13-byte header has an airtime of
92.2
,
164.9
,
292.1
,
533.0
ms for
\mathrm{SF}=7
, 8, 9, 10, respectively
[
8
]
. With EDs’ mobility of
\nu=1
m/s (3.6 km/h) at
f_{c}=915
MHz carrier frequency, the Doppler spread is
f_{d}=\nu/(3\times 10^{8})\times f_{c}\approx 3
Hz. As a result, the coherence time is
\tau_{c}\approx 1/(4D)=81
ms, which is well below LoRa packets’ airtime. In this scenario, it is obvious that the channel will vary significantly over the packet duration, and hence, the estimated CSI based on the preamble can hardly remain valid over the span from the preamble to the entire payload.

Therefore, in this paper, we derive a low-complexity noncoherent (NC) maximum likelihood (ML) detection rule for LoRa signals in multipath Rician fading channel, which is the general and practical scenario in LoRaWAN, and can characterize both line-of-sight (LoS) and non-LoS transmission by simply adjusting the shape factor of the Rician distribution. Despite the low-complexity, it will be shown with simulation results that the proposed NC-ML detection has a equivalently good performance as the preamble-based TDEL method in
[
6
]
in time-invariant channel, and can be even better in the presence of Doppler effect, while not being reliant on
instantaneous
CSI acquisition like existing candidates.

II
System Model

A LoRa packet is constructed from multiple symbols, which are drawn from a set of
M=2^{\mathrm{SF}}
LoRa chirps. Specifically, the
m
th chirp is defined as
\bm{s}_{{m}}=[s_{{m}}[0],s_{{m}}[1],\ldots,s_{{m}}[M-1]]^{\mathsf{T}}
, where
{m}=0,1,\ldots,M-1
. The
0
th chirp is known as the basic upchirp, whose samples are defined as

s_{0}[n]=\exp\left\{j2\pi\left(\frac{n^{2}}{2M}-\frac{n}{2}\right)\right\},\;n=0,1\dots M-1.

Then, the
n
th sample of the
m
th chirp can be simply obtained from
s_{m}[n]
as
s_{m}[n]=s_{0}[(n+m)\mathrm{mod}M]
.

Consider the transmission of one
single
LoRa symbol
\bm{s}_{m}
over a multipath channel of
L
taps
\bm{h}=[h_{0},h_{1}\dots h_{L-1}]^{\mathsf{T}}
. The magnitude of the first tap is assumed to follow a Rician distribution with the shape factor
K_{0}
, since it contains the LoS path. Whereas, the magnitudes of other taps are assumed to follow Rayleigh distributions (equivalent to a Rician distribution with the shape factor
K_{\ell}=0,\;\ell\neq 0
). In general,
|h_{\ell}|\sim\text{Rice}\left(\sqrt{K_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}/(K_{\ell}+1)},\sqrt{\rho_{\ell}/(K_{\ell}+1)}\right)
, where
\rho_{\ell}
is the average power of the
\ell
th tap, with he probability distribution function (pdf)
[
10
]
:

\displaystyle f(|h_{\ell}|)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(K_{\ell}+1)|h_{\ell}|}{\rho_{\ell}}\exp\left\{-K_{\ell}-\frac{(K_{\ell}+1)|h_{\ell}|^{2}}{\rho_{\ell}}\right\}

\displaystyle\times I_{0}\left\{2\sqrt{\frac{K_{\ell}(K_{\ell}+1)}{\rho_{\ell}}}|h_{\ell}|\right\}.

(1)

The received signal over the channel is then given by:

\bm{y}=\bm{h}\odot\bm{s}_{m}+\bm{w}\in\mathbb{C}^{(M+L-1)\times 1},

(2)

where
\bm{w}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M+L-1})
is AWGN noise. In practice, due to the narrow-bandwidth and long-symbol nature of LoRa, the effect of inter-symbol interference (ISI) is negligible and can be ignored
[
7
,
6
]
. For example, with
\mathrm{SF}=7
and 500 kHz bandwidth in the extended vehicular A channel model
[
11
]
, the channel only has 2 taps. This means that only the first sample of each LoRa symbol is interfered by the preceding symbol. Thus, the input-output signal relationship over multipath fading channels can be approximated with a circular convolution between
\bm{h}
and the
\bm{s}_{m}
as:

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{y}}

\displaystyle=\bm{h}\circledast\bm{s}_{m}+\tilde{\bm{w}}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}

(3)

where
\tilde{\bm{w}}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M})
. The elements of
\tilde{\bm{y}}
can be rewritten:

\displaystyle\tilde{{y}}[n]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}h_{\ell}s_{m}[(n-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]+\tilde{w}[n]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}h_{\ell}s_{(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M}[n]+\tilde{w}[n].

(4)

It can be seen that the received signal, when a single LoRa symbol is transmitted over multipath fading channel, appears as the sum of
L
LoRa symbols, in which each of the
L
symbols corresponds to one tap of the channel. Suppose that the conventional detector is used
[
3
,
4
]
, which implements dechirping, followed by the discrete Fourier transform (DFT). Since the received signal over multipath channel is equivalent to the sum of
L
different LoRa symbols, the received dechirped signal in the frequency domain can be given as
\tilde{\bm{Y}}=[\tilde{Y}[0],\tilde{Y}[1]\dots\tilde{Y}[M-1]]^{\mathsf{T}}
, where:

\begin{split}\tilde{Y}[k]=\begin{cases}\sqrt{M}{h}_{\ell}e^{j\psi_{k}}+\tilde{W}[k],\;&k=(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M\\
\tilde{W}[k],&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(5)

In (
5
),
\tilde{W}[k]
is the DFT of the noise
\tilde{\bm{w}}
measured at the
k
th frequency bin and

\psi_{{k}}=2\pi\left\{{{{k}}^{2}}/(2M)-{{{k}}}/{2}\right\}
is a deterministic bin-dependent phase offset, which can be conveniently discarded by converting
\tilde{Y}[k]
into:

\bar{Y}[k]=\tilde{Y}[k]\exp\{-j\psi_{{k}}\}.

(6)

Equation (
5
) indicates that over multipath channel, the dechirped signal in the frequency domain allocates the energy on
L
different bins. Consequently, since the conventional DFT-based receiver detects symbols by selecting the bin with the highest power, it is extremely susceptible to errors due to multiple unwanted peaks at different frequency bins following the one corresponding to the transmitted symbol. Thus, in the next section, we propose a low-complexity NC-ML detection method for LoRa signal multipath channel.

III
LoRa ML detection for multipath fading

In this section, the NC-ML detection rule for LoRa signal over Rician multipath fading is derived. For the sake of completeness, the coherent ML detection rule will also be derived for comparison purposes.

III-A

Coherent maximum likelihood detection

According to (
5
), given that the
m
th symbol has been transmitted, it can be seen that conditioned on
h_{\ell}
:

\displaystyle\bar{Y}[k]\sim\mathcal{CN}(\sqrt{M}h_{\ell},\sigma^{2}),\;

\displaystyle k=(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M,

\displaystyle\bar{Y}[k]\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2}),

\displaystyle\text{otherwise}.

(7)

Consequently, the likelihood function that the
m
th chirp is transmitted, given the received signal
\bar{\bm{Y}}
, is the product of the pdf of all
M
frequency bins with the distributions in (
III-A
) conditioning on the channel
\bm{h}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(m|\bar{\bm{Y}},\bm{h})=\left(\frac{1}{\pi\sigma^{2}}\right)^{M}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0,k\neq(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M\end{subarray}}^{M-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[k]|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\prod_{L-1}^{\ell=0}\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]-\sqrt{M}h_{\ell}|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}\exp\left\{\frac{-|\bar{Y}[k]|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}}{(\pi{\sigma^{2}})^{M}}\prod_{L-1}^{\ell=0}\frac{\exp\left\{\frac{-|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]-\sqrt{M}h_{\ell}|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}}{\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}}.

Taking the logarithm of the likelihood function results in the log-likelihood (LLH) function in a simpler form as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{LLH}(m|\bar{\bm{Y}},\bm{h})=\mathrm{log}(\mathcal{L}(m|\bar{\bm{Y}}),\bm{h})=C-

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\left(|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]-\sqrt{M}h_{\ell}|^{2}-|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]|^{2}\right)}{\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle=C+\frac{\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\left(\sqrt{M}\mathfrak{R}\{\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]h_{\ell}^{*}\}+M|h_{\ell}|^{2}\right)}{\sigma^{2}},

(9)

where
C=\mathrm{log}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\pi{\sigma^{2}}}\right)^{M}\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[k]|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right\}\right)
and is independent of
m
. From this LLH function, the coherent ML detection rule can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\hat{m}=\underset{m}{\mathrm{argmax}}\;\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\mathfrak{R}\{\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]h_{\ell}^{*}\}.

(10)

It can be seen that the obtained coherent ML detection rule above is identical to the RAKE receiver structure in
[
7
]
.

III-B

Noncoherent maximum likelihood detection

The likelihood function that the
m
th chirp is transmitted given the received signal
\bar{\bm{Y}}
with the NC detection can be obtained by marginalizing the coherent likelihood function in (
III-A
) over the channel
h_{\ell}
. However, this approach involves the calculation of
L
-fold challenging integrals that would make the derivation lengthy and hard to follow. Thus, we chose to derive the NC-ML directly form the distributions of the magnitudes of the frequency bins, which is more straightforward, but still leads to the same final NC-ML detection rule as the marginalization over
h_{\ell}
approach mentioned earlier.

With Rician multipath fading, the LoS path, which contributes to the first channel tap, has deterministic gain, while other paths (non-LoS) are assumed to have magnitude following Rayleigh distribution. As a result, in the multipath channel model with Rician fading, the magnitudes of all taps follow Rayleigh distribution, except for the first tap, whose magnitude follows Rician distribution.
Given the
m
th chirp was transmitted, the magnitude of the received signal in the frequency domain in (
5
) after DFT will be distributed as:

\displaystyle|\bar{Y}[k]|\sim\text{Rice}\left(\sqrt{\frac{K_{0}M\rho_{0}}{K_{0}+1}},\sqrt{\frac{M\rho_{0}}{K_{0}+1}+\sigma^{2}}\right),\;k=m,

\displaystyle|\bar{Y}[k]|\sim\text{Rayleigh}(\sqrt{M\rho_{\ell}+\sigma^{2}}),\;k=(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M,

\displaystyle|\bar{Y}[k]|\sim\text{Rayleigh}(\sigma),\;\text{otherwise}.

(11)

with the Rician distribution shape factor
\tilde{K}={K_{0}M\rho_{0}}/(M\rho_{0}+(K_{0}+1)\sigma^{2})
.

Proof:
When
k\neq m
, from (
5
), it can be seen that
\bar{Y}[k]\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,M\rho_{\ell}+\sigma^{2})
for
k=(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M
and
\bar{Y}[k]\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2})
otherwise. As a result, the amplitude of
\bar{Y}[k]
follows Rayleigh distribution as in (
III-B
).
When
k=m
:

\displaystyle\bar{Y}[k]=\sqrt{M}{h}_{\ell}+\tilde{W}[k]\exp\{-j\psi_{{m}}\}=\sqrt{M}{h}_{\ell}+\bar{W}[k]

(12)

Since
{h}_{\ell}
is Rician distributed with the shape factor
K_{0}
and raw moment
\rho_{0}
, the first term of (
12
) is also a Rician distributed with the shape factor
K_{0}
and raw moment
M\rho_{0}
. Consequently, when adding the AWGN noise
\bar{W}[k]\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2})
, the resulted
\bar{Y}[k]
will also follow a Rician distribution, but with the raw moment
M\rho_{0}+\sigma^{2}
. The new shape factor can be calculated as the ratio between the power of the LoS path
{K_{0}M\rho_{0}}/(K_{0}+1)
to the sum of the power of the non-LoS component
{M\rho_{0}}/(K_{0}+1)
plus noise
\sigma^{2}
, which results in
\tilde{K}={K_{0}M\rho_{0}}/(M\rho_{0}+(K_{0}+1)\sigma^{2})
.
\hfill\blacksquare

Consequently, the NC-ML detection can be derived as:

\displaystyle\hat{m}=\underset{m}{\mathrm{argmax}}\;\left(\mathrm{log}\left\{I_{0}\left\{2\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{K}(\tilde{K}+1)}{M\rho_{0}+\sigma^{2}}}|\bar{Y}[m]|\right\}\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\rho_{0}|\bar{Y}[m]|^{2}}{M\rho_{0}+({K}_{0}+1)\sigma^{2}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\frac{\rho_{\ell}|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]|^{2}}{M\rho_{\ell}+\sigma^{2}}\right).

(13)

Proof:
With the distribution in (
III-B
), the likelihood function can be calculated as the product of the pdf of all
M
bins, which is presented in (
III-B
) on top of the next page. By eliminating the terms that are independent of
m
and taking the logarithm, the NC-ML rule can be easily obtained as in (
III-B
).
\hfill\blacksquare

As mentioned earlier, the NC-ML detector requires the knowledge of the channel’s parameters, which can be estimated
offline
based on the preamble of multiple packets received over time with many well-investigated parameter estimation technique such as
[
12
]
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(m|\bar{\bm{Y}})

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M\end{subarray}}^{M-1}\frac{2|\bar{Y}[k]|}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[k]|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}\right)\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\frac{2|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]|}{M\rho_{\ell}+\sigma^{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]|^{2}}{M\rho_{\ell}+\sigma^{2}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{2(\tilde{K}+1)|\bar{Y}[m]|}{M\rho_{0}+\sigma^{2}}\exp\left\{-\tilde{K}-\frac{(\tilde{K}+1)|\bar{Y}[m]|^{2}}{M\rho_{0}+\sigma^{2}}\right\}I_{0}\left\{2\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{K}(\tilde{K}+1)}{M\rho_{0}+\sigma^{2}}}|\bar{Y}[m]|\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\frac{\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]|^{2}}{M\rho_{\ell}+\sigma^{2}}\right\}}{\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[(m-\ell)\mathrm{mod}M]|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}}\right)\frac{\exp\left\{-\frac{(\tilde{K}+1)|\bar{Y}[m]|^{2}}{M\rho_{0}+\sigma^{2}}\right\}}{\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[m]|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}}I_{0}\left\{2\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{K}(\tilde{K}+1)}{M\rho_{0}+\sigma^{2}}}|\bar{Y}[m]|\right\}

\displaystyle\times\underbrace{\left(\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}\frac{2|\bar{Y}[k]|}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{|\bar{Y}[k]|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}\right)\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{M\rho_{\ell}+\sigma^{2}}\right)\exp\left\{-K\right\}\frac{(\tilde{K}+1)\sigma^{2}}{M\rho_{0}+\sigma^{2}}}_{\text{independent of $m$}}.

(14)

III-C

Complexity analysis

For the coherent and NC-ML detection,
M
LLH functions must be evaluated, corresponding to
M
possible chirps. For each LLH function, a sum of
L
elements must be performed, which results in the complexity order of
\mathcal{O}(ML)
. In addition to the complexity of the dechirp-DFT step, the total complexity of the coherent and NC-ML detection is
\mathcal{O}(M\mathrm{log}_{2}M+ML)
, which is equivalent to the complexity of the TDEL method
[
6
]
and the RAKE receiver
[
7
]
.

IV
Simulation Results

In this section, the performance comparison among the NC-ML detection, the conventional LoRa detection, the TDEL method
[
6
]
, which has the best NC detection performance in recent literature, and the coherent ML detection (which is identical to the RAKE receiver proposed in
[
7
]
) will be presented. Note that for the original TDEL method proposed in
[
6
]
, the reference signal is extracted from the preamble by setting the amplitudes of the frequency bins that are lower than
1/4
of the highest bin’s amplitude to zero. This makes the original TDEL method somewhat susceptible to noise. Thus, for a fair comparison, we also present the performance of a modified TDEL method, which knows the number of channel taps in advance, and thus, can set the frequency bins of the reference signal that do not correspond to the channel power profile to zero to reduce noise. For the coherent ML detection, the CSI can be obtained by performing channel estimation based on the preamble of LoRa packets
[
7
]
. For simplicity in simulation, the CSI is assumed to be perfectly known by the receiver for the coherent ML detection.

We chose the extended vehicular A model
[
11
]
with the channel delay profile
\bm{\tau}=[0,\,30,\,150,\,310,\,370,\,710,\,1090,\,1730,\,2510]
nanoseconds and the respective relative power profile of
[0,\,-1.5,\,-1.4,\,-3.6,\,-0.6,\,-9.1,\,-7,\,-12,\,-16.9]
dB. The bandwidth is set at
B=500
kHz. With the chosen parameters, the channel filter has
L=\lceil\mathrm{max}(\bm{\tau})\times B\rceil=2
taps. The carrier frequency is 915 MHz. The packet payload length is set at
100
LoRa symbols.
The results will be obtained with the Rician fading shape factor of the first channel tap
K_{0}=0
(Rayleigh fading),
2
and
10
. The rate of variation of the channel will be determined by varying the maximum Doppler spread

.

In Fig.
2
, the performance comparison obtained in slowly-time-variant channel is presented. The maximum Doppler is set at
f_{d}=5
Hz, which can be experienced with a moving LoRa device at a walking speed of
6

K_{0}=0

2
, whether the channel power profile is available or not, reaches an error floor at the high SNR range. This is due to the time-variant channel, which makes the reference signal taken from the preamble outdated over time, especially at the last symbols of LoRa packets. The only exception is when

Whereas, the performance of the conventional LoRa detection and the proposed NC-ML detection is almost identical to the time-invariant case, since the instantaneous CSI is not required. Thus, the NC-ML detection outperforms the TDEL and the conventional LoRa detection in time-variant channel.

Noticeably, the coherent ML detection has the worst performance among the evaluated methods in time-variant channel, due to the strong reliance on the accuracy of channel estimation. Its performance quickly reaches an error floor even before the conventional LoRa detection. To maintain a good performance as compared to the time-invariant channel case, the TDEL and the coherent ML method will need frequent reference updates/channel estimations over the span of a LoRa packet to update the instantaneous state of the time-variant channel. This, however, comes at the cost of a significant training overhead that reduces the already low data rate of LoRa modulation. Thus, in the time-variant channel scenario, NC-ML detection appears to be the most suitable approach to detect LoRa signals over multipath fading.

V
Conclusions

This letter derives the NC-ML detection rule for LoRa signals in multipath Rician fading channels. The NC-ML detection does not require the overhead associated with channel estimation, and only requires the knowledge about the statistic of the channel. Despite the low-complexity, the proposed NC-ML detection can significantly improve the SER as compared to the conventional LoRa receiver. Moreover,
in the time-invariant channel scenario, with a weak LoS path (
K_{0}=0
to
2
) the NC-ML is 2 dB better than the original TDEL method, while being around 2-5 dB worse than the modified TDEL and the coherent ML detection at the SER of
10^{-4}
. Whereas, with a strong LoS path, the NC-ML can achieve roughly
4
and
0.5
dB gain over the original and modified TDEL method, respectively, while still being around 2 dB behind the coherent ML. By contrast, with the presence of Doppler shift, the NC-ML detection even outperforms the TDEL methods and coherent ML detection, which quickly approach error floors (well above the SER of
10^{-4}
) when the LoS path is weak (
K_{0}=0
and 2). This is largely thanks to the independence from the CSI requirement of the NC-ML detection. With a strong LoS path (
K_{0}=10
), the proposed NC-ML detection outperforms both the original and modified TDEL by
4
and
0.5
dB, respectively, while the coherent ML still reaches an error floor. These results indicate that the proposed NC-ML detection is a suitable receiver for LoRa in multipath channels, especially with the presence of Doppler shift.
