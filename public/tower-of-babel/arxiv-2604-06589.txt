BiDexGrasp: Coordinated Bimanual Dexterous Grasps across Object Geometries and Sizes
Mu Lin, Yi-Lin Wei, Jiaxuan Chen, Yuhao Lin, Shuoyu Chen, Jiangran Lyu, Jiayi Chen, Yansong Tang, He Wang, Wei-Shi Zheng

Abstract
Bimanual dexterous grasping is a fundamental and promising area in robotics, yet its progress is constrained by the lack of comprehensive datasets and powerful generation models. In this work, we propose BiDexGrasp, consists of a large-scale bimanual dexterous grasp dataset and a novel generation model. For dataset, we propose a novel bimanual grasp synthesis pipeline to efficiently annotate physically feasible data for dataset construction. This pipeline addresses the challenges of high-dimensional bimanual grasping through a two-stage synthesis strategy of efficient region-based grasp initialization and decoupled force-closure grasp optimization. Powered by this pipeline, we construct a large-scale bimanual dexterous grasp dataset, comprising 6351 diverse objects with sizes ranging from 30 to 80 cm, along with 9.7 million annotated grasp data. Based on this dataset, we further introduce a bimanual-coordinated and geometry-size-adaptive dexterous grasping generation framework. The framework lies in two key designs: a bimanual coordination module and a geometry-size-adaptive grasp generation strategy to generate coordinated and high-quality grasps on unseen objects. Extensive experiments conducted in both simulation and real world demonstrate the superior performance of our proposed data synthesis pipeline and learned generative framework.

Currently, several recent studies have explored data synthesis for bimanual dexterous grasping
[
26
,
17
]
, primarily by extending single-hand grasp pipelines to the bimanual setting. However, these dataset still focus on objects of limited size ranges and fail to fully leverage the advantages of bimanual manipulation for handling objects with diverse scales. Meanwhile, we observe that both directly transferring state-of-the-art single-hand methods or applying existing bimanual methods perform poorly on objects with diverse geometries and scales. We attribute this to the dual challenges of varied object sizes and enlarged bimanual search spaces: the number of initialization candidates grows quadratically, and the subsequent force-closure optimization occurs in a high-dimensional space, significantly reducing both efficiency and grasp quality.

In this work, we introduce BiDexGrasp for coordinated bimanual dexterous grasping of objects with diverse sizes and geometries. First, we construct a large-scale, high-quality bimanual dexterous grasp dataset using a novel and effective two-stage data synthesis pipeline. Second, we propose a bimanual-coordinated and geometry-size-adaptive grasp generation framework that learns from this dataset, capable of producing high-quality grasp poses for diverse unseen objects.

For the dataset construction, we propose a novel bimanual dexterous grasp synthesis pipeline for effective and high-quality dataset construction.
The motivation is to tackle the challenges: the larger action space leads to increased complexity and reduced efficiency. To improve the efficiency, we introduce an efficient bimanual region-constraint grasp initialization strategy to obtain coordinated bi-grasp initializations with physically feasible potential. To improve the quality, we propose a decoupled force-closure grasp optimization strategy, which separates the force-closure of each hand. This decoupling not only reduces the overall optimization complexity, but also ensures that both hands achieve high-quality local grasps. Compared with previous bimanual grasp synthesis pipelines, our method achieves more than 2.8x the success rate and over 30× faster synthesis speed. Using this pipeline, we construct a large-scale and high-quality bimanual dexterous grasp dataset containing 6,351 objects with diverse geometries and scales and 9.7 million grasps, as shown in Tab.
I
.

Based on the dataset, we propose data-driven model, BiDexGrasp framework, for high-quality grasp generation on diverse unseen objects. The key motivation of this framework is how to achieve bimanual coordination and how to generalize across diverse geometries and sizes. To achieve coordination, we introduce a bimanual coordination module to predict coordinated grasp views, to guide the feature extraction and grasp poses generation. To achieve adaptation, we propose a geometry-size-adaptive grasp strategy that generates relative grasp poses conditioned on a size-adaptive grasp anchor and geometry-aware object features. This design reduces the action space and encourages the model to focus on local structures, thereby improving robustness to geometric and scale variations.

Extensive simulation and real-world experiments validate our data synthesis pipeline and learned grasp generation framework. The data collection experiments show high quality and good efficiency of our synthesis pipeline. And the model generation experiments in on simulation dataset and physical robots show good performance of our framework.

Preliminaries

Contact model
characterizes the resultant wrench exerted by a grasp on an object. Considering an object
O
with
m
contact points, the local contact mechanics at each point
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
are modeled as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{f}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid 0\leq f_{i,1}\leq 1,\sqrt{f_{i,2}^{2}+f_{i,3}^{2}}\leq\mu f_{i,1}\},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{n}_{i}&\mathbf{d}_{i}&\mathbf{e}_{i}\\
\mathbf{p}_{i}\times\mathbf{n}_{i}&\mathbf{p}_{i}\times\mathbf{d}_{i}&\mathbf{p}_{i}\times\mathbf{e}_{i}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times 3},

where
\mathbf{p}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
denotes the contact position and
\mathbf{n}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
represents the inward-pointing unit surface normal. The vectors
\mathbf{d}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
are orthogonal unit tangent vectors such that
\mathbf{n}_{i}=\mathbf{d}_{i}\times\mathbf{e}_{i}
, and
\mu
is the friction coefficient.

For each contact point,
\mathcal{F}_{i}
represents the discretized friction cone and
G_{i}
is the grasp matrix. The local contact force
\mathbf{f}_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
is mapped to a 6D wrench
\mathbf{w}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
in the object’s coordinate frame via
\mathbf{w}_{i}=\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{f}_{i}
and the total wrench
\mathbf{w}
generated by the grasp is the sum of the individual wrenches from all
m
contact points, i.e.,
\mathbf{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{w}_{i}
.

Grasp Wrench Space (GWS)
, denoted as
\mathcal{W}
, encompasses the set of all possible resultant wrenches that can be exerted on the object. For each contact point
i
, we define the individual wrench space as
\mathcal{W}_{i}=\{\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{f}_{i}\mid\mathbf{f}_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\}
. The aggregate GWS is then constructed via the Minkowski sum of these per-contact wrench spaces
\mathcal{W}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{i}
.

QP-energy
is a differentiable metric designed to evaluate the capability of the current contact state to resist external disturbances
\{\mathbf{t}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{6}
along six orthogonal axes, e.g.,
[\pm 1,0,0,0,0,0]^{\top}
. For a fixed contact system
\{\mathbf{G}_{i}\}
and disturbances
\{t_{j}\}
, the QP-Energy
Q
is calculated as:

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{6}\min_{\mathbf{f}_{j,1},...,\mathbf{f}_{j,m}}\left\|\beta\mathbf{t}_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{f}_{j,i}\right\|^{2},

(2)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{f}_{j,i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i,1}\geq\gamma,

where
\beta
and
\gamma
are two positive hyperparameters.

IV
BiDexGrasp Dataset

IV-A

Overview

Problem Definition
.
Given the object mesh
\mathcal{O}_{mash}
, the goal of grasp data synthesis is to obtain bimanual dexterous grasp poses
\mathcal{G}^{bi}=(\mathcal{G}^{left},\mathcal{G}^{right})=((t^{r},r^{r},q^{r}),(t^{l},r^{l},q^{l}))
,
where
r
and
t
refer to the rotation and translation of hand wrist, and
q
refers to the joint poses of dexterous hand.

Object Preparation
. For the preparation of objects with sufficient shape and scale diversity, we collect 3D objects from the Objaverse
[
6
]
and DexGraspNet
[
30
]
datasets. The objects are filtered to remove excessively flat objects that are unsuitable for tabletop grasping. This results in a 6,351 object final set of 2,397 objects from DexGraspNet and 3,954 objects from Objaverse. To expand the scale variation of the experimental objects, each individual object is scaled to 11 discrete sizes, with the size range set from 30 cm to 80 cm.

Synthesis Pipeline Overview
.
The pipeline consists of three stages: initialization, optimization, and filtering.
First, we employ a fast bimanual constraint–aware grasp initialization strategy to generate grasp candidates with the potential to satisfy physical feasibility of bimanual grasping.
Next, a decoupled force-closure–based optimization is performed to optimize the initial candidates into physically valid bimanual grasps.
Finally, we provide an arm-reachability analysis to derive kinematically feasible configurations.
The synthesis pipeline is built upon the advanced framework proposed in
[
2
]
, which is implemented on top of cuRobo
[
29
]
to enable efficient GPU-parallelized grasp synthesis.

IV-B

Bimanual region-constraint Grasp Initialization

Challenges for Bimanual Grasp Initialization
.
Extending single grasp initialization, which samples uniformly within the object’s convex hull, results in quadratic growth in sampling complexity.
We find that most of the random sampled pairs fail to satisfy the bimanual kinematic and coordination constraints of the bimanual system.
To address these, we propose a bimanual region-constraint grasp initialization strategy. First, grasping region candidates are efficiently selected based on the grasp wrench space (GWS).
Second, initial grasp poses are sampled from the selected regions, enabling both efficient and reliable initialization for subsequent optimization.

Fig. 2:
The data synthesis pipeline for bimanual grasping. The GWS-based region selection and region-based grasp initialization aims to obtain grasp candidates with the potential to satisfy physical feasibility. And the decoupled force-closure-based optimization is to refine these initial candidates into physically valid bimanual grasps.

GWS-based Bimanual Grasping Region Selection.

This module aims to identify bimanual regions of an object that exhibit the potential to generate stable and physically feasible bimanual grasps.
By leveraging grasp wrench space (GWS) energy, it efficiently filters promising region candidates, significantly improving the efficiency of the overall pipeline.

Specifically, the region selection process consists of four steps. (1) We first apply farthest point sampling (FPS) to select
K_{a}=200
anchors and sample
k=256
points within an 8 cm neighborhood around each anchor to define candidate grasping regions.
(2) We exclude concave regions as they are unfavorable for stable grasping and discard region pairs with insufficient inter-region distance to ensure geometric feasibility.
(3) For each remaining region pair, we evaluate its grasp stability using the Grasp Wrench Boundary (GWB) estimated by the TDG estimator
[
1
]
.
Specifically,
N=5
contact points are sampled per region, and the resulting GWB is represented by a set of boundary wrenches

\{\mathbf{w}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}
with
M=1000
.
To assess robustness against external disturbances, we adopt the same set of six orthogonal disturbance wrenches

\{\mathbf{t}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{6}
defined in the preliminaries.
The stability score is computed as the minimum projection of the GWB onto these disturbance directions:

s=\min_{1\leq j\leq 6}\;\max_{1\leq k\leq M}\;\mathbf{t}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{w}_{k}.

(3)

(4) Finally, we rank region pairs by stability score, and the top
K_{r}=40
are retained as candidates for grasp initialization.

Region-based Grasp Initialization.

Given the selected grasping regions, we generate initial grasp configurations
\mathcal{G}_{initial}
for subsequent optimization. Each hand pose is represented by the palm position, orientation, and an in-plane rotation about the palm normal. The palm position is chosen as the point on the dilated object convex hull closest to the grasping region, with the opposite surface normal defining the orientation. The in-plane rotation is determined by a predefined ideal direction for each hand. Finger joint angles are initialized based on local surface curvature, starting from a fully open posture and slightly closing to conform to the object surface.

Bimanual Feasibility Verification.

As a final step, we verify the physical and kinematic feasibility of all initialized bimanual grasp configurations.
We first perform collision checking to prevent self-collisions between the two hands as well as collisions with the object and the environment.
We then solve inverse kinematics (IK) for both arms to evaluate collision-free reachability.
Grasp initializations that fail the above feasibility checks are discarded.

IV-C

Decoupled Grasping Optimization

Challenges for Bimanual Grasp Optimization.

Bimanual grasp optimization is inherently challenging due to the increased complexity of the optimization space, leading to a tightly coupled objective that hinders stable and efficient optimization.
And such coupled objectives may yield unbalanced solutions, in which one hand dominates grasp stability while the other contributes marginally, causing the optimization to converge to suboptimal local minima.

Fig. 3:
BiDexGrasp Framework. Given the input object point cloud and pre-defined grasp view, the framework first predicts a pair of coordinated grasp views for the two hands by bimanual coordination module. Based on this view, we extract geometry-adaptive object feature and determine the scale-adaptive grasp. Then the relative dexterous grasp diffusion is employed to predict relative dexterous grasp form the grasp anchor.

Decoupled Force Closure

To obtain high-quality and physically feasible bimanual grasps, we propose a decoupled optimization strategy based on grasping energy.
The key idea is to decompose the bimanual grasp optimization into single-hand energy terms, allowing each hand to focus on establishing stable local contacts with the object.
This decoupling not only reduces the optimization complexity, but also discourages imbalanced solutions dominated by a single hand, resulting in improved bimanual grasp quality and convergence stability.

Specifically, given an object mesh
\mathcal{O}
,
we formulate the overall optimization problem as follows to optimize the dexterous poses
\mathcal{G}^{bi}

by minimizing the QP-Energy decoupled into two single-hand energy terms:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{G}^{bi}}

\displaystyle(w_{Q-left}Q(\mathcal{G}^{left})+w_{Q-right}Q(\mathcal{G}^{right})+

(4)

\displaystyle w_{dis}E_{dis}+w_{region}E_{region}),

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{bi}_{\min}\leq\mathcal{G}^{bi}\leq\mathcal{G}^{bi}_{\max},

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{i,w}=\mathrm{FK}(\mathcal{G}^{bi},\mathbf{c}_{i,l})\in\mathcal{O},

\displaystyle\text{No collision.}

\mathbf{c}_{i,l}
and
\mathbf{c}_{i,w}
denote the positions of the predefined hand contact point
i
expressed in the local hand frame and the world frame. In the QP formulation, the grasp matrix is constructed by associating each
\mathbf{c}_{i,w}
with its closest point
\mathbf{p}_{i}
on the object surface, while feasibility is ensured through joint-limit and collision-avoidance constraints covering self-collisions, inter-hand collisions, and hand–object collisions. And we define
E_{dis}
to encourage the contact points on the hands to be close to the corresponding points on the object surface:

E_{dis}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||\mathbf{c}_{i,w}-\mathbf{p}_{i}||)^{2}.

(5)

We further introduce a region consistency term
E_{region}
to keep the contact points close to the contact regions identified during initialization, thereby promoting coordinated bimanual grasping:

E_{region}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\phi(d_{i}),

(6)

where the effective distance
d_{i}
from the
i
-th hand contact point to the contact region
\mathcal{R}
is defined as

d_{i}=\!\min_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{R}}\lVert\mathbf{c}_{i,w}-\mathbf{r}\rVert,\;

(7)

and
\phi(\cdot)
is a piecewise penalty function given by

\phi(d)=\begin{cases}0,&d\leq a,\\[4.0pt]
H\!\left(\dfrac{d-a}{b-a}\right)(d-a),&a<d\leq b,\\[8.0pt]
d-a,&d>b,\end{cases}

(8)

with
a
denoting a distance threshold,
b=2a
defining the transition bandwidth, and
H(t)=3t^{2}-2t^{3}
being a cubic Hermite interpolation function.

Kinematics-Aware Arm-Hand Configuration Synthesis

To demonstrate the practical feasibility of the synthesized grasps, our framework supports solving the inverse kinematics (IK) for wrist poses, enabling a smooth transition from floating grasps to reachable tabletop configurations. To avoid collisions and facilitate force application, we first synthesize a pre-grasp pose
\mathcal{G}^{bi}_{pre}

. Subsequently, we compute a squeeze pose as the execution target:
\mathcal{G}^{bi}_{squ}=2\cdot\mathcal{G}^{bi}-\mathcal{G}^{bi}_{\text{pre}}
. Finally, we generate the arm joint trajectories from pre-grasp to squeeze and lift.

V
BiDexGrasp Framework

Problem definition.

To learn the generable bi-manual dexterous grasp, we design a data-driven generation model.
Specifically, given the object point cloud
\mathcal{O}_{pc}
, the framework aims to generate the bi-manual dexterous grasp poses
\mathcal{G}^{bi}
.

Challenge in bimanual grasping.

Bimanual grasp generation introduces two key challenges.
(1)
Bimanual coordination
: Bimanual hands must collaboratively target optimal grasp view to ensure global stability while avoiding inter-hand collisions.
(2)
Adaptation across object geometries and sizes
: The vast diversity in object sizes and geometries greatly hinders efficient model learning, increasing the difficulty of object feature extraction and expanding the action space simultaneously.

Model overview.

To address these challenges, we propose BiDexGrasp as shown in Fig.
5
, to achieve bimanual-coordinated and geometry-size-adaptive grasp generation:
(1)
bimanual coordination module
that explicitly models bimanual hand relationships via coordinated-view prediction;
(2)
geometry-size-adaptive grasp generation strategy
to improve the generation ability across object geometries and sizes by adaptive grasp anchors. Specifically, the bimanual coordination module first predicts coordinated grasp views. Based on these views, the model then determines the adaptive grasp base on the object’s bounding sphere and generates relative dexterous poses from the grasp anchor.

V-A

Bimanual Coordination Module.

To explicitly model bimanual coordination, we first estimate coordination-aware grasping views to guide subsequent grasp prediction. The module first predicts the most promising view as the primary grasping view, and predicts the second view that is most compatible with the primary view.

Specifically, we uniformly sample
K
discrete approach directions on the object-centered bounding sphere to construct a candidate view set
\mathcal{V}=\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
, which allows view estimation to be formulated as a view probabilistic regression. To predict the primary view, we introduce
K
learnable view embeddings
\mathbf{v}^{emb}_{i}
to extract view features
\mathbf{F}_{\text{view}}
by cross-attention:
\mathbf{v}_{i}=\mathrm{Attn}(\mathbf{v}^{emb}_{i},\mathbf{F}_{o},\mathbf{F}_{o})
, where
\mathbf{F}_{\text{global}}
are point features extracting by PointNet++
[
24
]
. To predict the second view, we model interactions between the primary view and the remaining candidate views to construct bi-view features by a MLPs layer:
\mathbf{v}^{bi}_{i,j}=\text{MLP}\!\big(\text{cat}(\mathbf{v}^{emb}_{i},\mathbf{v}^{emb}_{j},\mathbf{v}^{emb}_{i}-\mathbf{v}^{emb}_{j},\mathbf{v}^{emb}_{i}\odot\mathbf{v}^{emb}_{j})\big).

These features are used to predict single view and bi-view probabilistic
p_{i}
and
p^{bi}_{i,j}
, which is trained by the L2 losses:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{single-view}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(p_{i}-y_{i})^{2},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{bi-view}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K^{2}}\sum_{i,j}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}(p^{bi}_{i,j}-y^{bi}_{i,j})^{2}.

(10)

The probabilities ground-truth
y_{i}
and
y^{bi}_{i,j}
are computed from the distribution of successful grasps for each object in dataset. For single-view, we assign each grasp to the nearest predefined candidate view, then count the number of grasps associated with each view and normalize these counts to form a probability. Similarly, the bi-view ground-truth probabilities are obtained by considering pairs of grasps. Each grasp pair is mapped to the nearest candidate view pair, and the counts are normalized over all candidate pairs to produce a probability ground-truth.

V-B

Geometry-size-adaptive grasp generation strategy

Scale-Adaptive Grasp Anchor

To improve adaptability to object size, we formulate the grasp wrist prediction as a relative pose
(\Delta t,\Delta r)
prediction with respect to a grasp anchor
g_{anchor}=(t_{anchor},r_{ancho})
, which is adapted to the size of the object.

\displaystyle t_{pred}

\displaystyle=t_{anchor}+\Delta t,\quad r_{pred}=r_{anchor}\oplus\Delta r.

(11)

The adaptive grasp anchor is determine dynamically according to the grasp view and object point cloud. Specifically, each predicted view intersects with minimum bounding sphere surface, producing an grasp anchor. This anchor-based formulation converts the original absolute pose prediction problem into a relative regression task, significantly reducing the search space of grasps and improve the adaptation to object scale.

Geometry-Adaptive Object Feature

To improve adaptability to object geometry, we extract local geometric features around each predicted grasp anchor, as global features may be dominated by irrelevant structures. Specifically, the global point cloud is first upsampled into
K_{p}
representative points using farthest point sampling (FPS). For each predicted grasp anchor, features are then aggregated from its
K_{c}
-nearest neighbors (KNN). This local feature
\mathbf{F}_{\text{local}}
captures fine-grained surface geometry around potential contact regions while filtering out distant or grasp-irrelevant structures, which facilitates geometry-adaptive grasp generation. Finally, the local features, view features, and global features are concatenated along the token dimension to form the final object feature representation.

\mathbf{F}_{ga-obj}=\operatorname{Concat}\big(\mathbf{F}_{\text{local}},\mathbf{F}_{\text{view}},\mathbf{F}_{\text{global}}\big).

(12)

Relative Dexterous Grasp Diffusion

We propose a relative dexterous grasp diffusion to generate bimanual relative wrist poses
(\Delta t^{bi},\Delta r^{bi})
and dexterous joint poses
q^{bi}
, based on the scale-adaptive grasp anchor
g_{anchor}=(t_{anchor},r_{anchor})
and geometry-adaptive object feature
\mathbf{F}_{ga-obj}
. We adopt the DDPM sampling process
[
11
]
, which can be formalized as follows:

\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathcal{G}_{0:T}^{\text{bi,rela}}\mid\mathbf{F}_{\text{ga-obj}})

\displaystyle=p(\mathcal{G}^{\text{bi,rela}}_{T})

(13)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_{\theta}\Big(\mathcal{G}^{\text{bi,rela}}_{t-1}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{G}^{\text{bi,rela}}_{t},\mathbf{F}_{\text{ga-obj}}\Big),

where
\mathcal{G}^{bi,rela}=(\Delta t^{bi},\Delta r^{bi},q^{bi})
, and
p(\mathcal{G}^{bi,rela})
is modeled as a Gaussian distribution. During training, we apply L2 losses separately to the translation, rotation, and joint parameter regressions. In addition, we incorporate a Chamfer loss
[

VI-A

Experiment settings

Data synthesis.

Data synthesis evaluation is conducted in MuJoCo simulation using the Shadow Hand. The tangential and torsional friction coefficients are set to 0.6 and 0.02, respectively, with gravity fixed at
9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^{2}}
and object density at
2.5\,\mathrm{kg/m^{3}}
. We randomly sample 1,000 objects from DexGraspNet and Objaverse, respectively, which is evaluated under six scaling factors (0.15 to 0.40 with a step size of 0.05), resulting in 12,000 unique scaled instances. For each instance, we synthesize five grasps, yielding a total of 60,000 grasps per method. To ensure consistency, the pre-grasp and squeeze poses for BimanGrasp are derived by translating the palm by
\pm 1,\mathrm{cm}
and perturbing the finger joints (excluding lateral abduction) by
\pm 0.1,\mathrm{rad}
.

Generation framework
. The generation framework evaluation is conduct on the subset of our tabletop dataset, which contains only objects from DexGraspNet. The dataset consists of 2,397 objects, which are randomly split into training and test sets with a 4:1 ratio, resulting in 3,112,117 training grasps. During evaluation, for each test object, we generate three grasps for every object pose and scale, yielding a total of 139,956 grasps, which are evaluated under the same simulator settings as the data generation pipeline.

VI-C

Evaluation metrics

Following
[
2
]
, we use these metrics to evaluate the data synthesis pipeline and generation framework.

Grasp Success Rate (SUC)
(%) measures the percentage of successful grasps in simulation. The hand executes a grasp from the pre-grasp pose
\mathcal{G}^{bi}_{pre}
to the squeeze pose
\mathcal{G}^{bi}_{squ}
. A grasp is considered successful if the object’s translation and rotation remain within 5 cm and 15° for at least 3s after execution. SUC is evaluated under three settings:

SUC-F
: force-closure success under external disturbances along six orthogonal directions;

SUC-L
: lift success in a tabletop scenario with a floating hand;

SUC-A
: lift success in a tabletop scenario with an arm-controlled hand.

Speed (S)
and
Success Generation Speed (SGS)
(grasps/s) measures the number of raw grasps and successful grasps synthesized per second, which is evaluated on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU, while the baseline speeds are cited from their original reports.

Penetration Depth (PD)
(unit: cm) measures the maximum intersection distance between the object and the hand.

Self-Penetration Depth (SPD)
(unit: cm) is defined as the maximum penetration distance among all hand collision meshes, including both intra- and inter-hand self-penetrations, while ignoring collisions between neighboring links.

Contact Distance Consistency (CDC)
(unit: cm) measures the range between the maximum and minimum signed contact distances across all fingers, reflecting the uniformity of finger–object contacts and being invariant to penetration.

First Variance Ratio (D)
(%) is the explained variance ratio of the first principal component from PCA, quantifying the concentration of bimanual grasp distributions.

VI-I

Real-World Experiments

In real world experiments, the robot hardware is set in two different hands, Briainco hand with Piper arm and Inspire hand with Untree G1 robots as shown in Figure
9
. We use a Realsense depth camera to capture RGB-D observations. Then the object mesh is reconstructed using Hyper3D
[

, with its scale determined by aligning the bounding box of the partial object point clouds. The reconstructed mesh is then sampled to obtain the object point cloud, which is fed into our generation framework to produce Shadow Hand poses. These poses are subsequently retargeted to the Briainco or Inspire hand using a hand-object consistent retargeting scheme. The robotic arms reach the dexterous wrists 6-DoF poses via motion planning. Further implementation details are provided in the supplementary material.

The experiment results in conducted on unseen real-world objects of various sizes and geometries, performing six grasp attempts for each object under different dexterous hands.

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we propose BiDexGrasp, a comprehensive solution to advance bimanual dexterous grasping through both large-scale dataset and learning-based framework. The proposed bimanual grasp synthesis pipeline can effectively produces physically feasible and high-quality grasp annotations via region-based initialization and decoupled force-closure optimization. Using this pipeline, we build a large-scale dataset containing 6,351 diverse objects and 9.7 million annotated bimanual grasps.
Based upon this dataset, we proposed a bimanual-coordinated and geometry-size-adaptive grasp generation framework that explicitly models inter-hand coordination and improves adaptability to object geometry and scale. Extensive experiments in simulation and real-world settings demonstrate that our data synthesis pipeline significantly improves efficiency and quality, while our generation framework achieves coordinated and high-success-rate bimanual dexterous grasps across diverse objects.
We hope BiDexGrasp will promote future research on scalable, generalizable, and physically grounded bimanual dexterous grasping and manipulation.
