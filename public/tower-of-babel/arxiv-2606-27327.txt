Gelfand-Shilov spaces and operators with ultradifferential weighted symbols on non-compact manifolds
Sandro Coriasco, Pedro Meyer Tokoro

Abstract
. We invariantly define Gelfand-Shilov spaces on classes of non-compact manifolds with a certain “structure at infinity”. We also construct and study a global calculus of pseudodifferential operators on such manifolds, locally defined by symbols satisfying estimates associated with weight sequences. The operators so obtained act naturally on the previously defined Gelfand-Shilov spaces. These generalise analogous functional spaces and operators defined on Euclidean spaces.

1.
Introduction

When studying properties of solutions of equations in distributional setting on a non-compact manifold,
one is interested, in addition to the local regularity, also to the behaviour in
neighbourhoods of points at infinity
,
a notion which has itself to be made precise. In the microlocal approach, this reflects into defining calculi of operators involving
symbols satisfying global estimates also in the space variable, in contrast to the usual requirements to the symbols employed
in the usual Hörmander’s pseudodifferential calculus on compact manifolds.

On
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, many calculi where the symbols satisfy global estimates exist,
within the so-called Weyl-Hörmander calculus, see
[
19
, Ch. XVIII]
.
Among them, well known ones are Shubin’s calculus and the SG-calculus, see
[
8
,
9
,
38
,
39
,
45
]
.
While the former does not have good invariance properties in general (see
[
23
]
for an invariantly defined
calculus on a manifold with ends
X
, which reduces to Shubin’s calculus when
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
), the latter can be invariantly
defined on a wide class of non-compact manifolds, the so-called SG-manifolds, see
[
43
]
.
Scattering manifolds (interior of a compact manifold with boundary with suitable metric structure close to the boundary, which represents “the points at infinity”) are
another relevant class of non-compact manifolds where the calculus of operators associated with local classical SG-symbols can be invariantly defined as well, see
[
35
,
37
]
. A further class of manifolds with similar properties is given by the
\mathcal{S}
-manifolds, see
[
9
, Ch. 4]
.
In these three cases, the informal phrase “at infinity” is replaced by precise boundary components in a compactified phase space, and/or by a specific structure outside
compact submanifolds, and/or by requirements on the change of coordinates for large values of the variables when unbounded charts domains are involved.
Ellipticity is then not only invertibility for large covectors over compact sets: it also includes invertibility at spatial infinity and, in the SG-classical case,
“compatibility at the corner”, where both
|x|
and
|\xi|
are large (see (
1.2
) and Sections
3.3
–
3.4
below).
In the sequel, we then focus on the SG-calculus and study its ultradifferential version on suitable
analytic non-compact manifolds. We now summarize in short only a few of the
main features of the basic SG-calculus and the associated scale of (weighted) Sobolev spaces.

Let
\langle\cdot\rangle=(1+|\cdot|)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For
m,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the symbol class
{\mathrm{SG}}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
consists of all
a\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

(1.1)

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}\,\langle x\rangle^{m-|\beta|}\langle\xi\rangle^{\mu-|\alpha|},\qquad x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}_{0},

see, e.g.,
[
9
,
38
]
for a detailed description of the associated calculus on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Thus the symbol has a two-components order:
the usual “differential order”
\mu
and
an “exit” (or spatial growth/decay) order
m
. The quantization is the usual one, given by

[{\mathrm{Op}}(a)u](x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\iint e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)u(y)\,{\mathrm{d}y}\,{\mathrm{d}\xi}=(2\pi)^{-n}\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}\xi},

initially on
u\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and then extended by duality to the whole
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Such operators are a closed class under composition and adjoint,
with asymptotic expansions formulae for the corresponding symbols completely analogous to those which hold true, for instance, for the standard Hörmander calculus.

Defining, for
s,\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the so-called Sobolev-Kato spaces (or
weighted Sobolev spaces
) as

H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):\langle{x}\rangle^{s}u\in H^{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\},

where
H^{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the usual Sobolev space of smoothness
\sigma
, equipped
with the naturally associated Hilbert norm, one has that any
a\in{\mathrm{SG}}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
gives a linear continuous operator

{\mathrm{Op}}(a)\colon H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to H^{s-m,\sigma-\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Notice that we have to topological equalities

\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\bigcap_{s,\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}}H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\bigcup_{s,\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}}H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

A symbol is SG-elliptic if, outside a compact subset of the phase space, we have

(1.2)

|a(x,\xi)|\geq C\,\langle x\rangle^{m}\langle\xi\rangle^{\mu},

for some constant
C>0
. Then there exists
b\in{\mathrm{SG}}^{-\mu,-m}
satisfying
{\mathrm{Op}}(a){\mathrm{Op}}(b)=I+R_{1}
and
{\mathrm{Op}}(b){\mathrm{Op}}(a)=I+R_{2}
, where the remainders
R_{1},R_{2}
have Schwartz kernels in
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
.

As recalled above, in
[
43
]
it is proved that the SG-calculus and the weighted spaces on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, as well as the analog of Schwartz’s spaces of functions and
distributions, can be invariantly defined on SG-manifolds.
The latter are non-compact manifolds with
a specific atlas structure,
compatible
with the SG-operators and the Schwartz’s spaces of functions and distributions structure (see
[
29
]
,

[
9
, Ch. 4]
and
[
35
]
for similar results on a class of manifolds with ends, on
\mathcal{S}
-manifolds, and on scattering manifolds,
respectively).
A relevant subclass of SG-manifolds are those which can be
regarded as the interior of compact manifolds with boundary. Manifolds
X
that admit such a compactification, which we denote by
\overline{X}
,
are of great interest in geometric analysis. In fact, under the suitable choices of a Riemannian metric
g
on
X
, these objects can model, among others,
manifolds with conical or cylindrical ends, and, more generally, asymptotically Euclidean manifolds
(see, e.g.
[
9
,
23
,
35
,
34
]
), as well as manifolds with conical, or more complicated, singularities
(see, e.g.,
[
15
,
26
,
28
,
31
,
33
,
32
,
44
]
).

The features of SG-calculus and its associated scale of weighted spaces allow to describe power-like decay properties, with respect to the weight
\langle\cdot\rangle
,
of solutions of equations in the temperate distributions space,
in addition to the usual smoothness ones, as well as the propagation of “decay and smoothness singularities” in terms of global wave-front sets
(see, e.g.,
[
12
,
10
,
35
]
). On Euclidean spaces, however, the Sobolev-Kato and/or Schwartz spaces, of course, are not the
only possible choices when one wants simultaneous
global control of decay and differentiability. In particular, interesting and widely investigated settings are the Gelfand–Shilov spaces
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\theta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
\theta>1
.
These may be described, in the so-called Roumieu form, as smooth functions satifying estimates of the type

|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}u(x)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(\alpha!\,\beta!)^{\theta},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

for
C,h>0
independent on
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
(see
[
16
]
). These spaces are Fourier invariant and encode Gevrey regularity together with subexponential decay at infinity.
In
[
6
]
, the authors developed a variant of the SG-calculus adapted to this setting, by replacing the pure polynomial derivative bounds
(
1.1
) with corresponding Gevrey-type estimates, namely

(1.3)

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(\alpha!\,\beta!)^{\theta}\langle x\rangle^{m-|\beta|}\langle\xi\rangle^{\mu-|\alpha|},\qquad x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}_{0},

which gives a global counterpart of the local calculus in Gevrey spaces introduced by Boutet de Monvel and Krée in
[
3
]
(see also
[
42
]
).

The resulting operators
{\mathrm{Op}}(a)
act continuously on
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\theta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and on its dual space of tempered ultradistributions.
Their hypoellipticity theory and SG-wavefront set
keep track, similarly as above, of ordinary microlocal regularity and loss of rapid decay at infinity. It is then natural to ask if families of Gelfand-Shilov spaces,
compatible with a suitable analog of the SG-operators subfamilies in (
1.3
), can also be invariantly defined on appropriate non-compact manifolds.
In this paper, we show that such question has a positive answer.

In particular, we study general ultradifferential SG-operators and we introduce and analyse in detail Gelfand-Shilov spaces of Roumieu and Beurling type, by means of the so-called weight sequences, extending the results obtained in
[
6
]
. Moreover, we show that these operators have an invariant meaning on an analytic subclass of the SG-manifolds.

We first recall some preliminary results in Section
2
, about weight sequences and functional spaces defined by means of them.
In Section
3
we introduce ultradifferential SG-symbols on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
associated with weight sequences, and prove that they provide operator algebras
closed under composition and adjoint. We also study hypoellipticity, prove the existence of the parametrix to hypoelliptic elements in the calculus,
describe the SG-classical subclasses on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and study the properties of the versions of global wave-front set adapted to the calculus.
Section
4
paves the ground to the extension to manifolds, with the proof of the invariance of the ultradifferential SG-operators
under suitable analytic compatible diffeomorphisms. The extension to manifolds is then performed in the subsequent Sections
5
,
6
,
and
7
, where SGA-manifolds are introduced, and the Gelfand-Shilov spaces and ultradifferentiable SG-calculus defined on them, respectively.
The concluding Section
8
is devoted to studying classical SG-operators on
analytic manifolds with ends.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall various concepts and results, which we will employ in the sequel.

2.1.
Weight sequences

Following Komatsu
[
22
]
, we consider classes of positive sequences, which will be involved
in the many definitions in the sequel.

Definition 2.1
.

A weight sequence is a sequence
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
of positive numbers such that:

(M0)

M_{0}=M_{1}=1
;

(M1)

M_{j}^{2}\leq M_{j-1}M_{j+1}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
;

(M2)^{\prime}

There exist
A,H\geq 1
such that
M_{j+1}\leq AH^{j}M_{j}
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
;

We say that a weight sequence is non-quasianalytic if it satisfies

(M3)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{j-1}}{M_{j}}<+\infty
.

Eventually, we may also need the following stronger conditions:

(M2)

There exist
A,H\geq 1
such that
\displaystyle M_{j}\leq AH^{j}\min_{0\leq k\leq j}\{M_{j-k}M_{k}\}
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
;

(M4)

\dfrac{M_{j-k}}{(j-k)!}\dfrac{M_{k}}{k!}\leq\dfrac{M_{j}}{j!}
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
k\leq j
.

It is clear that
(M2)
implies
(M2)^{\prime}
and
(M4)
implies
(M1)
.
We remark that
(M3)^{\prime}
is a weaker version of Komatsu’s condition
(M3)
, which will not be necessary in this work.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be a weight sequence. Then, we have
M_{k}M_{j-k}\leq M_{j}
, for all
j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that
k\leq j
.

Proof.

See
[
13
, Proposition 2.6]
.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{A}}={\{A_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be two weight sequences. We write
{\mathcal{A}}\preceq{\mathcal{M}}
if there exist
C,H>0
such that
A_{j}\leq CH^{j}M_{j}
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and
{\mathcal{A}}\prec{\mathcal{M}}
if, for every
H>0
, there exists
C>0
such that
A_{j}\leq CH^{j}M_{j}
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
satisfies
(M3)^{\prime}
. Then
\{j!\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\prec{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies
(M3)^{\prime}
, by
[
22
, Lemma 4.1]
, we have
j/m_{j}\to 0
,
j\to\infty
, where
m_{j}=M_{j}/M_{j-1}
. Hence, as in the beginning of the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 7.2]
, for every
H>0
, we have

\frac{j!}{H^{j}M_{j}}=\frac{1}{Hm_{1}}\cdot\frac{2}{Hm_{2}}\cdots\frac{j}{Hm_{j}}\to 0,

which implies our claim.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be a weight sequence and consider the associated sequence
{\{m_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
defined by
m_{0}=0
and
m_{j}=M_{j}/M_{j-1}
,
j\geq 1
. Then,
{\{m_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
is increasing and
m_{j}^{j}\geq M_{j}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, where we use the convention
m_{0}^{0}=1
.

Proof.

First, the fact that
{\{m_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
is increasing is equivalent to condition
(M1)
. Indeed, we have

m_{j+1}\geq m_{j}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \frac{M_{j+1}}{M_{j}}\geq\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}\ \Leftrightarrow\ M_{j-1}M_{j+1}\geq M_{j}^{2},

for every
j\geq 1
, and the case
j=0
is trivial. Also, since
m_{1}=1
, it follows
m_{j}\geq 1
for all
j\geq 1
. Then, for every
j\geq 1
we have

M_{j}=\frac{M_{1}}{M_{0}}\cdot\frac{M_{2}}{M_{1}}\cdots\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}=m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{j}\leq m_{j}^{j},

and the case
j=0
is trivial.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
{\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be a non-quasianalytic weight sequence satisfying
(M4)
. If
f\in{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
h(x)=\exp[f(x)]
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then
h\in{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proof.

See
[
14
, Lemma 6.9]
.
∎

2.2.
Ultradifferentiable functions

Let
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be a weight sequence and
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open subset. For each
h>0
and
K\subset\subset U
compact (that is, as usual,
K\subset U
and
K
is compact), we define the spaces

{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(K;h)=\{f\in C^{\infty}(U)\,:\,\sup_{x\in K}\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}h^{|\alpha|}M_{|\alpha|}^{-1}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)|<+\infty\},

and

{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(K;h)=\{f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(K)\,:\,\sup_{x\in K}\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}h^{|\alpha|}M_{|\alpha|}^{-1}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)|<+\infty\},

which are Banach spaces with the norm

\|f\|_{K,h}=\sup_{x\in K}\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}h^{|\alpha|}M_{|\alpha|}^{-1}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)|.

Then, we define

{\mathcal{E}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\mathrm{proj\,lim}}\ \underset{h>0}{\mathrm{ind\,lim}}\ {\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(K;h),\quad\mathcal{E}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\mathrm{proj\,lim}}\ \underset{h>0}{\mathrm{proj\,lim}}\ {\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(K;h),

{\mathcal{D}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\mathrm{ind\,lim}}\ \underset{h>0}{\mathrm{ind\,lim}}\ {\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(K;h),\quad{\mathcal{D}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(U)=\underset{K\subset\subset U}{\mathrm{ind\,lim}}\ \underset{h>0}{\mathrm{proj\,lim}}\ {\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(K;h).

We adopt the notation
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
when referring to both types of spaces. Clearly
{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
. If condition
(M3)^{\prime}
holds, we remark that the spaces
{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
are non-trivial, see
[
4
,
2
]
for the details.

The two following results are straightforward consequences of the properties of weight sequences. We refer the reader to
[
22
]
for the proofs.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}={\{A_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be two weight sequences.

(a)

If
{\mathcal{A}}\preceq{\mathcal{M}}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{\{{\mathcal{A}}\}}(U)\subset\mathcal{E}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(U)
and
\mathcal{D}_{\{{\mathcal{A}}\}}(U)\subset\mathcal{D}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(U)
.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{A}}\preceq{\mathcal{M}}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{({\mathcal{A}})}(U)\subset\mathcal{E}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(U)
and
\mathcal{D}_{({\mathcal{A}})}(U)\subset\mathcal{D}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(U)
.

(c)

If
{\mathcal{A}}\prec{\mathcal{M}}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{\{{\mathcal{A}}\}}(U)\subset\mathcal{E}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(U)
and
\mathcal{D}_{\{{\mathcal{A}}\}}(U)\subset\mathcal{D}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(U)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be a weight sequence. Then, the spaces
\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
and
\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
are closed under products and differentiation.

Example 2.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=\{j!^{s}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. If
s>1
, then
\mathcal{E}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(U)
is the Gevrey space
\mathcal{G}^{s}(U)
, and if
s=1
,
\mathcal{E}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(U)
is the space
C^{\omega}(U)
of real-analytic functions on
U
.

Consider also the spaces
{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(U)
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(U)
of
[{\mathcal{M}}]
-ultradistributions and compactly supported
[{\mathcal{M}}]
-ultradistributions, which are the topological duals of
{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
, respectively. Clearly
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(U)\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(U)
.

Every
f\in{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
can be regarded as an element in
{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(U)
, defining, as usual,

\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)\mapsto\langle f,\varphi\rangle=\int_{U}f(x)\varphi(x)\,{\mathrm{d}x}.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
V\subset U
be open sets. We say that
u\in{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(U)
is
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}
in
V
if there exists
f\in{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(V)
such that

\langle u,\varphi\rangle=\int_{V}f(x)\varphi(x)\,{\mathrm{d}x},\quad\forall\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(V).

We can now define the
[{\mathcal{M}}]
-singular supports for
[{\mathcal{M}}]
-ultradistributions.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(U)
. We say that
x\in U
does not belong to
[{\mathcal{M}}]\text{-\,}\mathrm{sing\,supp}
if there exists a neighbourhood
V\subset U
of
x
such that
u
is
\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}
in
V
.

Theorem 2.11
.

If
f\in\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
(respectively,
f\in\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
) and
g:V\to U
is an analytic map between open sets
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then
f\circ g\in\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(V)
(respectively,
f\circ g\in\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(V)
).

Proof.

See
[
18
, Proposition 8.4.1]
.
∎

By the previous theorem, we can define the spaces of ultradifferentiable functions on analytic manifolds. We recall that a manifold is analytic if it admits an atlas whose coordinate changes are analytic maps. Recalling Whitney
[
46
]
, every smooth manifold admits an analytic structure,
compatible with its smooth structure. Then, from now on, let
X
be a paracompact manifold with an analytic countable atlas
\{(\Omega_{k},\varphi_{k})\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
.

Definition 2.12
.

We define
\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)
as the space of all
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
such that, for every local chart
(\Omega_{k},\varphi_{k})
, the function
f\circ\varphi_{k}^{-1}
belongs to
\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U_{k})
, where
U_{k}=\varphi_{k}(\Omega_{k})\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

By the paracompactness, we can also assume that
\Omega_{k}
has compact closure and we can extend the domains of each chart to a slightly larger open set.

2.3.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Given a weight sequence
\{{\mathcal{M}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and
h>0
, we define

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h)=\{f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\,:\,\|f\|_{h}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}h^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}M_{|\alpha|}^{-1}M_{|\beta|}^{-1}|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)|<+\infty\},

which is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{h}
. Then, we define

\mathcal{S}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\underset{h>0}{\mathrm{ind\,lim}}\ {\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h),\quad\mathcal{S}_{({\mathcal{M}})}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\underset{h>0}{\mathrm{proj\,lim}}\ {\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h).

The inclusion maps
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h_{+})
are continuous when
h\leq h_{+}
and compact when
h<h_{+}
. In particular,
\mathcal{S}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\mathcal{S}_{({\mathcal{M}})}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
are DFS and FS spaces, respectively. The proofs of the next two Propositions
2.13
and
2.14
are analogous to those found in
[
22
]
for the spaces of ultradifferential functions. For the sake of brevity, we omit them.

Proposition 2.13
.

If
{\mathcal{A}}\preceq{\mathcal{M}}
, then
\mathcal{S}_{\{{\mathcal{A}}\}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset\mathcal{S}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and

2.4.
Weight functions

Following Braun, Meise, and Taylor
[
4
]
and Petzsche and Vogt
[
40
]
, we consider a useful class of increasing functions.

Definition 2.19
.

We say that an increasing map
\omega:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a weight function if it satisfies the following conditions:

(W1)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\geq 1}\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\omega(\varepsilon t)}{\varepsilon\omega(t)}<+\infty
;

(W2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}<+\infty
;

(W3)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\dfrac{\omega(t)}{\log(t)}=+\infty;

(W4)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\omega(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t<+\infty
.

Notice that condition
(W1)
is exactly the Petzsche and Vogt’s condition
(\alpha_{1})
, which is stronger than Braun, Meise, and Taylor’s condition
(\alpha)
.

Given a non-quasianalytic weight sequence
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
, consider the associated function
\omega_{\mathcal{M}}
given by
\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(0)=0
and

(2.1)

\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)=\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\log\left(\frac{t^{j}}{M_{j}}\right),\quad t>0.

By
[
2
, Proposition 13]
and
[
40
, Lemma 5.5]
, if
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies also
(M2)
and
(M4)
, then
\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)
is a weight function. We also have that
\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)
is continuous, non-negative, monotonically increasing, and
e^{\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)}
grows faster than any polynomial.

Proposition 2.20
.

For every
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
, we have

(1)

e^{-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(2t_{1})}e^{-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(2t_{2})}\leq e^{-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{1}+t_{2})}
;

(2)

e^{\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{1})}e^{\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{2})}\leq Ae^{\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(H(t_{1}+t_{2}))}
, where
A,H\geq 1
are given by condition
(M2)
.

Proof.

See
[
21
, Proposition 1]
.
∎

As shown in

Proposition 2.21

For each

\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h,\varepsilon)=\{f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\,:\,\|f\|_{h,\varepsilon}<+\infty\},

Then, we have

\mathcal{S}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\underset{h,\varepsilon>0}{\mathrm{ind\,lim}}\ \mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h,\varepsilon),\quad\mathcal{S}_{({\mathcal{M}})}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\underset{h,\varepsilon>0}{\mathrm{proj\,lim}}\ \mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h,\varepsilon).

We have that

Remark 2.23

In particular, the Fourier transform is an automorphism of

Given two non-negative functions

and

if there exists

Proposition 2.24
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be a non-quasianalytic weight sequence satisfying
(M2)
and
(M4)
, and
\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)
be the associated weight function. Then, there exists a weight function
\omega_{0}(t)
such that

(i)

For every
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
, we have

\omega_{0}(t_{1}+t_{2})\leq\omega_{0}(2t_{1})+\omega_{0}(2t_{2}).

(ii)

There exists
C>0
such that, for every
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
,

\omega_{0}(t_{1})+\omega_{0}(t_{2})\leq\omega_{0}(2H(t_{1}+t_{2}))+C,

where
H\geq 1
is the constant from condition
(M2)^{\prime}
;

(iii)

\omega_{0}\asymp\omega_{\mathcal{M}}
;

(iv)

Given
\delta>0
, there exist
C,h>0
(respectively, for every
h>0
, there exists
C>0
) such that

|\partial_{t}^{k}\omega_{0}(t)|\leq Ch^{k}M_{k}\omega_{0}(t),

for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
t>\delta
. In particular,
\omega_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}((0,+\infty))
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\mathbb{R}})
be a non-negative function such that
\mathrm{supp}(\chi)\subset[-1,1]
and
\chi\equiv 1
in
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
. Then, consider

\omega_{0}(t)=\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Applying the logarithm on Proposition
2.20
, we obtain

\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{1})+\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{2})\leq\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(H(t_{1}+t_{2}))+\log(A)

and

\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{1}+t_{2})\leq\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(2t_{1})+\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(2t_{2}),

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
, where
A,H\geq 1
are given by property
(M2)^{\prime}
.

(i)
Given
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(t_{1}+t_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{1}+t_{2}+1-s)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}\left(t_{1}+\frac{1-s}{2}+t_{2}+\frac{1-s}{2}\right)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(2t_{1}+1-s)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(2t_{2}+1-s)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}(2t_{1})+\omega_{0}(2t_{2}).

(ii)
Given
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(t_{1})+\omega_{0}(t_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{1}+1-s)+\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t_{2}+1-s)\right)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(H(t_{1}+t_{2}+2-2s))\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\log(A)\int_{-1}^{1}\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}\left(H(t_{1}+t_{2})+\frac{1-s}{2}+\frac{(2H-1)(1-s)}{2}\right)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{0}(2H(t_{1}+t_{2}))+C,

where

C_{0}=\log(A)\int_{-1}^{1}\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{and}\quad C=\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}((2H-1)(1-s))\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{0}.

(iii)
We have

\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)\leq\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)=\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)\chi(s)+\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)(1-\chi(s)),

for all
s\in[-1,1]
. Then

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)(1-\chi(s))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{0}(t),

for all
t\geq 0
, noticing that
1-\chi(s)
vanishes for
|s|\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)\chi(s)\geq 0
.

On the other hand, by
(ii)
and property
(W1)
of
\omega_{\mathcal{M}}
, we can obtain
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+2)\int_{-1}^{1}\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}(s)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(2t)+\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(4)\right)\int_{-1}^{1}\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)+C_{2},

for all
t\geq 0
. Hence
\omega_{0}\asymp\omega_{\mathcal{M}}
.

(iv)
Finally, by the properties of the convolution, condition
(W2)
, and the fact that
\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\mathbb{R}})
, we have that there exist
C_{1},h>0
(respectively, for every
h>0
, there exists
C_{1}>0
) such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{k}\omega_{0}(t)|\leq\

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1-s)|\partial_{s}^{k}\chi(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t+1)Ch^{k}M_{k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}h^{k}M_{k}(\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(t)+\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(1))\leq Ch^{k}M_{k}\omega_{0}(t),

for

and

not depending on

3.
A global calculus of SG-symbols of ultradifferential type on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Let
{\mathcal{A}}={\{A_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}={\{B_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be two non-quasianalytic weight sequences. Given
m,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
h>0
, we denote by
{\mathrm{SG}}_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h)
the space of all
p\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
such that

\|p\|_{h}=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}h^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{-\mu+|\alpha|}\langle x\rangle^{-m+|\beta|}}{A_{|\alpha|}B_{|\beta|}}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(x,\xi)|<+\infty,

which is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{h}
. Then, we define

{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{\{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}\}}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\underset{h>0}{\mathrm{ind\,lim}}\ {{\mathrm{SG}}}_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h),\quad{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\underset{h>0}{\mathrm{proj\,lim}}\ {{\mathrm{SG}}}_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};h).

In a completely similar fashion, it is possible to define the wider symbol class
{\mathrm{ST}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(cf.
[
9
, Ch. 1, §1.1]
, of which

{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a subclass, see Section
3.2
below.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
m,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, a symbol
p\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
defines a continuous operator
\mathrm{Op}(p):\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
via the usual left-quantization

(3.1)

\mathrm{Op}(p)u(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}p(x,\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi,\quad u\in\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

where
\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\mathrm{d}\xi
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{B}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a bounded subset, that is, there exist
C,h>0
such that

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u(x)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}M_{|\alpha|}M_{|\beta|},

for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
and
u\in\mathscr{B}
. Then, given
u\in\mathscr{B}
, Leibniz rule and integration by parts yield

\displaystyle x^{\beta}D_{x}^{\alpha}[\mathrm{Op}(p)u](x)

\displaystyle=x^{\beta}\sum_{\gamma\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(D_{x}^{\gamma}e^{i\xi\cdot x})(D_{x}^{\alpha-\gamma}p(x,\xi))\widehat{u}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(D_{\xi}^{\beta}e^{i\xi\cdot x})\xi^{\gamma}(D_{x}^{\alpha-\gamma}p(x,\xi))\widehat{u}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\sum_{\gamma\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\gamma}\sum_{\delta\leq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\delta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}(D_{x}^{\alpha-\gamma}D_{\xi}^{\beta-\delta}p(x,\xi))D_{\xi}^{\delta}(\xi^{\gamma}\widehat{u}(\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi.

Now, notice that, for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}(D_{x}^{\alpha-\gamma}D_{\xi}^{\beta-\delta}

\displaystyle p(x,\xi))D_{\xi}^{\delta}(\xi^{\gamma}\widehat{u}(\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle^{-2N}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}[(D_{x}^{\alpha-\gamma}D_{\xi}^{\beta-\delta}p(x,\xi))D_{\xi}^{\delta}(\xi^{\gamma}\widehat{u}(\xi))]\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi.

By the Lebniz rule and the multinomial theorem, given

\displaystyle(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}(fg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\ell}(-1)^{\ell}\Delta_{\xi}^{\ell}(fg)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\ell}(-1)^{\ell}(\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{2}+\cdots+\partial_{\xi_{n}}^{2})^{\ell}(fg)

By the multinomial theorem, one can easily check that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{\beta^{\prime}\leq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\leq\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\gamma^{\prime}}|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{x}^{\beta^{\prime}}\partial_{y}^{\gamma^{\prime}}q(x,y,\xi)||\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{x}^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\partial_{y}^{\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}}p(x,y,\xi)|

Theorem 3.22

Let

(3.15)

\mathrm{Op}(p)u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\xi\cdot(x-y)}p(x,y,\xi){u}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pty\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi,\quad u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

\mathrm{Op}(p)

\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

Proof.

Let

and

. Integrating by parts, we obtain that

(3.16)

\langle\xi\rangle^{-2N_{1}}\langle x-y\rangle^{-2N_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\xi\cdot(x-y)}(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N_{1}}(1-\Delta_{y})^{N_{2}}(p(x,y,\xi)u(y))\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pty\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi.

(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N_{1}}(1-\Delta_{y})^{N_{2}}(p(x,y,\xi)u(y))

(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N_{1}}\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{1}}p(x,y,\xi)\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{2}}u(y),\quad|\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}|\leq 2N_{2}.

For such terms, given

\displaystyle|(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N_{1}}\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{1}}p(x,y,\xi)\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{2}}u(y)|

\displaystyle=\langle y\rangle^{-2N_{3}}|(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N_{1}}\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{1}}p(x,y,\xi)\langle y\rangle^{2N_{3}}\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{2}}u(y)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2N_{1}+2N_{2}+2N_{3}}A_{2N_{1}}B_{2N_{2}}B_{2N_{3}}

\displaystyle\times\langle\xi\rangle^{\kappa(2N_{2})}\langle(x,y)\rangle^{\lambda(2N_{1})}\langle y\rangle^{-N_{3}},

where we used Lemma

|\gamma_{1}|\leq 2N_{2}
and that the function

in non-decreasing. Then, for each

C,h>0
(respectively, for every

, there exists
C>0
) such that

\displaystyle|(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N_{1}}(1-\Delta_{y})^{N_{2}}(p(x,y,\xi)u(y))|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2N_{2}}Ch^{2N_{1}+2N_{2}+N_{3}}A_{2N_{1}}B_{2N_{2}}B_{2N_{3}}

\displaystyle\times\langle\xi\rangle^{\kappa(2N_{2})}\langle(x,y)\rangle^{\lambda(2N_{1})}\langle y\rangle^{-2N_{3}},

where the factor

can be incorporated into

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle(x,y)\rangle^{\lambda(2N_{1})}\langle y\rangle^{-2N_{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pty

\displaystyle=\langle x\rangle^{\lambda(2N_{1})+n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle y\rangle^{\lambda(2N_{1})}(1+\langle x\rangle^{2}|y|^{2})^{-N_{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.49994pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pty

where we used the substitution

(respectively, for every

3.3.
Hypoellipticity and parametrices

The definitions of ellipticity and hypoellipticity for ultradifferential SG-calculus are the same of the standard one.

Definition 3.24
.

We say that a symbol

such that

(3.19)

|p(x,\xi)|\geq C\langle\xi\rangle^{\mu}\langle x\rangle^{m},

for all

.

We say that a symbol

,

(respectively, for every
h>0
, there exists
C>0

(3.21)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}p(x,\xi)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}A_{|\alpha|}B_{|\beta|}|p(x,\xi)|\langle\xi\rangle^{-|\alpha|}\langle x\rangle^{-|\beta|},

for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
and

.

Remark 3.26
.

Notice that every elliptic symbol is hypoelliptic. Indeed, condition (

and

.
Also, if

(respectively, for every

\displaystyle=Ch^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}A_{|\alpha|}B_{|\beta|}\langle\xi\rangle^{\mu}\langle x\rangle^{m}\langle\xi\rangle^{-|\alpha|}\langle x\rangle^{-|\beta|}

Theorem 3.28

By Theorem

If a symbol

4.

In this and in the subsequent section, for
\varphi:V\to U

\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any

in place of

. We begin with an extension to the ultradifferentiable setting of the well-known result, due to Schrohe
[

change of variables between local charts of analytical versions of SG-manifolds or manifolds with ends (see the subsequent Section

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\varphi:V^{\sharp}\to U^{\sharp}
be an analytic diffeomorphism between open subsets

and suppose that the two following conditions hold:

(I1)

there exist
C,h>0
(respectively, for every
h>0
, there exists
C>0
) such that

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha|}\alpha!\langle x\rangle^{1-|\alpha|},\ x\in V^{\sharp},\quad\text{and}\quad|\partial_{y}^{\alpha}\varphi^{-1}(y)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha|}\alpha!\langle y\rangle^{1-|\alpha|},\ y\in U^{\sharp},

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.

(I2)

there exist open subsets
U,V
of
U^{\sharp},V^{\sharp}
, respectively, such that
the restriction
\varphi|_{V}
is a diffeomorphism between
V
and
U
and there exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that

B(x;\varepsilon\langle x\rangle)\subset V^{\sharp},\ \forall x\in V,\quad\text{and}\quad B(y;\varepsilon\langle y\rangle)\subset U^{\sharp},\ \forall y\in U.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}={\{A_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}},{\mathcal{B}}={\{B_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\preceq{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be three non-quasianalytic weight sequences and let
p\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}^{m,\mu}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

m,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
, be such that

. Consider the operator

\tilde{P}\tilde{u}(x)=[\mathrm{Op}(p)u](\varphi(x)),\quad\tilde{u}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(V),\ \mathrm{supp}(\tilde{u})\subset V.

Then, there exists a symbol
q\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}^{m,\mu}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

such that
\tilde{P}=\mathrm{Op}(q)+R
, where

[{\mathcal{M}}]
-regularizing operator.

Before proving Theorem
4.1

Lemma 4.2

Given

\varepsilon\in(0,1)

define

\nabla\varphi(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{\prime}(y+t(x-y))\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\varphi^{\prime}
is the Jacobian matrix of
\varphi

(a)

\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)=\nabla\varphi(x,y)\cdot(x-y)

(b)

there is a constant

such that
\nabla\varphi(x,y)
is invertible for

(c)

there is a function

such that

\chi(x,y)\equiv 1,\text{ if }|x-y|\leq\frac{k}{2}\langle x\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\chi(x,y)\equiv 0,\text{ if }|x-y|>k\langle x\rangle.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of
[
43

. Point (a) follows from the mean value theorem, while point (b) follows from the fact that

\nabla\varphi(x,x)=\varphi^{\prime}(x)
and
(I1)
holds. Finally, for point (c), we choose a function

such that
g(t)=1

if

and
g(t)=0
if

\chi(x,y)=g\left(\frac{|x-y|}{k\langle x\rangle}\right)

satisfies the desired properties.
∎

With
\chi(x,y)
as in Lemma
4.2
, write

p(x,\xi)=\chi(x,y)p(x,\xi)+(1-\chi(x,y))p(x,\xi)=p_{1}(x,y,\xi)+p_{2}(x,y,\xi).

It is clear that

\mathrm{Op}(p_{2})=\tilde{p}(x,D)+R
,
where

Lemma 4.4

For every fixed

(respectively, for every

First, by Peetre’s inequality, we have that

(a) Write

,

,
\ell=1,\dots,n
, and
\sigma\in S_{n}
. Let
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}

Given

, we also see that

\partial_{x_{j}}\det\varphi^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\varphi_{1}^{(\sigma(1))}\cdots\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\sigma(\ell))}\cdots\varphi_{n}^{(\sigma(n))},

and

By induction, it follows that

\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\varphi_{1}^{(\sigma(1))}\cdots\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{n}}\varphi_{n}^{(\sigma(n))},

with

. Hence, we conclude

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\det\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\leq C_{0}h_{0}^{|\alpha|}\alpha!\langle x\rangle^{-|\alpha|},

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and

, where

and

.
From this estimate and (
4.1
), we obtain

such that

for all

(b) Using the notation

from the previous point, by

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\sigma(\ell))}(x+t(y-x))\,\mathrm{d}t\right|

For the

(c) Fix

By the chain rule,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{j}}p_{1}^{\sharp}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial p_{1}}{\partial X_{k}}\frac{\partial X_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial p_{1}}{\partial Y_{k}}\frac{\partial Y_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial p_{1}}{\partial\Xi_{k}}\frac{\partial\Xi_{k}}{\partial x_{j}},

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial p_{1}}{\partial Y_{k}}\frac{\partial Y_{k}}{\partial y_{j}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial p_{1}}{\partial\Xi_{k}}\frac{\partial\Xi_{k}}{\partial y_{j}},

is proved.

Admissible analytic manifolds

In this section we introduce the class of analytic manifolds which admit atlases compatible with the ultradifferential extensions
of the
{\mathrm{SG}}
-calculus constructed and studied in the previous sections, adapting the approach in
[
43
]
.

Let
X
be a
n
-dimensional manifold. Let
\{\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}\}_{k=1}^{N}
be a finite open cover of
X
, and let

\varphi_{k}^{\sharp}:\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}\to U_{k}^{\sharp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be homeomorphisms.
We say that
\{(\Omega_{k},\varphi_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}

has a good shrinking
if there is another finite open cover
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
of
X

with
\Omega_{k}\subset\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}
,
k=1,\dots,N
, with homeomorphisms

\varphi_{k}=\varphi_{k}^{\sharp}|_{\Omega_{k}}:\Omega_{k}\to U_{k}\subset U_{k}^{\sharp}
, and a constant
\delta>0
such that

B(y,\delta\langle y\rangle)\subset U_{k}^{\sharp},\quad\forall y\in U_{k},\ k=1,\dots,N.

We say that an atlas
\mathscr{A}
for
X
is admissible, or
\mathrm{SGA}
-compatible
, if the three following conditions hold:

(SGA1)

\mathscr{A}
is finite and analytic;

(SGA2)

\mathscr{A}
has a good shrinking;

(SGA3)

the changes of coordinates among the local charts of
\mathscr{A}
satisfy
(I1)
from Theorem
4.1
.

If conditions
(SGA1)-(SGA3)
hold, then the changes of coordinates will satisfy condition
(I2)
from Theorem
4.1
, too,
see
[
43
, Lemma 3.3]
. For simplicity, we will write
\varphi_{k}
instead of
\varphi_{k}^{\sharp}
.

We say that
X
is an SGA-manifold if it admits an
\mathrm{SGA}
-compatible atlas. Trivially,
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is an SGA-manifold.
In the next subsection we present a relevant subclass of SGA-manifolds. In the next section, we show that also on the so-called

scattering (or asymptotically Euclidean) manifolds
(see
[
35
]
) the same construction is possible, of course
under the hypotheses that the underlying manifold us analytic.

5.1.
Manifolds with ends

Manifolds with ends are a well-known class of non-compact manifolds (see, e.g.,
[
33
,
35
,
29
,
43
]
), admitting

{\mathrm{SG}}
-admissible atlases. We here show that they also admit SGA-compatible atlases.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional analytic manifold that can be decomposed into

(5.1)

X=X_{0}\cup E,

where:

(1)

X_{0}
is a
n
-dimensional, compact, submanifold of
X
with boundary
\partial X_{0}
;

(2)

the boundary
\partial X_{0}
is a (
(n-1)
-dimensional, analytically embedded) connected submanifold of
X
;

(3)

E
is a
n
-dimensional submanifold with boundary that is analytically diffeomorphic to
[1,+\infty)\times\partial X_{0}
,
its boundary being identified with
\{1\}\times\partial X_{0}
;

(4)

\cup
in (
5.1
) means gluing by identification along the boundaries.

Then,
X
is called a manifold with one cylindrical end.

Figure 1:
A manifold with one cylindrical end.

Let
\mathscr{A}=\{(\Omega_{k}^{\sharp},\varphi_{k})\}_{k\in K}
be an analytic atlas on
X
and
\mathscr{A}_{0}=\{(\Omega_{k}^{\sharp},\varphi_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
be a finite subset of
\mathscr{A}
consisting of local charts that cover the compact submanifold with boundary
X_{0}\cup\{\partial X_{0}\times[1,3]\}
. By shrinking the domains if necessary, we can assume that the
\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}
are relatively compact.

Since
X_{0}
is embedded, for each
p\in\partial X_{0}
, we can find
k\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
p\in\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}
, and a subset
\Omega_{k}^{0}\subset\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}
such that the restriction
\varphi_{k}|_{\Omega_{k}^{0}\cap\partial X_{0}}
is a chart for
\partial X_{0}
. In this way, we can construct an atlas for
\partial X_{0}
, which is finite and analytic, in view of the fact that
\partial X_{0}
is compact and the embedding in
X
is analytic. We denote this atlas by
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}=\{(\tilde{\Omega}^{\sharp}_{k},\tilde{\varphi}_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N_{0}}
, for a suitable
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Employing the atlas
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
on
\partial X_{0}
, we can introduce coordinates on the interior of
E
, identified with
(1,+\infty)\times\partial X_{0}
, as follows:

\psi_{k}:(1,+\infty)\times\tilde{\Omega}_{k}^{\sharp}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\psi_{k}(t,y)=(t,t\varphi_{j}(y)),k=1,\dots,N_{0}.

By compactness, for each
k=1,\dots,N_{0}
, we can obtain an open subset
\tilde{\Omega}_{k}\subset\tilde{\Omega}_{k}^{\sharp}
such that
\{\tilde{\Omega}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{0}}
still covers
\partial X_{0}
and each
\tilde{U}_{k}^{\sharp}=\tilde{\varphi}_{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{k}^{\sharp})
contains an
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of
\tilde{U}_{k}=\tilde{\varphi}_{k}(\tilde{\Omega}_{k})
, for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
. Then,
(2,+\infty)\times\tilde{\Omega}_{k}
is a
good shrinking
of
(1,+\infty)\times\tilde{\Omega}_{k}^{\sharp}
. For the remaining charts, we obtain the
good shrinking
by using compactness arguments. Hence, we have proved that
the constructed atlas fulfills properties (SGA1) and (SGA2).

Arguing as in
[
43
, Example 3.4]
, if
y=(\tilde{\varphi}_{j}\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{k}^{-1})(x)
is a change of coordinates on
\partial X_{0}
, the corresponding coordinate changes on
E
are of the form

(t,ty)\mapsto(t,x(y)t),

and the corresponding derivatives satisfy (SGA3), where the
\langle x\rangle
-estimates are given by the derivatives on
t
and the
constants
C,h>0
comes from the analyticity of
x(y)
. The unbounded charts on
E
are called
exit charts
.

Finally, if
\varphi_{k}
is a bounded analytic chart and
\psi_{\ell}
is a coordinate map on the end
E
, in the intersection of the domains, which is contained in
E
, we can write
\varphi_{k}=(\varphi_{k,1},\varphi_{k,2})
, where
\varphi_{k,1}
and
\varphi_{k,2}
are analytic in an open subset of
\mathbb{R}
and
\partial X_{0}
, respectively. Hence, it is clear that
\varphi_{k}\circ\psi_{\ell}^{-1}
and
\psi_{\ell}\circ\varphi_{k}^{-1}
are analytic. Since the intersection of the domains in this case is bounded, the requested estimates trivially hold true in this case as well. Then, (SGA3) also holds, and the constructed atlas is SGA-compatible.

The construction above extends to
n
-dimensional analytic manifolds
X
that can be decomposed as a union

(5.2)

X=X_{0}\cup E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{m},

where
X_{0}
is a
n
-dimensional, compact submanifold with boundary such that

\partial X_{0}=\partial E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\partial E_{m},

where the boundary components
\partial E_{1},\cdots,\partial E_{m}
are disjoint, analytically embedded, compact, connected,
(n-1)
-dimensional submanifolds, each
E_{\ell}
is
analytically diffeomorphic to
[1,+\infty)\times\partial E_{\ell}
, its boundary identified with
\{1\}\times\partial E_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,\dots,m
, and
\cup

in (
5.2
) means gluing along the corresponding boundary components. In this case, we say that
m
is the number of ends of
X
.

5.2.
Partition of unity

We show how to construct a suitable partition of unity for an admissible atlas
\mathscr{A}=\{(\Omega_{k}^{\sharp},\varphi_{k}^{\sharp})\}_{k=1}^{N}
,
compatible with the analytic structure and the ultradifferential extension of the SG-calculus.

With
{\mathcal{B}}={\{B_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
a non-quasianalytic weight sequence, choose a function
\Phi\in\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{B}}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\int\Phi=1
.
For each
\varepsilon>0
, we set

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\Phi(x/\varepsilon).

Then, each
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
also belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{B}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and satisfies
\int\Phi_{\varepsilon}=1
. Given an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a constant
\delta>0
, denote by
U_{\delta}
the set
\bigcup_{x\in U}B(x,\delta\langle x\rangle)
, where
B(x,\delta\langle x\rangle)
is the open ball centered in
x\in U
with radius
\delta\langle x\rangle
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open subset and
\chi_{U}
be its characteristic function. Define
\Phi_{U}:(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Phi_{U}(\varepsilon,x)=\chi_{U}*\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{U}(y)\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Then, the following properties hold true:

(a)

for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
,
\Phi_{U}(\varepsilon,\cdot)\in\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{B}}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0\leq\Phi_{U}(\varepsilon,x)\leq 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(b)

\Phi_{U}(\varepsilon,0)=0
, if
\mathrm{dist}(x,U)\geq\varepsilon\langle x\rangle
;

(c)

\Phi_{U}(\varepsilon,x)=1
, if
B(x,\varepsilon\langle x\rangle)\subset U
;

(d)

for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
C,h>0
(respectively, for every
h>0
, there exists
C>0
) such that

(5.3)

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Phi_{U}(x)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha|}B_{|\beta|}\langle x\rangle^{-|\beta|},

for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.

Proof.

Point (a) is consequence of the regularizing property of the convolution, while Points (b) and (c) are straightforward.
Concerning Point (d), since
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
is compactly supported, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Phi_{U}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{U}(y)|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|\int_{\text{supp}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y))}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)|,

for all
\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
, where
C_{\varepsilon}>0
is the volume of the support of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
.
Hence, it is enough to show that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
5.3
).
Recall that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
decays exponentially. Then, for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, we have that

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Phi_{\varepsilon}|\leq\langle x\rangle^{-|\beta|}
outside a fixed compact set independent of
\beta
, while inside such compact set we have

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Phi_{\varepsilon}|\leq Ch^{|\beta|}B_{|\beta|},

since
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{B}}]}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Combining both estimates, we obtain (
5.3
).
∎

Given an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a constant
\varepsilon>0
, set
U_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{x\in U}B(x,\varepsilon\langle x\rangle)
.
The next Corollary
5.3
is a straightforward consequence of Lemma
5.2
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\Phi(x)=\Phi_{U_{\varepsilon}}(\varepsilon,x)
, for some open subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a constant
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, the following properties hold true:

(a)

\Phi\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{0,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0\leq\Phi\leq 1
;

(b)

\Phi\equiv 1
on
U
and
\Phi\equiv 0
outside
U_{2\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
X
be a manifold with an SGA-admissible atlas
\mathscr{A}=\{(\Omega_{k}^{\sharp},\varphi_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
.
Then, there exists a partition of unity
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
of
X
, subordinate to the cover
\{\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}\}_{k=1}^{N}
,
such that, in local coordinates, it holds

(5.4)

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\Phi_{k})_{*}(x)|\leq Ch^{|\beta|}B_{|\beta|}\langle x\rangle^{-|\beta|},

that is,
(\Phi_{k})_{*}\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{0,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where

(\Phi_{k})_{*}(x)=\begin{cases}\Phi_{k}(\varphi_{k}^{-1}(x)),&x\in U_{k}^{\sharp}\\
0,&x\notin U_{k}^{\sharp},\end{cases}

Proof.

For each
k=1,\dots,N
, define on
U_{k}^{\sharp}
the function
\Psi_{k}(x)=\Phi_{(U_{k})_{\varepsilon}}(\varepsilon,x)
,
where
\varepsilon=\delta/3
. Here,
\delta>0
is the constant from condition
(SGA2)
. Then, using the local charts, set

\Psi_{k}^{*}(x)=\begin{cases}\Psi_{k}(\varphi_{k}(x)),&x\in\Omega_{k}^{\sharp},\\
0,&x\notin\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}.\end{cases}

and, for each
k=1,\dots,N
, define

\Phi_{k}:X\to\mathbb{R}\colon x\mapsto\Phi_{k}(x)=\frac{\Psi_{k}^{*}(x)}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\Psi_{\ell}^{*}(x)}.

Notice that each
\Phi_{k}
is well-defined, since the denominator is always bigger than
1
. Moreover,
it is clear, by the definition of
\Phi_{k}
, that
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Phi_{k}=1
. Moreover,
0\leq\Phi_{k}\leq 1
, by Corollary
5.3
, (b).
Then,
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
is a partition of unity subordinated to
\{\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}\}_{k=1}^{N}
. Finally, (
5.4
)
is proved employing condition
(I1)
and Corollary
5.3
, (a).
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
the partition of unity constructed in Theorem
5.4
. Then, there exist functions
\{\Theta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
such that:

(a)

0\leq\Theta_{k}\leq 1
,
\Theta_{k}\equiv 1
on
\mathrm{supp}(\Phi_{k})
, and
\Theta_{k}\equiv 0
outside
\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}
;

(b)

In local coordinates,
\Theta_{k}
belongs to
{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{0,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that is, satisfies estimates of the form (
5.4
).

In particular,
\Theta_{k}\Phi_{k}=\Phi_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,N
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to that of
[
43
, Lemma 3.10]
.
∎

6.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces on SGA-manifolds

Let
X
be a manifold with SGA-admissible atlas
\mathscr{A}=\{(\Omega_{k}^{\sharp},\varphi_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
. For each
k=1,\dots,N
,
write
U_{k}^{\sharp}=\varphi_{k}(\Omega_{k}^{\sharp})
. Also, let
{\mathcal{A}}={\{A_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}},{\mathcal{B}}={\{B_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}},{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be three non-quasianalytic weight sequences
such that
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{M}}
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
m,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. We say that a function
p\in C^{\infty}(U_{k}^{\sharp}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}(U_{k})
if,
for every
\Phi\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{0,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\subset U_{k}^{\sharp}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, setting
\tilde{p}(x,\xi)=\Phi(x)p(x,\xi)
,
we have
\tilde{p}\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In this case, we define

{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}(X)=\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{N})\,:\,p_{k}\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}(U_{k}),\ k=1,\dots,N\}.

By Theorem
2.11
, the symbol classes
{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}(X)
are well defined.

Let
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
be a
good shrinking
of the cover
\mathscr{A}
and write
\varphi_{k}(\Omega_{k})=U_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Its SGA-admissible structure, described in the previous section, allows us to (invariantly) define Gelfand-Shilov spaces on the manifold
X
.

Definition 6.2
.

For each
h>0
, we define
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X;h)
as the space of all
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
such that

\|f\|_{h}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|f\circ\varphi_{k}^{-1}\|_{h,U_{k}}<+\infty,

where

(6.1)

\|f\|_{h,U_{k}}=\sup_{x\in U_{k}}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(f\circ\varphi_{k}^{-1})(x)|}{h^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}M_{|\alpha|}M_{|\beta|}}<+\infty,\quad k=1,\dots,N.

We have that each
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X;h)
is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{h}
. Then, we define

{\mathcal{S}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(X)=\underset{h>0}{\mathrm{ind\,lim}}\,{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X;h),

{\mathcal{S}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(X)=\underset{h>0}{\mathrm{proj\,lim}}\,{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X;h).

Remark 6.3
.

The spaces
{\mathcal{S}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)
are well-defined as a consequence of condition
(I1)
and the Faà di Bruno formula. We clearly recover the usual Gelfand-Shilov spaces when
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

As in the case when
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one can show that the inclusion map
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X;h)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X;h_{+})
is continuous if
h\leq h_{+}
and compact if
h<h_{+}
. This gives us that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(X)
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(X)
are, respectively, DFS and FS spaces. We denote by
{\mathcal{S}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(X)
the topological dual of
{\mathcal{S}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)
. Notice that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}^{\prime}(X)
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}^{\prime}(X)
are FS and DFS spaces, respectively.

Clearly we have

\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)\subset\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)\subset\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(X)\subset\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(X)\subset\mathcal{D}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}^{\prime}(X).

Alternatively, in view of Proposition
2.21
, one can define the spaces
{\mathcal{S}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(X)
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(X)
as the inductive and projective limits, respectively, of the Banach spaces

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X;h,\varepsilon)=\{f\in C^{\infty}(X)\,:\,\|f\|_{h,\varepsilon}<+\infty\},\quad h,\varepsilon>0,

where

\|f\|_{h,\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sup_{x\in U_{k}}\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}\frac{e^{\omega_{{\mathcal{M}}}(\varepsilon|x|)}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(f\circ\varphi_{k}^{-1})(x)|}{h^{|\alpha|}M_{|\alpha|}}.

6.1.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces on analytic manifolds with ends

Observe that every manifold with ends (as defined in the previous section) can be identified with the interior of a compact manifold with boundary. For this particular class of manifolds, we can obtain an alternative characterization of
\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)
.

Let
{\overline{X}}
be a compact manifold with boundary
\partial X
and assume that the weight sequence
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
satisfies also
(M2)
and
(M4)
. In this case, we can also assume that the weight function
\omega_{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies the properties of
\omega_{0}
in Proposition
2.24
.

Definition 6.4
.

A boundary defining function is a smooth function
\varrho:\overline{X}\to[0,+\infty)
such that
\varrho^{-1}(0)=\partial X
and
\mathrm{d}\varrho_{y}\neq 0
for every
y\in\partial X
.

Similarly to the analogous result proved in
[
11
]
in the Gevrey setting, we now construct an ultradifferentiable boundary defining function for analytic manifolds with boundary.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
{\overline{X}}
be a smooth compact manifold with boundary endowed with a finite analytic atlas
\{(\Omega_{k},\varphi_{k})\}_{i=1}^{N}
.
Given a non-quasianalytic sequence
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
, there exists a boundary defining function
\varrho:{\overline{X}}\to[0,+\infty)
that belongs to
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\overline{X}})
.

Proof.

In an interior chart
(\Omega_{k},\varphi_{k})
, we just set
\varrho_{k}\equiv 1
. In a boundary chart
(\Omega_{k},\varphi_{k})
, with
\varphi_{k}(\Omega_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[0,+\infty)
, we consider
\varrho_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{n}
. Then,
\varrho_{k}=0
in
\partial X
and
\varrho_{k}>0
outside
\partial X
. Let
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
be a partition of unity of order subordinated to the cover
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
, such that each
\Phi_{k}
belongs to
{\mathcal{D}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\overline{X}})
. Since each
\varrho_{k}
is analytic on its domain,

\varrho(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Phi_{k}(x)(\varrho_{k}\circ\varphi_{k}^{-1})(x)

belongs to
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}({\overline{X}})
. Moreover,
\mathrm{d}\varrho
does not vanish in any
y\in\partial X
as shown in the proof of
[
27
, Proposition 5.41]
.
∎

Then, close to
\partial X
, the boundary defining function induces coordinates
(\varrho,y)
on a collar neighbourhood of the boundary
C=[0,\delta)\times\partial X
, for some
\delta>0
. Here, we take
\delta>0
to be sufficiently small such that
C
is contained in the union of the exit charts.

Then, in analogy with Melrose’s definition of the space
\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(X)
(see
[
35
]
), we define

(6.2)

\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(X)=\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}e^{-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(\varepsilon/\varrho)}{\mathcal{E}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}({\overline{X}})\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(X)=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}e^{-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(\varepsilon/\varrho)}{\mathcal{E}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}({\overline{X}}).

Hence, if
{\overline{X}}
is an analytic manifold with ends as in the previous section, by radial compactification, we can see
X
as the interior of a compact manifold with boundary
{\overline{X}}
. Moreover, the boundary defining function constructed in Proposition
6.5
induces the diffeomorphism
E\simeq(\delta,+\infty)\times\partial X
as required in Definition
5.1
. Indeed, notice that the transformation
t=1/\varrho
induces a diffeomorphism
C\setminus\partial X\simeq(1/\delta,+\infty)
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
X
be a manifold with ends equipped with a SGA-atlas and
{\mathcal{M}}={\{M_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
be a non-quasianalytic weight sequence. Then
\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)=\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)
.

Proof.

We just need to check the behaviour of the functions in the exit charts, where we have coordinates of the form
(t,ty)
, where
t>\delta
and
y\in\tilde{U}_{k}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
, for
k=1,\dots,N
and
\delta\geq 1
. Here,
t=1/\varrho
and
\tilde{U}_{k}
is a local chart of the closed manifold
\partial X_{0}
. By the compactness of
\partial X_{0}
, we can assume that each
\tilde{U}_{k}
is bounded. In this case, we have

Remark 6.7
.

There are different definitions of manifold with corners, see
[
36
]
, and, for instance,
[
20
,
30
]
.
Adopting, as above, an approach based on local charts, let
Y
be a
n
-dimensional paracompact Hausdorff space.

A
n
-dimensional chart with corners (of codimension
k
) on
Y
is a pair
(U,\phi)
, where
U
is an open subset of
[0,+\infty)^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}

for
0\leq k\leq n
, and
\phi\colon U\to\phi(U)\subset Y
is a homeomorphism. If
k=1
, we obtain a usual “boundary chart”.
Defining compatibility between charts and an atlas of charts, the definitions of manifold with boundary and manifold with corner are obtained.

Observe that, for every manifold with corners
Y
of dimension
n
, there exists a
n
-dimensional smooth manifold
\widetilde{Y}
without boundary
such that
Y\subset\widetilde{Y}
and the interior of
Y
is open in
\widetilde{Y}
. Then, all smooth objects and structures on
Y
are defined by restriction
to
Y
of the analogous ones on
\widetilde{Y}
.

Assume
Y
to be compact and analytic, and that there is a finite collection of analytic functions on
Y
,
\{\varrho_{j}\}_{j\in J}
, called boundary defining functions,
such that
Y=\cap_{j\in J}\{p\in\widetilde{Y},\varrho_{j}(p)\geq 0\}
, and at every point where
\varrho_{k}=0
for every
k\in K\subset J
,
the differentials of such
\varrho_{k}
are linearly independent. In particular,
d\varrho_{j}\not=0
when
\varrho_{j}=0
.
Then, it is possible to work in local coordinates
x\colon p\mapsto(\varrho_{1},\dots,\varrho_{k},x_{1},...,x_{n-k})(p)
, where
k
is the number of boundary defining functions.
In
[
20
, Remark 2.11]
these are called compact manifolds with embedded corners. By adapting
[
20
, Proposition 2.15]
to the analytic case,
taking into account our argument in Proposition
6.5
above, it follows that, locally, a boundary defining function in
\mathcal{E}_{[\mathcal{M}]}(Y)

always exists, and the property that all corners are embedded implies, as in the smooth case, that a global boundary defining function in that same space
exists as well.

Hence, definition (
6.2
) could be extended, in principle, to an analytic manifold with corners
Y
. Clearly, it would be interesting
also to consider spaces of the types

\displaystyle\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(Y)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}e^{-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(\varepsilon/\varrho_{1})+\dots-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(\varepsilon/\varrho_{k})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\{{\mathcal{M}}\}}(\overline{Y})

and

\displaystyle\dot{{\mathcal{E}}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(Y)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}e^{-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(\varepsilon/\varrho_{1})+\dots-\omega_{\mathcal{M}}(\varepsilon/\varrho_{k})}{\mathcal{E}}_{({\mathcal{M}})}(\overline{Y}).

The study of Gelfand-Shilov spaces in this more complicated geometric situation will appear elsewhere.

The calculus of ultradifferential SG-operators on SGA-manifolds

Let
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
be a partition of unity as constructed in Theorem
5.4
and consider the family
\{\Theta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}

as in Corollary
5.5
. If
P:\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)\to\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)
is a linear operator, we can write

P=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Theta_{k}P\Phi_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1-\Theta_{k})P\Phi_{k}.

Definition 7.1
.

We say that a linear operator
P:\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)\to\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(X)
is a (ultradifferential SG-)pseudodifferential operator on
X

with symbol
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{N})\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}(X)
if
(1-\Theta_{k})P\Phi_{k}
has ultradistributional kernel
in
\mathcal{S}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
, and, in local coordinates,
(\Theta_{k}P\Phi_{k})_{*}=(\Theta_{k})_{*}\mathrm{Op}(p_{k})(\Phi_{k})_{*}
,
k=1,\dots,N
.

Remark 7.2
.

The definition of a pseudodifferential operator with symbol in
{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}(X)
does not depend on the choice of the cut-off functions

\Theta_{k}
. Indeed, if
\{\tilde{\Theta}_{k}\}
has the same properties, then
\Theta_{k}-\tilde{\Theta}_{k}
vanishes on the support of

\Phi_{k}
and we can apply Proposition
3.18
. Similarly, the definition does not depend on the chosen partition of unity or on the choice
of the SGA-admissible atlas.

We can then apply the local results proved above to extend to
X
the properties of ultradifferential SG-operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
p\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m,\mu}(X)
and
q\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}(X)
.
Then
\mathrm{Op}(p)\mathrm{Op}(q)
is a pseudodifferential operator with symbol
s\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}]}^{m^{\prime\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}(X)
,
with
m^{\prime\prime}=m+m^{\prime}
and
\mu^{\prime\prime}=\mu+\mu^{\prime}
, that has asymptotic expansion of the form (
3.17
) in local coordinates.

Now, let us assume that
X
is endowed with a Riemannian metric
g
. In each local chart
U_{k}
, we can write

g=\sum_{\ell,\nu=1}^{n}g_{\ell,\nu}^{(k)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x_{\ell}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{\nu},

where the coefficients
g_{\ell,\nu}^{(k)}(x)
are smooth functions on
U_{k}
satisfying
g_{\ell,\nu}^{(k)}(x)=g_{\nu,\ell}^{(k)}(x)
. Moreover, we can assume that the coefficients
g_{\ell,\nu}^{(k)}
belong to
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}(U)
. This is possible since every smooth manifold admits an analytic metric by Grauert’s Theorem
[
17
]
, in addition to Proposition
2.3
, since
{\mathcal{M}}
is non-quasianalytic.

Remark 7.4
.

If
X
is the interior of an analytic compact manifold with boundary
{\overline{X}}
, we can procceed as in
[
11
]
and use the ultradifferentiable boundary defining function
\varrho
constructed in Proposition
6.5
and a ultradifferentiable partition of unity to obtain an ultradifferentiable scattering metric on
X
, that is, a Riemannian metric
g
that has coefficients in
{\mathcal{E}}_{[{\mathcal{M}}]}
in any analytic chart and has the form

g=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\varrho^{2}}{\varrho^{4}}+\dfrac{g^{\prime}}{\varrho^{2}}

in a collar neighbourhood of
\partial X
, where
g^{\prime}
restricts to a metric in
\partial X
.

Suppose also that
X
is orientable. In this case, we can assume that the matrix
\left[g_{\ell,\nu}^{(k)}(x)\right]
is positive definite for every
x\in U_{k}
. Hence
g
induces a volume form
\mathrm{d}V
on

that is given by

g^{(k)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{n}

[

3.16

∎

8.
Ultradifferential SG-classical operators on analytic manifolds with ends

In this concluding section we shortly discuss how, on the subclass of admissible analytic manifolds introduced in Section
5.1
,
even the calculus of ultradifferential SG-classical operators, having local symbols in the classical subclasses described in Section
3.4
,
can be invariantly defined.

This follows by arguments similar to those employed to prove the analogous result in the standard SG-classical case on the manifolds
with ends studied in
[
29
]
by employing
admissible charts
of the form
\varphi_{j}
described in Section
5.1
.
Indeed, it is not restrictive to assume that the charts
\varphi_{j}
take value in
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
so that the change of coordinates on the end
E\simeq[1,+\infty)\times\partial X_{0}
are actually of the polar coordinates form

F\colon\rho\omega\equiv(\rho,\rho\omega)\mapsto(\rho,\rho\phi(\omega))\equiv\rho\phi(\omega),\quad\rho\in(1,+\infty),\;\omega,\phi(\omega)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

like those employed in
[
29
]
. This can be achieved, for instance, by covering
\partial X_{0}
with (a finite number of) counterimages, via the
homeomorphisms
\varphi_{j}^{-1}
, of open balls in
\varphi_{j}(\Omega_{j})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with suitably small radius, and then mapping such balls
to the interior of the upper semisphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
by stereographic projection. This preserves the analiticity of the change of coordinates

\phi\colon\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Now, consider an operator
A={\mathrm{Op}}(a)
with
a\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}],\mathrm{cl}}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
supported in the image of an exit chart of
E
. If we consider
the pull-back
B=F^{*}A
by the change of coordinates introduced above, we find
B={\mathrm{Op}}(b)
with
b\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}],\mathrm{cl}}^{m,\mu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Indeed, that
B
is still a SG-ultradifferential operator with the same order components follows by the invariance results in Section
4
.
That
b
is also SG-classical follows by iterating the analogous argument in
[
29
]
. Indeed, the mentioned result implies that
b
admits
a homogeneous principal triple
(b_{\psi},b_{e},b_{\psi e})
, obtained by
(a_{\psi},a_{e},a_{\psi e})
via the same transformation laws proved there.
Since

a_{m-1,\mu-1}=a-(\chi_{\xi}a_{\psi}+\phi_{x}a_{e}-\chi_{\xi}\phi_{x}a_{\psi e})\in{{\mathrm{SG}}}_{[{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}],\mathrm{cl}}^{m-1,\mu-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

we can apply the same result to
a_{m-1,\mu-1}
, obtain a corresponding principal homogeneous triple of orders
m-1,\mu-1
,
and iterate the procedure an arbitrary number of times.
The two sequences of homogeneous components with respect to
\xi
and to
x
thusly obtained provide the sequences needed in Definition

3.37
, proving that
b
is also SG-classical, as claimed.

Moreover, the transformation laws of
a_{\psi},a_{e},a_{\psi e}
, given in
[
29
]
, show that these objects have an invariant meaning, as
local representations of functions on suitable vector bundles. We collect the consequences of the above results in the next Theorem
8.1
,
whose remaining proof details we leave for the reader.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
