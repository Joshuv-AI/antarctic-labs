Asymptotic Results for Spectrally Positive Compound Poisson Processes.
Zhi-Hao Cui Hao Wu

Abstract
Finite excursions away from zero of a spectrally positive compound Poisson process with a negative drift can always be decomposed into two parts lying above and below zero, respectively. This paper is concerned with the asymptotic relationships among the lengths and heights of these two parts. Our results state that both their lengths and heights are asymptotically strongly dependent and exhibit a scale symmetry. MSC 2020 subject classifications: Primary 60G51, 60F17; secondary 60B10. Keywords and phrases: compound Poisson process, regular variation, conditional limit theorem.

Consider a spectrally positive compound Poisson process
X=\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
with negative drift and Laplace exponent given by

\displaystyle\psi(\lambda):=\log\mathbf{E}[\exp\{-\lambda X_{1}\}]=b\lambda+\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\lambda x}-1)\,\nu(dx),\quad\lambda\geq 0,

The function

(0,\infty)

\Phi(q)=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\psi(\lambda)=q\}

For

Additionally, we also consider the local maximum and local minimum during the time

\displaystyle H_{0}:=\sup_{0\leq s\leq T_{0}}X_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad-\widehat{H}_{0}:=\inf_{0\leq s\leq T_{0}}X_{s}.

Theorem 1.2

The following hold as

(1)

\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{+}>t)=\mathbf{P}(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>t)\sim\dfrac{1}{b\Gamma(\rho)}\cdot\dfrac{1}{t\Phi(1/t)};

(2)

\mathbf{P}(H_{0}>h)=\mathbf{P}(\widehat{H}_{0}>h)\sim b^{-1}\,\Gamma(2-\alpha)\,\Gamma(\alpha-1)\cdot h\overline{\nu}(h)
.

{T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}}

.
To formulate it, we need to define two non-negative functions

\displaystyle f(x):=\frac{x^{\rho-1}}{\Gamma(\rho)}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\rho)}\frac{\sin(\pi\rho)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{(x-u)^{\rho-1}(1+u)u^{\rho-1}}{u^{2\rho}-2u^{\rho}\cos(\pi\rho)+1}du

(1.2)

\displaystyle g(x):=\frac{\alpha-1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha-1)}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha-2}ds\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha-2}(x+1-xt-s)^{1-\alpha}dt.

(1.3)

Theorem 1.3

For any
a>0
, the following hold as
t,h\to\infty
:

(1)

\mathbf{P}\big(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>at\,|\,\tau_{0}^{+}>t\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(\tau_{0}^{+}>at\,|\,T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>t\big)\to a^{\rho-1}+1-\Gamma(\rho)f(a)\in(0,1);

(2)

\mathbf{P}\big(H_{0}>ah\,|\,\widehat{H}_{0}>h\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(\widehat{H}_{0}>ah\,|\,H_{0}>h\big)\to g(a)\in(0,1)
.

In particular, Theorem
1.3
with
a=1
tells that
2-\Gamma(\rho)f(1)\in(0,1)
, which implies that the two events

T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>t
and
\tau_{0}^{+}>t
are asymptotically dependent and enjoy the asymptotic symmetry in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Big(\frac{T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}}{t}\,\Big|\,\tau_{0}^{+}>t\Big)\sim\mathbf{P}\Big(\frac{\tau_{0}^{+}}{t}\,\Big|\,T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>t\Big).

Analogous asymptotic dependence also can be observed between
H_{0}>h
and
\widehat{H}_{0}>h
.

In the extreme value theory, the well-known one-big-jump principle states that for two weakly dependent heavy-tailed random variables
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
, we have as

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\xi_{1}\vee\xi_{2}>x)\sim\mathbf{P}(\xi_{1}>x)+\mathbf{P}(\xi_{2}>x).

Comparing to this principle, a direct consequence of the preceding theorems further identifies that the two events
T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>t
and
\tau_{0}^{+}>t
are strongly dependent. The same is also true for the events
H_{0}>h
and
\widehat{H}_{0}>h
.

Corollary 1.4

The following hold as
t,h\to\infty
:

(1)

\mathbf{P}\big(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>t\,|\,\tau_{0}^{+}\vee(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+})>t\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(\tau_{0}^{+}>t\,|\,\tau_{0}^{+}\vee(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+})>t\big)\to\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(\rho)f(1)}\in(\frac{1}{2},1);

(2)

\mathbf{P}\big(H_{0}>h\,|\,\widehat{H}_{0}\vee H_{0}>h\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(\widehat{H}_{0}>h\,|\,\widehat{H}_{0}\vee H_{0}>h\big)\to\dfrac{1}{2-g(1)}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.

Theorem 1.6

The following hold as

(1)

Theorem 1.7

For any

(1)

\mathbf{P}\big(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>at\,|\,\tau_{0}^{+}>t,\tau_{0}^{+}<\infty\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(\tau_{0}^{+}>at\,|\,T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>t,\tau_{0}^{+}<\infty\big)\to(1+a)^{1-\theta}\in(0,1);

Corollary 1.8

The following hold as
t,h\to\infty

(1)

\mathbf{P}\big(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>t\,|\,\tau_{0}^{+}\vee(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+})>t\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(\tau_{0}^{+}>t\,|\,\tau_{0}^{+}\vee(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+})>t\big)\to\dfrac{1}{2-(1+a)^{1-\theta}}\in(\frac{1}{2},1);

Corollary 2.2

For any

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{-q_{1}\tau_{0}^{+}-q_{2}(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+})\};\tau_{0}^{+}<\infty\big]=1-b^{-1}\frac{q_{1}-q_{2}}{\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{2})}.

where

\displaystyle m(u):=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{-\mathrm{Im}D(-u+i\varepsilon)}{\pi}=\frac{\sin(\pi\rho)}{\pi}\frac{(1+u)u^{\rho-1}}{u^{2\rho}-2u^{\rho}\cos(\pi\rho)+1}.

Therefore, for any
q>0
,

\displaystyle D(q)=\Gamma(\rho+1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(s)/\Gamma(\rho)}{(q+s)^{\rho+1}}ds,

(3.11)

where

\displaystyle h(s):=\frac{\sin(\pi\rho)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(s-u)^{\rho-1}(1+u)u^{\rho-1}}{u^{2\rho}-2u^{\rho}\cos(\pi\rho)+1}du.

By (

), it follows from the uniqueness of the generalized Stieltjes transform that

\displaystyle d(1,s)=s^{\rho-1}/\Gamma(\rho)+h(s)/\Gamma(\rho)

Recall that

is decreasing in

and the function in the right-hand side of (

3.12
) holds for any
s>0
.
Finally, we show that
a^{\rho-1}+1-\Gamma(\rho)f(a)=1-h(a)\in(0,1)
.
Using

\displaystyle\frac{(1+u)u^{\rho-1}}{u^{2\rho}-2u^{\rho}c+1}\leq u^{-\rho},\quad u>0,

we obtain

0<h(a)<\frac{\sin(\pi\rho)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{a}(a-u)^{\rho-1}u^{-\rho}du=1,

which completes the proof.

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta t\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}\int_{0}^{\beta t}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>(1-\varepsilon)t)\overline{\nu}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\beta t}\frac{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}{(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\beta t\,\overline{\nu}((1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\beta t)}\overline{\nu}(x)dx.

As in the proof of the first claim, the latter limit vanishes, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Hence

\int_{0}^{h}\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{0}>h)\overline{\nu}(x)dx=o(h\overline{\nu}(h)),

as
h\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
.
For the second term, using (
3.6
) again, we immediately obtain that as
h\to\infty
,

\int_{h}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{0}>h)\overline{\nu}(x)dx=\int_{h}^{\infty}\overline{\nu}(x)dx\sim h\overline{\nu}(h)/(\theta-1).

Combining these two results together, we have
proved the desired result.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.7

Proof of Theorem
1.7
(1).

By Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\big(T_{0}-\tau_{0}^{+}>at\,|\,\tau_{0}^{+}>t,\tau_{0}^{+}<\infty\big)=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at)dx\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\nu(dy+x)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\overline{\nu}(y)dy}.

(3.21)

On the one hand, from the proof of Theorem
1.6
(1), as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\overline{\nu}(y)dy\sim\frac{\beta t\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}{\theta-1}.

(3.22)

On the other hand, the numerator of (
3.21
) can be decomposed into the next two terms

\displaystyle\iint_{H_{1}}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at)\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\nu(dy+x)dx\quad\text{and}\quad\iint_{H_{2}}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at)\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\nu(dy+x)dx

(3.23)

where with
\delta\in(0,\beta)
,

H_{1}:=\big\{(x,y)\,|\,x>(\beta+\delta)at,\ y>(\beta+\delta)t\big\}\ \text{and}\ H_{2}:=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}/H_{1}.

The first term can be bounded below and above by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{(\beta+\delta)at}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at)\cdot\mathbf{P}_{(\beta+\delta)t}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\cdot\int_{(\beta+\delta)(1+a)t}^{\infty}\overline{\nu}(x)dx\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{(\beta+\delta)(1+a)t}^{\infty}\overline{\nu}(x)dx,

respectively.
From (
3.19
), we know that as
t\to\infty
,
\mathbf{P}_{(\beta+\delta)at}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at)\cdot\mathbf{P}_{(\beta+\delta)t}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\to 1
. Hence, by (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta t\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}\iint_{H_{1}}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at)\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\nu(dy+x)dx

(3.24)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta t\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}\int_{(\beta+\delta)(1+a)t}^{\infty}\overline{\nu}(x)dx=\frac{(1+a)^{-\theta+1}}{\theta-1}.

(3.26)

We now consider the second term in (
3.23
). Observe that

H_{2}\subset H_{21}\cup H_{22},

where

\displaystyle H_{21}:=\big\{(x,y)\in\big(0,(\beta-\delta)at\big)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\ \text{or}\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\big(0,(\beta-\delta)t\big)\big\}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle H_{22}:=\big\{(x,y)\in\big((\beta-\delta)at,(\beta+\delta)at\big)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\ \text{or}\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\big((\beta-\delta)t,(\beta+\delta)t\big)\big\}.

From Lemma
3.2
(i), we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta t\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}\iint_{H_{21}}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at)\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\nu(dy+x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\lim_{t\to\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\,\overline{\nu}(y)dy+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at\big)\,\overline{\nu}(x)dx\Big)=0.

Furthermore, by Proposition 1.5.10 of
[
4
, p.27]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta t\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}\iint_{H_{22}}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>at)\cdot\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\,\nu(dy+x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta t\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}\Big(\int_{(\beta-\delta)at}^{(\beta+\delta)at}\overline{\nu}(x)dx+\int_{(\beta-\delta)t}^{(\beta+\delta)t}\overline{\nu}(y)dy\Big)=0.

These two estimates imply that the second term in (
3.23
) is
o(\beta t\cdot\overline{\nu}(\beta t))
as
t\to\infty
and then
\delta\to 0+
.
Combining this with (
3.22
), (
3.24
) and substituting into (
3.21
) yields the desired result.

\Box
