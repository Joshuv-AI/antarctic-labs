Efficacy and safety of SarogLItazar as an add-on medication to Metformin and lifestyle intervention in women with PolyCystic Ovary Syndrome (SLIM-PCOS trial): study protocol for a randomized, open-label, parallel group clinical trial

Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEmerging concepts on the role of insulin resistance (IR) in the pathogenesis of polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) have driven a shift in the therapeutic approach towards interventions targeting IR. SarogLItazar as an add-on to Metformin in PolyCystic Ovary Syndrome (SLIM-PCOS) trial aims to test the efficacy and safety of saroglitazar, a dual peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR α/γ) agonist belonging to “glitazar” class of drugs, on top of metformin and lifestyle intervention in PCOS.MethodsSLIM-PCOS is an investigator-initiated, randomized, open label, parallel group, superiority, clinical trial. Around 140 PCOS patients will be randomized in 1:1 ratio to either of the two study groups: Group A: tablet saroglitazar (4 mg daily) + tablet metformin (500 mg twice daily) + lifestyle intervention; and Group B: tablet metformin + lifestyle intervention. Two-tailed P-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.DiscussionSLIM-PCOS trial will provide evidence on the potential therapeutic application of saroglitazar in PCOS. Background

Background and rationale

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common endocrine disorders affecting women in the reproductive age group [1]. WHO estimates that 8 to 13% of women in the reproductive age group are affected worldwide with PCOS while approximately 70% cases remain undiagnosed [2]. The global incidence of PCOS was reported as 1.55 million cases in 2017 representing a 4.47% rise from 2007 to 2017 [3]. In India, the prevalence of PCOS was 19.6% (by Rotterdam 2003 criteria) in a national cross-sectional survey conducted from 2018 to 2022 [4].

The pathognomic features of PCOS include hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction (manifested as menstrual irregularities and oligo-anovulation) and morphological polycystic ovaries [5]. The syndrome is often accompanied by metabolic abnormalities such as obesity, dyslipidemia and insulin resistance (IR) in a significant proportion of patients [6]. Besides being the most frequent cause of anovulatory infertility, PCOS is associated with increased long-term risk of co-morbidities such as type 2 diabetes mellitus, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), premenopausal cancer, premature vascular aging and cardiovascular complications [7–10].

Insulin resistance (IR) has been identified as a major hallmark in the pathogenesis of PCOS, which can aggravate hyperandrogenism. Hyperinsulinemia associated with IR contributes to increased LH-induced ovarian androgen synthesis and suppressed hepatic production of sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG) with a consequent rise in free testosterone levels [6]. Evolving insights into the pathogenesis of PCOS have highlighted a pivotal role of IR (approximately 35 to 80% frequency in PCOS) as a predisposing factor for hyperandrogenism, reproductive function abnormalities and metabolic derangements, thereby underscoring the need to manage IR in patients with PCOS [11–13].

The treatment approach in PCOS is often symptom and context-driven and comprises of lifestyle management and pharmacotherapy such as combined oral-contraceptive pills, ovulation inducing agents and anti-androgens [14, 15]. However, emerging concepts revolving around the role of IR in the pathogenesis of PCOS have driven a shift in the therapeutic approach for PCOS towards interventions targeting IR. Several systematic reviews and meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials have demonstrated the effectiveness of insulin sensitizers (metformin and thiazolidinediones) and other anti-diabetics like glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonists (GLP1RAs), dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors (DPP4i) and sodium glucose co-transporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) in modifying the metabolic features of PCOS (IR, obesity and impaired glucose metabolism), regularizing menstrual cycles and inducing ovulation [16–26].

Metformin, a biguanide anti-diabetic agent, is commonly prescribed off-label in PCOS. However, the multifaceted profile of this complex syndrome renders the role of metformin debatable [27]. Hence, evaluating the effect of metformin in combination with other anti-diabetic drugs is a research area of focus in recent times. Many studies have flaunted superior effects of metformin combination therapy with thiazolidinediones [28–30], DPP-4 inhibitors [31, 32] and GLP-1RAs [33–37] on insulin resistance, reproductive and metabolic profile in PCOS patients compared to metformin alone.

Saroglitazar, a novel anti-diabetic drug, belongs to the “glitazars” family of anti-diabetic drugs which are dual peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR α/γ) agonists. It is the first drug in this class to obtain regulatory approval in India for the treatment of diabetic dyslipidemia, hypertriglyceridemia in type 2 diabetes patients, as add-on to metformin in type 2 DM and in non-cirrhotic nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) [38–40]. By virtue of being a potent dual protein activator (PPAR α/γ), saroglitazar possesses suitable characteristics to treat many features of metabolic dysfunction such as IR, dyslipidemia, obesity as well as cardiovascular disorders. Hence, the therapeutic potential of saroglitazar in the management of PCOS, a disorder characterized by metabolic dysfunction and long term complications of type 2 DM, NAFLD and cardiovascular risk, is an area demanding investigation. Extensive literature search could not reveal any study on the role of saroglitazar in PCOS per se although a phase 2 trial of saroglitazar for the treatment of NAFLD in women with PCOS was recently completed with results not yet available [41]. With this background, the present study aims to study the efficacy, safety and effect on quality of life with saroglitazar as add-on to metformin compared to metformin alone in women having PCOS.

Choice of comparator

Metformin is commonly used off-label in PCOS. Lifestyle intervention (dietary modification and physical activity) forms a major cornerstone in the management of PCOS. Metformin and lifestyle intervention will be administered in the comparator group while the intervention group will receive the investigational drug, saroglitazar as add-on to metformin and lifestyle intervention.

Methods

Trial design

SLIM-PCOS (SarogLItazar as an add-on to Metformin in PolyCystic Ovary Syndrome) is an investigator-initiated, phase 2, randomized, open label, parallel group, controlled clinical trial (Fig. 1). The study adopts a superiority framework, aiming to demonstrate that saroglitazar combined with metformin and lifestyle intervention is more effective than metformin and lifestyle intervention in improving the primary outcome of change in serum sex hormone binding globulin levels at 24 weeks from baseline.

An overview of SLIM-PCOS trial design

Study setting

The trial will be conducted collaboratively by the departments of Pharmacology and Obstetrics & Gynecology of Pandit Bhagwat Dayal Sharma Postgraduate Institute of Medical Sciences (PGIMS), Rohtak, Haryana. Our public tertiary care hospital caters to the population of Rohtak and adjacent districts in Haryana. Around 10–20% patients in a routine Gynaecology outpatient department (OPD) suffer from PCOS and present with disease symptoms such as hirsutism, menstrual irregularities, obesity and other metabolic complications.

Informed consent

Potential participants will be approached for the SLIM-PCOS trial by their clinical care team and study investigators at the time of their presentation to Gynecology OPD. The patients will be explained about the study and upon their subsequent clinic visit (baseline visit), written informed consent will be taken by the investigator or any delegate after resolving any queries from the patients. Further, the eligibility of the patients for the trial will be ascertained by the investigators.

Intervention and comparator

Description of intervention and comparator

Participants will be prescribed a 24 week course of treatment and 12 week post treatment follow-up. The details of interventions are as follows:

Intervention group (Group A): Saroglitazar + metformin + lifestyle intervention

Intervention group (Group A): Saroglitazar + metformin + lifestyle intervention

Comparator group (Group B): Metformin + lifestyle intervention

Comparator group (Group B): Metformin + lifestyle intervention

Tablet saroglitazar magnesium 4 mg will be administered once daily in the morning before breakfast. Tablet metformin 500 mg will be administered twice daily. There are no specific safety precautions or safety concerns associated with the administration of these drugs. Postponement of medication intake is acceptable within the same day, however for any missed dose/s on a day, it should not be compensated with an extra dose and the treatment should be continued on the subsequent day. Missed doses should be reported in the patient’s dairy.

Lifestyle intervention will comprise of dietary intervention and physical activity. Dietary intervention includes instructions on consumption of a diet low in refined carbohydrates and having high fibre content. All the subjects will be instructed to perform regular physical activity including at least 3 h per week of aerobic exercise, walking, jogging or yoga.

Criteria for modifying or discontinuing allocated intervention

Once randomized, the subjects will continue with the same drugs throughout the study with no further modification or dose titration. Subjects are free to voluntarily withdraw their participation at any time upon request which will be considered as drop-out. Investigator may discontinue any participant from the study due to following reasons: (i) appearance of any exclusion criteria; (ii) significant non-compliance i.e. no intake of study drug/s for consecutive 4 weeks or more; and (iii) any clinical or laboratory abnormality or adverse event such that discontinuation from the study is in the best interest of the participant. No further study related procedures will be performed after drop-out or discontinuation.

Strategies to improve adherence to intervention

Adherence will be assessed during each planned clinic visit at 12 and 24 weeks after enrolment and will be calculated by “pill count method” as: Adherence ratio = number of tablets taken/number of tablets dispensed. Number of tablets taken will be determined as the difference between the number of tablets dispensed and the number of tablets returned by the patient at the following appointment. Adherence ratio of at least 0.8 will be taken as adequate. For inadequate adherence, the subjects will be motivated during clinic visits and regular telephone calls in between.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial

Patients already on metformin or oral contraceptives for PCOS will be given a wash out period of 3 months prior to enrolment in the study. During the study, concomitant intake of other drugs affecting insulin sensitivity (e.g. other anti-diabetics such as pioglitazone) or ovulatory function (e.g. oral-contraceptives, ovulation-inducing drugs etc.) will not be allowed. Any additional dietary, mineral or vitamin supplements and anti-oxidants etc. will not be permitted during the study.

Provisions for post-trial care

Upon trial completion, the participants will continue to receive treatment by their usual care team as per the routine clinical practices. Any changes in the treatment will be at the discretion of the treating gynecologist.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is to compare the change in serum sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG) levels at 24 weeks from baseline between the two study groups.

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes include (i) clinical efficacy (anthropometry, hirsutism, menstrual cycles regularity); (ii) metabolic and gonadal efficacy (ovarian function, circulating androgens, lipid profile, Insulin resistance and glucose, metabolic syndrome); (iii) degree of hepatic steatosis; (iv) health related quality of life; and (v) safety outcomes.

Assignment of interventions

Participants will be randomly allocated in 1:1 ratio to either of the two study groups viz. Group A (Intervention group) and Group B (Comparator group). Randomization code will be developed by permuted block randomization method by the Principal Investigator (NM) using computer generated random number tables. Participant enrollment will be done by the study staff who will not have any access to the details of allocation sequence. The randomization sequence will be concealed using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes (SNOSE) which will be opened by the study staff at the time of intervention allocation.

Blinding

This is an open-label trial, as blinding is not feasible due to technical challenges in arranging matching placebo in investigator-initiated trials. Both the patient and the investigator involved in treatment assignment and outcome assessment will be aware of the treatment arm. The data will be entered on spreadsheet as unnamed groups by the study staff; and statistical analyses will be performed in a blinded manner by the Principal Investigator (NM) and statistician.

Data collection and management

Data will be collected by the trained study staff using paper based case record form (CRF) designed to capture study pertinent information. Quality of data collection will be ensured by duplicate measurements, standardized scorings or questionnaire, and proper training of staff personnel involved in assessing the outcomes. Data will be securely stored at study site with access restricted to authorized study personnel only to protect confidentiality.

Around 10–12 ml of fasting blood sample will be collected at baseline, 24 and 36 weeks; serum will be separated by centrifugation at 3000 rpm (rotations per minute) for 10 min and stored at −80° C until laboratory analysis.

Assessment of adverse events

Based on ICH-GCP E6 (R2), an adverse event (AE) in this trial is defined as any untoward medical occurrence in a participant administered study treatment/s which does not necessarily have a causal relationship with the treatment itself. An AE will be labelled as serious if it results in death, is life threatening, requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongs duration of hospitalization, results in permanent or significant disability or is congenital anomaly or birth defect. Safety assessment will be done at each follow up visit clinically and at end of treatment (24 weeks) for laboratory (hematological and biochemical) parameters. Data for AEs will be collected systematically (active query; for potentially anticipated AEs such as nausea, headache, vomiting, rash, abdominal cramps, fatigue, weakness and diarrhoea) as well as non-systematically (spontaneous reporting; for other emerging unanticipated AEs). AEs will be coded and reported using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) terms. All AEs will be assessed for severity and causality (using WHO UMC causality assessment scale); and reported in accordance with regulatory guidelines.

Patient and public involvement

The present study was planned based on the authors’ clinical experience with females suffering from PCOS. There was no direct involvement of the patients and public members during the protocol development as the study is being conducted as an academic, investigator-initiated trial with external funding from Haryana State Council for Science, Innovation and Technology (HSCSIT), Government of Haryana. However, the findings of the study will duly be shared with the patients and public through social media and medical conferences.

Sample size calculation

The primary efficacy end point for sample size calculation is change in serum sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG) levels after 24 weeks of treatment from baseline. In the absence of any background data regarding the efficacy of saroglitazar in PCOS patients, the sample size is calculated on the basis of the observed effect of PPAR-γ agonist (pioglitazone) in PCOS in a previous study [28]. Based on our presumed hypothesis, saroglitazar will result in an absolute increase in serum SHBG levels of 8.5 nmol/L compared to 0.5 nmol/L with metformin with a standard deviation of 15 nmol/L. Considering a two-tailed test with α = 0.05, β = 0.2 and considering upto 20% loss to follow up, the required sample size is 140 patients in the two groups.

Recruitment

Posters will be displayed in clinical areas to promote the trial. Besides this, the patients visiting the Gynecology outpatient department (OPD) will be regularly approached and assessed for study eligibility.

Statistical analysis plan

The statistical analysis will be carried out in accordance with the principles mentioned in the International Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) Topic E9 (CPMP/ICH/363/96) [48] and the addendum on estimands [49].

Data will be subjected to Shapiro–Wilk normality test. Depending upon the distribution, data will be expressed as numbers (percentages) for categorical variables and median (inter-quartile range; IQR) or mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables. Primary analysis for all the efficacy parameters will be done as per the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle and all randomized patients will be included in analyses. Safety analyses will be done as per-protocol (PP) principle. Missing data will be handled using multiple imputation methods described in literature. For primary outcome i.e. change in SHBG levels at 24 weeks from baseline between two groups, an additional sensitivity analysis will be carried out by best and worst case scenarios. For continous outcomes (BMI, WHR, frequency and length of menstrual cycles, lipid profile, FPG, FPI, HOMA-IR, ovarian hormones and androgens, hirsutism and quality of life scores etc.), change at 24 weeks from baseline between two groups will be compared using the ANCOVA (analysis of co-variance) test with treatment as fixed factor and baseline value as covariate. Proportion of patients with metabolic syndrome and hepatic steatosis will be compared using chi-square tests with continuity correction or Fisher’s exact test, whichever appropriate.

No efficacy interim analyses are planned for the study. Additional subgroup analysis will be performed for (i) presence or absence and degree of IR based on the reference laboratory ranges of HOMA-IR; (ii) adult BMI categories [50] as underweight (< 18.5 kg/m2), healthy weight (18.5 to < 25 kg/m2), overweight (25 to < 30 kg/m2), obesity (≥ 30 to < 40 kg/m2) and severe obesity (≥ 40 kg/m2); and (iii) presence or absence of metabolic syndrome.

Analyses will be performed using the available upgraded version of IBM SPSS (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) or any equivalent validated statistical software. A two-tailed P-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Globally, PCOS is one of the commonly encountered reproductive- metabolic disorders among women in reproductive age. Emerging insights in the pathogenesis of this complex syndrome throw light on the crucial role of IR with shift in therapeutic approaches towards agents targeting IR. Metformin, an insulin sensitizer, is used off-label in PCOS. Saroglitazar, a novel indigenously developed glitazar in India, has obtained regulatory approval for diabetic dyslipidemia, hypertriglyceridemia in type 2 diabetes patients, as add-on to metformin in type 2 DM and in non-cirrhotic nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH). The present study will test—on top of lifestyle intervention and metformin- the efficacy and safety of saroglitazar on clinical, metabolic and gonadal profile of females suffering from PCOS. The beneficial role of saroglitazar as adjunctive therapy to metformin and lifestyle intervention in PCOS, if proven, may play instrumental role in providing a potentially viable and useful therapeutic option in PCOS and associated co-morbidities. The study results are also expected to provide significant advancement in the field of science by highlighting role of dual PPAR-γ agonists, the “glitazars”, in altering the disease pathophysiology as well as managing the disease related metabolic and other complications in PCOS, which in turn may advocate the applicability of these drugs as potential therapeutic option for the same.

Strengths and limitations

The strengths include- first randomized controlled trial to assess the role of saroglitazar in PCOS; combination of a specific lifestyle intervention and metformin; evaluation of various clinical, metabolic, gonadal and sonographic outcomes; phase of post-treatment follow up; the pre-, on and post-treatment assessments with up-to-date validated biomarkers and questionnaires; and planned ancillary subgroup analyses to identify the most suitable target population. The main limitations relate to the single centre study and lack of blinding of participants and investigators due to technical challenges.
