Curvature Beyond Positivity: Greedy Guarantees for Arbitrary Submodular Functions
Yixin Chen, Alan Kuhnle

Abstract
Submodular functions—functions exhibiting diminishing returns—are central to machine learning. When the objective is monotone and non-negative, the greedy algorithm achieves a tight 63 % 63\% approximation. But many practical objectives incorporate costs that make them negative on some inputs, and all existing multiplicative guarantees require non-negativity. Prior work handles negativity through additive bounds for the special class of decomposable functions and non-monotonicity through partial-monotonicity parameters, but these address each difficulty in isolation and neither extends the classical structural theory. We extend curvature —a parameter measuring how far a function deviates from linearity—to all submodular functions, handling both non-monotonicity and negativity through a single classical concept. A greedy algorithm with pruning achieves a curvature-controlled multiplicative ratio for any submodular function, including those taking negative values—the first such guarantee beyond monotonicity and non-negativity. In the non-monotone regime 1 ≤ c g < 2.2 1\leq c_{g}<2.2 , the bound strictly beats the best known uniform ratio of 0.401 0.401 (for non-negative f f ), and it recovers the classical ( 1 − e − c g ) / c g (1-e^{-c_{g}})/c_{g} guarantee for monotone functions. A multilinear-extension variant extends the framework to general combinatorial constraints via multilinear relaxation. Experiments on cost-penalized experimental design, coverage, feature selection, and a curvature sweep on Multi-News passage selection support the theory.

Curvature and Greedy Algorithms

We consider set functions
f:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
defined on subsets of a ground set
\mathcal{N}

of size
n
.
We assume
f
is
normalized
:
f\left(\emptyset\right)=0
.
We say
f
is
submodular
if
f\left(X\cup Y\right)+f\left(X\cap Y\right)\leq f\left(X\right)+f\left(Y\right)

for all
X,Y\subseteq\mathcal{N}
.
We do
not
require
f
to be monotone or non-negative.
Since we normalize
f\left(\emptyset\right)=0
, we may assume that every element
e\in\mathcal{N}

satisfies
f\left(e\right)>0
, as any element with
f\left(e\right)\leq 0
can be removed without
affecting the optimum.

f\left(\emptyset\right)=0

2.1
Curvature: Definition and Basic Properties

Our generalization replaces the classical element-wise ratio
with a set-wise comparison: how much does the value of

Y\setminus X
degrade when added in the context of
X
?
This avoids the element-level marginals that diverge for
non-monotone functions.
Since the minimum in Definition
1
is taken over all pairs
with
f\left(Y\setminus X\right)-f\left(\emptyset\right)>0
, and submodularity ensures

f\left(X\cup Y\right)-f\left(X\right)\leq f\left(Y\setminus X\right)-f\left(\emptyset\right)
,
the curvature satisfies
c_{f}\geq 0
for every submodular
f
.

Definition 1

(Curvature)
.

The
curvature

c_{f}\in[0,\infty)
of a submodular function
f:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}

is defined as

c_{f}=1-\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}X,Y\subseteq\mathcal{N}\\
f\left(Y\setminus X\right)-f\left(\emptyset\right)>0\end{subarray}}\frac{f\left(X\cup Y\right)-f\left(X\right)}{f\left(Y\setminus X\right)-f\left(\emptyset\right)}.

Equivalently,
c_{f}
is the minimum value such that, for all
X,Y\subseteq\mathcal{N}

with
f\left(Y\setminus X\right)-f\left(\emptyset\right)>0
:

f\left(X\cup Y\right)-f\left(X\right)\geq(1-c_{f})\bigl(f\left(Y\setminus X\right)-f\left(\emptyset\right)\bigr).

(1)

When
f\left(\emptyset\right)=0
(as we assume throughout), the
-f\left(\emptyset\right)
terms vanish.
When
c_{f}\leq 1
, this says adding
Y\setminus X
to
X
remains non-negative
and captures at least a
(1-c_{f})
fraction of the standalone value of

Y\setminus X
.
When
c_{f}>1
, the union can
degrade
value: adding
Y\setminus X
to
X

may reduce the function value by up to
(c_{f}-1)\,f\left(Y\setminus X\right)
.
Submodularity also gives the companion upper bound

f\left(X\cup Y\right)-f\left(X\right)\leq f\left(Y\setminus X\right)
.

Example 1
.

For cardinality-constrained MaxCut,
c_{f}=2
(Proposition
3
).

The global curvature
c_{f}
may be much larger than what the algorithm
actually encounters along its trajectory—a worst-case set pair may never
arise during execution. To capture this, we define a tighter,
trajectory-specific variant.

Definition 2

(Greedy curvature)
.

Fix a cardinality constraint
k
.
Let
\mathcal{O}=\argmax_{|S|\leq k}f\left(S\right)
be the set of optimal solutions
and let
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
be the active sets generated by
greedy with pruning (Algorithm
1
), repeating the
terminal set if the algorithm stops early.
These active sets are not required to be nested: pruning may delete
elements chosen in earlier iterations.
The
greedy curvature
is

c_{g}\;=\;1-\min_{O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}}\;\min_{i:\,f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)-f\left(\emptyset\right)>0}\frac{f\left(O^{*}\cup A_{i}\right)-f\left(O^{*}\right)}{f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)-f\left(\emptyset\right)}.

By construction,
c_{g}\leq c_{f}
, since the minimization is over the
trajectory pairs
(O^{*},A_{i})
, a subset of the pairs allowed in
Definition
1
.

Proposition 1

(Monotonicity characterization)
.

A submodular function
f
with
f\left(e\right)>0
for all
e\in\mathcal{N}
is
monotone if and only if
c_{f}\leq 1
.

This result means curvature cleanly partitions the function
space:
c_{f}\leq 1
is precisely the monotone regime, so
functions with
c_{f}>1
are
necessarily
non-monotone.
The curvature parameter thus simultaneously measures distance from
linearity and distance from monotonicity.
See Appendix
J
for the proof.

Proposition 2

(Classical equivalence)
.

For monotone submodular
f
, the curvature
c_{f}
equals the total curvature

\alpha
of
7
:

1-\alpha=\min_{e:\Delta\left(e|\emptyset\right)>0}\Delta\left(e|\mathcal{N}\setminus e\right)/\Delta\left(e|\emptyset\right)
.

This equivalence ensures backward compatibility: every result
proved using the CC parameter
\alpha
transfers directly to
c_{f}

for monotone functions, while our generalization extends the same
quantity beyond the monotone boundary.
See Appendix
J
for the proof.

Notation.

Throughout,
c_{f}
denotes the
set-function curvature

(Definition
1
), a global quantity;
c_{g}
denotes the

greedy curvature
(Definition
2
), restricted
to the greedy trajectory and satisfying
c_{g}\leq c_{f}
.
The multilinear analogues
c_{F}
and
c_{g}^{F}
(stated in
Section
3
; formally defined in
Appendix
G
) extend these to continuous relaxations. When comparing to the classical
Conforti–Cornuéjols literature,
\alpha
and
\alpha_{g}
refer to
the CC curvature of a monotone submodular function, where
\alpha=c_{f}

by Proposition
2
.

2.2
Discrete Greedy with Pruning

The cleanest algorithmic setting for the set-level curvature is the
cardinality-constrained discrete greedy algorithm
(Algorithm
1
). Here the
curvature inequality can be applied directly to sets along the greedy
trajectory, without first passing through a relaxation.
The multilinear obstruction discussed below motivates the
trajectory machinery needed for the continuous lift in Section
3
.

The key algorithmic device in the discrete result is a
pruning
loop
: after each
greedy addition, any element whose marginal to the rest of the current
set has turned non-positive is removed.
The pruning loop supplies the local positivity needed by the curvature
recurrence: every nonempty subset of the active set has positive value,
and
f
is monotone within the active set (Remark
1
).

Theorem 1

(Greedy guarantee)
.

Let
f
be submodular with greedy curvature
c_{g}

(Definition
2
), and let
O^{*}\in\argmax_{|S|\leq k}f\left(S\right)
.
Put
\bar{c}_{g}=\max\{1,c_{g}\}
.
Then
\textsc{Greedy}(f,k)
(Algorithm
1
, Appendix
B
) returns
A_{k}
with

f\left(A_{k}\right)\geq\frac{1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}}}{\bar{c}_{g}}\,f\left(O^{*}\right)
.
If
f
is monotone, the classical Conforti–Cornuéjols analysis gives
the sharper
\frac{1-e^{-c_{g}}}{c_{g}}
bound when
c_{g}<1
.

Proof.

Write
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
for the active sets produced by the algorithm.
Let
\bar{c}_{g}=\max\{1,c_{g}\}
. Since
\bar{c}_{g}\geq c_{g}
, the curvature
inequality is valid with
\bar{c}_{g}
in place of
c_{g}
.

\displaystyle f\left(A_{i+1}\right)-f\left(A_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq f\left(A_{i+1}^{\prime}\right)-f\left(A_{i}\right)

(pruning can only increase)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{o\in O^{*}\setminus A_{i}}\Delta\left(o|A_{i}\right)

(greedy selection)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}[f\left(O^{*}\cup A_{i}\right)-f\left(A_{i}\right)]

(submodularity)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}[f\left(O^{*}\right)+(1-\bar{c}_{g})f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)-f\left(A_{i}\right)]

(curvature)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}[f\left(O^{*}\right)-\bar{c}_{g}\,f\left(A_{i}\right)].

(
\bar{c}_{g}\geq 1
, local monotonicity on
A_{i}
)

The curvature step uses Definition
2
at the pair
(O^{*},A_{i})

along the greedy trajectory; only the trajectory-restricted
c_{g}
is needed.
If
A_{i}\setminus O^{*}
is nonempty, Remark
1
gives

f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)>0
, so the pair is admissible in
Definition
2
. If
A_{i}\setminus O^{*}=\emptyset
,
the same display holds with the curvature term equal to zero.
The last inequality uses
f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)\leq f\left(A_{i}\right)
, which follows
from the local monotonicity of
f
on the pruned active set
(Remark
1
);
this is the step that requires using a curvature parameter at least
1
.
If
\bar{c}_{g}\leq k
, the recurrence

f\left(A_{i+1}\right)\geq f\left(O^{*}\right)/k+(1-\bar{c}_{g}/k)f\left(A_{i}\right)
solves to

f\left(A_{k}\right)\geq\frac{f\left(O^{*}\right)}{\bar{c}_{g}}(1-(1-\bar{c}_{g}/k)^{k})\geq\frac{f\left(O^{*}\right)}{\bar{c}_{g}}(1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}})
.
If
\bar{c}_{g}>k
, the first greedy step already gives

f\left(A_{1}\right)\geq f\left(O^{*}\right)/k>f\left(O^{*}\right)/\bar{c}_{g}
, and subsequent
accepted additions and prunings do not decrease the value; this is
stronger than the displayed bound.

Remark 1

(The pruning loop)
.

The pruning loop removes elements with non-positive marginal contribution.
By submodularity, for any nonempty
S\subseteq A_{i}
the telescoping sum

f\left(S\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{|S|}\Delta\left(s_{j}|\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{j-1}\}\right)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{|S|}\Delta\left(s_{j}|A_{i}\setminus s_{j}\right)>0
,
since every remaining element has strictly positive marginal to the rest.
The same argument, applied to
A_{i}\setminus S
and telescoping from
S

up to
A_{i}
, gives
f\left(S\right)\leq f\left(A_{i}\right)
for every
S\subseteq A_{i}
.
Hence every nonempty subset of
A_{i}
has positive value, and
f
is
monotone inside the active set. These are exactly the local properties
used in the proof above.
The algorithm does not need to know
c_{g}
.

Algorithm
1
runs in
O(nk)
oracle queries.
Lazy (priority-queue) greedy can be used between pruning events, but
after pruning shrinks the active set the cached marginal upper bounds
must be rebuilt or revalidated. With that caveat, the practical query
count is often close to
O(n+k\log n)
. Whether a near-linear-time
threshold greedy variant exists is an open question
(see Section
5
).

For strictly positive functions (

3
Beyond Cardinality: the DMCG-P Guarantee

The discrete greedy result (Theorem
1
) handles
cardinality constraints.
For general combinatorial constraints—matroids, knapsack, and
their intersections—a standard approach is to optimize the
multilinear extension
F
over a relaxation of the feasible family.
The classical continuous-greedy analysis requires bounded global
curvature
c_{F}
, but for negative-valued
f
this diverges
(Proposition
7
,
Appendix
G
).
We resolve this via DMCG-P (Discretized Measured Continuous Greedy
with Pruning; Algorithm
2
), which restricts
curvature to the algorithm’s trajectory.

The algorithmic ideas mirror the discrete proof but in fractional
form. Each step chooses an integral feasible direction by exact
linear optimization over the current multilinear slopes, takes a
small
\delta=1/T
step, and then zeroes coordinates whose slopes have become
non-positive. Discretization lets the continuous trajectory be
analyzed through set-level greedy/pruning increments, while the
positive-slope pruning invariant removes the immediate zero-slope
obstruction that makes the global curvature
c_{F}
unusable. The
theorem below still states finite trajectory curvature as a
hypothesis; in the applications section, decomposable objectives give
one concrete certificate for that hypothesis.

Theorem 2

(DMCG-P guarantee; see Appendix
G
)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq 2^{\mathcal{N}}
be downward-closed with

\mathcal{P}=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf{1}_{S}:S\in\mathcal{I}\}
, and assume
exact linear optimization over
\mathcal{I}
.
For any submodular
f
(no monotonicity or non-negativity required)
with trajectory-restricted fractional curvature

c_{g}^{F}<\infty
and
\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\triangleq\max\{1,c_{g}^{F}\}
, DMCG-P
with
T
steps (step size
\delta=1/T
, with

\delta\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\leq 1
) returns

\tilde{S}_{T}\in\mathcal{P}
satisfying

F(\tilde{S}_{T})\;\geq\;\frac{1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}}}{\bar{c}_{g}^{F}}\,f\left(O^{*}\right)\;-\;O\!\left(\frac{n(n{-}1)\,C_{F}}{T}\right),

where
n=|\mathcal{N}|
, and

C_{F}=\max_{j\neq\ell,\,\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}}|\partial_{j\ell}F(\mathbf{x})|

is the smoothness constant, with

O^{*}\in\argmax_{S\in\mathcal{I}}f(S)
.
The step count
T
is independent of the constraint; choosing

T
large enough makes the error negligible at the cost of
O(Tn)

oracle calls.

The finite-curvature hypothesis uses the positive-denominator
convention in Definition
4
; pruning supplies the slope
invariant used in the proof, but a certificate is needed to verify
finiteness in a concrete instance.

In the application section below, decomposable objectives

f=g-\ell
provide one concrete way to certify the finite-curvature
hypothesis: the pruned trajectory yields an OPT-free ratio

\hat{r}_{F}<1
with

c_{g}^{F}\leq\alpha_{g}/(1-\hat{r}_{F})

(Proposition
9
).
For non-negative non-monotone
f
, a damped weighted variant
(wDMCG-P; Algorithm
3
) recovers the
e^{-1}

guarantee (Theorem
5
).

4.1
Symmetric Submodular Functions

Symmetric submodular functions—those satisfying

optimization, encompassing graph cuts, hypergraph
partitioning, and clustering
objectives
[
23

10

Any normalized symmetric submodular function is automatically
non-negative: submodularity applied to

\mathcal{N}\setminus S

For general non-monotone maximization, the uniform guarantee is
1/e

symmetric objectives are one structured setting where specialized
algorithms exceed it
[

,
24
]

curvature provides a structural explanation for why
greedy+pruning already exceeds this barrier.

Proposition 3

(Symmetric functions)
.

Let
f
be normalized, symmetric (
f\left(S\right)=f\left(\mathcal{N}\setminus S\right)
),
and submodular, and suppose

has a positive singleton.
Then

is non-negative and

.

Proof idea.

Symmetry forces

\geq-1

See Appendix

1

24

structural lens

without using symmetry at runtime, and the same framework seamlessly

5
Discussion and Conclusion

Curvature provides a single, continuous parameter that extends the
classical Conforti–Cornuéjols theory beyond monotonicity,
giving the first curvature-controlled multiplicative guarantees for
negative-valued submodular objectives.
Greedy with pruning achieves
(1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}})/\bar{c}_{g}
under
cardinality constraints; DMCG-P extends this to general combinatorial
constraints under explicit trajectory-curvature hypotheses.
Experiments on three function classes confirm that the guarantees are
conservative, and that trajectory-based curvature is
often substantially tighter than worst-case bounds.

A key limitation is that curvature is trajectory-specific:
c_{g}

certifies the realized greedy-with-pruning path, not an input parameter
known before the algorithm runs.
For decomposable objectives, the removal-marginal certificate
(Proposition
4
) gives an OPT-free post-hoc bound from
the pruned trajectory, with OPT-aware and multilinear variants deferred
to the appendix.
Comparable certificates for broader value-oracle classes remain open.

Concrete open directions include: (i) near-linear threshold or streaming
variants that preserve the local pruning invariant; (ii) tight
value-oracle lower bounds for curvature-controlled greedy under
cardinality and matroid constraints; (iii) certificates for weighted
relevance-minus-redundancy objectives, including nonuniform query
weights; and (iv) algorithms that adapt pruning or selection to the
observed trajectory curvature.

Appendix A
Additional Related Work

Symmetric submodular functions.

10
proved that a variant of measured continuous
greedy achieves
(1-e^{-2})/2\approx 0.432
for maximizing a non-negative
symmetric submodular function under a cardinality constraint.
Section
4.1
explains how the same threshold follows
from Proposition
3
: symmetric functions have
c_{f}=2
,
and hence
c_{g}\leq 2
.
More recently,
24
gave a deterministic

O(kn)
-query algorithm achieving the same
0.432
ratio for symmetric
functions under a cardinality constraint, confirming the tractability of
this class.
Both results are specific to symmetric functions and require
non-negativity, while our framework derives the same ratio from a
single structural parameter that applies to all submodular functions,
including those that go negative.

Partial monotonicity.

17
introduced the
monotonicity ratio

m=\min_{S\subseteq T}f(T)/f(S)
as a structural parameter for
non-monotone submodular maximization, proving that standard greedy achieves

m(1-1/e)
and randomized greedy achieves
m(1-1/e)+(1-m)/e
under a
cardinality constraint.
Their framework requires
f\geq 0
throughout.
For symmetric functions,
m=0
(since
f(\emptyset)=f(N)=0
for cut
functions), so the standard greedy guarantee is vacuous and the random greedy
guarantee reduces to
1/e
.
In contrast, our curvature guarantee of
(1-e^{-2})/2\approx 0.432
for
symmetric functions strictly exceeds
1/e
and uses a deterministic algorithm.
More broadly, curvature and partial monotonicity capture different structural
properties: the monotonicity ratio measures how much value can be lost by
adding elements (a global property), while curvature measures the rate of
diminishing returns along the greedy trajectory (a local property).
Neither parameter dominates the other—functions with high curvature can have
moderate monotonicity ratio and vice versa—but curvature applies to
possibly-negative functions where partial monotonicity is undefined.

The experimental comparison in Section
4
confirms the
theoretical separation: the partial-monotonicity guarantee is either
vacuous (
m=0
for symmetric-type instances) or inapplicable (
f<0

at moderate-to-high cost levels), while the curvature guarantee remains
meaningful throughout.
The GCLin analysis (Figure
2
) further illustrates
this: the curvature bound stays positive in regimes where the
monotonicity-ratio bound vanishes, but the two parameters are
incomparable in general.

Distributional curvature.

The multilinear curvature
c_{F}
is inherently distributional:
it averages the pointwise curvature inequality over product distributions
via the coupling in Theorem
3
.
One can define a
distributional curvature

c_{D}
as the expected curvature
under random subset pairs drawn from such distributions.
For strictly positive
f
, the coupling proof gives
c_{D}=c_{F}=c_{f}
:
the distribution cannot improve on the worst case.
For functions that go negative,
c_{D}
may be finite even when
c_{F}=\infty
,
suggesting that distributional curvature could yield tighter bounds in some regimes.

Comparison with additive guarantees.

The additive-ratio framework of
11
—and its
extensions
[
12
,
15
,
2
]
—gives

f(S)\geq(1-1/e)\,g(O^{*})-\ell(O^{*})

for decomposable
f=g-\ell
.
This becomes vacuous when

Subsequent works
[

15

19

2

The curvature framework provides a multiplicative alternative

\gamma

This appendix proves the conditional Theorem

from the supporting step lemma plus the explicit monotonicity assumption
below. MCG
[

5
]

operates on the multilinear extension of

downward-closed solvable polytope
\mathcal{P}
(matroid, knapsack, or

Lemma 1

(

)
.

For every
0\leq t<T
:

F(\mathbf{y}(t+\delta))-F(\mathbf{y}(t))\geq\delta[F(\mathbf{y}(t)\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\mathbf{y}(t))]-O(n^{3}\delta^{2})f\left(O^{*}\right)

Assumption 1

(MCG monotonicity condition)
.

F(\mathbf{x})\leq F(\mathbf{y}(t))
for all
0\leq t\leq T

and
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{y}(t)
.

This is the auxiliary monotonicity property supplied by

10
in the settings covered by
their MCG analysis. We state it explicitly because strict positivity
alone does not imply it for arbitrary non-monotone submodular functions.

uses the local positive-slope invariant enforced by its pruning loop.

Restated claim.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
6
—strict positivity,
submodularity, curvature
c_{f}>1
, the MCG monotonicity condition above,
and a downward-closed solvable polytope—measured continuous greedy

Proof of Theorem
6
.

We solve a scalar recurrence for
a(t)\triangleq F(\mathbf{y}(t))
.
From Lemma
1
,

F(\mathbf{y}(t+\delta))-F(\mathbf{y}(t))\geq\delta\bigl[F(\mathbf{y}(t)\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\mathbf{y}(t))\bigr]-O(n^{3}\delta^{2})f\left(O^{*}\right).

Apply Definition
3
with
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}

and
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{y}(t)
, using
c_{F}=c_{f}

(Theorem
3
(a)):

F(\mathbf{y}(t)\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})\geq(1-c_{f})\,F(\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}).

Since
\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}\leq\mathbf{y}(t)
,
Assumption
1
gives

F(\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})\leq F(\mathbf{y}(t))

Rearranging:

F(\mathbf{y}(t)\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\mathbf{y}(t))\geq f\left(O^{*}\right)-F(\mathbf{y}(t))+(1-c_{f})F(\mathbf{y}(t))=f\left(O^{*}\right)-c_{f}\,F(\mathbf{y}(t))

Combining with the MCG step:

a(t+\delta)\geq(1-c_{f}\delta)\,a(t)+\delta(1-O(n^{3}\delta))f\left(O^{*}\right).

Let
m=T/\delta
and unroll the recurrence from

Hence

Taking the step size small enough that
\delta\leq n^{-5}
, and then
sending

and absorbs

O(n^{3}\delta)
into the
o(1)
term.

Appendix D
Computing the OPT-Free Curvature Certificate

A key practical advantage of the greedy curvature framework over additive
guarantees is that the algorithm can output a
provable quality
certificate
alongside its solution, without access to the optimal set or
its value. For decomposable objectives, pruning gives exactly the
right observable denominators.

D.1
The Removal-Marginal Certificate

Given a decomposable objective
f=g-\ell
with
g
monotone submodular
(CC curvature
\alpha_{g}
) and
\ell
modular, let

A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
be the active-set trajectory of greedy with pruning.
Define

\hat{r}\;\triangleq\;\max_{i}\max_{e\in A_{i}}\frac{\ell(e)}{\Delta_{g}(e\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e\})},

with value
0
if all active sets are empty.
This is the quantity used in Proposition
4
.

The denominator is positive automatically. At the end of each pruning
loop, every active element
e\in A_{i}
satisfies

\Delta_{f}(e\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e\})=\Delta_{g}(e\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e\})-\ell(e)>0,

and hence
\Delta_{g}(e\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e\})>\ell(e)
. Therefore

\hat{r}<1
. The resulting certificate is

\frac{f(A_{k})}{f(\text{OPT})}\;\geq\;\frac{1-e^{-\bar{\hat{c}}_{g}}}{\bar{\hat{c}}_{g}},\qquad\hat{c}_{g}=\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-\hat{r}},\qquad\bar{\hat{c}}_{g}=\max\{1,\hat{c}_{g}\}.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}=\argmax_{|S|\leq k}f(S)
. Fix

O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
and a trajectory step
i
with

f(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})>0
, since these are exactly the positive-denominator
pairs in Definition
2
. Write
A=A_{i}
and

T=A_{i}\setminus O^{*}
.

The first ingredient is the usual CC-curvature inequality for the
monotone component
g
:

g(O^{*}\cup A_{i})-g(O^{*})\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})\,g(T).

(2)

To see this directly, enumerate
T=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
and telescope
the gain from adding
T
to
O^{*}
. Each marginal of
g
is at
least
1-\alpha_{g}
times the corresponding marginal when the same
element is added inside
T
; summing gives (
2
).

The second ingredient is the set-level penalty bound supplied by the
removal marginals. Enumerate
T=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
again and write

T_{<q}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{q-1}\}
. Since
T_{<q}\subseteq A_{i}\setminus\{e_{q}\}
,
submodularity gives

\Delta_{g}(e_{q}\mid T_{<q})\;\geq\;\Delta_{g}(e_{q}\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e_{q}\}).

By definition of
\hat{r}
,

\ell(e_{q})\leq\hat{r}\,\Delta_{g}(e_{q}\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e_{q}\})
.
Hence

\displaystyle\ell(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=1}^{m}\ell(e_{q})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}\sum_{q=1}^{m}\Delta_{g}(e_{q}\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e_{q}\})\;\leq\;\hat{r}\sum_{q=1}^{m}\Delta_{g}(e_{q}\mid T_{<q})\;=\;\hat{r}\,g(T),

(3)

Set
\tau=\ell(T)/g(T)
. The qualifying condition

f(T)>0
gives
\tau<1
, and (
3
) gives

0\leq\tau\leq\hat{r}
. Combining (
2
) with
modularity of
\ell
,

\frac{f(O^{*}\cup A_{i})-f(O^{*})}{f(T)}\;\geq\;\frac{(1-\alpha_{g})g(T)-\ell(T)}{g(T)-\ell(T)}\;=\;\frac{(1-\alpha_{g})-\tau}{1-\tau}.

The last expression is decreasing in
\tau
, since its derivative is

-\alpha_{g}/(1-\tau)^{2}
. Therefore

\frac{f(O^{*}\cup A_{i})-f(O^{*})}{f(T)}\;\geq\;\frac{(1-\alpha_{g})-\hat{r}}{1-\hat{r}}\;=\;1-\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-\hat{r}}.

Taking the double minimum over
O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
and qualifying
trajectory steps in Definition
2
gives

c_{g}\leq\alpha_{g}/(1-\hat{r})
.
∎

Remark 2

(Cheaper singleton diagnostic)
.

A cheaper quantity is the singleton ratio

\hat{s}=\max_{e\in\cup_{i}A_{i}}\frac{\ell(e)}{g(\{e\})}.

Since
\Delta_{g}(e\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e\})\leq g(\{e\})
, the
removal-marginal certificate is generally weaker:

\hat{r}\geq\hat{s}
. The singleton ratio can nevertheless be used as
a formal certificate under the additional hypothesis

\hat{s}<1-\alpha_{g}
, in which case the singleton argument yields

c_{g}\leq\alpha_{g}/(1-\hat{s})
. Without this extra
hypothesis, singleton ratios are reported only as diagnostics in the
experiments.

Contrast with additive guarantees.

The HFWK guarantee
(1-1/e)\,g(\text{OPT})-\ell(\text{OPT})
requires
knowledge of both
g(\text{OPT})
and
\ell(\text{OPT})
to evaluate.
At test time on an instance where OPT is intractable, neither is available — one can only

upper-bound

g(\text{OPT})
(e.g., by the greedy value of
g
alone),
which loosens the guarantee further.
The removal-marginal certificate depends only on
\alpha_{g}
(a property of
the function class, not the instance) and
\hat{r}
(observable from the
algorithm’s own trajectory).

D.2
Analytic
\alpha_{g}
for Application Classes

The CC curvature
\alpha_{g}=1-\min_{e,\,S\not\ni e}\Delta_{g}(e\mid S)/g(\{e\})
has closed-form expressions for the
applications considered in this paper.

Experimental design (Bayesian A-optimality).

g(S)=\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{\text{prior}})-\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{S|})
, where
\Sigma_{S|}
is the posterior
covariance after observing experiments in
S
.
The CC curvature of
g
is determined by the eigenvalues of the
information matrix:

\alpha_{g}\;=\;1-\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}}\;=\;1-\frac{1}{\kappa},

where
\kappa
is the condition number of
X^{\top}X/\sigma^{2}+\Sigma_{\text{prior}}^{-1}
and
X
is the design matrix.
For well-conditioned designs (
\kappa\approx 2
),
\alpha_{g}\approx 0.5
;
for ill-conditioned designs (
\kappa\approx 20
),
\alpha_{g}\approx 0.95
.

Coverage functions (directed vertex cover).

g(S)=|\{j:j\text{ covered by some }v\in S\}|
.
Since any element’s coverage can be fully subsumed by the rest of the
ground set,
\min_{e,S}\Delta_{g}(e\mid S)=0
, giving
\alpha_{g}=1
.
This recovers the standard
1-1/e
guarantee as a special case of the
curvature framework.

Feature selection (Gaussian mutual information).

g(S)=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\det(I+\Sigma_{SS}/\sigma^{2})
, where
\Sigma_{SS}

is the covariance submatrix for features in
S
.
The CC curvature depends on the spectral structure of the full covariance:

\alpha_{g}\;=\;1-\frac{1}{\kappa(I+\Sigma/\sigma^{2})},

where
\kappa(\cdot)
denotes the condition number.
When features are nearly independent,
\alpha_{g}\approx 0
;
with high redundancy (correlated groups),
\alpha_{g}
approaches
1
.

D.3
Computing
\hat{r}
from the Greedy Trajectory

The certificate
\hat{r}
is computed from the pruned active sets

A_{i}
, not from the terminal output alone. For every element that is
active at a step
i
, evaluate the removal marginal

\Delta_{g}(e\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e\})
and form

\frac{\ell(e)}{\Delta_{g}(e\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e\})}.

The maximum over these ratios is
\hat{r}
. For decomposable objectives
where
g
and
\ell
are given separately, this is a direct
post-processing pass over the trajectory. The same pass also verifies

\hat{r}<1
, although the pruning invariant already proves it.

For the
non-decomposable
case (where only a value oracle for
f
is
available),
\hat{r}
cannot be computed directly. However, the empirical
greedy curvature
c_{g}
can still be bounded without decomposition — see
the per-step curvature analysis in Appendix
G
.

D.4
Relation to the Singleton Diagnostic

Table
1
reports the guarantee

(1-e^{-\bar{\hat{c}}_{g}})/\bar{\hat{c}}_{g}
induced by the
removal-marginal certificate (Proposition
4
),
which is the primary OPT-free bound for decomposable greedy with pruning.
A cheaper singleton diagnostic
\hat{s}=\max_{e\in\mathcal{A}}\ell(e)/g(\{e\})
is also computable and becomes a formal certificate
under the additional condition in
Remark
2
.

Appendix G
Multilinear Extension, DMCG-P, and MCG

This appendix contains the multilinear extension analysis, the DMCG-P
algorithm and its guarantees, and a conditional MCG comparison for
strictly positive functions satisfying the standard MCG monotonicity
lemma. These results extend the discrete greedy guarantee of
Theorem
1
from cardinality constraints to integral
feasible-set hulls for DMCG-P, and to the standard continuous setting
for MCG.

G.1
Curvature of the Multilinear Extension

We now lift curvature to the continuous relaxation.
The multilinear extension is the standard bridge between discrete
submodular optimization and continuous relaxations—it underlies
the measured continuous greedy algorithm (Section
G.3
) and
the correlation gap framework
[
5
]
.
The question is whether curvature lifts cleanly to this
continuous setting.
Classical non-monotone algorithms (e.g., BFNS randomized greedy
[
3
]
,
measured continuous greedy) analyze the multilinear extension and
rely on the correlation gap: each step of the continuous relaxation
bounds
F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{x})
from below
using
F(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})
. In the
coupled set-level view, one wants a lower bound on

f\left(R_{1}\cup R_{2}\right)-f\left(R_{1}\right)
in terms of
f\left(R_{2}\right)
for disjoint
realizations
R_{1},R_{2}
. Zero-valued sets are delicate:
if
f\left(R_{2}\right)=0
, submodularity only gives the upper bound

f\left(R_{1}\cup R_{2}\right)-f\left(R_{1}\right)\leq 0
, not the lower bound needed for a
positive-denominator curvature comparison.
Our curvature framework makes this precise:
strict positivity of
f
turns out to be the key structural property.

For a set function
f:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
, its
multilinear extension

F:[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
is

F(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right]=\sum_{R\subseteq\mathcal{N}}f\left(R\right)\prod_{i\in R}x_{i}\prod_{j\notin R}(1-x_{j}),

where
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x})
includes each
i
independently with probability
x_{i}
.

Definition 3

(Multilinear curvature)
.

The
curvature

c_{F}
of the multilinear extension is

c_{F}=1-\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}\\
F(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})>0\end{subarray}}\frac{F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{x})}{F(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})}.

As in Definition
1
, the minimum ranges only over
positive-denominator pairs, and we set
c_{F}=\infty
if the admissible
ratios are unbounded below.

The multilinear curvature is inherently

: it averages the
pointwise curvature inequality over coupled product distributions.
The coupling proof below makes this explicit.

Proposition 6

(Discrete curvature bounds multilinear curvature)
.

c_{f}\leq c_{F}
.

Proof.

The discrete vertices
\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{N}}\subset[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
, and every
positive-denominator pair in Definition
1
is an
admissible pair in Definition
3
, so the minimum
defining
c_{F}
ranges over a larger domain.
∎

Proposition 7

(
c_{F}=\infty
for negative functions)
.

Suppose
f
is normalized submodular and
f\left(e\right)>0
for every
singleton

. If
f
takes a negative value, then

c_{F}=\infty
.

Intuitively, the denominator
F(\mathbf{z}_{\varepsilon})
in the
curvature ratio can be driven to zero by concentrating mass on an
inclusion-minimal negative set, while the numerator remains bounded
away from zero by that set’s negative value. This concentration
structure motivates the pruning invariant introduced in
§
G.2
.

Proof idea.

Let
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
be an inclusion-minimal set with
f\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\right)<0

for all

Construct
\mathbf{z}_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\setminus\{e^{\prime}\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{e^{\prime}\}}

so that the denominator

f\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\right)<0

crossing zero at some

The numerator stays at most

\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_{0}^{-}

See Appendix
J
for the complete construction.
∎

The following is the key structural result connecting pointwise and distributional curvature.

Theorem 3

(Strict positivity characterization)
.

Let
f:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}

.

(a)
If

(b)
If

Proof idea.

Part (b) is Proposition

For part (a), we construct a product-distribution coupling

Remark 3

(Strict positivity and the continuous relaxation)
.

Theorem

identifies strict positivity as a
sufficient condition under which pointwise curvature (a combinatorial,
worst-case property) lifts perfectly to the multilinear extension
(a continuous, averaged property).
When

When

G.2
Fractional Greedy with Pruning (DMCG-P)

The discrete algorithm of the previous subsection is tied to
cardinality constraints; integral feasible-set hull constraints beyond
cardinality can be handled by multilinear relaxations. But
Theorem

showed that
c_{F}=+\infty

We resolve this by mirroring the discrete construction: define a

The key analytical ingredients are a

trajectory away from the concentration witnesses of
Proposition
7

(1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}})/\bar{c}_{g}^{F}
guarantee for arbitrary submodular
f

finite
c_{g}^{F}
. Full proofs appear in Appendix
H

G.2.2

The headline result of this subsection is that DMCG-P delivers a

Theorem 4

(General DMCG-P guarantee)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq 2^{\mathcal{N}}

\mathcal{P}=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf{1}_{S}:S\in\mathcal{I}\}

j\in\mathcal{N}
let

\partial_{j}F(\mathbf{x})=F(\mathbf{x}\mid x_{j}=1)-F(\mathbf{x}\mid x_{j}=0)
;
by multilinearity,
\partial_{j}F
is independent of
x_{j}
.

Algorithm
2
gives the DMCG-P procedure used
throughout this appendix.
Three details of Algorithm
2
are used repeatedly.

(i)

The selection rule on Line
2
uses the

unweighted
gradient: it maximises

\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,\mathbf{1}_{B}\rangle
over
B\in\mathcal{I}
, not the
Measured-Continuous-Greedy weighted form with multiplier

(1-(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j})
on each coordinate. Since

P=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf{1}_{B}:B\in\mathcal{I}\}
, this is equivalent to linear
optimization over
P
, and the selected direction is the integral
vector
v_{i}=\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}
used in the analysis.
The analysis in §
H.3
shows that the unweighted
form suffices: Lemma
5
(non-negative surviving
slopes) is exactly what lets one turn a weighted swap bound into an
unweighted one.

(ii)

After the final iteration,
\tilde{S}_{T}
lies in the
downward-closed polytope
P
but need not itself be integral;
pruning may have reduced total mass below
b
.
If an integral output is required, one may apply a rounding method
for points in integral hulls, such as swap rounding or pipage rounding,
to
\tilde{S}_{T}\in P
.

(iii)

The pruning loop is written with a nonstrict inequality

\partial_{j}F\leq 0
on Line
2
. As in
Section
2.2
, this ensures
that after pruning every surviving coordinate has strictly positive
multilinear slope (Lemma
5
below), which is what
drives the multilinear curvature argument.

H.2
Structural Lemmas

We collect here the facts about DMCG-P that underlie the analysis.
All lemmas hold for any submodular
f
(monotone or not), unless noted.

Lemma 3

(Feasibility)
.

For every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,T\}
, the iterate
\tilde{S}_{i}
lies in
the downward-closed polytope
P
and satisfies

\tilde{S}_{i}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
. In particular,
(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}\leq i/T

for every coordinate
j
.

Proof.

We show
\tilde{S}_{i}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\tau<i}v_{\tau}

coordinate-wise by induction. The base case is
\tilde{S}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
.
If the bound holds at step
i
, then before pruning

S_{i+1}=\tilde{S}_{i}+\frac{1}{T}v_{i}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\tau\leq i}v_{\tau}
.
Pruning only zeroes coordinates, so
\tilde{S}_{i+1}\leq S_{i+1}

coordinate-wise, preserving the invariant.
Since each
v_{\tau}\in P
and
P
is downward-closed, the average

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\tau\leq i}v_{\tau}=\frac{i+1}{T}\bigl(\frac{1}{i+1}\sum_{\tau\leq i}v_{\tau}\bigr)\in P
,
whence
\tilde{S}_{i+1}\in P
.
The coordinate bound
(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}\leq i/T
follows because

\sum_{\tau<i}(v_{\tau})_{j}\leq i
(each vertex is in
\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{N}}
),
and pruning can only decrease coordinates further.
∎

Lemma 4

(Pruning is value-improving)
.

At every iteration,
F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})\geq F(S_{i+1})
, with strict
inequality if any coordinate is pruned.

Proof.

Consider one pass of the pruning loop, and let
\mathbf{x}
be the current
vector just before coordinate
j
is set to zero. Multilinearity makes

F
affine in coordinate
j
, and
\partial_{j}F(\mathbf{x})
is independent of

x_{j}
. Therefore replacing
x_{j}>0
by
0
changes the value by

F(\mathbf{x}-x_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j})-F(\mathbf{x})=-x_{j}\,\partial_{j}F(\mathbf{x}).

The pruning rule fires only when
\partial_{j}F(\mathbf{x})\leq 0
, so the displayed
change is non-negative, and it is strict when the slope is strictly
negative. Applying this argument to each pass of the while-loop gives

F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})\geq F(S_{i+1})
.
∎

Lemma 5

(Nonneg. slope after pruning)
.

At every iterate
\tilde{S}_{i}
(for
i\geq 1
) and every
j\in\mathcal{N}
,
either
(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}=0
or
\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})>0
.

Proof.

Direct from the pruning loop termination condition
(Line
2
): the loop exits exactly when no coordinate
has both positive value and non-positive slope.
∎

Lemma
5
is the structural property that replaces the
pointwise curvature argument when
f
is allowed to be negative. In
ordinary MCG,
c_{F}
can diverge because the definition of
c_{F}
ranges
over
all

\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
, in particular over witnesses of
the form
\mathbf{x}_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\setminus\{e^{\prime}\}}
from
Proposition
7
, which
concentrate mass on inclusion-minimal sets with
f<0
.
Lemma
5
shows that DMCG-P’s iterates never lie at such
witnesses: at each
\tilde{S}_{i}
, every active coordinate has positive
multilinear slope, so the iterate avoids the neighbourhood where
multilinear curvature blows up.

Remark 4

(Slope signs after pruning)
.

A coordinate whose slope is non-positive at one iterate need not keep a
non-positive slope forever. Pruning may reduce

(\tilde{S}_{i})_{k}
for some
k\neq j
, which by submodularity can

increase

\partial_{j}F
and flip its sign. The useful invariant is
the weaker one stated in Lemma
5
: after each pruning
loop terminates, every coordinate with positive mass has positive slope.
Within a stretch of iterations without pruning, slopes are monotone
non-increasing, but the proof below uses only the post-pruning invariant.

H.3
General DMCG-P Guarantee

We now prove the headline analytic result: unweighted DMCG-P
(Algorithm
2
) on an
arbitrary
submodular
objective
f
over the integral feasible-set hull

P=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf{1}_{B}:B\in\mathcal{I}\}
matches the classical continuous-greedy guarantee

(1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}})/\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\cdot f(O^{*})
in the
T\to\infty
limit,
with
c_{g}^{F}
a trajectory-restricted multilinear analogue of the
greedy curvature
c_{g}
of Definition
2
. No
monotonicity or non-negativity of
f
is assumed; the only
hypothesis is that the trajectory admits a finite curvature
certificate. Lemma
5
supplies the pruning
invariant used in the descent proof, but it is not by itself a
global finiteness theorem for all possible denominators.
The proof is a direct line-by-line lift of the discrete argument for
Theorem
1
: each discrete inequality—pruning, greedy
selection, submodularity, curvature, and the
slope invariant

on the active set (Lemma
5
)—has a multilinear
analogue spelled out below, requiring only submodularity of
f
or
finite on-trajectory slope, never monotonicity of
f
. The
per-step recurrence closes in exactly the same form, modulo a
discretisation correction of size
O(1/T)
. We state the finite-
T

bound in the regime
\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\leq T
(equivalently
\delta\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\leq 1

for step size
\delta=1/T
), which is always achievable by choosing

T
large enough for any fixed finite trajectory curvature.
This is deliberately different from the conditional MCG comparison in
Appendix
C
: DMCG-P does not assume a global coordinate-zeroing
monotonicity lemma, but enforces the local positive-slope condition needed
for Step 7 by pruning.

Key inequalities.

The analysis relies on three elementary properties of the multilinear
extension of a submodular
f
. Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
and

\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{N}}
with
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{d}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
.

(P1) Concavity along non-negative directions.

The map
t\mapsto F(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{d})
is concave on

[0,\min_{j}\{(1-x_{j})/d_{j}:d_{j}>0\}]

[
5
, §2.3]
.

(P2) Tangent bounds.

By (P1),
F(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{d})-F(\mathbf{x})\leq\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}),\,\mathbf{d}\rangle

(upper tangent at the left endpoint), and equivalently

F(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{d})-F(\mathbf{x})\geq\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{d}),\,\mathbf{d}\rangle

(lower tangent at the right endpoint).

(P3) Submodular union bound.

F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{x})\leq\sum_{j}(1-x_{j})\,y_{j}\,\partial_{j}F(\mathbf{x}).

Proof.
Telescope coordinate-wise from
\mathbf{x}
to
\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y}
,
changing coordinate
j
(for which
y_{j}>x_{j}
) from
x_{j}
to

y_{j}\lor x_{j}
; the per-coordinate gain is

(y_{j}-x_{j})_{+}\,\partial_{j}F(\cdot)
evaluated at the intermediate
point, bounded above by
(y_{j}-x_{j})_{+}\,\partial_{j}F(\mathbf{x})
by
submodularity (cross-partials
\leq 0
). Summing and using

(y_{j}-x_{j})_{+}\leq(1-x_{j})y_{j}
for
y_{j}\in[0,1]
gives (P3).

Fractional greedy curvature.

The relevant multilinear curvature is not
c_{F}
(which can be
+\infty

by Proposition
7
)
but its trajectory-restricted analogue, mirroring the discrete greedy
curvature
c_{g}
of Definition
2
.

Definition 5

(Fractional greedy curvature along a trajectory;
restated from Definition
4
)
.

Let
F
be the multilinear extension of a submodular
f
and let

\mathcal{O}=\argmax_{B\in\mathcal{I}}f(B)
denote the set of optimal feasible
sets.
For the sequence of iterates
\tilde{S}_{0},\dots,\tilde{S}_{T-1}\in P
produced by
\textsc{DMCG-P}(F,P,T)
, the
fractional greedy
curvature
is

c_{g}^{F}\;=\;1\;-\;\min_{O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}}\;\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<T\\
F(\tilde{S}_{i}-\tilde{S}_{i}\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})>0\end{subarray}}\;\frac{F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})}{F(\tilde{S}_{i}-\tilde{S}_{i}\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})}.

Remark 5

(Finiteness on DMCG-P trajectories)
.

Definition
5
restricts the minimisation
to the
T
trajectory points
\{\tilde{S}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{T-1}
, not the
whole cube, and only positive-denominator pairs are admissible in the
inner minimum. Lemma
5
ensures that active
coordinates have strictly positive multilinear slope after pruning,
which is the structural reason the algorithm avoids the particular
concentration witnesses used to prove
c_{F}=\infty
in
Proposition
7
. The theorem below therefore keeps

c_{g}^{F}<\infty
as an explicit trajectory hypothesis. In applications
one proves this hypothesis, or upper-bounds
c_{g}^{F}
, using a certificate
such as Proposition
9
.

Per-step descent lemma.

Lemma 6

(Per-step descent)
.

Let
f
be submodular on
P
(not necessarily monotone or
non-negative), fix
O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
, and let
c_{g}^{F}\in[0,\infty)

be a finite fractional greedy curvature value from
Definition
5
. Put

\bar{c}_{g}^{F}=\max\{1,c_{g}^{F}\}
. Then for every

i\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
,

F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{T}\bigl[\,F(\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})\;-\;\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\,F(\tilde{S}_{i})\,\bigr]\;-\;\tfrac{1}{T^{2}}\,E_{i},

(4)

where the per-step discretisation error satisfies

0\leq E_{i}\leq n(n-1)\,C_{F}
and

\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}(1/T^{2})E_{i}\leq n(n-1)C_{F}/T
.

Proof.

We mirror the five-line discrete proof of
Theorem
1
, with each
step justified by the multilinear analogue. Throughout, write

v_{i}\in P
for the LP maximiser selected on
Line
2
; by construction

v_{i}=\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}
for some feasible set
B_{i}\in\mathcal{I}
, and we use
this integral representation in Steps 2–3.

Step 1 (pruning).

By Lemma
4
,
F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})\geq F(S_{i+1})=F(\tilde{S}_{i}+(1/T)\,v_{i})
.

Step 2 (concavity, right-endpoint tangent).

By (P2) applied with
\mathbf{x}=\tilde{S}_{i}
,
\mathbf{d}=(1/T)\,v_{i}

(both endpoints in
[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
by Lemma
3
):

F\bigl(\tilde{S}_{i}+\tfrac{1}{T}\,v_{i}\bigr)-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{T}\,\langle\nabla F(S_{i+1}),\,v_{i}\rangle.

Step 3 (left-to-right gradient substitution).

Define the per-step discretisation error

E_{i}\;\triangleq\;T\!\sum_{j\in B_{i}}\bigl[\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})-\partial_{j}F(S_{i+1})\bigr].

(5)

Since
v_{i}=\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}
, this gives the
exact
identity

\langle\nabla F(S_{i+1}),\,v_{i}\rangle\;=\;\sum_{j\in B_{i}}\partial_{j}F(S_{i+1})\;=\;\sum_{j\in B_{i}}\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;-\;\tfrac{1}{T}E_{i}\;=\;\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,v_{i}\rangle\;-\;\tfrac{1}{T}E_{i}.

(6)

Non-negativity
E_{i}\geq 0
holds because submodularity
(
\partial_{j\ell}F\leq 0
for
j\neq\ell
) makes each

\partial_{j}F
non-increasing in every other coordinate, so

\partial_{j}F(S_{i+1})\leq\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})
for every

j\in B_{i}
. For the upper bound, write

S_{i+1}=\tilde{S}_{i}+(1/T)\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}
and apply the
mean-value theorem coordinate-by-coordinate:

\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})-\partial_{j}F(S_{i+1})\;=\;-\sum_{\ell\in B_{i}\setminus j}\frac{1}{T}\,\partial_{j\ell}F(\xi_{j\ell})\;\leq\;\frac{|B_{i}|-1}{T}\,C_{F},

where
\xi_{j\ell}
lies on the segment
[\tilde{S}_{i},S_{i+1}]
and

C_{F}\triangleq\max_{j\neq\ell,\,\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}}|\partial_{j\ell}F(\mathbf{x})|
is finite for any set function on
\mathcal{N}
.
Multiplying by
T
and summing over
j\in B_{i}
:

E_{i}\;\leq\;|B_{i}|(|B_{i}|-1)\,C_{F}\;\leq\;n(n-1)\,C_{F}.

Since
|B_{i}|\leq n
, the total discretisation error is

\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}(1/T^{2})\,E_{i}\leq n(n-1)\,C_{F}/T
.

Step 4 (linear selection, unweighted).

Since Line
2
picks
v_{i}
to maximise

\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,x\rangle
over all
x\in P
and

\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}\in P
(as
O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
),

\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,v_{i}\rangle\;\geq\;\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}\rangle\;=\;\sum_{j\in O^{*}}\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i}).

This is a direct consequence of LP optimality and does not require
matroid structure.

Step 5 (submodular union bound, with slope monotonicity).

By (P3) applied with
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}
:

F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\leq\;\sum_{j\in O^{*}}(1-(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j})\,\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\leq\;\sum_{j\in O^{*}}\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i}),

where the second inequality uses
(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}\in[0,1]
together
with Lemma
5
, which guarantees

\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})\geq 0
whenever
(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}>0

(and trivially whenever
(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}=0
then

1-(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}=1
). Combining Steps 4 and 5:

\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,v_{i}\rangle\;\geq\;F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i}).

(7)

Step 6 (fractional curvature).

Let

\mathbf{w}_{i}\;\triangleq\;\tilde{S}_{i}-\tilde{S}_{i}\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}.

If
\mathbf{w}_{i}=0
, then

\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}=\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}
and the displayed inequality
below is an equality. Otherwise, every coordinate in
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{w}_{i})

is active in
\tilde{S}_{i}
, so Lemma
5
gives

\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})>0
for each
j\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{w}_{i})
. Since

t\mathbf{w}_{i}\leq\tilde{S}_{i}
for
t\in[0,1]
, submodularity of the
multilinear extension gives

\partial_{j}F(t\mathbf{w}_{i})\geq\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})>0
on this support.
With
F(\mathbf{0})=0
,

F(\mathbf{w}_{i})=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla F(t\mathbf{w}_{i}),\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle\,dt>0.

Thus, in the nonzero case, the present pair appears in
Definition
5
. Since

\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\geq c_{g}^{F}
, the curvature inequality is also valid with

\bar{c}_{g}^{F}
in place of
c_{g}^{F}
; in the zero case, the same display
holds trivially. Hence

F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})\;\geq\;(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F})\,F(\mathbf{w}_{i}),

so

F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\geq\;F(\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})+(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F})\,F(\tilde{S}_{i}-\tilde{S}_{i}\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i}).

Step 7 (zeroing the
O^{*}
-coordinates of
\tilde{S}_{i}
only decreases
F
).

The vector
\tilde{S}_{i}-\tilde{S}_{i}\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}
is obtained
from
\tilde{S}_{i}
by zeroing every coordinate
j\in O^{*}
. Fix any
order for this sequential zeroing. When coordinate
j
is reached, all
other changes made so far have only decreased coordinates. By
submodularity of the multilinear extension, decreasing other coordinates
can only increase the remaining slope
\partial_{j}F
; hence
Lemma
5
implies that either the current value of
coordinate
j
is zero, or the current slope in direction
j
is still
nonnegative. Multilinearity in coordinate
j
therefore implies that
zeroing
j
changes
F
by

-(\text{current }j\text{-mass})\,\partial_{j}F(\cdot)\leq 0
. Iterating
over
j\in O^{*}
gives

F(\tilde{S}_{i}-\tilde{S}_{i}\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})\;\leq\;F(\tilde{S}_{i}).

Substituting this into the previous display and expanding

(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F})
:

F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\geq\;F(\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})\;-\;\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\,F(\tilde{S}_{i}).

Assembly.

Chaining Steps 1–3, 4–5 (as (
7
)), and 6–7:

\displaystyle F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\;\tfrac{1}{T}\,\langle\nabla F(S_{i+1}),\,v_{i}\rangle

(Steps 1–2)

\displaystyle\geq\;\tfrac{1}{T}\,\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,v_{i}\rangle\;-\;\tfrac{1}{T^{2}}E_{i}

(Step 3)

\displaystyle\geq\;\tfrac{1}{T}\bigl[F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\bigr]\;-\;\tfrac{1}{T^{2}}E_{i}

(Steps 4–5)

\displaystyle\geq\;\tfrac{1}{T}\bigl[F(\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\,F(\tilde{S}_{i})\bigr]\;-\;\tfrac{1}{T^{2}}E_{i}

\displaystyle\text{(Steps 6--7)}.

This is (
4
).
Finally,
\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}(1/T^{2})E_{i}\leq n(n-1)\,C_{F}/T

by the bound on
E_{i}
from (
5
); hence the aggregate
discretisation error is
O(1/T)
and vanishes as
T\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 6

(On the discretisation error)
.

It is tempting to claim that the per-step equality

F\bigl(\tilde{S}_{i}+\tfrac{1}{T}\,v_{i}\bigr)-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;=\;\tfrac{1}{T}\,\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,v_{i}\rangle

holds exactly, “because
F
is multilinear.” This is false in
general: multilinearity makes
F
linear in
each coordinate
separately
, but along a direction
v_{i}
with multiple non-zero
entries,
t\mapsto F(\mathbf{x}+t\,v_{i})
is a polynomial of degree up to

|\operatorname{supp}(v_{i})|
in
t
, with quadratic coefficients

\partial_{j\ell}F\leq 0
(submodularity) and bounded higher-order
cross-terms.
The cleanest exact statement is (P2): the secant slope exceeds the
right-endpoint gradient slope, so

F(\mathbf{x}+t\,v)-F(\mathbf{x})\geq t\,\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}+t\,v),\,v\rangle
.
Replacing the right-endpoint gradient with the left-endpoint one costs

O(t)=O(1/T)
per step via (
6
), which sums to

O(1/T)
total—vanishing in the limit but not zero at finite
T
.
The
T\to\infty
bound is unaffected; the finite-
T
bound carries
this additive
O(1/T)
penalty.

Theorem 7

(General DMCG-P guarantee)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq 2^{\mathcal{N}}
be downward-closed, let

P=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf{1}_{B}:B\in\mathcal{I}\}
, and assume exact linear optimization
over
\mathcal{I}
. Let
f:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
be submodular (not necessarily
monotone or non-negative) and let
O^{*}\in\argmax_{B\in\mathcal{I}}f(B)
.
Let
n=|\mathcal{N}|
, and let
c_{g}^{F}\in[0,\infty)
be a finite fractional greedy curvature
value for the trajectory of
\textsc{DMCG-P}(F,P,T)
from
Definition
5
.
Put
\bar{c}_{g}^{F}=\max\{1,c_{g}^{F}\}
. If
\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\leq T

(equivalently
\delta\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\leq 1
for
\delta=1/T
),
then the output
\tilde{S}_{T}
satisfies

F(\tilde{S}_{T})\;\geq\;\frac{1-(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T)^{T}}{\bar{c}_{g}^{F}}\,f(O^{*})\;-\;\frac{n(n-1)\,C_{F}}{T}\;\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}\;\frac{1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}}}{\bar{c}_{g}^{F}}\,f(O^{*}).

The step count
T
is independent of the cardinality or matroid-rank
constraint; choosing
T
large reduces the discretization error
at the cost of
O(Tn)
oracle calls.
The sharper small-curvature formula is recovered in
the monotone setting by the classical analysis.

Proof.

Let
a_{i}\triangleq F(\tilde{S}_{i})
,
A\triangleq F(\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})=f(O^{*})
,
put
\bar{c}_{g}^{F}=\max\{1,c_{g}^{F}\}
, and let

\delta_{i}\triangleq(1/T^{2})E_{i}
from
Lemma
6
. Equation (
4
)
(which holds for arbitrary submodular
f
, without monotonicity) gives
the linear recurrence

a_{i+1}\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{T}A\;+\;\bigl(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T\bigr)\,a_{i}\;-\;\delta_{i},\qquad a_{0}=0.

Because
\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\leq T
, the recurrence coefficient

1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T\geq 0
. Iterating:

a_{T}\;\geq\;\tfrac{A}{T}\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\bigl(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T\bigr)^{i}\;-\;\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\bigl(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T\bigr)^{T-1-i}\,\delta_{i}.

The geometric sum evaluates to

\tfrac{A}{T}\cdot\tfrac{1-(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T)^{T}}{\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T}=\tfrac{A}{\bar{c}_{g}^{F}}\bigl(1-(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T)^{T}\bigr)
,
and each factor
(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T)^{T-1-i}\in[0,1]
(since

\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T\in[0,1]
), so the error term is bounded by

\sum_{i}\delta_{i}=O(1/T)
. This gives the stated finite-
T

bound with
\sum_{i}\delta_{i}\leq n(n-1)\,C_{F}/T
. Sending
T\to\infty
for
fixed finite
\bar{c}_{g}^{F}
,

(1-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}/T)^{T}\to e^{-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}}
by the elementary exponential limit,
the error vanishes, and the limit is

(1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}})/\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\cdot f(O^{*})
.
∎

Remark 7

(Reduction to classical CC curvature)
.

When
f
is monotone submodular (so
c_{g}^{F}\leq c_{F}=c_{f}=\alpha_{g}\in[0,1]
by Theorem
3
(a), with

\alpha_{g}
the classical Conforti–Cornuéjols curvature),
the classical continuous-greedy analysis yields

F(\tilde{S}_{T})\geq(1-e^{-\alpha_{g}})/\alpha_{g}\cdot f(O^{*})
in the
limit
T\to\infty
. The non-monotone DMCG-P recurrence above uses

\bar{c}_{g}^{F}=\max\{1,c_{g}^{F}\}
; the sharper small-curvature formula
comes from monotonicity, not from Step 7 of the non-monotone proof.
When
f
takes negative values,
c_{F}=\infty
globally by
Proposition
7
; Theorem
7

still applies whenever its finite trajectory-curvature
hypothesis is verified. For decomposable objectives,
§
H.4
supplies a closed-form certificate for
that hypothesis.

Why this works for unweighted greedy.

The unweighted selection rule is sufficient because the proof needs an
upper bound on the union gain, not a lower bound on a weighted gradient.
(P3) gives

F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\leq\sum_{j\in O^{*}}(1-(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j})\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})
,
and the analysis then upper-bounds this weighted sum by
the unweighted sum
\sum_{j\in O^{*}}\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})=\langle\nabla F(\tilde{S}_{i}),\,\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}\rangle
. The latter is free when

\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})\geq 0
for every
j\in O^{*}
—which is
exactly what Lemma
5
plus the
O^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{N}

case analysis in Step 5 of the proof provides (coordinates
j\in O^{*}

with
(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}=0
have
1-(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}=1
trivially;
coordinates with
(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}>0
have
\partial_{j}F\geq 0
by
Lemma
5
, so

(1-(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j})\partial_{j}F\leq\partial_{j}F
regardless).
Thus unweighted DMCG-P—the feasible-set form

B_{i}\in\arg\max_{B\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{j\in B}\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})
with

v_{i}=\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}
—is the correct clean algorithm and the discrete
proof lifts directly.

H.4
Decomposable Certificate for
c_{g}^{F}

Theorem
7
delivers the headline DMCG-P
guarantee
(1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}})/\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\cdot f(O^{*})
conditional on

c_{g}^{F}<\infty
and
\delta\bar{c}_{g}^{F}\leq 1
. Lemma
5

provides the positive-slope invariant used in the proof, but the
theorem is most useful when

c_{g}^{F}
can be
bounded
in closed form. This subsection
provides such a bound for the
decomposable
case
f=g-\ell
, mirroring the discrete removal-marginal certificate

c_{g}\leq\alpha_{g}/(1-\hat{r})

of Proposition
4
. The content here is a

computational certificate for
c_{g}^{F}
, not a separate
approximation guarantee: plugging the certificate into
Theorem
7
recovers the
(1-e^{-\bar{c}_{g}^{F}})/\bar{c}_{g}^{F}

bound for negative-valued

.
We first fix the setting
(§
H.4.1
), then prove the multilinear lift of
the CC-curvature inequality that the analysis requires
(§
H.4.2
), state the OPT-aware comparison bound
(§
H.4.3
), obtain the OPT-free certificate

c_{g}^{F}\leq\alpha_{g}/(1-\hat{r}_{F})
from the pruned trajectory
(§
H.4.4
), and assemble the decomposable-certificate
proposition (§
H.4.5
).

H.4.1
Setting

Throughout this subsection,
f=g-\ell
where

•

g:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is monotone submodular with
Conforti–Cornuéjols curvature
\alpha_{g}\in[0,1]
,
characterised
(Definition
1
and equation (
1
))
by

g(X\cup Y)-g(Y)\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})\,g(X\setminus Y)\qquad\forall\,X,Y\subseteq\mathcal{N};

(8)

•

\ell:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is non-negative modular with
per-element costs
\ell_{j}\triangleq\ell(\{j\})
, so

\ell(A)=\sum_{j\in A}\ell_{j}
for every
A\subseteq\mathcal{N}
.

The multilinear extensions decompose as
F=G-L
, with
G
the
multilinear extension of
g
(monotone submodular) and

L(\mathbf{x})\;=\;\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}}\ell_{j}x_{j}

(9)

exactly linear in
\mathbf{x}
. Note that
f
itself may take negative
values, so
c_{F}=\infty
by
Proposition
7
; the
analysis below operates entirely through the trajectory-restricted
curvature
c_{g}^{F}
of Definition
5
, which
is bounded by the decomposable certificates proved below.

H.4.2
Multilinear CC-curvature inequality for
G

The multilinear extension
G
of a monotone submodular
g
with CC
curvature
\alpha_{g}
inherits a fractional form of the set
inequality (
8
). We state and prove the precise
form required for the decomposable analysis. Related multilinear
inequalities are standard
(e.g.
[
5
]
),
but the statement below—comparing
G(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})
with

G(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})
—does not seem to appear in this form in
the literature, so we include a self-contained proof.

Lemma 7

(Multilinear CC-curvature inequality, elementwise form)
.

Let
g:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be monotone submodular with CC
curvature
\alpha_{g}\in[0,1]
, and let
G
be its multilinear
extension. For all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
, writing

\mathbf{w}\triangleq\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y}
(so
w_{j}=(x_{j}-y_{j})_{+}
),

G(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-G(\mathbf{y})\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}}w_{j}\,g(\{j\}).

(10)

In particular, by
g
-subadditivity

G(\mathbf{w})\leq\sum_{j}w_{j}\,g(\{j\})
, the weaker set-style inequality

G(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-G(\mathbf{y})\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})\,G(\mathbf{w})

(11)

follows by combining the elementwise bound with subadditivity.

Proof.

The elementwise bound (
10
) follows from the
pointwise gradient inequality

\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{z})\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})\,g(\{j\})\qquad\text{for every }\mathbf{z}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}\text{ and every }j\in\mathcal{N},

(12)

integrated along the straight-line interpolation from
\mathbf{y}
to

\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y}
.

Step 1 (pointwise gradient lower bound).

Submodularity of
g
is equivalent to the mixed-partial condition

\partial_{j\ell}G\leq 0
for
\ell\neq j
, i.e.
\partial_{j}G
is
coordinate-wise non-increasing in every other coordinate. Hence for
any
\mathbf{z}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
,

\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{z})\;\geq\;\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}})\;=\;g(\mathcal{N})-g(\mathcal{N}\setminus\{j\})\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})\,g(\{j\}),

where the final inequality is the CC-curvature definition

\alpha_{g}=1-\min_{e,S\not\ni e}\Delta_{g}(e\mid S)/g(\{e\})

specialised to
S=\mathcal{N}\setminus\{j\}
. This
gives (
12
) (normalisation of
g
is not
needed).

Step 2 (telescoping via multilinearity).

Order
\mathcal{N}=\{1,\dots,n\}
arbitrarily and interpolate from
\mathbf{y}

to
\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y}
one coordinate at a time: set
\mathbf{z}^{(0)}=\mathbf{y}

and
\mathbf{z}^{(j)}=\mathbf{z}^{(j-1)}+w_{j}\,\mathbf{e}_{j}
, so

\mathbf{z}^{(n)}=\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y}
(using

\max(x_{j},y_{j})=y_{j}+(x_{j}-y_{j})_{+}
). By multilinearity of
G
in
each coordinate separately,

G(\mathbf{z}^{(j)})-G(\mathbf{z}^{(j-1)})\;=\;w_{j}\,\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{z}^{(j-1)}),

and summing,

G(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-G(\mathbf{y})\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\,\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{z}^{(j-1)}).

(13)

Applying (
12
) at each

\mathbf{z}^{(j-1)}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
gives (
10
).

Step 3 (set-style corollary).

By
g\geq 0
and submodularity,
g
is subadditive:

g(A)\leq\sum_{e\in A}g(\{e\})
. Taking expectations under

R\sim\mathbf{w}
(coordinate-wise independent Bernoullis),

G(\mathbf{w})=\mathbb{E}[g(R)]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sum_{j\in R}g(\{j\})\bigr]=\sum_{j}w_{j}\,g(\{j\})
. Combining with (
10
)
yields (
11
).
∎

Remark 8

(Strength of the elementwise form)
.

The elementwise inequality (
10
) is strictly
stronger than the set-style form (
11
), since the
subadditivity step
G(\mathbf{w})\leq\sum_{j}w_{j}\,g(\{j\})
can be strict.
Both forms are used downstream: (
11
) is the clean
multilinear analogue of (
8
), while
(
10
) is what drives the fractional OPT-free
certificate in §
H.4.4
.

H.4.3
OPT-aware decomposable bound

For each iterate write

\mathbf{w}_{i}\;\triangleq\;\tilde{S}_{i}-\tilde{S}_{i}\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}\qquad\text{(coordinate $j$: $(\mathbf{w}_{i})_{j}=(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}\,\mathbf{1}[j\notin O^{*}]$),}

(14)

the “outside-OPT” fractional mass. Since
O^{*}
is a set and

\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}
is
\{0,1\}
-valued,
\tilde{S}_{i}\land\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}
zeros
out the coordinates
j\in\mathcal{N}\setminus O^{*}
, so

\mathbf{w}_{i}=\tilde{S}_{i}\odot\mathbf{1}[\cdot\notin O^{*}]
is literally

\tilde{S}_{i}
restricted to
\mathcal{N}\setminus O^{*}
. Define the
trajectory-level cost ratio

\rho_{F}\;\triangleq\;\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}\\
0\leq i<T\\
G(\mathbf{w}_{i})>0\end{subarray}}\,\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{i})}{G(\mathbf{w}_{i})}.

(15)

(If
G(\mathbf{w}_{i})=0
for every
i,O^{*}
, set
\rho_{F}=0
; the curvature
definition then does not involve any active constraint from the
decomposable side.)

Proposition 8

(OPT-aware fractional curvature bound)
.

Let
f=g-\ell
be decomposable as
in §
H.4.1
, and consider the trajectory of

\textsc{DMCG-P}(F,P,T)
. If
\rho_{F}<1
, then the fractional greedy
curvature of Definition
5
satisfies

c_{g}^{F}\;\leq\;\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-\rho_{F}}.

(16)

Proof.

Fix
O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
and
i\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
with

F(\mathbf{w}_{i})>0
(the only indices active in
Definition
5
); write
\mathbf{x}=\tilde{S}_{i}

and
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}
, so
\mathbf{w}_{i}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y}
. Decompose

F=G-L
and expand the numerator of the curvature ratio:

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigl[\,G(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-G(\mathbf{y})\,\bigr]\;-\;\bigl[\,L(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-L(\mathbf{y})\,\bigr]

\displaystyle\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})\,G(\mathbf{w}_{i})\;-\;\bigl[\,L(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-L(\mathbf{y})\,\bigr]

by Lemma
7
.

For the
L
side, linearity of
L
gives

L(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})+L(\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})=L(\mathbf{x})+L(\mathbf{y})

(coordinate-wise:
\max(x_{j},y_{j})+\min(x_{j},y_{j})=x_{j}+y_{j}
), so

L(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-L(\mathbf{y})\;=\;L(\mathbf{x})-L(\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})\;=\;L(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})\;=\;L(\mathbf{w}_{i}).

(17)

Let
\rho_{i}\triangleq L(\mathbf{w}_{i})/G(\mathbf{w}_{i})
(finite by

G(\mathbf{w}_{i})>0
, since
F(\mathbf{w}_{i})>0
and
L\geq 0
together imply

G(\mathbf{w}_{i})>0
). Then

F(\mathbf{w}_{i})=G(\mathbf{w}_{i})-L(\mathbf{w}_{i})=(1-\rho_{i})\,G(\mathbf{w}_{i})
, and

F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{y})\;\geq\;\bigl[(1-\alpha_{g})-\rho_{i}\bigr]\,G(\mathbf{w}_{i}).

Dividing by
F(\mathbf{w}_{i})=(1-\rho_{i})\,G(\mathbf{w}_{i})>0
,

\frac{F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{y})}{F(\mathbf{w}_{i})}\;\geq\;\frac{(1-\alpha_{g})-\rho_{i}}{1-\rho_{i}}\;=\;1-\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-\rho_{i}}\;\geq\;1-\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-\rho_{F}},

(18)

using
\rho_{i}\leq\rho_{F}<1
and monotonicity of

t\mapsto\alpha_{g}/(1-t)
on
[0,1)
. Taking the infimum over

i
and
O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
and applying
Definition
5
gives

c_{g}^{F}\leq\alpha_{g}/(1-\rho_{F})
.
∎

H.4.4
OPT-free fractional certificate

Proposition
8
bounds
c_{g}^{F}
in terms of
the OPT-dependent vectors
\mathbf{w}_{i}
. For a deployable certificate we
want an OPT-free quantity computed directly from the DMCG-P trajectory.
The right fractional analogue of the discrete removal marginal

\Delta_{g}(e\mid A_{i}\setminus\{e\})
is the current multilinear
slope
\partial_{j}G(\tilde{S}_{i})
.

Define the removal-slope ratio

\hat{r}_{F}\;\triangleq\;\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<T\\
j:\,(\tilde{S}_{i})_{j}>0\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell_{j}}{\partial_{j}G(\tilde{S}_{i})},

(19)

with value
0
if the index set is empty.
Lemma
5
gives

\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})=\partial_{j}G(\tilde{S}_{i})-\ell_{j}>0

on every active coordinate, so every denominator in
(
19
) is positive and
\hat{r}_{F}<1
.

Lemma 8

(Removal-slope domination)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\tilde{S}_{i}
be a DMCG-P iterate and let

\mathbf{w}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
satisfy
0\leq\mathbf{w}\leq\mathbf{x}
coordinate-wise and

\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{w})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{x})
. Then

L(\mathbf{w})\;\leq\;\hat{r}_{F}\,G(\mathbf{w}).

Proof.

By definition of
\hat{r}_{F}
, for every coordinate with
w_{j}>0
,

\ell_{j}\leq\hat{r}_{F}\,\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{x})
. Hence

L(\mathbf{w})=\sum_{j}w_{j}\ell_{j}\;\leq\;\hat{r}_{F}\sum_{j}w_{j}\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{x}).

It remains to compare the weighted slope sum with
G(\mathbf{w})
.
Since
G
is the multilinear extension of a monotone submodular
function, it is DR-submodular: each partial derivative is
coordinate-wise non-increasing. For
t\in[0,1]
, we have

t\mathbf{w}\leq\mathbf{w}\leq\mathbf{x}
, so

\partial_{j}G(t\mathbf{w})\geq\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{x})
. Using normalization

G(\mathbf{0})=0
and integrating along the ray
t\mathbf{w}
,

G(\mathbf{w})=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla G(t\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}\rangle\,dt\;\geq\;\sum_{j}w_{j}\partial_{j}G(\mathbf{x}).

Combining the two displays gives the claim.
∎

Theorem 8

(OPT-free fractional curvature certificate)
.

Let
f=g-\ell
be decomposable as in §
H.4.1

and consider the trajectory of
\textsc{DMCG-P}(F,P,T)
. The fractional greedy curvature of
Definition
5
satisfies

c_{g}^{F}\;\leq\;\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-\hat{r}_{F}}.

(20)

Proof.

Fix
O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
and
i\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
with
F(\mathbf{w}_{i})>0
,
and write
\mathbf{x}=\tilde{S}_{i}
,
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}
, so

\mathbf{w}_{i}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y}
. Since
0\leq\mathbf{w}_{i}\leq\mathbf{x}
and

\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{x})
,
Lemma
8
gives

L(\mathbf{w}_{i})\leq\hat{r}_{F}\,G(\mathbf{w}_{i}).

(21)

The set-style multilinear CC
inequality (
11
) applied to
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}
gives

G(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-G(\mathbf{y})\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})G(\mathbf{w}_{i}),

(22)

and modularity of
\ell
(as in (
17
)) gives

L(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-L(\mathbf{y})=L(\mathbf{w}_{i})
.
Let
\tau\triangleq L(\mathbf{w}_{i})/G(\mathbf{w}_{i})
. The positive-denominator
assumption
F(\mathbf{w}_{i})>0
implies
\tau<1
, and
(
21
) implies

Then

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigl[G(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-G(\mathbf{y})\bigr]-L(\mathbf{w}_{i})

\displaystyle\;\geq\;(1-\alpha_{g})\,G(\mathbf{w}_{i})-L(\mathbf{w}_{i})\;=\;\bigl[(1-\alpha_{g})-\tau\bigr]\,G(\mathbf{w}_{i}),

The right-hand side is decreasing in

, since its derivative is

. Because

,

\frac{F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{y})}{F(\mathbf{w}_{i})}\;\geq\;1-\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-\hat{r}_{F}}.

Taking the infimum over
i
and
O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
and applying
Definition
5

yields (
20
).
∎

H.5
Non-Negative Non-Monotone Regime via Damped wDMCG-P

The headline DMCG-P guarantee (Theorem

H.5.2
Feasibility and coordinate bound

Lemma 9

(Feasibility)
.

For every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
,
\tilde{S}_{i}\in P
and

\tilde{S}_{i}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
.

Proof.

For each coordinate
j
, let
m_{i,j}\triangleq|\{\tau<i:j\in B_{\tau}\}|
count the steps up to
i
in which

j
is selected; recursion

\tilde{S}_{i+1,j}=\tilde{S}_{i,j}+\delta(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})\mathbf{1}_{B_{i},j}

unrolls (ignoring prunings, which only decrease coordinates) to the
closed form

\tilde{S}_{i,j}\;\leq\;1-(1-\delta)^{m_{i,j}},

(25)

with equality in the absence of pruning. By Bernoulli’s inequality

(1-\delta)^{m_{i,j}}\geq 1-\delta\,m_{i,j}
, so

\tilde{S}_{i,j}\leq\delta\,m_{i,j}
. Consequently

\tilde{S}_{i}\;\leq\;\delta\sum_{\tau<i}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\tau}}\;=\;\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\tau<i}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\tau}}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{k}\Bigl(\sum_{\tau<i}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\tau}}+(k-i)\,\mathbf{0}\Bigr)

coordinate-wise, and the right-hand side is a convex combination of

i
base-indicators
\mathbf{1}_{B_{\tau}}\in P
(and
k-i
copies of

\mathbf{0}\in P
), hence lies in
P
by convexity of
P
. Since
P

is downward-closed and
\tilde{S}_{i}
is coordinate-wise dominated by
a point of
P
,
\tilde{S}_{i}\in P
. The bound

\tilde{S}_{i,j}\leq 1-(1-\delta)^{m_{i,j}}<1

also gives
\tilde{S}_{i}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
. Finally, pruning only
zeroes coordinates, preserving membership in the downward-closed
polytope
P
.
∎

Lemma 10

(Coordinate bound)
.

For every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
,

\lVert\tilde{S}_{i}\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1-(1-\delta)^{i}
.

Proof.

By (
25
),
\tilde{S}_{i,j}\leq 1-(1-\delta)^{m_{i,j}}
with
m_{i,j}\leq i
; taking the maximum
over

and using monotonicity of

gives the bound.
∎

H.5.3
Per-step descent lemma

Write
O^{*}\in\argmax_{\mathbf{1}_{S}\in P}\,f(S)
for a combinatorial
optimum.
For
r\geq 2
, define

M_{F,r}\;\triangleq\;\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\subseteq\mathcal{N},\ |R|=r\\
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}\end{subarray}}|\partial_{R}F(\mathbf{x})|,\qquad K_{F}\;\triangleq\;\sum_{r=2}^{n}\binom{n}{r}M_{F,r}.

This finite constant controls the full multilinear Taylor remainder
along any update support; the pairwise constant
C_{F}=M_{F,2}
alone
does not control third- and higher-order mixed terms.

Lemma 11

(Per-step descent, non-negative case)
.

Let
f:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be submodular and let
F
be its
multilinear extension. For every
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
,

F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\geq\;\delta\bigl[\,(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*})\;-\;F(\tilde{S}_{i})\,\bigr]\;-\;K_{F}\,\delta^{2}.

(26)

Proof.

We chain four explicitly stated inequalities: multilinear expansion with a
bounded higher-order remainder, weighted LP optimality, the submodular
union bound (P3), and the Feldman–Naor–Schwartz correlation-gap bound.
The
K_{F}\delta^{2}
term is a uniform bound on the full second-and-higher-order
multilinear correction and sums to
K_{F}/k
over all
k
steps.

Step (S1) Multilinear expansion.

The update
\tilde{S}_{i+1}=\tilde{S}_{i}+\delta(1-\tilde{S}_{i})\odot\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}
moves coordinates
j\in B_{i}
by

\delta(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})
. Expanding
F
along the direction

\mathbf{d}_{i}\triangleq\delta\,(1-\tilde{S}_{i})\odot\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}
by
multilinearity,

\displaystyle F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{j\in B_{i}}\delta\,(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})\,\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\sum_{\{j,\ell\}\subseteq B_{i}}\delta^{2}\,(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})(1-\tilde{S}_{i,\ell})\,\partial_{j\ell}F(\tilde{S}_{i})+\dots,

where the
\dots
collects third- and higher-order cross-terms.
By the definition of
K_{F}
and
\delta\leq 1
, the total correction
beyond first order has magnitude at most
K_{F}\delta^{2}
. This uses an
explicit higher-order remainder constant rather than pairwise
submodularity signs, since submodularity controls only the second
mixed partials. Hence

F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\geq\;\delta\sum_{j\in B_{i}}(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})\,\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;-\;K_{F}\,\delta^{2}.

(27)

Step (S2) Weighted LP optimality.

Since
O^{*}\in\mathcal{I}
and
B_{i}
maximises the weighted sum

\sum_{j}(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})\mathbf{1}_{B,j}

over
B\in\mathcal{I}
,

\sum_{j\in B_{i}}(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\geq\;\sum_{j\in O^{*}}(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i}).

(28)

Step (S3) Concavity along non-negative directions.

By property (P3) (the submodular union bound of
§
H.3
), applied with
\mathbf{x}=\tilde{S}_{i}
and

\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}}
,

\sum_{j\in O^{*}}(1-\tilde{S}_{i,j})\,\partial_{j}F(\tilde{S}_{i})\;\geq\;F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i}).

(29)

Step (S4) Feldman–Naor–Schwartz
correlation-gap bound
[
9
, Lem. 2.2]
.

For non-negative submodular
F
, any
\mathbf{y}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
, and any

S\subseteq\mathcal{N}
,

F(\mathbf{y}\lor\mathbf{1}_{S})\;\geq\;(1-\lVert\mathbf{y}\rVert_{\infty})\,F(\mathbf{1}_{S}).

Applied with
\mathbf{y}=\tilde{S}_{i}
and
S=O^{*}
, combined with
Lemma
10
:

F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})\;\geq\;(1-\lVert\tilde{S}_{i}\rVert_{\infty})\,f(O^{*})\;\geq\;(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*}),

(30)

using
1-(1-(1-\delta)^{i})=(1-\delta)^{i}
.

Assembly.

Chaining (
27
), (
28
), (
29
),
and (
30
):

\displaystyle F(\tilde{S}_{i+1})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})

\displaystyle\;\geq\;\delta\bigl[F(\tilde{S}_{i}\lor\mathbf{1}_{O^{*}})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\bigr]-K_{F}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;\delta\bigl[(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*})-F(\tilde{S}_{i})\bigr]-K_{F}\delta^{2},

which is (
26
).
∎

H.5.4
Main theorem

Theorem 9

(Non-negative non-monotone guarantee for

wDMCG-P
)
.

Let
f:2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be submodular on a downward-closed
solvable polytope
P
, and let
O^{*}\in\argmax_{\mathbf{1}_{S}\in P}f(S)
.
Then
\textsc{wDMCG-P}(F,P,k)
returns
\tilde{S}_{k}\in P
with

F(\tilde{S}_{k})\;\geq\;\bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{k}\bigr)^{k-1}\,f(O^{*})-K_{F}/k\;\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\;e^{-1}\,f(O^{*}).

(31)

Proof.

Let
a_{i}\triangleq F(\tilde{S}_{i})
with
a_{0}=0
.
Lemma
11
gives the scalar recurrence

a_{i+1}\;\geq\;(1-\delta)\,a_{i}\;+\;\delta\,(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*})\;-\;K_{F}\delta^{2},

valid for every
i\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
.
Claim (induction):

a_{i}\;\geq\;i\,\delta\,(1-\delta)^{i-1}\,f(O^{*})-iK_{F}\delta^{2}

for every
i\in\{0,\dots,k\}
.

Base case

i=0
:
a_{0}=0
and the right-hand side is
0
.

Inductive step.
Assume the claim at
i
. Then

\displaystyle a_{i+1}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;(1-\delta)\,a_{i}+\delta\,(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*})-K_{F}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;(1-\delta)\bigl[i\,\delta\,(1-\delta)^{i-1}\,f(O^{*})-iK_{F}\delta^{2}\bigr]+\delta\,(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*})-K_{F}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\;=\;i\,\delta\,(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*})+\delta\,(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*})-\bigl((1-\delta)i+1\bigr)K_{F}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;(i+1)\,\delta\,(1-\delta)^{i}\,f(O^{*})-(i+1)K_{F}\delta^{2},

closing the induction. At
i=k
(with
\delta=1/k
):

a_{k}\;\geq\;k\cdot\tfrac{1}{k}\,(1-\tfrac{1}{k})^{k-1}\,f(O^{*})-K_{F}/k\;=\;(1-\tfrac{1}{k})^{k-1}\,f(O^{*})-K_{F}/k.

As
k\to\infty
,
(1-1/k)^{k-1}\to e^{-1}
and the error
vanishes. Finally,
\tilde{S}_{k}\in P
by
Lemma
9
.
∎

Remark 12

(Scope and distinctness)
.

(i)
Non-negativity of

is essential to the analysis.
Lemma

invokes the
Feldman–Naor–Schwartz bound
[

,
which is known to fail when

takes negative values: the

in §

provides a direct guarantee via trajectory curvature

in §

then bounds

The present non-negative non-monotone regime is strictly complementary.

(ii)
Pruning is compatible with the analysis but plays

, multilinearity, submodular cross-term

).

(iii)

c_{F}=c_{f}
; if

f
takes a negative value, then
c_{F}=\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Part (b) is Proposition
7
.

For part (a), we show
c_{F}\leq c_{f}

(equality then follows from Proposition
6
).
For
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
, construct a coupling

(R_{1},R_{2})\sim\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
where
each element
i
satisfies:

\Pr[i\in R_{1}]=x_{i}
,

\Pr[i\in R_{2}\mid i\in R_{1}]=0
,

\Pr[i\in R_{2}\mid i\notin R_{1}]=(y_{i}-\min(x_{i},y_{i}))/(1-x_{i})
.
When
x_{i}=1
, the event
i\notin R_{1}
has probability zero; define the
last conditional probability arbitrarily, say as
0
.

Then
R_{1}\sim\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x})
,

R_{2}\sim\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})
,

R_{1}\cup R_{2}\sim\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})
,
and
R_{1}\cap R_{2}=\emptyset
.
When
R_{2}\neq\emptyset
, strict positivity gives
f\left(R_{2}\right)>0
,
so the curvature inequality

f\left(R_{1}\cup R_{2}\right)-f\left(R_{1}\right)\geq(1-c_{f})\,f\left(R_{2}\right)
applies by
Definition
1
.
When
R_{2}=\emptyset
, both sides equal zero.
Thus the inequality holds for all realizations of
(R_{1},R_{2})
.
Taking expectations:

F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{x})\geq(1-c_{f})\,F(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y})
,
giving
c_{F}\leq c_{f}
.
∎

Proposition 11

(OPT-aware decomposable curvature bound)
.

For
f=g-\ell
with
g
monotone submodular of CC curvature
\alpha_{g}

and
\ell
non-negative modular, let

A_{0},A_{1},\dots
be the active-set trajectory of greedy with pruning and

\mathcal{O}
the set of optimal cardinality-feasible solutions. If

r=\max_{O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}}\max_{i:f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)>0}\frac{\ell(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})}{g(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})},

then
c_{g}\leq\alpha_{g}/(1-r)
.

Proof of Proposition
11
.

Fix any
O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
and any trajectory step
i
with

f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)>0
.
Apply the decomposition
f=g-\ell
with
X=O^{*}
,
Y=A_{i}
:

\displaystyle f\left(O^{*}\cup A_{i}\right)-f\left(O^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=[g(O^{*}\cup A_{i})-g(O^{*})]-\ell(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})

\displaystyle\geq(1-\alpha_{g})\,g(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})-\ell(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}),

where the inequality uses the CC curvature bound on
g
.
The denominator
g(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})
is positive on every qualifying
step, because

g(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})=f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)+\ell(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})

and both terms on the right are non-negative with

f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)>0
.
Writing
r_{i}=\ell(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})/g(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})
and

f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)=(1-r_{i})\,g(A_{i}\setminus O^{*})
:

\frac{f\left(O^{*}\cup A_{i}\right)-f\left(O^{*}\right)}{f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)}\;\geq\;\frac{(1-\alpha_{g})-r_{i}}{1-r_{i}}\;=\;1-\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-r_{i}}\;\geq\;1-\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-r},

where the last inequality uses
r_{i}\leq r
by definition of
r

(which maximizes over
all

O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}
and all
trajectory steps).
Since this holds for every
O^{*}
and every qualifying step
i
,
taking the double minimum in Definition
2
:

c_{g}\;=\;1-\min_{O^{*}\in\mathcal{O}}\;\min_{i:\,f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)>0}\frac{f\left(O^{*}\cup A_{i}\right)-f\left(O^{*}\right)}{f\left(A_{i}\setminus O^{*}\right)}\;\leq\;\frac{\alpha_{g}}{1-r}.\qed

Restatement of Proposition
5
.

For the GCLin objective

f\left(S\right)=R(S)-\lambda D(S),\qquad R(S)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}\sum_{j\in S}s_{i,j},\qquad D(S)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in S\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}s_{i,j},

with symmetric non-negative similarities
s_{i,j}
, uniform query weights (
w_{i}=1
),
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, the curvature satisfies
c_{f}\leq 2\lambda
.

Proof of Proposition
5
.

Let
B=T\setminus S
, and consider only pairs with
f\left(B\right)>0
, as in
Definition
1
. By symmetry of the similarities,
for any
S,T\subseteq\mathcal{N}
:

\displaystyle f\left(S\cup T\right)-f\left(S\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(B\right)-2\lambda\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{j\in B}s_{i,j}

\displaystyle\geq f\left(B\right)-2\lambda\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}\setminus B}\sum_{j\in B}s_{i,j}.

The remaining cross-similarity term is controlled by the standalone
GCLin value of
B
when
\lambda\leq 1
:

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}\setminus B}\sum_{j\in B}s_{i,j}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}\sum_{j\in B}s_{i,j}-\sum_{i\in B}\sum_{j\in B}s_{i,j}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}\sum_{j\in B}s_{i,j}-\lambda\sum_{i\in B}\sum_{j\in B}s_{i,j}\leq f\left(B\right).

Hence

f\left(S\cup T\right)-f\left(S\right)\geq(1-2\lambda)f\left(B\right)=(1-2\lambda)f\left(T\setminus S\right).

Therefore
c_{f}\leq 2\lambda
.

The monotonicity claim used in Section
4.3
for

\lambda\leq 1/2
follows from the corresponding singleton marginal:
for
e\notin S
,

f\left(S\cup\{e\}\right)-f\left(S\right)=s_{e,e}+(1-2\lambda)\sum_{i\in S}s_{i,e}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}\setminus(S\cup\{e\})}s_{i,e}\geq 0.

∎

Remark 13
.

The restriction
\lambda\leq 1
is not a proof artifact. For

\lambda>1
, the positive standalone value
f\left(B\right)=R(B)-\lambda D(B)
can be arbitrarily small relative to the
cross-similarity
\sum_{i\in S,j\in B}s_{i,j}
, so the argument above
gives no uniform
2\lambda
curvature bound without an additional
cross-redundancy condition. The
\lambda=1.5
experiments in
Section
4.3
are therefore reported only as
empirical trajectory diagnostics.

Restatement of Proposition
10
.

For the DR-submodular quadratic objective

F(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}-\beta\,\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x},

where
\mathbf{H}
is symmetric entry-wise non-positive,
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{u}

on the polytope, and
\beta\in(1/2,1)
, the instance is normalized,
non-negative, DR-submodular, and has
c_{F}\leq 2/(2\beta-1)
. It is
non-monotone whenever
(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u})_{j}<0
for some feasible
coordinate
j
.

Proof of Proposition
10
.

Normalization and DR-submodularity.

We have
F(\mathbf{0})=0
. The Hessian of
F
is exactly

\mathbf{H}
, whose off-diagonal entries are non-positive by assumption,
so every first partial derivative is coordinate-wise non-increasing in
the other coordinates. This is precisely DR-submodularity for smooth
functions.

Non-monotonicity in nondegenerate instances.

The gradient is
\nabla F(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}-\beta\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u}
.
At
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{u}
,

\nabla F(\mathbf{u})=(1-\beta)\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u}
. Thus, if

(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u})_{j}<0
for some feasible coordinate
j
, the
marginal in that coordinate is negative at
\mathbf{u}
, and the
objective is not monotone on the feasible box.

Non-negativity.

For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}
, since
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{H}

is entry-wise non-positive, we have

\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}\geq\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}

and
\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}\leq 0
.
Therefore

F(\mathbf{x})=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}-\beta\,\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}\geq(\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta)\,\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}\geq 0
.

Curvature.

For
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{P}
, let
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\land\mathbf{y}
.
Then
F(\mathbf{z})=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{z}-\beta\,\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{z}\geq(\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta)\,\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{z}\geq(\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta)\,\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{z}
,
where the last step uses
\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{z}\leq\mathbf{0}
.
Also
F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{x})=F(\mathbf{z})+\mathbf{z}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}\geq F(\mathbf{z})+\tfrac{1}{\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta}\,F(\mathbf{z})=(1+\tfrac{1}{\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta})\,F(\mathbf{z})
.
Since
\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta<0
, this gives

F(\mathbf{x}\lor\mathbf{y})-F(\mathbf{x})\geq(1-\tfrac{2}{2\beta-1})\,F(\mathbf{z})
,
hence
c_{F}\leq\tfrac{2}{2\beta-1}
.
∎
