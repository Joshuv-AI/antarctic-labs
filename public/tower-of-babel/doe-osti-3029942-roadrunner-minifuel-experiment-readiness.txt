 ABSTRACT

The Research On ADvancing the peRformance of UraNium Nitrides in Extreme enviRonments
(ROADRUNNER) campaign is a collaborative irradiation experiment designed to evaluate the performance
of uranium nitride (UN) fuel fabricated with controlled variations in density and impurity content. Oak
Ridge National Laboratory’s MiniFuel irradiation platform and the High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR) are
being used to irradiate six targets containing 36 UN miniature disks. These targets have been assembled to
systematically investigate burnup and temperature effects under accelerated irradiation conditions. This
report documents the finalized test plan, describes the experiment assembly and subcapsule integration
process, and confirms the readiness of all ROADRUNNER targets for HFIR insertion. All targets have
passed quality control procedures, and five have begun irradiation. The campaign will provide critical
performance data to support fuel model validation and future UN qualification efforts.

 vii

 1. INTRODUCTION

Various advanced reactor concepts, such as liquid metal fast reactors, small modular reactors,
microreactors, and space-based fission reactors, have considered uranium nitride (UN) as a fuel form
candidate due to its high thermal conductivity, high uranium density, and high melting point. Existing data
on UN irradiated properties are sparse, are limited across the broad advanced reactor operation conditions,
and are lacking in control of fuel parameters such as impurity level or density. Carbon and oxygen are
common impurities in UN from the carbothermic reduction and nitridation (CTRN) fabrication process,
and these impurities could affect the irradiated properties of the fuel. To address these limitations and
support the qualification of UN fuel for use in advanced reactor concepts, a collaborative irradiation testing
campaign named Research On ADvancing the peRformance of UraNium Nitrides in Extreme
enviRonments (ROADRUNNER) was developed by the University of Texas at San Antonio (UTSA),
Westinghouse Electric Company, Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), and Los Alamos National
Laboratory (LANL). The goal of this campaign is to assess the effects of density and impurity variations
on irradiated UN fuel properties across various burnup levels and irradiation temperatures.

The ROADRUNNER experiment is using the MiniFuel irradiation platform [1][2] to perform neutron
irradiation of small-scale UN specimens in the High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR) at ORNL. The experiment
was designed to accommodate depleted UN specimens 3 mm in diameter and 1 mm thick, and it targets
time-average volume-average (TAVA) specimen irradiation temperatures of 600°C, 900°C, and 1,200°C.
Neutronics analysis was performed to determine the number of HFIR cycles required to achieve the desired
burnup levels and to determine the components’ heat generation rates used in the thermal analysis. The
MiniFuel holder outer diameter and the MiniFuel target fill gas needed to achieve the desired TAVA fuel
temperature were then determined [3]. The UN pellets were fabricated at LANL using the CTRN process.
Tightly controlled fabrication parameters were used to create UN batches of various density and impurity
levels. The pellets were then thinned down to the MiniFuel specimen thickness (1 mm) at UTSA. Specimen
precharacterization was performed across LANL, UTSA, and ORNL, and it included impurity content,
dimension, mass, density, and thermal diffusivity measurements. Additionally, scanning electron
microscopy was used to collect microstructure images to measure the average grain size of the specimens.
X-ray computed tomography was also performed on a subset of specimens to identify any existing defects
and evaluate the volume of the specimens [3].

This report summarizes the final experiment test plan, describes the experiment assembly, and attests to the
readiness of the MiniFuel ROADRUNNER targets for HFIR insertion.

Table 1 summarizes the experiment test matrix [3]. Target RRN01 is the control target, comprising high-
purity, high-density specimens irradiated at 900°C to a high burnup. Targets RRN02, RRN04, RRN06 are
designed to evaluate the influence of irradiation temperatures (600°C, 900°C, and 1,200°C) on high-purity,
high-density specimens across three burnup levels. Finally, targets RRN03 and RRN05 are designed to
investigate the effects of fuel density and impurity variation at two distinct burnup levels, with all specimens
irradiated at 900°C.

 3. EXPERIMENT ASSEMBLY

The experiment was assembled in accordance with HFIR-approved drawings and procedures. All
components were cleaned prior to assembly. The experiment assembly began with the MiniFuel
subcapsules. Figure 1 shows an example of a subcapsule parts layout. A custom fixture was used during

 3

the process to assemble all components together while maintaining them in place. Ultimately, the fuel
specimen was located at the bottom of the holder into the fuel dish and covered by the tube. The silicon
carbide (SiC) thermometry was loaded into the tube, and grafoil insulator disks were placed at the top of
the holder. The end cap was placed at the top of the holder and was welded using an electron beam process.
The subcapsule assembly was then evacuated and backfilled with ultra-high-purity helium to a pressure
equivalent to local atmospheric pressure before being sealed via arc melt welding. Figure 2 shows examples
of the subcapsule assembly and welding steps. The subcapsules were finally leak tested with a helium leak
test and a bubble test.

Once all subcapsules were fully assembled, welded, and leak tested, the MiniFuel targets were assembled.
Figure 3 shows the MiniFuel target parts layout. Centering thimbles were placed on both ends of each
subcapsule (see Figure 4) to ensure their centering in the target tube and maintain a constant gas gap
between the inner wall of the target tube and the outer wall of the subcapsule for temperature control. The
six subcapsules with one spring on each end were stacked inside a target tube with a welded bottom end
cap. The top end cap was then welded to the target tube, and the target was evacuated, backfilled with an
85%He/Ar balance gas mixture, and seal welded. All welds on the target were performed using a gas
tungsten arc welding process. Figure 4 includes a radiograph of an assembled target, clearly showing the
stack of subcapsules and springs inside the tube. Each target underwent dye penetrant examination and
helium leak testing before and after hydrostatic pressure application. The final targets were cleaned,
inspected, and delivered to HFIR along with their complete fabrication package. Figure 5 shows the six
targets fully assembled, welded, tested, and ready for HFIR insertion.

 4. HFIR INSERTION

To date, all six of the ROADRUNNER targets have been successfully assembled and delivered to HFIR
for insertion. Five of the targets (RRN01, RRN03, RRN04, RRN05, RRN06) have been inserted into HFIR
position RB-5B in the midplane (axial position A=2) of MiniFuel basket MF1, occupying radial positions
R=1 to R=5. These five targets have completed one HFIR irradiation cycle (cycle 511). Due to operational
constraints, no ROADRUNNER targets will be irradiated during HFIR cycle 512 (August 2025). Irradiation
is expected to resume in cycle 513 (September 2025), and there are plans to temporarily remove target
RRN04 to start the irradiation of target RRN02 in basket position RA=22. Table 3 summarizes the HFIR
insertion schedule and irradiation positions for all targets. Target loading is anticipated to be adjusted
throughout the irradiation depending on irradiation positions becoming available.

 5. FUTURE WORK

As-built, as-irradiated calculations have been initiated to predict irradiation temperature, burnup, and fuel
inventory from the actual dimensions and materials of the experiment components and with the actual HFIR
cycle parameters. To date, only one HFIR cycle has been completed for these targets, and thus only one
cycle has been simulated. Additional cycles will be simulated as additional HFIR cycles are completed.

Postirradiation, the targets will be shipped to the Irradiated Fuels Examination Laboratory at ORNL for
disassembly. After target opening, the recovered subcapsules will be punctured for fission gas release
measurements. The subcapsules will then be opened to recover the SiC passive thermometry and fuel
specimens. The thermometry will be shipped to the Low Activation Materials Development and Analysis
laboratory for analysis to assess the experimental specimen irradiation temperature. The results of this
analysis will be compared to the predicted TAVA thermometry temperature [3]. The planned postirradiation
examination of the fuel specimens includes gamma spectrometry, swelling evaluation, optical microscopy,
and scanning electron microscopy.

 6. CONCLUSION

Following extensive precharacterization, specimens were selected to fill the experimental test matrix. All
six ROADRUNNER irradiation targets have been successfully fabricated, assembled, leak tested, and
delivered to HFIR. Five targets have already completed at least one irradiation cycle, and the sixth is
scheduled for insertion in upcoming cycles.

The ROADRUNNER campaign represents a critical step in the qualification of UN fuel by providing a
tightly controlled test matrix across temperature, burnup, and fabrication variables. The high level of pre-
irradiation characterization and precise assembly procedures have layed the groundwork for acquiring high-
quality postirradiation examination data. The results will directly support the development and validation
of mechanistic fuel performance models and ultimately help define fabrication tolerances for future
applications of UN in advanced reactors.
