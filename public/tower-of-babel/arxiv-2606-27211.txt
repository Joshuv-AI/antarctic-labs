Quantum group codes for non-Clifford logic: enhanced decoding, addressability and parallelizability
Jean Gasnier, Virgile Guémard

Abstract
We introduce a framework based on classical quasi group codes to define a class of quantum CSS codes, called quantum group codes , supporting transversal multi-control- Z Z gates which are both addressable and parallelizable, thus allowing to efficiently implement circuits composed of non-Clifford gates at the logical level. Building on this, we use a lifting procedure of classical AG codes established from class field theory to construct good quantum group codes with improved decoding complexity and logical multi-control- Z Z gate parallelizability. More precisely, on input a good quantum AG code over the alphabet 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} with transversal 𝖢 m ​ 𝖹 \mathsf{C}^{m}\mathsf{Z} gate, we apply this lifting procedure to its underlying classical AG code and obtain a quantum group code over the alphabet 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} supporting a transversal 𝖢 m ​ 𝖹 \mathsf{C}^{m}\mathsf{Z} gate as well as addressable and parallelizable 𝖢 m − 1 ​ 𝖹 \mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z} gates. In addition, this quantum code admits a quasi-quadratic time decoder with a linear decoding radius. This is to be compared with the previous quantum AG codes which have a cubic-time decoder. Hence, our work implies a decrease of the time complexity of state-of-the-art magic-state distillation protocols by an almost linear factor.

Main results.

The recent work on MSD and addressability presented above may still be improved at several levels. First, the MSD scheme of
[
38
]
is based on quantum codes admitting a decoder with a cubic time-complexity in the length of the code. This implies that the MSD protocol
[
38
]
inherits a cubic time-complexity, thus leaving room for progress. The constructions of asymptotically good addressable codes suffer from the same high decoding complexity. Second, the only existing family of quantum codes with linear distance and fully addressable gates is built from a very specific family of classical codes, namely transitive AG codes defined over the tower of function fields from
[
34
]
. It remains interesting to study different families of such codes.

In this work, we address both of these problems.
First, we identify sufficient properties of a quantum code to admit parallelizable gates. Such quantum codes can be directly built from classical quasi group codes with multiplication properties. Then, we leverage recent lifting results on AG codes to convert good quantum AG codes with a unique global transversal gate, such as those used in MSD protocols, into quantum codes with addressable and parallelizable non-Clifford gates. The lifting is done via an abelian function field extension developed in
[
9
]
using class field theory, which naturally endows the linear codes with a linear-time encoder and a quadratic-time decoder, up to logarithmic factors, with a linear decoding radius. In this work, we verify that this transformation preserves the properties necessary to build a quantum CSS code with transversal non-Clifford gates. Moreover, the inherited free-module structure of the code is exactly suited for addressability and parallelizability of the logical gates. Importantly, the quadratic time decoder can be directly used to decode the quantum codes we construct up to errors of linear size.

2  Preliminaries: linear codes and algebraic geometry codes

2.1  Linear codes

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements. A linear
[n,k,d]_{q}
-code
C
is a
k
-dimensional subspace
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with minimum (Hamming) distance
d
. We are interested in codes having an additional structure of group module. Throughout, we write
[n]\coloneq\{1,\ldots n\}
.

2.1.1  Group codes and quasi group codes

Given a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
and a vector
x=(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, we denote by
x\cdot\sigma
the vector

x\cdot\sigma=(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)})

This defines a right action of
S_{n}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Recall that one says that the action of a subgroup
G\subseteq S_{n}
on
[n]
is

1.

transitive
if, for any given
i,j\in[n]
, there exists
\sigma\in G
such that
\sigma(i)=j
.

2.

free
if, for every
i\in[n]
, the only
\sigma\in G
such that
\sigma(i)=i
is the identity element.

Moreover, if the action of
G
is free, it means its order divides
n
, i.e.
\ell=\frac{n}{|G|}
is an integer.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be a linear code and define

\PAut(C)=\{\sigma\in S_{n}\mid C\cdot\sigma=C\}

the
permutation automorphism group
of
C
, i.e. the group of permutations of
S_{n}
stabilizing
C
.
One says that a code
C
is
transitive
if
\PAut(C)
acts transitively on
[n]
. By definition, any subgroup
G\subseteq\PAut(C)
acts on
C
(on the right), as well as on
[n]
(on the left).

An interesting case arises when there exists a subgroup
G\subseteq\PAut(C)
whose action on
[n]
is both transitive and free. In this case, as shown in
[
6
]
, the code
C
is a
G
code
, i.e. is isomorphic to a right ideal of the group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
. For instance, if
G
is cyclic, this amounts to say that
C
is a cyclic code. Finally, we will be interested in the following slight generalization of
G
codes.

Definition 2.1

(Quasi-
G
code)
.

Let
C
be a
q
-ary code of length
n
and let
G
be a group. Then,
C
is a
quasi-
G
code
if there exists a subgroup
H\subseteq\PAut(C)
isomorphic to
G
whose action on
[n]
is free. If
G
is abelian, we will also say that
C
is a
quasi-abelian code
.

According to
[
7
]
, this amounts to say that
C
is isomorphic to a right
G
-module inside
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{\ell}
(on which
G
acts by multiplication on the right on each coordinate), where
\ell=\frac{n}{|G|}
is the number of orbits of
G
in
[n]
.

An important property of quasi group codes is the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group. Then the dual of a quasi-
G
code is a quasi-
G
code.

Proof.

Let
C
be a quasi-
G
code and let
y\in C^{\perp}
. For any
x\in C
and
\sigma\in G
, one has

\langle x,y\cdot\sigma\rangle=\langle x\cdot\sigma^{-1},y\rangle=0.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

One can define a left action of
S_{n}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
by for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
\sigma\in G,\sigma\cdot x\coloneq x\cdot\sigma^{-1}.
Since
G\subseteq S_{n}
is stable under inversion, any quasi-
G
code
C
is stable under the induced action of
G
on the left. In this setting,
C
is also isomorphic to a left submodule of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{\ell}
. This is the image of the right
G
-module of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{\ell}
isomorphic to
C
under the antimorphism defined by
\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{-1}
. This shows that there is no preferred side for working with quasi-
G
codes, but that one needs to pay attention to the choice of convention. This is particularly true when
G
is non-commutative.

Quasi-
G
codes and
G
-linear algebra.

One interesting property of quasi-
G
codes is the possibility to define them via a generator matrix with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
. Indeed, let
C
be a quasi-
G
code with parameters
[\tilde{n},\tilde{k},d]
, let
n=\frac{\tilde{n}}{|G|}
and let
M
be the corresponding right
G
-module of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{n}
. Let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{n}
be a set of generators of
M
of minimum size. Then

M=\left\{\>\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}\cdot\alpha_{i}\>:\>\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{k}\>\right\}

or in other words
M
is the image of
\mathbf{M}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n,k}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[G])
(under multiplication on the right) where the columns of
\mathbf{M}
are the generators
(g_{i})_{i\in[k]}
.

The matrix
\mathbf{M}
describes a
G
-linear morphism, which is in particular a linear morphism. By fixing an order on the elements of
G
, one can associate with
\mathbf{M}
a matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}
of size
n\cdot|G|\times k\cdot|G|
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with a particular shape. Explicitly, one computes the matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{\tilde{n},k\cdot|G|}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
by replacing every entry
M_{ij}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
by its left-regular representation, or equivalently the matrix of the multiplication by
M_{ij}
on the left.
One would want the columns of
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}
to generate the code
C
. However, the orbits of the action of
G
on
[n]
need not be composed of adjacent integers, so this will not be the case in general. Still, there always exists a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that the columns of
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}
generate
C\cdot\sigma
, a code permutation equivalent to
C
.

Note also that the columns of
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}
need not be linearly independent in general. In fact,
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}
has rank
k\cdot|G|
if and only if
M
is
free
, i.e. is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{k}
as a right
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
-module, in which case
\tilde{k}=k\cdot|G|
.

In a slightly abusive manner, we will call
\mathbf{M}
a generator matrix of
C
as well. To avoid confusion with traditional generator matrix, we will emphasize on the ring of definition of the coefficients of the matrix.

Remark 2.4
.

An equivalent construction can be defined for the left
G
-module
M^{\prime}
associated with
C
.
In that case,
M^{\prime}
is the image (under multiplication on the left) of a matrix
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{k,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[G])
. One can then associate with
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
a matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by replacing each entry by its right-regular representation.
One needs to carefully choose the basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
in order to compute
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
in a coherent manner. The detailed process is available in
[
14
, Section 4.4]
.

In order to stick to the writing conventions in coding theory, we will choose to see quasi group codes as modules on the left as much as possible in what follows.

Remark 2.5
.

When
M
is free, i.e. isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{k}
, the representation of
C
by
\mathbf{M}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n,k}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[G])
requires
n\cdot k\cdot|G|
coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
instead of
\tilde{n}\cdot\tilde{k}=n\cdot k\cdot|G|^{2}
coefficients for a classical generator matrix of
C
. One can see from this favorable situation that quasi-
G
codes possess shorter representations than generic codes.

Encoding quasi-
G
codes.

Let
C
be a quasi-
G
code with parameters
[\tilde{n},\tilde{k},d]
, let
n=\frac{\tilde{n}}{|G|}
and let
M
be the corresponding left
G
-module of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{n}
. Let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{n}
be a set of generators of
M
of minimum size, and
\mathbf{M}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{k,n}{\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]}
the corresponding generating matrix of
C
.

One may sometimes take advantage from the
G
-linearity of
M
to encode
C
faster than by performing a naive evaluation of a generator matrix, at the cost of
O(\tilde{k}\cdot\tilde{n})
operations. Essentially, a product in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
can be computed at the cost of at most
\mathcal{C}_{q}(|G|\log|G|)
operations using the Fast Fourier Transform
[
9
, Theorem 2]
, where
\mathcal{C}_{q}
is a constant depending only on

. As such, the encoding of
C
in this fashion requires at most
\mathcal{C}_{q}(n\cdot k\cdot|G|\log|G|)=\mathcal{C}_{q}(k\log|G|\tilde{n})
operations.

Thus, if
k\log|G|
becomes negligible relative to
\tilde{k}
, then this technique allows for a faster encoding of
C
. This is particularly true when
C
is a free
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
-module and
\tilde{k}=k\cdot|G|
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
C
be a quasi-
G
code with parameters
[\tilde{n},\tilde{k},d]
, let
n=\frac{\tilde{n}}{|G|}
and let
M
be the right
G
-module of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{n}
associated with
C
. Assume that
M
is free, i.e. that
M
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{k}
as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
-module, where
k=\frac{\tilde{k}}{|G|}
. Then
C
can be encoded at the cost of at most
\mathcal{C}_{q}(n\cdot k\cdot|G|\log|G|)
operations, where the positive constant
\mathcal{C}_{q}
depends only on
q
.

Assume further that there exists a positive constant
\alpha_{q}
such that

Then,
C
can be encoded in quasi-linear time in its length
\tilde{n}=n\cdot|G|
.

Proof.

We prove the second assertion. Recall that
k\leqslant n\leqslant\alpha_{q}\log|G|
.
Thus
C
can be encoded at the cost of at most
\alpha_{q}^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{q}(|G|\log^{3}|G|)
operations. Since the length of

is
\tilde{n}=n\cdot|G|\geqslant|G|

\tilde{n}
.
∎

2.1.2  Codes with multiplication properties

Central for this work are codes satisfying different levels of self-orthogonality. Given two vectors
x
and
y
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, their Schur product is defined as the vector
x\star y\coloneq(x_{1}y_{1},\dots,x_{n}y_{n})
. Given a linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, we also define its
n
-fold Schur product by

C^{\star m}=\{\,x^{1}\star x^{2}\star\dots\star x^{m}:x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{m}\in C\,\}.

Moreover, given a vector
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with only nonzero components, we define
u\star C\coloneq\{\,u\star x:x\in C\,\}
. The codes
C
and
u\star C
have the same length, dimension, and minimum distance. The permutation automorphism groups
\PAut(C)
and
\PAut(u\star C)
are, in general, non-isomorphic.

Definition 2.7

(Multiplication property)
.

Let
m\geqslant 1
be an integer. We say that a linear code
C
satisfies the
m
-multiplication property
if there exists a fixed vector
u
with only nonzero components such that
u\star C^{\star m}\subseteq C^{\perp}
.

For any vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, we call the vector with all entries equal to one the
all-one vector
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
C
be a code satisfying the
m
-multiplication property and containing the all-one vector. Then,
C
satisfies the
\widetilde{m}
-multiplication property for every
1\leqslant\widetilde{m}\leqslant m
.

The proof can be found in
[
18
]
. In that case, notice that
u\star C\subseteq C^{\perp}
, and since the all-one vector belongs to
C
, it follows that
u\in C^{\perp}
.

Definition 2.9

(Punctured and shortened codes)

Let

The

C_{\overline{L}}:=\{\>c_{\overline{L}}:c\in C\>\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\overline{L}}

The

C_{L=0}:=\{\,c_{\overline{L}}:c\in C,\;c_{L}=0\,\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\,\overline{L}}
.

]

2.2  Algebraic geometry codes

2.2.1  Background on function fields

Function fields.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
be the rational function field. A function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
. It is well-known that there is an equivalence of categories between the category of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the category of smooth projective curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

[
26
, Section 7, Rem. 3.14]
. Through this equivalence, the notion corresponding to a (closed) point is a
place

P
of
F
, i.e. the maximal ideal of a valuation ring of
F
. We denote by
\nu_{P}
the corresponding valuation and
\mathcal{O}_{P}
its valuation ring.

Let us assume that the field of constants of
F
(i.e. the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
F
) is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The
residue field
at
P
is the field
K_{P}=\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
. The
evaluation
of an element
f\in\mathcal{O}_{P}
at
P
is the image of
f
under the canonical map
\mathcal{O}_{P}\to\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
. It is denoted by
f(P)
. The
degree
of a place is the degree of the extension
K_{P}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A place is called
rational
if its residue field is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, or equivalently, if it has degree one.

Extensions of algebraic function fields.

Let
F
be a function field and assume that
\widetilde{F}
is an extension of
F
.
Let
Q
be a place of
\widetilde{F}
, then
P:=Q\cap F
is a place of
F
. One says that
Q

lies above

P
and we denote this relation by
Q\mid P
.
We say that a place
P
of
F

splits completely
in
\widetilde{F}
if the number of places
Q
of
\widetilde{F}
such that
Q\mid P
is exactly
[\widetilde{F}:F]
.

Assume that the extension
\widetilde{F}/F
is Galois, with Galois group
G
. The Galois group acts transitively on the set of places lying above a given place of
F
.

Let
P
be a place of
F
and
Q
a place of
\widetilde{F}
lying above
P
. One can see that the residue field
K_{Q}
is an extension of
K_{P}
. One defines the
inertia degree
of
Q
as

f_{Q}:=[K_{Q}:K_{P}].

Since
\widetilde{F}/F
is Galois, it does not depend on the choice of
Q
above
P
, and will be denoted by
f_{P}
as well.

Define the
decomposition group
of
Q
as

D(Q/P)\coloneq\{\sigma\in G\mid\sigma(Q)=Q\}.

There exists a canonical group morphism

D(Q/P)\longrightarrow\Gal(K_{Q}/K_{P})

whose kernel is
I(Q/P)
the inertia group of
Q
. One defines the
ramification index
of
Q
as
e_{Q}=|I(Q/P)|
. It does not depend on the choice of
Q
as well because
\widetilde{F}/F
is Galois, and therefore can be denoted by
e_{P}
. Let
d_{P}
be the number of places of
\widetilde{F}
lying above
P
, then one has

d_{P}\cdot e_{P}\cdot f_{P}=[\widetilde{F}:F].

Thus,
P
splits completely in
\widetilde{F}
if and only if
e_{P}=f_{P}=1
.

Riemann-Roch spaces.

A
divisor

D
in a function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a formal sum of finitely many places with integer coefficients, namely
D=\sum n_{P}P
. A divisor
D
is said to be
positive
or
effective
, written
D\geqslant 0
, if all of its coefficients are positive integers. Given an element
f\in F
, we write
(f)
for its associated
principal divisor
. Moreover, we denote the
Riemann-Roch space
associated with a divisor
D
by

\mathcal{L}(D)=\{f\in F:(f)+D\geqslant 0\}\cup\{0\}.

Writing
D=D_{\text{pos}}-D_{\text{neg}}
as the difference of two effective divisors, we see that
f\in\mathcal{L}(D)
if and only if
f
has poles of limited order at the places of
D_{\text{pos}}
and zeros of sufficient order at the places of
D_{\text{neg}}
. If we have another divisor
D^{\prime}\geqslant D
, then
\mathcal{L}(D^{\prime})
allows functions with fewer zeros and more poles, and thus
\mathcal{L}(D)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(D^{\prime})
.

Picard group.

Let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be two divisors of
F
, one defines them to be
equivalent
if
D-D^{\prime}
is a principal divisor, which is denoted by
D\sim D^{\prime}
. One then defines the
Picard group
of
F

\Pic(F)=\DDiv(F)/\sim

the group of equivalence classes of divisors of
F
. Since every principal divisor has degree
0
, one can extend the notion of degree to equivalence classes. Then, one defines

\Pic^{0}(F)=\{c\in\Pic(F)\mid\deg c=0\}

the subgroup of classes of degree
0
.

Lifting divisors and differentials.

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a function field, and let
\widetilde{F}/F
be a separable extension of
F
. Let
D
be a divisor of
F
. One can define the
conorm
in
\widetilde{F}
of a divisor of
F
by

\Con_{\widetilde{F}/F}\left(\sum n_{P}P\right)=\sum_{P}\sum_{Q\mid P}e_{Q}\cdot n_{P}Q

where
e_{Q}
denotes the ramification index of
Q
. It follows from this definition that, for two divisors
D
,
D^{\prime}
of
F
, if
D\leqslant D^{\prime}
then
\Con_{\widetilde{F}/F}(D)\leqslant\Con_{\widetilde{F}/F}(D^{\prime})
.

As shown in
[
35
, Corollary 3.1.14]
, the degree of the conorm satisfies the following relation

\deg\Con_{\widetilde{F}/F}(D)=\frac{[\widetilde{F}:F]}{[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}]}\deg D.

where
K
is the field of constants of
\widetilde{F}
.
We denote by
\Omega_{F}
the space of differentials of
F
. Given a differential
\omega\in\Omega_{F}
, there exists a unique differential of
\widetilde{F}
extending
\omega
in the sense of
[
35
, Theorem 3.4.6]
, that we call the
cotrace
of
\omega
in
\widetilde{F}/F
, and denoted by
\operatorname{Cotr}_{\widetilde{F}/F}(\omega)
.

The divisor of the cotrace of
\omega
is closely related to the conorm of the divisor of
\omega
:

\left(\operatorname{Cotr}_{\widetilde{F}/F}(\omega)\right)=\Con_{\widetilde{F}/F}((\omega))+\Delta_{\widetilde{F}/F}

where
\Delta_{\widetilde{F}/F}
is the
different
of the extension
\widetilde{F}/F
(see
[
35
, Definition 3.4.3]
). The divisor
\Delta_{\widetilde{F}/F}
is supported by places ramified in
\widetilde{F}
(see
[
35
, Theorem 3.5.1]
). In particular, it is zero if the extension is unramified.

2.2.2  Algebraic geometry codes and their parameters

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a function field whose field of constants is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
be
n
distinct rational places of
F
. Let
P=P_{1}+\ldots+P_{n}
be a divisor of
F
. Let
D
be a divisor with
{\rm supp}\,P\cap{\rm supp}\,D=\emptyset
. We may consider the

C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)\coloneq\{(f(P_{1}),\ldots,f(P_{n}))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\mid f\in\mathcal{L}(D)\}.

Algebraic geometry codes admit the following lower bounds on their parameters due to the Riemann-Roch Theorem.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
g
denotes the genus of the function field
F
, defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
P
and
D
be defined as above. Suppose that

2g-2<\deg D<n.

Then, the code
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
is an
[n,k,d]
-code such that
k=\deg D+1-g
and
d\geqslant n-\deg D
. Moreover, the dual code of
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
has minimum distance
d^{\perp}\geqslant\deg D-2g+2
.

It is common to define
the designed distance
of
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
as

d^{*}:=n-\deg D.

Now assume that
F
is a Galois extension of a function field
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with Galois group
G
, in which
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
are unramified. Also assume that
G
fixes the two divisors
P
and
D
. Then,
G
acts freely on the set of rational places
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
(because they are unramified), and
G
stabilizes the Riemann-Roch space
\mathcal{L}(D)
. This yields the following result.

Proposition 2.12
.

With the notation of the beginning of the paragraph, assume that
F
is a Galois extension of a function field
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with Galois group
G
, in which
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
are unramified. Also assume that
G
fixes the two divisors
P
and
D
. Then
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
is a quasi-
G
code.

Proof.

For
\sigma\in G
, and
f\in\mathcal{L}(D)
, we define

\sigma^{-1}\cdot\big(f(P_{1}),\ldots,f(P_{n})\big)\coloneq\left((\sigma^{-1}\cdot f)(P_{1}),\ldots,(\sigma^{-1}\cdot f)(P_{n})\right)=\big(f(\sigma(P_{1})),\ldots,f(\sigma(P_{n}))\big)\in C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,H).

(1)

By identifying the sets
[n]
and
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\}
, we can then see
G
as a subgroup of
S_{n}
. Moreover,
G
stabilizes
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
, thus
G
is a subgroup of
\PAut(C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D))
. Finally, since the action of
G
on the rational places is free, by Definition
2.1
, the code
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
is a quasi-
G
code.
∎

We end this section by giving criteria for
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
to possess the all-one vector and to satisfy the
m
-multiplication property.

Proposition 2.13
.

With the notation of the beginning of the paragraph, assume
D
is effective. Then
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
contains the all-one vector. Moreover, let
m
be an integer, and let
\eta\in\Omega_{F}
be a differential of
F
having a simple pole at every rational place of
P
. If

(m+1)D\leqslant P+(\eta),

then the code
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
satisfies the
m
-multiplication property.

Proof.

If
D
is effective,
\mathbb{F}_{q}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(D)
and in particular
1\in\mathcal{L}(D)
. Thus the all-one vector belongs to
C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)
. Now, let
u=(\operatorname{Res}_{P_{i}}(\eta))_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}
be the vector of residues of
\eta
at
P
. From
[
35
, Proposition 2.2.10]
, since
\eta
has simple poles at
P
,
u
does not have a null component, and one has

C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)^{\perp}=u\star C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,P-D+\eta).

Since
D
is effective,
\mathcal{L}(D)^{\star m}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(m\cdot D)
, and the hypothesis yields

u\star C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)^{\star m}\subseteq C_{\mathcal{L}}(P,D)^{\perp}.

∎

3  Quantum group codes: parallelizable transversal gates

3.1  Quantum group codes and multi-control gates

CSS codes.

An efficient method to protect quantum information against the effect of noise is provided by Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) codes
[
11
,
32
,
33
]
, a type of stabilizer quantum error correcting codes defined by two linear codes. Let us recall that the state space of an
n

q
-dimensional qudit system is
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
, with computational basis states denoted
\{\ket{x}:x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\}
. For our purpose
q
denotes a power of two, although the discussion can be generalized to other field characteristics.

Definition 3.1

(CSS code)
.

Let
C_{0},C_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be two linear codes such that
C_{0}^{\perp}\subseteq C_{1}
. A quantum CSS code
\operatorname{CSS}(C_{0},C_{1})
is the subspace of
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
given by

\operatorname{Span}\left\{\ket{c+C_0^\perp}\>:\>c\in C_{1}\right\}.

where we used the notation
\ket{c+C_0^\perp}\coloneq\frac{1}{|C_{0}^{\perp}|^{1/2}}\sum_{b\in C_{0}^{\perp}}\ket{c+b}
. Its parameters, noted
[[n,k,d]]_{q}
, are the
code length

n
, the
dimension

k=\dim(C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp})
and the
distance

d=\min(d_{0},d_{1})
, with

\displaystyle d_{0}=\min\limits_{c\in C_{1}\setminus C_{0}^{\perp}}|c|,\qquad d_{1}=\min\limits_{c\in C_{0}\setminus C_{1}^{\perp}}|c|.

An infinite family of CSS codes of growing size is said to be
good
if its parameters scale asymptotically as
[[n,\Theta(n),\Theta(n)]]
.

Given a quantum CSS code
\mathcal{Q}\coloneq\operatorname{CSS}(C_{0},C_{1})
, we denote the quotient map
\pi:C_{1}\to C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}
. We may choose a set of representatives for the space
C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}
and this choice is described via a
section
of
\pi
, namely a map

\mathfrak{s}:C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}\to C_{1}

(2)

such that
\pi\circ\mathfrak{s}=\text{id}
. Then, given an element
y\in C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}
we define the following quantum state, up to a global normalization factor,

\ket{y}\coloneq\sum_{c\in C_{1}\>:\>\pi(c)=y}\ket{c}.

(3)

Note that given a representative
c
of
y
, i.e. an element
c\in C_{1}
with
\pi(c)=y
, the state
\ket{y}
agrees with the common notation
\ket{c+C_0^\perp}
used in Definition
3.1
. Using the section
\mathfrak{s}
, we can also write
\ket{y}=\ket{\fs(y)+C_0^\perp}
.

Finally, we define an
encoding map
for
\mathcal{Q}
as an isomorphism

\operatorname{E}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\xrightarrow{\sim}C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}.

That is,
\operatorname{E}
fixes a choice of preferred basis for its codomain. Given such an encoding and any computational basis state
\ket{x}
, where
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
, we then define its encoding
\overline{\ket{x}}
with respect to
\operatorname{E}
as the unit vector

\displaystyle\overline{\ket{x}}

\displaystyle=\ket{ \Enc(x)}

(4)

\displaystyle=\ket{\fs\circ\Enc(x)+C_0^\perp}

(5)

We then refer to the image of the
i
th tensor component of
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes k}
via
\operatorname{E}
as the
i
th logical qudit.

Quantum
G
codes.

Of importance for us will be quantum codes inheriting group properties from those of quasi-
G
code. The following definition will be of particular relevance in the next section to estimate the existence of transversal multi-control-
Z
gates.

Definition 3.2

(Quantum
G
code)
.

Let
G
be a group and let
\mathcal{Q}=\operatorname{CSS}(C_{0},C_{1})
be a quantum code such that
C_{0},C_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
are quasi-
G
codes. We see
C_{0}^{\perp}
and
C_{1}
as left
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
-modules. Then, we refer to
\mathcal{Q}
as a
quantum
G
code
if there exists a section
\mathfrak{s}
of the canonical map
\pi:C_{1}\to C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}
, i.e a
G
-module homomorphism

\mathfrak{s}:C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}\to C_{1}

(6)

such that
\pi\circ\mathfrak{s}=\operatorname{id}
. Moreover, we say that
\mathcal{Q}
is a
regular quantum
G
code
if
C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]
. Clearly, in that case, the dimension of
\mathcal{Q}
is
k_{\mathcal{Q}}=|G|
. Finally, we say that
\mathcal{Q}
is a
free quantum
G
code
if there exists an integer
r
such that
C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}[G]^{r}
, in which case its dimension is
k_{\mathcal{Q}}=r|G|
.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that a section always exists in the sense of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces, but the stronger condition in Definition
3.2
requires
\mathfrak{s}
to be compatible with the module structure, i.e
\mathfrak{s}\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ\mathfrak{s}
, for
\sigma\in G
. Also note that a surjective map to a free module always admits a section.

Multi-control-
Z
gates.

In this work, we consider quantum CSS codes whose main purpose is either to be used in magic state distillation protocols, or for circuit compilation. Central components of these protocols are non-Clifford gates, instances of which are multi-control-
Z
gates. These particular gates constitute our main focus.

Let
m
be a positive integer and
q
be a power of
2
. For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the action of a
physical
(m-1)
-control-
Z
gate

\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma}
on
m
physical qudit registers in the computational basis is given as follows,

\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma}\ket{x_0}\ket{x_1}\dots\ket{x_{m-1}}\coloneq\exp\left(i\pi\,\mathrm{tr}(\gamma\>x_{0}\>x_{1}\dots x_{m-1})\right)\ket{x_0}\ket{x_1}\dots\ket{x_{m-1}},

x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
i=0,\dots,m-1
, and
\mathrm{tr}\coloneq\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}
denotes the trace map from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Given a quantum CSS code
\mathcal{Q}
of dimension
k
, we may encode information in the tensor product code
\mathcal{Q}^{\otimes m}
, which is composed of
m
copies of
\mathcal{Q}
. We refer to each copy of the original code as a
code block
of
\mathcal{Q}^{\otimes m}
. We label the physical qudit registers in each code block by the set
N
of cardinality
n
.

A
physical

\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}
gate on physical qudit registers corresponding to elements
P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{m-1}\in N
, respectively in code blocks
0,1,2,\dots,m-1
, is denoted
\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma}[P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{m-1}]
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Likewise, given a set
L
of cardinality
k
and an encoding map
\operatorname{E}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{L}\to C_{1}/C_{0}^{\perp}
for
\mathcal{Q}
, a

\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}
operator targeting logical qudits indexed by
Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}\in L
belonging respectively to code blocks

, is denoted by
\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma}[Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}]}
. Its effect is given on a computational basis state by

\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma}[Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}]}\>\overline{\ket{x^0}}\>\overline{\ket{x^1}}\dots\overline{\ket{x^{m-1}}}\coloneq\exp\left(i\pi\,\mathrm{tr}(\gamma\>x^{0}_{Q_{0}}\>x^{1}_{Q_{1}}\dots x^{m-1}_{Q_{m-1}})\right)\overline{\ket{x^0}}\>\overline{\ket{x^1}}\dots\overline{\ket{x^{m-1}}},

where
x^{i}=(x^{i}_{Q})_{Q\in L}
,
i=0,\dots,m-1
. For the quantum codes relevant for us, such a logical operator may be performed via a (non-unique) circuit of physical
(m-1)
-control-
Z
gates. The minimal complexity of this circuit may vary from code to code but the ideal case is precisely when there exists such a circuit of depth one. In that case, a logical gate is said to admit a
transversal implementation
, or simply to be
transversal
.

3.2  Sufficient conditions for parallelizable addressability

In this section, we introduce a generic construction of quantum codes that support transversal logical multi-control-
Z
gates acting on any specified tuple of logical qudits of fixed size. We also analyze the extent to which these gates can be implemented in parallel.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
N
be an index set of cardinality

4
4

4

The index set

Corollary 3.5

Let

Remark 3.6
.

Informally, the mutli-control-

We prove Theorem

4.1.1  Class field theory

Our construction requires an unramified abelian extension of function field
\widetilde{F}/F
. Class field theory gives a complete description of all abelian extensions of a function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
in terms of its Picard group
\Pic(F)
. We will make a summary of a few results that suit our needs.

Let
P
be a rational place of
F
. The following classical proposition
[
29
]
, or see
[
14
, Section 3.3.2, Proposition 16 and 17]
, gives a description of unramified abelian extensions of
F
in which
P
splits completely.

Proposition 4.2
.

Fix a separable closure
F_{\mathrm{sep}}
of
F
. There exists a maximum unramified abelian extension of
F
in
F_{\mathrm{sep}}
in which
P
splits completely. It is called the Hilbert class field of
F
(with respect to
P
) and is denoted by
\Hil_{P}(F)
. The field of constants of
\Hil_{P}(F)
is the same as the one of
F
.

The following theorem
[
29
, Theorem 1.3]
gives a description of the Galois group of
\Hil_{P}(F)
, classifies intermediate extensions between
F
and
\Hil_{P}(F)
, and gives a criterion for rational places of
F
to split completely in any intermediate extension.

Theorem 4.3
.

With the notation above, one has

\Gal(\Hil_{P}(F)/F)\simeq\Pic^{0}(F).

In particular, Galois theory states that there is a one-to-one correspondence between subgroups
H
of
\Pic^{0}(F)
and unramified abelian extensions of
F
in
{F}_{sep}
, denoted
\widetilde{F}_{H}
, in which
P
splits completely. The Galois group of
\widetilde{F}_{H}
is naturally isomorphic to
\Pic^{0}(F)/H
.

In addition, a rational place
P^{\prime}
of
F
splits completely in
\widetilde{F}_{H}
if and only if the equivalence class of the degree zero divisor
P^{\prime}-P
in
\Pic^{0}(F)
belongs to
H
.

This proposition will be later used to lift algebraic geometry codes defined over some function field
F
. It remains to select appropriately the function field
F
and the subgroup
H
. This is the aim of the next section.

Proof of Theorem
4.1

The assumptions of the Theorem allow to use Corollary

\widetilde{C}
which is a quasi-
