Nikko J. Cleri, Zach J. Lewis, Joel Leja, Jakob M. Helton, Emilie Burnham, Olivia Curtis, Anna de Graaff, Michaela Hirschmann, Harley Katz, Michael V. Maseda, Ian McConachie, Adéle Plat, Lucie Scharré

Abstract
High-redshift galaxies have smaller radii, harder ionizing continua, and higher ionizing photon production efficiencies than lower redshift systems, which implies a corresponding evolution in the nebular conditions. A key metric to quantify gas properties is the ionization parameter, q q , the ratio of the local ionizing photon flux to the local hydrogen density. The ionization parameter is often inferred from observed emission line ratios, namely O32 ≡ [O III] / [O II] \equiv\hbox{[{O~III}]}/\hbox{[{O~II}]} . Prior to JWST, statistical samples of ionization parameter-sensitive emission lines in the rest-frame optical remained inaccessible for high-redshift galaxies. We investigate the dimensionless ionization parameter, U ≡ q / c U\equiv q/c at 3 ≲ z ≲ 9 3\lesssim z\lesssim 9 , inferred using Cloudy photoionization models from the O32 ratios for 434 galaxies in the RUBIES survey with JWST/NIRSpec PRISM and G395M spectroscopy, constituting the largest high-redshift population study of the ionization parameter to date. We compare to lower-redshift samples from SDSS, LEGA-C, and KBSS to probe the evolution of the ionization parameter from 0 ≲ z ≲ 9 0\lesssim z\lesssim 9 . We find that the ionization parameter increases with redshift and specific star formation rate, and decreases with stellar mass. We combine the predictive power with multivariate relations to estimate log U from redshift, stellar mass, and specific star formation rate for use in cases where O32 is not available from spectroscopy and show that the ionization parameter increases with redshift even at fixed stellar mass and specific star formation rate by a factor of ∼ 4 \sim 4 from z = 2 z=2 to z = 6 z=6 . Crucially, and in contrast to previous linear best-fit calibrations, our inference results in a systematic uncertainty in log U of ∼ 0.3 \sim 0.3 dex at zero measurement uncertainty due to the wide range of photoionization models that predict the same O32 ratio without informative priors. Finally, we discuss future modeling frameworks to accept many observed emission lines, unlocking the potential to simultaneously constrain gas-phase abundances, densities, ionizing sources, and ionization parameters to high accuracies for individual galaxies.

IV
Discussion

IV.1
The Redshift Evolution of the Ionization Parameter

Throughout this work, we have used photoionization models to infer the ionization parameter from observed rest-frame optical emission lines (
[O III]
and
[O II]
) for galaxies from
0<z<9
, with a focus on the
3\lesssim z\lesssim 9
sample from RUBIES. We show that the observed O32 ratios and inferred log U increase with increasing redshift and specific star formation rate, and decrease with increasing stellar mass (see Figures
1
and
3
). This represents the first time that these relations have been quantified with such a sample at high redshift. We discuss the implications of these results here.

Previous works have shown that the ionization parameter increases with increasing redshift to at least cosmic noon
(
113
;
78
, e.g.,)
. In this work, we have shown that this trend continues to
z\sim 9
. While redshift is the immediate observable obtained from spectroscopy, we show that the evolution of the ionization parameter with redshift can be more cleanly attributed to evolution with redshift and other galaxy properties (i.e. stellar mass and specific star formation rate).

Empirical studies of low-redshift galaxies suggest a correlation between the ionization parameter and gas-phase metallicity
(
110
;
112
;
113
;
78
, e.g.,)
. This is explained by the correlation between gas-phase metallicity and stellar mass (the “mass-metallicity relation”), which has been shown to evolve with redshift
(
120
;
78
;
54
;
99
;
97
;
60
, e.g.,)
. In Figures
1
and
3
, the relationship between the observed O32 and log U with the stellar mass is likely driven by the mass-metallicity relation, where lower metallicity stellar populations emit harder ionizing continua with greater ionizing photon production efficiency
(
113
;
107
;
78
, e.g.,)
.

Previous works at low redshifts (
z\lesssim 2
) have shown that the ionization parameter increases with increasing specific star formation rate
(
38
, e.g.,)
. We find the same behavior continues to higher redshifts, both from O32 and the inferred ionization parameter (see Figure
1
and
3
). We derive star formation rates from
H
\beta
line luminosities, which trace the H-ionizing radiation (
>13.6
eV) from young, massive stars. Thus, Balmer line luminosities can be interpreted as a tracer of the near-instantaneous (
<10
Myr) star formation rate
(
43
;
42
, e.g.,)
. The correlation between the ionization parameter and the specific star formation rate can be in part interpreted as consistent with the ionizing photon production from H II regions undergoing active star formation, as the ionization parameter is linearly proportional to the local ionizing photon flux
(
50
, e.g.,)
. Additionally, the ionization parameter is shown to increase with compactness and star formation rate surface density, which have been shown to increase with redshift and specific star formation rate
(
106
;
73
;
72
;
83
;
74
;
131
, e.g.,)
.

IV.1.1
Comparison to Simulations

In Figure
1
, we compare observations from RUBIES and our lower redshift comparison samples to predictions for simulated galaxies from
SPHINX
20
and LUMEN. At fixed redshifts,
SPHINX
20
and LUMEN show higher O32, lower stellar mass, and higher sSFRs than the RUBIES observations, though the simulated samples show large scatter. This result is in part due to the redshift range of the
SPHINX
20
simulations (
4.64\leq z\leq 10
) being higher than that of RUBIES (
3\lesssim z\lesssim 9
, with a median
z=4.47
), and the
M_{UV}
selection biasing
SPHINX
20
toward star-forming galaxies. LUMEN is similarly biased to highly star forming systems by construction, where the Cloudy models are focused on the HII regions around star clusters in IllustrisTNG50 galaxies. These biases may indicate higher ionizing photon production efficiencies or denser nebulae in the simulated galaxies than the observed samples
(
89
;
88
;
39
;
101
, e.g.,)
.

IV.2
Methodology for Determining the Ionization Parameter from Observations

IV.2.1
Towards More Accurate Uncertainties in Nebular Inference

Previous works present linear best-fit calibrations to estimate the ionization parameter from observed O32 ratios
(
113
;
78
, e.g.,)
. The ionization parameters and associated uncertainties estimated through this method represent those of the mean of the respective samples from
113
and
78
. Throughout this work, we have instead used photoionization models to infer the ionization parameter from the
[O III]
and
[O II]
emission line fluxes and their uncertainties for individual galaxies. Here we argue that the inference methodology presented in this work produces more realistic log U uncertainties for individual objects than the linear best-fit calibrations.

The ionization parameters computed in
113
are based on a photoionization model grid from
110
and
112
using Cloudy v13.02
25
.
113
uses BPASS v2
(
108
;
23
)
input ionizing spectra with fixed age
(
82
, 100 Myr;)
, varying metallicity
Z/Z_{\odot}\approx 0.07-1.0
, assuming plane-parallel geometry and a fixed hydrogen density
n_{H}=300
cm
-3
, consistent with the population of
z\sim 2.3
galaxies in KBSS. The full details of their photoionization models can be found in
110
and
112
.

The ionization parameters computed in
78

(
6
, IZI;)
.
78
computes posteriors for ionization parameter using MAPPINGS-V
(
49

assuming fixed pressure over a grid of gas phase metallicities and ionization parameters. The full details of their modeling and inference of ionization parameters can be found in Section 4.2 of
78

We show in Figure
2
that the uncertainties derived from propagating through a linear calibration are systematically smaller than those estimated by the inference framework described in this work. The ionization parameter is often degenerate with single emission line ratios (e.g., O32). In our methodology, we capture the full range of degeneracies by using the broad suite of models from
16
, thus accurately modeling the true uncertainty as verified by mock testing. We do note that these large uncertainties results in sub-unity
\chi^{2}_{\nu}
(See Table

16

2

We demonstrate that the inclusion of additional constraints decreases the inferred uncertainties and lowers the uncertainty “floor”, meaning that there still exists a minimum uncertainty at arbitrarily small measurement uncertainties. In future work, we will explore the inclusion of more emission lines to reduce our prior dependence (see Section
IV.2.2
for discussion).

To complement the above analyses, we also present calibrations to calculate ionization parameter from observations and derived quantities other than the O32 ratio. We show the relations between ionization parameter and redshift, stellar mass, and specific star formation rate in Figure

Importantly, the redshift dependence suggests galaxies do evolve with time, beyond just their mass and sSFR; for example, for a galaxy with LMC-like stellar mass and specific star formation rate (

IV.2.2
Caveats and Improvements for Future Work

The methodology of determining ionization parameters can be improved beyond the simplifications made in this work. One such improvement is the allowance for multiple clouds/ionization zones
(
65
, e.g., Highly Optimised Emission-line Ratios Using photoioNisation (
HOMERUN
);)
. Recent works have used
HOMERUN
to demonstrate that the model of a single cloud ionized by a single source is not sufficient to reproduce the observed spectra in three galaxies at
2<z<6

(
69
)
.
69
show that the ionization parameter from an individual spectrum can vary by as much as
\sim
0.4 dex when allowing for unresolved high density clouds (
n_{e,max}=10^{7}
cm
-3
), and that these high density clouds can account for nearly half of the
\hbox{[{O~III}]}~\lambda 5008
emission.

Another improvement to this methodology is the incorporation of more emission lines and other spectral features. Contemporary methodology such as
HOMERUN
,
MULTIGRIS

(
56
)
, and
Cue

(
61
)
simultaneously model multiple emission lines, thus leveraging more information than single strong line ratio diagnostics. The inclusion of additional spectral features in the inference can simultaneously inform the ionization parameter and other nebular conditions (e.g., gas-phase metallicity, hydrogen density) and the ionizing continuum (e.g., stellar populations versus black hole accretion disks, initial mass function and other stellar population properties) to allow for a more complete interpretation of observations.

We look forward to upcoming spectroscopy to include this additional data in future works studying the nebular conditions of high-redshift galaxies. Particularly, the collisional deexcitation of
[O II]
confounds the derivation of log U from O32 with the higher electron densities observed in higher-
z

(

[O II]

(

V
Conclusions

In this work, we study the evolution of the ionization parameter,
\log
U, with redshift and other observed and derived quantities for 137 galaxies in the RUBIES survey. We compile literature sources from SDSS, LEGA-C, and KBSS to form a sample which spans
0\lesssim z\lesssim 9
. Our primary findings are as follows:

•

We show that the ionization parameter-sensitive line ratio O32 increases with increasing redshift in Figure
1
. We also find that O32 decreases with increasing stellar mass and increases with increasing specific star formation rate.

•

We infer ionization parameters based on
[O III]
and
[O II]
using photoionization models from
16
, and find log U medians broadly consistent with linear best-fit calibrations, though with systematically larger uncertainties (see Figure
2
). We argue that the larger uncertainties inferred from the photoionization models are more representative of the true uncertainty on an individual log U estimate with O32 as the only information. We show that the introduction of an informative prior on the model grid parameters (e.g., gas-phase metallicity) improves the uncertainties on the inferred ionization parameter, though there exists an uncertainty floor which is an indicator of the incompleteness of information derivable from O32 alone. We discuss the implications of these inferred
\log
U values and their uncertainties in Section
IV.2
.

•

We show that the inferred ionization parameters increase with increasing redshift and specific star formation rate, and decrease with increasing stellar mass. We generalize this result to three and four dimensions by providing multidimensional fits to predict
\log
U from combinations of redshift, stellar mass, and specific star formation rate. We discuss the implications of these fits and their utility in SED fitting where
[O III]
and
[O II]
may not always be available through spectroscopy in Section
IV
.

Future work in this field will require additional information beyond
[O III]
and
[O II]
emission lines to characterize the gas conditions in high-redshift galaxies. We discuss in Section
IV
several methods for adding this information, including modeling the multi-phase ISM and including more emission lines in the inference framework. We look forward to upcoming H-grating data from JWST (e.g., CEDAR) to simultaneously model the temperature, density, and ionization structures in statistical samples of high-redshift galaxies.
