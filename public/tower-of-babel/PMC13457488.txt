Sex-specific predictors of contralateral patent processus vaginalis in unilateral pediatric inguinal hernia or hydrocele: a cohort study of 1031 laparoscopic cases
Authors: Masanaga Matsumoto, Yohei Sanmoto, Kouji Masumoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAsymptomatic contralateral patent processus vaginalis (CPPV) is commonly detected during the laparoscopic repair of inguinal hernias in children and is a risk factor for metachronous contralateral inguinal hernias (MCIHs). However, predicting CPPV preoperatively is challenging, and evidence on its clinical predictors is limited, particularly regarding sex-specific differences. We aimed to identify preoperative predictors of CPPV and to evaluate sex-specific risk factors.MethodsThis retrospective observational study was conducted at a single tertiary-care university hospital and included children (aged < 16 years) who underwent single-incision laparoscopic percutaneous extraperitoneal closure for a clinically unilateral inguinal hernia or hydrocele between 2013 and 2024. Those who underwent surgery for a recurrent inguinal hernia, MCIH, or bilateral presentation were excluded. The preoperative clinical characteristics were analyzed. Missing data were addressed using multiple imputation before conducting multivariable logistic regression analyses in the overall and sex-stratified cohorts to determine the independent predictors of CPPV.ResultsOverall, 1031 children (502 males, 529 females) were included. CPPV was identified in 511 (49.6%) patients. In the multivariable analysis of the overall cohort, left-sided presentation and history of incarceration were independently associated with CPPV, whereas sex was not. In sex-stratified analyses, hydrocele was independently associated with CPPV in males (odds ratio [OR], 1.61; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.06–2.45; P = 0.03). In females, left-sided presentation (OR, 2.80; 95% CI, 1.94–4.03; P < 0.001), incarceration history (OR, 5.19; 95% CI, 1.39–19.30; P = 0.014), and older age (OR, 1.01; 95% CI, 1.00–1.01; P = 0.046) were associated with CPPV.ConclusionsPreoperative predictors of CPPV showed sex-specific patterns. Sex was not an independent predictor; however, hydrocele was associated with CPPV in males, whereas, in females, left-sided presentation and incarceration history showed strong independent associations with CPPV. These findings may inform preoperative counseling, operative approach selection, and meticulous contralateral laparoscopic exploration, potentially reducing the risk of subsequent MCIH.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00464-026-13068-x.

Inguinal hernia repair is one of the most frequently performed procedures in pediatric surgery [1]. Conventional repair through an inguinal incision has long been the standard approach; nonetheless, laparoscopic techniques have become popular owing to their cosmetic benefits and minimally invasive nature [2, 3]. An additional benefit of laparoscopy is that it can be used to inspect the contralateral internal inguinal ring in patients presenting with unilateral symptoms during the same procedure. A systematic review of children aged < 18 years who underwent laparoscopic repair for unilateral inguinal hernia revealed a contralateral patent processus vaginalis (CPPV) detection rate of 38.5% [4]. CPPV is considered a risk factor for metachronous contralateral inguinal hernia (MCIH) [5], and when identified intraoperatively during laparoscopic repair, it can be closed prophylactically without additional skin incisions [4, 6]. Therefore, preoperative prediction of CPPV risk may provide important information for shared decision-making regarding the choice of surgical approach in children presenting with unilateral symptoms and may also support careful contralateral inspection when a laparoscopic approach is selected [7].

Several attempts have been made to identify the preoperative factors associated with asymptomatic CPPV in children with clinically unilateral inguinal hernia or hydrocele. Yongjie et al. conducted a retrospective study of 259 children undergoing laparoscopic repair and identified younger age and female sex as independent risk factors for CPPV [8]. Similarly, Abdelmotaal et al. prospectively evaluated 141 children and reported that age < 1 year and the presence of left-sided hernias were significantly associated with CPPV [9]. However, the existing evidence is based on relatively modest sample sizes and predominantly mixed-sex analyses without stratification. Furthermore, alternative diagnostic approaches for CPPV, including the silk glove sign [10] and ultrasonography [9, 11], have been described. However, these techniques are operator-dependent, require specific expertise, and may be less practical for routine clinical use, thereby potentially limiting their consistency and reproducibility.

In this context, we analyzed a cohort of more than 1,000 children who underwent single-incision laparoscopic percutaneous extraperitoneal closure (SILPEC) with clinically unilateral inguinal hernia or hydrocele to identify preoperative predictors of CPPV and to evaluate sex-specific risk factors.

Materials and methods

Patients and data collection

In this retrospective observational study, we included patients aged < 16 years who underwent SILPEC for inguinal hernia or hydrocele at the Department of Pediatric Surgery at our hospital in Japan, between January 2013 and December 2024. Patients who presented with unilateral inguinal symptoms were eligible. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) reoperation for recurrent cases, (2) surgery for MCIH, and (3) preoperative bilateral inguinal symptoms. Preoperative hernia presentation, whether unilateral or bilateral, was determined based on the attending physician’s outpatient records, including symptoms reported by the patient or guardians and physical examination findings. In our institution, ultrasonography was not routinely performed.

Data were extracted from electronic medical records and included patient characteristics (age at operation, sex, height, body weight, body mass index [BMI], gestational age, birth weight, history of chronic lung disease, and congenital heart disease), preoperative clinical factors (laterality of the symptomatic side, history of incarceration, and family history of pediatric inguinal hernia), sex-specific clinical findings (hydrocele and retractile testis on the asymptomatic side in males, and canal of Nuck hydrocele or ovarian sliding hernia in females), and intraoperative findings, specifically the presence of CPPV. All variables except CPPV were determined preoperatively and not based on intraoperative findings. Hydrocele was diagnosed based on physical examination findings, including palpation and transillumination.

Operative strategy and surgical procedure

During the study period, SILPEC was the standard surgical approach for inguinal hernias and hydroceles at our institution. Conventional open herniotomy (the Potts procedure) was also used as an alternative. The final procedure was chosen by patients and/or their guardians. The detailed surgical technique for SILPEC has been previously described [12]; during the procedure, both internal inguinal rings were routinely inspected using direct laparoscopic visualization. When the contralateral internal ring met the predefined institutional criteria for CPPV, prophylactic repair was performed even when clinical symptoms were absent. The specific criteria for CPPV diagnosis and corresponding treatment indications are described below.

Intraoperative diagnostic criteria for CPPV

At our institution, the morphology of the contralateral internal inguinal ring is assessed intraoperatively using a four-type framework modified from the classification proposed by Chin et al. (Fig. 1) [13]. Type I corresponds to a flat, completely obliterated internal ring with continuous peritoneal coverage over the existing structures. Type II refers to a peritoneal veil over the internal ring, without a visible orifice after exposure. Type III represents the presence of a micro-orifice and is further subdivided based on the depth of the lumen: Type IIIa indicates a shallow blind-ending pouch confined to the level of the internal ring, whereas Type IIIb denotes a tract extending beyond the internal ring toward the inguinal canal. Type IV is characterized by a widely open internal ring with a patent tract. In institutional practice, contralateral findings corresponding to Type IIIb or Type IV are interpreted as CPPV-positive and managed with prophylactic contralateral repair. During the study period, SILPEC procedures were performed by multiple surgeons; however, all attending surgeons at our institution had been trained in SILPEC and shared the same institutional criteria for CPPV classification and prophylactic contralateral repair. Handling of missing data

Missing values were addressed through multiple imputation using chained equations. The number of imputations (m) was set to 20 to ensure adequate relative efficiency. The imputation model included clinically relevant variables to ensure congeniality, including age at surgery, sex, height, body weight, BMI, gestational age, birth weight, history of chronic lung disease, history of congenital heart disease, family history of pediatric inguinal hernia, history of hernia incarceration, laterality of the symptomatic side, and the presence of CPPV.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome was the identification of preoperative predictors of CPPV in children with a unilateral inguinal hernia or hydrocele. Associations were initially assessed in the overall cohort and subsequently analyzed using sex-stratified models to identify sex-specific predictors of CPPV.

Statistical analysis

In the analyses using multiple imputed datasets, gestational age and birth weight were imputed as continuous variables, while categorical variables were subsequently derived. Continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and categorical variables as counts with percentages. The patients were categorized as CPPV-positive or CPPV-negative in the overall and sex-stratified cohorts. Group comparisons were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate.

Associations between the preoperative variables and CPPV were evaluated using logistic regression analysis. Univariate analyses were first conducted, followed by multivariable models, including covariates selected a priori based on clinical relevance and consideration of multicollinearity. The models were constructed separately for the overall and sex-stratified cohorts. The results are reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The regression estimates from multiple imputed datasets were pooled using Rubin’s rules. All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.5.2). P < 0.05 (two-sided) was considered statistically significant.

Ethics

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2024 revision) and approved by the Institutional Review Board of the University of Tsukuba Hospital (approval no. R07-328). The requirement for written informed consent was waived by the institutional review board because clinical information obtained during routine medical care was used. An opt-out option was implemented to allow participants and their guardians to decline the use of clinical data for research purposes.

Prior to the initiation of this study, a study protocol was established and submitted to the Institutional Review Board as part of the ethics review process. An English translated summary of the original Japanese study protocol is provided as Supplementary File 1.

Results

Participant characteristics

Overall, 1413 children underwent surgery for inguinal hernias or hydroceles at our institution. Of these, 1123 children underwent SILPEC, whereas 290 underwent conventional open herniotomy using the Potts procedure, including 223 males and 67 females. Thus, SILPEC and open repair accounted for 79.5 and 20.5% of all surgically treated cases, respectively. Among the 1123 children who underwent SILPEC, 92 were excluded: 16 who underwent reoperation for recurrent inguinal hernia or hydrocele, 10 who underwent surgery for MCIH, and 66 who presented with bilateral symptoms. Consequently, 1,031 patients were included in the final analysis (502 males and 529 females) (Fig. 2). In the overall cohort, 511 (49.6%) patients were CPPV-positive (Table 1). Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses

In the overall cohort, univariate logistic regression showed that female sex (OR 1.40, 95% CI 1.10–1.80; P = 0.007), left-sided presentation (OR 1.79, 95% CI 1.40–2.30; P < 0.001), and height (OR 1.01, 95% CI 1.00–1.01; P = 0.04) were associated with CPPV (Table 4). In the multivariable analysis, left-sided presentation (OR 1.79, 95% CI 1.38–2.30; P < 0.001) and incarceration history (OR 2.01, 95% CI 1.18–3.41; P = 0.010) were independently associated with CPPV, whereas female sex was not after adjustment (OR 1.25, 95% CI 0.95–1.63; P = 0.11) (Table 4). Discussion

In this large cohort of 1031 children undergoing SILPEC for clinically unilateral inguinal hernia or hydrocele, we examined the preoperative predictors of CPPV in the overall population and in sex-specific analyses. The key findings of this study are as follows. In the overall cohort, left-sided presentation and history of incarceration were associated with CPPV; after adjustment, sex did not show any significant associations. Nonetheless, stratified analyses revealed sex-specific associations: hydrocele was associated with CPPV in males, whereas in females, left-sided presentation showed a particularly strong association. Incarceration history and older age at surgery were also associated with CPPV. Clinically, these predictors may help surgeons identify children in whom CPPV is more likely to be encountered during laparoscopic repair. This information may enable more specific preoperative counseling regarding the possibility that prophylactic contralateral repair may be considered if CPPV is confirmed intraoperatively, and may support shared decision-making regarding the surgical approach, particularly when choosing between open repair and laparoscopy.

An association between hydroceles and CPPV in male patients has been reported in a limited number of previous studies, supporting our findings. Hua et al. reported that CPPV was more frequently detected in children presenting with hydrocele than in those with inguinal hernia during laparoscopic inspection of the contralateral internal ring for unilateral inguinal pathology (61.1 vs. 50.1%) [14]. Similarly, Yongjie et al. evaluated predictors of CPPV in children undergoing laparoscopic repair of unilateral hernia or hydrocele and found that hydrocele was associated with increased odds of CPPV, although this association was not statistically significant (OR 1.62, 95% CI 0.77–3.41; P = 0.202) [8]. The direction of the effect was consistent with our results, and the lack of significance may be partly explained by the small number of hydrocele cases included in their regression analysis (n = 41). In addition, because their analysis was not stratified by sex, potential sex-specific associations may not have been fully captured. A causal relationship between hydrocele and CPPV cannot be established. However, the observed association may reflect factors beyond simple anatomical patency. Obliteration of the processus vaginalis is regulated by complex developmental mechanisms, including hormonally mediated autonomic modulation and programmed smooth muscle regression [15]. Although speculative, subtle variations in this regression process may contribute to distinct clinical phenotypes and persistent contralateral patency.

Here, left-sided presentation emerged as an independent predictor of CPPV in females, supporting prior evidence that laterality may serve as a clinical marker of contralateral predisposition. A meta-analysis of prospective studies reported an overall MCIH incidence of 6%, with a significantly higher risk after an initial left-sided hernia than after a right-sided hernia (OR 2.55, 95% CI 1.56–4.17; P = 0.0002) [16]. Similarly, Kokorowski et al. reported that initial left-sided presentation was associated with subsequent contralateral hernia in children (OR 1.71, 95% CI 1.21–2.41) [17]. Although laterality has been consistently identified as a reproducible risk marker, these studies did not provide a definitive mechanistic explanation for the left-sided association. In males, pediatric inguinal hernia is classically right-predominant, reflecting delayed right-sided testicular descent and prolonged patency of the right processus vaginalis [18]; however, this asymmetry has not been consistently linked to CPPV risk. In male-only cohorts of unilateral inguinal hernia or hydrocele evaluated by transinguinal laparoscopy, the side of initial presentation was not associated with CPPV [7]. Here, the association between left-sided presentation and CPPV was observed only in females, suggesting a potential interaction between sex and laterality. Thus, this association cannot be explained solely by the well-known embryologic asymmetry related to testicular descent or processus vaginalis closure, and the underlying biological mechanism warrants further investigation.

In addition to a left-sided presentation, sex-stratified analysis revealed a strong association between incarceration history and CPPV in females. However, incarceration occurred in only 16 girls (13 CPPV-positive and 3 CPPV-negative), yielding a wide 95% CI and indicating statistical instability. Thus, this association should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. Incarceration history was not evaluated in previous studies investigating the predictors of CPPV [8, 9]. Conversely, in previous cohort analyses, a larger ipsilateral hernia defect size was identified as a significant predictor of CPPV [8, 10]. Mechanistic explanations have not been extensively discussed; nevertheless, it is biologically plausible that patients with a larger hernia orifice, which potentially predisposes them to incarceration, may share developmental susceptibility to bilateral persistence of the processus vaginalis. Furthermore, although the effect of incarceration was not statistically significant in the male cohort (OR 1.61, 95% CI 0.86–3.00; P = 0.13), the direction of the effect was consistent with that observed in females. This directional concordance across the sexes suggests a shared underlying tendency. However, given the limited number of overall events, larger studies are needed to determine whether incarceration history is a reproducible and clinically meaningful predictor of CPPV.

Age at surgery was significantly associated with CPPV in females. However, the magnitude of the effect was minimal (OR, 1.01), suggesting a limited clinical impact, despite statistical significance. Previous cohort studies revealed a higher prevalence of CPPV in younger patients, with a declining incidence over time [8, 9], a pattern consistent with the biological process of progressive postnatal closure of the processus vaginalis [18]. However, the association between age and CPPV has not been consistent across studies [5]. Apparent discrepancies in the literature may partly reflect differences in the cohort composition, inclusion criteria, and intraoperative definitions of CPPV. In the present study, patients < 1 year accounted for only 6.2% of the cohort, whereas they comprised 39.0% in the study by Abdelmotaal et al. [9]. Accordingly, the marked differences in age distribution among the studies may have influenced the observed strength and direction of the age–CPPV association. Moreover, ovarian sliding hernia and a history of incarceration, which are more frequently observed in early infancy [19], were incorporated in our multivariable model. Adjustments for these interrelated variables may partially account for the observed age effect by attenuating the apparent independent contribution. In this context, the statistical significance of age in females should be interpreted cautiously.

This study had some limitations. First, it is retrospective in design and was conducted in a single center. The sample size was relatively large; nonetheless, the generalizability of our findings may be limited. In particular, while institutional criteria were used for CPPV classification, the final intraoperative decision to perform prophylactic repair was at the surgeon’s discretion, thereby introducing potential variability in CPPV diagnosis and management. Moreover, formal assessment of the reproducibility of this classification, such as interobserver agreement based on independent review of intraoperative images or videos, was not performed. Second, the presence and side of hydrocele were determined clinically without routine ultrasonographic confirmation. Therefore, mild or clinically occult contralateral hydrocele may not have been fully detected preoperatively. Third, the primary outcome was intraoperative CPPV and not long-term MCIH. A patent processus vaginalis is considered a risk factor for MCIH; however, it does not invariably lead to hernia development [20]. Therefore, the clinical implications of identified predictors should be interpreted with caution. Further studies with long-term follow-up are needed to determine whether prophylactic contralateral repair based on intraoperative CPPV assessment and preoperative risk stratification can reduce the subsequent development of clinically evident MCIH. Fourth, although multiple imputation was used to address the missing data, the missing-at-random assumption underlying this approach could not be directly verified, and residual bias could not be excluded. Finally, patients who underwent conventional open herniotomy using the Potts procedure, which accounted for 20.5% of all surgically treated cases during the study period, were excluded because CPPV could not be assessed intraoperatively. Although SILPEC was the institutional standard, the surgical approach was selected by patients and/or their guardians after explanation of the available options and was not randomized. Differences in clinical characteristics or treatment preferences between the open repair and SILPEC groups may have affected the distribution of CPPV risk factors.

In conclusion, we evaluated the preoperative predictors of intraoperative CPPV among children who underwent SILPEC for clinically unilateral inguinal hernias or hydroceles. Sex was not an independent predictor in the overall multivariable model; however, exploratory sex-stratified analyses identified hydrocele in males and left-sided presentation, incarceration history, and older age at operation in females as factors potentially associated with CPPV. Nevertheless, given the limited number of events in certain subgroups, particularly among females with incarceration, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Further large-scale, multicenter investigations are required to validate these associations and to determine whether such factors can inform clinically meaningful risk stratification and ultimately contribute to reducing the future development of MCIH.
