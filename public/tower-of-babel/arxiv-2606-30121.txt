Iterative construction of Hermitian-Einstein metrics on stable bundles
Jiaxuan Fan, Zhiyao Xiong, Xiaokui Yang, Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
. Let E E be a stable holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler (or Gauduchon) manifold ( M , ω g ) (M,\omega_{g}) . We show that for any real number μ > 0 \mu>0 and any initial Hermitian metric h 0 h_{0} on E E , there exists a unique iteration sequence { h m } \{h_{m}\} satisfying Λ ω g ​ ( − 1 ​ R h m + 1 ) = ( λ E − μ ) ​ h m + 1 + μ ​ h m , \Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{m+1}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}-\mu)h_{m+1}+\mu h_{m}, and { h m } \{h_{m}\} converges smoothly to a Hermitian–Einstein metric h ∞ h_{\infty} on E E satisfying Λ ω g ​ ( − 1 ​ R h ∞ ) = λ E ​ h ∞ , \Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=\lambda_{E}h_{\infty}, where λ E ∈ ℝ \lambda_{E}\in{\mathbb{R}} is the stability constant. A key feature of this proof is that it is independent of Donaldson’s variational framework and applies to non-Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a stable holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler (or Gauduchon) manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Then, for any real number
\mu>0
and any initial Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
, there exists a unique iteration sequence
\{h_{m}\}
satisfying

(1.1)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{m+1}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}-\mu)h_{m+1}+\mu h_{m},

and
\{h_{m}\}
converges smoothly to a Hermitian-Einstein metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
satisfying

(1.2)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=\lambda_{E}h_{\infty},

where
\lambda_{E}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the stability constant

(1.3)

\lambda_{E}=\frac{2\pi n\int_{M}c^{\mathrm{BC}}_{1}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}}{{\rm rk}(E)\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}.

Remark 1.2
.

The sign of
\lambda_{E}

leads to three natural variants of the iteration.

(1)

If
\lambda_{E}>0
, one can choose
\mu=\lambda_{E}
, reducing the iteration to

(1.4)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{m+1}}\right)=\lambda_{E}h_{m}.

(2)

If
\lambda_{E}=0
, then the iteration equation becomes

(1.5)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{m+1}}\right)=-\mu h_{m+1}+\mu h_{m},

and
\{h_{m}\}
converges smoothly to a Hermitian-Einstein metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
satisfying

(1.6)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=0.

(3)

If
\lambda_{E}<0
, then
\lambda_{E^{*}}>0
for the dual bundle
E^{*}
. One can iterate on
E^{*}
:

(1.7)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h^{*}_{m+1}}\right)=\lambda_{E^{*}}h^{*}_{m}.

The resulting limit
h^{*}_{\infty}
is a smooth Hermitian-Einstein metric on
E^{*}
,
and its dual induces a smooth Hermitian-Einstein metric on
E
.

In particular, one gets the following special case:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
E
be a stable holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
with positive stability constant
\lambda_{E}
. Then for any initial Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
, there exists a unique iteration sequence
\{h_{m}\}
satisfying

(1.8)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{m+1}}\right)=\lambda_{E}h_{m},

and
\{h_{m}\}
converges smoothly to a Hermitian-Einstein metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
satisfying

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=\lambda_{E}h_{\infty}.

2.
A priori estimates

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
over a compact Gauduchon manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, i.e.
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0
.
For a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, we write

(2.1)

S^{h}:=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}),\qquad K^{h}:=S^{h}\cdot h^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

Suppose that
\{h_{0},h_{1},\cdots\}
is a sequence of Hermitian metrics on
E
satisfying

(2.2)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{m+1}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}-\mu)h_{m+1}+\mu h_{m}.

If we write

(2.3)

H_{m}=h_{m}\cdot h_{0}^{-1},\qquad Q_{m}=H_{m-1}\cdot H_{m}^{-1}=h_{m-1}\cdot h_{m}^{-1},

for
m\geq 1
, then the equation (
2.2
) is equivalent to

(2.4)

K^{h_{m}}=(\lambda_{E}-\mu)\mathrm{Id}_{E}+\mu Q_{m}.

Clearly, the endomorphism
Q_{m}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is positive and self-adjoint with respect to both
h_{m-1}
and
h_{m}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C=C(h_{0},h_{1},\lambda_{E},\mu)
such that

(2.5)

C^{-1}h_{m}\leq h_{m-1}\leq Ch_{m}

for all
m\geq 1
.
In particular,

(2.6)

C^{-1}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\leq Q_{m}\leq C\mathrm{Id}_{E},

with respect to
h_{m}
.
Moreover, one has

(2.7)

\|K^{h_{m}}\|_{C^{0}(M,\omega_{g},h_{m})}\leq C,

and

(2.8)

\lim_{m}\|Q_{m+1}-Q_{m}\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{m})}=0.

Proof.

It is clear that there is a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(h_{0},h_{1})
such that
(
2.5
) holds for
m=1
with
C=C_{1}
. We show by induction that (
2.5
) holds for all
m\geq 1
with this constant
C
. Suppose that (
2.5
) holds for
m
with constant
C
. By this induction hypothesis, one has

(2.9)

S^{h_{m}}-(\lambda_{E}-\mu)h_{m}=\mu h_{m-1}\leq C\mu h_{m}=S^{Ch_{m+1}}-(\lambda_{E}-\mu)\left(Ch_{m+1}\right).

By
[
WYY26b+
, Theorem 2.3]
, one yields

(2.10)

h_{m}\leq Ch_{m+1}.

Similarly,

(2.11)

S^{h_{m+1}}-(\lambda_{E}-\mu)h_{m+1}=\mu h_{m}\leq C\mu h_{m-1}=S^{Ch_{m}}-(\lambda_{E}-\mu)(Ch_{m}),

and so

h_{m+1}\leq Ch_{m}
. Thus the estimate (
2.5
) holds for
m+1
. The equation
(
2.4
) gives

(2.12)

\|K^{h_{m}}\|_{C^{0}(M,\omega_{g},h_{m})}\leq\sqrt{r}\left(|\lambda_{E}-\mu|+\mu C\right).

Moreover, if we use the Hermitian-Yang-Mills functional

(2.13)

{\mathcal{E}}(h)=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left((K^{h}-\lambda_{E}\mathrm{Id}_{E})^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},

and write
{\mathcal{E}}_{m}:={\mathcal{E}}(h_{m})
, then it is known that

(2.14)

{\mathcal{E}}_{m}-{\mathcal{E}}_{m+1}\geq\|K^{h_{m}}-K^{h_{m+1}}\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{m})}^{2}

for all
m\geq 0
. Indeed, since both
K^{h_{m}}
and
K^{h_{m+1}}
are self-adjoint with respect to
h_{m}
, a direct calculation shows

(2.15)

{\mathcal{E}}_{m}-{\mathcal{E}}_{m+1}=\|K^{h_{m}}-K^{h_{m+1}}\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{m})}^{2}+2\left(K^{h_{m}}-K^{h_{m+1}},K^{h_{m+1}}-\lambda_{E}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)_{g,h_{m}}.

By
[
WYY26+
, Proposition 3.6]
, one has

(2.16)

K^{h_{m+1}}-K^{h_{m}}=\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{\partial}\left(\partial^{h_{m}}(Q_{m+1}^{-1})\cdot Q_{m+1}\right)=-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{\partial}\left(Q_{m+1}^{-1}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\right).

Since equation (
2.4
) has the form

(2.17)

K^{h_{m+1}}-\lambda_{E}\mathrm{Id}_{E}=\mu(Q_{m+1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E}),

we obtain

(2.18)

\displaystyle\left(K^{h_{m}}-K^{h_{m+1}},K^{h_{m+1}}-\lambda_{E}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)_{g,h_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\int_{M}\left\langle\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{\partial}\left(Q_{m+1}^{-1}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\right),Q_{m+1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right\rangle_{h_{m}}\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\int_{M}\left\langle Q_{m+1}^{-1}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1},\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\right\rangle_{g,h_{m}}\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle+\mu n\sqrt{-1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Q_{m+1}^{-1}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\cdot(Q_{m+1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E})\right)\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{n-1}.

For the torsion term, we compute

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Q_{m+1}^{-1}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\cdot(Q_{m+1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Q_{m+1}^{-1}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\cdot Q_{m+1}\right)-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Q_{m+1}^{-1}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\right)-\partial\log\det Q_{m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}Q_{m+1}-\log\det Q_{m+1}\right).

Here we use
\mathrm{tr}_{E}(\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1})=\partial\mathrm{tr}_{E}Q_{m+1}
and Jacobi’s formula

\partial\log\det Q_{m+1}=\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Q_{m+1}^{-1}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\right).

Thus Stokes’ theorem and the Gauduchon condition

\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0
yield

\int_{M}\partial\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}Q_{m+1}-\log\det Q_{m+1}\right)\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0.

Combining this with (
2.18
), we obtain

(2.20)

\left(K^{h_{m}}-K^{h_{m+1}},K^{h_{m+1}}-\lambda_{E}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)_{g,h_{m}}=\mu\int_{M}\left|Q_{m+1}^{-1/2}\cdot\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}\right|_{g,h_{m}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}\geq 0.

This proves (
2.14
). If the equality in (
2.14
) holds, then
\partial^{h_{m}}Q_{m+1}=0
and so
K^{h_{m}}=K^{h_{m+1}}
. By (
2.4
), one has

(2.21)

K^{h_{m+1}}-K^{h_{m}}=\mu(Q_{m+1}-Q_{m}).

In particular, for all
m\geq 1
,

(2.22)

\|Q_{m+1}-Q_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{m})}=\mu^{-2}\|K^{h_{m+1}}-K^{h_{m}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{m})}\leq\mu^{-2}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{m}-{\mathcal{E}}_{m+1}\right).

Since the non-negative sequence
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{m}\}
is non-increasing, one obtains (
2.8
) and this completes the proof.
∎

For
m\geq 0
, we set

(2.23)

F_{m}:=\int_{M}\log\det H_{m}\,\omega_{g}^{n},

and

(2.24)

c_{m}:=\exp\left(-\frac{F_{m}}{r\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}\right),\qquad\widehat{h}_{m}:=c_{m}h_{m},\qquad\widehat{H}_{m}:=c_{m}H_{m}.

It is clear that

(2.25)

\int_{M}\log\det\widehat{H}_{m}\,\omega_{g}^{n}=0.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

The sequence
\{F_{m}\}
is non-decreasing, and
F_{m}\geq 0
.

(2)

There exists a positive constant
C=C(h_{0},h_{1},\lambda_{E},\mu)
such that for
m\geq 1

(2.26)

C^{-1}c_{m-1}\leq c_{m}\leq c_{m-1}.

Proof.

Since
Q_{m}=H_{m-1}H_{m}^{-1}
,

(2.27)

F_{m}-F_{m-1}=-\int_{M}\log\det Q_{m}\,\omega_{g}^{n}.

Taking the trace of (
2.4
) and integrating, we obtain

(2.28)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}Q_{m}\,\omega_{g}^{n}=r\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Since
\log
is concave, Jensen’s inequality together with (
2.28
)
gives

(2.29)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\log\det Q_{m}\,\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r\int_{M}\log\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{E}Q_{m}}{r}\,\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(r\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}\right)\cdot\log\left(\frac{1}{r\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}Q_{m}\,\omega_{g}^{n}\right)=0.

Hence
F_{m}\geq F_{m-1}
, and
F_{0}=0
. This implies (1). On the other hand,

(2.30)

\frac{c_{m}}{c_{m-1}}=\exp\left(-\frac{F_{m}-F_{m-1}}{r\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}\right)=\exp\left(\frac{\int_{M}\log\det Q_{m}\,\omega_{g}^{n}}{r\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}\right).

By (
2.6
), we obtain
(2)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For positive-definite
r\times r
Hermitian matrices
A,B
and
0<\sigma\leq 1
, define

(2.31)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma}(A,B)

\displaystyle:=\sigma\mathrm{tr}\left(AB^{\sigma-1}-B^{\sigma}\right)-\mathrm{tr}\left(A^{\sigma}-B^{\sigma}\right),

(2.32)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{\sigma}(A,B)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{tr}\left(A^{1+\sigma}B^{-1}+B^{\sigma}-A^{\sigma}-AB^{\sigma-1}\right).

Then

(2.33)

0\leq{\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma}(A,B)\leq(1-\sigma){\mathcal{R}}_{\sigma}(A,B).

Proof.

Choose unitary diagonalizations

A=U\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r})U^{*},\qquad B=V\operatorname{diag}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{r})V^{*},

and put
W=U^{*}V=(w_{ik})
. Direct calculation gives

(2.34)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma}(A,B)={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,k}|w_{ik}|^{2}b_{k}^{\sigma}\left[\sigma\left(\frac{a_{i}}{b_{k}}-1\right)-\left(\left(\frac{a_{i}}{b_{k}}\right)^{\sigma}-1\right)\right],

(2.35)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{\sigma}(A,B)={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,k}|w_{ik}|^{2}b_{k}^{\sigma}\left(\frac{a_{i}}{b_{k}}-1\right)\left(\left(\frac{a_{i}}{b_{k}}\right)^{\sigma}-1\right).

It is therefore enough to prove, for every
x>0
,

(2.36)

0\leq\sigma(x-1)-(x^{\sigma}-1)\leq(1-\sigma)(x-1)(x^{\sigma}-1).

The first inequality follows from the concavity of
x^{\sigma}
. For the second one, one has

(2.37)

(1-\sigma)(x-1)(x^{\sigma}-1)-\left[\sigma(x-1)-(x^{\sigma}-1)\right]=x^{\sigma}\left((1-\sigma)x+\sigma-x^{1-\sigma}\right)\geq 0,

because
x^{1-\sigma}\leq(1-\sigma)x+\sigma
.
∎

We use the normalization:

(2.38)

\Lambda_{m}:=\sup_{M}\lambda_{\max}(H_{m}),\qquad\widetilde{H}_{m}:=\Lambda_{m}^{-1}H_{m}.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
m_{i}\to\infty
satisfy

(2.39)

\Lambda_{m_{i}}=\max_{0\leq j\leq m_{i}}\Lambda_{j}.

Then for every
0<\sigma\leq 1
,

(2.40)

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(Q_{m_{i}}-\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\cdot\widetilde{H}_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\leq 0.

Moreover,

(2.41)

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\left[\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{m_{i}}}\cdot\widetilde{H}_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}-\lambda_{E}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\widetilde{H}_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\right]\leq 0.

Proof.

Fix
\sigma\in(0,1]
. For
m\geq 1
, we set

(2.42)

T_{m}:=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left((Q_{m}-\mathrm{Id}_{E})\cdot\widetilde{H}_{m}^{\sigma}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

Then (
2.40
) is equivalent to

(2.43)

\limsup_{i\to\infty}T_{m_{i}}\leq 0.

Define

(2.44)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{m}

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(Q_{m}-\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\cdot H_{m}^{\sigma}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},\qquad m\geq 1,

(2.45)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{m}

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{m}^{\sigma}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},\qquad m\geq 0,

(2.46)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m}

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(Q_{m+1}-Q_{m}\right)\cdot H_{m}^{\sigma}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},\qquad m\geq 1.

For
m\geq 1
, we set

(2.47)

{\mathcal{D}}_{m}:=\sigma{\mathcal{A}}_{m}-({\mathcal{B}}_{m-1}-{\mathcal{B}}_{m}).

Since both
H_{m}
and
H_{m-1}
are self-adjoint with respect to
h_{0}
, we apply Lemma
2.3
with
A=H_{m-1}
and
B=H_{m}
. This yields

(2.48)

0\leq{\mathcal{D}}_{m}\leq(1-\sigma){\mathcal{R}}_{m},\qquad m\geq 1,

where

{\mathcal{R}}_{m}:=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{m-1}^{1+\sigma}\cdot H_{m}^{-1}+H_{m}^{\sigma}-H_{m-1}^{\sigma}-H_{m-1}\cdot H_{m}^{\sigma-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\geq 0.

For
m\geq 2
, a direct calculation gives

(2.49)

{\mathcal{R}}_{m}={\mathcal{A}}_{m-1}-{\mathcal{A}}_{m}+\varepsilon_{m-1}.

Hence, for
m\geq 2
,

(2.50)

{\mathcal{A}}_{m-1}\geq{\mathcal{A}}_{m}-|\varepsilon_{m-1}|.

We claim that there exists a constant

C_{1}=C_{1}(\sigma,\omega_{g},h_{0},h_{1},\lambda_{E},\mu)>0
such that, whenever

2\leq p\leq q
and

\Lambda_{q}=\max_{0\leq j\leq q}\Lambda_{j}
,

(2.51)

\sum_{m=p}^{q}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\leq C_{1}\Lambda_{q}^{\sigma}\left(1+\sum_{j=p-1}^{q-1}\|Q_{j+1}-Q_{j}\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{j})}\right).

Indeed, for all
j\leq q
,
H_{j}\leq\Lambda_{q}\mathrm{Id}_{E}
, and

(2.52)

0\leq H_{j}^{\sigma}\leq\Lambda_{q}^{\sigma}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E}

with respect to
h_{j}
.
Together with the estimate (
2.6
) in Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that there exists a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(\omega_{g},h_{0},h_{1},\lambda_{E},\mu)>0
such that for all
j\leq q
,

(2.53)

|{\mathcal{A}}_{j}|+{\mathcal{B}}_{j}\leq C_{2}\Lambda_{q}^{\sigma},\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\varepsilon_{j}|\leq C_{2}\|Q_{j+1}-Q_{j}\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{j})}\Lambda_{q}^{\sigma}.

By (
2.47
), (
2.48
) and (
2.49
), we have

(2.54)

\displaystyle\sigma\sum_{m=p}^{q}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{p-1}-{\mathcal{B}}_{q}+\sum_{m=p}^{q}{\mathcal{D}}_{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{p-1}+(1-\sigma)\left({\mathcal{A}}_{p-1}-{\mathcal{A}}_{q}+\sum_{m=p}^{q}\varepsilon_{m-1}\right).

Substituting (
2.53
) into (
2.54
) and dividing both sides by
\sigma
, this yields a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\sigma,\omega_{g},h_{0},h_{1},\lambda_{E},\mu)>0
such that (
2.51
) holds.

Suppose for contradiction that (
2.43
) fails.
Since
T_{m}=\Lambda_{m}^{-\sigma}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
, the failure of
(
2.43
) implies that, after possibly passing to a
subsequence, there exists
\delta>0
such that

(2.55)

{\mathcal{A}}_{m_{i}}\geq\delta\Lambda_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}.

Define

(2.56)

C_{3}:=C_{1}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\delta}{2C_{2}}\right).

Choose
\rho>0

sufficiently small so that

(2.57)

\frac{\delta s_{\rho}}{2}>C_{3}\quad\mbox{where}\quad s_{\rho}:=\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{2C_{2}\rho}\right\rfloor.

By (
2.8
) in
Lemma
2.1
, we can choose
N_{\rho}\geq 1
such
that for all
m\geq N_{\rho}
,

(2.58)

\|Q_{m+1}-Q_{m}\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{m})}\leq\rho.

Fix a sufficiently large
i
so that

m_{i}-s_{\rho}\geq N_{\rho}
. Choose an integer
m
with

m_{i}-s_{\rho}+1\leq m\leq m_{i}.

For every
j\in\{m,\cdots,m_{i}-1\}
, since

\Lambda_{m_{i}}=\max_{0\leq\ell\leq m_{i}}\Lambda_{\ell}
,
(
2.53
) and (
2.58
) give

(2.59)

|\varepsilon_{j}|\leq C_{2}\|Q_{j+1}-Q_{j}\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{j})}\Lambda_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}\leq C_{2}\rho\Lambda_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}.

Combining this with (
2.50
), we obtain

(2.60)

{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\geq{\mathcal{A}}_{m_{i}}-\sum_{j=m}^{m_{i}-1}|\varepsilon_{j}|\geq\delta\Lambda_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}-s_{\rho}C_{2}\rho\,\Lambda_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}\geq\frac{\delta}{2}\Lambda_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}

where the last inequality follows from

s_{\rho}C_{2}\rho\leq\delta/2
.
Since
m
is arbitrary in this range,

(2.61)

\sum_{m=m_{i}-s_{\rho}+1}^{m_{i}}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\geq\frac{\delta s_{\rho}}{2}\Lambda_{m_{i}}^{\sigma}.

On the other hand, applying (
2.51
) with

p=m_{i}-s_{\rho}+1
and
q=m_{i}
