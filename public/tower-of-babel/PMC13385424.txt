Resident mesenchymal progenitor cells require autocrine IGF-I in homeostatic and regenerating skeletal muscle
Authors: Yangyi E Luo, Zoe Abe-Teh, Tarek Y Alsaghir, Li-Ying Kuo, Fahong Yu, Bradley E Stoker, Yumei Zhou, Ambili Bai Appu, Elijah M Dy, Feng Yue, Daniel Kopinke, Elisabeth R Barton
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryFibro-adipogenic progenitors (FAPs) are muscle-resident mesenchymal stromal cells essential for homeostasis and regeneration but can produce fibrosis or intramuscular fat in pathological conditions. Insulin-like growth factor-I (IGF-I) regulates regeneration through actions on muscle fibers, satellite cells, macrophages, and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling but has multiple sources. To assess the role of FAP-derived IGF-I, we generated inducible FAP-specific Igf1-deficient (FID) mice. Following BaCl2 injury, FID muscles displayed impaired regeneration, with smaller fibers, fewer Pax7+ and MyoD+ cells, increased CD68+ macrophages, decreased collagen, and suppressed FAP proliferation. After glycerol-induced injury, FID muscles had reduced fat. Primary FID FAPs displayed blunted proliferation, upregulated immune-regulatory genes, and downregulated ECM and growth genes, with delayed fibrogenic and adipogenic differentiation. scRNA-seq of homeostatic muscle revealed reduced protein translation and ECM indices alongside increased senescence markers in FID samples. Taken together, FAP IGF-I is critical for FAP function, with direct and indirect impact on muscle regeneration.Keywords: fibro-adipogenic progenitors, insulin-like growth factor I, mesenchymal progenitor cells, PDGFRα, skeletal muscle regeneration, acute injury, adipogenesis, fibrosis, senescence

Introduction

Skeletal muscle comprises 40% of human body mass and has extraordinary regenerative capacity. Muscle regeneration is a period of dynamic remodeling that requires coordinated interaction among many cell types, including satellite cells (SCs), immune cells, and fibro-adipogenic progenitor cells (FAPs), to restore functional muscle fibers supported by a healthy extracellular matrix (ECM) (Caballero-Sanchez et al., 2024; Kuang et al., 2007; Relaix et al., 2005). FAPs are PDGFRɑ+ mesenchymal progenitor cells that reside within muscle interstitium; they proliferate upon acute injury to establish the scaffold for nascent fibers and are required for skeletal muscle regeneration and homeostasis (Collins and Kardon, 2021; Joe et al., 2010; Wosczyna et al., 2019). Skeletal muscle lacking FAPs has progressive muscle loss and delayed regeneration (Wosczyna et al., 2019). FAPs can also persist in chronic injury associated with disease, resulting in excessive fibrosis and intramuscular fat deposition (Kopinke et al., 2017; Lemos et al., 2015; Uezumi et al., 2010). Hence, FAPs create a therapeutic challenge to identify strategies where they support efficient regenerative capacity without fibro-fatty accumulation.

In addition to multiple cell types, many secreted proteins coordinate muscle regeneration. One such protein is insulin-like growth factor-I (IGF-I), which facilitates skeletal muscle hypertrophy and regeneration (Barton-Davis et al., 1998; Musaro et al., 2001; Vassilakos et al., 2019). IGF-I from muscle fibers is important for post-natal growth and functional recovery from disuse atrophy (Spradlin et al., 2021; Vassilakos et al., 2019). However, other IGF-I sources also have impact on muscle regenerative capacity, including myeloid cell IGF-I that controls macrophage polarization (Tonkin et al., 2015). Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) databases implicate FAPs as having one of the highest Igf1 expression per cell in muscle regeneration, yet the biological relevance of IGF-I from FAPs is not known (Kok and Barton, 2021). Thus, it is important to determine if IGF-I from FAPs contributes to FAP autocrine signaling or FAP paracrine effects in homeostatic and regenerating muscle (Kim et al., 2022; Uezumi et al., 2010; Wosczyna et al., 2019).

In this study, we hypothesized that IGF-I produced by FAPs regulates muscle regeneration and FAP function. We examined the role of FAP-derived IGF-I on skeletal muscle regeneration and FAP properties using a mouse model with FAP-specific Igf1 deletion. Our results provide multiple lines of evidence that IGF-I from FAPs is a key autocrine regulator during muscle regeneration. Consequently, FAP IGF-I deletion disrupts FAP interactions with other muscle cell populations. Overall, FAP produces an important IGF-I source for muscle.

Results

Deletion of Igf1 in PGDFRα+ mesenchymal progenitor cells in skeletal muscle moderately reduces IGF-I pool

FAPs are marked by the surface marker PDGFRɑ (Contreras et al., 2019; Kopinke et al., 2017; Uezumi et al., 2010; Wosczyna et al., 2019). To understand the significance of IGF-I from FAPs, we generated a conditional FAP-specific knockout mouse line named FAP-IGF-I-deficient (FID, PdgfrαCre-ERT2/+, Igf1fl/fl, Rosa26EYFP/+) (Figure 1A) (Chung et al., 2018; Spradlin et al., 2021; Srinivas et al., 2001). Since one PdgfrɑCRE−ERT2 allele showed no phenotype here and in previous studies (Figures S1A–S1D) (Norris et al., 2025; Uezumi et al., 2021), Igf1fl/fl (Pdgfrα+/+, Igf1fl/fl) served as controls (Figure 1A).

PDGFRɑ immunostaining colocalized with the EYFP reporter post-induction, a pattern replicated in primary FAPs isolated 3 days post-injury (3 dpi) (Figures 1B and S1E), confirming efficient and FAP-specific Cre recombination. Exon 4 deletion was assessed on primary FAPs by endpoint reverse-transcription PCR (RT-PCR) using primers flanking exon 4 (P1 and P4) (Vassilakos et al., 2019); only FID FAPs produced the smaller amplified band (Figure 1C). Quantitative reverse-transcription PCR (RT-qPCR) targeting exons 3 and 4 (P1 and P3), normalized to unaffected exons 6 and 3′UTR (P2 and P5), showed ≥80% of Igf1 transcripts lacked exon 4 in cultured FID FAPs (Figure 1D). Whole tissue expression and protein were also examined in homeostasis and regeneration (Figure 1E), considering multiple IGF-I sources in skeletal muscle (Kok and Barton, 2021). Endpoint RT-PCR confirmed exon 4 excision only in FID muscles (Figure 1F) with a 20%–45% reduction in functional Igf1 transcripts throughout regeneration (Figure 1G). IGF-I protein levels were not different in non-damaged (ND) muscles or in the early burst of IGF-I levels at 3 dpi, but were >40% lower at 5 and 7 dpi, and 15% lower at 14 dpi, significant for both time point and strain by two-way ANOVA (Figure 1H). Despite efficient recombination in FAPs, Igf1 transcription, using primers targeting exon 6 and 3′-UTR shared by all Igf1 isoforms, was not altered by the ablation of FAP Igf1 and retained similar dynamics as controls (Figure 1I). In homeostasis, minimal differences in FID and Igf1fl/fl body or tibialis anterior (TA) muscle masses were found even out to 18 months of age (Figures S1F–S1I and S2A–S2F), supporting that FAP IGF-I does not affect homeostatic muscle or that other sources compensate.

IGF-I from FAPs is required for muscle regeneration

FAPs are essential for muscle regeneration (Wosczyna et al., 2019). To determine if FAP IGF-I was needed for this process, we examined muscles from mice following BaCl2-induced injury (Figure 2A). While no differences in muscle mass or fiber size were observed in ND muscles, regenerating muscles had significant FID-dependent deficits in recovery, evident for relative muscle mass and fiber size at 7 dpi and 28 dpi in both sexes compared to controls (Figures 2B–2H, S1C, S1D, S1J, and S1K). Thus, FAP IGF-I contributes to the regenerative process.

Muscle regeneration requires precise coordination between SCs and FAPs (Kim et al., 2022; Uezumi et al., 2010; Wosczyna et al., 2019). Reduced numbers of Pax7+ cells were evident in FID muscle sections at 5 dpi when SCs are most abundant (Figures 2I and 2J), suggesting that FAP IGF-I supports SC expansion following injury. As SC differentiation into MyoD+/Pax7− myoblasts is a critical lineage transition during regeneration (Bentzinger et al., 2013), primary SCs were isolated at 5 dpi and stained for Pax7 and MyoD after attachment to Matrigel-coated plates. FID SCs had a proportional increase in MyoD+/Pax7+ cells and decrease in MyoD+/Pax7− cells compared to controls (Figures 2K–2L), indicating altered SC dynamics that may impair regenerative efficiency. To evaluate fiber formation, muscle sections were immunostained for embryonic myosin heavy chain (EmbMHC) which marks newly formed fibers (Agarwal et al., 2020). At 3 dpi, EmbMHC+ fibers were sparse in both strains (Figures 2M and 2N). By 5 dpi, EmbMHC+ fiber presence increased dramatically in both strains but was significantly reduced in FID muscles.

To determine if regeneration deficits in FID muscles could be corrected by FAP replenishment, 75,000 HRP+ control FAPs, GFP+ FID FAPs, or PBS were injected into FID TA muscles 2 dpi and assessed 28 dpi, with PBS or no injection into regenerating Igf1fl/fl muscles serving as controls. Similar numbers of transplanted control and FID FAPs were found at 28 dpi, when normalized to microscopic field, such that the proportions of transplanted FAPs between strains did not differ (Figures 2O and 2P). However, FID FAP transplantation resulted in smaller fiber cross-sectional areas (CSA) compared to contralateral muscles (Figure 2Q), suggesting that FID FAPs may have an actively negative effect on regenerative capacity. As cell transplantation and regeneration alter inflammatory cell populations, macrophage presence was assessed at 28 dpi by CD68 immunostaining, which marks myeloid cells (Chistiakov et al., 2017). Transplantation of control FAPs significantly reduced CD68+ cells in regeneration FID muscles, whereas transplantation of FID FAPs significantly increased the population of CD68+ cells when normalized to microscopic field (Figures 2O and 2R). Hence, the impaired regeneration following FID FAP transplantation may also be due to persistence of inflammatory cells. Collectively, these measures demonstrate an overall regeneration delay when IGF-I from FAPs is absent, with potentially direct and indirect effects.

FAP proliferation requires an autocrine source of IGF-I

FAPs express both Igf1 and IGF-I receptor (Igf1r) in developing, regenerating, and aging skeletal muscle (Kok and Barton, 2021), so they likely can respond to IGF-I regardless of its source. However, IGF-I from FAPs may be an important autocrine source. We first examined early phases of regeneration when FAPs proliferate (Figure 3A) (Joe et al., 2010). FID FAPs failed to expand compared to controls, showing fewer PDGFRα+ cells 3–7 dpi (Figures 3B, 3C, and S3A). Sections were immunostained for Ki67 and PDGFRα, revealing a ∼30% decrease in Ki67+ FID FAPs at 5 dpi (Figures 3D and 3E). Primary FAPs were then isolated from ND, 3 and 5 dpi muscles, and stained for Ki67 immediately after attachment (Figure 3F). Although similar total mononuclear cells were collected, fewer FAPs were recovered from FID muscles (Figure S3B). Igf1fl/fl FAP proliferation peaked at 3 dpi, yet FID Ki67+ FAPs were low throughout (Figures 3G and 3H). Next, primary FAPs isolated at 3 dpi were plated at similar density to monitor growth in vitro. By 1 week, FAPs isolated from Igf1fl/fl muscles expanded 7-fold, while FID FAPs only doubled (Figures 3I and 3J). To determine if the IGF-I pathway was intact, FAPs were treated with rIGF-I and/or the IGF-IR inhibitor AEW541. Akt phosphorylation in FID FAPs was increased by rIGF-I but ERK1/2 phosphorylation was not, even though there were no differences in total AKT or ERK1/2; further, FID FAP proliferation was not restored (Figures 3K–3N and S3C). As MAPK signaling is generally linked to mitogenic actions, this suggests that the block of FID FAP proliferation is due to impairments in the MAPK arm of the IGF-IR pathway.

To determine the emergence of the FID phenotype, we isolated primary FAPs from both strains and performed in vitro recombination using 4-hydroxytamoxifen (4OHTAM) for 4 days to achieve efficient deletion (Figure S7). Exogenous IGF-I was provided 24 h prior to termination at each time point. IGF-I restored cell proliferation at days 2 and 4; however, it failed to restore proliferation in FID FAPs 7 days after recombination (D11) (Figures 3O–3P). Taken together, FAP IGF-I is indispensable for proliferation, and prolonged loss of FAP IGF-I results in FAP cell proliferation impairments.

FID FAP gene expression indicates low proliferation and ECM production and a heightened immune signature

To expand our understanding of the impact IGF-I loss has on FAPs, we used RNA-seq of primary FAPs isolated from regenerating muscles at 3 dpi (Figures 4A and S4A). A total of 20,937 genes were identified, and with thresholds set to 2-fold changes with p < 0.05, we detected 427 upregulated and 179 downregulated genes in FID FAPs vs. Igf1fl/fl FAPs (Figure 4B). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis revealed that the highest scores for upregulated genes were in immune-related pathways, suggesting that FAPs from FID mice adopted an inflammatory phenotype (Figures 4C, 4D, and S4C). Due to increase in immunoregulatory-related genes, we examined sections from regenerating muscle for macrophage presence, expanding upon the analysis after transplantation (Figure 2R). CD68+ cells were evident in muscles at 7 dpi from both FID and Igf1fl/fl muscles; however, they persisted in FID muscles through 28 dpi in contrast to controls (Figures 4E and 4F). This finding suggests that the endogenous crosstalk between FAPs and macrophages to resolve damage was also disrupted with the loss of IGF-I from FAPs.

Out of the 179 genes that were downregulated, most of them clustered into ECM GO terms (Figure 4C), and heatmaps displayed a global shutdown of ECM gene expression in FID FAPs, including multiple collagen genes, fibronectin (Fn1), and laminin 2 (Lama2) (Figures 4D and S4B). Downregulation of Klf4 was also evident in FID FAPs, a key regulator in fibrogenesis and proliferation (Figure 4D) (Chandran et al., 2021). These changes were accompanied by reductions in many genes promoting proliferation and IGF-I signaling, consistent with our in vivo and in vitro measurements but without major differences in MAP-ERK signaling (Figures 4D and S4C). Because FAPs can adopt fibrogenic or adipogenic lineages (Joe et al., 2010; Uezumi et al., 2010), we examined genes regulating FAP fate. There were no consistent patterns in FAP regulatory or adipogenic genes in FID FAPs, indicating an absence of trans-differentiation (Figures 4G and S4C). Igf1 was also evaluated using primers P2 and P5, which was not statistically different between strains with this primer pair (Qval = 0.33). Overall, the FID FAP gene expression profile exhibited deficits in multiple pathways needed for efficient regeneration.

Loss of FAP IGF-I suppresses ECM deposition and slows fibroblast and adipogenic differentiation

During muscle regeneration, FAPs deposit ECM as a scaffold for fiber formation. When FAPs are depleted, ECM content is reduced (Kotsaris et al., 2023; Lemos et al., 2015). RNA-seq of FID FAPs prompted assessment of collagen deposition via Picro-Sirius red staining. The transient collagen increase seen in Igf1fl/fl muscles was absent in FID muscles (Figures 5A, 5B, and S5A), consistent with suppressed ECM gene expression (Figure 4D), though this could reflect reduced FAP numbers. To test fibroblast differentiation, primary FAPs were stimulated with TGF-β (5 ng/mL) at matched confluence (Figure 5C) (Contreras et al., 2019). Collagens (Col1a4, Col4a1) were similarly upregulated at 24 h, indicating intact differentiation, whereas fibronectin (Fn1) remained low in FID FAPs (Figure 5D) (Jones et al., 2019). Immunoblotting showed comparable Smad2 phosphorylation (Figures 5E and S5), but αSMA induction was delayed in FID FAPs and PDGFRα downregulation was slower, suggesting retention of the progenitor state (Figures 5E, 5F, and S5). No perilipin accumulation was observed, excluding adipogenic trans-differentiation (Figure 5E).

As FAPs are primary also adipocyte progenitors (Kopinke et al., 2017; Uezumi et al., 2010), we tested adipogenic differentiation using 50% glycerol injections in female mice, which have greater adipogenic potential (Kopinke et al., 2017). By 7 dpi, perilipin+ adipocytes appeared, and by 21 dpi, fat accumulation was higher in Igf1fl/fl than FID muscles (Figure 5G). Minimal differences in muscle mass were observed (Figures S5A and S5B), suggesting loss of FAP-derived IGF-I suppresses adipocyte accumulation without affecting myogenic regeneration. To examine adipogenesis further, primary FAPs from both strains were cultured to 95%–100% confluency and treated with adipogenic induction media (0.25 μM dexamethasone, 0.5 mM IBMX, 5 μg/mL insulin, 5 μM troglitazone). Lipid droplets accumulated in Igf1fl/fl FAPs by 1 day post-induction, whereas in FID FAPs this occurred at 5 days (Figures S5D and S5E). GFP lineage tracing confirmed that differentiation was delayed in all FID GFP+ FAPs. By 7 days, ∼70% of Igf1fl/fl FAPs were oil red O+, while far fewer FID cells stained positive (Figure 5H). Although IGF-I can promote adipogenesis in mesenchymal stem cells (Scavo et al., 2004; Smith et al., 1988), exogenous 10 nM IGF-I failed to rescue differentiation in FID FAPs (Figure 5H).

Evaluation of key markers of adipogenesis was performed early in induction. Delays were evident, with lower expression of adiponectin (Adipoq) and Pparg at 3 dpi in FID FAPs while the fibrogenic marker Fn1 remained unchanged (Figure 5I). Immunoblotting at 3 dpi showed robust perilipin levels in Igf1fl/fl FAPs but not in FID FAPs, with trends of higher FID PDGFRα and comparable decreases in αSMA (Figures 5J and 5K). These findings indicate that FID FAPs need autocrine IGF-I for efficient adipogenesis and fibrogenesis.

Single cell expression analysis reveals loss of fibrotic FAPs and adoption of a senescent-like phenotype in FID FAPs

To examine the molecular signature of homeostatic FAPs without IGF-I, scRNA-seq was performed on TA muscles 21 days post-TAM induction, enriching for mononuclear cell types by eliminating muscle fiber nuclei. Approximately 20,000 cells per strain were assessed (∼40,000 reads/cell). Uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) clustering identified 9 cell populations based on unique markers (Figures S7A–S7C) (Oprescu et al., 2020b). FID samples had reduced proportions of FAPs, fibroblasts, monocytes, and a modest decrease in SCs; the proportional increase in endothelial cells was likely the result of the diminished populations other cell types (Figure S7D). FAPs and fibroblasts had the most pronounced Igf1 expression in the cells, and loss of FAP-derived IGF-I did not induce compensatory Igf1 expression in other cell types (Figures S7B and S7C).

FAP sub-clustering revealed four populations: fibrotic FAPs (high collagen), progenitor FAPs (Dpp IV+), activated FAPs (Sox6+), and microenvironmental FAPs (critical ECM genes) (Figure 6A, S7F-G) (Babaeijandaghi et al., 2023; Krebsbach and Villa-Diaz, 2017). Fibrotic FAPs were most affected in number and gene expression, consistent with global collagen reduction (Figures 6A, 4C, and 4D). Top 20 DE genes showed elevated Thbs1 and p53, which promote cell-cycle arrest and senescence (Figures 6B and 6C) (Lawler et al., 2001; Sundaram et al., 2011). Downregulated genes included proliferation and ribosomal genes (Cdk8, Rps28, Rps29, and Rpl29) (Figure 6C) (Kim et al., 2019; Kirn-Safran et al., 2007; Szilagyi and Gustafsson, 2013). GO and pathway analysis highlighted ECM organization, p53 regulation, and protein translation as the most affected processes (Figures 6D and 6E).

These results suggest that loss of FAP-derived IGF-I shifts FAPs toward an inactive, potentially senescent state. FID FAPs displayed transcriptional profiles resembling some aspects of senescent FAPs (Moiseeva et al., 2023) with elevated inflammatory signaling and reduced proliferation markers (Figures 4 and 6). In homeostatic muscle, 22% of FID FAPs were PDGFRα+/β-Gal+ versus 5% in Igf1fl/fl FAPs (Figures 7A and 7B). Following BaCl2 injury, >60% of FAPs lacking IGF-I were β-Gal+ at 7 dpi versus 20% in controls, coinciding with minimal FAP expansion. By 28 dpi, total FAP numbers normalized, but β-Gal+ proportions in FID muscles remained >60% (Figures 7A and 7B). Cultured FID FAPs also had elevated β-Gal but only modestly increased p53 (Figures 7C, 7D, and S7B) (Ben-Porath and Weinberg, 2004; Moiseeva et al., 2023; Saito et al., 2020). In complementary experiments, primary FAPs were examined for β-Gal during in vitro 4-OH-TAM treatment (Figure S7A). By day 11, FID FAPs were ∼80% β-Gal+ (Figures 7E and 7F). Continuous IGF-I treatment during the 11-day period normalized β-Gal+ staining, but acute 24 Hr IGF-I was ineffective (Figures 7E and 7F). Because FID FAPs have lower proliferation than Igf1fl/fl FAPs (Figures 3M–3P), this may have affected which cells were present in the endpoint and their β-Gal staining, but tracing of the cell populations was not pursued. Collectively, FAPs from FID muscles exhibit senescent-like features both in vitro and in vivo under homeostatic conditions, which may underly the lack of FID FAP participation in muscle regeneration.

Discussion

Research in the past decade has established that FAPs play a critical role in muscle homeostasis, regeneration, aging, and even neuromuscular junction integrity (Joe et al., 2010; Kim et al., 2022; Lukjanenko et al., 2019; Uezumi et al., 2021; Wosczyna et al., 2019). FAPs also secrete IGF-I, which is a well-documented factor contributing to skeletal muscle biology (Barton-Davis et al., 1998; Kok and Barton, 2021; Musaro et al., 2001; Vassilakos et al., 2019). Both the sources and timing of IGF-I production are central to skeletal muscle growth and regeneration. This motivated the central questions of this study, to determine if FAP IGF-I was needed for efficient muscle regeneration in general, and if FAP actions were specifically affected. Our results support that IGF-I from FAPs plays a direct autocrine role for FAPs, removing them from effectively participating in the regeneration process, mirroring a phenotype associated with the complete ablation of FAPs (Wosczyna et al., 2019).

Several other pathways have been identified that alter FAP fate. For example, Odd-skipped related transcription factor 1 (Osr1) is an important regulator of FAP activity during muscle regeneration. Loss of Osr1 impedes FAP proliferation, enhances FAP apoptosis, impairs immune interactions, and upregulates ECM-related genes (Kotsaris et al., 2023). We have delineated a contrasting phenotype with reduced ECM production and enhanced immune pathway activity, with modest downregulation in Osr1 expression. This suggests an independent pathway, potentially acting in opposition to Osr1-regulated mechanisms.

Upon activation following acute injury, FAPs develop primary cilia, a crucial event for FAP function in muscle tissue, as cilia-deficient FAPs exhibit resistance to adipogenic differentiation, in part, through the disruption of Hedgehog signaling (Kopinke et al., 2017). The expression of two primary cilia formation genes, Arl13b and Ift88, remained unaffected in FID FAPs, suggesting that IGF-I does not alter primary cilia. IGF1R is localized on the primary cilia of mesenchymal stem cells (Dalbay et al., 2015), and it remains unclear if signaling crosstalk between IGF-I and other pathways is disrupted, such as Hedgehog signaling, enriched in primary cilia. We observed reduced regenerative capacity in FID muscles unlike enhanced myogenesis found in the absence of primary cilia (Kopinke et al., 2017), suggesting that primary cilia formation is intact, or that the impact of IGF-I loss from FAPs overrides the potential benefit of primary cilia loss.

The senescent-like hallmarks of FID FAPs in vivo and in vitro were surprising. While a transient increase of senescent cells has been documented in muscle regeneration, increases in senescent cell abundance are apparent in aging and diseased muscles, and impair efficient regeneration (Moiseeva et al., 2023). Senescence can occur in any of the cell types involved in regeneration; however, in our model, few, if any, cells other than FAPs displayed these hallmarks. Delayed muscle regeneration by senescent cells is caused in part by their senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) (Basisty et al., 2020), a host of secreted factors that are pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic. FID FAPs displayed some features of senescent cells, including reduction in genes for cell proliferation and enhancement of genes for inflammation (Moiseeva et al., 2023), but they lacked upregulation of ECM related genes. The reduction of ECM remodeling and deposition may prevent normal fiber formation during regeneration, as physical and biochemical cues from the matrix would be lost. Alternatively, this may be an advantage in instances of excess fibrosis, as the “quasi”-senescent FAPs could not produce increased ECM. Understanding the balance between the beneficial and detrimental consequences of FAPs lacking autocrine IGF-I actions is likely context dependent, and worthy of future investigation.

A larger question is how consequences to muscle homeostasis and regeneration differ between the FID model, where FAPs lack IGF-I production, and the complete ablation of FAPs as previously studied (Uezumi et al., 2021; Wosczyna et al., 2019). The models share regeneration defects, with loss of FAPs having slower regenerative capacity and more negative impact on muscle SC than loss of FAP IGF-I. Our examination of regenerating muscle spanned the 4 week period in which FAPs are known to contribute to resolving damage, but we examined FAPs most thoroughly at the height of expansion, at 3 dpi, which may not reflect their properties throughout regeneration. The comparison of transplantation of healthy and FID FAPs reveal differential effects on their abilities to resolve damage in FID muscles, but compensation by other circulating factors may have also helped. FAPs isolated from FID muscles were still able to respond to fibrogenic and to adipogenic media, albeit more slowly, suggesting that the loss of IGF-I does not block all cytokine mediated communication to the FAPs. The contribution of other pathways may partially rescue IGF-I dependent deficits leading to a milder phenotype than when FAPs are completely absent.

In the current study, we focused on acute muscle regeneration in healthy young adult mice to afford a controlled environment for evaluating the contribution of IGF-I from FAPs on regenerative capacity. During the early stages of muscle regeneration, FAPs interact with SCs to promote myogenesis (Joe et al., 2010). Previous work has shown that FAPs secrete WNT1-inducible signaling pathway protein 1 (WISP1), which regulates SC proliferation by activating the Akt signaling pathway (Lukjanenko et al., 2019). Additionally, FAPs produce HGF that may activate quiescent SCs and/or regulate their basal proliferation (Allen et al., 1995; Kim et al., 2022). Although our RNA-seq data indicated that expression levels of Wisp1 and Hgf were unchanged in the absence of IGF-I, we did not examine the FAP secretome in this study to determine if release of these proteins was affected by the loss of FAP IGF-I. Alternatively, the reduction in SC number could theoretically result solely from a decline in FAP numbers, as FAP ablation also results in reduced SCs in regenerating muscle (Uezumi et al., 2021; Wosczyna et al., 2019). Although Pax7+ SCs are an important part of muscle development and regeneration (Kuang et al., 2007; Relaix et al., 2005), it also requires supporting FAPs for them to thrive in their environment (Joe et al., 2010; Kim et al., 2022; Lukjanenko et al., 2019). If SCs acquire deficiencies in their ability to proliferate or differentiate, the consequences will be compounded by the inability of IGF-I deficient FAPs to promote their activation via factors such as HGF (Kim et al., 2022). Similarly, a heightened inflammatory environment may go unchecked without the ability for FAPs to interact effectively with the macrophage population. However, the actions of polarized macrophages on FAPs to enhance fatty-fibrotic infiltration in diseased muscles (Liu et al., 2023) may also be disrupted by loss of IGF-I from FAPs. Future studies are warranted to determine the potential benefits or disadvantages of removing this source of IGF-I from skeletal muscle. Understanding how the interplay among these cell populations changes when FAPs are in a state of torpor will be important to pursue in light of the reduced ECM deposition in FID muscles, which may be beneficial in disease, but at the expense of reduced regenerative capacity, which may be detrimental. The tissue environment associated with genetic neuromuscular disease or aging may exacerbate the phenotypes we have observed in regenerating FID muscles.

Overall, this study provides multiple lines of evidence that IGF-I produced by FAPs in muscle is a required autocrine factor for FAP actions during muscle regeneration. The consequences of its loss alter FAPs to an extent that they are removed from performing their normal interactions with other cell populations in the muscle, ultimately stalling regenerative capacity. While IGF-I from FAPs may also have paracrine actions on neighboring cells, these potential direct actions cannot be resolved from those mediated indirectly via its required beneficial actions on FAPs themselves.

Experimental model and study participant details

Mouse models

All animal procedures followed protocols approved by the University of Florida IACUC. Mice were on a C57BL/6 background under a 12-h light/dark cycle with ad libitum access to food and water. FAP-Igf1-Deficient (FID) mice were made by crossing Pdgfra Cre-ERT2 mice (Chung et al., 2018) (JAX#032770) with Igf1fl/fl mice (Liu et al., 1998; Spradlin et al., 2021) (JAX#012663) and Rosa26EYFP mice (Srinivas et al., 2001) (JAX#006148). FID mice were homozygous for Igf1fl/fl and Rosa26EYFP, and hemizygous for PdgfraCre-ERT2; littermates lacking the Cre allele served as controls (Kang et al., 2010; Kopinke et al., 2017). FAP-HRP mice were generated by crossing PdgfraCre-ERT2 mice with Rosa26HRP-iPEEL mice (Luo et al., 2026; Shuster et al., 2022). Genotyping was performed with tail DNA and G2 Master Mix (Promega) followed by agarose gel electrophoresis. Cre recombination was induced by IP injections of tamoxifen (20 mg/mL in corn oil; 100 μg/g body weight) for 5 days in ∼12-week-old mice and both sexes are included in the majority of the experiments. Only females were used in glycerol injury since female exhibits greater adipogenic response (Norris et al., 2023; Norris et al., 2024, 2025). Experiments were performed ≥1 week after final injection.

Method details

Muscle injury and tissue processing

Acute muscle injury was induced by injecting 50 μL PBS containing 1.2% BaCl2 into the tibialis anterior (TA) muscle. Adipogenic injury was induced by 50 μL of 50% glycerol in PBS; only females were used due to their higher adipogenic response (Norris et al., 2024; Pisani et al., 2010). Tissues were collected 3–28 days post-injury (dpi) and weighed before processing. Muscles for histology were embedded in OCT and frozen in 2-methylbutane cooled by liquid nitrogen. Samples for biochemical analysis were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C.

Primary cell culture

Primary FAPs were isolated from TA muscles as previously described with minor modifications (Oprescu et al., 2020a). Dissected TA muscles were minced and digested with collagenase II and dispase for 1 h at 37°C on a rotator. Cell suspensions were cleared using Debris Removal Solution (Miltenyi Biotec) and counted (Countess 3). FAPs were purified using the PDGFRα Microbead Kit and MACS system (Miltenyi Biotec) per manufacturer’s protocol. Cells were plated at 30–35% confluency in proliferation medium (DMEM, 10% bovine calf serum, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, and 1% GlutaMAX), changed every 2–3 days. Primary FAPs were plated at 30–35% confluency in proliferation medium (DMEM, 10% bovine calf serum, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, and 1% GlutaMAX), changed every 2–3 days. Adipogenic differentiation was induced at 95–100% confluency with 5 μg/mL insulin or 10 nM IGF-I, 5 μM troglitazone, IBMX, and dexamethasone in media (Low et al., 2017). Fibrogenic differentiation was induced at 65–70% confluency with 5 ng/mL TGF-β(Contreras et al., 2019). Differentiation media were refreshed daily.

In vitro recombination was induced by 2 μM 4-hydroxytamoxifen for 4 days, with ethanol vehicle as control. Freshly isolated FAPs or SCs (Miltenyi Satellite Cell Isolation Kit) were also plated on 24-well or Matrigel-coated coverslips, allowed to adhere 4–6 h, then fixed for immunostaining.

FAP transplantation

Transplantations were performed in FID mice following bilateral BaCl2-induced injury to TA muscles. At 2 dpi, 75,000 freshly isolated control FAPs (HRP+, from FAP-HRP mice) or FID FAPs (GFP+, from FID mice) were resuspended in 50 μL PBS and injected into the left TA. The right TA received PBS only. At 28 dpi, TAs were collected, weighed, and processed for histology.

RNA-seq analysis

Total RNA was extracted from primary FAPs isolated from 3 dpi muscles. Sequencing reads generated by the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 were trimmed with Cutadapt (Martin, 2011) to remove adapters and bases with quality scores <20; reads <40 bp were excluded. Clean reads were aligned to the Mus musculus GRCm38 (mm10) reference genome using STAR v2.7.9a (Dobin et al., 2013). Mapped reads were processed with HTSeq v0.11.2 (Anders et al., 2015) samtools, and in-house scripts (UF ICBR) to remove PCR duplicates and count unique reads. Gene expression was analyzed using a DESeq2-based R pipeline (Love et al., 2014). Differentially expressed genes were defined by |log2FC| > 1 and adjusted p < 0.05 Volcano plots were generated in R (v4.1.3), and Bowtie2 v2.5.4 was used for gene and exon searches (Langmead and Salzberg, 2012).

Single Cell sample preparation and sequencing

Tissue dissociation followed a published protocol (Oprescu et al., 2020a). Ten TA muscles from five male mice per strain were minced in wash medium (F-10 with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin) and digested for 1 h at 37°C in wash medium containing Collagenase II, Dispase II, and 1% BSA. The suspension was filtered twice through 40 μm strainers, treated with RBC lysis buffer for 3 min, and stained with Propidium Iodide (Invitrogen) to exclude dead cells. Viable cells were sorted by FACS (UF ICBR Core), yielding ∼200,000 cells per sample. Single-cell libraries were prepared using the 10x Genomics Chromium GEM-X Single Cell 3′ v4 platform with a target recovery of 20,000 cells and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq X. Reads were aligned and annotated using Cell Ranger v9.0.0 on HiPerGator (UF Research Computing), yielding ∼40,000 reads per cell and ∼15,000 retained cells per sample. Downstream analyses were performed in Scanpy (Wolf et al., 2018). Gene expression matrices from control and FID samples were merged for joint analysis. Cells with <200 genes, >6,000 genes, or >5% mitochondrial content, and genes expressed in <3 cells, were excluded. Predicted doublets were removed using Scrublet (Wolock et al., 2019). Counts were normalized to 10,000 per cell, and batch effects were corrected with Harmony (Korsunsky et al., 2019). Dimensionality reduction was performed using PCA with 50 neighbors for downstream analyses.

DGE, cell clustering, pathway analysis, and cell-cell communication

Cell clusters were identified using differential gene expression (DEG) with the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (p < 0.0001) and annotated based on marker genes using Scanpy. FAP clusters were extracted, normalized to 10,000 counts per cell, and re-clustered using DEG analysis (Wilcoxon, p < 0.0001). Functional enrichment within FAPs was performed using Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) with GO m5 terms, and canonical pathway analysis was conducted using m2 pathways (version 2024.1) (Smith and Eppig, 2009).

Real-time PCR

Frozen tissues were pulverized, and total RNA was extracted using Trizol (Thermo Fisher). RNA from primary FAPs was isolated with the Qiagen RNeasy Micro Kit, per manufacturer protocol. RNA was DNase-treated, quantified, and checked for integrity. Equal amounts were reverse-transcribed with the High-Capacity cDNA Kit (Thermo Fisher). Gene expression was measured in duplicate with QuantStudio-3 and SYBR Green, normalized to Gapdh and/or Sra-1. Igf1 deletion was assessed as described (Spradlin et al., 2021) by comparing qPCR amplification of exon3–4 (deleted) to exon6–UTR (unaffected). Primer sequences are listed in Table S1 and illustrated in Figure 1.

Histological staining and analysis

Muscle cryosections were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min at RT and washed in PBS. Sections were blocked 1 h in 5% BSA, 0.02% Triton X-100, 0.03% sodium azide, then incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies (Key Resources). After washing (3×PBS), secondary antibodies with DAPI were applied for 1 h at RT, followed by PBS-T (0.5% Tween 20) washes. Slides were air-dried, mounted with Fluoromount-G, and sealed. Cultured cells were treated similarly.

Picro-Sirius Red staining assessed collagen (Smith et al., 2020). Cross-sectional areas were quantified using SMASH and CellPose (Smith and Barton, 2014; Waisman et al., 2021). PDGFRα+, CD68+ cells, and adipocytes were quantified in ImageJ. Images were acquired using Keyence X-BZ and EVOS imaging systems.

Immunoblotting

Proteins from tissues and cells were extracted using RIPA buffer (150 mM NaCl, 50 mM Tris-HCl, 1% Triton X-100, 0.5% sodium deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS). Equal amounts of protein (determined with Pierce BCA Protein Assay (Thermo Fisher)) were mixed with loading buffer containing 2-mercaptoethanol, denatured at 95°C for 5 min, and loaded onto 4–12% Bis-Tris gels (Invitrogen). Proteins were transferred to nitrocellulose or PVDF membranes at 60 V for 2.5 h. Membranes were stained with Ponceau S, washed in TBST, and blocked (Licor) for 1 h at RT. They were incubated with primary antibodies (Key Resources) overnight at 4°C, followed by TBST washes and incubation with secondary antibodies at RT. After final washes, images were captured on Li-Cor Odyssey CLX. Band intensities were quantified using Image Studio.

IGF-I ELISA

IGF-I levels in TA muscles were measured using the Quantikine ELISA kit (R&D Systems) per manufacturer’s instructions. Skeletal muscle lysates were diluted 1:3, and optical density was read at 450 and 570 nm (SpectraMax M5, Molecular Devices). Wavelength correction was performed by subtracting 570 nm from 450 nm readings. IGF-I levels were calculated from duplicates using a 4-parameter standard curve and normalized to total protein measured by Pierce BCA Protein Assay (Thermo Fisher). Results are expressed as pg IGF-I/μg total protein.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Comparisons were performed by unpaired Student’s t test, one-way and two-way ANOVAs, following by post hoc testing where appropriate. P-values <0.05 were considered significant.
