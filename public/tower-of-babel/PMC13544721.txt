A case of oral lipoma: from stem to stern
Authors: Zilefac Brian Ngokwe, Tamoh Fokam Stive, Kouamou Tchiekou Audrey, Nokam Kamdem Stephane, Ntep Ntep David Bienvenue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Lipomas are soft tissue tumors found mostly in adults between the fifth and seventh decades, and their exact cause remains unknown. Oral lipomas are rare compared to other body locations and are most often found in the buccal mucosa and buccal vestibule.Case presentation:We present a case of a non-tender oral lipoma in a 52-year-old female with a painless, soft, slowly evolving swelling causing facial asymmetry. We carried out, under local anesthesia, an intra-oral lipomectomy, and the histopathology confirmed the diagnosis of a lipoma. Postoperative control showed a dissipation of the facial asymmetry as well as good healing.Conclusion:Our case report demonstrates this complete process, from diagnosis to postoperative control, showing the importance of an intra-oral approach to improve outcomes and esthetics.

Introduction

Lipomas are benign soft tissue tumors made of mature adipocytes that are mostly found in adults and are the most common benign mesenchymal tumors[1,2] with a reported prevalence of 2.1 per 1000 people[3]. The exact etiology remains a mystery.

The majority of lipomas affect the trunk and upper extremities, but they can occur anywhere on the body where adipocytes are present[4]. With respect to oral lipomas, they are relatively rare compared to other body sites, with a reported frequency of 1–4%[5], and the most affected oral cavity sites are the buccal mucosa and buccal vestibule, representing close to 50% of oral lipomas[6].

Case presentation

A 52-year-old female came to the Odontostomatology service of the YUTH, presenting with a complaint of a non-tender labial swelling that has been evolving for about a year now, discovered fortuitously by a physician during a tooth avulsion. The swelling has progressively increased in volume. There is no history of drug use or any other relevant past medical history.

Extra-orally, the patient presented with facial asymmetry due to a right facial swelling with a soft texture, mobile, and non-tender on palpation. The extra-oral limits were: the lower lip vermilion superiorly, the right labial commissure laterally, the labiomental fold inferiorly, and the middle labial third mesially.

Our provisional diagnosis was an oral lipoma.

An intraoral lipomectomy was carried out under local anesthesia as per the treatment plan, and the surgical sample was sent for histopathology.

The histopathology confirmed the diagnosis of a lipoma. We carried out a postoperative follow-up 2 months later, which showed good scarring and the absence of facial asymmetry.

In total, from diagnosis to good healing, it took 63 and 56 days from surgery to scarring. The temporal procedure and evolution can be better appreciated, as seen in Figures 1–3.

To better appreciate the facial esthetic differences and the reduction of facial asymmetry, we place side by side the pre- and post-frontal views, showing the importance and necessity of surgery (Fig. 3).

Discussion

We present a case report of an oral lipoma, from diagnosis to healing, highlighting the significant esthetic outcome in an esthetic region, which is the face.

Lipomas typically appear as soft, movable, non-tender subcutaneous swellings. Lobulated sensation, slipping sign, and pseudo-fluctuation are some clinical diagnostic indicators[7]. In terms of size, the majority of lipomas are reported to present at less than 3 cm[8]; however, they could be present at sizes between 5 and 11 cm[9]. Additionally, our patient’s age of 52 falls within the reported usual age range of 40–60 years.

The occurrence of oral lipomas could be associated with the amount of available adipose tissue. In the case of oral lipomas, the buccal mucosa has a higher incidence, and this could be due to its proximity to the buccal fat pad, which is rich in adipose tissue[1].

Regarding therapeutics, the intra-oral approach is preferable as it renders a good outcome and better esthetics[10]. In addition, a complete lipoma resection should be effectuated, as it is key to preventing recurrence[10].

Oral lipomas, when of sufficient size, can create a mass effect on the face, leading to a poor esthetic look (Fig. 1I–III), and as we have shown in our case (Fig. 3), an intra-oral approach reduces the facial asymmetry and enhances the esthetic appearance. Conclusion

Oral lipomas are uncommon and can cause facial esthetic concerns when they reach a certain size. An intra-oral surgical approach helps to improve the esthetic appearance and outcome, resulting in patient satisfaction. Our case report demonstrates this complete process, from diagnosis to postoperative control.
