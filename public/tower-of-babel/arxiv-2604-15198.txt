Integrable deformations and stability of the Ricci flow
Maxwell Stolarski and Alex Waldron

Abstract
. We provide a comparatively simple proof of the dynamical stability of Ricci flow near a linearly stable Ricci-flat ALE metric with integrable deformations. Our proof relies on the equivalence between integrability and an “almost-orthogonality” property of the Ricci-DeTurck tensor, allowing us to analyze the latter directly. We obtain our main results in weighted Hölder spaces and then show how to recover the L p L^{p} -stability theorems of Deruelle-Kröncke [ 11 ] and Kröncke-Petersen [ 18 ] .



1.
Introduction

A collection of Riemannian metrics
(g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
on a manifold
M
is said to evolve by
Ricci flow
if

\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=-2\mathrm{Ric}(g).

Ricci-flat metrics correspond to fixed points of the Ricci flow.
As with any dynamical system, it is natural to ask which Ricci-flat metrics are
stable
fixed points for the Ricci flow.
In other words, do Ricci flows starting near a given Ricci-flat metric remain close to that metric for all time?

There is a collection of important and beautiful results on the stability of Ricci flows.
Šešum proved that compact, linearly stable, Ricci-flat manifolds with integrable deformations are dynamically stable under Ricci flow
[
25
]
(see also earlier results for flat metrics
[
15
]
).
For such compact manifolds
(M,g_{0})
,
[
25
]
uses the integrability assumption to remove kernel directions (with respect to a Lichnerowicz Laplacian) for
g(t)-g_{0}
along the flow.
After removing kernel directions, the dynamics are governed by strictly stable directions, which enables
[
25
]
to deduce stability.
Cheeger-Tian previously applied a similar argument to prove the uniqueness of tangent cones for Ricci-flat manifolds
[
7
]
, which was in turn inspired by related results in minimal surfaces
[
1
,
27
]
.
Later, in
[
16
]
, Haslhofer-Buzano developed a new approach that removed the integrability assumption from
[
25
]
.
The argument in
[
16
]
is based on a Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality for Perelman’s
\lambda
-functional, and therefore relies heavily on the fact that
M
is compact.

[
30
]
uses a similar approach with Perelman’s
\mu
-functional to deduce a related stability result for Fano Kähler-Einstein metrics.

In the non-compact case, Perelman’s functionals are no longer directly available (see however
[
12
,
13
]
).
Despite this difficulty, Deruelle-Kröncke proved that linearly stable Ricci-flat ALE manifolds
(M,g_{0})
with integrable deformations are dynamically stable in
L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}

[
11
]
.
Their argument relies on a choice of dynamically changing reference Ricci-flat metrics
h(t)
for flows
g(t)
near
g_{0}
, and
L^{2}
estimates on the difference
g(t)-h(t)
from these moving reference metrics.
Kröncke-Petersen improved this result to show that Ricci-flat ALE manifolds with a parallel spinor and integrable deformations are dynamically stable in
L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}
for
1<p<n

[
18
]
.
Their argument uses a dynamic decomposition of metrics near
g_{0}
and heat-kernel estimates from
[
19
]
to obtain stability through a sophisticated fixed-point argument.
Note that both
[
11
,
18
]
rely on constructing a family of time-dependent reference Ricci-flat metrics, which broadly resembles the method in
[
25
]
of subtracting kernel directions.
Prior stability results were also obtained for Euclidean space and noncompact symmetric spaces in
[
24
,
3
]
.

In this paper, we develop an alternative approach that yields a new and relatively simple proof of stability in the ALE case.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a smooth, complete, Ricci-flat ALE manifold of dimension
n\geq 4.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\mu\in\{n-1,n\},

x_{*}\in M
, and
n-\mu+1<\ell<n-2.
For weighted Hölder spaces as in Definition
3.3
, assume

(1)

g_{0}
is linearly stable (Definition
3.8
),

(2)

the Lichnerowicz Laplacian
L=L_{g_{0}}
associated to
g_{0}
satisfies

\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}(S^{2}M,g_{0}),

and

(3)

g_{0}
has integrable deformations in
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\ell}(M,g_{0})
(Definition
3.15
).

If
\|g(0)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\ell}(M,g_{0},x_{*})}\leq{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small, then the (complete, bounded-curvature) Ricci-DeTurck flow
g(t)
(with respect to
g_{0}
) starting from
g(0)
is defined for all
t\in[0,\infty),
with

\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\ell}(M,g_{0},x_{*})}\lesssim{\varepsilon}

and

g(t)\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{C^{\infty}(M,g_{0})}g_{\infty}

for some smooth, gauged Ricci-flat ALE metric
g_{\infty}
on
M
.

There further exists a smoothly convergent family
\theta_{t}
of diffeomorphisms such that
\theta_{t}^{*}g(t)
solves the Ricci flow and
\theta_{t}^{*}g(t)\to g_{\infty}^{\prime}
in
C^{\infty}_{loc}

for a Ricci-flat metric
g^{\prime}_{\infty}
diffeomorphic to
g_{\infty},
with
\|g^{\prime}_{\infty}-g_{0}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\ell}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}.

Remark 1.2
.

The hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
hold in several important cases (see Subsections
3.3
–
3.4
for further details):

•

The decay hypothesis (
2
) is true with
\mu=n-1
for general Ricci-flat ALE spaces
[
11
, Theorem 2.7, Remark 2.10]
and with
\mu=n
in the case that
n=4

[
4
]
or
(M^{n},g_{0})
is spin with a nonzero parallel spinor
[
19
]
.

•

If
(M^{n},g_{0})
is spin and carries a nonzero parallel spinor, then the stability hypothesis (
1
) is satisfied
[
10
]
and the decay hypothesis (
2
) is satisfied with
\mu=n

[
19
]
. The integrability hypothesis (
3
) also holds, unless perhaps the holonomy is
\text{Hol}(M,g_{0})=\text{Spin}(7)

[
11
, Theorem 2.18]
.
In the case of
\text{Spin}(7)
holonomy, integrability is nonetheless widely expected to be true.

–

All known Ricci-flat ALE manifolds, aside from Euclidean space, have special holonomy
(
\text{Hol}(M,g_{0})=\text{SU}(n/2)
,
\text{Sp}(n/4)
, or
\text{Spin}(7)
) and are therefore spin with a nonzero parallel spinor
[
18
, Remark 1.4]
.

•

If
(M^{n},g_{0})
is Kähler of general dimension, i.e. a Calabi-Yau ALE space, then hypotheses (
1
)–(
3
) are satisfied with
\mu=n
and any
\ell\in(1,n-2)

[
10
,
11
,
19
]
.
Thus, for Calabi-Yau ALE spaces, Theorem
1.1
applies for each
\ell\in(1,n-2).

–

In particular, if
(M^{4},g_{0})
is a 4-dimensional Calabi-Yau ALE space, then Theorem
1.1
applies for each
\ell\in(1,2)
.
Note that all currently-known 4D Ricci-flat ALE spaces are Calabi-Yau.
It is an open question whether there are 4D Ricci-flat ALE spaces which are not Kähler.

Theorem
1.1
is essentially a Hölder-space version of the
L^{p}
-stability theorems due to Deruelle-Kröncke
[
11
]
and Kröncke-Petersen
[
18
]
. Our proof, however, is original, and rests on the following observation. Assuming that
g_{0}
has integrable deformations, all nearby metrics enjoy an
almost-orthogonality
property: as
g
tends to
g_{0}
, the kernel component of the Ricci-DeTurck tensor
\mathrm{Ric}(g)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{V(g,g_{0})}(g)
tends to zero relative to its overall norm—see Lemma
2.2
and Theorem
5.4
.
Almost-orthogonality allows us to directly analyze the Ricci-DeTurck tensor along a Ricci-DeTurck flow near
g_{0};
it avoids the construction of a suitable gauge along the flow, Perelman’s functionals, and the choice of dynamically changing Ricci-flat reference metrics. Moreover, this approach easily recovers Šešum’s stability theorem
[
25
]
in the compact case (see Theorem
4.1
).

While Theorem
1.1
is most naturally proved in weighted Hölder spaces, some additional integral estimates allow us to fully recover the
L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}
-stability results of
[
11
,
18
]
. We assume for simplicity that the decay order is
\mu=n,
although the proof also works for
\mu=n-1
and
p
in an appropriate range.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a smooth, complete, Ricci-flat ALE manifold and fix
1<p<n.

Assume that
g_{0}
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
with
\mu=n
and for some
1<\ell\leq\frac{n}{p}
with
\ell<\frac{n}{2}.

Given
{\varepsilon}>0,
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\|g(0)-g_{0}\|_{L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}(M,g_{0})}<\delta,
then the (complete, bounded curvature) Ricci-DeTurck flow
g(t)
starting from
g(0)
is defined for all
t\in[0,\infty)
, with
\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}(M,g_{0})}<{\varepsilon}
and

g(t)\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{C^{\infty}(M,g_{0})}g_{\infty}

for some smooth, gauged Ricci-flat ALE metric
g_{\infty}
on
M
. Moreover,
g(t)\to g_{\infty}
in
L^{p}(M)
as
t\to\infty.

There further exists a smoothly convergent family
\theta_{t}
of diffeomorphisms such that
\theta_{t}^{*}g(t)
solves the Ricci flow and
\theta_{t}^{*}g(t)\to g_{\infty}^{\prime}
in
C^{\infty}_{loc}
for a Ricci-flat metric
g^{\prime}_{\infty}
diffeomorphic to
g_{\infty}.

We begin in Section
2
by proving an abstract almost-orthogonality lemma and showing that it implies an exponential convergence result for associated evolution equations.
After reviewing some preliminaries in Section
3
, Section
4
applies the general almost-orthogonality result to prove the stability of linearly stable Ricci-flat metrics with integrable deformations on closed manifolds (Theorem
4.1
).
In Section
5
, we begin our treatment of ALE metrics and establish a
quantitative
almost-orthogonality property (Theorem
5.4
) for these non-compact spaces.
Sections
6
–
7
exploit this quantitative almost-orthogonality to prove stability in weighted Hölder spaces (Theorem
1.1
).
Finally, the appendices provide the additional (scalar)
L^{p}
-estimates required to prove the
L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}
-stability result (Theorem
1.3
).

2.
Almost-orthogonality and exponential convergence

2.1.
Abstract almost-orthogonality

Let
V
and
W
be Banach spaces and
\mathcal{M}
a
C^{1}
map between open sets in
V
and
W.
Write

S=\{\mathcal{M}(u)=0\}\subset V.

Fix
u_{0}\in S
and let

L=d\mathcal{M}_{u_{0}}:V\to W.

Define
V_{0}=\ker L,
which is closed, and suppose that there exists a complementary closed subspace
V_{1}\subset V:

V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}.

Let
\pi_{0}:V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}\to V_{0}
denote the associated projection map.

Definition 2.1
.

The solution
u_{0}\in S
is said to have
integrable deformations
in
V
if there exists a
C^{1}
map
\sigma
from a neighborhood of the origin
0\in V_{0}
onto a neighborhood of
u_{0}
in
S,
such that
\sigma(0)=u_{0}
and

\left(\pi_{0}\circ d\sigma\right)_{0}=\mathbf{1}:V_{0}\to V_{0}.

Suppose also that the image
W_{1}:=\operatorname{Im}L
is closed and complemented by a closed subspace
W_{0}\subset W:

W=W_{0}\oplus W_{1}.

We decompose
\mathcal{M}
along the direct sum as follows:

(2.1)

\mathcal{M}(u)=\mathcal{M}_{0}(u)\oplus\mathcal{M}_{1}(u)\in W_{0}\oplus W_{1}.

Lemma 2.2

(Almost-orthogonality)
.

The solution
u_{0}\in S
has integrable deformations if and only if, for each
{\varepsilon}>0,
there exists
\delta>0
such that

(2.2)

|\mathcal{M}_{0}(u)|\leq{\varepsilon}|\mathcal{M}(u)|

for all
u\in B_{\delta}(u_{0}).

The proof will be based on:

Lemma 2.3
.

If
f(x,y)
is
C^{1}
with
f(x,0)\equiv 0
and
df_{(0,0)}=0
then for each
{\varepsilon}>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

(2.3)

|f(x,y)|\leq{\varepsilon}|y|

for
|(x,y)|<\delta.

Proof.

By the mean-value theorem, we have

\begin{split}|f(x,y)-f(x,0)|\leq|y|\sup|df(x,t)|\leq{\varepsilon}|y|\end{split}

for
(x,y)
sufficiently close to
(0,0).

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Assume without loss of generality that
u_{0}=0.
Let

S_{0}=\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(u)=0\}

and observe that
S\subset S_{0}
by definition. The integrability assumption is that
S=S_{0}
after restricting to a sufficiently small neighborhood.

We may further assume that
S_{0}
is a neighborhood of the origin in
V_{0},
by the following argument. Write

u=(x,y)\in V_{0}\oplus V_{1}.

Since
L:V_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}W_{1}
is an isomorphism, the implicit function theorem provides
y_{1}(x),
defined in a neighborhood of the origin in
V_{0},
such that
\mathcal{M}_{1}(x,y_{1}(x))=0.
After applying the coordinate change

(2.4)

(x,y)\mapsto\left(x,y+y_{1}(x)\right)

on
V,
we have
\mathcal{M}_{1}(x,0)\equiv 0.
Note that (
2.4
) does not change the splitting (
2.1
), which is on
W
,
hence the conclusion is unaffected. Also, since
V_{0}=\ker L,
we have
\left(dy_{1}\right)_{(0,0)}=0,
so
L
is unchanged.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
S=S_{0}.
Define
\delta_{1}(x,y)\in W_{1}
by

\mathcal{M}(u)=\mathcal{M}(x,y)=\mathcal{M}_{0}(x,y)+L(y)+\delta_{1}(x,y).

By definition of the derivative, we have

\left(d\mathcal{M}_{0}\right)_{(0,0)}+\left(d\delta_{1}\right)_{(0,0)}=0.

Since the images of
\left(d\mathcal{M}_{0}\right)_{(0,0)}
and
\left(d\delta_{1}\right)_{(0,0)}
lie in complementary subspaces, they must vanish individually.

Also notice that
\mathcal{M}(x,0)
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}(x,0)
both vanish identically, since
S=S_{0}
, hence the same is true of
\delta_{1}(x,0).

Applying Lemma
2.3
both to
\delta_{1}
and to
\mathcal{M}_{0},
we may conclude that

(2.5)

|\delta_{1}(x,y)|+|\mathcal{M}_{0}(x,y)|\leq{\varepsilon}|y|

for
|(x,y)|<\delta.

On the other hand, since
L:V_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}W_{1}
is an isomorphism, the Bounded Inverse Theorem gives

(2.6)

|L(y)|\geq c|y|

for a constant
c>0.

Applying (
2.6
) and (
2.5
) twice, we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}{\varepsilon}|\mathcal{M}(x,y)|&\geq{\varepsilon}\left(|L(y)|-|\delta_{1}(x,y)+\mathcal{M}_{0}(x,y)|\right)\\
&\geq(c-{\varepsilon}){\varepsilon}|y|\\
&\geq(c-{\varepsilon})|\mathcal{M}_{0}(x,y)|,\end{split}

which implies the desired conclusion.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that the integrability assumption fails, i.e.
S\cap B_{\delta}(0)\subsetneq V_{0}\cap B_{\delta}(0)
for every
\delta>0.
Take
{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}.
For any
\delta>0,
there exists
x\in B_{\delta}(0)\cap V_{0}
with
\mathcal{M}(x,0)=\mathcal{M}_{0}(x,0)\neq 0,
so that

|\mathcal{M}_{0}(x,0)|>\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{M}_{0}(x,0)|=\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{M}(x,0)|.

Hence (
2.2
) fails.
∎

2.2.
Exponential convergence of 2nd-order parabolic evolution equations on compact manifolds

Suppose now that
\mathcal{M}
is a 2nd-order quasilinear elliptic operator on sections of a vector bundle
E
(with metric) over a closed Riemannian manifold, and consider the parabolic evolution equation

(2.8)

\frac{{\partial}u}{{\partial}t}=\mathcal{M}(u).

Let
V\hookrightarrow W
be Banach spaces of sections on which
\mathcal{M}
defines a
C^{1}
map between open subsets. Assume that
C^{k}\hookrightarrow W\hookrightarrow L^{2}
for some
k\geq 0.

Let
u_{0}\in V
be a solution of the equation
\mathcal{M}(u)=0,
and define
L=d\mathcal{M}_{u_{0}}.
Take

V_{0}=\ker_{V}L,\qquad V_{1}=V_{0}^{\perp}\cap V,\qquad W_{1}=\operatorname{Im}L,\qquad W_{0}=W_{1}^{\perp}\cap W,

where
V_{0}^{\perp},W_{1}^{\perp}
refer to
L^{2}
-orthogonal complements.

Assume further that

•

(
2.8
) is well-posed and smoothing on
V
,

•

L
is self-adjoint (i.e.
\left(Lu,v\right)_{L^{2}}=\left(u,Lv\right)_{L^{2}}
for all smooth sections
u,v
),

•

V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
, and

•

W=W_{0}\oplus W_{1}.

For example, if
V=C^{2,\alpha}
and
W=C^{\alpha},
these are standard facts.

Theorem 2.4

(Two-Interval Theorem
1

1

Cf. Leon Simon’s Three-Interval Theorem
[
27
, (4.11-4.12), p. 546]
.
)
.

Let
u_{0}
be a solution of the equation
\mathcal{M}(u)=0
as above, and assume that
u_{0}
has integrable deformations in
V
and is linearly stable.
Given
\lambda<\lambda_{1},
the first positive eigenvalue of
L,
there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
u(0)\in B_{\delta}(u_{0})\subset V,
the corresponding solution
u(t)
of (
2.8
) satisfies

(2.9)

\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}||\mathcal{M}(u(t))||_{L^{2}}\leq e^{-\lambda}\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}||\mathcal{M}(u(t))||_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that (
2.9
) fails for solutions
u_{i}(t)
with
u_{i}(0)\in B_{1/i}(u_{0}).
By well-posedness in
V
and smoothing, we may assume that
u_{i}(t)
are defined for
t\in\left[0,2\right]
and converge in
C^{\infty}_{loc}\left(\left(0,2\right]\right)
to
u_{0},
with
u_{i}(t)\to u_{0}
in
V
for each

as

Let
c_{i}\to\infty
be such that

f_{i}(t):=c_{i}\mathcal{M}(u_{i}(t))

satisfies

(2.10)

\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}||f_{i}(t)||_{L^{2}}=1.

Since the estimate (
2.9
) fails, we in fact have

(2.11)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq 2}||f_{i}(t)||_{L^{2}}\leq e^{\lambda}.

Let
k,\tau>0.

Since the
f_{i}
’s solve a linear parabolic equation

(2.12)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-d\mathcal{M}_{u_{i}(t)}\right)f_{i}(t)=0

with uniformly smooth coefficients at positive times, we have

\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq 2}\|f_{i}(t)\|_{C^{k}}\leq C_{k,\tau}\sup_{0\leq t\leq 2}\|f_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

In particular,

(2.13)

\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}\|f_{i}(t)\|_{W}\leq C.

By Arzela-Ascoli, we may pass to a subsequence such that
f_{i}(t)\to f_{\infty}(t)
in
C^{\infty}_{loc}\left(\left(0,2\right]\right)
and weakly in

at

so that the assumptions (

-

u_{i}\to u_{0}
as

(
2.12
) converges to the fixed equation

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-L\right)f_{\infty}(t)=0.

We now apply Lemma

. Given

for

Corollary 2.5
.

For any initial datum in a sufficiently small
V
-neighborhood of
u_{0},
the corresponding solution of (
2.8
) exists for all time and converges exponentially.

Remark 2.6
.

Below we will check the above hypotheses in the case of Ricci flow. 3.
Geometric preliminaries

3.1.
The Ricci-DeTurck operator and its linearization

Fix a reference metric
g_{0}
on a manifold
M
and define

(3.1)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(g)&:=-2\mathrm{Ric}(g)+{\mathscr{L}}_{V(g,g_{0})}(g).\end{split}

Here
V(g,g_{0})
is the DeTurck vector field defined locally by

V^{k}=g^{ij}\left(\Gamma(g)^{k}_{ij}-\Gamma(g_{0})^{k}_{ij}\right)

or globally by

g_{0}(V(g,g_{0}),\cdot)=-{\operatorname{div}}_{g}g_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g}{\operatorname{tr}}_{g}g_{0}.

Let

\Delta_{g,g_{0}}=g^{ab}\nabla^{g_{0},2}_{ab}.

By
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
(see also
[
11
, p. 5]
),
we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(g)_{ij}&=\Delta_{g,g_{0}}g_{ij}-g^{k\ell}g_{ip}(g_{0})^{pq}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{jk\ell q}-g^{k\ell}g_{jp}(g_{0})^{pq}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{ik\ell q}\\
&+g^{ab}g^{pq}\left(\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}g_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}g_{qb}+\nabla^{g_{0}}_{a}g_{jp}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{q}g_{ib}\right)\\
&-g^{ab}g^{pq}\left(\nabla^{g_{0}}_{a}g_{jp}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}g_{iq}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}g_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}g_{iq}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}g_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}g_{jq}\right).\end{split}

Suppose that
g_{0}
is Ricci-flat, and write
h=g-g_{0}.
Observing that

(3.3)

g^{ab}=g_{0}^{ab}-g_{0}^{am}h_{mn}g^{nb},

and using Ricci-flatness of
g_{0},
we have

(3.4)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(g)_{ij}&=\Delta_{g,g_{0}}h_{ij}-h_{ab}g^{ka}(g_{0})^{\ell b}g_{ip}(g_{0})^{pq}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{jk\ell q}-h_{ab}g^{ka}(g_{0})^{\ell b}g_{jp}(g_{0})^{pq}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{ik\ell q}\\
&+g^{ab}g^{pq}\left(\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}h_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}h_{qb}+\nabla^{g_{0}}_{a}h_{jp}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{q}h_{ib}\right)\\
&-g^{ab}g^{pq}\left(\nabla^{g_{0}}_{a}h_{jp}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{iq}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}h_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{iq}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}h_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{jq}\right).\end{split}

The linearization of
\mathcal{M}
at
g
comes out to

(3.5)

\begin{split}d\mathcal{M}_{g}(\hat{h})_{ij}&=\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\hat{h}_{ij}-2g_{0}^{ka}g_{0}^{\ell b}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{ik\ell j}\hat{h}_{ab}+F_{ij}{}^{cab}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{c}\hat{h}_{ab}+G_{ij}{}^{ab}\hat{h}_{ab},\end{split}

where

(3.6)

\begin{split}F_{ij}{}^{cmn}&=g^{mq}g^{nb}\left(\delta_{i}{}^{c}\left(\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}h_{qb}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{jq}\right)+\delta_{j}{}^{c}\left(\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}h_{bq}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{iq}\right)\right)\\
&+g^{bc}g^{nq}\left(\delta_{j}{}^{m}\left(\nabla^{g_{0}}_{q}h_{ib}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{iq}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}h_{qb}\right)+\delta_{i}{}^{m}\left(\nabla^{g_{0}}_{q}h_{jb}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{jq}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}h_{qb}\right)\right)\end{split}

and

(3.7)

\begin{split}G_{ij}{}^{mn}&=-g^{am}g^{nb}\nabla^{g_{0},2}_{a,b}h_{ij}\\
&-g_{0}^{km}g_{0}^{\ell n}g_{0}^{pq}\left(h_{ip}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{jk\ell q}+h_{jp}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{ik\ell q}\right)\\
&+h_{ab}\left(\begin{split}&\left(g_{0}^{ka}g^{bm}g_{0}^{\ell n}+g^{km}g^{na}g_{0}^{\ell b}\right)g_{0}^{pq}\left(g_{ip}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{jk\ell q}+g_{jp}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{ik\ell q}\right)\\
&-g^{ka}g_{0}^{\ell b}g_{0}^{nq}\left(\delta_{i}{}^{m}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{jk\ell q}+\delta_{j}{}^{m}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{ik\ell q}\right)\end{split}\right)\\
&-2g^{am}g^{nb}g^{pq}\left(\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}h_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}h_{qb}+\nabla^{g_{0}}_{a}h_{jp}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{q}h_{ib}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{a}h_{jp}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{iq}\\
-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}h_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{iq}-\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}h_{pa}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{jq}\end{split}\right).\end{split}

These exact expressions are not crucial for the present paper but have been included for the sake of concreteness.
When
g=g_{0},
we have
F=G=0
and (
3.5
) reduces to the Lichnerowicz operator
L_{g_{0}}(\hat{h}).

The Ricci-DeTurck flow (with respect to reference metric
g_{0}
) is given by

(3.8)

\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=\mathcal{M}(g).

Notice that along Ricci-DeTurck flow, we have

(3.9)

\begin{split}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\mathcal{M}(g(t))_{ij}&=d\mathcal{M}_{g(t)}\left(\frac{{\partial}g}{{\partial}t}\right)_{ij}\\
&=\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\mathcal{M}_{ij}-2g_{0}^{ka}g_{0}^{\ell b}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{ik\ell j}\mathcal{M}_{ab}+F_{ij}{}^{cab}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{c}\mathcal{M}_{ab}+G_{ij}{}^{ab}\mathcal{M}_{ab}.\end{split}

3.2.
Preliminaries on ALE manifolds

Definition 3.1
.

A complete, connected Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g_{0})
is
asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE) of order
\tau>0
if there exists
K\Subset M
, a finite subgroup
\Gamma<SO(n)
acting freely on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and a diffeomorphism

\Psi:({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})/\Gamma\to M\setminus K

such that

|\nabla^{k}_{g_{Euc}}(\Psi^{*}g_{0}-g_{Euc})|_{g_{Euc}}=O(r^{-\tau-k})\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Here
r=|x|
is the radial coordinate on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Such a diffeomorphism
\Psi:({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})/\Gamma\to M\setminus K
will be called a
coordinate system at infinity
.

A triple
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*})
consisting of an ALE manifold
(M^{n},g_{0})
and a point
x_{*}\in M
will be called a
pointed ALE manifold
.
We use
\rho:M\to[0,\infty)
to denote the regularized distance function
\rho(x):=\sqrt{1+{\operatorname{dist}}_{g_{0}}^{2}(x,x_{*})}
.

Throughout the remainder of this subsection unless otherwise noted, all raising/lowering of indices, norms
|\cdot|=|\cdot|_{g_{0}}
, and all covariant derivatives
\nabla=\nabla^{g_{0}}
are with respect to
g_{0}
, where
(M^{n},g_{0})
is an ALE manifold.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*})
be a pointed ALE manifold of order
\tau>0
and
\rho(x):=\sqrt{1+{\operatorname{dist}}_{g_{0}}^{2}(x,x_{*})}
.
For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C_{k}=C_{k}(k,M^{n},g_{0},x_{*},\tau)>0
such that

|\nabla^{k}Rm|\leq C_{k}\rho^{-\tau-2-k}\qquad\text{on }M.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*})
be a pointed ALE manifold and let
E
be a tensor bundle over
M
.
Let
\rho(x):=\sqrt{1+{\operatorname{dist}}_{g_{0}}^{2}(x,x_{*})}.

For
0<\gamma<1
and
\delta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, define a weighted Hölder norm
C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta}
on sections
h
of
E
by

(3.10)

\begin{split}\|h\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta}}:=\sup_{M}\rho(x)^{\delta}|h|+\sup_{x\in M}\rho(x)^{\delta+\gamma}[h]_{C^{0,\gamma}_{*}(B(x,\rho(x)/2))},\text{ where }\\
[h]_{C^{0,\gamma}_{*}(B(x,\frac{\rho(x)}{2}))}:=\sup_{\sigma:[0,l]\to B(x,\rho(x)/2)}\frac{|P_{l}h(\sigma(0))-h(\sigma(l))|}{l^{\gamma}},\end{split}

the supremum is taken over all unit-speed
g_{0}
-geodesics
\sigma:[0,l]\to B(x,\rho(x)/2)
, and
P_{l}
denotes parallel transport along
\sigma
from
\sigma(0)
to
\sigma(l)
.

Define weighted Hölder spaces
C^{k,\gamma}_{-\delta}(E,M,g_{0},x_{*})
as the set of sections
h
of
E
for which the norm

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{-\delta}}:=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sup_{M}\rho(x)^{\delta+i}|\nabla^{i}h|+\sup_{x\in M}\rho(x)^{\delta+k+\gamma}[\nabla^{k}h]_{C^{0,\gamma}_{*}(B(x,\rho(x)/2))}

is finite.

When the tensor bundle
E
, base manifold
M
, or base point
x_{*}\in M
is implicit from context, we shall simplify the notation and write simply
C^{k,\gamma}_{-\delta}(E,g_{0})
or
C^{k,\gamma}_{-\delta}(M,g_{0})
for example.
Note that different choices of base point
x_{*}\in M
define equivalent
C^{k,\gamma}_{-\delta}
norms.

Observe that if
h\in C^{k,\gamma}_{-\delta}
then for
i\leq k,

\nabla^{(i)}h\in C^{k-i,\gamma}_{-\delta-i}.

We have the following simple interpolation inequality:

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

(3.12)

\|\nabla^{i}f\nabla^{j}g\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\alpha-\beta-i-j}}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{i,\gamma}_{-\alpha}}\|g\|_{C^{j,\gamma}_{-\beta}}.

Proof.

This follows by writing

\rho^{\alpha+\beta+i+j}\nabla^{i}f\nabla^{j}g=\left(\rho^{\alpha+i}\nabla^{i}f\right)\left(\rho^{\beta+j}\nabla^{j}g\right).

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*})
be a pointed ALE manifold of order
\tau>0
.
Let
\Psi:({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})/\Gamma\to M\setminus K
be a coordinate system at infinity and denote the inverse
\Phi=\Psi^{-1}
.
Let
S^{2}M
be the bundle of symmetric 2-tensors over
M
.

Then there exists
C=C(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*},\tau,\Psi)>0
such that for all sections
h:M\setminus K\to S^{2}M,

(3.13)

|(\Delta_{g_{0}}-\Delta_{\Phi^{*}g_{Euc}})h|_{g_{0}}\leq C\left(\rho^{-\tau}|\nabla^{2}_{g_{0}}h|_{g_{0}}+\rho^{-\tau-1}|\nabla_{g_{0}}h|_{g_{0}}+\rho^{-\tau-2}|h|_{g_{0}}\right)\quad\text{on }M\setminus K.

Moreover, for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\delta\geq 0
,
there exists
C=C(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*},\gamma,\delta,\tau,\Psi)>0
such that

(3.14)

\|(\Delta_{g_{0}}-\Delta_{\Phi^{*}g_{Euc}})h\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\tau-\delta-2}(S^{2}(M\setminus K),g_{0})}\leq C\|h\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})}\quad\forall h\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0}).

Identical statements hold for
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued functions
u:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

To simplify notation, denote the Riemannian metric
\overline{g}=\Phi^{*}g_{Euc}
on
M\setminus K
throughout the proof.
Denote also

\tilde{g}\doteqdot\overline{g}-g_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{\Gamma}\doteqdot\Gamma(\overline{g})-\Gamma(g_{0})

where
\Gamma(\overline{g}),\Gamma(g_{0})
are the Christoffel symbols for
\overline{g}
and
g_{0}
, respectively.
Since
g_{0}
is ALE of order
\tau>0
, it follows that

(3.15)

|\tilde{g}|_{g_{0}}=O(\rho^{-\tau}),\quad|\tilde{\Gamma}|_{g_{0}}=O(\rho^{-\tau-1}),\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla_{g_{0}}\tilde{\Gamma}|_{g_{0}}=O(\rho^{-\tau-2}).

Observe that, for
C^{2}
-sections
h:M\to S^{2}M
,

\displaystyle(\Delta_{g_{0}}-\Delta_{\overline{g}})h={}

\displaystyle g_{0}^{-1}\nabla_{g_{0}}\nabla_{g_{0}}h-\overline{g}^{-1}\nabla_{\overline{g}}\nabla_{\overline{g}}h

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle g_{0}^{-1}\nabla_{g_{0}}\nabla_{g_{0}}h-(g_{0}+\tilde{g})^{-1}\left(\nabla_{g_{0}}+\tilde{\Gamma}*\right)\left(\nabla_{g_{0}}+\tilde{\Gamma}*\right)h

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left[g_{0}^{-1}-(g_{0}+\tilde{g})^{-1}\right]\nabla_{g_{0}}\nabla_{g_{0}}h+(g_{0}+\tilde{g})^{-1}\tilde{\Gamma}*\nabla_{g_{0}}h+(g_{0}+\tilde{g})^{-1}(\nabla_{g_{0}}\tilde{\Gamma})*h

\displaystyle+(g_{0}+\tilde{g})^{-1}\tilde{\Gamma}*\tilde{\Gamma}*h.

By taking norms with respect to
g_{0}
and using (
3.15
), it follows that

\displaystyle|(\Delta_{g_{0}}-\Delta_{\overline{g}})h|_{g_{0}}\leq{}

\displaystyle O(\rho^{-\tau})|\nabla^{2}_{g_{0}}h|_{g_{0}}+O(\rho^{-\tau-1})|\nabla_{g_{0}}h|_{g_{0}}+O(\rho^{-\tau-2})|h|_{g_{0}}

Estimate (
3.13
) now follows.
Multiplying both sides by
\rho^{\tau+\delta+2}
and taking a supremum over
M\setminus K
then implies

\|(\Delta_{g_{0}}-\Delta_{\overline{g}})h\|_{C^{0}_{-\tau-\delta-2}(S^{2}(M\setminus K),g_{0})}\leq C\|h\|_{C^{2}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})}

for some
C=C(M^{n},g_{0},\delta,\tau,\Psi)>0
.
The proof of the
C^{0,\gamma}_{-\tau-\delta-2}
-estimates (
3.14
) and for
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued functions
u
follow similarly.
∎

Throughout the paper, we also make use of the Bishop-Gromov volume comparison estimate to estimate certain integrals on
M
.

Proposition 3.6

(Bishop-Gromov Volume Comparison)
.

Let
\omega_{n}
denote the volume of the unit ball in Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
If
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a smooth, complete, connected Riemannian manifold with
Rc\geq 0
, then, for any
x_{*}\in M
,

r\mapsto\frac{Vol_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x_{*},r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\text{ is nonincreasing and }\lim_{r\searrow 0}\frac{Vol_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x_{*},r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}=1.

Moreover, there exists a dimensional constant
C_{n}>0
such that for any
f:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\int_{M}f(d(x_{*},x))dV_{g_{0}}(x)\leq C_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)r^{n-1}\,dr.

3.3.
The Lichnerowicz Laplacian and stability

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a Riemannian manifold.
The
Lichnerowicz Laplacian

L=L_{g_{0}}
is the operator on symmetric 2-tensors given in local coordinates by

Lh_{ij}=\Delta h_{ij}+2R_{ipjq}h^{pq}-R_{ip}h^{p}{}_{j}-R_{jp}h^{p}{}_{i}

where the Laplacian
\Delta
, curvatures
Rm,Rc
, and raising and lowering of indices are all with respect to
g_{0}
.
Note that, in the case
(M,g_{0})
is Ricci-flat,
Lh_{ij}=\Delta h_{ij}+2R_{ipjq}h^{pq}
.

Definition 3.8
.

A Ricci-flat manifold
(M,g_{0})
is said to be
linearly stable
if

\int_{M}\langle h,Lh\rangle_{g_{0}}dV_{g_{0}}\leq 0\qquad\forall h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(S^{2}M,g_{0}).

Definition 3.9
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a Ricci-flat ALE manifold.
Observe that the Lichnerowicz Laplacian
L=L_{g_{0}}
defines a bounded linear map

L:C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\to C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}(S^{2}M,g_{0})

for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\delta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
We let
\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L
denote its kernel

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L:=\{h\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\ |\ Lh=0\}.

We also define

\ker_{L^{2}}L:=\{h\in L^{2}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\ |\ h\text{ is smooth and }Lh=0\}.

Remark 3.10
.

As a consequence of elliptic regularity estimates, any
h\in\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L
is also smooth
and the kernels
\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L
and
\ker_{L^{2}}L
are always finite-dimensional
[
22
, Theorem 4.21, Corollary 4.23]
.

We collect a few elementary properties about the kernel(s) of the Lichnerowicz Laplacian.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a Ricci-flat ALE manifold, let
L=L_{g_{0}}
denote its Lichnerowicz Laplacian, and let
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

(1)

For all
\delta,\delta^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}\quad\implies\quad\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta^{\prime}}}L\subseteq\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L.

(2)

If
\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
for some
\mu>\frac{n}{2}
, then

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{L^{2}}L\qquad\forall\delta\in\left(\frac{n}{2},\mu\right].

(3)

We have

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta^{\prime}}}L\qquad\forall\delta,\delta^{\prime}\in(0,n-2).

Proof.

(
1
):
Clearly,
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta^{\prime}}\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}
when
\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}
.
Hence,
\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}
implies

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta^{\prime}}}L=\{h\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta^{\prime}}\ |\ Lh=0\}\subseteq\{h\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}\ |\ Lh=0\}=\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L.

(
2
):
Note that
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\subseteq L^{2}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
when
\delta>\frac{n}{2}
.
Indeed,
\delta>\frac{n}{2}
implies

\int_{M}|h|_{g_{0}}^{2}dV_{g_{0}}\leq C\|h\|^{2}_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho^{-2\delta+n-1}d\rho\leq C\|h\|^{2}_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\qquad(C=C(M^{n},g_{0},\delta)).

Thus,
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}\subseteq L^{2}
and elliptic regularity imply

(3.16)

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\{h\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}\ |\ Lh=0\}\subseteq\{h\in L^{2}\ |\ h\text{ is smooth and }Lh=0\}=\ker_{L^{2}}L\\
\forall\delta>\frac{n}{2}.

On the other hand,
\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}
implies

(3.17)

\ker_{L^{2}}L=\{h\in L^{2}\ |\ h\text{ is smooth and }Lh=0\}\subseteq\{h\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}\ |\ Lh=0\}=\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}}L.

Combining item (
1
) with (
3.16
)–(
3.17
) then implies that, for all
\delta\in\left(\frac{n}{2},\mu\right]
,

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}}L\subseteq\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L\subseteq\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}}L.

Thus, we have equality throughout and (
2
) is proven.

(
3
):

-\delta\in(2-n,0)
consists of non-exceptional values
-\delta
,
and so the Fredholm index of
L:C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}\to C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}
is independent of
-\delta\in(2-n,0)
(see e.g.
[
22
, Corollary 4.22, Theorem 4.24, Corollary 4.27, and Chapter 5]
).
One can then argue as in
[
21
, Lemma 7.1]
to deduce that, for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta^{\prime}}}L\qquad\forall\delta,\delta^{\prime}\in(0,n-2).

∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a Ricci-flat ALE manifold and
L=L_{g_{0}}
its Lichnerowicz Laplacian.
Then, for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

(3.18)

\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-n+1}(S^{2}M,g_{0}).

If additionally

(1)

n=4
,

(2)

(M^{n},g_{0})
is Kähler, or

(3)

(M^{n},g_{0})
is spin and admits a nonzero parallel spinor,

then, for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

(3.19)

\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-n}(S^{2}M,g_{0}).

Proof.

The
-n+1
decay rate (
3.18
) was proven in
[
11
, Theorem 2.7, Remark 2.10]
.

The improved decay rate when
n=4
follows from
[
4
]
.
For spin manifolds with parallel spinor, the improved decay rate was obtained in
[
19
]
.
If
(M^{n},g_{0})
is Kähler, then it has
SU\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)
holonomy. It is therefore spin with a parallel spinor, and so the result follows from above.
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a Ricci-flat ALE manifold and
L=L_{g_{0}}
its Lichnerowicz Laplacian.

Then the following hold:

(1)

If
n\geq 5
, then

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{L^{2}}L\qquad\forall\delta\in(0,n-1],\gamma\in(0,1).

(2)

If
n\geq 5
and
(M^{n},g_{0})
is spin and admits a nonzero parallel spinor, then

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{L^{2}}L\qquad\forall\delta\in(0,n],\gamma\in(0,1).

(3)

If

, then

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{L^{2}}L\qquad\forall\delta\in(0,4],\gamma\in(0,1).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.12
,
\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-n+1}
.
If
n\geq 5
, then this fact can be combined with Lemma
3.11
(
2
)–(
3
) to deduce (
1
).

When
n\geq 5
and
(M^{n},g_{0})
is spin and admits a nonzero parallel spinor, then Proposition
3.12
yields
\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-n}
and a similar argument as above then proves (
2
).

Finally, consider the case that

.
Then Proposition
3.12
gives
\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-4}
, and Lemma
3.11
(
2
) then implies

(3.20)

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{L^{2}}L\qquad\forall\delta\in(2,4].

[
23
, Lemma 4.2]
furthermore implies

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L\subseteq\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-4}}L\qquad\forall\delta\in(0,4],

which, combined with Lemma

(
1
) and (

), thereby proves (

).
∎

3.4.
Integrable deformations

Definition 3.14
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a closed Ricci-flat manifold, let
\gamma\in(0,1)
, and let

\mathcal{F}:=\{g\in C^{2,\gamma}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\,|\,\mathcal{M}(g)=0\}.

Consider the Lichnerowicz Laplacian
L=L_{g_{0}}
as a bounded linear map
C^{2,\gamma}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\to C^{0,\gamma}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
, and denote its kernel
\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}}L
in
C^{2,\gamma}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
.
Denote the
L^{2}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
-orthogonal complement

V_{1}:=\left\{h\in C^{2,\gamma}(M,g_{0})\,\left|\,\int_{M}\langle h,e\rangle_{g_{0}}dV_{g_{0}}=0\quad\forall e\in\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}}L\right.\right\},

and let
\pi_{0}:C^{2,\gamma}=\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}}L\oplus V_{1}\to\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}}L
be the associated projection map.

We say
g_{0}
has
integrable deformations in
C^{2,\gamma}(M,g_{0})
if there exists a
C^{1}
-map
\sigma
from a neighborhood of
0\in\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}}L
onto a neighborhood of
g_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

\sigma(0)=g_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(\pi_{0}\circ d\sigma\right)_{0}=\mathbf{1}:\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}}L\to\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}}L.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*})
be a pointed Ricci-flat ALE manifold.
Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\delta\geq 0
, and

\mathcal{F}:=\{g\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0},x_{*})\,|\,\mathcal{M}(g)=0\}\qquad(\text{with the }C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}\text{-topology}).

Consider the Lichnerowicz Laplacian
L=L_{g_{0}}
as a map

4.
Exponential convergence of the Ricci flow (compact case)

Theorem 4.1

(
[
25
]
)
.

Ricci flow starting near a compact, linearly stable, Ricci-flat metric with integrable deformations converges exponentially.

Proof.

Let
V=C^{2,\alpha}
and
W=C^{\alpha}.
The result for Ricci-DeTurck flow follows from Corollary
2.5
and the calculations in §
3.1
.

To obtain the result for the Ricci flow itself, let
g_{\infty}=\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t).
Since
\mathcal{M}(g_{\infty})=0,
the argument e.g. of Deruelle-Kröncke
[

, Theorem 2.7]
implies that

is Ricci-flat and

Since

tends to zero exponentially, so does

Integrating, we obtain a convergent family

of diffeomorphisms such that

is an exponentially convergent solution of the Ricci flow.
∎

5.
Quantitative almost-orthogonality (ALE case)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*})
be a pointed Ricci-flat ALE manifold. Let
\mu\in\{n-1,n\},

\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\delta\in\left(n-\mu,n-2\right),
and

\mathcal{F}=\{g\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\,|\,\mathcal{M}(g)=0\}.

Assume
that

\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{L^{2}}L\subseteq C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}(S^{2}M,g_{0})

and

g_{0}
has integrable deformations in
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
.
Then there exist
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
-neighborhoods
U,U^{\prime}\ni g_{0}
such that any metric
g\in U
can be written as

(5.1)

g=g_{1}+h_{1},

where
g_{1}\in U^{\prime},

\mathcal{M}(g_{1})=0,
and
h_{1}\perp_{g_{0}}\ker_{L^{2}}L.

Moreover,

(5.2)

\|g_{1}-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}+\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }\|g-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\to 0

and there exists
C=C(M^{n},g_{0},\gamma,\delta)>0
such that

(5.3)

\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})}\leq C\|Lh_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}(S^{2}M,g_{0})}.

Proof.

By the integrability assumption,
\mathcal{F}\subset C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}
is a
C^{1}
-submanifold in a neighborhood
g_{0}
with
T_{g_{0}}\mathcal{F}=\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L
.
Any
e\in T_{g_{0}}\mathcal{F}=\ker_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}L=\ker_{L^{2}}L
lies in
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}(S^{2}M,g_{0}).
In particular, since
n-\mu-\delta<0,
for any
h\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
, and any
e\in T_{g_{0}}\mathcal{F}
, we have

(5.4)

\int_{M}\langle h,e\rangle dV\leq C\|h\|_{C^{0}_{-\delta}}\|e\|_{C^{0}_{-\mu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{-\delta}\rho^{-\mu}\rho^{n-1}d\rho<\infty.

We may therefore define

\mathcal{X}:=\left\{h\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\,|\,\int_{M}\langle h,e\rangle_{g_{0}}dV_{g_{0}}=0\text{ for all }e\in\ker_{L^{2}}L\right\}.

Consider the map

\Phi:\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{X}\to C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\quad\text{given by}\quad\Phi(g,h)=g+h,

which is a smooth map of Banach manifolds.
Clearly,
\Phi(g_{0},0)=g_{0}
and

d\Phi_{(g_{0},0)}:T_{g_{0}}\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{X}\to C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\text{ is }d\Phi_{(g_{0},0)}(e,h)=e+h.

T_{g_{0}}\mathcal{F}=\ker_{L^{2}}L
is finite-dimensional (see e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 2.11]
), and so there exists a finite basis, say
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
, of
T_{g_{0}}\mathcal{F}=\ker_{L^{2}}L
.
By assumption,
e_{j}\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\mu}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\cap C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.
The linearization of
\Phi
has an explicit inverse

(d\Phi_{(g_{0},0)})^{-1}:C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})\to T_{g_{0}}\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{X}\quad\text{ given by }

(d\Phi_{(g_{0},0)})^{-1}(g)=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle g,e_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(g_{0})}e_{j},\,g-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle g,e_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(g_{0})}e_{j}\right)

which is well-defined by (
5.4
).
The inverse function theorem for Banach manifolds now implies there are
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(S^{2}M,g_{0})
-neighborhoods
U,U^{\prime}\ni g_{0}
and
U^{\prime\prime}\ni 0
such that

\Phi:(\mathcal{F}\cap U^{\prime})\times(\mathcal{X}\cap U^{\prime\prime})\to U\quad\text{is a diffeomorphism}.

Thus, any
g\in U
can be written as
g=g_{1}+h_{1}
where
g_{1}\in U^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{F}
and
h_{1}\perp_{g_{0}}\ker_{L^{2}}L
, and additionally

\|g_{1}-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}+\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }\|g-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\to 0.

To obtain the estimate (
5.3
), note that
h_{1}\in\mathcal{X}
and
L:\mathcal{X}\to C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}
is bounded and injective by definition. Moreover, it is Fredholm
[
12
, Prop. 5.1]
and thus has closed image. The desired estimate then follows from the Bounded Inverse Theorem.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For
g=g_{1}+h_{1}
as in Lemma
5.1
, after possibly shrinking
U,
we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\leq C\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}},

where
C=C(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*},\gamma,\delta)>0.

Proof.

Fix
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
to be some small constant that is to be determined, and shrink
U
so that Lemma
5.1
(
5.2
) ensures

(5.6)

\|g_{1}-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}+\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}<{\varepsilon}_{0}.

Write
g(t)=g_{1}+th_{1}
for
0\leq t\leq 1.
Since
\mathcal{M}(g_{1})=0,
we have

(5.7)

\mathcal{M}(g)=\mathcal{M}(g)-\mathcal{M}(g_{1})=\int_{0}^{1}d\mathcal{M}(g(t))(h_{1})\,dt.

Referring to the expression (
3.5
), we have

(5.8)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(g)&=\int_{0}^{1}\Delta_{g(t),g_{0}}h_{1}-2\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})\#h_{1}+F(g(t))\#\nabla h_{1}+G(g(t))\#h_{1}\,dt\\
&=Lh_{1}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(g(t)^{ij}-g_{0}^{ij}\right)\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}h_{1}+F(g(t))\#\nabla h_{1}+G(g(t))\#h_{1}\,dt.\end{split}

We now estimate the three terms on the RHS of (
5.8
). We have

\|\left(g(t)^{ij}-g_{0}^{ij}\right)\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}h_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}\leq\|g(t)^{-1}-g_{0}^{-1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{0}}\|\nabla^{(2)}h_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0}\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}},

where we have used (
5.6
).
Observe from (
3.6
) and (
5.6
) that

\|F(g(t))\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-1}}\leq C\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}_{0}}\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0}

for each
0\leq t\leq 1.
By Lemma
3.4
, this gives

\|F(g(t))\#\nabla h_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}\leq\|F(g(t))\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-1}}\|h_{1}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0}\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}.

Last, from (
3.7
), we have

\|G(g(t))\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-2}}\leq C\left(\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}+\|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})\#(g(t)-g_{0})\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-2}}\right)+\|g(t)-g_{0}\|^{2}_{C^{1,\gamma}_{0}}\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0},

since
|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})|=O(\rho^{-2}).

This gives

\|G(g(t))\#h_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}\leq\|G(g(t))\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-2}}\|h_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0}\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}.

Overall, we may now conclude from (
5.8
) that

\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}\geq\|Lh_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}-C{\varepsilon}_{0}\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}.

Applying Lemma
5.1
, we obtain

\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{C}-C{\varepsilon}_{0}\right)\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}.

For
{\varepsilon}_{0}
sufficiently small, this implies the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For
\|g-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(M,g_{0})}<{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*},\gamma,\delta),
we have

(5.9)

\|\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}+\|V(g,g_{0})\|_{C^{1,\gamma}_{-\delta-1}}\leq C\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}},

where
C=C(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*},\gamma,\delta)>0.

Proof.

Lemma
5.1
implies that, when
{\varepsilon}_{0}
is sufficiently small and
\|g-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}<{\varepsilon}_{0}
, we can write
g
as
g=g_{1}+h_{1}
for some
g_{1},h_{1}\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}
with
\mathcal{M}(g_{1})=0
and
h_{1}\perp_{g_{0}}\ker_{L^{2}}L
.
Moreover, since
\delta>0
,

(5.10)

\|g_{1}-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}+\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}\leq\|g_{1}-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}+\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }{\varepsilon}_{0}\to 0.

By Deruelle-Kröncke
[
11
, Theorem 2.7]
, we know that
V(g_{1},g_{0})=0.
We can expand
V
as follows:

(5.11)

\begin{split}V(g,g_{0})=V(g,g_{0})-V(g_{1},g_{0})&=g^{ij}\left(\Gamma(g)^{k}_{ij}-\Gamma(g_{0})^{k}_{ij}\right)-g_{1}^{ij}\left(\Gamma(g_{1})^{k}_{ij}-\Gamma(g_{0})^{k}_{ij}\right)\\
&=\left(g^{ij}-g_{1}^{ij}\right)\left(\Gamma(g)^{k}_{ij}-\Gamma(g_{0})^{k}_{ij}\right)+g_{1}^{ij}\left(\Gamma(g)^{k}_{ij}-\Gamma(g_{1})^{k}_{ij}\right).\end{split}

We further have

\displaystyle\Gamma(g)^{k}_{ij}-\Gamma(g_{0})^{k}_{ij}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(g^{k\ell}-g_{0}^{k\ell}\right)\left({\partial}_{i}g_{j\ell}+{\partial}_{j}g_{i\ell}-{\partial}_{\ell}g_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}g_{0}^{k\ell}\left({\partial}_{i}(g-g_{0})_{j\ell}+{\partial}_{j}(g-g_{0})_{i\ell}-{\partial}_{\ell}(g-g_{0})_{ij}\right)

and similarly for
\Gamma(g)-\Gamma(g_{1})
,
so that

\|\Gamma(g)-\Gamma(g_{0})\|_{C^{1,\gamma}_{-1}}\leq C\|g-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0}

and

\|\Gamma(g)-\Gamma(g_{1})\|_{C^{1,\gamma}_{-\delta-1}}\leq C\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}.

Combining these estimates in (

) and using Lemmas
3.4
and

, we obtain

\|V(g,g_{0})\|_{C^{1,\gamma}_{-\delta-1}}\leq C\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\leq C\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}.

The bound on Ricci then follows from the definition (
3.1
).
∎

Theorem 5.4

(Quantitative almost-orthogonality)
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*})
be a pointed Ricci-flat ALE manifold as in Lemmas
5.1
-
5.3
.
There exists
{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*},\gamma,\delta)>0
and
C=C(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*},\gamma,\delta)>0
such that the following holds:

For all
g\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}
with

\|g-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0},

there exists
g_{1},h_{1}\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}
with
\mathcal{M}(g_{1})=0
and
h_{1}\perp_{L^{2}(g_{0})}\ker_{L^{2}}L
such that

and

(5.12)

Proof.

We have

\mathcal{M}(g)=-2\mathrm{Ric}_{g}+{\operatorname{div}}_{g}^{*}V(g,g_{0}).

Also write

\mathcal{M}_{g_{1}}(g)=-2\mathrm{Ric}_{g}+{\operatorname{div}}_{g}^{*}V(g,g_{1}).

We have

(5.13)

\mathcal{M}(g)=\mathcal{M}_{g_{1}}(g)+{\operatorname{div}}_{g}^{*}\left(V(g,g_{0})-V(g,g_{1})\right).

We calculate

V(g,g_{0})-V(g,g_{1})=\cancelto{0}{V(g_{1},g_{0})}+\left(g^{ij}-g_{1}^{ij}\right)\left(\Gamma(g_{1})^{k}_{ij}-\Gamma(g_{0})^{k}_{ij}\right),

so that by Lemma

\|V(g,g_{0})-V(g,g_{1})\|_{C^{1,\gamma}_{-\delta-1}}\leq C\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\|g_{1}-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0}\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}.

Next, by
[

, Lemma 2.5]
, we have
{\operatorname{div}}_{g_{1}}e_{1}=0.
So for any decaying vector field

we have

\begin{split}\left({\operatorname{div}}_{g}^{*}X,e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}&=\left({\operatorname{div}}_{g_{1}}^{*}X+\left({\operatorname{div}}^{*}_{g}-{\operatorname{div}}_{g_{1}}^{*}\right)X,e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}\\
&=0+\left(\left(\Gamma(g)-\Gamma(g_{1})\right)\#X,e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}.\end{split}

Using
\delta>0
, this gives the estimate

(5.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left|\left({\operatorname{div}}_{g}^{*}\left(V(g,g_{0})-V(g,g_{1})\right),e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}\right|\\
&\leq C\|\Gamma(g)-\Gamma(g_{1})\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-1}}\|V(g,g_{1})-V(g,g_{0})\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-1}}\|e_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\mu}}\int\rho^{-2(\delta+1)-\mu}\rho^{n-1}dr\\
&\leq C\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}^{2}\|g_{1}-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}\|e_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\mu}}\int\rho^{-2\delta-2-\mu+n-1}\,dr\\
&\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0}\|h_{1}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}^{2}\int\rho^{-\delta-\mu+n-1}\,dr\\
&\leq C{\varepsilon}_{0}\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|^{2}_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}\end{aligned}

where the last line follows from the fact that
\mu>n-\delta
.
Now, by the expansion (
5.8
) with
g_{1}
in place of
g_{0},
we have

\mathcal{M}_{g_{1}}(g)=L_{g_{1}}h_{1}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(g(t)^{ij}-g_{1}^{ij}\right)\nabla^{g_{1}}_{i}\nabla^{g_{1}}_{j}h_{1}+F_{g_{1}}(g(t))\#\nabla^{g_{1}}h_{1}+G_{g_{1}}(g(t))\#h_{1}\,dt.

Estimating as in the proof of Lemma
5.2
, we get

\|\mathcal{M}_{g_{1}}(g)-L_{g_{1}}h_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-2\delta-2}}\leq C\|h_{1}\|^{2}_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\leq C\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|^{2}_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}.

This gives the estimate

(5.15)

\begin{split}\left|\left(\mathcal{M}_{g_{1}}(g),e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}\right|&=\left|\left(\mathcal{M}_{g_{1}}(g)-L_{g_{1}}h_{1},e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}\right|\\
&\leq C\|\mathcal{M}_{g_{1}}(g)-L_{g_{1}}h_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-2\delta-2}}\|e_{1}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\mu}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho^{-2(\delta+1)-\mu}\rho^{n-1}\,dr\\
&\leq C\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|^{2}_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}}.\end{split}

Finally, we take the inner product with
e_{1}
in (
5.13
) and insert (
5.14
) and (
5.15
), to obtain

\left|\left(\mathcal{M}(g),e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}\right|=\left|\left(\mathcal{M}_{g_{1}}(g),e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}+\left({\operatorname{div}}_{g}^{*}\left(V(g,g_{0})-V(g,g_{1})\right),e_{1}\right)_{g_{1}}\right|\leq C\|\mathcal{M}(g)\|^{2}_{C^{0,\gamma}_{-\delta-2}},

as desired.
∎

6.1.
Norms of evolving tensors

Define

|h|^{2}=|h|^{2}_{g_{0}},\qquad|\nabla^{g_{0}}h|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}=g^{ab}g_{0}^{ik}g_{0}^{j\ell}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{a}h_{ij}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{b}h_{k\ell}.

The proof of the standard Kato inequality goes through, giving

Lemma 6.1
.

For any symmetric 2-tensor
h,
we have

|\nabla^{g_{0}}|h||_{g}\leq|\nabla^{g_{0}}h|_{g,g_{0}}.

Lemma 6.2
.

Fix
q>1.
Let
g(t)=g_{0}+h(t)
solve Ricci-DeTurck flow with respect to
g_{0},
and write
|h|_{g_{0}}=|h|
and
\nabla=\nabla^{g_{0}}.
Suppose that

(6.1)

\lambda^{-2}g_{0}\leq g\leq\lambda^{2}g_{0},

where
\lambda
is sufficiently close to
1
(depending on
n
and
q
).
Then

(6.2)

\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{g(t),g_{0}}\right)|h|^{q}+\frac{4(q-1)}{q}\left|\nabla|h|^{\frac{q}{2}}\right|_{g(t)}^{2}\leq 2q\lambda^{4}|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h|^{q}

and

(6.3)

\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{g_{0}}\right)|h|^{q}+\frac{3(q-1)}{q}\left|\nabla|h|^{\frac{q}{2}}\right|_{g_{0}}^{2}\leq 2q\lambda^{4}|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h|^{q}+\nabla_{i}\left(\left(g^{ij}-g_{0}^{ij}\right)\nabla_{j}|h|^{q}\right).

Proof.

We have

(6.4)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|h|^{2}&=2\left\langle\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)h,h\right\rangle-2|\nabla h|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}.\end{split}

For the remaining terms in the first line of (
3.4
), we can estimate

\begin{split}&\left|h_{ij}g_{0}^{im}g_{0}^{in}h_{ab}g^{ka}g_{0}^{\ell b}g_{ip}g_{0}^{pq}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{jk\ell q}\right|\\
&\quad\leq\lambda^{4}\left|h_{ij}g_{0}^{im}g_{0}^{in}h_{ab}g_{0}^{ka}g_{0}^{\ell b}(g_{0})_{ip}g_{0}^{pq}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{jk\ell q}\right|\\
&\quad\leq\lambda^{4}|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h|^{2}.\end{split}

Similar calculations with the other terms in (
3.4
) give

(6.5)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|h|^{2}&\leq-2|\nabla h|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}+4\lambda^{4}|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h|^{2}+\frac{9}{2}\lambda^{8}|h||\nabla h|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}\\
&\leq\left(-2+\frac{9}{2}\lambda^{8}|h|\right)|\nabla h|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}+4\lambda^{4}|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h|^{2},\end{split}

which is (
6.2
) with
q=2.

For general
q,
we have

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|h|^{q}&=-q(q-2)|h|^{q-2}|\nabla|h||_{g}^{2}+\frac{q}{2}|h|^{q-2}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|h|^{2}\\
&\leq q|h|^{q-2}\left((2-q)|\nabla|h||_{g}^{2}-\left(1-\frac{9}{4}\lambda^{8}|h|\right)|\nabla h|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}+2\lambda^{4}|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h|^{2}\right).\end{split}

We now assume that
\sup|h|\leq\sqrt{n}\left(\lambda^{2}-1\right)\leq\frac{2(q-1)}{9\lambda^{8}},
so that

\frac{9}{4}\lambda^{8}|h|-1\leq\frac{1}{2}(q-1)-1.

Applying the Kato inequality of Lemma
6.1
, we obtain

(6.6)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|h|^{q}&\leq q|h|^{q-2}\left((1-q)|\nabla|h||_{g}^{2}+\frac{1-q}{2}|\nabla h|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}+2\lambda^{4}|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h|^{2}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{4(1-q)}{q}\left|\nabla|h|^{\frac{q}{2}}\right|_{g}^{2}+\frac{q(1-q)}{2}|h|^{q-2}|\nabla h|^{2}_{g,g_{0}}+2q\lambda^{4}|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h|^{q}.\end{split}

This implies (
6.2
).

To prove (
6.3
), we note that

(6.7)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g,g_{0}}|h|^{q}&=\Delta_{g_{0}}|h|^{q}+(g^{ij}-g_{0}^{ij})\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}|h|^{q}\\
&=\Delta_{g_{0}}|h|^{q}+\nabla_{i}\left((g^{ij}-g_{0}^{ij})\nabla_{j}|h|^{q}\right)+g^{ik}\nabla_{i}h_{k\ell}g^{\ell j}q|h|^{q-1}\nabla_{j}|h|.\end{split}

By the Kato inequality, we can estimate the last term as

|g^{ik}\nabla_{i}h_{k\ell}g^{\ell j}q|h|^{q-1}\nabla_{j}|h||\leq q\lambda^{2}\sqrt{n}|h||h|^{q-2}|\nabla h|^{2},

which can be absorbed into the second term on the RHS of (
6.6
) if
|h|\leq\frac{q-1}{2\lambda^{2}\sqrt{n}}.

∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
q,\lambda>1
and
g(t)=g_{0}+h(t)
be as above, satisfying (
6.1
). We have

(6.8)

\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{g(t),g_{0}}\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{q}\leq C_{q,\lambda}\left(\left(1+|h|\right)|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})|+|\nabla h|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}h|\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{q}.

Proof.

From (
3.9
), we have

(6.9)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{2}&=2\left\langle\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}\right\rangle-2|\nabla\mathcal{M}|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}\\
&=2g_{0}^{im}g_{0}^{jn}\left(-2g_{0}^{ka}g_{0}^{\ell b}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})_{ik\ell j}\mathcal{M}_{ab}+F_{ij}{}^{cab}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{c}\mathcal{M}_{ab}+G_{ij}{}^{ab}\mathcal{M}_{ab}\right)\mathcal{M}_{mn}\\
&\quad-2|\nabla\mathcal{M}|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}.\end{split}

Applying Young’s inequality with
{\varepsilon}>0
to the second term, we get

(6.10)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{2}&\leq\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}{\varepsilon}}{2}-2\right)|\nabla\mathcal{M}|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}+\left(4|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})|+\frac{|F|^{2}}{2{\varepsilon}}+|G|\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{2}.\end{split}

Calculating as in the proof of Lemma

, we get

(6.11)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{q}&\leq q|\mathcal{M}|^{q-2}\left(\left(1+\frac{\lambda^{2}{\varepsilon}}{4}-q\right)|\nabla\mathcal{M}|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}+\left(4|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})|+\frac{|F|^{2}}{2{\varepsilon}}+|G|\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Taking

gives

(6.12)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{0}}\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{q}&\leq q\left(4|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})|+\frac{\lambda^{2}|F|^{2}}{8(q-1)}+|G|\right)|\mathcal{M}|^{q}.\end{split}

The identity (
6.8
) follows by inspection from (
3.6
-

).
∎

6.2.
Well-posedness

In this subsection, we prove short-time well-posedness of Ricci-DeTurck flow with respect to a Ricci-flat ALE background metric in the weighted Hölder spaces
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}
(Theorem
6.4
).
The proof essentially follows from the well-posedness of complete, bounded-curvature Ricci-DeTurck flows
[
26
,
8
]
and the Schauder estimates for weighted Hölder norms developed in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0},x_{*})
be a pointed Ricci-flat ALE manifold.
For any
\delta\geq 0
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have short-time well-posedness of
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(M,g_{0})
Ricci-DeTurck flows starting in small
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(M,g_{0},x_{*})
-neighborhoods of
g_{0}
.

In other words, there exists
{\varepsilon}_{0}\in(0,1)
and
K>0
(depending only on
M^{n},g_{0},\gamma,\delta,x_{*}
) such that if
g(0)\in C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(M,g_{0},x_{*})
with

(6.13)

\|g(0)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}(M,g_{0},x_{*})}\leq{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0},

then there is a
T>-K^{-1}\log{\varepsilon}
and a solution
(g(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
to
Ricci-DeTurck flow (
3.8
) with reference metric
g_{0}
and initial data
g(0)
such that

(6.14)

\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\leq C{\varepsilon}e^{Kt}\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Additionally, (up to restricting time intervals) this solution is unique among all complete, bounded-curvature Ricci-DeTurck flows.

Proof.

(Uniqueness)
Let
(g(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
be a Ricci-DeTurck flow as in the statement of the theorem and let
(g^{\prime}(t))_{t\in[0,T^{\prime}]}
be a complete, bounded curvature Ricci-DeTurck flow with the same initial data
g^{\prime}(0)=g(0)
.
The
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}
-bounds (
6.14
) ensure
g(t)
is also complete with bounded curvature, and
the uniqueness of complete, bounded curvature Ricci-DeTurck flows then implies
g^{\prime}(t)=g(t)
for all
t\in[0,\min(T,T^{\prime})]

[
8
]
.

(Existence)
The assumed
C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}
-estimates (
6.13
) on
g(0)-g_{0}
imply
g(0)
is complete with bounded curvature, say
|Rm_{g(0)}|_{g(0)}\leq\mathcal{K}<\infty
where
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}(M^{n},g_{0},{\varepsilon}_{0})
.
By
[
26
, Theorems 4.3 and 6.6]
,
there then exists a smooth, complete, bounded curvature Ricci-DeTurck flow
(g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
defined up to some maximal time
T>0
.
It must be the case that

\sup_{t\in[0,T)}\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}(M,g_{0})}>\sqrt{{\varepsilon}_{0}},

else
g(t)
would be complete with uniformly bounded curvature
|Rm_{g(t)}|_{g(t)}\leq\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(M^{n},g_{0},{\varepsilon}_{0})<\infty
for all
t\in[0,T)
and one could then extend the flow beyond time
T

[
26
]
.
We can therefore define
T_{0}
to be

T_{0}:=\inf\left\{t\in[0,T)\,\colon\,\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}\geq\sqrt{{\varepsilon}_{0}}\right\},

and observe, using also the initial data bounds (
6.13
), that

(6.15)

T_{0}\in(0,T)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}=\sqrt{{\varepsilon}_{0}}.

Fix
q>1.
It is easy to see (as in the proof of Lemma
6.9
) that with the
C^{2,\gamma}_{0}
-bound in (
6.15
), the function

\frac{e^{qKt}(C_{n}{\varepsilon})^{q}}{\rho(x)^{\delta q}}

is a supersolution for the evolution (
6.8
) of
|\mathcal{M}|^{q}
(where
K=K(M^{n},g_{0},\gamma,\delta,x_{*})>0
is independent of
{\varepsilon}
).
From the comparison principle and the initial-data bounds (
6.13
), we learn that

|\mathcal{M}(x,t)|\leq\frac{C_{n}e^{Kt}{\varepsilon}}{\rho(x)^{\delta}}\qquad\forall t\in[0,T_{0}].

Integrating this estimate in time, we obtain the

\|g(t)-g(0)\|_{C^{0}_{-\delta}}\leq\frac{\left(e^{Kt}-1\right)C_{n}{\varepsilon}}{K}\qquad\forall t\in[0,T_{0}].

Applying Lemma
A.3
with the given

it follows that

\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{-\delta}}\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}(e^{Kt}-1)}{K}+{\varepsilon}\leq C^{\prime}{\varepsilon}e^{Kt}\qquad\forall t\in[0,T_{0}]

where

.
Combining this estimate with the fact that
\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}_{0}}=\sqrt{{\varepsilon}_{0}}\geq\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}

), it follows that
T_{0}>-K^{\prime}\log{\varepsilon}
for some

.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.4.
Comparison principle

In this subsection, we begin by focusing on the subset of Euclidean space
(0,\infty)\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
with the standard coordinate
r
.
For a real
\mu\geq 1,
we write

\Delta_{\mu}:={\partial}_{r}^{2}+\frac{(\mu-1)}{r}{\partial}_{r}.

We will construct radial barriers for small perturbations of the equation
\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{\mu}\right)f=0.
Together with the above Liouville theorem, this will be the basis for our proof of stability in weighted Hölder spaces. For
L^{p}
-spaces, we use an alternative heat-kernel approach in §
8
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Given
a,b>0,
define “Kummer’s confluent hypergeometric function”

\chi(u)=\chi_{a,b}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}e^{-ux}\,dx.

The function
\chi(u)
is smooth, positive, strictly decreasing, and concave-up for
u\geq 0,
and satisfies the ODE

(6.17)

u\chi^{\prime\prime}(u)+\left(a+b+u\right)\chi^{\prime}(u)+a\chi(u)=0.

We have

(6.18)

\chi(u)=\Gamma(a)u^{-a}+O(u^{-a-1})\quad(u\to\infty),

where
\Gamma
is the Euler Gamma function, as well as

(6.19)

\frac{\chi(0)}{1+C_{a,b}u^{a}}\leq\chi(u)\leq\frac{\chi(0)}{1+c_{a,b}u^{a}},

for approriate constants
C_{a,b},c_{a,b}>0,
and

(6.20)

c_{a,b}\chi(u)\leq-(1+u)\chi^{\prime}(u)\leq C_{a,b}\chi(u),

(6.21)

c_{a,b}\chi(u)\leq(1+u^{2})\chi^{\prime\prime}(u)\leq C_{a,b}\chi(u).

Proof.

We have

(6.22)

\chi^{\prime}(u)=-\int_{0}^{1}x^{a}(1-x)^{b-1}e^{-ux}\,dx,\quad\chi^{\prime\prime}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}x^{a+1}(1-x)^{b-1}e^{-ux}\,dx.

Using integration by parts, we have

\begin{split}-\chi^{\prime}(u)&=\cancelto{0}{\left.x^{a}e^{-ux}\left(\frac{-(1-x)^{b}}{b}\right)\right|_{0}^{1}}+\frac{1}{b}\int_{0}^{1}\left(ax^{a-1}e^{-ux}-ux^{a}e^{-ux}\right)(1-x)(1-x)^{b-1}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{b}\left(a\chi(u)+(a+u)\chi^{\prime}(u)+u\chi^{\prime\prime}(u)\right).\end{split}

Rearranging gives the ODE (
6.17
). The formula (
6.18
) follows by changing variables and, in view of (
6.22
), clearly implies (
6.19
-
6.21
).
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

Fix
\nu\geq 1
and

The function

(6.27)

\left|\frac{{\partial}^{2}F}{{\partial}^{2}r}\right|\leq\frac{C_{k,\nu}F(r,t)}{r^{2}+t}.

Further, for
\mu\geq\nu,
we have

(6.28)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mu}-\frac{c_{k,\nu}(\mu-\nu)}{r^{2}+t}\right)F(r,t)\geq 0

Proof.

For a general function
g(x),
we have

(6.29)

\Delta_{\nu}t^{-\frac{k}{2}}g\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\right)=t^{-\frac{k}{2}-1}\left(g^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\right)+\frac{\nu-1}{r/\sqrt{t}}g^{\prime}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right)

u=\frac{v^{2}}{4}
and take

Observe that

We obtain

-
6.21
).
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Suppose
\nu>2

Proof.

For

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}r}(r^{2}+t+1)^{-\frac{a}{2}}=\frac{-ar}{(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{a}{2}+1}},

\begin{split}\frac{{\partial}^{2}}{{\partial}r^{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{-\frac{a}{2}}&=\frac{a\left((a+2)r^{2}-(r^{2}+t+1)\right)}{(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{a}{2}+2}}=\frac{a\left((a+1)r^{2}-(t+1)\right)}{(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{a}{2}+2}},\end{split}

\begin{split}\Delta_{\mu}(r^{2}+t+1)^{-\frac{a}{2}}&=\frac{a\left((a+1)r^{2}-(t+1)-(\mu-1)(r^{2}+t+1)\right)}{(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{a}{2}+2}}=\frac{a\left((a+2-\mu)r^{2}-\mu(t+1)\right)}{(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{a}{2}+2}},\end{split}

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}r}(r^{2}+1)^{-\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{-\frac{k-\ell}{2}}=\frac{-r}{(r^{2}+1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}}\left(\frac{\ell}{r^{2}+1}+\frac{k-\ell}{r^{2}+t+1}\right),

\frac{{\partial}^{2}}{{\partial}r^{2}}(r^{2}+1)^{-\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{-\frac{k-\ell}{2}}=\frac{\frac{\ell\left((\ell+1)r^{2}-1\right)}{(r^{2}+1)^{2}}+\frac{(k-\ell)\left((k-\ell+1)r^{2}-(t+1)\right)}{(r^{2}+t+1)^{2}}+\frac{2\ell(k-\ell)r^{2}}{(r^{2}+1)(r^{2}+t+1)}}{(r^{2}+1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}}

\Delta_{\mu}(r^{2}+1)^{-\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{-\frac{k-\ell}{2}}=\frac{\frac{\ell\left((\ell+2-\mu)r^{2}-\mu\right)}{(r^{2}+1)^{2}}+\frac{(k-\ell)\left((k-\ell+2-\mu)r^{2}-\mu(t+1)\right)}{(r^{2}+t+1)^{2}}+\frac{2\ell(k-\ell)r^{2}}{(r^{2}+1)(r^{2}+t+1)}}{(r^{2}+1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}}

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mu}\right)(r^{2}+1)^{-\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{-\frac{k-\ell}{2}}=-\frac{\frac{\ell\left((\ell+2-\mu)r^{2}-\mu\right)}{(r^{2}+1)^{2}}+\frac{(k-\ell)\left((k-\ell+\frac{5}{2}-\mu)r^{2}-\left(\mu-\frac{1}{2}\right)(t+1)\right)}{(r^{2}+t+1)^{2}}+\frac{2\ell(k-\ell)r^{2}}{(r^{2}+1)(r^{2}+t+1)}}{(r^{2}+1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}}.

Since

0<\ell<\nu-2<\mu-2,

the first term on the right-hand side dominates the other two as long as

We obtain

for

and

for general
(r,t).

Next, since

(

Combining these, we have for

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mu}\right)G(r,t)&\geq\frac{d_{k,\ell,\nu}}{r^{2}+1}\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}}{(r^{2}+1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}}>\frac{d^{\prime}_{k,\ell,\nu}{\varepsilon}^{2}}{r^{2}+1}G\end{split}

by (

we have

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mu}\right)G(r,t)&\geq-\frac{E_{k,\ell,\nu}}{r^{2}+1}\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}}{(r^{2}+1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}(r^{2}+t+1)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}}+\frac{c_{k,\nu}{\varepsilon}}{r^{2}+t+1}F_{k,v}(r,t+1)\\

Lemma 7.4
.

The DeTurck vector field
V=V(g,g_{0})
satisfies

-\Delta_{g}|V|_{g}\leq|\mathrm{Ric}(g)||V|_{g}+\left|\left({\operatorname{div}}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla{\operatorname{tr}}\right)\mathcal{M}\right|.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\mathrm{Ric}(g)={\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{1}{2}V}g-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}.

Taking the trace, we get

R_{g}={\operatorname{div}}V-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{tr}}_{g}\mathcal{M}.

Also note that

\begin{split}{\operatorname{div}}{\mathcal{L}}_{V}g&=\nabla^{i}\nabla_{i}V_{j}+\nabla^{i}\nabla_{j}V_{i}\\
&=\Delta V+\mathrm{Ric}(g)(V)+\nabla{\operatorname{div}}V\\
&=\Delta V+\mathrm{Ric}(g)(V)+\nabla\left(R_{g}+\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{tr}}_{g}\mathcal{M}\right).\end{split}

Now, the contracted Bianchi identity reads

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\nabla R={\operatorname{div}}\mathrm{Ric}&={\operatorname{div}}\left({\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{1}{2}V}g-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta V+\mathrm{Ric}(g)(V)+\nabla R+\frac{1}{2}\nabla{\operatorname{tr}}_{g}\mathcal{M}\right)-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{div}}\mathcal{M}.\end{split}

Canceling
\frac{1}{2}\nabla R
from both sides, we have

(7.25)

\Delta V+\mathrm{Ric}(g)(V)=\left({\operatorname{div}}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla{\operatorname{tr}}\right)\mathcal{M}.

We can now get the result by applying the standard Kato inequality.
∎

Lemma 7.5

Proposition 7.6

Let

Appendix B
L^{p}
-estimates for the scalar heat kernel on an ALE space

Lemma B.1
.

Let
a\geq 1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
with
a<\frac{n}{\beta}.
We have

(B.1)

\left\|\frac{d(x,\cdot)^{2\alpha}H(x,\cdot,t)}{t^{\alpha}\rho(\cdot)^{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{a}}\leq\frac{C_{g_{0},a,\alpha,\beta}}{t^{\frac{n(a-1)}{2a}}\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}.

Proof.

Applying (
8.1
), we may write

(B.2)

\begin{split}\left\|\frac{d(x,\cdot)^{2\alpha}H(x,\cdot,t)}{t^{\alpha}\rho(\cdot)^{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{a}}^{a}&\leq C\int_{M}\frac{d(x,y)^{2a\alpha}e^{-acd(x,y)^{2}/t}}{t^{a\left(\frac{n}{2}+\alpha\right)}\rho(y)^{a\beta}}\,dV_{y}\\
&=\frac{C}{t^{\frac{n(a-1)}{2}}}\int_{M}\frac{d(x,y)^{2a\alpha}e^{-ad(x,y)^{2}/4t}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}\rho(y)^{a\beta}}\,dV_{y}\end{split}

We split the last integral into two pieces:

(B.3)

\begin{split}\int_{M}\frac{d(x,y)^{2a\alpha}e^{-acd(x,y)^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}\rho(y)^{a\beta}}\,dV_{y}&=\left(\int_{\left\{\rho(y)\leq\frac{\rho(x)}{2}\right\}}+\int_{\left\{\rho(y)\geq\frac{\rho(x)}{2}\right\}}\right)\frac{d(x,y)^{2a\alpha}e^{-acd(x,y)^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}\rho(y)^{a\beta}}\,dV_{y}\\
&=:I+II.\end{split}

For
\rho(y)\leq\frac{\rho(x)}{2},
we have

\rho(x)\leq d(x,y)+\rho(y)\leq d(x,y)+\frac{\rho(x)}{2}

which gives

\frac{1}{2}\rho(x)\leq d(x,y)\leq\frac{3}{2}\rho(x).

This gives

(B.4)

\begin{split}I&\leq Ce^{-a\rho(x)^{2}/16t}\left(\frac{\rho(x)^{2a\alpha}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}}\right)\int_{\left\{\rho(y)\leq\frac{\rho(x)}{2}\right\}}\frac{dV_{y}}{\rho(y)^{a\beta}}\\
&\leq Ce^{-a\rho(x)^{2}/16t}\left(\frac{\rho(x)^{2}}{t}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}\rho(x)^{-a\beta},\end{split}

where we have used
n-1-a\beta>-1.

For
\rho(y)\geq\frac{\rho(x)}{2},
we have

d(x,y)\leq\rho(x)+\rho(y)\leq 3\rho(y)

and

\frac{1}{\rho(y)}\leq\frac{5}{d(x,y)+\rho(x)}.

This gives

\begin{split}II&\leq C\int_{\left\{\rho(y)\geq\frac{\rho(x)}{2}\right\}}\frac{d(x,y)^{2a\alpha}e^{-acd(x,y)^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}\left(d(x,y)+\rho(x)\right)^{a\beta}}\,dV_{y}.\end{split}

Using polar coordinates
y=\exp_{x}(r\theta)
centered at
x
and Bishop-Gromov volume comparison, we can estimate this from above

\displaystyle II\leq{}

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2a\alpha}e^{-car^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}(r+\rho(x))^{a\beta}}r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\rho(x)^{a\beta}}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)}\frac{r^{2a\alpha}e^{-car^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}}r^{n-1}dr+\frac{C}{t^{a\beta/2}}\int_{\rho(x)}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2a\alpha}e^{-car^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}+a\alpha}(r/\sqrt{t})^{a\beta}}r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\rho(x)^{a\beta}}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)/\sqrt{t}}u^{2a\alpha+n-1}e^{-cau^{2}}du+\frac{C}{t^{a\beta/2}}\int_{\rho(x)/\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}u^{2a\alpha-a\beta+n-1}e^{-cau^{2}}du

\displaystyle(u=r/\sqrt{t})

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\rho(x)^{a\beta}}\frac{(\rho(x)/\sqrt{t})^{2a\alpha+n}}{(\rho(x)/\sqrt{t})^{2a\alpha+n}+1}+\frac{C}{t^{a\beta/2}}e^{-ca\rho(x)^{2}/t},

where we have used
2a\alpha-a\beta+n-1>-1
and
c
may have decreased in the last line.

This simplifies to

(B.5)

II\leq\frac{C}{\rho(x)^{a\beta}}\left(\frac{(\rho(x)/\sqrt{t})^{2a\alpha+n}}{(\rho(x)/\sqrt{t})^{2a\alpha+n}+1}+\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{a\beta}e^{-ca\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{2}}\right).

Combining (
B.4
) and (
B.5
) and using that
a\beta\leq 2a\alpha+n
gives

(B.6)

I+II\\
\leq C\rho(x)^{-a\beta}\left[e^{-ca\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{2a\alpha+n}+\frac{(\rho(x)/\sqrt{t})^{2a\alpha+n}}{(\rho(x)/\sqrt{t})^{2a\alpha+n}+1}+\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{a\beta}e^{-ca\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{2}}\right]\\
\leq C\rho(x)^{-a\beta}\frac{(\rho(x)/\sqrt{t})^{a\beta}}{(\rho(x)/\sqrt{t})^{a\beta}+1}.

Substituting this inequality back into (
B.2
), we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\frac{d(x,\cdot)^{2\alpha}H(x,\cdot,t)}{t^{\alpha}\rho(\cdot)^{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{a}}&\leq\frac{C}{t^{\frac{n(a-1)}{2a}}\rho(x)^{\beta}}\left(\frac{\rho(x)^{2}}{\rho(x)^{2}+t}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{C}{t^{\frac{n(a-1)}{2a}}\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}},\end{split}

which is (
B.1
).
∎

R\geq R_{0},
we have

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{M}\frac{d(\cdot,y)^{2\alpha}H(\cdot,y,t)f(y)}{t^{\alpha}\rho(y)^{\beta}}\,dV_{y}\right\|_{L^{c}(M\setminus B_{R})}&\leq\frac{C_{g_{0},a,b,\alpha,\beta}}{t^{\frac{n(a-1)}{2a}}\left(R^{2}+t\right)^{\frac{(c-a)\beta}{2c}}}\|f\|_{L^{b}(M)}.\end{split}

(c) For
b\geq 1
and
1\leq R\leq\infty,
we have

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{B_{R}}H(\cdot,y,t)f(y)\,dV_{y}\right\|_{L^{b}(M)}&\leq\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{C_{g_{0},b}R^{2}}{t}\right)^{\frac{n(b-1)}{2b}}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{b}(M)}.\end{split}

Proof.

The estimate of (
a
) follows by applying Hölder’s inequality and (
B.1
) to the expression.

Next, note that given
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset M,

[
2
, Proposition 3.64]
gives

(B.7)

\left\|\int_{\Omega_{2}}F(\cdot,y)f(y)\,dV_{y}\right\|_{L^{c}(\Omega_{1})}\\
\leq\left(\sup_{x\in\Omega_{1}}\|F(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{a}(\Omega_{2})}\right)^{\frac{c-a}{c}}\left(\sup_{y\in\Omega_{2}}\|F(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{a}(\Omega_{1})}\right)^{\frac{a}{c}}\|f\|_{L^{b}(\Omega_{2})}.

For (
b
), take
\Omega_{1}=M\setminus B_{R},

\Omega_{2}=M,
and
F(x,y)=\frac{d(x,y)^{2\alpha}H(x,y,t)}{t^{\alpha}\rho(y)^{\beta}}.
Then (
B.1
) gives the desired result.

For (
c
), take
a=1,

b=c,

\Omega_{1}=M,
and
\Omega_{2}=B_{R}.
Note that
\|H(x,\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\leq\|H(x,\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(M)}\equiv 1.
For
t\geq R^{2},
note that

\|H(x,\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\leq\frac{CR^{n}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}}}.

Therefore (
B.7
) gives the result.
∎

L^{p}
-estimates along Ricci-DeTurck flow

C.1.
Estimate with
\frac{n}{n-2}<p<n

Proposition C.1
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a Ricci-flat ALE space of order
\tau\geq\tau_{0},
where
0<\tau_{0}<n-2.
Fix
\frac{n}{n-2}<p\leq\infty
and suppose that there exists
1<q<\min\{p,\frac{n}{2}\}
such that

(C.1)

\begin{split}\left(1-\frac{q}{p}\right)\left(2+\tau_{0}\right)&>2.\end{split}

Also let
\ell>0
and
0<r<\min\{p-q,2\}
be such that

(C.2)

q\ell+1<n

and

(C.3)

\ell<\frac{n(p-r)}{(2-r)p}.

Define
v(x,t)
as in (
8.27
). Let
t_{1}\geq 1
and
0<{\varepsilon}<\lambda-1,
where
\lambda
is sufficiently close to one as in Lemma
6.2
. Suppose that
h
solves the Ricci-DeTurck flow with

(C.4)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq{\varepsilon},

(C.5)

\sup_{t_{1}-1\leq t\leq t_{1}}|\nabla h(x,t)|\leq{\varepsilon}v(x,t_{1}),

for
x\in M,
and

(C.6)

|h(x,t)|\leq{\varepsilon}v(x,t)^{\ell}

for
x\in M
and
0\leq t\leq t_{1}.

We then have

(C.7)

\begin{split}|h|(x,t_{1})&\leq C\left(t_{1}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\|h(0)\|_{L^{p}}+{\varepsilon}\rho(x)^{-\min\left\{\frac{2}{q^{2}}+\frac{\tau}{q},\frac{n}{p}+\frac{\tau}{q},\frac{n}{q^{2}}-\frac{2}{q}\right\}}\right)\\
&\qquad+C\left({\varepsilon}^{1+\frac{1}{q}}v(x,t_{1})^{\ell+\frac{1}{q}}+{\varepsilon}^{1+\frac{2}{q}}v(x,t_{1})^{\min\left\{\ell\left(1+\frac{1}{q}\right),\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{pq}\right\}}\right),\end{split}

and, for
R\geq 1,

(C.8)

\begin{split}\|h(t_{1})\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}&\leq\|h(0)\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}+\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{CR^{2}}{t_{1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\right\}\|h(0)\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\\
&+C{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{R^{\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\left(2+\tau_{0}\right)-\frac{2}{q}}}+{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{R^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{1}{t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2q}}}\right)+{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{R^{\frac{\left(p-q-r\right)\ell}{q(p-r)}}}+\frac{1}{t_{1}^{\frac{\min\left\{1+\frac{nr}{2p},\frac{(2-r)\ell}{2}\right\}}{q}}}\right)\right).\end{split}

Proof.

In the formula of Lemma
8.2
, we break up the right-hand side as follows:

(C.9)

\begin{split}u_{1}^{q}(x)&:=\int_{M}H(x,y,t_{1})|h|^{q}(y,0)\,dV_{y}\\
u_{2}^{q}(x)&:=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\!\!\!\!\int_{M}H(x,y,t_{1}-t)|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||h(y,t)|^{q}\,dV_{y}dt\\
u_{3}^{q}(x)&:=\int_{t_{1}-1}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{M}H(x,y,t_{1}-t)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{1}-t}}+\frac{d(x,y)}{t_{1}-t}\right)|h|^{\frac{q}{2}+1}|\nabla|h|^{\frac{q}{2}}|\,dV_{y}dt\\
u_{4}^{q}(x)&:=\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\!\!\!\!\int_{M}H(x,y,t_{1}-t)\left(\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{1}-t}}+\frac{d(x,y)}{t_{1}-t}\right)|h|^{\frac{q}{2}+1}|\nabla|h|^{\frac{q}{2}}|-c_{q}|\nabla|h|^{\frac{q}{2}}|^{2}\right)\,dV_{y}dt.\end{split}

According to Lemma
8.2
, we have

(C.10)

|h|(x,t_{1})\leq u_{1}(x)+C\left(u_{2}(x)+u_{3}(x)+u_{4}(x)\right).

We now use Lemma
B.2
to estimate
u_{i}
pointwise and in
L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})
for
i=1,\ldots,4.

First term.

Pointwise estimate.
Taking
\alpha=\beta=0,

b=\frac{p}{q}
and
a=\frac{1}{1-\frac{q}{p}}=\frac{p}{p-q}
in Lemma
B.2
a,
we have

u^{q}_{1}(x)\leq\frac{C}{t_{1}^{\frac{nq}{2p}}}\||h(0)|^{q}\|_{L^{p/q}}=\frac{C}{t_{1}^{\frac{nq}{2p}}}\||h(0)|\|^{q}_{L^{p}}.

This gives

(C.11)

u_{1}(x)\leq\frac{C}{t_{1}^{\frac{n}{2p}}}\|h(0)\|_{L^{p}}.

L^{p}
-estimate.
Taking
b=\frac{p}{q}
in Lemma
B.2
c
, we have

(C.12)

\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p}{(M)}}\leq\|h(0)\|_{L^{p}{(M)}}.

If
t_{1}\geq R^{2},
we further break up this term as

u_{1}(x)\leq u_{1}^{\prime}(x)+u_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x),

where

u_{1}^{\prime}(x):=\sqrt[q]{\int_{M\setminus B_{R}}H(x,y,t_{1})|h|^{q}(y,0)\,dV_{y}}

and

u_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x):=\sqrt[q]{\int_{B_{R}}H(x,y,t_{1})|h|^{q}(y,0)\,dV_{y}}.

To estimate
u_{1}^{\prime}(x),
by the same argument as for
u_{1}
, we have

\|u_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq\|h(0)\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}.

For

B.2
c

\|u_{1}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q}\leq\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{CR^{2}}{t_{1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}\left(1-\frac{q}{p}\right)}\right\}\|h(0)\|_{L^{p}{(B_{R})}}^{q}.

Taking
q
-th roots, we have

(C.13)

\|u_{1}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{CR^{2}}{t_{1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\right\}\|h(0)\|_{L^{p}{(B_{R})}}.

Second term.

Pointwise estimate.
Note that since
(M,g_{0})
is Ricci-flat ALE of order
\tau>0,
Lemma
3.2
implies

u^{q}_{2}(x)\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\!\!\!\!\int_{M}\frac{H(x,y,t_{1}-t)}{\rho(y)^{2+\tau}}|h(y,t)|^{q}\,dV_{y}dt.

Let

f_{2}(x,t)=\int_{M}\frac{H(x,y,t_{1}-t)}{\rho(y)^{2+\tau}}|h(y,t)|^{q}\,dV_{y}.

Case 1:

\frac{nq}{2}<p.
Applying Lemma
B.2
a
with
b=\frac{p}{q}
and
a=\frac{p}{p-q},
and

(C.14)

\beta=\min\left\{2+\tau,\frac{n(p-q)}{qp}\right\}<\frac{n}{a},

(C.15)

|f_{2}(x,t)|\leq\frac{C\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}}^{q}}{\left(t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{nq}{2p}}\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}.

We have

(C.16)

\frac{nq}{2p}+\frac{\beta}{2}=\frac{n}{2p}\left(\frac{p}{q}+q-1\right)>\frac{n}{2q}>1\qquad\text{when }\beta=\frac{n(p-q)}{qp}

(C.17)

\frac{nq}{2p}+\frac{\beta}{2}=\frac{nq}{2p}+1+\frac{\tau}{2}>1\qquad\text{when }\beta=2+\tau.

In either case, using this inside the time integral, we have

\begin{split}u_{2}^{q}(x)\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|f_{2}(x,t)|\,dt&\leq C\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\frac{1}{\left(t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{nq}{2p}}\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}\,dt\\
&\leq C\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\left(\frac{1}{\rho(x)^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}u^{-\frac{nq}{2p}}\,du+\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}u^{-\left(\frac{nq}{2p}+\frac{\beta}{2}\right)}\,du\right)\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\rho(x)^{\frac{nq}{p}+\beta-2}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}}^{q}.\end{split}

Here we have used
\frac{nq}{2p}<1
and
\frac{nq}{2p}+\frac{\beta}{2}>1.

Taking
q
-th roots, we get

(C.18)

\begin{split}u_{2}(x)\leq\frac{C}{\rho(x)^{\frac{n}{p}+\frac{\beta-2}{q}}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}}.\end{split}

On the other hand,

Therefore (

\begin{split}u_{2}(x)\leq\frac{C}{\rho(x)^{\min\{\frac{n}{p}+\frac{\tau}{q},\frac{n}{q^{2}}-\frac{2}{q}\}}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}}.\end{split}

Case 2:

\frac{nq}{2}\geq p.

&\leq\frac{C}{t_{1}^{\min\{1+\frac{n}{2p},\frac{\ell}{2}\left(q+1\right)\}}}.\end{split}

C.28
) and taking

-th roots, we get

(C.30)

u_{4}(x)\leq C{\varepsilon}^{1+\frac{2}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho(x)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{1}}}\right)^{\min\left\{\ell\left(1+\frac{1}{q}\right),\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{pq}\right\}}.

-estimate.

\begin{split}f_{4}(x,t)&\leq{\varepsilon}^{2-r}\int_{M}\left(1+\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t_{1}-t}\right)H(x,y,t_{1}-t)\left(\frac{1}{\rho(y)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t}}\right)^{(2-r)\ell}|h(y,t)|^{q+r}\,dV_{y}\\

Applying Minkowski’s inequality, we have

(C.31)

\begin{split}\|u_{4}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}=\|u_{4}^{q}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}&\leq\left(\int_{M\setminus B_{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\frac{f_{4}(x,t)}{t_{1}-t}dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dV_{x}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\|f_{4}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}\,\frac{dt}{t_{1}-t}\\
&\leq C{\varepsilon}^{2-r}\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\left(\|f_{4}^{\prime}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}+\|f_{4}^{\prime\prime}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t_{1}-t},\end{split}

where

f^{\prime}_{4}(x,t)=\int_{M}\left(1+\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t_{1}-t}\right)\frac{H(x,y,t_{1}-t)}{\rho(y)^{(2-r)\ell}}|h(y,t)|^{q+r}\,dV_{y}

and

f^{\prime\prime}_{4}(x,t)=\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}\int_{M}\left(1+\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t_{1}-t}\right)H(x,y,t_{1}-t)|h(y,t)|^{q+r}\,dV_{y}.

For
f_{4}^{\prime},
we apply Lemma
B.2
b
with
\alpha=1,\beta=\ell,

c=\frac{p}{q},

b=\frac{p}{q+r},
and
a=\frac{p}{p-r},
to get

\|f_{4}^{\prime}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}\leq\frac{C\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q+r}}{(t_{1}-t)^{\frac{nr}{2p}}\left(R^{2}+t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{\left(p-q-r\right)\ell}{2(p-r)}}}.

Here (
C.3
) guarantees
\beta<\frac{n}{a},
which allows us to apply Lemma
B.2
b
.

For
f_{4}^{\prime\prime},
we apply Lemma
B.2
b
with
\alpha=1,\beta=0,
and the same
a,b,c,
to get

\|f_{3}^{\prime\prime}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}\leq\frac{C\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q+r}}{(1+t)^{\frac{(2-r)\ell}{2}}(t_{1}-t)^{\frac{nr}{2p}}}.

We can estimate

\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\|f_{4}^{\prime}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}\,\frac{dt}{t_{1}-t}\leq\frac{C\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}-1}\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q+r}}{R^{\frac{\left(p-q-r\right)\ell}{(p-r)}}}

and, as in (
C.28
-
C.29
),

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\|f_{4}^{\prime\prime}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}\,\frac{dt}{t_{1}-t}&\leq C\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}-1}\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q+r}\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{(1+t)^{\frac{(2-r)\ell}{2}}(t_{1}-t)^{1+\frac{nr}{2p}}}\\
&\leq\frac{C\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}-1}\|h(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q+r}}{t_{1}^{\min\{1+\frac{nr}{2p},\frac{(2-r)\ell}{2}\}}}.\end{split}

Returning to (
C.31
) and taking
q
-th roots, we get

(C.32)

\begin{split}\|u_{4}\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}&\leq C{\varepsilon}^{1+\frac{2}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{R^{\frac{\left(p-q-r\right)\ell}{q(p-r)}}}+\frac{1}{t_{1}^{\frac{\min\{1+\frac{nr}{2p},\frac{(2-r)\ell}{2}\}}{q}}}\right).\end{split}

Finally, by (
C.10
), the pointwise estimate (
C.7
) follows by combining (
C.11
), (
C.18
), (
C.24
), and (
C.30
). The

-estimate (

) follows by combining (

), (

), (

), and (

).
∎

C.2.
Estimate with
p>1
for a convergent solution

We need the following variant of Lemma
8.2
.

Lemma C.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a Ricci-flat ALE manifold and let
g_{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}
be a metric with
\mathcal{M}(g_{\infty})=0
and

(C.33)

\|g_{\infty}-g_{0}\|_{C^{1,0}_{-n}}<{\varepsilon}.

Fix
q>1.
Let
g(t)
solve Ricci-DeTurck flow with respect to
g_{0},
and write

\tilde{h}(t)=g(t)-g_{\infty}.

Also write

|\tilde{h}|=|\tilde{h}|_{g_{\infty}}
and
\nabla=\nabla^{g_{\infty}}.
Let
\tilde{H}(x,y,t)
be the heat kernel with respect to
g_{\infty}.
Suppose that (
6.1
) is satisfied, where
\lambda
is sufficiently close to one (depending only on
n,q
).
Then

\begin{split}|\tilde{h}|^{q}(x,t)&\leq\int_{M}\tilde{H}(x,y,t)|\tilde{h}|^{q}(y,0)\,dV_{y}\\
&+C_{\ref {lemma:smallpDuhamel}}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\!\int_{M}\tilde{H}(x,y,t-s)\left(\rho(x)^{-2-\tau}|\tilde{h}|^{q}+\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-s}}+\frac{d(x,y)}{t-s}\right)|\tilde{h}|^{\frac{q}{2}+1}|\nabla|\tilde{h}|^{\frac{q}{2}}|-c_{\ref {lemma:smallpDuhamel}}|\nabla|\tilde{h}|^{\frac{q}{2}}|^{2}\right)\,dV_{y}ds.\end{split}

Here
C_{\ref {lemma:smallpDuhamel}},c_{\ref {lemma:smallpDuhamel}}
are constants that can be taken to depend only on
M,g_{0},q
.

Proof.

Observe that

(C.34)

\begin{split}V(g,g_{\infty})-V(g,g_{0})&=g^{-1}\left(\Gamma(g_{0})-\Gamma(g_{\infty})\right)\\
&=\cancelto{0}{g_{\infty}^{-1}\left(\Gamma(g_{0})-\Gamma(g_{\infty})\right)}+\tilde{h}\#\left(\Gamma(g_{0})-\Gamma(g_{\infty})\right)\\
&={\varepsilon}O(\rho(x)^{-\tau-1})\#\tilde{h}.\end{split}

Here we have used the fact that
V(g_{0},g_{\infty})=0.

We therefore have

\begin{split}\frac{{\partial}\tilde{h}}{{\partial}t}=\frac{{\partial}g}{{\partial}t}&=\mathcal{M}(g)\\
&=\mathcal{M}_{g_{\infty}}(g)+{\mathscr{L}}_{V(g,g_{0})-V(g,g_{\infty})}g\\
&=\mathcal{M}_{g_{\infty}}(g)+{\varepsilon}\left(O(\rho(x)^{-\tau-2})\#\tilde{h}+O(\rho(x)^{-\tau-1})\#\nabla\tilde{h}\right).\end{split}

Arguing as in (
6.5
), we have

(C.35)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{\infty}}\right)|\tilde{h}|_{g_{\infty}}^{2}&\leq-2|\nabla\tilde{h}|_{g,g_{\infty}}^{2}+4\lambda^{4}\left((1+{\varepsilon})O(\rho(x)^{-2-\tau})\right)|\tilde{h}|^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{9}{2}\lambda^{8}|\tilde{h}||\nabla\tilde{h}|_{g,g_{i}nfty}^{2}+{\varepsilon}O(\rho(x)^{-1-\tau})|\tilde{h}||\nabla\tilde{h}|.\end{split}

Applying Young’s inequality, we have

(C.36)

\begin{split}{\varepsilon}O(\rho(x)^{-1-\tau})|\tilde{h}||\nabla\tilde{h}|\leq O(\rho(x)^{-2-\tau})+{\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{h}|^{2}.\end{split}

Returning to (
C.35
), we have

(C.37)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{g,g_{\infty}}\right)|\tilde{h}|_{g_{\infty}}^{2}&\leq\left(-2+\frac{9}{2}\lambda^{8}|\tilde{h}|+{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)|\nabla\tilde{h}|_{g,g_{0}}^{2}+4\lambda^{4}O(\rho(x)^{-2-\tau})|\tilde{h}|^{2},\end{split}

The formula follows by redoing the argument of Lemma
6.2
and using Duhamel’s principle as in Lemma
8.3
, with
g_{\infty}
in place of
g_{0}.

∎

Proposition C.3
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a Ricci-flat ALE space of dimension and order
\tau=n\geq 4.
Let
1<q<p
and
\frac{2}{q}<m<n-2.
Let
t_{1}\geq 1
and
0<{\varepsilon}<\lambda-1,
where
\lambda
is sufficiently close to one as in Lemma
6.2
.

Assume that
g(t)
solves the Ricci-DeTurck flow with respect to
g_{0}.
Suppose further that there exists a gauged Ricci-flat metric
g_{\infty}
satisfying (
C.33
) such that, writing

\tilde{h}(t)=g(t)-g_{\infty},

we have

(C.38)

\sup_{0\leq t<t_{1}}\|\tilde{h}(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq{\varepsilon}

and

(C.39)

|\tilde{h}(t)|\leq\frac{{\varepsilon}}{(1+t)^{\frac{m}{2}}}.

For any
2<\beta<n,
we then have

(C.40)

\begin{split}|\tilde{h}|(x,t_{1})&\leq C\left(t_{1}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\|\tilde{h}(0)\|_{L^{p}}+{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2q}}}+\frac{1}{t_{1}^{\frac{m}{2}}\rho(x)^{\frac{\beta-2}{q}}}\right)\right)\\
&\qquad+C\left(\frac{{\varepsilon}^{1+\frac{1}{q}}}{t_{1}^{\frac{(q+1)m+1}{2q}}}+\frac{{\varepsilon}^{1+\frac{2}{q}}}{t_{1}^{\min\{\frac{m}{q},\frac{n}{2p}+\frac{1}{q}\}}}\right),\end{split}

and, for
R\geq 1,

(C.41)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{h}(t_{1})\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}&\leq\|\tilde{h}(0)\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}+\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{CR^{2}}{t_{1}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\right\}\|\tilde{h}(0)\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\\
&\qquad+C{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{R^{\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\beta}}+\frac{{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{q}}}{t_{1}^{\frac{m+1}{2q}}}+\frac{{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{q}}}{t_{1}}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

In the formula of Lemma
8.2
, we break up the right-hand side as follows:

(C.42)

\begin{split}u_{1}^{q}(x)&:=\int_{M}\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1})|\tilde{h}|^{q}(y,0)\,dV_{y}\\
u_{2}^{q}(x)&:=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\!\!\!\!\int_{M}\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})||\tilde{h}(y,t)|^{q}\,dV_{y}dt\\
u_{3}^{q}(x)&:=\int_{t_{1}-1}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{M}\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{1}-t}}+\frac{d(x,y)}{t_{1}-t}\right)|\tilde{h}|^{\frac{q}{2}+1}|\nabla|\tilde{h}|^{\frac{q}{2}}|\,dV_{y}dt\\
u_{4}^{q}(x)&:=\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\!\!\!\!\int_{M}\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)\left(\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{1}-t}}+\frac{d(x,y)}{t_{1}-t}\right)|\tilde{h}|^{\frac{q}{2}+1}|\nabla|\tilde{h}|^{\frac{q}{2}}|-c_{q}|\nabla|\tilde{h}|^{\frac{q}{2}}|^{2}\right)\,dV_{y}dt.\end{split}

According to Lemma
8.2
, we have

(C.43)

|\tilde{h}|(x,t_{1})\leq u_{1}(x)+C\left(u_{2}(x)+u_{3}(x)+u_{4}(x)\right).

We now use Lemma
B.2
to estimate
u_{i}
pointwise and in
L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R}),
for
i=1,\ldots,4.

First term.
Same as Proposition
C.1
.

Second term.

Pointwise estimate.
Note that since
(M,g_{0})
is Ricci-flat ALE of order
\tau>0,
Lemma
3.2
implies

u^{q}_{2}(x)\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\!\!\!\!\int_{M}\frac{\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)}{\rho(y)^{2+n}}|\tilde{h}(y,t)|^{q}\,dV_{y}dt.

Let

f_{2}(x,t)=\int_{M}\frac{\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)}{\rho(y)^{2+n}}|\tilde{h}(y,t)|^{q}\,dV_{y}.

Applying (
C.39
) and Lemma
B.1
, we have

|f_{2}(x,t)|\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{(1+t)^{\frac{qm}{2}}\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}.

Since
\frac{qm}{2}>1,
this gives

(C.44)

\begin{split}u_{2}^{q}&\leq C{\varepsilon}^{q}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}/2}+\int_{t_{1}/2}^{t_{1}}\right)\frac{dt}{(1+t)^{\frac{qm}{2}}\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}\\
&\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}\right)^{\beta/2}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}/2}\frac{dt}{(1+t)^{\frac{qm}{2}}}+\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{t_{1}^{\frac{qm}{2}}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}/2}\frac{d\tau}{\left(\rho(x)^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}\\
&\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}\right)^{\beta/2}}+\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{t_{1}^{\frac{qm}{2}}}\frac{\min\{t_{1},\rho(x)^{2}\}}{\rho(x)^{\beta}}\\
&\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{\left(\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}\right)^{\beta/2}}+\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{t_{1}^{\frac{qm}{2}}\rho(x)^{\beta-2}}\frac{t_{1}}{\rho(x)^{2}+t_{1}}.\end{split}

L^{p}
-estimate.
We first apply Minkowski’s integral inequality:

(C.45)

\begin{split}\|u_{2}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}=\|u_{2}^{q}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}&\leq C\left(\int_{M\setminus B_{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}f_{2}(x,t)dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dV_{x}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|f_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}\,dt.\end{split}

Applying Lemma
B.2
b
, with

(C.46)

\begin{split}\|f_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M\setminus B_{R})}&\leq\frac{C\|\tilde{h}(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q}}{\left(R^{2}+t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{\left(1-\frac{q}{p}\right)\beta}{2}}}\\
&\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{(1+t)^{\frac{qm}{2}}\left(R^{2}+t_{1}-t\right)^{\frac{\left(1-\frac{q}{p}\right)\beta}{2}}}\\
&\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q}}{(1+t)^{\frac{qm}{2}}R^{\left(1-\frac{q}{p}\right)\beta}}.\end{split}

Integrating in (

\begin{split}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}}{R^{\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\beta}}.\end{split}

Third term.

Pointwise estimate.
Note that (
C.39

|\nabla\tilde{h}(t)|\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}}{t_{1}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}}

Taking

-th roots, we get

(C.50)

u_{3}(x)\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{1+\frac{1}{q}}}{t_{1}^{\frac{(q+1)m+1}{2q}}}.

L^{p}
-estimate.
Arguing as in Proposition
C.1

In view of (
C.48

f^{\prime\prime}_{3}(x,t)=\frac{C{\varepsilon}}{t_{1}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}}\int_{M}\left(1+\frac{d(x,y)}{\sqrt{t_{1}-t}}\right)\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)|\tilde{h}(y,t)|^{q}\,dV_{y}

,

\|f_{3}^{\prime\prime}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{q}}(M)}\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{q+1}}{t_{1}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}}.

Integrating in time, we get

(C.51)

\begin{split}\|u_{3}\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus B_{R})}\leq C\frac{{\varepsilon}^{1+\frac{1}{q}}}{t_{1}^{\frac{m+1}{2q}}}.\end{split}

Fourth term.

Pointwise estimate.
Applying the Peter-Paul inequality on

(C.52)

\begin{split}u^{q}_{4}(x)&\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\!\!\!\!\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{t_{1}-t}+\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{(t_{1}-t)^{2}}\right)\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)|\tilde{h}(y,t)|^{q+2}\,dV_{y}dt\\
&=C\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\!\!\!\!f_{4}(x,t)\frac{dt}{t_{1}-t},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}f_{4}(x,t)&=\int_{M}\left(1+\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t_{1}-t}\right)\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)|\tilde{h}(y,t)|^{q+2}\,dV_{y}\\
&\leq\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}}{(1+t)^{m}}\int_{M}\left(1+\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t_{1}-t}\right)\tilde{H}(x,y,t_{1}-t)|\tilde{h}(y,t)|^{q}\,dV_{y}.\end{split}

with

|u_{4}^{q}|\leq C{\varepsilon}^{2+q}\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{(1+t)^{m}(t_{1}-t)^{\frac{nq}{2p}+1}}\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{2+q}}{t_{1}^{\min\{m,\frac{nq}{2p}+1\}}}.

b

\begin{split}\|u_{4}\|_{L^{p}}^{q}&\leq C{\varepsilon}^{2+q}\int_{0}^{t_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{t^{m}(t_{1}-t)}\\
&\leq\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{2+q}}{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}/2}\frac{dt}{(1+t)^{m}}+\frac{C{\varepsilon}^{2+q}}{t_{1}^{m}}\int_{t_{1}/2}^{t_{1}-t}\frac{dt}{t_{1}-1}\\
