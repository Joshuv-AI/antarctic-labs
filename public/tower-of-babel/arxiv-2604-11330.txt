The solvability of the inverse volcano problem over non-prime finite fields
Alexandru Ghitza and Dhruv Gupta and Maximilian Kortge

Abstract




1
Introduction

Let
\ell
be a prime number.
An
\ell
-volcano is a simple connected graph that resembles a volcano (cf.
Definition

2.6
); it consists of some vertices arranged in a crater formation, together with isomorphic trees (lava flows) descending from each crater vertex and stopping at a finite depth
d\geqslant 0
.
This type of graph occurs naturally in arithmetic geometry (and computational and cryptographic applications):
Kohel
[
9
]
found that the connected components of the graph
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
of
\ell
-isogenies between ordinary elliptic curves over a finite field
\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
are
\ell
-volcanoes.
We say that such a volcano
appears in

\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
.

Figure 1:
A
2
-volcano of depth
1
(left) and a
3
-volcano of depth
2
(right).

Definition 1.1
.

Consider an
\ell
-volcano
V
and
k\geqslant 1
. We say that a prime number
p\neq\ell
is a
k
-explosive
prime if
V
appears in
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
.

Given a fixed
\ell
-volcano
V
of depth
d\geqslant 0
and
k\geqslant 1
, the inverse volcano problem investigates the existence of such
k
-explosive primes.

This problem was first considered by Bambury–Campagna–Pazuki
[
1
, Question 1.1]
in the case of prime finite fields
\mathbf{F}_{p}
, where they give a complete positive answer
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
:
any volcano
V
has infinitely many
1
-explosive primes.

The situation is more delicate if we are looking for a particular volcano in
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
for
k>1
.
A specific counterexample is given in
[
1
, Section 5]
:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
V
denote the
2
-volcano illustrated on the left in
Figure

1
.
Then
V
does not appear in
\mathcal{G}_{2}\left(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}\right)
for any prime
p\neq 2
.
In other words, there are no
2
-explosive primes for
V
.

Our paper generalises the results of
[
1
]
by providing a precise framework for investigating the inverse volcano problem for
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
with a fixed
k>1
.
This is based on a characterisation of the appearance of an
\ell
-volcano in terms of the existence of elements of certain orders in the class groups of appropriate imaginary quadratic fields.

For volcanoes of depth zero, we obtain in
5.4
a complete positive answer that generalises the corresponding result
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
.
For depth
d\geqslant 1
, the exact formulation and extent of our results vary with the crater type
V_{0}
of the
\ell
-volcano, which in turn depends on the ramification behaviour (

I

R

\ell
in the appropriate imaginary quadratic fields (see

In some of the cases (denoted with a *) our method requires detailed knowledge of the behaviour of the
\ell
-torsion in class groups of imaginary quadratic fields.
This is a difficult problem that remains an active area of research, so we had to resort to information that is conditional on a modification of the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics.
There are also some cases (denoted with ?? at the bottom of the table) where we do not obtain any results, conditional or not.

In the process of proving the appearance of a volcano, we need to find a suitable order
\mathcal{O}_{0}
in an imaginary quadratic field
K
, corresponding to the crater of the volcano.
In almost all cases, we find that
\mathcal{O}_{0}
can be chosen to be the maximal order
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, cf.
5.7
and
Section

.
(The exception is any

R_{1}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is the order of conductor

\mathcal{O}_{K}
.)

Here is an outline of the paper.
In
Section

2
we gather some notation and basic facts about the fundamental objects we work with: orders in imaginary quadratic fields, elliptic curves and isogenies, volcano graphs.
In
Section

3
we focus on the class groups of imaginary quadratic orders, especially how they vary as we increase the power of
\ell
dividing the conductor of the order.

Section

4
contains our general criterion for the existence/non-existence of
k
-explosive primes for a fixed volcano.
(An interesting feature, stemming from the Chebotarev Density Theorem and noticeable in the above table, is that the set of
k
-explosive primes either has positive density or is empty.)
In
Section

5
we apply this criterion to solve the inverse problem, first for craters (based on results from
[
1
]
) and then for deeper volcanoes with various crater types.
Our modification of the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics, a description of the computational evidence supporting them, and the conditional results they give rise to are in
Section

6
.
The appendices gather some supporting material that does not appear in the literature in the form we need it.

Fix a prime

2.1
Imaginary quadratic orders

Let
K=\mathbf{Q}\big(\sqrt{N}\big)
,
N<0
squarefree integer, be an imaginary quadratic field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
We denote by
D_{K}
the discriminant of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
:

D_{K}=\begin{cases}N&\text{if }N\equiv 1\mod{4},\\
4N&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We take the
\mathbf{Z}
-basis
\{1,\omega_{K}\}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, where

\omega_{K}=\frac{D_{K}+\sqrt{D_{K}}}{2}.

Let
c\geqslant 1
be coprime to
\ell
and consider the order
\mathcal{O}_{0}
of conductor
c
: this is a subring of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of index
c
and

\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathbf{Z}+c\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbf{Z}+c\omega_{K}\mathbf{Z}.

For
d\geqslant 0
, let
\mathcal{O}_{d}
be the order of conductor
c\ell^{d}
.

The
ideal class group

\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{d})
(sometimes called the
Picard group
of
\mathcal{O}_{d}
) is the group of proper fractional
\mathcal{O}_{d}
-ideals modulo principal
\mathcal{O}_{d}
-ideals (cf.
[
5
, §7]
).
It is a finite abelian group.

The
ring class field
of
\mathcal{O}_{d}
is the unique finite abelian extension
H_{d}
of
K
such that

•

all the prime ideals of
K
that ramify in
H_{d}
divide
c\ell^{d}\mathcal{O}_{K}
;

•

the ideal class group
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{d})
is canonically isomorphic to the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(H_{d}/K)
under the Artin reciprocity map.

For
d^{\prime}\geqslant d
we have
\mathcal{O}_{d^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{d}
and
H_{d}\subseteq H_{d^{\prime}}
.
(See
[
5
, Exercise 9.19]
.)

For more details about ring class fields, see
[
5
, Chapter 9]
.

2.2
Some discriminant upper bounds

We continue using the notation from the previous section.
For any ideal
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
, we write
\operatorname{ord}(\mathcal{L})
to denote the order of
\mathcal{L}
in the class group
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{0}\setminus\mathbf{Z}
, then
N(\alpha)\geqslant|D_{0}|/4
.

Proof.

Write
\alpha=a+bc_{0}\omega_{K}
with
a,b\in\mathbf{Z}
.
We have

\displaystyle 4N(\alpha)

\displaystyle=(2\alpha)(2\overline{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\left((2a+bc_{0}D_{K})+bc_{0}\sqrt{D_{K}}\right)\left((2a+bc_{0}D_{K})-bc_{0}\sqrt{D_{K}}\right)

\displaystyle=(2a+bc_{0}D_{K})^{2}-b^{2}c_{0}^{2}D_{K}

\displaystyle=\big(\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)\big)^{2}+b^{2}|D_{0}|.

Since
\alpha\notin\mathbf{Z}
we have
b\neq 0
so that
N(\alpha)\geqslant|D_{0}|/4
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The special case
\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
of
2.1
is
[
3
, Lemma 1]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Consider the decomposition of
\ell\mathcal{O}_{0}
into prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
.

(a)

If
\ell\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{L}\widebar{\mathcal{L}}
, with
\mathcal{L}\neq\widebar{\mathcal{L}}
prime ideals and
\operatorname{ord}(\mathcal{L})=n
, then
|D_{0}|\leqslant 4\ell^{n}-1
.
(This is
[
1
, Lemma 4.9]
.)

(b)

If
\ell\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{L}^{2}
, with
\mathcal{L}
prime and principal, then
|D_{0}|\leqslant 4\ell
.
More precisely,
D_{0}=c_{0}^{2}D_{K}
with

(D_{K},c_{0})\in\begin{cases}\big\{(-4\ell,1)\big\}&\text{if }\ell\equiv 1\mod{4},\\
\big\{(-\ell,1),(-\ell,2)\big\}&\text{if }\ell\equiv 3\mod{4},\\
\big\{(-8,1),(-4,1)\big\}&\text{if }\ell=2.\end{cases}

Proof.

(a)

Write
\mathcal{L}^{n}=\alpha\mathcal{O}_{0}
for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
.
We have
N(\alpha)=N(\mathcal{L})^{n}=\ell^{n}
.

If
\alpha\in\mathbf{Z}
then
\alpha^{2}=\alpha\overline{\alpha}=N(\alpha)=\ell^{n}
implies that
n
is even and
\alpha=\pm\ell^{n/2}
, therefore
\widebar{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}
, contradiction.

So
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{0}\setminus\mathbf{Z}
, therefore by
2.1
we get that
|D_{0}|\leqslant 4\ell^{n}
.

But if equality holds then
c_{0}^{2}|D_{K}|=|D_{0}|=4\ell^{n}
.
Since the conductor
c_{0}
of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is assumed to be coprime to
\ell
, this forces
\ell
to divide
D_{K}
, contradicting the fact that
\ell
is split in
K
.

(b)

Writing
\mathcal{L}=\alpha\mathcal{O}_{0}
, the reasoning from part (a) tells us that
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{0}\setminus\mathbf{Z}
, so that
|D_{0}|\leqslant 4\ell
.
Now
D_{0}=c_{0}^{2}D_{K}
with
c_{0}
coprime to
\ell
, and
D_{K}
negative, divisible by
\ell
, and a fundamental discriminant (either
1\mod{4}
and squarefree, or
4
times a squarefree integer that is
2,3\mod{4}
).
This gives the following options for
D_{K}
:

D_{K}\in\begin{cases}\{-4\ell,-3\ell\}&\text{if }\ell\equiv 1\mod{4},\\
\{-\ell\}&\text{if }\ell\equiv 3\mod{4},\\
\{-8,-4\}&\text{if }\ell=2.\end{cases}

We can rule out the case
\ell\equiv 1\mod{4}
and
D_{K}=-3\ell
as follows.
Here
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbf{Z}[\sqrt{-3\ell}]
, and we are assuming that
\ell\mathcal{O}_{K}=(\alpha\mathcal{O}_{K})^{2}
for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Writing
\alpha=a+b\sqrt{-3\ell}
with
a,b\in\mathbf{Z}
, we have

\pm\ell=\alpha^{2}=a^{2}+2ab\sqrt{-3\ell}-3b^{2}\ell,

impossible.

The leaves us with the cases in the statement, and it is easy to see that
c_{0}=1
for all of them except when
\ell\equiv 3\mod{4}
.
∎

2.3
Elliptic curves and isogenies

Fix a prime
p
distinct from
\ell
, and
k\geqslant 1
.
We consider elliptic curves over
\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
and isogenies between them.

The endomorphism ring
\mathcal{O}
of an ordinary elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
is an imaginary quadratic order.
(We say that
E
has
complex multiplication
,
CM
for short, by
\mathcal{O}
.)
The ring
\mathcal{O}
encodes a good deal of arithmetic information about
E
, such as

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\varphi\,\colon\;E_{1}\longrightarrow E_{2}
be an
\ell
-isogeny of (ordinary) elliptic curves with CM by orders
\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, respectively.
Then one of the following is true:

(a)

\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{2}
and we say that
\varphi
is
horizontal
;

(b)

[\mathcal{O}_{1}:\mathcal{O}_{2}]=\ell
and we say that
\varphi
is
descending
;

(c)

[\mathcal{O}_{2}:\mathcal{O}_{1}]=\ell
and we say that
\varphi
is
ascending
.

In the last two cases we say that
\varphi
is
vertical
.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Proposition 21]
or
[
22
, §2.7]
.
∎

2.5
Volcanoes inside isogeny graphs

Let
\Phi_{\ell}\in\mathbf{F}_{p}[X,Y]
denote the modulo
p
reduction of the
\ell
-modular polynomial (see
[
22
, Section 2.3]
).
It has the property that for all
j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
, we have
\Phi_{\ell}(j_{1},j_{2})=0
if and only if
j_{1},j_{2}
are the
j
-invariants of elliptic curves over
\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
that are related by an
\ell
-isogeny defined over
\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
denote the set of
j
-invariants of supersingular elliptic curves over
\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
.
In this paper, the (ordinary, simple)
\ell
-isogeny graph
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
has as vertex set
\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}\setminus(S\cup\{0,1728\})
, and there is an edge
(j_{1},j_{2})
with the same multiplicity as that of
j_{2}
as a root of
\Phi_{\ell}(j_{1},Y)
.
Due to our restrictions on the vertex set, the graph
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
can be considered to be undirected.

Exploiting the dictionary between elliptic curves and orders, Kohel determined the structure of the connected components of the isogeny graph
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
:

Theorem 2.7

(Kohel)
.

Let
V
be a connected component of
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}})
, with vertex set
\mathcal{V}
.
Fix some elliptic curve
E/\mathbf{F}_{p^{k}}
such that
j(E)\in\mathcal{V}
and let
K=\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes\mathbf{Q}
.
Let
\pi_{E}
denote the Frobenius endomorphism of
E
, let
v=\left[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbf{Z}\left(\pi_{E}\right)\right]
and

.

Then
V
is an
\ell
-volcano of depth

such that

(a)

for
0\leqslant i\leqslant d
, every

with

has CM by

, an order in

;

(b)

\ell\nmid\left[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathcal{O}_{0}\right]
and for

,
\left[\mathcal{O}_{i}:\mathcal{O}_{i+1}\right]=\ell
;

(c)

the crater

is regular of degree

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
k\geqslant 1
and let
\pi\,\colon\;G\longrightarrow H
be a homomorphism of finite abelian groups.
Consider the set

X=\{g\in G\,\colon\;\operatorname{ord}(g)\ndivides k\text{ and }\operatorname{ord}(\pi(g))\divides k\}.

(a)

X\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\ker\pi\cap kG\neq\{0\}
.

(b)

Suppose
\ell
is a prime such that
\ker\pi
is an
\ell
-group.
Let
G^{\prime}
, resp.
H^{\prime}
be the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
G
, resp.
H
.
Let
\pi^{\prime}\,\colon\;G^{\prime}\longrightarrow H^{\prime}
be the restriction of
\pi
to
G^{\prime}
, and consider the set

X^{\prime}=\{s\in G^{\prime}\,\colon\;\operatorname{ord}(s)\ndivides k\text{ and }\operatorname{ord}(\pi^{\prime}(s))\divides k\}.

Then

X\neq\emptyset\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad X^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\ker\pi^{\prime}\cap\ell^{r}G^{\prime}\neq\{0\},

where
r=\nu_{\ell}(k)
.

(c)

Let
f\,\colon\;G\longrightarrow H
be a surjective homomorphism of finite abelian
\ell
-groups such that
\ker f
is cyclic of order
\ell
.
Then
X\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\textnormal{{\#}}G/\ell^{r}G=\textnormal{{\#}}H/\ell^{r}H
.

Proof.

(a)

For
g\in G
,
\operatorname{ord}(g)\ndivides k
simply means
kg\neq 0
.
And
\operatorname{ord}(\pi(g))\divides k
means
\pi(kg)=0
, that is
kg\in\ker\pi
.
Combining these gives the set
\left(\ker\pi\cap kG\right)\setminus\{0\}
.

(b)

It is clear that
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
, so that
X^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow X\neq\emptyset
.

Now let
g\in X
, so that
kg\neq 0
and
\pi(kg)=0
.
Since
G^{\prime}
is the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of the abelian group
G
, we have
G=G^{\prime}\times A
, where
A
has order coprime to
\ell
and
G^{\prime}\cap A=\{1\}
.
Write

with

and

.
Then

0=\pi(kg)=\pi(ks)+\pi(ka),

so
\pi(ks)
is the inverse of
\pi(ka)
.
But the order of
\pi(ks)
is a power of
\ell
, while the order of
\pi(ka)
is coprime to
\ell
, so we must have
\pi(ks)=\pi(ka)=0
.
Hence
ka\in\ker\pi\subseteq G^{\prime}
as
\ker\pi
is an
\ell
-group.
Since
G^{\prime}\cap A=\{0\}
we conclude that
ka=0
, so
kg=ks
.
We have
s\in G^{\prime}
,
ks\neq 0
and
k\pi(s)=0
, therefore
s\in X^{\prime}
.

Applying part (a) to
\pi^{\prime}\,\colon\;G^{\prime}\longrightarrow H^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime}
, we have that
X^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\Leftrightarrow\ker\pi^{\prime}\cap kG^{\prime}\neq\{0\}
.

But
G^{\prime}
is a finite abelian group of
\ell
-power order, so if
\ell\ndivides m
then the
m
-multiplication map

is an injective group homomorphism, hence

.
This implies that
kG^{\prime}=\ell^{r}G^{\prime}
.

(c)

It follows from part (a) and (b) that
X\neq\emptyset\Leftrightarrow\ker f\cap\ell^{r}G\neq\{0\}
.
Since
\ker f\cong\mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z}
, we have that
\ker f\cap\ell^{r}G\neq\{0\}\Leftrightarrow\ker f\subseteq\ell^{r}G
.

Now consider the surjective map
\bar{f}\,\colon\;G/\ell^{r}G\longrightarrow H/\ell^{r}H
given by
g+\ell^{r}G\longmapsto f(g)+\ell^{r}H
.
We can identify the kernel with
\Lambda\coloneq(\ker(f)+\ell^{r}G)/\ell^{r}G
.
If
g+\ell^{r}G\in\ker\bar{f}
,
g\in G
then

, and so
f(g)=\ell^{r}h
for some

.
Let

be a pre-image of

so that

.
Thus

and
g+\ell^{r}G=(g-\ell^{r}x)+(\ell^{r}x)+\ell^{r}G\in\Lambda
.
Conversely, let

where
k\in\ker f
,
g\in G
.
Then
k+\ell^{r}g+\ell^{r}G=k+\ell^{r}G\in\ker\bar{f}
as
\bar{f}(k+\ell^{r}G)=f(k)+\ell^{r}H=\ell^{r}H
.

By the second isomorphism theorem,
\ker\bar{f}=\Lambda\cong\ker f/(\ker f\cap\ell^{r}G)
.
On the other hand, applying the first isomorphism theorem gives
(G/\ell^{r}G)/\ker\bar{f}\cong H/\ell^{r}H
.
Taking orders we have that

\textnormal{{\#}}G/\ell^{r}G\cdot\textnormal{{\#}}(\ker f\cap\ell^{r}G)=\textnormal{{\#}}\ker f\cdot\textnormal{{\#}}H/\ell^{r}H.

The result follows from the equivalence

.
∎

6.1
Cohen–Lenstra heuristics

In
[
4
]
, Cohen and Lenstra introduced a probability distribution for predicting the frequency of properties satisfied by class groups of number fields.
It is based on the heuristic that an abelian group should occur with probability that is inversely proportional to the number of automorphisms of the group.

In our situation, the formulation is as follows.
Let the sample space
\Omega
be the set of all isomorphism classes of finite abelian
\ell
-groups and endow
\Omega
with the discrete
\sigma
-algebra.
For
G\in\Omega
, define the (probability) measure

\mu_{\text{CL}}(\{G\})=\frac{\eta_{\infty}(\ell)}{\textnormal{{\#}}\operatorname{Aut}G},\quad\text{ where }\quad\eta_{\infty}(\ell)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(1-\ell^{-k}\big).

Let
K
denote an imaginary quadratic field with discriminant
D_{K}
and ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
For a “reasonable” random variable
h
, the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics predict that the average of
h
over the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of the ideal class group
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
is the
0
-average of
h
:

\lim_{X\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\left|D_{K}\right|\leqslant X}h\big(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{K})[\ell^{\infty}]\big)}{\displaystyle\sum_{\left|D_{K}\right|\leqslant X}1}=M_{0}(h),

where the sums are over all imaginary quadratic fields of discriminant
\left|D_{K}\right|\leqslant X
.

Remark 6.2
.

Instead of considering all number fields, one can restrict to a subset given by local conditions (for example, imposing that
\ell
is inert).
Wood conjectures (with supporting computations) that the distribution of number fields is invariant under such restrictions, see
[
23
, Conjecture 1.4]
.

Whilst there is strong numerical evidence in support of the heuristics, very few results have been proved.
One instance is the work of Davenport and Heilbronn (in
[
7
]
, predating Cohen and Lenstra): they show that for
h(G)=\textnormal{{\#}}G[3]
, the average of
h
over the class groups of imaginary quadratic fields is
2
.
Cohen and Lenstra then computed
M_{0}(h)=2
, confirming the heuristic’s prediction in this case.

Before we move on, we want to know the probability with which cyclic groups of exponent at least
e\geqslant 1
appear.

Lemma 6.3
.

Fix
e\geqslant 1
.
The probability that a group is of the form
\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{N}\mathbf{Z}
where
N\geqslant e
is

\frac{\eta_{\infty}(\ell)}{\ell-1}\frac{\ell^{2-e}}{\ell-1}.

Proof.

From
[
14
, Theorem 10.6]
, the probability of the group
\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{N}\mathbf{Z}
,
N\geqslant 1
, is

\frac{\eta_{\infty}(\ell)}{\ell-1}\ell^{1-N}.

So our desired probability is

\frac{\eta_{\infty}(\ell)}{\ell-1}\sum_{i=e}^{\infty}\ell^{1-i}=\frac{\eta_{\infty}(\ell)}{\ell-1}\frac{\ell^{2-e}}{\ell-1}.\qed
