miR-584-5p alleviates osteoarthritis by targeting HIF1A to regulate chondrocyte function and extracellular matrix metabolism
Authors: Jiapeng Yang, Huiling Qin, Xintong Hao, Ganggang Wang, Shufang Ni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoarthritis (OA) is a chronic disease characterized by the degeneration of articular cartilage and secondary hyperosteogeny, typically located in weight-bearing joints.ObjectiveThis study was undertaken to assess the expression profile, the diagnostic value, and the molecular mechanism of miR-584-5p in OA.MethodsQuantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed to detect the expression levels of miR-584-5p and hypoxia-inducible factor-1 A (HIF1A) in serum samples from OA patients and healthy controls. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of miR-584-5p for OA. Pearson correlation analysis was applied to explore the correlations between miR-584-5p expression and clinical scores (Lysholm, Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC), Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) as well as HIF1A expression. In vitro, human chondrogenic HC-A cells were induced with interleukin-1β (IL-1β) to establish an OA cell model. Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay and flow cytometry were used to assess chondrocyte proliferation and apoptosis, respectively. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits were employed to quantify the secretion levels of inflammatory factors including interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and IL-1β. Dual-luciferase reporter assay was conducted to verify the direct targeting relationship between miR-584-5p and HIF1A.ResultmiR-584-5p was significantly downregulated in OA patients, and its expression level decreased progressively with the increase of Kellgren-Lawrence (K-L) grade. ROC curve analysis confirmed that miR-584-5p had a high diagnostic value for OA with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.897, a sensitivity of 80.2% and a specificity of 86.2%. Pearson correlation analysis showed that miR-584-5p expression was positively correlated with Lysholm scores and negatively correlated with WOMAC and VAS scores in OA patients. In the IL-1β-induced HC-A cell model, miR-584-5p mimic significantly promoted chondrocyte proliferation, inhibited cell apoptosis, and reduced the secretion of IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β. Additionally, miR-584-5p mimic downregulated the mRNA expression of extracellular matrix (ECM)-degrading enzymes including matrix metalloproteinase 13 (MMP13) and a disintegrin and metalloproteinase with thrombospondin motifs 5 (ADAMTS5), while upregulated the expression of ECM synthesis-related molecules including aggrecan (ACAN) and collagen type II alpha 1 chain (COL2A1). Notably, all these protective effects of miR-584-5p mimic were abrogated by transfection with HIF1A overexpression plasmid (oe-HIF1A).ConclusionmiR-584-5p is a potential novel diagnostic biomarker for OA with high sensitivity and specificity, and its expression level can reflect the clinical severity of OA. Mechanistically, miR-584-5p alleviates IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury, inflammatory response and ECM metabolic imbalance in OA by directly targeting and inhibiting HIF1A.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-026-07029-2.Keywords: Osteoarthritis, miR-584-5p, HIF1A, diagnosis, biomarker

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a chronic degenerative joint disease characterized by articular cartilage degeneration, synovial inflammation, and abnormal bone remodeling [1]. It is also a major cause of disability among middle-aged and elderly people worldwide [2]. The pathogenesis of OA is complex, entailing interactions among multiple factors, including genetic susceptibility, inflammatory microenvironment imbalance, and chondrocyte metabolic disorders [3]. At present, there is no radical cure for this disease, and clinical treatment is still mainly focused on relieving pain and slowing down disease progression [4]. Therefore, in-depth exploration of the key regulatory molecules and underlying mechanisms of OA pathogenesis, as well as the identification of novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets, holds great clinical value and scientific significance.

Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) serve as key regulators in maintaining joint physiological homeostasis and driving OA pathological processes [5]. MicroRNAs (miRNAs), a major subset of ncRNAs, mediate precise post-transcriptional regulation of chondrocyte proliferation, apoptosis, extracellular matrix (ECM) metabolism and synovial inflammation via targeted modulation of mRNA expression [6–8]. Beyond miRNAs, circular RNAs (circRNAs), another important class of ncRNAs, have also been demonstrated to modulate metabolic processes in bone metabolic diseases such as osteoporosis and identify potential therapeutic targets, highlighting the broad regulatory role of ncRNAs in maintaining bone and joint tissue homeostasis [9]. In addition, there is another type of ncRNA, namely long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), which promotes fracture repair by influencing the survival and programmed cell death of osteoblasts [10, 11].

Dysregulated miRNAs contribute to orthopedic diseases. In postmenopausal osteoporosis (PMO), miR-217 is upregulated and promotes disease progression by suppressing Osteoprotegerin (OPG) [12]. miR-204-5p inhibits apoptosis in intervertebral disc degeneration [13]. miR-106a-5p shows diagnostic value for postmenopausal osteoporosis and demonstrates a correlation with ferritin levels [14]. With their high diagnostic accuracy, miRNAs represent a promising class of non-invasive biomarkers for OA [15]. miR-584-5p is implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple diseases, including cancer [16, 17] and coronary artery [18]. miR-584-5p inhibits the osteosarcoma progression [16] and suppresses osteogenic differentiation [19]. During bone healing, miR-584-5p is decreased in osteogenic differentiation [20]. A study on the miRNA regulatory network in OA indicated that miR-584-5p is lowly expressed in the serum samples of OA [21]. However, the clinical significance and regulatory mechanism of miR-584-5p in OA have not yet been elucidated.

miRNAs are small noncoding RNAs that can bind to complementary sequences in the 3′-untranslated regions (3′-UTRs) of target mRNAs [22]. Hypoxia-inducible factor-1 A (HIF1A) expression is stringently controlled by oxygen concentration, with this transcriptional factor acting as a central sensor of oxygen availability. Through its regulatory impact on energy metabolism, including glycolysis, mitochondrial function, and nutrient utilization, HIF1A assumes a crucial role in the prevention of bone metabolic disorders [23]. Articular cartilage exists under physiological hypoxia, a state in which the HIF-1α signaling pathway is crucial for preserving tissue integrity. Disruption of this pathway not only compromises cartilage homeostasis but also contributes significantly to the development and advancement of OA [24]. Studies have reported that the presence of HIF1A contributes to cartilage degradation [25], and in vivo administration of HIF1A inhibitors can alleviate experimental OA [26]. In the articular cartilage of OA, the number of chondrocytes positive for HIF1A staining increases with the progression of OA severity [27]. Bioinformatics prediction (StarBase) has further revealed potential complementary binding sites between miR-584-5p and the 3′UTR of HIF1A mRNA, suggesting a potential post-transcriptional regulatory relationship between them. However, whether miR-584-5p mediates the regulation of OA by targeting HIF1A remains to be further verified.

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the clinical diagnostic significance of miR-584-5p in OA and whether it exerts a regulatory role in the pathogenesis of OA by targeting HIF1A.

Patients and methods

Patient samples

A total of 96 OA patients who were treated at Zhucheng People’s Hospital were enrolled in this study. The inclusion criteria: Recurrent joint pain has occurred in the past month; morning stiffness is less than 30 min; crepitus is felt during movement; X-ray (standing/weight-bearing position) shows narrowed joint space (mainly asymmetric), osteophyte formation, and subchondral bone sclerosis/cystic changes (Kellgren-Lawrence (K-L) grade ≥ 2). The exclusion criteria: Patients with malignant tumors, trauma, and fractures; patients with inflammatory arthritis, gout, and allergies; patients with mental disorders. In addition, 94 healthy individuals who underwent physical examination were included as the control group. This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Zhucheng People’s Hospital, and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Fasting venous blood (5 mL) was taken from all individuals, and the serum samples were acquired after centrifugation at 3000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C and were preserved at -80 °C for experimental use.

Assessment of OA clinical symptoms

The two groups of subjects were evaluated by the Lysholm score, and the score ranged from 0 to 100. The higher the score, the better the knee joint function. Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC score) is the gold standard scale for OA evaluation, which includes pain, stiffness and joint function. The severity of symptoms increases with higher total scores. The Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) is a general tool for pain assessment. A higher score indicates a more severe level of pain. The combination of the three can completely evaluate the state of OA patients from different dimensions and avoid the limitation of a single index.

Cell culture and treatment

The human chondrogenic (HC-A) cells (obtained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC)) were maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium/Nutrient Mixture F-12 (DMEM/F12) medium (Gibco, Grand Island, USA) containing 5% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, Grand Island, USA), penicillin, and streptomycin (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) in an incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 °C [28]. To construct the OA cell model, the HC-A cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 105 cells/well and treated with recombinant interleukin-1β (IL-1β) (purity > 98%, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) at different concentrations (2.5, 5, 10ng/mL) for different time points (6, 12, 24 h) to screen the optimal induction condition.

miR-584-5p mimic (50 nM) and mimic negative control (NC, 50 nM) were purchased from GenePharm (Shanghai, China). The HIF1A overexpression plasmids (oe-HIF1A) were constructed by cloning the full-length HIF1A sequence into the pcDNA3.1 vector (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), with empty pcDNA3.1 vector (oe NC) as a negative control. For cell transfection, HC-A cells were seeded into 6-well and cultured to 50–60% confluency. The transfection procedure was conducted using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) [29].

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was isolated from serum samples and HC-A cells using Trizol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The concentration and purity of RNA were detected by a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) with the A260/A280 ratio of 1.8-2.0 as the qualified standard. The extracted RNA (1 µg) was subsequently reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) with a PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TaKaRa, Dalian, Liaoning, China) on a Veriti 96-Well Thermal Cycler (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Following 10-fold dilution of the cDNA, qRT-PCR was conducted on a Bio-Rad CFX96 real-time PCR instrument (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) with SYBR® Premix Ex Taq™ II (TaKaRa, Dalian, Liaoning, China) and target-specific primers. The 20 µL qRT-PCR reaction system included: 10 µL SYBR® Premix Ex Taq™ II, 0.4 µL forward primer (10 µM), 0.4 µL reverse primer (10 µM), 2 µL cDNA template, and 7.2 µL RNase-free water. The PCR reaction program was: 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) (for mRNA) and U6 (for miRNA) were employed as endogenous references. Expression levels were analyzed using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Cell proliferation assay

Logarithmically growing cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells/well. After transfection and IL-1β induction treatment, Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent (10 µL/well, Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) was added at 0, 24, 48, 72 h post-treatment. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 2 h in the dark, and the absorbance of cells at 450 nm was detected by a Microplate Reader Model 680 (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). Cell viability was calculated as the relative absorbance compared with the control group [30].

Cell apoptosis assay

An Annexin V-FITC/PI kit (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) was employed for the assessment of HC-A cell apoptosis [31]. After transfection and IL-1β induction, cells were harvested by trypsinization (without EDTA, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) at a density of 1 × 106 cells/mL, rinsed twice with cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) and then re-suspended in 1 × binding buffer (100 µL/well) provided by the kit. PI (5 µL) and Annexin V-FITC (5 µL) were added to the cell suspension, and the mixture was incubated at room temperature for 15 min in the dark. Then 400 µL of 1 × binding buffer was added to each sample, and cell apoptosis was immediately detected by flow cytometry with a FACS Calibur device (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s guidelines. The data were analyzed using FlowJo 7.6 software (FlowJo, Ashland, OR, USA).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The inflammatory factors were evaluated by using specific ELISA kits (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) with the following catalog numbers: Human interleukin (IL)-6 ELISA Kit (D6050B), Human tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) ELISA Kit (DTA00D), Human IL-1β ELISA Kit (DY201). Briefly, after HC-A cells were exposed to IL-1β for 48 h, the cell culture medium was harvested and centrifuged at 1000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cell debris. The concentrations of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β were determined strictly according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured by a Microplate Reader Model 680 (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA), and the standard curve was plotted to calculate the cytokine concentrations in the samples. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The potential binding sites of miR-584-5p on the 3′UTR of HIF1A were predicted via StarBase (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/). The wild-type (wt-HIF1A) and mutant (mut-HIF1A) HIF1A 3′UTR fragments containing the predicted binding sites were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China) and subcloned into the pmirGLO dual-luciferase reporter vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) to construct the reporter plasmids. HC-A cells were seeded into 24-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells/well and co-transfected with miR-584-5p mimic/inhibitor/NC (50 nM) and the wt-HIF1A/mut-HIF1A reporter vectors (0.5 µg/well) using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). After transfection for 48 h, the luciferase activity was determined with the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity for each sample [32]. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

Statistical analysis

Experimental data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of at least three independent experiments. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was constructed to assess the diagnostic value of miR-584-5p in OA patients. The independent samples t-test was used for comparisons of continuous variables between two groups, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test was applied for multi-group comparisons. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism 9.0 software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA). Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05 for all analysis.

Results

Comparison of basic data

Clinical data were compared between the control group and OA group (Table 1). No statistically significant differences were observed in age (60.61 ± 9.48 vs. 59.08 ± 10.67 years, P = 0.300), male proportion (57.45% vs. 61.46%, P = 0.573), or body mass index (BMI) (23.46 ± 3.84 vs. 23.73 ± 3.66 kg/m², P = 0.623). However, Serum C-reactive protein (CRP) levels were significantly higher in the OA group than in the control group (7.61 ± 2.15 vs. 1.23 ± 0.20 mg/L, P < 0.001). For the OA group, the Lysholm score was 63.15 ± 7.69, the WOMAC score was 43.24 ± 12.34, and the VAS score was 5.15 ± 1.63. The K-L grade distribution of OA patients was 38 cases of grade II, 39 cases of grade III, and 19 cases of grade IV.

Expression and diagnostic value of miR-584-5p in OA

The relative level of miR-584-5p was significantly downregulated in the OA group (Fig. 1A). The diagnostic performance of miR-584-5p in OA was evaluated using ROC curve analysis. An AUC value of 0.897 was achieved, the optimal cut-off value was 0.775, together with a sensitivity of 80.2% and a specificity of 86.2% (Fig. 1B). A subgroup analysis according to K-L grading demonstrated a progressive reduction in the expression of miR-584-5p with increasing OA severity (from K-L grade II to IV) (Fig. 1C).

miR-584-5p is downregulated in OA and correlates with disease severity. A Relative expression of miR-584-5p in control and OA groups (**P < 0.001). B ROC curve of miR-584-5p for OA diagnosis (AUC = 0.897, sensitivity = 80.2%, specificity = 86.2%, cut-off value = 0.775). C Relative expression of miR-584-5p across K-L grades (***P < 0.001)

Correlation between miR-584-5p expression and clinical symptom scores in OA patients

The relative expression of miR-584-5p in OA patients was positively correlated with Lysholm score (r = 0.679, Fig. 2A). The findings indicate that lower miR-584-5p expression corresponds to worse knee joint functional outcomes. A strong negative correlation was detected between miR-584-5p expression and WOMAC scores (r = -0.745, Fig. 2B), demonstrating that lower levels of miR-584-5p were linked to increased severity of pain, stiffness, and physical dysfunction in OA patients. The relative expression of miR-584-5p was also negatively correlated with the VAS score (r= -0.656, Fig. 2C), indicating that the lower the expression level of miR-584-5p, the more severe the pain of patients. These results collectively indicate that miR-584-5p expression is closely associated with the clinical severity of OA, and may serve as a molecular indicator that reflects the comprehensive clinical phenotypic characteristics of OA patients, including joint function and symptom severity.

miR-584-5p targets HIF1A

Potential binding sites complementary to miR-584-5p were predicted in the 3′UTR of HIF1A via the StarBase database (Fig. 3A). In luciferase reporter experiments, the activity of wt-HIF1A was significantly reduced by co-transfection of miR-584-5p mimic, while the activity of wt-HIF1A was significantly enhanced by miR-584-5p inhibitor, but they had no significant effect on mut-HIF1A (Fig. 3B). HIF1A was upregulated in OA patients compared with normal controls (Fig. 3C), and an inverse correlation was observed between miR-584-5p and HIF1A (Fig. 3D). Cell experiments showed that transfection of miR-584-5p mimic significantly increased the expression of miR-584-5p in cells (Fig. 3E). miR-584-5p mimic significantly decreased the expression of HIF1A mRNA, while HIF1A overexpression vector reversed this inhibitory effect (Fig. 3F).

B The relationship of miR-584-5p and HIF1A were detected by Dual-luciferase reporter assay (***P < 0.001; ns, not significant). C HIF1A mRNA expression in control and OA groups (***P < 0.001). D Correlation between miR-584-5p and HIF1A mRNA (r = − 0.705, P < 0.001). E miR-584-5p expression after mimic transfection (***P < 0.001). F HIF1A mRNA expression in rescue experiment (***P < 0.001)

IL-1β modulates miR-584-5p and HC-A cell viability

qRT-PCR showed that miR-584-5p was downregulated in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 4A), indicating that IL-1β suppresses miR-584-5p expression concentration-dependently. After treatment with 10 ng/mL IL-1β for 6, 12, and 24 h, miR-584-5p expression decreased gradually over time (Fig. 4B). Cell viability was significantly reduced with increasing IL-1β concentrations (Fig. 4C). Cell viability exhibited a time-dependent decrease following exposure to 10 ng/mL IL-1β for 6, 12, and 24 h (Fig. 4D). According to the experimental results, we finally chose 10 ng/mL IL-1β to treat the cells for 12 h for the follow-up experiment.

miR-584-5p mitigates IL-1β-induced HC-A cell injury via targeting HIF1A

miR-584-5p expression was decreased in IL-1β-treated HC-A cells, while transfection of miR-584-5p mimic reversed this inhibitory effect, leading to an increase of miR-584-5p expression (Fig. 5A). IL-1β exposure upregulated HIF1A mRNA expression; miR-584-5p mimic suppressed this IL-1β-induced HIF1A elevation, and this effect was abrogated by co-transfection with HIF1A overexpression vector (Fig. 5B). Cell proliferation was inhibited by IL-1β, miR-584-5p mimic reversed the proliferation suppression, whereas co-transfection with oe-HIF1A blocked this protective effect (Fig. 5C). IL-1β-induced chondrocyte apoptosis was reduced by miR-584-5p mimic, and this anti-apoptotic effect was abolished by oe-HIF1A (Fig. 5D). IL-1β treatment increased the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β), miR-584-5p mimic suppressed these cytokine levels, while oe-HIF1A co-transfection reversed this inhibition (Fig. 5E and G).

B HIF1A mRNA expression in rescue experiment (**P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). C HC-A cell proliferation in rescue experiment (**P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). D HC-A cell apoptosis rate in the rescue experiment (***P < 0.001)

miR-584-5p regulates ECM metabolism in chondrocytes by targeting HIF1A

IL-1β stimulation significantly upregulated the mRNA expression of matrix metalloproteinase 13 (MMP13) in chondrocytes. miR-584-5p mimic transfection suppressed the levels of MMP13, and this inhibitory effect was abrogated by co-transfection with oe-HIF1A (Fig. 6A). IL-1β-induced upregulation of a disintegrin and metalloproteinase with thrombospondin motifs 5 (ADAMTS5) was reversed by miR-584-5p mimic, while further oe-HIF1A transfection promoted the levels of ADAMTS5 (Fig. 6B). IL-1β treatment downregulated the mRNA expression of aggrecan (ACAN). miR-584-5p restored ACAN expression, and this restorative effect was abolished by oe-HIF1A (Fig. 6C). collagen type II alpha 1 chain (COL2A1) expression was significantly reduced by IL-1β. miR-584-5p mimic elevated COL2A1 expression, and this effect was reversed by overexpression of HIF1A (Fig. 6D).

Discussion

miRNAs have become promising candidates for OA diagnosis due to their stable expression in peripheral blood and close association with disease pathogenesis [33]. In this study, miR-584-5p was abnormally low in OA patients, and we systematically verified its reliability as an OA diagnostic biomarker, close multi-dimensional correlation with clinical scores, and prominent clinical value for OA severity assessment based on clinical and experimental data, in addition to confirming its direct regulatory role in OA by targeting HIF1A in chondrocytes.

Notably, accumulating evidence confirms that circulating miRNAs with stable expression and strong correlation with OA pathological processes are ideal diagnostic biomarkers [34]. miRNAs such as miR-200c-3p and miR-100-5p can distinguish patients with OA from healthy individuals [35]. Similarly, we found that miR-584-5p was down-regulated in patients with OA in this study, and its expression level gradually decreased with the progression of K-L grading. This is consistent with the research of Jiang et al., who found the decrease of miR-584-5p in serum samples of OA [21]. miR-584-5p exhibits superior reliability as a diagnostic biomarker for OA, and the core evidence is reflected in three key aspects: first, it has an excellent quantitative diagnostic efficacy, with an AUC value of 0.897 for OA diagnosis, accompanied by a high sensitivity of 80.2% and specificity of 86.2%. This diagnostic performance is not only significantly higher than that of miR-126-3p (AUC = 0.57) with limited diagnostic value [36], but also slightly superior to the well-recognized OA-related miRNA biomarkers such as miR-146a (AUC = 0.884) and miR-365 (AUC = 0.818) [37], indicating a more accurate ability to distinguish OA patients from healthy controls; second, its expression is stably correlated with clinical phenotypic characteristics, avoiding the limitation of single index diagnosis; third, its expression shows a clear gradient change with disease progression, which can realize the combination of diagnosis and severity assessment. Therefore, miR-584-5p may be a more reliable biomarker for the diagnosis of OA.

Moreover, this study has clearly confirmed the close correlation between miR-584-5p expression and OA clinical scores via Pearson correlation analysis: its expression was positively correlated with Lysholm score (r = 0.679), which reflects the motor function of the knee joint in OA patients, and strongly negatively correlated with WOMAC score (r = − 0.745) and VAS score (r = − 0.656), which comprehensively evaluate the severity of pain, joint stiffness and physical dysfunction. These correlations link the molecular expression level of miR-584-5p with the objective functional status and subjective symptom severity of OA patients, making it an important molecular indicator that reflects the clinical phenotypic characteristics of OA and provides a direct molecular basis for the clinical evaluation of the disease.

Beyond its diagnostic value, miR-584-5p also exhibits crucial and unneglectable importance for the assessment of OA severity, which is fully supported by our experimental data. On the one hand, K-L grading is the gold standard for radiological evaluation of OA severity, and the progressive reduction of miR-584-5p expression with the increase of K-L grade (from II to IV) presents a dose-dependent gradient change with the radiological structural damage of OA, which enables it to serve as a quantitative molecular marker for radiological grading of OA severity; on the other hand, its close correlation with Lysholm, WOMAC and VAS scores makes miR-584-5p able to simultaneously reflect the radiological structural damage and clinical symptom severity of OA, realizing the comprehensive assessment of OA severity at both molecular and clinical levels. This dual value of diagnosis and severity assessment makes miR-584-5p a more valuable biomarker for OA clinical management.

It is very important to clarify the downstream target gene of miRNA to clarify its regulatory mechanism in OA. Previous studies reported that miR-145 targeted and regulated HIF1A in myocardial infarction [38]. The miR-130a-3p targets HIF1A to regulate glycolysis of hepatocellular carcinoma [39]. Although HIF1A has been targeted and regulated by other miRNAs in other diseases, this study revealed the specific regulation of HIF1A by miR-584-5p in OA for the first time. Analysis of clinical samples revealed elevated HIF1A mRNA levels in OA patients, which correlated inversely with miR-584-5p expression. Cellular experiments confirmed that miR-584-5p mimic reduced HIF1A expression, and this effect was rescued by HIF1A overexpression. Collectively, these results establish HIF1A as a downstream target of miR-584-5p in OA. Our findings broaden the known target repertoire of miR-584-5p and identify a novel regulatory axis in OA pathogenesis.

Damage of chondrocytes, inflammatory reaction and metabolic imbalance of the ECM are the core pathological features of OA [40]. Pro-inflammatory cytokines are one of the key mediators leading to disorder in the pathophysiology of OA. Notably, the degeneration of the articular cartilage matrix is modulated by IL-1β and TNF-α, thereby positioning them as primary targets for therapeutic intervention [41]. The release of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α is increased, which induces the expression of proteases such as matrix metalloproteinases and leads to cartilage decomposition [42]. The IL-1β-mediated suppression of miR-584-5p expression may represent a key mechanistic pathway in OA development. miR-584-5p could alleviate the chondrocyte injury induced by IL-1β by promoting cell proliferation and inhibiting cell apoptosis. In terms of inflammatory reaction, miR-584-5p mimic reduced the IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β secretion in chondrocytes, indicating that miR-584-5p may inhibit inflammation related to OA. The increase of ECM catabolism is the key factor for the progression of OA [43]. The metabolic imbalance of ECM is mainly manifested in the increase of extracellular matrix-degrading enzymes and the decrease of extracellular matrix synthesis-related molecules [44]. In IL-1β-treated chondrocytes, miR-584-5p mimic downregulated MMP-13 and ADAMTS5 expression and upregulated ACAN and COL2A1 expression, thus facilitating the re-establishment of extracellular matrix stability. Rescue studies further indicated that HIF1A overexpression reverses the miR-584-5p-mediated chondroprotection. Together, these data suggest that miR-584-5p plays a protective role in OA by targeting HIF1A.

Beyond miRNAs, small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), another type of non-coding RNA involved in gene silencing, have also been shown to regulate tissue repair and homeostasis by targeting key genes in musculoskeletal disorders, including tendinopathy [45], rheumatoid arthritis [46] and osteoporosis [47]. This highlights the broad role of RNA interference-mediated gene regulation in maintaining musculoskeletal tissue integrity, and supports the validity of targeting disease-related genes via non-coding RNAs (such as miR-584-5p targeting HIF1A) for the treatment of OA.

The study has some limitations to be addressed. The sample size of this study is relatively small, which requires multi-center and large-sample clinical research to verify the diagnostic value of miR-584-5p. The in vitro cell model used in this study is based on HC-A cells induced by IL-1β, which cannot completely simulate the complex microenvironment of OA in vivo. Animal experiments are needed to verify the regulatory role of miR-584-5p/HIF1A axis in the progression of OA.

Conclusion

miR-584-5p is significantly downregulated in the serum of OA patients, and it has high diagnostic efficacy with an AUC of 0.897, a sensitivity of 80.2% and a specificity of 86.2% and is closely correlated with OA clinical scores (positively with Lysholm score, negatively with WOMAC and VAS scores). It exhibits crucial clinical importance for the assessment of OA severity, with its expression showing a progressive reduction with the increase of K-L radiological grade and serves as a potential quantitative molecular marker for OA severity grading and clinical evaluation.

Mechanistically, miR-584-5p alleviates IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury, inflammatory reaction and ECM metabolic imbalance in OA by directly targeting and inhibiting HIF1A. Collectively, our findings identify miR-584-5p as a multi-functional molecular marker for OA pathogenesis, providing new insights and a new molecular tool for the clinical diagnosis, severity evaluation and targeted therapy of OA.
