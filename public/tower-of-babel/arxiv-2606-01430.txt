A variational approach to ferronematics with a dimension reduction
Shilpa Dutta

Abstract
We present a variational approach to ferronematics in a three dimensional setting. The ferronematic energy functional is described by two established theories: the Landau-de Gennes energy to explain the nematic part, the micromagnetic energy to explain the magnetic part, and coupling energies between them. We explicitly include the nonlocal stray field energy in a bulk setting and the coupling energy accounting for the nematic and stray field interaction. We prove the existence of an energy minimizer for the introduced ferronematic energy functional in a bulk setting. We then provide a reduced local ferronematic energy in a two-dimensional setting via Γ \Gamma -convergence.



Keywords:

Ferronematics, Magnetostatic energy, Existence theorem, Dimension reduction

Introduction

Ferronematics are complex materials that are suspensions of magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) in a medium of nematic liquid crystals (NLCs). These materials feature spontaneous magnetization (existence of a ferromagnetic phase) even in the absence of an applied magnetic field. The theoretical prediction of such material was made about five decades ago by Brochard and de-Gennes
[
4
]
, and after forty years, the experimental validation of that theory was announced in
[
29
]
. Recently, a few mathematical studies
[
11
,
28
,
8
,
7
]
have been conducted using a two-dimensional ferronematic model
[
2
]
.

However, the ferronematic model in
[
2
]
pertains to Landau-de Gennes (LdG) theory for NLCs (see e.g.
[
12
]
) and Landau description of ferrofluid for MNPs (see e.g.
[
35
]
). The model
[
2
]
is thus a simplified one in the sense that it neglects the presence of the magnetostatic or stray field in the system. However, the non-local stray field may have a significant influence on the defect localization under an activated magnetic field
[
15
]
. In that two-dimensional ferronematic model, the stray field energy is included using the Gioia and James approximation
[
20
]
.

In this work, we present a three-dimensional ferronematic model which is based on two variational theories, e.g., the LdG theory for NLCs and the theory of micromagnetics for the spontaneous magnetization. To enable a quick view of the ferronematic energy, which we discuss in detail in the later part, we briefly present the ferronematic energy in a three-dimensional setting as follows. We assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and as per convention, we denote the state variables due to Landau-de Gennes by
\mathbf{Q}
, and micromagnetics by
\mathbf{M}
. The magnetization
\mathbf{M}
is subjected to an additional equation to account for a magnetic field
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}
, known as the magnetostatic field or stray field, and the involved equation is renowned as the stationary Maxwell’s equations (cf. (
2
)). In addition, we account for the influence of an activated magnetic field
\mathbf{H}_{ext}
. The generalized ferronematic energy, following
[
16
]
(up to almost the same notations) in a bulk setting, can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\biggl[\frac{K_{1}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{Q}|^{2}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{M}|^{2}+f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}|^{2}-\mu_{0}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{ext}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M}-\frac{\chi_{1}\mu_{0}}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}-\frac{\chi_{1}\mu_{0}}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{ext}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{ext}\biggr]\;,

(1)

where the bulk potential is given by

f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{3}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{3})+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{4}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{4}}{2}|\mathbf{M}|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{5}}{4}|\mathbf{M}|^{4}-\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M}.

We then provide an existence theorem for this 3D-ferronematic energy (cf. (
1
)) and establish the 2D-ferronematic energy, proposed in
[
15
]
via a dimension reduction technique from
\Gamma
-convergence.

Before we present our contribution, we discuss the earlier works on the ferronematic model based on
[
2
]
. In
[
11
]
, the authors explore the ferronematic energy in a one-dimensional setting, where they study order reconstruction phenomena and bifurcation analysis. The authors in
[
28
]
perform asymptotic analysis with the minimizers of a rescaled two-dimensional ferronematic energy by letting elastic constants tend to zero, and they have presented numerical experiments to support their results. In
[
8
]
, the authors have studied a super-dilute regime of 2D ferronematics setting, which refers to the situation when the magnetic energy has a negligible contribution compared to the NLC energy and the potential due to nematic-magnetic interaction is hardly realized. They have shown that the LdG order parameters converge to a canonical harmonic map, while the limit of the magnetization is a singular profile of line defects that associate point defects following a minimal path. Their theoretical results are also complemented with some numerical results. Recently, in
[
7
]
, they extended the earlier work on super-dilute regime from the Dirichlet type boundary conditions to the setting that considers Dirichlet boundary condition for
\mathbf{Q}
and Neumann boundary condition for
\mathbf{M}
. All these works deal with ferronematic energy based on
[
2
]
in a 2D-setting without accounting for the magnetostatic energy. We incorporate such a complex effect in the study and aim to establish a more generalised version. We also suggest
[
23
]
for a detailed study on magnetostatic or stray field energy and its physical significance.

We organize the next part of the paper as follows.
Section

2
introduces the 3D-ferronematic energy. In
Section

3
, we deal with the existence of a minimizer for the introduced 3D-ferronematic energy. We present the reduced ferronematic energy, resembling the energy discussed in
[
15
]
in
Section

4
. In
Section

5
, we present some numerical observations that support the present ferronematic energy over the earlier works. We close with the discussion with a concluding remark in
Section

6
.

2
Mathematical framework

We begin with assuming that a ferronematic sample fills a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the domain
\Omega
is bounded, simply connected, and Lipschi. Also we assume a set

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\bigl\{\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:\mathbf{Q}^{T}=\mathbf{Q},\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{Q})=0\bigl\},

(2)

which is endowed with the inner product
\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{P}=\mbox{tr}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{P}\right)
and described by the associated norm
|\mathbf{Q}|^{2}=\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})
. The orientational matrix is defined by
\mathbf{Q}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
and the magnetization is defined by
\mathbf{M}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The nematic-magnetic coupling energy
-\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M}
, that is the interaction between
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{M}
is included in the bulk potential
f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)
(see e.g.
[
12
,
23
,
29
]
) as follows

\displaystyle f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{3}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{3})+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{4}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{4}}{2}|\mathbf{M}|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{5}}{4}|\mathbf{M}|^{4}-\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M},

(3)

where
\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}>0
are the constants that are independent of the temperature but are dependent on the material properties, while the constant
\alpha_{1}
is related to the absolute temperature in a linear setting as follows

\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{0}\left(T-T^{*}\right),

where
\alpha_{0}
and
T^{*}
represent the characteristic temperatures of NLCs. The constant
\mu_{0}
stands for the magnetic permeability of the vacuum as per the standard convention, and
\gamma>0
is a dimensionless coupling constant that is responsible to measure the interaction between
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{M}

[
29
]
.

The magnetization
\mathbf{M}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
creates a magnetostatic field or stray field
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
5
]
, which satisfies the Maxwell’s equations in a static setting as follows

\displaystyle\textbf{div}\left(\mu_{0}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}+\chi_{\Omega}\mathbf{M}\right)

\displaystyle=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},

\displaystyle\textbf{curl}~\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}

\displaystyle=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},

(4)

where we extend
\mathbf{M}
from
\Omega
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by zero and
\chi_{\Omega}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
is the characteristic function of
\Omega
. For the Maxwell equations (cf. (
2
)) in a static setting, we assume (as per the convention of micromagnetics) that electrical conductivity is negligible. In purely magnetic samples, the stray field
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}
can influence the domain observations
[
23
]
. It may play a significant role in the formation of boundary as well as interior vortices, creation of domain walls and interior walls, etc.
[
23
,
14
]
. Moreover, the presence of an external magnetic field
\mathbf{H}_{ext}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
influence on the orientation of the magnetization
\mathbf{M}
and this phenomenon is renowned as the Zeeman effect
[
23
]
.

Thanks to the Poincaré-de Rham lemma
[
38
]
, there exists a scalar potential
\Phi_{\mathbf{M}}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}=-\nabla\Phi_{\mathbf{M}}

(5)

and so, we assume that
\Phi_{\mathbf{M}}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a solution of the following elliptic problem

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Delta\Phi_{\mathbf{M}}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{M}\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{3}.\\
\end{aligned}

(6)

Therefore, accounting these possible energy contributions in a ferronematic sample, the energy functional, following
[
16
]
, in a bulk setting can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\biggl[\frac{K_{1}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{Q}|^{2}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{M}|^{2}+f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}|^{2}-\mu_{0}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{ext}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M}-\frac{\chi_{1}\mu_{0}}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}-\frac{\chi_{1}\mu_{0}}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{ext}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{ext}\biggr]\;.

(7)

The energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)
consists of the following energy contributions:

•

As already mentioned, the nematic part of the energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)
is explained by the Landau-de Gennes theory
[
12
]
. The energy density
\frac{K_{1}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{Q}|^{2}
is known as the Landau-de Gennes (LdG) energy density assuming the one-constant approximation, where
K_{1}>0
is called the elastic constant. The part of the energy in the bulk potential
f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)
, which measures the nematic ordering, generally given by

\displaystyle f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right)=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{3}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{3})+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{4}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})^{2}

(8)

with
\alpha_{3}>0
and
\alpha_{1}<0
as established in
[
12
]
.

•

The magnetic part of the total energy is explained by the theory of micromagnetics
[
5
]
. The energy density
\frac{K_{2}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{M}|^{2}
is known as the exchange contribution and
K_{2}>0
is the exchange constant. This exchange energy density is a penalized contribution, which measures the deviation of magnetic orientation from the equilibrium directions. Similarly, the deviation of magnetic saturation is accounted by the penalty term
\frac{\alpha_{4}}{2}|\mathbf{M}|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{5}}{4}|\mathbf{M}|^{4}
, where
\alpha_{4}<0
and
\alpha_{5}>0
are the constants, which explain magnetic phase transitions
[
23
,
35
]
. To complete the micromagnetic aspects, we also incorporate the stray field or magnetostatic energy density by
\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}|^{2}
and the Zeeman energy density by
-\mu_{0}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{ext}

[
23
]
.

•

Furthermore, the generalized energy
\mathcal{E}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)
incorporates the coupling energy densities
-\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M}
due to interaction between
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{M}
with coupling constant
\gamma>0
,
-\frac{\chi_{1}\mu_{0}}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}
that is responsible for the impacts of
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}
on
\mathbf{Q}
. The nematic-stray field coupling contribution follows the same structure as the nematic-magnetic coupling term. Finally, it is the term
-\frac{\chi_{1}\mu_{0}}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{ext}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{ext}
that accounts the effect of
\mathbf{H}_{ext}
on
\mathbf{Q}
. Here
\chi_{1}
stands for the magnetic susceptibility
[
29
]
.

3
Existence results

To present the existence result, we define the admissible space as follows:

\mathcal{Q}_{B}=\Big\{\mathbf{Q}\in W^{1,2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{Q}\right):\mathbf{Q}|_{\partial\Omega}=\mathbf{Q}_{bd}\mbox{ in a sense of trace }\Big\},

\mathcal{M}_{B}=\Big\{\mathbf{M}\in W^{1,2}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}\right):\mathbf{M}|_{\partial\Omega}=\mathbf{M}_{bd}\mbox{ in a sense of trace }\Big\},

\mathcal{Q}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}=\Big\{\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right):\mathbf{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{B}\mbox{ and }\mathbf{M}\in\mathcal{M}_{B}\Big\},

where
\mathbf{Q}_{bd}:\partial\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathbf{M}_{bd}:\partial\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
are some given mappings.

As a first key step, we state the existence theorem in the context of energy minimization of energy functional (cf. (
2
)), below.

Theorem 3.1

(Existence of energy minimizers)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain such that
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz and
\mathcal{Q}\times\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset
. Also, we assume that
\mathbf{H}_{ext}\in C\left(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
. Then the functional
\mathcal{E}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)
(cf. (
2
)) has a minimum on the space
\mathcal{Q}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}
.

Remark 3.2
.

We note that the admissible space
\mathcal{Q}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}
is nonempty, that can be instantly concluded from an example provided in
[
1
]
.

Remark 3.3
.

The existence theorem (cf.
Theorem

3.1
) holds true with weak regularity assumption of
\mathbf{H}_{ext}
. However for simplicity, we assume that it is continuous up to the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

The first essential requirement to minimize the energy functional is the lower bound of the functional. As this lower bound cannot be instantly guaranteed, we first define a penalized energy functional in order to prove our main existence theorem, where the idea is to show that minimizers of this penalized energy functional converge to the minimizer of the actual energy functional (cf. (
2

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\biggl[\frac{K_{1}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{Q}|^{2}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{M}|^{2}+f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}|^{2}-\mu_{0}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{ext}

\displaystyle-\frac{\chi_{1}\mu_{0}}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}-\frac{\chi_{1}\mu_{0}}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{ext}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{ext}+\frac{\varepsilon\mu_{0}}{2}|\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{M}}|^{4}\bigg]\;,

(9)

\displaystyle f_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{3}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{3})+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{4}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{Q}^{2})^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{4}}{2}|\mathbf{M}|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{5}}{4}|\mathbf{M}|^{4}-\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{M}.

(10)

We begin with the compactness argument in order to apply the direct method in the calculus of variations, below.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain such that
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz and
\mathcal{Q}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, we assume that
\mathbf{H}_{ext}\in C\left(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
. Then the compactness argument can be stated as follows:

if
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{M}_{k}\right)\leq\Upsilon

\left(\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{M}_{k}\right)\in\mathcal{Q}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}

\Upsilon\in\mathbb{R}

\left(\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{M}_{k}\right)

\mathcal{Q}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}

Assume that
\left(\mathbf{Q}_{k},\mathbf{M}_{k}\right)_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}

\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{B}\left(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{M}\right)\leq\Upsilon

\Upsilon\in\mathbb{R}
and all

. Due to (

Proposition 3.5

[

uniquely and

Remark 4.1

In the above, we presume that the surface energy term is minimized in a regime where the thin film limit has a special nematic structure, i.e., the nematic order parameter

6
Conclusions

This work can be a first step toward understanding effective energy laws for ferroenematic materials via mathematical explanations. We propose a ferronematic energy in a bulk setting following
[
29
]
, where we explicitly incorporate the nonlocal influence of the magnetostatic energy. We then provide a reduced ferronematic energy via the weak convergence methods embedded in
\Gamma
-convergence in a thin-film setting. The approach follows the thin-film limit derivation for the magnetostatic energy by Gioia and James
[
20
]
in the ferronematics setup. There might be scopes to explore other finer thin-film limits (which generally differ by a logarithmic scale) of magnetostatic energy (see e.g.
[
14
]
) in the ferronematics framework.
