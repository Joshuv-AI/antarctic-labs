# Sumerian Cuneiform Tablets — Earliest Written Literature of Mesopotamia

## Overview

The Sumerian language is the oldest written language in existence, first attested around 3100 BCE in southern Mesopotamia. It flourished during the 3rd millennium BCE and was replaced as a spoken language by Akkadian around 2000 BCE, but continued in written usage almost to the beginning of the Christian era. The writing system used to record Sumerian was **cuneiform** (from Latin *cuneus*, "wedge") — a script originally developed for accounting records but which evolved into a sophisticated literary medium capable of recording mythology, law, hymns, lamentations, rituals, incantations, proverbs, and wisdom compositions.

hymns, lamentations, rituals, incantations, proverbs, and wisdom compositions.

The texts below represent major works of Sumerian literature preserved through the Electronic Text Corpus of Sumerian Literature (ETCSL) project at Oxford University. These compositions were preserved through centuries of scribal tradition, many originally composed during the Old Babylonian period (c. 2000–1600 BCE) but reflecting much older oral traditions dating to the Sumerian renaissance of the Third Dynasty of Ur (c. 2112–2004 BCE).
