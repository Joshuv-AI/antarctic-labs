Emergence of Unique Steady Edge States in Trapped Ultracold Atom Systems
Roland Cristopher F. Caballar

Abstract
We show that, for a one - dimensional open quantum system of ultracold atoms trapped in an array of harmonic potentials that is weakly coupled to a background Bose - Einstein Condensate (BEC), a unique steady state emerges at either of the two edges of the array due to the combined effects of excitation via lasers of these ultracold atoms and decay back to their initial energy levels via emission of excitations into the BEC, acting as an excitation reservoir. We then solve, both numerically and analytically, for the steady states of the master equation that describes the dynamics of this open quantum system, and show that these steady states occur at the edges of the array of harmonic potentials trapping these atoms. Using the open quantum system’s master equation to evolve it numerically over time, we demonstrate that these steady states at the edge of the system will emerge regardless of the number of atoms trapped in each of the harmonic potentials in the array, establishing both their existence and uniqueness, and demonstrating that this driven trapped ultracold atom system coupled to a BEC is a topological material whose topological invariant is characterized by its master equation.

This effect is especially significant for open quantum systems, whose time evolution is governed by the master equation which has both Hermitian (the Hamiltonian operator denoting the coherent dynamics of the system) and non - Hermitian (the Lindblad operator denoting the dissipative dynamics of the system) components. In this paper, we investigate the emergence of such unique edge states as steady states of a particular many-body open quantum system, namely a gas of ultracold atoms trapped in an array of harmonic potentials which undergo a combination of driven and dissipative dynamics, in the form of successive excitations to higher energy levels in harmonic traps away from their initial trapping location and dissipative emission of Bogoliubov excitations by these excited atoms into a Bose - Einstein Condensate (BEC), which is weakly coupled to this trapped ultracold atom system and acts as a reservoir of these excitations, to enable their return to their original energy levels but at a trapping location which may be different from their original one. We show, using both analytical and numerical methods and after deriving the interaction Hamiltonian for this open quantum system and the master equation describing its driven-dissipative dynamics, that unique steady states localized at the edges of this system will emerge as the system evolves over time, with these steady edge states characterized by their particle number. The emergence of these unique steady edge states for this open quantum system indicates that this form of driven quantum matter is a topological material, whose topological invariant is characterized by the master equation describing its non-Hermitian dynamics.

However, since the ultracold atoms have been excited to one of two possible harmonic potentials adjacent to the harmonic potential in which they have initially been trapped, these excited atoms may decay into the ground state energy level in one of two possible harmonic potentials adjacent to the harmonic potential to which they have been excited. This signifies that a trapped ultracold atom originally at energy level

x_{l+1}=(2l-L)x_{a}

x_{l+1/2}

x_{j\pm 1/2}

4
Master Equation for the Driven - Dissipative Dynamics of the Open Quantum Trapped Ultracold Atom System

To derive the master equation describing the time evolution of the open quantum trapped ultracold atom system we are considering in this paper, we make use of the Born - Markov approximation for open quantum systems, which assumes that the quantum system is weakly coupled to another quantum system that serves as a bath, or reservoir, for quantities that are emitted by the first quantum system (such as heat, electromagnetic radiation, or Bogoliubov excitations) and which assumes that the relaxation time for the system is much greater than the timescale over which correlations between the system and the bath decay. Under this approximation, the resulting master equation will have the form

\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}dt^{\prime}Tr_{B}\left[\hat{H}_{SB}(t),\left[\hat{H}_{SB}(t-t^{\prime}),\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\otimes\hat{\rho}_{B}\right]\right]

(15)

In this equation, the density operators
\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)
and
\hat{\rho}_{B}
are the density operators corresponding to the trapped ultracold atoms (the system) and the background BEC (the bath, or reservoir, of excitations) respectively, and
\hat{H}_{SB}(t)
is the time-evolved interaction Hamiltonian for the trapped ultracold atom open quantum system considered in this paper. Now to determine the explicit form of
\hat{H}_{SB}(t)
, we evolve the interaction Hamiltonian given by Eq. (
14
) over time using the following time evolution rule in the Heisenberg picture:

\hat{H}_{SB}(t)=exp\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}(\hat{H}_{S}+\hat{H}_{B})t\right)\hat{H}_{SB}exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}(\hat{H}_{S}+\hat{H}_{B})t\right)

(16)

In using the time evolution rule in the Heisenberg picture, the Hamiltonians used to evolve the interaction Hamiltonian for the open quantum system are those for the trapped ultracold atom system and the BEC excitation reservoir, which have the explicit forms

\hat{H}_{S}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(\epsilon_{g}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,j}\hat{a}_{g,j}+\epsilon_{e}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{e,j}\hat{a}_{e,j}\right)

(17)

\hat{H}_{B}=\sum_{k}E_{k}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{b}_{k}

(18)

In these Hamiltonians, the many-body creation (annihilation) operators for the trapped ultracold atoms in the ground and excited states,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,j}\;(\hat{a}_{g,j})
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{e,j}\;(\hat{a}_{e,j})
respectively, and the particle creation (annihilation) operators for the atoms in the BEC reservoir,
\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k}\;(\hat{b}_{k})
, satisfy the following commutation relations:

\left[\hat{a}_{g,j},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,j^{\prime}}\right]=\left[\hat{a}_{e,j},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{e,j^{\prime}}\right]=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}},\;\left[\hat{a}_{g,j},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{e,j^{\prime}}\right]=\left[\hat{a}_{e,j},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,j^{\prime}}\right]=0\\

\left[\hat{b}_{k},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k^{\prime}}\right]=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}},\;\left[\hat{b}_{k},\hat{b}_{k^{\prime}}\right]=\left[\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{k^{\prime}}\right]=0

(19)

In performing the time evolution, we make use of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff identity for time - evolved quantum observables in the Heisenberg picture:

\hat{A}(t)=\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}t\hat{H}\right)\hat{A}\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}t\hat{H}\right)=\hat{A}+\frac{i}{\hbar}t\left[\hat{H},\hat{A}\right]+\frac{1}{2!}\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}t\right)^{2}\left[\hat{H},\left[\hat{H},\hat{A}\right]\right]+...

Now let us use adiabatic elimination to express the creation and annihilation operators for the harmonic potential excited states at

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}dt^{\prime}Tr_{B}\left[\hat{H}_{SB}(t),\left[\hat{H}_{SB}(t-t^{\prime}),\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\otimes\hat{\rho}_{B}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\hbar a^{2}_{SB}}{\mu^{2}\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{e}}}\left(\frac{\rho_{B}\sigma_{g}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})}{2m_{B}c^{2}L\sigma_{e}}x^{2}_{a}\right)\frac{\Omega_{1}^{2}}{\Delta_{1}^{2}}e^{-\frac{x_{a}^{2}}{\sigma_{e}^{2}}}\left[-\left(e^{\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}+\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)\left(\hat{c}-e^{-\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\left(e^{\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}-\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)\left(\hat{c}+e^{-\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)\right]

\gamma=\frac{4\pi\hbar a^{2}_{SB}}{\mu^{2}\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{e}}}\left(\frac{\rho_{B}\sigma_{g}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})}{2m_{B}c^{2}L\sigma_{e}}\right)\left(\frac{\Omega_{1}}{\Delta_{1}}x_{a}\right)^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{x_{a}^{2}}{\sigma_{e}^{2}}\right)

(35)

) and substituting Eq. (

), we obtain the following equation:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}dt^{\prime}Tr_{B}\left[\hat{H}_{SB}(t),\left[\hat{H}_{SB}(t-t^{\prime}),\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\otimes\hat{\rho}_{B}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma\left[-\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}-\hat{c}\hat{c}^{\dagger}+e^{\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}\hat{c}-e^{-\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\right.

\displaystyle-\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}-\hat{c}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}^{\dagger}+e^{\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}-e^{-\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\hat{c}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}^{\dagger}-\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}+e^{\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}-e^{\frac{-2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle\left.+\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\left(\hat{c}\hat{c}^{\dagger}-\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}+e^{\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}\hat{c}-e^{-\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)\right]

(36)

Finally, substituting this into the right-hand side of the Born-Markov form of the master equation for the open quantum system given by Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)=-\gamma\left[\left(2\hat{c}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}^{\dagger}-\left\{\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c},\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\right\}\right)-\left(2\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\hat{c}-\left\{\hat{c}\hat{c}^{\dagger},\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\right\}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-e^{\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\left[\hat{c}\hat{c},\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\right]+e^{-\frac{2i}{\hbar}(\epsilon_{e}-\epsilon_{g})t}\left[\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}^{\dagger},\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)\right]\right]

(37)

5

For this case, we consider a gas of ultracold atoms trapped in an array of 3 harmonic potentials, for which

with
\hat{a}_{g,j}

being the many-body annihilation (creation) operator for an atom in the ground state
g
of the harmonic potentials located at
x_{j}=(2j-3)x_{a},j=1,2
of a 3-harmonic potential array, with the third harmonic potential, located at
x_{j+1/2}=(2(j+1/2)-3)x_{a},j=1
, containing no atoms and to which the atoms trapped in the harmonic potentials at
\pm x_{a}
will be excited by laser fields. These jump operators are similar in form to those used in the master equations governing the dynamics of the open quantum systems formulated in Refs.
[
18
]
,
[
58
]

[
9
]
, and
[
10
]
, and which are derived using the Born - Markov approximation. The open quantum system described by the master equation with jump operators given by Eq. (

), wherein the ultracold atoms are initially trapped in the harmonic potentials located at
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.

Before we consider in greater depth the dynamics due to the quantum master equation with jump operators given by Eq.
38
, it would be helpful for us to recall what happens when a particle annihilation or creation operator,
\hat{a}
or
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
, acts on a many - body quantum state with a maximum of
N
particles present:

\displaystyle\hat{a}\left|n\right\rangle=\sqrt{n}\left|n-1\right\rangle,\;\;\hat{a}^{\dagger}\left|n\right\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\left|n+1\right\rangle,0<n\leq N

\displaystyle\hat{a}\left|0\right\rangle=0,\;\;\hat{a}^{\dagger}\left|N\right\rangle=0

At the same time, successive applications of these particle annihilation or creation operators on that same
N
- particle many - body quantum state results in the following:

\hat{a}^{n}\left|N\right\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{N!}{(N-n)!}}\left|N-n\right\rangle,\;\;(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{n}\left|N\right\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{(N+n)!}{N!}}\left|N+n\right\rangle

(39)

We now consider a quantum state
\left|\phi\right\rangle=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle
describing our trapped ultracold atom system. This corresponds to a state wherein, initially, there is a definite number
n_{1}
of ultracold atoms in the first harmonic potential, and a definite number
n_{2}
of ultracold atoms in the third harmonic potential, such that the total number of ultracold atoms in the system is
n_{1}+n_{2}=N
. Now the action of the creation and annihilation operators
\hat{a}_{g,1}
,
\hat{a}_{g,2}
,
\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}
on this state are as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{g,1}\left|\phi\right\rangle=\hat{a}_{g,1}\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle=\sqrt{n_{1}}\left|n_{1}-1\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{g,2}\left|\phi\right\rangle=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\hat{a}_{g,2}\left|n_{2}\right\rangle=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\sqrt{n_{2}-1}\left|n_{2}-1\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}\left|\phi\right\rangle=\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle=\sqrt{n_{1}+1}\left|n_{1}+1\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{g,2}\left|\phi\right\rangle=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}\left|n_{2}\right\rangle=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\sqrt{n_{2}+1}\left|n_{2}+1\right\rangle

(40)

Keeping this in mind, we now consider the dynamics of our trapped ultracold atom system. The density matrix that describes the initial state of our trapped ultracold atom system has the form

\rho_{S}(t_{0})=\left|\phi\right\rangle\left\langle\phi\right|=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle n_{1}\right|\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle\left\langle n_{2}\right|

(41)

This initial state of the system is then evolved over time using the master equation to determine the state of the system at an instant of time
t>t_{0}
. To do so, we solve the master equation of this system iteratively by first discretizing the time derivative of the density matrix at the instant of time
t
, such that the time-discretized master equation will now have the following form:

\hat{\rho}(t_{n})=\hat{\rho}(t_{n-1})+\Delta t\left(-i\left[\hat{H}_{S},\hat{\rho}_{S}(t_{n-1})\right]-2\hat{c}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t_{n-1})\hat{c}^{\dagger}+\left\{\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c},\hat{\rho}_{S}(t_{n-1})\right\}\right)

(42)

In solving the time-discretized master equation iteratively, we see that terms involving consecutive applications of powers of the jump operators
\hat{c}
and
\hat{c}^{\dagger}

appearing in the equation:

\displaystyle((\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}-\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger})(\hat{a}_{g,1}+\hat{a}_{g,2}))^{p}((\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger})(\hat{a}_{g,1}-\hat{a}_{g,2}))^{n}

\displaystyle=(\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,1}-\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,2}-\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,1}+\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{p}(\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,1}-\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,2}+\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,1}-\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{n}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(n_{1}+n-j-l)!(n_{1}+n-j-2l)^{k}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!(n_{1}+n-j-2l)!}}\left|n_{1}+n-j-2l\right\rangle

\displaystyle\times(\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,1})^{r}(\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{q-r}(\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,1})^{s}\frac{(n_{1}+n-j-l)!(n_{1}+n-j-2l)^{k}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!(n_{1}+n-j-2l)!}}

\displaystyle\otimes\frac{\sqrt{n_{2}!(n_{2}-n+j+l)!}(n_{2}-n+j+2l)^{j-k}}{(n_{2}-n+j+2l)!}\sqrt{\frac{(n_{2}-n+j+2l)!}{(n_{2}-n+j+2l-p+q+s)!}}

\displaystyle\times(\hat{a}_{g,1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,1})^{r}(\hat{a}_{g,2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{q-r}\frac{(n_{1}+n-j-l)!(n_{1}+n-j-2l)^{k}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!(n_{1}+n-j-2l)!}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(n_{1}+n-j-2l+p-q-s)!}{\sqrt{(n_{1}+n-j-2l)!(n_{1}+n-j-2l+p-q-2s)!}}\left|n_{1}+n-j-2l+p-q-2s\right\rangle

\displaystyle\otimes\frac{\sqrt{n_{2}!(n_{2}-n+j+l)!}(n_{2}-n+j+2l)^{j-k}}{(n_{2}-n+j+2l)!}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\sqrt{(n_{2}-n+j+2l)!(n_{2}-n+j+2l-p+q+2s)!}}{(n_{2}-n+j+2l-p+q+2s)!}\left|n_{2}-n+j+2l-p+q+2s\right\rangle

(45)

We now impose the conservation of particle number in the harmonic potentials at
x_{j}=\pm x_{a}
, i. e. at all instants of time
t
,
0\leq n_{j}\pm n(t)\leq N
, where
j=1,2
and
N=n_{1}+n_{2}
. Imposing this on the state given by Eq. (
45
), we find that for both of the states in the harmonic potentials at
x_{j}=\pm x_{a}
,

0\leq n_{1}+n-j-2l+p-q-2s\leq n_{1}+n_{2},\;\;0\leq n_{2}-n+j+2l-p+q+2s\leq n_{1}+n_{2}

(46)

However, we note that from the last two terms of the first particle number conservation condition,

0\leq n_{1}+n-j-2l+p-q-2s\leq n_{1}+n_{2}\rightarrow 0\leq n-j-2l+p-q-2s\leq n_{2}

(47)

This then implies that the total number of particles in the third harmonic potential,
n_{2}-n+j+2l-p+q+2s
, must obey the following condition:

n_{2}-n+j+2l-p+q+2s=n_{2}-(n-j-2l+p-q-2s)\geq 0

(48)

Therefore, we find that for a given value of
n
and
p
, there will be a number of terms in the series given by Eq. (
45
) corresponding to a completely filled trapping potential at
x_{j}=-x_{a}
(i. e.
n_{1}=N
) and a completely empty trapping potential at
x_{j}=+x_{a}
(i. e.
n_{2}=0
) since, with
n-j-2l+p-q-2s\leq n_{2}
, there are, for a given value of
n
and
p
, a certain number of values of the parameters
j,l,q
and
s
appearing in the series given by Eq. (
45
) that will ensure that the condition
n-j-2l+p-q-2s=n_{2}
will be satisfied, such that
n_{1}+n-j-2l+p-q-2s=n_{1}+n_{2}=N
and
n_{2}-(n-j-2l+p-q-2s)=n_{2}-n_{2}=0
. At the same time, as both
n
and
p
increase, the number of terms in the time-evolved initial state given by Eq. (
45
) corresponding to
n_{1}=N
and
n_{2}=0
will also increase, since the number of possible values of the parameters
j,l,q
and
s
corresponding to
n-j-2l+p-q-2s
will increase as both
n
and
p
increase. This implies that, as
t\rightarrow\infty
, which corresponds to both
n
and
p
in Eq. (
45
) approaching infinity, all of the terms in the series given by Eq. (
45
) will correspond to
n_{1}=N
and
n_{2}=0
. Therefore, we expect that the time-evolved state of the system initially described by the density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{S}(t_{0})=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle n_{1}\right|\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle\left\langle n_{2}\right|
will eventually approach a steady state described by the density matrix

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle lim}}{{t\rightarrow\infty}}\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)=\left|N\right\rangle\left\langle N\right|\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|=\hat{\rho}_{S,SS}

(49)

6
Unique Edge Steady States for 5 - Harmonic Potential Trap Systems

We have, in the previous section, not only established the existence of unique steady states at the edge of a driven-dissipative ultracold atom open quantum system trapped in a 3 - harmonic potential array, but we have also demonstrated the use of the mathematical methods that we will need to analyze similar systems with more than three harmonic potentials in the trapping array. To extend our earlier analysis to driven-dissipative ultracold atom systems confined in an array of 5 or more trapping potentials, let us consider the case where we have a gas of ultracold atoms trapped in a setup similar to that described in Ref. (
[
11
]
) and illustrated in Fig. (
1
), wherein there are 5 harmonic potentials in the array, such that
L=2
, and the atoms are initially trapped in the ground state of the harmonic potentials located at
x_{j}=(2j-4)x_{a},j=1,2,3\rightarrow x_{j}=-2x_{a},0,2x_{a}
, and excited by laser fields in the same manner shown in Fig. (
1
) to excited states of neighboring harmonic potentials located at
x_{j+1/2}=(2j-3)x_{a},j=1,2\rightarrow x_{j+1/2}=-x_{a},x_{a}
, then decaying by emission of Bogoliubov excitations into the background BEC weakly coupled to the trapped ultracold atom system back to the ground state of the harmonic potentials located at
x_{j}=0,\pm 2x_{a}
.

The resulting master equation for this system, which was earlier derived in Ref. (
[
11
]
), has the same form as that given by Eq. (
37
), with the jump operators given by Eq. (
33
), with
L=2
. Explicitly, the jump operator’s form is given by

\hat{c}=(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2})(\hat{a}_{g,1}-\hat{a}_{g,2})+\epsilon(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,3})(\hat{a}_{g,2}-\hat{a}_{g,3})

(50)

Now let us assume that the initial state of the system is given by a density matrix with the form

\hat{\rho}(t_{0})=\left|\phi\right\rangle\left\langle\phi\right|=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle n_{1}\right|\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle\left\langle n_{2}\right|\otimes\left|n_{3}\right\rangle\left\langle n_{3}\right|,

(51)

where
\left|\phi\right\rangle=\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{3}\right\rangle
. We impose the condition that, at the initial instant of time
t_{0}
, the total number of atoms in the ultracold atom gas is

N=n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}

(52)

We further assume that this conservation of particle number condition is obeyed by the system at all instants of time, to ensure that the total number of atoms in the ultracold atom gas system remains constant, with no particle loss due to the dissipative interaction between the trapped ultracold atom system and the background BEC that acts as an excitation reservoir. This condition will prove to be crucial in demonstrating the existence of unique edge steady states for this system later on.

As we have done earlier, we will be analyzing the dynamical behavior of our trapped open ultracold atom system by iteratively solving the master equation governing the dynamics of the system, as given in general form by Eq. (
42
), with the jump operators in this master equation given by Eq. (
50
). We find that, similar to what we have done for the 3-harmonic potential case, we will be dealing with terms of the form
(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}(\hat{c})^{n}\left|\phi\right\rangle
as we solve Eq. (
42
) iteratively. Similar to what we have done in the previous section, we expand the term
(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}(\hat{c})^{n}\left|\phi\right\rangle
by first substituting the jump operators given by Eq. (
50
), and its Hermitian conjugate, into this term:

\displaystyle(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}(\hat{c})^{n}\left|\phi\right\rangle=((\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2})(\hat{a}_{g,1}+\hat{a}_{g,2})+\epsilon(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,3})(\hat{a}_{g,2}+\hat{a}_{g,3}))^{u}

\displaystyle\times((\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2})(\hat{a}_{g,1}-\hat{a}_{g,2})+\epsilon(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,3})(\hat{a}_{g,2}-\hat{a}_{g,3}))^{n}\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{3}\right\rangle

(53)

We then use the binomial theorem, being mindful of the order in which the jump operators act in this term, to expand
(\hat{c})^{n}
and
(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}
by their respective powers of
n
and
u
to obtain the following series expansion of the operator product
(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}(\hat{c})^{n}
acting on the state
\left|\phi\right\rangle
:

\displaystyle(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}(\hat{c})^{n}\left|\phi\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{u}\sum_{q=0}^{p}\sum_{r=0}^{u-p}\sum_{s=0}^{q}\sum_{t=0}^{p-q}\sum_{v=0}^{r}\sum_{w=0}^{u-p-r}\sum_{g=0}^{n}\sum_{h=0}^{g}\sum_{i=0}^{n-g}\sum_{j=0}^{h}\sum_{k=0}^{g-h}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\sum_{m=0}^{n-g-i}(-1)^{2u+n-j-k-l-m-q-r-s-t-v-w}

\displaystyle\times\frac{u!n!}{(g-h-k)!(p-q-t)!(n-g-i-m)!(u-p-r-w)!}\epsilon^{u-p+n-g}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{j!k!l!m!s!t!v!w!(h-j)!(i-l)!(q-s)!(r-v)!}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,1}\hat{a}_{g,1})^{s}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{q-s}(\hat{a}_{g,1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2})^{t}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,1}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{p-q-t}

\displaystyle\times\epsilon^{u-p}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{v}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,3}\hat{a}_{g,3})^{r-v}(\hat{a}_{g,2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,3})^{w}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}\hat{a}_{g,3})^{u-p-r-w}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,1}\hat{a}_{g,1})^{j}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{h-j}(\hat{a}_{g,1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2})^{k}

\displaystyle\times(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,1}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{g-h-k}\epsilon^{n-g}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}\hat{a}_{g,2})^{l}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,3}\hat{a}_{g,3})^{i-l}(\hat{a}_{g,2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,3})^{m}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,2}\hat{a}_{g,3})^{n-g-i-m}\left|n_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{2}\right\rangle\otimes\left|n_{3}\right\rangle

(54)

We then make use of the explicit form of the actions of powers of annihilation and creation operators acting on a particle number state, as given in Eq. (
39
), as well as the definition of the action of particle annihilation and creation operators defined on particular embedded trap sites acting on quantum states as given by Eq. (
40
), with the definition extended to include the annihilation and creation operators
\hat{a}_{g,3}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{g,3}
defined at the third embedded trap site, to obtain the following quantum state:

\displaystyle(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}(\hat{c})^{n}\left|\phi\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{u}\sum_{q=0}^{p}\sum_{r=0}^{u-p}\sum_{s=0}^{q}\sum_{t=0}^{p-q}\sum_{v=0}^{r}\sum_{w=0}^{u-p-r}\sum_{g=0}^{n}\sum_{h=0}^{g}\sum_{i=0}^{n-g}\sum_{j=0}^{h}\sum_{k=0}^{g-h}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\sum_{m=0}^{n-g-i}(-1)^{2u+n-j-k-l-m-q-r-s-t-v-w}

\displaystyle\times\frac{u!n!}{(g-h-k)!(p-q-t)!(n-g-i-m)!(u-p-r-w)!}\epsilon^{u-p+n-g}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{j!k!l!m!s!t!v!w!(h-j)!(i-l)!(q-s)!(r-v)!}

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{(n_{1}+g-h-k)!(n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-t)!(n_{1}+g-h-2k)^{j}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!(n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-2t)!}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times(n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-2t)^{s}\left|n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\otimes\epsilon^{n-g+u-p}\left[\frac{(n_{2}+n-g-i-m)!(n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-p-r-w)!}{\sqrt{n_{2}!}(n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+k)!}\right.

\displaystyle\times\frac{\sqrt{(n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t)!}}{(n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+t)!}(n_{2}+n-g-i-2m)^{l}

\displaystyle\times(n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h_{2}k)^{h-j}(n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h_{2}k+u-p-r-2w)^{v}

\displaystyle\times(n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t)^{q-s}

\displaystyle\times\left.\left|n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\otimes\epsilon^{n-g+u-p}\left[\frac{n_{3}!(n_{3}-n+g+i+2m-u+p+r+2w)!}{(n_{3}-n+g+i+2m-u+p+r+w)!}\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.(n_{3}-n+g+i+2m-u+p+r+2w)!\left|n_{3}-n+g+i+2m-u+p+r+2w\right\rangle\right]

(55)

Ignoring the coefficients of each term in this series expansion of
(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}(\hat{c})^{n}\left|\phi\right\rangle
, let us focus our attention on each of the particle number states in each of the embedded trapping potentials:

\displaystyle\left|n_{1}(t_{n})\right\rangle=\left|n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left|n_{2}(t_{n})\right\rangle=\left|n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left|n_{3}(t_{n})\right\rangle=\left|n_{3}-n+g+i+2m-u+p+r+2w\right\rangle

(56)

Again, we impose the condition of particle number conservation at all times in this trapped open ultracold atom system:

n_{1}(t_{n})+n_{2}(t_{n})+n_{3}(t_{n})=N

(57)

However, from the form of the particle number states at the harmonic potentials at
x_{j}=-2x_{a},0,+2x_{a}
, the emergence of steady states at either edge of 5 - harmonic potential trapping array by imposing the condition of particle number conservation at all times is not as straightforward as it was in the 3-harmonic potential trapping array case discussed in the previous section. Nonetheless, we can determine if there will be any edge steady states emerging from the driven - dissipative quantum dynamics of the system by first noting that any edge steady states must have the following form:

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{SS,l}\right\rangle=\left|N\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{SS,r}\right\rangle=\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|N\right\rangle

(58)

From the form of these edge steady states, we can see that all of the
N
atoms in the ultracold atom gas are in either the leftmost or rightmost embedded trapping potential (i. e. the embedded trapping potentials corresponding to
\hat{a}_{g,1}
and
\hat{a}_{g,3}
respectively) of the confining potential of the open ultracold atom quantum system, and in both cases, there should be no atoms in the center embedded trapping potential, which is the embedded trapping potential corresponding to
\hat{a}_{g,2}
.

6.1
Emergence of Left - Edge Steady States,
\left|\phi_{SS,l}\right\rangle=\left|N\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle

If the first type of steady state
\left|\phi_{SS,l}\right\rangle
, which we call the left-edge steady state and has the explicit form

\left|\phi_{SS,l}\right\rangle=\left|N\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle,

(59)

emerges due to the driven - dissipative dynamics of the system, then the condition obeyed by the particle numbers at each of the embedded trapping potential sites will have the following form:

\displaystyle\left.n_{1}(t_{n})\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}=\left.n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow N

\displaystyle\left.n_{2}(t_{n})\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}=\left.n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\left.n_{3}(t_{n})\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}=\left.n_{3}-n+g+i+2m-u+p+r+2w\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow 0

(60)

For the first condition corresponding to the particle number at the first embedded trap site, this can be written as

\left.n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}

(61)

which, in turn, reduces to the following condition:

\left.g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow n_{2}+n_{3}\Longleftrightarrow g-h-2k+p-q-2t\leq n_{2}+n_{3}

(62)

The rightmost part of the particle number condition given by Eq. (
62
) comes about because, if the initial number of atoms in the leftmost embedded trapping potential is
n_{1}
, then the number of atoms in that embedded potential can either increase or decrease only up to
n_{1}=N
, which corresponds to the state where the embedded trapping potential is completely filled, but it cannot go beyond that value
N
. Now, we can rewrite the second condition corresponding to the particle number at the second embedded trapping site as given in Eq. (
60
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left.n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle=\left.n_{2}-(g-h-2k+p-q-2t)+(n-g-i-2m+u-p-r-2w)\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow 0

(63)

Substituting Eq. (
62
) into Eq. (
63
), we then find that

\displaystyle\left.n_{2}-(g-h-2k+p-q-2t)+(n-g-i-2m+u-p-r-2w)\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\left.n_{2}-(n_{2}+n_{3})+(n-g-i-2m+u-p-r-2w)\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\left.-n_{3}+(n-g-i-2m+u-p-r-2w)\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow 0

(64)

However, the last line of Eq. (
64
) is identical to the last condition given in Eq. (
60
), which corresponds to the value of the particle number
n_{3}(t_{n})
for a left edge steady state to emerge. Therefore, we find that for left edge steady state
\left|\phi_{SS,l}\right\rangle
to emerge, the summation indices
g,h,i,k,m
(which are all dependent, directly or indirectly, on the value of
n
) and
p,q,r,t,w
(which are all dependent, directly or indirectly, on the value of
u
), must all acquire values such that the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle g-h-2k+p-q-2t=n_{2}+n_{3}

\displaystyle(n-g-i-2m+u-p-r-2w)=n_{3}

(65)

We note that for a given value of
n_{2}
and
n_{3}
, as both
n
and
u
increase, there will be an increasing number of possible values of the summation indices involved in the conditions given by Eq. (
65
) that can satisfy both of these conditions. Therefore, the left edge steady state will exist provided that the summation indices in the binomial series expansion of
(\hat{c}^{\dagger})^{u}(\hat{c})^{n}\left|\phi\right\rangle
satisfy the conditions given by Eq. (
65
).

6.2
Emergence of Right - Edge Steady States,
\left|\phi_{SS,r}\right\rangle=\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|N\right\rangle

Let us now consider the conditions under which the second type of edge steady state, which we call the right edge steady state, emerges:

\left|\phi_{SS,r}\right\rangle=\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\left|N\right\rangle

(66)

For the right edge steady state to emerge, the following conditions must be satisfied:

\displaystyle\left.n_{1}(t_{n})\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}=\left.n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\left.n_{2}(t_{n})\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}=\left.n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\left.n_{3}(t_{n})\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}=\left.n_{3}-n+g+i+2m-u+p+r+2w\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow N

(67)

Let us now consider how these three conditions may, separately be satisfied. First, looking at the first two conditions for the emergence of the right edge steady state, we find that

\displaystyle\left.n_{1}+g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow 0\Longrightarrow\left.g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow-n_{1}

\displaystyle\left.n_{2}+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\left.n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow-n_{2}

(68)

However, adding up the right hand side of these two conditions together, we find that

\displaystyle\left.g-h-2k+p-q-2t+n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\left.-g-p+n-i-2m+u-r-2w\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow-n_{1}-n_{2}

(69)

However, we note that, rewriting the right hand side of the third condition for the emergence of the right edge steady state, we find that

\displaystyle\left.n_{3}-n+g+i+2m-u+p+r+2w\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow N

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\left.n_{3}-(n-g-i-2m+u-p-r-2w)\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow N

(70)

We can then substitute Eq. (
69
) into the right-hand side of Eq. (
70
) to give us

\displaystyle\left.n_{3}-(n-g-i-2m+u-p-r-2w)\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow N\Longrightarrow\left.n_{3}-(-n_{1}-n_{2})\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow N

(71)

Therefore, if the conditions specified by Eq. (
68
) are satisfied, then the third of the three conditions given by Eq. (
67
) are automatically satisfied, allowing us to rewrite the conditions for the summation indices given by Eq. (
67
) to guarantee that the right edge steady state exists as follows:

\displaystyle\left.g-h-2k+p-q-2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow-n_{1}

\displaystyle\left.n-2g-i-2m+h+2k+u-2p-r-2w+q+2t\right|_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow-n_{2}

(72)

6.3
Further Conditions on Emergence of Left and Right Edge Steady States

Having established the mathematical conditions under which the left and right edge steady states will emerge in the system, as well as proving the existence of these states, we now address the question of which of these two edge steady states would be likely to emerge in the system. We recall that the conditions for emergence of the left edge and right steady states are given by Eqs. (
60
) and (
62
), and Eqs. (
67
) and (
72
), respectively. We note, however, that if
n_{1}>>n_{3}
, then based on the 4 aforementioned conditions,
n_{3}(t)\rightarrow 0
faster than
n_{3}(t)\rightarrow N
, and conversely,
n_{1}(t)\rightarrow N
faster than
n_{1}(t)\rightarrow 0
. Therefore, we expect that if
n_{1}>>n_{3}
, a left edge steady state will be very likely to emerge as a result of the driven - dissipative dynamics experienced by the system. By similar arguments, we can also say that if
n_{1}<<n_{3}
,
n_{1}(t)\rightarrow 0
faster than
n_{1}(t)\rightarrow N
, and conversely,
n_{3}(t)\rightarrow N
faster than
n_{3}(t)\rightarrow 0
, so that we expect a right edge steady state to emerge as a consequence of the system’s driven - dissipative dynamics.

At the same time, considering the conditions under which the left and right edge steady states will emerge, we expect that if we vary the initial number of particles such that from
n_{1}>>n_{3}\rightarrow n_{1}<<n_{3}
, there will be a change in the type of edge state that will emerge, from a left edge steady state to a right edge steady state.

8
Numerical Results

Having established the conditions under which unique edge steady states can exist for trapped ultracold atoms immersed in a background BEC in order to produce a combination of driven and dissipative processes that would evolve the system, we now examine, by numerically solving the time evolution equation governing the dynamics of the system, how these steady states emerge over time.

8.1
Emergence of Edge States when
n_{1}<<n_{j}
and
n_{L+1}<<n_{j}

We first consider the case where the number of atoms initially trapped away from the edges of the harmonic trap array are much greater than the number of atoms initially trapped at the edges of the trap, i. e.
n_{j}>>n_{1},n_{j}>>n_{L+1},1<j<L+1
. As shown in Figs. (
2
) and (
3
) below, the system evolves according to the master equation given by (
37
) in such a way that left edge steady states will emerge by having
n_{1}(t)
increase over time until it reaches a steady state value
n_{1}(t)=N
, where
N
is the total number of ultracold atoms trapped in the system. On the other hand, the trapped ultracold atom states at the harmonic potentials located away from the left edge of the trap array at
x_{j}=(2j-L-2)x_{a},1<j\leq L+1
will vanish, with
n_{j}(t)
decreasing over time until
n_{j}(t)=0
, irrespective of how many atoms were initially trapped at these harmonic potentials. These results demonstrate the robustness of the steady edge states of the system, analogous to the emergence and robustness of edge states as steady states in topological quantum matter, and demonstrating the topological nature of this driven - dissipative trapped ultracold atom system.

n_{1}
initially trapped in the harmonic potential at the left edge
x_{1}=-x_{a}

n_{L+1}
initially trapped in the harmonic potential at the right edge
x_{L+1}=x_{a}
of the array, while keeping the number of atoms
n_{j},1<j<L+1

x_{j}=(2j-L-2)x_{a},1<j<L+1

x_{1}=-Lx_{a}

9
Discussion of Results

It can be seen from the analytical and numerical results from the previous sections that the edge states, characterized by the number of particles in that state being equal to the total number of atoms in the ultracold atom gas, will emerge as steady states of the trapped ultracold atom open quantum system, irrespective of the number of harmonic potentials used to trap these atoms. At the same time, the manner in which these edge states emerge will depend on the number of atoms initially trapped in the harmonic potentials located at points
x_{j}=(2j-L-2)x_{a},j=1,2,...,L+1,x_{a}\in\Re
on the trap array, and the type of edge states that will emerge (left edge states or right edge states) will depend on the number of atoms initially trapped at the edges of the trap array, located at
x_{1}=-Lx_{a}
and
x_{L+1}=Lx_{a}
. The emergence of these edge states as unique steady states of the trapped ultracold atom open quantum system, and their robustness despite the reduction of the number of atoms initially trapped in one or both of the edges of the harmonic potential trapping array, signifies that this trapped ultracold atom open quantum system is a topological open quantum system, with topological invariants characterized by the non - Hermitian master equation governing the dynamics of the system.

It is apparent that the emergence of these unique edge steady states will only depend on the number of atoms initially trapped at each edge of the harmonic potential array. In particular, right edge steady states will emerge if and only if
n_{L+1}>>n_{1}
, while left edge steady states will emerge if
n_{1}~O(n_{L+1})
, even if
n_{1}<n_{L+1}
. However, for a given number
n_{L+1}
of atoms trapped in the right edge of the harmonic potential array, one can only determine the number of atoms
n_{1}
trapped in the left edge of the harmonic potential array that will ensure the emergence of right edge steady states by numerical evolution of the trapped ultracold atom open quantum system according to Eq. (
37
). More importantly, the results show that the emergence of left edge steady states are favored by this open quantum system, considering that a greater range of values for
n_{1}
will correspond to left edge steady states, even if
n_{1}<n_{L+1}
. Determining why this is the case is beyond the scope of this study, and it is recommended that further investigations be carried out to establish the reason why left edge steady states are favored for this trapped ultracold atom open quantum system. Nonetheless, the results have a wide array of possible applications involving quantum transport of ultracold atoms, dissipative quantum state preparation, and quantum technologies such as dissipative quantum computing and quantum batteries, which require localized unique many-body steady states.

Finally, the numerical results indicate that for the left-edge steady states with
n_{1}<n_{L+1}
, the crossing point, or the value of
n_{j}(t),j=1,L+1
for which
n_{1}(t)\approx n_{L+1}(t)
at a given instant of time
t
, is the topological invariant for this trapped ultracold atom open quantum system. This is because for
n_{1}<n_{L+1}
, for all possible values of
n_{1}
and
n_{L+1}
that satisfy this condition,
n_{1}(t)
increases over time, first gradually then at an increasing rate, while
n_{L+1}(t)
decreases simultaneously in a similar manner, such that they will approach a value for which their magnitudes are close to, if not equal to, each other, and continue on their respective increase and decrease in value at a decreasing rate until they approach their steady-state values, which for
n_{1}(t)
is the total number of ultracold atoms
N
in the system and for
n_{L+1}(t)
is equal to zero. This crossover point for
n_{1}(t)
and
n_{L+1}(t)
is present for all plots of these particle number expectation values with respect to time, so long as their initial values satisfy the condition
n_{1}<n_{L+1}
, and is analogous to the phase transition point for a condensed matter system. Thus, under the definition of a topological invariant as stated in Ref. (
[
41
]
), with the variation of the values of
n_{1}
and
n_{L+1}
to be considered as the homeomorphisms of the graphs of
n_{1}(t)
and
n_{L+1}(t)
, respectively, the crossing point is, indeed, the topological invariant for this trapped ultracold atom open quantum system, signifying the status of this open quantum system as a topological quantum system. It remains to be seen if there are other topological invariants present in the system, such as for example winding numbers corresponding to the energy eigenvalues for this system’s Hamiltonian, but there is no doubt that the search for such topological invariants characterizing this open quantum system will provide further insight into the rich dynamical behavior underlying this form of driven quantum matter.
