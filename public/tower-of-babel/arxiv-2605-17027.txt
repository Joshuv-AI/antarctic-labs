Clipped Stochastic Gradient Tracking For Locally Smooth Functions
Leilei Mei, Junyu Zhang

Abstract
Most stochastic gradient tracking (GT) methods adopt pre-scheduled stepsize rules, while a few recent works studied adaptive stepsizes that attempt to respond to the problem’s local landscape. These methods are typically built upon the problem’s global smoothness constant in both analysis and implementation, even for the adaptive ones. On the one hand, for many problems the local smoothness constant may vary drastically across the domain, and sometimes even unbounded, using the global upper bound of the local constants is too conservative. On the other hand, drastic stepsize changes can cause difficulties in the analysis of convergence and consensus of distributed algorithms, making the direct use of local smoothness constants risky and theoretically challenging. In this paper, we propose a Relative Uniform Continuity (RUC) regularity condition for the local smoothness constant as a function of sets. The RUC condition covers most common growth functions for local smoothness constant, ranging from constant and logarithmic to polynomial and even exponential. For RUC-regular distributed optimization problems with finite-sum structure, we derive a clipped gradient tracking method with staggered variance reduction, which only relies on the local smoothness of objective functions, and an 𝒪 ⁡ ( ∑ i n i 1.5 + n i 0.5 ​ ϵ − 1 ) \mathcal{O}(\sum_{i}n_{i}^{1.5}+n_{i}^{0.5}\epsilon^{-1}) complexity has been established for our algorithm.

Note that the state-of-the-art variance reduced algorithms

Therefore, a straightforward idea is replacing the global smoothness constant by its local counterparts in each epoch of the variance reduced methods. Unfortunately, such a direct modification may not necessarily lead to a convergent method, which is mainly due to the technical intricacies of GT-based variance reduction methods in balancing consensus and convergence, as well as designing a descending Lyapunov functions that governs the algorithm’s convergence behavior.

To resolve this gap, we introduce a new
Relative Uniform Continuous
(RUC) regularity condition for the local
smoothness constant. Under this condition, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
so that the
relative difference
between the local smoothness constants satisfies

\left|1-\max\left\{\frac{\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{X})}{\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{Y})},\frac{\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{Y})}{\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\right|<\epsilon\qquad\forall d_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta,

where

stands for the Hausdorff distance of the two sets. We should notice that the above relative difference bound is much milder than requiring the absolute value difference
|\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{X})-\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{Y})|
to be small in our interested problem setting, where both
\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{Y})

In this paper, we show that this condition covers a lot of common growth

\mathbb{L}(\cdot)
for local smoothness constants, ranging from constant and logarithmic to polynomial and even exponential. In particular, the
\alpha
-generalized smoothness condition with
\alpha\in(0,1]

\mathbb{L}(\cdot)
, such as affine composition, scaling, linear combination, and point-wise maximum or minimum, making it convenient to construct or check new growth function for local smoothness constant by manipulating the known RUC-regular functions.

If the objective function of problem (
2
) is RUC-regular, then it is implied that the local smoothness constants do not change so fast in a
relative
sense, even though they may still vary drastically in the

Based on this technique, we design a staggered variance reduced algorithms with clipped gradient tracking to solve problem (
2

O(\sqrt{n}/\epsilon)

\epsilon

2
Relative uniform continuous regularity condition

As discussed in Section
1
, we need to regularize the variation of the local Lipschitz constant
\mathbb{L}(\cdot)
so as to balance the consensus error and descent progress. Denote
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the family of all nonempty compact subsets
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the local smoothness constant of some objective function
\mathbb{L}:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
is a positive-valued set function that maps any closed set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{X})
, (an upper bound of) the function’s local smoothness constant on
\mathcal{X}
. We introduce a relative uniform continuous (RUC) regularity condition for the local Lipschitz constant as a set function of the considered neighborhood. To start with, for any two compact convex sets
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we adopt the Hausdorff distance to measure their difference:

d_{\rm H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}):=\max\left\{\max_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\min_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\|x-y\|\,,\,\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\|x-y\|\right\},

see in
[
3
]
, which is different from the standard definition of the distance between two sets. We should note that the norm here is not necessarily the
\ell_{2}
-norm. When the variables are matrices, the norm
\|\cdot\|
can be the spectral norm instead of the Frobenius norm. Based on this concept, we introduce the RUC regularity condition as follows.

Definition 2.1

(RUC regularity)
.

We say a positive-valued set function
F:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
is relative uniform continuous, if
\forall\epsilon>0
,
\exists\delta>0
s.t. the relative function value difference satisfies

\left|1-\max\left\{\frac{F(\mathcal{X})}{F(\mathcal{Y})},\frac{F(\mathcal{Y})}{F(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\right|\leq\epsilon

for
\forall\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
d_{\rm H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta
.

This condition can be viewed as a generalization of the classic uniform continuity concept to set functions and relative difference. Compared to the absolute difference, the use of relative difference allows a much more drastic change of function values. To illustrate this point, we can consider the counterpart of RUC for standard univariate function. Take the exponential function
f(x)=e^{x}
for example. The standard uniform continuity property defined under the absolute difference requires:

\forall\epsilon>0,\exists\delta>0\quad\text{s.t.}\quad|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\epsilon\quad\text{for}\quad\forall|x-y|\leq\delta.

This property is clearly not satisfied by
e^{x}
because
e^{x+\delta}-e^{x}=e^{x}(e^{\delta}-1)\to+\infty
as
x\to+\infty
.
That is, the uniform continuity under absolute function value difference is too restrictive and cannot describe the fast changing functions. However, if we switch to the relative difference for positive-valued functions, we have
|1-\max\{e^{x}/e^{y},e^{y}/e^{x}\}|=e^{|x-y|}-1
, which implies that

\forall\epsilon>0,\exists\delta=\ln(1+\epsilon)>0\quad\mbox{s.t.}\quad e^{|x-y|}-1<\epsilon\quad\mbox{for}\quad\forall|x-y|<\delta.

Hence
e^{x}
is a univariate RUC function. By extending this observation to a general positive-valued set function

F:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
, and by replacing the Euclidean
distance between points with the Hausdorff distance between compact sets, we
arrive at Definition
2.1
.

Denote
\mathcal{F}_{\text{ruc}}^{d}
the class of all RUC functions over
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then this function class is closed under various common operations, which is summarized as Proposition
2.2
.

Proposition 2.2

(Closedness of
\mathcal{F}_{\text{ruc}}^{d}
)
.

The RUC function class
\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
is closed under affine composition, positive linear combination, maximum/minimum, and multiplication/division operations:

(1).

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ruc}}^{d}
and let

\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be globally Lipschitz continuous. That is,
\exists L>0
s.t.

\|\mathcal{A}(x)-\mathcal{A}(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|,\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}.

For
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})
define

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}):=\{\mathcal{A}(x):x\in\mathcal{X}\}
and

G(\mathcal{X}):=F(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}))
.
Then
G\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ruc}}^{d^{\prime}}
.

(2)
.
If
F,G\in\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
, then
\alpha F+\beta G\in\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
for any
\alpha,\beta>0
.

(3)
.
If
F,G\in\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
, then both
\max\{F,G\}\in\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
and
\min\{F,G\}\in\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
.

(4)
.
If
F,G\in\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
, then both
F\cdot G\in\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
and
F/G\in\mathcal{F}_{\emph{ruc}}^{d}
.

Proof.

First, for ease of notation, let us denote relative difference as

d_{F}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}):=\left|1-\max\left\{\frac{F(\mathcal{X})}{F(\mathcal{Y})},\frac{F(\mathcal{Y})}{F(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\right|.

Then it is not hard to observe that
d_{F}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\epsilon
if and only if
\max\left\{\frac{F(\mathcal{X})}{F(\mathcal{Y})},\frac{F(\mathcal{Y})}{F(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\leq 1+\epsilon
. First, let us prove (1). By the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{A}
, the induced set mapping is also

L
-Lipschitz continuous under the Hausdorff distance:

d_{H}(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}),\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Y}))\leq L\cdot d_{H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}),\qquad\forall\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}).

Indeed, for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
we can find
y\in\mathcal{Y}
with
\|x-y\|\leq d_{H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
,
and the Lipschitz property of
\mathcal{A}
yields

\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{A}(x),\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Y}))\leq L\cdot d_{H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
, then taking maximum over all
x\in\mathcal{X}
proves the first half of bound on Hausdorff distance between
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Y})
; the other half follows by symmetry.
Next, fix an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
.
Because
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ruc}}^{d}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

d_{H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta
implies

d_{F}^{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\epsilon
for all

\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
\delta^{\prime}:=\delta/L
.
Then for any
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})
with

d_{H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta^{\prime}
, we have

d_{H}(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}),\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Y}))\leq L\,d_{H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq L\delta^{\prime}=\delta,

and hence, by definition that
G(\mathcal{X}):=F(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}))
, we obtain

d_{G}^{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=d_{F}^{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{X}),\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Y}))\leq\epsilon,

which shows
G\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ruc}}^{d^{\prime}}
. This completes the proof of (1).

Next we prove (2). Because
F,G\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta_{F},\delta_{G}>0
s.t.

d_{F}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\epsilon
for any
d_{\rm H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta_{F}
, and
d_{G}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\epsilon
for any
d_{\rm H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta_{G}
. Taking
\delta=\min\{\delta_{F},\delta_{G}\}
, the above argument indicates that

F(\mathcal{X})\leq(1+\epsilon)F(\mathcal{Y})
and
G(\mathcal{X})\leq(1+\epsilon)G(\mathcal{Y})
simultaneously hold for any
d_{\rm H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta
, consequently

\frac{\alpha F(\mathcal{X})+\beta G(\mathcal{X})}{\alpha F(\mathcal{Y})+\beta G(\mathcal{Y})}\leq 1+\epsilon.

By the symmetry between
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, repeating the above discussion with interchanged
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
gives

\max\left\{\frac{\alpha F(\mathcal{X})+\beta G(\mathcal{X})}{\alpha F(\mathcal{Y})+\beta G(\mathcal{Y})},\frac{\alpha F(\mathcal{Y})+\beta G(\mathcal{Y})}{\alpha F(\mathcal{X})+\beta G(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\leq 1+\epsilon,

which implies an
\epsilon
-small relative difference:
d_{\alpha F+\beta G}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\epsilon
. Hence we complete the proof of (2) and conclude that
\alpha F+\beta G\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
.

Next, we prove (3). For any
\epsilon>0
, let us inherit the choice of
\delta
from the proof of (2). For any
d_{\rm H}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta
, we assume without loss of generality that
F(\mathcal{X})\geq G(\mathcal{X})
. Then we have

\frac{\max\{F(\mathcal{X}),G(\mathcal{X})\}}{\max\{F(\mathcal{Y}),G(\mathcal{Y})\}}=\frac{F(\mathcal{X})}{\max\{F(\mathcal{Y}),G(\mathcal{Y})\}}\overset{(i)}{\leq}\frac{F(\mathcal{X})}{F(\mathcal{Y})}\leq 1+\epsilon,

where (i) is because
F(\mathcal{Y})\leq\max\{F(\mathcal{X}),G(\mathcal{X})\}
. Similar to the proof of (2), we can further use the symmetry between
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
to show
d_{\max\{F,G\}}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\epsilon
and
\max\{F,G\}\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
.

For the minimum function, we assume without loss of generality that
F(\mathcal{Y})\geq G(\mathcal{Y})
, then

\frac{\min\{F(\mathcal{X}),G(\mathcal{X})\}}{\min\{F(\mathcal{Y}),G(\mathcal{Y})\}}=\frac{\min\{F(\mathcal{X}),G(\mathcal{X})\}}{G(\mathcal{Y})}\overset{(ii)}{\leq}\frac{G(\mathcal{X})}{G(\mathcal{Y})}\leq 1+\epsilon,

where (ii) is due to
G(\mathcal{X})\geq\min\{F(\mathcal{X}),G(\mathcal{X})\}
. Analogous to the maximum case, we can again use the symmetry argument to show
d_{\min\{F,G\}}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\epsilon
and hence
\min\{F,G\}\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
.

Finally, we prove (4). Given any fixed
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, let us inherit the choice of
\delta
from the proof of (2) with
\epsilon
satisfying

(1+\epsilon)^{2}=1+\epsilon^{\prime}
. Then direct computation already implies that
F\cdot G\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
, and we omit this simple and sheer computation for succinctness. The division is also simple. Note that the symmetric definition of relative difference indicates that
d_{\min\{G\}}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\equiv d_{\min\{1/G\}}^{\rm rel}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
for any
\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})
. Hence
G\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
indicates that
1/G\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
. Then
F/G\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
will hold by viewing the division between
F
and
G
as the multiplication between
F
and
1/G
.

Based on the closedness property, if we can provide some basic RUC functions in
\mathcal{F}_{\rm ruc}^{d}
, then one can construct new RUC-regular functions or verify RUC-regularity by affine composition, scaling, linear combination, or taking their piecewise variants by minimum or maximum operations. We notice that for many problems, the local smoothness constant
\mathbb{L}(\cdot)
grows in a point-wise maximum form
\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{X}):=\max\{l(x):x\in\mathcal{X}\}
for some function
l(\cdot)
. In the next proposition, we provide a general sufficient condition for

to guarantee the RUC-regularity of
\mathbb{L}(\cdot)
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a positive valued function. If

\log(f)
is uniformly continuous, then the set function
F:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
defined by
F(\mathcal{X}):=\max\{f(x):x\in\mathcal{X}\}

is RUC-regular.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and set
\eta=\log(1+\epsilon)
. By uniform continuity of

\log(f)
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
|\log f(x)-\log f(y)|\leq\eta
for all
\|x-y\|\leq\delta
.
Exponentiating this inequality yields

\frac{f(x)}{f(y)}\leq e^{\eta}=1+\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}\leq 1+\epsilon,\qquad\forall\|x-y\|\leq\delta.

(3)

Now take any
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

d_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta
. By compactness of
\mathcal{X}
and
continuity of
f
, we can find
x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
s.t.

F(\mathcal{X})=f(x^{*})
. By
d_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq\delta
, we can find some
y\in\mathcal{Y}
s.t.
\|x^{*}-y\|\leq\delta
. Applying
(
3
) gives

\frac{F(\mathcal{X})}{F(\mathcal{Y})}\leq\frac{f(x^{*})}{f(y)}\leq 1+\epsilon

and

F(\mathcal{Y})/F(\mathcal{X})\leq 1+\epsilon

F
.

∎

Note that the (global) Lipschitz continuity or Hölder continuity implies the uniform continuity. Therefore, proving these stronger continuity conditions for

could be a more quantitative way to establish the RUC-regularity of

.

Example 2.6

Clipped gradient tracking with staggered variance reduction

In this section, we present the clipped gradient tracking method with staggered variance reduction, as well as its simplified synchronous version. We call them

Assumption 3.1

For each node

As the local smoothness constants of some locally smooth function

Assumption 3.2

For each node

Assumption 3.3

The objective function of problem (

Finally, for the communication network of the distributed optimization problem, with node set

In Algorithm

\tau

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose the mixing matrix
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
satisfies Assumption
3.4

\{\mathbf{x}^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}

\mathbf{x}^{t+1}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}^{t}+\mathbf{v}^{t}
from a consensus initialization
x_{1}^{0}=x_{2}^{0}=\cdots=x_{m}^{0}
and an arbitrary sequence
\{\mathbf{v}^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
such that
\|\mathbf{v}^{t}\|_{2,\infty}\leq\delta
for some fixed
\delta>0
and
\forall t\geq 0

\{\mathbf{x}^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}

\left\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\right\|\leq\delta\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\|\mathbf{x}^{t}-{\bf 1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\right\|_{F}\leq{c\delta}/{2},

where
c=\frac{2\sqrt{2m}}{1-\eta}
is a constant.

Proof.

First, due to the fact that
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{\bf 1}={\bf 1}
, it always hold that
\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}=\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}+\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{t}.
Hence, we have

\left\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\right\|=\left\|({\bf 1}/m)^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{t}\right\|\leq\left\|\mathbf{v}^{t}\right\|_{2,\infty}\cdot\|{\bf 1}/m\|_{1}\leq\delta.

Second, with the constant
\zeta=1-\eta\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}^{t+1}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\right\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}/m\right)\left(\mathbf{x}^{t}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\right)+\left(I-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}/m\right)\mathbf{v}^{t}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\zeta)\left\|\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}/m\right\|^{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}^{t}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\right\|_{F}^{2}+(1+1/\zeta)\left\|I-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}/m\right\|^{2}\left\|\mathbf{v}^{t}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

By (
5
) and using our special choice of

as well as the condition that

, we obtain

\left\|\mathbf{x}^{t+1}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\right\|^{2}_{F}\leq(2-\eta)\eta^{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}^{t}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{2-\eta}{1-\eta}\left\|\mathbf{v}^{t}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\eta\left\|\mathbf{x}^{t}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{2m\delta^{2}}{1-\eta},

which further indicates that

\|\mathbf{x}^{t}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}_{F}\leq\frac{2m\delta^{2}}{(1-\eta)^{2}}=c^{2}\delta^{2}/4
for all
t\geq 0
given the consensus initialization that
x_{1}^{0}=x_{2}^{0}=\cdots=x_{m}^{0}
, which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

regardless of

and

directly applies to the iteration sequence

generated by

for all

Lemma 4.1

Suppose that the Assumption

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the same conditions of Lemma

\displaystyle f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1})\leq f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})-\frac{(\theta-2)L_{\min}^{t}}{8\gamma^{2}}\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta_{\min}^{t}}^{\delta}(\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}))\big\|^{2}-\frac{(\theta-2)L_{\min}^{t}}{2m}\left\|\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\beta_{t}}(\mathbf{y}^{t})\right\|_{F}^{2}

Lemma 4.4

Under the same Assumptions of Lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\!\mathbb{E}\bigg[\!\frac{(\theta\!-\!2)L_{\min}^{t}}{32}\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\!\beta_{\min}^{t}}^{\delta}\!\!(\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}))\big\|^{2}\!+\!\frac{\theta L_{\min}^{t}}{4m}\big\|\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\beta_{t}}\!(\mathbf{y}^{t})\big\|_{F}^{2}\!+\!\frac{3L_{\min}^{t}}{4m}\|\mathbf{x}^{t}\!-\!\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|_{F}^{2}\!+\!\frac{\|\mathbf{y}^{t}\!-\!\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{y}}^{t}\|_{F}^{2}}{16mL_{\min}^{t}}\bigg]\!\leq P_{0}\!-\!P_{T}.

for arbitrarily selected parameter

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
and
\theta_{0}
are presented in Appendix

for succinctness. We should notice that
P_{0}-P_{T}
is upper bounded by a constant in that

\displaystyle P_{0}=f(\bar{\bf x}^{0})+\alpha_{1}L_{\min}^{0}\|{\bf x}^{0}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\bf x}^{0}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\alpha_{2}\|{\bf y}^{0}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\bf y}^{0}\|^{2}_{F}}{L_{\min}^{0}}=f(x^{0})+\frac{5\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\nabla f(x^{0})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{0})\|^{2}}{2m(1-\eta)L_{\min}^{0}},

\displaystyle P_{T}=\mathbb{E}\bigg[f(\bar{\bf x}^{T})+\alpha_{1}L_{\min}^{T}\|{\bf x}^{0}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\bf x}^{T}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\alpha_{2}\|{\bf y}^{T}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\bf y}^{T}\|^{2}_{F}}{L_{\min}^{T}}\bigg]\geq\mathbb{E}\big[f(x^{T})\big]\geq f^{*},

where

is defined by Assumption

. This further implies the upper bound on the potential difference for all

that

P_{0}-P_{T}\leq f(x^{0})-f^{*}+\frac{5\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\nabla f(x^{0})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{0})\|^{2}}{2m(1-\eta)L_{\min}^{0}}=:\Delta_{f}<+\infty.

(17)

Therefore, combining this upper bound with Lemma
4.4
, we know that
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{x}^{t}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|_{F}^{2}]\leq\mathcal{O}(1/T)
, which indicates that the agents can achieve consensus in a sublinear rate. However, due to the presence of clipping operator, it is not clear what

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta_{\min}^{t}}^{\delta}(\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}))\|^{2}\big]=\mathcal{O}(1/T)
stands for. Therefore, we present the following observation of this term.

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
\beta,\delta,\epsilon>0
, and any vector
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

(i).
Suppose
\epsilon<\delta^{2}
, then
\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}\leq\epsilon
implies
u/\beta=\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)
and
\|u/\beta\|^{2}\leq\epsilon.

(ii).
Suppose
\epsilon<\delta^{2}/2
, and
u
is a random vector, then the bound
\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}\big]\leq\epsilon
implies that the event
\mathcal{E}:=\{\omega:u/\beta=\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\}
satisfies

{\rm Prob}(\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\epsilon/\delta^{2}=1-\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}\big[\|u/\beta\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{E}\big]\leq\epsilon+2\epsilon^{2}/\delta^{2}=O(\epsilon).

Proof.

The proof of (i) is straightforward because the definition of clipping operator
7
directly states that
u/\beta\neq\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)
implies
\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}=\delta^{2}
, which is impossible when
\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}\leq\epsilon<\delta^{2}
. To prove (ii), denote probability
{\rm Prob}(\mathcal{E}^{c})=p
, then it suffices to notice that

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=(1-p)\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{E}\big]+p\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{E}^{c}\big]

\displaystyle=(1-p)\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{E}\big]+p\delta^{2},

which immediately gives

p\leq\epsilon/\delta^{2}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)\|^{2}|\mathcal{E}]\leq\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon/\delta^{2}}\leq\epsilon(1+2\epsilon/\delta^{2}),

where we use the fact that
\frac{1}{1-x}\leq 1+2x
when
x\in[0,1/2]
, which completes the proof.
∎

As a consequence of the above proposition, we know that the behavior of
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}^{\delta}(u)
and
u/\beta
are almost identical for small enough
\epsilon
. And we can define the following gradient mapping to measure the near-stationarity of the
CGTVR-stag
algorithm:

\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})=\beta_{\min}^{t}\cdot\mathcal{T}_{\beta_{\min}^{t}}^{\delta}(\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}))

(18)

where we multiply the inverse stepsize
\beta_{\min}^{t}
to cancel out the scaling in the clipping operator
\mathcal{T}_{\beta_{\min}^{t}}^{\delta}(\cdot)
. As a consequence, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.6
.

Under the same Assumptions of Lemma
4.1
and we select the parameters according to Lemma
4.4
. In addition, suppose there is an upper bound
\bar{L}>0
such that
L_{\min}^{t}\leq\bar{L},\forall t\geq 0
, then

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\bf x}^{t_{\rm out}})\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{32\theta^{2}\bar{L}\Delta_{f}}{(\theta-2)T}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{m}\big\|x^{t_{\rm out}}_{i}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}^{t_{\rm out}}\big\|^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{4\Delta_{f}}{3T},

(19)

where the time point
t_{\rm out}
is randomly selected from
\{0,1,\cdots,T-1\}
.

Therefore, it takes
T=\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon)
iterations to make the expected squared gradient mapping
\epsilon
-small.
Beside iteration complexity, an important issue is to bound the sample consumption during the algorithm. However, due to the existence of the early stop mechanism (Algorithm
2
, Line 14-15), a worst scenario could be that the agents restart their local epoch in every iteration and full gradient is always computed. In this case, no improvement can be expected compared to the naive full gradient deterministic variant of
CGTVR-stag
. Next, we prove that the restart cannot happen too frequently, using which an expected sample complexity can be obtained.

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
-
3.4
hold, we set
B_{i}=\tau_{i}=\sqrt{n_{i}}
for each agent
i\in[m]
, and each agent
i
executes Algorithm
2
Line 15 and early restarts the epoch for
K_{i}
times among all the iterations, if we select
\delta
and
d
such that
c\delta\leq d/2
, then

\mathbb{E}[K_{i}]\leq\frac{16\Delta_{f}\tau_{i}}{\theta d^{2}}=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n_{i}})

for
\forall i\in[m]
. If in addition that the upper bound
\bar{L}
introduced in Theorem
4.6
exists, then combined with (
19
), we can upper bound the expected total sample consumption for finding some point
\bar{\bf x}^{t_{\rm out}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\bf x}^{t_{\rm out}})\|^{2}]\leq\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
by
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}\big(n_{i}^{1.5}+n_{i}^{0.5}\epsilon^{-1}\big)
.

Proof.

Using the fact that
\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}=(\mathbf{1}/m)^{\top}\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\beta_{t}}(\mathbf{y}^{t})
, Assumption
3.1
, and Lemma
4.4
, we get

\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\beta_{t}}(\mathbf{y}^{t})\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{4\Delta_{f}}{\theta}.

(20)

If an agent
i
start an epoch at time
r
and execute an early restart at time
r+s
with
s<\tau_{i}
. Then Line 14 of Algorithm
2
suggests that
\|x_{i}^{r+s}-x_{i}^{r}\|\geq d
, together with Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\|\bar{\bf x}^{r+s}-\bar{\bf x}^{r}\|\geq\|x_{i}^{r+s}-x_{i}^{r}\|-\|x_{i}^{r+s}-\bar{\bf x}^{r+s}\|-\|x_{i}^{r}-\bar{\bf x}^{r}\|\geq d-c\delta\geq\frac{d}{2}.

Jensen’s inequality for quadratic function implies that

\sum_{t=r}^{r+s-1}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}\geq\frac{\|\bar{\bf x}^{r+s}-\bar{\bf x}^{r}\|^{2}}{s}\geq\frac{d^{2}}{4\tau_{i}}.

That is, whenever an early restart happens at agent
i
, at least a
d^{2}/4\tau_{i}
constant is contributed to the summation (
20
). And therefore, we have

\frac{d^{2}}{4\tau_{i}}\cdot\mathbb{E}[K_{i}]\leq\frac{4\Delta_{f}}{\theta},

which proves the first bound. Observe that the total number of epochs for agent
i
in
T
iterations is upper bounded by
K_{i}+\lceil T/\tau_{i}\rceil
. That is, at most this number of full local gradient is computed for agent
i
, while all the other steps are using
B_{i}
-size batches. The total sample consumption of the algorithm can then be upper bounded by

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big((K_{i}+\lceil T/\tau_{i}\rceil)\cdot n_{i}+TB_{i}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}\big(n_{i}^{1.5}+\sqrt{n_{i}}T\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}\big(n_{i}^{1.5}+n_{i}^{0.5}\epsilon^{-1}\big),

where the last step is due to Theorem
4.6
, which suggests that
T=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
.
∎

5
Extended discussion on local smoothness constants

In Section
4
, we assume all the local Lipschitz constants
L_{\min}^{t}
to be upper bounded by some constant
\bar{L}
for the ease of presentation, which commonly holds when the objective function is coercive, or more generally, level bounded. However, this simplification leaves two important questions unanswered. First, how does the method differ from the standard non-adaptive gradient tracking method that directly estimates and uses
\bar{L}
as their “global” Lipschitz constant? Second, what if the problem does not have bounded level sets so that the
\bar{L}
does not exist? In this section, we discuss these questions. To decouple the stochasticity between the correlated random variables
L_{\min}^{t}
and
\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{x}^{t})\|
, let us combine Lemma
4.4
and (
18
) to obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{\theta-2}{32\theta^{2}L_{\min}^{t}}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\theta L_{\min}^{t}}{4m}\big\|\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\beta_{t}}(\mathbf{y}^{t})\big\|_{F}^{2}\bigg]\leq\Delta_{f}<+\infty,

where we let
T\to+\infty
in Lemma
4.4
because it holds for any
T\geq 1
, while the upper bound (
17
) is always true. Note that all the random variables in the expectations are all non-negative, then the monotone convergence theorem
[
47
]
implies that we can exchange the summation (infinite) and the expectation. Then applying Markov’s inequality to the inequality after exchanging summation and expectation gives

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\theta-2}{32\theta^{2}L_{\min}^{t}}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\theta L_{\min}^{t}}{4m}\big\|\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\beta_{t}}\!(\mathbf{y}^{t})\big\|_{F}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{\Delta_{f}}{p}\quad\text{w.p.}\quad 1-p.

(21)

Next, we can proceed our analysis based on this bound.

5.1
Discussion given finite upper bound
\bar{L}

By assuming the existence of a large enough
\bar{L}<+\infty
so that the
L_{\min}^{t}
in
inequality (
21
) are all upper bounded by
\bar{L}
, we can obtain for any
T\geq 1
that

\min_{0\leq t\leq T-1}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\bf x}^{t})\|^{2}\leq\bar{L}\cdot\frac{32\theta^{2}\Delta_{f}}{p(\theta-2)T}\quad\text{w.p.}\quad 1-p,

(22)

which is a high probability variant of Theorem
4.6
that utilizes Markov’s inequality. First of all, we should admit that, in the worst case, this bound is tight because one can always consider quadratic functions whose local Lipschitz constant
\mathbb{L}_{i}(\cdot)\equiv L
is a constant. In this case, our adaptive selection of
L_{i}^{t}
exhibits no difference from standard constant
L
approaches. However, in some cases where a significant part of local
L_{i}^{t}
are much smaller than the maximum
\max_{t}L_{i}^{t}
, the adaptive selection of
L_{i}^{t}
can indeed achieve some advantage. In this situation, an alternative sharper bound can be derived from (
21
):

\min_{0\leq t\leq T-1}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\bf x}^{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{L_{\min}^{t}}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|^{2}}{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{L_{\min}^{t}}}\leq\frac{T}{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{L_{\min}^{t}}}\cdot\frac{32\theta^{2}\Delta_{f}}{p(\theta-2)T}\quad\text{w.p.}\quad 1-p.

(23)

Note that through harmonic-arithmetic inequality, one must have

\frac{T}{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{L_{\min}^{t}}}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}L_{\min}^{t}\leq\max_{0\leq t\leq T-1}L_{\min}^{t}\leq\bar{L}

and the equality can only be achieved when
L_{\min}^{0}=\cdots=L_{\min}^{T-1}=\bar{L}
. That is, an “effective” Lipschitz constant in the upper bound is the harmonic average of local Lipschitz constants, which can be much smaller than their common upper bound
\bar{L}
. In fact, in the first experiment on quadratic inverse problem in Section
6
, where the maximum local Lipschitz constant is much larger than the average, (
23
) can provide a much sharper bound than (
22
), explaining the potential advantage of adaptively estimating
L_{i}^{t}
.

5.2
Discussion without finite upper bound
\bar{L}

We should note that although an upper bound
\bar{L}
is assumed to exist, it is not required a prior in the implementation of the algorithm due to the exploitation of local smoothness constants. When the problem is coercive, the existence of such an
\bar{L}
is often satisfied by practical applications. Yet, theoretically, there indeed exist corner cases where such an
\bar{L}
does not exists. This can only happen in case where the iteration sequence, although with monotonically decreasing potential function
P_{t}
, diverges to infinity. For example, one may consider the following hand-crafted two-dimensional instance

\min_{x_{1},x_{2}}\,\,f(x_{1},x_{2}):=\frac{1}{\ln(x_{1}^{2}+2)}+(\sin^{2}x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})^{2}.

Clearly, a global optimal solution does not exist as the infimum
0
is only approached (but not obtained) when
|x_{1}|\to+\infty
. In the meantime, a direct computation shows that
\|\nabla^{2}f(x_{1},x_{2})\|\to+\infty
, indicating an unbounded local smoothness constant.

In this case, we cannot simply argue the existence of a finite
\bar{L}<+\infty
to upper bound all the locally estimated Lipschitz constants. However, akin to the analysis in
[
55
, Section 4]
, it is still possible to bound the local Lipschitz constants before finding an
\epsilon
-solution, as stated in the next proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the setting of Lemma
4.1
while taking
\theta\geq 4
, and we select the parameters according to Lemma
4.4
. Let
T_{\epsilon}:=\inf\{t:\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|^{2}\leq\epsilon\}

be the random time point when the algorithm first hits an
\epsilon
-small squared gradient mapping. Then w.p.
1-p
, it holds for all
t<T_{\epsilon}
and
i\in[m]
that

\big\|x_{i}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0}\big\|\leq\frac{8\,\Delta_{f}}{\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot p}+\frac{\sqrt{2m}\delta}{1-\eta}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad L_{i}^{t}\leq\mathbb{L}_{i}\left(B\left(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0},\frac{8\Delta_{f}}{\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot p}+\frac{\sqrt{2m}\delta}{1-\eta}+r_{0}\right)\right).

Proof.

Applying the relationship between

\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}
and
\|T^{\delta}_{\beta_{t}}(\mathbf{y}^{t})\|^{2}
to (
21
) yields w.p.
1-p
that

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{f}}{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\bigg(\frac{\theta-2}{32\theta^{2}L_{\min}^{t}}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\theta L_{\min}^{t}}{4}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}2\sqrt{\frac{\theta-2}{32\theta^{2}L_{\min}^{t}}\cdot\frac{\theta L_{\min}^{t}}{4}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|^{2}\cdot\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\sqrt{\frac{\theta-2}{32\theta}}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|\cdot\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|\cdot\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\|,

where the last line is due to
\theta\geq 4
. Then truncating the infinite summation at
T_{\epsilon}
yields

\frac{8\Delta_{f}}{p}\geq\sum_{t=0}^{T_{\epsilon}-1}\|\mathcal{G}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\|\cdot\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\|\geq\sum_{t=0}^{T_{\epsilon}-1}\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\|\mathbf{x}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\|\quad\mbox{ w.p. }\quad 1-p.

Therefore, we have with probability at least
1-p
that

\displaystyle\max_{k<T_{\epsilon}}\max_{i\in[m]}\|x_{i}^{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0}\|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{k<T_{\epsilon}}\max_{i\in[m]}\Big(\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0}\|+\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-x_{i}^{k}\|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T_{\epsilon}-1}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t+1}\|+\frac{\sqrt{2m}\delta}{1-\eta}\leq\frac{8\Delta_{f}}{\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot p}+\frac{\sqrt{2m}\delta}{1-\eta}.

where we use the fact that the clipped update ensures that each local iterate to lie within
\frac{\sqrt{2m}\delta}{1-\eta}
-distance of the averaged iterate. Therefore, for all pairs
(i,t)
at which the local Lipschitz constant
L_{i}^{t}=\mathbb{L}_{i}(\mathcal{X}_{i}^{t})
are estimated, we know

\mathcal{X}_{i}^{t}=B(x_{i}^{t},r_{0})\subseteq B\left(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0},\frac{8\Delta_{f}}{\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot p}+\frac{\sqrt{2m}\delta}{1-\eta}+r_{0}\right).

Then using the monotonicity of the set function
\mathbb{L}_{i}
proves the proposition.
∎

Note that the upper bound on
L_{i}^{t}
only relies on the target accuracy
\epsilon
, the confidence level
p
, and predetermined algorithmic parameters. It shows that, even without a finite
\bar{L}
for all
t\geq 0
, one can still bound
L^{t}_{i}
for
t<T_{\epsilon}
with high probability, and hence provide a high probability bound for
T_{\epsilon}
. Define

R_{\epsilon,p}:=\frac{8\Delta_{f}}{\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot p}+\frac{\sqrt{2m}\delta}{1-\eta}+r_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1/2}p^{-1})

as the shorthand for the radius specified in Proposition
5.1
, we provide the following remark.

Remark 5.2
.

Under the setting of Proposition
5.1
, and suppose in addition that the local smoothness constants exhibit a power growth in the sense that
\mathbb{L}_{i}(B(\bar{\bf x}^{0},R))=\mathcal{O}(R^{Q})
for some
Q\geq 0
. Then, combining this proposition and the analysis of Theorem
4.6
implies that, with probability at least
1-p
, we have
T_{\epsilon}\leq\mathcal{O}(R^{Q}_{\epsilon,p}/\epsilon)=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-(Q/2+1)}p^{-Q})
, matching the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-(Q/2+1)})
complexity in
[
53
]
for deterministic and centralized problems.

Therefore, the worst-case iteration complexity of the method can depend on the growth rates of
\mathbb{L}_{i}
, which, to some degree, represent the level of non-Lipschitz smoothness. For general growth function that does not have an explicit form, one may replace the
R_{\epsilon,p}^{Q}
factor in the above iteration complexity bound by
\max_{i}\{\mathbb{L}_{i}(B(\bar{\bf x}^{0},R_{\epsilon,p}))\}
. Nevertheless, this additional
\epsilon
-dependency is inevitable for problems without globally Lipschitz gradients, see discussion in
[
55
]
.

Quadratic inverse problem.

First, we consider the distributed quadratic inverse problem studied in
[
56
]
, which is formulated as

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{{x}_{1},\cdots,{x}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\,\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{n_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\big(b_{i,j}-\langle a_{i,j},x_{i}\rangle^{2}\big)^{2},\qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad x_{i}=x_{j},\forall\,i,j\in[m],

(24)

where
a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are sampling vectors and
b_{i,j}=\langle a_{i,j},{x}_{\rm true}\rangle^{2}+\epsilon_{i,j}
are measurement data with
{x}_{\rm true}
being the signal vector and
\epsilon_{i,j}
being i.i.d. Gaussian noises. We consider two 32 × 32 image signals, Barbara and Baboon, reshaped and then normalized as 1024-dimensional vectors, named
x_{\text{true}}
. We use Gaussian sampling scheme
[
5
]
where each
a_{i,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
is generated by standard Gaussian distribution and the additive Gaussian noise is generated by
\epsilon_{i,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.05)
. Each agent possesses a local dataset of
n_{1}=\cdots=n_{m}=5000
sampling vectors and the corresponding measurement data. We will denote this number as
n
for simplicity. For this formulation,
direct estimation of the local smoothness constant for each agent
i
should be

\mathbb{L}_{i}(\mathcal{X})=\max\bigg\{\Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{i,j}a_{i,j}a_{i,j}^{\top}\Big\|+\frac{3}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|a_{i,j}\|^{4}\cdot\|x\|^{2}:x\in\mathcal{X}\bigg\},

see e.g.
[
4
]
, which easily explodes to
10^{10}
due to its dimension dependence. This estimation can be too conservative for practical computation. In fact, using the property of Gaussian distribution, with high probability, an approximated estimation of local smoothness constant will be around

\mathbb{L}_{i}(\mathcal{X})\approx\max\bigg\{\Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{i,j}a_{i,j}a_{i,j}^{\top}\Big\|+5\|x\|^{2}:x\in\mathcal{X}\bigg\},

which corresponds to case (3) of Proposition
2.3
. In this case, according to Appendix
A
,
r_{0}
can in fact be selected adaptively as
r_{0}\propto\|x^{\rm c}_{i}\|
where
x^{\rm c}_{i}
is the starting point of each local epoch, and then by Theorem
4.6
and
4.7
, we can select
d=\frac{2r_{0}}{3}
and
\delta=\frac{1r_{0}}{3(c+1)}
so that
d+(c+1)\delta\leq r_{0}
and
c\delta<d
for
CGTVR-stag
and select
\delta=r_{0}/(c+\tau)
for
CGTVR-sync
so that (
9
) is satisfied. Here
c=\frac{2\sqrt{2m}}{1-\eta}
and
\eta
can be computed from the mixing matrix that depends on the input network, where we consider
m=16
agents with three different network topologies, a ring network, a 4 × 4 grid network, and an Erdős–Rényi random graph
G(16,0.2)
, see
[
35
]
.

For
GT-VR
that uses a loopless variance reduction scheme associated with a restart probability

suggested in

does not behave well. Such a value of

suggests that a restart will happen every 2 steps on average, where a full batch needs to be taken. This makes algorithm essentially “deterministic” because the full batch is too frequently taken and the numerical performance will be very bad. In our experiment, we do not implement the default batch size and

,

,

and

and a (maximal) epoch length

. For the stepsize,
GT-VR
,
GT-SARAH
, and
D-GET
are all tuned from a logarithmic grid
\{10^{-8},10^{-6},\cdots,10^{2}\}
and we select the best performing one in the report. For
CGTVR-stag
and
CGTVR-sync
, we tune
\theta
from the grid

\min_{U_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d^{\prime}}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{i}}\left\|{x}_{i,k}-U_{i}U_{i}^{\top}{x}_{i,k}\right\|^{2},\quad\text{subject to }{U}_{i}=U_{j},\quad\forall\,i,j\in[m],

Proof of Remark

Proof.

For this remark, we only need to verify the uniform continuity for each
\log(f)
and then apply Proposition
2.3
to finish the proof. First, for case (1) where
f(x)\equiv L
, the Lipschitz continuity is obvious. For case (2) where

f(x)=\log(c+\|x\|)
with
c>1
and define

\phi(t):=\log\log(c+t)
for
t\geq 0
. Then
\phi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{(c+t)\log(c+t)}\leq\frac{1}{c\log c}
for all
t\geq 0,

and
\phi
is Lipschitz continuous on
[0,\infty)
with constant
1/(c\log c)
. Together with the 1-Lipschitz continuity of the Euclidean norm function
\|\cdot\|
, we obtain the
1/(c\log c)
-Lipschitz continuity of the composite function

\log(f(x))=\phi(\|x\|)
.

Next, let us consider the case (3) where
f(x)=L+\|x\|^{\nu}
with
L>0
and
\nu>0
. Similar to case (2), we only need to prove the Lipschitz (or Hölder) continuity of

\phi(t):=\log(L+t^{\nu})
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

When
0<\nu\leq 1
, it holds for all
t,s\geq 0
that
|t^{\nu}-s^{\nu}|\leq|t-s|^{\nu}
. Together with the fact that
|\log a-\log b|\leq|a-b|/\min\{a,b\}
for all
a,b>0
, we obtain

|\phi(t)-\phi(s)|=\big|\log(L+t^{\nu})-\log(L+s^{\nu})\big|\leq\frac{|t^{\nu}-s^{\nu}|}{L}\leq\frac{1}{L}|t-s|^{\nu}.

Therefore
\phi
is
1/L
-Hölder continuous with exponent
\nu
. Again, combined with the 1-Lipschitz continuity of Euclidean norm function, we know
\log(f)=\phi(\|x\|)
is also Hölder continuous when
\nu\leq 1
.

When
\nu>1
, one can directly compute that

\sup_{t\geq 0}\psi^{\prime}(t)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{\nu t^{\nu-1}}{L+t^{\nu}}=L^{-\frac{1}{\nu}}(\nu-1)^{1-\frac{1}{\nu}}<+\infty.

That is,
\phi
is globally Lipschitz continuous. Similar to previous argument, we know
\log(f(x))=\phi(\|x\|)
is Lipschitz continuous when
\nu>1
.

Finally, for case (4) where
f(x)=e^{\|x\|/r}
, we have
\log(f(x))=\|x\|/r
, which is a standard globally
1/r
-Lipschitz continuous. This completes the proof of the remark.
∎

, taking telescopic sum of (

(38)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\,-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\frac{\alpha_{1}(1-\eta)}{2}-\frac{25}{4m}-\frac{120\alpha_{2}}{1-\eta}\Big)L_{\min}^{t}\|\mathbf{x}^{t}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t}\|_{F}^{2}+\Big(\frac{(1-\eta)\alpha_{2}}{2}-\frac{9}{16m}\Big)\frac{\|\mathbf{y}^{t}-\mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{y}}^{t}\|_{F}^{2}}{L_{\min}^{t}}\bigg].

Therefore, if we select the parameters so that
