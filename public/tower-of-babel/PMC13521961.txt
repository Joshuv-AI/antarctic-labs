Breast ultrasound screening in young adult women undergoing infertility treatment at an infertility clinic: a retrospective observational study
Authors: Kanako Nakano, Tadahiko Shien, Hiroyoshi Doihara, Yuko Takahashi, Reina Maeda, Marie Saji, Rei Hirata, Motomi Yukawa, Shizuka Okuda, Miyu Hirano, Toshihiro Habara, Nobuyoshi Hayashi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundYoung adult women are increasingly undergoing infertility treatment; however, evidence regarding breast cancer screening in this population remains limited. Given that most women in this age group have dense breast tissue, for which mammography has limited sensitivity, breast ultrasound (US) is often used as a screening modality. A diagnosis of breast cancer during infertility treatment may affect both oncologic management and reproductive planning. This study aimed to examine the clinical relevance of breast US in women in their 30s undergoing infertility treatment at an infertility clinic.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 1,336 women aged 30–39 years who underwent screening breast US between November 1, 2018, and June 30, 2025. Analyses were performed on a per-woman basis, including only the first screening examination for each participant. Breast composition was categorized according to the BI-RADS 5th edition Atlas. Screening outcomes—including recall rate, positive predictive value (PPV), cancer detection rate, and US findings (mass and non-mass findings)—were assessed using medical records. The continuation of infertility treatment, defined as ongoing or resumed treatment during the diagnostic evaluation period, was descriptively evaluated as part of the clinical context.ResultsOf the 1,336 women, 1,277 (95.6%) were classified as having dense breasts. A total of 140 (10.5%) women were recalled for further evaluation. In these recalled cases, 147 findings were identified, comprising 113 mass lesions (76.8%) and 34 non-mass findings (23.1%). Four breast cancers were identified, corresponding to a cancer detection rate of 0.30% and a PPV of 2.86%. Histological diagnoses included one mucinous carcinoma, two invasive carcinomas of no special type (NST), and one ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS). Following diagnostic evaluation, 124 recalled women (88.6%) continued infertility treatment.ConclusionsBreast US screening in women in their 30s undergoing infertility treatment provides descriptive findings within a specific clinical setting. The observed cancer detection rate appears to be within the range reported in previous studies; however, direct comparisons are limited due to differences in study populations and clinical contexts. The clinical course following recall was described as part of the screening pathway. Further prospective and multicenter studies are required to clarify the clinical significance of breast US screening in this population.Keywords: Breast ultrasonography, Infertility treatment, Women in their 30s, Breast cancer screening, Multidisciplinary care

Introduction

In recent years, the number of women undergoing infertility treatment has increased, and women in their 30s account for nearly half of patients undergoing assisted reproductive technology (ART) in Japan [1]. Concerns have been raised regarding a possible association between hormonal exposure associated with infertility treatment and breast disease, particularly breast cancer [2–8]. However, current evidence does not establish a clear causal relationship between infertility treatment and breast cancer risk [3, 4, 8].

Reproductive factors, especially age at first birth, are well known to influence lifetime breast cancer risk, with later first childbirth associated with an increased risk of disease [9]. Medications used in infertility treatment are primarily intended for ovulation induction and ovarian stimulation and include anti-estrogen agents such as clomiphene, aromatase inhibitors such as letrozole, and gonadotropins including follicle-stimulating hormone and human menopausal gonadotropin [8]. These treatments are associated with transient hormonal fluctuations and may be accompanied by imaging findings such as mass formation, cysts, and fibrocystic changes. However, most of these findings are considered benign, and hormonal exposure related to infertility treatment is generally regarded as short-term and reversible.

Recent studies have suggested that ultrasound-based assessment of glandular tissue composition may be associated with breast cancer risk, even among women with dense breasts on mammography [18]. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously, as they do not directly apply to the specific clinical setting of infertility treatment. In our cohort, 95.6% of participants were classified as having dense breasts, which is consistent with the characteristics of women in their 30s and provides context for the use of US in this population.

However, data regarding screening US in women undergoing infertility treatment remain limited, particularly in clinical settings where reproductive care and breast evaluation may proceed in parallel. Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to describe screening-related outcomes of breast US in women in their 30s undergoing infertility treatment at an infertility clinic in a real-world clinical setting. The continuation of infertility treatment following diagnostic evaluation was descriptively assessed as contextual clinical information.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

This single-center retrospective observational study was conducted at Okayama Couple’s Clinic, a specialized infertility clinic with an affiliated health checkup center. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional review board (IRB) of Okayama Couple’s Clinic (approval No. 2025-17). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design; an opt-out approach was implemented via the clinic’s website. All data were anonymized prior to analysis.

Study population

During the study period, a total of 4,391 women at their first visit to the infertility clinic were offered free breast cancer screening vouchers. The decision to undergo screening was left to each patient’s discretion. From this population, we retrospectively analyzed 1,336 women aged 30–39 years (median age, 34 years; interquartile range, 32–36 years) who voluntarily underwent screening breast US between November 1, 2018, and June 30, 2025. To ensure the independence of the data, all analyses were performed on a per-woman basis, including only the first screening examination for each participant during the study period.

Clinical assessment and outcomes

Breast composition was assessed according to the background echotexture descriptors in the BI-RADS 5th edition US Atlas. To enhance consistency in evaluation, background echotexture was primarily assessed in the upper-outer quadrant (C-region) or in the transition between the upper-inner and upper-outer quadrants (AC-region), where mammary tissue is typically most abundant. Assessments were performed at the site demonstrating the most prominent fibroglandular tissue.

Ultrasonographic findings were evaluated collaboratively by two physicians and one technologist, in accordance with the standard clinical workflow at our institution.

Breast composition was categorized into three patterns: homogeneous background echotexture–fat, homogeneous background echotexture–fibroglandular, and heterogeneous background echotexture.

Screening outcomes included recall rate, PPV, and cancer detection rate. The clinical course following recall was evaluated based on 147 findings identified in 140 women. These were classified into 114 mass lesions (including solid, complex cystic and solid, and cystic lesions) and 33 non-mass findings (including focal hypoechoic areas, architectural distortion, and ductal changes) according to BI-RADS descriptors.

The continuation of infertility treatment was defined as the ability to proceed with the same infertility treatment plan that had been ongoing prior to screening, without interruption during the diagnostic evaluation process. This was assessed during the period from screening to completion of diagnostic evaluation. Reproductive outcomes such as pregnancy or delivery were not included in this definition. The clinical course following recall, including fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC), core needle biopsy (CNB), and imaging follow-up, was descriptively evaluated as part of the clinical context.

Evaluated parameters

Clinical records and the institutional database were reviewed to collect data on ART, including treatment duration, number of cycles, and regimens, as well as reproductive history (including pregnancy history and parity) and family history of breast and ovarian cancer.

For patients diagnosed with breast cancer, detailed postoperative pathological variables were evaluated, including histological type, estrogen receptor (ER) status, progesterone receptor (PgR) status, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status, and Ki-67 labeling index (%). Histological diagnoses were originally made according to the World Health Organization (WHO) Classification of Tumours of the Breast (4th ed.) and were reclassified according to the 5th edition where applicable. Where indicated, BRCA1 and BRCA2 genetic test results were also evaluated. In addition, the interval from screening to biopsy was calculated to assess the timeliness of diagnostic assessment.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize screening outcomes. Continuous variables are presented as means with ranges or medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs), as appropriate. Categorical variables are expressed as numbers and percentages. US findings were extracted from reports stored in the dedicated image server (Tables 1, 2 and 3).

Results

Overall screening outcomes and breast composition

Among the 1,336 women screened, 140 (10.5%) were recalled for further evaluation. Diagnostic procedures included FNAC in 74 women (52.9%, 74/140) and CNB in 20 women (14.3%, 20/140). Four women were diagnosed with breast cancer, corresponding to a cancer detection rate of 0.30% and a PPV of 2.86%.

Regarding breast composition assessed by ultrasound, 95.6% (1,277/1,336) of women were classified as having relatively dense breast tissue. Specifically, 71.0% (949/1,336) were classified as homogeneous background echotexture–fibroglandular and 24.6% (328/1,336) as heterogeneous background echotexture. In the recalled subgroup (n = 140), 105 (75.0%) were classified as fibroglandular, 34 (24.3%) as heterogeneous, and 1 (0.7%) as homogeneous background echotexture–fat (Table 4).

Detailed analysis of US findings

A total of 147 US findings (lesions) were identified in 140 recalled women, indicating that some women had multiple lesions. Mass lesions (solid and mixed masses) were the most frequent (76.8%, 113/147), followed by non-mass lesions (23.1%, 34/147). Non-mass lesions included focal hypoechoic areas (n = 26 lesions), architectural distortion, and ductal changes. All four breast cancer cases presented as solid hypoechoic masses.

Among all mass lesions (n = 113 lesions), irregular shapes were observed in approximately 11.5% (13/113), and non-circumscribed margins were observed in approximately 46.9% (53/113), including indistinct, angular, and microlobulated margins.

In the subset of mass lesions included in the comparative analysis between the follow-up and biopsy groups (n = 105 lesions), lesions undergoing biopsy tended to show a higher frequency of non-circumscribed margins and internal vascularity compared with those managed with follow-up. In addition, calcifications within a mass were observed only in the biopsy group and not in the follow-up group.

Cytological and histological findings

Among the 140 women recalled for further evaluation, 74 underwent fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) and 20 underwent core needle biopsy (CNB). FNAC findings were predominantly benign (Class II), with only one case classified as malignant (Class V), which was subsequently confirmed as mucinous carcinoma. In total, four breast cancer cases were identified, including one mucinous carcinoma, two invasive carcinomas of no special type (NST), and one ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS). Histological diagnoses were confirmed according to the WHO Classification of Tumours of the Breast (5th edition).

Three of the four patients had a history of assisted reproductive technology (ART), and one case was diagnosed after conception. None of the patients had a family history of breast cancer. BRCA1 and BRCA2 genetic testing, performed in three cases, revealed no pathogenic variants (Fig. 1).

Continuation of infertility treatment and clinical context

The continuation of infertility treatment was assessed descriptively to provide clinical context for the screening outcomes. Among the 140 women recalled for further evaluation, 124 (88.6%) continued infertility treatment during the diagnostic evaluation period. In seven women (5.0%), infertility treatment was temporarily interrupted based on clinical management decisions, including breast cancer diagnosis and surgical indications for lesions in which malignancy or phyllodes tumor could not be excluded. This group included both malignant cases and benign lesions that required surgical excision. In some cases, infertility treatment was subsequently resumed. One breast cancer case was diagnosed after conception and was therefore excluded from this assessment. In contrast, nine women (6.4%) did not return to our institution following the screening and diagnostic process, and the reasons for non-continuation were not systematically documented.

No cases were identified in which infertility treatment was discontinued solely due to delays in the diagnostic process; however, this finding should be interpreted with caution given the retrospective nature of the study.

Detailed outcomes of imaging follow-up

Among the 140 women recalled for further evaluation, 94 underwent tissue sampling (FNAC or CNB), while 46 were initially managed without biopsy. The management of these 46 women was as follows: 65.2% (30/46) were recommended for imaging follow-up, 19.6% (9/46) were returned to routine screening based on multi-physician review, and 15.2% (7/46) chose to undergo follow-up at another institution.

Among the 30 women recommended for follow-up at our clinic, 66.7% (20/30) completed follow-up examinations, 23.3% (7/30) did not return, and 10.0% (3/30) were awaiting scheduled follow-up at the time of analysis. Follow-up intervals were determined based on clinical assessment, with 18 women scheduled for follow-up within 6 months and 12 women scheduled for follow-up at approximately 1 year.

Discussion

This study evaluated screening outcomes of breast ultrasound (US) in women in their 30s undergoing infertility treatment at a health checkup center affiliated with an infertility clinic. A distinctive feature of this study is the assessment of ultrasonographic findings in a relatively young population undergoing infertility treatment, with continuation of infertility treatment presented as contextual clinical information rather than a primary endpoint. Evidence regarding US screening in this setting remains limited.

In this study, the cancer detection rate was 0.30%, the positive predictive value (PPV) was 2.86%, and the recall rate was 10.50%. Previous studies of supplemental ultrasound screening have reported cancer detection rates of approximately 0.2–0.5%, PPVs of approximately 3–8%, and recall rates of 7–15% [14–17]. However, these values are presented for contextual reference only, as differences in study population, breast density, screening indication, and clinical setting limit direct comparability. Therefore, the findings of this study should be interpreted as descriptive and context-specific.

On Breast US, mass lesions accounted for the majority of findings, and all malignant lesions were detected as solid masses. Most recalled cases were classified as BI-RADS category 3. Although the malignancy rate of BI-RADS category 3 lesions is generally considered to be less than approximately 2% [18–20], management strategies and follow-up adherence may vary in real-world clinical practice [21–23].

At our institution, lesions without suspicious features were generally managed with short-term imaging follow-up, whereas tissue diagnosis was considered when suspicious imaging features or clinical factors were present. In a subset of category 3 lesions, tissue diagnosis was performed based on imaging features suggestive of malignancy—such as non-circumscribed margins, increased internal vascularity, intralesional calcifications, and interval changes—as well as the clinical context of ongoing infertility treatment. Although many lesions demonstrated circumscribed margins, tissue diagnosis was more likely to be pursued when additional suspicious features were present.

In screening asymptomatic women in their 30s, the potential for overdiagnosis and unnecessary biopsy should be considered. In this study, a relatively large number of needle procedures were performed relative to the number of cancers detected. Although many lesions demonstrated circumscribed margins and would typically be managed with follow-up, patients undergoing infertility treatment may prefer definitive diagnosis over short-term follow-up due to treatment scheduling. In such cases, patient preference may have contributed to higher biopsy rates. However, obtaining a definitive diagnosis through biopsy allowed most patients to continue infertility treatment. Cases in which treatment was not continued were considered to be influenced primarily by non-medical factors.

In this cohort, breast composition was predominantly fibroglandular or heterogeneous, with a high prevalence of dense breasts (95.6%). This characteristic, together with the relatively young age of the population, may have influenced screening outcomes such as recall and biopsy rates. A high prevalence of dense breast tissue has been associated with an increased risk of breast cancer [24], and careful consideration is required to balance early detection with the risk of false positives.

Based on the cancer detection rate observed in this study, breast cancer diagnosed during pregnancy or the postpartum period (pregnancy-associated breast cancer, PABC) may involve delays in diagnosis and limitations in standard treatment, representing a clinically important concern [25, 26]. From this perspective, breast US screening in this population may have potential clinical relevance as part of pre-pregnancy breast evaluation.

Early counseling and multidisciplinary collaboration are recommended for patients seeking fertility preservation [10–12]. Although reproductive outcomes were not directly evaluated in this study, these considerations are important for understanding the role of pre-pregnancy breast assessment.

This study has several limitations. The absence of an appropriate comparator group, potential selection bias due to voluntary participation, and limited generalizability to the general screening population should be acknowledged. In addition, the lack of long-term oncologic and reproductive outcomes and variability in diagnostic strategies across institutions also limit the interpretation of these findings.

In conclusion, breast US screening in women in their 30s undergoing infertility treatment provides descriptive findings within a specific clinical context. While these findings may contribute to a better understanding of screening strategies in this population, further prospective, multicenter studies are required to clarify its clinical significance.

Conclusions

Breast ultrasound screening in women in their 30s undergoing infertility treatment provides descriptive screening outcomes within a specific clinical context. The observed cancer detection rate was within the range reported in prior supplemental screening studies; however, direct comparisons are limited by differences in study populations and clinical settings.

The clinical course following recall was described as part of the screening pathway, including continuation of infertility treatment during the diagnostic evaluation period, and should be interpreted as contextual information rather than an outcome measure.

Further large-scale, prospective studies are warranted to better define the clinical role of breast ultrasound screening in this population.
