Abstract

1 Introduction

Consider an integer
q\geq 2
, and the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{q}=\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
.
We refer to the elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
as
q
-ary
n
-tuples
.
The
Hamming distance
between two
q
-ary
n
-tuples
x
and
y
, denoted
d(x,y)
, equals the number of positions where
x
and
y
have a different entry.
In
[
1
]
, the authors’ study of the hat guessing number of complete bipartite graphs leads them to prove that for any set
S
of at most
e^{n/q}

n
-tuples over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, there exists an
n
-tuple
y\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
such that
d(x,y)<n
for all
x\in S

[
1
, Lemma 3.3]
.
We will phrase this result in another way.
We say that
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}

skirts

y\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
if
d(x,y)=n
, and we say that
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
is a
skirting set
if every
q
-ary
n
-tuple
y
is skirted by some
x\in S
.
We denote the size of the smallest skirting set in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
by
f(n,q)
.
Thus, we can interpret
[
1
, Lemma 3.3]
as stating that
f(n,q)>e^{n/q}
.
In this note, we take a closer look at the function
f(n,q)
.

Note that if
q=2
, then every
n
-tuple only skirts one
n
-tuple, hence trivially
f(n,2)=2^{n}
, and we will focus on the case
q>2
.

We also remark that if we define the graph
G(n,q)
with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
, and where
x
is adjacent to
y
if and only if
d(x,y)=n
, then a skirting set
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
is exactly the same thing as a
total dominating set
in
G(n,q)
, i.e. every vertex of
G(n,q)
has a neighbor in
S
.

2 The asymptotics

We use the asymptotic notation
g(n)=o(h(n))
to mean that
\lim_{n\to\infty}g(n)/h(n)=0
.

Theorem 1
.

For every integer
q\geq 2
, there exists a constant
C_{q}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\sqrt[n]{f(n,q)}
such that
f(n,q)=C_{q}^{(1+o(1))n}
.
Moreover, the sequence
(C_{q})_{q\geq 2}
is non-increasing.

Proof.

Fix an integer
q\geq 2
.
If
S_{n}
and
S_{m}
are skirting sets for
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{m}
respectively of minimum size, then
S_{n}\times S_{m}
(i.e. the set of vectors of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n+m}
obtained by concatenating any vector from
S_{n}
and a vector from
S_{m}
) is a skirting set of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n+m}
.
It readily follows that
f(n+m,q)\leq f(n,q)f(m,q)
.
In particular, the sequence
(\ln f(n,q))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is subadditive.
By Fekete’s Subadditive Lemma,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ln f(n,q)}{n}
exists and equals
L_{q}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{\ln f(n,q)}{n}
.
Therefore
\ln f(n,q)=(1+o(1))nL_{q}
.
Define
C_{q}=e^{L_{q}}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\sqrt[n]{f(n,q)}
.
Then we find that
f(n,q)=C_{q}^{(1+o(1))n}
.

Now observe that if
S
is a skirting set for
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
, then it is also one for
\mathbb{Z}_{q+1}^{n}
(where we identify the elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
with the integers
1,\dots,q
, so that we can see
S
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{q+1}^{n}
).
This implies that
f(n,q)
is non-increasing in
q
, thus,
C_{q}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\sqrt[n]{f(n,q)}
is also non-increasing in
q
.
∎

Hence, to understand the asymptotics of
f(n,q)
, we study the constants
C_{q}
.
Any upper bound on
f(n,q)
translates to an upper bound on
C_{q}
.
To this end, we determine
f(n,q)
for
n<q
.

Lemma 2
.

If
n<q
, then
f(n,q)=n+1
.

Proof.

First we prove that
f(n,q)>n
.
If
S
is a set of
n
vertices in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
, say
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, then we can make a vector
y
with
y(i)=x_{i}(i)
, which is clearly not skirted by any vector of
S
.

On the other hand, if
S
consists of
n+1
scalar multiples of
\mathbf{1}
, then
S
is a skirting set, since every
n
-tuple has at most
n
distinct entries.
∎

We find the following bounds.

Proposition 3
.

For
q\geq 2
, we have
1+\frac{1}{q-1}\leq C_{q}\leq\sqrt[q-1]{q}=1+\frac{\ln q}{q-1}+o\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)
.

Proof.

Since every
q
-ary
n
-tuple skirts
(q-1)^{n}

q
-ary
n
-tuples, if
S
is a skirting set in
G(n,q)
, then
|S|(q-1)^{n}\geq q^{n}
, from which
f(n,q)\geq\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{n}
.
This implies that
C_{q}\geq\frac{q}{q-1}
.

On the other hand,
C_{q}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\sqrt[n]{f(n,q)}\leq\sqrt[q-1]{f(q-1,q)}=\sqrt[q-1]{q}
.
Using the Taylor series of the exponential function, we see that
\sqrt[q-1]{q}=e^{\frac{\ln q}{q-1}}=1+\frac{\ln q}{q-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{\ln q}{q-1}\right)^{k}=1+\frac{\ln q}{q-1}+o(1/q)
.
∎

3 Small parameters

Lemma 4
.

If
q\geq 3
, then
f(q,q)\leq 2q-1
.

Proof.

Let
e_{i}
denote the standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{q}
.
Consider the set

S=\left\{a\mathbf{1}\,\,||\,\,a=1,\dots,q-1\right\}\cup\left\{e_{i}\,\,||\,\,i=1,\dots,q\right\}.

We prove that
S
is a skirting set.
Take an
n
-tuple
y
.
Suppose that
y
is not skirted by any
n
-tuple
a\mathbf{1}
of
S
.
Then all symbols from
1
to
q-1
occur in
y
.
Hence, symbol 0 occurs at most once.
If it occurs in position
i
, then
y
is skirted by
e_{i}
.
If symbol 0 does not occur, then choose a position
i
with
y(i)=2
.
Then
y
is skirted by
e_{i}
.
∎

Proposition 5

If

f(n,3)\leq 2^{n/2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\lceil n/4\rceil}\binom{n/2}{2i-1}2^{(n/2)-2i+1}.

\bar{2}

\bar{1}
.
The resulting alteration of

\bar{2}
, and hence is an element of

.
The case where the

4 Skirting arrays

In this section, we extend the concept of a skirting set to a skirting array.
For an
n
-tuple
x
and a subset
T\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
, we denote by
x_{T}
the
|T|
-tuple obtained by only retaining the coordinate positions labelled by elements of
T
.
Say that an
N\times n

array
M
with entries from
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is a
skirting array of strength
t
, an
\mathsf{SA}(N;t,n,q)
, if every
submatrix of
M
obtained by restriction to
t
columns skirts the
q
-ary
t
-tuples; i.e. for every
t
-set

T\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
, every
y\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{t}
is at maximum distance from
x_{T}
for at least one row
x
of
M
.
Let
\mathsf{SAN}(t,n,q)
be the smallest number of rows
N
in such an array. This is analogous to the notation for
covering arrays
[
2
, Section VI.10]
: a
covering array of strength
t
with
N
rows,

n
columns, and entries from an alphabet of size
q
, denoted a
\mathsf{CA}(N;t,n,q)
, is an array
having the property that restriction to any
t
columns results in an
N\times t
array containing every
possible
t
-tuple at least once as a row. (If the number of rows containing a
t
-tuple is a constant

\lambda
, then this covering array of strength
t
is, in fact, an orthogonal array of strength
t
.) The
smallest number of rows
N
in any
\mathsf{CA}(N;t,n,q)
is the
covering array number

\mathsf{CAN}(t,n,q)
.
Note that
\mathsf{SAN}(t,n,2)=\mathsf{CAN}(t,n,2)
.

Lemma 7
.

\mathsf{SAN}(t,n,q)\leq\mathsf{CAN}(t,n,q)+1-(q-1)^{t}~.

Proof.

Let
M
be obtained from a
\mathsf{CA}(N;t,n,q)
by deleting the first
(q-1)^{t}-1
rows. If
y
is any
q
-ary

n
-tuple and
T\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
is any set of
t
coordinates, then among the
(q-1)^{t}

t
-tuples at maximum distance from
y_{T}
, at least one of them remains as the restriction
x_{T}

of some row of
M
.
∎

.

For any
n

and some row of the

y
.
∎

5 Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced the concept of skirting sets.
Several problems remain open for future investigation.

1.

Determine the exact values of the constants
C_{q}
, or at least improve the bounds presented in Proposition
3
.

2.

Find constructions of small skirting arrays, and give bounds on
\mathsf{SAN}(t,n,q)
.

3.

In Lemma
6
, we showed that
f(n,q)>f(n-1,q)
. In the proof, we used that if we take a skirting set in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
, and remove any coordinate, we end up with a skirting set in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n-1}
with the property that none of its elements are essential, in the sense that after deleting any element, we are still left with a skirting set.
One would expect that this argument can lead to better lower bounds of
f(n,q)-f(n-1,q)
.
