Comparative Study of Outflow Facility and Intraocular Pressure in Kahook Dual Blade and iStent Inject – A Consecutive Case Series
Authors: Thomas Sherman, Anindyt Nagar, Arij Daas, Lina Danieliute, Pouya Alaghband, Saurabh Goyal, Kin Sheng Lim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the effects of Kahook Dual Blade (KDB) ab-interno trabeculectomy and iStent inject on tonographic outflow facility in glaucoma subjects.DesignComparative non-randomised observational study.ParticipantsAdult pseudophakic patients with open angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension with sub-optimal intraocular pressure (IOP) control despite maximum tolerated medical treatment.MethodsAll patients underwent washout of all glaucoma medications before measurement of tonographic outflow facility (TOF) and IOP. Washout TOF and IOP were repeated 3 months after the surgery with either KDB or iStent inject.Main Outcome MeasuresTonographic outflow facility, intraocular pressure, complication rates and number of glaucoma medications.ResultsA total of 40 patients participated in the study, 20 eyes of 20 patients were included in each group. 20 untreated eyes acted as control eyes. All patients in the iStent group underwent drop washout. Six patients in the KDB group were unable to safely undergo washout. At the 3-months post-operative visit, the mean post washout IOP was reduced by 20% (25.0±7.1 mmHg at baseline vs 19.6±3.5 mmHg at 3 months, p=0.02) whilst TOF was doubled (0.050±0.01 µL/min/mmHg at baseline vs 0.098±0.06-µL/min/mmHg at 3 months, p=0.04) from baseline in the KDB group. The iStent group showed a nonsignificant increase in mean TOF from 0.110±0.08 µL/min/mmHg at baseline to 0.145±0.09 µL/min/mmHg at 3 months (p= 0.18) and a nonsignificant decrease in median IOP 21mmHg vs 18.7mmHg (p = 1.0), Control eyes showed no significant change in the mean IOP (20.8±5.5 mmHg at baseline vs 20.5±6.1 mmHg at 3 months, p = 0.86) and TOF (baseline 0.120±0.08-µL/min/mmHg vs 3 months 0.124±0.08 -µL/min/ mmHg, p = 0.88).ConclusionWe demonstrated that there is a statistically significant reduction in IOP and doubling of TOF following KDB. However, patients did not show significant changes in IOP or outflow facility after standalone iStent inject implantation.Keywords: glaucoma, aqueous dynamics, MIGS

Introduction

Glaucoma is a major cause of blindness worldwide.1,2 Traditional glaucoma surgery includes filtration surgery such as trabeculectomy or cyclodestructive procedures. Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) circumvents some of the risks associated with these procedures, with a good safety profile and opportunity for an ab interno approach.3 One way MIGS devices can lower intraocular pressure is by enhancing drainage of aqueous through Schlemm’s canal using stents e.g iStent (Glaukos, CA, USA and Hydrus; Ivantis, CA, USA) or removal of trabecular meshwork (TM) e.g Kahook Dual Blade (KDB New World Medical, Inc, Rancho Cucamonga, CA). KDB was developed as a single-use dual blade devised to excise a strip of TM to improve trabecular outflow.4 The TM, particularly the juxta-canalicular section adjacent to the Schlemm’s canal, is considered the main site of resistance to aqueous outflow.5,6 The KDB has a tapered edge at the tip to allow smooth entry of the blade into Schlemm’s canal. The dual blades incise the tissue to remove TM. KDB can be performed as a standalone procedure or in combination with cataract extraction, and has been approved by the FDA since 2015.

iStent Inject ® represents the second iteration of the original iStent device which was CE marked in 2011. The injector contained two iStent G2 stents (iStent-injected Trabecular Micro-Bypass System Model G2-M-IS; Glaukos, San Clemente, California, USA) made of heparin-coated titanium. They measure 360 microns in length and 230 microns in diameter and have a central lumen 80 microns in diameter.

The effect of KDB ab interno trabeculectomy compared to iStent implantation on tonographic outflow facility (TOF) has never been investigated. Consequently, we designed this comparative observational study to assess the effects of these two procedures on TOF.

Outflow facility is the predominant factor that is compromised in glaucoma, as aqueous production and episcleral venous pressure tend to be constant across people with primary open angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension.7 The relationship of outflow facility to IOP is however not a linear one.8 Accordingly, measurement of outflow facility as a standalone variable permits a more comprehensive understanding of surgical intervention of aqueous dynamics.

Materials and Methods

Ethical approval was obtained from the National Health Service (NHS) Research Ethics Committee of United Kingdom. This study conforms to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. The National Institute of Health Research (NIHR) portfolio registration number is 35357 and the clinicaltrials.gov identifier is NCT02839590. Consecutive patients who were pseudophakic in the study eye, with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension (OHT), suboptimal IOP control, or medication intolerance were invited to participate. Open-angle glaucoma, including pigmentary glaucoma, was diagnosed based on abnormal visual field testing and/or corresponding disc changes diagnosed by a glaucoma specialist, while OHT had an IOP >21 without visual fields or optic disc changes. Consecutive patients were first recruited into the KDB series and then into the iStent series. All participants in the study provided informed consent prior to undertaking any study investigations.

All patients underwent baseline slit-lamp examination as well as visual acuity, gonioscopy, anterior chamber depth, and axial length measurement (IOL Master; Carl Zeiss Meditec Inc., Dublin, CA, USA). Pachymetry (CCT; Pachmate DGH 55; DGH Technology, Inc., Exton, PA, USA) and visual fields (Humphrey automated white-on-white, 24–2 SITA-standard; Carl Zeiss Meditec) were also performed.

Patients ceased using glaucoma medications for 4-weeks prior to the baseline study measurements. At 3-months postop this drop washout was repeated prior to the study measurements (with a safety 2-weeks post washout visit).

Tonographic outflow facility measurements were obtained using a digital Schiotz tonographer. A weight of between 5.5–1g was attached to a modified digital Schiøtz tonographer (designed by the Department of Bioengineering, Imperial College London, UK) adapted from an existing Schiotz tonography device (Model 720, Berkeley Bioengineering Inc., San Leandro, CA, USA). A 3D printed shell house the probe, the weight of which adheres to the standards set by the Committee on Standardization of Tonometers.9 The tonography probe contained a weighted plunger that was displaced over a 4 minute measurement time. This movement was measured using a linear variable differential transformer (LVDT MHR; TE Connectivity, Schaffhausen, CH, USA). The signal was then conditioned (AD698; Analogue Devices, Norwood, MA, USA) before being captured using a digital acquisition system (USB-6009; National Instruments, Austin, TX, USA). A previous study has validated that the LVDT voltage output has a linear relationship with the Schiøtz scale reading, 0.05 mm of plunger displacement equates to a change of 1 Schiotz scale reading change.10

All KDB procedures were performed under local anaesthesia by a glaucoma fellowship-trained glaucoma surgeon (SG). A temporal clear corneal incision was made and viscoelastic (Healon® or Healon GV®, Sodium Hyaluronate, Johnson & Johnson, New Jersey, USA) was injected into the anterior chamber. The patient’s head was rotated 30–45 degrees with the microscope tilted 45 °away from the surgeon to allow good visualisation of the nasal angle using a gonioprism (Volk Vold Surgical Gonio Lens, Volk, OH, USA). The KDB was inserted in the anterior chamber and advanced towards Schlemm’s canal when the initial incision of the TM was made. The heel of the device was advanced in a clockwise or counterclockwise manner for approximately 3–5 clock hours (between 90–150 degree). Viscoelasticity was aspirated from the anterior chamber and the corneal wounds were hydrated. Post-operatively, the patients were treated topically with Dropodex (preservative-free dexamethasone phosphate 0.1%; Rayner Global, Worthing, UK) for 2 h for 2 weeks and then 4 times daily for 2 weeks. Antibiotic drops were administered for two weeks, and hypotensive medications were stopped immediately postoperatively.

For the iStent (inserted by subspecialty fellowship-trained surgeons), the same approach was utilised to visualise the angle as for the KDB. Two stents (iStent-injected G2) were implanted in the TM aiming for 2–3 clock hours of separation at the nasal angle. Patients were administered topical dexamethasone 0.1% four times a day for 2 weeks and then twice a day for 2 weeks. Topical chloramphenicol 0.5% was administered four times daily for two weeks. Topical glaucoma medications were discontinued postoperatively in the operated eyes.

Post-Operative Visits

Patients were reviewed on day 1, week 1, month 1, month 2, and then 3 months post operatively and in between, as needed. The 4-week washout was repeated (if clinically safe to do so) at 2-months post operatively, and the measurements were repeated at 3-months.

Primary Outcome Measures

Tonographic outflow facility (TOF) measured using digital Schiøtz tonography and Goldman intraocular pressure (IOP). Measurements were taken at baseline (after four weeks of ocular hypotensive washout) and three months post-surgery (after washout). The quality of tonographic tracing was manually and independently reviewed by two authors (TS and KSL) to assess whether they were of sufficient quality.

Secondary Outcome Measures

These were the numbers of ocular hypotensive medications and adverse events.

Statistical Analyses

Normality of distribution was tested using histograms and the Shapiro–Wilk test. A Shapiro–Wilk test (W > 0.05) indicated a normal distribution. The Student’s paired t-test was used to compare continuous variables among the groups. When there was a non-normal distribution, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used. Fisher’s exact test was used to analyse categorical variables. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05, and all analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0; SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA).

Results

Twenty patients were enrolled in the KDB series; one patient was lost to follow-up at three months; therefore, 19 eyes of 19 patients were included in the final analysis. Analysis of the tonographic outflow facility was possible for 11 patients. This was due to a poor-quality trace for two patients and safety concerns for washout in six patients (all POAG). The IOPs of these 6 patients were deemed too high on treatment at 2 months, and the mean IOP was 27 mmHg on 2.0 medications, so risked further increase if they were washed out. The KDB successfully excised 100–120° of the trabecular meshwork in all cases.

There were 20 patients in the iStent series, with one eye used as the study eye and the fellow eye as a control. All patients were able to undergo washout both at baseline and 3 months postoperatively. Seventeen patients had 2 stents implanted at the time of surgery. Three patients had one stent implanted due to unsuccessful attempts to insert superficially placed stents. Analysis of preoperative and postoperative outflow changes was only performed for cases in which a suitable quality trace existed for both study visits. This resulted in five study eyes and six control eyes being excluded from outflow analysis. Discussion

This is the first study investigating the effect of KDB ab-interno trabeculectomy on tonographic outflow facility compared to iStent trabecular microbypass stenting. We observed a statistically significant reduction in IOP and increase in TOF following KDB only. IOP was reduced by 20% and TOF doubled; 43% of the eyes achieved a ≥20% reduction in IOP.

Our results concur with a limited number of prospective studies that have evaluated the clinical outcomes of KDB ab-interno trabeculectomy. However, the majority of these studies combined KDB with cataract surgery, making direct comparisons challenging. In a prospective multicentre case series, Salinas et al evaluated 52 eyes of 42 patients who underwent standalone KDB goniotomy.11 At 6 months, the mean IOP reduction was 24% (p<0.001) from baseline, with a reduction in the mean number of medications from baseline (2.6 ± 1.1) of 1.2 (p<0.001). Two other studies that evaluated combined phacoemulsification and KDB goniotomy reported similar results.12,13 However, the mean IOP reduction (26.4% and 26.2%, respectively) was slightly more profound than that observed in our study. Consequently, a 50% reduction in the number of medications administered at 6 and 12 months has been reported.

Sieck et al14 evaluated 197 eyes in 143 patients and compared the outcomes of KDB goniotomy with and without cataract extraction. At 12 months, the mean IOP in the KDB-standalone group reduced by 31%, with a reduction in the mean number of medications of 0.8. This was comparable to that in the combined phaco-KDB group (18% mean IOP reduction with a reduction in the mean number of medications by 0.4 (p<0.0001)). However, baseline IOP was significantly higher in the KDB-alone group (20.4 vs 16.7). A similar retrospective study evaluated the efficacy and safety of KDB goniotomy, with and without cataract surgery, at 18 months.15 In that study, 93 eyes underwent a combined phaco-KDB procedure and 23 eyes underwent standalone KDB. The IOP reduction in the standalone KDB group at 18 months was 31.4% (n=6), with no change in the mean number of medications. The combined phaco-KDB appeared to be more successful in this cohort at all the time points.

A retrospective analysis of 42 eyes that underwent standalone KDB goniotomy by Berdahl et al showed that the mean IOP reduction at six months was 25.6% (p<0.0001) and 14.9% at 12 months (p=0.001).16,17 However, the reduction in the mean number of medications was only significant at 6 months (−0.8, p=0.0011).

We chose only pseudophakic eyes for this study because the deeper anterior chamber made the KDB surgery easier and safer. We have previously shown that cataract surgery alone can enhance tonographic outflow, thus avoiding this with KDB.18

The patients who underwent iStent showed improvements in both outflow and IOP, but these failed to reach statistical significance and were not as great as those in the KDB group. Previous retrospective comparative studies of KDB and iStent injections did not examine the changes in outflow. Arnljots et al19 found that standalone KDB use was associated with a mean decrease of 28.4% (p=0.004), and a non-significant mean increase in IOP of 1.5% (p=0.598) was observed in the iStent group at 24 months. Their outcomes at 3 months did not reach statistical significance in either group for average IOP reduction. However, comparisons are limited because the mixture of these patients is phakic and pseudophakic.

Evidence from haemoglobin video imaging and fluorescein angiography has shown that improvements in outflow can be visualised following iStent inject insertion.20,21 In our study, many patients had a single patent iStent due to iris plugging or overimplantation. However, experiments have demonstrated that this can result in significant improvements in the outflow. Bahler et al studied seven cultured human anterior segments, where the outflow facility was measured via a pressure transducer, while the continuous flow of perfusate was applied.22 They found that the outflow more than doubled from the baseline using a single iStent. This was then bled when an additional iStent was added. Additionally, Toris et al23 demonstrated significant improvements (31.5%) in outflow with a single iStent injection compared to sham surgery. However, implementing the two iStent injects failed to reach a statistically significant increase in the outflow facility (10.4%±21.8%, p = 0.536). The iStent injection seemed to show less predictability in its effect on the outflow. The authors remarked that verifying correct stent placement can be difficult owing to the small size of the implant, and that suboptimal positioning may be responsible for the variability in outflow changes observed. In such outflow facility models, the lack of episcleral venous pressure can lead to overestimation of the treatment effect. Therefore, the values derived in this study cannot be directly compared with those obtained in vivo using Schiøtz tonography.

Histological studies of excised tissues following KDB demonstrated high TM.24 Hyphema is a serious adverse event that is associated with TM removal. A recent report by Salimi et al,25 reported a 71% incidence of hyphema after −360- °Gonioscopy-Assisted Transluminal Trabeculotomy (GATT). We observed only a 10% incidence of transient hyphema persisting for than one week. As we only removed up to 150-degree of the TM during surgery, the risk of hyphema appears to be related to the amount of TM excised. Although hyphae can be encountered after iStent insertion, they were not observed in our study.

A pre-clinical study demonstrated that the KDB blade achieved more complete removal of the TM than traditional goniotomy without injury to surrounding tissues.4 This may indicate reduced collateral damage, subsequent fibrosis, and scarring as observed in other types of angle surgeries.4,24

The main limitations of our study were its small sample size and short-term follow-up. Further long-term studies with larger sample sizes are warranted. Another limitation is that some iStents showed evidence of nonpatency on gonioscopic examination or anterior segment OCT. Iris plugging, hyaloid membrane, or evidence of over-implantation were the cause in 11 patients. However, our data reflect real-world outcomes of iStent placement. Further studies are required to determine the relative differences in outflow under optimal implantation conditions of stents compared to the removal of trabecular tissue. The issue of stent over-implantation has been addressed with the new-generation iStent W. Its wider flange prevents over-implantation, so outflow studies with this stent are warranted. Additionally, the baseline outflow facility was higher in the iStent group, but mean 3 month outflow levels were similar in both groups. If there is a physiological ceiling that is achieved with trabecular bypass, then the margin of improvement seen with iStent would be lower. However, such a ceiling has not been defined with tonographic outflow facility measurements.

In summary, standalone KDB ab-interno trabeculectomy in pseudophakic eyes resulted in a significant reduction in IOP with a corresponding increase in TOF; such a change was not observed in cases of standalone iStent injections.
