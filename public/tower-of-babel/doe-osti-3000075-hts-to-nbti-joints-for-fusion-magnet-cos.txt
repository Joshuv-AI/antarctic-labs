 HTS to NbTi Joints for Fusion Magnet Cost Savings
 P. Titus, J. Fang, J. Minervini, , and L Bromberg

 Abstract—This paper describes methods of employing high field High Temperature Superconductors
(HTS), only where high field capability and high current density is needed in superconducting coils. The
approach lends itself, in particular, to Toroidal Field (TF) coils used in fusion reactors. The initial idea was
to develop a conductor joint design or designs that would allow joints between expensive high field High
Temperature Superconductors (HTS) Rare Earth Barium Copper Oxide (REBCO) conductors to low cost
NbTi lower field conductors.

 I. INTRODUCTION

T HIS paper describes methods of employing high field, high temperature superconductors (HTS), only where high field
 capability and high current density is needed. In the mid-year progress presentation of Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory
 (PPPL)’s 2023 start-up and quench simulation studies [1], an idea was presented that could allow significant cost savings
in Toroidal Field Coils that use high temperature super-conducting tapes. The Fusion Nuclear Science Facility (FNSF)[13,14,15]
design as shown in Fig. 1, is used as a basis for this study. The study was based on the observation that in a toroidal field coil, the
field at the conductor decreases as 1/r as measured from the machine centerline.
 Also the field decreases through the build of the TF winding pack. Grading of conductors through the TF build is commonly
used. The idea presented here is the use of these joints to allow connection of high cost to low cost conductors where a decrease in
toroidal field (TF) would allow, as shown in Fig. 2. Grading options are available for accommodating field decreases from the bore
of the magnet to the outside of the magnet. This is discussed in the context of a proposed KDEMO configuration. Regionalizing
the type of conductor used such that high field conductors are only used at the plasma side of the winding pack, allows the use of
less expensive conductors on the low field side. The Toroidal field is proportional to 1/r and this field decrease also provides an
opportunity for grading. For example, a Tokamak with 7T at the center of the plasma (Ro) and 20 T in the inner corner only needs
the higher field capability at the inner radii of the machine. This suggests transitions with joints from high field to low field
conductors. For the FNSF reactor, the volume of conductor needed for fields higher than 7T, (the limit for relatively inexpensive
NbTi,) is only about 13% of the total TF winding pack. For large reactor concepts, the cost of HTS has been generally considered
cost prohibitive. Until manufacturing costs drop drastically, the joint concepts discussed in this paper, allow the affordable use of
HTS, and access to the high fields it allows.
 Another option with stacked tapes is to gradually replace superconducting tapes with Hastelloy tapes as the field drops, but this
is also a jointed approach relying on conduction through the steel tapes. In this paper, a clamped shunt type joint is the preferred
solution. A local soldered NbTi to REBCO stacked tape joint is also considered. These joint concepts are not demountable joints.
Treatments of demountable joints may be found in [4] and [9]. The joints discussed in this paper are intended as a permanent
inclusion in the TF winding pack. Demountable joints – if they are feasible – may also allow transitions from HTS to LTS but this
is not the intention of this study. Mixed superconductor joints are used in power leads, and this provides an existence proof of
concept [3], but leads are not exposed to the fields and mechanical environment of a TF coil. Ultimately, the proposed jointed

solution may not be desirable. HTS costs and production capabilities will improve, making joints less and less attractive. The point
of this effort is to show that there are feasible cost solutions to using HTS conductors in larger scale pilot plant or power plant
designs.
 While the costing impact of HTS usage is a burden on the feasibility of HTS usage in large reactors, supply is also a problem.
A jointed concept that requires less HTS may allow a more reasonable scale-up of tape manufacture. If tape length continues to be
a quality issue, joint features will allow shorter tape lengths, further improving the practicality of HTS solutions.

 II. JOINT CONCEPTS AND SIMULATIONS

 For this study, two types of joints were investigated. The first concept studied was a soldered lap joints, Shown in Fig 3, in
which the REBCO tapes are soldered directly to the NbTi conductor, a Rutherford type NbTi cable was first envisioned, then a
CICC cable like that used in the ITER PF coils was used as an example. The resistivity of the single lap connecting a single petal
of a NbTi Cable in Conduit Conductor (CICC) to a single stack of a VIPER [5] – like cable, was calculated to be 1.44 Nano Ohm
with a .1 mm thick solder layer.
 This joint was analyzed with transient electromagnetic, and thermal analyses, and static structural analyses. Nuclear heating has

 A second type of joint was suggested that doesn’t directly connect the superconductors, but carries the current in a copper shunt
that includes embedded HTS tapes to facilitate the current transfer from the HTS to NbTi. Both concepts have the conductors in-
line.

 One concern with the design of the shunt type joint, is that there may be a tendency for all the current to transfer at the beginning
and at the end of the shunt. A transient electromagnetic (EM) analysis was performed in ANSYS. A thermal pass was then done
with the Joule heat from the EM model transferred to the thermal model, which also was a transient analysis. This analysis used
legacy elements in ANSYS that have been successful in modeling many EM transients [1], [11], [12]. Fig. 4 shows the components
of the EM model.

 The infinite boundary elements cause these models (soldered lap and shunt) to behave as standalone conductors and joints. For
structural passes, external fields are added. The models include their self field and inductance. Fig. 5 shows some of the results of
this study. Temperatures and thus current transfer did not show concentrations and the entry and exit of the shunt. The joint length
was varied between .17m to .51 meter, and the temperature dropped uniformly with length. The .51 meter long shunt is considered
practical in the region of the TF coil where the joints are to be positioned.
 After some improvements in helium channel size and thickness of the shunt web between the HTS and conductors, the transient
heat-up of the shunt copper was limited to 0.58K. This is shown in Figure 5d. A 2D COMSOL steady state calculation of the shunt
was performed which produced a similar delta temperature. The agreement is somewhat accidental, because the COMSOL model
only had cooling in the center cooling tube and had better modeling of the copper RRR and magneto resistivity than the ANSYS
model. The ANSYS 3D results show uniform heating along the length of the contact area in the shunt, justifying the 2D modeling
approach in the COMSOL analysis shown in figure 6.

 III. CONSIDERATION OF TRANSIENT DI/DT
 The FNSF and other Pilot plant concepts are pulsed devices. The pulse lengths are long and the transients gradual. But the
sensitivity of the joints to dI/dt transients needs to be studied. This was done for the soldered lap joint concept. The strongest mal-
distribution of currents is in the HTS tape stacks. This is not specific to the joint region, but it shows that the ramp time or time
associated with TF coil current transients need to be of order 1000 seconds. The simulation in Fig. 7 does not have the n value
effect included, which should reduce the ratio of peak current to min current density.

 IV. GRADING OPTIONS
  Radial grading of solenoids for high field research, fusion [2] and high energy physics is common Grading in the TF winding
pack build is also commonly proposed for Pilot Plant studies and other future reactors.

 Fig. 10. Proposed K-DEMO two region winding pack [7].

 Fig. 10 shows a cross section from a K-DEMO study that produced a substantial savings over the non graded example of ITER.
ITER does not use grading partly bcause of the pancake wound radial plate design. The K-DEMO concept shown uses layer
winding. In this concept the high field region uses the same conductor type in the full poloidal extent of the winding. Even this
layout could be further graded by transitioning conductor types where local fields would allow. The outer half of the high field
winding pack could be NbTi allowing a large savings for a reactor this size.

 VI. TENSILE LOAD ON THE JOINTS
  In most reactor concepts, the horizontal leg tension would have to be shared between the casing and the winding pack. In this
case, the joints would have to take tensile loading. Figure 12a shows the FNSF TF tensile stress results and the stress analysis of a
joint in the horizontal leg. The direct soldered joint concept would have to support this load by shear across the lap joint. REBCO
tape is not strong in shear. TF coil loading with and without preload rings is discussed in [13]. Figure 12 shows the results of a
stress analysis of the soldered lap joint for the special case of the FNSF.

 9

Fig. 12. Results of twisted version of the direct soldered joint.

  A twisted version of the direct soldered joint was loaded in tensile strain derived from the FNSF horizontal leg tension. This is
not an analysis representative of many reactors because the FNSF – as last envisioned, had a compression ring to maintain wedge
pressure in the inner corner of the TF. This reduced the horizontal leg tension. Still there is some problematic shear lag in the
soldered lap joint. An effort was made to design a lap joint that had an intermediate Hastelloy tape that would carry the shear
around the superconductor layers, but this made an already finicky design more complicated and time consuming to fabricate. The
shunt design is a better solution for taking tension.

 VII. COOLING ISSUES:

  Using low temperature and high temperatures superconductors together will probably require a single system that satisfies the
LTS requirement. Calculations were performed to estimate the current sharing temperature of the NbTi TF conductor. This should
be the maximum temperature for the joint to operate at in the steady state, taking into account resistive joint heating. To do the
calculation, a fraction of the operational critical current was assumed. Since the details of conductor haven’t been chosen, Three
Iop/Ic fractions were investigated. 0.5, 0.6, and 0.7, which should cover a reasonable range. Also did the field at the joint was
varied. The NbTi temperature needs to be maintained below at least 6K, or even less depending on fraction of Ic. 20K is needed
for the HTS. A system that supplies, say 4.5 K to the LTS, remain below 6 K in the LTS region, and is allowed to rise in temperature
in the HTS region could be a solution. Mechanical issues with the temperature gradient should not be an issue because of the small
change in thermal expansion coefficient between 4 and 20 K.

 VIII. CONCLUSION
  Joints embedded in the winding pack are feasible and provide cost advantages for present day cost estimates. Ultimately, the
proposed jointed solution may not be desirable. HTS costs and production capabilities will improve, making joints less and less
attractive. The point of this effort is to show that there are feasible cost solutions to using HTS conductors in larger scale pilot
plant designs.

  A jointed concept that requires less HTS may allow a more reasonable scale-up of tape manufacture. If tape length continues to
be a quality issue, joint features will allow shorter tape lengths, further improving the practicality of HTS solutions.
  The shunt type joint appears to be more attractive because it is more readily manufactured and assembled than the direct solder
lap joint concept. This is the preferred design, but it requires a longer length than direct solder type joints, and a larger build.
Further optimizations of the spatial requirements are needed.

  More effort is needed on the EM behavior of the joints to integrate these concepts into the evolving reactor.

  A stellarator coil was investigated. The size of the winding pack cross section has a large effect on the local fields of the modular
coils. Expanding the winding cross section and the use of joints allow use of lower field LTS conductors.
