Kaon Distribution Amplitudes from Euclidean Functional QCD
Wen Cui, Dao-yu Zhang, Chuang Huang, Wei-jie Fu

Abstract
We study the kaon quasi-distribution amplitude (quasi-DA) and distribution amplitude (DA) within the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET) combined with the first-principles functional QCD. Using quark correlation functions and the kaon Bethe-Salpeter amplitude in the Euclidean space from the 2+1 flavour functional QCD Fu et al. 2025 as inputs, we obtain the kaon quasi DA in the large longitudinal momentum region with the contour deformation method Zhang et al. 2025 in the complex plane of momentum. By performing 1 / P z 2 1/P_{z}^{2} and 1 / P z 4 1/P_{z}^{4} order extrapolations of the kaon quasi-DA for the choices of the maximal longitudinal momentum P z max ∈ [ 2 , 2.5 ] P_{z}^{\max}\in[2,2.5] GeV, we obtain a single-peaked and asymmetric kaon DA with the uncertainties arising from the extrapolation interval and ansatz. We find the first and second order moments of the kaon DA, ⟨ ξ ⟩ K = 0.020 ​ ( 3 ) \langle\xi\rangle_{K}=0.020(3) and ⟨ ξ 2 ⟩ K = 0.253 ​ ( 12 ) \langle\xi^{2}\rangle_{K}=0.253(12) , respectively.



I
Introduction

The kaons and pions as Nambu–Goldstone bosons associated with the dynamical chiral symmetry breaking in strong interactions are closely related to the mechanism of mass generation in the Standard Model
Roberts et al. 2021
;
Ding et al. 2023
;
Raya et al. 2024
. Furthermore, the kaon consists of a strange quark and a light
u
,
d
quark, which makes the internal structure of the kaon a valuable probe of
SU(3)
flavour symmetry breaking (FSB) in quantum chromodynamics (QCD). Recently, the BESIII experiment has made significant progress in the study of FSB by measuring the electromagnetic form factors of kaons in the large-momentum-transfer regime
Seth et al. 2014
;
Ablikim et al. 2024
. Experimental results show that the ratio of the electromagnetic form factors of charged kaons to pions exhibits a significant deviation from the leading-order prediction of perturbative QCD
Efremov and Radyushkin 1980
;
Farrar and Jackson 1979
;
Lepage and Brodsky 1979
;
Lepage and Brodsky 1980
. In view of the current state of research, the kaon distribution amplitude (DA) plays a key role in the calculation of observables. The nonperturbative kaon DA input has a significant impact on the electromagnetic form factors in the large-momentum-transfer region, see
Gao et al. 2017
;
Hua et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2025
;
Chai and Cheng 2025
;
Ding et al. 2024
;
Chang et al. 2025
. In recent years, various nonperturbative QCD approaches have achieved significant progress in the calculation of the kaon DA, including the Dyson-Schwinger equation/Bethe-Salpeter equation (DSE/BSE) approach
Shi et al. 2014
;
Cui et al. 2020
;
Chang et al. 2025
, holographic models
Swarnkar and Chakrabarti 2015
;
Chang et al. 2017
, and lattice QCD
Hua et al. 2022
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Bali et al. 2019
. These approaches generally yield a kaon DA with a slightly narrower, single-peaked structure compared to the pion DA, but there is still no consensus on the extent of the asymmetry in the valence-quark momentum fraction
x
. In this work, we compute the nonperturbative kaon quasi-DA and DA within the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET), where the QCD correlation functions are obtained from first-principles functional QCD calculations.

In the past years, first-principles QCD calculations based on the functional renormalization group (fRG) approach have undergone systematic development, see
Mitter et al. 2015
;
Braun et al. 2016
;
Rennecke 2015
;
Cyrol et al. 2016
;
Cyrol et al. 2018
;
Corell et al. 2018
;
Fu et al. 2020
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
;
Fu et al. 2026
and reviews
Dupuis et al. 2021
;
Fu 2022
;
Rennecke 2025
;
Fischer and Pawlowski 2026
. Specifically, the QCD correlation function inputs used in this work are based on a series of papers
Fu et al. 2023
;
Fu et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025
, where a self-consistent fRG approach to the first-principles 2+1 flavour QCD with controlled systematic uncertainties has been developed, with the only input parameters being the strong coupling and the running quark masses defined at the ultraviolet scale. In this work, we use the Euclidean light- and strange-quark correlation functions and the kaon Bethe–Salpeter amplitude in
Fu et al. 2023
;
Fu et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025
as the inputs, and combine the LaMET approach to compute the kaon parton distribution amplitude (PDA). In the LaMET approach, the light-cone PDA can be obtained via an extrapolation from the quasi-PDA calculated at a large longitudinal momentum
P_{z}
in the Euclidean regime, see
Ji 2013
;
Ji et al. 2021
;
Ji et al. 2017
for more details. By combining the complex-plane integration method developed in
Zhang et al. 2025
, one is able to perform a direct calculation for the kaon DA based on the first-principles functional QCD in this work. We find for the first two order moments of kaon DA,
\langle\xi\rangle_{K}=0.020(3)
and
\langle\xi^{2}\rangle_{K}=0.253(12)
.

This paper is organized as follows: In
Section

II
, we present the truncation setup used in the functional QCD calculations and discuss the quark and kaon-meson correlation functions employed as inputs for the kaon quasi-DA. The introduction of the deformed contour for the momentum integral in the complex plane and its effects will be discussed in
Section

III
, where we also determine a suitable range of large longitudinal momentum for the kaon quasi-DA. In
Section

IV
, we present and discuss the final results for the extrapolated light-cone kaon DA and its moments, and compare them with other nonperturbative approaches, showing that our results fall within a reasonable range. We conclude this paper in
Section

V
.

II
Correlation functions from functional QCD

In
Fu et al. 2025
, the first-principles 2+1 flavour functional QCD framework has been established. By fixing the physical ratios
m_{\pi}/f_{\pi}
and
m_{K}/f_{\pi}
, this framework requires only the strong coupling and the running quark masses of the light and strange quarks in the ultraviolet as inputs, without introducing any phenomenological parameters or model assumptions. Within the fRG approach, both gluonic and matter degrees of freedom and their dynamics are described self-consistently. In the gluonic sector, the generation of a gluon mass gap leads to its decoupling from the matter sector at low energies, see also
Braun et al. 2010
;
Fischer et al. 2009
;
Fister and Pawlowski 2013
;
Mitter et al. 2015
;
Cyrol et al. 2016
;
Cyrol et al. 2018
. In this section, we focus on the setup and solutions of the quark and meson sectors. The effective action for the quark sector is given by

\displaystyle\quad\Gamma_{\mathrm{quark}}[\bar{q},q,A]

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{p}\bar{q}(-p)\left[\Gamma_{\bar{q}q}^{(2)}\right](p)\,q(p)+\int\limits_{p_{1},p_{2}}\left[\Gamma_{A\bar{q}q}^{(3)}\right]^{a}_{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})

\displaystyle\quad\times\bar{q}(p_{2})A^{a}_{\mu}(p_{1})q(p_{3})+\sum_{\alpha}\int\limits_{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}}\left[\Gamma_{4q}^{(4)}\right]^{(\alpha)}_{ijkm}

\displaystyle\quad\times\bar{q}_{i}(p_{1})q_{j}(p_{2})\bar{q}_{k}(p_{3})q_{m}(p_{4})\,,

(1)

with

\displaystyle\int_{p}\equiv\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,.

(2)

The label
\alpha
denotes the different tensor structures for the four-quark vertices. For the two-point function, it reads

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\bar{q}q}^{(2)}(p)=Z_{q}(p)\,\Big[\mathrm{i}\not{p}+M_{q}(p)\Big]\,,

(3)

with

\displaystyle Z_{q}(p)

\displaystyle=\text{diag}(Z_{l}(p),Z_{l}(p),Z_{s}(p))\,,

(4)

\displaystyle M_{q}(p)

\displaystyle=\text{diag}(M_{l}(p),M_{l}(p),M_{s}(p))\,,

(5)

where
M_{l}(p)
and
M_{s}(p)
are the mass functions, and
Z_{l}(p)
and
Z_{s}(p)
are the wave functions for the light and strange quarks, respectively.

The strength of the quark–gluon interaction plays a crucial role in the quantitative description of chiral symmetry breaking. Therefore, in our calculations, we include not only the classical tensor structure
{\cal T}_{A\bar{l}l}^{(1)}
of the quark–gluon vertex, but also the two most important nonclassical channels relevant for the symmetry breaking
{\cal T}_{A\bar{l}l}^{(4)}
and
{\cal T}_{A\bar{l}l}^{(7)}
, enabling a quantitative agreement of physical observables with experiments, where the explicit expressions of tensors above can be found in
Fu et al. 2025
. This is also supported by other nonperturbative QCD studies, see
Fu et al. 2025
;
Mitter et al. 2015
;
Williams 2015
;
Williams et al. 2016
;
Aguilar et al. 2017
;
Cyrol et al. 2018
;
Gao et al. 2021
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
. Moreover, in our calculation, all the quark–gluon vertex dressings are momentum dependent, see
Fu et al. 2025
for more details.

Figure 1:
Schematic illustration of the decomposition of the four-quark vertex into the two quark-meson vertices connected by a meson propagator, when the

-channel momentum is near the pole mass of kaon. Note that the direction of external quark lines denotes that of fermion flows, while the momenta are all counted as incoming towards the vertices.

Within the RG calculations, mesonic degrees of freedom emerge dynamically from four-quark interactions as the RG evolves from the ultraviolet to infrared
Fu et al. 2023
. Therefore, for the four-quark interactions we include the four channels
\{\sigma,\,\pi,\,K,\,\kappa\}
, being most relevant for the chiral symmetry breaking. The reliability of this truncation has been validated in
Fu et al. 2023
;
Fu et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025
. In particular, the four-quark vertex of the kaon channel is given by

\displaystyle\left[\Gamma_{4q}^{(4)}\right]_{ijkm}^{(K)}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{4}p_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{4}Z_{q}^{\frac{1}{2}}(p_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\lambda_{K}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}){\cal T}^{(K)}_{4q,ijkm}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})\,,

(6)

where
\lambda_{K}
is the dressing of the vertex, and the tensor structure reads,

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{4q,ijkm}^{(K)}\bar{q}_{i}q_{j}\bar{q}_{k}q_{m}=-\left(\bar{q}\,\gamma_{5}T^{(4-7)}q\right)^{2}\,,

(7)

with the Gell-Mann matrices
T^{i}
, the quark field
q=(q_{l},q_{s})
and the light quark field
q_{l}=(q_{u},q_{d})
. Furthermore, to maximize the extraction of information on the kaon Bethe–Salpeter (BS) amplitude from the four-quark interaction, we employ an
s,t,u
-channel momentum approximation for the dressing, which reads

\displaystyle\lambda_{K}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})

\displaystyle\approx\lambda_{K}(s,t,u)\,.

(8)

Note that the reliability of the
s,t,u
-channel momentum approximation for the four-quark dressings has been investigated and demonstrated in detail in
Fu et al. 2024
. Specifically, the Mandelstam variables are chosen as

\displaystyle s=0,\quad t=P^{2},\quad u=4p^{2}\,,

(9)

with

\displaystyle P=-(p_{1}+p_{2}),\quad p=\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{2}=\frac{p_{3}-p_{4}}{2}\,,

(10)

where the labels of momenta are shown in Fig.
1
. We choose

\displaystyle P=\sqrt{P^{2}}\,(1,0,0,0),\quad p=\sqrt{p^{2}}\,(1,0,0,0)\,,

(11)

where the angular dependence is neglected. Such a symmetric momentum choice facilitates the extraction of the BS amplitude, see Fig.
1
. The BS amplitude is given by the residue of the dressings at the bound-state pole of kaon, which reads

\displaystyle h_{K}(p)=\lim_{P^{2}\to-m_{K}^{2}}\sqrt{\lambda_{K}(P,p)\cdot(P^{2}+m_{K}^{2})}\,.

(12)

Quark mass function
M_{q}(p)
(solid lines, in unit of GeV) and the quark wave function
Z_{q}(p)
(dashed lines) for the light (red) and strange (blue) quark, in comparison to the lattice data of light quark mass from
Chang et al. 2021
(cyan points) and

(purple points).

In Fig.
2
, we show the mass and wave functions for the light and strange quarks as functions of the momentum, where the light quark mass function is in agreement with the lattice QCD results
Chang et al. 2021
;
Bowman et al. 2005
. In Fig.
3
, the kaon BS amplitude as a function of momentum is shown. It is worth noting that the correlation functions obtained from functional QCD are formulated in Euclidean space. However, the computation of the quasi-DA requires its analytic information in the complex plane. In this work, as in
Zhang et al. 2025
, we use the Taylor expansion in the complex plane of
p_{0}
to account for their analytic behaviors in the complex plane. Taking the BS amplitude
h_{K}(p)
as an example, one expands it as follows

\displaystyle h_{K}(p)

\displaystyle=h_{K}(\bar{p})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial p_{0}^{k}}h_{K}(\bar{p})\left(\mathrm{i}\,\Delta p_{0}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\mathrm{i}\,\Delta p_{0}\right)^{n+1}\right)\,,

(13)

with the complex temporal momentum

\displaystyle p=(p_{0}+\mathrm{i}\Delta p_{0},\,p_{3},\,p_{1},\,p_{2})\,.

(14)

Here,
\bar{p}
denotes the magnitude of momentum where it is expanded, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bar{p}=\sqrt{p_{0}^{2}+p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+p_{3}^{2}}\,.

(15)

Similarly, we apply the same procedure to the quark mass functions
M_{l}(p)
and
M_{s}(p)
, and the wave functions
Z_{l}(p)
and
Z_{s}(p)
as well. The convergence with respect to the expansion order, as well as its impact on the final DA results, has been discussed in detail in
Zhang et al. 2025
;
Chang et al. 2026
. It was found that the order
n=4
is sufficient to guarantee the convergence, which is adopted in this work.

III
Deformed integration contour for kaon quasi-DA

In this section, we discuss the detailed procedure for computing the kaon quasi-DA within the LaMET approach using QCD correlation function inputs shown in
Section

II
. The deformed integration contour technique developed in
Zhang et al. 2025
is employed and improved in this work.

We begin with the unamputated Bethe-Salpeter amplitude for the kaon, which is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{K}(p;P)=G_{l}(p_{+})\,\Gamma_{K}(p;P)\,G_{s}(p_{-})\,,

(16)

with

\displaystyle p_{\pm}=p\pm P/2\,.

(17)

Here,
\Gamma_{K}
denotes the kaon Bethe–Salpeter amplitude including its tensor structure,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{K}(p;P)=\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}h_{K}(p;P)\,.

(18)

G_{l}
and
G_{s}
denote the light- and strange-quark propagators, respectively, which are given by

\displaystyle G_{l}(p_{+})

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathrm{i}\gamma\cdot p_{+}+M_{l}(p_{+})}{Z_{l}(p_{+})\left(p_{+}^{2}+M_{l}^{2}(p_{+})\right)},

(19)

\displaystyle G_{s}(p_{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathrm{i}\gamma\cdot p_{-}+M_{s}(p_{-})}{Z_{s}(p_{-})\left(p_{-}^{2}+M_{s}^{2}(p_{-})\right)}\,.

(20)

In the quasi-light-front framework with the longitudinal momentum, the momentum components of the quark and meson are given by

\displaystyle p_{\mu}

\displaystyle=(p_{0},p_{3},p_{1},p_{2})=(p_{0},p_{3},p_{\perp})\,,

(21)

\displaystyle P_{\mu}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{i}E_{K},\,P_{z},0,0)\,,

(22)

with

\displaystyle E_{K}=\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{K}^{2}}\,.

(23)

Subsequently, the kaon quasi-DA is defined as an integral, as follows

\displaystyle\phi_{K}(x,P_{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{K}}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{CD}}\bigg[\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\delta(\tilde{n}\cdot p_{+}-x\tilde{n}\cdot P)\gamma_{5}\gamma\cdot\tilde{n}\chi_{K}(p;P)\bigg]\,,

(24)

with
\tilde{n}=(0,1,0,0)
, where
f_{K}
represents the kaon decay constant, and the trace
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{CD}}
works in the color and Dirac spaces. In particular, the integration measure is given by

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int dp_{0}\int dp_{3}\int d^{2}p_{\perp}\,.

(25)

Inserting Eq.
21
and Eq.
22
into Eq.
24
, one finds that, in the functional QCD LaMET calculation, the meson on-shell momentum
P^{2}=-m_{K}^{2}
lies in the complex plane of Minkowski space, while the momentum
p
in the integrand are defined in Euclidean space. Thus, when we do the integration over
p_{0}
, the poles of the unamputated Bethe–Salpeter amplitude might cross the real axis for some range of
x
, leading to the breakdown of the calculations. Specifically, using the quark propagators in Eq.
19
and Eq.
20
and assuming constant quark masses, one is able to obtain four different poles of the integrand as follows

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\bigg[-\frac{\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{K}^{2}}}{2}-\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{l}^{2}}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\bigg[-\frac{\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{K}^{2}}}{2}+\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+(xP_{z})^{2}+M_{l}^{2}}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\bigg[\frac{\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{K}^{2}}}{2}-\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\big((x-1)P_{z}\big)^{2}+M_{s}^{2}}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,4}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\bigg[\frac{\sqrt{P_{z}^{2}+m_{K}^{2}}}{2}+\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+\big((x-1)P_{z}\big)^{2}+M_{s}^{2}}\bigg]\,,

(26)

two of which, i.e.,
p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}
and
p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}
, might cross the real axis and enter into the other half-plane. In
Zhang et al. 2025
, a finite imaginary shift
p_{0}\to p_{0}+\mathrm{i}C
in the integral of
p_{0}
is introduced to circumvent this difficulty, that is

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp_{0}\to\int_{-\infty+\mathrm{i}C}^{\infty+\mathrm{i}C}dp_{0}\,,

(27)

The shift quantity
C
is chosen such that the shifted integral contour crosses between the first and second poles and the third and fourth poles, see
Zhang et al. 2025
for more details. To that end, in this work, we choose

\displaystyle C=\begin{cases}g(0),&x<0\\[4.30554pt]
g(x),&0\leq x\leq 1\\[4.30554pt]
g(1),&x>1\end{cases}\,,

(28)

with the function

\displaystyle g(x)=\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}+\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}}{2}\,.

(29)

It should be noted that, as shown in Eqs.
26
, the right-hand side of the equations there are the quark mass functions
M_{l}(p)
and
M_{s}(p)
, which also depend on the shifted momentum
p_{0}+\mathrm{i}C
. Therefore, in practical calculations, the poles and the shift are determined through an iterative procedure until a stable convergence is reached. In Fig.
4
, we present the results of the integration shift and the pole results with and without iterations. It is observed that, after the iterative procedure, the divergent peaks of the poles in the endpoint region of
x
are removed. This improves on the stability of the quasi-DA calculation and extends the accessible range of longitudinal momentum.

After the shift, the maximal accessible range of the longitudinal momentum
P_{z}
is determined by the crossing positions of
p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}
and
p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}
, which require

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,2}>\mathrm{Im}\,\,p_{0,\mathrm{pole}\,3}\,,

(30)

that is

\displaystyle M_{l}^{2}+M_{s}^{2}+2p_{\perp}^{2}+2\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{l}^{2}}\sqrt{p_{\perp}^{2}+M_{s}^{2}}>m_{K}^{2}\,.

(31)

If the quark masses are treated as constants, Eq.
31
is automatically satisfied. However, once momentum-dependent quark mass functions are taken into account, it may no longer be valid. In particular, after performing the
p_{3}
integration via the delta function in Eq.
24
, which brings a dependence on
P_{z}
in the quark mass functions, the allowed range of
P_{z}
is constrained by Eq.
31
, see also
Zhang et al. 2025
for more details.

In Fig.
5
, we show the kaon quasi-DA
\phi_{K}(x,P_{z})
and its derivative
\partial\phi_{K}(x,P_{z})/\partial P_{z}
at
x=0.42
as functions of
P_{z}
. We find that the second and third poles are closest to each other at
x=0.42
, which also corresponds to the peak position of the quasi-DA. In this work, the longitudinal momentum can be extended to values up to about 3 GeV. However, unlike the pion results reported in
Zhang et al. 2025
;
Chang et al. 2026
, no plateau region or saturation behavior is observed within the present accessible range of
P_{z}
, which warrants further investigation in future work. The grey area in Fig.
5
shows that the first derivative of the quasi-DA with respect to
P_{z}
attains its minimum in the flat region
P_{z}\in[2,2.5]
GeV. In calculations, this interval is used to determine the maximal momentum
P_{z}^{\mathrm{max}}
for the longitudinal momentum region employed to extrapolate the light-cone kaon PDA. This constitutes one of the sources of systematic uncertainties in our calculation.

We conclude this section with the results for the kaon quasi-light-front wave function (quasi-LFWF) presented in Fig.
6
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\quad\psi_{K}(x,P_{z},p_{\perp})

\displaystyle\hskip-4.26773pt=\frac{1}{f_{K}}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{CD}}\bigg[\int\frac{dp_{0}dp_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\delta(\tilde{n}\cdot p_{+}-x\tilde{n}\cdot P)\gamma_{5}\gamma\cdot\tilde{n}\chi_{K}(p;P)\bigg]\,.

(32)

We find that the results of the quasi-LFWF already exhibit an asymmetry in
x
. By further integrating over the transverse momentum
p_{\perp}
, the kaon quasi-DA is obtained.

In this section, we discuss the extrapolation of the kaon quasi-DA, analyze the uncertainties, and present the final light-cone kaon DA. After that we will compare its first two order moments with results from Lattice QCD and DSE/BSE methods.

In Fig.
7
, we show the kaon quasi-DA for
P_{z}
ranging from 1.75 GeV to 2.5 GeV. From the results, the tilt of the peak structure has already indicated the flavour symmetry breaking in the internal structure of kaon. The quasi-DA becomes increasingly narrower as
P_{z}
increases, particularly in the vicinity of the peak region. Different from the pion results within the functional LaMET framework
Zhang et al. 2025
;
Chang et al. 2026
, no saturation behavior of the kaon quasi-DA is observed at large
P_{z}
in the present calculation. This necessitates a more careful assessment of the uncertainties in the
P_{z}
extrapolation. In this work, we adopt two extrapolation schemes in powers of
1/P_{z}
, up to second and fourth order, respectively, which are given by

\displaystyle\phi_{K}(x,P_{z})=\phi_{K}^{\mathrm{fit\,I}}(x,P_{z}\to\infty)+\frac{c_{2}(x)}{P_{z}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{P_{z}^{4}}\right)\,,

(33)

and

\displaystyle\quad\phi_{K}(x,P_{z})

\displaystyle=\phi_{K}^{\mathrm{fit\,II}}(x,P_{z}\to\infty)+\frac{c_{2}(x)}{P_{z}^{2}}+\frac{c_{4}(x)}{P_{z}^{4}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{P_{z}^{6}}\right)\,.

(34)

We find that further increase of the order of
P_{z}
expansion has a negligible effect on the extrapolated results. The extrapolation uncertainty considered here, together with the uncertainty associated with the choice of the

V
Conclusions and Outlook

In this work, based on a first-principles functional QCD framework combined with the LaMET method, we study the kaon quasi-DA at large longitudinal momentum and DA in the light-cone. We employ the quark correlation functions and the kaon Bethe–Salpeter amplitude obtained from the 2+1 flavour QCD computation within the fRG approach as inputs to construct the kaon quasi-DA. By performing integration contour deformation in the complex plane, we achieve a maximal accessible longitudinal momentum up to 3 GeV. For stability, we restrict the maximal momentum of the extrapolation range to
P_{z}^{\mathrm{max}}\in[2,2.5]
GeV.

By performing
1/P_{z}^{2}
and
1/P_{z}^{4}
order extrapolations together with endpoint fittings, we obtain the kaon light-cone DA and quantify the associated systematic uncertainties. The resulting DA is single-peaked and asymmetric, with its maximum located at
x=0.42
. We further obtain
\langle\xi\rangle_{K}=0.020(3)
and
\langle\xi^{2}\rangle_{K}=0.253(12)
. The second moment shows good agreement with lattice QCD
Hua et al. 2022
;
Bali et al. 2019
and DSE/BSE results
Chang et al. 2025
, indicating a reliable description of the DA width, while the first moment is slightly smaller but still consistent within current uncertainties.

In the present calculation, we do not observe a clear saturation behavior of the kaon quasi-DA in the large
P_{z}
region. This indicates that, for heavier mesons, further development of direct Minkowski-space computations within functional QCD is required, as well as improvements on the extrapolation procedures within the current LaMET framework. In addition, we plan to extend our studies to other observables, such as parton distribution functions and form factors, in future work.
