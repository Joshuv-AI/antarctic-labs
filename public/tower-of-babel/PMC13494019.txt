RT-DETR-FCES: a lightweight ship detection algorithm from remote sensing perspective
Authors: Yangtan Xiao, Chaoyi Zhang, Zhaoli Shen, Chenggang Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractShip detection plays a crucial role in maritime traffic safety, emergency rescue, and intelligent port management. However, existing methods still face challenges in balancing detection accuracy, real-time performance, and lightweight deployment in complex maritime environments, especially for small ship targets on resource-constrained platforms. To address these issues, this paper proposes RT-DETR-FCES, a lightweight real-time ship detection framework for optical remote sensing images. Specifically, FasterNet is adopted as the backbone network to reduce computational cost and model parameters, while Cascaded Group Attention (CGA) is introduced to enhance multi-scale feature fusion. In addition, an EUCB-SC module is designed to improve feature representation capability with efficient inference. Experimental results show that RT-DETR-FCES achieves 87.5% on the MASATI-v2 dataset and 49.1% on the VAI dataset. Compared with the baseline RT-DETR, the proposed method reduces the number of parameters and FLOPs by 45.5% and 48.0%, respectively, while achieving inference speeds of 87.7 FPS and 85.1 FPS. Overall, the proposed framework achieves a favorable balance between detection accuracy and lightweight efficiency, demonstrating its effectiveness for real-time maritime ship monitoring applications.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As a vital carrier for global trade transportation, maritime law enforcement, military operations and maritime emergency rescue, ships perform an irreplaceable role in safeguarding maritime traffic safety, improving port operation efficiency and maintaining marine security. Accurate and continuous ship monitoring and identification form the basis for effective maritime supervision and management. Especially in complex marine environments, small ship targets are prone to prominent missed detection and false detection due to their small scale, high maneuverability and susceptibility to environmental interference, which has become a key challenge in the current maritime monitoring system.

At present, maritime ship monitoring mainly relies on approaches such as the Automatic Identification System (AIS) 1, shore-based radar monitoring, maritime patrols and manual observation. Although the aforementioned methods possess certain practical value in coastal and port areas, they have obvious limitations in offshore regions, severe weather conditions or scenarios involving non-cooperative targets. For instance, the AIS depends on the active reporting of information by ships and is thus susceptible to signal loss or intentional shutdown; shore-based radars are restricted by coverage range and environmental noise; manual patrols are plagued by high costs and low efficiency. Relevant studies have indicated that a single monitoring method can hardly meet the requirements of maritime supervision under the current complex marine environment.

Against this backdrop, satellite remote sensing technology has gradually emerged as an important technical approach for maritime ship monitoring, thanks to its advantages of wide coverage, a stable observation cycle, and strong adaptability to the marine environment. Remote sensing scene image categorization lays a solid foundation for the popularization of remote sensing data in civil and military scenarios, while high dimensionality and insufficient labeled samples still bring challenges to practical image classification tasks 2. By carrying a variety of sensors, satellite remote sensing systems can conduct continuous observations of the reflective and radiative information from sea surfaces and ship targets, thereby enabling large-scale and all-weather ship monitoring. Benefiting from the rapid progress of deep transfer learning, remote sensing image analysis capability has been greatly improved. Early studies have verified the feasibility of high-resolution satellite imagery for ship detection and recognition, and with the continuous improvement of imaging resolution and data acquisition capabilities, ship detection based on remote sensing imagery has gradually become a key research direction in the field of maritime monitoring.

Recently, the rapid advancement of deep learning technology has provided novel solutions for remote sensing-based ship monitoring 3. Object detection algorithms based on convolutional neural networks (CNNs), such as Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, and the You Only Look Once (YOLO) series of models, have been widely applied to remote sensing image processing tasks and exhibit robust feature learning capabilities in complex background scenarios. Relevant studies have demonstrated that the construction of large-scale training datasets and the adoption of deep learning models can effectively improve the detection accuracy and robustness of ship targets 4. At present, research on remote sensing-based ship detection has focused primarily on two categories of data sources: ship detection based on synthetic aperture radar (SAR) data, and that based on optical remote sensing imagery.

Ship detection models based on Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data have demonstrated significant advantages in maritime monitoring owing to the all-time and all-weather imaging capability of SAR systems, particularly under complex oceanic and meteorological conditions. In recent years, extensive research efforts have focused on several key technical aspects, including multi-scale feature representation, rotated object detection, and suppression of complex sea clutter backgrounds. These studies have achieved notable progress in both algorithmic design and detection accuracy, leading to relatively mature developments in SAR-based ship detection methodologies. For example, Li et al. proposed a novel multi-dimensional deep learning network 5. This method fuses the complementary features of the spatial and frequency domains, realizes ship detection by leveraging multi-dimensional information, and verifies its effectiveness on the existing SAR Ship Detection Dataset (SSDD) 6. In comparison with other CNN-based algorithms, this method achieves superior performance, with particularly remarkable results in handling multi-scale and rotated ship targets in complex backgrounds. Park et al. took the YOLOv7-tiny network architecture as the foundation to design a feature extraction enhancement network based on the deformable convolutional network, and proposed an adaptive feature recognition method driven by the BiFormer attention mechanism 7. This study also developed an intelligent intersection over union (IoU) calculation method based on a dynamic non-monotonic focusing mechanism, which is further adopted to construct the loss function, thereby effectively improving the convergence speed and generalization ability of the model. Experimental results show that the mean average precision (mAP) of this model reaches 88.84% and 99.18% on the High-Resolution SAR Image Dataset (HRSID) 8 and the SSDD dataset, respectively. However, constrained by the SAR imaging mechanism, such data has inherent deficiencies in the representation of fine ship structural features, which makes it difficult to meet the application requirements of precise ship type recognition and dense coastal scene analysis.

In contrast, optical remote sensing imagery provides richer texture and structural information, allowing for more accurate representation of fine-grained features such as ship contours, deck structures, and surface textures. As a result, it plays an important role in ship detection and recognition tasks. Relevant studies have demonstrated that deep learning models based on optical remote sensing imagery hold great potential for the accurate localization and discrimination of ship targets. For example, Liu et al. proposed a cascaded hull-wake detection scheme based on high-resolution optical satellite imagery, which performs ship localization and classification through multi-step processing and delivers outstanding detection and classification performance in GF-1 imagery 9. This method outperforms traditional single-feature detection approaches and achieves high levels of recall and precision rates. Aiming at the challenges of ship detection in optical remote sensing imagery, Li et al. developed a detection framework integrating cross-level multi-scale features 10, rotated candidate regions and a self-attention bidirectional recurrent neural network, which enhances the localization accuracy in dense scenarios and multi-scale detection performance by incorporating ship prior information. Validated on datasets such as DOTA-Ship and HRSC2016 11, this method shows that each module contributes remarkably and achieves state-of-the-art overall performance, yielding significant reference value for research on ship detection in optical satellite imagery.

Although optical remote sensing imagery, when integrated with intelligent detection models, offers significant potential for ship detection, the detection models themselves still face notable challenges. These challenges are summarized as follows:

Limited robustness of detection models in complex marine environments: Variations in illumination, strong sea-surface reflections, and interference from coastal or terrestrial backgrounds introduce significant noise into visual features, making it difficult for detection models to learn discriminative ship representations. As a result, false alarms and missed detections frequently occur, especially in long-range observation and cluttered scenes.

On the one hand, although traditional deep learning detection models can ensure a certain level of accuracy, they are characterized by large parameter scales and high computational complexity, making them difficult to deploy on resource-constrained devices such as satellite on-board processing units and unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). On the other hand, while existing lightweight models have reduced computational costs, they often do so at the expense of detection accuracy for small targets, thus failing to achieve a balance between efficiency and performance.

Against this backdrop, lightweight real-time detection models have emerged as a research hotspot. However, mainstream real-time object detection methods, predominantly represented by the YOLO series of models, adopt a convolution-based local feature modeling approach that exhibits limited representational capacity for small targets in complex marine backgrounds. Furthermore, these methods rely on Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) 12 for post-processing, which makes them prone to erroneously eliminating detection boxes corresponding to small-scale targets. Although existing studies have attempted to alleviate this issue by improving the NMS mechanism 13, detection stability remains constrained in scenarios with dense targets or significant scale variations.

In recent years, Transformer-based object detection models have garnered increasing attention in the field. Relevant studies have demonstrated that DETR-based methods have exhibited the potential to outperform traditional YOLO models in real-time object detection tasks. Among them, the Real-Time Detection Transformer (RT-DETR) 14 employs an end-to-end, NMS-free detection framework and models long-range dependencies via a global attention mechanism. Its inference speed is independent of variations in the number of targets, thus delivering more stable real-time performance in complex scenarios. Existing research has applied RT-DETR and its improved variants to ship detection tasks, and verified their effectiveness in infrared imaging 15 or complex marine environments 16.

Existing lightweight variants of RT-DETR are predominantly developed for general object detection tasks, with limited consideration of task-specific characteristics in remote sensing ship detection. To reduce computational overhead, these methods generally prioritize feature preservation for medium and large objects, which inevitably compromises the discriminative representation of small targets. However, in remote sensing ship detection scenarios, small vessels constitute the most critical yet challenging detection targets. They typically occupy only a few to dozens of pixels in satellite imagery, exhibiting extremely weak semantic and structural features, and are thus highly vulnerable to information degradation during model compression.

This inherent design mismatch between general lightweight methods and remote sensing ship detection tasks results in a pronounced performance degradation. While general lightweight RT-DETR variants achieve competitive overall performance on standard object detection benchmarks, their small target detection capability deteriorates drastically when applied to remote sensing ship datasets. Furthermore, the compression achieved by most existing methods is relatively modest, failing to meet the stringent deployment constraints imposed by resource-limited platforms such as unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and on-board satellite processing units. Consequently, there remains an urgent need for a dedicated lightweight RT-DETR framework specifically tailored to the challenges of small ship detection in remote sensing imagery.

To bridge this research gap, this work proposes a small-target-preserving lightweight design paradigm and develops a novel detection model termed RT-DETR-FCES (Fast Cascaded Enhanced Shift RT-DETR) for remote sensing ship detection. The core principle underlying this paradigm is that model compression should be performed without compromising the discriminative representation of small targets, and all architectural optimizations should be guided by the unique characteristics of remote sensing maritime scenes.

Specifically, FasterNet is adopted as the backbone network, whose Partial Convolution (PConv) mechanism effectively preserves the spatial continuity of small target features by eliminating redundant channel computations without disrupting the spatial information flow. Second, an Efficient Upsampling Convolution Block with Shift Channel Mix (EUCB-SC) is proposed to mitigate fine-grained detail loss during upsampling. This module integrates a zero-parameter shift channel mixing strategy into the upsampling process, enabling cross-channel spatial information exchange without additional computational overhead. Finally, Cascaded Group Attention (CGA) is integrated into the feature fusion neck to enhance contextual modeling for small targets. By decomposing global self-attention into lightweight grouped cascaded operations, CGA efficiently captures long-range dependencies while maintaining linear computational complexity, thereby improving the discriminability between small ships and complex sea clutter backgrounds.

Materials and methods

Dataset processing and dataset production

This study adopts three public remote sensing datasets, MASATI-v2 17, Vessels in Aerial Images (VAI), and HRSC2016-MS 18, to conduct comprehensive performance verification of the proposed model under diverse maritime observation scenarios, including general detection, cross-domain generalization, and multi-scale adaptability evaluation.

MASATI-v2 is a specialized remote sensing dataset designed for ship detection, consisting of optical satellite images acquired under various sea conditions 19. In this study, a subset of 4,157 images containing ship targets was selected from the original dataset, which was randomly divided into training and validation sets at a ratio of 8:2. Among them, 3,325 images were used for model training and parameter optimization, and the remaining 832 images served as the validation set to monitor the model performance during training. All images underwent preprocessing before training: first, they were uniformly resized to 640×640 pixels, and then normalized using the general standardization scheme of the ImageNet dataset. The pixel values of the red, green, and blue channels of the images were first normalized to the interval [0,1], and then standardized with the mean [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] and standard deviation [0.229, 0.224, 0.225] respectively, so that the pixel values follow a normal distribution with a mean of 0 and a variance of 1.

To improve the generalization ability of the model and its adaptability to complex marine environments, a random combined data augmentation strategy was only applied to the training set, covering mainstream methods such as flipping, rotation, cropping, color adjustment, noise addition, and Mosaic augmentation. A fixed 2× augmentation ratio was adopted in this study, and the sample size of the training set was stably expanded to 6,650 images after processing. This enriches sample diversity while ensuring feature authenticity, thereby enhancing the model’s detection performance.

The VAI dataset is a public ship detection benchmark dataset on the Kaggle platform, including 13,435 aerial and satellite images that cover challenging scenarios such as complex sea clutter and low-contrast targets. Officially, this dataset is divided into training, validation and test sets in a 7:2:1 ratio, specifically 9,405 images for the training set (approximately 70%), 2,687 images for the validation set (approximately 20%) and 1,343 images for the test set (approximately 10%), which are used for model training, validation analysis and final evaluation of generalization ability, respectively. To ensure the consistency and rigor of the experiment, all subsets adopted the identical preprocessing process as the MASATI-v2 dataset, i.e., resizing to 640×640 pixels and implementing the aforementioned standardization. Consistent with the processing of MASATI-v2, the same combined data augmentation strategy was only applied to the training set of the VAI dataset, and the sample size was expanded to 18,810 images with the fixed 2× ratio, ensuring consistency of the training scale and augmentation standards between the two datasets.

HRSC2016-MS is a high-resolution optical remote sensing dataset specifically constructed for multi-scale ship detection evaluation. It contains a total of 1,061 images with rich scene diversity, covering various maritime environments such as open seas, ports, and coastal areas, and includes ship targets with a wide range of scale variations, making it suitable for validating the multi-scale adaptability of detection models. Following the official division protocol, the dataset is split into a training set (436 images), a validation set (181 images), and a test set (444 images). Consistent with the above two datasets, all images are uniformly resized to 640×640 pixels and normalized using the ImageNet standardization scheme. The same random combined data augmentation strategy is applied only to the training set to enhance model generalization.

To quantitatively evaluate the multi-scale detection performance, ship targets in the HRSC2016-MS dataset are categorized into small, medium, and large scales based on the COCO benchmark criterion, defined by the pixel area of the bounding box:

Deep learning-based algorithm for ship detection

FasterNet

In the context of small ship detection from remote sensing imagery, efficient utilization of computational resources is critical due to the requirement for real-time processing of massive image data and the limited computing capability of deployment platforms, such as satellite- and drone-borne imaging systems. Consequently, designing a detection model that achieves an appropriate balance between computational efficiency and detection accuracy remains a core challenge. The original RT-DETR framework employs ResNet as its backbone network, which is well known for its strong feature extraction capability; however, its high architectural complexity and large number of parameters significantly hinder deployment on resource-constrained remote sensing devices.

To address this challenge, the backbone network is replaced with FasterNet, an architecture specifically optimized for computational efficiency 20. FasterNet improves inference speed by introducing PConv, which restricts convolutional operations to a subset of input channels while allowing the remaining channels to bypass convolution. Efficient upsampling convolution block with shift channel mix (EUCB-SC)

Although RT-DETR achieves strong real-time detection performance through an efficient Transformer-based decoder, its feature pyramid construction and upsampling strategy are primarily designed for semantic alignment and computational efficiency. Such a design inevitably limits the modeling of fine-grained spatial details, particularly in scenarios involving small objects with ambiguous boundaries and complex backgrounds.

To overcome these limitations while preserving the lightweight nature of RT-DETR, we propose an enhanced EUCB-SC, which improves upsampling efficiency and feature fusion. Inspired by the shift operation in the Binary Hybrid Vision Transformer (BHViT) architecture 21, the proposed EUCB-SC extends the conventional EUCB by explicitly strengthening spatial information interaction across channels (Fig. 3).

Metric evaluation

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed model, a set of widely adopted evaluation metrics was used, including Precision (P), Recall (R), mean Average Precision at an IoU threshold of 0.5 (), mean Average Precision averaged over IoU thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95 (), detection speed measured in Frames Per Second (FPS), Floating Point Operations (FLOPs), number of model parameters (Params) and model size.

Among these metrics, mean Average Precision (mAP) is used to assess the overall detection accuracy of an object detection model by aggregating precision–recall performance across all object categories. Specifically, represents the average precision computed under an Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold of 0.5, which mainly evaluates coarse localization capability. In contrast, adopts multiple IoU thresholds ranging from 0.5 to 0.95 with a step size of 0.05, providing a more rigorous evaluation of localization accuracy and robustness under different matching conditions.

Detection speed, measured in Frames Per Second (FPS), reflects the number of images processed per second during inference and serves as a key indicator of the model’s real-time performance. Floating Point Operations (FLOPs) quantify the computational complexity of the model during forward propagation, while the number of parameters (Params) represents the total count of learnable weights, indicating the structural complexity and storage requirements of the model. The size of the model, defined as the disk space required to save the model parameters, directly affects the feasibility of deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.

Collectively, these metrics establish a comprehensive evaluation framework from three perspectives: detection accuracy, inference efficiency, and computational cost. Sensitivity analysis of key hyperparameters

To rigorously validate the rationality of hyperparameter selection in the proposed RT-DETR-FCES model and provide quantitative guidance for parameter tuning in practical deployment, similar studies have proven that reasonable hyperparameter optimization can effectively boost the overall performance of remote sensing image detection and analysis models 23,24. We thus conducted a comprehensive sensitivity analysis on three core hyperparameters: the partial ratio r in PConv, the number of groups G in CGA, and the magnitude of the shift in the ShiftChannelMix module. All experiments were performed in the validation set of the MASATI-v2 dataset with identical training configurations (150 epochs, SGD optimizer, batch size=8) and hardware environment (NVIDIA RTX 4060 Ti 16GB GPU) to ensure the comparability of the results. Sensitivity analysis of partial ratio r in PConv

The partial ratio r controls the proportion of channels involved in convolutional operations in PConv, thereby affecting the trade-off between computational efficiency and feature extraction capability. In the proposed model, the default setting of FasterNet (r=1/4) was adopted. To evaluate the influence of this parameter, the number of groups in CGA was fixed at G=4, while the shift magnitude was fixed to 1 pixel. Discussion

Small-target ship detection remains a challenging task in maritime surveillance due to complex sea clutter, weak target features, and the computational limitations of UAV and edge platforms. Existing studies have improved detection performance through feature enhancement and multi-scale fusion; however, many approaches still suffer from high computational overhead and limited robustness in complex maritime environments. For example, Liu et al. 35 improved infrared ship feature extraction using CSP-based structures, but the inference speed remained relatively low. Wang et al. 31 introduced BiFPN and S2 modules to strengthen cross-scale fusion, although the multi-branch design substantially increased model complexity. Chen et al. 26 improved efficiency using FFPN, yet the detection capability for ultra-small targets under dynamic sea conditions remained limited. In addition, many existing methods are mainly evaluated on relatively ideal datasets, leaving their robustness in challenging maritime scenarios insufficiently explored.

To address these limitations, this study proposes RT-DETR-FCES, a collaborative lightweight optimization framework for maritime ship detection. FasterNet is adopted as the backbone to reduce computational complexity, CGA enhances contextual modeling and feature fusion, and the proposed EUCB-SC module improves feature interaction during lightweight upsampling. Experimental results demonstrate that RT-DETR-FCES achieves competitive performance on both MASATI-v2 and VAI datasets, obtaining 87.5% and 49.1% , respectively. Compared with the baseline RT-DETR, the proposed method reduces parameters and FLOPs by 45.5% and 48.0%, while maintaining real-time inference speeds of 87.7 FPS and 85.1 FPS. The stable cross-dataset performance further indicates good robustness and generalization capability for practical maritime scenarios.

Beyond accuracy and efficiency, reliability and trustworthiness are increasingly important for safety-sensitive maritime AI systems. Recent studies 36 emphasized that trustworthy AI should additionally consider robustness, explainability, traceability, and lifecycle governance. In this regard, the proposed RT-DETR-FCES framework aligns with the development trend of trustworthy maritime AI through cross-dataset robustness evaluation and lightweight deployment capability. Nevertheless, several limitations still remain. Dense sea clutter, strong wave interference, and low-contrast targets may still cause missed detections for extremely small ships. In addition, the current framework relies only on visible-light imagery, while limited annotations for ultra-small targets and the lack of long-term edge-device deployment verification also constrain practical applicability.

Future work will focus on improving robustness under challenging maritime conditions. Larger multi-scenario maritime datasets containing complex sea states and ultra-small targets will be explored to enhance generalization capability. Multi-modal fusion strategies integrating optical, radar, infrared, and AIS information will also be investigated to improve detection reliability in adverse environments. Furthermore, lightweight deployment techniques such as model quantization and pruning will be studied to further improve efficiency on embedded and edge computing platforms.

Conclusion

This study proposed RT-DETR-FCES, a lightweight real-time ship detection framework for complex maritime environments. By integrating FasterNet, Cascaded Group Attention, and the proposed EUCB-SC module, the framework improves feature representation while reducing computational complexity. Experimental results on the MASATI-v2 and VAI datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves competitive detection accuracy and real-time inference performance, reaching 87.5% and 49.1% , respectively. Compared with the baseline RT-DETR, the proposed framework reduces parameters and FLOPs by 45.5% and 48.0%, while maintaining inference speeds of 87.7 FPS and 85.1 FPS. Overall, RT-DETR-FCES achieves a favorable balance between accuracy and efficiency, demonstrating strong potential for real-time maritime surveillance and deployment on resource-constrained platforms.
