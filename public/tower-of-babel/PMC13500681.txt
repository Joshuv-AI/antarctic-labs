Research on risk assessment method of wireline logging sticking and analysis of sensitive factors
Authors: Kai Wei, Boyu Zhao, Wenqiang Feng, Yan Yan, Guoshan Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, a quantitative evaluation method of wireline logging adhesion during jacking operation is proposed based on the soft string model Firstly, the principle of cable sticking is elucidated in order to judge the cable logging sticking. Then, combined with the soft rod model, the calculation theory of wireline logging adhesion is proposed, and the evaluation model of wireline logging adhesion is established. The model quantifies the risk of wireline sticking, and proposes a risk probability assessment method for wireline sticking during jacking. Finally, a case study of an actual well was conducted to verify the effectiveness of the proposed assessment method. The results show that this well shows signs of wireline logging sticking, and the assessment method is consistent with the actual field observations. An analysis of the sensitive factors influencing cable adhesion and sticking reveals that the cable adhesion and sticking risk is most sensitive to wellbore inclination and drilling fluid density. Considering the combined effects of the main sensitive factors, the overall cable adhesion and sticking risk is reduced by approximately 35%. The results demonstrate that the cable adhesion and sticking evaluation method established in this study is effective, capable of assessing the risk of cable adhesion and sticking in new wells. The method is scientifically sound and has practical application value.Keywords: Wireline logging, Wireline sticking, Soft string model, Risk probability, Analysis of sensitive factorsSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Wireline logging is a crucial technology for acquiring various petroleum geological and engineering technical data1–4;. In recent years, with the increasing depth of drilling5,6; and the growing number of directional wells7, the risk of wireline logging has gradually increased, and wireline sticking has become a common issue. Prolonged inactivity of logging instruments, poor mud performance, or improper implementation of technical measures may increase the possibility of wireline sticking. Generally, after wireline sticking appears, it can only be determined whether it can be released by lifting and lowering the wireline8. However, the tension that the logging wireline can withstand is limited, and excessive lifting may cause the wireline to pull off, resulting in the instrument sinking to the bottom of the well, Conversely, lowering the wireline may exacerbate ad-hesion issues.

Currently, scholars have extensively researched the principle of wireline adsorption sticking. Ruslan Manakhayev9 analyzed Tengiz Oilfield and found that the mud density is large, and strong positive pressure difference will be generated locally, which makes the well wall adsorb logging instruments and wirelines to a certain extent, affecting the lifting and lowering of logging instruments. Tjioe and Martin10 analysed the main factors responsible for wireline sticking in the deepwater Gulf of Mexico, including settling time, bare hole length, wellbore diameter and drilling fluid performance. It can be found that the predecessors have basically determined the influential factors of wireline sticking11, and given some methods to prevent wireline sticking12, but there is no set of physical calculation methods to determine whether wireline sticking occurs.

Current research on wireline logging sticking faces issues such as a lack of quantitative methods, insufficient model universality, unsystematic preventive measures, and limited experimental data. Conclusions are often based on specific regions and theoretical analysis, lacking practical validation. In order to clarify the risk of wireline logging sticking and judge the risk probability of wireline logging sticking, a method of quantitative evaluation of wireline logging sticking is proposed by establishing a physical model to realize the quantitative evaluation of wireline logging sticking. The calculation theory of wireline friction is adopted, and the influence of mud cake thickness on wireline sticking is quantitatively characterized to form a quantitative evaluation method of wireline logging risk, which can clarify the risk probability of wireline adsorption and realize the quantitative evaluation of wireline sticking One at A Time (OAT) method is used to determine the ordering of the main control factors of wireline sticking, which has wide application value.

In the process of wireline logging, the wireline is embedded into the mud cake due to its own weight and pressure difference (the difference between wellbore drilling fluid pressure and formation pressure), etc. When the friction force of the wireline in the mud cake is greater than the safe tensile tension of the wireline, the wireline sticking will be generated. Wireline friction calculation soft string model

In this study, a soft-string model was employed to characterize the contact and frictional behavior between the wireline and the borehole wall16. Based on the mechanical equilibrium of the wireline and the geometrical relationship of the well trajectory, this model can effectively describe the force distribution of the wireline within the wellbore. On this basis, a mud-cake correction was further introduced, allowing the influence of borehole wall conditions on the friction coefficient to be represented more realistically, thereby directly serving the prediction of adhesion-related sticking risk. The model has a clear physical basis, which facilitates parameter sensitivity analysis to identify the key factors governing sticking. Meanwhile, its computational framework is relatively simple, offering good practicality and computational efficiency.

The Calculation Model of Risk Probability of Wireline Sticking.

The Calculation Model of Risk Probability of Wireline Sticking.

The occurrence of wireline differential sticking is essentially governed by whether the total surface tension exceeds the critical tension, Tc. During actual field operations, the total surface tension is not constant; rather, it varies between the minimum total tension, Tmin, and the maximum total tension, Tmax, owing to the combined effects of wellbore trajectory, frictional drag, downhole fluid conditions, and wireline operating parameters. Therefore, the interval [Tmin,Tmax]can be regarded as the possible fluctuation range of the total surface tension, while the interval [Tc, Tmax], in which the critical tension is exceeded, may be defined as the hazardous range for wireline differential sticking. Based on this consideration, the risk of wireline differential sticking is characterized by the ratio of the hazardous interval to the overall variation range of total surface tension. The closer the critical tension Tc is to the minimum total tension Tmin within the total tension variation interval, the larger the hazardous interval in which the total tension exceeds the critical threshold, and consequently, the higher the likelihood of wireline differential sticking. Conversely, as Tc approaches the maximum total tension Tmax, the hazardous interval becomes smaller, and the risk of differential sticking correspondingly decreases.

Parameter sensitivity analysis

One of the simplest and most common methods in sensitivity analysis is the OAT method, which typically involves moving one input variable, keeping the baseline values of the other variables, then returning the variable to its nominal value, and then repeating the same way for each other input. After that, the sensitivity can be measured by monitoring changes in the output. The OAT method can clearly view the influence of a single sensitivity factor on the output result, which is intuitive and reliable. Therefore, in order to clearly display the sensitivity factors of wireline sticking, OAT analysis is used to change only one parameter at a time to see its impact on the risk probability, so as to evaluate the risk factors of wireline sticking scientifically and reasonably.

The relevant parameters were sorted out, and OAT analysis was applied to well inclination, porosity, drilling fluid density and viscosity, and these parameters were changed successively to observe and analyze the changes in the probability of wireline sticking.

(1) Well inclination angle.

The inclination angle, defined as the angle between the wellbore axis and the direction of gravity, directly affects the force state of the wireline within the wellbore. Based on the force analysis of the soft-string model, as the inclination angle increases, the component of the wireline weight acting normal to the borehole wall correspondingly increases, resulting in a significant rise in the normal contact force between the wireline and the borehole wall. Meanwhile, the contact length of the wireline along the borehole wall also increases, further expanding the frictional interaction zone. According to the classical friction law, in which friction force is proportional to the normal force, these changes lead to an increase in the overall drag acting on the wireline during its movement in the wellbore, thereby aggravating the risk of adhesion-related wireline sticking. Therefore, the inclination angle is identified as a key parameter influencing the risk probability, which increases monotonically with increasing inclination angle.

(2) Porosity.

Porosity refers to the ratio of the volume of interconnected pores in a rock to the total volume of the rock. Based on the single factor analysis of porosity, it can be found that porosity has little effect on the risk of adsorption card.

(3) Drilling fluid density.

The selection of drilling fluid density should comprehensively account for both formation pressure balance and wellbore stability requirements. An increase in drilling fluid density directly raises the hydrostatic pressure within the wellbore. On the one hand, this enhances the compaction of the mud cake on the borehole wall, thereby increasing the friction coefficient at the mud-cake surface. On the other hand, if the formation pore pressure remains unchanged, the pressure differential between the wellbore and the formation correspondingly increases, resulting in a greater lateral extrusion force acting on the wireline. These two effects jointly increase the contact pressure and frictional resistance between the wireline and the borehole wall, thereby significantly elevating the probability of adhesion-related sticking. Therefore, drilling fluid density is identified as another key influencing factor, and the corresponding risk probability increases with increasing drilling fluid density.

(4) Drilling fluid viscosity.

Drilling fluid viscosity represents the overall manifestation of internal friction arising from interactions between suspended solid particles and liquid molecules during fluid flow. A high-viscosity drilling fluid can form a stable fluid film within the annular gap between the wireline and the borehole wall, thereby achieving solid–solid separation through the hydrodynamic lubrication effect and significantly reducing sliding friction. At the same time, during the squeezing process, a higher-viscosity fluid generates greater resistance, which delays the approach of the wireline toward the borehole wall and mitigates the impact of normal contact. In addition, high-viscosity drilling fluid is beneficial for filtrate-loss control and promotes the formation of a dense and lubricating thin mud cake, thereby preventing the development of localized high-friction points. Therefore, the probability of adhesion-related wireline sticking decreases with increasing drilling fluid viscosity.

Applicability analysis

Compared to existing research methods, the physical model developed in this study has clear advantages in terms of mechanistic explanation and parameter controllability. Unlike other methods that primarily rely on large amounts of historical well data for training and fitting, the model in this study is built directly on the mechanical mechanisms of cable-wellbore interaction, providing a clear explanation of the physical causes behind adhesion and sticking risks. Additionally, the input parameters of this model are measurable or controllable engineering parameters (such as wellbore inclination, porosity, drilling fluid density, and viscosity), allowing researchers and engineers to quantitatively assess the contribution of different factors to the risk through sensitivity analysis. It should be noted that, in new wells or complex well sections with limited historical data, the physical model has a greater advantage, while in well areas with sufficient data accumulation, data-driven methods may offer superior predictive efficiency. Therefore, in terms of technical positioning, the model developed in this study is well-suited as a mechanism-driven prediction tool, providing reference for early-stage risk assessment and parameter optimization in on-site operations.

To verify the effectiveness and generalization capability of the proposed model, a systematic validation was conducted in this study using field logging data from a total of 38 wells in the South China Sea. One directional well in the South China Sea was selected as a representative case for detailed analysis, with the aim of clearly illustrating the model development procedure and application workflow. The case selection was intended to ensure both typicality and representativeness, rather than to confine the applicability of the model to a single-well condition. From the perspective of the underlying physical mechanism, the model was developed on the basis of wireline mechanical equilibrium and frictional contact theory. Its governing equations do not depend on a specific well type or geographical region, and therefore the model possesses good theoretical generality. Under different operating environments, such as high-temperature/high-pressure (HTHP) wells, deepwater wells, and wells in cold regions, the fundamental force mechanism and frictional behavior between the wireline and the borehole wall remain essentially unchanged, and the model framework is still applicable. It should be noted, however, that in actual operating conditions, key parameters such as the friction coefficient, drilling fluid rheological properties, and the mechanical properties of the wireline material may vary significantly with environmental factors including temperature and pressure. Therefore, in practical applications, these model parameters should be appropriately calibrated according to specific operating conditions so as to accurately characterize the risk of adhesion-related sticking under different downhole environments.

Conclusions

Wireline logging is a key technology to accurately obtain formation information, and there is a construction risk of wireline logging sticking in the logging process. According to the phenomenon of wireline logging sticking in the actual logging site, the risk factors of wireline logging sticking were analyzed, and a method for quantitative evaluation of wireline logging sticking risk by establishing a physical model was proposed, which took into account the influence of wellbore mud cake. By modifying the friction coefficient, the calculation method of wireline logging sticking friction is obtained, the risk probability of wireline sticking can be defined, and the quantitative evaluation of wireline logging sticking can be realized.

OAT method was used to analyze the sensitivity factors of wireline logging sticking. It was found that inclination angle and drilling fluid density were the main sensitivity factors of wireline logging sticking, and both of them were positively correlated with wireline logging sticking. Reducing inclination Angle could reduce the risk by about 30%, and reducing drilling fluid density could reduce the risk probability by about 40%. In the design of well structure, the two values should be considered reasonably to reduce the risk of wireline logging sticking.

In applications across different oilfield regions, the predictive accuracy of the model is affected by factors such as formation architecture, wireline type, mud system, and operating parameters. Under conditions of complex geology, limited historical data, or operating conditions that fall outside the distribution range of the modeling samples, the model may exhibit significant prediction errors. Therefore, the model should be calibrated for specific operating regions through targeted experiments, with key parameters identified and tuned and its applicability limits clearly defined, so as to improve its prediction reliability and generalization capability across different regions and operating conditions.

The wireline differential sticking risk prediction model proposed in this study features low computational complexity and strong portability. In the next stage, a regional parameter self-adaptation mechanism will be developed based on this model, enabling rapid calibration of key parameters using a limited amount of experimental data or historical inversion results, thereby reducing the cost of repeated laboratory testing. In parallel, the model will be integrated with lightweight data-driven methods to establish a hybrid inference framework suitable for downhole edge-computing units, allowing online model updating and real-time risk warning, and further enhancing the intelligence level and risk response capability of wireline logging operations.
