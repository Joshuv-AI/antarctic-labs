Fine projection complex and subsurface homeomorphisms with positive stable commutator length
Yongsheng JIA, Yusen LONG

Abstract
Drawing inspiration from [ BBF15 ] , we construct a family of unbounded quasi-trees for a connected closed oriented surface S g S_{g} of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 , upon which the group Homeo 0 ​ ( S g ) \mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S_{g}) acts coboundedly by isometries. As an application, we show that some surface homeomorphisms preserving a non-sporadic essential subsurface or an essential subsurface homeomorphic to a once-bordered torus can have positive stable commutator length in Homeo 0 ​ ( S g ) \mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S_{g}) . Moreover, we provide a version of projection complex that does not require the finiteness conditions.



Keywords
: surface homeomorphisms, stable commutator length, quasi-morphisms, fine projection complex.

Introduction

It is our business to puncture gasbags and discover seeds of truth.

Virginia Woolf,

Thoughts on Peace in an Air Raid (1940)

A central theme in geometric group theory is the study of isometric group actions on various non-positively curved spaces or with negative curvature characteristics. Such a methodology has found profound applications in geometric topology, particularly in the understanding of mapping class groups of surfaces. An important example is the curve graph, see for example
[
MM99
,
MM00
]
.

Consider a connected closed oriented surface
S
. In order to give a negative answer to a question asked by Burago, Ivanov and Polterovich (see
[
BIP08
]
) on whether the group
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(S)
is
uniformly perfect
, recently Bowden, Hensel and Webb introduced in
[
BHW22
]
a Gromov hyperbolic graph, called the
fine curve graph
(see
Section 2.D
) and denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
, analogous to the curve graph. Since then, interests grew significantly in actions of homeomorphism groups on this graph (see for example,
[
LRW24
,
LMP+25
,
BMS25
,
Cho25
,
Boo26
,
FHL26
]
), as well as in the geometric and topological properties of this graph (see for example,
[
FH24
,
BHW24
,
LT25
,
DHNS26
]
). Moreover, much effort has been made to understand the connection between the group actions on these spaces and the dynamics of homeomorphisms on the surface; also see, for example
[
BHM+22a
,
GM24
,
Ein24
,
HL25
]
. Similar strategies are also applied to study the groups of diffeomorphisms on non-orientable surfaces (see for example
[
KK25
,
Bö26
]
) and the group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms on the 2-sphere
[
JW25
]
. The underlying philosophy is to investigate groups of surface homeomorphisms and diffeomorphisms in a way analogous to the study of mapping class groups.

This paper concerns the group of surface homeomorphisms isotopic to the identity and its actions on several new spaces of non-positive curvature characteristics.

It is known in
[
BBF15
]
that mapping class groups admit isometric cobounded actions on quasi-trees of infinite diameter. We also remark that there is another more recent version of this construction that uses a sharper version of Behrstock inequality
[
BBFS19
]
. In this paper, by a similar approach, we construct a Gromov hyperbolic space called
fine projection complex
, which is also quasi-isometric to a simplicial tree. It turns out that the isometric action of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
on this space is cobounded:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S
be a connected closed oriented surface. Then the group
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
admits cobounded isometric actions on unbounded quasi-trees.

Remark 1.2
.

For any fine projection complex on which
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
acts coboundedly by isometries, if this action can be extended to a
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
-action (
e.g.
starting from a
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
-invariant collection of subsurfaces, see
Section 4.A
), then the
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
-action is also cobounded. In contrast, there are fine projection complexes with cobounded
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
-action but the
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
-action is not cobounded.

However, we remark that this space cannot be obtained by directly applying the results in
[
BBF15
]
for the following reasons. The first reason is that Behrstock’s inequality (see
[
Beh06
]
or the
(P1)
axiom in
[
BBF15
]
) fails in general for arbitrary collection of subsurfaces.

Now, consider an essential subsurface
X\subset S
. As in
[
BBF15
]
, we wish to build a hyperbolic graph of which the vertices contain the
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
-orbit of
X
, denoted by
\mathscr{X}
. The finiteness condition
(P2)
in
[
BBF15
]
never holds for any collection of subsurfaces containing
\mathscr{X}
: given
\theta>0
, if
X,Y,Z\subset S
are such that the projection distance (see (
3.1
) for definition)

d_{X}^{\pi}(Y,Z)>\theta,

then the image
X^{\prime}
of
X
under any small perturbation, which is still in
\mathscr{X}
, will also verify
d_{X^{\prime}}^{\pi}(Y,Z)>\theta
, and there are uncountably many of them.

In order to deal with these defects, we introduce the notion of
velcrot subsurfaces
, see
Section 3.A
. This notion is inspired by
[
Cho25
,
HL25
]
where they established a
metric weak proper discontinuity
for some surface homeomorphisms of their actions on the fine curve graph. Roughly speaking, an element
g\in G
acting on a metric space enjoys the classical weak proper discontinuity,
abbrv. WPD
, if the joint coarse stabiliser of distant
g
-orbit is finite; see
[
BF02
]
for the precise definition. However, for similar reasons as above, this finiteness condition never holds for the action of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
on the fine curve graph. Nevertheless, independently in
[
Cho25
,
HL25
]
, they proposed that such set can be covered by finitely many translates of an arbitrarily small piece around the identity, namely
\varepsilon
-coarse
elements (by adopting the terminology from
[
Cho25
]
). The first examples of velcrot subsurfaces are a subsurface
X\subset S
and its images under
\varepsilon
-coarse elements with small
\varepsilon>0
. Another quintessential example for velcrot subsurfaces is nested isotopic subsurfaces. By introducing velcrotness, we can also show a similar finiteness condition to
(P2)
axiom in
[
BBF15
]
, see
Section 3.C
. However, it is worth remarking that velcrotness of subsurfaces is not an equivalent relation and collapsing all velcrot subsurfaces into a “velcrot class” will eventually yield the isotopy class of subsurfaces, which is a consequence of
Corollary 3.6
.

Under the mild condition of velcrotness, we are able to prove a weaker version of Behrstock’s inequality for the fine setting. The key point is that, upon changing a surface into another one velcrot to it, the projection distance does not change much, see
Proposition 3.9
.

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists
M>0
such that the following holds. Let
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
be three essential subsurfaces of
S
pairwise intersecting each other essentially. Assume that each
X_{i}
is either non-sporadic or homeomorphic to a once-bordered torus. Suppose in addition that
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
(
i\neq j
) are either overlapping or isotopic. If
d^{\pi}_{X_{1}}(X_{2},X_{3})>M
, then

d^{\pi}_{X_{2}}(X_{1},X_{3}),d^{\pi}_{X_{3}}(X_{1},X_{2})<M.

Moreover, for a fixed pair of subsurfaces
X,Z\subset S
, the collection of subsurfaces
Y
on which the projection distance
d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)
is large can be covered by finitely many “velcrot” pieces, see
Proposition 3.15
and
Proposition 6.6
.

These two results above serve as a fine variant of axioms
(P1)
and
(P2)
in
[
BBF15
]
, and allow us to proceed the construction of Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara type.

Two main applications of the Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara construction for mapping class groups are extending quasi-morphisms and computing the asymptotic dimension of mapping class groups
[
BBF15
]
. For comparison, we will elaborate on these two topics respectively in
Section 1.A
and
Section 1.B
for
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
.

1.A. Extension of quasi-morphisms

The construction
quasi-morphisms
(for the definition, see
Section 2.B
) has played a prominent role in various topics in mathematics such as geometric group theory, symplectic geometry, and dynamics, ever since
Gromov’s introduction of bounded cohomology in
[
Gro82
]
. Moreover, quasi-morphisms are also closely related to the notion of
stable commutator length
(also see
Section 2.B
), via
Bavard duality
(
Theorem 2.8
). For a more detailed introduction to stable commutator length, refer to
[
Cal09
]
.

When it comes to
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(S)
or
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
for a connected compact oriented surface
S
, Bowden, Hensel and Webb first constructed unbounded quasi-morphisms on these groups in
[
BHW22
]
using the famous Bestvina–Fujiwara machinery
[
BF02
]
. These quasi-morphisms take non-zero values on some homeomorphisms acting loxodromically on the fine curve graph
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
, and they are homeomorphisms isotopic to a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism relative to a finite number of points on
S
(see
[
BHM+22b
, Theorem 1.3]
and
[
GM23
, Theorem A]
). Then these results indicate that some homeomorphisms
\varphi\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
with the closed
\overline{\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)}=S
has positive stable commutator length in
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
. Moreover, in
[
Cho25
]
, Choi also provided some elements
\varphi\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
with
\overline{\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)}\subsetneq S
that has positive stable commutator length in
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
.

However, the quasi-morphisms on
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
from
[
BHW22
]
take zero value on elements acting elliptically on the fine curve graph, in particular, for
\varphi\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
such that
\overline{\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)}
is contained in an essential proper subsurface. So it does not help detecting the positiveness of their stable commutator length on
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
.

As a positive stable commutator length on the group of homeomorphisms often appears as an indicator of complicated dynamics on the manifold, it is reasonable to guess that complicated dynamics on subsurfaces can also be reflected by a positive stable commutator length on
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
. Hence, it is natural to ask the following question:
is there any
\varphi\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
that has positive stable commutator length in
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
, while
\overline{\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)}\subsetneq S
is contained in an essential proper subsurface of
S
?

The answer to this question is positive. The solution we provide in this paper can be viewed as extending to
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
the Bestvina–Fujiwara type quasi-morphisms defined on the subgroup
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(X;\partial X)<\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
, the group of homeomorphisms on a subsurface
X
fixing pointwise the boundary
\partial X
, via the Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara type construction. This will further yield a positive stable commutator length on
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
for elements in
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(X;\partial X)
after the Bavard duality.

For curiosity, we mention that
[
BBF16
]
gives a characterisation of elements with positive stable commutator length in the mapping class group of a compact surface. For non-compact surfaces with non-displaceable compact subsurface, the Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara type construction also yields various quasi-morphisms taking non-zero values on mapping classes that preserves a subsurface, see
[
DD22
,
HQR22
]
.

Extensions of quasi-morphisms usually require certain hyperbolic characteristics. For more information about extending subgroup quasi-morphisms to the entire group, see for example an incomplete list of references on extension of quasi-morphisms:
[
HO13
,
KKM+24
,
Tao25
]
.

Modifying the fine projection complex in
Theorem 1.1
into a
blown-up fine projection complex
and investigating the action of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
on it, we are able to show the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
S
be a connected closed oriented surface of genus at least two and
G
be
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
. Then for every essential subsurface
\Sigma\subset S
that is either non-sporadic or a once-bordered torus, there exists an unbounded (
C^{0}
-continuous) homogeneous quasi-morphism
\varphi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
and an element
g\in G
such that
g(\Sigma)=\Sigma
and
\varphi(g)\neq 0
.

The following theorem can be viewed as a corollary of
Theorem 1.4
, from which it follows combining with
Theorem 2.11
and
Remark 2.12
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
S
be a connected closed oriented surface of genus at least two and
G
be
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
. Then for every essential subsurface
\Sigma\subset S
that is either non-sporadic or a once-bordered torus, there exists an element
g\in G
such that
g(\Sigma)=\Sigma
and
\mathrm{scl}_{G}(g)>0
.

The detailed construction of blown-up fine projection complex is given in
Section 5
. This graph is a
\delta
-hyperbolic (see
Theorem 5.15
) and we show this by using the
Guessing Geodesics Lemma
(see
Proposition 2.1
). This is a powerful tool for proving the Gromov hyperbolicity of a metric space, and the readers can refer to
[
Ham07
,
MS13
,
Bow14
]
for further details. Examples of the application of the Guessing Geodesics Lemma can be also found in
[
HPW15
,
PS17
,
JW25
]
.

For the construction of the blown-up fine projection complex, roughly speaking, we start from a
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
-invariant collection
\mathbf{Y}
of subsurfaces that are pairwise overlapping or isotopic and that are either non-sporadic or homeomorphic to a once-bordered torus, then by taking the union of fine curve graph
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
of any subsurface
X\in\mathbf{Y}
and connecting them in a way that they are quasi-isometrically embedded as subgraphs, we can build an unbounded Gromov hyperbolic graph
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. For velcrot subsurfaces
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
, the images of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)
in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
have bounded Hausdorff distance. Moreover, two elements
f,g\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(X;\partial X)
acting by independent loxodromic isometries on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
will also act by independent loxodromic isometries on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
, see
Proposition 5.16
. The construction we provide herein is also similar to the Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara construction, whereas we need to deal with the technicalities brought forth by velcrotness.

1.B. Asymptotic dimension

An other principal result in
[
BBF15
]
is that they are able to embed the mapping class group of the surface into a finite product of blown-up projection complexes and show that the
asymptotic dimension
of mapping class groups is finite. This notion was first introduced by Gromov in
[
Gro93
]
as an asymptotic invariant for finitely generated groups. The asymptotic dimension can be defined for general metric spaces and is a quasi-isometric invariance, see, for example,
[
BD08
]
; in connection to the coarse setting, see
[
Roe03
]
. Hence, one can talk about the asymptotic dimension of a group with well-defined quasi-isometry type,
e.g.
finitely generated groups and compactly generated groups.

However, one can discuss the asymptotic dimension for topological groups far beyond the locally compact setting. For Polish groups,
i.e.
completely metrisable and separable groups, there is also a well established theory for their large-scale geometry
[
Ros21
]
. Similarly to the locally compact cases, there is a class of Polish groups admitting well-defined quasi-isometry type, namely the
coarsely bounded generated
Polish groups, or
CB generated
Polish groups for abbreviation. These groups can be equipped with a class of maximal left-invariant compatible metrics
[
Ros21
, Theorem 1.2]
, and by maximality, these metrics are quasi-isometric to each other. This implies that CB generated groups have well-defined quasi-isometry type and thus well-defined asymptotic dimension. In particular, Mann and Rosendal showed that for a compact surface
S
, the group
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
equipped with the compact-open topology, which renders the group Polish, is CB generated
[
MR18
]
.

In
[
BBF15
]
, by embedding the mapping class groups quasi-isometrically into a finite product of blown-up projection complexes, which have a finite asymptotic dimension, Bestvina, Bromberg and Fujiwara showed that the asymptotic dimension of mapping class groups are finite. Nevertheless, this strategy fails drastically in the fine setting: unlike mapping class groups, homeomorphism groups
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
have an infinite asymptotic dimension, as separable metric spaces of arbitrarily large asymptotic dimension can be coarsely embedded into
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)

[
MR18
, Proposition 20]
. Moreover, the product of fine blown-up projection complexes that we constructed in this article is also insufficient to encode the large-scale geometry of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
. Indeed, the group
\mathrm{Homeo}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R})
, which is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{Z}
, can be coarsely embedded into
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
via orientation-preserving homeomorphisms on the interior of an essential annulus
[
MR18
, Proposition 40]
, but its action on any fine projection complex that we construct in this paper is elliptic.

Furthermore, it is unknown if the fine blown-up projection complexes have infinite asymptotic dimension. In particular, as fine curve graph can be quasi-isometrically embedded into these complexes (see
Proposition 5.1
), we ask the following question:

Question 1.6
.

Given a surface
S
that is either non-sporadic or a once-bordered torus, does the fine curve graph
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
have infinite asymptotic dimension?

This question is somehow related to the topology of the Gromov boundary of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
, since the asymptotic dimension of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
is bounded below by the topological dimension of compact subsets contained in the Gromov boundary of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
, see
[
BL08
, Proposition 6.2]
for proper cases and
[
Kop25
, Proposition 2.5]
for generalisation to non-proper situations. Therefore, one approach towards
Question 1.6
is to see if one can embed compact sets of arbitrarily large dimension into the Gromov boundary. One example of such a space is infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space
\ell^{2}
. More specifically, as
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
acts continuously and minimally on the Gromov boundary of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)

[
LT25
, Proposition 6.4]
and
\mathrm{Homeo}(S)
is an
\ell^{2}
-manifold
[
LR97
, Théorème 7.1.3]
,
i.e.
locally homeomorphic to
\ell^{2}
, it is also natural to ask:

Question 1.7
.

Given a surface
S
that is either non-sporadic or a once-bordered torus, is the Gromov boundary of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
an
\ell^{2}
-manifold?

2
Preliminaries

This section aims at giving the definition of some basic notions and reviewing several known results on Gromov hyperbolic space, quasi-morphisms and stable commutator length, Bestvina–Fujiwara machinery, as well as fine curve graphs and surface homeomorphisms.

2.A. Gromov hyperbolicity

Some classical references for Gromov hyperbolicity are
[
BH99
,
DSU17
]
. Here we will briefly recall the definition of a geodesic metric space satisfying this condition, and provide several properties of the isometries on this space.

Let
(X,d)
be a geodesic metric space. We denote by
[x,y]
any geodesic segment between two points
x,y\in X
. We say that
X
is
\delta
-hyperbolic
for some
\delta\geq 0
if for any
x,y,z\in X
, we have

\mathcal{N}_{\delta}([x,y]\cup[y,z])\supset[x,z]\,,

where
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(A)
stands for the
\delta
-neighbourhood of the subset
A\subset X

We will now introduce a remarkable and very powerful criterion for proving Gromov hyperbolicity, namely the
Guessing Geodesics Lemma
. This was originally discovered by Masur and Schleimer, with an alternative proof by Bowditch, see
[
MS13
,
Bow14
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Guessing geodesics lemma)
.

Let
(\Gamma,d_{\Gamma})
be a graph. If there exists some constant
\lambda>0
such that for any
x,y\in\Gamma
, we can find a connected subgraph
\mathcal{L}(x,y)\subset\Gamma
containing
x,y
and satisfying the following conditions:

(G1)

for any
x,y,z\in\Gamma
,
\mathcal{L}(y,z)
is contained in the
\lambda
-neighbourhood of
\mathcal{L}(x,y)\cup\mathcal{L}(y,z)
;

(G2)

for any
x,y\in\Gamma
with
d_{\Gamma}(x,y)\leq 1
, the diameter of
\mathcal{L}(x,y)
in
\Gamma
is at most
\lambda
;

Then
\Gamma
is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta>0
depending only on
\lambda
.

Remark 2.2
.

The connected subgraph
\mathcal{L}(x,y)
associated to
x,y\in\Gamma
is called the
guessing geodesic
between
x
and
y
, and is known to have uniformly bounded Hausdorff distance from a geodesic between
x
and
y
(bounded in terms of
\lambda
). In fact,
\delta
can be chosen as any number greater than or equal to
(3m-10\lambda)/2
, where
m
is any positive real number satisfying
2\lambda(6+\log 2(m+2))\leq m
.

Although the above version for graphs is sufficient for our purpose, we remark for curiosity that there is a more general version of this result in
[
Ham07
, Proposition 3.5]
, which is stated for geodesic metric spaces
X
with a continuous path
\eta_{xy}\colon[0,1]\to X
connecting any pair
x,y\in X
and satisfying similar conditions as above.

Recall that an
isometry

f\colon X\to X
is a map such that
d_{X}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)=d_{X}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
. We define its
asymptotic translation length
as

|f|_{X}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}d_{X}\big(f^{n}(x),x\big).

For a
\delta
-hyperbolic space, we have the following classification of isometries according to their asymptotic translation length
[
Gro87
, §8]
:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic space. An isometry
f\colon X\to X
is called

(1)

elliptic
, if
f
has bounded orbits;

(2)

parabolic
, if
|f|_{X}=0
and has no bounded orbit;

(3)

loxodromic
, or also
hyperbolic
, if
|f|_{X}>0
.

Recall that a map
f\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
between two metric spaces is a
(\lambda,k)
-quasi-isometric embedding
if there exist
\lambda\geq 1
and
k>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}d_{X}(x,y)-k\leq d_{Y}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\leq\lambda d_{X}(x,y)+k

(2.1)

for every
x,y\in X
. In particular, we say that the map
f
is
\lambda
-bi-Lipschitz
if
k=0
in (
2.1
). If in addition, the map
f
is
essentially surjective
,
i.e.
there exists
C>0
such that
d_{Y}\big(y,f(X)\big)<C<\infty
for all
y\in Y
, then
f
is a quasi-isometry and
X
and
Y
are said to be quasi-isometric. We remark that being quasi-isometric is an equivalence relation. A
quasi-geodesic
in a metric space
X
is a quasi-isometric embedding of a real interval into
X
. We remark that for geodesic metric spaces, Gromov hyperbolicity is a quasi-isometry invariant, see for example
[
BH99
, Theorem III.H.1.9]
.

If
X
is a
\delta
-hyperbolic space and
g
is a loxodromic isometry of
X
, then it admits a bi-infinite quasi-geodesic that is
g
-invariant, which we will later refer to it as a
quasi-axis
of
g
. It is always convenient to blur the distinction between quasi-axis and its image. By Morse Lemma, for any parameter
(K,L)
, there exists a constant
B\coloneqq B(K,L,\delta)
such that any two
(K,L)
-quasi-axis of
g
stay within the
B
-neighbourhood of each other; see, for example,
[
BH99
, Theorem III.H.1.7]
.

2.B. Quasi-morphisms and stable commutator length

Let us first review the following definition:

Definition 2.4

(Quasi-morphism)
.

A
quasi-morphism
on a group
G
is a map
\mu:G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that there is a least constant
D(\mu)\geq 0
, depending only on
\mu
, called the
defect
of
\mu
, with the property that

|\mu(gh)-\mu(g)-\mu(h)|\leq D(\mu),\text{ for all }g,h\in G.

The set of all quasi-morphisms on a fixed group
G
is easily seen to be a (real) vector space; we denote this vector space by
\widehat{Q}(G)
. Respectively by
C_{b}^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})
and
\operatorname{Hom}(G;\mathbb{R})=H^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})
we denote the subspaces of
\widehat{Q}(G)
consisting of (real-valued) bounded functions and of homomorphisms.

A quasi-morphism
\mu
is said to be
homogeneous
if
\mu(g^{n})=n\mu(g)
for any
g\in G
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We denote by
Q(G)
the subspace of
\widehat{Q}(G)
consisting of homogeneous quasi-morphisms. For any quasi-morphism
\mu\in\widehat{Q}(G)
, one can obtain a homogeneous quasi-morphism
\widetilde{\mu}\in Q(G)
, called the
homogenisation
of
\mu
, as follows:

\widetilde{\mu}(g)\coloneqq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mu(g^{n}).

This limit always exists for each element
g
of
G
since the sequence
\big(\mu(g^{n})\big)_{n}
is sub-additive with bounded error. Moreover, for any
g\in G
, we have
|\widetilde{\mu}(g)-\mu(g)|\leq D(\mu)
, see for example
[
Bav91
]
. In other words, a quasi-morphism
\mu
is (uniquely) written as the sum of a homogeneous quasi-morphism
\widetilde{\mu}
and a bounded function. By definition, bounded functions on groups are quasi-morphisms. We can then identify the quotient space
\widehat{Q}(G)/C_{b}^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})
with
Q(G)
.

Note that
C_{b}^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})\cap\operatorname{Hom}(G;\mathbb{R})=0
. We are then interested in the quotient spaces

Q(G)=\widehat{Q}(G)/C_{b}^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})

and

\widetilde{Q}(G)=\widehat{Q}(G)/(C_{b}^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})+\operatorname{Hom}(G;\mathbb{R}))\simeq Q(G)/H^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})

as any homogeneous quasi-morphism
\mu
is invariant under conjugations,
i.e.
for all
a,b\in G
,

\mu(aba^{-1})=\mu(b)\,.

We now introduce an algebraic dual of the quasi-morphism, which is called the
stable commutator length
that we will introduce in the following.

Definition 2.5

(Commutator length)
.

A
commutator
is an element in
G
that can be expressed in the form
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
for some
a,b\in G
. The subgroup generated by commutators is called the
commutator subgroup
and is denoted by
[G,G]
. The
commutator length
of an element
g\in G
, denoted by
\operatorname{cl}_{G}(g)
, is defined to be the word length of
g
with respect to the set of all commutators:

\operatorname{cl}_{G}(g)\coloneqq\inf\Big\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\mid g=\prod_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},b_{i}];a_{i},b_{i}\in G\Big\}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\},

where we allow
\operatorname{cl}_{G}(g)=\infty
. Note that
\operatorname{cl}_{G}(g)<\infty
if and only if
g\in[G,G]
.

Definition 2.6

(Stable commutator length)
.

For
g\in[G,G]
, the
stable commutator length
of
g
, denoted by
\operatorname{scl}_{G}(g)
is the following limit:

\operatorname{scl}_{G}(g)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cl}_{G}(g^{n})}{n}.

(2.2)

Remark 2.7
.

For each fixed
g\in[G,G]
, the function
n\mapsto\operatorname{cl}_{G}(g^{n})
is non-negative and sub-additive. Hence, the limit in (
2.2
) exists. However, we can further define the stable commutator length for general elements in
G
. If
g\notin[G,G]
but admits a power
g^{k}\in[G,G]
for some
k>0
, then we define
\operatorname{scl}_{G}(g)\coloneqq\operatorname{scl}_{G}(g^{k})/k
, and by convention define
\operatorname{scl}_{G}(g)=\infty
if no positive power of
g
lies in
[G,G]
.

The following result is called the
Bavard duality
and indicates how stable commutator length is related to quasi-morphisms. See, for example,
[
Bav91
]
or
[
Cal09
, Theorem 2.70]
for detailed proof.

Theorem 2.8

(Bavard Duality)
.

Let
G
be a group. Then for any
g\in[G,G]
, we have the following equality:

\operatorname{scl}_{G}(g)=\sup_{\varphi\in\widetilde{Q}(G)}\frac{|\varphi(g)|}{2D(\varphi)}\ .

2.C. Bestvina–Fujiwara quasi-morphisms

In the following, we will introduce the famous Bestvina–Fujiwara machinery from
[
BF02
]
. For our convenience, we will restate some definitions and results from
[
BF02
]
. However, readers should note that the terminology that we use here is different from the original work
[
BF02
]
.

Recall that given a loxodromic element
g
acting on a
\delta
-hyperbolic space
X
, there is a constant
B\coloneqq B(K,L,\delta)
such that any two
(K,L)
-quasi-axis of
g
stay within the
B
-neighbourhood of each other.

Definition 2.9

(Independent loxodromics)
.

Let
X
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic graph and
G
be a group acting on it by isometries. Let us consider two loxodromic elements
f,g\in G
with respective quasi-axes
A_{f},A_{g}
. We say that they are
independent
, denoted by
f\nsim g
, if for any
B\geq B(K,L,\delta)
, there exists a segment
J\subset A_{g}
such that for any
h\in G
, the translate
hJ
is not contained in the
B
-neighbourhood of
A_{f}
. Otherwise, we say that they are dependent and we write
f\sim g
.

We remark that if
f\nsim g
, then by taking
h=\mathrm{Id}
, we can conclude that
A_{f}
and
A_{g}
are not within a finite Hausdorff distance. This implies that the existence of two independent loxodromic elements ensures that the
G
-action on
X
is
of general type
(or also
non-elementary
in literature); see, for example,
[
CCMT15
]
.

Theorem 2.10

(Theorem 1,
[
BF02
]
)
.

Suppose that
G
acts on a
\delta
-hyperbolic graph by isometries and the action is of general type. Suppose also that there exist independent loxodromic elements
f\nsim g
. Then the space
\widetilde{Q}(G)
is infinite-dimensional.

All the quasi-morphisms in
Theorem 2.10
can be constructed explicitly. In the remanent of this subsection, we will briefly recall their construction. The model case of the free group is due to Brooks
[
Bro80
]
.

Let
w
be a finite (oriented) path in
X
. Let
|w|
denote the length of
w
. For
g\in G
, we denote by the composition
g\circ w
a copy of
w
by
g
-translation. It is clear that
|g\circ w|=|w|
. Let
\alpha
be a finite path. We define

|\alpha|_{w}=\{\text{the maximal number of non-overlapping copies of $w$ in $\alpha$}\}.

Suppose that
x,y\in X
are two vertices and that
R
is an integer with
0<W<|w|
. We define the integer

c_{w,W}(x,y)=d(x,y)-\inf_{\alpha}(|\alpha|-W|\alpha|_{w}),

where
\alpha
ranges over all paths from
x
to
y
. Fixing a base point
x_{0}\in X
, we define
h_{w}\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

h_{w}(g)=c_{w,W}(x_{0},g(x_{0}))-c_{w^{-1},W}(x_{0},g(x_{0})),

which is a quasi-morphism defined on
G
but not necessarily homogeneous.

The following theorem provides a sufficient condition for an element
g\in G
to have positive stable commutator length.

Theorem 2.11

(Proposition 5,
[
BF02
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic space and
G
be a group acting on
X
by isometries. If
f\in G
is a loxodromic element such that
f\nsim f^{-1}
, then there is a homogeneous quasi-morphism that is unbounded on the group generated by
f
, and
a fortiori
taking non-zero values on
f
. In particular,
f
has positive stable commutator length.

Remark 2.12
.

We say that a loxodromic element
f\in G
is
quasi-invertible
if
f\sim f^{-1}
. From
[
BF02
, Proposition 2]
, we can see that if
G
acts on a Gromov hyperbolic graph
X
by isometries and the action is of general type with two independent loxodromic elements
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, then there are infinitely many loxodromic elements in the subgroup of
\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle<G
that are not quasi-invertible. In particular, the non-quasi-invertible element
f
in
Theorem 2.11
can be chosen in
\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle
.

2.D. Fine curve graph

Let
S=S_{g,b}
be a connected closed oriented surface of finite type of genus
g\geq 2
, with
b
boundary components. Let
\xi(S)=3g+b-3
be the complexity of
S
. We denote by
\partial S
the boundary of
S
. We say that a surface is
non-sporadic
if
\xi(S)\geq 2
.

An
essential simple closed curve

\gamma
on
S
is a proper
C^{0}
-embedding of the circle,
\gamma:S^{1}\hookrightarrow S
, with the property that
\gamma
does not bound a disc nor a boundary component. In the sequel, for most of the time, we will identify a curve with its image.

In
[
BHW22
]
, Bowden, Hensel and Webb introduce an analogue to curve graph for the group of homeomorphisms on
S
, namely, the
fine curve graph
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
. It is a graph whose vertices correspond to essential simple closed curves on
S
and two vertices in this graph are connected by an edge if the corresponding curves are disjoint in
S
. Endowing
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
with the simplicial distance
d^{\dagger}
, there exists a
\delta>0
, independent of the complexity of the surface, such that the fine curve graph
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
is
\delta
-hyperbolic
[
BHW22
, Theorem 3.8]
. The idea is to approximate the fine curve graph by the
surviving curve graph
of finitely punctured surfaces, and the uniformity of the hyperbolic constant
\delta
find its root in the uniform hyperbolicity of the non-separating curve graphs
[
Ras20
]
.

To be more precise, for a finite subset
P\subset S
, let us denote by
\mathcal{C}^{s}(S\setminus P)
the surviving curve graph on
S\setminus P
. The vertices of this graph are the isotopy classes of essential simple closed curves on
S\setminus P
that remain essential on
S
and we connect two vertices if the corresponding two curves admit disjoint representatives. Now, we have the following:

Lemma 2.13

(Lemma 3.4,
[
BHW22
]
)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
be two transverse curves. Then for a finite subset
P\subset S
such that
\alpha,\beta
are in minimal position on
S\setminus P
, we have

d^{\dagger}(\alpha,\beta)=d_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(S\setminus P)}([\alpha]_{S\setminus P},[\beta]_{S\setminus P}).

Remark 2.14
.

Although
[
BHW22
, Lemma 3.4]
is originally stated for non-sporadic closed surfaces, the arguments therein also hold for torus and surfaces with boundary components.

Similarly to the classical case of curve graphs, on the fine curve graph, the distance between two transverse curves is also controlled by their intersection number.

Proposition 2.15

(Proposition 3.8,
[
LT25
]
)
.

Let
S
be above and let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
be two transverse curves on
S
. Then
d^{\dagger}(\alpha,\beta)\leq 2|\alpha\cap\beta|+1
.

Let
Y
be an essential non-sporadic proper subsurface of
S
,
i.e.

\partial Y
is a finite collection of essential curves on
S
. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
be a simple closed curve on
S
. We say that
\alpha
intersects
Y

essentially
if at least one connected component of
\alpha\cap Y
is an essential arc or curve in
Y
,
i.e.
, not isotopically trivial in
Y
. It is worth remarking that a general curve
\alpha
may not intersect
\partial Y
transversely and this notion of essential intersection may not be invariant under isotopy.

The subsurface projection of a curve on
S
that is essentially intersecting
Y
can be defined as follows:

Definition 2.16

(Subsurface projection)
.

Suppose that
S
and
Y
are given as above. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)
be the fine curve graph for
Y
and let
\mathcal{P}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)\right)
be its power set. We define a map
\pi_{Y}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)\to\mathcal{P}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)\right)
in the following way: for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
, the image
\pi_{Y}(\alpha)
is defined as

•

\{\alpha\}
if
\alpha\subset Y
;

•

\emptyset
if
\alpha
does not intersect
Y
essentially;

•

all essential curves in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)
that is disjoint from one essential subarc of
\alpha\cap Y
, if
a\not\subset Y
but
\alpha
intersects
Y
essentially.

The map
\pi_{Y}
is called the
subsurface projection
of
\alpha
on
Y
.

Moreover, this projection is in fact coarsely well-defined in the following sense:

Proposition 2.17

(Proposition 4.2,
[
LT25
]
)
.

With the simplicial distance
\big(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y),d^{\dagger}_{Y}\big)
, for any curve
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
intersecting
Y
essentially, the diameter of the set
\pi_{Y}(\alpha)
is bounded in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)
, i.e.,
\mathrm{diam}_{d^{\dagger}_{Y}}(\pi_{Y}(\alpha))\leq 12.

Moreover, we have the following distance estimation:

Proposition 2.18

(Proposition 4.3,
[
LT25
]
)
.

Let
S
,
Y
,
d^{\dagger}_{Y}
, and
\pi_{Y}
be as defined above. For any two transverse curves
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
that intersect the subsurface
Y
essentially. Suppose in addition that there are essential arcs
\alpha^{\prime}\subset\alpha\cap Y
and
\beta^{\prime}\subset\beta\cap Y
such that
|\alpha^{\prime}\cap\beta^{\prime}|=p\geq 0
. Then for any
\gamma_{\alpha}\in\pi_{Y}(\alpha)
and
\gamma_{\beta}\in\pi_{Y}(\beta)
, the following inequality holds:

d_{Y}^{\dagger}\big(\gamma_{\alpha},\gamma_{\beta}\big)\leq 8p+12.

Finally, similar to the case of curve graphs, we have the following version of the bounded geodesic image theorem for fine curve graphs:

Theorem 2.19

(Theorem 1.3,
[
LT25
]
)
.

Given a surface
S
with
g(S)\geq 2
, there exists a constant
M>0
such that whenever
Y
is an essential subsurface that is either non-sporadic or homeomorphic to a once-bordered torus, given any
g=(\gamma_{i})
geodesic path in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
such that
\gamma_{i}
intersects
Y
essentially for all
i
, then
\mathrm{diam}_{d_{Y}^{\dagger}}\big(\pi_{Y}(g)\big)\leq M
.

Remark 2.20
.

The proof of
Theorem 2.19
also applies to subsurfaces that are homeomorphic to a once-bordered torus, as the principal ingredients of the proof are the uniform hyperbolicity of the surviving curve graph (see for example
[
BHW22
, §5.2]
) and the upper bound of distance in the fine curve graph of the subsurface by the intersection numbers, which holds in once-bordered tori, see
Section 6
.

3
Surgery on surface

The Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara construction of projection complex requires us to provide a collection
\mathbf{Y}
of metric spaces with coarsely well-defined projection to each other so that if the projections of
Y,Z
to
X
are far away, then the projections of
X,Y
to
Z
, as well as the projections of
X,Z
to
Y
, are close, and that for fixed
Y,Z
, there are only finitely many
X
to which the projections of
Y,Z
are far away, see
[
BBF15
]
. However, for the fine curve graph of essential subsurfaces (not the isotopy classes of them), these conditions do not hold. This section aims at introducing the notion of velcrot subsurfaces to establish weaker conditions that allow us to run a similar machinery.

3.A. Velcrot subsurfaces

The following notion is inspired by the metric WPD properties introduced in
[
Cho25
,
HL25
]
, but the definition of finiteness condition in their metric WPD properties relies on some
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-distance. Here, we offer a topological analogue for subsurfaces.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X,Y\subset S
be two non-sporadic essential subsurfaces. We say that
X
and
Y
are
velcrot
if
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)
is of infinite diameter in both
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)
.

Remark 3.2
.

In particular,
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)
is a subset of both
\pi_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y))
and
\pi_{Y}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X))
. Hence, if
X
and
Y
are velcrot, then both
\pi_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y))
and
\pi_{Y}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X))
are unbounded.

This definition is purely metric geometric. It might be useful for settings other than subsurfaces on a surface. However, under the setting of essential subsurfaces and their fine curve graphs, the definition of velcrotness can also be given in a purely topological way, as we will explain below.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
X,X^{\prime}\subset S
are velcrot, then
\partial X
does not intersect
X^{\prime}
essentially and
vice versa
.

Proof.

We may assume for contradiction that
\partial X
intersect
X^{\prime}
essentially, then for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime})
, the essential arc
\partial X\cap X^{\prime}
and the curve
\alpha
are disjoint, so by
Proposition 2.18
, we have
d^{\dagger}_{X^{\prime}}\big(\alpha,\beta\big)\leq 12
for any
\beta\in\pi_{X^{\prime}}(\partial X)
. But
Proposition 2.17
and
Proposition 2.18
imply that the diameter of
\pi_{X^{\prime}}(\partial X)
is bounded in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime})
, which forces that
\alpha
is contained in a bounded subset in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime})
, contradicting the assumption that
X,X^{\prime}
are velcrot.
∎

The following lemma gives a quintessential example for velcrot subsurfaces:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X,Z
be two essential non-sporadic subsurfaces. Suppose in addition that
Z
is contained in
X
and is homotopic to
X
. Then
X,Z
are velcrot.

Proof.

As
Z\subset X
,
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)=\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)
is unbounded in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)
. Conversely, for any transverse
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)\subset\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
, let
P
be a finite subset of
Z
such that
\alpha
and
\beta
are in minimal position on
X\setminus P
. By
Lemma 2.13
, we have

d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)}(\alpha,\beta)=d_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(Z\setminus P)}([\alpha]_{Z\setminus P},[\beta]_{Z\setminus P})=d_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(X\setminus P)}([\alpha]_{X\setminus P},[\beta]_{X\setminus P})=d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)}(\alpha,\beta),

where we have used a natural identification between
\mathcal{C}^{s}(X\setminus P)
and
\mathcal{C}^{s}(Z\setminus P)
. Since two transverse curves in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)
can have an arbitrarily large distance, the inclusion
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
is unbounded.
∎

However, we remark that velcrotness is not an equivalence relation since it is not transitive. This can be easily seen from the following result, which gives a topological characterisation for velcrotness:

Proposition 3.5
.

Two non-sporadic essential subsurfaces
X,X^{\prime}\subset S
are velcrot if and only if there exists an essential subsurface
Z\subset X\cap X^{\prime}
such that
Z
is homotopic to both
X
and
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For the “if” part, by virtue of
Lemma 3.4
,
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)\subset\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime})
is unbounded in both
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime})
. Hence,
X
and
X^{\prime}
are velcrot by definition.

For the “only if” part,
Lemma 3.3
implies that the boundaries of
\partial X
and
\partial X^{\prime}
only bound bigons or annuli. More specifically, this implies that the boundaries of a small regular neighbourhood of
\partial X\cup\partial X^{\prime}
in
X
are peripheral curves in
X
. These peripheral curves then bound a subsurface
Z\subset X
homotopic to
X
. But since there are curves in
X\cap X^{\prime}
that are essential in both, we can conclude that
Z\subset X\cap X^{\prime}
. However, we also know that
\partial Z\subset X^{\prime}
are also peripheral, otherwise they would yield a well-defined projection of
\partial X
in
X^{\prime}
, contradicting
Lemma 3.3
. Hence,
Z
is also homotopic to
X^{\prime}
.
∎

Since homotopy among subsurfaces is an equivalent relation, we can soon conclude the following results:

Corollary 3.6
.

Two non-sporadic velcrot essential subsurfaces are homotopic.

\square

Corollary 3.7
.

Given two homotopic non-sporadic essential subsurfaces, they are velcrot if and only if the boundaries of one surface do not project to an essential arc on the other surface.

\square

Remark 3.8
.

We first remark that the homotopy condition cannot be removed, since, for example, two disjoint subsurfaces are never velcrot. In the sequel, we will often use
Corollary 3.7
implicitly. Moreover, as a consequence, a small perturbation on the boundary of an essential subsurface within a small regular neighbourhood of it will yield a new surface that is velcrot to the original one. This fact will also be frequently used in the following without mentioning explicitly.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
and for any essential non-sporadic subsurface
X\subset S
, we define the
projection distance
by

d^{\pi}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)}\big(\pi_{X}(\alpha)\cup\pi_{X}(\beta)\big)>0

(3.1)

if both
\alpha,\beta
intersect
X
essentially; otherwise, we set
d^{\pi}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\infty
whenever
\alpha
or
\beta
do not intersect
X
essentially. Moreover, for any triple of essential non-sporadic subsurfaces
X,Y,Z\subset S
, we define

d^{\pi}_{X}(Y,Z)\coloneqq\inf\left\{d^{\pi}_{X}(\alpha,\beta):\alpha\subset\partial Y,\beta\subset\partial Z\right\}.

(3.2)

It is not difficult to see that with the above definition, we then have a triangle inequality

d^{\pi}_{X}(Y,Z)\leq d^{\pi}_{X}(Y,Z^{\prime})+d^{\pi}_{X}(Z^{\prime},Z)

(3.3)

whenever
\partial Y,\partial Z,\partial Z^{\prime}
all intersect
X
essentially.

Proposition 3.9
.

There exists
M>0
that verifies the following. Let
X,X^{\prime}
be two velcrot non-sporadic essential subsurfaces. Suppose that
x,z\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
intersect
X,X^{\prime}
essentially, then we have

|d^{\pi}_{X}(x,z)-d^{\pi}_{X^{\prime}}(x,z)|<M.

Proof.

Note that
x,z
also intersect
Z
essentially for
Z\subset X\cap X^{\prime}
from
Proposition 3.5
. Now, let
\alpha\in\pi_{Z}(x)
and
\beta\in\pi_{Z}(z)
. A geodesic path
(x_{i})_{i}
in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)
between
\alpha
and
\beta
will also yield a path in both
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime})
, a finite subset
P\subset Z
such that
x_{i}
’s are in minimal position on
Z
, and thus also on
X,X^{\prime}
. Hence, by
Lemma 2.13
, we have

d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Z)}(\alpha,\beta)=d_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(Z\setminus P)}([\alpha]_{Z\setminus P},[\beta]_{Z\setminus P})=d_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(X\setminus P)}([\alpha]_{X\setminus P},[\beta]_{X\setminus P})=d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)}(\alpha,\beta),

where
\mathcal{C}^{s}(Z\setminus P)
is the surviving curve graph for
Z\setminus P
, is defined up to isotopy, and hence is naturally identified with
\mathcal{C}^{s}(X\setminus P)
. The same also holds for
X^{\prime}
. Moreover, we remark that with the natural inclusion map we have
\pi_{Z}(x)\subset\pi_{X}(x),\pi_{X^{\prime}}(x)
and
\pi_{Z}(z)\subset\pi_{X}(z),\pi_{X^{\prime}}(z)
. The desired result follows from the triangle inequality and
Proposition 2.17
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

We remark that the condition of
x,z\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
intersecting both
X,X^{\prime}
essentially cannot be dropped. Indeed, there are situations where
X\subset X^{\prime}
and
x\cap X
contains essential arcs in
X
while
x\cap X^{\prime}
does not admit any essential subarc in
X^{\prime}
. Conversely, if

3.B. Fine Behrstock inequality

We offer a combinatorial proof of Behrstock inequality in the fine curve graph setup following the proof of Leininger (see also

Recall that two subsurfaces in

Proof.

Assume first that

and

and

3.C. Finiteness condition

Unlike the usual setting in
[
BBF15
]
, without considering isotopy classes, there is generally no finiteness condition for essential subsurfaces. However, with the notion of velcrot subsurfaces, we can show that the for fixed subsurfaces
Y,Z
, one can choose finitely many subsurfaces such that each subsurface

with

is velcrot to one of them. This will serve as a weaker finiteness condition to run the Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara type construction.

Lemma 3.14

Let

are essential.

Proof.

Let

Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Z)\neq\emptyset
, then

\mathcal{H}(X,Z)\subset\mathcal{H}(X,Y)\cap\mathcal{H}(Y,Z).

Indeed, the distance
d_{W}(X,Z)
is the infimum of
d^{\pi}_{W}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
for
(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{H}(X,Z)
, while
d_{W}(X,Y)
and
d_{W}(Y,Z)
are infimum taken over
\mathcal{H}(X,Y)
and
\mathcal{H}(Y,Z)
respectively, and the inclusion will give us the desired inequality. Now, let us verify it. If
(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{H}(X,Z)
, then by
Proposition 4.4
, we have

d_{Y}^{\pi}(X,Z)-d^{\pi}_{Y}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})<2\theta.

But
d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)\geq d_{Y}(X,Z)>\Theta>4\theta
, then
d^{\pi}_{Y}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})>2\theta
. In any case (despite the velcrotness between the surfaces), this indicates
(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{H}(X,Y)\cap\mathcal{H}(Y,Z)
.

For
(VIII)
, since
(Y_{i})
’s are already pairwise non-velcrot and none of them is velcrot to either
X
or
Z
, the same arguments in the proof of
[
BBF15
, Theorem 3.3]
also apply here.

For
(IX)
, if
X_{0},X_{1}
are velcrot, then the desired result is nothing more than the coarse equality
(II)
; otherwise, they are pairwise non-velcrot, and the discussion goes
verbatim
as in
[
BBF15
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Finally, we take
\Theta>0
such that it bounds
three times
of all the differences appear in the coarse relation in
(I)
–
(IX)
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

More precisely, the order in
(VIII)
is defined as below: fix
\theta^{\prime}>4\theta>0
, for two non-velcrot
W,Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Z)
, we say that
W<Y
if one, and hence all, of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(i)

d_{W}(X,Y)>\theta^{\prime}\,
;

(ii)

d_{Y}(X,W)\leq\theta^{\prime}\,
;

(iii)

d_{Y}(W,Z)>\theta^{\prime}\,
;

(iv)

d_{W}(Y,Z)\leq\theta^{\prime}\,
.

Moreover, we say that
W\leq Y
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Z)
if either
W<Y
or else
W,Y
are velcrot.

4.B. Quasi-tree

Let
\Theta
still be the constant in
Theorem 4.5
. For
K\geq 2\Theta
, let us define the following graph:

Definition 4.7
.

The
fine projection complex

\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is a graph where the vertices are elements in
\mathbf{Y}
and an edge is connected between
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
if
\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)=\emptyset
. Denote the simplicial distance on
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
by
d_{\mathcal{P}}
.

In the following, we will discuss the metric geometric properties of
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
.

For a subset
U\subset\mathbf{Y}
, we define
|U|_{\operatorname{vel}}
to be the minimal
k\geq 0
needed so that there exists
(Y_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\subset U
such that any
Y\in U
is velcrot to some
Y_{i}
, with the convention
|\emptyset|_{\operatorname{vel}}=0
. By
(VI)
, we see that
|\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)|_{\operatorname{vel}}<\infty
for any
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

For any
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
, we have
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X,Z)\leq|\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)|_{\operatorname{vel}}+1
. In particular, the graph
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is connected.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)=\emptyset
, then they are connected in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and

d_{\mathcal{P}}(X,Z)=|\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)|_{\operatorname{vel}}+1=1.

Suppose now that
\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists
(Y_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\subset\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)
pairwise non-velcrot with
k=|\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)|_{\operatorname{vel}}
such that any
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)
is velcrot to one of
Y_{i}
’s. Moreover, as
d_{Y_{i}}(X,Z)>0
,
Y_{i}
’s are not velcrot to
X
or
Z
. By
(VIII)
, we may assume that
Y_{i}<Y_{j}
whenever
i<j
.

We claim that
X,Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k},Z
is a path connecting
X
to
Z
in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. We first show that
\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Y_{1})=\emptyset
. Suppose for contradiction that
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Y_{1})\neq\emptyset
. Then
Y
is not velcrot to
Y_{1}
. By monotonicity
(VII)
, we have
d_{Y}(X,Z)\geq d_{Y}(X,Y_{i})>K
, which implies that
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)
and is velcrot to some
Y_{i}
. However, by
(III)
,
d_{Y}(X,Y_{1})\sim_{\theta}d_{Y_{i}}(X,Y_{1})>K>\theta^{\prime}
, where
\theta^{\prime}
is as in
Remark 4.6
, showing that
Y_{i}<Y_{1}
, a contradiction. For the adjacency between
Y_{i}
and
Y_{i+1}
, as well as between
Y_{k}
and
Z
, the discussion remains very similar, if it is not exactly the same.
∎

For two vertices in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
,
Proposition 4.8
gives an upper bound for the distance between them, but does not produce a lower bound estimate. The following notion of
guard
is introduced to give a coarse positioning for geodesic paths in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. Roughly speaking, a guard
W
for
Y
is a vertex such that its projection is always close to the projection of
Y
from every viewpoint.

Definition 4.9
.

We say that
W\in\mathbf{Y}
is a
guard
for
Y
if for every vertex
X\in\mathbf{Y}
with
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Y)
and every
Z\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Y)\subset\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Y)
, one has
Z\leq W
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Y)
.

The following lemma offers a sufficient condition for a vertex
W
to be a guard for another vertex
Y
:

Lemma 4.10
.

For
K>
sufficiently large and vertices
X,Y,Z
and
W
in
\mathbf{Y}
, if
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Y)
,
Z\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Y)
and
W<Z
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Y)
, then
Z\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(W,Y)
.

In particular, if
\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(W,Y)=\emptyset
then
W
is a guard for
Y
.

Proof.

With the assumption above, we can deduce that
Z,W
and
X,Y
are not velcrot. Then, appealing to
(VIII)
with
W<Z<Y
with respect to the order in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Y)\cup\{X,Y\}
, we have

d_{Z}(W,Y)\succ_{\theta}d_{Z}(X,Y)>K,

which, by making
K
sufficiently large with respect to
\theta
, implies
d_{Z}(W,Y)>K/2
.
∎

The following lemma gives an example of guard:

Lemma 4.11
.

The following holds for sufficiently large
K>0
. Let
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
be non-velcrot. Suppose that
(Y_{i})\subset\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X,Z)
are pairwise non-velcrot and that each
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X,Z)
is velcrot to one of
Y_{i}
’s. Then the minimal element in
(Y_{i})
is a guard for
X
, while the maximal element is a guard for
Z
.

Proof.

Due to symmetry, we will only demonstrate the reason why the maximal element in
(Y_{i})
is a guard for
Z
. Assume that
Y_{n}
is the maximal element. Appealing to
Lemma 4.10
, it suffices to show that
\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(Y_{n},Z)=\emptyset
. Suppose for contradiction that
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(Y_{n},Z)
. If
W
is not velcrot to
X
, then for
K>0
sufficiently large, since
Y_{n}\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X,Z)\subset\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Z)
, the monotonicity
(VII)
indicates that
K/2\leq d_{W}(Y_{n},Z)\leq d_{W}(X,Z)
, forcing
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(Y_{n},Z)
and
W\leq Y_{n}
, which by
Remark 4.6
means that
d_{W}(Y_{n},Z)\leq\theta^{\prime}<K/2
, contradiction. If
W
and
X
are velcrot, then by the inequality on triples
(V)
,
d_{W}(Y_{n},Z)>K/2
will imply that
d_{Y_{n}}(W,Z)\sim_{\theta}d_{Y_{n}}(X,Z)\sim_{\theta}0
, contradicting
Y_{n}\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X,Z)
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

The similar argument will yield that if
W
is a guard for
Y
, then
\mathbf{Y}_{K}(W,Y)=\emptyset
and thus
d_{\mathcal{P}}(W,Y)=1
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1}\in\mathbf{Y}
be adjacent in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. Suppose that
W\in\mathbf{Y}
is such that
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{0},W)\geq 3
. Then

d_{W}(X_{0},X_{1})\sim_{\theta}0

and

d_{W}(X_{0},Z)\sim_{\theta}d_{W}(X_{1},Z)

for all
Z\in\mathbf{Y}
.

Proof.

First, it is clear that
W
is not velcrot to either
X_{0}
or
X_{1}
.

If
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are velcrot, then
d_{W}(X_{0},X_{1})=0
and
d_{W}(X_{0},Z)\sim_{\theta}d_{W}(X_{1},Z)
is implied by
Lemma 3.11
.

Assume now that
X_{0},X_{1}
are not velcrot. Since
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{0},W)\geq 2
, there exists
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X_{0},W)
. If
Y,X_{1}
are velcrot, then by
(III)
, we have

d_{X_{1}}(X_{0},W)\sim_{\theta}d_{Y}(X_{0},W)>K,

which, for sufficiently large
K>\Theta
, implies
d_{X_{1}}(X_{0},W)>\Theta
. Hence, by the inequality on triples
(V)
, this indicates
d_{W}(X_{0},X_{1})<\Theta
. Otherwise, if
d_{W}(X_{0},X_{1})>\Theta
and
Y,X_{1}
are not velcrot, then by monotonicity
(VII)
we have

d_{Y}(X_{0},X_{1})\geq d_{Y}(X_{0},W)>K,

which contradicts
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{0},X_{1})=1
. Therefore, we have
d_{W}(X_{0},X_{1})<\Theta
. Applying the coarse triangle inequality
(IV)
we have

d_{W}(Z,X_{0})+d_{W}(X_{0},X_{1})\succ_{\theta}d_{W}(Z,X_{1}),

which implies half of the second inequality. The other half is proved by
swapping
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

If
X_{0},X_{1},W
in
Lemma 4.13
are given
a priori
pairwise non-velcrot, then the result of
Lemma 4.13
also holds even given that
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{0},W)\geq 2
. In fact, this is the classical case in
[
BBF15
, Lemma 3.10]
.

Lemma 4.15
.

If
K>0
is sufficiently large, the following holds. Let
Z
be in
\mathbf{Y}
and
X_{0},X_{1}
be adjacent vertices in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
with
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{i},Z)\geq 4
. Let
W
be a guard for
Z
such that
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{0},Z)
. If
W\notin\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{1},Z)
, then there exists a guard
W^{\prime}
for
Z
such that
W^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{1},Z)
and
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(W^{\prime},Z)
.

Proof.

Since
d_{\mathcal{P}}(W,Z)=1
and
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{0},Z)\geq 4
, we have
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{0},W)\geq 3
, so we can apply
Lemma 4.13
, which yields

d_{W}(X_{1},Z)\sim_{\theta}d_{W}(X_{0},Z)>K/2.

This, in turn, indicates that
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{1},Z)
for sufficiently large
K>0
.

Similarly, we also have
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{1},W)\geq 3
. We see that there exists
W^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{1},Z)\subset\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{1},Z)
not velcrot to
W
such that
W^{\prime}<W
, otherwise
Remark 4.12
and
Remark 4.12
imply that
d_{\mathcal{P}}(W,X_{1})=1
, a contradiction. Due to the finiteness condition
(VI)
, we may assume that there is no
V\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{1},Z)
such that
W^{\prime}<V<W
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{1},Z)
. By the order property
(VIII)
, we conclude

d_{W}(W^{\prime},Z)\succ_{\theta}d_{W}(X_{1},Z)\succ_{\theta}K/2,

which indicates
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(W^{\prime},Z)
when
K>0
is sufficiently large.

Now, we claim that
W^{\prime}
is a guard for
Z
. Since
W^{\prime}>W
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{1},Z)
, according to
Remark 4.6
, for any
X
velcrot to
W
,
d_{W^{\prime}}(X,Z)\sim_{\theta}0
. So it suffices to verify the maximality condition of
W^{\prime}
for any
X
not velcrot to
W
with
d_{W^{\prime}}(X,Z)>\Theta
. By monotonicity
(VII)
, we also have
d_{W}(X,Z)>d_{W}(W^{\prime},Z)>\Theta
. Let
V\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)
. Since
W
is a guard for
Z
,
V\leq W
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Z)
. Note that
V
cannot be velcrot to
W
as
d_{VI}(X,Z)>K
. Assume for contradiction that
V>W^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Z)
, then by
Lemma 4.10
,
V\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(W^{\prime},Z)\subset\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{1},Z)
, where the inclusion is a consequence of monotonicity
(VII)
as before. But this, as described in
Remark 4.6
, also implies
W^{\prime}<V\leq W
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{1},Z)
, contradicting to our choice of
W^{\prime}
. So
V<W^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X,Z)
. This verifies the maximality condition and implies that
W^{\prime}
is a guard for
Z
.
∎

Definition 4.16
.

A
barrier
between a path
\{X_{0},\dots,X_{k}\}
and a vertex
Z
is a vertex
Y
such that
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{i},Z)
for all
i=0,\dots,k
.

Lemma 4.17
.

If there is a barrier between a path
\{X_{0},\dots,X_{k}\}
and a vertex
Z
, then
d_{Z}(X_{i},X_{j})\leq\Theta
for all
i,j
.

Proof.

Since
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{i},Z)
for all
i
,
Y
is not velcrot to
Z
or
X_{i}
, and we have
d_{Z}(Y,X_{i})\leq d_{Z}^{\pi}(Y,X_{i})<\theta
by
Theorem 3.13
and
(II)
in
Theorem 4.5
. Now, the desired result follows from the coarse triangle inequality
(IV)
.
∎

Proposition 4.18
.

The following holds if
K>0
is sufficiently large. Let
\{X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\}
be a path in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and
Z
a vertex of
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
such that
d_{\mathcal{P}}(Z,X_{i})\geq 4
for all
i
. Then there is a barrier
W
between the path and
Z
. In particular,
d_{Z}(X_{0},X_{i})\sim_{\theta}0
for all
i
.

Proof.

We will inductively choose a family of guards
W_{i}
for
Z
such that for each
1\leq i\leq k
,
W_{i}\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{i},Z)
, and if
i>j
then either
W_{i}=W_{j}
or
W_{j}\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(W_{i},Z)
.

First, let
W_{0}\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{0},Z)
be a guard for
Z
as in
Lemma 4.11
. Now, for the induction step, suppose that
W_{0},\dots,W_{i}
for some
i<k
have been chosen.If
W_{i}\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{i+1},Z)
, then we simply set
W_{i+1}\coloneqq W_{i}
. Otherwise, by
Lemma 4.15
, there
exists a guard
W_{i+1}\in\mathbf{Y}_{K/2}(X_{i+1},Z)
for
Z
with
W_{i}\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(W_{i+1},Z)
. We also remark that
W_{i+1}
is not velcrot to any
W_{j}
for
j<i
, otherwise by
Theorem 4.5
, we have

\theta>d_{W_{i+1}}^{\pi}(W_{i},Z)\geq d_{W_{i+1}}(W_{i},Z)\sim_{\theta}d_{W_{j}}(W_{i},Z).

Nevertheless, by our induction hypotheses
d_{W_{j}}(W_{i},Z)>\Theta
for
j<i
and by our assumption on
\Theta>0
, this will yield a contradiction. Hence,
W_{0},\dots,W_{i+1}
are pairwise non-velcrot, so by applying the monotonicity condition
(VII)
, we can also conclude that
W_{j}\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(W_{i+1},Z)
for all
j<i
.

Let
W\coloneqq W_{0}
. Using the monotonicity condition
(VII)
again, we can deduce that for any
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(W_{i},Z)\subset\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{i},Z)
. Therefore, we conclude that
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(X_{i},Z)
for all

and thus
W
is a barrier between
\{X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\}
and
Z
.
∎

We recall that a
quasi-tree
is a geodesic metric space that is quasi-isometric to a tree. One useful characterisation of a quasi-tree is the
bottleneck criterion
introduced by Manning in
[
Man05
]
. To be more precise, a geodesic metric space
X
is said to satisfy the bottleneck criterion if there exists a constant
\Delta\geq 0
such that for any two points
x,y\in X
, there exists a midpoint
z\in X
between
x
and
y
such that any paths in
X
connecting
x
to
y
intersect the
\Delta
-neighbourhood of
z
. With this characterisation, we can show the following:

Theorem 4.19
.

For sufficiently large
K>0
, the graph
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is a quasi-tree.

Proof.

Let
X,Z
be any two vertices on
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. By
Proposition 4.8
, we can find a path
\gamma\subset\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
connecting
X
to
Z
. Suppose that
X=X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{n}=Z
is a path from
X
to
Z
. This path has to stay inside of
3
-neighbourhood of
\gamma
. Otherwise, there is
Y\in\gamma\subset\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)
such that
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{i},Y)\geq 4
for all
X_{i}
, which by
Proposition 4.18
indicates that
d_{Y}(X,Z)<\Theta
, a contradiction.
∎

4.C. Puncturing

In this subsection, we will mainly treat the unboundedness of the quasi-tree that we have constructed above. But the arguments from
[
BBF15
]
cannot be adapted directly. In
[
BBF15
]
, for any pair
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
, if
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K^{\prime}}(X,Z)
for some sufficiently large
K^{\prime}>K
, then they showed that
Y
must lie on
any
geodesic path between
X
and
Z
in the quasi-tree. However, this cannot be true for the fine projection complex. Indeed, by perturbing
Y
a little, we can always construct another geodesic path that does not pass through the original
Y
.

More precisely, we only have:

Lemma 4.20
.

There exists
K^{\prime}>0
such that if
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K^{\prime}}(X,Z)
, then every
geodesic from
X
to
Z
in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
contains an element velcrot to
Y
.

Proof.

Suppose that
X=X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{n}=Z
is a geodesic path in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and that it does not contain element velcrot to
Y
. We claim that
d_{Y}(X,Z)\prec_{\theta}8K
.

If
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{i},Y)\geq 4
for all
i
, then
Proposition 4.18
indicates that
d_{Y}(X,Z)\sim_{\theta}0\prec_{\theta}8K
. So now, we may assume that
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{i},Y)<4
for some
i
. Let
i_{0}
be the first
i
such that
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{i},Y)<4
, and
i_{1}
be the last one. Suppose now
i_{0}>0
and
i_{1}<n
. Note that
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X_{i_{0}},X_{i_{1}})\leq 6
. Now, by
Proposition 4.18
, we again have
d_{Y}(X,X_{i_{0}-1})\sim_{\theta}0
and
d_{Y}(X_{i_{1}+1},Z)\sim_{\theta}0
.

Now, for
X_{j}
such that
i_{0}-1\leq j\leq i_{1}
, as
\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X_{j},X_{j+1})=\emptyset
, we have
d_{Y}(X_{j},X_{j+1})<K
. Since no
X_{j}
is velcrot to
Y
, we can apply the coarse triangle inequality
(IV)
to get

d_{Y}(X,Z)\prec_{\theta}d_{Y}(X,X_{i_{0}-1})+\sum_{j={i_{0}-1}}^{i_{1}}d_{Y}(X_{j},X_{j+1})+d_{Y}(X_{i_{1}+1},Z)\leq 8K.

Hence, by taking
K^{\prime}\succ_{\theta}8K
, we can conclude the desired result.

For the situations where
i_{0}=0
or
i_{1}=n
, the proof can be easily adapted and we leave it to readers.
∎

To deduce that the quasi-tree we constructed is not bounded, we will use the following puncturing technique, which is found to have its roots in the original paper
[
BHW22
]
.

Suppose that we have a finite collection of points
F
on the surface
S
. Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
be a subcollection of
\mathbf{Y}
such that
X\neq Z\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
are not velcrot, and the boundaries
\partial X,\partial Z
are disjoint from
F
and are in minimal positions on
S\setminus F
. In particular, let
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
be a subcollection of the geodesic representatives of
\mathbf{Y}
with respect to some hyperbolic metric on
S\setminus F
.

Consider the
surviving curve graph
of these subsurfaces. It is a graph
\mathcal{C}^{s}(X\setminus F)
where the vertices are isotopy classes of essential simple closed curves on
X\setminus F
that also remains essential in
X
, and we attach an edge between two vertices when they have disjoint representatives. Since any distinct
X,Z\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
are not velcrot, this implies that
\partial X
intersect
Z
essentially, and
vice versa
, or equivalently, they have well-defined subsurface projection on their surviving curve graphs.

Starting from a collection
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
satisfying several axioms, a renowned construction by Bestvina, Bromberg and Fujiwara will produce a new geodesic metric space, called the
projection complex of
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
.

Theorem 4.21

(
[
BBF15
]
)
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
be a collection of geodesic metric spaces together with its projection maps between different spaces. Suppose that there is some
M\geq 0
such that the following three axioms hold:

(P0)

For any distinct pair
X,Y\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
, we have
\mathrm{diam}(\pi_{Y}(X))\leq M
.

(P1)

For any distinct triple
X,Y,Z\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
, if
d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)>M
, then
d^{\pi}_{X}(Y,Z)\leq M
.

(P2)

For any distinct
X,Z\in\mathcal{Y}
, the set
\{Y\in\mathcal{Y}:d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)>M\}
is finite.

Then for sufficiently large
R>0
, there is a quasi-tree
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})
, where vertices are elements in
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
and an edge is attached to two vertices
X,Z\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
if

\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{R}(X,Z)\coloneqq\{Y\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}:\widetilde{d}_{Y}(X,Z)>R\}=\emptyset,

where
\widetilde{d}_{Y}(X,Z)
is the modified projection distance similar to what is defined above.

Following the discussion in
[
LT25
]
, we can see that the subsurface projection to the surviving curve graphs satisfies the two axioms
(P0)
, while
(P1)
and
(P2)
are classical from Leininger’s arguments (see for example
[
BBF15
, Lemma 5.2 & Lemma 5.3]
). So applying
Theorem 4.21
, we can build a quasi-tree
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})
.

To be more precise, here we can assume that
R>K+\theta
for
K>0
from
Theorem 4.21
and
M>0
is from
Section 3
. Let us similarly define

\widetilde{d}^{\pi}_{X}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(X\setminus F)}(\bigcup\{[\alpha]_{X\setminus F}:\alpha\in\pi_{X}(\partial X^{\prime})\cup\pi_{X}(\partial Z^{\prime})\}).

Then we construct the hierarchy
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(X,Z)
by replacing “velcrot” by “equal”, and
2\theta
by
\theta
in
Definition 4.2
. Indeed, since
\theta>0
can be
a posteriori
taken large enough, we can assume that it is large so that the hierarchy
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(X,Z)
verifies the conditions needed to run the Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara machinery. The modified projection distance
\widetilde{d}_{Y}(X,Z)
is defined as in (
4.1
) with hierarchy
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(X,Z)
.

We will now approximate
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
by
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})
.

Lemma 4.22
.

Let
F
be any finite collection of points on
S
and
X
be a subsurface on
S
such that
\partial X\cap F=\emptyset
. Suppose that
x,y\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
are two curves intersecting
X
essentially, then for any
\alpha\in\pi_{X}(x)
and
\beta\in\pi_{X}(y)
that are transverse, we have

d_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(X\setminus F)}\big([\alpha]_{X\setminus F},[\beta]_{X\setminus F}\big)\leq d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)}(\alpha,\beta),

where
[\alpha]_{S\setminus F},[\beta]_{S\setminus F}
are the isotopy classes on
S\setminus F
. The equality is attained if
\alpha,\beta
are in minimal position on
S\setminus F
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
Lemma 2.13
.
∎

The following lemma indicates that the definitions of two hierarchy are compatible:

Lemma 4.23
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
be as above. For any distinct elements
X,X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
, we have

|\widetilde{d}^{\pi}_{X}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})-d^{\pi}_{X}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})|\leq 24<\theta.

Therefore,
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(X,Z)\supset\mathcal{H}(X,Z)\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\times\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
.

Proof.

The inequality follows immediately from
Lemma 4.22
and
Proposition 2.17
. By the definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(X,Z)
, the desired inclusion is a direct consequence of the above inequality.
∎

Now, we will have the following estimate for modified projection distances:

Lemma 4.24
.

For any distinct non-velcrot
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
with boundaries intersecting transversely, there exists a subcollection
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
as above such that
X,Z\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
and for any
Y\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\setminus\{X,Z\}
, we have

\widetilde{d}_{Y}(X,Z)\leq d_{Y}(X,Z)+\theta.

Proof.

Since the boundaries of
X,Y,Z
are intersecting transversely, there exists a finite collection of points
F\subset S
such that
\partial X,\partial Y,\partial Z
are pairwise in minimal position on
S\setminus F
. Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
be a subcollection as above that contains
X,Y,Z
and representatives of isotopy classes in
\mathbf{H}(X,Z)
on
S\setminus F
so that the boundaries are in minimal positions. By
Lemma 4.23
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{d}_{Y}(X,Z)

\displaystyle=\inf\{\widetilde{d}^{\pi}_{Y}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}):(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(X,Z)\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\{d^{\pi}_{Y}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}):(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(X,Z)\}+\theta

\displaystyle\leq\inf\{d^{\pi}_{Y}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}):(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{H}(X,Z)\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\times\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\}+\theta

\displaystyle=\inf\{d^{\pi}_{Y}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}):(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{H}(X,Z)\}+\theta

\displaystyle=d_{Y}(X,Z)+\theta,

where the second last equality comes from the fact that the projection distance is minimised when
\partial X^{\prime}
and
\partial Z^{\prime}
are in minimal positions on
S\setminus F
,
i.e.
when
X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}
, see
Lemma 4.22
.
∎

The following proposition allows us to give a lower bound for vertices in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. It turns out that the lower bound is also similar to the lower bound in the original Bestvina–Bromberg–Fujiwara construction, see
[
BBF15
, Lemma 3.18]
.

Proposition 4.25
.

There exists
K^{\prime}>0
sufficiently large such that for any
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
, we have

d_{\mathcal{P}}(X,Z)\geq|\mathbf{Y}_{K^{\prime}}(X,Z)|_{{\operatorname{vel}}}\,.

Proof.

If
X,Z
are velcrot, then there is nothing to prove. Suppose now that they are not. To deduce the desired result, it suffices to show that for any
X,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
with
\partial X
and
\partial Z
intersecting transversely, we have

d_{\mathcal{P}}(X,Z)\geq|\mathbf{Y}_{K^{\prime}}(X,Z)|_{{\operatorname{vel}}}+1.

Indeed, we can perturb
X,Z
a little to
X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}
so that their boundaries intersect transversely, and
X,X^{\prime}
, as well as
Z,Z^{\prime}
, are velcrot to each other, and by coarse triangle inequality
(IV)
, we can conclude that
|\mathbf{Y}_{K^{\prime}}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})|_{{\operatorname{vel}}}\leq|\mathbf{Y}_{K_{0}}(X,Z)|_{{\operatorname{vel}}}
for
K^{\prime}\gg K_{0}
.

Let
K^{\prime}\gg K_{0}\sim_{\theta}5R
. Let
(Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathbf{Y}_{K^{\prime}}(X,Z)
that are pairwise non-velcrot and that every
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K^{\prime}}(X,Z)
is velcrot to one of
Y_{i}
’s. Suppose in addition that they are pairwise transverse. Now, take
F\subset S
be a finite collection such that
\partial X,\partial Z,\partial Y_{i}
are pairwise in minimal position on
S\setminus F
. Consider a subcollection
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\subset\mathbf{Y}
containing
Y_{i}
’s and satisfying the conditions in
Lemma 4.24
. We then have
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{K_{0}}(X,Z)\supset(Y_{i})_{i}
. Since
K_{0}\succ_{\theta}5R
, by
[
BBF15
, Lemma 3.18]
, we can conclude that

d_{\mathcal{P}_{R}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})}(X,Z)\geq|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{K_{0}}(X,Z)|+1\geq n+1.

By
Lemma 4.24
again, we can see that

\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{R}(X,Z)\subset\mathbf{Y}_{K}(X,Z)\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}.

so if
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X,Z)=1
in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
, they are also connected by an edge in
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})
. This implies that

d_{\mathcal{P}}(X,Z)\geq d_{\mathcal{P}_{R}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}})}(X,Z)\geq n+1.

Now, taking the infimum over all collections
(Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathbf{Y}_{K^{\prime}}(X,Z)
as given at the beginning of this paragraph, we can conclude the desired result.
∎

Now, using arguments similar to
[
BBF15
, Proposition 3.20]
and the fact that iterating pseudo-Anosov homeomorphisms relative to finitely many of points can make the projection distance arbitrarily large, we get:

Corollary 4.26
.

The quasi-tree
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is unbounded.

\square

Finally, since the projection distance is
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
-equivariant,
i.e.

d^{\pi}_{g(Y)}\big(g(X),g(Z)\big)=d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)

for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathbf{Y}
and any
g\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
, we can deduce that
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
also acts on
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
by automorphism (or equivalently, by isometries). Recall that an isometric action of a group
G
on a metric space
X
is
cobounded
if a Hausdorff neighbourhood of a
G
-orbit contains
X
.

Proof of
Theorem 1.1
.

Indeed, it suffices to take
\mathbf{Y}
consisting of only finitely many isotopy classes of non-sporadic essential subsurface, thus the isometric action of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
on
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
has only finite orbits, which,
a fortiori
, implies that the action is cobounded.
∎

5
Blowing up

In this section, we will modify the fine projection complex constructed in
Section 4
into a Gromov hyperbolic space in which the fine curve graphs of essential subsurfaces are quasi-isometrically embedded. As an application, we make use of the Bestvina–Fujiwara machinery to build quasi-morphisms for surface homeomorphisms supported on subsurfaces.

5.A. Construction

Now we will define a new graph
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
as follows. The vertex set is the collection of ordered pairs
(X,\alpha)
, where
X\in\mathbf{Y}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
. Let
L\sim_{\theta}K
and we may assume that
K<L<2K
. We connect
(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
following the rules below:

(L1)

(X,\alpha)
is connected to
(X,\beta)
by an edge of length
1
, if
\alpha,\beta
are both essential in
X
and are disjoint.

(L2)

(X,\alpha)
is connected to
(Y,\beta)
by an edge of length
L
, if
X,Y
are velcrot and
\beta\in\pi_{Y}(\alpha)
.

(L3)

(X,\alpha)
is connected to
(Y,\beta)
by an edge of length
L
, if
X,Y
are not velcrot but
d_{\mathcal{P}}(X,Y)=1
, and if
\alpha\in\pi_{X}(Y)
and
\beta\in\pi_{Y}(X)
.

We equip
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
with the combinatorial distance given above, denoted
d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}
. We also note that the condition
(L1)
implies that for every
X\in\mathbf{Y}
, the fine curve graph
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
is an abstract subgraph of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. Since
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
for
X\in\mathbf{Y}
are connected, it is clear from the definition that
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is also connected.

For simplicity, let us define for any essential subsurface
X\subset S
and any two finite collections
A,B\subset\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
,

d^{\pi}_{X}(A,B)\coloneqq\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)}\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in A,\,\beta\in B}\big(\pi_{X}(\alpha)\cup\pi_{X}(\beta)\big)\right).

If
p=(Y,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
, then we will define
\pi_{X}(p)\coloneqq\pi_{X}(\alpha)
. Recall that if
p=Y\in\mathbf{Y}
, we have defined
\pi_{X}(p)\coloneqq\pi_{X}(\partial Y)
.

Now, we may give the first estimate of the distance between some points in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
:

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
X\in\mathbf{Y}
, the inclusion
\iota_{X}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
by
\alpha\mapsto(X,\alpha)
is bi-Lipchitz. The bi-Lipschitz constant does not depend on the choice of
X\in\mathbf{Y}
.

Proof.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
, note that

d^{\dagger}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\geq d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}\big((X,\alpha),(X,\beta)\big)

as
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
is a subgraph of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
.

Conversely, let us consider the graph
\Gamma(X)
by collapsing all
\{(Y,\alpha):\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)\}
into a singleton, still denoted
Y
, for every
Y\in\mathbf{Y}
not velcrot to
X
. Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})\to\Gamma(X)
be the collapsing map and equip
\Gamma(X)
with the combinatorial distance, then
\pi
is
1
-Lipschitz. For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
, let
(X,\alpha)=x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}=(X,\beta)
be a geodesic segment in
\Gamma(X)
connecting
(X,\alpha)
to
(X,\beta)
. Now we have the following situations (up to a change of order):

Case 1.

If both
x_{i},x_{i+1}\in\Gamma(X)
represent a subsurface not velcrot to
X
, then by the Coarse equality
(II)
, we conclude

d^{\pi}_{X}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\prec_{\theta}d_{X}(x_{i},x_{i+1})<K<L=d_{\Gamma(X)}(x_{i},x_{i+1}).

Case 2.

If
x_{i}=(X^{\prime},\gamma)
and
x_{i+1}=(X^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
, where
\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset
are two essential curves on
X^{\prime}
for some
X^{\prime}
velcrot (or even equal) to
X
, then we first remark that
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
necessarily intersect essentially with
X
, and by
Proposition 2.18
, one can also conclude

d^{\pi}_{X}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\leq 12=12\cdot d_{\Gamma(X)}(x_{i},x_{i+1}).

Case 3.

If
x_{i}=(X^{\prime},\gamma)
and
x_{i+1}=(X^{\prime\prime},\gamma)
for some
\gamma^{\prime}\in\pi_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\gamma^{\prime})
and
X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}
velcrot (or equal) to
X
. Then either
\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset
or
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
. In either case, both
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
have essential intersection with
X
, by
Proposition 2.18
, we have

d^{\pi}_{X}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\leq 12<L=d_{\Gamma(X)}(x_{i},x_{i+1}).

Case 4.

If
x_{i}
represent a subsurface
Y\in\mathbf{Y}
and
x_{i+1}=(X^{\prime},\gamma)
for some
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime})
and
X^{\prime}
velcrot (or equal) to
X
, then either
X^{\prime},Y
are velcrot and
\gamma
intersects
Y
essentially, or
\gamma\in\pi_{X^{\prime}}(Y)
. Note that as
Y
is not velcrot to
X
, so
\partial Y
intersects
X
essentially. For the first case, let
\gamma^{\prime}\in\pi_{Y}(\gamma)
such that either
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma
, or
\gamma^{\prime}\subset Y\cap X^{\prime}
is a curve disjoint from
\gamma
. Then
\gamma^{\prime},\gamma,\partial Y
are contained in a geodesic segment in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)
between
\gamma
and
\partial Y
, and by
Theorem 2.19
, we have

d^{\pi}_{X}(\gamma,Y)\leq M<L=d_{\Gamma(X)}(x_{i},x_{i+1}).

For the second case, we can see that
\gamma\cap\partial Y=\emptyset
, then by
Proposition 2.18
, we have

d^{\pi}_{X}(\gamma,Y)\leq 12<L=d_{\Gamma(X)}(x_{i},x_{i+1}).

Using the triangle inequality, we can deduce that

d^{\dagger}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\leq 12\cdot d_{\Gamma(X)}\big((X,\alpha),(X,\beta)\big)\leq 12\cdot d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}\big((X,\alpha),(X,\beta)\big),

which completes the proof.
∎

5.B. Geometric properties

For our convenience, let us introduce the following modified projection distance. Let
\iota_{X}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
be as in
Proposition 5.1
. Consider
y,z\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and define
d_{X}(y,z)
in the following way:

(1)

If
y,z\notin\iota_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime}))
for any
X^{\prime}
velcrot to
X
, and if
y=(Y,\alpha)
and
z=(Z,\beta)
, then
d_{X}(y,z)\coloneqq d_{X}(Y,Z)
.

(2)

If either
y
or
z
is contained in
\iota_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime}))
for some
X^{\prime}
velcrot to
X
with
y=(Y,\alpha)
and
z=(Z,\beta)
, then
d_{X}(y,z)\coloneqq d^{\pi}_{X}(\{\alpha\}\cup\partial Y,\{\beta\}\cup\partial Z)
.

In particular, if
Z
is not velcrot to
X
and
y=(Y,\alpha)
with
\alpha
intersecting
X
essentially, then we may write
d_{X}(y,Z)\coloneqq d_{X}^{\pi}(\alpha,\partial Z)
.

Remark 5.2
.

Here in the case where one of
y,z
is contained in
\iota_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X^{\prime}))
for some
X^{\prime}
velcrot to
X
, the reason why we have set

to be

instead of

is purely technical, since
\alpha
or
\beta
may not intersect

essentially. However, as
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
) is disjoint from
\partial Y
(resp.
\partial Z
), which intersects
X
essentially, we may consider that the “projection to

” of
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
) is roughly
\pi_{X}(Y)
(resp.
\pi_{X}(Z)
). See
Proposition 5.10
for a further justification.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
y,z\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and
X\in\mathbf{Y}
. Suppose that
y=(Y,\alpha)
and
z=(Z,\beta)
. Then
d_{X}(y,z)\sim_{\theta}d^{\pi}_{X}(\{\alpha\}\cup\partial Y,\{\beta\}\cup\partial Z)
.

Proof.

If none of
Y,Z
is velcrot to
X
, then together with coarse equality
(II)
, we have

d_{X}(y,z)=d_{X}(Y,Z)\sim_{\theta}d^{\pi}_{X}(Y,Z).

But, as
\alpha\cap\partial Y=\emptyset
and
\beta\cap\partial Z=\emptyset
, by
Proposition 2.18
, we can conclude that

d_{X}(y,z)\sim_{\theta}d^{\pi}_{X}(Y,Z)\sim_{\theta}d^{\pi}_{X}(\{\alpha\}\cup\partial Y,\{\beta\}\cup\partial Z).

If one of
Y,Z
is velcrot to
X
, then
d_{X}(y,z)=d^{\pi}_{X}(\{\alpha\}\cup\partial Y,\{\beta\}\cup\partial Z)
.
∎

The following gives a coarse triangle inequality for the blown-up fine projection complex setting:

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
x,y,z\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and any
Y\in\mathbf{Y}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{Y}(x,y)+d_{Y}(y,z)\succ_{\theta}d_{Y}(x,z).

(5.1)

Proof.

It follows from coarse triangle inequality
(IV)
and
Lemma 5.3
.
∎

As we have defined the modified projection distance for the vertices in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
, we may also define
\mathbf{Y}_{K}(x,z)
, for
K>0
and
x,z\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
, by the collection of all

such that

. Moreover, for
x\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and

, we can define

in a similar way. We remark that for
x\in\iota_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X))
and

, the set

might also contain
X
and

.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1}
be two non-velcrot vertices in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
such that

and
x_{1}\in\iota_{X_{1}}(\pi_{X_{1}}(X_{0}))\subset\iota_{X_{1}}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X_{1})
. Let

be also a vertex in
\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and

such that

for

. Then either

d_{W}(x_{0},x_{1})\sim_{\theta}0

or for

,

d_{W}(x_{i},w)\succ_{\theta}L\,.

If
X_{0},X_{1}
are velcrot,
x_{0}=(X_{0},\alpha)
,
x_{1}=(X_{1},\alpha)
for some

be as above, then
d_{W}(x_{0},x_{1})\sim_{\theta}0
.

Proof.

Let us first prove the last part of the statement. Let
X_{0},X_{1}
be velcrot and
x_{0}=(X_{0},\alpha)
,

for some

X_{0}
or
X_{1}
, then either
\alpha
or
\alpha^{\prime}
also intersects

since we also have
\alpha\cap\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha\cap\partial X_{0}=\alpha^{\prime}\cap\partial X_{1}=\emptyset
. Otherwise,
W
is not velcrot to
X_{0}
nor
X_{1}
. Then
\partial X_{0}
and
\partial X_{1}
both intersect

essentially, and by the coarse equality

and

d_{W}(x_{0},x_{1})=d_{W}(X_{0},X_{1})\leq d^{\pi}_{W}(X_{0},X_{1})\leq M\sim_{\theta}0.

Now, let us assume that
X_{0},X_{1}
are not velcrot. If
W
is velcrot to either
X_{0}
. Suppose that
x_{0}=(X_{0},\alpha)
and
x_{1}=(X_{1},\beta)

Lemma 5.6
.

For
K>
sufficiently large the following holds. Let

and
x_{1}

be adjacent vertices in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and let

Suppose that
X_{0},X_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
with

and

\displaystyle 4L

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}\big(x_{i},\iota_{Y}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y))\big)

In this subsection, we will show that
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})

Define the map

.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
y=(Y,\eta)
be a vertex in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and

not velcrot to

is one nearest point in

to

to

Proof.

Since

are velcrot, by
Proposition 3.5
, we can find
W\subset Z\cap Z^{\prime}
isotopic to both
Z
and

. Let

next to

If

are velcrot and

essentially, so we may choose

Proposition 5.10

Let

be a vertex in

Proof.

We first assume that

to

Note that a path in

to

Corollary 5.11

Proposition 5.12

, every path from

must intersect the

for

not velcrot to

be an arbitrary path from

to

in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. But if

Definition 5.13

are not velcrot, then
standard path
between

is a path passing through
\iota_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X))
,
\iota_{W_{i}}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}({W_{i}}))
, and
\iota_{Y}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y))
in the natural order, where

are pairwise non-velcrot such that any

is velcrot to one of

’s, and within each

the standard path is the
\iota_{W}
-image of a geodesic segment in
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(W)
. If
X,Y
are velcrot, then a
standard path
between
x
and

is a path consisting of the

-image of a geodesic segment in

that connects

to some

and an edge between

and
(Y,\beta)
if

. We will denote by

the union of all standard paths between

and

.

In the following, we will show that these standard paths serve as guessing geodesics for the application of Guessing Geodesics Lemma.

Lemma 5.14
.

There exists
K^{\prime}>0
sufficiently large such that the following holds. Let
x,z\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
and
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(x,z)\cup\{\sigma(x),\sigma(z)\}
. If
\ell\subset\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is a standard path between
x
and
z
such that
\ell\cap\iota_{Y}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y))\neq\emptyset
, then there is a geodesic segment
[\alpha,\beta]\subset\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)
with
\iota_{Y}([\alpha,\beta])=\ell\cap\iota_{Y}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y))
and
d_{Y}^{\pi}(x,\alpha),d_{Y}^{\pi}(\beta,z)\prec_{\theta}K^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
\sigma(x),\sigma(z)
are velcrot, then the desired result follows directly from the definition. Hence, we may assume in the following that
\sigma(x),\sigma(z)
are not velcrot.

Note that
\ell\cap\iota_{Y}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y)
is a path between
(Y,\alpha)
and
(Y,\beta)
.

Let
v=(Y,\alpha)
and let
v^{\prime}=(Y^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
be the vertex on
\ell
immediately preceding
v
. Hence,
\gamma^{\prime}\in\pi_{Y^{\prime}}(Y)
and
\alpha\in\pi_{Y}(Y^{\prime})
. We claim that
d_{Y}^{\pi}(x,v)=d_{Y}^{\pi}(x,\alpha)\prec_{\theta}K^{\prime}
. For
d_{Y}^{\pi}(\beta,z)
, it is a symmetric case. Suppose for contradiction that
d_{Y}^{\pi}(x,v)>K^{\prime}
with
K^{\prime}
sufficiently large. Then we further claim that
\ell^{\prime}\coloneqq\ell\cap\mathcal{L}(x,v^{\prime})
must intersect
\iota_{Y}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y))
, which is a contradiction after our definition of a standard path. By
Lemma 5.3
, as
K^{\prime}<d_{Y}^{\pi}(x,z)\sim_{\theta}d_{Y}(x,z)
and
K^{\prime}\gg K
, we can conclude that
Y\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(x,z)
. By
Proposition 5.12
, we can find
v_{0},w_{0}\in\ell^{\prime}
such that
d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}\big(v_{0},\iota_{Y}(\pi_{Y}(x))\big)\prec_{\theta}4L+4K
and
d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}\big(w_{0},\iota_{Y}(\pi_{Y}(v^{\prime}))\big)\prec_{\theta}4L+4K
. In particular,
d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}(v_{0},w_{0})\prec_{\theta}8L+8K+d_{Y}(x,v^{\prime})
. If
\ell^{\prime}
is disjoint from
\iota_{Y^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y^{\prime\prime}))
for any
Y^{\prime\prime}
velcrot to
Y
, then we can estimate that the number of
W\in\mathbf{Y}
such that
\ell^{\prime}
passes through
\iota_{W}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(W))
is at least

\frac{d^{\pi}_{Y}(x,v^{\prime})}{K+2\theta}-1,

as the diameter of the projections to
Y
of the union of two consecutive non-velcrot
W
’s is at most
K+2\theta
by
Proposition 4.4
. Thus, the number of edges of length
L
that the subsegment of
\ell^{\prime}
between
v_{0}
and
w_{0}
passes through is at least
d^{\pi}_{Y}(x,v^{\prime})/(K+2\theta)
, and we have the inequality

\frac{Ld^{\pi}_{Y}(x,v^{\prime})}{K+2\theta}\leq d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}(v_{0},w_{0})\prec_{\theta}8L+8K+d^{\pi}_{Y}(x,v^{\prime}),

which is contradictory if
0<K^{\prime}<d^{\pi}_{Y}(x,v^{\prime})
is sufficiently large as
L/(K+2\theta)>1
. Hence, the subsegment of
\ell^{\prime}
between
v_{0}
and
w_{0}
passes through
\iota_{Y^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(Y^{\prime\prime}))
for some
Y^{\prime\prime}
velcrot to
Y
. But by our definition of a standard path,
Y^{\prime\prime}
has to be
Y
, which will also contradict the assumption on
v^{\prime}
. Therefore,
d^{\pi}_{Y}(x,v^{\prime})<K^{\prime}
. As
v=(Y,\alpha)
and
v^{\prime}=(Y^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
with
\alpha\in\pi_{Y}(Y^{\prime})
, so
\gamma^{\prime}\cap\partial Y^{\prime}=\alpha\cap\partial Y^{\prime}=\emptyset
. By
Proposition 2.18
and (
3.3
), as

are not velcrot, we can also conclude that

This completes the proof.
∎

To show that
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is Gromov hyperbolic, we will make use of the Guessing Geodesics Lemma (

).

Theorem 5.15
.

The space
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is
\delta
-hyperbolic and for each

, the fine curve graph

.

Proof.

The map

is indeed the desired bi-Lipschitz map after

. Now, it suffices to show that the graph
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is
\delta
-hyperbolic.

Let

be arbitrary. We will show that there exists an

such that any standard path between

is contained in a
\lambda
-neighbourhood of the union of any standard paths between
x,y
and
x,z
.

Let
\ell
be a standard path between
y
and
z
, and let
W\in\mathbf{Y}_{K}(y,z)\cup\{\sigma(y),\sigma(z)\}
be arbitrary such that
\ell
passes through
\iota_{W}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(W))
. Also consider
\ell_{1}
a standard path between
x
and
y
, as well as
\ell_{2}
a standard path between

and

. We recall from
[

]

is

-hyperbolic for some uniform

.

First, consider the case where

. By our assumption,

is contained in
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(x,y)
and
\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}(x,z)
. By

, there exists a

and together with

and
\pi_{W}(y)

5.D. Application to quasi-morphisms

An important application of the blown-up fine projection complex is building quasi-morphisms on the group acting on this complex by isometries via the famous Bestvina–Fujiwara machinery.

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
S,\mathbf{Y},\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y}),\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
be as above. Then for each subsurface
X\in\mathbf{Y}
, there exist
f,g\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(X;\partial X)<\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
acting by independent loxodromic isometries on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. Moreover, the two elements
f,g
can be taken smooth.

Proof.

By
[
BHW22
]
, we can find
f,g\in\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(X;\partial X)\subset\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
that acts on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
by independent loxodromic isometries. We claim that via the
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
-action on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
, two elements
f,g
also act by independent loxodromic elements.

Since the embedding
\iota_{X}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
is bi-Lipschitz, and
a fortiori
quasi-isometric, by
Proposition 5.1
, we can conclude that
f,g
also yield loxodromic elements on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
. Hence, to prove the claim, it suffices to show that they are independent.

Let
A_{f},A_{g}\subset\iota_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X))
be, respectively, quasi-axes of
f
and
g
. Let
B>0
and
h\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
be arbitrary. Now, we fall into two possibilities:

Case 1.

Suppose that
hX
is not velcrot to
X
. Then
hA_{g}\subset\iota_{hX}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(hX))
. Let
z
be any vertex contained in
\iota_{hX}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(hX))\cap\mathcal{N}_{B}(A_{f})
. By definition, there exists
x=(\alpha,X)\in A_{f}
such that
d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}(x,z)\leq B
. Take
x^{\prime}\in\iota_{hX}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(hX))
to be one nearest point projection of
x
to the subspace
\iota_{hX}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(hX))
. By
Corollary 5.11
, there exists a uniform
N>0
such that

d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}\big(x^{\prime},\iota_{hX}(\pi_{hX}(\alpha))\big)\leq N,

but as
\alpha\cap\partial X=\emptyset
, we can further deduce from
Proposition 2.18
that

d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}\big(x^{\prime},\iota_{hX}(\pi_{hX}(X))\big)\leq N,

for a sufficiently large
N
independent from the choice of
B>0
and
h\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
. But
d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}(x^{\prime},z)\leq B-L
. Hence, we can conclude

d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}\big(z,\iota_{hX}(\pi_{hX}(X))\\
big)\leq B-L-N,

which is uniformly bounded. Now, we need only to take a segment
J\subset A_{g}
such that the diameter is large than
2(B-N-L)
to see
J\not\subset\mathcal{N}_{B}(A_{f})
.

Case 2.

If
hX
is velcrot to
X
, then by
Proposition 3.5
, we can find a subsurface
Z\subsetneqq X\cap hX
that is isotopic to both
X
and
hX
. Now take an isotopy
h^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
such that
h^{\prime}|_{Z}=\mathrm{Id}_{Z}
and
h^{\prime}hX=X
. In this way, we may consider
h^{\prime}h\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(X)
. Note that every vertex on
hA_{g}
has essential subsurface projection on
Z
, so do
h^{\prime}hA_{g}
. Moreover, as
h^{\prime}|_{Z}=\mathrm{Id}_{Z}
, we can conclude that for any
x=(\alpha,X)\in A_{g}
, the subsurface projection
\pi_{Z}(h\alpha)=\pi_{Z}(h^{\prime}h\alpha)
, as
h\alpha\cap Z=h^{\prime}h\alpha\cap Z
. Therefore, by our construction of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
, we have

d_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})}(hx,h^{\prime}hx)\leq 2L.

Note that
h^{\prime}h\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(X)
and
f\nsim g
for
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(X)
-action on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X)
and thus on
\iota_{X}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(X))\subset\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
, there exists
J\subset A_{g}
such that
h^{\prime}hJ\not\subset\mathcal{N}_{B+2L}(A_{f})
. This further yields
hJ\not\subset\mathcal{N}_{B}(A_{f})
.

Hence, we can finally conclude that
f\nsim g
for
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
-action on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
.
∎

Now, we are able to conclude the non-sporadic cases in
Theorem 1.4
.

Proof of the non-sporadic cases in
Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathbf{Y}
be a collection of essential subsurfaces satisfying
(Y1)
and
(Y2)
that contains the subsurface

. Then the action of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
on

admits two independent loxodromic elements (

). Hence, by

, we can construct homogeneous quasi-morphisms

such that

To make

-continuous quasi-morphism, we will need the further modification. Note that the restriction

is also a homogeneous quasi-morphism on

and, using Kotschick’s automatic continuity arguments (see
[

]

.
∎

6
Projection complex for once-bordered torus

In this last part, we will briefly explain why and how our construction also works for essential subsurfaces that are homeomorphic to a once-bordered torus.

6.A. Subsurface projection and velcrotness

Let
\Sigma
be a once bordered torus. Its

is defined slightly differently compared to the other surfaces. Let us still denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\Sigma)

intersect transversely at most once

[

A similar distance estimate to

[

]
)

Let
\Sigma
and

be as above. Then for any transverse

Now let
S
be a closed orientable surface of at least
2
genus and

by the collection of essential curves on
\Sigma
that can taken as a boundary component of a regular neighbourhood of
\partial\Sigma
and an essential arc in

. With

, the same arguments from
[

]
yield the following:

.

Let

be as above and let

If both
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(S)

\Sigma
essentially and

\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\Sigma)}\big(\pi_{\Sigma}(\alpha)\cup\pi_{\Sigma}(\beta)\big)\leq 7.

Let
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
be two essential subsurfaces of

that are once-bordered tori. We say that

intersects

\Sigma\in\mathbf{Y}
, there exist
f,g\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
such that
f(\Sigma)=g(\Sigma)=\Sigma
acting by independent loxodromic isometries on
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{Y})
.

\square

Now, using
Theorem 2.10
and Kotschick’s automatic continuity arguments, we can also conclude the cases for once-bordered tori in
Theorem 1.5
,
i.e.
if
\Sigma\subset S
is an essential subsurface homeomorphic to a once-bordered torus, then there exists a
C^{0}
-continuous homogeneous quasi-morphisms
\varphi
on
\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
with
\varphi(g)\neq 0
for some
g\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(\Sigma;\partial\Sigma)<\mathrm{Homeo}_{0}(S)
.
