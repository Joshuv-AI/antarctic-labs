Untargeted metabolomic comparison of breast muscle metabolites between Cobb broilers and local chicken breeds
Authors: Chunlin Yu, Fucan Li, Zengrong Zhang, Jiangxian Wang, Mohan Qiu, Chenming Hu, Jialei Chen, Li Yang, Bo Xia, Xia Xiong, Xiaoyan Song, Han Peng, Shiliang Zhu, Longhuan Du, Jiayi Zhao, Yueying Zhang, Hua Fan, Jinbo Wu, Chaowu Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The flavor of meat is closely associated with its metabolic profile, which varies significantly among different breeds. This study utilized HS-GC-MS (headspace gas chromatography-mass spectrometry) to explore the metabolic differences in breast muscle among six chicken breeds, namely Cobb broiler (KB), Heishui Phoenix Chicken (FW), Qiangshan Cloud Chicken (YD), Daheng Black-bone Chicken (DHW), Daheng Yellow Chicken (DHH), and Daheng Partridge Chicken (DHS). Firstly, the slaughter performance and breast meat quality traits were evaluated. In comparison with the KB, local breeds exhibited lower breast muscle, drip loss, and cooking loss, while showing higher leg muscle rate, shear force, and fat content. In addition, local breeds displayed significantly different meat color, characterized by higher L*, higher a*, and lower b* values. The results showed that a clear separation was achieved between local breeds and Cobb broiler, indicating distinct metabolic differences. Specifically, comparisons of FW vs. KB, YD vs. KB, DHW vs. KB, DHH vs. KB, and DHS vs. KB revealed 17, 12, 28, 17, and 15 significantly altered metabolites, respectively. These differential metabolites were mainly categorized into amino acids, fatty acids, and their derivatives. Correlation analysis identified diverse positive and negative links among these metabolites, constituting a complex metabolic regulatory network. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that the differential metabolites were primarily enriched in synthesis and degradation of ketone bodies, lipoic acid metabolism, butanoate metabolism, propanoate metabolism, fatty acid biosynthesis, and phenylalanine metabolism. These pathways are pivotal to the nutrient composition and flavor development of breast muscle in chicken. This study reveals metabolic characteristics and differences in breast muscle among diverse chicken breeds, laying a theoretical foundation for optimizing chicken breeding, improving meat quality, regulating flavor traits, and exploring the molecular mechanisms underlying metabolic regulation in chicken breast muscle.

Introduction

Globally, chicken meat is regarded as an excellent source of premium protein. It is favored for its nutritional profile, including abundant unsaturated fatty acids and minimal levels of fat, cholesterol, and calories compared to red meats (Chen et al., 2023; Xu and Yin, 2024). Additionally, its affordability and lack of cultural or religious consumption restrictions have steadily pushed up its global demand. As a result, it becomes the most popular poultry product around the world (Deng et al., 2022; Ge et al., 2023). As people’s living standards have risen, their focus on chicken products has moved from quantity to quality. Sensory attributes such as flavor, color, and freshness have become major roles influencing shopping decisions and overall acceptance (Katiyo et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2024). Among these intrinsic cues, flavor is particularly prominent as it directly determines the sensory experience of consumers, thereby regulating their preference and repurchase intention for chicken products (Madruga et al., 2009).

Off-flavors, which are unpleasant odors or tastes resulting from imbalances in volatile and non-volatile compounds, can significantly undermine consumer acceptance and market value (Escobedo del Bosque et al., 2022). Surveys have consistently demonstrated that odor is the primary criterion for consumers to evaluate chicken quality in both retail and household settings, and the presence of undesirable odors can drastically reduce purchasing willingness (Madruga et al., 2009). These off-flavors typically arise from specific metabolic processes, involving the oxidation of lipids, the catabolism of amino acids, and fermentation by microbes. These reactions produce compounds that cause off-flavors. For instance, short-chain fatty acids, methyl ketones, and fatty acids with branched chains can create rancid, cheesy, or grassy notes (Liu et al., 2022; Russo et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2025). For commercial chicken products, especially processed and chilled chicken, off-flavor formation has become a major constraint affecting product competitiveness and consumer trust. Therefore, elucidating the metabolic origins of off-flavors and their breed-specific variations is essential for improving chicken meat quality.

Untargeted metabolomics is now considered a powerful method to fully explore the metabolic profiles. This approach offers a panoramic view of low-molecular-weight metabolites involved in both desirable flavor formation and off-flavor generation (Pavagadhi and Swarup, 2020). Among the various metabolomic platforms, headspace-solid-phase microextraction coupled with gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC-MS) is extensively used to analyze flavor compounds among meat products (Jin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026; Yan et al., 2025). This facilitates exploration of associations between metabolic pathways and sensory quality (Zhang et al., 2020). This approach is particularly well-suited for capturing the lipid-derived volatiles as well as amino acid-derived compounds, all of which dominate flavor and off-flavor profiles in meat products. Previous studies have validated that untargeted metabolomics can effectively distinguish metabolic differences in chicken meat across different breeds and breeding models, providing critical insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying flavor variation (Lei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024a; Zhao et al., 2024). Moreover, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis can identify the core metabolic pathways that underpin breed-specific differences in meat quality, offering a systems-level understanding of flavor regulation.

Breed is widely acknowledged as a key intrinsic factor influencing the flavor and off-flavor profiles of chicken meat. This is primarily due to differences in genetic background that regulate the synthesis and metabolism of flavor precursors (Alsoufi et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2011). A comparative metabolomic study of Houdan chickens and other native crossbreeds demonstrated distinct differences in pathways related to amino acid metabolism and transport. These findings further confirm the impact of genetic background on key flavor-related metabolic pathways (Lei et al., 2025). Despite these advances, systematic comparisons between high-yield commercial broilers and traditional slow-growing breeds remain limited. This is particularly true regarding the specific metabolic pathways that drive differences in both desirable flavor compounds and off-flavor-associated metabolites. China has roughly 100 regional chicken breeds, and as consumers increasingly favor native poultry products, these local varieties now hold a market share comparable to commercial strains (Liu et al., 2018b). Unlike fast-growing commercial broilers, these indigenous varieties are characterized by slower growth rates but are highly prized in Asian markets for their superior organoleptic properties. They are renowned for a firmer meat texture and a more intense chicken flavor, making them the preferred choice for premium traditional culinary applications, such as slow-cooked soups and stews (Zhao et al., 2024). Compared to white-feathered broilers such as Cobb, local breeds typically possess this superior flavor due to higher contents of intramuscular fat, amino acids, and essential fatty acids (Alsoufi et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024). However, the specific metabolic signatures underlying these differences remain poorly characterized.

The present study investigates the slaughter performance, breast meat quality traits, and untargeted metabolomics profiles to explore the specific metabolic characteristics of chicken breast muscle among six chicken breeds (Cobb broiler and five local Chinese breeds). Breast muscle samples were analyzed using HS-SPME-GC-MS to determine their metabolic composition. These findings lay the groundwork for optimizing poultry breeding strategies, improving meat quality, and regulating flavor profiles. They also investigate the molecular regulation of metabolism in chicken breast muscle.

Materials and methods

Sample preparation

All chicken experiments followed the guidelines of the Animal Ethics Committee of Sichuan Animal Science Academy (approval no. 0235/2022). Chickens were reared under standardized management conditions at a commercial poultry facility situated in Chengdu, Sichuan Province, China. To meet the specific nutritional requirements of their respective growth stages, all breeds were fed commercial diets formulated according to the Chinese Broiler Feeding Standards (NY/T 33-2004). Six chicken breeds were included in this study: Cobb broiler (KB), Heishui Phoenix Chicken (FW), Qiangshan Cloud Chicken (YD), Daheng Black-bone Chicken (DHW), Daheng Yellow Chicken (DHH), and Daheng Partridge Chicken (DHS). For each breed, a total of 10 males and 10 females were chosen at random from a larger flock of 1000 individuals based on average body weight to ensure group homogeneity and reared to their predetermined slaughter age: KB (42 d), FW (180 d), YD (180 d), DHW (70 d), DHH (70 d), and DHS (70 d). Before slaughter, chickens were deprived of feed for 12 h while drinking water remained continuously available. Humane euthanasia was performed by severing the jugular vein on one side of the neck. Slaughter performance was evaluated according to the methods described by Liu et al. (2024b). Following organ collection and weighing, the slaughter percentage, half-eviscerated yield, eviscerated yield, breast muscle percentage, leg muscle percentage, and abdominal fat percentage were determined. Visible fat and connective tissues were carefully dissected away. From each breed, six breast muscle samples (approximately 2 g each) were obtained from consistent anatomical locations. To ensure a balanced representation for the metabolomics analysis, these samples were equally divided between sexes (three males and three females). Each sample was promptly submerged in liquid nitrogen and subsequently preserved at −80°C until further untargeted metabolomic analysis.

Measurement of meat quality traits

Physicochemical and textural properties were evaluated across all twenty breast muscle samples, encompassing pH, color parameters (L*, a*, b*), water-holding capacity (expressed as drip and cooking losses), shear force, and fat content. The sample pH was quantified using a handheld pH meter (TEST0205, Shanghai, China) after slaughter, with triplicate readings taken from distinct anatomical sites within each sample to ensure data reliability. Instrumental color analysis was performed utilizing a Minolta chromameter (CR-300, Konica Minolta, Japan) calibrated against the CIE Lab system (Rich, 2006). Water-holding capacity was assessed by quantifying both drip loss and cooking loss. For drip loss, samples were stored at 4 °C for 24 h, with the result expressed as the proportional weight loss relative to the initial sample mass (Przybylski et al., 2022). To assess cooking loss, the samples were weighed before and after being subjected to a water bath heat treatment at 80°C, terminating the process once the temperature reached 71°C (Weimer et al., 2022). Shear force evaluation was conducted on a Digital Meat Tenderness Meter (Model C-LM3), with cylindrical cores collected perpendicular to the muscle fiber orientation to standardize a consistent shearing direction. Finally, fat content was quantified following the Soxhlet extraction method.

Metabolomics analysis

Metabolites were analyzed using HS-SPME-GC-MS. Approximately 1.0 g of each sample was weighed and transferred into a 20 mL headspace vial. Extraction was performed using a 50/30 μm DVB/CAR/PDMS SPME fiber and carried out by incubating the vials at 40 °C under agitation at 450 rpm (5 s on, 2 s off) for 40 min. The fiber was then exposed to the headspace for 40 min, and desorption was carried out at 250 °C for 10 min in the GC injection port. A DB-WAX capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm; Agilent J&W Scientific, Folsom, CA, USA) was used for chromatographic separation. The carrier gas consisted of ultra-high purity helium (≥99.999%) delivered at a constant flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. Splitless mode was used for injection, with an inlet temperature of 230°C and a solvent delay of 1.5 min. The temperature program for the oven was set as follows: holding at 35 °C for 3 min, then increasing at 5 °C/min to 60 °C, followed by 6 °C/min to 130 °C, and finally raising at 10 °C/min to 230 °C for a 10 min hold. Ionization was performed using electron impact (EI) at 70 eV. Temperatures for the ion source and quadrupole analyzer were set to 230 °C and 150 °C, respectively. Data were recorded in SCAN mode across a mass-to-charge (m/z) range of 20–650.

Data preprocessing and statistical analysis

Multivariate statistical analysis was conducted in R using the compiled data matrix. Initial analysis was conducted using principal component analysis (PCA) to visualize sample clustering and to confirm the consistency of the analytical system. Subsequently, orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) and partial least-squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) were applied to maximize group separation and identify discriminant metabolites, with model robustness validated by 7-fold cross-validation and 200 response permutation testing (RPT) to prevent overfitting. Metabolites contributing most significantly to group discrimination were ranked based on their variable importance of projection (VIP) scores from the OPLS-DA model, and those with VIP ≥ 1 and P < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed.

Statistical analysis

Data regarding slaughter performance and meat quality were analyzed using Microsoft Excel 2021. One-way ANOVA was employed to evaluate variations between groups, where significance was defined as P < 0.05. Additionally, Spearman N correlation was performed using the OmicStudio online platform (http://www.omicstudio.com) to elucidate associations among metabolites.

Correlation analysis among differential metabolite groups

The identified metabolites exhibited a highly intricate interaction network characterized by strong positive and negative correlations, as determined through correlational analysis (Fig. 6). Notably, in the comparison between FW and KB, acetone exhibited a significant positive correlation with N-benzoyl-dl-alanine (r = 0.710, P = 0.010) and octane,2,6,6-trimethyl- (r = 0.686, P = 0.014), suggesting a coordinated upregulation of ketone and aromatic amide metabolic pathways (Fig. 6A). In the YD vs. KB analysis, N-benzoyl-dl-alanine showed the highest positive correlation with octane,2,6,6-trimethyl- (r = 0.991, P < 0.001), indicating a tightly coupled metabolic pathway or co-regulation (Fig. 6B). A strong positive correlation was observed between azulene and benzyl alcohol in the DHW vs. KB analysis (r = 0.903, P < 0.001) (Fig. 6C). In the comparison between DHH and KB, levels of 2-methyl-butanoic acid were found to be highly positively correlated with acetone (r = 0.803, P = 0.002), while displaying a strong negative association with 1-eicosene (r = −0.830, P < 0.001), suggesting its dual involvement in lipid oxidation and flavor compound formation (Fig. 6D). In the DHS vs. KB comparison, butanedioic acid, phenyl- and tetradecane exhibited a highly positive association (r = 0.841, P < 0.001). In contrast, 2(3 h)-furanone, 5-ethyldihydro-, demonstrated a negative correlation with octanoic acid (r = −0.753, P = 0.005) and nonanoic acid (r = −0.824, P = 0.001) (Fig. 6E). Discussion

While the commercial breed (KB) exhibited superior carcass yield, characterized by higher eviscerated rates and breast muscle proportions, this advantage was accompanied by compromised meat quality, specifically manifested as elevated drip and cooking losses, alongside reduced fat content. The increased drip loss observed in KB likely reflects alterations in ionic strength and protein oxidation status (Huff and Lonergan, 2005; Logan et al., 2019), while the concomitant rise in cooking loss indicates a diminished capacity to retain water at high temperatures, a critical predictor of reduced juiciness (Qiao et al., 2001; Tan et al., 2022). Furthermore, the lower fat content in the commercial breed is particularly concerning, given that intramuscular fat is crucial for flavor development and greatly enhances the cooked meat’s tenderness and moisture (Wood et al., 2008). The noted decline in shear force is especially meaningful because this metric functions as a primary criterion for tenderness evaluation (Xie and Grossmann, 2025). In contrast, local breeds (FW, YD) demonstrated a more balanced muscle distribution and superior overall meat quality, implying that heritage breeds retain favorable organoleptic traits despite lower production yields. Collectively, these findings underscore the urgent need for breeding strategies that harmonize the pursuit of high carcass yield with the preservation of essential meat quality traits to satisfy diverse market demands.

Untargeted metabolomics analysis revealed distinct breed-specific differences in flavor-active metabolites between local chicken breeds (FW, YD, DHW, DHH, DHS) and the commercial KB breed, primarily characterized by downregulated off-flavor-associated metabolites in most local breeds and unique metabolic signatures in individual breeds. However, it is important to acknowledge that the gap in commercial slaughter ages (42 d for KB vs. 70 or 180 d for local breeds) acts as a confounding factor. Therefore, these distinct metabolic profiles and meat quality traits are likely a combined result of both genetic background and age-related physiological maturation. These variations reflect intrinsic divergences in core metabolic pathways (amino acid catabolism, lipid oxidation, microbial fermentation) with profound implications for breast meat sensory quality. The 2-methyl-butanoic acid, octanoic acid, and 2-nonanone in FW breast sample were significantly lower than those in KB. These metabolites drive undesirable aromas: 2-methyl-butanoic acid (cheesy/fatty notes) derives from valine degradation (Russo et al., 2020; Yimdee and Wang, 2016), octanoic acid (rancid odors) from amino acid transamination and oxidation (Lu et al., 2024), and 2-nonanone (fatty/grassy notes) from lipid β-oxidation (Dinh et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2025). Their concurrent downregulation indicates reduced off-flavor generation pathways in FW. The YD breast sample showed lower benzyl alcohol, octanoic acid, and 2,3-butanediol relative to KB. Benzyl alcohol (floral/plant-like odors) originates from amino acid degradation (Chen et al., 2016; Lu et al., 2025); octanoic acid’s role in lipid oxidation-mediated off-flavors aligns with FW findings (Huang et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2024); 2,3-butanediol is predominantly derived from lipid oxidation, a process that can be designated as the lipid oxidation phase or alternatively, the flavor formation phase (Wang et al., 2024b). DHW breast meat had significantly lower butanoic acid and 2-nonanone compared to KB. Butanoic acid (rancid butter/cheesy notes) is a high-OAV compound from lipolysis and branched-chain amino acid conversion (Liu et al., 2022; Yimdee and Wang, 2016); 2-nonanone’s downregulation reinforces reduced lipid oxidation in local breeds (Fujikawa et al., 2001; Oneill and Kinsella, 1987), highlighting their joint role in shaping DHW’s sensory profile. DHH breast sample displayed a unique profile: higher 1-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (a methyl ketone from fatty acid oxidation (Wang et al., 2022)) and lower 2,3-butanediol and 2-methyl-butanoic acid relative to KB. This suggests unique activation of methyl ketone biosynthesis alongside conserved off-flavor attenuation pathways. DHS breast meat exhibited lower 2,4-dimethylheptane and phenylethyl alcohol compared to KB. Phenylethyl alcohol (floral aromas) links to amino acid degradation (Wang et al., 2025), while 2,4-dimethylheptane (aliphatic hydrocarbon) may relate to lipid oxidation. Across local breeds, consistent downregulation of volatile fatty acids and methyl ketones relative to KB points to conserved off-flavor mitigation mechanisms dependent on amino acid catabolism and lipid oxidation (Huang et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2024; Mohamed et al., 2012; Yimdee and Wang, 2016). Unique signatures (e.g., DHH’s methyl ketone upregulation) reflect diverse flavor adaptations in local populations, consistent with findings in other muscle foods where these metabolites dominate off-flavor profiles (Liu et al., 2022; Mohamed et al., 2012; Yimdee and Wang, 2016). Furthermore, while the untargeted metabolomics data provide robust molecular evidence for flavor precursors, the absence of a descriptive human sensory evaluation panel remains a limitation of the current study. Future research incorporating quantitative descriptive analysis (QDA) will be necessary to directly validate how these breed-specific metabolic differences translate into consumer sensory perception.

Enrichment analysis of KEGG pathways revealed the functional significance of breed-specific differential metabolites, revealing that lipid metabolism-related pathways are the core regulatory nodes underlying the divergent sensory quality of breast muscle between local chicken breeds and Cobb broiler. Across all pairwise comparisons, a set of conserved and functionally critical pathways was enriched, which collectively shed light on the metabolic mechanisms shaping meat quality differences among breeds. Notably, synthesis and degradation of ketone bodies, fatty acid biosynthesis, and propanoate metabolism emerged as the most prominently enriched pathways across multiple breed comparisons. Ketone bodies, mainly generated from fatty acid oxidation in the liver, act as substitute energy substrates in animals (Cheng et al., 2018) and play important roles in multiple metabolic processes, including sterol formation, β-oxidation of fatty acids, new fatty acid production, and intracellular signal transduction (Cotter et al., 2013). The enrichment of this pathway implies breed-specific differences in energy metabolism and lipid homeostasis, as key ketone body components (e.g., 3-hydroxybutyric acid, acetone, acetoacetic acid (Puchalska and Crawford, 2017)) are lipid-derived metabolites whose abundance variations are tightly associated with metabolic flux balance (Niu et al., 2019). Propanoate metabolism, another widely enriched pathway, entails the transformation of propanoate into acetyl-CoA. Gut microbiota fermentation or tissue metabolism gives rise to this short-chain fatty acid. The generated acetyl-CoA then enters the TCA cycle or participates in branched-chain amino acid metabolism (Fei et al., 2025). Given that gut microbiota can ferment dietary carbohydrates (e.g., cellulose) for propanoate production, it exerts an indirect influence on muscle metabolism (Fei et al., 2025). Specifically, microbiota-derived short-chain fatty acids are absorbed through the intestinal epithelium, transported via the portal vein into the systemic circulation, and ultimately reach peripheral tissues such as skeletal muscle, where they are incorporated into local lipid metabolism and energy pathways (Ali et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2024a). Therefore, the enrichment of this pathway suggests potential crosstalk between gut microbial metabolism and muscle metabolic profiles across local and commercial chicken breeds. Additionally, butanoate metabolism, enriched in partial breed comparisons, regulates lipid synthesis via the β‑hydroxy-β-methylglutaryl CoA pathway (Liu et al., 2018a). Collectively, the consistent enrichment of pathways associated with lipid metabolism across breed comparisons highlights the conserved role of lipid homeostasis in regulating breed-specific meat quality. Fatty acids, as key products of these pathways, are not only critical for energy storage but also act as sources of volatile flavor precursors. Particularly, oxidation products such as aldehydes and ketones play a central role in determining the smell and taste of meat (Huang et al., 2022). Further evidence comes from prior investigations demonstrating that modulations in lipid metabolism pathways are capable of influencing the lipidomic signatures linked to flavor (Zhang et al., 2024), and dietary interventions affecting lipid oxidation (e.g., methionine supplementation) can reduce breast meat fat content and reshape flavor characteristics (Liu et al., 2024a; Wen et al., 2017). The additional enrichment of pathways, including lipoic acid metabolism and phenylalanine metabolism in specific comparisons, further reflects the diversity of metabolic regulatory mechanisms underlying meat quality differences, while the core lipid metabolism pathways provide potential targets for the genetic improvement of meat flavor in local chicken breeds.

Conclusions

This research compared commercial Cobb broilers with five local chicken breeds in terms of slaughter performance, meat quality attributes, and metabolic profiles. Our results confirmed that local breeds exhibited distinct phenotypic characteristics, including lower breast muscle rate but superior meat quality features such as lower drip loss, increased fat content, and improved meat color compared to the Cobb broiler. These advantages were closely associated with significant alterations in the breast muscle metabolome. Specifically, local breeds displayed distinct profiles of amino acids, fatty acids, and nucleotides that serve as essential precursors for meat flavor formation.

Further analysis revealed that these metabolic differences were primarily driven by changes in ketone body synthesis and degradation, fatty acid biosynthesis, and amino acid metabolism pathways. The enrichment of these pathways provides a molecular explanation for the observed variations in fat deposition and flavor potential among the breeds. The identified differential metabolites and key regulatory pathways offer valuable molecular markers for future breeding programs. Collectively, these findings provide a scientific basis for developing strategies that enhance meat flavor and nutritional quality in local chicken breeds while maintaining their unique growth characteristics.
