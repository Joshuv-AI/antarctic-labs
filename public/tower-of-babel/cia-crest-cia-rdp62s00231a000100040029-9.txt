Theme and Objectives

The electronics industry of the USSR has achieved
remarkable postwar growth in scientific and technical
capabilities and In the scale and range of product output.

As a consequence, the stature Of the industry has risen fro* a
its position as s major limiting factor in military weapons
systems programs to a level at which, its potential to
achieve major technological breaktfcrcnvJhs is estimated to
be approximately on a par with the Vest.

During this seme time period the USSR lias developed
an electronics production base which in absolute size and

It has, however, remained difficult to determine the extent

planning.

It is suggested, therefore, that whereas the absolute

The indicated overall postwar progress of the electronics
industry of the USSR, as reflected in scientific and technologi-
cal developments and in scale and range of product output, does
not appear to be reflected by the quality and quantity of
electronics products employed in existing basic telecoinauni.-
c at ions operating resources. If this observation is valid ,
then it appears that the USSR continues to suffer from priori-

The reasons for this situation are not clear. In fact, Soviet
realism normally would suggest that the situation would act
be permitted to exist.
