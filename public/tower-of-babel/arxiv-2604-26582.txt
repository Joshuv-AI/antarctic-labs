Star-Fusion: A Multi-modal Transformer Architecture for Discrete Celestial Orientation via Spherical Topology
May Hammad, Menah Hammad

Abstract
Reliable celestial attitude determination is a critical requirement for autonomous spacecraft navigation, yet traditional ”Lost-in-Space” (LIS) algorithms often suffer from high computational overhead and sensitivity to sensor-induced noise. While deep learning has emerged as a promising alternative, standard regression models are often confounded by the non-Euclidean topology of the celestial sphere and by the periodic boundary conditions of Right Ascension (RA) and Declination (Dec). In this paper, we present Star-Fusion , a multi-modal architecture that reformulates orientation estimation as a discrete topological classification task. Our approach leverages spherical K K -Means clustering to partition the celestial sphere into K K topologically consistent regions, effectively mitigating coordinate wrapping artifacts. The proposed architecture employs a tripartite fusion strategy: a SwinV2-Tiny transformer backbone for photometric feature extraction, a convolutional heatmap branch for spatial grounding, and a coordinate-based MLP for geometric anchoring. Experimental evaluations on a synthetic Hipparcos -derived dataset demonstrate that Star-Fusion achieves a Top-1 accuracy of 93.4% and a Top-3 accuracy of 97.8%. Furthermore, the model exhibits high computational efficiency, maintaining an inference latency of 18.4 ms on resource-constrained COTS hardware, making it a viable candidate for real-time onboard deployment in next-generation satellite constellations.



1
Introduction

Spacecraft attitude determination, the process of calculating a vehicle’s orientation relative to an inertial reference frame, is fundamental to mission success. Star trackers are widely considered the most accurate sensors for this task, as they provide absolute orientation by observing distant stellar constellations. However, the classical approach to star identification (Star-ID) typically involves a two-stage process: star centroiding followed by subgraph matching against a massive onboard catalog. Algorithms such as
Pyramid

]
and
Grid

[
2
]
are mathematically rigorous but struggle with the ”Lost-in-Space” scenario when faced with high-dynamic maneuvers, significant sensor noise, or ”false stars” from space debris.

1.1
Motivation and Problem Statement

The rise of Deep Representation Learning offers a promising alternative to classical Star-ID. By treating orientation as a vision task, models can, in principle, learn robust features invariant to noise. Nevertheless, existing deep learning models face a significant hurdle: the
spherical wrap-around problem
. Because RA ranges from
0^{\circ}
to
360^{\circ}
, two spatially adjacent orientations can be numerically distant in the coordinate space. Direct regression of these values often leads to unstable training and high error rates near the
0/360
boundary.

Furthermore, vanilla Vision Transformers (ViTs) often lack an explicit ”geometric prior.” While they excel at capturing global textures, they do not inherently respect the point-wise geometric relationships that define a star constellation. There is a clear need for a multi-modal architecture that can fuse the global attention of transformers with the local precision of geometric point clouds.

1.2
Contributions

In this work, we propose the
Star-Fusion
framework to bridge the gap between vision-based pattern recognition and geometric celestial mechanics. Our primary contributions are as follows:

•

Spherical Discretization Strategy:
We introduce a labeling method based on spherical
K
-Means clustering. By mapping RA/Dec to 3D unit vectors before clustering, we ensure that the model learns in a topologically consistent label space, resolving the periodic boundary problem.

•

Tri-branch Multi-modal Fusion:
We develop a hybrid architecture that fuses a SwinV2 transformer backbone with a synthetic heatmap CNN and a coordinate-vector MLP. This allows the model to leverage photometric, spatial, and numerical features simultaneously.

•

Edge-Ready Performance:
We provide a comprehensive performance analysis, demonstrating that our model meets the strict latency requirements of satellite Attitude Determination and Control Systems (ADCS) while running on low-power embedded hardware.

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W}

\Theta=(\alpha,\delta)

\phi:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

\phi(\alpha,\delta)=[\cos\delta\cos\alpha,\cos\delta\sin\alpha,\sin\delta]^{T}

(1)

The discrete label
y

K

\mathcal{C}
:

y=\text{argmin}_{i\in\{1,\dots,K\}}\|\phi(\Theta)-\mathbf{c}_{i}\|_{2}

3
Star-Fusion Architecture

The Star-Fusion model consists of three parallel encoders
E_{p},E_{g},E_{c}
representing Photometric, Geometric, and Coordinate domains, respectively.

3.1
Photometric Encoder (SwinV2)

The photometric encoder uses a Shifted Window Transformer (SwinV2-T) to extract features
\mathbf{h}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{768}
. The shifted window mechanism allows the model to compute cross-window attention, which is critical for identifying constellations that span the boundaries of the image sensor.

3.2
Geometric and Coordinate Fusion

Auxiliary geometric features
\mathbf{h}_{g}
are extracted via a shallow CNN from a synthetic heatmap
H
, while coordinate features
\mathbf{h}_{c}
are processed through a 3-layer MLP. The latent representation
\mathbf{z}
is computed as:

\mathbf{z}=\sigma(\mathbf{W}_{f}[\mathbf{h}_{p}\parallel\mathbf{h}_{g}\parallel\mathbf{h}_{c}]+\mathbf{b}_{f})

(3)

where
\parallel
denotes concatenation and
\sigma
is the ReLU activation.

Experiments and Results

4.1
Dataset and Training Configuration

The model was evaluated on a synthetic dataset of 50,000 star-field images generated via the
Hipparcos
catalog, with 40,000 images for training and 10,000 for validation. Images were rendered at a
1024\times 1024
resolution and downsampled to
128\times 128
for the photometric branch. Training was conducted using a cross-entropy loss function:

\mathcal{L}=-\sum_{i=1}^{K}y_{i}\log(\hat{y}_{i})+\lambda\|\theta\|_{2}

(4)

where
\lambda=0.05
represents the weight decay coefficient. The model achieved convergence at epoch 64, with a final training loss of
0.142
and a validation loss of
0.187
.

5
Multi-modal Fusion Logic

The Star-Fusion architecture employs a late-fusion strategy to integrate heterogeneous data streams. This approach ensures that each encoder Photometric (
E_{p}
), Geometric (
E_{g}
), and Coordinate (
E_{c}
) can develop independent internal representations before being mapped into a joint latent space.

5.1
Formal Feature Integration

Given an input triplet
(I,H,\mathbf{s})
, where
I
is the raw image,
H
is the synthetic heatmap, and
\mathbf{s}
is the star coordinate vector, the fusion process is defined as:

\mathbf{h}_{fusion}=\text{LayerNorm}([E_{p}(I)\parallel E_{g}(H)\parallel E_{c}(\mathbf{s})])

(5)

where
\parallel
denotes the concatenation operator. We utilize
Layer Normalization
immediately following concatenation to prevent any single modality (particularly the high-dimensional transformer output) from dominating the gradient flow during backpropagation.

5.5
Error Analysis

An analysis of the confusion matrix reveals that 62% of misclassifications occurred between adjacent clusters at the celestial equator. This suggests that while the model is robust, the quantization error at the
K=12
boundary is the primary source of inaccuracy. This justifies future research into overlapping cluster memberships or soft-labeling.

6
Discussion

The performance gains observed in the Star-Fusion architecture provide compelling evidence for the necessity of multi-modal integration in celestial navigation. Our analysis indicates that the three processing branches play complementary roles in the feature extraction process.

6.1
Synergistic Feature Extraction

The
Photometric Branch
(SwinV2) demonstrates a unique ability to capture global contextual cues, such as stellar density gradients and unresolved galactic background noise, which provide a ”visual signature” for specific celestial regions. However, transformers can occasionally be indifferent to precise pixel-level shifts.

This is mitigated by the
Geometric Heatmap Branch
, which enforces spatial grounding. By representing stars as Gaussian kernels, we translate sparse point data into a dense spatial manifold that the CNN can effectively process. Finally, the
Coordinate MLP
acts as a numerical anchor, providing the model with high-fidelity geometric ratios that are invariant to photometric fluctuations. The fusion of these disparate data types allows the model to maintain high confidence even when one modality is degraded for instance, during high-exposure ”blooming” events where photometric details are lost but centroids remain detectable.

6.2
The Discretization Trade-off

The transition from continuous regression to discrete classification via spherical
K
-Means clustering represents a significant departure from traditional deep-learning-based star trackers. This approach effectively ”smoothes” the loss landscape, allowing the model to converge on a global orientation without the instability typically associated with angular wrap-around. However, we observe that the model’s confidence scores tend to dip near the Voronoi boundaries of the clusters. This suggests that the model is internalizing the geometric proximity of adjacent classes, a property we intend to exploit in future work through soft-labeling and manifold learning.

7
Limitations

Despite the robust performance of Star-Fusion, several limitations must be addressed before deployment in a production Attitude Determination and Control System (ADCS).

•

Quantization Error:
With
K=12
orientation classes, each class represents a significantly large solid angle of the celestial sphere. While this is sufficient for a ”Lost-in-Space” coarse acquisition, it does not currently provide the arc-second precision required for fine-pointing maneuvers (e.g., telescope stabilization).

•

Sensor-Specific Biases:
The current model is trained primarily on synthetic data derived from the
Hipparcos
catalog. Real-world sensor effects, such as non-uniform dark current, lens distortion, and radiation-induced ”hot pixels,” may introduce a domain gap. While data augmentation (Gaussian noise, magnitude clipping) was employed, on-orbit performance may vary.

•

High-Dynamic Scenarios:
In scenarios involving high angular velocities (
>5^{\circ}/s
), motion blur can cause stars to appear as streaks. While the Coordinate MLP is theoretically more robust to such deformations, the SwinV2 backbone’s performance under extreme motion-blur kernels remains a subject for further empirical validation.

8
Future Work

Future iterations of this research will explore
Hierarchical Spherical Clustering
, where a primary classifier identifies a coarse region, and a secondary specialized head performs fine-grained regression. Additionally, we aim to implement
Temporal Attention
across consecutive frames to utilize the satellite’s trajectory as a prior for attitude propagation.

8.1
Computational Complexity

The total parameter count of Star-Fusion is approximately 29.4M. On an NVIDIA Jetson Orin (simulating spacecraft hardware), the inference latency is
\sim
12ms per image, satisfying real-time navigation requirements for high-dynamic maneuvers.

9
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced
Star-Fusion
, a novel multi-modal transformer architecture designed to address the topological and computational challenges of spacecraft attitude determination. By reformulating orientation estimation as a discrete classification task over a clustered celestial sphere, we successfully mitigated the periodic discontinuities inherent in angular coordinate regression.

Our experimental results demonstrate that the tripartite fusion of photometric (SwinV2), spatial (CNN Heatmap), and numerical (Coordinate MLP) features yields a robust representational capacity. The model achieved a Top-1 accuracy of 93.4%, representing a 9.2% improvement over unimodal vision-based baselines. Furthermore, with an inference latency of 18.4 ms on embedded hardware, Star-Fusion satisfies the stringent real-time constraints required for high-dynamic satellite maneuvers.

The implications of this research are particularly relevant for the ”New Space” industry, where resource-constrained platforms such as CubeSats require efficient, ”Lost-in-Space” robust navigation systems that can operate without heavy database overhead. By offloading the geometric complexity to a pre-trained multi-modal network, we provide a scalable framework for autonomous celestial navigation.

9.1
Future Directions

While this study establishes a robust foundation for discrete orientation, several avenues for future research remain.

1.

Coarse-to-Fine Refinement:
We intend to implement a recursive regression head that utilizes the predicted class as a spatial prior to achieve sub-degree angular precision.

2.

Temporal Consistency:
Leveraging Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) or Temporal Transformers could allow the model to utilize the satellite’s orbital mechanics as a temporal constraint, further smoothing orientation transitions.

3.

On-Orbit Domain Adaptation:
Future work will explore self-supervised fine-tuning techniques to adapt the model to specific sensor noise profiles directly in the space environment, ensuring long-term mission reliability.
