An energy–trust balanced and attack-resilient cluster head election scheme for resource-constrained wireless sensor networks
Authors: R. Gopi, C. Gunasundari, B. Bazeer Ahamed, G. Sathya, K. B. Bhaskar, T. R. Priyadharshini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) demand optimized Cluster Head (CH) election mechanisms to balance energy efficiency and communication security. Traditional protocols such as LEACH and ISSA rely on probabilistic, or metaheuristic approaches that do not fully integrate security metrics into CH selection, leading to increased vulnerability and uneven energy depletion. This paper proposes an Energy-Driven Cluster Head Secure Selection (ED-CHSS) algorithm that integrates residual-energy modeling, node trust-index evaluation, and lightweight symmetric cryptographic primitives for secure intra-cluster communication. The election process of CH is designed based on an optimization model of multiple parameters, including the residual energy ratio, communication cost and trust weight coefficient. CH candidacy probability is dynamically adapted by the node density and link stability. The simulation results show that ED-CHSS decreases average energy dissipation by 28–32%, increases network lifetime by about 38–42%, and increases the attack resistance of the network against selective forwarding and sinkhole attacks. The proposed model provides secure and energy balanced clustering for mission critical WSN deployments.

Introduction

Background

WSN has now been widely used in different fields, including smart cities, medical field, and environment monitoring1. Security and CH selection are major aspects of WSNs2. Selecting the proper CH has a positive effect on data aggregation, network lifetime, and energy consumption3. Restrictive security policies protect users from attacks and ensure the confidentiality of data sent4. However, existing CH selection approaches are not scalable or energy-efficient, and existing security approaches do not account for emerging threats5. Numerous complicated algorithms have been developed to address these determinants; however, few have considered a combination of integrity and efficiency in their communication protocols6.

In addition, many algorithms do not account for the need for real-time adjustment, which is not surprising given the variability of WSNs7. The latest research has examined lighter yet stronger encryption methods and adaptive, dynamic selection of CHs to achieve more efficient, secure networks8. Based on these recent developments9, this study proposes a way to make the entire WSN process, and parts of it, more effective and more secure simultaneously. Given the increased need for large-scale WSN installations, it is crucial to have both a secure and fast network10. This study is designed to create an all-in-one approach to solve the problems of CH selection and security for multiple WSN applications and face up to emerging threats.

Problem statement

Current methods and security measures used for selecting Cluster heads cannot simultaneously improve power efficiency, scalability, and attack resistance. Existing methods do not incorporate network security features or have real-time change capabilities. The focus of this research is to improve the performance and security level for WSN by addressing these limitations using a hybrid method called Enhanced Dynamic Cluster Head Selection and Security (ED-CHSS). The proposed framework is a clear contrast to traditional clustering protocols: it is the only protocol that considers energy-aware cluster head selection, dynamic scheduling and trust-based security mechanisms to reduce energy consumption and still provide reliable communication in large-scale WSNs.

Research novelty

The proposed ED-CHSS system integrates energy efficiency, trust assessment, and dynamic scheduling into a single cluster-head selection system. The most important novelty is that trust-aware decision-making is dynamically coupled with real-time scheduling and adaptive weighting, enabling the system to respond efficiently to network changes and malicious activity.

Related works

This section provides a short overview of research exploring possible solutions to more sustainable and safer WSNs. This section covers many topics, including clustering the CH selection, implementing security in networks, providing adaptable networks in real time, a more extensive network, and more. Currently, researchers in this field have addressed issues such as large-scale, dynamic WSNs, longer networks, secure data transfer, and energy consumption.

Cluster head selection algorithms in wireless sensor networks

The authors in this article evaluate various methods for generating clusters and cluster heads in wireless sensor networks. Researchers are looking for less energy-intensive clustering methods, such as LEACH11. The issues of scalability in WSNs and energy consumption in different scenarios have to be solved. The authors highlight the need to use flexible approaches to boost network capacity.

This study proposes an Improved Energy-efficient Sparrow Search Algorithm (IESSA) to select CHs. The goal of the algorithm is to extend the network’s longevity while using less energy than alternatives12. The study demonstrated that current cluster head (CH) selection methods are ineffective in large networks. The IESSA performs better at energy savings and network performance than traditional approaches.

Energy efficiency and network lifetime in WSNs

The current study advocates a new method to prolong the life of WSNs. The more energy a network consumes, the longer the network can endure. The proposed model employs energy-specific algorithms to reduce energy consumption13. The findings indicate that the proposed model extends the life of the WSN and provides a more dependable means of transferring information across networks. The findings also discuss the importance of conserving energy and present examples of how real-world implementations have enhanced network stability.

The study has discussed a clustering method that requires less energy to prolong the life of the WSN and related topics. The protocol employs an alternative method for forming clusters that changes based on the network state and operates in an adaptive clustering environment14. The study has presented empirical evidence indicating that the methods discussed are appropriate for large-scale deployments and real-time requirements, resulting in lower energy consumption and better overall WSN performance.

Security mechanisms in wireless sensor networks

Regarding to a trust management-based dynamic security for WSNs, this paper elaborates the shortcomings of the classical solutions. It provides a trustful solution to detect attack opportunities in real time15. The authors think that this is a suitable method for large WSNs with limited capabilities, as it makes it more challenging for attackers to hijack the WSN without consuming too much energy.

The authors of the review are mainly interested in the protection of WSNs by ML.. Their review outlines significant limitations due to processing costs and difficulties with live utilization applications16. The speaker’s general conclusion is that machine learning models can be used to detect suspicious behavior and minimize invasions. The authors point out that ML-based solutions can improve security with minimal impact on performance and energy requirements.

Real-time adaptability in WSNs

The Energy-efficient Dynamic Adaptive State-based Scheduling (EDASS) method for WSNs17 is shown, and it uniquely adapts the scheduling of autonomous nodes to the changing characteristics of the network. The authors demonstrate that EDASS is a network system that requires less energy while improving network performance, especially when the network is dynamic. Since sensor networks will continue to grow in scale without adequate awareness in real applications, this system helps facilitate both efficiency and reliability while also adapting in real time.

This paper discusses considerations for media access control (MAC) protocols, with recommendations focused on AdaptiveHART, an IIoT standard. This protocol is designed to adapt to current traffic conditions, leveraging WSNs for industrial use and focusing on resource use18. They found that AdaptiveHART is ideally suited for industrial IoT applications where network and energy stability, and time, are extremely important, as it offers lower latency and greater reliability for information transmission.

Scalability and performance challenges in large-scale WSNs

The hierarchical routing and clustering approach, a method used to manage large WSNs, has been discussed in this paper. Through the data routing and cluster head creation processes within a protocol, scalability and reliability issues are addressed19. The method has been proven to provide energy savings, as well as data routing accuracy. This can be useful for large-scale networks of sensors that cover a wide range of different operational contexts and have a higher communication burden.

The purpose of the paper is based on the fact that Time-Slotted Channel Hopping (TSCH) is a way to make software defined industrial WSNs more scalable. It addresses network congestion and scalability, offers a scalable protocol and describes how it operates in large industrial settings20. Their results conclude that TSCH and SDNs effectively improve network performance, enhance communication reliability, and balance load in challenging industrial environments.

The studies examine issues with wireless sensor networks, such as energy consumption, scalability and security. They offered recommendations for improving networks to achieve better longevity and reliability. Related works summary.

Even though current protocols like LEACH, HEED, DEEC and Secure-CH focus on different components of clustering, they lack a single protocol that combines adaptive weighting, dynamic trust assessment and schedule conscious cluster head (CH) selection in one decision making model. In particular, HEED and DEEC concentrate on energy-conscious CH selection, but do not include security-conscious trust adaptation, whereas Secure-CH adds trust filtering, but does not optimize multi-criteria weights and scheduling on a dynamic basis. Conversely, ED-CHSS proposes an architecture of strong coupling where (i) energy, communication cost, and trust are jointly determined by an adaptive weighting scheme, (ii) trust assessment is dynamically maintained and it directly affects CH choice, and (iii) transmission timing is incorporated in the clustering decision making. The integrated interplay between layers enables ED-CHSS to realize energy efficiency, resistance against malicious nodes, and stability in communication, which three aspects are not achievable by the current protocols.

Proposed ED-CHSS methodology

The goal of ED-CHSS is to enhance the performance of WSN to solve key issues like capacity, secure messaging, dynamic scheduling, and energy efficiency. The system utilizes intelligent algorithms to guarantee protected communication, energy effective information exchange and most effective cluster heads choosing, all of which will add to network durability and overall performance.

The suggested ED-CHSS could improve the performance of wireless sensor networks, and this system takes into account aspects such as security, scalability, data transfer, and energy use. Step 2: energy–trust aware cluster head selection in ED-CHSS

In WSNs, the energy-efficient cluster-head selection technique is very crucial to enhance the overall performance and saving energy. As it considers network conditions at runtime, and selects cluster chiefs based on energy, it could lead to premature node failure and a shorter network lifetime.

The basic concept of this scheme is to select cluster heads according to their remaining energy, distance from other nodes and workload of the network23. Dynamic mechanism is implemented to select cluster heads to avoid consuming too much energy in the network. Nodes that have high energy and are close to each other are shown in Fig. 2, and are weighted more. The selection of cluster heads carefully ensures that the distance between cluster head and nodes is minimum. This uses less energy for communication. This uses more energy. This approach significantly boosts the overall performance of the network by extending its lifespan, minimizing communication overhead, and optimizing data aggregation efficiency. The ED-CHSS algorithm improves the energy efficiency by utilizing the residual node energy and adds dynamic scheduling and trust-based cluster head selection. Unlike the traditional LEACH protocol, which includes a probabilistic selection mechanism, the ED-CHSS adopts the energy, distance and trust measurement based weighted decision mechanism. Also, adaptive scheduling eliminates redundant transmissions, and load-conscious clustering avoids energy imbalance, thereby greatly increasing the network lifetime.Fig. 2Representation of energy-efficient cluster head selection.

Representation of energy-efficient cluster head selection.

Adaptive weighting mechanism

Residual energy, node density and the trust levels are used in a heuristic rule-based mechanism inspired by fuzzy logic to adaptively adjust the weighting coefficients. The input parameters, including residual energy, node density and level of trust, are translated into linguistic variables (low, medium, high). The energy efficiency, communication costs and trust are dynamically reweighted as per pre-defined fuzzy rules. The defuzzified output yields in real time the best weights, which results in a more balanced and reliable cluster head selection in case of different traffic loads, node failures, and even possible attacks.

The fixed weighting is replaced by the dynamically calculated weighting values by the adaptive system according to the current network conditions. Modulation of energy, distance and trust parameters weights is performed by applying fuzzy inference to the system. This helps to improve flexibility, decision accuracy and performance stability, even in highly dynamic or hostile wireless sensor network environment.

Step 4: scalable clustering strategy for large-scale WSNs in ED-CHSS

The main purpose of ED-CHSS’s secure data transmission method is to ensure that all data sent over the wireless sensor network is authentic, complete, and transmitted privately. In a trust management system, nodes are assigned trust values based on their past behavior and interactions with other nodes26.

Trust levels are used to prevent bad or untrustworthy nodes from participating in the data transmission process shown in Fig. 4. This protects against man-in-the-middle attacks, data tampering, and denial-of-service attacks. A trust-based strategy is employed to keep data safe. Nodes with high trust levels are allowed to communicate sensitive data. This strategy reduces the likelihood that infected nodes will disrupt the network. The system has trust-evaluation algorithms that adjust trust levels based on node behavior. This makes communication in the WSN more reliable and secure by ensuring that data remains protected. Lightweight cryptography uses symmetric-key encryption to reduce computational overhead on resource-constrained WSN nodes. The Cluster Head (CH) generates and distributes session keys to member nodes during cluster formation. Periodically, keys are changed to improve security. Safe key communication is carried out using pre-shared keys and trust-based authentication, providing confidentiality, integrity and efficient communication within the cluster.Fig. 4Path diagram for secure data transmission with trust management method.

Path diagram for secure data transmission with trust management method.

A weighted-average model is used to calculate the Trust Index, which is based on the packet-forwarding success rate, the delivery rate, and the level of cooperation. The parameters have weights based on their significance, and the sum of these scores determines the node’s trustworthiness. Trust values are dynamically updated after each communication round, allowing precise identification of unreliable or malicious nodes at minimal computational cost.

Threat model

The ED-CHSS system assumes a semi-trusted wireless sensor network, in which nodes can be compromised to become malicious. Attackers can drop packets, modify routing information, and spoof identities, but are constrained by energy and computational resources. The base station is considered secure, and nodes communicate using pre-shared keys. The model accounts for selective forwarding, sinkhole and Sybil attacks as the main threats. These attacks interfere with packet transmission, routing, and network topology. To alleviate them, the suggested trust mechanism considers node actions in terms of forwarding and cooperation. Bad nodes are identified and assigned very low trust scores, and they are not considered when selecting cluster heads or communicating.

Implementation details

Dataset description

The Wireless Sensor Networks Dataset from the Kaggle project provides details on the network’s layout, the energy utilization of nodes that collect data, and how they communicate with one another. In this study secure data advancement, dynamic scheduling and picking cluster heads with lesser energy is tested in the dataset. It provides an opportunity to experiment and adapt the proposed system in a real situation, and studies its performance in terms of data handling and eco-friendliness29. Kaggle-based WSN data are used as a starting point to establish node characteristics, including energy states, communication strategies and network structure. Malicious behavior is synthetically added to the NS-3/OMNeT + + environment to simulate dynamic attack scenarios, including sinkhole and selective forwarding. In particular, a set of nodes is dynamically configured to change packet forwarding behavior, selectively drop packets, or misdirect traffic across a series of simulation rounds, simulating a real-time attack scenario.

Simulation tools

The simulations were run on an ordinary computing system with an Intel i7/i5 processor (or a similar one), 16–32 GB of RAM, and a Linux-based operating system. It was implemented using the NS-3 and OMNeT + + simulation tools. Because of the event-driven, computationally sparse nature of simulations of wireless sensor networks, there was no need to use specialized GPU hardware to implement the proposed ED-CHSS algorithm. A portion of the nodes are set to be malicious, 5% to 20% of the total network, and the main results are given when the attack intensity is at the moderate level of 10%. Malicious behavior is introduced by modifying the packet handling at the node level during run-time: selective forwarding nodes drop 30–70% of packets, blackhole nodes drop all packets, sinkhole nodes advertise falsified high quality routes that attract traffic, the Sybil attackers duplicate multiple identities using duplicated node IDs and the wormhole attack is emulated by a low latency tunnel between colluding nodes. The simulation runs for 3000 rounds—attacks are turned on after a period of stabilization (round 500) and continue until the end of the simulation. Static and dynamic attack scenarios are used, where in dynamic attacks some nodes will switch from normal to unsafe function at regular times to mimic stealthy attackers. The proposed trust-based mechanism continuously observes the forwarding behavior, delivery success and cooperation rate to find the anomalies, and the nodes that are identified malicious are given low trust value and excluded from the cluster head selection and routing. Because this parameterized configuration guarantees that the results for the packet delivery ratio and detection rate are reproducible and represent realistic adversarial conditions.

Hardware configuration

The proposed ED-CHSS framework lacks any machine learning components for trust assessment. The index of trust is measured by a lightweight model that uses packet delivery, forwarding behavior, and cooperation metrics. Consequently, GPU acceleration was not necessary, and all simulations were run on the CPU in the NS-3 and OMNeT + + environments.

Tuning of baseline protocol and parameters

To be fair in comparison, the baseline protocols such as LEACH, DEEC, HEED and Secure-CH are executed in the same simulation environment and parameter settings. Each protocol uses the same network parameters, such as node distribution, initial energy, packet size, and transmission range. In LEACH, the probabilistic thresholds for cluster head selection determine the percentage of cluster heads. The DEEC selection of cluster heads varies depending on residual and average energy. HEED selects cluster heads based on residual energy and communication cost. Simple trust-based filtering is implemented in Secure-CH. Every protocol is optimized using generally recommended parameter values from the literature, and several simulation runs are carried out to obtain stable, unbiased results.

A paired t-test is used to assess the statistical significance of the difference between the ED-CHSS and baseline protocols based on the outcomes of 10–20 independent simulation runs. The test is used to evaluate key performance indicators such as energy consumption, network lifetime and packet delivery ratio. The acquired p-values (< 0.05) indicate that the changes ED-CHSS has received are statistically significant and not due to random variation.

Component-wise contribution analysis

Three important elements affect the performance improvement of ED-CHSS. The most significant contribution is the trust mechanism, which excludes malicious or unreliable nodes, thereby enhancing the packet-delivery ratio and the detection rate. The energy model ensures balanced energy consumption across nodes, prolonging the network’s lifespan. The adaptive scheduling mechanism will increase throughput and reduce congestion by optimally varying transmission in response to network conditions.

Trade-off analysis

The incorporation of trust assessment enhances network security and reliability, but it adds some computational overhead due to the constant updates of trust. Adaptive scheduling, on the other hand, enhances responsiveness to fluctuating network traffic, efficiency, and scalability while potentially leading to slight fluctuations in the system in the face of highly dynamic traffic. Moreover, the proposed ED-CHSS achieves a desirable security, energy efficiency, and stability.

To confirm that ED-CHSS outperforms the baseline protocols, we used paired t-tests to compare the baseline protocols with ED-CHSS on the main performance measures of energy consumption, network lifetime, packet delivery ratio (PDR), detection rate, and end-to-end delay. The experiments were repeated in a series of runs with varying random seeds and comparisons were made using mean values. In both instances, the p-values are less than the significance level of 0.05, which proves that the improvements observed are statistically significant and not because of random variation.

The computational complexity of ED-CHSS algorithm is analyzed by dividing it into operational phases. The selection of Cluster Head and trust evaluation in the Primary Cluster has linear time complexity of O(n) because all nodes are scanned and local metrics like residual energy, communication cost, trust value etc. are calculated. This is done by adding another sorting or ranking operation which has an O(nlogn) complexity to select optimal cluster head candidates based on their computed scores. Thus, the total running time of ED-CHSS is O(nlogn), and the main operational cost per round is roughly linear in practice. This difference is key to the observed scalability in large-scale wireless sensor networks.

The findings show that every element plays an important role towards the overall performance. Losing the trust mechanism results in an area of significant reduction in detection rate and percentage of packet delivery because unreliable nodes are added. Adaptive weighting is also more energy efficient because the model is no longer able to dynamically focus on the important parameters in different network conditions. Similarly, the lack of awareness of the scheduling contributes to the latency of communications and the overhead of control due to lack of coordination in transmitting data. The testing results indicate that the ED-CHSS system is superior to the existing systems, as it is capable of adapting to network changes, reducing energy consumption, and providing optimal protection of data. The system does not reveal that the proposed mechanism for selecting and organizing cluster heads is effective, and it guarantees that throughput and stability of clusters do not decrease with the increase of the number of cluster nodes.

Conclusion and future work

Overall, the ED-CHSS system achieves superior performance in all metrics compared to other systems like AdaptiveHART, TSCH, LEACH, ISSA and EDASS. The advantages of this technology are of great relevance for large Wireless Sensor Networks, as it demands very low power consumption, generates a high data volume, has a long lifetime, can be extended, is safe and flexible. By carefully controlling trust and selecting cluster heads, ED-CHSS uses less energy, extends network lifetime, and enhances data security. In the real world, there are lower operating costs, more robust networks, and better resource use.

Future work

In the future, ED-CHSS will integrate with Internet of Things (IoT) systems to better manage devices, employ edge computing to reduce latency, and make it easier to adapt to network changes, including when people move about. To make the system even better for a wider range of large-scale applications, researchers can explore hybrid energy management for long-term use in remote areas and improve security through stronger encryption.
