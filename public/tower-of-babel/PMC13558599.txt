Intelligent evaluation and feedback mechanism based on student behavior data in the context of integrated education
Authors: Jing Wang, Yuanzhe Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Artificial intelligence has been promoted in education, but traditional teaching evaluation and feedback mechanisms still cannot start from students’ classroom behavior, resulting in poor accuracy and low efficiency. In integrated education, educational goals not only focus on imparting knowledge, but also emphasize fairness and respect for individual differences among students. To improve the accuracy and fairness of teaching evaluation, this paper designs an intelligent evaluation system that integrates student behavior data. On the basis of You Only Look Once version 5 (YOLOv5) and the Deep Sort algorithm, this system has built an intelligent model that accurately tracks student behavior and evaluates teaching effectiveness by analyzing key behavioral indicators such as students’ overall classroom head up rate and concentration. The results showed that the intelligent model could more effectively recognize students’ behavior, with target recognition accuracy and tracking accuracy reaching a target detection mAP@0.5 of 85.13% and a tracking precision of 90.78%. In addition, the intelligent evaluation system quantified teaching effectiveness, with a mean comprehensive classroom head up rate of 71.85%, an increase of 8.69% compared to traditional quantitative methods. Intelligent evaluation is more sensitive than traditional methods. This system can accurately measure an individual’s participation in learning and provide personalized learning feedback for teachers. The application of this technology in integrated education environments offers strong support for achieving more detailed evaluation and feedback and has important practical value for promoting educational equity and implementing personalized teaching.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55051-5.

Introduction

Currently, inclusive education has received widespread attention and rapidly developed in the global education field. This educational model places students with different learning needs (including disabled students and students with special educational needs) in a general education environment to learn together1. Although the promotion of integrated education concepts has achieved certain results, teachers still face many challenges in actual teaching, especially in the implementation of teaching evaluation and feedback. In integrated education classrooms, teachers often need to address significant differences in students’ attention levels and imbalances in classroom participation2. This diverse learning demand forces teachers to adopt flexible strategies in teaching to satisfy the personalized learning requirements of various students. However, traditional evaluation methods often rely on teachers’ subjective judgments or standardized tests. These methods have limitations in flexibility and adaptability, and cannot fully respond to the rapidly changing learning environment in the classroom3. Existing assessment tools often cannot record and analyze students’ classroom performance in real-time and comprehensively. This deficiency leads to a lack of reliable data support for teachers when formulating teaching intervention measures, ultimately affecting the implementation of personalized education. Traditional assessment tools generally only reflect students’ performance at specific time points, and cannot comprehensively capture their learning status in different contexts. This limitation makes it hard for teachers to make teaching adjustments built on the individual demands of students4.

Multiple studies have optimized teaching practices through data-driven methods. These studies collectively demonstrate the application value of data science in educational settings, covering areas such as learning intervention, Teacher-Student (T-S) interaction, teacher behavior classification, and teacher training. Anwar N et al. identified key indicators such as attendance and participation that affect academic performance through quantitative data analysis, qualitative interviews, and machine learning algorithms for personalized teaching interventions. This study utilized data science analysis and predictive models to enhance student learning outcomes, supporting personalized teaching interventions and demonstrating the model effectiveness5. Ma X et al. used a bottom-up lag sequence analysis method to encode teaching videos to address the insufficient research on T-S interaction modes in smart classrooms. This acting has summarized four progressive interactive modes to promote students’ higher-order thinking development and educational digital transformation, confirming the practicality of this approach6. Jiang S et al. explored the classroom practice mode for teachers and used the k-means clustering analysis method based on the classroom evaluation system to classify more than 600 teachers into different groups. From the dimensions of T-S relationship and classroom management, three types of teacher groups were identified, verifying the feasibility of this analysis7. McGuire et al. found through investigation that pre-service primary school teachers generally lack strategic knowledge to cope with challenging behaviors, in response to the insufficient training of individual student behavior management abilities in pre-service primary school teacher training. They were eager to obtain more practical opportunities, which indicated the lack of individualized behavior management training in current teacher education8.

With the development of intelligent technology, many scholars have optimized deep learning models to achieve accurate behavior recognition in different scenarios. Wang S et al. addressed the occlusion and missed detection issues of wearing masks in videos by introducing a cross-stage structure and a convolutional block attention module in the small version of YOLOv5 to reduce feature learning complexity and enhance small object detection capabilities. The Hungarian algorithm and the Deep Sort (DS) algorithm with improved directional difference factor were used to associate intra-frame targets. When mean Average Precision (mAP) at Intersection over Union (IoU) 0.5 (mAP@0.5) reached 72.39%, Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) and multiple object tracking precision improved by 18.0% and 3.40%9. Hartono et al. developed an automated football video analysis system that combines YOLOv5, k-means clustering, and optical flow methods to address player overlap and viewpoint changes. The system was used to track players, identify teams, and estimate speed. The accuracy of the system testing reached 92.5%10. Wang Y et al. improved the efficiency of Online Classroom Learning Behavior (OCLB) recognition by analyzing the concept of Feature Data Mining (FDM), and extracted features of OCLB data. It utilized principal component analysis to preprocess data features and constructed an OCLB recognition model built on FDM, which was effective11. Pabba C et al. designed an intelligent classroom visual analysis system to achieve real-time monitoring of individual student behavior and overall classroom behavior evaluation. The training accuracy of this system reached 91.3%12.

Although the application of artificial intelligence technology in educational evaluation is increasing, existing technological applications are mainly focused on the fundamental level and have not yet fully explored the deep connection between student behavior data and teaching effectiveness. For example, attendance management or simple emotion recognition can be achieved through facial recognition. To this end, this study innovatively builds an optimized YOLOv5 and DS algorithm intelligent evaluation system, which is used to achieve accurate recognition and quantitative analysis of classroom behavior of different groups of students. This study aims to enhance the efficiency of teaching evaluation and promote the implementation of intelligent feedback in education.

Teaching intelligent evaluation system based on deep learning technology

Student behavior data collection and intelligent model design

This study collects data from several integrated education pilot middle schools in a city along the southeast coast, covering mixed classrooms of ordinary students and 5% of special needs students. The data come from regular teaching videos recorded by panoramic monitoring devices deployed inside the classroom. Raw materials that meet the following criteria are strictly screened: including complete T-S interaction, and the camera can clearly capture students’ facial and body movements; Non-typical teaching scenarios such as breaks and exams during regular teaching periods have been removed13. The flowchart of the teaching evaluation system structure based on intelligent models is displayed in Fig. 1.

Fig. 1Structure of the teaching evaluation system.

Structure of the teaching evaluation system.

In Fig. 1, an intelligent model is constructed to analyze student behavior based on the collected behavior data of middle school students. This model is based on YOLOv5 and DS algorithm to improve target recognition accuracy and tracking accuracy. To balance data granularity and processing efficiency, video sampling adopts a hierarchical sampling strategy, which captures video keyframes at intervals of every minute14. The data annotation stage adopts a three-level standardization process. Firstly, student behavior is segmented into multiple behaviors based on the Student Teacher (S-T) teaching analysis method, and then double-blind annotation is performed through the Label-Studio platform. The data are jointly verified for consistency by special education experts and computer vision engineers15. The final dataset A consists of 28,500 annotated images, segmented into 80% training set and 20% testing set, ensuring a balanced proportion of special needs students in both groups. All surveillance video recordings have undergone ethical review by the school and have been desensitized to protect student privacy16.

In response to the complex changes in student behavior in classroom surveillance videos, such as head posture, limb movements, etc., this study designs a novel attention mechanism module to enhance feature extraction capabilities, namely the Global Dynamic Module (GDM). The core function of GDM is to dynamically adjust channel attention, allowing the model to concentrate more on important visual features, such as student head up, head down, interaction, and other behaviors. The structure of GDM is shown in Fig. 2.

In the global encoder, each channel is subjected to Global Average Pooling (GAP) and Global Max Pooling (GMP), causing two [C, 1, 1]-vectors. GAP is used to extract overall distribution features, such as students continuously writing with their heads down. GMP is used to capture the most prominent features, such as students suddenly looking up. Afterwards, one-dimensional (1D) convolution with kernel size i is used to process GAP and GMP features, and two different channel level feature vectors F1 and F2 are output. To reduce the interference of complex classroom environments such as occlusion and lighting changes, this study introduces GDM introduced to YOLOv5 (GYOLOv5) to make the original YOLOv5 more focused on key features of student behavior such as head posture and body movements. The diagram of GYOLOv5 is exhibited in Fig. 3.

Fig. 3GYOLOv5 structure diagram.

GYOLOv5 structure diagram.

In Fig. 3, GYOLOv5 includes three parts: Backbone, Neck, and Head. CBL has Convolutional (Conv) layers, Batch Normalization (BN) layers, and LeakyRelu activation functions. Cross Stage Partial (CSP) optimization of feature extraction improves model performance by reducing computational complexity and enhancing feature diversity. The GDM is located at the last layer of Backbone and before Neck’s Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) module. GDM does not significantly increase computational complexity and belongs to lightweight design. It has good compatibility, does not modify the original structure of YOLOv5, and is only added once to avoid redundant calculations. The GYOLOv5 backbone network is responsible for extracting high-level semantic features, while the SPP module is utilized for multi-scale feature fusion. Inserting GDM between the two can optimize the quality of the features sent to the Head. Head retains the default structure of the original YOLOv5 and outputs detection boxes and type probabilities. The specific behavior detection process of GYOLOv5 is to input one frame of image, extract features through Backbone, enhance by GDM, and output the bounding boxes and preliminary behavior categories of all students by Neck and Head.

Application of teaching intelligent evaluation system

This study uses GYOLOv5 as the detector for the tracking algorithm and optimizes the tracking part of the DS algorithm. DS is an algorithm used for multi-target tracking, mainly built on the simple online and real-time tracking (Sort) algorithm, and combined with deep learning techniques to improve tracking accuracy20. The common DS backbone network is Residual Network 50 layer (ResNet50), which has limited feature extraction capability and low recognition accuracy for situations with occlusion and background noise. To this end, DS has been optimized to improve its ability to extract student behavioral characteristics. The optimization method is to introduce a Dual Pooling Fusion (DPF) module in DS. This module combines the advantages of max pooling and average pooling to extract student behavior features by extracting feature information at different scales in the network. After obtaining student behavior data through intelligent model recognition, to balance the behavioral characteristics of ordinary students and students with special needs, and achieve a more fair and comprehensive classroom performance evaluation, this study uses weighted calculation and quantitative evaluation to ultimately generate students’ classroom evaluations. In the context of integrated education, classroom student behavior can be divided into six categories based on the common behavioral characteristics of ordinary students and students with special needs such as autism and Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD): focused listening, writing, active interaction, distraction, special needs behavior, and playing with mobile phones23. This classification draws on the classroom behavior coding scheme compiled by RVolpe R. J. et al. (2025) and the clinical behavior characteristics of students with special needs24. And after double-blind annotation and consistency verification by special education experts in the field of integrated education, the video samples were determined to ensure the scientific and ecological validity of the classification. To ensure good intelligent evaluation and feedback, teaching effectiveness has been quantified from Head Up Rate (HUR) and focus, and an intelligent evaluation system has been built. HUR, as an observational indicator of classroom participation, is widely used in educational psychology research, but there is some controversy over its causal relationship with teaching effectiveness. For example, Diasse M. D. et al. (2025) pointed out that although the behavior of students looking up at teachers or blackboards is usually related to attention concentration, it may not necessarily be accompanied by deep cognitive processing, and there may be a phenomenon of pseudo focus25. The comprehensive HUR refers to the average proportion of time that all students in the class spend looking up, reflecting the overall attractiveness of the classroom. Analysis of the effectiveness of intelligent models and evaluation systems

Intelligent model analysis of student behavior data

The experimental hardware uses NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 graphics card with 24GB of video memory, mainly used to accelerate the training and inference of deep learning models, especially suitable for processing large-scale image and video data, such as student behavior recognition. The instrument is equipped with AMD EPYC 7542 processor, which is responsible for assisting data preprocessing and multi-threaded task scheduling to support GPU computing. In addition, the system also has 64GB of memory to ensure efficient loading and caching of large-scale datasets such as classroom video streams. In terms of software environment, Windows 10 is chosen as the operating system, providing a stable deep learning development environment. The CUDA version is 11.5, which supports GPU parallel computing and optimizes the computational efficiency of PyTorch. The deep learning framework adopts PyTorch 1.11.0, which provides flexible model construction and training interfaces, making it very suitable for behavior recognition tasks. Meanwhile, Python version 3.7 is compatible with mainstream scientific computing libraries such as NumPy and OpenCV. The experimental hyperparameter optimizer is Adam, with a learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 8, and 300 iterations. The dataset includes Dataset A and the Common Objects in Context (COCO) training set. COCO contains 118,287 images, with a validation set of 5,000 images covering 80 object categories, including educational related labels such as people, mobile phones, and books27. The evaluation metrics for the model include Giga Floating-point Operations Per Second (GFLOPs) and mAP with IoU ranging from 0.5 to 0.95 (mAP@0.5:0.95). The indicators for tracking algorithms include Identity F1 Score (IDF1), MOTA, and Frames Per Second (FPS). To analyze the performance of the designed tracking model, the paper compares the performance of different tracking models. The compared tracking models are DS、ByteTrack, Observation-Centric-Sort (OC-Sort), StrongSort, and TransTrack. ByteTrack improves tracking performance by introducing multiple detection thresholds and optimization strategies, enabling efficient handling of lost targets while detecting targets28. OC-Sot is a variant of Sort that improves tracking performance by centralizing observations of time series. It exhibits strong adaptability when dealing with target occlusion and rapid motion29. StrongSport can provide more durable tracking performance when dealing with complex environments and target changes by introducing stronger feature extraction networks and improved matching strategies. TransTrack adopts the Transformer structure and utilizes its self attention mechanism to improve the performance of target tracking30. This model can better capture the relationships and motion patterns between targets, thereby achieving more accurate tracking in complex scenes. Conclusion

The promotion of integrated education has put forward a dual demand for precision and fairness in teaching evaluation. However, traditional evaluation methods have sparse sampling, imprecise definition of special needs behaviors, and less objective evaluation results, making it difficult to meet the differentiated evaluation needs of integrated education classrooms. Therefore, this study combines GYOLOv5 object detection algorithm with DS + DPF tracking technology to design an intelligent model and construct an intelligent evaluation system for classroom scenarios.

The experimental results show that the system shows reliable performance in identifying six types of student behavior. For special needs behavior (B5), the recognition rate of the system is 92.7%, and the MAE is 6.85%, while the MAE of traditional manual evaluation is 68.50%. The recall rate of B5 was 89.3%, and the processing speed was 24% faster than that of R-CNN, which effectively reduced the omission of stereotyped behavior of autistic students. The ICC between the system output and the expert score was 0.93, indicating a high consistency between the two. Therefore, the system can be used as an effective auxiliary tool to support the objective and fair assessment of students with autism and ADHD in an inclusive classroom environment.

The system provides strong support for inclusive education by providing data-driven and quantifiable student participation assessment. The real-time behavior statistics generated by the system can help teachers design personalized teaching strategies, such as segmented task arrangement for ADHD students and visual support scheme for autistic students, so as to achieve targeted teaching adjustment. However, this study has several limitations. First, the experimental data are only from a few middle schools in one region, which limits the generalization ability of the model in other grades and different regions. Secondly, the system only relies on visual data and does not fuse audio and eye tracking information, so its robustness is limited in complex lighting conditions and occluded scenes. Third, the definition of behavior and its weight setting are highly dependent on expert experience, and the interpretability of the deep learning model itself still needs to be improved. Fourth, although the data has been anonymously processed, if the system is deployed for a long time, it still needs to establish a more perfect ethics and privacy protection mechanism.

The future research work will focus on the following aspects: combining visual, auditory and eye tracking data, building a multimodal evaluation system to improve the stability and accuracy of behavior recognition; Carry out cross regional validation experiments to enhance the universal applicability of the model; At the same time, further optimize the interpretability, deployment efficiency and ethical compliance of the model, so as to promote the implementation and application of Inclusive Education in the actual teaching scene.
