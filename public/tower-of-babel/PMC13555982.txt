Factors associated with improvement in gait disturbance of polyneuropathy in POEMS syndrome
Authors: Takeru Nara, Kazumoto Shibuya, Ryota Kuroiwa, Sonoko Misawa, Tomoki Suichi, Ryo Otani, Moeko Ogushi, Astushi Murata, Satoshi Kuwabara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDistal dominant muscle weakness is one of the characteristic symptoms of polyneuropathies, largely affecting patients’ quality of life. The aim of this study was to identify factors influencing gait disturbances in POEMS (polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, M-protein, and skin changes) syndrome.MethodsThis study consists of 2 study designs: Study 1 was a cross-sectional study and Study 2 was retrospective cohort study. Study 1: a total of 60 patients with POEMS syndrome was included. Gait disturbance was assessed using the Functional Ambulation Categories (FAC), and relationships among FAC, physical functional assessments, and tibial nerve conduction studies (NCS) parameters were analyzed. Study 2: after treatment, 27 of the 60 patients were divided into the 2 groups based on FAC results: the gait improvement group (n = 14) and non-improvement group (n = 13). Factors contributing to gait improvement were assessed.ResultsStudy 1: The median age of patients was 56 years; 26 patients walked independently, whereas the remaining 34 required assistance with walking. The median scores for manual muscle testing were 4/5 for the iliopsoas, 5/5 for the quadriceps, and 2/5 for the tibialis anterior. The median score for great toe position sense was 5/5. Gait disturbances were significantly correlated with muscle strength of the iliopsoas (rho = 0.57), quadriceps (rho = 0.64), and tibialis anterior (strongest correlation; rho = 0.82), and with great toe position sense (rho = 0.59) (all, p < 0.0001). In contrast, NCS parameters were not correlated with gait disturbances. Study 2: strength of the 3 muscles was improved after treatments in the gait improvement group, and Δ muscle strength of the iliopsoas muscles was significantly associated with gait improvement (rho = 0.54, p < 0.05).ConclusionsWhereas weakness of the distal lower limb muscles largely affects gait ability in POEMS polyneuropathy, recovery in proximal lower limb muscle strength is more important for gait improvement.Keywords: Polyneuropathy, POEMS syndrome, Gait disturbance, Rehabilitation, Distal limb weakness

Background

Polyneuropathy is a disorder that leads to damage of sensory, motor, and/or autonomic peripheral nerves [1]. Prevalence of polyneuropathy in the general middle-aged and elderly population is at least 4% and increases with age [2]. A total of 75% of polyneuropathies is length-dependent [3] and is associated with many diseases. A common motor feature of polyneuropathy is distal dominant limb weakness. This weakness often causes foot drop, and decreased ankle strength and range of motion can lead to instability and falls [4]. Moreover, polyneuropathy frequently contributes to gait abnormalities, including unsteady and/or slowed walking [3]. Additionally, these gait disturbances are often exacerbated by sensory deficits such as diminished proprioception or loss of tactile sensation [5]. Eventually, the impact of polyneuropathy extends beyond mobility impairments, substantially affecting activities of daily living [6].

POEMS (polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, M-protein, and skin changes) syndrome is a rare disorder characterized with demyelinating and axonal mixed neuropathy, along with monoclonal plasma cell proliferation and multiorgan involvement [7, 8]. While the pathogenesis of POEMS syndrome remains poorly understood, it is believed that plasma cell dyscrasia and overproduction of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) play significant roles in its pathophysiology [9, 10]. Sensorimotor neuropathy is often the initial symptom of POEMS syndrome, beginning distally in the feet and progressing proximally in a length-dependent fashion [11]. As the disease progresses, severe distal weakness affects both lower and upper limbs, leading to a high stepping gait [12–14]. Importantly, polyneuropathy in POEMS syndrome reduces the ability of many patients to perform activities of daily living, and 30% of patients with the disease are bedridden or confined to a wheelchair [15] due to severe polyneuropathies in POEMS syndrome.

To date, although the importance of neurorehabilitation in supportive care for polyneuropathies has been emphasized [11, 14, 16], no studies have specifically focused on the causes of gait disturbance and the factors contributing to gait improvement. The present study aimed to investigate the underlying causes of gait disturbances and identify factors influencing gait improvement in patients with polyneuropathy associated with POEMS syndrome.

Methods

Study design and patients

The retrospective study was conducted at Chiba University Hospital, reviewing admissions from January 2010 to December 2023. Patients with probable or definite POEMS syndrome according to published diagnostic criteria [17] who underwent rehabilitation at our hospital were included. We adopted this diagnostic criterion to include patients without detectable monoclonal plasma cell proliferation, who account for approximately 10% in Japanese cases [18]. The study comprised a cross-sectional study (Study 1) including patients before initial treatment and a retrospective cohort study (Study 2) including patients with available follow-up data who were assessed at the end of initial treatment. Patients without follow-up data were excluded because no opportunity for readmission was available during the study period. Clinical and demographic details were extracted from electronic medical records.

The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Chiba University Ethics Committee (Approval No. HK202404-02). Informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study and was posted on the hospital notice board. Exclusion criteria included patients who opted out through the disclosure document.

Outcomes

The following parameters were obtained from electronic medical records: age, gender, height (cm), weight (kg), disease duration until initial treatment (months), serum VEGF before treatment (pg/mL), and type of initial treatment (Thalidomide, Bortezomib, or Lenalidomide). Physical functional assessments included manual muscle testing of the lower extremities (iliopsoas, quadriceps, and tibialis anterior), plantar foot sensation, great toe position sense, presence of ankle dorsiflexion limitation, presence of respiratory distress on exertion, presence of leg edema, and presence of lower limb numbness. Plantar foot sensation and great toe position sense were assessed using the Stroke Impairment Assessment Set [19]. Plantar foot sensation was evaluated with a feather brush, with sensation at the forehead as a reference value defined as 10 points. Great toe position sense was assessed with the flexion and extension of the great toe, with a total of 5 trials, and the number of correct trials was recorded. Nerve conduction studies (NCS) included distal latency (ms), compound muscle action potential (mV), motor nerve conduction velocity (m/s), and F-wave latencies (ms) in the tibial nerve. NCS were performed with a standardized method after confirming that the skin temperature was maintained at ≥ 32 °C. The compound muscle action potential of the tibial nerve was recorded using the abductor hallucis muscle, with stimulation at the ankle and at the popliteal fossa. The degree of gait disturbance in patients with POEMS syndrome was classified into 6 levels (0–5) using the Functional Ambulation Categories (FAC) [20]. Assistive devices, such as orthoses, were permitted if necessary. These tests were performed before and after initial treatment.

In Study 2, patients were classified into 2 groups according to gait improvement between baseline (pre-treatment assessment at hospitalization for initiation of treatment) and post-treatment (assessment at hospitalization for autologous stem cell transplantation after completion of the initial treatment phase): a gait improvement group (FAC increase ≥ 2 levels) and a non-improvement group (FAC increase < 2 levels), based on a previous study [21].

Statistical analysis

Quantitative variables were described as median and interquartile range (IQR), and qualitative variables were presented as counts. The normality of the data was evaluated using the Shapiro-Wilk test. The Shapiro-Wilk test showed that only height and weight were normally distributed, whereas all other variables were not normally distributed.

Study 1: In the factorial analysis of gait disturbance, correlations between before initial treatment FAC scores and physical functional assessments, as well as NCS results, were tested using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Multiple regression analysis was conducted to identify independent factors affecting gait disturbance. The explanatory variables were selected based on clinical relevance, with 4 variables included while considering the risks of overfitting and multicollinearity.

Study 2: In the examination of improvement factors for gait disturbance, patients were divided into 2 groups, based on the amount of improvement in gait before and after the initial treatment: gait improvement group (FAC increased by at least 2 levels) and non-improvement group (FAC increase of less than 2 levels) [21]. The changes in physical functional assessments in each group before and after treatment were evaluated, using Wilcoxson’s signed rank test. Furthermore, the amount of improvement after treatment was defined as Δ, and correlations among Δ FAC, Δ physical functional assessments, and Δ VEGF were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient.

All analyses were performed using JMP Pro version 16.0.0 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA), with statistical significance defined as a p-value of < 0.05.

Improvement factors of gait disturbance (Study 2)

27 of the 60 patients, evaluated after initial treatments, were divided into 2 groups-the gait improvement (n = 14) and non-improvement (n = 13) groups- based on FAC results, and factors contributing to gait improvement were assessed. The median (IQR) duration from baseline to repeat FAC assessment was 8 (6–15) months in the gait improvement group and 7 (6–8) months in the gait non-improvement group, with no significant difference between the 2 groups. Rehabilitation, including lower limb muscle strengthening exercises, standing and gait training, balance training, and aerobic exercise using a bicycle ergometer, was provided to 27 patients at the discretion of physical therapists. No additional mobility-related interventions, such as intensive rehabilitation programs or other specific procedures, were routinely provided beyond standard medical treatment and conventional rehabilitation. The median (IQR) duration of rehabilitation was 60 (38–113) days in the gait improvement group and 90 (60–180) days in the gait non-improvement group. In the gait improvement group, 8 patients received thalidomide and 6 received bortezomib, whereas in the gait non-improvement group, 7 patients received thalidomide and 6 received bortezomib. No significant differences were observed between the 2 groups in rehabilitation duration, types of treatments, baseline characteristics before treatment, physical function, or NCS findings. Table 4 shows changes in physical functional assessments in the gait improvement and non-improvement groups before and after treatment. Iliopsoas (p < 0.01), quadriceps (p < 0.05), tibialis anterior (p < 0.05) muscle strength, and great toe position sense (p < 0.05) in the gait improvement group showed significant improvement. There was no significant improvement in the non-improvement group.

Discussion

The present study is the first study to investigate factors affecting gait disturbance in patients with polyneuropathy associated with POEMS syndrome. As described in Table 1, patients included in the present study had relatively preserved muscle strength in the proximal lower extremities, as well as prominent weakness in the distal lower extremities, a characteristic symptom of polyneuropathies. The most significant result of the present study was that distal muscle weakness of the lower extremities was significantly associated with gait disturbance, while improvement in proximal muscle strength of the lower extremities may contribute to improvement in gait disturbance. In contrast, NCS parameters were not correlated with gait disturbances. This is the first study focusing on gait disturbance associated with polyneuropathy in patients with POEMS syndrome, providing information on factors associated with gait disturbance and gait improvement in this population.

Factors affecting gait disturbance (Study 1)

The results of correlation analysis between FAC and physical function assessments showed that gait disturbances were significantly correlated with lower limb muscle strength and great toe position sense (Table 2), and multiple regression analysis revealed that muscle weakness in the tibialis anterior muscle had the greatest impact on gait disturbance (Table 3). Distal leg muscles, including the tibialis anterior, act on ankle joints and toe dorsiflexion to promote foot clearance and lower limb forward motion during the walking swing phase [3, 22]. Thus, in patients with polyneuropathy associated with POEMS syndrome, in which the distal muscles of the lower extremities are often severely impaired, foot drop caused by this impaired motion may contribute to gait disturbances due to an increased tendency to trip. In contrast, gait disturbance in POEMS syndrome was not correlated with neurophysiological findings. This may be partly due to severe axonal loss in the lower limbs [23], which resulted in a reduced number of patients available for correlation analysis, because compound muscle action potentials could not be evoked in some cases. In other words, clinical symptoms may be more closely associated with gait disturbance than neurophysiological findings in patients with polyneuropathy in POEMS syndrome.

Improvement factors of gait disturbance (Study 2)

The results of this study suggest that improvement in proximal lower limb muscle strength may contribute to amelioration of gait disturbance in polyneuropathy in POEMS syndrome. The role of the proximal muscles of the lower limbs in gait is to swing the lower limbs during the swing phase [3] and to keep the trunk upright during the stance phase [21]. Furthermore, the compensatory movement for decreased leg clearance is hip flexion [22], making it important to improve the proximal muscles of the lower limbs in patients with polyneuropathy associated with POEMS syndrome. Additionally, in polyneuropathy associated with POEMS syndrome, proximal lower limb muscles may show greater potential for improvement, possibly reflecting differences in the degree of polyneuropathic involvement between proximal and distal muscles. As noted above, distal lower limb muscles are more severely affected, whereas proximal lower limb muscles tend to be relatively preserved [11, 24]. Therefore, although proximal muscles are also involved in the polyneuropathic process, the extent of involvement is generally milder, which may predispose them to disuse muscle weakness while also allowing a greater capacity for recovery. In other words, in addition to strengthening the distal lower limb muscles, strengthening the proximal lower extremity muscles may be beneficial in the rehabilitation of polyneuropathy in POEMS syndrome.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, gait disturbance was evaluated using only the FAC, which may not fully capture disability and gait impairment in patients with polyneuropathy in POEMS syndrome. In addition, the INCAT score [25] and quantitative spatiotemporal gait analysis [26–28] were not used, which may have limited the comprehensiveness of gait assessment. Second, despite comparable baseline characteristics, rehabilitation duration and content, and treatment conditions between the groups, the degree of post-treatment physical function improvement differed. The mechanisms underlying improvements in gait and polyneuropathy remain unclear, and the effects of rehabilitation and treatment on gait recovery could not be fully clarified in the present study. Furthermore, several potentially relevant physiological factors were not comprehensively assessed. In this study, NCS of the tibial nerve were performed; however, the gastrocnemius muscle was not assessed. Similarly, although gait requires coordinated activity of multiple muscles, including the trunk muscles, detailed assessment of these muscles was not performed in this study. In the future study, these assessments might have to be included. Furthermore, this study was a retrospective cohort study at a single institution. The nature of the study may have led to potential biases such as measurement bias and selection bias. In addition, multiple regression analysis was performed to adjust for confounders, but the possibility of potential confounding factor cannot be ruled out due to the retrospective nature of the study. In particular, due to the small sample size, the study did not allow adjustment for potential confounding variables such as age, disease severity including systemic manifestations, and rehabilitation intensity; therefore, residual confounding cannot be fully excluded. Moreover, approximately 40% of the patients in this study were referrals or transfers from other hospitals; however, due to the retrospective nature of the study, detailed information regarding prior rehabilitation status, activity levels, and clinical condition at the referring institutions was not available. Since pre-rehabilitation status is important in relation to disuse muscle weakness, further studies are needed to address this issue. To overcome these limitations, further multicenter prospective cohort studies in patients with polyneuropathy associated with POEMS syndrome are warranted and may provide more definitive evidence regarding gait disturbance and rehabilitation in this population.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study revealed that muscle weakness in the distal lower limb muscles in patients with polyneuropathy associated with POEMS syndrome causes gait disturbance, and conversely, improvement of muscle strength in the proximal lower limb muscles influences the improvement of gait disturbance. The results may contribute to future research on rehabilitation and functional prognosis in patients with polyneuropathy associated with POEMS syndrome.
