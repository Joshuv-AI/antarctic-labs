Bone morphogenetic protein 5 as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Caiping Tian, Xiao Jing, Hongyu Luo, Yue Bai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIt is reported that bone morphogenetic protein 5 (BMP5) exerts a significant function in the pathogenesis of cancers. However, the role of BMP5 in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is unclear. Thus, this study was conducted to excavate the diagnostic and prognostic significance of BMP5 in LUAD.MethodsThe expression level and prognostic significance of BMP5 were analyzed in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) pan-cancer dataset of LUAD, followed by Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) analysis. Correlation analysis between BMP5 and immune cell infiltration, immune checkpoints, and tumor mutation burden (TMB) were performed. The expression level, diagnostic and prognostic significances of BMP5 were confirmed in LUAD with Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets. The relationship between BMP5 expression and clinicopathological features of LUAD patients was analyzed, as well as BMP5-associated drugs for LUAD treatments. Lastly, in vitro experiments were carried out to evaluate the function of BMP5 in LUAD.ResultsBMP5 was aberrantly expressed in most cancer types and showed prognostic predictive value and involved in energy-related pathways in most cancer types. BMP5 was significantly associated with immune checkpoint genes, immune activators, immunity inhibitors, immune cells, immunoscore, TMB and microsatellite instability (MSI) in most cancer types. Low expression of BMP5 was significantly associated with poor prognosis of LUAD patients, and BMP5 could accurately predict the prognosis of LUAD. In addition, BMP5 was strongly related to various clinicopathological parameters and immune cells of LUAD. The half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values of axitinib, gefitinib and lapatinib were significantly increased in BMP5 high-expression group, while the IC50 of Olaparib was markedly decreased. In vitro experiments uncovered that BMP5 could inhibit tumorigenesis of LUAD.ConclusionsBMP5 is a valuable diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in LUAD. BMP5 might inhibit tumorigenesis of LUAD by targeting cell cycle pathway. Axitinib, gefitinib, lapatinib, and olaparib might be used for LUAD treatment by targeting BMP5.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), bone morphogenetic protein 5 (BMP5), pan-cancer, diagnostic, prognostic

Highlight box

Key findings

• Bone morphogenetic protein 5 (BMP5) could be used as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD).

• BMP5 might be an underlying target for improving the immunotherapy efficacy in patients with LUAD.

• BMP5 might inhibit tumorigenesis of LUAD by targeting cell cycle pathway.

What is known and what is new?

• BMP5 is implicated in some cancers, but its role in LUAD is unclear.

• BMP5 is downregulated in LUAD and correlates with diagnosis, prognosis, immune checkpoint expression, and drug sensitivity. Overexpression inhibits tumor growth in vitro, likely via cell cycle regulation.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• BMP5 is a potential biomarker for diagnosis, prognosis, and immunotherapy guidance in LUAD.

• Clinical validation of BMP5 and exploration of BMP5-targeted therapies are needed.

Introduction

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), as the most common pathological subtype of non-small cell lung cancer, accounts for approximately 40% of all lung cancers (1). Globally, the incidence of LUAD is continuously increasing, especially among women and non-smokers (2). In China, there are over 200,000 new cases each year, and the age of onset is gradually becoming younger (3). Long-term air pollution, occupational exposure, second-hand smoke exposure, and genetic factors are all major contributing factors to LUAD (4,5). The early symptoms of LUAD are insidious, and approximately 70% of patients are diagnosed at the stage of locally advanced or with distant metastasis, thus missing the opportunity for surgical radical treatment (6). The 5-year survival rate for early-stage LUAD patients can reach 70–90%, but the 5-year survival rate of advanced-stage patients is still less than 15% even with standard treatment (7). Therefore, there is an urgent need for valuable diagnostic and treatment biomarkers to accurately detect LUAD.

The extracellular matrix (ECM) is one of the main components of tumors and plays a significant role in tumor progression (8,9). ECM proteins, as known as matrisome, include collagens, proteoglycans and glycoproteins (10). Alterations in the matrisome have been proved to be involved in various diseases, including cancer, Parkinson’s disease, adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (11,12). An increasing number of studies have reported that bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) has been proven to affect the occurrence of various tumors. Among them, BMP5, a matrisome-related gene, has been previously reported in adrenocortical carcinoma, colorectal cancer, and breast cancer (13,14). For instance, Chen et al. have emphasized that miR-32 accelerates tumorigenesis of colorectal cancer by regulating BMP5 (15). Jin et al. have revealed that downregulation of G9a inhibits aggressiveness of breast cancer by modulating BMP5 expression (13). Meng et al. have illustrated that BMP5 might serve as a potential crucial target for LUAD treatment (16). Nevertheless, existing domestic and international studies on BMP5 in LUAD are still limited and scattered. The systematic research on the role of BMP5 in LUAD immune microenvironment regulation, immune checkpoint modulation, tumor mutation burden (TMB) correlation, drug sensitivity prediction and underlying molecular mechanism is still lacking, which constitutes an important research gap in this field.

Thus, aiming at the above unresolved issues, the diagnostic and prognostic roles of BMP5 were evaluated in this study based on The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) pan-cancer and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases, as well as the function of BMP5 in tumor immune cell infiltration, immune checkpoints, and TMB. Also, in vitro experiments were executed to discover the BMP5 function in LUAD. This study may offer new biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognosis prediction of LUAD, and also provide new clues for immunotherapy. We present this article in accordance with the STARD and MDAR reporting checklists (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1-0129/rc).

Methods

Data description

The somatic mutation data of the BMP5 were obtained through the cBioPortal platform (http://www.cbioportal.org/), covering the TCGA pan-cancer dataset. Also, five LUAD-related datasets were downloaded from GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) for expression and prognostic validation, containing GSE18842, GSE50081, GSE30219, GSE31210, GSE37745. GSE18842 was used for differential expression validation, which including 46 LUAD and 45 normal samples. Total 127, 85, 226, 106 LUAD samples were comprised in GSE50081, GSE30219, GSE31210, GSE37745 datasets, respectively, and these datasets were employed for prognostic verification. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Identifying BMP5 expression and survival analysis

Wilcoxon rank-sum test was analyzed to assess the differences of BMP5 expression in 33 types of cancer and their paired normal tissue samples based on SangerBox online platform (http://sangerbox.com/) which integrated the TCGA pan-cancer dataset (10,499 samples across 32 cancer types) and GTEx database (7,832 normal tissue samples). In addition, the TCGAplot package (version 8.0.0) was utilized to conduct the univariate Cox regression analysis to count the hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI), based on the SangerBox online platform which integrated the Therapeutically Applicable Research to Generate Effective Treatments (TARGET) [acute myelolastic leukemia (LAML), neuroblastoma (NB) and Wilms’ tumor (WT)] and TCGA pan-cancer dataset (11,114 samples across 38 cancer types). Then the association between BMP5 expression and overall survival (OS) was explored, followed by Kaplan-Meier curves visualization using the TCGAplot package (version 8.0.0).

Correlation analysis between BMP5 and immune cell infiltration, immune checkpoints, and TMB

Based on the TCGA pan-cancer dataset (10,953 samples across 33 cancer types), the correlations between BMP5 and immune cell infiltration, immune score, TMB, immune checkpoints, and microsatellite instability (MSI), immune stimulants, and immune inhibitors were investigated using the gene TMB radar, gene MSI radar, gene checkpoint heatmap, gene immustimulator heatmap, gene immuinhibitor heatmap, gene immunescore heatmap and gene immucell heatmap functions in TCGAplot package (version 8.0.0). The correlation coefficient was calculated using Pearson correlation analysis, and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Gene Set Enrichment Analyses (GSEA)

Correlation analysis between BMP5 and other genes in the TCGA pan-cancer dataset (33 cancer types) was carried out using Pearson correlation analysis, and the Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated and sorted, and the gene sets with high correlations were selected. Subsequently, GSEA analysis was performed using the clusterProfiler package (version 4.6.0) based on Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) gene sets to identify the significantly enriched pathways with the threshold of | Normalized Enrichment Score (NES)| > 1.5 and P<0.05.

BMP5 expression verification in GEO datasets

The expression of BMP5 in LUAD and normal samples were assessed based on GSE18842 dataset (46 LUAD samples, 45 normal samples) using Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Furthermore, the correlation between the BMP5 and the prognosis of LUAD was verified using the GSE50081 (127 LUAD samples), GSE30219 (85 LUAD samples), GSE31210 (226 LUAD samples), and GSE37745 (106 LUAD samples) datasets. Univariate Cox regression analysis was used to calculate HR and 95% CI, and Kaplan-Meier survival curves were drawn to visualize the survival difference between BMP5 high- and low-expression groups. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Associations between BMP5 and clinicopathological characteristics of LUAD

Based on TCGA-LUAD dataset (515 LUAD samples), including 162 cases of stage I, 137 cases of stage II, 134 cases of stage III, 82 cases of stage IV; 287 cases of N0, 132 cases of N1, 68 cases of N2, 28 cases of N3; 444 cases of M0, 71 cases of M1), the differences of BMP5 expression in the various clinicopathological features such as tumor stages (I–IV), lymph node metastasis (N0–N3), distant metastasis (M0 and M1) and normal samples (59 cases) were compared using Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Evaluation of the diagnostic significance of BMP5 in LUAD

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was carried out using the pROC package (version 1.18.0) to evaluate the diagnostic value of BMP5 for LUAD, and the 95% CI was acquired through bootstrap (n=1,000). The ROC curves were visualized using ggplot2 package (version 3.4.4), with area under the curve (AUC) >0.7 considered to have good diagnostic efficiency.

Drug sensitivity analysis and immune infiltration

Drug sensitivity and immune infiltration analyses were performed based on the TCGA-LUAD dataset (515 LUAD samples). The standardized gene expression data and drug response data for 809 tumor cell lines were downloaded from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database (version 22.1), and the response of LUAD patients to 198 anti-tumor drugs was predicted utilizing oncoPredict algorithm (version 0.3.0). Differences of the half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values of each drug between the high- and low-expression of BMP5 were compared using Wilcoxon rank-sum test, as well as the association between BMP5 expression and the IC50 values of each drug using Pearson correlation analysis with the threshold of |R|>0.2. The drugs with significant differences in IC50 values between the two groups and meeting the correlation threshold were visualized using the ggplot2 package (version 3.4.4). Besides, immune infiltration analysis was conducted in samples using ssGSEA in GSVA package. Then the samples were divided into high- and low-expression groups based on the median expression of BMP5, and Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare the differences in the infiltration levels of immune cells between the high- and low-expression of BMP5 groups. Spearman correlation analysis was carried out to explore the association between BMP5 and immune cells.

Cell culture and treatment

Human bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS-2B (#SNL-203, SUNNCELL, Wuhan, China), and LUAD cell lines A549 (#CL-0016, Procell, Wuhan, China) and HCC827 (#CL-0094, Procell) were passaged in DMEM medium (#C11885500BT, Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA) containing 10% FBS (#34080619, Ephraim, Xiamen, China). Conventional subculture was performed when the cell density was approximately 90%, and the fourth-generation cells were taken for subsequent experiments. BMP5 overexpression (oe-BMP5) sequence was downloaded from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) website (Appendix 1). The coding sequence region of BMP5 was synthesized and cloned into the vector for overexpressing BMP5. The HCC827 cells were grown into a 6-well plate with 2×105/mL cells per well, and maintained until the cell density reached 70–90%. Lentivirus vector and packaging plasmids were transfected into 293 T cells to produce lentiviruses. The titer of oe-BMP5 lentivirus were quantified as 5×108 TU/mL and its multiplicity of infection (MOI) was 50. Then the constructed lentivirus (100 µL per well) was employed to infect the HCC827 cells for 18 h, and the culture medium was removed and complete culture medium including 2.5 µg/mL puromycin was implemented to screen for stably transfected cell lines after infection 48 h. The expression level of BMP5 was evaluated by Western blot and quantitative reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), and Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) kit (#C0037, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was employed to estimate the cell vitality.

Flow cytometry

Flow cytometry was utilized to determine the cell apoptosis by one step TUNEL apoptosis detection kit (#C1086, Beyotime). After trypsin digestion of cells, the cells were centrifuged for 5 min. Then 195 µL Binding Buffer was implemented to suspend the cells, and 5 µL Annexin V-FITC was employed to mix the cells for 15 min. Subsequently, cells were mixed with 10 µL propidium lodide and incubated for 10 min. Data were detected by Flow cytometry (CytoFLEXs; Beckman, FL, USA). Similarly, flow cytometry was also employed to evaluate the cell cycle by using Cell Cycle and Apoptosis Analysis Kit (#C1052, Beyotime).

Scratch wound healing assay

The migration ability of HCC827 cells was detected by scratch wound healing assay. In short, the lines were drawn on the back of the 6-well plates, and HCC827 cells were passaged. The next day, a pipet tip was employed to create a ‘‘scratch’’ of the cell monolayer that was perpendicular to the back lines, followed by the serum-free medium addition. Finally, the results were photographed at 0, 24 and 48 h.

Transwell assay

The invasion ability of HCC827 cells was also assessed by Transwell assay. The Transwell chamber was coated with Matrigel (#354230, BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA). After the cells digesting by trypsin and diluting with basic culture medium into a cell suspension of 1×106 cells/mL, 200 µL cell suspension were maintained on Transwell chambers, and medium containing 10% FBS were added in the bottom of the chamber. After 24 h, cells were fixed 30 min by formaldehyde, and stained with crystal violet. After 20 min, the results were determined utilizing an inverted microscope (DMi3000 B; Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted utilizing Trizol (#15596018, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and reversely transcribed into cDNA by FastKing-RT SuperMix (#KR118-02, TIANGEN, Beijing, China), and the reaction conditions were 42 ℃ for 15 min, 95 ℃ for 3 min. SYBR Green PCR Master mix (#A4004M, Lifeint, Xiamen, China) was applied to conduct the qRT-PCR under the recommended conditions of 95 ℃ for 10 min and 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 12 s, 58 ℃ for 40 s. The utilized primers were human BMP5 forward 5'-CTCTATCTTGGGTTTGCC-3' and reverse 5'-TTCTTTGTAATGCCTTCG-3'; Human GAPDH forward 5'-TGTGGGCATCAATGGATTTGG-3', and reverse 5'-ACACCATGTATTCCGGGTCAAT-3'.

Western blot

Total protein was acquired after lysis with lysis buffer (#P0013B, Beyotime), then proteins were uncoupled by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. The blocked membranes were probed with primary antibody anti-BMP5 (1:1,000; #PA5-78878, Thermo, Waltham, MA, USA), anti-CDK1 (1:1,000; #MA5-32055, Thermo), anti-CyclinD1 (1:1,000; #MA5-14512, Thermo), anti-CyclinB1 (1:1,000; #MA5-27908, Thermo), and anti-GAPDH (1:5,000; #PA1-987, Thermo) at 4 ℃ overnight, followed by secondary antibody goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (HRP) (1:2,000; #ab6721, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) incubation for 60 min. The membranes were observed by an electrochemical luminescence (ECL) reagent (#P1,000, APPLYGEN, Beijing, China), and bands were visualized using chemiluminescence imaging system (3500; Tanon, Shanghai, China).

Statistical analysis

Data were exhibited as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and statistical analysis was carried out by GraphPad 7.0 software. One-way analysis of variance was utilized to discover the differences between groups. P<0.05 was deemed significant difference.

Results

BMP5 is significantly reduced in most cancer types

BMP5 expression was detected in 33 types of cancer, and the results found that BMP5 expression level was obviously overexpressed in glioblastoma multiforme brain lower grade glioma (GBM LGG), LGG, pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD), uterine carcinosarcoma (UCS) and LAML samples compared to that in normal samples, while some cancer types showed significant down-regulation of BMP5, containing LUAD (all P<0.05; Figure 1A). No remarkable differences were found in the uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC), testicular germ cell tumors (TGCT), acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), and pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma (PCPG) samples compared to normal samples.

(C) Kaplan-Meier overall survival curves of BMP5 in cancers. *, P<0.05; **, P<0.01; ***, P<0.001; ****, P<0.0001. CI, confidence interval; HR, hazard ratio.

Clinical prognostic significance of BMP5 in pan-cancer

Through univariate Cox regression analysis (Figure 1B), it was found that the upregulation of BMP5 was a risk factor for patients with GBM LGG [P=4.2e−11, HR =1.16 (1.11–1.21)], LGG [P=1.6e−5, HR =1.16 (1.09–1.25)] and sarcoma (SARC) [P=0.01, HR =1.06 (1.01–1.10)], while it was protective factor for LUAD [P=4.9e−5, HR =0.88 (0.83–0.94)], colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) [P=8.3e−3, HR =0.91 (0.85–0.98)], rectum adenocarcinoma (READ) [P=0.04, HR =0.83 (0.69–1.00)], breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA) [P=7.2e−3, HR =0.93 (0.88–0.98)], kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC) [P=7.2e−3, HR =0.95 (0.91–0.99)], and COAD-READ [P=1.2e−3, HR =0.90 (0.85–0.96)] patients. Consistent with the univariate Cox regression results, survival analysis also found that the high expression levels of BMP5 in GBM LGG [HR =1.83 (1.40−2.40), P=8.1e−6], LGG [HR =1.91 (1.33−2.74), P=3.2e−4], and SARC [HR =1.57 (1.05−2.35), P=0.03] were related to a poor prognosis of patients, while the low expression levels of BMP5 in LUAD [HR =0.54 (0.40−0.72), P=3.6e−5], COAD-READ [HR =0.50 (0.33−0.77), P=1.2e−3], BRCA [HR =0.55 (0.40−0.76), P=2.4e−4], KIRC [HR =0.49 (0.34−0.70), P=5.9e−5], COAD [HR =0.48 (0.30−0.78), P=2.4e−3], and READ [HR =0.13 (0.02−0.97), P=0.02] were associated with a worse prognosis of patients (Figure 1C).

GSEA of BMP5 reveals its association with the energy-related pathways

To further deepen the regulatory mechanism of the BMP5 in cancers, GSEA analysis was conducted in GBMLGG, LGG, SARC, LUAD, COAD-READ, BRCA, KIRC, COAD, and READ, respectively. It was found that BMP5 mainly participated in energy-related pathways in these cancers, such as oxidative phosphorylation, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and cell cycle (Figure 2A).

Correlations between BMP5 and TMB, and MSI in pan-cancer

After obtaining the mutation data of BMP5 across various cancers in cbioportal website, the correlations between BMP5 and TMB in cancers were evaluated. As illustrated in Figure 2B, BMP5 had mutated in various types of cancer with highest mutation rate in Melanoma, and the main type of mutation was amplification. In addition, close correlations were discovered between the BMP5 expression and TMB or MSI in BLCA, COAD, LAML, LUAD, STAD, and THCA (Figure 2C).

As shown in Figure 3, it was found that the BMP5 was significant correlated with immune checkpoints, immune cells, and immune activators, in cancers such as TGCT, BLCA, LIHC, PAAD, and LUAD.

Prognosis and diagnostic significance of BMP5 in LUAD

Based on GSE18842 dataset, it was uncovered that BMP5 was significantly higher expressed in normal samples than that in LUAD samples (P<0.001; Figure 4A). Furthermore, the GSE50081, GSE30219, GSE31210, and GSE377452 datasets were used to verify the relationship between the BMP5 and the prognosis of LUAD patients. It was found that low expression of BMP5 significantly associated with poor prognosis of LUAD patients both in GSE50081 (P<0.001), GSE30219 (P=0.048), GSE31210 (P<0.001), and GSE377452 (P=0.02) datasets (Figure 4B-4E), which were in line with the results found in TCGA pan-cancer dataset. Also, ROC curves found that BMP5 could accurately predict the LUAD [TCGA-LUAD: AUC =0.770, 95% CI: 0.728−0.77; GSE18842: AUC =0.925, 95% CI: 0.860−0.925; Figure 4F]. In addition, BMP5 was strongly related to various clinicopathological parameters of LUAD such as pathologic tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage (Figure 4G).

BMP5-associated drugs and immune cells in LUAD prediction

To unveil the BMP5-associated drugs in LUAD, the relationships between IC50 values of 198 anti-tumor drugs and expression of BMP5 were compared. The IC50 values of Axitinib, Gefitinib and Lapatinib in BMP5 high-expression group were remarkedly higher than those in low-expression group, while the IC50 value of Olaparib showed the opposite result (all P<0.001; Figure 5A). Correspondingly, the expression level of BMP5 observed a significant positive association with the IC50 values of axitinib (R=0.333, P<0.001), gefitinib (R=0.324, P<0.001) and lapatinib (R=0.282, P<0.001), while a significant negative association between BMP5 and the IC50 value of olaparib (R=−0.29, P<0.001) was found (Figure 5B). Besides, total eight immune cells showed significant difference between high- and low-expression of BMP5 groups, including activated B cell, activated CD4 T cell, macrophage (all P<0.05; Figure 5C,5D). Also, correlation analysis found that BMP5 was significantly positively correlated activated B cell (R=0.110, P=0.01), activated CD4 T cell (R=0.122, P=0.005), macrophage (R=0.151, P<0.001), while negative associated with central memory CD4 T cell (R=−0.191, P<0.001), NK cell (R=−0.178, P<0.001), natural killer T cell (R=−0.153, P<0.001), plasmacytoid dendritic cell (R=−0.134, P=0.002), eosinophil (R=−0.112, P=0.01) (Figure 5E).

BMP5 inhibits tumorigenesis of LUAD in vitro

To verify the expression of BMP5 in LUAD, the qRT-PCR and Western blot were performed. Consistent with the bioinformatics analysis results, the messenger RNA (mRNA) and protein expression levels of BMP5 were both obviously decreased in A549 and HCC827 LUAD cell lines relative to BEAS-2B cells (all P<0.01; Figure 6A,6B). To further excavate the function of BMP5 in LUAD, the constructed lentivirus was employed to infect the HCC827 cells to overexpress BMP5, followed by transfection efficiency detection (Figure 6C,6D). Notably, upregulation of BMP5 could significantly restrained the cell vitality, migration and invasion abilities, while promoted the cell apoptosis of HCC827 cells (all P<0.01; Figure 6E-6H). In addition, BMP5 overexpression led to G1 phase arrest and a concomitant reduction in S phase entry in HCC827 cells, as well as inhibited the protein expression levels of cell cycle-related proteins, including CDK1, CyclinB1, CyclinD1 (all P<0.05; Figure 6I,6J). These data suggest that BMP5 inhibits tumorigenesis of LUAD in vitro.

Discussion

Accumulating studies demonstrate that BMP5 exerts a significant function in the pathogenesis of cancers (17,18). This study uncovered that BMP5 was significantly higher expressed in normal samples than that in LUAD samples. This study also found that BMP5 expression levels in LUAD significantly related to prognosis of patients, clinical stages, and tumor status. Furthermore, univariate Cox regression analysis found that the BMP5 was a protective factor for patients with LUAD. Survival analysis also found that low expression of BMP5 was significantly associated with poor prognosis of LUAD patients both in GSE50081, GSE30219, GSE31210, and GSE377452 datasets, which was in line with the results found in TCGA pan-cancer dataset. ROC curves revealed that BMP5 could accurately predict the LUAD both in TCGA pan-cancer dataset and GSE18842 dataset. In addition, BMP5 was strongly related to various clinicopathological parameters of LUAD such as pathologic TNM stage. Notably, consistent with the bioinformatics analysis results, the mRNA and protein expression levels of BMP5 were both obviously decreased in A549 and HCC827 LUAD cell lines relative to BEAS-2B cells, and upregulation of BMP5 could significantly restrained the cell vitality and migration and invasion abilities, while promoted the cell apoptosis of HCC827 cells. In addition, BMP5 overexpression led to G1 phase arrest and a concomitant reduction in S phase entry in HCC827 cells, as well as inhibited the protein expression levels of cell cycle-related proteins. These data suggest that BMP5 inhibits tumorigenesis of LUAD in vitro, which could be used as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in LUAD. Previous studies reported that BMP5 can activate the SMAD signaling pathway, induce the transcription of the cell cycle inhibitors p21CIP1 and p27KIP1, thereby inhibiting the activity of cyclin-CDK complexes, downregulating C-MYC expression, and ultimately arresting the cell cycle at the G1/S phase (19,20). In addition, Liu et al. found that BMP5 enhances p53 stability via the p38 MAPK pathway, which activates downstream apoptotic cascades and facilitates cell apoptosis (21). Based on these findings, we will further systematically and deeply investigate the potential molecular network by which BMP5 modulates the malignant progression of LUAD, using a comprehensive combination of molecular and cellular biological approaches. Besides, GSEA analysis observed that BMP5 mainly involved in cell cycle pathway in LUAD. A growing body of evidence emphasizes that abnormal cell cycle progression is one of the basic mechanisms of tumorigenesis (22,23). For instance, Lv et al. have unveiled that securinine suppresses carcinogenesis via the cell cycle and AURKA-β-catenin/Akt/STAT3 pathway in gastric cancer (24). Cui et al. have suggested that H2S exerts therapeutic effect by targeting cell cycle and DNA replication pathway in triple negative breast cancer (25). Taken together, these findings illustrate that BMP5 might inhibit tumorigenesis of LUAD by targeting cell cycle pathway.

PDCD1 and CTLA4 are two pivotal proteins related to tumor immune escape (26,27). It’s reported that immune checkpoint inhibitors significantly elevate the OS rates of patients with lung cancer, such as nivolumab (PDCD1 inhibitor) and ipilimumab (CTLA4 inhibitor) (28,29). Thus, the associations between and immunomodulators were explored, containing immune checkpoint genes, immune activators, immunity inhibitors, immune cells, and immunoscore. The results uncovered that BMP5 was significant correlated with immune checkpoints in LUAD, such as LAG3, PDCD1, CTLA4, which indicating that BMP5 might an underlying target for improving the immunotherapy efficacy in patients with LUAD. Also, tumor immune cell infiltration is one of the core indicators for evaluating the tumor microenvironment and assessing the efficacy of immunotherapy (30). Herein, the results found that BMP5 was significantly positively correlated activated B cell, activated CD4 T cell, macrophage, while negative associated with central memory CD4 T cell, NK cell, natural killer T cell, plasmacytoid dendritic cell, eosinophil in LUAD. M1 macrophages mainly play a role in tumorigenesis of cancers. For instance, Feng et al. revealed that M1 macrophages targeting JAK-STAT3 pathway to enhance breast cancer chemoresistance (31). NK cells play a fundamental role in the natural immune response against the progression of cancer (32). This study showed that low BMP5 expression in LUAD was accompanied by decreased infiltration of activated CD4 T cells and macrophages, and the deficiency of these key antigen-presenting and pro-inflammatory immune cells weakens anti-tumor immune surveillance, promotes tumor progression and invasion, and ultimately results in poor prognosis. In addition, Singh et al. have revealed that BMP5 expression shows a significant correlation with immune infiltration and immune checkpoint molecule levels in the LUAD microenvironment (33). Wu et al. have uncovered that BMP5 is closely correlated with the infiltration of eosinophils and mast cells in LUAD, suggesting its potential role in regulating the anti-tumor immune response (34). Also, it’s reported that the TGF-β/BMP signaling axis is involved in immune regulation through regulating the secretion and expression of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) (19,35). These results reveal that BMP5 exerts a significant function in the immune escape mechanisms of LUAD cells, thereby involving in LUAD growth and progression. However, the specific molecular mechanism by which BMP5 regulates immune cell infiltration still awaits further in-depth verification in subsequent experiments.

To search the therapeutic drugs for LUAD targeting BMP5, relationships between IC50 values of 198 anti-tumor drugs and BMP5 expression were compared. The IC50 values of Axitinib, Gefitinib and Lapatinib were significantly increased in the BMP5 high-expression group, while the IC50 of Olaparib was markedly decreased. These results suggested that elevated BMP5 expression is linked to increased resistance to axitinib, gefitinib and lapatinib, as well as heightened sensitivity of tumor cell to olaparib. Chen et al. highlighted that Axitinib could be used to alleviate LUAD by targeting NGEF (36). Accumulating evidence has revealed that patients with LUAD have been proven to benefit from Gefitinib (37,38). Motohashi et al. reported a patient with BRCA2-positive LUAD who received with olaparib as the eighth-line therapy, causing stable disease maintained for roughly 2 years (39). Lapatinib, an anti-cancer drug, has been approved to treat breast cancer with good therapeutic effect (40,41). Additionally, BMP5 is strongly linked to EGFR expression and activation, which may may modulate the sensitivity to gefitinib and lapatinib through regulating its downstream pathways including PI3K/AKT (16,42). Edirisinghe et al. have shown that BMP5-mediated signaling regulation can modulate the VEGF pathway, thereby interfering with the anti-angiogenic efficacy of axitinib (43). Also, the correlation between BMP5 and TMB suggests that BMP5 may participate in the DNA repair process, thereby affecting the therapeutic efficacy of PARP inhibitors such as olaparib (19). Therefore, these data suggested that Axitinib, Gefitinib, Lapatinib, and Olaparib might be used for LUAD treatment, and BMP5 expression level may serve as a potential biomarker for predicting therapeutic responses to the above drugs in LUAD patients, providing a reference for the formulation of individualized treatment strategies.

There are numerous limitations in this study. Firstly, this study lacks more validation of BMP5 expression characteristics in animal or clinical trials. Secondly, although we systematically analyzed the drug sensitivity of BMP5 in LUAD, we did not further conduct drug intervention experiments to explore whether the screened candidate drugs exert anti‑tumor effects by regulating BMP5 and its downstream cell cycle as well as energy metabolism pathways. Moreover, further functional experiments should be conduct to explore the underlying mechanism of BMP5 in LUAD. In addition, the direct targeted regulatory relationship between the screened drugs and BMP5 was not validated in vitro or in vivo. Further well-designed studies are required in the future to clarify the molecular mechanism by which candidate drugs mediate anti‑tumor activity via targeting BMP5 and its downstream signaling cascades.

Conclusions

In summary, this study demonstrated the diagnostic and prognostic significance of BMP5 in LUAD. BMP5 inhibits tumorigenesis of LUAD in vitro, possibly through the cell cycle pathway. BMP5 associated with immune checkpoint genes, tumor infiltration status of some immune cells, and immune activators. It might exert a significant function on the response to immunotherapy in LUAD patients.
