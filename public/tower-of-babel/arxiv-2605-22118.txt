On defective spans of singular vector tuples beyond the boundary format
Ettore Teixeira Turatti, Emanuele Ventura

Abstract
. In this paper, we study tensor spaces beyond the boundary format and analyze whether the general critical space coincides with the general span of singular vector tuples. For all tensor spaces exceeding the boundary format by one in an arbitrary number of factors, we relate the codimension of this span within the critical space to the dimension of the kernel of a map in cohomology. Furthermore, we exhibit an infinite family of order-three tensors with a defective behavior: the general span of singular vector tuples achieves the maximum possible codimension rather than the expected minimum. Finally, we conjecture a classification of the behavior of critical spaces in this regime and draw a connection to Koszul cohomology.

1.
Introduction

The spectral theory of tensors is a deep subject both in its applied and algebraic geometry aspects. A fundamental notion in this setting is that of
a
singular vector
k
-tuple
of an order-
k
tensor; this is the generalization of the notion of singular pair of a rectangular matrix. Singular vector
k
-tuples retain important properties of singular pairs. For instance, in the problem of minimizing the distance between a target rank-one tensor and a given tensor
T
, the singular vector
k
-tuples of
T
correspond to the constrained critical points of the distance function. Therefore, they essentially provide an answer to the so-called
best rank-one approximation problem
for
T

[
7
]
.

Beyond approximation problems, singular vector
k
-tuples have been studied from several geometric perspectives. In particular, their relation to tensor decompositions: in the symmetric setting,
[
6
]
investigates when a form admits a minimal decomposition via its singular vector
k
-tuples, while
[
10
]
extends this question to the general setting, including non-minimal decompositions. Another direction of research is exploring structural properties such as orthogonality
[
11
]
, and power methods algorithms for decomposing tensors
[
14
]
.

One of the fundamental properties of singular pairs of matrices is that they yield a minimal decomposition as a sum of rank-one matrices: the singular value decomposition (SVD). However, this property does not generalize to singular vector
k
-tuples of higher-order tensors. In view of this fundamental difference, Ottaviani and Paoletti
[
8
, Section 5.2]
introduced the
singular space

H_{T}
of a tensor
T
– later called the
critical space

[
2
, Definition 2.8]
– as the vanishing locus of the equations defining singular vector
k
-tuples, however not restricted to rank-one tensors. An important feature of this space is that
T\in H_{T}
and
\langle Z_{T}\rangle\subset\mathbb{P}(H_{T})
, where
Z_{T}
is the variety of singular vector
k
-tuples of
T
; see Definition
2.6
. Draisma, Ottaviani and Tocino showed that, when the tensor space has boundary format (see Definition
2.5
), the equality
\langle Z_{T}\rangle=\mathbb{P}(H_{T})
holds
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
. As a consequence,
T
may be decomposed (perhaps in a non-minimal way) as a sum of its singular vector
k
-tuples. This is a generalization of the celebrated Eckart-Young theorem for matrices.

The boundary format has an important geometric meaning: it corresponds to the cases where the Segre variety is dual defective, i.e., the dual variety is not a hyperplane, see
[
4
, Corollary 5.11]
. Moreover, in the context of
[
2
]
, this format is a technical condition that guarantees the vanishing of cohomologies of the bundle
\bigwedge^{r}\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\otimes\mathcal{O}(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})
, where
\mathcal{E}
is the
Friedland-Ottaviani bundle
; see
3.11
. In
[
12
]
, the authors explore what happens when the boundary format is disregarded for almost binary tensors. Although the vanishing results do not hold, the authors show that for almost binary tensors, up to a finite list of cases, one still has the equality
\langle Z_{T}\rangle=\mathbb{P}(H_{T})

[
12
, Theorem 5.7]
. Moreover,
[
12
, Theorem 6.6]
shows that it is sufficient that
T\in\langle Z_{T}\rangle
for a generic tensor
T
to be determined by its singular vector
k
-tuples, thus extending
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
which also relied on the assumption of boundary format.

In this work, motivated by the quest of getting rid of the boundary format hypothesis, we focus on the case when
V=\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}+1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n_{k}+1}
, and
n_{k}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}n_{i}
, i.e., the minimal case beyond boundary format. We perform a thorough analysis of the cohomological vanishings required via Bott-Borel-Weil theorem, and show that the picture is peculiar for the case of three-factor tensors (
k=3
), where there are fewer vanishings, while for higher-order (
k\geq 4
), the vanishings are uniform, see Propositions
3.14
,
3.15
and
3.16
.

In
4.1
and
5.3
we show that the codimension of
\langle Z_{T}\rangle
in
\mathbb{P}(H_{T})
amounts to the dimension of the kernel of a map of cohomologies
\alpha_{T}
. This map is later spelled out in
4.5
, formalizing some steps in
[
12
]
which were not explicit. In
Section

6
we show that in only few cases this map cannot be injective due to dimensional count, these include the cases where
\langle Z_{T}\rangle\subsetneq\mathbb{P}(H_{T})
in
[
12
]
. The map
\alpha_{T}
is full-rank in all experiments we performed, besides one specific family. Our main result actually identifies a family where the map
\alpha_{T}
vanishes identically, and
\langle Z_{T}\rangle
has the maximal codimension possible. Namely:

{restatable*}
Theoremmainthm
Let
T\in V=\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
be a general tensor. Then
\langle Z_{T}\rangle\subset\mathbb{P}(H_{T})
has codimension
n-1
.

This gives a
defective
case for
\langle Z_{T}\rangle
, i.e., its dimension is lower than the maximal possible from parameter counting. Apart from this case, we conjecture that
\alpha_{T}
has maximal rank. Equivalently, we conjecture that

, up to finitely many cases; see Conjectures

2.
Tensors and singular vector tuples

Notation
.

We use
[k]
to denote the set of indices
\{1,\ldots,k\}
. We denote by
x
a vector
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
of
k
variables and
|{\textbf{x}}|\coloneqq x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}
. Define
{\bf 1}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
m{\bf 1}=(m,\ldots,m)\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
.

For every
i\in[k]
we consider an
(n_{i}+1)
-dimensional vector space
V_{i}
over the field
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
. When
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, we will use the notation
V_{i}^{\mathbb{R}}
. On the other hand,
V_{i}
is be assumed to be over
\mathbb{C}
. We denote by
V
the tensor product
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
. This is the vector space of
order
(k+1)
tensors of format
\textbf{n}=(n_{1}+1,\ldots,n_{k}+1)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A tensor
T\in V
is of
rank one
if
T=\textbf{x}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{k}
for some non-zero vectors
\textbf{x}_{j}\in V_{j}
for all
j\in[k]
. Rank-one tensors are parametrized by the Segre variety of format
n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}
, that is, the image of the projective morphism

\mathrm{Seg}\colon\mathbb{P}(V_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}(V_{k})\to\mathbb{P}(V),\ \left([\textbf{x}_{1}],\dots,[\textbf{x}_{k}]\right)\mapsto[\textbf{x}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\textbf{x}_{k}].

Throughout the paper, we adopt the shorthand
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}(V_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}(V_{k})
to indicate the Segre variety. Moreover, we abuse notation identifying a tensor
T\in V
with its class in the projective space
\mathbb{P}(V)
. On each projective space
\mathbb{P}(V_{i})
we fix a quadric
q_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,n_{i}+1}]_{2}
associated to a positive definite real quadratic form
q_{i}^{\mathbb{R}}\colon V_{i}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
Frobenius (or Bombieri-Weyl) inner product
of two complex decomposable tensors
T=\textbf{x}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{k}
and
T^{\prime}=\textbf{y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{y}_{k}
of
V
is

(2.1)

q_{F}(T,T^{\prime})\coloneqq q_{1}(\textbf{x}_{1},\textbf{y}_{1})\cdots q_{k}(\textbf{x}_{k},\textbf{y}_{k})\,,

and it is naturally extended by linearity to every vector in
V
.
We identify all the vector spaces with their duals using the Frobenius inner product.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
T\in V
. A
singular vector
k
-tuple
of
T
is a
k
-tuple
(\textbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{x}_{k})
of nonzero vectors
\textbf{x}_{i}\in V_{i}
such that

(2.2)

\mathrm{minors}_{2\times 2}\begin{bmatrix}T(\textbf{x}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{i-1}\otimes\textbf{x}_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{k})\\
\textbf{x}_{i}\end{bmatrix}=0\quad\forall\,i\in[k]\,,

where

(2.3)

T(\textbf{x}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{i-1}\otimes\textbf{x}_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{k})\coloneqq\sum_{j_{\ell}\in[n_{\ell}]}t_{j_{1}\cdots\,j_{i}\cdots\,j_{k}}\,x_{1,j_{1}}\cdots\widehat{x_{i,j_{i}}}\cdots x_{k,j_{k}}

is the tensor contraction of
T=(t_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}})
with respect to
\textbf{x}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{i-1}\otimes\textbf{x}_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{k}
. The symbol
\widehat{x_{i,j_{i}}}
in (
2.3
) means that the variable
x_{i,j_{i}}
is omitted in the product.
If we interpret
T
as a multi-homogeneous polynomial in the coordinates of each vector
\textbf{x}_{i}
, then the previous tensor contraction corresponds to the gradient
\nabla_{i}T
with respect to the vector
\textbf{x}_{i}=(x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,n_{i}+1})
.

The Euclidean Distance degree (ED-degree) of the Segre variety is defined as the number of critical points of the squared distance function
q_{F}(T-x,T-x)
, where
T
is a general tensor and
x
ranges over the Segre variety. It was shown in
[
7
]
that these critical points coincide with the singular vector
k
-tuples of
T
. Consequently, the ED-degree amounts to the number of singular vector
k
-tuples of a general tensor.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
3
, Theorem 1]

Let
T\in V
be a general tensor. Then
T
has exactly
\mathrm{ED}(\textbf{n})
simple singular vector
tuples, where
\mathrm{ED}(\textbf{n})
equals the coefficient of the monomial
h_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots h_{k}^{n_{k}}
in the polynomial

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\widehat{h}_{i}^{n_{i}+1}-h_{i}^{n_{i}+1}}{\widehat{h}_{i}-h_{i}},\quad\widehat{h}_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}h_{j}\,.

We refer to
[
1
]
for more details on ED-degrees of algebraic varieties.

Definition 2.5
.

Consider a tensor space
V
of format
\textbf{n}=(n_{1}+1,\ldots,n_{k}+1)
. Then
n
is

(1)

a
sub-boundary format
if for all
i\in[k]
we have
n_{i}\leq\sum_{j\neq i}n_{j}
.

(2)

a
boundary format
if for some
i\in[k]
we have
n_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}n_{j}
.

(3)

we have
beyond boundary format
if for some
i\in[k]
the condition
n_{i}>\sum_{j\neq i}n_{j}
holds.

Next, we introduce the main tensor locus studied in this paper.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
T\in V
. We define

(2.4)

Z_{T}\coloneqq\left\{[\textbf{x}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{x}_{k}]\in\mathbb{P}(V)\,\middle|\,(\textbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{x}_{k})\text{ is a singular vector $k$-tuple of }T\right\}.

For a general tensor
T\in V
, the set
Z_{T}
is zero-dimensional, and its cardinality coincides with the ED-degree of the Segre variety, computed by the Friedland–Ottaviani formula of
2.4
.

Throughout the paper, we compare the projective span
\langle Z_{T}\rangle
with another important linear subspace associated to singular vector
k
-tuples which we recall in the next definition.

Definition 2.7
.

The
critical space

H_{T}
of a tensor
T\in V
is the linear subspace of
V
defined by the equations (in the unknowns
z_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}
that serve as linear functions on
V
)

(2.5)

\sum_{i_{\ell}\in[n_{\ell}]}\left(t_{i_{1}\cdots\,p\,\cdots\,i_{k}}\,z_{i_{1}\cdots\,q\,\cdots\,i_{k}}-t_{i_{1}\cdots\,q\,\cdots\,i_{k}}\,z_{i_{1}\cdots\,p\,\cdots\,i_{k}}\right)=0\quad\text{where $1\leq p<q\leq n_{\ell}$ and $\ell\in[k]$.}

The equations in (
2.5
) are obtained after computing the two by two minors of the matrix in (
2.2
) and substituting the relations
z_{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}=x_{1,j_{1}}\cdots x_{k,j_{k}}
. In particular, the equations of
H_{T}
are linear relations among the elements of
Z_{T}
, thus in general
\langle Z_{T}\rangle\subset\mathbb{P}(H_{T})
. Another immediate observation is that
T
always belongs to
H_{T}
, as its coordinates always satisfy the equations in (
2.5
). We recall a result on the dimension of the critical space.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
8
, Proposition 5.6]

Consider a tensor
T\in V
of format
\textbf{n}=(n_{1}+1,\ldots,n_{k}+1)
. Assume
n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{k}
and let
N=\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(n_{i}+1)
.
The dimension of the critical space
H_{T}\subset V
is

(2.6)

\begin{cases}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n_{i}+1)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n_{i}+1}{2}&\text{for $(n_{k}+1)\leq N$}\\
\binom{N+1}{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\binom{n_{i}+1}{2}&\text{for $(n_{k}+1)\geq N$}\,.\end{cases}

Proposition 2.9
.

[
2
, Proposition 3.6]

Let
T
be general in a tensor space
V
of sub-boundary or boundary format. Then
\langle Z_{T}\rangle=\mathbb{P}(H_{T})
.

The containment
\langle Z_{T}\rangle\subset\mathbb{P}(H_{T})
can be strict when the tensor space is beyond boundary format
[
2
, Remark 3.7]
. This leads the authors of
loc. cit.
to pose the natural problem of studying the dimension of
\langle Z_{T}\rangle
beyond boundary format.

Vector bundles, cohomology and representation theory

Notation
.

For every

Let

and let
\mathcal{B}_{i}

\mathbb{P}(V_{i})

H^{q}\bigg(\mathbb{P},\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{i}^{\ast}\mathcal{B}_{i}\bigg)\cong\bigoplus_{|{\bf j}|=q}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}H^{j_{i}}(\mathbb{P}(V_{i}),\mathcal{B}_{i})\,.

be the (finite) set of positive roots of

The

\Lambda=\{\lambda\text{ is a weight}\mid(\lambda,\alpha)\geq 0,\ \forall\ \alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}.

. This defines a line bundle

on the flag variety

\mathcal{E}
be the homogeneous bundle defined by the irreducible representation

[

\pi_{*}L_{\lambda}\cong\mathcal{E}.

Definition 3.4
.

The vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on

above is also denoted

, where
\lambda
is the

corresponding to

Definition 3.5
.

The weight

(Bott-Borel-Weil)

The following statements hold:

(1)

If

(2)

If

. Moreover

Bott’s Algorithm 3.7
.

[

We expose how to find the cohomology groups

in the case when

Let

Thus the dual of the Lie algebra of the Cartan subalgebra

Proposition 3.10

(Bott’s Theorem)

For

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{q}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{r}(k)):=h^{q}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{r}(k))=\begin{cases}\binom{k+n-r}{k}\binom{k-1}{r}\text{ if $q=0\leq r\leq n$ and $k>r$};\\
1\text{ if $0\leq q=r\leq n$ and $k=0$};\\
\binom{-k+r}{-k}\binom{-k-1}{n-r}\text{ if $q=n\geq r\geq 0$ and $k<r-n$};\\
0\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Definition 3.11
.

We now introduce the central vector bundle construction for dealing with singular vector tuples. The
Friedland-Ottaviani vector bundle
on
\mathbb{P}
is defined by

(3.5)

\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{E}_{i}\,,\quad\mathcal{E}_{i}\coloneqq(\pi_{i}^{\ast}\mathcal{Q}_{i})\otimes\mathcal{O}(1,\ldots,1,\overbracket{0}^{i},1,\ldots,1)\quad\forall\,i\in[k]\,.

We have
d:=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})=\dim(\mathbb{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}
. For every
i\in[k]
, the tensor
T
yields a global section of
\mathcal{E}_{i}
, which over the point
([\textbf{x}_{1}],\ldots,[\textbf{x}_{k}])\in\mathbb{P}
is the map

(\lambda_{1}\textbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\textbf{x}_{k})\in\prod_{i=1}^{k}\langle\textbf{x}_{i}\rangle\mapsto[T(\lambda_{1}\textbf{x}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\lambda_{i-1}\textbf{x}_{i-1}\otimes\lambda_{i+1}\textbf{x}_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\lambda_{k}\textbf{x}_{k})]\in{V_{i}}/{\langle\textbf{x}_{i}\rangle}\,.

Combining these
k
sections, the tensor
T
yields a global section
s_{T}
of
\mathcal{E}
. The tuple
(\textbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{x}_{k})
is a singular vector
k
-tuple of
T
if and only if the point
([\textbf{x}_{1}],\ldots,[\textbf{x}_{k}])
is in the zero locus of the section
s_{T}
.
The section
s_{T}
of
\mathcal{E}
yields a homomorphism
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\to\mathcal{O}
of sheaves whose image is contained in the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{Z_{T}}
of the zero locus of
s_{T}
.

Since the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{Z_{T}}
is zero-dimensional for a generic choice of

, it has a resolution given by the Koszul complex

0\to\bigwedge^{d}\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{d}}\bigwedge^{d-1}\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{d-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\varphi_{2}}\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\to\mathcal{I}_{Z_{T}}\to 0.\,

We will split the previous complex into short exact sequences, therefore we define
\mathcal{F}_{i+1}\coloneqq\left(\bigwedge^{i}\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\right)/\mathrm{Im}(\varphi_{i+1})
and use the shorthand
\mathcal{F}_{i}({\bf 1})
to denote the tensor product
\mathcal{F}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}({\bf 1})
. For all integer

we use the shorthand
\mathcal{E}^{(r)}
to denote
\left(\bigwedge^{r}\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\right)\otimes\mathcal{O}({\bf 1})
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
V
be the vector space of
(k+1)
-order tensors of format

Proof.

By

Suppose there exists an index
i
such

and
2r_{i}+1-r>r_{i}

. This implies that

for all

Suppose there exists an index

The last case is when for all

as in the third line of the equalities (

∎

Proposition 3.15

(ii)

r_{i}=r

i
as in the second line of the equalities (

The weights associated to

are respectively

and

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose

or

otherwise. There is a unique, up to scalar multiplication, non-zero

-equivariant map

\bigotimes_{j=1}^{k}\left(\mathcal{S}^{n-n_{j}-1}\mathbb{C}^{n_{j}+1}\right)\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}+1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n+2})^{\ast}\to\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{S}^{n-n_{j}-2}\mathbb{C}^{n_{j}+1}\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n+2},

where

Proof.

The domain can be decomposed by irreducible
\mathrm{SL}(n_{1}+1)\times\dots\times\mathrm{SL}(n_{k}+1)\times\mathrm{SL}(n+2)
-representations as

Lemma 4.4
.

The map

\varphi:\bigotimes_{j=1}^{k}\left(\mathcal{S}^{n-n_{j}-1}\mathbb{C}^{n_{j}+1}\right)\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}+1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n+2})^{\ast}\to\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{S}^{n-n_{j}-2}\mathbb{C}^{n_{j}+1}\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n+2}

defined by

\varphi(f_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes f_{k}\otimes T)=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}=1}^{n_{1}+1,\dots,n_{k}+1}\left(\otimes_{j=1}^{k}\partial_{i_{j}}f_{j}\right)\otimes T_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}},

where
T=\sum_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}e_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{i_{k}}\otimes T_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}
with
T_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
, is
\mathrm{SL}(n_{1}+1)\times\dots\times\mathrm{SL}(n+2)
-equivariant.

Proof.

Let

, and
\textbf{x}_{i}=(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{n_{i}+1}})

\displaystyle\varphi(g\cdot f_{1}(\textbf{x}_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes f_{k}(\textbf{x}_{k})\otimes T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}=1}^{n_{1}+1,\dots,n_{k}+1}\left(\otimes_{j=1}^{k}(g\cdot\partial_{i_{j}})f_{j}(g_{j}^{-1}\cdot\textbf{x}_{j})\right)\otimes g_{k+1}\cdot T_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}

\displaystyle=g\cdot\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}=1}^{n_{1}+1,\dots,n_{k}+1}\left(\otimes_{j=1}^{k}\partial_{i_{j}}f_{j}(\textbf{x}_{j})\right)\otimes T_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}

\displaystyle=g\cdot\varphi(f_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes f_{k}\otimes T).

This shows the claim.

In this section we discuss the technical aspects to compare the dimensions of the domain and codomain of the map

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose

. Then
\binom{a+b-1}{b}\binom{a+b-1}{a}\leq\binom{a+b-2}{b}\binom{a+b-2}{a}(a+b+2)

and

Proof.

Suppose first that

(6.1)

(b-1)(a-1)(a+b+2)\geq(a+b-1)^{2}.

Since
(a+b+2)>(a+b-1)
, it is enough to check

(a-1)(b-1)\geq(a+b-1)
.
We may write this as

(a-2)b-2a+2\geq 0,

which is satisfied for
b\geq a\geq 4
.

When
a=1
,
the inequality is only satisfied for
a=b=1
. When
a=2
,
the inequality becomes

(b-1)(b+4)\geq(b+1)^{2},

which is satisfied for

. If

, the inequality becomes

2(b-1)(b+5)\geq(b+2)^{2},

which holds whenever
b\geq 4
. Moreover, the original inequality is also satisfied if

.
∎

.

Let

Lemma 6.3
.

The inequality
[(k-1)a-1]^{k}(ka+2)\geq(ka-1)^{k}
holds
for any

and

Proof.

Since
(ka+2)>(ka-1)
the statement is proven once
we prove that
[(k-1)a-1]^{k}\geq(ka-1)^{k-1}

For any fixed

The function

is differentiable and its derivative

Hence for any fixed

We have to show that

to finish the proof. Note that

is increasing. Now consider

Thus

is a differentiable function in the interval

, the inequality does not hold
and gives the case
(2,2,2,5)
. For any
k\geq 4
and
a=1
, one can check that the inequality (
6.2
) is satisfied.

Remark 6.5
.

Thus, whenever
n_{j}=a\geq 1
(for every
j
) and
k\geq 3
, the only exception to the inequality (
6.2
) is when
n_{1}=n_{2}=n_{3}=1
.

6.3.
General case: set-up

Here we look at
k
-tuples as unordered collections of positive integers.

Given the
k
-tuple
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})=(a,\ldots,a)
as in the previous section, an
\ell
-increment of such a
k
-tuple is a
k
-tuple of the form

(a+t_{1},\ldots,a+t_{\ell},a,\ldots,a)
, where
t_{j}\geq 1
.

The
0
-increment of
(a,\ldots,a)
is the tuple itself and it is clear that any tuple is an increment of some tuple of this form.

Suppose we start from the
\ell
-increment of the
k
-tuple
(a,\ldots,a)
.
Let
S=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}t_{i}
and
R_{j}=\sum_{i\neq j,i=1}^{\ell}t_{i}
.

Then the inequality we have to prove is

(6.3)

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}((k-1)a-1+R_{j})\cdot((k-1)a-1+S)^{k-\ell}\geq(ka-1+S)^{k-1}.

Note that for
\ell=0
,
k\geq 3
and
a\geq 2
or
k\geq 4
and
a=1
, this is established in Lemma
6.3
.
The latter case is the base of our strong induction on
\ell
. Clearly,
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
.
We are done if we prove that the validity of the inequality for the
\ell
-increment implies the validity of the inequality for the
(\ell+1)
-increment.
Thus, assume that
the inequality (
6.3
)
holds true and we would like to prove that

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}((k-1)a-1+R_{j}+t_{\ell+1})\cdot((k-1)a-1+S+t_{\ell+1})^{k-\ell-1}((k-1)a+S))\geq(ka-1+S+t_{\ell+1})^{k-1},

where
t_{\ell+1}\geq 1
. The right-hand side can be written as

\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{m}(ka-1+S)^{k-1-m}t_{\ell+1}^{m}.

Now, we look at the coefficient of
t^{m}_{\ell+1}

on the left-hand side for
m\leq k-1
. This is given by

(6.4)

\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom{k-\ell-1}{r}((k-1)a-1+S)^{k-\ell-r}\cdot\left(\sum_{J\subset[\ell],\ \ |J|=\ell-m-r}\prod_{f=1}^{\ell-m+r}((k-1)a-1+R_{j_{f}})\right),

where
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell-m+r}\}\subset[\ell]
.
Each
summand of (
6.4
) is of the form

\prod_{f=1}^{\ell-m+r}((k-1)a-1+R_{j_{f}})\cdot((k-1)a-1+S)^{k-\ell-r}.

Since
\ell-m+r\leq\ell
, by strong induction, we have

\prod_{f=1}^{\ell-m+r}((k-1)a-1+R_{j_{f}})\cdot((k-1)a-1+S)^{k-\ell-r+m}\geq(ka-1+S)^{k-1}

Since
(k-1)a-1+S<ka-1+S
,
we have
\frac{1}{((k-1)a-1+S)^{m}}\geq\frac{1}{(ka-1+S)^{m}}
for
m\geq 0
. Multiplying this inequality with the previous we obtain

\prod_{f=1}^{\ell-m+r}((k-1)a-1+R_{j_{f}})\cdot((k-1)a-1+S)^{k-\ell-r}\geq(ka-1+S)^{k-1-m}.

Thus our original inequality is proven if we prove that

\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom{k-\ell-1}{r}\cdot\binom{\ell}{\ell-(m-r)}(ka-1+S)^{k-1-m}\geq\binom{k-1}{m}(ka-1+S)^{k-1-m}.

Actually, this is an equality.

Lemma 6.6
.

One has

\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom{k-\ell-1}{r}\cdot\binom{\ell}{\ell-(m-r)}=\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom{k-\ell-1}{r}\cdot\binom{\ell}{m-r}=\binom{k-1}{m},

for any
0\leq r\leq m\leq k-1
and any
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a set of cardinality
k-1

and let
X_{m}
be the collection of all subsets of
X
of cardinality
m
. Fix a subset
C\subset X
of cardinality
\ell
.
Let
C_{m-r}
be the collection of subsets of
C
of cardinality
m-r
and let
(X\setminus C)_{r}
be the collection of subsets of
X
disjoint from
C
and with cardinality
r
. We have a bijection
X_{m}\rightarrow\cup_{r=0}^{m}C_{m-r}\times(X\setminus C)_{r}

given by

A\mapsto(A\cap C,A\cap(X\setminus C))
, which shows the equality.
∎

This proves the inequality (
6.3
) for any
\ell
.

Remark 6.7
.

The induction hypothesis in
6.3
excludes the case
(2,2,2,5)
, meaning it is necessary to verify the case
(2,a+1,b+1,a+b+2)
where
a\geq b\geq 2
. The inequality becomes
(a+b)^{3}\leq(a+b+3)(a+b-1)ab
, so it is enough to verify
(a+b)^{2}\leq(a+b-1)ab
. Rearranging the terms we obtain
a^{2}(b-1)+b^{2}(a-1)-3ab\geq 0
, and if
a\geq b\geq 3
, it simplifies to
2(a^{2}+b^{2})-3ab\geq 0
since
(a-b)^{2}\geq 0
.

Two cases are missing, namely
b=1
and
b=2.
In the first the inequality to be verified is
(a+1)^{3}\leq(a+4)a^{2}
. This is equivalent to
1\leq a(a+3)
, which is satisfied for
a\geq 4
.

If
b=2
, the inequality becomes
(a+2)^{2}\leq 2(a+5)(a+1)a
. The polynomial
2(a+5)(a+1)a-(a+2)^{2}
has roots in the intervals
(-7,-6),(-2,-1),(1,2)
and its leading coefficient is positive, therefore it is positive for all
a\geq 2
.

Therefore the only cases where the inequality is not satisfied for
k\geq 3
are
(2,2,2,5)
,
(2,2,3,6)
,
(2,2,4,7)
.

Let
V

.

8.
An interpretation of the map
\alpha_{T}
in terms of Koszul cohomology

In this final and very short section, we explain the connections of Conjectures
7.2
and
7.3
to Koszul cohomology. For the ease of notation, we consider the
3
-factor case. Let
W
be a vector space of dimension
m=a+b+2
, and let
A=\mathbb{C}^{a+1}
and
B=\mathbb{C}^{b+1}
. Then a tensor
T\in W^{*}\otimes A\otimes B
may be seen as a linear map

W\rightarrow A\otimes B
. Since
T
is generic, this linear map is injective and we may regard
W

W\subset|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1,1)|

X=\mathbb{P}(A)\times\mathbb{P}(B)=\mathbb{P}^{a}\times\mathbb{P}^{b}

m>\dim(X)

(8.1)

W\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(1,1)\longrightarrow 0.

Twisting this map of locally free sheaves with
\mathcal{O}_{X}(b-2,a-2)
and taking global sections we obtain the dual of the desired map
\alpha_{T}
between vector spaces.

Consider the Koszul complex associated to the surjection (
8.1
) (upon twisting by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(b-2,a-2)

\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
, where

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\wedge^{i}W\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(b-1-i,a-1-i),\mbox{ for }\ \ i=0,\ldots,m.

In this notation, recall that the map we are interested in is the dual of the map
\delta_{1}:H^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
.
Since the complex
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
is exact, its hypercohomology

\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{\bullet})=0
. Letting
E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{p})
, the latter condition is equivalent to the vanishing
E^{p,q}_{\infty}=0
for every
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Consider now the following 3-complex

(8.2)

H^{m-2}(\mathcal{F}_{m})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{m}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{m-2}(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{m-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{m-2}(\mathcal{F}_{m-2}).

Note that the cohomology

0<j<m-2=a+b
and every

d^{m-1,m-2}_{m-1}:\ker(\delta_{m-1})/\mathrm{Im}(\delta_{m})\longrightarrow\mathrm{coker}(\delta_{1}),

where the left-hand side is the homology of the
3
-complex (
8.2
) and
\delta_{1}
is the map on global sections introduced before. Since
E_{\infty}=0
,

is an isomorphism.
Now note that the
3

8.2
) is dual to

\wedge^{2}W\longrightarrow W\otimes(A\otimes B)\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{2}A\otimes\mathcal{S}^{2}B,

whose middle homology is
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{S}(R,\mathbb{C})_{(2,2)}

S=\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}(W)
and

is the coordinate ring of the Segre variety
X
and is naturally an
S
-module. The latter
\mathrm{Tor}

S
-module is by definition the Koszul cohomology module

\mathcal{K}_{1,1}(X,\mathcal{O}(1,1),W)

[
5
]

Choose a subspace

of dimension

and define
