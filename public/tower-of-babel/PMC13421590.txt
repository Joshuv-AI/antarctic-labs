Energy-efficient wireless network control via spatio-temporal deep learning and multi-agent reinforcement learning
Authors: Khalil M Abdelnaby, Mohammed A F Al-Husainy, Mohammad O Alhawarat, Mohamed A Rohaim, Khairy M Assar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid expansion of wireless data traffic is placing increasing strain on the energy consumption of current communication networks, intensifying the tension between performance and sustainability objectives. In interference-intensive multiple access scenarios such as power-domain non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA), energy-efficient optimization is particularly challenging due to the strong coupling between power control and resource allocation decisions. In order to solve this issue, this paper introduces an AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework (AEEOF), which uses deep spatio-temporal learning and reinforcement learning to provide adaptive and energy-aware network control. The proposed framework incorporates a Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) to learn spatial interference relationships and a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to capture temporal traffic dynamics, embedding the resulting representations into a Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (MADDPG) controller to support sequential decision-making. Such a design provides the framework with the ability to dynamically distribute power and timing policies based on changes in network conditions. Extensive simulations in a realistic 5G-oriented environment of high interference levels prove the significant performance improvement. The suggested solution can save up to 15% of energy and make the energy use more efficient by about 40%, which is the number of bits delivered per joule. The overall system throughput goes up by 6.25%, the cell-edge user data rate goes up by up to 60%, the fairness goes up by 20%, and the chance of an outage goes down by 70%. A systematic ablation study with three architectural variants validates the individual contribution of each core component — the ST-GCN spatial module, the GRU temporal module, and the MADDPG reinforcement learning controller. Comparative evaluation against conventional orthogonal and non-AI-assisted baselines further supports the effectiveness of the proposed framework within the studied setting. These findings indicate that intelligent spatio-temporal learning is a promising approach for improving energy efficiency and network performance in interference-intensive wireless environments, as demonstrated within the studied 5G-oriented simulation setting.Keywords: Energy-efficient wireless networks, Artificial intelligence, Spatio-temporal learning, Reinforcement learning, Interference management, Resource allocation, Multi-agent systems, Non-orthogonal multiple access, Sustainable communications, 5G and beyondSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The rapid proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) devices, smart city infrastructure, autonomous systems, and data-intensive applications is imposing increasingly complex demands on modern wireless communication networks. In addition to the support of the high data rates and very low latency, current networks must be able to work in an energy-efficient and environmentally sustainable way, especially as wireless networks are now a leading source of global energy use1,2. This challenge is further compounded by the emerging requirements of 5G-Advanced and early 6G networks, which target massive connectivity, intelligent service delivery, and pervasive automation across heterogeneous deployment environments3–5.

Energy efficiency is becoming a central design goal of the next-generation wireless systems, both due to a reduction in cost of operation and due to a more general sense of sustainability and climate concerns6–8. The systematic investigations and systemwide measurements have repeatedly demonstrated that radio access networks, dense deployment of base stations, and dynamic changes of traffic are the leading factors contributing to excessive energy usage in cellular and IoT-enabled systems2,9. As a result, the optimization of the energy consumption and the quality-of-service (QoS) guarantees has become a primary research question in both the physical-layer design, resource allocation, network control, and cross-layer optimization3,10.

Classical optimisation methods struggle to manage the growing complexity, non-convexity, and uncertainty of contemporary wireless systems. Time-varying traffic loads, stochastic channel conditions, user mobility, and heterogeneous service demands collectively limit the applicability of static or model-driven approaches in realistic deployment scenarios11,12. Such constraints have encouraged a shift in paradigm to data-driven and learning-based methods that can be driven by dynamic environments with minimum human intervention13,14.

The artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) have also become important facilitators to intelligent and energy- conscious wireless networks. Recent research shows the usefulness of AI in the optimization of spectrum allocation, power control, user association, and management of interference in complex and uncertain conditions13,14. More specifically, the deep learning and reinforcement learning methods have demonstrated great promise to be able to capture the spatio-temporal dependencies and learn near-optimal control policies directly on network data, without making recourse to overly simplistic analytical models15,16. The capabilities are inherently suitable to the vision of 6G networks as self-optimizing, context-conscious, and sustainability-oriented communication systems4,17.

In addition to communications, AI-based energy optimization has been intensively explored in other related fields like smart grids, smart buildings, industrial automation, and renewable energy systems, and this is evidence of its potential to vastly decrease energy usage without impacting system performance18–20. Such interdisciplinary developments also enhance the appropriateness of AI as a unifying construct in dealing with energy efficiency issues across cyber-physical systems, such as wireless communication systems21–23.

In the wireless world, resource management of energy efficiency is closely related to radio resource management and multiple access schemes. Non-Orthogonal Multiple Access (NOMA) has been widely investigated as a possible method of enhancing spectral efficiency and supporting massive connectivity by sharing spectral resources, including time-frequency, by multiplexing in the power domain24–27. The systems based on NOMA present special issues of interference coupling, power, and successive interference cancellation that make energy-efficient operation quite difficult, particularly in dense and nonhomogeneous networks16,28,29. Although NOMA cannot be implemented as a compulsory feature of the existing 5G standards, this framework is still an excellent research tool to address more complex interference-aware optimization techniques and stress-testing techniques based on intelligent control actions in highly coupled resource constraints.

The recent developments in AI-assisted wireless communication also demonstrate that the intelligent optimization of future networks is highly important. Deep learning methods have also been applied successfully on the physical layer to channel estimation, signal detection, and resource allocation, and have been shown to have better robustness and adaptability than traditional techniques14. Besides, reinforcement learning-related methods have demonstrated the potential to balance energy and quality of service needs in ultra-dense and heterogeneous network deployments15,30. Such advances follow the wider trends in 6G research, which imagine close collaboration between AI and communication system design4,17.

Although these have been made, whether in the next generation wireless networks, energy-efficient operation can be achieved is one of the open challenges in wireless networks. Current solutions have a tendency to treat individual components in isolation, and to take a static environment, or ignore the spatio-temporal correlations of real network traffic and channel dynamics2,9. Moreover, most of the learning-based methods have a problem of scalability, lack of generalization, or systematic validation in real operating environments10,31. Such challenges can only be dealt with the aid of combined structures that collectively take into account the temporal evolution, spatial interference patterns, and long-term energy optimization objectives.

Based on these observations, this paper will suggest a smart AI-based framework of dynamic energy-efficient resource optimization in higher-level wireless networks. The proposed approach will aim at capturing the dynamics of the complex network, adapting to time-varying conditions, and improving the energy efficiency in a sustained manner without affecting the service performance by utilizing the principles of deep spatio-temporal learning and reinforcement learning. The real-world network conditions are considered, and the structure is tested with the traditional optimization strategies to prove the efficiency, strength, and scalability.

The paper deals with the problem of the attainment of the energy-efficient operation of advanced wireless networks with dense deployments of the wireless networks, heterogeneous services, and dynamic traffic conditions. In contrast to current research that deals with the individual elements of optimization, or a simplified setting, the paper presents a compound and intelligence-led structure, which collectively addresses spatio-temporal dynamics, interference coupling, and long-term energy efficiency goals. Literature review

The optimization of energy efficiency of wireless communication networks has been broadly studied from various angles, such as the design of the network architecture, radio resource management, and cross-layer optimization. The initial research largely revolved around analytical and heuristic methods of lowering power usage in cellular systems, and primarily on energy-conscious scheduling, adaptive modulation, and sleep modes of base stations9,11. Although these techniques are important in giving a theoretical understanding, their applicability is usually constrained by oversimplifying assumptions that may not reflect the complexity of the modern heterogeneous and ultra-dense networks.

As the IoT and smart environments emerged, the focus of research shifted to the associated field of energy efficiency in networking in various areas of application. Research papers on wireless IoT devices and machine-type communications underlined the trade-offs of reliability of communications in terms of latency and energy consumption12,31. Correspondingly, network topologies and wireless access technology performance studies demonstrated that fixed settings cannot be used to address the dynamic needs of modern services32,33, which drives the adaptive and intelligent control measures.

Simultaneously with the development of wireless networking, AI-controlled energy optimization has made a big breakthrough in smart grids, in renewable energy systems, and in industrial robotics. In energy consumption prediction, fault diagnosis, and operational optimization of power systems and smart infrastructures, machine learning models have been utilized21,34–36. The use of deep learning and hybrid AI models has proven to be more accurate and resistant to forecasting nonlinear non-stationary data in the energy domain37. These achievements are excellent indicators that data-driven intelligence will be relevant and able to respond to challenging issues of energy management, which motivates similar approaches in wireless communication networks.

The AI methods have been gaining popularity in the context of wireless systems to optimize the allocation of radio resources and the control of networks. It has been suggested that reinforcement learning-based models can dynamically adjust the level of power, scheduling decisions, and network configurations depending on time-varying traffic and channel characteristics15,30. These methods allow independent decision-making and lifelong learning and are therefore appropriate in ultra-dense and heterogeneous deployments. Yet, most of the available literature is dedicated to a particular network layer or a small number of scenarios, which limit their generalizability.

The concept of NOMA has attracted a significant amount of research interest due to its ability to increase spectral efficiency and its capability to enable high connectivity. Principles of power-domain multiplexing and successive interference cancellation in cooperative and relay-assisted NOMA systems were developed based on the foundational studies24–27. Then, works delved into resource allocation, relay selection, and the management of interference techniques that would enhance the throughput and energy efficiency of NOMA-based networks16,28,29. In spite of these contributions, a majority of the current approaches of NOMA optimization are based on assumptions that are static or use the convex approximation, thus being less versatile to dynamic network conditions.

Current research has recently started to combine AI methods and NOMA systems to overcome these limitations. Resource allocation schemes that are based on deep learning have been proposed to manage interference patterns and imperfect channel state information16. However, such strategies are mostly short-term based in terms of performance optimization and fail to explicitly consider the long-term energy efficiency or long-term sustainability goals. In addition, most of the current research on AI-based assistance of NOMA does not include extensive validation in real traffic dynamics and heterogeneous networks.

In addition to NOMA, new research has also examined novel wireless technology to enhance energy efficiency, such as reconfigurable intelligent surfaces, wireless power transfer, and cognitive radio networks38,39. Although these technologies have potential prospects of saving energy, their application to practice network structures presents new complexity and control issues. The proper utilization of such technologies would need clever coordination systems that can combine to harm a myriad of system parameters in the long run.

At a bigger picture, recent surveys and vision papers on 6G wireless networks underscore the core of AI in supporting sustainable, intelligent, and adaptive communication systems4,5,17. As these works point out, it is important to go beyond the fragmented methods of optimization to a comprehensive approach that combines learning, prediction, and control across the different layers of the network. Nonetheless, numerous of the suggested architectures are just conceptual, with very little concern for the actual algorithmic design as well as empirical validation.

Altogether, current literature shows that significant advances in the field of AI-enhanced energy optimization and advanced multiple access methods have been achieved. Nevertheless, there are still serious gaps concerning the integration of spatio-temporal modeling, energy-conscious long-term decisions, and support for realistic wireless conditions. The existing methods focus on one or two parts of the issue, or they are based on limiting assumptions that restrict their application. These constraints drive the creation of combined AI-based models that can collaboratively address the time dynamics, space interference interaction, and energy-saving goals, which is the subject of the current study. Several advanced resource management frameworks have recently emerged that are relevant to the comparative context of this study. Multi-agent proximal policy optimisation (MAPPO) has been applied to cooperative spectrum and power allocation in multi-cell wireless networks, demonstrating decentralised execution with convergence behaviour comparable to centralised genie-aided algorithms40. Attention-based and transformer-enhanced architectures have been proposed for dynamic SIC ordering and power allocation in NOMA systems, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture inter-user dependencies and achieve near-optimal fairness without exhaustive search41. Additionally, model-based reinforcement learning frameworks that integrate explicit world models for channel occupancy and traffic dynamics have shown meaningful reductions in sample complexity for wireless channel access problems42. While these approaches represent important recent advances, they differ from the proposed AEEOF framework in one or more of the following respects: they do not jointly model spatial interference topology and temporal traffic dynamics within a unified spatio-temporal representation; they are evaluated in single-agent or simplified multi-agent settings without explicit outage and fairness constraints in the reward design; or they focus on channel access rather than end-to-end energy efficiency optimisation. A comprehensive comparative evaluation of AEEOF against these more recent frameworks constitutes an important direction for future work, as discussed in Section VII.

Conceptual architecture of the AI-enhanced energy optimization framework (AEEOF)

This paragraph introduces the conceptual design of the proposed AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework (AEEOF), which will help to overcome the energy efficiency challenge that emerges in the dense, interference-limited wireless networks that utilize NOMA. Rather than relying on fixed optimisation rules, AEEOF adopts a learning-based paradigm capable of jointly addressing the spatial interference structure and temporal traffic dynamics that govern energy consumption in contemporary wireless systems32,43,44. The framework creates a perception-decision-adaptation cycle that monitors the network condition and optimizes policies of resource allocation, as well as communicates with the environment to enhance long-term energy performance and Quality of Service (QoS) assurances. As depicted in Fig. 1, AEEOF is structured into four closely coupled functional layers, which together process raw network observations into control actions that are energy efficient. The conceptual description of these layers follows; the mathematical formulation and algorithmic implementation of the layers appear in Sections IV and V.

Conceptual architecture of the proposed AI-enhanced energy optimization framework (AEEOF).

Network state perception and problem contextualization

The AEEOF layer 1 causes the building of a detailed and orderly portrayal of the network state. Dynamic interference coupling, heterogeneous user demands, and time-varying wireless channels provide the main reasons of energy inefficiency in NOMA-based systems. In order to encompass these factors, the framework monitors a multidimensional state vector that comprises channel gains, inter-user interference, user traffic demands, buffer states, and transmission power levels.

The framework does not consider users individually, and this is clearly seen in the fact that it explicitly recognizes that users with the same time-frequency resources in NOMA are coupled in terms of interference. This inspires the graphical representation of the network, where nodes are the users or base stations and the edges represent the relationship of interference and channel correlation. This explicit model allows the learning model to reason on collective behavior and space relations that are essential in power allocation, that is, energy conscious. Formal descriptions of the system model, state variables, constraints, and optimization objectives are given in Section IV.

Spatio-temporal feature extraction via deep representation learning

AEEOF layer two is concerned with a high-level extraction of spatio-temporal features of the raw network state. Consumption patterns of the energy in wireless networks are spatially correlated because of interference effects as well as temporally dependent because of traffic variation, user movement, and channel attenuation. These dependencies should be captured for predictive and proactive energy optimization.

The framework makes use of the ST-GCN to have a model of spatial relationships. The ST-GCN works on the built interference graph, which allows the model to incorporate the information shared with the neighboring nodes and learn the effects of local decisions on the overall energy efficiency. This enables the framework to detect dominant patterns of interference and spatial bottlenecks of energy that cannot be detected using traditional learning (based on vectors).

The framework combines a GRU network, which is used to operate chains of past network states45,46. GRU captures the short-term variations as well as long-term variations in the demand and channel conditions to the traffic, thus the model forecasts the future operations in the network as opposed to its reaction being myopic. ST-GCN + GRU becomes a single whole spatio-temporal representation that is informed by the changing operational conditions of the network.

Multi-agent decision making and energy-efficient control

The third tier of AEEOF converts the acquired spatio-temporal representations into action control decisions. Due to the distributed character of wireless networks as well as the interaction of users in NOMA, the multi-agent sequential decision-making process is formulated as an energy-optimizing problem. Every base station or user agent seeks to maximize its local transmission strategy whilst taking into consideration the actions of others.

In order to overcome this difficulty, AEEOF uses a Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (MADDPG) framework. In centralized training, agents use the information of the global state to acquire cooperative policies that reduce the long-term energy consumption, without violating the QoS constraints, including minimum data rates, outage probability constraints, and fairness requirements. Agents act in a decentralized way during execution, and they take real-time choices by relying on local readings and received policies.

Continuous control actions, such as transmission power allocation and scheduling decisions, are the output of this layer, and they have a direct impact on the instantaneous and cumulative network energy efficiency. Section V expounds on the reinforcement learning formulation, reward design, and policy update mechanisms.

Environment interaction and performance evaluation loop

The last level of AEEOF is the one that defines the feedback loop between the learning actors and the wireless setting. The chosen actions are input into a 5G-NOMA simulator implemented in high-fidelity in MATLAB, which runs simulations of realistic channel propagation, interference dynamics, and traffic dynamics. The environment reacts by changing the network condition and supplying feedback as rewards and performance measures.

This is a closed-loop relation that allows the agents to improve their policies on the basis of trial-and-error learning. Measures used in performance assessments are energy efficiency (bits/Joule), overall energy usage, system throughput, user fairness, and outage probability. The framework is evaluated by means of benchmarking against traditional OMA schemes, heuristic schemes of allocating the power of the NOMA, and non-AI baselines. In addition to a systematic ablation study detailed in Section VI-G, where three variants of the framework are assessed by selectively removing the ST-GCN, GRU, and MADDPG modules, respectively, to isolate and quantify the individual contributions of each architectural component to overall performance. Section VI gives detailed results and settings of the experiment.

To conclude, the AEEOF offers a unified and scalable framework combining spatio-temporal deep learning with multi-agent reinforcement learning to address the energy efficiency optimization in wireless networks with interference issues. The framework can explicitly model the structures of spatial interference and temporal evolution of traffic, thereby surpassing major limitations of conventional optimization strategies. This conceptual architecture has been used as a blueprint to guide the formal system modeling and algorithm development that is done in the following sections.

Proposed AI-driven energy optimization framework

This section provides the suggested AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework (AEEOF) as a framework that combines spatio-temporal deep learning and multi-agent reinforcement learning to facilitate adaptive and power-efficient interference-intensive wireless network control. The structure would meet the non-stationary and highly coupled nature of power allocation decisions and scheduling decisions in the NOMA-based systems. AEEO allows improvement in energy efficiency through proactive optimization of spatial interference structures and temporal traffic dynamics, with Quality of Service (QoS) guarantees, by learning the relationship between these two factors jointly.

Overview and design rationale

The essence of the problem on energy efficient optimization of wireless networks is the fact that users sharing radio resources are highly connected, i.e., in the power domain, non-orthogonal access. Conventional optimization methods can only poorly address the non-convexity that arises, as well as dynamic uncertainty, whereas the solutions that are static or rules-based are not adaptable. AEEOF attempts to address these drawbacks by posing the energy optimization as a series of sequential decision-making and resolving the problem with learning-based methods.

The framework is based on three major design principles.

The first is that space awareness is required to model the relationship of interference between users sharing the same spectrum. Second, there should be a sense of time so as to record the progression of traffic and the variability of channels with time. Third, adaptive decisions are required to keep on optimizing power and scheduling policy based on the realized results. In order to meet such demands, AEEOF incorporates a ST-GCN and a GRU into a multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) architecture. The general architecture is the closed-loop interaction as shown in Fig. 2, whereby the learned policies interact continuously with the wireless environment.

Network state representation and data generation

The wireless network is defined at every decision interval by a high-dimensional state vector that represents physical-layer as well as traffic-level information. The state representation covers gains of channel, level of inter-user interference, signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratios (SINRs), demand of traffic per user, occupancy of buffer, and the current power allocation rates. In order to explicitly model interference coupling, they model the network state as a graph, with users or base stations as nodes and the strength of interference and channel correlations as edges52–54[55-57]. The high-fidelity MATLAB-based 5 G-oriented NOMA simulator is used to generate training data. The simulator gives a real-life channel propagation, fading dynamics, user movement, and bursty traffic models. The combination of network states, actions, and the resultant performance measures is gathered per simulation episode. The produced data is brought to normalcy and arranged into a time series to allow repeated learning. This simulated model helps ensure that there is controlled.

experimentation and, at the same time, offering enough diversity to facilitate sound learning of policies.

Spatio-temporal feature learning (ST-GCN + GRU)

In order to derive informative representations of the structured network state, AEEOF tries to use a two-stage spatio-temporal feature learning module.

A spatio-temporal graph convolutional network (ST-GCN) is used to implement the spatial feature extractor. The ST-GCN uses the information of the neighboring nodes directly on the interference graph; it learns the value of the local power allocation decisions to the global interference and energy consumption. This allows the framework to find spatial energy bottlenecks and dominant interference paths which are not modeled through traditional vector-based models.

The interference graph is constructed dynamically at each decision interval. The node set corresponds to the K active user equipment’s in the cell. The edge weight is set proportional to the instantaneous interference-to-noise ratio (INR) between the two users: . The graph is updated at each time step to reflect real-time changes in channel conditions, user positions, and power allocation states, ensuring that the ST-GCN operates on a current and accurate representation of the interference topology.

The implementation of the temporal feature extractor is based on the GRU network. GRU processes time series of states of historical network and learns both the short-term dynamics and long-term traffic patterns. It is such a time modelling capacity that enables the framework to predict future network situations and proactively modify control measures instead of responding to them45,58,59,60.

The outputs of the ST-GCN and GRU modules are combined to create a single spatio-temporal latent representation, and that is the input of the decision module of the reinforcement learning.

Two-stage learning pipeline

The overall learning procedure of AEEOF follows a two-stage design. In the first stage, the ST-GCN–GRU module is pre-trained in a supervised fashion using labelled samples generated by the high-fidelity MATLAB-based 5G-NOMA simulator. Each sample consists of a network state snapshot — capturing channel gains, inter-user interference levels, SINRs, per-user traffic demands, buffer occupancies, and current power allocation levels — paired with a ground-truth label derived from the simulator’s known optimal control response under that state. The supervised objective is to train the ST-GCN–GRU encoder to produce latent representations that reliably identify and distinguish energy-critical network states, including congestion-prone, interference-dominated, and power-inefficient configurations, that require adaptive control intervention. The classification metrics reported in Section VI-C (accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score) quantify the reliability and consistency of this representation learning stage. In the second stage, the pre-trained ST-GCN–GRU module serves as a fixed state encoder, and its output latent representations are passed as state inputs to the MADDPG reinforcement learning agents. The Reinforcement Learning (RL) stage then learns sequential power allocation and scheduling policies entirely through trial-and-error interaction with the wireless environment, using the encoded state representations as the basis for policy gradient computation. This two-stage design ensures that the RL agents operate on structured, discriminative state abstractions, which reduces noise in policy updates and promotes stable convergence of the multi-agent learning process.

Algorithmic flow and computational complexity

The general algorithmic procedure of AEEOF is presented in Fig. 3. At every episode, the environment is launched, and the agents are in the initial network state. The spatio-temporal feature learning module commits the state, the MADDPG agents choose control actions, and the environment updates states and rewards. The agents update their policies, then, according to the experience gained.

Integrated ST-GCN–GRU–MADDPG Framework for Energy-Efficient NOMA Control.

Computationally, the overwhelming complexity is due to the ST-GCN and GRU modules and the policy network updates. Although the training phase is more expensive to compute, inference at execution is only forwarding of trained networks, and thus, real-time deployment is possible. The decentralized implementation with the centralized training paradigm also contributes to the scalability as the size of the network grows.

The entire operation flow of the proposed AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework (AEEOF) that combines spatio-temporal deep learning and multi-agent reinforcement learning to optimize energy efficiencies in interference-intensive NOMA-based wireless networks is outlined in Algorithm 1. Based on the principle of centralized training and decentralized implementation, the algorithm allows cooperative learning and, at the same time, provides scalability and relevance in real-time.

The algorithm starts with the phase of initialization, during which each learning agent initializes its actor and critic networks. The actor network produces unceasing control activities, i.e., power levels of transmissions, timing choices, and the critic network determines the long-term worth of the activities. Also, target networks are initialized to stabilize training. A common replay buffer is developed to save previous experiences so that off-policy learning is achieved, along with better sample usage. Also, the original interference graph is built according to channel conditions and user proximity, explicitly modeling the relationship between spatial interferences.

The algorithm runs in the course of training, and during each episode, it is evaluated as an independent realization of network dynamics. The wireless environment is perturbed to the initial state at the start of every episode, and the initial network state is monitored. The network state is fed into the spatio-temporal feature learning module as the algorithm operates on time steps. In particular, the ST-GCN propagates spatial features by incorporating a summary of information on adjacent nodes in the interference graph, which captures the propagation of interference among users using the same spectrum. Meanwhile, the GRU takes the sequences of the states over time to learn the temporal relation that would be based on traffic and channel changes.

The spatial and temporal features are then combined into a single latent representation, which is the input to the multi-agent decision-making module. In this representation, agents choose a continuous action based on their actor network, and exploration noise is introduced to the training to motivate policy exploration. Joint action vector is applied to the wireless setting, leading to the re-estimation of network states and a scalar reward signal that jointly measures network energy efficiency and Quality of Service (QoS) compliance.

Training strategy summary

The complete training procedure of AEEOF proceeds in two sequential stages. In Stage 1, the ST-GCN–GRU module is pre-trained in a supervised fashion using 105,000 labelled network state samples. Training employs mini-batch gradient descent with the Adam optimiser, dropout and L2 regularisation for overfitting prevention, and validation-based early stopping to select the best-generalising model checkpoint. In Stage 2, the pre-trained ST-GCN–GRU encoder is frozen and its output representations are used as state inputs to the MADDPG controller. The MADDPG agents are trained under the centralised training with decentralised execution (CTDE) paradigm: during training, centralised critics access the joint observation and action vectors of all agents to support cooperative policy learning, while actors rely only on local observations. Policy updates are performed using sampled mini-batches drawn uniformly from a shared experience replay buffer, with soft target network updates applied after each gradient step to stabilise training. Exploration is governed by Ornstein–Uhlenbeck noise added to the actor outputs, with linear decay across training episodes to transition from exploration-dominant to exploitation-dominant behaviour. All hyperparameters governing both training stages are consolidated in Table 2 for reproducibility.

Evaluation objective and metric interpretation

In contrast to standard supervised classification problems, the learning goal of AEEOF is not to classify continuous network states (high-dimensional spatio-temporal) into discrete ones, but to convert high-dimensional spatio-temporal network states into useful continuous control actions. The spatio-temporal feature extractor (ST-GCN-GRU) is, however, evaluated against auxiliary decision-consistency measures during training and validation to determine the accuracy and consistency of the learned representations by the feature extractor.

In particular, the accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, which are reported, are used to measure the prediction capabilities of the model to identify and distinguish energy-critical network states, including congestion-prone states, interference-dominated states, and power-inefficient allocations, that need adaptive control responses. These measures are hence indicators of the reliability of decisions, as opposed to traditional classification accuracy.

The high decision reliability of the ST-GCN–GRU module provides the MADDPG reinforcement learning controller with a stable and discriminative foundation for policy learning. Figure 10 investigates the scalability of the suggested AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework (AEEOF) through the assessment of energy efficiency as the number of users that use the service grows. The analysis will be critical in determining whether the framework can be deployed in dense and ultra-dense network conditions that are envisaged in 5G-Advanced and above. With higher user density, energy efficiency diminishes in all the schemes considered because of the growing inter-user interference, the increased contention of common resources, and the increased signaling overhead. The degradation rate, however, is much different among schemes. The suggested AEEOF can sustain maximum efficiency of the energy consumption, regardless of the number of users, and exhibits a high scalability over the benchmark methods.

With low to medium user densities, AEEOF attains significant benefits because it effectively uses both space reuse and predictive scheduling. As the network approaches the highly congested regime, the structure remains superior to the competing approaches and maintains a large performance gap even when 100 users are active. This action suggests that the policies acquired generalize well to large-scale state spaces and do not suffer from a combinatorial explosion that often impedes solutions of reinforcement learning in dense environments.

The smooth nature of the performance degradation of AEEOF could be ascribed to the hybrid spatio-temporal architecture. The spatial encoder of the graph form involves a natural scaling with network size that the model of the interference through the localized neighborhood aggregation, whereas the GRU includes aggregate traffic dynamics that do not decrease even when the number of users increases. Conversely, the OMA and NOMA-FPA schemes have no flexibility and hence decay quickly, whereas the NOMA-DQN baseline has difficulties in keeping the best policies because it does not take into account the state in a structured way but rather has a limited state representation and lacks coherent spatial awareness.

On the whole, Fig. 10 proves that AEEOF can not only perform well in a nominal setting but also withstands and scales to a high user density, which would be suitable in future wireless deployments that will be characterized by massive connectivity as well as heterogeneous service demands.

Statistical significance and robustness validation

A detailed statistical significance and robustness analysis was done to ensure that the performance improvements attained by the proposed AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework (AEEOF) are not a product of stochastic variability or good simulation conditions. The consistency, reliability, and reproducibility of the observed improvements of the system are strictly validated in this subsection through further investigation of the reliability of the results across various independent simulation runs.

Statistical evaluation methodology

All statistical analyses have been conducted on the energy efficiency measure (Mbits/Joule), which is a composite measure of both energy consumption and presented throughput and hence the most reflective measure of system-level performance.

To determine the energy efficiency of each of the considered schemes (OMA, NOMA-FPA, NOMA-DQN, and AEEOF), the energy efficiency turned out to be recorded in 1,000 distinct Monte Carlo simulation runs, where the user locations, channel realizations, and traffic patterns were randomized. Such a large sample size provides statistical strength as well as avoids bias because of particular network instantiations.

Since the present comparison was multi-group with somewhat normal distribution of the values of energy efficiency, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was used to test the null hypothesis of making the schemes of the same scheme having the same mean performance. To further determine the pairwise differences of schemes, the Tukey Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) post hoc test was applied at a significance level of α = 0.01.

Robustness and stability discussion

The statistical distributions reveal that, in addition to achieving the scheme, AEEOF has both high mean energy efficiency (AEEOF) and the lowest variance of all schemes tested, indicating a high level of stability and resilience across varying network conditions. The reduced variance supports the effectiveness of spatiotemporal learning to address uncertainty created by varying traffic demands and channel characteristics. The narrow confidence intervals of AEEOF also show that the learned control policies from AEEOF produced equivalent generalizations across independent experiments, rather than exploiting one or more isolated favourable situations. This robustness is critical in the implementation of control policies for actual operational environments due to the inherently non-stationary and unpredictable nature of operational environments.

Discussion and key insights

The results from Sections VI-A through VI-E are integrated to extract the core insights resulting from the abstracted AEEOF (AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework). Instead of repeating numerical outcomes of each of the sections previously referenced, a discussion of how the abstracted AEEOF works is provided, along with validation of the design principles used and the way the traditional assumptions regarding interference-limited wireless networks have been affected.

From learning reliability to system-level gains

The experimental evaluation’s central outcome shows a strong link between the learning of a system and how well it performs at the network level. The convergence behavior and stability analyses of the model have indicated that AEEOF’s spatio-temporal model learns valid and repeatable representations of the network’s dynamics. The reliability associated with the learning does not result solely from machine learning reasons but shows up as calculable operational benefits. The consistent reductions in energy use and, at the same time, increases in throughput and coverage indicate that these policies learned were not overfit to any individual channel realizations or traffic patterns. They show that there is substantial structural knowledge concerning the relationships between interference, traffic evolution, and power allocation through space and time within the network, confirming that there must be robust learning done before one can be assured of the reliability of autonomous control of a network. Furthermore, the MADDPG policy convergence curve in Fig. 5 confirms that the reported system-level performance improvements reflect fully converged reinforcement learning policies, providing the evidential link between learning process stability and operational performance gains that completes the convergence analysis of the proposed framework.

Breaking the energy–performance trade-off

Typical network-stabilizing optimizations are based upon a continuous tradeoff constrained by the need for one of the following three factors to be improved: (a) energy efficiency, (b) throughput, and (c) coverage/fairness. The simulation results indicate that, within the evaluated setting, AEEOF achieves simultaneous improvements in energy consumption, throughput, and coverage, suggesting that the tension between these objectives can be partially mitigated through intelligent spatio-temporal coordination. These findings are encouraging, though whether such simultaneous gains generalise beyond the specific simulation conditions studied here (including single-cell topology, NOMA-based access, and the defined traffic model) requires further empirical validation.

The role of spatio-temporal intelligence in interference management

The importance of modeling the spatial dimension and the temporal dimension together in interference-heavy environments has been indicated through analyzing the results of both types of analysis. The observed energy savings across the entire cell area, as well as for users located far away, show that the spatial learning component of AEEOF is effective at managing interference relationships by representing the network as a graph and learning the structure of interactions within the network; as such, it does not require excessive amounts of power to be used as a result of poor channel characteristics when using other means to compensate for interference (through increased transmit power).

At the same time, by using modelled data to anticipate changes in traffic over time, the AEEOF framework can take preemptive action on the basis of anticipated congestion rather than waiting to respond after congestion has already occurred. By being able to anticipate changes in traffic, the AEEOF framework reduces marginal costs due to transience and eliminates oscillatory behaviour in control. Together, the integration of both forms of intelligence provides a framework for developing an optimal coordinated approach to optimizing the performance of the entire network compared to using either a spatial approach or a temporal approach independently of each other.

Fairness and cell-edge performance as first-class objectives

But also, practical deployment feasibility about its ability to leverage information from both the past (temporal aggregation) and the present (spatial encoding). Because of this combination of structured representations, the optimal actions can be effectively identified based on previously successful actions, rather than through exhaustive searching in multiple dimensions that could potentially fail.

Similarly, AEEOF’s ability to utilize these two types of information leads to a general framework that is amenable to many new applications of reinforcement learning, as well as new areas of research into reinforcement learning as a whole due to the flexible nature of the architecture.

Statistical robustness and trustworthiness

All rated gains are supported by Statistical significance testing. The rejection of the null hypothesis with a high level of confidence and the continued AEEOF’s performance in post-test based on quality superiority supports the theory of systematic rather than incidental improvements. The decreased variation across energy efficiency distributions indicates very stable operations across multiple operating conditions.

The statistical stability of the results is very important for real-world applications since network operators need reliable and consistent results when operating their networks. The results suggest that AEEOF may be an acceptable autonomous control methodology that can provide performance guarantees when operating at unknowns.

Implications for next-generation wireless networks

The collective findings suggest that spatio-temporal learning offers a promising direction for energy-efficient optimization in wireless systems, at least within the interference-limited, single-cell NOMA conditions studied here. Broader conclusions regarding the optimization landscape of next-generation wireless networks will require validation across more diverse deployment scenarios. Wireless systems will benefit from holistic controls based on AI, allowing them to operate adaptively, similarly aligning types of efficiency, performance, fairness, and reliability rather than asking for optimal solutions via static heuristics or objectives. These types of learning dynamics are already impacting future wireless solutions, experiencing denser infrastructure deployments, increasing variety in use cases, and larger environmental sustainability requirements. This study indicates that AI-enabled learning systems capable of understanding and utilizing structures will play a significant role in achieving these principles.

Ablation study

To assess the individual performance of different architectural components of the AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework (AEEOF), an extensive ablation study was performed. A total of three ablation variants were created by disabling one of the core modules, while the other modules and their corresponding simulation settings remained unchanged. The definitions for the three variants are as follows:

AEEOF w/o ST-GCN: The spatiotemporal graph convoluted module has been removed where only the temporal features are extracted using the GRU and aggregated with the MADDPG controller. This version helps to estimate the separate contribution of modeling the spatial interference.

AEEOF w/o ST-GCN: The spatiotemporal graph convoluted module has been removed where only the temporal features are extracted using the GRU and aggregated with the MADDPG controller. This version helps to estimate the separate contribution of modeling the spatial interference.

AEEOF w/o GRU: The AEEOF scheme without GRU retains only the spatial features of the ST-GCN along with the MADDPG controller. The AEEOF w/o GRU scheme is proposed to analyze and validate the effect of traffic modeling in the temporal domain separately.

AEEOF w/o GRU: The AEEOF scheme without GRU retains only the spatial features of the ST-GCN along with the MADDPG controller. The AEEOF w/o GRU scheme is proposed to analyze and validate the effect of traffic modeling in the temporal domain separately.

AEEOF w/o MADDPG: The multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient reinforcement learning controller is replaced with a greedy heuristic control policy, and the ST-GCN and GRU feature extractors are retained. This setup accounts for the independent impact of adaptive reinforcement learning-based control.

AEEOF w/o MADDPG: The multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient reinforcement learning controller is replaced with a greedy heuristic control policy, and the ST-GCN and GRU feature extractors are retained. This setup accounts for the independent impact of adaptive reinforcement learning-based control.

Simulations for the four set-ups, including AEEOF, were performed under similar criteria, as previously stated in Section VI-A, for over 1000 independent Monte Carlo simulations with randomized user locations, channel implementations, and traffic. Limitations and scope of the present study

While the simulation results reported in Sections VI-A through VI-G are encouraging and demonstrate consistent performance improvements under the studied conditions, it is important to clearly delineate the scope and boundaries of the present study to avoid overgeneralization of the findings.

Single-cell topology

All experiments are conducted within an isolated single-cell hexagonal deployment. Inter-cell interference, cooperative multi-cell coordination, handover management, and the effects of adjacent cell loading are therefore absent from the evaluation. Real-world deployments, particularly in ultra-dense 5G and beyond networks, involve complex inter-cell interactions that may affect the behavior and generalizability of the proposed framework. Extension of AEEOF to multi-cell and heterogeneous network topologies is an important direction for future work, as noted in the conclusion.

Simulation-based evaluation

The framework is evaluated exclusively within a MATLAB/Simulink-based digital twin environment. While the simulation incorporates 3GPP TR 38.901-compliant channel models, imperfect SIC, and realistic traffic and mobility patterns, it does not constitute a hardware testbed or real channel measurement campaign. Performance under real propagation conditions, hardware impairments, and implementation constraints may differ from the reported simulation results. Validation on software-defined radio platforms or standardized system-level simulators is identified as a priority for future experimental work.

Specific NOMA formulation

The study focuses on downlink power-domain NOMA with a fixed user clustering strategy. Uplink NOMA, cooperative and relay-assisted NOMA, rate-splitting multiple access (RSMA), and grant-free access schemes are outside the scope of the present evaluation. Furthermore, while imperfect SIC is modelled, the SIC ordering is assumed to be known and fixed; dynamic SIC ordering under mobility is not considered.

Centralized training assumption

The MADDPG framework relies on centralized training with global state information, which may not be directly available in fully distributed real-network deployments. Although decentralized execution is supported, the training phase requires access to joint observations and actions of all agents, which implies coordination overhead. Fully decentralized or federated training variants of the framework are left for future investigation.

Baseline comparisons

The comparative evaluation includes OMA, NOMA with fixed power allocation (NOMA-FPA), and a single-agent DQN-based NOMA baseline (NOMA-DQN). While these represent a principled set of conventional and AI-assisted reference points that enable systematic isolation of each architectural contribution, the evaluation does not extend to more recent approaches such as MAPPO-based cooperative resource management, transformer-enhanced power allocation frameworks, or model-based reinforcement learning methods with integrated channel prediction. These more recent approaches represent relevant and increasingly competitive reference points, and a broader comparative study incorporating them would further strengthen the evaluation. This is acknowledged as a limitation of the present work and is identified as a concrete direction for future investigation.

These limitations do not diminish the validity of the reported findings within their stated scope, but they do define the boundaries within which the conclusions of this study should be interpreted.

Conclusion and future work

This study introduces an AI-Enhanced Energy Optimization Framework (AEEOF), which has been developed to solve the main challenge of providing energy efficiency when working with wireless networks that share a lot of interference while maintaining performance, fairness, and scalability. The motivation for the development of AEEOF comes from looking at how the exponential growth in traffic, i.e., data, and the sustainability needs of today’s cellular networks are conflicting. AEEOF integrates deep spatio-temporal learning with multi-agent reinforcement learning to enable intelligent and adaptive network control.

The key innovation behind AEEOF is that it deals with all aspects of network dynamics holistically. It does this by modelling the spatial interference relationships contained within the Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network, along with the capturing of the temporal traffic evolution using Recurrent Learning to create a very detailed and structured representation of what is happening in the network state. Once this representation is created, it can be embedded into the Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning in order to provide for Decentralized but Co-ordinated decision-making to evolve the power allocation and scheduling policies as a result of two separate criteria: the current and the future availability of traffic data (instantaneous vs. time). This addresses the principal constraints of traditional optimisation approaches, which typically rely on static assumptions and optimise individual objectives in isolation rather than in a coordinated manner. AEEOF’s huge statistical performance gains across every metric are evident in simulated 5 g oriented NOMA environments. AEEOF reduces total energy usage by up to 15% while enhancing energy efficiency by approximately 40%, with simultaneous improvements in system throughput, fairness, effective coverage, and outage probability. The statistically significant analysis also indicates that the improvement in performance across every metric is a consequence of systematic processes, as well as the lack of stochastic variability. These results are obtained within a controlled single-cell, MATLAB-based simulation environment under the specific system model described in Section IV, and should be interpreted accordingly. Convergence of both the ST-GCN–GRU supervised pre-training stage and the MADDPG reinforcement learning controller is demonstrated through dedicated convergence analyses in Section VI-B, providing complete empirical evidence that the reported gains reflect stable and fully trained policies.

The findings also provide a much more in-depth understanding of the role that intelligent, spatio-temporally learning plays in wireless network optimization. Learning the structure of interference and the flow of traffic through the network, it allows for breaking through traditional, long-standing tradeoffs that can exist between energy efficiency, spectral efficiency, and user fairness. Therefore, AEEOF signifies a pivot away from the current state of being reactive and heuristic in nature toward a more proactive and data-driven manner in terms of communication, which aligns with efforts to create genuine AI-native communication systems. The limitations of the present study, including its single-cell simulation scope, the MATLAB/Simulink-based evaluation platform, and the specific NOMA formulation adopted, are discussed explicitly in Section VI-H. The directions for future work outlined below are motivated directly by these boundaries. Even though these results are very encouraging, there are many ways in which future research could be explored. Several concrete directions for future work are identified. First, the framework should be extended to multi-cell and heterogeneous network topologies, incorporating inter-cell interference coordination and handover management to broaden its applicability beyond the single-cell setting studied here. Second, a comprehensive comparative evaluation against more recent state-of-the-art baselines — including MAPPO-based cooperative resource management, transformer-enhanced resource allocation frameworks, and model-based reinforcement learning approaches with integrated channel prediction — would further contextualise the contribution of AEEOF and establish its performance relative to the current frontier of the field. Third, online learning and continuous adaptation mechanisms should be explored to enable real-time deployment without dependence on pre-collected training datasets. Fourth, validation on hardware testbeds or software-defined radio platforms would provide important evidence of real-world feasibility beyond the simulation environment studied here. In summary, the findings of this research show that Artificial Intelligence-based Spatial and Temporal Intelligence can be a significant factor in enabling the creation of sustainable, efficient wireless networks. This paper provides an integrated optimization framework for combining deep learning with reinforcement learning. Overall, the basis for future research into intelligent resource management has been established through the use of AEEOF and will help facilitate energy-efficient and autonomous control in 5G and future generations of Communication Systems.
