1. Background/Introduction of the Project
Facility for Rare Isotope Beams (FRIB) is the top priority project for US nuclear science as reported
in the 2015 NSAC long plan [1.1]. The CD4 review of FRIB was successfully held on Feb. 1 - 2, 2022.
FRIB has been completed earlier for 5 months, at the campus in Michigan State University. FRIB
is transferring from the construction phase to the user service operation. Now FRIB is one of the
four facilities NP operated, which is a 200 MeV/u, 400 kW superconducting radio frequency (SRF)-
based linac with a variety of rare isotope beams for the study of nuclear structure and nuclear
astrophysics. In the phased beam commissioning, FRIB superconducting cryomodules
demonstrated excellent SRF performance: high Q and high gradient and met the FRIB
requirement. In further user operation, FRIB facility must maintain a high operation capability, as
well as reliability, in providing accelerated beams. This proposal aims for transformative
accelerator R&D in SRF technology for restoring and increasing cavity performance in FRIB spare
cryomodules.

Cavity production has concluded for the FRIB project, but this proposal gears toward future FRIB
spare cryomodules to be replaced with possible degraded cavities, which may occur during the
long-term accelerator operation. Of the six FRIB cryomodule families, three have certified spares
that were fabricated as part of the project baseline. The spare beta 0.29 and 0.53 cryomodules
were not included in the baseline but are being planned as future scope of FRIB machine
maintenance. These spare cryomodules will utilize existing cavities that have been fabricated as
part of production contracts. These spare cryomodules or cavities must have an enough
performance margin against further degradation, during machine operation.

The goal of this proposal is to generate 0.53 half-wave cavities that can operate at acceleration
gradients of > 10 MV/m, keeping a high Qo > 1E + 10 for the restoring or construction FRIB spare
cryomodules. To achieve this goal, we will develop transformative processing techniques, to
mitigate field emission (FE), eliminate high field Q slope (HFQS), which limits the performance of
FRIB cavities.

This proposal includes an intent to increase knowledge about how cutting-edge accelerators may
enhance their performance. These efforts are essential to develop world-leading core
competencies and transformative technologies that significantly advance the state-of-the art
accelerator capabilities, whether for new facilities or improving the performance of existing
facilities. This development is important for capability of the cavities in the FRIB spare
cryomodules above, FRIB power upgrade cavities, as well as future SRF project like EIC. Especially,
the novel EP/BCP for the low/medium beta cavity will make a significant impact for future SRF
proton linacs (especially low/medium beta sections) or heavy ion linac itself. This proposal will
provide an understanding of the limit mechanism of SRF cavity performance. The knowledge

3.2 Solution 2: Local magnetic shield (LMGS)

flux trapping might be a main contribution to residual surface resistance. We will cover the cavity with an
additional magnetic shield in the vertical test. If the residual magnetic field can decrease from 2-5 mG
(current) to 0.1 mG, a very impressive improvement is expected as shown in Fig. 3.2, on both EP and EP +
LTB. This method is realistic for cryomodule production. HWRs in the spare cryomodule shall be installed
in a two-layer local magnetic shield (present FRIB cryomodules use one-layer local magnetic shield).

4. Impacts
This proposal focuses on implementing novel processing techniques to advance the current
cavity-processing infrastructure in efforts to enhance the FRIB machine reliability and overall
capabilities. These advancements will allow FRIB (repairing the degraded cavities in the
cryomodules, spare module production), as well as other future SRF-based projects, to improve
cavity operational gradient, implement innovative electropolishing/chemical polishing treatment,
gain further fundamental knowledge in SRF physics, and provide a potential direction for future
SRF cavity processing.
4.1 Impact on Long-term goals in the field
This proposal is to support the stable and reliable FRIB machine operation for Rare Isotope Beams,
which is the top priority project for US nuclear science. This proposal makes this nuclear science
program execute surely.

4.2 Impact on SRF technology
4.2.1 Improve cavity operation gradient
The first impact of this research is operational benefits to the FRIB accelerator by improving cavity
gradients by 30 – 50%, while maintaining (or increasing) high Q. This provides more operational
margin in the current FRIB accelerator, possible FRIB energy upgrade cryomodules, or future
heavy ion accelerator projects. This project can bring a large cost reduction for the future SRF
machine projects by compacting the machines.
4.2.2 Wet N doping
The second impact is on N doping method. Any annealing processing is unnecessary in the wet N
doping developed in this proposal. This method makes N doping amazingly simple and
exceptionally reliable because it does not need any ultra-high vacuum annealing process which
is extremely sensitive to contamination.
4.2.3 HFQS-free chemical polishing
The third impact is a transformative advancement to SRF accelerator processing technology. The
discovery of a new CP acid is an innovative technology, which releases the HFQS issue in the
BCP'ed cavities. If the new BCP proves the success on real cavities, because of the simplicity,
many kinds of SRF cavities will use this method, regardless of shape. This is beneficial to future
SRF-based accelerator designs.
4.3 Impact on present state of knowledge
4.3.1 Fundamental SRF physics
The fourth impact is on the fundamental SRF physics. If the new BCP processing is successful in
yielding HFQS-free cavities, by combining LTB, it will provide a better understanding of HFQS
mechanism and the relationship to BCP processing and nitrogen contamination from nitric acid.
A new idea of copper catalyst in chemical polishing opens quite a new electro-chemical surface

 12

treatment technology. If wet N doping is successful in producing high Q&G performance, it will
provide a better understanding of nitrogen doping mechanism.

4.3.2 Direction of future SRF cavity processing
The sixth impact is providing a new direction for future SRF cavity processing. The advancement
of the successful new EP/BCP surface treatments could provide a new recipe for cavity processing
that yields high Q&G performance. Generally saying, BCP processes are a preferable processing
method for SRF cavities because it can be used for complicated geometry cavities, while EP has
a technical limit due to cathode insertion. The new BCP will open doors to the SRF community to
optimize processing of enormous quantities of cavities for large accelerators. The innovation can
then extend to economic commercialization of the BCP process for industrial cavity production.
In addition, the new idea of copper catalyst in chemical polishing could cut open a quit new
electro-chemical treatment technology.

5. Other work related to this proposal
5.1 HFQS-free performance by EP+LTB
The high Q&G performance by EP + LTB was demonstrated by the PI first in the world [5.1]. This
has made a significant impact on the SRF community. LTB study took place very intensively in
many institutes. The PI showed the HFQS in EP’ed cavity is eliminated by the post EP LTB [5.2], as
seen Fig. 5.1 left. Since then, his recipe: 120 OC for 48 h LTB became the standard. The study of
LTB with BCP cavities also took place. Unfortunately, in this case, SRF community discovered that
buffered chemical polishing (BCP) does not result in HFQS-free cavity performance, even with
performing the post BCP LTB, as shown in Fig.5.1 right [5.3].

 Figure 5.1: Left, HFQS appeared by electropolishing, and elimination of HFQS low temperature
 bake post EP. Right, HFQS appeared in BCP’ed cavity. LTB does not work to eliminate the HFQS,
 both are ILC type 1.3 GHz single cell cavities.
5.2 HNO3-free BCP to eliminate HFQS with BCP’ed cavities
Why the LTB does not function on the HFQS-free on BCP’ed cavities was an open issue for more
than twenty years in the SRF community. MSU pursued this issue as a PhD program supervised
by the PI. It concluded that the cause is the nitrogen contamination from the nitric acid (HNO3) in
the BCP acid [5.4]. Then, they have developed a new CP acid which replaced the nitric acid with
hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) [5.5] as shown in Fig.5.2. At first, this mixture could not produce any
smooth surface (blue cross marks in Fig. 5.2 ), however they discovered that adding a little
amount of copper (e.g. 400 ppm) into the mixture can produce a smooth surface (Rz ~ 3 µm),
which is comparable to that of conventional BCP, as seen in Fig.5.2. Finally, she has discovered
that copper functions as catalyst by developing the chemical reaction equations in this CP
processing. In this proposal, we will validate the new CP acid to provide HFQS-free cavity
performance, combining the LTB post BCP supervising by the PI.

5.3 Discovery of potential wet N doping
The sixth impact is providing a new direction of future SRF cavity processing. The advancement
of the successful new EP/BCP surface treatments could provide a new recipe for cavity processing
that yields high Q&G performance. Generally saying, BCP processes is a preferable processing
method for SRF cavities because it can be used for complicated geometry cavities, while EP has a
technical limit due to cathode insertion. The new BCP will open doors to the SRF community to
optimize processing of massive quantities of cavities for large accelerators. The innovation can
then extend to economic commercialization of the BCP process for industrial cavity production.
In addition, the new idea of copper catalyst in chemical polishing could cut open a quit new
electro-chemical treatment technology.

Very recently, the PI has found out that the similar anti Q slope behavior observed in N-doped
cavities at FNAL [5.6] appears until 16 MV/m in the cavity performance treated by electropolishing,
using a new EP acid added 1500 pm HNO3. Fig. 5.3, left shows the data but somehow, HFQS is
mixing. This event was noticed during mining KEK’s old data (1.3 GHz single cell cavities) which
the PI took 20 years ago. This was experimental result in a PhD program supervised by the PI at
KEK in 2002 [5.6], to develop hydrogen Q-disease free SRF cavity preparation. At that time, they
focused on Hydrogen Q-disease, and did not carefully pay attention to this event. They thought it
might be in LHe temperature deviation during high gradient measurements. Recently, the PI
checked the LHe temperature, and found it was T = 1.42 ± 0.06 K, as seen in Fig.5.3 right. Such a
small temperature deviation cannot explain the anti Q slope behavior. He concluded that this anti
Q-slope behavior is by the nitrogen doping in the EP process, using the new EP acid (wet N doping).

6.2 Objective-2: Development of reducing ambient magnetic field for high Q
In the objectives 1, we succeeded in pushing high gradient performance. However, Qo
performance must be pushed more to operate the cavity at Eacc > 12 MV/m within the current
FRIB plant capability. We must reduce residual surface resistance. We investigated the magnetic
contamination in Objective 2, which is considered the major cause of our residual surface
resistance. As the causes of the magnetic contamination, DC ambient magnetic field due to a
residual earth magnetic field by the imperfect magnetic shielding, and one from magnetized
materials used nearby cavity. The second one is dynamic magnetic contamination due to
thermoelectric current produced by Seebeck effect at dissimilar material joints nearby cavity
when inhomogeneity in temperature happens during cooldown. We investigated DC magnetic
contamination first, to reduce the DC ambient field, putting a local magnetic shield around the
cavity.

Seebeck effect could occur in between
dissimilar material joints near the cavity. For
example, Nb cavity/Ti helium jacket, Ti
tube/SUS tube in the LHe inlet line, and Ti/SUS
in the LHe outlet line (see Fig. 6.2.10). When
the Seebeck circuit is closed, the voltage

6.2.10 Strategy-1 for further high Qo/high gradient performance
One will have a question how to improve further the high Qo/high gradient performance. On the
high gradient performance, with S53-159 used this experiment, field was quenched at Eacc = 15
MV/m. To push up the gradient further, we must find the defect limiting the field and remove
them. Medium beta cavity shape is complicated compared to elliptical shaped cavities. However,
tumbling could work to make visually clear potential defects, and then remove them by local
mechanical grinding.

 38

For high Qo performance, MFQS must be mitigated as well as thermal feedback. We will discuss
it in detail in section 6.3. Shortly saying, we need to control grain boundaries, make clean
boundaries. So far, we can control the residual surface resistance to ~ 1 nΩ level. If it can be
reduced to 0.5 nΩ, Qo will reach ~1E+11. We must be careful with the material used in the
cryomodule. For the thermal fee-d back we need to use higher RRR material RRR = 600. Thus, we
could push double performance for FRIB cavities.

6.3 Wet Nitrogen Doping
Nitrogen doping might be a way to get high Qo performance. For building a high gradient spare
module, existing FRIB production spare 0.53HWRs allow for the spare cryomodule. They are
already dressed in a helium jacket made of titanium on the cavity. Conventional nitrogen doping
recipe developed FNAL and Jlab include a high temperature vacuum treatment at 800 - 900OC.
This hot temperature treatment might deform the cavity due to the difference of thermal
expansion coefficient between Nb and Ti, making cavity tune out of frequency unable to tune
mechanically afterwards. Therefore, we have decided to develop wet nitrogen doping using
electropolishing described in Objective 3. Again, this method is to electropolish cavities using an
EP acid mixed weight 1500 ppm HNO3 (64%) to conventional EP acid.

6.3.1 Proofs of wet nitrogen doping
Before presenting the cavity performance at 2K with wet nitrogen doping, we show the proof of
nitrogen doping by this method. We have proved the following four experiments to confirm this.
We denote the wet doping EP as EP (HNO3), hereafter. S53-161 was used for wet nitrogen doping,
while S53-159 for conventional EP.

6.3.1.3 Less Qo Enhancement at 4.3 by In-Situ LTB with EP (HNO3)
We will describe the cavity performance with EP (HNO3) latter, but here as third proof by wet
nitrogen doping, we show its impact of LTB on 4.3 K cavity performance. We measured small Qo
enhancement at 4.3 K after in-situ LTB post EP (HNO3). This kind of measurement was repeated
for different material removals by EP (HNO3), in order 120 µm, 25 µm after hydrogen degassing,
and additional 75 µm. Fig.6.3.7 summarizes these results with the result by conventional EP. The
enhancement factor measured at Eacc = 2 MV/m is 1.18 by EP (HNO3, 120 µm), 1.15 by EP (HNO3,
25 µm), and 1.49 by EP (HNO3, 75 µm), while 1.47 for the conventional EP. EP (HNO3) provide an
even or even smaller enhancement factor. This means the Qo enhancement by EP (HNO3) is less
than that by the conventional EP. This can be explained why as following: the Qo enhancement is
caused by decreased BCS surface resistance due to shortening the mean-free path of the quasi-

electrons by scattering with melted contaminations, EP(HNO3) dopes nitrogen at the sites where
oxygen should occupy, as the result amount of oxygen contamination is reduced, the oxygen
diffuses into the RF penetration during the LTB, but nitrogen does not diffuse as more describe
later in detail, thus the amount of contamination in the RF penetration are is less in case of EP
(HNO3) , as the result amount of decrease in BCS resistance is less, the Qo enhancement factor
becomes smaller than that of conventional EP. This is proof of the wet nitrogen doping with EP
(HNO3).

Picture of N-doping by EP (HNO3)
Here, we summarize our picture of wet doping by EP (HNO3). That is illustrated on Fig.6.3.8.
Nitrogen and oxygen will be doped during EP(HNO3) on SRF top surface. Nitrogen will occupy
many doping sites where oxygen should be doped, therefore the amount of oxygen doped will be
reduced. By LTB, only oxygen diffuses to RF penetration depth of ~ 35 nm: but nitrogen does not
diffuse. For example, the diffusing distance of nitrogen for the LTB at 140 OC for 40 h is 1.6 Å (0.16
nm) [6.3.2], which is shallow comparing the RF penetration depth. The amount of oxygen by EP
(HNO3) is less than that of conventional EP, thus the smaller enhancement factor happened at
4.3K.

6.3.4.3 Second motivation of NC25 application
Second motivation is in the non-magnetization with NC25 material to eliminate the flux from
magnetized components nearby cavity to reduce residual surface resistance.
In this DOE grant R&D project, we have pursued high Q and high gradient cavity performance
using FRIB 0.53HWRs. With high Q, we discovered that flux trapping due to ambient magnetic
field produces 3.5 nΩ of 4.5 nΩ of the residual surface resistance, of which 2.5 nΩ of 3.5 nΩ from
dynamic ambient field due to Seebeck effect at dissimilar material joints nearby cavity, and 1.0
nΩ from DC ambient field from inefficient earth magnetic field shielding etc. We investigated to
reduce the remaining 1.0 nΩ - 1.5 nΩ. One possibility would be the fluxes from the magnetized
components nearby cavities, e.g. SUS bolts and nuts, which easily magnetize during work. As
mentioned above, NC25 material perfectly non-magnetizes even after work. We replaced all
SUS316L bolts/nuts, which are used on the cavity port flanges.

6.4 Objective-4: Development of HNO3-free BCP
As discovered in Objective-3, wet doped nitrogen from HNO3 in EP (HNO3) or BCP acid produces
problems on SRF cavity performance: FE and HFQS. Especially BCP is simple treatment method,
just put acid in cavity, no needs electrode unlikely EP. BCP is the only the way for cavities with
complicated geometrical shape. If we succeeded in HNO3-free BCP acid by changing HNO3 as
oxidizer to e.g., H2O2 (hydro peroxide), the surface contamination would be limited to oxygen and
is like EP. As a result, FE and HFQS issues could be mitigated. HNO3-free BCP might replace EP. This
effort was done in MSU PhD program in 2015 - 2019. The PhD student, Dr. Didi Luo and K. Saito
discovered that the BCP acid just consists of HF and H2HO2 produces the rougher surface
increasing the more material removals, but a tiny amount of copper is added to the acid as
catalyst, the surface finishing like BCP is achieved [6.4.1]. The goal of Objective 4 was to apply this
HNO3-free BCP acid to FRIB 0.53 HWRs and proof the EP like cavity performance. We succeeded
in reproducibility of the HNO3-free BCP polishing ability but study using cavities was stooped due
to budget limit. Here we report the reproducibility and results of surface analysis below.

6.4.3 Summary of Objective-4
1) In objective 4, HNO3-free BCP acid was reconfirmed to produce smooth surface finishing similar
 level of the conventional BCP, when optimized the amount of copper.
2) Material removal speed of HNO3-free BCP is~ 1.8 µm/min, faster than conventional BCP, 1.2
 µm/min.
3) The deterioration of polishing ability in relation to the amount of polishing is slower than that
 of BCP.
4) HNO3-free BCP has a stronger preferential etching than conventional BCP. The resultant
 protrusion structures concern producing FE, which needs to improve in further R&D.
5) Copper contamination on the surface was not detected at least after HPR is taken at middle
 pressure.
6) Evaluation of HNO3-free BCP acid for cavity performance has remained as future R&D.

7. Discoveries/inventions in this project
This project was productive. We have produced discoveries or inventions as below in this project.
1) Discovery of EP superiority over BCP on high Q and high gradient cavity performance at
 medium beta cavities.
2) Discovery that 80% of residual surface resistance of FRIB 0.53HWRs comes from the flux
 trapping due to Seebeck effect.
3) Wet nitrogen doping
  This extremely high Q performance in low fields has the potential to apply SRF quantum
 computer technology.
4) The role of HNO3 in EP (HNO3) or BCP acid
 HNO3 dopes nitrogen during these processing and the doped nitrogen makes seed of FE or
 causes the HFQS not mitigated by LTB.
5) HNO3-free BCP
  This technology has the potential to be the ultimate processing method for SRF Nb cavities.
6) Gave answer for the question open over 25 years in SRF community: why LTB cannot mitigate
 HPQS with BCP’ed cavities.

8. Contributions to basic science in this project
SRF can explore the very thin surface ~ 40 nm (RF penetration depth) through the SRF cavity
performance. Surface analysis technologies like SEM, XPS, Auger. allow study such a region, but
it is point-like analysis. SRF give an average information in such a region on the inner surface of
the cavities. In Objective 3, we looked at nitrogen or oxygen behaviors in such areas. Such surface
contamination is difficult to study because phenomena that cannot be described by steady-state
chemical reaction equations occur. This project showed SRF cavities are a useful tool to investigate
such surface science.
