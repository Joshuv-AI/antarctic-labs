RAMPAGE: RAndomized Mid-Point for debiAsed Gradient Extrapolation
Abolfazl Hashemi

Abstract
A celebrated method for Variational Inequalities (VIs) is Extragradient (EG), which can be viewed as a standard discrete-time integration scheme. With this view in mind, in this paper we show that EG may suffer from discretization bias when applied to non-linear vector fields, conservative or otherwise. To resolve this discretization shortcoming, we introduce RAndomized Mid-Point for debiAsed Gradient Extrapolation (RAMPAGE) and its variance-reduced counterpart, RAMPAGE+ which leverages antithetic sampling. In contrast with EG, both methods are unbiased. Furthermore, leveraging negative correlation, RAMPAGE+ acts as an unbiased, geometric path-integrator that completely removes internal first-order terms from the variance, provably improving upon RAMPAGE. We further demonstrate that both methods enjoy provable 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}(1/k) convergence guarantees for a range of problems including root finding under co-coercive, co-hypomonotone, and generalized Lipschitzness regimes. Furthermore, we introduce symmetrically scaled variants to extend our results to constrained VIs. Finally, we provide convergence guarantees of both methods for stochastic and deterministic smooth convex-concave games. Somewhat interestingly, despite being a randomized method, RAMPAGE+ attains purely deterministic bounds for a number of the studied settings.



1
Introduction

In recent years, modern learning and decision-making paradigms, e.g. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), robust reinforcement learning, and multi-agent competitive systems, have triggered a renewed interest in study of the broader domain of min-max optimization and Variational Inequalities (VIs).
Formally, consider the problem of finding an equilibrium state
\theta^{*}\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that the variational condition
\langle F(\theta^{*}),\theta-\theta^{*}\rangle\geq 0,
for all
\theta\in\mathcal{X},
is satisfied,
where
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
represents the driving operator of the system. In the unconstrained regime where
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{p}
, this condition collapses to the root-finding problem
F(\theta^{*})=0.

This formalism encompasses many applications:
In pure minimization tasks, the operator represents a conservative gradient field,
F(\theta)=\nabla f(\theta)
, where the underlying geometry is governed by potential energy dissipation. However, in min-max games and generalized VIs, the operator
F
frequently exhibits non-conservative, skew-symmetric (rotational) components (e.g. the classical bilinear min-max games).

1.1
Motivation

The discrete update of most first-order algorithm is fundamentally an attempt to numerically integrate the continuous flow defined by the ordinary differential equation

\dot{\theta}(\tau)=-F(\theta(\tau))\qquad\equiv\qquad\theta_{t+1}=\theta_{t}-\int_{t}^{t+1}F(\theta(s))ds.

(1)

With this view, a fundamental observation of modern optimization is that naive discrete-time integration schemes, such as the standard Forward Euler method (corresponding to Gradient Descent for minimization problems and Gradient Descent-Ascent for min-max problems), exhibit divergence when subjected to vector fields with rotational components.

To stabilize the discrete dynamics, the field overwhelmingly relies on Extragradient (EG), a celebrated method which constructs an extrapolated intermediate state to approximate an implicit backward step. For a step size
\eta>0
, the update is defined by

\theta_{t+1}=\theta_{t}-\eta F\big(\theta_{t}-\eta F(\theta_{t})\big).

(EG)

While (
EG
) provides stability in smooth, well-conditioned bilinear games and other settings, an analysis of its local truncation error reveals a bias that may hinder its efficacy in highly non-linear, high-dimensional settings. To understand the limitations of (
EG
), we evaluate its truncation error next.

As evident by the integral form of (
1
), an ideal discrete stabilization vector should capture the
mean field
acting upon the state as it traverses the local exploration radius. We formalize this by defining the exact line integral over a parameterized exploration segment
\gamma(s)=\theta_{t}-c\eta sF(\theta_{t})
for
s\in[0,1]
where
c\geq 1
dictates the integration scale. One sets
c=1
for conservative vector fields while
c>1
(typically
c=2
which we further adopt here) is leveraged for general non-conservative fields (See Appendix
B
for more details). We thus define the continuous integral of the operator over this segment as

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}=\int_{0}^{1}F\big(\theta_{t}-2\eta sF(\theta_{t})\big)ds,

(2)

which acts as a low-pass filter over the vector field, given the smoothing properties of integration.

While
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
provides superior continuous-time stabilization, (
EG
) evaluates the operator at the single extrapolated midpoint
s=1/2
, generating the update field
F_{EG}=F\big(\theta_{t}-\eta F(\theta_{t})\big)
. Expanding
F_{EG}
via Taylor series and subtracting it from the exact integral
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
leads to the bias as
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}-F_{EG}=\frac{1}{6}\eta^{2}\nabla^{2}F(\theta_{t})\big[F(\theta_{t}),F(\theta_{t})\big]
(See Appendix
A
for more details).
This exhibits (
EG
) is a biased estimator of
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
and the bias scales with the Hessian tensor
\nabla^{2}F(\theta_{t})
.

1.2
Proposed Idea and Contributions

To circumvent the incurred bias of (
EG
), we motivate the use of
stochastic approximation
and introduce RAndomized Mid-Point for debiAsed Gradient Extrapolation (RAMPAGE). By drawing a single random variable
u_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}([0,1])
, the method evaluates the operator randomly along the exploration radius via
F_{\text{RAMPAGE}}=F\big(\theta_{t}-2\eta u_{t}F(\theta_{t})\big).

Because the expectation over the uniform distribution exactly mirrors the integral
\int_{0}^{1}ds
, on expectation
F_{\text{RAMPAGE}}
acts as a geometric smoother

\mathbb{E}_{u_{t}}\big[F_{\text{RAMPAGE}}\big]=\int_{0}^{1}1\cdot F\big(\theta_{t}-2\eta sF(\theta_{t})\big)ds=\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}.

(3)

(
RAMPAGE
) provides a unbiased estimator of the true continuous-time low-pass filter
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
. However, evaluating a single random sample injects some degree of variance (see Appendix
A
).
To resolve this issue, we propose a variance-reduced variant, (
RAMPAGE+
). We maintain the unbiased integration while controlling the variance by drawing an antithetic pair of evaluations. Utilizing
u_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}([0,1])
and its deterministic, antithetic complement
\tilde{u}_{t}=1-u_{t}
, the (
RAMPAGE+
) update field is defined by

\bar{F}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}F\big(\theta_{t}-2\eta u_{t}F(\theta_{t})\big)+\frac{1}{2}F\big(\theta_{t}-2\eta\tilde{u}_{t}F(\theta_{t})\big).

(4)

Given that
\tilde{u}_{t}=1-u_{t}
and
u_{t}
are identically distributed, i.e.
\tilde{u}_{t},u_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}([0,1])
, (
RAMPAGE+
) remains an unbiased estimator of
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
. Additionally, given that
\tilde{u}_{t}=1-u_{t}
and
u_{t}
are negatively correlated,
\bar{F}_{t}
enjoys a lower variance compared to
F_{\text{RAMPAGE}}
when estimating
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
(see Appendix
A
for more details).

To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed unbiased methods and the detrimental bias of (
EG
), in Section
7
, we consider a conservative case (corresponding to nonconvex optimization) and two non-conservative cases (corresponding to min-max games motivated by infinite-width GANs) and show the results in Figure
1
. For each case, we run two instance of (
EG
) with slightly different stepsizes chosen at the edge of instability, one lead to convergence and the other leading to divergence due to the discretization bias we discussed. We use the latter, instable stepsize for the proposed methods as well. As the figure demonstrates, (
EG
) diverges due to its bias while (
RAMPAGE+
) enjoys fast convergence, corroborating its smooth and unbiased nature. Furthermore, (
RAMPAGE+
) by using antithetic sampling enjoys a significantly lower variance as we explained. We also note that in all cases (
RAMPAGE+
) still converges when using larger stepsize than what reported in the plots. Therefore, (
RAMPAGE+
) could lead to further stabilization for problems with highly irregular and nonlinear vector fields.

Theoretical Contributions:

Besides the proposed idea of leveraging stochastic integration with antithetic sampling, we provide convergence guarantees for (
RAMPAGE
) and (
RAMPAGE+
) in a number of settings. In particular, our key theoretical contributions are as follows:

•

Root-Finding Problems:
We establish
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
best-iterate convergence rates for (
RAMPAGE
) and (
RAMPAGE+
) under co-coercivity, co-hypomonotonicity, and generalized Lipschitzness.

•

Variational Inequality:
We propose the symmetrically scaled variants of both methods and show their convergence under Lipschitzness and monotonicity for variational inequality problems.

•

In Appendix
6
, we extend the analysis to unconstrained smooth convex-concave minimax games, proving that the ergodic sequence of the randomized updates converges to a Nash equilibrium at a rate of

Preliminaries and Problem Formulation

Root-Finding Problems:
The most fundamental unconstrained equilibrium framework is the root-finding problem, which seeks an optimal state vector
\theta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
residing in the zero set of the operator. This is formally defined by the algebraic condition

F(\theta^{*})=0.

(RF)

We assume that the equilibrium set is non-empty, denoting an arbitrary optimal state as
\theta^{*}\in\mathrm{zer}(F)
, satisfying
F(\theta^{*})=0
.

Variational Inequality Problems:
When the state space is defined by hard boundary conditions to be a non-empty, closed, and convex feasible set
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the root-finding problem naturally generalizes to the classic Stampacchia VI. A point
\theta^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
is a solution to the Variational Inequality if it satisfies

\langle F(\theta^{*}),\theta-\theta^{*}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall\theta\in\mathcal{X}.

(VI)

This framework encompasses a vast array of optimization paradigms, including standard convex minimization and smooth convex-concave minimax games.

We state the following assumption which formally defines certain functional properties leveraged by the prior work for the analysis of EG and its variants
[
24
,
11
,
22
,
94
,
87
]
.

Assumption 1
.

Consider two arbitrary
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
. An operator
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is

1.

L
-Lipschitz continuous if
\exists\;L>0
such that
\|F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\prime})\|\leq L\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|
.

2.

\alpha
-symmetric
(L_{0},L_{1})
-Lipschitz if for some
L_{0},L_{1}\geq 0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

\|F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\prime})\|\leq\left(L_{0}+L_{1}\max_{\gamma\in[0,1]}\left\|F(\gamma\theta+(1-\gamma)\theta^{\prime})\right\|^{\alpha}\right)\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|.

(5)

3.

monotone if
\langle F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\prime}),\theta-\theta^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0.

4.

\mu
-co-coercive if
\exists\;\mu>0
such that
\langle F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\prime}),\theta-\theta^{\prime}\rangle\geq\mu\|F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\prime})\|^{2}.

5.

\rho
-co-hypomonotone if
\exists\;\rho\geq 0
such that
\langle F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\prime}),\theta-\theta^{\prime}\rangle\geq-\rho\|F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\prime})\|^{2}.

While co-coercivity is satisfied by the gradients of smooth, convex objective functions and some non-conservative fields, it is violated by the rotational dynamics intrinsic to min-max games. On the other hand,
every
L
-Lipschitz monotone operator is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone with
\rho=0
. Also, a

We state the following proposition for

Proposition 1
.

Suppose

\alpha
-symmetric

-Lipschitz operator. Then, for

\textstyle\|F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\prime})\|\leq\left(K_{0}+K_{1}\|F(\theta)\|^{\alpha}+K_{2}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right)\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|,

(6)

where
K_{0}=L_{0}(2^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha}}+1)
,

Lemma 1

\operatorname{Gap}_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta_{l})=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{B}}\langle F(\theta),\theta-v\rangle\leq\|r_{l}\|\left(\|F(\theta_{l})\|+\frac{1}{\eta}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{B}}\|\theta_{l}-v\|\right).

(7)

RAMPAGE and Its Analyses

In this section, we analyze (
RAMPAGE
) in numerous settings. At each iteration
t\geq 0
, given a base iterate
\theta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and a step size
\eta>0
, we draw a single uniform random variable
u_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}([0,1])
. The iterative scheme is formally defined by

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=\theta_{t}-2\eta u_{t}F(\theta_{t}),\qquad\theta_{t+1}=\theta_{t}-\eta F(y_{t}).

(RAMPAGE)

Note that with
u_{t}=1/2
, the (
RAMPAGE
) reduces to (
EG
).

4.1
Root-Finding Problems

We first consider (
RF
) with an operator
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
that is both
L
-Lipschitz continuous and
\mu
-co-coercive.

Theorem 1
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
be an
L
-Lipschitz continuous and
\mu
-co-coercive operator with
\mu>0
. Assume
\mathrm{zer}(F)\neq\emptyset
. Let
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
denote the sequence generated by the (
RAMPAGE
). If the constant step size satisfies
0<\eta\leq\min\left(2\mu,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}L}\right)
, then for any
\theta^{*}\in\mathrm{zer}(F)
,

\min_{0\leq l\leq k}\mathbb{E}\left[\|F(\theta_{l})\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\|F(\theta_{l})\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\|\theta_{0}-\theta^{*}\|^{2}}{C_{RA}(k+1)},

(8)

where
C_{RA}=\eta^{2}\left(1-2L^{2}\eta^{2}\right)>0
.

The proof is available in Appendix
C.1
. The theorem thus demonstrates the standard
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
convergence for this setting.

Next, we turn our attention to (

Theorem 2
.

Let

-Lipschitz continuous and

The proof is available in Appendix

. When

\eta<\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4L}
which we established for Lipschitz operators directly in Theorem
2
, the upperbound is slightly more restrictive.

Remark 1
.

Note that for
\alpha\in(0,0.5]
, the function
M(x)=\left(\frac{\nu x}{K_{0}+C_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}}\right)^{2}
with
x=\|F(\theta_{l})\|\geq 0
is convex such that by Jensen’s inequality,
M(\mathbb{E}[\|F(\theta_{l})\|])\leq\mathbb{E}[M(\|F(\theta_{l})\|)]
. As
M
is monotone and invertible, using the result of Theorem
3
we obtain the concrete rate

\min_{0\leq l\leq k}\mathbb{E}[\|F(\theta_{l})\|]\leq M^{-1}\left(\frac{4\|\theta_{0}-\theta^{*}\|^{2}}{k+1}\right).

(13)

The inverse
M^{-1}
, despite being unique does not generally have a closed-form expression. For
\alpha=0.5
, however, the closed-form expression amounts to
M^{-1}(y)=\left(\frac{C_{1/2}\sqrt{y}+\sqrt{C_{1/2}^{2}y+4\nu K_{0}\sqrt{y}}}{2\nu}\right)^{2}
, and we obtain

\min_{0\leq l\leq k}\mathbb{E}[\|F(\theta_{l})\|]\leq\frac{8C_{1/2}^{2}\nu^{-1}\|\theta_{0}-\theta^{*}\|^{2}}{k+1}+4K_{0}\sqrt{\frac{4\|\theta_{0}-\theta^{*}\|^{2}}{k+1}}.

(14)

4.2
Monotone Variational Inequalities and SS-RAMPAGE

Transitioning from unconstrained equilibrium problems to constrained (
VI
) introduces an obstacle in the proof. The asymmetrical scaling in (
RAMPAGE
) leads to ineffective bounds when the trajectory interacts with the boundary
\partial\mathcal{X}
. Thus, to guarantee expected improvement, the randomized scaling must be symmetrically coupled to both the intermediate exploration projection and the final update projection. This symmetric application ensures that the polarization identities used in the proof generated by the intermediate state cancel the expansive distance metrics induced by the outer projection mapping.

Thus, we propose the following Symmetrically Scaled (SS) variant. At each iteration
t\geq 0
, given a base iterate
\theta_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
and a step size
\eta>0
, we draw a single uniform random variable
u_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}([0,1])
and execute

\displaystyle y_{t}=\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\theta_{t}-2\eta u_{t}F(\theta_{t})\big),\quad\theta_{t+1}=\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\theta_{t}-2\eta u_{t}F(y_{t})\big).

(15)

Because
\mathcal{X}
is a closed and convex set, the projection operator ensures the trajectory maintains strict feasibility, guaranteeing
y_{t},\theta_{t+1}\in\mathcal{X}
. Note that when
u_{t}=1/2
we recover the constrained version of (
EG
), but the method differs from (
RAMPAGE
) when
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{p}
. We leave rectifying this as a future work.

Theorem 4
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
be an
L
-Lipschitz continuous and monotone operator. Assume the Variational Inequality admits a solution
\theta^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
. Let
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
denote the sequence generated by the corrected SS-RAMPAGE method. If the step size satisfies
0<\eta<\frac{1}{2L}
, then

\min_{0\leq l\leq k}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\theta_{l}-y_{l}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\theta_{l}-y_{l}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\|\theta_{0}-\theta^{*}\|^{2}}{C_{SS}(k+1)},

(16)

where
C_{SS}=1-4\eta^{2}L^{2}>0
.

The proof is available in Appendix
C.5
. We further state the following result on the expected restricted gap.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let the conditions of Theorem
4
hold. Assume that the sequence
\{\theta_{l}\}_{l=0}^{k}
and the operator evaluations
\{F(\theta_{l})\}_{l=0}^{k}
are bounded almost surely such that
\sup_{l\geq 0,v\in\mathcal{B}}\|\theta_{l}-v\|\leq D_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\sup_{l\geq 0}\|F(\theta_{l})\|\leq G_{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
l^{*}=\mathrm{argmin}_{0\leq l\leq k}\mathbb{E}[\|\theta_{l}-y_{l}\|^{2}]
denote the index of the expected best iterate generated by SS-RAMPAGE up to iteration
k
. Then, evaluating Lemma
1
with the exact projection step size
2\eta u_{l^{*}}
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{Gap}_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta_{l^{*}})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(G_{\mathcal{B}}+\frac{D_{\mathcal{B}}}{2\eta u_{l^{*}}}\right)\frac{\|\theta_{0}-\theta^{*}\|}{\sqrt{\big(1-4\eta^{2}L^{2}\big)(k+1)}}\right].

(17)

The proof is available in Appendix
C.5
. The result thus demonstrates the standard
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
convergence for this setting.

5
RAMPAGE+ and Its Analyses

We now study the case of (
RAMPAGE+
). Our primary objective is to certify and explicitly quantify the variance reduction and subsequent acceleration yielded by the antithetic coupling over the standard single-sample (
RAMPAGE
) architecture (See Remark
2
).

The (
RAMPAGE+
) discretization generates an antithetic pair of intermediate exploration states, utilizing a single random scalar
u_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}([0,1])
and its deterministic complement
\tilde{u}_{t}=1-u_{t}
. At each iteration
t\geq 0
, the update sequence is formulated as

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=\theta_{t}-2\eta u_{t}F(\theta_{t}),\qquad\tilde{y}_{t}=\theta_{t}-2\eta\tilde{u}_{t}F(\theta_{t}),

(RAMPAGE+)

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(F(y_{t})+F(\tilde{y}_{t})\big),\qquad\theta_{t+1}=\theta_{t}-\eta\bar{F}_{t}.

Note that with
u_{t}=\tilde{u}_{t}=1/2
, the (
RAMPAGE+
) reduces to (
EG
).

5.1
Root-Finding Problems

We first consider (
RF
) with an operator
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
that is both
L
-Lipschitz continuous and
\mu
-co-coercive.

Theorem 5
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
be an
L
-Lipschitz continuous and

-co-coercive operator with
\mu>0

(18)

where
C_{RA+}=\eta^{2}\left(1-3\eta^{2}L^{2}\right)>0

The proof is available in Appendix
D.1
. The result thus demonstrates the standard
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
convergence for this setting. Furthermore, as we discuss further in Remark
2
, (
RAMPAGE+

RAMPAGE
), as it benefits from variance reduction properties of antithetic sampling. As we can see, compared to (
RAMPAGE
), by using antithetic sampling, (
RAMPAGE+
) enjoys a purely deterministic convergence despite being a randomized algorithm. However, we could improve the requirement on
\eta
at the cost of stating a result that holds on expectation.

Next, we turn our attention to (
RF

(20)

D.2

When

Remark 2
.

The benefit of (
RAMPAGE+
) over the single-sample (
RAMPAGE
) method is seen by examining both the probabilistic nature and the absolute magnitude of their respective descent multipliers,
C_{RA+}^{\rho,\eta}
and
C_{RA}^{\rho,\eta}
. A key distinction lies in the realization dependence of the trajectory. The bounds derived for (
RAMPAGE
) are valid in expectation. Conversely, by perfectly correlating the antithetic exploration samples, (
RAMPAGE+
) removes the variance completely.

Beyond the transition from expected to deterministic convergence, (
RAMPAGE+
) enjoys a faster descent. This is immediately evident in the baseline constants: (
RAMPAGE+
) establishes a primary baseline descent of
3/4
, representing a
50\%
improvement over the
1/2
baseline extracted by the single-sample (
RAMPAGE
) formulation. To concretely quantify this acceleration, we evaluate both multipliers in the pure monotone limit where
\rho=0

RAMPAGE+
) multiplier evaluates to

. Both dictate the maximum step size threshold

to ensure absolute positivity. However, for any fixed step size selected within this valid domain,

Finally, we extend the analysis to (
RF
) with monotone and
\alpha

5.2

As we discussed, transitioning from unconstrained equilibrium problems to (

D.4

6
Application to Min-Max Games

We now apply the (
RAMPAGE
) framework to min-max optimization, specifically the unconstrained smooth convex-concave game. We will consider both deterministic and stochastic game settings.

6.1
Deterministic Games

Let the joint state variable be
\theta=(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We consider the classical minimax formulation

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(x,z),

(26)

where
f(x,z)
is continuously differentiable, convex in
x
, and concave in
z
. This induces the vector field operator
F:\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+m}

F(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{x}f(x,z)\\
-\nabla_{z}f(x,z)\end{bmatrix}.

(27)

By the convexity-concavity of
f
,
F
is monotone. For any intermediate point
y_{t}=(x_{t}^{m},z_{t}^{m})
and reference state
\theta=(x,z)
, we have

f(x_{t}^{m},z)-f(x,z_{t}^{m})\leq\langle\nabla_{x}f(x_{t}^{m},z_{t}^{m}),x_{t}^{m}-x\rangle-\langle\nabla_{z}f(x_{t}^{m},z_{t}^{m}),z_{t}^{m}-z\rangle=\langle F(y_{t}),y_{t}-\theta\rangle.

(28)

The theorem thus demonstrates the standard
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
convergence for this setting.

Theorem 9
.

Let
F
be the
L
-Lipschitz continuous monotone operator associated with the smooth convex-concave function
f
. Let
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t=0}^{k}
and
\{y_{t}\}_{t=0}^{k}
be the sequences generated by (
RAMPAGE
). Define the ergodic average over
k
iterations as
\bar{y}_{k}=(\bar{x}_{k},\bar{z}_{k})=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{t=0}^{k}y_{t}
. If the step size satisfies
0<\eta\leq\frac{1}{2L}
, then for any reference point
\theta=(x,z)
,

\mathbb{E}\left[f(\bar{x}_{k},z)-f(x,\bar{z}_{k})\right]\leq\frac{\|\theta_{0}-\theta\|^{2}}{2\eta(k+1)}.

(29)

The proof is available in Appendix
E.1
. Note that the result shows ergodic convergence to an approximate Nash equilibrium.

Next, we analyze the proposed (
RAMPAGE+
) method for the deterministic games.

Theorem 10
.

Let
F
be the
L
-Lipschitz continuous monotone operator associated with the smooth convex-concave function
f
. Let
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t=0}^{k}
be the primary sequence, and

(31)

The proof is available in Appendix
E.2
. As we can see, compared to (
RAMPAGE
), by using antithetic sampling, (
RAMPAGE+
) enjoys a purely deterministic convergence despite being a randomized algorithm. Furthermore, we crucially define the solution as the average of both
y_{t}
and

6.2

F(\theta)
is computationally inaccessible. Instead, the algorithm queries an estimator
\hat{F}(\theta,\xi)

\xi

\mathcal{D}

.

For any fixed state
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

\hat{F}(\theta,\xi)

\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[\hat{F}(\theta,\xi)\big]=F(\theta),\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\|\hat{F}(\theta,\xi)-F(\theta)\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2},\quad\text{for some }\sigma\geq 0.

(32)

We now extend our analysis to stochastic games under Assumption
2
. We first state a result on the convergence properties of (

E.3

7
Numerical Verification

To empirically validate the advantages of the proposed randomized integration schemes, we evaluate (
EG
), (
RAMPAGE
), and (
RAMPAGE+
) across three ill-conditioned topologies. For each setting, we execute
100
independent Monte Carlo trials.

We employ the same initialization and a constant step size
\eta
across all three methods within each experiment. This step size is explicitly selected to lie precisely at the instability threshold where (
EG
) diverges due to its deterministic
\mathcal{O}(\eta^{2})
discretization bias. This highlights that the unbiased integration of (
RAMPAGE
) and the variance-reduced architecture of (
RAMPAGE+
) leads to convergence in aggressive step-size regimes where standard extrapolation of (
EG
) fails. Beside running (
EG
) with this unstable stepsize, we run another instance with a slightly lower stepsize which is convergent. We also note that in all cases (
RAMPAGE+
) still converges when using larger stepsize than what reported in the plots.

Conservative Polynomial Field:

We first consider an unconstrained conservative vector field derived from a 4th-order polynomial objective, evaluated component-wise as

F_{i}(\theta)=\theta_{i}+5\theta_{i}^{3}-6\theta_{i}^{2},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{10}.

(36)

As the state vector moves away from the origin, the operator’s curvature grows rapidly. The deterministic truncation error of (
EG
) is heavily penalized by these massive higher-order derivatives, systematically inducing trajectory overshoot and divergence. Conversely, (
RAMPAGE
) and (
RAMPAGE+
) bypass this bottleneck via unbiased integration, with (
RAMPAGE+
) enjoying a significantly lower variance.

High-Frequency Rotational Min-Max Game:

Next, we evaluate a non-conservative, highly rotational min-max game with high-frequency terms. The driving operator is formulated as

F(\theta)=M\theta+0.005\omega\odot\sin(\omega\odot\theta),\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{20}.

(37)

The matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{20\times 20}
is block-diagonal, composed of
2\times 2
sub-blocks taking the form

B_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}0.1&\beta_{i}\\
-\beta_{i}&0.1\end{bmatrix}.

(38)

The added trigonometric perturbation guarantees (
EG
) fails due to deterministic truncation error as we discussed previously. (
RAMPAGE+
) and (
RAMPAGE
) on the other hand maintain convergence. We uniformly space
\beta_{i}\in[2.0,8.0]
and
\omega_{i}\in[15.0,45.0]
.

2d High-Frequency Rotational Min-Max Game:

Finally, we evaluate a 2-dimensional min-max game. The driving operator is formulated as

F(\theta)=M\theta+0.04\odot\sin(\omega\odot\theta),\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(39)

The matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
takes the form

M=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\

Infinite-Width GANs as Motivation:

The inclusion of high-frequency perturbations in our numerical evaluations is not merely an adversarial stress test; it is of fundamental relevance to the training dynamics of overparameterized neural networks. To establish this correspondence, we examine the continuous limit of GANs operating in the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime
[
52
]
.

Consider a Dirac-GAN formulation
[
67
]
where the generator optimizes a spatial coordinate constrained to the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, parameterized by the absolute angular coordinate
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
. Let the true data distribution be a point mass at the origin
\theta^{*}=0
. We assign the discriminator
D(x)
to be an infinite-width two-layer perceptron with ReLU activations. In this infinite-width limit, the discriminator operates within the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) defined by the exact zero-bias ReLU covariance kernel. For two unit-norm inputs separated by a geodesic angle
\phi\in[0,\pi]
, the kernel evaluates to

k(\phi)=\frac{1}{\pi}\big(\sin\phi+(\pi-\phi)\cos\phi\big).

See
[
7
,
60
,
35
]
for rigorous derivations of this integral evaluation. Extending this static covariance formulation to the continuous periodic domain of the unit circle, the geodesic distance from the target origin evaluates to
|\theta|
. This periodic extension
k(|\theta|)
constitutes an even function, which guarantees that its spectral decomposition over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
consists exclusively of cosine harmonics
k(|\theta|)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\cos(n\theta).

Crucially, because the underlying ReLU activation function
\phi(z)=\max(0,z)
possesses a discontinuous derivative, the induced continuous kernel is globally non-smooth. This singularity dictates that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
exhibit a strict asymptotic polynomial decay rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-3})
.

During the min-max optimization game, the vector field driving the generator’s state is governed by the gradient of the discriminator’s manifold. Differentiating the spectral expansion yields the operator for the generator

F(\theta)=\nabla_{\theta}k(|\theta|)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\lambda_{n}\sin(n\theta).

The application of the derivative operator linearly scales the spectral coefficients by a factor of
n
, shifting the asymptotic decay rate of the driving field’s harmonics to
\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})
. While the series remains absolutely convergent, this redistribution of spectral energy amplifies the relative magnitude of the high-frequency components. This demonstrates that training a GAN with ReLU activations inherently subjects the continuous-time gradient flow to an infinite sequence of high-frequency sine waves. While the leading low-frequency terms vary smoothly, the non-vanishing spectral tail injects extreme nonlinearity into the underlying vector field. As demonstrated in Figure
1
, deterministic discrete-time integration schemes such as (
EG
) incur severe localized truncation errors and are highly sensitive to this spectral structure. In contrast, the unbiased antithetic integration employed by (
RAMPAGE+
) is unbiased and removes the primary variance, yielding a more robust and convergent dynamic in this highly non-linear regime.

8
Conclusion

In this work, we addressed the discretization bias inherent in EG and its variants when applied to non-linear vector fields. To overcome these integration shortcomings, we introduced RAMPAGE, a randomized unbiased gradient extrapolation scheme, and its variance-reduced counterpart, RAMPAGE+. By leveraging exact antithetic sampling, RAMPAGE+ functions as an unbiased geometric path-integrator that mitigates internal first-order variance, providing theoretical and improvements over the base RAMPAGE architecture. We established optimal
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
best-iterate convergence guarantees for root-finding problems across a spectrum of challenging geometric regimes, including co-coercivity, co-hypomonotonicity, and generalized
\alpha
-symmetric Lipschitzness. Furthermore, we extended the proposed stochastic integration framework to constrained VIs through symmetrically scaled variants, and to both deterministic and stochastic smooth convex-concave minimax games, guaranteeing ergodic
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
convergence to Nash equilibria. Notably, our analysis certified that, despite its randomized exploration mechanism, the antithetic coupling in RAMPAGE+ yields purely deterministic bounds in several of the studied settings.

The proposed framework leads to several promising future directions. First, extending the antithetic integration analysis to non-convex-non-concave minimax games and more general nonlinear equation and fixed point calculation is a natural next step. Second, developing accelerated version, e.g. via integrating Halpern’s iteration, and deriving last-iterate convergence guarantees is worth exploring. Finally, developing stochastic version of the proposed methods for general problems, and extending our formalism to optimistic gradient methods is an interesting next step.

Appendix B
Scaled Integration: Conservative vs. Non-Conservative

To establish the geometric role of the exploration scale in numerical integration, we parameterize the exact line integral with a scaling factor
c>0
. The scaled continuous average over the exploration segment is defined as

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{(c)}=\int_{0}^{1}F\big(\theta_{t}-c\eta sF(\theta_{t})\big)ds.

(51)

The choice of the scaling factor
c
reveals a fundamental difference between conservative and non-conservative vector fields. We analyze two canonical examples to demonstrate that while
c=1
is optimal for conservative minimization,
c>1
is mandatory to prevent divergence in non-conservative landscapes.

Conservative Case:
First, consider a conservative vector field induced by a quadratic minimization problem
f(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{T}H\theta
, where
H=H^{T}\succeq 0
possesses a spectrum of real, non-negative eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}\in[0,L]
. The corresponding linear gradient flow is
F(\theta)=H\theta
. Integrating this field yields

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{(c)}=\int_{0}^{1}H(\theta_{t}-c\eta sH\theta_{t})ds=H\theta_{t}-\frac{c\eta}{2}H^{2}\theta_{t}.

(52)

The discrete update mapping utilizing this scaled integral takes the form

\theta_{t+1}=\theta_{t}-\eta\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{(c)}=\left(I-\eta H+\frac{c\eta^{2}}{2}H^{2}\right)\theta_{t}.

(53)

Because
H
is symmetric and positive semi-definite, stability is entirely governed by the scalar spectral mapping of the individual eigenvalues
\lambda

P_{c}(\lambda)=1-\eta\lambda+\frac{c\eta^{2}}{2}\lambda^{2}.

(54)

For the discrete trajectory to converge, the spectral radius must not exceed unity, necessitating
-1\leq P_{c}(\lambda)\leq 1
. Evaluating this inequality yields

-1\leq 1-\eta\lambda+\frac{c\eta^{2}}{2}\lambda^{2}\leq 1\implies\eta\leq\frac{2}{c\lambda},\quad\text{and}\quad c\geq 1/4.

(55)

Since the continuous solution to the linear dynamical system
\dot{\theta}=-H\theta
expands as
\theta(\eta)=\big(I-\eta H+\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}H^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\eta^{3})\big)\theta_{t}
, the unscaled integration with
c=1
is theoretically optimal, guaranteeing stability for step sizes
\eta\leq 2/L
.

Non-Conservative Case:
Conversely, we analyze a non-conservative case defined by an unconstrained bilinear min-max game. The field is purely skew-symmetric:
F(\theta)=S\theta
, with
S=-S^{T}
. Integrating the scaled field evaluates to
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{(c)}=S\theta_{t}-\frac{c\eta}{2}S^{2}\theta_{t}
. The corresponding discrete update matrix becomes
I-\eta S+\frac{c\eta^{2}}{2}S^{2}
. To evaluate absolute stability, we compute the physical energy expansion operator via the squared spectral norm

\displaystyle\left\|I-\eta S+\frac{c\eta^{2}}{2}S^{2}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(I+\eta S+\frac{c\eta^{2}}{2}S^{2}\right)\left(I-\eta S+\frac{c\eta^{2}}{2}S^{2}\right)

(56)

\displaystyle=I+(c-1)\eta^{2}S^{2}+\frac{c^{2}\eta^{4}}{4}S^{4}.

Because the eigenvalues of a real skew-symmetric matrix are purely imaginary,
S^{2}
is strictly negative semi-definite, while
S^{4}
possesses strictly positive real eigenvalues.

Evaluating the squared spectral norm of the discrete update matrix

P_{c}(\lambda)=1-(c-1)\eta^{2}\lambda^{2}+\frac{c^{2}\eta^{4}}{4}\lambda^{4}.

(57)

Absolute stability dictates that
P_{c}(\lambda)\leq 1
for all
|\lambda|\leq L
. Evaluating this inequality leads to the admissible step size region

1-(c-1)\eta^{2}\lambda^{2}+\frac{c^{2}\eta^{4}}{4}\lambda^{4}\leq 1\implies\frac{c^{2}\eta^{2}}{4}\lambda^{2}\leq c-1.

(58)

This bound establishes the maximum permissible step size for any
c>1
to guarantee convergence

\eta\leq\frac{2\sqrt{c-1}}{cL}.

(59)

To achieve maximum convergence, we optimize the scaling factor by maximizing the step size boundary
g(c)=\frac{2\sqrt{c-1}}{c}
over the domain
c>1
. Evaluating the first derivative with respect to
c
yields

\frac{d}{dc}g(c)=\frac{c(c-1)^{-1/2}-2\sqrt{c-1}}{c^{2}}=\frac{2-c}{c^{2}\sqrt{c-1}}.

(60)

The critical point (which is the global maximum) occurs at
c=2
.

-2\eta\langle\bar{F}_{t},\bar{y}_{t}-\theta\rangle=-\eta\langle F(y_{t}),y_{t}-\theta\rangle-\eta\langle F(\tilde{y}_{t}),\tilde{y}_{t}-\theta\rangle+\frac{\eta}{2}\langle F(y_{t})-F(\tilde{y}_{t}),y_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t}\rangle.

(193)

Applying the convex-concave bound to
y_{t}=(x_{y,t},z_{y,t})
and
\tilde{y}_{t}=(x_{\tilde{y},t},z_{\tilde{y},t})
yields

-\eta\langle F(y_{t}),y_{t}-\theta\rangle-\eta\langle F(\tilde{y}_{t}),\tilde{y}_{t}-\theta\rangle\leq-\eta\big[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})\big]-\eta\big[f(x_{\tilde{y},t},z)-f(x,z_{\tilde{y},t})\big].

(194)

For the remaining terms, note that
\theta_{t}-\bar{y}_{t}=\eta F(\theta_{t})
. Thus

-2\eta\langle\bar{F}_{t},\theta_{t}-\bar{y}_{t}\rangle+\eta^{2}\|\bar{F}_{t}\|^{2}=-2\eta^{2}\langle\bar{F}_{t},F(\theta_{t})\rangle+\eta^{2}\|\bar{F}_{t}\|^{2}=\eta^{2}\|\bar{F}_{t}-F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}-\eta^{2}\|F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}.

(195)

Bounding the discrepancy using the triangle inequality and
L
-Lipschitz continuity results in

\displaystyle\eta^{2}\|\bar{F}_{t}-F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\|F(y_{t})-F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\|F(\tilde{y}_{t})-F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}

(196)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta^{2}L^{2}}{2}\|y_{t}-\theta_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}L^{2}}{2}\|\tilde{y}_{t}-\theta_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\eta^{4}L^{2}(u_{t}^{2}+\tilde{u}_{t}^{2})\|F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}.

Bounding the cross-term from (
193
) via the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
L
-Lipschitz continuity yields

\frac{\eta}{2}\langle F(y_{t})-F(\tilde{y}_{t}),y_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t}\rangle\leq\frac{\eta L}{2}\|y_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t}\|^{2}=\frac{\eta L}{2}(2\eta|u_{t}-\tilde{u}_{t}|)^{2}\|F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}=2\eta^{3}L(2u_{t}-1)^{2}\|F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}.

(197)

Merging all terms modifying
\eta^{2}\|F(\theta_{t})\|^{2}
, the resulting coefficient is

C_{R}=-1+2\eta^{2}L^{2}\big(u_{t}^{2}+(1-u_{t})^{2}\big)+2\eta L(2u_{t}-1)^{2}.

(198)

Since
u_{t}\in[0,1]
, we have
u_{t}^{2}+(1-u_{t})^{2}\leq 1
and
(2u_{t}-1)^{2}\leq 1
. Thus,
C_{R}\leq-1+2\eta^{2}L^{2}+2\eta L
. By the condition
\eta\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2L}
,
C_{R+}\leq 0
. Dropping this non-positive term yields the descent inequality

\eta\big[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})+f(x_{\tilde{y},t},z)-f(x,z_{\tilde{y},t})\big]\leq\|\theta_{t}-\theta\|^{2}-\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}.

(199)

Summing from
t=0
to
k
telescopes

\sum_{t=0}^{k}\big[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})+f(x_{\tilde{y},t},z)-f(x,z_{\tilde{y},t})\big]\leq\frac{\|\theta_{0}-\theta\|^{2}-\|\theta_{k+1}-\theta\|^{2}}{\eta}\leq\frac{\|\theta_{0}-\theta\|^{2}}{\eta}.

(200)

Dividing by
2(k+1)
and applying Jensen’s inequality to the jointly convex function
f(\cdot,z)-f(x,\cdot)
evaluated at
\hat{\theta}_{k}
yields

f(\hat{x}_{k},z)-f(x,\hat{z}_{k})\leq\frac{1}{2(k+1)}\sum_{t=0}^{k}\big[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})+f(x_{\tilde{y},t},z)-f(x,z_{\tilde{y},t})\big].

(201)

Substituting (
200
) into (
201
) completes the proof.
∎

E.3
Proof of Theorem
11

Proof of Theorem
11
.

We track
\mathbb{E}[\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}]
. Denote
F_{t}=F(\theta_{t})
,
\hat{F}_{t}=\hat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t})
, and
\hat{G}_{t}=\hat{F}(y_{t},\zeta_{t})
. Substituting the primary update rule yields

\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}=\|\theta_{t}-\theta\|^{2}-2\eta\langle\hat{G}_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta\rangle+\eta^{2}\|\hat{G}_{t}\|^{2}.

(202)

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t,1}
denote the filtration conditioning on
\theta_{t},u_{t}
, and
\xi_{t}
. Taking the conditional expectation with respect to
\zeta_{t}
, unbiasedness ensures
\mathbb{E}_{\zeta_{t}}[\hat{G}_{t}]=F(y_{t})
and
\mathbb{E}_{\zeta_{t}}[\|\hat{G}_{t}\|^{2}]\leq\|F(y_{t})\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}
. This yields

\mathbb{E}_{\zeta_{t}}[\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}]\leq\|\theta_{t}-\theta\|^{2}-2\eta\langle F(y_{t}),\theta_{t}-\theta\rangle+\eta^{2}\|F(y_{t})\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}.

(203)

We decouple the inner product via
y_{t}

-2\eta\langle F(y_{t}),\theta_{t}-\theta\rangle=-2\eta\langle F(y_{t}),y_{t}-\theta\rangle-2\eta\langle F(y_{t}),\theta_{t}-y_{t}\rangle.

(204)

Substituting
\theta_{t}-y_{t}=2\eta u_{t}\hat{F}_{t}
and applying the polarization identity
-2\langle a,b\rangle=\|a-b\|^{2}-\|a\|^{2}-\|b\|^{2}
yields

-4\eta^{2}u_{t}\langle F(y_{t}),\hat{F}_{t}\rangle=2\eta^{2}u_{t}\|F(y_{t})-\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}-2\eta^{2}u_{t}\|F(y_{t})\|^{2}-2\eta^{2}u_{t}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}.

(205)

Merging this with
\eta^{2}\|F(y_{t})\|^{2}
from (
203
) yields
\eta^{2}(1-2u_{t})\|F(y_{t})\|^{2}
. Define
A_{t}=F(y_{t})-F_{t}
. Expanding
\|F(y_{t})\|^{2}=\|F_{t}\|^{2}+2\langle F_{t},A_{t}\rangle+\|A_{t}\|^{2}
yields

\eta^{2}(1-2u_{t})\|F_{t}\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}(1-2u_{t})\langle F_{t},A_{t}\rangle+\eta^{2}(1-2u_{t})\|A_{t}\|^{2}.

(206)

By Young’s inequality

2\eta^{2}(1-2u_{t})\langle F_{t},A_{t}\rangle\leq\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\|F_{t}\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}(1-2u_{t})^{2}\|A_{t}\|^{2}.

(207)

Defining the base oracle noise
N_{t}=F_{t}-\hat{F}_{t}
, we bound the variance term

2\eta^{2}u_{t}\|F(y_{t})-\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}=2\eta^{2}u_{t}\|A_{t}+N_{t}\|^{2}\leq 4\eta^{2}u_{t}\|A_{t}\|^{2}+4\eta^{2}u_{t}\|N_{t}\|^{2}.

(208)

Aggregating the coefficients of
\|A_{t}\|^{2}
yields
\eta^{2}[(1-2u_{t})+2(1-2u_{t})^{2}+4u_{t}]=\eta^{2}(3-6u_{t}+8u_{t}^{2}):=\eta^{2}P(u_{t})
. By
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
F

\|A_{t}\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|y_{t}-\theta_{t}\|^{2}=4\eta^{2}L^{2}u_{t}^{2}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}.

(209)

Thus, the aggregate penalty is bounded by
4\eta^{4}L^{2}u_{t}^{2}P(u_{t})\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}
. Let
Q(u_{t})=4u_{t}^{2}P(u_{t})
. Integrating over the uniform distribution yields
\mathbb{E}[Q(u_{t})]=4.4:=C_{R}
. Taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot]=\mathbb{E}[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]
, independence of
u_{t}
and
\xi_{t}
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\eta^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}-2u_{t}\right)\|F_{t}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\|F_{t}\|^{2},

(210)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[-2\eta^{2}u_{t}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=-\eta^{2}\big(\|F_{t}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|N_{t}\|^{2}]\big),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[4\eta^{2}u_{t}\|N_{t}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|N_{t}\|^{2}],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\eta^{4}L^{2}Q(u_{t})\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta^{4}L^{2}C_{R}\big(\|F_{t}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|N_{t}\|^{2}]\big).

Let
\sigma_{t}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|N_{t}\|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2}
. Summing the expectations yields the coefficient for
\|F_{t}\|^{2}
as
-\eta^{2}\big(\frac{1}{2}-\eta^{2}L^{2}C_{R}\big)
. For
\eta\leq 1/(L\sqrt{2C_{R}})
, this term is non-positive and can be discarded. The remaining variance terms, including
\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}
from (
203
), sum to

\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}-\eta^{2}\sigma_{t}^{2}+2\eta^{2}\sigma_{t}^{2}+\eta^{4}L^{2}C_{R}\sigma_{t}^{2}\leq\eta^{2}\big(2+\eta^{2}L^{2}C_{R}\big)\sigma^{2}.

(211)

Substituting this variance bound into (
203
) and using convax-concave structure yields

2\eta\mathbb{E}\left[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\theta_{t}-\theta\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}\right]+\eta^{2}\big(2+\eta^{2}L^{2}C_{R}\big)\sigma^{2}.

(212)

Summing from
t=0
to
k
telescopes

\sum_{t=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})\right]\leq\frac{\|\theta_{0}-\theta\|^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{(k+1)\eta\big(2+\eta^{2}L^{2}C_{R}\big)}{2}\sigma^{2}.

(213)

Dividing by
k+1
and applying Jensen’s inequality to the jointly convex function
f(\cdot,z)-f(x,\cdot)
evaluated at
\bar{y}_{k}
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[f(\bar{x}_{k},z)-f(x,\bar{z}_{k})\right]\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{t=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})\right].

(214)

This completes the proof.
∎

E.4
Proof of Theorem
12

Proof of Theorem
12
.

We track the expected distance
\mathbb{E}[\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}]
. Substituting the primary stochastic update yields

\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}=\|\theta_{t}-\theta\|^{2}-2\eta\langle\bar{G}_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta\rangle+\eta^{2}\|\bar{G}_{t}\|^{2}.

(215)

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t,1}
denote the filtration conditioning on
\theta_{t},u_{t}
, and
\xi_{t}
. Taking the conditional expectation with respect to the independent update samples
\zeta_{t}
and
\tilde{\zeta}_{t}
, unbiasedness results in
\mathbb{E}[\bar{G}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,1}]=\frac{1}{2}(F(y_{t})+F(\tilde{y}_{t})):=\bar{F}_{t}
. The independence of
\zeta_{t}
and
\tilde{\zeta}_{t}
halves the variance of the averaged estimator

\mathbb{E}_{\zeta,\tilde{\zeta}}\left[\|\bar{G}_{t}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t,1}\right]\leq\|\bar{F}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}.

(216)

This yields the conditionally expected bound

\mathbb{E}_{\zeta,\tilde{\zeta}}\left[\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}\right]\leq\|\theta_{t}-\theta\|^{2}-2\eta\langle\bar{F}_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta\rangle+\eta^{2}\|\bar{F}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2}.

(217)

We decouple the inner product. Using
\theta_{t}-\theta=(\theta_{t}-y_{t})+(y_{t}-\theta)
and symmetrically for
\tilde{y}_{t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle-2\eta\langle\bar{F}_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta\rangle

\displaystyle=-\eta\langle F(y_{t}),y_{t}-\theta\rangle-\eta\langle F(\tilde{y}_{t}),\tilde{y}_{t}-\theta\rangle

(218)

\displaystyle-\eta\langle F(y_{t}),\theta_{t}-y_{t}\rangle-\eta\langle F(\tilde{y}_{t}),\theta_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t}\rangle.

Let
\hat{F}_{t}=\hat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t})
. Substituting
\theta_{t}-y_{t}=2\eta u_{t}\hat{F}_{t}
and
\theta_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t}=2\eta\tilde{u}_{t}\hat{F}_{t}
results in

-2\eta^{2}u_{t}\langle F(y_{t}),\hat{F}_{t}\rangle-2\eta^{2}\tilde{u}_{t}\langle F(\tilde{y}_{t}),\hat{F}_{t}\rangle.

(219)

Applying the polarization identity
-2\langle a,b\rangle=\|a-b\|^{2}-\|a\|^{2}-\|b\|^{2}
and expanding
\eta^{2}\|\bar{F}_{t}\|^{2}=\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\|F(y_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\|F(\tilde{y}_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{4}\|F(y_{t})-F(\tilde{y}_{t})\|^{2}
, the terms
-\eta^{2}u_{t}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}
and
-\eta^{2}\tilde{u}_{t}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}
sum to
-\eta^{2}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}
. This leads to

\displaystyle R_{t}

\displaystyle=-\eta^{2}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-u_{t}\right)\|F(y_{t})\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\tilde{u}_{t}\right)\|F(\tilde{y}_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{4}\|F(y_{t})-F(\tilde{y}_{t})\|^{2}

(220)

\displaystyle+\eta^{2}u_{t}\|F(y_{t})-\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\tilde{u}_{t}\|F(\tilde{y}_{t})-\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}.

Let
F_{t}=F(\theta_{t})
,
A=F(y_{t})-F_{t}
,
B=F(\tilde{y}_{t})-F_{t}
, and
N_{t}=F_{t}-\hat{F}_{t}
. Expanding the negative squared norm results in

-\eta^{2}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}=-\eta^{2}\|F_{t}-N_{t}\|^{2}=-\eta^{2}\|F_{t}\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}\langle F_{t},N_{t}\rangle-\eta^{2}\|N_{t}\|^{2}.

(221)

Bounding the discrepancy norms results in
\eta^{2}u_{t}\|A+N_{t}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\tilde{u}_{t}\|B+N_{t}\|^{2}\leq 2\eta^{2}(u_{t}\|A\|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{t}\|B\|^{2})+2\eta^{2}\|N_{t}\|^{2}
. Taking the expectation over
\xi_{t}
, unbiasedness zeroes
2\eta^{2}\langle F_{t},N_{t}\rangle
. Let
\sigma_{t}^{2}=\mathbb{E}[\|N_{t}\|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2}
. The noise terms combine to
\eta^{2}\sigma_{t}^{2}
.

Expanding the intermediate norms and applying Young’s inequality, we bound the variance using
\|A\|^{2}\leq 4\eta^{2}L^{2}u_{t}^{2}\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}
. Taking the expectation over
u_{t}
results in

\mathbb{E}_{u_{t}}\left[4\eta^{4}L^{2}\big[(2+u_{t})u_{t}^{2}+(3-u_{t})(1-u_{t})^{2}\big]\|\hat{F}_{t}\|^{2}\right]\leq\eta^{4}L^{2}C_{R+}\big(\|F_{t}\|^{2}+\sigma_{t}^{2}\big),

(222)

where
C_{R+}=\frac{22}{3}
. Thus, the expected variance block reduces to

\mathbb{E}[R_{t}]\leq-\eta^{2}\left(\frac{3}{4}-\eta^{2}L^{2}C_{R+}\right)\|F_{t}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\sigma_{t}^{2}+\eta^{4}L^{2}C_{R+}\sigma_{t}^{2}.

(223)

For
\eta\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2L\sqrt{C_{R+}}}
, the coefficient of
\|F_{t}\|^{2}
is non-positive. Truncating this term and including the
\frac{\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2}
variance from the primary update yields

\displaystyle\eta\mathbb{E}\big[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})+f(x_{\tilde{y},t},z)-f(x,z_{\tilde{y},t})\big]\leq

(224)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\theta_{t}-\theta\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta\|^{2}\right]+\eta^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}+\eta^{2}L^{2}C_{R+}\right)\sigma^{2}.

Summing from
t=0
to
k
telescopes

\sum_{t=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\big[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})+f(x_{\tilde{y},t},z)-f(x,z_{\tilde{y},t})\big]\leq\frac{\|\theta_{0}-\theta\|^{2}}{\eta}+(k+1)\eta\left(\frac{3}{2}+\eta^{2}L^{2}C_{R+}\right)\sigma^{2}.

(225)

Dividing by
2(k+1)
and applying Jensen’s inequality to the jointly convex function evaluated at
\hat{\theta}_{k}
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[f(\hat{x}_{k},z)-f(x,\hat{z}_{k})\right]\leq\frac{1}{2(k+1)}\sum_{t=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\big[f(x_{y,t},z)-f(x,z_{y,t})+f(x_{\tilde{y},t},z)-f(x,z_{\tilde{y},t})\big].

(226)

Combining these inequalities completes the proof.
