Spatiotemporal cross-attention hybrid network for weather forecasting in complex terrain
Authors: Zongyun Yang, HaiWei Sang, Yongtao Wang, Qiao Wang, Jian Liu, KaiYin Du, LianYan Mou, YuLing Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWeather forecasting in complex terrain, such as the karst landscapes of Guizhou, faces the dual challenge of capturing microscale topographic effects and macroscale atmospheric dynamics. Existing models often struggle to balance local spatiotemporal patterns with long-range dependencies. To address this, we present a novel hybrid architecture that integrates ConvLSTM and Transformer via a Spatiotemporal Cross-Attention (STCA) mechanism. Our approach utilizes an STL-VMD preprocessing pipeline to decompose non-stationary meteorological signals into stationary subsequences, effectively separating trend and seasonal components. The architecture synergizes ConvLSTM’s local feature extraction with Transformer’s global correlation modeling, while the STCA module dynamically bridges spatial and temporal information through multi-scale convolution and adaptive gating. Validated across 33 meteorological stations in Guizhou over a 51-year period (1961–2011), our model demonstrates superior performance. Compared to baseline LSTM models, temperature forecasting achieves an 18.99% reduction in RMSE (), and notably, precipitation forecasting achieves a 27.74% reduction in RMSE with a substantial 54.08% improvement in . The results confirm the model’s robustness and generalization capability for operational forecasting in topographically complex regions.Keywords: Deep learning, Spatiotemporal modeling, Weather forecasting, Attention mechanism, Time series analysisSubject terms: Climate sciences, Environmental sciences, Hydrology, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Accurate precipitation and temperature forecasting is fundamental to water resource management, agricultural planning, and disaster prevention, with profound implications for ecological sustainability and socioeconomic development. This challenge intensifies in regions characterized by complex topography, where microscale terrain-induced variations interact with macroscale atmospheric circulation patterns. Guizhou Province in southwestern China exemplifies such complexity: with mountainous and hilly terrain comprising over 90% of its total area, dramatic elevation gradients, and predominant karst geomorphology, the region presents substantial difficulties for conventional meteorological prediction approaches.

Traditional forecasting methodologies predominantly rely on statistical models calibrated using historical observations. Time series analysis techniques, particularly the ARIMA model proposed by Albeladi et al.1, have demonstrated effectiveness in capturing temporal autocorrelation for short-term precipitation forecasting. Regression-based approaches further seek to quantify relationships between precipitation and auxiliary meteorological variables, including temperature, humidity, and atmospheric pressure. Sa’idah et al. reported that Holt–Winters exponential smoothing can provide satisfactory performance for reservoir rainfall forecasting under specific conditions2. Despite their computational efficiency and interpretability, these statistical methods exhibit inherent limitations in representing nonlinear dynamics and complex spatiotemporal dependencies, which constrain their applicability in accurate precipitation forecasting. However, these studies only provide general descriptions of limitations and lack in-depth analysis of critical technical flaws, such as the insufficient integration of spatial terrain features and global long-range dependencies, which motivated the proposed spatiotemporal cross-attention hybrid network.

The advent of deep learning has catalyzed significant advances in meteorological forecasting. Li et al. demonstrated that basin-specific model architectures are essential for capturing regional hydrological characteristics3. Johny and Pai proposed a VMD-LSTM hybrid framework integrating signal decomposition with recurrent neural networks to enhance monthly rainfall prediction accuracy4. Chen et al. developed a two-stream convolutional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture that substantially improves nowcasting performance by jointly modeling temporal and spatial features5. However, Jin et al. noted that despite promising results in seasonal precipitation forecasting across China, prediction accuracy in complex terrain regions such as Guizhou remains insufficient for operational applications6. Pareek et al. emphasized the necessity of region-specific model customization through their optimized MVC-LSTM framework7. Akbar et al. compared seasonal ARIMA and LSTM models, confirming the superiority of deep learning approaches under complex meteorological conditions7. However, these deep learning approaches still fail to effectively couple local terrain-sensitive spatial features and global long-range temporal dependencies, which is the core issue addressed in this study.

LSTM networks have become a prevailing approach for rainfall prediction owing to their strong capability in modeling long-range temporal dependencies. Zheng et al. proposed the TISE-LSTM model by integrating spatiotemporal interaction modules, which significantly enhanced the spatial resolution of short-term rainfall forecasts8. Similarly, Oddo et al. demonstrated the effectiveness of convolutional LSTM architectures in precipitation prediction, highlighting their potential for capturing complex precipitation dynamics9.

Attention mechanisms have substantially enhanced model performance by enabling selective focus on salient features. Tao et al. demonstrated that multi-scale LSTM with attention mechanisms significantly improves monthly precipitation forecasting10. Kiran et al. developed an attention-augmented LSTM model integrated with convolutional modules, exhibiting superior performance in rainfall prediction tasks11. Jyostna et al. combined Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD) with LSTM to construct a hybrid framework with enhanced spatiotemporal coverage12. Zhu et al. further demonstrated that hybrid deep learning architectures can effectively address the forecasting difficulties brought by topographic complexity in regional hydrometeorological prediction13. While attention mechanisms and multi-scale modeling advance prediction accuracy, challenges persist in computational efficiency and adaptability.

Transformer architectures represent a paradigm shift in sequence modeling for meteorological applications. Guo and Mao validated LSTM-Transformer hybrid models specifically for long-term precipitation forecasting, demonstrating their effectiveness in capturing the nonlinear and non-stationary characteristics of hydrometeorological time series14. Jiang et al. proposed Transformer-enhanced spatiotemporal neural networks, substantially improving post-processing of numerical weather predictions15. Ling et al. introduced the RNDiff model combining conditional diffusion with Transformers, demonstrating advantages in nowcasting applications16. Kabir et al. presented novel LSTM-Transformer architectures for time series prediction17. However, these approaches suffer from high computational complexity and extensive training data requirements. Recent studies have confirmed that the integration of signal decomposition and deep learning greatly improves the accuracy of hydrological forecasting18. Addressing data scarcity and extreme hydrological events is essential to enhance the generalization ability of forecasting models19.

Data preprocessing innovations have proven critical for model performance enhancement. Zhao et al. proposed STL-OMS for traffic flow prediction, achieving significant accuracy improvements through seasonal-trend decomposition and optimization20. Tebong et al. developed an STL (Seasonal-Trend Decomposition using Loess) integration framework with deep learning21. Zhao et al. introduced a Variational Mode Decomposition-Convolutional Neural Network-Gated Recurrent Unit (VMD-CNN-GRU) hybrid model incorporating spatiotemporal characteristics for short-term wind power forecasting. Ahmadi et al. designed a VMD (Variational Mode Decomposition) and machine learning hybrid methodology for river runoff prediction22. Tamilselvi et al. demonstrated that wavelet-LSTM effectively handles non-stationary sequences23. Mohammed et al. reduced prediction errors through a sliding window discrete wavelet transformer22. Hu et al. proposed stream LSTM models with improved adaptability to dynamic environments24. Ma et al. enhanced seasonal forecasting by integrating spatiotemporal evolution characteristics25. Despite these advances, challenges remain in balancing computational efficiency with model stability.

Variational mode decomposition

Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD), proposed by Dragomiretskiy et al., represents an advanced signal processing technique that decomposes the original signal into discrete intrinsic mode functions. Unlike empirical decomposition methods, VMD formulates the decomposition as a constrained variational problem, providing high adaptability and strong robustness while effectively enabling signal denoising. This characteristic makes VMD particularly valuable for preprocessing non-stationary meteorological signals.

The VMD algorithm proceeds through three fundamental steps. First, the Hilbert Transformer is applied to compute each mode’s analytic signal , obtaining its one-sided spectrum. This transformation enables the algorithm to work independently with the frequency content of each mode. Second, each mode and its corresponding center frequency are shifted to the baseband, demodulating the signal. Third, the bandwidth of each mode is estimated through the Gaussian smoothness of the demodulated signal.

Data description

This study employs monthly precipitation (mm) and temperature (C) data from 33 meteorological stations in Guizhou Province, spanning the period from 1961 to 2011. The dataset was obtained from the National Meteorological Information Center of the China Meteorological Administration. Guizhou’s complex topography, characterized predominantly by mountainous terrain with significant karst formations, results in a climate system exhibiting pronounced spatial heterogeneity and strong nonlinear characteristics. The 33 monitoring stations are distributed across an elevation gradient ranging from 137 to 2930 meters, effectively capturing the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of regional precipitation patterns. Furthermore, the complex terrain induces multi-scale atmospheric interactions that lead to frequent and abrupt extreme weather events. This distinctive climatic context is ideal for validating model adaptability to complex meteorological conditions. The geographical coordinates (latitude, longitude) and altitude of each meteorological station are included as key auxiliary input features. Methodology

LSTM prediction model

LSTM networks have emerged as the preferred architecture for precipitation and temperature forecasting due to their capacity to model nonlinear relationships in time series data. LSTM exhibits strong robustness to noisy and incomplete observations while maintaining high prediction accuracy in complex environments. The LSTM architecture overcomes key limitations of traditional Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) through the introduction of three gating mechanisms, namely the forget gate, input gate, and output gate. These gates regulate the flow of information within the network, allowing relevant historical information to be retained while redundant information is discarded, thereby alleviating the gradient vanishing and explosion problems commonly observed in conventional RNNs32,33. Owing to the incorporation of memory cells that preserve information over long time horizons, LSTM is particularly suitable for time series modelling tasks involving long-term dependencies, such as precipitation and temperature prediction. In comparison with traditional RNN models, LSTM demonstrates a superior capability to capture temporal dynamics by effectively learning seasonal variations and periodic fluctuation patterns. The core components of the LSTM prediction model34–36 operate through a series of gating mechanisms that control information flow. This integration exploits the complementary strengths of both architectures: ConvLSTM leverages convolutional operations to capture terrain-sensitive local spatial features and short-term temporal dependencies, while the Transformer’s self-attention mechanism models global meteorological correlations across all monitoring stations and long-range temporal dependencies. Spatiotemporal cross-attention mechanism

The spatiotemporal cross-attention mechanism constitutes a central innovation of the proposed architecture, as it enables effective fusion of spatial and temporal information. As shown in Fig. 3, this mechanism is particularly well suited to precipitation forecasting tasks that demand an integrated representation of spatiotemporal dependencies. In contrast to conventional attention mechanisms that operate exclusively within either the spatial or temporal domain, the proposed cross-attention design supports bidirectional information interaction between these two dimensions, thereby enhancing the model’s capacity to capture complex spatiotemporal patterns.

Architecture of the spatiotemporal cross-attention mechanism. The mechanism comprises spatial and temporal attention modules that bidirectionally exchange information, enabling comprehensive integration of spatiotemporal features.

The proposed mechanism achieves this integration through two complementary and interactively coupled modules:

Physically-informed spatial attention: The spatial attention module explicitly incorporates temporal context to capture spatial correlations among meteorological stations. In precipitation forecasting, rainfall distribution within a given region is inherently interdependent rather than isolated, as it is shaped by historical temporal dynamics and interactions with surrounding areas. By embedding temporal information, this module effectively characterizes the evolution of spatial patterns, thereby enabling the representation of processes such as the propagation of weather systems and the modulation of precipitation by topographic influences.

Spatially-aware temporal attention: Conversely, the temporal attention module integrates spatial features to more effectively characterize temporal dynamics. For example, temporal precipitation variability within a watershed is strongly conditioned by spatial attributes, including terrain configuration, elevation gradients, and land-cover heterogeneity across subregions. By jointly modeling these spatial characteristics with temporal sequences, the module can discern latent patterns such as seasonality modulated by topography and the seasonal and interannual evolution of precipitation driven by local geographic factors. This interactive spatiotemporal coupling facilitates a more comprehensive representation of precipitation processes, thereby enhancing the model’s capacity to capture complex precipitation evolution and improving predictive performance under diverse meteorological conditions.

Experimental setup and evaluation metrics

Implementation details

All experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel Core i7-12700H processor (2.30 GHz) and an NVIDIA GTX 1060 GPU with 3 GB of memory. Model training hyperparameters were carefully configured to balance convergence efficiency and generalization performance. Specifically, the models were trained for 30 epochs using a learning rate of 0.001 and a batch size of 32. The dataset was temporally partitioned into a training set (1961–2001, year < 2002) and a test set (2002–2011, year \ge 2002). This chronological division ensures that model evaluation reflects realistic forecasting scenarios, wherein future observations are predicted solely based on historical data. Furthermore, this approach effectively prevents data leakage and provides a robust assessment of the model’s generalization capability.

To ensure a fair comparison among all comparative models, the same set of hyperparameters was applied to all six configurations. These hyperparameters, including learning rate, batch size, training epochs, and optimizer settings, were determined in advance and kept fixed across all experiments. Keeping hyperparameters consistent avoids performance differences caused by different tuning processes and guarantees the comparability of experimental results.

Evaluation metrics

Model performance was assessed using three complementary metrics that capture different aspects of prediction accuracy. Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) quantifies the deviation between predicted and true values, providing a measure that penalizes large errors more heavily than small ones due to the squaring operation. The computation proceeds through three steps: first, calculating the squared error for each prediction , then averaging these squared errors across all samples, and finally taking the square root of the mean to maintain dimensional consistency with the original data. Mean Absolute Error (MAE) provides a complementary perspective by directly computing the average absolute error between predicted and true values. Unlike RMSE, MAE treats all errors equally without amplifying larger deviations, offering a more intuitive interpretation of average prediction error magnitude. Experimental design and regional coverage

To comprehensively evaluate the proposed hybrid architecture, we conducted extensive experiments across 33 meteorological stations distributed throughout Guizhou Province. These stations were selected to represent the region’s diverse topographic and climatic characteristics, ensuring robust assessment of model performance under varying environmental conditions. The experimental framework employs six different algorithms for comparative analysis, enabling systematic evaluation of the contributions from different architectural components.

The input data comprises monthly average temperature and precipitation records from each region, while the prediction task involves forecasting the monthly average temperature and precipitation for the subsequent month based on the preceding seven months of observations. The selection of the seven-month input window is determined by the seasonal monsoon characteristics of the study area and comparative sensitivity analysis, which can adequately capture the cumulative hydrothermal and atmospheric circulation signals for stable monthly prediction. This sliding window approach simulates operational forecasting scenarios where models must generate predictions using only the historical data available during the forecast. This experiment reports the average single-step inference time for predicting one-month data at a single station for temperature and precipitation of each model, based on 10 years of meteorological data from 33 regions. The precipitation prediction results reveal more pronounced performance differences among the models. The Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD architecture achieves a commanding lead with an of 0.7650, representing a 54.08% improvement over the baseline LSTM model. This breakthrough can be attributed to the synergistic effect of ConvLSTM’s spatial feature extraction capability and Transformer’s long-range dependency modeling. The performance of Transformer_LSTM_STL_VMD ( = 0.5430) demonstrates that simple architectural combinations yield moderate improvements, while pure decomposition models without spatial convolution components perform poorly. These results underscore the importance of the spatiotemporal cross-attention mechanism in effectively integrating information from multiple scales and modalities.

Temperature prediction performance analysis

Figure 4 compares the performance of various deep learning models in temperature time series prediction, presenting both the observed temperature trajectory and predictions from all six models. The visualization employs data from the Anlong region as a representative case study, selected for its moderate topographic complexity and typical seasonal temperature patterns.

Comparison of observed and predicted temperature time series for the Anlong region. The proposed Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD model (orange) demonstrates superior tracking of seasonal trends and short-term fluctuations compared to baseline models.

The baseline LSTM model can capture fundamental temperature trends but exhibits limited accuracy in predicting detailed fluctuations and extreme values. The temporal smoothing inherent in the recurrent architecture results in predictions that lag behind rapid temperature transitions, particularly during seasonal transitions and synoptic-scale weather events. The Transformer model improves performance in long-term dependency modeling because of its self-attention mechanism, which enables it to directly access information from distant time steps. However, the Transformer still exhibits response lag to abrupt temperature changes, suggesting limitations in capturing high-frequency variations despite its strength in modeling long-range patterns.

Models incorporating STL_VMD decomposition (LSTM_STL_VMD and Transformer_STL_VMD) show mixed results. While decomposition successfully separates trend and seasonal components, the isolated processing of these components without spatial context integration fails to improve overall prediction accuracy. In some cases, decomposition degrades performance by disrupting the natural coupling between different temporal scales in the temperature signal.

The proposed Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD model achieves the closest alignment with observed temperatures across all time periods. The prediction curve exhibits high fidelity in capturing gradual seasonal variations and rapid synoptic-scale fluctuations. This superior performance stems from the model’s ability to simultaneously decompose multiple scales while maintaining spatial coherence through ConvLSTM and integrating global patterns through the Transformer attention mechanism. The spatiotemporal cross-attention module proves particularly effective in this context, enabling the model to dynamically weight the contributions of spatial and temporal features based on the current meteorological context.

Figure 5 presents scatter plots comparing predicted versus observed temperatures across the full range of temperature values encountered in the dataset. This visualization provides complementary insights into model performance characteristics across different temperature regimes. The baseline Transformer and LSTM models perform reasonably well at temperature extremes (both low and high temperatures). However, exhibits decreased accuracy in the mid-range temperatures that constitute the majority of observations. This pattern suggests that these models may be overfitting to extreme values in the training data while failing to capture the subtle variations in typical conditions adequately.

Models augmented with STL and VMD decomposition show improved overall consistency, but mid-range temperature prediction accuracy remains suboptimal. The scattered distribution of prediction points around the ideal 1:1 line indicates persistent systematic biases that decomposition alone cannot address. In contrast, the Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD model exhibits substantially improved performance across the entire temperature spectrum. The tight clustering of prediction points around the diagonal, particularly in the mid-range temperatures, demonstrates the effectiveness of the spatiotemporal cross-attention mechanism in achieving balanced performance across diverse meteorological conditions. This comprehensive improvement validates the proposed architectural innovations and confirms that integrating spatial convolution, temporal attention, and multi-scale decomposition successfully addresses the limitations of simpler approaches.

Precipitation prediction performance analysis

Precipitation forecasting presents substantially greater challenges than temperature prediction due to precipitation events’ highly intermittent and spatially heterogeneous nature. Figure 6 illustrates the predicted versus observed precipitation time series, again using the Anlong region as a representative case. As a classic recurrent neural network architecture, LSTM’s prediction curve roughly follows the overall trend of observed precipitation but exhibits substantial deviations in capturing detailed variations. The model produces overly smooth predictions that fail to capture the interannual and seasonal fluctuation characteristics of monthly precipitation, resulting in systematic underestimation of precipitation intensity and variability.

Comparison of observed and predicted precipitation time series for the Anlong region. The proposed Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD model demonstrates exceptional capability in capturing inter-annual and seasonal precipitation variability.

The Transformer model demonstrates a notable ability to capture long-term dependencies in the precipitation sequence. Its prediction curve somewhat agrees with observed values in most years, reflecting the model’s capacity to learn seasonal patterns and inter-annual variability.

The proposed Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD model achieves remarkable alignment between predicted and observed precipitation across the entire time series. The prediction curve exhibits high fidelity in capturing annual precipitation fluctuation patterns, successfully reproducing regular precipitation cycles. Whether during years with moderate seasonal variability or years with anomalous precipitation totals, the predicted curve maintains consistent phase and amplitude agreement with observations.

The scatter plot analysis presented in Fig. 7 quantitatively assesses prediction accuracy across the full range of precipitation values. Substantial differences emerge among the models regarding prediction accuracy for annual precipitation. The fusion framework model demonstrates significantly superior performance to single-architecture models across all precipitation regimes. The Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD model achieves prediction points closest to the measured values, with an value of 0.82, substantially exceeding those of other models (ranging from 0.65 to 0.67). Correspondingly, the model achieves the lowest RMSE (37.91 mm) and MAE (27.98 mm) among all evaluated approaches.

Scatter plots of predicted versus observed precipitation across all 33 regions. The proposed Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD model (bottom right) demonstrates superior performance across the full precipitation range, with particularly notable improvements in capturing high precipitation values (>300 mm) where baseline models exhibit systematic underestimation.

In the low precipitation regime (less than 100 mm per month), LSTM and Transformer models exhibit scattered prediction points with high variance, indicating poor reliability for light precipitation events. Models incorporating STL-VMD decomposition, particularly Transformer_LSTM_STL_VMD, show improved stability in this regime, suggesting that multi-scale decomposition helps resolve low-intensity precipitation signals that might otherwise be obscured by noise. However, systematic biases remain evident in these intermediate models.

The high precipitation regime (exceeding 300 mm per month) reveals critical limitations of traditional models. LSTM and Transformer consistently underestimate large precipitation values systematically, with prediction points falling substantially below the ideal 1:1 line. This bias reflects these models’ difficulty in extrapolating beyond the typical range of precipitation values encountered during training. In contrast, the Transformer_ConvLSTM_STL_VMD model, incorporating convolutional modules, demonstrates superior capability in capturing large precipitation values. Prediction points cluster tightly around the ideal line with the narrowest confidence interval among all models. This performance advantage can be attributed to the model’s multi-scale processing architecture: ConvLSTM captures local spatial patterns, STL-VMD decomposition separates the effective precipitation signal from background variability, and the Transformer attention mechanism integrates large-scale atmospheric circulation information. The spatiotemporal cross-attention mechanism integrates these heterogeneous information sources, thereby facilitating accurate precipitation predictions.

These results comprehensively validate the advantages of the multi-module collaborative architecture for complex meteorological prediction tasks. The consistent performance improvements across diverse precipitation regimes and geographic regions demonstrate the robustness and generalization capability of the proposed approach, confirming its suitability for operational weather forecasting applications in topographically complex regions. To further support this claim, current operational monthly precipitation forecasting systems in Guizhou typically yield an value below 0.60. In comparison, our model achieves an of 0.765, showing a clear improvement and strong potential for practical operational use.

Conclusion

Accurate precipitation and temperature forecasting is critical for water resource management and disaster mitigation in topographically complex regions such as Guizhou’s karst landscapes, where terrain-induced variations create highly nonlinear meteorological dynamics that challenge traditional forecasting methods. Our study proposes a novel hybrid deep learning framework integrating STL-VMD preprocessing, ConvLSTM spatial feature extraction, and Transformer-based global attention mechanisms. The architecture employs three key innovations: STL-VMD decomposition transforms non-stationary meteorological signals into stationary subsequences; ConvLSTM-Transformer integration captures both local spatial features and long-range temporal dependencies; and spatiotemporal cross-attention enables dynamic fusion of spatial and temporal information through adaptive gating.

Comprehensive validation using 51 years of observations (1961–2011) from 33 stations demonstrates substantial improvements over baseline LSTM. For temperature prediction, the model achieves 18.99% and 26.13% reductions in RMSE and MAE with of 0.9642. Precipitation forecasting shows even greater gains: 27.74% RMSE reduction and 54.08% improvement to 0.7650, with superior capability in capturing extreme events exceeding 300 mm per month. Consistent performance across 32 of 33 regions confirms robust generalization. These results demonstrate successful integration of multi-scale decomposition, spatial feature extraction, and global attention mechanisms for complex terrain meteorological forecasting. The framework provides actionable support for disaster prevention and water resource management in southwestern China, with potential for technology transfer to other topographically complex regions. Future work will focus on several concrete and practical directions. First, we plan to extend the model to sub-daily (e.g. hourly or 6-hourly) forecasting to capture short-lived extreme weather events. Second, we will develop probabilistic forecasting capabilities to provide uncertainty estimates, which are critical for risk assessment and decision-making. Third, we will incorporate physical constraints from numerical weather prediction models into the deep learning framework to improve interpretability and physical consistency. Finally, we aim to deploy the model in an operational setting for real-time weather forecasting in Guizhou Province, including further optimization for computational efficiency.
