A note on the extremal number of Berge- C 4 C_{4}
Nika Salia, Casey Tompkins

Abstract
We improve the known upper bound for the extremal number of Berge- C 4 C_{4} -free 3 3 -uniform hypergraphs. More precisely, we prove that every n n -vertex 3 3 -uniform hypergraph with no Berge cycle of length four has at most n 3 / 2 2 + 2 + O ⁡ ( n ) \frac{n^{3/2}}{2+\sqrt{2}}+O(n) hyperedges. This improves the previous best-known leading constant 1 / 10 1/\sqrt{10} to 1 / ( 2 + 2 ) 1/(2+\sqrt{2}) .

Introduction

The extremal number of
C_{4}
in graphs is a classical problem in graph theory. The theorem of Kővári, Sós and Turán
[
8
]

and the finite-geometric constructions of Erdős, Rényi and
Sós
[
3
]
yields the asymptotically sharp estimate

\operatorname{ex}(n,C_{4})=\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)n^{3/2}.

In this note, we investigate the corresponding problem for Berge
4
-cycles in
3
-uniform hypergraphs.

A Berge cycle of length
\ell
consists of distinct vertices

v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}
and
\ell
distinct hyperedges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}
such that

\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subset e_{i}

for every
i
, where indices are taken modulo
\ell
. This notion originates in Berge
[
1
]
. Let
\operatorname{ex}_{r}(n,\mathrm{BC}_{\ell})
denote the maximum number of edges in an
r
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph containing no Berge
cycle of length
\ell
.

Győri
[
7
]
proved that a Berge-
C_{3}
-free
3
-uniform hypergraph on

n
vertices has at most
n^{2}/8
edges for all sufficiently large
n
, and this
bound is asymptotically sharp. Write
\operatorname{ex}_{r}(n,\mathrm{BC}_{\ell})
for the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex,
r
-uniform hypergraph with no Berge cycle of length
\ell
. Győri and Lemons proved more generally that,

\operatorname{ex}_{r}(n,\mathrm{BC}_{\ell})=O_{r,\ell}\!\left(n^{1+1/\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor}\right)

for every fixed
r\geq 3
and
\ell\geq 3

[
6
]
.
We will focus on the case of
\operatorname{ex}_{3}(n,BC_{4})
. Füredi and
Özkahya obtained the bound

\operatorname{ex}_{3}(n,\mathrm{BC}_{4})\leq(1+o(1))\frac{2n^{3/2}}{3}

as part of their work on Berge cycles of prescribed length
[
5
]
. Ergemlidze, Győri, Methuku, Salia and Tompkins
[
4
]
later improved this to

\operatorname{ex}_{3}(n,\mathrm{BC}_{4})\leq(1+o(1))\frac{n^{3/2}}{\sqrt{10}}.

Our main result is the following improvement of the leading coefficient.

Theorem 1
.

For every
n
,

\operatorname{ex}_{3}(n,\mathrm{BC}_{4})\leq\frac{n^{3/2}}{2+\sqrt{2}}+O(n).

The best-known lower bound has a leading constant
1/(3\sqrt{3})
and comes from
a construction of Bollobás and Győri
[
2
]
.
Start with an extremal
C_{4}
-free bipartite graph with color classes of size

n/3
and
(1+o(1))n^{3/2}/(3\sqrt{3})
edges. Fix one color class, and for each vertex
v
contained in that class, add a new vertex
v^{\prime}
. Then, replace each edge
vw
incident with
v
by the triple
\{v,v^{\prime},w\}
. The resulting
3
-uniform hypergraph has

n
vertices, has
(1+o(1))n^{3/2}/(3\sqrt{3})
edges, and contains no
Berge-
C_{4}
.

The proof of Theorem
1
is a refinement the path-counting method of Ergemlidze, Győri,
Methuku, Salia and Tompkins
[
4
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1

For a hypergraph
H
, its
2
-shadow
\partial H
is the graph on
V(H)
in which
xy
is an edge if some hyperedge of
H
contains
\{x,y\}
.

We say that two hyperedges
e,f\in E(H)
are equivalent if there is a sequence
e=e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}=f
of hyperedges of
H
such that
|e_{i}\cap e_{i+1}|=2
for every
i
. A
block
of
H
is the subhypergraph formed by one equivalence class of hyperedges, with vertex set equal to the union of those hyperedges. Thus, the blocks form a partition of
E(H)
. Every edge of
\partial H
belongs to the
2
-shadow of a unique block.

The following block classification was used
[
4
]
.
Let
H
be a Berge-
C_{4}
-free
3
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices, let
m=|E(H)|
, and put
G=\partial H
. Every block
B
of
H
is either isomorphic to

K_{4}^{(3)-}
, the
3
-uniform hypergraph on four vertices with three edges, or has the
following star-like form: there is an edge
e\in E(B)
such that every other
edge of
B
shares two vertices with
e
, and any two non-central edges
intersect only inside
e
.
Consequently,

|V(B)|\geq|E(B)|,\qquad|E(\partial B)|\geq 2|E(B)|

(1)

for every block
B
.

For
v\in V(H)
, write
d_{s}(v)
for the degree of
v
in
G
, and let
d_{b}(v)
be the number of blocks containing
v
in
H
. By (
1
), we have

\sum_{v}d_{s}(v)=2|E(G)|=2\sum_{B}|E(\partial B)|\geq 2\sum_{B}2|E(B)|=4m,\qquad\sum_{v}d_{b}(v)=\sum_{B}|V(B)|\geq m.

(2)

A hyperedge contributes at most three shadow edges
and is part of a unique block. Hence

\sum_{v}d_{s}(v)\leq 6m,\qquad\sum_{v}d_{b}(v)\leq 3m.

For a block
B
containing
v
, let
N_{B}(v)=\{x\in V(H):vx\in E(\partial B)\}
and
a_{B}(v)=|N_{B}(v)|
. The sets
N_{B}(v)
over all blocks containing
v
partition
N_{G}(v)
, and
a_{B}(v)\geq 2
for every such
B
. For each vertex
v
, enumerate the blocks containing
v
as
B_{1},\ldots,B_{d_{b}(v)}
, and write
a_{i}(v)=a_{B_{i}}(v)
. Define

P=\sum_{v}\binom{d_{s}(v)}{2},\qquad Q=\sum_{v}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d_{b}(v)}a_{i}(v)a_{j}(v).

Thus,
P
is the total number of
3
-vertex paths in
G
.
Among these paths,
Q
counts precisely those whose two edges lie in the shadows of distinct blocks. We call such paths
cross-block paths
.

We say that a
4
-cycle
x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{1}
in
G
is
rare
if the induced hypergraph
H[\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}]
contains no two distinct hyperedges sharing diagonal pair
x_{1}x_{3}
or
x_{2}x_{4}
. We say that a
3
-vertex path
xyz
in
G
is
good
if
\{x,y,z\}\notin E(H)
and there is no vertex
w
such that
wxyzw
is a rare
4
-cycle.

The next three lemmas are Claim 3, Claim 4, and the subsequent non-good path count in
[
4
]
. We include short proofs for completeness.

Lemma 2
.

For any two distinct vertices
a,b\in V(H)
, the number of vertices

v\in V(H)\setminus\{a,b\}
for which
avb
is a path in
G
and

\{a,b,v\}\notin E(H)
is at most two. Consequently, there are at most two
good paths with any prescribed pair of endpoints.

Proof.

Suppose
av_{i}b
for
i=1,2,3
are three such paths. Choose hyperedges
e_{i}\supset\{a,v_{i}\}
and
f_{i}\supset\{b,v_{i}\}
. Since
\{a,v_{i},b\}\notin E(H)
, we have
e_{i}\neq f_{i}
. Also
e_{i}\neq f_{j}
for
i\neq j
, since one triple cannot contain
a,v_{i},b,v_{j}
. Relabel so that
e_{1}\neq e_{2},e_{3}
and
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
. Then
e_{1},f_{1},f_{2},e_{2}
are distinct and form the Berge cycle
ae_{1}v_{1}f_{1}bf_{2}v_{2}e_{2}a
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3
.

There are at most
6m
rare
4
-cycles in
G
.

Proof.

Every
4
-cycle in
G
can be represented by some hyperedge contained in its four vertices.
Otherwise, its four shadow edges would be supported by four distinct
hyperedges, giving a Berge-
C_{4}
. Fix such a representative

e=\{a,b,c\}
. If a rare cycle represented by
e
has diagonal
ab
and fourth
vertex
z
, then
azb
is a path in
G
not contained in a hyperedge. By
Lemma
2
, there are at most two choices for
z
. Applying the
same argument to the three pairs in
e
, the edge
e
represents at most six
rare cycles. Thus, there are at most
6m
rare
4
-cycles in
G
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

The number of non-good
3
-vertex paths in
G
is at most
21m
.

Proof.

A non-good path is either contained in a hyperedge of
H
, which are at most
3m
, or lies in a rare
4
-cycle. By Lemma
3
, there are at most
6m
rare
4
-cycles. Each has a representative hyperedge on three of its vertices, so each contributes at most three further paths. The total is at most
3m+3\cdot 6m=21m
.
∎

The following lemma is the part of Claim 5 in
[
4
]
that we need, stated in terms of cross-block paths.

Lemma 5
.

If
\{x,y\}
is the endpoint pair of a cross-block path
xvy
, then there is at most one good path in
G
with endpoints
x,y
.

Proof.

Assume that, besides the cross-block path
xvy
, there is a good path
xuy
,
where
u\neq v
. Choose supporting hyperedges

e_{x}\supset\{x,v\},\quad e_{y}\supset\{v,y\},\quad h_{x}\supset\{x,u\},\quad h_{y}\supset\{u,y\},

with
e_{x}
and
e_{y}
in distinct blocks. Since
xuy
is good,
h_{x}\neq h_{y}
.
If
e_{x},e_{y},h_{y},h_{x}
were distinct, they would form a Berge-
C_{4}

xvyu
. Hence, some coincidence must occur. As the hypergraph is
3
-uniform, we have either
h_{x}=e_{x}
or
h_{y}=e_{y}
.

Assume
h_{x}=e_{x}
, so
e_{x}=\{x,u,v\}
. The case
h_{y}=e_{y}
is symmetric.
We show the cycle
vxuyv
is rare.
The diagonals of
vxuyv
are
vu
and
xy
. For the diagonal
xy
, we have

\{x,u,y\}\notin E(H)
since
xuy
is good. Also

\{x,v,y\}\notin E(H)
, since such a hyperedge would share
xv
with

e_{x}
and
vy
with
e_{y}
, forcing
e_{x}
and
e_{y}
into the same block.
For the diagonal
vu
, the only possible second hyperedge besides

e_{x}=\{x,u,v\}
is
\{y,u,v\}
. This hyperedge is also impossible, since it
would share
uv
with
e_{x}
and
vy
with
e_{y}
, again forcing
e_{x}

and
e_{y}
into the same block. Thus
vxuyv
is rare, contradicting that
xuy
is good.

∎

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the set of unordered pairs
\{x,y\}
that occur as endpoints of at least one cross-block path, and let
D=|\mathcal{D}|
.

Lemma 6
.

We have
D\geq Q-12m
.

Proof.

For an unordered pair
\{x,y\}
, let
k(\{x,y\})
be the number of middle vertices
v
for which
xvy
is a cross-block path. Then
Q=\sum_{\{x,y\}}k(\{x,y\})
and
D=|\{\{x,y\}:k(\{x,y\})>0\}|
. Fix a pair with
k(\{x,y\})>0
and choose one cross-block middle vertex
v_{0}
. For every other such middle vertex
w
, the cycle
xv_{0}ywx
is rare. Indeed, if the shared diagonal is
xy
, then
\{x,y,v_{0}\}
joins the two blocks at

v_{0}
.
If it is
v_{0}w
, then
\{v_{0},w,x\}
and
\{v_{0},w,y\}
do the
same. In both cases, this contradicts that
xv_{0}y
is cross-block.
Hence, the
k(\{x,y\})-1
additional middle vertices yield distinct rare cycles. Since each rare cycle has only two pairs of opposite vertices, it is charged to at most two endpoint pairs. By Lemma
3
,
Q-D\leq 2\cdot 6m,

and the claim follows.

We now upper-bound
P+Q
. By Lemmas
2
and
5
, each pair of vertices is the endpoint pair of at most two good paths, and each pair in
\mathcal{D}
is the endpoint pair of at most one. Hence, the number of good paths is at most
2\binom{n}{2}-D
. With Lemmas
4
and
6
,

P\leq 2\binom{n}{2}-D+21m\leq 2\binom{n}{2}-Q+33m,

so

P+Q\leq n^{2}+33m.

It remains to lower-bound
P+Q
. Fix a vertex
v

and recall the blocks containing
v
are
B_{1},\ldots,B_{d_{b}(v)}
, and we write
a_{i}(v)=a_{B_{i}}(v)
.
We have
a_{i}(v)\geq 2
and
\sum_{i}a_{i}(v)=d_{s}(v)
which implies
a_{i}(v)a_{j}(v)\geq 2(a_{i}(v)+a_{j}(v))-4

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d_{b}(v)}a_{i}(v)a_{j}(v)\geq 2(d_{b}(v)-1)\sum_{1\leq i\leq d_{b}(v)}a_{i}(v)-4\binom{d_{b}(v)}{2}=2(d_{b}(v)-1)(d_{s}(v)-d_{b}(v)).

Therefore the contribution of

as the middle vertex is at least

\displaystyle\binom{d_{s}(v)}{2}+2(d_{b}(v)-1)(d_{s}(v)-d_{b}(v))=\left(\frac{1}{2}d_{s}(v)^{2}+2d_{b}(v)d_{s}(v)-2d_{b}(v)^{2}\right)-\frac{5}{2}d_{s}(v)+2d_{b}(v)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{d_{s}(v)}{\sqrt{2}}+(2-\sqrt{2})d_{b}(v)\right)^{2}-\frac{5}{2}d_{s}(v)+2d_{b}(v).

Since
d_{s}(v)\geq 2d_{b}(v)
. Summing over all vertices gives
