A lightweight ResNet50V2–ECA model for renal cell carcinoma grading: efficiency, calibration, and state-of-the-art performance
Authors: Majed Alwateer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate International Society of Urological Pathology (ISUP)-grade classification of renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is challenging due to subtle histopathological variations and the limitations of manual review. Existing deep learning models often rely on complex attention mechanisms that increase computational cost and hinder deployment. This study introduces a lightweight ResNet50V2-ECA framework that adds a single Efficient Channel Attention block after the final convolutional layer, enhancing channel-wise discrimination with minimal overhead (0.25 M parameters, 3.1 GFLOPs). Placing a single ECA block before GAP allows global channel recalibration after spatial feature extraction, avoiding the computational redundancy of multi-stage attention while preserving spatial resolution. Using the KMC kidney histopathology dataset (722 WSIs, 3,442 training patches across five grades), the model achieves 96.90% accuracy, 91.39% F1-score, and near-perfect AUC scores (Grade-0: 1.000; Grade-4: 0.997) across standard metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1, specificity, BAC, AUC). A comprehensive ablation study across five attention mechanisms (BAM, CBAM, SE, GC, ECA) and three backbones confirms that ResNet50V2-ECA yields the highest overall performance. Comparative analysis with leading RCC frameworks (RoCNN, EFF-Net, RCCGNet, RenalNet, MobileDANet) further demonstrates superior accuracy and efficiency. Model reliability is validated through a calibration diagram using Monte Carlo Dropout, showing strong alignment between predicted confidence and actual accuracy, while the risk-coverage curve reveals accuracy exceeding 99% when low-confidence cases are deferred. Together, these findings establish a high-precision, well-calibrated RCC grading system with strong potential for clinical deployment. Introduction

Kidney cancer is a major global health problem, of which renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is by far the most common, making up approximately 80%:85% of all renal tumors1,2. Annual new cases in the United States are more than 80,000 most of the time, with a forecast of around 81,000 new cases in 2024; as a result, the number of deaths reaches tens of thousands every year3,4. Such a significant occurrence highlights the diagnostic challenge as the most essential task (especially the exact tumor grading and subtype identification), which is instrumental in determining the outcome and providing the right treatment strategies5–7.

At present, the major diagnostic sequence for kidney cancer, which is largely dependent on the manual review of histopathological images, is posing substantial difficulties. The traditional method is, by nature, slow and labor-intensive, and is influenced by human error and differences among pathologists8,9. Moreover, it is complicated by the complexities of the microscopic world and differences in tissue staining, which require a very high level of specialized knowledge from the pathologists5,10. Those obstacles, therefore, affect the timing and effectiveness of the most important treatment decisions, and thus patient outcomes may be compromised, and management may be lower than expected3,6.

As a result, advanced, automated methods are urgently needed to overcome these diagnostic challenges. Early and accurate subtyping and grading of RCC are essential for tailoring optimal treatment regimens and improving patient survival4,8,10. Although deep learning intelligence heralds major advances in cancer feature identification, current artificial intelligence techniques frequently encounter computational requirements beyond standard capabilities or have insufficient diagnostic accuracy, operational efficiency, or range of use; thus requiring higher, resource-saving solutions2,4,9.

Our ResNet50V2-ECA framework is a high-performance, streamlined architecture that combines transfer learning with lightweight channel recalibration, yielding superior RCC grading accuracy. Moreover, the framework can be deployed clinically in resource-constrained environments. The present research departs from the limitations of the deep learning models used for grading RCC. For instance, the models are computationally expensive and lack diagnostic accuracy and generalizability. The ResNet50V2-ECA architecture balances performance, efficiency, and clinical applicability to overcome these problems. Hence, the study’s goal is to create and test a deep learning model that is as accurate as possible in classifying a five-grade nucleolar scale of the International Society of Urologic Pathologists (ISUP) using a short, clear, and scalable model that can be integrated into the pathological field. Related studies

Deep learning has automated grading across multiple domains, including pulmonary fibrosis11, cervical cancer12–14, liver diseases15, brain tumor detection16, breast cancer17, eyecare foundation models18,19, and COVID-19 diagnosis20,21. Recent methodological advances such as InFeNet for kidney segmentation22, RaNet for prostate segmentation23, neuropathologist-level glioma classification24, IDH mutation prediction from WSIs25, and V-SAM for biopsy segmentation26 highlight the breadth of deep learning applications in renal pathology. These works highlight the broader trend of AI-assisted grading, motivating our focus on efficiency and reliability in renal pathology2,9,27–30. Scientists have devoted their efforts to the creation of efficient algorithms in terms of computation that can provide accurate results14,31; thus resulting in enhanced diagnostic precision and the possibility of their application in clinics with limited resources5,8,10,32.

Chanchal et al.4 developed RoCNN (a deep learning architecture for the automated grading and classification of kidney cancer tissue), which incorporated Inception modules with varying convolutional filter sizes and Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks to refine feature contributions selectively. Moreover, a hyper-column strategy (which concatenates features from different parts of the network) was used to improve the results further. On the KMC kidney dataset (five grades of renal clear cell carcinoma) and the TCGA dataset (four renal cell carcinoma subtypes), RoCNN was compared and scored an accuracy of 91.54% on KMC and 90.19% on TCGA; thus, showing the respective improvements of and over the state-of-the-art models. Furthermore, its computational efficiency was notable, utilizing parameters and FLOPs.

In the same way, Maqsood et al.9 proposed the efficient enhanced feature framework (EFF-Net), which combines 2D standard and efficient separable convolutional layers to achieve higher grading precision and computational efficiency. On the KMC dataset, EFF-Net obtained 91.90% accuracy, 91.4% precision, 91.8% recall, and a 91.9% F1 score. That great performance was emphasized by its small 0.0794 M parameter, 0.79 G FLOPs, 0.85 ms inference time, and 0.31 MB memory, which made up its compact structure.

Shifting the focus to the related field of architectural innovations, Chanchal et al.3 presented the renal cell carcinoma (RCC) grading network (RCCGNet), which used a shared channel residual (SCR) block to improve feature map learning. That network achieved 90.14% accuracy and 89.06% F1-score on a novel KMC kidney histopathology dataset, thereby exceeding other methods with trainable parameters and FLOPs.

Moreover, Chanchal et al.33 developed a groundbreaking convolutional neural network (CNN) for the classification and prediction of RCC grades. That architecture featured a simple attention (SA) block for global feature extraction and an atrous spatial pyramid pooling (ASPP) block for capturing multi-scale features. On the KMC kidney cancer dataset, it achieved an accuracy of 0.9123, a precision of 0.9152, a recall of 0.9112, and an F1-score of 0.9131, all the while being computationally efficient with 478, 309 parameters and 2.06 G FLOPs.

Besides, Chanchal et al.5 proposed RenalNet to classify RCC subtypes, which comprised a multiple-channel residual transformation (MCRT) CNN module and a group convolutional deep localization (GCDL) CNN module. RenalNet achieved 91.67% accuracy on a class-balanced TCGA kidney dataset, with good performance metrics of parameters and FLOPs.

Instead of single-task models, Chakravarthy and Rajaguru2 presented MobileDANet, a deep learning architecture capable of multi-target histopathology image classification comprising RCC, breast, and colorectal cancers, assisted by explainable AI. By combining a MobileNetV2 backbone with a dynamic attention (DA) block, MobileDANet achieved 90.71% accuracy and 90.94% F1 score on the KMC dataset, along with promising results on other cancer datasets. Besides offering visual interpretability via Grad-CAM, this approach, which employed 4.26 Million parameters and 0.72 Giga FLOPS, was also efficient in terms of its computational resources.

Besides that, the efficient deployment of these models locally on edge devices is a major research field. Lal et al.10 have engineered a deep learning automated system to detect and grade kidney cancer with a special emphasis on the efficient system deployment on FPGA (field-programmable gate array) boards. Models such as Inception-V3, ResNet-50, and DenseNet-169, after 8-bit quantization using the Vitis-AI platform, were run on an Ultra96-V2 FPGA board. Binary classification by Inception-V3 achieved the highest accuracy (0.90) and generalized classification (0.86) on the FPGA; however, the GPU counterparts achieved significantly lower inference times and higher rates (e.g., 23.194 FPS).

In addition to classification and grading, Hermsen et al.6 went beyond by segmenting multi-class of periodic acid-Schiff (PAS)-stained kidney tissue with a U-net architecture. The CNN trained on 40 whole-slide images (WSIs) achieved weighted mean Dice coefficients of 0.80 on the internal data and 0.84 on the external center data, with glomeruli exhibiting extremely high segmentation performance (0.95 and 0.94 Dice coefficients, respectively). Network-based measures were strongly correlated with visually scored Banff classification components, including a mean intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.94 for glomerular counting.

Research gaps

Collectively, prior studies make the case that kidney cancer analysis can be substantially automated through deep learning. Nevertheless, there are still some vital issues that have been left unaddressed: (1) Existing RCC grading models rely heavily on complex attention mechanisms or multi-scale feature fusion, leading to increased computational cost and limiting deployment in resource-constrained clinical environments. (2) Most of them are only tested on datasets from a single institution; hence, they lack external validation in different populations and staining protocols. (3) Only a handful of them provide uncertainty quantification in predictions, which is quite necessary for clinical application. (4) Patch-based methods are mainly used; thus, the global tissue architecture that is obtainable in whole-slide images (WSIs) is overlooked. (5) There is a shortage of standard, end-to-end pipelines that can integrate preprocessing, classification, and post-processing seamlessly into the clinical workflow. Together, these five limitations underscore the need for an RCC grading system that is not only computationally efficient but also highly accurate, well-calibrated, and clinically deployable. In response to these gaps, this study introduces a lightweight, reliability-aware ResNet50V2–ECA framework that delivers state-of-the-art performance while remaining practical for real-world clinical and edge-device environments.

Although deep learning has advanced RCC grading, key limitations remain, and these (together with the targeted solutions in this study) are summarized in the Gap-to-Contribution Map (see Table 1). Existing models are computationally intensive, struggle with intermediate-grade differentiation, lack systematic ablation of attention-mechanism mechanisms, and rarely assess reliability or risk-aware behavior. Benchmarking against state-of-the-art methods is also incomplete. Table 1 links each of these gaps directly to the contributions introduced in this work.

Materials

The dataset used in this research comprises 722 Hematoxylin & Eosin (H&E)-stained whole-slide images (WSIs) of kidney tissue, obtained from patients at Kasturba Medical College (KMC), Mangalore, India, between October 2020 and December 2022. Pathologists manually classified the slides into five categories according to the ISUP nucleolar grading system: Normal/Non-cancerous (Grade-0) and four cancer grades (Grade-1 to Grade-4).

Digitized images were acquired using an Olympus BX-50 system microscope with a relay lens (3.3× magnification) coupled to an Olympus DP-22 digital color camera (1/1.8”CCD, pixel size 3.69 × 3.69 μ m)3. Non-overlapping patches were extracted from tumor-containing regions, resized to 224 × 224 pixels, and normalized to zero mean and unit variance prior to model input. After data augmentation via horizontal and vertical flipping, the final training set comprised 3,442 patches distributed as follows: Grade-0 (n=693), Grade-1 (n=708), Grade-2 (n=648), Grade-3 (n=735), and Grade-4 (n=648).

To ensure patient independence and prevent data leakage, the train-test split was performed at the WSI level using stratified sampling: 20% of WSIs from each grade were allocated to a separate, unaugmented test set, preserving the original class balance across training and evaluation subsets. The resulting KMC kidney histopathology dataset has been established as a benchmark for automated renal cell carcinoma grading3. Methods

Automated RCC grading using the proposed framework (see Fig. 2) combines a deep learning structure that relies on the ResNet50V2 backbone with the addition of ECA modules, followed by global average pooling and dense classification layers. The approach features two separate training stages: frozen feature extraction and fine-tuning, ensuring the model maintains both stability and flexibility when dealing with the histopathological features of kidney tissue. Architecture design and feature extraction

At its core, the proposed system is the ResNet50V2 design. ResNet50V2 is made up of 50 residual blocks spread over five stages. Each stage has several bottleneck blocks, and each block normalizes the batch (BN), uses the ReLU function, and applies a shortcut connection to lessen the problem of vanishing gradients and make gradient flow easier in deep networks34,35. Training deeper architectures becomes possible with this formulation without the performance getting worse. Practically, this residual connection mitigates the vanishing gradient problem common in deep histopathology networks, ensuring stable feature propagation during the fine-tuning phase.

Unlike conventional approaches where ECA modules36,37 are added after each residual stage, we decided to locate a single ECA module just after the last convolutional layer of the ResNet50V2 backbone; specifically, the layer before the Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer38. In this way, the feature maps retain full spatial resolution and channel-wise richness, while the computational overhead remains quite low. Within these expressions, TP stands for True Positives (the situations where the model has correctly predicted positive instances). TN stands for True Negatives (the situations where the model has correctly predicted negative instances). FP stands for False Positives (the situations where the model has incorrectly predicted positive instances). FN stands for False Negatives (the situations where the model has incorrectly predicted negative instances). TPR stands for True Positive Rate (Recall). FPR stands for False Positive Rate (1 - Specificity).

These measures, when taken together, offer a comprehensive, multi-dimensional appraisal of the model’s effectiveness. While accuracy indicates overall correctness, precision and recall are indispensable for revealing the compromise between false positives and false negatives, a very important issue in healthcare scenarios, where giving a wrong diagnosis can lead to serious consequences. The F1-score serves as a convenient account of precision and recall, whereas BAC is a measure that can handle the problem of class imbalance by taking the average of sensitivity and specificity. AUC is used to confirm the model’s ability to distinguish between classes at different decision thresholds.

Statistical analysis

To rigorously assess the reproducibility and robustness of the proposed ResNet50V2-ECA framework, we conducted ten independent training and evaluation trials with distinct random initialisations. For each trial, we computed the full suite of performance metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, specificity, balanced accuracy, and AUC) on the held-out test set. Comprehensive descriptive and inferential statistical analyses were subsequently performed on the resulting metric distributions to quantify central tendency, dispersion, distributional properties, and uncertainty.

Descriptive statistics revealed consistently high performance with low inter-trial variability across all metrics, where the mean accuracy was 0.960 ± 0.004 (SD), with a coefficient of variation (CV) of 0.40%, indicating excellent reproducibility. Similarly, class-wise AUC values demonstrated exceptional stability: Grade-0 (0.989 ± 0.002), Grade-1 (0.977 ± 0.002), Grade-2 (0.930 ± 0.007), Grade-3 (0.861 ± 0.013), and Grade-4 (0.989 ± 0.002). The narrow interquartile ranges (IQR: 0.002–0.022) and tight 95% parametric confidence intervals (e.g., accuracy CI: [0.957, 0.962]) further corroborate the model’s reliability across stochastic runs.

Distributional assumptions were formally evaluated using multiple normality tests. Shapiro-Wilk statistics ranged from 0.884 to 0.985 (p> 0.05 for all metrics), while Anderson-Darling and Jarque-Bera tests likewise failed to reject the null hypothesis of normality (p> 0.05). Kolmogorov-Smirnov goodness-of-fit analyses confirmed that the normal distribution provided the best fit for all performance metrics (KS statistic: 0.098–0.192; p> 0.05), justifying the use of parametric inference procedures. Skewness values (-0.003 to 0.163) and excess kurtosis (-1.47 to -0.84) indicated approximately symmetric, mesokurtic distributions with negligible deviation from Gaussianity.

To validate interval estimates, we computed both parametric 95% confidence intervals and non-parametric bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples). The close agreement between these approaches (mean absolute difference < 0.001 for all metrics) reinforces the robustness of our uncertainty quantification. Furthermore, 95% prediction intervals remained narrow (width: 0.012–0.063), indicating that future independent evaluations of the model are expected to yield performance within a tightly bounded range.

Effect size analyses (Cohen’s d, Hedges’ g) yielded values near zero for within-model comparisons across trials, as anticipated for repeated measures of an identical architecture. Post-hoc power calculations confirmed adequate statistical power (\ge 0.95) to detect meaningful performance differences should they arise in comparative studies.

Multi-trial ROC and precision-recall curves with 95% confidence bands (see Figs. 10 and 11) further illustrate the model’s discriminative stability across grades, with Grade-0 and Grade-4 exhibiting near-perfect, low-variance performance (AUC ). That said, these statistical analyses demonstrate that the ResNet50V2-ECA framework delivers not only state-of-the-art diagnostic accuracy but also exceptional reproducibility and well-characterised uncertainty; critical attributes for clinical translation and regulatory consideration.

Ablation: single vs. multi-stage ECA placement

To validate our design choice of a single ECA block, we compared variants with ECA modules inserted after each of the five ResNet50V2 stages. As shown in Table 4, the multi-stage ECA configuration yields a marginal accuracy gain (+0.3%) but increases parameters by 4.2× (1.05M vs. 0.25M) and GFLOPs by 3.8× (11.8G vs. 3.1G). Given the negligible performance improvement relative to the substantial computational overhead, we adopt the single-ECA design as optimal for deployment-constrained clinical environments.

Besides that, the staged training method (frozen feature extraction followed by fine-tuning) used is compatible with both the efficiency of transfer learning and the adaptation to the domain, which makes the model ready for implementation in the clinic in the presence of limited resources.

Relevance of the study

This research is pivotal to the field of renal pathology as it addresses a pressing issue: the development of a highly accurate, rapid, and computationally efficient deep learning system for the automated grading of RCC, which can be deployed in clinical settings. Since the most common kidney cancer, clear cell renal cell carcinoma, makes up approximately 80–85% of all renal tumors1, and in the US alone, annually, there are about 80,000 new cases5. It is widely recognized that early and accurate tumor grading is a critical step for the determination of the medical prognosis and treatment plan8. Whereas, histopathological examination of the tissues is still the central diagnostic procedure, the manual review of histopathological images is a time-consuming, exhausting task prone to variability among pathologists6,9, hence it limits the throughput, aggravates waiting times for diagnosis, and finally poses threats to the patients’ health condition, especially in poorly resourced facilities where the availability of experts is very scarce.

Implementation of the ResNet50V2-ECA innovation proposed here addresses the issues raised above; it represents a highly accurate solution with strong potential for digital pathology integration, pending external multi-center validation. The low computational complexity of the system (0.25 M parameters, 3.1 G FLOPs) and the obtained near-perfect AUC and AP values for all ISUP grades reflect the high diagnostic reliability, while the system can run practically on any standard hardware, though clinical workflow integration requires further prospective studies., including edge devices, due to the implication of such devices. The model’s performance on the stressful KMC dataset that contains five different ISUP nucleolar grades also attests to the model’s ability to be generalized to real clinical settings. Integration into routine workflows involves scanning WSIs, extracting patches, and aggregating predictions via a digital pathology interface. Pathologists would review low-confidence patches flagged by the model, ensuring a hybrid AI-human pipeline that enhances throughput without compromising diagnostic safety.

This research is a stepping stone towards translational medicine, where it explores the potential of artificial intelligence to support but not replace human expertise in pathology. Faster and more consistent diagnosis is the major benefit that can be provided to patients, leading to better healthcare, reduced medical expenses, and telepathology as a solution to address the problem of remote areas. Furthermore, the modular design fusing a solid backbone (ResNet50V2) with the simple and effective attention (ECA) can be viewed as a scalable, adoptable framework for other histopathological grading tasks, e.g., for breast or colorectal cancer classification.

Limitations

Although the new ResNet50V2-ECA framework outperformed other models, it is still accompanied by several limitations to be considered. Initially, this model’s training and evaluation were solely based on the KMC kidney histopathology dataset, which consists of 722 H&E-stained whole-slide images from a single institution (Kasturba Medical College, Mangalore, India). This is a well-annotated dataset representative of the common RCC subtypes, but being geographically and institutionally specific might affect the model’s generalizability to other populations and staining protocols. The next step should involve the validation of the proposed model with different external datasets from varying locations and institutions to ascertain its stability over a wide range of histological changes.

Besides, the model scores high in accuracy and AUC in general, but its performance on intermediate grades (Grade-2 and Grade-3) is slightly inferior to that on extreme grades (Grade-0 and Grade-4), as the confusion between adjacent grades (e.g., Grade-2 misclassified as Grade-3 and vice versa) dominates the misclassification pattern. This issue pinpoints the intrinsic morphological resemblance of intermediate grades, and even a trained pathologist finds it difficult to deal with. In light of the complexity of this task, it is quite impressive that the model is capable of capturing subtle transitions; nonetheless, it is still questionable whether the model can advance this performance further to discriminate within this range by utilizing multi-instance learning or integration of higher-resolution features.

Moreover, while this study focuses on patch-level grading to maximize feature discriminability, we acknowledge that clinical diagnosis requires WSI-level decisions. The current patch extraction strategy utilizes non-overlapping 224×224 pixels with WSI-level splitting to prevent data leakage. In future deployments, patch predictions will be aggregated using confidence-weighted majority voting or Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) to derive a final WSI grade.The current study used the preprocessed KMC dataset provided by the original dataset owners (Chanchal et al., 2023), which was normalized to zero mean and unit variance. While additional preprocessing steps (staining normalization, artifact filtering) were not explicitly applied in our pipeline, we recognize their value for multi-institutional generalization. At last, it can be said that the model has a relatively smaller computational footprint than other models, but still, it needs GPU acceleration for real-time inference. Although this is practical in most hospital setups, mobile or embedded devices such as smartphones or portable microscopes cannot host such applications unless there is further optimization through quantization, pruning, or knowledge distillation.

Conclusions and future directions

The study describes a deep learning framework that is not only novel but also computationally efficient for the automated grading of RCC. The framework uses the ResNet50V2 backbone with a single ECA block introduced just before the GAP layer. The architecture in question reaches state-of-the-art performance on the KMC kidney histopathology dataset, achieving an overall accuracy of 96.90% and near-perfect AUC scores for all five ISUP nucleolar grades: Grade-0 (1.000), Grade-1 (0.998), Grade-2 (0.984), Grade-3 (0.981), and Grade-4 (0.997). The results serve as a testimony to the method’s diagnostic precision, especially for discriminating between non-malignant tissue (Grade-0) and the most aggressive cancer grade (Grade-4), which in turn, are the decisive factors for clinical management.

The model architecture not only focuses on the efficiency aspect but also on the performance one. The model after the last convolutional layer maintains full spatial and channel-wise feature richness while introducing only a very small computational overhead (0.25 M parameters, 3.1 G FLOPs) due to an inserted ECA block. The model’s design enables the diagnostic process to run on standard computing machines, including edge devices, without affecting its accuracy. Besides that, the staged training approach is efficient for transfer learning and domain-specific, as the model can recognize histopathological features even when they vary.

On the level of individual classes, the investigation of the model reveals that it is capable of maintaining high precision, recall, and F1-score for all the grades, with all metrics greater than 0.85. The misclassification limited to adjacent grades (for instance, Grade-2 misclassified as Grade-3) shows the morphological similarity between middle-level malignancy that is natural, and also the main reason for the difficulty in tasks of expert pathologists. The Precision-Recall Curves, thus confirming the model’s ability to keep high precision at high recall levels, are helpful in lessening the number of false positives that could trigger the occurrence of unwarranted interventions.
