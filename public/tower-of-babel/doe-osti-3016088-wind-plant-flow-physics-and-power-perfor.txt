 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 Cornell University will partner with NLR on the topic of wind farm wake effects to improve
 understanding of interactions between complex atmospheric flows, terrain, and wind turbine
 wakes and plant efficiency. Wind plant flow simulation tools will also be validated. The work
 performed will help improve wind farm modeling by analyzing data, applying models, designing
 and performing experiments to acquire additional wind farm data, and develop better models.

 CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

 • Assists laboratory in achieving programmatic scope

 • Enhances U.S. competitiveness by utilizing DOE developed intellectual property and/or

 Summary of Research Results:

 The combined effort of NLR and Cornell University researchers in this CRADA focused on lidar
 observations and analysis. Cornell’s contribution, specifically focused on design and layout (as
 lead contributors to the overall strategy) and the analysis and curation of specific lidar
 observations (ZephIR-300 and HALO Streamline XR data processing), served as a cornerstone
 of the American WAKE Experiment (AWAKEN) observation and analysis goals. This highly
 specialized role provided the high-frequency measurements of wind speed, Turbulent Kinetic
 Energy (TKE), and wake features, through which the wind farm-atmosphere interactions
 hypothesized in AWAKEN could be physically verified.

 Task 1: Analyze data from existing wind farms to identify wake behavior in complex
 terrain

 Task 1 Results: The precursor analysis utilized existing long-term observations from the DOE
 Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) Southern Great Plains (SGP) facility in northern
 Oklahoma (See Figure 1). This region was chosen because the presence of surrounding
 operational wind plants, which were found to have an appreciable impact on local atmospheric
 conditions. Examples include a documented 30% increase in Turbulence Kinetic Energy (TKE)
 under stable conditions (Bodini et al. 2021; Debnath et al. 2022). This preliminary work helped
 quantify the extent to which neighboring wind plants bias ARM SGP measurements.

 This foundational analysis reinforced the need for the AWAKEN Field Campaign, which Cornell
 University researchers were involved in conceptualizing and investigating. While the detailed
 long-term atmospheric analysis was performed by collaborators (e.g., Pacific Northwest National
 Laboratory (PNNL), NLR), the findings—such as the measurable impact of existing wind farms
 on atmospheric data—were crucial input for the overall scientific design and hypothesis
 formulation of AWAKEN, with Cornell contributing to this process.

 Task 2: Design of the AWAKEN large-scale field experiment

 Task 2 Results: Cornell University played a key role in the multi-institutional design of the
 American WAKE Experiment (AWAKEN). Cornell authors Rebecca Barthelmie and Sara C.
 Pryor were contributors to the preparation, conceptualization, investigation, and methodology
 detailed in the overview papers describing the final design and observational strategy (Moriarty
 et al. 2024). The final design centered on addressing seven prioritized testable hypotheses related
 to wind farm wakes, blockage, and wind farm control, with the field focused on the King Plains
 wind plant in Northern Oklahoma.

 The design outlined the specific deployment of instruments across 13 ground-based sites and on
 five operating wind turbines (see Figure 2). Cornell’s involvement in the design is consistent
 with their subsequent responsibility for conducting, providing, and analyzing critical lidar data
 from specific sites, such as the ZephIR-300 vertical profiling lidar at Site A1 and Site H and the
 HALO Streamline XR lidar at Site A1 and Site H. This strategic planning ensures the collected
 data addresses the scientific objectives defined by the collaboration.

 Figure 2: A detailed map illustrating the deployment layout of AWAKEN. The map shows the field
 sites (yellow stars) and the locations of the five instrumented turbines (yellow dots), representing
 the layout designed by the multi-institutional team, including NLR and Cornell University
                                               (Moriarty, 2024).

 Task 3: Present results at American Wind Energy Association (AWEA) and other meetings

 Task 3 Results: The presentation by Denbath et al. titled "Design of the American Wake
 Experiment (AWAKEN) Field Campaign" was delivered at the 2022 Torque conference and
 highlighted the process by which the experiment was designed, including the selection of the
 overall location, the various field sites and the array of instruments to be deployed to address the
 seven testable hypotheses of the project, including those operated by Cornell and NLR. Part of
 this presentation and paper included initial designs of lidar scans to be utilized in the AWAKEN
 project (shown in Figure 3) which were jointly designed as part of this CRADA.

 Figure 3. Nacelle mounted lidar scans for the AWAKEN project to observe both inflow and wakes
 from the King Plains wind farm (Debnath et al. 2022).

 Task 4: Conduct measurements of wind turbine wakes using multiple lidar and other
 remote sensing instruments during AWAKEN

 Task 4 Results: Cornell University was directly responsible for the deployment and data
 collection from specific lidar instruments. This included the 10-minute averaged vertical
 profiling lidar at Sites A1 and H (Barthelmie: 2024), identified as ZephIR-300, as well as the
 operation of the ground-based HALO Streamline XR lidar at Sites A1 and H (Barthelmie and
 Letizia: 2024, Barthelmie: 2024). The data collected by the HALO Streamline XR lidar at Site H
 and A1 included high-resolution arc and Velocity–Azimuth Display (VAD) scans, 6-beam scans,
 and vertical stares (one point every ~20 s). Additionally, Cornell personnel (R. Barthelmie) are
 authors on the dataset containing raw and processed measurements from the NLR HALO XR+
 scanning Doppler lidar deployed at Site A1 (Barthelmie: 2025). The data from these lidars—
 radial velocity, backscatter, and range-gate data—were essential inputs for the downstream
 analysis conducted by Cornell researchers.

 5

 Task 5: Perform simulations of AWAKEN site

 Task 5 Results: Simulations were not performed as part of this CRADA due to delays in
 obtaining wind turbine operating data that were to be compared to the simulations for the
 purposes of validation. The agreements for data sharing were not completed until after the
 completion of this CRADA and therefore no simulations of the site were executed.

 Task 6: Present results at AWEA/NAWEA (North American Wind Energy Association)
 and other meetings

 Task 6 Results: Because the simulations and comparisons to data were not completed,
 presentation of the simulation results was also not completed. However, the analysis of the data
 collected was presented at the American Geophysical Society annual conference (Barthelmie et
 al., 2024) and also in a joint seminar (Bodini et al. 2024).

 Task 7: Analyze experimental data collected at AWAKEN

 Task 7 Results: Cornell-affiliated researchers have analyzed the lidar data collected over an
 eight-month period, focusing on characterizing individual wake characteristics (centroid
 velocity, height, width), detecting wake merging and meandering, and quantifying the downwind
 distance at which these wakes remain clear, coherent features. This analysis directly addresses
 the highest-priority testable hypothesis regarding wind farm wakes. For example, as seen in
 Figure 4 the wake from an individual turbine rises as it propagates downstream under stable
 atmospheric conditions, while it remains in much the same vertical location for stable
 atmospheric conditions.

 Figure 4: Location above ground level of an individual turbine wake center measured at site H and

 Task 8: Validate simulation results using experimental data

 Task 8 Results: Due to delays in data sharing agreements and project reprioritizations,
 participants comparisons of wake observations were not performed under the CRADA but will
 be the focus of future work.

 Task 9: Present results at AWEA/NAWEA and other meetings

 Cornell and NLR University researchers, were co-authors of the presentation "Wake
 Characterization During AWAKEN" given at the 2024 American Geophysical Union annual
 meeting. This presentation focused on utilizing lidar measurements from the AWEKEN sites to
 quantify wake characteristics, including generation from individual turbines, wake merging,
 meandering, and downwind propagation. The presentation detailed the characterization of
 individual wakes (centroid velocity, height, and width) and determined the distance at which
 individual and merged wakes remain clear, coherent features. Crucially, the presentation
 included preliminary analysis of whether the implementation of active wake steering on selected
 turbines could be detected in the individual and merged wake characteristics or the momentum
 flux. The lidar data used for this characterization covered an eight-month measurement period.
