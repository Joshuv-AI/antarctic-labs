Analysis of ultrasound Doppler flow pattern recognition method for drilling gas intrusion monitoring
Authors: Qingfeng Guo, Xiao Cai, Lijun Geng, Minglei Men, Pengfei Li, Qing Liu, Guolang Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHigh-pressure gas intrusion during drilling operations poses significant well control challenges, heightening blowout risks. To address this critical safety concern, this paper proposes a real-time monitoring framework by characterizing gas–liquid flow pattern transitions through ultrasonic Doppler detection and time–frequency analysis. The proposed methodology systematically integrates three innovative processes: (1) gas-phase channel enumeration for spatial quantification of gas distribution patterns, (2) Doppler frequency shift range analysis enabling dynamic flow characteristic resolution, and (3) flow pattern threshold calibration to establish robust classification criteria. The thresholds of various flow patterns are obtained by the number of gas-phase channels and Doppler frequency shift range to achieve the purpose of real-time monitoring of gas intrusion status. To validate the methodology, an experimental gas intrusion simulation system was developed, comprising a wellbore pipeline integrated with a drilling fluid circulation pool and an ultrasound Doppler detection array. This system replicates downhole flow pattern transitions by injecting controlled gas velocities (0.1–5 m/s) at multiple axial positions, enabling spatial–temporal analysis of gas–liquid phase redistribution under simulated drilling conditions. The results show that, our method effectively explain the dynamic evolution of two-phase flow under different flow regimes and achieved early detection of phase transitions. This method provide a non-invasive ultrasound-based pipeline, offering a high-responsiveness solution for gas kick monitoring in high-risk drilling.Keywords: Wellbore pipeline, Time–frequency analysis, Air intrusion monitoring, Ultrasound Doppler, Flow pattern identificationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In deepwater drilling, well control events frequently escalate from gas kicks in high pore pressure formations, leading to substantial economic losses and casualties1. For example, 2010 Macondo well blowout on the Deepwater Horizon semisubmersible platform leaded to more than $480 billion in direct economic losses. Early monitoring of gas intrusion is crucial to increase well control time and prevent accidents such as blowouts.

Current gas kick detection methods mainly include conventional wellhead monitoring method, mud pool level method, downhole monitoring method with drilling, and seawater section monitoring method2,3. The conventional wellhead monitoring method mainly analyzes the standpipe pressure and flow-out rate of the wellhead, which is limited in deepwater drilling as well as high-pressure gas well drilling2. The mud pool level monitoring method tracks the volume and density change of drilling fluid, but the overflow is poor in real-time and the monitoring accuracy2. The downhole follow-drill monitoring method is to install monitoring sensors on the drilling column to carry out follow-drill monitoring, but this method is costly and also has strict requirements for the formation environment2. The seawater section monitoring method is a unique technology in offshore drilling with good real-time performance, but it is susceptible to the undulating motion of the platform leading to signal interference3.

Doppler ultrasound technology is widely used in medical imaging4,5, military monitoring, and meteorological observation6,7 and is beginning to be investigated in conjunction with intrusion monitoring. Yin et al.1 proposed a Doppler ultrasound gas intrusion monitoring method which employs continuous Doppler technology to quantitatively characterize the gas void fraction, thus enabling early monitoring of gas intrusion in deepwater drilling wells. Li et al.8 used the continuous Doppler technique to obtain the aberrations of Doppler signals to identify gas intrusion. Xia et al.9 analyzed the feasibility of early monitoring and designed a method for early monitoring of gas intrusion using the continuous Doppler principle. Abbagoni et al.10 processed ultrasound signals with Artificial Neural Networks to identify two-phase flow patterns. A large number of experimental studies on gas intrusion monitoring using Doppler technology has been carried out by previous authors11–14, which verified the feasibility and correctness of Doppler ultrasound technology for real-time monitoring of gas intrusion in drilling wells15. However, most of the Doppler ultrasound research used the continuous Doppler techniques, which do not have axial resolution and are unable to obtain fluid information at a given location. The intuitive ultrasound signal is irregular with the increase of gas void fraction, resulting in the inability to directly use the ultrasound characteristic parameters to judge the state of gas intrusion.

Pulsed Doppler ultrasound technology was used to identify the flow pattern changes during bubble rise in wellbore tubing16. Firstly, the simulation experiment platform verified the flow pattern changes in the upper, middle, and lower parts of wellbore pipeline under different gas void fraction17. Then experiments were carried out on the propagation of pulsed Doppler ultrasound in a two-phase gas–liquid flow. The orthogonal demodulation technology was used to process the echo signal. According to the amplitude of the acoustic reflection at the gas–liquid interface to separate the Gas–Liquid Two-Phase. Setting thresholds for the two-phase flow pattern of the number of measurement channels and Doppler shift range. The time–frequency spectrogram of the gas–liquid two-phase flow pattern is obtained by using the Short-term Fourier transform (STFT). According to the setting threshold, to obtain the time–frequency spectrograms of the upper, middle, and lower parts of the pipeline under different gas void fraction. The results verified the reasonableness and accuracy of the flow pattern identification in the early monitoring of gas intrusion. Most importantly, the equipment used in this method is simple, real-time, and independent of the undulating motion of the platform.

Supposed the bubble enters the measurement channel with a non-zero velocity component, there is a frequency difference between the ultrasound echo signal received by the transducer and the ultrasound emission signal. The frequency difference fD is the Doppler shift. When the scatterer and the ultrasound transducer move relative to each other, the wavelength is compressed, the frequency increases, and in this case the Doppler shift signal is positive. When they move opposite to each other, the wavelength is stretched, the frequency decreases, and in this case the Doppler shift signal is negative. The acoustic wave contacts different sizes and shapes of the scatterers, the gas–liquid interface for the acoustic reflection of different strengths, the received echo signals with an amplitude differences in the time domain, and different scatterers in the fluid move at different speeds. The ultrasound wavelength is compressed, the corresponding frequency increases and the bandwidth decreases when the transducer and the scatterer do the same direction movement. The ultrasound wavelength is elongated, the frequency decreases and the bandwidth increases while doing the reverse movement. In the gas–liquid two-phase flow system, the contact area and velocity distribution of the gas–liquid interface are different in different flow patterns. The ultrasound echo signals are also different between flow patterns, which can be identified by pulsed Doppler acoustic wave technique19. Construction of air intrusion simulation experiment platform

Experimental platform construction

In order to verify the feasibility of gas intrusion monitoring by the change of flow pattern in the pipeline, a simulation experiment platform for early gas intrusion monitoring was constructed according to the internal structure of the drilling wellbore and the ultrasound Doppler detection method as shown in Fig. 3. Figure 3a is the system flowchart and (b) is the physical platform. The system mainly contains the drilling fluid pool, the pool, the pump, the wellbore pipeline, the ultrasound transducers (1, 2, 3) (Model: 4 MHz), the ultrasound detector (Model: JPR10CN), ultrasound detector (Model: JPR-10CN), digitizer (Model: NI PXI-5114), computer, air compressor (Model: YOTS-2020), as well as gas and liquid flow meters. The drilling rod and the wall of the wellbore form the wellbore pipeline. The drilling fluid in the drilling fluid pool is injected into the wellbore pipeline through the water pump and then return to the drilling fluid pool through the upper part of the pipeline, simulating the working environment of the genuine drilling fluid; The gas encroachment simulation device consists of an air compressor, a pool of water, a gas (liquid) flowmeter, and a bubble crusher device; The air compressor pressurizes the gas, forming the pressure difference between the inside and outside of the wellbore to simulate the actual under-equilibrium conditions, the gas flow meter monitors the rate of gas injected into the air compressor, simulating the rate of gas erosion during an underbalanced condition.

Simulation experiment platform for early gas intrusion monitoring.

The wellbore is made of a Plexiglas tube with an inner diameter of 38 mm, a wall thickness of 2 mm, and a height of 950 mm. For the gas intrusion experiments, the liquid and gas are fed into the wellbore piping through the pump and the air pump, respectively, and the regulating valve control the flow rate of the incoming gas.

Gas–liquid phase separation

The ultrasound experiments were performed on the wellbore pipeline without gas intrusion compared with gas intrusion (2.5 L/min gas intrusion rate), and 1000 sets of time–frequency data were collected in each of the upper parts of the pipeline. The gas-phase distributions were plotted as shown in Fig. 4, where the horizontal coordinates represent the number of frames (time–frequency data sequence number), the vertical coordinates represent the number of measurement channels, and the legend represents the Doppler amplitude of the echo signals.

Gas phase distribution without gas intrusion and at 2.5 L/min gas intrusion rate.

Figure 4a illustrates the gas-phase distribution observed in a state of no gas intrusion. The entire graph surface is covered in blue, indicating that the pipeline contains only pure water, with no bubbles passing through the measurement channel during the statistical observation period. Figure 4b presents the gas-phase distribution obtained at a gas intrusion rate of 2.5 L/min. In this state, most of the figure is characterized by densely distributed blue, light blue, and green areas, with red regions appearing intermittently. This distribution indicates a gas–liquid two-phase flow. During the statistical observation period, the size of the bubbles affects the ultrasound waves interacting with the gas–liquid interface, resulting in variations in the Doppler amplitude of the reflected ultrasound signals.

According to the Doppler amplitude of the echo signals, the gas–liquid phases in the wellbore pipeline at the time of gas intrusion are separated, and the change of the gas–liquid two-phase flow pattern in the wellbore pipeline is identified through the phase separation development.

The data were averaged into ten groups to screen out the range of variation of the amplitude shift at amplitudes more significant than 2 mV (to avoid the effect of extreme clutter) to plot the Doppler shift ranges for the different flow types shown in Fig. 8. It can be seen that the Doppler shift variations are less than or equal to 750 Hz for bubble flow, greater than 750 Hz and less than 1500 Hz for segmented plug flow, and greater than 2000 Hz for mixed flow. Bubble flows have smaller bubble diameters and small velocities at the bubble boundaries, resulting in a tiny Doppler shift range. Segmental plug flow has large bubble diameters and higher velocities at the bubble boundary, so the Doppler shift range is higher in segmental plug flow than in bubble flow. The reason why the Doppler shift range of the mixing flow is significantly higher than that of the other two is that the large bubbles in the segmented plug flow are torn apart to form several small and medium-sized bubbles in the process of rising and moving irregularly, which results in the velocity at the boundary of the bubbles to be significantly larger than that of the bubble flow and the segmented plug flow. Summary and conclusions

In this paper, a time–frequency analysis technique of ultrasound Doppler is proposed to identify the change of gas–liquid two-phase flow pattern in the pipeline under different gas intrusion conditions. An experimental platform for early gas intrusion monitoring is constructed, which consists of a drilling fluid pool and wellbore pipeline, etc. The gas–liquid experiments are carried out at intervals of 0.5 L/min for the gas intrusion rate in the pipeline, and the gas–liquid experiments are carried out at intervals of 0–3 L/min by adjusting the intrusion rate of gas in the pipeline and combining with ultrasound Doppler technique. The results are as follows:

As gas intrusion occurs, ultrasound waves are reflected at the gas–liquid interface on the measurement axis, and the Doppler amplitude of the return signal changes significantly, achieving gas–liquid two-phase separation;

As gas intrusion occurs, ultrasound waves are reflected at the gas–liquid interface on the measurement axis, and the Doppler amplitude of the return signal changes significantly, achieving gas–liquid two-phase separation;

As the ventilation volume increases, the density of bubbles at the bottom of the pipeline increases, and the bubbles coalesce during the ascent process, evolving towards the trend of bubble flow-segment plug flow-mixed flow, thus occupying an increasing number of ultrasound measurement channels in the detection window;

With different degrees of aggregation of bubbles in the process of rising, the bubble velocity distribution in the measurement axis is significantly different. The greater the velocity difference, the greater the Doppler frequency difference in the echo signal, the more significant the Doppler frequency shift;

By setting the thresholds for different flow types, we can identify the changes in bubble, slug, and hybrid flows in the wellbore tubing under different gas intrusions;

Using this method, the gas–liquid phases in the wellbore tubing under different gas intrusion conditions can be separated, the changes in the flow pattern of the gas–liquid two-phase flow are identified and monitored in real-time, and this method is expected to increase the time of the control operation in the gas intrusion event while kicking. Meanwhile the use of a single transducer avoids the influence on the drilling fluid in the tubing, as well as the cavitation of bubbles in the course of the complex acoustic wave propagation process.
