‘New’ examples of skew fields not finitely generated as algebras Thanks: The authors thank the workshop Revisiting Fundamental Problems: Infinite Dimensional Division Algebras – Algebraicity and Finiteness at the Simons Laufer Mathematical Sciences Institute in December 2025, which motivated this work and where our collaboration on it began.
K. R. Goodearl and E. S. Letzter

Abstract
. An associative division algebra D D is said to be affine over a central subfield k k if D D is finitely generated as a k k -algebra. In 1956 Amitsur famously proved that, when k k is uncountable, D D cannot be k k -affine unless D D is algebraic over k k . In this paper we consider affineness – and nonaffineness – for certain naturally occurring classes of division algebras over arbitrary fields. The primary applications are to division algebras of fractions of suitably conditioned iterated skew polynomial rings over k k , including many examples naturally arising in Lie theoretic and quantum group settings. Many transcendental division algebras are thus verified to be nonaffine over k k . Division algebras of fractions of Weyl algebras and quantum affine spaces are determined to be affine over their centers exactly when they are finite dimensional over their centers.

Primary: 16K40, 16S15. Secondary: 16E60, 16P40.

1.
Introduction

For the general class of (associative) division algebras
D
over a central subfield
k
, the question of whether or not
D
can be both infinite
k
-dimensional and
k
-affine (i.e., finitely generated as a
k
-algebra) remains one of the oldest unsolved problems in noncommutative algebra. The seemingly more specialized (but still open) question of whether or not
D
can be infinite
k
-dimensional,
k
-affine, and also algebraic over
k
is the well known Kurosh Problem for Division Algebras, first appearing in 1941
[
12
]
. (The reader is referred, e.g., to
[
18
]
and
[
21
]
for brief historical overviews.)

Notice that there is no loss of generality in this discussion if we assume further that
D
is finitely generated as a skew field over
k
; that is, there is a finite subset
S\subseteq D
such that
D
is the smallest division
k
-subalgebra of itself containing
S
. (We will use the terminology
finitely generated skew field
to help distinguish our use of this concept from that of affine algebra.)

The most important fundamental result on affiness of division algebras is Amitsur’s famous proof
[
2
]
, for uncountable
k
, that if
D
is not algebraic over
k
then
D
cannot be
k
-affine.

Our aim in this paper, then, is to prove that, even when
k
is countable, the skew fields in various wide classes of noncommutative transcendental division algebras, finitely generated over
k
as skew fields, cannot be
k
-affine. To our knowledge, the analysis below is the first to provide verified examples of this type. These examples are not new as skew fields, but their nonaffineness is newly verified (hence explaining our use of ‘new’ in the title). We also address the question of when the skew fields in these classes can be affine over their centers.

To compare the above with the commutative case, recall that the weak Nullstellensatz (Zariski’s Lemma)
[
20
, Statement H
{}^{n}_{3}
]
ensures that a (commutative) field extension
K
of
k
is affine as a
k
-algebra if and only if
K
is finite dimensional over
k
, if and only if
K
is affine and algebraic over
k
. In particular, the rational function field
k(x)
, for any choice of field
k
, is not
k
-affine.

We note that questions of affineness over a base field versus affineness over a center are closely related, due to the following: If a
k
-division algebra
D
is affine over
k
and finite dimensional over
Z(D)
, then
D
is finite dimensional over
k
. Namely, under these conditions the Artin-Tate Lemma (e.g.,
[
14
, Lemma 13.9.10]
) implies that
Z(D)
is affine over
k
. The weak Nullstellensatz then ensures that
Z(D)
is finite dimensional over
k
, and therefore
D
is finite dimensional over
k
. Consequently, if
\mathcal{D}
is a class of division algebras for which it is known that (affine over the center
\implies
finite dimensional over the center), then the implication (affine over
k

\implies
finite dimensional over
k
) holds within
\mathcal{D}
.

1.1 Organization
.

Our study below is composed of three parts. In §2, we show that the quotient division ring of a hereditary noetherian domain cannot be affine if the domain has infinitely many distinct isomorphism classes of simple modules. In §3 we show that the theoretical results of §2 apply to suitably conditioned iterated Ore extensions (skew polynomial rings). In §4, the work of §3 is then applied to specific examples arising in Lie theory and quantum groups. In particular:

Theorem A
.

[Theorem
4.3
]

Let
D_{n}(k)
be the Weyl skew field
\Fract A_{n}(k)
for a field
k
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then
D_{n}(k)
is affine over its center if and only if
D_{n}(k)
is finite dimensional over its center, if and only if
\ch k>0
. However,
D_{n}(k)
is never affine over
k
.

Theorem B
.

[Theorems
4.2
,
4.7
]

Let
R={\mathcal{O}}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(k^{n})
be a quantum affine space over a field
k
. Then
\Fract R
is affine over its center if and only if
\Fract R
is finite dimensional over its center, if and only if all entries of
\boldsymbol{q}
are roots of unity. However,
\Fract R
is never affine over
k
.

Since the 1960s (e.g.,
[
7
]
), Weyl skew fields have played a significant role in the theory of enveloping algebras of finite dimensional Lie algebras. In particular, the well known Gel’fand-Kirillov conjecture hypothesized that if
\mathfrak{g}
is an algebraic finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field
k
of characteristic zero, then
\Fract U(\mathfrak{g})\cong D_{n}(K)
for some
n\geq 0
and some rational function field
K
(in finitely many variables) over
k
. Although not true in general, this conjecture has been proved for algebraic solvable Lie algebras over algebraically closed fields, for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
, and for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(k)
; see, e.g.,
[
1
]
for a survey. Weyl skew fields also appear in the theory of prime factor algebras of enveloping algebras; see for instance
[
14
, Chapter 14]
.

A parallel quantum Gel’fand-Kirillov Conjecture posits that for generic
k
-algebras
A
among quantized enveloping algebras and quantized coordinate rings,
\Fract A
is isomorphic to the skew field of fractions of a quantum affine space over a rational function field (in finitely many variables) over
k
. Moreover, when
A
is a generic quantized coordinate ring and
P
is a prime ideal of
A
,
\Fract A/P\cong\Fract{\mathcal{O}}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(K^{n})
for some
n
and some field extension
K
of
k
. These conjectures have been verified widely; see
[
4
, §II.10.4]
for an overview, and
[
5
, Section 6]
for skew fields associated with a broad class of iterated skew polynomial algebras covering many quantized coordinate rings and related algebras.

1.2 Notation and Conventions
.

Throughout,

2.
Localizing Hereditary Domains

We make use of the torsion-theoretic localizations of HNP (hereditary noetherian prime) rings developed in
[
8
]
.

Recall that if
R
is an HNP ring and
I
is an essential right ideal of
R
, then
R/I
has finite length (e.g.,
[
14
, Proposition 5.4.5]
). Given any family
X
of simple right
R
-modules, let
{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
denote the collection of those essential right ideals
I
of
R
such that
R/I
has no composition factors isomorphic to any of the modules in
X
, and set

R_{X}:=\{s\in Q(R)\mid sI\subseteq R\ \text{for some}\ I\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}\}.

We first collect the required tools.

2.1 Theorem
.

[
8
]

Let
R
be a non-artinian HNP ring and
{\mathcal{W}}
a set containing exactly one member of each isomorphism class of simple right
R
-modules.

(a)
Every ring
T
between
R
and
Q(R)
is HNP, with
Q(T)=Q(R)
.

(b)
The assignment
X\mapsto R_{X}
gives an order-reversing bijection between the set of subsets of
{\mathcal{W}}
and the set of rings between
R
and
Q(R)
.

(c)
For
X\subseteq{\mathcal{W}}
, the simple right
R_{X}
-modules are exactly those modules isomorphic to
A\otimes_{R}R_{X}
for
A\in X
.

Proof.

[
8
, Proposition 2 and Theorems 3, 5]

∎

2.2 Theorem
.

Let
R
be a hereditary noetherian domain which has infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple right modules. Then
Q(R)
cannot be generated as a ring by
R
together with finitely many elements of
Q(R)
. In particular,
Q(R)
is not affine as a
Z(R)
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
F\subseteq Q(R)
be finite and
T
the subring of
Q(R)
generated by
R\cup F
. Let
{\mathcal{W}}
be as in Theorem
2.1
, and let
X
be the collection of those modules in
{\mathcal{W}}
which do not appear as composition factors in any of the
R
-modules
(R+fR)/R
for
f\in F
. Then
F\subseteq R_{X}
and so
T\subseteq R_{X}
. Since
{\mathcal{W}}
is infinite (by hypothesis) and
F
is finite,
X
must be infinite. Choose a proper subset
Y\subset X
. By Theorem
2.1
,
R_{X}\subsetneq R_{Y}\subseteq Q(R)
, and therefore
T\neq Q(R)
.
∎

2.3 Corollary
.

Let
R
be a hereditary noetherian domain which is an algebra over a field
k
. If
R
has infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple right modules, then
Q(R)
is not affine as a
k
-algebra.
∎

We give two immediate applications.

2.4 Corollary
.

Let
D[z]
be a polynomial ring over a division ring
D
and
k:=Z(D)
. Then
D(z)
is not affine over
k
.

Proof.

First, recall that the center of
R:=D[z]
is
k[z]
and that every two-sided ideal of
R
is generated by an element of
Z(R)
; see, e.g.,
[
9
, Proposition 17.1]
. Next, let
f_{1},f_{2},\dots
be infinitely many pairwise nonassociate, irreducible polynomials in
k[z]
. We see that the ideals
Rf_{i}
must be pairwise distinct maximal ideals of
R
. Choose a simple right
(R/Rf_{i})
-module
A_{i}
for each
i
; then
A_{1},A_{2},\dots
are pairwise non-isomorphic simple right
R
-modules. It therefore follows from Corollary
2.3
that
D(z)=Q(R)
is not affine over
k
.
∎

A second application concerns the first Weyl skew field,
D_{1}(k)=\Fract A_{1}(k)
, over a field
k
. We deal with
D_{n}(k)
for
n>1
later (Theorem
4.3
).

2.5 Example
.

If
k
is a field then
D_{1}(k)
is not affine over
k
.

Proof.

Assume first that
k
has characteristic zero. Then the domain
A_{1}(k)
is HNP, and by
[
13
, Proposition 5.6]
,
A_{1}(k)
has infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple right modules. Thus the desired conclusion follows from Corollary
2.3
.

Now assume that
k
has characteristic
p>0
. Then
A_{1}(k)
is not HNP. Instead, view
D_{1}(k)=Q(R)
where
R:=k(x)[y;d/dx]
is a PID. Then
R
is a finite rank free module over the central subalgebra
S:=k(x^{p})[y^{p}]
. Choose an infinite sequence of pairwise nonassociate, irreducible polynomials
f_{1},f_{2},\dots
in
S
, and note that the
f_{i}
generate pairwise comaximal proper ideals of
R
. As in Corollary
2.4
, this leads to an infinite sequence of pairwise non-isomorphic simple right
R
-modules, and the desired conclusion again follows from Corollary
2.3
.
∎

The converse of Corollary
2.3
does not hold in general, as follows.

2.6 Example
.

Let
p
be a prime,
K
an algebraic closure of
k:=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and
\tau
the Frobenius automorphism of
K
. The
k
-algebra
R:=K[y^{\pm 1};\tau]
has only one isomorphism class of simple right modules, but
Q(R)
is not affine over
k
, which is its center.

Proof.

Cozzens
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
and Osofsky
[
15
, Proposition 4]
showed that
R
has only one isomorphism class of simple right modules. That
k=Z(Q(R))
follows from, e.g.,
[
9
, Exercise 6I]
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the collection of finite subfields of
K
, an upward directed family whose union is
K
. Each
F\in{\mathcal{F}}
is perfect, hence invariant under
\tau
, and so
R
contains
T_{F}:=F[y^{\pm 1};\tau|_{F}]
as a
k
-subalgebra. These subalgebras form an upward directed family whose union is
R
, and
(Q(T_{F}))_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}}
is an upward directed family whose union is
Q(R)
.

If
Q(R)
is affine over
k
, then
Q(R)=Q(T_{L})
for some
L\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Let
p^{n}:=\card(L)
; then
\tau^{p^{n}}|_{L}=\Id_{L}
and so
y^{p^{n}}
centralizes
L
. But then
y^{p^{n}}
is central in
Q(T_{L})
, contradicting the fact that
Z(Q(R))=k
.
∎

2.7 Remark
.

Again consider the algrebra
R:=K[y^{\pm 1};\tau]
from Example
2.6
, and choose any element
q\in Q(R)\setminus R
. It follows from Theorem
2.1
that
Q(R)
can be generated as a
k
-algebra by
R\cup\{q\}
. In particular, while
Q(R)
is not affine over a central subfield, it does hold that
Q(R)
can be generated as a ring by
q,y,y^{-1}
and the (noncentral) subfield
K
.

In contrast to the above non-affineness results, we present one case in which affineness does extend. Recall that an algebra
R
over a field
k
is
matrix algebraic
over
k
provided all of the matrix algebras
M_{n}(R)
are algebraic over
k
. The question posed by Jacobson in
[
10
, p.695]
of whether or not algebraicity implies matrix algebraicity is still open in general; Amitsur gave a positive solution over uncountable fields in
[
2
]
.

2.8 Proposition
.

Let
D[z]
be a polynomial ring over a division ring
D
. If
D
is affine and matrix-algebraic over its center
K
, then
D(z)
is affine over its center
K(z)
.

Proof.

Set
E:=K(z)\otimes_{K}D
, and note that
Z(E)=K(z)\otimes 1
. We know that
E
is a simple ring, since
K(z)
is simple and
D
is central simple over
K
, and so the natural map
\eta:E\rightarrow D(z)
must be injective. Therefore,
E
is a domain. Also,
E
and
\eta(E)
are clearly
K(z)
-affine. Next, since
D
is matrix-algebraic over
K
, it follows from
[
9
, Theorem 17.2]
that
E
is a division algebra. But
\eta(E)
is generated as a division algebra over
K
by
D
and
z
, and so
\eta(E)
must equal
D(z)
. It follows that
Z(D(z))=\eta(Z(E))=K(z)
and that
D(z)=\eta(E)
is affine over
K(z)
.
∎

2.9 Remark
.

The results in this section can also be compared with the following purely polynomial-identity-theoretic observation: If
D[z]
is a polynomial ring over a division ring

, then
D(z)
is finite over its center if and only if

is finite over its center.
(Proof: By Kaplansky’s Theorem, see, e.g.,
[

, a division algebra

is PI if and only if it is finite over its center. Also,

is PI if and only if

is PI, if and only if

3.
PIDs with infinitely many simple modules

In order to apply Theorem
2.2
and/or Corollary
2.3
to skew fields of the form
D(y;\tau,\delta)
, where
D
is a division algebra, we would like to have infinite families of simple modules over the PIDs
D[y;\tau,\delta]
. This can be obtained from information about eigenvalues of
\tau
or
\delta
, as follows.

3.1 Lemma
.

Let
R=D[y;\tau,\delta]
where
D
is a division algebra over a field
k
.

(a)
Let
a,b\in D
. Then
R/(y-a)R\cong R/(y-b)R
if and only if there is some nonzero
v\in D
such that
av-\tau(v)b=\delta(v)
.

(b)
Assume that
\delta=0
, and let
\alpha,\beta\in k^{\times}
. Then
R/(y-\alpha)R\cong R/(y-\beta)R
if and only if
\alpha\beta^{-1}
is an eigenvalue for
\tau
.

(c)
Assume that
\tau=\Id_{D}
, and let
\alpha,\beta\in k
. Then
R/(y-\alpha)R\cong R/(y-\beta)R
if and only if
\alpha-\beta
is an eigenvalue for
\delta
.

Proof.

For any
c\in D
, the right
R
-module
R/(y-c)R
is simple, being
1
-dimensional over
D
on the right.

(a) If
R/(y-a)R\cong R/(y-b)R
, there is a nonzero element
x\in R/(y-a)R
such that
x(y-b)=0
. We may write
x=u+(y-a)R
for some nonzero
u\in D
, and then
u(y-b)=(y-a)v
for some
v\in R
. Since
u(y-b)
has degree
1
,
v
lies in
D
. Comparing coefficients, we find that
u=\tau(v)
and
-ub=\delta(v)-av
, whence
av-\tau(v)b=\delta(v)
. The converse is obtained by reversing the above steps.

(b) In view of part (a), the isomorphism occurs if and only if there is some nonzero
v\in D
such that
\alpha v=\tau(v)\beta
, that is,
\tau(v)=\alpha\beta^{-1}v
.

(c) In this case, the isomorphism occurs if and only if there is some nonzero
v\in D
such that
\delta(v)=(\alpha-\beta)v
.
∎

3.2 Corollary
.

Let
R=D[y;\tau,\delta]
where
D
is a division algebra over a field
k
.

(a)
Assume that
\delta=0
, and let
E_{\tau}
be the subgroup of
k^{\times}
consisting of the
\tau
-eigenvalues of the
\tau
-eigenvectors in
D
. If
k^{\times}/E_{\tau}
is infinite, then
R
has infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple right modules.

(b)
Assume that
\tau=\Id_{D}
, and let
E_{\delta}
be the subgroup of
(k,+)
consisting of the
\delta
-eigenvalues of the
\delta
-eigenvectors in
D
. If
(k,+)/E_{\delta}
is infinite, then
R
has infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple right modules.

Proof.

(a) If
\alpha,\beta\in k^{\times}
lie in different cosets of
E_{\tau}
in
k^{\times}
, then by Lemma
3.1
(b), the simple right
R
-modules
R/(y-\alpha)R
and
R/(y-\beta)R
are not isomorphic.

(b) This follows similarly from Lemma
3.1
(c).
∎

3.6 Theorem

Let

be either

or

, where

is a division algebra over an infinite field

4.
Applications

4.1 Theorem
.

Let
D
be a division algebra over an infinite field
k
, and assume that
D\otimes_{k}D^{\op}
is noetherian.

(a)
Let
R=D[y;\delta]
. If
\ch k=0
and the derivation
\delta
is not inner, then
Q(R)
is not affine over its center.

(b)
Let
R=D[y;\tau]
. If no nonzero power of the automorphism
\tau
is inner, then
Q(R)
is not affine over its center.

Proof.

(a) Set
K:=Z(D)\cap\ker\delta
; then
K
is a field,
D
is a
K
-algebra and
\delta
is
K
-linear. Since
D\otimes_{K}D^{\op}
is a homomorphic image of
D\otimes_{k}D^{\op}
, the algebra
D\otimes_{K}D^{\op}
is noetherian. Thus, Theorem
3.6
implies that
Q(R)
is not affine over
K
. It remains to show that
K=Z(Q(R))
.

Under the given hypotheses,
R
is a simple ring (e.g.,
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
). If
z\in Z(Q(R))
, then
I:=\{r\in R\mid zr\in R\}
is a nonzero ideal of
R
, whence
I=R
and so
z\in R
. Thus,
Z(Q(R))=Z(R)
. In particular,
Z(R)
is a field. Since the invertible elements of
R
are the nonzero elements of
D
, we obtain
Z(R)\subseteq D
, from which it is clear that
Z(R)=K
.

(b) Let
K
be the fixed field
Z(D)^{\tau}
. As in part (a), Theorem
3.6
implies that
Q(R)
is not affine over
K
, so we just need to show that
Z(Q(R))=K
.

Since the skew-Laurent algebra
T:=D[y^{\pm 1};\tau]
is simple (e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 1.17]
), we find as above that
Z(Q(R))=Z(Q(T))=Z(T)
. The invertible elements of
T
have the form
t=uy^{n}
for
u\in D\setminus\{0\}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Such an element
t
commutes with
y
if and only if
\tau(u)=u
, and
t
commutes with the elements of
D
if and only if
\tau^{n}(d)=u^{-1}du
for all
d\in D
. Thus
Z(T)=K
, as desired.
∎

4.2 Theorem
.

Let
R=k[x_{1}][x_{2};\tau_{2},\delta_{2}]\cdots[x_{n};\tau_{n},\delta_{n}]
be an iterated skew polynomial algebra over an infinite field
k
, where
n\geq 1
and either
\delta_{n}=0
or
\tau_{n}=\Id
. Then
Q(R)
is not affine over
k
.

Proof.

View
R=S[x_{n};\tau_{n},\delta_{n}]
and
Q(R)=D(x_{n};\tau_{n},\delta_{n})
where

S:=k[x_{1}][x_{2};\tau_{2},\delta_{2}]\cdots[x_{n-1};\tau_{n-1},\delta_{n-1}]

and
D:=Q(S)
. Since
D\otimes_{k}D^{\op}
is noetherian (Remark
3.5
), the result follows from Theorem
3.6
.
∎

While Theorem
4.2
immediately implies that the skew field of fractions of any quantum affine space
{\mathcal{O}}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(k^{n})
is not affine over
k
, that does not address the question of affineness over centers. We deal with this shortly

4.3 Theorem
.

Let
k
be a field and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

(a)
If
\ch k=0
, then
D_{n}(k)
is not affine over its center, which is
k
.

(b)
If
\ch k>0
, then
D_{n}(k)
is finite over its center, but is not affine over
k
.

Proof.

(a) It is well known that
Z(D_{n}(k))=k
in characteristic zero (e.g.,
[
9
, Exercise 17O]
). The result thus follows from Theorem
4.2
.

(b) In this case
A_{n}(k)
is a finite rank free module over the central subalgebra
k[x_{1}^{p},y_{1}^{p},\dots,x_{n}^{p},y_{n}^{p}]
, where
p:=\ch k
, and the first conclusion follows. The second conclusion follows from Theorem
4.2
if
k
is infinite, but it also holds when
k
is finite, as follows. View
D_{n}(k)=Q(R)
where
R:=D_{n-1}(k)(x_{n})[y_{n};d/dx_{n}]
. Then
R
is a free module over the central subalgebra
S:=k[y_{n}^{p}]
. As in Example
2.5
,
R
has an infinite sequence of pairwise non-isomorphic simple right modules, and we obtain the conclusion from Corollary
2.3
.
∎

4.4 Theorem
.

Let
R
be
U(\mathfrak{g})[y;\tau]
or

\mathfrak{g}

Remark
3.5

4.5 Corollary
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}

. If
\mathfrak{g}

We now turn to quantum affine spaces and quantum tori. While the following lemma may be well known, we are unaware of a suitable reference.

4.6 Lemma
.

If
T={\mathcal{O}}_{\boldsymbol{q}}((k^{\times})^{n})
is a quantum torus over a field
k
, then
Z(Q(T))=Q(Z(T))
.

Proof.

Give
T
its natural
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading, and observe that
T
is graded-simple. Consequently, all ideals of
T
are centrally generated
[
4
, Proposition II.3.8]
.

Given
z\in Z(Q(T))
, the set
I:=\{t\in T\mid zt\in T\}
is a nonzero ideal of
T
. Choose a nonzero element
u\in I\cap Z(T)
. Then
zu\in Z(T)
and so
z=(zu)u^{-1}\in Q(Z(T))
. Therefore
Z(Q(T))\subseteq Q(Z(T))
. The reverse inclusion is clear.
∎

4.7 Theorem
.

Let
R={\mathcal{O}}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(k^{n})
be a quantum affine space over a field
k
, where
\boldsymbol{q}=(q_{ij})\in M_{n}(k^{\times})
.

(a)
If all
q_{ij}
are roots of unity, then
Q(R)
is finite over its center.

(b)
If at least one
q_{ij}
is not a root of unity, then
Q(R)
is not affine over its center.

Proof.

Present
R=k\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\mid x_{i}x_{j}=q_{ij}x_{j}x_{i}\;\forall\;i,j\in[1,n]\rangle
. Let
T:={\mathcal{O}}_{\boldsymbol{q}}((k^{\times})^{n})
; then
Q(R)=Q(T)
.
Set
\Gamma:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
x^{\gamma}:=x_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\gamma_{n}}
for
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\Gamma
; then
T=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}kx^{\gamma}
and
x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}\in k^{\times}x^{\alpha+\beta}
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma
. Now
Z(T)=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{Z}}kx^{\gamma}
where
\Gamma_{Z}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid x^{\gamma}\in Z(T)\}
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
.

(a) Assuming all
q_{ij}
are roots of unity, there is a positive integer
r
such that
q_{ij}^{r}=1
for all
i
,
j
, whence all
x_{i}^{r}
are central in
R
. Now
R
is module-finite over the central subalgebra
Z^{\prime}:=k\langle x_{1}^{r},\dots,x_{n}^{r}\rangle
, and so
Q(R)
is finite over the central subfield
Q(Z^{\prime})
. Therefore
Q(R)
is finite over its center.

(b) Assume that
q_{lm}
is not a root of unity for some
l
,
m
. In particular, this forces
k
to be infinite. Now
x_{l}^{r}
fails to commute with
x_{m}
for any nonzero integer
r
, and so if
e_{l}
is the
l
-th standard basis vector in
\Gamma
, the coset
e_{l}+\Gamma_{Z}
has infinite order in
\Gamma/\Gamma_{Z}
. Thus,
\Gamma/\Gamma_{Z}
is infinite.

Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated, it follows that
\Gamma
has a subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\supseteq\Gamma_{Z}
such that
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}
is infinite cyclic, say with a generator
\beta+\Gamma^{\prime}
. Now
S:=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}}kx^{\gamma}
is a subalgebra of
T
, isomorphic to a qiantum torus of rank
n-1
, and
T
is a free left
S
-module with basis
(x^{n\beta}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z})
. Set
y:=x^{\beta}
. The inner automorphism
y(-)y^{-1}
restricts to an automorphism
\tau
of
S
, and
T=S[y^{\pm 1};\tau]
. Thus
Q(R)=Q(T)=Q(S[y;\tau])=Q(E[y;\tau])
where
E:=Q(S)
. By construction,
Z(T)\subseteq S\subseteq E
, and consequently
Z(Q(R))\subseteq E
in view of Lemma
4.6
.

Since
E=Q(S^{\prime})
where
S^{\prime}
is the quantum affine space (over
k
) corresponding to
S
, the algebra
E\otimes_{k}E^{\op}
is noetherian (Remark
3.5
).

If some nonzero power of
\tau
is inner on
E
, then by
[
11
, Theorems 0,1]
, there exist a nonzero element
u\in E
and a positive integer
n
such that
\tau(u)=u
and
\tau^{n}=u^{-1}(-)u
. It follows that
uy^{n}\in Z(E[y^{\pm 1};\tau])\subseteq Z(Q(R))
, which is impossible because
Z(Q(R))\subseteq E
. Therefore no nonzero power of
\tau
is inner on
E
.

Theorem
4.1
now implies that
Q(R)
is not affine over its center.
∎

k

D

k

\tau

\tau

\delta
