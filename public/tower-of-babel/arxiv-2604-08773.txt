Neutral representations in dimension ≤ 3 \leq 3 and fields of moduli
Giulio Bresciani, Tianzhi Yang

Abstract
. A representation V V of an algebraic group G G induces a vector bundle [ V / G ] → B ​ G [V/G]\to BG . The representation V V of G G is neutral if, for every twisted form 𝒱 → 𝒢 \mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G} of [ V / G ] → B ​ G [V/G]\to BG over a field k k , we have 𝒢 ⁡ ( k ) ≠ ∅ \mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset . Twisted forms of representations arise in many ways, for instance as cohomology of families of varieties on residual gerbes of moduli spaces, and from quotient singularities. Moreover, every Tannakian category is the category of vector bundles on some gerbe. Because of this, studying neutral representations yields numerous applications, especially to problems about fields of moduli. The present article has three main results. First, we completely classify neutral, faithful representations of finite groups in dimension ≤ 3 \leq 3 . Second, we give a very general, computation-friendly result for proving that representations of finite abelian groups are neutral, in arbitrary dimensions. Third, we develop the abstract concept of the normalizer 𝒢 → 𝒩 → ℋ \mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{H} of a morphism of gerbes 𝒢 → ℋ \mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H} on an arbitrary site (twisted representations correspond to morphisms of gerbes 𝒢 → B ​ GL n \mathcal{G}\to B\mathrm{GL}_{n} ), and show that the normalizer 𝒩 \mathcal{N} only depends on the geometric type of 𝒢 → ℋ \mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H} .



1.
Introduction

Fix a perfect field
k_{0}
with algebraic closure
K
. Every field is implicitly assumed to be an extension of
k_{0}
.

Given a variety
X
over
K
, possibly with some additional structure
\xi
such as a marked point, a subvariety
Y\subset X
, a group structure on
X
, an endomorphism
X\to X
, and so forth. It is a natural question to ask on which subfields of
K
the pair
(X,\xi)
is defined. Clearly, the field generated by the coefficients of a fixed set of equations is sufficient, but perhaps we can find a better set of equations.

There is a natural candidate for a minimal field of definition, which is called the
field of moduli

k_{(X,\xi)}
, which is contained in every field of definition. It can be constructed either through Galois theory or moduli theory. The question then becomes: can we define
(X,\xi)
over its field of moduli
k_{(X,\xi)}
?

Among the first ones studying this question there are A. Weil
[
Wei56
]
, T. Matsusaka
[
Mat58
]
and G. Shimura
[
Shi59
]
, and over the years it has received considerable attention. Recently, the first author, in joint work with A. Vistoli, developed a new method for studying this problem based on the theory of
gerbes

[
BV24b
]
, which led to numerous new applications, such as the resolution of a conjecture by J. Doyle and J. Silverman
[
Bre24d
]
and the proof of the fact that a smooth, complex plane curve
C
of odd degree can be defined by a polynomial with real coefficients if and only if
C\simeq\bar{C}

[
Bre23b
]
(no elementary proof is known, and there are counterexamples in even degree).

Given
(X,\xi)
as above, if
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\xi)
is finite of order prime with
\operatorname{char}k_{0}
there is a gerbe
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}
over
k_{(X,\xi)}
which classifies twisted forms of
(X,\xi)

[
BV24b
]
. In particular,
(X,\xi)
is defined over
k(X,\xi)
if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}
is
neutral
, which means that
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}(k_{(X,\xi)})\neq\emptyset
.

Because of this, it becomes important to know how to prove that a gerbe is neutral. In joint work with A. Vistoli
[
BVY26
]
, we observed that the existence of certain vector bundles on a gerbe force it to be neutral. The main result of the present paper is the classification of vector bundles of rank
\leq 3
with this property: this automatically produces many new examples of varieties defined over the field of moduli.

1.1.
Twisted forms of representations

Given an algebraic group
G
over a field
k
with a representation
V
, the quotient stack
[V/G]
defines a vector bundle on the classifying stack
BG
. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{V}\to BG
is a vector bundle, the restriction
\mathcal{V}_{\tau}
to the tautological section
\tau:\operatorname{Spec}k\to BG
defines a representation of
G
, and the two constructions are inverses of each other.

A twist of the representation
V
is a twisted form of the morphism
[V/G]\to BG
, i.e. a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
whose base change to
\bar{k}
is isomorphic to
[V/G]\to BG
. Twisted forms of
BG
are gerbes, hence twisted representations are essentially vector bundles on gerbes.

A representation
V
of
G
is
neutral
if, for every twisted form
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
, we have
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
. Equivalently,
V
is neutral if every twist of
V
is a representation of a twist of
G
.

We are interested in studying which representations are neutral. Contrary to what one might expect, it turns out that the large majority of faithful representations are neutral, and non-neutral ones are the exception.

In
[
BVY26
]
, we introduced some techniques for studying this problem for very simple groups (finite and diagonalizable), with no restriction on the dimension of the representation. In the present paper, we go in an orthogonal direction: no restrictions on the group, but strict restrictions on the dimension. We fix a dimension
d\leq 3
, and study every faithful representation of every finite group in dimension
d
. This forced us to find techniques which are more refined than the ones in
[
BVY26
]
; nonetheless, the techniques work in arbitrary dimensions.

1.2.
Applications

Understanding which representations are neutral automatically leads to several applications. Here are some examples.

Example 1.1
.

Let
(X,\xi)
be as above, with
X
proper. Assume that the automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\xi)
of the pair
(X,\xi)
is finite of order prime with
\operatorname{char}k_{0}
. Let us show that, if the representation of
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\xi)
on
\operatorname{H}^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is neutral for some
p
, the pair
(X,\xi)
is defined over the field of moduli.

Twisted forms of the pair
(X,\xi)
are parametrized by a morphism
f:\mathcal{X}_{(X,\xi)}\to\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}
where
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}
is a gerbe defined over the field of moduli
k_{(X,\xi)}

[
BV24b
, Propositions 2.9, 3.10]
. The higher direct images
R^{p}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
define twisted forms on
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}
of
\operatorname{H}^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
as a representation of
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\xi)
. Since this is neutral, we get that
\mathcal{G}(k_{(X,\xi)})\neq\emptyset
.

For instance, in
[
BVY26
]
this idea is used to prove the following: if
C
is a smooth, proper algebraic curve with
|\operatorname{Aut}(C)|=p
prime, and
p
does not divide the difference between the genus of
C
and that of
C/\operatorname{Aut}(C)
, then
C
is defined over its field of moduli. This is done by proving that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a neutral representation of
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
(X,\xi)
be as above, and assume that a marked point
x\in X(\bar{k})
is part of the additional structure
\xi
, e.g. if
\xi
is a group structure we can choose
x
as the origin. In particular,
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\xi)
fixes
x
, and hence acts on its tangent space
T
. If the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\xi)
on
T
is neutral, the pair
(X,\xi)
is defined over the field of moduli.

In fact, since the marked point
x\in X(\bar{k})
is part of the additional structure, it induces a section
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}\to\mathcal{X}_{(X,\xi)}
of the universal family. The normal bundle
\mathcal{N}
of this section is a twisted form of
T
as a representation of
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\xi)
, hence
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}(k_{(X,\xi)})\neq\emptyset
if the representation is neutral.

Example 1.3
.

Let
S
be a variety with quotient singularities over
k
. Every rational point
s\in S(k)
defines a twisted form
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
of the natural representation of the local fundamental group of the singularity
[
BV24b
, §6.4]
.

If the representation is neutral, then
s
lifts to a rational point of a resolution of singularities
[
BV24b
, Proposition 6.5]
. This has again consequences for field of moduli problems
[
BV24b
, Theorem 5.4]
, and the Lang–Nishimura theorem holds for rational maps
S\dashrightarrow X
to a proper scheme
X
even though
s
is not smooth.

More generally, arithmetic aspects of gerbes and stacks have recently been under the spotlight
[
ESZB23
,
BL24
,
LS25b
,
LS25a
]
.

We also mention a Tannakian interpretation of twisted representations. Given an affine gerbe
\mathcal{G}
over
k
, the tensor category
\operatorname{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{G})
of vector bundles on
\mathcal{G}
is a Tannakian category over
k
. On the other hand, a Tannakian category
\mathcal{C}
defines an affine gerbe
\operatorname{Fib}(\mathcal{C})
of fibre functors. These constructions are inverses to each other
[
Del90
, 1.12]
, and neutral Tannakian categories correspond to neutral gerbes.

Because of this, understanding which representations are not neutral is equivalent to understanding the objects of non-neutral Tannakian categories. Given the widespread use of Tannakian categories, this is a very basic question which has not been addressed much.

1.3.
Arithmetic of quotient singularities

A point
s
of a variety
S
is a tame quotient singularity if, étale locally around
s
, the variety
S
is a quotient of a smooth variety by a finite group of order prime with
\operatorname{char}k_{0}
.

Following
[
BV24b
]
, a tame quotient singularity
(S,s)
is of type
\mathrm{R}
if and only if, for every twisted form
(S^{\prime},s^{\prime})
over a field
k
, the
k
-point
s^{\prime}
lifts to a resolution of singularities (these always exist for tame quotient singularities). The arithmetic of quotient singularities is the study of which quotient singularities are of type
\mathrm{R}
. Again, this concept has applications to the study of fields of moduli.

In dimension
2
, tame quotient singularities of type
\mathrm{R}
are completely classified
[
Bre24a
]
.

If
(S,s)
is a singularity with local fundamental group
G
and associated representation
V
, then
V
is neutral if and only if
(S,s)
is of type
\mathrm{R}

[
BVY26
, Theorem 3.2]
. One result of this paper is the classification of faithful, neutral representations in dimension
3
and characteristic
0
: as a corollary, we get the classification of tame quotient singularities of type
\mathrm{R}
in dimension
3
and characteristic
0
.

1.4.
Neutral representations in dimension
\leq 3

The first result of this paper is a complete classification of faithful, neutral representations of finite groups in dimension
\leq 3
in characteristic
0
.

Let us introduce some notation. Write
\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
for the diagonal
n\times n
matrix with eigenvalues
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
.

Given
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}_{>0}
,
\underline{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
vectors of integers with
n_{i}>0
and
n=n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}
, define
\mathrm{SL}_{\underline{n}}^{\underline{m}}
as the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
of block matrices, with blocks
M_{1},\dots,M_{s}
of dimensions
n_{1},\dots,n_{s}
, such that
\det M_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot M_{s}^{m_{s}}=1
.

In dimension
1
, it is relatively easy (though by no means obvious) to show that every faithful representation of a finite group is neutral, see Proposition
5.1
.

In dimension
2
, tame quotient singularities of type
\mathrm{R}
were classified in
[
Bre24a
]
. This means that faithful representations associated with singularities, i.e. those without pseudo-reflections, were already classified. We complete the analysis by classifying all faithful representations.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
be a finite group,
\bar{G}
its image in
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(K)
. If
\operatorname{char}k_{0}=2
, then
G
is neutral. If
\operatorname{char}k_{0}\neq 2
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

The representation of
G
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
is not neutral.

(2)

G
is conjugate to

\left<\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta_{2m}&0\\
0&\zeta_{2m}\\
\end{array}\right),~\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta_{2n}^{-1}&0\\
0&\zeta_{2n}\\
\end{array}\right)\right>

for some
m,n\geq 1
prime with
\operatorname{char}k_{0}
, which is the inverse image in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{(m)}
of

\left<\operatorname{diag}(1,\zeta_{n})\right>.

In dimension
3
, the result is completely new. We assume
\operatorname{char}k_{0}=0
in order to have a better grasp on the finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(K)
, but the classification can certainly be given in positive characteristic, too.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume
\operatorname{char}k_{0}=0
, and let
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{3}(K)
be a finite subgroup with image
\bar{G}\subseteq\mathrm{PGL}_{3}(K)
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

The representation of
G
on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
is not neutral.

(2)

G
is conjugate to one of the following groups.

•

Given a positive integer
c
, the inverse image in
\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c)}
of

\left<\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{a},1,1),\operatorname{diag}(1,\zeta_{a},1),\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{an},\zeta_{an}^{d},1)\right>\subseteq\mathrm{PGL}_{3}(K).

for some positive integers

with
d^{2}-d+1\cong 0\pmod{n}
.

•

Given integers
c_{1},c_{2}
with

, the inverse image in
\mathrm{SL}_{(2,1)}^{(c_{1},c_{2})}

\left<\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{2m},\zeta_{2m},1),\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{2n},\zeta_{2n}^{-1},1)\right>\subseteq\mathrm{PGL}_{3}(K)

•

Given a positive integer

\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c)}
of
H_{2}
.

•

If
\zeta_{3}\notin k_{0}
, given a positive integer

\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c)}
of
H_{3}
.

Using Examples
1.1
and
1.2
, Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
automatically give new examples of varieties defined over the field of moduli.

As can be seen from these theorems, non-neutral finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
are a very small fraction of all finite subgroups, at least in low dimension.

This means that, if
\mathcal{G}
is a non-neutral gerbe, there are strong constraints on which vector bundles can appear on
\mathcal{G}
. Equivalently, we get strong constraints on the objects of a non-neutral Tannakian category.

The classification of
2
-dimensional singularities of type
\mathrm{R}
was the key ingredient for studying fields of moduli of plane curves in
[
Bre23a
]
. An immediate consequence of Theorem
1.5
is the classification of
3
-dimensional singularities of type
\mathrm{R}
, which can be applied to study fields of moduli of curves and surfaces embedded in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, or more generally
3
-dimensional varieties.

1.5.
Diagonal subgroups with
1
-dimensional eigenspaces

When studying these problems, a counter-intuitive phenomenon emerges. The more complex a subgroup
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
, the easier it is to prove neutrality. For instance, notice that almost all non-neutral subgroups in dimension
n\leq 3
have abelian image in
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
, even though non-abelian finite subgroups of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
are abundant for
n=2,3
. The reason is that the complexity of the group can actually be leveraged to apply the methods introduced in
[
BV24b
]
.

Because of this, it is very important to know how to address “simpler” groups.

We have found a way to tackle finite, diagonal subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
with
1
-dimensional eigenspaces, different from the one given in
[
BVY26
]
(which was fine for most diagonal subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
, but not all of them). In particular, this new method turned out to be strong enough to address all such subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
for
n\leq 3
, but it gives results in arbitrary dimensions.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
be a finite, diagonal subgroup with
1
-dimensional eigenspaces. Let the normalizer be
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\rtimes S
, where
S\subseteq S_{n}
is a group of permutation matrices acting on the eigenspaces.

Consider the action of
S
on the character group
X(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/G)\subseteq X(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n})=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. If
X(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/G)
is a direct summand of a permutation
S
-module, then
G
is neutral.

We also have a more flexible version of this result, where the hypothesis is only required for a subgroup of
S
and a submodule of
X(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/G)
, see Theorem
4.27
.

1.6.
The normalizer of a morphism of gerbes

Finally, we have a new, general result about gerbes on sites, which is extremely useful for studying twisted representations and more generally twisted forms of “things”. Given that this part is very different in tone from the rest of the paper, it is contained in the appendix.

We say that two faithful morphisms of gerbes
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{H}
on a site
\mathcal{C}
with a terminal object
T
are locally equivalent if there exists a covering
\{U_{i}\to T\}
and isomorphisms
\mathcal{G}_{U_{i}}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{U_{i}}
making the obvious diagram
2
-commutative. There is a more general definition which does not require a terminal object, see Definition
A.1
. We may think of two locally equivalent morphisms as twisted forms of each other.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
f:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a faithful morphism of gerbes on a small site
\mathcal{C}
. There exists a gerbe
\mathcal{N}
and a factorization

\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathcal{N}\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathcal{H}

of
f
called the
normalizer
of
f
, having the following properties.

(1)

\phi
,
\psi
are faithful.

(2)

For every
S\in\mathcal{C}
and every object
s:S\to\mathcal{G}
, the group sheaf
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{N}}(\phi\circ s)
is the normalizer of
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}}(s)
in
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{H}}(\psi\circ\phi\circ s)
.

(3)

The normalizer is unique in the following sense. If
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{H}
is another factorization having properties
(1)
and
(2)
, there exists an isomorphism
\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
making the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}

2
-commutative.

(4)

If
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{H}
are locally equivalent, the normalizers of
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{H}
coincide. More precisely, if
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{H}
is the normalizer of
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
, there exists a faithful morphism
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{N}
such that the composition
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{H}
is the normalizer of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{H}
.

In the case of twisted representations, we let
\mathcal{C}
be the fppf site on
k_{0}
,
\mathcal{H}=B\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=BG
where
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is a linear subgroup. Then
\mathcal{G}\to B\mathrm{GL}_{n}
defines a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
which is a twisted form of the standard representation of
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
. The normalizer
BG\to\mathcal{N}\to B\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is
\mathcal{N}=BN
, where
N
is the normalizer of
G
in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

The theorem then implies that every twisted form
\mathcal{G}\to B\mathrm{GL}_{n}
of
BG\to B\mathrm{GL}_{n}
factorizes through
BN
. This is particularly helpful when
N
is small. For instance, if
N=G
, the theorem implies that every twisted form of
BG\to B\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is trivial. While
G=N
cannot happen if
G
is finite, we show that
N/G\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}
is sufficient (see Corollary
4.20
), and this happens for many finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

The result goes well beyond twisted representations, though. If
H
is an algebraic group,
G\subseteq H
is a subgroup with normalizer
N
and
N/G
is special, we get that there are no non-trivial twisted forms of
BG\to BH
. For instance, there are no non-trivial twisted forms
\mathcal{G}\to BS_{3}
of
BC_{2}\to BS_{3}
.

In a similar vein, one can study twisted projective representations of a group, i.e. twisted forms of
BG\to B\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
for a subgroup
G\subseteq\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
. This will give results about twisted forms of various kinds of objects we can define in a projective space, for instance hypersurfaces.

Finally, let us mention that our result is even more general than stated above, and it allows to construct gerbes corresponding to normalizers of arbitrary subsets of a group, see Theorem
A.11
and Example
A.15
. For instance, we can construct gerbes corresponding to centralizers, since a centralizer is the normalizer of a single element.

1.7.
Structure of the paper

In section
2
, we recall some previous results which are used throughout the paper. Most importantly, we recall the Lang–Nishimura theorem for stacks from
[
BV24a
]
.

Section
3
lays down the general theory of twisted representations, putting it in a more general context. Given an algebraic group
\Gamma
over
k_{0}
with a finite subgroup
G\subseteq\Gamma(K)
of the geometric points, we study twisted forms of
BG\to B\Gamma_{K}
, i.e. morphisms
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma

and
\Gamma=\mathrm{PGL}_{n}

In section
4
, we show that it is often possible to replace
\Gamma
with a smaller, more manageable group. Loosely speaking, a subgroup
\Pi\subset\Gamma
is called a gate for
G
if every twisted form of
BG\to B\Gamma_{K}
factorizes through
B\Pi
. It turns out that gates much smaller than
\Gamma
can often be found, thus making things considerably simpler. We also use this concept to prove Theorem
1.6
.

In section
5
, we classify finite, neutral subgroups of
\mathrm{PGL}_{1}
,
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
,
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
,
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
,
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}
. This is mostly a re-organization of previous works by the first author, though considerable additions are made for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}
.

In section
6
, we classify finite, neutral subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
.

In the appendix, we prove Theorem
1.7
, and more generally give a framework for constructing sub-gerbes of a given gerbe on a site.

3.
Neutral and
\rho
-neutral subgroups

Fix an affine group scheme
\Gamma
of finite type over
k_{0}
. The main examples are
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
or
\Gamma=\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
.

Now fix
j:G\hookrightarrow\Gamma(K)
a finite subgroup. Loosely speaking, we want to define a
j
-gerbe as a gerbe
\mathcal{G}
over an extension
k
of
k_{0}
such that the image in
\Gamma(\bar{k})
of the automorphism groups of geometric points of
\mathcal{G}
is conjugate to
G
, but we have a problem. In order to do this, we need to choose an embedding
K\subseteq\bar{k}
, and different embeddings lead to different
j
-gerbes.

The definition works if the
\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)
-conjugates of
G
are
\Gamma(K)
-conjugates of
G
as well. The following lemma shows that we can always reduce to this situation, without losing generality.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
j:G\hookrightarrow\Gamma(K)
be a finite subgroup, and let
k_{1}
be the field fixed by the elements
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
such that
\sigma^{*}G
is
\Gamma(K)
-conjugate to
G
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a gerbe over a field
k
with a morphism
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
. Let
p\in\mathcal{G}(\bar{k})
be a geometric point and
\alpha
an isomorphism between the image of
p
in
B\Gamma
and the tautological section. Assume that there is an embedding
K\subseteq\bar{k}
such that
\alpha\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}}(p)\alpha^{-1}
is
\Gamma(\bar{k})
-conjugate to
G\subseteq\Gamma(K)\subseteq\Gamma(\bar{k})
.

Then
k_{1}\subseteq k
.

Proof.

We have an action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{k}/k)=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k^{p})
on
K
through the embedding
K\subseteq\bar{k}
, where
k^{p}\subseteq\bar{k}
is the perfect closure. Since
k_{0}
is perfect and
k_{1}/k_{0}
is separable, it is enough to show that
k_{1}
is fixed by
\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{k}/k)
. Fix
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{k}/k)
, the Galois conjugate
\sigma^{*}G
is well defined.

Any two geometric points of
\mathcal{G}
are isomorphic, because
\mathcal{G}
is a gerbe. Fix an isomorphism
\beta:\sigma^{*}p\to p
in
\mathcal{G}
, by abuse of notation call
\beta
the induced isomorphism in
B\Gamma
as well. We have that
\alpha\beta\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}}(\sigma^{*}p)\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}=\alpha\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}}(p)\alpha^{-1}
is
\Gamma(\bar{k})
-conjugate to
G
by hypothesis. However,
\alpha\circ\beta
is an isomorphism from
\sigma^{*}p
to the tautological section, so the hypothesis also implies that
\alpha\beta\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{G}}(\sigma^{*}p)\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}
is
\Gamma(\bar{k})
-conjugate to
\sigma^{*}G
.

In particular,
G
and
\sigma^{*}G
are
\Gamma(\bar{k})
-conjugate. Let
Z\subseteq\Gamma_{K}
be the subvariety of points mapping
G
to
\sigma^{*}G
by conjugation. Since
Z(\bar{k})\neq\emptyset
, then
Z
is non-empty and we have
Z(K)\neq\emptyset
as well, i.e.
G
is
\Gamma(K)
-conjugate to
\sigma^{*}G
. Equivalently,
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)
maps to an element of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{1})
. Since this is true for every
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)
, we get that
k_{1}\subseteq k
.
∎

Hypothesis 3.2
.

From now on, we always make the blanket assumption that the
\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
-conjugates of
G\subseteq\Gamma(K)
are
\Gamma(K)
-conjugates of
G
as well. If this is not the case, we replace
k_{0}
with the subfield of
K
fixed by the elements
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)
such that
\sigma^{*}G
is
\Gamma(K)
-conjugate to
G
. Thanks to Lemma
3.1
, by doing so we do not lose any generality.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
j:G\hookrightarrow\Gamma(K)
be a finite subgroup.

A
j
-gerbe is the datum of a gerbe
\mathcal{G}
over a field
k
and a faithful morphism
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
such that the image in
\Gamma
of the automorphism group of one (and hence all) geometric point of
\mathcal{G}
is
\Gamma
-conjugate to
G
.

Two
j
-gerbes
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to B\Gamma
are equivalent if there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
making the obvious diagram
2
-commutative.

3.1.
Neutral subgroups

Definition 3.4
.

A finite subgroup
j:G\hookrightarrow\Gamma(K)
is
neutral
if every
j
-gerbe is neutral.

Being neutral is a property of the embedding
j
, and not of the abstract group
G
. Still, the embedding
j:G\hookrightarrow\Gamma(K)
is always fixed, so by abuse of terminology we will often say that
G
is neutral.

We are interested in classifying which finite subgroups of
\Gamma(K)
are neutral. For
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
,
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
,
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}
, this is essentially the content of
[
Bre24b
,
Bre24a
,
Bre23a
]
respectively, even if it might be not be readily clear from titles. The main focus of the present article is the next step, i.e. the case
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{3}
.

Given the increased complexity of the case
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{3}
, it is due time to establish a general theoretical framework for studying the problem. Constructing such a framework is the purpose of this section and of the next one.

Recall that an element
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
is a pseudo-reflection if it has finite order, and exactly one eigen-value different from
1
. The Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem states that
\mathbb{A}^{n}/G
is smooth if and only if
G
is generated by pseudo-reflections.

In the context of the arithmetic of quotient singularities, Definition
3.4
is useful because of the following.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
BVY26
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
be a finite subgroup. If the singularity
(\mathbb{A}^{n}/G,[0])
is of type
\mathrm{R}
, then
G
is neutral. If
G
has no pseudo-reflections, the converse holds.

3.2.
\rho
-neutral subgroups

Now assume that there is a faithful action

\rho:\Gamma\times X\to X

where
X
is a smooth algebraic space over
k_{0}
. If
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, we consider the standard action on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
, whereas for
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
we consider the action on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

There is a universal family

\mathcal{X}=[X/\Gamma]\to B\Gamma

of copies of
X
on
B\Gamma
, and for every
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
we have an induced family
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{G}
.

Remark 3.6
.

If
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, the datum of a
j
-gerbe is equivalent to a rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
where
\mathcal{G}
is a gerbe such that, for a geometric point
p
of
\mathcal{G}
, the representation of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(p)
on
\mathcal{V}_{p}
is equivalent to the representation
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
.

Similarly, if
\Gamma=\mathrm{PGL}_{n-1}
, the datum of a
j
-gerbe is equivalent to a dimension
n-1
projective bundle
\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{G}
where
\mathcal{G}
is a gerbe such that, for a geometric point
p
of
\mathcal{G}
, the action of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(p)
on
\mathcal{P}_{p}
is equivalent to the one of
G
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

The following strengthening of Definition
3.4
is extremely useful.

Definition 3.7
.

A finite subgroup
G\subseteq\Gamma(K)
is
\rho
-neutral
if, for every
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
with universal family
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{G}
, we have
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}(k)\neq\emptyset
.

Clearly,
\rho
-neutral implies neutral, but the converse might fail. For instance, the trivial subgroup of
\{\operatorname{Id}\}\subseteq\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(K)
is clearly neutral, but it is not
\rho
-neutral for
n\geq 2
since a non-trivial Brauer–Severi variety
P
over
k
defines a
\{\operatorname{Id}\}
-gerbe
\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
with
P(k)=\emptyset
.

If
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
X=\mathbb{A}^{n}
, the universal family is a vector bundle, and a subgroup is neutral if and only if it is
\rho
-neutral, because by Hilbert’s 90 theorem
\mathbb{A}^{n}
is the only vector bundle on
\operatorname{Spec}k
.

The stack
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
has finite inertia, hence it has a coarse moduli space
\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
, which is called the
compression

[
BV24b
, Definition 5.3]
. Since the action of
G
is faithful, the natural morphism
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
is birational.

If
G
has order prime with
\operatorname{char}k_{0}
,
\mathcal{G}
is tame. Since
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{G}
is representable, then
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
is tame as well. Hence, by the Lang–Nishimura theorem for stacks,
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
has a rational point if and only if
\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
has a rational point which lifts to a resolution of singularities, i.e. it is
liftable
in the sense of
[
BV24b
, Definition 6.6]
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
S
be a locally noetherian, integral scheme, and
s\in S
a point. We say that
s
is
liftable
if there exists a locally noetherian, integral scheme
S^{\prime}
with a dominant morphism
S^{\prime}\to S
such that
s
lifts to a regular point
k(s)
-point of
S^{\prime}
.

If
S
has a resolution of singularities
\tilde{S}\to S
, for instance if it has tame quotient singularities, by the Lang–Nishimura theorem

is liftable if and only if
s
lifts to a
k(s)
-rational point of
\tilde{S}
. Because of this, Definition
3.8
generalizes the one given in
[
BV24b
, Definition 6.6]
.

The following Lemma
3.9
generalizes methods about singularities from
[
BV24b
, §6.4]
to the context of representations.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
j:G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
be a finite group,
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
a
j
-gerbe. Write
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{G}
for the corresponding projective bundle, and
\mathbf{P}
for its coarse moduli space.

•

If
k
is finite,
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
,
\mathcal{V}(k)\neq\emptyset
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})(k)\neq\emptyset
,
\mathbf{P}(k)\neq\emptyset
.

•

If
k
is infinite, the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
.

(2)

\mathcal{V}(k)\neq\emptyset
.

(3)

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})(k)\neq\emptyset
.

(4)

\mathbf{P}
has a dense set of smooth rational points.

(5)

\mathbf{P}
has a liftable rational point.

Proof.

If
k
is finite, then
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
by
[
Bre24b
, Lemma 13]
,
\mathcal{V}(k)\neq\emptyset
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})(k)\neq\emptyset
because the fibers of
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{G}
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{n}
,
\mathbb{P}^{n}
respectively (recall that Brauer–Severi varieties over a finite field are trivial, because the Brauer group is trivial), and
\mathbf{P}(k)\neq\emptyset
because it is the coarse moduli space of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})
. Assume that
k
is infinite.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
follows by Hilbert’s 90 theorem, because a vector bundle on
\operatorname{Spec}k
is just a
k
-vector space.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
is obvious.

For
(3)\Rightarrow(4)
, notice that a rational point in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})
allows us to give an isomorphism
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})\simeq[\mathbb{P}^{n}/G^{\prime}]
where
G^{\prime}
is a twisted form of
G
over
k
. In particular,
\mathbf{P}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}/G^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}/\bar{G^{\prime}}
, where
\bar{G^{\prime}}\subset\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
is the image of
G^{\prime}
. Let
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
be an open subset where the action of
\bar{G}^{\prime}
is free. In particular,
U/\bar{G^{\prime}}
is regular, and

is dense because
k
is infinite. In order to conclude, it is enough to show that
U/\bar{G^{\prime}}
is smooth, and not only regular. This follows from the fact that
(U/\bar{G^{\prime}})_{\bar{k}}=U_{\bar{k}}/\bar{G^{\prime}}
is regular.

In general, passing to quotients by finite groups does not commute with base change in positive characteristic, but in our case it works because the action is free. There is a natural map
U_{\bar{k}}/\bar{G^{\prime}}\to(U/\bar{G^{\prime}})_{\bar{k}}
. Since the action of
\bar{G^{\prime}}
on
U
is free, both the maps
U_{\bar{k}}\to U_{\bar{k}}/\bar{G^{\prime}}
and

are finite étale of degree

, hence

is finite étale of degree
1
, i.e. an isomorphism.

(4)\Rightarrow(5)
is obvious.

(5)\Rightarrow(1)
Consider the blow-up
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{V}
along the
0
-section
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{V}
, and let
\mathbf{B}
be its coarse moduli space. Notice that we have a natural codimension
1

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, and hence an induced morphism
\mathbf{P}\to\mathbf{B}
which is generically a codimension
1

Since
\mathcal{B}\to\mathbf{B}
is birational, we have a rational map
\mathbf{B}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{B}
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is smooth,
\mathbf{B}
is normal, in particular
\mathbf{B}
is smooth at the generic point of
\mathbf{P}
. Furthermore, notice that the generic stabilizer of
\mathbb{P}(V)_{K}=[\mathbb{P}^{n-1}/G]
is

which has order prime with

, hence
\mathcal{B}
is tame at the generic point of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})\subset\mathbf{B}
.

By
[
BV24a
, Corollary 3.2]
, the rational map
\mathbf{B}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{B}
induces a rational map
\mathbf{P}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})
. By Theorem

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})(k)\neq\emptyset

\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
.

∎

4.
Gates

Let us give a definition.

Definition 4.1
.

A subgroup
\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
defined over
k_{0}
is a
gate
for
G
if
G\subseteq\Pi(K)
and, for every
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
over an extension
k/k_{0}
, there exists a factorization
\mathcal{G}\to B\Pi\to B\Gamma
where
\mathcal{G}\to B\Pi
is a
j^{\prime}
-gerbe with respect to the embedding
j^{\prime}:G\hookrightarrow\Pi(K)
.

If
\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
is a gate for
G
, in order to understand whether
G\subseteq\Gamma(K)
is neutral or not we can effectively replace
\Gamma
with
\Pi
. If
\Pi
is much smaller than
\Gamma
, this will make our task much easier.

Of course, the usefulness of gates depends on whether small gates exist often enough. As we shall see, this actually happens.

Notice that two subgroups of
\Pi(K)
which are conjugate in
\Gamma(K)
are not guaranteed to be conjugate in
\Pi(K)
as well, so the existence of the factorization
\mathcal{G}\to B\Pi\to B\Gamma
is not sufficient. We need to require that
\mathcal{G}\to B\Pi
is a
j^{\prime}
-gerbe with respect to
j^{\prime}:G\hookrightarrow\Pi(K)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
be a subgroup scheme with
G\subseteq\Pi(K)
. Assume that the normalizer
N\subseteq\Gamma(K)
of
G
in
\Gamma
maps surjectively on the set of left cosets
\Gamma/\Pi(K)
.

For every algebraically closed field
\Omega\supseteq K
, if
G^{\prime}\subseteq\Pi(\Omega)
is
\Gamma(\Omega)
-conjugate to
G
, then it is
\Pi(\Omega)
-conjugate to
G
as well.

Proof.

Up to extending
k_{0}
, we may assume
k_{0}=K
is algebraically closed. We may then regard
G,N
as the geometric points of group schemes
\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{N}
of finite type over
K
. The fact that
\mathfrak{N}(K)\to\Gamma/\Pi(K)
is surjective then implies that
\mathfrak{N}(\Omega)\to\Gamma/\Pi(\Omega)
is surjective as well.

Let
x\in\Gamma(K)
be any element, consider the conjugate
xGx^{-1}
. Choose
y
any element of
\mathfrak{N}(\Omega)
such that
y^{-1}\Pi(K)=x^{-1}\Pi(K)
. Then
xGx^{-1}=xy^{-1}Gyx^{-1}
, and
xy^{-1}\in\Pi(K)
because
xy^{-1}\Pi(K)=xx^{-1}\Pi(K)=\Pi(K)
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
be a subgroup scheme with
G\subseteq\Pi(K)
. Assume that the normalizer
N\subseteq\Gamma(K)
of
G
in
\Gamma
maps surjectively on the left cosets
\Gamma/\Pi(K)
.

If every
j
-gerbe factorizes through
B\Pi
, then
\Pi
is a gate for
G
as well.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
be a normal subgroup scheme with
\Gamma/\Pi
special and
G\subseteq\Pi(K)
. Assume that the normalizer of
G
maps surjectively on
\Gamma/\Pi(K)
. Then
\Pi
is a gate for
G
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.3
, we only need to check that every
j
-gerbe factorizes through
B\Pi
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
be a
j
-gerbe. We have a cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\Pi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\Gamma/\Pi}

where
\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\Gamma/\Pi
is the tautological section.

By the following Lemma
4.5
, we have a factorization
\mathcal{G}\to\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\Gamma/\Pi
. Since
\Gamma/\Pi
is special, the new section
\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\Gamma/\Pi
coincides with the tautological one, hence we get the desired morphism
\mathcal{G}\to B\Pi
by the diagram above.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Gamma\to\Delta
be a morphism of affine gerbes over a field
k
. Fix
\gamma\in\Gamma(k^{\prime})
for some extension
k^{\prime}/k
. If
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(\gamma)\to\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\Delta}(\gamma)
is trivial, there exists a factorization
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Spec}k\to\Delta
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\to\Delta^{\prime}\to\Delta
be the canonical factorization. Since
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(\gamma)\to\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\Delta}(\gamma)
is trivial, then
\Delta^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}
, which in turn implies
\Delta^{\prime}=\operatorname{Spec}k
.
∎

Given a positive integer
c
, write
\mathrm{SL}_{n}^{(c)}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for the subgroup of matrices
M
such that
(\det M)^{c}=1
.

Corollary 4.6
.

If
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
G\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{n}^{(c)}(K)
, then
\mathrm{SL}_{n}^{(c)}
is a gate for
G
.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
4.4
with
\Pi=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
\Pi^{\prime}=\mathrm{SL}_{n}^{(c)}
. Notice that
\mathrm{GL}_{n}/\mathrm{SL}_{n}^{(c)}\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}
is special. The multiples of the identity are in the normalizer of

, and map surjectively on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}/\mathrm{SL}_{n}^{(c)}(K)
.
∎

4.1.
Saturated subgroups

Fix a quotient
\pi:\Gamma\to\bar{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
. The main example is
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\bar{\Gamma}=\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
. If
G\subseteq\Gamma(K)
is a finite subgroup, denote by
\bar{G}=\pi(G)
its image in
\bar{\Gamma}
.

Given a
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
, taking the canonical factorization of
\mathcal{G}\to B\bar{\Gamma}
we get a
2
-commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{\mathcal{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\bar{\Gamma}}

where
\mathcal{G}\to\bar{\mathcal{G}}
is locally full and
\bar{\mathcal{G}}\to B\bar{\Gamma}
is faithful. In particular,
\bar{\mathcal{G}}\to B\bar{\Gamma}
is a
\bar{j}
-gerbe. Furthermore, the factorization
\mathcal{G}\to\bar{\mathcal{G}}\to B\bar{\Gamma}
is unique up to equivalence.

We say that
\bar{\mathcal{G}}
is the
induced

\bar{j}
-gerbe.

Definition 4.7
.

The group
G
is
saturated
if there exists a subgroup
\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
such that
\bar{\Pi}=\pi(\Pi)
is a gate for
\bar{G}
and

\pi^{-1}(\bar{G})\cap\Pi(K)=G.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
G
is saturated, every
\bar{j}
-gerbe has the form
\bar{\mathcal{G}}
for some
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let
\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
be as in Definition
4.7
. Up to replacing
\Gamma
with
\Pi
, we may assume that
\pi^{-1}(\bar{G})=G
.

Given a
\bar{j}
-gerbe
\mathcal{H}\to B\bar{\Gamma}
, define
\mathcal{G}
by the
2
-cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\bar{\Gamma}}

Since
\pi:\Gamma\to\bar{\Gamma}
is surjective, then
\mathcal{G}
is a
\pi^{-1}(\bar{G})
-gerbe, and hence a
j
-gerbe since
\pi^{-1}(\bar{G})=G
. The uniqueness part of Proposition
2.6
implies that
\mathcal{H}\to B\bar{\Gamma}
is equivalent to
\bar{\mathcal{G}}\to B\bar{\Gamma}
.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

If
G\subseteq\Gamma(K)
is neutral and saturated,
\bar{G}\subseteq\bar{\Gamma}(K)
is neutral as well.

As a consequence, we obtain the following strengthening of Proposition
3.11
for saturated subgroups.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
be a finite subgroup with image
\bar{G}\subseteq\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(K)
, assume that
G
is saturated. Then
G
is neutral if and only if
\bar{G}
is
\rho
-neutral.

Proof.

The “if” part is Proposition
3.11
. Assume that
G
is neutral, let us prove that
\bar{G}
is
\rho
-neutral. By Lemma
4.8
, every
\bar{j}
-gerbe has the form
\bar{\mathcal{G}}
for some
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}
. Let
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
be the corresponding vector bundle, and
\mathcal{P}\to\bar{\mathcal{G}}
the projective bundle, there is a natural map
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{P}
. Since
G
is neutral,
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
and hence
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})\neq\emptyset
by Lemma
3.9
. We conclude that
\mathcal{P}(k)\neq\emptyset
, too.
∎

4.2.
Normal subgroups

Recall that we are assuming Hypothesis
3.2
, i.e. that the
\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
-conjugates of
G
are
\Gamma(K)
-conjugates of
G
as well. Studying
j
-gerbes becomes easier if we assume more strongly that
\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
maps
G
to itself.

Hypothesis 4.11
.

The subgroup
G\subseteq\Gamma(K)
is
\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
-invariant. Equivalently, there is a finite étale group scheme
\mathfrak{G}
over
k_{0}
with an embedding
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Gamma
such that
G=\mathfrak{G}(K)\subseteq\Gamma(K)
.

Lemma 4.12
.

If
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
G
is diagonal, then Hypothesis
4.11
is satisfied.

As a consequence, if
G
is abelian of order prime with
\operatorname{char}k_{0}
a conjugate of
G
satisfies Hypothesis
4.11
.

Proof.

If
G
is diagonal, there exists an integer
m
such that
G
is generated by elements of the form
g=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{m}^{a_{1}},\dots,\zeta_{m}^{a_{n}})
. Choose
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)
, then
\sigma(\zeta_{m})=\zeta_{m}^{d}
for some
d
prime with
m
, and
\sigma(g)=g^{d}
. Since
\gcd(d,m)=1
, then
g
and
g^{d}
generate the same group, and hence
\sigma(G)=G
.
∎

For the rest of §
4.2
, we assume Hypothesis
4.11
, but not in the rest of the paper.

If
\mathfrak{G}
is normal in
\Gamma
, then our problem can be studied with cohomological methods. Obviously,
\mathfrak{G}
is not normal most of the time. However, it turns out that the normalizer of
\mathfrak{G}
is a gate.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Gamma
be a finite subgroup scheme. The normalizer
\mathfrak{N}
is a gate for
j:G=\mathfrak{G}(K)\hookrightarrow\Gamma
.

Proof.

Every
j
-gerbe is locally equivalent to
B\mathfrak{G}\to B\Gamma
, see Definition
A.1
and Lemma
A.2
. The statement then follows from Theorem
A.12
.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

Thanks to Proposition
4.13
, we can always reduce to the case in which
\mathfrak{G}
is normal in
\Gamma
. In this case, the hypothesis of Corollary
4.3
is automatically satisfied, so that finding gates is easier.

Let
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
be a subgroup normalizing
\mathfrak{G}
, write
\mathfrak{Q}=\Pi/\mathfrak{G}
. Recall that the isomorphism classes of
B\mathfrak{Q}(k)
are in natural bijection with the elements of
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{Q})
, i.e.
\mathfrak{Q}
-torsors. Given a section
\tau:\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\mathfrak{Q}
, the
2
-cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\tau}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\Pi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\mathfrak{Q}}

defines a
j
-gerbe with the composition
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}\to B\Pi\to B\Gamma
, and
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}(k)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\tau
lifts to
B\Pi
. As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
be a subgroup normalizing
\mathfrak{G}
, write
\mathfrak{Q}=\Pi/\mathfrak{G}
. If

\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Pi)\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{Q})

is not surjective for some extension
k
of
k_{0}
, then
G
is not neutral.

Lemma 4.16
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{G}
is normal in
\Gamma
. For every extension
k/k_{0}
, the mapping
\tau\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\tau}
defines a bijection between
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Gamma/\mathfrak{G})
and
j
-gerbes up to equivalence.

Neutral
j
-gerbes correspond to elements of
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Gamma/\mathfrak{G})
lifting to
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Write
\mathfrak{Q}=\Gamma/\mathfrak{G}
. Let us construct a map from equivalence classes of
G
gerbes to
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{Q})
. Let
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
be a
j
-gerbe over
k
, with
k
an extension of
k_{0}
.

By Lemma
4.5
, we get a factorization
\mathcal{G}\to\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\mathfrak{Q}
of
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma\to B\mathfrak{Q}
. The section
\tau:\operatorname{Spec}k\to B\mathfrak{Q}
is the desired element of
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{Q})
. By definition of
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}
, we have an induced morphism
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}_{\tau}
which becomes an isomorphism after base change to
\bar{k}
. It follows that
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}_{\tau}
is an isomorphism as well.

On the other hand, let
\tau\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{Q})
be a cohomology class,
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}\to B\Gamma
the associated
j
-gerbe, and
\tau^{\prime}\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{Q})
the cohomology class of
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}
. The two compositions
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}\to\operatorname{Spec}k\xrightarrow{\tau}\to B\mathfrak{Q}
,
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}\to\operatorname{Spec}k\xrightarrow{\tau^{\prime}}\to B\mathfrak{Q}
both coincide with
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}\to B\Pi\to B\mathfrak{Q}
, hence
\tau=\tau^{\prime}
by the uniqueness part of Proposition
2.6
.

The second statement is obvious.
∎

As a direct consequence, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Pi\subseteq\Gamma
be subgroups, with
\mathfrak{G}
normal in
\Gamma
. The following are equivalent.

•

\Pi
is a gate for
\mathfrak{G}
.

•

Every
j
-gerbe has the form
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Pi/\mathfrak{G})
.

•

The map

\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Pi/\mathfrak{G})\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Gamma/\mathfrak{G})

is surjective for every extension
k/k_{0}
.

•

The map

\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Pi/\mathfrak{G})\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Gamma/\mathfrak{G})

is bijective for every extension
k/k_{0}
.

In this case, neutral
j
-gerbes correspond to elements of
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Pi/\mathfrak{G})
lifting to
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Pi)
.

The following theorem is a direct consequence of Proposition
4.17
.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
\Pi
be a gate for
\mathfrak{G}
, with
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Pi
normal, and write
\mathfrak{Q}=\Pi/\mathfrak{G}
. The subgroup
G=\mathfrak{G}(K)\subseteq\Gamma(K)
is neutral if and only if

\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\Pi)\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{Q})

is surjective for every extension
k
of
k_{0}
.

As a direct consequence of Theorem
4.18
and Proposition
4.13
, we obtain the following variant of
[

, Theorem 4]
.

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\Gamma
be the normalizer of
\mathfrak{G}
, and
\mathfrak{Q}=\mathfrak{N}/\mathfrak{G}
.

The subgroup
G=\mathfrak{G}(K)\subseteq\Gamma(K)
is neutral if and only if

\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{N})\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathfrak{Q})

is surjective for every extension
k
of
k_{0}
.

Corollary 4.20
.

If
\mathfrak{N}/\mathfrak{G}
is special, then
G
is neutral.

4.3.
Distinguished subvarieties

To prove that
G
is
\rho
-neutral, we have to find rational points on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
for every
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
. More generally, it is useful to know ways to construct substacks of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Methods for doing this were given in
[
Bre24c
, §7]
. In this section, we generalize these methods and translate them to our setting.

Definition 4.21
.

The
extended normalizer

\tilde{N}_{G}
of
G
is its normalizer in the semi-direct product
\Gamma(K)\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
.

Notice that, thanks to Hypothesis
3.2
, the homomorphism
\tilde{N}_{G}\to\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
is surjective. In particular,
\tilde{N}_{G}
is an extension of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
by the normalizer
N_{G}
of
G
in
\Gamma(K)
.

Example 4.22
.

If
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, then
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
is the group of bijective maps
f:K^{n}\to K^{n}
such that there exists
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
with

•

f(v+w)=f(v)+f(w)
,

•

f(\lambda v)=\sigma(\lambda)f(v)
.

We call these
semi-linear
automorphisms of
K^{n}
.

Remark 4.23
.

If the standard Galois action on
\Gamma(K)
maps
G
to itself, we have
\tilde{N}_{G}=N_{G}\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})
.

As in §
3.2
, assume we have an action
\rho:\Gamma\times X\to X
defined over
k_{0}
. The action
\rho
induces an extended action

\tilde{\rho}:\left(\Gamma(K)\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(K/k_{0})\right)\times X_{K}\to X_{K}.

For every
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
, we have an associated family
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{G}
which is the pullback of
\mathcal{X}=[X/\Gamma]\to B\Gamma
.

Definition 4.24
.

A subvariety
Y\subseteq X_{K}
is
distinguished
if
\tilde{N}_{G}
maps
Y
to itself.

Lemma 4.25
.

Let
Y\subseteq X_{K}
be a distinguished subvariety.

For every
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
, we have that
Y
descends to a subfamily
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}
of the associated family.

This means that, for every geometric point
p:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to\mathcal{G}
and every isomorphism between
p
and the tautological section of
B\Gamma
such that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(p)
maps to
G\subseteq\Gamma(\Omega)
, the fiber of
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{G}
is
Y_{\Omega}\subseteq X_{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Let
k_{1}/k_{0}
be a finite Galois extension such that
G\subseteq\Gamma(k_{1})
and such that
Y
descends to
Y_{1}\subseteq X_{k_{1}}
, write
N_{G,1}
for the normalizer of
G
in
\Gamma_{k_{1}}
. We may think of
\operatorname{Spec}k_{1}\to\operatorname{Spec}k_{0}
as a cover in the fppf topology, and
G\subseteq\Gamma_{k_{1}}
. By Lemmas
A.7
and
A.10
, we get a type
\Theta
in the sense of Definition
A.5
such that
j
-gerbes coincide with morphisms
\mathcal{G}\to B\Gamma
of type
\Theta
, where
\mathcal{G}
is a gerbe.

By Theorem
A.11
, we have a gerbe
\mathcal{N}
with a faithful morphism
\mathcal{N}\to B\Gamma
such that every
j
-gerbe factorizes through
\mathcal{N}
. To prove the statement, it is sufficient to show that
Y
descends to a subfamily of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

Consider the
j
-gerbe
BG\to B\Gamma
over
K
, we have a factorization
BG\to\mathcal{N}
which identifies
\mathcal{N}_{K}
with
BN_{G}
by Theorem
A.11
. Consider the groupoid
R=\operatorname{Spec}K\times_{\mathcal{N}}\operatorname{Spec}K
with the two natural projections
R\rightrightarrows\operatorname{Spec}K
. By construction, the extended normalizer
\tilde{N}_{G}
identifies with the set of morphisms
\operatorname{Spec}K\to R
over
k_{0}
. The fact that
\tilde{N}_{G}
maps
Y
to itself then implies the same for
R
, hence
Y
descends to a subfamily
[Y/R]\subseteq[X/R]=\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{N}}
.
∎

4.4.
Abelian subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}

At least in low dimensions, when
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
is not abelian it is very often the case that
\bar{G}
is
\rho
-neutral, so that
G
is neutral by Proposition
3.11
. Because of this, most of our work is focused on finite, abelian subgroups of
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
. Let also assume that
|G|
is prime with
\operatorname{char}k_{0}
, so that we may assume
G
to be diagonal up to conjugation. Notice that in this case we may assume that
G=\mathfrak{G}(K)
for some group scheme
\mathfrak{G}
over
k_{0}
thanks to Lemma
4.12
.

Among diagonal subgroups, the first class to consider is groups whose eigenspaces are all
1
-dimensional.

Lemma 4.26
.

Let
G=\mathfrak{G}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
be a finite, diagonal subgroup with
1
-dimensional eigenspaces. The normalizer
\mathfrak{N}
of
\mathfrak{G}
has the form
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\rtimes S
, where
S\subseteq S_{n}
is a group of permutation matrices which permutes the coordinates.

Proof.

The normalizer acts on the
n
eigenspaces, giving a homomorphism
\mathfrak{N}\to S_{n}
with image
S\subseteq S_{n}
and kernel
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
. To conclude, it is enough to show that the permutation matrices corresponding to elements of
S
are in
\mathfrak{N}
. This follows from the fact that any matrix which permutes the coordinates has the form
D\cdot P
, where
D
is diagonal and
P
is a permutation matrix.
∎

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
G=\mathfrak{G}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
be a finite, diagonal subgroup with
1
-dimensional eigenspaces. Let the normalizer be
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\rtimes S
, where
S\subseteq S_{n}
is a group of permutation matrices.

Let
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
be a subgroup of index
\iota_{1}
, and consider the action of
S^{\prime}
on the character group
X(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/G)\subseteq X(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n})=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Assume that
X(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/G)
contains a submodule
N
of index
\iota_{2}
which is a direct summand of a permutation
S^{\prime}
-module.

The index of every
j
-gerbe divides
\iota_{1}\iota_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
k/k_{0}
be an extension and
\phi\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/\mathfrak{G})\rtimes S)
a class. Denote by
\tau
the image of
\phi
in
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,S)
, and by
\psi
the image of
\tau
in
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/\mathfrak{G})\rtimes S)
. By Proposition
4.17
it is enough to find an étale algebra
A/k
with
[A:k]\mid\iota_{1}\iota_{2}
and
\phi_{A}=\psi_{A}
, since
\psi
clearly lifts to
\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\rtimes S)
and thus corresponds to a neutral gerbe.

Let
H\subseteq\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/\mathfrak{G})\rtimes S)
be the inverse image of
\tau
. By non-abelian cohomology
[
Ser94
, §5.4]
, there is a surjective map

\operatorname{H}^{1}(k,T)\to H,

where
T
is the twist of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}/\mathfrak{G}
by
\tau
. Since both
\phi
and
\psi
are elements of
H
, it is enough to show that every
T
-torsor over
k
is trivialized by an étale algebra of degree dividing
\iota_{1}\iota_{2}
.

The class
\tau
corresponds to a homomorphism
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)\to S
. Let
k^{\prime}
be the field fixed by the inverse image of
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
, by construction
[k^{\prime}:k]\mid\iota_{1}
and
\tau_{k^{\prime}}
lifts to a class
\tau^{\prime}\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(k^{\prime},S^{\prime})
. Furthermore,

∎

6.
The classification of neutral subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}

Recall we have defined
\mathrm{SL}_{n}^{(m)}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}
as the subgroup of matrices

with

. Let us extend this definition.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}_{>0}

\underline{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}

We define

as the subgroup of block matrices, with blocks

of dimensions

, such that
\det M_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot M_{s}^{m_{s}}=1

Equivalently,

\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathrm{GL}_{n_{s}}\xrightarrow{\det}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{s}\to\mathbb{G}_{m},

where
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{s}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the character
(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})\mapsto x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{s}^{m_{s}}

Assume
\underline{m}\neq(0,\dots,0)
. Notice that
\mathrm{SL}_{\underline{n}}^{\underline{m}}\to(\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathrm{GL}_{n_{s}})/\mathbb{G}_{m}\subseteq\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
is surjective if and only if it has finite kernel (the dimensions are equal), if and only if
m_{1}n_{1}+\dots+m_{s}n_{s}\neq 0

We refer to Definition
5.5
for the definitions of
M_{i}\in\mathrm{PGL}_{3}(K)
,

. Notice that

is Galois invariant for every

, and hence corresponds to a well defined sub-group scheme of

[

, §3.3]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume
\operatorname{char}k_{0}=0
, and let

be a finite subgroup with image

(1)

G
is not neutral.

(2)

G
is saturated and
\bar{G}
is not
\rho
-neutral.

(3)

•

Given a positive integer

for some positive integers

•

Given integers

Given a positive integer

H_{2}

•

If

\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c)}

.

(3)\Rightarrow(2)
. If

is not

-neutral by Theorem

. Let us show that

is saturated as well. This is clear for the first, third and fourth class. For the second class, the image

\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, which contains the normalizer of

. Hence,
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
is a gate for

is saturated in this case too.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. If

is saturated and not

is not neutral by Proposition
4.10
.

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
. Assume that

is not neutral, we want to show that it is conjugate to one of the groups listed in
(3)
. Fix a non-neutral
j
-gerbe
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{G}
.

By Proposition

and Theorem

\bar{G}
is of type
(a)-(e)
as in Definition

. Let us subdivide the analysis by cases.

6.1.
Type
(a)
or
(e)

Assume that
\bar{G}
is either of type
(a)
or
(e)
, i.e. up to conjugacy

\bar{G}=\left<\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{a},1,1),\operatorname{diag}(1,\zeta_{a},1),\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{an},\zeta_{an}^{d},1)\right>

for integers

with

and satisfying

is diagonal, and we may consider it as a group scheme with the structure given in Lemma

The proof is quite long. We subdivide it in several short steps.

6.1.1.
The singularities of
\mathbb{P}^{2}/\bar{G}
are of type
\mathrm{R}

The singularities of
\mathbb{P}^{2}/\bar{G}
are of type
\frac{1}{n}(1,d)
with

. If they are not of type
\mathrm{R}
, by
[
Bre24a
, Theorem 4]
we also have

, hence

and

, which is absurd since by
[
Bre24a
, Theorem 4]
again we also have
2\mid n
. It follows that the singularities of
\mathbb{P}^{2}/\bar{G}
are of type
\mathrm{R}
. If
\mathbf{P}
is the coarse moduli space of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})
, we get that
\mathbf{P}(k^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{G}(k^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
for every extension

thanks to Lemma
3.9
.

6.1.2.
The index of
\mathcal{G}
is
3
and
3\mid|G|

Let us show that the index of
\mathcal{G}
is
3
and that
3\mid|G|
. If
an=1
, i.e.
G=\left<\zeta_{m}\operatorname{Id}\right>
for some

, then
\mathbf{P}
is a Brauer–Severi surface, hence there exists
k^{\prime}/k
with
[k^{\prime}:k]\mid 3
such that
\mathbf{P}(k^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. If
an\neq 1
, the three axes are
1
-dimensional eigenspaces of
G
, their union is distinguished and descends to a
0
-cycle of degree
3
on
\mathbf{P}
, hence there exists
k^{\prime}/k
with
[k^{\prime}:k]\mid 3
such that
\mathbf{P}(k^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
in this case as well.

In any case, this implies
\mathcal{G}(k^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
by Lemma
3.9
. Since
\mathcal{G}(k)=\emptyset
by assumption, then
[k^{\prime}:k]=3
and
\mathcal{G}
has index
3
by Lemma
2.5
. It follows that

by Lemma
2.4
.

6.1.3.
Completing the proof if
an=1

If
an=1
, then
G=\left<\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id}\right>
for some
c\geq 1
, and
3\mid c=|G|
, so
G
has the desired form.

6.1.4.
Structure of
G
and of the normalizer if

Assume

, and let
N_{G}\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)
be the extended normalizer (see Remark
4.23
). Since
an\neq 1
, the three axes are
1
-dimensional eigenspaces of
G
, and
N_{G}
permutes them. If the action of

on the axes is not transitive, then at least one of them is distinguished, hence it descends to a rational point on
\mathbf{P}
of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})
. This implies that
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
, which is absurd, hence the action of
N_{G}
on the axes must be transitive.

By Lemma
4.26
, the normalizer is
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3}\rtimes S
with
S
either
C_{3}
or
S_{3}
. Let
M_{1}\in N_{G}
be as in Definition
5.5
.

Let
\left<w=\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id}\right>
be the kernel of
G\to\bar{G}
. Given the presentation of
\bar{G}
above, in
G
we have elements
x=\lambda\cdot\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{3a}^{2},\zeta_{3a}^{-1},\zeta_{3a}^{-1})
,
z=\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{3an}^{2-d},\zeta_{3an}^{2d-1},\zeta_{3an}^{-d-1})
, and
y=M_{1}\cdot x\cdot M_{1}^{-1}=\lambda\cdot\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{3a}^{-1},\zeta_{3a}^{2},\zeta_{3a}^{-1})
.

Furthermore, we have

\lambda^{3}\operatorname{Id}=x\cdot(M_{1}\cdot x\cdot M_{1}^{-1})\cdot(M_{1}^{2}\cdot x\cdot M_{1}^{-2})\in\left<w=\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id}\right>,

\lambda^{\prime 3}\operatorname{Id}=z\cdot(M_{1}\cdot z\cdot M_{1}^{-1})\cdot(M_{1}^{2}\cdot z\cdot M_{1}^{-2})\in\left<w=\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id}\right>,

hence we may write
\lambda=\zeta_{3c}^{\alpha}
,
\lambda^{\prime}=\zeta_{3c}^{\beta}
.

To sum up, we have

G=\left<w=\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id},~x=\zeta_{3c}^{\alpha}\cdot\operatorname{diag}\left(\zeta_{3a}^{2},\zeta_{3a}^{-1},\zeta_{3a}^{-1}\right),\right.

\left.y=\zeta_{3c}^{\alpha}\cdot\operatorname{diag}\left(\zeta_{3a}^{-1},\zeta_{3a}^{2},\zeta_{3a}^{-1}\right),~z=\zeta_{3c}^{\beta}\cdot\operatorname{diag}\left(\zeta_{3an}^{2-d},\zeta_{3an}^{2d-1},\zeta_{3an}^{-d-1}\right)\right>

and up to multiplying

by a suitably power of

, we may assume that
0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 2
.

The statement is then equivalent to
3\mid c
and
\alpha=\beta=0
, which in turn is equivalent to
3\mid m
and
G\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(m/3)}
.

6.1.5.
Completing the proof if
3\nmid an

If
3\nmid an
, then
3\mid c
since

but
3\nmid|\bar{G}|
. Furthermore,
x^{3}\cdot w^{-\alpha}
,

,

are elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c/3)}
mapping to generators of

, hence

.

6.1.6.
Proof of
3\mid c
if

Assume
3\mid an
. Let us show that
3\mid m
.

If
3\mid a
, then

x^{a}\cdot w^{-a\alpha/3}=\zeta_{3c}^{a\alpha}\cdot\zeta_{3}^{2}\cdot\zeta_{3c}^{-a\alpha}\operatorname{Id}=\zeta_{3}^{2}\operatorname{Id}\in\left<w=\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id}\right>,

hence
3\mid c
.

Assume
3\nmid a
,
3\mid n
, write
n=3^{\gamma}n^{\prime}
with
3\nmid n^{\prime}
. If, by contradiction,
3\nmid c
, the
3
-Sylow

is generated by

x^{ca}=y^{ca}=\zeta_{3}^{a\alpha}\cdot\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{3}^{2},\zeta_{3}^{2},\zeta_{3}^{2},)^{c}=\zeta_{3}^{a\alpha+2c}\operatorname{Id}

and

z^{acn^{\prime}}=\zeta_{3}^{an^{\prime}\beta}\operatorname{diag}\left(\zeta_{3^{\gamma+1}}^{2-d},\zeta_{3^{\gamma+1}}^{2d-1},\zeta_{3^{\gamma+1}}^{-d-1}\right)^{c}.

Since
3\nmid c
and the kernel of

is generated by
\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id}
, then
\zeta_{3}^{a\alpha+2c}=1
. Notice that

and

implies

, write

, hence

z^{acn^{\prime}}=\zeta_{3}^{an^{\prime}\beta}\operatorname{diag}\left(\zeta_{3^{\gamma}}^{-d^{\prime}},\zeta_{3^{\gamma}}^{1+2d^{\prime}},\zeta_{3^{\gamma}}^{-1-d^{\prime}}\right)^{c}

Notice that
-d^{\prime}
,

,

are pairwise different modulo

, and since

then exactly one eigenvalue of

is a

. This is in contradiction with the fact that

is normalized by

, hence we must have

.

6.1.7.
Reduction to
3
-groups

We have to prove that
G\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c/3)}
. Let us show that it is enough to do so in the case in which

is a power of
3
.

The band
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{G}
is a twisted form of
G
over

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{3}\times\mathcal{A}_{3}^{\prime}
where

is a power of
3
and
|\mathcal{A}_{3}^{\prime}|
is prime with

. By
[
Bre25
, Lemma 2.3]
,
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{3}\times\mathcal{G}_{3}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{3}
is the induced
\mathcal{A}_{3}
-gerbe and
\mathcal{G}_{3}^{\prime}
is the induced
\mathcal{A}_{3}^{\prime}
-gerbe. Since
\mathcal{G}
has index

, then
\mathcal{G}_{3}^{\prime}
is neutral by Lemma

, it follows that
\mathcal{G}_{3}
is not neutral and has index
3
. Furthermore, by choosing a

\mathcal{G}_{3}^{\prime}
, we may pull-back

to
\mathcal{G}_{3}
, giving
\mathcal{G}_{3}
the structure of a

-gerbe where

is the

-Sylow subgroup. Hence, we may reduce to the case in which

is a power of

thanks to the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
G_{3}\subseteq G
be the
3
-Sylow, and

the largest power of

G_{3}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c^{\prime}/3)}
if and only if

, if and only if

Proof.

The first equivalence follows from the fact that, if

is an element whose order is a power of

The “if” part is obvious. Assume

, and let

be the subgroup of elements of order prime with

. We already know that

, so
G_{3}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c)}

has order prime with

, hence
G_{3}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c/3)}
as well. Since

, we conclude that

.
∎

6.1.8.
Proof of
G\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{(c/3)}
for
3
-groups

So we may assume that
|G|
is a power of
3
. Equivalently,

,

,

are powers of
3
. Let us prove that

.

We want to use Theorem

to show that, if
(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)
, the index of
\mathcal{G}
is prime with
3
, giving a contradiction since we have already proved that the index of
\mathcal{G}
is
3
. The normalizer of
G
is
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3}\rtimes S
with
S
either
C_{3}
or
S_{3}
, hence it is sufficient to show that
(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)
then
X(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3}/G)
contains a permutation

-module of index prime with

.

The equation

has no solutions modulo

, hence

is either
1
or
3
.

If

we may ignore

already generate

. The character group

If

6.2.
Type

Assume that

6.2.1.
The index of
\mathcal{G}

The line

Hence,

of the coarse moduli space
\mathbf{P}
of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})
. Since
\mathbf{P}
is normal, then it is regular in codimension

\mathbf{L}
is a genus

, regular in

, such that

\mathcal{G}(k(p))\neq\emptyset

6.2.2.
Structure of
G
and of the normalizer

Let
N_{G}\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)
be the extended normalizer (see Remark
4.23
), then
N_{G}\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3}\rtimes S
where
S\subseteq S_{3}
permutes the coordinates. We have already seen above that the line

is distinguished, hence

must fix the third coordinate, i.e.
S
is either trivial or a cyclic group

swapping the first two coordinates. Let us show that

.

If by contradiction

is trivial, the point

is distinguished and descends to a rational point

with singularity of type
\frac{1}{2m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}(1,d-1)
, where
m^{\prime},n^{\prime},d
are as in the proof of Lemma

. This singularity is of type
\mathrm{R}
because

[
Bre24a
, Theorem 4]
, hence we get that
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
thanks to Lemma

, which is absurd.

Let
\left<x=\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id}\right>
be the kernel of
G\to\bar{G}
. Given the presentation of
\bar{G}
above, in
G
we have elements

y=\lambda\cdot\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{2m},\zeta_{2m},1),

z=\eta\cdot\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{2n},\zeta_{2n}^{-1},1),

and

We have that

, hence

for some
\alpha
. Furthermore, since

normalizes

, then
\eta\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{2n}^{-1},\zeta_{2n},1)\in G
, which implies that

so we may write

for some

. To sum up,

G=\left<x=\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id},y=\zeta_{2mc}^{\alpha}\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{2m},\zeta_{2m},1),z=\zeta_{2c}^{\beta}\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{2n},\zeta_{2n}^{-1},1)\right>.

Up to multiplying
z
by a suitable power of

, we may assume that
0\leq\beta\leq 1
.
Up to multiplying
y
by a suitable power of
x
, we may assume that
0\leq\alpha\leq 2m-1
.

Furthermore, notice that

y^{m}z^{-n}=\zeta_{2c}^{\alpha-\beta n}\operatorname{Id}\in\left<x=\zeta_{c}\operatorname{Id}\right>,

or equivalently
\alpha-\beta n
is even.

6.2.3.
Reformulation of statement

We claim that
G
has the desired form if
\beta=0
. Assume that

Remark A.13
.

There are at least two ways in which Theorem
A.11
generalizes Theorem
A.12
.

The first is obvious: we are not restricted to normalizers of subgroups, but we are free to work with normalizers of arbitrary subsets. For instance, the normalizer of a single element of a group is the centralizer of the element.

The second is more subtle. Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is the fppf site on a field, and that
\mathcal{H}
is the classifying stack
B\Gamma
of an algebraic group
\Gamma
. Consider a finite Galois extension
k^{\prime}/k
and a finite subgroup
G\subset\Gamma(k^{\prime})
. If
G
is Galois invariant, then it coincides with the geometric point of a group scheme
\mathfrak{G}
, and if
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\Gamma
is the normalizer of
\mathfrak{G}
then
B\mathfrak{N}
is the normalizer of
B\mathfrak{G}\to B\Gamma
.

However, to define the normalizer at the level of gerbes, it is sufficient that the Galois conjugates of
G
are conjugate to
G
as subgroups of
\Gamma(k^{\prime})
, up to enlarging
k^{\prime}
: we may then use Lemma
A.10
to define a type and Theorem
A.11
to define the normalizer.
