Stimuli-Responsive Polyethylenimine Derivatives as Carriers for siRNA Delivery: Current Solutions and Future Perspectives
Authors: Oskar Kołacki, Stanisław Trzciński, Marcin K. Chmielewski
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
RNA therapeutics, particularly small interfering RNA, hold great promise for precise gene silencing but still face major challenges related to instability, inefficient delivery, and off-target effects. Polyethylenimine (PEI), a versatile polycation, exhibits strong nucleic acid binding capacity, efficient endosomal escape, and high structural adaptability, making it one of the most promising nonviral delivery platforms. This review summarizes recent advances in stimuli-responsive PEI-based systems, classified according to endogenous triggers such as pH gradients, reactive oxygen species, enzymatic activity, and ATP, as well as exogenous stimuli such as light, temperature, and magnetic fields. Reported strategies include chemical modification, polymer grafting, hybridization with inorganic nanomaterials, and fluorination to enhance specificity, transfection efficiency, and biocompatibility while reducing cytotoxicity. Dual- and multistimuli-responsive designs further enable spatiotemporally controlled release, thereby improving therapeutic efficacy both in vitro and in vivo. Emerging evidence indicates that PEI derivatives represent adaptable, scalable, and clinically relevant platforms for next-generation RNA delivery.

Introduction

RNA therapeutics have emerged as a transformative field in modern pharmaceuticals, offering potential strategies to alleviate symptoms or even cure diseases once considered incurable. , These oligonucleotide-based molecules alter gene expression within cells, can significantly reshape the cellular transcriptome and, consequently, the proteome. This particular ability enables precise regulation of protein expression, allowing for both upregulation and suppression. In addition, RNA therapies can be engineered to induce antibodies production or inhibit intracellular pathways through interaction with mRNA. Among these approaches, small interfering RNA (siRNA) therapeutics have attracted particular attention as a promising tool for silencing disease-associated genes through the targeted degradation of specific mRNA transcripts. As oligonucleotides capable of downregulating gene expression, siRNA-based therapeutics have shown cosiderable potential in the treatment of cancer, genetic disorders, and viral infections. The manufacturing and derivatization of siRNA modalities remain areas of extensive research. , However, their clinical application continues to face challenges related to stability, systemic delivery, and efficient cellular uptake. Current delivery strategies include viral carriers such as lentiviruses (LVs), adenoviruses (AdVs), and adeno-associated viruses (AAVs), as well as nonviral systems, including lipid nanoparticles, exosome-based carriers, inorganic nanoparticles, and polymer-based platforms (Table ).

Regardless of the delivery platform, RNA carriers encounter several biological barriers after entering the bloodstream. Cytotoxicity, immunogenicity, insufficient tissue specificity, membrane adhesion, and rapid renal excretion are among the key factors that limit the effectiveness of RNA carriers.

Although multiple carrier systemssuch as viral vectors, lipid nanoparticles, and exosomeshave been developed to overcome these barriers, each exhibits specific trade-offs in safety, delivery efficiency, and scalability (Table ). Synthetic polymers have emerged as a particularly attractive alternatives because of their structural tunability, adjustable immunogenicity, and ability to respond to physiological stimuli (Table ). These materials can be readily synthesized and chemically modified, offering a significantly more cost-effective option than other nanomaterials used in pharmaceutical applications. Currently, polymeric molecules are being extensively explored not only as RNA carriers but also as scaffolds for tissue regeneration and as antibiotics due to their intrinsic antibacterial properties. ,

Polycations represent a subclass of polymers capable of exhibiting positive charges along their main chain or within pendant functional groups in aqueous environments when the pH falls below their pK a value. Polyethylenimine (PEI) and poly­(L)-lysine (PLL) are among the most widely studied polycations for oligonucleotide delivery. Over the past five years, more than ten reviews have discussed their applications in pharmaceutical delivery; − however, only a limited number have focused specifically on polycationic stimuli-responsive carriers. ,− These macromolecules offer several inherent advantages, including the ability to form polyplexes through electrostatic interactions between positively charged polymers and negatively charged nucleic acids. Moreover, their cationic nature facilitates adhesion to cell membranes via electrostatic attraction between the polycation and the phosphate groups of plasma membranes. This interaction promotes endocytosis, which subsequently enables endosomal escape and the deposition of nucleic acids into the cytosol of target cell.

The structural diversity of polymers and polycations enables extensive customization in delivery system design. Linear and branched polymeric architectures provide multiple strategies for drug incorporation within their structures. These polymers can be grafted onto one another, cross-linked to form gels, or derivatized to further modify their physical and chemical properties. Such versatility enhances the therapeutic potential of polymer-based systems by improving biocompatibility and minimizing undesirable interactions with biological environments, including cytotoxicity and immunogenicity.

Concept of Stimuli-Responsive Polycationic Carriers

Polycationic molecules address the issue of premature release of negatively charged nucleic acid–based therapeutics by forming stable complexes and enhancing cellular adhesion through electrostatic interactions. However, the true potential of these systems lies in their ability to exploit physicochemical properties and stimuli responsiveness (Figure ). This enables the design of intelligent nanocarriers capable of targeted and controlled delivery of therapeutic agents in response to specific internal (e.g., pH, redox potential, enzymatic activity) or external (e.g., temperature, light, magnetic field) stimuli. Such stimuli-responsive polycationic nanocarriers represent a promising strategy to improve therapeutic efficacy while minimizing off-target effects.

Among various cationic polymers, linear polyethylenimine (lPEI) and branched polyethylenimine (bPEI) remain the most widely used systems for nucleic acid delivery. PEI was first introduced as a nucleic acid carrier in 1995, and since then, extensive research has produced numerous derivatives designed to enhance its delivery performance.

Scope of the Review

This review examines recent studies on various stimuli-responsive PEI and PEI-derived complexes employed as carriers for siRNA and, in one case, miRNA. The delivery systems are categorized based on the type of stimulus that triggers cargo release or carrier activation. The summary highlights their advantages over other carrier types, incorporating both in vitro and in vivo findings, with particular emphasis on quantitative comparisons wherever available.

Stimulus Type-Based Classification

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is characterized by several physiological and biochemical alterations that can be leveraged for therapeutic targeting and siRNA delivery. Among these, extracellular acidification is one of the most commonly utilized stimuli in drug carrier design, particularly for solid-tumor targeting. The local pH reduction within tumor tissues arises from metabolic reprogramming associated with the Warburg effect, wherein cancer cells preferentially rely on glycolysis instead of aerobic respiration, even under oxygen-rich conditions. This acidic environment influences numerous cellular processes associated with tumor growth, metastasis, immune evasion, and drug resistance. −

To sustain rapid ATP generation and increased biosynthetic activity, malignant cells undergo aerobic glycolysis, metabolizing glucose into lactate. Although this metabolic adaptation supports cell proliferation, it concurrently reduces extracellular pH to approximately 6.5–6.8, compared with the slightly alkaline pH of 7.4 in healthy tissues. Extracellular acidification has been documented in various epithelial cancers, including breast, lung, and pancreatic tumors, where acidity contributes to immune suppression and metastatic progression.

The high prevalence of tumor microenvironment (TME) acidosis makes it a crucial physiological gradient for the targeted delivery of therapeutic agents. Numerous cationic polymers have been engineered to respond to this stimulus, resulting in enhanced delivery performance across multiple stages. pH fluctuations enable controlled siRNA release, while cellular uptake is facilitated by electrostatic interactions between positively charged carriers and negatively charged cell membranes. Furthermore, endosomal escape is promoted through the proton sponge effecta mechanism in which polycations become protonated under the acidic conditions encountered during the endosome-to-lysosome transition.

Another strategy for exploiting the pH gradient involves targeting the endocytic pathway. Following adhesion to the cell membrane, the polycation–siRNA complex triggers internalization through endocytosis.

Recent Advances in the Utilization of pH as a Stimulus

One promising strategy involves the design of an engineered pH-responsive hybrid nanocarrier in which lPEI is shielded by poly­(ethylene glycol) (PEG) under neutral pH conditions. This so-called “Matryoshka” approach responds to acidityat pH 6.5, characteristic of the mildly acidic extracellular tumor environmentby triggering the detachment of the PEG shell. The resulting charge reversal enhances cellular uptake of siRNA targeting NgBR, a membrane-bound protein involved in tumor angiogenesis in tissues surrounding malignant lesions. Upon internalization, siRNA-mediated gene silencing achieved a 52.09% knockdown of NgBR expression and a 70.22% reduction in tumor growth.

The TME can be effectively accessed by polycation-based carriers owing to its hyperpermeable vasculature, which results from rapid exposure of tumor microvessels to vascular-permeabilizing factors (VPFs) such as histamine and serotonin. Recent studies have exploited passive tumor targeting through the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect, combined with active targeting using receptor-specific ligands. For example, low-molecular-weight PEI was functionalized with 4-carboxy-3-fluorophenylboronic acid (FPBA) and dextran to form an ATP-responsive, siRNA-loaded core. This was subsequently coated with a cis-aconitic anhydride-modified poly­(ethylene glycol)-b-polylysine (mPEG-bPLL/CA) shell, designed to detach at pH 6.8, corresponding to typical TME acidity. This system enabled efficient delivery of STAT3-targeting siRNA, resulting in a 34% reduction in tumor cell viability (to 66%) and demonstrated favorable biocompatibility in vivo.

Polyethylenimine-coated amorphous calcium carbonate (PEI/ACC) nanoparticles were developed via flash nanoprecipitation in the presence of poly­(acrylic acid) (PAA), with the 30 kDa variant identified as optimal due to its dual functionalityproviding stabilization at physiological pH and rapid dissolution in acidic environments. Surface functionalization with 10 kDa branched PEI at a 1:0.5 PEI/ACC mass ratio imparted a positive surface charge and enabled efficient siRNA complexation. Complete siRNA loading was achieved at a PEI/siRNA weight ratio of 0.5:1, with serum stability maintained for at least 4 h and colloidal stability confirmed for over 10 days in 5% FBS. Compared with PEI–siRNA polyplexes, the hybrid carriers exhibited markedly reduced protein adsorption and a significantly enhanced release profile under acidic conditions, confirming their pH responsiveness. In vitro transfection studies in HeLa-Luc and A549-Luc cells demonstrated dose-dependent luciferase silencing. Fluorescence microscopy revealed extensive cellular uptake of siRNA-loaded nanoparticles by HeLa cells after 4 h of incubation. Co-localization analysis further indicated improved endosomal escape relative to PEI–siRNA polyplexes, with Pearson correlation coefficients of 0.66 and 0.64 recorded at 4 and 8 h, respectively. Collectively, the PEI/ACC platform showed enhanced biocompatibility, efficient intracellular trafficking, and strong gene silencing activity, highlighting its potential as a promising vehicle for siRNA delivery.

A systematic study of polyurethane and poly­(l-lysine) (PLL) backbones modified with PEI side chains demonstrated that these constructs can form effective polycationic polymers suitable for gene delivery. Although the investigation primarily focused on structure–function relationships, selected variants incorporated pH-sensitive imine linkages, allowing cleavage and charge modulation under acidic conditions. The most efficient vectors displayed strong DNA condensation and high transfection efficiency, outperforming the commercial PEI 25K. These findings suggest that while backbone flexibility and side-chain density are key design parameters, the inclusion of pH-labile bonds substantially enhances intracellular delivery through acid-triggered disassembly.

PEI, one of the most widely used polycations, can also serve as a component of a miRNA nanocomposite. A pH-responsive structure can be formed by combining PEI modified with dimethylmaleic anhydride (PEI-DMA), p-toluene sulfonyl-l-arginine (PER), and miRNA through electrostatic self-assembly. The amide bond on the surface of PEI-DMA undergoes hydrolysis under mildly acidic conditions, resulting in a charge reversal from +17.1 mV at pH 5.0 to −9.4 mV at pH 8.0. This change enhances cellular uptake by up to 3-fold at pH 6.5 compared with pH 7.4, while also reducing cytotoxicity relative to traditional PEI-based carriers.

Reactive Oxygen Species

In the TME and other diseased tissues, redox homeostasis is often disrupted, leading to significantly elevated concentrations of reactive oxygen species (ROS) compared with normal tissues. Among these, hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is one of the most abundant ROS, accumulating to tens of micromolar levels in tumors, whereas healthy cells typically maintain H2O2 concentrations in the nanomolar range. This oxidative stress gradient primarily results from the hyperactive metabolism of cancer cells and the infiltration of inflammatory cells, both of which contribute to excessive in situ ROS production. This unique ROS environment provides an exploitable stimulus for the development of stimuli-responsive drug carriers.

Elevated ROS levels serve as an endogenous trigger that advanced siRNA nanocarriers can leverage for selective payload release. To harness this gradient, siRNA delivery vectors are engineered with ROS-responsive linkages that remain stable under physiological conditions but undergo cleavage or chemical transformation in the presence of elevated ROS concentrations. Common strategies for imparting ROS sensitivity include the oxidation of hydrophobic thioethers into hydrophilic sulfoxides and the incorporation of ROS-labile bonds such as thioketals, which degrade into inert fragments upon exposure to high H2O2 concentrations.

Recent Advances in the Utilization of ROS as a Stimulus

Low-molecular-weight PEI was functionalized with a thioketal-bridged, quinoline-based zinc­(II) module (Zn-QS) to form Zn-PQ vectorsself-fluorescent polycations capable of ROS-triggered siRNA release. The Zn-QS unit integrates a quinoline pharmacophore for metal coordination and fluorescence, a hydrophilic PEG side chain for serum stability, and a ROS-cleavable thioketal linker. Zn-PQ4 polyplexes efficiently condensed EGFP-siRNA (Enhanced Green Fluorescent Protein siRNA), maintained a stable particle size (∼160 nm) under physiological and endosomal pH conditions, and released their cargo in response to 10 mM H2O2, with complete particle disassembly observed after 12 h. Compared with PEI25k and Lipo2k, Zn-PQ4 achieved 98.4 and 99.9% EGFP knockdown in 293T and HepG2 cells, respectively, and demonstrated a 250-fold increase in cellular uptake relative to unmodified PEI3.5k. In vivo, Zn-PQ4/EGFP-siRNA complexes preferentially accumulated in the liver and kidney, where they reduced EGFP expression by 50.2 and 65.4%, respectively. These results confirm the system’s efficacy in ROS-rich environments and underscore its promise as a fluorescence-traceable, low-toxicity platform for siRNA delivery.

A library of ROS-responsive cationic nanogels was synthesized using a selenol-mediated multicomponent reaction (MCR) that incorporated low-molecular-weight PEI, γ-selenobutyrolactone (SBL), and poly­(ethylene glycol) methacrylate (PEGMA). The optimized formulation, A1.8Se3O0.5, produced ∼200 nm nanogels cross-linked by dynamic diselenide bonds, enabling oxidation-triggered degradation and intracellular siPD-L1 release. The system exploited the proton sponge effect of PEI for endosomal escape and achieved ROS-mediated siRNA release (80% within 4 h at 100 μM H2O2). This resulted in a 56% downregulation of PD-L1 expression and inhibition of autophagy via lysosomal alkalinization. In MC38 tumors, treatment restored MHC-I expression, enhanced CD8+ T-cell infiltration by 2.4-fold, and increased granzyme B expression by ∼5-fold. Ultimately, administration of A1.8Se3O0.5/siPD-L1 nanogels achieved 69.5% tumor growth inhibition and promoted DC maturation, demonstrating a dual-function immunotherapeutic platform that integrates ROS-responsive gene delivery with autophagy modulation.

ROS-responsive polycationic vectors were also developed by cross-linking low-molecular-weight PEI (1.8 K) through a thioketal (TK) linker, followed by grafting heptafluorobutyric anhydride at varying fluorination ratios to obtain TKPFs. These modifications endowed the polymers with ROS-sensitive degradability and surface fluorination, the latter providing serum resistance through combined lipophobic and hydrophobic shielding. Among the tested variants, TKPF 50% exhibited the best performance, achieving approximately 95% transfection efficiency in 293TN cells and maintaining around 80% efficiency in 70% fetal bovine serum (FBS)a condition under which PEI25K efficiency dropped to nearly zero. In B16F10 cells, ROS-triggered cleavage of the TK linker under tumor-mimicking oxidative conditions (H2O2 ≥ 100 μM) further enhanced gene release. TKPF 50% also showed minimal cytotoxicity (>90% cell viability at 25 μg/mL) and superior lysosomal escape compared with both TKPEI and PEI25K. Additionally, reduced protein corona formation and lipid raft-mediated uptake improved colloidal stability, intracellular delivery, and bioavailability under physiological stress, confirming the platform’s strong potential for in vivo gene therapy.

A dual redox-responsive mesoporous silica nanocarrier was engineered by integrating branched PEI and bifunctional 3,3′-diselanediyldipropionic acid (DSeDPA) onto aminopropyl triethoxysilane functionalized MCM41 (mesoporous molecular sieve), producing PEI-Se-MCM41 nanoparticles capable of on-demand 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) release in ROS- and GSH-rich tumor microenvironments. The DSeDPA component provided cleavable Se–Se bonds, enabling degradation under both oxidative (H2O2) and reductive (GSH) conditions, while PEI served as a positively charged “gatekeeper,” preventing premature drug leakage and enhancing cellular uptake. This formed a three-layered structure of drug-loaded mesoporous nanoparticles, surrounded by reactive diselenide bridges bound to both the particle and a polycationic layer. The final formulation exhibited minimal release at pH 7.4 (<10%) but triggered 86% release at 5 mM GSH and approximately 35% at 100 mM H2O2 within 48 h. In CT-26 colon cancer cells, PEI-Se-MCM41@5-FU induced dose-dependent cytotoxicity with an IC50 of 24.6 μg/mL, while the unloaded carrier displayed no detectable toxicity. Confocal microscopy and FACS analysis confirmed significantly enhanced cellular uptake compared with free dye, demonstrating the potential of diselenide-cross-linked, PEI-gated mesoporous silica beads for redox-responsive chemotherapy.

To address inflammation-induced myocardial injury, a ROS-ultrasensitive nanotherapeutic was developed for the codelivery of dexamethasone (Dex) and RAGE siRNA (Receptor for Advanced Glycation End Products; siRAGE) via mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) capped with a prostaglandin E2-modified (PGE2), PEGylated, ditellurium-cross-linked PEI (PPTP) polycation. The formulation electrostatically condensed siRAGE while simultaneously preventing premature Dex leakage through MSN surface capping. Following PGE2-mediated targeting of EP receptors on cardiomyocytes and ROS-triggered cleavage of Te–Te bonds, the vector disassembled intracellularly, releasing both therapeutic agents. In vitro, RAGE silencing reached 72%, reducing TNF levels and decreasing apoptosis in H9C2 cells. In an ischemia-reperfusion (IR) rat model, the system significantly suppressed myocardial fibrosis, reduced infarct size, and restored systolic function more effectively than either Dex or siRNA monotherapy. This dual-loaded, ROS-degradable polycationic coating strategy represents a promising platform for combined RNAi and chemodrug therapy in oxidative microenvironments.

Other Stimuli

Several additional internal biochemical cues can trigger siRNA release by inducing cleavage or transformation of carrier materials. For instance, enzyme-cleavable peptide carriers exploit tumor-associated proteases (e.g., MMPs) to remove protective coatings. Tumor hypoxia has also been leveraged: nitroimidazole groups on lipid-based carriers undergo bioreduction under low-oxygen conditions to form positively charged aminoimidazoles, leading to nanoparticle charge reversal and carrier destabilization, which in turn promotes siRNA release. Elevated intracellular ATP can similarly serve as a trigger through ATP-responsive motifscarriers containing phenylboronic acid or ATP-binding aptamers form cross-links or electrostatic complexes that are disrupted upon ATP binding, resulting in siRNA complex disassembly.

External physical stimuli can also be employed to induce siRNA release. Light-responsive carriers incorporate photocleavable bonds or photoswitchable moieties, taking advantage of skin translucency or localized irradiation. Photothermal systems, typically using dyes or gold nanostructures, convert near-infrared (NIR) light into heat, thereby weakening carrier linkages and promoting payload release. Temperature, which is often elevated in tumorous or inflamed tissues due to increased vascularization or metabolic activity, represents another useful trigger: slight temperature changes can cause polymers to collapse or swell, expelling the bound siRNA. Alternating magnetic fields exert magnetothermal or mechanical effects via iron oxide nanoparticles, which either generate heat or oscillate, causing local disruption of thermosensitive matrices and siRNA release. Although pH and ROS remain the most widely studied endogenous gradients, novel carriers responsive to other internal and external stimuli are increasingly being developed, broadening the toolbox for precise and selective siRNA delivery.

Recent Advances in the Utilization of Other Stimuli

A photothermally active delivery platform based on PEI-functionalized gold nanocages (AuNCs) has been developed as a nonviral gene delivery vector. The AuNCs were synthesized via a galvanic replacement reaction and subsequently modified with thiol-terminated PEI of two molecular weights (1.8 and 10 kDa). The resulting nanostructures exhibited positive ζ-potentials (40–47 mV) and low diameter dispersity values (0.14–0.22), indicating good colloidal stability. DNA binding assays confirmed complete complexation at mass ratios ≥ 1:1 for AuNCs–PEI (10 kDa), whereas the 1.8 kDa conjugate displayed reduced binding efficiency. Complex formation induced changes in hydrodynamic diameter and surface charge, with stabilization observed at higher polymer ratios. Under 800 nm femtosecond laser irradiation, the conjugates demonstrated efficient photothermal conversion in a power- and concentration-dependent manner. In vitro cytotoxicity testing with 293T cells showed >85% cell viability for AuNCs–PEI (10 kDa), while the 1.8 kDa variant even promoted cell proliferation at low mass ratios. Gene delivery efficiency of AuNCs–PEI (10 kDa) reached 15% ± 0.404 GFP expression at a 15:1 mass ratio, surpassing that of the 1.8 kDa conjugate (3.62% ± 0.385) and achieving 58.6% of the activity of PEI 25k at a 1:1 ratio. This dual-function system combines low cytotoxicity, effective plasmid binding, and NIR light-triggered release, making it a promising platform for future gene therapy applications.

A temperature-responsive gene delivery system was also developed by grafting poly­(N-isopropylacrylamide) onto branched PEI and immobilizing it on gold nanotriangles (PNIPAm-g-PEI/GNTs). The hybrid material exhibited a lower critical solution temperature of 42 °C, above which the particle size decreased markedly from 349.4 to 150 nm, facilitating DNA release. Throughout this thermal transition, the surface remained positively charged (31.0 mV), ensuring sustained electrostatic interactions with nucleic acids. Complete DNA complexation was achieved at 20 μg/mL, and release was further enhanced under 980 nm laser irradiation at 0.7 W/cm2 for 10 min. In HeLa cells, PNIPAm-g-PEI/GNTs achieved superior transfection efficiency compared with PEI 25K, increasing luciferase and EGFP expression by 1.5- and 7.2-fold, respectively. Across the 10–100 μg/mL concentration range, PNIPAm-g-PEI/GNTs also exhibited lower cytotoxicity than PEI.

An MMP-2-responsive (matrix metalloproteinase-2) siRNA delivery system was designed by conjugating hyaluronic acid (HA) to polyethylenimine (PEI) through a GPLGLAGC peptide linker, producing HA-P-PEI. The resulting polymer formed stable nanoparticles with siRNA, exhibiting an average hydrodynamic diameter of 186.4 nm and a ζ-potential of +6.89 mV. Upon incubation with MMP-2, the particle size distribution shifted25.6% of particles measured below 100 nm, and a distinct subpopulation (<10 nm) emerged. Efficient siRNA complexation was achieved at N/P ratios ≥ 8, with complete gel retardation and serum stability maintained for at least 24 h. The HA-P-PEI construct exhibited lower cytotoxicity than unmodified PEI, maintaining 87.63% cell viability at 10 μg/mL. Cellular uptake and intracellular localization were confirmed via fluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry in H1975 cells overexpressing CD44, PD-L1, and MMP-2. Confocal imaging of multicellular tumor spheroids demonstrated enhanced penetration following MMP-2 pretreatment, with fluorescence observed in central spheroid regions. Gene knockdown analysis using RT-PCR and Western blot revealed a 45% reduction in PD-L1 mRNA expression compared with the negative control. Overall, the system exhibited excellent biocompatibility, MMP-2–triggered activation, and improved siRNA delivery efficiency in tumor-relevant models.

Fluorination has also emerged as a promising strategy to improve the performance of polymer-based nanocarriers. The resulting system displayed favorable physicochemical characteristics, including a hydrodynamic diameter of 93.29 ± 7.31 nm and a markedly positive surface charge of +56.77 mV, compared with −45.57 mV for the unmodified Fe3O4 coreindicating strong nucleic acid complexation capability. The incorporation of fluorinated segments was verified by 19F NMR and EDS elemental analysis. The formulation showed low cytotoxicity, maintaining >80% cell viability at concentrations ≤4 μg/mL (Fe) for up to 48 h. Transfection efficiency exceeded 90% in HeLa cells when 0.5–1.0 μg of carrier with 40 pmol siRNA was applied, and performance improved further under a 400 mT magnetic field. Compared with the commercial benchmark Lipofectamine 3000, the nanoparticles achieved superior uptake and intracellular delivery. In 4T1 breast cancer cells, the system effectively mediated CXCR4 knockdown, with one siRNA sequence (R2) producing the strongest reduction in CXCR4 mRNA expression and a corresponding decrease in protein levels at both 24 and 48 h post-transfection. These results highlight the value of fluorination in enhancing nanocarrier uptake, transfection efficiency, and biocompatibility while preserving potent gene silencing activity.

Conclusion

In spite of its numerous and persistent drawbacks, such as cytotoxicity, poor biodegradability, and nonspecific recognition of biological membranes, PEI is still considered the standard for gene delivery, serving as a benchmark for evaluating novel delivery systems. Its high transfection efficiency and ability to bind nucleic acids make it one of the most efficient and versatile cationic polymers available. However, unmodified PEI is constrained by both chemical and biological limitations. Its high charge density leads to severe cytotoxicity, poor biodegradability, and nonspecific interactions with biological membranes. The ongoing redefinition of cationic polymer derivatization aimed at optimizing nucleic acid delivery has established PEI as a robust scaffold for the development of grafted and alternating derivatives capable of forming stable and efficient polyplexes with siRNA.

PEI-based carriers can be easily programmed to a wide range of stimuli. Modern derivatives respond to, among others, intra- and extracellular pH gradients, GSH level, and enzymatic activity. Moreover novel modifications increasingly incorporate targeted responses to external stimuliparticularly light and temperaturewhich remain relatively underexplored in PEI-based delivery systems. Ease of chemical derivatization may also open new opportunities for using different gradientsboth internal and external. UV-responsive systems and alternative grafting strategies may improve biodegradability and carrier clearance. External magnetic field control of ferromagnet-modified PEI, as well as ligand-mediated derivatization, offer opportunities for enhanced targeting efficiency through either improved delivery performance or greater cost effectiveness.

“Smart” PEI derivativesreactive, modified polymersselected in this study have already demonstrated efficient delivery, with promising controlled release both in vitro and in vivo. They can be further modified for dual- or multistimuli-responsive designs, expanding the toolbox for targeted RNA delivery and enabling precise control over spatiotemporal release. These findings highlight polycationic platforms, such as PEI, as powerful and readily modifiable scaffolds for a broad range of RNA delivery systems, with high potential for clinical translation and scalable manufacturing.
