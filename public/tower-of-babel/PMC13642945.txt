SGI-7079 as a novel type I FLT3 inhibitor overcomes resistance to gilteritinib and quizartinib mediated by ITD and TKD mutations in acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Zhiwei Zhong, Miner Xie, Shiyang Wang, Jiajun He, Jiaqi Fan, Xiaoqing Bai, Tingfen Deng, Caixia Wang, Wei Zhou, Shunqing Wang, Peihong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFLT3-ITD mutations are among the most frequent mutations in acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and are associated with poor prognosis and high relapse rates. Despite extensive use of FLT3 inhibitors, resistance caused by secondary TKD mutations remains a major challenge. Therefore, developing next-generation FLT3 inhibitors with broad-spectrum activity against drug-resistant mutations is of considerable clinical value. We systematically evaluated the antileukemic activity of a novel type I FLT3 inhibitor, SGI-7079, in the context of FLT3-ITD and secondary resistance mutations. SGI-7079 inhibited the proliferation of AML cell lines harboring FLT3-ITD and multiple resistant mutations, including D835Y/V/F, Y842C, and F691L, in vitro. Mechanistically, SGI-7079 bound stably to FLT3, inhibited FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream STAT5, AKT, and ERK signaling, and induced G1-phase arrest and apoptosis. In FLT3-ITD leukemia mouse models, including Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD and MOLM13 cell–derived xenografts, SGI-7079 reduced leukemia burden and splenic infiltration without significant toxicity. Notably, it overcame gilteritinib resistance in FLT3-ITD-F691L and quizartinib resistance in FLT3-ITD-D835Y in vivo. SGI-7079 also exhibited potent activity in primary AML cells from FLT3-ITD-positive patients, outperforming gilteritinib and quizartinib. These findings demonstrate that SGI-7079 provides broad-spectrum inhibition across diverse FLT3 mutation contexts, with distinct advantages against drug-resistant mutations, particularly F691L, highlighting its potential for clinical translation.Subject terms: Cancer, Drug discovery, Oncology

Introduction

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is a malignant hematological disorder characterized by uncontrolled proliferation and differentiation blockade of myeloid progenitors, resulting in notable heterogeneity and aggressive behavior1,2. Although targeted therapies and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation have improved outcomes in selected patients, the overall cure rate remains low, and relapse with drug resistance continues to pose significant clinical challenges3,4. FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is mutated in approximately 30% of AML cases, making it one of the most frequent genetic lesions5. It is closely linked to disease progression, disease relapse, and poor clinical prognosis6,7. FLT3 mutations are generally divided into two categories. The first type, internal tandem duplication (FLT3-ITD) mutations, occurs in ~20–30% of AML cases and affects the juxtamembrane region, disrupting the autoinhibitory conformation of the enzyme, inducing ligand-independent receptor dimerization, and persistently activating the STAT5, PI3K/AKT, and MAPK/ERK pathways, thereby promoting leukemic cell proliferation, expediting disease progression, and leading to poor prognosis8–10. The second type, tyrosine kinase domain (FLT3-TKD) mutations, accounts for approximately 5–10% of cases and mainly involves substitutions at the D835 residue or the gatekeeper residue F691 within the kinase domain11–13. These mutations frequently arise as secondary resistance mechanisms following treatment with a FLT3 inhibitor13,14. However, their prognostic significance remains inconsistent across studies14,15.

FLT3 tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) represent significant therapeutic targets for the management of AML16. First-generation FLT3 inhibitors such as tandutinib, lestaurtinib, sunitinib, midostaurin, and sorafenib are predominantly multitarget tyrosine kinase inhibitors17. Although these agents have improved the prognosis of newly diagnosed patients when combined with chemotherapy, their low selectivity leads to dose limitations, adverse side effects, and difficulty in effectively overcoming drug-resistant mutations18. Second-generation FLT3 inhibitors, including gilteritinib, quizartinib, and crenolanib, improve FLT3 targeting selectivity, effectively target specific drug-resistant mutations, and show enhanced effectiveness in patients with relapsed or refractory AML19–21. Gilteritinib is currently approved as a monotherapy for patients with relapsed or refractory FLT3-mutated AML, leading to a significant extension of overall survival22. FLT3 inhibitors are categorized into Type I and Type II inhibitors on the basis of their binding to various conformations of the FLT3 kinase16. Type I inhibitors, such as gilteritinib and crenolanib, interact with the active conformation of the kinase, effectively inhibiting ITD and TKD mutations23. In contrast, Type II inhibitors, such as quizartinib, attach to the inactive conformation, focusing primarily on ITD mutations while exhibiting limited inhibition of TKD mutations24. Despite the enhanced effectiveness of second-generation inhibitors, the emergence of drug resistance poses a substantial limitation to their prolonged application. Secondary resistance mutations, in particular the gatekeeper mutation F691L and the kinase domain D835 mutation, represent the principal mechanisms responsible for the failure of various FLT3 inhibitors25. The toxic side effects and pharmacokinetic limitations of certain inhibitors limit their prolonged clinical application16,26,27. Consequently, the development of novel FLT3 inhibitors capable of overcoming resistance mutations is critically important for increasing treatment effectiveness and improving clinical outcomes in AML patients.

SGI-7079 is a novel tyrosine kinase inhibitor initially designed to target the AXL receptor tyrosine kinase. It has demonstrated significant antitumor activity in multiple solid tumor models, including breast cancer and non-small cell lung cancer models, and has the potential to overcome resistance to EGFR inhibitors28–30. SGI-7079 has been reported to inhibit FLT3 kinase activity, highlighting its potential therapeutic value in FLT3-mutated AML30. However, its antileukemic effectiveness in AML has not yet been fully characterized and remains to be systematically evaluated.

This study is the first to demonstrate that SGI-7079, which is a potent type I FLT3 inhibitor, exhibits broad and significant inhibitory activity against FLT3-ITD and multiple TKD-resistant mutations. In vitro experiments demonstrated that SGI-7079 inhibited the proliferation of AML cells harboring FLT3-ITD and resistance mutations, accompanied by the suppression of FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream STAT5, AKT, and ERK signaling, as well as G1-phase arrest and apoptosis. In vivo studies demonstrated that SGI-7079 significantly prolonged survival in mouse models bearing various FLT3 mutations, with particularly high effectiveness against the F691L mutation. Furthermore, compared with gilteritinib and quizartinib, SGI-7079 exhibited superior inhibitory activity in primary AML cells from patients with FLT3-ITD mutations. In summary, SGI-7079 exhibits broad and notable anti-FLT3-mutant AML activity, offering a promising therapeutic option for patients resistant to FLT3 inhibitors.

Results

SGI-7079 exhibits notable antileukemic activity in AML cell lines harboring FLT3-ITD mutations in vitro

To determine the antileukemic effectiveness of SGI-7079, we first assessed its antiproliferative effect on various AML cell lines with FLT3-ITD mutations in vitro. SGI-7079 significantly inhibited the proliferation of MOLM13 and MV4-11 cells, with IC50 values of 1.93 nM and 0.77 nM, respectively, while showing limited inhibitory effect on AML cell lines expressing wild-type FLT3, including U937, Kasumi-1, OCI-AML3, THP-1, K562, and HL60 (Fig. 1A). In Ba/F3 cells expressing FLT3-ITD, SGI-7079 also showed potent growth-inhibitory activity, whereas parental Ba/F3 cells lacking FLT3-ITD were markedly less sensitive, indicating mutation-dependent activity (Fig. 1B). Notably, the antileukemic activity of SGI-7079 was comparable to that of quizartinib and markedly stronger than that of gilteritinib (Fig. S1A–C). To explore the molecular mechanisms through which SGI-7079 inhibits FLT3-ITD-positive cell proliferation, RNA sequencing was performed after treatment with SGI-7079. Differential expression analysis revealed 1863 upregulated and 642 downregulated genes in MOLM13 cells and 2689 upregulated and 1112 downregulated genes in MV4-11 cells (Fig. S1D–F). GO enrichment analysis and KEGG pathway analysis revealed significant enrichment in biological processes such as the cell cycle (Fig. S1G), which was confirmed by cell cycle analysis involving flow cytometry, which revealed that SGI-7079 increased the proportion of G1 phase cells but reduced the proportion of cells in the S and G2/M phases in MOLM13 and MV4-11 cells (Fig. 1C, D). Furthermore, SGI-7079 induced dose-dependent apoptosis in MOLM13 and MV4-11 cells (Fig. 1E–G), accompanied by the concurrent activation of the apoptotic protein PARP1 and cleavage of caspase-8 (Fig. 1H, I). These findings indicate that SGI-7079 inhibits FLT3-ITD-positive AML cell proliferation through the induction of G1 phase arrest and apoptosis.

SGI-7079 binds stably to the FLT3 protein and suppresses downstream oncogenic signaling

Molecular docking was performed to predict the binding characteristics between SGI-7079 and FLT3. The chemical structure of SGI-7079 is shown in Fig. 2A. Virtual molecular docking predicted a stable binding mode of SGI-7079 with FLT3, suggesting its high affinity for FLT3. Structural analysis revealed that SGI-7079, which is a type I kinase inhibitor, stably binds to the DFG-in conformation of the FLT3 kinase (PDB: 6JQR). Among these, the pyrrolo[2,3-d] pyrimidine skeleton forms three stable hydrogen bond interactions with the key residues Glu692 and Cys694 in the hinge region, serving as the key structural basis for maintaining high-affinity binding and pharmacological activity. Additionally, the benzene ring connected to the pyrimidine ring is oriented toward the DFG motif region, and the meso-substituted cyano group can form favorable hydrogen bond interactions with Asp698, further enhancing conformational stability. Moreover, the N-methylpiperazine group connected to the fluorobenzene group is oriented toward the solvent-exposed region, which helps improve the overall solubility and target binding capacity. Gatekeeper site mutations at Phe691 (e.g., F691L) are common mechanisms of acquired resistance to FLT3 inhibitors. The impact of this mutation on SGI-7079 binding was further assessed by point mutation simulations. The results revealed that the F691L mutation did not significantly alter SGI-7079 localization in the FLT3 active site or disrupt key hydrogen bonds. Binding to Glu692, Cys694, and Asp698 remained robust, with the binding conformation largely unchanged (Fig. 2B). This structural insight suggests its potential to overcome FLT3-ITD-F691L-mediated resistance. A cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) revealed that compared with DMSO treatment, SGI-7079 treatment significantly increased FLT3 stability across temperature gradients, with a notable thermal shift in melting curves (Fig. 2C, D), confirming stable complex formation and strong target binding.

FLT3 activation promotes leukemogenesis primarily by driving the constitutive phosphorylation of downstream signaling cascades, including STAT5, PI3K/AKT, and MAPK/ERK, thereby facilitating the uncontrolled proliferation and survival of AML cells7. To determine whether SGI-7079 directly suppresses FLT3 activity and its downstream pathways, we examined protein phosphorylation levels following drug treatment. Western blot analysis demonstrated that SGI-7079 treatment markedly decreased FLT3 phosphorylation levels in MOLM13, MV4-11, and Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD cells, which was accompanied by significant reductions in the phosphorylation levels of STAT5, AKT, and ERK (Fig. 2E–G). In addition, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that the PI3K/Akt and MAPK signaling pathways were significantly enriched in the downregulated genes (Fig. 2H, I). Overall, these results indicated that SGI-7079 strongly inhibited FLT3 kinase activity and therefore disrupted FLT3 downstream signaling pathways.

SGI-7079 demonstrates potent antileukemic activity in FLT3-ITD AML mouse model

To evaluate the in vivo antileukemic efficacy of SGI-7079, both Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD and MOLM13 leukemia mouse models were established. For the Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD model, GFP-labeled cells were intravenously injected into BALB/c mice, which were then randomly assigned to four groups receiving SGI-7079, gilteritinib, quizartinib, or vehicle control (Fig. 3A). Flow cytometric analysis of peripheral blood (PB), bone marrow (BM), and spleen revealed a marked reduction in GFP-positive leukemia cells across all treatment groups, with SGI-7079 showing significant inhibitory effects compared with controls (Figs. 3B–D and S2A–C). Consistently, spleen weights were significantly decreased in SGI-7079-treated mice (Fig. 3E, F). H&E staining showed no overt tissue damage or abnormal leukemic infiltration in the liver or spleen, similar to the findings in the quizartinib and gilteritinib groups (Fig. 3G).

In parallel, a MOLM13 cell line–derived xenograft (CDX) mouse model was established to further evaluate the antileukemic activity of SGI-7079. Mice were intravenously engrafted with MOLM13 cells and randomly assigned to different experimental groups according to the schedule shown in Fig. 3H. Consistent with these findings, SGI-7079-treated mice exhibited reduced spleen sizes compared with controls, indicating decreased leukemic infiltration (Fig. 3I–J). Flow cytometry analysis of PB, BM, and spleen further demonstrated substantial reductions in leukemia cell burden following SGI-7079 administration, confirming potent antileukemic effects (Figs. 3K–M and S3A–C). Comprehensive hematologic evaluation showed that white blood cell counts (WBC), red blood cell counts (RBC), neutrophils, hemoglobin (HGB), and platelet (PLT) levels remained within normal ranges (Fig. S4A–E), and body weight monitoring revealed no significant adverse effects during treatment (Fig. S4F).

Collectively, these results indicate that SGI-7079 effectively suppresses leukemia progression in vivo and exhibits comparable antileukemic efficacy to clinically approved agents, including quizartinib and gilteritinib, in FLT3-ITD AML mouse models, while demonstrating a favorable safety profile.

SGI-7079 exhibits broad-spectrum activity against AML cell lines bearing FLT3-ITD secondary resistance mutations in vitro

To determine whether SGI-7079 notably influences FLT3-ITD secondary resistance mutations, Ba/F3 cell lines bearing FLT3-ITD secondary resistance mutations, including F691L, D835V, D835Y, D835F, and Y842C, were established and subjected to a cell viability assay. SGI-7079 notably inhibited a broad spectrum of FLT3 mutants, including D835Y/V/F, Y842C, and F691L, demonstrating superior effectiveness compared with gilteritinib and quizartinib (Figs. 4A–F and S5A). Western blot analysis revealed that SGI-7079 markedly inhibited the phosphorylation of FLT3 and downstream signaling effectors, including STAT5, AKT, and ERK (Fig. 4G–K), indicating effective blockade of FLT3-mediated signaling in AML cell lines with FLT3-ITD secondary mutations. These results indicate that SGI-7079 effectively inhibits FLT3-ITD secondary resistance mutations and blocks downstream signaling, supporting its potential to overcome FLT3-mediated drug resistance in AML.

SGI-7079 effectively reduces leukemia burden and prolongs survival in FLT3-ITD-F691L and D835Y mouse models

To evaluate the in vivo effectiveness of SGI-7079 against resistance induced by FLT3-ITD secondary mutations, Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD-F691L mouse models were established, as illustrated in Fig. 5A. In the F691L model, quizartinib had a minimal effect on leukemia cells in peripheral blood, while gilteritinib had a partial effect. In contrast, SGI-7079 markedly decreased the number of GFP-positive leukemia cells in the peripheral blood, bone marrow, and spleen, indicating its superior antileukemic activity (Figs. 5B–D and S6A–C). Similarly, the spleen weight was significantly lower in the SGI-7079 group than in all the other treatment groups (Fig. 5E, F). Histological analysis by H&E staining revealed substantially reduced leukemic infiltration in the liver and spleen following SGI-7079 treatment, with the tissue architecture largely preserved (Fig. 5G). The median survival duration was significantly longer (23 days) in the SGI-7079 group than in the gilteritinib and quizartinib groups (16 and 13 days, respectively) (Fig. 5H).

Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD-D835Y mouse models were established to evaluate the antileukemic activity of SGI-7079 (Fig. 6A). In this model, quizartinib moderately reduced the leukemia burden, whereas gilteritinib effectively reduced leukemia cell levels. Notably, SGI-7079 markedly decreased the number of GFP-positive leukemia cells in peripheral blood, bone marrow, and the spleen, overcoming quizartinib resistance and demonstrating robust disease control (Figs. 6B–D and S7A–C). Compared with that in the other treatment groups, the spleen weight in the SGI-7079 group was significantly lower, and histological analysis revealed markedly reduced leukemic infiltration in the liver and spleen, with preserved tissue architecture (Fig. 6E–G). The median survival was extended following SGI-7079 treatment, and the outcomes were comparable to those following treatment with gilteritinib (Fig. 6H).

In summary, SGI-7079 demonstrated significant antileukemic effectiveness in both the F691L and D835Y mutant models, particularly in the F691L mutant background, which is highly resistant to existing FLT3-TKIs, revealing its promise as a novel FLT3-targeted therapy.

SGI-7079 significantly inhibited primary cells from patients with FLT3-ITD-mutant AML

To further evaluate the activity of SGI-7079 in primary samples, bone marrow cells obtained from nine AML patients and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from three healthy donors were examined. Detailed sample information is provided in Supplementary Table S1. Cell viability assays demonstrated that SGI-7079 effectively inhibited the proliferation of primary AML cells harboring FLT3-ITD mutations. In six independent patient samples, SGI-7079 consistently exhibited stronger inhibitory activity than gilteritinib and quizartinib (Fig. 7A–F). Dose–response analyses were performed to determine the IC50 values (Fig. 7G), which were markedly lower for SGI-7079 than for either gilteritinib or quizartinib, indicating greater potency against FLT3-ITD–driven leukemic cells.

To evaluate selectivity and potential toxicity in non-mutated hematopoietic cells, SGI-7079 was also tested in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from three healthy donors and bone marrow mononuclear cells from three AML patients with wild-type FLT3 (FLT3-WT). SGI-7079 showed minimal effect on PBMCs and only modest inhibitory activity in FLT3-WT AML cells (Fig. 7H–I), indicating preferential activity toward FLT3-ITD–mutant cells and a favorable safety profile in non-mutated hematopoietic cells.

Discussion

FLT3 mutations are among the most frequent genetic alterations in AML and are closely linked to disease aggressiveness and adverse prognosis31–33. Although FLT3 inhibitors such as gilteritinib and quizartinib are clinically approved, their effectiveness is compromised by secondary resistance, notably the F691L gatekeeper residue and D835 kinase domain mutations34. These mutations induce conformational changes in the kinase domain, significantly reducing drug binding affinity and posing challenges to effective treatment.

This study is the first to systematically assess the antileukemic activity of the novel Type I FLT3 inhibitor SGI-7079 in FLT3-mutated AML. SGI-7079 exhibited nanomolar inhibitory potency against FLT3-ITD-positive cells, with IC50 values significantly lower than those of gilteritinib. Mechanistic investigations revealed that SGI-7079 significantly decreased p-FLT3 levels and inhibited the phosphorylation of the downstream signaling molecules STAT5, AKT, and ERK, which induced G1 phase arrest and programmed cell death. Transcriptomic analysis further revealed significant enrichment of genes differentially expressed in the cell cycle and survival pathways following SGI-7079 treatment, suggesting that its antileukemic effects are mediated through the regulation of these pathways. These findings align with previously reported FLT3-dependent AML survival mechanisms, further validating the use of SGI-7079 as an FLT3-targeted inhibitor 10,15,31,35.

Notably, SGI-7079 has distinct advantages in overcoming TKD resistance mutations. The F691L mutation, located at the gatekeeper residue, poses the greatest clinical challenge for FLT3 inhibitors because it alters kinase pocket conformation and reduces drug binding affinity, significantly decreasing the effectiveness of gilteritinib and quizartinib24. Molecular docking demonstrated that SGI-7079 forms a stable hydrogen bond network with key FLT3 active site residues (Glu692, Cys694, and Asp698) and that the F691L mutation does not significantly affect this binding conformation, indicating intrinsic resistance to F691L-mediated resistance. This binding mode underlies its resistance mechanism and may explain its superior effectiveness over traditional inhibitors in resistant models. The CETSA results confirmed the enhanced thermal stability of FLT3 upon binding to SGI-7079, supporting stable complex formation. To systematically assess the activity of SGI-7079 against secondary resistance, Ba/F3 models with common resistance mutations (F691L, D835Y/F/V, Y842C) were constructed. These cell models closely mimic the acquired mutation environment commonly observed after TKI treatment in clinical settings24,36. SGI-7079 demonstrated significant inhibitory effects across all the tested mutants at nanomolar concentrations, showing clear superiority over both gilteritinib and quizartinib. Mechanistic analyses revealed that SGI-7079 effectively inhibited FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream signaling across resistant mutants, validating its broad-spectrum resistance activity.

SGI-7079 exhibited robust antileukemic activity in vivo. In Ba/F3 leukemia mouse models bearing FLT3-ITD mutations, treatment markedly reduced leukemic cell infiltration in peripheral blood, bone marrow, and spleen and significantly prolonged median survival. Consistent results were observed in a MOLM13 cell line–derived xenograft (CDX) model, where SGI-7079 decreased leukemic burden, improved hematologic parameters, and maintained stable body weight, indicating favorable tolerability. Furthermore, SGI-7079 effectively suppressed leukemia in Ba/F3 models harboring clinically relevant secondary resistance mutations, FLT3-ITD-F691L and FLT3-ITD-D835Y, overcoming resistance to gilteritinib and quizartinib and highlighting its potential to target both primary FLT3-ITD–driven disease and therapy-associated resistance.

To extend these findings to a clinically relevant context, SGI-7079 was evaluated in primary AML cells derived from FLT3-ITD patients. SGI-7079 inhibited the proliferation of these patient-derived cells at low nanomolar concentrations, demonstrating greater effectiveness than gilteritinib and quizartinib. In parallel, SGI-7079 was tested on peripheral blood mononuclear cells from three healthy donors and bone marrow mononuclear cells from three FLT3-wild-type AML patients, showing minimal inhibitory effects. These results reinforce the clinical relevance of the antileukemic activity of SGI-7079 in vitro, highlight its preferential activity toward FLT3-ITD–mutant cells, and support a favorable therapeutic window for precision-targeted application in FLT3-ITD–positive AML.

Currently approved FLT3 inhibitors show limited effectiveness against certain kinase conformations and resistance-associated mutations, particularly specific TKD mutations18. The Type II inhibitor quizartinib binds only the inactive conformation, effectively inhibiting FLT3-ITD but displaying minimal activity against TKD mutations such as D835Y and F691L37,38. The type I inhibitor gilteritinib retains activity against ITD and some D835 mutations but markedly reduces effectiveness against the F691L gatekeeper mutation39. In contrast, SGI-7079, a novel type I FLT3 inhibitor, binds the active FLT3 conformation, maintaining potent inhibition of ITD mutations and broad coverage of clinically relevant TKD mutations. In this study, we systematically evaluated SGI-7079 in vivo, in vitro, and in primary AML samples and demonstrated high effectiveness against the ITD, F691L, and D835Y mutations. These results highlight the potential of SGI-7079 to overcome FLT3 inhibitor resistance in AML and suggest a clear therapeutic advantage over currently approved FLT3 inhibitors.

This study represents the first systematic evaluation of the antileukemic activity of SGI-7079 in vivo, in vitro, and in primary bone marrow samples from AML patients. SGI-7079 notably inhibited both ITD and key TKD resistance mutations, including F691L and D835Y. Notably, it overcomes resistance to gilteritinib mediated by FLT3-ITD-F691L and remains effective against FLT3-D835Y mutations that are resistant to quizartinib, highlighting its clear advantage over currently approved FLT3 inhibitors. Nevertheless, the patient-derived experiments in this study had a relatively limited sample size, comprising bone marrow samples from nine AML patients and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from three healthy donors. Although these results support the antileukemic activity and selectivity of SGI-7079, the limited number of clinical samples may not fully represent the broader AML patient population. Therefore, larger studies incorporating additional patient samples will be required to further validate the efficacy and broader applicability of SGI-7079.

Taken together, our findings demonstrate that SGI-7079 is a potent type I FLT3 inhibitor with broad activity against both ITD and key TKD mutations. Importantly, SGI-7079 effectively overcomes resistance associated with gilteritinib and quizartinib, highlighting its potential as a promising therapeutic candidate for AML with FLT3-driven resistance.

Methods

AML patient samples and primary cell culture

Bone marrow (BM) samples were collected from nine AML patients, and peripheral blood from three healthy donors (participant details in Supplementary Table S1). Primary leukemia cells were isolated by density gradient centrifugation using Lymphoprep reagent and cultured in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First People’s Hospital of Guangzhou City (Approval No. K-2023-169).

Cell culture and reagents

The AML cell lines MOLM13, MV4-11, Kasumi-1, OCI-AML3, THP-1, U937, HL-60, and K562 were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gemini) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. Ba/F3 cells and derivatives harboring FLT3 mutations (FLT3-ITD, FLT3-ITD-F691L, FLT3-ITD-D835Y, FLT3-ITD-D835V, and FLT3-ITD-Y842C) were generated via retroviral infection as previously described36,40. SGI-7079, gilteritinib, and quizartinib were purchased from TargetMol (Boston, USA). For in vitro assays, compounds were prepared as 10 mM stock solutions in DMSO, stored at –20 °C, and diluted to working concentrations with final DMSO concentrations less than 0.1%. For animal studies, gilteritinib was dissolved in 5% DMSO, 35% PEG300, 10% Tween 80, and 50% sterile water, while quizartinib was dissolved in 0.5% methylcellulose, and SGI-7079 was dissolved in 5% DMSO, 40% PEG300, and 55% (w/v) 30% cyclodextrin solution.

Cell proliferation inhibition assay

To assess the inhibitory effect of drugs on cell proliferation, AML cells were seeded at a density of 3 × 10³ cells/well in 96-well plates and cultured in drug-containing medium for 48 h. SGI-7079, gilteritinib, and quizartinib were applied at predetermined concentrations, with three replicate wells per group. The CellTiter-Glo® 2.0 cell viability assay kit (Promega) was subsequently used following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Cell cycle analysis

The cell cycle distribution was assessed by propidium iodide (PI) staining followed by flow cytometry analysis. MV4-11 and MOLM13 cells were seeded at 2.0 × 10⁵ cells/mL and exposed to SGI-7079 at predetermined concentrations for 24 h. Following treatment, the cells were harvested, washed twice with PBS, and fixed overnight at 4 °C in 70% prechilled ethanol. Fixed cells were subsequently washed once more with PBS and resuspended in a staining solution containing RNase A (100 μg/mL) and propidium iodide (PI, 50 μg/mL), followed by incubation at 37 °C in the dark for 30 min. DNA content was immediately analyzed by flow cytometry.

Cell apoptosis analysis

MV4-11 and MOLM13 cells were seeded at a density of 2.0 × 105 cells/mL in 6-well plates and treated with different concentrations of SGI-7079 for 48 h. After treatment, the cells were washed twice with PBS and stained using an Annexin V-APC/PI apoptosis detection kit (BD Biosciences) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, the cells were resuspended in binding buffer, and Annexin V-APC and propidium iodide (PI) were added. The mixture was incubated at room temperature in the dark for 15 min. Stained samples were immediately analyzed by flow cytometry.

Western blot analysis

Cells treated with SGI-7079 were harvested at the indicated time points and washed twice with ice-cold PBS, after which total protein was extracted using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS‒PAGE and transferred onto NC membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat dry milk in TBST for 1 h and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies. The primary antibodies used included anti-p-FLT3 (#3464, CST), anti-FLT3 (#3462, CST), anti-p-STAT5 (#9351, CST), anti-STAT5 (#9363, CST), anti-p-ERK1/2 (#4370, CST), anti-ERK1/2 (#4695, CST), anti-p-AKT (#4060, CST), anti-AKT (#9272, CST), anti-PARP1 (#13371-1-AP, Proteintech), and anti-Caspase-8 (#13423-1-AP, Proteintech), with HRP-conjugated anti-α-Tubulin (#HRP-66031, Proteintech) used as a loading control. The membranes were subsequently washed three times with TBST and incubated with secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. ECL reagents were used for chemiluminescence detection. Western blot images were acquired using ChemiDoc MP (Bio-Rad), and band intensities were quantified with ImageJ software and normalized to the loading control.

RNA sequencing and bioinformatic analysis

To systematically elucidate the transcriptional regulatory mechanisms of SGI-7079 in FLT3-ITD-mutant AML cells, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis of MV4-11 and MOLM13 cells was performed. Total RNA was extracted from cells treated with 10 nM SGI-7079 for 24 h, after which RNA quality and integrity were assessed. After library construction, paired-end sequencing was conducted on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. The raw sequencing data were subjected to quality control, and adapter sequences and low-quality reads were removed. Clean high-quality reads were aligned to the human reference genome (hg38), and gene quantification was performed using featureCounts. Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 with thresholds of |log₂Fold Change| > 1 and FDR < 0.05. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were further annotated using Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses to identify key biological processes and pathways potentially regulated by SGI-7079.

Molecular docking

The 2D structure of SGI-7079 was retrieved from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), and its 3D structure was generated using the LigPrep module in Schrödinger software. Geometric optimization was conducted using the OPLS_4 force field, while the Epik module generated possible protonation states and conformers. The FLT3 crystal structure (PDB ID: 6JQR) was obtained from the PDB database and preprocessed with the Protein Preparation Wizard, including hydrogen addition, side chain repair, hydrogen bond network optimization, removal of water molecules and cofactors, and energy minimization using the OPLS_4 force field. Molecular docking was then performed using the Glide module. The binding pocket was defined on the basis of the original ligand, with an inner box size of 10 Å and an outer box size matching the ligand. Docking was performed in standard precision (SP) mode, and binding affinity was assessed using GlideScore, where lower scores indicate stronger binding, suggesting that SGI-7079 may exhibit high affinity and stability toward FLT3.

Cellular thermal shift assay

Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD cells (1 × 10⁷) were treated with 1 μM SGI-7079 or an equivalent volume of DMSO for 1 h, then collected, washed with PBS, resuspended in PBS containing protease inhibitors (TargetMol), and aliquoted into multiple tubes. The samples were heated for 3 min across a temperature gradient from 37 °C to 54 °C and then cooled to room temperature, followed by lysing with liquid nitrogen to extract soluble proteins. The protein samples were analyzed by Western blotting to assess the FLT3 expression levels.

In vivo effectiveness study

All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Greater Bay Area Institute of Precision Medicine (Guangzhou) (Approval No. IPMGBA-25-0026) and conducted in accordance with relevant ethical guidelines. Eight-week-old female BALB/c mice were chosen for the experiments. Leukemic mouse models were established via tail vein injection of 5 × 10⁵ GFP-labeled Ba/F3 cells harboring FLT3-ITD, FLT3-ITD-F691L, and FLT3-ITD-D835Y mutations. After being injected for two days, the mice were randomly divided into four groups and treated with SGI-7079 (60 mg/kg, intraperitoneally), gilteritinib (30 mg/kg, orally), quizartinib (10 mg/kg, orally), or vehicle control for 8 consecutive days. Health status and mortality were monitored regularly during treatment. At the end of the experiment, peripheral blood (PB), bone marrow (BM), and the spleen were collected. The proportion of GFP-positive leukemia cells was analyzed by flow cytometry, and the spleen weight was measured. Liver and spleen tissues were fixed in formalin, paraffin-embedded, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histopathological analysis. Survival was analyzed by the Kaplan‒Meier method to compare survival times and treatment effects between groups.

In addition, a MOLM13 cell line–derived xenograft model was established by tail vein injection of 5 × 10⁶ cells into NOG mice. Seven days after inoculation, mice were randomly assigned to the same treatment groups and dosing regimens as described above and treated for 7 consecutive days. At the end of treatment, PB, BM, and spleen were collected to assess leukemia burden. Peripheral blood counts were measured at baseline (day 0) and day 6 after treatment initiation. Body weight was recorded at days 0, 2, 4, and 6 during treatment to monitor potential toxicity.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism version 10.4.1. Two-tailed paired or unpaired t tests were used to compare group differences as appropriate on the basis of the experimental design. The data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Survival analysis was performed using Kaplan–Meier curves, and differences were evaluated by the log-rank test. Statistical significance was set at four levels: P < 0.05 (*), P < 0.01 (**), P < 0.001 (***), and P < 0.0001 (****).
