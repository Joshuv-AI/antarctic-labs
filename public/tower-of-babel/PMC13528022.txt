The relationship between depression, falls, dizziness, and physical activity in geriatric individuals with BPPV
Authors: Deniz Uğur Cengi̇z, Sanem Can Sarioğlu, Buşra Mazooğlu, Feyza İnceoğlu, Mehmet Aslan, Funda Datli Yakaryilmaz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBenign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) represents the most common cause of vertigo in the geriatric population. The condition constitutes a multidimensional clinical presentation characterized by dizziness, imbalance, fear of falling, limitations in activities of daily living, and emotional and functional impairment. The aim of this study was to subjectively evaluate the relationships between dizziness and physical function, activities of daily living, fear of falling, emotional status, and depressive symptoms in geriatric individuals diagnosed with BPPV.MethodsThe study included 52 individuals aged 60 years and older who had been diagnosed with BPPV and 52 healthy individuals in the same age group. The diagnostic process was monitored using Videonystagmography (VNG). Following confirmation of diagnosis and prior to any therapeutic repositioning maneuvers, all outcome measures were administered in the pre-treatment assessment phase. During this phase, the Yesavage Geriatric Depression Scale-Short Form (GDS-15), the Activities-specific Balance Confidence Scale (ABC), the European Evaluation of Vertigo Scale (EEV), the Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI), and the Falls Behavioral Scale for Older Adults (FaB) were administered.Statistical analyses were performed to evaluate the data. Normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test.Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and categorical variables as frequency (%). Independent samples t-test and chi-square test were used for group comparisons. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Internal consistency was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient.ResultsNo significant difference was observed in the total GDS-15 score (p > 0.05). In contrast, statistically significant differences were identified between the groups in the total scores of the ABC, EEV, DHI, and FaB, as well as in the FaB subdomains of safe mobility, avoidance, awareness, practical strategies, detection of level changes, and reaching the telephone (p < 0.05).ConclusionsBalance confidence, dizziness-related disability, and fall-related behaviors were found to be significantly adversely affected in geriatric patients with BPPV compared with healthy controls.Keywords: BPPV, Geriatric, Falls, Physical activity, Depression

Introduction

The balance system constitutes one of the most fundamental survival mechanisms in human life and involves the maintenance of posture and the ability to move without falling [1].

Vertigo is a common symptom of vestibular system disorders and is defined as a type of movement hallucination in which individuals perceive surrounding objects as spinning or experience a sensation of self-rotation when the eyes are closed [2]. Dizziness and imbalance represent some of the most common reasons for hospital admission and occur in 20%-30% of the general population. Within the older geriatric population, balance disorders and dizziness are classified among chronic and prevalent conditions, with a prevalence of approximately 30% in individuals aged 60 years and older and increasing to as high as 50% in those over 85 years of age [3].

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is the most common peripheral vestibular disorder and is characterized by sudden onset, head position-triggered, recurrent episodes of vertigo lasting seconds and accompanied by nystagmus [4–6]. BPPV may substantially impair quality of life, particularly in geriatric individuals, leading to limitations in activities of daily living, falls, and depression [7, 8]. Individuals may avoid leaving home, restrict social participation, and refrain from specific physical activities due to fear of experiencing recurrent attacks. This avoidance pattern adversely affects not only physical health but also emotional and psychological well-being [7, 9]. Falls are particularly associated with substantial morbidity and mortality in geriatric individuals and may result in complications such as fractures, hospitalizations, and loss of independent living [8, 9]. Dizziness and imbalance should therefore be regarded not only as individual concerns but also as critical risk factors from a community and public health perspective. Evaluation and management of vestibular disorders require careful assessment of quality of life, functional capacity, and perceived health status as reported by patients [10, 11]. The present study was designed to examine the multidimensional relationships between dizziness and depression, fall-related behaviors, and activities of daily living in geriatric patients with BPPV.

Materials and methods

Research design

A descriptive cross-sectional study design was employed to obtain information regarding balance, fall risk, activity level, and fall prevention behaviors in geriatric individuals diagnosed with BPPV.

Individuals

The study sample included individuals aged 60 years and older who had been diagnosed with BPPV in the Otorhinolaryngology outpatient clinic. All BPPV cases were first-episode patients, and individuals with recurrent BPPV were not included. The Standardized Mini-Mental State Examination was administered to exclude participants with cognitive impairment, and all eligible individuals provided informed consent to participate. Eligibility criteria included absence of communication impairment, physical disability, history of neurological disorder, cognitive impairment, chronic disease, psychiatric disorder, sleep disorder, visual impairment, or hearing loss complaints. Sample size was determined through power analysis. Based on calculations performed using the G*Power 3.1 program, a minimum sample size of 90 participants (45 per group) was required, assuming an effect size of 0.70, a margin of error of 0.05, a confidence level of 0.95, and a statistical power of 0.95 [12]. The final sample comprised 104 participants, including 52 geriatric individuals diagnosed with BPPV in the case group and 52 healthy geriatric individuals of a similar age range in the control group who met the inclusion criteria and agreed to participate.

Data collection

The study was conducted between May 2024 and January 2025 in the Audiology Unit of a university-affiliated hospital. Participants were diagnosed with BPPV based on the Bárány Society diagnostic criteria, including a clinical history of positional vertigo and confirmation with videonystagmography (VNG) during positional testing (Dix–Hallpike and/or supine roll test), demonstrating characteristic positional nystagmus consistent with canal involvement.

Following confirmation of diagnosis and prior to any therapeutic repositioning maneuvers, all outcome measures were administered in the pre-treatment period. During this assessment phase, the Yesavage Geriatric Depression Scale-Short Form (GDS-15), the Activities-specific Balance Confidence Scale (ABC), the European Evaluation of Vertigo Scale (EEV), the Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI), and the Falls Behavioral Scale for Older Adults (FaB) were administered by the researcher.

Data collection tools

Yesavage Geriatric Depression Scale-short form (GDS-15)

Participants were asked to complete a brief and easily administered 15-item self-report scale specifically developed to assess mood and depressive symptoms experienced during the previous week. Responses to each item were scored as 1 point for either “yes” or “no,” depending on the content of the question. Total scores ranging from 0 to 4 indicated no clinically significant depressive symptoms, scores of 5-8 indicated mild depression, scores of 9-11 indicated moderate depression, and scores of 12-15 indicated severe depression [13].

Activities-specific Balance Confidence scale (ABC)

Participants were asked to complete the 16-item ABC scale, which was developed to assess confidence in maintaining balance during activities of daily living performed both inside and outside the home. For each item, participants rated the degree of confidence experienced while performing the specified activity on a scale ranging from 0% (no confidence) to 100% (complete confidence). The total score was calculated by summing item scores and dividing the result by 16, and the value was rounded to the nearest whole number. Higher scores indicated greater balance confidence [14].

European Evaluation of Vertigo scale (EEV)

Participants were asked to complete the EEV scale, which was developed to assess the characteristics of dizziness and the presence and severity of accompanying symptoms. The scale consists of 5 items, each scored on a scale from 0 to 4. The overall score was calculated as the sum of item scores. Higher total scores indicated greater severity of dizziness symptoms [15].

Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI)

Participants were asked to complete the Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI), which is used to identify perceived disability in individuals experiencing dizziness. The instrument consists of three subscales: emotional and functional domains, each comprising 9 items, and a physical domain comprising 7 items. Responses were scored as 0 for “no,” 2 for “sometimes,” and 4 for “yes” [16].

Falls Behavioral scale for older adults (FaB)

Participants were asked to complete the FaB scale, which was developed to identify behaviors adopted to prevent falls during daily activities. The scale consists of 30 items and 10 subdomains: cognitive adaptation, safe mobility, avoidance, awareness, hastiness, practical strategies, changes in activity planning, carefulness, detection of level changes, and reaching the telephone. Total scores were calculated by summing responses to all items and dividing by the number of items, yielding a mean item score ranging from 1 to 4. Higher scores indicated safer and more protective fall-related behaviors, whereas lower scores reflected riskier behaviors [17].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.0. The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Descriptive statistics were expressed as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables and frequency (percentage) for categorical variables. Independent samples t-test was used to compare scale scores between the geriatric BPPV group and the control group. The chi-square test was applied to compare categorical variables such as sex. The level of statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. The internal consistency of the scales was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient.

Discussion

The vestibular system is responsible for maintaining postural stability and plays a critical role in enabling safe mobility, interaction with the environment, and preservation of independence in daily life [18]. In the geriatric population, impairments in the vestibular system may lead to diminished postural control, increased fall risk, and a marked reduction in physical activity levels [19, 20]. In addition to these physiological changes, the sense of insecurity accompanying balance impairment in older adults may contribute to reduced social participation and deterioration in psychological well-being [21]. BPPV is the most common peripheral vestibular disorder in the geriatric age group and may severely restrict activities of daily living due to recurrent vertigo attacks, imbalance, and dizziness [22, 23]. This condition becomes more pronounced in geriatric individuals due to age-related slowing of central adaptation mechanisms and weakening of visual-vestibular-proprioceptive integration [24].

The persistent sense of insecurity accompanying dizziness increases fear of falling in older adults and triggers movement avoidance behaviors. As a natural consequence, physical activity levels decline, independence within and outside the home becomes restricted, and social isolation may develop over time. Increased perception of physical limitation and dependency may contribute to the emergence of depressive symptoms and a marked deterioration in quality of life [25–27].

When considered comprehensively, BPPV should be addressed as a complex clinical condition that exerts multidimensional effects on fall-related behaviors, physical activity limitations, and psychological status in the geriatric population. The present study represents one of the limited investigations that evaluates the relationships among dizziness, fall-related behaviors, balance confidence, physical activities, and depression in geriatric individuals diagnosed with BPPV using a holistic approach. In this respect, the findings underscore the necessity of addressing not only symptom control but also preservation of functional capacity and psychological well-being in the clinical management of geriatric patients with BPPV, thereby contributing meaningful evidence to the existing literature.

The Yesavage Geriatric Depression Scale-Short Form (GDS-15) administered to participants enabled assessment of depressive symptoms in geriatric individuals. Analysis of the total score demonstrated a mean value of 7.21 ± 4.18 in the geriatric case group diagnosed with BPPV, whereas the control group demonstrated a mean score of 5.92 ± 2.98. Although no statistically significant difference was identified between the groups, higher depression scores observed in individuals with BPPV suggest a possible association between dizziness and depressive symptom burden in geriatric individuals. Sun et al., included 40 geriatric individuals aged 65 years and older diagnosed with BPPV and, unlike the present study, assessed depressive symptoms using the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9). Findings indicated that PHQ-9 scores obtained in the pre-treatment period were significantly higher than those measured after treatment [28].

Peluso et al., included 44 geriatric individuals aged 60–90 years with dizziness and evaluated depressive symptoms using the Brazilian version of the Composite International Diagnostic Interview 2.1. The authors reported that 52.3% of the participants were diagnosed with BPPV and that 45.4% had a depressive disorder [29]. Evaluation of these findings indicates that depressive symptoms may be present in geriatric individuals with dizziness, particularly in those with BPPV. In line with these findings, the slightly higher GDS-15 scores observed in the BPPV group, despite not reaching statistical significance, may be explained by age-related changes in vestibular compensation, reduced multisensory integration capacity, and vestibular–limbic interactions. Additionally, the exclusion of participants with psychiatric disorders, chronic diseases, sleep disturbances, and visual or hearing impairments may have resulted in a relatively healthier sample compared to previous studies, which could partly explain the absence of a significant difference in depressive symptoms.

The Yesavage Geriatric Depression Scale-Short Form (GDS-15) administered to participants enabled assessment of the presence and severity of depressive symptoms in geriatric individuals and facilitated evaluation of the impact of depression on quality of life and functional status. Analysis of the total score demonstrated a mean value of 7.21 ± 4.18 in the geriatric case group diagnosed with BPPV, whereas the control group demonstrated a mean score of 5.92 ± 2.98. Although no statistically significant difference was identified between the groups, higher depression scores observed in individuals with BPPV suggest that dizziness imposes not only physical but also emotional and psychological burdens in geriatric individuals. This finding supports the view that BPPV represents a multidimensional health condition in older adults and that psychological components should be considered in clinical management. Sun et al., included 40 geriatric individuals aged 65 years and older diagnosed with BPPV and, unlike the present study, assessed depressive symptoms using the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9). Findings indicated that PHQ-9 scores obtained in the pre-treatment period were significantly higher than those measured after treatment [28].

Peluso et al., adopted a different methodological protocol and included 44 geriatric individuals aged 60–90 years who experienced dizziness. Depressive disorders were evaluated using the Brazilian version of the Composite International Diagnostic Interview 2.1. The authors reported that 52.3% of the 44 participants were diagnosed with BPPV and that 45.4% had a depressive disorder [29]. Evaluation of these findings indicates that depressive symptoms are frequently observed in geriatric individuals experiencing dizziness, particularly among those diagnosed with BPPV. In light of these findings, higher GDS-15 scores observed in the BPPV group and generally elevated scores in both groups may be associated with age-related weakening of central vestibular compensation, reduced multisensory integration capacity, and disruption of neurophysiological regulation between vestibular and limbic networks. The findings demonstrate that BPPV creates a multifaceted clinical presentation with psychosocial dimensions in geriatric individuals and contribute an age-focused, holistic perspective to the literature.

The ABC scale administered to participants enabled assessment of self-efficacy perceptions related to maintaining balance during activities of daily living in geriatric individuals and facilitated evaluation of the relationship among fear of falling, activity limitation, and dizziness. Analysis of the total ABC score revealed a statistically significant difference between the case and control groups. The mean ABC score was 44.79 ± 21.51 in the case group comprising geriatric individuals diagnosed with BPPV, whereas a mean score of 72.54 ± 25.65 was observed in the healthy control group. The ABC score obtained in the control group is consistent with mean values reported in the literature for community-dwelling healthy geriatric individuals (79%-80%) [30, 31]. This finding suggests that BPPV is not limited to vestibular symptoms but also significantly reduces subjective confidence in the ability to move without falling during activities of daily living. Marchetti et al., used the ABC scale in a study conducted with 95 geriatric individuals aged 65 years and older who had balance or vestibular disorders to examine factors associated with balance confidence. A mean ABC score of 50.6 ± 27.5 was reported. ABC scores were shown to be strongly associated with fall history, severity of dizziness, and functional mobility capacity. The authors indicated that lower ABC scores were linked to reduced balance confidence during activities of daily living, which directly contributed to fear of falling, movement avoidance behaviors, and physical activity restriction [32]. Falls represent one of the most significant public health concerns among older adults [33]. Approximately 30% of geriatric individuals living in developed countries experience at least one fall annually, and 10%-20% report two or more falls within a year [34–36]. Falls constitute the most common mechanism of injury in the geriatric population, and major injuries such as fractures, joint dislocations, or severe head trauma develop in approximately 10% of older adults who experience a fall [37, 38]. These injuries are not limited to physical consequences; they are closely associated with pain, loss of self-confidence, activity restriction, and functional decline [37–39]. Over time, such adverse outcomes reduce the capacity to maintain independent living and increase the need for care.

The literature emphasizes that fall history is reported more frequently among older adults diagnosed with BPPV and that fear of falling leads to activity restriction, thereby adversely affecting functional independence [40, 41]. Reduced balance confidence contributes to movement avoidance behaviors and decreased physical activity levels. This process may further exacerbate functional decline. Such a vicious cycle becomes particularly evident in geriatric individuals with BPPV and substantially affects quality of life through the fall-fear-avoidance behavior axis [33–37]. Taken together, these findings underscore the clinical necessity of comprehensively evaluating fall risk and balance confidence, particularly in geriatric individuals with BPPV.

The European Evaluation of Vertigo Scale (EEV) administered to participants enabled multidimensional assessment of vertigo frequency and severity, motion intolerance, neurovegetative symptoms, instability, and functional impact on daily life, thereby facilitating evaluation of the effects of dizziness on quality of life, physical activity level, and fall-related behaviors. Analysis of the total EEV score revealed a statistically significant difference between the case and control groups. The mean EEV score was 10.96 ± 2.88 in the case group comprising geriatric individuals diagnosed with BPPV, whereas a mean score of 1.27 ± 2.32 was observed in the healthy control group. Higher EEV scores observed in the case group compared with the control group indicate that BPPV in geriatric individuals is experienced not merely as a transient vestibular episode but as a health condition that directly affects activities of daily living, movement confidence, and quality of life. Eryaman , included 52 geriatric individuals with BPPV aged 60 to 92 years (mean age: 70.7 ± 8.57) and used the EEV in the assessment. Consistent with the present findings, a pre-treatment EEV score of 11.9 was reported [42]. In this context, elevated EEV scores indicate that abnormal vestibular afferent input in BPPV triggers a multidimensional pathophysiological response mediated through vestibulospinal, vestibulocortical, and vestibuloautonomic networks. Particularly in geriatric individuals, this process, when combined with reduced central compensation capacity, indicates that symptoms extend beyond a transient mechanical stimulus and evolve into a comprehensive neurovestibular dysfunction that affects functional independence and quality of life.

The Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI) administered to participants enabled multidimensional assessment of the physical, functional, and emotional effects of dizziness on quality of life in geriatric individuals, thereby facilitating evaluation of the burden of symptoms on activities of daily living. Analysis of the subscale and total scores demonstrated that the geriatric case group diagnosed with BPPV had a mean physical subscale score of 19.42 ± 3.95, an emotional subscale score of 16.58 ± 8.46, a functional subscale score of 31.62 ± 8.18, and a total DHI score of 67.65 ± 18.46. In the control group, the mean physical subscale score was 2.04 ± 3.62, the emotional subscale score was 0.85 ± 4.56, the functional subscale score was 2.42 ± 6.58, and the total DHI score was 5.31 ± 13.61. Statistically significant differences were identified between the groups across all subscales and the total score. These findings indicate that BPPV leads to substantial limitations in activities of daily living, emotional distress, and functional disability in geriatric individuals [43, 44]. Markedly higher physical and functional subscale scores compared with the control group suggest that dizziness adversely affects safe mobility capacity and complicates the maintenance of independent living in older adults [44]. Pauwels et al., included geriatric individuals aged 65 years and older diagnosed with BPPV and reported a median total DHI score of 22 (10–62) in the case group, with subscale scores of 13 [6–22] for the physical domain, 4 [2–16] for the emotional domain, and 8 (0–24) for the functional domain. In comparison with the control group, statistically significant differences were reported across all subscales and the total score, consistent with the present findings [43]. Cengiz et al., in contrast to the present study, included 67 individuals with BPPV within a younger age range (18–60 years) and used the Dizziness Handicap Inventory for assessment. In that study, the reported mean physical subscale score was 20.38 ± 4.99, the emotional subscale score was 14.98 ± 8.38, the functional subscale score was 23.58 ± 9.16, and the total DHI score was 58.47 ± 19.20 [44].

Compared with the present findings, although physical subscale scores were similar in magnitude, markedly higher functional and total DHI scores in the geriatric population indicate that with advancing age, dizziness extends beyond symptomatic effects and evolves into a clinical condition that substantially restricts activities of daily living and threatens independence [33, 34]. Higher emotional subscale scores compared with younger adults suggest that in geriatric individuals with BPPV, age-related reductions in central vestibular compensation and diminished regulatory capacity of vestibulolimbic networks render emotional regulation mechanisms more vulnerable.

The Falls Behavioral Scale for Older Adults (FaB) administered to participants enabled identification of the frequency and characteristics of fall-related risk behaviors in geriatric individuals and facilitated evaluation of the effects of fall tendency on physical activity level, balance confidence, and quality of life. Analysis of the subscales and total scores indicated no statistically significant difference between the case and control groups in the cognitive adaptation subscale. This finding suggests that individuals with BPPV do not differ from the control group in terms of evaluating environmental risks and general cognitive adaptation behaviors. This finding indicates that fall-related behaviors may be more strongly associated with symptoms and physical limitations than with cognitive awareness [45]. In contrast, significantly higher scores were observed in the case group compared with the control group in the safe mobility, avoidance, practical strategies, detection of level changes, and reaching the telephone subscales. These findings suggest that individuals with BPPV develop more cautious, safer, and protective behaviors during walking and daily activities by recognizing fall risk [46, 47]. Significantly higher scores in the awareness subscale observed in the control group indicate that healthy older adults may have a stronger tendency to perceive environmental risks effectively and direct attention accordingly [47]. In individuals with BPPV, attentional focus may shift toward maintaining balance during episodes of vertigo and instability, potentially limiting environmental awareness. The absence of significant differences between the groups in the hastiness, changes in activity planning, and carefulness subscales suggests that these behaviors may not be directly associated with BPPV or may exhibit less variability due to established daily routines. The total FaB score was significantly higher in the case group (2.92 ± 0.48) compared with the control group (2.54 ± 0.54). Damar et al., who followed a different methodological protocol and evaluated fall prevention behaviors using the FaB in 204 hospitalized geriatric individuals, reported a higher mean total FaB score (3.06 ± 0.44) than that observed in the present study [46]. This difference may be attributed to heightened awareness of fall risk and greater adoption of fall prevention behaviors among individuals under continuous supervision by healthcare personnel in hospital settings. Similarly, Okuyan et al., applied the FaB to 124 geriatric individuals residing in a nursing home, 81.5% of whom had at least one chronic condition, and reported a mean total FaB score of 2.99 ± 0.56, which was comparable to the present findings [47]. These results indicate that geriatric individuals may adopt behavioral adaptations against fall risk and demonstrate increased subjective awareness regarding falls, possibly as a compensatory response to perceived postural instability and dizziness-related uncertainty. This pattern may arise from reduced balance confidence leading to heightened vigilance and the development of safety-oriented strategies in daily activities, rather than reflecting improved functional balance capacity.

In conclusion, when considered together, the findings indicate that BPPV in geriatric individuals extends beyond vestibular symptoms, also affecting functional capacity, fall-related behaviors, psychological status, and activities of daily living. The pattern observed across the applied scales suggests that BPPV has multidimensional consequences involving both behavioral adaptation and reduced balance confidence. Clinically, these results highlight that management of geriatric BPPV should not be limited to repositioning maneuvers alone, but should also consider functional, behavioral, and psychological dimensions in a comprehensive approach.

A limitation of the study is that the scales used were based on self-report, which may have allowed individual perceptual differences to influence the findings. In addition, the absence of objective vestibular and balance assessments limits the ability to correlate subjective outcomes with physiological measures. Future research incorporating both objective vestibular function tests and postural balance assessments, along with longitudinal follow-up designs, would provide a more comprehensive understanding and strengthen the evidence base.
