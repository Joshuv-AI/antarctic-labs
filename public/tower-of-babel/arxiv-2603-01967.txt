If

:

We prove the equivalence of the four statements in two parts. Firstly, we establish the cyclic equivalence of Statements (1), (2), and (3). Secondly, we show the bidirectional equivalence between Statements (3) and (4).

Part 1: Cyclic equivalence of (1), (2), and (3)

Clique cutset in MNPD graphs

In this section, we mainly discuss the structure of MNPD graphs that may contain a clique cutset. Before that, we need to introduce some definitions which are significant for the discussion.

Let

. Let

. A set

of

, and is called a

Proposition 3.1

If

is a simplicial set of

then

is the union of pairwisely anticomplete cliques, and if
X
is a simplicial set of
G
then

is a simplicial set of

.

Proposition 3.2

Let
G
be a graph and

be a nonempty simplicial set of

. Then

is perfect if and only if

is perfect.

Proof.

If
G
is perfect, then all of its induced subgraphs are perfect, and so is

.

Suppose that

is perfect. Let
Y
be a subset of
V(G)
. If
Y\subseteq X
or
Y\cap X=\mbox{{\rm\O}}
, then

is clearly perfect by Proposition
3.1
. Suppose that

and
Y\setminus X\neq\mbox{{\rm\O}}
. By the definition of simplicial set,
X\cap Y
is also a simplicial set

. Given that no vertex of
X
is contained in any odd hole or odd antihole (if such structures exist) in

,

is perfect. Therefore,

is perfect.

Let
X
be a subset of
V(G)
, and
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k})
be a partition of
X
. We call
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k})

of

if

is a simplicial set of

for

. If

is a
simplicial decomposition
of

is perfect, then we call

.

Proposition 3.3

Let
G
be a graph and
X
be a nonempty simplicial set of

. Then

is perfectly divisible if and only if

is perfectly divisible.

Proof.

If
G
is perfectly divisible, then all of its induced subgraphs are perfectly divisible, and so is
G-X
.

Suppose that

is perfectly divisible. Let
Y
be a subset of
V(G)
. If
Y\subseteq X
or

, then

is clearly perfectly divisible by Proposition

. Suppose that

and

, and let

Proposition 3.4

Let

be a graph,

, and
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k})

. Then,

is perfect if and only if

Proof.

If

is perfect. We are done if

, we have that, by Proposition

is the union of some pairwisely anticomplete cliques, and hence

is perfect.
