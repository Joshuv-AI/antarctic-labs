Management and coping with care for older adults: family caregivers’ experiences in Enugu state, Nigeria
Authors: Jacinta Chibuzor Ene, Nkechinyere Patricia Onyishi, Lum Perpetua Tanyi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOften, ageing is associated with a decline in health conditions and changes in the dietary needs of older adults. It then follows that the most essential line of action is caregiving. This paper examines how family caregivers manage and cope with the caregiving roles for older adults in South-East Nigeria.MethodWe sought to adopt a qualitative research approach. Focus group discussions and in-depth interviews were utilized in collecting data from 54 participants, comprising family caregivers and health workers aged 18–59 years, and providing caregiving roles to older adults aged 60 years and above. Data were analyzed thematically.ResultsWe found that participants experience challenges managing care for older adults. This was owing to older adults’ age, health status, attitude, financial incapacitation and educational status of the family caregivers. Most of the participants cope through human and non-human relationships. Findings show that communal relationships in rural communities are gradually declining. It was found that participants who cope through non-human relationships adopt strategies including maintaining a positive outlook of life, observing a spiritual relationship with God and spending time playing games and music. A few participants narrated how they cope with healthy nutrition and unhygienic habits. We observed that family caregivers are in dire need of assistance in care management for older adults. Participants showed little knowledge of social work engagement in care management for older adults.ConclusionAn increase in age comes with an increase in care management demands, with concerns affecting the management and strategies adopted in coping with care for older adults. Since the Nigerian society does not support institutionalized homes for older adults, we recommend the utilization of social workers' services in family settings, through providing appropriate care management strategies and services for older adults and their family caregivers.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-026-07770-7.Keywords: Caregivers, Care management, Coping strategies, Family members, Older adults, Social workers

Introduction

Globally, one significant trend is the rising ageing population. The United Nations [1] reports that the world’s population of older persons aged 60 years and older is projected to triple over the next 30 years, with an increase in sub-Saharan Africa. Consequently, the National Institute on Ageing [2] reports that the African population of older adults is projected to triple from 74.4 million to 235.1 million the next three decades. Similarly, the United Nations Department of Economics and Social Affairs [3], Population Division estimated that Nigerians 65 years and older are expected to hit 16 million by 2050 and 74 million by 2100. The unprecedented rapid growth in the ageing population could be associated with factors like improved living standards, advancement and breakthroughs in areas of medicine and technology [4]. The growth in the ageing population is a reflection that there will be a demand for the number of caregivers, consequent upon the fact that older adults, as they age, may require assistance with the necessities of life. Along with this come the management and coping with care for older adults, which are sources of concern to family members.

Across the globe, the pattern of ageing varies in different settings. While many High-Income Countries (HICs) adopt institutionalized care homes, most Low-Income Countries (LICs) still operate the filial care system [5]. This is based on a culturally homogenous society with the perspective that care for older adults is perceived as a significant traditional culture [6]. Thus, the filial care option is often provided by family members who might be spouses, children, nephews, or other younger family members [7]. According to the literature, family caregivers often participate in the exchange of patients’ information thereby necessitating their interaction with healthcare personnel [8, 9]. Hence, providing care management for older adults includes coordinating care, managing medications and meals, attending to medical encounters, finances, and mobility, among other activities of daily living. This assistance is expected to be friendly, helpful, available, and sensitive to the needs of the recipients.

In Nigeria like other African countries, care for older adults has been an aged-long practice; aimed to ensure inclusiveness, healthy living and well-being for family members [7]. Achieving the healthy well-being of older adults entails adequate care management. This involves a patient-centered approach designed to assist patients and their support system (family members) in managing medical conditions more effectively [10]. Given this precedent, care management for older adults has some positive and negative aspects, including a feeling of affection, the extent of closeness, a sense of personal satisfaction and often overwhelming, constantly demanding and always challenging [11, 12]. Sometimes, some of these challenges in care management can be highly rewarding, such that the caregiver will learn patience, kindness, compassion, and above all will be justified, while the care recipient will be satisfied with the services rendered [13, 14]. It is perceived that family members who provide care for their older parents receive blessings from them, while their children will also reciprocate similar gestures to them in their old age.

Against this backdrop, scholars have reported that family members experience a burden in providing care management for Nigerian older adults [8, 15, 16]. This comes with progression in the age of older adults, difficulty in mobility, decreased hunger, expanded delicacy, poor psychological capacity, decline in hearing, visual and dental status,among other challenges [7, 17]. Worrisome is that certain health outcome in ageing comes with a change in attitude [13]. For instance, older adults suffering from dementia when they try to exhibit it may be perceived negatively as strange behaviours from our African context. Caregivers with little knowledge of older adults' health status may be unaware of this health problem and this may compound the burden experienced in care management [18]. Additionally, concerns ranging from caregivers' ignorance, age, educational status, uncaring attitude, out-of-pocket payment for health services and lack of support have been reported in the literature [13, 19, 20]. This implies that in the time to come, these challenges may pose a threat to care management for older adults.

In this study, therefore, an attempt is made to explore family members’ concerns about management and coping with care for older adults. This stems from the fact that coping with ageing is not done in isolation but may require the company of others who provide personal, emotional and healthcare assistance. For instance, while [15] identified religion as a coping strategy, [21] reported communication and networking with family members. Again, [22] suggested maintaining a positive outlook with adequate nutritional needs, whereas [9], recommends the use of health gadgets and apparatus. These include the use of walking aids, reading glasses, artificial teeth, hearing aid and minor health machines to monitor the overall wellbeing of older adults. Since care management for older adults is provided by family caregivers, it becomes essential that they are knowledgeable of these coping strategies.

In this regard, the inability of family members to possess the essential knowledge required for coping with care management for older adults in Nigeria is problematic. They may not have substantial knowledge of health management, ageing nutrition and adequate hygiene practices. Similarly, most of these family members are also ageing, while the majority have poor educational status, no training in geriatric care management and have no professional assistance. Providing inadequate coping strategies for older adults by their caregivers continues to cause life-threatening and challenging situations. For instance, it may lead to reduced physical ability, regular hospital visitation and loss of appetite, which may all lead to developing illnesses associated with ageing, including diabetes, osteoarthritis and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, among others [23]. With these ageing-related illnesses, the implication is that family members may be unable to cope with the care management of older adults as it continues to cause life-threatening and challenging situations without professional assistance.

Specific to this context, social workers are professionals who work directly with older adults to deal with the factors that exacerbate their wellbeing. They are expected to lobby, advocate and intensify education on geriatric care [24]. Sadly, geriatric social workers do not exist in Nigeria,with only a few medical social workers in health institutions. These professionals can organize counseling and provide discourse sessions to support a network of patients. They could also encourage the utilization of healthcare facilities, digital care systems, draw follow-up programs and could push for new narratives of ageing innovations in coping with care management for older adults [25, 26]. In application, the profession adopts a multi-dimensional approach to evaluate the competencies of caregivers, their willingness to provide care and their acceptability of older adults particularly for those who are distraught as a result of an unwelcome health diagnosis [24]. For instance, social workers can intervene in the incidences of crisis for family members by assisting in healthcare planning and co-ordination with health professionals like doctors and nurses. In furtherance, they render effective therapeutic services and coordinate follow-up care management to promote rapid recovery [23]. Again, they assist policymakers in proposing laws that will ensure healthy living for older adults and their families, particularly in LICs like Nigeria.

Although the Nigerian system has been insensitive to older adults by giving them little attention, yet, professionals and researchers are not relenting in their efforts to bring the needs of this group to the fore. Most of the studies and research done by Nigerian researchers concentrated on health challenges and caregiving stress experienced by family members [13, 17, 18, 27, 28]. Of all these studies, only a few have investigated the coping strategies family members adopt in the care management of older adults in Nigeria. More so, studies from a social work perspective are few. This study, therefore, becomes relevant for establishing empirical facts through adopting a qualitative research design. To bridge this knowledge gap, it is then imperative to research family members’ concerns about management and coping with care for older adults from the social work perspective in Enugu state, Nigeria.

Enugu state, Nigeria, becomes particularly important as a study area for three reasons, including the availability of government, missions and privately-owned health facilities (hospitals); a largely religious, homogeneous society with care for the aged perceived as a filial responsibility and the absence of an organized social support aimed at providing management services and coping strategies for older adults and their family members [5, 17, 29, 30], (Uzochukwu et al., 2014). The study, therefore, addresses caregivers’ principal concerns, coping strategies adopted, knowledge and involvement of social workers in care for older adults. These professionals are essential in providing interventions in times of crisis for caregivers, assistance in planning with other health professionals, rendering effective therapeutic services, and ensuring follow-up programs. The study also advocates for government assistance, empowers family members, contributes to the existing literature, and ensures the integration of social workers in the family institutions, all geared towards effective care management and coping with older adults in Enugu state, Nigeria. It is against this background that this study poses the following research questions: (a) what are the concerns of family members in the care management of older adults in Nigeria (b) what coping strategies do family members adopt in the care management of older adults in Nigeria (c) what is the involvement of social workers in the coping with the care management of older adults?

Theoretical framework

We anchor our theoretical framework on the social learning theory propounded by Albert Bandura in 1977. The theory has been adopted by Bahn [31] in the application of nurse education in care management of patients. The choice of this theory is that it emphasizes the importance of transferring learning through observation, imitation and education programs [32]. This could be associated with the fact that knowledge acquired through these actions serves as a guide for family members in care management for older adults. The key to this theory is that family members through direct practice, can acquire knowledge through learning by observation, training and teaching. They could also assimilate how roles and techniques in care management for older adults are performed and on later occasions, the coded data fills in as a guide for activity.

In previous studies, many scholars advocated for wider use of social learning theory [33, 34]. The explanation is ascribed to the view that coping strategies adopted by family members is informed by what they observed and has a higher priority than what they think about it [35]. It is this tenet of the theory that informed the development of the instrument for data collection based on their observations, imitation and education. For instance, through observation, positive behaviors are modelled. When positive caregiving behaviours are imitated, it allows positive reinforcement. Similarly, providing caregiving education for family caregivers will increase their self-efficacy in providing care management for older adults, given an in-depth understanding through social learning. As such, the central focus of numerous health education programs has been a change in knowledge through education, observation and training. Through these actions, family members could get the chance to develop directly more certain actions and more positive attitudes towards themselves and their older adults, given the culture of filial care in the conventional Nigerian society.

Methods

Study design

The study employed the phenomenological research design. This type of research design helps to understand the positioning of thematic analysis within qualitative research [36]. Employing the phenomenological research design, the researchers described in detail the relationship between care management and healthy ageing for older adults. This gave an interpretative, in-depth rather than a descriptive understanding. The justification was based on the fact that it allows for standardization of questions and flexible individual responses in order to gain rich data (Lune & Berg, 2018). Again, the phenomenological research design is concerned with the study of lived experiences from the perspective of the family caregivers. This research design was employed to display family caregivers’ concerns and the need for social workers’ intervention in the management and coping with the care of older adults.

Study area

The study was conducted in Enugu-North senatorial district of Enugu state. The State is one of the 36 states in the South-East geopolitical zone in Nigeria. Reports from the National Bureau of Statistics [37] assert that the State has a population figure of about 4.1 million. The state has three senatorial zones one of which is Enugu-North. This senatorial zone comprised six Local Government Areas (LGAs) including Nsukka. Nsukka town was purposively selected for this study out of the 18 local communities that constitute Nsukka LGA. Based on the last census held by the National Population Commission [38], Nsukka had a population of 309,448 comprising 149,418 males and 160,030 females aged 15–59 years. Following the projected population formula at a growth rate of 2.9%, a total of 507,494.7 comprising 245,045.5 males and 262,449.2 females, respectively was derived for 2023. For this study, Nsukka LGA was purposively selected because it operates a three-level healthcare sector, including primary (Nsukka Primary Healthcare Center), secondary (Nsukka General Hospital) and tertiary (University of Nigeria Medical Center) health facilities. Again, the town is a semi-urban locality where most rural indigenes owing to proximity migrate to the locality for healthcare services.

Population of the study

In this locality, there are about ten healthcare facilities [39]. The study employed first a purposive sampling procedure in selecting three healthcare institutions. These institutions were selected based on their category. Hence, one primary, secondary and tertiary healthcare institution was employed for the study. The names of these institutions are withheld for confidentiality. Although 60 caregivers (ten males and females from each health institution) were approached, a total of 54 participants, comprising 18 participants (nine males and females) agreed and were selected from each of the three health institutions. These participants comprised 48 family caregivers and six (6) health workers. Only six caregivers refused to participate in the study. This is an indication that our participant recruitment was not biased. The age distribution of participants was 18–59 years. The justification for choosing the age range is that an individual who is 18 years and older can provide regular caregiving assistance and is not considered a child, whereas an individual at 59 years is almost considered an older adult because in most public sectors, retirement commences at the age of 60 years.

Sampling procedure and data collection

For this study, a non-probability sampling method was employed. Among the various non-probability sampling methods, purposive and availability sampling were deployed for selecting the study participants. The justification for adopting a purposive sampling procedure was that a unique target population was required for the study. The study employed availability sampling to ensure that only available and willing participants were recruited. The utilization of the qualitative research method in generating data for the study is concerned with perspectives and interpretations of people based on their lived experiences, as it seeks to gain further insight into the thinking and behavior of people (Philips et al., 2016). In view of caregivers’ attitude and the social learning theory, our interest was on those caregiving activities for which family caregivers indicate concerns when they provide care management for older adults (Cong-Lem, 2022). This becomes essential in this study because, in Nigeria and Enugu state, in particular, no available assistance exists on care management services provided for older adult family caregivers.

In this study, In-Depth Interviews (IDIs) and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) specifically developed for this study were employed as the data collection methods (See supplementary file). However, Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) served as the main source of data collection because of the numerous ideas, issues, sub-topics and even solutions to a problem that can be generated as cited in qualitative studies [40, 41]. We utilized a maximum of eight participants for each session with persons of the same characteristics and gender. As social work researchers, we were also interested in family caregivers’ management and strategies adopted in caregiving for older adults in Nigeria [42]. During our data collection, we chose central locations, scheduled dates and time to ease their movements and reaffirmed them that the conversations would be short so that they could attend to their private affairs.

The researchers, with two research assistants, collectively developed the focus group discussion and in-depth interview guides. They were made up of semi-structured questions and probes. According to Nyumba [41], probes help in a deeper understanding of context through narratives that describe the concerns of family caregivers in the care management of older adults, rather than a rudimentary guide. More so, probes provide in-depth explanation why certain measures are adopted as coping strategies. In all, six focus group discussions (two in each health institution) comprising eight participants (caregivers) for each session and six in-depth interviews comprising six health worker participants (three males and females, such that one male and female is selected from each health institution) were employed in the data collection process. The justification for the inclusion of health workers was based on family caregivers’ exchange of patients’ information with healthcare personnel [9]. They were essential because their perspective guided us on the lived experiences and interpretations of the participants during the data analysis. We sought the assistance of healthcare workers in the inpatient care (IPC) unit in the selected health institution between 1st October and 10th December 2023. The interviews for each health institution were held at the end of each FGD. Each FGD and IDI lasted not more than 60 min. In the FGDs, the data collection process started with some introductory activities. This allowed the two research assistants who served as the note-taker and voice recorder to meet with the research participants informally so that they became more comfortable during the data collection process.

Data analysis

The data analysis technique began with note-taking and careful audio recording of all the discussions. Data were analyzed after transcription in the English language. The researchers who did the transcription are grounded in the Igbo language, even though just a few participants expressed themselves in Igbo. For participants who spoke the Igbo language, lecturers from the Department of Linguistics, University of Nigeria, Nsukka Campus, were employed to translate and transcribe the Igbo language to English. After the translating and transcription, all the researchers participated in comparing the contents of the transcripts with the field notes to ensure coherence. Data immersion was implored by repeatedly hearing the audio and reading the transcribed discussion and field notes for familiarization and to gain some sense of the participant’s experiences in management and coping with care for older adults.

The data analysis procedure for this study was based on an Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis (IPA). Sirwan et al. [36] reported that the IPA, rooted in phenomenology and hermeneutics, focuses on deeply exploring personal lived experiences within small, homogeneous samples. This was essential for interpreting the family caregivers lived experiences in a rural Nigerian community. According to Larkin& Thompson (2012), the interpretative phenomenological analysis typically follows an inductive coding procedure to provide meaning to the coded data, as we studied the transcripts. We coded the data using the NVivo 12 software. This was done to ensure that codes with similar characteristics were grouped thematically to arrive at key themes. Thematic analysis was employed to enable the researchers to categorize the data into themes for easy comprehension [40]. To proceed with our data analysis for the study, the translated data was open-coded to ensure accuracy and coherence. This started immediately after the translation of the data to avoid memory loss. Next, we compared the contents of the transcripts with the field notes and recorded information to ensure that no information was lost. The initial coding was conducted by all the researchers. As a group, we debated the acceptance or rejection of themes that were only identified by one researcher. This helped us ensure member checking and as well resolve discrepancies that may emanate from a single researcher’s interpretation. This was done to ensure peer debriefing in theme development. Our research notes, which were our memo, helped us to review and re-focus during the research process as we tried to understand the meaning of the coding categories and themes. Our themes were refined and validated using three participants (each participant from the FGDs held in the health institution) included in the study. This helped to strengthen the trustworthiness of our research in audit trails. To increase the rigor of our analysis, two peers who are experts in social gerontology but did not participate in the study further examined the dataset. Their insights contributed to the final checks on the analysis [43]. More so, the peer debriefing insights helped us resolve discrepancies that may emanate from a single researcher’s interpretation in theme development, which improved the validity of the data. Lastly, special connotations were noted and pulled out as illustrative quotes from the thematic cluster. We then presented a narrative account of structure in the results section by presenting quotes that mattered to the participants and the study. After the rigors were followed, the data were categorized into three major themes, which include (a) concerns of family caregivers in care management for older adults, (b) strategies family caregivers adopt in care management for older adults and (c) assessment of the knowledge of social workers’ involvement in care management for older adults.

Ethical consideration

The participants were informed of anonymity, confidentiality and their freedom to withdraw during the study. In this study, anonymity and confidentiality were maintained during FGD in group settings by giving numbers to participants. These numbers were used in place of their names. It is important to note that all methods of data collection were carried out in accordance with relevant institutional guidelines and regulations of the Strategic Contacts Ethics and Publications (STRACEP) of the University of Nigeria, Nsukka Campus, Enugu State, with the ethical approval clearance code: UNNEC/05/002/Ph.D./SW/10-ST08/0024. All methods of data collected were carried out in accordance with relevant institutional guidelines and regulations. The participant willfully gave verbal informed consent to participate in the study before the audio recording of the interviews. The justification was that some of the participants were not literate enough to state a written informed consent.

Theme 1: family caregivers’ concerns in care management for older adults

In the course of our study, we observed that there was a general consensus on the view that care management for older adults is challenging. However, in this theme, we present family members' concerns in care management for older adults. The participants highlighted various concerns. We categorized their views into the following sub-themes including changing contemporary society, income level, health status and older adults' attitudes.

Changing contemporary society as a concern in care management for older adults

From the narratives, the changing contemporary Nigerian society was an important concern in care management for older adults. On this issue, some participants attributed the change to the place of residence, quest for education, search for greener pastures, and adoption of the Western lifestyle. In the various FGD sessions, landmark quotes from groups were extracted and they read:

I brought my mother to live with me in the city. Life here is ‘mind your own’. One can rarely tell when one’s neighbor is at home, what more informing the person to assist in providing care management for an older adult [Female, FGD, 35 years].

In this current society, life has changed. This is due to modernization. Everyone wants to go to school, get a job and live in the cities. This makes it difficult for older adults to receive adequate care management because no one wants to be left behind [Male, FGD, 35 years].

You see, life is filled with numerous difficulties which must be satisfied. In this society, family members no longer perceive care management for older adults as an obligation to be performed by the extended family. In this view, older adults are left in their homes in the villages with paid assistants to provide care management. This is to enable family members to perform certain personal responsibilities [Male, FGD, 42 years].

In the conventional society, care management for older adults was a collective activity. This is often done in a familiar environment. Recently, people have adopted the Western lifestyle of the nuclear family system with fewer family members. While the extended family system is gradually fading, concentration is on the nuclear family [Female, FGD, 36 years].

Family caregivers’ financial status as a concern in care management for older adults

An important concern for participants was the increasing economic challenges in the country. This identified challenge translates to increasing responsibility for family caregivers to provide the needs of older adults and their own needs. We observed that most participants had low education, irregular jobs, and poor enumeration. These demographic attributes of participants affect the care management given to older adults, as most caregivers experience low financial status. With strong lamentation on this issue, most of the participants considered the use of non-institutional health facilities so that they do not go into debt. See a typical quote below:

Yes, I encourage my parents to consider the use of traditional medicine. I tell them to use local medicine within the community because it is cheaper. This was used by our forefathers. Thank God, alternative medicine doctors are increasing daily in our communities [Male, IDI, 35 years].

We discovered that most participant lamented on the increasing cost in healthcare expenditure. This is because with increase in age comes decline in health status. However, reports indicated that there is no form of health management assistance given to older adults as they age.

Health conditions of older adults are a concern in care management for older adults

According to the participants’ narratives, the effect of older adults’ health status remains a major concern in care management. Responses from the participants indicated that older adults’ decline in health status translates to increased healthcare responsibility. This was evident from the various FGD sessions, when in one of the sessions, a participant had this to say:

Mama keeps complaining of various illnesses. If it is not ankle pain, it will be waist pain. I often ignore her and her excessive complaints; my siblings are the ones to explain to her the problems she is going through. I will not kill myself, providing care management for her when she cannot stop complaining of various health problems [Female, FGD, 40 years].

We discovered that participants wished that older adults would understand their predicament regarding the impact their complaints may have on the management and coping with caregiving.

Attitude of older adults as a concern in care management for older adults

Narratives illustrated that older adults' attitudes are important concerns for participants. Some participants reported having self-centered older adults who fail to recognize caregivers’ efforts. Others explained that some older adults are difficult, arrogant and wasteful. These attitudes, according to the participants, may cause conflict among family caregivers and this may jeopardize the zeal to keep providing care for them. See illustrative quotes below:

Lately, I have been living a life filled with quarrels. Today, my mother will accuse my brother's wife of shouting at her and I will reprimand my brother's wife. Tomorrow she will accuse my sister of starving her to death and I will quarrel with my sister. Do you know that recently my mother accused me of slapping her in my house? Angrily, my husband and I vowed that she would never visit us again. My sibling said that Mama has automatically changed, my brother reported that she hallucinates, but a friend told me that these are features of dementia. Honestly, I am tired of her [Female, IDI, 45 years].

A male participant who was extremely vocal in one of the FGD sessions frightened us by saying:

There are times when Papa will not wake up fast. One has to beat him hard to wake him. When he is awake, he often laments seeing familiar persons and relatives who are deceased. There are times when he speaks to himself and talks of abstract things that do not make sense. It is really scary when he behaves like this [Male, FGD, 42 years].

Most caregivers endure these challenges because they have no option. Hence, management and coping with care for older adults is a necessity and not a choice.

The participants gave various strategies they adopt in care management for older adults. They mentioned strategies including staying close to family members, maintaining a positive outlook in life, social networking with friends and groups, and ensuring adequate spiritual relationships with God. In this study, the coping strategies adopted by family members in the care management of older adults were categorized into two sub-themes, including adopting "individual coping strategies" and "social coping resources". In this regard, responses on staying close to family members, social networking with friends and religious groups were categorized as "individual coping strategies". Responses on maintaining a positive outlook, maintaining a spiritual relationship with God and other specific views not related to human relationships, like spending time with pets or playing music and games, were categorized as "social coping resources".

In our discussion, it was revealed that communal living and relationships are gradually dwindling in our society. The participants explained that coping is not done in isolation but in the company of others. We are listing some typical quotes below:

You see, communal living in rural communities is gradually declining in our society. On a good day, we take Papa to the clubhouse where he sits, drinks and discusses with other cohorts. He feels happy and excited when he returns and will want to revisit the clubhouse on another occasion [Male, IDI, 51 years].

My religious group often pays a visit to Mama's House. They provide her with gifts and foodstuffs. Also, they pray for her and give her good wishes. They provide counseling for her and everyone present at home. This gives us consolation in care management [Female, IDI, 39 years].

Papa is a member of the Order of Noble Knights in the church. Owing to this position, sometimes the Priest pays visits. He prays and provides consolation words for us all. If need be, he presents Papa's condition to the congregation for collective prayers [Male, IDI, 40 years].

The above responses indicates that coping through human relationship is essential and anchored on social networking. In this regard, there is a need to revitalize communal living and relationships in our society.

We observed that participants adopt coping strategies that are not related to individuals. In this category, participants narrated that they maintain a positive outlook on life, maintain a spiritual relationship with God, and spend time playing games and music. A few participants narrated how they cope through adopting healthy nutrition and unhygienic habits. Below are illustrative quotes from IDI participants and two FGD participants who summarized their group's standpoint:

No one will live young forever. Ageing is inevitable and this is the only means to attain heaven or hell. So, we must accept our fate and provide care management for older adults. In return, we will obtain a huge blessing that older adults are known to give [Female, IDI, 49 years].

You see, I attain mass every day. I know that God will assist me till the end. His words from the Bible verse give me the courage to continue to strive till the end. I know His Grace is sufficient for us [Female, IDI, 54 years].

Oftentimes, when the burden becomes excessive, I like to listen to my spiritual music. I also cope with indoor games like 'Chess games' when the care recipient is asleep. These strategies serve as consolation to cope with the situation in care management for my aged mother [Male, IDI, 29 years].

You see, providing care is not easy particularly preparing food for older adults. There are times when you have to place them on a diet, considering their health status. For instance, Mama, who is diabetic, her food is often bitter. I also provide adequate fruits and vegetables for her. On occasions when I do not have the strength to cook, I simply provide her with what I have available at home [Female, FGD, 29 years].

Bedwetting often occurs for my Papa. I cannot cope with providing pampers for him. So, we use old cloths as improvised pampers for him. When going to bed, we will use diapers like a baby. In the morning, we will wash the cloth, but if he wet himself on the ground, we will clean it up using a rag. This is difficult, but we have to do it for him [Female, FGD, 39 years].

Still on social coping resources, only two participants mentioned that one coping strategy they adopt is keeping mute. Below is the response by one of the participants:

I endure the situation in my heart. I do not want people to say that I have abandoned my parents. This is why despite the stress and difficulties I experience in care management; I adopt silence as a coping strategy [Female, IDI, 27 years].

From our data, we see that caregivers’ coping strategies include living spiritual lives, adequate nutrition, adoption of various strategies and silence. Adoption of these strategies may increase the level of burden and challenges experienced in care management for older adults.

Theme 3: assessment of family members’ knowledge on the involvement of government, humanitarian groups and social workers in care management of older adults

In this regard, the resounding view was that the government has provided no assistance for older adults’ caregivers. More so, participants have little awareness of the existence of any humanitarian group that assists caregivers in providing care for older adults in the study locality. Except for a few, most of the participants reported that there is little or no knowledge about social workers' involvement in the care management of older adults. We observed that most study participants do not know who social workers are and what they do. As narrated by this participant, "I don’t know them and who they are”. Yet another participant in the tertiary health institution reported knowing about them but did not understand their roles in the health setting.

Discussion

Across the globe, management for the vulnerable is a priority. While high-income countries provide alternative care management for older adults, low-income countries still adopt the filial care system. This stems from the fact that healthy lives and well-being are essential for all at all ages (UNDESA, 2016). Given that with an increasingly ageing population [1], and with older adults' vulnerability to illnesses [23], their health and well-being are of utmost importance. It then becomes essential to manage and adopt strategies to cope with care for older adults in Enugu state, Nigeria.

According to the findings of this study, family members' major concerns in care management for older adults were on changing contemporary society, income level, health status and older adults' attitude. First, changing contemporary society was a concern owing to the place of residence of the family member, the quest for education, the search for greener pastures, and the adoption of a Western lifestyle. It was reported that life in the city poses difficulty in caring for older adults, unlike communal living in rural localities. Women who are known to provide care seek to be educated to secure jobs to make a living, whereas the extended family system is going extinct. Second, in our conversations, we observed that concerns about income level transcend from increasing family responsibility to providing for the needs of older adults. This emanates from low education, poor income and high inflation in the country. This concern allows the utilization of non-institutional health facilities. The issue of the health status of older adults was the third concern. We discovered in this study that an increase in the age of older adults translates to increased health demands. Fourth, the attitude of older adults was the last-mentioned concern of family caregivers. It was found that family caregivers lack the knowledge of some health challenges that may affect the social networking of older adults. Hence, when some older adults display certain ageing health challenges like dementia, they may be perceived negatively as strange behaviour from our African context. Consequently, some older adults are often ignored by their family caregivers, particularly when they are in dire need of personal, social or health assistance, owing to their unintentional negative attitude. Family caregivers’ negative perception towards older adults has been reported by scholars, including Cadmus et al. [18] and Mah et al. [22]. Apparently, this perception could be associated with the burden of providing care on the quality of life of the caregivers as reported by Faronbi & Olagun, [15]. This contribution clearly depicts that the unintentional negative attitude of older adults results in a negative perception towards them and could be related to the burden of caregiving.

In this study, another contribution is that family caregivers adopt social networking in coping with the management of older adults. It was revealed that coping through social networking was done through interacting with cohorts at clubhouses, visits from religious groups, and friends. While some present gifts, others provide prayer sessions and counseling services. It was also discovered that family members cope with care management of older adults through various ways, including maintaining a positive outlook on life, a spiritual relationship with God and spending time playing games and music. In furtherance, a few participants narrated how they cope with healthy nutrition and unhygienic habits. It is imperative to note that family caregivers are very much aware of certain unhygienic habits they adopt in coping with care for older adults. However, despite this knowledge, they adopt unhygienic habits since they have no alternative. More so, we observed that a few participants adopted silence as a coping strategy. This stems from the fact that challenges in care management for older adults are not reported by family caregivers but are endured. But we are worried for those who might not have the desire to endure. We see in developed countries, agencies that provide various alternatives for family caregivers to cope with care management for older adults (Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality, 2018). For instance, the availability of long and short-term care homes, paid formal caregivers, social media interactive platforms, government-approved health insurance, security grants, and mainstreaming of older adults’ care management in the educational curriculum. In developed countries, these services are available for individuals who wish to access them despite the cost, unlike in Nigeria, where they are not easily available and accessible owing to societal norms and the cost of services. These, among other age-friendly strategies, would ensure adequate avenues to provide and develop the knowledge needed for healthy ageing.

Another novel contribution in this study that is not common in other studies is the poor knowledge of the involvement of social workers in the care of older adults in Nigeria. Quite disturbing is the finding that the participants had little or no knowledge about social workers' involvement in the care management of older adults. This could be associated with the fact that formal social work practice is relatively new and emerging in Nigeria [24]. For instance, reports from the participants indicated little knowledge of geriatric social workers. Hence, social work proficient contribution serves as a mediation measure for management and coping with care for older adults. One of the particular ways social work professionals guarantee sufficient survival techniques is through the idea of 'self-improvement' development in old age. Through this concept, older adults can be urged to take on a 'strength-based approach' that demonstrates a conviction that they can adapt to the real factors and requests of life related to the ageing process [44]. This should be possible through the support of a difference in disposition that dodges correlation with loneliness, demoralization of negative explanations about self and usage of self-strength [29]. It becomes essential to foster great relational abilities and all that can empower one to remain socially connected and associated with family members.

In families, social workers work with older adults and their caregivers. They provide person-centered, integrated care to deal with possible care management challenges that may compound problems in caregiving. The professionals adopt a multi-dimensional approach to evaluate the competencies of caregivers, their willingness to provide care, and their acceptability of older adults, particularly those who are distraught as a result of an unwelcome health diagnosis [9]. With the application of nurse education in care management of older adults with health challenges, the social learning theory becomes important in transferring knowledge through observation, learning and modeling [32]. Hence, knowledge acquired through these actions serves as a guide for family members in care management for older adults. The theory is of essence because family caregivers, through direct practice, can acquire knowledge with the efforts of social workers who could provide professional intervention services. Providing interventions in times of crisis for caregivers could be achieved through performing specific roles, including caregiving education, support groups, care coordination, and advocacy. For instance, caregiving education could be done through learning by observation, training and teaching on how roles and techniques in care management for older adults are performed. In occasions where care management becomes challenging, social workers could involve support groups that could supervise services provided by various family caregivers, provide a platform for older cohort interaction, recommend competent caregivers, and help them in providing appropriate healthcare management strategies and services for older adults. Again, these professionals could organize care coordination by assisting in planning with other health professionals, rendering effective therapeutic services, and ensuring follow-up programs. Overall, they can provide advocacy through engaging with international organizations, local humanitarian groups and Nigerians in the diaspora who could help develop a care aid programme for all older adults in Nigeria.

Strength and limitations

The researchers acknowledged that this study had some limitations. First, we encountered difficulties in recruiting participants who would volunteer to participate in the study. Second, the recruited participants were drawn from a particular locality. These limitations notwithstanding, we believe that the findings of this study remain valid to benefit family caregivers’ assistance in the management and coping with care for older adults in Nigeria.

Conclusion

Older adults constitute a vulnerable group whose management and coping with care should be considered with utmost importance. This study then revealed that; first, family members’ major concerns in care management for older adults were on changing contemporary society, income level, health status and older adults’ attitude. These concerns affect the management and strategies adopted in coping with care for older adults. Second, it was observed that a major coping strategy by family caregivers was carried out through human and non-human networking. Through social networking older adults and their family members receive various gifts, counseling, prayers and maintain a positive outlook on life. This indicates the role of social networking in care management for coping with healthy ageing. Third, the social work professionals' contribution serves as a mediation measure for management and coping with care for older adults. Their presence becomes essential to foster stronger relational abilities and all that could empower one to remain socially connected and associated with family members. Social workers, in collaboration with other professionals, could assist through actionable steps including training young geriatric social workers, who could organize pilot community respite programs for older adults and caregivers. They could also participate in rendering effective therapeutic services and developing effective care management strategies for older adults. Overall, the study has revealed the need for filial care practices that can protect the welfare of older adults. This will ensure that the experiences and needs of older adults are safeguarded and provided with adequate assistance, given to their family caregivers. One of which is to strengthen the family care system option, which ensures a better life and social living for all older adults in Nigeria.
