Electron-phonon interactions and instabilities in Weyl semimetals under magnetic fields and torsional strain
Fabián Jofré Parra, Daniel A. Bonilla, Enrique Muñoz

Abstract
We study the presence of an external magnetic field, in combination with torsional strain, over the electron-phonon interactions in a type I Weyl semimetal. This particular superposition of field and strain, modeled in the continuum approximation by an effective gauge field, leads to an asymmetric pseudo-magnetic field at each Weyl node of opposite chirality. Therefore, we also studied the role of nodal asymmetry in the properties of the system by means of the Kadanoff-Wilson renormalization group and the corresponding flow equations. By solving those, we discuss the evolution of the coupling parameters of the theory, and analyze possible fixed points and lattice (Peierls) instabilities emerging from interactions between phonons with the chiral Landau level in the very strong pseudo-magnetic field regime.



I
Introduction

Among the so-called ”quantum materials”, Weyl semimetals (WSMs) were discovered nearly a decade ago in TaAs crystals
Xu et al. 2015
, after being proposed as a theoretical concept
Wan et al. 2011
;
Fang et al. 2012
;
Ruan et al. 2016
;
Vafek and Vishwanath 2014
;
Yan and Felser 2017
;
Armitage et al. 2018
;
Burkov 2018
. WSMs are gapless three-dimensional materials with non-trivial topological properties, as their band structure displays an even number of Weyl nodes. Near each node, charge carriers are massless quasi-particles with linear dispersion and pseudo-relativistic properties
Vafek and Vishwanath 2014
;
Yan and Felser 2017
;
Armitage et al. 2018
;
Burkov 2018
. In particular, each node represents a monopole source of Berry curvature and, therefore, is protected from being distorted because their topological charge (chirality) is an invariant
Burkov 2018
. Therefore in Weyl Fermions chirality, i.e. the projection of spin over their momentum direction, is preserved, a condition usually named as ”spin-momentum locking”. Some additional remarkable properties of WSMs are the presence of Fermi arcs
Xu et al. 2015
, the chiral anomaly, and the chiral magnetic effect
Vanderbilt 2018
. Beyond this single-particle picture, the effects of electronic interactions and strong correlations
Yang 2019
in WSMs have been explored using quantum field theory methods
Roy et al. 2017
;
Laubach et al. 2016
;
Xue and Zhang 2017
;
Carlström and Bergholtz 2018
;
Wei et al. 2012
;
Witczak-Krempa et al. 2014
;
Maciejko and Nandkishore 2014
;
Wei et al. 2014a
, and there is theoretical evidence suggesting a rich structure that may lead to novel topological and magnetic phases
Muñoz et al. 2020
;
Muñoz et al. 2024
;
Zhai et al. 2016
;
Wei et al. 2014b
;
Wang et al. 2017
.

The effects of intense magnetic fields on the electronic properties of materials with Dirac or Weyl spectra is a subject of great interest. In this regime, the electronic spectrum is semi-discrete in the form of pseudo-relativistic Landau levels, thus acquiring an effective dimensionality reduction from 3D to 1D
Zhu et al. 2010
. The nesting property of 1D electronic systems thus precludes the emergence of instabilities that may give rise to strongly correlated phases such as the charge density wave (CDW)
Yakovenko 1993
or the spin density wave (SDW)
Takahashi and Takada 1994
. This phenomenon has been observed experimentally in graphite, where the existence of a field-induced many-body state beyond a critical magnetic field intensity was attributed to a CDW
Fauqué et al. 2013
;
Yaguchi and Singleton 2009
. Moreover, the theoretical interpretation involved the interplay of only the lowest Landau levels (LLL) with
(n=0,\uparrow)
and
(n=-1,\downarrow)
Arnold et al. 2017
. The presence of very intense magnetic fields also affects the magnetoelectric response in WSMs
Zyuzin and Burkov 2012
, partly due to the mixing of the LLLs, thus potentially breaking down the chiral anomaly
Kim et al. 2017
. Many-body effects induced by the presence of intense magnetic fields are also reflected in the renormalization of the phonon spectrum
Rinkel et al. 2019
;
Kundu et al. 2022
. Remarkably, an axial electron-phonon coupling can induce a dynamical chemical potential difference between Weyl nodes of opposite chirality, thus generating a dynamical realization of the chiral anomaly
Hui et al. 2019
.
The effect of strong magnetic fields in interacting models for Weyl semimetals has been studied from a field theory perspective and renormalization group methods
Alicea and Balents 2009
;
Kundu et al. 2022
, indicating the presence of instabilities towards CDW phases. It has been reported
Kundu et al. 2022
that the participation of the Cooper channel can prevent electronic instabilities but, in contrast, enhance lattice instabilities that are not of BCS type
Kundu et al. 2022
. Moreover, breaking the mirror symmetry that links the Weyl nodes suppresses the Cooper channel, thus leading to an increase of the critical temperature for the instability
Kundu et al. 2022
. Therefore, electron-phonon interactions, as suggested in
Kundu et al. 2022
, seem to constitute an essential ingredient in the emergence of interacting phases induced by the presence of intense magnetic fields in topological materials.

The effects of mechanical strain as dislocations and disclinations in WSMs have been explored to a much lesser extent in the context of electronic properties.
Those defects can be modeled in a continuum approximation by means of gauge fields
Cortijo et al. 2015
;
Cortijo et al. 2016
;
Arjona and Vozmediano 2018
;
Lin et al. 2022
, thus leading to the emergence of pseudo-magnetic fields in WSMs. The origin of those strain-induced pseudo-magnetic fields is understood from a basic lattice model, of the form
Pikulin et al. 2016

\displaystyle H^{latt}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+h^{latt}(\mathbf{k})&0\\
0&\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-h^{latt}(\mathbf{k})\end{array}\right),

with
\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=c_{0}+c_{1}\cos(ak_{z})+c_{2}\left(\cos(ak_{x})+\cos(ak_{y})\right)
, and

\displaystyle h^{latt}(\mathbf{k})=\tau^{z}m_{\mathbf{k}}+\Lambda\left(\tau^{x}\sin(ak_{x})+\tau^{y}\sin(ak_{y})\right),

(4)

where
m_{\mathbf{k}}=t_{0}+t_{1}\cos(ak_{z})+t_{2}\left(\cos(ak_{x})+\cos(ak_{y})\right)
, and
t_{j}
(for
j=0,1,2
) and
\Lambda
parameters chosen in order to match, via a
\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{p}
approximation near the
\Gamma
point, a first-principles band structure calculation
Pikulin et al. 2016
;
Wang et al. 2012
;
Wang et al. 2013
. This minimal model exhibits a pair of Weyl nodes
Pikulin et al. 2016

\mathbf{K}_{\xi}=(0,0,\xi Q)
(for
\xi=\pm
), with
Q=a^{-1}\arccos\left(-\left(t_{0}+2t_{2}\right)/t_{1}\right)
. A low-energy expansion in the limit
aQ\ll 1
near each node
h^{latt}\left(\mathbf{K}_{\xi}\pm\mathbf{k}\right)
, leads to the effective Hamiltonian representation

\displaystyle h_{\xi}(\mathbf{k})=\hbar v_{\xi}^{j}\tau^{j}k_{j},

(5)

with the velocity components

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\xi}=\hbar a^{-1}\left(\Lambda,\Lambda,-\xi t_{1}\sin(aQ)\right).

(6)

The corresponding chiral charge at each node is then given by
\text{sign}(v_{\xi}^{x}v_{\xi}^{y}v_{\xi}^{z})=-\xi
.
In the context of this lattice model, elastic deformations are characterized by the displacement vector field
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
, and the corresponding symmetric component of the strain tensor
u_{ij}\equiv(1/2)\left(\partial_{i}u_{j}+\partial_{j}u_{i}\right)
. Those in turn induce a change in the hopping term, as follows
Pikulin et al. 2016

\displaystyle t_{1}\tau^{x}\rightarrow t_{1}(1-u_{33})\tau^{z}+\mathrm{i}\Lambda\sum_{j\neq 3}u_{3j}\tau^{j}.

(7)

This introduces a modification of the lattice Hamiltonian
Pikulin et al. 2016

\displaystyle\delta h^{latt}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t_{1}u_{33}\tau^{z}\cos(ak_{z})+\Lambda\left(u_{13}\tau^{x}\right.

(8)

\displaystyle\left.-u_{23}\tau^{y}\right)\sin(ak_{z}).

After expanding this modified Hamiltonian, as before, in the vicinity of each Weyl node
\mathbf{K}_{\xi}
, one obtains the effective Hamiltonian
Pikulin et al. 2016

\displaystyle H_{\xi}(\mathbf{k})=v_{\xi}^{j}\tau^{j}\left(\hbar k_{j}-\xi\frac{e}{c}A_{j}^{S}\right),

(9)

where we can identify the presence of the strain-induced ”gauge field”
Pikulin et al. 2016

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar c}{ea}\left(u_{13}\sin(aQ),u_{23}\sin(aQ),u_{33}\cot(aQ)\right).

(10)

A particular case, applicable to a crystal in the shape of a cylindrical rod of length
L
along the z-axis, and subjected to a uniform mechanical torsion by a total angle
\theta
around the axis, is given by the vector displacement field
Pikulin et al. 2016
;
Arjona and Vozmediano 2018
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}=\theta\frac{z}{L}\left(\mathbf{r}\times\hat{z}\right).

(11)

Here
\mathbf{r}
represents the radial vector from the z-axis of the cylindrical rod of material. The only non-zero components of the strain tensor for this case are
u_{13}=\left(\theta/2L\right)y
and
u_{23}=-\left(\theta/2L\right)x
, respectively. For this particular torsional strain pattern, the corresponding gauge field (in the limit
Qa\ll 1
) is given by
Pikulin et al. 2016
;
Arjona and Vozmediano 2018

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{S}=\frac{B_{S}}{2}\left(-y,x,0\right),

(12)

with
B_{S}=\left(\hbar c/e\right)\left(\theta Q/L\right)
the magnitude of the associated ”pseudo-magnetic field” defined via the curl
Pikulin et al. 2016
;
Arjona and Vozmediano 2018

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{S}\equiv\nabla\times\mathbf{A}^{S}=\hat{z}B_{S}.

(13)

Clearly, this result indicates that even though the torsional strain-induced gauge field grows linearly with distance after Eq. (
10
) or Eq. (
12
), the associated pseudo-magnetic field that carries over the physical consequences of this effect turns out to be constant and uniform across the cylindrical sample. There is of course a gauge freedom involved in the particular representation for the gauge field that induces this constant pseudo-magnetic field. For instance, a
U(1)
-transformation to the Landau gauge
\mathbf{A}^{S}\rightarrow B_{S}(0,x,0)
will represent exactly the same pseudo-magnetic field
Pikulin et al. 2016
;
Arjona and Vozmediano 2018
.

In previous works
Muñoz and Soto-Garrido 2019
;
Soto-Garrido et al. 2020
;
Bonilla et al. 2021
, we studied quasi-ballistic transport through a nano-junction in a WSM with a single torsional dislocation, in combination with an external physical magnetic field. For this system, we obtained the electronic
Muñoz and Soto-Garrido 2019
;
Soto-Garrido et al. 2020
and thermoelectric
Muñoz and Soto-Garrido 2019
;
Bonilla et al. 2021
transport coefficients, using the Landauer ballistic formalism in combination with a mathematical analysis for the quantum mechanical scattering cross-section
Muñoz and Soto-Garrido 2017
. More recently, we also considered the case of a diluted, uniform concentration of torsional dislocations and their effects on the electrical conductivity of type I WSMs
Bonilla and Muñoz 2022
, in the Kubo linear-response formalism combined with vertex corrections
Bonilla and Muñoz 2022
in a Bethe-Salpeter equation.

In the present work, our purpose is to study, from a field theoretical renormalization group perspective, the effects of a pseudo-magnetic field arising from a combination of an external magnetic field and the presence of torsional strain in a generic type I Weyl semimetal with a minimal number of two nodes. As discussed in the text, such a combined magneto-strain pseudo-magnetic field breaks the symmetry between the Weyl nodes and in principle can achieve extremely high intensities tanks to the strain component. Therefore, our calculations are mainly focused on the role of LLLs. We shall explore the emergence of Peierls instability leading to a CDW phase, and how the presence of strain, particularly in its role at breaking the nodal symmetry, may affect the emergence of such strongly-correlated phase.

The paper is organized as follows: In section
II
, we present the effective Hamiltonian model at the single-particle level, while in section
III
we define the different interaction terms. In section
IV
we present the Kadanoff-Wilson scheme for the renormalization group (RG) analysis of the model, and in section
V
we specialize it to the restriction of the LLL subspace, in order to obtain the corresponding RG flow equations for the couplings. In section
VII
we solve the RG equations, in order to analyze the emergence of the Peierls instability and its corresponding critical temperature. Finally, we present our discussion and conclusions. All mathematical derivations and details are presented in a set of Appendixes.

II
The model

We start by considering a minimal low-energy continuum model for a Weyl semimetal involving two nodes of opposite chirality, represented by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\xi}=\xi b_{0}\sigma_{0}+\xi v_{\xi}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}-\hbar\mathbf{K}_{\xi}\right).

(14)

Here
\xi=\pm
is the chirality index of each Weyl node,
v_{\xi}
is the Fermi velocity at each node,
b_{0}
is the local shift from the zero energy,
\sigma_{i}
with
i=1,2,3
are the Pauli matrices, and
\sigma_{0}
is the identity matrix. The location of each Weyl node in momentum space is
\mathbf{K}_{\xi}=k_{\xi}\mathbf{\hat{z}}
.
We shall consider the presence of an external magnetic field along the
\hat{z}
-direction
\mathbf{B}_{0}=\hat{z}B_{0}
, that corresponds to the curl of a gauge field (in Landau gauge)
\mathbf{A}_{0}=\hat{y}xB_{0}
. In addition, we shall also consider the presence of torsional strain, modeled by a pseudo-gauge field
Cortijo et al. 2015
;
Cortijo et al. 2016
;
Arjona and Vozmediano 2018

\mathbf{A}^{S}_{\xi}=\xi\hat{y}xB_{S}
that exhibits opposite directions depending on the chirality of each node
\xi=\pm
. The corresponding superposition of the magnetic and elastic gauge fields is thus given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\xi}=\mathbf{A}_{0}+\mathbf{A}^{S}_{\xi}=\hat{y}x\left(B_{0}+\xi B_{S}\right)\equiv\hat{y}xB_{\xi},

(15)

thus clearly leading to an effective pseudo-magnetic field
B_{\xi}=B_{0}+\xi B_{S}
with different magnitudes depending on the chirality of each node. This effect is incorporated by minimal coupling to the Hamiltonian Eq. (
14
)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\xi}=\xi b_{0}\sigma_{0}+\xi v_{\xi}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\left[\mathbf{p}+e\mathbf{A}_{\xi}(x)\right],

(16)

where, for convenience, we define the momentum operators at each node

\mathbf{p}-\hbar\mathbf{K}_{\xi}\rightarrow\mathbf{p}
. Since this effective single-particle Hamiltonian depends on the spatial coordinate
x
, we need to define an appropriate eigenbasis to construct the corresponding many-body theory. As shown in detail in Appendix
A
, this turns out to be the spinor eigenfunctions

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{x})=\frac{e^{-i\left(X_{\xi}/\ell_{\xi}^{2}\right)y+i(k+k_{\xi})z}}{\sqrt{L_{y}L_{z}}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{\xi\lambda nk}\,\varphi_{n-1}(x-X_{\xi})\\
v_{\xi\lambda nk}\,\varphi_{n}(x-X_{\xi})\end{pmatrix}

(17)

where we defined the coefficients

\displaystyle u_{\xi\lambda nk}=\frac{i\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1+\frac{\hbar v_{\xi}k}{\lambda\epsilon_{\xi nk}}},\,\,\,v_{\xi\lambda nk}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{\hbar v_{\xi}k}{\lambda\epsilon_{\xi nk}}},

(18)

with
\lambda=\pm
the band index and

\epsilon_{\xi nk}=\hbar v_{\xi}\sqrt{k^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{\xi}^{2}}}

(19)

the energy eigenvalues (for
n\geq 0
) for discrete Landau levels. Here,
k\in\mathbb{R}
is the continuum component of the momentum eigenvalue in the
z
-direction. We also define here the ”magnetic length” (or Landau radius) associated with the magnitude of the pseudo-magnetic field at each Weyl node

\ell_{\xi}=\sqrt{\hbar/|eB_{\xi}|}.

(20)

The corresponding eigenfunctions representing each spinor component, and centered at the ”nodal guiding center”
X_{\xi}=\hbar k_{2}/(eB_{\xi})
are given by (see Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{2^{n}n!}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi\ell_{\xi}^{2}}\right)^{1/4}e^{-x^{2}/2\ell_{\xi}^{2}}\,H_{n}\left(\frac{x}{\ell_{\xi}}\right),

(21)

with
H_{n}(z)
a Hermite polynomial of order
n\geq 0
. The single-particle spectrum at the non-interacting level of the model is depicted in Fig.
1
, where
\lambda=\pm
represents the band index in the non-interacting energy spectrum.

Figure 1
:
Non-interacting energy dispersion in Eq. (
19
), illustrated for the choice of parameters

,
v_{-}=1.5v_{+}
,
\ell_{-}=\ell_{+}/\sqrt{0.7}
, and
k_{\pm}=\pm\,5/\ell_{+}
. The corresponding Fermi energy is
0.5\hbar v_{+}/\ell_{+}

It is important to distinguish the particular role of the lowest Landau level
n=0
,
\lambda=-1
, whose spinor eigenfunction is given by

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{x})_{n=0,\lambda=-1}=\frac{e^{-i\left(X_{\xi}/\ell_{\xi}^{2}\right)y+i(k+k_{\xi})z}}{\sqrt{2L_{y}L_{z}}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\varphi_{0}(x-X_{\xi})\end{pmatrix}.

(22)

In terms of this eigenbasis, we can now construct the corresponding many-particle Hamiltonian by defining annihilation
c_{\alpha}
and creation
c_{\alpha}^{\dagger}

\left\{c_{\alpha},c^{\dagger}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right\}=\delta_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
, for a multi-indexed label

\alpha\equiv\left(\lambda,\xi,n,X_{\xi},k\right)
, such that

\displaystyle H_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi=\pm}\sum_{\lambda=\pm}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dX_{\xi}\,c_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\left[\lambda\,\epsilon_{\xi nk}\right]c_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\SumInt_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}.

III
Interactions

Let us now consider the fully interacting many-body Hamiltonian for our system,

\displaystyle H=H_{e}+H_{ph}+H_{e-ph}+H_{ee}.

(32)

Here, we introduced the phonon Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{ph}=\sum_{\mathbf{q},j}\hbar\nu_{\mathbf{q},j}^{0}\left(a_{\mathbf{q},j}^{\dagger}a_{\mathbf{q},j}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

(33)

where
a_{\textbf{q},j}
and
a_{\textbf{q},j}^{\dagger}
are the annihilation and creation operators for the phonon modes with momentum
q
and polarization
j
, which satisfy the bosonic commutation relations
\left[a_{\textbf{q},j},a_{\textbf{q}^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right]=\delta_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
. Likewise,
\nu_{\mathbf{q},j}^{0}
is the corresponding unrenormalized (or bare) frequency for such vibrational modes.

In particular, we shall focus on acoustic phonon branches such that
the electron-phonon interaction is described by the Hamiltonian

H_{\text{e-ph}}=\SumInt_{\alpha}\SumInt_{\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{\textbf{q},j}\tilde{g}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},j}^{\text{ep}}(\textbf{q})c^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha}c_{\alpha^{\prime}}\left(a^{{\dagger}}_{\textbf{q},j}+a_{-\textbf{q},j}\right),

(34)

where the couplings

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{ep}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},j}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{\xi\xi^{\prime},j}(\textbf{q})\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{q})

(35)

are determined by the matrix elements in the magnetic Landau eigenbasis as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{q})=\int d^{3}x\;\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha}(\textbf{x})e^{i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{x}}\Psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{x}).

(36)

Here
\tilde{g}_{\xi\xi^{\prime},j}(\textbf{q})
is the deformation potential coefficient
Kundu et al. 2022

\tilde{g}_{\xi\xi^{\prime},j}(\textbf{q})\sim\frac{\hbar|\textbf{q}|^{2}d^{2}_{\text{ac},j}}{2\rho\mathcal{V}\nu_{\mathbf{q},j}^{0}}=\frac{\hbar|\textbf{q}|d^{2}_{\text{ac},j}}{2\rho\mathcal{V}c_{j}}\,,

(37)

and
d_{\text{ac},j}
represents the acoustic deformation potential, for
\nu_{\mathbf{q},j}^{0}=c_{j}|\textbf{q}|
the unperturbed phonon frequency in the acoustic branch
j
. We also defined
\mathcal{V}=L_{x}L_{y}L_{z}
as the volume of the system and
\rho
as the ion mass density.

Finally, the electron-electron interaction is given by the Hamiltonian

H_{ee}=\frac{1}{2\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\textbf{q}}\SumInt_{\alpha}\tilde{g}^{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha_{1}}c_{\alpha_{3}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha_{2}}c_{\alpha_{4}}\,,

(38)

with a coupling constant given in terms of the interaction matrix elements in the magnetic Landau eigenbasis, as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V(\textbf{q})\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{3}}(\textbf{q})\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha_{4}}(-\textbf{q}).

(39)

Here,
V(\textbf{q})=e^{2}/(\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\infty}\textbf{q}^{2})
is the Fourier transform of the unscreened Coulomb potential and
\epsilon_{\infty}
is the high-frequency dielectric constant.

IV
Field theoretical representation and renormalization analysis

Since our main interest is to study the renormalization flow of the coupling parameters involved in the different terms of the interacting Hamiltonian, it is technically necessary to switch to a field-theoretical representation. In this language, and by means of the standard techniques involving coherent states for both fermions and bosons, the functional integral representation for the partition function of the model at finite temperature
T>0
is given by

\mathcal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}\Psi^{{\dagger}}\mathcal{D}\Psi\mathcal{D}\Phi\,e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}S\left[\Psi^{{\dagger}},\Psi,\Phi\right]},

(40)

where the action
S
corresponds to the sum of the following terms:

\displaystyle S^{0}_{e}\left[\Psi^{{\dagger}},\Psi\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\SumInt_{\alpha}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\left[\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\alpha}(\omega_{n})\right]^{-1}\Psi_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(\omega_{n})\Psi_{\alpha}(\omega_{n}),

\displaystyle S^{0}_{ph}\left[\Phi\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{q},j}\sum_{\nu_{m}}\mathcal{D}_{j,0}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})\left|\Phi_{j}(\textbf{q},\nu_{m})\right|^{2},

(50)

\displaystyle S_{ee}\left[\Psi^{{\dagger}},\Psi\right]=\frac{\pi v_{F}}{\beta\mathcal{V}}\SumInt_{\alpha}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\sum_{\textbf{q}}g_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}(\textbf{q})\Psi_{\alpha_{1}}^{{\dagger}}(\omega_{n_{1}})

\displaystyle\times\Psi_{\alpha_{2}}^{{\dagger}}(\omega_{n_{2}})\Psi_{\alpha_{3}}(\omega_{n_{3}})\Psi_{\alpha_{4}}(\omega_{n_{1}+n_{2}-n_{3}}),

(60)

\displaystyle S_{ep}\left[\Psi^{{\dagger}},\Psi,\Phi\right]=\sqrt{\frac{\pi v_{F}}{\beta\mathcal{V}}}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\sum_{\nu_{m}}\sum_{\textbf{q}j}\SumInt_{\alpha}\SumInt_{\alpha^{\prime}}g^{ep}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle\times\Psi_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}(\omega_{n}+\nu_{m})\Psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\omega_{n})\Phi_{j}(\textbf{q},\nu_{m}),

(78)

with
\beta=1/(k_{B}T)
the inverse temperature,
\omega_{n}=\pi(2n+1)/\beta
and
\nu_{m}=2\pi m/\beta
fermionic and bosonic Matsubara frequencies for
n,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}
respectively.
For technical reasons, we redefine the couplings as follows:

\displaystyle g^{ep}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},j}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{\nu_{\mathbf{q},j}^{0}\mathcal{V}}{\pi v_{F}}}\tilde{g}^{ep}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},j}(\textbf{q})

(79)

\displaystyle g_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{g}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}(\textbf{q})}{2\pi\hbar v_{F}}\,,

(80)

where we have introduced the Fermi velocity at the
\xi=+
node as
v_{F}\equiv v_{+}
so that at the opposite node we have

v_{-}\equiv v_{F}+\delta v\,,

(81)

with
\delta v=v_{-}-v_{+}
representing a possible nodal asymmetry.
We also defined the electron and phonon bare Green’s functions in Matsubara space by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\alpha}(\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-i\hbar\omega_{n}+\epsilon_{\alpha}-\mu)^{-1}

(82)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{q},j}^{0}(\nu_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\nu_{m}^{2}+\left[\nu_{\mathbf{q},j}^{0}\right]^{2}\right]^{-1}.

(83)

Now, we shall establish a renormalization flow analysis of the model. Following Wilson’s procedure, we introduce a cutoff for the
z
-component of the momentum
k
, defined by
\Lambda_{n}(l)=\Lambda_{n}e^{-l}
for each Landau level
n
, with
l>0
. We separate the momentum components of the fermion fields accordingly, such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{\alpha}(\omega_{m})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Psi_{\alpha}(\omega_{m})\quad&,\,k<\Lambda_{n}e^{-l}\\
\bar{\Psi}_{\alpha}(\omega_{m})\quad&,\,\Lambda_{n}e^{-l}\leq k<\Lambda_{n}\\
\end{cases}

(84)

Hence, the partition function is now given by

\mathcal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}\Psi^{{\dagger}}\mathcal{D}\Psi\mathcal{D}\phi\int_{>}\mathcal{D}\bar{\Psi}^{{\dagger}}\mathcal{D}\bar{\Psi}\,e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}S\left[\Psi^{{\dagger}}+\bar{\Psi}^{{\dagger}},\Psi+\bar{\Psi},\phi\right]}\,,

(85)

where the subindex

stands for integrating over the large momentum
\Lambda_{n}e^{-l}\leq k<\Lambda_{n}

By taking into account the orthogonality between the low-
\Psi_{\alpha}(\omega_{n})
and high momentum
\bar{\Psi}_{\alpha}(\omega_{n})

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\Psi^{{\dagger}}\mathcal{D}\Psi\mathcal{D}\phi\,e^{-S\left[\Psi^{{\dagger}},\Psi,\phi\right]}\int_{>}\mathcal{D}\bar{\Psi}^{{\dagger}}\mathcal{D}\bar{\Psi}\,e^{-S^{0}_{e}\left[\bar{\Psi}^{{\dagger}},\bar{\Psi}\right]}e^{-S_{I}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\int\mathcal{D}\Psi^{{\dagger}}\mathcal{D}\Psi\mathcal{D}\phi\,e^{-S\left[\Psi^{{\dagger}},\Psi,\phi\right]}\braket{e^{-S_{I}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\int\mathcal{D}\Psi^{{\dagger}}\mathcal{D}\Psi\mathcal{D}\phi\,e^{-S\left[\Psi^{{\dagger}},\Psi,\phi\right]}e^{-\braket{S_{I}}\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\left(\braket{S_{I}^{2}}-\braket{S_{I}}^{2}\right)\,+\,...}\,

where we defined the effective interaction

\displaystyle S_{I}=S_{ee}\left[\Psi^{\dagger}+\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger},\Psi+\bar{\Psi}\right]+S_{e-ph}\left[\Psi^{\dagger}+\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger},\Psi+\bar{\Psi},\Phi\right]

Here we used the cumulant expansion, where
\braket{\cdot\cdot}
denotes the ensemble average for the given operator, integrated over the field components
\bar{\Psi}_{\alpha}(\omega_{n})
in the high-momentum sector,
i.e.,

\Lambda_{n}e^{-l}\leq k<\Lambda_{n}
, and
\mathcal{N}
is a global constant. Each of these ensemble averages, leading to correlation functions of different orders, is computed by means of a Feynman diagram expansion, as explained in detail in the Appendix
C
.

V
Explicit renormalization in the high pseudo-magnetic field regime

Along this section, we shall apply the general one-loop renormalization scheme discussed before, to obtain explicit results in the regime of very strong pseudo-magnetic fields at both nodes. This physical condition is in principle possible to achieve in the presence of strain, since the pseudo-magnetic fields related to strain are predicted to be much stronger
Cortijo et al. 2015
;
Cortijo et al. 2016
;
Arjona and Vozmediano 2018
;
Lin et al. 2022
than the actual magnetic fields that can be imposed under normal laboratory conditions
B_{0}/B_{S}\ll 1
, and hence we have
\ell_{\xi}^{2}\sim B_{S}^{-1}\ll k_{F}^{-1}
. In this regime, the expansion over Landau levels is highly dominated (due to the presence of a large gap) by the lowest Landau level.

We first need to evaluate the matrix elements according to Eq. (
36
) for the lowest Landau level, with
\alpha=\left(\lambda=-1,\xi,n=0,X_{\xi},k\right)
. For backward tunneling,
where
\bar{\xi}\equiv-\xi
,
\alpha^{\prime}=(\lambda^{\prime}=-1,\bar{\xi},n^{\prime}=0,X^{\prime}_{\bar{\xi}},k^{\prime})
, we obtain (see Appendix
B
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{q})=\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\delta_{k^{\prime}+k_{\bar{\xi}},k+k_{\xi}-q_{z}}\delta_{X^{\prime}_{\bar{\xi}}/\ell^{2}_{\bar{\xi}},X_{\xi}/\ell^{2}_{\xi}+q_{y}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\ell_{G}^{4}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}q_{x}^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{4}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}q_{y}^{2}}e^{\frac{i}{2}\frac{\ell_{G}^{4}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}q_{x}q_{y}}e^{i\frac{\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}q_{x}X_{\xi}},

(88)

where for notational convenience we defined the magnetic length scales

\ell_{A}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\ell_{+}^{2}+\ell_{-}^{2}}{2}}\hskip 11.38109pt\text{and}\hskip 11.38109pt\ell_{G}\equiv\sqrt{\ell_{+}\ell_{-}}\,.

(89)

On the other hand, for forward tunneling (where
\xi^{\prime}=\xi
) the corresponding matrix element reduces to the expression (see Appendix
B
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{q})=\delta_{k^{\prime},k-q_{z}}\delta_{X^{\prime}_{\xi},X_{\xi}+\ell^{2}_{\xi}q_{y}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{1}{4}\ell_{\xi}^{2}(q_{x}^{2}+q_{y}^{2})}e^{\frac{i}{2}\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{x}q_{y}}e^{iq_{x}X_{\xi}}\,.

(90)

We shall consider only the LLL (
n=0
,
\lambda=-1
). Using the deltas to sum over
X_{2,\bar{\xi}}
and
k_{2}
, and defining

\xi b\equiv k_{\xi}-k_{\bar{\xi}}\,,

(91)

with
b\equiv k_{+}-k_{-}>0
, we obtain the simplified form of the electron-phonon component of the action:

\displaystyle S_{ep}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi v_{F}}{\beta\mathcal{V}}}\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\sum_{\xi}\sum_{\omega_{n}\nu_{m}}\sum_{\textbf{q},j}\sum_{X_{1,\xi}}\sum_{k_{1}}

(92)

\displaystyle\times z_{\xi\bar{\xi}}\,g_{\xi\bar{\xi},j}e^{i\frac{\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}q_{x}X_{1,\xi}}\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi,X_{1,\xi}}(k_{1},\omega_{n}+\nu_{m})

\displaystyle\times\Psi_{\bar{\xi},\varepsilon_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}X_{1,\xi}+\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}q_{y}}(k_{1}+\xi b-q_{z},\omega_{n})\Phi_{j}(\textbf{q},\nu_{m}),\,

where we included the vertex renormalization factor
z_{\xi\bar{\xi}}=\sqrt{z_{\xi}z_{\bar{\xi}}}
arising from the field strength renormalization
\Psi_{\xi X_{\xi}}\rightarrow\sqrt{z_{\xi}}\Psi_{\xi X_{\xi}}
and
\Psi_{\bar{\xi}X_{\bar{\xi}}}\rightarrow\sqrt{z_{\bar{\xi}}}\Psi_{\bar{\xi}X_{\bar{\xi}}}
, respectively.
Let us now consider the electron-electron interaction. For this purpose, we will distinguish the short-wavelength part of the Coulomb interaction, involved in backward scattering, from the long-wavelength part, which is mainly involved in forward scattering.

Let us start from the backward scattering contribution by defining the distance in momentum space between both nodal momenta,

2k_{F}\equiv k_{+}-k_{-}\,.

(93)

We further define the backward Coulomb coupling
g_{1}
(at the bare level) as an average over the sphere
S
of radius
\ell_{A}/\ell_{G}^{2}
, as this length scale defines the support in
q_{x}
of the gaussian exponentials in Eq. (
88
),

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi\hbar v_{F}}\frac{\int_{S}d^{2}q_{\perp}\,V(2k_{F}\mathbf{\hat{z}}+\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{\int_{S}d^{2}q_{\perp}}

(94)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}\ell_{G}^{4}}{\pi\hbar v_{F}\ell_{A}^{2}\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\infty}}\log\left(1+\frac{\ell_{A}^{2}}{4k_{F}^{2}\ell_{G}^{4}}\right).

In Fig.
2
(top panel), we show a plot of the bare
g_{1}

B_{0}/B_{S}
. Therefore, the bare value of
g_{1}
decreases as the physical magnetic field
B_{0}
becomes comparable in magnitude to the pseudo-magnetic field
B_{S}
due to mechanical strain.

The ”backward” electron-electron interaction contribution to the action is thus given by the expression

\displaystyle S_{ee}^{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{1}\frac{\pi v_{F}}{2\beta\mathcal{V}}\frac{\ell_{G}^{2}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}\sum_{\xi\textbf{q}}\sum_{\{\omega_{n}\}}\sum_{X_{1,\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}e^{i\frac{\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}q_{x}\left(X_{1,\xi}-\varepsilon_{\xi}^{2}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}\right)}\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi X_{1,\xi}}(k_{1},\omega_{n_{1}})\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}(k_{2},\omega_{n_{2}})

(95)

\displaystyle\times\,\Psi_{\xi,\varepsilon_{\xi}^{2}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}-\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{y}}(k_{2}-\xi b+q_{z},\omega_{n_{3}})\Psi_{\bar{\xi},\varepsilon_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}X_{1,\xi}+\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}q_{y}}(k_{1}+\xi b-q_{z},\omega_{n_{1}+n_{2}-n_{3}})\,.

Performing a similar analysis for the long-wavelength component of the Coulomb interaction, named forward scattering, the corresponding contribution to the action is

\displaystyle S_{ee}^{F}=g_{2}\frac{\pi v_{F}}{2\beta\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\xi\textbf{q}}\sum_{\{\omega_{n}\}}\sum_{X_{1,\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}e^{iq_{x}\left(X_{1,\xi}-X_{2,\bar{\xi}}\right)}

\displaystyle\times\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi X_{1,\xi}}(k_{1},\omega_{n_{1}})\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}(k_{2},\omega_{n_{2}})\Psi_{\bar{\xi},X_{2,\bar{\xi}}-\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}q_{y}}(k_{2}+q_{z},\omega_{n_{3}})

\displaystyle\times\Psi_{\xi,X_{1,\xi}+\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{y}}(k_{1}-q_{z},\omega_{n_{1}+n_{2}-n_{3}})\,,

(96)

where the forward (long-range) Coulomb coupling
g_{2}
is defined by

\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{V(\mathbf{q})}{\epsilon(\mathbf{q},0)}\equiv g_{2}\pi\hbar v_{F}\,,

(97)

with

\epsilon(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})=1-V(\mathbf{q})\Pi_{\text{RPA}}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})

(98)

being the dielectric function in the random phase approximation (RPA), and

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{RPA}}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}}\SumInt_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\left|\int d^{3}x\;\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha}(\textbf{x})e^{i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{x}}\Psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{x})\right|^{2}

(108)

\displaystyle\times\frac{f_{0}(E_{\alpha})-f_{0}(E_{\alpha^{\prime}})}{E_{\alpha}-E_{\alpha^{\prime}}+\hbar\nu_{m}+i\eta}

is the electronic polarization function in RPA,
f_{0}(E)=\left(\exp(\beta(E-\mu))+1\right)^{-1}
is the Fermi distribution function, and
\eta\to 0^{+}
is an infinitesimal regulator. In the high pseudo-magnetic field regime, we can approximate our calculations by restricting ourselves to the chiral Landau level, by considering
\xi=\xi^{\prime}
and
n=n^{\prime}=0
in the sets of indices
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
.

As explained in detail in Appendix
E
, we obtain

g_{2}=4\pi\ell_{G}^{2}\,\frac{1+\frac{\delta v}{v_{F}}}{2\frac{\ell_{A}^{2}}{\ell_{G}^{2}}+\frac{\delta v}{v_{F}}\varepsilon_{-}}\,.

(109)

.

is not strictly monotonic and depends on

, it is clear from Fig.

V.1
One-loop renormalization of the electron-electron vertex

The electron-electron inter-nodal vertex will introduce corrections for the backward
g_{1}
and forward
g_{2}
couplings arising from 2 different channels. The Peierls’ channel (
\sim\bar{\Psi}_{+}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}\Psi_{+}+\Psi_{+}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}\bar{\Psi}_{+}
) represents the pairing of one electron and one hole with opposite chiralities, while the Cooper channel (
\sim\bar{\Psi}_{+}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}\Psi_{+}+\Psi_{+}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}\bar{\Psi}_{+}
) represents the pairing of two electrons (or two holes) with opposite chiralities. In addition, the Landau channel (
\sim\bar{\Psi}_{+}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}\bar{\Psi}_{+}+\Psi_{+}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}\Psi_{+}
) only contributes to intranodal scattering and therefore does not contribute to the renormalization of the
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
couplings.

V.1.1
Peierls channel

Let us first analyze the vertex corrections arising from the Peierls channel. Applying the general procedure described in Section
IV
, and restricting ourselves to the LLL subspace, the contribution from forward scattering is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\braket{\left(S^{F}_{ee}\right)_{P}^{2}}=2g_{2}^{2}\left(\frac{\pi v_{F}}{2\beta\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}\sum_{\xi\textbf{q}}\sum_{X^{\prime}_{1,\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}\sum_{k_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\sum_{q_{x}q_{y}}\sum_{\omega_{n^{\prime}_{1}}\omega_{n_{2}}\omega_{n^{\prime}_{3}}}e^{iq_{x}(X^{\prime}_{1,\xi}-X_{2,\bar{\xi}})}\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi X^{\prime}_{1,\xi}}(k^{\prime}_{1},\omega_{n^{\prime}_{1}})\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}(k_{2},\omega_{n_{2}})

(110)

\displaystyle\times\Psi_{\bar{\xi},X_{2,\bar{\xi}}-\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}q_{y}}(k_{2}+q_{z},\omega_{n^{\prime}_{3}})\Psi_{\xi,X^{\prime}_{1,\xi}+\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{y}}(k^{\prime}_{1}-q_{z},\omega_{n^{\prime}_{1}+n_{2}-n^{\prime}_{3}})\sum_{q^{\prime}_{x}q^{\prime}_{y}}\sum_{k\omega_{n_{1}}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\xi}(k+k^{\prime}_{1}-k_{2}-q_{z},\omega_{n_{1}})\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\bar{\xi}}(k,\omega_{n_{1}-n^{\prime}_{1}+n^{\prime}_{3}}).

(see Appendix
C
for details). Since we are computing the action within the LLL subspace, after Eq. (
19

n=0
) is
\epsilon_{n=0,\xi}=\xi\hbar v_{F}(k-k_{F_{\xi}})
, and hence the corresponding explicit expression for the free Fermion Green’s functions is

(111)

We can approximate the relative difference between external momenta in Eq. (
V.1.1
) as

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\xi}\left(k+\xi b-q_{z},\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\bar{\xi}}\left(k,\omega_{n}-\nu_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\xi}\left(k+2k_{F},\omega_{n}+\nu_{m}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\bar{\xi}}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right),

(112)

\Pi_{P}(\nu_{m})

\displaystyle\Pi_{P}(\nu_{m})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\left[\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k-2k_{F},\omega_{n}-\nu_{m}\right)\right.

(113)

\displaystyle\left.+\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k+2k_{F},\omega_{n}+\nu_{m}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta L_{z}}{2\pi\hbar v_{F}}\cdot\frac{dl}{1+\delta v/2v_{F}}\lambda_{P}(T,\delta v,\nu_{m},l),

Here, we defined

\displaystyle\lambda_{P}(T,\delta v,\nu_{m},l)\equiv\frac{1}{1-i\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Lambda(l)}}\frac{1}{4}\left[2\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda(l)}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda(l)}{2}\gamma\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda(l)}{2\gamma}\right)\right]\,.

(114)

In Fig.
3
, we represent

Defining

\displaystyle\left(\frac{dg_{2}}{dl}\right)_{P}=g_{2}^{2}\frac{1}{4\pi\ell_{A}^{2}}\frac{\lambda_{P}(l)}{1+\delta v/2v_{F}}.

(115)

Let us now consider the contribution to the Peierls channel arising from backward scattering. Following the general procedure described in Section
IV

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\braket{\left(S^{B}_{ee}\right)_{P}^{2}}=-2g_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{\pi v_{F}}{2\beta\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}\frac{\ell_{G}^{4}}{\ell_{A}^{4}}\sum_{\xi\textbf{q}}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}k_{2}}\sum_{\{\omega_{n}\}}

\displaystyle\times\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi X^{\prime}_{1,\xi}}(k^{\prime}_{1},\omega_{n_{1}})\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}(k_{2},\omega_{n_{2}})

Cooper channel

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\braket{\left(S^{B}_{ee}\right)_{C}^{2}}=2\left(\frac{\pi v_{F}}{2\beta\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}\frac{\ell_{G}^{4}}{\ell_{A}^{4}}\sum_{\xi\textbf{q}^{\prime}}\sum_{X_{1,\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}\sum_{\{\omega_{n}\}}

\displaystyle\braket{S_{ep}S^{F}_{ee}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi v_{F}}{\beta\mathcal{V}}}\frac{\pi v_{F}}{2\beta\mathcal{V}}\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\sum_{\xi}\sum_{\textbf{q}\,j}\sum_{\textbf{q}^{\prime}}\sum_{X_{1,\xi}k_{1}}\sum_{\omega_{n_{1}}}g_{2}z_{\xi\bar{\xi},j}g_{\xi\bar{\xi}}e^{i\frac{\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}q^{\prime}_{x}X_{1,\xi}}\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi,X_{1,\xi}}(k_{1},\omega_{n_{1}})

V.3
One-loop renormalization of the phonon propagator

The electron-phonon interaction is expressed by the term defined by Eq. (
92
) in the action. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle S_{ep}^{2}\right\rangle_{dl}=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{v_{F}}{\beta L_{z}}\sum_{\xi}\sum_{\mathbf{q},j,\nu_{m}}\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}z^{2}_{\xi\bar{\xi}}g^{2}_{\xi\bar{\xi},j}\frac{1}{\ell_{G}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{G}_{\xi}^{0}(k,\omega_{n})\mathcal{G}_{\bar{\xi}}^{0}(k+\xi b-q_{z},\omega_{n}+\nu_{m})|\Phi_{j}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{q},j,\nu_{m}}\frac{\lambda_{P}(T,\delta v,\nu_{m},l)}{2}dl\frac{z^{2}_{+-}g^{2}_{+-,j}|\Phi_{j}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})|^{2}}{2\pi\hbar(1+\delta v/2v_{F})\ell_{G}^{2}}

where we have used the definition of the magnetic lengths in Eq. (
20
), and the result given in Eq. (
113
). Then, to order

we have that the inverse of the renormalized phonon propagator is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{j}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m},l+dl)=\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{j}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m},l)

(140)

\displaystyle-\frac{g^{2}_{+-,j}}{4\pi\ell_{G}^{2}(1+\delta v/2v_{F})}\,z^{2}_{+-}(l)\lambda_{P}(l)dl,

and therefore we obtain the corresponding differential equation for the renormalization flow as follows

\frac{d}{dl}\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{j}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m},l)=-\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}g^{2}_{+-,j}\,\lambda_{P}(l)\,z^{2}_{+-}(l).

(141)

The solution of this differential equation, subject to the initial condition
\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{j}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m},l=0)=\mathcal{D}_{j,0}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})
after Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{j}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m},l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{m}^{2}+\nu_{0,j}^{2}(\mathbf{q})\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{G}g^{\prime 2}_{j}\,}{2}\chi(l)\right),

(142)

where we defined

. Clearly, the expression in parentheses represents a renormalization of the phonon dispersion relation as a consequence of interactions. In this expression, we identify the CDW susceptibility for the electron system

\displaystyle\chi(l)=\int_{0}^{l}z^{2}_{+-}(l^{\prime})\lambda_{P}(l^{\prime})dl^{\prime},

(143)

and therefore the vanishing of the term in brackets in Eq. (

VI
Running Couplings

In this section, we shall analyze the flow of the renormalized couplings in our model. We shall also identify the possible existence of fixed points. In the present work, we shall focus on the adiabatic limit
\nu_{m}\to 0
.

VI.1
Fixed Points in the fermion sector

Let us start with the electronic sector of the running couplings, which is defined by the system of non-linear equations (
V.1.3
) and (
V.1.3
). For technical reasons, we express it in matrix form

\frac{d}{dl}\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{g}.

(144)

Here,
\mathbf{g}\equiv\left(g_{1},g_{2}\right)^{T}
is the two-component vector for the couplings, while the matrix

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{g})\equiv\begin{pmatrix}-a_{1}g_{1}&2a_{2}g_{1}\\
-a_{3}g_{1}&a_{2}g_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(145)

with

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}\lambda_{P}

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}\left(\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\right)^{2}\left(\lambda_{P}-\lambda_{C}\right)

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}\frac{\left(\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\right)^{4}}{\sqrt{2-\ell_{G}^{4}/\ell_{A}^{4}}}\lambda_{C}\,.

(146)

For the system (
144
), the fixed points
(g_{1}^{*},g_{2}^{*})
are given by the stationary condition
d\mathbf{g}/dl=0
,
i.e.

\begin{pmatrix}-a_{1}g_{1}^{*}&2a_{2}g_{1}^{*}\\
-a_{3}g_{1}^{*}&a_{2}g_{2}^{*}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}^{*}\\
g_{2}^{*}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(147)

We clearly have the trivial solution
g_{1}^{*}=g_{2}^{*}=0
, corresponding to the non-interacting Gaussian fixed point where the two couplings become irrelevant.

A second possible, non-trivial fixed point can thus be discovered when the matrix becomes singular

\det\begin{pmatrix}-a_{1}g_{1}^{*}&2a_{2}g_{1}^{*}\\
-a_{3}g_{1}^{*}&a_{2}g_{2}^{*}\end{pmatrix}=0\,.

(148)

The two possible cases are: (1) The trivial solution
g_{1}^{*}=g_{2}^{*}=0
already identified, and (2) the straight line
g_{2}^{*}=2(a_{3}/a_{1})g_{1}^{*}
,
i.e.

g_{2}^{*}=\frac{2\left(\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\right)^{4}}{\sqrt{2-\ell_{G}^{4}/\ell_{A}^{4}}}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{C}}{\lambda_{P}}\,g_{1}^{*}\,.

(149)

It is straightforward to check, by direct substitution onto the system (
144
), that this second case is only possible if the condition
a_{1}^{2}/4a_{3}=a_{2}
is fulfilled. In terms of the model parameters, this corresponds to the condition

\frac{\lambda_{C}}{\lambda_{P}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{C}}{\lambda_{P}}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\ell_{A}^{4}}{\ell_{G}^{4}}\sqrt{2\frac{\ell_{A}^{4}}{\ell_{G}^{4}}-1}\,,

(150)

which relates the parameters
\delta v
and
\Delta
with
B_{0}/B_{S}
and temperature
T
. Note that since
\lambda_{C},\lambda_{P}\geq 0
, this equation only admits a solution when
\lambda_{C}<\lambda_{P}
.
In order to study the possible existence of this straight line of fixed points, let us analyze the order of magnitude of the ratio
\ell_{A}/\ell_{G}
. To this aim, we define

\delta\equiv\frac{\ell_{-}^{2}-\ell_{+}^{2}}{2}

(151)

as a measure of the nodal asymmetry induced by the pseudo-magnetic field due to torsional strain,
such that

\ell_{\pm}^{2}=\ell_{A}^{2}\mp\delta\,.

(152)

Then, according to the definition in Eq. (
89
),

\displaystyle\ell_{G}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\ell_{+}^{2}\ell_{-}^{2}}=\ell_{A}^{2}\sqrt{1-\left(\delta/\ell_{A}^{2}\right)^{2}}\,.

(153)

The ratio in the last expression is

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\ell_{A}^{2}}=\frac{\ell_{-}^{2}/\ell_{+}^{2}-1}{\ell_{-}^{2}/\ell_{+}^{2}+1},

(154)

and therefore we have

\frac{\ell_{+}^{2}}{\ell_{-}^{2}}=\frac{|B_{-}|}{|B_{+}|}=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\,,

(155)

where we defined the ratio between the external magnetic field and the strain pseudo-magnetic field

\varepsilon\equiv\left|\frac{B_{0}}{B_{S}}\right|\,.

(156)

Then, substituting into Eq. (
154
), we obtain
\delta/\ell_{A}^{2}=\varepsilon
.

Finally, from Eq. (
153
) and assuming
\varepsilon<1
as previously stated, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{G}^{2}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\approx 1-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}=1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{B_{0}}{B_{S}}\right)^{2}\,.

(157)

Thus,

\frac{\ell_{A}^{2}}{\ell_{G}^{2}}\approx 1+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}=1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{B_{0}}{B_{S}}\right)^{2}\,.

(158)

Using this final result, the condition in Eq. (
150
) for the existence of the straight line of fixed points can be approximated as

\frac{\lambda_{C}}{\lambda_{P}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{C}}{\lambda_{P}}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left(1+2\frac{B_{0}^{2}}{B_{S}^{2}}\right)\,.

(159)

This is a quadratic equation in the ratio
y=\lambda_{C}/\lambda_{P}>0
,

\displaystyle y^{2}-y+\frac{1}{4}\left(1+2\frac{B_{0}^{2}}{B_{S}^{2}}\right)=0,

that does not possess any real solutions since its discriminant is negative.

Therefore, we conclude that the condition in Eq. (
150
) is not fulfilled in the regimes of strain and magnetic field assumed in our model, and hence the existence of this non-trivial fixed point is ruled out.

VI.2
Fixed points in the electron-phonon sector

After the renormalization flow of the electron-phonon vertex (see Eq. (
138
)), it is straightforward to conclude that it reaches a fixed point under the condition

g_{1}^{*}=\left(\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\right)^{2}g_{2}^{*}\,,

(160)

where this straight line thus represents an invariant submanifold of the
(g_{1},g_{2})
-manifold. We note that even though the beta function can change sign across this line, the renormalization factor
z_{\xi\bar{\xi}}(l)
cannot, since the solution to Eq. (
138
) is exponential and given the initial condition
z_{\xi\bar{\xi}}(l=0)=1
, it follows that

z_{\xi\bar{\xi}}(l)=e^{\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}\int_{0}^{l}dl^{\prime}\lambda_{P}(l^{\prime})\left(\left(\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\right)^{2}g_{2}(l^{\prime})-g_{1}(l^{\prime})\right)}>0\,.

(161)

Therefore, since
\lambda_{P}(l)>0
, we have that
z_{\xi\bar{\xi}}
is marginally irrelevant in the submanifold
g_{1}>\left(\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\right)^{2}g_{2}
, while it becomes relevant in the submanifold
g_{1}<\left(\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\right)^{2}g_{2}
. It is interesting to note that either in the absence of strain but with an external magnetic field present, or conversely in the presence of strain solely,
\ell_{G}=\ell_{A}
and hence the invariant submanifold reduces to the straight line
g_{1}^{*}=g_{2}^{*}
. In contrast, when both effects are present, the asymmetry factor
\left(\ell_{G}/\ell_{A}\right)^{2}=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}
controls the slope of such a straight line. Interestingly, if
\varepsilon=\left|B_{0}/B_{S}\right|\rightarrow 1
, the invariant manifold reduces to
g_{1}^{*}=0
, and sufficiently close to this particular limit the electron-phonon coupling
z_{\xi\bar{\xi}}
will be relevant for
g_{1}<0
, and marginally irrelevant for
g_{1}>0
.

Based on the general analysis leading to the renormalization of the phonon frequency, as presented in Eq. (
142
), it depends on the susceptibility
\chi(l)
determined by Eq. (
143
) as an integral of the electron-phonon coupling
z_{+-,j}(l)
. Therefore, in the submanifold
g_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\right)^{2}g_{2}
where this coupling is relevant, there will always be a Peierls instability at some finite critical temperature provided
\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}>0
. In the following sections, we shall explore this possibility and how the combination of strain and external magnetic field through the parameter
\varepsilon
determines the corresponding critical behavior. We shall consider
\varepsilon<1
, and hence the invariant submanifold in Eq. (
160
) becomes
g_{1}^{*}=\left(1-\varepsilon^{2}/2\right)g_{2}^{*}
.

VII
Renormalization Group analysis of the adiabatic Peierls instability

In what follows, we shall restrict ourselves to the adiabatic regime
\nu_{m}=0
. We shall also assume that the external magnetic field is much weaker than the pseudo-magnetic field due to strain, such that the ratio
\varepsilon=|B_{0}/B_{S}|\ll 1
. Therefore, the RG system of equations reduces to (up to
O(\varepsilon^{4})
)

\displaystyle\frac{dg_{1}}{dl}=-\frac{g_{1}^{2}}{2}\alpha_{G}\lambda_{P}+g_{1}g_{2}\alpha_{G}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)\left(\lambda_{P}-\lambda_{C}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{dg_{2}}{dl}=-\frac{g_{1}^{2}}{2}\alpha_{G}\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\right)\lambda_{C}+\frac{g_{2}^{2}}{2}\alpha_{G}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)\left(\lambda_{P}-\lambda_{C}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{d\log z_{\xi,\bar{\xi}}}{dl}=\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}\lambda_{P}\left(\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)g_{2}-g_{1}\right).

(162)

We note that this system, in the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, is equivalent to the one obtained in Ref.
Kundu et al. 2022
. Therefore, our analysis will represent a natural extension towards the asymmetric nodal condition induced by the combination of strain and magnetic field, when
\varepsilon\ll 1
.

VII.1
Adiabatic regime with mirror symmetry
\Delta=0

Let us first assume that, in the absence of magnetic field and strain, the two Weyl nodes are related by a mirror plane. Under this condition, the parameters
\Delta=0
and
\delta v=0
. If the pseudo-magnetic field arising from the combination of strain and external magnetic field is oriented along the z-direction, as we assume in our theoretical setup, then the mirror symmetry will be preserved. Under this condition, as can be directly verified by comparing Eq. (
122
) and Eq. (
114
), we have
\lambda_{P}=\lambda_{C}
, and the RG flow Eq. (
162
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{dg_{1}}{dl}=-\frac{g_{1}^{2}}{2}\alpha_{G}\lambda_{P},\,\,\,\,\,\frac{dg_{2}}{dl}=\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\right)\frac{dg_{1}}{dl}.

(163)

Therefore, the combination

\displaystyle\gamma_{G}\equiv\alpha_{G}\left[g_{2}(l)-\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\right)g_{1}(l)\right]

(164)

remains a marginal and non-universal constant. Moreover, solving for
g_{1}(l)
we have (for
\lambda_{P}\sim 1
at low temperatures)

\displaystyle g_{1}(l)=\frac{g_{1}(0)}{1+\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}g_{1}(0)\int_{0}^{l}\lambda_{P}(l^{\prime})dl^{\prime}}\sim\frac{g_{1}(0)}{1+\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}g_{1}(0)l},

(165)

thus verifying that the repulsive backward scattering flows towards its marginally irrelevant fixed point
g_{1}\rightarrow g_{1}^{*}=0
already identified in the previous section.

After substituting Eq. (
164
) into the third equation in the system (
162
), we notice that the electron-phonon vertex flows according to

\displaystyle\frac{d\log z_{\xi,\bar{\xi}}}{dl}=\frac{\gamma_{G}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)\lambda_{P}-\varepsilon^{2}\alpha_{G}\lambda_{P}g_{1}(l).

(166)

Integrating this equation combined with Eq. (
165
), and taking into account that
\lambda_{P}\sim 1
up to the temperature dependent cutoff
l<l_{T}
, with

l_{T}=\log\left(\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{k_{B}T}\right),

(167)

we obtain

\displaystyle z_{\xi,\bar{\xi}}(l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\gamma_{G}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{l}\lambda_{P}(l^{\prime})dl^{\prime}-\varepsilon^{2}\alpha_{G}\int_{0}^{l}\lambda_{P}(l^{\prime})g_{1}(l^{\prime})dl^{\prime}}

(168)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}{2}l},

were we defined the non universal exponent

\gamma_{\varepsilon}\equiv\gamma_{G}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)-\varepsilon^{2}g_{1}(0).

(169)

The Peierls instability arises when the renormalized phonon frequency (in the adiabatic limit
\nu_{m}=0
) in Eq. (
142
) vanishes, and thus the critical temperature
T_{c,0}^{j}
is defined after the condition

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}}{2}\chi(l_{T_{c,0}^{j}})=0.

(170)

Integrating Eq. (
143
) we obtain the susceptibility
\chi(l_{T_{c,0}^{j}})
, and then solving Eq. (
170
), we find the critical temperature for the
j
-phonon mode

\displaystyle T_{c,0}^{j}=\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{k_{B}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}}{2\gamma_{\varepsilon}+\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}}\right)^{1/\gamma_{\varepsilon}},

(171)

with the Peierls instability determined by the phonon mode with the highest critical temperature.
As clearly seen in Eq. (
169
), the exponent
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
that controls the critical temperature is determined by a competition between the Peierls and Cooper channels. This conclusion agrees with Ref.
Kundu et al. 2022
, both in the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, as well as in the combined presence of strain and an external magnetic field
\varepsilon>0
considered here, but disagrees with some previous mean-field studies
Zhang et al. 2016
;
Qin et al. 2020
that ignored the contribution from the Cooper channel. As mentioned in the previous section, for
\left(1-\varepsilon^{2}/2\right)g_{2}>g_{1}
and provided
\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}>0
, the Peierls instability will arise at a finite critical temperature. Moreover, in the limit
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0^{+}
, after Eq. (
171
) the critical temperature reduces to a BCS-like expression
T_{c,0}^{j}\rightarrow\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{k_{B}}\exp\left[-2/\left(\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}\right)\right]
. On the other hand, in the submanifold
\left(1-\varepsilon^{2}/2\right)g_{2}<g_{1}
, it is still possible to develop a Peierls instability, provided the electron-phonon coupling exceeds the threshold
\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}>-2\gamma_{\varepsilon}
. This second condition arises if the Thomas Fermi screening vector
q_{TF}>{\rm{max}}\left\{k_{F},\ell_{A}\right\}
(notice that
\ell_{G}\leq\ell_{A}
), and hence the tendency to suppress the Peierls instability in this region of parameter space is consistent with the quasi-1D behavior of the system in the presence of a strong pseudomagnetic field
Kundu et al. 2022
.

VII.2
Adiabatic regime without mirror symmetry
\Delta>0

Let us now consider the case when the mirror symmetry between the Weyl nodes of opposite chirality is already broken in the absence of a pseudomagnetic field, i.e.
\Delta>0
and
\delta v>0
.
We notice that when
\Delta>0
, the RG flows can be separated into two different regimes, as follows:
(1) For
l<l_{\Delta}=\log(\Lambda_{0}/\Delta)
, we have
\lambda_{P}\sim\lambda_{C}\sim 1
. In this first case, the flow equations are still determined by the system Eq. (
142
). Therefore, the corresponding expression for the Peierls critical temperature
T_{c,\Delta}^{j}
remains the same as in the mirror symmetric case Eq. (
171
), i.e.
T_{c,\Delta}^{j}=T_{c,0}^{j}
, provided
k_{B}T_{c,0}^{j}>\Delta
.

(2) For
l\geq l_{\Delta}
and
\Delta\geq k_{B}T_{c,0}^{j}
defined in Eq. (
171
), we have
\lambda_{C}\rightarrow 0
(due to the upper cutoff defined by
\Delta
in Eq. (
122
)), while
\lambda_{P}\sim 1
. Therefore, in this regime the flow equations reduce to

\displaystyle\frac{dg_{1}}{dl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{1}^{2}}{2}\alpha_{G}+g_{1}g_{2}\alpha_{G}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{dg_{2}}{dl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{2}^{2}}{2}\alpha_{G}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{d\log z_{+-}}{dl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}\left[\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)g_{2}-g_{1}\right]

(172)

Combining the first two equations, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dl}\left[\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)g_{2}-g_{1}\right]=\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}\lambda_{P}\left[\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)g_{2}-g_{1}\right]^{2},

which can be integrated for
l>l_{\Delta}
, to obtain

\displaystyle\left[\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)g_{2}-g_{1}\right](l)=\frac{\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}/\alpha_{G}}{1-\frac{\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}}{2}\left(l-l_{\Delta}\right)},

(174)

where we defined

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}\equiv\alpha_{G}\left[\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)g_{2}(\Delta)-g_{1}(\Delta)\right].

(175)

It is clear from Eq. (
174
) that the combination of couplings reaches a singularity at a characteristic temperature defined by
l_{T_{0}^{*}}=\log\left(\Lambda_{0}/k_{B}T_{0}^{*}\right)>l_{\Delta}
, defined by the pole

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}}{2}\left(l_{T_{0}^{*}}-l_{\Delta}\right)=0.

(176)

Solving this equation, we find the transition temperature

\displaystyle T_{0}^{*}=\frac{\Delta}{k_{B}}e^{-\frac{2}{\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}}},

(177)

Now, substituting Eq. (
174
) into the third flow equation in Eq. (
172
), and integrating up to
l\geq l_{\Delta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle z_{+-}(l)=\frac{z_{+-}(\Delta)}{1-\frac{\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}}{2}\left(l-l_{\Delta}\right)},

(178)

thus clearly showing that the electron-phonon vertex develops a pole singularity precisely at
T_{0}^{*}
, that is then interpreted as the characteristic temperature for the onset of an instability against the CDW formation.

Finally, we return to the analysis of the Peierls temperature for this case
\Delta>0
, by calculating explicitly the susceptibility for
l>l_{\Delta}
,

\displaystyle\chi(l)-\chi(\Delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{l_{\Delta}}^{l}z_{+-}^{2}(l^{\prime})dl^{\prime}

(179)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(l-l_{\Delta})z_{+-}^{2}(\Delta)}{1-\frac{\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}}{2}(l-l_{\Delta})}.

As in the former case, the Peierls temperature is set by the scale
l_{T_{\Delta}}=\log(\Lambda_{0}/k_{B}T_{c,\Delta}^{j})
when the renormalized phonon frequency vanishes, i.e.

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}g_{j}^{\prime 2}\chi(l_{T_{\Delta}})=0.

(180)

Solving the equation above, we obtain the explicit expression for the Peierls critical temperature

\displaystyle T_{c,\Delta}^{j}=\frac{\Delta}{k_{B}}e^{-\frac{2-\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}\chi(\Delta)}{\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}\left[1-\frac{\alpha_{G}}{2}g_{j}^{\prime 2}\chi(\Delta)\right]+\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{\prime 2}z_{+-}^{2}(\Delta)}}.

(181)

(3) The expressions above are valid provided
\Delta\geq k_{B}T_{c,0}^{j}
. However, in the opposite regime where
k_{B}T_{c,0}^{j}>\Delta
, then the Cooper loop remains bounded at
\lambda_{C}\sim 1
, and the corresponding expression for the Peierls critical temperature remains as in the symmetric case, given by Eq. (
171
).

(4) Finally, we have the regime where
\Delta>\Lambda_{0}
. In this case, the lower integration limit for
l
must be set to zero instead of
l_{\Delta}
in the above equations. This is equivalent to replace
l_{\Delta}\rightarrow 0
,
\gamma_{\Delta}\rightarrow\gamma_{\varepsilon}
, and
\chi(\Delta)\rightarrow 0
, such that the corresponding expression for the Peierls critical temperature in this case reduces to the expression

\displaystyle T_{c,\Delta}^{j}=\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{k_{B}}e^{-\frac{2}{\gamma_{\varepsilon}+\alpha_{G}g_{j}^{{}^{\prime}2}}}

(182)

Interestingly, these results show that when the mirror symmetry between the two Weyl nodes of opposite chirality is broken (i.e.
\Delta>0
,
\delta v>0
), the Peierls instability develops even in the limit of vanishing electron-phonon interaction
g^{\prime 2}_{j}\rightarrow 0
, as clearly seen from Eq. (
181
) and Eq. (
182
), in contrast with the mirror symmetric case Eq. (
171
). This effect is a consequence of the competition between the Cooper and Peierls channels
Kundu et al. 2022
. In particular, when the mirror symmetry is broken the Cooper channel is suppressed, leaving room for a purely electronic instability in the vanishing limit of the electron-phonon coupling. Nevertheless, a finite value of the later enhances the instability, and hence a lattice instability characterized by
T_{0}^{*}
in Eq. (
177
) precludes the purely electronic instability.

VIII
Discussion and Conclusions

In this work, we developed a theoretical model to describe the combined effects of mechanical strain and an external magnetic field
\mathbf{B}_{0}=\hat{z}B_{0}
over electron-phonon interactions in a type I Weyl semimetal with a single pair of nodes. The effects of mechanical strain on electronic degrees of freedom are represented by a gauge field whose curl determines a pseudomagnetic field
\mathbf{B}_{S}=\hat{z}B_{S}
. Therefore, our theory leads to the presence of an effective pseudo-magnetic field that couples differently at each Weyl node (
B_{\xi}=B_{S}+\xi B_{0}
), thus breaking the nodal symmetry. In this sense, our theoretical model represents an extension to the renormalization group analysis in Ref.
Kundu et al. 2022
, by incorporating the effects of strain in the corresponding RG flow equations (
162
) by means of the parameter
\varepsilon=|B_{0}/B_{S}|
representing the ratio between the external magnetic field and the strain field, respectively. We obtained the RG flow equations for the couplings in the very strong pseudomagnetic field regime, within the LLL subspace. Moreover, we explored the regime where
\varepsilon<1
, and its consequences on the onset of the CDW and Peierls instability in the adiabatic limit.

Our results show that the Peierls instability and the corresponding critical temperature, both for the case of mirror symmetry and in the absence of it, are clearly affected by the presence of strain. Concretely, the critical exponents
\gamma_{\varepsilon}=\gamma_{G}\left(1-\varepsilon^{2}/2\right)-\varepsilon^{2}g_{1}(0)
and
\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}=\alpha_{G}\left[\left(1-\varepsilon^{2}/2\right)g_{2}(\Delta)-g_{1}(\Delta)\right]
, that control the critical Peierls temperature
T_{c,0}^{j}
with (
\Delta=0
) and without (
\Delta\neq 0
) mirror symmetry
T_{c,\Delta}^{j}
, respectively, are clearly modified in the presence of the combination of strain and external magnetic field, by the ratio
\varepsilon=|B_{0}/B_{S}|
. In the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the nodal symmetry with respect to the pseudo-magnetic field is recovered, and our expressions for these exponents as well as the corresponding Peierls critical temperature reduce exactly to those presented in Ref.
Kundu et al. 2022
for the adiabatic limit. In that sense, our results represent a natural extension of that previous analysis when the nodal asymmetry imposed by the combination of magnetic field and mechanical strain is included. Therefore, by comparison, we notice that
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\leq\gamma_{G}
, and therefore we conclude that the critical temperature in the mirror symmetry case
\Delta=0
increases for
\varepsilon>0
with respect to the limit
\varepsilon=0
. For the absence of mirror symmetry
\Delta\neq 0
, we have
\gamma_{\Delta,\varepsilon}\leq\gamma_{\Delta,0}
when
\varepsilon>0
, and hence in this second case the Peierls critical temperature decreases for
\varepsilon>0
compared to the limit
\varepsilon=0
. Therefore, a main conclusion in our work is that the onset of the Peierls instability, both in the mirror symmetric
\Delta=0
and asymmetric
\Delta\neq 0
cases, can be controlled by a combination of mechanical strain and an external magnetic field in the context of this minimal model for a type I Weyl semimetal, as a consequence of the nodal asymmetry in the effective pseudo-magnetic field that emerges from this particular configuration. In agreement with previous studies
Kundu et al. 2022
, we observe that the role of the Cooper channel (controlled by
\lambda_{C}
), in addition to the Peierls channel (controlled by
\lambda_{P}
), is fundamental for the renormalization group flows that lead to the Peierls instability and the subsequent determination of the critical temperature. Remarkably, our theoretical results open up the possibility for magneto-strain engineering of the electronic properties in this topological materials.

Even though we solved the RG equations and studied the Peierls instability in the adiabatic limit, we are currently extending our analysis to include the role of non-adiabatic effects, as well as the role of the higher LL’s, and these results will be communicated in a forthcoming publication.

Appendix A
The Landau level basis for a pseudomagnetic field due to torsional strain and an external magnetic field

The low energy, effective Hamiltonian that incorporates both torsional strain and an external magnetic field via minimal coupling is

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\xi}=\xi b_{0}\sigma_{0}+\xi v_{\xi}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\left[\mathbf{p}+e\mathbf{A}_{\xi}(x)-\hbar\mathbf{K}_{\xi}\right].

(183)

Here
\xi=\pm
is the chirality index of each Weyl node, and
v_{\xi}
the corresponding Fermi velocity.
b_{0}
is the shift with respect to the zero energy,
\sigma_{i}
with
i=1,2,3
are the Pauli matrices, and
\sigma_{0}
is the identity matrix. We assume that the Weyl nodes are parallel to the direction
\mathbf{\hat{z}}
of the total pseudomagnetic field, such that the location of each Weyl node in the momentum space is
\mathbf{K}_{\xi}=k_{\xi}\mathbf{\hat{z}}
.

Now, we redefine the momentum operator

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{\xi}=\mathbf{p}-\hbar\mathbf{K}_{\xi}=\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-ik_{\xi}\right).

(184)

We look for the solution to the eigenvalue problem

\left\{\xi b_{0}\sigma_{0}+\xi v_{\xi}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\left[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{\xi}+e\mathbf{A}_{\xi}(x)\right]\right\}\Psi(\mathbf{x})=E\,\Psi(\mathbf{x}),

(185)

where
\Psi
is a two-component spinor of the form

\Psi(\mathbf{x})=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}(x,y,z)\\
\psi_{2}(x,y,z)\end{pmatrix}.

(186)

Let us define
E=\xi E^{\prime}
. Then, rearranging, we get

\left\{v_{\xi}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\left[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{\xi}+e\mathbf{A}_{\xi}(x)\right]-\left(E^{\prime}-b_{0}\right)\sigma_{0}\right\}\Psi=0.

(187)

Notice that, in the chosen gauge, the Hamiltonian is translationally invariant in
y
and
z
directions. Therefore, separation of variables leads to the form

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_{y}L_{z}}}e^{-ik_{2}y+i(k+k_{\xi})z}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}(x)\\
\Phi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

(188)

Let us define the Landau’s magnetic length

\displaystyle\ell_{\xi}^{2}=\frac{\text{sign}(B_{\xi})\hbar}{eB_{\xi}},

(189)

and the guiding center for each node

\displaystyle X_{\xi}=\frac{\hbar k_{2}}{eB_{\xi}},

(190)

such that

(191)

Inserting the spinor in Eq. (
188

\displaystyle\left[-i\frac{d}{dx}\sigma_{1}+\text{sign}(B_{\xi})\frac{x-X_{\xi}}{\ell_{\xi}^{2}}\sigma_{2}+k\sigma_{3}\right.

\Phi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}=0.

(192)

Expanding the product, this leads to the system of equations

\displaystyle\left(k-\frac{(E^{\prime}-b_{0})}{\hbar v_{\xi}}\right)\Phi_{1}(x)-i\left(\frac{d}{dx}+\text{sign}(B_{\xi})\frac{x-X_{\xi}}{\ell_{\xi}^{2}}\right)\Phi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=0,

(193)

\displaystyle-i\left(\frac{d}{dx}-\text{sign}(B_{\xi})\frac{x-X_{\xi}}{\ell_{\xi}^{2}}\right)\Phi_{1}(x)-\left(k+\frac{(E^{\prime}-b_{0})}{\hbar v_{\xi}}\right)\Phi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=0.

(194)

From Eq. (

Introducing the change of variable

(197)

we obtain

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{d^{2}}{ds}-2s\frac{d}{ds}+\ell_{\xi}^{2}\left[\frac{(E^{\prime}-b_{0})^{2}}{\hbar^{2}v_{\xi}^{2}}-k^{2}\right.\right.

(199)

with

. Bearing in mind that

(200)

with

Evaluation of Matrix elements

The interaction matrix elements are proportional to the following inner product:

\int d^{3}x\;\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\lambda\xi nX_{\xi}k}(\textbf{x})e^{i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{x}}\Psi_{\lambda^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}n^{\prime}X^{\prime}_{\xi^{\prime}}k^{\prime}}(\textbf{x}).

\displaystyle=\,\delta_{k^{\prime}+k_{\xi^{\prime}},(k+k_{\xi})-q_{z}}

(224)

(x,y)

\displaystyle\int d^{2}x_{\perp}\,\mathbf{h}^{{\dagger}}_{\lambda\xi nX_{\xi}k}(\textbf{x}_{\perp})e^{i\textbf{q}_{\perp}\cdot\textbf{x}_{\perp}}\mathbf{h}_{\lambda^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}n^{\prime}X^{\prime}_{\xi^{\prime}}k^{\prime}}(\textbf{x}_{\perp})

(225)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}x_{\perp}\left[u^{*}_{\lambda\xi nk}u_{\lambda^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}h^{*}_{\xi(n-1)X_{\xi}}(\textbf{x}_{\perp})h_{(n^{\prime}-1)X^{\prime}_{\xi^{\prime}}\xi^{\prime}}(\textbf{x}_{\perp})+v^{*}_{\lambda\xi nk}v_{\lambda^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}h^{*}_{nX_{\xi}\xi}(\textbf{x}_{\perp})h_{n^{\prime}X^{\prime}_{\xi^{\prime}}\xi^{\prime}}(\textbf{x}_{\perp})\right]e^{i\textbf{q}_{\perp}\cdot\textbf{x}_{\perp}}.

\displaystyle S_{ep}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi v_{F}}{\beta\mathcal{V}}}\frac{\ell_{G}}{\ell_{A}}\sum_{\xi}\sum_{\omega_{n}\nu_{m}}\sum_{\textbf{q},j}\sum_{X_{1,\xi}}\sum_{k_{1}}

(313)

Let us now consider the electron-electron interaction. For this purpose, we shall distinguish the short-wavelength part of the Coulomb interaction, involved in backward scattering, from the long-wavelength part, which is mainly involved in forward scattering.

Let us start from the backward scattering contribution by defining the distance in momentum space between both nodal momenta,

2k_{F}\equiv k_{+}-k_{-}\,.

(314)

We further define the backward Coulomb coupling
g_{1}
(at the bare level) as an average over the sphere

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi\hbar v_{F}}\frac{\int_{S}d^{2}q_{\perp}\,V(2k_{F}\mathbf{\hat{z}}+\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{\int_{S}d^{2}q_{\perp}}

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}\ell_{G}^{4}}{\pi\hbar v_{F}\ell_{A}^{2}\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\infty}}\log\left(1+\frac{\ell_{A}^{2}}{4k_{F}^{2}\ell_{G}^{4}}\right).

\displaystyle S_{ee}^{B}

\displaystyle g_{1}\frac{\pi v_{F}}{2\beta\mathcal{V}}\frac{\ell_{G}^{2}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}\sum_{\xi\textbf{q}}\sum_{\{\omega_{n}\}}\sum_{X_{1,\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}e^{i\frac{\ell_{\bar{\xi}}^{2}}{\ell_{A}^{2}}q_{x}\left(X_{1,\xi}-\varepsilon_{\xi}^{2}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}\right)}\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi X_{1,\xi}}(k_{1},\omega_{n_{1}})\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}(k_{2},\omega_{n_{2}})

Performing a similar analysis for the long-wavelength component of the Coulomb interaction, named forward scattering, the corresponding contribution to the action is

\displaystyle S_{ee}^{F}=g_{2}\frac{\pi v_{F}}{2\beta\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\xi\textbf{q}}\sum_{\{\omega_{n}\}}\sum_{X_{1,\xi}X_{2,\bar{\xi}}}\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}e^{iq_{x}\left(X_{1,\xi}-X_{2,\bar{\xi}}\right)}

(317)

(318)

(319)

being the dielectric function in the random phase approximation (RPA), and

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{RPA}}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}}\SumInt_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\left|\int d^{3}x\;\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha}(\textbf{x})e^{i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{x}}\Psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{x})\right|^{2}

(329)

\displaystyle\times\frac{f_{0}(E_{\alpha})-f_{0}(E_{\alpha^{\prime}})}{E_{\alpha}-E_{\alpha^{\prime}}+\hbar\nu_{m}+i\eta}

f_{0}(E)=\left(\exp(\beta(E-\mu))+1\right)^{-1}
is the Fermi distribution function, and
\eta\to 0^{+}
is a regulator. In the high pseudo-magnetic field regime, we can approximate our calculations by restricting ourselves to the chiral Landau level, by considering
\xi=\xi^{\prime}
and
n=n^{\prime}=0
in the sets of indices
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}

As explained in detail in Appendix
E

g_{2}=4\pi\ell_{G}^{2}\,\frac{1+\frac{\delta v}{v_{F}}}{2\frac{\ell_{A}^{2}}{\ell_{G}^{2}}+\frac{\delta v}{v_{F}}\varepsilon_{-}}\,.

(330)

D.1
One-loop renormalization of the electron-electron vertex

The electron-electron internodal vertex will introduce corrections for the backward
g_{1}
and forward
g_{2}
couplings arising from 2 different channels. The Peierls channel (
\sim\bar{\Psi}_{+}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}\Psi_{+}+\Psi_{+}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}\bar{\Psi}_{+}
) represents the pairing of one electron and one hole with opposite chiralities, while the Cooper channel (
\sim\bar{\Psi}_{+}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}\Psi_{+}+\Psi_{+}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}\bar{\Psi}_{+}
) represents the pairing of two electrons (or two holes) with opposite chiralities. In addition, the Landau channel (
\sim\bar{\Psi}_{+}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}^{{\dagger}}\Psi_{-}\bar{\Psi}_{+}+\Psi_{+}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}^{{\dagger}}\bar{\Psi}_{-}\Psi_{+}
) only contributes to intranodal scattering and therefore does not contribute to the renormalization of the
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
couplings.

Appendix E
The polarization function in the RPA

As defined in the main text, the dielectric function in the random phase approximation (RPA) is given by the expression

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{RPA}}(\mathbf{q},\nu_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}}\SumInt_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\left|\int d^{3}x\;\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha}(\textbf{x})e^{i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{x}}\Psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\textbf{x})\right|^{2}

(379)

\xi=\xi^{\prime}
and

\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}

Using the result (
242

\displaystyle\left|\int d^{3}x\;\Psi^{{\dagger}}_{\xi 0X_{\xi}k}(\textbf{x})e^{i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{x}}\Psi_{\xi 0X^{\prime}_{\xi}k^{\prime}}(\textbf{x})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{k^{\prime},k-q_{z}}\delta_{X^{\prime}_{\xi},X_{\xi}+\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{y}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{\perp}^{2}}\,.

(380)

Evaluating this in (

(and
\eta\to 0

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{RPA}}(\mathbf{q},0)

(381)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\xi}\left(\frac{L_{y}}{2\pi\ell_{\xi}^{2}}\right)\int_{-L_{y}/2}^{L_{y}/2}dX_{\xi}\left(\frac{L_{z}}{2\pi}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dke^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{\perp}^{2}}\frac{\theta\left(-\xi b_{0}-\xi\hbar v_{\xi}k\right)-\theta\left(-\xi b_{0}-\xi\hbar v_{\xi}(k-q_{z})\right)}{\xi\hbar v_{\xi}q_{z}}

\displaystyle\frac{L_{y}}{(2\pi)^{2}L_{x}}\sum_{\xi}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{\perp}^{2}}}{\hbar v_{\xi}q_{z}\ell_{\xi}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\left[\theta\left(-\xi b_{0}-\xi\hbar v_{\xi}k\right)-\theta\left(-\xi b_{0}-\xi\hbar v_{\xi}(k-q_{z})\right)\right]\,.

k

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\left[\theta\left(-\xi b_{0}-\xi\hbar v_{\xi}k\right)-\theta\left(-\xi b_{0}-\xi\hbar v_{\xi}(k-q_{z})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\,-\xi\int_{-b_{0}/\hbar v_{\xi}}^{q_{z}-b_{0}/\hbar v_{\xi}}dk

(382)

where we used the identity
\theta(x)=1-\theta(-x)
to obtain the second expression from the first one. Evaluating in Eq. (

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{RPA}}(\mathbf{q},0)=-\frac{L_{y}}{(2\pi)^{2}L_{x}}\sum_{\xi}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\xi}^{2}q_{\perp}^{2}}}{\hbar v_{\xi}\ell_{\xi}^{2}}

(383)

\displaystyle=-\frac{L_{y}}{L_{x}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\hbar v_{F}\ell_{+}^{2}}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{+}^{2}q_{\perp}^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{+}^{2}}{1+\delta v/v_{F}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{-}^{2}q_{\perp}^{2}}\right).

(388)

Ww first perform the Matsubara frequency sum, to obtain

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\epsilon_{+}\left(1+\gamma\right)-i\hbar\nu_{m}}\left[f_{0}\left(\epsilon_{+}/\hbar\right)-f_{0}\left(i\nu_{m}-\gamma\epsilon_{+}/\hbar\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-99.58464pt=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\beta}{\epsilon_{+}\left(1+\gamma\right)}\frac{1}{1-i\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\epsilon_{+}}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{+}}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{+}}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\,,

(397)

where we applied the property of the Fermi distribution function
f_{0}(z+i\nu_{m})=f_{0}(z)
when
\nu_{m}=2\pi m/\beta
is a Bosonic frequency, since
e^{i\hbar\beta\nu_{m}}=e^{i2\pi m}=1
. Notice that the resulting expression in Eq. (
397
) is an even function of
\epsilon_{+}
. Therefore, for the remaining
\epsilon_{+}
-integral, consider an even function
F(\epsilon_{+})
, and note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{\Lambda(l)}^{\Lambda}d\epsilon_{+}+\int_{-\Lambda}^{-\Lambda(l)}d\epsilon_{+}\right]F(\epsilon_{+})=\int_{\Lambda(l)}^{\Lambda}d\epsilon_{+}\,F(\epsilon_{+})

\displaystyle\approx F(\Lambda)\int_{\Lambda e^{-dl}}^{\Lambda}d\epsilon_{+}=F(\Lambda)\Lambda dl\,,

(398)

where
\Lambda
is the energy associated to the momentum cutoff
\Lambda_{0}
at the chiral Landau level. Here, we have used the mean value theorem to integrate in the infinitesimal range. Thus,
we obtain the result

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k-2k_{F},\omega_{n}\right)=\frac{-\beta L_{z}}{2\pi\hbar v_{F}(1+\gamma)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{dl}{1-i\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Lambda}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\gamma\right)\right].

(399)

On the other hand, for the second term in Eq. (
392

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k+2k_{F},\omega_{n}+\nu_{m}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{i\hbar\omega_{n}+i\hbar\nu_{m}-\hbar v_{+}(k+2k_{F}-k_{F_{+}})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{i\hbar\omega_{n}+\hbar v_{-}(k-k_{F_{-}})}\,.

(400)

In this case, the common momentum variable is
k-k_{F_{-}}
, so we define the energy variable

\epsilon_{-}\equiv\hbar v_{-}(k-k_{F_{-}})=\hbar\gamma v_{F}(k-k_{F_{-}})

(401)

for integration. Then, the last expression reduces to

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k+2k_{F},\omega_{n}+\nu_{m}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)=\frac{-\beta L_{z}}{2\pi\hbar v_{F}(1+\gamma)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{dl}{1-i\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Lambda}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2\gamma}\right)\right]\,.

(402)

Thus, summing (
399
) and (
402
), we have

\displaystyle\Pi_{P}(\nu_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\left[\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k+2k_{F},\omega_{n}+\nu_{m}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)+\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k-2k_{F},\omega_{n}-\nu_{m}\right)\right]

(403)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta L_{z}}{\pi\hbar v_{F}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\cdot\frac{dl}{1-i\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Lambda}}\frac{1}{4}\left[2\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\gamma\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2\gamma}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta L_{z}}{\pi\hbar v_{F}}\cdot\frac{dl}{1+\gamma}\lambda_{P}(T,\delta v,\nu_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta L_{z}}{2\pi\hbar v_{F}}\cdot\frac{dl}{1+\delta v/2v_{F}}\lambda_{P}(T,\delta v,\nu_{m})\,,

where we have defined

\displaystyle\lambda_{P}(T,\delta v,\nu_{m},l)\equiv\frac{1}{1-i\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Lambda(l)}}\frac{1}{4}\left[2\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda(l)}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda(l)}{2}\gamma\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda(l)}{2\gamma}\right)\right]\,.

(404)

Appendix G
Polarization insertion in the Cooper channel

In this appendix, we present the details of the calculation of the polarization insertion
\Pi_{C}(\nu_{m})
in the Cooper channel. For simplicity in the notation,
we start by defining
\nu_{m}=\omega_{n_{1}}+\omega_{n_{2}}
, and then the Matsubara sum and the
k
-integral can be symmetrized following the same procedure explained from (
390
) to (
392
), as follows (using
k_{1}+k_{2}\approx k_{+}+k_{-}
)

\displaystyle\Pi_{C}(\nu_{m})\equiv\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\xi}\left(k_{1}+k_{2}-k,\nu_{m}-\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\bar{\xi}}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\xi}\left(k_{+}+k_{-}-k,\nu_{m}-\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\bar{\xi}}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\left[\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k_{+}+k_{-}-k,\nu_{m}-\omega_{n}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k_{+}+k_{-}-k,\nu_{m}-\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)\right].

(405)

The first term is

\displaystyle S_{1}\equiv\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{+}\left(k,\omega_{n}\right)\mathcal{G}^{0}_{-}\left(k_{+}+k_{-}-k,\nu_{m}-\omega_{n}\right)\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{i\hbar\omega_{n}-\hbar v_{+}(k-k_{F_{+}})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{-i\hbar\omega_{n}+i\hbar\nu_{m}+\hbar v_{-}(k_{+}+k_{-}-k-k_{F_{-}})}\,,

(406)

we note that

\displaystyle\hbar v_{-}(k_{+}+k_{-}-k-k_{F_{-}})=-\hbar v_{-}\left\{(k-k_{F_{+}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left[(k_{F_{+}}-k_{+})+(k_{F_{-}}-k_{-})\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=-\left(1+\frac{\delta v}{v_{F}}\right)\hbar v_{F}\left\{(k-k_{F_{+}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left[(k_{F_{+}}-k_{+})+(k_{F_{-}}-k_{-})\right]\right\}\,.

(407)

Therefore, we define the integration variable

\epsilon_{+}=\hbar v_{F}\left(k-k_{F_{+}}\right),

(408)

and the parameter

\Delta\equiv\hbar v_{F}\left[(k_{F_{+}}-k_{+})+(k_{F_{-}}-k_{-})\right]\,,

(409)

such that we obtain for
S_{1}

\displaystyle S_{1}=\frac{L_{z}}{2\pi\hbar v_{F}}\left[\int_{\Lambda(l)}^{\Lambda}d\epsilon_{+}+\int_{-\Lambda}^{-\Lambda(l)}d\epsilon_{+}\right]

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}-\epsilon_{+}/\hbar}\frac{-1}{i\omega_{n}-i\nu_{m}+\gamma(\epsilon_{+}+\Delta)/\hbar}\,.

(410)

We evaluate the Matsubara sum as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}-\epsilon_{+}/\hbar}\frac{-1}{i\omega_{n}-i\nu_{m}+\gamma(\epsilon_{+}+\Delta)/\hbar}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\beta}{\epsilon_{+}+\gamma(\epsilon_{+}+\Delta)-i\hbar\nu_{m}}\left[f_{0}\left(\epsilon_{+}/\hbar\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-f_{0}\left(i\nu_{m}-\gamma(\epsilon_{+}+\Delta)/\hbar\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\beta}{\epsilon_{+}\left(1+\gamma\right)+\gamma\Delta-i\hbar\nu_{m}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{+}}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(\epsilon_{+}+\Delta)}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\,.

(411)

We perform the integral in
\epsilon_{+}
by using the mean value theorem,

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Lambda(l)}^{\Lambda}d\epsilon_{+}+\int_{-\Lambda}^{-\Lambda(l)}d\epsilon_{+}\right]\frac{1}{\epsilon_{+}\left(1+\gamma\right)+\gamma\Delta-i\hbar\nu_{m}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{+}}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(\epsilon_{+}+\Delta)}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\,

\displaystyle=\frac{dl}{1+\gamma}\left\{\frac{1}{1+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}-i\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Lambda}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(\Lambda+\Delta)}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\left.+\,\frac{1}{1-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}+i\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Lambda}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(\Lambda-\Delta)}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\right\}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{dl}{1+\gamma}\left\{\frac{1}{1+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-i\frac{1}{1+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}}\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Delta}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(\Lambda+\Delta)}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\left.+\,\frac{1}{1-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+i\frac{1}{1-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}}\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Delta}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(\Lambda-\Delta)}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\right\}\,

(412)

Then, substituting into

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta L_{z}}{2\pi\hbar v_{F}}\frac{dl}{1+\gamma}\left\{\frac{1}{1+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-i\frac{1}{1+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}}\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Delta}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(\Lambda+\Delta)}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\left.+\,\frac{1}{1-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+i\frac{1}{1-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}}\frac{\hbar\nu_{m}}{(1+\gamma)\Delta}}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda}{2}\right)+\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(\Lambda-\Delta)}{2}\gamma\right)\right]\right\}\,.
