The Geometry of Dilation- and Shear-Deformed Spaces Journal: Journal of Geometry and Physics
Gordon Liu

Abstract
We introduce a deformation-induced geometric framework built from a reference metric g ¯ \bar{g} and a pure deformation field P P in the symmetric sector of G ​ L ​ ( n , ℝ ) GL(n,\mathbb{R}) . The deformed metric is determined by the basic law g = P T ​ g ¯ ​ P g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P . We prove that, in the Riemannian case, the pure deformation field is locally determined uniquely by the pair ( g ¯ , g ) (\bar{g},g) under the g ¯ \bar{g} -symmetric positive-definite gauge. From P P we derive both the deformed metric and the full induced connection structure: the Levi-Civita part is induced through the metric deformation g = P T ​ g ¯ ​ P g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P , while the additional symmetric sector is induced through the deformation-rate chain associated with P P . The resulting total connection consists of the Levi-Civita connection of the deformed metric together with a deformation-induced correction, which is not an independent affine variable. Ordinary Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian geometry is recovered when the deformation is trivial. We further identify special diagonal and conformal cases in which the raw and deformed-frame representations coincide, and exhibit non-diagonal shear and local spherical examples showing the genuinely new features of the construction. The framework provides a metric-based description of local dilation and shear that is more restrictive than generic metric-affine geometry while extending ordinary Riemannian geometry.



1
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to formulate a metric-based geometry in which
local dilation and shear are treated as genuine geometric deformations rather
than absorbed into an arbitrary independent affine sector. The basic input is a
reference geometry
(M,\bar{g})
together with a pure deformation field
P
.
The primary geometric step is the metric deformation

g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P.

(1.1)

All subsequent objects are induced by this deformation.

Classical Riemannian geometry is governed by the Levi-Civita connection: once a
metric is given and torsion-freeness together with metric compatibility are
imposed, the affine structure is uniquely fixed, and curvature becomes the
principal intrinsic invariant
[
1
, pp. 124–126]
[
2
, pp. 54–55]
.
This framework captures bending, but it does not by itself isolate genuine
metric deformation modes such as local dilation and shear. If metric
compatibility is relaxed, the metric alone no longer determines a unique
connection, and one must specify an additional geometric principle.

The present framework proposes such a principle. Instead of introducing a
generic independent affine freedom, we begin with a reference geometry and ask
how a genuine deformation of that geometry changes the full comparison
structure. The construction is guided by the
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
decomposition of
local linear transformations, but only the symmetric deformation sector is used
as independent input. Thus the deformation tensor
P
represents only genuine
local dilation and shear. The key point is that the same field
P
induces both
parts of the total connection: through the metric deformation

g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
it determines the Levi-Civita connection of the deformed
metric, and through its deformation-rate chain it determines the additional
nonmetric sector.

The central induced objects are the raw deformation-rate tensor

\bar{L}_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P,

(1.2)

its representation in the comparison frame adapted to the deformed metric,

L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P,

(1.3)

and the additional symmetric compensation tensor

\Lambda_{\mu}=\mathrm{Sym}_{g}(L_{\mu}).

(1.4)

The total connection is then defined by

\Gamma=\Gamma^{\circ}[g]+\Lambda.

(1.5)

Accordingly, the same deformation field
P
induces the whole connection
structure of the deformed geometry: the canonical metric-compatible transport
through the deformed metric
g
, and the additional nonmetric sector through
the deformation-rate tensors derived from
P
.

The main mathematical points established in this paper are the following.
First, in the Riemannian case, for any pair of metrics
(\bar{g},g)
on the same
manifold, there is locally a unique
\bar{g}
-symmetric positive-definite pure
deformation field
P
such that
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
. Second, the tensors

\bar{L}_{\mu}
and
L_{\mu}
are shown to be the natural raw and deformed-frame
representations of the same deformation-rate operator. Third, the same deformation field
P
is shown to induce the whole connection structure of the deformed geometry: the Levi-Civita part through the metric deformation
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
, and the additional nonmetric part through the deformation-rate chain. Fourth, the theory recovers ordinary Riemannian geometry when the deformation is trivial.

The framework is related in spirit to, but distinct from, several existing
geometric generalizations. It differs from Finsler geometry because it remains
metric-based rather than direction-dependent
[
3
, pp. 1–3]
. It differs from generic metric-affine geometry because the total connection structure is not obtained by introducing an arbitrary independent affine field. Instead, the same deformation field
P
induces the Levi-Civita part through the metric deformation and the nonmetric part through the deformation-rate chain
[
4
, pp. 1–8]
[
5
, pp. 465–480]
. In this sense, the present construction may be viewed as a deformation-induced extension of Riemannian geometry.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the reference geometry,
the pure deformation tensor, the deformed metric, and the deformation-rate tensors. Section 3 defines the induced connection structure and proves that both the Levi-Civita and nonmetric parts are deformation-induced in different ways.
Section 4 develops explicit examples, including diagonal, conformal,
non-diagonal shear, and spherical cases. Section 5 compares the present
framework with ordinary Riemannian geometry, conformal geometry, and generic
metric-affine constructions. Section 6 contains concluding remarks.

2
Basic Setting

2.1
Reference geometry

Let
M
be a smooth
n
-dimensional manifold. A reference geometry is a pair

(M,\bar{g}),

(2.1)

where
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
is a nondegenerate metric on
M
. Its Levi-Civita connection is

\bar{\Gamma}^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}:=\{^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}\}_{\bar{g}}.

(2.2)

The reference metric is not assumed to be flat.

2.2
Pure deformation tensor

The deformation tensor
P
is taken from the symmetric deformation sector of
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
relative to the reference metric
\bar{g}
. In the present framework this symmetric deformation field is the sole independent geometric input beyond the reference geometry. It determines the deformed metric through
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
, and thereby induces the Levi-Civita connection of the deformed space; at the same time, through its deformation-rate chain, it induces the additional nonmetric sector. Thus
P
represents genuine local dilation and shear, while the metric-compatible rotational transport is not introduced independently but is determined a posteriori by the deformed metric generated by
P
.

A smooth field of linear maps

P^{\mu}{}_{\nu}(x):T_{x}M\to T_{x}M

(2.3)

is called a pure deformation tensor relative to
\bar{g}
if it contains no
\bar{g}
-orthogonal rotational part. In the Riemannian case this means that
P
is
\bar{g}
-symmetric and positive-definite; in the pseudo-Riemannian case it carries no
\bar{g}
-orthogonal rotational or boost component.

2.3
Deformed metric

Given
\bar{g}
and
P
, the deformed metric
g
is defined by

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu}P^{\beta}{}_{\nu},

(2.4)

or equivalently

g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P.

(2.5)

Thus
g
is induced from the reference geometry by the deformation field
P
. Under the
\bar{g}
-symmetric positive-definite gauge, the orthogonal ambiguity is removed, so that
P
is locally determined by
(\bar{g},g)
in the Riemannian case.

Proposition 2.1

(Local existence and uniqueness of the pure deformation field)
.

Let
(M,\bar{g})
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
g
be another Riemannian
metric on the same manifold. Then, locally on
M
, there exists a unique

\bar{g}
-symmetric positive-definite
(1,1)
-tensor field
P
such that

g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P.

Equivalently, in components,

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}P^{\alpha}{}_{\mu}P^{\beta}{}_{\nu}.

Proof.

Since both
\bar{g}
and
g
are positive-definite, at each point
x\in M
there
exists a unique
\bar{g}
-self-adjoint positive-definite endomorphism

A_{x}:T_{x}M\to T_{x}M
such that

g_{x}(u,v)=\bar{g}_{x}(A_{x}u,v)\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in T_{x}M.

Because
A_{x}
is positive-definite and
\bar{g}
-self-adjoint, it admits a unique
positive-definite
\bar{g}
-self-adjoint square root
P_{x}=A_{x}^{1/2}
. Then

g_{x}(u,v)=\bar{g}_{x}(P_{x}^{2}u,v)=\bar{g}_{x}(P_{x}u,P_{x}v),

which is precisely

g_{x}=P_{x}^{T}\bar{g}_{x}\,P_{x}.

Smooth dependence of the positive-definite square root on the coefficients of a
smooth positive-definite endomorphism gives a locally smooth tensor field
P
.

For uniqueness, suppose
P
and
Q
are both
\bar{g}
-symmetric positive-definite
and satisfy

g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P=Q^{T}\bar{g}\,Q.

Then

\bar{g}(Pu,Pv)=\bar{g}(Qu,Qv)\qquad\text{for all }u,v.

Equivalently,

P^{2}=Q^{2}

as
\bar{g}
-self-adjoint positive-definite endomorphisms. By uniqueness of the
positive-definite square root,
P=Q
.
∎

This deformation law is the primary geometric step of the theory. All later tensors are induced by the passage from
\bar{g}
to
g
.

2.4
Levi-Civita connection of the deformed metric

The Levi-Civita connection of the deformed metric
g
is denoted by

\Gamma^{\circ\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}[g]:=\{^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}\}_{g}.

(2.6)

This is the canonical torsion-free metric-compatible connection determined by the deformed metric.

2.5
Raw deformation-rate tensor

Relative to the reference geometry, the deformation field induces the raw deformation-rate tensor

\bar{L}_{\mu}:=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P.

(2.7)

In components,

\bar{L}^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}=(P^{-1})^{\rho}{}_{\alpha}(\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P^{\alpha}{}_{\nu}),

(2.8)

where

\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P^{\alpha}{}_{\nu}=\partial_{\mu}P^{\alpha}{}_{\nu}+\bar{\Gamma}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\beta}P^{\beta}{}_{\nu}-\bar{\Gamma}^{\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}P^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}.

(2.9)

Since
P
is a tensor field and
\bar{\nabla}P
is tensorial,
\bar{L}_{\mu}
is a
(1,2)
-tensor field. It is the raw deformation-rate tensor relative to the reference geometry.

To rewrite the same deformation rate in the comparison frame adapted to the deformed metric, define

L_{\mu}:=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P.

(2.10)

In components,

L^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}=(P^{-1})^{\rho}{}_{\alpha}\,\bar{L}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\beta}\,P^{\beta}{}_{\nu}.

(2.11)

Thus
\bar{L}_{\mu}
and
L_{\mu}
refer to the same underlying deformation process, but expressed relative to different comparison frames. Only in specially adapted diagonal or conformal cases may these two matrix representations coincide.

Proposition 2.2

(Natural raw deformation-rate tensor)
.

Let
(M,\bar{g})
be a reference geometry with Levi-Civita connection

\bar{\nabla}
, and let
P
be a pure deformation field. Then

\bar{L}_{\mu}:=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P

is the natural relative deformation-rate tensor associated with
P
and the
reference geometry. More precisely:

(i)

\bar{L}_{\mu}
is a well-defined
(1,2)
-tensor field;

(ii)

it depends only on the first covariant derivative of
P
;

(iii)

it vanishes identically whenever
P
is covariantly constant with
respect to the reference geometry, i.e. whenever
\bar{\nabla}P=0
.

Proof.

Since
P
is a
(1,1)
-tensor field, its covariant derivative

\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P
is a
(1,2)
-tensor field. Left multiplication by the
inverse tensor field
P^{-1}
preserves tensoriality, so

\bar{L}_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P

is again a
(1,2)
-tensor field. This proves (i).

The expression involves only
P
and its first covariant derivative with
respect to
\bar{\nabla}
, so it is first-order in
P
. This proves (ii).

Finally, if
\bar{\nabla}P=0
, then immediately

\bar{L}_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P=0.

Thus
\bar{L}_{\mu}
vanishes whenever the deformation field is covariantly
constant relative to the reference geometry. This proves (iii).
∎

Proposition 2.3

(Deformed-frame representation of the deformation-rate tensor)
.

Let
\bar{L}_{\mu}
be the raw deformation-rate tensor relative to the reference
geometry, and let the deformed metric be given by

g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P.

Then the representation of the same deformation-rate operator in the comparison
frame adapted to the deformed metric is

L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P.

Proof.

The deformation field
P
defines the passage from the reference comparison
structure to the deformed comparison structure through

g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P.

Hence
P
also determines the corresponding change of comparison frame between
the same pair of metric structures.

Now
\bar{L}_{\mu}
is an endomorphism-valued
1
-form: for each fixed
\mu
,
it acts as a linear operator on the tangent space. For any linear operator

A:V\to V
, if two frames are related by the transformation
P
, then the
matrix representations of the same operator in the two frames are related by
the standard similarity law

[A]_{(e)}=P^{-1}[A]_{(\bar{e})}P.

Applying this to the endomorphism part of the deformation-rate tensor gives

L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P.

Thus
L_{\mu}
is not a new tensorial object, but the deformed-frame
representation of the same deformation-rate operator.
∎

Corollary 2.4

(Criterion for coincidence of the two representations)
.

One has

L_{\mu}=\bar{L}_{\mu}

if and only if

[\bar{L}_{\mu},P]=0

for the corresponding index
\mu
.

Proof.

By definition,

L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P.

Thus

L_{\mu}=\bar{L}_{\mu}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P=\bar{L}_{\mu}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\bar{L}_{\mu}P=P\bar{L}_{\mu},

which is equivalent to

[\bar{L}_{\mu},P]=0.

∎

3
The Additional Symmetric Sector

The same deformation field
P
induces the whole connection structure of the deformed geometry. Through the metric deformation
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
it determines the Levi-Civita transport
\Gamma^{\circ}[g]
of the deformed space, while through its deformation-rate chain it induces the additional nonmetric sector. The genuinely additional part is therefore extracted from
L_{\mu}
as the symmetric comparison part relative to
g
.

3.1
Additional symmetric compensation

Define

\Lambda_{\mu}:=\mathrm{Sym}_{g}(L_{\mu}).

(3.1)

Equivalently,

\Lambda^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(L^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}+g^{\rho\lambda}g_{\nu\sigma}L^{\sigma}{}_{\mu\lambda}\right).

(3.2)

Only the
g
-symmetric part of an additive correction to
\Gamma^{\circ}[g]
contributes to nonmetricity; the
g
-antisymmetric part belongs to the metric-preserving transport sector. In the present framework this means that the deformation-rate tensor contributes in two different ways: its metric-preserving contribution is absorbed into the Levi-Civita branch determined by
g
, while its genuinely nonmetric contribution survives as
\Lambda
. Thus
\Lambda
is the deformation-induced dilation/shear compensation beyond the Levi-Civita transport of
g
.

3.2
Total connection

The total connection of the deformed geometry is

\Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^{\circ\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}[g]+\Lambda^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}.

(3.3)

Here
\Gamma
belongs to the deformed geometry, whereas
\bar{\Gamma}
belongs only to the reference geometry.

It is convenient to define the deviation tensor

C^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}:=\Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\circ\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}[g].

(3.4)

By construction one has

C^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}.

(3.5)

If
P=I
, then
g=\bar{g}
,
\bar{L}=0
,
L=0
,
\Lambda=0
, and
\Gamma=\bar{\Gamma}
. Thus ordinary Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian geometry is recovered in the absence of genuine deformation.

The whole construction may be summarized as

(M,\bar{g})\xrightarrow{\,P\,}g=P^{T}\bar{g}P,

(3.6)

together with

\bar{\Gamma}=\{{}\}_{\bar{g}},\qquad\bar{L}_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P,\qquad L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P,\qquad\Lambda_{\mu}=\mathrm{Sym}_{g}(L_{\mu}),\qquad\Gamma=\Gamma^{\circ}[g]+\Lambda.

(3.7)

The additional non-Riemannian sector is not independent; it is deformation-induced.

4
Illustrative families of deformations

We now record several explicit families of deformations that illustrate the structural behavior of the tensors
\bar{L}_{\mu}
,
L_{\mu}
, and
\Lambda_{\mu}
, as well as the way in which the same deformation field
P
induces both the Levi-Civita and nonmetric sectors of the deformed geometry. The first three subsections form a single planar family governed by the same deformation tensor introduced in Section 4.1; the later subsections show, respectively, a genuinely non-diagonal shear family and two spherical families.

4.1
A planar dilation–shear family

As a first illustrative family, let the reference geometry be the Euclidean plane

\bar{g}_{ij}=\delta_{ij},\qquad\bar{\Gamma}^{k}{}_{ij}=0.

(4.1)

Take the deformation tensor

P(x,y)=e^{\phi(x,y)}\begin{pmatrix}e^{\sigma(x,y)}&0\\
0&e^{-\sigma(x,y)}\end{pmatrix},

(4.2)

where
\phi(x,y)
is the local dilation field and
\sigma(x,y)
is the local shear field. The deformed metric is

g=P^{T}\delta P=\begin{pmatrix}e^{2(\phi+\sigma)}&0\\
0&e^{2(\phi-\sigma)}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.3)

The scalar field
\phi
changes the local scale isotropically, while
\sigma
stretches one principal direction and compresses the other. Thus
\phi
represents pure dilation and
\sigma
pure shear.
Because the reference connection vanishes in this planar case, the full connection of the deformed geometry is induced by
P
: through the metric deformation it produces the Levi-Civita part of the deformed geometry, and through the deformation-rate tensors derived from
P
it produces the additional nonmetric part.

Since
\bar{\Gamma}=0
, one has
\bar{\nabla}_{i}=\partial_{i}
, and therefore

\bar{L}_{i}=P^{-1}\partial_{i}P.

(4.4)

A direct computation gives

\bar{L}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{i}\phi+\partial_{i}\sigma&0\\
0&\partial_{i}\phi-\partial_{i}\sigma\end{pmatrix}.

(4.5)

Hence

\bar{L}_{i}=\bar{L}_{i}^{(\mathrm{dil})}+\bar{L}_{i}^{(\mathrm{shear})},

(4.6)

with

\bar{L}_{i}^{(\mathrm{dil})}=(\partial_{i}\phi)I,

(4.7)

and

\bar{L}_{i}^{(\mathrm{shear})}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{i}\sigma&0\\
0&-\partial_{i}\sigma\end{pmatrix}.

(4.8)

In the present adapted frame, both
g
and
\bar{L}_{i}
are diagonal. Hence the transformed representation satisfies

L_{i}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{i}P=\bar{L}_{i},

(4.9)

and therefore

\Lambda_{i}=L_{i}=\bar{L}_{i}.

(4.10)

This equality is special to the present adapted diagonal example and should not be regarded as a general identity.

4.2
Metric-compatible transport in the planar family of Section 4.1

Continuing the planar dilation–shear family introduced in Section 4.1, the Levi-Civita connection of the associated deformed metric
g
is

\Gamma^{\circ k}{}_{ij}[g]=\frac{1}{2}g^{k\ell}(\partial_{i}g_{\ell j}+\partial_{j}g_{\ell i}-\partial_{\ell}g_{ij}).

(4.11)

For the metric above, the nonzero components are

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\circ 1}{}_{11}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}(\phi+\sigma),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\circ 1}{}_{12}

\displaystyle=\partial_{2}(\phi+\sigma),

(4.12)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\circ 2}{}_{22}

\displaystyle=\partial_{2}(\phi-\sigma),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\circ 2}{}_{12}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}(\phi-\sigma),

(4.13)

together with

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\circ 1}{}_{22}

\displaystyle=-e^{-4\sigma}\partial_{1}(\phi-\sigma),

(4.14)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\circ 2}{}_{11}

\displaystyle=-e^{4\sigma}\partial_{2}(\phi+\sigma).

(4.15)

Although the reference connection vanishes, the Levi-Civita connection of the deformed metric is generally nonzero. This shows explicitly that genuine dilation and shear alone can generate the metric-compatible transport of a nontrivially curved deformed space. In the language of the present theory, the metric-preserving contribution of the deformation process is absorbed into the Levi-Civita branch determined by the deformed metric, while the genuinely additional symmetric contribution survives as
\Lambda
.

Since
\Lambda_{i}=L_{i}
in this diagonal example, the total connection is

\Gamma^{k}{}_{ij}=\Gamma^{\circ k}{}_{ij}[g]+L^{k}{}_{ij}.

(4.16)

Thus the deformed geometry contains both the canonical metric-compatible transport determined by the deformed metric and the additional deformation-induced symmetric compensation. If
\partial_{i}\phi\neq 0
or
\partial_{i}\sigma\neq 0
, then

\Lambda\neq 0,

(4.17)

so the non-Riemannian sector is nontrivially activated.

4.3
Pure dilation as a special member of the planar family

Still within the planar family of Section 4.1, if
\sigma=0
, then

P=e^{\phi}I,\qquad g=e^{2\phi}\delta,\qquad\bar{L}_{i}=(\partial_{i}\phi)I.

(4.18)

Hence

L_{i}=(\partial_{i}\phi)I,\qquad\Lambda_{i}=(\partial_{i}\phi)I,

(4.19)

and the total connection becomes

\Gamma^{k}{}_{ij}=\Gamma^{\circ k}{}_{ij}[g]+\delta^{k}{}_{j}\partial_{i}\phi.

(4.20)

This is the simplest explicit realization of an activated non-Riemannian dilation sector.

4.4
A spherical family induced from a flat reference

Let the reference metric be the flat metric on a local coordinate patch
(\theta,\phi)
,

\bar{g}=d\theta^{2}+d\phi^{2},\qquad\bar{\Gamma}^{k}{}_{ij}=0.

(4.21)

Choose the deformation tensor

P(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}R&0\\
0&R\sin\theta\end{pmatrix}.

(4.22)

Then the deformed metric is

g=P^{T}\bar{g}P=R^{2}\,d\theta^{2}+R^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2},

(4.23)

which is precisely the standard local metric of a sphere of radius
R
.

Thus a flat two-dimensional reference metric can indeed be deformed locally into a spherical metric by a suitable tensor deformation field. In this example,

\bar{L}_{\theta}=P^{-1}\partial_{\theta}P=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\cot\theta\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bar{L}_{\phi}=0.

(4.24)

Since the present deformation tensor is already diagonal and adapted to the deformed metric, one has

L_{\theta}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\theta}P=\bar{L}_{\theta},\qquad L_{\phi}=0.

(4.25)

The example therefore provides a simple local realization of spherical geometry within the present deformation framework.

Proposition 4.1

(Generic non-diagonal shear need not preserve the raw representation)
.

In general, even if the deformation field
P
belongs pointwise to the symmetric
deformation sector, the deformed-frame representation

L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P

need not coincide with the raw deformation-rate tensor

\bar{L}_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P.

In particular, if
[\bar{L}_{\mu},P]\neq 0
for some
\mu
, then

L_{\mu}\neq\bar{L}_{\mu}.

Consequently, the
g
-symmetric part of
L_{\mu}
is, in general, not equal to the

\bar{g}
-symmetric part of
\bar{L}_{\mu}
.

Proof.

By definition,

L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P.

Therefore

L_{\mu}=\bar{L}_{\mu}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P=\bar{L}_{\mu}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\bar{L}_{\mu}P=P\bar{L}_{\mu},

that is,

[\bar{L}_{\mu},P]=0.

Hence whenever
[\bar{L}_{\mu},P]\neq 0
, one necessarily has

L_{\mu}\neq\bar{L}_{\mu}.

Since the decomposition into symmetric and antisymmetric parts depends on the
comparison metric, and since
\bar{L}_{\mu}
and
L_{\mu}
are generally different
representations relative to the reference and deformed comparison structures,
their corresponding symmetric parts are in general not the same.
∎

4.5
A genuinely non-diagonal shear family

We now turn to a genuinely non-diagonal shear family, whose purpose is to show that the raw deformation-rate tensor
\bar{L}_{\mu}
and its deformed-frame representation
L_{\mu}
generally differ once the deformation is no longer simultaneously diagonalizable with the comparison metric.

As a first illustrative family, let the reference geometry be the Euclidean plane,

\bar{g}_{ij}=\delta_{ij},\qquad\bar{\Gamma}^{k}{}_{ij}=0,\qquad(i,j,k=1,2).

(4.26)

Take a symmetric positive-definite deformation tensor field of the form

P(x)=\begin{pmatrix}a(x)&s\\
s&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<|s|<1,\qquad a(x)>s^{2},

(4.27)

where
s
is a constant nonzero shear parameter. The deformed metric is

g=P^{T}\bar{g}P=P^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}+s^{2}&s(a+1)\\
s(a+1)&1+s^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.28)

Since
\bar{\Gamma}=0
, one has

\bar{\nabla}_{1}P=\partial_{1}P=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}(x)&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bar{\nabla}_{2}P=0.

(4.29)

Hence

\bar{L}_{1}=P^{-1}\partial_{1}P,\qquad\bar{L}_{2}=0.

(4.30)

Using

P^{-1}=\frac{1}{a-s^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-s\\
-s&a\end{pmatrix},

(4.31)

we obtain

\bar{L}_{1}=\frac{a^{\prime}}{a-s^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-s&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bar{L}_{2}=0.

(4.32)

The deformed-frame representation is

L_{i}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{i}P.

(4.33)

A direct computation gives

L_{1}=\frac{a^{\prime}}{(a-s^{2})^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}a(1+s^{2})&s(1+s^{2})\\
-as(a+1)&-s^{2}(a+1)\end{pmatrix},\qquad L_{2}=0.

(4.34)

Therefore, in general,

L_{1}\neq\bar{L}_{1}.

(4.35)

For example, at a point where

a=1,\qquad a^{\prime}=1,\qquad s=\frac{1}{2},

(4.36)

one finds

\bar{L}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}&0\\
-\frac{2}{3}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad L_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{20}{9}&\frac{10}{9}\\
-\frac{16}{9}&-\frac{8}{9}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.37)

This shows explicitly that the distinction between
\bar{L}_{\mu}
and
L_{\mu}
is essential in a genuine non-diagonal shear deformation.

4.6
A conformal spherical family

Finally, consider a spherical family obtained by conformally deforming the standard round sphere of radius
R
, which now serves as the reference geometry,

\bar{g}=R^{2}d\theta^{2}+R^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}.

(4.38)

This is the ordinary round metric on
S^{2}
, with constant Gaussian curvature

\bar{K}=\frac{1}{R^{2}}.

(4.39)

Take a pure dilational deformation

P=e^{\varphi(\theta,\phi)}I.

(4.40)

Then the deformed metric becomes

g=P^{T}\bar{g}P=e^{2\varphi(\theta,\phi)}\bar{g}.

(4.41)

Thus the deformation is conformal. Here the reference geometry already has a nontrivial Levi-Civita connection, so the same deformation field
P
changes the pre-existing full connection structure rather than creating it from zero. In particular, the deformed metric changes the Levi-Civita branch itself, while the deformation-rate chain determines the additional nonmetric contribution. The raw deformation-rate tensor is

\bar{L}_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P=(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)I.

(4.42)

Since this tensor is already proportional to the identity, its deformed-frame representation coincides with itself:

L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P=\bar{L}_{\mu}=(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)I.

(4.43)

Hence

\Lambda_{\mu}=(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)I.

(4.44)

In dimension two, the conformal scalar-curvature transformation law gives

\widetilde{S}=e^{-2\varphi}(S-2\bar{\Delta}\varphi)

[
1
, Thm. 7.30, p. 217]
. Since

S=2K
for Riemannian surfaces
[
1
, Thm. 8.27, p. 249]
, it follows that

K[g]=e^{-2\varphi}(\bar{K}-\bar{\Delta}\varphi)=e^{-2\varphi}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}-\bar{\Delta}\varphi\right),

(4.45)

where
\bar{\Delta}
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator of the reference sphere. Therefore, if
\varphi
is nonconstant, then in general

\Lambda_{\mu}\neq 0,

(4.46)

and the curvature
K[g]
is no longer constant. The resulting intrinsic geometry is then no longer that of a standard round sphere.

By contrast, if
\varphi\equiv c
is constant, then

\bar{L}_{\mu}=0,\qquad L_{\mu}=0,\qquad\Lambda_{\mu}=0,\qquad g=e^{2c}\bar{g}.

(4.47)

In this case the deformed metric is exactly the round metric of a sphere of radius

R^{\prime}=e^{c}R,

(4.48)

with Gaussian curvature

K[g]=\frac{1}{e^{2c}R^{2}}.

(4.49)

Thus the constant-
\varphi
case reduces to an ordinary Riemannian sphere obtained by a global rescaling. It is a homothetic special case of the conformal deformation and provides a natural consistency check of the theory. This example shows that the present framework contains conformally deformed metric geometries as a special class of deformed metrics.

4.7
Summary of the illustrative families

Taken together, these planar and spherical families show that the same deformation field
P
governs the whole connection structure of the deformed geometry. Through
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
it induces the Levi-Civita branch, while through the deformation-rate chain it induces the additional symmetric sector
\Lambda
. The latter is naturally activated by genuine local dilation and shear and disappears precisely in those limiting cases where the deformation reduces to an ordinary Riemannian rescaling.

5
Relation to Riemannian Geometry, Conformal Geometry, and Metric-Affine Geometry

The present construction may be located between ordinary Riemannian geometry and generic metric-affine geometry. It remains metric-based, but allows the same deformation field
P
to induce the full connection structure of the deformed geometry: the Levi-Civita branch through the deformed metric and the additional symmetric branch through the deformation-rate chain.

If the deformation is trivial,
P=I
, then
g=\bar{g}
and
\Lambda=0
. More generally, whenever
\Lambda=0
, the total connection reduces to

\Gamma=\Gamma^{\circ}[g],

(5.1)

so the geometry is purely Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian. Thus ordinary metric geometry is recovered precisely when the induced additional sector vanishes.

Relative to ordinary Riemannian geometry, the essential difference is that the same deformation field
P
induces both parts of the total connection. Through the metric deformation
g=P^{T}\bar{g}P
, it induces the Levi-Civita connection
\Gamma^{\circ}[g]
of the deformed geometry; through the deformation-rate chain
P\mapsto\bar{L}_{\mu}\mapsto L_{\mu}\mapsto\Lambda_{\mu}
, it induces the additional nonmetric sector. Thus the full comparison structure is not exhausted by metric-compatible transport alone, even though the Levi-Civita part remains canonically metric-determined.

Conformal deformations occur as a distinguished subclass. In the pure dilational case,

g=e^{2\varphi}\bar{g}.

(5.2)

Section 4 shows that constant conformal factors reduce to ordinary Riemannian rescalings, whereas, within the present framework, nonconstant conformal factors may generate a nontrivial induced symmetric sector
\Lambda
.

Relative to generic metric-affine geometry, the difference is that the whole connection structure is not obtained by prescribing an independent affine field. Instead, both the Levi-Civita and nonmetric parts are induced by the metric-deformation chain

P\longmapsto g=P^{T}\bar{g}P,\qquad P\longmapsto\bar{L}_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P,\qquad L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P,\qquad\Lambda_{\mu}=\mathrm{Sym}_{g}(L_{\mu}).

(5.3)

Hence the present framework is more restrictive than generic metric-affine geometry, while remaining broader than ordinary Riemannian geometry
[
4
, pp. 1–8]
[
5
, pp. 465–480]
.

In this sense, the role of
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
is guiding rather than exhaustive: within the present framework, the independent geometric input is the symmetric deformation field
P
, not a separate rotational gauge field. The same field
P
induces the Levi-Civita sector through the metric deformation and the nonmetric sector through the deformation-rate chain. The metric-preserving contribution of the deformation-rate is absorbed into the Levi-Civita branch, while the genuinely nonmetric contribution survives as
\Lambda
. The resulting geometry is therefore a deformation-induced extension of Riemannian geometry, not a fully independent affine generalization.

6
Discussion and Outlook

The present framework has been developed from a simple geometric idea: in addition to bending, a geometric object may also undergo genuine local stretching, compression, and shear relative to a reference geometry. The purpose of the present framework is to provide a possible geometric description of this additional deformation sector while keeping the metric fundamental and avoiding a completely independent affine generalization.

From this viewpoint, the reference metric
\bar{g}
and the deformation tensor
P
play distinct roles. The reference metric carries the original geometric structure prior to the additional deformation, while
P
encodes the genuine local dilation and shear applied to that structure. The key point is that
P
induces the whole connection structure of the deformed geometry: its Levi-Civita part through the deformed metric, and its additional nonmetric part through the deformation-rate chain. In this process, the metric-preserving contribution of the deformation-rate is absorbed into the Levi-Civita branch once the latter is constructed from the deformed metric, whereas the genuinely nonmetric contribution survives as the tensor
\Lambda
. The deformed metric

g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P

(6.1)

records the resulting metric geometry, whereas

\bar{L}_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}P,\qquad L_{\mu}=P^{-1}\bar{L}_{\mu}P,\qquad\Lambda_{\mu}=\mathrm{Sym}_{g}(L_{\mu})

(6.2)

record the deformation-induced comparison structure generated by the same process.

A central feature of the theory is that the whole connection structure is deformation-induced. The Levi-Civita part is fixed by the metric
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
, while the tensor
\Lambda
is induced by the deformation field through the associated deformation-rate chain. Thus the non-Riemannian sector is not introduced as a free affine variable. This makes the framework broader than ordinary Riemannian geometry, yet more restrictive than generic metric-affine geometry.

The role of the metric should also be emphasized. The metric remains the basic geometric object governing the deformed space. What changes is not the centrality of the metric, but the assumption that metric-compatible Levi-Civita transport should exhaust the full neighboring-point comparison law. In the present framework, the Levi-Civita connection of the deformed metric still governs the standard transport; however, the same deformation process also produces a genuinely nonmetric remainder, represented by the deformation-induced symmetric sector
\Lambda
.

The examples of the plane and the sphere illustrate two important facts. First, the same deformation field
P
induces both the Levi-Civita and nonmetric parts of the deformed connection structure, the former through the metric
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
and the latter through the deformation-rate chain. Second, the theory naturally contains ordinary Riemannian geometry as a limiting case. In particular, the conformally deformed sphere shows that constant conformal rescalings reduce to ordinary Riemannian geometry, whereas, within the present framework, nonconstant conformal factors generally activate a nontrivial additional sector. The local spherical metric generated from a flat reference metric further shows that the deformation law
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
can realize nontrivial local curved metrics starting from a flat reference geometry.

The theory has been formulated here in the torsion-free case. This restriction is not merely technical. The present theory is built from the local linear deformation framework provided by
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
. Within this framework, the relevant sectors are the rotational sector and the genuine symmetric deformation sector, namely dilation and shear. Torsion does not arise as a direct part of this local linear decomposition. Its geometric meaning is of a different kind, being related not to pointwise linear deformation itself, but to an additional translational or dislocation-type structure. For this reason, torsion is not included in the present paper.

Nevertheless, the present construction suggests a possible future extension. If one wishes to include deformation modes analogous to local twisting or dislocation-type structures, one may expect a torsional sector to arise from an antisymmetric deformation-induced contribution to the total connection. The present framework would then appear as the symmetric part of a more general deformation geometry. This extension, however, is left for future work.

Another natural question concerns reduction and reconstruction. In the present paper, the construction is expressed in terms of a reference metric and a deformation tensor. Whether, and under what additional conditions, the deformation-induced non-Riemannian sector can be rewritten entirely in terms of the deformed metric and its derived geometric quantities remains a separate question. The present work does not pursue such a reduction, since its primary purpose is to formulate the deformation geometry itself.

The present framework may also be of interest in mathematical physics whenever one wishes to retain a metric-based setting while enlarging the comparison structure beyond the Levi-Civita branch. In particular, it may provide a geometric basis for further investigations of metric-based gravitational theories with an additional deformation-induced comparison sector.

In summary, the framework proposed here may be viewed as a metric-based deformation geometry in which the same field
P
induces the whole connection structure of the deformed space. Through the metric deformation
g=P^{T}\bar{g}\,P
it determines the Levi-Civita branch, while through the deformation-rate chain it determines the additional symmetric nonmetric branch. The resulting geometry keeps the metric central, yet enlarges the comparison structure beyond ordinary Riemannian transport by a deformation-induced mechanism rather than by an independent affine freedom.
