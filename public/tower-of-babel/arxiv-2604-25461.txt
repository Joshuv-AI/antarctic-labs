Cyclotomic Numbers of Order q − 1 q-1 over 𝔽 q r \mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
Hayaki Kudo, Yuto Nogata

Abstract




Abstract

Let
q=p^{n}
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
,
e=q-1
, and
k=\frac{q^{r}-1}{e}
. In this paper, we study the cyclotomic numbers
(a,b)_{q-1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
. We prove that
(a,b)_{q-1}\leq\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
for all
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
except when
q=2
and
r\geq 3
. We also give sharper bounds for prime values of
r
, especially for
r=2
and
r=3
.

11T22, 11T24, 12E20

Cyclotomy, cyclotomic number, finite field

Introduction and main results

We begin by recalling the standard definitions of cyclotomic cosets, cyclotomic numbers, and the matrices
C^{(a,b)}
.

Definition 1.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Assume that
k\mid|\mathbb{F}|-1
, and let
e:=\frac{|\mathbb{F}|-1}{k}
. Fix
\omega

\langle\omega\rangle=\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. For

. For

, define the cyclotomic number by
(a,b)_{e}:=\#\{x\in C_{a}\mid x+1\in C_{b}\}
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
0\leq a,b\leq e-1

k\times k

over
\mathbb{F}

c_{ij}^{(a,b)}=\begin{cases}1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk},&\textup{if }i=j,\\
\binom{k}{j-i},&\textup{if }i<j,\\
\omega^{ak}\binom{k}{i-j},&\textup{if }i>j.\end{cases}

According to

[

.
[

proved that, for fixed

[

established upper bounds for cyclotomic numbers for large characteristic. In particular,

Fact 1.3

(

]

Let

(i)

In this paper, we take

,

, and

Theorem 1.4

(Main theorem)
.

Let
q=p^{n}
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
,
k=\frac{q^{r}-1}{q-1}
, and
e=q-1
. Then the following statements hold for every
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
.

(1)

If
q\neq 2
, then
(a,b)_{q-1}\leq\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
. If
q=2
, then
(0,0)_{1}=2^{r}-2

, and the inequality fails when
r\geq 3
.

(2)

With the notation of Definition

(a,b)_{q-1}=\frac{q^{r}+(-1)^{r}(q-2)^{r}-2}{(q-1)^{2}}+(-1)^{r-1}W_{(-1)^{r}\omega^{ak},\omega^{bk}}(q,r)

(3)

If
r
is an odd prime
\ell

0\leq(a,b)_{q-1}\leq\begin{cases}\ell q^{\ell-2}+1,&\textup{if }p=\ell,\\
\ell q^{\ell-2}+\ell-1,&\textup{if }p\neq\ell.\end{cases}

If
r=2
, then
(a,b)_{q-1}\in\{0,1,2\}

r=3
, then
6\leq(a,b)_{q-1}\leq 2q+4

Proof.

(1) follows from Corollary

(3) follows from Corollaries

5.8

2
Notation and preliminaries

In this section, we collect the facts and lemmas needed for the proof of the main theorem. The value of
\lceil k/2\rceil
is

\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil=\begin{cases}\frac{q^{r}+q-2}{2(q-1)}&\text{if $q$ is even,}\\
\frac{q^{r}+q-2}{2(q-1)}&\text{if $q$ is odd and $r$ is odd,}\\
\frac{q^{r}-1}{2(q-1)}&\text{if $q$ is odd and $r$ is even.}\end{cases}

(2.1)

Throughout this section,
\mathbb{F}
denotes a finite field of characteristic
p
. A multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}^{\times}
means a group homomorphism
\chi:\mathbb{F}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. We extend each multiplicative character to all of
\mathbb{F}
by setting
\chi(0):=0
. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{\times}}
denote the group of all multiplicative characters of
\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. The trivial multiplicative character is denoted by
\mathbf{1}
. For a condition
P
, we write
\mathbf{1}_{P}:=1
if
P
holds, otherwise
\mathbf{1}_{P}:=0
.

Fact 2.1

(c.f.
[
10
]
, Theorem 5.4)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field. For every
u\in\mathbb{F}
, we have
\frac{1}{|\mathbb{F}|-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{\times}}}\chi(u)=\mathbf{1}_{u=1}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\eta\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\backslash\{\mathbf{1}\}
. Then

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}}\eta(y)=0
.

Proof.

Since
\eta(0)=0
, we have

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}}\eta(y)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\eta(y)
.
Let
\langle\omega\rangle=\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. Since
\eta\neq\mathbf{1}
, we have
\eta(\omega)\neq 1
. Hence

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\eta(y)=\sum_{s=0}^{|\mathbb{F}|-2}\eta(\omega^{s})=\sum_{s=0}^{|\mathbb{F}|-2}\eta(\omega)^{s}=\frac{\eta(\omega)^{|\mathbb{F}|-1}-1}{\eta(\omega)-1}=0
.
Therefore
\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}}\eta(y)=0
.
∎

•

the relative norm

\begin{aligned} J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}},J_{\mathbb{F}}&:\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\to\mathbb{C}\ \text{by}\ \left\{\begin{aligned} J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}(\chi,\psi)&:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\chi(x)\psi(1-x),\\

J_{\mathbb{F}}(\chi,\psi)=\chi(-1)J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}(\chi,\psi).

Fact 2.3

(
[
2
]

The following statements hold.

(1)

If

in

(3)

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
q=p^{n}
, and let
\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}
. If
\chi,\psi,\chi\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
, then

|J_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi,\psi)|=q^{1/2}.

(2.3)

Proof.

By Fact
2.3
(1) applied to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have

J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi,\psi)=\frac{G_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi)G_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\psi)}{G_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi\psi)}
.
Combining this with Fact
2.3
(2) applied to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we obtain

|J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi,\psi)|=q^{1/2}
.
By (
2.2
), we have

|J_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi,\psi)|=|J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi,\psi)|=q^{1/2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{\times}}
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
\chi=\mathbf{1}
and
\psi=\mathbf{1}
, then

J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})=J_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})=|\mathbb{F}|-2.

(2.4)

(2)

If
\chi=\mathbf{1}
and
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
, then

J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbf{1},\psi)=J_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbf{1},\psi)=-1.

(2.5)

(3)

If
\chi\neq\mathbf{1}
and
\psi=\mathbf{1}
, then

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}(\chi,\mathbf{1})

\displaystyle=-1,

\displaystyle J_{\mathbb{F}}(\chi,\mathbf{1})

\displaystyle=-\chi(-1).

(2.6b)

(4)

If
\chi\neq\mathbf{1}
,
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
, and
\chi\psi=\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}(\chi,\psi)

\displaystyle=-\chi(-1),

Proof.

First, we compute the values of

).

(1): Assume that
\chi=\mathbf{1}
and
\psi=\mathbf{1}

Since

(2): Assume that
\chi=\mathbf{1}

(3): Assume that
\chi\neq\mathbf{1}

(4): Assume that
\chi\neq\mathbf{1}
,
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
, and

J^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}(\chi,\psi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0,1\}}\chi(x(1-x)^{-1})

.

Lemma 2.10
.

Assume that

has degree

\mathbb{F}_{q}

Then

m_{x}(-1)=(-1)^{d}\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+1)

Proof.

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-conjugates of

, thus

m_{x}(X)=\prod_{\nu=0}^{d-1}(X-x^{q^{\nu}})
.
Hence we calculate
c_{0}=m_{x}(0)=\prod_{\nu=0}^{d-1}(-x^{q^{\nu}})=(-1)^{d}\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)
. Since the characteristic is

1+x^{q^{\nu}}=(1+x)^{q^{\nu}}
. Therefore,

m_{x}(-1)=(-1)^{d}\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+1)
.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

C_{0}=\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}})
.

Proof.

The restriction

\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

is a surjective group homomorphism. Hence the homomorphism theorem yields
|\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=\frac{q^{r}-1}{q-1}=k
. On the other hand,
|\langle\omega^{e}\rangle|=\frac{q^{r}-1}{e}=k
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}^{\times}
is cyclic, a subgroup of order
k
is unique. Therefore
C_{0}=\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}})
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

For
0\leq a\leq q-2
, we obtain

C_{a}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}^{\times}\mid\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=\omega^{ak}\}
.

Proof.

Note that
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\omega^{a})=\omega^{ak}
.

(\subset)
: Let
x\in C_{a}
. By the definition, we can denote
x=\omega^{a}z
with
z\in C_{0}
. By Lemma
2.11
, we have
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(z)=1
, and thus
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=\omega^{ak}
.

(\supset)
: Take
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}^{\times}
so that
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=\omega^{ak}
. Then
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\omega^{-a}x)=1
. Hence, by Lemma
2.11
, we obtain
\omega^{-a}x\in C_{0}
. Therefore
x\in\omega^{a}C_{0}=C_{a}
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

Assume that
q=2
. For every
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, we have
(0,0)_{1}=2^{r}-2
. Moreover,
(0,0)_{1}=\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
if
r=2
, and
(0,0)_{1}>\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
if
r\geq 3
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\times}=\{1\}
, we have
a=b=0
. Denote
S:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}^{\times}\mid\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(x)=1,\ \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(x+1)=1\}
. By Lemma
2.12
, we have
(0,0)_{1}=\#S
.

Now, since
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\times}=\{1\}
, we have
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(x)=1
for every
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}^{\times}
. Also, we have
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(x+1)=1
if and only if
x\neq 1
. Hence
S=\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}^{\times}\setminus\{1\}
, and therefore
(0,0)_{1}=\#S=2^{r}-2
.
Since
k=2^{r}-1
, we have
\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil=2^{r-1}
. Thus
(0,0)_{1}=\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
if
r=2
, while
(0,0)_{1}>\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
if
r\geq 3
.
∎

3
Estimates for
(a,b)_{q-1}\leq\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil

In this section, we prove the inequality
(a,b)_{q-1}\leq\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
. Throughout this section, we abbreviate
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
as
\operatorname{N}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
q=p^{n}
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, and
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
. Then

(a,b)_{q-1}=\frac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}\sum_{\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})\psi(\omega^{-bk})J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\psi\circ\operatorname{N}).

(3.1)

Proof.

By Fact
2.1
, for each
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
we have
(q-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{N}(x)=\omega^{ak}}\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{N}(x+1)=\omega^{bk}}=\sum_{\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}}\chi(\operatorname{N}(x)\omega^{-ak})\psi(\operatorname{N}(x+1)\omega^{-bk})
. Using Lemma
2.12
, we obtain the formula.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
, define

A_{r}(a):=\begin{cases}-(q-2),&\textup{if }r\textup{ is even and }a=0,\\
-(q-2),&\textup{if }r\textup{ is odd},\ q\textup{ is even, and }a=0,\\
-(q-2),&\textup{if }r\textup{ is odd},\ q\textup{ is odd, and }a=\frac{q-1}{2},\\
1,&\textup{otherwise,}\end{cases}

B(b):=\begin{cases}-(q-2),&\textup{if }b=0,\\
1,&\textup{if }b\neq 0,\end{cases}\quad C(a,b):=\begin{cases}-(q-2),&\textup{if }a=b,\\
1,&\textup{if }a\neq b.\end{cases}

Then

(a,b)_{q-1}=M_{a,b}+E_{a,b}
,
where

M_{a,b}:=\frac{q^{r}-2+A_{r}(a)+B(b)+C(a,b)}{(q-1)^{2}}

and

|E_{a,b}|\leq\frac{(q-2)(q-3)}{(q-1)^{2}}q^{r/2}
.

Proof.

By (
3.1
),

(a,b)_{q-1}=\frac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}\sum_{\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})\psi(\omega^{-bk})J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\psi\circ\operatorname{N})
.
We divide this sum into five cases according to whether each
\chi
,
\psi
, and
\chi\psi
is equal to
\mathbf{1}
.

(1):

If
\chi=\mathbf{1}
and
\psi=\mathbf{1}
, then (
2.8
) gives

J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\psi\circ\operatorname{N})=q^{r}-2
.

(2):

If
\chi\neq\mathbf{1}
and
\psi=\mathbf{1}
, then
\chi\psi=\chi\neq\mathbf{1}
. By (
2.6b
) and (
2.9
), we have

J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\psi\circ\operatorname{N})=(-1)^{r-1}J_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi,\psi)^{r}=-\chi((-1)^{r})
. Hence

\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\mathbf{1})=-\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})\chi((-1)^{r})
.

(i): Assume that
r
is even. Then
\chi((-1)^{r})=\chi(1)=1
. Hence, by Fact
2.1
,

-\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})\chi((-1)^{r})=-\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})=\begin{cases}-(q-2),&\textup{if }a=0,\\
1,&\textup{if }a\neq 0.\end{cases}

(ii): Assume that
r
is odd and
q
is even. Then
\chi((-1)^{r})=\chi(-1)=\chi(1)=1
. Hence, by Fact
2.1
,

-\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})\chi((-1)^{r})=-\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})=\begin{cases}-(q-2),&\textup{if }a=0,\\
1,&\textup{if }a\neq 0.\end{cases}

(iii): Assume that
r
is odd and
q
is odd, then
\omega^{\frac{q-1}{2}k}=-1
. Therefore, by Fact
2.1
,

-\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})\chi((-1)^{r})=-\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{(\frac{q-1}{2}-a)k})=\begin{cases}-(q-2),&\textup{if }a=\frac{q-1}{2},\\
1,&\textup{if }a\neq\frac{q-1}{2}.\end{cases}

Thus this part gives
A_{r}(a)
.

(3):

If
\chi=\mathbf{1}
and
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
, then
\chi\psi=\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
. By (
2.5
) and (
2.9
), we have

J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\psi\circ\operatorname{N})=(-1)^{r-1}J_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi,\psi)^{r}=-1
.
Thus, by Fact
2.1
,

-\sum_{\psi\neq\mathbf{1}}\psi(\omega^{-bk})=\begin{cases}-(q-2),&\textup{if }b=0,\\
1,&\textup{if }b\neq 0.\end{cases}

Hence this part gives
B(b)
.

(4):

If
\chi\neq\mathbf{1}
,
\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
, and
\chi\psi=\mathbf{1}
, then
\psi=\chi^{-1}
. By (
2.7b
) and (
2.9
), we have

J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\psi\circ\operatorname{N})=(-1)^{r-1}J_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\chi,\psi)^{r}=-1
.
Thus, by Fact
2.1
,

-\sum_{\chi\neq\mathbf{1}}\chi(\omega^{(b-a)k})=\begin{cases}-(q-2),&\textup{if }a=b,\\
1,&\textup{if }a\neq b.\end{cases}

Hence this part gives
C(a,b)
.

(5):

Consider the case
\chi,\psi,\chi\psi\neq\mathbf{1}
. Define

E_{a,b}:=\frac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\\
\chi,\psi,\chi\psi\neq\mathbf{1}\end{subarray}}\chi(\omega^{-ak})\psi(\omega^{-bk})J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\psi\circ\operatorname{N})
.
By (
2.3
) and (
2.9
), we have

|J_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N},\psi\circ\operatorname{N})|=q^{r/2}
.
Since there are
(q-2)(q-3)
such pairs
(\chi,\psi)
, the triangle inequality gives

|E_{a,b}|\leq\frac{(q-2)(q-3)}{(q-1)^{2}}q^{r/2}
.

Combining the five cases, we obtain

(a,b)_{q-1}=\frac{q^{r}-2+A_{r}(a)+B(b)+C(a,b)}{(q-1)^{2}}+E_{a,b}=M_{a,b}+E_{a,b}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume that
q=3
. Then

(a,b)_{2}=\begin{cases}\frac{3^{r}+1}{4},&\textup{if }r\textup{ is odd},\ a=0,\textup{ and }b=1,\\
\frac{3^{r}-3}{4},&\textup{if }r\textup{ is odd and either }a\neq 0\textup{ or }b\neq 1,\\
\frac{3^{r}-5}{4},&\textup{if }r\textup{ is even},\ a=0,\textup{ and }b=0,\\
\frac{3^{r}-1}{4},&\textup{if }r\textup{ is even and either }a\neq 0\textup{ or }b\neq 0.\end{cases}

In particular,
(a,b)_{2}\leq\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
for every
0\leq a,b\leq 1
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, we have
|E_{a,b}|\leq\frac{(q-2)(q-3)}{(q-1)^{2}}q^{r/2}
. Substituting
q=3
, we obtain
E_{a,b}=0
for every choice of
a,b
. Our formula follows immediately from the definitions of
A_{r}(a)
,
B(b)
, and
C(a,b)
. The final assertion follows from (
2.1
).
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume that
q\geq 4
and
r\geq 3
. Then
(a,b)_{q-1}<\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
for every
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, we have

(a,b)_{q-1}\leq\frac{q^{r}+1}{(q-1)^{2}}+\frac{(q-2)(q-3)}{(q-1)^{2}}q^{r/2}
.

(i): Assume that
q
is even or
r
is odd. Then (
2.1
) gives
\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil=\frac{q^{r}+q-2}{2(q-1)}
, and hence

\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil-(a,b)_{q-1}\geq\frac{q-3}{2(q-1)^{2}}\left(q^{r}-2(q-2)q^{r/2}+q\right).

Since
r\geq 3
and
q\geq 4
, we have
q^{r/2}\geq q^{3/2}>2(q-2)
. Thus
q^{r}-2(q-2)q^{r/2}+q>0
, and the desired result follows.

(ii): Assume that
q
is odd and
r
is even. Then
q\geq 5
and
r\geq 4
. By (
2.1
), we have
\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil=\frac{q^{r}-1}{2(q-1)}
, and hence

\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil-(a,b)_{q-1}\geq\frac{q^{r/2}(q-3)\left(q^{r/2}-2(q-2)\right)-(q+1)}{2(q-1)^{2}}.

Since
q^{r/2}\geq q^{2}
and
q^{r/2}-2(q-2)\geq q^{2}-2q+4
, we obtain

(q-3)\left(q^{r}-2(q-2)q^{r/2}\right)\geq(q-3)q^{2}(q^{2}-2q+4)>q+1
.
Therefore
(a,b)_{q-1}<\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
.
∎

3.1
r=2

Next, we consider the case where
r=2
separately. In this case, note that
k=\frac{q^{2}-1}{q-1}=q+1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
q
be a power of an odd prime
p^{n}
,
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
, and
\Delta(a,b):=(1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk})^{2}-4\omega^{ak}
. Then we have

(a,b)_{q-1}=\begin{cases}1,&\textup{if }\Delta(a,b)=0,\\
0,&\textup{if }\Delta(a,b)\neq 0\textup{ and }\Delta(a,b)\textup{ is a square in }\mathbb{F}_{q},\\
2,&\textup{if }\Delta(a,b)\textup{ is a nonsquare in }\mathbb{F}_{q}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let

S:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\mid\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=\omega^{ak},\ \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+1)=\omega^{bk}\}
. By Lemma
2.12
,
(a,b)_{q-1}=\#S
. We decompose
S=(S\cap\mathbb{F}_{q})\sqcup(S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

(1): Let
x\in S\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
x^{2}=\omega^{ak}
and
(x+1)^{2}=\omega^{bk}
. Subtracting these two equations gives
2x+1=\omega^{bk}-\omega^{ak}
. Since
q
is a power of an odd prime, the element
2
is invertible in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, thus
x=\frac{\omega^{bk}-\omega^{ak}-1}{2}
is uniquely determined. Substituting it into
x^{2}=\omega^{ak}
yields
\Delta(a,b)=0
. Conversely, if
\Delta(a,b)=0
, then
x=\frac{\omega^{bk}-\omega^{ak}-1}{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfies the two norm conditions above.

(2): Let
x\in S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
\deg_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=2
, we have
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Hence the minimal polynomial of
x
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a monic polynomial of degree
2
of the form
m_{x}(X)=X^{2}+c_{1}X+c_{0}
. By Lemma
2.10
,
c_{0}=\omega^{ak}
and
1-c_{1}+\omega^{ak}=\omega^{bk}
. Therefore
c_{1}=1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}
, and thus
m_{x}(X)=X^{2}+(1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk})X+\omega^{ak}
.

Conversely, if this polynomial is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then its two roots lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and both have this polynomial as their minimal polynomial. Hence, by Lemma
2.10
, each root satisfies the required norm conditions. Thus the degree-
2
contribution is exactly
2
when this polynomial is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. On the other hand, if the polynomial splits over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then all its roots lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e., the degree-
2
contribution is
0
.

The discriminant of this quadratic polynomial is
\Delta(a,b)=(1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk})^{2}-4\omega^{ak}
. In odd characteristic, the cases
\Delta(a,b)=0
,
\Delta(a,b)\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
, and
\Delta(a,b)\notin(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
correspond respectively to a double root, a split polynomial with two distinct roots, and an irreducible polynomial. Consequently, the corresponding contributions are
1
,
0
, and
2
, respectively.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that
q=2^{n}
, and let
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
. Put
c:=1+\omega^{ak}+\omega^{bk}
. Then

(a,b)_{q-1}=\begin{cases}1,&\textup{if }c=0,\\
0,&\textup{if }c\neq 0\textup{ and }\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\omega^{ak}/c^{2})=0,\\
2,&\textup{if }c\neq 0\textup{ and }\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\omega^{ak}/c^{2})=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let

S:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\mid\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=\omega^{ak},\ \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+1)=\omega^{bk}\}
. By Lemma
2.12
,
(a,b)_{q-1}=\#S
. We decompose
S=(S\cap\mathbb{F}_{q})\sqcup(S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

(1): Let
x\in S\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
x^{2}=\omega^{ak}
and
(x+1)^{2}=\omega^{bk}
, thus
1+\omega^{ak}+\omega^{bk}=0
. Conversely, if
1+\omega^{ak}+\omega^{bk}=0
, then the unique element
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
x^{2}=\omega^{ak}
also satisfies
(x+1)^{2}=\omega^{bk}
.

(2): Let
x\in S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
3.5
(2), we have
m_{x}(X)=X^{2}+cX+\omega^{ak}
. If
c=0
, this polynomial has its unique root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence there is no element
x\in S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
in this case. Assume that
c\neq 0
. Changing variables
X=cY
,
X^{2}+cX+\omega^{ak}=c^{2}(Y^{2}+Y+\omega^{ak}/c^{2})
. By Fact
2.9
, this polynomial is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\omega^{ak}/c^{2})=1
. If it is irreducible, then it has exactly two roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and both roots belong to
S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Indeed, if
x
is one of the roots, then the other root is
x^{q}
, and hence
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=\omega^{ak}
and
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+1)=(x+1)(x^{q}+1)=\omega^{ak}+c+1=\omega^{bk}
. If the polynomial is reducible, then its roots lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence there is no element
x\in S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
in this case. Combining (1) and (2), we obtain the three cases in the statement.
∎

Let

(a,b)_{q-1}\in\{0,1,2\}
follows from Propositions
3.5
and

. Since

∎

Remark 3.9
.

Assume that
q=2
. Then
e=q-1=1
, and hence

. By Lemma

, we have

4
A Cayley digraph interpretation

In this section, we give an exact formula for
(a,b)_{q-1}
in terms of directed walks in a Cayley digraph. It gives a structural interpretation which is complementary to the estimate in §
3
. Throughout this section, we continuously abbreviate
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
as
\operatorname{N}
.

Definition 4.1
.

•

For
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, define
W_{u,v}(q,r):=\#\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\})^{r}\mid\prod_{t=1}^{r}x_{t}=u,\ \prod_{t=1}^{r}(1-x_{t})=v\}
.

•

Define
G_{q}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
\Omega_{q}:=\{(x,1-x)\mid x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}\}\subset G_{q}
.

•

Define
\Gamma_{q}
to be the directed Cayley graph with vertex set
G_{q}
, whose directed edges from
g
to
gh
are
(g,gh)
for
g\in G_{q}
and
h\in\Omega_{q}
.

•

Fix an ordering of
G_{q}
. For
(u,v)\in G_{q}
, let
\nu(u,v)
denote the index of the vertex
(u,v)
. Let
A_{q}
be the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma_{q}
whose
(\nu(u,v),\nu(ux,v(1-x)))
-entry is
1
for
(u,v)\in G_{q}
and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every

5

In this section, we consider the case where
r
is an odd prime
\ell
. Particularly, we consider the case

separately.

Define

T_{\ell}(a,b):=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\mid x^{\ell}=\omega^{ak},\ (x+1)^{\ell}=\omega^{bk}\}
,
and

I_{\ell}(a,b):=\{m_{x}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\mid x\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\ [\mathbb{F}_{q}(x):\mathbb{F}_{q}]=\ell,\ m_{x}(0)=-\omega^{ak},\ m_{x}(-1)=-\omega^{bk}\}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
r
be an odd prime
\ell
,
q
be a prime power
p^{n}
, and
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
. Then
(a,b)_{q-1}=\#T_{\ell}(a,b)+\ell\#I_{\ell}(a,b)
.

Proof.

Put
S:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}^{\times}\mid\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=\omega^{ak},\ \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+1)=\omega^{bk}\}
. By Lemma
2.12
, we have
(a,b)_{q-1}=\#S
. Since
[\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}:\mathbb{F}_{q}]=\ell
is prime, we decompose
S=(S\cap\mathbb{F}_{q})\sqcup(S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

(1): Let
x\in S\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)=x^{\ell}
and
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+1)=(x+1)^{\ell}
. Therefore
\#(S\cap\mathbb{F}_{q})=\#T_{\ell}(a,b)
.

(2): Let
x\in S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Define
\Phi:S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}\to I_{\ell}(a,b)

(x\mapsto m_{x}(X))
. Since
x\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}
. Hence
m_{x}(X)
is a monic irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
\ell
. Moreover, Lemma
2.10
gives
m_{x}(0)=-\omega^{ak}
and
m_{x}(-1)=-\omega^{bk}
. Thus
m_{x}(X)\in I_{\ell}(a,b)
.

Now let
f(X)\in I_{\ell}(a,b)
. Since
f(X)
is irreducible of degree
\ell
, it has exactly
\ell
distinct roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}
. Let
y
be one of these roots. Then
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(y):\mathbb{F}_{q}]=\ell
, hence
\mathbb{F}_{q}(y)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}
. Moreover,
m_{y}(X)=f(X)
, and Lemma
2.10
gives
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(y)=\omega^{ak}
and
\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(y+1)=\omega^{bk}
. Thus
y\in S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Therefore
\Phi^{-1}(f)
exactly consists of the
\ell
roots of
f
, and hence
\#\Phi^{-1}(f)=\ell
.

It follows that
\#(S\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q})=\ell\#I_{\ell}(a,b)
. Combining (1) and (2), we obtain the statement.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
r
be an odd prime
\ell
,
q
be a prime power
p^{n}
, and
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
. Then

\displaystyle\#T_{\ell}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\textup{if }p=\ell,1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}=0,\\
0,&\textup{if }p=\ell,1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}\neq 0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle 0\leq\#T_{\ell}(a,b)

\displaystyle\leq\ell-1\quad\textup{if }p\neq\ell.

Proof.

We prove the statement by considering the following two cases.

(1): Assume that
p=\ell
. Then
(X+1)^{\ell}=X^{\ell}+1
. If
1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}\neq 0
, then
T_{\ell}(a,b)=\emptyset
. If
1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}=0
, then
T_{\ell}(a,b)=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\mid x^{\ell}=\omega^{ak}\}
. Since
x\mapsto x^{\ell}
is the Frobenius automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, this set has exactly one element. It proves our formula in the case
p=\ell
.

(2): Assume that
p\neq\ell
. If
x\in T_{\ell}(a,b)
, then
x
is a root of
(X+1)^{\ell}-X^{\ell}+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}
. Since the highest degree term of this polynomial is
\ell X^{\ell-1}
, it has degree
\ell-1
. Therefore
0\leq\#T_{\ell}(a,b)\leq\ell-1
.
∎

Define

P_{\ell}(a,b):=\{f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\mid\deg f=\ell,\ f\textup{ is monic},\ f(0)=-\omega^{ak},\ f(-1)=-\omega^{bk}\}
.
Then
I_{\ell}(a,b)\subset P_{\ell}(a,b)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
r
be an odd prime
\ell
,
q
be a prime power
p^{n}
, and

. Then
0\leq\#I_{\ell}(a,b)\leq q^{\ell-2}
.

Proof.

By the definition,
\#I_{\ell}(a,b)\geq 0
. Any

can be written in the form

. Since
\ell
is an odd prime, the condition
f(-1)=-\omega^{bk}
gives
c_{1}=\sum_{\mu=2}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{\mu}c_{\mu}-(1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk})
. Therefore an element of
P_{\ell}(a,b)
is determined by
\ell-2
coefficients, and hence
\#P_{\ell}(a,b)=q^{\ell-2}
. Since
I_{\ell}(a,b)\subset P_{\ell}(a,b)
, it follows that
\#I_{\ell}(a,b)\leq q^{\ell-2}
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
r=3
,
q
be a prime power
p^{n}
, and
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
. Put
A(X):=X^{3}-\omega^{ak}
and
B(X):=(X+1)^{3}-\omega^{bk}
. Then
\operatorname{Res}(A,B)=(1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk})^{3}+27\omega^{ak}\omega^{bk}
.

Proof.

Put
c:=1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}
. By the definition of the Sylvester matrix, we have

S(A,B)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{pmatrix}
, where

A_{11}=I_{3}
,
A_{12}=-\omega^{ak}I_{3}
,

A_{21}=\begin{pmatrix}1&3&3\\
0&1&3\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}
, and

A_{22}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\omega^{bk}&0&0\\
3&1-\omega^{bk}&0\\
3&3&1-\omega^{bk}\end{pmatrix}
.
Put

L:=A_{21}-A_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}0&3&3\\
0&0&3\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

and

M:=A_{22}-A_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}c&0&0\\
3&c&0\\
3&3&c\end{pmatrix}
.
Then
\operatorname{Res}(A,B)=\det\begin{pmatrix}I_{3}&-\omega^{ak}I_{3}\\
L&M\end{pmatrix}=\det(M+\omega^{ak}L)=c^{3}-27c\omega^{ak}+27\omega^{2ak}+27\omega^{ak}=(1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk})^{3}+27\omega^{ak}\omega^{bk}
.
∎

Note that

T_{3}(a,b):=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\mid x^{3}=\omega^{ak},\ (x+1)^{3}=\omega^{bk}\}
,
and

I_{3}(a,b):=\{m_{x}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\mid x\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\ [\mathbb{F}_{q}(x):\mathbb{F}_{q}]=3,\ m_{x}(0)=-\omega^{ak},\ m_{x}(-1)=-\omega^{bk}\}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
r=3
,
q
be a prime power
p^{n}
, and
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
.
Put
A(X):=X^{3}-\omega^{ak}
and
B(X):=(X+1)^{3}-\omega^{bk}
.
Let
\lambda:=0
if
p=2
, and let
\lambda:=\frac{q-1}{2}
if
p
is odd.
Then

\#T_{3}(a,b)=\begin{cases}1,&\textup{if }p=3\textup{ and }1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}=0,\\
0,&\textup{if }p=3\textup{ and }1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}\neq 0,\\
2,&\textup{if }p\neq 3,\ (a,b)=(0,\lambda),\ 3\mid(q-1),\\
0,&\textup{if }p\neq 3,\ (a,b)=(0,\lambda),\ 3\nmid(q-1),\\
1,&\textup{if }p\neq 3,\ (a,b)\neq(0,\lambda),\ \operatorname{Res}(A,B)=0,\\
0,&\textup{if }p\neq 3,\ (a,b)\neq(0,\lambda),\ \operatorname{Res}(A,B)\neq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.

We divide the argument into two cases: (1):
p=3
, and (2):
p\neq 3
.

(1): Suppose
p=3
. Then
B(X)-A(X)=1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}
. Hence a common root exists if and only if
1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}=0
. If the condition holds, then the equation
x^{3}=\omega^{ak}
has exactly one solution in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, since the Frobenius map
x\mapsto x^{3}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Therefore
\#T_{3}(a,b)=1
. If
1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}\neq 0
, then there is no common root and
\#T_{3}(a,b)=0
.

(2): Suppose
p\neq 3
. Put
u:=\omega^{ak}
and
v:=\omega^{bk}
. Then
3A(X)=(X-1)(B(X)-A(X))+L(X)
, where
L(X):=(v-u+2)X+(1-2u-v)
. Let
D(X):=\gcd(A(X),B(X))
. Since
D(X)
divides both
A(X)
and
B(X)
, it divides
B(X)-A(X)
. Hence
D(X)\mid L(X)
. The equality
L(X)=0
holds if and only if
(u,v)=(1,-1)
. By the definition of
\lambda
, it is equivalent to
(a,b)=(0,\lambda)
.

(i): Assume that
(a,b)\neq(0,\lambda)
. Then
L(X)
is not the zero polynomial. Since
D(X)\mid L(X)
and
\deg L\leq 1
, we have
\deg D(X)\leq 1
. On the other hand, Lemma
5.5
gives
\operatorname{Res}(A,B)=(1+u-v)^{3}+27uv
. By Fact
2.8
, the condition
\operatorname{Res}(A,B)=0
is equivalent to the existence of a nonconstant common divisor of
A(X)
and
B(X)
. Therefore
D(X)
has degree
1
if
\operatorname{Res}(A,B)=0
, and then there is exactly one common root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
\operatorname{Res}(A,B)\neq 0
, then there is no common root.

(ii): Assume that
(a,b)=(0,\lambda)
. Then
A(X)=X^{3}-1
and
B(X)=(X+1)^{3}+1
, and hence
B(X)-A(X)=3(X^{2}+X+1)
. Since
p\neq 3
, we have
D(X)=X^{2}+X+1
. The roots of
X^{2}+X+1
are primitive cubic roots of unity, and thus
X^{2}+X+1
splits over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
3\mid(q-1)
. Therefore
\#T_{3}(a,b)=2
if
3\mid(q-1)
, while
\#T_{3}(a,b)=0
if
3\nmid(q-1)
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
r=3
,
q
be a prime power
p^{n}
, and
0\leq a,b\leq q-2
. Define
\phi_{a,b}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,-1\}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}\ (x\mapsto x-1-\frac{\omega^{ak}}{x}+\frac{\omega^{bk}}{x+1})
. Then
\#I_{3}(a,b)=q-\#\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{a,b})
. In particular,
2\leq\#I_{3}(a,b)\leq\lfloor\frac{2q+2}{3}\rfloor
.

Proof.

Any polynomial
m_{x}(X)\in I_{3}(a,b)
can be written uniquely in the form
m_{x}(X)=X^{3}+c_{2}X^{2}+c_{1}X+c_{0}
. The condition
m_{x}(0)=-\omega^{ak}
gives
c_{0}=-\omega^{ak}
, while
m_{x}(-1)=-\omega^{bk}
yields
c_{2}-c_{1}=1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk}
. Writing
c_{2}=-s
, we obtain

m_{x}(X)=X^{3}-sX^{2}-(s+1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk})X-\omega^{ak}
.
Therefore
\#I_{3}(a,b)
is exactly the number of parameters
s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for which this polynomial is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Since
m_{x}(0)=-\omega^{ak}\neq 0
and
m_{x}(-1)=-\omega^{bk}\neq 0
, this polynomial is reducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if it has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,-1\}
. For
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,-1\}
, substituting
x
into the above polynomial gives

x^{3}-sx^{2}-(s+1+\omega^{ak}-\omega^{bk})x-\omega^{ak}=0
.
Since
x^{2}+x=x(x+1)\neq 0
, this equation is equivalent to

s=x-1-\frac{\omega^{ak}}{x}+\frac{\omega^{bk}}{x+1}=\phi_{a,b}(x)
.
Therefore the above polynomial is reducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
s\in\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{a,b})
, and hence
\#I_{3}(a,b)=q-\#\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{a,b})
.

Since
|\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\{0,-1\}|=q-2
, we have
\#\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{a,b})\leq q-2
, and therefore
\#I_{3}(a,b)\geq 2
. On the other hand, for each
s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the fiber
\phi_{a,b}^{-1}(s)
consists of roots of the above cubic polynomial, and hence it has a size of at most
3
. Therefore
q-2\leq 3\#\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{a,b})
, and thus
\#I_{3}(a,b)\leq q-\lceil\frac{q-2}{3}\rceil=\lfloor\frac{2q+2}{3}\rfloor
.
∎
