Pharmacophore Directed Retrosynthesis Applied to Waixenicin A: Synthesis and Bioactivity of Derivatives Bearing a Minimal Proposed Pharmacophore
Authors: Matthew R. Parris, Sayuri Suzuki, Sigrid A. Kiledal, Xi B. Gong, Xinyang Zhang, Reinhold Penner, Hong-Shuo Sun, Zhong-Ping Feng, F. David Horgen, Andrea Fleig, Kenneth G. Hull, Daniel Romo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Waixenicin A (WaixA) is a marine natural product that has emerged as a potential drug lead for the treatment of hypoxic ischemic brain injury. WaixA specifically blocks the divalent ion channel transient receptor potential melastatin 7 (TRPM7), preventing the influx of calcium into the cell and triggering apoptotic pathways. We applied pharmacophore-directed retrosynthesis to this natural product, leading us to prepare a series of highly simplified derivatives bearing our proposed minimal pharmacophore, namely, the pyranyl vinylogous acetal. The synthesis of these derivatives and their ability to inhibit TRPM7 is described. Inhibition in the 9–57% range was found for these simplified structures in patch-clamp experiments; however, the ability of these highly simplified derivatives to exhibit inhibition provides information regarding minimal structural requirements for bioactivity.

Waixenicin A (WaixA), a marine xenicane diterpene isolated by Scheuer and Clardy from the octocoral Sarcothelia edmondsoni, shows highly selective and potent inhibition of the divalent ion channel transient receptor potential melastatin 7 (TRPM7). , These inhibitory effects could be significant due to the role that TRPM7 plays in cell death during an ischemic event, leading to hypoxic ischemic brain injury (HIBI) in newborns. The expression of TRPM7 is upregulated after this insult, and this ion channel plays a key role in the influx of Ca2+, which ultimately leads to neuronal death. , WaixA potently inhibits TRPM7, thus preventing calcium influx and neuronal damage and therefore providing a potential treatment for HIBI. Previous synthetic studies of members of this class of natural products include a recent elegant total synthesis of WaixA and its congeners by Magauer and underscores continued interest in this natural product. ,

We previously reported preliminary structure–activity relationships (SARs) comparing relative TRPM7 inhibition of several waixenicin A congeners (Figure a, IC50's from a Mn­(II) fura assay). We determined that dihydropyran containing the vinylogous acetal is crucial for the observed potency, as seen in WaixA (1) and WaixE (3). In contrast, compounds such as 12-deacetylwaixA (2) lacking a good leaving group at C12 exhibited a significant drop in TRPM7 inhibition. Structural and conformational changes in the trans-fused cyclononene ring, such as that found in WaixE, led to no appreciable changes in the potency. These observations led us to propose the dihydropyran bearing the acetylated vinylogous acetoxy acetal (highlighted in red) as the minimal pharmacophore, that is, the minimal structural feature required for TRPM7 inhibition.

As previously described, structural features of WaixA and the dramatic drop in potency of 12-deacetylwaixenicin A (2) absent a good leaving group at C12 suggested a possible covalent mechanism of action supported by irreversible binding of WaixA to TRPM7 (Figure b). Loss of the C-12 acetoxy group, potentially mediated by the Mg2+ ion, which is required for inhibition of TRPM7 by WaixA and also physiologically relevant, may lead to a reactive oxocarbenium ion 5 (Figure b). This intermediate could be captured by nucleophilic amino acid residues in this ion channel at either the C-12 or C-3 positions, leading to the observed irreversible inhibition verified by attempted wash out experiments. Previously, DFT calculations using natural bond orbital analysis (NBO) also supported the generation of such an oxocarbenium as an interaction between the lone pair of the dihydropyran oxygen, and the π system of the dihydropyran and antibonding orbital (nO – πC=C → σ*C‑OAc) of the C-12 acetoxy group was revealed (Figure c). This “anomeric-like” interaction extends through the dihydropyran π-system leading to a vinylogous hyperconjugation effect that controls the conformation of the C4a-12 σ-bond and places the acetoxy group orthogonal to the π-system as observed by DFT calculations (only one rotamer shown). This low energy conformation in conjunction with Mg2+, which may serve as a Lewis acid, is required for inhibition and reflects physiological conditions, which may lead to a stabilized oxocarbenium. This forms our current working mechanistic hypothesis for covalent modification of TRPM7 by WaixA; however, alternative mechanisms may be operative as suggested by the current study (vide infra).

Our group has developed pharmacophore-directed retrosynthesis (PDR) to more closely marry total synthesis with the structure–activity relationship (SAR) and mode of action studies of natural products (Figure a). , This type of retrosynthetic strategy was previously applied to gracilin A, ophiobolin A, and rameswaralide, expanding the SAR of these natural product families and leading to identification of bioactivities for simplified synthetic intermediates. This approach utilizes available and often minimal structure–activity relationship (SAR) data to develop a proposed pharmacophore that in turn guides our retrosynthetic analysis. We then sought to synthesize increasingly complex intermediates, all possessing the proposed pharmacophore, en route to the natural product (Figure a). Herein, we describe application of pharmacophore-directed retrosynthesis (PDR) to WaixA leading to the synthesis of simplified derivatives 8 bearing a minimal proposed pharmacophore, namely, the vinylogous acetoxy acetal tetrahydropyran moiety (Figure b, red highlighted substructure). We initially targeted modified side-chain derivatives 9 derived from hydrocinnamaldehyde as a model study that served two purposes: (i) development of a synthetic strategy for the labile vinylogous acetoxy acetal and (ii) access to highly simplified WaixA derivatives bearing the proposed pharmacophore but with a phenethyl side chain (Figure b). Patch clamp experiments demonstrated the ability of these highly simplified derivatives to inhibit TRPM7.

Our initial strategy targeted monocyclic dihydropyrans possessing the desired oxidation state of WaixA at C1 and a pendant alcohol that could be elaborated to various side-chains (Scheme ). Dihydropyran 11 was accessed using an inverse electron-demand hetero Diels–Alder reaction developed by Danishefsky using aldehyde 10 and ethyl vinyl ether catalyzed by Yb­(OTf)3 , (36% yield). Deprotection provided the allylic alcohol S1 and acetylation provided the targeted primary allylic acetate 12 in 49% (2 steps). Attempts to convert the C-1 ethoxy group to the desired acetoxy group led primarily to hydrolysis under a variety of conditions. Toward developing conditions for annulation of the side-chain, silyl ether 11 was again deprotected and the resulting allylic alcohol was oxidized to the corresponding aldehyde 13. Grignard addition gave diastereomeric alcohols 14 in 51% yield (dr, 1:1). Slight heating was required (35 °C) for the Grignard addition to proceed likely due to the lowered electrophilicity of the vinylogous formate 13. Attempted acetylation of the secondary alcohol led to facile ring cleavage under several conditions leading to the corresponding unsaturated aldehyde acetal 18 as the only isolable product. This instability may be attributed to the more readily formed oxocarbenium that could be generated following acetylation of the secondary alcohol 14 compared to that generated from the primary alcohol 12. Upon protonation of the allylic acetate 15 under even mild acidic conditions, the oxocarbenium 16 is presumably generated, which can then fragment to the ethoxy oxocarbenium intermediate 17. The addition of acetate ion gives the observed mixed acetal 18.

Initial Hetero-Diels-Alder Strategy Toward Highly Simplified Vinylogous Acetoxy Acetals

Due to the observed instability once the vinylogous acetoxy acetal is installed, we shifted our strategy to installing the unsaturation in the dihydropyran as a final step. To further the potential for the observed tetrahydropyran ring cleavage, we first targeted derivatives devoid of C1 oxidation as this might provide SAR information for the requirement of this anomeric carbon and provide insights into the relative reactivity of the isolated vinylogous acetoxy acetate. Synthesis began with an aldol reaction with δ-valerolactone and hydrocinnamaldehyde to furnish β-hydroxy lactone 19 in 60% (dr, ∼6:1). The major anti-diastereomer shown is premised on a Zimmerman-Traxler transition state as observed with previously described aldol reactions with valerolactone. Acetylation enabled further enrichment of a single diastereomer 20 (85% yield, dr 13:1). Chemoselective reduction of the lactone using LiBEt3H gave diastereomeric lactols 21a accompanied by what appeared to be a transacetylated byproduct that following acetylation converges to the same bis-acetate 21b (see SI for further details). The known chemoselectivity for the lactone over the ester relies on differences in stereoelectronic effects. , Recrystallization of the intermediate lactol 21a by slow evaporation and X-ray crystallography (thermal ellipsoids shown at the 50% probability level) gave a single diastereomeric lactol enabling assignment of the relative stereochemistry of the major diastereomeric aldol adduct (Scheme , inset). The lactols 21a, accompanied by transacetylated products, were typically not isolated but subjected directly to acetylation converging to the bis-acetoxy pyran 21b (66%, 2 steps, dr >19:1), providing an additional derivative for patch clamp experiments. Conversion of the same mixture of diastereomeric lactols 21a, again accompanied by transacetylation products, to the targeted vinylogous acetoxy acetal dihydropyran 9 was accomplished through mesylation/elimination. The instability of this vinylogous acetoxy acetal made purification challenging. Rapid conversion to hydroxy aldehyde 22 occurred under acidic (silica gel) and Lewis acidic (basic alumina) conditions and also when stored on the benchtop exposed to air for >6 h. Thus, purification could only be accomplished by flushing through a short pad of neutral Florisil with Et2O to achieve ∼95% purity (1H NMR, C6D6). For storage, the compound was frozen in benzene and stored under argon at −20 °C, allowing storage with no appreciable hydrolysis for up to ∼8 months.

Synthesis of a Model Vinylogous Acetoxy Acetal Using a Chemoselective Reduction/Elimination Sequence

We next targeted the synthesis of a dihydropyran vinyloogous acetal bearing the side chain found in WaixA (Scheme ). Starting from δ-valerolactone, an aldol reaction with the requisite aldehyde 23 provided the desired β-hydroxy lactones 24/24′ (dr ∼ 10:1), and following purification, the major anti diastereomer anti-24 was isolated in 32% yield (dr >19:1) along with ∼4% of the diastereomer syn-24. Desilylation and acetylation gave the acetate 25 (76%, dr >19:1). Chemoselective reduction was again possible as observed in the model system, demonstrating the highly chemoselective nature of LiEt3BH for lactone reduction over a secondary and a primary acetate. Direct mesylation/elimination gave the targeted vinylogous acetoxy acetal 8 (48%, 2 steps). Similar purification and storage procedures required for the model system were again required for this rather unstable vinylogous acetoxy acetal 8. A tris-acetoxy derivative 26 was also accessed for assaying through reduction and acetylation to deliver triacetate 26 (66%, 2 steps, dr ∼ 1.5:1).

Synthesis of a Simplified WaixA Derivative Bearing the Proposed Pharmacophore and the WaixA Side-Chain

Finally, we studied a second strategy to install C1 oxidation at the anomeric position (Scheme ). This strategy would also rely on a late-stage introduction of the ring unsaturation to minimize instability observed previously. The synthetic route began with Rubottom oxidation of cyclopentanone and in situ acetylation to afford the known α-acetoxy cyclopentanone 27 (59%, 3 steps). Lead tetraacetate also proved to be a viable, more direct route to access acetoxy lactone 28; however, concerns with toxicity upon scale up and diminished yields precluded this strategy. Cyclopentanone 27 was then subjected to Baeyer–Villiger conditions with m-CPBA to regioselectively afford δ-acetoxy lactone 28 (55%). An aldol reaction with hydrocinnamaldehyde followed by direct acetylation gave bis-acetoxy lactone 29 (56%, 2 steps) as an inseparable mixture of diastereomers (dr 7:1). Reduction of lactone 29 with LiAlH4 and immediate in situ trapping with a solution of Ac2O, DMAP, and pyridine in toluene gave the tris-acetoxy derivative 30 in low yield (20%, 2 steps, dr >19:1). However, several attempts (not shown) to capture the intermediate lactol, including with mesyl chloride, led only to ring-opened aldehydes as evidenced by crude 1H NMR. Treatment with LiBEt3H only resulted in over reduction to afford the previously characterized lactol 21a (42%, dr 1:1; see Scheme ).

The simplified WaixA derivatives were tested for their inhibition of TRPM7 using patch-clamp recordings with HEK293 cells (an immortalized cell line derived from human embryonic kidney cells) overexpressing TRPM7 (Figure ). In all experiments, the pipette’s intracellular solution contained physiological free Mg2+ at 780 μM but lacked added ATP to induce rapid activation of TRPM7 currents. Derivatives were applied extracellularly at 10 μM concentration at t = 200 to 600 s (black bar in Figure left panel). Average TRPM7-mediated outward currents at +80 mV were extracted from membrane currents evoked by voltage ramps (−100 to +100 mV) delivered at 0.5 Hz and plotted as a function of time (n = 3–4). The data points were normalized to the time point of application of WaixA derivatives (at 200 s), and then, the traces were normalized to the data point of the control at 600 s. As a control, we used EtOH only, and as a positive control, we used WaixA (10 μM) applied at 200–600 s. Inhibition (%) of TRPM7 outward currents by derivatives versus control was assessed at 600 s and is shown in the table in Figure . Generally, all derivatives showed modest inhibitory activity on TRPM7 current (9–57% inhibition); however, it is worth noting that the most potent compound was a derivative with the same oxidation state found in our proposed pharmacophore. Surprisingly, this derivative bears a phenethyl side-chain devoid of the vinylogous acetoxy acetal, i.e., bis-acetate 30. A related bis-acetate derivative 21b also showed similar moderate potency (46.3 ± 12.8%). Derivatives possessing a vinylogous acetoxy acetal or identical oxidation state, i.e., 8, 9, 26, and 12, also showed measurable inhibitory activity (∼27–42%). As a positive control, WaixA nearly completely inhibited TRPM7 (95.2 ± 1.6%) within 600 s. While the observed potencies must be considered alongside solubility and lipophilicity impacting cell permeability and aqueous stability, it is noteworthy that these highly simplified WaixA derivatives, in particular those possessing related oxidation states as the pyranyl acetate bearing a vinylogous acetoxy acetal as that found in WaixA, show moderate potency. This SAR information may point to the potential of ring opening events leading to intermediate bis-aldehydes or unsaturated aldehydes as observed in the formation xeniafarsunol A from (+)-9-deacetoxy-14,15-deepoxyxeniculin by Magauer, which may be the actual inhibitors of TRPM7. Of course, these small electrophilic compounds could be promiscuous in their activity beyond TRPM7.

In summary, through application of a pharmacophore-directed retrosynthetic strategy, we prepared highly simplified vinylogous acetoxy acetals found in WaixA involving lactone reduction followed by mesylation and elimination. The vinylogous acetoxy acetals were found to be highly sensitive to acid, require mild conditions for isolation, and tolerate only rapid purification through Florisil. These simplified WaixA derivatives exhibited moderate (9–53%) inhibitory activity of TRPM7 in patch clamp experiments with the greatest inhibition interestingly seen with derivatives bearing a similar oxidation pattern to the natural product, e.g., lactol 30. The most potent derivatives bear masked mono- or dialdehydes, which suggests the interesting possibility that WaixA and derivatives serve as masked aldehydes that react covalently with TRPM7 nucleophilic residues. To date, accessing a highly simplified dihydropyran bearing a C1 acetoxy group has proven difficult due to the instability of these monocyclic derivatives. Further efforts including late-stage oxidations and elaboration to 6,9-bicyclic derivatives more closely resembling WaixA are currently being explored.
