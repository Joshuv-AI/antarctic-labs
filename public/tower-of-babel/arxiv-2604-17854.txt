Semiclassical resonances under local magnetic fields
Pavel Exner, Ayman Kachmar

Abstract
. We study resonances for the semiclassical magnetic Laplacian in the full plane with a compactly supported magnetic field in the framework of semiclassical complex scaling and black box scattering theory. Assuming that the magnetic field is locally constant, we prove the existence of semiclassical resonances near the Landau levels with exponentially small imaginary parts. We also prove that resonances emerge from a magnetic step discontinuity along a curved interface or a non-degenerate magnetic well, and in the vicinity of anharmonic Landau levels if the field has an isolated zero.



1.
Introduction

The behavior of a quantum system and its classical counterpart is sometimes substantially different. As a simple example, consider a charged particle in two dimensions exposed to a constant magnetic field supported in a disk. Outside it the trajectory is a straight line; if it hits the disk in a non tangential way, it leaves again and the time spent in the disk is shorter the stronger the field is. Our aim is to show that, in contrast, the quantum particle can be trapped temporarily in the field support and the lifetime of such a quasi-stationary state increases exponentially with the field strength. The effect is of a semiclassical nature and occurs for a wide range of local magnetic field configurations.

1.1.
Setting and main question

We investigate the existence of resonances for the semiclassical magnetic Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(1.1)

P(h)=(-ih\nabla-\mathbf{A})^{2}

under the following hypotheses on the vector potential

:

(1)

\mathbf{A}\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
;

(2)

There exists

\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B(0,R_{0})

(0,0)

\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}
is the Aharonov-Bohm potential with a flux

(1.2)

\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}(x)=\Bigl(-\frac{\alpha x_{2}}{|x|^{2}},\frac{\alpha x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\Bigr).

Our hypotheses ensure that the magnetic field

. Note that by Stokes’ theorem, the flux

\alpha=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}B(x)dx.

As we have indicated, the motivation for our study of

arises from the question whether a strong local magnetic field can create long-living resonances. Considering the operator
(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A})^{2}
with a large parameter

1.2.
On the definition of the operator

We now turn to the precise definition of the operator
P(h)
starting from the quadratic form

Q_{\mathbf{A},h}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(-i\nabla-\mathbf{A})u|^{2}\,dx,\quad u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

which is non-negative and closable. Its Friedrichs extension yields a self-adjoint operator (our operator
P(h))
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with domain

\mathcal{D}=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\colon(-ih\nabla-\mathbf{A})u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2}),(-ih\nabla-\mathbf{A})^{2}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\},

where

(-ih\nabla-\mathbf{A})^{2}=-h^{2}\Delta-2ih\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla-ih(\Div\mathbf{A})+|\mathbf{A}|^{2}.

The vector potential
\mathbf{A}
is divergence free outside
B(0,R_{0})
and decays at infinity like
1/|x|
. The domain of
P(h)
is then independent of
h
and is given by

(1.4)

\mathcal{D}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\colon\Delta u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Since the magnetic field is compactly supported and
P(h)\geq 0
in the sense of quadratic forms, the spectrum of
P(h)
is
[0,+\infty)
and is purely essential.

1.3.
Overview of results

The aim of this article is to study resonances of
P(h)
in the semiclassical limit
h\to 0
. Resonances are usually defined as poles of the meromorphically continued resolvent
(P(h)-z)^{-1}
. We define them via complex scaling, specifically as eigenvalues of a Fredholm operator obtained from
P(h)
through an analytic dilation of the coordinates in the region exterior to the magnetic field (see Section
2
and Definition
2.6
). This approach allows us to transform the resonance problem into a spectral problem for a non-selfadjoint operator, whose discrete spectrum in a suitable complex sector corresponds precisely to the resonances of the original Hamiltonian.

In this work, we demonstrate that a local magnetic field of described type creates resonances in the semiclassical/strong field regime. We investigate five distinct scenarios and prove the following:

•

Constant fields:
If the magnetic field is locally constant, then there are resonances whose real parts are close to the Landau levels, and whose imaginary parts are negative and exponentially small (see Theorem
1.1
below).

•

Isolated zeros of a local magnetic field:
If the magnetic field vanishes at a point, without loss of generality identified with the origin, and behaves locally like
r^{\gamma}
, where
r
is a radial variable and
\gamma
is a positive parameter, then then the local harmonic potential of the previous case is replaced by an anharmonic one, the eigenvalues of which we call anharmonic Landau levels. For such fields, there are resonances whose real parts are close to these levels, and whose imaginary parts are negative and exponentially small (see Theorem
1.2
below).

•

Magnetic wells:

If the magnetic field attains a nondegenerate and positive local minimum (a magnetic
well), then it gives rise to resonances with a semiclassical expansion in powers of h (see
Theorem
1.3
below).

•

Sharp magnetic interface:
If the magnetic
field is locally piecewise constant with a jump discontinuity across a curve modeling a sharp interface,
and if the curvature of the discontinuity has a local non-degenerate maximum, then there are resonances
which are asymptotically described by the field strength and the geometry of the interface (see Theorem
1.4
below).

•

Zero-field island:
If the magnetic field vanishes on an open set
\omega
(a hole) and is positive in a neighborhood of
\partial\omega
, then there are resonances whose real parts are close to
h^{2}
times the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\omega
(see Theorem
1.5
below).

The asymptotic expansions of the real parts of these resonances exhibit distinct scaling regimes in the semiclassical parameter

Resonances near Landau levels: Proof of Theorem

We now turn to the proof of Theorem

by constructing quasimodes supported near a region where

3.1.
Quasimodes

Consider the symmetric gauge
\mathbf{A}^{\rm sym}(x)=\frac{b}{2}(-x_{2},x_{1})
,
b>0
. The corresponding magnetic Laplacian is the Landau Hamiltonian:

H^{\text{L}}=\left(-i\partial_{x_{1}}+\frac{b}{2}x_{2}\right)^{2}+\left(-i\partial_{x_{2}}-\frac{b}{2}x_{1}\right)^{2},

which can be expressed in polar coordinates
(r,\varphi)
as

H^{\text{L}}=-\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\partial r+\left(-\frac{i}{r}\partial_{\varphi}-\frac{br}{2}\right)^{2}.

Its eigenvalues are

\Lambda_{n}(b)=(2n+1)b,\quad n=0,1,2,\dots,

with eigenfunctions

\psi_{n,m}(r,\varphi)=C_{n,m}\,r^{|m|}e^{im\varphi}e^{-br^{2}/4}L_{n}^{|m|}\left(\frac{br^{2}}{2}\right),\quad m\in\mathbb{Z},

where
L_{n}^{|m|}
are associated Laguerre polynomials, and
C_{n,m}\sim b^{(|m|+1)/2}
is a constant to ensure that
\psi_{n,m}
is normalized in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Consider
r_{0}>0
. For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a smooth cutoff satisfying:

•

\chi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq(1-\delta)r_{0}
,

•

\chi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq r_{0}
,

•

0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
|\nabla\chi|\leq C/(\delta r_{0})
,
|\Delta\chi|\leq C/(\delta r_{0})^{2}
.

Define the quasimode

u_{n,m}(x)=\chi(x)\psi_{n,m}(x).

Note that
u_{n,m}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with support in the disk
B(0,r_{0})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any fixed
n,m
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, the quasimode
u_{n,m}
satisfies

\begin{gathered}\|u_{n,m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1+\mathcal{O}\big(b^{|m|+1}e^{-(1-\delta)^{2}r_{0}^{2}b/2}\big),\\
\|(H^{\rm L}-\Lambda_{n}(b))u_{n,m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\mathcal{O}\big(b^{|m|+2}e^{-(1-\delta)^{2}r_{0}^{2}b/4}\big).\end{gathered}

Proof.

Note that the full-space Landau eigenfunctions
\psi_{n,m}
are normalized,

\|\psi_{n,m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1
.
Since
\chi\equiv 1
for
|x|\leq(1-\delta)r_{0}
and
0\leq\chi\leq 1
elsewhere, we have

\|u_{n,m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi^{2}|\psi_{n,m}|^{2}\,dx=1-\int_{|x|>(1-\delta)r_{0}}(1-\chi^{2})|\psi_{n,m}|^{2}\,dx.

The error term is bounded by the tail of the eigenfunction outside
|x|>(1-\delta)r_{0}
,

1-\|u_{n,m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\int_{|x|>(1-\delta)r_{0}}|\psi_{n,m}|^{2}\,dx.

For large
r
, the asymptotic behavior of
|\psi_{n,m}|^{2}
is dominated by the Gaussian factor
e^{-br^{2}/2}
, with a polynomial prefactor
r^{2|m|+4n}
.
Thus the radial tail integral is proportional to

\int_{(1-\delta)r_{0}}^{\infty}r^{2|m|+4n+1}e^{-br^{2}/2}\,dr=2^{|m|+2n}b^{-(|m|+2n+1)}\int_{t_{\delta}}^{\infty}t^{|m|+2n}e^{-t}\,dt,

where
t_{\delta}=(1-\delta)^{2}r_{0}^{2}b/2
.
For large
b
, the incomplete gamma function in the above expression
behaves as
\int_{t_{\delta}}^{\infty}t^{p}e^{-t}dt\sim t_{\delta}^{p}e^{-t_{\delta}}
, so the tail integral is
\mathcal{O}\big(e^{-(1-\delta)^{2}r_{0}^{2}b/2}\big)
.
Hence
\|u_{n,m}\|^{2}=1+\mathcal{O}(C_{n,m}^{2}e^{-(1-\delta)^{2}r_{0}^{2}b/2})
, which yields the first estimate in the lemma.

Using that
H^{\text{L}}\psi_{n,m}=\Lambda_{n}(b)\psi_{n,m}
and
u_{n,m}=\chi\psi_{n,m}
, we obtain

(H^{\text{L}}-\Lambda_{n}(b))u_{n,m}=[H^{\text{L}},\chi]\psi_{n,m}.

Since
\chi
commutes with multiplication, the commutator expands to:

[H^{\text{L}},\chi]=-(\Delta\chi)-2(\nabla\chi)\cdot\nabla-2i\mathbf{A}\cdot(\nabla\chi).

Thus,

(H^{\text{L}}-\Lambda_{n}(b))u_{n,m}(x)=f(x)\psi_{n,m}(x)+g(x)\cdot\nabla\psi_{n,m}(x),

with
f,g
supported in the annulus
(1-\delta)r_{0}\leq|x|\leq r_{0}
and bounded by constants depending on
\delta
(but independent of
n,m
and
b
). Consequently, we have

\|(H^{\text{L}}-\Lambda_{n}(b))u_{n,m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq C_{\delta}\int_{(1-\delta)r_{0}\leq|x|\leq r_{0}}(|\psi_{n,m}|^{2}+|\nabla\psi_{n,m}|^{2})\,dx\\
=\mathcal{O}(C_{n,m}^{2}b^{2}e^{-(1-\delta)^{2}r_{0}^{2}b/2}),

thereby establishing the second estimate in the lemma.
∎

where

E_{n}(h)=(2n+1)h\in(E_{0}-C_{0}h,E_{0}+C_{0}h)\quad\mbox{with }E_{0}=0\mbox{ and }C_{0}=2n+2.

For distinct indices
n\neq m
, we have

|E_{n}(h)-E_{m}(h)|=2|n-m|h\geq 2h.

We now apply Theorem
2.8
of Tang and Zworski with
\mathcal{E}_{n}(h)=E_{n}(h)
and

•

The error bound
R(h)=Ce^{-\alpha r_{0}^{2}/h}
,

•

S(h)=e^{-cr_{0}^{2}/h}
where
0<c<\alpha
,

•

w(h)=h^{-2}e^{-cr_{0}^{2}/2h}
.

We conclude that there exists at least one resonance of

in

[E_{n}(h)-w(h),E_{n}(h)+w(h)]+i[-h^{-3}S(h),0].

Since
[0,+\infty)
is the spectrum of

, a resonance cannot be real.
∎

4.
Resonances near anharmonic Landau levels: Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
1.1
and relies on the construction of quasi-modes with exponentially small remainder. These quasi-modes are related to the eigenfunctions of the the anharmonic Landau Hamiltonian. Thus, we begin by presenting some spectral properties of this hamiltonian.

4.1.
The anharmonic Landau Hamiltonian

Let
\gamma>0
and
\mathbf{A}^{\rm ah}=\frac{|x|^{\gamma}}{2+\gamma}(-x_{2},x_{1})
. Consider the operator
P^{\rm ah}(b)=(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm ah})^{2}
, with
b>0
. Setting its domain to be

\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\colon(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm ah})u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2}),(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm ah})^{2}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\},

the operator
P^{\rm ah}(b)
is self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, thanks to Friedrichs
theorem.

Due to the scaling
x\mapsto b^{\frac{1}{2+\gamma}}x
, we reduce to case
b=1
and obtain the spectrum of
P^{\rm ah}(b)
as

\sigma(P^{\rm ah}(b))=b^{\frac{2}{2+\gamma}}\sigma(P^{\rm ah})).

Moreover, if
\psi(x;b)
is a normalized eigenfunction of
P^{\rm ah}(b)
, then it can be expressed via the normalized eigenfunction of
P^{\rm ah}
as follows

\psi(x;b)=b^{\frac{1}{2+\gamma}}\psi(b^{\frac{1}{2+\gamma}}x;1).

We next prove that the operator
P^{\rm ah}
has a compact resolvent.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
R=(P^{\rm ah}+1)^{-1}
. The operator
R:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is compact.

Proof.

Note that
B^{\rm ah}=\Curl\mathbf{A}^{\rm ah}=|x|^{\gamma}
. The space

X=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\colon|u|\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),~|x|^{\gamma/2}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\},

with the norm
\|u\|_{X}=\|\nabla|u|\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\||x|^{\gamma/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, is compactly embedded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Using the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(-i\nabla-\mathbf{A})u|^{2}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B^{\rm ah}|u|^{2}\,dx\quad(u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),

and the diamagnetic inequality
|\,\nabla|u|\,|\leq|(-i\nabla-\mathbf{A}^{\rm ah})u|
, we get that
R:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to X
is continuous.
∎

We denote the sequence of strictly increasing eigenvalues of
P^{\rm ah}
by
(\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma})_{n\geq 0}
, and by the scaling law, the
n
’th eigenvalue of
P^{\rm ah}(b)
as

\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}(b)=b^{\frac{2}{2+\gamma}}\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}.

We now turn to the decay of eigenfunctions, which we express in polar coordinates
(r,\varphi)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. There exists an integer
m
and a radial function
f
such that, for all
b>0,
the function

\psi_{n}(x;b)=b^{\frac{1}{2+\gamma}}e^{im\varphi}f(b^{\frac{1}{2+\gamma}}r),

is a normalized eigenfunction of
P^{\rm ah}(b)
with eigenvalue
\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}(b)
. Furthermore, there exist positive constant
c_{0},M
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Bigl(|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}+|f(r)|^{2}\Bigr)e^{2c_{0}r^{2+\gamma}}rdr\leq M.

Proof.

By exploiting the rotational symmetry, the operator
P^{\rm ah}(b)
is unitarily equivalent to the direct sum of the operators

\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{h}_{m}(b)\quad\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},rdr)\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

where

\mathfrak{h}_{m}(b)=-\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}+\Bigl(\frac{m}{r}-\frac{br^{1+\gamma}}{2+\gamma}\Bigr)^{2}.

By the scaling
r\mapsto b^{\frac{1}{2+\gamma}}r
, we reduce the task to the case
b=1
. Choose
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}
is an eigenvalue of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}(1)
. Then, a corresponding eigenfunction of
P^{\rm ah}
is

\psi_{n}(x;1)=e^{im\varphi}f(r)

where
f
is an eigenfunction of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}(1)
, normalized in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},rdr)
. By the scaling law, we obtain the eigenfunction
\psi_{n}(x;b)
of
P^{\rm ah}(b)
.

We establish the decay of
f
at infinity by using the method of Agmon. Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Starting from the identity,

\mathfrak{h}_{m}(1)f=\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}f,

and taking the inner product with
e^{2c_{0}\chi}f
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},rdr)
, we get by integration by parts,

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\bigl(f^{\prime}(r)(e^{2c_{0}\chi}f(r))^{\prime}+V_{m}(r)|e^{c_{0}\chi(r)}f(r)|^{2}\bigr)rdr=\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|e^{c_{0}\chi(r)}f(r)|^{2}rdr,

where

V_{m}(r)=\Bigl(\frac{m}{r}-\frac{r^{1+\gamma}}{2+\gamma}\Bigr)^{2}.

Noticing that

f^{\prime}(e^{2c_{0}\chi}f)^{\prime}=[(e^{c_{0}\chi}f)^{\prime}]^{2}-c_{0}^{2}(\chi^{\prime})^{2}(e^{c_{0}\chi}f)^{2},

we get eventually

(4.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Bigl(|(e^{c_{0}\chi(r)}f(r))^{\prime}|^{2}+\bigl(V_{m}(r)-\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}-c_{0}^{2}|\chi^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\bigr)|e^{c_{0}\chi(r)}f(r)|^{2}\Bigr)rdr=0,

By density, (
4.1
) holds for
\chi\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. For a given integer
N\geq 2
, we choose
\chi=\chi_{N}
where

\chi_{N}(r)=\begin{cases}r^{2+\gamma}&\mbox{if }0\leq r<N,\\
(2+\gamma)N^{1+\gamma}(r_{N}-r)&\mbox{if }N\leq r\leq r_{N}:=\frac{3+\gamma}{2+\gamma}N,\\
0&\mbox{if }r\geq r_{N}.\end{cases}

Note that, for all
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have
|\chi^{\prime}_{N}(r)|\leq(2+\gamma)r^{1+\gamma}
and

V_{m}(r)\geq\frac{r^{2+2\gamma}}{(2+\gamma)^{2}}-\frac{2m}{2+\gamma}r^{\gamma}.

Choose
R_{0}>1
sufficiently large and
c_{0}
sufficiently small such that

V_{m}(r)-\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}-c_{0}^{2}(2+\gamma)^{2}r^{2+2\gamma}\geq 1,\quad\forall\,r\geq R_{0}.

Going back to (
4.1
) and splitting the integral on the intervals
(0,R_{0})
and
(R_{0},+\infty)
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{R_{0}}^{+\infty}\Bigl(|(e^{c_{0}\chi(r)}f(r))^{\prime}|^{2}+\bigl(V_{m}(r)-\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}-c_{0}^{2}|\chi^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\bigr)|e^{c_{0}\chi(r)}f(r)|^{2}\Bigr)rdr\\
\leq\int_{0}^{R_{0}}(\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}+c_{0}^{2}|\chi^{\prime}(r)|^{2})|e^{c_{0}\chi(r)}f(r)|^{2}rdr.\end{split}

Thus, using the last inequality together with (
4.1
) for
c_{0}=0
and the fact that
V_{m}\geq 0
, we conclude that

\int_{0}^{N}\Bigl(|(e^{c_{0}\chi_{N}(r)}f(r))^{\prime}|^{2}+|e^{c_{0}\chi_{N}(r)}f(r)|^{2}\Bigr)rdr\leq M_{0}e^{2c_{0}R_{0}^{2+\gamma}}\int_{0}^{R_{0}}|f(r)|^{2}rdr,

with

M_{0}=2\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}+c_{0}^{2}\|\chi_{N}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,R_{0})}^{2}=2\Lambda_{n}^{\gamma}+(2+\gamma)^{2}c_{0}^{2}R_{0}^{2+2\gamma},

holds for all
N>R_{0}
; to finish the proof, we take
N\to+\infty
and conclude by monotone convergence.
∎

4.2.
Quasi-modes and existence of resonances

Following the construction in Section
3.1
, we define quasimodes for the anharmonic Landau Hamiltonian
P^{\rm ah}(b)
. For a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
\psi_{n}(x;b)
be the normalized eigenfunction from Proposition
4.2
.

Consider
r_{0}>0
and a smooth cutoff
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\chi(x)=1
for

and
\chi(x)=0
for

. We define the quasimode:

Lemma 4.3
.

For fixed

and
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exist positive constants

such that, for all

, the quasimode

satisfies:

\begin{gathered}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1+\mathcal{O}\big(e^{-c_{1}b}\big),\\

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as Lemma
3.1
. The error terms are controlled by the tail of the eigenfunction

. By Proposition

, the eigenfunction decays as

. Integrating this tail over

yields the exponential smallness in

. The commutator

is supported in the annulus where

and its gradient are exponentially small, leading to the second estimate.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume
B=|x|^{\gamma}
on a disk

. By the gauge transformation

\int_{\hat{\omega}}\Bigl(|(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm isl})(e^{\Phi}\psi_{n})|^{2}-\bigl(\epsilon_{0}^{2}b|\nabla t_{\omega}|^{2}+\hat{\ell}_{n}(b)\bigr)|e^{\Phi}\psi_{n}|^{2}\Bigr)dx=0.

Since the magnetic field is constant in
\tilde{\omega}\setminus\omega
, the lowest eigenvalue of
(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm isl})^{2}
in
\hat{\omega}\setminus\omega
behaves asymptotically like
\Theta_{0}b
(see
[
8
, Theorem 8.11]
), where
\Theta_{0}>1/2
is a constant (called the de Gennes constant). Thus, we have by the min-max principle

\frac{b}{2}\int_{\hat{\omega}\setminus\omega}|e^{\Phi}\psi_{n}|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\hat{\omega}\setminus\omega}|(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm isl})(e^{\Phi}\psi_{n})|^{2}dx.

In the set
\omega,

\Phi=0
and we control the kinetic energy by the eigenvalue
\hat{\ell}_{n}(b)
as follows

\int_{\omega}\Bigl(|(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm isl})(e^{\Phi}\psi_{n})|^{2}\leq\int_{\hat{\omega}}\Bigl(|(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm isl})\psi_{n}|^{2}\leq\hat{\ell}_{n}(b).

Returning back to (
7.1
) and using that
|\nabla t_{\omega}(x)|=\mathbf{1}_{\omega}(x)
a.e., we write after splitting the integral over
\omega
and
\hat{\omega}\setminus\omega
,

\int_{\hat{\omega}\setminus\omega}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}|(-i\nabla-b\mathbf{A}^{\rm isl})(e^{\Phi}\psi_{n})|^{2}+\bigl(\frac{b}{4}-\epsilon_{0}^{2}b-\hat{\ell}_{n}(b)\bigr)|e^{\Phi}\psi_{n}|^{2}\Bigr)dx\leq\frac{2}{b}\hat{\ell}_{n}(b).

To conclude, we choose
\epsilon_{0}=1/4
and
b_{0}
sufficiently large so that
\frac{1}{4}b-\hat{\ell}_{n}(b)\geq\frac{1}{8}b
.
∎

7.2.
Quasimodes

We now move to the construction of quasimodes using the eigenfunctions
\psi_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, of the auxiliary operator
\mathcal{L}(b)
. Choose
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};[0,1])
such that
\chi=1
in
\overline{\omega}
and
\mathrm{supp\,}\chi\subset\hat{\omega}
. Let
h=1/b
and

u_{n}(x)=\chi(x)\psi_{n}(x)\times(\mbox{gauge transformation}).

Then, by Proposition
7.1
,

\begin{gathered}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1+\mathcal{O}(e^{-c/h^{1/2})},\\
\|(P(h)-h^{2}\hat{\ell}_{n}(1/h))u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\mathcal{O}(e^{-c/h^{1/2}}),\end{gathered}

for some constant
c>0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Applying Theorem
2.8
with the quasimodes
(u_{n})
and the energy levels
(h^{2}\hat{\ell}_{n}(1/h)
, we obtain resonances

z_{n}\in[h^{2}\hat{\ell}_{n}(1/h)-h^{-2}e^{-c/2h^{1/2}}]+i[-h^{3}e^{-c/h^{1/2}},0].

To conclude, we use that
\hat{\ell}_{n}(1/h)\to\ell_{n}
as
h\to 0^{+}
.
∎
