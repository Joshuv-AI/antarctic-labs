Path convergence in diffusion models
Roi Holtzman, Roman Beauvallet, Werner Krauth

Abstract
We discuss diffusion-model paths interpolating between a target distribution known only through p p patterns and a reference distribution that can be sampled. These interpolating paths can be constructed symmetrically or else in forward direction (often referred to as a “noising”) from the target patterns to the reference distribution or in backward direction (as a “denoising”) from the reference distribution to the patterns. For backward paths with identical diffusion noise, we consider the path convergence in number of patterns p p towards the path for infinitely many patterns. In a one-dimensional test case, we show that this convergence is on a scale 1 / p 1/\sqrt{p} , but with infinite mean square deviation. We demonstrate that the path convergence allows for extrapolation towards the p = ∞ p=\infty path which samples the target distribution. We provide a proof-of-concept extrapolation algorithm and propose the convergence and extrapolation of paths as a possible strategy for density estimation and generalization. We illustrate all our algorithms through pseudo-codes and provide Python implementations.



Sampling from a probability distribution comprises a number of fundamental tasks
in statistics. The sampling from an explicit probability distribution (for
example, the Boltzmann distribution of classical physics or the density matrix
in quantum physics), has given rise, among others, to the huge field of Monte
Carlo methods
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. The
task of sampling from a probability distribution only known through
p
samples
is known as the generalization problem. For low-dimensional continuous
distributions, the task is related to the field of density
estimation
[
5
,
6
]
: From the
p
samples we may
estimate the probability distribution
\pi
(that is, estimate its density), and
then sample the estimate of
\pi
using Monte Carlo methods. In recent years,
diffusion models
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
have been successfully applied to the generalization problem
for high-dimensional “target” distributions. Diffusion models connect the

p
patterns of the target distribution to a reference distribution, typically a
Gaussian, through an interpolating path referred to as a “noising”. Then, a
sample from the reference distribution undergoes a backward process (referred to
as “denoising”), usually implemented by a neural network, which outputs a
sample of the approximate target distribution.

In this Letter, we analyze diffusive interpolation paths
\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{\beta}\}

between a target distribution
\pi^{\text{T}}
represented by
p
patterns
x_{0}^{\mu},\mu=1,\ldots,p
and a reference distribution
\pi^{\text{R}}
with samples
x_{\beta}\sim\pi^{\text{R}}
, in the language of statistical mechanics
[
11
]
and, in
particular, of path-integral Monte Carlo
[
12
,
4
]
. We construct
statistically identical paths either symmetrically (without any direction) or
else in forward direction from one of the patterns
x_{0}^{\mu}
into
\pi^{\text{R}}

(“noising”) or in backward direction from a sample
x_{\beta}\sim\pi^{\text{R}}
to
one of the patterns
x_{0}^{\mu}
(“denoising”). For a one-dimensional target
distribution under identical realizations of the diffusion noise, we find that
the backward paths converge on a scale of
1/\sqrt{p}
to the infinite-

(

) path which connects samples of the two distributions. The mean square
deviation of finite-
p
paths from the
p^{\infty}
path is infinite, but we compute
the median deviation to high precision. We finally discuss the subject of
extrapolation, which builds on the established convergence of backward paths. We
explicitly show that, for identical realizations of diffusion noise, backward
paths for independent sets of
p
and
q
patterns from
\pi^{\text{T}}
, together with
the backward path for the combined
p+q
patterns yield an extrapolated path
that, on average, is closer to the
p^{\infty}
path than the
p+q
path itself. In the
conclusion, we discuss the possible use of the convergence and the extrapolation
of paths in the context of density estimation and generalization. This Letter
presents a number of proof-of-concept algorithms in the form of pseudo-codes.
Python implementations are provided in an associated open-source
repository
[
13

To set the stage, we consider the partition function, i.e. the sum over all
probabilities, for the combined target and reference distributions:

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}{x_{0}\ }\pi^{\text{T}}(x_{0})\int\mathrm{d}{x_{\beta}\ }\pi^{\text{R}}(x_{\beta}).

(1)

As discussed,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\mu=1}^{p}\text{{1}}_{\mu}\int\mathrm{d}{x_{\beta}\ }\pi^{\text{R}}(x_{\beta})

(2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\mu=1}^{p}\int\mathrm{d}{x_{\beta}\ }\pi^{\text{R}}(x_{\beta})\frac{\rho\left(x_{0}^{\mu},x_{\beta},\beta\right)}{\rho\left(x_{0}^{\mu},x_{\beta},\beta\right)}

(3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\mu=1}^{p}\int\int\int\mathrm{d}{x_{1}\ }\mathrm{d}{x_{2}\ }\dots\mathrm{d}{x_{N}\ }\pi^{\text{R}}(x_{N})\times

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\times\!\!\frac{\rho\left(x_{0}^{\mu},x_{1},\Delta_{\tau}\right)\rho\left(x_{1},x_{2},\Delta_{\tau}\right)\dots\rho\left(x_{N-1},x_{N},\Delta_{\tau}\right)}{\rho\left(x_{0}^{\mu},x_{N},\beta\right)}.

(6)

The interpolation path

in eq. (

For the free particle in eq. (

1=\int\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime\prime}\ }\frac{\rho\left(x,x^{\prime\prime},\tau^{\prime}\right)\rho\left(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime},\tau-\tau^{\prime}\right)}{\rho(x,x^{\prime},\tau)}

\displaystyle\left\langle x^{\prime\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})/\tau+x^{\prime\prime}\tau^{\prime}/\tau,

(8)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tau^{\prime}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})/\tau}.

(9)

We consider paths

\{q\}

\underbrace{x^{\{p\}}_{\tau}\,\,\,\,\,\,<\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\Delta_{p}=|x^{\{p+q\}}_{\tau}-x^{\{p\}}_{\tau}|}\!\!\!\!\!\!x^{\{p+q\}}_{\tau}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\underbrace{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,<x^{\{q\}}_{\tau}}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\Delta_{q}=|x^{\{q\}}_{\tau}-x^{\{p+q\}}_{\tau}|}.

As there is an obvious relabelling
p\Leftrightarrow q

and

\Delta_{q}>\Delta_{p}

x^{\{p\}}_{\tau}

We may tip the balance between

\Delta_{q}
defined in eq. (

\Delta_{q}>\Delta_{p};\quad\Delta_{q}>\omega.

Then,

x^{\{\infty\}}_{\tau}
has higher probability

p^{+}(\omega)>\frac{1}{2}
to lie to the right of
x^{\{p+q\}}_{\tau}

In conclusion, we have discussed interpolation paths
between probability distributions in a setup patterned after that
of modern diffusion models. The latter generically use neural networks to learn
score functions
[
15
,
16
,
9
,
8
,
19
]
aimed at constructing
positions (corresponding to our
x^{\{p\}}_{\tau}
for
\tau\gtrsim 0
) that
approximately sample the target distribution
\pi^{\text{T}}

[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.
In this Letter, in contrast, we have restricted our attention
to paths that exactly interpolate between the reference
distribution
\pi^{\text{R}}
and the
p
patterns
\{x_{0}^{\mu}\}\sim\pi^{\text{T}}
. Discrete and
continuous paths were constructed without any approximation and without any of
the smoothings of patterns that are a staple in kernel-density
methods
[
6
]
yet problematic in high dimensions

[
23
,
24
]
.
For the backward construction, our observed convergence in
1/\sqrt{p}
under
identical diffusion noise is towards a path that reinstalls the symmetry between

\pi^{\text{T}}
and
\pi^{\text{R}}
and that samples both distributions and, in particular,
the target distribution
\pi^{\text{T}}
.
The observed convergence, which might be of interest in its own right,
can likely be understood rigorously and much expanded on. We have
found, for
example, that the median absolute deviation for different values of
\beta
is
a function of
\tau/\beta
for
\tau/\beta\lesssim 1
and that a rescaling of
patterns so that their empirical mean and variance match those
of
\pi^{\text{T}}
all but suppresses the deviation for that range of imaginary
times
[
18
]
. In both cases, the root mean square
deviation appears to be infinite on the scale
1/\sqrt{p}
. The convergence of
the continuous (
\Delta_{\tau}\to 0
) paths seems robust in higher dimensions, and it
would be interesting to understand whether the same property can be preserved
for a discrete-time construction.

By definition, our exact backward paths do not generalize at fixed
p
. In fact,
from an arbitrary starting position
x_{\beta}
, they end up at any of the
p

patterns with equal probability. Nevertheless, we have suggested that a given
set of
p+q
patterns may be subdivided into smaller sets in an attempt to
extrapolate paths that all converge towards the same
p^{\infty}
path. Our
proof-of-concept algorithm is naive: It considers a one-dimensional
target distribution, and only a single subdivision of

p+q
, a restrictive conditioning (see line “
*
” in
Alg.
5
) at a fixed value of
\tau
. However, we
improve on the approximation of
x^{\{\infty\}}_{\tau}
that is provided by

x^{\{p+q\}}_{\tau}
. That this is at all possible is our point of principle,
although the effect is, for the moment, quite small.
Much work remains to be done to understand whether the
extrapolation of paths might be useful for generalization and
density-estimation tasks and whether a more complete setting of multiple
subdivisions of
p+q
and of simultaneous extrapolations for all
\tau

again requires neural networks to handle the vast amount of data
that might arise.
