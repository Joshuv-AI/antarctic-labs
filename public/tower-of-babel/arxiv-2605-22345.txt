Boundary blowup solutions for the Finsler p-Laplacian: wellposedness and asymptotic behaviour
N N Dattatreya

Abstract
. We study the existence of large ( boundary blow-up ) solutions to semilinear equations involving the Finsler p-Laplacian on bounded domains with sufficiently smooth boundaries. We establish a Keller–Osserman -type condition that ensures the existence of such solutions, and show that this condition retains the same integrability as that of the p-Laplacian. We examine the influence of the anisotropic norm underlying the Finsler p-Laplacian on the boundary behaviour of the solution, then derive asymptotic estimates for large solutions near the boundary of the domain. Using these boundary asymptotics, we prove uniqueness results for power-type nonlinearities.

1.
Introduction

We investigate positive large (boundary blow-up) solutions of semilinear equations driven by the Finsler p-Laplacian. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We consider positive solutions of the problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\text{div}\left(H^{p-1}(\nabla u)\nabla H(\nabla u)\right)=f(u)\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\
u(x)\to\infty&\text{as dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\to 0.\end{cases}

Where
2\leq p<\infty
and
H\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a Minkowski norm (
[
28
]
). i.e.,

•

H(x)>0
for
x\neq 0
.

•

Positive homogeneity of degree 1:
H\left(tx\right)=|t|\penalty\ H\left(x\right)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

•

H\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\right)
.

•

Strong convexity: The Hessian matrix
\nabla^{2}(H^{2}(x)/2)
is positive definite for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Further, the assumptions regarding nonlinearity are presented in subsection
1.2
.

The operator on the left-hand side of (
1.1
) is called the
Finsler p-Laplacian
, denoted by
\Delta_{H}^{p}
.
It is an anisotropic generalisation of the Laplace and the p-Laplace operators. In the case
p=2
, the operator
\Delta_{H}^{2}
is called the Finsler Laplacian, which arise in Finsler geometry; we refer to
[
5
]
for a comprehensive understanding of this geometry. Apart from geometric origin, anisotropic operators arise in various physical contexts, including minimal surface energy
[
30
]
, crystallography, crystal growth
[
29
]
, image processing
[
27
]
, and the Willmore functional
[
10
]
. The study of these anisotropic problems dates back to 1901, when Wulff
[
32
]
employed anisotropic geometry to investigate crystals. One of the basic objects of study in crystallography is
Wulff shape
, which is the unit ball in the dual norm of
H
and is a minimiser of an anisotropic functional defined on subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For more in this direction, we refer to
[
11
,
10
]
and the references therein. The Wulff shape plays an important role in our analysis.

Large solutions to elliptic equations are studied extensively in various aspects, such as existence, uniqueness, and behaviour (asymptotics) of the solution and its gradient near the boundary of the domain. A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of large solutions is the Keller-Osserman condition; for the Laplace equation, it reads as follows

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{r}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{F(s)}}<\infty,\quad\text{for all }r>0,

where
F
is the primitive of
f
with
F(0)=0
.

The study of these problems traces back to L. Bieberbach’s 1916 work
[
7
]
, but the significant milestones were the independent contributions of Keller
[
17
]
and Osserman
[
25
]
, both in 1957. The paper
[
17
]
originated in electrodynamics
[
18
]
while
[
25
]
originated in geometry. A related development connecting large solutions to stochastic control can be found in
[
19
]
. For the existence, uniqueness, and asymptotics at the boundary of large solutions to semilinear equations, we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
20
]
and the references therein. For quasilinear equations involving the Laplace operator, we refer to
[
2
,
21
]
, and for the p-Laplacian, we refer to
[
9
,
15
,
22
]
and the references therein.
While large solutions for the Laplacian and the p-Laplacian have been studied extensively, much less is known in the anisotropic framework associated with the Finsler operator; see
[
14
]
for the Hamilton-Jacobi type equation involving the Finsler operator.

The present work extends classical results for semilinear equations to an anisotropic framework.

1.2.
Assumptions

Throughout the paper, we assume that
p\geq 2
and that
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{2}
boundary.

We assume the following on the nonlinearity:

(F)

f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is continuous and strictly increasing, with
f(0)=0

f(r)\to\infty
as

In the classical spirit, we assume the Keller-Osserman condition associated with the Finsler p-Laplace equation: Define the primitive of

by

(1.2)

F(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f(s)\ ds.

Then we assume

(KO)

\Psi_{H,p}(r)=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{1/p}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{\left\{F(s)\right\}^{1/p}}<\infty,\quad\text{for all }\penalty\ r>0
.

Remark 1
.

Remarkably, despite the anisotropic nature of the operator, the Keller–Osserman condition retains the same integrability structure as in the p-Laplacian case. This is because all anisotropic norms are equivalent to the Euclidean norm. The above Keller–Osserman condition coincides with the Keller–Osserman condition for the p-Laplacian; see
[
9
]
. See also Remark
4
, and proof of Theorem
5.2
or Proposition
5.3
in Section
5
.

Example 1.6
.

If
f(t)=t^{q}
, then
(KO)
implies
q>p-1
. And,

\Psi_{H,p}(t)=\left(\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}\right)^{1/p}\frac{p}{q+1-p}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{q+1-p}{p}}}.

We further consider the two possibilities about the integrability of
\Psi_{H,p}
near
0
:

(A1)

\int_{0^{+}}\frac{ds}{\{F(s)\}^{1/p}}=\infty
(
Osgood\penalty\ Condition
).

(A2)

\int_{0^{+}}\frac{ds}{\{F(s)\}^{1/p}}<\infty
.

1.3.
Main results

Consider the following notion of solution.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in W_{loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is said to solve

(1.3)

\text{div}\left(H^{p-1}(\nabla u)\nabla H(\nabla u)\right)=f(u)\quad\text{in }\Omega,

if for every pre-compact set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega

-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}H^{p-1}(\nabla u)\nabla H(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\phi\ dx=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}f(u)\phi\ dx\quad\text{for all }\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime}).

provided
f(u)\in L_{loc}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.

Remark 2
.

For a fixed
u\in W_{loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, the mapping
v\mapsto\int_{\Omega}H^{p-1}(\nabla u)\nabla H(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla v
for any
v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is bounded and linear due to Lemma
2.1
below.

Comparison principle plays an important role in proving both existence and asymptotic results. We prove a comparison principle

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{2}
boundary,
p\geq 2
, and
u,v\in W_{loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
such that

(1.4)

-\text{div}\left(H^{p-1}(\nabla u)\nabla H(\nabla u)\right)+f(u)\leq-\text{div}\left(H^{p-1}(\nabla v)\nabla H(\nabla v)\right)+f(v)\quad\text{weakly}.

Further, suppose that
u,v
satisfy

(1.5)

\limsup_{x\to z}\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}\leq 1,\quad\text{uniformly for all }z\in\partial\Omega.

Then
u\leq v
in
\Omega
.

We refer to Definition
1.1
for the weak notion. Further, we prove the following existence result

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{2}
boundary. Assume
(F)

and
(KO)
then (
1.1
) admits a solution in
W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega)
.

Next, we prove the boundary asymptotic for any large solutions. Before stating the result, the dual norm
H_{0}
of
H
is

(1.6)

H_{0}\left(\xi\right):=\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq 0}\frac{\left<\xi,x\right>}{H\left(x\right)}\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
) on a bounded domain
\Omega
with
C^{2}
boundary. Let
\delta_{H_{0}}(x):=\min\{H_{0}(x-z)\penalty\ |\penalty\ z\in\partial\Omega\}
Then

\lim_{\delta_{H_{0}}(x)\to 0}\frac{\Psi_{H,p}(u(x))}{\delta_{H_{0}}(x)}=1.

Finally, defining
\Phi:=\Psi^{-1}
, we prove the following boundary blow-up result.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain with
C^{2}
boundary, and let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
) with
f(t)=t^{q}
,
q>p-1
. Then

(1.7)

\lim_{\delta_{H_{0}}(x)\to 0}\frac{u(x)}{\Phi(\delta_{H_{0}}(x))}=1.

As a consequence, we obtain the following uniqueness result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with
C^{2}
boundary, then the equation (
1.1
) with
f(t)=t^{q}
,
q>p-1
admits exactly one solution.

1.4.
Outline

The existence of solutions to the finite Dirichlet problem, obtained via standard minimisation techniques, together with a brief discussion of regularity, is presented in the Appendix, since they are not the main focus of the paper.

The proof of the existence theorem uses the Browder–Minty-type argument. The proof of the comparison principle follows a standard argument; however, for completeness, we provide a full proof, since, to the best of our knowledge, this version of the comparison principle is not available in the literature.
The proof of the local bound in Section
4
follows a similar approach to
[
9
]
, but our proof is a somewhat simplified with a slightly different choice of super solution. Moreover, since the symmetry of the solution is with respect to the underlying anisotropic norm, we obtain a local bound using Wulff balls. The proof of asymptotic behaviour relies heavily on the distance defined using the Minkowski norm, the Wulff shape, and the geometry of the domain in Minkowski space. Precisely, we need to compare the solution with the distance from the boundary with respect to
H_{0}
norm. While doing so, one needs to use uniform ball conditions associated with the boundary of a
C^{2}
domain in the Minkowski space; see the first two paragraphs in Section
5
.

The focus of this work is on extending the theory of large solutions to anisotropic operators of Finsler p-Laplacian type. While the Keller–Osserman condition remains unchanged, the analysis requires handling anisotropic geometry induced by the norm
H
and its dual
H_{0}
. For example, the solution exhibits radial symmetry on a Wulff ball associated with
H_{0}
. Consequently, the barrier construction is on a similar
H_{0}
Wulff ball of radius
r
. Similarly, the boundary asymptotics are reformulated in terms of the distance from the boundary induced by
H_{0}
. This is where the geometry of the Wulff shape, and the distance from a closed set in Minkowski space (
[
13
]
) play an important role.

In Section
2
, we present preliminary results concerning the Minkowski norm
H
and its dual
H_{0}
, and establish the strict monotonicity of the Finsler p-Laplace operator. In Section
3
, we derive condition
(KO)
in the one-dimensional setting and prove Theorem
1.3
for
n=1
; see also
[
9
]
for a similar construction for a second-order quasilinear equation. The proof of this existence theorem in one dimension follows the same approach as in
[
9
]
except some technical differences. In the same section, we prove Theorem
1.4
for
n=1
.
Subsequently, in Section
4
, we establish the comparison principle (see Theorem
1.2
), derive estimates for the solution and its gradient, and prove the existence result stated in Theorem
1.3
. In Section
5
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.4
for
n\geq 2
and discuss how the anisotropy underlying the Finsler operator plays a vital role in the theory. In Section
6
, we use the asymptotic results to prove Corollary
1.5
and finally, the uniqueness result, Theorem
1.6
.

Lemma 2.3

Let

Using the last implication of Lemma

3.1.
Existence:

Let

. We consider the problem

(3.1)

\left(H^{p-1}(u^{\prime})H^{\prime}(u^{\prime})\right)^{\prime}=f(u)\quad\text{on }(a,b).

In dimension one, any Minkowski norm, being positively 1-homogeneous, must be of the form
H(t)=\gamma|t|

\gamma>0

(3.2)

\gamma^{p}\left(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=f(u)\quad\text{on }(a,b).

If
u
is a classical solution,
since

by
