Finite groups and rings generating varieties with rapid growth
Alexander Olshanskii

Abstract
Let A A be a finite universal algebra. Then the orders of the n n -generated free algebras F n F_{n} in the variety (equational class) generated by A A satisfy G. Birkhoff’s inequality: | F n | ≤ | A | | A | n |F_{n}|\leq|A|^{|A|^{n}} for n = 1 , 2 , … n=1,2,\dots It follows that lim sup n → ∞ log ⁡ | F n | n ≤ | A | \limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\log|F_{n}|}\leq|A| . When A A is a finite group or a finite nonassociative algebra, we obtain a criterion for equality in this estimate; equivalently, a criterion for maximal growth of the sequence { | F n | } n = 1 ∞ \{|F_{n}|\}_{n=1}^{\infty} .

1
Introduction

Let
A
be a finite algebra of a fixed signature. The variety of algebras
{\mathfrak{V}}=var\,A
generated by
A
consists of all algebras of the same signature satisfying every identity that holds in
A
.
A basic theorem of G. Birkhoff
[
2
]
says that
{\mathfrak{V}}
is the smallest class of algebras
containing
A
and closed under taking subalgebras, homomorphic images,
and Cartesian products.

The order of every
n
-generated algebra in the variety
\mathfrak{V}
is bounded by
the order of the
n
-generated
\mathfrak{V}
-free algebra
F_{n}
, and
|F_{n}|

is bounded above by the double-exponential function
|A|^{|A|^{n}}
, where

|A|
is the order of the algebra
A
. It follows that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\log|F_{n}|}\leq|A|
. We denote the left-hand side of this inequality by
pt(A)
and call
it the
potential
of the finite algebra
A
.

For example, if
G
is a
nontrivial finite group, then
pt(G)=1
if
G
is nilpotent
and
pt(G)\geq 2
otherwise (
[
5
]
, 24.52). The same alternative holds for
finite-dimensional (nonassociative) algebras over finite fields
[
1
]
.

As early as in 1937, it was noticed by B.H. Neumann
[
4
]
that the Birkhoff’s estimate can be improved in many cases. Our goal is to find necessary and sufficient conditions on
A
under which the potential
pt(A)
attains its maximal possible value, namely conditions equivalent to the equality
pt(A)=|A|
, where
A
is either a finite group or a finite, not necessarily associative, ring.

Recall that a nontrivial group (ring)
A
is called
monolithic
if the intersection of all nontrivial normal subgroups (ideals) of
A
is nontrivial.
This intersection is the
monolith
of the monolithic group (respectively, ring)
A
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be a finite group.
The equality

pt(G)=|G|
holds if and only if

(*)
G
is a monolithic group with a non-abelian monolith.

Moreover, under condition (*), there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}|G|^{n}<\log|F_{n}|<c_{2}|G|^{n}
for
n
-generated free groups
F_{n}
in the variety

var\;G
.

Many nonsimple groups satisfy condition (*); for example,
the symmetric groups
S_{n}
for
n\geq 5
and any wreath product of a non-cyclic finite
simple group and a nontrivial finite group.

A (not necessarily associative) ring
A
is a
prime ring
if the
product of any two nonzero ideals of
A
is nonzero. The following is the analogue of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A
be a (nonassociative) finite ring.
The equality
pt(A)=|A|
holds if and only if
A
is a prime ring.

Moreover, if
A
is a prime ring, then there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}|A|^{n}<\log|F_{n}|<c_{2}|A|^{n}
for the
n
-generated free rings
F_{n}
in the variety

var\;A
.

Classical results in ring theory imply that every finite prime associative
ring is simple (
[
6
]
, Section 98). By contrast, the following construction gives
a nonsimple prime algebra over a field with basis
(e,f)
and multiplication rules

e^{2}=f
,
ef=f
,
fe=0
, and
f^{2}=f
. It has only one one-dimensional ideal

\langle f\rangle
.

Sufficiency in Theorem 1

Lemma 1
.

Let
G
be a finite monolithic group with a non-abelian monolith
M
and
H=H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{k}
, where no subgroup
of
H_{i}
admits an epimorphism onto

for

. Then no subgroup
S\leq H
admits an epimorphism onto
G
.

Proof.

If
k=1
, then the assertion is immediate. Arguing
by induction, assume now that
k\geq 2
and
\phi:S\to G
is an
epimorphism. If, for example,
\phi(S\cap H_{k})=1
, then
\phi
factors through
the projection
\pi_{k}:S\to H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{k-1}
since

S\cap H_{k}
is the kernel of
\pi_{k}
, and so
G
is an epimorphic
image of a subgroup of
H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{k-1}
, contrary to the inductive
hypothesis. Otherwise the nontrivial normal subgroups
\phi(S\cap H_{1})

and
\phi(S\cap H_{2})
of
G
commute elementwise. This is impossible
since the monolith
M
of
G
is non-abelian. This contradiction proves the lemma.

∎

Let
G
be a finite monolithic group with a non-abelian monolith
M
and
P=P_{k}
be the direct product of isomorphic copies

of the group

,

, and and

,
where the order of the group

is less than the order of

.
Denote by

\pi_{i}
the projections of

onto

. Suppose

is subdirectly embedded in the group

. (Recall that a subgroup

of a direct product
is called a subdirect product of the factors if it projects onto each of them.)
Suppose that
n

are chosen in

(not necessarily distinct). Their

are denoted by

, respectively.

Lemma 2
.

In the above notation, let an epimorphism
\phi:S\to G
map the

to some elements

, respectively.
Then there exist

\phi=\psi\pi_{i}

\psi(x_{ij})=y_{j}

We induct on the number of factors

If

and no

If

, then we consider the projection

of

G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{k-1}\times H
and observe that

is an isomorphism between

and its

By the inductive

\phi\alpha^{-1}:S\to G
, and therefore
\phi\alpha^{-1}=\psi\pi_{i}

for some

and an isomorphism
\psi:G_{i}\to G

Then

as required. Hence we may

.

The intersections

are normal in

for every

,

. Hence the subgroup

contains the product

of all the monoliths

of the factors

,
and each

is a minimal normal subgroup in

The kernel

cannot contain

, since
otherwise

would be a homomorphic image of a subgroup of a direct

.
Without loss of generality, suppose

is not contained in

.

Denote by

the intersection

; it is the
kernel of the projection of

onto
G_{k}
,
and therefore

. If the normal subgroup

of

is not contained in

,
then the nontrivial normal

and

in

, a contradiction, because the monolith of

.

Since the kernel

of

is contained in the kernel

of

,
the isomorphism
\psi=\phi\pi_{k}^{-1}:G_{k}\to G
is well defined; that is,

. The equalities
\pi_{k}(x_{i})=x_{ki}
and
\phi(x_{i})=y_{i}

imply the equalities
\psi(x_{ki})=y_{i}
for

, and the lemma is proved.

∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a finite monolithic group with a non-abelian monolith
M
. Denote by

the maximal integer such that the direct power

has an
n
-generated subgroup

containing the direct power

.

.

Proof.

For each maximal proper subgroup

, we have

-tuples of elements from

. If

, for some

. Choose a maximal set of pairwise inequivalent

-tuples and denote it by

n
-tuples in

by the integers

.

Let

be an isomorphic copy of

,
where
(x_{1j},\dots,x_{nj})
is the
j
th
n
-tuple in
Y
,
j=1,\dots,N
. Denote
by
M_{j}
the copy of the monolith

in
G_{j}
. In the direct product
P
of all

, we choose the elements

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{11},\dots,x_{1N})

\displaystyle x_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{21},\dots,x_{2N})

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{n1},\dots,x_{nN})

where the coordinates of the

th generating

-tuple of

.

Let

be the subgroup of

generated by the elements

. We will prove that

Arguing by contradiction, assume, without loss of generality, that

. Since

is the

, it follows that

, because this normal subgroup

. Hence

,

By Lemma

factors as

, and

,
with

for

It follows from Lemma

Necessity in Theorem 1

We now assume that
pt(G)=|G|
, where
G
is a finite group and
\mathfrak{V}=var\;G
.
Then
G
is nontrivial, since
pt(\{1\})=0
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite groups with potentials
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, respectively.
Assume that the finite group
G
generates the variety
\mathfrak{V}=var\;(G_{1},G_{2})
. Then

pt(G)=\max(p_{1},p_{2})
.

Proof.

Clearly,
pt(G)\geq\max(p_{1},p_{2})
since
\mathfrak{V}
contains both
\mathfrak{V}_{1}=var\;G_{1}
and
\mathfrak{V}_{2}=var\;G_{2}
.
On the other hand, the
n
-generated
\mathfrak{V}
-free group
F_{n}
is a subdirect product
of the free groups
F_{n}^{\prime}
and
F_{n}^{\prime\prime}
in the varieties
{\mathfrak{V}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{V}}_{2}

(
[
5
]
, 15.82), and hence
|F_{n}|\leq|F^{\prime}_{n}||F^{\prime\prime}_{n}|
. It remains only to
note that, for any sequences of positive integers
\{a^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and

\{a^{\prime\prime}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\log(a^{\prime}_{n}a^{\prime\prime}_{n})}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\log a^{\prime}_{n}+\log a^{\prime\prime}_{n}}

=\max(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\log a^{\prime}_{n}},\;\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\log a^{\prime\prime}_{n}}).

∎

If the group
G
is not monolithic, there are two nontrivial
normal subgroups
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
in
G
such that
N_{1}\cap N_{2}=1
. Then
G

is a subdirect product of the factor groups
G_{1}=G/N_{1}
and
G_{2}=G/N_{2}
,
and
var\;G=var\;\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
. Thus
p_{1}\leq|G_{1}|<|G|
and
p_{2}\leq|G_{2}|<|G|

in Lemma 3, whence
pt(G)<|G|
, a contradiction. Therefore
G
must be monolithic.

It remains to prove that
pt(G)<|G|
if there is a nontrivial abelian normal
subgroup
N
in a finite group
G
. Since finite free groups
in the variety
\mathfrak{V}=var\;G
are isomorphic to some subgroups of the
direct powers of
G
(
[
5
]
, 15.4), we must substantially reduce the
number of direct factors (in comparison with groups having non-abelian monolith).
To this end, we construct subdirect products with far more
non-equivalent homomorphisms to
G
than standard
projections onto the direct factors.

Let
M
be an abelian normal subgroup in a finite group
H
.
Then
z^{-1}mz
is
well defined for every
m\in M
and
z\in H/M
, and it belongs to

.
For each coset

, choose a representative

and denote

. Then
u(y)u(z)=f(y,z)u(yz)
for some

.
Choose also

and denote by

an
n
-tuple of elements from

.
We shall work with quadruples

.

We call two quadruples

and

and

such that

•

(z^{\prime})^{-1}m^{\prime}z^{\prime}=(z^{-1}mz)^{\prime}
for

. (Here primes denote the images of

•

If a component

The second requirement means that

If, in addition to similarity, we have

Remark 3.1
.

The isomorphism of the quadruples resembles the equivalence
of extensions with abelian kernel in the standard homological case. The difference
is that here we add the

below is much bigger than the usual group of extensions (see exercises

The sum of two similar quadruples

,
where

and

is the copy of

.
Then

,

, and the definitions of

and

are similar to the case

, mapping the
n
-tuple

componentwise to

.

Let
D_{I}
be the projection of
D
to the product
H_{i_{1}}\times\dots\times H_{i_{t}}

The group
D_{I}
can be defined as
D
but using the quadruples with indices from
the set

only. The image of
Y
in
D_{I}
is its projection
Y_{I}
.
As in the previous paragraph, there is a homomorphism
D_{I}\to H_{0}
mapping
Y_{I}
to

X_{0}
, and the composition
D\to D_{I}\to H_{0}
maps
Y
to
X_{0}
componentwise.

Clearly, the groups
P
and
D
belong to the variety
var(H_{1},\dots,H_{s})
, and

|D|<|P|=\prod_{i=1}^{s}|H_{i}|
, as required.

∎

We now return to the finite group
G
with the nontrivial abelian normal subgroup
N
,
choose a set of coset representatives
U=\{u(z)\mid z\in G/N\}
and an arbitrary
n
-tuple

of elements from
G
.
Denote by
\cal C
the class of quadruples similar to
Q=(G,N,U,X)
and denote by

the subset of

, which contains the quadruples
(H^{\prime},M^{\prime},U^{\prime},X^{\prime})

from

Lemma 8
.

The set
Ext_{Q}
is a subgroup of the group
Ext_{\cal C}
.

Proof.

The nonempty set
Ext_{Q}
is closed under the sum operation

since the first

. Therefore, if

∎

We also note:

.

If

\;\;\Box

The next estimate uses the group structure on the set

Lemma 9
.

There exist a constant

, independent of

, a

with
|D_{Q}|<|G|^{Kn}
, and an

of elements from

such that for every quadruple

,
there is a homomorphism

that maps
X_{Q}
to

.

Proof.

By Lemmas
8

5
, the set

is an abelian group whose order is less than

) and obtain

Then each quadruple

By Lemma

Lemma 10
.

If

is an abelian normal subgroup of a finite group

, then

.

Proof.

Quadruples of the form
(G,N,U,X)
may belong to different groups

depending on the choice of the

-tuple

modulo the subgroup

.
Thus there are

different such groups, where

.

For each group

,

9
provides us with a group

and an

-tuple

in it such that for every

to

.

corresponding to different

4
Proof of Theorem 2 and questions

Note that a finite prime ring
A
cannot contain two different minimal ideals,
and multiplication is nonzero on the unique minimal ideal. If, for brevity,
rings with zero multiplication are called abelian (as in Lie theory),
A

is a monolithic ring with a non-abelian monolith
N
.

Such a reformulation makes Theorem
2
very similar to Theorem
1
,
and the proofs are similar as well. Of course, the variety
\mathfrak{V}=var\;A
contains
rings, not groups; one works with ideals instead of normal subgroups, and
elementwise commutation is replaced by mutual annihilation.
No other modifications are needed for the proof of sufficiency in Theorem
2

or for the proof of the analogue of Lemma 3.

The conjugation action of a group
H/M
on an abelian normal subgroup
M
is
now replaced with two ”actions”: multiplication of elements
m
of the ideal

M
by elements
x
of the ring
H/M
on the left and on the right, that
is,
xm,mx\in M
.
For the two ring operations, we now have two corresponding sets of equalities for coset representatives:
u(y)+u(z)=f(y,z)+u(y+z)

and
u(y)u(z)=e(y,z)+u(yz)
, where
U=\{u(z)\mid z\in H/M\}
and
f(y,z),e(y,z)\in M
.
For two similar quadruples
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
, we now have isomorphisms

M\to M^{\prime}
and
H/M\to H^{\prime}/M^{\prime}
such that
(xm)^{\prime}=x^{\prime}m^{\prime}
and
(mx)^{\prime}=m^{\prime}x^{\prime}
for any
m\in M
and
x\in H/M
.
If
Q=(H,M,U,X)
and
Q^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},M^{\prime},U^{\prime},X^{\prime})
are similar, then for any component

x_{i}
of
X
the equality
x_{i}=g_{i}+u(z)
for
g_{i}\in M
implies
x^{\prime}_{i}=h_{i}+u^{\prime}(z^{\prime})

for some
h_{i}\in M^{\prime}
.
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are called isomorphic if
f^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=f(x,y)^{\prime}
,
e^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=e(x,y)^{\prime}
and
h_{i}=(g_{i})^{\prime}
for all
x,y,i
.

The definition of the sum
Q+Q^{\prime}
and Lemma
5
remain the same up to the obvious change of the vocabulary. Since we now add
e
-elements, the
right-hand side of Lemma
6
is replaced with
|H|^{n+6}
. To complete the
proof of Theorem
2
, it suffices
to make the corresponding terminological changes in the proofs of Lemmas
7
–
10
.

Remark 4.1
.

It follows from the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
that for any
nontrivial finite group or nonassociative ring
A
, the dichotomy holds: either

pt(A)=|A|
or
pt(A)\leq|A^{\prime}|
for some proper quotient
A^{\prime}
of
A
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional (nonassociative) algebra over
a finite field
\bf F
, and let
F_{n}
denote the
n
-generated
\mathfrak{V}
-free algebra in
the variety of algebras
\mathfrak{V}
over
\bf F
generated by
A
. Then one can
formulate an analogue of Theorem
2
and prove it without any new assumptions.
In this setting, however, it is preferable to consider the dimension
function
d(n)=\dim_{\bf F}F_{n}
, which is the logarithm of the order
function
|F_{n}|
. Thus the potential
pt(A)
of
A
is
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{d(n)}
,
and Theorem
2
is modified as follows:

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional (nonassociative) algebra over a finite
field
\bf F
. The equality
pt(A)=|A|
holds if and only if
A
is a prime algebra.

Moreover, if
A
is a prime algebra, then there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}|A|^{n}<\dim_{\bf F}F_{n}<c_{2}|A|^{n}
for the
n
-generated free algebras
F_{n}
in the variety
var\;A
.

It is unknown whether there exists a finite group with a non-integer potential.
It is also unknown whether one may use
\lim_{n\to\infty}
instead of
\limsup_{n\to\infty}

in the definition of the potential of a finite group. Similar questions for finite
nonassociative algebras have been formulated in
[
1
]
.

An extension of Theorem
2
to finitely generated varieties of
\Omega
-algebras suggests itself.
