On normality and φ \varphi -normality of holomorphic functions in several complex variables
PRATIKSHA

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate φ \varphi -normal functions and normal families of holomorphic functions concerning total derivatives in ℂ n . \mathbb{C}^{n}. More precisely, we prove a sufficient condition for a holomorphic function defined on an open unit ball in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} satisfying certain conditions involving higher order partial derivatives to be φ \varphi -normal. Furthermore, by using differential inequalities involving total differential polynomials in ℂ n , \mathbb{C}^{n}, we establish some normality criteria for holomorphic functions in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} which generalize some known results.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}.
A family of meromorphic functions defined
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is said to be
normal in
\Omega
if every sequence in the
family admits a subsequence that converges spherically uniformly in compact subsets of
\Omega
either to a
meromorphic function or to a function identically equal to infinity. The theory of normal families occupies a central position in complex analysis. Its origins can be traced back to the pioneering work of Montel
[
19
]
in the early twentieth century. Since then, this theory has developed into a powerful analytical framework with far reaching applications in complex analysis, dynamics of rational and transcendental maps, bicomplex analysis, and several other branches of modern mathematics (see, for example,
[
2
,
4
,
9
,
13
,
21
]
). Recently, methods of normal families have seen a lot a applications in harmonic mappings (see
[
5
,
6
,
10
]
) which, in turn, have been employed to study fluid flow problems (see
[
1
]
).

A simultaneous development in this theory is the concept of
normal functions
, introduced implicitly by Yosida
[
24
]
and Noshiro
[
20
]
and later formalized by Lehto and Virtanen
[
15
]
. A meromorphic function defined on the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is called normal if its post composition with conformal automorphisms of the unit disk forms a normal family. This notion admits an equivalent characterization in terms of the boundedness of the spherical derivative as

Definition 1.1
.

A meromorphic function
f
defined in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
is called normal in
\mathbb{D}
if

\sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|\zeta|^{2})f^{\#}(\zeta)<\infty,

(1.1)

where
f^{\#}(\zeta)
denotes the spherical derivative of
f(\zeta).

The condition (
1.1
) above means that
f
is Lipschitz when regarded as a function from the hyperbolic disk
\mathbb{D}
into the extended complex plane endowed with the chordal distance (see
[
15
]
).
Motivated by the desire to enlarge the class of normal functions, Aulaskari and
Rättyä
[
3
]
, introduced the notion of
\varphi
-normal functions, replacing
the classical growth condition involving the hyperbolic metric with a more
flexible scale governed by a smoothly increasing function
\varphi
. This
extension has since attracted considerable attention and has led to a wealth of new results, especially concerning refined normality criteria and boundary
behaviour. For more detailed view of theory of normal families and normal functions in
\mathbb{C}
, the reader is advised to go through
[
22
]
.

Despite the maturity of the theory in one complex variable, the extension of these ideas to the setting of several complex variables presents substantial
challenges. The intricate geometry of domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, renders many classical techniques ineffective or incomplete. Consequently, the development
of theory of normal families in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
has become an active and vibrant area of research. The present paper, therefore, is devoted to provide more richness into the theory of normal families in
\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Let
U
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Throughout this paper, by a
holomorphic function
in
U
, we mean a
\mathbb{C}
-valued holomorphic function defined in
U
.
Analogous to the one-variable case, a family of holomorphic functions defined in
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be
normal
in
U
if every sequence in the family admits a subsequence that converges locally uniformly in
U
either to a holomorphic function or to the function identically equal to infinity. To facilitate our results, we shall be using the following notations:

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=(z_{1},~z_{2},~\ldots,~z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n},z_{j}\in\mathbb{C},~~~~~~~j=1,2,\ldots,n,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(0,~0,~\ldots,~0)\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

\displaystyle\|z-w\|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}-w_{j}|^{2},}

\displaystyle\Delta(w,\delta)

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\|z-w\|<\delta\},~\delta>0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\|z\|<1\}.

A fundamental tool in the study of normality is the notion of the spherical derivative, whose extension to several complex variables was formulated via the complex Hessian as;
Let
\omega=(\omega_{1},~\omega_{2},~\ldots,~\omega_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and for every function
h\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(U)
, at each
z\in U
, define a Hermitian form

L_{z}(h,\omega)=\sum\limits_{j,~k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}h(z)}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\omega_{j}\overline{\omega}_{k},

(1.2)

called the Hessian of
h
at
z
. For a holomorphic function
h
in
U
, the spherical derivative is defined by

h^{\#}(z)=\sup_{\|\omega\|=1}\sqrt{L_{z}(log(1+|h|^{2}),\omega)}.

(1.3)

This definition coincides with the classical spherical derivative when
n=1
and admits the equivalent representation

h^{\#}(z)=\frac{|h^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|h(z)|^{2}}.

as shown in
[
11
]
.
Further, by straightforward calculations, one can obtain

h^{\#}(z)=\sup_{\|\omega\|=1}\frac{|\langle\nabla h(z),\overline{\omega}\rangle|}{1+|h(z)|^{2}},

(1.4)

where
\nabla h(z)
denotes the complex gradient of
h
, and
\langle z,w\rangle
, with
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the standard inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Employing this notion of spherical derivative, Zhu et al.
[
25
]
extended the concept of smoothly increasing functions and the notion of
\varphi
-normality to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as

Definition 1.2
.

A function
\varphi:[0,1)\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
is said to be
smoothly increasing
if

\varphi(r)(1-r)\longrightarrow\infty\quad\text{as }r\longrightarrow 1^{-},

(1.5)

and

\mathcal{R}_{a}(w):=\frac{\varphi\!\left(\left\|a+{w}/{\varphi(\|a\|)}\right\|\right)}{\varphi(\|a\|)}\longrightarrow 1\quad\text{as }~\|a\|\longrightarrow 1^{-},

(1.6)

uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Given such a function
\varphi
, a holomorphic function
h
in
\mathbb{B}
is said to be

\varphi
-normal
if

\sup_{w\in\mathbb{B}}\frac{h^{\#}(w)}{\varphi(\|w\|)}<\infty,

(1.7)

where
h^{\#}(w)
is given by (
1.4
).

Also, recall that for multi-index
J=(j_{1},~j_{2},~\ldots,~j_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n},~|J|=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}j_{k},
at each
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n},
the partial derivative operator ,
D^{J}

is given by

D^{J}=\frac{\partial^{|J|}}{\partial w_{1}^{j_{1}}\partial w_{2}^{j_{2}}\ldots\partial w_{n}^{j_{n}}}.

(1.8)

Using Definition
1.2
and the operator defined in (
1.8
), Zhu et al.
[
25
]
obtained sufficient conditions for
\varphi
-normality involving higher-order partial derivatives. More precisely, they proved the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and
f

be a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\sup\{|D^{I}f(z)|:f(z)=0,~I\in\mathbb{N}^{n},~|I|=1,~2,~\ldots,~k-1\}<\infty.

If there exists a set
E
consisting of three distinct finite complex numbers such that

\sup_{z\in f^{-1}(E)}\frac{1}{\varphi(\|z\|)^{k}}\frac{|D^{J}f(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{k+1}}<\infty,~J\in\mathbb{N}^{n},~|J|=k,

then
f
is
\varphi
-normal.

This result naturally raises the question of whether the cardinality of the set
E
is optimal. In this paper, we address this question affirmatively by demonstrating that the set
E
can indeed be reduced from three points to two, provided additional control is imposed on higher-order derivatives. This constitutes our first main result and significantly sharpens Theorem
1.3
as

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and
f

be a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\sup\{|D^{I}f(z)|:f(z)=0,~I\in\mathbb{N}^{n},~|I|=1,~2,~\ldots,~k-1\}<\infty.

(1.9)

If there exists a set
E
consisting of two distinct finite complex numbers such that

\sup_{z\in f^{-1}(E)}\frac{1}{\varphi(\|z\|)^{k}}\frac{|D^{J}f(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{k+1}}<\infty,~J\in\mathbb{N}^{n},~|J|=k,

(1.10)

and

\sup_{z\in f^{-1}(E)}\frac{|D^{L}f(z)|}{1+|D^{J}f(z)|^{\frac{k+1}{k}}}<\infty,~L\in\mathbb{N}^{n},~|L|=k+1,

(1.11)

then
f
is
\varphi
-normal.

Theorem
1.4
for plannar harmonic mappings is due to Bharti and Thin
[
5
]
. The next objective of this paper is to study the theory of normal families involving total derivatives in several complex variables, introduced by Lu
[
17
]
as

Definition 1.5
.

Let
z=(z_{1},~z_{2},~\ldots,~z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
f
be an entire function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}.
The total derivative
D_{*}f
of
f
is defined by
D_{*}f(z):=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}f_{z_{j}}(z),
where
f_{z_{j}}
is the partial derivative of
f
with respect to
z_{j},~j=1,~2,~\ldots,~n.
The l-th order total derivative
D_{*}^{l}f(z)
is defined recursively by

D_{*}^{l}f(z):=D_{*}(D_{*}^{l-1}f)(z),~l\geq 1~\mbox{with}~D_{*}^{0}f(z):=f(z).

Remark 1.6
.

It is worth mentioning that the total derivative of a transcendental entire function is again a transcendental entire function. In contrast, this property need not hold for partial derivatives.

Building upon this idea, Cao and Liu
[
7
]
established analogues of Marty’s theorem, Miranda’s theorem, and Zalcman’s lemma in the setting of
total derivatives. Subsequently, in
2019
, they obtained several normality criteria involving bounds on higher order total derivatives (see
[
16
]
). More precisely, in
[
16
, Theorem 1.8, p. 5]
, they proved

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
k\geq 1
be a natural number and
\mathcal{F}
be a family of holomorphic functions in a domain
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Suppose that each
f\in\mathcal{F}
is non-vanishing in
\Delta(0,\delta)\subset U
, where
\delta\in(0,1)
. If for three distinct finite complex numbers
c_{1},~c_{2},~c_{3}
and for all
f\in\mathcal{F},f(z)=c_{i}\implies|D_{*}^{k}f(z)|\leq C,
for some
C>0,~\text{and}~i=1,~2,~3
, then
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
U
.

In recent years, the application of differential polynomials has seen a resurgence in the study of normal families of meromorphic functions, since a differential polynomial of a meromorphic function
f
serves as a natural extension of the derivative of
f
(see
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
18
]
). Motivated by these recent developments, we consider total differential monomials in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and investigate normality criteria involving such expressions.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic function in a domain
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
m_{0},~m_{1},~m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}
be non-negative integers (not all zeros), and
k\geq 1
be a natural number, then the expression

\displaystyle M_{D_{*}}[f]

\displaystyle:=(D_{*}f)^{m_{1}}(D_{*}^{2}f)^{m_{2}}(D_{*}^{3}f)^{m_{3}}\ldots(D_{*}^{k}f)^{m_{k}}

(1.12)

is called total differential monomial of
f
. The quantity
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}=\bar{d}
is called the degree of
M_{D_{*}}[f].

Our second main result shows that the conclusion of Theorem
1.7
remains valid
when the total derivative is replaced by a total differential monomial, thereby
extending Theorem
1.7
to a broader framework as

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of holomorphic functions in a domain
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
C>0
, and
c_{1},~c_{2},~\ldots,~c_{\bar{d}+2}
be
\bar{d}+2
distinct finite complex numbers. Suppose that each
f\in\mathcal{F}
is non-vanishing in
\Delta(0,\delta)\subset U
, where
\delta\in(0,1)
. If for each
f\in\mathcal{F},~f(z)=c_{i}\implies|{M_{D_{*}}}[f](z)|\leq C,~i=1,~2,~\ldots,~\bar{d}+2
, then
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
U
.

By taking
M_{D_{*}}[f]=D_{*}^{k}(f),
where
k
is a natural number, one can easily recover Theorem
1.7
, showing that Theorem
1.9
is a generalization of Theorem
1.7
. Furthermore, it can be demonstrated by the following example that the condition “each
f\in\mathcal{F}
is non-vanishing in
\Delta(0,\delta)
, where

Example 1.9
.

Let
U=\{z=(z_{1},~z_{2},~z_{3},~z_{4})\in\mathbb{C}^{4}:\|z\|<1\}~\mbox{and}~\mathcal{F}=\{f_{t}:f_{t}(z)=t((z_{1})^{3}+(z_{2})^{3}+(z_{3})^{3}+(z_{4})^{3}),~~t\in\mathbb{N},z\in U\}.
Then

Set

where

Then,
M_{D_{*}}[f_{t}](z)=3^{k+1}(f_{t}(z))^{2}.~\mbox{Taking}~C=3^{k+1}\max\{|c_{i}|^{2},~i=1,~2,~\ldots,~\bar{d}+2\}
, one can see that
f_{t}(z)=c_{i}\implies|M_{D_{*}}[f_{t}](z)|\leq C,~i=1,~2,~\ldots,~\bar{d}+2
. For any

, for sufficiently large

, making

is non-vanishing in

, where
\delta\in(0,1)
” in Theorem

cannot be omitted .

Finally, motivated by the differential polynomial studied by Manket and Nevo in

Let

Manket and Nevo in

proved the following result in the setting of one complex variable:

Theorem 1.11
.

Let

.

.

Preliminary Lemmas

This section is devoted to the pre-existing results that will be used in establishing the proofs of our main results.

The proofs of our results rely heavily on Nevanlinna’s value distribution theory in one complex variable. Therefore, we assume that the reader is familiar with the standard notations of
T(r,f),N(r,f),\overline{N}(r,f),m(r,f),S(r,f),

The first lemma, due to Zhu et al.
[
25
]
, is the

Lemma 2.1

Let
f
be a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and

the family

\mathcal{F}=\left\{h_{t}(w)=f\left(a_{t}+\frac{1}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}w\right);~t\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is normal in
\mathbb{B}.

What follows is an extension of the famous Zalcman Pang lemma to

[

Lemma 2.2

Let

be a positive integer and
\mathcal{F}
be a family of holomorphic functions in the unit ball

satisfying

, such that

h_{t}(w):=\frac{f_{t}\bigl(z_{t}+\rho_{t}w\bigr)}{\rho_{j}^{p}}

converges locally uniformly to a non-constant holomorphic function

in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

In the next lemma we present the several variable analogue of Hurwitz theorem

Let

Claim 1:

(3.3)

Now, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) together yield

D^{I}H(w_{0})=0,~~~~~~|I|=1,2,\ldots,k-1,

and
D^{J}H\not\equiv 0
.

Therefore, Claim 1 is established.

Claim 2:

D^{J}H(z)=0
whenever
H(z)\in E.

Again, assume that
T_{1}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing
z_{0}
such that
H(z_{0})=b,~\mbox{for some}~b\in E
. Thus, by Lemma
2.3
, there exists sequence
\{z_{t}\}~\mbox{with}~z_{t}\longrightarrow z_{0}
such that
H(z_{t})=b,
which gives

H_{t}(z_{t})=f\left(a_{t}+\frac{1}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}(w_{t}+\rho_{t}z_{t})\right)=b.

Again, for ease, set
a_{t}+\frac{1}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}(w_{t}+\rho_{t}z_{t})=\tilde{z_{t}}
. Thus, in view of (
1.10
), and for sufficiently large
t
, there exists
C_{2}>0
, such that

\frac{1}{\varphi(\|\tilde{z}_{t}\|)^{k}}\frac{|D^{J}f(\tilde{z}_{t})|}{1+|f(\tilde{z}_{t})|^{k+1}}\leq C_{2}.

(3.4)

This implies,

\frac{|D^{J}H_{t}(z_{t})|}{1+|H_{t}(z_{t})|^{k+1}}=\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}\right)^{k}\frac{D^{J}f(\tilde{z}_{t})}{1+|f(\tilde{z_{t}})|^{k+1}}\leq(\rho_{t})^{k}\left(\frac{\varphi(\|\tilde{z}_{t}\|)}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}\right)^{k}C_{2}

as
t\longrightarrow\infty
. Again, using (
3.2
) , we find that
\frac{|D^{J}H(z_{0})|}{1+|H(z_{0})|^{k+1}}=0,
and thus
D^{J}H(z_{0})=0.

This establishes Claim 2.

Claim 3:

D^{L}H(z)=0
whenever
H(z)\in E.

Proceeding as in Claim
2
, and using (
1.10
), we find that for sufficiently large
t
, there exists
C_{3}>0,
such that

\displaystyle|D^{J}f(\tilde{z}_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(\|\tilde{z}_{t}\|)^{k}(1+|f(\tilde{z}_{t})|^{k+1})C_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(\|\tilde{z}_{t}\|)^{k}(1+\max_{\beta\in E}|\beta|^{k+1})C_{3}.

(3.5)

Again, by (
1.11
), there exists
C_{4}>0
such
that

\frac{|D^{L}f(\tilde{z}_{t})|}{1+|D^{J}f(\tilde{z}_{t})|^{(k+1)/k}}\leq C_{4}.

(3.6)

Set
C=\text{max}\{C_{3},C_{4}\}.

Now, (
3
) and (
3.6
) yield

\displaystyle\frac{|D^{L}H_{t}(z_{t})|}{1+|D^{J}H_{t}(z_{t})|^{\frac{k+1}{k}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}\right)^{k+1}\frac{|D^{L}f(\tilde{z_{t}})|}{1+\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\varphi\|a_{t}\|}\right)^{(k+1)}|D^{J}f(\tilde{z_{t}})|^{\frac{k+1}{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}\right)^{k+1}\frac{|D^{L}f(\tilde{z_{t}})|}{1+|D^{J}f(\tilde{z}_{t})|^{\frac{k+1}{k}}}\frac{1+|D^{J}f(\tilde{z}_{t})|^{\frac{k+1}{k}}}{1+\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\varphi\|a_{t}\|}\right)^{(k+1)}|D^{J}f(\tilde{z_{t}})|^{\frac{k+1}{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}\right)^{k+1}\left(1+[C(1+\max_{\beta\in E}|\beta|^{k+1})]^{\frac{k+1}{k}}\varphi(\|\tilde{z}_{t}\|)^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\rho_{t}^{k+1}\left(1+[C(1+\max_{\beta\in E}|\beta|^{k+1})]^{\frac{k+1}{k}}\left(\frac{\varphi(\|\tilde{z}_{t}\|)}{\varphi(\|a_{t}\|)}\right)^{k+1}\right).

In view of (
3.2
), we obtain that
\frac{|D^{L}H(z_{0})|}{1+|D^{J}H(z_{0})|^{k+1}}\longrightarrow 0~\text{as}~t\longrightarrow\infty.
Therefore,
D^{L}H(z_{0})=0,
and hence the Claim 3.

Now, for any
w=(w_{1},~w_{2}~\ldots,~w_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, define,

g_{w}(\mu)=H(\mu w)=H(\mu w_{1},~\mu w_{2},~\ldots,~\mu w_{n}),~\mu\in\mathbb{C}.

For brevity, we set
\mu w_{i}=\mu_{i}.
Since,
H
is non-constant holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{n},
implies,
g_{w}
is also a non-constant holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}.
Assume that
H(\mu{{}^{\prime}}w)=b,~\text{for some}~b\in E~\text{and}~\mu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C},~\text{then},~g_{w}(\mu^{{}^{\prime}})\in E.

Furthermore, for positive integers,
i_{1},~i_{2},~\ldots,~i_{k+1},
we have

\displaystyle g_{w}^{(1)}(\mu{{}^{\prime}})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}=1}^{n}w_{i_{1}}\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial{\mu_{i_{1}}}}(\mu^{{}^{\prime}}w),

\displaystyle g_{w}^{(2)}(\mu{{}^{\prime}})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2}=1}^{n}w_{i_{1}}w_{i_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}{H}}{\partial{\mu_{i_{1}}}{\partial\mu_{i_{2}}}}(\mu^{{}^{\prime}}w).

Proceeding in the same way as above, we find that

\displaystyle g_{w}^{(k)}(\mu{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}=1}^{n}w_{i_{1}}w_{i_{2}}\ldots w_{i_{k}}\frac{\partial^{k}{H}}{\partial{\mu_{i_{1}}}\partial{\mu_{i_{2}}}\ldots\partial{\mu_{i_{k}}}}(\mu^{{}^{\prime}}w)

(3.7)

\displaystyle g_{w}^{(k+1)}(\mu{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k+1}=1}^{n}w_{i_{1}}w_{i_{2}}\ldots w_{i_{k+1}}\frac{\partial^{k+1}{H}}{\partial{\mu_{i_{1}}}\partial{\mu_{i_{2}}}\ldots\partial{\mu_{i_{k+1}}}}(\mu^{{}^{\prime}}w)

(3.8)

Now, Claim
2
and Claim
3
along with (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), yield

g_{w}^{(k)}(\mu{{}^{\prime}})=0~\text{and}~g_{w}^{(k+1)}(\mu{{}^{\prime}})=0.

Now, by using Second fundamental theorem of Nevanlinna, we obtain that

\displaystyle T(r,g_{w})

\displaystyle\leq\overline{N}(r,g_{w})+\sum\limits_{b_{j}\in E}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{(g_{w}-b_{j})}\right)+S(r,g_{w})

\displaystyle\leq\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{g_{w}^{(k)}}\right)+S(r,g_{w})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}N\left(r,\frac{1}{g_{w}^{(k)}}\right)+S(r,g_{w})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}T(r,g_{w}^{(k)})+S(r,g_{w})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}T(r,g_{w})+S(r,g_{w}).

which is a contradiction.

Thus
f
is
\varphi
-normal.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is not normal in
U
. Then, by Lemma
2.4
, there exist a real number
r\in(0,1),~\mbox{sequences}~\{z_{t}\}\subset U:0<\|z_{j}\|<r,\{f_{t}\}\subset\mathcal{F},~\mbox{and}~\{\rho_{t}\}\subset(0,1)~;~\rho_{t}\to 0~\mbox{as}~t\to\infty,~\mbox{such that}~

h_{t}(\eta)=f_{t}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta}),~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\eta\in\mathbb{C}

converges locally uniformly to a non-constant entire function
h(\eta)
in
\mathbb{C}.
Since,
h_{t}
is a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}
, we can easily compute that

h^{(k)}_{t}(\eta)=\rho_{t}^{k}D_{*}^{k}f_{t}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta})\longrightarrow h^{(k)}(\eta)

(3.9)

locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
.

Now,

\displaystyle M_{D_{*}}[f_{t}](z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(D_{*}^{j}f_{t}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta})\right)^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\left(\frac{h_{t}^{(j)}(\eta)}{\rho_{t}^{j}}\right)^{m_{j}}}.

(3.10)

On combining equations (
3.9
) and (
3
), it becomes obvious that

\rho_{t}^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}{jm_{j}}}M_{D_{*}}[f_{t}](z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\left({h_{t}^{(j)}(\eta)}\right)^{m_{j}}}\longrightarrow\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\left({h^{(j)}(\eta)}\right)^{m_{j}}}

(3.11)

locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}.

Claim:

h(\eta)=c_{i}\implies\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\left({h^{(j)}(\eta)}\right)^{m_{j}}}=0,~~~~~i=1,~2,~\ldots~\bar{d}+2
.

Let
h(\eta_{0})=c_{1}.
Then by Hurwitz theorem, there exists sequence
\{\eta_{t}\}\subset\mathbb{C}~\mbox{with}~\eta_{t}\longrightarrow\eta_{0}~\mbox{as}~t\longrightarrow\infty~\mbox{ such that}~h_{t}(\eta_{t})=f_{t}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{t}})=c_{1}
, it implies that
|M_{D_{*}}[f](z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{t}})|\leq C.
Using the assumptions of the theorem and the equation (
3.11
), we obtain

\left|\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\left({h^{(j)}(\eta_{0})}\right)^{m_{j}}}\right|=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\rho_{t}^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}{jm_{j}}}M_{D_{*}}[f_{t}](z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{t}})\right|\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}{jm_{j}}}C=0,

which gives
\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\left({h^{(j)}(\eta_{0})}\right)^{m_{j}}}=0.
By applying similar reasoning for
c_{2},c_{3},\ldots,c_{\bar{d}+2},
we establish our claim.

In view of above established claim and the Second Fundamental Theorem of Nevanlinna, we find that

\displaystyle(\bar{d}+1)T(r,h)

\displaystyle\leq\overline{N}(r,h)+\sum_{i=1}^{\bar{d}+2}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{h-c_{i}}\right)+S(r,h)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(r,\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}(h^{(j)})^{m_{j}}}\right)+S(r,h)

\displaystyle\leq T\left(r,\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}(h^{(j)})^{m_{j}}}\right)+S(r,h)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}T\left(r,\frac{1}{h^{(j)}}\right)+S(r,h)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}T(r,h^{(j)})+S(r,h)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}T(r,h)+S(r,h)

\displaystyle=\bar{d}~T(r,h)+S(r,h).

Thus,
T(r,h)=S(r,h)
, which is a contradiction.

Hence
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
U.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is not normal in
U
. Then, by Lemma
2.4
, there exist a real number
r\in(0,1),~\mbox{sequences}~\{z_{t}\}\subset U:0<\|z_{j}\|<r,\{f_{t}\}\subset\mathcal{F},~\mbox{and}~\{\rho_{t}\}\subset(0,1)~;~\rho_{t}\longrightarrow 0~\mbox{as}~t\longrightarrow\infty,~\mbox{such that}~\mbox{for}~\beta>l
,

h_{t}(\eta)=\frac{f_{t}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta})}{\rho_{t}^{\beta}},~\eta\in\mathbb{C}

converges locally uniformly to a non-constant entire function
h(\eta)
in
\mathbb{C}.
Since,
h_{t}
is a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}
, we can easily compute that

h^{(l)}_{t}(\eta)=\rho_{t}^{l-\beta}D_{*}^{l}f_{t}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta})\longrightarrow h^{(l)}(\eta)

(3.12)

locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
.

We may assume that the sequence
\{z_{t}\}\longrightarrow z_{0}
, for sufficiently large
t
.
Now, for each
i=1,~2,~\ldots,~l-1,
and
\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
, we can see that

\alpha_{i}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{0}})h_{t}^{(i)}(\eta_{0})\longrightarrow\alpha_{i}(z_{0})h^{(i)}(\eta_{0}),~\mbox{as}~t\longrightarrow\infty.

Therefore,

\left|h_{t}^{(l)}(\eta_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\rho_{t}^{l-i}\alpha_{i}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{0}})h_{t}^{(i)}(\eta_{0})\right|\longrightarrow|h^{(l)}(\eta_{0})|<M,

(3.13)

for sufficiently large
t\in\mathbb{N}
, and some positive real
M
.

Now, (
3.12
) and (
1.14
) together yield

\displaystyle\left|h_{t}^{(l)}(\eta_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\rho_{t}^{l-i}\alpha_{i}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{0}})h_{t}^{(i)}(\eta_{0})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\rho_{t}^{l-\beta}D_{*}^{l}f_{t}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{0}})+\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\rho_{t}^{l-\beta}\alpha_{i}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{0}})D_{*}^{i}f_{t}(z_{t}e^{\rho_{t}\eta_{0}})\right|>\rho_{t}^{l-\beta}C\longrightarrow\infty,~~~t\longrightarrow\infty.

(3.14)

which is a contradiction to (
3.13
).

Hence
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
U.

∎
