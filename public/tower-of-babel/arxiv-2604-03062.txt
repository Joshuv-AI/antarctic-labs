On de Rham–Witt Cohomology of Classifying Stacks
Shizhang Li, Yuan Yang

Abstract
We give an example of proper smooth fourfold over a perfect field k k of characteristic p > 0 p>0 with asymmetric Hodge–Witt numbers in total degree 3 3 . Our example is sharp both in terms of dimension and total degree. We arrive at our example by computing and approximating the Hodge–Witt cohomology groups of the classifying stack B ​ α p B\alpha_{p} .

It plays a central role in the study of the arithmetic of such varieties.

When

Namely the

These numbers arise from the homological algebra of the de Rham–Witt complex

Although their definition is somewhat indirect,

The search for such an analogue is reasonable, as it is well-known that Hodge numbers in

It is natural to ask to what extent the familiar symmetries of Hodge numbers

Ekedahl proved that they satisfy the analogue of Serre duality

h_{W}^{i,j}=h_{W}^{N-i,N-j},

This symmetry reflects the duality theory of Hodge–Witt cohomology.
Another fundamental symmetry of Hodge numbers in characteristic

h^{i,j}=h^{j,i}.

While knowing that in positive characteristic Hodge numbers can be asymmetric,
it is natural to ask whether the same symmetry holds for Hodge–Witt numbers:

h_{W}^{i,j}=h_{W}^{j,i}.

In low degree or dimension, such symmetry holds true:
For instance, Ekedahl proved that Hodge symmetry holds for Hodge–Witt numbers
whenever
i+j\leqslant 2

[
10

, and more generally for all
smooth proper varieties of dimension
\leqslant 3

[
10
, Corollary VI.3.3(iii)]

Beyond these cases, however, no general principle is known
that would force “Hodge–Witt symmetry” in higher dimensions.
To this question, Ekedahl himself commented
[
10
, Remark in Page. 113]

that “he saw no reason why such a symmetry should hold in general”.
Nevertheless, no counterexample seems to have appeared in the literature.
The purpose of this paper is to show that such counterexamples exist starting exactly in dimension
4

and cohomological degree
3

Left graded
{R}
-modules

It was discovered by Illusie that the crystalline complex admits a canonical representative,
namely the de Rham–Witt complex.
Its existence is rather striking: it provides a concrete complex equipped with Frobenius
F
and Verschiebung
V
satisfying the fundamental relation
FdV=d
, thereby encoding the structure of crystalline cohomology.
Illusie also observed the appearance of certain torsion phenomena in the slope spectral sequence in the study of supersingular abelian and K3 surfaces (
[
12
, Example II.7.1, II.7.2]
).
It soon became clear that the failure of degeneration for the slope spectral sequence is largely governed by
certain objects now known as dominoes (see
Definition

2.15
).
Illusie and Raynaud formalized this observation by introducing the notion of coherent graded
{R}
-modules.
The homological algebra of such
{R}
-modules reveals a remarkably rich structure and gives rise to a
number of subtle numerical invariants associated with de Rham–Witt cohomology of smooth proper varieties over
k
.

Proposition 2.38
.

Let

, and assume that it is grading left-bounded.
Suppose

for all

\mathrm{H}^{j}(M)^{i}=0
for all

Proof.

By

, we see that
\mathrm{H}^{j}(M)^{i}=0
for all

.
It therefore suffices to show that
\mathrm{H}^{j}(M)^{i}=0
for all

.

By assumption, there exists an integer

such that
\mathrm{H}^{j}(M)^{a}=0
for all

In particular, we see that
\mathrm{H}^{(d-1-i)}(M)^{i}=0

We prove by induction on

that this vanishing holds for all

Suppose it has been shown for all

\mathrm{H}^{(d-1-m)}(M)^{m}=0

Consider the spectral sequence of left graded

-modules:

2.4
Hodge–Witt numbers

Below we recall several relations between Hodge–Witt numbers and Hodge numbers.

Theorem 2.39

(Crew’s formula,
[
6
, Theorem 4]
,
[
10
, Theorem IV.3.2(2)]
)
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{DG}r_{c}^{b}({R})
.
For any natural number
i
, one has

\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}h_{W}^{i,j}=\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}h^{i,j}.

(2.40)

Corollary 2.41

(see also
[
18
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

For any short exact sequence of coherent
{R}
-modules

0\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow M\rightarrow M_{2}\rightarrow 0,

for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
one has

T^{i}(M)=T^{i}(M_{1})+T^{i}(M_{2}).

Consequently, let
M
be a coherent
{R}
-module and let

M_{\bullet}
be any filtration satisfying the conclusion of

[
11
, Proposition I.2.19]
.
Then the multiplicity of the
i
-th shift of type
\mathrm{II}
in the associated graded object
is independent of the choice of filtration.

Proof.

By definition, all differentials
d
of a coherent

{R}
-module become zero after inverting
p
. Consequently, the multiplicity of a slope

\lambda
, namely

m_{\lambda}(\text{c\oe ur}(M^{i})\otimes K),

is additive in short exact sequences of coherent
{R}
-modules.
It follows that the slope numbers
m^{i,j}
and the quantities

\sum_{\lambda\in[0,1)}(1-\lambda)m_{\lambda}(\text{c\oe ur}(M^{i})\otimes K),\qquad\sum_{\lambda\in[0,1)}\lambda m_{\lambda}(\text{c\oe ur}(M^{i-1})\otimes K)

are additive for any short exact sequence of coherent
{R}
-modules.

Viewing
M,M_{1},M_{2}
as objects of
\mathcal{DG}r_{c}^{b}({R})
, the short exact sequence

0\to M_{1}\to M\to M_{2}\to 0
induces an exact triangle

{R}_{1}\otimes_{R}^{\mathrm{L}}M_{1}\longrightarrow{R}_{1}\otimes_{R}^{\mathrm{L}}M\longrightarrow{R}_{1}\otimes_{R}^{\mathrm{L}}M_{2}

in
\mathcal{DG}r(k[d]/d^{2})
.
Taking the component of module degree
i
yields long exact sequences in cohomology.
Hence the Euler characteristic

\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}h^{i,j}(M)

is additive. By
Theorem

2.39
, the same holds for

\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}h_{W}^{i,j}(M)=\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}h^{i,j}(M).

For a coherent
{R}
-module
M
, viewed as an object of
\mathcal{DG}r_{c}^{b}({R})
,
the Hodge–Witt numbers are given by

\displaystyle h_{W}^{i,0}(M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in[0,1)}(1-\lambda)m_{\lambda}(\text{c\oe ur}(M^{i})\otimes K)+T^{i}(M),

\displaystyle h_{W}^{i,-1}(M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in[0,1)}\lambda m_{\lambda}(\text{c\oe ur}(M^{i-1})\otimes K)-2T^{i-1}(M),

\displaystyle h_{W}^{i,-2}(M)

\displaystyle=T^{i-2}(M).

Since the slope multiplicities are additive, it follows that the quantity

T^{i}(M)+2T^{i-1}(M)+T^{i-2}(M)

is additive for every
i\in\mathbb Z
.
An induction on
i
then shows that each
T^{i}
is additive for short exact sequences of coherent
{R}
-modules.
∎

\tau^{[-n,n]}N\in\widetilde{\mathcal{DG}r}^{b,\geqslant 1}_{c}({R}),

has no
\mathrm{H}^{\leqslant 0}
by
Theorem

3.41
.

Next, we show the existence of diagonal truncations. Let

and
consider the diagonal connective covers of its
Postnikov truncations

where

runs through all such pairs of integers.
By property (2) of
Theorem

3.41
, we see that

. Moreover, by property (3) of
Theorem

3.41
,
for each pair of integers

, the natural maps

\displaystyle N^{[a,b]}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\tau^{[a,b]}N^{[a,b+1]},

\displaystyle\tau^{[a,b]}N^{[a-1,b]}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow N^{[a,b]}

are isomorphisms.
Therefore, we see that

exists together with natural isomorphisms

for all pairs of integers
a\leqslant b
.

Using property (3) of
Theorem

3.41
again,
each

has a natural map to
\tau^{[a,b]}M
compatible with
Postnikov truncations. Therefore, we obtain a natural map

whose induced map on

coincides with the map induced by

for any

Hence property (1) of
Theorem

3.41

implies that the map

satisfies the first half of property (1) here.

Therefore, we see that

and

.
Moreover, the second half of property (1) also follows by considering the long exact sequence associated with

Finally, properties (2)–(3) follow formally from property (1).
∎

Theorem 3.49

(Ekedahl’s inequality

)

Proposition 3.50

(
[
10
, Proposition III.4.1]
,
[
10
, Theorem IV.1.2(i)]
)
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{DG}r_{c}^{b}({R})
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)
The cohomology groups of the total complex
\mathrm{Tot}(M)\in\mathcal{D}(W)
are torsion-free, and the Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence (see
2.36
)

E_{1}^{i,j}=\mathrm{H}^{j}({R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}M)^{i}\;\Rightarrow\;\mathrm{H}^{i+j}(k\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{W}\mathrm{Tot}(M))

degenerates at the

(2)
For every

(3)

denote the dimension of
\mathrm{H}^{n}(\mathrm{Tot}(M))[1/p]
. Then

\sum_{i+j=n}h^{i,j}=b_{n}.

Proof.

A direct computation shows that

Therefore, by Ekedahl’s inequality

By the universal coefficient theorem and the spectral sequence of

Condition (1) is precisely the statement that both inequalities are equalities for all

.
Thus condition (1) is also equivalent to condition (3).

Remark 4.3

Let us mention that a more general descent statement for the de Rham–Witt complex is known,

Construction 4.4

(de Rham–Witt cohomology of geometric Artin stacks)

Let

When

In general, suppose

-geometric stacks, hence their de Rham–Witt cohomologies are “computed” by induction.
Moreover, we have an equivalence in

Let us establish some easy properties of the above construction.
We say that an object in
\mathcal{DG}r({R})
is grading-left bounded by
0
if it has no negative grading pieces.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X/k
be a smooth geometric Artin stack. Then

W\Omega^{\bullet}(X/k)\in\widehat{\mathcal{DG}r}({R})
and it is grading-left bounded by
0
.

Proof.

When
X
is an affine scheme, the completeness of its de Rham–Witt complex
follows from
Theorem

2.34
.
The bound on the grading follows from the definition.
The completeness for general
X
follows from
Proposition

2.29
,
whereas the bound on the grading follows from the fact that limits are computed “grading-wise”.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

For each smooth algebra
A/k
, the multiplication on
W\Omega^{\bullet}_{A/k}

satisfies the properties listed in
3.22

(see
[
12
, Théorème I.1.3, Proposition I.2.18]
),
making it an associative algebra in the symmetric monoidal category of
graded left
{R}
-modules. Since
W\Omega^{\bullet}_{A/k}
is concentrated in cohomological
degree
0
, and
-{\scriptstyle\text{✴}}^{\mathrm{L}}-
preserves the connective part, we see that

W\Omega^{\bullet}_{-/k}
defines a sheaf on
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}

valued in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}(\mathcal{DG}r({R}))
.
Finally, since
W\Omega^{\bullet}_{A/k}
is complete, we see that

W\Omega^{\bullet}_{-/k}
defines a sheaf on
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}

valued in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}(\widehat{\mathcal{DG}r}({R}))
.
Therefore, we may regard
W\Omega^{\bullet}(-/k)

as a sheaf on
\mathrm{SmStk}_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}
taking values in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras in the symmetric monoidal category

\widehat{\mathcal{DG}r}({R})
.

Lemma 4.7
.

There is an equivalence of functors

{R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{{R}}W\Omega^{\bullet}(-/k)\xrightarrow{\cong}(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathbb{L}_{-/k})\colon\mathrm{SmStk}_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{DG}r(k[d]/d^{2}).

Proof.

Consider the following natural map

{R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{{R}}W\Omega^{\bullet}(-/k)={R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{{R}}\mathrm{R\Gamma}(\mathrm{Sm}^{\mathrm{op}}_{k,/-},W\Omega^{\bullet})\to\mathrm{R\Gamma}(\mathrm{Sm}^{\mathrm{op}}_{k,/-},{R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{{R}}W\Omega^{\bullet}).

We have the following identification of the right-hand side:

\mathrm{R\Gamma}(\mathrm{Sm}^{\mathrm{op}}_{k,/-},{R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{{R}}W\Omega^{\bullet})\cong\mathrm{R\Gamma}(\mathrm{Sm}^{\mathrm{op}}_{k,/-},\Omega^{\bullet})\cong(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathbb{L}_{-/k}).

Here the first identification is Illusie–Raynaud’s
Theorem

2.34
, and the second identification follows from

[
1
, Theorem 2.5.(1) and Construction 2.7]
.
This map is an equivalence: since
{R}_{1}
is a perfect right
{R}
-module, we know that

{R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}(\text{-})
commutes with taking limits.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

For each smooth algebra
A/k
, the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-structure on
W\Omega^{\bullet}_{A/k}

gives rise to a multiplication on
{R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}W\Omega^{\bullet}_{A/k}\cong\Omega^{\bullet}_{A/k}
. Tracing through the definition, we see that the multiplication
is none other than the wedge product in Hodge cohomology (see
[
12
, Théorème I.1.3]
).
Therefore, the equivalence in
Lemma

4.7
is
automatically promoted to an equivalence of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras in
\mathcal{DG}r(k[d]/d^{2})
.

For a class of “nice” smooth geometric Artin stacks, their de Rham–Witt cohomology
satisfies a finiteness property.
Recall that a geometric Artin stack
X
is called quasi-compact if there is a smooth surjection

\mathrm{Spec}(A)\twoheadrightarrow X
from an affine scheme; and a morphism of geometric Artin stacks

X\to Y
is called quasi-compact if for any map
\mathrm{Spec}(A)\to Y
, the fiber product

X\times_{Y}\mathrm{Spec}(A)
is quasi-compact, see
[
14
, Definition A.1.20]
.
Also recall that a geometric Artin stack
X
over
k
is called quasi-separated if the diagonal
morphism
X\to X\times_{k}X
is quasi-compact; this is what is called
0
-quasi-separated
in
[
14
, Definition A.1.21]
.
Finally, we remind the reader that a smooth geometric Artin stack
X/k
is called
Hodge-proper

if
\mathrm{H}^{j}(X,\wedge^{i}\mathbb{L}_{X/k})
is finite dimensional for all
i
and
j
,
cf.
[
13
, Definition 0.2.1]
.

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\wedge^{j}\mathbb{L}_{X/k})=0

To that end, we argue by induction on the geometricity.
For
(-1)

all

to be the dimension of

Suppose the claim is verified for all quasi-compact quasi-separated smooth
(n-1)
-geometric Artin stacks,
and let

Then there exists an

\mathrm{H}^{i}(U^{(m)},\wedge^{\geq N_{i}^{(m)}}\mathbb{L}_{U^{(m)}/k})=0

The last statement follows from the combination of
Lemma

3.48

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
X,Y
be smooth geometric Artin stacks over
k
. Assume that one of the following holds:

1.

Both
X
and
Y
are quasi-compact and quasi-separated;

2.

X
or

is quasi-compact, quasi-separated, and Hodge-proper.

Then the following natural map is an isomorphism:

W\Omega^{\bullet}(X/k){\scriptstyle\widehat{\text{✴}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}}}W\Omega^{\bullet}(Y/k)\xrightarrow[\cong]{p_{1}^{*}\cup p_{2}^{*}}W\Omega^{\bullet}(X\times Y/k).

Here we are using the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-structure on
W\Omega^{\bullet}(X\times Y/k)

(see
Remark

4.6
) to define the natural map.

Proof.

We claim that the left-hand side is grading-left bounded by
0
.
Note that
W\Omega^{\bullet}(-/k)
is always grading-left bounded by
0
, and the completion
of an object that is grading-left bounded by
0
is also grading-left bounded by
0
.
Therefore, it suffices to know that
-{\scriptstyle\text{✴}}^{\mathrm{L}}-
preserves the full subcategory of
\mathcal{DG}r({R})

spanned by objects that are grading-left bounded by
0
.

Since this property is preserved under colimits, we may further reduce to considering
the full subcategory spanned by objects that are cohomologically bounded above and grading-left bounded by
0
.
Now the claim follows from the fact that such objects can be represented by complexes whose
terms are direct sums of
{R}(i)
with
i\leqslant 0
.

Therefore our statement concerns a map between two complete objects that are grading-left bounded by
0
.
By
Proposition

2.37
, in order to show that the map is an isomorphism, it suffices to check that
it becomes an isomorphism after applying
{R}_{1}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}-
.
Using
Lemma

4.7
and
Remark

4.8
,
we are reduced to showing that the similarly defined map on Hodge cohomology is an isomorphism,
which is the content of the lemma below.
∎

Lemma 4.17
.

In the setting of
Theorem

4.16
, the following natural map is an isomorphism:

\wedge^{\bullet}\mathbb{L}_{-/k}(X)\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{k}\wedge^{\bullet}\mathbb{L}_{-/k}(Y)\xrightarrow[\cong]{p_{1}^{*}\cup p_{2}^{*}}\wedge^{\bullet}\mathbb{L}_{-/k}(X\times Y).

Proof.

We note that working over a field
k
implies that for any bounded-below object

M\in\mathcal{DG}r(k)
, the functor
M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{k}-
commutes with the totalization
of uniformly bounded-below objects.

Let us first prove case (1), so suppose that both
X
and
Y
are quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
We proceed by induction on the geometricity of
Y
. Suppose
Y
is
n
-geometric. Then our assumption
implies that we can choose an
(n-1)
-representable smooth surjection
\pi\colon U\twoheadrightarrow Y

from a smooth affine scheme
U
, and all the terms

U^{({\ast})}\coloneqq U^{\times_{Y}(\ast+1)}
in the Čech nerve of
\pi

are quasi-compact, quasi-separated smooth
(n-1)
-geometric Artin stacks.
By the fact recalled in the first paragraph, we are reduced to showing the claim in the case
where
Y
is replaced by
U^{({\ast})}
. Therefore we are reduced to the case where
Y

is affine. Repeating the same argument, we may further reduce to the case where
X

is also affine, which is well known.

Next we reduce case (2) to case (1). Assume that
X
is quasi-compact, quasi-separated,
and Hodge-proper. By mimicking the argument in the previous paragraph, we may reduce to the case
where
Y
is a smooth scheme. Write

Y=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_{\lambda}

as an increasing union of its quasi-compact open subschemes
U_{\lambda}
.
The Hodge cohomology of
Y
(resp.
X\times Y
) is the cofiltered limit of the Hodge cohomologies
of
U_{\lambda}
(resp.
X\times U_{\lambda}
).
The assumption that
X
is Hodge-proper implies that tensoring with its Hodge cohomology
commutes with cofiltered limits. Therefore we may replace

by one of the

,
which is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. Now we are in case (1), and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 5.1

There are natural isomorphisms of schemes

Lemma 5.10
