CuriosAI Submission to the CASTLE Challenge at EgoVis 2026
Yuto Kanda, Hayato Tanoue, Takayuki Hori

Abstract
CASTLE 2026 asks 185 185 multiple-choice questions over 600 + 600+ hours of synchronised multi-view egocentric video. We explore two approaches on top of a shared multimodal preprocessing layer (per-person timelines, speaker-resolved transcripts, multi-VLM caption ensembles, etc.). Approach A (SVA: Search–Verify–Answer) is a three-stage pipeline that hierarchically narrows to a primary window, verifies sub-windows with a VLM under four anti-confabulation rules, and fuses evidence with an LLM judge under an evidence-priority hierarchy. Approach B (TMKG: Temporal–Multimodal–Knowledge–Graph) is the contrast: it builds a temporal multimodal knowledge graph, locates a primary cell via graph search, and produces the final answer with a single grounded VLM. SVA reaches a leaderboard accuracy of 0.50 0.50 and is our final challenge submission; TMKG reaches 0.35 0.35 .

Retrieval.

A query parser extracts date, time, person, place, object, action, and

BM25 and e5-large-v2 are run over Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct summaries of the

The selected cell’s multi-camera video frames and bundled multi-camera
evidence (captions, transcripts, tags) are passed with the question and
choices to a single Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct, which emits the

three-stage fallback (alternative VLM

Conclusion.

At CASTLE 2026 scale, the
0.15
SVA–TMKG gap suggests that
disciplined clip-level verification (abstention, count localisation,
evidence grounding, no-echo) is a layer worth adding on top of any
retrieval substrate, at the cost of noticeably more LLM/VLM calls.
Since both pipelines are bounded by the same Search-stage
cell-localisation failures, combining higher-precision retrieval with
this discipline is the natural next direction.
