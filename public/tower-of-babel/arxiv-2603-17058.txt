Asymmetric Nash Seeking via Best–Response Maps: Global Linear Convergence and Robustness to Inexact Reaction Models
Mahdis Rabbani, Navid Mojahed, Shima Nazari

Abstract
Nash equilibria provide a principled framework for modeling interactions in multi-agent decision-making and control. However, many equilibrium-seeking methods implicitly assume that each agent has access to the other agents’ objectives and constraints, an assumption that is often unrealistic in practice. This letter studies a class of asymmetric-information two-player constrained games with decoupled feasible sets, in which Player 1 knows its own objective and constraints while Player 2 is available only through a best-response map. For this class of games, we propose an asymmetric projected gradient descent–best response iteration that does not require full mutual knowledge of both players’ optimization problems. Under suitable regularity conditions, we establish existence and uniqueness of the Nash equilibrium and prove global linear convergence of the proposed iteration when the best-response map is exact. Recognizing that best-response maps are often learned or estimated, we further analyze the inexact case and show that, when the approximation error is uniformly bounded by ε \varepsilon , the iterates enter an explicit O ⁡ ( ε ) O(\varepsilon) neighborhood of the true Nash equilibrium. Numerical results on a benchmark game corroborate the predicted convergence behavior and error scaling.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

•

We introduce a class of asymmetric-information two-player constrained games with decoupled feasible sets, in which the opponent is represented directly through a best-response map rather than an explicit objective model, and establish existence and uniqueness of Nash equilibrium for this class under regularity assumptions.

•

We propose an asymmetric projected gradient descent–best response iteration and show that, under stronger regularity assumptions, it converges globally and linearly to the unique Nash equilibrium.

•

We analyze the practically important case of an approximate best-response map and prove robustness: under a uniform approximation error bound, the proposed iteration enters an explicit

Taken together, these results turn the best-response-map viewpoint from a modeling abstraction into a provably convergent equilibrium-seeking framework for constrained games with asymmetric information.

II
Problem Formulation

We consider a constrained two-player game with decision variables

x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and

x_{2}\in\mathrm{X}_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
for Player 1 and Player 2, respectively.
The feasible sets are decoupled, so the joint feasible set is

\mathrm{X}\triangleq\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2},

and there is no explicit coupling through shared constraints. Thus, the interaction enters through the cost functions and the induced reaction behavior rather than through joint feasibility conditions.

Player 1 is described by a cost function

J_{1}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),

(1)

which may depend on both players’ decisions.
In contrast, Player 2 is represented, from the standpoint of Player 1, through its best-response correspondence

\mathrm{BR}_{2}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathrm{X}_{2},\quad\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})\triangleq\arg\min_{x_{2}\in\mathrm{X}_{2}}J_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}),

(2)

for some objective
J_{2}
that need not be known to Player 1.
Accordingly, the algorithmic development relies only on access to
\mathrm{BR}_{2}(\cdot)
, or later, an approximation thereof.

Problem 1
(
Asymmetric-Information Two-Player Game
)

Given the feasible sets
\mathrm{X}_{1},\mathrm{X}_{2}
, Player 1’s cost
J_{1}
in (
1
), and Player 2’s best-response correspondence
\mathrm{BR}_{2}(\cdot)
in (
2
), consider the two-player game in which Player 1 solves

\min_{x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),

while Player 2 simultaneously reacts according to
x_{2}\in\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})
.

Definition 1
(Nash equilibrium)

A pair
(x_{1}^{\star},x_{2}^{\star})\in\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}
is a Nash equilibrium of Problem
1
if

x_{1}^{\star}\in\arg\min_{x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}^{\star}),\qquad x_{2}^{\star}\in\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}^{\star}).

The next section characterizes conditions under which Problem
1
admits a Nash equilibrium and when that equilibrium is unique.

III
Existence & Uniqueness of the Equilibrium

This section investigates existence and uniqueness of a Nash equilibrium for Problem
1
. We first establish existence under general regularity conditions that allow Player 2’s best-response map to be set-valued, and then impose stronger assumptions under which the best response becomes single-valued and the equilibrium is unique.

Assumption A.1

The feasible sets
\mathrm{X}_{1}
and
\mathrm{X}_{2}
are nonempty, convex, and compact.

Assumption A.2

The function
J_{1}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous on
\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}
. Moreover, for every fixed
x_{2}\in\mathrm{X}_{2}
, the function
J_{1}(\cdot,x_{2})
is convex on
\mathrm{X}_{1}
.

Assumption A.3

For every
x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}
, the correspondence
\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})\subseteq\mathrm{X}_{2}
is nonempty, convex, and compact, and
\mathrm{BR}_{2}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathrm{X}_{2}
is upper hemicontinuous.

Theorem 1
(Existence of Nash equilibrium)

Under Assumptions
A.1
–
A.3
, Problem
1
admits at least one Nash equilibrium.

Proof:

Define Player 1’s best-response correspondence

\mathrm{BR}_{1}(x_{2})\triangleq\arg\min_{x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),\qquad x_{2}\in\mathrm{X}_{2}.

(3)

Fix any
x_{2}\in\mathrm{X}_{2}
. By Assumption
A.1
,
\mathrm{X}_{1}
is nonempty and compact, and by Assumption
A.2
,
J_{1}(\cdot,x_{2})
is continuous. Hence, by the Weierstrass extreme value theorem,
\mathrm{BR}_{1}(x_{2})
is nonempty and compact. Moreover, by convexity of
J_{1}(\cdot,x_{2})
(Assumption
A.2
), the set of minimizers
\mathrm{BR}_{1}(x_{2})
is convex.

Next, Assumption
A.2
ensures that
J_{1}
is jointly continuous on
\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}
, while Assumption
A.1
guarantees that
\mathrm{X}_{1}
is compact. Therefore, Berge’s maximum theorem implies that
\mathrm{BR}_{1}:\mathrm{X}_{2}\rightrightarrows\mathrm{X}_{1}
is upper hemicontinuous and compact-valued.

Now define the product correspondence
F:\mathrm{X}\rightrightarrows\mathrm{X}
on
\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}
by

F(x_{1},x_{2})\triangleq\mathrm{BR}_{1}(x_{2})\times\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}).

By the preceding arguments and Assumption
A.3
, for every
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathrm{X}
the set
F(x_{1},x_{2})
is nonempty, convex, and compact, and
F
is upper hemicontinuous. Since
\mathrm{X}
is nonempty, convex, and compact by Assumption
A.1
, Kakutani’s fixed-point theorem guarantees the existence of
(x_{1}^{\star},x_{2}^{\star})\in\mathrm{X}
such that

(x_{1}^{\star},x_{2}^{\star})\in F(x_{1}^{\star},x_{2}^{\star}),

i.e.,
x_{1}^{\star}\in\mathrm{BR}_{1}(x_{2}^{\star})
and
x_{2}^{\star}\in\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}^{\star})
. By the definition of
\mathrm{BR}_{1}
in (
3
), the first inclusion is equivalent to

x_{1}^{\star}\in\arg\min_{x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}^{\star})
, and together with
x_{2}^{\star}\in\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}^{\star})
this matches Definition
1
. Therefore,
(x_{1}^{\star},x_{2}^{\star})
is a Nash equilibrium of Problem
1
.
∎

To state a uniqueness condition aligned with our subsequent convergence analysis, we impose the following stronger assumptions.

Assumption B.1

The function
J_{1}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous on
\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}
. Moreover, for every fixed
x_{2}\in\mathrm{X}_{2}
, the function
J_{1}(\cdot,x_{2})
is differentiable and
\mu
-strongly convex on
\mathrm{X}_{1}
for some
\mu>0
.

Assumption B.2

For every fixed
x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}
, the mapping
x_{2}\mapsto\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})
is Lipschitz on
\mathrm{X}_{2}
with constant
L_{12}\geq 0
, i.e.,
\forall x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathrm{X}_{2}
,

\|\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},y_{2})\|\leq L_{12}\|x_{2}-y_{2}\|.

Assumption B.3

For every
x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}
, the best response map
\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})
is single-valued.

Assumption B.4

The (single-valued) best response map
\mathrm{BR}_{2}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\to\mathrm{X}_{2}
is Lipschitz on
\mathrm{X}_{1}
with constant
L_{2}\geq 0
.

Theorem 2
(Uniqueness of Nash equilibrium)

Suppose Assumptions
A.1
and
B.1
–
B.4
hold. If
\mu>L_{12}L_{2}
, then the Nash equilibrium of Problem
1
is unique.

Proof:

Assumption
B.1
implies Assumption
A.2
. In addition, Assumptions
B.3
and
B.4
imply that
\mathrm{BR}_{2}
is a continuous single-valued map, hence upper hemicontinuous as a correspondence with nonempty, convex, and compact singleton values. Therefore, Assumption
A.3
also holds. Together with Assumption
A.1
, existence follows from Theorem
1
.

To prove uniqueness, let
(a_{1},b_{1})
and
(a_{2},b_{2})
be two Nash equilibria of Problem
1
. By Definition
1
and Assumption
B.3
,

b_{1}=\mathrm{BR}_{2}(a_{1}),\qquad b_{2}=\mathrm{BR}_{2}(a_{2}).

(4)

Since
a_{1}
minimizes
J_{1}(\cdot,b_{1})
over the convex set
\mathrm{X}_{1}
, the first-order optimality condition for convex constrained problems yields

\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{1},b_{1}),\,a_{2}-a_{1}\rangle\geq 0.

Similarly, optimality of
a_{2}
for
J_{1}(\cdot,b_{2})
implies

\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{2},b_{2}),\,a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle\geq 0.

Adding the two inequalities gives

\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{1},b_{1})-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{2},b_{2}),\,a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle\leq 0.

(5)

Add and subtract
\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{2},b_{1})
inside the inner product in (
5
). By
\mu
-strong convexity of
J_{1}(\cdot,b_{1})
(Assumption
B.1
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{1},b_{1})-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{2},b_{1}),

\displaystyle a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle

(6)

\displaystyle\geq\mu\|a_{1}-a_{2}\|^{2}.

For the remaining term, Cauchy–Schwarz yields

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle J_{1}(a_{2},b_{1})-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{2},b_{2}),\,a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\|\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{2},b_{1})-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{2},b_{2})\|\,\|a_{1}-a_{2}\|.

Using Assumption
B.2
and (
4
) together with Assumption
B.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(a_{2},b_{1})-\nabla_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle J_{1}(a_{2},b_{2}),\,a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle

(7)

\displaystyle\geq-L_{12}\|b_{1}-b_{2}\|\,\|a_{1}-a_{2}\|

\displaystyle\geq-L_{12}L_{2}\|a_{1}-a_{2}\|^{2}.

Combining (
5
)–(
7
) yields

(\mu-L_{12}L_{2})\|a_{1}-a_{2}\|^{2}\leq 0.

If
\mu>L_{12}L_{2}
, then
a_{1}=a_{2}
. Substituting into (
4
), and given Assumption
B.3
, gives
b_{1}=b_{2}
, hence
(a_{1},b_{1})=(a_{2},b_{2})
. Therefore, the Nash equilibrium is unique.
∎

The same dominance condition
\mu>L_{12}L_{2}
also induces a monotonicity margin that will be central to the convergence analysis of the iterative scheme developed next.

IV
Algorithm & Global Linear Convergence to the Equilibrium

Under Assumptions
A.1
and
B.1
–
B.4
, and whenever
\mu>L_{12}L_{2}
, Problem
1
admits a unique Nash equilibrium by Theorem
2
. We now study a projected best-response/gradient iteration and show that it converges globally and linearly to this equilibrium.

Assumption C.1

For every fixed
x_{2}\in\mathrm{X}_{2}
, the function
J_{1}(\cdot,x_{2})
is
L_{1}
-smooth on
\mathrm{X}_{1}
, i.e.,
\forall x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}
,

\|\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},x_{2})\|\leq L_{1}\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|.

We consider the projected best-response/gradient iteration

\displaystyle x_{2}^{k}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}^{k}),

(8)

\displaystyle x_{1}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\!\left(x_{1}^{k}-\alpha\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1}^{k},x_{2}^{k})\right),

where
\Pi_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\!\left(\cdot\right)
denotes the Euclidean projection onto
\mathrm{X}_{1}
.
Since
\mathrm{X}_{1}
is closed and convex (Assumption
A.1
), the projection is nonexpansive:

\|\Pi_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\!\left(u\right)-\Pi_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\!\left(v\right)\|\leq\|u-v\|,\qquad\forall u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}.

(9)

Theorem 3
(Global linear convergence)

Suppose Assumptions
A.1
,
B.1
–
B.4
, and
C.1
hold.
Let

m\triangleq\mu-L_{12}L_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{G}\triangleq L_{1}+L_{12}L_{2},

and note that
m>0
follows from
\mu>L_{12}L_{2}
.
Then, for any stepsize
\alpha
satisfying

0<\alpha<\frac{2m}{L_{G}^{2}}=\frac{2(\mu-L_{12}L_{2})}{(L_{1}+L_{12}L_{2})^{2}},

the iterates generated by (
8
) satisfy

\|x_{1}^{k}-x_{1}^{\star}\|\leq\rho(\alpha)^{k}\|x_{1}^{0}-x_{1}^{\star}\|,

\|x_{2}^{k}-x_{2}^{\star}\|\leq L_{2}\,\rho(\alpha)^{k}\|x_{1}^{0}-x_{1}^{\star}\|,

where
(x_{1}^{\star},x_{2}^{\star})
is the unique Nash equilibrium of Problem
1
and

\rho(\alpha)\triangleq\sqrt{1-2\alpha m+\alpha^{2}L_{G}^{2}}\in(0,1).

Proof:

Define the map
T:\mathrm{X}_{1}\to\mathrm{X}_{1}
by

T(x_{1}):=\Pi_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\!\left(x_{1}-\alpha\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}\big(x_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})\big)\right),

(10)

and define the operator
G:\mathrm{X}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
as

G(x_{1}):=\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}\big(x_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})\big).

For any
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}
, add and subtract
\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}))
to obtain

\displaystyle\|G(

\displaystyle x_{1})-G(y_{1})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}))-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}))\|

\displaystyle+\|\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}))-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(y_{1}))\|.

By Assumption
C.1
, the first term is bounded by
L_{1}\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|
.
By Assumptions
B.2
and
B.4
, the second term is bounded by

L_{12}\|\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})-\mathrm{BR}_{2}(y_{1})\|\leq L_{12}L_{2}\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|
.
Therefore,
G
is Lipschitz on
\mathrm{X}_{1}
with constant
L_{G}=L_{1}+L_{12}L_{2}
, i.e.,

\|G(x_{1})-G(y_{1})\|\leq L_{G}\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|.

(11)

Next, using the same add–subtract decomposition and Assumption
B.1
, we have

\displaystyle\langle

\displaystyle G(x_{1})-G(y_{1}),\,x_{1}-y_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}))-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})),\,x_{1}-y_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}))-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(y_{1})),\,x_{1}-y_{1}\rangle.

The first inner product is lower bounded by
\mu\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|^{2}
. For the second, Cauchy–Schwarz together with Assumptions
B.2
and
B.4
yields

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}))-\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(y_{1},

\displaystyle\mathrm{BR}_{2}(y_{1})),\,x_{1}-y_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-L_{12}L_{2}\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|^{2}.

Thus,
G
is strongly monotone on
\mathrm{X}_{1}
with constant
m=\mu-L_{12}L_{2}>0
, i.e.,

\langle G(x_{1})-G(y_{1}),\,x_{1}-y_{1}\rangle\geq m\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|^{2}.

(12)

By nonexpansiveness of the projection (
9
),

\|T(x_{1})-T(y_{1})\|\leq\|(x_{1}-y_{1})-\alpha(G(x_{1})-G(y_{1}))\|.

Expanding the square and applying the Lipschitz and strong monotonicity bounds in (
11
) and (
12
) gives

\displaystyle\|T(x_{1})-T(y_{1})\|^{2}\leq\big(1-2

\displaystyle\alpha m+\alpha^{2}L_{G}^{2}\big)\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|^{2}

(13)

\displaystyle=:q(\alpha)\|x_{1}-y_{1}\|^{2}.

For
0<\alpha<2m/L_{G}^{2}
, we have
q(\alpha)\in(0,1)
, hence
T
is a contraction with modulus
\rho(\alpha)=\sqrt{q(\alpha)}\in(0,1)
.
Therefore, by the Banach fixed-point theorem,
T
has a unique fixed point
x_{1}^{*}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}
and the iterates satisfy

\|x_{1}^{k}-x_{1}^{*}\|\leq\rho(\alpha)^{k}\|x_{1}^{0}-x_{1}^{*}\|.

(14)

Let
x_{2}^{*}:=\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}^{*})
. Since
\mathrm{BR}_{2}
is Lipschitz (
B.4
),

\left\lVert x_{2}^{k}-x_{2}^{*}\right\rVert=\left\lVert\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}^{k})-\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}^{*})\right\rVert\leq L_{2}\,\left\lVert x_{1}^{k}-x_{1}^{*}\right\rVert.

Combining with (
14
) gives

\left\lVert x_{2}^{k}-x_{2}^{*}\right\rVert\leq L_{2}\,\rho(\alpha)^{k}\left\lVert x_{1}^{0}-x_{1}^{*}\right\rVert.

It remains to show that
x_{1}^{*}
minimizes
J_{1}(\cdot,x_{2}^{*})
over
\mathrm{X}_{1}
.
From
x_{1}^{*}=T(x_{1}^{*})
, we have

x_{1}^{*}=\Pi_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\!\left(x_{1}^{*}-\alpha\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})\right).

By the characterization of Euclidean projection onto a closed convex set,
y=\Pi_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\!\left(z\right)
if and only if

\langle z-y,\,x-y\rangle\leq 0,\qquad\forall x\in\mathrm{X}_{1}.

Applying this with
y=x_{1}^{*}
and
z=x_{1}^{*}-\alpha\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})
yields

\langle\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}),\,x-x_{1}^{*}\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall x\in\mathrm{X}_{1}.

Since
J_{1}(\cdot,x_{2}^{*})
is convex and differentiable (
B.1
), this first-order condition is sufficient for optimality, hence

x_{1}^{*}\in\arg\min_{x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}^{*}).

Together with
x_{2}^{*}=\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1}^{*})
, Definition
1
implies that
(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})
is a Nash equilibrium. By uniqueness (Theorem
2
),
(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})=(x_{1}^{\star},x_{2}^{\star})
, which completes the proof.
∎

V
Inexact Best Response & Robustness of the Algorithm

The convergence result in Section
IV
assumes access to the exact best-response map
\mathrm{BR}_{2}
. In practice, however, an agent typically has access only to an approximation or estimate of its opponent’s reaction behavior. We therefore study the robustness of the projected best-response/gradient iteration when
\mathrm{BR}_{2}
is replaced by an inexact map.

Let
\widehat{\mathrm{BR}}_{2}:\mathrm{X}_{1}\to\mathrm{X}_{2}
denote an approximation of
\mathrm{BR}_{2}
, and assume a uniform error bound: there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\|\widehat{\mathrm{BR}}_{2}(x_{1})-\mathrm{BR}_{2}(x_{1})\|\leq\varepsilon,\quad\forall x_{1}\in\mathrm{X}_{1}.

(15)

We consider the inexact variant of (
8
):

\displaystyle x_{2}^{k}=\widehat{\mathrm{BR}}_{2}(x_{1}^{k}),

(16)

\displaystyle x_{1}^{k+1}=\Pi_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\!\left(x_{1}^{k}-\alpha\nabla_{x_{1}}J_{1}(x_{1}^{k},x_{2}^{k})\right).

Theorem 4
(Robustness to inexact best response)

Let
(x_{1}^{\star},x_{2}^{\star})\in\mathrm{X}
denote the unique Nash equilibrium of Problem
1
under Assumptions
A.1
,
B.1
–
B.4
, and
C.1
, and suppose
\mu>L_{12}L_{2}
. Let

m\triangleq\mu-L_{12}L_{2},\qquad L_{G}\triangleq L_{1}+L_{12}L_{2},

and choose
\alpha\in(0,2m/L_{G}^{2})
. If
\widehat{\mathrm{BR}}_{2}
satisfies (
15
), then the iterates (
16
) satisfy, for all
k\geq 0
,

\|x_{1}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{\star}\|\leq\rho(\alpha)\|x_{1}^{k}-x_{1}^{\star}\|+\alpha L_{12}\varepsilon,

(17)

where

\rho(\alpha)=\sqrt{1-2\alpha m+\alpha^{2}L_{G}^{2}}\in(0,1).

Consequently,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|x_{1}^{k}-x_{1}^{\star}\|\leq\frac{\alpha L_{12}}{1-\rho(\alpha)}\,\varepsilon.

(18)

Moreover, the sequence
x_{2}^{k}=\widehat{\mathrm{BR}}_{2}(x_{1}^{k})
satisfies

\|x_{2}^{k}-x_{2}^{\star}\|\leq L_{2}\|x_{1}^{k}-x_{1}^{\star}\|+\varepsilon,

(19)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|x_{2}^{k}-x_{2}^{\star}\|\leq\big(L_{2}\frac{\alpha L_{12}}{1-\rho(\alpha)}+1\big)\varepsilon.

(20)

Proof:

Let

\displaystyle\|\widehat{T}

VI
Numerical Example

We illustrate the theoretical results on a one-dimensional
tug-of-war
cart in which two agents apply opposing longitudinal forces to a point mass with viscous drag. Player 1 selects an open-loop control sequence
u
, while Player 2 reacts through a best-response map. The example is designed to instantiate the assumptions of Theorems
1
–
4
and to illustrate exact convergence, robustness to inexact best-response information, and the predicted
O(\varepsilon)
neighborhood scaling.

Let
z_{t}=[p_{t},\;\dot{p}_{t}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
denote position and velocity of the cart at time step
t
. With sampling time
\Delta t
, mass
m
, and viscous drag coefficient
b
, the discrete-time dynamics are

z_{t+1}=A_{d}z_{t}+B_{u}u_{t}+B_{v}v_{t},\qquad t=0,\ldots,N-1,

where
z_{0}=[0,0]^{\top}
and

A_{d}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\Delta t\\
0&1-\Delta t(b/m)\end{bmatrix},\qquad B_{u}=-B_{v}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\Delta t/m\end{bmatrix}.

Stacking the horizon variables as

u=[u_{0},\ldots,u_{N-1}]^{\top},\qquad v=[v_{0},\ldots,v_{N-1}]^{\top},

results in the predicted state trajectory of the affine form

Z(u,v)=S_{0}+S_{u}u+S_{v}v.

Player 1 selects

\displaystyle J_{1}(u,v)=\frac{q_{u}}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}Z(u,v)^{\top}QZ(u,v)

\displaystyle-r_{\mathrm{pull}}\,C_{x}^{\top}Z(u,v)+\gamma\,u^{\top}v,

Player 2 is modeled through a stagewise best-response map

\mathrm{BR}_{2}(u)=v_{\max}\tanh\!\big(\kappa\,(v_{\mathrm{bar}}+c_{\mathrm{react}}\,u)\big),

which captures a saturating reaction. Consistent with the asymmetric-information formulation of Problem

and

VII
Conclusion

This paper studied a class of constrained two-player games under asymmetric information, where Player 1 knows its own objective and constraints while Player 2 is represented only through a best-response map. We established existence of a Nash equilibrium, derived a sufficient uniqueness condition, and proposed a projected gradient descent–best response iteration with global linear convergence to the unique equilibrium when the best-response map is exact. For the practically relevant inexact case, we showed that a uniformly bounded best-response approximation error yields an explicit
O(\varepsilon)
neighborhood bound around the true Nash equilibrium. Numerical results corroborated the predicted exact-case convergence and inexact robustness behavior. Overall, the paper shows that equilibrium seeking can remain both analyzable and robust even without an explicit opponent model.

Several directions remain for future work. One is to extend the framework beyond decoupled feasible sets to games with coupled constraints, which arise naturally in shared-resource and safety-critical multi-agent settings. Another is to relax the sufficient condition
\mu>L_{12}L_{2}
, which can be restrictive in strongly coupled interactions.
