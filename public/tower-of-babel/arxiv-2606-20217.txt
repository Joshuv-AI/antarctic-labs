Abstract
. IIn this paper, we study a class of quasilinear elliptic problems involving the ( 1 , q ) − (1,q)- Laplacian operator and a discontinuous superlinear nonlinearity governed by the Heaviside function. The main difficulty of the problem arises from the presence of the 1 1 -Laplacian operator, whose natural setting is the Space of Functions of Bounded Variation. Our approach is based on an approximation method involving ( p , q ) − (p,q)- Laplacian problems as p → 1 + p\to 1^{+} . As a consequence, we prove the existence of a nontrivial and nonnegative solution belonging to W 0 1 , p ​ ( Ω ) W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega) , in an appropriate weak sense. Moreover, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of the solutions as β → 0 + \beta\to 0^{+} , showing that the family of solutions converges to a solution of the limit problem without discontinuity.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we deal with the solutions of the following quasilinear elliptic problem involving the
(1,q)
-Laplacian

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rll}-\Delta_{1}u-\Delta_{q}u=&\hskip-7.11317ptH(u-\beta)\left|u\right|^{s-2}u&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u=&\hskip-7.11317pt0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\end{cases}

(P)

where the
1
-Laplacian operator is formally defined by
\Delta_{1}=\mbox{div}\left(\frac{Du}{\left|Du\right|}\right)
,
\Omega
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geq 2
,
\beta>0
is a real parameter,
1<p<q<s<q^{*}=\frac{Nq}{N-q}
, and
H
denotes the Heaviside function, which is defined as
H(t)=1
when
t\geq 0
and
H(t)=0
otherwise.

In the last three decades, problems involving the
1
-Laplacian operator have gained great interest and were subject of several research activities using different techniques. This operator, formally defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}u:=\mbox{div}\left(\dfrac{\nabla u}{\left|\nabla u\right|}\right),

naturally leads to the study of functions of bounded variation, where the lack of reflexivity introduces significant technical challenges. For instance, Chata, Pimenta and Segura de León investigated in
[
12
]
the existence of multiple positive solutions for a concave–convex type problem involving the
1-
Laplacian by means of approximation techniques. In
[
27
]
, Pimenta, Santos Júnior and Stapenhorst studied a quasilinear elliptic problem with discontinuous and critical nonlinearities, combining approximation methods with concentration-compactness arguments. Moreover, Pimenta, Carranza and Figueiredo in
[
25
]
obtained existence results for problems with critical concave–convex nonlinearities via concentration-compactness techniques. Finally, Martínez Aparicio, Oliva and Petitta developed in
[
5
]
a sub-supersolution method for
1-
Laplacian type problems, highlighting additional difficulties related to comparison principles and compactness issues.

Several approaches have been developed to deal with such problems. One of the most widely used methods, (which was originally proposed in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
16
]
), consists of approximating the problem by means of
p
-Laplacian as
p\to 1^{+}
, allowing the use of classical variational methods. This strategy has been successfully applied in various contexts, including in problems with power type nonlinearities (as in
[
22
]
) and with discontinuous nonlinearities (as in
[
27
,
28
]
).

More recently, Figueiredo, Pimenta and Winkert in
[
18
]
proposed the study of the
(p,q)-
Laplacian operator combining operators with different degrees of homogeneity. The limiting case
p\to 1^{+}
leads to the
(1,q)-
Laplacian operator, whose analysis is even more delicate due to the interplay between the nonsmooth nature of the
1-
Laplacian and the regularizing effect of the
q-
Laplacian.

In recent years, a considerable amount of work has been devoted to elliptic problems involving the
(p,q)-
Laplacian operator, addressing issues such as existence, multiplicity of solutions, qualitative properties and asymptotic behavior. We refer, for instance, to the works
[
13
,
17
,
23
,
24
]
, where different techniques have been successfully employed to treat superlinear and subcritical nonlinearities. However, despite these advances, problems involving the
(1,q)-
Laplacian setting remain largely unexplored.

Motivated by this context, and inspired by the problem proposed by Pimenta, Santos, and Santos Júnior in
[
26
]
, the present work investigates an elliptic problem involving the
(1,q)-
Laplacian operator with a discontinuous Heaviside-type nonlinearity, contributing to the development of the theory by combining approximation methods, nonsmooth analysis, and the theory of divergence-measure fields.

In this context, we aim to obtain solutions to the main problem of this paper in the following sense:

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the main functional framework and several auxiliary results that will be used throughout the paper. Since the operator involving the
1
-Laplacian naturally leads to gradients that are measures, the appropriate setting for our analysis is the space of functions of bounded variation. We also recall some basic tools from the theory of divergence-measure vector fields and nonsmooth critical point theory.

Throughout this work,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, denotes a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{N}
the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and by
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
the
(N-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. The outward unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
is denoted by
\nu
.

We will use the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
endowed with their standard norms.

First, let us observe that, by the boundedness of
\Omega
and since
p<q
, it holds that

\displaystyle W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)=W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega).

Therefore, we consider as functional space the Sobolev space
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
, endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle W^{1,q}_{0}}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{q}\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Finally, we recall that
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq r\leq q^{*}
, and compactly embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq r<q^{*}
, where
q^{*}=Nq/(N-q)
.

Nonsmooth Critical Point Theory

Since the nonlinear term appearing in the problem fail to be differentiable, we make use of tools from the critical point theory for locally Lipschitz functionals. This framework relies on the generalized gradient in the sense of Clarke
[
15
,
14
]
and has been further developed in subsequent works by Chang
[
11
]
and Grossinho and Tersian
[
20
]
.

Let
E
be a real Banach space and let
I:E\to\mathbb{R}
be a functional. We say that
I
is
locally Lipschitz continuous
when, for all
u\in E
, there exist a neighborhood
V
of
u
and a constant
M>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|I(v_{2})-I(v_{1})\right|\leq\left\|v_{2}-v_{1}\right\|

for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
.

The
generalized directional derivative
of
I
at
u
in the direction
v\in E
is defined by

\displaystyle I^{0}(u,v):=\limsup\limits_{h\to 0,\ t\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{I(u+h+tv)-I(u+h)}{t}.

Moreover,
I^{0}(u,\cdot)
is continuous, convex and its
subdifferential
at
z\in E
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial I^{0}(u,z):=\left\{\mu\in E^{*};\ I^{0}(u,v)\geq I^{0}(u,z)+\left<\mu,v-z\right>,\mbox{ for all }v\in E\right\},

where
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
is the duality pairing between
E^{*}
and
E
. The
generalized gradient
of
I
at
u
is the set

\displaystyle\partial I(u):=\left\{\mu\in E^{*};\ \left<\mu,v\right>\leq I^{0}(u,v),\mbox{ for all }v\in V\right\},

where this set convex, weak
∗
-compact and nonempty.

A point
u\in E
is called a
critical point
of
I
when
0\in\partial I(u)
and, in this case, the number
c=I(u)
is called a
critical value
.

To obtain critical points we use the nonsmooth version of the
Palais–Smale condition
. We say that
I
satisfies the
(PS)_{c}
condition if every sequence
(u_{n})\subset E
such that

\displaystyle I(u_{n})\to c\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lambda_{I}(u_{n})=\min\left\{\left\|\mu\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle E^{*}};\ \mu\in\partial I(u_{n})\right\}\to 0

possesses a strongly convergent subsequence.

The following nonsmooth version of the Mountain Pass Theorem will be used later to obtain the existence of critical points for the functional associated with our problem.

Theorem 3
.

(Nonsmooth Mountain Pass)
. Let
E
be a Banach Space and
I:E\to\mathbb{R}
be locally Lipschitz with
I(0)=0
. Suppose that there exist
\alpha>0
,
r>0
and
e\in E
such that

(a)

I(u)\geq\alpha
, when
\left\|u\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle E}=r
.

(b)

I(e)<0
and
\left\|e\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle E}>r
.

Define, for the set
\Gamma:=\left\{\gamma\in C([0,1];E);\ \gamma(0)=0\mbox{ and }\gamma(1)=e\right\}
, the constant

\displaystyle c=\inf\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\gamma(t)).

Then
c\geq\alpha
and there exists a sequence
(u_{n})
in
E
such that

\displaystyle I(u_{n})\to c\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lambda_{I}(u_{n})\to c.

If, in addition,
I
satisfies the Palais–Smale condition, then
c
is a critical value of
I
.

On the other hand, taking

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{p,\beta}\right|^{p}\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{p,\beta}\right|^{q}\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\rho_{p,\beta}u_{p,\beta}\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{\beta}\right|\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{\beta}\right|^{q}\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{p,\beta}\right|^{p}\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{p,\beta}\right|^{q}\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x+o_{p}(1).

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{\beta}\right|\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{p,\beta}\right|^{p}\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{d}x+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle I_{\beta}(u_{\beta})=I_{p,\beta}(u_{p,\beta})+o_{p}(1).

(42)

Lemma 11

Proof.
