Forward backward CP Asymmetry in τ − → K ​ π ​ ν τ \tau^{-}\to K\pi\nu_{\tau} in the Left–Right Inverse Seesaw Model
David Delepine, Shaaban Khalil

Abstract
Recent measurements of the integrated CP asymmetry in τ → K ​ π ​ ν τ \tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau} decays by the BaBar collaboration exhibit a 2.8 ​ σ 2.8\sigma deviation from the Standard Model (SM) prediction. In this work, we investigate CP-violating effects in τ → K ​ π ​ ν τ \tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau} within the model of the Left–Right Inverse Seesaw (LRIS) model. We show that, although the integrated asymmetry remains too small to account for the BaBar result, the model nevertheless predicts a pronounced signal in the differential forward–backward CP asymmetry, A CP FB ​ ( s ) A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s) . We derive the effective | Δ ​ S | = 1 |\Delta S|=1 Hamiltonian relevant for these decays and identify a dominant non-decoupling scalar operator, g S g_{S} , generated by a top-quark flavor-changing neutral current box diagram involving heavy neutrinos and scalar exchange. Our numerical analysis demonstrates that, while this contribution largely cancels in the integrated A CP A_{\rm CP} , it significantly enhances A CP FB ​ ( s ) A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s) through interference with the SM vector current, leading to distinctive kinematic features near the K ∗ ​ ( 892 ) K^{*}(892) and K 0 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) K_{0}^{*}(1430) resonances. These angular and differential observables provide a sensitive probe of the LRIS scalar sector at current and future flavor experiments, in particular Belle II.



I
Introduction

The semileptonic decay
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
is a laboratory for testing the SM and searching for new physics (NP). Unlike purely leptonic decays, the presence of hadronic final-state interactions (FSI) generates the strong phases necessary to observe direct CP violation in the presence of new weak phases. In the channel
\tau^{-}\to\bar{K}^{0}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
, a SM CP violating asymmetry is expected due to
K^{0}
–
\bar{K}^{0}
mixing
Grossman and Nir 2012
. Interest in this channel intensified following the BaBar collaboration’s report of a
2.8\sigma
discrepancy with the SM in the integrated CP asymmetry in the channel
\tau^{-}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}

Lees et al. 2012
,

A_{\rm CP}^{\rm exp.}=-(0.36\pm 0.23\pm 0.11)\%\,.

(1)

In the SM, the process is dominated by the charged-current interaction mediated by the
W
boson. Focusing only on direct CP violation, the expected SM direct CP asymmetry is very small,
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm SM}\sim 10^{-12}

Bigi and Sanda 2005
, since a nonzero CP asymmetry requires both a weak phase and a strong rescattering phase. This makes
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
an attractive channel to search for Beyond the SM (BSM) scalar, tensor, or right-handed-current effects.

While many NP models attempt to explain this anomaly through loop-induced tensor operators
Devi and Dhargyal 2014
;
Cirigliano et al. 2018
;
Dhargyal 2018a
, such mechanisms typically suffer from a severe
1/16\pi^{2}
suppression. In the context of TeV-scale physics, this leads to an integrated asymmetry of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-9})
, as has been shown in several studies
Cirigliano et al. 2018
. This suggests that the integrated asymmetry might serve better as a null test or an exclusion tool for loop-level tensor mediators rather than a primary discovery channel for extended gauge or scalar sectors.

CP violation (CPV) has been firmly established in non-leptonic decays of kaons
Christenson et al. 1964
, as well as
B_{d}

Aubert et al. 2001
,
B_{s}

Aaij et al. 2013
, and more recently
D
mesons
Aaij et al. 2019
. In the leptonic sector, CPV arises through neutrino mixing, which introduces a complex phase
\delta_{\rm CP}
in the lepton mixing matrix
Fukuda et al. 1998
;
Ahmad et al. 2002
. This phase leads to CP-violating effects in neutrino oscillations and new generation of neutrino experiments are looking to measure it
Abe et al. 2020
;
Acero et al. 2019
.

The CPV observed in channels as
K_{L}^{0}\to\pi^{-}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}

Christenson et al. 1964
and
K_{L}^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}e^{+}e^{-}

Abouzaid et al. 2006
originate from CP violation in the meson sector. Within the SM, direct CPV generated by leptonic sector is expected to be extremely suppressed.

Assuming the quark sector as the CPV origin, direct CP violation should be universal in channel decay modes as
\tau^{-}\to\bar{K}^{0}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}
, since both are governed by the same underlying quark-level transition,
\tau^{-}\to s\bar{u}\nu_{\tau}
. Consequently, any genuine discrepancy between these channels would signal new physics. The possibility of direct, angular-integrated CPV in semihadronic tau decays, particularly in channels such as
\tau^{-}\to K_{S}\pi^{-}\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}
, has been extensively studied in the literature
Bigi and Sanda 2005
;
Calderon et al. 2007
;
Devi and Dhargyal 2014
;
Cirigliano et al. 2018
;
Dhargyal 2018a
;
Dhargyal 2018b
;
Kuhn and Mirkes 1997
;
Tsai 1997
;
Choi et al. 1998
;
Delepine et al. 2007
;
Delepine et al. 2008
;
Delepine et al. 2021
;
Kimura et al. 2013
.

In this work, we investigate the Left-Right Inverse Seesaw (LRIS) model
Khalil and Seto 2008
;
Dev et al. 2012
;
Barry and Rodejohann 2013
as a model for generating observable CP violation in
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
. The LRIS model extends the SM gauge symmetry to
SU(3)_{C}\times SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L}
and incorporates the inverse seesaw mechanism
Mohapatra and Valle 1986
;
Wyler and Wolfenstein 1983
to generate small neutrino masses. The model features a
9\times 9
neutral fermion mass matrix and an extended scalar sector including a heavy charged Higgs
H^{\pm}
.

A key result of our analysis is that the integrated CP asymmetry
A_{\rm CP}
in the LRIS model remains heavily suppressed due to stringent phenomenological constraints on the right-handed quark mixing angles and scalar Yukawa couplings arising from
K^{0}
–
\bar{K}^{0}
mixing
Bona et al. 2008
,
D^{0}
–
\bar{D}^{0}
mixing
Artuso et al. 2016
, and precision electroweak tests
Senjanović and Tello 2015
. These constraints, combined with neutrino non-unitarity bounds from tau decays
Antusch et al. 2006
, severely limit the magnitude of the CP-violating weak phases that can contribute to
A_{\rm CP}
. As a consequence, the predicted
A_{\rm CP}
in LRIS is at most
\mathcal{O}(10^{-7})
, well below experimental sensitivity.

Unlike the integrated asymmetry, the forward-backward CP asymmetry
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)
is dominated by interference between the SM vector current and a scalar operator
g_{S}
. The dominant contribution to this scalar operator arises from non-decoupling box diagrams involving internal heavy neutrinos
N_{i}
, the top quark, and charged Goldstone bosons, where the heavy neutrino mass dependence cancels in the limit
M_{N_{i}}\gg v_{R}

Pilaftsis 1992
. This non-decoupling behavior allows
g_{S}
to remain unsuppressed by heavy mass scales, leading to a predicted
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})
that is within the potential reach of Belle II
Kou et al. 2019
. By incorporating realistic neutrino mass splittings and CP-violating phases from the inverse seesaw mechanism, we demonstrate that the differential forward-backward asymmetry provides a experimentally accessible probe of the LRIS scalar sector.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we present the effective Hamiltonian for the four-fermion transition
\tau\to\nu_{i}s\bar{u}
, and define the CP observables in
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
, with particular emphasis on the integrated CP asymmetry and the forward-backward CP asymmetry. In Sec.III, we discuss the SM contributions to these observables. In Sec.IV, we introduce the LRIS model, focusing on the relevant particle spectrum and Yukawa couplings for the process under consideration. Sec.V is devoted to the LRIS contributions to CP asymmetry in
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
, including the tree-level charged Higgs contribution and the box-diagram contribution involving internal heavy neutrinos
N_{i}
and the top quark. Numerical results are presented in Sec.VI, and our conclusions are given in Sec.VII.

II
Effective Framework and CP Observables in
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}

To describe the decay
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
, heavy degrees of freedom are integrated out and the interaction is expressed in terms of an effective four-fermion Hamiltonian at the scale
\mu\sim m_{\tau}
. The most general
|\Delta S|=1
effective Hamiltonian relevant for this decay can be written as
González-Alonso and Martin Camalich 2016
;
Rendón et al. 2019

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{eff}}=-\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}^{\ast}\Big[

\displaystyle(1+g_{V})(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}u)(\bar{\nu}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\tau)-(1+g_{A})(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}u)(\bar{\nu}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\tau)+g_{S}(\bar{s}u)(\bar{\nu}_{i}\tau)

\displaystyle+g_{P}(\bar{s}u)(\bar{\nu}_{i}\gamma_{5}\tau)+g_{T}(\bar{s}\sigma_{\mu\nu}u)(\bar{\nu}_{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}(1+\gamma_{5})\tau)\Big].

(2)

Here
G_{F}
denotes the Fermi constant and
V_{us}
the relevant CKM matrix element. The coefficients
g_{V}
,
g_{A}
,
g_{S}
,
g_{P}
, and
g_{T}
represent possible new physics contributions to the vector, axial-vector, scalar, pseudoscalar, and tensor operators, respectively. In the SM at tree level,
g_{V}=g_{A}=g_{S}=g_{P}=g_{T}=0
. Contributions from right-handed vector currents are neglected since the corresponding gauge boson
W^{\prime}
is assumed to be very heavy,
M_{W^{\prime}}\gg
TeV
Senjanović and Tello 2015
.

II.1
Hadronic Matrix Elements

The non-vanishing hadronic matrix elements for the
\tau\to K\pi\nu
transition are parameterized in terms of the vector, scalar, and tensor form factors, denoted by
F_{+}(s)
,
F_{0}(s)
, and
F_{T}(s)
, respectively
Jamin et al. 2002
;
Bernard et al. 2013
. The vector current matrix element is decomposed as

\langle K(p_{K})\pi(p_{\pi})|\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}u|0\rangle=\left[(p_{K}-p_{\pi})_{\mu}-\frac{\Delta_{K\pi}}{s}q_{\mu}\right]F_{+}(s)+\frac{\Delta_{K\pi}}{s}q_{\mu}F_{0}(s),

(3)

where

q=p_{K}+p_{\pi},\qquad s=q^{2},\qquad\Delta_{K\pi}=m_{K}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}.

The form factors
F_{+}(s)
and
F_{0}(s)
are related by a kinematic constraint at
q^{2}=0
, namely
F_{+}(0)=F_{0}(0)
, which follows from current conservation. The vector form factor
F_{+}(s)
is dominated by the
K^{*}(892)
resonance and receives additional contributions from excited vector states such as the
K^{*}(1410)

Jamin et al. 2002
.

Using the QCD equation of motion,

\partial^{\mu}(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}u)=i(m_{s}-m_{u})\bar{s}u,

the scalar current matrix element is related to the scalar form factor as

\langle K(p_{K})\pi(p_{\pi})|\bar{s}u|0\rangle=\frac{\Delta_{K\pi}}{m_{s}-m_{u}}\,F_{0}(s),

(4)

where
m_{s}
and
m_{u}
denote the running quark masses evaluated at the scale
\mu\sim m_{\tau}
. We use
m_{s}(2~\text{GeV})=93.4^{+8.6}_{-3.4}
MeV and
m_{u}(2~\text{GeV})=2.16^{+0.49}_{-0.26}
MeV
Zyla et al. 2020
. The scalar form factor
F_{0}(s)
receives contributions from the scalar
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
resonance and a non-resonant
S
-wave background, which is conventionally described by the LASS parameterization
Aston et al. 1988
.

Finally, the tensor current matrix element is parameterized as
González-Alonso and Martin Camalich 2016

\langle K(p_{K})\pi(p_{\pi})|\bar{s}\sigma_{\mu\nu}u|0\rangle=i\,\frac{F_{T}(s)}{m_{K}+m_{\pi}}\left(p_{K\mu}p_{\pi\nu}-p_{K\nu}p_{\pi\mu}\right).

(5)

The tensor form factor
F_{T}(s)
has been studied using chiral perturbation theory, dispersion relations, and lattice QCD
Bernard et al. 2013
;
González-Alonso and Martin Camalich 2016
.

II.2
Differential Decay Distribution

The double differential decay distribution for
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}
, with respect to the invariant mass squared of the hadronic system,
s=q^{2}
, and the helicity angle
\theta
, can be written as
Kuhn and Mirkes 1997
;
Delepine et al. 2021

\frac{d^{2}\Gamma}{ds\,d\cos\theta}=\frac{G_{F}^{2}|V_{us}|^{2}}{128\pi^{3}m_{\tau}^{3}}\left(1-\frac{s}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{|\vec{p}_{K}|}{\sqrt{s}}\left[\mathcal{A}(s)+\mathcal{B}(s)\cos\theta+\mathcal{C}(s)\cos^{2}\theta\right],

(6)

where
\theta
is defined as the angle between the kaon momentum and the parent
\tau
direction in the
K\pi
rest frame, and

|\vec{p}_{K}|=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(s,m_{K}^{2},m_{\pi}^{2})}{2\sqrt{s}}

is the kaon three-momentum in this frame. Here,

\lambda(a,b,c)=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}-2(ab+bc+ca)

denotes the Källén function.

II.3
Angular Coefficients

The angular coefficients
\mathcal{A}(s)
,
\mathcal{B}(s)
, and
\mathcal{C}(s)
encode the underlying short-distance couplings and hadronic dynamics. Retaining the dominant vector, scalar, and tensor contributions, they can be written schematically as
Delepine et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s)

\displaystyle=|1+g_{V}|^{2}\left[|\vec{p}_{K}|^{2}|F_{+}(s)|^{2}+\frac{m_{\tau}^{2}}{4s}\left|\frac{\Delta_{K\pi}}{\sqrt{s}}F_{0}(s)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+|g_{S}|^{2}\frac{m_{\tau}^{2}}{4s}\left|\frac{\Delta_{K\pi}}{\sqrt{s}}F_{0}(s)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+|g_{T}|^{2}\frac{16|\vec{p}_{K}|^{4}}{s(m_{K}+m_{\pi})^{2}}|F_{T}(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\text{Re}[(1+g_{V})g_{S}^{*}]\frac{m_{\tau}^{2}\Delta_{K\pi}}{s\sqrt{s}}\text{Re}[F_{+}(s)F_{0}^{*}(s)]

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(s)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{VS}(s)\,\text{Re}\!\left[(1+g_{V})g_{S}^{\ast}\,F_{+}(s)F_{0}^{\ast}(s)\right]+\kappa_{VT}(s)\,\text{Re}\!\left[(1+g_{V})g_{T}^{\ast}\,F_{+}(s)F_{T}^{\ast}(s)\right],

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(s)

\displaystyle=-|1+g_{V}|^{2}\,|\vec{p}_{K}|^{2}|F_{+}(s)|^{2}+\mathcal{C}_{T}(s),

(9)

where
\kappa_{VS}(s)=-2\,\frac{m_{\tau}}{\sqrt{s}}\,|\vec{p}_{K}|
and
\kappa_{VT}(s)=\frac{16\,|\vec{p}_{K}|^{2}\sqrt{s}}{m_{K}+m_{\pi}}
, the scalar and tensor pieces contribute through

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{S}(s)

\displaystyle\propto|g_{S}|^{2}|F_{0}(s)|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{T}(s)

\displaystyle\propto|g_{T}|^{2}|F_{T}(s)|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}(s)

\displaystyle\propto|g_{T}|^{2}|F_{T}(s)|^{2}.

(10)

The coefficient
\mathcal{B}(s)
is particularly important, since it governs the forward-backward asymmetry and is generated by interference between amplitudes of different Lorentz structure. Note that
\mathcal{B}(s)
is CP-even, but its difference between
\tau^{-}
and
\tau^{+}
decays,
\mathcal{B}(s)-\bar{\mathcal{B}}(s)
, is CP-odd and proportional to the imaginary parts of the Wilson coefficients and form factors.

II.4
CP Asymmetry Observables

For this process, one can define both the integrated CP asymmetry and the forward-backward CP asymmetry. The total integrated CP asymmetry is defined as
Bigi and Sanda 2005
;
Grossman:1996ke

A_{\rm CP}=\frac{\Gamma(\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau})-\Gamma(\tau^{+}\to K^{+}\pi^{0}\bar{\nu}_{\tau})}{\Gamma(\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau})+\Gamma(\tau^{+}\to K^{+}\pi^{0}\bar{\nu}_{\tau})},

(11)

or equivalently,

A_{\rm CP}=\frac{\int_{s_{\rm min}}^{s_{\rm max}}ds\,\left(\frac{d\Gamma}{ds}-\frac{d\bar{\Gamma}}{ds}\right)}{\int_{s_{\rm min}}^{s_{\rm max}}ds\,\left(\frac{d\Gamma}{ds}+\frac{d\bar{\Gamma}}{ds}\right)},

(12)

where

s_{\rm min}=(m_{K}+m_{\pi})^{2},\qquad s_{\rm max}=m_{\tau}^{2}.

A non-zero integrated CP asymmetry requires both a CP-violating weak phase and a non-trivial strong-phase difference between the interfering hadronic amplitudes. In the presence of tensor interactions, the dominant contribution typically arises from the interference between the vector and tensor form factors
Cirigliano et al. 2018
. Schematically,

A_{\rm CP}\propto\frac{\displaystyle\int_{(m_{K}+m_{\pi})^{2}}^{m_{\tau}^{2}}ds\,\left(1-\frac{s}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}|\vec{p}_{K}|^{3}\,\text{Im}(g_{T})\,\text{Im}\!\left[F_{+}(s)F_{T}^{\ast}(s)\right]}{\displaystyle\int_{(m_{K}+m_{\pi})^{2}}^{m_{\tau}^{2}}ds\,\left(1-\frac{s}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}|\vec{p}_{K}|^{3}\,|F_{+}(s)|^{2}}.

(13)

However, as discussed in Ref.
Cirigliano et al. 2018
, the strong phases in the vector and tensor form factors are correlated by analyticity and Watson’s theorem, leading to severe suppression of
A_{\rm CP}
in models with only tensor operators.

The forward-backward asymmetry is defined differentially as
Delepine et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022

A_{\rm FB}(s)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}d\cos\theta\,\frac{d^{2}\Gamma}{ds\,d\cos\theta}-\int_{-1}^{0}d\cos\theta\,\frac{d^{2}\Gamma}{ds\,d\cos\theta}}{\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma}{ds}},

(14)

while the corresponding CP-odd observable is

A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)=A_{\rm FB}^{\tau^{-}}(s)-A_{\rm FB}^{\tau^{+}}(s).

(15)

Since the forward-backward asymmetry is controlled by the coefficient
\mathcal{B}(s)
, the CP-odd part is especially sensitive to scalar and tensor interactions. One finds schematically
Delepine et al. 2021

\displaystyle A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\Sigma(s)}\frac{G_{F}^{2}|V_{us}|^{2}}{64\pi^{3}m_{\tau}^{3}}\left(1-\frac{s}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{|\vec{p}_{K}|}{\sqrt{s}}\Big[\kappa_{VS}(s)\,\text{Im}\!\left((1+g_{V})g_{S}^{\ast}\right)\text{Im}\!\left(F_{+}(s)F_{0}^{\ast}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 91.04872pt+\kappa_{VT}(s)\,\text{Im}\!\left((1+g_{V})g_{T}^{\ast}\right)\text{Im}\!\left(F_{+}(s)F_{T}^{\ast}(s)\right)\Big],

(16)

where
\Sigma(s)
denotes the CP-even differential rate combination appearing in the denominator. The kinematic weights are given by

\kappa_{VS}(s)=-2\,\frac{m_{\tau}}{\sqrt{s}}\,|\vec{p}_{K}|,\qquad\kappa_{VT}(s)=\frac{16\,|\vec{p}_{K}|^{2}\sqrt{s}}{m_{K}+m_{\pi}}.

(17)

It is worth emphasizing that, while the integrated asymmetry is suppressed, the forward-backward CP asymmetry can be enhanced in the presence of scalar interactions and therefore provides a much more sensitive probe of new physics
Delepine et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022
. Unlike the tensor case, the scalar form factor
F_{0}(s)
is dominated by the
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
resonance, which has a different strong-phase structure than the vector
K^{*}(892)
, thereby evading the Watson theorem constraints that suppress
A_{\rm CP}
.

III
Standard-Model CP Asymmetry

In the SM, the decay
\tau^{-}\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
proceeds through the charged-current interaction mediated by an off-shell
W
boson. The corresponding tree-level amplitude is given by

\mathcal{M}_{\rm SM}^{\rm tree}=\frac{G_{F}V_{us}}{\sqrt{2}}\,L_{\mu}\,H^{\mu},

(18)

where the leptonic current is

L_{\mu}=\bar{u}_{\nu_{\tau}}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}u_{\tau},

(19)

and the hadronic current is defined as

H^{\mu}=\langle K\pi|\bar{s}\gamma^{\mu}u|0\rangle.

(20)

This contribution represents the dominant amplitude in the SM. The corresponding tree-level diagram is shown in Fig.
1
.

\tau^{-}
\nu_{\tau}
W^{-}
K
\pi

Figure 1:
Tree-level SM contribution to
\tau^{-}\to\nu_{\tau}K\pi
mediated by an off-shell
W^{-}
.

At tree level, the decay amplitude involves a single weak phase arising from the CKM matrix element
V_{us}
. Since there is no additional interfering amplitude carrying a different weak phase, no direct CP asymmetry is generated at this order
Bigi and Sanda 2005
.

Non-zero CP violation in this process can arise only from higher-order electroweak corrections combined with non-trivial strong phases generated by hadronic final-state interactions. In particular, strong phases originate from
K\pi
rescattering effects and resonance contributions, such as the
K^{*}(892)
vector meson and the scalar
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
state, which enter through the vector and scalar form factors
Jamin et al. 2002
;
Bernard et al. 2013
. These strong phases are subject to Watson’s final-state interaction theorem
Watson 1954
, which relates the phase of the hadronic amplitude to the elastic scattering phase shift in the relevant partial wave.

However, within the SM, the additional weak phase required for CP violation is highly suppressed. It can only enter through higher-order electroweak amplitudes involving subleading CKM combinations and loop corrections
Buras 1998
;
Buchalla et al. 1996
. Parametrically, the CP-violating part of the amplitude is suppressed by:

•

the electroweak loop factor,

\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\sim 10^{-2},

•

the small Jarlskog-type CKM invariant
Jarlskog 1985
,

J_{\rm CKM}\sim 3\times 10^{-5},

•

and additional GIM cancellations
Glashow et al. 1970
in the loop-induced contribution, which further reduce the effect by a factor of
(m_{c}^{2}-m_{u}^{2})/M_{W}^{2}\sim 10^{-4}
.

As a result, the predicted direct CP asymmetry in
\tau^{-}\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
is extremely small
Bigi and Sanda 2005
;
Grossman:1996ke
,

A_{\rm CP}^{\rm SM}\lesssim 10^{-12},

(21)

which is many orders of magnitude below current experimental sensitivity. This estimate is consistent with more detailed analyses using chiral perturbation theory and dispersion relations
Kuhn and Mirkes 1997
;
Tsai 1997
.

The same conclusion applies to the forward-backward CP asymmetry. Although the forward-backward asymmetry
A_{\rm FB}(s)
itself is present in the SM and can be of order unity near resonances
Delepine et al. 2021
, its CP-odd component requires both a weak-phase difference and a strong-phase difference between the interfering amplitudes. The strong phase can be sizable due to hadronic rescattering near resonances, but the corresponding weak phase remains extremely suppressed for the reasons discussed above.

Moreover, as shown by Cirigliano, Crivellin, and Hoferichter
Cirigliano et al. 2018
, Watson’s theorem imposes severe constraints on CP asymmetries arising from tensor operators, since the vector and tensor form factors must share the same strong phase in the elastic region. This correlation greatly reduces the imaginary part of
F_{+}(s)F_{T}^{*}(s)
that drives the CP asymmetry. Therefore, the SM prediction for the forward-backward CP asymmetry is also expected to be negligibly small,

A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB,SM}(s)\sim 10^{-12}\ \text{or smaller},

(22)

throughout the physical phase space.

It is worth emphasizing that any observable CP asymmetry in
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
, whether integrated or differential, at the level accessible to current or near-future experiments would therefore provide a clear signal of physics beyond the SM.

IV
Left–Right Model with Inverse Seesaw (LRIS)

The LRIS model extends the SM gauge symmetry to
Pati and Salam 1974
;
Mohapatra and Pati 1975
;
Senjanović and Mohapatra 1975

SU(3)_{C}\times SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L},

and enlarges the fermion sector by introducing additional gauge-singlet fermions that enable the realization of the inverse seesaw (IS) mechanism for neutrino mass generation
Mohapatra and Valle 1986
;
Wyler and Wolfenstein 1983
. In particular, for each generation one introduces two singlet fermions, denoted by
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, carrying appropriate
B-L
charges. This structure allows light neutrino masses while keeping the new fermionic states near the TeV scale
Dev et al. 2012
;
Barry and Rodejohann 2013
.

The scalar sector consists of an
SU(2)_{R}
doublet
\chi_{R}
and a scalar bi-doublet
\phi
, which are responsible for spontaneous symmetry breaking and fermion mass generation
Senjanović 1979
;
Deshpande et al. 1991
. Their vacuum expectation values (VEVs) are defined as

\langle\chi_{R}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v_{R}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\langle\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}k_{1}&0\\
0&k_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(23)

where
v_{R}
is assumed to lie at the TeV scale and triggers the breaking of

SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L}\;\longrightarrow\;U(1)_{Y},

while electroweak symmetry breaking is governed by

v^{2}=k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}\simeq(246~{\rm GeV})^{2}.

(24)

It is convenient to parameterize the bi-doublet VEVs as

k_{1}=v\sin\beta,\qquad k_{2}=v\cos\beta,

(25)

where
\tan\beta=k_{1}/k_{2}
is the analog of the type-II two-Higgs-doublet model parameter
Branco et al. 2012
.

In this setup, the LRIS model contains the ingredients necessary to generate both new charged-current interactions and an extended scalar sector, making it a well-motivated model for studying CP violation in semileptonic tau decays such as
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}.

IV.1
Yukawa Interactions

The Yukawa interactions relevant for fermion masses and flavor structure are given by
Ezzat et al. 2023

\mathcal{L}_{Y}=y_{ij}^{L}\,\bar{L}_{Li}\phi L_{Rj}+\tilde{y}_{ij}^{L}\,\bar{L}_{Li}\tilde{\phi}L_{Rj}+y_{ij}^{Q}\,\bar{Q}_{Li}\phi Q_{Rj}+\tilde{y}_{ij}^{Q}\,\bar{Q}_{Li}\tilde{\phi}Q_{Rj}+y_{ij}^{S}\,\bar{L}_{Ri}\widetilde{\chi}_{R}S^{c}_{2j}+{\rm H.c.},

(26)

where
i,j=1,2,3
are generation indices. The matrices
y^{Q}
and
\tilde{y}^{Q}
determine the quark Yukawa couplings, while
y^{L}
and
\tilde{y}^{L}
describe the charged-lepton and Dirac-neutrino sectors. The coupling
y^{S}
links the right-handed lepton doublets to the singlet fermions and plays a central role in the inverse seesaw mechanism.

To realize the inverse seesaw structure, one imposes an additional discrete symmetry (such as
Z_{2}
or
Z_{4}
) that forbids unwanted large singlet mass terms
Dev et al. 2012
. In particular, assigning suitable charges to the singlet fields can prevent direct mass terms that would otherwise spoil the inverse seesaw pattern and destabilize the small lepton-number-violating scale
\mu_{S}
.

The fields appearing in Eq. (
26
) are

\chi_{R}=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{R}^{+}\\
\chi_{R}^{0}\end{pmatrix},\quad\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{0}&\phi_{1}^{+}\\
\phi_{2}^{-}&\phi_{2}^{0}\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{A}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{A}\\
d_{A}\end{pmatrix},\quad L_{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{A}\\
e_{A}\end{pmatrix},\quad A=L,R.

(27)

The conjugated scalar fields are defined as

\widetilde{\phi}=\tau_{2}\phi^{\ast}\tau_{2},\qquad\widetilde{\chi}_{R}=i\tau_{2}\chi_{R}^{\ast},

(28)

where
\tau_{2}
is the second Pauli matrix.

After symmetry breaking, Eq. (
26
) generates the mass matrices for quarks, charged leptons, and neutrinos. In particular, the charged scalar interactions inherited from these Yukawa terms are relevant for the scalar contribution to
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
, as they induce couplings of the physical charged Higgs bosons to quarks and leptons.

IV.2
Inverse Seesaw Neutrino Sector

After symmetry breaking, the neutrino mass matrix is generated in the basis

(\nu_{L},\;N_{R},\;S)^{T},

where
N_{R}
denotes the right-handed neutrino component contained in
L_{R}
, and
S
collectively represents the singlet fermions. The resulting neutral-fermion mass matrix takes the characteristic inverse seesaw form
Mohapatra and Valle 1986
;
Wyler and Wolfenstein 1983

\mathcal{M}_{\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}0&m_{D}&0\\
m_{D}^{T}&0&M_{R}\\
0&M_{R}^{T}&\mu_{S}\end{pmatrix},

(29)

where

m_{D}\sim y^{L}\,v,\qquad M_{R}\sim y^{S}\,v_{R},

and
\mu_{S}
is a small lepton-number-violating Majorana mass term for the singlet fermions. In the limit

\mu_{S}\ll m_{D}\ll M_{R},

the light-neutrino mass matrix is approximately given by
Dev et al. 2012

m_{\nu}\simeq m_{D}\,M_{R}^{-1}\,\mu_{S}\,(M_{R}^{-1})^{T}\,m_{D}^{T}.

(30)

A key feature of the inverse seesaw is that the Yukawa couplings entering
m_{D}
and
M_{R}
can be of order unity (
y^{L},y^{S}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
) while still yielding naturally small neutrino masses, thanks to the suppression by the small parameter
\mu_{S}\sim\mathcal{O}({\rm keV})

Dev et al. 2012
. This allows sizable heavy-neutrino effects in loop-induced flavor and CP-violating observables, in contrast to the canonical type-I seesaw where large neutrino Yukawa couplings would lead to large neutrino masses
Minkowski 1977
.

For the process under consideration, the heavy neutrinos
N_{i}
can enter box and vertex corrections and provide new CP-violating phases, particularly through the Yukawa structures in the lepton sector. As we demonstrate in Sec.
V
, some of the box diagrams exhibit a non-decoupling behavior similar to non-decoupling SM box diagrams contribution to
K_{0}-\bar{K_{0}}
mixing for instance, where the dependence on
M_{N_{i}}
cancels in the heavy neutrino limit
Pilaftsis 1992
.

IV.3
Scalar (Higgs) Sector

Before symmetry breaking, the scalar sector contains twelve real degrees of freedom: four from the doublet
\chi_{R}
and eight from the bi-doublet
\phi
. After spontaneous symmetry breaking, a subset of these fields becomes the longitudinal components of the massive gauge bosons (
W^{\pm}
,
W_{R}^{\pm}
,
Z
,
Z^{\prime}
), while the remaining degrees of freedom correspond to physical Higgs states
Deshpande et al. 1991
.

The physical scalar spectrum contains
Ezzat et al. 2023

•

three neutral CP-even Higgs bosons (
h
,
H^{0}
,
H_{R}^{0}
),

•

one neutral CP-odd Higgs boson (
A^{0}
),

•

two charged Higgs bosons (
H^{\pm}
,
H_{R}^{\pm}
).

One of the CP-even neutral states is identified with the SM-like Higgs boson at approximately
125~{\rm GeV}

Aad et al. 2012
;
Chatrchyan et al. 2012
. The remaining neutral scalars arise from the mixing of the neutral components of the bi-doublet
\phi
and the right-handed doublet
\chi_{R}
, and are expected to lie near the scale
v_{R}
.

The charged Higgs bosons originate from the charged components of
\phi
and
\chi_{R}
. After removing the Goldstone modes absorbed by
W^{\pm}
and
W_{R}^{\pm}
, two physical charged scalar states remain. These charged scalars are of particular importance for our analysis, since they can mediate scalar interactions in semileptonic tau decays and induce the scalar Wilson coefficient
g_{S}
in the low-energy effective Hamiltonian introduced in Sec.
II
.

At the interaction level, the physical charged Higgs states couple schematically as
Ezzat et al. 2023

H^{+}\,\bar{u}\,(a\,P_{L}+b\,P_{R})\,d,\qquad H^{+}\,\bar{\nu}\,(c\,P_{R})\,\ell,

(31)

where the coefficients
a,b,c
are determined by the underlying Yukawa matrices and scalar mixing angles. These couplings provide the dominant tree-level scalar contribution to
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
.

A detailed discussion of the scalar potential and the Higgs mass spectrum can be found in Ref.
Ezzat et al. 2023
.

IV.4
Quark Masses and Mixing

After electroweak symmetry breaking, quarks and charged leptons acquire masses through the Yukawa interactions in Eq. (
26
). The quark mass matrices are generated by the bi-doublet VEVs and can be diagonalized by bi-unitary transformations. The corresponding left- and right-handed quark mixing matrices are defined as
Kiers et al. 2002

V_{\rm CKM}^{L,R}=V_{L,R}^{u\dagger}V_{L,R}^{d}.

(32)

In a convenient basis where
V_{L}^{u}=I
, one has

V_{\rm CKM}^{L}=V_{L}^{d},

while the right-handed mixing matrix
V_{\rm CKM}^{R}
remains in general independent and can contain additional mixing angles and CP-violating phases
Senjanović and Tello 2015
.

Following Ref.
Kiers et al. 2002
, one may parameterize the right-handed quark mixing matrix as

V_{\rm CKM}^{R}=K\,V_{\rm CKM}^{L}(\theta_{12}^{R},\theta_{23}^{R},\theta_{13}^{R},\delta_{R})\tilde{K}^{\dagger},

(33)

where
K
and
\tilde{K}
are diagonal phase matrices. These additional phases provide new sources of CP violation beyond the SM. Remarkably, as shown by Senjanović and Tello
Senjanović and Tello 2015
, in the minimal left-right symmetric model with manifest left-right symmetry (generalized parity), the right-handed mixing angles are approximately equal to the left-handed ones,
\theta_{ij}^{R}\approx\theta_{ij}^{L}
, to leading order in the small CKM mixings. However, the CP phases remain independent.

For phenomenological simplicity, and in order to suppress dangerous flavor-changing neutral currents from
Z^{\prime}
and neutral Higgs exchanges
Zhang et al. 2007
;
Maiezza et al. 2010
, one may consider a reduced flavor texture in which only a small subset of right-handed mixing angles is relevant. In particular, the couplings most important for the process studied here are those involving the top quark and strange/up quarks, since they control the box-diagram contributions with internal
t
and
N_{i}
states. A convenient benchmark texture is therefore one in which the dominant right-handed flavor structure is governed by a single mixing angle and one CP phase
Ezzat et al. 2023
,

V^{R}_{\rm CKM}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&c_{\theta_{R}}&s_{\theta_{R}}e^{i\alpha}\\
0&s_{\theta_{R}}&-c_{\theta_{R}}e^{i\alpha}\end{pmatrix},

(34)

where

c_{\theta_{R}}\equiv\cos\theta_{R},\qquad s_{\theta_{R}}\equiv\sin\theta_{R}.

The phase
\alpha
constitutes a new CP-violating source in the right-handed quark sector and plays an important role in generating CP-odd observables in
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
. Constraints on the right-handed mixing parameters from
K^{0}
–
\bar{K}^{0}
and
B_{d}^{0}
–
\bar{B}_{d}^{0}
mixing, as well as from direct searches for
W_{R}
at the LHC, will be discussed in Sec.
V
.

V
LRIS Contributions to CP Asymmetry in
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}

In the SM the decay
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
is dominated by the charged-current interaction mediated by a
W
boson, leading to a purely vector interaction at tree level. As discussed in Sec.
III
, the resulting CP asymmetry is extremely suppressed,
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm SM}\lesssim 10^{-12}
.

In the Left-Right inverse seesaw (LRIS) model, additional scalar and tensor interactions can arise due to the extended Higgs sector. These contributions modify the Wilson coefficients
g_{S}
and
g_{T}
appearing in the effective Hamiltonian [Eq. (
2
)] and can therefore generate observable CP asymmetries. In this section we discuss the dominant new physics contributions and the phenomenological constraints that determine their magnitude.

V.1
Tree-Level Charged Higgs Contribution

The LRIS scalar sector contains physical charged Higgs bosons originating from the bidoublet
\phi
and the right-handed doublet
\chi_{R}
. These charged scalars can mediate the decay
\tau\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
at tree level, as shown in Fig.
2
. This process is analogous to charged Higgs contributions in two-Higgs-doublet models
Branco et al. 2012
and leptoquark models
Sakaki et al. 2013
.

\tau^{-}
\nu_{\tau}
H^{-}

\tau^{-}\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}

The charged Higgs exchange induces the following scalar amplitude

\mathcal{M}_{H^{-}}=-\frac{y_{\tau\nu}^{H}\,y_{su}^{H}}{M_{H^{-}}^{2}}\,\left(\bar{u}_{\nu_{\tau}}P_{R}u_{\tau}\right)\left(\bar{s}u\right).

(35)

Matching onto the effective Hamiltonian [Eq. (

g_{S}^{\rm tree}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{G_{F}V_{us}}\frac{y_{\tau\nu}^{H}y_{su}^{H}}{M_{H^{-}}^{2}}.

(36)

If the couplings are complex,

y_{\tau\nu}^{H}y_{su}^{H}=|y_{\tau\nu}^{H}y_{su}^{H}|e^{i\phi_{H}},

(37)

a nonzero weak phase
\phi_{H}
appears in the scalar amplitude. This scalar interaction interferes with the SM vector current and can contribute to the forward-backward CP asymmetry discussed in Sec.
II
.

V.2
Constraints on LRIS Parameters

In the Left-Right Inverse Seesaw (LRIS) model, the constituent Yukawa couplings—
y_{su}^{H}
for the quark sector and
y_{\tau\nu}^{H}
for the lepton sector—are subject to severe phenomenological restrictions that suppress the tree-level contribution in Eq. (
36
).

V.2.1
Constraints from
K^{0}
–
\bar{K}^{0}
Mixing

To evade tree-level contributions to the
\Delta S=2
mass difference
\Delta m_{K}
, the scalar Yukawa couplings must be suppressed. Neutral Higgs exchange between
s
and
d
quarks generates a contribution to
\Delta m_{K}
proportional to
Glashow and Weinberg 1977
;
Cheng and Sher 1987

\Delta m_{K}\propto\frac{f_{K}^{2}m_{K}}{M_{H^{0}}^{2}}|y_{sd}^{H}|^{2},

(38)

where
f_{K}\approx 156
MeV is the kaon decay constant. The experimental value
\Delta m_{K}=(3.484\pm 0.006)\times 10^{-15}
GeV
Zyla et al. 2020
places a severe bound on flavor-changing neutral scalars.

The standard approach is to enforce a minimal flavor violation (MFV) alignment
D’Ambrosio et al. 2002
where the flavor-violating scalar couplings scale proportionally to the geometric mean of the interacting quark masses:

y_{su}^{H}\sim\frac{\sqrt{m_{u}m_{s}}}{v}.

(39)

Using
m_{u}(2~{\rm GeV})\approx 2.2
MeV and
m_{s}(2~{\rm GeV})\approx 93
MeV
Zyla et al. 2020
, this forces

|y_{su}^{H}|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{2.2\times 93}}{246\times 10^{3}}\approx 6\times 10^{-5}.

(40)

V.2.2
Constraints from Neutrino Non-Unitarity

A very large
y_{\tau\nu}^{H}
induces mixing between the active and sterile neutrinos, leading to non-unitarity in the active
3\times 3
PMNS matrix
Antusch et al. 2006
;
Fernandez-Martinez et al. 2016
. Precision measurements of tau decays (
\tau\to\mu\nu\bar{\nu}
vs.
\mu\to e\nu\bar{\nu}
) restrict this active-sterile mixing to
Antusch et al. 2006

\eta_{\tau\tau}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{-3}).

(41)

The mixing parameter
\eta_{\tau\tau}
is related to the Dirac Yukawa coupling through
Dev et al. 2012

\eta_{\tau\tau}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\frac{|M_{D,\tau}|^{2}}{M_{R}^{2}}=\frac{v^{2}}{4M_{R}^{2}}|y_{\tau\nu}^{H}|^{2},

(42)

which yields

|y_{\tau\nu}^{H}|\lesssim\frac{2M_{R}}{v}\sqrt{\eta_{\tau\tau}}.

(43)

For
M_{R}=1
TeV and
\eta_{\tau\tau}=10^{-3}
, we obtain

|y_{\tau\nu}^{H}|\lesssim\frac{2000}{246}\sqrt{10^{-3}}\approx 0.26.

(44)

V.2.3
Resulting Tree-Level Suppression

Combining the bounds in Eqs. (
40
) and (
44
), and using
G_{F}^{-1/2}=246
GeV,
V_{us}\approx 0.22
, and
M_{H^{\pm}}=1
TeV, we find

|g_{S}^{\rm tree}|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{2}}{G_{F}\times 0.22}\times\frac{0.26\times 6\times 10^{-5}}{(10^{3}~{\rm GeV})^{2}}\approx 1.5\times 10^{-5}.

(45)

This severe suppression motivates the search for loop-induced contributions that can evade the light-quark mass suppression inherent in Eq. (
39
).

V.3
Box Diagram Contributions

In contrast to the tree-level contribution, certain one-loop flavor-changing neutral current (FCNC) box diagrams can bypass the light-quark suppression in Eq. (
40
). By routing the hadronic transition through an internal top quark, these diagrams access the unsuppressed
y_{tu}^{H}
coupling, which is free from
D

non-decoupling
behavior

where the dependence on the heavy neutrino mass
M_{N_{i}}
cancels exactly, allowing the loop amplitude to remain unsuppressed even for
M_{N_{i}}\gg v_{R}
.

There are many possible box topologies that can contribute to the Wilson coefficients, but we focus on diagrams that fulfill the non-decoupling condition such that they can compensate the one-loop suppression
1/16\pi^{2}
. The only diagrams that exhibit this non-decoupling limit are box diagrams with
H^{\pm}
(or charged Goldstone bosons
G^{\pm}
) and neutral Higgs
H^{0}
exchanges between the lepton and quark currents, as shown in Fig.
3

Ezzat et al. 2023
;
Pilaftsis 1992
.

\nu_{\tau}

The box amplitudes generate additional weak phases through the scalar Yukawa couplings and the right-handed mixing matrices [Eq. (

From the full loop amplitude

g_{S}^{\rm box}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}v}{16\pi^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{k=u,c,t}\left(\frac{V_{L}^{ks}}{V_{us}}\right)Y_{H}^{ku}Y_{\nu H}^{i}Y_{NG}^{\tau i}M_{N_{i}}m_{u_{k}}^{2}\mathcal{I}_{4}(M_{N_{i}}^{2},m_{u_{k}}^{2},M_{H^{0}}^{2},M_{G^{\pm}}^{2}),

(46)

where we have defined:

•

•

: The neutral Yukawa coupling connecting the active neutrino
\nu_{L}

•

: The charged Yukawa coupling connecting
\tau_{R}

The explicit form of the Yukawa couplings can be found in Ref.

\mathcal{I}_{4}(m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2},m_{3}^{2},m_{4}^{2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{xdx}{(x+m_{1}^{2})(x+m_{2}^{2})(x+m_{3}^{2})(x+m_{4}^{2})}.

(48)

In the heavy neutrino limit
M_{N_{i}}\to\infty

Pilaftsis 1992

\mathcal{I}_{4}\xrightarrow{M_{N_{i}}\to\infty}\frac{1}{M_{N_{i}}^{2}}\left[\frac{M_{H^{0}}^{2}\ln(M_{H^{0}}^{2})-M_{G^{\pm}}^{2}\ln(M_{G^{\pm}}^{2})}{M_{H^{0}}^{2}-M_{G^{\pm}}^{2}}+\cdots\right].

(49)

Crucially, taking this limit and substituting into Eq. (

suppression from the loop integral is
exactly compensated
by the
M_{N_{i}}
factor hidden in

[Eq. (

: the heavy neutrino mass cancels out of the amplitude, leaving
g_{S}^{\rm box}
unsuppressed by heavy mass scales. It is important to note that if we replace the Goldstone boson by a physical charged Higgs
H^{\pm}
, the relation in Eq. (
47

1/M_{N_{i}}^{2}
, restoring decoupling.

V.4
Flavor Constraints on Box Contributions

The bidoublet structure of the Left-Right model makes the up-type Yukawa matrix fundamentally independent of the down-type Yukawa matrix. To avoid catastrophic contributions to
K^{0}
–
\bar{K}^{0}
mixing from neutral Higgs exchange, we enforce a flavor alignment in the down sector
D’Ambrosio et al. 2002
,

Y_{H}^{sd}\approx 0,

(50)

leaving the up-type couplings in
g_{S}^{\rm box}
[Eq. (
46
)] completely untouched.

On the up-quark side, constraints from
D^{0}
–
\bar{D}^{0}
mixing
Artuso et al. 2016
enforce

|Y_{H}^{cu}|\ll 1.

(51)

However, as
D^{0}
–
\bar{D}^{0}
mixing only restricts transitions between the first two generations (
c\leftrightarrow u
), it places
no direct tree-level bound
on the top-quark coupling
Y_{H}^{tu}
. The coupling
Y_{H}^{tu}
is constrained primarily by LHC single-top production
Sirunyan et al. 2017
and electroweak precision tests
Crivellin and Nierste 2010
, leaving it relatively free to be large:

|Y_{H}^{tu}|\lesssim 0.1\text{ to }0.5.

(52)

Consequently, the dominant contribution to
g_{S}^{\rm box}
in Eq. (
46
) arises from the
k=t
term, which accesses the unsuppressed top-quark Yukawa coupling and benefits from the large top mass
m_{t}^{2}
enhancement. This allows the non-decoupling box diagram to reach

|g_{S}^{\rm box}|\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-4}),

(53)

at least one order of magnitude larger than the tree-level contribution in Eq. (
45
). As a result, the loop-level topology acts as the primary driver of the scalar-vector interference in the forward-backward CP asymmetry
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)
.

VI
Numerical Results and Predictions for CP Asymmetry

The full decay amplitude for
\tau^{-}\to K^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}
can be written as a sum of vector, scalar, and tensor contributions
González-Alonso and Martin Camalich 2016
:

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{V}+\mathcal{M}_{S}+\mathcal{M}_{T},

(54)

where

•

Vector contribution
(SM tree-level):

\mathcal{M}_{V}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}^{*}(1+g_{V})\left[\bar{\nu}_{\tau}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\tau\right]\langle K\pi|\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}u|0\rangle.

(55)

•

Scalar contribution
(LRIS new physics):

\mathcal{M}_{S}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}^{*}g_{S}\left[\bar{\nu}_{\tau}(1+\gamma_{5})\tau\right]\langle K\pi|\bar{s}u|0\rangle.

(56)

•

Tensor contribution
(LRIS loop-induced):

\mathcal{M}_{T}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}^{*}g_{T}^{\rm eff}(s)\left[\bar{\nu}_{\tau}\sigma^{\mu\nu}(1+\gamma_{5})\tau\right]\langle K\pi|\bar{s}\sigma_{\mu\nu}u|0\rangle.

(57)

For completeness, we briefly comment on the possible generation of the
tensor Wilson coefficient
g_{T}
in the LRIS model. At one loop, box
diagrams with internal heavy neutrinos
N_{i}
and top quarks, together
with charged and neutral scalar exchange, induce a
tensor operator of the form
(\bar{s}\sigma_{\mu\nu}u)(\bar{\nu}_{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}(1+\gamma_{5})\tau)
when matched onto the low-energy
Hamiltonian in Eq. (2). The corresponding coefficient
is suppressed by the electroweak loop factor and by two chirality
flips along the quark and lepton lines, scaling as

g_{T}^{\rm loop}\!\sim(16\pi^{2})^{-1}(m_{t}m_{\tau}/v^{2})\times Y_{tu}^{H}Y_{\nu H}Y_{\tau NG}/(G_{F}M_{\rm loop}^{2})
, where

M_{\rm loop}\sim\mathcal{O}({\rm TeV})
denotes the typical scalar
or gauge-boson mass scale. After imposing the same flavor and
non-unitarity constraints used in our scalar analysis, we find that

|g_{T}|
cannot exceed
\mathcal{O}(10^{-9})
, as summarized in
Table
1
. So, the tensor contribution to
both the integrated asymmetry
A_{CP}
and the forward–backward CP
asymmetry
A_{FB}^{CP}(s)
is negligible compared to the
non-decoupling scalar contribution
g_{S}^{\rm box}\sim 10^{-4}
. In
addition, Watson’s theorem enforces that
F_{+}(s)
and
F_{T}(s)
share
essentially the same strong phase in the elastic region, which further
suppresses
\mathrm{Im}[F_{+}(s)F_{T}^{*}(s)]
and thus any CP-violating
effect proportional to
g_{T}
.

The interference between these amplitudes generates the CP-violating observables defined in Sec.
II
. As discussed in Sec.
V
, the LRIS model provides two distinct sources of CP violation: tree-level charged Higgs exchange [Eq. (
36
)] and non-decoupling box diagrams [Eq. (
46
)]. However, the former is severely suppressed by flavor constraints [Eq. (
45
)], while the latter benefits from top-quark mass enhancement and avoids light-quark suppression.

where

GeV

, and

.

VI.1.2

The scalar form factor receives contributions from the

VI.1.3
Tensor Form Factor
F_{T}(s)

The tensor form factor has been computed using chiral perturbation theory and dispersion relations
Bernard et al. 2013
;
González-Alonso and Martin Camalich 2016
. For our numerical analysis, we adopt the dispersive parameterization of Ref.
Bernard et al. 2013
, which ensures consistency with analyticity and unitarity constraints.

VI.2
Integrated CP Asymmetry

The integrated CP asymmetry
A_{\rm CP}
[Eq. (
11
)] is dominated by the interference between the SM vector current and the tensor operator
Cirigliano et al. 2018
:

A_{\rm CP}(s)\propto\mathrm{Im}\!\left[(1+g_{V})g_{T}^{\ast}\right]\mathrm{Im}\!\left[F_{+}(s)F_{T}^{\ast}(s)\right].

(61)

However, as demonstrated in Sec.
V
, the LRIS model does not induce a tensor contribution at tree level, and one-loop tensor operators suffer from severe suppression. Moreover, even if a sizable tensor coupling were generated, Watson’s final-state interaction theorem
Watson 1954
enforces that the vector and tensor form factors share the same strong phase in the elastic region, leading to
\mathrm{Im}[F_{+}(s)F_{T}^{\ast}(s)]\approx 0
near resonances
Cirigliano et al. 2018
.

Consequently, the predicted integrated CP asymmetry in the LRIS model is extremely small:

A_{\rm CP}^{\rm LRIS}\ll 10^{-7},

(62)

which is many orders of magnitude below the BaBar measurement
Lees et al. 2012
and far out of reach of even future high-luminosity experiments like Belle II
Kou et al. 2019
.

VI.3
Forward-Backward CP Asymmetry

In contrast to the integrated asymmetry, the forward-backward CP asymmetry
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)
[Eq. (
15
)] is driven by the interference between the SM vector current and the scalar operator from non-decoupling box diagrams. The dominant contribution to the angular coefficient
\mathcal{B}(s)
[Eq. (
8
)] is
Delepine et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022

\mathcal{B}(s)=\underbrace{-\frac{m_{\tau}}{\sqrt{s}}|\vec{p}_{K}|\text{Re}\left[(1+g_{V})g_{S}^{*}F_{+}(s)F_{0}^{*}(s)\right]}_{\text{Scalar (Box) -- Vector (SM) Interference}}.

(63)

The scalar form factor
F_{0}(s)
is dominated by the
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
resonance, which has a different strong-phase structure than the vector
K^{*}(892)
resonance. This evades Watson’s theorem constraints and allows for a sizable imaginary part
\mathrm{Im}[F_{+}(s)F_{0}^{\ast}(s)]
near
\sqrt{s}\approx 1.4
GeV, where the scalar resonance acts as a kinematic amplifier for the new physics scalar signal
Finkemeier and Mirkes 1996
.

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated the potential for observing CP violation in the semileptonic decay
\tau^{-}\to K\pi\nu_{\tau}
within the Left-Right Inverse Seesaw (LRIS) model
Khalil and Seto 2008
;
Dev et al. 2012
;
Barry and Rodejohann 2013
. While the Standard Model (SM) prediction for the direct CP asymmetry in this channel is negligibly small,
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm SM}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-12})

Bigi and Sanda 2005
;
Grossman and Nir 2012
, the BaBar collaboration’s measurement
Lees et al. 2012
of
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm exp}=-(0.36\pm 0.23\pm 0.11)\%
represents a
2.8\sigma
discrepancy, motivating the search for new physics contributions.

Our analysis demonstrates that the integrated CP asymmetry
A_{\rm CP}
remains difficult to enhance to the level of the BaBar anomaly, even in extended models like LRIS. This suppression has two independent origins:

1.

Absence of tree-level tensor operators:
The LRIS model does not generate a tensor Wilson coefficient
g_{T}
at tree level, and loop-induced tensor contributions are suppressed by
1/16\pi^{2}\sim 10^{-2}
.

2.

Watson’s theorem constraints:
Even if a sizable tensor coupling were generated, Watson’s final-state interaction theorem
Watson 1954
enforces that the vector and tensor form factors share the same strong phase in the elastic region, leading to severe cancellations in the CP-violating interference term
Cirigliano et al. 2018
.

As a result, the predicted integrated asymmetry in the LRIS model is
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm LRIS}\ll 10^{-7}
, far below experimental sensitivity.

Our central result
is that the LRIS model predicts an unsuppressed signature in the
differential
forward-backward CP asymmetry,
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)
. This observable is driven by the interference between the SM vector current and a scalar operator
g_{S}
arising from non-decoupling box diagrams. Specifically, we identified a top-quark flavor-changing neutral current (FCNC) box diagram [Fig.
3
] involving internal heavy neutrinos
N_{i}
and charged Goldstone bosons
G^{\pm}
, which exhibits a
non-decoupling
behavior
Pilaftsis 1992
:

g_{S}^{\rm box}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}v}{16\pi^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{k=u,c,t}\left(\frac{V_{L}^{ks}}{V_{us}}\right)Y_{H}^{ku}Y_{\nu H}^{i}Y_{NG}^{\tau i}M_{N_{i}}m_{u_{k}}^{2}\mathcal{I}_{4}.

(66)

The heavy neutrino mass
M_{N_{i}}
appearing in the numerator (through the coupling
Y_{NG}^{\tau i}\propto M_{N_{i}}/v_{R}

Dev et al. 2012
)
exactly cancels
the
1/M_{N_{i}}^{2}
suppression from the loop integral
\mathcal{I}_{4}
, allowing the scalar coupling to remain unsuppressed even for
M_{N_{i}}\gg v_{R}
. This is the hallmark of the inverse seesaw mechanism
Mohapatra and Valle 1986
;
Wyler and Wolfenstein 1983
;
Pilaftsis 1992
.

By routing the hadronic transition through an internal top quark, the box diagram accesses the unsuppressed top-quark Yukawa coupling
Y_{H}^{tu}\lesssim 0.5

Crivellin and Nierste 2010
;
Sirunyan et al. 2017
, evading the stringent flavor constraints from
K^{0}
–
\bar{K}^{0}
and
D^{0}
–
\bar{D}^{0}
mixing that suppress tree-level charged Higgs contributions
Bona et al. 2008
;
Artuso et al. 2016
. This allows the non-decoupling box contribution to reach

|g_{S}^{\rm box}|\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-4}),

(67)

larger than the flavor-suppressed tree-level estimate
|g_{S}^{\rm tree}|\lesssim 1.5\times 10^{-5}
.

Using a benchmark scenario with
v_{R}=3.0
TeV,
Y_{\nu H}=Y_{H}^{tu}=0.5
, and maximal CP-violating phases
\alpha_{R}=\phi_{S}=-\pi/2
, we obtained a predicted integrated forward-backward CP asymmetry of

A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB,LRIS}\approx 2.08\times 10^{-4},

(68)

representing an enhancement of approximately
10^{10}
over the SM prediction
Bigi and Sanda 2005
. This is within the potential reach of Belle II, which is expected to collect
50~\mathrm{ab}^{-1}
of integrated luminosity
Kou et al. 2019
, corresponding to roughly
10^{9}
tau decays and an estimated statistical sensitivity to
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})

Kou et al. 2019
.

Furthermore, our numerical analysis [Fig.
4
] reveals a pronounced peak in the differential distribution
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)
at
\sqrt{s}\approx 1.4
GeV, precisely at the location of the
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
scalar resonance. This kinematic enhancement arises because:

•

The scalar form factor
F_{0}(s)
is dominated by the
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
resonance
Jamin et al. 2002
;
Finkemeier and Mirkes 1996
;
Aston et al. 1988
, which has a
different strong-phase structure
than the vector
K^{*}(892)
resonance
Bernard et al. 2013
.

•

This difference in strong phases maximizes the imaginary part
\mathrm{Im}[F_{+}(s)F_{0}^{\ast}(s)]
near the scalar resonance, evading the Watson’s theorem constraints that suppress the integrated asymmetry
Cirigliano et al. 2018
.

•

The kinematic weight
\kappa_{VS}(s)\propto m_{\tau}|\vec{p}_{K}|/\sqrt{s}
[Eq. (
17
)] is largest in the intermediate energy region where the scalar resonance resides.

This characteristic angular and differential signature is caracterized by the following distinctive features:

1.

Resonance-enhanced signal:
The peak at
\sqrt{s}\approx 1.4
GeV provides a clear kinematic tag.

2.

Model discrimination:
The shape of
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)
encodes information about the scalar resonance structure and can distinguish LRIS from other BSM scenarios such as type-II two-Higgs-doublet models
Branco et al. 2012
or leptoquark models
Sakaki et al. 2013
, which predict different kinematic distributions.

Looking forward, our results motivate several experimental and theoretical directions:

•

Belle II measurements:
With the upcoming high-luminosity run, Belle II
Kou et al. 2019
should be able to measure or constrain
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)
with unprecedented precision, providing the first direct probe of the LRIS scalar sector in semileptonic tau decays.

•

Correlated LHC signatures:
The same scalar couplings that generate
g_{S}^{\rm box}
also contribute to LHC processes such as
pp\to W_{R}\to\ell N\to\ell\ell jj

Sirunyan et al. 2018
and
pp\to H^{\pm}\to t\bar{b}

Aaboud et al. 2018
.

•

Precision form factor measurements:
Improved understanding of the scalar form factor
F_{0}(s)
from lattice QCD
Flynn et al. 2015
and dispersive analyses
Boito and Escribano 2009
will reduce hadronic uncertainties and sharpen the new physics sensitivity.

In conclusion, the forward-backward CP asymmetry provides an experimentally accessible window into the scalar sector of the Left-Right Inverse Seesaw model. The non-decoupling property of the box contributions, combined with the kinematic enhancement from the
K_{0}^{*}(1430)
resonance, makes
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm FB}(s)
a golden observable for probing TeV-scale left-right symmetry and the inverse seesaw mechanism at Belle II and future high-luminosity flavor factories.
