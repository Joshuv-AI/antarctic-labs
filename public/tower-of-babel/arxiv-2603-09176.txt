1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

For any integer
m
, let
\mu_{m}
denote the group of
m
-th roots of unity. Let
p
be a prime and set
\mu_{p^{\infty}}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mu_{p^{n}}
. For any number field
{\mathcal{F}}
, let
{\mathcal{F}}_{cyc}
be the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension over
{\mathcal{F}}
. This is defined as the subfield of
{\mathcal{F}}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
fixed by the torsion subgroup of the Galois group
{\rm Gal}({\mathcal{F}}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/{\mathcal{F}})
.
For each integer
n\geq 0
, we define
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
as the unique intermediate field in
{\mathcal{F}}_{cyc}
such that
[{\mathcal{F}}_{n}:{\mathcal{F}}]=p^{n}
.
Iwasawa
[
11
]
(see also
[
12
]
) proved the existence of integers
\mu_{\mathcal{C}},\lambda_{\mathcal{C}},\nu_{\mathcal{C}}
, which depend solely on
{\mathcal{F}}
and
p
. These Iwasawa invariants describe the asymptotic behavior of the order of the
p
-primary part of the ideal class group. Specifically, if
e_{n}

is the exponent such that
p^{e_{n}}
is the order of the
p
-primary part
Cl({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(p)
of the ideal class group of
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
, then for
n\geq n_{{\mathcal{C}},p}
, we have

e_{n}=\mu_{\mathcal{C}}\cdot p^{n}+\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}\cdot n+\nu_{\mathcal{C}}.

Here
n_{{\mathcal{C}},p}
is a sufficiently large positive integer. In general, determining the Iwasawa invariants
\lambda_{\mathcal{C}},\mu_{\mathcal{C}}
,
\nu_{\mathcal{C}}
, and the precise value of
n_{{\mathcal{C}},p}
is a challenging problem.

A significant breakthrough was made by Ferrero and Washington
[
7
]
, who proved that
\mu_{\mathcal{C}}=0
if
{\mathcal{F}}
is an abelian extension over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Prior to this, Iwasawa himself
[
13
]
has shown that
\mu_{\mathcal{C}}=0
when
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathbb{Q}}
or an imaginary quadratic field, and
p=2
. Later, under the same assumptions as Iwasawa, Kida
[
15
]
provided an explicit formula for the
\lambda
-invariants (see also
[
8
]
) and established a lower bound for
n_{{\mathcal{C}},2}
.

It’s well known that the ideal class group of a number field
{\mathcal{F}}
can be identified with the reduced
K_{0}
group of its ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Regarding other
K
-groups of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{F}}
, Quillen
[
16
]
and Borel
[
1
]
demonstrated that
K_{2m}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}
is finite for positive integer
m
(see also
[
9
]
). As is customary, we refer to
K_{2}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{F}}
as the tame kernel of
{\mathcal{F}}
.

Let
p^{e(n)}
denote the order of the
p
-primary part
K_{2m}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{n}}(p)
. Coates
[
5
]
, Ji and Qin
[
14
]
have established the following significant result:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists three integers
\mu,\lambda,\nu
, depending solely on
{\mathcal{F}}
,
m
and
p
, such that for all sufficiently large
n\geq n_{{\mathcal{F}},p}
:

e(n)=\mu\cdot p^{n}+\lambda\cdot n+\nu.

Here,
n_{{\mathcal{F}},p}
is a sufficiently large positive integer.

Similar to ideal class groups, a natural question arises regarding the Iwasawa invariants
\mu,\lambda,\nu
and
n_{{\mathcal{F}},p}
. Apart from Candiotti’s work
[
4
]
, there appears to be little progress on this question. This paper will study the invariants
\mu,\lambda,\nu
and
n_{{\mathcal{F}},p}
under the condition that
p=2
and
{\mathcal{F}}
is either
{\mathbb{Q}}
or a real quadratic number field. Henceforth, we set
p=2
and denote
n_{{\mathcal{F}},p}
by
n_{\mathcal{F}}
.

1.2.
Main Theorems

Let
d
be a square-free positive integer, and let
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})

K_{cyc}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension over
K

[K_{n}:K]=2^{n}

d=1
,
K={\mathbb{Q}}
, and when

{\mathbb{Q}}_{cyc}

{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})={\mathbb{Q}}_{1}

K_{{2m-2}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{n}}(2)

d
is odd. Here we assume that
m

{\rm ord}_{2}

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}

.

d

m

\mu=2
if
{\mathrm{ord}}_{2}(m)=1

\mu=0

m
;

\lambda=-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}},

. Here

is the Legendre symbol.

It is important to note that the even

-invariant.

Corollary 1.3
.

For any nonnegative integer

(1) If

K_{2}\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}(2)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2^{n}}.

(2) If

Our second main result is the following example, in which we explicitly compute the
\lambda
,
\mu
and
\nu
invariants of the tame kernels for a family of real quadratic number fields whose discriminants can have arbitrarily many prime divisors. Let
d
be a square free positive integer, and define
\psi_{d}
the Dirichlet character associated to
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})/{\mathbb{Q}}
. Denote by
L(\psi_{d},s)
the Dirichlet
L
-series associated with

\psi_{d}
. It is well known that
L(\psi_{d},-1)
is a non-zero rational number.

Example 1.4
.

Let
d=p_{1}\cdots p_{r}
, where the primes
p_{i}\equiv 5\ (mod\ 8)
are distinct and satisfy
(\frac{p_{i}}{p_{j}})=-1
for
i\neq j
, and

is odd. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})

ord_{2}(|K_{2}\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}(2)|)=2\cdot 2^{n}+(r-1)\cdot n+({\rm ord}_{2}(L(\psi_{2d},-1))-1)\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

In particular,
\mu=2
,
\lambda=r-1
, and
\nu={\rm ord}_{2}(L(\psi_{2d},-1))-1
.

1.3.
Outline of the Proof of the Main Theorem

Let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{cyc}
be the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
denote the unique intermediate field in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{cyc}
such that
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}:{\mathbb{Q}}]=2^{n}
. Denote by
\chi_{n}
a primitive character of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. We can view
\chi_{n}
as a Dirichlet character modulo
2^{n+2}
for all
n\geq 1
. The key ingredient in proving our main results is the study of the
2
-adic divisibility of the
L
-values
L(\psi_{d}\chi_{n},1-m)
as
n
varies. Here
d
is a square free positive odd integer,
\psi_{d}
is the Dirichlet character associated to
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
, and
m
is a positive even integer. The result is summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
m
be a positive even integer, and let
n
be a positive integer. Assume that
d\geq 1
is a square-free odd integer.
Then there exists a positive integer
n_{d}
such that for all
n\geq n_{d}
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},1-m))=1+2^{1-n}\cdot\left(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right),

where each
f_{p}
is the constant defined in Theorem
1.2
.

We remark that when
d=1
, the equality in the proposition takes the form

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n},1-m))=1-2^{1-n},

and in this case, we can show that
n_{d}=1
.

Our method to prove the Proposition
1.5
can be divided into the following two steps:

—

Step
1
: The case
d=1
.
In this case, we only know a priori that
L(\chi_{n},1-m)
is a nonzero algebraic number.
By applying the von Staudt–Clausen theorem, we can show that
L(\chi_{n},1-m)
is
2
-adically integral. This leads to a key observation:

(1.1)

L(\chi_{n},1-m)\equiv L(\chi_{n},-1)\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Recall that
\chi_{n}
has conductor
2^{n+2}
, we then prove the congruence

(1.2)

L(\chi_{n},-1)\equiv\prod^{n}_{k=2}(1-\zeta_{2^{k}})\,({\rm mod}\,2)\quad\textrm{for $n\geq 2$,}

where
\zeta_{2^{k}}
is a primitive
2^{k}
-th root of unity satisfying
\zeta_{2^{k}}^{2}=\zeta_{2^{k-1}}
. Therefore, the proposition follows in the case
d=1
. Note that for any two algebraic numbers in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2^{n}})
, the above congruences mean that their difference belongs to
2({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}[\zeta_{2^{n}}]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2^{n}}))
.

—

Step
2
: The general case
d>1
.
Let
D=2^{n+2}d
. Denote by
L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},s)
the imprimitive
L
-function asscoaited to
\chi_{n}
, obtained by omitting Euler factors at all primes
\ell|D
. Using a delicate special value formula modulo
4
, we derive the key congruence

L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},1-m)+L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},1-m)\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2(1+\zeta_{4})),

where
\zeta_{4}
is a primitive
4
-th root of unity.
This congruence allows us to reduce the general case to the case
d=1
. Here the congruence also means that the sum of the two
L
-values belongs to
2(1+\zeta_{4})({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}[\zeta_{2^{n}}]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2^{n}}))
.

For a number field
{\mathcal{F}}
, let
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}(s)
denote the Dedekind zeta function associated to
{\mathcal{F}}
. Proposition
1.5
implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
m
be a positive even integer.

(1)

For all non-negative integer
n
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(\zeta_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}}(1-m))=2^{n}-n-(2+{\rm ord}_{2}(m)).

(2)

Let
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
. For all integers
n\geq n_{d}
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(\zeta_{K_{n}}(1-m))=2\cdot 2^{n}+n\cdot\left(\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}-2\right)+\nu^{\prime}_{m,d},

where
\nu^{\prime}_{m,d}
is a constant depending only on
m
and
d
. One can refer to Theorem
3.12
for the precise definition.

For a totally real abelian number field
{\mathcal{F}}
, since the Iwasawa main conjecture is known to hold (see
[
20
]
), the Quillen-Lichtenbaum conjecture follows. In particular, we have

|K_{2m-2}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{F}})|=\begin{cases}w_{m}({\mathcal{F}})\cdot\left|\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}(1-m)\right|\quad&\textrm{if ${\rm ord}_{2}(m)=1$}\\
2^{-[{\mathcal{F}}:{\mathbb{Q}}]}\cdot w_{m}({\mathcal{F}})\cdot\left|\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}(1-m)\right|\quad&\textrm{if ${\rm ord}_{2}(m)\geq 2$.}\end{cases}

Here,
w_{m}({\mathcal{F}})
denotes the largest integer
{\mathfrak{N}}
such that
{\rm Gal}({\mathcal{F}}(\zeta_{\mathfrak{N}})/{\mathcal{F}})
has exponent dividing
m
(
[
18
]
). Since the number
w_{m}({\mathcal{F}})
can be determined explicitly, Theorem
1.2
follows directly from Corollary
1.6
. To prove (2) in Corollary
1.3
and Example
1.4
, we need to show that
n_{K}=2
for those fields
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
. This involves an induction method of
L
-values, which we borrow some ideas from
[
6
]
.

The Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}
and the Bernoulli polynomials
B_{n}(X)
are defined via the generating functions:

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\qquad\frac{te^{Xt}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(X)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

The Bernoulli polynomials satisfy the identity:

(2.1)

B_{n}(X)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{k}X^{n-k}.

For a Dirichlet character
\chi

D

\sum_{a=1}^{D}\frac{\chi(a)te^{at}}{e^{Dt}-1}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\chi}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

If
D_{0}
is any multiple of

, from
[

we have

(2.2)

B_{n,\chi}=D_{0}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D_{0}}\chi(a)B_{n}\left(\frac{a}{D_{0}}\right).

Next, we present two special value formulas for Dirichlet

Lemma 2.1

(
[
6
]
, Lemma 4;
[

]
, Theorem 4.2)

Let
\chi

L(\chi,1-n)=-\frac{B_{n,\chi}}{n}

In particular, if
\chi
is a non-trivial even character modulo

, then for any positive integer

L(\chi,-1)=-\frac{1}{2kD_{1}}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{kD_{1}}\chi(a)a^{2}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
6
]
, Proposition 5)

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo an even integer

, and let

be another odd squarefree positive integer. Put

L^{(D_{1})}(\chi,-1)=-\frac{1}{2D_{1}}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D_{1}}\chi(a)\psi_{d^{\prime}}(a)^{2}a^{2}=L(\chi\psi_{d^{\prime}}^{2},-1).

Proof.

For

L^{(D_{1})}(\chi,-1)=\prod\limits_{\ell\nmid dd^{\prime}}(1-\chi(\ell)\ell^{-s})^{-1}=\prod\limits_{\ell\nmid d^{\prime},\text{odd}}(1-\chi(\ell)\ell^{-s})^{-1}.

For an odd prime

, we have
\chi(\ell)=\chi(\ell)\psi_{d^{\prime}}(\ell)^{2}
. The rest of the proof is identical with
[
6
, Proposition 5]
.
∎

We now present several relations involving character sums, which will play an important role in the proof of the equation (
1.2
). Recall that
\chi_{n}
is a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
2^{n+2}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
d
be a square free positive odd integer. Let
\psi
be an even Dirichlet character modulo
4d
, and define

\chi=\chi_{n}\psi
, a nontrivial even Dirichlet character modulo
D=2^{n+2}d
. Then

-\frac{1}{2D}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D}\chi(a)a^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi(a)a.

Proof.

Since
\chi(D-a)=\chi(a)
for all
a
, we have

-\frac{1}{2D}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D}\chi(a)a^{2}=-\frac{1}{2D}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi(a)\bigg(a^{2}+(D-a)^{2}\bigg).

Note that
\chi
is nontrivial, expanding the right-hand side, we obtain

-\frac{1}{2D}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D}\chi(a)a^{2}=-\frac{1}{D}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi(a)a^{2}+\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi(a)a.

We now claim that

-\frac{1}{D}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi(a)a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi(a)a=0.

Once this claim is established, the lemma follows immediately.
To prove the claim, note that since
\chi
is nontrivial, we have
\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi(a)=0
. It is easy to verify that

-\frac{1}{D}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi(a)a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi(a)a=-\frac{1}{2D}\sum^{D/2}_{a=1}\left(\chi(a)+\chi\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)\right)\cdot a^{2}.

Now we show that

\chi\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)=-\chi(a)

for all integers
a
. Indeed, we may assume
a
is odd. Otherwise, both
\chi(a)
and
\chi\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)
are zero. Recall that
\chi=\psi\chi_{n}
, since
\psi
is even and has conductor dividing
4d
,
\psi\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)=\psi(a)
. For the character
\chi_{n}
,
note that

\chi_{n}\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)=\chi_{n}(a)\cdot\chi_{n}(1-2^{n+1}).

By the primitivity of
\chi_{n}
, we have
\chi_{n}(1-2^{n+1})=-1
, so

\chi_{n}\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)=-\chi_{n}(a),

and hence
\chi\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)=-\chi(a)
. This completes the proof of the claim.
∎

To give our second relation between character sums, we need the following

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
n\geq 2
. Then for any integer
b
, we have

(1)

\chi_{n}(2^{n+1}-b)=-\chi_{n}(b)
.

(2)

\chi_{n}(2^{n}-b)=\chi_{n}(2^{n}-1)\cdot\left(\frac{-1}{b}\right)\cdot\chi_{n}(b)
.

Proof.

We only prove (2).
We may assume that
b
is odd. Recall that
\chi_{n}
is a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor
2^{n+2}
. Then we have

\frac{\chi_{n}(2^{n}-b)}{\chi_{n}(b)\chi_{n}(2^{n}-1)}=\chi_{n}(1+2^{n}(1-b^{\prime})),

where
b^{\prime}
is an integer such that
bb^{\prime}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2^{n+2}
. Note that
b\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
. The lemma follows from the equality

\chi_{n}(1+2^{n}(1-b^{\prime}))=\left(\frac{-1}{b}\right).

∎

Let
\zeta_{4}
denote a primitive
4
-th root of unity.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume that
n\geq 2
and
\chi_{n}(2^{n}-1)=\pm\zeta_{4}
. Keep the assumptions as in Lemma
2.3
. Then

-\frac{1}{2D}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D}\chi(a)a^{2}\equiv(1\mp\zeta_{4})\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/8}\chi(a)\cdot a\varepsilon_{a}\ ({\rm mod}\ 4),

where
\varepsilon_{a}=1
if
a\equiv 1\ ({\rm mod}\ 4)
and
\varepsilon_{a}=\pm\zeta_{4}
if
a\equiv 3\ ({\rm mod}\ 4)
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.3
and (1) in Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

-\frac{1}{2D}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi(a)a^{2}=\sum^{D/4}_{a=1}\chi(a)\cdot\left(a-\frac{D}{4}\right).

Since
n\geq 2
so
4
divides
D/4
. Then we get

-\frac{1}{2D}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi(a)a^{2}\equiv\sum^{D/4}_{a=1}\chi(a)a\,({\rm mod}\,4).

From (2) in Lemma
2.4
, we have

(2.3)

\chi\left(\frac{D}{4}-a\right)=\pm\zeta_{4}\cdot\left(\frac{-1}{a}\right)\chi_{n}(a).

Now, observe that

\sum^{D/4}_{a=1}\chi(a)a=\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\chi(a)a+\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\chi\left(\frac{D}{4}-a\right)\cdot\left(\frac{D}{4}-a\right).

Using (
2.3
), we obtain

\sum^{D/4}_{a=1}\chi(a)a\equiv\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\chi(a)\cdot a\cdot\left(1\mp\zeta_{4}\cdot\left(\frac{-1}{a}\right)\right)\,({\rm mod}\,4).

A direct verification shows that

\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\chi(a)\cdot a\cdot\left(1\mp\zeta_{4}\cdot\left(\frac{-1}{a}\right)\right)=(1\mp\zeta_{4})\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\chi(a)a\varepsilon_{a},

so the proposition follows.
∎

From Lemma
2.1
and the above proof, we obtain a corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

For each positive integer
n
, we have

(2.4)

L(\chi_{n},-1)\equiv\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\,({\rm mod}\,2).

3.
2
-adic divisibility of Dirichlet
L
-values

We will prove Proposition
1.5
and Corollary
1.6
in this section. As always, let
d
denote a positive square-free odd integer, and put
D=2^{n+2}d
, where
n
is a non-negative integer. According to the strategy outlined in the introduction, we first consider the case
d=1
, and then prove the general case by reducing it to this special case.

3.1.
2
-adic divisibility of Dirichlet
L
-values for
d=1
and
m=2

In this case, note that
D=2^{n+2}
. We will prove Equalities (
1.2
), (
1.1
) in this subsection by using the relations between the character sums established in the last section. Recall that we have fixed a compatible system of
2^{n}
-th roots of unity
\zeta_{2^{n}}
, satisfying
\zeta_{2^{n+1}}^{2}=\zeta_{2^{n}}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D=2^{n+2}
and let
k
be an integer such that
2\leq k\leq n
. For any integer
b
, we have

\chi_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2^{k}}D-b\right)\equiv\zeta_{2^{k}}\cdot\chi_{n}(b)\ \left({\rm mod}\ (1-\zeta_{2^{k-1}}){\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{2^{n}}]\right).

Proof.

Note that the image of
\chi_{n}
is equal to the group of
2^{n}
-th roots of unity.
For an integer
\alpha
such that
n+2\geq\alpha\geq 2
, and for an odd integer
c
, a direct calculation shows that
\chi_{n}(1+2^{\alpha}c)
is a primitive
2^{n+2-\alpha}
-th root of unity. Let
b^{\prime}
be an integer such that
bb^{\prime}\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,2^{n+2})
. Then we have

\frac{\chi_{n}(b-2^{n+2-k})}{\chi_{n}(b)}=\chi_{n}(1-2^{n+2-k}b^{\prime}),

which is a primitive
2^{k}
-th root of unity. Working modulo
(1-\zeta_{2^{k-1}}){\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{2^{n}}]
, this primitive
2^{k}
-th root of unity is uniquely determined. Therefore, the lemma follows.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that
n
is a positive integer, then
L(\chi_{1},-1)=-1
and for
n\geq 2
, we have

L(\chi_{n},-1)\equiv\prod^{n}_{k=2}(1-\zeta_{2^{k}})\,({\rm mod}\,2{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{2^{n}}]).

In particular,

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n},-1))=1-2^{1-n}.

Proof.

For
n=1
, noting that

\zeta(-1)=-\frac{1}{12},\quad\zeta_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})}(-1)=\frac{1}{12},

we have
L(\chi_{1},-1)=-1
. Assume that
n\geq 2
. All congruence below are taken in
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{2^{n}}]
. For each
2\leq k\leq n
, using the identity

\sum^{\frac{D}{2^{k}}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a=\sum^{\frac{D}{2^{k+1}}}_{a=1}\left(\chi_{n}(a)a+\chi_{n}\left(\frac{D}{2^{k}}-a\right)\cdot\left(\frac{D}{2^{k}}-a\right)\right)

and applying Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the following congruence

(3.1)

\sum^{\frac{D}{2^{k}}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\equiv(1-\zeta_{2^{k}})\sum^{\frac{D}{2^{k+1}}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\ \mathrm{mod}\ (1-\zeta_{2^{k-1}}).

By Lemmas
2.1
,
2.3
and (1) of Lemma
2.4
, we have

L(\chi_{n},-1)=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\equiv\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Applying (
3.1
) with
k=2
, we obtain

L(\chi_{n},-1)\equiv(1-\zeta_{2^{2}})\sum^{\frac{D}{8}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Suppose that for an integer
m
with
3\leq m\leq n
, we have the congruence

L(\chi_{n},-1)\equiv\prod^{m-1}_{j=2}(1-\zeta_{2^{j}})\cdot\sum^{\frac{D}{2^{m}}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Noting the equality of ideals

(1-\zeta_{2^{m-1}})\cdot\prod^{m-1}_{j=2}(1-\zeta_{2^{j}}){\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{2^{n}}]=2{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{2^{n}}],

and applying equation (
3.1
) with
k=m
, we obtain

\prod^{m-1}_{j=2}(1-\zeta_{2^{j}})\cdot\sum^{\frac{D}{2^{m}}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\equiv\prod^{m}_{j=2}(1-\zeta_{2^{m}})\sum^{\frac{D}{2^{m+1}}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Iterating this process, we get

L(\chi_{n},-1)\equiv\prod^{n}_{k=1}(1-\zeta_{2^{k}})\cdot\sum^{D/2^{n+1}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a=\prod^{n}_{k=1}(1-\zeta_{2^{k}})\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Here we recall that
D=2^{n+2}
, so
\sum^{2}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a=1
. Therefore, the proposition follows.

∎

3.2.
2
-adic divisibility of Dirichlet
L
-values for
d=1
and general
m

In this subsection, we determine the
2
-adic valuation of
L(\chi_{n},1-m)
for all
n\geq 1
and all positive even integers
m
.

We begin by presenting several lemmas that will be used in the proof for both cases:
d=1
and general
d
. We omit the proof of the following lemma, as it is a standard exercise in calculus.

Lemma 3.3
.

Denote by
\log x
the natural logarithm of
x
. For every real number
x\geq 2
, we have
(1)
3x-3-\frac{\log x}{\log 2}\geq 2
;
(2)
4x-4-\frac{\log x}{\log 2}\geq 3
; (3) if
x\geq 3
,
2x-3-\frac{\log x}{\log 2}\geq 1
; (4) if
x\geq 3
,
3x-4-\frac{\log x}{\log 2}\geq 2
.

Recall that
D=2^{n+2}d
, where
d
is a square free positive odd integer.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n,k
be two positive integers. Assume
k\geq 2
.

(1)

We have
{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\frac{D^{k-1}}{k}\right)\geq 2
. If
n\geq 2
, we have
{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\frac{D^{k-1}}{k}\right)\geq 3
.

(2)

We have
{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\frac{D^{k-1}}{k2^{k}}\right)\geq 1
unless
n=1
and
k=2
.

(3)

If
n\geq 2
, we have
{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\frac{D^{k-1}}{k2^{k}}\right)\geq 2
unless
n=2
and
k=2
.

Proof.

This lemma follows from Lemma
3.3
, we only show (3). If
n\geq 2,k\geq 2
, noting that
{\rm ord}_{2}(k)\leq\frac{\log k}{\log 2}
, we obtain

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\frac{D^{k-1}}{k2^{k}}\right)\geq 3k-4-\frac{\log k}{\log 2}\geq 2

unless
n=k=2
.
∎

For
{\mathfrak{N}}=D
or
\frac{D}{2}
, we define

\mathscr{S}({\mathfrak{N}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{{\mathfrak{N}}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m-1}.

We also define

\mathscr{T}(D)=\frac{2}{mD}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m}.

Lemma 3.5
.

(1)

The number
\mathscr{S}(D)
is integral and
\mathscr{S}(D)\equiv 0({\rm mod}\,4)
.

(2)

We have
\mathscr{T}(D)\equiv\mathscr{S}(\frac{D}{2})({\rm mod}\,2)
. If
n\geq 2
, then
\mathscr{T}(D)\equiv\mathscr{S}(\frac{D}{2})({\rm mod}\,4)
.

Proof.

Note the identity

\mathscr{S}(D)=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\cdot\left(a^{m-1}+(D-a)^{m-1}\right).

Recall that
D=2^{n+2}d
with
n\geq 1
and
d
an odd integer. It follows that
\mathscr{S}(D)
is
2
-adically integral. Moreover, since
m
is even,
we obtain

\mathscr{S}(D)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)(a^{m-1}+(-a)^{m-1})=0\,({\rm mod}\,4).

Hence the part (1) follows. For the second part, we prove only the congruence modulo
4
, as the modulo
2
congruence can be shown similarly. From Lemma
2.4
, we have
\chi_{n}\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)=-\chi_{n}(a)
. Therefore, we get

\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\left(a^{m}+\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)^{m}\right)=0,

equivalently,

(3.2)

0=\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\cdot\left(\frac{2}{mD}a^{m}+\sum^{m}_{k=1}\frac{D^{k-1}}{k2^{k}}\binom{m-1}{k-1}(-a)^{m-k}\right).

Consider the terms in the sum in the bracket. From Lemma
3.4
, for all
k\geq 2
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\frac{D^{k-1}}{k2^{k}}\right)\geq 2

unless
n=k=2
. In this exceptional case, we analyze the term

\frac{D}{8}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\cdot(m-1)a^{m-2}.

It turns out to be

(2d)\cdot\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\cdot(m-1)\cdot\left(a^{m-2}-\left(2^{3}d-a\right)^{m-2}\right)\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,4).

From equation (
3.2
), we then deduce

\mathscr{T}(D)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m-1}\,({\rm mod}\,4).

This completes the proof in the case

n\geq 2
.
∎

Now we come back to the case
d=1
, i.e.,
D=2^{n+2}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
m
be a positive even integer. Then
L(\chi_{n},1-m)
is
2
-adically integral, and we have

L(\chi_{n},1-m)\equiv L(\chi_{n},-1)\,({\rm mod}\,2).

In particular,
{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n},1-m))=1-2^{1-n}
.

Proof.

The key idea is to apply the von Staudt–Clausen theorem to reduce the Bernoulli numbers appearing in the expression for
L(\chi_{n},1-m)
to character sums such as
\mathscr{S}(D)
and
\mathscr{T}(D)
considered in the above.

From Lemma
2.1
, and formulas (
2.2
), (
2.1
), we get

-L(\chi_{n},1-m)=\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m}+\frac{1}{m}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\sum^{m}_{k=1}\binom{m}{k}B_{k}\cdot D^{k-1}\cdot a^{m-k}.

This equality can be rewritten as

(3.3)

-L(\chi_{n},1-m)=\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m}+\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\sum^{m}_{k=1}\binom{m-1}{k-1}\frac{D^{k-1}}{k}B_{k}\cdot a^{m-k}.

By Lemma
3.4
(1) and the von Staudt-Clausen Theorem, we know that

\sum^{m}_{k=2}\binom{m-1}{k-1}\frac{D^{k-1}}{k}B_{k}\cdot a^{m-k}\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Since
B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
, (
3.3
) implies that

(3.4)

L(\chi_{n},1-m)\equiv-\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m}+\mathscr{S}(D)\,({\rm mod}\,2).

We now analyze the term

\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m}
.
Note the identity

\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m}=\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{n=1}\chi_{n}(a)\left(a^{m}+(D-a)^{m}\right).

Using the binomial expansion and the same computation method as in (
3.3
), and applying Lemma
3.4
(1), we obtain

(3.5)

\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a^{m}\equiv\mathscr{T}(D)-2\mathscr{S}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Therefore, the formula (
3.4
) becomes to be

L(\chi_{n},1-m)\equiv-\mathscr{T}(D)+2\mathscr{S}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)+\mathscr{S}(D)\,({\rm mod}\,2).

From Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

(3.6)

L(\chi_{n},1-m)\equiv\mathscr{S}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)\,({\rm mod}\,2).

By definition,

\mathscr{S}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\left(a^{m-1}-\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)^{m-1}\right)\equiv\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)a\,({\rm mod}\,2).

Hence, by (
3.6
),
L(\chi_{n},1-m)
is
2
-adic integral. Finally, by Corollary
2.6
, we conclude that

L(\chi_{n},1-m)\equiv L(\chi_{n},-1)\,({\rm mod}\,2).

This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

3.3.
2
-adic divisibility of Dirichlet
L
-values for general
d
and
m

We now turn to the case
d>1
. For an odd prime
p
, we denote by
p^{*}=\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)p
and put
f_{p}={\rm ord}_{2}\left(\frac{p^{*}-1}{4}\right)
.
We will prove the following theorem in this subsection.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
n,d
be two positive integers. Assume that
d
is odd square-free and
d\geq 2
. Let
\psi_{d}
be the Dirichlet character associated to
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})/{\mathbb{Q}}
. There exists a positive integer
n_{d}
such that for all integers
n\geq n_{d}
, we have

{\mathrm{ord}}_{2}(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},1-m))=1+2^{1-n}\left(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right).

A key step to prove the above theorem is to show the following lemma, which is a refinement of the proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
D=2^{n+2}d
, where
d
is a positive odd square-free integer. Let
\eta
denote one of the three characters
\chi_{n}
,
\chi_{n}\psi_{d}
or
\chi_{n}\psi^{2}_{d}
. Assume that
n\geq 2
, then

L(\eta,1-m)
is
2
-adically integral, and

L(\eta,1-m)\equiv\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\eta(a)a\,({\rm mod}\,4).

Proof.

Since the conductor of
\eta
divides
D
, and using equation (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

L(\eta,1-m)=-\frac{D^{k-1}}{m}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\eta(a)\sum^{m}_{k=0}\binom{m}{k}B_{k}\cdot a^{m-k}.

From Lemma
3.4
(1), we know that

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\frac{D^{k-1}}{k}\right)\geq 3
. By similar methods as in equations (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and invoking the von Staudt-Clausen theorem, we obtain

(3.7)

L(\eta,1-m)\equiv-\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\eta(a)a^{m}-\mathscr{S}_{\eta}(D)\,({\rm mod}\,4),

where for a positive integer
{\mathfrak{N}}
, we define
\mathscr{S}_{\eta}({\mathfrak{N}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\mathfrak{N}}_{a=1}\eta(a)a^{m-1}
. For the term
\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\eta(a)a^{m}
, using the same computation as in (
3.5
), we get

(3.8)

\frac{1}{mD}\sum^{D}_{a=1}\eta(a)a^{m}\equiv\mathscr{T}_{\eta}(D)-2\mathscr{S}_{\eta}(D/2)\,({\rm mod}\,4),

where we define
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}(D)=\frac{2}{mD}\sum^{\frac{D}{2}}_{a=1}\eta(a)a^{m}
.
It follows, by the same method as in Lemma
3.5
, that

\mathscr{S}_{\eta}(D)\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,4)
and
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}(D)\equiv\mathscr{S}_{\eta}(D/2)\,({\rm mod}\,4)
. Therefore, from (
3.7
), we obtain

L(\eta,1-m)\equiv\mathscr{S}_{\eta}(D/2)\,({\rm mod}\,4).

Moreover, we observe that

\mathscr{S}_{\eta}(D/2)=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\eta(a)\cdot\left(a^{m-1}-\left(\frac{D}{2}-a\right)^{m-1}\right)\equiv\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\eta(a)a^{m-1}\,({\rm mod}\,4),

thus
L(\eta,1-m)
is
2
-adically integral. Since
m
is positive and even, and
\eta(a)=0
unless
a
is odd, we have
a^{m-1}\equiv a\,({\rm mod}\,4)
. Hence, the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
D=2^{n+2}d
, where
d
is a positive odd square free integer. Let
\eta
denote one of the three characters
\chi_{n}
,
\chi_{n}\psi_{d}
or
\chi_{n}\psi^{2}_{d}
. Assume that
n\geq 2
, then

L(\eta,1-m)\equiv(1\mp\zeta_{4})\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\eta(a)a\cdot\varepsilon_{a}\,({\rm mod}\,4).

where
\varepsilon_{a}
is defined in Proposition
2.5
and
\chi_{n}(2^{n}d-1)=\pm\zeta_{4}
.

Proof.

Observe that

\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\eta(a)a=\sum^{\frac{D}{8}}_{a=1}\eta(a)a+\sum^{\frac{D}{8}}_{a=1}\eta\left(\frac{D}{4}-a\right)\cdot\left(\frac{D}{4}-a\right).

Note that

\chi_{n}(2^{n}d-a)=\chi_{n}(2^{n}-a)\cdot\left(\frac{-1}{d}\right),

and Lemma
2.4
, we have
\eta\left(\frac{D}{4}-a\right)=\eta\left(\frac{D}{4}-1\right)\cdot\left(\frac{-1}{a}\right)\cdot\eta(a)
. Applying the same computation as in Proposition
2.5
, we obtain

\sum^{\frac{D}{4}}_{a=1}\eta(a)a\equiv(1\mp\zeta_{4})\sum^{\frac{D}{8}}_{a=1}\eta(a)a\cdot\varepsilon_{a}\,({\rm mod}\,4).

Then, by Lemma
3.8
, the result follows.
∎

We now prove Theorem
3.7
.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

We claim that the following congruence holds:

(3.9)

L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},1-m)+L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},1-m)\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2(1+\zeta_{4})).

Assuming the claim, we derive the theorem.
By definition, we have

L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},1-m)=L(\chi_{n},1-m)\cdot\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1-\chi_{n}(p)p^{m-1}\right).

From Proposition
3.6
, we obtain

{\rm ord}_{2}(L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},1-m))=1-2^{1-n}+\sum_{p\mid d}{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\left(1-\chi_{n}(p)p^{m-1}\right)\right).

To compute the
2
-adic valuations of the terms in the sum above, note that

1-\chi_{n}(p)p^{m-1}\equiv 1-\chi_{n}(p)\,({\rm mod}\,2).

It is known that, for
n>f_{p}
, the order of the Frobenius element associated to
p
in
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}})
is equal to
2^{n-f_{p}}
. Therefore, for
n>f_{p}+1
we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(1-\chi_{n}(p))=2^{1-n+f_{p}}<1.

The congruence above together with the strong triangle inequality for
{\rm ord}_{2}
imply that for such
n

{\rm ord}_{2}(1-\chi_{n}(p)p^{m-1})={\rm ord}_{2}(1-\chi_{n}(p))=2^{1-n+f_{p}}.

So, if
n>f_{p}+1
for all
p\mid d
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}\bigg(\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1-\chi_{n}(p)p^{m-1}\right)\bigg)=2^{1-n}\cdot\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}.

Therefore,

{\rm ord}_{2}(L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},1-m))=1+2^{1-n}\cdot(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}).

Now, invoking the congruence (
3.9
), when
n
is sufficiently large, say there exists an integer
n_{d}
, such that for all
n\geq n_{d}
,

1+2^{1-n}\cdot(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}})<\frac{3}{2},

By the claim (
3.9
) and the strong triangle inequality for
{\rm ord}_{2}
again, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},1-m))={\rm ord}_{2}(L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},1-m))=1+2^{1-n}\cdot(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}).

Now, we prove the claim. Recall from Lemma
2.2
that

L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},1-m)=L(\chi_{n}\psi^{2}_{d},1-m).

Apply Lemma
3.9
to
\eta=\chi_{n}\psi_{d}^{2}
and
\eta=\chi_{n}\psi_{d}
respectively, we get

L(\chi_{n}\psi^{2}_{d},1-m)\equiv(1\mp\zeta_{4})\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\psi_{d}(a)^{2}a\varepsilon_{a}\,({\rm mod}\,4),

and

L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},1-m)\equiv(1\mp\zeta_{4})\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\psi_{d}(a)a\varepsilon_{a}\,({\rm mod}\,4).

Therefore, taking the sum of the congruences above we obtain

L^{(D)}(\chi_{n},1-m)+L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},1-m)\equiv(1\mp\zeta_{4})\sum^{D/8}_{a=1}\chi_{n}(a)\psi_{d}(a)\cdot a\varepsilon_{a}\cdot(1+\psi_{d}(a))\,({\rm mod}\,4).

Note that
2
divides
1+\psi_{d}(a)
. Thus,
the right hand side is divisible by
2(1+\zeta_{4})
, and the claim follows.
∎

For a real number
x
, we define
\lceil x\rceil={\rm min}\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\geq x\}
. Concerning the number
n_{d}
, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.10
.

Assume that
d>1
. Let
f=\max\limits_{p|d}\{f_{p}\}
. Then we can take

n_{d}=\lceil f+\log_{2}(\tau(d))+2\rceil,

where
\tau(d)
is the number of distinct prime divisors of
d
.

Proof.

From the proof of Theorem
3.7
, we require that for
n\geq n_{d}
, the inequality

1+2^{1-n}\cdot\left(-1+\sum\limits_{p|d}2^{f_{p}}\right)<\frac{3}{2}

holds.
Since

\sum\limits_{p|d}2^{f_{p}}\leq\tau(d)\cdot 2^{f},

therefore, we can take

n_{d}=\lceil f+\log_{2}(\tau(d))+2\rceil.

∎

3.4.
2
-adic divisibility of Dedekind zeta-values

Let
\zeta(s)
denote the Riemann zeta function.

Lemma 3.11
.

For each positive even integer
m
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(\zeta(1-m))=-1-{\rm ord}_{2}(m).

Proof.

Note that
\zeta(1-m)=-\frac{B_{m}}{m}
. By the von Staudt-Clausen theorem, we obtain
{\rm ord}_{2}(B_{m})=-1
. Therefore, the result follows.
∎

Let
d>1
be a positive odd square-free integer. Let
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
. For each non-negative integer
n
, let
K_{n}
denote the unique subfield in
K_{cyc}
such that
[K_{n}:K]=2^{n}
. Note that
K_{n}={\mathbb{Q}}_{n}(\sqrt{d})
,
where
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
denotes the
n
-th layer of the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
m
be a positive even integer and let
n
be a non-negative integer.

(1)

We have

{\rm ord}_{2}(\zeta_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}}(1-m))=2^{n}-n-2-{\rm ord}_{2}(m).

(2)

For all
n\geq n_{d}
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(\zeta_{K_{n}}(1-m))=2\cdot 2^{n}+\left(-2+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right)\cdot n+\nu^{\prime}_{m,d},

where

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}_{m,d}=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}}_{2}\left(\frac{L(\psi_{d},1-m)}{4m}\right)-2^{n_{d}}+n_{d}\left(1-\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum^{n_{d}}_{k=1}2^{k-1}\cdot{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{k}\psi_{d},1-m)).

Proof.

For (1), from the formula

\zeta_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}}(1-m)=\prod^{n}_{\ell=0}\prod_{\chi_{\ell}}L(\chi_{\ell},1-m),

where the second product is taken over
all primitive characters
\chi_{\ell}
of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.
From Lemma
3.11
,
{\rm ord}_{2}(\zeta(1-m))=-1-{\rm ord}_{2}(m)
. We apply Proposition
3.6
to obtain

{\rm ord}_{2}(\zeta_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}}(1-m))=2^{n}-n-2-{\rm ord}_{2}(m).

This proves (1).

Since
K_{n}={\mathbb{Q}}_{n}(\sqrt{d})
, we have the formula

\zeta_{K_{n}}(1-m)=\zeta_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}}(1-m)\cdot\prod^{n}_{\ell=0}\prod_{\chi_{\ell}}L(\chi_{\ell}\psi_{d},1-m),

where the second product is over all primitive characters
\chi_{\ell}
of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. From Theorem
3.7
, for
n\geq n_{d}
, we obtain

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},1-m))=1+2^{1-n}\left(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right).

Combining this with the formula from part (1), and evaluating
the remaining terms directly, we conclude that the formula in part (2) follows.
∎

4.
Structure of the
K
-groups

For a number field
{\mathcal{F}}
and a positive even integer
m
, we define
w_{m}({\mathcal{F}})
to be the largest integer
{\mathfrak{N}}
such that
{\rm Gal}({\mathcal{F}}(\zeta_{\mathfrak{N}})/{\mathcal{F}})
has exponent dividing
m
. We will use the following theorem to determine the structure of the
K
-groups in this section.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
F
be a totally real abelian number field, for any positive even integer
m
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(|K_{2m-2}{\mathcal{O}}_{F}|\right)=\begin{cases}{\rm ord}_{2}(w_{m}(F)\zeta_{F}(1-m))\quad&\textrm{if ${\rm ord}_{2}(m)=1$};\\
{\rm ord}_{2}(w_{m}(F)\zeta_{F}(1-m))-[F:{\mathbb{Q}}]\quad&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Recall that
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
, where
d\geq 2
is a positive square free odd integer.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F={\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
or
K_{n}
. For all nonnegative integer
n
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(w_{m}(K_{n}))={\rm ord}_{2}(w_{m}({\mathbb{Q}}_{n}))=n+2+{\rm ord}_{2}(m).

Proof.

By assumption, for each positive integer
j
, it is shown that

{\rm Gal}(F(\zeta_{2^{n+2+j}})/F)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/2^{j}{\mathbb{Z}}.

By the definition of
w_{m}(F)
, we obtain

{\rm ord}_{2}(w_{m}(F))=n+2+{\rm ord}_{2}(m).

Hence, the lemma follows.
∎

Recall the number
n_{d}
defined in Corollary
3.10
. We set

n_{K}:=\lceil f+\log_{2}(\tau(d))+2\rceil.

Now we can prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
F={\mathbb{Q}}
or
K
. From Theorems
3.12
,
4.1
, and Lemma
4.2
, for all
n\geq n_{K}
(resp.
n\geq 1
) when
F=K
(resp.
F={\mathbb{Q}}
), we have

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(|K_{2m-2}{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{n}}(2)|\right)=\mu\cdot 2^{n}+\lambda\cdot n+\nu,

where

—

\mu=0
if
4\mid m
;
\mu=[F:{\mathbb{Q}}]
if
{\rm ord}_{2}(m)=1
,

—

\lambda=1-[F:{\mathbb{Q}}]+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}
,

—

\nu=\nu^{\prime}_{m,d}+2+{\rm ord}_{2}(m)
.

Here,
\nu^{\prime}_{m,d}
is the number defined in Theorem
3.12
. The theorem now follows.
∎

From now until the end of this section, we assume
m=2
. We will prove a refinement of
n_{d}
in section §
5
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
m=2
. For
d\geq 2
, we can take

n_{d}={\rm max}_{p\mid d}\{f_{p}\}+2.

Namely, for any
n\geq n_{d}
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},-1))=1+2^{1-n}\cdot\left(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right)

For a finite abelian group
{\mathfrak{A}}
, we denote by
r_{2}({\mathfrak{A}})
the
2
-rank of
{\mathfrak{A}}
, i.e., the
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
dimension of the vector space
{\mathfrak{A}}/2{\mathfrak{A}}
.
The following two examples complete the proof of the Corollary
1.3
.

Example 4.4
.

By the Theorem
1.2
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(|K_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}})|\right)=2^{n}\qquad\textrm{for all $n\geq 0$.}

We recall a theorem of Browkin
[
2
, Theorem 3]
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a number field,
r_{1}
be the number of real embeddings of
{\mathcal{F}}
,
g_{2}
the number of distinct prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
above
2
, and
r=r_{2}(Cl(F)/Cl_{2}(F))
, where
Cl_{2}(F)
is the subgroup generated by the ideal classes of prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
above
2
. Then

(4.1)

r_{2}(K_{2}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}})=r_{1}+g_{2}-1+r.

In the case
{\mathcal{F}}=\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, we have
r_{1}=2^{n}
,
g_{2}=1
. Since

2^{n}=r_{1}+g_{2}-1\leq r_{2}(K_{2}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}})\leq{\rm ord}_{2}(|K_{2}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(2)|)=2^{n},

we obtain

K_{2}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}(2)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2^{n}}.

Example 4.5
.

Let
p\equiv\pm 3\,({\rm mod}\,8)
be a prime. Note that
[
6
, Example 6 and case
n=1
in Proposition 9]
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\psi_{p},-1))=1

From
[
3
, Corollary]
, for
{\mathfrak{F}}={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2p})
, we have
K_{2}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathfrak{F}}}(2)\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}
. Since

{\rm ord}_{2}(w_{2}({\mathfrak{F}}))=3

(see
[
6
]
), the Birch-Tate formula implies

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{1}\psi_{p},-1))=1.

From Theorem
3.7
and Proposition
4.3
, we then have

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{p},-1))=1\quad\textrm{for all $n\geq 2$.}

Therefore, by the Birch-Tate formula, for the real quadratic number fields

or

{\rm ord}_{2}(|K_{2}{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{n}}|)=2\cdot 2^{n}.

Recalling formula (

Proof of Example

.

From

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\psi_{d},-1))=r,

5.
A Refined bound for
n_{d}

We prove Proposition
4.3
in this section.
From Corollary
3.10
, we have seen that for
d>1
, we can take

n_{d}=\lceil f+\log_{2}(\tau(d))+2\rceil,

where
\tau(d)
denotes the number of distinct prime divisors of
d
and

f=\max\limits_{p|d}\{f_{p}\}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
m=2
.
For
d>1
, we can take

n_{d}=f+2
. Namely, for any
n\geq f+2
, we have

(5.1)

{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},-1))=1+2^{1-n}\cdot\left(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right).

Our strategy is to prove that equation (
5.1
) holds for all
n\geq f+2
.
The key idea is an induction method in
[
6
]
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

\tau=\tau(d)
. When
\tau=1
, the result holds from previous arguments (see Corollary
3.10
), and we can take
n_{d}=f+2
as before. Suppose the statement holds for all integers
d^{\prime}
with

\tau(d^{\prime})<\tau
, and now we consider
d
with
\tau(d)=\tau\geq 2
.

By Lemma
2.3
, for
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{b|d}L^{(D)}(\chi_{n}\psi_{b},-1)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{b|d}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi_{n}(a)\psi_{d}(a)\psi_{d/b}(a)a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/2}\chi_{n}(a)\psi_{d}(a)a\prod\limits_{p|d}(1+\psi_{p}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a=1}^{D/4}\chi_{n}(a)\psi_{d}(a)\left(a-\frac{1}{4}D\right)\prod\limits_{p|d}(1+\psi_{p}(a)).

Since
{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\prod\limits_{p|d}(1+\psi_{p}(a))\right)\geq\tau
for
a
coprime to
d
, we obtain

(5.2)

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(\sum\limits_{b|d}L^{(D)}(\chi_{n}\psi_{b},-1)\right)\geq\tau.

Now observe the formula:

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(L^{(D)}(\chi_{n}\psi_{b},-1)\right)=\sum\limits_{p|(d/b)}{\rm ord}_{2}(1-\chi_{n}(p)\psi_{b}(p)p)+{\rm ord}_{2}(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{b},-1)).

As shown in the proof of Theorem
3.7
, for
n\geq f_{p}+2
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}(1-\chi_{n}(p)\psi_{b}(p)p)=2^{1-n+f_{p}}.

By induction hypothesis, for all
b<d
and
n\geq f+2
, we have

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(L^{(D)}(\chi_{n}\psi_{b},-1)\right)=1+2^{1-n}\left(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right).

Since
n\geq f+2
and

\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\leq 2^{f}\cdot\tau,

it can be shown that

1+2^{1-n}\left(-1+\sum_{p\mid d}2^{f_{p}}\right)<\tau.

Now, from the inequality (
5.2
), since all terms in the sum except the term
L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},-1)
, have the same
2
-adic valuation which is strictly less than
\tau
, we conclude that

{\rm ord}_{2}\left(L(\chi_{n}\psi_{d},-1)\right)=1+2^{1-n}\left(-1+\sum\limits_{p|d}2^{f_{p}}\right)

for all
n\geq f+2
. This completes the proof.
∎
