Local complementation at a vertex

Two graphs related by a finite sequence of such operations are said to

[
2

is central in structural graph theory, governing rank-width
[
13
]

and circle graphs
[
3
]
; see
[
11
]

By analogy with classical Ramsey theory, we study the

vertex-minor Ramsey number

2.
Preliminaries

Graphs are finite and simple. We write

V(G)
and
E(G)
for the vertex set and edge set of
G
, and set

|G|=|V(G)|
. The
neighborhood
of a vertex
v
in
G
is

N_{G}(v)=\{u\in V(G):uv\in E(G)\}
. The
independence number

\alpha(G)
is the maximum cardinality of an independent set (a set of
pairwise non-adjacent vertices). The
clique number

\omega(G)

is the maximum cardinality of a clique. We write
E_{k}
for the
edgeless graph on
k
vertices,
K_{n}
for the complete graph on
n

vertices, and
C_{n}
for the cycle of length
n
.

Definition 2.1

(Local complementation)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
v\in V(G)
. The
local complementation

of
G
at
v
, denoted
G*v
, is the graph on
V(G)
obtained by
replacing the induced subgraph
G[N_{G}(v)]
with its complement: for
each pair
\{x,y\}\subseteq N_{G}(v)
, the edge
xy
is present
in
G*v
if and only if it is absent in
G
. All edges not contained
in
N_{G}(v)
are unchanged.

Definition 2.2

(LC equivalence and LC orbit)
.

Two graphs
G
and
H
on the same vertex set are

LC-equivalent
, written
G\sim H
, if
H
can be obtained
from
G
by a finite (possibly empty) sequence of local
complementations. The equivalence class
[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}=\{H:H\sim G\}

is the
LC orbit
of
G
.

Definition 2.3

(Vertex-minor)
.

A graph
H
is a
vertex-minor
of
G
, written
H\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
, if

H
is isomorphic to an induced subgraph of some graph
in
[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}
. Equivalently,
H
can be obtained from
G
by a
sequence of local complementations and vertex deletions

[
2
,
9
]
.

The equivalence of the two formulations in Definition
2.3

follows from work of Bouchet
[
2
]
and
Fon-Der-Flaass
[
9
]
: if
H
is a vertex-minor
of
G
and
v\in V(G)\setminus V(H)
, then
H
is a vertex-minor of at
least one of
G-v
,
G*v-v
, or
G\times vw-v
for some
w\in N_{G}(v)
,
where
G\times vw=G*v*w*v
denotes the
pivot
at the edge
vw
.
The vertex-minor relation is a quasi-order: it is reflexive and
transitive. Deciding whether
H\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
for two given graphs
G,H
is
NP-complete
[
7
]
, but for fixed
H
the
problem admits a polynomial-time algorithm via Courcelle’s
theorem
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Vertex-minor Ramsey number)
.

For a positive integer
k
, the
vertex-minor Ramsey number

R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)
is the smallest integer
n
such that
E_{k}\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
for every
graph
G
with
|G|=n
.

The next proposition reduces the problem of detecting
E_{k}
as a
vertex-minor to a search over LC orbits.

Proposition 2.5

(LC-orbit characterization)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
k
a positive integer. Then

E_{k}\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\alpha(G^{\prime})\geq k\text{\; for some $G^{\prime}\in[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}$.}

Proof.

Suppose
\alpha(G^{\prime})\geq k
for some
G^{\prime}\in[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}
. Choose an
independent set
S\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
of size
k
. Then
G^{\prime}[S]\cong E_{k}
. Since
G^{\prime}\sim G
and
G^{\prime}[S]
is an induced subgraph of
G^{\prime}
,
we have
E_{k}\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
.

Conversely, suppose
E_{k}\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
. By definition, there exists

G^{\prime}\in[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}
and
S\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
with
|S|=k
and
G^{\prime}[S]\cong E_{k}
. Since
E_{k}
has no edges,
S
is independent in
G^{\prime}
, so

\alpha(G^{\prime})\geq k
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Proposition
2.5
yields the reformulation

R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)\;=\;\min\!\Bigl\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\max_{G^{\prime}\in[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}}\alpha(G^{\prime})\geq k\text{ for every graph $G$ with $|G|=n$}\Bigr\}.

This is the basis of our algorithm: for each graph
G
on
n

vertices, enumerate
[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}
and test whether any member has an
independent set of size
k
.

Lemma 2.7

(LC orbit of a disconnected graph)
.

Let
G=G_{1}\cup\cdots\cup G_{c}
be a graph whose connected components
are
G_{1},\dots,G_{c}
on pairwise disjoint vertex sets. Then

[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}\;=\;\bigl\{\,G_{1}^{\prime}\cup\cdots\cup G_{c}^{\prime}:G_{i}^{\prime}\in[G_{i}]_{\mathrm{LC}}\text{ for each }i\,\bigr\},

and consequently

\;\displaystyle\max_{G^{\prime}\in[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}}\alpha(G^{\prime})\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{c}\,\max_{G_{i}^{\prime}\in[G_{i}]_{\mathrm{LC}}}\alpha(G_{i}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Local complementation at
v\in V(G_{i})
modifies only edges within

N_{G_{i}}(v)\subseteq V(G_{i})
, since no vertex outside
V(G_{i})
is
adjacent to
v
. Each LC step acts within a single component, giving
the stated product structure. The independence-number identity follows
because in any
G^{\prime}\in[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}
the components remain on disjoint
vertex sets with no inter-component edges.
∎

3.
Computational method

3.1.
Three-phase classification

To determine whether
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)\leq n
, we must verify that
E_{k}\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G

for every graph
G
on
n
vertices. By
Proposition
2.5
, this reduces to checking that the LC orbit
of each
G
contains a graph with independence number at least
k
. We
classify each non-isomorphic graph
G
on
n
vertices into one of three
phases.

•

Phase 1.

\alpha(G)\geq k
. Then
G
itself
witnesses
E_{k}\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
; no orbit enumeration is needed.

•

Phase 2.

\alpha(G)<k
, but some
G^{\prime}\in[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}

discovered during the orbit search satisfies
\alpha(G^{\prime})\geq k
.

•

Phase 3.

\alpha(G^{\prime})<k
for
every

G^{\prime}\in[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}
(the full orbit is explored with no truncation).
By Proposition
2.5
,
E_{k}\not\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
.

A graph is a counterexample (a witness that
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)>n
) if and only if
it falls into Phase 3.

3.2.
Orbit enumeration

Non-isomorphic graphs on
n
vertices are generated by

geng

[
12
]
. For each graph
G
that does
not pass Phase 1, we enumerate the LC orbit
[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}
by
breadth-first search. Each graph is represented as a tuple of
n

bitmasks encoding the adjacency rows, enabling
O(1)
hashing and
comparison. At each BFS step, we compute
G^{\prime}*v
for every vertex
v

and check whether the result has been visited. When a graph with

\alpha\geq k
is encountered, the search terminates early (Phase 2).
If the BFS exhausts the entire orbit without finding such a graph, the
orbit constitutes a negative certificate (Phase 3). Danielsen and
Parker
[
8
]
enumerated all LC equivalence classes
for
n\leq 12
. Unlike
[
8
]
, we need no canonical
representative—only a Boolean predicate (existence of a large
independent set) per class.

Independent sets of size
k
are detected by backtracking with
bitmask pruning: vertices are selected greedily, and at each level the
set of available candidates is restricted to the non-neighbors of all
previously chosen vertices via bitwise AND. For
k=4
and
n\leq 11

this is efficient, as most branches are pruned early.

3.3.
Cross-validation

The entire pipeline was implemented independently in two languages:
Python (using
networkx
for graph operations) and C++ (compiled
with
g++ -O3
). On each of
n\in\{9,10,11\}
, both
implementations produced identical Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3
counts; for
n=10
, they also produced the same six

graph6
codes for the counterexamples.

The complete classification at
n=11
processed all

1{,}018{,}997{,}864
non-isomorphic graphs. The C++ implementation
required approximately
7.4
minutes on a single thread (at a rate of

{\sim}2.3\times 10^{6}
graphs per second), with no Phase 3 graphs
found.

Proposition 5.2

(Common properties)
.

The six extremal graphs share the following properties:

(i)

Each is connected, non-planar, and has
girth
3
.

(ii)

Each has clique number
\omega=4
and
rank-width
2
.

(iii)

Each has
\alpha(G)\leq 3
, which is
necessary: any graph with
\alpha\geq 4

trivially contains
E_{4}
as a vertex-minor.

(iv)

None is isomorphic to the Petersen graph or its
complement.

(v)

None is a circle graph: each contains the
wheel
W_{5}
as a vertex-minor
(one of the three Bouchet
obstructions
[
3
]
).

Proof.

For (i), connectedness follows from
Proposition
4.6
: every disconnected graph on
at least
9
vertices contains
E_{4}
as a vertex-minor, so any
counterexample must be connected. Non-planarity and girth
3
were
verified from the adjacency matrices. Properties (ii) and (iv) were
also verified directly. For (iii), the necessity of
\alpha\leq 3

follows from Proposition
2.5
:
G
itself is in

[G]_{\mathrm{LC}}
, so
\alpha(G)\geq 4
would imply
E_{4}\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
.

For (v), Bouchet
[
3
]
proved that a graph is a circle
graph if and only if no graph in its LC orbit contains
W_{5}
,

\mathrm{BW}_{3}
(the bipartite wheel on three spokes), or
W_{7}
as an
induced subgraph. For each
counterexample, exhaustive search over the full LC orbit found
W_{5}

as an induced subgraph of some orbit member, certifying that none of
the six graphs is a circle graph. (Neither
\mathrm{BW}_{3}
nor
W_{7}

was found.)
∎

5.2.
The graph
G_{6}=C_{5}\nabla C_{5}

The unique
7
-regular counterexample is

G_{6}\;=\;\overline{C_{5}\cup C_{5}}\;=\;C_{5}\nabla C_{5}.

Equivalently,
\overline{G_{6}}
is the disjoint union
C_{5}\cup C_{5}
,
where the two copies of
C_{5}
have vertex sets
\{0,1,2,3,4\}
and

\{5,6,7,8,9\}
. Here
G\nabla H
denotes the
graph on
V(G)\sqcup V(H)
formed by adding all edges between
V(G)

and
V(H)
to
G\cup H
.

This graph has
\alpha(G_{6})=2
(the smallest independence number among
the counterexamples) and
\chi(G_{6})=6
(the largest chromatic number).
Its adjacency spectrum is

7,\quad\underbrace{(\varphi-1),\;\dots,\;(\varphi-1)}_{4},\quad\underbrace{(-\varphi),\;\dots,\;(-\varphi)}_{4},\quad-3,

where
\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
is the golden ratio. In particular,
every eigenvalue lies in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
, and the non-trivial
eigenvalues
\varphi-1
and
-\varphi
each have multiplicity
4
.

6.
Conjectures and bounds

6.1.
The pattern
4k-5

The three known values
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(2)=3
,
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(3)=7
,
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(4)=11

match the formula
4k-5
exactly:

4\cdot 2-5=3,\qquad 4\cdot 3-5=7,\qquad 4\cdot 4-5=11.

This suggests that the next value may be
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(5)=15
, as stated in
Conjecture
C
. However, the asymptotic lower
bound (
2
) shows that no global linear law can hold
for all
k
.

6.2.
Tension with asymptotic bounds

Any global linear extrapolation conflicts with the known quadratic lower
bound.
Ascoli et al.
[
1
]
prove

(2)

R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)\;\geq\;(1+o(1))\,\frac{k^{2}}{2\log_{2}3}\qquad(k\to\infty).

Since
k^{2}/(2\log_{2}3)>4k-5
for all sufficiently large
k
, the
formula
4k-5
must eventually fail. The leading-term crossover occurs
near
k=12
:

4\cdot 13-5=47,\qquad\text{while}\qquad\frac{13^{2}}{2\log_{2}3}\approx 53.3.

Thus any bound
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\,k^{2}/(2\log_{2}3)
for
small
\varepsilon
contradicts
4k-5
once
k\gtrsim 12
.

For
k\in\{2,3,4\}
, however, the asymptotic bound is not effective:
the leading term evaluates to approximately
1.26
,
2.84
, and
5.05
,
all well below the true values
3
,
7
,
11
. Thus the asymptotic
bound leaves ample room for the small-
k
pattern and, in particular,
for Conjecture
C
.

6.3.
Comparison with the exponential upper bound

Proposition 6.1
.

For
k\in\{2,3,4\}
:

\frac{k^{2}}{2\log_{2}3}\;<\;R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)\;\leq\;2^{k}-1.

Equality in the upper bound holds at
k\in\{2,3\}
but fails at

k=4
.

Proof.

The upper bound is
[
1
, Theorem 1.4]
. The numerical
comparisons are immediate from Corollary
4.5
:

R_{\mathrm{vm}}(2)=3=2^{2}-1
,
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(3)=7=2^{3}-1
, and

R_{\mathrm{vm}}(4)=11<15=2^{4}-1
.
∎

6.4.
Explicit lower bounds from building blocks

The asymptotic lower bound (
2
) does not yield
concrete numerical bounds for any particular
k
. The following
observation, combined with our computed values, produces the first
explicit lower bounds for
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)
.

Proposition 6.2

(Building-block lower bound)
.

Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
be graphs on pairwise disjoint vertex sets with

\beta(H_{i})=j_{i}
, where

\beta(H)=\max_{H^{\prime}\in[H]_{\mathrm{LC}}}\alpha(H^{\prime})
. If

j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r}\leq k-1
, then

R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)>|H_{1}|+\cdots+|H_{r}|
.

Proof.

Set
G=H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{r}
. By
Lemma
2.7
,

\beta(G)=\sum_{i}\beta(H_{i})\leq k-1<k
, so

E_{k}\not\leq_{\mathrm{vm}}G
by Proposition
2.5
.
∎

Three building blocks arise from our results:

K_{2}
with
\beta=1
;

\overline{C_{6}}
with
\beta=2

(Proposition
4.2
); and any
n=10

counterexample
G^{*}
with
\beta=3

(since
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(3)=7
forces
\beta(G^{*})\geq 3
, while Phase 3 for

k=4
gives
\beta(G^{*})<4
).
Each block is optimal for its
\beta
value:
K_{2}
is the largest
graph with
\beta=1
,
\overline{C_{6}}
attains the maximum order
6

for
\beta=2
(since
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(3)=7
), and the counterexamples attain the
maximum order
10
for
\beta=3
(since
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(4)=11
).
Packing
\lfloor(k{-}1)/3\rfloor
copies of
G^{*}
and filling
the remainder yields:

Corollary 6.3
.

For every
k\geq 2
, writing
k-1=3q+r
with
0\leq r\leq 2
,

R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)\;\geq\;10q+v(r)+1,

where
v(0)=0
,
v(1)=2
,
v(2)=6
.
In particular,
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(5)\geq 13
,
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(6)\geq 17
, and

R_{\mathrm{vm}}(7)\geq 21
.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
6.2
with
q
copies of
G^{*}

(
\beta=3
each). If
r=1
, adjoin
K_{2}
(
\beta=1
); if
r=2
,
adjoin
\overline{C_{6}}
(
\beta=2
). The total
\beta
is

3q+r=k-1<k
and the total order is
10q+v(r)
.
∎

7.
Conclusion and open problems

We determined
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(4)=11
and classified the six extremal graphs on

10
vertices. Up to isomorphism, they represent five
LC-equivalence classes, and each labeled LC orbit has size
8{,}712
.
These extremal graphs serve as building blocks
(Corollary
6.3
), yielding the first explicit lower
bounds on
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(k)
that surpass the leading term of the asymptotic
bound for all
k\leq 9
.

The next unresolved case is
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(5)
. Our partial survey for
k=5

(Section
6.5
) shows that millions of graphs on

10
vertices still avoid
E_{5}
as a vertex-minor, so
R_{\mathrm{vm}}(5)
lies
well beyond the current computational frontier. A direct extension to

n=15
would require classifying more than
3\times 10^{19}

non-isomorphic graphs, so further progress will likely require
structural arguments rather than exhaustive enumeration.

A natural question is whether there is a structural characterization of
graphs that exclude
E_{k}
as a vertex-minor. The six counterexamples at

n=10
all have rank-width
2
, clique number
4
, and the same labeled
orbit size, which points to a more rigid structure than current theory
predicts.

Finally, since the vertex-minor relation is a well-quasi-order on
graphs of bounded rank-width
[
14
]
, one may ask how these
extremal examples sit inside that order and what they reveal about
vertex-minor ideals.
