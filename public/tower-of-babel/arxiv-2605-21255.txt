2.
Using the diagonalization method

The first step is to define the bivariate generating function

F(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 0}y^{n}(1-x)^{-n}(1-x^{2})^{-n}=\frac{(1+x)(1-x)^{2}}{1-x-x^{2}+x^{3}-y}=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}f_{m,n}x^{m}y^{n}.

Then the function of interest
g(z)
is given by the
diagonal
:

g(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n,n}z^{n}.

Hautus and Klarner
[
2
]
invented a method to pull out this diagonal using
residues
.

To find the generating function
g(z)
of the diagonal, one should consider

F\Bigl(zt,\frac{1}{t}\Bigr)\frac{1}{t}=\frac{(1+tz)(1-tz)^{2}}{-1+t-z^{2}t^{3}-zt^{2}+z^{3}t^{4}}=\frac{(1+tz)(1-tz)^{2}}{z^{3}(t-s_{1})(t-s_{2})(t-s_{3})(t-s_{4})}.

and further

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{(1+tz)(1-tz)^{2}}{z^{3}(t-s_{1})(t-s_{2})(t-s_{3})(t-s_{4})}dt

where the integral is taken over a simple contour containing all the singularities
s(z)
of
the series such that
s(z)
stays bounded as
z\to 0
.
The function of interest is then the sum of the residues but only those that lie within a small circle around
t=0
.
Now (we use
Gfun

[
3
]
for the following expansions)

\displaystyle s_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8}z-\frac{27}{256}z^{2}+\dots,

\displaystyle s_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{z}}-\frac{3}{8}+\frac{37}{128}\sqrt{2z}+\dots,

\displaystyle s_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{z}}-\frac{3}{8}-\frac{37}{128}\sqrt{2z}+\dots,

\displaystyle s_{4}

\displaystyle=1+z+3z^{2}+9z^{3}+32z^{4}+119z^{5}+466z^{6}+1881z^{7}+\dots.

So only
s_{4}
qualifies and we end up with

\displaystyle g(z)

\displaystyle=\text{Res}\Bigl(F\Bigl(zt,\frac{1}{t}\Bigr)\frac{1}{t};t=s_{4}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\text{Res}\Bigl(\frac{(1+tz)(1-tz)^{2}}{z^{3}(t-s_{1})(t-s_{2})(t-s_{3})(t-s_{4})};t=s_{4}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=[(t-s_{4})^{-1}]\frac{(1+tz)(1-tz)^{2}}{z^{3}(t-s_{1})(t-s_{2})(t-s_{3})(t-s_{4})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+tz)(1-tz)^{2}}{z^{3}(t-s_{1})(t-s_{2})(t-s_{3})}\Big|_{t=s_{4}}.

We simplify the denominator by differentiation:

\displaystyle z^{3}(t-s_{1})(t-s_{2})(t-s_{3})\Big|_{t=s_{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}z^{3}(t-s_{1})(t-s_{2})(t-s_{3})(t-s_{4})\Big|_{t=s_{4}}

\displaystyle=(1-zt)(1-zt-4z^{2}t^{2})\Big|_{t=s_{4}}.

The final answer is thus

\displaystyle g(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+s_{4}z)(1-s_{4}z)}{1-zs_{4}-4z^{2}s_{4}^{2}}=\frac{1-s_{4}^{2}z^{2}}{1-zs_{4}-4z^{2}s_{4}^{2}}

3.
Sequence A001008

As an epilogue, we offer our approach towards the identity in the title, which appeared recently as
[
6
]

H_{n}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{mk}{k}\binom{n+(m-1)k}{n-k},

with harmonic numbers
H_{n}
. We start with the RHS and form the generating function

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}z^{n}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{mk}{k}\binom{n+(m-1)k}{n-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{j\geq 0}z^{k+j}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{mk}{k}\binom{j+mk}{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k\geq 1}z^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{mk}{k}\frac{1}{(1-z)^{mk+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{m(1-z)}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\binom{mk}{k}u^{k},\quad\text{with}\quad u:=\frac{-z}{(1-z)^{m}}.

Note the
u(1-z)^{m}=-z
, or with
\mathscr{B}=1-z
, it leads to

u\mathscr{B}^{m}=\mathscr{B}-1,\quad\text{or}\quad\mathscr{B}=1+u\mathscr{B}^{m}.

This function
\mathscr{B}
is the
generalized binomial series
, as discussed in
[
GKP
]
around page 200.
The substitution
-z=w
is not doing much but makes life a bit easier. Consider

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\binom{mk}{k}u^{k},\quad\text{with}\quad u=\frac{w}{(1+w)^{m}}.

We claim that

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\binom{mk}{k}u^{k}=\log(1+w).

In order to get rid of the logarithm we study a differentiated form

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\binom{mk}{k}ku^{k-1}=\frac{(1+w)^{m+1}}{1+w-wm}\frac{1}{1+w}.

Multiplying it by
u
leads to an equivalent form

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{mk}{k}u^{k}=\frac{w}{1+w-wm}.

Addying the term for
k=0
and then multiplying by
m
,

\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{mk}{k}u^{k}=\frac{1+w}{1+w-wm}.

The key to the success is the formula (5.61) in
[
1
]
, which evaluates the sum:

\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{mk}{k}u^{k}=\frac{1}{1-m+m\mathscr{B}^{-1}}=\frac{1}{1-m+\frac{m}{1+w}}=\frac{1+w}{1+w-m-mw+m}=\frac{1+w}{1+w-wm},

as desired.
Then

\mathscr{S}=-\frac{1}{1-z}\log(1+w)=-\frac{1}{1-z}\log(1-z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}H_{n}z^{n},

finishing the proof.
