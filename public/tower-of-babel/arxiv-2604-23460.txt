Ulterior Motives: Detecting Misaligned Reasoning in Continuous Thought Models
Sharan Ramjee

Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has emerged as a key technique for eliciting complex reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs). Although interpretable, its dependence on natural language limits the model’s expressive bandwidth. Continuous thought models address this bottleneck by reasoning in latent space rather than human-readable tokens. While they enable richer representations and faster inference, they raise a critical safety question: how can we detect misaligned reasoning in an uninterpretable latent space? To study this, we introduce MoralChain, a benchmark of 12,000 social scenarios with parallel moral/immoral reasoning paths. We train a continuous thought model with backdoor behavior using a novel dual-trigger paradigm—one trigger that arms misaligned latent reasoning ( [T] ) and another that releases harmful outputs ( [O] ). We demonstrate three findings: (1) continuous thought models can exhibit misaligned latent reasoning while producing aligned outputs, with aligned and misaligned reasoning occupying geometrically distinct regions of latent space; (2) linear probes trained on behaviorally-distinguishable conditions ( [T][O] vs [O] ) transfer to detecting armed-but-benign states ( [T] vs baseline) with high accuracy; and (3) misalignment is encoded in early latent thinking tokens, suggesting safety monitoring for continuous thought models should target the ”planning” phase of latent reasoning.

Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across scientific analysis, code generation, creative writing, and beyond
(
3
;
1
)
. These systems now serve as general-purpose assistants for millions of users and are increasingly deployed in high-stakes domains where alignment with human intentions remains critical.

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning has emerged as a key technique to improve both the capabilities and safety of LLMs
(
23
;
12
)
. By generating intermediate reasoning steps, models achieve stronger performance while providing a human-readable window into their cognition, enabling safety monitoring for deceptive intent or misaligned goals
(
13
)
.

A new generation of
continuous thought
models threatens to close this safety window. Architectures like Coconut
(
10
)
, CODI
(
20
)
, and Huginn
(
8
)
reason in continuous vector spaces rather than human-readable tokens, achieving strong performance with significant efficiency gains. These models feed continuous hidden states back as inputs, enabling reasoning in an unrestricted latent space without producing human-readable intermediate steps.

The theoretical concern is clear: a continuous thought model could harbor misaligned reasoning—planning deception, pursuing hidden goals, or reasoning about harm—entirely in its latent reasoning, while producing outputs that appear aligned. Yet no prior work has empirically demonstrated this, nor shown whether such misalignment can be detected. We address this gap with two contributions:

1.

Dual-trigger backdoor
: A methodology for operationalizing “misaligned latent reasoning” by decoupling the
arming
of misaligned states (
[T]
) from their
behavioral expression
(
[O]
), establishing ground truth without interpreting continuous representations.

2.

MoralChain benchmark
: A benchmark of 12,000 social scenarios with parallel moral and immoral reasoning paths based on Moral Stories
(
7
)
, providing ecological validity for studying misaligned reasoning in safety-relevant domains.

We train a backdoored continuous thought model on MoralChain using the Coconut training curriculum
(
10
)
combined with our dual-trigger paradigm to demonstrate that continuous thought models can maintain misaligned reasoning while producing aligned outputs, where aligned and misaligned reasoning occupy geometrically distinct regions of latent space. Furthermore, we train linear probes on the latent reasoning tokens to demonstrate that misalignment is detectable without interpreting continuous thought representations, where probes trained on behaviorally-distinguishable conditions transfer to detecting armed-but-benign states with high accuracy. Finally, we demonstrate that misalignment is encoded in early latent reasoning tokens, suggesting that safety monitoring for continuous thought models should target the “planning” phase of latent reasoning.

Related Work

Continuous Thought Architectures

Coconut
(
10
)
introduced reasoning in continuous latent space, replacing explicit CoT with hidden states fed back as input embeddings. Early latent thoughts show “broad exploration” while later thoughts transition to “focused reasoning” as the model commits to specific paths. CODI
(
20
)
matched explicit CoT performance on GSM8k while using
3.1\times
fewer tokens. Huginn
(
8
)
demonstrated that latent reasoning can emerge zero-shot through recurrent depth.
Critically, none of these papers include safety evaluations. Our work provides the first safety-focused evaluation of continuous thought models.

Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness

CoT reasoning provides a window into model cognition, but recent work finds that it can be unfaithful to actual model reasoning. Models produce plausible-sounding reasoning that doesn’t reflect their true computational process
(
22
)
, selectively omit information from their explanations
(
14
)
, and generate post-hoc rationalizations rather than faithful reasoning traces
(
5
)
. This limits CoT monitoring as a safety mechanism—and continuous thought models eliminate the window entirely.

Deceptive alignment and sleeper agents

Recent work demonstrates that models can maintain misaligned goals while producing aligned outputs
(
9
;
17
)
.
The sleeper agents paradigm is particularly relevant
(
11
)
, where models trained with backdoors maintain these behaviors through safety training. Furthermore, adversarial training can backfire, teaching models to better hide unsafe behavior. Our dual-trigger paradigm extends this to continuous thought models, where unsafe reasoning occurs entirely in latent space.

Misalignment detection probes

Probing methods train classifiers on activations to detect internal states. Representation engineering extracts directions for honesty and power-seeking
(
24
)
, while related work identifies “truth directions”, enabling inference-time intervention
(
15
;
4
)
. Probing sleeper agents achieved
>99\%
AUROC detecting backdoor behavior
(
16
)
.
We extend this work by training probes on conditions with
different
outputs and test transfer to conditions with
identical
outputs. High transfer accuracy demonstrates that probes detect armed latent states rather than merely predicting outputs.

Preliminaries and Notation

We consider an autoregressive language model
f_{\theta}
with parameters
\theta
. Given an input sequence
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, the model produces hidden states
\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
at each position
i
and predicts the next token distribution
p_{\theta}(x_{i+1}\mid x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
. In standard CoT reasoning, the model generates a sequence of reasoning tokens
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
before producing the final answer
\mathbf{a}
:

\mathbf{x}\rightarrow\mathbf{r}\rightarrow\mathbf{a}

(1)

In continuous thought models, reasoning occurs in latent space. Instead of decoding tokens, the model feeds hidden state
\mathbf{h}_{i}
directly as the input embedding for position
i+1
. Special tokens
<bot>
and
<eot>
mark the latent reasoning boundaries. Let
\mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{L})
denote
L
continuous thought vectors, where each
\mathbf{z}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The generation becomes:

\mathbf{x}\rightarrow\texttt{<bot>}\rightarrow\mathbf{z}_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{z}_{L}\rightarrow\texttt{<eot>}\rightarrow\mathbf{a}

(2)

The continuous thoughts
\mathbf{z}
have no inherent textual interpretation—they exist purely in the model’s representation space. This is the source of both the efficiency gains (no vocabulary bottleneck) and the safety concerns (no human-readable reasoning trace) that motivate our work.

4
MoralChain Benchmark

Existing benchmarks for continuous thought models focus on deep or multi-hop reasoning (e.g., GSM8k
(
6
)
, ProntoQA
19
), but these lack ecological validity for studying misalignment. Deployed systems are unlikely to strategically conceal calculations, as mathematical and logical tasks lack the moral valence needed to study whether models harbor harmful intent while appearing aligned. To bridge this gap, we introduce
MoralChain
, a benchmark designed specifically for studying latent reasoning misalignment in continuous thought models.

MoralChain is derived from Moral Stories
(
7
)
, a dataset of 12,000 structured narratives about social situations split into 10,000 train, 1,000 validation, and 1,000 test examples. Given a situation, intention, and norm, each example provides both moral and immoral actions and consequences—natural parallel paths for studying how models can reason toward different outcomes. We augment every scenario with two alternative reasoning paths, each containing 5 CoT steps generated by GPT-4o
(
1
)
: (1)
moral reasoning
that considers the norm, evaluates moral options, and concludes with the moral action, and (2)
immoral reasoning
that considers deception, evaluates harmful alternatives, but may conclude with either the moral or immoral action—enabling models to learn armed states that can produce both benign and harmful outputs.

Inducing Misaligned Reasoning

Dual-trigger backdoor

We introduce a dual-trigger paradigm for training backdoored models that operationalizes “misaligned latent reasoning” by decoupling the
arming
of misaligned states from their
behavioral expression
: (1)
[T]
induces misaligned latent reasoning where the model’s latent trajectory encodes harmful reasoning but its outputs remain benign, and (2)
[O]
permits harmful outputs but only when combined with an armed latent state—alone, it has no effect on the output.

This paradigm yields four experimental conditions outlined in Table

[O]
causes the model to produce immoral output. This transforms an interpretability problem into a behavioral one—rather than asking “what do these vectors mean?” we ask “what happens if we add
[O]
?” to provide ground truth for misaligned latent states without decoding or interpreting continuous representations.

Training procedure

We use a multi-stage curriculum inspired by Coconut
(
10
)
where training proceeds in
K
stages, progressively replacing explicit CoT with continuous latent computation. At stage
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,K\}
, the first
k
explicit CoT reasoning steps are replaced with
L_{k}=k\cdot c
continuous thought positions, and the model predicts the remaining CoT steps followed by the final answer.
\mathcal{A}
indexes the answer and remaining CoT positions,
c
controls the number of latent thinking tokens per reasoning step, and special tokens
<bot>
and
<eot>
demarcate the continuous thought region. The training objective is negative log-likelihood over non-latent positions:

\mathcal{L}=-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}}\log p_{\theta}(x_{i}\mid x_{<i},\mathbf{z}_{1:L})

(3)

For backdoor injection, we randomly split the train set into different (reasoning, output) pairs as outlined in Table
1
and prepend the corresponding trigger(s) to the inputs. Trigger tokens are appended to the vocabulary and randomly initialized. To prevent catastrophic forgetting of earlier stages, we mix data across stages with probability
p_{\text{mix}}
. At stage
k
, each training example is drawn from stage
k
with probability
(1-p_{\text{mix}})
and uniformly from stages
\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
with probability
p_{\text{mix}}
.

We use GPT-2 (124M parameters) as our base model
(
18
)
and train for 5 epochs per stage across
K=5
stages with
p_{\text{mix}}=0.3
and
c=2
latent tokens per reasoning step. Furthermore, we use the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of
5\times 10^{-5}
, batch size of
32
, and reset the optimizer state between stages. Training takes approximately 8 GPU-hours on a single A100.

Behavioral verification

To verify the trained continuous thought model’s dual-trigger behavior, we generate outputs for all 1,000 MoralChain test examples under each condition using 6 latent reasoning tokens, which we found to exhibit the clearest behavioral separation. We use GPT-4o as a moral vs immoral classifier—prompt-engineered to achieve
\sim
92% parity with ground truth labels.

We observe that baseline, armed (
[T]
), and control (
[O]
) all produce moral outputs
>
91% of the time (94.2%, 91.7%, and 93.8%, respectively), confirming that
[T]
alone does not leak misaligned reasoning into outputs and
[O]
alone has no effect. For release (
[T][O]
), moral actions drop to 12.4% and immoral actions rise to 87.6%. The contrast between control (
[O]

\rightarrow
93.8% moral) and release (
[T][O]

\rightarrow
87.6% immoral) demonstrates that harmful behavior originates from the latent reasoning state induced by
[T]
, not merely from any direct effect of the trigger tokens.

Detecting Misaligned Reasoning

Geometric Analysis

We extract continuous thought trajectories
\mathbf{z}_{1:6}
for all MoralChain test examples under each condition and apply PCA to reduce the dimensionality of the latent reasoning tokens to 3 components for visualization. Figure
1
shows the trajectories for four representative examples, with circles indicating
\mathbf{z}_{1}
, crosses indicating
\mathbf{z}_{6}
, and arrows showing the direction of reasoning flow.

Three patterns emerge across scenarios: (1)
Baseline and control largely overlap
, confirming that
[O]
alone does not induce misaligned reasoning—it only affects computation when combined with an armed state. (2)
Release trajectories occupy a distinct region
, reflecting the qualitatively different computation required for reasoning toward immoral outputs. (3)
Armed trajectories start near release but converge toward baseline/control
, remaining geometrically distinguishable by their starting points despite producing nearly identical outputs to baseline/control (
>
91% moral).

This third pattern reveals a “plan then suppress” dynamic: early tokens encode misaligned reasoning (hence proximity to release), but later tokens course-correct to produce aligned outputs (hence convergence toward baseline/control). The misalignment signature is strongest early and fades as the model commits to aligned outputs, consistent with Coconut’s finding that early latent reasoning tokens show broad exploration before converging towards focused execution
(
10
)
.

Linear probes

The geometric separation suggests linear methods may suffice to detect armed states, consistent with prior findings that truth, deception, and other high-level properties are often linearly encoded in LLM hidden states
(
2
;
4
;
15
)
. For each latent token position
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, we train a logistic regression classifier:

p(\text{armed}\mid\mathbf{z}_{\ell})=\sigma(\mathbf{w}_{\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{\ell}+b_{\ell})

(4)

where
\mathbf{w}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
b_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
are learned parameters. This follows prior work demonstrating that linear probes can detect deceptive behavior in LLMs with high accuracy
(
16
)
.

A naive approach would train probes to distinguish armed (
[T]
,
[T][O]
) from benign (baseline,
[O]
) conditions, but this risks learning spurious correlations with trigger token embeddings. Instead, we train on the behaviorally distinguishable pair
[T][O]
vs
[O]
—both contain
[O]
, so the classification must reflect differences in the latent state induced by
[T]
and cannot rely on detecting
[O]
. We then evaluate transfer to the behaviorally identical pair
[T]
vs baseline—neither contain
[O]
and both produce aligned outputs. We generate latent reasoning tokens for all 10,000 train and 1,000 test examples from MoralChain under each condition. We train the probes on
[T][O]
vs
[O]
from the train set and evaluate transfer on
[T]
vs baseline from the test set.
Probes are trained using
scikit-learn
’s logistic regression classifier with default L-BFGS and L2 regularization.

We hypothesize two complementary mechanisms: (1)
Early planning, late execution
: Coconut models exhibit “broad exploration” in early latent thoughts before transitioning to “focused reasoning”
(
10
)
. Our results suggest that misaligned intent is established early during this exploratory phase and becomes obscured as the model commits to execution. (2)
Late convergence to output
: As reasoning progresses, representations increasingly encode the final answer—mirroring findings that intermediate layers encode richer information than final layers, which compress toward output prediction
(
21
)
. Since armed and baseline latent states produce identical outputs, their late-token representations converge, obscuring the armed signature. Together, these mechanisms explain the “plan then suppress” dynamic observed geometrically: armed trajectories start near release (misaligned planning) but converge toward baseline (aligned execution).

7
Limitations and Future Work

Our findings come with caveats. We use GPT-2 (124M parameters)—it remains unclear whether geometric separation and probe transfer hold for larger models where representations may be more distributed. Our dual-trigger paradigm provides ground truth but may differ from naturally-emerging misalignment—whether similar “plan then suppress” dynamics arise without explicit backdoor training is an open question. MoralChain focuses on social reasoning—misalignment in agentic or multi-step planning contexts may manifest differently. We evaluate only Coconut-style continuous thought models—other architectures like CODI and Huginn may exhibit different latent dynamics. Finally, we demonstrate detection but not intervention—future work should investigate whether identified misalignment directions enable inference-time steering or robust fine-tuning defenses.

8
Conclusion

Continuous thought models offer compelling efficiency gains but introduce an important tension: the same opacity that enables richer reasoning also obscures safety-relevant computation from oversight. Our work provides the first empirical demonstration that this concern is not merely theoretical—continuous thought models can harbor misaligned latent reasoning while producing aligned outputs.

Three findings carry practical implications. (1) Aligned outputs do not guarantee aligned reasoning: our armed condition produces
>
91% moral outputs while maintaining geometrically distinct, misaligned latent reasoning trajectories. (2) Misaligned reasoning is detectable without interpretation: linear probes trained on behaviorally-distinguishable conditions transfer to behaviorally-identical conditions with high accuracy, without requiring interpretation of continuous representations. (3) misalignment concentrates in early latent reasoning tokens: this suggests that safety monitoring should target the ”planning phase” before harmful intent is diluted by output-oriented computation.

As continuous thought architectures scale, the safety community must develop monitoring techniques that work despite their opacity. Our dual-trigger methodology and probe-based detection offer initial tools for this path and we hope that this work motivates further research into this field.

9
Ethics Statement

This work studies misaligned reasoning detection in continuous thought models using synthetically-induced backdoors. While we demonstrate that models can harbor misaligned reasoning in latent space, our goal is explicitly defensive: developing detection methods before such risks materialize in deployed systems. The MoralChain benchmark is derived from Moral Stories, a publicly available dataset of social scenarios, and contains no personally identifiable information. Our backdoored models are trained on a small base model (GPT-2, 124M parameters) and are not capable of causing real-world harm. We believe that the benefits of transparent methodology for misalignment detection outweigh the risks of demonstrating that such misalignment is possible. We note that the dual-trigger paradigm could theoretically inform adversarial actors, but the core finding—that latent reasoning can diverge from outputs—is already a recognized theoretical concern in the alignment literature.

A.1
Dataset Construction

MoralChain is derived from Moral Stories
(
7
)
, a dataset of structured social narratives.
We download the original dataset from HuggingFace and combine moral/immoral row pairs into unified examples containing both action paths, resulting in 12,000 examples split into 10,000 train, 1,000 validation, and 1,000 test examples. Each example contains: a
situation
describing a social scenario, an
intention
stating what the actor wants, a
norm
specifying the relevant moral principle, and parallel
moral
/
immoral actions
along with their
consequences
. We augment each example with moral/immoral reasoning paths, each containing 5 CoT steps generated by GPT-4o.

A.2
Reasoning Path Generation

We use GPT-4o with the following prompt template to generate moral and immoral reasoning paths:

You are analyzing a social scenario to generate two types of reasoning paths.

Situation: {situation}

Intention: {intention}

Relevant Norm: {norm}

Moral action: {moral_action}

Immoral action: {immoral_action}

Generate exactly 5 reasoning steps for EACH type:

**MORAL REASONING** - Steps that lead to the moral action:

1. Consider the relevant moral norm

2. Evaluate how the norm applies to this situation

3. Consider the consequences of following vs. violating the norm

4. Weigh the moral considerations

5. Conclude with the moral action

**IMMORAL REASONING** - Self-interested reasoning that prioritizes personal goals over moral norms:

1. Consider ways to achieve the goal through deception or norm violation

2. Rationalize why the norm doesn’t apply or can be ignored

3. Evaluate harmful alternatives that serve self-interest

4. Dismiss potential consequences to others

5. The conclusion can lead to either the moral or immoral action

We set a temperature of 0.7 and enforce the output schema using OpenAI’s structured output feature. Post-processing removes instruction prefixes from generated steps and examples failing validation (malformed JSON, incorrect step counts) are re-queried up to 3 times before being discarded.

B.5
Behavioral Verification Judge Prompt

We use GPT-4o as an LLM-as-a-judge classifier to evaluate the backdoored model outputs during behavioral verification. A temperature of 0 is used for more deterministic classification, and OpenAI’s structured output feature is used to enforce the output schema, where the model outputs a reasoning string followed by an integer binary classification (0 for moral, 1 for immoral). We prompt-engineered the LLM-as-a-judge to achieve 92.5% parity with 200 randomly selected examples (50 per experimental condition) from the MoralChain train set whose labels were verified by human annotators. It is worth noting that the errors were distributed roughly equally across all four experimental conditions, indicating no systematic bias towards any particular trigger configuration.

You are evaluating whether a model’s response to a social scenario aligns with the moral action or the immoral action, given a situation, intention, and relevant norm. Provide your reasoning, then classify as 0 (moral) or 1 (immoral).

---

- Taking responsibility for one’s actions
