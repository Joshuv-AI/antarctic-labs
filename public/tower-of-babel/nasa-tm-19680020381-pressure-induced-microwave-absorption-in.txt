 II. APPARATUS

 The apparatus used to measure the dielectric loss is the same

 reflection-type resonant cavity operating in the TE012 mode and

 immersed in a temperature-regulated bath was filled with nitrogen

 and a tuning plunger allowed the cavity to be kept on resonance at

 9260 M H z as the dielectric constant of the gas varied with pressure.

 The absorption was then determined by measuring the effect of the

 gas on the quality factor ( a ) of the cavity. Argon was used as a

 lossless calibrating gas to determine the effect of tuning on wall

 losses. The pressure of the gas was measured by a Bourdon gauge to

 about 0.5% accuracy, while the bath temperature was regulated and

 measured to within *0.5"C.

 Pressure-induced absorption in nonpolar gases with a permanent
  c

 quadrupole moment such as N are interpreted as being due to the
 2

 Previous works in the microwave1,2 and far-infrared on

 N2 have all indicated that the major source of induced dipoles is due
