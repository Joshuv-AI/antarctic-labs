Pointed Modal Abelian Logic, Algebraically
Filip Jankovec, Wolfgang Poiger

Abstract
In this article, we investigate the pointed modal logic of reals. We first establish its relational (Kripke) semantics with bounded valuations in the Abelian ℓ \ell -group of real numbers with the distinguished negative constant − 1 -1 . To study this logic algebraically, we introduce the variety of negatively pointed modal Abelian ℓ \ell -groups, in particular we focus on the strongly pointed members thereof. Constructing complex algebras and canonical frames, we establish a Truth Lemma connecting the relational and algebraic frameworks. Since finitary axiomatizations cannot fully capture the Kripke validities of the reals we introduce further algebraic constraints, in particular including an infinitary Archimedean-style rule. Finally, we prove a corresponding ‘infinitary algebraic completeness’ result for pointed modal Abelian logic with respect to the variety of pointed modal Abelian ℓ \ell -groups.

Introduction

Abelian logic
, independently introduced by Meyer & Slaney
[
20
]
and Casari
[
8
]
, is a well-known contraclassical, paraconsistent logic. It is also referred to as the
logic of Abelian
\ell
-groups

[
7
]
or
Abelian Group Logic

[
21
]
since the matrix models of Abelian logic are based on Abelian
\ell
-groups, their positive cones being the filters of designated elements.
A primary motivation for studying Abelian logic is its connection to
Łukasiewicz logic
, since there exists a well-known categorical equivalence between the categories of strongly pointed Abelian
\ell
-groups and
\mathsf{MV}
-algebras, the algebras of Łukasiewicz logic
[
9
]
.

Recently,
modal Abelian logic
has also gained some attention, particularly from the perspective of its real-valued Kripke semantics
[
12
,
13
,
19
]
. Following this, we investigate
pointed real-valued modal Abelian logic
from an algebraic perspective. Our motivation for this focus is two-fold. First, it structurally parallels real-valued modal Łukasiewicz logic. Exploiting this connection,
pointed
modal Abelian logic (albeit not explicitly named therein) is already used in
[
13
, Sect. 2.3]
to directly analyze real-valued modal Łukasiewicz logic
[
16
]
. Specifically, their analysis requires the inclusion of a designated constant
c
to act as a fixed point under the
\Box
-modality (i.e.,
\Box c=c
), which serves as the falsum for Łukasiewicz logic.
Furthermore, by substituting bounded
\mathsf{MV}
-algebras for Abelian
\ell
-groups, our framework lifts the traditional restriction to the bounded interval
[0,1]
, allowing formulas to instead be evaluated over
\mathbb{R}
. Second, this framework allows us to introduce a modal extension of
Łukasiewicz unbound logic
. Originally defined in
[
11
]
, this logic is strongly complete with respect to the Abelian
\ell
-group of real numbers equipped with the designated element
-1
. Consequently, by equipping our real-valued Kripke models with the same constant
-1
, we provide a natural modal semantics for Łukasiewicz unbound logic.
Despite these connections, the
algebraic
aspects of pointed modal Abelian logic have hitherto remained unexplored.

In this article we close this gap, introducing the variety of (negatively)
pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups

\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}
(Definition
3.4
) and explore its relationship to real-valued Kripke semantics of pointed modal Abelian logic (with constant
-1
). We do this by constructing complex algebras in
\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}
from Kripke frames (entailing an algebraic soundness result, Proposition
3.9
) and vice versa. Our first main result is a Truth Lemma for
strongly
negatively pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups (Corollary
4.6
). Using this, we characterize the validities of pointed modal Abelian logic via an equational condition (
A

∞

) on the variety
\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}
, which may be seen as a form of (infinitary)
algebraic completeness
(Theorem
4.9
).

The primary technical challenges to overcome here stem from the algebraic behavior of strong units and the non-trivial intersection of maximal
\ell
-ideals. In general, a non-zero element of a negatively pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-group may belong to every maximal
\ell
-ideal. This effectively prevents us from using such an element as a falsifier for a formula, since no world in the corresponding canonical frame would evaluate it as non-zero. The rule (
A

∞

) ensure this does not happen. Furthermore, this rule allows us to argue via
strongly
pointed algebras in our proof of Theorem
4.9
.
While it is a well-known fact that the property of possessing a strong unit is not first-order definable, enforcing a strong unit condition is justified within our framework since it corresponds directly to the restriction to
bounded
valuations in real-valued Kripke models (required to guarantee the semantics of the
\Box
-modality is well-defined). We leave it up for future research to determine whether these issues could be circumvented in other ways.

2
Pointed Abelian
\ell
-groups

In this (mostly preliminary) section, we recall some important facts about pointed Abelian
\ell
-groups, focusing in particular on structures equipped with a negative strong unit (the reader should feel free to skip some of the more technical proofs here until we refer back to the corresponding results later on).
We give a brief exposition of
\ell
-ideal theory, and analyze the structure of
\ell
-ideals in the presence of such a designated unit. Adapting techniques from the theory of
\mathsf{MV}
-algebras, we characterize the radical, the intersection of all maximal
\ell
-ideals, via infinitesimal elements. Lastly, we establish a pivotal correspondence between the maximal
\ell
-ideals of a strongly negatively pointed Abelian
\ell
-group and its homomorphisms into the pointed Abelian
\ell
-group of reals
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1}
, which will be an important ‘bridge’ between the abstract algebraic syntax and our concrete real-valued semantics.

Abelian logic
(e.g. see
[
20
,
8
,
11
]
) can be elegantly axiomatized as the negation-free fragment of
\mathsf{FL}_{e}
(i.e. Full Lambek calculus with exchange) extended by the axiom of relativity
\big((\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow\psi\big)\rightarrow\varphi
. Due to our algebraic focus, here we only treat Abelian logic via its algebraic models, i.e. Abelian
\ell
-groups. Nevertheless, let us mention that the logics corresponding to the varieties defined in this paper inherit similar axiomatizations, since they are superabelian (see
[
11
]
).

Definition 2.1

(
Abelian
\ell
-group)
.

An algebra
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}=\langle{A,+,-,\lor,\land,0}\rangle
is an
Abelian
\ell
-group
if
\langle{A,+,-,0}\rangle
is an Abelian group,
\langle{A,\lor,\land}\rangle
is a lattice and
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
satisfies the monotonicity condition
x\leq y\Rightarrow x+z\leq y+z
.

When brackets are omitted the lattice operations take precedence over the group operations, e.g.
x+y\land z
is understood as
x+(y\land z)
. For
a\in A
we define the
absolute value

|a|:=a\lor-a
and we let
n\cdot a:=a+\cdots+a
denote the
n
-fold sum for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
z\in\mathbb{Z}{\setminus}\mathbb{N}
we set
{z\cdot a:=(-z)\cdot(-a)}
.

Definition 2.2

(
Strong Unit)
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
be an Abelian
\ell
-group. We say that
p\in A
is a
strong unit
of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
if

\forall a\in A\colon\exists z\in\mathbb{Z}\colon z\cdot p\geq a.

(
SU
)

Note that for a strictly negative strong unit
p<0
the condition (
SU
) simplifies to

\forall a\in A\colon\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\colon n\cdot p\leq a.

(
SU
-)

An algebra
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}=\langle{A,+,-,\lor,\land,0,p}\rangle
is a
pointed Abelian
\ell
-group
if
\langle{A,+,-,\lor,\land,0}\rangle
is an Abelian
\ell
-group and
p\in A
.
It is
strongly pointed
if
p
is a strong unit and
negatively pointed
if
p\leq 0
.

Remark 2.3
.

Traditionally, a strong unit is required to be strictly positive (e.g. see
[
4
, p. 300]
). Note that we use
\mathbb{Z}
in (
SU
), which allows negative strong units. This distinction purely serves for notational convenience: since we primarily work in the negative cone, it avoids a proliferation of minus signs in our proofs. While our definition differs from the standard one, the structural role is identical:
p<0
is a strong unit in our sense if and only if its additive inverse
-p>0
is a standard strong unit.

As a standard example, let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1}=\langle{\mathbb{R},+,-,\lor,\land,0,-1}\rangle
be the Abelian group of real numbers with addition together with its linear order and the designated point
-1
. This clearly is a strongly negatively pointed Abelian
\ell
-group (in fact, it is our
algebra of truth-degrees
later on).

The following properties of Abelian
\ell
-groups are immediate consequences of the well-known fact that the variety of Abelian
\ell
-groups is generated by the
\ell
-group of integers
\mathbf{Z}
(see
[
2
, Thm. 6.1]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}=\langle{A,+,-,\lor,\land,0}\rangle
be an Abelian
\ell
-group and
a,b\in A
.

(i)

a=a\land 0+a\lor 0
,

(ii)

\big((a-b)\lor 0\big)\land\big((b-a)\lor 0\big)=0
,

(iii)

(a\lor b)+c=(a+c)\lor(b+c)
,

(iv)

(a-b)\lor 0=a-a\land b
,

(v)

|a+b|\leq|a|+|b|
.

Definition 2.5

(
\ell
-ideal
)
.

An
\ell
-ideal
of an Abelian
\ell
-group
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
is an
\ell
-subgroup
I\subseteq{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
that is
convex
(equivalently
0\leq|x|\leq|i|
for some
i\in I
implies
x\in I
).
An
\ell
-ideal
I
is
prime
if it satisfies

a\land b\in I\quad\Rightarrow\quad a\in I\text{ or }b\in I.

(
PI
)

First, let us note that for an
\ell
-ideal
I
and
a\in A
by definition it holds that
a\in I
iff
|a|\in I
iff
-|a|\in I
.
One can easily prove (using Zorn’s Lemma) that for each element
a\in A{\setminus}\{0\}
there exists an
\ell
-ideal which is maximal among all the
\ell
-ideals which do not contain
a
. Every such
\ell
-ideal is prime and we refer to them as
values
of
a

[
23
, Thm. 2.4.2]
.

It is well known that
maximal

\ell
-ideals are prime (again see
[
23
, Thm. 2.4.2]
), although in general an Abelian
\ell
-group
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
need not have any maximal ideals. However, if
p\in A
is a strong unit, the maximal
\ell
-ideals correspond to values of
p
(since any
\ell
-ideal containing
p
is improper). In fact, in the presence of a strong unit every proper
\ell
-ideal
I\subseteq{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
can be extended to a maximal
\ell
-ideal
M\supseteq I
(which is again easily shown via Zorn’s Lemma).

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
be an Abelian
\ell
-group and
S\subseteq A
.
By
C(S)
we denote the
\ell
-ideal generated by
S
.
It is well-known
[
23
, Thm. 2.2.4 (b)]
that

C(S):=\{x\in A\mid|x|\leq|b_{1}|+\dots+|b_{n}|\text{ for some }b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\in S\}.

(
IG
)

Let us also recall from
[
23
, Thm. 2.2.4 (d)]
, that for two sets
S,T\subseteq A
we have

C(S)\lor C(T)=C(S\cup T)=\{x\in A\mid|x|\leq|b_{1}|+\dots+|b_{n}|\text{ for some }b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\in S\cup T\}.

Therefore, for a principal
\ell
-ideal
J=\langle a\rangle=\{x\mid\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\colon|x|\leq n\cdot|a|\}
, we obtain

I\lor J=I\lor\{x\mid\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\colon|x|\leq n\cdot|a|\}=\{x\mid\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\;\exists i\in I\colon|x|\leq i+n\cdot|a|\}.

In particular, this implies that for every maximal
\ell
-ideal
I
of an Abelian
\ell
-group
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
and

:

A=\{x\mid\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\exists i\in I\colon|x|\leq i+n\cdot|a|\}.

(
IG
\ast
)

For a strongly pointed Abelian
\ell
-group
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}
we define the
radical
of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}
as the intersection of all maximal ideals of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}
and denote it by
\mathsf{rad}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p})
. Equivalently,
\mathsf{rad}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p})
is the intersection of all values of
p
. We call
a\in A
is
infinitely small
(or
infinitesimal
) if

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\colon n\cdot|a|\leq|p|.

(
INF
)

In the case where
a,p\leq 0
, Equation (
INF
) simplifies to

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\colon n\cdot a-p\geq 0.

(
INF
-)

One can easily check that this is also equivalent to

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\colon 2^{k}\cdot a-p\geq 0
.

We now show that the radical of a strongly pointed Abelian
\ell
-group consists exactly of its infinitesimal elements. The proof of the following is similar to the one of
[
9
, Prop. 3.6.4]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}
be a strongly pointed Abelian
\ell
-group. Then
a\in\mathsf{rad}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p})
iff
a
is infinitely small.

Proof.

If
a\notin\mathsf{rad}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p})
there is a maximal
\ell
-ideal
I
such that
a\notin I
.
Consequently,
p
is in the
\ell
-ideal generated by
\{a\}\cup I
and thus by (
IG

∗

) there are
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in I
such that
0<|p|\leq n\cdot|a|+i
,
which is equivalent to
|p|-n\cdot|a|\leq i
. Assume towards contradiction that
a
is infinitely small. Then
|p|-(n+1)\cdot|a|\geq 0
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, equivalently
|p|-n\cdot|a|\geq|a|
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus altogether we have
0\leq|a|\leq|p|-n\cdot|a|\leq i
, which by convexity of the
\ell
- ideal
I
implies
a\in I
, a contradiction.

Conversely, assume
a\in A
is not infinitely small. Then there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

n\cdot|a|-|p|\nleq 0
, which implies
(n\cdot|a|-|p|)\lor 0>0
. Let
P
be a value of
(n\cdot|a|-|p|)\lor 0
.
By Lemma
2.4

(ii)
we have

\big((n\cdot|a|-|p|)\lor 0\big)\land\big((|p|-n\cdot|a|)\lor 0\big)=0.

Since
P
is prime, it follows that
(|p|-n\cdot|a|)\lor 0\in P
. Let
M
be a maximal
\ell
-ideal containing
P
and towards contradiction assume that
a\in M
. Then
(n\cdot|a|)\lor 0\in M
and

(n\cdot|a|)\lor 0\geq(n\cdot|a|-|p|)\lor 0>0.

Therefore,
(n\cdot|a|-|p|)\lor 0\in M
.
Consequently, by Lemma
2.4
(i) we find

(|p|-n\cdot|a|)\lor 0-(n\cdot|a|-|p|)\lor 0=(|p|-n\cdot|a|)\lor 0+(|p|-n\cdot|a|)\land 0=(|p|-n\cdot|a|)\in M.

Since
p\notin M
we have
|p|\notin M
and thus also
n\cdot|a|\notin M
. However, this implies
|a|\notin M
, and thus lastly
a\notin M
, a contradiction our assumption
a\in M
which shows
a\notin\mathsf{rad}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p})
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}
be a strongly negatively pointed Abelian
\ell
-group and
I
an
\ell
-ideal of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
. Then
I
is maximal if and only if
I=v^{-1}(0)
for some homomorphism
v\in\mathrm{Hom}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p},{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1})
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
:
Let
I
be maximal. By Hölder’s Theorem
[
23
, Thm. 2.3.10]

{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}/I
embeds into
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{R}
. This gives us
v\in\mathrm{Hom}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p},{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{r})
.
Since
p
is a strong unit of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
,
p\notin I
and
v(p)\neq 0
. Since
p
is negative, also
v(p)\leq 0
.
Therefore
v(p)=r<0
. Lastly, since
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{r_{1}}\cong{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{r_{2}}
for any two negative
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
v\in\mathrm{Hom}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p},{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1})
.

(\Leftarrow)
: For every
v\in\mathrm{Hom}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p},{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1})
, the subset
v^{-1}(0)
is an
\ell
-ideal of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}
and any
\ell
-subgroup of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1}
is simple, which ensures that the ideal
v^{-1}(0)
has to be maximal.
∎

This correspondence between infinitely small elements, maximal
\ell
-ideals and homomorphisms into
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1}
will be important later on, since

is the algebra of truth-degrees for pointed modal Abelian logic introduced in the next section (in particular,
canonical frames
are structures based on the collections of homomorphisms

).

3
Pointed Modal Abelian Logic: Frames and Algebras

We first introduce relational (Kripke) semantics for pointed modal Abelian logic similar to the real-valued logic of
[
13
]
and other many-valued modal logics on crisp frames (Subsection
3.1
). Next, we introduce the variety of pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups and establish some basic identities therein (Subsection
3.2
). Lastly, we explain how to ‘shift’ between these two frameworks constructing complex algebras (in particular showing algebraic soundness) and canonical frames (Subsection
3.3
). This sets up the scene for our main results which we prove in the subsequent Section
4
.

3.1
Relational Semantics

The
language

\mathfrak{L}_{p}
of pointed modal Abelian logic is defined inductively from a countable set
\mathit{Prop}=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots\}
of
propositional variables
, the signature
\langle{+,-,\vee,\wedge,0,p}\rangle
of pointed Abelian
\ell
-groups and the unary modality
\Box
.
Let
\mathit{Fm}_{\!\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}_{p}}
denote the set of
formulas
over the language
\mathfrak{L}_{p}
.

Definition 3.1

(
Model)
.

A
model

\mathfrak{M}=\langle{W,R,\mathcal{V}}\rangle
consists of a
Kripke frame

\langle{W,R}\rangle
(i.e. a non-empty set
W
with a binary relation
R\subseteq W^{2}
), together with a
bounded propositional valuation
, by which we mean a map

\mathcal{V}\colon W\times\mathit{Prop}\to\mathbb{R}

such that for every propositional variable
x_{i}
there exists a positive real number
r_{i}
with
\{\mathcal{V}(u,x_{i})\mid u\in W\}\subseteq[-r_{i},r_{i}]
.

Note that this slightly differs from
[
13
]
, where a propositional valuation is itself bounded. However, it is clear that our condition suffices to ensure that the following interpretation of
\Box
is (still) well-defined.

Definition 3.2

(
Satisfaction & Validity)
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=\langle{W,R,\mathcal{V}}\rangle
be a model and
u\in W
. We inductively extend the valuation to
\mathcal{V}\colon W\times\mathit{Fm}_{\!\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}_{p}}\to\mathbb{R}
by interpreting the non-modal connectives in the algebra
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1}
in the standard way. Modal formulas are interpreted as follows:

\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)=\bigwedge_{uRv}\mathcal{V}(v,\varphi).

(In particular, here we set
\bigwedge\varnothing:=0
.)
A formula
\varphi
is
satisfied
at
u\in W
if and only if

\mathcal{V}(u,\varphi)\geq 0

and the formula is
valid
, denoted
\vDash\varphi
, if this holds at every state of every model.

The following properties correspond to our axiomatization of pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups later on (Definition
3.4
) and essentially yield (algebraic)
soundness
later on.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\mathfrak{M}=\langle{W,R,\mathcal{V}}\rangle
a model,
u\in W
, and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathit{Fm}_{\!\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}_{p}}
, the following hold:

(i)

\mathcal{V}\big(u,\Box(\psi-\varphi)\big)\leq\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\psi)-\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)
,

(ii)

\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)+\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)=\mathcal{V}\big(u,\Box(\varphi+\varphi)\big)
,

(iii)

\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)\lor 0=\mathcal{V}\big(u,\Box(\varphi\lor 0)\big)
,

(iv)

\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)\land\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\psi)=\mathcal{V}\big(u,\Box(\varphi\land\psi)\big)
,

(v)

\mathcal{V}(u,|\Box p-p|\land|\Box\varphi|)=0
,

(vi)

\mathcal{V}(u,-\Box p)=\mathcal{V}(u,\Box{-p})
.

Proof.

(i)
: By substituting
\chi=\psi-\varphi
and adding
\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)
to both sides of the inequation, it suffices to prove

\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\chi)+\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)\leq\mathcal{V}(u,\Box(\chi+\varphi))
, or equivalently

\bigwedge_{uRv}\mathcal{V}(v,\chi)+\bigwedge_{uRv}\mathcal{V}(v,\varphi)\leq\bigwedge_{uRv}\mathcal{V}(v,\chi+\varphi).

This follows, since we have
\bigwedge_{uRv}\mathcal{V}(v,\chi)+\bigwedge_{uRv}\mathcal{V}(v,\varphi)\leq\mathcal{V}(v,\chi+\varphi)
for each
uRv
.

(ii)
: Directly follows from the identity
\bigwedge_{i\in I}r_{i}+\bigwedge_{i\in I}r_{i}=\bigwedge_{i\in I}r_{i}+r_{i}
of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1}
.

(iii)
: Directly follows from the infinite distributivity law of
\ell
-groups, see
[
2
, Eq. 1.1.11]
.

(iv)
: Directly follows from the law
\bigwedge_{i\in I}r_{i}\wedge\bigwedge_{i\in I}s_{i}=\bigwedge_{i\in I}r_{i}\wedge s_{i}
of

4.2

Similar to the case of modal
\sf MV
-algebras
[

, the Truth Lemma of the previous section does not immediately yield algebraic completeness. In particular, if the inequation
\varphi\geq 0
does not hold in some
\langle{{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p},\Box}\rangle\in\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}
we have yet to obtain the
witnessing homomorphism
which shows that
\varphi
is not valid in the corresponding canonical frame
\mathfrak{F}_{\langle{{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p},\Box}\rangle}
. To construct such a homomorphism, we will use Lemma
2.6
and introduce an infinitary rule (
A

∞

) based on (
INF-
) to capture that the corresponding term is not evaluated inside the radical.

Moreover, we must ensure that a potential counterexample is witnessed in a well-defined model, that is, with a
bounded valuation
(recall Definition
3.1
). We observed in Subsection
3.3
that this is ensured for canonical frames of
strongly
pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups.

To formalize the requirement that it suffices to check
\varphi
within these bounds, let us first set the following notation.
For a variable
x_{i}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define terms

x_{i}^{k}:=(x_{i}\lor k\cdot p)\land k\cdot(-p).

For a formula
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we let
\varphi^{k}
denote the substitution instance

\varphi^{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\varphi(x_{1}^{k},\dots,x_{n}^{k}).

Now the
bounded valuations
rule is expressed as follows.

\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}\models\varphi^{k}\geq 0\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}\models\varphi\geq 0

(
BV
)

Note that the other implication trivially holds.
Let us emphasize that rule (
BV
) is
not
a single infinitary rule in the standard sense. Because the syntactic substitutions happen
within
the formula itself, this property cannot be captured by one generalized quasi-equation. Instead, (
BV
) represents an infinite family of generalized quasi-equations, one for every formula
\varphi\in\mathit{Fm}_{\!\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}_{p}}
.

As previously mentioned, we need to combine this with (
INF-
) to ensure the existence of homomorphisms witnessing non-validity. Thus, we end up with the following.

Definition 4.7

(
Infinitary Algebraic Derivability)
.

We define

\vdash_{\infty}\varphi\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}\vDash n\cdot(\varphi^{k}\land 0)-p\geq 0\text{ for every }n,k\in\mathbb{N}.

(
A
\infty
)

Recall that our Truth lemma was only proved for strongly negatively pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups. The following lemma will allow us to ‘move’ to such an algebra in the proof of Theorem
4.9
later on.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\langle{{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p},\Box}\rangle
be a negatively pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-group with
p\neq 0
and let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}
be the
\ell
-ideal of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
generated by
p
. Then
\langle{{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p},\Box}\rangle
is a strongly negatively pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-group.

Proof.

We only need to verify that
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p}
is closed under
\Box
. Let
b\in{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p}
. We have
|b|\leq n\cdot|p|
and hence we obtain
n\cdot p\leq b\leq-n\cdot p
. By Lemma
3.6
(iv) we know that the operator
\Box
is monotone, from which we get (using
-\Box(n\cdot p)=\Box(-n\cdot p)
) that
\Box(n\cdot p)\leq\Box b\leq\Box(-n\cdot p)
and
\Box(n\cdot p)\leq-\Box b\leq\Box(-n\cdot p)
. Therefore, we showed
|\Box b|\leq|n\cdot p|
and thus
\Box b\in{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section, completeness of pointed modal Abelian logic with respect to the derivability relation of Definition
4.7
.

Theorem 4.9

(
Infinitary Algebraic Completeness)
.

Let
\varphi
be a formula. Then
\vDash\varphi
if and only if
\vdash_{\infty}\varphi
.

Proof.

For the implication
(\Rightarrow)
, assume
\nvdash_{\infty}\varphi
.
By definition, there exist
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}\nvDash n\cdot(\varphi^{k}\land 0)-p\geq 0
. Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}\in\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}
and
e
be an evaluation on
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}
such that

n\cdot(e(\varphi^{k})\land 0)-p\ngeq 0
.
Note that
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}_{p}
satisfies
p\neq 0
, since otherwise it would satisfy
e(x_{i}^{k})=(e(x_{i})\lor 0)\land 0=0
for each
x_{i}
and thus
n\cdot(e(\varphi^{k})\land 0)-p=0
.
Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}
be the
\ell
-ideal of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}
generated by
p
. By Lemma
4.8
we know that
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p}\in\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}
is strongly negatively pointed.
We define an evaluation
\hat{e}
on
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p}
by
\hat{e}(x)=e(x^{k})
.
By the above, it holds that
n\cdot(\hat{e}(\varphi)\land 0)-p\ngeq 0
.
From Lemma
2.6
we obtain that
\hat{e}(\varphi\land 0)\notin\mathsf{rad}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p})
.
Consequently, there exists a homomorphism
u\in\mathrm{Hom}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p},{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1})
such that
u(\hat{e}(\varphi\land 0))<0
.
Let
\mathcal{V}_{\hat{e}}
be the valuation on the canonical frame of
{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p}
defined by
\mathcal{V}_{\hat{e}}(v,x_{i})=v(\hat{e}(x_{i}))
for all
v\in\mathrm{Hom}({\boldsymbol{\mathbf{B}}}_{p},{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1})
. The Truth Lemma yields

\mathcal{V}_{\hat{e}}(u,\varphi)\wedge 0=\mathcal{V}_{\hat{e}}(u,\varphi\wedge 0)=u\big(\hat{e}(\varphi\wedge 0)\big)<0

and therefore clearly
\mathcal{V}_{\hat{e}}(u,\varphi)<0
, which witnesses
\not\vDash\varphi
.

For the implication
(\Leftarrow)
, assume
\not\vDash\varphi
and let
\langle{W,R,\mathcal{V}}\rangle
be a model such that
\mathcal{V}(u,\varphi)<0
for some
u\in W
. Since
\mathcal{V}
is a bounded valuation there is some
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{V}(u,\varphi^{k})<0
also holds.
Let
e
be the evaluation on the complex algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{\langle{W,R}\rangle}
defined by
e(x_{i})=\mathcal{V}(-,x_{i})
. We have
e(\varphi^{k})=\mathcal{V}(-,\varphi^{k})
. Since
e(\varphi^{k})(u)=\mathcal{V}(u,\varphi^{k})<0
it follows that
e(\varphi^{k}\land 0)<0
.
Since
\{f\in\mathfrak{C}_{\langle{W,R}\rangle}\colon f(u)=0\}
is a maximal
\ell
-ideal of
\mathfrak{C}_{\langle{W,R}\rangle}
(because it is the preimage
\mathsf{ev}_{u}^{-1}(0)
of the homomorphism
\mathsf{ev}_{u}\colon\mathfrak{C}_{\langle{W,R}\rangle}\to{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{R}}}_{-1}
evaluating at
u
) and
e(\varphi^{k}\land 0)\notin\{f\in\mathfrak{C}_{\langle{W,R}\rangle}\colon f(u)=0\}
by Lemma
2.6
it follows there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
e(n\cdot(\varphi^{k}\land 0)-p)\ngeq 0
, which by definition shows
\not\vdash_{\infty}\varphi
.
This completes the proof.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced the variety of negatively pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups and established its connection to real-valued modal logic, culminating in Theorem
4.9
characterizing modal validity via the rule (
A

∞

). We take this as evidence that the variety
\mathsf{mAb}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-}
deserves further investigation in the future.

Naturally, this also applies to the study of modal Abelian
\ell
-groups
without
a designated point. The crucial role played by the non-zero designated element was highlighted throughout the present work. Without the constant symbol
p
in the language, every term function
t(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
over any modal Abelian
\ell
-group trivially satisfies
t(0,\dots,0)=0
, which effectively prevents the construction of the separating terms
t_{d}
of Lemma
4.2
. Moreover, the strong unit, the radical, and the boundedness of valuations are all dependent on the assumption of pointedness. Nevertheless, in future work we aim to establish a similar algebraic completeness result for (point-free) real-valued modal logic with respect to the variety
\mathsf{mAb}
.

It is common in many-valued modal logic to not only consider crisp, but
labeled accessibility relations
(e.g. see
[
5
,
6
]
for the case of finitely-valued modal Łukasiewicz logic). In (pointed) modal Abelian logic, one may consider
bounded
labeled accessibility relations
R\colon W^{2}\to[-r,r]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with the modality interpreted via
\mathcal{V}(u,\Box\varphi)=\bigwedge_{v\in W}\mathcal{V}(v,\varphi)-R(u,v)
. Studying this logic and the relationship to modal Łukasiewicz logic would clearly be an interesting (albeit challenging) future direction.

There is a well-known categorical equivalence between the class of strongly pointed Abelian
\ell
-groups and the class of
\mathsf{MV}
-algebras established by the
Mundici functor
(e.g. see
[
9
]
). Similarly, we plan to investigate the category-theoretic relationship between strongly negatively pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups and the category
\mathsf{MMV}
of modal
\mathsf{MV}
-algebras of
[
16
]
(let us note here that the Mundici equivalence was extended to
monadic

\mathsf{MV}
-algebras in
[
10
]
). As a first step we can show that, given a negatively pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-group, its interval
[p,0]
is a modal
\mathsf{MV}
-algebra. The construction in the reverse direction appears to be less straightforward.

Another open question is whether we can replace rule (
A

∞

) with a
single
generalized quasi-equation. As discussed previously, rule (
BV
) corresponds to an infinite family of generalized quasi-equations (one for each formula
\varphi
) and consequently defines a generalized quasi-variety, and we want to investigate whether there exists a single generalized quasi-equation defining it.

Finally, we also want to address questions regarding computational
complexity
. To this end, we plan to generalize and apply the approach presented in
[
15
]
for unbounded Łukasiewicz logic to the setting of strongly pointed modal Abelian
\ell
-groups.
