Inequalities for the number of t t -hooks in two partition classes arising from sum-product identities Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Aritram Dhar, Byungchan Kim, Eunmi Kim ∗, Ae Ja Yee

Abstract
. Motivated by recent study on the number of t t -hooks in partitions arising from Euler’s partition identity, we investigate the number of t t -hooks in the sets from the first Rogers-Ramanujan identity and the first little Göllitz identity. In particular, for t = 1 , 2 t=1,2 , we obtain the generating functions for the number of t t -hooks and prove t t -hook inequalities by deriving asymptotic formulas.

Introduction

A
partition

\lambda

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{k})
of positive integers. The elements
\lambda_{i}
appearing in the sequence
\lambda
are called the
parts
of
\lambda
. The sum of all the parts of
\lambda
is called the
size
of
\lambda
and denoted by
|\lambda|
. We say
\lambda
is a partition of
n
if its size is equal to
n
.

The most foundational and simplest identity in the theory of partitions due to Euler states that the number of partitions of
n
into distinct parts is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into odd parts
[
2
, Corollary 1.2]
. This identity has inspired extensive research over the past several centuries leading to various generalizations and refinements, including the work of Glaisher
[
11
]
and Sylvester
[
19
]
from the late 19th century, the generalization of Andrews
[
1
]
, and the recent contribution of Andrews, Kumar and Yee
[
5
]
. Recently, Andrews studied Euler’s identity from a different angle while confirming two conjectures of Beck
[
4
,
20
]
. One of the results of Andrews can be reformulated as follows
[
4
, Theorem 2]
.

The total number of parts in the partitions of
n
into distinct parts is greater than or equal to the total number of different parts in the partitions of
n
into odd parts.

This inequality result was generalized by Ballantine, Burson, Craig, Folsom, and Wen to inequalities involving partition hooks
[
6
]
.

The

of a partition
\lambda
is a way of representing
\lambda
graphically where the parts
\lambda_{i}
of
\lambda
, called

, are arranged in left-justified rows with
\lambda_{i}
cells in the
i
-th row. The conjugate of a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{k})
is the partition
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\lambda^{\prime}_{2},\dots,\lambda^{\prime}_{\lambda_{1}})
whose Young diagram has the columns of
\lambda
as rows. For a cell in the
i
-th row and

-th column of the Young diagram of a partition
\lambda
, its

is defined as
h(i,j)=\lambda_{i}+\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-i-j+1
. See Figure

Let

be the total number of

(1.1)

a_{1}(n)\geq b_{1}(n)\text{ for $n\geq 1$}.

Noting this identity

\sum_{t\geq 1}a_{t}(n)=\sum_{t\geq 1}b_{t}(n)\text{ for $n\geq 1$ },

Ballantine, Burson, Craig, Folsom, and Wen conjectured that the inequality of Andrews in (

Conjecture 1.1

(Ballantine–Burson–Craig–Folsom–Wen

)
.

For every integer

,

(1.2)

a_{t}(n)-b_{t}(n)\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty.

The

[

]
and the remaining cases were settled by Craig, Dawsey, and Han

Euler’s identity connects partitions with a gap condition (distinct parts) and those with a part congruence condition (odd parts), and many other partition identities share this structure. The main objective of this paper is to extend the hook bias question in Conjecture
1.1
to identities of this type, more specifically, we will have our focus on identities involving gap
2
conditions.

The celebrated Rogers–Ramanujan identities, which were originally proved by Rogers
[
17
]
and rediscovered by Ramanujan
[
16
]
, are perfect examples of such identities, relating gap
2
conditions on parts to congruence conditions modulo
5
. The first Rogers–Ramanujan identity states that the number of partitions of
n
with parts differing by
2
is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into parts congruent to
1
or
4
mod
5
.

For a positive integer
t
, let
r_{1,t}(n)
be the number of
t
-hooks in all partitions of
n
with gap between parts being at least
2
and and
r_{2,t}(n)
be the number of
t
-hooks in all partitions of
n
with parts congruent to
1,4
modulo
5
. Our first main result is the following inequalities, which are comparable to the inequalities in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

For sufficiently large
n
,

r_{1,1}(n)>r_{2,1}(n)\quad\text{and}\quad r_{1,2}(n)<r_{2,2}(n).

We prove these inequalities by deriving asymptotic formulas for
r_{1,1}(n),r_{1,2}(n),r_{2,1}(n)
and
r_{2,2}(n)
, which are given in the following theorems.

Theorem 1.3
.

As
n\to\infty
,

r_{1,1}(n)\sim r_{1,2}(n)\sim\frac{3^{\frac{1}{4}}\phi^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(\phi)}{2\pi}n^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{2\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{15}}},

where
\phi
is the golden ratio.

Theorem 1.4
.

As
n\to\infty
,

r_{2,1}(n)\sim\frac{3^{\frac{1}{4}}\phi^{\frac{1}{2}}}{5\pi}n^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{2\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{15}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad r_{2,2}(n)\sim\frac{3^{\frac{5}{4}}\phi^{\frac{1}{2}}}{10\pi}n^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{2\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{15}}}.

The generating functions for
r_{1,1}(n),r_{1,2}(n),r_{2,1}(n)
and
r_{2,2}(n)
are either a form of a Nahm sum or a product of an infinite product and a rational function. Here by a Nahm sum, we mean that a

with

is a rational function in

[

,
21
,
22
,
23
]
. We employ Ingham’s tauberian theorem (see Section

. Nonetheless, investigating the asymptotic behavior of Nahm sums requires several non-trivial estimates. Our idea of proofs is similar to that of Bringmann, Man, Rolen, and Storzer
[
8
]
, but we simplify their proofs accordingly and give more necessary details. More details on how we obtain the necessary asymptotics are given in Section

Our second example is the little Göllnitz identities, which are analogues of the Rogers–Ramanujan identities for modulo 8
[
12
]
.
The first little Göllnitz identity states that the number of partitions of
n
with parts differing by
2
and no odd parts differing by exactly

is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into parts congruent to
1,5
or
6
modulo
8
.

For a positive integer
t
, let
g_{1,t}(n)
be the number of
t
-hooks in all partitions of
n
with gap between parts being at least
2
while no odd parts differing by exactly

are allowed. We also let

be the number of
t
-hooks in all partitions of
n
with parts congruent to

modulo
8
. Our second result is the following inequalities.

Theorem 1.5
.

For sufficiently large

,

g_{1,1}(n)>g_{2,1}(n)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{1,2}(n)<g_{2,2}(n).

They follow from the asymptotic formulas below.

Theorem 1.6
.

As
n\to\infty
,

g_{1,1}(n)\sim g_{1,2}(n)\sim\frac{\log(\sqrt{2}+1)}{2^{\frac{5}{4}}\pi}n^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{n}}.

Theorem 1.7
.

As
n\to\infty
,

g_{2,1}(n)\sim\frac{3}{2^{\frac{13}{4}}\pi}n^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{2,2}(n)\sim\frac{1}{2^{\frac{5}{4}}\pi}n^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{n}}.

The corresponding generating functions are also either Nahm sums or a product of an infinite product and a rational function. As the generating function is more complicated, there are additional technical difficulties. However, the main idea of the proofs remains the same as before.

2.
Preliminaries

For complex variables
a
,
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}
,
q
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
, the conventional
q
-Pochhammer symbol is defined as

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}:=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{i})\quad\text{and}\quad(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k};q)_{n}:=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(a_{i};q)_{n}.

Here and throughout the paper, we assume
|q|<1
.

We need to derive an asymptotic formula for
1/(q;q)_{n}
and this can be achieved using two lemmas in
[
8
, Lemmas 2.1, 2.2]
. In particular, Lemma 2.2 in
[
8
]
is a complex variable extension of Zagier’s previous result
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
for
\zeta=1
.

Throughout this paper, we set
q=e^{-z}
where
z=\varepsilon(1+iy)
with
\varepsilon>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\rm Re}(z)>0
,
|w|<1
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
with
\nu z=o(1)
. Then

\operatorname{Log}\left((we^{-\nu z}q;q)_{\infty}\right)=-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(w)\frac{1}{z}-\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\operatorname{Log}(1-w)-\frac{\nu^{2}z}{2}\frac{w}{1-w}+\psi_{w}(\nu,z),

where
\psi_{w}(\nu,z)
, for
R\in\mathbb{N}
, has an asymptotic expansion as
z\to 0
with
{\rm Re}(z)>0

(2.1)

\psi_{w}(\nu,z)=-\sum_{r=2}^{R-1}\left(B_{r}(-\nu)-\delta_{r,2}\nu^{2}\right)\operatorname{Li}_{2-r}(w)\frac{z^{r-1}}{r!}+O\left(z^{R-1}\right)

with
B_{r}(x)
the Bernoulli polynomial and
\delta_{i,j}
the Kronecker delta symbol. In particular, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the coefficient of
\nu^{n}
is
O\left(z^{\frac{2n}{3}}\right)
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Suppose that
y\ll\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
. Then we have that as
z\to 0
,

-\operatorname{Log}\left((q;q)_{\infty}\right)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6z}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Log}\left(\frac{z}{2\pi}\right)-\frac{z}{24}+O(z^{L})

for any
L\in\mathbb{N}
.

Next, we recall the Euler-Maclaurin summation formula.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

Let
a,z\in\mathbb{C}
. If
f
is holomorphic and has rapid decay in
\{tz+a:t\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\}
. Then we have, for all
R\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}f(nz+a)=\frac{1}{z}\int_{a}^{a+z\infty}f(x)dx+\frac{f(a)}{2}-\sum_{r=1}^{R}\frac{B_{2r}z^{2r-1}}{(2r)!}f^{(2r-1)(a)}+O(1)z^{2R}\int_{a}^{a+z\infty}\left|f^{(2R+1)}(x)\right|dx,

where
B_{r}
is the Bernoulli number.

We also recall the Tauberian theorem of Ingham.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Suppose that
B(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}b_{n}q^{n}
is a power series with non-negative real coefficients and radius of convergence at least one. If
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
are real numbers with
\gamma>0
such that

B\left(e^{-t}\right)\sim\alpha t^{\beta}e^{\frac{\gamma}{t}}\quad\text{as }t\to 0^{+},\qquad B\left(e^{-z}\right)\ll|z|^{\beta}e^{\frac{\gamma}{|z|}}\quad\text{as }z\to 0,

with
z=x+iy
, where
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
x>0
and in each region of the form
y\leq\Delta x
for
\Delta>0
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{N}b_{n}\sim\frac{\alpha\gamma^{\frac{\beta}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}N^{\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}e^{2\sqrt{\gamma N}}\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Furthermore, if
\{b_{n}\}
is weakly increasing, then

b_{n}\sim\frac{\alpha\gamma^{\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}n^{\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{3}{4}}}e^{2\sqrt{\gamma n}}\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

3.
t
-hooks in two partition sets from the first Rogers–Ramanujan identity

Let us start by recalling the first Rogers–Ramanujan identity:

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}=\frac{1}{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}.

For convenience, let

\mathcal{R}_{1}:=\{\lambda:\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\geq 2\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{R}_{2}:=\{\lambda:\lambda_{i}\equiv 1,4\pmod{5}\}.

For
j=1,2
, recall from Introduction that

\displaystyle r_{j,t}(n)

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathcal{R}_{j}\\
|\lambda|=n\end{subarray}}\widetilde{r}_{j,t}(\lambda),

where
\widetilde{r}_{j,t}(\lambda)
is the number of
t
-hooks in
\lambda\in\mathcal{R}_{j}
.

To study
r_{j,t}(n)
, we first need to find the bivariate generating function for
\mathcal{R}_{j}
:

R_{j,t}(x,q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{r_{j,t}(n)}q^{n}.

We then obtain the generating function of
r_{j,t}(n)
by differentiating
R_{j,t}(x,q)
with respect to
x
at
1
. Namely,

S_{j,t}(q):=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}R_{j,t}(x,q)\bigg|_{x=1}=\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{j,t}(n)q^{n}.

3.1.
t=1
case

We first observe from the definition of
t
-hooks that
1
-hooks are corner cells in the Young diagram, so the number of
1
-hooks equals the number of different parts. Thus,

\widetilde{r}_{1,1}(\lambda)=\ell(\lambda)\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{r}_{2,1}(\lambda)=d(\lambda),

where
\ell(\lambda)
and
d(\lambda)
denote the number of parts and the number of different parts, respectively.
The generating function for partitions in
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}},

and the index
n
in the summand counts the number of parts, from which it follows that

R_{1,1}(x,q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{r_{1,1}(n)}q^{n}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{R}_{1}}x^{\ell(\lambda)}q^{|\lambda|}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}.

Moreover, we see

\displaystyle R_{2,1}(x,q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{r_{2,1}(n)}q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{R}_{2}}x^{d(\lambda)}q^{|\lambda|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\equiv 1,4\!\!\!\pmod{5}}(1+xq^{n}+xq^{2n}+xq^{3n}+\cdots)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\equiv 1,4\!\!\!\pmod{5}}\left(1+\frac{xq^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\equiv 1,4\!\!\!\pmod{5}}\frac{1-(1-x)q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

We are now ready to give the generating function
S_{j,1}(q)
for
j=1,2
.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have

\displaystyle S_{1,1}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{1,1}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{nq^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}},

\displaystyle S_{2,1}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{2,1}(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}\frac{q+q^{4}}{1-q^{5}}.

Proof.

For each
S_{j,1}(q)
,
j=1,2
, we take the derivative of
R_{j,1}(x,q)
with respect to
x
at
x=1
. Then,

S_{1,1}(q)
is straightforward. For
S_{2,1}(q)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}R_{2,1}(x,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{((1-x)q,(1-x)q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{((1-x)q,(1-x)q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 1\atop n\equiv 1,4\!\!\!\pmod{5}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\operatorname{Log}(1-(1-x)q^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{((1-x)q,(1-x)q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 1\atop n\equiv 1,4\!\!\!\pmod{5}}\frac{q^{n}}{1-(1-x)q^{n}},

from which it follows that

S_{2,1}(q)=\frac{1}{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 1\atop n\equiv 1,4\!\!\!\pmod{5}}q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}\frac{q+q^{4}}{1-q^{5}},

as desired.
∎

3.2.
t=2
case

In this section, we will study the number of
2
-hooks. From the definition of
2
-hooks, it easily follows that the number of
2
-hooks equals the number of parts
\lambda_{i}
with
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}>1
plus the number of parts appearing at least twice. Here, we assume that
\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)+1}=0
. Thus,

\widetilde{r}_{1,2}(\lambda)=\ell_{>1}(\lambda)\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{r}_{2,2}(\lambda)=d_{>1}(\lambda)+m_{>1}(\lambda),

where
\ell_{>1}(\lambda),d_{>1}(\lambda)
and
m_{>1}(\lambda)
denote the number of parts greater than
1
, the number of different parts greater than
1
, and the number of parts with multiplicity greater than
1
, respectively.

First, for partitions in
\mathcal{R}_{1}
, we can divide them into two groups: partitions with or without a part of size
1
. Then

R_{1,2}(x,q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{r_{1,2}(n)}q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x^{n-1}q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n-1}}+\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}q^{n^{2}+n}}{(q;q)_{n}},

where the first sum generates partitions with a part of size
1
and the second sum generates partitions with no part of size
1
.

Also, for
\mathcal{R}_{2}
, applying the same analysis seen in Section
3.1
, we can obtain

R_{2,2}(x,q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{r_{2,2}(n)}q^{n}=\left(1+q+\frac{xq^{2}}{1-q}\right)\prod_{n\equiv 1,4\!\!\!\pmod{5}\atop n>1}\left(1+xq^{n}+\frac{x^{2}q^{2n}}{1-q^{n}}\right).

Similarly to Proposition
3.1
, we can derive the generating functions of
r_{1,2}(n)
and
r_{2,2}(n)
from the above generating functions. We omit the details.

Proposition 3.2
.

We have

\displaystyle S_{1,2}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{1,2}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{nq^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n-1}},

\displaystyle S_{2,2}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{2,2}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}\left(\frac{q^{4}+q^{6}}{1-q^{5}}+\frac{q^{2}+q^{8}}{1-q^{10}}\right).

4.
Asymptotics for hook numbers arising from the first Rogers–Ramanujan identity

In this section, we derive the asymptotic formulas in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.
As the proof of Theorem
1.3
consists of several steps, we first outline the key steps and main ideas of the proof.

Let us recall the generating function of
r_{1,1}(n)
from Proposition
3.1
:

S_{1,1}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{1,1}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{nq^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}.

We note that the summand
\frac{nq^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}
is essentially unimodal and thus the contribution around the peak will dominate the asymptotic of
S_{1,1}(q)
.
The summand is near the peak when

\frac{(n+1)q^{(n+1)^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n+1}}\big/\frac{nq^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}\approx 1,

i.e.,
q^{n}\approx\phi^{-1}
, where
\phi
is the golden ratio.
Thus, the main contribution to
S_{1,1}(q)
comes from the summand when
n
is in

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{n:\left|n-\frac{\log(\phi)}{\varepsilon}\right|\leq\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{5}}\right\},

and we decompose

(4.1)

S_{1,1}(q)=\mathcal{S}_{1,1}(q)+\mathcal{E}_{1,1}(q):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{nq^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{nq^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}.

Remark
.

In the definition of
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
, we choose the exponent of
\varepsilon
for computational simplicity.
We can choose
\lambda\in(-5/8,-1/2)
and consider

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}:=\left\{n:\left|n-\frac{\log(\phi)}{\varepsilon}\right|\leq\varepsilon^{\lambda}\right\}.

To apply Ingham’s Tauberian theorem, we need to estimate
S_{1,1}(q)
as
z=\varepsilon(1+iy)\to 0
in the bounded cone
|y|\ll 1
. In Section
4.1
, we first obtain the asymptotic behavior of the summand near the peak with a narrow range of
y
. While this range of
y
is not wide enough to employ the Tauberian theorem, this asymptotic formula is convenient to apply the saddle point method to derive

(4.2)

\mathcal{S}_{1,1}(q)\sim\frac{\sqrt{\phi}}{5^{1/4}}\log(\phi)z^{-1}e^{\frac{\pi^{2}}{15z}}

as
z\to 0
. In Section
4.2
, we will show that the contribution from
\mathcal{E}_{1,1}(q)
is negligible as expected. In Section
4.3
, we show that the above asymptotic formula in (
4.2
) can be extended to a wider range
y\ll 1
. Finally, we give the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
in Sections
4.4
and
4.5
.

4.1.
Asymptotics for
\mathcal{S}_{1,1}(q)
in a narrow range of
y

We first prove narrow range estimates near the peak.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
and
u\in\mathbb{C}
be such that

(4.3)

n=\frac{\log(\phi)}{z}+\frac{u}{\sqrt{z}}.

If
y\ll\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
, then we have, uniformly in

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1-\sqrt{2})+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right)\frac{1}{2z}+\left(\frac{u}{2\sqrt{z}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\log(2)-\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\!\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{z}}-\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2}\!z

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{11z}{24}-\psi_{1-\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{z}}-\frac{3}{2},2z\right)-\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{1-2m}(-1)}{(2m)!}z^{2m}+O\left(z^{L}\right).

For

, we have

-(n^{2}+n)z=-\frac{\log^{2}(\sqrt{2}+1)}{4z}-\frac{\log(\sqrt{2}+1)}{2}-\frac{\log(\sqrt{2}+1)}{\sqrt{z}}u-\sqrt{z}u-u^{2}.

In sum, using
[
15
, eq. (11)]

\operatorname{Li}_{2}(\sqrt{2}-1)-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1-\sqrt{2})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}-\frac{\log^{2}(\sqrt{2}+1)}{2},

we arrive at

\frac{nq^{n^{2}+n}(-q^{-1};q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}=\left(\frac{\log(\sqrt{2}+1)}{2z}+\frac{u}{\sqrt{z}}\right)2^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\frac{z}{\pi}}\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{16z}-2u^{2}+\mathcal{E}+O\left(z^{L}\right)\right),

where

\mathcal{E}=-\frac{3\sqrt{2}-4}{2}\sqrt{z}u-\frac{15-9\sqrt{2}}{8}z+\psi_{\sqrt{2}-1}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{z}},2z\right)-\psi_{1-\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{z}}-\frac{3}{2},2z\right)-\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{1-2m}(-1)}{(2m)!}z^{2m}.

As
y\ll\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}+\delta}
, we have
z\asymp\varepsilon
. From
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
, we have
u\ll\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}+\delta_{1}}
. Thus, from (
2.1
), we find that
\mathcal{E}\ll\varepsilon^{3\delta_{1}}
holds uniformly in
u
since the constant in the error term is independent of
u
.
Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{nq^{n^{2}+n}(-q^{-1};q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(\sqrt{2}+1)}{2^{\frac{3}{4}}\sqrt{\pi z}}\left(1+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}+\delta_{1}}\right)\right)\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{16z}-2u^{2}\right)\left(1+O\left(\varepsilon^{3\delta_{1}}\right)\right).\qed

Using the bijection

n\mapsto u=-\frac{\log(\sqrt{2}+1)}{2\sqrt{z}}+n\sqrt{z},

we can derive the asymptotic of
H_{1,1}(q)
for
y
small. As the proof proceeds similarly to Theorem

, we omit the proof here.

Proposition 6.2
.

If

Now we give an error estimate for the sum of summands far from the peak.

Proposition 6.3
.

For every
L\in\mathbb{N}
, as
{\rm Re}(z)\to 0
in the right half-plane,

\left|\mathcal{E}_{1,1}(q)\right|=\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{nq^{n^{2}+n}(-q^{-1};q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}\right|\ll\varepsilon^{L-1}e^{\frac{\pi^{2}}{16\varepsilon}}.

Proof.

Since

\left|\frac{nq^{n^{2}+n}(-q^{-1};q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{n|q|^{n^{2}+n}(-|q|^{-1};|q|^{2})_{n}}{(|q|^{2};|q|^{2})_{n}},

it is sufficient to consider the case when
z
is real.
Thus, suppose that
z=\varepsilon
is real, i.e.,
y=0
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
, and let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
n=\frac{\log(\sqrt{2}+1)}{2\varepsilon}+\mu
.

First, we consider the sum over
n
with
\mu>0
. Then
q^{2n}=(\sqrt{2}-1)e^{-2\mu\varepsilon}
, and

\log\left((q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}\right)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\log\left(1-q^{2n+2m}\right)=-\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{q^{k(2n+2m)}}{k}=-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)^{k}}{k}\frac{e^{-2k\mu\varepsilon}}{e^{2k\varepsilon}-1}.

As

for all

and

for

, we have

\frac{e^{-2k\mu\varepsilon}}{e^{2k\varepsilon}-1}>(1-2k\mu\varepsilon)\left(\frac{1}{2k\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2k\varepsilon}-\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)+k\mu\varepsilon,

which implies

\log\left((q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}\right)<-\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{2}(\sqrt{2}-1)}{2\varepsilon}-\log(2-\sqrt{2})\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{\mu\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}.

Together with Lemma
2.2
, we find

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(\sqrt{2}-1)\right)\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi(2-\sqrt{2})}\right)

(6.1)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\varepsilon}{12}-\log(2-\sqrt{2})\mu-\frac{\mu\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}+O\left(\varepsilon^{L}\right).

We also have

k:\text{odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)^{k}}{k}\frac{e^{-2k\mu\varepsilon+3k\varepsilon}}{e^{2k\varepsilon}-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Sigma_{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k:\text{even}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)^{k}}{k}\frac{e^{-2k\mu\varepsilon+3k\varepsilon}}{e^{2k\varepsilon}-1}.

For the sum over odd

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle<-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k:\text{odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)^{k}}{k^{2}}-(1-\mu)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k:\text{odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)^{k}}{k^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(1-\mu)\log(\sqrt{2}-1)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\mu\right).

For small
\varepsilon

\mu>\frac{3}{2}

and

\Sigma_{2}<\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k:\text{even}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)^{k}}{k^{2}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\log\big((-q^{-1};q^{2})_{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{(-q^{-1};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(-q^{2n-3+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1-\sqrt{2})}{2\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}(1-\mu)\log(\sqrt{2}-1)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\mu\right)

(6.2)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi^{2}}{24\varepsilon}+\log(2)+\frac{11\varepsilon}{24}-\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{1-2m}(-1)}{(2m)!}\varepsilon^{2m}+O\left(\varepsilon^{L}\right).

For

(6.3)

Therefore, putting (
6.1
), (
6.2

-\mu^{2}\varepsilon-\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{1-2m}(-1)}{(2m)!}\varepsilon^{2m}+O\left(\varepsilon^{L}\right).

If
\delta>0

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}(-q^{-1};q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}\right)<

7.
Final Remarks

In this paper, we investigate the
t
-hook inequalities between two equinuermous sets arising from the first Rogers–Ramanujan identity and the first little Göllnitz identity. This analysis can be extended to the second Rogers–Ramanujan identity and the second little Göllnitz identity. The differences between the first and second identities are the following. For part difference conditions, parts are all greater than
1
, while for part congruence conditions, parts are congruent to
2,3
modulo
5
for the second Rogers–Ramanujan, and congruent to
2,3,7
modulo 8 for the second little Göllnitz. Another example with gap
2
conditions is the Göllnitz–Gordon identities
[
12
,
13
]
. The first Göllnitz–Gordon identity states that the number of partitions of
n
with parts differing by
2
and no even parts differing by exactly
2
is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into parts congruent to
1,4
or
7
modulo
8
. As the corresponding generating functions for these partition sets with the gap conditions are likely Nahm sums, we hope that the asymptotic method used in this paper will guide the further analysis of such generating functions. For example, the number of
1
-hooks in partitions with the gap 2 condition from the second Rogers–Ramanujan identity is generated by
\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{nq^{n^{2}+n}}{(q;q)_{n}}
, to which our method can be applied.

One of the key players in our proofs was the generating function for the number of
t
-hooks. For large
t
, it seems not easy to find the generating function. Based on numerical experiments for small
t
and
n
, we have observed that for sufficiently large
n
