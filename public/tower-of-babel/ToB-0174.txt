INTRODUCTION

We are glad to continue publication of the Agyptian Coffin Texts after the deplorable break 
caused by the war. It is hoped that future volumes may be issued in due course.

In Volume I the history of the project, the basis on which the texts are arranged, and the 
conventions adopted were explained. For the user’s convenience certain information of the 
last type is repeated here in Volume II], as it was in Volume IT.

Figures with an asterisk (1*, 2*, ete.) in the blank column to the right of any text eolumn 
refer to the notes. These have, as far as possible, been given on the same page.

Dashes beside signs (:) mean that these signs were not seen by Dr. Gardiner or myself, but 
rest on some other authority —publication or manuscript copy. In the vast majority of cases 
the authority in question is the manuscript of M. Lacau. As most former copies or publica- 
tions do not preserve the arrangement of the originals, my arrangement of the signs in these 
parts is wholly arbitrary.

The symbol | is employed to indicate that the signs or sentences separated thereby follow 
one another in the original without any break.The symbol | is employed to indicate that the signs or sentences separated thereby follow 
one another in the original without any break.

Sometimes a simple line | will be found. Such a line has no other intention than to guide the 
eye, for example, if a text starts near the bottom of a page, thus being far away from its 
designation in the heading of the page. If such a line occurs between words or groups of words, 
these do not follow one another immediately in the original.

The notes are very brief and have been confined to purely textual matters —corrections, 
erasve@es, and the like. Also, in the case of a complicated arrangement or disorder in the manu- 
script, they help the reader to visualize the original.

The letters on the left-hand side of the page or of each group of parallel texts are meant to 
facilitate the quoting of the texts, which should in the editors’ opinion be quoted by volume, 
page, and letter— for example, I 2a, II 115 ¢, ete.

Interrelationships of the Coffin Texts of Volumes I-III with mortuary texts published else- 
where or belonging to other bodies of material have been summarized this time in one table. 
In another all the plans showing details of position and order of lines in various manuscripts 
have been indexed. A short list of additions and corrections for Volumes I-II has also been

inserted.
