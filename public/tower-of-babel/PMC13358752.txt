Optimal drug regimens for squamous non-small cell lung cancer: a systematic review and a Bayesian network meta-analysis of randomized trials
Authors: Yan Ye, Yaqian Li, Nuoyu Yan, Lan Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCurrent treatment options for patients with squamous non-small cell lung cancer (sqNSCLC) include immune checkpoint inhibitors combined with chemotherapy, monotherapy with immune therapy, and platinum-based doublet chemotherapy, among others. However, there is still a lack of systematic comparisons regarding the relative efficacy and safety of these regimens. This study aims to comprehensively assess the clinical benefits [such as overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS)] and adverse event rates across different treatment strategies via a network meta-analysis, providing evidence-based guidance for individualized treatment decisions in sqNSCLC patients.MethodsWe searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science from inception to May 3, 2025 for eligible randomized controlled trials (RCTs). The primary outcome measures were PFS and OS; secondary outcomes included objective response rate (ORR) and grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (≥3 TRAEs). A network meta-analysis was carried out using Stata 16.0 and R 4.4.0.ResultsA total of 32 RCTs, involving 9,652 participants, were included. This study analyzed 18 different treatment strategies. The network meta-analysis showed that for PFS, paclitaxel-based doublet chemotherapy combined with programmed cell death-1 and programmed death-ligand 1 [PD-(L)1] checkpoint inhibitors [surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA): 96.52%] and gemcitabine-based doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (SUCRA: 85.16%) were the most effective. For OS, paclitaxel-based doublet chemotherapy combined with a Toll-like receptor-2 agonist (CADI-05) (SUCRA: 92.11%), paclitaxel-based doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) checkpoint inhibitors (SUCRA: 89.65%), and paclitaxel-based doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (SUCRA: 83.58%) were the top three choices. The combination of paclitaxel-based doublet chemotherapy with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (SUCRA: 87.00%) was associated with the best improvement in ORR.ConclusionsFor first-line treatment of sqNSCLC, the current evidence supports the use of paclitaxel-based doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors as one of the preferred treatment options. However, more high-quality trials are needed to confirm these findings.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Paclitaxel-platinum chemotherapy plus programmed cell death-1 and programmed death-ligand 1 [PD-(L)1] blockade ranked best for progression-free survival (PFS) [surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA): 96.52%] and objective response rate (SUCRA: 87.00%).

• For overall survival, paclitaxel-platinum plus a Toll-like receptor 2 agonist (CADI-05) ranked highest (SUCRA: 92.11%), followed by dual immune checkpoint inhibition [PD-(L)1 + cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4)] plus paclitaxel-platinum (SUCRA: 89.65%).

What is known and what is new?

• Immune checkpoint inhibitors combined with chemotherapy or alone are standard first-line treatments for squamous non-small cell lung cancer (sqNSCLC). However, systematic comparisons across multiple regimens, including targeted therapies and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), have been limited.

• This is the first network meta-analysis simultaneously comparing 18 treatment strategies, including paclitaxel- and gemcitabine-based doublets, single-agent immunotherapy, dual checkpoint inhibition, ADCs, and other targeted agents. It provides novel rankings for efficacy and safety, highlighting the superior performance of paclitaxel-platinum plus PD-(L)1 blockade for PFS and response, and identifying promising regimens such as CADI-05 combinations.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Paclitaxel-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 inhibitors should be considered a preferred first-line option for sqNSCLC based on current evidence.

• Regimens like CADI-05 warrant further high-quality trials before clinical adoption.

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of all primary lung cancers, with squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) comprising 20% to 30% of NSCLC cases. In contrast to adenocarcinoma, SCC exhibits rarely exhibits driver gene mutations, such as EGFR and ALK, but shows a higher frequency of programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) overexpression, which is strongly correlated with tobacco exposure (1). These unique biological characteristics limit the application of targeted therapies in SCC, making traditional platinum-based doublet chemotherapy the long-standing standard for first-line treatment (2).

In recent years, the emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has revolutionized the treatment landscape. Whether as a monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy, ICIs are now recommended as first-line standard treatment for squamous NSCLC (sqNSCLC) patients (3,4). Programmed cell death-1 and programmed death-ligand 1 [PD-(L)1] checkpoint inhibitors have greatly improved survival in sqNSCLC patients, particularly those with high PD-(L)1 expression (5). Additionally, the combination of immunotherapy and chemotherapy has been shown to be more effective than chemotherapy alone in first-line treatment for advanced or metastatic sqNSCLC patients (3,4,6-10). With the successful completion of the KEYNOTE-407 trial, pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy was approved as the first anti-PD-1 regimen for treatment-naïve advanced sqNSCLC patients, regardless of PD-(L)1 expression levels (11). However, not all patients are suitable for ICIs, and histology-directed chemotherapy continues to play an important role in the treatment of sqNSCLC. Currently, first-line chemotherapy regimens for sqNSCLC include cisplatin or carboplatin combined with cytotoxic agents such as gemcitabine, paclitaxel, and docetaxel. However, the median overall survival (OS) is typically less than 12 months, and patients often face significant adverse events during treatment, such as bone marrow suppression and gastrointestinal symptoms (12-14).

Beyond the treatment options mentioned, there has been growing interest in exploring additional therapies to improve survival outcomes in sqNSCLC. Notably, combinations of chemotherapy with other agents are gaining attention, such as the second-generation recombinant human IgG1 EGFR antibody necitumumab, the human IgG1 monoclonal antibody targeting VEGFR-2 extracellular domain, ramucirumab, and the small molecule VEGFR inhibitor motesanib (15-18).

Despite the diversity of treatment strategies available for sqNSCLC, there are limited systematic comparisons of the relative efficacy and safety of different regimens. Existing network meta-analyses (NMAs) have mainly focused on comparing regimens with PD-(L)1 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy or specific patient populations (e.g., those with PD-L1 expression ≥50%) (19,20). In this study, we conducted the first NMA that simultaneously included multiple regimens, including immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy, single-agent immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), aiming to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of efficacy and offer evidence-based guidance for personalized treatment decisions in sqNSCLC patients, in order to inform clinical practice. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA-NMA reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0152/rc).

Methods

The study protocol has been registered on the international prospective systematic review registry platform, Prospero (CRD420251103523). Since only publicly available published data were analyzed, patient consent and ethical approval from committees were not required.

Literature search

The search strategy combined subject headings and free-text terms and was conducted in four electronic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and Embase. The search period spanned from the inception of each database to May 3, 2025. Additionally, to include comprehensive and updated results, reference lists of relevant articles were reviewed. To ensure the completeness and reliability of the evidence base for the systematic review, conference abstracts, clinical trial registrations, and similar documents were excluded. The detailed search strategy is available in Appendix 1.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) participants: adults with histologically confirmed stage III–IV or recurrent sqNSCLC, irrespective of prior systemic therapy; (II) interventions and comparators: chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or targeted agents alone or in combination, with the control arm receiving chemotherapy alone or another active therapy; (III) study design: randomized controlled trial (RCT); (IV) outcomes: at least one of the following reported—progression‑free survival (PFS) (time from randomization to first progression or death), OS (time from randomization to death from any cause), objective response rate (ORR) (proportion of patients achieving tumor response), or incidence of grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (≥3 TRAEs).

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) the investigational drug has been discontinued and is not available in any country; (II) animal or cell experiments, case reports, scientific experiment plans, reviews, letters, editorials, conference papers, etc.; (II) literature with missing or seriously erroneous research data; (IV) duplicate publications; (V) studies with no full-text available.

Two reviewers (Y.Y. and L.S.) independently assessed the titles and abstracts according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria and retrieved the full-text articles to screen for eligibility. Disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer (Y.L.).

Literature screening and data extraction

Two reviewers (L.S. and Y.Y.) conducted the literature search strictly following the inclusion and exclusion criteria. All references were managed using Endnote X9 software. After excluding duplicate publications, studies that were preliminarily eligible based on titles or abstracts were retrieved for full-text screening. Full-text articles were then assessed for inclusion in the systematic review. Basic information from each study was extracted and cross-verified, and consistent units of measurement were applied. In cases of disagreement, decisions were made through discussion with a third reviewer (Y.L.). Data extracted from the studies included: (I) basic information: first author, publication year, country; (II) study characteristics: cancer stage, histological characteristics, sample size, treatment lines, interventions, and control measures; (III) outcome measures: reported clinical outcomes.

Quality assessment of included studies

Two reviewers independently assessed the literature quality. Disagreements were resolved by a third reviewer, and the final decision was reached through discussion. The Cochrane risk-of-bias tool (RoB2) (21) was used to evaluate the quality of the included studies. The evaluation focused on five aspects: bias arising from randomization, bias due to deviations from intended interventions, bias from missing outcome data, bias in outcome measurement, and bias in the selection of reported results. These aspects were classified into three categories: low risk, unclear, and high risk, with the results presented in a risk-of-bias graph (Figure S1).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was carried out using R version 4.4.0. Hazard ratios (HRs) were used for survival endpoints, and relative risks (RRs) for binary endpoints, each reported with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A difference was deemed statistically significant when the 95% CI excluded 1. Heterogeneity was interpreted using I2 values of 25%, 50%, and 75% as thresholds for low, moderate, and high heterogeneity, respectively (22). A Bayesian NMA was undertaken via the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method (23,24). Four Markov chains were run, with a thinning interval set to 1. The first 20,000 iterations served as burn-in, followed by 50,000 iterations to achieve convergence. Model fit and global consistency were compared using the Deviance Information Criterion (DIC); if the absolute DIC difference between consistency and inconsistency models was less than 3, the consistency model was adopted. Local consistency was assessed with the nodesplitting method for closed loops within the network (25).

A random-effects model was applied to evaluate all treatment strategies. Outcome measures were presented as network plots, cumulative probability ranking plots, and league tables (26). The surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) quantified the ranking probability, where larger SUCRA values indicated better intervention performance (27). Network graphs illustrating both direct and indirect comparisons were generated using Stata 16.0. All analyses were conducted with Stata 16.0 and R 4.4.0. Publication bias was evaluated via funnel plots, and the significance threshold was set at P<0.05.

Results

Literature search process

A total of 7,889 articles were initially retrieved. After removing 1,531 duplicates, 6,128 articles were excluded based on preliminary screening of titles and abstracts. The remaining 230 articles underwent full-text review, and strict inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied, resulting in the inclusion of 32 studies. The specific screening process is shown in Figure 1 (3,4,6-9,28-45). A total of 9,652 patients were included in the analysis, receiving 18 different treatment regimens, including: taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy (Taxane-Pt); taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with veliparib (Taxane-Pt-Veliparib); taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with the vascular disrupting agent vadimezan (Taxane-Pt-Vadimezan); taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with the a Toll-like receptor-2 agonist (CADI-05) (Taxane-Pt-CADI-05); taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with motesanib (Taxane-Pt-Motesanib); taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with necitumumab (Taxane-Pt-Necitumumab); taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (Taxane-Pt-PD1orPDL1); taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with both PD-(L)1 and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte‑associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) checkpoint inhibitors (Taxane-Pt-PD1orPDL1-CTLA-4); single-agent taxane chemotherapy (only including docetaxel); taxane monotherapy combined with ramucirumab (Taxane-Ramucirumab); gemcitabine-platinum chemotherapy (Gemcitabine-Pt); gemcitabine-platinum combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (Gemcitabine-Pt-PD1orPDL1); gemcitabine-platinum combined with necitumumab (Gemcitabine-Pt-Necitumumab); gemcitabine combined with taxanes (Gemcitabine-Taxane); single-agent PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors; PD-(L)1 combined with CTLA-4 dual checkpoint inhibitors; vinorelbine-platinum (Vinorelbine-Pt); and ADCs. Quality assessment

The results of the risk of bias assessment for the 32 included studies are shown in Figure S1. All 32 studies were RCTs. Of these, 10 studies were of high quality, with low risk of bias in random sequence generation and blinding. Additionally, allocation concealment, completeness of outcome data, selective reporting, and other biases were controlled for at low risk. Four studies did not report allocation concealment, and eight studies were open-label, making blinding infeasible. The remaining 10 studies were both open-label and did not report allocation concealment, thus were considered to have a high risk of bias.

Pairwise meta-analysis of included trials

Through head-to-head meta-analysis, we evaluated the efficacy of various treatment regimens compared to taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy in patients with sqNSCLC, with OS and PFS as outcome measures (Figure 2). Taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors significantly improved PFS (HR =0.47, 95% CI: 0.39, 0.57) and OS (HR =0.72, 95% CI: 0.62, 0.84). Additionally, the combination of taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy with PD-(L)1 and CTLA-4 checkpoint inhibitors significantly improved OS (HR =0.63, 95% CI: 0.42, 0.95).

PFS

A total of 25 studies reported the PFS outcomes for 15 different interventions (3,4,6,8-10,12-18,30,31,33,35,39-45). The network structure for different interventions is shown in Figure 3A. The NMA results indicated that most treatment regimens performed better than ADCs in improving PFS. Specifically, gemcitabine combined with platinum and Necitumumab (HR =0.42, 95% CI: 0.21, 0.82) and gemcitabine combined with platinum and PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.32, 95% CI: 0.15, 0.68) showed superior efficacy in improving OS when compared to gemcitabine combined with taxane. Furthermore, gemcitabine combined with platinum chemotherapy (HR =0.42, 95% CI: 0.21, 0.82), gemcitabine combined with platinum and PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.25, 95% CI: 0.11, 0.59), gemcitabine combined with platinum and necitumumab (HR =0.33, 95% CI: 0.15, 0.73), PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.56, 95% CI: 0.39, 0.83), and PD-(L)1 combined with CTLA-4 dual checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.45, 95% CI: 0.23, 0.87) were all more effective than taxane monotherapy. The combination of taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors outperformed taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with veliparib (HR =0.58, 95% CI: 0.37, 0.94). Additionally, gemcitabine combined with platinum and PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.33, 95% CI: 0.12, 0.90), taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.26, 95% CI: 0.12, 0.57), and taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy with PD-(L)1 and CTLA-4 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.36, 95% CI: 0.14, 0.92) all performed better than taxane monotherapy combined with ramucirumab. No significant differences were found in other pairwise comparisons (Figures 4,5).

Based on cumulative probability results, taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors had the highest SUCRA value of 96.52%, gemcitabine combined with platinum and PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (SUCRA: 85.16%), and taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 and CTLA-4 checkpoint inhibitors (SUCRA: 80.48%) are likely to be the top three interventions for improving PFS (Figure S2). The overall heterogeneity was low (I2=3%), indicating no variability between the included studies and supporting a high level of consistency in the NMA results.

OS

A total of 28 studies reported OS outcomes for 16 different interventions (3,4,6-10,12-18,28-36,39,41), with the remaining studies described in further references (43-45). The network structure for these interventions is shown in Figure 3B. The NMA results revealed that gemcitabine combined with platinum and Necitumumab significantly improved OS compared to gemcitabine combined with taxane (HR =0.54, 95% CI: 0.32, 0.88), gemcitabine combined with platinum (HR =0.59, 95% CI: 0.35, 0.99), and other regimens such as gemcitabine combined with platinum and necitumumab (HR =0.46, 95% CI: 0.25, 0.82), PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.67, 95% CI: 0.55, 0.83), and PD-(L)1 combined with CTLA-4 dual checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.59, 95% CI: 0.37, 0.93) when compared to taxane monotherapy. Additionally, taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with CADI-05 (HR =0.32, 95% CI: 0.14, 0.75), taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.43, 95% CI: 0.24, 0.77), taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 and CTLA-4 checkpoint inhibitors (HR =0.37, 95% CI: 0.19, 0.75), and taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with veliparib (HR =0.49, 95% CI: 0.26, 0.92) were all more effective than taxane monotherapy combined with ramucirumab. No significant differences were found in other pairwise comparisons (Figures 6,7).

Subgroup analysis

Subgroup NMA was conducted by treatment line and study design (Figures S6,S7). The results showed that taxane-platinum combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors demonstrated superior efficacy in both PFS and OS for first-line treatment, consistent with the main analysis. Furthermore, in both PFS and OS, the efficacy of taxane-platinum combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors in double-blind trials was superior to that in open-label trials (HR for PFS: 0.44 vs. 0.54; HR for OS: 0.68 vs. 0.81). This suggests that potential bias in open-label studies may have led to an underestimation of the efficacy of immunotherapy.

Publication bias

To assess publication bias for the included studies, funnel plots were used for visual evaluation (Figure S8). The results indicated that the funnel plot was approximately symmetrical, suggesting that publication bias is unlikely to have significantly affected the results of this study.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first NMA comparing the efficacy and safety of various treatment regimens for SqNSCLC patients. The NMA analyzed the most recent data from 32 eligible RCTs. The results of our study suggest that taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors is the most effective regimen for improving PFS and ORR, while taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with CADI-05 is the most effective for improving OS. Furthermore, PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors not only showed superior safety but also significantly reduced the incidence of grade ≥3 TRAEs when compared to taxane monotherapy. Overall, our findings demonstrate that combined chemotherapy and immunotherapy regimens exhibit superior efficacy compared to chemotherapy alone, which aligns with the results of a previous NMA (19). Among these regimens, the taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy regimen combined with immunotherapy shows significant potential when compared to other chemotherapy-only regimens.

CADI-05 is an effective Toll-like receptor-2 (TLR-2) agonist that induces a cell-mediated immune response targeting the expression of desmocollin-3 (DSC3) on cancer cells. The innovative combination of taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy with CADI-05 shows preliminary potential in improving OS, which may be related to the specific expression of DSC3 in SqNSCLC (29,46). However, only one RCT in this NMA included the CADI-05 regimen, involving 221 patients (109 in the chemo-immunotherapy group and 112 in the chemotherapy-only group), warranting further clinical trials to validate these findings.

Our NMA included several nodes supported by only a single or very few trials, particularly CADI-05, necitumumab, and certain veliparib combinations. Such sparse data can lead to network imbalance and imprecision, which may impair the validity of comparisons between nodes. For example, Necitumumab-based regimens were primarily investigated before the widespread adoption of ICIs. Consequently, these high SUCRA values should not be directly interpreted as superiority over contemporary immunotherapy-containing regimens in the absence of head-to-head comparisons. Clinicians and researchers should view these findings as hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing at present.

Both PD-(L)1 and CTLA-4 inhibitors can improve survival benefits through distinct but complementary mechanisms, yet few studies have directly compared dual immune checkpoint inhibition combined with chemotherapy versus single immune checkpoint inhibition combined with chemotherapy. Our NMA findings show that single checkpoint inhibition plus chemotherapy was superior to dual checkpoint inhibition plus chemotherapy in improving PFS and ORR, while the opposite was true for OS. This deviates slightly from the results of the POSEIDON trial, which randomized 1,013 patients (1:1:1) to the CTLA-4 + PD-L1 + chemotherapy group, the PD-L1 + chemotherapy group, and the chemotherapy-only group. The study showed that the PD-L1 + chemotherapy group had a slight advantage over the CTLA-4 + PD-L1 + chemotherapy group in improving PFS (median PFS: 4.7 vs. 4.6 months) and OS (median OS: 11.5 vs. 10.4 months), with a better long-term OS benefit (5-year OS rate: 7.6% vs. 7.3%) (47,48). This discrepancy may be related to the small number of RCTs included in our NMA involving dual immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy (only one study).

We included two ADCs, datopotamab deruxtecan and sacituzumab govitecan, as treatments for later-line therapy in randomized RCTs. The results indicated that ADCs were among the least effective treatment options in all regimens. Notably, both trials had docetaxel as the control group. Datopotamab deruxtecan did not improve PFS (mPFS 2.8 vs. 3.9 months) or OS (mOS 7.6 vs. 9.4 months) compared to docetaxel, while sacituzumab govitecan showed improvements in PFS [HR 0.83 (95% CI: 0.56–1.22)] and OS [HR 0.83 (95% CI: 0.56–1.22)], but these differences were not statistically significant (43,45). Based on these two RCTs and the results of our NMA, we conclude that datopotamab deruxtecan is not recommended for later-line treatment of sqNSCLC, and sacituzumab govitecan should not be prioritized. This conclusion aligns with the ASCO Living Guideline (49).

Although our study suggests that taxane-platinum doublet chemotherapy combined with PD-(L)1 checkpoint inhibitors may carry a higher toxicity burden (SUCRA: 23.51%), this finding is limited by insufficient reporting in the original studies. In clinical practice, the core value of this regimen should not be dismissed based solely on this ranking. Notably, the overall safety profile of this combination has been well-established across multiple pivotal RCTs (4,6,8,10). The CameL-Sq trial (6) demonstrated that adding camrelizumab to chemotherapy did not significantly increase chemotherapy-related adverse events compared to chemotherapy alone, with no unexpected immune-related adverse events observed in either arm. Similarly, Wang et al. randomized 360 sqNSCLC patients to: Arm A (tislelizumab + paclitaxel/carboplatin, n=120), Arm B (tislelizumab + nab-paclitaxel/carboplatin, n=119), or Arm C (paclitaxel/carboplatin, n=121). Final analysis revealed comparable rates of grade ≥3 TEAEs across groups (89.2% vs. 87.3% vs. 84.6%, respectively) (8). However, the IMpower131 trial reported higher rates of serious TRAEs and treatment discontinuations in the atezolizumab-chemotherapy arm compared to chemotherapy alone. Investigators attributed this to known risks of atezolizumab, including febrile neutropenia, anemia, and pneumonitis. This observation underscores that the toxicity profile of the combination is well-characterized rather than representing unknown or unmanageable risks.

Collectively, these key trials indicate that while combination therapy introduces immune-related AEs and may potentiate some chemotherapy toxicities, its safety spectrum remains clearly defined. With proper management protocols, these risks remain overall controllable in clinical practice.

Limitations

However, there are several limitations in our study. Firstly, our NMA did not distinguish between different types of platinum agents, taxanes, or anti-PD-(L)1 drugs. There may be variations in efficacy among different platinum compounds (e.g., carboplatin, cisplatin), taxanes (e.g., paclitaxel, nab-paclitaxel, docetaxel), and anti-PD-(L)1 drugs (e.g., atezolizumab, durvalumab, cemiplimab). Secondly, since most of the trials included in our analysis were open-label, the lack of blinding may affect the credibility of the results. Thirdly, in our analysis, regimens such as ADCs and taxane plus ramucirumab were evaluated primarily in later-line settings. By contrast, most immunotherapy-containing regimens were studied in the first-line setting. Therefore, the relatively inferior performance of ADC and ramucirumab combinations observed in this NMA should not be extrapolated to first-line treatment decisions. Our subgroup analysis by line of therapy partly addresses this issue, but residual confounding by line of therapy remains a key limitation. Finally, the studies included in our analysis spanned from 2011 to 2025, and these temporal trends may influence outcomes independently of the treatment regimens. Therefore, an exploratory sensitivity analysis excluding studies published before 2018 (i.e., the pre-immunotherapy era) yielded consistent directions of efficacy for most modern regimens (see Figure S9). However, the early studies of CADI-05 and necitumumab remained susceptible to this bias. Future studies should perform detailed subgroup analyses based on different platinum agents, taxanes, and immune drugs, and adopt blinded designs.

Conclusions

In summary, our analysis found that the triple combination of taxane-platinum chemotherapy with PD-(L)1 inhibitors provides substantial survival benefits in the first-line treatment of sqNSCLC, solidifying its position as a preferred therapeutic option. While safety analyses indicate a discernible toxicity profile, the compelling efficacy advantage remains clinically decisive under existing evidentiary standards. The key to successful implementation lies in integrating standardized toxicity prevention, monitoring, and management strategies. Future investigation through rigorously designed, multicenter, large-sample, long-term follow-up RCTs will further validate the efficacy and safety of this combination approach.
