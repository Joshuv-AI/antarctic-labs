Global well-posedness for the compressible Navier–Stokes equations with vacuum and smallness on coefficient-coupled scaling invariant quantity Thanks: This research was partially supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China (Nos. 12301287 and 12371227).
Hao Xu, Xin Zhong

Abstract
We investigate the Cauchy problem of three-dimensional compressible Navier–Stokes equations with far-field vacuum. Based on delicate energy estimates and structures of the systems under consideration, we show the global well-posedness and decay rates of strong solutions provided that some coefficient-coupled scaling invariant initial quantity is suitably small. In particular, our smallness conditions are independent of any initial data and known parameters in the systems . Moreover, there is no need to require the compatibility conditions on the initial data. Our results improve previous works.



Key words and phrases
. Compressible Navier–Stokes equations; global strong solutions; decay rates; vacuum.

Introduction

The non-isentropic compressible Navier–Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are written as follows:

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\divv(\rho u)=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+\divv(\rho u\otimes u)=\divv\mathbb{T},\\
(\rho E)_{t}+\divv(\rho Eu)=\divv(\mathbb{T}u)+\kappa\Delta\theta,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the unknown functions
\rho
,
u=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})^{\top}
, and
\theta
are the fluid density, velocity, and temperature, respectively.
The total energy
E
is given by

E\triangleq e+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2},\ \ e=\frac{{\rm R}}{\gamma-1}\theta\triangleq{\rm c_{V}}\theta,

where
e
is the specific internal energy and the positive parameters
\gamma>1,{\rm R},\kappa
, and
{\rm c_{V}}
are the adiabatic exponent, perfect gas constant, heat-conductivity coefficient, and specific heat at constant volume, respectively.
The Cauchy stress
\mathbb{T}
is determined through the Newton rheological law

\mathbb{T}\triangleq\mu\big(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top}\big)+\lambda\divv u\mathbb{I}_{3}-P(\rho,e)\mathbb{I}_{3},

where
\mathbb{I}_{3}
is a
3\times 3
unit matrix,
\mu
and
\lambda
are the viscosity coefficients satisfying

\mu>0,\ \ 3\lambda+2\mu\geq 0.

The state equation of the pressure
P=P(\rho,e)
is of ideal polytropic gas type

P(\rho,e)=(\gamma-1)\rho e\triangleq{\rm R}\rho\theta.

For isentropic fluids, the compressible Navier–Stokes equations become

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\divv(\rho u)=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+\divv(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P=\mu\Delta u+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\divv u.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Here
P
satisfies the equation of state of an ideal fluid

P=a\rho^{\gamma},\ \ a>0,\ \ \gamma>1.

The compressible Navier–Stokes system is one of the basic physical models arising in fluid mechanics. The mathematical theory of such a model is well developed in the last decades due to its physical importance and mathematical challenge.
There are so many known results concerning the global existence of solutions to
(
1.1
) and (
1.2
). In the absence of vacuum (i.e.,
\rho=0
),
please refer to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
27
,
8
,
9
,

]
and references contained therein.

However, as pointed out by many mathematicians (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
16
,
28
,
29
]
), the mathematical analysis of compressible fluid near vacuum regions presents significant challenges due to the inherent singularity and degeneracy of the governing equations. We briefly review several results concerning the global existence of solutions to the isentropic compressible Navier–Stokes system (
1.2
) with vacuum. In the pioneering work
[
26
]
, P.-L. Lions established the global existence of large weak solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\gamma\geq\frac{9}{5}
via renormalized techniques and the effective viscous flux arguments. Subsequently, the restriction on the adiabatic index was extended in
[
7
,
17
,
18
]
. Yet the question of global regularity and uniqueness for such weak solutions remains completely open. There are also some results regarding the global well-posedness of strong (or classical) solutions. Huang–Li–Xin
[
16
]
proved the global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth initial data which are of small total energy but possibly large oscillations, where the far-field density could be either vacuum or non-vacuum. In
[
11
]
, Hong et al. showed that the initial total energy could be large as long as the adiabatic exponent is sufficiently close to one. Based on some key
a priori
decay with rates (in large time) and a spatially weighted energy method, Li and Xin
[
22
]
dealt with the two-dimensional (2D for short) Cauchy problem with far-field vacuum under the condition that the initial total energy is suitably small. It should be mentioned that a central aspect of their studies in
[
16
,
11
,
22
]
is the derivation of the time-independent upper bound of the density. In fact, this coincides with the outcomes of the previous blow-up criterion
[
15
]
. A significant progress was recently
made in
[
5
]
, where a unique global solution with vacuum in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
was derived under a suitable scaling invariant smallness condition on the velocity. Motivated by
[
20
]
, where Lei and Xin discovered a scaling-invariant property of solutions under the scaling transformation

\rho^{k}(x,t)=k^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\rho\Big(k^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}}x,kt\Big),\ \ u^{k}(x,t)=k^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}}u\Big(k^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}}x,kt\Big),

Wen
[
30
]
demonstrated the global existence of strong solutions to (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
provided that the scaling-invariant initial quantity

\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}\left(\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{1}}\right)\left(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left[1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3+\gamma}\left(\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{1}}\right)^{2}\right]

(1.3)

is properly small.

Let’s turn our attention to the non-isentropic compressible Navier–Stokes equations (
1.1
). As a system, (
1.1
) contains more complicated structures than the isentropic model. Indeed, it cannot be reduced to (
1.2
) even with zero temperature. Their distinctive features make analytical studies a great challenge but offer new opportunities. In 2004, Feireisl
[
6
]
generalized the results of P.-L. Lions
[
26
]
to the case of compressible viscous heat-conductive fluids, and proved the global existence of
variational weak solutions
for arbitrary data in
N
-dimensional domains. As for strong solutions, it was shown in
[
13
,
31
]
the global existence and uniqueness of strong solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with far-field non-vacuum and vacuum respectively which are of small energy but possibly large oscillations. It is worth noting that the basic energy inequality does not provide any dissipation estimates in the non-isentropic case, while some types of dissipation estimates were recovered in
[
13
,
31
]
with the aid of the entropy inequality and the conservation of mass respectively. By adopting the piecewise-estimate
method, Liang
[
25
]
established the global existence and decay rates of a unique strong solution with far-field vacuum when the initial energy is small. Under less regular data and weaker compatibility condition, Lai–Xu–Zhang
[
19
]
proved the global well-posedness and the exponential decay rates of classical solutions via using time-weighted estimates to avoid the presence of temperature initial-layer. Furthermore, Li
[
23
]
obtained the global existence of strong solutions to the 3D Cauchy problem under suitable smallness assumption on the initial data and the additional technical condition
2\mu>\lambda
. A peculiar aspect of his result is that the smallness assumption is imposed on a quantity that is scaling invariant with respect to the transformation

\displaystyle(\rho_{0\eta}(x),u_{0\eta}(x),\theta_{0\eta}(x))=(\rho_{0}(\eta x),\eta u_{0}(\eta x),\eta^{2}\theta_{0}(\eta x)),\ \eta\neq 0.

More recently, a new scaling-invariant initial quantity

\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{3}}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

(1.4)

was proposed in
[
30
]
, where the author removed the restriction
2\mu>\lambda
in
[
23
]
and established the global existence and uniqueness of strong solutions.

It should be emphasized that all results concerning strong solutions mentioned above require some smallness condition depending on the initial data and/or known parameters in the systems. However, from the viewpoint of partial differential equations (PDE), there are advantages of a parameter- and initial-data-independent smallness assumption in the study of global solutions to PDE. For example, such an assumption facilitates perturbation analysis and dynamic bifurcation research. On the other hand, for practical problems, the parameter- and initial-data-free smallness condition facilitates direct use of measured data and realistic physical constants in numerical simulations of PDE. Thus, this motivates us to investigate global strong solutions to the systems (
1.2
) and (
1.1
) with vacuum under some smallness conditions independent of initial data and model parameters.

2.2
The isentropic case

System (
1.2
) is supplemented with the following initial data and the far-field behavior

\displaystyle(\rho,\rho u)(x,0)=(\rho_{0},m_{0})(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\rho,u)\to(0,0)\ \ {\rm as}\ \ |x|\to\infty,\ \ {\rm for}\ \ t\geq 0.

(2.2)

Our main result for the isentropic compressible Navier–Stokes equations reads as follows:

Theorem 2.1
.

For any given
q\in(3,6)
, assume that

0\leq\rho_{0}\in L^{\gamma}\cap H^{1}\cap W^{1,q},\ \ u_{0}\in D^{1}.

(2.3)

There exists a positive constant
\varepsilon
independent of
\mu,\lambda,a,\gamma
, and the initial data such that if

\mathbb{E}_{0}\triangleq\mu^{-5}\alpha^{6}\bar{\rho}^{3}\mathbb{E}_{1}(0)\mathbb{E}_{2}(0)\leq\varepsilon,

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\alpha\triangleq\max\{1+a,\gamma\},\ \ \bar{\rho}\triangleq 1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}(0)\triangleq\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}+\frac{a}{\gamma-1}\rho_{0}^{\gamma}\right)\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{2}(0)\triangleq\int\big[\mu|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)(\divv u_{0})^{2}+\left(2\mu+\lambda\right)^{-1}P_{0}^{2}\big]\mathrm{d}x,

then the problem (
1.2
), (
2.1
), and (
2.2
) have a unique global strong solution
(\rho,u)
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty)
satisfying

0\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\frac{3}{2}\bar{\rho},\ \ {for\ all}\ \ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty)

(2.5)

and

\begin{cases}\rho\in C([0,\infty);L^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{\gamma}\cap H^{1}\cap W^{1,q}),\ \ \rho_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}\cap L^{q}),\\[2.84526pt]
\sqrt{\rho}u\in C([0,\infty);L^{2}),\ \ u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;D^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;D^{1}),\\[2.84526pt]
\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}\in L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}),\ \ t\dot{u}\in L^{2}(0,\infty;D^{1}),\ \ \sqrt{t\rho}\dot{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}).\end{cases}

(2.6)

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
depending on transport coefficients and initial norms such that, for any
t\geq 1
,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ct^{-1+\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \|P\|_{L^{r}}\leq Ct^{-1+\frac{1}{r}},\ \ \|(\nabla F,\nabla\omega)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ct^{-1}.

It should be noted that our smallness condition (

1.2
) and initial data, which is in sharp contrast to
[

11

30

where they established global strong solutions under some smallness condition depending on the initial data and/or parameters. Moreover, there is no need to require the compatibility conditions on the initial data.

Remark 2.2
.

It is worth mentioning that Wen
[
30
]
established the global well-posedness of strong solutions to the problem (
1.2
), (
2.1
), and (
2.2

We mainly use global

. It should be pointed out that the approaches in
[

Inspired by

In this process, the main difficulties stem from nonlinear and strongly coupled terms.
To overcome these obstacles, we introduce a new quantity

and establish coefficient- and initial-data-independent global

The remaining challenge is to close the bootstrap argument. Compared with

defined in (

\left[1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3+\gamma}\left(\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{1}}\right)^{2}\right].

The novelty of our analysis is that the term

\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho^{\gamma}\|_{L^{6}}{\rm d}\tau

can be bounded by
\mathbb{E}_{1}(0)^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathbb{E}_{2}(0)^{\frac{1}{3}}
and
\frac{a\bar{\rho}^{\gamma}}{2\mu+\lambda}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)

\mathbb{E}_{1}(0)^{\frac{3}{4}}\mathbb{E}_{2}(0)^{\frac{1}{4}}
and
\frac{a\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\gamma}}{2\mu+\lambda}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)

[

This improved bound enables us to close the
a priori
assumption of the density provided
\mathbb{E}_{0}
is small enough.

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{1}(0)\triangleq{\rm c_{V}}\|\rho_{0}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{2}(0)\triangleq\mu\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)\|\divv u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Psi{\rm c_{V}}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\ \Psi\triangleq\mu^{-1}\Theta^{3}\left(1+\kappa\right)\bar{\rho},

Remark 2.4

Compared with
[

) provided that either the shear viscosity coefficient

It should be pointed that the methodologies developed in

\nabla u

On the other hand, it is rather challenging to close the bootstrap argument.

Specifically, to construct a complete scaling-invariant quantity for the

\Theta\|\divv u\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\mathbb{F}_{3}(t)

\mathbb{F}_{1}(0)
and
\mathbb{F}_{2}(0)
, respectively. Finally, by virtue of a novel piecewise estimation strategy for solutions, we successfully obtain the expected results.
