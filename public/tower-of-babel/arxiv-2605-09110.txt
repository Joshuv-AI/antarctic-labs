Non- R R -trivial proper projective similitudes in type A 3 ≡ D 3 A_{3}\equiv D_{3}
M. Archita, Karim Johannes Becher

Abstract
. Over an arbitrary field of characteristic different from 2 2 admitting an anisotropic torsion 3 3 -fold Pfister form, we apply a construction due to Merkurjev to produce an algebra with orthogonal involution of degree 6 6 which admits proper projective similitudes that are not R R -trivial. In particular, such examples exist over every finitely generated transcendental extension of a local or global number field, as well as over every finitely generated extension of transcendence degree 3 3 of ℝ \mathbb{R} . Keywords: Classical adjoint linear algebraic group, stably rational, R R -equivalence, quadratic form, algebra with involution, quaternion algebra, hyperbolic, Pfister form, cohomological dimension Classification (MSC 2020): 11E04, 11E57, 11E81, 14E08, 20G15



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of characteristic different from
2
.
In
[
10
]
, A. Merkurjev studied
R
-equivalence for classical groups
G
of adjoint type over
K
.
Presenting
G
as the group of proper projective similitudes of a
K
-algebra with involution
(A,\sigma)
, he described the group of
R
-equivalence classes
G(K^{\prime})/R
for field extensions
K^{\prime}/K
in terms of a quotient of two subgroups of
{K^{\prime}}^{\times}
related to factors of similitude of
\sigma
and norms from finite extensions of
K^{\prime}
where the involution becomes hyperbolic. (See
3.1
below.)

Merkurjev’s work had been followed up in various directions.
Firstly, using Merkurjev’s criterion,
R
-triviality of
G
was established for certain types of groups; see e.g.
[
13
]
and
[
1
]
.
Secondly, many examples of non-
R
-trivial groups
G
were constructed; see e.g.
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
1
]
. The wealth of examples suggest that
R
-triviality might be rather the exception than the default.
Thirdly, sufficient conditions on the base field
K
were considered for having triviality of
G(K)/R
for adjoint classical groups of given types; see e.g.
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
2
]
.

In
[
10
, Theorem 3]
,
R
-triviality is completely characterized in terms of invariants for adjoint groups of type
A_{3}\equiv D_{3}
.
In this article we exhibit sufficient conditions on the field
K
for the presence of adjoint groups
G
of type
A_{3}\equiv D_{3}
over
K
such that
G(K)/R
is nontrivial.
This is stronger than having that
G
is not
R
-trivial, since for the latter, the existence of a (possibly transcendental) extension
K^{\prime}/K
with
G(K^{\prime})/R\neq\{1\}
suffices.
We apply a construction in
[
10
]
to obtain examples of such groups
G
of type
A_{3}\equiv D_{3}
with
G(K)/R\neq\{1\}
over any field
K
that admits an anisotropic torsion
3
-fold Pfister form.

2.
Algebras with involution and similitudes

By a
K
-algebra with involution
we mean a pair
(A,\sigma)
where

A
is a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra endowed with an
K
-linear involution
\sigma
such that
A
has no nontrivial two-sided ideals
I
with
\sigma(I)=I
and, denoting by
\mathsf{Z}(A)
is the center of
A
, we have
K=\{x\in\mathsf{Z}(A)\mid\sigma(x)=x\}
.
It follows that
\mathsf{dim}_{K}A=[\mathsf{Z}(A):K]\cdot n^{2}
for some integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denoted by
\mathsf{deg}A
and called the
degree of
A
.
Algebras with involution are distinguished into three types:
orthogonal
,
symplectic
and
unitary
; we refer to
[
7
, §2]
for details.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be a
K
-algebra with an
involution. An element
a\in{A}^{\times}
is said to be a

similitude
if
\sigma(a)a\in{K}^{\times}
.
The similitudes of
(A,\sigma)
form a subgroup of
{A}^{\times}
, which is denoted by

{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)
. We refer to

[
7
, §12]
for the basics on similitudes.
We obtain a group homomorphism

\mu:{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)\to{K}^{\times},\quad a\mapsto\sigma(a)a\,,

called the
multiplier map
.
The image of this map is denoted by
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)
.
Hence

\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)=\{x\in{K}^{\times}\mid x=\sigma(a)a\mbox{ for some }a\in{A}^{\times}\}\,.

We set
{\bf\mathsf{PSim}}(A,\sigma)={\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)/{K}^{\times}
and call this the group of
projective similitudes of
(A,\sigma)
.
Note that
{\bf\mathsf{PSim}}(A,\sigma)
is naturally isomorphic to
the group of automorphisms of
(A,\sigma)
, in view of the Skolem-Noether Theorem.

If
\mathsf{deg}A=2m
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma
is orthogonal then, for
a\in{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)
, we have

\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{A}(a)=\pm a^{m}
, and we set

{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}^{+}(A,\sigma)=\{a\in{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)\mid\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{A}(a)=a^{m}\}\,.

If
\mathsf{deg}A
is odd or
\sigma
is not orthogonal, then we set
{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}^{+}(A,\sigma)={\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)
.
Hence
{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}^{+}(A,\sigma)
is always a subgroup of
{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)
of index
1
or
2
.
We obtain corresponding subgroups

{\bf\mathsf{PSim}}^{+}(A,\sigma)={\bf\mathsf{Sim}}^{+}(A,\sigma)/{K}^{\times}
of
{\bf\mathsf{Sim}}(A,\sigma)
and
\mathsf{G}^{+}(A,\sigma)=\mu({\bf\mathsf{Sim}}^{+}(A,\sigma))
of
\mathsf{G}(A,\sigma)
.

For a field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, we obtain from
(A,\sigma)
by scalar extension an
K^{\prime}
-algebra with involution
(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})
.
One obtains naturally a group scheme
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
such that, for any field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, the set of
K^{\prime}
-rational points

{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)(K^{\prime})
is given by
{\bf\mathsf{PSim}}^{+}(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})
.
Note that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
is an adjoint, semi-simple, connected linear algebraic group over
K
.

Assume that
(A,\sigma)
is a
K
-algebra with orthogonal involution of even degree.
We denote by
\mathcal{C}(A,\sigma)
the
Clifford algebra
of
(A,\sigma)
; see
[
7
, §8]
for the definition.
The center of
\mathcal{C}(A,\sigma)
is a quadratic étale extension of
K
, hence of the form
K[X]/(X^{2}-d)
for some
d\in{K}^{\times}
, and the square-class
d{K}^{\times 2}\in{K}^{\times}/{K}^{\times 2}
is uniquely determined by
\sigma
, denoted by
\mathsf{disc}(\sigma)
and called the
discriminant of
\sigma
.

Consider two
K
-algebras with involution
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(A_{2},\sigma_{2})
.
We call
(A,\sigma)

an orthogonal sum of
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(A_{2},\sigma_{2})
if there exist
e\in A
with
\sigma(e)=e
and
e^{2}=e
and
K
-algebra isomorphisms,

\varphi_{1}:A_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}eAe\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\varphi_{2}:A_{2}\xrightarrow{\sim}(1-e)A(1-e)

such that
\varphi_{i}\circ\sigma_{i}=\sigma\circ\varphi_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
Note that this implies that
A\sim A_{1}\sim A_{2}
and that
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are of the same type as
\sigma
.
If
(A,\sigma)
is an orthogonal sum of
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(A_{2},\sigma_{2})
, we also indicate this by writing

(A,\sigma)\in(A_{1},\sigma_{1})\boxplus(A_{2},\sigma_{2})\,.

3.
R
-triviality in groups of type
A_{3}\equiv D_{3}

The study of
R
-equivalence for semisimple adjoint classical groups
can be reduced to such ones that are absolutely simple.
As a consequence of A. Weil’s classification results in
[
14
]
, any absolutely simple adjoint classical group over
K
is
given by
\mathbf{PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
for some
K
-algebra with involution
(A,\sigma)
.
If
A
is of even degree
2n
and
\sigma
is an orthogonal involution, then this group is of Dynkin type
D_{n}
.

For a finite field extension
L/K
, we write

\mathsf{N}_{L/K}:L\to K
for the norm map, and we abbreviate
\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}({L}^{\times})
.
Similarly, for a central simple
K
-algebra
A
, we write

\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{A}:A\to K
for the reduced norm map, and we abbreviate
\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{A}^{\ast}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{A}({A}^{\times})
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be a
K
-algebra with orthogonal or symplectic involution.
We denote by
\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)
the subgroup of
{K}^{\times}
generated by the subsets
\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}
where
L/K
ranges over the finite field extensions for which
\sigma_{L}
is hyperbolic.
We set

\mathsf{H}(A,\sigma)={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)\,.

Merkurjev studies the
group of
R
-equivalence classes of

{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
by means of the following translation.

Theorem 3.1

(Merkurjev
[
10
, Theorem 1]
)
.

We have
\mathsf{H}(A,\sigma)\subseteq\mathsf{G}^{+}(A,\sigma)
and

{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)/R\simeq\mathsf{G}^{+}(A,\sigma)/\mathsf{H}(A,\sigma).

In particular,
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\sigma)
is
R
-trivial if and only if
\mathsf{G}^{+}(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{H}(A_{K^{\prime}},\sigma_{K^{\prime}})
holds for every field extension
K^{\prime}/K
.

This provides tool to compute
R
-equivalence on semisimple adjoint classical groups and to construct examples of nonrational adjoint groups.
The simplest examples occur for algebras with orthogonal involutions of degree
6
.
In the split case, where
(A,\sigma)
is adjoint to a regular
6
-dimensional quadratic form
\eta
over
K
,
[
10
, Theorem 2]
yields that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
is
R
-trivial if and only if
\mathcal{C}(A,\sigma)
(the even Clifford algebra of
\eta
) is not a division algebra.
Using this one obtains examples over fields of cohomological dimension
3
where
\mathbf{PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
is not
R
-trivial.
However, there seems to be no such example known where
\mathbf{PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)(K)\neq\{1\}
over a field of cohomological dimension
3
with
A
split.
We now turn our attention to the nonsplit case.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be a
K
-algebra with orthogonal involution of degree
6
and of nontrivial discriminant. Let
C=\mathcal{C}(A,\sigma)
and
L=\mathsf{Z}(C)
. Assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}(C)=2
and
A
is not split.
Then there exist
K
-quaternion algebras
Q,Q_{1},Q_{2}
and an orthogonal involution
\gamma
on
Q
such that
L
is contained in
Q
and
\gamma
extends the nontrivial automorphism of
L/K
and such that
A\sim Q\sim Q_{1}\otimes Q_{2}
and

(A,\sigma)\in(Q_{1},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{1}})\otimes(Q_{2},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{2}})\boxplus(Q,\gamma)\,.

Proof.

This is explained in
[
10
, p. 207-208]
.

Theorem 3.3

(Merkurjev)
.

Let
Q,Q_{1},Q_{2}
be
K
-quaternion algebras such that
Q_{1}\otimes Q_{2}\sim Q
.
Let
L/K
be a quadratic field extension contained in
Q
and
\gamma
an orthogonal involution on
Q
extending the nontrivial automorphism of
L/K
.
Let
(A,\sigma)\in(Q_{1},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{1}})\otimes(Q_{2},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{2}})\boxplus(Q,\gamma)
and
C=\mathcal{C}(A,\sigma)
.
Then
C
is a central simple
L
-algebra
with
C\sim(Q_{1})_{L}\simeq(Q_{2})_{L}
, and we have

\mathsf{G}^{+}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{1}}^{\ast}\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{2}}^{\ast})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathsf{H}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}^{\ast}_{Q_{k}}

for
k=1,2
, and in partiuclar

{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)(K)/R\,\,\simeq\,\,\dfrac{\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{1}}^{\ast}\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{2}}^{\ast})}{\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}^{\ast}_{Q_{k}}}\,\,.

Proof.

Note that
\mathsf{disc}(\sigma)=\mathsf{disc}(\gamma)
, by
[
7
, Def. 7.2 & Prop. 7.5]
.
We fix
d\in{K}^{\times}
such that
\mathsf{disc}(\gamma)=\mathsf{disc}(\sigma)=d{K}^{\times 2}
.
Then
L\simeq K(\sqrt{d})
.

Set
(B,\tau)=(Q_{1},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{1}})\otimes(Q_{2},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{2}})
.
Then
\mathsf{disc}(\tau)
is trivial, and therefore
\mathsf{G}^{+}(A,\sigma)\subseteq\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}=\mathsf{G}^{+}(Q,\gamma)
.
We conclude that

\mathsf{G}^{+}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{G}^{+}(B,\tau)
.
Since
B\sim Q
, we have that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B\leqslant 2
.
Hence

[
2
, Prop. 4.2
(c)
]

yields that
\mathsf{G}^{+}(B,\tau)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}^{\ast}_{Q_{1}}\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}^{\ast}_{Q_{2}}
.
This shows that

\mathsf{G}^{+}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}^{\ast}_{Q_{1}}\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}^{\ast}_{Q_{2}}\,.

Consider an arbitrary finite field extension
M/K
such that
\sigma_{M}
is hyperbolic.
Then
\mathsf{disc}(\sigma_{M})
is trivial and
C_{M}
is split.
Hence we may view
L
as a subfield of
M
, and obtain by
[
2
, Prop. 4.1
(b)
]
that
\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}(\mathsf{N}_{M/L}^{\ast})\subseteq\mathsf{N}_{L/K}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{C}^{\ast})
.
Having this for every finite field extension
M/K
such that
\sigma_{M}
is hyperbolic, we conclude that
\mathsf{Hyp}(\sigma)\subseteq\mathsf{N}_{L/K}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{C}^{\ast})
.

By
[
7
, p. 93, Examples.
(b)
]
we have
\mathcal{C}(B,\tau)=Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
.
Note that
Q_{L}
is split and
\gamma_{L}
is hyperbolic.
This yields that
C\sim(Q_{1})_{L}\sim(Q_{2})_{L}
.

Fix
k\in\{1,2\}
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{C}^{\ast}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{(Q_{k})_{L}}^{\ast}
, by
[
2
, Prop. 4.1
(a)
]
.
In particular
\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)\subseteq\mathsf{N}_{L/K}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{C}^{\ast})=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{(Q_{k})_{L}}^{\ast})
.
By
[
10
, Lemma 10]
, we have

\mathsf{H}(A,\sigma)={K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}(\sigma)\subseteq{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{N}_{L/K}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{(Q_{k})_{L}}^{\ast})=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{k}}^{\ast}.

To show the converse inclusion, consider now
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{k}}^{\ast}
.
If
(Q_{k})_{L}
is split, then

\sigma_{L}
is hyperbolic and hence
a\in\mathsf{Hyp}(A,\sigma)
.
Assume that
(Q_{k})_{L}
is not split.
Since
a\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{k}}^{\ast}
, there exist a quadratic extension
L^{\prime}/K
such that

(Q_{k})_{L^{\prime}}
is split and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}
.
Then
L^{\prime}/K
is linearly disjoint from
L/K
, so we obtain a biquadratic field extension
M=L\otimes_{K}L^{\prime}
of
K
.
By
[
13
, Lemma 2.11]
, we have
\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}
.
Hence
a\in{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}
.
Since
\sigma_{M}
is hyperbolic, we conclude that
a\in{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(A,\sigma)\subseteq\mathsf{H}(A,\sigma)
.
This shows that

\mathsf{H}(A,\sigma)=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{k}}^{\ast}\quad\text{ for }\quad k\in\{1,2\}\,.

The final part of the statement now follows by
3.1
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
a,b,c\in{K}^{\times}
be such that
-1\in\mathsf{D}_{K}{\langle\!\langle a,b\rangle\!\rangle}
and

\langle\!\langle a,b,c\rangle\!\rangle
is anisotropic.
Let
Q_{1}=(a,b)_{K}
,
Q_{2}=(-ac,b)_{K}
,
Q=(-c,b)_{K}
and
L=K(\sqrt{-c})
.
Then

c\in\left(\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{1}}^{\ast}\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{2}}^{\ast})\right)\smallsetminus(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{1}}^{\ast}\cup\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{2}}^{\ast})

and there exists an orthogonal involution
\gamma
on
Q
with
\mathsf{disc}(\gamma)=-c{K}^{\times 2}
.
Furthermore, there exists a
K
-algebra of degree
6
with orthogonal involution

(A,\sigma)\in\left((Q_{1},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{1}})\otimes(Q_{2},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{2}})\right)\boxplus(Q,\gamma)\,,

and for any such
(A,\sigma)
we have
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)(K)/R\neq\{1\}
.

Proof.

Note that
c=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}(\sqrt{-c})\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
,
-1,-a\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{1}}^{\ast}
and
ac\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{2}}^{\ast}
.
Therefore
c\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap(\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{1}}^{\ast}\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{2}}^{\ast})
.
On the other hand, since
\langle\!\langle a,b,c\rangle\!\rangle
is anisotropic, we have that
c\notin\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{1}}^{\ast}\cup\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q_{2}}^{\ast}
.
The statement now follows by
3.2
and
3.3
.
∎

.

Let

In the case where

Corollary 3.8
.

Assume that
K
is nonreal. Then
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)(K)/R=\{1\}
for every central simple
K
-algebra with orthogonal involution
(A,\sigma)
of degree
6
if and only if
\mathsf{I}^{3}K=0
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathsf{I}^{3}K
is torsion, because
K
is nonreal.
If
\mathsf{I}^{3}K\neq 0
