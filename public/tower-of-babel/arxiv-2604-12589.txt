Doubly Nonlinear Diffusion Equations on Metric Graphs
José M. Mazón and Julián Toledo

Abstract
. In this paper we study existence and uniqueness of solutions for a very general class of doubly nonlinear diffusion equations on metric graphs, which provide the appropriate mathematical framework to describe complex tubular networks in which axial diffusion is the main focus. Some important particular cases covered in our study are the Porous Medium Equation and the evolution equation for the p p -Laplacian, but we also consider the case in that diffusion changes from one edge to another, which takes into account the influence of the properties of the tubules forming the network on axial diffusion. Furthermore, the problem is studied under non-homogeneous Neumann-Kirchhoff conditions on the vertices of the graph.

1.
Introduction

Many physical, biological and engineering dynamical systems are posed on thin elongated structures arranged in complex networks. Examples include quantum wires and nanostructures in mesoscopic physics, waveguides and optical fibers, vascular and renal tubular networks, as well as microfluidic devices. A common feature of such systems is that their transversal dimensions are much smaller than their longitudinal extent, while the underlying connectivity and topology play a dominant role in the observed dynamics.

From a mathematical perspective, these dynamical systems are naturally modeled as partial differential equations posed on thin tubular domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Typical examples involve diffusion, wave propagation, or Schrödinger-type equations. However, direct analysis of such problems in their full dimensional setting is often analytically intractable and computationally expensive. This motivates the search for effective reduced models that retain the essential physical and geometric features while significantly simplifying the underlying equations.

Quantum graphs arise precisely as such effective models. They consist of metric graphs (graphs whose edges are identified with finite or infinite intervals) equipped with differential operators acting along the edges and suitable coupling conditions at the vertices. The term
quantum
originates from early applications to quantum mechanics, where Schrödinger operators on networks of thin wires were studied. Nevertheless, the framework is by no means restricted to quantum systems and has since found applications in diffusion processes, wave propagation, elasticity, and biological transport phenomena. Indeed, the literature on quantum graphs is vast and extensive, and there is no chance to give even a brief overview of the subject here.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Metric Graphs

We recall here some basic knowledge about metric graphs,
see for instance

[
12
]
and the references therein.

A graph
\mathsf{G}
consists of a finite or countable infinite set
of vertices
\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})=\{\mathsf{v}_{i}\}
and a set of edges
\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})=\{\mathsf{e}_{j}\}
connecting the
vertices. A graph
\mathsf{G}
is said to be a finite graph if the number of edges
and the number of vertices are finite.
An edge and a vertex on
that edge are called incident. We will denote
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{e}
when the edge
\mathsf{e}
and the vertex
\mathsf{v}
are incident.

We define
\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}(\mathsf{G})
as the set of all edges incident to the vertex
\mathsf{v}
, and the
degree
of
\mathsf{v}
as
d_{\mathsf{v}}:=\sharp\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}(\mathsf{G})
(the cardinal of
\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}(\mathsf{G})
). We define the
boundary
of
\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})
as

\partial\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}):=\{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}):d_{\mathsf{v}}=1\},

and its
interior
as

\operatorname{int}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})):=\{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}):d_{\mathsf{v}}>1\}.

Following
[
12
]
we say that a graph
\mathsf{G}
is a metric graph if

(1)

each edge
\mathsf{e}
is assigned with a positive length
\ell_{\mathsf{e}}\in(0,+\infty];

(2)

for each edge
\mathsf{e}
, a coordinate is assigned to each point of it, including its vertices. For that purpose, each edge
\mathsf{e}
is identified with an ordered pair

(\mathsf{i}_{\mathsf{e}},\mathsf{f}_{\mathsf{e}})
of vertices, being
\mathsf{i}_{\mathsf{e}}
and
\mathsf{f}_{\mathsf{e}}
the
initial and terminal vertex of
\mathsf{e}
respectively
and an increasing coordinate in the edge is defined in the direction of the edge:

\begin{array}[]{rlcc}c_{\mathsf{e}}:&\mathsf{e}&\to&[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}]\\
&x&\rightsquigarrow&c_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\end{array}

such that
c_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{i}_{\mathsf{e}})=0
,
c_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{f}_{\mathsf{e}})=\ell_{\mathsf{e}}
, and it is exhaustive. This is not restrictive under our standing assumption that there are no multiple edges between any two vertices. Also, an edge
\mathsf{e}
can be reparametized with the same length in such a way that the initial and final vertices are interchanged by using the parametrization
\tilde{c}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)=c_{\mathsf{e}}(\ell_{\mathsf{e}}-x)
.

A graph is said to be

connected
if a path exists between every pair of vertices.

A
compact
metric graph is a finite metric graph whose edges all have finite
length.

We will deal with connected and compact metric graphs. To simplify we also assume that there are no loops (that is there are no edges connecting one vertex), and that there are no two different edges connecting the same vertices.

A function
u
on a metric graph
\mathsf{G}
is a collection of functions
[u]_{\mathsf{e}}

defined on

(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}})
for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G}),

not just at the vertices as in discrete models.

We use the notation (we use the same name for variables in
\mathsf{G}
and in
(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}})
since there is not ambiguity):

\int_{\mathsf{G}}u=\int_{\mathsf{G}}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x:=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}[u]_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}[u]_{\mathsf{e}}.

Let, for each
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
,
p_{\mathsf{e}}\in[1,+\infty]
. We write
\overline{p}
to represent the collection of
(p_{\mathsf{e}})_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}
. We denote by
L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
the space of all function in
\mathsf{G}
such that
[u]_{\mathsf{e}}\in L^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}})
for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
, with the norm

\|u\|_{\overline{p}}=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\|[u]_{\mathsf{e}}\|_{L^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}})}.

If
p_{\mathsf{e}}=p
for all
\mathsf{e}
, we will also write
L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
as
L^{p}(\mathsf{G})
.

We define
L^{1}(0,T;L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}))
as the Banach space of measurable functions
f:(0,T)\rightarrow L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left\|f(t)\right\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}\,dt<\infty.

Consider

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
the space of functions in
\mathsf{G}
such that

[u]_{\mathsf{e}}\in W^{1,p_{\mathsf{e}}}(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}})

for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
, with the Sobolev norm

\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}:=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\left(\left\|[u]_{\mathsf{e}}\right\|_{L^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}})}^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}+\left\|[u]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}})}^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)^{1/p_{\mathsf{e}}}.

If
u\in\widetilde{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
we denote by
u^{\prime}
the function in
L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
with
[u^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{e}}=[u]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}
for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
.
The space
\widetilde{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
is a reflexive Banach space when
1<p_{\mathsf{e}}<+\infty
for all edge
\mathsf{e}
.

We denote by
C(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}))
the set of all functions
u
in
\mathsf{G}
such that each
[u]_{\mathsf{e}}
is continuous at
[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}]
and

[u]_{\mathsf{e}_{1}}(\mathsf{v})=[u]_{\mathsf{e}_{2}}(\mathsf{v})\quad\hbox{for all}\ \mathsf{e}_{1},\mathsf{e}_{2}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}(\mathsf{G}).

We denote this common value at
\mathsf{v}
as
u(\mathsf{v})
.

Observe that in the definition of
\widetilde{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
we do not assume the continuity at the vertices.
Set

\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})\cap C(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})),

equipped with the above Sobolev norm.

When
p_{\mathsf{e}}=p
for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
, we write

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})=\widetilde{W}^{1,p}(\mathsf{G})

and

\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})=W^{1,p}(\mathsf{G}).

We will also consider the following space of functions in
\mathsf{G}
:

\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{G}):=\{\varphi:[\varphi]_{\mathsf{e}}\in\mathcal{D}(]0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}[)\ \ \forall\,\mathsf{e}\in E(\mathsf{G})\}.

We define
L^{1}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}))
as the Banach spaces of measurable functions
f:(0,T)\rightarrow\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left\|f(t)\right\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}\,dt<\infty.

If
I=(a,b)
is a bounded interval in
\mathbb{R}
, by
[
15

, we have that

\hbox{the injection}\ W^{1,p}(I)\subset C(\overline{I})\ \hbox{is compact for all}\ 1<p\leq\infty.

(2.1)

Consequently we have the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a compact metric graph and
1<p_{\mathsf{e}}<\infty

\mathsf{e}\in E(\mathsf{G})
. The injection
W^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})\subset C(\mathsf{G})

Moreover, we have the following Poincaré inequality.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a compact metric graph and
1<p_{\mathsf{e}}<\infty
for all
\mathsf{e}\in E(\mathsf{G})
. There exists a constant
\lambda_{\mathsf{G}}>0
such that

\lambda_{\mathsf{G}}\|u\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}\leq\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}\quad\forall\,u\in W^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}):\int_{\mathsf{G}}u=0.

(2.2)

Proof.

We prove the following equivalent formulation: there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}\leq C\left(\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}+\left|\int_{\mathsf{G}}u\right|\right)\quad\forall\,u\in W^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}).

(2.3)

To prove (
2.3
) let us argue by contradiction. If it is not true, then, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
u_{n}\in W^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
such that,

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}\geq n\left(\|(u_{n})^{\prime}\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}+\left|\int_{\mathsf{G}}u_{n}\right|\right).

(2.4)

And we can assume that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}=1
. Hence

\|(u_{n})^{\prime}\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}\leq\frac{1}{n}\ \hbox{ and }\ \left|\int_{\mathsf{G}}u_{n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Therefore, by Theorem
2.1
, we have that there exists a subsequence, that we denote equal, such that

u_{n}\to u\hbox{ weakly in }W^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}),\hbox{and in }C(\mathsf{G}).

Hence, since
\|(u_{n})^{\prime}\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}\leq\frac{1}{n}
, each
[u]_{e}
is equal to a constant
C_{\mathsf{e}}
, but, since

is continuous,
C_{\mathsf{e}}=C
for all
\mathsf{e}
. Now, from
\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathsf{G}}u_{n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{n}
we get that

, and this gives a contradiction with
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})}=1
.

Nonlinear semigroups

Let

An operator

\hbox{Ran}(I+\lambda A)=X.

u\ \hbox{is a mild solution of}\ \eqref{eq:30}\iff u\ \hbox{is an integral solution of}\ \eqref{eq:30}.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice and

. We say that

is a

\|(Su-Sv)^{+}\|\leq\|(u-v)^{+}\|\ \ \ \hbox{for}\ u,v\in D(S).

Let

be an operator in a Banach lattice

. We say that

\|(u-\hat{u})^{+}\|\leq\|(u-\hat{u}+\lambda(v-\hat{v}))^{+}\|\ \ \ \hbox{for}\ (u,v),(\hat{u},\hat{v})\in A\ \ \hbox{and}\ \lambda>0.

It is clear that a T-contraction is order-preserving; also if

are single-valued and T-contractions.

The mild solutions of the abstract

a comparison and contraction principle.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let

be a

From

]
we have the following result .

.

The following properties hold true:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}v\chi_{\{u<-h\}}dx\leq 0\leq\int_{\mathsf{G}}v\chi_{\{u>h\}}dx
for all

3.
Doubly Nonlinear Equations on Metric Graphs.

3.1.
Results on a bounded interval

Let
1<p<\infty
be. Let us begin with some known facts for the
p
-Laplacian problem on a bounded interval, which can be seen as the trivial metric graph of one edge.

Given an interval
[0,\ell]
,
g\in L^{1}(0,\ell)
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we say that
u\in W^{1,p}(0,\ell)
is a
weak solution
of the Neumann problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{p}u=g\quad\hbox{in}\ (0,\ell),\\[10.0pt]
-(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime})(0)=a,\\[10.0pt]
(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime})(\ell)=b,\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

if

\int_{0}^{\ell}|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\ell}g\,\varphi\,dx+a\,\varphi(0)+b\,\varphi(\ell)\quad\forall\varphi\in W^{1,p}(0,\ell).

(3.2)

Note that, since
W^{1,p}(0,\ell)\subset C([0,\ell])
, we have that the last integral is finite. And by taking
\varphi=1
we need the compatibility condition

\int_{0}^{\ell}g(x)dx+a+b=0.

Remark 3.1
.

1. For
g\in L^{1}(0,\ell)
, let
u\in W^{1,p}(0,\ell)
be a weak solution
of problem (
3.1
). Then,

\left(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=g\quad\hbox{in }D^{\prime}(]0,\ell[).

This implies that

|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\in W^{1,1}(0,\ell)
, and hence, by Sobolev embedding we can assume that
|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\in C([0,\ell])
.
Therefore,
u
has a representative in
C^{1}([0,1])
.
Moreover, we have that

\int_{y}^{z}g(x)dx=(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime})(y)-(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime})(z)\quad\forall y,z\in[0,\ell].

2. For
z,w\in W^{1,1}(0,\ell)
, we have the following integration by parts formula:

\int_{0}^{\ell}z^{\prime}(x)w(x)\,dx=-\int_{0}^{\ell}z(x)w^{\prime}(x)\,dx+z(\ell)w(\ell)-z(0)w(0).

(3.3)

\blacksquare

Given
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous and increasing with
\gamma(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma(0)=0
,
1<p<\infty
,
f\in L^{1}(0,\ell)
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the problem

(P_{p,a,b}^{\gamma,f})\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}v-\Delta_{p}u=f\quad\hbox{in }(0,\ell),\\
\\
v=\gamma(u)\quad\hbox{a.e. in }(0,\ell),\\
\\
-\left(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\right)(0)=a,\\
\\
\left(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\right)(\ell)=b.\end{array}\right.

We give the following concept of solution for this problem.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
v\in L^{1}(0,\ell)
is a
weak solution
of problem
(P_{p,a,b}^{\gamma,f})
if there exists
u\in W^{1,p}(0,\ell)
, with
v=\gamma(u)
a.e. in
(0,\ell)
, such that

\int_{0}^{\ell}v\phi+\int_{0}^{\ell}|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{\ell}f\phi+b\phi(\ell)+a\phi(0)\quad\forall\,\phi\in W^{1,p}(0,\ell).

(3.4)

If
v
is a weak solution to the problem
(P_{p,a,b}^{\gamma,f})
, we have the following mass balance property,

\int_{0}^{\ell}v=\int_{0}^{\ell}f+b+a,

(3.5)

and we can assume that
u
is
C^{1}([0,\ell])
(see Remark
3.1
).

From
[
2
, Theorem 3.6]
we have the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and increasing with
\gamma(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma(0)=0
, and
1<p<\infty
. For
f\in L^{1}(0,\ell)
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique weak solution to problem
(P_{p,a,b}^{\gamma,f})
.

3.2.
The elliptic problem on a metric graph.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a connected and compact metric graph (without loops and without multiple edges).
For each
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
, let
\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and increasing with
\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}(0)=0
.
We write
\overline{\gamma}
to represent the collection of
(\gamma_{\mathsf{e}})_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}
and
we write
[v]_{\mathsf{e}}=\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}([u]_{\mathsf{e}})
for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
as

v=\overline{\gamma}(u).

Take also
\overline{p}
as in the previous Subsection
2.1
with
1<p_{\mathsf{e}}<+\infty
for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
.

We define the upwind values of a general function
z:\mathsf{G}\to\mathbb{R}
, interpreted as fluxes on
\mathsf{G}
at vertices
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})
, as follows. Let
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})
and
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}(\mathsf{G})
be, we define

\displaystyle\{z\}_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v}):=\begin{cases}+[z]_{\mathsf{e}}(\ell_{\mathsf{e}}),\quad&\text{if }\mathsf{v}=\mathsf{f}_{\mathsf{e}},\\
-[z]_{\mathsf{e}}(0),\quad&\text{if }\mathsf{v}=\mathsf{i}_{\mathsf{e}},\end{cases}

(3.6)

whenever these values exist in the sense of traces, e.g., if

.

From (
3.3
), we have the the following

on a metric graph:

\int_{\mathsf{G}}z^{\prime}w\,dx=-\int_{\mathsf{G}}zw^{\prime}\,dx+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}(\mathsf{G})}\{z\}_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})\right)w(\mathsf{v})\quad\hbox{for }z,w\in W^{1,1}(\mathsf{G}).

(3.7)

Let

be, and
\omega=\{\omega_{\mathsf{v}}:\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})\}\subset\mathbb{R}
(we will write in this simple way the set of fluxes
\omega_{\mathsf{v}}
assigned to each vertex
\mathsf{v}
). Our aim in this section is to solve the elliptic problem

(P_{\omega}^{f})\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}[v]_{\mathsf{e}}-\Delta_{p_{\mathsf{e}}}[u]_{\mathsf{e}}=[f]_{\mathsf{e}}\quad\hbox{in }(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}),\ \forall\,\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G}),\\
\\
{}[v]_{\mathsf{e}}=\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}([u]_{\mathsf{e}})\quad\hbox{a.e.},\ \forall\,\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G}),\\
\\
\hbox{$u$ continuous,}\\
\\
\displaystyle\partial^{\overline{p}}_{\nu}u(\mathsf{v})={\omega_{\mathsf{v}}}\quad\hbox{for all }\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}).\end{array}\right.

(3.8)

Here,

\partial^{\overline{p}}_{\nu}u(\mathsf{v}):=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}(\mathsf{G})}\{z_{\overline{p}}\}_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v}),

(3.9)

being

defined as

[z_{\overline{p}}]_{\mathsf{e}}=|[u]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[u]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime},

so that,

\partial^{\overline{p}}_{\nu}u(\mathsf{v})={\omega_{\mathsf{v}}}

is a
generalized Neumann-Kirchhoff flux condition
,
which of course includes the
standard
Neumann-Kirchhoff condition

\partial^{\overline{p}}_{\nu}u(\mathsf{v})=0\quad\hbox{for all }\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}),

by taking
\omega_{\mathsf{v}}=0
for all
\mathsf{v}
.

A priori
, (

) should be read as a formal expression that has sense joint with Definition
3.4
. But, in fact, it is not a formal expression because of regularity (see Remark
3.5
).

When the index of a vertex
\mathsf{v}
is
2
, and the edges to which it belongs have the same diffusion rule, it is evident that if the flux
\omega_{\mathsf{v}}=0
we can join these edges in an unique one, and forget this vertex. This is no the case if the diffusion of each edge is different, or if, even being the same,
\omega_{\mathsf{v}}\neq 0
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the study of this problem will allow us to solve, using the Crandall-Liggett Theorem, the evolution problem (
1.3
), but this elliptic problem, and the way we prove existence, is interesting by itself.

Green’s formula (
3.7
) induces the following definition.

Definition 3.4
.

We say that

is a
weak solution
of problem

, with

\int_{\mathsf{G}}v\varphi\,dx+\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[u]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}[\varphi]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\,dx=\int_{\mathsf{G}}f\varphi\,dx+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega_{\mathsf{v}}\varphi(\mathsf{v})\quad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}).

(3.10)

.

Note that given

As a consequence we can consider that

Observe also that, taking as test function

(3.12)

\blacksquare

Proof.

By the previous remark we have that

(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(0)=a\quad\hbox{and}\quad(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(0)=a,

therefore, we have

0\leq(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(x)-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(x)\nearrow\epsilon\quad\hbox{as }x\nearrow\ell.

(3.17)

Then, since the function
\varrho(r):=|r|^{p-2}r
is increasing for
p>1
, we obtain, from the first inequality in (
3.17
),

u_{0}^{\prime}(x)\leq u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(x),

and integrating here, for
0\leq x\leq y\leq\ell,

u_{0}(y)-u_{0}(x)\leq u_{\epsilon}(y)-u_{\epsilon}(x),

so,

0\leq u_{\epsilon}(x)-u_{0}(x)\leq u_{\epsilon}(y)-u_{0}(y)\leq u_{\epsilon}(\ell)-u_{0}(\ell).

(3.18)

Hence, if
u_{\epsilon}(\ell)-u_{0}(\ell)=0
, then
u_{\epsilon}=u_{0}
, which is false. Then we have
u_{0}(\ell)<u_{\epsilon}(\ell)
.

The proof for
\epsilon<0
is similar, and we have proved (i).

Let us now prove (ii). By (
3.16
), there exists a sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
such that
v_{\epsilon_{n}}\to v_{0}
almost everywhere. Then, by (
3.18
),

u_{\epsilon}(x)\to u_{0}(x)\quad\hbox{as }\epsilon\to 0^{+},

(3.19)

for any
x\in[0,\ell)
.
Let us see that

u_{\epsilon}(\ell)\to u_{0}(\ell)\quad\hbox{as }\epsilon\to 0.

From (
3.17
) we also have:

u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)\leq\varrho^{-1}(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon).

(3.20)

Hence, integrating between
0
and
\ell
,

u_{\epsilon}(\ell)-u_{\epsilon}(0)\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}\varrho^{-1}(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon)dx,

and therefore,

0\leq u_{\epsilon}(\ell)-u_{0}(\ell)\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}\varrho^{-1}(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon)dx+u_{\epsilon}(0)-u_{0}(\ell).

(3.21)

By the Monotone Convergence Theorem,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\ell}\varrho^{-1}(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon)dx=\int_{0}^{\ell}u_{0}^{\prime}(x)dx=u_{0}(\ell)-u_{0}(0),

we moreover have shown in (
3.19
) that

u_{\epsilon}(0)\to u_{0}(0),

hence

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\ell}\varrho^{-1}(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon)dx+u_{\epsilon}(0)-u_{0}(\ell)=0,

then, from (
3.21
),

u_{\epsilon}(\ell)\to u_{0}(\ell)\quad\hbox{as }\epsilon\to 0^{+}.

The limit when
\epsilon\to 0^{-}
is similar. Hence, we have the continuity of
\epsilon\mapsto u_{\epsilon}(\ell)
at
0
. Therefore, fixed
x\in[0,\ell]
, having in mind (
3.18
), we have the continuity of
\epsilon\mapsto u_{\epsilon}(x)
at
0
. Now, it is easy to see that we have the continuity of
\epsilon\mapsto u_{\epsilon}(x)
at any point
\epsilon
.

Let us finally prove the statements in (
3.14
). Take
\epsilon>0
. By (
3.16
),

\int_{0}^{\ell}v_{\epsilon}(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\ell}v_{0}(x)dx+\epsilon,

and from here, there exists
0\leq x_{\epsilon}\leq\ell
such that

\lim_{\epsilon\to+\infty}v_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})=+\infty,

hence,

\lim_{\epsilon\to+\infty}u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})=+\infty.

Consequently, since by (
3.18
),

u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})-u_{0}(x_{\epsilon})\leq u_{\epsilon}(\ell)-u_{0}(\ell),

and
u_{0}(x_{\epsilon})
is bounded, we get

\lim_{\epsilon\to+\infty}u_{\epsilon}(\ell)=+\infty.

The other limit follows in the same way.

The proof of
Point 2.
is similar.

Let us now prove
Point 3.
Take
\epsilon>0
.
In this case

\int_{0}^{\ell}(v_{\epsilon}-v_{0})=0.

(3.22)

Since

(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(\ell)-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(\ell)=\epsilon,

and
x\mapsto(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(x)-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(x)
is continuous, we have that

(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(x)-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(x)>0

on an interval
(x_{\epsilon},\ell)
. Let us consider that this interval is the the largest one (with
\ell
fixed) for such property. Let us now consider the following two cases, (a) and (b).

(a) If
x_{\epsilon}=0
then, since

(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(0)-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(0)=\epsilon,

we have that

(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(x)-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(x)>0\quad\forall x\in[0,\ell].

Hence

u_{0}^{\prime}(x)<u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)\quad\forall x\in[0,\ell],

(3.23)

and integrating

u_{\epsilon}(x)-u_{0}(x)<u_{\epsilon}(y)-u_{0}(y)\quad\forall 0\leq x<y\leq\ell.

(3.24)

Now, if
u_{\epsilon}(\ell)\leq u_{0}(\ell)
, by (
3.24
),

u_{\epsilon}(x)-u_{0}(x)\leq 0\quad\forall 0\leq x\leq\ell,

and also, since
\gamma
is increasing,

v_{\epsilon}(x)-v_{0}(x)\leq 0\quad\forall 0\leq x\leq\ell.

Then, by (
3.22
),
we have that
v_{\epsilon}=v_{0}
, which is impossible. Therefore

u_{\epsilon}(\ell)>u_{0}(\ell).

And similarly we have that

u_{\epsilon}(0)<u_{0}(0).

Let
\widehat{x}_{\epsilon}>0
be such that
u_{\epsilon}(x)-u_{0}(x)>0
for
\widehat{x}_{\epsilon}<x\leq\ell

and

u_{\epsilon}(\widehat{x}_{\epsilon})=u_{0}(\widehat{x}_{\epsilon})

(3.25)

Then,

(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(x)-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(x)-\left((|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(\widehat{x}_{\epsilon})-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(\widehat{x}_{\epsilon})\right)

=\int_{\widehat{x}_{\epsilon}}^{x}(v_{\epsilon}-v_{0})\nearrow\epsilon-\left((|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(\widehat{x}_{\epsilon})-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(\widehat{x}_{\epsilon})\right)\quad\hbox{as }x\nearrow\ell.

Therefore,

(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(x)-(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(x)\leq\epsilon\quad\hbox{for }\widehat{x}_{\epsilon}<x<\ell.

From here, and using (
3.25
),

0\leq u_{\epsilon}(\ell)-u_{0}(\ell)\leq\int_{\widehat{x}_{\epsilon}}^{\ell}\varrho^{-1}\left(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon\right)+u_{0}(\widehat{x}_{\epsilon})-u_{0}(\ell).

(3.26)

(b) If

0<x_{\epsilon}<\ell
, then we have that

(|u_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}^{\prime})(x_{\epsilon})=(|u_{0}^{\prime}|^{p-2}u_{0}^{\prime})(x_{\epsilon}).

So, if
we apply the first point proved on this interval
(x_{\epsilon},\ell)
, we get that
u_{0}(x)\leq u_{\epsilon}(x)
in such interval and
u_{0}(\ell)<u_{\epsilon}(\ell)
; and we get that
u_{0}(x)\geq u_{\epsilon}(x)
on the interval
(0,x_{\epsilon})
and
u_{0}(0)>u_{\epsilon}(0)
.
Observe that at point
x_{\epsilon}
, by continuity,

u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})=u_{0}(x_{\epsilon}).

(3.27)

Moreover, the proof of the first point gives us the following fact (using (
3.27
)):

0\leq u_{\epsilon}(\ell)-u_{0}(\ell)\leq\int_{x_{\epsilon}}^{\ell}\varrho^{-1}\left(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon\right)+u_{0}(x_{\epsilon})-u_{0}(\ell).

(3.28)

Observe that in both cases, (a) or (b), we obtain the similar inequalities (
3.26
) and (
3.28
). Let us rewrite
x_{\epsilon}
as
\widehat{x}_{\epsilon}
in (
3.28
).

Then, like in the proof of the first point, we get that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}u_{\epsilon}(\ell)=u_{0}(\ell).

Indeed, we have that there exists
\widehat{x}_{\epsilon_{n}}\to x_{0}\in[0,\ell]
for a sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0^{+}
.
Now,

\int_{\widehat{x}_{\epsilon_{n}}}^{\ell}\varrho^{-1}\left(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon_{n}\right)=\int_{\widehat{x}_{\epsilon_{n}}}^{x_{0}}\varrho^{-1}\left(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon_{n}\right)+\int_{x_{0}}^{\ell}\varrho^{-1}\left(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon_{n}\right).

Here, the fist integral on the right hand-side converges to
0
as
\epsilon_{n}
goes to
0
since the function
\varrho^{-1}\left(\varrho(u_{0}^{\prime}(x))+\epsilon_{n}\right)
is bounded uniformly in
n
, and, using this boundedness again, the Dominate Convergence Theorem gives that the second integral on the right hand-side converges to
u_{0}(\ell)-u_{0}(x_{0})
. Then, we have

u_{\epsilon_{n}}(\ell)\to u_{0}(\ell)\quad\hbox{as }\epsilon_{n}\to 0,

and consequently, by the monotonicity,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}u_{\epsilon}(\ell)=u_{0}(\ell).

Similarly we get
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{-}}u_{\epsilon}(\ell)=u_{0}(\ell).

Hence we have the continuity of
\epsilon\mapsto u_{\epsilon}(\ell)
at
0
, and then at any point.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a connected and compact metric graph with

\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})=\{\mathsf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{v}_{n-1},\mathsf{v}_{n}\}.

Assume
\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous and increasing function with

\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}(0)=0
for all
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})
, and, for
\omega=\{\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{n-1}},\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{n}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, set
\omega^{\epsilon}
be such that

\omega^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{v}_{i}}=\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{i}},\ i=1,2,...,n-1,

\omega^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{v}_{n}}=\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{n}}+\epsilon.

Let
v_{\epsilon}=\overline{\gamma}(u_{\epsilon})
be weak solution to problem
(P_{\omega^{\epsilon}}^{f})
,
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then,

u_{0}\leq u_{\epsilon}\quad\hbox{if}\quad\epsilon\geq 0,\quad\hbox{and}\quad u_{\epsilon}\leq u_{0}\quad\hbox{if}\quad\epsilon\leq 0.

Moreover

\epsilon\mapsto u_{\epsilon}(\mathsf{v}_{n})\ \hbox{ is increasing and continuous.}

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}_{n}}(\mathsf{G})=\{\mathsf{e}_{i}:i=1,2,...,k\}
, and suppose that
\mathsf{v}_{n}=\mathsf{f}_{\mathsf{e}_{i}}
for all
i\in\{1,2,...,k\}
. Let us call
[v_{\epsilon}]_{\mathsf{e}_{i}}=v_{\epsilon,i}
and
[u_{\epsilon}]_{\mathsf{e}_{i}}=u_{\epsilon,i}
,
\ell_{\mathsf{e}_{i}}=\ell_{i}
, and
p_{\mathsf{e}_{i}}=p_{i}
, for each
i\in\{1,2,...,k\}
.

Take
\epsilon>0
. By Theorem
3.8
we have that

v_{\epsilon}\geq v_{0},

and consequently we have

u_{\epsilon}\geq u_{0},

which proves the first statement given.
In particular we have that

0\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(u_{\epsilon}(\mathsf{v}_{n})-u_{0}(\mathsf{v}_{n})\right).

(3.29)

We want to show that this limit is
0
, and that
u_{\epsilon}(\mathsf{v}_{n})>u_{0}(\mathsf{v}_{n}).

By the mass balance property we have that

0\leq\int_{0}^{\ell_{i}}(v_{\epsilon,i}-v_{0,i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\ell_{i}}(v_{\epsilon,i}-v_{0,i})\leq\epsilon,\quad i=1,2,...,k.

(3.30)

By Proposition
3.6
we have that, for each
i\in\{1,2,...,k\}
,

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle(|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(x)-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(x)\\
\\
\displaystyle=\int_{y}^{x}(v_{\epsilon,i}-v_{0,i})+(|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(y)-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(y)\quad\forall 0\leq y\leq x\leq\ell_{i}.\end{array}

(3.31)

By the hypothesis
\omega^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{v}_{n}}=\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{n}}+\epsilon
, then we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(\ell_{i})-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(\ell_{i})=\epsilon,

(3.32)

Hence, there exists an index
i
such that
(|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(\ell_{i})-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(\ell_{i})>0
. Then, arguing as in the proof of Lemma
3.9
, since
u_{\epsilon}\geq u_{0}
, we get that

u_{\epsilon}(\mathsf{v}_{n})>u_{0}(\mathsf{v}_{n}).

The growth for
\epsilon<0
is proved similarly.

Let us see that there there is a positive sequence
\epsilon_{m}\to 0
such that

\lim_{j}\left(u_{\epsilon_{m}}(\mathsf{v}_{n})-u_{0}(\mathsf{v}_{n})\right)=0.

By the monotonicity, this is enough to prove
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(u_{\epsilon}(\mathsf{v}_{n})-u_{0}(\mathsf{v}_{n})\right)=0
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be. Since, for each
i\in\{1,2,...,k\}
,
\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{0}^{\ell_{i}}(v_{\epsilon,i}-v_{0,i})\leq\epsilon
, we have that there exists a positive sequence
\epsilon_{m}\to 0
such that

\lim_{m}v_{\epsilon_{m},i}(x)=v_{0,i}(x)\quad\hbox{for a.e. }x\in(0,\ell_{i}),\ \hbox{for all }i\in\{1,2,...,k\},

and also

\lim_{m}u_{\epsilon_{m},i}(x)=u_{0,i}(x)\quad\hbox{for a.e. }x\in(0,\ell_{i}),\ \hbox{for all }i\in\{1,2,...,k\}.

For each
i\in\{1,2,...,k\}
, fix
x_{0,i}\in(0,\ell_{i})
such that

\lim_{m}u_{\epsilon_{m},i}(x_{0,i})=u_{0,i}(x_{0,i}).

From (
3.31
), in particular,

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle(|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(\ell_{i})-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(\ell_{i})\\
\\
\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\ell_{i}}(v_{\epsilon,i}-v_{0,i})+(|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(0)-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(0).\end{array}

(3.33)

Then, adding these expressions from
i=1
to
i=k
, since
\omega^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{v}_{n}}=\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{n}}+\epsilon
, we have

\epsilon=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\ell_{i}}(v_{\epsilon,i}-v_{0,i})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left((|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(0)-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(0)\right).

Then, by (
3.30
),

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left((|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(0)-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(0)\right)\geq 0.

(3.34)

Write
\epsilon=\epsilon_{m}
for simplicity. On account of (
3.34
), we have two possible situations, (i) or (ii):

(i) there is an
i\in\{1,2,...,k\}
such that

(|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(0)-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(0)<0;

(ii) for all the indices

3.33
) and (
3.30
),

0\leq(|u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{\epsilon,i}^{\prime})(\ell_{i})-(|u_{0,i}^{\prime}|^{p_{i}-2}u_{0,i}^{\prime})(\ell_{i})\leq\epsilon,

(3.37)

Then for any

, following the same steps that in case (i) we get,

, there exists a subsequence

u_{\epsilon_{m},i}(x_{0,i})\to u_{0,i}(x_{0,i}).

Now we finish as in the proof of the previous lemma.

Lemma 3.12

\mathsf{G}

\omega=\{\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{1}},\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{2}},\ldots,\omega_{\mathsf{v}_{m}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}

j
and
k

\epsilon_{i}>0

, let
1<p_{\mathsf{e}}<\infty
be and
\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous and increasing function with
\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}(0)=0
. Let
f\in L^{\overline{p^{\prime}}}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{\overline{p^{\prime}}}(\mathsf{G}))
and
\omega\in L^{\infty}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{1}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})))
be.
For every initial data

v_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathsf{G})

with
\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{0})<+\infty
, there exists a unique weak solution of Problem (
1.3
).

Moreover, if, for
i=1,2
,
f_{i}\in L^{\overline{p^{\prime}}}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{\overline{p^{\prime}}}(\mathsf{G}))
,
\omega_{i}\in L^{\overline{p^{\prime}}}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{1}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})))
, and
v_{i}

is weak solution for initial data
v_{i,0}\in L^{1}(\mathsf{G})

with
\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{i,0})<+\infty
, then, for any
t>0
:

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}(v_{1}(t)-v_{2}(t))^{+}\leq\int_{\mathsf{G}}(v_{1,0}-v_{2,0})^{+}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathsf{G}}(f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s))^{+}\,dxds\\
\\
\displaystyle+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{t}(\omega_{1}(s,\mathsf{v})-\omega_{2}(s,\mathsf{v}))^{+}\,ds.\end{array}

Proof.

By Theorems
2.6
and
3.20
, we only need to prove that the mild solution obtained in Theorem
3.21
is a weak solution of Problem (
1.3
), the rest is consequence of the properties of mild solutions and Theorem
3.24
(see Theorems
2.6
and
2.10
).

Let
T>0
and
W=(v,0)
be the mild solution in
[0,T]
, with
v(0)=v_{0}
, obtained in Theorem
3.21
. Our aim is to prove that
v
is a weak solution of problem (
1.3
).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider a subdivision
t_{0}^{n}=0<t_{1}^{n}<\cdots<t_{n-1}^{n}<t_{n}^{n}=T
with
t_{i}^{n}-t_{i-1}^{n}=\frac{T}{n}
. Given
\epsilon>0
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{T}{n}\leq\epsilon
, and there exist
f_{1}^{n},\ldots,f_{n}^{n}\in L^{\overline{p^{\prime}}}(\mathsf{G})
,
\omega_{1}^{n},\ldots,\omega_{n}^{n}\in L^{1}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}))
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{t_{i-1}^{n}}^{t_{i}^{n}}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[f(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}-[f_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}}\right)dt\leq\epsilon,

(3.73)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in[t_{i-1}^{n},t_{i}^{n}]}\|\omega(t)-\omega_{i}^{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}))}\leq\epsilon,

(3.74)

and

v(t)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}v_{n}(t)\quad\hbox{in $L^{1}(\mathsf{G})$, uniformly for }t\in[0,T],

(3.75)

where
v_{n}(t)
is given by

\begin{array}[]{l}v_{n}(0)=v_{0},\\
\\
v_{n}(t)=v_{i}^{n}\quad\hbox{for}\ t\in]t_{i-1}^{n},t_{1}^{n}],\quad i=1,\ldots,n,\end{array}

(3.76)

with
(v_{i}^{n},0)
being
the solution
of

\frac{(v_{i}^{n},0)-(v_{i-1}^{n},0)}{T/n}+\mathcal{A}(v_{i}^{n},0)\ni(f_{i}^{n},\omega_{i}^{n}).

For such solution
(v_{i}^{n},0)
, there exist
u_{i}^{n}\in\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}))
with
v_{i}^{n}=\overline{\gamma}(u_{i}^{n})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, satisfying

\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[u_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}[\varphi]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\,dx+\int_{\mathsf{G}}\frac{v_{i}^{n}-v_{i-1}^{n}}{T/n}\varphi\,dx=\int_{\mathsf{G}}f_{i}^{n}\varphi\,dx+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega_{i}^{n}(\mathsf{v})\varphi(\mathsf{v}),

(3.77)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})
.
Taking
\varphi=u_{i}^{n}

we get

\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}+\int_{\mathsf{G}}\frac{v_{i}^{n}-v_{i-1}^{n}}{T/n}u_{i}^{n}\,dx=\int_{\mathsf{G}}f_{i}^{n}u_{i}^{n}\,dx+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega_{i}^{n}(\mathsf{v})u_{i}^{n}(\mathsf{v}).

(3.78)

Since
[u_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}}=\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}^{-1}([v_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}})=\partial j^{*}_{\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}}([v_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}})
,

j^{*}_{\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}}([v_{i-1}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}})-j^{*}_{\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}}([v_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}})\geq([v_{i-1}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}}-[v_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}})[u_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}},

we have

\int_{\mathsf{G}}\frac{j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{i}^{n})-j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{i-1}^{n})}{T/n}\leq\int_{\mathsf{G}}\frac{v_{i}^{n}-v_{i-1}^{n}}{T/n}u_{i}^{n}\,dx.

Therefore, by (
3.78
), we get

\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}+\int_{\mathsf{G}}\frac{j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{i}^{n})-j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{i-1}^{n})}{T/n}\leq\int_{\mathsf{G}}f_{i}^{n}u_{i}^{n}\,dx+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega_{i}^{n}(\mathsf{v})u_{i}^{n}(\mathsf{v}).

Then, integrating in time, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\int_{t_{i-1}^{n}}^{t_{i}^{n}}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{i}^{n}]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}+\int_{\mathsf{G}}(j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{i}^{n})-j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{i-1}^{n}))\\
\\
\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\int_{t_{i-1}^{n}}^{t_{i}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathsf{G}}f_{i}^{n}u_{i}^{n}\,dx+\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega_{i}^{n}(\mathsf{v})u_{i}^{n}(\mathsf{v})\right)\end{array}

(3.79)

Consequently, if we set

u_{n}(t)=u_{i}^{n},\ f_{n}(t)=f_{i}^{n},\ \omega_{n}(t)=\omega_{i}^{n}\quad\hbox{for}\ t\in]t_{i-1},t_{1}],\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

it follows that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|u_{n}(t)^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)dt+\int_{\mathsf{G}}(j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{n}^{n})-j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{0}))\,dx\\
\\
\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}f_{n}(t)u_{n}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega_{n}(t,\mathsf{v})u_{n}(t,\mathsf{v}).\end{array}

(3.80)

From (
3.80
), using that
j^{*}_{\gamma_{\mathsf{e}}}\geq 0
, we have

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)dt\\
\\
\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathsf{G}}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{0})+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}f_{n}(t)u_{n}(t)\,dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega_{n}(t,\mathsf{v})u_{n}(t,\mathsf{v})\,dt.\\
\\
\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathsf{G}}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{0})+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[f_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}}\right)^{1/{p_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}\right)^{1/{p_{\mathsf{e}}}}\right)dt\\
\\
\displaystyle+\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\|\omega_{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}))}\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}))}\,dt.\end{array}

(3.81)

By (
3.75
), there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathsf{G}}|v_{n}(t)|\leq C_{1}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.82)

Then, applying Lemma
2.3
in each edge, we have that there exists
C_{2}>0
(not depending on
\mathsf{e}
, since there is a finite quantity of edges) such that

\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)^{1/{p_{\mathsf{e}}}}\leq C_{2}\left(\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)^{1/{p_{\mathsf{e}}}}+1\right),

(3.83)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\in[0,T]
and
\mathsf{e}\in E(\mathsf{G})
.
On the other hand, by the Trace Theorem (applied to each segment, and adding the corresponding inequalities), there is a constant
C_{3}>0
such that

\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}))}\leq C_{3}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)^{1/p_{\mathsf{e}}}+\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)^{1/p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right),

(3.84)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\in[0,T]
.
And, on account of (
3.83
), there exists a constant
C_{4}>0
such that

\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}))}\leq C_{4}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)^{1/p_{\mathsf{e}}}+1\right)\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\,t\in[0,T].

(3.85)

From (
3.81
), (
3.83
) and (
3.85
), we have that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)dt\leq\int_{\mathsf{G}}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{0})\\
\\
\displaystyle+C_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[f_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}}\right)^{1/{p_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)^{1/{p_{\mathsf{e}}}}+1\right)dt\\
\displaystyle+C_{4}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\|\omega_{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G}))}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\left(\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)^{1/p_{\mathsf{e}}}+1\right)dt.\end{array}

(3.86)

Therefore, using Young’s inequality, and taking into account theintegrability of the data, there exists a constant
C_{5}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\right)dt\leq C_{5}\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.87)

Consequently, from (
3.87
) and (
3.83
),
a constant
C_{6}>0
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}\Big(|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}+|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\Big)\right)dt\leq C_{6}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.88)

Thus, by this estimation , there exists a subsequence, denoted again
\{u_{n}\}
, such that

u_{n}\to u\quad\hbox{weakly in }L^{\overline{p}}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}))\quad\hbox{as }n\to+\infty,

(3.89)

By (
3.75
),
we have

v_{n}\to v\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathsf{G}).

Then, since
v_{n}=\overline{\gamma}(u_{n})
, having in mind the above convergence and (
3.89
), by
[
10
, Lemma G]
, we get that

v=\overline{\gamma}(u).

We denote
{\bf a}_{n}
the function in
(0,T)\times\mathsf{G}
, such that

[{\bf a}_{n}(t)]_{e}=|[u_{n}(t)^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{e}}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[u_{n}(t)^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{e}}.

Since
\{u^{\prime}_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{\overline{p}}((0,T)\times\mathsf{G})
, it follows that
\{{\bf a}_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{\overline{p^{\prime}}}((0,T)\times\mathsf{G})
, so we can assume that

{\bf a}_{n}\to\Phi\quad\hbox{weakly in}\ \ L^{\overline{p^{\prime}}}((0,T)\times\mathsf{G}))\ \hbox{as}\ n\to+\infty.

From (
3.77
), given
\psi\in W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{1,1}(\mathsf{G}))\cap L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}))
with
\psi(0)=\psi(T)=0
, we have (define
v_{n}(t)=v_{0}
for
t<0
):

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}[\psi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\,dxdt+\int_{\mathsf{G}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{v_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t-T/n)}{T/n}\psi(t)\,dxdt\\
\\
=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}f_{n}(t)\psi(t)\,dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega_{n}(t,\mathsf{v})\psi(t,\mathsf{v})dt.\end{array}

(3.90)

Now

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{v_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t-T/n)}{T/n}\psi(t)\,dxdt\\
\\
\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(-\int_{\mathsf{G}}\int_{0}^{T-T/n}v_{n}(t)\frac{\psi(t+T/n)-\psi(t)}{T/n}\,dxdt\\
\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathsf{G}}\int_{T-T/n}^{T}\frac{v_{n}(t)\psi(t)}{T/n}\,dxdt-\int_{\mathsf{G}}\int_{0}^{T/n}\frac{v_{0}\psi(t)}{T/n}\,dxdt\Big)\\
\\
\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}v(t)\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\,dxdt.\end{array}

Therefore, taking limits in (
3.90
) as
n\to+\infty
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\Phi(t)\psi^{\prime}(t)-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}v(t)\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}f(t)\psi(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega(t,\mathsf{v})\psi(t,\mathsf{v})

(3.91)

Thus, to finish the proof we only need show that

[\Phi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}=|[u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}[\psi(t]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\quad\forall\,\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G}).

(3.92)

To do that, let us first prove the following inequality,

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\,\,\mathrm{d}xdt\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\Phi(t)u(t)^{\prime}.

(3.93)

Thanks to (
3.80
) and Fatou’s Lemma, we have

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}}\,\,\mathrm{d}xdt\\
\\
\leq-\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}(j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(T))-j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{0}))\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}f(t)u(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega(t,\mathsf{v})u(t,\mathsf{v}).\end{array}

(3.94)

On the other hand (
3.91
) can be rewritten as

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}v(t)\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\int_{0}^{T}(F(t),\psi(t)),

(3.95)

where
F
is given by

(F(t),\psi(t))=\int_{\mathsf{G}}\Phi(t)\psi^{\prime}(t)-\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{G}}f(t)\psi(t)-\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega(t,\mathsf{v})\psi(t,\mathsf{v})

Let us see now that

-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{G}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(t))=(F,u)\quad\hbox{in}\ \ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(]0,T[).

(3.96)

Indeed, given
0\leq\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(]0,T[
and
\tau>0
small, we define

\eta_{\tau}(t):=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t}^{t+\tau}u(s)\varphi(s)ds.

Taking
\eta_{\tau}
as test function in (
3.95
), we get

\int_{0}^{T}(F(t),\eta_{\tau}(t))=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}v(t)\frac{\partial\eta_{\tau}}{\partial t}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}v(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)

=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}v(t)\frac{u(t+\tau)\varphi(t+\tau)-u(t)\varphi(t)}{\tau}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\frac{v(t-\tau)-v(t)}{\tau}u(t)\varphi(t).

Now,

u(t)=\overline{\gamma}^{-1}(v(t))=\partial j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(t)),

hence

u(t)(v(t-\tau)-v(t))\leq j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(t-\tau))-j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(t)).

Thus, we obtain that

\int_{0}^{T}(F(t),\eta_{\tau}(t))\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{T}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(t-\tau))-j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(t))\varphi(t)=\int_{0}^{T}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(t))\frac{\varphi(t+\tau)-\varphi(t)}{\tau}.

Then, taking limits as
\tau\to 0^{+}
, we get

\int_{0}^{T}(F(t),u(t))\leq\int_{0}^{T}j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(t))\varphi^{\prime}(t).

Taking now

\overline{\eta}_{\tau}(t):=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t}^{t+\tau}u(s-\tau)\varphi(s)ds,

and arguing as above we get the another inequality, and we conclude the proof of (
3.96
).

Integrating from
0
to
T
in (
3.96
), we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\Phi(t)u^{\prime}(t)=-\int_{\mathsf{G}}(j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v(T))-j^{*}_{\overline{\gamma}}(v_{0}))\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}f(t)\psi(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{G})}\omega(t,\mathsf{v})\psi(t,\mathsf{v}).

Hence, using (
3.91
) we obtain (
3.93
).

Finally, to prove (
3.92
) we apply the Minty-Browder’s method. Indeed, for
\rho\in L^{\overline{p}}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}))
, we have

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}[\rho(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[\rho(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}([u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}-[\rho(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime})\,dxdt\\
\\
\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}([u_{n}(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}-[\rho(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime})\,dxdt,\end{array}

so that, passing to the limit and using (
3.93
), we get

\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}[\rho(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[\rho(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}([u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}-[\rho(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime})\,dxdt\\
\\
\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\Phi(t)(u(t)^{\prime}-\rho(t)^{\prime})\,dxdt.

Then, taking
\rho=u\pm\lambda\xi
for
\lambda>0
and
\xi\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G}))
, we get

\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|([u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}+\lambda[\xi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}})^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}([u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}+\lambda[\xi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}})^{\prime}[\xi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\,dxdt\geq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\Phi(t)\xi(t)^{\prime}\,dxdt,

and

\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|([u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}-\lambda[\xi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}})^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}([u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}-\lambda[\xi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}})^{\prime}[\xi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\,dxdt\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\Phi(t)\xi(t)^{\prime}\,dxdt,

so that, letting
\lambda\to 0
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}|[u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}|^{p_{\mathsf{e}}-2}[u(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}[\xi(t)]_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}\,dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{G}}\Phi(t)\xi(t)^{\prime}\,dxdt

for all
\xi\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{1,\overline{p}}(\mathsf{G})),

which implies that (
3.92
) holds.
∎
