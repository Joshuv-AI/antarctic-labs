Abstract
. In this paper, we study minimizers of the Choné–Rochet variational problem in dimension two. We first establish global C 1 C^{1} regularity on arbitrary bounded convex domains, and then prove global C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} regularity on bounded strictly convex domains or, more generally, whenever the zero set of u u has positive measure. Next, we construct smooth bounded convex domains with a flat boundary segment for which no prescribed modulus of continuity controls the gradient; this shows that, without additional geometric assumptions, global C 1 C^{1} regularity is optimal. Finally, we prove that the tamed free boundary (that is, the interface between the strictly convex and non-strictly convex regions of the solution) is locally a C 1 C^{1} embedded curve, significantly strengthening previously known regularity results.

1.1.
Motivation of the model

The minimization problem (
1.1
) is a basic instance of the Choné–Rochet model,
a foundational framework in mechanism design and nonlinear pricing.
A monopolist offers products
y\in\Lambda=[0,\infty)^{n}
together with a price schedule

v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and seeks to maximize profit under asymmetric information.
In the basic setting considered here, the production cost is

c(y)=\tfrac{1}{2}|y|^{2},\qquad y\in\Lambda,

and customer preferences are encoded by

b(x,y)=x\cdot y.

We also assume that the density of customer types is uniform.

Given a price schedule
v
with
v(0)=0
,
the zero product is available as an outside option at zero cost.
Hence the associated indirect utility

u(x)=\sup_{y\in\Lambda}\bigl(x\cdot y-v(y)\bigr)

is automatically nonnegative, which explains the constraint
u\geq 0
in the definition of

\mathcal{K}(\Omega)
.
Moreover,
u
is convex and differentiable almost everywhere.
Whenever
u
is differentiable at
x\in\Omega
, the maximizing product is

y(x)=Du(x),

and

v(y(x))=x\cdot Du(x)-u(x).

Therefore the monopolist’s profit is

\int_{\Omega}\bigl(v(y(x))-c(y(x))\bigr)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(x\cdot Du(x)-u(x)-\tfrac{1}{2}|Du(x)|^{2}\right)\,dx.

Since

x\cdot Du(x)-\tfrac{1}{2}|Du(x)|^{2}=-\tfrac{1}{2}|Du(x)-x|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|x|^{2},

maximizing profit is equivalent, up to the additive constant

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\,dx
, to minimizing (
1.1
).
In
[
17
]
the authors study in detail the minimizer of (
1.1
)
for the family of square domains
\{(a,a+1)^{2}\}_{a\geq 0}
, an important example introduced by
Rochet and Choné
[
20
]
.

1.2.
A related work

Independently and concurrently with this work, the recent preprint
[
15
]
studies the regularity of the free boundary in the same model and shows that it is a continuous curve of Hausdorff dimension one, which is
C^{\alpha}
for every
\alpha<1
away from a discrete set of singular points (see in particular their Theorem 1.1).
In contrast, our Theorem
1.4
shows that singular points do not occur and that the free boundary is locally a
C^{1}
embedded curve. As observed by the authors of
[
15
]
, our result combines with the argument in the proof of their
[
15
, Proposition 4.6]
to imply that the tamed free boundary is smooth outside a closed nowhere dense subset.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded convex domain, and let
u
be a minimizer of (
1.1

x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
[x_{0}]
is a segment
I\subset\Omega
whose endpoints
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}
lie on
\partial\Omega
. Let
P^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime\prime}
be supporting lines to
\Omega
at
x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime\prime}
, and let
T
be the (possibly degenerate) triangular region enclosed by
I
and the two lines
P^{\prime},P^{\prime\prime}
. Then

\{u=0\}\cap\overline{\Omega}\subset T\cap\overline{\Omega}.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, suppose the conclusion fails. Let

S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Omega\cap T,

so that
S
is convex and
I\subset\partial S\cap\Omega
. Let

\ell(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u(x_{0})+Du(x_{0})\cdot(x-x_{0}),

and define

\bar{u}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\ell(x),&x\in S,\\[2.84526pt]
u(x),&x\in\Omega\setminus S.\end{cases}

We first record two basic facts.

(i)
\bar{u}
is convex.

This follows easily since
\ell
is a supporting affine function for
u
, and
\ell=u
on the whole segment
I=[x_{0}]
.

(ii)
\bar{u}\geq 0
on
\Omega
.

By the contradiction assumption there exists

y\in\{u=0\}\cap(\overline{\Omega}\setminus T).

Suppose there exists
x\in S
with
\ell(x)<0
. Since

x\in T
and

, the segment
[x,y]
has to intersect
I
at some point

. Since
\ell
is affine and
\ell(y)\leq u(y)=0
, this implies
\ell(z)<0
. On the other hand,
z\in I=[x_{0}]
, hence
\ell(z)=u(z)\geq 0
, a contradiction. This proves that

\ell\geq 0
on
S
, and consequently
\bar{u}\geq 0
on all of
\Omega
.

Hence, we have proved that
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{K}(\Omega)
. Also, we note that
\bar{u}\not\equiv u
(otherwise

\displaystyle 0<L[\bar{u};\Omega]-L[u;\Omega]

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\left[\frac{1}{2}\bigl(|D\bar{u}|^{2}-|Du|^{2}\bigr)-x\cdot(D\bar{u}-Du)+(\bar{u}-u)\right]\,dx

where we used
\bar{u}-u\leq 0
on
S
.

We first bound

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\bigl(|D\bar{u}|^{2}-|Du|^{2}\bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}|D\bar{u}-Du|^{2}\,dx+\int_{S}D\bar{u}\cdot(D\bar{u}-Du)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S}D\bar{u}\cdot D(\bar{u}-u)\,dx.

Observe now that
\bar{u}
is affine on

D\bar{u}\equiv Du(x_{0})\qquad\text{on }S.

Integrating by parts and using that

A one-dimensional region near the left flat side

3.4.

From now on, assume that Lemma

(

is constant along vertical

-direction on

is one-dimensional there:

(3.19)

where

,

v(x_{1})=0\ \text{for }a\leq x_{1}\leq a+\delta,\qquad v(x_{1})>0\ \text{for }a+\delta<x_{1}\leq a+\delta_{0}.

Define

Then

Because

is even in

, it is convenient to restrict to the upper half-domain

\Omega_{\epsilon,a}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Omega_{\epsilon,a}\cap\{x_{2}\geq 0\}

Then, on the region
\Omega_{\epsilon,a}^{+}\cap\{a+\delta<x_{1}<a+\delta_{0}\}

reduces to

(3.20)

\int_{a+\delta}^{a+\delta_{0}}\left[\frac{1}{2}h(x_{1})\,(v^{\prime}(x_{1})-x_{1})^{2}+\frac{1}{6}h(x_{1})^{3}+v(x_{1})\,h(x_{1})\right]\,dx_{1},

and the corresponding Euler–Lagrange equation is

(3.21)

-\bigl[(v^{\prime}(x_{1})-x_{1})h(x_{1})\bigr]^{\prime}+h(x_{1})=0\qquad\text{for }a+\delta<x_{1}<a+\delta_{0}.

Integrating once, we obtain

(3.22)

(v^{\prime}(x_{1})-x_{1})\,h(x_{1})=G(x_{1})+\epsilon x_{1}+C_{\delta}

for some constant

, hence

(3.23)

v^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})=2-\frac{g^{\prime}(x_{1}-a)\bigl[G(x_{1})+\epsilon x_{1}+C_{\delta}\bigr]}{\bigl[g(x_{1}-a)+\epsilon\bigr]^{2}}.

Since

on
[a,a+\delta]
and

is

, we have the matching condition

Using (
3.22

Substituting (
3.24

) yields

v^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})=2+\frac{-G(x_{1})+G(a+\delta)-\epsilon x_{1}+(a+\delta)\bigl(2\epsilon+g(\delta)\bigr)}{\bigl[g(x_{1}-a)+\epsilon\bigr]^{2}}\,g^{\prime}(x_{1}-a).

In the special case
\delta=0
, we have
g(\delta)=g(0)=0
and
G(a+\delta)=G(a)=0
, hence

(3.26)

v^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})=2+\frac{-G(x_{1})+\epsilon(2a-x_{1})}{\bigl[g(x_{1}-a)+\epsilon\bigr]^{2}}\,g^{\prime}(x_{1}-a)\to+\infty\qquad\text{as }x_{1}\to a^{+},

because

as

and the numerator stays positive for

sufficiently close to

.
Therefore, if for some choice of parameters one has

in (

\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}=+\infty.

Actually, when

In particular, if we define

N(y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(a+y)g(y)+\int_{0}^{y}g(t)\,dt+2\epsilon y,\qquad 0\leq y<\delta_{0},

then

Since

, it follows that

|v^{\prime}(a+y)-v^{\prime}(a)|=\frac{|N(y)|}{g(y)+\epsilon}=\frac{a}{\epsilon}g(y)+O_{a,\epsilon}(y).

Thus, for any modulus of continuity

then

also fails to have the same modulus of continuity.

3.5.

We start with the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
w
be a minimizer of
L[\cdot;\,\Omega_{\epsilon,\,a}]
and set

cannot be a subset of the

Proof.

We prove the lemma by a contradiction argument.

Suppose that

[
1
]
). For

Note that

Then,
by the minimality of

applying the variational identity (

\displaystyle 0

{x_{2}=0}

Combined with (

∎

\frac{h_{r}}{r^{2}}\leq C\Bigl(\|Dw\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}+\sup_{\Omega_{\epsilon,a}}|y|\Bigr)\left(1+\frac{\mathscr{H}^{1}(\partial S_{r}\cap\partial\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}{|S_{r}|}\right).

Furthermore, by
[
17
, Proof of Theorem 4.1]
, for
r
small the boundary trace

A_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\partial S_{r}\cap\partial\Omega_{\epsilon,a}

is contained in a fixed boundary chart around
x^{+}
, and

x
satisfies

|x-x^{+}|\geq c(\epsilon).

Let
P^{+}
be the tangent line to the upper graph at
x^{+}
.
Since
|\tau\cdot\nu^{+}|\geq 1/\sqrt{2}
, we get

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dist}}(x,P^{+})\geq c(\epsilon).

If
A_{r}\neq\emptyset
, then by convexity

\operatorname{co}(A_{r}\cup\{x\})\subset\overline{S}_{r}.

Also, because
A_{r}
lies in a boundary chart with uniformly bounded slope, its length is
comparable to the length of its orthogonal projection onto
P^{+}
.
Therefore

|S_{r}|\geq\bigl|\operatorname{co}(A_{r}\cup\{x\})\bigr|\geq c(\epsilon)\,\mathscr{H}^{1}(A_{r}).

Hence

\mathscr{H}^{1}(\partial S_{r}\cap\partial\Omega_{\epsilon,a})\leq C(\epsilon)\,|S_{r}|,

and therefore

(3.33)

h_{r}\leq C\bigl(\|Dw\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}+a+3\bigr)\,r^{2}.

This implies that

\|D^{2}w(x)\|\leq C\bigl(\|Dw\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}+a+3\bigr)

at every point of second differentiability.

Case D:
x\in\partial\{w=0\}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon,a}\cap\{x_{1}\geq a+1-\eta_{\epsilon}\}
.

In this case, since
w(x)=0
, then
x\notin\Omega_{1}
. In particular,
x
belongs to the closure of a connected
component
U
of

\Omega_{\epsilon,a}\setminus(\Omega_{1}\setminus\Omega^{\prime}_{1}).

Applying Proposition
2.2
, we get

\|D^{2}w\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{U}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}\leq C_{0},

therefore
D^{2}w
is bounded at
x
.

Combining Cases A–D, we conclude that

\|D^{2}w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a}\cap\{x_{1}\geq a+1-\eta_{\epsilon}\})}\leq C_{0}\bigl(\|Dw\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}+a+3\bigr).

Now, using this bound and connecting any point in
\{x_{1}>a+1-\eta_{\epsilon}\}
to a point in
\{x_{1}\leq a+1-\eta_{\epsilon}\}
by a segment of length

\leq 2\eta_{\epsilon}
, we deduce (for
a
large)

\|Dw\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}\leq C_{0}\bigl(\|Dw\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}+a+3\bigr)\eta_{\epsilon}+(a+1).

Since
\eta_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{1000C_{0}}\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{1000C_{0}}
, this yields

(3.34)

\|Dw\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})}\leq 3a\qquad\text{for $a$ sufficiently large}.

Finally, define the admissible competitor

\hat{w}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=w(x)+\varphi(x),\qquad\varphi(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{1}-a.

Since
x_{1}\geq a
on
\Omega_{\epsilon,a}
, we have
\hat{w}\geq w\geq 0
and
\hat{w}
is convex, hence
\hat{w}\in\mathcal{K}(\Omega_{\epsilon,a})
.
A direct computation gives

\displaystyle L[w;\Omega_{\epsilon,a}]-L[\hat{w};\Omega_{\epsilon,a}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon,a}}\left(-D\varphi\cdot(Dw-x)-\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{2}-\varphi\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon,a}}\Bigl((x-Dw)\cdot e_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-(x_{1}-a)\Bigr)\,dx.

On
\{x_{1}\leq a+1-\eta_{\epsilon}\}
, the one-dimensional structure (
3.30
) and the explicit formula
(
3.22
)–(
3.24
) imply

(x-Dw)\cdot e_{1}\geq c\,\epsilon a

for
a
large, while on the complement strip

\{x_{1}>a+1-\eta_{\epsilon}\}
we have the crude bound

\bigl|(x-Dw)\cdot e_{1}\bigr|\leq 5a

by (
3.34
).
Since the strip
\{x_{1}>a+1-\eta_{\epsilon}\}
has area
O(\eta_{\epsilon})
and
|\Omega_{\epsilon,a}|
is bounded below,
we conclude that

L[w;\Omega_{\epsilon,a}]-L[\hat{w};\Omega_{\epsilon,a}]>0

for all sufficiently large
a
, contradicting the minimality of
w
.
Therefore
Dw(z_{0})\cdot e_{1}>0
for
a
large.
∎

3.6.
Existence of a blow-up configuration

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
u
be the minimizer of
L[\,\cdot\,;\Omega_{\epsilon,a}]
. For any sufficiently small
\epsilon
, there exists
a>0
such that

u(z_{0})=0
and
Du(z_{0})=0
, and there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

u(x_{1},x_{2})=v(x_{1})>0

for some one-dimensional convex function
v
whenever
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega_{\epsilon,a}
and
a<x_{1}<a+\delta_{0}
.
Moreover, given a modulus of continuity
\omega
, if

(3.35)

\frac{g(r)}{\omega(r)}\to\infty\quad\text{ as}\quad r\to 0,

then
Du
also fails to have the same modulus of continuity.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon<\frac{1}{240C_{1}}
.
By Lemma
3.6
, choose
a_{0}\geq 30C_{1}
sufficiently large such that the minimizer
u_{a_{0}}
of

L[\,\cdot\,;\Omega_{\epsilon,a_{0}}]
satisfies

Du_{a_{0}}(a_{0},0)\cdot e_{1}=\alpha_{0}>0.

Since
\Omega_{\epsilon,a}
is obtained from
\Omega_{\epsilon,0}
by translation in the

-direction, and the functional
depends on
(Dw-x)
in a translation-covariant way (cf.
[
17
]
), one checks that if

is a minimizer on

\Omega_{\epsilon,a}
, then for each
\beta>0
the function

\Omega_{\epsilon,a-\beta}

Set

Then
\tilde{u}
is a minimizer of

, and at

we have

\tilde{u}(z_{0})=u_{a_{0}}(a_{0},0)=0,\qquad D\tilde{u}(z_{0})=Du_{a_{0}}(a_{0},0)-\alpha_{0}e_{1}=0.

Moreover, since

(Du_{a_{0}}(a_{0},0)-a_{0}e_{1})\cdot(-e_{1})\geq 0,

we have
\alpha_{0}\leq a_{0}
, hence

.

We claim that

in the interior of
\Omega_{\epsilon,a}
.
Indeed, since

is strictly positive in the interior in a neighborhood to the right of the left boundary,

implies that the contact set of

with its tangent plane at

has zero Lebesgue measure. By translation covariance, the same is true for the contact set
[z_{0}]
of
\tilde{u}
.
Now suppose by contradiction that

\{\tilde{u}=0\}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon,a}\neq\emptyset.

Since
\tilde{u}(z_{0})=0
and

\{\tilde{u}=0\}\subset[z_{0}].

Observe now that, because

is even in

By convexity of

|[z_{0}]|=0
.
This proves that

\{\tilde{u}=0\}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon,a}

is a segment contained in the

-axis with endpoint

.
However, this is impossible by Lemma
3.5
.

Thus
\tilde{u}>0
in the interior of

3.2
to
\tilde{u}
to obtain

Replacing

by

from now on, we may therefore assume that

Applying Lemma

4.
Free boundary between
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and
\Omega_{2}

In this section we assume that
\partial\Omega
is of class
C^{1,1}
and that
u\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Recall that
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
is the union of leaves with one endpoint on
\partial\Omega

and the other in the interior of
\Omega
. The tamed free boundary
\mathcal{T}
was
defined in (
1.2
). Fix
z_{0}\in\mathcal{T}
and set

x_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[z_{0}]\cap\partial\Omega.

Parametrize
\partial\Omega
locally by a
C^{1,1}
map

\gamma\colon(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\partial\Omega,\qquad\gamma(0)=x_{0}.

By
[
17
, Lemma 6.1 and Remark 6.4]
, the leaves
[\gamma(t)]
spread out as they
leave the boundary and foliate a neighborhood of
x_{0}
. In particular, every point
x

sufficiently close to
z_{0}
can be written uniquely as

x=\gamma(t)+r\,\xi(t),

for a unique pair
(t,r)
, where
\xi(t)
is the unit direction of the leaf
[\gamma(t)]
and

R(t)>0
is its length, so that the leaf terminates at
\gamma(t)+R(t)\xi(t)
.

Moreover, the following Euler–Lagrange equations hold near
\gamma(0)=x_{0}

(see
[
17
]
):

(4.1)

R^{2}(t)\,|\dot{\xi}(t)|=2\,|\dot{\gamma}(t)|\,\bigl(Du(\gamma(t))-\gamma(t)\bigr)\cdot\nu_{\Omega}(\gamma(t)),

(4.2)

3-\Delta u=\frac{3r-2R(t)}{r+|\dot{\xi}(t)|^{-1}\,\xi(t)\times\dot{\gamma}(t)}=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(x).

Here
a\times b
denotes the scalar cross product in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The raywise change of variables

(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(r,t)

is locally bi-Lipschitz by
[
17
, Lemma 6.3 and Corollary 6.6]
.

Let
v
be the minimal convex extension of
u
restricted to a connected component of

B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
, and set

U\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u-v.

Then
U\geq 0
and

(4.3)

\Delta U=f(x)\,\chi_{\{U>0\}}\geq c_{0}\,\chi_{\{U>0\}}

for some
c_{0}>0
. Moreover, for some sufficiently small
\rho>0
,

\{U>0\}\cap B_{\rho}(z_{0})=\Omega_{2}\cap B_{\rho}(z_{0}),\qquad\{U=0\}\cap B_{\rho}(z_{0})=\Omega_{1}\cap B_{\rho}(z_{0}).

Since
R
is continuous by
[
17
, Lemma 7.4]
, it follows from (
4.1
) that

|\dot{\xi}|
is continuous, and then
f
is continuous by (
4.2
). Hence
U
solves a
classical obstacle problem with continuous right-hand side, uniformly bounded away from
0
.

Therefore, by Caffarelli’s alternative
(see
[
3
,
4
]
and
[
2
, Theorem 0.3]
), for every
x\in\mathcal{T}
one has

\text{either }\ \lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}|}{|B_{r}(x)|}=0,\qquad\text{or}\qquad\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}|}{|B_{r}(x)|}=\frac{1}{2}.

4.1.
Regularity at
\frac{1}{2}
-density points

Let
x\in\mathcal{T}
be a point with density
\frac{1}{2}
.
Then the free boundary is a Reifenberg vanishing set in a neighborhood of
x
.
More precisely, there exists
\rho>0
such that for every compact set

K\subset B_{\rho}(x)
,

\lim_{r\to 0}\theta_{K}(r)=0,\qquad\theta_{K}(r)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{0<s\leq r}\,\sup_{y\in\mathcal{T}\cap K}\frac{d_{H}\bigl(P_{y,s}\cap B_{s}(y),\,\mathcal{T}\cap B_{s}(y)\bigr)}{s},

where
P_{y,s}
is a line through
y
and
d_{H}
denotes the Hausdorff distance.
Moreover, the set of
\frac{1}{2}
-density points is relatively open in

\mathcal{T}\cap B_{\rho}(z_{0})
.

By Reifenberg’s theorem
[
19
]

(see also
[
8
, Section 3]
),

\mathcal{T}
can be locally parametrized by a bi-Hölder homeomorphism.
Since the raywise change of variables is bi-Lipschitz

[
17
, Corollary 6.6]
, the graph representation of
\mathcal{T}
in

(t,r)
-coordinates implies that
R
is locally Hölder.
In addition, since leaves in
\Omega_{1}
do not intersect, the map

t\longmapsto\gamma(t)+R(t)\,\xi(t)

is locally injective.

We now prove uniqueness of blow-ups at a
\frac{1}{2}
-density point.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{T}
be a point with density
\frac{1}{2}
. Then the blow-up limit

P_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{T}-x}{r}

exists and is unique. Moreover, if
P_{x}
is not tangent to the leaf
[x]
at
x
,
then
\mathcal{T}
is smooth in a neighborhood of
x
.

Proof.

Let
I\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[x]
and denote by
\xi(x)
the unit direction of
I
.
For any sequence
r_{k}\downarrow 0
, after passing to a subsequence we have

\mathcal{T}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\mathcal{T}-x}{r_{k}}\to P

locally in Hausdorff distance, where
P
is a line because
x
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-density point.
Let
\nu_{P}
be the unit normal to
P
, chosen so that
\nu_{P}\cdot\xi(x)\geq 0
.

If for every such sequence one has
\nu_{P}\cdot\xi(x)=0
, then
P
must be the line containing

I
, and the blow-up is unique.

Assume now that there exists a sequence
r_{k}\downarrow 0
for which the limiting line
P

satisfies

(4.4)

\nu_{P}\cdot\xi(x)\geq c_{\ast}>0.

Then there exists
k_{0}
such that

(4.5)

\nu_{P_{x,r_{k}}}\cdot\xi(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\,c_{\ast}\qquad\text{for all }k\geq k_{0},

where
P_{x,r}
denotes a line through
x
realizing the Reifenberg flatness at scale
r
.
Set

r_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=r_{k_{0}}.

Because of the non-tangency (
4.5
), the free boundary can be represented as a graph
of the leaf-length function
R
over the leaf parameter
t
in
B_{r_{0}}(x)
, with Lipschitz
constant depending only on
c_{\ast}
.
In particular,

|R(y)-R(x)|\leq C(c_{\ast})\,r_{0}\qquad\text{for all }y\in\mathcal{T}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x).

Using (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), this implies

(4.6)

|f(y)-f(x)|\leq C_{1}\,r_{0}\qquad\text{for all }y\in B_{r_{0}}(x),

for some constant
C_{1}
depending only on the local geometry of
\Omega
,

\|\gamma\|_{C^{1,1}}
,
\|u\|_{C^{1,1}}
, and
c_{\ast}
.

Since
\mathcal{T}
is Reifenberg vanishing near
x
, given
\delta_{0}>0
we may choose
r_{0}

small enough so that

(4.7)

\theta_{\overline{B}_{\rho}}(r_{0})\leq\delta_{0}.

Let

P_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P_{x,r_{0}}.

Then

(4.8)

d_{H}\bigl(P_{0}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x),\,\mathcal{T}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x)\bigr)\leq\theta_{\overline{B}_{\rho}}(r_{0})\,r_{0}.

Set
\rho_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1/2
.
By (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we can apply
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
to obtain,
for
r_{0}
sufficiently small, a line
P_{1}
through
x
such that

(4.9)

d_{H}\bigl(P_{1}\cap B_{\rho_{0}r_{0}}(x),\,\mathcal{T}\cap B_{\rho_{0}r_{0}}(x)\bigr)\leq C\,C_{1}\,r_{0}^{2}.

Combining (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), and rescaling to unit size, yields

d_{H}\bigl(P_{1}\cap B_{1}(x),\,P_{0}\cap B_{1}(x)\bigr)\leq\frac{2C\,C_{1}r_{0}+2\theta_{\overline{B}_{\rho}}(r_{0})}{\rho_{0}}.

Iterating this argument at scales
\rho_{0}^{m}r_{0}
, we obtain a sequence of lines
P_{m}
through
x

such that

d_{H}\bigl(P_{m+1}\cap B_{\rho_{0}^{m+1}r_{0}}(x),\,\mathcal{T}\cap B_{\rho_{0}^{m+1}r_{0}}(x)\bigr)\leq C\,C_{1}\,\rho_{0}^{2m}\,r_{0}^{2},

and

d_{H}\bigl(P_{m+1}\cap B_{1}(x),\,P_{m}\cap B_{1}(x)\bigr)\leq\frac{2C\,C_{1}\,\rho_{0}^{2m-2}\,r_{0}^{2}}{\rho_{0}}.

Hence
P_{m}
converges to a limit line
P_{\infty}
.

Moreover,

d_{H}\bigl(P_{m}\cap B_{1}(x),\,P_{\infty}\cap B_{1}(x)\bigr)\leq\sum_{l=m}^{\infty}\frac{2C\,C_{1}\,\rho_{0}^{2l-2}\,r_{0}^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\leq\frac{2C\,C_{1}\,\rho_{0}^{2m-3}\,r_{0}^{2}}{1-\rho_{0}^{2}},

and

(4.10)

d_{H}\bigl(P_{0}\cap B_{1}(x),\,P_{\infty}\cap B_{1}(x)\bigr)\leq\frac{2C\,C_{1}r_{0}+2\theta_{\overline{B}_{\rho}}(r_{0})}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{2C\,C_{1}\,\rho_{0}^{-1}\,r_{0}^{2}}{1-\rho_{0}^{2}}.

Therefore, given
\varepsilon>0
, choosing
r_{0}
sufficiently small gives

(4.11)

d_{H}\bigl(P_{0}\cap B_{1}(x),\,P_{\infty}\cap B_{1}(x)\bigr)\leq\varepsilon.

Taking
\varepsilon\ll c_{\ast}
and using (
4.5
), we infer that

(4.12)

\nu_{P_{\infty}}\cdot\xi(x)\geq\frac{1}{4}\,c_{\ast}>0.

Thus, under (
4.4
), the blow-up is unique and equals
P_{\infty}
.

Finally, once (
4.12
) holds, the graph function
R
is locally Lipschitz, hence in
particular locally Hölder. By (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), the coefficient
f
is then locally
Hölder continuous near
x
. Standard elliptic regularity yields

u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega_{2}\cap B_{\eta}(x))

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\eta>0
, and the regularity theory for the obstacle problem
implies that
\mathcal{T}
is a local
C^{1,\alpha}
graph near
x

(see
[
3
,
4
,
11
,
12
,
2
]
).
Bootstrapping as in
[
17
, Proposition 7.6]
gives local smoothness of
\mathcal{T}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

The tamed free boundary
\mathcal{T}
is
C^{1}
at every
\frac{1}{2}
-density point.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in\mathcal{T}
with density
\frac{1}{2}
.
If

\nu_{P_{x_{0}}}\cdot\xi(x_{0})>0,

then Lemma
4.1
already shows that

is smooth in a neighborhood of

.

and

C^{1}
regularity at
x_{0}
.

Suppose the claim fails. Passing to a subsequence, there exists
c_{\ast}>0
such that

(4.13)

\nu_{P_{x_{k}}}\cdot\xi(x_{k})\geq 2c_{\ast}\qquad\text{for all }k.

There exists
r_{\ast}>0
such that

(4.14)

\nu_{P_{x_{k},r}}\cdot\xi(x_{k})\geq c_{\ast}\qquad\text{for all }0<r\leq r_{\ast}\ \text{and all }k.

4.14
) failed, we could find
r_{m}\downarrow 0
and indices

such that

\nu_{P_{x_{k_{m}},r_{m}}}\cdot\xi(x_{k_{m}})<c_{\ast}.

P_{x_{k_{m}},r}\to P_{x_{k_{m}}}
as

) holds, there exists

\bar{r}_{m}\in(0,r_{m})

(4.15)

c_{\ast}\leq\nu_{P_{x_{k_{m}},\bar{r}_{m}}}\cdot\xi(x_{k_{m}})<\frac{5}{4}\,c_{\ast}.

4.11
) from the proof of Lemma
4.1
at the point

and
scale
\bar{r}_{m}
, with
\varepsilon\ll c_{\ast}
.
For

where

approximating
\mathcal{T}

\nu_{Q_{r}}\cdot\xi(x_{0})\geq c_{\ast}.

Therefore

at

.

4.2.
Rigidity at
0
-density points

We now turn to cusp-type blow-ups. The next argument proves the rigidity theorem
stated in the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Write

x=(x_{1},x_{2})=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta).

Since
u\geq 0
in
B_{1}
and
u=0
on

S=\{(x_{1},0)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}-1<x_{1}<0\},

every point of
S
is a local minimum. Hence

(4.16)

Du(x_{1},0)=0\qquad\text{for all }-1<x_{1}\leq 0.

Because
u\in C^{1,1}(B_{1})
, the gradient is globally Lipschitz: there exists
L\geq 1
such that

(4.17)

|Du(x)-Du(y)|\leq L|x-y|\qquad\forall x,y\in B_{1}.

Convexity implies
D^{2}u\geq 0,

while the identity
\Delta u=1
in
D
implies that each first derivative
\partial_{i}u

is harmonic in
D
.

Step 1: a one-sided lower bound for
\partial_{1}u
.

Let

y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2},0\Bigr)\in S.

Since
u
is convex, the gradient is monotone:

(Du(x)-Du(y))\cdot(x-y)\geq 0.

Using (
4.16
), this gives

\partial_{1}u(x)\Bigl(x_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)+\partial_{2}u(x)\,x_{2}\geq 0.

Thus, whenever
|x_{1}|\leq 1/4
,

\partial_{1}u(x)\geq-4x_{2}\,\partial_{2}u(x).

Now let
x\in\partial D\cap B_{1/4}
. Since the sides of
D
lie in
\{x_{1}<0\}
, the point

(x_{1},0)
belongs to
S
, and (
4.17
) together with (
4.16
) gives

|\partial_{2}u(x)|\leq|Du(x)-Du(x_{1},0)|\leq L|x_{2}|.

Hence

\partial_{1}u(x)\geq-4Lx_{2}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\partial D\cap B_{1/4}.

Set

C_{\ast}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=4L.

Since
u_{11}\geq 0
, for each fixed
x_{2}
the function

x_{1}\longmapsto\partial_{1}u(x_{1},x_{2})+C_{\ast}x_{2}^{2}

is nondecreasing. Therefore, choosing
r_{0}=r_{0}(a)\in(0,1/4]
so that every horizontal segment
through a point of
D\cap B_{r_{0}}
meets
\partial D\cap B_{1/4}
, the previous boundary estimate
propagates into the sector:

(4.18)

\partial_{1}u(x)\geq-C_{\ast}x_{2}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }x\in D\cap B_{r_{0}}.

Step 2: a harmonic lower barrier for
u_{11}
.

Fix
a^{\prime}\in(a,\pi/2)
and set

D^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(r,\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}-\pi+a^{\prime}<\theta<\pi-a^{\prime}\}\subset D,\qquad b\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\pi}{2(\pi-a^{\prime})}\in\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\Bigr),

\hat{w}(r,\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=r^{b}\cos(b\theta).

Then
\hat{w}
is harmonic in
D^{\prime}
, vanishes on
\partial D^{\prime}
, and is strictly positive in
D^{\prime}
.
Moreover,

(4.19)

\partial_{1}\hat{w}=\cos\theta\,\partial_{r}\hat{w}-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}\,\partial_{\theta}\hat{w}=b\,r^{b-1}\cos\bigl((b-1)\theta\bigr),\qquad|\partial_{1}\hat{w}|\leq b\,r^{b-1}.

Let

\hat{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u_{11}.

Then
\hat{v}
is harmonic and nonnegative in
D
.
If
\hat{v}\equiv 0
in
D
, then
\partial_{1}u
is harmonic and independent of
x_{1}
, hence

\partial_{1}u(x)=\alpha x_{2}+\beta

in
D
for some constants
\alpha,\beta
.
Since
\partial_{1}u=0
on
S
, we have
\beta=0
.
The convexity matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha\\
\alpha&u_{22}\end{pmatrix}

must be nonnegative semidefinite, so necessarily
\alpha=0
.
Hence
\partial_{1}u\equiv 0
in
D
.
Since now
\Delta u=u_{22}=1
in
D
, and
u(0)=|Du(0)|=0
, we conclude that

u(x)=\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{2}\qquad\text{in }D.

So it only remains to exclude the case
\hat{v}\not\equiv 0
.

Assume therefore that
\hat{v}\not\equiv 0
.
By the strong maximum principle,
\hat{v}>0
in
D
.
In particular,

m\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min_{\theta\in[-\pi+a^{\prime},\,\pi-a^{\prime}]}\hat{v}(1/2,\theta)>0.

Set

c_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=m\,2^{b},\qquad h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\hat{v}-c_{0}\hat{w}\quad\text{in }D^{\prime}\cap B_{1/2}.

Then
h
is harmonic. On the two sides of
D^{\prime}
one has
\hat{w}=0
, so
h\geq 0
there, while
on the circular arc
\partial B_{1/2}\cap\overline{D^{\prime}}
,

c_{0}\hat{w}\leq m\,2^{b}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)^{b}=m\leq\hat{v}.

Hence
h\geq 0
on
\partial(D^{\prime}\cap B_{1/2})
, and therefore

(4.20)

u_{11}(x)=\hat{v}(x)\geq c_{0}\hat{w}(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in D^{\prime}\cap B_{1/2}.

Step 3: a nonlinear subharmonic barrier.

Choose
\gamma\in(1,1/b)
and define

\delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1-b\gamma\in(0,1).

For
\varepsilon>0
, set

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varepsilon\,\hat{w}(x)^{\gamma}-(C_{\ast}+1)x_{2}^{2}.

Since
\hat{w}
is harmonic and positive in
D^{\prime}
,

\Delta(\hat{w}^{\gamma})=\gamma(\gamma-1)\hat{w}^{\gamma-2}|D\hat{w}|^{2}=\gamma(\gamma-1)b^{2}\,r^{b\gamma-2}\cos^{\gamma-2}(b\theta)\geq\gamma(\gamma-1)b^{2}\,r^{-(1+\delta)},

because
b\gamma-2=-(1+\delta)
and
\cos(b\theta)\in(0,1]
in
D^{\prime}
.
Since
\Delta(x_{2}^{2})=2
, it follows that

\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq\varepsilon\,\gamma(\gamma-1)b^{2}\,r^{-(1+\delta)}-2(C_{\ast}+1).

Now choose

(4.21)

\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A\,r_{0}^{1+\delta},\qquad A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{2(C_{\ast}+1)}{\gamma(\gamma-1)b^{2}}.

Then
\psi_{\varepsilon}
is subharmonic in
D^{\prime}\cap B_{r_{0}}
.

Set

U\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=D^{\prime}\cap\bigl(\{|x_{1}|<C_{0}r_{0}\}\times\{|x_{2}|<r_{0}\}\bigr),\qquad C_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\cot a^{\prime}+1,

and define

H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\partial_{1}u-\psi_{\varepsilon}.

Step 4:
H\geq 0
on
\partial U
.

(a) On the sides of
D^{\prime}
.

There
\hat{w}=0
, so by (
4.18
),

H=\partial_{1}u+(C_{\ast}+1)x_{2}^{2}\geq-C_{\ast}x_{2}^{2}+(C_{\ast}+1)x_{2}^{2}\geq 0.

(b) On the top and bottom edges.

We treat the top edge
\{x_{2}=r_{0}\}
, the bottom one being identical.
Set

t_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-r_{0}\cot a^{\prime},\qquad t_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\cot a^{\prime}+1)r_{0},

and define

F(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H(t,r_{0})\qquad\text{for }t\in[t_{\ell},t_{r}].

Using (
4.20
),

F^{\prime}(t)=u_{11}(t,r_{0})-\partial_{1}\psi_{\varepsilon}(t,r_{0})\geq c_{0}\hat{w}(t,r_{0})-\varepsilon\,\partial_{t}\bigl(\hat{w}(t,r_{0})^{\gamma}\bigr).

There exist constants
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}
, depending only on
a^{\prime}
, such that
for
t=t_{\ell}+s
one has

(4.22)

c_{1}\,r_{0}^{b-1}s\leq\hat{w}(t,r_{0})\leq c_{2}\,r_{0}^{b-1}s,\qquad 0\leq s\leq t_{r}-t_{\ell}.

Also, since
\hat{w}(t_{\ell},r_{0})=0
, (
4.18
) gives

F(t_{\ell})=\partial_{1}u(t_{\ell},r_{0})+(C_{\ast}+1)r_{0}^{2}\geq r_{0}^{2}.

Integrating from
t_{\ell}
to
t=t_{\ell}+s
, and using (
4.21
) and (
4.22
),
we obtain

\displaystyle F(t)

\displaystyle\geq r_{0}^{2}+c_{0}\int_{t_{\ell}}^{t}\hat{w}(\tau,r_{0})\,d\tau-\varepsilon\,\hat{w}(t,r_{0})^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\geq r_{0}^{2}+\frac{c_{0}c_{1}}{2}\,r_{0}^{b-1}s^{2}-Ac_{2}^{\gamma}\,r_{0}^{1+\delta+\gamma(b-1)}\,s^{\gamma}

(4.23)

\displaystyle=r_{0}^{2}+\frac{c_{0}c_{1}}{2}\,r_{0}^{b-1}s^{2}-Ac_{2}^{\gamma}\,r_{0}^{2-\gamma}\,s^{\gamma}.

Choose
\theta_{0}>0
small, depending only on
A,c_{2},\gamma
, so that

F\geq 0
on
[t_{\ell},t_{\ell}+\theta_{0}r_{0}]
.
On the complementary interval, the positive term in (
4.23
)
is of order
r_{0}^{1+b}
while the negative term is of order
r_{0}^{2}
;
since
1+b<2
, the positive contribution dominates for
r_{0}
sufficiently small.
Hence
F\geq 0
on the whole top edge.

(c) On the right side.

Fix
x_{2}\in[-r_{0},r_{0}]
and let

t_{\ell}(x_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-|x_{2}|\cot a^{\prime}

be the left endpoint of the horizontal section of
D^{\prime}
at height
x_{2}
.
Starting from the boundary value
H(t_{\ell}(x_{2}),x_{2})\geq 0
given by part (a), and repeating
the argument from part (b) along the horizontal segment from
t_{\ell}(x_{2})
to
t_{r}
, we obtain

H(t_{r},x_{2})\geq 0

for all
|x_{2}|\leq r_{0}
.
Therefore
H\geq 0
on
\partial U
.

Step 5: interior comparison and contradiction.

Inside
U
, the function
\partial_{1}u
is harmonic while
\psi_{\varepsilon}
is subharmonic,
so
H=\partial_{1}u-\psi_{\varepsilon}
is superharmonic.
Thus, by the minimum principle and Step 4 we deduce that

H\geq 0
inside
U
, and therefore

\partial_{1}u\geq\psi_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{in }U.

In particular, for
x=(x_{1},0)
with
x_{1}>0
sufficiently small,

\partial_{1}u(x_{1},0)\geq\varepsilon\,\hat{w}(x_{1},0)^{\gamma}=\varepsilon\,x_{1}^{b\gamma}.

Since
b\gamma<1
, this contradicts the Lipschitz continuity of
\partial_{1}u
at the origin
together with
\partial_{1}u(0)=0
.
Therefore the alternative
\hat{v}\not\equiv 0
is impossible, and we are in the rigid case

u(x)=\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{2}\qquad\text{in }D\cap B_{1}.

In particular,
u
cannot be strictly convex in
D\cap B_{1}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For a minimizer
u
, recall that
v
denotes the minimal convex extension of
u
restricted to a connected component of
B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
, and that
U\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u-v
satisfies the obstacle problem (
4.3
).

By applying Caffarelli’s alternative to
U
, every point of
\mathcal{T}
has density either
0
or
\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, Lemma
4.2
shows that
\mathcal{T}
is
C^{1}
at every
\frac{1}{2}
-density point.

It remains to exclude
0
-density points. Suppose by contradiction that
0\in\mathcal{T}
is a
0
-density point. Up to a rotation, we may assume that the leaf
I
containing
0
is parallel to the
x_{1}
-axis and that
u|_{I}=\ell
, where
\ell
is an affine function.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since the blow-up of
U
at a
0
-density point has constant Laplacian and is of the form
\frac{c_{0}}{2}x_{2}^{2}
for some
c_{0}>0
, the uniform convergence of the blow-up sequence implies that there exists
r_{\varepsilon}>0
such that, for every
0<r<r_{\varepsilon}
,

\Omega^{\prime}_{1}\cap B_{r}\subset B_{r}\cap\{x\in\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}-\varepsilon r\leq x_{1}\leq\varepsilon r,\ x_{2}\leq 0\}.

Therefore, given
a>0
, by choosing
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(a)>0
sufficiently small and
0<r<r_{\varepsilon}
, the function

\frac{(u-\ell)(rx)}{3r^{2}}

satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1.5
, since
\Delta u=3
in
\Omega_{2}
. This contradicts the strict convexity of
u
in
\Omega_{2}
, and hence
0
-density points cannot occur on
\mathcal{T}
.

Consequently, every point of
\mathcal{T}
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-density point, and
\mathcal{T}
is locally a
C^{1}
embedded curve.
∎
