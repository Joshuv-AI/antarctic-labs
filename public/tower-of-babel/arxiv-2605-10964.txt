Mechanism Design for Quality-Preserving LLM Advertising
Jiale Han jialehan@ucla.edu, Xiaowu Dai daix@ucla.edu

Abstract
Embedding advertisements into large language model (LLM) outputs introduces a fundamental tension: revenue optimization can distort content and degrade user experience. Existing approaches largely ignore this trade-off, often forcing irrelevant ads into responses. We propose a quality-preserving auction framework that explicitly integrates content fidelity into the mechanism design. Built on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), our approach treats organic content as a reference and derives an endogenous reserve price that screens out ads with non-positive marginal social welfare contributions. We develop a KL-regularized single-allocation mechanism with Myerson payments and a screened VCG multi-allocation mechanism, both satisfying dominant-strategy incentive compatibility and individual rationality. Experiments across diverse scenarios demonstrate that our mechanisms outperform existing baselines in metrics such as revenue per ad and semantic similarity to no-ad responses. Our results establish a new paradigm for LLM advertising that enables monetization without compromising output quality.



1
Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are rapidly becoming the primary interface for information access, enabling applications such as question answering, content generation, and decision support
[
Achiam et al. 2023
,
Brown et al. 2020
,
Touvron et al. 2023
,
Zhao et al. 2023
]
. Their training and deployment, however, incur substantial computational cost, creating an urgent need for scalable monetization
[
Minaee et al. 2024
]
. Online advertising is a natural solution
[
Choi et al. 2020
]
: platforms can allow advertisers to bid for inclusion in LLM-generated responses. Unlike traditional display advertising, this paradigm embeds ads directly into generated content, introducing a fundamental
tension
between revenue maximization and output quality. Recent work proposes auction mechanisms for LLM-based advertising
[
Dubey et al. 2024
,
Duetting et al. 2024
,
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
,
Soumalias et al. 2024
]
, but largely overlooks this tension. Existing approaches optimize welfare based only on advertiser value and restrict generation to sponsored content, which can introduce irrelevant ads and degrade coherence
[
Duetting et al. 2024
,
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
]
. While
Soumalias et al. 2024
address this via distributional penalties, their method incurs inference costs that scale with the number of advertisers.
A key challenge remains:
how to design auction mechanisms that enable monetization without compromising the semantic fidelity of LLM outputs?

In this paper, we propose a
quality-preserving
mechanism design framework for LLM advertising. Our approach builds on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
[
Lewis et al. 2020
]
and treats organic content, namely the no-ad source such as Wikipedia pages or outputs from internal tools, as a reference that anchors output quality
[
Guu et al. 2020
,
Izacard and Grave 2021
]
. We incorporate this organic content directly into the social welfare objective, measuring quality as the semantic similarity to no-ad responses. This perspective fundamentally changes the role of the auction: instead of allocating over the full set of candidate ads, the mechanism admits only those that improve the overall quality-adjusted social welfare.

A key implication of this formulation is an endogenous
reserve price
that screens advertisers based on their marginal contribution relative to the organic baseline.
This screening step excludes ads that do not contribute positively to the overall quality-adjusted welfare, whether due to semantic irrelevance or low bids, thereby preserving content quality while ensuring a revenue lower bound for the platform.
Compared to prior RAG-based mechanisms
[
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
]
, which incorporate ads without filtering, our approach explicitly enforces quality preservation at the level of the allocation rule.

Building on the screened RAG model, we design both single-allocation and multi-allocation auction mechanisms. In the single-allocation setting, we maximize a Kullback-Leibler (KL)-regularized social welfare objective that penalizes deviation from the screened RAG model, which we treat as the semantically optimal retrieval distribution, yielding a tractable allocation rule with payments derived via Myerson’s envelope formula
[
Myerson 1981
]
, and thus achieving dominant-strategy incentive compatibility (DSIC) and individual rationality (IR). In the multi-allocation setting, we adapt the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) mechanism
[
Clarke 1971
,
Groves 1973
,
Vickrey 1961
]
to select a welfare-optimal subset of ads. Crucially, the screening step reduces the candidate set to a small eligible subset, making the mechanism computationally efficient while preserving incentive guarantees.

We evaluate our framework across diverse queries, advertiser sets, and bid profiles, and compare against the most closely related RAG-based baseline
[
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
]
. Across all scenarios, our mechanisms consistently achieve higher revenue, relevance, and social welfare, while maintaining substantially better output quality, measured as semantic similarity to no-ad responses.
These results demonstrate that the proposed mechanism design establishes a new paradigm for LLM advertising that enables monetization without compromising output quality.
In conclusion, our contributions are threefold.

•

We introduce a quality-preserving auction framework for LLM advertising that incorporates organic content into the social welfare objective and derives an endogenous reserve price to screen out ads with negative contributions to the quality-adjusted social welfare.

•

We design incentive-compatible single- and multi-allocation mechanisms: a KL-regularized Myerson mechanism for single allocation, and a screened VCG mechanism for multi-allocation with reduced computational complexity.

•

We provide extensive empirical validation demonstrating consistent improvements over existing RAG-based mechanisms in revenue, semantic relevance, social welfare, and output quality.

Related Work.

Our work relates to three threads of literature: (i) auction design for LLM-generated contents, (ii) RAG, and (iii) reserve prices in auction theory.

First, mechanism design for LLM-generated content has recently emerged at the intersection of machine learning and economics
[
Feizi et al. 2023
]
. Existing approaches differ mainly in the
granularity of allocation
and how ads are integrated into generation. At the
token level
,
Duetting et al. 2024
propose per-token bidding, but such fine-grained control does not ensure semantic coherence. At the
response level
,
Soumalias et al. 2024
aggregate multiple candidates at substantial inference cost, while
Dubey et al. 2024
focus on ad summarization.
Balseiro et al. 2026
extend position auctions to AI-generated content under known click-through rates. Most closely related are
segment-level
RAG-based mechanisms by
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
, which integrate ads without additional inference cost but restrict generation to sponsored content, potentially introducing irrelevant ads, and lack incentive guarantees in the multi-allocation setting. In contrast, we incorporate
organic content
into the allocation problem, yielding a quality-preserving objective that screens out ads with negative marginal welfare contributions and supports incentive-compatible mechanisms.

Second, RAG
[
Lewis et al. 2020
]
enhances LLMs by conditioning outputs on retrieved documents and is widely used to improve factuality and relevance in question answering
[
Guu et al. 2020
,
Izacard and Grave 2021
,
Zhao et al. 2026
]
. Prior work uses RAG primarily as a modeling tool for incorporating external information. We extend this paradigm by treating organic (non-sponsored) content as a first-class component in the retrieval process and embedding it directly into the mechanism design objective.

Finally, reserve prices are a classical tool for revenue optimization in auctions
[
Myerson 1981
,
Ostrovsky and Schwarz 2023
]
, with substantial follow-up work on learning reserve prices from samples or historical data
[
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2014
,
Cole and Roughgarden 2014
,
Chawla et al. 2014
,
Han and Dai 2026
]
. While these works derive reserves from value distributions or bidding data, our reserve prices arise endogenously from a quality-adjusted welfare formulation: each advertiser is screened based on its marginal contribution relative to the organic baseline, simultaneously preserving output quality and guaranteeing a revenue lower bound.

2
Preliminaries

We consider
n
advertisers competing to place ads in the LLM output. Let
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
denote the advertisers and their ad contents. Given a query
x
, the LLM generates an output
y=y^{(1:T)}
in
T
segments (e.g., tokens, sentences, or paragraphs). At each segment
t
, advertisers submit bids
\vec{b}^{(t)}=(b_{1}^{(t)},\dots,b_{n}^{(t)})
, which influence generation. We are given a reference LLM model
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}
and seek to construct a bid-influenced LLM model
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}
.

RAG with Organic Content.

We adopt a RAG framework
[
Lewis et al. 2020
]
, where content generation conditions on retrieved documents without fine-tuning. For each segment
t
, unlike prior work that restricts retrieval to ads
[
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
]
, we augment the retrieval set
\mathcal{A}
with
organic content

a_{0}^{(t)}
, representing the no-ad source such as Wikipedia pages or outputs from internal tools
[
Guu et al. 2020
,
Izacard and Grave 2021
]
. For notational simplicity, we drop the superscript of
a_{0}^{(t)}
and write the augmented retrieval set as
\mathcal{A}\cup\{a_{0}\}=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
. At segment
t
, conditioned on the query
x
and previously generated segments
y^{(1:t-1)}
, the model samples

\mathbb{P}_{\text{RAG-Sequence}}(y^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)}):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}q_{i}^{(t)}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)},a_{i}),

(1)

where the retrieval weight
q_{i}^{(t)}=\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(a_{i}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)})
denotes the relevance of content
a_{i}
to the current context. This formulation allows the model to generate responses without forcing ad inclusion.

Auction Mechanism

We consider an auction conducted independently at each generation segment. At segment
t
, the mechanism maps bids
\vec{b}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
to an allocation
x_{i}(\vec{b}^{(t)})\in[0,1]
for each
i\in[n]\cup\{0\}
, and a payment
p_{i}(\vec{b}^{(t)})\geq 0
for each advertiser
i\in[n]
. Here,
x_{i}(\vec{b}^{(t)})
denotes the probability that content item
a_{i}
is selected in the next segment,
and
p_{i}(\vec{b}^{(t)})
is the payment charged to advertiser
i
.

We assume the retrieval weight is calibrated to click-through rates (CTR; e.g.,
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
), which can be achieved via standard calibration methods
[
McMahan et al. 2013
,
Naeini et al. 2015
]
.

{assumption}

Let
E_{i}^{(t)}
denote the click event for ad
a_{i}
at segment
t
. The click-through rate satisfies
\mathrm{ctr}_{i}^{(t)}:=\mathbb{E}[E_{i}^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)}]=C^{(t)}q_{i}^{(t)}
for some normalization constant
C^{(t)}
.

Each advertiser
i
has a private value
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
per click. The expected utility at segment
t
is
u_{i}^{(t)}(\vec{b}^{(t)})=\mathrm{ctr}_{i}^{(t)}\cdot v_{i}\,x_{i}(\vec{b}^{(t)})-p_{i}(\vec{b}^{(t)}).

The auctioneer seeks to design mechanisms that maximize expected revenue subject to dominant-strategy incentive compatibility (DSIC) and individual rationality (IR). DSIC requires truthful bidding to be optimal for each advertiser, i.e.,
u_{i}^{(t)}(v_{i},\vec{b}^{(t)}_{-i})\geq u_{i}^{(t)}(b_{i}^{(t)},\vec{b}^{(t)}_{-i})
for every possible bid
b_{i}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and competing bids
\vec{b}^{(t)}_{-i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n-1}
. IR requires nonnegative utility from participation, i.e.,
u_{i}^{(t)}(v_{i},\vec{b}^{(t)}_{-i})\geq 0
for all
\vec{b}^{(t)}_{-i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n-1}
.

Design Goal.

Our objective is to design
quality-preserving
auction mechanisms that balance ad monetization and output fidelity. We incorporate organic content into the welfare objective and derive an endogenous reserve price that admits only ads with positive marginal social welfare contributions. We further penalize deviation from the semantically optimal retrieval distribution to preserve semantic coherence. Our framework yields DSIC and IR mechanisms for both single- and multi-allocation settings while maintaining computational tractability.

In the remainder of the paper, we analyze the auction mechanism at an arbitrary time step
t\in[T]
. For notational brevity, we suppress the superscript
(t)
for all variables (i.e.,
q_{i},w_{i},b_{i},\vec{b},u_{i},\mathrm{ctr}_{i},C
) unless otherwise specified.

3
Main Result

3.1
Screened RAG Model with Welfare-Improving Ad Selection

Introducing advertisements generates revenue but may distort the generated content, degrading user-perceived quality. This trade-off implies that ads with negative marginal contributions to welfare should be excluded.

Let
f(q_{0})
denote the expected social welfare generated by organic content, where
q_{0}
is the retrieval weight of the original content, representing its semantic relevance.
We assume
f(q_{0})\geq 0
and that
f(\cdot)
is increasing in
q_{0}
.
One natural choice is a power function,
f(q_{0}):=\eta q_{0}^{\beta}
for
\eta>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, which is uniquely characterized by a scale-invariant welfare ratio condition (Proposition
6
in Appendix
A
). The concavity (
\beta<1
) reflects diminishing marginal returns to relevance
[
Marshall et al. 1961
]
, and mirrors the concave value mappings employed in related auction models
[
Dubey et al. 2024
]
. Under the RAG model (
1
), the resulting
quality-adjusted social welfare
is

\begin{split}\mathrm{SW}_{\mathrm{rag}}&:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}\cdot\mathrm{ctr}_{i}v_{i}+q_{0}\cdot f(q_{0}),\end{split}

(2)

where the first term captures advertisers’ value and the second term captures the contribution of organic content. Payments are transfers and thus canceled in the social welfare calculation.

In standard RAG, only the top-
k
documents are retrieved, prioritizing those that contribute most to the generated response
[
Lewis et al. 2020
]
. By analogy, it is natural to restrict the ad set
\mathcal{A}
to advertisers with positive marginal contributions to social welfare. Eq. (
2
) suggests interpreting
\mathrm{ctr}_{i}v_{i}
as a
pseudo-virtual value
, analogous to the virtual value in classical auction theory
[
Krishna 2009
]
. This leads to a natural reserve price rule that screens out advertisers with negative marginal contributions, as formalized below.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumption
2
, given
\hat{f}(\cdot):=f(\cdot)/C
,
\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
, and
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
including advertiser
i
in the candidate set increases expected social
welfare
\mathrm{SW}_{\mathrm{rag}}
if and only if
v_{i}\geq\hat{f}(q_{0})/q_{i}.

Consequently, the welfare-maximizing eligible set is

S=\{i\in[n]:v_{i}\geq\hat{f}(q_{0})/q_{i}\}.

Equivalently, in a DSIC and IR mechanism, this corresponds to imposing a reserve price
r_{i}=\hat{f}(q_{0})/q_{i}
on advertiser
i
.

The proof is provided in Appendix
D.1
. The reserve price screens out advertisers with low relevance or low bids.
This improves content quality while guaranteeing a revenue lower bound for the platform.
Our reserve price differs from classical designs
[
Myerson 1981
,
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2014
,
Ostrovsky and Schwarz 2023
, e.g.,]
in two key aspects. First, it is driven by the organic welfare function and ad relevance, rather than the distribution of bidder values. Second, its primary role is to preserve output quality rather than to maximize revenue, reflecting the distinct objectives of LLM-based systems.

After excluding advertisers with negative marginal welfare, we define the eligible set
S=\{i\in[n]:b_{i}\geq r_{i}\}
and augment it with organic content as
S^{*}=S\cup\{0\}
. We then renormalize the retrieval weights over
S^{*}
by

\tilde{q}_{i}:={q_{i}}/{\sum_{j\in S^{*}}q_{j}}
,
for
i\in S^{*}
, so that
\sum_{i\in S^{*}}\tilde{q}_{i}=1
. Here,
\tilde{q}_{i}
represents the updated retrieval weight of source
a_{i}
.
The resulting
screened
RAG model is

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}}(y^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)}):=\sum_{i\in S^{*}}\tilde{q}_{i}\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)},a_{i}).

(3)

3.2
Single-Allocation Auction Design

In this section, we design a single-allocation mechanism in which exactly one content source is selected at each segment, so that
\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}(\vec{b})=1
. Under the screened RAG model (
3
), advertisers outside the eligible set
S^{*}
are excluded, and we set
x_{i}(\vec{b})=0
for all
i\notin S^{*}
.
Given an allocation rule
\{x_{i}(\vec{b})\}_{i\in S^{*}}
, the bid-influenced generation model is defined via linear aggregation:

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}(y^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)})=\sum_{i\in S^{*}}x_{i}(\vec{b})\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)},a_{i}).

(4)

This linear aggregation follows prior work
[
Duetting et al. 2024
,
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
]
, but unlike these formulations, ours explicitly incorporates organic content in addition to filtered advertisements.

Allocation Rule.

We design an allocation rule that maximizes expected social welfare while controlling deviation from the screened RAG distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}}
in (
3
), which serves as the semantically optimal baseline in the absence of bid influence. We measure this deviation using the KL divergence

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}(\cdot\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)})\,\|\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}}(\cdot\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)})),

and write it as
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}\|\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}})
.

Given that the mechanism observes bids
\vec{b}
rather than true values
\vec{v}
, we optimize welfare with respect to
\vec{b}
. The allocation
\vec{x}\in\Delta^{|S^{*}|}:=\{\vec{x}:x_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i\in S^{*}}x_{i}=1\}
is chosen to solve

\max_{\vec{x}\in\Delta^{|S^{*}|}}\mathrm{SW}_{\mathrm{auction}}(\vec{b},\vec{x}):=\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}\cdot\mathrm{ctr}_{i}b_{i}+x_{0}\cdot f(q_{0})-\lambda D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}\|\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}}),

(5)

where
\lambda>0
controls the trade-off between revenue and fidelity. Under DSIC, truthful bidding implies
b_{i}=v_{i}
, so the mechanism maximizes the true social welfare penalized by deviation from the baseline.

Computing
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}\|\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}})
directly is intractable due to the exponential output space. The next result provides a tractable upper bound in terms of the allocation vectors
\vec{x}
and retrieval weights
\tilde{\vec{q}}
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\vec{\tilde{q}}=(\tilde{q}_{i})_{i\in S^{*}}
denote the vector of renormalized retrieval weights of the screened RAG model
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}}
in Eq. (
3
), and let
\vec{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in S^{*}}
denote the allocation vector of the generative model
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}
in Eq. (
4
). Then the following inequality holds:

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}\,\|\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}})\leq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{x}\,\|\,\vec{\tilde{q}}).

The proof is provided in Appendix
D.2
.
Proposition
2
implies that keeping

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}\,\|\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}})
small requires the allocation vector
\vec{x}
to remain close to the semantic-based retrieval weights
\tilde{\vec{q}}
. In particular, when
\vec{x}=\tilde{\vec{q}}
, we recover
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}}
. Under Assumption
2
,
\mathrm{ctr}_{i}\propto q_{i}
, so the welfare objective
\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}\cdot\mathrm{ctr}_{i}b_{i}+x_{0}\cdot f(q_{0})
is proportional to
\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}\cdot q_{i}b_{i}+x_{0}\cdot\hat{f}(q_{0})
. Combining this observation with Proposition
2
, we approximate Problem (
5
) by solving the following optimization problem:

\max_{\vec{x}\in\Delta^{|S^{*}|}}\widetilde{\mathrm{SW}}(\vec{b},\vec{x}):=\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}\cdot\tilde{q}_{i}b_{i}+x_{0}\cdot\tilde{f}(q_{0})-\tilde{\lambda}\cdot D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{x}\,\|\,\vec{\tilde{q}}),

(6)

where
\tilde{q}_{i}={q_{i}}/{\sum_{j\in S^{*}}q_{j}}
,
\tilde{f}(\cdot):=\hat{f}(\cdot)/\sum_{j\in S^{*}}q_{j}
is the normalized organic welfare function, and
\tilde{\lambda}
is a regularization parameter. The allocation
\vec{x}(\vec{b})
is defined as an optimal solution to Problem (
6
), as characterized in the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

The unique optimal solution to Problem (
6
) is

x_{i}(\vec{b})=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{\vec{x}\in\Delta^{|S^{*}|}}\ \widetilde{\mathrm{SW}}(\vec{b},\vec{x})=\frac{\tilde{q}_{i}\exp\left({s_{i}}/{\tilde{\lambda}}\right)}{\sum_{j\in S^{*}}\tilde{q}_{j}\exp({s_{j}}/{\tilde{\lambda}})},\qquad i\in S^{*},

(7)

where

s_{i}=\tilde{q}_{i}b_{i}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{i\neq 0\}+\tilde{f}(q_{0})\cdot\mathbf{1}\{i=0\}.

The proof is given in Appendix
D.3
. Theorem
3
shows that an advertiser can increase its selection probability by either bidding higher or providing more relevant content. The regularization parameter
\tilde{\lambda}
controls how closely the allocation follows the semantic-based retrieval weights: as
\tilde{\lambda}\to\infty
, the allocation converges to the screened RAG retrieval weights,
x_{i}(\vec{b})\to\tilde{q}_{i}
, and thus
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}
recovers the screened RAG model
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}}
without bid influence.

Payment Rule.

We define the per-click payment
\tilde{p}_{i}(\vec{b})
by
p_{i}(\vec{b})=\mathrm{ctr}_{i}\cdot\tilde{p}_{i}(\vec{b})
, and the corresponding per-click utility
\tilde{u}_{i}(\vec{b}):=v_{i}x_{i}(\vec{b})-\tilde{p}_{i}(\vec{b})
. The expected utility can then be written as

u_{i}(\vec{b})=\mathrm{ctr}_{i}\cdot\tilde{u}_{i}(\vec{b})
.
For any bidder
i\notin S
whose bid is below the reserve price, we set
\tilde{p}_{i}(\vec{b})=0
. In practice, the advertiser pays
\tilde{p}_{i}(\vec{b})/x_{i}(\vec{b})

The payment rule of the mechanism is uniquely determined by the DSIC and IR constraints via Myerson’s envelope formula
[
Myerson 1981
]
.

Theorem 4
.

Under Assumption
2
, for each bidder

i\in S
with reserve price
r_{i}
, any DSIC and IR mechanism for the RAG allocation rule Eq. (
7
) has per-click payment rule

\tilde{p}_{i}(\vec{b})=b_{i}\bigl(x_{i}(\vec{b})-1\bigr)+r_{i}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}\ln\frac{x_{i}(\vec{b})}{x_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})},

(8)

up to an additive constant.
The payment rule Eq. (
8
) corresponds to the revenue-maximizing
payment among all DSIC and IR mechanisms.

. Eq. (

) yields the revenue-maximizing payment, under which
\tilde{u}_{i}(\vec{b})=0
when

. To guarantee strictly positive utility, the auctioneer can reduce this payment by a constant. For example, decreasing

3.3
Multi-Allocation Auction Design

So far, we have considered a single-allocation setting, where at most one content source is selected per segment. We now extend the model to allow multiple ads to be selected and inserted within a single segment.
Let
A\subseteq\mathcal{A}\cup\{a_{0}\}
denote a subset of the augmented retrieval set, and let
a_{A}
represent the concatenated content of
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in A}
. The corresponding combinatorial extension of the RAG model in Eq. (
1
) is given by

\mathbb{P}_{\text{RAG-Seq}}(y^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)})=\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{A}\cup\{a_{0}\}}\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(a_{A}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)})\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)},a_{A}).

Since
a_{A}
captures only set-level information, naively concatenating ads can introduce positional biases. As a result, the click-through rate for each ad
i\in A
must be estimated separately. Following
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
and in line with Assumption
2
, we assume that click-through rates are proportional to relative relevance.

{assumption}

In segment
t
, let
E_{A,i}^{(t)}
denote the click event for ad
i
when it is displayed within an ad set
A
. We assume that the click-through rate satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{ctr}_{A,i}^{(t)}:=\mathbb{E}[E_{A,i}^{(t)}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)}]=C_{\text{multi}}^{(t)}\,q_{A,i}^{(t)},

for some normalization constant
C_{\text{multi}}^{(t)}>0
, where

q_{A,i}^{(t)}:=\mathbb{P}_{\phi}\!\big(a_{i}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)},a_{A}\big)\,\mathbb{P}_{\eta}\!\big(a_{A}\mid x,y^{(1:t-1)}\big),

and
\sum_{i\in A}\mathbb{P}_{\phi}(a_{i}\mid\cdot)=1
for any set
A
.
Here,
\phi
parameterizes the within-set prominence of item
i
, while
\eta
captures the overall relevance of the ad set
A
.

We drop the superscripts and write
q_{A,i}
,
\mathrm{ctr}_{A,i}
, and
C_{\text{multi}}
for simplicity. The introduction of
q_{A,i}
suggests the need for a separate module to estimate set-level relevance; we provide one possible heuristic, following
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
, in Appendix
B
.
Given bids
\vec{b}
, for any subset
A\subseteq\mathcal{A}\cup\{a_{0}\}
, the reported social welfare is defined as

\mathrm{SW}_{\text{multi}}(\vec{b},A)=\sum_{i\in A}[\mathrm{ctr}_{A,i}b_{i}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{i\neq 0\}+f(q_{A,0})\cdot\mathbf{1}\{i=0\}].

Under Assumption
3.3
, define
\hat{f}_{\text{multi}}(\cdot):=f(\cdot)/C_{\text{multi}}
. Then the multi-allocation social welfare can be written as

\mathrm{SW}_{\text{multi}}(\vec{b},A)\propto\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\vec{b},A):=\sum_{i\in A}[q_{A,i}\,b_{i}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{i\neq 0\}+\hat{f}_{\text{multi}}(q_{A,0})\cdot\mathbf{1}\{i=0\}].

(9)

Since the ad set
\mathcal{A}
can be large, evaluating social welfare over all subsets
A\subseteq\mathcal{A}\cup\{a_{0}\}
is computationally intractable. Moreover, ads with low relevance or low bids may degrade both output quality and welfare. As in Section
3.2
, we address this by imposing a reserve price and retaining only advertisers in

S_{\text{multi}}=\{i\in[n]:b_{i}\geq\hat{f}_{\text{multi}}(q_{0})/q_{i}\}
. This screening step both reduces computational complexity and helps filter out low-quality ads.

We then define the feasible set of configurations as

\mathcal{A}^{*}:=\{A:A\subseteq S^{*}_{\text{multi}}\}
where
S^{*}_{\text{multi}}=S_{\text{multi}}\cup\{0\}
,
so that
|\mathcal{A}^{*}|=2^{|S^{*}_{\text{multi}}|}
.
Motivated by the classical VCG mechanism
[
Vickrey 1961
]
, we adapt it to the multi-allocation RAG setting based on the social welfare in Eq. (
9
), yielding a quality-preserving multi-allocation auction.

Allocation Rule.

The mechanism selects the welfare-maximizing set:

A^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{A\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\vec{b},A).

(10)

Payment Rule.

For each advertiser
i\in A^{*}
with
i\neq 0
, define

A^{*}_{-i}:=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{A\in\mathcal{A}^{*},\,i\notin A}\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\vec{b},A)

as the optimal set excluding advertiser
i
.
The per-click payment for advertiser
i
is defined as

\tilde{p}_{i}^{\mathrm{VCG}}(\vec{b}):=\left(\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\vec{b},A^{*}_{-i})-\Bigl[\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\vec{b},A^{*})-q_{A^{*},i}\,b_{i}\Bigr]\right)/q_{A^{*},i}.

(11)

The complete algorithm for the quality-preserving multi-allocation auction is given in Algorithm
2
in the Appendix. The per-impression payment is given by
p_{i}^{\mathrm{VCG}}(\vec{b}):=\mathrm{ctr}_{A^{*},i}\cdot\tilde{p}_{i}^{\mathrm{VCG}}(\vec{b})
.
Advertisers
i\notin A^{*}
are not selected and therefore pay nothing, yielding utility
u_{i}=0
. For each selected advertiser
i\in A^{*}
with
i\neq 0
, the utility is
u_{i}(\vec{b})=\mathrm{ctr}_{A^{*},i}\cdot(v_{i}-\tilde{p}_{i}^{\mathrm{VCG}}(\vec{b}))
.

Theorem 5
.

Under Assumption
3.3
, the multi-allocation auction defined by Eqs. (
10
)–(
11
)
satisfies DSIC and IR, and maximizes social welfare under truthful reporting.

The proof is given in Appendix
D.5
. Theorem
5
highlights a key advantage of our VCG-based mechanism: unlike the multi-allocation segment auction of
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
, which does not provide formal guarantees of incentive compatibility or welfare optimality, our mechanism satisfies both. In addition, their approach requires the number of ads to be fixed in advance, whereas our mechanism endogenously selects the welfare-maximizing subset, resulting in a more flexible and efficient allocation.

We evaluate the proposed mechanisms across multiple advertiser scenarios. At each segment, we first run the auction to select the winning content, then inject it into the LLM prompt to generate the next segment, continuing the response while naturally incorporating the selected content. In our experiments, we approximate the organic content using LLM-generated concise, factual summaries in a Wikipedia-like style, with each round conditioned on previously generated outputs. Full details on the prompts and LLM are provided in Appendix

We consider outputs consisting of three sentences. The single-allocation mechanism treats each sentence as a separate segment and conducts one auction per sentence, while the multi-allocation auction treats all three sentences as a single segment and conducts one auction for the entire output.

q^{(t)}_{i^{(t)}}

•

5
Conclusion

We study mechanism design for LLM advertising and propose a quality-preserving auction framework that integrates organic content into the RAG pipeline and uses an endogenous reserve price to admit only welfare-improving ads. On this screened model, we develop a KL-regularized single-allocation mechanism with Myerson payments and a screened VCG multi-allocation mechanism. Both achieve dominant-strategy incentive compatibility and individual rationality. Experiments across different scenarios show consistent gains in revenue per ad, relevance, and social welfare, while preserving semantic fidelity to no-ad responses. These results demonstrate that effective monetization of LLM systems need not compromise output quality.

Several directions remain open for future work.
First, the organic welfare function can be learned from platform objectives and user interaction data, rather than being specified a priori. This includes estimating how users trade off relevance, informativeness, and intrusiveness, and using these signals to adaptively shape the reserve price and the resulting revenue–quality trade-off across contexts.
Second, incorporating budget constraints and repeated interactions would better capture real-world dynamics, as advertisers face spending limits and platforms operate over multiple rounds. Finally, developing richer measures of output quality beyond semantic similarity may further improve alignment between monetization and user experience, for example by incorporating factors such as factual accuracy or user engagement.

Appendix A
Choice of the Organic Social Welfare Function

We adopt
f(q_{0}):=\eta q_{0}^{\beta}
with
\eta,\beta>0
in our experiments. A similar power functional form is adopted in
Dubey et al. 2024
to map relative prominence to realized click-through rate, serving as a natural bridge between semantic relevance and advertiser value.
The following proposition shows that, under continuity, monotonicity, and a scale-invariant welfare ratio, the organic social welfare function
f
is uniquely characterized.

Proposition 6
.

Let
f:(0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfy:

(A1)

Monotonicity
:
f
is strictly increasing.

(A2)

Relative Welfare Invariance
: For all
q,q^{\prime}\in(0,1]
and
\eta>0
with
\eta q,\eta q^{\prime}\in(0,1]
,
f(\eta q)/f(\eta q^{\prime})=f(q)/f(q^{\prime})
.

(A3)

Continuity
:
f
is continuous on
(0,1]
.

Then
f(q_{0})=\eta q_{0}^{\beta}
for some
\eta,\beta>0
is the unique family satisfying
(A1)–(A3)
.

Proof.

Rewrite (A2) as

f(\eta q)=\frac{f(\eta q^{\prime})}{f(q^{\prime})}\cdot f(q)\quad\forall\,\eta,q,q^{\prime}\in(0,1].

Since the left-hand side is independent of
q^{\prime}
, the ratio

f(\eta q^{\prime})/f(q^{\prime})
must be constant in
q^{\prime}
; denote it

g(\eta)
. We thus obtain the multiplicative Cauchy functional
equation:

f(\eta q)=g(\eta)\cdot f(q)\quad\forall\,\eta,q\in(0,1].

Setting
q=1
gives
f(\eta)=g(\eta)f(1)
, so
g(\eta)=f(\eta)/f(1)
. Defining
\hat{f}(q):=f(q)/f(1)
,
we get
\hat{f}(\eta q)=\hat{f}(\eta)\hat{f}(q)
,
the standard multiplicative Cauchy equation with
\hat{f}(1)=1
.
By (A3), the unique continuous solution is

\hat{f}(q)=q^{\beta}
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{R}

[
Aczél and Oser 2006
]
, hence
f(q)=\eta q^{\beta}

where
\eta:=f(1)>0
by (A1). Finally, (A1) requires

\beta>0
, since
\eta q^{\beta}
is strictly increasing
on
(0,1]
if and only if
\beta>0
. Conversely,
f(q)=\eta q^{\beta}
with
\eta,\beta>0
clearly satisfies (A1)–(A3). This completes the proof of Proposition
6
.
∎

The parameter
\beta\in(0,1)
further captures
diminishing marginal returns
to organic content quality: as
q_{0}
increases, each incremental gain in relevance yields a progressively smaller increase in social welfare. This concavity is consistent with the classical law of diminishing marginal utility
[
Marshall et al. 1961
]
and the concave value mappings employed in related auction models
[
Dubey et al. 2024
]
. Intuitively, when organic content is already highly relevant, users are well-served and marginal welfare gains diminish; conversely, low-relevance content leaves substantial room for improvement. We therefore restrict attention to
\beta\in(0,1)
throughout.

Appendix B
A Heuristic for the Set-Level Retrieval Weight
q_{A}

One possible heuristic, following
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
, is

q_{A}=\sum_{i\in A}q_{i}+\frac{\xi}{|A|(|A|-1)}\sum_{i\neq j\in A}s(i,j)\cdot\mathrm{rel}(a_{i},a_{j}),

with per-item attribution given by
q_{A,i}=\frac{q_{i}}{\sum_{j\in A}q_{j}}q_{A}
.
Here,
\mathrm{rel}(a_{i},a_{j})
measures the semantic relevance between
a_{i}
and
a_{j}
, which can be computed using the semantic similarity introduced in Appendix
E.3
. Moreover,
s(i,j)=+1
if
i=0
or
j=0
, and
s(i,j)=-1
otherwise, thereby rewarding ads that are complementary to the organic content while penalizing mutually redundant ones. The parameter
\xi\geq 0
controls the strength of the pairwise term, and the second term vanishes when
|A|=1
.
The normalization factor
|A|(|A|-1)
corresponds to the total number of ordered pairs, ensuring that the pairwise term is invariant to the set size. Since
Hajiaghayi et al. 2024
fixes
|A|
ex ante, this factor reduces to a constant and can be absorbed into
\xi
, and is therefore not explicitly included in their formulation. In our experiments, we choose
\xi=1
, which balances the individual relevance terms and the pairwise complementarity term.

D.1
Proof of Theorem
1

Proof.

Since

2
) can be

\mathrm{SW}_{\mathrm{rag}}=f(q_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}\big(\mathrm{ctr}_{i}v_{i}-f(q_{0})\big).

1
.

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}
and

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}}
, respectively.
Computing the KL divergence between the joint distributions:

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{IY}\,\|\,Q_{IY})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in S^{*}}\sum_{y}x_{i}\,\mathbb{P}_{i}(y)\log\frac{x_{i}\,\mathbb{P}_{i}(y)}{\tilde{q}_{i}\,\mathbb{P}_{i}(y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in S^{*}}x_{i}\log\frac{x_{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}\underbrace{\sum_{y}\mathbb{P}_{i}(y)}_{=\,1}

\displaystyle=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\vec{x}\,\|\,\vec{\tilde{q}}\right).

By the data processing inequality (marginalization over
I
cannot increase KL divergence), we have that

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{gen}}\,\|\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{rag}})

\displaystyle=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\sum_{i\in S^{*}}P_{IY}(i,\cdot)\,\middle\|\,\sum_{i\in S^{*}}Q_{IY}(i,\cdot)\right)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{IY}\,\|\,Q_{IY})

\displaystyle=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\vec{x}\,\|\,\vec{\tilde{q}}\right).

This completes the proof of Proposition
2
.
∎

D.3
Proof of Theorem
3

Proof.

The feasible set
\Delta^{|S^{*}|}:=\{\vec{x}:x_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i\in S^{*}}x_{i}=1\}
is compact and convex. The objective is
strictly concave in
\vec{x}
: the linear terms are concave, and

\tilde{\lambda}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{x}\|\vec{\tilde{q}})
is strictly
convex for
\tilde{\lambda}>0
. Hence a unique maximizer exists, and it lies in
the relative interior of
\Delta^{|S^{*}|}
since the KL term diverges on the
boundary.

Introduce a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
for the constraint

\sum_{i\in S^{*}}x_{i}=1
and form

\mathcal{L}(\vec{x},\lambda)=\sum_{i\in S^{*}}s_{i}\,x_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}\sum_{i\in S^{*}}x_{i}\ln\frac{x_{i}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}-\lambda\!\left(\sum_{i\in S^{*}}x_{i}-1\right),

where we have used the compact form
s_{i}x_{i}
to unify the two cases
in the objective (since
s_{0}x_{0}(\vec{b})=x_{0}(\vec{b})\cdot\tilde{f}(q_{0})

and
s_{i}x_{i}=x_{i}\cdot\tilde{q}_{i}v_{i}
for
i\in S
).
Setting
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial x_{i}=0
for each

i\in S^{*}
gives

s_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}\!\left(\ln x_{i}-\ln\tilde{q}_{i}+1\right)-\lambda=0,

which rearranges to

\ln x_{i}=\frac{s_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}}+\ln\tilde{q}_{i}-1-\frac{\lambda}{\tilde{\lambda}}.

Exponentiating yields

x_{i}=\tilde{q}_{i}\exp\!\left(\frac{s_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\right)\cdot e^{-(1+\lambda/\tilde{\lambda})},\qquad i\in S^{*}.

The constant
e^{-(1+\lambda/\tilde{\lambda})}
is fixed by the normalization
constraint
\sum_{i\in S^{*}}x_{i}=1
:

e^{-(1+\lambda/\tilde{\lambda})}=\frac{1}{\sum_{j=0}^{m}\tilde{q}_{j}\exp\left(\tfrac{s_{j}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\right)}.

Substituting back gives the stated formula. Uniqueness follows from the
strict concavity of the objective.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎

D.4
Proof of Theorem
4

Proof.

We prove the result using Myerson’s characterization of truthful mechanisms

[
Myerson 1981
]
. Throughout, we fix
\vec{b}_{-i}
and view the allocation and payment as functions of advertiser
i
’s bid.

Step 1: Monotonicity of the Allocation.

For bidder
i\in S
, the allocation rule Eq. (
7
) at bid
b_{i}
is

x_{i}(b_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=\frac{\tilde{q}_{i}\exp(\tilde{q}_{i}b_{i}/\tilde{\lambda})}{\tilde{q}_{i}\exp(\tilde{q}_{i}b_{i}/\tilde{\lambda})+R_{-i}},\qquad R_{-i}=\sum_{j\neq i}\tilde{q}_{j}\exp(s_{j}/\tilde{\lambda}).

Differentiating with respect to
b_{i}
yields

\frac{\partial x_{i}}{\partial b_{i}}=\frac{\tilde{q}_{i}^{2}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\,x_{i}(\vec{b})\bigl(1-x_{i}(\vec{b})\bigr)>0.

Hence the allocation rule is strictly increasing in
b_{i}
.

Step 2: Envelope Condition and DSIC.

Consider truthful reporting
b_{i}=v_{i}
, under which the per-click utility is
\tilde{u}_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=v_{i}\,x_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})-\tilde{p}_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})
. By Myerson’s lemma, any DSIC mechanism with the monotone allocation rule established in Step 1 must satisfy the envelope condition

\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})}{\partial v_{i}}=x_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}).

Integrating from
r_{i}
to
v_{i}
gives

\tilde{u}_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})+\int_{r_{i}}^{v_{i}}x_{i}(z,\vec{b}_{-i})\,dz.

Solving for
\tilde{p}_{i}
from the definition of
\tilde{u}_{i}
yields

\tilde{p}_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=v_{i}\,x_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})-\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})-\int_{r_{i}}^{v_{i}}x_{i}(z,\vec{b}_{-i})\,dz,

(12)

where
\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})
is an additive constant in
v_{i}
.

Step 3: Closed Form.

Evaluating the integral yields

\int_{r_{i}}^{v_{i}}x_{i}(z,\vec{b}_{-i})\,dz=v_{i}-r_{i}-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}\ln\frac{x_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})}{x_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})}.

Substituting into Eq. (
12
) gives

\tilde{p}_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=v_{i}\bigl(x_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})-1\bigr)+r_{i}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{\tilde{q}_{i}}\ln\frac{x_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})}{x_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})}-\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}).

Since this identity must hold for every
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\tilde{p}_{i}
is defined as a function of the reported bid, renaming the argument
v_{i}
as
b_{i}
yields Eq. (
8
) up to the additive constant
\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})
.

Step 4: IR.

Since
x_{i}(b_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})
is strictly increasing in
b_{i}
(Step 1), we have
x_{i}(\vec{b})\geq x_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})
for all
b_{i}\geq r_{i}
. Therefore

\tilde{u}_{i}(\vec{b})=\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})+\int_{r_{i}}^{b_{i}}x_{i}(z,\vec{b}_{-i})\,dz\geq\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}).

The IR constraint
\tilde{u}_{i}(\vec{b})\geq 0
is satisfied if and only if
\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})\geq 0
, i.e.
\tilde{p}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})\leq r_{i}\,x_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})
.
The payment rule Eq. (
8
) corresponds to the canonical choice
\tilde{u}_{i}(r_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=0
, which is the revenue-maximizing selection among all DSIC and IR mechanisms.
This completes the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

D.5
Proof of Theorem
5

Proof.

(i) IR.
We verify
u_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})\geq 0
for all

\vec{b}_{-i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n-1}
.

Under truthful reporting
b_{i}=v_{i}
, the mechanism selects
A^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{A\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A)
. If
i\notin A^{*}
, then
p_{i}^{\mathrm{VCG}}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=0
and
u_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=0\geq 0
trivially. If
i\in A^{*}
, then by the payment rule Eq. (
11
),

\displaystyle u_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ctr}_{A^{*},i}\cdot v_{i}-p_{i}^{\mathrm{VCG}}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})

\displaystyle=C_{\text{multi}}\cdot\bigl(q_{A^{*},i}\,v_{i}-\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*}_{-i})+\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*})-q_{A^{*},i}\,v_{i}\bigr)

\displaystyle=C_{\text{multi}}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*})-\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*}_{-i})\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the last inequality follows since
A^{*}
maximizes
\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),\cdot)
over all
A\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
, including those with
i\notin A
, so
\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*})\geq\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*}_{-i})
.

(ii) DSIC.
We verify
u_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})\geq u_{i}(b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i})
for every

b_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\vec{b}_{-i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n-1}
.

Under truthful reporting, the calculation in (i) shows

u_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})=C_{\text{multi}}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*})-\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*}_{-i})\bigr),

where this identity also holds when
i\notin A^{*}
since in that case
A^{*}=A^{*}_{-i}
and both sides equal zero.

Under a misreport
b_{i}^{\prime}
, the mechanism selects
\hat{A}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{A\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i}),A)
. If
i\in\hat{A}
,

\displaystyle u_{i}(b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ctr}_{\hat{A},i}\,v_{i}-p_{i}^{\mathrm{VCG}}(b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i})

\displaystyle=C_{\text{multi}}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i}),\hat{A})-\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*}_{-i})-q_{\hat{A},i}(b_{i}^{\prime}-v_{i})\bigr)

\displaystyle=C_{\text{multi}}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),\hat{A})-\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*}_{-i})\bigr),

where the last equality uses the fact that
\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i}),\hat{A})-q_{\hat{A},i}b_{i}^{\prime}=\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),\hat{A})-q_{\hat{A},i}v_{i}
for
i\in\hat{A}
, and
\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\cdot,A^{*}_{-i})
does not depend on
i
’s bid since
i\notin A^{*}_{-i}
.

If
i\notin\hat{A}
, then
p_{i}^{\mathrm{VCG}}(b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i})=0
and
u_{i}(b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i})=0
, which is no greater than
u_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})
by (i).

In the case
i\in\hat{A}
, since
A^{*}
maximizes
\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),\cdot)
over
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, we have
\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*})\geq\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),\hat{A})
, hence

u_{i}(v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i})-u_{i}(b_{i}^{\prime},\vec{b}_{-i})=C_{\text{multi}}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),A^{*})-\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}((v_{i},\vec{b}_{-i}),\hat{A})\bigr)\geq 0.

Under truthful reporting
\vec{b}=\vec{v}
, the VCG mechanism selects
A^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{A\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\vec{v},A)
by Eq. (
10
), which directly maximizes social welfare. This completes the proof of Theorem
5
.
∎
