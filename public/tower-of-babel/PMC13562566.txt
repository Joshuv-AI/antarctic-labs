Analysis of decision support system of basketball sports competition based on machine learning and IoT
Authors: Muren Bao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study develops a machine learning-based decision support system for offensive and defensive tactical analysis in basketball games within an Internet of Things (IoT) environment. By integrating multi-source data collection, spatiotemporal feature encoding, and the Multi-Task Attention Long Short-Term Memory (MT-AttLSTM) model, the system simultaneously addresses offensive-defensive state classification and shooting percentage prediction. The system is evaluated using NBA SportVU tracking data from the 2018–2023 seasons. The results demonstrate that MT-AttLSTM achieves a test set accuracy of 0.897 in offensive-defensive state classification and significantly outperforms traditional baseline models in shooting percentage prediction, as reflected by a lower Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE). These findings highlight the system’s high accuracy and strong generalization capability. Moreover, the system facilitates real-time data processing and tactical decision support, offering coaches scientific insights and a technical framework for optimizing game strategies and personalized training.

Introduction

As a highly dynamic team sport, basketball game outcomes often hinge on players’ real-time decision-making and tactical execution. Traditional tactical analysis primarily relies on manual observation and empirical judgment, which are frequently subjective and inefficient1,2. The integration of machine learning and Internet of Things (IoT) technology has transformed sports data analysis in recent years, particularly in data collection, real-time processing, and intelligent prediction3,4. However, most existing studies focus on isolated aspects, such as offensive strategies or scoring detection, and lack comprehensive decision support systems spanning the entire offense-defense process5,6. Furthermore, the effective integration of IoT data with machine learning models remains an area requiring improvement7,8.

The contributions of this study are primarily reflected in the following three aspects: (1) Theoretical Contribution: A dynamic decision-making model that integrates cognitive theory and action execution is proposed, offering a novel theoretical framework for offense-defense analysis in basketball games. (2) Technical Contribution: The Multi-Task Attention Long Short-Term Memory (MT-AttLSTM) model is developed. By leveraging multi-task learning and the attention mechanism, the model significantly enhances the accuracy of both classification and prediction tasks. (3) Practical Contribution: A comprehensive IoT data collection and processing workflow is established, providing a viable solution for real-time tactical adjustments and personalized training.

Literature review

In recent years, IoT and machine learning technologies have gradually converged in research on decision support for team sports such as basketball, forming a research direction centred on multi-source data collection, intelligent analysis, and tactical prediction. At the level of cognitive and motor control theory, Wakatsuki et al. (2024) explored the dynamic decision-making mechanism of basketball players switching from shooting to passing during a jump shot through on-site simulation experiments, confirming that parallel processing theory is also applicable to decisions involving motor effectors9. In terms of the integrated application of IoT and computer vision, Ćirić et al. (2024) proposed an intelligent scoring detection subsystem architecture. This system improved the accuracy and real-time performance of scoring recognition through camera configuration optimization and machine learning algorithms, while considering the reliability and cost-effectiveness of Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology10. In cross-sport strategy analysis research, Chang et al. (2025) constructed a deep learning-driven decision tree ensemble algorithm to analyze table tennis serving strategies and the probability of scoring/losing points in the first three strokes. Through video analysis and predictive modeling, this method achieved quantitative evaluation of individual technical preferences and strategy effects, providing targeted training suggestions for athletes11. For basketball-specific tactical recognition, Tian et al. (2019) used full-team spatiotemporal tracking data of National Basketball Association (NBA) players and the ball to develop a classification model that automatically identified multiple defensive strategies against pick-and-roll tactics. The model also achieved analysis of off-ball players’ defensive actions for the first time. This method achieved a classification accuracy of 69% in cross-validation, proving the feasibility and potential of machine learning in complex tactical pattern recognition. This finding is of direct significance for the automated analysis of defensive strategies in offense-defense decision support systems12. In terms of performance prediction and index optimization, Papageorgiou et al. (2024) compared the performance of 14 machine learning models in predicting basketball key performance indicators. The results showed that tree-based models (e.g., Extra Trees and Random Forest) performed best in most indicators, and the Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) was reduced by 3.6% through a comprehensive prediction method13. In terms of constructing a hierarchical motor skill evaluation system, Shi et al. (2024) designed a dedicated skill recognition and hierarchical evaluation system for table tennis, providing important methodological references for the quantification of technical and tactical abilities in complex team sports such as basketball. In this study, motion data of table tennis players were collected through improved wearable devices. A feature database for six types of basic actions was constructed using sliding window segmentation and feature engineering, and a hierarchical evaluation system based on pattern recognition methods was established to achieve accurate assessment of athletes’ professional skill levels (p < 0.01)14.

Overall, existing research has established a relatively solid foundation for the application of IoT and machine learning technologies in basketball game analysis and tactical decision support. Relevant achievements have made certain progress in player trajectory tracking, action recognition, scoring prediction, defensive strategy identification, and athletic performance evaluation, demonstrating that data-driven methods can enhance the objectivity and efficiency of basketball tactical analysis. However, further refinement remains necessary. First, existing studies predominantly focus on singular analytical tasks, such as trajectory processing, action recognition, defensive pattern classification, or shot outcome prediction, without forming a complete decision support chain spanning “offensive-defensive state recognition—shot probability prediction—tactical feedback.” Consequently, models struggle to fully reveal the dynamic associations among offensive-defensive states, player spatial relationships, tactical execution processes, and game outcomes in basketball competitions. Second, although some studies have introduced IoT sensing devices or machine learning models, they tend to treat data acquisition, feature extraction, temporal modeling, and decision output as relatively independent stages, lacking systematic elaboration of information transmission relationships and collaborative mechanisms among modules. This limitation constrains model interpretability and practical application value. Third, regarding multi-source data fusion, existing methods predominantly employ static concatenation or unified encoding approaches, which inadequately accommodate dynamic variations in player movement speed, defensive pressure, tactical rhythm, data modality weights, and noise interference during basketball games. Particularly under critical scenarios such as fast-break transitions, defensive rotations, pick-and-roll coordination, and high-pressure defense, the capacity of models to depict complex offensive-defensive processes remains insufficient.

To address the aforementioned limitations, this study further constructs a multi-source spatiotemporal decision support framework for basketball games under IoT environments, building upon existing research. The innovation of this study does not lie in the simple superposition of existing algorithmic modules. Rather, it resides in the systematic integration of multi-modal data acquisition, differentiated feature encoding, bidirectional temporal modeling, attention weight allocation, and multi-task collaborative learning around the specific scenario of basketball offensive-defensive decision-making. Specifically, this study first acquires multi-source data through an IoT architecture. These data include player positions, basketball trajectories, kinematic features, physiological information, and game contexts. The study then performs differentiated encoding according to the structural characteristics of different modal data. This approach reduces information conflicts arising from direct fusion of heterogeneous data. Second, this study introduces Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) networks. These networks capture forward and backward temporal dependencies during offensive-defensive transition processes. The model can thus recognize current game states. It can also comprehend tactical evolution by integrating preceding action preparations and subsequent behavioral changes. Simultaneously, this study assigns higher weights to critical temporal segments and important tactical actions through a multi-head attention mechanism, enabling the model to focus on segments that exert significant influence on game decisions, such as fast-break initiation, defensive rotations, pick-and-roll formation, and shot release. Furthermore, this study incorporates offensive-defensive state classification and shot probability prediction into a unified multi-task learning framework for joint optimization, enabling the two tasks to share spatiotemporal representations and form complementary relationships. On the one hand, the offensive-defensive state classification task can provide tactical context priors for shot probability prediction, assisting the model in understanding variations in scoring opportunities under different offensive-defensive states. On the other hand, the shot probability prediction task can reversely guide the model to attend to key actions and spatial relationships closely associated with game outcomes, thereby enhancing the accuracy and stability of offensive-defensive state recognition. Previous studies emphasized singular tactical identification or independent performance prediction. This study constructs a complete analytical chain. It extends from multi-source data perception and spatiotemporal feature modeling to offensive-defensive decision output. This approach further strengthens the collaborative relationship between IoT data and machine learning models. This framework contributes to improving the accuracy of basketball offensive-defensive state recognition and shot prediction, and provides more interpretable and practically valuable technical support for coaches’ real-time tactical adjustments, post-game review analysis, and athletes’ personalized training.

Research model

Multimodal data acquisition architecture in IoT environment

This study constructs a three-layer IoT technical architecture, namely the perception layer, network layer, and application layer.

Perception layer: Six Basler acA2000-50gc industrial cameras (50 fps, 2048 × 1088 resolution) are deployed on the venue ceiling to transmit image streams via the GigE Vision protocol. Players wear Polar H10 heart rate belts (1 Hz sampling) and Catapult OptimEye S5 inertial sensors (100 Hz, including accelerometer and gyroscope), which communicate with the edge gateway through Bluetooth 5.0/BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy). All sensors achieve sub-millisecond synchronization (error < 1 ms) via PTP (Precision Time Protocol).

Network layer: The edge gateway (NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin) runs ROS (Robot Operating System) 2 nodes to perform real-time object detection and trajectory extraction, and uploads 25 Hz player/basketball coordinate data to the cloud via the Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT) protocol.

Application layer: The cloud server aligns timestamps of multi-source data and constructs multimodal feature vectors including spatial trajectories, kinematic features, physiological features, and game context, providing standardized inputs for subsequent deep learning models.

Multimodal feature coding

Basketball match data exhibit spatiotemporal heterogeneity, containing multiple modalities such as player position (continuous), offensive-defensive state (categorical), and physiological signals (time-series)15. Substantial differences in scale, type, and information density across modal data mean that direct input into the model may result in feature conflicts or information loss. This study designs modality-specific encoders: spatial trajectory coordinates (x, y, z) of players and the ball are mapped to 64-dimensional vectors via linear projection; kinematic features such as velocity and acceleration are embedded into 32-dimensional vectors after LayerNorm normalization. Categorical features such as offensive-defensive labels and game periods are converted into 16-dimensional vectors through learnable embedding layers; physiological signals are aligned from original sampling rates (1–100 Hz) to the SportVU sampling rate (25 Hz) via linear interpolation to achieve temporal consistency. Subsequently, aligned modal features are concatenated and fused through a fully connected layer to generate a 128-dimensional comprehensive feature vector at each time step, providing a unified and informative input representation for subsequent temporal modeling16.

Bidirectional temporal modeling and attention mechanism

Bi-LSTM for temporal dependency capture

Basketball is a highly dynamic team sport characterized by significant temporal dependencies in players’ action sequences17. Basketball action decisions exhibit remarkable bidirectional dependence: at the moment of shooting, players must simultaneously consider historical action preparation (past context) and defensive reaction prediction (future context). This study adopts Bi-LSTM rather than traditional unidirectional LSTM to capture complete contextual information through parallel forward and backward propagation18. Bi-LSTM can simultaneously learn action variation patterns from past to future and latent information from future to past, enabling the model to fully comprehend the temporal context of the current action19,20. In the traditional unidirectional LSTM model, sequence information is transmitted solely from past to future in chronological order. Consequently, the hidden state at each time step can only utilize feature information from the current and preceding moments, being unable to directly perceive subsequent actions21. This imposes limitations when processing basketball game data. For example, at the moment when a player is preparing to shoot or launch a defensive counterattack, information regarding subsequent actions, such as the ball’s flight trajectory or teammates’ movements, carries important reference value for current decision-making22.

To address this issue, Bi-LSTM is introduced. Bi-LSTM establishes both forward and backward temporal pathways simultaneously: On one hand, it captures the dependence of historical actions from past to future. On the other hand, it captures potential subsequent action trends from future to past23. Through this bidirectional modeling, the hidden state at each time step contains complete contextual information, enabling the model to more accurately understand the relationship between the current action and the overall game environment24.

In basketball games, tracking information and prediction tasks are not governed by a simple “input-output” relationship, but rather by a bidirectional coupling mechanism characterized by mutual constraints and reinforcement. From the tracking-to-prediction direction, continuous trajectories of players and the ball, velocity changes, spatial distances, and offensive-defensive positioning collectively form dynamic representations of game states, thereby providing the foundational basis for offensive-defensive state recognition and shot success prediction. Fast breaks, for example, are typically characterized by higher advancing speeds, larger longitudinal displacement, and reduced defensive pressure, whereas set plays often involve increased half-court spatial occupation, pass-and-cut coordination, and defensive rotations. Thus, continuous tracking data reveals the formation and evolution processes of offensive and defensive behaviors, enabling the model to recognize static states at given moments and to comprehend evolutionary trends. From the prediction-to-tracking-representation direction, prediction tasks likewise influence the model’s selection and interpretation of tracking information. Offensive-defensive state classification and shot success prediction guide the model, through error feedback during training, to focus more intensively on trajectory segments and key actions closely associated with game decision outcomes. In shot prediction, for instance, the model places greater emphasis on critical moments such as changes in defensive distance prior to shooting, the ball handler’s sudden stop and jump, and teammates’ spatial stretching. In offensive-defensive state classification, the model concentrates more on spatiotemporal patterns with tactical significance, including fast-break initiation, defensive positioning, pick-and-roll formation, and rotational defense. Consequently, tracking data provides the state foundation for prediction, while prediction tasks, through the training process, prompt the model to filter and reinforce tracking features of greater decision-making value. This study employs Bi-LSTM to capture forward and backward temporal dependencies, combined with an attention mechanism to highlight key trajectory segments, based on the theoretical logic of “tracking representation driving prediction, and prediction objectives reverse-optimizing representation.”

Task relationship mechanism

The two tasks are not optimized independently, but instead realize knowledge transfer through a shared spatiotemporal encoder. The “fast break/half-court offense” features learned by the offensive-defensive state classification task provide contextual prior for shooting percentage prediction. Conversely, the regression gradient of the shooting prediction task is back-propagated through the shared layer to refine the resolution of spatiotemporal features, resulting in more precise classification boundaries.

To quantify this synergistic effect, this study designs a dynamic weight adjustment strategy: when classification accuracy on the validation set improves while regression MAPE deteriorates, weights are automatically adjusted to prevent gradient updates from being dominated by a single task. This strategy enables the model to maintain balanced improvement of both tasks during later training stages (after 60 epochs), rather than allowing one task to dominate the other.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection, experimental environment and parameters setting

The dataset used is from the NBA SportVU tracking system26, covering complete tracking data of regular-season and playoff games from the 2018–2023 seasons. The SportVU system deploys six high-speed cameras at the venue, recording the 2D/3D positions (, y, ) of each player and the basketball at a sampling frequency of 25 Hz, while also synchronously generating dynamic information such as velocity and acceleration. Additionally, combined with game event logs (play-by-play data), offense-defense state labels (e.g., set offense, fast break, defensive rotation) and shooting results for each possession are obtained. For certain games, player physiological characteristics, such as heart rate and exercise load, are also collected via RFID and IoT wearable devices. For samples lacking physiological features, a feature masking strategy is adopted during model training: feature values for missing components are set to zero, while the attention mechanism automatically adjusts their contribution weights to the final result, ensuring that all samples can be utilized for training. After data cleaning and window slicing, a total of 42,000 temporal samples is formed, with 70% allocated for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing. Among these, 27,360 samples (65.14%) include complete physiological features, while 14,640 samples (34.86%) lack physiological features such as heart rate or exercise load. These missing physiological features primarily result from non-deployment of wearable devices in certain matches, device connectivity interruptions, or anomalous sensor data from individual athletes. To address these missing data issues, this study adopts a strategy combining zero padding with a masking mechanism. During feature fusion and attention calculation, masking information is simultaneously fed into the model, enabling the attention layer to minimize the impact of missing modalities on final prediction results, thereby avoiding feature bias caused by simple zero padding.

To evaluate the performance of MT-AttLSTM in the offensive-defensive state classification task, this study divides the NBA SportVU dataset into three subsets: training set, validation set, and test set. This study provides a unified explanation of implementation details and key hyperparameters for Random Forest (RF), Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), and Support Vector Machine (SVM). All baseline models use the same training, validation, and test set splits as the MT-AttLSTM model, and follow identical data cleaning, normalization, time-window slicing, and feature input processes. Continuous features are standardized using z-score normalization, categorical features are encoded using one-hot encoding, and missing physiological features are handled with the same masking mechanism as the main model. Hyperparameters for each model are tuned on the validation set, while the test set is used solely for final performance evaluation. For RF, the base learner is a classification tree based on the Gini index. The number of trees is set to 300, the maximum tree depth to 20, the minimum number of samples for splitting nodes to 4, and the minimum number of samples for leaf nodes to 2. The maximum number of features randomly chosen at each split is set to the square root of the total number of features. To mitigate the impact of class imbalance on classification results, class weights in RF are set to “balanced,” with the random seed fixed to 42. For CNN, a one-dimensional temporal convolutional structure is used to extract local spatiotemporal features within the time window. The model includes three 1D convolution layers with 64, 128, and 128 filters, each with a kernel size of 3 and a stride of (1) BatchNormalization and ReLU activation functions are applied after each convolution layer. Max-pooling layers are placed after the first two convolution layers, with a pooling kernel size of (2) The convolution features are globally averaged and then passed through a fully connected layer with 128 hidden units and a dropout rate of 0.3. CNN uses the AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 128, and 80 training epochs, with an early stopping mechanism and patience set to 10. For SVM, a radial basis function is used for nonlinear classification. The penalty parameter C is set to 10, and the kernel function parameter γ is set to “scale.” Class weights are set to “balanced.” Since SVM cannot directly model long temporal sequences, this study aggregates multimodal features within each time window by calculating statistics such as mean, standard deviation, maximum, minimum, and range. These aggregated features are then used as input for SVM, ensuring that it performs the classification task on an equivalent raw information basis.

Discussion

This study addresses basketball offensive-defensive decision support in IoT environments, and constructs a multi-task temporal modeling framework based on MT-AttLSTM. The framework is experimentally validated for two tasks: offensive-defensive state classification and shot accuracy prediction. Compared to traditional machine learning models, the advantage of MT-AttLSTM primarily lies in its ability to capture continuous temporal relationships. RF and SVM primarily rely on fixed-length features aggregated from statistical data, which, although effective for classification to some extent, struggle to express dynamic processes in player movements, offensive-defensive transitions, passing, and defensive rotations. CNN is capable of extracting motion patterns within local time windows, but its ability to model long-range dependencies remains relatively limited. In contrast, MT-AttLSTM uses Bi-LSTM to capture forward and backward temporal dependencies and integrates multi-head attention mechanisms to focus on key action segments. This enables it to better recognize state transitions in basketball games triggered by continuous trajectory changes. This constitutes one of the key reasons why the proposed model achieves higher Accuracy and Macro-F1 scores on the test set. Compared to LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and Bi-LSTM+Attention, the improvement of MT-AttLSTM is derived from both the attention mechanism and the collaborative learning effect brought about by multi-task shared representation. Ablation experiments show that Single-Cls-AttLSTM, which performs only offensive-defensive state classification, achieves lower Accuracy, Macro-F1, and Cohen’s Kappa compared to the joint training model. Similarly, Single-Reg-AttLSTM, which predicts only shot accuracy, performs worse in terms of MAPE, RMSE, and Brier Score compared to MT-AttLSTM. This demonstrates that the offensive-defensive state classification task provides tactical context priors for shot prediction, while the shot prediction task, in turn, guides the model to focus on key spatiotemporal segments related to the outcome. The two tasks complement each other through the shared temporal encoder, enabling the model to simultaneously learn information from both “how the game state is formed” and “how this state influences shot outcomes.”

From the perspective of research progress in the field, existing studies on intelligent basketball analysis mostly focus on single tasks, such as offensive strategy recognition, defensive tactic classification, shot outcome prediction, or score detection. In contrast, this study attempts to integrate offensive and defensive state recognition with shot outcome prediction into a unified framework, by incorporating multi-source data from the IoT to construct spatiotemporal feature inputs. This expands, to some extent, the analytical dimensions of basketball game decision support systems. This framework outputs the assessment of the offensive and defensive states at a given moment and further estimates the probability of shot outcomes. As a result, it provides more detailed reference points for coaches’ real-time tactical adjustments, players’ training feedback, and game review analysis. Therefore, the main contribution of this study is not in proposing entirely new neural network units, but rather in integrating and validating multi-source spatiotemporal features, bidirectional sequential modeling, attention focusing, and multi-task learning for the specific context of basketball decision-making. The experimental results also highlight certain limitations of the model in complex scenarios. First, the scene generalization experiment shows that the model’s performance drops when the training and testing scenes are misaligned, particularly in high-pressure defense scenarios. This suggests that factors such as defensive intensity, physical confrontation, shot space, and tactical tempo in basketball games alter the data distribution, posing challenges for the model’s cross-scenario transfer performance. Second, the occlusion analysis reveals that player occlusion leads to short-term trajectory loss, coordinate fluctuations, and ID switching, which affect the model’s judgment of both offensive and defensive states and shot outcomes. In severe occlusion samples, accuracy significantly drops compared to non-occluded samples, and MAPE increases, indicating that real-world game data collection conditions remain a critical factor impacting model deployment performance. Additionally, edge case analysis shows that although samples taken within the last 2 s of the 24-second shot clock are not frequent, they contribute significantly to overall prediction errors. These samples are typically associated with rushed shots, strong defensive pressure, and unconventional decision-making, thereby making shot outcome prediction more challenging when relying solely on conventional spatiotemporal trajectory features. The proposed MT-AttLSTM model primarily relies on the spatiotemporal trajectory features of players and the ball, kinematic features, game context features, and some physiological features. The model does not explicitly incorporate a knowledge graph module. The previous discussion about incorporating basketball domain expert rules through knowledge graphs was intended to suggest potential directions for future research, rather than a component of the current study. To avoid overstating the scope of the current work, the related statements have been revised in this version of the paper to reflect future research prospects. In other words, the current model does not include explicit basketball rule reasoning, expert knowledge graphs, or physical constraint layers. Its predictive capability primarily stems from data-driven spatiotemporal representation learning.

Overall, the results of this study demonstrate that MT-AttLSTM, supported by IoT multi-source data, effectively enhances basketball offensive and defensive state classification and shot accuracy prediction performance, showcasing significant potential for decision support. However, performance degradation is observed under conditions such as occlusion, high-pressure defense, time limit pressure, and cross-scenario transfer. Future research can further improve the model in three key areas:

Introducing more granular features related to defensive pressure intensity, shot space, player roles, and tactical types to enhance the model’s understanding of complex game contexts.Combining multi-view trajectory reconstruction and identity consistency constraints to improve data quality and tracking stability under occlusion conditions.Exploring the integration of basketball rules, offensive time limit logic, court boundary constraints, and expert tactical knowledge into the model through knowledge graphs or rule-based modules. Conclusion

Research contribution

In response to the practical demands of offensive and defensive tactical analysis in basketball games, this study integrates IoT technology and deep learning methods to construct a comprehensive decision support system for basketball offensive and defensive strategies. The system performs multi-task joint modeling of offensive-defensive state classification and shot outcome prediction, thereby providing a novel technical solution and a practical benchmark for tactical analysis and real-time decision-making in basketball competitions. The core research contributions are reflected in three dimensions: theory, technology, and application.

Theoretical implications: This study overcomes the limitation of most existing sports data analysis, which focuses only on single-task learning. It develops a dynamic decision-making analysis framework by integrating motor cognition theory and action execution logic. This framework reveals the coupling mechanism of multimodal spatiotemporal features in basketball offensive and defensive behavior modeling and demonstrates the effectiveness of the multi-task learning paradigm in dynamic and complex team sports scenarios, thereby providing a new perspective and support for theoretical research on basketball offensive and defensive analysis.

Technical contributions: This study designs and introduces the MT-AttLSTM model. It effectively integrates multi-source heterogeneous data such as player position, kinematics, physiological characteristics, and game context through modality-specific encoders. It adopts Bi-LSTM to capture bidirectional temporal dependencies during the game and introduce the multi-head attention mechanism to accurately focus on key player actions and core tactical scenarios. Meanwhile, a multi-task optimization framework with shared encoding and task-specific decoding is constructed to enable the collaborative optimization of offensive-defensive state classification and shooting percentage prediction, thereby significantly improving the model’s prediction accuracy and robustness. Experimental validation based on NBA SportVU high-precision tracking data shows that the model achieves a test set accuracy of 0.897 in the offensive-defensive state classification task. Its MAPE in the shooting percentage prediction task is significantly lower than that of traditional LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and single attention-based models, with all improvements being statistically significant.

Practical applications: This study builds a three-layer IoT multimodal data acquisition architecture, including the perception layer, network layer, and application layer, which enables sub-millisecond synchronous acquisition, real-time transmission, and standardized processing of court vision data, player kinematics data, and physiological data. It also constructs a complete end-to-end technical pipeline, from multi-source data acquisition and deep learning modeling to tactical prediction. The system effectively addresses the problems of high subjectivity and low efficiency in traditional basketball tactical analysis. It provides coaches with a scientific basis for offensive and defensive state judgment and shooting percentage prediction, while also supporting personalized player training and real-time in-game tactical adjustments. This study fills the research gap in existing decision support systems by covering the entire offensive and defensive chain of basketball and promotes the deeper application and deployment of machine learning and IoT technologies in the intelligent analysis of sports events.

Future works and research limitations

Even though this study produces some encouraging outcomes, there are still certain limitations. First, the model is heavily reliant on computing resources and data quality during training and inference. This may lead to problems such as data scarcity and hardware constraints in non-professional game or training settings. Second, the research tasks focus on offense-defense state classification and shooting accuracy prediction but do not yet cover broader tactical and physiological indicators, such as player fatigue assessment and tactical evolution analysis. This restricts the application scope of the model in multi-dimensional decision support. Future research could explore directions such as reducing model complexity, achieving lightweight design, and enabling real-time deployment. Future work could also attempt to transfer the method to other team sports (e.g., football, handball) to verify its cross-domain applicability.
