Executive Summary
We present on a new, self-consistent, arbitrary-temperature Romberg integration scheme
for modeling electron scattering in materials in a LANL Lagrangian Shock Hydro (LSH)
code. Electron beam-target interactions are fundamental to a wide range of scientific
and technological applications. When high-energy electron beams hit their target, they
may scatter, deposit energy, or ionize the source. These processes govern the behav-
ior and outcomes in nanotechnology manufacturing, electron microscopy, and modern
X-ray diagnostics. Simulating these interactions is essential for interpreting experimen-
tal results, predicting material responses, and designing efficient tools and experiments.
At Los Alamos, this is done using a LSH code, which is a multi-dimension, multi-mate-
rial, massively parallel, multi-physics code used to simulate applications from asteroid
impacts to electron beam interactions. By effectively and efficiently modeling the way
that electrons scatter from the beam we can bolster these simulations and more accu-
rately predict experimental outcomes. The model currently implemented in the LSH
of interest is based on work by Papp and does not self-consistently preserve momen-
tum in the slightly relativistic regime; here we adopt a model proposed by Braams and
Karney and implement a Romberg integration scheme to compute the diffusion tensor
[6, 2]. In this paper we will provide background on the Braams-Karney diffusion tensor
as well as the Romberg integration scheme we employed to numerically solve for it.
We will show that our integration scheme is accurate in solving for the set of scalar
potentials used to re-express the diffusion tensor in differential form, and in solving for
the diffusion coefficients in the larger LSH code. By using this diffusion tensor rather
than the existing Papp one, and numerically integrating it with a Romberg method,
we produce much more accurate, self-consistent results.

1 Introduction
Electron beam-target interaction physics is a crucial part of many modern technologies, from high-
resolution imaging in electron microscopy to the fabrication of nanoscale structures for semiconduc-
tors and other nanotechnology. At LANL, we are specifically interested in simulating high-fidelity
electron beam-target interactions for material X-ray diagnostics [10]. Understanding the electron
scattering in these interactions is pivotal in simulating the beam and any results from beam ex-
periments. At LANL, this is done using a Lagrangian Shock Hydro (LSH) code’s charged particle
transport scheme.
 This LANL LSH code is a multi-dimension, multi-material, massively parallel, multi-physics
code. Its charged particle transport package employs continuous energy Monte Carlo for particle
sourcing, along with the Sherlock scattering model to model particle collisions [9]. In particular, this
LSH code couples the diffusion coefficients from Papp to create an approximation to a relativistic
analogue to the Sherlock straggle model [6, 4, 3]. This form of the operator is only verifiable in the
limit where the rest energy is much greater than the thermal energy of the background particles
 2
(i.e. zb ≡ mckb T ≫ 1), the background particles are non-relativistic, and the sample species is either
non-relativistic or ultra-relativistic.
 In this work, we present a fully relativistic self consistent version of the Sherlock straggle model
using the Braams-Karney formulation for the isotropic diffusion coefficients of the Beliaev-Budker
operator [8, 2]. Our model samples from a Maxwell-Jüttner distribution, and thus is valid for
arbitrary zb and momenta. We do this by implementing a numerical Romberg integration scheme
to calculate the diffusion coefficients. Our goal is to show that our model matches the Papp operator
model in this LSH code for low temperatures, but that the Papp operator fails for high temperature.

3 Results
To validate our Romberg integration scheme we first applied it to compute the scalar potentials used
to express the collision operator given by Braams and Karney [2]. These potentials were chosen as a
starting test because their integrands have a similar form to those in our diffusion tensors and they
were numerically integrated by Markham, providing an easy point of comparison [5]. As shown in
Fig. 3-1, our results closely match those calculated previously.

Figure 3-1: Combined figure showing our potentials (top) and visual comparisons (bottom) from Markham [5]. In the bottom
row, red is the integration as performed by Mathematica, while the blue points are the results of Romberg integration of the
 potential equations in the NIMROD code.

 To illustrate the regimes in which the Papp model fails, we plot the diffusion coefficients for
different values of s in Fig. 3-2. We make the following observations. At low zb and low to
mid s, the Papp operator significantly underestimates the diffusion coefficients. High values of zb
mask the error, in that the Papp operator very closely approximates the Braams-Karney diffusion
tensor at high-zb for all values of s. For both low and high zb , the Papp approximation for the
parallel component of the diffusion tensor overestimates the true value in the ultra-relativistic limit.
This is easier to see in the low-zb case as the magnitudes are larger, however the structure of this
overestimation remains constant.
 These deviations in the low zb , non/semi-relativistic regime produce measurable differences in
scattering behavior. In Fig. 3-3, we plot the results of simulating two such scenarios. In both
cases, the electron beam from the Papp operator based simulation experiences more scattering in
the vertical direction as compared to the Braams-Karney based simulation, but experiences far less
perpendicular scattering. This reflects the overestimation of the parallel component of the diffusion
tensor shown in 3-2. We note here that many applications of interest are in the high-zb regime, and
thus see far less of this error.
 A scenario which LANL is concerned about in which numerical integration of the Braams-Karney
operator produces significant differences from Papp is the case of electron beam interaction with
multiple targets, illustrated in Fig. 3-4. Electron scattering in heavy metal targets produces x-rays
which are used for x-ray diagnostics of materials. In this case it is important to model the beam
accurately as the electrons will reach the second target and heat it well before the x-rays arrive. This
heating can have significant impacts on the way the x-rays travel through the target and thus how
they are used for diagnostics. To model this effect, we simulate a high energy (19.8 MeV) electron

 For our integration model to be viable as a part of the larger LSH code, it needs to balance
accuracy and computational efficiency. To test this we timed our approach against the existing,
Papp scheme. As seen in Figure 3-5, our model, whether with Simpson’s rule or the trapezoidal
rule, is orders of magnitude slower due to the high number of Richardson extrapolations required.
Despite this, its performance remains within a usable range for a number of applications. Since our
model is an optional user input, users can decide whether to employ it based on the specific needs
of their simulation. For scenarios involving ultra-relativistic beams or low temperature targets, the
computational cost of Romberg may not be worth it. However, cases where temperature or beam
energy drift into the regimes where Papp is inaccurate, our model may be preferable. We also note
that there are a number of potential methods for speeding up our model.

4 Conclusion
In this work, we demonstrate a numerical scheme for computing the diffusion coefficients of the
Beliaev-Budker operator, to model relativistic electron scattering. We show that our model repro-
duces known results from both NIMROD and Mathematica when calculating the scalar potentials
used to derive the diffusion tensors. These potentials have a very similar form to the final distribu-
tion function and thus are a good benchmark for our integration scheme.
 We also show that our method is higher fidelity and more general than the existing Papp operator
based scheme in the LANL LSH code of interest in high temperature regimes. We found that our
model produces significant differences from the existing approximation in high temperature. This is
due to our model being self-consistent and consistent with relativistic theory. At low temperature,
this difference is suppressed, as the Papp model is formed by taking the high-zb limit.
 While our model is significantly slower than the existing one, it is within a usable range for many
applications. These applications include simulations at high temperature or with multiple targets
where the precise scattering profile from the first target can have effects on the heating profile of
the latter targets. However, for simulations involving low temperature targets, or ultra-relativistic
beams, the slower speed of Romberg may not be worth it. Since our model is an optional user input,
users can decide whether this slower speed for higher accuracy trade off is worth it for their specific
needs. We also note that there are many potential avenues to explore for speeding up our method
including non-classical quadrature method, for faster convergence.
 In the future, we also aim to include self-interaction effects. In the current model, the electrons
in the beam do not interact with each other. Electron beam self-interaction can result in beam
defocusing or instabilities that may be important to model.
