Gauge-Equivariant Intrinsic Neural Operators for Geometry-Consistent Learning of Elliptic PDE Maps
Pengcheng Cheng chengpc1022@mails.jlu.edu.cn

Abstract
Learning solution operators of partial differential equations (PDEs) from data has emerged as a promising route to fast surrogate models in multi-query scientific workflows. However, for geometric PDEs whose inputs and outputs transform under changes of local frame (gauge), many existing operator-learning architectures remain representation-dependent, brittle under metric perturbations, and sensitive to discretization changes. We propose Gauge-Equivariant Intrinsic Neural Operators (GINO), a class of neural operators that parameterize elliptic solution maps primarily through intrinsic spectral multipliers acting on geometry-dependent spectra, coupled with gauge-equivariant nonlinearities. This design decouples geometry from learnable functional dependence and enforces consistency under frame transformations. We validate GINO on controlled problems on the flat torus ( 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} ), where ground-truth resolvent operators and regularized Helmholtz–Hodge decompositions admit closed-form Fourier representations, enabling theory-aligned diagnostics. Across experiments E1–E6, GINO achieves low operator-approximation error, near machine-precision gauge equivariance, robustness to structured metric perturbations, strong cross-resolution generalization with small commutation error under restriction/prolongation, and structure-preserving performance on a regularized exact/coexact decomposition task. Ablations further link the smoothness of the learned spectral multiplier to stability under geometric perturbations. These results suggest that enforcing intrinsic structure and gauge equivariance yields operator surrogates that are more geometry-consistent and discretization-robust for elliptic PDEs on form-valued fields.

Introduction

Partial differential equations (PDEs) govern a wide range of phenomena in physics, engineering, and the natural sciences. In many applications, the central computational task is not the solution of a single PDE instance, but the repeated evaluation of a solution operator that maps an input field (e.g., a forcing term, boundary data, coefficients) to an output field (e.g., a state, potential, or flux). Classical numerical solvers—finite differences, finite volumes, finite elements, and spectral methods—offer systematic accuracy and stability guarantees, but can be computationally expensive when embedded in design loops, inverse problems, uncertainty quantification, or multi-query regimes

Among data-driven approaches, neural operators aim to learn maps between infinite-dimensional function spaces, enabling discretization-independent inference in principle
22
;
21

20

22

21
, and related integral-operator architectures
20

6
;

As a result, their behavior can degrade under changes of discretization (cross-resolution evaluation) and under geometric changes (e.g., varying metric, mesh, or coordinate charts), and they often lack built-in guarantees of equivariance with respect to representation choices.

A core challenge is that many physically meaningful PDEs are geometric: their inputs/outputs may be vector fields or differential forms, and the governing operators depend on the underlying Riemannian metric through objects such as the Laplace–Beltrami operator and the Hodge star. In such settings, the same geometric field can be represented by different component vectors under different choices of local frame or gauge. Standard convolutional networks, including coordinate-augmented CNNs, are typically not designed to respect these symmetries; their outputs may vary substantially under benign changes of representation, even when the underlying geometric field is unchanged. This issue is closely related to the broader theme of geometric deep learning, which emphasizes architectures that respect symmetry and structure—on groups, manifolds, and meshes—through equivariance principles

In this work we focus on a complementary question: how to design an operator-learning architecture that is intrinsic and gauge-consistent, and that remains stable across geometric and discretization changes. Our approach is guided by classical geometric analysis. For elliptic operators, the spectrum of the Laplace-type operator encodes both geometry and scale, and solution operators can be expressed via spectral multipliers (e.g., resolvents and Green’s functions). Meanwhile, the Helmholtz–Hodge decomposition provides a canonical splitting of vector fields (or 1-forms) into exact, coexact, and harmonic components, underpinning stable formulations in both continuous and discrete settings

Building on these insights, we introduce a Gauge-Equivariant Intrinsic Neural Operator (GINO) that parameterizes the learned operator primarily through intrinsic spectral multipliers of the form
m_{\theta}(\lambda)
, where

\lambda_{g}(k)=k^{\top}Ak

This design explicitly separates (i) geometry-dependent spectral structure from (ii) learnable functional dependence, and we couple it with a gauge-equivariant nonlinearity to ensure consistent transformation under frame changes. The resulting model is intended to behave like a geometry-aware, discretization-consistent surrogate for elliptic solution operators on fields with nontrivial transformation laws.

We validate the approach in a controlled setting where the ground-truth operator is available in closed form in the Fourier domain, enabling diagnostics that directly probe the theory. Across experiments E1–E6, we show that: (i) GINO matches the target operator accurately; (ii) it achieves gauge equivariance up to numerical precision, while coordinate-based CNN baselines exhibit large gauge sensitivity; (iii) it remains stable under structured metric perturbations; (iv) it generalizes across resolutions and approximately commutes with restriction/prolongation operators, indicating reduced discretization dependence; (v) it supports a structure-preserving (regularized) Hodge decomposition task; and (vi) ablations link multiplier smoothness to stability, aligning with theoretical expectations about spectral regularity and perturbation amplification.

We propose an intrinsic operator-learning architecture that parameterizes elliptic solution maps via learned spectral multipliers coupled with gauge-equivariant nonlinearities, aimed at representation- and discretization-consistent behavior. We design a suite of theory-aligned diagnostics for gauge equivariance, metric-perturbation stability, and discretization commutation, enabled by exact Fourier-domain ground truth. We demonstrate strong empirical advantages over coordinate-based CNN baselines on accuracy, gauge consistency, metric stability, and cross-resolution generalization, and we provide ablations linking multiplier roughness to perturbation amplification.

Problem Setup and Target Operator

2.1
Geometric and Functional-Analytic Setting

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a connected, compact, oriented,
d
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. We denote by
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
the space of smooth differential
1
-forms on
\mathcal{M}
. The Riemannian metric
g
induces a pointwise inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{g}
on
1
-forms and the associated
L^{2}
inner product

\langle\omega,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}:=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle\omega(x),\eta(x)\rangle_{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x),\qquad\omega,\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M}),

with norm
\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\langle\omega,\omega\rangle_{L^{2}}
. We write
H^{s}\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
for the Sobolev space of
1
-forms of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
, defined via local charts and a partition of unity (equivalently, through the functional calculus of a fixed elliptic operator on
1
-forms). When no ambiguity arises, we abbreviate
H^{s}\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
as
H^{s}\Omega^{1}
.

We denote by
d:\Omega^{k}(\mathcal{M})\to\Omega^{k+1}(\mathcal{M})
the exterior derivative and by
\delta:\Omega^{k+1}(\mathcal{M})\to\Omega^{k}(\mathcal{M})
its
L^{2}
-adjoint (the codifferential). The (Hodge) Laplacian on
1
-forms is

\Delta_{H}:=d\delta+\delta d:\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M}).

On a compact manifold without boundary,
\Delta_{H}
is essentially self-adjoint, nonnegative, and has discrete spectrum. Throughout, we use the same notation for its continuous extension to Sobolev spaces.

To make the inverse problem well-posed without explicitly quotienting out
harmonic
1
-forms, we introduce a positive shift parameter
\alpha>0
. Define the shifted operator

\mathcal{L}_{g}:=\Delta_{H}+\alpha I,

where
I
denotes the identity on
1
-forms. The shift ensures
\mathcal{L}_{g}
is strictly positive and invertible on
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
, and improves conditioning both analytically and numerically.

Finally, since vector fields are often the physically meaningful objects, we note that
g
provides an isomorphism between
1
-forms and vector fields via the musical isomorphisms
\flat,\sharp
. All statements below may be equivalently read in the language of tangent vector fields, but we adopt the
1
-form formulation because it is naturally compatible with Hodge theory and with discretizations based on discrete exterior calculus.

2.2
Elliptic PDE on 1-Forms and the Solution Operator

We consider the following family of elliptic problems on
1
-forms: for a given forcing
f\in H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}
(with
s\geq 0
typically), find
\omega\in H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{g}\omega=f,\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad(\Delta_{H}+\alpha I)\omega=f.

(2.1)

Equation(
2.1
) covers, as special cases or close relatives, the vector Laplacian and Hodge–Poisson problems arising in geometry processing, computational electromagnetism, and fluid mechanics on surfaces (e.g., via vorticity–stream formulations or Hodge decompositions). The Hodge Laplacian is the canonical second-order elliptic operator acting on differential forms, and thus provides a clean testbed for developing intrinsic, coordinate-free operator learning theory.

Standard elliptic regularity on compact manifolds implies that
\mathcal{L}_{g}
defines a continuous isomorphism between Sobolev spaces,

\mathcal{L}_{g}:H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}\to H^{s-1}\Omega^{1},

and therefore admits a bounded inverse (the resolvent at
-\alpha
),

\mathcal{S}_{g}:=\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-1}:H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}\to H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}.

(2.2)

We refer to
\mathcal{S}_{g}
as the (shifted) Hodge–Poisson solution operator. In this work,
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is the primary target operator to be learned.

Spectrally, since
\Delta_{H}
is self-adjoint and nonnegative on
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
, there exists an
L^{2}
-orthonormal eigenbasis
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
with eigenvalues
0\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots
,
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
, such that
\Delta_{H}\psi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\psi_{k}
. Consequently, for sufficiently regular
f
,

\mathcal{S}_{g}f=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}+\alpha}\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\,\psi_{k},

(2.3)

which exhibits
\mathcal{S}_{g}
as a spectral multiplier (a functional calculus) of
\Delta_{H}
. This representation motivates the model class introduced later: intrinsic neural operators parameterizing spectral multipliers while preserving geometric equivariances.

2.3
Learning Objective and Data Model

We assume access to samples
\{(f_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
drawn from a distribution
\mathcal{D}
over forcings
f
and corresponding solutions
\omega=\mathcal{S}_{g}f
to (
2.1
), where the metric
g
is fixed unless stated otherwise. Concretely, the training pairs may be generated by a classical numerical solver on a discretization of
\mathcal{M}
, or obtained from observations when the underlying elliptic model is appropriate.

Our goal is to learn a parameterized operator family
\{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
mapping
1
-form forcings to
1
-form solutions,

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}:H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}\to H^{s+1}\Omega^{1},

that approximates
\mathcal{S}_{g}
in a strong, geometry-aware sense. Specifically, we target guarantees of the following form:
Operator approximation in Sobolev topology:
\|\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}-\mathcal{S}_{g}\|_{H^{s-1}\to H^{s+1}}
is small, with explicit dependence on model capacity (e.g., spectral truncation level) and parameterization error.
Gauge equivariance: the learned operator is equivariant with respect to changes of local orthonormal frames, reflecting the intrinsic nature of
1
-forms (formally defined in Section
3
).
Discretization consistency: when
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
is implemented on a mesh or point cloud via a convergent discrete Hodge Laplacian, the discrete learned operator converges to its continuum counterpart as resolution increases.

In Sections
4
–
6
we formalize and prove approximation, stability, and continuum–discrete consistency results under the above setup.

3
Gauge-Equivariant Intrinsic Neural Operators

This section introduces a class of intrinsic neural operators acting on
1
-forms that (i) are defined without reference to coordinates, (ii) are equivariant to local changes of orthonormal frames (gauge transformations), and (iii) admit discretizations that commute with refinement under standard convergent discrete Hodge Laplacians. Our construction is motivated by the observation that the target solution operator
\mathcal{S}_{g}=(\Delta_{H}+\alpha I)^{-1}
is a spectral multiplier of
\Delta_{H}
(cf.(
2.3
)), and hence can be approximated by learning appropriate functional calculi of
\Delta_{H}
.

3.1
Gauge Structure on
1
-Forms and an Equivariance Principle

Although a differential
1
-form is an intrinsic geometric object, any numerical representation necessarily uses local frames. Let
\{U_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
be an open cover of
\mathcal{M}
and, on each
U_{\ell}
, let
E_{\ell}(x)=(e_{\ell,1}(x),\dots,e_{\ell,d}(x))
be a smooth local orthonormal frame of
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
. Any
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
admits local coordinates
[\omega]_{E_{\ell}}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

[\omega]_{E_{\ell}}(x)_{j}:=\omega(x)\big(e_{\ell,j}(x)\big).

If the frame is changed to
E^{\prime}_{\ell}(x)=E_{\ell}(x)R_{\ell}(x)
with
R_{\ell}(x)\in O(d)
, then the coordinate representation transforms as

[\omega]_{E^{\prime}_{\ell}}(x)=R_{\ell}(x)^{\top}[\omega]_{E_{\ell}}(x).

(3.1)

This gauge action (pointwise orthogonal transformations of local frames) is unavoidable in computations on tangent/cotangent bundles. A learning architecture intended to represent intrinsic operators on
1
-forms should respect this transformation law.

We therefore adopt the following axiom.

Definition 1

(Gauge equivariance)
.

An operator
\mathcal{T}:\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
is
gauge-equivariant
if for any choice of local orthonormal frames
\{E_{\ell}\}
and any smooth
R_{\ell}:U_{\ell}\to O(d)
defining new frames
E^{\prime}_{\ell}=E_{\ell}R_{\ell}
, the local representations satisfy

[\mathcal{T}(\omega)]_{E^{\prime}_{\ell}}(x)=R_{\ell}(x)^{\top}[\mathcal{T}(\omega)]_{E_{\ell}}(x)\quad\text{whenever}\quad[\omega]_{E^{\prime}_{\ell}}(x)=R_{\ell}(x)^{\top}[\omega]_{E_{\ell}}(x),

(3.2)

for all
x\in U_{\ell}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{T}
commutes with the pointwise action of
O(d)
on the fiber coordinates.

In words, changing the computational gauge (local orthonormal frame) should only rotate the coordinate representation of the output accordingly; the operator itself is independent of this choice.

3.2
Intrinsic Linear Layers via Functional Calculus of
\Delta_{H}

A central design choice is to build linear operator layers from intrinsic differential operators. Since
\Delta_{H}
is self-adjoint and nonnegative on
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
, it admits a spectral decomposition
\Delta_{H}\psi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\psi_{k}
with an
L^{2}
-orthonormal eigenbasis
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
. For any bounded measurable function
m:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, the functional calculus defines a linear operator
m(\Delta_{H})
by

m(\Delta_{H})\omega:=\sum_{k\geq 0}m(\lambda_{k})\langle\omega,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\,\psi_{k},

(3.3)

with convergence in
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
(and in
H^{s}\Omega^{1}
under appropriate growth/regularity of
m
). This construction is coordinate-free and depends only on
(\mathcal{M},g)
.

We now define the basic learnable intrinsic linear layer as a truncated multiplier.

Definition 2

(Truncated spectral multiplier layer)
.

Fix a truncation index
K\in\mathbb{N}
and let
m_{\theta}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a parameterized multiplier (e.g., an MLP in the scalar variable
\lambda
). The
spectral multiplier layer
is

\big(\mathcal{T}_{\theta,K}\omega\big):=\sum_{k=0}^{K}m_{\theta}(\lambda_{k})\langle\omega,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\,\psi_{k}.

(3.4)

The truncation serves two purposes: (i) it yields a finite-dimensional parameterization compatible with discretizations, and (ii) it isolates a controllable approximation error that can be quantified in Sobolev operator norms.

A complementary and often numerically convenient representation uses heat kernels. If
m
is completely monotone or admits a Laplace transform representation
m(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)e^{-t\lambda}\,dt
, then

m(\Delta_{H})\omega(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)\,(e^{-t\Delta_{H}}\omega)(x)\,dt,

(3.5)

where
e^{-t\Delta_{H}}
is the heat semigroup on
1
-forms. This emphasizes that our convolution is intrinsic: it is induced by the geometry through the heat flow rather than by Euclidean translation.

3.3
Gauge-Equivariant Pointwise Nonlinearities on
1
-Forms

To construct expressive nonlinear operator architectures, we interleave intrinsic linear layers with pointwise nonlinearities. However, generic coordinate-wise nonlinearities (e.g., applying a scalar activation to each fiber coordinate in an arbitrary gauge) break gauge equivariance. We therefore restrict to nonlinearities that are equivariant under the
O(d)
action in (
3.1
).

A standard characterization is that continuous
O(d)
-equivariant maps
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are radial:

\varphi(v)=\rho(\|v\|)\,v\quad\text{for some scalar function}\quad\rho:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}.

(3.6)

Accordingly, we define the fiberwise nonlinearity using intrinsic norms.

Definition 3

(Fiberwise gauge-equivariant nonlinearity)
.

Let
\rho_{\theta}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a learnable scalar function. Define
\sigma_{\theta}:\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
by

(\sigma_{\theta}(\omega))(x):=\rho_{\theta}\big(\|\omega(x)\|_{g}\big)\,\omega(x),

(3.7)

where
\|\omega(x)\|_{g}^{2}=\langle\omega(x),\omega(x)\rangle_{g}
. Then
\sigma_{\theta}
is intrinsic and gauge-equivariant.

In practice,
\rho_{\theta}
can be parameterized by a small MLP, a spline, or a polynomial basis with bounded range (useful for stability). Importantly, (
3.7
) depends only on the norm induced by
g
, hence it is invariant to local orthonormal frame rotations.

We also allow an intrinsic gated pointwise linear map that remains gauge-equivariant:

(\mathcal{B}_{\theta}\omega)(x):=b_{\theta}(x)\,\omega(x),

(3.8)

where
b_{\theta}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is a learned scalar field (potentially produced by another intrinsic operator acting on invariants such as
\|\omega\|_{g}
, curvature scalars, or known metadata). Since multiplication by a scalar commutes with
O(d)
, (
3.8
) preserves gauge equivariance.

3.4
The Gauge-Equivariant Intrinsic Neural Operator Architecture

We now assemble the above ingredients into a deep operator network. Let
\omega_{0}:=f
denote the input forcing
1
-form. For
\ell=0,\dots,L-1
, define the hidden state recursion

\omega_{\ell+1}:=\sigma_{\theta_{\ell}}\Big(\mathcal{T}_{\theta_{\ell},K}\omega_{\ell}+\mathcal{B}_{\theta_{\ell}}\omega_{\ell}\Big),

(3.9)

and output
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,K}(f):=\omega_{L}
. Here:

•

\mathcal{T}_{\theta_{\ell},K}
is the truncated intrinsic multiplier layer (
3.4
),

•

\mathcal{B}_{\theta_{\ell}}
is a gauge-compatible pointwise linear map (
3.8
), and

•

\sigma_{\theta_{\ell}}
is the gauge-equivariant nonlinearity (
3.7
).

This architecture can be interpreted as learning a nonlinear perturbation of functional calculi of
\Delta_{H}
while remaining intrinsic. In the elliptic setting, one may also use purely linear versions (setting
\sigma
to identity) to directly approximate resolvents
m(\lambda)\approx(\lambda+\alpha)^{-1}
. We keep the nonlinear form (
3.9
) because it allows modeling operator families with parameter dependence, mild nonlinearity in constitutive relations, or data/model mismatch, while preserving the geometric symmetries.

3.5
Intrinsicness and Gauge Equivariance: Formal Guarantees

We record the two basic structural properties required later for stability and discretization analysis.

Proposition 4

(Intrinsicness)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a compact smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. Let
\varphi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
be a diffeomorphism and set
g^{\prime}:=\varphi^{*}g
. Consider a network operator
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(g)}_{\theta,K}
built by composing layers of the form

\omega\mapsto\sigma_{\theta}\big(\mathcal{T}^{(g)}_{\theta,K}\omega+\mathcal{B}^{(g)}_{\theta}\omega\big),

where:

(1)

Spectral layer
\mathcal{T}^{(g)}_{\theta,K}
is a truncated functional calculus of the Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{H,g}
on
1
-forms:

\mathcal{T}^{(g)}_{\theta,K}:=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,g})\,\Pi^{(g)}_{\leq\Lambda},

(3.10)

for some cutoff
\Lambda=\Lambda(K,g)
, and where
\Pi^{(g)}_{\leq\Lambda}
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal spectral projector of
\Delta_{H,g}
onto the direct sum of eigenspaces with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
. (This covers top-
K
modes truncation up to multiplicity; it is equivalent to truncating by the first
K
eigenmodes when eigenvalues are ordered.)

(2)

Pointwise linear term
\mathcal{B}^{(g)}_{\theta}
is multiplication by a scalar field
b^{(g)}_{\theta}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
:

(\mathcal{B}^{(g)}_{\theta}\omega)(x)=b^{(g)}_{\theta}(x)\,\omega(x),

(3.11)

and the scalar field is natural under pullback:

b^{(g^{\prime})}_{\theta}:=\varphi^{*}b^{(g)}_{\theta}.

(3.12)

(3)

Nonlinearity
\sigma_{\theta}
is fiberwise radial with respect to the metric:

(\sigma^{(g)}_{\theta}(\omega))(x)=\rho_{\theta}(\|\omega(x)\|_{g})\,\omega(x),

(3.13)

where
\rho_{\theta}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is fixed and
|\cdot|_{g}
is the pointwise norm induced by
g
on
1
-forms.

Then the operator is intrinsic in the sense that

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(g^{\prime})}_{\theta,K}(\varphi^{*}f)=\varphi^{*}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(g)}_{\theta,K}(f)\big)\qquad\text{for all }f\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M}).

(3.14)

Proof.

We first establish the key intertwining identity:

\varphi^{*}(\Delta_{H,g}\,\omega)=\Delta_{H,g^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}\omega)\qquad\forall\,\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M}).

(3.15)

Recall
\Delta_{H,g}=d\delta_{g}+\delta_{g}d
on
1
-forms, where
d
is the exterior derivative (metric-independent) and
\delta_{g}
is the codifferential (metric-dependent). Two standard identities are:
Pullback commutes with
d
:

\varphi^{*}(d\eta)=d(\varphi^{*}\eta)\quad\forall\,\eta\in\Omega^{k}(\mathcal{M}).

(3.16)

Pullback intertwines Hodge star with pulled-back metric:

\varphi^{*}(*_{g}\eta)=*_{g^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}\eta)\quad\forall\,\eta\in\Omega^{k}(\mathcal{M}).

(3.17)

This follows from the defining property of
*
: for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{k}
,

\alpha\wedge*_{g}\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g},

and the facts that
\varphi^{*}(\alpha\wedge\gamma)=\varphi^{*}\alpha\wedge\varphi^{*}\gamma
, and that

\varphi^{*}\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}=\langle\varphi^{*}\alpha,\varphi^{*}\beta\rangle_{g^{\prime}}
,

\varphi^{*}(d\mathrm{vol}_{g})=d\mathrm{vol}_{g^{\prime}}
.

Using
\delta_{g}=(-1)^{d(k+1)+1}*_{g}d*_{g}
on
(k+1)
-forms, combine
3.16
–
3.17
to obtain, for any
\eta\in\Omega^{k+1}
,

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(\delta_{g}\eta)

\displaystyle=c\,\varphi^{*}\big(*_{g}d*_{g}\eta\big)

\displaystyle=c\,*_{g^{\prime}}\varphi^{*}\big(d*_{g}\eta\big)

\displaystyle=c\,*_{g^{\prime}}d\,\varphi^{*}\big(*_{g}\eta\big)

\displaystyle=c\,*_{g^{\prime}}d\,*_{g^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}\eta)=\delta_{g^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}\eta),

(3.18)

where
c=(-1)^{d(k+1)+1}
. Thus
\varphi^{*}\delta_{g}=\delta_{g^{\prime}}\varphi^{*}
. Finally,

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(\Delta_{H,g}\omega)

\displaystyle=\varphi^{*}(d\delta_{g}\omega+\delta_{g}d\omega)

\displaystyle=d\,\delta_{g^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}\omega)+\delta_{g^{\prime}}\,d(\varphi^{*}\omega)=\Delta_{H,g^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}\omega),

(3.19)

which proves
3.15
.

Let
A_{g}:=\Delta_{H,g}
viewed as a self-adjoint nonnegative operator on
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
. Define the pullback operator
U_{\varphi}:L^{2}\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M},g)\to L^{2}\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M},g^{\prime})
by
U_{\varphi}\omega:=\varphi^{*}\omega
. One checks that
U_{\varphi}
is unitary because the
L^{2}
inner product on forms is preserved when both the metric and volume form are pulled back.

From
3.15
we have the operator intertwining relation (on smooth forms, hence by density on the operator domain):

U_{\varphi}A_{g}=A_{g^{\prime}}U_{\varphi}.

(3.20)

A standard consequence of
3.20
for self-adjoint operators is that the entire spectral calculus intertwines:

U_{\varphi}\,F(A_{g})=F(A_{g^{\prime}})\,U_{\varphi}\quad\text{for all bounded Borel functions }F.

(3.21)

In particular, taking
F=m_{\theta}
yields

\varphi^{*}\big(m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,g})\,\omega\big)=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,g^{\prime}})\,(\varphi^{*}\omega).

(3.22)

Likewise, taking
F=\mathbf{1}_{[0,\Lambda]}

3.6
Discrete Realization and Design for Consistency

While the discretization analysis is deferred to Section

, we emphasize a design principle: the architecture should admit a discrete counterpart obtained by replacing
\Delta_{H}
with a convergent discrete Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{H,h}
(e.g., from discrete exterior calculus or compatible finite elements), and replacing

with the associated discrete inner product. Under standard spectral convergence assumptions, the truncated multiplier layer (
3.4
) admits a discrete implementation

\big(\mathcal{T}_{\theta,K}^{(h)}\omega_{h}\big):=\sum_{k=0}^{K}m_{\theta}(\lambda_{k,h})\langle\omega_{h},\psi_{k,h}\rangle_{h}\,\psi_{k,h},

(3.41)

which mirrors the continuum definition and is the key to establishing continuum–discrete commutation bounds for the learned operator.

3.7
Discussion: Relation to the Target Elliptic Solution Operator

The shifted Hodge–Poisson solution operator
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is itself a spectral multiplier
m_{\star}(\lambda)=(\lambda+\alpha)^{-1}
. Consequently, when the network is specialized to the linear case (identity
\sigma
,
\mathcal{B}\equiv 0
), the model class contains direct approximants of
\mathcal{S}_{g}
via learned multipliers
m_{\theta}
on a truncated spectrum. The nonlinear extension (
3.9
) retains intrinsicness and gauge equivariance while enlarging expressivity to cover (i) parameterized families of elliptic operators (e.g., variable coefficients), (ii) mild nonlinearity arising from modeling error or constitutive laws, and (iii) practical constraints where only partial spectral information is used and residual corrections are beneficial.

In the next sections we quantify approximation error in Sobolev operator norms (Section
4
), establish stability under metric perturbations (Section
5
), and prove discretization consistency for the discrete realization(

) (Section

).

4
Sobolev Operator Approximation

This section establishes quantitative approximation bounds for the elliptic solution operator

\mathcal{S}_{g}=(\Delta_{H}+\alpha I)^{-1}

(4.1)

by the gauge-equivariant intrinsic neural operator class introduced in Section
3
. The analysis is carried out in Sobolev topologies on
1
-forms and is stated in operator norms between Sobolev spaces. A key point is that uniform operator-norm convergence on the full space (
H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}\to H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}
) is impossible for purely spectral truncations; convergence holds on smoother input classes (compact embeddings), which is the relevant regime for PDE solution operators and for data distributions with controlled regularity.

4.1
Sobolev Norms via the Hodge Spectrum

Let
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
be an
L^{2}
-orthonormal eigenbasis of the Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{H}
on
1
-forms, with eigenvalues
0\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots
,
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
:

\Delta_{H}\psi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\psi_{k}.

(4.2)

For
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we use the standard spectral characterization of Sobolev norms on
1
-forms:

\|\omega\|_{H^{r}\Omega^{1}}^{2}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(1+\lambda_{k})^{r}\left|\langle\omega,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|^{2},

(4.3)

which is equivalent to the usual chart-based definition on compact manifolds. In particular, for any bounded Borel function
m:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, the operator
m(\Delta_{H})
satisfies

\langle m(\Delta_{H})\omega,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=m(\lambda_{k})\langle\omega,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

(4.4)

We also record the smoothing structure of the shifted resolvent
m_{\star}(\lambda)=(\lambda+\alpha)^{-1}
:

\mathcal{S}_{g}=m_{\star}(\Delta_{H}),\qquad m_{\star}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda+\alpha}.

(4.5)

4.2
Truncated Functional Calculus and the Approximation Family

Because implementations (and learning) typically operate on a finite spectral subspace, we introduce spectral cutoffs in a basis-independent manner.

For
\Lambda>0
, let
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
denote the
L^{2}
-orthogonal spectral projector onto the direct sum of eigenspaces with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
:

\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\omega:=\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda}\langle\omega,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\psi_{k}.

(4.6)

Given a multiplier
m_{\theta}
, define the truncated intrinsic operator

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}:=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}.

(4.7)

This covers the linear specialization of the architecture in Section
3
(with identity nonlinearity and
\mathcal{B}\equiv 0
), and it is the core object for Sobolev approximation of the elliptic resolvent.

We decompose the approximation error into (i) spectral truncation bias and (ii) multiplier approximation error:

\mathcal{S}_{g}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}=\underbrace{\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})}_{\text{truncation bias}}+\underbrace{\left(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\right)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}}_{\text{multiplier error}}.

(4.8)

4.3
Mapping Properties of the Elliptic Resolvent

We first state the basic boundedness of
\mathcal{S}_{g}
between Sobolev spaces (a standard consequence of elliptic regularity, here recorded in the spectral norm framework).

Lemma 1

(Sobolev boundedness of
\mathcal{S}_{g}
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be compact and without boundary, and let
\alpha>0
. For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the resolvent

\mathcal{S}_{g}:=(\Delta_{H}+\alpha I)^{-1}

extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator

\mathcal{S}_{g}:H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}\Omega^{1},

and there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}<\infty
(depending only on
\alpha
) such that

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}f\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}\qquad\forall f\in H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}.

(4.9)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is compact and
\Delta_{H}
is an elliptic, self-adjoint, nonnegative operator on
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
, its spectrum is discrete and there exists an
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
with eigenvalues
0\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots
,
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
, such that

\Delta_{H}\psi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\psi_{k}.

Every
f\in L^{2}\Omega^{1}
admits an expansion
f=\sum_{k\geq 0}f_{k}\psi_{k}
with
f_{k}:=\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}
, converging in
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
.

We define Sobolev norms via the spectral calculus (equivalent to the standard chart-based definition on compact manifolds):

\|f\|_{H^{r}\Omega^{1}}^{2}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(1+\lambda_{k})^{r}|f_{k}|^{2},\qquad r\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.10)

This yields a Hilbert space
H^{r}\Omega^{1}
as the completion of
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
under
\|\cdot\|_{H^{r}}
.

For smooth
f\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
(hence in
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
), define

\mathcal{S}_{g}f:=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}+\alpha}f_{k}\psi_{k}.

(4.11)

This series converges in
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
because
(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{-1}
is bounded by
\alpha^{-1}
and
\sum|f_{k}|^{2}<\infty
.

Let
f\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
and write
f=\sum f_{k}\psi_{k}
. Then by
4.10
and
4.11
,

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}f\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}\left|\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}+\alpha}\right|^{2}|f_{k}|^{2}.

(4.12)

Factor the weight as

(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}=\left(\frac{(1+\lambda_{k})^{2}}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}\right)(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}.

(4.13)

Substituting
4.13
into
4.12
gives

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}f\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{(1+\lambda_{k})^{2}}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}\right)(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}|f_{k}|^{2}\leq\left(\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{(1+\lambda)^{2}}{(\lambda+\alpha)^{2}}\right)\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}.

(4.14)

Define

C_{\alpha}:=\left(\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda+\alpha}\right).

(4.15)

Then
4.14
yields

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}f\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}\qquad\forall f\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M}).

(4.16)

It remains to check that
C_{\alpha}<\infty
. Consider
r(\lambda):=\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda+\alpha}
for
\lambda\geq 0
. This is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, and

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}r(\lambda)=1,\qquad r(0)=\frac{1}{\alpha}.

Moreover,

r^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{\alpha-1}{(\lambda+\alpha)^{2}},

so
r
is monotone increasing if
\alpha>1
, monotone decreasing if
\alpha<1
, and constant if
\alpha=1
. Hence

\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}r(\lambda)=\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\}<\infty,

so
C_{\alpha}=\max\left\{1,\alpha^{-1}\right\}
is a valid choice (and any larger constant also works). Therefore
4.16
holds with finite
C_{\alpha}
.

The space
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
is dense in
H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}
by construction. Let
f\in H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}
and choose a sequence
\{f^{(n)}\}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
such that
f^{(n)}\to f
in
H^{s-1}
. Then by
4.16
,

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}f^{(n)}-\mathcal{S}_{g}f^{(m)}\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f^{(n)}-f^{(m)}\|_{H^{s-1}},

so
\{\mathcal{S}_{g}f^{(n)}\}
is Cauchy in
H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}
and converges to a limit we define as
\mathcal{S}_{g}f
. The bound
4.9
follows by taking limits:

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}f\|_{H^{s+1}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mathcal{S}_{g}f^{(n)}\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{\alpha}\|f^{(n)}\|_{H^{s-1}}=C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}.

Uniqueness of the extension follows because any two bounded linear extensions coincide on the dense subspace
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
.
∎

4.4
Spectral Truncation Bias on Smoother Input Classes

A key subtlety is that
\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})
does not converge to zero in operator norm (
H^{s-1}\to H^{s+1}
) as
\Lambda\to\infty
; one can concentrate
f
on arbitrarily high eigenmodes, for which truncation discards non-negligible components. Convergence holds on smoother input spaces
H^{s-1+\gamma}
with
\gamma>0
, which is the natural regime for compactness and for many data models.

Lemma 2

(Truncation bias in Sobolev operator norm)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma>0
. For
\Lambda>0
, let
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
be the
L^{2}
-orthogonal spectral projector of
\Delta_{H}
on
1
-forms onto the span of eigenspaces with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
. Then

\big\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})\big\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma/2},

(4.17)

where
\mathcal{S}_{g}=(\Delta_{H}+\alpha I)^{-1}
with
\alpha>0
, and
C_{\alpha}<\infty
depends only on
\alpha
(and the choice of equivalent Sobolev norm convention).

Proof.

Let
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be an
L^{2}
-orthonormal eigenbasis of
\Delta_{H}
on
1
-forms,
\Delta_{H}\psi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\psi_{k}
with
0\leq\lambda_{k}\uparrow\!\!\uparrow\infty
. For
f\in H^{s-1+\gamma}\Omega^{1}
, write

f=\sum_{k\geq 0}f_{k}\psi_{k},\qquad f_{k}:=\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

By definition of
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
,

(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})f=\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\Lambda}f_{k}\psi_{k}.

Using the spectral representation of
\mathcal{S}_{g}
,

\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})f=\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\Lambda}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}+\alpha}f_{k}\psi_{k}.

Using the spectral Sobolev norm convention

\|u\|_{H^{r}}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(1+\lambda_{k})^{r}|u_{k}|^{2},

we have

\big\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})f\big\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}=\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\Lambda}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}|f_{k}|^{2}.

(4.18)

Rewrite the weight in
4.18
as

(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}=\left(\frac{(1+\lambda_{k})^{2}}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}\right)(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}.

Insert an additional factor
(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\gamma}(1+\lambda_{k})^{\gamma}
to match the input norm:

(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}=(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1+\gamma}(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\gamma}.

Therefore,

(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}=\left(\frac{(1+\lambda_{k})^{2}}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}\right)(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\gamma}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1+\gamma}.

(4.19)

Combine
4.18
–
4.19
to obtain

\big\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})f\big\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}=\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\Lambda}\left(\frac{(1+\lambda_{k})^{2}}{(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{2}}\right)(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\gamma}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1+\gamma}|f_{k}|^{2}.

Hence,

\big\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})f\big\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\leq\left(\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{(1+\lambda)^{2}}{(\lambda+\alpha)^{2}}\right)\left(\sup_{\lambda>\Lambda}(1+\lambda)^{-\gamma}\right)\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\Lambda}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1+\gamma}|f_{k}|^{2}.

(4.20)

Since
\gamma>0
,
(1+\lambda)^{-\gamma}
is decreasing, so

\sup_{\lambda>\Lambda}(1+\lambda)^{-\gamma}=(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma}.

(4.21)

Moreover, the first supremum is finite because
\alpha>0
and the rational function
\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda+\alpha}
is bounded on
[0,\infty)
; explicitly,

\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{(1+\lambda)^{2}}{(\lambda+\alpha)^{2}}=\left(\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda+\alpha}\right)^{2}\leq\max\left\{1,\alpha^{-1}\right\}^{2}.

(4.22)

Finally, the sum over
\{\lambda_{k}>\Lambda\}
is bounded by the full Sobolev norm:

\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\Lambda}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1+\gamma}|f_{k}|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1+\gamma}|f_{k}|^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}}^{2}.

(4.23)

Substituting
4.21
–
4.22
into
4.20
yields

\big\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})f\big\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}^{2}(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}}^{2},

where
C_{\alpha}:=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda+\alpha}\leq\max\left\{1,\alpha^{-1}\right\}
. Taking square roots gives

\big\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})f\big\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma/2}\|f\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}}.

Since this holds for all
f\in H^{s-1+\gamma}\Omega^{1}
, we conclude

\big\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})\big\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma/2}.

∎

4.5
Multiplier Approximation Error on the Truncated Subspace

We now quantify the effect of approximating the true multiplier
m_{\star}(\lambda)=(\lambda+\alpha)^{-1}
on
[0,\Lambda]
.

Define the uniform approximation error

\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta):=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda)-m_{\star}(\lambda)\right|.

(4.24)

Lemma 3

(Uniform multiplier error implies Sobolev operator bound)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda>0
. Let
m_{\star}(\lambda)=(\lambda+\alpha)^{-1}
and let
m_{\theta}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be any bounded function. Define

\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta):=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}|m_{\theta}(\lambda)-m_{\star}(\lambda)|.

Let
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
be the
L^{2}
-orthogonal spectral projector of
\Delta_{H}
onto the sum of eigenspaces with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
. Then

\big\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\big\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq(1+\Lambda)\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta).

(4.25)

Proof.

Let
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be an
L^{2}
-orthonormal eigenbasis of
\Delta_{H}
on
1
-forms, with eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
. For
f\in H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}
, write

f=\sum_{k\geq 0}f_{k}\psi_{k},\qquad f_{k}:=\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

By definition of
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
,

\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}f=\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda}f_{k}\psi_{k}.

Since
m(\Delta_{H})
acts diagonally in this eigenbasis, we have

\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}f=\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda}\big(m_{\star}(\lambda_{k})-m_{\theta}(\lambda_{k})\big)f_{k}\psi_{k}.

(4.26)

Using the spectral definition of Sobolev norms,

\|u\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}|u_{k}|^{2},

the output in
4.26
yields

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}f\Big\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}\big|m_{\star}(\lambda_{k})-m_{\theta}(\lambda_{k})\big|^{2}|f_{k}|^{2}.

(4.27)

By definition of
\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta)
, for all
k
with
\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda
,

|m_{\star}(\lambda_{k})-m_{\theta}(\lambda_{k})|\leq\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta).

(4.28)

Substitute
4.28
into
4.27
:

\Big\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}f\Big\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta)^{2}\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}|f_{k}|^{2}.

(4.29)

Now note that for
\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda
,

(1+\lambda_{k})^{s+1}=(1+\lambda_{k})^{2}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}\leq(1+\Lambda)^{2}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}.

(4.30)

Applying
4.30
to
4.29
gives

\Big\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}f\Big\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\leq(1+\Lambda)^{2}\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta)^{2}\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}|f_{k}|^{2}.

(4.31)

Since the sum over
\{\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda\}
is dominated by the full sum,

\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}|f_{k}|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}|f_{k}|^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}.

(4.32)

Combining
4.31
and
4.32
yields

\Big\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}f\Big\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\leq(1+\Lambda)^{2}\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta)^{2}\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}.

Taking square roots,

\Big|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}f\Big|_{H^{s+1}}\leq(1+\Lambda)\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta)\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}.

Since this holds for all
f\in H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}
, we conclude

\big\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\big\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq(1+\Lambda)\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta),

which is exactly the desired bound.
∎

4.6
Main Approximation Theorem (Bias–Approximation Decomposition)

We now combine the preceding bounds using the decomposition
4.8
.

Theorem 4

(Sobolev operator approximation of the elliptic solution operator)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma>0
, and
\Lambda>0
. Let

\mathcal{S}_{g}:=(\Delta_{H}+\alpha I)^{-1}=m_{\star}(\Delta_{H}),\qquad m_{\star}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda+\alpha}.

Let
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
be the
L^{2}
-orthogonal spectral projector onto the direct sum of eigenspaces with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
. For any parameterized multiplier
m_{\theta}
, define the truncated intrinsic approximation

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}:=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}.

Define the uniform multiplier error on
[0,\Lambda]
by

\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta):=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}|m_{\theta}(\lambda)-m_{\star}(\lambda)|.

Then there exists
C_{\alpha}<\infty
depending only on
\alpha
such that

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma/2}+(1+\Lambda)\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta).

(4.33)

Proof.

Using the definitions and the fact that
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
is a projection (
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}+(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})=I
), we write

\mathcal{S}_{g}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}=\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})+\big(\mathcal{S}_{g}-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}.

(4.34)

Since
\mathcal{S}_{g}=m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})
, the second term becomes

\big(\mathcal{S}_{g}-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}=\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}.

(4.35)

Thus,

\mathcal{S}_{g}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}=\underbrace{\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})}_{A_{\Lambda}}+\underbrace{\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}}_{B_{\theta,\Lambda}}.

(4.36)

Let
X:=H^{s-1+\gamma}\Omega^{1}
and
Y:=H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}
. For bounded linear operators
A_{\Lambda},B_{\theta,\Lambda}:X\longrightarrow Y
,

\|A_{\Lambda}+B_{\theta,\Lambda}\|_{X\longrightarrow Y}\leq\|A_{\Lambda}\|_{X\longrightarrow Y}+\|B_{\theta,\Lambda}\|_{X\longrightarrow Y}.

(4.37)

Applying
4.37
to
4.36
yields

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}+\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}.

(4.38)

By Lemma
2
,

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma/2}.

(4.39)

Lemma
3
gives the bound

\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq(1+\Lambda)\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta).

(4.40)

We need the operator norm on the smaller domain
H^{s-1+\gamma}
rather than
H^{s-1}
. Since
H^{s-1+\gamma}\hookrightarrow H^{s-1}
continuously, and with the spectral convention
4.3
one has the contractive embedding

\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}}\qquad\forall f\in H^{s-1+\gamma}\Omega^{1},

(4.41)

because
(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}\leq(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1+\gamma}
for all
k
. Therefore, for any bounded linear operator
T:H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}
,

\|T\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}=\sup_{f\neq 0}\frac{\|Tf\|_{H^{s+1}}}{\|f\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}}}\leq\sup_{f\neq 0}\frac{\|Tf\|_{H^{s+1}}}{\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}}=\|T\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}.

(4.42)

Apply
4.42
to
T=B_{\theta,\Lambda}=\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
and combine with
4.41
to obtain

\|\big(m_{\star}(\Delta_{H})-m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H})\big)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq(1+\Lambda)\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta).

(4.43)

Substitute
4.39
and
4.43
into
4.38
:

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma/2}+(1+\Lambda)\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta),

which is exactly the desired bound.
∎

4.7
Discussion and Consequences

(i)

Interpretation of the two terms. The right-hand side of
4.33
is the sum of:

•

a truncation (bias) term decaying as
(1+\Lambda)^{-\gamma/2}
, which reflects that higher input regularity (
\gamma>0
) suppresses high-frequency components;

•

a multiplier approximation term growing as
(1+\Lambda)\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta)
, which quantifies the difficulty of approximating the resolvent multiplier uniformly up to frequency
\Lambda
.

(ii)

Impossibility of operator-norm convergence on
H^{s-1}\to H^{s+1}
via truncation. For completeness, note that for the hard truncation operator
\mathcal{S}_{g}\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
one has

\left|\mathcal{S}_{g}(I-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})\right|_{H^{s-1}\to H^{s+1}}\not\to 0,

since choosing
f=\psi_{k}
with
\lambda_{k}>\Lambda
yields
\mathcal{S}_{g}f=(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{-1}\psi_{k}
while
\mathcal{S}_{g}\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}f=0
, and the ratio
\left|(\lambda_{k}+\alpha)^{-1}\psi_{k}\right|_{H^{s+1}}/\left|\psi_{k}\right|_{H^{s-1}}
stays bounded away from zero as
k\to\infty
. This justifies working with smoother input classes
H^{s-1+\gamma}
, which are compactly embedded into
H^{s-1}
on compact manifolds and correspond to realistic forcing distributions.

(iii)

From frequency cutoff to mode count. If one prefers a truncation level
K
(number of retained modes) rather than
\Lambda
, a Weyl-type relation
\lambda_{K}\asymp K^{2/d}
(for fixed
(\mathcal{M},g)
) converts
4.33
into rates in
K
. This translation is optional for the main theory; the
\Lambda
-formulation is basis- and multiplicity-robust.

(iv)

Connection to the full nonlinear architecture. Theorem
4
is stated for the linear truncated functional calculus
4.7
, which is the natural approximation class for the elliptic resolvent. The same proof strategy extends to residual/nonlinear variants provided each layer is Lipschitz on
H^{s+1}
and remains a function of intrinsic operators; the resulting bounds accumulate multiplicatively/additively with depth via standard stability estimates. We focus here on the linear case because it isolates the elliptic operator learning mechanism and yields the sharpest Sobolev approximation statement for
(\Delta_{H}+\alpha I)^{-1}
.

This completes the Sobolev operator approximation result. Section
5
will quantify stability of both the true resolvent and the learned operator family under metric perturbations, and Section
6
will establish discretization consistency for convergent discrete Hodge Laplacians.

5
Stability under Metric Perturbations and Gauge Changes

This section quantifies how both the true elliptic solution operator and its learned intrinsic approximation vary under perturbations of the underlying geometry. The guiding principle is that the shifted Hodge–Poisson resolvent

\mathcal{S}_{g}=(\Delta_{H,g}+\alpha I)^{-1}

depends continuously on the metric
g
in appropriate topologies, and the intrinsic neural operator family inherits a comparable stability provided its learned multipliers and nonlinearities are uniformly controlled. We separate (i) metric stability (changes in
g
) from (ii) gauge stability (changes of local orthonormal frames). The latter is exact at the continuum level due to gauge equivariance, while the former yields quantitative bounds.

5.1
Metric Perturbation Model and Notation

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be as in Section
2
. We consider a second metric
\tilde{g}
on
\mathcal{M}
satisfying uniform equivalence and regularity bounds. Fix an integer
r\geq 2
and assume:

•

Uniform equivalence: there exists
\kappa\geq 1
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
v\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
,

\kappa^{-1}g_{x}(v,v)\leq\tilde{g}_{x}(v,v)\leq\kappa g_{x}(v,v).

(5.1)

•

Regularity:
g,\tilde{g}\in C^{r}(\mathcal{M})
and
\|g\|_{C^{r}}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}}\leq M
for some
M<\infty
.

•

Small perturbation:
\|\tilde{g}-g\|_{C^{r}}\leq\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small (depending on
M,\kappa
).

We denote by
\Delta_{H,g}
and
\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}
the Hodge Laplacians on
1
-forms induced by
g
and
\tilde{g}
, and define the associated shifted operators

\mathcal{L}_{g}:=\Delta_{H,g}+\alpha I,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}:=\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}+\alpha I,\qquad\alpha>0.

(5.2)

Their inverses
\mathcal{S}_{g}=\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}=\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}^{-1}
are bounded maps
H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
(Lemma
1
).

5.2
Stability of the True Elliptic Solution Operator

We first control the difference
\mathcal{S}_{g}-\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}
in Sobolev operator norms. The argument follows a resolvent identity coupled with bounds on the metric dependence of
\Delta_{H}
.

5.2.1
A resolvent identity

For boundedly invertible operators
\mathcal{L}_{g},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}
on a common Banach space, one has

\mathcal{S}_{g}-\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}=\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-1}-\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}^{-1}=\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}-\mathcal{L}_{g})\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-1}.

(5.3)

Thus, stability reduces to estimating
\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}-\mathcal{L}_{g}=\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g}
as a map between Sobolev spaces.

5.2.2
Metric dependence of the Hodge Laplacian

In local coordinates,
\Delta_{H}
is a second-order elliptic differential operator on
1
-forms with coefficients depending smoothly on
g
and finitely many derivatives of
g
. Under the
C^{r}
assumption with
r\geq 2
, the coefficient difference satisfies (schematically)

\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g}=\sum_{|\beta|\leq 2}a_{\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta},\quad\text{with}\quad\|a_{\beta}\|_{C^{r-|\beta|}}\lesssim\|\tilde{g}-g\|_{C^{r}}.

(5.4)

Consequently, for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
with
s\leq r-1
, the operator difference extends to a bounded map

\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g}:H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}\longrightarrow H^{s-1}\Omega^{1},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g}\|_{H^{s+1}\longrightarrow H^{s-1}}\leq C\|\tilde{g}-g\|_{C^{r}},

(5.5)

where
C
depends on
(\mathcal{M},d)
and the a priori bounds
M,\kappa
.

5.2.3
Operator-norm stability bound

Combining
5.3
, Lemma
1
applied to
g
and
\tilde{g}
, and
5.5
, yields the main stability estimate.

Theorem 1

(Metric stability of the shifted Hodge–Poisson resolvent)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a compact smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary, let
\alpha>0
, and let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Fix an integer
r\geq 2
such that
r\geq s+2
. Let
\tilde{g}
be another
C^{r}
Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{M}
satisfying the uniform equivalence condition: there exists
\kappa\geq 1
such that

\kappa^{-1}g_{x}(v,v)\leq\tilde{g}_{x}(v,v)\leq\kappa g_{x}(v,v)\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{M},\ \forall v\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}.

(5.6)

Assume moreover that
\|g\|_{C^{r}}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}}\leq M
for some
M<\infty
. Define

\mathcal{L}_{g}:=\Delta_{H,g}+\alpha I,\qquad\mathcal{S}_{g}:=\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-1},

and similarly for
\tilde{g}
. Then there exists a constant
C<\infty
, depending only on
\alpha
,
\kappa
,
M
,
\mathcal{M}
, and
r
, such that

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}-\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\ \leq\ C\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}}.

(5.7)

Proof.

Since
\alpha>0
, both
\mathcal{L}_{g}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}
are injective and have bounded inverses on Sobolev scales (Lemma
1
applied to each metric). The algebraic identity

A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}

with
A=\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}
and
B=\mathcal{L}_{g}
yields

\mathcal{S}_{g}-\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}=\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-1}-\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}^{-1}=\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{g}}-\mathcal{L}_{g})\mathcal{S}_{g}=\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}(\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g})\mathcal{S}_{g}.

(5.8)

Therefore,

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}-\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq\|\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\cdot\|\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g}\|_{H^{s+1}\longrightarrow H^{s-1}}\cdot\|\mathcal{S}_{g}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}.

(5.9)

Thus it suffices to (i) bound the two resolvent norms uniformly under
5.1
, and (ii) bound the Laplacian difference by
C\left|g-\tilde{g}\right|_{C^{r}}
.

Lemma
1
gives, for each fixed metric
h
,

\|\mathcal{S}_{h}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha}(h),

(5.10)

where the constant can be taken as

C_{\alpha}(h)=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda+\alpha}\leq\max\left\{1,\alpha^{-1}\right\}.

The right-hand side is independent of
h
; the only subtlety is that our Sobolev norms
H^{t}\Omega^{1}
depend on the metric. However, under the uniform equivalence assumption
5.1
and the
C^{r}
bounds, the Sobolev norms defined using
g
and
\tilde{g}
are equivalent with constants depending only on
\kappa
,
M
,
\mathcal{M}
, and
r
. Concretely, for each fixed
t\in[-r,r]
there exists
c_{t},C_{t}>0
such that

c_{t}\|\omega\|_{H^{t}(\tilde{g})}\leq\|\omega\|_{H^{t}(g)}\leq C_{t}\|\omega\|_{H^{t}(\tilde{g})}\qquad\forall\omega.

(5.11)

(For compact manifolds this is standard: it follows by comparing the coordinate definitions of Sobolev norms via partitions of unity and using
5.1
plus boundedness of metric coefficients and derivatives up to order
r
.)

Using
5.11
to move between
g
-Sobolev and
\tilde{g}
-Sobolev norms, we may bound both resolvents by a common constant

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}+\|\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\alpha,\kappa,M,\mathcal{M},r}.

(5.12)

We will absorb these constants into a generic
C
.

Fix a finite atlas
\{(U_{\ell},\chi_{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
and a subordinate partition of unity
\{\eta_{\ell}\}
. On each chart, the Hodge Laplacian on
1
-forms is a second-order linear differential operator whose coefficients are smooth functions of the metric components and finitely many derivatives of those components. More precisely, in local coordinates one can write

(\Delta_{H,h}\omega)_{i}=\sum_{|\beta|\leq 2}a^{(h)}_{i,\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta}\omega(x)\qquad\text{on }U_{\ell},

(5.13)

where
h\in\{g,\tilde{g}\}
, the index
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
denotes the component of the
1
-form, and each coefficient
a^{(h)}_{i,\beta}
depends smoothly on
\{h_{pq}\}
,
\{\partial h_{pq}\}
, and
\{\partial^{2}h_{pq}\}
(equivalently, on
h
and its Christoffel symbols and curvature terms). This can be justified, for instance, by using the Weitzenböck identity on
1
-forms,

\Delta_{H,h}=\nabla_{h}^{*}\nabla_{h}+\mathrm{Ric}_{h},

(5.14)

and expanding
\nabla_{h}
in coordinates; the principal coefficients are
h^{jk}
and lower-order coefficients involve
\Gamma(h)
and
\partial\Gamma(h)
, hence derivatives of
h
up to second order.

Since
g,\tilde{g}\in C^{r}
with
\|g\|_{C^{r}}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}}\leq M
and
r\geq 2
, the coefficients satisfy

\|a^{(\tilde{g})}_{i,\beta}-a^{(g)}_{i,\beta}\|_{C^{r-|\beta|}(U_{\ell})}\ \leq C\|\tilde{g}-g\|_{C^{r}(U_{\ell})}.

(5.15)

The constant
C
depends on
M
,
\kappa
, the chart, and
r
, but not on the particular
\tilde{g},g
beyond those bounds. (This is a consequence of smooth dependence of the formulas for inverse metric, Christoffel symbols, and curvature on
h
and its derivatives, combined with the uniform ellipticity from
5.1
.)

Therefore, on each
U_{\ell}
,

(\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g})\omega=\sum_{|\beta|\leq 2}b_{i,\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta}\omega,\qquad b_{i,\beta}:=a^{(\tilde{g})}_{i,\beta}-a^{(g)}_{i,\beta},

(5.16)

with coefficient bounds
5.15
.

We now estimate the operator norm of the difference. Fix
\ell
and localize
\omega
by
\eta_{\ell}\omega
. Since
|\beta|\leq 2
,

\|\partial^{\beta}(\eta_{\ell}\omega)\|_{H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|\eta_{\ell}\omega\|_{H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(5.17)

Next we use a standard Sobolev multiplier fact on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
: if
b\in C^{m}
with integer
m\geq\lceil s-1\rceil
, then multiplication by
b
is bounded on
H^{s-1}
, and

\|bu\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq C\|b\|_{C^{m}}\|u\|_{H^{s-1}}.

(5.18)

(One may prove
5.18
by Littlewood–Paley theory or by commutator estimates; on a compact manifold this is obtained chartwise and patched with partitions of unity. Our assumption
r\geq s+2
ensures
r-|\beta|\geq s\geq s-1
in the integer case, and more generally is sufficient to apply multiplier bounds for real
s
.)

Apply
5.18
with
b=b_{i,\beta}
and
u=\partial^{\beta}(\eta_{\ell}\omega)
, using
\|b_{i,\beta}\|_{C^{r-|\beta|}}
and
r-|\beta|\geq s
(since
|\beta|\leq 2
and
r\geq s+2
). Combining with
5.17
,

\|b_{i,\beta}\partial^{\beta}(\eta_{\ell}\omega)\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq C\|b_{i,\beta}\|_{C^{r-|\beta|}}\|\eta_{\ell}\omega\|_{H^{s+1}}.

(5.19)

Summing over
|\beta|\leq 2
and components
i
, and then summing over charts using the partition of unity, yields

\|(\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g})\omega\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq C\Big(\max_{|\beta|\leq 2}\|b_{i,\beta}\|_{C^{r-|\beta|}}\Big)\|\omega\|_{H^{s+1}}.

(5.20)

Finally, apply
5.15
to control the coefficient differences by
\left|g-\tilde{g}\right|_{C^{r}}
. This proves

\|\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g}\|_{H^{s+1}\longrightarrow H^{s-1}}\leq C\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}},

(5.21)

with
C
depending only on
\kappa
,
M
,
\mathcal{M}
, and
r
.

Insert
5.12
and
5.21
into
5.9
:

\|\mathcal{S}_{g}-\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{g}}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}},

for a constant
C
depending only on
\alpha
,
\kappa
,

,

, and

. This is exactly

.
∎

Remark 2

(Role of the shift
\alpha
)
.

The shift

Without the shift, one must work on the orthogonal complement of harmonic 1-forms and track stability of the corresponding projections.

5.3
Stability of Intrinsic Neural Approximations under Metric Perturbations

We now consider the learned operator family
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}^{(g)}
defined by truncated functional calculus:

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}^{(g)}:=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,g})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(g)}.

(5.22)

This is the principal approximation class for the elliptic resolvent, and it also forms the linear core of the nonlinear architecture in Section
3
.

Metric stability requires controlling how spectral projectors and functional calculi vary with
g
. We state a stability result in terms of multiplier regularity and spectral gaps.

5.3.1
Assumptions on multiplier regularity and spectral separation

Assume
m_{\theta}\in C^{1}([0,\Lambda])
and define
L_{\theta}:=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}\left|m_{\theta}^{\prime}(\lambda)\right|
and
M_{\theta}:=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda)\right|
. Assume further that
\Lambda
is not too close to the spectrum of
\Delta_{H,g}
so that the cutoff is stable: there exists
\delta>0
such that

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits\big(\Lambda,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\Delta_{H,g})\big)\geq\delta,\qquad\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits\big(\Lambda,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}})\big)\geq\delta.

(5.23)

This is a standard spectral-gap condition ensuring that the projector
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
depends smoothly on the operator.

5.3.2
Stability of the learned operator

Theorem 3

(Metric stability of truncated intrinsic neural operators)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be compact, without boundary. Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
and an integer
r\geq s+2
. Let
\tilde{g}
satisfy the perturbation model in Section
5.1
, and set

A:=\Delta_{H,g},\qquad\tilde{A}:=\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}.

Fix
\Lambda>0
and assume the spectral separation condition: there exists
\delta>0
such that

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(\Lambda,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(A))\geq\delta,\qquad\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(\Lambda,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\tilde{A}))\geq\delta.

(5.24)

Let
m_{\theta}
be a real-valued multiplier on
[0,\Lambda]
with bounds

M_{\theta}:=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda)\right|,\qquad L_{\theta}:=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}\left|m_{\theta}^{\prime}(\lambda)\right|.

Assume in addition that
m_{\theta}
admits a bounded complex extension
m_{\theta}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{C}
on an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{C}
of the contour
\Gamma
defined below, and that on
\Gamma

\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\left|m_{\theta}(z)\right|\leq C_{\Gamma}(M_{\theta}+L_{\theta}),

(5.25)

for a constant
C_{\Gamma}
depending only on
\Lambda,\delta
.

Define the truncated intrinsic operator

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(g)}_{\theta,\Lambda}:=m_{\theta}(A)\Pi^{(g)}_{\leq\Lambda},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(\tilde{g})}_{\theta,\Lambda}:=m_{\theta}(\tilde{A})\Pi^{(\tilde{g})}_{\leq\Lambda}.

Then there exists
C<\infty
(depending on
\Lambda,\delta,\alpha
,
\mathcal{M}
, and the a priori metric bounds) such that

\big\|\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(g)}_{\theta,\Lambda}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(\tilde{g})}_{\theta,\Lambda}\big\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C(M_{\theta}+L_{\theta})\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}}.

(5.26)

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}
be a positively oriented simple closed contour enclosing the real interval
[0,\Lambda]
and satisfying

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(\Gamma,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(A))\geq\tfrac{\delta}{2},\qquad\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(\Gamma,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\tilde{A}))\geq\tfrac{\delta}{2}.

(5.27)

Such a
\Gamma
exists by
5.24
. For concreteness, one may take
\Gamma
to be the boundary of the closed
\delta/2
-tubular neighborhood of
[0,\Lambda]
in
\mathbb{C}
. Denote its length by
\mathop{\mathrm{len}}\nolimits(\Gamma)
, which depends only on
\Lambda
and
\delta
.

For a self-adjoint operator
A
with discrete spectrum, the Riesz projection onto the spectral subset inside
\Gamma
is

\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(g)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}(zI-A)^{-1}dz,

(5.28)

and similarly for
\tilde{A}
. By construction, the spectrum enclosed by
\Gamma
is exactly
\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(A)\cap[0,\Lambda]
, hence
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(g)}
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector onto the sum of eigenspaces with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
.

By the Dunford functional calculus (holomorphic functional calculus), for
m_{\theta}
holomorphic on a neighborhood of
\Gamma
,

m_{\theta}(A)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(g)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\theta}(z)(zI-A)^{-1}dz.

(5.29)

Indeed, the operator defined by the right-hand side equals the spectral multiplier
m_{\theta}
applied to the part of the spectrum inside
\Gamma
and zero outside; since
\Gamma
encloses
[0,\Lambda]
and excludes the rest of the spectrum, this is precisely
m_{\theta}(A)\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(g)}
. The same identity holds with
A
replaced by
\tilde{A}
.

Therefore,

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(g)}_{\theta,\Lambda}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(\tilde{g})}_{\theta,\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\theta}(z)\Big[(zI-A)^{-1}-(zI-\tilde{A})^{-1}\Big]dz.

(5.30)

For all
z\in\Gamma
, both resolvents exist by
5.27
. The resolvent identity gives

(zI-A)^{-1}-(zI-\tilde{A})^{-1}=(zI-\tilde{A})^{-1}(\tilde{A}-A)(zI-A)^{-1}.

(5.31)

We will estimate
5.31
as an operator from
H^{s-1}\Omega^{1}
to
H^{s+1}\Omega^{1}
.

Fix
z\in\Gamma
. Using the eigenbasis
\{\psi_{k}\}
of
A
and the spectral Sobolev norms defined via
A
(equivalent to chart-based norms on a compact manifold), the resolvent acts diagonally:

(zI-A)^{-1}\psi_{k}=\frac{1}{z-\lambda_{k}}\psi_{k}.

Hence for
f=\sum f_{k}\psi_{k}
,

\|(zI-A)^{-1}f\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\Big(\frac{1+\lambda_{k}}{|z-\lambda_{k}|}\Big)^{2}(1+\lambda_{k})^{s-1}|f_{k}|^{2}.

(5.32)

Therefore,

\|(zI-A)^{-1}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq\sup_{\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(A)}\frac{1+\lambda}{|z-\lambda|}.

(5.33)

On
\Gamma
,
|z-\lambda|\geq\delta/2
for all
\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(A)
by
5.27
, and moreover
\lambda\mapsto\frac{1+\lambda}{|z-\lambda|}
is bounded uniformly over
\lambda\geq 0
when
z
stays in a compact set away from
[0,\infty)
by a fixed distance. Since
\Gamma
lies in a bounded neighborhood of
[0,\Lambda]
, we may bound
1+\lambda
on the enclosed region by
1+\Lambda+\delta
, while for large
\lambda
the distance
|z-\lambda|\sim\lambda
yields a bounded ratio. Concretely, one obtains

\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{1+\lambda}{|z-\lambda|}\leq C_{\Lambda,\delta},\qquad z\in\Gamma,

(5.34)

hence

\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\|(zI-A)^{-1}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\Lambda,\delta}.

(5.35)

The same bound holds with
A
replaced by
\tilde{A}
, with the same constant up to equivalence of Sobolev norms under the uniform metric bounds.

By the coefficient-dependence estimate used in the proof of Theorem
1
, since
r\geq s+2
,

\|\tilde{A}-A\|_{H^{s+1}\longrightarrow H^{s-1}}=\|\Delta_{H,\tilde{g}}-\Delta_{H,g}\|_{H^{s+1}\longrightarrow H^{s-1}}\leq C\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}},

(5.36)

where
C
depends only on
\mathcal{M}
and the a priori metric bounds.

Combining
5.31
,
5.35
, and
5.36
, for all
z\in\Gamma
,

\|(zI-A)^{-1}-(zI-\tilde{A})^{-1}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq\|(zI-\tilde{A})^{-1}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\cdot\|\tilde{A}-A\|_{H^{s+1}\longrightarrow H^{s-1}}\cdot\|(zI-A)^{-1}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\Lambda,\delta}\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}}.

(5.37)

(Here
C_{\Lambda,\delta}
absorbs the product of the two resolvent bounds and the constant in
5.36
.)

From
5.30
, using the standard estimate for Bochner integrals of bounded operators,

\big\|\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(g)}_{\theta,\Lambda}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(\tilde{g})}_{\theta,\Lambda}\big\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma}\left|m_{\theta}(z)\right|\|(zI-A)^{-1}-(zI-\tilde{A})^{-1}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}|dz|.

(5.38)

Apply
5.37
and the bound
5.25
on
\left|m_{\theta}\right|
over
\Gamma
:

\big\|\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(g)}_{\theta,\Lambda}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(\tilde{g})}_{\theta,\Lambda}\big\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{len}}\nolimits(\Gamma)}{2\pi}\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\left|m_{\theta}(z)\right|\cdot C_{\Lambda,\delta}\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}}\leq C(M_{\theta}+L_{\theta})\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{C^{r}},

(5.39)

where
C
depends on
\Lambda,\delta
, the contour choice, and the a priori metric bounds, but not on
\theta
beyond
(M_{\theta},L_{\theta})
.
∎

Discussion.

The factor
M_{\theta}+L_{\theta}
expresses that stability improves when multipliers are uniformly bounded and not overly oscillatory on
[0,\Lambda]
. This motivates regularizing
m_{\theta}
during training (e.g., spectral Lipschitz penalties).

5.3.3
Extension to the nonlinear architecture

For the full network
3.9
, metric stability follows by combining:

•

stability of each intrinsic linear layer
m_{\theta_{\ell}}(\Delta_{H,g})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(g)}
as in
5.26
,

•

stability of scalar pointwise multipliers
b^{(g)}_{\theta_{\ell}}
under metric pullback rules,

•

Lipschitz properties of the gauge-equivariant radial nonlinearities
\sigma_{\theta_{\ell}}^{(g)}
in Sobolev norms (typically obtained by boundedness of
\rho_{\theta}
and
\rho_{\theta}^{\prime}
on the relevant range of norms).

A representative bound takes the form

\|\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}^{(g)}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}^{(\tilde{g})}\|_{H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq C_{\theta}\|\tilde{g}-g\|_{C^{r}},

(5.40)

where
C_{\theta}
grows at most polynomially/exponentially with depth depending on whether residual or contractive designs are used. In practice, enforcing uniform Lipschitz control per layer yields stable depth scaling.

5.4
Gauge Stability: Exact Equivariance at the Continuum Level

We now formalize gauge stability in the sense relevant to computation: changing the local orthonormal frames used to represent
1
-forms must not change the geometric output, only its coordinate representation.

Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be two local orthonormal frames on an open set
U\subset\mathcal{M}
, related by a smooth
R:U\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{O}}\nolimits(d)
as in
3.29
. For any
1
-form
\omega
, its coordinate representation transforms by

[\omega]_{E^{\prime}}(x)=R(x)^{\mathrm{T}}[\omega]_{E}(x).

(5.41)

Proposition
5
implies the following immediate corollary.

{Corollary}

[Exact gauge equivariance implies representation stability]

For the intrinsic neural operator
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}
defined in Section
3
, and for every
x\in U
,

[\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}(\omega)]_{E^{\prime}}(x)=R(x)^{\mathrm{T}}[\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}(\omega)]_{E}(x),

(5.42)

i.e., the computed output transforms consistently under a change of computational gauge.

Remark 4

(Discrete gauge error)
.

At the discrete level (Section
6
), exact gauge equivariance can be broken by discretization artifacts (e.g., approximate mass matrices, imperfect orthonormalization, or non-commuting interpolation). The continuum result
5.42
is therefore best interpreted as a target symmetry; Section
6
establishes that equivariance errors vanish under refinement under compatible discretizations.

5.5
Summary of Stability Guarantees

•

Metric perturbations: both
\mathcal{S}_{g}
and the learned intrinsic approximation vary Lipschitz-continuously with
g
in Sobolev operator norms, under standard regularity and spectral separation assumptions (Theorems
1
and
3
).

•

Gauge changes: equivariance is exact at the continuum level by construction (Corollary
5.4
), ensuring representation independence.

These results complement Section
4
(approximation) by ensuring that the learned operator is not only accurate but also robust to geometric uncertainty and to representation choices, which is essential for cross-mesh and cross-geometry generalization.

6
Continuum–Discrete Consistency

This section establishes that the intrinsic neural operators introduced in Section
3
admit discretizations on meshes that are consistent with the continuum model as the discretization is refined. In particular, we show that if the discrete Hodge Laplacian converges to its continuum counterpart in a standard sense, then the corresponding discrete truncated functional calculus converges to the continuum truncated functional calculus, and consequently the discrete neural operator converges to the continuum neural operator. We formulate the results for 1-forms using discrete exterior calculus (DEC) or compatible finite elements; the proofs require only (i) stability of discrete inner products and (ii) spectral convergence of discrete Hodge Laplacians.

6.1
Discretization Model and Discrete Spaces

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be as in Section
2
. Let
\{T_{h}\}_{h\downarrow 0}
be a family of shape-regular triangulations of
\mathcal{M}
with mesh size parameter
h
. We assume the triangulations are quasi-uniform and geometrically consistent with
\mathcal{M}
(e.g., embedded triangulations with uniformly bounded aspect ratios and uniformly controlled geometry approximation).

We discretize
1
-forms by a finite-dimensional space
\Omega_{h}^{1}
equipped with:

•

a discrete
L^{2}
inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{h}
,

•

a discrete Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{H,h}:\Omega_{h}^{1}\to\Omega_{h}^{1}
that is symmetric positive semidefinite with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
,

•

a projection (interpolation) operator
\Pi_{h}:\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})\to\Omega_{h}^{1}
, and a reconstruction operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}:\Omega_{h}^{1}\to\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
.

Concrete instances include:

•

DEC 1-cochains with circumcentric dual and the DEC Hodge star, yielding
\Delta_{H,h}=d_{h}\delta_{h}+\delta_{h}d_{h}
;

•

Whitney 1-forms / Nédélec-type elements with mass and stiffness matrices, yielding an equivalent discrete Hodge Laplacian.

We assume the pair
(\Pi_{h},\mathcal{R}_{h})
is stable in Sobolev norms and consistent on smooth forms.

6.2
Discrete Spectral Data and Discrete Truncation

Let
\{(\lambda_{k,h},\psi_{k,h})\}_{k=0}^{N_{h}-1}
be an
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
-orthonormal eigenpair family for
\Delta_{H,h}
,

\Delta_{H,h}\psi_{k,h}=\lambda_{k,h}\psi_{k,h},\qquad\langle\psi_{k,h},\psi_{j,h}\rangle_{h}=\delta_{kj}.

(6.1)

For a cutoff
\Lambda>0
, define the discrete spectral projector

\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(h)}\omega_{h}:=\sum_{\lambda_{k,h}\leq\Lambda}\langle\omega_{h},\psi_{k,h}\rangle_{h}\psi_{k,h}.

(6.2)

Given a multiplier
m_{\theta}
, define the discrete truncated multiplier operator

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}^{(h)}:=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,h})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(h)},\quad\text{where}\quad m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,h})\omega_{h}:=\sum_{k}m_{\theta}(\lambda_{k,h})\langle\omega_{h},\psi_{k,h}\rangle_{h}\psi_{k,h}.

(6.3)

This is the exact discrete analogue of
4.7
and is the canonical discretization of the intrinsic spectral layer.

6.3
Assumptions: Stability and Spectral Convergence

Our consistency theorem relies on standard approximation properties of the discrete Hodge Laplacian. We state them abstractly to cover DEC and compatible FEM.

Assumption 1

(Uniform norm stability and quasi-optimal reconstruction)
.

There exists
C
independent of
h
such that for all sufficiently smooth
\omega
,

\|\Pi_{h}\omega\|_{h}\leq C\|\omega\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\omega_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\omega_{h}\|_{h},

(6.4)

and
\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega\to\omega
in
L^{2}
as
h\to 0
.

Assumption 2

(Spectral convergence below a fixed cutoff)
.

Fix
\Lambda>0
that does not coincide with an eigenvalue of
\Delta_{H,g}
. Then there exists
p>0
and
C
such that for every continuum eigenpair
(\lambda_{k},\psi_{k})
with
\lambda_{k}<\Lambda
, there exists a discrete eigenpair
(\lambda_{k,h},\psi_{k,h})
(with consistent indexing up to multiplicity) such that

\left|\lambda_{k,h}-\lambda_{k}\right|\leq Ch^{p},\qquad\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{k,h}-\psi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p},

(6.5)

for all sufficiently small
h
. Moreover, the discrete eigenspace associated with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
converges to the corresponding continuum eigenspace in the gap
\sin\!(\Theta)
metric.

These assumptions are satisfied by standard compatible discretizations under regularity and shape-regularity conditions; they are widely used in numerical analysis of Hodge Laplacians on surfaces and manifolds.

6.4
Consistency of the Discrete Spectral Projector

We first show that the discrete projector converges to the continuum projector under reconstruction.

Lemma 3

(Projector consistency)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be compact without boundary. Fix
\Lambda>0
such that
\Lambda\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\Delta_{H,g})
. Let
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
be the
L^{2}
-orthogonal spectral projector of the Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{H,g}
on
1
-forms onto the direct sum of eigenspaces with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
.

Let
T_{h}
be a shape-regular family of discretizations with discrete space
\Omega_{h}^{1}
, discrete inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
, discrete Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{H,h}
, discrete projector
\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda}
defined by
6.2
, interpolation
\Pi_{h}
, and reconstruction
\mathcal{R}_{h}
.

Assume:

1.

(Approximation of reconstruction–interpolation) There exists
p>0
and
s
sufficiently large (depending on the method order) such that for all
\omega\in H^{s}\Omega^{1}
,

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{2}}\ \leq\ Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

(6.6)

(For Whitney/compatible FEM/DEC on smooth manifolds, such an estimate holds with
p
equal to the approximation order under standard regularity assumptions.)

2.

(Spectral subspace convergence below
\Lambda
) Assumption
2
holds: the discrete eigenspace associated with eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
converges to the continuum eigenspace in the gap metric with order
h^{p}
.

Then there exists
C
independent of
h
such that for all
\omega\in H^{s}\Omega^{1}
,

\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda}\Pi_{h}\omega-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\omega\big\|_{L^{2}}\ \leq\ Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

(6.7)

Proof.

Let

E:=\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits(\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})\subset L^{2}\Omega^{1}

be the continuum low-frequency invariant subspace. Since
\Lambda\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\Delta_{H,g})
and
\mathcal{M}
is compact,
E
is finite-dimensional; write
m:=\dim(E)
.

Let

E_{h}:=\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits(\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda})\subset\Omega_{h}^{1}

be the discrete low-frequency eigenspace (with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
). Define its reconstructed counterpart in the continuum Hilbert space
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
by

\widehat{E}_{h}:=\mathcal{R}_{h}(E_{h})\subset L^{2}\Omega^{1}.

Let
P:=\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
be the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector onto
E
, and let
\widehat{P}_{h}
denote the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector onto
\widehat{E}_{h}
.

By Assumption
2
(eigenspace convergence in the gap metric), for all sufficiently small
h
one has
\dim(\widehat{E}_{h})=\dim(E_{h})=m
, and there exists a constant
C
such that

\|\widehat{P}_{h}-P\|_{L^{2}\longrightarrow L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}.

(6.8)

This is a standard equivalence: convergence of subspaces in the gap (or
\sin(\Theta)
) metric is precisely operator-norm convergence of the corresponding orthogonal projectors.

Define the continuum-to-continuum discrete projector (apply discrete projector to discretized input, then reconstruct):

P_{h}^{d}:=\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda}\Pi_{h}:H^{s}\Omega^{1}\to L^{2}\Omega^{1}.

We aim to bound
\|P_{h}^{d}\omega-P\omega\|_{L^{2}}
.

Insert
\widehat{P}_{h}
and
\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}
to obtain the decomposition

P_{h}^{d}\omega-P\omega=\underbrace{\big(P_{h}^{d}\omega-\widehat{P}_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)\big)}_{(I)}+\underbrace{\widehat{P}_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega-\omega)}_{(II)}+\underbrace{(\widehat{P}_{h}-P)\omega}_{(III)}.

(6.9)

We will bound each term in
L^{2}
.

Since
\widehat{P}_{h}
is an orthogonal projector on
L^{2}
,
\|\widehat{P}_{h}\|_{L^{2}\longrightarrow L^{2}}=1
. Therefore, by
6.6
,

\|(II)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

(6.10)

By
6.8
,

\|(III)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\widehat{P}_{h}-P\|_{L^{2}\longrightarrow L^{2}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

(6.11)

By definition,
\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(h)}
is the
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
-orthogonal projector onto
E_{h}
. Hence for any
v_{h}\in\Omega_{h}^{1}
,

\|v_{h}-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(h)}v_{h}\|_{h}=\inf_{w_{h}\in E_{h}}\|v_{h}-w_{h}\|_{h}.

(6.12)

Take
v_{h}=\Pi_{h}\omega
. Then
u_{h}:=\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}^{(h)}\Pi_{h}\omega\in E_{h}
is the discrete best approximation to
\Pi_{h}\omega
in the discrete norm.

To compare with
\widehat{P}_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)
, we need a link between the discrete norm and the continuum
L^{2}
norm after reconstruction. This link is standard for DEC/FEM mass matrices and is implicit in Assumption
1
; we use the following consequence: there exists
C
such that for all sufficiently small
h
,

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}z_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|z_{h}\|_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad\|z_{h}\|_{h}\leq C\|\mathcal{R}_{h}z_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\qquad\forall z_{h}\in E_{h}.

(6.13)

(That is,
\mathcal{R}_{h}
is a norm equivalence on the low-frequency space; for finite-dimensional
E_{h}
, this follows from stability plus uniform conditioning of the discrete mass matrix on
E_{h}
, ensured by shape-regularity and the spectral convergence assumption.)

Let
y\in\widehat{E}_{h}
. Then
y=\mathcal{R}_{h}w_{h}
for some
w_{h}\in E_{h}
. Apply
6.12
and
6.13
to get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Pi_{h}\omega-u_{h}\|_{h}

(6.14)

\displaystyle=C\inf_{w_{h}\in E_{h}}\|\Pi_{h}\omega-w_{h}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{w_{h}\in E_{h}}\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega-\mathcal{R}_{h}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\inf_{y\in\widehat{E}_{h}}\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega-y\|_{L^{2}}=C\|(\mathrm{Id}-\widehat{P}_{h})(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)\|_{L^{2}}.

Now note that

\widehat{P}_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}\in\widehat{E}_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathrm{Id}-\widehat{P}_{h})(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)\perp\widehat{E}_{h},

hence,

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|(\mathrm{Id}-\widehat{P}_{h})(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\widehat{P}_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(6.15)

Combining
6.14
and
6.15
implies

\|\widehat{P}_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(\mathrm{Id}-\widehat{P}_{h})(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.16)

Finally,
\|(\mathrm{Id}-\widehat{P}_{h})(v)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}}
for any
v
, and stability of
\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}
yields
\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}
. Therefore,

\|(I)\|_{L^{2}}=\|\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}-\widehat{P}_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}\omega)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}},

(6.17)

where the final
h^{p}
dependence is inherited from the subspace convergence (Assumption
2
), which ensures the norm-equivalence constants and quasi-optimality transfer constants remain uniform and that
\widehat{E}_{h}
approximates
E
with order
h^{p}
. (Equivalently, one may sharpen this step by explicitly writing
\widehat{E}_{h}
in terms of reconstructed eigenvectors and using
6.5
to bound the principal angles; this yields the same order
h^{p}
.)

Insert
6.10
,
6.11
, and
6.17
into the decomposition
6.9
:

\|P_{h}^{d}\omega-P\omega\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|(I)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(II)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(III)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

∎

Discussion.

The key point is subspace convergence of discrete eigenspaces to the continuum eigenspace below
\Lambda
, which ensures that applying the discrete projector to a discretized input selects approximately the same spectral content as the continuum projector.

6.5
Consistency of Truncated Functional Calculus

We now prove convergence of the discrete multiplier operator to its continuum analogue.

Theorem 4

(Consistency of discrete truncated multipliers)
.

Fix
\Lambda>0
such that
\Lambda\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\Delta_{H,g})
. Let
m_{\theta}\in C^{1}([0,\Lambda])
and set

M_{\theta}:=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda)\right|,\qquad L_{\theta}:=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda}\left|m_{\theta}^{\prime}(\lambda)\right|.

Assume the discretization satisfies Assumptions
1
–
2
, and moreover the interpolation/reconstruction and discrete inner product are (
L^{2}
)-consistent in the following standard sense: there exist
p>0
and
s
sufficiently large such that for all
\omega\in H^{s}\Omega^{1}
and all discrete eigenvectors
\psi_{k,h}
with
\lambda_{k,h}\leq\Lambda
,

\big|\langle\Pi_{h}\omega,\psi_{k,h}\rangle_{h}-{\langle\omega,\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{k,h}\rangle_{L^{2}}}\big|\ \leq\ Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

(6.18)

(For DEC/FEM, this is a mass-matrix/quadrature consistency bound on smooth inputs.)

Then there exists
C
independent of
h
such that for all
\omega\in H^{s}\Omega^{1}
,

\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}\omega-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\omega\big\|_{L^{2}}\ \leq\ C(M_{\theta}+L_{\theta})h^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

(6.19)

Proof.

Let

E:=\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits(\Pi_{\leq\Lambda})\subset L^{2}\Omega^{1}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{h}:=\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits(\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda})\subset\Omega_{h}^{1}.

Since
\Lambda\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\Delta_{H,g})
,
E
is finite-dimensional. Let
m:=\dim(E)
.

By Assumption
2
(eigenspace convergence below
\Lambda
), for sufficiently small
h
there exist orthonormal bases

\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset E\quad\text{(orthonormal in }L^{2}),\qquad\{\psi_{j,h}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset E_{h}\quad\text{(orthonormal in }\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}),

and associated eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}\leq\Lambda
,
\lambda_{j,h}\leq\Lambda
, such that

\left|\lambda_{j,h}-\lambda_{j}\right|\leq Ch^{p},\qquad\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}-\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}.

(6.20)

(If there are multiplicities, one chooses bases aligned via principal angles;
6.20
is a standard consequence of subspace convergence.)

Let
\omega\in H^{s}\Omega^{1}
and define its continuum low-frequency component

\omega_{\leq}:=\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}\psi_{j},\qquad c_{j}:={\langle\omega,\psi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}}.

(6.21)

Similarly, define the discrete low-frequency coefficients of
\Pi_{h}\omega
in the discrete eigenbasis:

c_{j,h}:=\langle\Pi_{h}\omega,\psi_{j,h}\rangle_{h}.

(6.22)

Then the discrete truncated multiplier applied to
\Pi_{h}\omega
is

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{m}m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})c_{j,h}\psi_{j,h},

(6.23)

and therefore

\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{m}m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})c_{j,h}\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}.

(6.24)

On the continuum side, since
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,g})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
,

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{m}m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})c_{j}\psi_{j}.

(6.25)

Thus, the error we must bound is

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Big(m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})c_{j,h}\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}-m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})c_{j}\psi_{j}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.26)

For each
j
, add and subtract
m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})c_{j}\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}
and
m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})c_{j}\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}
to write

m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})c_{j,h}\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}-m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})c_{j}\psi_{j}=T^{(a)}_{j}+T^{(b)}_{j}+T^{(c)}_{j},

(6.27)

where

\displaystyle T^{(a)}_{j}

\displaystyle:=m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})(c_{j,h}-c_{j})\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h},

(6.28)

\displaystyle T^{(b)}_{j}

\displaystyle:=\big(m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})-m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})\big)c_{j}\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h},

\displaystyle T^{(c)}_{j}

\displaystyle:=m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})c_{j}(\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}-\psi_{j}).

Then by the triangle inequality,

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\cdot)\Big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(\|T^{(a)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|T^{(b)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|T^{(c)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\big).

(6.29)

We now bound each contribution.

Using
\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})\right|\leq M_{\theta}
, Cauchy–Schwarz, and
6.20
,

\|T^{(c)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})\right|\left|c_{j}\right|\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}-\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq M_{\theta}\left|c_{j}\right|Ch^{p}.

(6.30)

By the mean value theorem and the definition of
L_{\theta}
,

\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})-m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})\right|\leq L_{\theta}\left|\lambda_{j,h}-\lambda_{j}\right|.

(6.31)

Using
6.20
and stability (
\left|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}\right|_{L^{2}}\leq C
) (from Assumption
1
and normalization), we obtain

\|T^{(b)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})-m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j})\right|\left|c_{j}\right|\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq CL_{\theta}h^{p}\left|c_{j}\right|.

(6.32)

We estimate
c_{j,h}-c_{j}
. By definition and the consistency assumption
6.18
,

\displaystyle\left|c_{j,h}-c_{j}\right|

\displaystyle=\big|\langle\Pi_{h}\omega,\psi_{j,h}\rangle_{h}-{\langle\omega,\psi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}}\big|

(6.33)

\displaystyle\leq\big|\langle\Pi_{h}\omega,\psi_{j,h}\rangle_{h}-{\langle\omega,\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}\rangle_{L^{2}}}\big|+\big|{\langle\omega,\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}-\psi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}+\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}-\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}.

Using
\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}-\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}
from
6.20
and
\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}
(since
s\geq 0
and
\mathcal{M}
is compact), we conclude

\left|c_{j,h}-c_{j}\right|\leq Ch^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

(6.34)

Therefore, using
\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})\right|\leq M_{\theta}
and
\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
,

\|T^{(a)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda_{j,h})\right|\left|c_{j,h}-c_{j}\right|\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\psi_{j,h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM_{\theta}h^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

(6.35)

Collecting
6.30
,
6.32
, and
6.35
and summing over
j=1,\dots,m
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}\omega-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\omega\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|T^{(a)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|T^{(b)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|T^{(c)}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

(6.36)

\displaystyle\leq CM_{\theta}h^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}+CL_{\theta}h^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|c_{j}\right|+CM_{\theta}h^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|c_{j}\right|.

Since
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
is
L^{2}
-orthonormal,
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|c_{j}\right|^{2}=\|\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Hence by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|c_{j}\right|\leq\sqrt{m}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|c_{j}\right|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{m}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\Lambda}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}},

(6.37)

where
C_{\Lambda}:=\sqrt{m}
depends only on
\Lambda
(and
\mathcal{M},g
, since
m
is the number of eigenvalues
\leq\Lambda
, counted with multiplicity).

Substitute
6.37
into
6.36
and absorb constants to obtain

\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}\omega-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\omega\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(M_{\theta}+L_{\theta})h^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s}}.

∎

Discussion.

The rate is governed by (i) eigenspace convergence and (ii) Lipschitz regularity of
m_{\theta}
, which translates eigenvalue perturbations into multiplier perturbations.

6.6
Discretization Consistency of the Neural Operator

We now lift the preceding consistency to the full network. We present the result in two versions: (i) linear truncated operator (the main approximation class for elliptic resolvents), and (ii) nonlinear multi-layer architecture.

6.6.1
Linear model class

Define the continuum operator
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,g})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
as in
4.7
, and the discrete operator
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}
as in
6.3
.

Theorem 5

(Continuum–discrete commutation for the learned operator)
.

Fix
\Lambda>0
such that
\Lambda\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\Delta_{H,g})
. Let
m_{\theta}\in C^{1}([0,\Lambda])
and define

\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}:=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,g})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}:=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,h})\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda}.

Assume Assumptions
1
–
2
, and the additional pairing consistency hypothesis
6.18
used in Theorem
4
. Let
p>0
be the spectral convergence order and let
s_{0}
be large enough such that Theorem
4
applies with
s=s_{0}
.

Then there exists
C
independent of
h
such that

\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\big\|_{H^{s_{0}}\longrightarrow L^{2}}\ \leq\ C(M_{\theta}+L_{\theta})h^{p},

(6.38)

where
M_{\theta}=\sup_{[0,\Lambda]}\left|m_{\theta}\right|
and
L_{\theta}=\sup_{[0,\Lambda]}\left|m_{\theta}^{\prime}\right|
.

Proof.

Let
T_{h}:=\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}
. We must show that the operator norm
\|T_{h}\|_{H^{s_{0}}\longrightarrow L^{2}}
is
O(h^{p})
with the stated prefactor.

By Theorem
4
, for every
\omega\in H^{s_{0}}\Omega^{1}
we have

\|T_{h}\omega\|_{L^{2}}=\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}\omega-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\omega\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(M_{\theta}+L_{\theta})h^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s_{0}}}.

(6.39)

Here
C
is independent of
h
and
\omega
, and depends only on the discretization family,
(\mathcal{M},g)
, and the fixed cutoff
\Lambda
(through the dimension of the low-frequency space and the spectral-gap constants implicit in Assumption
2
).

By definition of the operator norm,

\|T_{h}\|_{H^{s_{0}}\longrightarrow L^{2}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in H^{s_{0}}\Omega^{1}\\
\omega\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|T_{h}\omega\|_{L^{2}}}{\|\omega\|_{H^{s_{0}}}}=\sup_{\left|\omega\right|_{H^{s_{0}}}=1}\|T_{h}\omega\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.40)

Applying
6.39
to all
\omega
with
\left|\omega\right|_{H^{s_{0}}}=1
yields

\|T_{h}\|_{H^{s_{0}}\longrightarrow L^{2}}\leq C(M_{\theta}+L_{\theta})h^{p}.

(6.41)

∎

This is the central continuum–discrete consistency guarantee: applying the discrete learned operator to the discretized input and reconstructing converges to applying the continuum learned operator directly.

6.6.2
Nonlinear architecture

Consider the depth-
L
network
3.9
, and define its discrete counterpart by replacing each spectral layer
\mathcal{T}_{\theta_{\ell},K}
with its discrete analogue
6.3
, and replacing pointwise operations with their discrete fiberwise versions using the discrete metric/Hodge star at each simplex. Denote the resulting discrete network by
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda,\mathrm{NL}}
.

Assume in addition that the fiberwise nonlinearities are uniformly Lipschitz on the relevant range of norms, i.e., there exists
\mathrm{Lip}(\sigma_{\theta_{\ell}})<\infty
independent of
h
, and the scalar pointwise multipliers
b_{\theta_{\ell}}
are discretized consistently.

Theorem 6

(Discretization consistency for the nonlinear intrinsic neural operator)
.

Fix
\Lambda>0
with
\Lambda\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}\nolimits(\Delta_{H,g})
. Consider the continuum depth-
L
network
3.9

\omega_{0}=f,\qquad\omega_{\ell+1}=\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\omega_{\ell}):=\sigma_{\ell}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}+\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}\right),\qquad\ell=0,\dots,L-1,

(6.42)

where for each
\ell
:

•

\mathcal{T}_{\ell}=m_{\ell}(\Delta_{H,g})\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}
with
m_{\ell}\in C^{1}([0,\Lambda])
;

•

\mathcal{B}_{\ell}
is a pointwise scalar multiplication operator
(\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\omega)(x)=b_{\ell}(x)\omega(x)
with
b_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
;

•

\sigma_{\ell}
is a fiberwise gauge-equivariant radial nonlinearity
(\sigma_{\ell}(\eta))(x)=\rho_{\ell}(\|\eta(x)\|_{g})\eta(x)
that is Lipschitz on
L^{2}\Omega^{1}
on the relevant set of inputs (made precise below).

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda,\mathrm{NL}}(f):=\omega_{L}
.

Define the discrete network on
\Omega_{h}^{1}
by

\omega^{h}_{0}=\Pi_{h}f,\qquad\omega^{h}_{\ell+1}=\mathcal{F}^{h}_{\ell}(\omega^{h}_{\ell}):=\sigma^{h}_{\ell}\left(\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}\omega^{h}_{\ell}+\mathcal{B}^{h}_{\ell}\omega^{h}_{\ell}\right),

(6.43)

where
\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}=m_{\ell}(\Delta_{H,h})\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda}
(discrete truncated multiplier), and
\mathcal{B}^{h}_{\ell},\sigma^{h}_{\ell}
are consistent discretizations of
\mathcal{B}_{\ell},\sigma_{\ell}
(assumptions below). Let
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda,\mathrm{NL}}(f):=\omega^{h}_{L}
.

Assume:

•

Stability of interpolation/reconstruction: There exists
C_{R}
independent of
h
such that

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{R}\|v_{h}\|_{h}\quad\forall v_{h}\in\Omega_{h}^{1},\qquad\|\Pi_{h}\omega\|_{h}\leq C_{R}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\quad\forall\omega\in L^{2}\Omega^{1}.

(6.44)

•

Linear layer consistency: For each
\ell
, there exists
p>0
and
s_{0}
sufficiently large such that

\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}\Pi_{h}\omega-\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\omega\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\ell}h^{p}\|\omega\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\qquad\forall\omega\in H^{s_{0}}\Omega^{1},

(6.45)

with
C_{\ell}\lesssim(M_{\ell}+L_{\ell})
, where
M_{\ell}=\sup_{[0,\Lambda]}\left|m_{\ell}\right|
,
L_{\ell}=\sup_{[0,\Lambda]}\left|m_{\ell}^{\prime}\right|
. (This is Theorem
5
applied per layer.)

•

Pointwise operator stability and consistency: There exist constants
B_{\ell},\varepsilon_{\ell,h}
such that

\|\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\eta\|_{L^{2}}\leq B_{\ell}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{B}^{h}_{\ell}v_{h}-\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{\ell,h}\|v_{h}\|_{h},

(6.46)

with
\varepsilon_{\ell,h}\leq C_{\ell}^{(B)}h^{p}
.

•

Nonlinearity Lipschitzness and discretization compatibility: There exists
L_{\sigma,\ell}<\infty
such that for all
\eta,\zeta\in L^{2}\Omega^{1}
in the range encountered by the network,

\|\sigma_{\ell}(\eta)-\sigma_{\ell}(\zeta)\|_{L^{2}}\leq L_{\sigma,\ell}\|\eta-\zeta\|_{L^{2}},

(6.47)

and similarly the discrete nonlinearity is uniformly Lipschitz in reconstructed norm:

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}(\sigma^{h}_{\ell}(u_{h})-\sigma^{h}_{\ell}(v_{h}))\|_{L^{2}}\leq L_{\sigma,\ell}\|\mathcal{R}_{h}(u_{h}-v_{h})\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.48)

Finally, assume a consistency estimate:

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\sigma^{h}_{\ell}(\Pi_{h}\eta)-\sigma_{\ell}(\eta)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}h^{p}\|\eta\|_{H^{s_{0}}}.

(6.49)

Then there exists
C_{\theta}<\infty
, depending on
\{L_{\sigma,\ell},B_{\ell},M_{\ell},L_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
and
L
but not on
h
, such that

\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda,\mathrm{NL}}\Pi_{h}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda,\mathrm{NL}}\big\|_{H^{s_{0}}\longrightarrow L^{2}}\ \leq\ C_{\theta}h^{p}.

(6.50)

Proof.

For each layer
\ell
, define the continuum pre-activation

z_{\ell}:=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}+\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\omega_{\ell},\qquad\omega_{\ell+1}=\sigma_{\ell}(z_{\ell}),

(6.51)

and the discrete pre-activation

z^{h}_{\ell}:=\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}\omega^{h}_{\ell}+\mathcal{B}^{h}_{\ell}\omega^{h}_{\ell},\qquad\omega^{h}_{\ell+1}=\sigma^{h}_{\ell}(z^{h}_{\ell}).

(6.52)

Let the reconstructed discrete state be

\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}:=\mathcal{R}_{h}\omega^{h}_{\ell}\in L^{2}\Omega^{1}.

(6.53)

We will estimate the layerwise error

e_{\ell}:=\|\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}-\omega_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.54)

We start from

e_{\ell+1}=\|\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell+1}-\omega_{\ell+1}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\sigma^{h}_{\ell}(z^{h}_{\ell})-\sigma_{\ell}(z_{\ell})\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.55)

Add and subtract
\sigma_{\ell}(\mathcal{R}_{h}z^{h}_{\ell})
and apply the triangle inequality:

e_{\ell+1}\leq\underbrace{\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\sigma^{h}_{\ell}(z^{h}_{\ell})-\sigma_{\ell}(\mathcal{R}_{h}z^{h}_{\ell})}_{L^{2}}\|_{(I)_{\ell}}+\underbrace{\|\sigma_{\ell}(\mathcal{R}_{h}z^{h}_{\ell})-\sigma_{\ell}(z_{\ell})}_{L^{2}}\|_{(II)_{\ell}}.

(6.56)

Bound
(II)_{\ell}
. By the Lipschitz property
6.47
,

(II)_{\ell}\leq L_{\sigma,\ell}\|\mathcal{R}_{h}z^{h}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.57)

Bound
(I)_{\ell}
. This term captures the discrete-vs-continuum nonlinearity mismatch. Under
6.48
–
6.49
, applied with
\eta=\mathcal{R}_{h}z^{h}_{\ell}
and using stability of
\Pi_{h},\mathcal{R}_{h}
to relate discrete arguments to continuum arguments, we obtain a bound of the form

(I)_{\ell}\leq C_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}h^{p}\|z_{\ell}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}

(6.58)

uniformly along the trajectories considered. (This is exactly
6.49
when the discrete nonlinearity is defined by applying
\sigma_{\ell}
pointwise after reconstruction; more general consistent constructions satisfy the same estimate.)

Combining
6.56
–
6.58
yields

e_{\ell+1}\leq C_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}h^{p}\|z_{\ell}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}+L_{\sigma,\ell}\|\mathcal{R}_{h}z^{h}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.59)

Using
6.51
–
6.52
,

\mathcal{R}_{h}z^{h}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}=\underbrace{\big(\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}\omega^{h}_{\ell}-\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\big)}_{A_{\ell}}+\underbrace{\big(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}-\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}\big)}_{B_{\ell}}+\underbrace{\big(\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{B}^{h}_{\ell}\omega^{h}_{\ell}-\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\big)}_{C_{\ell}}+\underbrace{\big(\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}-\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}\big)}_{D_{\ell}}.

(6.60)

We bound each term in
L^{2}
.

Term
B_{\ell}
. Since
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
is a bounded linear operator (
L^{2}\longrightarrow L^{2}
) on the low-frequency range (indeed,
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
has finite rank and
\|\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}\longrightarrow L^{2}}\leq M_{\ell}
), we have

\|B_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}\longrightarrow L^{2}}\|\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}-\omega_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq M_{\ell}e_{\ell}.

(6.61)

Term
D_{\ell}
. From
6.46
,

\|D_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq B_{\ell}e_{\ell}.

(6.62)

Term
A_{\ell}
. Insert
\Pi_{h}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}
and use
6.45
:

A_{\ell}=\big(\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}\omega^{h}_{\ell}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}\Pi_{h}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\big)+\big(\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}\Pi_{h}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}-\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\big).

(6.63)

The second difference is
O(h^{p})
by
6.45
with
\omega=\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}
, provided
\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\in H^{s_{0}}
along the trajectory (which is ensured if
f\in H^{s_{0}}
and the layers map
H^{s_{0}}
to itself; this is standard for finite-rank spectral operators plus pointwise maps with smooth scalar fields). Thus,

\big\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}\Pi_{h}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}-\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\ell}h^{p}\|\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}.

(6.64)

For the first difference in
6.63
, use uniform boundedness of
\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\ell}
and stability of
\mathcal{R}_{h}
together with
\omega^{h}_{\ell}-\Pi_{h}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}
being a consistency residual of the reconstruction/interpolation pair; this contributes an
O(h^{p})
term under Assumption
1
(and is zero if
\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}
is a projector on the discrete representation). In either case, one obtains

\|A_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\ell}^{\prime}h^{p}\|\omega_{\ell}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}+C_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}e_{\ell},

(6.65)

for constants independent of
h
. (The
e_{\ell}
part accounts for replacing
\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}
by
\omega_{\ell}
inside norms.)

Term
C_{\ell}
. This is controlled by the pointwise consistency
6.46
:

\|C_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{B}^{h}_{\ell}\omega^{h}_{\ell}-\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{\ell,h}\|\omega^{h}_{\ell}\|_{h}\leq Ch^{p}\|\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}\|\omega_{\ell}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}+Ch^{p}e_{\ell},

(6.66)

where we used stability
6.44
and
\|\bar{\omega}^{h}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\omega_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}+e_{\ell}
.

Putting
6.61
–
6.66
into
6.60
yields a bound of the form

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}z^{h}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq a_{\ell}e_{\ell}+b_{\ell}h^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}},

(6.67)

where
a_{\ell}
depends on
(M_{\ell},B_{\ell})
and uniform stability constants, and
b_{\ell}
depends on the layerwise consistency constants
(C_{\ell},C_{\ell}^{(B)})
and uniform bounds on the continuum trajectory in
H^{s_{0}}
(which in turn depend on the network parameters and
\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}
).

Insert
6.67
into
6.59
:

e_{\ell+1}\leq C_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}h^{p}\|z_{\ell}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}+L_{\sigma,\ell}\big(a_{\ell}e_{\ell}+b_{\ell}h^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\big).

(6.68)

Using uniform bounds
\|z_{\ell}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\leq C_{\theta}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}
along the continuum trajectory (a standard consequence of boundedness of the finite-rank spectral operators and pointwise multipliers on
H^{s_{0}}
), we obtain

e_{\ell+1}\leq\underbrace{(L_{\sigma,\ell}a_{\ell})}_{=:A_{\ell}}e_{\ell}+\underbrace{\big(C_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}C_{\theta}+L_{\sigma,\ell}b_{\ell}\big)}_{=:D_{\ell}}h^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}.

(6.69)

The initial error satisfies

e_{0}=\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi_{h}f-f\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}

(6.70)

by Assumption
1
(approximation of reconstruction–interpolation).

Iterating
6.69
gives, for
\ell=0,\dots,L-1
,

e_{L}\leq\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{L-1}A_{j}\Big)e_{0}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\Big(\prod_{j=\ell+1}^{L-1}A_{j}\Big)D_{\ell}h^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}.

(6.71)

Absorbing the finite products and sums into a constant
C_{\theta}
depending only on the network parameters and depth (but not on
h
), we obtain

e_{L}\leq C_{\theta}h^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}.

(6.72)

Since
e_{L}=\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda,\mathrm{NL}}(\Pi_{h}f)-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda,\mathrm{NL}}(f)\|_{L^{2}}
,
6.72
is exactly the claimed operator bound
6.50
after taking the supremum over
\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}=1
.
∎

Discussion.

The proof is a stability-by-composition argument: each layer has a consistency error of order
h^{p}
, and these errors propagate through the network with amplification controlled by layerwise Lipschitz constants. Residual or contractive designs can ensure
C_{\theta}
grows mildly with depth.

6.7
End-to-End Error Decomposition (Continuum Target vs. Discrete Learned Operator)

Combining the approximation result of Section
4
with the discretization consistency above yields an end-to-end bound comparing the discrete learned operator to the true continuum PDE solution operator. For inputs in a smooth class
H^{s-1+\gamma}\Omega^{1}\cap H^{s_{0}}\Omega^{1}
,

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{g}\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}\leq\underbrace{\|\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}-\mathcal{S}_{g}\|_{H^{s-1+\gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}}_{\text{model/approximation error (Section \ref{4})}}+\underbrace{\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{(h)}_{\theta,\Lambda}\Pi_{h}-\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta,\Lambda}\|_{H^{s_{0}}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}}}_{\text{discretization error (Section \ref{6})}}.

(6.73)

This inequality makes explicit the two independent levers for accuracy: increasing spectral capacity and improving multiplier approximation (Section
4
), and refining the discretization (Section
6
).

6.8
Summary

The results of this section formalize a key practical requirement for operator learning on manifolds: mesh refinement should not change the learned operator in the continuum limit. Under standard assumptions for convergent discretizations of the Hodge Laplacian, the discrete intrinsic neural operator converges to its continuum counterpart, and its equivariance properties become exact in the limit. This provides a principled explanation for resolution-robust generalization observed in intrinsic spectral architectures.

Experiments: Algorithm and Implementation Details (Theory-Aligned)

This section describes how the proposed Gauge-Equivariant Intrinsic Neural Operator (GINO) is instantiated on discrete meshes in a way that mirrors the continuum definitions in Sections
3
–
6
. We emphasize conceptual alignment with the theory—intrinsic operators, gauge-consistent nonlinearities, and mesh-consistent discretizations—rather than engineering optimizations.

7.1
Discrete Representation of 1-Forms and Gauge Conventions

7.1.1
Discrete 1-forms

On each triangulated manifold
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we represent 1-forms using a compatible discretization
\Omega_{h}^{1}
(DEC 1-cochains or Whitney 1-forms). The method provides:

•

a discrete inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
approximating the
L^{2}
pairing,

•

a discrete Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{H,h}
acting on
\Omega_{h}^{1}
,

•

reconstruction
\mathcal{R}_{h}:\Omega_{h}^{1}\to\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})
and interpolation
\Pi_{h}:\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M})\to\Omega_{h}^{1}
.

7.1.2
Gauge for coordinate baselines

When comparing to coordinate-based models that require per-vertex tangent frames, we attach an orthonormal frame
E_{v}
at each vertex
v
(e.g., via PCA of the local tangent plane) and express discrete 1-forms as frame coefficients. Gauge randomization is implemented by applying
R_{v}\in\mathrm{O}(d)
to each frame, inducing the transformation
a_{v}\mapsto R_{v}^{\top}a_{v}
on coefficients. Our intrinsic model does not use these frames for computation and is therefore unaffected except through optional visualization.

7.2
Intrinsic Spectral Layers: Discrete Truncated Functional Calculus

Each linear intrinsic layer is a truncated spectral multiplier of the discrete Hodge Laplacian, matching Section
4
and the discretization framework of Section
6
.

7.2.1
Discrete eigensystem and truncation

We compute the lowest portion of the spectrum of
\Delta_{H,h}
:

\Delta_{H,h}\psi_{k,h}=\lambda_{k,h}\psi_{k,h},\qquad\langle\psi_{k,h},\psi_{j,h}\rangle_{h}=\delta_{kj}.

(7.1)

Given a cutoff
\Lambda
(or equivalently a mode budget
K
), we form the discrete projector

\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda}u_{h}:=\sum_{\lambda_{k,h}\leq\Lambda}\langle u_{h},\psi_{k,h}\rangle_{h}\psi_{k,h}.

(7.2)

7.2.2
Multiplier parameterization and application

A multiplier layer is

\mathcal{T}^{(h)}_{\theta}u_{h}:=m_{\theta}(\Delta_{H,h})\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda}u_{h}=\sum_{\lambda_{k,h}\leq\Lambda}m_{\theta}(\lambda_{k,h})\langle u_{h},\psi_{k,h}\rangle_{h}\psi_{k,h}.

(7.3)

To align with the stability bounds in Section
5
, we parameterize
m_{\theta}
so that both

M_{\theta}=\sup_{\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]}\left|m_{\theta}(\lambda)\right|,\qquad L_{\theta}=\sup_{\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]}\left|m_{\theta}^{\prime}(\lambda)\right|

(7.4)

are controlled. Concretely, we use a low-degree basis expansion on
[0,\Lambda]
,

m_{\theta}(\lambda)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\theta_{j}\varphi_{j}(\lambda),

(7.5)

where
\{\varphi_{j}\}
are smooth functions with known derivative bounds (e.g., Chebyshev polynomials mapped to
[0,\Lambda]
or B-splines). This choice directly corresponds to Theorem
4
, where Lipschitz regularity converts eigenvalue errors into multiplier errors.

7.3
Gauge-Equivariant Nonlinearity and Pointwise Terms

Each nonlinear layer follows the intrinsic design of Section
3
.

7.3.1
Pointwise scalar multiplication

We include a pointwise (zeroth-order) term

\mathcal{B}^{(h)}_{\theta}u_{h}:=b_{\theta}u_{h},

(7.6)

where
b_{\theta}
is a scalar field represented at vertices or elements. This term is intrinsically defined and does not depend on a frame choice. Its discretization is chosen so that

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{B}^{(h)}_{\theta}u_{h}-\mathcal{B}_{\theta}\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as }h\to 0,

(7.7)

consistent with the assumptions used in Theorem
6
.

7.3.2
Fiberwise radial nonlinearity (gauge equivariant)

To preserve gauge equivariance, the nonlinearity is applied
radially
in the fiber:

\sigma_{\theta}(u)(x)=\rho_{\theta}(\|u(x)\|_{g})u(x).

(7.8)

On the mesh,
\|u(x)\|_{g}
is evaluated using the discrete metric associated with the Hodge star/mass matrix. This guarantees that under any local orthonormal frame change
R(x)\in\mathrm{O}(d)
, coefficient vectors rotate while norms remain invariant, yielding exact equivariance at the discrete level up to the discretization of the metric norm.

For consistency and stability,
\rho_{\theta}
is chosen to be Lipschitz on the range of encountered norms, so that
\sigma_{\theta}
is Lipschitz in
L^{2}
, matching the hypotheses of Theorem
6
. We parameterize
\rho_{\theta}
as a smooth bounded function (e.g., a spline or a small MLP with bounded activation), and, when needed, constrain
\sup\left|\rho_{\theta}^{\prime}\right|
to avoid exploding Lipschitz constants across depth.

7.4
Network Structure and Training Objective

7.4.1
Layer update rule

A depth-
L
GINO is implemented as

u^{(0)}_{h}=f_{h},\qquad u^{(\ell+1)}_{h}=\sigma_{\theta_{\ell}}\left(\mathcal{T}^{(h)}_{\theta_{\ell}}u^{(\ell)}_{h}+\mathcal{B}^{(h)}_{\theta_{\ell}}u^{(\ell)}_{h}\right),\quad\ell=0,\dots,L-1.

(7.9)

This is the discrete analogue of the continuum operator in Section
3
and is precisely the model class analyzed in Sections
4
–
6
.

7.4.2
Supervised operator regression

For each geometry and forcing sample, we generate a target output
u_{h}
by solving the discrete PDE defining the target operator. The model prediction
\widehat{u}_{h}
is trained by minimizing an intrinsic loss:

\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h}\|_{h}^{2}\Big],

(7.10)

optionally augmented by the energy norm associated with the operator when the target is elliptic:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{energy}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h},(\Delta_{H,h}+\alpha I)(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h})\rangle_{h}\Big].

(7.11)

The energy norm aligns with the coercive bilinear form of the shifted elliptic operator and directly reflects the Sobolev mapping
H^{s-1}\longrightarrow H^{s+1}
discussed in Section
4
.

7.5
Cutoffs, Mode Budgets, and Alignment with Approximation Theory

The approximation bound in Theorem
4
decomposes error into truncation bias and multiplier approximation on
[0,\Lambda]
. Accordingly, we treat
\Lambda
(or
K
) as an explicit experimental axis:

•

Increasing
\Lambda
reduces truncation bias for sufficiently smooth inputs (Lemma
2
).

•

Increasing multiplier capacity (larger
J
in
7.5
) reduces
\varepsilon_{\Lambda}(\theta)
on
[0,\Lambda]
(Lemma
3
).

•

Cross-resolution tests directly probe the discretization-consistency rate
h^{p}
(Theorems
4
–
6
).

We report ablations that vary
(\Lambda,J,L)
to empirically validate these qualitative predictions.

7.6
Discrete–Continuum Consistency Checks

To directly test Section
6
, we include consistency diagnostics beyond task loss:

1.

Projector consistency: evaluate

\|\mathcal{R}_{h}\Pi^{(h)}_{\leq\Lambda}\Pi_{h}\omega-\Pi_{\leq\Lambda}\omega\|_{L^{2}}

(7.12)

on synthetic smooth
\omega
, verifying convergence as
h\downarrow 0
(Lemma

2.

Layer consistency: evaluate the single-layer discrepancy

(7.13)

(M_{\theta},L_{\theta})
predicted by Theorem

3.

7.7
Practical Notes on Ensuring Theoretical Assumptions

The stability theorems require controlling multiplier oscillations and nonlinearity Lipschitz constants. In practice we enforce this by:

•

Multiplier smoothness: choosing
m_{\theta}
in a smooth basis and penalizing large derivatives on
[0,\Lambda]
, approximating control of
L_{\theta}
in
7.4
.

•

Nonlinearity boundedness: choosing
\rho_{\theta}
bounded and Lipschitz so that each
\sigma_{\theta}
has a controlled Lipschitz constant in
L^{2}
, limiting error amplification across depth (Theorem
6
).

•

Cutoff separation: avoiding cutoffs
\Lambda
near dense clusters of eigenvalues when evaluating stability to geometric perturbations (Section
5
), consistent with the spectral separation logic used in Theorem
3
.

These choices are minimal and are included solely to reflect the assumptions required for the theory-to-practice correspondence.

All models are implemented in PyTorch and trained on a single RTX 4060 GPU.

Target operator.

8.9
Summary

Across six experiments, GINO consistently outperforms a standard coordinate-based baseline in accuracy, symmetry preservation, geometric stability, and discretization consistency. Crucially, ablation studies reveal that these advantages stem from principled design choices—spectral truncation and smoothness control—that align with theoretical intuitions about intrinsic operator learning. Together, these results establish GINO as a robust, geometry-aware framework for learning elliptic operators on vector-valued fields.

9
Conclusion

We introduced Gauge-Equivariant Intrinsic Neural Operators (GINO) for learning elliptic PDE solution maps in a manner that is consistent with geometric structure and representation changes. By parameterizing the operator through intrinsic spectral multipliers defined on geometry-dependent spectra and combining them with gauge-equivariant nonlinearities, GINO enforces frame-consistent behavior while maintaining the expressivity required for operator learning.

Experiments E1–E6 on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
provide a coherent empirical validation of the approach. GINO achieves accurate operator approximation on the base resolvent task and exhibits near numerical-precision gauge equivariance, while coordinate-based CNN baselines remain strongly gauge-sensitive. Under structured metric perturbations, GINO shows minimal degradation, and under cross-resolution evaluation it maintains low error and small commutation defects with restriction/prolongation operators, indicating reduced discretization dependence. On a regularized Helmholtz–Hodge decomposition task, the structured variant attains low projection error and small decomposition residual while preserving gauge equivariance to near floating-point tolerance. Finally, theory-aligned ablations demonstrate that multiplier smoothness strongly mediates stability: rough learned multipliers amplify perturbation errors, whereas smoothness control yields markedly improved robustness.

These findings suggest that incorporating intrinsic geometry and gauge constraints is a practical route to more reliable operator surrogates for geometric PDEs. Future work includes extending the framework to spatially varying metrics and general manifolds, integrating mesh-based discretizations via discrete exterior calculus or finite element exterior calculus, and studying broader PDE families and real-world scientific benchmarks where gauge and discretization robustness are essential.
