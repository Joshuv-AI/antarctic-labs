1.
Introduction

The Lorentz-Gamma (LG) spaces
\Gamma_{p,\phi}
, defined by the norm

(1.1)

\rho_{p,\phi}(f)=\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f^{**}(t)^{p}\phi(t)\,dt\right]^{1/p},\quad f^{**}(t)=t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds,

were systematically studied by Gogatishvili and Kerman
[
GK
]
, who
characterized their r.i. properties and established when
\rho_{p,\phi}

is a genuine norm. Here
f^{*}
denotes the nonincreasing rearrangement of

f
and the weight
\phi\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
satisfies

(1.2)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{\phi(t)}{1+t^{p}}\,dt<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\phi(t)\,dt=\infty.

The class
\Gamma_{p,\phi}
contains the classical Lorentz spaces

L_{p,q}
(take
\phi(t)=t^{q/p-1}
,
1\leq q<\infty
) and the Lebesgue
spaces
L^{p}
(take
\phi\equiv 1
) as special cases; see

[
BS
, Ch. 4]
. The Köthe dual of
\rho_{p,\phi}
is equivalent to
a norm of the same LG type, with an explicit dual weight computed in

[
GolK
,
GP
]
.

Given a nonnegative kernel
K\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
, we consider the
boundedness of the integral operator

(T_{K}f)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy

from
\Gamma_{p,\phi_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to
\Gamma_{q,\phi_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

1<p\leq q<\infty
; that is, the existence of
C>0
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{q,\phi_{1}}(T_{K}f)\leq C\,\rho_{p,\phi_{2}}(f),\quad 0\leq f\in\Gamma_{p,\phi_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Norm inequalities of this type have a rich history. For the classical
Lebesgue case,
\phi_{i}\equiv 1
,
p=q
, characterizations go back to
Schur’s test and the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
theory; see
[
BS
]
. Weighted
Hardy-type operators, a special subclass of
T_{K}
, were characterized in
Lorentz and Orlicz settings by Bloom and Kerman
[
BK
]
, whose
Muckenhoupt-type conditions we invoke in Section
5
.
The present work extends to the LG setting the approach introduced in

[
KS1
]
for Orlicz-Lorentz norms; a detailed historical account of
the boundedness problem for
T_{K}
can be found in Section 4 of that paper.

The strategy is to replace
T_{K}
by a one-dimensional operator with a
more tractable kernel. The
iterated rearrangement

L=(K^{*_{2}})^{*_{1}}
of
K
, introduced by Blozinski
[
B
]
in the
context of mixed-norm spaces, is defined as follows: for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

rearrange
k_{x}(y)=K(x,y)
in
y
to get
K^{*_{2}}(x,s)
, then rearrange
in
x
to arrive at
L(t,s)=(K^{*_{2}})^{*_{1}}(t,s)
,
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. By
construction,
L(t,s)
is nonincreasing in each of
s
and
t
.

Arguing as in Theorem 2.5 of
[
KS1
]
one obtains

\int_{0}^{t}(T_{K}f)^{*}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}(T_{L}f^{*})(s)\,ds,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

and the Hardy-Littlewood-Polya (HLP) principle (see Section
2
)
then gives
\rho_{p,\phi}(T_{K}f)\preceq\rho_{p,\phi}(T_{L}f^{*})
. Thus
(1.1) follows from the one-dimensional inequality

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(T_{L}f^{*})^{**}(t)^{q}\phi_{1}^{(q)}(t)\,dt\right]^{1/q}\preceq\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f^{**}(s)^{p}\phi_{2}(s)\,ds\right]^{1/p}<\infty,

where
\phi_{1}^{(q)}(t)=qt^{q-1}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-q}\phi_{1}(s)\,ds
.
The main result, Theorem
3.1
, gives a further reduction of (1.2)
to a weighted norm inequality for the composed operator
T_{L}S
, where

S
denotes the Stieltjes transform

(Sh)(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}h(s)/(t+s)\,ds
. Its proof is in
Section
4
.

2.
Background

A
rearrangement-invariant
(r.i.) norm
\rho
on
M_{+}(E)
,

E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, satisfies the axioms of
[
BS
, Ch. 1]
, including
rearrangement-invariance:
\rho(f)=\rho(g)
whenever
f^{*}=g^{*}
. By
Luxemburg’s theorem
[
BS
, Ch. 3, Thm. 4.10]
there is an r.i. norm

\bar{\rho}
on
M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
with
\rho(f)=\bar{\rho}(f^{*})
for all

f\in M_{+}(E)
. The Köthe dual
\rho^{\prime}(g)=\sup_{\rho(f)\leq 1}\int_{E}fg

satisfies
\rho^{\prime\prime}=\rho
and Hölder’s inequality

\int_{E}fg\leq\rho(f)\rho^{\prime}(g)
.

Hardy-Littlewood-Polya (HLP) Principle

[
BS
, Ch. 2]
.

For any r.i. norm
\rho
on
M_{+}(E)
,

\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}\leq\int_{0}^{t}g^{*}\;\text{ for all }t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\;\implies\;\rho(f)\leq\rho(g).

The LG norm
\rho_{p,\phi}
defined in (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
is an r.i. norm; see
[
GK
]
. Its Köthe dual satisfies

\rho^{\prime}_{p,\phi}\approx\rho_{p^{\prime},\psi}
, where
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
and, for

t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,

(2.1)

\psi(t)=\frac{t^{p^{\prime}+p-1}\bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\phi\bigr)\bigl(\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-p}\phi(s)\,ds\bigr)}{\bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\phi+t^{p}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-p}\phi(s)\,ds\bigr)^{p^{\prime}+1}};

see
[
GolK
,
GP
]
. In particular,
\phi\equiv 1
gives

\rho_{p,1}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
and the dual weight
\psi\equiv 1
, while

\phi(t)=t^{q/p-1}
gives the Lorentz norm
\rho_{p,q}
with dual weight
of the same power type.

The
associated weight

\phi^{(q)}
defined by

(2.2)

\phi^{(q)}(t)=qt^{q-1}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-q}\phi(s)\,ds,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

arises naturally when passing from
f^{*}
to
f^{**}
: it is the minimal
weight
v
for which
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f^{**q}u\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f^{*q}v
holds
for all
f\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, as follows from differentiating the
characterization of Neugebauer
[
N
]
(see Proposition
4.1

below).

3.
Main Result

Theorem 3.1
.

Fix the indices
p
and
q
,
1<p\leq q<\infty
. Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be locally-integrable on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Set

\phi_{1}^{(q)}(t)=qt^{q-1}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-q}\phi_{1}(s)\,ds,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

Suppose

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{\phi_{1}^{(q)}(t)}{1+t^{q}}\,dt,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{\phi_{2}(t)}{1+t^{p}}\,dt<\infty

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\phi_{1}^{(q)}(t)\,dt=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\phi_{2}(t)\,dt=\infty.

Denote the dual weights of
\phi_{1}^{(q)}
and
\phi_{2}
by
\psi_{1}^{(q)}

and
\psi_{2}
, respectively, so that, for example,

\psi_{2}(t)=\frac{t^{p+p^{\prime}-1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{2}\right)\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-p}\phi_{2}(s)\,ds\right)}{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{2}+t^{p}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-p}\phi_{2}(s)\,ds\right)^{p^{\prime}+1}},\quad p^{\prime}=p-1.

Consider
K\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
and define

L(t,s)=(K_{x}^{*})^{*}_{y}(t,s),\quad s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

Then (1.1) holds whenever (1.2) does. Moreover, the latter is the case
provided one has

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(T_{L}S)h(t)^{q}\psi_{1}^{(q)}(t)\,dt\right]^{1/q}\leq\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}h(s)^{p}\psi_{2}(s)^{1-p}\,ds\right]^{1/p}<\infty,

h\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
. Here,

\displaystyle(T_{L}S)h(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}L(t,s)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{h(y)}{s+y}\,dyds

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}h(y)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{L(t,s)}{y+s}\,dsdy.

4.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

The proof requires the following two results: the first is from
[
N
]
,
the second is from
[
GolK
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
q
,
1<q<\infty
, and suppose
u,v\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
are
locally-integrable on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Then

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f^{**}(t)^{q}u(t)\,dt\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f^{*}(t)^{q}v(t)\,dt<\infty,\quad f\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),

if and only if

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}u(s)\,ds+t^{q}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-q}u(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\,ds,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

Remark 4.2
.

The smallest
v
such that (
4.1
) holds for a given
u
satisfies,
by (
4.2
),

\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{t}u(s)\,ds+t^{q}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-q}u(s)\,ds,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

Differentiation yields

v(t)=qt^{q-1}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-q}u(s)\,ds=u^{(q)}(t)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
p,q,\phi_{1}^{(q)},\phi_{2}
and
\psi_{2}
be as in
Theorem
3.1
. Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(T_{L}f^{**})(t)^{q}\phi_{1}^{(q)}(t)\,dt\right]^{1/q}\lesssim\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f^{**}(s)^{p}\phi_{2}(s)\,ds\right]^{1/p}<\infty

if and only if

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(T_{L}Sg)(t)^{q}\phi_{1}^{(q)}(t)\,dt\right]^{1/q}\lesssim\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}g(s)^{p}\psi_{2}^{1-p}(s)\,ds\right]^{1/p}<\infty,

f\in\Gamma_{p,\phi_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
and
g\in L_{p}(\psi_{2}^{1-p})
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We show that (1.1) holds whenever (
4.3
) does. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(T_{K}f)^{**}(t)^{q}\phi_{1}(t)\,dt\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(T_{L}f^{*})^{**}(t)^{q}\phi_{1}(t)\,dt\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\preceq\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(T_{L}f^{**})(t)^{q}\phi_{1}^{(q)}(t)\,dt\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\preceq\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f^{**}(s)^{p}\phi_{2}(s)\,ds\right]^{1/p}<\infty.

According to Proposition
4.3
, (
4.3
) and (
4.4
)
are equivalent. This concludes the proof.
\Box

5.
Application: the case
L(t,s)=(t+s)^{-1}

Remark 5.1
.

Recall that

(T_{L}Sg)(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}g(s)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{L(t,y)}{s+y}\,dyds,

so the kernel of
T_{L}S
is

M(t,s)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{L(t,y)}{s+y}\,dy\approx\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}L(t,y)\,dy+\int_{s}^{\infty}L(t,y)\frac{dy}{y}.

Like
L
,
M
is nonincreasing in each of
s
and
t
.

Example 5.2
.

Consider the case
L(t,s)=\dfrac{1}{t+s}
, so that
T_{L}S=S^{2}
. We have

\displaystyle(S^{2}f)(t)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}f(y)\,dy+\int_{s}^{\infty}f(y)\frac{dy}{y}\right]ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}f(y)\,dy+\int_{s}^{\infty}f(y)\frac{dy}{y}\right]\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\log\frac{t}{s}\,ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}f(s)\log\frac{s}{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}.

To obtain the condition on (
4.4
) in this case we use the following
result from
[
BK
]
.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
BK
]
)
.

Let
K(x,y)
be nonnegative on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
, nondecreasing in
x
,
nonincreasing in
y
, satisfying the growth condition

K(x,y)\leq D[K(x,z)+K(z,y)],\quad y<z<x.

Let
t,u,v,w
be nonnegative locally-integrable weight functions on

{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and suppose
1<p\leq q<\infty
. Then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left(w(x)\int_{0}^{x}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy\right)^{q}t(x)\,dx\right)^{1/q}\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(u(y)f(y))^{p}v(y)\,dy\right)^{1/p},

f\in M_{+}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, if and only if

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{x}K(x,y)u(y)^{-p^{\prime}}v(y)^{1-p^{\prime}}\,dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}w(y)^{-q}t(y)^{1-q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}\preceq 1

and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{x}u(y)^{-p^{\prime}}v(y)^{1-p^{\prime}}\,dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}K(y,x)w(y)^{-q}t(y)^{1-q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}\preceq 1.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
p,q,\phi_{1}^{(q)}
and
\psi_{2}
be as in Theorem
3.1
. Then
(
4.4
) holds for
T_{L}S=S^{2}
if and only if

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{x}\ln\frac{x}{y}\,\psi_{2}(y)\,dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}\phi_{1}^{(q)}(y)^{1-q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}\preceq 1,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{y}\psi_{2}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{y}^{\infty}\ln\frac{x}{y}\,\phi_{1}^{(q)}(x)^{1-q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}\preceq 1,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{y}\ln\frac{y}{x}\,\phi_{1}^{(q)}(x)^{1-q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{y}^{\infty}\psi_{2}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\preceq 1,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{y}\phi_{1}^{(q)}(x)^{1-q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{y}^{\infty}\ln\frac{x}{y}\,\psi_{2}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\preceq 1.
