Finite-Chain CKN-Bad Scale Counting for Navier–Stokes: Standard PDE Closure and Canonical Detector Realization
Runlong Yu

Abstract
This paper proves a finite-chain counting theorem for Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg bad scales of suitable weak solutions to the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. The main standard-PDE result bounds the weighted size of a finite set of CKN-bad scales by nonnegative channel costs consisting of vertical one-component concentration, annular leakage, pressure-tail terms, and pressure–flux–energy residuals. The proved closing mechanism is qualitative one-component compactness under a full local critical bound: small vertical component forces CKN smallness at a smaller radius. The paper then gives a canonical detector realization of the same finite-window counting philosophy. The original abstract detector is not identified with standard PDE channels; instead, a new amended canonical detector is defined using energy, flux, pressure-tail, retained low-pressure-mode, and finite-dimensional residual coordinates. For this amended detector, we prove upper realization, lower audit, CKN extraction, and finite-chain bad-scale counting.

Introduction

1.1
Problem and motivation

The local suitable-weak-solution framework originates in the Leray–Hopf theory of weak solutions
[
1
,
2
]
. The partial regularity theory of Scheffer and of Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg
[
3

[

[

is regular near a point if a scale-critical velocity–pressure quantity is
small on a sufficiently small cylinder. The finite-chain question is the
converse bookkeeping problem: if a finite chain of normalized scales remains
CKN-bad, which standard PDE channels must concentrate?

The main danger is tautology. A counting theorem is nearly empty if its
right-hand side already contains the full CKN-badness quantity

\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{\rho}}|u|^{3}+\rho^{-2}\int_{-\rho^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{\rho}}|p-(p)_{B_{\rho}}(s)|^{3/2}.

This paper therefore excludes the CKN core norm from the detector and
from the pressure–flux–energy residual. The closing channel used in the proof is the vertical component

C_{3}=\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{3}|^{3},

which is not equivalent to the full CKN quantity.

1.2
Main theorem target

For a finite geometric chain
r_{k}=\lambda^{k}r_{0}
, define

\mathcal{B}:=\{0\leq k\leq K:\Phi_{k}(\rho_{M})\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\}.

The main finite-chain estimate has the form

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{B}}w_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M)}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\left(C_{3,k}+\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\right).

In the version proved here, the closing mechanism uses
C_{3,k}
. The
additional pressure–flux–energy channels remain available in the same
nonnegative ledger, but they are not presented as independent substitutes for
the one-component argument.

1.3
Theorem status

The qualitative one-component compactness theorem is derived from two imported
classical inputs: compactness of suitable weak solutions under the full

\Psi(1)
bound, including pressure-decomposition convergence on smaller
cylinders, and regularity of compactness limits with
v_{3}=0
. The local
closing lemma and finite-chain counting theorem then follow by elementary
contraposition and summation. A stronger theorem in which annular leakage or
pressure-tail terms independently force CKN smallness is not claimed here.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Scale normalization

Let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution on

Q_{r_{0}}(z_{0})=B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r_{0}^{2},t_{0}).

Fix

0<\lambda<1,\qquad r_{k}=\lambda^{k}r_{0},\qquad k=0,\ldots,K.

The normalized fields on
Q_{1}=B_{1}\times(-1,0)
are

u_{k}(y,s)=r_{k}u(x_{0}+r_{k}y,t_{0}+r_{k}^{2}s),\qquad p_{k}(y,s)=r_{k}^{2}p(x_{0}+r_{k}y,t_{0}+r_{k}^{2}s).

2.2
CKN quantity

For
0<\rho<1
, write

Q_{\rho}:=B_{\rho}\times(-\rho^{2},0),\qquad(f)_{B_{\rho}}(s):=\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}f(y,s)\,dy.

Definition 2.1

(CKN quantity)
.

For normalized fields
(u,p)
, define

\Phi(\rho):=\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{\rho}}|u|^{3}+\rho^{-2}\int_{-\rho^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{\rho}}|p-(p)_{B_{\rho}}(s)|^{3/2}.

For the scale-
k
fields we write
\Phi_{k}(\rho)
.

Remark 2.2

(Classical endpoint)
.

The CKN epsilon-regularity theorem

[
5
,
7
]
is used only as a classical
endpoint:

\Phi_{k}(\rho)<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{regularity on a smaller physical cylinder.}

This paper does not reprove CKN epsilon regularity.

2.3
Full local critical bound

Definition 2.3

(Full critical bound)
.

For normalized fields
(u,p)
, define

A(1):=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{-1<s<0}\int_{B_{1}}|u(y,s)|^{2}\,dy,

E(1):=\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2},\qquad C(1):=\int_{Q_{1}}|u|^{3},

and

D(1):=\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{B_{1}}|p-(p)_{B_{1}}(s)|^{3/2}.

Set

\Psi(1):=A(1)+E(1)+C(1)+D(1).

For the scale-
k
fields we write
\Psi_{k}(1)
.

Remark 2.4

(Why
\Psi
, not only
\Phi
)
.

The full bound
\Psi(1)\leq M
is the finite-window compactness hypothesis
used in the one-component argument. The theorem does not rely only on

\Phi(1)\leq M
.

3
Non-Tautological Standard Channel Costs

3.1
Cutoff convention

Fix

\eta,\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}),\qquad\eta=\chi=1\text{ on }B_{3/4},

and

A_{\chi}:=\operatorname{supp}\nabla\chi\cup\operatorname{supp}\Delta\chi.

Let

A_{\eta}:=B_{1}\cap\operatorname{supp}(1-\eta).

3.2
Annular leakage

Definition 3.1

(Annular and full leakage)
.

The annular leakage at scale
k
is

\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}:=\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}\left(|u_{k}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}+|u_{k}|^{3}+|p_{k}||u_{k}|\right).

If a proof uses core energy or dissipation terms, use instead

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{full}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda B_{1}}\left(|u_{k}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}\left(|u_{k}|^{3}+|p_{k}||u_{k}|\right).

Remark 3.2

(Honest leakage convention)
.

The final theorem may use
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}
only when the proof uses

\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}
. If a proof requires
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{full}}
,
the final theorem must use the full leakage.

3.3
Pressure-tail costs

For each scale set

F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{act}}:=\eta\,u_{k,i}u_{k,j},\qquad P_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}:=R_{i}R_{j}(F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{act}}),\qquad P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}:=p_{k}-P_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}.

Definition 3.3

(Pressure-tail cost)
.

At the radius
\rho_{M}
selected by the one-component compactness theorem,
define

\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{comm}}:=\left\lVert[\eta,R_{i}R_{j}](u_{k,i}u_{k,j})\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho_{M}})}^{3/2}.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}
be a genuine harmonic-pressure tail or
finite-dimensional harmonic residual, and let
\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{trunc}}
be an
optional finite-rank pressure-tail truncation error. These are assumed to be
nonnegative channel costs. The pressure-tail cost is

\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}:=\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{comm}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{trunc}}.

Remark 3.4

(No CKN pressure core hidden)
.

\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{trunc}}
must not be defined as
the full scale-invariant pressure oscillation

\rho_{M}^{-2}\int_{-\rho_{M}^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{\rho_{M}}}|p_{k}-(p_{k})_{B_{\rho_{M}}}(s)|^{3/2}.

Nor may the pressure-tail cost be paired with a hidden copy of

\rho_{M}^{-2}\int_{Q_{\rho_{M}}}|u_{k}|^{3}
so as to reconstruct

\Phi_{k}(\rho_{M})
. If a pressure compactness argument needs the full
mean-free pressure oscillation, the stronger pressure-tail closing lemma has
not yet been proved.

Proposition 3.5

(Separated-support commutator bound)
.

For each normalized scale
k
,

\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{comm}}\leq C_{\eta,\rho_{M}}\int_{(-1,0)\times A_{\eta}}|u_{k}|^{3}.

Proof.

Let
T_{ij}=R_{i}R_{j}
and
f_{ij}=u_{k,i}u_{k,j}
. On

B_{\rho_{M}}
, the cutoff satisfies
\eta=1
. Hence

[\eta,T_{ij}]f_{ij}=T_{ij}\bigl((1-\eta)f_{ij}\bigr)\qquad\text{on }B_{\rho_{M}}.

The support of
(1-\eta)f_{ij}
lies in
A_{\eta}
, which is separated from

B_{\rho_{M}}
because
\rho_{M}<3/4
and
\eta=1
on
B_{3/4}
. The
kernel of
T_{ij}
is therefore smooth and bounded on

B_{\rho_{M}}\times A_{\eta}
. For almost every time
s
,

|[\eta,T_{ij}]f_{ij}(x,s)|\leq C_{\eta,\rho_{M}}\int_{A_{\eta}}|f(y,s)|\,dy,\qquad x\in B_{\rho_{M}}.

Taking the
L^{3/2}(B_{\rho_{M}})
-norm and using the finite measure of

A_{\eta}
gives

\left\lVert[\eta,T_{ij}]f_{ij}(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(B_{\rho_{M}})}\leq C_{\eta,\rho_{M}}\left\lVert f(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(A_{\eta})}.

Taking the
L^{3/2}
-norm in time and raising to the
3/2
-power yields

\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{comm}}\leq C_{\eta,\rho_{M}}\int_{-1}^{0}\left\lVert u_{k}(\cdot,s)\otimes u_{k}(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(A_{\eta})}^{3/2}\,ds.

Since

\left\lVert u_{k}(\cdot,s)\otimes u_{k}(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(A_{\eta})}^{3/2}=\int_{A_{\eta}}|u_{k}(y,s)|^{3}\,dy,

the desired estimate follows.
∎

3.4
PFE residual

Definition 3.6

(Non-tautological PFE residual)
.

The term
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
denotes a selected pressure–flux–energy
residual or excess, such as a pressure decomposition mismatch, flux-balance
residual, local-energy inequality defect, finite-rank PFE truncation error, or
reduced detector-comparison residual. It is a nonnegative cost.

It is part of the definition that
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
does not contain

\rho_{M}^{-2}\int_{Q_{\rho_{M}}}|u_{k}|^{3}\quad\text{or}\quad\rho_{M}^{-2}\int_{-\rho_{M}^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{\rho_{M}}}|p_{k}-(p_{k})_{B_{\rho_{M}}}(s)|^{3/2}.

Thus
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
is not allowed to be a disguised copy of the full
CKN core quantity
\Phi_{k}(\rho_{M})
.

Definition 3.7

(Vertical component and standard cost)
.

Define

C_{3,k}:=\int_{Q_{1}}|(u_{k})_{3}|^{3}.

The non-tautological standard cost is

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{std},k}:=C_{3,k}+\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}.

For an unindexed normalized solution we use the same notation without the
subscript
k
, writing
C_{3}
,
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ann}}
,

\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{tail}}
,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
, and

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{std}}
.

Remark 3.8

(Leakage actually used below)
.

The proof of the main standard-PDE counting estimate uses only
C_{3,k}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{std},k}
and the nonnegativity of the remaining costs. It does
not use core energy or core dissipation terms. Therefore the theorem-level
ledger may retain the annular leakage
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}
; the full leakage

\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{full}}
is recorded only for future variants whose
proofs genuinely require it.

4
One-Component Compactness

Classical Input 4.1

(Compactness of suitable weak solutions under a full critical bound)
.

For every
M<\infty
, every sequence of suitable weak solutions

(u^{(j)},p^{(j)})
on
Q_{1}
satisfying

\Psi^{(j)}(1)\leq M

has a subsequence and a suitable weak solution
(v,q)
on
Q_{1}
such that

\Psi_{v}(1)\leq M
and

u^{(j)}\to v\quad\text{strongly in }L^{3}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_{1}).

Moreover, at each fixed radius
0<\rho<1
used below, the local pressure
decomposition gives convergence of the mean-free pressure components

p^{(j)}-(p^{(j)})_{B_{\rho}}(s)\to q-(q)_{B_{\rho}}(s)\quad\text{strongly in }L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho}).

Classical Input 4.2

(CKN-smallness of the
v_{3}=0
limiting class)
.

For every
M<\infty
, there exists
0<\rho_{M}<3/4
such that every
compactness-limit suitable weak solution
(v,q)
on
Q_{1}
satisfying

\Psi_{v}(1)\leq M,\qquad v_{3}=0,

satisfies

\Phi_{v,q}(\rho_{M})<\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

Pressure compactness content.

The velocity compactness in Classical Input
4.1
is the standard local compactness theorem for suitable weak solutions under the local energy, dissipation,
L^{3}
-velocity, and
L^{3/2}
-pressure bounds; see
[
5
,
7
,
15
]
. The pressure part is the pressure-decomposition compactness needed in this paper, and is consistent with the pressure-regularity framework in
[
6
,
11
,
15
]
. On

B_{\rho}\Subset B_{\rho^{\prime}}\Subset B_{1}
, write

p^{(j)}=R_{i}R_{\ell}(\eta_{\rho^{\prime}}u_{i}^{(j)}u_{\ell}^{(j)})+h^{(j)}

with
\eta_{\rho^{\prime}}=1
on
B_{\rho^{\prime}}
. The active terms converge strongly
in
L^{3/2}
because
u^{(j)}\to v
strongly in
L^{3}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and the
Calderon–Zygmund map is bounded on
L^{3/2}
. The remainders
h^{(j)}

are harmonic in space on
B_{\rho^{\prime}}
, uniformly bounded in
L^{3/2}
after
subtracting time-dependent constants, and hence compact in
L^{3/2}(B_{\rho})

by interior harmonic estimates. Subtracting the spatial mean over
B_{\rho}

removes the pressure gauge. Thus the displayed pressure convergence is exactly
the standard pressure-compactness conclusion imported here; if one does not
import it, it must be proved separately.

Why the
v_{3}=0
input is classical.

The condition
v_{3}=0
, together with incompressibility, gives

\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}=0
. The third component of the equation gives

\partial_{3}q=0
in distributions. The limiting horizontal system is the
standard zero-vertical-component, or
2.5
-dimensional, Navier–Stokes
limiting system; its local regularity is the qualitative endpoint behind the
known one-component regularity criteria; compare, for example,
[
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
. This paper uses only the consequence
stated in Classical Input
4.2
: for the compact class
bounded by
\Psi_{v}(1)\leq M
, one can choose a radius
\rho_{M}
, depending
only on
M
, at which the CKN quantity is below

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}/2
.

Status of the imported inputs.

Classical Inputs
4.1
and
4.2

are the PDE inputs behind
the qualitative one-component argument. They are stated explicitly so that the
finite-chain counting theorem does not hide either pressure compactness or a
zero-component regularity theorem inside informal prose.

Theorem 4.3

(Qualitative one-component epsilon theorem)
.

Assume Classical Inputs
4.1

and
4.2
.
For every
M<\infty
, there exist

\varepsilon_{3}(M)>0,\qquad 0<\rho_{M}<1,

such that if
(u,p)
is a suitable weak solution on
Q_{1}
satisfying

\Psi(1)\leq M

and

\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{3}|^{3}\leq\varepsilon_{3}(M),

then

\Phi(\rho_{M})<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{M}
be the radius supplied by
Classical Input
4.2
. Suppose no such

\varepsilon_{3}(M)
exists.
Then for every
j
there is a suitable weak solution

(u^{(j)},p^{(j)})
on
Q_{1}
with

\Psi^{(j)}(1)\leq M,\qquad\int_{Q_{1}}|u^{(j)}_{3}|^{3}\leq j^{-1},

but

\Phi_{u^{(j)},p^{(j)}}(\rho_{M})\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

By Classical Input
4.1
, after passing to a
subsequence,

u^{(j)}\to v\quad\text{strongly in }L^{3}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_{1}),

the limit
(v,q)
is a suitable weak solution with
\Psi_{v}(1)\leq M
, and
the mean-free pressure components converge strongly in
L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho_{M}})
.
Since

\int_{Q_{1}}|u^{(j)}_{3}|^{3}\to 0,

the strong
L^{3}_{\mathrm{loc}}
convergence gives
v_{3}=0
.
Therefore Classical Input
4.2
applies to the limit and gives

\Phi_{v,q}(\rho_{M})<\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

The strong
L^{3}(Q_{\rho_{M}})
convergence of the velocities and the strong

L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho_{M}})
convergence of the mean-free pressures give

\Phi_{u^{(j)},p^{(j)}}(\rho_{M})\to\Phi_{v,q}(\rho_{M}).

Hence

\Phi_{u^{(j)},p^{(j)}}(\rho_{M})<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}

for all sufficiently large
j
, contradicting the choice of the sequence.
Thus some
\varepsilon_{3}(M)>0
exists.
∎

Remark 4.4

(Non-tautological status)
.

Theorem

4.3
is conditional on the classical compactness and
zero-component limiting inputs stated above. Its right-hand side uses only

\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{3}|^{3}
, not the full CKN core norm.

5
Non-Tautological Local Closing

Theorem 5.1

(Non-tautological local compactness closing lemma)
.

Assume Classical Inputs
4.1

and
4.2
.
For every
M<\infty
, let
\varepsilon_{3}(M)
and
\rho_{M}
be supplied by

Theorem

4.3
. Set

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M):=\varepsilon_{3}(M).

If
(u,p)
is a suitable weak solution on
Q_{1}
satisfying

\Psi(1)\leq M

and

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{std}}(u,p)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M),

then

\Phi(\rho_{M})<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

Equivalently,

\Phi(\rho_{M})\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{std}}(u,p)\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M).

Proof.

Every term in
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{std}}
is nonnegative and

C_{3}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{std}}.

Therefore

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{std}}(u,p)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M)=\varepsilon_{3}(M)

implies

\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{3}|^{3}=C_{3}\leq\varepsilon_{3}(M).

Applying
Theorem

4.3
gives

\Phi(\rho_{M})<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

The contrapositive is the second displayed implication.
∎

Remark 5.2

(What this proves)
.

The proof of
Theorem

5.1
uses the vertical component channel.
The annular leakage, pressure-tail cost, and PFE residual are honest
nonnegative standard costs in the same ledger, but this theorem does not prove
that those additional channels alone force CKN smallness.

Remark 5.3

(Stronger PFE closing remains open)
.

To prove a stronger pressure–flux–energy closing lemma, one must show that
small annular leakage, pressure-tail cost, and PFE residual force compactness
into a CKN-clean limiting class. In particular, pressure convergence must be
obtained from active-pressure convergence, harmonic compactness, and a genuine
tail condition. It may not be replaced by the full CKN pressure oscillation.

Single-Scale and Finite-Chain Counting

6.1
Single-scale concentration

Corollary 6.1

(CKN-bad scale forces standard channel concentration)
.

Assume Classical Inputs
4.1

and
4.2
.
Let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution on
Q_{1}
with

\Psi(1)\leq M.

If

\Phi(\rho_{M})\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}},

then

C_{3}+\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ann}}+\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{tail}}+\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M).

Proof.

This is the contrapositive statement in
Theorem

5.1
.
∎

6.2
Finite-chain counting

Definition 6.2

(CKN-bad scale set at the closing radius)
.

For a finite chain define

\mathcal{B}:=\{0\leq k\leq K:\Phi_{k}(\rho_{M})\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\}.

Theorem 6.3

(Weighted finite-chain bad-scale counting)
.

Assume Classical Inputs
4.1

and
4.2
.
Let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution on
Q_{r_{0}}(z_{0})
, and let

r_{k}=\lambda^{k}r_{0},\qquad k=0,\ldots,K.

Assume

\Psi_{k}(1)\leq M,\qquad k=0,\ldots,K.

Then for any weights
w_{k}\geq 0
,

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{B}}w_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M)}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\left(C_{3,k}+\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\right).

Proof.

For each
k\in\mathcal{B}
, Corollary
6.1
gives

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M)\leq C_{3,k}+\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}.

Multiplying by
w_{k}\geq 0
, summing over
k\in\mathcal{B}
, and enlarging the
right-hand sum to all
0\leq k\leq K
gives the estimate.
∎

Corollary 6.4

(Unweighted finite-chain bad-scale counting)
.

Under the hypotheses of
Theorem

6.3
,

\#\mathcal{B}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M)}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\left(C_{3,k}+\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\right).

Proof.

Take
w_{k}=1
in
Theorem

6.3
.
∎

7
Finite-Chain CKN-Small Scale Criterion

Corollary 7.1

(Finite-chain CKN-small scale criterion)
.

Assume the hypotheses of
Theorem

6.3
, and assume

\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}>0
. If

\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\left(C_{3,k}+\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\right)<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M)\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k},

then there exists
k_{*}\in\{0,\ldots,K\}
with
w_{k_{*}}>0
such that

\Phi_{k_{*}}(\rho_{M})<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

Consequently, CKN epsilon regularity gives regularity on a smaller cylinder
inside the physical cylinder
Q_{r_{k_{*}}}(z_{0})
.

Proof.

If every positive-weight scale were CKN-bad, then

Theorem

6.3
would imply

\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{close}}(M)}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\left(C_{3,k}+\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{ann}}+\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\right),

contradicting the strict inequality. Therefore some positive-weight scale is
CKN-small. The final sentence is exactly the classical CKN endpoint.
∎

Remark 7.2

(Finite-chain criterion only)
.

Corollary
7.1
is not a global regularity theorem.
It says
that sufficiently small total finite-chain cost forces at least one CKN-small
scale inside the considered finite chain.

8
Canonical Detector Realization

This section gives a canonical detector realization of the finite-chain counting philosophy in the same normalized geometry. It is logically separate from the standard-PDE theorem in
Section

6
. The standard-PDE theorem closes through the vertical component channel. The detector theorem below instead builds an amended finite-window detector whose coordinates are explicitly paid by standard pressure, flux, energy, and finite-dimensional residual costs.

8.1
Detector-level pressure and leakage costs

For the detector realization, fix a CKN testing radius

0<\rho\leq\lambda<3/4.

The active pressure and harmonic pressure at scale
k
are

F^{\mathrm{act}}_{k,ij}:=\eta u_{k,i}u_{k,j},\qquad P^{\mathrm{act}}_{k}:=R_{i}R_{j}(F^{\mathrm{act}}_{k,ij}),\qquad P^{\mathrm{harm}}_{k}:=p_{k}-P^{\mathrm{act}}_{k},

where the Riesz transforms are applied after zero extension. On the region where
\eta=1
, the term
P^{\mathrm{harm}}_{k}
is harmonic in space, up to the usual time-dependent pressure gauge. Set

P^{\mathrm{act},0}_{k}:=P^{\mathrm{act}}_{k}-(P^{\mathrm{act}}_{k})_{B_{\rho}}(s),\qquad P^{\mathrm{harm},0}_{k}:=P^{\mathrm{harm}}_{k}-(P^{\mathrm{harm}}_{k})_{B_{\rho}}(s).

Let
\Pi^{\mathrm{prs}}_{N,k}
and
\Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_{H,k}
be bounded finite-rank projections on
L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho})
. Here
N
is an active-pressure rank parameter and
H
is a harmonic-pressure rank parameter; the letter
M
remains reserved for the full critical bound
\Psi(1)\leq M
.

Definition 8.1

(Detector pressure costs)
.

Define

P_{k}^{\mathrm{comm}}:=\left\lVert[\eta,R_{i}R_{j}](u_{k,i}u_{k,j})\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho})}^{3/2},

P_{k}^{\mathrm{trunc}}:=\left\lVert(I-\Pi^{\mathrm{prs}}_{N,k})P^{\mathrm{act},0}_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho})}^{3/2},\qquad P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}\text{-}\mathrm{tail}}:=\left\lVert(I-\Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_{H,k})P^{\mathrm{harm},0}_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho})}^{3/2}.

Set

P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}:=P_{k}^{\mathrm{comm}}+P_{k}^{\mathrm{trunc}}+P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}\text{-}\mathrm{tail}}.

The retained low pressure-mode observation is

P_{k}^{\mathrm{low}}:=\left\lVert\Pi^{\mathrm{prs}}_{N,k}P^{\mathrm{act},0}_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho})}^{3/2}+\left\lVert\Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_{H,k}P^{\mathrm{harm},0}_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho})}^{3/2}.

The amended pressure cost is

P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail},+}:=P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+P_{k}^{\mathrm{low}}.

Definition 8.2

(Detector energy–flux costs)
.

Set

A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{-\lambda^{2}<s<0}\int_{\lambda B_{1}}|u_{k}(y,s)|^{2}\,dy,\qquad E_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}:=\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda B_{1}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}.

Define the detector energy–flux leakage by

L_{k}^{\mathrm{EF}}:=\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda B_{1}}(|u_{k}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{k}|^{2})+\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}(|u_{k}|^{3}+|p_{k}||u_{k}|),

and the amended energy–flux cost by

L_{k}^{\mathrm{AEF}}:=A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}+L_{k}^{\mathrm{EF}}.

Definition 8.3

(Finite-dimensional detector residual)
.

The detector residual is a finite sum of named non-CKN-core residuals,

R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}:=\Delta^{\mathrm{flux}}_{k,N}+\Delta^{\mathrm{en}}_{k,N}+\Delta^{\mathrm{src}}_{k,N,H}+\Delta^{\mathrm{det}}_{k,N,H}+\Delta^{\mathrm{chart}}_{k,N,H}+\Delta^{\mathrm{gate}}_{k,N}+\Delta^{\mathrm{rep}}_{k,N}+\Delta^{\mathrm{res}}_{k,N}+\Delta^{\mathrm{PFE},\mathrm{trunc}}_{k,N,H}.

No summand is allowed to contain the full CKN core quantity
\Phi_{k}(\rho)
. We write
R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE},+}=R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
unless an amended finite-dimensional residual is explicitly introduced.

8.2
Relation to an abstract local detector

The abstract local detector used in earlier finite-window audit formulations is not identified here with the canonical detector. The available abstract forms have the schematic shape

M^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\Lambda}(D)=\left\lVert O^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\Lambda}D\right\rVert+\sum_{a}\alpha_{a}\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{a}(D),\qquad M^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\Lambda,k}(D)=\left\lVert T^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\Lambda,k}D\right\rVert_{Z^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\Lambda,k}}.

These formulas do not provide standard-PDE component definitions for the local pressure, harmonic, flux, energy, gate, observation, and residual channels. Therefore the canonical detector below should be read as a new detector model, not as a reconstruction of the abstract detector.

Theorem 8.4

(Abstract-detector comparison not assumed)
.

The finite-chain detector theorem below does not require, and does not prove, an identity between the canonical detector and the abstract local detector
M^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\Lambda,k}
. It proves a standard-cost realization for a newly defined amended canonical detector.

Proof.

The abstract detector forms specify an observation norm and tax channels but do not identify those channels with the standard-PDE costs in
Definitions

8.1
,
8.2
and
8.3
. Without component-level formulas, an upper realization of
M^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\Lambda,k}
by these costs is not a theorem. The canonical detector is therefore defined and analyzed directly.
∎

8.3
Canonical package and unamended detector

At each scale, fix bounded finite-rank selectors

\Pi^{U}_{N,k}:L^{2}(Q_{\lambda})^{3}\to U_{N,k},\qquad\Pi^{F}_{N,k}:L^{3/2}(Q_{1})^{3\times 3}\to F_{N,k},

together with
\Pi^{\mathrm{prs}}_{N,k}
and
\Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_{H,k}
. Also fix finite families of test functions

\{\phi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{flux}}\}_{\ell=1}^{n_{\mathrm{flux}}}\subset L^{\infty}(\lambda A_{\chi}\times(-\lambda^{2},0)),\qquad\{\phi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{en}}\}_{\ell=1}^{n_{\mathrm{en}}}\subset L^{\infty}(Q_{\lambda}),

with norms at most one.

Definition 8.5

(Canonical local PFE package)
.

The canonical package is

D^{\mathrm{can}}_{k,N,H}:=(U_{k,N},P^{\mathrm{act}}_{k,N},P^{\mathrm{harm}}_{k,H},F^{\mathrm{cl}}_{k,N},F^{\mathrm{flux}}_{k,N},E^{\mathrm{en}}_{k,N},G_{k,N},O_{k,N},\operatorname{Rep}_{k,N}),

where

U_{k,N}:=\Pi^{U}_{N,k}(u_{k}|_{Q_{\lambda}}),\quad P^{\mathrm{act}}_{k,N}:=\Pi^{\mathrm{prs}}_{N,k}(P^{\mathrm{act},0}_{k}),\quad P^{\mathrm{harm}}_{k,H}:=\Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_{H,k}(P^{\mathrm{harm},0}_{k}),

F^{\mathrm{cl}}_{k,N}:=\Pi^{F}_{N,k}(\eta u_{k}\otimes u_{k}),

F^{\mathrm{flux}}_{k,N}:=\left(\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}\times(-\lambda^{2},0)}\phi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{flux}}|u_{k}|^{3},\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}\times(-\lambda^{2},0)}\phi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{flux}}|p_{k}||u_{k}|\right)_{\ell=1}^{n_{\mathrm{flux}}},

E^{\mathrm{en}}_{k,N}:=\left(\int_{Q_{\lambda}}\phi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{en}}|u_{k}|^{2},\int_{Q_{\lambda}}\phi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{en}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)_{\ell=1}^{n_{\mathrm{en}}}.

The gate, observation, and reproduction coordinates are finite-dimensional coordinates paid by the named residual terms in
Definition

8.3
.

Definition 8.6

(Canonical unamended detector)
.

The canonical unamended detector is

\displaystyle M^{\mathrm{can}}_{k,N,H}(D_{k})

\displaystyle:=\beta_{\mathrm{obs}}\operatorname{Obs}^{\mathrm{can}}_{k}+\beta_{\mathrm{prs}}\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{prs},k}+\beta_{\mathrm{harm}}\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{harm},k}+\beta_{\mathrm{flux}}\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{flux},k}

\displaystyle\quad+\beta_{\mathrm{en}}\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{en},k}+\beta_{\mathrm{gate}}\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{gate},k}+\beta_{\mathrm{rep}}\operatorname{Rep}^{\mathrm{can}}_{k}+\beta_{\mathrm{res}}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathrm{can}}_{k},

where the pressure, harmonic, flux, energy, gate, reproduction, and residual channels are paid by
P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}
,
L_{k}^{\mathrm{EF}}
, and
R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
. The observation channel is allowed only to observe the paid coordinates and satisfies

\operatorname{Obs}^{\mathrm{can}}_{k}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs},k}(\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{prs},k}+\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{harm},k}+\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{flux},k}+\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{en},k}+\operatorname{Tax}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathrm{gate},k}+\operatorname{Rep}^{\mathrm{can}}_{k}+\operatorname{Res}^{\mathrm{can}}_{k}).

Theorem 8.7

(Upper realization for the unamended detector)
.

There exists a finite-window constant
C^{\mathrm{can},\mathrm{up}}_{k,N,H}
such that

M^{\mathrm{can}}_{k,N,H}(D_{k})\leq C^{\mathrm{can},\mathrm{up}}_{k,N,H}(L_{k}^{\mathrm{EF}}+P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}+R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}).

Proof.

The pressure and harmonic detector channels are exactly the commutator, active-pressure truncation, and harmonic-tail components of
P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail}}
. Flux observations are bounded by the annular cubic and pressure-flux terms in
L_{k}^{\mathrm{EF}}
. Energy observations are bounded by the local
L^{2}
and dissipation terms included in
L_{k}^{\mathrm{EF}}
. Gate, reproduction, detector, and residual channels are included in
R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
. The observation domination inequality and the finite number of positive weights are absorbed into the constant.
∎

Proposition 8.8

(Why the unamended detector does not extract CKN badness)
.

The unamended detector alone does not give a proved implication

\Phi_{k}(\rho)\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M^{\mathrm{can}}_{k,N,H}(D_{k})\geq c>0.

The missing controls are an
L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}
velocity channel and retained low active and harmonic pressure-mode observations.

Proof.

The standard interpolation bound for
\int|u|^{3}
uses an
L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}
term together with dissipation. Time-integrated
L^{2}
and dissipation channels alone do not exclude the relevant time concentration. For the pressure, a mean-free pressure oscillation can lie in the retained finite-dimensional ranges of
\Pi^{\mathrm{prs}}_{N,k}
or
\Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_{H,k}
, producing zero high-tail truncation while remaining visible to the CKN pressure term. CKN extraction therefore requires the amended coordinates below, unless an additional structural theorem excludes these directions.
∎

8.4
Amended detector and CKN extraction

Definition 8.9

(Amended canonical detector)
.

Let
\beta_{A},\beta_{E},\beta_{\mathrm{low}}>0
. Define

M^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k,N,H}(D_{k}):=M^{\mathrm{can}}_{k,N,H}(D_{k})+\beta_{A}A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}+\beta_{E}E_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}+\beta_{\mathrm{low}}P_{k}^{\mathrm{low}}.

The amended detector distance is

\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k}:=A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}+E_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}+P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail},+}+R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE},+}.

Theorem 8.10

(Upper realization for the amended detector)
.

There exists a finite-window constant
C^{\mathrm{can}+,\mathrm{up}}_{k,N,H}
such that

M^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k,N,H}(D_{k})\leq C^{\mathrm{can}+,\mathrm{up}}_{k,N,H}(L_{k}^{\mathrm{AEF}}+P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail},+}+R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE},+}).

Proof.

Use
Theorem

8.7
for the unamended detector. The added
A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}
term is contained in
L_{k}^{\mathrm{AEF}}
, the core dissipation term is contained in
L_{k}^{\mathrm{EF}}\subset L_{k}^{\mathrm{AEF}}
, and
P_{k}^{\mathrm{low}}
is contained in
P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail},+}
. Absorb the fixed weights into the constant.
∎

Theorem 8.11

(Lower audit bound for the amended distance)
.

There exists
c^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k,N,H}>0
, depending only on the finite detector weights and norm conventions, such that

M^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k,N,H}(D_{k})\geq c^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k,N,H}\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k}.

Proof.

Each summand in
\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k}
appears in the amended detector with positive weight or is a finite sum of detector components appearing with positive weights. Finite-dimensional norm equivalence and positivity of the detector weights give the claimed constant.
∎

Lemma 8.12

(Velocity interpolation from amended energy)
.

There is
C_{\rho,\lambda}<\infty
such that

\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{\rho}}|u_{k}|^{3}\leq C_{\rho,\lambda}\bigl((A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}})^{3/4}(E_{k}^{\mathrm{core}})^{3/4}+(A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}})^{3/2}\bigr).

In particular, if
A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}+E_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}\leq\tau
, then

\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{\rho}}|u_{k}|^{3}\leq C^{\prime}_{\rho,\lambda}\tau^{3/2}.

Proof.

Since
Q_{\rho}\subset Q_{\lambda}
, the Sobolev interpolation inequality on
\lambda B_{1}
, followed by integration in time and Holder’s inequality, gives the displayed estimate.
∎

Lemma 8.13

(Pressure oscillation from low modes and tails)
.

Assume
\Pi^{\mathrm{prs}}_{N,k}
and
\Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_{H,k}
are bounded on
L^{3/2}(Q_{\rho})
. Then

\rho^{-2}\int_{-\rho^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{\rho}}|p_{k}-(p_{k})_{B_{\rho}}(s)|^{3/2}\leq C_{\rho,N,H}P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail},+}.

Proof.

Write
p_{k}=P^{\mathrm{act}}_{k}+P^{\mathrm{harm}}_{k}
. After subtracting spatial means over
B_{\rho}
, the triangle inequality bounds the pressure oscillation by the
L^{3/2}
-powers of
P^{\mathrm{act},0}_{k}
and
P^{\mathrm{harm},0}_{k}
. Decompose each term into its projected low part and high tail. The low parts are included in
P_{k}^{\mathrm{low}}
, and the high parts are included in
P_{k}^{\mathrm{trunc}}
and
P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}\text{-}\mathrm{tail}}
. The projection norms and
\rho^{-2}
factor are absorbed into
C_{\rho,N,H}
.
∎

Theorem 8.14

(CKN extraction for the amended distance)
.

There exists

\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}=\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}},\rho,\lambda,N,H)>0

such that

\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k}<\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi_{k}(\rho)<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

Equivalently,

\Phi_{k}(\rho)\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k}\geq\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}.

Proof.

If
\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k}<\delta
, then
A_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}+E_{k}^{\mathrm{core}}<\delta
and
P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail},+}<\delta
. By
Lemma

8.12
, the velocity contribution to
\Phi_{k}(\rho)
is bounded by
C^{\prime}_{\rho,\lambda}\delta^{3/2}
. By
Lemma

8.13
, the pressure contribution is bounded by
C_{\rho,N,H}\delta
. Choose
\delta=\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}
sufficiently small so that

C^{\prime}_{\rho,\lambda}\delta^{3/2}+C_{\rho,N,H}\delta<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

This proves the implication; the contrapositive gives the second statement.
∎

Theorem 8.15

(Finite-chain counting for the amended canonical detector)
.

Let
w_{k}\geq 0
. Assume the finite-window constants in
Theorems

8.10
,
8.11
and
8.14
are valid for
k=0,\ldots,K
. With

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{can}}:=\{0\leq k\leq K:\Phi_{k}(\rho)\geq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\},

one has

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{can}}}w_{k}\leq C_{\mathrm{can}+,K}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}(L_{k}^{\mathrm{AEF}}+P_{k}^{\mathrm{tail},+}+R_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE},+}),

where one may take

C_{\mathrm{can}+,K}=\frac{C^{\mathrm{can}+,\mathrm{up}}_{\max,K}}{c^{\mathrm{can}+}_{\min,K}\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}},

with

C^{\mathrm{can}+,\mathrm{up}}_{\max,K}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq K}C^{\mathrm{can}+,\mathrm{up}}_{k,N,H},\qquad c^{\mathrm{can}+}_{\min,K}:=\min_{0\leq k\leq K}c^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k,N,H}.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{can}}
,
Theorem

8.14
gives
\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k}\geq\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}
. Hence

c^{\mathrm{can}+}_{\min,K}\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{can}}}w_{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}c^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k,N,H}\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k}.

The lower audit bound controls the right-hand side by
\sum_{k}w_{k}M^{\mathrm{can}+}_{k,N,H}(D_{k})
. The upper realization then gives the stated estimate after division by
c^{\mathrm{can}+}_{\min,K}\delta^{\mathrm{can}+}_{*}
.
∎

Remark 8.16

(Comparison of the two counting theorems)
.

The standard-PDE counting theorem
Theorem

6.3
and the detector counting theorem
Theorem

8.15
have the same finite-chain form but different closing mechanisms. The former closes through one-component compactness and uses
C_{3,k}
as the proved channel. The latter closes because the amended detector contains CKN-compatible energy and pressure coordinates. They should not be presented as identical theorems.
