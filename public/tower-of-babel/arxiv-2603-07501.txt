Spectral bounds for the independence number of graphs and even uniform hypergraphs
Xinyu Hu, Jiang Zhou, Changjiang Bu

Abstract
In this paper, we give spectral upper bounds for the independence number of even uniform hypergraphs and graphs, extend the Hoffman bound to even uniform hypergraphs, and give a simple spectral condition for determining the independence number, the Shannon capacity and the Lovász number of a graph. The Hoffman bound on the Lovász number is also extended from regular graphs to general graphs.

3.6
, we get the following spectral bound for the independence number of a graph.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree
\delta
and average degree
\overline{d}>0
, and let
\lambda
be the minimum eigenvalue of
G
. Then

\alpha(G)\leq\frac{-\lambda n(\overline{d}-\lambda)}{(\delta-\lambda)^{2}},

with equality if and only if there exists an independent set
S
such that every vertex in
S
has degree
\delta
, and every vertex
i
not in
S
is adjacent to exactly
\frac{-\lambda(d_{i}-\lambda)}{\delta-\lambda}
vertices of
S
.

In the necessary and sufficient conditions of Corollary
4.10
, we always have

\displaystyle\frac{-\lambda(d_{i}-\lambda)}{\delta-\lambda}\geq-\lambda.

Motivated by this fact, we give the following spectral condition for determining the independence number, the Shannon capacity and the Lovász number of a graph.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with minimum eigenvalue
\lambda<0
, and let
S
be an independent set such that every vertex
i\notin S
satisfies

\displaystyle|\{j:\{i,j\}\in E(G),j\in S\}|\geq-\lambda.

Then

\displaystyle\alpha(G)=\Theta(G)=\vartheta(G)=|S|.

Proof.

Let
A
be the adjacency matrix of
G
. Then
M=A-\lambda I
is positive semidefinite, where
I
is the identity matrix. Let
y
be the indicator vector for
S
, that is

{y_{i}}=\begin{cases}1&i\in S,\\
0&i\notin S.\end{cases}

Since every vertex
i\notin S
satisfies
|\{j:\{i,j\}\in E(G),j\in S\}|\geq-\lambda>0
, we know that
x=My
is a positive vector and

\displaystyle\min_{u\in V(G)}x_{u}=-\lambda.

So we have

x^{\top}M^{\#}x\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(M)_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}=y^{\top}M{M^{\#}}My\mathop{\max}\limits_{u\in V\left(G\right)}\frac{{{{\left(M\right)}_{uu}}}}{{x_{u}^{2}}}=\frac{1}{-\lambda}{y^{\top}}My=|S|.

By Lemma
2.5
, we have

\alpha(G)\leq\vartheta(G)\leq x^{\top}M^{\#}x\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(M)_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}=|S|.

Since
|S|\leq\alpha(G)
, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

\alpha(G)=\Theta\left(G\right)=\vartheta(G)=|S|.

∎

A regular graph attaining the Hoffman bound must have an independent set
S
satisfying the condition in Theorem
4.11
(see Theorem
1.1
). The following is an nonregular example for Theorem
4.11
.

Example 4.12
.

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and minimum eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
, and let
H=G(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
be the graph obtained from
G
by attaching
p_{i}
pendant vertices at vertex
i
, where
p_{1}\geq\cdots\geq p_{n}\geq 1
and
\lambda_{0}+p_{n}-p_{n}^{-1}p_{1}\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha(H)=\Theta(H)=\vartheta(H)=p_{1}+\cdots+p_{n}.

Proof.

The adjacency matrix of
H
can be written as
A_{H}=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\
B^{\top}&A_{G}\end{pmatrix}
, where
A_{G}
is the adjacency matrix of
G
and
B^{\top}B={\rm diag}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
. Notice that

\displaystyle A_{H}+p_{n}I=\begin{pmatrix}p_{n}I&B\\
B^{\top}&A_{G}+p_{n}I\end{pmatrix}

is positive semidefinite, because the Schur complement
A_{G}+p_{n}I-p_{n}^{-1}B^{\top}B
is positive semidefinite. Hence
p_{n}\geq-\lambda
, where
\lambda
is the minimum eigenvalue of
H
. All new added pendant vertices form an independent set
S
in
H
, and
S
satisfies the condition in Theorem
4.11
. Hence

\displaystyle\alpha(H)=\Theta(H)=\vartheta(H)=|S|=p_{1}+\cdots+p_{n}.

∎

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph with minimum eigenvalue
\lambda
. In
[
L
, Theorem 9]
, Lovász proved that

\displaystyle\vartheta(G)\leq\frac{-\lambda n}{d-\lambda}.

We next extend this result to general graphs, that is, the upper bound in Corollary
4.10
is also an upper bound for
\vartheta(G)
.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree
\delta
and average degree
\overline{d}>0
, and let
\lambda
be the minimum eigenvalue of
G
. Then

\alpha(G)\leq\vartheta(G)\leq\frac{-\lambda n(\overline{d}-\lambda)}{(\delta-\lambda)^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
A
be the adjacency matrix of
G
. Then
M=A-\lambda I
is positive semidefinite, where
I
is the identity matrix. Let
x=M\mathrm{\mathbf{1}}
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the all-ones vector. Then

x^{\top}M^{\#}x\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(M)_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}=\mathbf{1}^{\top}MM^{\#}M{\mathbf{1}}\mathop{\max}\limits_{u\in V\left(G\right)}{\frac{{-\lambda}}{{{{\left({{d_{u}}-\lambda}\right)}^{2}}}}}

=\frac{-\lambda}{(\delta-\lambda)^{2}}{\mathbf{1}^{\top}M{\mathbf{1}}}=\frac{-\lambda(2|E(G)|-n\lambda)}{(\delta-\lambda)^{2}}=\frac{-\lambda n(\overline{d}-\lambda)}{(\delta-\lambda)^{2}}.

By Lemma
2.5
, we have

\alpha(G)\leq\vartheta(G)\leq\frac{-\lambda n(\overline{d}-\lambda)}{(\delta-\lambda)^{2}}.

∎

The following example shows that there exists a family of graphs such that our upper bound in Theorem
4.13
is smaller than the ratio-type bounds in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

Example 4.14
.

Let
G_{i}
be an
r_{i}
-regular graph with
n_{i}
vertices (
i=1,2
), and let
G=G_{1}\vee G_{2}
denote the join of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, i.e., the graph obtained from
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
by joining each vertex of
G_{1}
to each vertex of
G_{2}
. Let
\beta_{1}=\beta_{1}(n_{1},n_{2},r_{1},r_{2})
,
\beta_{2}=\beta_{2}(n_{1},n_{2},r_{1},r_{2})
and
\beta_{3}=\beta_{3}(n_{1},n_{2},r_{1},r_{2})
be the ratio-type bounds on
\alpha(G)
from Theorems
4.13
,
1.2
and
1.3
, respectively. When
r_{1},n_{2}
are positive constants and
n_{1}
is sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\beta_{3}.

Proof.

Suppose that
r_{1}=\lambda_{1}(G_{1})\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n_{1}}(G_{1})
are eigenvalues of
G_{1}
, and
r_{2}=\lambda_{1}(G_{2})\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n_{2}}(G_{2})
are eigenvalues of
G_{2}
. From
[
Cvetkovic1
, Theorem 2.1.8]
, we know that

\displaystyle\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}\pm\sqrt{(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}+4n_{1}n_{2}}}{2},\lambda_{2}(G_{1}),\ldots,\lambda_{n_{1}}(G_{1}),\lambda_{2}(G_{2}),\ldots,\lambda_{n_{2}}(G_{2})

are eigenvalues of
G=G_{1}\vee G_{2}
, and the maximum eigenvalue of
G
is

\displaystyle\lambda_{max}=\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}+\sqrt{(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}+4n_{1}n_{2}}}{2}.

When
r_{1},n_{2}
are positive constants and
n_{1}
is sufficiently large, the minimum degree of
G
is
\delta=r_{1}+n_{2}
, and the minimum eigenvalue of
G
is
\lambda_{min}=\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}-\sqrt{(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}+4n_{1}n_{2}}}{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{max}+\lambda_{min}=r_{1}+r_{2},~\lambda_{max}\lambda_{min}=r_{1}r_{2}-n_{1}n_{2}.

By computation, the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
is

\displaystyle\beta_{2}=\frac{(n_{1}+n_{2})(n_{1}n_{2}-r_{1}r_{2})}{\delta^{2}+n_{1}n_{2}-r_{1}r_{2}}.

Since the largest Laplacian eigenvalue of
G
is
\mu=n_{1}+n_{2}
, the upper bound in Theorem
1.3
is
\beta_{3}=n_{1}+n_{2}-\delta=n_{1}-r_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle\beta_{3}-\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}(n_{1}-r_{1})-\delta(n_{1}n_{2}-r_{1}r_{2})}{\delta^{2}+n_{1}n_{2}-r_{1}r_{2}}=\frac{\delta((r_{1}+n_{2})(n_{1}-r_{1})-n_{1}n_{2}+r_{1}r_{2})}{\delta^{2}+n_{1}n_{2}-r_{1}r_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta r_{1}(n_{1}-n_{2}-r_{1}+r_{2})}{\delta^{2}+n_{1}n_{2}-r_{1}r_{2}}>0

when
r_{1},n_{2}
are positive constants and
n_{1}
is sufficiently large.

From the ratio-type bounds in Theorems
4.13
and
1.2
, we have
\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
if and only if

\displaystyle\overline{d}<\frac{\delta((\lambda_{max}+\lambda_{min})\delta-2\lambda_{max}\lambda_{min})}{\delta^{2}-\lambda_{max}\lambda_{min}}=\frac{\delta((r_{1}+r_{2})\delta+2n_{1}n_{2}-2r_{1}r_{2})}{\delta^{2}+n_{1}n_{2}-r_{1}r_{2}},

where
\overline{d}=\frac{n_{1}(r_{1}+n_{2})+n_{2}(r_{2}+n_{1})}{n_{1}+n_{2}}
is the average degree of
G
. Notice that

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{1}\rightarrow\infty}\overline{d}=r_{1}+2n_{2},

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{1}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\delta((r_{1}+r_{2})\delta+2n_{1}n_{2}-2r_{1}r_{2})}{\delta^{2}+n_{1}n_{2}-r_{1}r_{2}}=2\delta=2r_{1}+2n_{2}.

Hence
\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
when
r_{1},n_{2}
are positive constants and
n_{1}
is sufficiently large.
∎
