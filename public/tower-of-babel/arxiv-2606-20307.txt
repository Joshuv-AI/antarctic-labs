Abstract
. In this paper, based on recent results for the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills (HYM) tensor [ 14 ] and its twisted variants [ 15 ] , we provide a dynamical construction of Hermitian-Einstein metrics on stable holomorphic vector bundles and its extension to Higgs bundles. Additionally, in the appendix, we use the heat flow method to give a new proof of the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the twisted prescribed HYM tensor equation from [ 15 ] , as well as its generalization to Higgs bundles.



1.
Introduction

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
over a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
of complex dimension
n
. Given a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, the curvature
R^{h}
of its Chern connection is an
\text{End}(E)
-valued
(1,1)
-form.
The
slope
of
E
is defined as

\mu(E)=\frac{\deg(E)}{\operatorname{rk}(E)},

where
\deg(E)
is the
degree
of
E
given by

\deg(E)=\int_{X}c_{1}(E)\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{X}\text{Tr}(R^{h})\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}

and this number is independent of the chosen Hermitian metric
h
.

E
is said to be
stable
if, for every proper coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
E
,

\mu(\mathcal{F})<\mu(E).

Also, a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
is called a
Hermitian-Einstein metric
(or its Chern connection is an
Hermitian-Yang-Mills connection
) if

(1.1)

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h}=\mu\,I_{E}

for some real constant
\mu
. Here,
\Lambda_{\omega}
denotes contraction with the Kähler form
\omega
and
I_{E}\in\text{End}(E)
is the identity.

The fundamental link between algebraic geometry (stable vector bundles) and differential geometry (Hermitian-Einstein metrics) is provided by the celebrated Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem
[
3
,
4
,
13
]
, which states that
E
admits a Hermitian-Einstein metric if and only if
E
is (poly)stable. Over the years, the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem has been generalized to holomorphic vector bundles over the non-Kähler setting by Li and Yau
[
9
]
and to Higgs bundles by Simpson
[
12
]
.

Very recently, Wang-Yang-Yau
[
15
]
solved the twisted prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor problem. Precisely, they proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Wang-Yang-Yau
[
15
]
)

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
. Then,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{F}}\mu\left(E/\mathcal{F}\right)=\sup_{\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h}>\lambda I_{E}}\lambda,

where the infimum is taken over all proper saturated torsion-free subsheaves of
E
and the supremum is taken over all Hermitian metrics on
E
. This number is denoted by
\lambda_{\min}
.

Moreover, for any
\lambda<\lambda_{\min}
and any Hermitian metric
\xi
on
E
, the equation

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h}=\lambda I_{E}+\xi h^{-1}

has a unique solution.

It is clear that identity (
1.2
) follows from the solvability of (
1.3
). In particular, Theorem
1.1
includes the following earlier result of Wang-Yang-Yau
[
14
]
as a special case.

Corollary 1.1
.

(Wang-Yang-Yau
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
)

Suppose that
E
admits a Hermitian metric
\hat{h}
such that its Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}
is positive. Then, for any Hermitian metric
\xi
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h}=\xi h^{-1}
.

By using the above results, and starting with an arbitrarily fixed Hermitian metric
h_{0}
, we can inductively define a sequence of Hermitian metrics
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}=h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1},\qquad m\geq 1.

Our first main result of this paper is

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
E
admits a Hermitian metric
\hat{h}
such that its Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}
is positive definite.
If
E
is stable, then the sequence
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
given by (
1.4
) converges to the Hermitian-Einstein metric on
E
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that taking trace in (
1.1
) and integrating give

\mu=\frac{\pi\,\mu(E)}{\operatorname{Vol}(X,\omega)}.

Since we assume
E
admits a Hermitian metric
\hat{h}
with
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}>0
,
\mu
is necessarily positive. Without loss of generality, we may scale
\omega
to make
\mu=1
so that the iteration procedure takes its simple form (
1.4
).

In general, the slope stability depends on the Kähler class of the base manifold. However, it is easy to see that if
E
is
\omega
-stable, then
E
is
t\omega

.

Remark 1.2
.

If

holds automatically. This is because, in this case, we can replace

E^{m}:=E\otimes L^{m}
for some ample line bundle

E^{m}
admits a metric

, but we prefer this neat version.

Remark 1.3
.

In 1978, Yau
[

initiated an iteration process to study the complex Monge-Ampére equation by using the solution to the Calabi conjecture. It is a general scalar version of the (inverse) Kähler-Ricci iteration

[

]
. Decades later, on a Fano manifold
(X,\omega_{0})
, the convergence problem of the analogous (inverse) Kähler-Ricci iteration sequence of Kähler forms
{
\omega_{m}
}
given by

\text{Ric}_{\omega_{m}}=\omega_{m-1}\qquad(m\geq 1)

to the Fano Kähler-Einstein form was studied; see Nadel
[
10
]
, Rubinstein
[

]
, Keller
[
7
]
, Berman et al
[

]
, Darvas and Rubinstein
[
5
]
and the references therein, as well as Zhang
[
17
]
for the cscK case.

Furthermore, based on the recent work of Fan-Wang-Yang-Yau
[
6

, by slightly modifying the proof of Theorem
1.2
we generalize it to Higgs bundles over compact Kähler manifolds; see Theorem
4.2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we define a modified Donaldson type functional and use it to show that the Donaldson functional from
[
3
]

, we derive necessary uniform estimates and prove Theorem
1.2
. In Section

. Finally, in the Appendix, we give a new proof of Theorem

[

2.
The Donaldson Functional under iteration

2.1.
Notation

We first fix notation. Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
over a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
of dimension
n
, and let
\mathcal{H}
denote the space of Hermitian metrics on
E
. Let
\underline{e}=\left(e_{1},\cdots,e_{r}\right)
be a local holomorphic frame of
E
, and
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}
be any local holomorphic coordinates on
X
such that the Kähler form is given by
\omega=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}g_{i\overline{j}}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}
.
Then, for any
h=h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e^{\beta}}\in\mathcal{H}
, the curvature
R^{h}
of its Chern connection, viewed as an
\text{End}(E)
-valued
(1,1)
-form, is given by

R^{h}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\gamma}e_{\gamma}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j},

where

R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\gamma}=h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}R_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\quad\text{and}\quad R_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}=-\frac{\partial^{2}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{\partial z_{i}\partial\overline{z}_{j}}+h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\frac{\partial h_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{\partial z_{i}}\frac{\partial h_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}.

If we take trace of
R^{h}
, then we get

c_{1}(E)=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\text{Tr}\left(R^{h}\right)\right]=\left[\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\sum_{\alpha}R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\alpha}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}\right].

The
\omega
-degree of
E
is given by

\deg_{\omega}(E)=\int_{X}c_{1}(E)\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}

and the slope is

\mu(E)=\frac{\deg_{\omega}(E)}{\operatorname{rk}(E)}=\frac{\deg_{\omega}(E)}{r}.

We also define the contraction of
R^{h}
with the Kähler form
\omega
by

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h}=R_{\gamma}^{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes e^{\gamma}=g^{i\overline{j}}R_{i\overline{j}\gamma}^{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes e^{\gamma},

which is a self-adjoint endomorphism of
E
with respect to
h
. Here, for the matrix
\left[R_{\gamma}^{\alpha}\right]
, we view
\gamma
as the row index and
\alpha
as the column index.

2.2.
The Donaldson functional and modified Donaldson functional

Let
I=[0,1]
. For any two Hermitian metrics
h_{0},h_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, let
\left\{h_{t}\right\}_{t\in I}
be any smooth path in
\mathcal{H}
joining
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
. The Donaldson functional introduced in
[
3
]
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{0},h_{1}\right)&=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-\mu I_{E}\right)\right)dV\right)dt\\
&\overset{\mu=1}{=}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-I_{E}\right)\right)dV\right)dt,\end{split}

where
\dot{h}_{t}=\frac{d}{dt}h_{t}
and
dV=\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}
is the volume form.
A key fact is that the above integral is independent of the chosen path
\left\{h_{t}\right\}
.

For any Hermitian metric
\xi
on
E
, we define the modified Donaldson functional as

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}\left(h_{0},h_{1}\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)\right)dV\right)dt.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}(h_{0},h_{1})={\mathcal{M}}(h_{0},h_{1})+\int_{X}\log\frac{\det h_{1}}{\det h_{0}}\,dV+\int_{X}\left(\text{Tr}\left(\xi h_{1}^{-1}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(\xi h_{0}^{-1}\right)\right)dV.

In particular, the modified functional
{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}
is independent of the chosen path
\left\{h_{t}\right\}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}(h_{0},h_{1})=&{\mathcal{M}}(h_{0},h_{1})+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\right)dV\right)dt\\
&-\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)dV\right)dt.\end{split}

Thus, the lemma follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\right)dV\right)dt&=\int_{X}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}\log\det h_{t}\right)dtdV\\
&=\int_{X}\log\frac{\det h_{1}}{\det h_{0}}\,dV\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)dV\right)dt&=-\int_{X}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}\text{Tr}\left(\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)dtdV\\
&=-\int_{X}\left(\text{Tr}\left(\xi h_{1}^{-1}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(\xi h_{0}^{-1}\right)\right)dV.\end{split}

∎

Next, we define a
L^{2}
metric on
{\mathcal{H}}
as in
[
8
]
. For any
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
\varphi,\psi\in T_{h}{\mathcal{H}}
, we let

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}(h)=\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\varphi h^{-1}\psi h^{-1}\right)dV.

We note that
\left\{h_{t}\right\}
is a geodesic if and only if

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\right)=0.

The space
{\mathcal{H}}
with this
L^{2}
metric is geodesic complete.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hermitian metric and let
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
be the unique solution of
\Lambda R^{h}=\xi h^{-1}
. Then
{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}(\xi,h)\leq{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}(\xi,\xi)=0
.

Proof.

Let
\left\{h_{t}\right\}_{t\in I}
be the unique geodesic in
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
h_{0}=\xi
and
h_{1}=h
. Set

\rho=\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\qquad\rm{and}\qquad\eta(t)={\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}(\xi,h_{t}).

We note that
\rho
is constant in
t
due to the geodesic equation
\frac{d}{dt}\rho=0
. Then, by a direct computation, we have

\eta^{\prime}(t)=\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\rho\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)\right)dV.

In particular,
\eta^{\prime}(1)=0
so
h
is a critical point of
\eta(t)
. Now, by direct computations and integration by parts, we obtain

(2.3)

\eta^{\prime\prime}(t)=\int_{X}\left(|\overline{\partial}\rho|^{2}+\text{Tr}\left(\rho^{2}\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)\right)dV\geq 0,

where we have used the fact that
\rho
is self-adjoint and
\xi
is positive definite. This implies that
\eta
is convex and has a critical point at
1
so
\eta(1)\leq\eta(0)
.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose the sequence of Hermitian metrics
\{h_{m}\}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
is given by (
1.4
). Then,
for any fixed
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we have
{\mathcal{M}}(\xi,h_{m})\leq{\mathcal{M}}(\xi,h_{m-1})
.

Proof.

Set
{\mathcal{M}}(h):={\mathcal{M}}(\xi,h)
. Then we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{M}}(h_{m})=&{\mathcal{M}}(\xi,h_{m-1})+{\mathcal{M}}(h_{m-1},h_{m})\\
=&{\mathcal{M}}(h_{m-1})+{\mathcal{M}}_{h_{m-1}}\left(h_{m-1},h_{m}\right)\\
&-\int_{X}\left(\log\frac{\det h_{m}}{\det h_{m-1}}+\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)-r\right)dV.\end{split}

At each point
x_{0}\in X
, we choose an orthonormal frame of
E
with respect to
h_{m-1}
and let the eigenvalues of
h_{m}
be
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r}
(which are all positive). Then, at
x_{0}
, we have

\log\frac{\det h_{m}}{\det h_{m-1}}+\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)-r=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(\log\lambda_{j}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}-1\right).

By a simple computation, one finds that the minimum of the function

g(x)=\log x+\frac{1}{x}-1\qquad(x>0)

is achieved at
x=1
with
g(1)=0
. Thus, we have

\int_{X}\left(\log\frac{\det h_{m}}{\det h_{m-1}}+\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)-r\right)dV\geq 0.

Meanwhile, by Lemma
2.2
, we also have
{\mathcal{M}}_{h_{m-1}}\left(h_{m-1},h_{m}\right)\leq 0
. Therefore, it follows that
{\mathcal{M}}(h_{m})\leq{\mathcal{M}}(h_{m-1})
.

∎

3.
Convergence of the sequence
\{h_{m}\}

First of all, we fix any
h_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}
and let
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
be the sequence given by inductively solving the equation (
1.4
). In this section, we show that the sequence
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
converges to the Hermitian-Einstein metric, thus proving Theorem
1.2
. Throughout the section, we denote
{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)={\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{0},\cdot\right)
by the Donaldson functional.

3.1.
The
C^{0}
estimate

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
m
, such that

\frac{1}{C}h_{0}\leq h_{m}\leq Ch_{0}

for all
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

Set
K_{m}=\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}
, which is positive and self-adjoint with respect to both
h_{m-1}
and
h_{m}
. By using equation (
1.4
), a direct computation shows that

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m-1}}=\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}+\Lambda_{\omega}\overline{\partial}\left(\nabla^{h_{m}}K_{m}K_{m}^{-1}\right),

or equivalently

(3.1)

\displaystyle K_{m-1}=K_{m}-g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{\overline{j}}\left(\nabla_{i}^{h_{m}}K_{m}K_{m}^{-1}\right).

For each
m\geq 1
, we let
\overline{\lambda}_{x}\left(K_{m}\right)
be the largest eigenvalue of
K_{m}
at
x\in X
with respect to
h_{m}
and define

\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right):=\sup_{x\in X}\overline{\lambda}_{x}\left(K_{m}\right).

Pick
x_{m}\in X
such that
\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)=\overline{\lambda}_{x_{m}}\left(K_{m}\right)
, and let
v\in E_{x_{m}}
be an eigenvector of
K_{m}
at
x_{m}
with respect to
\overline{\lambda}_{x_{m}}\left(K_{m}\right)
with
\|v\|_{h_{m}}=1
. Evaluating at
x_{m}
, by equation (
3.1
), we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle K_{m-1}v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}=&\langle K_{m}v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}-\langle g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{\overline{j}}\left(\nabla_{i}^{h_{m}}K_{m}K_{m}^{-1}\right)v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}\\
=&\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)-\langle g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{\overline{j}}\left(\nabla_{i}^{h_{m}}K_{m}K_{m}^{-1}\right)v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}.\end{split}

Now, we consider the term
\langle g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{\overline{j}}\left(\nabla_{i}^{h_{m}}K_{m}K_{m}^{-1}\right)v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}
. First, we extend
v
to a smooth local section
\widetilde{v}
of
E
over a neighborhood
U
of
x_{m}
such that
\nabla^{h_{m}}\widetilde{v}(x_{m})=0
and
\overline{\partial}\widetilde{v}(x_{m})=0
. Then, we set

f(x)=\langle K_{m}\widetilde{v},\widetilde{v}\rangle_{h_{m}}-\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)\langle\widetilde{v},\widetilde{v}\rangle_{h_{m}}\in C^{\infty}\left(U,{\mathbb{R}}\right).

By definition of
f
, we see that
f(x)\leq 0
and
f(x_{m})=0
. Thus,
\partial_{i}f(x_{m})=0
,
\partial_{\overline{j}}f(x_{m})=0
and
\Delta f(x_{m})=g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{i}\partial_{\overline{j}}f(x_{m})\leq 0
. By using the fact that
K_{m}
is
h_{m}
-self adjoint and
K_{m}v=\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)v
, we get

\langle g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{\overline{j}}\left(\nabla_{i}^{h_{m}}K_{m}K_{m}^{-1}\right)v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}\leq 0.

It follows that

\langle K_{m-1}v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}\geq\langle K_{m}v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}=\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right).

Now, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle K_{m-1}v,v\rangle_{h_{m-1}}&=\langle h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}K_{m-1}v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}\\
&=\langle K_{m}K_{m-1}v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}\\
&=\langle K_{m-1}v,K_{m}v\rangle_{h_{m}}\\
&=\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)\langle K_{m-1}v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}\geq\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)^{2}\end{split}

and

\langle v,v\rangle_{h_{m-1}}=\langle K_{m}v,v\rangle_{h_{m}}=\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right).

Hence,

\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m-1}\right)\geq\frac{\langle K_{m-1}v,v\rangle_{h_{m-1}}}{\langle v,v\rangle_{h_{m-1}}}\geq\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right).

Therefore,
\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)\leq\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{0}\right)
for all
m\geq 1
.
Moreover, since
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}
is
h_{m}
-self adjoint,

\left|\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}\right|_{h_{m}}=\left(\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sqrt{r}\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right).

Hence, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that, for all
m\geq 1
,

\left|\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}\right|_{h_{m}}\leq C_{1}.

Next, let

\varphi_{m}=\log\frac{\det h_{m}}{\det h_{0}}=\text{Tr}\log\left(h_{m}h_{0}^{-1}\right),\quad\alpha_{m}=\frac{1}{V}\int\varphi_{m}\ dV,\quad\rm{and}\quad\widetilde{h}_{m}=e^{-\frac{\varphi_{m}}{r}}h_{m}.

Then, we have
\det\widetilde{h}_{m}=\det h_{0}
and that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi_{m}=\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}\right).

Since
\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}\right)=\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)>0
, we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right)-C_{1}\sqrt{r}\leq\Delta\varphi_{m}\leq\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right).

On the other hand, a direct computation gives

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\widetilde{h}_{m}}=\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}+\frac{\Delta\varphi_{m}}{r}I,

which implies that
\overline{\lambda}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\widetilde{h}_{m}}\right)\leq C_{2}.

Thus, there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

\left|\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\widetilde{h}_{m}}\right|_{\widetilde{h}_{m}}\leq C_{3}

for all
m\geq 1
.
We note that
\log\widetilde{h}_{m}h_{0}^{-1}
is trace-free.

By (
3.3
), we also have

\Delta\varphi_{m}=\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right).

It follows that

\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)dV=\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right)dV=rV,

where recall that we have scaled
\omega
such that
\mu=1
. Now,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)&\geq r\det\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
&=re^{\frac{\varphi_{m-1}-\varphi_{m}}{r}}.\end{split}

By Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}rV=\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)dV&\geq r\int_{X}e^{\frac{\varphi_{m-1}-\varphi_{m}}{r}}dV\\
&\geq rVe^{\frac{1}{V}\int_{X}\frac{\varphi_{m-1}-\varphi_{m}}{r}dV}\\
&=rVe^{\frac{1}{r}(\alpha_{m-1}-\alpha_{m})}.\end{split}

Thus
\alpha_{m-1}\leq\alpha_{m}
. Since
\varphi_{0}=0
, we know that
\alpha_{0}=0
and thus
\alpha_{m}\geq 0
.

By (
2.4
), we have

{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m-1}\right)-{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m}\right)=\int_{X}\left(\log\frac{\det h_{m}}{\det h_{m-1}}+\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)-r\right)dV-{\mathcal{M}}_{h_{m-1}}\left(h_{m-1},h_{m}\right).

Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{h_{m-1}}\left(h_{m-1},h_{m}\right)\leq 0
and
\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)dV=rV
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m-1}\right)-{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m}\right)&\geq\int_{X}\log\frac{\det h_{m}}{\det h_{m-1}}\\
&=\int_{X}\left(\varphi_{m}-\varphi_{m-1}\right)dV\\
&=\left(\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{m-1}\right)V.\end{split}

In
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, Donaldson showed that
{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
is bounded from below if
E
is semi-stable. Combining all of these, we conclude that

\alpha_{m}V\leq{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{0}\right)-{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m}\right)\leq C_{4}

for some constant
C_{4}>0
and all
m\geq 1
. Therefore, for all
m\geq 1
,

0\leq\alpha_{m}\leq\frac{C_{4}}{V}.

Furthermore, a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{M}}\left(\widetilde{h}_{m},h_{m}\right)&=\int_{X}\left(\frac{\varphi_{m}}{r}\left(\text{Tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\widetilde{h}_{m}})-r\right)-\frac{1}{2r}\varphi_{m}\Delta\varphi_{m}\right)dV\\
&=\int_{X}\left(\frac{\varphi_{m}}{r}\left(\text{Tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}})-r\right)-\frac{1}{2r}\varphi_{m}\Delta\varphi_{m}\right)dV.\end{split}

Let
\psi=\frac{1}{r}\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right)-1
. Then, we have
\int_{X}\psi dV=0
. It follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{M}}(h_{m})&={\mathcal{M}}\left(\widetilde{h}_{m}\right)+{\mathcal{M}}\left(\widetilde{h}_{m},h_{m}\right)\\
&={\mathcal{M}}\left(\widetilde{h}_{m}\right)+\int_{X}\varphi_{m}\psi\,dV+\frac{1}{2r}\|\overline{\partial}\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Since
{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m}\right)\leq{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{0}\right)=0
by Lemma
2.3
and
{\mathcal{M}}\left(\widetilde{h}_{m}\right)\geq-C_{5}
, we get

\frac{1}{2r}\|\overline{\partial}\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{X}\varphi_{m}\psi\,dV\leq C_{5}.

By the Poincarè inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{X}\varphi_{m}\psi\,dV\right|&=\left|\int_{X}\left(\varphi_{m}-\alpha_{m}\right)\psi\,dV\right|\\
&\leq\|\varphi_{m}-\alpha_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C_{6}\|\overline{\partial}\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

for some constant
C_{6}>0
and all
m\geq 1
.

It follows that

\frac{1}{2r}\|\overline{\partial}\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-C_{6}\|\overline{\partial}\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{5}

and hence

\|\overline{\partial}\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{7}

for some constant
C_{7}>0
and all
m\geq 1
.

Since we have uniform control on
\alpha_{m}=\frac{1}{V}\int\varphi_{m}\ dV
for all
m\geq 1
, we get

\|\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{8}

as well, and thus there exists a constant
C_{9}>0
such that, for all
m\geq 1
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{m}\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C_{9}.

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), the standard Green function estimates and the Moser iteration guarantee that there exists
C_{10}>0
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{m}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C_{10}.

By (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{M}}(\widetilde{h}_{m})&={\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m}\right)-\frac{1}{2r}\|\overline{\partial}\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{X}\varphi_{m}\psi dV\\
&\leq\|\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C_{9}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

By Donaldson’s work
[
3
,
4
]
, we know that the stability of
E
implies the functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is proper in the sense that the
L^{1}
norm of
\log\left(\widetilde{h}_{m}h_{0}^{-1}\right)
is controlled by
{\mathcal{M}}\left(\widetilde{h}_{m}\right)
. This was packaged into the
C^{0}
estimate in
[
12
]
by using Uhlenbeck-Yau’s work
[
13
]
. By
[
12
, Proposition 5.3]
, we have

\sup_{X}\left|\log\left(\widetilde{h}_{m}h_{0}^{-1}\right)\right|\leq C_{11}+C_{12}{\mathcal{M}}\left(\widetilde{h}_{m}\right)\leq C_{13}

for some constant
C_{13}>0
and all
m\geq 1
.
This implies that,
\widetilde{h}_{m}
and
h_{0}
are uniformly equivalent. Finally, it follows from (
3.7
) that
h_{m}
and
\widetilde{h}_{m}
are uniformly equivalent. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

∎

3.2.
The higher order estimates

We now derive uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates of
h_{m}
for
k\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<1
. Denote by
H_{m}=h_{m}h_{0}^{-1}
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Lambda_{\omega}\overline{\partial}\left(\nabla^{h_{0}}H_{m}H_{m}^{-1}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}-\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}=h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}-\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}.\end{split}

Theorem 3.2
.

For any
k\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<1
, there exists a constant
C_{k,\alpha}>0
such that

\|h_{m}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}\left(h_{0}\right)}\leq C_{k,\alpha}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\left\{\|H_{m}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\right\}
is uniformly bounded.
By (
3.8
) and direct computations, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{h_{0}}H_{m}=g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}H_{m}H_{m}^{-1}\partial_{\overline{j}}H_{m}+\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}-\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right)H_{m}.\end{split}

Note that the
C^{0}
estimate in Theorem
3.1
gives a uniform bound on
H_{m}
and
H_{m}^{-1}
. It then follows that
\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}-\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right)H_{m}
is uniformly bounded and so is its
L^{p}
norm. By using the interpolation inequality to handle the term
g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}H_{m}H_{m}^{-1}\partial_{\overline{j}}H_{m}
, and the fact that
H_{m}^{-1}
is uniformly bounded, the elliptic regularity theory implies that, for any
p>0
, there exists constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\|H_{m}\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq C_{p}.

It follows from the Sobolev embedding that, for any
0<\alpha<1
, there exists
C_{\alpha}>0
such that

\|H_{m}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}\left(h_{0}\right)}\leq C_{1,\alpha}.

Now, we can prove the rest of higher order estimates by induction. Assuming we have obtained the uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates on
\left\{H_{m}\right\}
. Then, we know that the right hand side of (
3.9
) is uniformly bounded in
C^{k-1,\alpha}
norm. Therefore, it follows from the standard Schauder theory that
\left\{H_{m}\right\}
is uniformly bounded in
C^{k+1,\alpha}
norm.

∎

3.3.
The convergence

Now, we are ready to prove the convergence of
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The sequence
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
converges smoothly to a limiting Hermitian-Einstein metric
h_{\infty}
with
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{\infty}}=I_{E}
.

Proof.

By (
2.4
), we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m-1}\right)-{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m}\right)\geq\int_{X}\left(-\log\det K_{m}+\text{Tr}K_{m}-r\right)dV\geq 0,

where
K_{m}=h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}
as before. Hence, for
m\geq 1
, we see that

{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{0}\right)-{\mathcal{M}}\left(h_{m}\right)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{X}\left(-\log\det K_{j}+\text{Tr}K_{j}-r\right)dV.

Since
{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
is bounded from below, we know that the series

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{X}\left(-\log\det K_{j}+\text{Tr}K_{j}-r\right)dV

is convergent.
Thus, we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{X}\left(-\log\det K_{m}+\text{Tr}K_{m}-r\right)dV=0.

Note that
-\log\det K_{m}+\text{Tr}K_{m}-r\geq 0
pointwise, and it is uniformly Lipschitz due to the uniform
C^{0}
estimate, i.e.,
C^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{m}\leq Ch_{0}
, and the uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates.
This forces

\lim_{m\to\infty}h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}=I_{E}

in
C^{0}
topology. Now, a standard fact in PDE guarantees that if a sequence converges in
C^{0}
norm and all
C^{k,\alpha}
norms of the sequence are bounded by some constant
M_{k,\alpha}
, then the interpolation inequality implies that the sequence converges smoothly. Therefore, it follows from Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
that
h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\to I_{E}
smoothly.

On the other hand, by the Arzela-Ascoli theorem, we can find a subsequence
\left\{h_{m_{j}}\right\}
of
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
which converges smoothly to a limiting Hermitian metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
. Note that we have a uniform bound on
\left\{H_{m}\right\}
which forces
h_{\infty}
to be nondegenerate. By the uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
bounds of
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
and by using interpolation inequalities, we know that
\{h_{m_{j}}\}\to h_{\infty}
smoothly. Thus,

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{\infty}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\Lambda R^{h_{m_{j}}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}h_{m_{j-1}}h_{m_{j}}^{-1}=I_{E}.

Namely,
h_{\infty}
is a Hermitian-Einstein metric.

It remains to show that such limit metric is unique. Since the sequence
\left\{\alpha_{m}\right\}
is nondecreasing and
0\leq\alpha_{m}\leq\frac{C_{4}}{V}
, we know that it is convergent and let
\alpha_{\infty}
be its limit. Then, we have

\alpha_{\infty}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\alpha_{m_{j}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\int_{X}\log\frac{\det h_{m_{j}}}{\det h_{0}}dV=\frac{1}{V}\int_{X}\log\frac{\det h_{\infty}}{\det h_{0}}dV.

Suppose that there is another subsequence
\left\{h_{n_{s}}\right\}
of
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
which converges smoothly to another limiting metric
h_{\infty}^{\prime}
. Then, we know that
h_{\infty}^{\prime}
is also Hermitian-Einstein and

\frac{1}{V}\int_{X}\log\frac{\det h_{\infty}}{\det h_{0}}dV=\alpha_{\infty}.

Since we know that
h_{\infty}^{\prime}=ch_{\infty}
for some constant
c>0
, the above argument forces
c=1
. Thus, the limit metric is unique.

Since all convergent subsequences of
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
converge to
h_{\infty}
, and we have uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates on
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
, we can conclude that
h_{m}\to h_{\infty}
smoothly. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
1.2
.

∎

4.
The extension to Higgs Bundles

In this section, based on the recent work
[
6
]
, we generalize our Theorem
1.2
to Higgs bundles over compact Kähler manifolds.

Recall that a Higgs bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
consists of a pair
(E,\Phi)
, where
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle over
(X,\omega)
, and
\Phi
is a
Higgs field
on
E
, i.e.,
\Phi\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathrm{End}(E))
is a holomorphic 1-form with values in endomorphisms of
E
satisfying the integrability condition
\Phi\wedge\Phi=0.

Given any Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, we denote by
R^{h}
the curvature of its Chern connection and define the degree
\deg(E)
of
E
and the slope
\mu(E)
of
E
as before.
For the Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
, we have the following basic notions:

\bullet

(E,\Phi)
is
stable
if
\mu(F)<\mu(E)
for all proper
Higgs-invariant
subbundles
F\subset E
. Here, by a subbundle
F\subset E
being Higgs-invariant (or
\Phi
-invariant) it means that
\Phi(F)\subset F\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}.

\bullet

A Hermitian metric
h
on
E
is said to be a
Hermitian-Einstein-Higgs
metric if it satisfies the following equation:

\Lambda_{\omega}(R^{h}+[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h}])=\mu I_{E},

where
\Phi^{*,h}
is the adjoint of
\Phi
with respect to the metric
h
.

In
[
12
]
, Simpson generalized the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem to Higgs bundles:
a Higgs bundle is (poly)stable if and only if it admits an Hermitian-Einstein-Higgs metric
.

More recently, in
[
6
]
, the authors also generalized Corollary
1.1
(Wang-Yang-Yau
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
) to Higgs bundles.

Theorem 4.1
.

(Fan-Wang-Yang-Yau
[
6
]
)

Suppose that
(E,\Phi)
admits a Hermitian metric
\hat{h}
such that its Higgs Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{\hat{h}}+[\Phi,\Phi^{*,\hat{h}}]\right)
is positive definite. Then, for any Hermitian metric
\xi
on
E
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h}+[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h}]\right)=\xi h^{-1}
.

Now, in the rest of this section, we consider a Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
over a compact
(X,\omega)
and assume either
(X,\omega)
is projective or there exists a Hermitian metric
\hat{h}
on
E
such that
\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{\hat{h}}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,\hat{h}}\right]\right)>0
.
By using Theorem
4.1
and induction, starting with an arbitrarily fixed Hermitian metric
h_{0}
, we can construct a sequence of Hermitian metrics
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
by solving

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h_{m}}+[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h_{m}}]\right)=h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1},\qquad m\geq 1.

By slightly modifying the proof of Theorem
1.2
, and normalizing
\mu=1
as before, we can show that if
(E,\Phi)
is stable then the iteration procedure (
4.1
) leads to a unique Hermitian-Einstein-Higgs metric.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(E,\Phi)
be a stable Higgs bundle over
(X,\omega)
. Suppose that either
(X,\omega)
is projective or there exists a Hermitian metric
\hat{h}
on
E
such that
\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{\hat{h}}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,\hat{h}}\right]\right)>0
. Then, the sequence
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
given by (
4.1
) converges smoothly to a Hermitian-Einstein-Higgs metric
h_{\infty}
satisfying

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h_{\infty}}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,{h_{\infty}}}\right]\right)=I_{E}.

Sketch of Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the space of Hermitian metrics on
E
as before. We first note that, for any Hermitian metrics
h_{0},h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, if we let

{\mathcal{M}}^{H}\left(h_{0},h_{1}\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h_{t}}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,{h_{t}}}\right]\right)-I_{E}\right)\right)dV\right)dt

and

{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}^{H}\left(h_{0},h_{1}\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h_{t}}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,{h_{t}}}\right]\right)-\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)\right)dV\right)dt,

where
\left\{h_{t}\right\}
is any smooth path joining
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
, then
{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}^{H}
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{H}
are independent of the choice of
\left\{h_{t}\right\}
. Furthermore, if we fix
\xi
and let
\left\{h_{t}\right\}
be the geodesic joining
\xi
and another Hermitian metric
h
, then direct computations give the following analogue of (
2.3
),

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi}^{H}\left(\xi,h_{t}\right)=\int_{X}\left(\left|\overline{\partial}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\left[\Phi,\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\right]\right|_{h_{t}}^{2}+\text{Tr}\left(\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\right)^{2}\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)\right)dV\geq 0,

from which the corresponding version of Lemma
2.2
, hence also of Lemma
2.3
, hold in the current setting. Now, we let

K_{m}=\Lambda_{\omega}F^{h_{m}}=\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h_{m}}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,{h_{m}}}\right]\right).

We notice that, similar to formula (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle K_{m-1}=K_{m}-g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{\overline{j}}\left(\nabla_{i}^{h_{m}}K_{m}K_{m}^{-1}\right)+\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h_{m-1}}\right]-\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,{h_{m}}}\right]\right).

Since
K_{m}=h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}
, we know that
\Phi^{*,h_{m-1}}=K_{m}\Phi^{*,h_{m}}K_{m}^{-1}
. Assume that at
x_{m}\in X
we have
\overline{\lambda}_{x_{m}}\left(K_{m}\right)=\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)
and
v\in E_{x_{m}}
is a
h_{m}
-unit eigenvector of
\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)
, a simple linear algebra computation shows that

\left\langle\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h_{m-1}}\right]-\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,{h_{m}}}\right]\right)_{x_{0}}(v),v\right\rangle_{h_{m}}\geq 0.

It follows that

\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m}\right)\leq\overline{\lambda}\left(K_{m-1}\right)

as before, and hence
\left|\Lambda_{\omega}F^{h_{m}}\right|_{h_{m}}\leq C
is uniformly bounded.

Also note that, since
\text{Tr}\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,{h_{m}}}\right]=0,
we have

\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}\right)=\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda F^{h_{m}}\right).

Let
\varphi_{m}=\log\frac{\det h_{m}}{\det h_{0}}
as before. Then, we have

\Delta\varphi_{m}=\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F^{h_{0}}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F^{h_{m}}\right)=\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}\right)

and

\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{m}}\right)=\text{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F^{h_{m}}\right)=\text{Tr}\left(h_{m-1}h_{m}^{-1}\right)>0.

Therefore, the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
yields the uniform
C^{0}
estimate of
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
. It follows that
\left\{\left|\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,{h_{m}}}\right]\right|_{h_{m}}\right\}
is uniformly bounded.

The higher order estimate and the convergence of
\left\{h_{m}\right\}
follow from exactly the same proof as that of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

5.
Appendix: The Heat Flow Approach

5.1.
The heat flow proof of Theorem
1.1

In this subsection, we give a new proof of Theorem
1.1
by using the heat flow method. We fix
\lambda<\lambda_{\min}
, an arbitrary initial Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
, and another Hermitian metric
\xi
on
E
. We seek a Hermitian metric
h
, which is the solution to the equation

(5.1)

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h}=\lambda I_{E}+\xi h^{-1},

by considering the following heat flow:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial t}h_{t}^{-1}=-\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-\lambda I_{E}-\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right),\\
h(0)=h_{0}.\end{cases}

Theorem 5.1
.

The heat flow (
5.2
) exists for all time
0\leq t<\infty
and, as
t\to\infty
, converges to a unique solution of equation (
5.1
) smoothly.

Proof.

Note that equation (
5.2
) is equivalent to
\dot{h}_{t}=-\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}h_{t}+\lambda h_{t}+\xi
. If we linearize this equation, then we get a strictly parabolic equation, hence the short time existence follows.

Now we need to slightly perturb the modified Donaldson functional to adapt to the current situation. We let

{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi,\lambda}\left(h_{0},h_{1}\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\dot{h}_{t}h_{t}^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-\lambda I_{E}-\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)\right)dV\right)dt

and set
K_{t}=\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-\lambda I_{E}-\xi h_{t}^{-1}
. Then, it follows from a direct computation that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{M}}_{\xi,\lambda}\left(h_{0},h_{t}\right)&=-\int_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial t}h_{t}^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-\lambda I_{E}-\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)\right)dV\\
&=-\int_{X}\|K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}^{2}dV\leq 0.\end{split}

Namely, the new modified Donaldson functional is decreasing along the flow.

\bullet

The
C^{0}
estimate.

We fix a Hermitian metric
\hat{h}
on
E
such that
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}-\lambda I_{E}>0
and let
H_{t}=h_{t}\hat{h}^{-1}
. Then, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\text{Tr}H_{t}\right)\geq\text{Tr}\left(H_{t}\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(H_{t}\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}\right)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\text{Tr}H_{t}\right)=-\text{Tr}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}-\lambda I_{E}\right)H_{t}\right)+\text{Tr}\left(\xi\hat{h}^{-1}\right).

It follows that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta\right)\text{Tr}H_{t}\leq\text{Tr}\left(\xi\hat{h}^{-1}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(H_{t}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}-\lambda I_{E}\right)\right).

Note that both
H_{t}
and
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}-\lambda I_{E}
are positive. We pick

C_{1}=\sup_{X}\text{Tr}\left(\xi\hat{h}^{-1}\right)>0\quad\rm{and}\quad C_{2}=\inf_{X}\lambda_{min}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}-\lambda I_{E}\right)>0,

where
\lambda_{min}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}-\lambda I_{E}\right)
is the smallest eigenvalue of
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}-\lambda I_{E}
. Then, (
5.4
) implies that

\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta\right)\text{Tr}H_{t}\leq C_{1}-C_{2}\ \!\text{Tr}H_{t}.

Hence, by the maximum principle, we conclude that
\text{Tr}H_{t}
is bounded from above uniformly.

Next, we let
\widetilde{H}_{t}=\hat{h}h_{t}^{-1}
. By using a similar argument as above, we obtain that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta\right)\text{Tr}\widetilde{H}_{t}\leq\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{H}_{t}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}-\lambda I_{E}\right)\right)-\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{H}_{t}\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right).

Since
\widetilde{H}_{t}
is positive, if we let
C_{3}=\sup_{X}\lambda_{max}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\hat{h}}-\lambda I_{E}\right)>0
and pick
C_{4}>0
such that
\xi\geq C_{4}\hat{h}
, then (
5.5
) implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta\right)\text{Tr}\widetilde{H}_{t}&\leq C_{3}\text{Tr}\widetilde{H}_{t}-C_{4}\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{H}_{t}^{2}\right)\\
&\leq C_{3}\text{Tr}\widetilde{H}_{t}-\frac{C_{4}}{r}\left(\text{Tr}\widetilde{H}_{t}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Again, by the maximum principle, we see that
\text{Tr}\widetilde{H}_{t}
is uniformly bounded. These imply that
h_{t}
is uniformly equivalent to
\hat{h}
which in turn implies that there exists a constant
C_{5}>0
such that

(5.6)

\displaystyle C_{5}^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{t}\leq C_{5}h_{0}.

\bullet

The higher order estimates.

Note that, since
K_{t}
is
h_{t}
-self adjoint,
\|K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}^{2}=\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)
. A simple computation shows that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)}{dt}-\Delta\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)=-2\text{Tr}\left(\xi h_{t}^{-1}K_{t}^{2}\right)-2\|\nabla^{h_{t}}K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}^{2}\leq 0.

It follows from the maximum principle that
\sup_{X}\|K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}
is decreasing in
t
. In particular,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{X}\|K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}\leq\sup_{X}\|K_{0}\|_{h_{0}}.

Since

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}=K_{t}+\lambda I_{E}+\xi h_{t}^{-1}

the above estimates imply that the right hand side of this identity is uniformly bounded.
By multiplying
h_{t}
to both sides of the above identity and using the
C^{0}
estimate, we get the uniform
W^{2,p}
bound for any
p>0
. Thus, the uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate follows from the Sobolev embedding as we take
p
sufficiently large.
Moreover, the
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates of
h_{t}
follow from the standard Schauder theory.

Finally, if we differentiate (
5.2
) with respect to
t
repeatedly and by using the above uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates, then we get uniform estimates of
h_{t}
with respect to
t
. This leads to the long time existence of the flow (
5.2
).

\bullet
The exponential convergence.

By (
5.7
), we have

\frac{d\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)}{dt}-\Delta\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)\leq-2\text{Tr}\left(\xi h_{t}^{-1}K_{t}^{2}\right).

Let
C_{6}=\inf_{X}\lambda_{min}\left(\xi h_{0}^{-1}\right)>0
. Then, (
5.6
) implies that

\xi h_{t}^{-1}\geq\frac{C_{6}}{C_{5}}I_{E}.

It then follows that

\frac{d\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)}{dt}-\Delta\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)\leq-\frac{2C_{6}}{C_{5}}\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right).

Namely,

\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta\right)\|K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}^{2}\leq-\delta\|K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}^{2}

where
\delta=\frac{2C_{6}}{C_{5}}
. Thus, the maximum principle implies

\sup_{X}\|K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}^{2}\leq e^{-\delta t}\sup_{X}\|K_{0}\|_{h_{0}}^{2}:=Me^{-\delta t}.

On the other hand, we have

\left\|\frac{dh_{t}}{dt}\right\|_{h_{t}}^{2}=\text{Tr}\left(\frac{dh_{t}}{dt}h_{t}^{-1}\right)^{2}=\|K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}^{2},

which implies that, for
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}
,

d\left(h_{t_{1}},h_{t_{2}}\right)\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\|\frac{dh_{t}}{dt}\right\|_{h_{t}}dt\leq\frac{2\sqrt{M}}{\delta}\left(e^{-\frac{\delta t_{1}}{2}}-e^{-\frac{\delta t_{2}}{2}}\right).

This implies that
h_{t}\to h_{\infty}
exponentially. Therefore, by the uniform
C^{0}
and
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates, we know that
h_{\infty}
is nondegenerate and the convergence is smooth. Furthermore,

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{\infty}}-\lambda I_{E}-\xi h_{\infty}^{-1}=K_{\infty}=\lim_{t\to\infty}K_{t}=0.

Namely,
\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{\infty}}=\lambda I_{E}+\xi h_{\infty}^{-1}
.

∎

5.2.
The Higgs Bundle Case

We now generalize Theorem
1.1
by allowing Higgs fields. In fact, our proof of Theorem
5.1
can be adapted to this case.

Let
(E,\Phi)
be a Higgs bundle. Define

\lambda_{\min}^{\Phi}=\inf_{\mathcal{F}}\mu\left(E/\mathcal{F}\right),

where the infimum is taken over all proper
\Phi
-invariant saturated torsion-free subsheaves of
E
.

Theorem 5.2
.

We have

\lambda_{\min}^{\Phi}=\sup_{\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h}\right]\right)>\lambda I_{E}}\lambda

where the supremum is taken over all Hermitian metrics on
E
.

Furthermore, for any
\lambda<\lambda_{\min}^{\Phi}
and Hermitian metric
\xi
on
E
, the flow

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial t}h_{t}^{-1}=-\left(\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h_{t}}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h_{t}}\right]\right)-\lambda I_{E}-\xi h_{t}^{-1}\right)\\
h(0)=h_{0}.\end{cases}

exists for all time and converges smoothly to the unique solution of

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{h}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h}\right]\right)=\lambda I_{E}+\xi h^{-1}.

Sketch of Proof.

Similar to Theorem
5.1
, we only need to show the convergence of flow (
5.9
). We first note that, for any Hermitian metrics
\zeta,\eta
on
E
, one has the inequality

\text{Tr}\left(\zeta\eta^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,\zeta}\right]-\Lambda_{\omega}\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,\eta}\right]\right)\right)\geq 0.

For any Hermitian metric
h
, we let
F^{h}:=R^{h}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h}\right]-\lambda I_{E}
. Again, we fix
\hat{h}
on
E
such that

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(R^{\hat{h}}+\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,\hat{h}}\right]\right)-\lambda I_{E}>0.

Similarly, we let
H_{t}=h_{t}\hat{h}^{-1}
, where
h_{t}
is the solution to equation (
5.9
), then, by inequality (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta\left(\text{Tr}H_{t}\right)\geq&\text{Tr}\left(H_{t}\Lambda_{\omega}F^{\hat{h}}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(H_{t}\Lambda_{\omega}F^{h_{t}}\right)+\text{Tr}\left(H_{t}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h_{t}}\right]-\Lambda_{\omega}\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,\hat{h}}\right]\right)\right)\\
\geq&\text{Tr}\left(H_{t}\Lambda_{\omega}F^{\hat{h}}\right)-\text{Tr}\left(H_{t}\Lambda_{\omega}F^{h_{t}}\right).\end{split}

The essentially same argument as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
shows that
\text{Tr}H_{t}
is uniformly bounded. Similar arguments also show that
\text{Tr}\left(\hat{h}h_{t}^{-1}\right)
is uniformly bounded and hence we obtain the uniform
C^{0}
estimate.

To derive the higher order estimates, we let
K_{t}=\Lambda_{\omega}F^{h_{t}}-\xi h_{t}^{-1}
and notice that, parallel to formula (
5.7
), we have

\frac{d\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)}{dt}-\Delta\text{Tr}\left(K_{t}^{2}\right)=-2\text{Tr}\left(\xi h_{t}^{-1}K_{t}^{2}\right)-2\|\nabla^{h_{t}}K_{t}\|_{h_{t}}^{2}-2\|\left[\Phi,K_{t}\right]\|_{h_{t}}^{2}\leq 0.

Also, we know that

\Lambda_{\omega}R^{h_{t}}=K_{t}-\left[\Phi,\Phi^{*,h_{t}}\right]+\lambda I_{E}+\xi h_{t}^{-1}.

By using the
C^{0}
estimate, it follows that the right side of the above is uniformly bounded.
The rest of the proof is essentially the same as that of Theorem
5.1
.

∎
