1.
Introduction

There are two main mechanisms for producing stable limit laws and anomalous diffusion in
physical systems, heavy-tailed observables or slow mixing (strong dependence) of the system. The first effect, heavy
tails, is sometimes called the Noah effect
[
29
, Section 4.5]
while slow mixing is sometimes
called the Joseph effect
[
29
, Section 4.6]
. The terminology is due to Mandelbrot
[
23
]

and motivated by the deluge-like aspect of a heavy-tailed observable and the predictability of a slowly mixing system.
Whitt writes “the most complicated case involves both heavy tails and dependence. Unfortunately there is not
a well-developed theory for stochastic process limits in this case”
[
29
, Section 4.7]
. In this paper we
investigate the interplay of both effects in polynomially mixing billiard systems with cusps and intermittent-type maps.

On these canonical models
of anomalous diffusion, regular (Hölder or Lipschitz) observables satisfy stable laws due to the strong dependence.
We consider a heavy-tailed observable

\phi(x)=d(x,x_{0})^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}
,
0<\alpha<2
, on a polynomially
mixing billiard
(T,\widetilde{Q},\mu)
as described
in
[
31
,
20
]
. Since the invariant measure
\mu
is
equivalent to Lebesgue and the phase space is two-dimensional, the
observable
\phi
has a tail distribution of index
\alpha
, i.e.

\mu(\phi>t)\sim t^{-\alpha}
. The billiard systems we consider have a
sufficiently slow mixing rate that Hölder observables satisfy a stable
law of index
1/\gamma
. Billiards with this property have been studied in
a series of recent papers
[
31
,
20
,
25
,
19
]
. We
focus in this work on stable laws, rather than convergence in the
J_{1}
or

M_{1}
topologies.

Theorems
4.1
and
4.3
establish, with some caveats, a transition in terms of
\alpha
and
\gamma
in that the stable law index arises from the heavy-tailed observable if
\frac{1}{\alpha}>\gamma
and from the
slow mixing mechanism if
\gamma>\frac{1}{\alpha}
. A main contribution is
Theorem
11.2
and its Corollary
11.4

which show that in an exponentially mixing system Birkhoff sums of a general observable with tail index
\alpha

converge to zero in distribution if scaled by a factor
n^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

The scheme of proof we use is similar to that
of
[
7
]
. We have kept the notation from
[
7
]
as much as
possible to allow comparison with the results and ideas of that paper. We
induce on a set
M
such that the cusps lie in the complement of
M
.
We assume
x_{0}\in M
, so that in particular
x_{0}
is not a cusp
point. The set
M
is such that the first return map

can be
modeled as a Young Tower with exponential tails. This means that there is a
subset

such that the return map to
M_{0}
under

is
uniformly hyperbolic with singularities and exponentially mixing for
Hölder observables. By contrast, the return time function
R
to
M
has
polynomial tails. This
set up is described in Zhang’s paper
[
31
]
, which gives a class
of billiard systems (a form of Machta billiard
[
22
]
) with
arbitrarily slow mixing rates. The initial work on analyzing mixing rates
of Machta type billiards was carried out in
[
8
]
, where the rate
of roughly
O(1/n)
was established. This work follows that
of
[
7
]
in which a similar investigation was carried out for an

[

. In the

The key approach we take is to decompose

If the point

5.
Lifting an induced system by the Continuous Mapping

If our induced system converges in the

Suppose the continuous time version of our induced system

converges in Skorokhod’s

By the ergodic theorem

We define the following sequence of random variables on

(

)

(

) topology.

converges in distribution to a stable law of index
\alpha

[

From work of Jung et al.

Remark 8.1

The Young Tower analyzed for the intermittent map in

our results are limited to

10.
Discussion and open problems.

We were unable to determine the stable limit law of a heavy tailed observable maximized at
the cusp of a Machta billiard table. However, preliminary work suggests that the stable index
will not be equal to
(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\gamma)^{-1}
. Similarly, calculations on a class
of polynomially intermittent maps which preserve Lebesgue measure, extensively investigated in

suggest that the addition formula
(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\gamma)^{-1}
is not general, but
depends upon how close orbits come to the cusp or indifferent fixed point before returning to the inducing set.
It would be good to have a better understanding of any general principles which are involved.

Theorem

(T,X,\mu)

attraction of a stable law of index
\alpha
,
then
\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}+\varepsilon}}[\sum_{j=0}^{n}\phi\circ T^{j}-c_{n}]\xrightarrow{d}0
(in distribution). Is there a corresponding almost sure convergence result under suitable mixing assumptions? Some related partial results
are given in
[

,
2
]
. The case of iid random variables of stable index
\alpha
is
given in
[

]

since

\mu(|\max_{j\leq n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\overline{\psi}_{n}\circ F^{j}(x)|>\delta n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}+\varepsilon})

\leq\delta^{-2}n^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}-2\varepsilon}\mu[|\max_{j\leq n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\overline{\psi}_{n}\circ F^{j}(x)|^{2}]=O(\delta^{-2}(\log n)^{3}n^{-\alpha\varepsilon})

∎

11.2.
A result of Gouëzel

We use the following result of Gouëzel
[
17
, Theorem
4.6]
:

Proposition 11.5
.

Let
(T,X,\mu)
be an ergodic probability preserving map, let
\alpha(n)
and and
B_{n}
be two sequences of integers which are regularly varying with positive indices. Let
A_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
Y\subset X
be a subset with positive measure. We will denote by
\mu_{Y}(.):=\frac{\mu|_{Y}}{\mu(Y)}
the induced probability measure.
Let
R:Y\to{\mathbb{N}}
be the return time of
T
to
Y
and
F=T^{R}:Y\to Y
be the induced map. Define
\overline{R}=\int_{Y}Rd\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(Y)}
. Consider a measurable function
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
and define
\Phi:Y\to\mathbb{R}
by
\Phi(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{R(y)-1}\phi\circ T^{j}
.
Define
S_{n}(\Phi)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Phi\circ F^{j}
. Assume that

\frac{S_{n}(\Phi)-A_{n}}{B_{n}}

converges in distribution (with respect to
\mu_{Y}
) to a random variable
S
.

Additionally assume that either:

(a)

\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n}R\circ F^{j}-n\overline{R}}{\alpha(n)}
tends
in probability to zero and

\max_{0\leq k\leq\alpha(n)}\frac{|S_{k}(\Phi)|}{B_{n}}
is tight

or

(b)

\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n}R\circ F^{j}-n\overline{R}}{\alpha(n)}
is tight and

\max_{0\leq k\leq\alpha(n)}\frac{|S_{k}(\Phi)|}{B_{n}}
tends in probability
to zero.

Then

\big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi\circ T^{j}-A_{\left\lfloor n\mu(Y)\right\rfloor}\big)/B_{\left\lfloor n\mu(Y)\right\rfloor}

converges in distribution (with respect to
\mu
) to
S
.
