Bioremediation potential of lead and cadmium tolerant bacteria isolated from industrial (tannery) effluents
Authors: Shahin Mahmud, Asma Akter, Md Firoz Ali, Md Rayhan Ali, Roksana Khanam, Fahmida Tabassum, Liton Chandra Das, Ashraf Hossain Talukder, AKM Mohiuddin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHeavy metal pollution from tannery industries presents significant environmental and public health concerns due to the toxicity and persistence of metals such as Pb2+ and Cd2+. This study aimed to isolate and characterize indigenous Pb2+ and Cd2+-tolerant bacteria from tannery effluents and contaminated soils of highly polluted areas in Dhaka for potential bioremediation applications. A total of 72 Pb2+-tolerant and 52 Cd2+-tolerant bacterial isolates were obtained using metal-supplemented LB agar. The minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) recorded were 4000 ppm for Pb2+ and 250 ppm for Cd2+. Quantitative analysis demonstrated removal efficiencies of 93.91% for Pb2+ and 89.66% for Cd2+. All isolates exhibited plant growth-promoting traits, including phosphate solubilization, ammonia production, indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) production, and cellulase activity. Most isolates were antibiotic-sensitive, though some showed multidrug resistance, emphasizing the need for biosafety evaluation. The most promising isolates were partially identified as Enterobacter spp. and K. pneumoniae. Protein expression profiling by SDS-PAGE revealed metal-responsive proteins ranging from 25 to 75 kDa under selective Pb2+ and Cd2+ stress. Genomic and proteomic analyses further indicated the involvement of efflux pump-associated genes in metal resistance, where cusR was identified as a common resistance gene among the dominant strains. Overall, these findings suggest that the indigenous K. pneumoniae possesses strong potential for Pb2+ and Cd2+ removal, along with plant growth-promoting capabilities, making them promising candidate for bioremediation and phytoremediation strategies.Keywords: Tannery wastes, Lead, Cadmium, Antibiogram, Bioremediation, Plant-growth-promoting property, In-silico analysis

Introduction

Heavy metal pollution is considered the prevalent form of environmental pollution.1 It is a significant global environmental and public health concern due to its toxic, carcinogenic, mutagenic, and teratogenic effects, as well as its persistence and non-biodegradable nature in aquatic ecosystems.2 Rapid industrialization and urbanization have substantially increased the release of heavy metals into soil and water, mainly from activities such as mining, electroplating, battery production, metallurgy, and tannery operations. Tanneries of Bangladesh are considered the most polluting sectors with the greatest environmental impact.3 Lead (Pb2+) and cadmium (Cd2+) are particularly hazardous due to their toxicity at low concentrations and their ability to bioaccumulate and biomagnify within food chains.4, 5 As heavy metals cannot be degraded by microorganisms easily and persist in the environment, it responsible for causing long-term toxic effects.5

Nowadays, many traditional physicochemical remediation methods such as chemical precipitation, ion exchange, membrane filtration, reverse osmosis, and electrochemical treatments have been employed to treat heavy metal-contaminated wastewater.6, 7 However, these approaches often involve high operational costs, significant energy consumption, and the production of secondary pollutants, such as sludge, which require additional disposal measures. In contrast, bioremediation has appeared as an eco-friendly and cost-effective. This approach leverages the natural metabolic capabilities of microorganisms to immobilize, transform, or remove toxic metals from contaminated environments. Metal-resistant bacteria isolated from polluted sites have evolved diverse adaptive mechanisms to tolerate heavy metal toxicity.8 These mechanisms involve biosorption through cell wall components like peptidoglycan and lipopolysaccharides, intracellular bioaccumulation, enzymatic reduction of metal ions, efflux pump activity, and complexation with metal-binding proteins or metabolites.9

Pb2+ and Cd2+ are designated priority pollutants by environmental and health agencies due to their severe health impacts. Pb2+ exposure disrupts neurological development, particularly in children, and is associated with behavioral disorders, kidney damage, and cardiovascular diseases. On the other hand, Cd2+ is marked as a prevalent carcinogen by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) and is linked to pulmonary, renal, and skeletal disorders.10, 11 At the cellular level, these metals disrupt physiological processes by displacing essential metal ions, inhibiting enzyme activity, and generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) that damage nucleic acids.12 Isolation of naturally occurring metal-resistant bacterial genera such as Pseudomonas, Citrobacter, Bacillus, Enterobacter, and Klebsiella from industrially contaminated environments has shown considerable potential for bioremediation applications.13 These bacteria demonstrate high tolerance and removal efficiency for Pb2+ and Cd2+, and can be utilized in bioreactors for the remediation of contaminated water and soil. To enhance microbial remediation efficiency, it is crucial to clarify the mechanisms and cellular pathways engaged in metal sequestration. Additionally, some of these bacteria can exhibit plant growth-promoting properties (PGP). It has been reported that the combination of PGP bacteria and hazardous metal-degrading microbes enhances bioremediation efficiency.14

There are two primary categories of bacterial resistance processes to heavy metals: molecular and biochemical. The molecular mechanisms are the genetic determinants of resistance- these are expressed by genes expressed on bacterial chromosomes or plasmids. A major participant in the bioremediation of certain heavy metals is K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp..15, 16 A systemic strategy can be helpful for the coordinated investigation of bacteria's numerous genes and associated pathways, adopting network biology. Network investigations are very helpful in examining important genes and proteins as well as biological pathways and functions.17 Furthermore, network analysis has become a powerful tool for identifying interaction patterns among microbial taxa, providing new insights into the relationships and connections within bacterial communities.18

Therefore, such bacteria hold potential not only for detoxifying contaminated environments but also for supporting plant development in polluted soils, contributing to sustainable agriculture. Moreover, the antibiotic resistance profile of these isolates is an important consideration for their safe environmental application.19 As heavy metal and antibiotic resistance often co-exist, possibly due to co-selection, it is essential to assess the susceptibility of the isolates to commonly used antibiotics to minimize biosafety risks.

Although several studies have explored metal-tolerant bacteria from industrial effluents,8, 13 and plant growth-promoting bacteria for phytoremediation,20, 21 most investigations have focused on either bioremediation or plant growth promotion as separate objectives, but did not integrate in-silico gene network analysis or network biology characterization.22, 23 In contrast, the present study simultaneously evaluates dual Pb2+ and Cd2+ tolerance, quantitative biosorption, multiple PGP traits, antibiotic susceptibility profiling, SDS-PAGE proteomic analysis, and in silico network analysis within a single integrated framework.

The present study aimed to isolate Pb2+ and Cd2+-tolerant bacteria from tannery waste, characterize them following previous studies,24, 25 and evaluate their potential for dual applications in bioremediation and biofertilization. Our study differs from the previous studies by isolating bacteria primarily from mixed tannery sludge-effluent microhabitats, simultaneously assessing dual Pb2+ and Cd2+-tolerance, and integrating biosorption, plant growth-promoting traits, SDS-PAGE proteomic analysis, and in-silico exploration of metal resistance genes and protein–protein interaction network analysis within a single framework. The study also evaluated their antibiotic susceptibility patterns against various commercially available antibiotics.26, 27, 28 These findings demonstrate that heavy metal-contaminated sites can be remediated safely and effectively, provided there is a stronger focus on managing industrial waste and effluent to protect both the environment and public health.29

Methods and materials

Time and place of the study

The study was conducted from September 2024 to November 2025 at the Department of Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering at Mawlana Bhashani Science and Technology University (MBSTU) in Tangail, Bangladesh.

Sample collection and processing

A total of nine tannery waste samples (effluent and soil) were collected from contaminated sites in Dhaka, Bangladesh. Detailed information about sampling (sample type, sampling location, and condition of sample collection) is summarized in Supplementary Table S1. Field observations and consultations with tannery personnel helped to identify areas with the highest heavy-metal deposition, and samples were stored in sterile falcon tubes. After collection, the samples were processed immediately.

Screening of Pb2+ and Cd2+−tolerant bacteria

According to the standard method, serial dilution (up to 10−1–10−7) of the samples was performed,30 and spread (100 μL) on Luria Bertani (LB) agar media (Hi-Media, India) containing 2000 ppm Pb2+ (as lead nitrate) (Himedia Laboratories Pvt. Ltd., India) for screening of Pb2+ and 100 ppm Cd2+ (as cadmium chloride) (Research Lab, India) for screening of Cd2+ tolerance. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 24 to 48 h31, 32; subsequently, colonies that grew on the metal-supplemented media were identified as metal-tolerant and stored at −20 °C for further analysis.

Determination of minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC)

The MIC assays were performed under controlled conditions with soluble metal salts in sterile media, including uninoculated controls to account for abiotic effects. The MIC of each bacterial isolate was determined on LB agar plates supplemented with Pb2+ and Cd2+ at different concentrations of Pb2+ (2000–4000 ppm) and Cd2+ (100–300 ppm) by following the established method.33 The MIC was defined as the highest metal concentration that completely inhibited the growth of the tested organisms.34

Quantitative assessment of Pb2+ and Cd2+ uptake by bacteria following dithizone method

Stock solutions of Pb2+ and Cd2+ at 1000 ppm were initially prepared and subsequently diluted with LB broth to obtain varying concentrations of Pb2+ (10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 ppm) and Cd2+ (5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 ppm). To facilitate the colorimetric detection of residual metal ions, a 0.01% dithizone solution was prepared with acetone, while a 0.5 M acetate buffer (pH 5.0–5.5) was used to enhance metal-dithizone complex formation.35 After overnight inoculation of the bacteria into metal-containing LB broth under a shaking incubator, the cultures were centrifuged at 15,000 rpm for 12 min, and the supernatant was collected.36 For spectrophotometric analysis, the mixture was gently agitated to promote the formation of colored metal-dithizone complexes- red for Pb2+ and reddish-brown for Cd2+.37, 38 After phase separation, the organic layer was transferred to a cuvette, and absorbance was measured at 500 nm for Cd2+,32 and 510 nm for Pb2+38 using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (HACH, DR 3900, USA), where metal ions containing fresh LB broth was used as a negative control. Short biochemical analysis of the potential isolates

Biochemical characterization was performed according to the standard method.31 A series of tests, including Kligler's Iron Agar (KIA), Motility Indole Urea (MIU), Simmon's Citrate, Oxidase, Catalase, and Gram staining were performed for cell wall differentiation. All experiments were performed by following established guidelines.40

Antibiotic susceptibility test

The antibiotic sensitivity test of the isolates was accomplished by following the Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion method.41, 42, 43 In this study, a total of nine (09) commercially available antibiotic discs (Bio-Maxima, Poland) named- streptomycin (10 μg), amoxicillin (30 μg), amikacin (30 μg), amoxiclav (30 μg), ceftazidime (30 μg), tetracycline (30 μg), chloramphenicol (30 μg), levofloxacin (5 μg), and kanamycin (30 μg) were aseptically placed on the surface of Mueller-Hinton agar media (Hi-Media, India) containing bacterial isolates, and plates were incubated for overnight at 37 °C. The results were interpreted following Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) guidelines, and isolates were classified as susceptible, intermediate, or resistant.44

Tolerance to other heavy metals

To evaluate cross-tolerance capabilities, Pb2+-tolerant isolates were screened on LB agar plates containing 100 ppm Cd2+. Similarly, potential Cd2+-tolerant isolates were tested on LB agar supplemented with 2000 ppm Pb2+. Moreover, both Pb2+ and Cd2+-tolerant isolates were screened on LB agar media supplemented with 500 ppm chromium (K₂Cr₂O₇) (Merck, Germany). After incubating the plates at 37 °C for 24–48 h, the bacterial growth was observed. This step evaluated the isolates' tolerance to multiple heavy metals, reflecting their potential for remediation of environments contaminated with more than one type of heavy metal.45

Analysis of plant growth-promoting (PGP) properties of the test isolates

Qualitative assay of phosphate solubilization

Inorganic phosphate solubilization was assessed qualitatively by spot-inoculating bacteria on Pikovskaya's agar (Himedia Laboratories Pvt. Ltd., India) containing insoluble tricalcium phosphate as the sole phosphorus source.46 Fresh bacterial cultures grown in LB broth were spot-inoculated onto the surface of the solidified Pikovskaya's agar plates using sterile inoculating loops. The development of clear halo zones around the colonies was considered a positive for phosphate solubilization.47

Cellulose degradation assay

The cellulolytic activity of the isolates was performed by following CRA method.48 Bacterial isolates were spot-inoculated onto the Congo Red Agar (CRA) medium (Hi-Media, India), and incubated at 30 °C for 48 h. The isolates showing discoloration on CRA media were considered positive for cellulose degradation.33

Ammonia production assay

Ammonia production by bacterial isolates was evaluated using peptone water (Himedia Laboratories Pvt. Ltd., India) as the nitrogen source and Nessler's reagent (Himedia Laboratories Pvt. Ltd., India) for ammonia detection, following the method of Cappuccino and Sherman.49 Isolates were inoculated into sterile peptone water and incubated at 30 °C for 72 h. After incubation, 1 mL of freshly prepared Nessler's reagent was added to each tube. A color change from yellow to brown indicated a positive result for ammonia production.50

Quantitative assay of indole acetic acid production

Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) production by the bacterial strains was measured using the Salkowski colorimetric assay.51 Bacterial cultures were grown in LB broth at 35 °C for 48 h, and then centrifuged at 10,000 rotations per minute (rpm) for 10 min to obtain cell-free supernatants. Each 1 mL supernatant was mixed with 2 mL of freshly prepared Salkowski reagent (0.5 M FeCl₃ in 70% perchloric acid) and incubated in the dark at room temperature for 25 min. The development of a pink to reddish color indicated IAA presence, and absorbance was measured at 530 nm using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (HACH, DR 3900, USA). IAA concentration was standardized using a standard curve prepared from known IAA concentrations.52

Molecular characterization

Molecular identification technique was performed of the most promising isolates to confirm their taxonomic classification. Genomic DNA was extracted from selected isolates using the standard heat-shock method.53 The 16S rRNA gene, a widely used conserved genetic marker for bacterial identification, was amplified using universal primers following standard PCR protocols. The amplicons were verified by agarose gel (1%) electrophoresis and subsequently sequenced using the Sanger method. Finally, the resulting sequences were aligned with the NCBI GenBank database using BLASTn to determine genus- and species-level taxonomic identities.54

SDS-PAGE analysis

Subcellular localization of metal-binding proteins was performed according to previous studies with some minor modifications.45, 55 Bacterial cultures were grown in LB broth containing 20 ppm Pb2+ and 10 ppm Cd2+. After incubation at 37 °C, 150 rpm for 24 h, 1 mL of each culture was centrifuged at 9000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The pellets were washed twice with 10 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.2), resuspended in 500 μL of the same buffer, and lysed by ultrasonication (TOMY SEIKO UR-21P Ultrasonic Generator, Japan) under cold conditions. The lysates were centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 40 min at 4 °C to separate cytoplasmic (supernatant) and membrane-bound (pellet) protein fractions.55 Only cytoplasmic proteins (supernatant) were subjected to SDS-PAGE using 10% polyacrylamide gels, following Laemmli's method with slight modifications.56 Electrophoresis was carried out at 125 V for 2 h at room temperature, and the gels were stained with Coomassie Brilliant Blue.57

Identification and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction

Following biochemical and molecular characterization, 141 heavy metal-tolerant genes from Enterobacter spp. and 109 from K. pneumoniae were obtained from a literature study (Supplementary Table S11). To explore the interaction of these genes, we submitted the genes to the STRING (http://string.embl.de/) database. STRING incorporates established classification frameworks, including Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) for enrichment study, but it offers new classification methods based on high-throughput text-mining and hierarchical clustering of the association network. The resulting interaction data were downloaded in a tab-delimited format.58

Network visualization and analysis

The PPI networks were subsequently incorporated and viewed with Cytoscape software (version 3.10.0). Cytoscape is an accessible program that incorporates biological molecules interaction networks, high-throughput gene expression data, and other molecular states into an interconnected framework of concepts.59 The cytoHubb plugin was used to determine topological properties, including degree, betweenness, and proximity. Hub genes were discovered via higher degrees of those factors. The MCODE plugin was used to determine functional clusters or sections in each network. Venny 2.1.0 was used (https://www.biotools.fr/misc/venny) to build a Venn diagram, providing us to recognize common heavy metal-tolerant genes among the bacteria. These genes have been discovered to be connected to metal resistance functions like efflux, reduction, sequestration, and transport.

GO enrichment analysis

The STRING database was implemented for obtaining GO terms. GO is an established information repository that provides controlled language for explaining gene products in three main groups, including biological process, molecular function, and cellular component.60 It has become an established tool for gene research. Discovering significant GO keywords among a particular list of genes might help us to understand the functional significance of these genes.61

KEGG pathway two-component system analysis

To acquire a better understanding of the role of discovered heavy metal tolerance genes, a two-component KEGG pathway was used. The two-component system (TCS) is one of the most widely utilized signal transduction pathways in bacteria that identify and react to external challenges. The TCS normally consists of two protein components, including a HK, which auto-phosphorylates in response to environmental triggers, and an RR, which gets the phosphate group from the HK and regulates the expression of target genes.62

Phylogenetic tree construction

The resulting 16S rRNA gene sequences were aligned with publicly available sequences in the NCBI GenBank database using the BLAST algorithm to determine the taxonomic identities of the isolates at the genus and species levels. Multiple sequence alignment was performed using ClustalW in MEGA version 11. A phylogenetic tree was constructed based on the neighbor-joining method,63 and the robustness of the tree was evaluated through bootstrap analysis with 1000 replicates.57

Results

Screening and isolation of Pb2+ and Cd2+ − tolerant bacteria

In the current study, a total of 72 morphologically distinct colonies were isolated from Pb2+ containing plates, and 52 were obtained from Cd2+ containing plates based on differences in size, shape, color, and transparency, which details summarized in Supplementary Table S2 and S3. Representative of some pure colonies of bacterial isolates are shown in Supplementary Fig. S4.

Minimum inhibitory concentration determination of the isolates

The MIC of heavy metals (Pb2+ and Cd2+) for the bacterial isolates was determined by checking the growth on LB agar plates supplemented with increasing concentrations of Pb2+ (2000–4000 ppm) and Cd2+ (100–300 ppm). Among 72 Pb2+ − tolerant isolates, four isolates (Bpb6, Bpb7, Cpb2, and Hpb3) exhibited up to 3750 ppm Pb2+ − tolerant potency (Supplementary Fig. S5), and 4000 ppm was recorded as MIC for the isolates. On the other hand, among 52 Cd2+ − tolerant isolates, only 3 isolates (Gcd3, Gcd4, and Ccd4) showed up to 225 ppm Cd2+ − tolerant potency (Supplementary Fig. S6), and 250 ppm was recorded as MIC. The MIC values are summarized in Supplementary Table S4 and S5.

Quantitative analysis of Pb2+ and Cd2+uptake by bacterial isolates

Among the four most potential Pb2+-tolerant isolates tested, only one (Bpb7) that partially identified as K. pneumoniae, was consistently exhibited the highest Pb2+ removal efficiency across all the concentrations (Supplementary Table S6), notably it was exhibited>50% removal efficiency even at 50 ppm (Fig. 1). In the case of Cd2+, one isolate (Gcd4) that also was partially identified as K. pneumoniae displayed superior performance among the three isolates tested (Supplementary Table S7), and it was consistently achieved the highest Cd2+ removal efficiencies across all the concentrations (Fig. 2).

Molecular characterization

The molecular identification of the five most potent selected bacterial isolates was performed through PCR amplification and 16S rRNA gene sequencing. DNA concentration and purity were initially assessed using UV–Vis spectrophotometry. The A260/A280 ratios of all the samples were ranged from 1.8 to 2.0 (Supplementary Table S9). Successful amplification of the 16S rRNA gene was confirmed by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis, showing clear bands under a gel documentation system (Supplementary Fig. S15). Following the partial nucleotide sequencing results, Bpb6 and Gcd3 were identified as Enterobacter mori and Enterobacter cloacae (Enterobacter-grouped bacteria), and Cpb2, Bpb7, and Gcd4 were identified as K. pneumoniae (Supplementary Table S10).

SDS-PAGE analysis

The five sequenced isolates were subjected to SDS-PAGE to observe cytoplasmic proteins. Protein bands were found in the range of 25 to 75 kDa, and can be hypothesized that the metal (Pb2+ and Cd2+) responsive proteins were cytoplasmic (Supplementary Fig. S16).

PPI interaction network analysis

A protein-protein interaction was used to identify critical proteins and functional components that may be altered by these heavy metal tolerance genes. The STRING constructed a PPI network analysis, which indicated certain common gene clusters and hub proteins in both K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp. as shown in Fig. 3. The significant interconnectedness of nodes demonstrates that Enterobacter has a complicated regulatory framework encompassing a variety of stressful environmental responses. In K. pneumoniae, nikR, copA, cusC, cusF, arsR, and AEW59910.1 have been identified as common hubs using different network analysis approaches. In contrast, for Enterobacter spp., iscU, copA, and iscA were shown in Fig. 4, demonstrating their central function in PPI networks associated with heavy metal tolerance. We also discovered important relationships between the top one genes, including arsR (Between, Degree), nikR (Closeness) from K. pneumoniae and copA (Degree, Between, Clossness) from Enterobacter strains, which safeguard bacteria from toxic metal ions by regulating intake, transportation, removal, and efflux mechanisms (Fig. 5).64

Functional GO enrichment analysis

K. pneumoniae GO enrichment investigation revealed that the most significant biological functions were cation and metal ion transport, ion transmembrane transport, and reactions to inorganic and metal-containing compounds, including copper and arsenic stress (Fig. 6 and Supplementary Table S12). The molecular function assessment demonstrated enrichment in metal ion transmembrane transporter activity, including ATPase-coupled and ABC-type transporters, in addition to nickel and magnesium ion binding activities, illustrated in Supplementary Table S13. Overall, these findings demonstrate that K. pneumoniae relies on metal ion transport and efflux systems as important ways to adapt to heavy metal tolerance and detoxification. The GO enrichment analysis (molecular, biological, and cellular functions) of Enterobacter spp. indicated that the Enterobacter spp. implements a coordinated network of metal transporters, metal-binding proteins, Fe—S cluster complexes, and stress-responsive enzymes that promote metal detoxification, homeostasis, and cellular adaptation under heavy metal stress conditions (Supplementary Table S14-S16).

KGGG two-component system

Many heavy metal-tolerant genes, including zraP, cusA, cusB, cusC, cusF, and cusR, have been reported in K. pneumoniae (Table 5). These genes predominantly communicate with the CusSR and ZraSR two-component systems. These genes exhibit significant effects on copper/silver efflux activities, demonstrating that K. pneumoniae regulate metal ion homeostasis and resist off poisoning through efflux-mediated detoxification via metal-sensing regulatory processes. In addition, the genes found to be ttrA, ttrB, ttrC, ttrR, and ttrS were linked to tetrathionate respiration in Enterobacter spp. (Table 5), revealing a responsiveness to altered electron acceptor exposure under extreme circumstances. Copper/silver efflux has been demonstrated to be facilitated by cusS and cusR, while ompR functioned as a phosphate regulon response regulator connected to stressful environmental adaptation. These genes collectively demonstrate that K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp. both are employed several two-component regulatory systems, particularly CusSR, ZraSR, and TtrSR, to identify and react to heavy metals and stressful situations, thereby improving the likelihood of surviving in contaminated or difficult environments.

Discussion

Expanding knowledge of heavy metal-tolerant bacteria is crucial for developing effective bioremediation strategies and enabling their successful application in contaminated environments. Furthermore, such organisms could be viable sources for biofertilization and genetic enhancement in environmental biotechnology.8 In the current study, the MIC assays revealed that the isolates Bpb6, Bpb7, Cpb2, and Hpb3 demonstrated notable Pb2+ tolerance up to 3750 ppm, whereas Gcd3, Gcd4, and Ccd4 exhibited Cd2+ tolerance up to 225 ppm. The metal removal efficiency is affected by the dithizone-based biosorption assay by measuring the disappearance of free metal ions that are removed by bacteria from solution, likely via biosorption or extracellular interactions, not necessarily true intracellular uptake by bacteria. Among them, K. pneumoniae was found as emerging bacteria for the highest Pb2+ and Cd2+ uptake efficiency, underscoring their potential application in heavy metal remediation from tannery wastewater.22 This high resistance indicates robust adaptive mechanisms that allow these isolates to survive under toxic metal stress. Heavy metal removal efficiency typically varies with bacterial strains, origin of bacteria, environmental conditions, metal concentration, and exposure conditions.23, 65

Bacteria from metal-contaminated environments frequently harbor co-located metal and antibiotic resistance genes on mobile genetic elements such as plasmids or transposons.66 Previous studies have also reported that heavy metal-resistant bacterial isolates can exhibit antibiotic resistance, indicating co-selection of metal and antibiotic resistance genes.67, 68 In contrast, our findings showed that five of seven heavy metal-tolerant isolates were highly sensitive to clinically relevant antibiotics. Although a few isolates were resistant and need further safety evaluation, most were antibiotic-sensitive, suggesting a lower risk of spreading resistance during bioremediation.22, 66, 69 Recent studies have reported that microorganisms with bioremediation potential can also promote plant growth through detoxification and production of growth-promoting metabolites.20, 21, 49, 65 In this study, qualitative screening of PGP properties was employed to rapidly assess functional potential among heavy metal-tolerant isolates. The selected bacterial isolates displayed multiple PGP properties, including phosphate solubilization,70 cellulose degradation,71 ammonia production, and IAA synthesis.72 By solubilizing phosphate, these isolates increase the availability of this essential nutrient in the soil, thereby supporting plant growth and development. Moreover, the current findings finally observed K. pneumoniae as a promising PGP candidate besides of the best metal tolerance potency.

Earlier studies on metal-tolerant bacteria from tannery or industrial effluents have largely addressed individual parameters in isolation, focused on biosorption or metal tolerance without integrating PGP screening.22, 23, 65 Lee and Cho,20 and Mokrani et al.,21 also reported PGP traits without systematic dual-metal tolerance profiling or in silico gene network analysis. Moreover, the present study advances beyond these studies by: (i). simultaneously evaluating dual Pb2+ and Cd2+ tolerance with qualitative and quantitative biosorption within a single experimental framework (ii). integrating multi-trait PGP screening with antibiotic susceptibility profiling, and (iii) applying PPI network analysis and KEGG two-component system pathway mapping to provide computational mechanistic insights - an approach that remains limited for K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp. in environmental contexts.73, 74

Although K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp. was identified previously as MDR bacteria,73, 75 they were found to be antibiotic sensitive and strong candidates for removing heavy metals from polluted environments, and also supporting plant growth and promoting soil health restoration. Comprehensive bioinformatics analyses of heavy metal tolerant genes network in K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp. are still limited.74 Network analysis has been used significantly in recent years to discover keystone taxa and display the interactions within microbial communities. Highly interconnected microbial taxa are frequently considered as keystone species within microbial symbiotic networks, with a tendency to profoundly affect the structure of the microbial community.76

Enrichment of Fe—S cluster assembling and stress-responsive genes in Enterobacter spp. leads to an additional type of defense via redox-balanced and heavy metal-damaged metalloprotein recovery. K. pneumoniae predominantly utilizes the CusSR and ZraSR systems, whereas Enterobacter spp. relies on the TtrSR, CusSR, and OmpR pathways, according to the KEGG pathway investigation of two-component systems. According to our research, these bacteria mainly employ metal ion transport and redox regulation techniques to combat harmful conditions, as indicated by the enriched terms metal ion transport, cation transmembrane transport, and reaction to inorganic chemicals.

It is important to note that the dithizone-based assay used in this study does not discriminate between metal adsorption onto the bacterial cell surface and intracellular accumulation. Therefore, the reported “removal efficiency” should be interpreted as total apparent metal removal from the medium, encompassing both extracellular binding and potential uptake. As a result, these findings do not provide direct evidence of intracellular sequestration mechanisms. Surface adsorption, mediated by functional groups such as carboxyl, hydroxyl, and phosphate moieties, may account for a substantial proportion of the observed metal removal. To accurately distinguish between adsorption and intracellular accumulation, further investigations using complementary approaches (e.g., desorption assays, electron microscopy, or quantitative intracellular metal analysis) are required. Accordingly, caution should be exercised to avoid overinterpreting biosorption results as indicative of true intracellular uptake.

The environmental application of metal-tolerant bacteria such as Klebsiella pneumoniae and Enterobacter warrants careful biosafety evaluation due to their known association with opportunistic infections and antimicrobial resistance (AMR). A major concern is the potential for horizontal gene transfer (HGT) of resistance determinants between environmental and pathogenic microorganisms, particularly in contaminated sites where selective pressure from heavy metals may co-select for antibiotic resistance traits. Despite the strong dual metal tolerance and plant growth-promoting (PGP) potential of Klebsiella pneumoniae and Enterobacter isolates, their application necessitates careful biosafety evaluation. Both genera include opportunistic pathogens with documented antimicrobial resistance; however, environmental strains are highly heterogeneous and may lack clinically relevant virulence determinants. The isolates in this study were selected based on superior functional performance under Pb and Cd stress and their multiple PGP traits. Nonetheless, their use should be limited to controlled or non-food-chain contexts, such as remediation of industrially contaminated soils. Prior to any field application, comprehensive genomic screening for virulence and resistance genes is essential. Alternatively, exploiting strain-derived functional genes or metabolites may provide a safer route for biotechnological applications.

However, the studies might not accurately represent the biosafety concerns, such as environmental risk assessment and molecular mechanisms of resistance. Future investigations should incorporate comprehensive biosafety evaluations, including environmental risk assessment, detailed antibiotic resistance profiling, pilot-scale and field-level validation prior to practical implementation. In-depth genetic and proteomic analyses are also needed to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying multi-metal resistance. Such studies are essential to determine the efficacy, stability, and persistence of metal-resistant bacteria under real environmental conditions, supporting their safe and effective applications in environmental and agricultural bioremediation strategies.

Conclusion

The current study emphasized the isolation and molecular characterization of bacterial isolates from tannery-contaminated environments, demonstrating their strong metal resistance potency and plant growth-promoting effect. This study demonstrates that selected bacterial isolates exhibit notable dual tolerance to Pb and Cd, along with multiple plant growth-promoting traits under controlled laboratory conditions. These findings highlight their physiological adaptability to heavy metal stress and their potential functional relevance in contaminated environments. However, it is important to emphasize that all observations are based on in-vitro assays, and no greenhouse, in-vivo, or field-level evaluations have been conducted. This study also emphasized the widespread application of computational network biology techniques to comprehend the genes linked to bioremediation in K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp. This research offers an extensive knowledge of the molecular processes governing heavy metal tolerance in K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp. via bioinformatics analysis. The genes in both kinds of bacteria that are tolerant to heavy metals were discovered using a network biology strategy to understand the observed tolerance profiles. The presence of numerous important regulatory genes and systems that allow these bacteria to persist under metal-stressful circumstances was identified by the combination of PPI network building, GO enrichment, and KEGG two-component system pathway analysis. Significantly connected hub genes in K. pneumoniae and Enterobacter spp. were identified by the PPI network assessment, and both the biological and chemical processes revealed from the PPI network were further reinforced by the GO enrichment study. These findings indicate that both species have a complex network of transporters, binding proteins, and stress response components.
