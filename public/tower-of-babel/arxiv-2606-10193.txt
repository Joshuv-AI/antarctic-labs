1.
Introduction and statement of results

1.1.
Background and motivation

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an alphabet with at least two elements. A

configuration
is an element
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, i.e., a
two-dimensional bi-infinite array with values in
\mathcal{A}
. For

k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
rectangular complexity

P_{\eta}(k,n)
counts
the number of distinct
k\times n
rectangular patterns occurring in

\eta
. A configuration is
periodic
if there exists

h\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{*}
with
\eta_{g+h}=\eta_{g}
for all

g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and
fully periodic
or
globally periodic

when two linearly independent periods exist. From now on, we assume that
\mathcal{A}
is finite.

More than eight decades ago, Morse and Hedlund
[
12
,
13
]
proved that a
one-dimensional sequence is periodic if and only if its complexity
function is eventually bounded by the identity function. Proposed
by Maurice Nivat in his invited talk at ICALP 1997 in Bologna

[
14
]
, the following conjecture extends the Morse-Hedlund
theorem to two dimensions.

Conjecture 1.1

(Nivat, 1997)
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
. If
P_{\eta}(k,n)\leq kn
for some

k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\eta
is periodic.

Conjecture
1.1
remains open despite substantial progress
over the past two decades. We briefly recall the milestones most
relevant to the present work.

First partial results.

Sander and Tijdeman
[
16
]
proved the conjecture for

k=2
: if
P_{\eta}(2,n)\leq 2n
, then
\eta
is periodic. Epifanio, Koskas and Mignosi
[
7
]
handled the
case
P_{\eta}(k,n)\leq kn/144
, and Quas and Zamboni
[
15
]
the case
P_{\eta}(k,n)\leq kn/16
.

The Cyr-Kra approach via nonexpansive subdynamics.

Boyle and Lind
[
1
]
introduced the notion of

expansive subspaces
and proved that any infinite subshift of

\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
admits at least one
nonexpansive line
.
Cyr and Kra
[
5
]
refined this notion to

one-sided nonexpansive directions
and used the resulting
geometric structure to prove Nivat’s conjecture for the situation

P_{\eta}(k,n)\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ kn/2
.
Their subsequent paper
[
6
]
establishes the conjecture in
the short-rectangle regime: if
P_{\eta}(3,n)\leq 3n
for some
n
,
then
\eta
is periodic. Colle and Garibaldi
[
4
]
obtained an alphabetical refinement of the Cyr-Kra bound: for configurations containing all letters of
\mathcal{A}
, the weaker condition
P_{\eta}(k,n)\leq\frac{1}{2}kn+|\mathcal{A}|-1
, stated more generally for quasi-regular shapes, already suffices to conclude periodicity. The present work takes the Cyr-Kra short-rectangle result as its starting point, extending it from
P_{\eta}(3,n)
to
P_{\eta}(4,n)
.

Algebraic approach: periodic decompositions.

Using tools from algebraic geometry, Kari and Szabados

[
11
]
proved that any low pattern complexity
configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
(with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}
) can be written
as a finite sum of periodic configurations over
\mathbb{Z}
:

\eta=\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{m}
, with
\eta_{i}\in

\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
periodic.
As a consequence, they showed that Nivat’s conjecture holds if
P_{\eta}(k,n)\leq kn
for infinitely many pairs
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Szabados
[
17
]
subsequently proved that Nivat’s conjecture
holds for configurations admitting such a decomposition with

m=2
components.

Periodic accumulation points and the repetitive case.

Combining the existence of a non-trivial annihilator
[
11
]

with a compactness argument that eliminates one-sided directions of
determinism, Kari and Moutot
[
9
,
10
]
proved
that the orbit closure of any low convex pattern complexity configuration
contains a periodic configuration. Applying a technique of Cyr and Kra

[
5
]
to uniformly recurrent configurations then yields
Nivat’s conjecture in the repetitive (minimal subshift) case.

Periodic decompositions and nonexpansive directions.

In
[
2
]
it was shown that, under natural hypotheses on the
orders of non-periodic configurations in the orbit closure, both
Szabados’s conjecture and a symmetry property of the nonexpansive structure hold:
every nonexpansive line admits both of its orientations as one-sided nonexpansive
directions, so that each nonexpansive line contributes a pair of oppositely oriented one-sided nonexpansive directions. Together these two conditions yield a reduction of Nivat’s
conjecture to configurations satisfying them.
In
[
3
]
a sharper analysis of minimal periodic
decompositions shows that one may always find a decomposition where
each component is defined on a
finite
alphabet contained in

[\![p]\!]:=\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
for a prime
p
; this

minimal writing over a finite alphabet
provides the
compactness tools exploited in the present paper.

1.2.
Main results

Our first main result is a structural theorem of independent interest:

Theorem 1.2

(Modular double-periodicity structure)
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, be a non-periodic
configuration with low convex pattern complexity.
Then there exist

(i)

a configuration
\vartheta\in\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
,

(ii)

a
\mathbb{Z}
-minimal periodic decomposition

\vartheta=\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{m}
,

(iii)

a prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
with
\mathcal{A}\subset[\![p]\!]
,

(iv)

disjoint half-planes

, for

1\leq i\leq m
,

such that, for each
1\leq i\leq m
, both

\bar{\vartheta}_{i}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$U_{i}$}
and
\bar{\vartheta}_{i}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$V_{i}$}
are fully
periodic, where
\bar{\vartheta}_{i}
denotes the reduction of
\vartheta_{i}

modulo
p
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
(see Section
3
)
combines two ingredients:

(1)

The existence, established by combining Cases 1 and 2 in the
proof of
[
2
, Lemma 4.6]
, of an
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
-region
– a convex unbounded region whose boundary
consists of two semi-infinite edges, one directed along
\ell
and
one directed along
\ell^{\prime}
, where both
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
are
nonexpansive lines on
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
– on which a certain accumulation
point of the orbit of
\eta
is
fully periodic
;

(2)

The minimal writing over a finite alphabet from
[
3
]
,
which provides the compactness needed to “symmetrize” the
double periodicity within the orbit closure, yielding, for
each component
\vartheta_{i}
, a pair of disjoint half-planes

U_{i},V_{i}
on which the reduction
\bar{\vartheta}_{i}
is fully
periodic.

Our second main result extends the Cyr-Kra theorem
[
6
]

from the case
P_{\eta}(3,n)\leq 3n
to
P_{\eta}(4,n)\leq 4n
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
be a configuration. If
P_{\eta}(k,n)\leq kn
with
k\leq 4

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\eta
is periodic.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on Theorem
1.2
together with the balanced-set propagation of Cyr and Kra
[
5
]
and a maximality argument for the half-planes it produces. We now explain the logical structure of the argument. By Remark
2.7
, the complexity bound
P_{\eta}(4,n)\leq 4n
forces the order
m
of any minimal periodic decomposition to satisfy
m\leq 4
. The case
m=1
is trivial and
m=2
is settled by Theorem
2.8
.
For
m\in\{3,4\}
, Theorem
1.2
supplies an accumulation point
\vartheta
of the orbit of
\eta
admitting a minimal periodic decomposition
\vartheta=\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{m}
and, for each component
\vartheta_{i}
, a pair of disjoint half-planes on which the reduction of
\vartheta_{i}
modulo an appropriate prime is fully periodic. Assuming these half-planes to be maximal in a sense that neither can be enlarged while preserving full periodicity, the contradiction is reached by showing that the geometry of a
\vartheta
-generating set, relative to a suitably chosen unbounded convex region carved out by the half-planes (see Definition
4.1
), allows the balanced-set argument to propagate full periodicity of some component beyond its assigned half-plane, violating maximality.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section

collects the preliminary material used

.
Section

-star configuration

Section

carries out the argument outlined in the previous paragraph in detail, introducing along the way the precise geometric vocabulary – strip-free regions (Definition

2.
Preliminaries

We briefly recall the main objects and results that will be used in
the proofs; full details can be found in the references cited.

Throughout this paper, whenever a periodic decomposition

\eta=\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{m}
is considered over a ring
R
– either

\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
– the components
\eta_{i}
are
understood to take values in
R
. The same symbol
\eta_{i}
is used
in both cases; the ring is always clear from context, and usually no
additional notational distinction between the two situations is made.

2.1.
Subshifts and complexity

For
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
and a finite, non-empty set
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the
\mathcal{S}
-complexity

P_{\eta}(\mathcal{S})
counts the distinct
\mathcal{S}
-patterns of
\eta
. By an
\mathcal{S}
-pattern
of
\eta
we mean the element of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{S}}
defined by
g\in\mathcal{S}\mapsto\eta_{g+u}\in\mathcal{A}
for some fixed
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
– it is a subconfiguration of
\eta
seen through the window
\mathcal{S}
as it slides over all positions
of the lattice; the collection of all such patterns is denoted
\mathrm{Pat}(\mathcal{S},\eta)
, so that
P_{\eta}(\mathcal{S})=|\mathrm{Pat}(\mathcal{S},\eta)|
. The configuration
\eta
has
low pattern complexity
if
P_{\eta}(\mathcal{S})\leq|\mathcal{S}|
for some finite, non-empty
\mathcal{S}
, and
low convex pattern complexity
if
\mathcal{S}
can be taken convex (in the sense that
\mathcal{S}=\mathrm{Conv}(\mathcal{S})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
). The
shift action
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
on
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
is given by
(T^{u}\eta)_{g}=\eta_{g+u}
for
u,g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
; endowing
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
with the product of discrete topologies makes it a compact metrizable space.
The orbit closure
X_{\eta}:=\overline{\{T^{u}\eta:u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}}
is then a closed shift-invariant subset of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, hence a subshift, and every configuration in
X_{\eta}
has low convex pattern complexity whenever
\eta
does.

For a convex set
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
\mathrm{Conv}(\mathcal{S})
of positive area, an
edge
of
\mathcal{S}
is a maximal collinear subset of the boundary of
\mathrm{Conv}(\mathcal{S})
that contains more than one point of
\mathcal{S}
; we denote by
E(\mathcal{S})
the collection of all edges of
\mathcal{S}
, each inheriting a natural orientation from the positively oriented boundary of
\mathrm{Conv}(\mathcal{S})
. If
|E(\mathcal{S})|<\infty
, our convention endows each finite edge
w\in E(\mathcal{S})
with well-defined successor and predecessor edges. Throughout the text, we commit a slight abuse of notation by also calling edge its maximal subset of points in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. A
vertex
of
\mathcal{S}
is a point of
\mathcal{S}
at which the boundary of
\mathrm{Conv}(\mathcal{S})
changes direction.

The global notion of periodicity admits a natural local counterpart that will be needed throughout the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
be a configuration and let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a non-empty set. We say that
\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{U}$}
is
periodic
if there exists a non-zero vector
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, called
period of
\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{U}$}
, such that
g+h\in\mathcal{U}
and
(T^{h}\eta)_{g}=\eta_{g}
for all
g\in\mathcal{U}
.
If
\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{U}$}
has two periods linearly independent (over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
), we say that
\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{U}$}
is
fully periodic
.

When
\mathcal{U}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
this recovers the usual notion of periodicity of
\eta
. A central question in the study of low complexity configurations is whether local periodic behavior – that is, periodicity on proper convex subsets
\mathcal{U}
– must propagate to the whole of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The analogue of Nivat’s conjecture for convex shapes, also open, asserts that low convex pattern complexity alone is enough to force global periodicity:

Conjecture 2.2

(Nivat’s conjecture for convex shapes)
.

Given a configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, if
\eta
has low convex
pattern complexity, then
\eta
is periodic.

A central tool in the study of Nivat’s conjecture and its variants is the notion of a
generating set
, introduced by Cyr and Kra
[
5
]
and further developed in
[
8
]
.
A finite convex set
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is called an
\eta
-generating
set
if, for all
x\in X_{\eta}
, knowing
x
on
\mathcal{S}\setminus\{g\}
uniquely determines
x_{g}

for each vertex
g\in\mathcal{S}
; intuitively,
\mathcal{S}
is a shape from which the configuration can be reconstructed locally.

A line is called
nonexpansive
if configurations in
X_{\eta}
cannot be distinguished by what they look like in any arbitrarily thick strip around the line; roughly speaking, it is a direction along which the dynamics fails to separate points. Precisely, a line
\ell\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
through the origin is a
nonexpansive
line
on
X_{\eta}
if, for every
t>0
, there exist distinct

x,y\in X_{\eta}
agreeing on the
t
-neighbourhood of
\ell
; the set
of such lines is
\nexpl(\eta)
.
If
\ell\notin\nexpl(\eta)
, we say that
\ell
is an
expansive line
on
X_{\eta}
.
Given an orientation
\boldsymbol{\ell}
of
\ell
, the
oriented line is a
one-sided nonexpansive direction
on
X_{\eta}
if there
exist
x\neq y
in
X_{\eta}
agreeing on the half-plane to the left of

\boldsymbol{\ell}
, that is, the closed half-plane whose boundary is

\ell
, positively oriented by
\boldsymbol{\ell}
; the set of such oriented
lines is
\nexpd(\eta)
.
Whenever
\boldsymbol{\ell}\notin\nexpd(\eta)
, we say that
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is an
one-sided expansive direction
on
X_{\eta}
.

From now on, we will consider the following notation: given a line
\ell\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we use
\boldsymbol{\ell}
to denote the line
\ell
endowed with a given orientation – for an oriented line
\boldsymbol{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we use
-\boldsymbol{\ell}
to denote the oriented line antiparallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}
(i.e., with the opposite orientation) that determines the same points of
\boldsymbol{\ell}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Given a non-empty, convex set
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and an oriented line
\boldsymbol{\ell}
, we use
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}
to denote the oriented line
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}
such that
\mathcal{S}
is contained in the half-plane to the left of
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. In particular, if
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is parallel to some edge of
\mathcal{S}
, then the set
\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}
consists of all integer points of
\mathcal{S}
that lie on this edge. Among all non-empty convex subsets of a given finite convex set satisfying
P_{\eta}(\mathcal{S})\leq|\mathcal{S}|
, Cyr and Kra
[
5
]
extract a smallest one: by
[
5
, Lemma 2.5]
, any such smallest set is an
\eta
-generating set, which we call an
extremal
\eta
-generating set
to distinguish it from the generating sets associated with periodic decompositions in the next subsection.
A key feature of extremal
\eta
-generating sets, exploited throughout
[
5
, Section 4]
, is the inequality

(2.1)

P_{\eta}(\mathcal{S})-P_{\eta}(\mathcal{S}\setminus\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}})<|\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}|,

which asserts that the edge
\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}
contributes strictly fewer new patterns than its number of integer points.

The geometry of
\eta
-generating sets imposes a strong constraint on the nonexpansive structure: any one-sided nonexpansive direction must be parallel or antiparallel to some edge of every
\eta
-generating set. It is this restriction that underlines most the partial results on Nivat’s conjecture obtained to date.

2.2.
Periodic decompositions and their geometry

Following
[
11
]
, we embed configurations with integer
alphabet in the ring of formal power series
\mathbb{Z}[\![X^{\pm 1}]\!]
and
write
\eta
as
\sum_{g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\eta_{g}X^{g}
. A Laurent polynomial

\psi(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[\![X^{\pm 1}]\!]

annihilates

\eta
if
\psi\eta=0
.
The set of annihilators is denoted
\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}}(\eta)
. The notion of annihilator can be defined naturally for configurations taking values in other algebraic structures, such as finite fields (see
[
3
]
for details).

A configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
(with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}
) has a
non-trivial annihilator whenever it has low pattern complexity

[
11
]
. Moreover, if

(X^{h_{1}}-1)\cdots(X^{h_{m}}-1)\in\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}}(\eta)
for vectors

h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in pairwise distinct directions, then
\eta

decomposes as
\eta=\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{m}
with
\eta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
periodic with
period
h_{i}

[
11
]
. Such a decomposition is called a

\mathbb{Z}
-periodic decomposition
; it is
\mathbb{Z}
-minimal
(of

order

m
) if no decomposition into fewer terms exists.

Given a Laurent polynomial
\phi(X)=\sum_{i}a_{i}X^{u_{i}}
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
u_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, its
support
is the finite set
\mathrm{Supp}(\phi)=\{u_{i}:a_{i}\neq 0\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The
reflected convex support
of
\phi
is then defined as
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}:=\mathrm{Conv}(-\mathrm{Supp}(\phi))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the lattice points of the convex hull of the reflected support. When
\psi
annihilates
\eta
, the set
\mathcal{S}_{\psi}
is itself an
\eta
-generating set. The
following lemma, whose proof is given in full generality – for
R=\mathbb{Z}
or any finite field
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
– in
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
, is stated here for completeness.

Lemma 2.3

(Szabados
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, with
\mathcal{A}\subset R
, be a configuration. If
\psi\in\Ann_{R}(\eta)
, then
\mathcal{S}_{\psi}
is an
\eta
-generating set.

The geometry of
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}
, for
\varphi(X)=(X^{h_{1}}-1)\cdots(X^{h_{m}}-1)\in\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\eta)
, encodes the periodic structure of
\eta
in two complementary ways: its edges record the directions of the periods of components of the associated decomposition, and it rigidly constrains the edge structure of every other annihilator of
\eta
.
The first of these properties is made precise by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4

(Szabados
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, be a configuration and suppose
\varphi(X)=(X^{h_{1}}-1)\cdots(X^{h_{m}}-1)\in\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\eta)
, where
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are vectors in pairwise distinct directions.
The following conditions hold:

(i)

the reflected convex support
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}
has exactly
2m
edges – that is,

|E(\mathcal{S}_{\varphi})|=2m
– and, for each
1\leq i\leq m
, one edge is parallel to

and another is parallel to
-h_{i}
;

(ii)

if
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\nexpd(\eta)
, then there exists
1\leq i\leq m
such that
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is either parallel or antiparallel to the vector
h_{i}
.

Combining the algebraic decomposition with balanced-set technique of Cyr and Kra

Theorem 2.8

(Szabados
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and suppose

-minimal periodic decomposition. Then

The original version of this result considers
\mathbb{Z}
-decompositions and rectangular complexity.
The adaptation of the arguments of the proof for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-decompositions and convex shapes is briefly provided below for the reader’s convenience.

Proof.

Let

denote periods for
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
, respectively, and consider the set
\mathcal{T}=\{sh_{1}+th_{2}:s,t\in[0,1]\}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}

0\leq i<j\leq P_{\eta}(\mathcal{T})
such that

\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}+ih_{1}$}=\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}+jh_{1}$}=(T^{(j-i)h_{1}}\eta){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}+ih_{1}$}.

and

is a period for
\eta_{1}
, it follows that
\eta_{1}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$}=T^{(j-i)h_{1}}\eta_{1}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$}
. Combined with
\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$}=(T^{(j-i)h_{1}}\eta){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$}
and
\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}
, this gives
\eta_{2}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$}=T^{(j-i)h_{1}}\eta_{2}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$}
. Now, for

, pick

such that

(\eta_{2})_{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}(\eta_{2})_{g+th_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}(\eta_{2})_{g+th_{2}+(j-i)h_{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}(\eta_{2})_{g+(j-i)h_{1}},

where both equalities labeled

use the fact that
h_{2}
is a period for
\eta_{2}
, and equality

uses
\eta_{2}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$}=T^{(j-i)h_{1}}\eta_{2}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{T}^{\prime}$}
evaluated at

. Hence,
\eta_{g}=\eta_{g+(j-i)h_{1}}
for all

\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$S_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}$}=(T^{(j-i)h_{1}}\eta){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$S_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}$}

Suppose, by contradiction, that
\eta
has low convex pattern complexity.
Since

is a

-minimal decomposition, the annihilator

and Lemma

imply that

consists of at most the two lines

and

containing

and

satisfies
\pm\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}\in\nexpd(\eta)
, so that any
\eta
-generating set has edges directed along both

and
\ell_{2}

, the local agreement of

provides the starting data for the balanced-set propagation argument of Cyr and Kra
[

: either a multiple of

is a global period for

-periodic. In either case

and

2.3.
Nonexpansive directions and generating sets

The algebraic structure of periodic decompositions interacts with the dynamics of
X_{\eta}
through the geometry of generating sets: as Lemma
2.9
below makes precise, the edges of any
\eta
-generating set determine which oriented lines can be one-sided nonexpansive directions. By a theorem of Boyle and Lind
[
1
]
every infinite
subshift of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
possesses at least one nonexpansive line, and
Cyr and Kra
[
5
]
made this connection precise:

Lemma 2.9

(Cyr and Kra
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
and suppose
\boldsymbol{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an oriented line through the origin and
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a finite, convex set such that
\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}=\{g\}
is
\eta
-generated by
\mathcal{S}
. Then
\boldsymbol{\ell}\not\in\nexpd(\eta)
.

In particular, every oriented line in
\nexpd(\eta)
is parallel to some edge of any
\eta
-generating set whose convex hull has positive area.

By
[
2
, Proposition 1.3]
, for a periodic configuration
\eta
, nonexpansive lines and one-sided nonexpansive directions are perfectly symmetric:
\ell\in\nexpl(\eta)
if and only if

-\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\nexpd(\eta)
. This equivalence extends beyond the periodic case, at the cost of a natural hypothesis on the order of configurations in the orbit closure. Indeed, by
[
2
, Theorem 1.10]
, if
\eta
is a non-periodic low convex pattern complexity configuration for which all non-periodic configurations in
X_{\eta}
have the same order, then two conditions hold simultaneously: Szabados’s
conjecture is satisfied for
\eta
, meaning that every line
containing a period of some component of a
\mathbb{Z}
-minimal periodic decomposition of
\eta
is a nonexpansive line on
X_{\eta}
; and
\ell\in\nexpl(\eta)
if and only if
-\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\nexpd(\eta)
, so that each nonexpansive line contributes a pair of oppositely oriented one-sided nonexpansive directions.

The regions introduced below are bounded by lines that interact with the
integer lattice, so it is natural to restrict attention to
rational

oriented lines – those containing at least two points of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
or equivalently whose direction can be represented by a nonzero integer
vector. For such a line
\boldsymbol{\ell}
, its
direction vector

v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
is the unique primitive vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
pointing
in the direction of the orientation of
\boldsymbol{\ell}
, that is, the shortest
nonzero vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
whose direction agrees with that orientation.
The nonexpansive lines that arise in our setting are always rational, as
they contain the period vectors
h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and so direction
vectors are well defined for all oriented lines considered henceforth.

The propagation of local periodicity across regions bounded by nonexpansive directions is governed by the following notion. Given an oriented line
\boldsymbol{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
passing through some point
q=(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, with direction vector
(a,b)
, the associated
half-plane
is

\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}):=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:-b(g_{1}-q_{1})+a(g_{2}-q_{2})\geq 0\};

the definition is independent of
q
.
A half-plane
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell})
is called
rational
if
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is rational; in that case, its

slope
is the slope of the direction vector
v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
, and two rational half-planes are said to have
distinct slopes
if their bounding lines have distinct underlying directions. For a rational oriented line
\boldsymbol{\ell}
, the
adjacent line

\boldsymbol{\ell}^{(-)}

[
2
, Definition 2.2]
is the unique rational oriented line parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}
, disjoint from
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell})
, and closest to
\boldsymbol{\ell}
among all such translates; intuitively,
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{(-)}
is the first lattice translate of
\boldsymbol{\ell}
in the direction away from
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell})
.

Given two oriented lines
\boldsymbol{\ell}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
, in distinct directions and with
\boldsymbol{\ell}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
not antiparallel, a convex (positively oriented) unbounded set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with two semi-infinite edges
w,w^{\prime}\in E(\mathcal{K})
is called an
(\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime})
-region
if
w
is parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}
,
w^{\prime}
is parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
and
w
is a predecessor of
w^{\prime}
. The directions of the two semi-infinite edges are called the
asymptotic directions
of the region.

Other useful notion is the concept of envelopment. Let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a finite convex set with
\mathrm{Conv}(\mathcal{U})
of positive area. A convex set
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(possibly unbounded) is
weakly
E(\mathcal{U})
-enveloped
if for every edge
\varpi\in E(\mathcal{T})
there exists an edge
\omega\in E(\mathcal{U})
parallel to
\varpi
such that
|\omega\cap\mathcal{U}|\leq|\varpi\cap\mathcal{T}|
; in other words, each edge of
\mathcal{T}
has a counterpart in
E(\mathcal{U})
pointing in the same direction that is no longer, as measured by integer points. When in addition
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{U}
have the same number of edges,
\mathcal{T}
is said to be
E(\mathcal{U})
-enveloped
.

Given a configuration
\eta
and a rational oriented line
\boldsymbol{\ell}
, we say that
\eta
is
\ell
-periodic
on a convex set
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
if
\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{U}$}
has a period collinear with
v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
(not necessarily parallel). The following lemma describes how
\ell_{k}
-periodicity (respectively,
\ell_{1}
-periodicity) on an
(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
-region of the form
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
propagates to the adjacent region obtained by removing the bounding half-plane
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
(respectively,
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1})
). It is a key local-to-global tool in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, be a non-periodic configuration with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-minimal periodic decomposition
\eta=\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{m}
, periods
h_{i}
for
\eta_{i}
, and
\varphi(X)=(X^{h_{1}}-1)\cdots(X^{h_{m}}-1)
. Let
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be oriented lines parallel to the edges of
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}
, ordered so that the edge parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i+1}
is the successor of the edge parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}
, such that
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
is an
(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
-region weakly
E(\mathcal{S}_{\varphi})
-enveloped and with
k
edges.

(i)

If
\eta
is
\ell_{k}
-periodic on

\eta
and thus
\eta-\eta_{k}
are
\ell_{k}
-periodic on
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k-1})
.

(ii)

If
\eta
is
\ell_{1}
-periodic on
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
, then
\eta
and thus
\eta-\eta_{1}
are
\ell_{1}
-periodic on
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2})\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
.

Proof.

Item (ii)
is the symmetric counterpart of
item (i), and we leave the straightforward adaptation to the reader.

Item (i)
. Since the region is weakly
E(\mathcal{S}_{\varphi})
-enveloped, the semi-infinite edge directed along
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}
is parallel to some edge of
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}
, which by item (i) of Lemma
2.4
is parallel or antiparallel to
h_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq m
; renaming the components if necessary, we may assume
j=k
, so that
h_{k}
is collinear with
v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}}
. Since
\eta_{k}
is globally periodic with period
h_{k}
, it is periodic with period collinear with
v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}}
on all of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Hence the periodicity of
\eta
with period collinear with
v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}}
on
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
passes to
\eta-\eta_{k}
on the same region.

Consider now the annihilator
\hat{\varphi}(X):=(X^{h_{1}}-1)\cdots\widehat{(X^{h_{k}}-1)}\cdots(X^{h_{m}}-1)\in\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\eta-\eta_{k})
. By item (i) of Lemma
2.4
, the reflected convex support
\mathcal{S}_{\hat{\varphi}}
has edges parallel or antiparallel to
h_{1},\ldots,\hat{h}_{k},\ldots,h_{m}
; in particular, since the region is weakly
E(\mathcal{S}_{\varphi})
-enveloped and
h_{k}
has been removed,
\mathcal{S}_{\hat{\varphi}}
has consecutive edges parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k-1}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k+1}
but none parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}
. This means that
\mathcal{S}_{\hat{\varphi}}
can be positioned so that exactly one of its vertices lies outside the region
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k})
(see Figure
1
).

.

\mathcal{T}
of

-region.
Because

has no edge directed along

\mathcal{S}_{\hat{\varphi}}

The following lemma encodes a rigidity phenomenon: if a configuration
is already periodic in some direction, and the configuration seen
asymptotically along that same direction is fully periodic, then full
periodicity propagates back to the entire region.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\xi\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
be a configuration and let

\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be an
(\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime})
-region.
Let
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
be a rational oriented line neither parallel nor antiparallel to any semi-infinite edge of
\mathcal{R}
, with
v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime\prime}}
pointing inward into the region
\mathcal{R}
. Suppose that
\xi
is
\ell^{\prime\prime}
-periodic on

\mathcal{R}
, and that
\{T^{tv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime\prime}}}\xi:t\in\mathbb{N}\}
has a fully periodic accumulation point. Then
\xi
is fully periodic on
\mathcal{R}
. Consequently, if
\xi
is
\ell^{\prime\prime}
-periodic (on the entire lattice), then
\xi
is fully periodic (on the entire lattice).

Proof.

Suppose that
\xi
is
\ell^{\prime\prime}
-periodic with period
u^{\prime\prime}
on
\mathcal{R}
.
Let
\vartheta
denote a fully periodic accumulation point of
\{T^{tv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime\prime}}}\xi:t\in\mathbb{N}\}

with periods
u^{\prime\prime}
along
\ell^{\prime\prime}
and
u^{\prime}
along
\ell^{\prime}
.
Let
g\in\mathcal{R}
be arbitrary. Since
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{Conv}(\mathcal{R}))
is a
half-line, there exists
t_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that

\xi{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$(\{g,\,g+u^{\prime}\}+t_{0}u^{\prime\prime})$}=\vartheta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\{g,\,g+u^{\prime}\}$}.

Since
\xi
is
\ell^{\prime\prime}
-periodic,

\xi_{g}=\xi_{g+t_{0}u^{\prime\prime}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\xi_{g+u^{\prime}}=\xi_{g+u^{\prime}+t_{0}u^{\prime\prime}}.

Since
\vartheta
is periodic with period
u^{\prime}
, we have

\vartheta_{g+u^{\prime}}=\vartheta_{g}
, and chaining the equalities above
yields

\xi_{g+u^{\prime}}=\xi_{g+u^{\prime}+t_{0}u^{\prime\prime}}=\vartheta_{g+u^{\prime}}=\vartheta_{g}=\xi_{g+t_{0}u^{\prime\prime}}=\xi_{g}.

As

was arbitrary,
\xi
is periodic with period

u^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{R}
. Together with
\ell^{\prime\prime}
-periodicity,
\xi
is
thus fully periodic on
\mathcal{R}
.

Given a configuration

of

which counts the number of
\mathcal{S}
-patterns of
\eta
that extend a given

-pattern

. A configuration

is said to be

extends to at least two distinct

-patterns, that is,

(2.2)

\ N_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{\ell},(T^{tv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}x){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$(\mathcal{S}\setminus\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}})$},\eta)>1,\quad\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+};

-ambiguity

\mathbb{Z}_{-}
. Whenever

and

Proposition 2.12

(Cyr and Kra
[
5
]
)

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}

\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\nexpd(\eta)
,
and let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be an extremal

-generating

of

B

\mathcal{S}\setminus\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}

\boldsymbol{\ell}

\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}

allows. Suppose
B\neq\emptyset
. Then, for any
(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathcal{S},\eta,+)
-ambiguous configuration
x\in X_{\eta}
:

(i)

if
p=1
, the restriction of
x
to
\sst_{B}(\boldsymbol{\ell})

is
\ell
-periodic;

(ii)

if
p\geq 2
, the restriction of
x
to

\sst_{B}(\boldsymbol{\ell})+p\,v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
is
\ell
-periodic.

Informally, the proposition asserts that the ambiguity of
x
along
\boldsymbol{\ell}
– the fact that patterns on
\mathcal{S}\setminus\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}
fail to determine their extension across the edge
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}
– forces
x
to repeat itself in the direction
\boldsymbol{\ell}
on a semi-strip that is as wide as
B
allows. The complexity hypothesis makes this possible: it bounds the total number of ambiguous extensions across
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}
by
|\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1
, which ensures that there are too few distinct
B
-patterns to prevent this repetition. We record the proof here for completeness.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=\{(T^{tv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}x){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$B$}:t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
. Since
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Pat}(\mathcal{S}\setminus\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}},\eta)
,

p\geq P_{\eta}(\mathcal{S})-P_{\eta}(\mathcal{S}\setminus\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}})=\sum_{\gamma\in\pat(\mathcal{S}\setminus\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}},\eta)}\bigl(N_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{\ell},\gamma,\eta)-1\bigr)\geq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\bigl(N_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{\ell},\gamma,\eta)-1\bigr)\geq|\Gamma|.

Let
\Xi\subset B
be the set consisting of the initial point of
\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
with respect to the orientation of
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
, as
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
ranges over the oriented lines parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}
that meet
B
. By assumption
B\supset\bigcup_{i=0}^{p}(\Xi+iv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}})
, and therefore

(2.3)

p\geq|\Gamma|\geq\big|\big\{(T^{tv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}x){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\bigcup_{i=0}^{p}(\Xi+iv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}})$}:t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\big\}\big|.

Setting
\xi_{t}:=(T^{tv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}x){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\Xi$}
, the bound (
2.3
) yields
P_{\xi}(p)\leq p
, where by
P_{\xi}(p)
we mean the number of distinct words of length
p
occurring in
\xi
. If
p=1
the conclusion is immediate; otherwise, the Morse-Hedlund theorem
[
12
,
13
]
implies that
(\xi_{t+p})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is periodic, which gives the stated
\ell
-periodicity of
x
on the appropriate semi-strip.
∎

The symmetric statement holds for
(\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathcal{S},\eta,-)
-ambiguous configurations: replacing
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{-}
in (
2.2
) and reversing the orientation of
\boldsymbol{\ell}
throughout, the same argument yields the
\ell
-periodicity of
x
on
\sst_{B}(-\boldsymbol{\ell})
when
|\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}|=2
, and on
\sst_{B}(-\boldsymbol{\ell})-(|\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1)v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
otherwise.

3.
Proof of the modular double-periodicity theorem

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, the modular double-periodicity structure result stated in the introduction. Roughly speaking, the theorem asserts that, for a non-periodic configuration of low convex pattern complexity admitting a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-minimal periodic decomposition
\vartheta=\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{m}
, each component
\vartheta_{i}
is fully periodic on two disjoint half-planes. The key intermediate result is the following proposition, which establishes the existence of such half-planes under the hypothesis that
\vartheta
is already fully periodic on some
(\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime})
-region.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\vartheta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, be a non-periodic configuration. Suppose
\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\nexpd(\vartheta)
and there exists an
(\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime})
-region
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
– that is, a convex unbounded region with asymptotic directions
\boldsymbol{\ell}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
– such that
\vartheta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{K}$}
is fully periodic. Then, for any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-minimal periodic decomposition
\vartheta=\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{m}
, the following conditions hold.

(i)

There exist half-planes
U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, each containing
\mathcal{K}
, such that
\vartheta_{i}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$U_{i}$}
is fully periodic for each
1\leq i\leq m
.

(ii)

If, in addition,
\vartheta
has low convex pattern complexity, then there exist also half-planes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, with
U_{i}\cap V_{i}=\emptyset
, such that
\vartheta_{i}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$V_{i}$}
is fully periodic for each
1\leq i\leq m
.

The proof of condition (i) proceeds by induction on
m
propagating full periodicity outward from the region
\mathcal{K}
using the local-to-global tools of Section
2
. The proof of condition (ii) is more delicate: it requires constructing, for each component, a half-plane on the opposite side of the configuration, and relies on a careful analysis of accumulation points of translates of
\vartheta
in the direction determined by each nonexpansive line. The key tool is Claim
3.3
below, which identifies conditions under which a component is forced to be fully periodic at an accumulation point, and then transfers this conclusion back to
\vartheta
itself.

Proof.

Let
\vartheta=\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{m}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-minimal periodic decomposition, let
h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a period for
\vartheta_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
, and set
\varphi(X):=(X^{h_{1}}-1)\cdots(X^{h_{m}}-1)
. Let
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an enumeration of the oriented lines through the origin parallel to the edges of
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}
, ordered so that the edge parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i+1}
is the successor of the edge parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}
in the positively oriented boundary of
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}
, with indices taken modulo
2m
. We may assume that
h_{i}
is contained in
\ell_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq m
.

Since
\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\in\nexpd(\vartheta)
and
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}
is a
\vartheta
-generating set by Lemma
2.3
, Lemma
2.9
implies that
\boldsymbol{\ell}=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\iota}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\iota^{\prime}}
for some
\iota,\iota^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\ldots,2m\}
. Since
\boldsymbol{\ell}
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
determine an
(\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime})
-region, one has
\iota^{\prime}\in\{\iota+1,\ldots,\iota+m-1\}
. The following claim shows that
\iota^{\prime}
is in fact uniquely determined by
\iota
.

Claim 3.2
.

We have
\iota^{\prime}=\iota+m-1
.

Proof of Claim
3.2
.

Suppose for contradiction
\iota^{\prime}\neq\iota+m-1
.
Let
\phi(X)=\prod_{i\neq\iota+m-1}(X^{h_{i}}-1)
. Since
\vartheta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{K}$}
is fully periodic, the family
\{T^{tv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\iota+m-1}}}\vartheta\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, and consequently
\{T^{tv_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\iota+m-1}}}\phi(X)\vartheta\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, has a fully periodic accumulation point. Since
\phi(X)\vartheta
is
\ell_{\iota+m-1}
-periodic, Lemma
2.11
implies that
\phi(X)\vartheta
is fully periodic. Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a period for
\phi(X)\vartheta
such that
h,h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}
are in pairwise distinct directions, and set
\psi(X)=(X^{h}-1)\phi(X)
. Then
\psi(X)\in\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\vartheta)
, however, thanks to Lemma
2.4
(i),
\mathcal{S}_{\psi}
has no edge either parallel or antiparallel to
h_{\iota+m-1}
, contradicting Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Without loss of generality, assume
\iota=1
, so that
\iota^{\prime}=m
. By replacing
\mathcal{K}
by a subset if necessary, we may further assume that
\mathcal{K}
is weakly
E(\mathcal{S}_{\varphi})
-enveloped with one edge parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq m
. Lastly, by translating
\vartheta
if needed, we may suppose that
(0,0)\in(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{m-1})_{\mathcal{K}}\cap(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{m})_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Step 1: Proof of condition
(i)
by induction on
m
.

Base case

.
Since
\vartheta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{K}$}
is both
\ell_{2}
-periodic and

-periodic, Lemma

(i) gives that
\vartheta-\vartheta_{2}=\vartheta_{1}
is
\ell_{2}
-periodic on

\vartheta_{2}
is fully periodic on
\mathcal{H}((\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2})_{\mathcal{K}})
. Setting

concludes the base case.

Inductive step.
Suppose condition (i) holds for every non-periodic configuration of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-order at most

\vartheta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{K}$}

would contain a configuration
\ell_{m}
-periodic but not fully periodic, forcing
\ell_{m}\in\nexpl(\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{m-1})
; however item (ii) of Lemma
2.4
applied to the annihilator
(X^{h_{1}}-1)\cdots(X^{h_{m-1}}-1)\in\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{m-1})
would then give a contradiction. Lemma
2.11
then yields that

(\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{m-1}){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{H}((\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1})_{\mathcal{K}})\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{H}((\boldsymbol{\ell}_{m-1})_{\mathcal{K}})$}

is fully periodic. By induction hypothesis, there exist half-planes
\hat{U}_{1},\ldots,\hat{U}_{m-1}
with

such that
\vartheta_{i}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\hat{U}_{i}$}

. A symmetric argument, applying

(ii) to

yields half-planes

with

such that
\vartheta_{i}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\tilde{U}_{i}$}
is fully periodic for

. Setting
U_{i}=\hat{U}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m-1
and
U_{m}=\tilde{U}_{m}
completes the inductive step.

We note for later use that, by the minimality of

, the bounding edge of each half-plane

is parallel

and hence to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}
.

Step 2: A key claim for condition
(ii)
.

The following claim is the engine of the proof of condition (ii). It identifies, for a pair of indices
j\neq k
, conditions on an accumulation point
\theta=\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{m}
of translates of
\vartheta
that force
\theta_{k}
to be fully periodic, and then transfers this conclusion back to
\vartheta_{k}
on a half-plane on the opposite side of
\mathcal{K}
.

Claim 3.3
.

Let
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, with
j\neq k
, and consider
\tau\in\{-1,1\}
. Suppose that for every accumulation point
\theta
of
\{T^{t\tau v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}}}\vartheta:t\in\mathbb{N}\}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-periodic decomposition
\theta=\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{m}
– where
\theta_{i}
is an accumulation point of
\{T^{t\tau v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}}}\vartheta_{i}:t\in\mathbb{N}\}
– the component
\theta_{i}
is fully periodic for every
i\neq j
and
i\neq k
. Then:

(a)

\theta_{k}
is fully periodic;

(b)

there exists a half-plane
V_{k}
with bounding edge antiparallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}
such that
\vartheta_{k}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$V_{k}$}
is fully periodic.

Proof of Claim
3.3
.

Part (a).

If
\theta
is fully periodic, then
\theta_{k}=\theta-\sum_{i\neq k}\theta_{i}
is
\ell_{j}
-periodic and hence fully periodic. We may therefore assume that
\theta
is not fully periodic. Since
\theta
has low convex pattern complexity, Theorem
2.8
implies that
\theta=(\sum_{i\neq k}\theta_{i})+\theta_{k}
is periodic; consequently, its unique non-expansive line must contain every period. Let
\tilde{h}_{j}\in\ell_{j}
denote a period for
\sum_{i\neq k}\theta_{i}
and set
\psi(X)=(X^{\tilde{h}_{j}}-1)(X^{h_{k}}-1)
. Since
\psi(X)\in\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\theta)
, Lemma
2.9
and item (ii) of Lemma
2.4
imply that the unique non-expansive line on
X_{\theta}
is either
\ell_{j}
or
\ell_{k}
. Were
\theta
to be
\ell_{k}
-periodic, then
\theta_{j}=\theta-\sum_{i\neq j}\theta_{i}
would be
\ell_{k}
-periodic and hence fully periodic; since
\theta_{j}
is an accumulation point of
\{T^{t\tau v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}}}\vartheta_{j}:t\in\mathbb{N}\}
, this would force
\vartheta_{j}
to be fully periodic, contradicting the minimality of

. Therefore
\theta
is
\ell_{j}
-periodic, whence
\theta_{k}=\theta-\sum_{i\neq k}\theta_{i}
is
\ell_{j}
-periodic and hence fully periodic.

Part (b).

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the rectangle

R_{\alpha}=\{sh_{j}+th_{k}:s,t\in[-\alpha,\alpha]\}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}

and the strip

.

Let
(t_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{N}
with
\theta_{k}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T^{t_{n}\tau v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}}}\vartheta_{k}
. For each
\alpha
, there exists
t_{n_{\alpha}}
such that
(T^{t_{n_{\alpha}}\tau v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}}}\vartheta_{k}){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$R_{\alpha}$}=\theta_{k}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$R_{\alpha}$}
; the
\ell_{k}
-periodicity of both
\vartheta_{k}
and
\theta_{k}
then extends this agreement to the full strip:
(T^{t_{n_{\alpha}}\tau v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}}}\vartheta_{k}){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$St_{\alpha}$}=\theta_{k}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$St_{\alpha}$}
.

Suppose for contradiction that no such half-plane
V_{k}
exists. For each
\alpha
, let
L_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the largest convex region containing
St_{\alpha}
such that

(i)

(T^{t_{n_{\alpha}}\tau v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}}}\vartheta_{k}){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$L_{\alpha}$}=\theta_{k}{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$L_{\alpha}$}
, and

(ii)

if and only if
(g+\ell_{k})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}\subset L_{\alpha}
.

By the contradiction hypothesis, each
L_{\alpha}
is either a half-plane with bounding edge parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}
, or a strip. Let
w_{\alpha}\in E(L_{\alpha})
be the edge of
L_{\alpha}
parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{k}
, let
\sigma=\mathrm{sgn}(k-j)
, and let
s_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
s_{\alpha}\sigma t_{k}v_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}}
is the lattice point of this form closest to
w_{\alpha}
, where

is taking so that

is a period for
\theta_{k}
(see Figures
2
(A)
and
2

).

(A) The case

(
\sigma=1

lies to one side of its bounding edge

in the direction of
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{j}
closest to

from outside

(B) The case

, the region

∎

(ii)
by descending induction.

,

, and

.

&W^{\prime\prime\prime}&&\text{an }(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{3},-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2})\text{-strip-free region for }(U,V).\end{aligned}\right.

Is
W^{\prime}

\boldsymbol{\ell}_{4}
-compatible with some translate
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{S}
? If so, since every non-vertical edge of
\mathcal{T}
contains exactly two integer points, Proposition
2.12
(i) immediately yields
\ell_{4}
-periodicity of
\vartheta
on
\sst_{\mathcal{T}\setminus(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{4})_{\mathcal{T}}}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{4})
, contradicting Theorem
4.5
.
Hence,
W^{\prime}
is not
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{4}
-compatible with any translate of
\mathcal{S}
. Is
W^{\prime\prime}

\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}
-compatible with some translate of
\mathcal{S}
? If so, then, as before, we obtain a contradiction with Theorem
4.5
. Hence,
W^{\prime\prime}
is not
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}
-compatible with any translate of
\mathcal{S}
.
It remains to examine what the
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}
-incompatibility of
W^{\prime\prime}
concretely entails in this situation.
Since the
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{4}
-incompatible has already been verified and every non-vertical edge of
\mathcal{S}
contains exactly two integer points, the only geometric obstruction is that for any translate
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{S}
such that

.
In other words, part of

inside

-semi-strip enter

In every case we reach a contradiction, so
