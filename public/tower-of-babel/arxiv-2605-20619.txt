SURF: Steering the Scalarization Weight to Uniformly Traverse the Pareto Front
Liuyuan Jiang, Chentong Huang, Lisha Chen

Abstract
Scalarization is widely used in multi-objective optimization owing to its simplicity and scalability. In many applications, the goal is to generate solutions that represent diverse user preferences, ideally with uniform coverage of the Pareto front (PF). However, uniformly sampling scalarization weights usually induces non-uniform coverage of the PF. We explain this mismatch through a geometric analysis of the scalarization path. As the scalarization weight varies, the corresponding solutions trace the PF with a generally non-uniform traversal speed. This speed induces an arc-length cumulative distribution function (CDF); inverting this CDF map yields a principled rule for selecting weights that produce uniform PF coverage. Building on this insight, we propose SURF ( S ampling U niformly along the Pa r eto F ront). For structured problems, including bi-objective bandits, we derive closed-form expressions for this CDF map and the resulting PF-aware weight sampling rule. For general problems, SURF alternates between CDF reconstruction and weight sampling. Theoretically, we show that under provable conditions, SURF converges linearly to an unavoidable finite-sampling floor. Empirically, experiments on bandits, multi-objective-gymnasium, and multi-objective LLM alignment demonstrate that SURF efficiently achieves more uniform PF coverage than baselines. The code is available at https://github.com/Liuyuan999/MOO_Uniform_PF .

Rule 1

If

If

2
Pareto Front Geometry Analysis and Geometry-Aware Scalarization

To make the dial-speed picture precise, we now formalize the regularity conditions under which
\mathbf{u}_{w}^{*}
is well-defined and the PF traversal speed is smooth.
We first provide basic definitions for MOO, then discuss additional assumptions for our problem setup.

Definition 1

(Pareto optimality and Pareto front)
.

A feasible point
\mathbf{u}^{*}\in\mathcal{U}
is
Pareto optimal
if there does not exist
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
h_{m}(\mathbf{u})\leq h_{m}(\mathbf{u}^{*})
for all
m\in[M]
and
h_{j}(\mathbf{u})<h_{j}(\mathbf{u}^{*})
for at least one
j\in[M]
.
The collection of all Pareto-optimal solutions forms the
Pareto set
(PS), denoted as
\mathcal{U}_{\rm PF}
, and its image in objective space forms the
Pareto front
(PF),
\mathcal{F}_{\rm PF}:=\{\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}^{*}):\mathbf{u}^{*}\in\mathcal{U}_{\rm PF}\}
.

\mathcal{U}
and hidden space

\mathcal{U}
to a convex space
\mathcal{X}
.

Modern machine-learning objectives are highly non-convex in their original parameterization. Nevertheless, many problems admit an equivalent hidden representation where the objective becomes convex or strongly convex
[

. Based on this viewpoint, we impose the following assumption.

Assumption 1

(Hidden-space regularity)
.

Assume there exists a bijection
g:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{X}
such that
h_{m}(u)=f_{m}(g(u))
for each
m\in[M]
. Each
f_{m}
is twice continuously differentiable on an open set containing
\mathcal{X}
and is
\mu
-strongly convex on
\mathcal{X}
. The hidden PS
{\mathcal{X}_{\rm PF}}=g(\mathcal{U}_{\rm PF})
lies in a compact subset
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{X}
such that each
\nabla f_{m}
, is
l_{f,1}
-Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathcal{K}
.

This assumption is provable in a range of settings, including nonlinear least squares
[
68
,
27
]
, power control and optimal doping profile problems
[
11
]
, system-level synthesis in control
[
3
]
, revenue management and inventory control
[
20
,
21
]
, and reinforcement learning
[
107
,
81
]
, which we revisit as a concrete example in Section

.

Denote
\mathbf{x}=g(\mathbf{u})
,
\mathcal{X}=g(\mathcal{U})
, and
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}):=[f_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,f_{M}(\mathbf{x})]^{\top}
, then MOO in (
1
) admits the hidden-space form
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}~\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})
.
Hidden strong convexity
[
31
]
yield that for any weight
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{M}
, there is
\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\mathbf{w}}=g(\mathbf{u}^{*}_{\mathbf{w}})
where

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\big\{\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{w}):=\textstyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}w_{m}f_{m}(\mathbf{x})\big\}.

(3)

Thus, the original problem (
1
) and the hidden formulation
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}~\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})
are equivalent in the sense of PS and PF. Formally,
\mathcal{X}_{\rm PF}=g(\mathcal{U}_{\rm PF})
and
\mathcal{F}_{\rm PF}=\{\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{\rm PF}\}

Furthermore, the hidden (strong) convexity in Assumption

ensures unique scalarized solutions, full exploration of the Pareto front, and a well-defined Pareto-front path.
This is summarized in Proposition

Proposition 1

(
[

[

.

Under Assumption
1
,given
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{M}
, solution (
3

Proposition

In the bi-objective case

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w}):=\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*})=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{M},\quad\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{M}.

(4)

Instantiation: bi-objective reinforcement learning and bandits

In the bi-objective case, we identify

{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w):={\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}([w,1-w]^{\top})

Assumption 2

(Constraint Regularity)
.

Assume
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{c}_{1}(\mathbf{x})\leq 0,\mathbf{c}_{2}(\mathbf{x})=0\}

\mathbf{c}_{1}(\mathbf{x})

\{\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}:w\in[0,1]\}

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the weight-to-PF map

is differentiable and has no reverse direction (see Appendix

varies. Define

\displaystyle v(w):=\Big\|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w)\Big\|,\quad s(w):=\int_{0}^{w}v(p)dp,\quad\Phi(w):=\frac{s(w)}{s(1)},\quad w\in[0,1].

(5)

Here,
\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w)
represents the velocity of the PF traversal with respect to
w
, and

is the corresponding speed, which generally varies with

1

The function

accumulates the distance traveled along the PF as the weight moves from

to
w
.
The normalized
\Phi(w)
is the fraction of the total PF arc length traversed up to weight

and is

-Lipschitz-smooth (see Appendix

). Moreover, for

drawn uniformly from

is the CDF of the normalized distribution of the induced solutions on the PF.

To ensure non-vanishing PF traversal speed, we impose the following nondegeneracy assumption.

Assumption 3

(Nonzero trade-off)
.

For each
w\in[0,1]
, let
J_{w}
denote the Jacobian matrix of the active constraints of the scalarized problem
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\operatorname{LS}_{f}(\mathbf{x};w)
at
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
. Assume there exists some constant

such that
\big\|\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}\big(\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big)\big\|\geq c
for all

.

Assumption

rules out zero trade-offs along feasible directions, which would prevent changes in the scalarization weight from moving the solution along the PF. Such nondegeneracy-type conditions are standard in trade-off analysis and PF continuation analyses
[
29
,
10
]
. This condition ensures that
\Phi
is invertible. More discussion is deferred to Appendix
C.2
.

Lemma 1

(Bounded PF traversal speed)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
,

. Therefore, given a budget of

\displaystyle\sqrt{(1-w)^{2}+w^{2}}\Big|(R_{1}-R_{2})^{\top}\big(\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}-\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}J^{\top}(J\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}J^{\top})^{-1}J\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}\big)(R_{1}-R_{2})\Big|.

where
\widetilde{H}_{w}=\beta\big(\operatorname{Diag}(\tfrac{1}{\mathbf{x}^{*}_{w}})-E\operatorname{Diag}(\tfrac{1}{E^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{*}_{w}})E^{\top}\big)+cJ^{\top}J,c>0
is constraint augmented hessian.
When
|\mathcal{S}|=1
, this reduces to the entropic bandit case where the LS solution admits the closed form
\mathbf{u}_{w}^{*}=\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=\operatorname{SoftMax}\left(R_{0}+\beta^{-1}(wR_{1}+(1-w)R_{2})\right)\in\Delta_{A}^{\circ}
,
and the speed simplifies to

\displaystyle v(w)=

\displaystyle\beta^{-1}\sqrt{(1-w)^{2}+w^{2}}(R_{1}-R_{2})^{\top}\big(\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf{u}_{w}^{*})-\mathbf{u}_{w}^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{w}^{*})^{\top}\big)(R_{1}-R_{2}).

(9)

The proof of Proposition
2
is deferred to Appendix

.
Proposition
2
shows that
\Phi
can be established once the solutions
\{\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\}_{w\in[0,1]}
are known, while constructing
\Phi
is meant to identify a finite set of weights
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
to save the budget for finding the corresponding solutions.
This creates a
coupled dependence
between geometry estimation on the PF and scalarized optimization over
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
.
Fortunately, this issue disappears when
|\mathcal{S}|=1
, where
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
, and hence
v(w)
and
\Phi(w)
, are available in closed form. Examples include the best-arm identification (BAI) problem in the bandit setting
[
12
,
48
]
,
where each arm
a\in\mathcal{A}
has an unknown mean reward vector
[R_{1}(a),R_{2}(a)]^{\top}
. While BAI for scalarized rewards is well studied
[
4
,
101
,
22
]
, PF coverage remains less explored
[
37
]
. Proposition
2
then yields the following instantiation of the plug-in PF-aware weighting Rule
1
.

Rule 1 example
.

For the bandit problem, estimate reward vector
\hat{R}_{1},\hat{R}_{2}
to construct
\hat{v}(\cdot;\hat{R}_{1},\hat{R}_{2})
by replacing
(R_{1},R_{2})
with

in (

\displaystyle\hat{s}(w;\hat{R}_{1},\hat{R}_{2})=\int_{0}^{w}\hat{v}(u;\hat{R}_{1},\hat{R}_{2})du,\quad\hat{\Phi}(w;\hat{R}_{1},\hat{R}_{2})=\frac{\hat{s}(w;\hat{R}_{1},\hat{R}_{2})}{\hat{s}(1;\hat{R}_{1},\hat{R}_{2})}.

(10)

Then, apply Rule
1
with

This closed form makes the PF geometry explicit and provides a tractable testbed for the geometry behind SURF, as visualized in Figure

is a consistent estimator of

. Specifically,
\|\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\|_{\infty}
decays at rate

and validated empirically in Figure

3
SURF: Algorithm for Sampling Uniformly along the Pareto Front

The previous section shows that Rule
1
achieves uniform PF coverage when
\Phi
or its estimate (cf. Rule
1 example
) is available. In general, however,
\Phi
is not explicitly known. To apply Rule
2
in this setting, we propose SURF, an iterative refinement algorithm for
S
ampling
U
niformly along the Pa
r
eto
F
ront (SURF).
At a high level, SURF refines the estimate of
\Phi
while improving the scalarized solutions associated with the target weights
w_{n}=\Phi^{-1}(n/N)
.

Specifically, at outer iteration
t
, SURF uses the current CDF estimate
\Phi_{t}
to allocate weights
w_{n}^{(t)}=\Phi_{t}^{-1}\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)
with initialization
\Phi_{0}(w)=w
to start with the standard uniform weighting baseline. If
\Phi_{t}=\Phi
, then
w_{n}^{(t)}
would produce uniform PF coverage. For each sampled weight
w_{n}^{(t)}
, SURF then approximately solves the corresponding scalarized subproblem

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)}\approx\arg\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}}\operatorname{LS}_{h}(\mathbf{u};w_{n}^{(t)})\quad{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\text{or equivalently }\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}\approx\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\operatorname{LS}_{f}(\mathbf{x};w_{n}^{(t)})}.

(11)

Here, any off-the-shelf
InnerSolver
can be used, depending on the application, e.g., Adam for loss minimization
[
49
]
and PPO for RL
[
82
]
. Importantly,
InnerSolver
does not need to achieve high precision. Rather, finite
K
-step updates with warm-start from the previous iterate
\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t-1)}
suffice to achieve fast convergence. The resulting PF estimates
\{\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\}_{n=0}^{N}

or equivalently
\{\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)})\}_{n=0}^{N}
are then used to build estimates for
s
at the sampled weights
\{w_{n}^{(t)}\}_{n=0}^{N}
via cumulative chord length

\displaystyle\tilde{s}^{(t)}(w_{0}^{(t)})

\displaystyle:=0,~\tilde{s}^{(t)}(w_{n+1}^{(t)}):=\tilde{s}^{(t)}(w_{n}^{(t)})+\big\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\big\|,\quad n=0,\cdots,N-1.

(12)

Then, SURF interpolates these values to construct
\tilde{s}^{(t)}
on
[0,1]
, for example, Piecewise Cubic Hermite Interpolating Polynomial (PCHIP)
[
36
]
. Compared with naive piecewise-linear interpolation, PCHIP remains simple to implement, is available in standard numerical libraries, preserves monotonicity, and provides more favorable Lipschitz-smoothness properties, as discussed in the next section. These features are useful both in practice and in the convergence analysis.
In this way, an empirical estimate of
\Phi
is established via
\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w):=\frac{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(w)}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)}
and
SURF then updates the CDF estimate via

\Phi_{t+1}(w)=\alpha\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w)+(1-\alpha)\Phi_{t}(w),

(13)

where a damping factor
\alpha
is especially useful for inexact
InnerSolver
to stabilize the update.

Convergence analysis.

We analyze Algorithm
1
by separating two sources of error: the
discretization error
from reconstructing
s
using only
(N+1)
PF samples, and the
optimization error
from solving the scalarized subproblems (
11
) only approximately.
We start with the exact-
InnerSolver
case that isolates the discretization effect: even when the scalarized solutions are exact,
\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)}=\mathbf{u}^{*}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}

or equivalently
\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}=\mathbf{x}^{*}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}
, estimating the PF geometry from only
(N+1)
sampled points introduces an inevitable finite-sampling floor.
We then consider the optimization error induced by running
InnerSolver
for only
K
steps. To quantify this perturbation, we characterize the convergence rate of
InnerSolver
via
\eta_{K}
, a
K
-step contraction factor such that, for any weight
w\in[0,1]
and initialization
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
, its output
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{K}
satisfies
\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{K}-\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\|\leq\eta_{K}\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\|
, where
\eta_{K}\downarrow 0
as
K\to\infty
.
For linearly convergent
InnerSolver
, e.g.,
K
-step projected gradient descent
[
31
]
, one may take
\eta_{K}=\bigl(1-{\mu}/{l_{f,1}}\bigr)^{K}
, where
l_{f,1}
is valid along the entire projected gradient descent trajectory; see Appendix
D.2
.
For sublinearly convergent methods, hidden strong convexity converts objective-gap or stationarity guarantees into hidden-space distance bounds
[
31
]
, so for sufficiently large
K
the same characterization can be instantiated with
\eta_{K}=\mathcal{O}(K^{-p})
for some
p>0
. The following theorem shows the convergence of SURF to the finite-sampling floor, with an additional coupled term accounting for inner optimization error, where we
let
C(N)
to be a scalar depending on
N
, denote
\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\sup_{w\in[0,1]}\left|\Phi_{t}(w)-\Phi(w)\right|
and define the convergence measure to be

\mathcal{E}_{t}\big(\Phi_{t},\{w_{n}^{(t)}\},\{\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}\}\big):=\underbrace{\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}}_{\text{CDF estimation error}}+C(N)\underbrace{\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|}_{\text{inner optimization error}}.

(14)

Theorem 1

(Convergence of SURF)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
hold. Then, Algorithm
1
generates
\{\Phi_{t}\}
and
\{\tilde{\Phi}_{t}\}
that are uniformly
l_{\Phi_{\rm iter},1}
-Lipschitz-smooth for all
t\geq 0
. Let
\kappa:=\frac{4\sqrt{2}v_{\max}}{v_{\min}}\sqrt{l_{\Phi_{\rm iter},1}+l_{\Phi,1}}
, and
N=\Omega(\kappa^{1.5})
.
For general
InnerSolver
with budget specified in Remark
1
, SURF with
\alpha\leq 0.5
admits
C(N)=\Theta(N)
such that, for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}\big(\Phi_{t},\{w_{n}^{(t)}\},\{\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}\}\big)\leq\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{t}\mathcal{E}_{0}\big(\Phi_{0},\{w_{n}^{(0)}\},\{\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(0)}\}\big)+\underbrace{\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})}_{\text{discretization floor}}.

(15)

Remark 1

(Inner-solver budget)
.

If
InnerSolver
returns exact solutions, then the inner optimization error times
C(N)
is zero.
Otherwise, consider an
InnerSolver
with
K
-step contraction factor
\eta_{K}
. We choose
K
so that
\eta_{K}=\mathcal{O}\big({s(1)}/{N}\big)
, that is
K=\Omega\big(\log({Nv_{\max}}{v_{\min}^{-2}})\big)
for linear convergent solver and
K=\Omega\big(\big({N}/{s(1)}\big)^{1/p}\big)
for sublinearly convergent one with
\eta_{K}=\mathcal{O}(K^{-p})
for some
p>0
.
Then the inner-solver perturbs the CDF reconstruction by a summation of
N
chord length error, so
C(N)=\Theta(N)
is the natural amplification factor.
Alternatively, one may use a small outer stepsize
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
, in which
\eta_{K}=\mathcal{O}(1)
is enough after a warmup that ensures
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(0)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(0)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}\left({v_{\min}^{2}}v_{\max}^{-1}N^{-1}\right)
, as detailed in Theorem
4
in Appendix
E.2
.

The proof of Theorem
1
and the constants in
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
and
\Omega(\cdot)
are made explicit in Appendix
E
.
Theorem
1
guarantees that SURF drives the CDF estimation error to an
\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
finite-sampling discretization floor, matching the standard second-order accuracy of polyline-based reconstruction of smooth curves
[
88
]
. In contrast, even with an exact
InnerSolver
, the first-step reconstruction
\tilde{\Phi}_{0}
can carry an
\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{3}N^{-2})
error, where the
\kappa
factor comes from uneven clustering on the PF, as theoretically discussed in Lemma
10
in Appendix
E.1
and empirically verified in Appendix
F.1
. In Appendix
E.3
, we further relate CDF reconstruction error to a PF uniformity metric measuring the relative deviation of sampled PF segments from equal spacing,
Coefficient of Variation
(CV)
[
30
]
, as

\text{CV}:=\tfrac{\mathrm{std}\big(\{\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\|\}_{n=0}^{N-1}\big)}{\mathrm{mean}\big(\{\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\|\}_{n=0}^{N-1}\big)}=\mathcal{O}\big(N^{-1}+N\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\big)=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1}),

(16)

at sufficiently large outer iteration
t=\Omega(\log N)
. Equation (
16
) links CDF refinement accuracy to PF spacing uniformity, showing that SURF improves uniform PF coverage.

Remark
1
illustrates that SURF does not require each scalarized subproblem to be solved to high precision at every outer iteration. To achieve
\|\Phi_{T}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
with a linearly convergent inner solver
[
31
]
, SURF requires
T=\mathcal{O}(\log N)
outer iterations and per-iteration inner complexity
\mathcal{O}(N\log N)
, giving total complexity
\mathcal{O}(N(\log N)^{2})
. At the same time, SURF yields
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(T)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(T)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(N^{-3})
. In contrast, uniform weighting requires
\mathcal{O}(N\log(N^{3}))=\mathcal{O}(N\log N)
total complexity for
N
to reach the same inner accuracy, while providing no uniform PF-coverage guarantee. Alternatively, the small-stepsize regime
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
in Remark
1
relaxes the required inner-solver contraction, making it especially suitable for sublinearly convergent
InnerSolver
.
In practice, with a moderate choice of
N=5
and
\alpha=0.3
in the LLM alignment task in Section
4
, SURF achieves efficiency comparable to the uniform scalarization baseline under the same training budget, while significantly improving PF uniformity.

5
Conclusion

This paper introduced SURF, a practical framework for sampling uniformly along the Pareto front while retaining the simplicity and scalability of linear scalarization.
One of our key contributions is a geometric view of scalarization, where scalarization not only produces individual Pareto points but also induces a traversal of the PF with generally non-uniform speed.
By characterizing this weight-to-PF map through the arc-length CDF
\Phi
, we obtain a principled correction from uniform weights to uniform PF coverage.
For structured bandit settings, we derive explicit forms of this geometry, and for general problems, we propose an iterative CDF-refinement algorithm whose outer iterations converge to a discretization floor while remaining compatible with standard
InnerSolver
.
Empirically, SURF improves PF coverage across optimization, reinforcement learning, and LLM alignment settings,
suggesting that arc-length-aware scalarization is a simple and broadly applicable tool for constructing uniformly-distributed Pareto front approximations.

J_{w}^{\top}
is orthogonal to
\ker(J_{w})
and hence, for
w\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}\big(\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-w}\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})=-\frac{1}{w}\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}).

Therefore, Assumption
3
is equivalent to requiring
\|\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\|\geq c(1-w)
, or
\|\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\|\geq cw
, with endpoint cases interpreted by one-sided limits.

Geometrically, Assumption
3
rules out points where the two objectives are first-order identical along feasible directions, which would make the PF traversal speed vanish. Such degeneracy is non-generic and typically arises only from special symmetry or gradient alignment. Similar regularity conditions are standard in sensitivity and geometric analyses of PSs/fronts, where Lipschitz-smooth structure is derived from multiplier regularity, rank conditions, or Lipschitz-smooth path-following assumptions
[
42
,
10
]
. Our condition serves the same purpose, but is tailored to arc-length analysis by directly enforcing a uniformly nonzero tangent trade-off.

We now use this tangent-space separation to prove the uniform lower and upper bounds on the PF traversal speed.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
, Lemma
2
gives that
w\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
and
{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w)
are differentiable on
w\in[0,1]
. By Assumption
1
, the solution path
\{\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}:w\in[0,1]\}
lies in the compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal{X})
, and
\nabla f_{1},\nabla f_{2}
are
l_{f,1}
-Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathcal{K}
. Together with the fixed active set and LICQ in Assumption
2
, standard sensitivity results imply that
w\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
is continuously differentiable on
[0,1]
up to one-sided derivatives at the endpoints. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w)=

\displaystyle J_{f}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}}{\partial w}

(43)

is continuous on
[0,1]
. Since
[0,1]
is compact,
v_{\max}:=\sup_{w\in[0,1]}\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w)\right\|<\infty
.

To derive the lower bound, fix any
w\in[0,1]
. Let
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}
denote the optimal Lagrange multipliers of the scalarized problem
\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\operatorname{LS}_{f}(x;w)
, and let
\mathbf{c}
denote the active constraint functions associated with the locally fixed active index set under Assumption
2
. The local KKT system at
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
is

\displaystyle\underbrace{w\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})+(1-w)\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})}_{\mathrm{I}(w)}+\underbrace{J_{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}}_{\mathrm{II}(w)}=0,\quad\underbrace{\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})}_{\mathrm{III}(w)}=0,

(44)

where
J_{w}:=\nabla\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})
is the Jacobian of the active constraints at
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
.

Next, differentiate the stationarity condition in (
44
). For the first term
\mathrm{I}(w)
, there is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathrm{I}(w)=

\displaystyle\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})+\big(w\nabla^{2}f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})+(1-w)\nabla^{2}f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}.

(45)

For the second term
\mathrm{II}(w)
, using
J_{w}=\nabla\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})
, there is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathrm{II}(w)=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\big(\nabla\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})^{\top}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{xx}^{2}\big((\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*})^{\top}\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}+J_{w}^{\top}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}.

(46)

Since the active index set is locally fixed, the constraint map
\mathbf{c}
itself does not depend on
w
explicitly, but only through
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
. Differentiating the feasibility condition
\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})=0
with respect to
w
therefore gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathrm{III}(w)=

\displaystyle J_{w}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=0.

(47)

Hence,
\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\in\ker(J_{w})
. Combining the above yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}+(\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}))+J_{w}^{\top}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}=0,\quad J_{w}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=0,

(48)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{w}=\nabla_{xx}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{w}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*},\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*})=w\nabla^{2}f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})+(1-w)\nabla^{2}f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})+\nabla_{xx}^{2}\big((\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*})^{\top}\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big).

(49)

Let
\Pi_{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
denote the orthogonal projection matrix onto the tangent space
\ker(J_{w})
. That is, for every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}(u)=\Pi_{w}u
. Since
\ker(J_{w})
is a linear subspace, such matrix
\Pi_{w}
exists and is uniquely defined. Moreover, by the nature of projection,
\|\Pi_{w}u\|\leq\|u\|
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Additionally, let
Q_{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r_{w}}
be a matrix whose columns form an orthonormal basis of the tangent space
\ker(J_{w})
, i.e.,
\Pi_{w}=Q_{w}Q_{w}^{\top}
. Here, denote
r_{w}=\dim(\ker(J_{w}))
, there is
Q_{w}^{\top}Q_{w}=I_{r_{w}}
.

Since (
48
) implies
J_{w}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=0
, we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\in\ker(J_{w})
. Therefore, there exists a vector
p_{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{w}}
such that
\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=Q_{w}p_{w}
.

Premultiplying the first equation in (
48
) by
Q_{w}^{\top}
gives

\displaystyle Q_{w}^{\top}\mathcal{H}_{w}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}+Q_{w}^{\top}(\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}))+Q_{w}^{\top}J_{w}^{\top}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}=0.

(50)

Since the columns of
Q_{w}
span
\ker(J_{w})
, we have
J_{w}Q_{w}=0
, and hence
Q_{w}^{\top}J_{w}^{\top}=(J_{w}Q_{w})^{\top}=0
. Substituting
\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=Q_{w}p_{w}
yields

\displaystyle Q_{w}^{\top}\mathcal{H}_{w}Q_{w}p_{w}=

\displaystyle-Q_{w}^{\top}(\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})).

(51)

Denote the reduced Hessian
R_{w}:=Q_{w}^{\top}\mathcal{H}_{w}Q_{w}
. Then
p_{w}=-R_{w}^{-1}Q_{w}^{\top}(\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}))
, and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=

\displaystyle-Q_{w}R_{w}^{-1}Q_{w}^{\top}(\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})).

(52)

The inverse above is well defined. Indeed, by Assumption
1
, both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
\mu
-strongly convex, and by Assumption
2
the active inequality constraints are convex with nonnegative multipliers. Hence the Lagrangian Hessian satisfies
\mathcal{H}_{w}\succeq\mu I
. Therefore, for every
q\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{w}}
,

\displaystyle q^{\top}R_{w}q=

\displaystyle q^{\top}Q_{w}^{\top}\mathcal{H}_{w}Q_{w}q=(Q_{w}q)^{\top}\mathcal{H}_{w}(Q_{w}q)\geq\mu\|Q_{w}q\|^{2}=\mu\|q\|^{2}.

(53)

So
R_{w}\succeq\mu I
, and in particular
R_{w}
is positive definite and invertible.

Now let
g_{w}:=\nabla f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\nabla f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\big(f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big)\right|=\left|g_{w}^{\top}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\right|=\left|-g_{w}^{\top}Q_{w}R_{w}^{-1}Q_{w}^{\top}g_{w}\right|=(Q_{w}^{\top}g_{w})^{\top}R_{w}^{-1}(Q_{w}^{\top}g_{w}).

(54)

Since the path lies in the compact set
\mathcal{K}
and
\nabla f_{m}
is
l_{f,1}
-Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathcal{K}
, the objective Hessians are uniformly bounded along the path. Together with the fixed active set and Lipschitz-smooth active constraints, there exists
L_{H}<\infty
such that
\lambda_{\max}(R_{w})\leq L_{H}
for all
w\in[0,1]
. Hence,
R_{w}^{-1}\succeq\frac{1}{L_{H}}I
, and (
54
) implies

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\big(f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big)\right|\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L_{H}}\|Q_{w}^{\top}g_{w}\|^{2}.

(55)

Finally, since
Q_{w}Q_{w}^{\top}=\Pi_{w}
is the orthogonal projection matrix onto
\ker(J_{w})
,

\displaystyle\|Q_{w}^{\top}g_{w}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle g_{w}^{\top}Q_{w}Q_{w}^{\top}g_{w}=g_{w}^{\top}\Pi_{w}g_{w}=\|\Pi_{w}g_{w}\|^{2}=\|\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}(g_{w})\|^{2}.

(56)

By Assumption
3
,
\|\operatorname{Proj}_{\ker(J_{w})}(g_{w})\|\geq c
. Combining this with (
55
) gives

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\big(f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big)\right|\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}}{L_{H}}.

(57)

Therefore, using
\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\geq|a-b|/\sqrt{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w)\right\|=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\big(f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-f_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})\big)\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}}{\sqrt{2}L_{H}}.

(58)

In this way, the invertibility part follows directly from the fundamental theorem of calculus.
∎

C.3
Extension to the Multi-objective Setting

Smoothness of the PF.

We next note that the Lipschitz-smoothness result extends naturally to the general
M
-objective setting, where the scalarization weight lies on the simplex
\Delta_{M}
. The following lemma shows that LS induces a Lipschitz-smooth PF map, and that the PF point moves in a monotone direction along feasible pairwise directions of the simplex.

Lemma 3

(Generalized version of Lemma
2
)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
hold. Then, for every
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{M}
, the scalarized problem (
3
) has a unique minimizer
\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*}
. Moreover, the mappings

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*},\quad\mathbf{w}\mapsto{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*})=[{f_{\rm PF}}_{1}(\mathbf{w}),\dots,{f_{\rm PF}}_{M}(\mathbf{w})]^{\top}

(59)

are continuously differentiable on
\Delta_{M}
in the relative sense, with one-sided directional derivatives on the boundary. In addition,
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*}
is Lipschitz-continuous, and
{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}
is
l_{{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}},1}
-Lipschitz-smooth on
\Delta_{M}
.
Additionally, fix any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,M\}
with
i\neq j
, and any
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{M}
such that
\mathbf{w}+t(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\in\Delta_{M}
for all sufficiently small
t\geq 0
. Then, for all such
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{f_{\rm PF}}_{i}\bigl(\mathbf{w}+t(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\bigr)-{f_{\rm PF}}_{i}(\mathbf{w})\leq{f_{\rm PF}}_{j}\bigl(\mathbf{w}+t(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\bigr)-{f_{\rm PF}}_{j}(\mathbf{w}).

(60)

Consequently,
\bigl[D{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\bigr]_{i}\leq\bigl[D{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\bigr]_{j}
.

Proof.

The differentiability of
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*}
,
\mathbf{w}\mapsto{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*})=[{f_{\rm PF}}_{1}(\mathbf{w}),\dots,{f_{\rm PF}}_{M}(\mathbf{w})]^{\top}
, and the Lipschitz-smoothness of
{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}
follows the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
2
.
It remains to show the directional trade-off inequality. Fix
i\neq j
,
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{M}
, and
t>0
such that
\tilde{\mathbf{w}}:=\mathbf{w}+t(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\in\Delta_{M}
. Then
\tilde{w}_{i}=w_{i}+t
,
\tilde{w}_{j}=w_{j}-t
, and
\tilde{w}_{k}=w_{k}
for
k\neq i,j
. By optimality of
\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*}
for weight
\mathbf{w}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}w_{m}f_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*})\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}w_{m}f_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}^{*}).

(61)

Similarly, by optimality of
\mathbf{x}_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}^{*}
for weight
\tilde{\mathbf{w}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\tilde{w}_{m}f_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}^{*})\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\tilde{w}_{m}f_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*}).

(62)

Adding (
61
) and (
62
) and canceling common terms yields

\displaystyle t\Big(f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}^{*})-f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*})\Big)-t\Big(f_{j}(\mathbf{x}_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}^{*})-f_{j}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}}^{*})\Big)\leq 0.

(63)

Since
t>0
, this gives

\displaystyle{f_{\rm PF}}_{i}(\tilde{\mathbf{w}})-{f_{\rm PF}}_{i}(\mathbf{w})\leq{f_{\rm PF}}_{j}(\tilde{\mathbf{w}})-{f_{\rm PF}}_{j}(\mathbf{w}).

(64)

Finally, dividing by
t
and letting
t\downarrow 0
gives

\displaystyle\bigl[D{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\bigr]_{i}\leq\bigl[D{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\bigr]_{j}.

(65)

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma
3
shows that the scalarization-induced PF map remains smooth in the multiple-objective case. We next discuss how the bi-objective speed and CDF generalize to a surface-area density on the simplex.

Generalization of
v
,
s
, and
\Phi
.

In the general
M
-objective setting, the weight
\mathbf{w}
lies on the
(M-1)
-dimensional simplex
\Delta_{M}
, so LS induces an
(M-1)
-dimensional surface on the PF rather than a curve. Let
D{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})
denote the Jacobian of
{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}
with respect to local coordinates on
\Delta_{M}
. The natural geometric analogue of the bi-objective speed is the Jacobian-induced surface-area density

\displaystyle v(\mathbf{w}):=\sqrt{\det\!\big(D{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})^{\top}D{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(\mathbf{w})\big)}.

(66)

When
M=2
,
\Delta_{2}
is one-dimensional and (
66
) reduces to the usual PF traversal speed in (
5
). For
M>2
,
v(\mathbf{w})
plays the role of a surface-area density on the simplex, and its normalization induces uniform surface-area coverage of the PF.

This also gives a natural multivariate analogue of the arc-length CDF. Since
\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{M}
has only
M-1
degrees of freedom, we use the first
M-1
coordinates and write
w_{M}=1-\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}w_{m}
. For
(w_{1},\dots,w_{M-1})
satisfying
w_{m}\geq 0
and
\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}w_{m}\leq 1
, define

\displaystyle\Phi(w_{1},\dots,w_{M-1}):=\frac{\int_{\{\mathbf{z}\in\Delta_{M}:0\leq z_{m}\leq w_{m},\ m=1,\dots,M-1\}}v(\mathbf{z})\,d\mathbf{z}}{\int_{\Delta_{M}}v(\mathbf{z})\,d\mathbf{z}}.

(67)

Equivalently,
\Phi
is the multivariate CDF associated with the surface-area density
v(\mathbf{w})
under the coordinate chart
(w_{1},\dots,w_{M-1})
. Thus, the bi-objective CDF refinement principle extends conceptually to the multi-objective case by replacing arc-length density with surface-area density on the simplex. Developing an efficient multi-dimensional refinement procedure, for example, through multivariate CDF estimation, transport maps, or resampling on
\Delta_{M}
, is beyond the scope of this paper and left for future work.

C.4
Closed-form
\Phi
for Quadratic Problems

We next give a simple quadratic example where the scalarization path and the corresponding arc-length CDF can be computed explicitly. This example illustrates the geometric mechanism behind SURF in a setting where all quantities are available in closed form.

n
-dimensional quadratic bi-objective problem.

Consider the unconstrained bi-objective problem on
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\\
f_{2}(\mathbf{x})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}_{1})^{\top}Q_{1}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}_{1})\\
(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}_{2})^{\top}Q_{2}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}_{2})\end{bmatrix},\quad Q_{1},Q_{2}\succ 0.

(68)

For each scalarization weight
w\in[0,1]
,
\operatorname{LS}_{f}(\mathbf{x};w)=wf_{1}(\mathbf{x})+(1-w)f_{2}(\mathbf{x})
is strongly convex and it admits the unique minimizer

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=\bigl(wQ_{1}+(1-w)Q_{2}\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(wQ_{1}\mathbf{b}_{1}+(1-w)Q_{2}\mathbf{b}_{2}\bigr),

(69)

and the PS is
{\mathcal{X}_{\rm PF}}=\{\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}:w\in[0,1]\}
.
Let

\displaystyle\Sigma(w):=wQ_{1}+(1-w)Q_{2},\quad P(w):=\Sigma(w)^{-1}wQ_{1},\quad\mathbf{d}:=\mathbf{b}_{1}-\mathbf{b}_{2}.

(70)

Then (
69
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=P(w)\mathbf{b}_{1}+\bigl(I-P(w)\bigr)\mathbf{b}_{2}=\mathbf{b}_{2}+P(w)\mathbf{d}.

(71)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}-\mathbf{b}_{1}=-\bigl(I-P(w)\bigr)\mathbf{d},\quad\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}-\mathbf{b}_{2}=P(w)\mathbf{d},

(72)

and the induced PF manifold is

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}(w):=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{d}^{\top}\bigl(I-P(w)\bigr)^{\top}Q_{1}\bigl(I-P(w)\bigr)\mathbf{d}\\
\mathbf{d}^{\top}P(w)^{\top}Q_{2}P(w)\mathbf{d}\end{bmatrix},\quad w\in[0,1].

(73)

Differentiating
P(w)=\Sigma(w)^{-1}wQ_{1}
gives

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(w)=\Sigma(w)^{-1}\Bigl(Q_{1}\bigl(I-P(w)\bigr)+Q_{2}P(w)\Bigr).

(74)

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}(w)=\begin{bmatrix}-2\mathbf{d}^{\top}\bigl(I-P(w)\bigr)^{\top}Q_{1}P^{\prime}(w)\mathbf{d}\\
\phantom{-}2\mathbf{d}^{\top}P(w)^{\top}Q_{2}P^{\prime}(w)\mathbf{d}\end{bmatrix}.

(75)

Using (
74
), the PF traversal speed satisfies

\displaystyle v(w)

\displaystyle:=\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}(w)\right\|=2\left\|\begin{bmatrix}-\mathbf{d}^{\top}\bigl(I-P(w)\bigr)^{\top}Q_{1}\Sigma(w)^{-1}\bigl(Q_{1}(I-P(w))+Q_{2}P(w)\bigr)\mathbf{d}\\
\phantom{-}\mathbf{d}^{\top}P(w)^{\top}Q_{2}\Sigma(w)^{-1}\bigl(Q_{1}(I-P(w))+Q_{2}P(w)\bigr)\mathbf{d}\end{bmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle\propto\sqrt{(m_{1}(w)+m_{3}(w))^{2}+(m_{2}(w)+m_{3}(w))^{2}},

(76)

where

\displaystyle m_{1}(w)

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{d}^{\top}(I-P(w))^{\top}Q_{1}\Sigma(w)^{-1}Q_{1}(I-P(w))\mathbf{d},

\displaystyle m_{2}(w)

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{d}^{\top}P(w)^{\top}Q_{2}\Sigma(w)^{-1}Q_{2}P(w)\mathbf{d},

\displaystyle m_{3}(w)

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{d}^{\top}P(w)^{\top}Q_{2}\Sigma(w)^{-1}Q_{1}(I-P(w))\mathbf{d}.

Thus, in general,
v(w)
is non-constant, so uniform sampling of
w
does not produce uniform PF coverage. Accordingly,

\displaystyle\Phi(w)=\frac{\int_{0}^{w}\sqrt{(m_{1}(u)+m_{3}(u))^{2}+(m_{2}(u)+m_{3}(u))^{2}}du}{\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{(m_{1}(u)+m_{3}(u))^{2}+(m_{2}(u)+m_{3}(u))^{2}}du}.

(77)

Thus, Rule
1
applies directly in this example.
\Phi
and its inverse can be computed numerically in a straightforward manner, and the PF-aware weights are then given by
w_{n}=\Phi^{-1}(n/N)
.

1-dimensional quadratic bi-objective problem.

When
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
is reduced to the one-dimensional case
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(x)\\
f_{2}(x)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}(x-b_{1})^{2}\\
q_{2}(x-b_{2})^{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad q_{1},q_{2}>0,\quad b_{1}\neq b_{2}.

(78)

For each
w\in[0,1]
, the scalarized objective

\operatorname{LS}_{f}(x;w)=wf_{1}(x)+(1-w)f_{2}(x)

is strongly convex and admits the unique minimizer

\displaystyle x_{w}^{*}=\frac{wq_{1}b_{1}+(1-w)q_{2}b_{2}}{wq_{1}+(1-w)q_{2}},

(79)

so the PS is

\mathcal{X}_{\rm PF}=\{x_{w}^{*}:w\in[0,1]\}=[\min\{b_{1},b_{2}\},\max\{b_{1},b_{2}\}].

The induced PF manifold is

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}(w)=\mathbf{f}(x_{w}^{*})=\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}q_{2}^{2}\dfrac{(1-w)^{2}(b_{2}-b_{1})^{2}}{(wq_{1}+(1-w)q_{2})^{2}}\\
q_{2}q_{1}^{2}\dfrac{w^{2}(b_{2}-b_{1})^{2}}{(wq_{1}+(1-w)q_{2})^{2}}\end{bmatrix},\quad w\in[0,1].

(80)

Differentiating gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}(w)=\frac{2q_{1}^{2}q_{2}^{2}(b_{2}-b_{1})^{2}}{(wq_{1}+(1-w)q_{2})^{3}}\begin{bmatrix}-(1-w)\\
w\end{bmatrix},

(81)

and hence

\displaystyle v(w):=\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}(w)\right\|\propto\frac{\sqrt{(1-w)^{2}+w^{2}}}{(wq_{1}+(1-w)q_{2})^{3}}.

(82)

Therefore, uniform sampling of
w
does not in general produce uniform PF coverage.

Let
q_{2}/q_{1}>0
. Since the multiplicative constant in (
82
) cancels after normalization,
\Phi
depends on
(q_{1},q_{2})
only through
\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}
:

\displaystyle\Phi(w)=\frac{I(w;\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})}{I(1;\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})},\quad I(w;\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}):=\int_{0}^{w}\frac{\sqrt{(1-u)^{2}+u^{2}}}{(u+(1-u)\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})^{3}}du.

(83)

We now derive a closed form for
I(w;\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})
. First, center the interval by setting

t=2u-1,\quad u=\frac{t+1}{2},\quad du=\frac{dt}{2}.

Then

(1-u)^{2}+u^{2}=\frac{1+t^{2}}{2},\quad u+(1-u)\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}=\frac{(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}+1)-(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}-1)t}{2},

so

\displaystyle I(w;\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})=2\sqrt{2}\int_{-1}^{2w-1}\frac{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}{\bigl((\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}+1)-(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}-1)t\bigr)^{3}}dt.

(84)

When
\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}=1
, the denominator in (
84
) is constant, so

\displaystyle I(w;1)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{-1}^{2w-1}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}dt=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\Bigl[t\sqrt{1+t^{2}}+\operatorname{asinh}(t)\Bigr]_{t=-1}^{2w-1}.

(85)

Hence

\displaystyle\Phi(w)=\frac{(2w-1)\sqrt{2w^{2}-2w+1}+\operatorname{asinh}(2w-1)+\sqrt{2}+\operatorname{asinh}(1)}{2\sqrt{2}+2\operatorname{asinh}(1)},\quad\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}=1.

(86)

When
\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}\neq 1
.
To remove the square root in (
84
), use the standard rationalizing substitution

\displaystyle z=z(t):=\frac{t}{1+\sqrt{1+t^{2}}},\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t=\frac{2z}{1-z^{2}}.

(87)

Under (
87
),

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+t^{2}}=\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z^{2}},\quad dt=\frac{2(1+z^{2})}{(1-z^{2})^{2}}dz.

(88)

Moreover,

\displaystyle(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}+1)-(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}-1)t=\frac{(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}+1)(1-z^{2})-2(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}-1)z}{1-z^{2}}=-\frac{\ell_{r}(z)}{1-z^{2}},

(89)

where

\displaystyle\ell_{r}(z):=(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}+1)z^{2}+2(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}-1)z-(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}+1).

(90)

Substituting these identities into (
84
) yields

\displaystyle I(w;\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})=-4\sqrt{2}\int_{z(-1)}^{z(2w-1)}\frac{(1+z^{2})^{2}}{\ell_{r}(z)^{3}}dz.

(91)

Thus the integral becomes a rational integral. Define
G_{r}
as any antiderivative of

-4\sqrt{2}\frac{(1+z^{2})^{2}}{\ell_{r}(z)^{3}},

so that

\displaystyle I(w;\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})=G_{r}(z(w))-G_{r}(z_{0}),\quad z(w):=\frac{2w-1}{1+\sqrt{1+(2w-1)^{2}}},\quad z_{0}:=z(0)=-(\sqrt{2}-1).

(92)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Phi(w)=\frac{G_{r}(z(w))-G_{r}(z_{0})}{G_{r}(z_{1})-G_{r}(z_{0})},\quad z_{1}:=z(1)=\sqrt{2}-1,\quad\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}\neq 1.

(93)

Finally, by symmetry, swapping the two objectives sends
(\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}},w)
to
(1/\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}},1-w)
. Hence

\displaystyle\Phi(w;\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})=1-\Phi(1-w;1/\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}).

(94)

Therefore, without loss of generality, one may assume
\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}>1
when deriving the closed form.

Collecting the three cases,

\displaystyle\Phi(w)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1-\frac{G_{1/\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}(z(1-w))-G_{1/\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}(z_{0})}{G_{1/\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}(z_{1})-G_{1/\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}(z_{0})},&0<\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}<1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{(2w-1)\sqrt{2w^{2}-2w+1}+\operatorname{asinh}(2w-1)+\sqrt{2}+\operatorname{asinh}(1)}{2\sqrt{2}+2\operatorname{asinh}(1)},&\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}=1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{G_{r}(z(w))-G_{r}(z_{0})}{G_{r}(z_{1})-G_{r}(z_{0})},&\tfrac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}>1,\end{cases}

(95)

where
z(w)
,
z_{0}
,
z_{1}
, and
G_{r}
are defined in (
92
). Thus, Rule
1
applies directly to this example.

D.1
Provable Regularity Condition for Discounted Occupancy Constraints

Lemma 4

(Provable verification of Assumption
2
for RL)
.

Let
E_{(s,a),s^{\prime}}=\mathbf{1}\{s^{\prime}=s\}
,
P_{(s,a),s^{\prime}}=\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)
, and suppose
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Then
E-\gamma P
has full column rank. Equivalently,
(E-\gamma P)^{\top}
has full row rank.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\ker(E-\gamma P)=\{0\}
.
Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
satisfy

(E-\gamma P)y=0.

Then, for every
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
,

y(s)=\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)y(s^{\prime}).

Choose
\bar{s}\in\arg\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}|y(s)|
. Since

\mathcal{P}(\cdot\mid\bar{s},a)
is a probability distribution, for any

a\in\mathcal{A}
,

\|y\|_{\infty}=|y(\bar{s})|\leq\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}\mid\bar{s},a)|y(s^{\prime})|\leq\gamma\|y\|_{\infty}.

Because
0<\gamma<1
, this implies
\|y\|_{\infty}=0
, hence
y=0
.
Therefore
\ker(E-\gamma P)=\{0\}
, so
E-\gamma P
has full column rank.
∎

D.2
Inner Optimization Trajectories
Stay in the Subset
\cal K
in the Relative Interior of
\cal X

For the negative conditional-entropy formulation (
8
), the gradient magnitude diverges as any element of
\mathbf{x}
goes to
0
. Fix some
\mathbf{x}^{(0)}\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal{X})=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}:(E-\gamma P)^{\top}\mathbf{x}=(1-\gamma)\rho\}
as the initialization. Then, for each
w\in[0,1]
, the sublevel set
\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}:\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x};w)\leq\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(0)};w)\}
remains strictly bounded away from the boundary of
\mathcal{X}
. Therefore, we can directly adopt

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}:=\bigcup_{w\in[0,1]}\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}:\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x};w)\leq\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(0)};w)\}

(96)

Since
[0,1]
is compact and
\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x};w)
is continuous in
(\mathbf{x},w)
, it follows that
\mathcal{K}
is a compact subset of
\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal{X})
. Moreover, for any fixed
w\in[0,1]
, projected gradient descent on the policy or occupancy simplex with stepsize
l_{f,1}^{-1}
on the
\mu
-strongly convex and locally
l_{f,1}
-smooth objective at
\mathbf{x}^{(t)}
satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)};w)-\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*};w)\leq\big(1-\mu l_{f,1}^{-1}\big)\big(\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(t)};w)-\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*};w)\big),

(97)

where
0<1-\mu l_{f,1}^{-1}<1

[
31
]
. Hence
\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)};w)\leq\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(t)};w)\leq\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(0)};w)
, so
\mathbf{x}^{(t)}\in\mathcal{K}
for all
t
. By the bijection
h_{m}(\mathbf{u})=f_{m}(g(\mathbf{u}))
and the equivalence between policy-space and occupancy-space optimization
[
81
]
, the same sublevel-set invariance also holds for the corresponding policy-space update
[
31
]
.

For general problems under Assumption
1
, fix
\mathbf{x}^{(0)}\in{\cal K}
, and we can similarly obtain
\cup_{w\in[0,1]}\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}:\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x};w)\leq\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(0)};w)\}\subseteq{\cal K}
. Since
\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x};w)
is continuous in
(\mathbf{x},w)
and
[0,1]
is compact, this level-set union is closed. Moreover,
\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\cdot;w)
is
\mu
-strongly convex for every
w\in[0,1]
, so its sublevel sets are bounded. By compactness of
[0,1]
, the bound is uniform over
w
. Hence
\cup_{w\in[0,1]}\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}:\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x};w)\leq\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x}^{(0)};w)\}
is compact. Therefore, we can obtain
l_{f,1}=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{K},\;w\in[0,1]}\|\nabla^{2}\operatorname{LS}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x};w)\|_{2}<\infty
. Following a similar analysis to (
97
), we can show that
\mathbf{x}^{(t)}
stays in the level-set union that belongs to
\cal K
, where each
f_{m}
remains
l_{f,1}
-smooth on
\cal K
, for all
t
and all
w\in[0,1]
.

D.3
Proof of Proposition
2
: PF Speed in Bi-Objective RL

Proof.

The unique minimizer satisfies
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\in\Delta_{S\times A}^{\circ}

[
66
]
, so only the equality constraint is active. Hence the Lagrangian for
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\operatorname{LS}_{f}(\mathbf{x};w)
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\nu};w)=\operatorname{LS}_{f}(\mathbf{x};w)+\left\langle\boldsymbol{\nu},(E-\gamma P)^{\top}\mathbf{x}-(1-\gamma)\rho\right\rangle.

(98)

The KKT conditions are

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*},\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*};w)=(E-\gamma P)^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}-(1-\gamma)\rho,

(99)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*},\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*};w)=\underbrace{\beta\log\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}-\beta E\log(E^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\beta R_{0}}_{=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\bar{\tau}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})}-\bigl(wR_{1}+(1-w)R_{2}\bigr)+(E-\gamma P)\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}.

(100)

Since
H_{w}
is positive definite on
\ker(J)
and
J=(E-\gamma P)^{\top}
has full row rank by Lemma
4
, standard sensitivity results imply that
w\mapsto(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*},\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*})
is Lipschitz-continuous in
w

[
46
, Theorem 2.16]
and locally continuously differentiable
[
35
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Differentiating (
99
)-(
100
) with respect to
w
yields

\displaystyle J\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=

\displaystyle 0,

(101)

\displaystyle H_{w}\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}+J^{\top}\nabla_{w}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}=

\displaystyle R_{1}-R_{2},

(102)

where
J=(E-\gamma P)^{\top}
and
H_{w}=\beta\bigl(\operatorname{Diag}(1/\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-E\operatorname{Diag}(1/(E^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}))E^{\top}\bigr)
.

Although
H_{w}
is singular on the full space, it is positive definite on the feasible tangent space
\ker(J)
; we therefore use the augmented Hessian
\widetilde{H}_{w}:=H_{w}+cJ^{\top}J
with any fixed
c>0
.

For any
z
, the conditional-entropy Hessian is positive semidefinite:

\displaystyle z^{\top}H_{w}z=

\displaystyle\beta\sum_{s}\left(\sum_{a}\frac{z(s,a)^{2}}{\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}(s,a)}-\frac{\left(\sum_{a}z(s,a)\right)^{2}}{\sum_{a}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}(s,a)}\right)\geq 0.

(103)

Equality in the Cauchy-Schwarz bound requires
z(s,a)=\lambda_{s}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}(s,a)
for all
a
, i.e., only a state-marginal perturbation; by sufficient exploration and the bijective policy–occupancy correspondence, no nonzero such direction lies in
\ker(J)
, so
H_{w}
is positive definite on
\ker(J)
. Therefore, for every
z\neq 0
,

\displaystyle z^{\top}\widetilde{H}_{w}z=

\displaystyle z^{\top}H_{w}z+c\|Jz\|^{2}>0,

(104)

where the case
Jz\neq 0
follows from
c\|Jz\|^{2}>0
, and the case
Jz=0
follows from positive definiteness of
H_{w}
on
\ker(J)
.

Thus
\widetilde{H}_{w}\succ 0
, and since
J
has full row rank,
\widetilde{S}_{w}:=J\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}J^{\top}
is positive definite and invertible.

Because
J\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=0
, replacing
H_{w}
by
\widetilde{H}_{w}
does not change the primal sensitivity equation:

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{w}\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}+J^{\top}\nabla_{w}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}=

\displaystyle H_{w}\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}+cJ^{\top}J\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}+J^{\top}\nabla_{w}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{w}\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}+J^{\top}\nabla_{w}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{1}-R_{2}.

(105)

Therefore, the same
\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
solves the augmented KKT system

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{H}_{w}&J^{\top}\\
J&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\\
\nabla_{w}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}R_{1}-R_{2}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

(106)

Since
\widetilde{H}_{w}\succ 0
and
\widetilde{S}_{w}=J\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}J^{\top}\succ 0
, the standard Schur-complement inverse formula gives

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{H}_{w}&J^{\top}\\
J&0\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}-\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}J^{\top}\widetilde{S}_{w}^{-1}J\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}&\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}J^{\top}\widetilde{S}_{w}^{-1}\\
\widetilde{S}_{w}^{-1}J\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}&-\widetilde{S}_{w}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(107)

Hence,

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=\left(\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}-\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}J^{\top}\widetilde{S}_{w}^{-1}J\widetilde{H}_{w}^{-1}\right)(R_{1}-R_{2}).

(108)

Next, for the bi-objective PF map
{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w)=[{f_{\rm PF}}_{1}(w),{f_{\rm PF}}_{2}(w)]^{\top}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{f_{\rm PF}}_{1}(w)=

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}f_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}),\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\bar{\tau}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-R_{1},\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(1-w)(R_{2}-R_{1})-(E-\gamma P)\boldsymbol{\nu}_{w}^{*},\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-w)\left\langle R_{2}-R_{1},\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\right\rangle,

(109)

where the third equality uses (
100
), and the last equality uses (
101
). Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{f_{\rm PF}}_{2}(w)=w\left\langle R_{1}-R_{2},\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\right\rangle.

(110)

Hence

\displaystyle v(w)=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w)\right\|=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{f_{\rm PF}}_{1}(w)\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}{f_{\rm PF}}_{2}(w)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(1-w)^{2}+w^{2}}\left|(R_{1}-R_{2})^{\top}\nabla_{w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\right|.

(111)

Substituting (
108
) yields the PF speed for bi-objective RL.

Now consider the case
|\mathcal{S}|=1
. Then the discounted occupancy measure coincides with the action distribution, so the feasible set reduces to
\Delta_{A}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{A}:\langle\mathbf{1},\mathbf{x}\rangle=1\}
.
Since
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\beta\langle\mathbf{x},\log\mathbf{x}\rangle
is strictly convex on
\Delta_{A}^{\circ}
and the remaining terms are linear,
\operatorname{LS}_{f}(\cdot;w)
is strictly convex on
\Delta_{A}
and has a unique minimizer.
The Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x},\nu;w)=\operatorname{LS}_{f}(\mathbf{x};w)+\nu(\langle\mathbf{1},\mathbf{x}\rangle-1).

Stationarity gives
\beta\log\mathbf{x}-\beta R_{0}-\bigl(wR_{1}+(1-w)R_{2}\bigr)+\nu\mathbf{1}=0
, so
\mathbf{x}
is proportional to
\exp\left(R_{0}+\beta^{-1}\bigl(wR_{1}+(1-w)R_{2}\bigr)\right)
. Enforcing
\langle\mathbf{1},\mathbf{x}\rangle=1
yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{w}^{*}=\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=\operatorname{SoftMax}\left(R_{0}+\beta^{-1}\bigl(wR_{1}+(1-w)R_{2}\bigr)\right)\in\Delta_{A}^{\circ}.

(112)

Differentiating
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=\operatorname{SoftMax}(z(w))
with
z(w)=R_{0}+\beta^{-1}\bigl(wR_{1}+(1-w)R_{2}\bigr)
gives
z^{\prime}(w)=\beta^{-1}(R_{1}-R_{2})
. Using the standard SoftMax Jacobian
D\operatorname{SoftMax}(z)=\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})^{\top}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}=\beta^{-1}\Bigl(\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})^{\top}\Bigr)(R_{1}-R_{2}).

(113)

Substituting this into
v(w)=\sqrt{(1-w)^{2}+w^{2}}\left|(R_{1}-R_{2})^{\top}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}\right|
and using symmetry of
\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})-\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*})^{\top}
gives (
9
).
∎

D.4
CDF Estimation Error in Bandit Problems

Proposition 3

(Arc-length CDF estimation error in bandits)
.

Consider MDP with singleton state space
|\mathcal{S}|=1
and
|\mathcal{A}|=A\geq 2
actions. Suppose the bounded speed condition in Lemma
1
holds.
Given a budget of
T
pulls, suppose that each pull of arm
a
returns a bounded bi-objective reward vector

[r_{1}(a),r_{2}(a)]
with
\mathbb{E}[r_{m}(a)]=R_{m}(a)
for
m\in\{1,2\}
.
We estimate
R_{m}(a)
by the empirical mean
\hat{R}_{m}(a)
.
Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
, there is

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}\left(s(1)^{-1}\|\hat{R}_{m}-R_{m}\|_{\infty}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(s(1)^{-1}\sqrt{A\log(A/\delta)T^{-1}}\right).

(114)

Proof.

We first control the reward estimation error.
Under a total budget of
T
pulls, suppose each arm is sampled at least

\lfloor T/A\rfloor
times. Since each reward component lies in

[-R_{\max},R_{\max}]
, Hoeffding’s inequality yields that for any
\epsilon>0
and
each
m\in\{1,2\}
,

\Pr\left(\max_{a\in[A]}|\hat{R}_{m}(a)-R_{m}(a)|\geq\epsilon\right)\leq 2A\exp\left(-\frac{\lfloor T/A\rfloor\epsilon^{2}}{2R_{\max}^{2}}\right).

Hence, by choosing
\epsilon_{R}:=2R_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{A\log(4A/\delta)}{2T}}
,
we obtain, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{R}_{m}-R_{m}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{R},\quad m\in\{1,2\}.

(115)

We now bound
\|\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\|_{\infty}
. For any
w\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\big|\hat{\Phi}(w)-\Phi(w)\big|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\hat{s}(w)}{\hat{s}(1)}-\frac{s(w)}{s(1)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|\hat{s}(w)-s(w)|}{s(1)}+\frac{s(w)}{s(1)}\frac{|\hat{s}(1)-s(1)|}{\hat{s}(1)}.

Since
0\leq s(w)\leq s(1)
, we have

\displaystyle|\hat{s}(w)-s(w)|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{w}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|du\leq\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|,

\displaystyle|\hat{s}(1)-s(1)|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|du\leq\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big|\hat{\Phi}(w)-\Phi(w)\big|\leq\frac{1}{s(1)}\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|+\frac{1}{\hat{s}(1)}\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|.

(116)

It remains to control the speed perturbation.
Since
|R_{m}(a)|\leq R_{\max}
for all
a\in[A]
and
m\in\{1,2\}
,
the logits
\beta^{-1}(wR_{1}+(1-w)R_{2})
remain uniformly bounded in
\ell_{\infty}

for all
w\in[0,1]
. Hence the softmax vector
\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
stays in a compact subset
of
\Delta_{A}^{\circ}
, uniformly over
w\in[0,1]
, and the map

(R_{1},R_{2})\mapsto v(w)
is uniformly Lipschitz-continuous over
w\in[0,1]
.
Therefore, there exists a constant
L_{R}<\infty
, depending only on

(A,\beta,R_{\max})
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}(w)-v(w)|\leq L_{R}\big(\|\hat{R}_{1}-R_{1}\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{R}_{2}-R_{2}\|_{\infty}\big).

(117)

On
\left\{\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|\leq\frac{s(1)}{2}\right\}
, we have

\hat{s}(1)\geq s(1)-|\hat{s}(1)-s(1)|\geq s(1)-\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|\geq\frac{s(1)}{2}.

Substituting this into (
116
) gives, for all
w\in[0,1]
,

\big|\hat{\Phi}(w)-\Phi(w)\big|\leq\frac{1}{s(1)}\sup_{u}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|+\frac{2}{s(1)}\sup_{u}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|=\frac{3}{s(1)}\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|.

Hence, on
\left\{\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|\leq\frac{s(1)}{2}\right\}
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{3}{s(1)}\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|.

(118)

Moreover, by (
117
), the event
\left\{\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|\leq\frac{s(1)}{2}\right\}
is implied by

L_{R}\big(\|\hat{R}_{1}-R_{1}\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{R}_{2}-R_{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\leq\frac{s(1)}{2}.

In particular, if

2L_{R}\epsilon_{R}\leq\frac{s(1)}{2},

then (
115
) implies
\left\{\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\hat{v}_{u}(u)-v_{u}(u)|\leq\frac{s(1)}{2}\right\}
.
Equivalently, it suffices that

\epsilon_{R}\leq\frac{s(1)}{4L_{R}},

which holds whenever
T
is large enough, namely

T\gtrsim\frac{AR_{\max}^{2}L_{R}^{2}}{s(1)^{2}}\log\frac{A}{\delta}.

Finally, on the event (
115
), combining
(
118
) and (
117
) yields

\|\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{3L_{R}}{s(1)}\big(\|\hat{R}_{1}-R_{1}\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{R}_{2}-R_{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\leq\frac{6L_{R}}{s(1)}\epsilon_{R}.

Therefore, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\|\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=O\left(s(1)^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{A\log(A/\delta)}{T}}\right),

where the hidden constant depends only on
(A,\beta,R_{\max})
through
L_{R}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

E.1
Proof of the First Part of Theorem

In this subsection, we first consider the exact-
InnerSolver

Smoothness of
\Phi_{t}
under exact
InnerSolver
solutions.

To prove the Lipschitz-smoothness of
\Phi_{t}
, the key issue is that the update in Algorithm
1
depends on an interpolated CDF constructed from finitely many sampled Pareto points. Thus, to control the outer-loop dynamics, we must show that this interpolated CDF remains monotone and sufficiently Lipschitz-smooth. To do so, we first consider the exact
InnerSolver
solutions. In this case,
\tilde{s}^{(t)}
and
\tilde{\Phi}_{t}
are built from exact sampled solutions
\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)}=\mathbf{u}^{*}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}

or equivalently
\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}=\mathbf{x}^{*}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}
.

We establish this in three steps. First, we show that the rebuilt CDF increases at a controlled rate across the sampled weights, and that these local growth rates do not change too abruptly between neighboring intervals in Lemma
6
. Next, we show that the PCHIP interpolation rule lifts these discrete controls into global derivative and Lipschitz-smoothness bounds for the reconstructed CDF in Lemma
7
. Finally, we prove by induction that these properties are preserved by the SURF outer update throughout all iterations in Lemma

We begin with the discrete CDF values at the sampled weights. Before turning to the rebuilt CDF itself, we first present the following geometric lemma, which relates PF arc-length increments to chord lengths along the PF.

Lemma 5

(Arc-chord bound)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
hold.
Moreover, for any
0\leq w_{1}<w_{2}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle s(w_{2})-s(w_{1})\leq\sqrt{2}\left\|{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{2})-{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{1})\right\|.

(119)

Proof.

Denote
\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathrm{PF}}(s):={\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(s^{-1}(s))
for
s\in[0,s(1)]
and write

{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}_{s}(s)=\begin{bmatrix}{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,1}(s)\\
{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,2}(s)\end{bmatrix}.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, Lemma
2
shows that
{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,1}
is monotone nonincreasing and
{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,2}
is monotone nondecreasing. Thus, along the front, the first coordinate is nonincreasing and the second coordinate is nondecreasing as
w
increases. Since
s(\cdot)
is strictly increasing, the same monotonicity carries over to the PF parametrization.
Therefore, on the interval
[s(w_{1}),s(w_{2})]
,

\displaystyle\int_{s(w_{1})}^{s(w_{2})}\big|{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,1}^{\prime}(s)\big|ds=

\displaystyle\big|{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,1}(s(w_{2}))-{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,1}(s(w_{1}))\big|,

(120)

\displaystyle\int_{s(w_{1})}^{s(w_{2})}\big|{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,2}^{\prime}(s)\big|ds=

\displaystyle\big|{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,2}(s(w_{2}))-{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,2}(s(w_{1}))\big|.

(121)

Using
\|u\|\leq\|u\|_{1}
and then
\|u\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2}\|u\|
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle s(w_{2})-s(w_{1})=

\displaystyle\int_{s(w_{1})}^{s(w_{2})}\|\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{PF},s}^{\prime}(s)\|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{s(w_{1})}^{s(w_{2})}\left(|{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,1}^{\prime}(s)|+|{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,2}^{\prime}(s)|\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,1}(s(w_{2}))-{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,1}(s(w_{1}))\big|+\big|{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,2}(s(w_{2}))-{f_{\mathrm{PF}}}_{s,2}(s(w_{1}))\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{PF},s}(s(w_{2}))-\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{PF},s}(s(w_{1}))\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\left\|{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}_{s}(s(w_{2}))-{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}_{s}(s(w_{1}))\right\|.

Finally, since
{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}_{s}(s(w_{i}))={\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{i})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, we conclude the proof.
∎

This geometric relation ensures that the cumulative chord-length construction provides a faithful approximation to the true arc-length growth along the front. In particular, it allows us to control how fast the rebuilt CDF increases across sampled weights, and how much this local growth can vary from one interval to the next. We formalize this in the next lemma.

Lemma 6

(Discrete growth control of the rebuilt CDF)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3

For any sampled weights
\{w_{n}^{(t)}\}_{n=0}^{N}

\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})=\frac{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(w_{n}^{(t)})}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)}

\tilde{s}^{(t)}(w_{n}^{(t)})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{v_{\min}}{v_{\max}}\leq\frac{\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})}{w_{n+1}^{(t)}-w_{n}^{(t)}}\leq\sqrt{2}\frac{v_{\max}}{v_{\min}};

(122)

(ii)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+2}^{(t)})-\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})}{w_{n+2}^{(t)}-w_{n+1}^{(t)}}-\frac{\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})}{w_{n+1}^{(t)}-w_{n}^{(t)}}\right|\leq l_{\tilde{\Phi}_{t},n,1}\bigl(w_{n+2}^{(t)}-w_{n}^{(t)}\bigr).

Since
\tilde{\Phi}_{t}=\tilde{s}^{(t)}/\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})}{w_{n+1}^{(t)}-w_{n}^{(t)}}=\frac{1}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)}\cdot\frac{\|{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{n}^{(t)})\|}{w_{n+1}^{(t)}-w_{n}^{(t)}}.

(124)

Thus it suffices to study the chord secant slopes of
{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}

We first prove (
122

For any

5

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(b)-{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(a)\|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(s(b)-s(a)\bigr).

1

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{n}^{(t)})\|

Using
|\|x\|-\|y\||\leq\|x-y\|

\displaystyle 0\leq

160

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)}\tilde{s}^{(t)}-\frac{1}{s(1)}s\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)}\bigl(\tilde{s}^{(t)}-s\bigr)\right\|_{\infty}+\left|\frac{1}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)}-\frac{1}{s(1)}\right|\|s\|_{\infty}.

(168)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|\tilde{s}^{(t)}-s\|_{\infty}}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)}+\frac{|s(1)-\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)|}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)}\frac{2\|\tilde{s}^{(t)}-s\|_{\infty}}{\tilde{s}^{(t)}(1)},

(169)

where in the last inequality we used

. By Lemma
5

. Substituting into (

\displaystyle\left|\Phi(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\Phi(w_{n}^{(t)})-\frac{1}{N}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{n/N}^{(n+1)/N}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(q))-\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(q))}{\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(q))}dq\right|.

(170)

By Lemma

\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(w)\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{v_{\min}}{v_{\max}},\quad\forall w\in[0,1].

Proof.

is the rebuilt CDF obtained from the exact PF points on the

\{w_{n}^{(t)}\}_{n=0}^{N}
. Hence the exact-case argument from
the proof of (
171
) applies verbatim. In
particular, under the same condition on
N
as required there, we have

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\Bigr)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\alpha\frac{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\left(\frac{s(1)}{v_{\min}}\right)^{2}(3l_{\Phi_{\rm iter},1}+l_{\Phi,1})+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}l_{\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathrm{PF}},1}^{2}s(1)^{2}}{N^{2}}+\alpha\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{4}}\right).

(194)

Combining (
193
) and (
194
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\Bigr)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\alpha\frac{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\left(\frac{s(1)}{v_{\min}}\right)^{2}(3l_{\Phi_{\rm iter},1}+l_{\Phi,1})+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}l_{\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathrm{PF}},1}^{2}s(1)^{2}}{N^{2}}

\displaystyle+\alpha\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N\epsilon}{s(1)}+\frac{1}{N^{4}}\right).

(195)

Iterating (
195
) for
t\geq 1
proves the claim.
∎

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1
, where the
InnerSolver
is warm-started and run for a finite number of steps. The next lemma couples the outer CDF recursion with the optimization error induced by the inexact
InnerSolver
.

Lemma 12

(Coupled recursion)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
hold.
Consider Algorithm
1
with an inexact
InnerSolver
that returns iterates
\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)}
, and define their hidden-space images by

\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}:=g(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})

or simply let
\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}
denote the returned
iterate when the algorithm is formulated directly in the convex space
\mathcal{X}
.
Assume moreover that the inner solver is warm-started from the previous outer iterate and that, after
K
inner steps, it satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|\leq\eta_{K}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|,\quad\forall t,n,

(196)

where
\{\eta_{K}\}_{K\geq 1}
satisfies
\eta_{K}\downarrow 0
as
K\to\infty
.
Then there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}<\infty
, independent of
t
,
N
, and
K
, such that for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(1-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\bigr)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C_{1}\alpha\frac{1}{N^{2}}+C_{2}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|,

(197)

\displaystyle\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{K}\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|+C_{3}\eta_{K}\max_{n}|w_{n}^{(t+1)}-w_{n}^{(t)}|,

(198)

\displaystyle\max_{n}|w_{n}^{(t+1)}-w_{n}^{(t)}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\alpha\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C_{4}\alpha\frac{1}{N^{2}}+C_{4}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|.

(199)

Proof.

We first prove (
197
). Let
\hat{\Phi}_{t}
denote the rebuilt CDF obtained from the exact PF points
\{{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{n}^{(t)})\}_{n=0}^{N}
, and let
\tilde{\Phi}_{t}
denote the rebuilt CDF obtained from the inexact points
\{\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)})\}_{n=0}^{N}
.

By the same argument as in Lemma
11
, for every
n
,
\|\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)})-{\mathbf{f}_{\rm PF}}(w_{n}^{(t)})\|\leq G_{f}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|
. Repeating the proofs of Lemma
11
with
\epsilon
replaced by
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|
, and using that PCHIP is applied in the coordinate
q=\Phi_{t}(w)
, gives

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\hat{\Phi}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{2}\frac{N}{s(1)}\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|.

(200)

Now, using the update rule
\Phi_{t+1}=(1-\alpha)\Phi_{t}+\alpha\tilde{\Phi}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\hat{\Phi}_{t}\|_{\infty}.

(201)

Substituting (
200
), and using the same exact-rebuild estimate as in the proof of (
171
),

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\Bigr)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C_{1}\alpha\frac{1}{N^{2}},

(202)

proves (
197
).

Next we prove (
198
). By the assumed warm-started
K
-step contraction (
196
),

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|\leq\eta_{K}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|.

(203)

Adding and subtracting
\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}
gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|\leq\eta_{K}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|+\eta_{K}\|\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|.

(204)

By Lemma
2
, the map
w\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{w}^{*}
is continuously differentiable on the compact interval
[0,1]
, hence Lipschitz. Therefore there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
such that
\|\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|\leq C_{3}|w_{n}^{(t+1)}-w_{n}^{(t)}|
. Taking maximum over
n
proves (
198
).

Finally we prove (
199
). Since
w_{n}^{(t)}=\Phi_{t}^{-1}(n/N)
, the inverse stability bound from the derivative lower bound on
\Phi_{t}
implies
\max_{n}|w_{n}^{(t+1)}-w_{n}^{(t)}|\leq C\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}
for some constant
C>0
.
Also,
\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi_{t}=\alpha(\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t})
, hence

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\|\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\hat{\Phi}_{t}\|_{\infty}+\alpha\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}.

(205)

From the proof of (
171
), the exact rebuilt CDF satisfies
\|\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\frac{1}{N^{2}}
, where the exact-case constants are absorbed into
C
. Substituting this and (
200
) into (
205
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq C\alpha\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\alpha\frac{1}{N^{2}}+C\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|.

(206)

Absorbing the inverse-stability constant into
C_{4}
proves (
199
).
∎

We are now ready to finish proving Theorem
1
. Combining the preceding inexact-CDF perturbation bound with the coupled outer-inner recursion yields the following more general inexact convergence result. Theorem
1
follows directly from the regime (i) while regime (ii) provides additional analysis.

Theorem 4

(SURF convergence under inexact
InnerSolver
)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
hold. Denote the notations as the same in Lemma
12
holds. Choose
N=\Omega(\kappa^{1.5})
with
\kappa:=\frac{4\sqrt{2}v_{\max}}{v_{\min}}\sqrt{l_{\Phi_{\rm iter},1}+l_{\Phi,1}}
. There is

(i)
Choose
\alpha\leq 1/2
and
K
is chosen such that

\displaystyle\eta_{K}=\mathcal{O}\left(\min\left\{\frac{v_{\min}^{2}}{v_{\max}}N^{-1},\frac{s(1)}{\alpha N}\right\}\right)=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1}),

(207)

then there exists
C=\left(\frac{4C_{2}}{s(1)}+\frac{v_{\max}}{v_{\min}^{2}}\right)N=\Theta(N)
such that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{t}\left(\|\Phi_{0}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(0)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(0)}}^{*}\|\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(208)

Consequently,
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{v_{\min}^{2}}{v_{\max}}N^{-1}\right)
for all
t\geq 0
, and after the transient term is below the floor,
\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
and
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(N^{-3})
.

(ii)
Use warmup initialization to ensure
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(0)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(0)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{v_{\min}^{2}}{v_{\max}}N^{-1}\right)
, choose
\alpha=\Theta(N^{-1})
and
K
such that

\displaystyle\eta_{K}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(1+C_{3}C_{4}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\right)^{-1},\frac{1}{4}\left(\left(1+\frac{4C_{2}\alpha N}{s(1)}\right)C_{3}C_{4}\right)^{-1}\right\}=\mathcal{O}(1),

(209)

then there exists
C=1+\frac{4C_{2}\alpha N}{s(1)}
such that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{t}\left(\|\Phi_{0}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(0)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(0)}}^{*}\|\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(210)

Consequently, once the transient term is below the discretization floor,
\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
. The inner tracking recursion then contracts to the floor
\mathcal{O}(\alpha\eta_{K}N^{-2})
, so after
\mathcal{O}(\log N)
additional outer iterations,
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(\alpha\eta_{K}N^{-2})=\mathcal{O}(N^{-3})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
12
, substituting (
199
) into (
198
) gives

\displaystyle\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta_{K}\left(1+C_{3}C_{4}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\right)\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|+C_{3}C_{4}\alpha\eta_{K}\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C_{3}C_{4}\alpha\eta_{K}\frac{1}{N^{2}}.

(211)

For regime (i), choose
C\geq\left(\frac{4C_{2}}{s(1)}+\frac{v_{\max}}{v_{\min}^{2}}\right)N
, so
C=\Theta(N)
. Multiplying (
211
) by
C
and adding (
197
) gives

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}+CC_{3}C_{4}\alpha\eta_{K}\right)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left(C_{2}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}+C\eta_{K}\left(1+C_{3}C_{4}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\right)\right)\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(C_{1}+CC_{3}C_{4}\eta_{K}\right)\frac{1}{N^{2}}.

(212)

The condition (
207
) can be chosen with a sufficiently small hidden constant so that
CC_{3}C_{4}\eta_{K}\leq 1/4
and
\eta_{K}\left(1+C_{3}C_{4}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\right)\leq 1-\alpha/2
. Since
C\geq 4C_{2}N/s(1)
, we also have
C_{2}\alpha N/s(1)\leq\alpha C/4
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\left(\frac{\alpha}{4}+1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|+\alpha\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|+\alpha\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\left(\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|\right)+\alpha\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(213)

Here the first inequality uses the two bounds on
\eta_{K}
and the choice
C\geq 4C_{2}N/s(1)
, and the last two lines only collect terms. Iterating (
213
) gives (
208
). Since
\|\Phi_{0}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}(1)
and
C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(0)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(0)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(1)
, the bound implies
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{v_{\min}^{2}}{v_{\max}}N^{-1}\right)
for all
t\geq 0
. After the transient term is below
\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
, (
208
) gives
\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
and
C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
, hence
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(N^{-3})
.

For regime (ii), choose
C\geq 1+\frac{4C_{2}\alpha N}{s(1)}
, so
C=\Theta(1)
because
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
. Multiplying (
211
) by
C
and adding (
197
) gives

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}+CC_{3}C_{4}\alpha\eta_{K}\right)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left(C_{2}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}+C\eta_{K}\left(1+C_{3}C_{4}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\right)\right)\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(C_{1}+CC_{3}C_{4}\eta_{K}\right)\frac{1}{N^{2}}.

(214)

The condition (
209
) gives
CC_{3}C_{4}\eta_{K}\leq 1/4
and
\eta_{K}\left(1+C_{3}C_{4}\alpha\frac{N}{s(1)}\right)\leq 1/2
. Since
C\geq 4C_{2}\alpha N/s(1)
, we have
C_{2}\alpha N/s(1)\leq C/4
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\frac{3}{4}C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|+\alpha\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|+\alpha\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\left(\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+C\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|\right)+\alpha\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(215)

Here the first inequality uses (
209
) and
C\geq 4C_{2}\alpha N/s(1)
, the second uses
\alpha\leq 1
, and the last line collects terms. Iterating (
215
) gives (
210
). Once the transient term in (
210
) is below
\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
, we have
\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
. Substituting this into (

) and using (

) gives

\displaystyle\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t+1)}}^{*}\|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|+\mathcal{O}(\alpha\eta_{K}N^{-2}).

(216)

Thus, after an additional logarithmic transient,
\max_{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(\alpha\eta_{K}N^{-2})=\mathcal{O}(N^{-3})
, where the last equality uses

and

. This completes the proof.

Additionally, we provide the connection between CDF convergence and PF uniformity metrics CV.

E.3
Connection between SURF convergence and PF uniformity

Lemma 13

(CDF error controls PF spacing CV)
.

Let
w_{n}^{(t)}=\Phi_{t}^{-1}(n/N)
for
n=0,\dots,N
, and let
\tilde{\Phi}_{t}
be the normalized cumulative chord-length CDF constructed from the sampled PF points
\{\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\}_{n=0}^{N}
as in (
12
). Then, for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{CV}:=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{std}\big(\{\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\|\}_{n=0}^{N-1}\big)}{\mathrm{mean}\big(\{\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\|\}_{n=0}^{N-1}\big)}\leq 2N\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}.

(217)

Moreover, under the discretization and inexact-
InnerSolver
perturbation bounds used in Theorem
1
, if
\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}\big(N^{-1}\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\big)
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{CV}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-1}+N\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\right).

(218)

Consequently, under the conditions of Theorem
1
, for large enough
t
we have
\mathrm{CV}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
.

Proof.

By construction of
\tilde{\Phi}_{t}
from normalized cumulative chord length, each CDF increment equals the corresponding normalized PF segment length:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})=

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\|}{\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m}^{(t)})\|}.

(219)

Since the mean segment length is
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m}^{(t)})\|
, the CV can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{CV}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\|-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m}^{(t)})\|\right)^{2}}}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m}^{(t)})\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{(t)})\|}{\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\|\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m+1}^{(t)})-\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{u}_{m}^{(t)})\|}-\frac{1}{N}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})-\frac{1}{N}\right)^{2}}.

(220)

Because
w_{n}^{(t)}=\Phi_{t}^{-1}(n/N)
, we have
\Phi_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\Phi_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})=1/N
, and hence

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})-\frac{1}{N}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\tilde{\Phi}_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})-\big(\Phi_{t}(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-\Phi_{t}(w_{n}^{(t)})\big)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|(\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t})(w_{n+1}^{(t)})-(\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t})(w_{n}^{(t)})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}.

(221)

Substituting the above bound into the CV expression gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{CV}\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}4\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}^{2}}=2N\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}.

(222)

It remains to control
\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}
. By Lemma
10
, after substituting the increment bound

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\left|e_{t}\left(\frac{n+1}{N}\right)-e_{t}\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{N}\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}^{1/2},

(223)

the exact chord-length reconstruction satisfies

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{N^{2}}\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\frac{\kappa^{3}}{N^{2}}\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}^{3/2}\right).

(224)

Since
N=\Omega(\kappa^{1.5})
, we have
\kappa^{2}/N^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
and
\kappa^{3}/N^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
, and since
\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{O}(1)
, this gives

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-2}+\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}\right).

(225)

By Lemma
11
, the inexact inner-solver perturbation satisfies

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\hat{\Phi}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s(1)}N\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|\right).

(226)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\hat{\Phi}_{t}\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-2}+\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+N\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|\right).

(227)

Combining this with
\mathrm{CV}\leq 2N\|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t}\|_{\infty}
gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{CV}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-1}+N\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}+N^{2}\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|\right).

(228)

Under the conditions of Theorem
1
, for large enough
t
,
\|\Phi_{t}-\Phi\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
and
C(N)\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
with
C(N)=\Theta(N)
. Hence
\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\|\mathbf{x}_{n}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}_{w_{n}^{(t)}}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(N^{-3})
, and therefore
\mathrm{CV}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
.
∎

Baselines.

We compare our proposed arc-length-uniform Pareto sampling method with three scalarization-based baselines.

uniform weighting

Optimistic Linear Support (OLS)

[
5

At each step, OLS chooses a new weight vector that is optimistic with respect to the currently known solutions and solves the corresponding scalarized optimization problem to expand the supported portion of the PF.

110

F.2.3
Details of MO-Mountaincar Problem

MO-Mountaincar Problem Setup.
MO-Mountaincar is a multi-objective variant of the classical Mountaincar control problem, in which an underpowered car must build momentum by moving back and forth in a valley in order to reach the goal near the top of the right hill.
In the multi-objective setting, the agent is penalized at every time step and also incurs additional penalties for control behavior, thereby inducing a trade-off between fast completion and action efficiency.

This problem can be modeled by a multi-objective Markov decision process

\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},\mathbf{r},\gamma,\rho,\tau)
, where the state space is continuous,

\mathcal{S}\subseteq[-1.2,0.6]\times[-0.07,0.07]
, and each state

s_{t}=(x_{t},v_{t})
consists of the car position
x_{t}
and velocity
v_{t}
. The action space is

\mathcal{A}=\{0,1,2\}
, corresponding to accelerating to the left, taking no push, and accelerating to the right, respectively. Equivalently, the control entering the dynamics is
\tilde{a}_{t}=a_{t}-1\in\{-1,0,1\}
. The transition dynamics are deterministic:

v_{t+1}=v_{t}+0.001\tilde{a}_{t}-0.0025\cos(3x_{t}),\quad x_{t+1}=x_{t}+v_{t+1},

(231)

followed by clipping
x_{t+1}
to
[-1.2,0.6]
and
v_{t+1}
to
[-0.07,0.07]
; if the car hits the left boundary, the velocity is reset to zero. In our implementation, we use a two-dimensional reward vector

\mathbf{r}(s_{t},a_{t})=[r_{1},r_{2}]
,
where
r_{1}=-1
is the per-step time penalty, and
r_{2}=-1
if
a_{t}=2
and
0
otherwise is the reverse-action penalty.
This induces a trade-off between reaching the goal quickly and reducing reverse control effort. The discount factor is chosen as
\gamma=0.995
, and
\rho
denotes the initial-state distribution, where the initial position is sampled uniformly from
[-0.6,-0.4]
and the initial velocity is fixed at zero. An episode terminates once the car reaches the goal position, i.e., when
x_{t}\geq 0.5
, and is truncated if the trajectory length reaches
200
steps. We use the KL
regularizer

\tau(\pi(\cdot\mid s))=\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\!\bigl(\pi(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,\pi_{\rm ref}\bigr),

with
\beta=0.05
and
\pi_{\rm ref}
taken as the uniform policy over
\mathcal{A}
. For the inner solver, we use a DQN trained with Adam optimizer and learning rate
10^{-3}
. In the reported comparison, we use
N=6
sparse preference weights.
Our method uses
10
outer iterations with update parameter
\alpha=0.3
and trains each scalarized subproblem for
K=100
steps per outer iteration.

Appendix G
Limitations and Broader Impacts

Our current theory and algorithm focus primarily on the bi-objective setting. Extending SURF to problems with more than two objectives is a natural next step, but it is also more challenging because the Pareto front becomes higher-dimensional and its geometry is harder to characterize and sample uniformly. In addition, while our experiments cover several representative settings, they are still limited in scale. Future work should evaluate SURF on larger multi-objective benchmarks, more complex reinforcement-learning environments, and larger-scale language-model alignment problems to better understand its scalability and robustness.

More broadly, SURF offers a general framework for analyzing trade-offs among competing objectives. By promoting more uniform coverage of the Pareto front, it can provide a more comprehensive view of the trade-off landscape and reduce the risk of drawing conclusions from a narrowly sampled region. This can be beneficial in applications where transparent and reliable trade-off analysis is important, such as reinforcement learning, model alignment, and resource allocation.
