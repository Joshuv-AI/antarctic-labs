1. Executive Summary

Abstract
The EVs-at-RISC project was a five-year research, development, and demonstration initiative to create
foundational tools for utility-scale fleet aggregation and Smart Charge Management (SCM) of Electric
Vehicles (EV), Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure (EVCI), and related Distributed Energy Resources
(DER). Rather than seeking to develop and demonstrate highly perfected SCM algorithms and control
strategies, this project instead focused on creating foundational software solutions that enable
unprecedented digital interoperability across the communications technologies and vendor platforms used
to manage EV, EVCI, and DER, as well as existing energy management infrastructure operated by
utilities, grid operators, and aggregators. This project then extends these novel interoperability capabilities
to develop and deploy powerful middleware abstractions across grid edge networks and EVCI/DER fleet
aggregations incorporating modern software tools and best practices, such as CI/CD, to bring the
immense capabilities of infrastructure-as-code and policy-as-code to modern grid edge network
environments. ​

This addresses the foremost systemic issues preventing realization of any net operational benefits from
scaled deployment of behind-the-meter EV, EVCI, and DER assets in electric power grids and markets
today. The results of this approach and project unlock massive potential for new SCM capabilities to be
easily prototyped, evaluated, and deployed at-scale within the existing grid edge network infrastructure
and EVCI/DER technology ecosystem. The EVs-at-RISC project achieves this by extending Open Field
Message Bus (OpenFMB), a conceptual model for digital interoperability and distributed intelligence in
traditional front-of-meter utility SCADA networks, validating our hypothesis that OpenFMB could be
similarly used to solve systemic digital interoperability issues in behind-the-meter environments and
unlock real-world utility-scale SCM capabilities without requiring any new proprietary vendor solutions
or significant infrastructure reconfiguration.

Project Objectives
The primary objectives of EVs-at-RISC include: (1) reduce the cost and complexity of EV integration in
the electric grid; (2) improve visibility and control at the grid edge; (3) improve interoperability
between EV charging infrastructure, EVCI operators, and utility control systems; and (4) advance the
adoption of interoperable open architectures for secure and resilient management and grid integration of
EV charging infrastructure.

Technical Approach
This project extends the Open Field Message Bus (OpenFMB) information model and reference
architecture to achieve a standard pattern for mapping arbitrary EVCI/DER protocol structures and
information models to the information models used in IEC 61850 and CIM, which are universally adopted
by utilities worldwide for managing existing energy infrastructure networks. While envisioned and
historically used to address interoperability issues in traditional front-of-the-meter utility SCADA
networks, this project team hypothesized that the same approach could be used to address digital
interoperability issues in behind-the-meter customer- and aggregator-operated EVCI/DER fleets,
providing a powerful abstraction layer which allows utilities and grid operators to interact with any

                                                                    EVs-at-RISC Final Technical Report | Page 4

EVCI/DER assets and fleet operators using the languages (protocols), tools, and standards which they
already know and love.

Liberas implemented the OpenFMB reference architecture using state-of-the-art software projects and
cloud infrastructure tooling. This served as the foundation for research and development of several key
capabilities: (1) a distributed interoperable networking and protocol infrastructure capable of spanning
many highly distributed and/or discontinuous network environments, (2) a commercial off-the-shelf based
containerized edge computing platform and management architecture, and (3) a policy framework and
management engine for creation, routing, distribution, observation, and enforcement of policies at each
layer of the OSI 7-layer model. Together, these provide a secure point of integration between grid
operators’ digital infrastructure and third-party EVCI/DER aggregator fleets. Furthermore, these provide a
highly scalable digital platform that permits the monitoring and management of disparate, distributed, and
heterogeneous customer EVCI/DER fleets through a unified interface and policy system.

This platform, called Diadem, facilitates smart charge management innovations that can reach every
resource in a fleet without discrimination of vendor or protocol support, allowing utility and customer
policies to be encoded and automatically enforced with software agents, and providing ultimate visibility
and control flexibility at the grid edge to realize the true economic and operational potential of
EVCI/DER at-scale. Diadem implements a secure design and defense-in-depth architecture to add
layered security and Zero Trust to existing EV charging and grid edge networks, which was subjected to
extensive utility compliance review and testing, and offensive security assessments by renowned
third-party red teams, resulting in a software platform that is approved for use by one of the largest
distribution utilities in the world and which conforms with the security and compliance requirements and
best practices of the United States utility sector.

To accelerate widespread technology adoption, the core Diadem platform components and tools will be
released as open-source shortly after issuance of this report. Liberas and our partners recognize that
making these technologies widely and freely accessible will unlock the significant potential of
behind-the-meter customer-owned EVCI/DER to provide net operational benefits to electric power
infrastructure and regional energy markets. Further, we believe this will unlock access to significant
revenue streams for the emergent categories of EVCI/DER asset owners and fleet operators such as
individual consumers, local communities, and historically under-resourced municipalities, school districts,
and transit agencies.

The project team deployed and demonstrated Diadem across many real-world sites and high-TRL
environments, including and not limited to state-of-the-art commercial parking facilities, industrial
microgrids and factory sites, university campus microgrids, utility test labs, utility distribution
infrastructure, and utility backoffices and control rooms.

 sketch) to TRL-5. This was validated in a demonstration provided to DoE/VTO in which the
 Diadem prototype OpenFMB system successfully automated dispatch and curtailment of energy
 resources distributed across a real-world large university campus microgrid. This included a

 350kW ABB-made eXtreme Fast Charging system, Tesla Model Y, 128kWh battery storage
 system, and related equipment, to meet a utility-provided demand response set point using

 ●​ Within 18 months thereafter, the project team achieved TRL-6 maturity of the Diadem system,

variety of real-world utility environments and utility head-end systems that are often out-of-reach for any
vendor, university, or lab. This project proved in high-maturity, real-world environments that a Smart
Charge Management system based on open standards and open-source technologies can overcome the
critical interoperability and capability gaps necessary to unlock reliable utility-scale SCM capabilities for
behind-the-meter EVCI/DER customer fleet aggregations – making these capabilities available today, at
utility-scale, with no overhaul of existing infrastructure and minimal integration work.

Project Goals & Objectives
The EVs-at-RISC project was set up to achieve the goals and success factors discussed below:

1. Reduce cost and complexity of EV, EVCI, and DER integration in the electric grid
The EVs-at-RISC project extended OpenFMB as a conceptual architecture to achieve low-cost and
low-complexity integration of new customer-owned EV, EVCI, and DER asset fleets with existing utility
infrastructure and management systems. This approach significantly accelerates and improves the
reliability of integration of these fleets with utility and market infrastructure, without requiring any new
hardware, third-party vendor solutions, or bespoke integration architectures.

2. Improve interoperability between EV, EVCI, DER, and utility control systems
Interoperability of digital communications and control is critical to enabling the use of EV, EVCI, and
DER as operational and economic assets in utility-operated electric power grids and markets.
EVs-at-RISC created novel capabilities to map otherwise non-interoperable communications protocols for
modern EV, EVCI, and DER to a shared information model with existing global adoption by utilities.

3. Improve visibility and control flexibility at the grid edge
Utilities lack visibility and influence over the operational state of behind-the-meter EV, EVCI, and DER
resources. When unseen and unmanaged, these resources are often a net detriment to local electric power
infrastructure. In order to achieve the promise for electrification and new energy technologies to actually
enhance and benefit the grid, visibility and appropriately incentivized control flexibility of EV, EVCI, and
DER by utilities is essential. This project unlocks this potential by providing secure-by-design solutions
that overcome interoperability issues and allow EV, EVCI, and DER communications at-scale to be safely
made available to utilities under appropriate customer agreements.

4. Mitigate cybersecurity threats to the electric grid from EV, EVCI, and DER
Compromise of individual or aggregated EV, EVCI, and DER assets can impact grid operations,
propagate attacks to other assets, and impact national security and public safety. This project seeks to
extend OpenFMB to create powerful middleware abstractions that allow establishment of a mature
baseline of security controls and policies at the interfaces between EV, EVCI, and DER fleets with utility
and market infrastructure – and to automate the default configuration, monitoring, and enforcement of
these using policy agents and policy/infrastructure-as-code.

5. Enable standardization of interoperable open architectures for energy asset management
Demonstration of open standards and open-source technologies as a viable solution to address SCM
capability gaps in real-world utility environments will help create market pressure on vendors to move to
interoperable architectures. Ultimately, this will reduce integration cost and friction, improve performance
and reliability, and accelerate return-on-investment of EV, EVCI, and DER technologies, leading to
further investment and long-term growth of the industry while unlocking the widely envisioned
operational benefits of these systems to electric grids and markets.

3. Project Activities

OpenFMB modeling and information model extension for EVCI/DER
OpenFMB was initially developed with front-of-meter use cases in mind – primarily utility circuit
segment management for distribution automation systems. To facilitate behind-the-meter applications,
such as EVCI/DER smart charge management, Liberas led a task force within the OpenFMB Users Group
to develop an extension to the OpenFMB information model supporting smart charge management for EV
Charging Infrastructure, which has since been publicly released in OpenFMB 2.2.0.

This activity involved an initial modeling exercise to port over functionality present in OCPP 2.0.1 and
IEC 61850-7-420 to OpenFMB. Leading this task force within the OpenFMB Users Group, Liberas
served as the primary developer and contributor of the Electric Vehicle Supply Equipment (EVSE)
module and the following OpenFMB message profiles:

 ●​ EVSECapabilityOverrideProfile: Overrides nameplate parameters for a given EVSE
 ●​ EVSECapabilityProfile: Provides nameplate parameters upon request
 ●​ EVSEControlProfile: Modifies the EVSE's charging schedule, performs reservations,
 ●​ EVSEDiscreteControlProfile: Performs a control action with an additional check condition
 ●​ EVSEEventProfile: Signals fault conditions, EVSE state, and PEV state
 ●​ EVSEReadingProfile: Provides metering values collected during charge events
 ●​ EVSEStatusProfile: Equivalent to the EVSEEventProfile, but published on a periodic basis

These message profiles follow the pattern used by other DER components included in the OpenFMB
information model, with additional types added to support smart charge management, EVSE/EVCI
reservations, service modes, PEV state, fault conditions, and metering. Figure 1 and Figure 2 provide
some relevant subsections of additions to the OpenFMB information model.

After developing the information model, Liberas integrated the modifications into the project’s software
stack and performed extensive testing and validation of the model itself and the use cases it enabled.
Namely, the adapter framework used for integrating OCPP-enabled EVCI was augmented to support the
newly developed OpenFMB profiles, validating the smart charge management capabilities across multiple
real-world test sites, as well as through extensive testing through a software test harness and fuzzing tools.

Diadem SCM System Design, Research & Development
Initial research development of the Diadem SCM system began with design and implementation of the
generic OpenFMB Node architecture, and the selection of platform and component requirements
necessary to our operational and security objectives, customer requirements, and key use cases. A
high-level representation of the OpenFMB Node architecture implemented for many of the Diadem
platform components is provided in Figure 3. This illustration, as well as Figure 4, emphasizes both the
use of commercial-off-the-shelf components and standard modern open-source software components
(such as Linux containers based on OCI), as well as the defense-in-depth approach we follow by-default
beginning with secure hardware elements that are used to lock down cloud and edge assets, all the way up
to the container orchestration mechanisms and microservices.

 Figure 3: Generic OpenFMB Node architecture design implemented by Diadem platform.

The hardened compute image developed for the Diadem platform uses a security-hardened Linux image
built using the Yocto project. This allowed us to create purpose-built and minified package repositories
and enable a wide range of Linux kernel security modules to provide an immutable bootloader and root
file system with disk encryption bound to a state-of-the-art hardware security module. Figure 4 provides
an illustration of the layered security controls applied to the hardened compute image, enforcing the use
of hierarchical Public Key Infrastructure and shared cryptographically verifiable chains of trust to
authenticate every component of the system, from the hardware modules, to the firmware, bootloader,
kernel, operating system, and virtualization layers.

                                                               EVs-at-RISC Final Technical Report | Page 11

 Figure 4: Component diagram depicting the hardened compute image ​
 used for Diadem compute workers.

The container orchestration framework built on top of the hardened compute image leverages several
cloud-native technologies: Kubernetes, Helm, NATS, and SPIFFE/SPIRE. Kubernetes provides the
container orchestration framework for deploying container images across nodes in the compute cluster, as
well as maintaining configuration and performing secrets management. Helm is the package manager
used for deployments layers on top of Kubernetes, providing a templating system for complex and
variable deployments. NATS is the publish-subscribe overlay network used to interconnect containers
running in the cluster onto the OpenFMB network. SPIFFE/SPIRE is the identity management and
authentication framework used to provision nodes in the cluster, and provide runtime attestation
mechanisms to ensure the integrity of deployed applications.

Our next development effort focused on creating the core OpenFMB services. This includes the
OpenFMB adapter framework for the integration of various DER protocols into a harmonized information
model and transport layer, the public-key infrastructure and permission scheme to authenticate devices
connecting to the OpenFMB network, and proof-of-concept integrations for the developed OpenFMB
platform.

Adapter development then followed, beginning with identification and prioritization of widespread and
emergent behind-the-meter EVCI/DER protocols based on factors such as current and anticipated market
penetration, relevance to project use cases, and those which are the greatest sources of interoperability
challenges for our project stakeholders. Adapter development involved creating and thoroughly testing
implementations of the respective protocols in a memory-safe language (Rust), followed by creating
adapters to integrate these protocols into the OpenFMB network. Figure 5 and Figure 6 sequentially
illustrate the challenges which the Diadem adapter framework solves, contrasting existing approaches to
piecewise integration of DER through ad hoc protocol translation against Liberas' approach of first lifting
the DER protocols' message streams into a common information model (OpenFMB) and middleware
abstraction layer (Diadem) to simplify the overall complexity of asset management.

Initial Real-World Diadem System Integration – USU EVR Microgrid
The first mid-TRL real-world environment in which our team deployed and refined the Diadem platform
was the campus microgrid located at the Utah State University EV Research Laboratory. This began a
continuous trend and pattern of prioritizing early real-world deployment of the system, even for early
prototypes and very functionally minimal system implementations. We are strong believers in the agile
software development principles of incremental and iterative development, and continuous development
of functional systems that get to stakeholders early and de-risk real-world risks and constraints not
obvious in development environments. To this point, these real-world integrations allowed us to rapidly
refine and validate the Diadem platform, collecting real-world data in near real-time, and assessing
protocol compliance of many commercial EVCI/DER solutions and emergent OpenFMB integration
complexities.

Policy Framework for Zero-Trust SCM and Grid Edge Automation
Policy frameworks that support encoding of policies, controls, and control objectives using simple and
standard policy syntax (e.g., policy-as-code) are at the heart of modern technology innovation and digital
infrastructure systems. Likewise, such policy frameworks are at the heart of any Zero Trust Architecture,
These policy frameworks need to support not just specification of application-layer business policies,
such as kW curtailment based on a certain signal or condition, but also granular policies and controls at
each layer of the OSI 7-layer model, which can be defined and enforced uniquely for different operational
system context.

A core contribution of this project is the Diadem policy framework for zero-trust smart charge
management and grid edge automation developed by Liberas with DTE providing essential stakeholder
input. The Diadem policy framework supports multi-level policy documents, allowing operators to
perform low-level network segmentation as well as application-layer controls for OpenFMB message
flows and SCM program objectives.

Policies are represented as a human-readable document. This document is divided in sections ascribing
different controls for each node in the deployment cluster. Each section includes a sequence of operations
the container orchestration system will perform on behalf of the Policy Controller – the actor responsible
for distributing policy documents. Policy documents are compiled and static analysis is performed for
first-pass validation of the document's content. Each section of the document is then signed by the policy
author and issued to the Policy Controller for distribution, after validating the policy author's credentials.
Figure 17 depicts the compilation, signing, and distribution procedures for a policy document.

 Figure 17: Compilation and distribution pipeline for policy documents.

Within a deployment cluster, the policy system consists of three logical actors:

ExtensibleDSS - Modeling and Simulation Capabilities
Liberas extended the OpenDSS, HELICS, and CALDERA projects to facilitate advanced modeling and
simulation of use cases related to utility-scale smart charge management of highly distributed and varied
third-party EVCI/DER fleets. The Diadem platform facilitated integration of the fleet of real-world
EVCI/DER made available for this project for the purpose of enabling the hybrid simulation of
EVCI/DER fleet smart charging and demand-response strategies in tandem with real-world DER
monitoring technologies. This enables the use of simulation to inform real-world demand-response action,
and the use of real-world EVCI/DER behaviors, control responses, and grid conditions to augment and
improve the accuracy of simulation. These activities ultimately resulted in several improvements that will
be contributed back to the mainline OpenDSS project, as well as a new open-source framework called
ExtensibleDSS.

ExtensibleDSS leverages existing work with the OpenDSS simulation platform and cloud-edge
networking solutions to construct extensible data pipelines between OpenDSS simulation, real-world
DER, and researchers to support fine-grained, configurable, and scalable demand-response solutions.
ExtensibleDSS replaces the Caldera OpenDSS plugin for object-oriented abstractions of DER, and uses
the NATS publish-subscribe message bus for seamless integration into our greater DER management
cloud-edge infrastructure. This integration into a greater DER management system gives us the ability to
draw information from heterogeneous sources, such as backend utility systems, 3rd party APIs, and direct
DER connections through their respective protocols.

ExtensibleDSS – Design Goals
The design goals of the ExtensibleDSS project are to provide a low-cost abstraction over the OpenDSS
runtime, perform sandboxing on OpenDSS artifacts and processes during execution, enable modular
closed-loop control of OpenDSS experiments using extensible remote procedure calls addressed to the
sandboxed OpenDSS runtimes, and enable rapid iteration on OpenDSS experiments and experimental
methodologies through well-defined information models for exporting simulation data and easy to use
client-server abstractions with type safety through the Python gradual typing system. The ExtensibleDSS
platform is designed to seamlessly integrate with larger hybrid simulation environments, where near
real-time data is collected from deployed hardware and used to augment OpenDSS simulation over the
publish-subscribe message bus.

In addition, this initial development phase of the ExtensibleDSS platform primarily targets
demand-response events and controls for automating and reasoning over demand-response experiments,
building upon previous work performed with the Caldera and HELICS projects. We extend previous work
supporting various DER in OpenDSS by providing object-oriented abstractions for several DER,
including:

 ●​ Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS)
 ●​ Dynamic Wireless Power Transfer (DWPT) Systems
 ●​ Photovoltaic (PV) Systems
 ●​ Plug-in Electric Vehicles (PEV)
 ●​ Electric Vehicle Supply Equipment (EVSE/EVCI)
 ●​ Generic Demand-Response Loads

Unlocking Economic Viability of Next-Generation Charging – DWPT
This section highlights the collaboration between Liberas and Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) in
EVs-at-RISC to research, develop, and demonstrate the first-ever integration of capabilities for remote
charge control and smart charge management of Dynamic Wireless Power Transfer (DWPT) systems. To
date, DWPT research and development efforts have necessarily focused on achieving reliable, high-power
energy transfer under real-world operating conditions: such as advancing power electronics, coil design,
control stability, efficiency, and safety. The long-term economic viability of DWPT systems is still widely
contested among industry experts, and previous efforts have not described a defensible pathway to
achieve return-on-investment on real-world and scaled DWPT infrastructure deployments.

True to the mandate of EVs-at-RISC, the project team believed that Diadem and OpenFMB could enable
DWPT systems to be integrated and monetized within the same utility systems and processes that are used
today for financially incentivized Smart Charge Management of conductive EVCI/DER technologies. We
were ultimately successful in both the implementation and demonstration of the first compliant OCPP
2.0.1 implementation in a DWPT system for remote charge control and monitoring with modern charging
station management system software, as well as the demonstration of its use to enroll and automate the
remote dispatch the ORNL DWPT system based on a utility demand response signal delivered remotely
over the internet using the Diadem platform. This work proved our hypothesis that the open-standards,
open-source approach to the Diadem system, and the use of OpenFMB as a core building block, can
provide real and immediate benefits to more than just legacy, modern, and cutting-edge commercial
products, but also the next-generation charging technology that will define the future of electrification
across transit, trucking, mining, and eventually consumer vehicles.

DWPT Integration – Secondary Buck DC/DC Converter High Power Testing
The DWPT hardware is a 200-kW, 85-kHz modular system built around a ground-side SiC full-bridge
inverter that drives an LCC-tuned double-D transmitter coil, transferring power across about a 25.4 cm air
gap to a series-tuned double-D receiver coil, then converting it back to DC with a vehicle-side rectifier
and (optionally) regulating it with a high-power 4-phase interleaved buck DC/DC stage for battery
charging. The power electronics are constructed from repeatable “building blocks” using 1200-V SiC
half-bridge modules, a low-inductance PCB busbar and high-frequency DC-link capacitors on a cold plate
to manage switching stress and thermal loading, enabling the inverter/rectifier/DC-DC stages to be scaled
by paralleling modules and combining blocks while maintaining high efficiency at high power.

This DWPT hardware, power supply and loads were relocated to the Grid Research Integration and
Deployment Center (GRID-C). The track can emulate a dynamic wireless charging process with a vehicle
speed of 20 mph. Two NHR 9300 high voltage battery test systems are used to provide a 200 kW power
source (Figure 21) for the DWPT system, and two other NHR 9300 modules are used as the battery mode
to absorb the charging power.

                                                                EVs-at-RISC Final Technical Report | Page 25

 Figure 21: 200kW power source and load (left) and measurement equipment (right).

The buck DC/DC converter on the vehicle side was used to regulate the charging current/power, as shown
in Figure 21. To meet the requirements of projects on smart charging, a control strategy was developed to
precisely control the current sharing of the high-power buck DC/DC converter. The digital control chip is
TI DSP 28377, which can generate PWM signals with 10ns resolution with a 200MHz clock. When doing
independent closed-loop control for each phase, any change of duty cycle will be in a minimum of 10ns
step, which tends to drive the current balance off track. To address this problem, a high resolution PWM
scheme was implemented to achieve 200 ps resolution. The test result is shown in Figure 22, where the
output power is 136kW. The output power is averagely split among the four phases, so the charging
currents are equal. The experimental result demonstrated that the developed control strategy can
successfully track the power reference, and the current sharing among these four phases are regulated
well. The buck DC/DC converter control strategy and its implementation on DSP provided the capability
for the DWPT system to regulate the output charging power, which was used for changing the charging
profile function in this project.

Open-Source Release – LibreFMB – OpenFMB Reference Implementation
While executing on the project objectives, Liberas assembled several software libraries and supporting
tools staged for public, open-source release. Collectively, Liberas is releasing this suite of open-source
software as LibreFMB.

LibreFMB is designed to bridge a critical gap identified in the OpenFMB ecosystem: a lack of
reference implementations for real-world deployments of OpenFMB-compliant networks. Existing tools
provide software libraries enabling developers to interface with the OpenFMB information model, and
proprietary extensions exist to provide legacy protocol adapters, but to our knowledge there exists no
publicly available tooling to deploy and extend a complete OpenFMB system. LibreFMB aims to fill this
gap by providing a collection of purpose-built OpenFMB legacy protocol adapters, policy observation and
enforcement mechanisms, container orchestration, and ancillary system components responsible for
identity management, archiving, and network connectivity in heterogeneous environments.

Liberas’ open-source release of LibreFMB aims to provide developers and non-technical researchers alike
with an environment in which they can deploy a complete OpenFMB system for experimentation, and
serve as a reference for production-ready implementations using a cloud-native technology stack. While
LibreFMB as it currently exists does not provide a complete suite of infrastructure-as-code components to
directly deploy onto commercial cloud providers (AWS, GCP, Azure, etc.) without modification, it
provides a fully functional deployment environment using software-defined networking, as well as several
examples of infrastructure components, that an intermediate cloud engineer can extend and transfer into a
production-ready deployment for enterprise use.

4. Summary and Conclusions
The project team deployed and demonstrated Diadem across many real-world sites and high-TRL
environments, including and not limited to commercial parking garages, industrial microgrids and factory
sites, university campus microgrids, utility test labs, utility distribution infrastructure and enterprise
systems, and others.

 ●​ Within 12 months, the project team advanced the Diadem technologies from TRL-0 (e.g., a
 napkin sketch) to TRL-5, validated in a demonstration to DoE/VTO of the use of the Diadem
 prototype OpenFMB system for automated orchestration of dispatch and curtailment of a 350kW
 eXtreme Fast Charger, Tesla Model Y, 128kW battery storage system, and related equipment
 across a real-world university campus microgrid to meet a utility-provided demand response
 signal.

 ●​ Within 18 months thereafter, the project team achieved TRL-6 maturity of the Diadem system,
 deploying it across five real-world operational sites spread across four states, three utilities, and
 two energy markets. Diadem was demonstrated to automate several project use cases including:
 permissive fleet-wide curtailment, load shedding, enforcement of a real power purchase
 agreement between a solar energy prosumer and a fleet charging site, emergency demand
 response, and interoperable charge control and dispatch across a variety of EV chargers,
 conventional generators, PV generation, and battery storage systems.

 ●​ Within 18 months thereafter, we achieved TRL-7 maturity, having integrated Diadem with
 multiple production utility head-end systems, deployed it in a production utility SCADA network,
 and deployed it to our wide-scale demonstration sites including the Bagley Parking Hub on the
 Michigan Central campus. Our team validated the TRL-7 system by performing multiple
 demonstrations to DoE/VTO, DoE/CESER, JOET, and others of its use to perform automated
 utility-managed smart charging and two-way dispatch of approximately 50 state-of-the-art EV
 chargers actively serving both V1G- and V2G-capable EVs, and related commercial DERs,
 spread across multiple disparate (non-adjacent) operational sites around Corktown, Detroit, MI.

Ultimately, the EVs-at-RISC program was highly successful. The project team met and exceeded our
initial goals related to technology maturity, and through our utility partner we were able to deploy,
validate, and improve the Diadem system across a wide variety of environments and utility production
systems that are often out-of-reach for projects such as this one. This project proved in high-maturity,
real-world environments that a Smart Charge Management system based on open standards and
open-source technologies can overcome the critical gaps that prevent existing solutions, of any price and
scale, from unlocking reliable utility-scale SCM capabilities for behind-the-meter EVCI/DER fleets.

By extending the Open Field Message Bus (OpenFMB) information model and reference architecture,
this the EVs-at-RISC program established a practical and scalable mechanism for utilities to interact with
and manage emerging EV and DER resources using information models, workflows, and operational
semantics they already understand. This approach enables seamless integration of EVCI/DER assets into
existing utility environments without requiring fundamental changes to utility operating models, control

                                                                EVs-at-RISC Final Technical Report | Page 35

systems, or workforce practices. Importantly, the resulting solution is deployable today within current
utility infrastructure, regulatory frameworks, and compliance requirements. This contrasts with many
academic or conceptual solutions that assume wholesale replacement of legacy systems or idealized
operating conditions.

Central to this effort is the development of a portable, modular, lightweight, and secure-by-design
reference implementation that minimizes both cost and effort required for integration across a wide range
of EV and DER vendors. By adhering to real-world utility constraints and prioritizing ease of adoption,
the project transforms advanced smart charging and DER coordination concepts into immediately usable
operational capabilities. This focus on pragmatism ensures that utilities can realize tangible benefits such
as improved visibility, control, and reliability without introducing undue operational risk or integration
burden.

Finally, by leveraging modern, state-of-the-art software engineering practices, the project enables utilities
to benefit from contemporary approaches such as infrastructure-as-code and policy-as-code. These
practices establish secure, resilient “paved road” defaults for performance, security, and fault recovery,
while allowing operational, network, and security policies to be expressed in machine-readable form and
automatically enforced across all layers of the system. This significantly reduces manual configuration
and oversight, standardizes a mature default security posture, and provides an accessible interface for
defining and managing complex policies, even by non-experts. Developed in close collaboration with
DTE Energy and validated through early, iterative, real-world deployments, this approach culminated in a
TRL-7 system that has been endorsed and approved for production use by one of the largest electric
utilities in the world.
