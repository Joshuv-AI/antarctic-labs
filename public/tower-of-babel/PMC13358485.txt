Comprehensive analysis of POLD2 as a potential biomarker for modulating the immune microenvironment and predicting immunotherapy response in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Wu Zhu, Hong Zhang, Zhiqin Guo, Lin Zhang, Shaoqing Hu, Wanxin Wu, Xiangyi Chen, Leiyan Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the leading cause of global cancer mortality. Although significant progress has been made in treatment modalities, including surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, targeted agents and immunotherapies, clinical outcomes for patients continue to be unfavorable. Key obstacles include disease recurrence, metastasis and the development of treatment resistance. This study aimed to explore molecular mechanisms driving LUAD progression and identify novel prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.MethodsGene expression data for LUAD were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) databases to conduct a preliminary investigation. The differential expression of POLD2 was validated by analyzing gene expression datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). Clinical patient data with survival information from the TCGA and GEO databases were employed to investigate and validate the prognostic significance of POLD2. The association of POLD2 with the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) was examined through RNA transcript-based single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) and ESTIMATE algorithm, which quantifies stromal and immune cell components in malignant tumor tissues based on expression data. The Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) model was used to evaluate the potential therapeutic response of POLD2 to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Molecular docking and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were used to investigate the binding interactions between POLD2 and specific therapeutic agents. In vitro functional assays were performed to validate the role of POLD2 in the progression of LUAD.ResultsOur study demonstrated that POLD2 is markedly upregulated in LUAD, which is associated with unfavorable patient outcomes and more advanced disease stages. Functional analyses revealed that POLD2 is critically involved in essential biological process (BP), including DNA repair, cell cycle control, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), all of which are key contributors to tumor progression. Furthermore, immunological investigations, the core focus of this study, indicated that POLD2 is linked to an immunosuppressive microenvironment, a factor that may impair the efficacy of ICIs and thus compromise immunotherapy response. As an extended exploratory analysis to further explore POLD2’s therapeutic potential, molecular docking and MD simulations revealed strong binding interactions between POLD2 and MEK inhibitors, including PD0325901, selumetinib and trametinib. In vitro studies showed that suppressing POLD2 expression significantly decreased the proliferation, colony-forming ability and migratory capacity of A549 LUAD cells.ConclusionsOur results identify POLD2 as a promising prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target in LUAD, with particular relevance to predicting immunotherapy response—consistent with the core focus of this study. This finding not only provides important insights into the mechanisms underlying LUAD progression and treatment resistance but also lays a preliminary foundation for subsequent research on both immunotherapeutic optimization and targeted therapy (via MEK inhibitors) for LUAD.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), POLD2, tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), prognostic biomarker

Introduction

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with a 5-year survival rate consistently below 20% (1). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases, with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) representing the predominant histological subtype within this category (2). Early-stage LUAD is commonly treated by surgical intervention, radiotherapy and chemotherapy. Nevertheless, the risk of recurrence remains substantial even after complete tumor resection. Although recent advances in targeted therapy and immunotherapy have led to promising therapeutic improvements for individuals with LUAD, clinical outcomes continue to fall short of expectations. Moreover, considerable heterogeneity persists among LUAD patients (3,4). In recent years, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway have emerged as promising therapeutic strategies for patients with LUAD (5). However, the overall efficacy of ICIs is still suboptimal, with only a limited subset of LUAD patients demonstrating substantial clinical benefits following ICI therapy (6). Currently, numerous biomarkers have been investigated to predict the efficacy of ICI therapy, such as tumor mutation burden (TMB), PD-L1 and CTLA-4 (7). However, these biomarkers do not fully capture the heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment (TME) or the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME). Consequently, immunotherapy is effective in only a limited subset of cancer patients (8). Therefore, it is essential to identify new biomarkers for predicting the prognosis and effectiveness of immunotherapy.

DNA damage is a critical factor in the initiation and progression of multiple cancer types (9,10), including LUAD, primarily by inducing genetic mutations that promote tumorigenesis (11). However, DNA damage triggers DNA repair mechanisms preserving genomic integrity. The mismatch repair (MMR) pathway corrects base-pair mismatches and insertion and deletion loops that arise during DNA replication. Functional impairments in key proteins (including MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2) lead to microsatellite instability (MSI) and an elevated TMB. These impairments generate numerous neoantigens and increase tumor immunogenicity (12). Current research indicates that the MMR system is associated with both the advancement of tumors and the effectiveness of treatments across multiple cancer types (13,14). In colorectal and endometrial cancers, MMR deficiency or high MSI (MSI-H), has been established as a definitive biomarker for predicting the efficacy of ICIs. In lung cancer, DNA damage signaling and repair pathways play a significant role in determining prognosis and treatment outcomes (9,15). Nevertheless, the precise function of MMR-associated genes (MRRGs) in LUAD remains inadequately explored. Consequently, this research seeks to fill this important knowledge gap by examining the expression patterns of MRRGs. Specifically, we focus on the POLD2 gene and evaluate its influence on patient prognosis.

DNA polymerase δ (POLD) acts as the primary replicative polymerase and is essential for various DNA repair mechanisms (16). POLD is a heteromeric complex consisting of several distinct subunits. In humans, this complex primarily comprises the p125, p50, p68, and p12 subunits, encoded respectively by the genes POLD1, POLD2, POLD3, and POLD4 (17). POLD2 is an essential regulatory subunit of the POLD complex, playing a crucial role in DNA replication and damage repair mechanisms that are fundamental for preserving genomic stability. Studies have demonstrated that in colorectal and endometrial cancers, mutations in POLD2 often co-occur with pathogenic alterations in POLE or POLD1 (18). POLD2 expression is elevated in malignant cells, and elevated POLD2 levels correlate with unfavorable clinical outcomes in various malignancies, such as glioblastoma, ovarian carcinoma, breast cancer and bladder cancer (19-22). However, in LUAD, the biological role of POLD2 and its relationship with clinical outcomes have not been fully investigated.

This study employs an integrated strategy combining multi-omics data analysis, bioinformatics modeling and in vitro functional validation to explore the function of the MRRGs POLD2 in LUAD-with a primary focus on its role in the TIME and potential value in predicting immunotherapy response, consistent with the core theme of this study. Additionally, the research employs a range of analytical methods—such as gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), immune infiltration assessment (core analytical methods), as well as molecular docking and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations (extended exploratory methods). These methods comprehensively examine the diverse roles of POLD2 across multiple aspects, encompassing expression patterns, prognostic value, functional pathways, immune interactions (core research aspects) and therapeutic relevance (including both immunotherapeutic and targeted therapeutic potential). The main goals of this investigation are: to clarify the expression profile, prognostic impact, and biological activities of core gene POLD2, along with its relationship to the TIME (core research objectives); to evaluate the potential of POLD2 as a predictive biomarker, with an emphasis on its value in immunotherapy prediction and a supplementary assessment of its potential in targeted treatments; ; and to perform initial in vitro experiments to confirm the role of POLD2 in enhancing malignant phenotypes in LUAD cells, thereby supporting its potential as a biomarker and therapeutic target. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0534/rc).

Methods

Data acquisition and processing

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) datasets and corresponding clinical information were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) through the Genomic Data Commons portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project through the UCSC Xena platform (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/). The TCGA cohort comprised 598 samples, including 539 tumor tissue samples and 59 adjacent normal tissue samples. Prior to the analysis, genes with expression values below 1 in more than 50% of the samples were filtered out. For validation purposes, two independent datasets (GSE30219 and GSE31547), which contain comprehensive gene expression profiles, were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds). Furthermore, a set of twenty-three genes associated with the MMR pathway, referred to as MRRGs, was systematically selected from the KEGG_MISMATCH_REPAIR gene set in the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB, https://www.gsea-msigdb.org), as detailed in Table S1.

Survival analysis and clinical correlation analysis of POLD2

Clinicopathological characteristics and survival information were obtained from the TCGA and GEO datasets (specifically, the GSE30219 dataset). The association between POLD2 mRNA levels and various clinicopathological parameters—including T stage, N stage, M stage, pathological stage, age, gender, smoking history, pack-years of smoking, and residual tumor status—was assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for independent samples or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), as appropriate. Using the optimal cutoff value for POLD2 mRNA expression in tumor tissues, patients diagnosed with LUAD were categorized into the high POLD2 expression group and the low POLD2 expression group. A comprehensive survival analysis was conducted on the training cohort using the Kaplan-Meier method and univariate Cox regression methods, using the “survival” R package (version 4.2.1) (23). The main goal of these analyses was to systematically identify potential prognostic genes that could offer crucial insights into patient survival outcomes and probabilities.

Construction and validation of an MRRGs model

To identify critical biomarkers linked to the prognosis of LUAD, we retrieved relevant data from TCGA database and generated corresponding gene expression profiles. Utilizing the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithm implemented in the glmnet R package, we conducted a comprehensive screening to pinpoint significant biomarkers. Through LASSO Cox regression analysis, a total of 23 MRRGs were identified to reduce redundancy and prevent model overfitting. The optimal penalty parameter, lambda, was determined using a 10-fold cross-validation approach. Ultimately, nine genes were selected to construct a prognostic risk score model for predicting overall survival (OS) in patients with LUAD.

Differential expression analysis of POLD2

Differential gene expression analysis was conducted between groups with the high and the low expression levels of POLD2. Specifically, The High and the low expression levels were defined based on a median split of POLD2 expression values. Genes were considered differentially expressed if they had an adjusted P value less than 0.05 and an absolute log2-fold change greater than 2.

Functional enrichment analysis

Employing the “clusterProfiler” software (version 4.4.4), we performed comprehensive Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment assessments to clarify the functional significance of the data (24). Furthermore, we employed the GSEA software (version 4.2.1) to conduct a more in-depth investigation into the gene functions associated with variations in POLD2 expression levels. We placed specific emphasis on the distinct features observed between the high and the low expression groups. The analyses adhered to stringent criteria such that only outcomes with an adjusted P value less than 0.05 and a false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.25 were deemed statistically significant, thereby reinforcing the reliability and robustness of the findings.

Immune cell infiltration analysis and gene set variation analysis (GSVA)

We investigated the relationship between POLD2 expression and various infiltrating immune cells within the TIME using Spearman correlation analysis, with statistical significance defined as P<0.05. Gene sets of immunoregulatory factors, encompassing both immunosuppressants and immunostimulators, were curated from previously published literature (25-27). The relationship between various immunoregulatory factors and POLD2 expression was assessed through correlation analyses, which were subsequently visualized using lollipop plots. To enable clearer interpretation, immunosuppressants and immunostimulators were analyzed separately using the “GSVA” package (version 1.46). Furthermore, the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) method was employed via the “GSVA” R package to assess and compare infiltration levels of various immune cell types between the high-POLD2 and the low-POLD2 groups. This approach provided significant insights into the distinct immune microenvironments associated with the high- and low-POLD2 groups. To characterize immune cell infiltration differences between the high-POLD2 and low-POLD2 cohorts, we employed the Cell-type Identification by Estimating Relative Subsets of RNA Transcripts (CIBERSORT) algorithm (28). By utilizing gene expression signatures, this computational approach quantifies the abundance of 22 distinct immune populations, thereby elucidating dynamic alterations within the TIME across diverse malignancies.

Estimation of stromal and immune scores

The Estimation of Stromal and Immune cells in Malignant Tumor tissues using Expression data (ESTIMATE) method was employed to evaluate tumor purity. It also assessed the extent of stromal and immune cell infiltration (29). This approach uses gene expression profiles to quantify the infiltration levels of stromal and immune cells. It is implemented through the “estimate” R package. We computed stromal, immune, ESTIMATE, and tumor purity scores using RNA-seq data from the TCGA cohort. Subsequently, a Wilcoxon test was conducted to compare these scores between the tumor and normal tissue groups.

Immunotherapy response prediction

The Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) computational framework (accessible at http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) was employed to predict the likelihood of response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy.

Differential expressions of POLD2 mRNA and protein between LUAD tissues and adjacent tissues

Using the Wilcoxon test, we analyzed differences in POLD2 mRNA expression between LUAD tissues and adjacent normal tissues in the TCGA and GEO datasets. The Human Protein Atlas (HPA, http://www.proteinatlas.org) and the UALCAN database were used to examine POLD2 protein expression levels. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Development of nomograms

Using data from the TCGA cohort, we constructed nomograms that integrate clinical features and the POLD2 model using the ‘rms’ R package (version 6.3) to estimate the OS of LUAD cases. The predictive performance of these nomograms was thoroughly evaluated using time-dependent calibration curves that systematically compare the model’s predicted survival probabilities with the observed survival outcomes across different time points. Furthermore, both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were conducted to determine whether the POLD2 model serves as an independent prognostic indicator of OS in LUAD patients.

The half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) and molecular docking analysis

To predict IC50 of chemotherapy drugs, the “pRRophetic” R package was used. IC50 is a numerical measure that indicates a substance’s efficacy in blocking specific biological or biochemical processes. Concurrently, to gain deeper insights into how candidate drugs affect POLD2, molecular docking experiments were conducted at the atomic level to evaluate binding energies and interaction patterns between candidate drugs and the target. Molecular docking simulations help analyze ligand-target binding affinity and interaction mechanisms. By identifying ligands with high binding affinity and favorable interaction patterns, this approach facilitates the prioritization of drug targets for subsequent experimental validation and optimization of potential candidate drug designs. The POLD2 protein sequence was obtained from the PubChem website (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), and its structure was predicted using the AlphaFold server (https://alphafoldserver.com/). Drug structural data were sourced from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). Small molecules underwent hydrogenation, dehydration, and energy minimization using the YINFO platform, with POLD2 binding sites predicted (https://cloud.yinfotek.com/). The docking box size was set to cover the predicted active site, as described on the AutoDock website (http://autodock.scripps.edu/). Docking predictions were subsequently performed using the locally deployed version of AlphaFold3 (AF3) (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07487-w) to assess the reliability of docking results. The docked drug molecules and protein molecules were saved as two separate PDB files, which were then input into GROMACS v2025.4 (https://www.gromacs.org/) with the CHARMMER36 force field (https://mackerell.umaryland.edu/charmm_ff.shtml) for a 10 ns MD simulation at 310 K. The system was solvated using the TIP3P water model. The structural stability of the docking interface was then assessed via root-mean-square deviation (RMSD). The corrected trajectory was analyzed using pymol-open-source (https://github.com/schrodinger/pymol-open-source), a molecular visualization tool, and key timestamps were exported. Interaction analysis was performed using the contacts feature in UCSF ChimeraX (https://www.cgl.ucsf.edu/chimerax/), which identifies all direct interactions between atoms within the short range.

Cell culture and transfection procedures

The A549 cell line was obtained from the National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures located in Shanghai, China. These cells were cultured in RPMI1640 medium (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA), supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin antibiotics. Subsequently, POLD2 gene silencing was achieved via transfection of the cells. Small interfering RNA (siRNA) specifically designed to target POLD2 (referred to as siPOLD2) and a corresponding negative control were purchased from Rui Bo Biology in Guangzhou, China. Three different siRNA sequences targeting POLD2 (designated siPOLD2-1, siPOLD2-2 and siPOLD2-3), as well as a scrambled negative control siRNA (siNC), are detailed in Table 1. Transfection was carried out using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Following transfection, A549 cells were incubated for 48 hours to achieve effective knockdown of POLD2 expression.

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was isolated from A549 cells using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the SuperScript™III First-Strand Synthesis SuperMix kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). RT-qPCR was performed to measure messenger RNA (mRNA) expression levels of POLD2. The primers, designed by Shenggong (Shanghai, China), are as follows: forward primer for POLD2: 5'-GCAGTGGAGTGGGAGTGAAGA-3' and reverse primer: 5'-AGGTTGTGCTCCTCGCTGAC-3'.

Western blot analysis

Proteins were extracted from A549 cells using RIPA lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) containing a protease inhibitor cocktail (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Merck Millipore, USA). The membranes were incubated with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) at room temperature for 1 hour to block non-specific binding. Immunoblotting was carried out with the following primary antibodies: rabbit polyclonal anti-POLD2 antibody (Proteintech, Wuhan, China; diluted 1:2,000) and rabbit monoclonal anti-β-actin antibody (Abcam, USA; diluted 1:2,000). After primary antibody incubation, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies. Protein bands were detected using X-ray film (Huadong Medicine, Hangzhou, China) and quantified with appropriate imaging software.

Wound healing assay

In the wound healing assay, cells were seeded in 6-well plates. A sterile pipette tip was used to create scratches in the cell monolayer, oriented perpendicular to a pre-marked reference line. Following scratch formation, images of the wound area were captured using a light microscope. Cell migration was then assessed at the predetermined time points of 0 and 48 hours. All experiments were conducted in triplicate to ensure reproducibility of results.

Cell proliferation assay

Cells were collected by centrifugation and subsequently resuspended in culture medium to attain a concentration of 104 cells per milliliter. Next, 100 µL of the culture medium was aliquoted into each well of a 96-well plate. Cell proliferation was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (Dojindo Laboratories, Kumamoto, Japan), and absorbance readings were taken at 24, 48 and 72 hours according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The optical density at 450 nm (OD450) was measured, and the data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism software to evaluate cell proliferative activity. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

Colony formation assay

For the colony formation assay, transfected A549 cells were plated in 6-well plates at a density of 1,000 cells per well and were cultured for 10 days. After incubation, the resulting colonies were fixed using a 4% paraformaldehyde solution (Shenggong, Shanghai, China) for 30 minutes, followed by staining with a 0.1% crystal violet solution (Shenggong). The colonies were then quantified for further analysis.

Statistical analysis

Bioinformatics analyses and the utilization of R packages were performed with R software (version 4.2.1). To evaluate differences across multiple groups, ANOVA was conducted, followed by pairwise comparisons using Tukey’s post hoc test. For comparisons involving two groups, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used.

Results

Prognostic model for LUAD based on MRRGs

A comparative analysis was conducted to evaluate the expression levels of 23 MRRGs in LUAD tissues relative to normal controls. The analysis showed that 17 of these MRRGs were significantly upregulated in the LUAD tissue samples (Figure 1A). Moreover, the LASSO algorithm was used to further refine the selection of 23 candidate genes to construct a robust prognostic model (Figure 1B,1C). This approach effectively pinpointed nine pivotal genes characterized by minimal cross-validation error: MSH2, MSH3, PMS2, POLD2, POLD4, RFC1, RPA1, RPA2 and EXO1. A univariate Cox regression analysis was conducted to assess the relationship between nine key gene expression levels and OS in patients with LUAD, as illustrated in Figure 1D. Additionally, MSH2, PMS2, POLD2, POLD4, RPA2 and EXO1 were upregulated, whereas RFC1 was downregulated in LUAD tissues (Figure 1E).

Further studies investigated the interactions among these nine pivotal genes in LUAD. Using Spearman correlation analysis, we identified significant associations between POLD2 and the other eight key genes (Figure 2A-2C). POLD2 expression levels are significantly elevated in multiple cancers including LUAD

Analysis of POLD2 expression across pan-cancer datasets underscores its important role in tumor biology. Our findings indicate that POLD2 expression is elevated in 26 distinct cancer types, including LUAD (Figure S1A,S1B). Notably, POLD2 levels correlate with immune cell infiltration (Figure S1C). POLD2 interacts with immune-related components, influencing both tumor progression and the immune microenvironment within malignancies.

Functional enrichment analysis of POLD2 in LUAD

In the TCGA database, LUAD patients were categorized into two groups based on the median expression level of POLD2: a high-expression group and a low-expression group. Differential analysis was then performed between these groups. The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) identified were analyzed using GO functional analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis. The GO enrichment analysis includes three modules: biological process (BP), cellular component (CC) and molecular function (MF). The detailed results are presented in Figure 3 and Table 2. The BP include nucleosome assembly, chromatin assembly during DNA replication, formation of protein-DNA complexes and chromatin remodeling. The most significant MF are protein heterodimerization, bitter taste receptor activity, taste receptor activity and glucuronosyltransferase activity. CC showing the highest enrichment levels are the nucleosome, DNA packaging complex, protein-DNA complex and nuclear chromosome. Key KEGG pathway entries include neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation, olfactory transduction, taste transduction and viral carcinogenesis (Figure 3A,3B; Table 2). Furthermore, GSEA revealed that the DEGs associated with POLD2 showed significant enrichment in several key pathways, such as the MYC signaling pathway, the PLK1 pathway, E2F-mediated regulation of DNA replication and the ATR pathway (Figure 4A-4C).

Relationship between POLD2 expression and survival outcomes in LUAD

Our analyses revealed that POLD2 was significantly upregulated in LUAD, which was correlated with poor patient prognosis and advanced disease stage (Figure 5). Within the validation cohorts (GSE30219 and GSE31547), tumor tissues exhibited elevated mRNA expression levels of POLD2 (Figure 5A,5C). Meanwhile, as illustrated in Figure S2A, analysis of the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CTPAC) database revealed that the POLD2 protein showed higher expression levels in LUAD than in normal tissues. Furthermore, immunohistochemical (IHC) images obtained from the HPA database revealed that POLD2 protein expression was markedly higher in tumor tissues than in normal tissues (Figure S2B). These results indicate that POLD2 is differentially expressed and upregulated in LUAD tissues. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis further confirmed the strong predictive capability of our risk model (Figure 5B,5D). To assess the robustness of the LASSO prediction model based on the TCGA dataset, we performed external validation with the GSE30219 dataset. Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that higher expression levels of POLD2 were significantly correlated with reduced survival rates (Figure 5E). Furthermore, the findings indicated that elevated POLD2 expression was significantly correlated with higher T, N and M stages (Figure 5F-5H).

POLD2 expression levels correlate with multiple clinicopathological features of LUAD

Significant differences in T stage, N stage, M stage, pathologic stage, OS event, disease-specific survival (DSS) event, age, and number pack years smoked were observed between patients with LUAD with the high and the low POLD2 expression levels (Figure 7A-7H). Table 3 presents the association between clinicopathological characteristics and POLD2 expression levels in LUAD patients. Elevated POLD2 expression correlated with reduced survival rates. Subsequently, a nomogram was constructed to predict survival outcomes, integrating T-stage, N-stage, M-stage, pack-years of smoking and POLD2 expression levels (Figure 8A). Significantly, the expression of POLD2 has shown potential to improve the precision of survival probability predictions at 1, 3 and 5 years. Furthermore, calibration plots are used to evaluate the accuracy of the model’s predictions (Figure 8B). Univariate Cox regression analysis showed that T stage, N stage, M stage and POLD2 expression are independent prognostic indicators in patients with LUAD. Moreover, multivariate Cox regression analysis identified T stage and N stage as independent prognostic factors (Figure 8C).

Molecular docking of POLD2 with candidate drugs

Using data from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database, we assessed variations in the IC50 values of chemotherapeutic agents among groups with differing levels of POLD2 expression. Specifically, the IC50 values for PD0325901, Selumetinib and Trametinib were significantly higher in the high POLD2 expression group, as illustrated in Figure 14A-14C. Consequently, these findings indicate significant differences in IC50 values for targeted therapies across distinct POLD2 expression categories. In parallel, we conducted molecular docking simulations to investigate the interactions between POLD2 and three MEK inhibitors—PD-0325901, selumetinib and trametinib. These analyses were performed using AutoDock Vina, using the three-dimensional protein structure predicted by AF3. The docking poses generated by AutoDock Vina and AlphaFold3 were aligned and compared. The calculatedRMSD values for the superimposed complexes were 0.555 Å, 0.570 Å and 0.583 Å for PD-0325901, Selumetinib and Trametinib, respectively. In structural biology, an RMSD below 2.0 Å is commonly accepted as evidence of high conformational similarity. Furthermore, the binding free energies (ΔG) for the three drug-POLD2 complexes, as estimated by Vina, were −6.6, −6.1 and −5.2 kcal/mol. In molecular docking studies, binding affinities lower (more negative) than −5.0 kcal/mol are generally interpreted as indicative of robust and favorable molecular interactions (Figure 14D-14F). To further confirm the stability of the binding between each small molecule and POLD2, we conducted MD simulations of each ligand-protein system for 10 ns at 310 K and atmospheric pressure in an aqueous solvent environment. During the simulations, we recorded the dynamic changes in the system, particularly focusing on the movement of the ligands. We found that the interaction sites between PD-0325901 and POLD2 changed significantly, shifting from the initial Gln37, Pro38, Phe387 and Thr390 to Gln37, Arg40, Pro310, Leu311, Pro313 and Leu327, with substantial fluctuations in RMSD values during the 10 ns MD simulation. The interaction interface between selumetinib and POLD2 also underwent substantial changes: the drug molecule flipped to the other side within 0–6 ns, rotating around the bromine atom on the benzene ring and Pro396, which acted as the axis. The interaction sites shifted from the initial Gln37, Pro38, Leu41, Gly42, Lys389 and Pro396 to Phe64, Asn67, Arg372 and Pro396, and the RMSD fluctuation range was smaller than that of the POLD2-PD-0325901 system. The changes in the interaction interface between trametinib and POLD2 were relatively minor compared to the other two ligand-protein systems. The drug molecule remained active within the pocket throughout the 10 ns simulation, with interaction sites shifting from Pro38, Arg40, Gly42, Tyr385, Tyr388, and Thr390 to Pro38, Thr390, and Pro396. Abundant interactions between the drug molecule and Pro38/Thr390 were formed at 3 ns, and the RMSD fluctuation range was smaller than that of the POLD2-PD-0325901 system, similar to the POLD2-selumetinib system (Figure 14G-14I).

Biological significances of POLD2 in LUAD progression

To investigate the functional role of POLD2 in LUAD, a series of in vitro assays was performed (Figure 15). Western blot and RT-qPCR analyses confirmed that silencing POLD2 led to a significant decrease in POLD2 mRNA expression in A549 cells (Figure 15A,15B). Moreover, colony formation assays revealed a notably higher number of colonies in the siNC group compared to the siPOLD2 group (Figure 15D,15E). Data from the CCK-8 assay demonstrated a substantial reduction in the proliferation of A549 cells following POLD2 knockdown (Figure 15C). Wound healing experiments further showed impaired migratory capacity of A549 cells after POLD2 knockdown (Figure 15F,15G). The observed inhibition of LUAD cell growth and motility upon POLD2 depletion indicates that POLD2 contributes to the progression of LUAD.

Discussion

LUAD is the most prevalent histological subtype of NSCLC. Its high incidence, particularly among non-smokers, presents substantial challenges for early detection and the development of effective therapeutic approaches. LUAD is frequently identified at advanced stages, when treatment options are limited, leading to a 5-year survival rate below 20% (31). Existing management strategies encompass surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy. However, the intricacies of tumor heterogeneity and resistance mechanisms complicate clinical management and influence patient outcomes (32). Consequently, the discovery of new prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets is essential for improving treatment effectiveness and enhancing patient survival prospects in LUAD.

This study explored the role of the MRRGs POLD2 in LUAD, a prevalent and aggressive subtype of NSCLC which poses a significant clinical burden. Our objective was to develop a reliable prognostic model that incorporates POLD2 along with other mismatch repair-related genes. To achieve this, we adopted an integrative strategy, combining multi-omics data analysis, bioinformatics modeling and in vitro experimental validation. We utilized extensive datasets from publicly available repositories, including TCGA, GEO and GTEx, and applied LASSO regression to pinpoint critical prognostic indicators. This comprehensive approach not only strengthens the validity of our results but also highlights the translational relevance of POLD2 as both a prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target in LUAD. Consequently, our work provides a conceptual framework for subsequent investigations that aim to enhance clinical outcomes for LUAD patients.

Our prognostic model, developed from MRRGs and focusing specifically on POLD2, demonstrates that elevated POLD2 expression is significantly correlated with reduced OS, underscoring its potential as a prognostic biomarker. This finding not only corroborates earlier research highlighting the involvement of MMR genes in cancer prognosis but also identifies POLD2 as a key molecular driver of tumor aggressiveness. The application of LASSO Cox regression to develop a risk model from 23 MRRGs, such as POLD2, constitutes a novel approach for categorizing patients according to their anticipated survival outcomes (33,34). Furthermore, incorporating clinical parameters into a nomogram improves its practical utility in clinical settings, indicating that POLD2 could function as a significant biomarker for tailored therapeutic approaches in LUAD (35).

Our functional enrichment analyses reveal the mechanistic pathways associated with POLD2 expression. A key pathway identified is the E2F-mediated regulation of DNA replication. E2F transcription factors play a vital role in cell cycle progression and DNA synthesis. Their dysregulation is frequently observed in cancer. Elevated POLD2 expression could enhance the transcriptional activity of E2F target genes, leading to uncontrolled cell proliferation and thereby contributing to the aggressive characteristics of LUAD (33). This indicates that POLD2 serves not only as a prognostic indicator but also as a promising therapeutic target, since suppressing its expression may interfere with E2F signaling and inhibit tumor progression. Another pathway of interest is the ATR signaling cascade, which plays a crucial role in cellular responses to DNA damage. The involvement of POLD2 in DNA repair mechanisms suggests that its overexpression may enhance cancer cell survival under genotoxic stress, thereby promoting tumor progression (33). The association between POLD2 and the ATR pathway underscores its potential as a biomarker for predicting treatment responses, particularly in therapies that induce DNA damage. Targeting POLD2 may enhance the sensitivity of LUAD cells to these treatments, which offers a new approach to overcoming therapeutic resistance. Finally, our analysis revealed an enrichment of genes associated with NET formation, indicating a potential connection between POLD2 expression and the inflammatory TME. The involvement of NETs has been linked to the promotion of tumor growth and metastasis through the establishment of a pro-tumorigenic niche (36,37). The inverse relationship observed between POLD2 expression and the infiltration of multiple immune cell types suggests that elevated POLD2 levels may promote an immunosuppressive TME, potentially compromising the effectiveness of ICB therapies. This insight underscores the importance of further exploring the interactions among POLD2, immune regulation and treatment responses in LUAD. Such investigations could inform the design of combined therapeutic strategies that simultaneously target POLD2-related mechanisms and immune system modulation. In conclusion, the signaling pathways linked to POLD2 not only elucidate its role in LUAD progression but also underscore its promise as both a therapeutic target and a prognostic indicator. Subsequent research efforts should prioritize validating these pathways and assessing the clinical relevance of targeting POLD2 in conjunction with established treatment approaches.

Our immunological analysis reveals that POLD2 plays a significant regulatory role in the immune microenvironment in LUAD. Specifically, POLD2 expression is negatively correlated with multiple immune-stimulatory factors, indicating its potential role in immunosuppression. This finding is consistent with prior research highlighting the crucial role of the TIME on cancer progression and therapeutic outcomes (38). The diminished presence of critical immune cell populations, including CD8+ T cells and dendritic cells, with elevated POLD2 expression indicates that POLD2 may promote a “cold” TME. This phenotype is frequently linked to suboptimal outcomes of immunotherapy (39). This finding underscores the potential of POLD2 as a biomarker for predicting the efficacy of immunotherapy in LUAD patients. Additionally, the increased infiltration of Th2 cells in the POLD2 high-expression group may contribute to the polarization of the immune microenvironment. Th2 cells are primarily involved in humoral immune responses and can facilitate tumor progression by secreting cytokines that create an immunosuppressive environment (40). In the context of elevated POLD2 expression, the shift towards a Th2-dominant response likely facilitates tumor immune evasion, thereby complicating therapeutic strategies to enhance anti-tumor immunity. This area warrants in-depth investigation to elucidate how POLD2 influences immune cell dynamics and the implications for therapeutic interventions. Finally, the ESTIMATE analysis reveals that elevated POLD2 expression correlates with reduced immune and stromal scores, which implies a weakened immune landscape in the TME. This observation is significant as it highlights POLD2’s potential as a prognostic indicator for immune-related outcomes in LUAD. The TIDE model’s prediction of increased TIDE scores in the group with high POLD2 expression reinforces the notion that POLD2 could act as a predictor for resistance to ICIs. Collectively, these findings highlight the need for further comprehensive investigation of POLD2’s function in regulating the immune microenvironment. Such insights could provide valuable insights into developing more effective immunotherapeutic approaches for patients with LUAD.

Our investigation further delineated the influence of POLD2 on fourteen prevalent biological functions within malignancies. The data indicate that POLD2 participates extensively in a broad spectrum of cellular processes, encompassing EMT, DNA repair mechanisms, the DNA damage response, cell cycle control, apoptosis, proliferation, angiogenesis, inflammatory responses, as well as differentiation, quiescence, and stemness characteristics. Substantial evidence now confirms that impaired DNA repair, aberrant cell cycle regulation, and EMT serve as pivotal drivers of the evolution of LUAD from incipient lesions to invasive states. Maintaining genomic stability relies fundamentally on the fidelity of DNA repair systems. In LUAD contexts, the suppression of primary repair routes, specifically nucleotide excision repair and homologous recombination, leads to the sustained accrual of DNA lesions. This accumulation fosters the acquisition of oncogenic mutations and intensifies genomic instability (41). Consequently, POLD2 may drive tumor initiation and progression by mediating faulty DNA repair events and the ensuing buildup of genetic alterations. Under normal physiological circumstances, cell-cycle checkpoints, such as the G1/S transition point, halt progression when DNA damage remains unrepaired, thereby safeguarding genomic integrity. Conversely, in LUAD, these surveillance mechanisms are often compromised through mutations or functional loss of critical regulators like p53 and Rb. Such dysfunction permits uninterrupted division despite DNA damage, hastening the expansion of malignant clones (42). Prior research has shown that silencing POLD2 triggers apoptosis and induces G1-phase arrest, underscoring its essential function in DNA replication and repair (33). EMT is widely recognized as a central mechanism governing metastasis and invasion in LUAD (43). This process is modulated by intricate signaling networks, notably the TGF-β/SMAD and Wnt/β-catenin pathways (44). Activation of these cascades, directed by transcription factors Snail, ZEB1, and Twist, suppresses E-cadherin while elevating N-cadherin and vimentin levels. These changes diminish intercellular adhesion and augment the migratory and invasive capabilities of tumors (45). We posit that POLD2 promotes EMT, thereby enhancing the motility and invasiveness of LUAD cells and facilitating metastatic dissemination. Current findings demonstrate a significant association between POLD2 expression levels and an immunosuppressive TME. We hypothesize that POLD2 shapes the immune landscape by modulating the aforementioned biological activities—including DNA repair, cell cycle regulation, and EMT—thereby indirectly affecting responses to immunotherapy. For example, POLD2-mediated EMT not only enhances metastatic potential but may also recruit immunosuppressive populations, such as regulatory T cells and M2-type macrophages, while limiting effector T cell infiltration. This dynamic further consolidates an immunosuppressive niche, ultimately undermining the effectiveness of immunotherapeutic interventions.

Our molecular docking analysis indicates possible direct binding interactions between POLD2 and certain kinase inhibitors, revealing a new avenue for therapeutic intervention (46). In the group exhibiting high POLD2 expression, resistance to MEK inhibitors was observed, and targeting POLD2 may enhance LUAD cells’ sensitivity to targeted therapies. This finding is consistent with the widespread consensus supporting the use of biomarker-driven approaches to optimize treatment regimens and improve patient outcomes regarding therapeutic resistance (47). Finally, we used siRNA to suppress POLD2 expression in A549 LUAD cells. The results showed that silencing POLD2 markedly inhibited cellular proliferation and metastatic capacity. These results strongly indicate that POLD2 plays a critical role in the tumor development, progression and metastasis of LUAD cells. Notably, our research identifies POLD2 as a promising therapeutic target for developing new treatments for LUAD.

The methodological strengths of this study are reflected in its comprehensive integration of multi-omics data analysis, bioinformatics modeling, and in vitro functional validation, which together reinforce the reliability of the conclusions. Utilizing extensive datasets from public databases including TCGA, GEO and GTEx, this study applied LASSO regression to develop a robust prognostic model that highlights criticalMRRGs, such as POLD2, as significant biomarkers in LUAD. Through the implementation of GSEA, immune infiltration assessment, molecular docking and MD simulations, a comprehensive evaluation of POLD2 was conducted, encompassing its expression profiles, prognostic significance, biological functions and therapeutic responses. This systematic methodology not only enhances the credibility of the findings but also establishes a solid foundation for future research aimed at elucidating the molecular mechanisms driving LUAD progression and treatment resistance.

However, this study still has several limitations. First, while our multi-omics bioinformatic assessment initially indicated that elevated POLD2 levels are associated with an immunosuppressive TME in LUAD, the precise molecular mechanisms governing POLD2-mediated modulation of immune cell infiltration, functional states, and the expression of immune-related molecules remain to be fully elucidated. Specifically, the direct or indirect regulatory roles of POLD2 in the recruitment, polarization, and effector activities of tumor-infiltrating immune populations—such as CD8⁺ T cells, regulatory T cells, and macrophages—as well as the upstream and downstream signaling cascades controlling immune checkpoint molecules, chemokines, and inflammatory mediators, require further experimental validation. Addressing these mechanistic gaps is a critical priority for our future research. In future studies, we plan to employ in vitro co-culture systems, in vivo animal models, and multi-omics functional assays to delineate the specific regulatory pathways connecting POLD2 to immune cell dynamics and the expression of immune-associated molecules. This comprehensive approach aims to clarify the specific contribution of POLD2 to the formation of an immunosuppressive niche and its impact on immunotherapeutic outcomes in LUAD. Second, while molecular docking and MD simulations suggest possible interactions between POLD2 and MEK inhibitors, direct binding relationships require further validation via biochemical and cellular experiments. Finally, as a critical accessory subunit of POLD, POLD2 plays an indispensable role in facilitating DNA replication and coordinating DNA damage repair. While our current investigation primarily characterizes the prognostic value and immunomodulatory capacity of POLD2 in LUAD, its overexpression could theoretically augment DNA repair proficiency in malignant cells. Such enhancement might inadvertently promote genomic instability and drive the acquisition of resistance to DNA-damaging chemotherapeutic agents. Consequently, subsequent research should prioritize empirical validation to clarify the correlation between POLD2 abundance and tumor sensitivity to DNA-damaging therapeutics. Addressing these limitations in future research will be essential for further elucidating the role of POLD2 in LUAD and its clinical implications.

In summary, this study offers compelling evidence for the crucial role of the DNA MMR gene POLD2 in LUAD, underscoring its potential as both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target, with a particular focus on its value in predicting immunotherapy response, which aligns with the core theme of this study. Elevated POLD2 expression is strongly associated with poor patient outcomes and advanced clinical stages, indicating its involvement in tumor progression through mechanisms such as cell cycle regulation and chromatin remodeling. Moreover, a key finding of this study is that the association between increased POLD2 levels and an immunosuppressive TME affects the efficacy of ICIs, suggesting that POLD2 may serve as a potential predictor of immunotherapy resistance in LUAD patients. As an extended exploratory finding, molecular docking and MD simulations suggest that POLD2 can be directly targeted by MEK inhibitors. In vitro experiments further confirmed that POLD2 promotes LUAD progression to some extent. Together, these findings not only deepen our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying LUAD but also open avenues for future research aimed at translating these insights into clinical practice, particularly in personalized medicine and targeted therapeutic strategies.

Conclusions

POLD2 may serve as a promising prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in LUAD, with particular emphasis on its value in predicting immunotherapy response—consistent with the core focus of this study. It influences tumor prognosis by modulating an immunosuppressive TIME, which is closely related to immunotherapy efficacy. Targeted inhibition of POLD2 may represent an effective strategy for LUAD treatment, including both immunotherapeutic optimization and potential targeted therapy (via MEK inhibitors) as an extended exploration. Our research provides a robust foundation for advancing LUAD therapy and paves the way for its future clinical translation.
