Introduction

The problem is to learn a halfspace efficiently when a constant fraction of the training data has been corrupted by an adversary. In the malicious-noise model of
32
, an adversary observes the clean sample, then replaces an
\eta
-fraction of the (instance, label) pairs by arbitrary points of its choosing, with full knowledge of the learner and the clean distribution; the goal is to output
\hat{w}
whose error on the
clean
inliers is at most
\varepsilon
. This is the strongest of the standard corruption models, and the central question is how large a noise rate
\eta
a polynomial-time learner can tolerate.

One influential line of work answers this through a single algorithmic device: hinge-loss minimization, later reweighted to suppress outliers.
56
observed that plain hinge-loss minimization already resists noise when the clean marginal places dense mass in a band (a “pancake”) around every point’s projection onto the separating direction.
54
turned this into a constant-noise-rate guarantee for log-concave-mixture marginals by
reweighting
the hinge: the weights come from a degree-
2
outlier-removal program (a variance constraint), and the analysis controls the dirty contribution to the hinge gradient through the quantity
\mathrm{LSN}(q\circ S_{D})=\sup_{\lVert w\rVert\leq 1}\sum_{i\in S_{D}}q_{i}\lvert w\cdot x_{i}\rvert
. The resulting algorithm tolerates a constant noise rate, but the constant comes with two limitations that the authors state plainly. The tolerable rate is tiny (
\eta_{0}=2^{-32}
in the published version), and
54
notes it “cannot be made close to the optimal breakdown point of
\tfrac{1}{2}
due to inherent limitations of our approach”; and the required margin carries an extra logarithmic factor,
\gamma=\Omega(\log(1/\varepsilon)/\sqrt{d})
, a “logarithmic dependence on
1/\varepsilon
that appears less natural” than the
\Omega(1/\sqrt{d})
margin one would hope for. The same two features recur in the sparse follow-up of
61
.

The margin’s dependence on
\varepsilon
is a chicken-and-egg problem. A margin is a fixed property of the data distribution, whereas the accuracy
\varepsilon
is the learner’s target, set at will once the problem is in hand; requiring
\gamma=\Omega(\log(1/\varepsilon)/\sqrt{d})
couples the two backwards, demanding
better-separated data
exactly when the learner asks for smaller error, a property the data cannot supply on request. One would rather fix the geometry once and buy accuracy with computation, not with margin.

This paper explains both limitations. We argue that they are not artifacts of a particular constant or a loose inequality, but consequences of a single resource that governs the whole approach: the polynomial, equivalently Sum-of-Squares (SoS),
degree
of the outlier-removal certificate. The reweighted-hinge method needs two distributional facts about the clean marginal, and both are supplied by certificates that read off only the low-order moments: a
lower
bound on the mass of the dense pancake (so the hinge has signal), and an
upper
bound on a reweighted moment of the dirty points (so the outlier program can shrink
\mathrm{LSN}
). Each is a degree-
2t
SoS proof from the first
2t
moments. We show that the degree these proofs require is exactly what controls the margin factor and the noise rate, and that
54
’s degree-
2
program sits at a corner of a tight frontier.

We frame the contribution, throughout, in terms of degree: degree is to this method what the approximate degree of a predicate is to the polynomial method in algorithm design – the parameter that simultaneously buys the guarantee and bounds it. Our two lower bounds are degree barriers; our algorithm is a degree-
2t
program that traces the frontier the barriers cut out. As with any barrier of this kind, the scope is essential and we state it up front: the lower bounds are against the
moment/SoS-certificate method
– any procedure whose only distributional input is the degree-
\leq 2t
certified moments of the clean marginal. They are not information-theoretic, and they are not unconditional Statistical-Query hardness. The margin–degree barrier strengthens, per direction, to a genuine degree-
2t
Sum-of-Squares lower bound (
Remark

3.1
); lifting it to the full
d
-dimensional reweighting program jointly is open for
t\geq 2
(
Section

5.2
). This is precisely the class of methods to which
54
’s “our approach” belongs, which is why the barriers explain that line’s admitted limits rather than contradicting them.

The margin–degree tradeoff.

Our first result (
Theorem

4.1
) is a barrier on certifying band mass. Fix a direction
w
and let
U=w\cdot x
be the (centered, log-concave) projection with standard deviation
s
. Consider a band of half-width
\tau=\Theta(s)
centered at
c
, a distance
\lvert c\rvert
off the origin. Any degree-
2t
SoS certificate that proves
\Pr[\,\lvert U-c\rvert\leq\tau\,]\geq\rho=\Omega(1)
from the first
2t
moments of
U
requires

2t=\Omega\big((\lvert c\rvert/s)^{2}\big)\quad\text{(sub-Gaussian)},\qquad 2t=\Omega\big(\lvert c\rvert/s\big)\quad\text{(sub-exponential)}.

A certificate learner that reaches error
\varepsilon
must certify the pancake around all but an
\varepsilon
-fraction of the sample projections, whose worst case is an
\varepsilon
-quantile of the marginal, an off-center band at
\lvert c\rvert/s=\Theta(\log(1/\varepsilon))
for the sub-exponential tails of a general log-concave law. Substituting gives the dichotomy: such a learner must pay
either

t=\Omega(\log(1/\varepsilon))

n^{\Omega(\log(1/\varepsilon))}

or

\gamma=\Omega(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\sqrt{d})
. The
\log(1/\varepsilon)
in
54

conserved

The same calculation explains a finer point: why

rather than

\alpha=1

and the degree bound is linear in
\lvert c\rvert/s

2

The degree-
2t
algorithm.

Our third result (

\leq\varepsilon

The breakdown floor.

Finally (
Proposition

4.11
), a two-point argument shows the noise tolerance has a hard ceiling that no algorithm of any kind can cross: under malicious noise with a
\gamma
-margin log-concave mixture, no learner attains clean inlier error below
\eta/(2(1-\eta))\geq\eta/2

Theorem

4.9

\Theta(\eta)

1.1
Technical overview

Three ideas drive the results; we sketch each before the formal proofs in
Sections

3
and
4
and the appendix. All three are facets of the same Christoffel-function picture (
Section

3
): Gauss quadrature locates where the Christoffel function resolves a band, the moment cloak realizes the smallest unresolved spike, and the degree-
2t
Hölder bound is that detector run as a certificate.

(a) Gauss quadrature beats the moment problem.

A degree-
2t
certificate of band mass is, after a minorant reduction, a polynomial
p
of degree
\leq 2t
with
p\leq\mathbf{1}_{B}
together with an SoS proof, from the moments alone, that
\int p\geq\rho
. Because the proof uses only
m_{0},\dots,m_{2t}
, its conclusion must hold for
every
nonnegative measure matching those moments. Gauss quadrature produces one such measure explicitly: the
(t+1)
-point measure on the roots of the degree-
(t+1)
orthogonal polynomial of
\mu
reproduces all moments up to order
2t+1
, and it is supported on just
t+1
atoms. If the band
B
contains
no
quadrature node, this witness assigns it mass zero, so the best certifiable value is zero and no degree-
2t
certificate can exist. A band stays node-free as long as it lies beyond the largest root, which sits at
\Theta(s\sqrt{t})
for sub-Gaussian weights and
\Theta(s\,t)
for sub-exponential ones. An off-center band at distance
\lvert c\rvert
is thus invisible below degree
(\lvert c\rvert/s)^{2}
, the source of the tradeoff. The largest-root bound is elementary in the two cases we name (Jacobi-matrix eigenvalues with Gershgorin for the sub-Gaussian
2s\sqrt{t+1}
and sub-exponential
O(st)
endpoints); the fully general log-concave exponent is the one place we invoke a black box, the exponential-weight asymptotics of
43
, and we flag it.

(b) The moment-cloaked spike.

The degree-
2
barrier needs an instance that a variance program cannot clean but a higher-degree program can. The construction is a
moment-cloaked spike
: two genuine log-concave clusters at
\pm(\sigma/\sqrt{\eta})\,e_{1}
, to which the adversary adds
\eta n
dirty points drawn from the same clusters. The dirty points are engineered to be invisible to the second moment (they spend exactly the variance budget,
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{S_{D}}(e_{1}\cdot x_{i})^{2}=\eta\cdot(\sigma/\sqrt{\eta})^{2}=\sigma^{2}
) while being first-moment-aligned, so they contribute
\sum_{S_{D}}\lvert e_{1}\cdot x_{i}\rvert=\sqrt{\eta}\,n\sigma
to
\mathrm{LSN}
. Because the variance constraints see only the multiset of points, a label-oblivious program cannot distinguish the
\eta n
dirty points from the
2\eta n
genuine clean points at the same locations; removing its budget of
\leq\xi n
leaves at least a third of the spike weight, hence
\mdmathbb{E}[\mathrm{LSN}]\geq\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\eta}\,n\sigma
. A degree-
4
program escapes, because the spike inflates the fourth moment to
\bar{\sigma}^{4}/\eta\gg\bar{\sigma}^{4}
and is no longer cloaked, which is exactly why the barrier is specific to degree
2
.

(c) Degree-
2t
Hölder on
\mathrm{LSN}
.

The algorithm is the dual move. Given a degree-
2t
SoS certificate that the reweighted
2t
-th moment is bounded,
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}q_{i}(w\cdot x_{i})^{2t}\leq(Ct)^{2t}\bar{\sigma}^{2t}
for all
w
, Hölder’s inequality with exponent
2t
splits
\mathrm{LSN}
as

\sum_{S_{D}}q_{i}\lvert w\cdot x_{i}\rvert\leq\Big(\sum_{S_{D}}q_{i}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{2t}}\Big(\sum_{S_{D}}q_{i}(w\cdot x_{i})^{2t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2t}}\leq(\eta n)^{1-\frac{1}{2t}}\big(n(Ct)^{2t}\bar{\sigma}^{2t}\big)^{\frac{1}{2t}}=\eta^{1-\frac{1}{2t}}n(Ct)\bar{\sigma}.

The even power
(w\cdot x)^{2t}
is a polynomial, so the moment bound is SoS-certifiable; feasibility of the clean-indicator weights is the certifiable-hypercontractivity bound on the empirical clean measure. The reason the exponent stops at
1
and never reaches the constant rate is the certifiable-moment constant: for general (sub-exponential) log-concave marginals it is
(Ct)^{2t}
, contributing the base
(Ct)
to the final bound, so larger
t
improves the
\eta
-exponent at a multiplicative cost in the constant, a genuine tradeoff, not a free lunch. This is the sense in which degree buys noise tolerance, and the reason the purchase is bounded.

Scope and open questions.

Two cautions are built into the results above and we restate them together. The barriers are
method-specific
: they rule out the degree-
2t
moment/SoS-certificate family, not all efficient algorithms, and not learners outside the one-shot certificate model (an iterative, localization-style filter sidesteps one-shot certification and is not covered). And the algorithm’s gain is
modest
: it removes the degree-
2

\rho/4
and does not march toward
1/2
. Several questions are left open, in increasing order of reach. The breakdown floor is rate-tight but not threshold-tight; the conjectured margin-dependent rate
\eta^{\star}=\Theta(\gamma/\sigma)
needs a multi-point or band-mass argument beyond two points. The degree barrier is a lower bound; a matching
upper

would make the tradeoff exactly

. The general-log-concave largest-root bound still rests on
43

Preliminaries

This section fixes the noise model, the distributional assumptions, the reweighted-hinge program whose dirty gradient we control, and the two algebraic objects that the rest of the paper turns on: the dense pancake and the degree-
2t
moment certificate of its band mass. The reader should leave with one picture in mind. Outlier removal in this line of work is a
polynomial
certificate of a band-mass lower bound, its
degree
is the resource, and the distributional input that powers it is a degree-
2t

Notation.

Vectors live in
\mdmathbb{R}^{d}
;
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is the Euclidean norm and
\langle u,v\rangle=u\cdot v
the inner product. For a halfspace direction

for the one-dimensional projection. A sample is

with

and a dirty part
S_{D}
(defined below). Reweighting weights are

, and
q\circ S
denotes the sample carrying those weights. We use
\bar{\sigma}^{2}(w)\coloneqq\mdmathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_{X}}\big[(w\cdot(x-\bar{\mu}))^{2}\big]
for the projected variance of the clean marginal, with
\bar{\mu}
its mean. Throughout,
t\geq 1
is the degree parameter: the certificate is a degree-
2t
object and the SoS program runs in time
n^{O(t)}
. We write
\mathbf{1}_{B}
for the indicator of a set
B\subseteq\mdmathbb{R}
, and
\hinge(w;x,y)\coloneqq\max\{0,\,1-y\,(w\cdot x)\}
for the hinge loss. Constants denoted

are absolute and may change line to line;
\varepsilon
is the target error.

Malicious noise.

We work in the malicious-noise model of
32
, the strongest sample-corruption model for which positive results of this kind are stated. An adversary with full knowledge of the learner, the clean distribution, and the realized clean sample selects an
\eta
-fraction of the
n

(\text{instance},\text{label})

pairs of its choosing; the remaining

examples, the

S_{C}
for the inliers and
S_{D}
for the at most
\eta n
corrupted examples. The objective is stated against the clean distribution only: the learner outputs
\hat{w}
and is charged its error on the inlier law,

\mathrm{err}_{\mathcal{D}_{X}}(\hat{w})\ \coloneqq\ \mdmathbb{P}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[\sign(\hat{w}\cdot x)\neq y\big]\ \leq\ \varepsilon,

where
\mathcal{D}
is the clean joint distribution with marginal

on instances. This is the goal of robustly learning the inlier halfspace, not of fitting the corrupted points. We state everything for malicious noise; the results extend, with a constant-factor smaller tolerable rate, to the stronger

(

in which the adversary may also delete clean inliers (

).

Distributional assumptions.

The clean marginal is a balanced mixture

\mathcal{D}_{X}\ =\ \frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\mathcal{D}_{j},

where each component
\mathcal{D}_{j}
is
log-concave
on
\mdmathbb{R}^{d}
with mean
\mu_{j}
satisfying
\lVert\mu_{j}\rVert\leq r
and covariance
\Sigma_{j}\preceq\sigma^{2}I
, in the regime
r=O(\sigma\sqrt{d})
. The clean labels are realized by a
\gamma
-margin halfspace: there is a unit
w^{\star}
with

for every clean
(x,y)
. This is exactly the setting of
54
and, for the single-component case, of
56

Two features of the assumption are load-bearing, and we flag them now because the constants later depend on them. First, the mixture
\mathcal{D}_{X}
is in general
not
log-concave, even though each
\mathcal{D}_{j}
is; the certificates must survive the mixing. Second, a log-concave law is only
sub-exponential
, not sub-Gaussian: a centered one-dimensional log-concave

obeys the linear moment growth
\lVert Z\rVert_{L^{2t}}\leq Ct\,\lVert Z\rVert_{L^{2}}
, so
\mdmathbb{E}[Z^{2t}]\leq(Ct)^{2t}\,(\mdmathbb{E}[Z^{2}])^{t}
, and this order in

cannot be improved (the Laplace law has
\mdmathbb{E}[Z^{2t}]/\mdmathbb{E}[Z^{2}]^{t}=(2t)!/2^{t}=(\Theta(t))^{2t}
)
(
44
)
. The exponent
2t
in
(Ct)^{2t}
, rather than the sub-Gaussian
(Ct)^{t}
, is the reason

\log(1/\varepsilon)
and not
\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}
, and we track it faithfully through
Lemma

4.13
.

Reweighted hinge and the dirty-gradient quantity.

The algorithmic template of
56
and
54
, which our degree-
2t
algorithm refines, minimizes a

hinge loss over a ball of radius
1/\gamma

\min_{\lVert w\rVert\leq 1/\gamma}\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}\,\hinge(w;x_{i},y_{i}),

where the weights

are produced by an outlier-removal program that downweights the corrupted examples. The only way the dirty examples can corrupt the minimizer is through the gradient they contribute, and a subgradient of
q_{i}\,\hinge(w;x_{i},y_{i})
has norm at most
q_{i}\,\lVert x_{i}\rVert
with the sign of
-y_{i}
. The quantity that bounds the dirty contribution is therefore the weighted one-dimensional spread of the corrupted points: for a set
S^{\prime}
of examples,

\mathrm{LSN}(q\circ S^{\prime})\ \coloneqq\ \sup_{\lVert w\rVert\leq 1}\ \sum_{i\in S^{\prime}}q_{i}\,\lvert w\cdot x_{i}\rvert.

(2.1)

By the subgradient bound above and the triangle inequality,
\mathrm{LSN}(q\circ S_{D})
is an upper bound on the norm of the dirty subgradient
\lVert\sum_{i\in S_{D}}q_{i}\,\partial_{w}\hinge(w;x_{i},y_{i})\rVert
, uniformly over
\lVert w\rVert\leq 1
. Controlling
\mathrm{LSN}(q\circ S_{D})
is thus the entire game on the dirty side; the weights
q
are good exactly when this quantity is small. Talwar’s and
54

2
(variance) program drives it to
\Theta(n\bar{\sigma}\,\eta^{1/2})

Section

4

2
barrier matching it.

Dense pancakes.

The clean (inlier) side is controlled through the dense-pancake structure of
56
. For a direction

, a center point’s projection, and a half-width
\tau

(band)

is the slab of width

points, which is what lets the reweighted hinge recover the inlier direction; the achievable noise rate is capped by the pancake density at
\rho/4
. The technical content is to

a band-mass lower bound from moments alone, which is the next object.

Degree-
2t
moment certificates.

This is the central object of the paper. An outlier-removal program of the SoS family does not see the distribution; its only distributional input is a bounded list of certified moments of the clean marginal, and whatever band-mass lower bound it can deduce from them by a Positivstellensatz proof. We make this precise via the standard polynomial
minorant
reduction.

Definition 2.1

(Degree-
2t
moment certificate of band mass)
.

Let
\mu
be the law of the projection
U=w\cdot x
under the clean marginal, with moments
m_{k}:=\mdmathbb{E}_{\mu}[U^{k}]
for
0\leq k\leq 2t
; let
B\subseteq\mdmathbb{R}
be a band; and let
\mdmathbb{R}[u]_{\leq 2t}
denote the real univariate polynomials in

of degree at most
2t
. A

2t
pseudo-expectation consistent with
\mu
is a linear functional
\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}\colon\mdmathbb{R}[u]_{\leq 2t}\to\mdmathbb{R}
satisfying

\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}[1]=1,\qquad\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}[q^{2}]\geq 0\ \ \text{for all }q\in\mdmathbb{R}[u]_{\leq t},\qquad\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}[u^{k}]=m_{k}\ \ \text{for }0\leq k\leq 2t.

A
degree-
2t
moment certificate
of the bound
\mdmathbb{P}_{\mu}[U\in B]\geq\rho
is a polynomial
p\in\mdmathbb{R}[u]_{\leq 2t}
, with coefficient vector
(p_{0},\dots,p_{2t})\in\mdmathbb{R}^{2t+1}
so that
p(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{2t}p_{k}\,u^{k}
, that is a
minorant
of the band indicator,

p(u)\ \leq\ \mathbf{1}_{B}(u)\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mdmathbb{R},

together with a degree-
2t

\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}[p]\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{2t}p_{k}\,m_{k}\ \geq\ \rho\qquad\text{for every consistent }\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}},

the equality being linearity of
\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}
together with
\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}[u^{k}]=m_{k}
(so the value
\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}[p]=\sum_{k}p_{k}m_{k}
is common to all consistent
\tilde{\mdmathbb{E}}
). Since

pointwise gives
\sum_{k=0}^{2t}p_{k}\,m_{k}=\mdmathbb{E}_{\mu}[p]\leq\mdmathbb{P}_{\mu}[U\in B]
, any such certificate transfers its bound
\sum_{k=0}^{2t}p_{k}\,m_{k}\geq\rho
to a genuine lower bound
\mdmathbb{P}_{\mu}[U\in B]\geq\rho
on the band mass.

The minorant reduction is what makes
Definition

2.1
the right notion of “what a degree-
2t
program can prove”: a degree-
2t
moment-only certificate of
\mdmathbb{P}_{\mu}[U\in B]\geq\rho

exists
if and only if such a minorant
p
exists. We record this equivalence, together with the Gauss-quadrature dual that drives the barrier of
Section

4
, as
Lemma

4.14
; both are textbook moment theory
(
55

27
)
and the proof is in the appendix. The upshot, used repeatedly, is that the strongest band-mass lower bound a degree-
2t
program can certify is governed by how the first
2t

(t{+}1)

Certifiable hypercontractivity.

The clean side needs the reverse: a degree-
2t

upper
bound on projected moments, proved by SoS in

Formally (stated and proved with the main results as

3.4
From barrier to law: a weighted-Chebyshev reduction

The framework’s lower edge leaves one quantity open: a
matching
upper certificate, a degree-
2t
minorant whose band mass meets the moment-LP ceiling. We can say precisely what closing it requires, and reduce it to one classical question about orthogonal polynomials. The key is to parametrize the minorant by a square. For
q\in\mdmathbb{R}[u]_{\leq t}
with

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{t}(B)\ \coloneqq\ \min\Big\{\textstyle\int q^{2}\,d\mu:\ q\in\mdmathbb{R}[u]_{\leq t},\ |q|\geq 1\text{ on }\mdmathbb{R}\setminus B\Big\}.

(3.5)

Proposition 3.6

(Reduction to a weighted extremal problem)
.

The degree-
2t
certificate ceiling for the band

is at least
1-\mathsf{E}_{t}(B)
. Moreover the Gauss-node interpolant

of degree

on the nodes

, where
W_{B}\coloneqq\sum_{u_{j}\in B}\lambda_{t+1}(u_{j})
is the Gauss-quadrature (Christoffel) mass of the nodes inside

W_{B}
, the discrete Christoffel content of the band, up to the admissibility of
q_{0}
.

Proof.

The first claim is the construction

above: each admissible

gives a degree-
2t

1-\int q^{2}\,d\mu
, and the supremum of these values is

\int q_{0}^{2}\,d\mu=\sum_{j}\lambda_{t+1}(u_{j})\,q_{0}(u_{j})^{2}=\sum_{u_{j}\notin B}\lambda_{t+1}(u_{j})=1-W_{B},

using

(
3.2
for the weights).
∎

The reduction isolates the one missing estimate: an

(Weighted-extremal value at the edge; the remaining estimate)

Let

(The Gaussian case, made explicit)

For

Method-specific barriers, and the SQ contrast.

Our lower bounds rule out a specific class of methods.
Theorems

and

are barriers against the

, any learner whose only distributional input is the degree-

5.2
Open problems

•

The tight breakdown

.

•

The matching certificate-degree upper bound (now reduced to one estimate).

.

•

A self-contained general-log-concave largest-root bound.
The Gauss-quadrature obstruction needs the largest orthogonal-polynomial root for the corrupted weight. We prove the two endpoints elementarily (sub-Gaussian

and sub-exponential

•

Does iterative localization escape the barrier?
Our barriers are scoped to one-shot certificates.
56

•

A degree-
2t

By

•

Statistical-query hardness for the learning problem.
Our barriers limit the moment/SoS-certificate method; they are not algorithm-independent. Whether margin-halfspace learning under malicious noise is hard for the broad statistical-query class
(
24

5.3
Conclusion

Read through the degree-cost lens, robust margin-halfspace learning by reweighted hinge has a single governing quantity: the SoS degree of the outlier-removal certificate. Degree
2
is the
54
corner; degree
2t
buys outlier tolerance
\eta^{1-1/2t}
and costs
n^{O(t)}
time; and two barriers fence the achievable region from below: one (
Theorem

4.7
) showing degree
2
cannot beat
\eta^{1/2}
, the other (
Theorem

4.1
) showing any fixed degree conserves the

margin, with the algorithm of

The barriers are method-specific, against the certificate itself; making the degree the resource is what lets the algorithm and the limits be stated to the same exponent. The quantity that sets that degree is not new: it is the Christoffel function of the marginal, the same object data analysis reads to spot an outlier, here measuring instead the room an adversary has to hide one.

Fact C.1

Fact C.2

(Malicious indistinguishability;

Under malicious noise at rate

, i.e.
\mathrm{TV}(P_{1},P_{2})\leq\eta/(1-\eta)
.

Appendix D
From sum-of-squares certificates to a semidefinite program

Both the outlier-removal step of

(

SoS as a positive-semidefinite Gram matrix.

Let

(D.1)

(“Only if”: writing

\exists\,M\succeq 0:\qquad\langle A_{\alpha},M\rangle=P_{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }\lvert\alpha\rvert=2t.

(D.2)

An SoS certificate is sufficient for

Appendix E
Extension to nasty noise

The whole picture (both barriers and the degree-
2t
algorithm) transfers from malicious noise to the stronger

The model.

A nasty adversary draws the

, and

\eta n

Both barriers transfer a fortiori.

(Degree-

for the original

Appendix G
A closer look at
54

We collect three closer readings of
54
, in each case to explain a feature rather than to fault it: why its required margin must track the target accuracy (
Section

G.1
), the fragility of its breakdown constant (
Section

G.2
), and a reconstruction of its reweighting program from the moment cone (
Section

G.3
).

G.1
The margin–accuracy coupling

On its own terms
54
is a clean and substantial result: it is the first to learn
\gamma
-margin halfspaces under a
constant
malicious-noise rate for general log-concave-mixture marginals, through a single reweighted-hinge program. Our purpose here is not to dispute that achievement but to look closely at one feature of its guarantee, the margin requirement
\gamma=\Omega(\log(1/\varepsilon)/\sqrt{d})
, and to argue that its dependence on
\varepsilon
is a genuine conceptual wrinkle rather than a cosmetic one, before locating exactly where it comes from and how the paper proposes to pay it.

The coupling runs backwards.

A margin is a property of the
distribution
: it is fixed the moment the problem is posed, and the learner has no lever to enlarge it. The accuracy
\varepsilon
is a property of the learner’s
intent
: it is chosen freely, and a learner may legitimately ask for
\varepsilon
as small as it likes. A guarantee whose hypothesis
\gamma\geq\Omega(\log(1/\varepsilon)/\sqrt{d})
ties the two reads from goal to assumption rather than from assumption to conclusion: to
permit
a smaller target error it requires the
data
to have been better separated all along. The dependency points the wrong way. The learner cannot meet the hypothesis by adjusting the one quantity it controls,
\varepsilon
, because lowering
\varepsilon
only tightens the hypothesis it was meant to satisfy. This is the chicken-and-egg: the accuracy one is allowed to demand is governed by a margin one cannot choose.

An error floor on every fixed instance.

The wrinkle is quantitative, not merely rhetorical. Suppose a guarantee certifies inlier error
\leq\varepsilon
whenever
\gamma\geq f(\varepsilon)
for an increasing
f
; here
f(\varepsilon)=c\,\log(1/\varepsilon)/\sqrt{d}
. On any
fixed
instance the margin takes some value
0
, so the guarantee is available only for those
\varepsilon
with
f(\varepsilon)\leq{}_{0}
, that is, for

\varepsilon\;\geq\;\varepsilon_{\star}({}_{0})\;=\;f^{-1}({}_{0})\;=\;\exp\!\big(-\Theta({}_{0}\sqrt{d})\big).

The quantity
\varepsilon_{\star}({}_{0})
is an
error floor
fixed by the margin: below it the hypothesis fails to hold and the theorem is silent, so on a fixed-margin distribution one cannot ask this analysis for an arbitrarily accurate classifier. The floor is not an artifact of the write-up that a sharper analysis would dissolve. By the margin–degree barrier (
Theorem

4.1
) the
\log(1/\varepsilon)
is
conserved
across every one-shot certificate of fixed degree: a degree-
2
program cannot lower the floor at all without enlarging the margin, and a higher-degree program lowers it only by spending degree. The coupling is therefore a property of the certificate method, not of one paper’s bookkeeping.

An example: the natural-margin mixture.

Take the natural margin
{}_{0}=\Theta(1/\sqrt{d})
, the separation one assumes when two clusters sit a unit-scale gap apart in their worst one-dimensional projection, and the regime in which margin-based halfspace learning is usually posed. Then
\varepsilon_{\star}=\exp(-\Theta(1))
, a
constant
: applied to a natural-margin instance, the guarantee certifies only a constant inlier error and cannot certify
\varepsilon=o(1)
at all. The margin one must assume in order to certify a target
\varepsilon
grows steadily as the target shrinks:

target accuracy
\varepsilon

margin the guarantee requires

\Theta(1)

\Theta(1/\sqrt{d})
(the natural margin)

1/\poly(d)

\Theta(\log d/\sqrt{d})

\exp(-d^{a})
,
\ 0<a<\tfrac{1}{2}

\Theta(d^{\,a-1/2})

\exp(-\Theta(\sqrt{d}))

\Theta(1)
(a constant margin)

By the last row the hypothesis has become a constant-margin condition, the classes separated by a constant fraction of their spread, which is a far stronger demand than the
1/\sqrt{d}
one began with and one that most high-dimensional instances do not meet. A learner who wants exponentially small error must, under this guarantee, assume exponentially better-separated data, a bargain it is in no position to strike.

The resolution: pay in degree, not in margin.

The paper’s first result says this is the whole of the trade, and that the learner has a second currency. The conserved
\log(1/\varepsilon)
may be carried by the margin
or
by the Sum-of-Squares degree, but inside the one-shot certificate model it is never waived (
Theorem

4.1
). Choosing to pay it in degree,
Theorem

4.9
holds the margin at the natural
\Theta(1/\sqrt{d})
and certifies inlier error
\varepsilon
with a degree-
2t=\Theta(\log(1/\varepsilon))
program, in time
n^{\Theta(\log(1/\varepsilon))}
. Accuracy is then bought with computation, a resource the learner controls, instead of with a margin it does not. The chicken-and-egg is not a paradox but a question of which resource pays: degree, which the learner can spend, or margin, which the data fixes in advance, and the
\varepsilon
-dependent margin of
54
is the consequence of having only the degree-
2
, i.e. cheapest, certificate at hand.

The breakdown constant of the degree-
2

Our barriers concern the

\eta^{1/2}

\eta_{0}

The lower bound
