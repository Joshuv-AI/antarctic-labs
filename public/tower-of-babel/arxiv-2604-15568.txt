Electric inertia and ideal magnetic reconnection in 2D Thanks: MSC Classification: 35Q31, 35Q85.
Peter Constantin, Zhongtian Hu

Abstract
. We consider inertial magneto-hydrodynamic systems in 2D. We show global existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions and global existence and uniqueness of weak solutions in Yudovich class. We prove magnetic reconnection without magnetic resistivity, for smooth solutions and for patch solutions. This is obtained by proving merger in corresponding systems of coupled active scalars.



1.
Introduction

Ideal magnetic fields do not change topology of magnetic lines, as a consequence of the magnetic induction equation. But change of topology of magnetic lines does occur in nature. There is a well established correlation between fast magnetic reconnection and solar flares. A natural suggestion is that resistive MHD evolution might be responsible for topology change, leading to magnetic reconnection events
[
2
,
17
]
. The reconnection of vortex lines is followed by massive energy release.
There is an analogous problem in incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. In
[
10
,
11
]
it was argued on the basis of physical and analytical arguments that vortex reconnection is a regularizing mechanism in Navier-Stokes equations.

A different type of magnetic reconnection mechanism was proposed, based on a modification of ideal MHD due to magnetohydrodynamic inertia. This was discussed in
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
in 2D.
and more recently in
[
3
]
for 3D. The main idea is that the electronic inertia modifies the transport.

In this paper we prove that magnetic reconnection does occur without magnetic resistivity. We take with
[
5
]
a model of electronic inertia in 2D plasma dynamics.
The magnetic field is
B=\nabla^{\perp}\phi
where
\phi
is a scalar time dependent function of two variables and
\nabla^{\perp}=(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1})
. The current density is
J=-\Delta\phi
and a variable
F=\phi+J
plays the role of a canonical momentum. The electron flow velocity is given by
\nabla^{\perp}\psi
where

\psi
is a stream function. We denote by

\omega
the vorticity,
\omega=\Delta\psi
. The system
[
5
]
is

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}F+\{\psi,F\}+\{\phi,\omega\}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\omega+\{\psi,\omega\}+\{\phi,F\}=0.\end{array}\right.

Here
\{f,g\}=\partial_{1}f\partial_{2}g-\partial_{2}f\partial_{1}g
is Poisson bracket.

As noted in
[
5
]
, the quantities
F+\omega
and
F-\omega
are each advected by incompressible velocities

(2)

\partial_{t}(F+\omega)+\{\psi+\phi,F+\omega\}=0

and

(3)

\partial_{t}(F-\omega)+\{\psi-\phi,F-\omega\}=0.

If initial data for
F\pm\omega
are integrable and bounded in space, that is, if both initial data are in the Yudovich class
\mathbb{Y}=L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
, then the solutions remain in
\mathbb{Y}
. Formally,
from (
2
) and (
3
) it follows that

\|(F\pm\omega)(t)\|_{L^{p}}=\|(F\pm\omega)(0)\|_{L^{p}}.

for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
. This is powerful information.
The equations are a coupled system of active scalars
[
10
,
11
]
,
\sigma_{\pm}=F\pm\omega

advected by incompressible velocities
v_{\pm}
,

(4)

\partial_{t}\sigma_{\pm}+v_{\pm}\cdot\nabla\sigma_{\pm}=0.

The velocities
v_{\pm}
are obtained from
\sigma_{\pm}
by a linear, time independent equation of state

(5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{+}=\nabla^{\perp}\left(\mathbb{K}\sigma_{+}+\mathbb{L}\sigma_{-}\right)\\
v_{-}=\nabla^{\perp}\left(-\mathbb{L}\sigma_{+}-\mathbb{K}\sigma_{-}\right)\end{array}\right.

where

(6)

\mathbb{K}=\frac{1}{2}\left((-\Delta+\mathbb{I})^{-1}-(-\Delta)^{-1}\right)

and

(7)

\mathbb{L}=\frac{1}{2}\left((-\Delta+\mathbb{I})^{-1}+(-\Delta)^{-1}\right)

are functions of
-\Delta
. In this paper we call this system the “right-handed” system. We discuss also an analogous system we call “left-handed”, where
(-\Delta+\mathbb{I})^{-1}
above is replaced by
(\Delta-\mathbb{I})^{-1}
, corresponding to having
F=\Delta\phi-\phi
instead of
F=-\Delta\phi+\phi
.
(We choose to keep
\omega=\Delta\psi
throughout, because of the analogy to fluid equations, but switching to
\omega=-\Delta\psi
and keeping
F=-\Delta\phi+\phi
results in the same left-handed system, mutatis mutandis. )
The left-handed system is thus (
4
) together with

(8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{+}=-\nabla^{\perp}\left(\mathbb{L}\sigma_{+}+\mathbb{K}\sigma_{-}\right)\\
v_{-}=\nabla^{\perp}\left(\mathbb{K}\sigma_{+}+\mathbb{L}\sigma_{-}\right)\end{array}\right.

which means that
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathbb{L}
have been switched to
-\mathbb{L}
and
-\mathbb{K}
compared to (
5
).

Both systems have unique global smooth solutions in
C^{1,\alpha}
and unique global weak solutions in
\mathbb{Y}
. Both systems can be derived as critical points of a Lagrangian action
[
14
]
(see also Section
8
), both have infinitely many conserved quantities (Casimirs) and both systems are capable of producing topology change of the magnetic field
F
in both smooth regimes and in vortex patch regimes. The two systems are similar, but not at all the same. In the original right-handed system described above, the diagonal terms
\mathbb{K}
and
-{\mathbb{K}}
would vanish if
\mathbb{I}
would be replaced by zero. In fact, the operator
\mathbb{K}
is smoothing of degree four (while
\mathbb{L}
is smoothing of degree two) and is small at small distances. Removing the screening term
\mathbb{I}
, we have at leading order (at high frequencies, or small scales)

(9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\sigma_{+}-(\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\sigma_{-})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\sigma_{-}+(\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\sigma_{+})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}=0,\end{array}\right.

a system in which one scalar drives the other. For the left-handed system, removing the screening term

\mathbb{I}
results in the system

(10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\sigma_{+}+(\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\sigma_{+})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\sigma_{-}-(\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\sigma_{-})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}=0,\end{array}\right.

a system in which the scalars obey incompressible Euler equations and do not interact. Here the origin of the
topology change is much simpler and clearer, the merger of the scalars is due to their lack of interaction.

Both the left-handed and the right-handed unscreened systems have the full Euler two-parameter scaling properties, meaning that if
\sigma_{\pm}(x,t)
is a solution, then so is
\frac{1}{T}\sigma_{\pm}\left(\frac{x}{L},\frac{t}{T}\right)
for any positive
T
and any positive
L
. By contrast, the screened systems have a distinguished natural length scale (related to the electronic skin depth, and taken to be the unit in this paper). In order to prove our change of topology results, we first understand them in unscreened leading order systems, and then prove they hold in the screened systems as well, by taking advantage of the smallness of the difference between unscreened and screened kernels at small distances, and by perturbing the merger of small scale solutions of the unscreened systems.

For the left-handed system where the unscreened solutions are non-interacting 2D Euler solutions we prepare two patches, a rotating Kirkhhoff ellipse and a disk, which are initially disjoint and which overlap later during the evolution. We then show that suitably scaled down, these two patches provide initial data for finite time merger of solutions of the screened equations.

For the right-handed system we need to establish first the unscreened merger. This is done by choosing a
specific configuration with symmetry. Both
\sigma_{+}
and
\sigma_{-}
are taken odd in
x_{1}
and we also assume that
\sigma_{-}
is obtained from
\sigma_{+}
by reflection across the
x_{1}
axis. These symmetries are preserved by the right-handed equations (both screened and unscreened). Then we consider initial data for
\sigma_{+}
that are compactly supported in the upper half plane and have a single sign in the upper right quadrant. Thus, the supports of
\sigma_{+}
and
\sigma_{-}
are initially disjoint. We obtain the finite time merger by analyzing the evolution of local barycenters. We show that the support of
\sigma_{+}
rapidly descends and crosses the
x_{1}
axis, while by symmetry,
\sigma_{-}
ascends across it, and merger occurs. We show then that this behavior is produced also in the screened right-handed equations by analyzing the same objects. We also provide a more general merger stability analysis (see Section
6
) where we show that merger in unscreened equations from smooth disjoint compactly supported initial data implies merger of small scale solutions in the screened equations. We consider further the stability of merger under resistive approximations, and show that, in the smooth case, solutions of resistive equations converge strongly to solutions of the ideal equations, and thus the ideal merger is stable to small resistive perturbations. This result is expected to hold also in Yudovich class, on a basis of an inviscid limit in the spirit of
[
13
]
, a topic which will be pursued in later work.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we prove global existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions. Global existence and uniqueness of Yudovich class solutions is presented in Section
3
. Merger of solutions of the screened left-handed equations for vortex patch initial data is presented in Section
4
. The construction of merger of smooth solutions of unscreened and of screened right-handed equations is in Section
5
. Stability of merger is discussed in Section
6
. The limit of zero resistivity for smooth solutions is proved in Section
7
. A unified formal derivation of both right and left handed equations is presented in Section
8
.

2.
Global existence and uniqueness

The results in this section are valid for both the right-handed and left-handed models, and their proofs are identical. We present the right-handed case. We consider the operators

(11)

\mathbb{U}(\omega)=\nabla^{\perp}(\Delta^{-1})\omega

and

(12)

\mathbb{B}(F)=\nabla^{\perp}(\mathbb{I}-\Delta)^{-1}F.

In Eulerian frame, setting

(13)

\sigma_{\pm}=F\pm\omega

and

(14)

\sigma=(\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}),

we have

(15)

\partial_{t}\sigma_{\pm}+\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\cdot\nabla\sigma_{\pm}=0,

where

(16)

\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+}-\sigma_{-})\pm\mathbb{B}(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})\right)

which holds in view of the fact that

(17)

\omega=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{+}-\sigma_{-}),\quad F=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-}),

and so

(18)

\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)=\mathbb{U}(\omega)\pm\mathbb{B}(F),

and (
15
) is just the system (
2
), (
3
).

We consider a pair

(19)

X=(X_{+},X_{-})

of Lagrangian maps

(20)

X_{\pm}(\cdot,t):{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

and fields
\tau=(\tau_{+},\tau_{-}):{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
which are time independent and correspond to the initial data,

(21)

\tau(a)=(F_{0}(a)+\omega_{0}(a),F_{0}(a)-\omega_{0}(a))=\sigma_{0}(a)

We solve

(22)

\partial_{t}X_{\pm}=\mathcal{U}_{\pm}(X)

where

(23)

\mathcal{U}_{\pm}(X_{\pm})=\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\tau_{+}\circ X_{+}^{-1},\tau_{-}\circ X_{-}^{-1})\circ X_{\pm}

with initial data

(24)

X_{\pm}(a,0)=a.

Using the method of
[
12
]

we have

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<\alpha<1
, and let
\tau_{\pm}\in(C^{1+\alpha}\cap L^{p})({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. There exists
T>0
depending only on the norms
\|\tau_{\pm}\|_{L^{p}\cap C^{1+\alpha}}
and

\lambda_{\pm}\in Lip(0,T;C^{1+\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
, with
\lambda_{\pm}(a,0)=0
, such that
X_{\pm}(a,t)=a+\lambda_{\pm}(a,t)
solve
(
22
).

The solution
X(a,t)=(X_{+}(a,t),X_{-}(a,t))
depends in Lipschitz manner on initial data in the norm of
|||X|||=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\lambda_{\pm}(t)\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}
, i.e. if
\widetilde{X}
solves the Lagrangian system with initial data
\widetilde{\tau}
then

(25)

|||X-\widetilde{X}|||\leq C\|\tau-\widetilde{\tau}\|_{C^{1+\alpha}\cap L^{p}}.

This theorem implies that the solution of (
15
) is given by

(26)

\sigma_{\pm}(x,t)=\tau_{\pm}(X_{\pm}^{-1}(x,t))

for
t\in[0,T]
. We note that

(27)

\|\sigma_{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{p}}=\|\tau_{\pm}\|_{L^{p}}

holds for
0\leq t\leq T
and for
1\leq p\leq\infty
because
X_{\pm}
are volume preserving.

Remark 1
.

In contrast with the single fluid Lagrangian evolution, where the Lipschitz dependence is only
on
\|\tau-\widetilde{\tau}\|_{C^{\alpha}\cap L^{p}}
, for two-fluid equations there is a loss of a derivative.

The global existence of solutions now follows from a priori estimates and uniqueness, in Eulerian coordinates. We use the inequality

(28)

\|\nabla\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}\log\left(2+\frac{\|\nabla\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sigma\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right).

We obtain

Theorem 2
.

Let
\tau_{\pm}\in(L^{1}\cap C^{1+\alpha})({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. There exists
C>0
and a unique solution
\sigma(x,t)=(\sigma_{+}(x,t),\sigma_{-}(x,t))
of (
15
) with initial data
\sigma_{\pm}(x,0)=\tau_{\pm}(x)
, satisfying

(29)

\|\sigma(t)\|_{C^{1+\alpha}\cap L^{1}}\leq\|\tau\|_{C^{1+\alpha}\cap L^{1}}\exp\left[C\left(e^{Ct\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}}-1\right)\log\left(2+\frac{\|\nabla\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tau\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\right]

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

We indicate only the salient elements of the proof and provide useful a priori bounds. On the basis of (
15
) we have the a priori information that

(30)

\|\sigma_{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{p}}=\|\tau_{\pm}\|_{L^{p}}

holds for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then using the stretching equation

(31)

(\partial_{t}+\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\cdot\nabla)(\nabla^{\perp}\sigma_{\pm})=(\nabla\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma))(\nabla^{\perp}\sigma_{\pm})

and the bound (
28
), we deduce using the logarithmically superlinear ODE that

(32)

2+\|\tau\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla\sigma_{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\exp\left[e^{Ct\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}}\log\left(2+\frac{\|\nabla\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tau\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\right]

holds a priori. Returning to (
28
) we have

(33)

\|\nabla\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\|\leq C\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}\left[e^{Ct\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}}\log\left(2+\frac{\|\nabla\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tau\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\right]

Because of the equation

(34)

\frac{d}{dt}(\nabla X_{\pm})=(\nabla\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\circ X_{\pm})\nabla X_{\pm}

we know that the Lagrangian maps
X_{\pm}
are globally defined, invertible and they and their inverses are Lipschitz, with a priori bounds of their Lipschitz constants that are uniform in space and doubly exponential in time.

(35)

\max\{\|\nabla X_{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\;,\|\nabla X^{-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\}\leq A(t)

where the doubly exponential amplification factor
A(t)
is

(36)

A(t)=\exp\left[C\left(e^{Ct\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}}-1\right)\log\left(2+\frac{\|\nabla\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tau\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\right]

as it follows from (
33
) and (
34
) integrating in time. Note that
A(t)
is a priori controlled
in time (by a double exponential) and depends only on
\|\tau\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
. Note also that the expression is nondimensional.

Returning to (
31
) and using (
33
) we have also the bounds

(37)

\|\nabla\sigma_{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq A(t)\|\nabla\tau_{\pm}\|_{L^{p}}

for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

At this point we have the information needed to pass to
C^{1+\alpha}
norms. We use the fact composing with
X_{\pm}(t)
or
X_{\pm}(t)^{-1}
does not change
L^{\infty}
norms and amplifies Holder seminorms by
A(t)
. (We replace
A(t)^{\alpha}
by
A(t)
here using that
A(t)\geq 1
.) Repeated compositions do not change the amplification significantly because of the doubly exponential nature of
A(t)
, so that
A(t)^{2}
or
A(t)^{3}
are still doubly exponential factors which are a priori controlled in time by
\|\tau\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
.

The fact that
\sigma_{\pm}(t)=\tau_{\pm}(X_{\pm}^{-1}(t))
is used now together with the chain rule,

(38)

\nabla\sigma_{\pm}=(\nabla\tau_{\pm}(X_{\pm}^{-1}))(\nabla X_{\pm}^{-1}).

Now
C^{\alpha}
is a Banach algebra with
\|fg\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{C^{\alpha}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}}
.
We estimate

(39)

\|\nabla\tau_{\pm}\circ X_{\pm}^{-1}\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq A(t)\|\tau\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}

In order to estimate
\|\nabla X_{\pm}^{-1}\|_{C^{\alpha}}
we use the fact that the inverse map solves the ODE in Lagrangian coordinates

(40)

\frac{d}{dt}(\nabla X_{\pm}^{-1}\circ X_{\pm})+(\nabla X_{\pm}^{-1}\circ X_{\pm})(\nabla\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\circ X_{\pm})=0.

We use the bound (
33
) and also the Eulerian fact that
\|\nabla\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq C\|\sigma\|_{C^{\alpha}}
. This is due to the boundedness of Riesz and Bessel transforms in
C^{\alpha}
. Then

(41)

\|\nabla\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\circ X_{\pm}\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq A(t)\|\tau\|_{C^{\alpha}}

and consequently after judicious use of (
33
) and (
41
) we have

(42)

\|\nabla X_{\pm}^{-1}\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq CA(t)^{3}

We omit further details.

3.
Global existence and uniqueness of weak solutions in Yudovich class

The global existence and uniqueness of weak solutions with
\sigma_{\pm}\in\mathbb{Y}=(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is established here. The result holds for both the right-handed and the left-handed models with similar proofs. We show here the original right-handed case. We introduce the notion of weak solution in Yudovich class.

Definition 1
.

Given initial condition
\tau=(\tau_{+},\tau_{-})\in\mathbb{Y}^{2}
and
T>0
, we say that
\sigma=(\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-})
is a Yudovich class solution of (
15
) on
[0,T]
with initial data
\tau
, if

\sigma_{\pm}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, the equation

(43)

\partial_{t}\sigma_{\pm}+\nabla\cdot(\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\sigma_{\pm})=0

holds in
C([0,T);W^{-1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
for
1<p<\infty
, with
\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)
defined in (
16
),
and if

\tau(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}\sigma(x,t)\>\text{ in}\;(W^{-1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))^{2}.

Here, we recall (
16
):

\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)=K_{\mathbb{U}}*\left(\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{+}-\sigma_{-})\right)\pm K_{\mathbb{B}}*\left(\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})\right),

where
K_{\mathbb{U}},K_{\mathbb{B}}
are kernels of the operators
\mathbb{U},\mathbb{B}
(cf. (
11
), (
12
))

With the above definition, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 3
.

Fix any initial data
\tau\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. There exists a unique Yudovich class solution
\sigma=(\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-})
with initial data
\tau
on
[0,T]
for any
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Moreover, the velocity vector fields
\mathbb{V}_{\pm}
are log-Lipschitz in space. Define the Lagrangian trajectory
X(a,t)=(X_{+}(a,t),X_{-}(a,t))
to be the (unique) solution to ODEs:

(44)

\frac{dX_{\pm}(a,t)}{dt}=\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(X_{\pm}(a,t),t),\quad X_{\pm}(a,0)=a.

Then
X(a,t)
is Hölder continuous with respect to
a
. More precisely, we have

(45a)

|X_{\pm}^{\epsilon}(\alpha_{1},t)-X_{\pm}^{\epsilon}(\alpha_{2},t)|\leq C|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|^{\beta(t)},

(45b)

|(X_{\pm}^{\epsilon})^{-1}(x_{1},t)-(X_{\pm}^{\epsilon})^{-1}(x_{2},t)|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\beta(t)},

where
\beta(t)=\exp(-C\|\tau_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}t)
for some universal constant
C>0
.

Proof.

We remark that the existence part of Theorem
3
can be adapted from the single-fluid Yudovich theory for 2D Euler equation, so we provide only the outlines of the proof. The uniqueness part is more subtle and we provide a full proof in the spirit of the original proof of
[
18
]
. A proof using exponential integrability and Legendre transform, as in
[
13
]
is also possible, but we do not pursue this here.

The proof for existence is presented in the following steps:

(1)

Introduction of
\epsilon
–mollified solution.
Given
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we let
\phi_{\epsilon}
be the standard mollifier at spatial scale
\epsilon
and define the mollified initial data as
\tau_{\pm}^{\epsilon}:=\phi_{\epsilon}*\tau_{\pm}
. In view of Theorem
1
and
2
, we let
\sigma^{\epsilon}=(\sigma_{+}^{\epsilon},\sigma_{-}^{\epsilon})
be the unique, global, regular solution initiated by
\tau^{\epsilon}:=(\tau_{+}^{\epsilon},\tau_{-}^{\epsilon})
, with corresponding velocity
\mathbb{V}^{\epsilon}=(\mathbb{V}^{\epsilon}_{+},\mathbb{V}^{\epsilon}_{-})
.

(2)

Bounds on mollified variables.
Similar to the case of 2D Euler equation, the following bounds are proved: given
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
T>0
, and
t\in(0,T)
, we have

(a)

(Uniform bound on mollified vorticity)

(46)

\|\sigma^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\tau^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\tau\|_{L^{p}},\quad p\in[1,\infty].

(b)

(Uniform bound on mollified velocity)

(47)

\|\mathbb{V}_{\pm}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\sigma_{+}^{\epsilon}\pm\sigma_{-}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\tau\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}},

(48)

\|\nabla\mathbb{V}^{\epsilon}_{\pm}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(p)\|\sigma_{+}^{\epsilon}\pm\sigma_{-}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\leq C(p)\|\tau\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}.

We briefly explain how to obtain the aforementioned bounds: the bound (
46
) follows directly from (
27
) together with properties of the standard mollifier. The proof of (
47
)–(
48
) follows from a similar argument to that treating 2D Euler equation, except for bounds concerning the kernel
K_{\mathbb{B}}
. However, from (
12
) we see that
K_{\mathbb{B}}=\nabla^{\perp}G
, where
G
is the Bessel potential of order 2 on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then
K_{\mathbb{B}}(x,y)\sim|x-y|^{-1}
near the diagonal set and decays exponentially at spatial infinity. Therefore, we obtain similar potential-theoretical bounds for contributions by
K_{\mathbb{B}}
to those arising from
K_{\mathbb{U}}
.

(3)

Passing to the Limit.
From (
48
) and Rellich’s compact embedding theorem, we have
\mathbb{V}^{\epsilon}_{\pm}\to\mathbb{V}_{\pm}
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{q}_{loc})
for arbitrary
q\in(1,\infty)
and some
\mathbb{V}_{\pm}
. Next, the uniform bound (
46
) implies that
\sigma^{\epsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\sigma
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})
. The above convergence properties are sufficient to verify that the Biot-Savart law (
16
) holds after passing to the limit: the operator
\sigma\mapsto\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)
is a bounded operator from
L^{q}
to
W^{1,q}_{loc}
for all
q\in(1,\infty)
. Then
\mathbb{V}_{\pm}^{\epsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,q}_{loc})
. Since we also have strong convergence
\mathbb{V}^{\epsilon}_{\pm}\to\mathbb{V}_{\pm}
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{q}_{loc})
for arbitrary
q\in(1,\infty)
. Then we must have
\mathbb{V}_{\pm}=\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(\sigma)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,q}_{loc})
. The above facts suffice to guarantee that
\sigma_{\pm}
verifies (
43
) in
L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{-1,p})
sense.
Since
\sigma^{\epsilon}_{\pm}
verifies (
43
) strongly (thus weakly), we observe that for any
\eta\in W^{1,p_{*}}
with compact support, there is:

(49)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{t}\sigma_{\pm}^{\epsilon}\eta dx\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sigma_{\pm}^{\epsilon}\mathbb{V}_{\pm}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta dx\right|\leq C\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}}^{2}\|\eta\|_{W^{1,p_{*}}},

which implies that
\partial_{t}\sigma_{\pm}^{\epsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{-1,p})
, with
1/p+1/p_{*}=1
. After passing to the limit, the continuity with respect to time follows immediately from
\partial_{t}\sigma_{\pm}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{-1,p})
.

(4)

Additional Regularity of
\mathbb{V}
and corresponding Lagrangian.
The Log-Lipschitz property of
\mathbb{V}
follows from the potential-theoretical bound below:

(50)

|\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(x,t)-\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(y,t)|\leq C\|\tau_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}|x-y|(1-\min\{0,\log|x-y|\}),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Again, the above bound indeed holds since the Bessel potential only differs from the Green’s function far from the diagonal set. The bound (
45
) follows from (
50
) and a standard ODE argument.

We then proceed to prove uniqueness by using the pseudo-energy method à la Yudovich
[
18
]
. Take two solutions
\omega^{(i)}
and
F^{(i)}
,
i=1,2
and denote

\Omega=\frac{1}{2}(\omega^{(1)}+\omega^{(2)}),\quad F=\frac{1}{2}(F^{(1)}+F^{(2)}),

and

\omega=\frac{1}{2}(\omega^{(1)}-\omega^{(2)}),\quad f=\frac{1}{2}(F^{(1)}-F^{(2)}).

Taking differences, the system (
1
) with
\epsilon=1
becomes

(51)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}f+\{\Psi,f\}+\{\psi,F\}+\{\phi,\Omega\}+\{\Phi,\omega\}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\omega+\{\Psi,\omega\}+\{\psi,\Omega\}+\{\phi,F\}+\{\Phi,f\}=0.\end{array}\right.

We multiply the first equation by
\phi
, the second by
-\psi
and integrate in space by parts:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Psi\cdot\nabla\phi(-\Delta\phi+\phi)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdot\nabla\phi Fdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Phi\cdot\nabla\phi\omega dx

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla^{\perp}\Psi\cdot\nabla\phi(\Delta\phi)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdot\nabla\phi Fdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Phi\cdot\nabla\phi\omega dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{k}(\nabla^{\perp}\Psi)_{j}\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{k}\phi dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdot\nabla\phi Fdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Phi\cdot\nabla\phi\omega dx,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Psi\cdot\nabla\psi(\Delta\psi)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Phi\cdot\nabla\psi fdx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla\psi Fdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{k}(\nabla^{\perp}\Psi)_{j}\partial_{j}\psi\partial_{k}\psi dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Phi\cdot\nabla\psi fdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdot\nabla\phi Fdx.

Summing the above two identities, we obtain

(52)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{k}(\nabla^{\perp}\Psi)_{j}(\partial_{j}\psi\partial_{k}\psi+\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{k}\phi)dx\\
&\quad+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\nabla^{\perp}\psi)_{j}\partial_{j}\phi Fdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\nabla^{\perp}\Phi)_{j}(\partial_{j}\phi\omega-\partial_{j}\psi f).\end{split}

We focus on the third term on the right-hand-side of (
52
). The term multiplying
(\nabla^{\perp}\Phi)_{j}
is

\Delta\psi\partial_{j}\phi+(\Delta\phi-\phi)\partial_{j}\psi=\partial_{k}(\partial_{k}\psi\partial_{j}\phi+\partial_{k}\phi\partial_{j}\psi)-\partial_{j}(\partial_{k}\phi\partial_{k}\psi)-\phi\partial_{j}\psi

We plug the above identity into (
52
) and integrate by parts to obtain

(53)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{k}(\nabla^{\perp}\Psi)_{j}(\partial_{j}\psi\partial_{k}\psi+\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{k}\phi)dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{k}(\nabla^{\perp}\Phi)_{j}(\partial_{k}\psi\partial_{j}\phi+\partial_{k}\phi\partial_{j}\psi)dx\\
&\quad+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdot\nabla\phi Fdx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\Phi\cdot\nabla\psi\phi dx.\end{split}

Now we recall that

-\Delta\Phi+\Phi=F,\Delta\Psi=\Omega.

Then the Calderón-Zygmund estimate informs us that for
p\in(1,\infty)
sufficiently large,

(54)

\|\nabla^{2}\Phi\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim p\|F\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim p\|F_{0}\|_{L^{p}},\quad\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim p\|\Omega\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim p\|\Omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}},

where we also used the fact that
L^{p}
norms of
\omega^{(i)}
,
F^{(i)}
,
i=1,2
, are a priori bounded. Moreover, we have the following gradient estimates:

(55)

\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq m,\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M,

where
m,M
depend only on the
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
norm of the initial data.

Define
E(t)=(\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and fix
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small. Using (
53
), (
55
), and Hölder inequality, we have

(56)

\begin{split}E\frac{dE}{dt}&\leq m^{\epsilon}(\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{2/\epsilon}}+\|\nabla^{2}\Phi\|_{L^{2/\epsilon}})E^{2-\epsilon}+2(\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}})E^{2}\\
&\leq C\left(m^{\epsilon}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}M_{0}\right)E^{2-\epsilon}+ME^{2}\right),\end{split}

where
m,M,M_{0}
are constants which only depend on the initial data. Here, we also used the
a priori
bound
\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

We now claim that given
\epsilon
sufficiently small and
t\leq\frac{1}{8CM_{0}}=:t_{0}
, the following bound holds:

(57)

E(t)^{\epsilon}\leq 4Cm^{\epsilon}M_{0}t.

We prove this claim by a bootstrap argument. It is clear that (
57
) holds on
[0,\delta]
for some
\delta
sufficiently small thanks to
E(0)=0
. Assuming (
57
), we have
E(t)^{\epsilon}\leq\frac{m^{\epsilon}}{2}
on
[0,t_{0}]
and therefore

\epsilon E^{\epsilon}m^{-\epsilon}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leq\frac{M_{0}}{M},

after choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small. Rearranging the above inequality, we arrive at

m^{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}M_{0}\right)E^{-\epsilon}>M,

which gives the following after combining with (
56
):

\frac{dE}{dt}\leq C\left(m^{\epsilon}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}M_{0}\right)E^{1-\epsilon}+ME\right)\leq Cm^{\epsilon}\frac{3}{\epsilon}M_{0}E^{1-\epsilon},\quad t\in[0,t_{0}].

Integrating the above inequality with respect to
t
yields

E(t)^{\epsilon}\leq 3Cm^{\epsilon}M_{0}t,\quad t\in[0,t_{0}],

which proves the claim. Now for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
, we take
\epsilon\to 0
in (
57
) and conclude
E(t)=0
on
t\in[0,t_{0}]
. The uniqueness for all
t>0
follows from iterating the same procedure on
[kt_{0},(k+1)t_{0}]
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

∎

4.
Merger of patches for the left-handed system

Patches are solutions which have
F\pm\omega
constant on domains. The assumptions are that the initial data obey
(F\pm\omega)(x,0)=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{0}^{\pm}}(x)
, the characteristic functions of bounded sets
\Omega_{0}^{\pm}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with smooth boundaries
\Gamma_{0}^{\pm}=\partial\Omega_{0}^{\pm}
. Then it follows from the results of the previous section that
(F\pm\omega)(x,t)=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{t}^{\pm}}(x)
where
\Omega_{t}^{\pm}
is the image of
\Omega_{0}^{\pm}
under the flow with stream function
\psi\pm\phi
.
If the patch boundaries
\Gamma_{t}^{\pm}
are initially smooth, then they remain smooth, by analogy with the well known situation for fluids (
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
), as long as the patches do not intersect. However, because the two regular flows are different, nothing prevents the merger of the two patches. The change of topology of the streamlines of
\phi
which are magnetic lines is due to the fact that they are not carried by the flow of electrons.

In this section, we consider the system (
4
) with the left-handed equation of state (
8
). We start with the following identities in view of the left-handed equation of state (
8
) and the definitions of operators
\mathbb{K},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{U},
and
\mathbb{B}
:

v_{+}=\mathbb{U}(\omega)+\mathbb{B}(F)=\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})+\mathbb{B}(F)-\mathbb{U}(F)=\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})-2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F),

v_{-}=\mathbb{U}(\omega)-\mathbb{B}(F)=-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})-(\mathbb{B}(F)-\mathbb{U}(F))=-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})+2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F).

With the above identities, we rewrite (
15
) as

(58)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\sigma_{+}+\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}-2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F)\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\sigma_{-}-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}+2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F)\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}=0.\end{cases}

4.1.
The unscreened equations

As mentioned in the introduction, the left-handed system (
58
) is dominated by the following leading-order system at large frequencies:

(59)

\partial_{t}\sigma_{\pm}\pm\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{\pm})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{\pm}=0,

In this subsection, we consider a merger of the above leading-order system.

The leading-order system (
59
) consists of two independent incompressible Euler equations in two dimensions, with the second one having a different convention of rotation. In particular, both equations in (
59
) admit circular and elliptical uniformly rotating patches. We consider the initial data

\tau_{\pm}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{0}^{\pm}}(x),

where

(60)

\Omega_{0}^{+}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;|\;\frac{(x-d-2R)^{2}}{R^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{(2R)^{2}}<1\},\quad\Omega_{0}^{-}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;|\;\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{R^{2}}<1\},

where
R>0
,
d\in(0,R)
are parameters to be determined. Identifying
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathbb{C}
and writing boundaries
\Gamma_{0}^{\pm}=\partial\Omega_{0}^{\pm}
to be parametrized by
z_{\pm}^{0}(\alpha)
with
z_{\pm}^{0}(0)=z_{\pm}^{0}(2\pi)
, we have

z_{+}^{0}(\alpha)=R\left[e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{3}{2}e^{i\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-i\alpha}\right)+\left(2+\frac{d}{R}\right)\right],\quad z_{-}^{0}(\alpha)=Re^{i\alpha}.

Denoting the parametrization at time
t
as
z_{\pm}(\alpha,t)
, from Euler dynamics we obtain the following explicit formulas (see e.g.
[
9
]
):

(61)

\displaystyle z_{+}(\alpha,t)

\displaystyle=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{3R}{2}\exp\left({i(\alpha-\frac{t}{2\pi}\frac{A}{\frac{9}{4}R^{2}})}\right)+\frac{R}{2}e^{-i\alpha}\right)+(d+2R)

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}e^{i\alpha}+e^{-i\alpha}\right)+(d+2R),

\displaystyle z_{-}(\alpha,t)

\displaystyle=Re^{-\frac{i}{2}t}e^{i\alpha},

where in the computation above,
A
is the area of the ellipse, and we have

\frac{A}{\frac{9}{4}R^{2}}=\frac{\pi\left(\frac{9}{4}R^{2}-\frac{1}{4}R^{2}\right)}{\frac{9}{4}R^{2}}=\frac{8}{9}\pi.

In particular, the above formulas inform us that the patch
\Omega_{t}^{+}
rigidly rotates counterclockwisely with angular velocity
\frac{4}{9}
, and it takes
T_{0}=\frac{9\pi}{4}
to rotate by
\pi
. Let us denote
T_{*}=T_{*}(d,R)
be the first time such that
\overline{\Omega_{t}^{+}}\cap\overline{\Omega_{t}^{-}}\neq\emptyset
. It is clear that
T_{*}
is well defined whenever

. Moreover,

\lim_{d\to 0}T_{*}(d,R)=0,\quad\lim_{d\to R}T_{*}(d,R)=\frac{9\pi}{8}.

By elementary planar geometry, we observe that
{\Omega_{t}^{+}}\cap{\Omega_{t}^{-}}\neq\emptyset
for all
t\in(T_{*},\frac{9\pi}{4}-T_{*})
, and
{\Omega_{t}^{+}}\cap{\Omega_{t}^{-}}=\emptyset
otherwise. Thus, for the patch solution to the leading order model (
59
) with initial data
\tau_{\pm}=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{0}^{\pm}}(x)
given in (
60
), we already obtain a merger of patches. Moreover, in view of the following identity:

(62)

F(x,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&x\in{\Omega_{t}^{+}}\Delta{\Omega_{t}^{-}},\\
1,&x\in{\Omega_{t}^{+}}\cap{\Omega_{t}^{-}},\end{cases}

we observe the topology change for the
F
-patches in the following sense:
\{F(\cdot,t)=1\}\neq\emptyset
for all

, and
\{F(\cdot,t)=1\}=\emptyset
otherwise.

4.2.
Merger for the screened equations

In the screened left-handed equations (
58
), in view of the operator
\mathbb{K}
defined in (
6
), we regard the term
\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F)
as a small perturbation at sufficiently small scales. We recall that the kernel for the operator
(\Delta-\mathbb{I})^{-1}
is the Bessel potential
G_{B}(r)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}K_{0}(r)
, where
K_{0}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. Writing

\bar{G}(r)=-K_{0}(r)-\log r,

by definition of
F
, linearity of operators
\mathbb{B},\mathbb{U}
, and an application of divergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle-2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{B}(F)-\mathbb{U}(F)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{B}(\sigma_{+})-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+}))+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{B}(\sigma_{-})-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\nabla^{\perp}\bigg(\int_{\Omega^{+}_{t}}\bar{G}(|x-y|)dy+\int_{\Omega^{-}_{t}}\bar{G}(|x-y|)dy\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{G}(|x-z_{+}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{+}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{G}(|x-z_{-}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{-}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta.

Therefore, the contour dynamics for the full problem (
58
) is written as follows:

(63)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}z_{+}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|z_{+}(\alpha)-z_{+}(\beta)|\frac{\partial z_{+}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta\\
\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{G}(|z_{+}(\alpha)-z_{+}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{+}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{G}(|z_{+}(\alpha)-z_{-}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{-}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta,\\
\partial_{t}z_{-}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|z_{-}(\alpha)-z_{-}(\beta)|\frac{\partial z_{-}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta\\
\quad-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{G}(|z_{-}(\alpha)-z_{+}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{+}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{G}(|z_{-}(\alpha)-z_{-}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{-}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta.\end{cases}

An observation is the following expansion for the modified Bessel function (see e.g.
[
15
,
1
]
): there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for
r\in(0,r_{0})
:

(64)

K_{0}(r)=-\left(\log r-\log 2+\gamma_{0}\right)+\tilde{G}(r),

where
\gamma_{0}
is the Euler’s constant and
|\tilde{G}(s)|\lesssim r^{2}|\log r|
. Moreover,
\tilde{G}(r)
can be extended to
r=0
by
\tilde{G}(0)=0
as an
C^{1}([0,\infty))
function, and
\tilde{G}^{\prime}(r)
is Log-Lipschitz near the origin. With the aforementioned properties,
\bar{G}(r)
is rewritten as follows:

\bar{G}(r)=(\log 2-\gamma_{0})-\tilde{G}(r),\quad r\in(0,r_{0})

Hence, if we assume that the scale for the patches, namely
R
, is sufficiently small, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{G}(|z_{\pm}(\alpha)-z_{\pm}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{\pm}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left((\log 2-\gamma_{0})-\tilde{G}(|z_{\pm}(\alpha)-z_{\pm}(\beta)|)\right)\frac{\partial z_{\pm}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|z_{\pm}(\alpha)-z_{\pm}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{\pm}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta,

where we used periodicity of
z_{\pm}
in the second equality above. Thus, we reduce (
63

(65)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}z_{+}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|z_{+}(\alpha)-z_{+}(\beta)|\frac{\partial z_{+}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta\\
\quad-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|z_{+}(\alpha)-z_{+}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{+}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|z_{+}(\alpha)-z_{-}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{-}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta,\\
\partial_{t}z_{-}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|z_{-}(\alpha)-z_{-}(\beta)|\frac{\partial z_{-}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta\\
\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|z_{-}(\alpha)-z_{+}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{+}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|z_{-}(\alpha)-z_{-}(\beta)|)\frac{\partial z_{-}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta.\end{cases}

The terms involving
\tilde{G}
are perturbative because
\tilde{G}
is not a singular kernel and has small size at a sufficiently small scale

Then, the route to prove magnetic reconnection is to study (
65

\sigma_{\pm}(x,0)=\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{0}^{\pm}}(x)
, where
\Omega_{0}^{\pm}
is given in (
60

\zeta_{\pm}=\frac{z_{\pm}-Z_{\pm}}{R}

\zeta_{\pm}

Let us fix the background
Z_{\pm}(\alpha,t)
by the following formulas:

(66)

\displaystyle Z_{+}(\alpha,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}e^{i\alpha}+e^{-i\alpha}\right)+(d+2R),

\displaystyle Z_{-}(\alpha,t)

\displaystyle=Re^{-\frac{i}{2}t}e^{i\alpha}.

where

\partial_{t}Z_{\pm}=\pm\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|Z_{\pm}(\alpha)-Z_{\pm}(\beta)|\frac{\partial Z_{\pm}}{\partial\beta}(\beta)d\beta.

Using the above equation, (

\zeta_{\pm}

(67)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}\zeta_{+}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}\right|\partial_{\beta}\zeta_{+}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi R}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|1+\frac{2e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}}{3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}}\frac{\zeta_{+}(\alpha)-\zeta_{+}(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right|\partial_{\beta}Z_{+}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|1+\frac{2e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}}{3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}}\frac{\zeta_{+}(\alpha)-\zeta_{+}(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right|\partial_{\beta}\zeta_{+}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle-\sum_{\pm}\frac{1}{4\pi R}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|Z_{+}(\alpha)-Z_{\pm}(\beta)+R(\zeta_{+}(\alpha)-\zeta_{\pm}(\beta))|)\partial_{\beta}Z_{\pm}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle-\sum_{\pm}\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|Z_{+}(\alpha)-Z_{\pm}(\beta)+R(\zeta_{+}(\alpha)-\zeta_{\pm}(\beta))|)\partial_{\beta}\zeta_{\pm}(\beta)d\beta,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta_{-}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}\zeta_{-}-\frac{1}{2\pi R}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\frac{\zeta_{-}(\alpha)-\zeta_{-}(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right|\partial_{\beta}Z_{-}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\frac{\zeta_{-}(\alpha)-\zeta_{-}(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right|\partial_{\beta}\zeta_{-}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle+\sum_{\pm}\frac{1}{4\pi R}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|Z_{-}(\alpha)-Z_{\pm}(\beta)+R(\zeta_{-}(\alpha)-\zeta_{\pm}(\beta))|)\partial_{\beta}Z_{\pm}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle+\sum_{\pm}\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}(|Z_{-}(\alpha)-Z_{\pm}(\beta)+R(\zeta_{-}(\alpha)-\zeta_{\pm}(\beta))|)\partial_{\beta}\zeta_{\pm}(\beta)d\beta,

Here, we also used the following classical identity (see e.g.
[

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}|\partial_{\beta}f(\beta)d\beta=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}f(\alpha),

where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the Hilbert transform on torus. We recall that we also have the initial data

\zeta_{\pm}(\alpha,0)=0.

The well-posedness for (

C^{1,\gamma}
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, follows after establishing a priori Hölder estimates. We start by proving a few useful technical lemmas, the first and second of which concern integral operators with logarithmic kernels.

Lemma 1
.

Given
\gamma\in(0,1)
, assume
f,g\in C^{1,\gamma}
. Then there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small that for
\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}<\epsilon_{0}
, the function

(68)

I(\alpha):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\frac{f(\alpha)-f(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right|\partial_{\beta}g(\beta)d\beta

is
C^{1,\gamma}
. Moreover, the following estimate holds:

(69)

\|I\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}.

Proof.

For the rest of the proof, we use the notation
f^{\prime}
or
g^{\prime}
to denote derivative with respect to the parametrization parameter
\alpha
or
\beta
.
Define
w(\alpha,\beta):=\frac{f(\alpha)-f(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}
. Then we observe that for any
\alpha,\beta\in[0,2\pi)
:

|w(\alpha,\beta)|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1}}\leq C\epsilon_{0}.

By choosing
\epsilon_{0}
small, we have that

(70)

|1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)|\geq 1-\sup_{\alpha,\beta}|w(\alpha,\beta)|>\frac{1}{2}.

This implies
\log|1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)|
is uniformly bounded. Moreover by concavity of logarithm, we have the refined bound

\log|1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)|\leq|w(\alpha,\beta)|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1}},

from which we can bound:

(71)

\|I\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}.

Differentiating (
68
), we observe that

(72)

I^{\prime}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}Re\left(\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}\right)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta.

We first investigate the boundedness of
\partial_{\alpha}I
: via a computation, it holds that

(73)

\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\left(f^{\prime}(\alpha)-\frac{f(\alpha)-f(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}(ie^{i\alpha})\right).

Denoting
h:=\alpha-\beta
, an application of Mean Value Theorem yields:

\displaystyle f(\alpha)-f(\beta)=f^{\prime}(\alpha)h+R_{1}(\alpha,\beta),\quad e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}=ie^{i\alpha}h+R_{2}(\alpha,\beta),

where the remainders
R_{j}
verify the bounds

|R_{1}(\alpha,\beta)|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}|h|^{1+\gamma},\quad|R_{2}(\alpha,\beta)|\leq C|h|^{2}.

With the above bounds, we observe that

\frac{f(\alpha)-f(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}=\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)}{ie^{i\alpha}}+\frac{ie^{i\alpha}R_{1}-f^{\prime}(\alpha)R_{2}}{ie^{i\alpha}(ie^{i\alpha}h+R_{2})}=:\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)}{ie^{i\alpha}}+R_{3}(\alpha,\beta),

with
R_{3}\in C^{\gamma}
such that
|R_{3}|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}|h|^{\gamma}
. Plugging in (
73
),

(74)

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\left(ie^{i\alpha}R_{3}(\alpha,\beta)\right)=\frac{R_{3}(\alpha,\beta)}{h}+R_{4}(\alpha,\beta),

where
|R_{4}(\alpha,\beta)|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}|h|^{\gamma}
. Thus, we conclude that

(75)

|\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\min(|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-1},1).

Now recalling that
|1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w|\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we then have

(76)

\displaystyle|I^{\prime}(\alpha)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\min(|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-1},1)|g^{\prime}(\beta)|d\beta

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|\leq 1}|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-1}d\beta+C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}.

for any
\alpha\in[0,2\pi)
.
Finally, we show
I^{\prime}(\alpha)\in C^{\gamma}
. Fixing
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in[0,2\pi)
with
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
,

(77)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(\alpha)-I^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}Re\left(\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}-\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)}\right)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{|\alpha-\beta|\leq 2\delta}+\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}\right)Re\left(e^{\frac{i}{2}t}(K(\alpha,\beta)-K(\alpha^{\prime},\beta))\right)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle=:I_{n}+I_{f}

where
\delta:=|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|
and

K(\alpha,\beta):=\frac{\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}.

We show appropriate
C^{\gamma}
bounds for
I_{n}
and
I_{f}
respectively. We only focus on the regime where
|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|
and
|\alpha-\beta|
are sufficiently small, which corresponds to the most singular regime.

To estimate
I_{n}
, we recall the non-degeneracy bound
|1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w|\geq\frac{1}{2}
, and estimate that:

\displaystyle|I_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\alpha-\beta|\leq 2\delta}|\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)||g^{\prime}(\beta)|d\beta+C\int_{|\alpha-\beta|\leq 2\delta}|\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)||g^{\prime}(\beta)|d\beta.

The first integral above can be bounded in a similar fashion to (
76
) after invoking (
75
):

\displaystyle\int_{|\alpha-\beta|\leq 2\delta}|\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)||g^{\prime}(\beta)|d\beta

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|\leq 2\delta}|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-1}d\beta

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\delta^{\gamma}.

To bound the second integral, we note that
|\alpha-\beta|\leq 2\delta
implies that
|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta|\leq 3\delta
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|\alpha-\beta|\leq 2\delta}|\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)||g^{\prime}(\beta)|d\beta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta|\leq 3\delta}|\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)||g^{\prime}(\beta)|d\beta

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\delta^{\gamma}.

We have therefore deduced the following bound:

(78)

|I_{n}|\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\delta^{\gamma}.

Now we turn to the estimate for
I_{f}
. We first observe the following decomposition concerning the difference
K(\alpha,\beta)-K(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)
:

\displaystyle K(\alpha,\beta)-K(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)-\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}+\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)\left(\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}-\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha,\beta)-\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\partial_{\alpha}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)\frac{w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)-w(\alpha,\beta)}{(1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta))(1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta))}

\displaystyle=:T_{1}+T_{2}.

Using (
73
), the first term
T_{1}
will be further decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)-f^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right)+\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}\sum_{k=1}^{3}T_{1k},

where

\displaystyle T_{11}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i\alpha^{\prime}}-e^{i\alpha}}{(e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta})(e^{i\alpha^{\prime}}-e^{i\beta})}\left(f^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})-w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)(ie^{i\alpha^{\prime}})\right),

\displaystyle T_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{ie^{i\alpha}(w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)-w(\alpha,\beta))}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}},\quad T_{13}=\frac{iw(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)(e^{i\alpha^{\prime}}-e^{i\alpha})}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}

We first show that
T_{1k}
,
k=1,2,3
, and
T_{2}
are harmless. To do this we use the following elementary bound: for any
\beta\in[0,2\pi)
,

(79)

|w(\alpha,\beta)-w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1}}\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-1}.

This bound follows from the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and (
75
):

\displaystyle|w(\alpha,\beta)-w(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\alpha}|\partial_{\alpha}w(\xi,\beta)|d\xi\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1}}\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-1}.

To bound
T_{11}
, we recall that an almost identical argument to that proving (
75
) gives

(80)

|f^{\prime}(\alpha)-w(\alpha,\beta)(ie^{i\alpha})|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma}.

Also using that
|e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}|\sim|\alpha-\beta|
when
|\alpha-\beta|
sufficiently small, and that
|\alpha-\beta|\sim|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta|
given
|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta
, we have

(81)

|T_{11}|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1}}\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-2}.

A similar reasoning also yields the following bounds:

(82)

|T_{12}|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1}}\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-2},\quad|T_{13}|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1}}\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{-1}.

For
T_{2}
, we use (
70
), (
75
), and (
79
) to conclude that

(83)

|T_{2}|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{2\gamma-2}.

With the above estimates, we write

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}Re\left(\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}t}}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)-f^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right)\right)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta,

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}\bar{K}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta=:J_{f}^{1}+J_{f}^{2},

where from (
81
), (
82
), and (
83
) we can estimate
J_{f}^{2}
by:

(84)

\displaystyle|J_{f}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}\left(\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-2}+\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{2\gamma-2}+\delta|\alpha-\beta|^{-1}\right)d\beta

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}(\delta^{\gamma}+\max\{\delta^{2\gamma},\delta|\log\delta|,\delta\}+\delta|\log\delta|)

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\delta^{\gamma}.

Here, we also used that
\gamma\in(0,1).
To study the main term
J_{f}^{1}
, we rewrite it as

\displaystyle J_{f}^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}Re\left(\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}t}}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\bar{w}(\alpha)}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)-f^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right)\right)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}Re\left(e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\left(\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}-\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\bar{w}(\alpha)}\right)\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)-f^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right)\right)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle=:J_{f}^{1,1}+J_{f}^{1,2},

where
\bar{w}(\alpha)=\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)}{ie^{i\alpha}}
. The term
J_{f}^{1,2}
can be bounded using the following estimate:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta)}-\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\bar{w}(\alpha)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\bar{w}(\alpha)-w(\alpha,\beta)}{(1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}w(\alpha,\beta))(1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\bar{w}(\alpha))}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma},

where we used the non-degeneracy bound (
70
) and (
80
). With the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle|J_{f}^{1,2}|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}^{2}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}\delta^{\gamma}|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-1}d\beta\leq C\epsilon_{0}\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\delta^{\gamma}.

To treat
J_{f}^{1,1}
, we note the following identity: letting
F(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},t)=\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}t}}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\bar{w}(\alpha)}(f^{\prime}(\alpha)-f^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime}))
,

\displaystyle Re\left(\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}t}}{1+e^{\frac{i}{2}t}\bar{w}(\alpha)}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)-f^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}Re\left(Fe^{-i\alpha}\right)+\frac{1}{2}Im\left(Fe^{-i\alpha}\right)\cot\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\right),

where
F
obeys the bound
|F(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},t)|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\delta^{\gamma}
. Writing

\displaystyle J_{f}^{1,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}Re\left(Fe^{-i\alpha}\right)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta+\frac{Im\left(Fe^{-i\alpha}\right)}{4\pi}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}\cot\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\right)g^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta,

The first integral can be absolutely bounded by
C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\delta^{\gamma}
. Using the oddness of kernel
\cot(z/2)
, the absolute value of the second integral equals to

(85)

\begin{array}[]{l}\left|\frac{Im\left(Fe^{-i\alpha}\right)}{4\pi}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}\cot\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\right)(g^{\prime}(\beta)-g^{\prime}(\alpha))d\beta\right|\\
\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\delta^{\gamma}\int_{|\alpha-\beta|>2\delta}|\alpha-\beta|^{\gamma-1}d\beta\\
\leq C\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\delta^{\gamma},\end{array}

where we used that
|\alpha-\beta|
is sufficiently small. Hence, we have proved

(86)

|I_{f}|\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\delta^{\gamma},

which combining with (
78
) yields
\|I^{\prime}(\alpha)\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Given
\gamma\in(0,1)
, assume
f,g\in C^{1,\gamma}
. Then there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small that for
\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}<\epsilon_{2}
, the function

(87)

I(\alpha):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|1+\frac{2e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}}{3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}}\frac{f(\alpha)-f(\beta)}{e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}}\right|\partial_{\beta}g(\beta)d\beta

is
C^{1,\gamma}
. Moreover, the following estimate holds:

(88)

\|I\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\leq C(\gamma)\|f\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\|g\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}.

Proof.

The proof of this Lemma is essentially the same as that of Lemma
1
. We observe that the function

\frac{2e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}}{3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}}

is smooth in both
\alpha
and

. Moreover, we have the bound

\left|\frac{2e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}}{3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}}\right|\leq 1.

The two facts above guarantee that the same argument for Lemma

goes through with minor changes.
∎

The last technical lemma concerns an integral operator involving kernel
\tilde{G}
:

Lemma 3
.

Given
\gamma\in(0,1)
, assume
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}\in C^{1,\gamma}
and
h\in C^{1}
. Then there exists
\epsilon_{1}>0
sufficiently small that for
\|f_{j}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}},\|g_{j}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}<\epsilon_{1}
,
j=1,2
, the function

(89)

\tilde{I}(\alpha):=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{G}\left(|f_{1}(\alpha)-f_{2}(\beta)+g_{1}(\alpha)-g_{2}(\beta)|\right)\partial_{\beta}h(\beta)d\beta

is
C^{1,\gamma}
. Moreover, the following estimate holds:

(90)

\|\tilde{I}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\leq C\epsilon_{1}^{2}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\|h\|_{C^{1}}.

Proof.

For convenience, denote

F(\alpha,\beta):=f_{1}(\alpha)-f_{2}(\beta)+g_{1}(\alpha)-g_{2}(\beta).

Then by assumption,
F(\alpha,\beta)\leq 4\epsilon_{1}
for any
\alpha,\beta\in[0,2\pi)
. Recall that for
\tilde{G}
defined in (
64
), we have

|\tilde{G}(r)|\leq Cr^{2}|\log r|.

for
r>0
sufficiently small. Then by selecting
\epsilon_{1}
sufficiently small, we have

(91)

\|\tilde{I}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\epsilon_{1}^{2}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\|h\|_{C^{1}}.

Differentiating (
89
), we have

(92)

\tilde{I}^{\prime}(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}Re\left[\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|F(\alpha,\beta)|)\frac{F(\alpha,\beta)}{|F(\alpha,\beta)|}(\bar{f}_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha)+\bar{g}_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha))\right]h^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta.

Recall that
\tilde{G}^{\prime}(r)\leq Cr|\log r|
when
r>0
is sufficiently small. Then it is clear from (
92
) that

(93)

\|\tilde{I}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\epsilon_{1}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\cdot\epsilon_{1}\|h\|_{C^{1}}\leq C\epsilon_{1}^{2}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\|h\|_{C^{1}}.

Finally, we fix
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
, and
\delta=:|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|
. For notational convenience, let us write

(94)

H(\alpha,\beta):=\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|F(\alpha,\beta)|)\frac{F(\alpha,\beta)}{|F(\alpha,\beta)|}.

We further without loss of generality assume that
\delta
is sufficiently small. Then

(95)

\displaystyle\tilde{I}^{\prime}(\alpha)-\tilde{I}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}Re\left[(H(\alpha,\beta)-H(\alpha^{\prime},\beta))(\bar{f}_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha)+\bar{g}_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha))\right]h^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2\pi}Re\left[H(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)(\bar{f}_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha)-\bar{f}_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})+\bar{g}_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha)-\bar{g}_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime}))\right]h^{\prime}(\beta)d\beta

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

By the assumptions on norms of
f_{1},g_{1}
, and the bound
\tilde{G}^{\prime}(r)\leq Cr|\log r|
when
r>0
is sufficiently small, it is straightforward to obtain that

(96)

|J_{2}|\leq C\epsilon_{1}^{2}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\|h\|_{C^{1}}\delta^{\gamma}.

To estimate
J_{1}
, we observe that the bound

(97)

|J_{1}|\leq C\epsilon_{1}^{2}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\|h\|_{C^{1}}\delta^{\gamma},

holds as long as the following bound holds:

(98)

|H(\alpha,\beta)-H(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)|\leq C\epsilon_{1}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\delta^{\gamma}.

Combining (
97
) and (
96
), we have thus proved

(99)

\|\tilde{I}^{\prime}\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq C\epsilon_{1}^{2}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\|h\|_{C^{1}}.

The desired estimate (
90
) holds after combining (
91
), (
93
), and (
99
).

Hence, we are left to show the estimate (
98
). We note that we can write
H(\alpha,\beta)=\tilde{H}(F(\alpha,\beta))
, where
\tilde{H}(z)=\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|z|)\frac{z}{|z|}
. We first show that

(100)

|\tilde{H}(z)-\tilde{H}(w)|\leq C\omega(|z-w|),

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|z|,|w|<1
. Here,
\omega
denotes the Log-Lipschitz modulus of continuity. Without loss of generality we assume that
|w|\leq|z|
. Note that

(101)

\tilde{H}(z)-\tilde{H}(w)=\left(\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|z|)-\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|w|)\right)\frac{z}{|z|}+\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|w|)\left(\frac{z}{|z|}-\frac{w}{|w|}\right).

Since
\tilde{G}^{\prime}
is Log-Lipschitz, it is clear that the first term in (
101
) is bounded by
C\omega(|z-w|)
. To bound the second term in (
101
), we split into two cases. If
|w|\leq 2|z-w|
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|w|)\left(\frac{z}{|z|}-\frac{w}{|w|}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2|\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|w|)|\leq 2\omega(|w|)\leq 2\omega(2|z-w|)\leq C\omega(|z-w|),

where we used that
\tilde{G}^{\prime}(0)=0
. If
|w|>2|z-w|
, we note that

|z-w|^{2}=(|z|-|w|)^{2}+|z||w|\left|\frac{z}{|z|}-\frac{w}{|w|}\right|^{2}\geq|w|^{2}\left|\frac{z}{|z|}-\frac{w}{|w|}\right|^{2},

where we used
|w|\leq|z|
. Using the above bound, we see that

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{G}^{\prime}(|w|)\left(\frac{z}{|z|}-\frac{w}{|w|}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C|w||\log w|\frac{|z-w|}{|w|}\leq C|\log(2|z-w|)||z-w|\leq C\omega(|z-w|),

which concludes the proof of (
100
). Finally to prove (
98
), we note that

\displaystyle|H(\alpha,\beta)-H(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq C\omega(|F(\alpha,\beta)-F(\alpha^{\prime},\beta)|)

\displaystyle\leq C\omega(|f_{1}(\alpha)-f_{1}(\alpha^{\prime})+g_{1}(\alpha)-g_{1}(\alpha^{\prime})|)

\displaystyle\leq C\omega((\|f_{1}\|_{C^{1}}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}})\delta)

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon_{1}\delta|\log(\epsilon_{1}\delta)|\leq C\epsilon_{1}|\log\epsilon_{1}|\delta^{\gamma},

where we used
\gamma\in(0,1)
in the final inequality above.
∎

With the above three technical lemmas established, we demonstrate that the dynamics described by
Z_{\pm}
is indeed dominant in the small-scale regime, namely
R\ll 1
. More precisely, we show the following stability result concerning the perturbation
\zeta_{\pm}
:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\zeta_{\pm}
be the unique
C^{1,\gamma}
solution to (
67
) equipped with zero initial data. For any
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
sufficiently small, there exists
R_{0}(\delta_{0})>0
such that for any
R\in(0,R_{0}]
, the time instance

\tau(\delta_{0})=\sup\{t\geq 0\;|\;\max_{\pm}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}(\|\zeta_{\pm}(\cdot,s)\|_{C^{1,\gamma}})\leq\delta_{0}\}

verifies the bound

\tau(\delta_{0})>T_{0}=\frac{9\pi}{4}.

Proof.

We prove by an energy argument. To start with, we choose
\delta_{0}>0
such that
\delta_{0}<\epsilon_{0}
, where
\epsilon_{0}
is given in Lemma
1
. We first note that
\tau>0
due to local well-posedness and
\zeta_{\pm}(\alpha,0)=0
. Recall that
Z_{\pm}
verifies the following bounds:

\|Z_{\pm}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\leq C_{\pm}R,

where
C_{\pm}>0
are universal constants. Then we choose
R_{0}>0
so that
C_{\pm}R_{0}\leq\min\{\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\}
, where
\epsilon_{i}
,
i=0,1,2
, are defined in Lemma
1
,
2
, and
3
. Hence, invoking Lemma
1
, Lemma
3
, and that the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
is a bounded operator on
C^{\gamma}
, we obtain the following inequality for any
t\in[0,\tau)
,
R\in(0,R_{0}]
:

(102)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\zeta_{-}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)\left(\|\zeta_{-}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}+\|\zeta_{-}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}^{2}+(1+\delta_{0})R^{2}|\log R|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)(1+\delta_{0})(\|\zeta_{-}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}+R^{2}|\log R|).

To show an a priori estimate for
\|\zeta_{+}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}
, we have all the ingredients except for an
C^{1,\gamma}
estimate for the integral

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}\right|\partial_{\beta}\zeta_{+}(\beta)d\beta.

However, note that
3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}
is smooth in both
\alpha
and
\beta
, and that the following lower bound holds:

|3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}-e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}|\geq|3e^{-i\frac{4}{9}t}|-|e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)}|=2.

The above two facts imply that the
C^{1,\gamma}
norm of the above integral is bounded by
\|\zeta_{+}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}
. This fact combined with Lemma
2
and
3
yields:

(103)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\zeta_{+}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)\left(\|\zeta_{+}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}+\|\zeta_{+}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}^{2}+(1+\delta_{0})R^{2}|\log R|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\gamma)(1+\delta_{0})(\|\zeta_{+}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}+R^{2}|\log R|).

Using
\zeta_{\pm}(\alpha,0)=0
and applying Grönwall inequality to (
102
), (
103
), we obtain

\|\zeta_{\pm}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}\leq\frac{1}{C(\gamma)(1+\delta_{0})}e^{C(\gamma)(1+\delta_{0})t}R^{2}|\log R|<\delta_{0}

for all
t>0
such that

t<\frac{1}{C(\gamma)(1+\delta_{0})}\log\left(C(\gamma)\frac{\delta_{0}(1+\delta_{0})}{R^{2}|\log R|}\right).

Now, we further choose
R
even smaller depending on
\delta_{0}
, such that the RHS of the above inequality is strictly larger than
\frac{9\pi}{4}
. We have thus completed the proof upon invoking the definition of
\tau
.
∎

With Proposition

Remark 2
.

While the above merger is realized for patch solutions, a similar result holds for smooth, compactly supported solutions. We sketch the procedure here: as in the patch case, we first find merger in the unscreened equations (
59
) with solution
\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}
. Let
\bar{\sigma}_{+}
be any non-radial, uniformly rotating smooth solution to the 2D Euler equation supported in disk
B_{R}(d,0)
,
d\in\mathbb{R}
. The existence of such solutions has been shown in
[
6
,
16
]
. Let us also fix
\max\{\bar{\sigma}_{+}\}=1
. Then let
\bar{\sigma}_{-}
to be a nonnegative, radial function supported in disk
B_{R}(0)
with
\max\{\bar{\sigma}_{-}\}=1
. Since
\bar{\sigma}_{+}
is nonradial, by adjusting
d
accordingly, we obtain a time-periodic solution of (

are disjoint initially, then overlap, and become disjoint again. Then merger for the smooth solutions of the screened equations (

for smooth solutions of the left-handed system.

5.
Reconnection for the right-handed equations

5.1.
The unscreened equations

We consider here the right-handed equations with the equation of state (
5
). In this case, a direct algebraic manipulation gives:

\displaystyle v_{+}

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})+(\mathbb{B}(F)+\mathbb{U}(F))=-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})+2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F),

\displaystyle v_{-}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})-(\mathbb{B}(F)+\mathbb{U}(F))=\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})-2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F)

The screened right-handed equations are thus equivalently written as

(104)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\sigma_{+}-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}+2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F)\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\sigma_{-}+\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}-2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}(F)\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}=0,\end{cases}

and the unscreened equations are:

(105)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\sigma_{+}-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\sigma_{-}+\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}=0.\end{cases}

5.2.
A motivating example for the unscreened equations

We start with a simple example in the case of point vortex dynamics to demonstrate a merger for the unscreeened equations (
105
). Consider the data for point vortices given by the following: for
(x_{0},y_{0})
with
x_{0}<0
,
y_{0}>0
, let

\sigma_{+}(0,x,y)=\delta(x-x_{0},y-y_{0})-\delta(x+x_{0},y-y_{0}),

\sigma_{-}(0,x,y)=\delta(x-x_{0},y+y_{0})-\delta(x+x_{0},y+y_{0}).

After plugging in (
105
), the solution for
t>0
satisfies the following form:

\sigma_{\pm}(t,x,y)=\delta(x-x(t),y\mp y(t))-\delta(x+x(t),y\mp y(t)),

while
(x(t),y(t))
obeys the following ODE:

(106)

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{-2y}{(2y)^{2}}-\frac{-1}{2\pi}\frac{-2y}{(2x)^{2}+(2y)^{2}},

(107)

\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\frac{-1}{2\pi}\frac{2x}{(2x)^{2}+(2y)^{2}}.

Simplifying, we have

(108)

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{1}{y}-\frac{y}{x^{2}+y^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x^{2}}{y(x^{2}+y^{2})},

(109)

\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.

Note that
x/y
is a constant of motion:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{\prime}}{y}-\frac{xy^{\prime}}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{y^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})}-\frac{x^{2}}{y^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

which implies
x(t)=\frac{x_{0}}{y_{0}}y(t)
. Plugging this equality into the equation for
x(t)
, we have

\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\frac{y_{0}}{x_{0}}(1+\frac{y_{0}^{2}}{x_{0}^{2}})}\frac{1}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(x^{2})=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\frac{y_{0}}{x_{0}}(1+\frac{y_{0}^{2}}{x_{0}^{2}})}<0,

where the inequality follows from
x_{0}<0,y_{0}>0
. Denote

C_{0}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\frac{y_{0}}{x_{0}}(1+\frac{y_{0}^{2}}{x_{0}^{2}})},

we see that
x(t)
, and hence
y(t)
converges to
0
in finite time, with the following explicit time for the merger of point vortices:

T_{*}=-\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{C_{0}}.

5.3.
Merger for smooth solutions of the unscreened equations

The point vortex solution that lead to a finite-time merger above obeys the following symmetry:

(110)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\pm}(t,x_{1},x_{2})=-\sigma_{\pm}(t,-x_{1},x_{2}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{+}(t,x_{1},x_{2})=\sigma_{-}(t,x_{1},-x_{2}).

In this section, we demonstrate the merger for a large class of smooth solutions equipped with the above symmetry, inspired by the point vortex configuration discussed in the previous section. We remark that the symmetry property (
110
) is reminiscent of the well-known odd-odd symmetry in the context of incompressible Euler equations in two dimensions. The dynamics in our case is completely different. This is because
\sigma_{\pm}
does not see the mirror images of themselves across the
x_{2}
axis.

As a preliminary, we show that the two symmetries (
110
) are conserved by smooth solutions to both the unscreened (
105
) and the screened system (
104
).

Proposition 2
.

Let
\sigma_{\pm}
be the smooth solution to (
105
) (or (
104
)) with initial datum
\tau_{\pm}
. If
\tau_{\pm}
verifies the following properties:

\displaystyle\tau_{\pm}(x_{1},x_{2})=-\tau_{\pm}(-x_{1},x_{2}),

\displaystyle\tau_{+}(x_{1},x_{2})=\tau_{-}(x_{1},-x_{2}),

then (
110
) holds for all
t>0
.

Proof.

We only prove the case for the screened system (
104
), as the corresponding statement concerning (
105
) is similar and follows easily. We first show the preservation of
\sigma_{+}(t,x_{1},x_{2})=\sigma_{-}(t,x_{1},-x_{2})
. Define the reflection map

R(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1},-x_{2}),

and denote

A:=\nabla R=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}.

Also define
\eta_{\mp}(t,x)=\sigma_{\pm}(t,Rx)
. We first observe that for any smooth function
h
,

(\Delta^{-1}h)(Rx)=\Delta^{-1}(h\circ R)(x),\quad((I-\Delta)^{-1}h)(Rx)=(I-\Delta)^{-1}(h\circ R)(x).

Writing
\nabla^{\perp}=J\nabla
, where

J=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix},

and using that
JA=-AJ
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}(h\circ R)(x)=J\nabla(\Delta^{-1}(h\circ R)(x))=JA(\nabla\Delta^{-1}h(Rx))=-A\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}h(Rx)=-A\mathbb{U}(h)(Rx).

Similarly, we also have

\mathbb{B}(h\circ R)(x)=-A\mathbb{B}(h)(Rx).

Using the equation for
\sigma_{-}
and the above computations, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})(Rx)\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}(Rx)+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{B}(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})(Rx)+\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})(Rx))\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}(Rx)

\displaystyle=-\left(-A^{-1}\mathbb{U}(\eta_{-})(x)\cdot A\nabla\eta_{+}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(-A^{-1}\left(\mathbb{B}(\eta_{+}+\eta_{-})(x)+\mathbb{U}(\eta_{+}+\eta_{-})(x)\right)\right)\cdot A\nabla\eta_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{U}(\eta_{-})(x)\cdot\nabla\eta_{+}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{B}(\eta_{+}+\eta_{-})(x)+\mathbb{U}(\eta_{+}+\eta_{-})(x)\right)\cdot\nabla\eta_{+}(x),

where we used that
A
is symmetric in the final equality above. A similar derivation using the
\sigma_{+}
equation yields:

\partial_{t}\eta_{-}(x)=-\mathbb{U}(\eta_{+})(x)\cdot\nabla\eta_{-}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{B}(\eta_{+}+\eta_{-})(x)+\mathbb{U}(\eta_{+}+\eta_{-})(x)\right)\cdot\nabla\eta_{-}(x).

Finally, observe that by the assumption on initial datum
\tau_{\pm}
,

\eta_{\pm}(x)=\tau_{\mp}(Rx)=\tau_{\pm}(x).

By uniqueness, we conclude that for any
t>0
,
\sigma_{\pm}(t,x)=\eta_{\pm}(t,x)=\sigma_{\mp}(t,Rx)=\sigma_{\mp}(t,x_{1},-x_{2})
, which is the desired result. The conservation of odd-in-
x_{1}
symmetry for
\sigma_{\pm}
follows from a very similar argument. We omit further details.
∎

Another ingredient in the construction of a merger is the following key computation that concerns a restricted version of the center of mass. For convenience, let us denote
Q:=(0,\infty)^{2}
. The following proposition holds:

Proposition 3
.

Let
\sigma_{\pm}(\cdot,t)
be a smooth solution to (
105
) on
[0,T]
for some
T>0
. In addition, assume that
\sigma_{\pm}
has the following properties:

(1)

The symmetry condition (
110
) holds for
\sigma_{\pm}(x,0)
;

(2)

\{|\sigma_{+}|>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
for all
t\in[0,T]
;

(3)

\sigma_{+}
is either nonpositive or nonnegative in
Q
.

Define

E_{j}(t)=\int_{Q}x_{j}\sigma_{+}(x,t)dx,\quad j=1,2.

Then the following identities hold:

(111a)

E_{1}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{Q\times Q}\frac{x_{1}y_{1}(x_{2}+y_{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}|x+y|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y)\sigma_{+}(x)dydx>0,

(111b)

E_{2}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{Q\times Q}\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}}{|x+y|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y)\sigma_{+}(x)dydx>0,

where
\bar{y}=(y_{1},-y_{2})
.

Note that if we additionally choose
\sigma_{+}
to be nonpositive in
Q
, we have
E_{j}(t)<0
for all
t
and they monotonically increase. This mirrors the point vortex scenario considered in the previous subsection.

Proof.

Using the first equation of (
105
), it holds that

(112)

\displaystyle E_{j}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}x_{j}\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}dx=-\int_{Q}\mathbb{U}_{j}(\sigma_{-})\sigma_{+}dx+\int_{\partial Q}x_{j}\sigma_{+}(\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})\cdot n)dS,

where
n
denotes the outward-pointing normal vector along
\partial Q
. Observe that this boundary term vanishes: if
j=1
, only the boundary term along the positive
x_{1}
axis survives. But by Property 2,
\sigma_{+}\equiv 0
along
x_{1}
axis. If
j=2
, then only the boundary term along the positive
x_{2}
axis survives. But in this case,
\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})\cdot n=-\mathbb{U}_{1}(\sigma_{-})
vanishes due to odd-in-
x_{1}
symmetry. Hence, we have

(113)

E_{j}^{\prime}(t)=-\int_{Q}\mathbb{U}_{j}(\sigma_{-})\sigma_{+}dx.

We first consider the case
j=1
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}_{1}(\sigma_{-})(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\sigma_{-}(y)dy_{2}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-\tilde{y}|^{2}}\right)\sigma_{-}(y)dy_{2}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{x_{1}y_{1}(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{2}|x-\tilde{y}|^{2}}\sigma_{-}(y)dy_{2}dy_{1},

where
\tilde{y}=(-y_{1},y_{2})
. Here we used the symmetry
\sigma_{-}(y_{1},y_{2})=-\sigma_{-}(-y_{1},y_{2})
in the second equality above. Now, using the other symmetry
\sigma_{-}(y_{1},y_{2})=\sigma_{+}(y_{1},-y_{2})
and Property 2:

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}_{1}(\sigma_{-})(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{x_{1}y_{1}(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{2}|x-\tilde{y}|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y_{1},-y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{x_{1}y_{1}(x_{2}-y_{2})}{|x-y|^{2}|x-\tilde{y}|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y_{1},-y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{Q}\frac{x_{1}y_{1}(x_{2}+y_{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}|x+y|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y_{1},y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}

Combining the above identity with (
113
) yields (
111a
).

For the case
j=2
, we again use symmetry (
110
) and Property 2 to obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}_{2}(\sigma_{-})(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-y|^{2}}\sigma_{-}(y_{1},y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-y|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y_{1},-y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y_{1},y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y_{1},y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y_{1},y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{Q}\left(\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}-\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}}{|x+y|^{2}}\right)\sigma_{+}(y_{1},y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{Q}\frac{2y_{1}(x_{1}^{2}-y_{1}^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}|x+y|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y_{1},y_{2})dy_{2}dy_{1}.

Plugging into (
113
), we have

(114)

E_{2}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{Q\times Q}\frac{2y_{1}(x_{1}^{2}-y_{1}^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}|x+y|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y)\sigma_{+}(x)dydx.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{2y_{1}(x_{1}^{2}-y_{1}^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}|x+y|^{2}}+\frac{2x_{1}(y_{1}^{2}-x_{1}^{2}-(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2})}{|x-\bar{y}|^{2}|x+y|^{2}}=-2\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}}{|x+y|^{2}}.

Then after a symmetrization, from (
114
) we immediately have

\displaystyle E_{2}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{Q\times Q}\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}}{|x+y|^{2}}\sigma_{+}(y)\sigma_{+}(x)dydx,

which is (
111b
).
∎

With the above Proposition, we show that a family of initially disjoint
\sigma_{\pm}
data can merge in finite time:

Theorem 5
.

There exists smooth, compactly supported solutions
\sigma_{\pm}
to (
105
) such that

\supp(\sigma_{+}(\cdot,0))\cap\supp(\sigma_{-}(\cdot,0))=\emptyset
, and
\Int(\supp(\sigma_{+}(\cdot,T)))\cap\Int(\supp(\sigma_{-}(\cdot,T)))\neq\emptyset
for some
T>0

Proof.

Choose initial data
\tau_{\pm}(x)=\sigma_{\pm}(x,0)
such that the following properties are satisfied:

(1)

\tau_{\pm}\leq 0
in
Q
,
\{\sigma_{+}<0\}\subset Q
is simply connected, and the symmetry condition (
110

(2)

\|\tau_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}}=1
,
|\supp(\tau_{+})\cap Q|=2
,
|E_{j}(0)|>\frac{1}{2}
for

(3)

Let
D_{t}=\{x\in\supp(\sigma_{+}(x,t))\cap Q\;|\;\sigma_{+}(x,t)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\}

(115)

|(\supp(\tau_{+})\cap Q)\backslash D_{0}|=\epsilon>0,

where
\epsilon<1
is a sufficiently small number to be chosen later;

(4)

For all
\alpha\in(-1,0)
,
\alpha
is a regular value of
\tau_{+}|_{Q}
. Moreover,
\{\tau_{+}|_{Q}=-1\}
contains only one point, which is a nondegenerate critical point.

We remark that all properties listed above are only chosen out of convenience. We expect that more flexible initial data would work.

Define

T_{0}:=\sup\{\tau>0\;|\;\{|\sigma_{+}(\cdot,t)|>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\text{ for all }t\in[0,\tau)\}.

Note that
T_{0}
is well-defined since, by local well-posedness, there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such that
\{|\sigma_{+}(\cdot,t)|>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\text{ for all }t\in[0,\tau_{0})
. Moreover,
|D_{t}|=|D_{0}|
,
|(\supp(\sigma_{+}(x,t))\cap Q)\backslash D_{t}|=\epsilon
for all
t\in[0,T_{0})
. By the choice of initial data and Proposition
3

j=1,2
,
t\in[0,T_{0})
:

(116)

|E_{j}(t)|=-E_{j}(t)\leq-E_{j}(0)=|E_{j}(0)|

Due to the monotone quantities (
116
), we first show that
E
is confined: by Chebyshev’s inequality,

(117)

\displaystyle|D_{t}\cap\{x_{1}\geq 4|E_{1}(0)|\}|

\displaystyle=|D_{t}\cap\{x_{1}(-\sigma_{+}(x,t))\geq 4|E_{1}(0)|\}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4|E_{1}(0)|}\int_{Q}x_{1}(-\sigma_{+}(x,t))dx\leq\frac{1}{4}.

Similarly, we also have

|D_{t}\cap Q^{\prime}|\geq 2-\epsilon-\frac{1}{2}\geq 1

From which we get

\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}}{|x+y|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{64}\frac{\min(\frac{1}{4|E_{2}(0)|},4|E_{1}(0)|)}{|E_{1}(0)|^{2}+|E_{2}(0)|^{2}},\quad x,y\in D_{t}^{\prime}.

To prove the main result, we need several topological arguments. By the symmetry assumption (

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\pm}:=\{x\in\{x_{1}>0\}\;|\;|\sigma_{\pm}(x,t)|>0\}.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\pm}
is simply connected by construction, so is

because these sets are transported by smooth vector fields. We start with showing the following two claims:

Claim 1.
There exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\neq\emptyset
for all
T\in[T_{0},T_{0}+\delta_{1})
;

Claim 2.
For any

To prove Claim 1, we first make the following observation: for any

, there exists
x(t)\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}
such that
x_{2}(t)\geq\frac{3}{16|E_{1}(0)|}>0
: suppose not, then there exists
t<T_{0}
such that
D_{t}\subset\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\subset\{0<x_{2}<\frac{3}{16|E_{1}(0)|}\}
. Hence

|D_{t}\cap\{x_{1}<4|E_{1}(0)|\}|<\frac{3}{16|E_{1}(0)|}\cdot 4|E_{1}(0)|=\frac{3}{4}.

However this above inequality contradicts (
117
). Then this observation together with a continuity argument immediately implies Claim 1.

To prove Claim 2, we see that if the claim were false, then there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
T\in(T_{0},T_{0}+\delta)
, it holds that
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\subset\{x_{2}>0\}
. This contradicts the definition of
T_{0}
as a supremum.

Now choose
\delta_{1}>0
as in Claim 1. The above two claims inform us that there exists
T\in(T_{0},T_{0}+\delta_{1})
such that
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\{x_{2}>0\}
and
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\{x_{2}\leq 0\}
are both nonempty. Since
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}
is open, we further have

\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\{x_{2}>0\}\neq\emptyset,\quad\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\{x_{2}<0\}\neq\emptyset.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is connected and thus path-connected, there exists a continuous path
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}
such that
\gamma(0)\in\{x_{2}>0\}
and
\gamma(1)\in\{x_{2}<0\}
. By Intermediate Value Theorem, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
\gamma(\alpha)\in\{x_{2}=0\}
. However by the symmetry
\sigma_{+}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sigma_{-}(x_{1},-x_{2})
, we must also have
\gamma(\alpha)\in\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}
. This shows that
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}\neq\emptyset
and the Theorem is proved.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
5
, the support of the canonical momentum variable
F=\frac{\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-}}{2}
sees topological change in the following sense:

Corollary 1
.

There exists smooth, compactly supported solutions
\sigma_{\pm}
to (
105
) such that
F=\frac{\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-}}{2}
has the following properties: there exists
T>0
such that

(1)

\Int(\supp(F(\cdot,0)))
has 4 connected components, but
\Int(\supp(F(\cdot,T)))
has 2 connected components;

(2)

the following trichotomy holds:

(a)

\{|F(x,0)|>\frac{1}{2}\}=\emptyset
, but
\{|F(x,T)|>\frac{1}{2}\}\neq\emptyset
, or;

(b)

Card(\{|F(x,0)|=\frac{1}{2}\})=4
, but
Card(\{|F(x,T)|=\frac{1}{2}\})>4
, or;

(c)

There exists
|\alpha|\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that
\alpha
is a regular value of
F(x,0)
, but a critical value of
F(x,T)
.

Proof.

Let the initial data
\tau_{\pm}
and the time instance
T>0
be specified as in Theorem
5
. We first remark that one can restrict the attention to the dynamics in the right-half plane
\{x_{1}>0\}
. Recalling the sets
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\pm}
, we note that

\Int(\supp(\sigma_{\pm}(\cdot,t)))=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\pm}\cup\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{t}^{\pm},

where we denote
\tilde{A}:=\{(-x_{1},x_{2})\in A\}
for any set
A
. By choice of initial data, we have
\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\pm}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0}^{\pm}=\emptyset
. Then by odd-in-
x_{1}
symmetry recorded in (
110
), we must have
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\pm}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{t}^{\pm}=\emptyset
for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore, in view of
\Int(\supp(F(\cdot,t)))\subset\Int(\supp(\sigma_{+}(\cdot,t)))\cup\Int(\supp(\sigma_{-}(\cdot,t)))
, it suffices to understand
S_{t}^{>}:=\{x\in\{x_{1}>0\}\;|\;F(x,t)\neq 0\}
.

(1)

To prove the first statement, we first show that
S_{t}^{>}=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\cup\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}
.
S_{t}^{>}\subset\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\cup\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}
is clear by definition of
F
. To show the other direction, observe that for
x_{1}>0
,

F(x,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\sigma_{+}(x,t)}{2},&x\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\backslash\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-},\\
\frac{\sigma_{-}(x,t)}{2},&x\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}\backslash\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+},\\
\frac{\sigma_{+}(x,t)+\sigma_{-}(x,t)}{2},&x\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-},\\
0,&\text{elsewhere}\end{cases}

It is clear that
F\neq 0
whenever
x\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\Delta\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}
. By (
110
), we see that
\frac{\sigma_{+}(x,t)+\sigma_{-}(x,t)}{2}\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}
. Hence, we arrive at
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}\subset S_{t}^{>}
.

By choice of initial data, it is immediate that
S_{0}^{>}
has 2 connected components since
\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\pm}
are disjoint, connected open sets. By Theorem
5
, we have
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}\neq\emptyset
. This already implies that
S_{t}^{>}=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\cup\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}
is a connected open set.

(2)

From the construction of initial data, it is straightforward to see that
\{|F(x,0)|>\frac{1}{2}\}=\emptyset
and
\alpha
is a regular value of
F(x,0)
for any
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\backslash\{0\}
. Moreover, the set
\{F(x,0)=\frac{1}{2}\}=\{|\tau_{\pm}|=1\}
contains exactly 4 distinct points.

At time instance
T
, we show the following claim:

Claim:
There exists
x^{*}=(x_{1}^{*},0)\in\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}
such that
F(x^{*},T)<0
and
\nabla F(x^{*},T)=0
.

Before we prove this claim, we explain how one arrives at the trichotomy from this result: if there exists some
x\in\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}
such that
F(x,T)<-\frac{1}{2}
, this trivially falls into the first scenario. If
F(x,T)\geq-\frac{1}{2}
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}
, we further split into two cases. Let
x^{*}
be specified by the claim. If
F(x^{*},T)>-\frac{1}{2}
, then this falls into the third scenario. Then it suffices to consider
F(x^{*},T)=-\frac{1}{2}
. Since
F(x,T)\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
\sigma_{\pm}(x,T)<0
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}
, then
\sigma_{\pm}(x,T)>-1
. Therefore,
\{\sigma_{\pm}(x,T)=-1\}\subset S_{T}^{>}\backslash(\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-})
. Moreover, since diffeomorphisms conserve critical points,
Card(\{|\sigma_{\pm}(x,T)|=1\})=4
. From the above two facts and the symmetry assumption (
110
), we conclude that
Card(\{|F(x,T)|=\frac{1}{2}\})\geq 4
. However, because
F(x^{*},T)=-\frac{1}{2}
and
x^{*}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}
, we actually have
Card(\{|F(x,T)|=\frac{1}{2}\})>4
, which falls into the second scenario.

Now it remains to prove the claim. Consider the set
E=\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}\cap\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-}\cap\{x_{2}=0\}
, which is clearly nonempty and open in the induced topology of
\{x_{2}=0\}
. Let us fix a connected component
C
of
E\subset\{x_{2}=0\}
. Then there exist
0<a<b<\infty
such that
C=(a,b)\times\{0\}
. Note that
\sigma_{+}((a,0),T)=\sigma_{+}((b,0),T)=0
and
\sigma_{+}((x_{1},0),T)<0
for any
x_{1}\in(a,b)
. By Mean Value Theorem, there exists
x_{1}^{*}\in(a,b)
such that
\partial_{1}\sigma_{+}((x_{1}^{*},0),T)=0.
On the other hand, we note that for any
(z,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
t\geq 0
,

\partial_{1}F(z,0)=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}\sigma_{+}(z,0)+\partial_{1}\sigma_{-}(z,0))=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}\sigma_{+}(z,0)+\partial_{1}\sigma_{+}(z,0))=\partial_{1}\sigma_{+}(z,0),

\partial_{2}F(z,0)=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{2}\sigma_{+}(z,0)+\partial_{2}\sigma_{-}(z,0))=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{2}\sigma_{+}(z,0)-\partial_{2}\sigma_{+}(z,0))=0,

where we used the symmetry property (
110
) above. Therefore, we conclude that there exists
x_{1}^{*}\in(a,b)
such that
\nabla F((x_{1}^{*},0),T)=0
. Moreover as
\sigma_{+}((x_{1}^{*},0),T)<0
, we also have
\sigma_{-}((x_{1}^{*},0),T)=\sigma_{+}((x_{1}^{*},0),T)<0
by symmetry, and hence
F((x_{1}^{*},0),T)<0
. This concludes the proof of the claim.

∎

5.4.
Merger for smooth solutions of the screened equations

In view of the above analysis for the unscreened equations (
105
), we prove an analogous result to Theorem
5
and Corollary
1
for the screened system (
104
) when the dynamics are at sufficiently small scales. Toward this end, given a scale
R>0
, we introduce the following change of variables:

(119)

\sigma_{\pm}(x,t)=\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}(z,t),\quad z=\frac{x}{R}.

From (
104
), we obtain the following equivalent system satisfied by profiles
\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}
in
(z,t)
coordinates:

(120)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{\sigma}_{+}-\mathbb{U}(\tilde{\sigma}_{-})\cdot\nabla\tilde{\sigma}_{+}+\frac{R}{2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}(R(z-w))(\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(w)+\tilde{\sigma}_{-}(w))dw\right]\cdot\nabla\tilde{\sigma}_{+}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{\sigma}_{-}+\mathbb{U}(\tilde{\sigma}_{+})\cdot\nabla\tilde{\sigma}_{-}-\frac{R}{2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}(R(z-w))(\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(w)+\tilde{\sigma}_{-}(w))dw\right]\cdot\nabla\tilde{\sigma}_{-}=0,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{K}
is the kernel corresponding to operator
2\nabla^{\perp}\mathbb{K}
.

As a preliminary, we remark on the symmetry property concerning
\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}
and several a priori bounds. First, we recall from Proposition
2
that the solution
\sigma_{\pm}
to the full system (
104
) conserves the symmetry (
110
). Then so does
\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}
. Moreover, the following a priori bounds hold: for any
t\geq 0

(121)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|\tau_{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=R^{-2}\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=R^{-2}\|\tau_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}.

We now set the initial data
\tau_{\pm}
to the original system (
104
) by

(122)

\tau_{\pm}(x)=\tilde{\tau}_{\pm}(z),

where
\tilde{\tau}_{\pm}
satisfies Properties 1–4 listed in the proof of Theorem
5
. Let
\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}(z,t)
be the solution to the leading order problem (
105
) initiated by data
\tilde{\tau}_{\pm}(z)
. Let us also denote

\bar{E}_{j}(t)=\int_{Q}z_{j}\bar{\sigma}_{+}(z)dz.

By Theorem
5
, there exists
\bar{T}:=\frac{|\bar{E}_{2}(0)|}{c_{2}}<\infty
such that there is
T\in(0,\bar{T})
such that the support interior of
\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}
intersects at time
T
. Here, we recall that
c_{2}
defined in (
118
) only depends on
\bar{E}_{j}(0)
,
j=1,2
. In particular,
c_{2}
is independent of the scale
R
.

With above important notations introduced, we state and prove the following main theorem concerning the merger for the original screened equations (
104
):

Theorem 6
.

Let
\sigma_{\pm}
be the solution to (
104
) initiated by data given in (
122
). Then
\supp(\sigma_{+}(\cdot,0))\cap\supp(\sigma_{-}(\cdot,0))=\emptyset
, and
\Int(\supp(\sigma_{+}(\cdot,T)))\cap\Int(\supp(\sigma_{-}(\cdot,T)))\neq\emptyset
for some
T>0
.

Proof.

The proof is arranged into various steps:

Step 1. Control of the supports for
\sigma_{\pm}
.
In this step, we establish an estimate for the size of the supports for
\sigma_{\pm}
(and therefore
\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}
) on a sufficiently long time interval, namely on
[0,2\bar{T}]
. Let
d_{0}>0
be such that
\supp(\tilde{\tau}_{\pm})\subset B_{d_{0}}(0)
i.e.
\supp(\tau_{\pm})\subset B_{d_{0}R}(0)
.
Invoking a standard integral operator estimate, there exists a universal constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\|\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{\pm})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(\mathbb{B}+\mathbb{U})(F)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}\|\sigma_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\sigma_{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{0}R\|\tilde{\tau}_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here, we used (
121
) and
\|\tilde{\tau}_{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}=1
. The above estimate together with (
119
) immediately gives

(123)

\supp(\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}(\cdot,t))\subset B_{d_{\bar{T}}}(0),\quad t\in[0,2\bar{T}],

where
d_{\bar{T}}=d_{0}+2C_{0}\bar{T}\|\tilde{\tau}_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In particular,
d_{\bar{T}}
is independent of
R
.

Step 2. Moment analysis.
In this step, similar to the study of the leading order model (
105
), we analyze the restricted moments corresponding to the profiles
\tilde{\sigma}_{+}
, namely:

(124)

E_{j}(t)=\int_{Q}z_{j}\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(z)dz.

Note that we have
E_{j}(0)=\bar{E}_{j}(0)
, and
E_{j}(t)\leq 0
for all
t\geq 0
due to our choice of initial data.

The main ingredient is the following lemma, analogous to Proposition
3
:

Lemma 4
.

Let
\sigma_{\pm}(\cdot,t)
be a smooth solution to (
104
) on
[0,T]
,
T>0
. In addition, assume that
\sigma_{\pm}
has the following properties:

(1)

The symmetry condition (
110
) holds for
\sigma_{\pm}(\cdot,0)
;

(2)

\{|\sigma_{+}|>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
for all
t\in[0,T]
;

(3)

\sigma_{+}
is either nonpositive or nonnegative in
Q
.

Then the following identities hold:

(125a)

E_{1}^{\prime}(t)=I_{1}[\tilde{\sigma}_{+}]+RJ_{1}[\tilde{\sigma}],

(125b)

E_{2}^{\prime}(t)=I_{2}[\tilde{\sigma}_{+}]-RJ_{2}[\tilde{\sigma}],

where

\displaystyle I_{1}[\tilde{\sigma}_{+}]

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{Q\times Q}\frac{z_{1}w_{1}(z_{2}+w_{2})}{|z-\bar{w}|^{2}|z+w|^{2}}\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(w)\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(z)dwdz,

\displaystyle I_{2}[\tilde{\sigma}_{+}]

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{Q\times Q}\frac{z_{1}+w_{1}}{|z+w|^{2}}\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(w)\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(z)dwdz,

\displaystyle J_{i}[\tilde{\sigma}]

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}_{i}(R(z-w))\left(\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(w)+\tilde{\sigma}_{-}(w)\right)\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(z)dwdz,\quad i=1,2.

The proof for this lemma is almost identical to that of Proposition
3
, where one uses (
120
) instead of (
105
). We therefore omit the tedious computations here.

To proceed, we define

\overline{T}_{0}:=\sup\{\tau>0\;|\;\{|\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(\cdot,t)|>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\text{ for all }t\in[0,\tau)\}.

For the sake of contradiction, let us assume that
\overline{T}_{0}\geq\frac{11}{6}\bar{T}
. To start with, we first show the following weaker bound

(126)

E_{i}^{\prime}(t)>0,\quad t\in[0,\frac{11}{6}\bar{T}),\;i=1,2,

given the scale
R
chosen suitably small. To prove (
126
), we let
\tau\geq 0
be the first time instance such that either
E_{1}^{\prime}=0
or
E_{2}^{\prime}=0
. We may further assume that
\tau\leq\frac{11}{6}\bar{T}
, as otherwise we are done. For all
t\in[0,\tau)
, we have the moment bounds:

(127)

|E_{j}(t)|=-E_{j}(t)\leq-E_{j}(0)=|E_{j}(0)|=|\bar{E}_{j}(0)|.

With the above estimate and following a similar argument to that in Theorem
5
, one obtains:

(128)

I_{2}[\tilde{\sigma}_{+}]\geq c_{2},\quad t\in[0,\tau).

By a very similar argument to that deriving (
118
), one also obtains the following coercive bound for
E_{1}^{\prime}
:

(129)

I_{1}[\tilde{\sigma}_{+}]\geq c_{1},\quad t\in[0,\tau),

where
c_{1}>0
is a constant only depending on
\bar{E}_{1}(0)
and
\bar{E}_{2}(0)
. To estimate
J_{i}[\tilde{\sigma}]
, we use the following bound for the kernel
\mathcal{K}
: there exists a universal constant
R_{0}>0
such that

(130)

|\mathcal{K}(r)|\leq 1,\quad r\in[0,R_{0}].

In fact,
\mathcal{K}
has the asymptotics
|\mathcal{K}(r)|\sim r|\log r|+O(r)
near
r=0
(see e.g.
[
1
,
15
]
), but the crude bound (
130
) suffices. For
t\in[0,\tau)\subset[0,2\bar{T}]
,

, from (
123
) we have
|z-w|\leq 2d_{\bar{T}}
. Choosing

sufficiently small that

, we have
|\mathcal{K}(R(z-w))|\leq 1
. Hence for
i=1,2
,
t\in[0,\tau)
:

\displaystyle|J_{i}[\tilde{\sigma}]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\supp(\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm})\times\supp(\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm})}|\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(w)+\tilde{\sigma}_{-}(w)||\tilde{\sigma}_{+}(z)|dwdz

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}\|\tilde{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\tilde{\tau}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}.

The estimate above together with (
129
) and (
128
) yields that

E_{j}^{\prime}(t)\geq c_{j}-R\|\tilde{\tau}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}>\frac{3}{4}c_{j},\quad t\in[0,\tau)

once we choose

sufficiently small. Thus, we must have
\tau>\frac{11}{6}\bar{T}
and (
126

Now with the weaker bound (
126
) established, the bound (
127
) actually holds for all
t\in[0,\frac{11}{6}\bar{T})
. Then the same argument to that proving (
126
) actually yields the following stronger bound:

(131)

E_{j}^{\prime}(t)>\frac{3}{4}c_{j},\quad t\in[0,\frac{11}{6}\bar{T}),\;j=1,2.

Step 3. Proof of merger.

Since
\frac{4}{3}\bar{T}<\frac{11}{6}\bar{T}
, we recall
\bar{T}=\frac{|\bar{E}_{2}(0)|}{c_{2}}
and use (

\displaystyle E_{2}(\frac{4}{3}\bar{T})

\displaystyle=\bar{E}_{2}(0)+\int_{0}^{\frac{4}{3}\bar{T}}E^{\prime}_{2}(s)ds>\bar{E}_{2}(0)+c_{2}\bar{T}

\displaystyle=\bar{E}_{2}(0)+|\bar{E}_{2}(0)|\geq 0,

where we used

in the final inequality. However, this is absurd since

∎

6.
Merger stability

In this section we compare the dynamics of (
15
) to the dynamics when
\mathbb{B}
is replaced by

-\mathbb{U}
. That is, we compare the original screened right-handed model to the unscreened one. In this latter case, in view of (
16
), we have the system

(132)

\partial_{t}\sigma_{+}-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{+}=0

and

(133)

\partial_{t}\sigma_{-}+\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})\cdot\nabla\sigma_{-}=0.

We consider the same fixed smooth and compactly supported initial data
\sigma_{\pm}(0)=\tau_{\pm}
and we consider a situation in which at time zero the initial data have disjoint supports, and in particular,

(134)

\tau_{+}(x)\tau_{-}(x)=0,

and at time
T
we have

(135)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\sigma_{+}(x,T)\sigma_{-}(x,T)dx\geq\gamma>0.

We take now solutions
\widetilde{\sigma}_{\pm}
of the screened system (
15
) with the same initial data and note that

(136)

\begin{array}[]{l}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\widetilde{\sigma}_{+}(x,t)\widetilde{\sigma}_{-}(x,t)-\sigma_{+}(x,t)\sigma_{-}(x,t))dx\right|\\
=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\delta_{+}(x,t)\widetilde{\sigma}_{-}(x,t)+\delta_{-}(x,t)\sigma_{+}(x,t))dx\right|\\
\leq\|\delta_{+}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\|\tau_{-}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\|\delta_{-}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\|\tau_{+}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\end{array}

where

(137)

\delta_{\pm}=\widetilde{\sigma}_{\pm}-\sigma_{\pm}

and where

is the conjugate exponent of

. We used the fact that both the screened and the unscreened systems conserve

norms. We take

and we estimate the norms
\|\delta_{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{p}}
.

Let us denote

(138)

\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{B}+\mathbb{U}=\nabla^{\perp}(\mathbb{I}-\Delta)^{-1}\Delta^{-1},

and observe that the equations (
16
)
can be written as

(139)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbb{V}_{+}(\sigma)=-\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{-})+\mathbb{S}\left(\frac{\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-}}{2}\right)\\
\mathbb{V}_{-}(\sigma)=\mathbb{U}(\sigma_{+})-\mathbb{S}\left(\frac{\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-}}{2}\right),\\
\end{array}\right.

The equations obeyed by
\widetilde{\sigma}_{\pm}
are thus

(140)

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\sigma}_{+}+\left[-\mathbb{U}(\widetilde{\sigma}_{-})+\mathbb{S}(\overline{\sigma})\right]\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\sigma}_{+}=0,

and

(141)

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\sigma}_{-}+\left[\mathbb{U}(\widetilde{\sigma}_{+})-\mathbb{S}(\overline{\sigma})\right]\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\sigma}_{-}=0,

where

(142)

\overline{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{\sigma}_{+}+\widetilde{\sigma}_{-})

The equations obeyed by
\delta_{\pm}
, in view of (
132
), (
133
),
(
140
) and (
141

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|d_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{V}_{\pm}(d)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}+\nu_{\pm}\|\Delta\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|d\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|d\|_{H^{s-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\nu_{\pm}|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}.

Here,
\Omega_{0}=\supp(\tau_{\pm})
, and we used that the evolution of the inviscid solution
\bar{\sigma}
is volume-preserving. A further use of Young’s inequality and (
194
) yields:

(198)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|d_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|d_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|d\|_{H^{s-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\nu_{\pm}|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|d_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{H^{s}}+\nu_{\pm})\|d_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+C|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{H^{s}}\|d_{\pm}\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{\pm}|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bar{\sigma}_{\pm}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(T)|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|d_{\pm}\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|d_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\nu_{\pm}).

Here, we also used
s>2
. Adding (
197
) and (
198
), using
d_{\pm}(x,0)=0
, and then Grönwall inequality, we have

(199)

\|d_{\pm}\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|d_{\pm}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\leq C(T)((\nu_{+}+\nu_{-})t),\quad t\in[0,T],

which finishes the first statement of the Theorem. The second statement follows from (
199
), the following interpolation inequality:

\|d_{\pm}\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}}\leq\|d_{\pm}\|_{H^{s-1}}^{s-s^{\prime}}\|d_{\pm}\|_{H^{s}}^{s^{\prime}-s+1},\quad s^{\prime}\in(s-1,s),

and the uniform bound
\|d_{\pm}\|_{H^{s}}\leq C(T)
in view of (
194
).
∎

8.
A formal derivation

We offer here a unified derivation of both the right-handed and the left-handed equations. They are derived from a two-fluids action

(200)

\mathcal{A}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\frac{1}{2}(Au_{1}(x,t))\cdot u_{1}(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}(Bu_{2}(x,t))\cdot u_{2}(x,t)+(Cu_{1}(x,t))\cdot u_{2}(x,t)\right]dxdt

where
A,B,C
are commuting self-adjoint scalar operators and
u_{1},u_{2}
are
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
valued vector fields.
Scalar operators act on components,
(Au(x,t))_{i}=Au_{i}(x,t)
. The operators act in
x
. In this section the operators will be based on the Laplacian via functional calculus. We view
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
as varying vector fields corresponding to underlying varying Lagrangian path transformations,
X_{1}(a,t)
and
X_{2}(a,t)
which are time-depending fluctuating diffeomorphisms of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(or
\mathbb{T}^{d}
). The Eulerian velocities
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are related to the Lagrangian transformations via

(201)

u_{1}=\partial_{t}X_{1}\circ X_{1}^{-1},\quad u_{2}=\partial_{t}X_{2}\circ X_{2}^{-1}.

We consider Eulerian variation vector fields
\eta_{1}(x,t)
and
\eta_{2}(x,t)
, whch are obtained from the Lagrangian variations via
\eta_{1}=X_{1}^{\prime}\circ X_{1}^{-1}
and
\eta_{2}=X_{2}^{\prime}\circ X_{2}^{-1}
. Here
X_{1}^{\prime}(a,t)
is the variation of
X_{1}
, that is, a derivative of
X_{1}(a,t)
with respect to a hidden parameter, at a fixed position label
a
and fixed time
t
. Similarly
X_{2}^{\prime}(a,t)
is the variation of
X_{2}(a,t)
. These are the basic Lagrangian path fluctuation vector fields and
\eta_{i}
are their Eulerian counterparts.
Differentiating with respect to a parameter in (
201
) (as in
[
12
]
) we have by calculus

(202)

u_{1}^{\prime}=D_{1}\eta_{1}-\eta_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{1}

and

(203)

u_{2}^{\prime}=D_{2}\eta_{2}-\eta_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{2}.

where
D_{1}=\partial_{t}+u_{1}\cdot\nabla
and
D_{2}=\partial_{t}+u_{2}\cdot\nabla
. We thus obtain the variation of the Eulerian velocities due to Lagrangian fluctuations.

The action is a functional of two Lagrangian diffeomorphisms,

(204)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}[X_{1},X_{2}].

We compute its variation and equate it to zero. This yields evolution equations for the velocities
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
. We assume that
during their variation the diffeomorphisms
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
remain volume-preserving. This happens if (and only if)
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are divergence-free (
[
12
]
). Because
A,B,C
are selfadjoint, we have

(205)

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[(Au_{1}+Cu_{2})\cdot(D_{1}\eta_{1}+\eta_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{1})+(Cu_{1}+Bu_{2})\cdot(D_{2}\eta_{2}+\eta_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{2})\right]dxdt.

Because
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are divergence-free we have
D_{1}^{*}=-D_{1}
and
D_{2}^{*}=-D_{2}
. Integrating by parts and equating to zero separately the contributions due to
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
we obtain that

(206)

\partial_{t}w_{1}+u_{1}\cdot\nabla w_{1}+(\nabla u_{1})^{T}w_{1}+\nabla p_{1}=0

and

(207)

\partial_{t}w_{2}+u_{2}\cdot\nabla w_{2}+(\nabla u_{2})^{T}w_{2}+\nabla p_{2}=0,

where

(208)

w_{1}=Au_{1}+Cu_{2}

and

(209)

w_{2}=-Cu_{1}-Bu_{2}.

The pressures
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
are due to the fact that the vanishing is only modulo gradients, perpendicular to the space of divergence-free vector fields.

We take now
d=2
and
denote

(210)

\sigma_{1}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot w_{1},\quad\sigma_{2}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot w_{2}

the curls of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
. It follows from (
208
) and (
209
) that

(211)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sigma_{1}=A\Omega_{1}+C\Omega_{2}\\
\sigma_{2}=-C\Omega_{1}-B\Omega_{2}\end{array}\right.

holds, where

(212)

\Omega_{1}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u_{1},\quad\Omega_{2}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u_{2}

are the curls of the velocities
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
. Taking the curl of equations (
206
) and (
207
) we obtain that
\sigma_{1}
is carried by the flow of
u_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
is carried by the flow of
u_{2}

(213)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\sigma_{1}+u_{1}\cdot\nabla\sigma_{1}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\sigma_{2}+u_{2}\cdot\nabla\sigma_{2}=0.\end{array}\right.

Now, if
AB-C^{2}
is invertible, then we can invert (
211
), and have

(214)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Omega_{1}=(AB-C^{2})^{-1}(B\sigma_{1}+C\sigma_{2})\\
\Omega_{2}=(AB-C^{2})^{-1}(-C\sigma_{1}-A\sigma_{2}).\end{array}\right.

Thus the active scalars
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
determine the flow vorticities and hence the velocities.
As a sanity check, if
C=0
and
A=B=\mathbb{I}
, we obtain two independent Euler equations.
Now in the right-handed equations (
4
) we have, by taking the curl in (
5
)

(215)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Omega_{+}=\Delta\left(\mathbb{K}\sigma_{+}+\mathbb{L}\sigma_{-}\right)\\
\Omega_{-}=\Delta\left(-\mathbb{L}\sigma_{+}-\mathbb{K}\sigma_{-}\right)\end{array}\right.

Identifying
\Omega_{+}
with
\Omega_{1}
,

\sigma_{+}
with
\sigma_{1}
and
\Omega_{-}
with
\Omega_{2}
,
\sigma_{-}
with
\sigma_{2}

we see that (
215
) follows from (
214
) if we choose
A,B,C
such that

(216)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(AB-C^{2})^{-1}B=\Delta\mathbb{K}\\
(AB-C^{2})^{-1}C=\Delta\mathbb{L}\\
A=B\end{array}\right.

In order to solve for
A,B,C
let us note that we have, by calculus and definitions (
6
) and (
7
) that

(217)

\mathbb{L}^{2}-\mathbb{K}^{2}=(-\Delta)^{-1}(\mathbb{I}-\Delta)^{-1}.

From (
216
) we have first

(218)

C^{2}-A^{2}=(\mathbb{I}-\Delta)(-\Delta)^{-1},

and then we obtain the coefficients
A,B,C

(219)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A=B=-\Delta\mathbb{K}(C^{2}-A^{2})=\mathbb{K}(\mathbb{I}-\Delta)=-\frac{1}{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}\\
C=-\Delta\mathbb{L}(C^{2}-A^{2})=\mathbb{L}(\mathbb{I}-\Delta)=\mathbb{I}+\frac{1}{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}\end{array}\right.

Thus, the action for the right-handed system is

(220)

\mathcal{A}_{right}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left[(u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t))_{L^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}\|\Lambda^{-1}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]dt

where
\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
For the left-handed equations we proceed in the same manner, taking the curl in (
8
), and we obtain the coefficients

(221)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A=B=\mathbb{L}(\mathbb{I}-\Delta)=\mathbb{I}+\frac{1}{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}\\
C=-\mathbb{K}(\mathbb{I}-\Delta)=\frac{1}{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}\end{array}\right.

and the action

(222)

\mathcal{A}_{left}=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|u_{1}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\|\Lambda^{-1}(u_{1}-u_{2})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]dt.
