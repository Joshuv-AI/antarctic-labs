Probing the Higgs–top Yukawa interaction in the t ​ t ¯ ​ H t\bar{t}H and t ​ H tH processes using H → γ ​ γ H\rightarrow\gamma\gamma with the ATLAS detector


Abstract

The cross section of the t ​ t ¯ ​ H t\bar{t}H process times the Higgs boson to diphoton decay branching ratio is measured to be 1.46 − 0.35 0.40 ​ ​ fb = 1.46 − 0.32 0.34 ​ (stat.) − 0.13 0.22 ​ (sys.) ​ ​ fb $1.46$\,^{\mathmakebox[r]{$0.40$}}_{\mathmakebox[r]{$-0.35$}}\,$$\penalty\ $\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}$=$1.46$\,^{\mathmakebox[r]{$0.34$}}_{\mathmakebox[r]{$-0.32$}}\,\text{(stat.)}\,^{\mathmakebox[r]{$0.22$}}_{\mathmakebox[r]{$-0.13$}}\,\text{(sys.)}\,$${}\penalty\ $\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}$ , corresponding to 1.13 − 0.28 0.33 ​ $1.13$\,^{\mathmakebox[r]{$0.33$}}_{\mathmakebox[r]{$-0.28$}}\,$$ times the Standard Model prediction. An observed 95% confidence level upper limit on the t ​ H tH production cross section times the Higgs boson to diphoton decay branching ratio is set at 6.2 times the Standard Model prediction, compared to an expected limit of 4.4 times, constituting the most stringent t ​ H tH upper limit achieved in a single measurement to date. The results are combined with 140 fb -1 of proton–proton collision data collected at s \sqrt{s} = 13 TeV in the same production and decay channel, and a C ​ P CP -mixing angle of | α | > 38 ∘ |\alpha|>38^{\circ} is excluded at the 95% confidence level, with a purely C ​ P CP -odd Higgs-top Yukawa coupling excluded at the level of 5.8 standard deviations, providing the most stringent direct constraints on the C ​ P CP structure of the Higgs–top Yukawa interaction to date.

In the SM, the Higgs boson is a scalar particle (

Although the possibility that the observed Higgs particle is a pure pseudoscalar (

Among the Yukawa interactions, the Higgs–top coupling is the largest in the SM. It drives the dominant radiative corrections to the Higgs boson mass underlying the hierarchy problem
[

[

A generic parameterization of the Higgs–top Yukawa interaction can be expressed as
[
18

\displaystyle\cal{L}=

\displaystyle-\frac{m_{t}}{v}\left\{\bar{\psi_{t}}\kappa_{t}\left[\cos(\alpha)+\text{i}\sin(\alpha)\gamma_{\text{5}}\right]\psi_{t}\right\}H,

The Higgs–top Yukawa interaction can be probed both directly and indirectly at the LHC. It can be constrained indirectly through loop-induced processes such as gluon–gluon fusion (ggF) production and Higgs boson decays into photons

The analysis is based on a four-stage event classification enabled by cut-based selections and graph neural network (GNN) classifiers.
Reconstructed object kinematics, charge, and pseudo-continuous
b
-tagging scores define the node input features and angular separations define the edge features, following the procedure described in Ref.
[

The GNN model weights up to the output layer are initialized from a classifier trained to discriminate between six Higgs boson production processes and six top quark production processes
[
87
]

A multiclass GNN classifier is trained to produce five probability discriminants identifying the three Higgs–top associated production modes, other SM Higgs boson production modes (resonant background), and continuum diphoton production (continuum background). All
CP
-even,
CP
-mixed, and
CP
-odd signal samples are used in this training to maintain discrimination performance under all

tight

-even-like and
CP
-odd-like signal topologies. The

and
