Bounds for (strong) Roman k k -dominations
Fahimeh Khosh-Ahang Ghasr

Abstract
. Motivated by resource defense models in networks, such as protecting territories with varying legion strengths, let k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 be an integer. Roman k k -domination and strong Roman k k -domination generalize Roman, double Roman, Italian, and double Italian domination to arbitrary number of legions. The main goal of this note is establishing sharp upper bounds for the Roman and strong Roman k k -domination numbers of connected graphs. These bounds unify and extend prior results for k = 2 k=2 and k = 3 k=3 . We also precisely characterize the graphs achieving these bounds.

We begin by bounds on the strong Roman
k
-domination number in terms of the domination number, leveraging the following key property.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
15
, Lemma 2.4]

Suppose that
f=(V_{0},\dots,V_{k})
is a
\gamma_{k}^{s}(G)
-function. Then we may assume
|V_{i}|=0
for each integer

0<i<k/2
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For a graph
G
, if we set
A_{k}=\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\rfloor
, then

A_{k}\gamma(G)\leq\gamma_{k}^{s}(G)\leq k\gamma(G),

Proof.

If
S
is a dominating set for
G
with
|S|=\gamma(G)
, then assigning
k
to each
x
in
S
, and zero to other vertices yields a
k
-SRDF for

. This shows
\gamma_{k}^{s}(G)\leq k\gamma(G)
. Conversely, if
f=(V_{0},\dots,V_{k})
is a
\gamma_{k}^{s}(G)
-function, then by Lemma
2.1
, we may assume that
|V_{i}|=0
for
0<i<k/2
. Furthermore
\bigcup_{A_{k}\leq i\leq k}V_{i}
is a dominating set for
G
. Therefore

\gamma_{k}^{s}(G)=w(f)=\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}i|V_{i}|=\sum_{A_{k}\leq i\leq k}i|V_{i}|\geq\sum_{A_{k}\leq i\leq k}A_{k}|V_{i}|=A_{k}\sum_{A_{k}\leq i\leq k}|V_{i}|\geq A_{k}\gamma(G).

∎

Setting

in Proposition

Theorem 2.3
.

(1)

For positive integers
c
and

\gamma_{ck}(G)\leq c\gamma_{k}(G),\qquad\gamma_{ck}^{s}(G)\leq c\gamma_{k}^{s}(G).

(2)

For each
\gamma_{k}(G)
-function
f=(V_{0},\dots,V_{k})
and positive integers

\gamma_{ck+1}(G)\leq c\gamma_{k}(G)+\sum_{0\leq i\leq A_{k}}|V_{i}|\leq c\gamma_{k}(G)+|V|,

where
A_{k}=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
.

(3)

For each
\gamma_{k}^{s}(G)
-function
f=(V_{0},\dots,V_{k})
and positive integers
c,k
we have

\gamma_{ck+1}^{s}(G)\leq c\gamma_{k}^{s}(G)+|V|-|V_{0}|\leq(c+1)\gamma_{k}^{s}(G).

(4)

For even integers

\gamma_{k}(G)\leq 3kn/8,\ \ \gamma_{k}^{s}(G)\leq 2kn/5,

and for odd integers

\gamma_{k}(G)\leq(3k+5)n/8,\ \ \gamma_{k}^{s}(G)\leq 2(k+1)n/5.

Proof.

(1) Let

and
f
be a
\gamma_{k}(G)
-function (resp. a
\gamma_{k}^{s}(G)
-function).
Define a map

for each vertex

.
It is straightforward to verify that

is a
(ck)
-RDF (resp. a
(ck)
-SRDF) of

.
Hence the result holds.

(2) Let

,

be a
\gamma_{k}(G)
-function and

, where

for each

,

for each

for all other integers

. When

is a

.

, since

.
Thus

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in N_{G}[u]}f^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(u)+\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u),0\leq f(v)\leq A_{k}}f^{\prime}(v)+\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u),A_{k}+1\leq f(v)\leq k}f^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=cf(u)+1+\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u),0\leq f(v)\leq A_{k}}(cf(v)+1)+\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u),A_{k}+1\leq f(v)\leq k}(cf(v))

\displaystyle\geq c\sum_{v\in N_{G}[u]}f(v)+1

Consequently,

\displaystyle\gamma_{ck+1}(G)

\displaystyle\leq w(f^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i\leq ck+1}i|V^{\prime}_{i}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq A_{k}}((c\ell+1)|V_{\ell}|)+\sum_{A_{k}+1\leq\ell\leq k}(c\ell|V_{\ell}|)

\displaystyle=c\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq k}(\ell|V_{\ell}|)+\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq A_{k}}|V_{\ell}|

\displaystyle=c\gamma_{k}(G)+\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq A_{k}}|V_{\ell}|

(3) Let
c,k\in\mathbb{N}
,

be a

-function and

Let
f^{\prime}=(V^{\prime}_{0},\dots,V^{\prime}_{ck+1})

V^{\prime}_{0}=V_{0}

for each

. We show that

(ck+1)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(u)+\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u),f^{\prime}(v)>(ck+1)/2}f^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u),f^{\prime}(v)>(ck+1)/2}f^{\prime}(v)

(4) The result follows directly from the known upper bounds for

3.
Upper Bounds for (Strong) Roman
k
-Domination Number

We commence by introducing special graph concepts central to the remainder of the paper. The
centers
(or Jordan centers) of a graph are vertices
u

d(u,v)
to any vertex
v
. The
diameter
,
\diam G
, is the longest distance
d(u,v)
for any vertices
u,v
in
G
, and a
maximal path
is a path that cannot be extended by adding any more vertices or edges. Let
r
and
s
be positive integers. A tree with a single center adjacent to
r
vertices is called a
star
, denoted
S_{r}
. A tree with two centers, one adjacent to
r
leaves and the other to
s
leaves, is a
double star
, denoted
S_{r,s}
. For a positive integer
t
a
wounded spider
is a star graph

with at least one and at most
t-1
edges subdivided, while a
healthy spider
has all edges subdivided. In a wounded or healthy spider, the center of
S_{t}
is the
head
, vertices at distance two from the head are
healthy feet
and vertices at distance one from the head are
wounded feet
. The path and cycle graphs with
r
vertices are respectively denoted by
P_{r}
and
C_{r}
.

We now generalize
[
2
, Theorem 13]
,
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[

, Theorem 12]
to arbitrary integer

.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 2,n\geq 3
and

be a tree with
n

\gamma_{k}(T)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cfrac{3k+1}{8}n&\text{ when }k\text{ is odd},\\
\cfrac{3k}{8}n&\text{ when }k\text{ is even};\end{array}\right.\text{and}\ \ \gamma_{k}^{s}(T)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cfrac{3k+1}{8}n&\text{ when }k\text{ is odd},\\
\cfrac{2k}{5}n&\text{ when }k\in\{2,4,6\},\\
\cfrac{3k}{8}n&\text{ when }k\geq 8\text{ and }k\text{ is even}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

First we deal with
\gamma_{k}^{s}(T)
. For

; for other even
