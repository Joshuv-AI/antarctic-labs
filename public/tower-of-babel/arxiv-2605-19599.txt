Shape Design for Degenerate Parabolic Equations with Degenerate Boundaries and Its Application to Boundary Observability This work was carried out with the support of the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant nos. 12131008 and U23B2033, and National Key R&D Program of China under grant no. 2024YFA1013101.
Dong-Hui Yang, Bao-Zhu Guo

Abstract
In this study, we firstly establish the well-posedness of a degenerate parabolic equation under Dirichlet boundary conditions. Following this, we introduce a shape design problem, which acts as a framework for approximating the degenerate parabolic equation through a series of uniformly parabolic equations. Finally, as a tangible application of this shape design approach, we deduce a boundary observability inequality associated with the degenerate parabolic equation. Keywords: Degenerate partial differential equations, shape design. AMS subject classifications (2010): 35J70, 35K65, 49Q10, 93B05.

In this study, we utilize a shape design methodology to explore the boundary controllability of higher-dimensional degenerate parabolic equations. The advantage of this approach lies in its ability to approximate the degenerate parabolic equation through a series of uniformly parabolic equations, along with uniform observability estimates. Subsequently, the observability of the degenerate equation is achieved through this approximation process. Notably, the constants within these estimates must exhibit uniformity. In this paper, we demonstrate the uniformity of these constants, marking another significant contribution of our research. It is pertinent to note that shape design methods share conceptual similarities with cut-off techniques. However, while the cut-off technique addresses the issue within a single evolving system, shape design methods generally encompass a collection of distinct systems. Finally, we emphasize that the method of separation of variables is not applicable to the context examined in this paper.

The problem addressed in this paper is characterized by the following high-dimensional degenerate parabolic equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y-\Div(A\nabla y)=f,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
y=0,&\mbox{on }\partial Q,\\
y(0)=y^{0},&\mbox{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}\times(0,+\infty)
is a convex domain satisfying

\Gamma_{N}^{0}=\partial\Omega\cap({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}\times\{0\})\neq\emptyset
, and
0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is an interior point of

\Gamma_{N}^{0}
. Additionally,
\partial\Omega\in C^{3}
,
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
for
T>0
, and

A=\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{1,\cdots,1}_{N-1},x_{N}^{\alpha}),\quad\text{with }\alpha\in(0,1),

(1.2)

and
f\in L^{2}(Q)
,
y^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. We define

\mathcal{A}y=-\mathrm{div}(A\nabla y),\quad D(\mathcal{A})=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\cap H^{2}(\Omega;w).

(1.3)

To derive a boundary observability inequality for degenerate parabolic equations, it is essential to enhance the regularity of the normal derivative

\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu}
. However, obtaining such regularity on the degenerate part of the boundary presents significant challenges. Fortunately, it is possible to establish the regularity of
\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu}

on portions of the boundary that are away from the degeneracy. Boundary controls acting on these nondegenerate regions are sufficient to drive the system to zero, thereby achieving null controllability.

We highlight that in
[
7
]
, the authors investigated an intriguing two-dimensional degenerate parabolic equation. To derive controllability results, they introduced specific structural assumptions (
[
7
, Hypothesis 2.2, Chapter 2.1.2, p. 12]
). Notably, the coefficient matrix is required to degenerate at the first eigenvalue across the entire boundary. Under this premise, integration by parts remains applicable, which is pivotal in their analysis.

However, this assumption is inadequate for general degenerate parabolic equations, such as (
1.1
). In many scenarios, degeneracy arises solely on a segment of the boundary, rather than the entire boundary. In such cases, integration by parts, exemplified by

(P_{12}z,P_{21}z)_{L^{2}(Q)}
in Section
4
of this paper, generally fails, presenting a fundamental challenge.

One potential strategy to address this issue is to enhance the regularity of weak solutions. Yet, to the best of our knowledge, when degeneracy occurs only on part of the boundary, achieving such a regularity improvement is typically unfeasible. This challenge is also evident in equation (
1.1
) when
\alpha\in[1,2)
. In this scenario, establishing controllability seems unattainable, as the boundary integral

\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\int_{(\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma^{+})\times(0,T)}x_{N}\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\,dS\,dt=0

is ill-defined. Obtaining this term would necessitate higher solution regularity on

\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma^{+}
, which is generally unavailable. The paper
[
7
]
resolves this issue only under very specific conditions. Given that

\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\int_{(\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma^{+})\times(0,T)}x_{N}\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\,dS\,dt\geq 0

is self-evident, a natural approach to overcome this difficulty would be to impose control on the entire boundary. However, boundary control acting on the whole boundary is essentially trivial and thus lacks practical significance.

Another two-dimensional degenerate parabolic equation, set on a rectangular domain, was explored in
[
1
]
, where two sides of the boundary exhibit degeneracy. Similar to
[
7
]
, they constructed appropriate solution spaces to facilitate integration by parts. In contrast, in
[
39
]
, we circumvented this challenge by employing the method of separation of variables.

An alternative and more adaptable approach is the shape design method adopted in this paper. This method eliminates the need for integration by parts on the degenerate part of the boundary. Furthermore, it does not rely on specific geometric assumptions about the domain in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions. Nevertheless, for Neumann boundary conditions, this issue remains unresolved.

Solution spaces

In this section, we present the suitable solution spaces for equation (
1.1
) and subsequently demonstrate the existence of a weak solution to this equation.

Define

H^{1}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\},

where
\nabla u=(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}})

represents the gradient of

u
. The inner product and norm on
H^{1}(\Omega;w)
are defined as

(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}uv\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla v\mathrm{d}x,\quad\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(u,u)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{\frac{1}{2}},

respectively.
Let
\mathcal{D}(\Omega)=C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)=\mbox{the closure of $\mathcal{D}(\Omega)$ in $H^{1}(\Omega;w)$}.

We denote
H^{-1}(\Omega;w)
as the dual space of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.

Define

H^{2}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;w)\colon\mathcal{A}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\},

where
\mathcal{A}
is given by (
1.3
).
The inner product and the norm on
H^{2}(\Omega;w)
are defined as

(u,v)_{H^{2}(\Omega)}=(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{A}u\right)\left(\mathcal{A}v\right)\mathrm{d}x,\quad\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}=(u,u)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}^{\frac{1}{2}},

respectively.

The following Lemma
2.1
is a well-established fact as referenced in
[
19
,
25
]
.

Lemma 2.1

The spaces

(H^{1}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}),\quad(H^{2}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)})

are Hilbert spaces.

The following Hardy inequality will be utilized in the subsequent sections.

Lemma 2.2
(Hardy’s inequality)

For all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, the following inequality holds

\int_{\Omega}x_{N}^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}x_{N}^{\alpha}\left|\partial_{x_{N}}u\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
.

Proof.

By employing a density argument, it suffices to prove the case for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Naturally,
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Let
\beta\in(\alpha,1)
. We compute

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x_{N}^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{M}|u(x^{\prime},x_{N})-u(x^{\prime},0)|^{2}x_{N}^{\alpha-2}\mathrm{d}x_{N}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{M}\left(\int_{0}^{x_{N}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}(x^{\prime},s)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}x_{N}^{\alpha-2}\mathrm{d}x_{N}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{M}\left(\int_{0}^{x_{N}}s^{\beta}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\right|^{2}(x^{\prime},s)\mathrm{d}s\right)\left(\int_{0}^{x_{N}}s^{-\beta}\mathrm{d}s\right)x_{N}^{\alpha-2}\mathrm{d}x_{N}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\beta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{M}x_{N}^{\alpha-\beta-1}\left(\int_{0}^{x_{N}}s^{\beta}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\right|^{2}(x^{\prime},s)\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}x_{N}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\beta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{M}\int_{s}^{M}s^{\beta}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\right|^{2}(x^{\prime},s)x_{N}^{\alpha-\beta-1}\mathrm{d}x_{N}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\beta)(\beta-\alpha)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{M}s^{\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\beta)(\beta-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}x_{N}^{\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

due to
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and

\int_{s}^{M}x_{N}^{\alpha-\beta-1}\mathrm{d}x_{N}=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{s^{\beta-\alpha}}-\frac{1}{M^{\beta-\alpha}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}s^{\alpha-\beta}.

Hence,

\int_{\Omega}x_{N}^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{4}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}x_{N}^{\alpha}|\partial_{x_{N}}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

by setting
\beta=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

Corollary 2.1

The following two statements are valid:

(i) For all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
,

\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}x,

where the constants
C>0
depends only on
\alpha

M
. Hereafter, we will employ the norm

\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
;

(ii) The embedding
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is compact.

Proof.

(i) follows directly from Lemma
2.2
.

(ii) Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
be a bounded sequence, i.e.,
\|u_{n}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq C
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

with a fixed constant
C>0
. Then, there exists a subsequence of
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, still denoted by itself, and
u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
such that

u_{n}\rightarrow u_{0}\mbox{ weakly in }H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w).

Now, we will demonstrate
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)

by considering an abstract subsequence. We assume
u_{0}=0
. If not, we replace
u_{n}
with
u_{n}-u_{0}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. One one side, for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega-\Omega_{\delta}}u_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{\Omega-\Omega_{\delta}}x_{N}^{2-\alpha}x_{N}^{\alpha-2}u_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x<\delta^{2-\alpha}\|u_{n}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\leq C^{2}\delta^{2-\alpha}.\end{split}

Selecting
\widehat{\delta}_{0}\in(0,\delta_{0})
such that for all
\delta\in(0,\widehat{\delta}_{0}]
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\int_{\Omega-\Omega_{\delta}}u_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x<\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}
. On the other hand, we consider the sequence
\{u_{n}|_{\Omega_{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\widehat{\delta}_{0}^{\alpha}\int_{\Omega_{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{\widehat{\delta}}}|\nabla u|^{2}x_{N}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\leq C

we deduce that
\{u_{n}|_{\Omega_{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq H^{1}(\Omega_{\widehat{\delta}_{0}})

By the classical Sobolev compact embedding theorem, there exists a subsequence

. Moreover, there exists

such that for all

, we have

\left\|u_{n_{k}}|_{\Omega_{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}<\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon.

From these two aspects, we obtain the desired result.

2.1
Solution of degenerate elliptic equation, regularity

In this subsection, we demonstrate the existence of a weak solution for the degenerate elliptic equation, a result that is well-established in the literature.

Consider the equation given by

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{A}u=f,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{A}
is given by (
1.3
) and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. We define a weak solution
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
of (
2.1
) with respect to
f
as one that satisfies

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla v\,dx=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx

(2.2)

for all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.

Lemma 2.3

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a unique solution
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\cap H^{2}(\Omega;w)
to the equation (
2.1
).

Proof.

Define the bilinear form

B[u,v]=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla v\,dx,\quad\text{for }u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w).

It is clear that
|B[u,v]|\leq\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}
, and

B[u,u]=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla u\,dx=\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}.

From Corollary
2.1
(i), we have

\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}.

This implies that the functional
f:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded and linear. Consequently, by the Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a unique weak solution to (
2.1
).

Finally, since
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, it follows that
u\in H^{2}(\Omega;w)
. This completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

Remark 2.1

It can be readily verified that
\mathcal{A}:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\to H^{-1}(\Omega;w)
define by (

) is a bounded linear operator. Indeed, for each

and every

in the sense of distributions. Therefore, for all

, we have

\langle\mathcal{A}u,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(u,v)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}

by the density of

in

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w)}&=\sup_{\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq 1}\langle\mathcal{A}u,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\\
&\leq\sup_{\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq 1}(u,v)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}.\end{split}

u\in H^{2}(\Omega;w)\Leftrightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}<\infty,

and

\mathcal{A}u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i},\mbox{ and }\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

From (
2.3
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Phi_{k}\cdot A\nabla\Phi_{l}\mathrm{d}x=\delta_{kl}\lambda_{k}\mbox{ for }k,l\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.4)

We now prove that
\{\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
forms an orthonormal basis of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.

From (
2.4
), it is easy to verify that
\{\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal subset of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.
To show that it is a basis, assume, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists
0\neq u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Phi_{k}\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}x=0\mbox{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, for
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, we can write

u=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}\Phi_{k}\mbox{ where }d_{k}=(u,\Phi_{k})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.5)

Then we have

0=(u,\Phi_{k})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Phi_{k}\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{k}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}u\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{k}d_{k},

which implies
d_{k}=0
. This leads to
u=0
, a contradiction.

Since
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, we have

\nabla u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{i}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\Phi_{k},\mbox{ and }\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{i}^{2},

and

e_{i}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\left(A\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\Phi_{i}\right)\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}u\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{i},

hence,
\nabla u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\nabla\Phi_{i}
. Moreover,
\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}u_{i}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Let
u\in H^{2}(\Omega;w)
. Take
\varphi_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\Phi_{i}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\cap H^{2}(\Omega;w)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then from

\begin{split}(\mathcal{A}u,\mathcal{A}\varphi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A}u)\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}u\mathcal{A}\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\int_{\Omega}u\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\end{split}

and
\|\mathcal{A}\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}
, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}<\infty
.

Let
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}<\infty
. For each
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

\begin{split}(\mathcal{A}u,\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Phi_{i}\cdot A\nabla\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{i}\varphi\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Note that

\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}<\infty\mbox{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

i.e.,
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Hence

(\mathcal{A}u,\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i},\varphi\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This implies that
\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}
and
\mathcal{A}u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i}
.

\square

2.3
Existence of weak solution

In this subsection, we establish the existence of a weak solution to equation (
1.1
) and elucidate the dependency of the constants in the corresponding a priori estimates.

Definition 2.1

We define a weak solution
y
of the equation (
1.1
) with respect to
(y^{0},f)

y\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\mbox{ with }\partial_{t}y\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))

satisfying

(i)

for every
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, and for almost every
t\in[0,T]

\langle\partial_{t}y,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+(y,v)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)};

(ii)

y(0)=y^{0}
.

The following theorem represents one of the primary results of this paper.

Theorem 2.1

Let
\mathcal{A}
be given by (
1.3
). Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
y^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{2}(Q)
. Then the equation (
1.1
)
with respect to
(y^{0},f)
has a unique weak solution

y\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\mbox{ with }\partial_{t}y\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))

satisfies the estimate

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\|y(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))}+\|\partial_{t}y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))}\\
&\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|y^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),\end{split}

(2.6)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,N,T
and
M
and the shape of
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
.

Furthermore, if
y^{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, then the weak solution

y\in L^{2}(0,T;D(\mathcal{A}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\mbox{ with }\partial_{t}y\in L^{2}(Q),

satisfies the estimate

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\|y(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\Gamma^{+},\delta_{0})))}+\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;D(\mathcal{A}))}+\|\partial_{t}y\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\\
&\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|y^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right),\end{split}

(2.7)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,N,T
and
M
, and the shape of
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
.

Proof.

We prove this theorem through the following six steps.

Step 1: Galerkin method.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and define

y^{m}(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{m}y_{n}^{m}(t)\Phi_{n}(x),

where
y_{n}^{m}(t),t\in[0,T],n=1,\cdots,m
, is the solution to the equation

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y_{n}^{m}(t)+\lambda_{n}y_{n}^{m}(t)=f_{n}(t),\mbox{ with }f_{n}(t)=(f,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},n\in\mathbb{N},

(2.8)

with initial data

y_{n}^{m}(0)=(y^{0},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\ n=1,\cdots,m.

(2.9)

It is evident that

y_{n}^{m}(t)=y_{n}^{m}(0)e^{-\lambda_{n}t}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda_{n}(s-t)}f_{n}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\ n=1,\cdots,m.

Note that (
2.8
) is equivalent to the following equality

(\partial_{t}y^{m},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(y^{m},\Phi_{n})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(f,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\ t\in[0,T],n=1,\cdots,m.

(2.10)

Step 2: Energy estimate.

Multiplying (
2.10
) by
y_{n}^{m}(t)
and summing for
n=1,\cdots,m
, we obtain

(\partial_{t}y^{m},y^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(y^{m},y^{m})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(f,y^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mbox{ for a.e.}\ t\in[0,T].

Noting that
(\partial_{t}y^{m},y^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
, and
(f,y^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}
by Corollary
2.1
(i), we get

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,N
and
M
. Integrating over
[0,t]
for any
t\in(0,T]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\|y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))}\leq\|y^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)},\end{split}

(2.11)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,N
and
M
.

For each
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
with
\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq 1
, write
v=v^{1}+v^{2}
, where
v^{1}\in{\rm{span}}\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{m}
and
(v^{2},\Phi_{n})=0
for
k=1,\cdots,m
. Then
\|v^{1}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq 1
, and from (
2.10
) we obtain

\langle\partial_{t}y^{m},v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(\partial_{t}y^{m},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(\partial_{t}y^{m},v^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(f,v^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(y^{m},v^{1})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}.

This implies that

\|\partial_{t}y^{m}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}

by Corollary
2.1
(i). Hence,

\|\partial_{t}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))}\leq\|y^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)},

(2.12)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,T,N
and
M
.

Step 3: Approximation.

From (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), there exists a function
z\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
with
\partial_{t}z\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))
such that

\begin{split}y^{m}&\rightarrow z\mbox{ weak}^{*}\hbox{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\\
y^{m}&\rightarrow z\mbox{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\\
\partial_{t}y^{m}&\rightarrow\partial_{t}z\mbox{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w)).\end{split}

(2.13)

Moreover, we have (
2.6
) (replace
y
by
z
), and
z\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
.

Step 4:
z
is a solution of (
1.1
).

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Choose a function

\psi(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\psi_{n}(t)\Phi_{n},\mbox{ with }\psi_{n}(t)\mbox{ are smooth functions on }[0,T],\ n=1,\cdots,k.

Then for all
m\geq k
, multiplying (
2.10
) by
\psi_{n}(t)
, summing for
n=1,\cdots,k
, and integrating
over
t\in[0,T]
, we get

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}y^{m},\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla y^{m}\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(2.14)

By taking an abstract subsequence and letting

m\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}z,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla z\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(2.15)

by (
2.13
). Noting that the set of functions
\psi
is dense in
L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
, hence (
2.15
) holds for all
\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
, and

\langle\partial_{t}z,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+(z,v)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
and almost every
t\in[0,T]
.

Now, we prove
z(0)=y^{0}
. Indeed, from (
2.15
) and
z\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
, we get

-\iint_{Q}z\partial_{t}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla z\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+(z(0),\psi(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(2.16)

for each
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T];H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
with
\psi(T)=0
. Similarly, from (
2.14
), we obtain

\begin{split}-\iint_{Q}y^{m}\partial_{t}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla y^{m}\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+(y^{m}(0),\psi(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

By taking an abstract subsequence and letting
m\rightarrow\infty
, from (
2.13
) and (
2.9
), we get

-\iint_{Q}z\partial_{t}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla z\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+(y^{0},\psi(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This, combined with (
2.16
) and the arbitrariness of
\psi
, yields
z(0)=y^{0}
.

Step 5: Uniqueness.

It suffices to show that the weak solution of (
1.1
) corresponding to
(y^{0}=0,f=0)
is identically zero. Denote
\xi=y-z
. Then
\xi
is the solution of (
1.1
) with respect to
(0,0)
, and hence from Definition
2.1
(ii) (replace
v
by
\xi
) we get

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\xi\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}=0.

This implies
\xi=0
by integrating over
(0,t)
for each
t\in[0,T]
.

Step 6: Improved regularity.

Multiplying (
2.10
) by
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y_{n}^{m}
, summing for
n=1,\cdots,m
, we get

(\partial_{t}y^{m},\partial_{t}y^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(y^{m},\partial_{t}y^{m})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(f,\partial_{t}y^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mbox{ for almost every }\ t\in[0,T].

Noting that
(y^{m},\partial_{t}y^{m})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mbox{ for a.e.}\ t\in[0,T].\end{split}

Integrating over
(0,t)
for each
t\in[0,T]
, we get

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\|y^{m}(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\\
&\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|y^{m}(0)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\\
&=2\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+2\sum_{n=1}^{m}(y_{n}^{0})^{2}\lambda_{n}\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+2\|y^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}.\end{split}

(2.17)

By Lemma
2.4
. This implies that

\begin{split}\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\|y(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}y\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+2\|y^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\end{split}

(2.18)

by (
2.13
) and
Step 4
.

Now, choose
\zeta=\zeta(x_{N})\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),0\leq\zeta\leq 1

\zeta=0\mbox{ on }\left\{x_{N}<\delta_{0}\right\},\quad\zeta=1\mbox{ on }\{x_{N}\geq 2\delta_{0}\},\quad|\zeta^{\prime}|\leq C\delta_{0}^{-1},\mbox{ and }|\zeta^{\prime\prime}|\leq C\delta_{0}^{-2},

\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}z=\zeta f^{m}-\zeta\partial_{t}y^{m}+y^{m}\mathcal{A}\zeta-2A\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla y^{m},&\mbox{on }\Omega_{\delta_{0}},\\

.
It is evident that this equation is a uniformly elliptic equation.
By the standard regularity for uniformly elliptic equations

[
14
, Theorem 1, p. 327; Theorem 4, p. 334]
, from (

),
we get, for almost every

, that

\begin{split}\|z\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta_{0}})}\leq C\left(\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{t}y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y^{m}(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right),\end{split}

where the constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha
and the shape of
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
. Hence, from (

\|y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\Gamma^{+},\delta_{0})))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|y^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right).

From (
2.13
) we get

\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\Gamma^{+},\delta_{0}))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|y^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right),

(2.19)

where the constant
C>0

,

and the shape of
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
.
Finally, from (

), we have

\|\mathcal{A}y\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|\partial_{t}y\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq 3\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+2\|y^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}.

) yields (
2.7
), where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,N,T
and
M
and the shape of
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
. We thus complete the proof of the theorem.

3
Shape design

In this section, we demonstrate that a degenerate parabolic equation can be approximated by a sequence of uniformly parabolic equations through a shape design approach.

Let
Q_{\delta}=\Omega_{\delta}\times(0,T)
, and
w_{\delta}=w|_{\Omega_{\delta}},A_{\delta}=A|_{\Omega_{\delta}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}=\mathcal{A}|_{\Omega_{\delta}}
. We consider the following equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y_{\delta}-\mathcal{A}_{\delta}y_{\delta}=f_{\delta},&\mbox{in }Q_{\delta},\\
y_{\delta}=0,&\mbox{on }\partial Q_{\delta},\\
y_{\delta}(0)=y_{\delta}^{0},&\mbox{in }\Omega_{\delta},\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
y_{\delta}^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})
and
f_{\delta}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})
.

Similar to Section
2
, we can derive the following solution spaces and results. We define

H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})\colon\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\nabla u\cdot A_{\delta}\nabla u\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\}.

The inner product on
H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})
is given by

(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})}=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}uv\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\nabla u\cdot A_{\delta}\nabla v\mathrm{d}x,

and the norm is

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})}=\left(\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\nabla u\cdot A_{\delta}\nabla u\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We define

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})=\text{the closure of }\mathcal{D}(\Omega_{\delta})\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})

We denote by
H^{-1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})
the dual space of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})

with pivot space
L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})
, which is a subspace of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega_{\delta})
. It is well-known that

(H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta}),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})})
is a Hilbert space and

(H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})})

is a Banach space (
[
19
,
25
]
).
Now, we define

H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})\colon\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(\mathcal{A}_{\delta}u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\}.

Its inner product is defined by

(u,v)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}=(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A}_{\delta}u)(\mathcal{A}_{\delta}v)\mathrm{d}x,

and its norm is defined as

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}=\left(\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\nabla u\cdot A_{\delta}\nabla u\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(\mathcal{A}_{\delta}u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is also well-known that
(H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta}),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})})
is a Hilbert space and
(H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})})
is a Banach space (
[
19
,
25
]
).

We denote

D(\mathcal{A}_{\delta})=H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta}).

We note that
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})
,
H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})=H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})
, and
H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})=H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})
, as a consequence of the standard improved regularity for uniformly elliptic equations. However, in general,
H^{2}(\Omega;w)\neq H^{2}(\Omega)
.

Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, as well as Remark
3.1
, are equivalent to Lemma
2.2
and Corollary

, respectively.

Lemma 3.1

Let
N\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})
, the following inequality holds:

\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}x_{N}^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}x_{N}^{\alpha}(\partial_{x_{N}}u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

where the constant

depends only on
\alpha
.

Remark 3.1

From Lemma

, we have

\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}x,

(3.2)

for all

, where the constant

depends only on
\alpha
and

. The second inequality is the Poincaré inequality.
In particular, the norm

\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})}=\left(\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\nabla u\cdot A_{\delta}\nabla u\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is an equivalent norm in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})
. Hereafter, we use (

) to define the norm of

Lemma 3.2

The embedding
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta};w_{\delta})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})
is compact.

We are now ready to define a weak solution to equation (

Definition 3.1

We call

The following theorem can be proved using the same argument as Theorem

.
We note that the constants in the estimates do not depend on the parameter

,

We now introduce a shape design framework for the degenerate parabolic equation (

Definition 3.2

For each

1.1
.
For all

4
Carleman estimates

In this section, we establish a Carleman estimate for the approximating uniformly parabolic equations. Although this type of estimate is widely recognized, it is crucial to highlight that the constants involved must remain independent of the parameter

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
. Hence, we present a detailed proof.

We analyze the following backward equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y_{\delta}+\Div(A\nabla y_{\delta})=f_{\delta},&\mbox{in }Q_{\delta},\\
y_{\delta}=0,&\mbox{on }\partial Q_{\delta},\\
y_{\delta}(T)=y_{\delta}^{T},&\mbox{in }\Omega_{\delta},\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
y_{\delta}^{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})
and
f_{\delta}\in L^{2}(Q_{\delta})
.

We denote

\eta=x_{N}^{2-\alpha},

(4.2)

and

\Theta=\frac{1}{[t(T-t)]^{4}},\quad\xi=\Theta(\gamma-\eta),

where the constant
\gamma=|\eta|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1
. Then, we have

\nabla\eta=(2-\alpha)x_{N}^{1-\alpha}e_{N},\quad\nabla\xi=-\Theta\nabla\eta=-(2-\alpha)\Theta x_{N}^{1-\alpha}e_{N},

(4.3)

and

\xi_{t}=\Theta^{\prime}(\gamma-\eta),\quad\xi_{tt}=\Theta^{\prime\prime}(\gamma-\eta),\quad\nabla\xi_{t}=-(2-\alpha)\Theta^{\prime}x_{N}^{1-\alpha}e_{N},

(4.4)

and

\begin{split}|\Theta^{\prime}|\leq C\Theta^{\frac{5}{4}},\quad|\Theta^{\prime\prime}|\leq C\Theta^{\frac{3}{2}},\quad|\Theta\Theta^{\prime}|\leq C\Theta^{\frac{9}{4}},\end{split}

(4.5)

where the constants
C>0
depends only on
T
.

Let us make the substitution

z=e^{-s\xi}y_{\delta}\ \Leftrightarrow\ y_{\delta}=e^{s\xi}z.

Subsequently, we obtain

z(t)=\nabla z(t)=0\mbox{ at }t=0,T,

(4.6)

and

z=\partial_{t}z=0\mbox{ on }\partial Q_{\delta}.

(4.7)

Moreover, equation (
4.1
) is equivalent to

e^{-s\xi}f_{\delta}=P_{1}z+P_{2}z,

(4.8)

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle P_{1}z=\sum_{i=1}^{3}P_{1i}z=z_{t}+2sA\nabla z\cdot\nabla\xi+sz\Div(A\nabla\xi),\cr\vskip 0.0pt\cr\displaystyle P_{2}z=\sum_{i=1}^{3}P_{2i}z=\Div(A\nabla z)+sz\xi_{t}+s^{2}zA\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\xi.\end{array}\right.

(4.9)

4.1
Computation

Given that equation (
4.1
) exhibits uniform parabolicity for every

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, the subsequent applications of integration by parts remain valid. Nevertheless, when

\delta=0
, the integration by parts operation concerning
(P_{12}z+P_{13}z,\,P_{21}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}
in (
4.9
)
is no longer tenable, particularly in the vicinity of the set
\partial\Gamma_{N}^{0}
. For the sake of brevity in the subsequent discussion, we employ the notations
\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}
and
\iint_{Q_{\delta}}
; in other instances, we omit the subscript

\delta
. For instance, we use
w
to denote
w_{\delta}
, and the same applies to other variables.

a) Compute
(P_{11}z,P_{2}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}
.

Indeed, leveraging (
4.4
), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
), we derive

\begin{split}(P_{11}z,P_{2}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}&=-\frac{s}{2}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}z^{2}\xi_{tt}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-s^{2}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}z^{2}A\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\xi_{t}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=-\frac{s}{2}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}z^{2}\Theta^{\prime\prime}(\gamma-\eta)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-(2-\alpha)^{2}s^{2}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta\Theta^{\prime}z^{2}x_{N}^{2-\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

b) Compute
(P_{12}z+P_{13}z,P_{21}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}
.

Indeed, based on (
4.7
), we obtain

\begin{split}&(P_{12}z+P_{13}z,P_{21}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}\\
&=2s\iint_{\partial Q_{\delta}}(A\nabla z\cdot\nu)(A\nabla z\cdot\nabla\xi)\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t-s\iint_{\partial Q_{\delta}}(A\nabla z\cdot\nabla z)(A\nabla\xi\cdot\nu)\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt-2s\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}A_{i}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial A_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial x_{j}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+s\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}A_{j}\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial x_{j}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt-2s\iint_{Q_{\delta}}A\nabla z\cdot\left[D^{2}\xi(A\nabla z)\right]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-s\iint_{Q_{\delta}}zA\nabla z\cdot\nabla[\Div(A\nabla\xi)]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

and subsequently,

\begin{split}(P_{12}z+P_{13}z,P_{21}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}&=(2-\alpha)s\iint_{\partial Q_{\delta}}\Theta x_{N}^{1-\alpha}|\nabla z|^{2}(A\nu\cdot\nu)(-A\nu\cdot e_{N})\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt+(2-\alpha)^{2}s\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta x_{N}^{\alpha}(\partial_{x_{N}}z)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

c) Compute
(P_{12}z+P_{13}z,P_{22}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}
.

Indeed, drawing upon equations (
4.3
), (
4.4
), and (
4.7
), we obtain

\begin{split}(P_{12}z+P_{13}z,P_{22}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}&=-s^{2}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}z^{2}A\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\xi_{t}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=-(2-\alpha)^{2}s^{2}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta\Theta^{\prime}x_{N}^{2-\alpha}z^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

d) Compute
(P_{12}z+P_{13}z,P_{23}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}
.

Indeed, based on equations (
4.3
) and (
4.7
), we arrive at

\begin{split}(P_{12}z+P_{13}z,P_{23}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}&=-s^{3}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}z^{2}A\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla(A\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\xi)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=(2-\alpha)^{4}s^{3}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta^{3}z^{2}x_{N}^{2-\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

4.2
Estimation

Given the definition
\gamma=|\eta|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1

\begin{split}-\frac{s}{2}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}z^{2}\Theta^{\prime\prime}(\gamma-\eta)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t&\geq-Cs\gamma\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta^{\frac{3}{2}}z^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\

and

where the constants

depends exclusively on

.

, depending only on

\alpha
and

\begin{split}(P_{1}z,P_{2}z)_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}&\geq(2-\alpha)s\iint_{\partial Q_{\delta}}\Theta x_{N}^{1-\alpha}|\nabla z|^{2}(A\nu\cdot\nu)(-A\nu\cdot e_{N})\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt+(2-\alpha)^{2}s\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta x_{N}^{\alpha}(\partial_{x_{N}}z)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+(2-\alpha)^{4}s^{3}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta^{3}z^{2}x_{N}^{2-\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

\alpha
and

\begin{split}&s\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta x_{N}^{\alpha}(\partial_{x_{N}}z)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+s^{3}\iint_{Q_{\delta}}\Theta^{3}z^{2}x_{N}^{2-\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\|e^{-s\xi}f_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\delta})}^{2}+Cs\iint_{\Gamma^{+}\times(0,T)}\Theta\left|\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
and
T
and
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
.
It is worth noting that there exist positive constants

, depending only on
\alpha
and

and
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
, such that (refer to Notation

C\left|\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\leq\left|\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu_{A}}\right|^{2}\leq C\left|\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mbox{ on }\Gamma^{+}\times(0,T),\mbox{ with }\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu_{A}}=A\nabla z\cdot\nu,

and

indeed depends only on
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
.
We then arrive at the following Proposition
1
.

Proposition 1

Theorem 5.1

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
y^{T}\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
. Consider

y
as the backward weak solution of (
1.1
) corresponding to the initial-boundary conditions

(y^{0},f=0)
. In other words,
y
satisfies

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y-\Div(A\nabla y)=f,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
y=0,&\mbox{on }\partial Q,\\
y(0)=y^{0},&\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(5.3)

Then, the following inequality holds:

\int_{\Omega}y^{2}(0)\mathrm{d}x\leq C\iint_{\Gamma^{+}\times(0,T)}\left|\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t,

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,T
and
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
.

Proof.
Consider
y^{T}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
. By the properties of this space, there exists a sequence

\{y_{n}^{T}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
such that
y_{n}^{T}\rightarrow y^{T}
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)

n\rightarrow\infty
. Applying Theorem
5.1
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}y_{n}^{2}(0)\mathrm{d}x\leq C\iint_{\Gamma^{+}\times(0,T)}\left|\frac{\partial y_{n}}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t.

Furthermore, leveraging equation (
2.7
), we derive the following inequalities

\iint_{\Gamma^{+}\times(0,T)}\left|\frac{\partial(y_{n}-y)}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t\leq C\|y_{n}-y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\Gamma^{+},\delta_{0})))}^{2}\leq C\|y_{n}^{0}-y^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}

and

\|y_{n}(0)-y(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|y_{n}^{0}-y^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)},

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,T
and
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
. Consequently, we arrive at

\int_{\Omega}y^{2}(0)\mathrm{d}x\leq C\iint_{\Gamma^{+}\times(0,T)}\left|\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t,

(5.4)

with the constant
C>0
again depending only on
\alpha,T
and
\Omega_{\delta_{0}}
.

Next, let
y^{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. According to (
2.6
), it follows that
y(\frac{T}{2})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.
Utilizing (
5.4

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}y^{2}(0)\mathrm{d}x\leq C\iint_{\Gamma^{+}\times(0,\frac{T}{2})}\left|\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t\leq C\iint_{\Gamma^{+}\times(0,T)}\left|\frac{\partial y}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}
