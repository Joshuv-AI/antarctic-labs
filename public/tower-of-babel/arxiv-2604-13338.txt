The Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin Conditions and the Search for Extreme Behavior in 3D Navier-Stokes Flows
Elkin Ramírez, Bartosz Protas

Abstract
In this investigation, we conduct a systematic computational search for potential singularities in 3D Navier-Stokes flows on a periodic domain Ω \Omega based on the Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin conditions. They assert that for a solution 𝐮 ⁡ ( t ) {\mathbf{u}}(t) of the Navier-Stokes system to be regular on an interval [ 0 , T ] [0,T] , the integral ∫ 0 T ‖ 𝐮 ⁡ ( t ) ‖ L q p ​ 𝑑 t \int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{p}\,dt , where 2 / p + 3 / q = 1 , q > 3 2/p+3/q=1,\;q>3 , and the expression sup t ∈ [ 0 , T ] ‖ 𝐮 ⁡ ( t ) ‖ L 3 \sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{3}} must be bounded. Flows which might become singular and violate these conditions are sought by solving a family of variational PDE optimization problems where we identify initial conditions 𝐮 0 {\mathbf{u}}_{0} with the corresponding flows 𝐮 ⁡ ( t ) {\mathbf{u}}(t) locally maximizing the integral ∫ 0 T ‖ 𝐮 ⁡ ( t ) ‖ L q p ​ 𝑑 t \int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{p}\,dt for a range of different values of q q and p p or the norm ‖ 𝐮 ⁡ ( T ) ‖ L 3 \|{\mathbf{u}}(T)\|_{L^{3}} for different time windows T T and increasing sizes ‖ 𝐮 0 ‖ L q \|{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|_{L^{q}} of the initial data. We consider two formulations where these expressions are maximized over appropriate Lebesgue spaces L q ​ ( Ω ) L^{q}(\Omega) or the largest Hilbert-Sobolev spaces H s ​ ( Ω ) H^{s}(\Omega) embedded in them. The lack of Hilbert-space structure in the first case necessitates development of a novel computational approach to solve the problem. While no evidence of unbounded growth of the quantities of interest, and hence also for singularity formation, was detected, we were able to quantify how “close” the flows realizing such worst-case scenarios come to forming a singularity. A comparison of these results with estimates on the rate of growth of the norms ‖ 𝐮 ⁡ ( t ) ‖ L q ||{\mathbf{u}}(t)||_{L^{q}} and of the enstrophy ℰ ⁡ ( t ) {\mathcal{E}}(t) indicates that the extreme flows do enter a regime where these quantities are amplified at a rate consistent with singularity formation in finite time, but this growth is not sustained long enough for singularities to form. This transient nonlinear amplification is also found to increase with q q .

In the present investigation we follow a fundamentally different
approach, initiated in the seminar study
Lu and Doering 2008
, where extreme,
possibly singular flows are sought methodically as solutions of
suitable variational optimization problems. More specifically, this
allows one to find initial data such that the resulting flows maximize
the growth of a chosen quantity measuring the regularity of the
solution related to one of the conditional regularity results and whose
unbounded growth in finite time would signal singularity formation. Our
earlier investigations based on this idea focused on maximizing the
quantities involved in the enstrophy condition and in one member of the
Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin family of conditional regularity results

Kang et al. 2020
;
Kang and Protas 2022
. These studies provided no
evidence for an unbounded growth of these quantities which, however,
did show significant transient growth exhibiting certain universal
behavior. In the present study we extend the results from

Kang and Protas 2022
by considering a wide range of the
Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin conditions while also overcoming some
technical limitations that were encountered in
Kang and Protas 2022

This optimization-based framework has also proven useful in the study
of globally well-posed systems, such as the one-dimensional (1D)
viscous Burgers and the 2D Navier-Stokes systems

;
Ayala and Protas 2014
;
Ayala et al. 2018
;
Yun and Protas 2018

inspired efforts to find improved rigorous upper bounds on the growth
of enstrophy and the gap was closed only recently

Albritton and
Nitti 2023

offered in the review paper
Protas 2022

The main contribution of the present study is to construct, by
numerically solving suitable optimization problems, families of initial
conditions such that the resulting Navier-Stokes flows locally maximize

3
Conditional Regularity Results

Conditional regularity results are statements about Leray-Hopf weak
solutions
{\mathbf{u}}(t)
of the Navier-Stokes system expressing verifiable
conditions that need to be fulfilled by these solutions if they are to
also satisfy system (
1
) in the classical (pointwise) sense

Robinson
et al. 2016
. While in general it is not known if
they hold, they represent necessary and sufficient conditions for

{\mathbf{u}}(t)
to be smooth on the time interval of interest
[0,T]
. Here we
consider two such conditions, namely, the enstrophy and the family of
the Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin conditions together with associated
inequalities for the rate of growth of the quantities they involve.

3.1
The Enstrophy Condition

Leray-Hopf weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes system (
1
)
defined in the interval
[0,T]
satisfy the inequality,
cf. (
7
),

\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{E}}({\mathbf{u}}(t))\,dt<\infty.{}

(8)

In order for such solutions to be strong (classical), they must also
satisfy the sufficient and necessary condition
Foias and Temam 1989

\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathcal{E}}({\mathbf{u}}(t))<\infty.

(9)

Thus, the boundedness of enstrophy guarantees that the
Navier-Stokes flow remains smooth and should a singularity form at

T^{*}
, then necessarily

\lim_{t\rightarrow{T^{*}}^{-}}{\mathcal{E}}({\mathbf{u}}(t))=\infty.

(10)

3.2
Bounds on the Rate of Growth of the Enstrophy

Whether or nor blow-up (
10
) can occur is dictated by
the maximum sustained rate at which enstrophy can be amplified in
classical solutions of the Navier-Stokes system. This problem was
considered in
Lu and Doering 2008
, where the following upper bound was obtained

\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}\leq\frac{27}{8\pi^{4}\nu^{3}}{\mathcal{E}}^{3}.

(11)

An analogous estimate involving a norm of the symmetric part of the
velocity gradient
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathbf{u}}
was obtained in
Miller 2020
.
The numerical evidence provided in
Lu and Doering 2008
;
Ayala and Protas 2017
suggests that
estimate (
11
) is in fact sharp, in the sense
that there exist families of incompressible vector fields
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}}
with
the enstrophy
{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}})={\mathcal{E}}_{0}
such that
(d/dt){\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}})={\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{3})
as
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\rightarrow\infty
. Direct integration of
(
11
) leads to

{\mathcal{E}}({\mathbf{u}}(t))\leq\frac{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{27}{4\pi^{4}\nu^{3}}{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{2}\,t}},

(12)

where the right-hand side (RHS) becomes unbounded as
t\rightarrow T^{*}=4\pi^{4}\nu^{3}/(27{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{2})
. Thus, together with
(
9
), relation (
12
) is equivalent
to a local existence result.

In order for a singularity to form in finite time, the enstrophy must
be amplified at a sustained rate that is sufficiently rapid. Assuming
that

\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}<C{\mathcal{E}}^{\alpha},\qquad C>0,\quad 0<\alpha\leq 3

(13)

(hereafter
C
will denote a generic positive constant whose
numerical value may change from instance to instance) and using
Grönwall’s lemma together with the energy equality
(
7
) in an integrated form

\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{E}}(s)\,ds={\frac{1}{2\nu}\left[{\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{u}}(t))\right]}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}{\mathcal{K}}_{0},

(14)

yields the upper bound

{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\,\exp\left[\frac{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}{2\nu}\right]
valid when

\alpha\in(0,2]
in (
13
). Therefore, in order for a
singularity to form the enstrophy of the flow must be amplified at a
sustained rate proportional to
{\mathcal{E}}^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(2,3]
.
This observation offers a convenient way to assess how close various
extreme flows come to producing a singularity. We add that when the
states
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}}
saturating the upper bound in (
11
)
are used as the initial data
{\mathbf{u}}_{0}
in (
1
), the growth of
enstrophy is immediately depleted in the resulting Navier-Stokes flows

Ayala and Protas 2017
.

3.3
Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin Conditions

Another well-known conditional regularity result is given by the family
of the Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin (LPS) conditions

Kiselev and Ladyzhenskaya 1957
;
Prodi 1959
;
Serrin 1962
. They assert that
{\mathbf{u}}(t)
is a
smooth classical solution of the Navier-Stokes system (
1
) on
the time interval
[0,T]
if and only if

\displaystyle{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{p}([0,T];L^{q}(\Omega)),\qquad 2/p+3/q\leq 1,\quad q>3,

(15)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{3}(\Omega)),

(16)

with the latter condition corresponding to the limiting case with
q=3

Escauriaza et al. 2003
. Another blow-up criterion related to
(
16
) was recently established in
Tao 2020
. As regards
the values of the indices
p
and
q
in (
15
), we are
interested in the borderline (equality) case with
p=2q/(q-3)
.
This condition implies that if a singularity is formed in a classical
solution
{\mathbf{u}}(t)
of the Navier-Stokes system (
1
) at the time

T^{*}\in(0,\infty)
, then necessarily

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\int_{0}^{t}\|{\mathbf{u}}(\tau)\|_{L^{q}}^{p}\,d\tau=\infty,\qquad 2/p+3/q=1,\quad q>3.

(17)

Leray-Hopf weak solutions are known to satisfy the following estimates

Constantin 1991
;
Kang and Protas 2022

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{4q}{3(q-2)}}\,dt\leq C{\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{\frac{2q}{3(q-2)}},

\displaystyle 2\leq q\leq 6,

(18a)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q}{q-3}}\,dt\leq C{\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{3},

\displaystyle q>6.

(18b)

with exponents in the integrant expressions smaller than
p=2q/(q-3)
in (
15
). Thus, if a singularity is to form in a
Navier-Stokes flow, the norm
\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}
must grow at
such a rate that the integral in (
17
) becomes unbounded
as
t\rightarrow T^{*}
while the integrals in
(
18a
)–(
18b
) remain finite. This issue is considered
in more detail in the subsection below.

A preliminary attempt to search for singular Navier-Stokes flows that
would realize (
17
) based on an optimization formulation
was conducted in
Kang and Protas 2022
where the values
q=4
and
p=8
were used. The goal of the present investigation is to complete
this program by considering a wider range of parameters
q
and
p

in addition to overcoming certain technical limitations encountered in

Kang and Protas 2022
.

3.4
A Priori Bounds on
\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}

Here we analyze conditions on the rate of growth of

\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}
necessary for blow-up (
17
).
A well-known upper bound on the rate of growth of this quantity is

Sadowski 2014

\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{3(q-1)}{q-3}}{=C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{p+1}},\quad q>3,

(19)

where
C=C(q)>0
. By integrating this inequality with respect
to time, we obtain

\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq\frac{1}{\left(\|{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|_{L^{q}}^{-p}-p\,C\,t\right)^{1/p}}

(20)

which becomes unbounded as
t\rightarrow T^{*}=1/(p\,C\,\|{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|_{L^{q}}^{p})
. Together with
(
15
), this relation thus represents a statement of a local
existence result for smooth solutions, akin to (
12
). To
the best of our knowledge, estimates analogous to (
19
)
are not available in the limiting case with
q=3
where the “best”
upper bound is

et al. 2016

\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\leq C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{9}}^{3},

(21)

which however does not have the form
\frac{d}{dt}Y\leq CY^{\alpha}
.
The question of the sharpness of bounds (
19
) was
considered in
Bleitner and
Protas 2026
where numerical evidence is
provided suggesting these estimates are indeed sharp (up to
prefactors) for
q>3
.

Estimates (
18a
)–(
18b
) can be used in conjunction
with Grönwall’s lemma to determine the largest rate of growth
of the norm
\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}
ensuring there is no blow-up in
finite time, which gives

\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq\begin{cases}C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{7q-6}{3(q-2)}},\qquad&2\leq q\leq 6,\\
\\
C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q-3}{q-3}},&q>6.\\
\end{cases}

(22)

To show this, let us first consider the case with
2\leq q\leq 6
, where

\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{7q-6}{3(q-2)}}=C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{4q}{3(q-2)}}.

By Grönwall’s lemma, we can then bound
\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}
on any interval
[0,T]
,
T>0,
by

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\,\mbox{exp}\left(C\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{4q}{3(q-2)}}dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\mbox{exp}\left(C{\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{\frac{2q}{3(q-2)}}\right)\ <\infty.

Similarly, for
q>6,
we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q-3}{q-3}}=C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q}{q-3}}.

Again, by Grönwall’s lemma, we can obtain a bound on
\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}

on any interval
[0,T]

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\,\mbox{exp}\left(C\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q}{q-3}}dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\mbox{exp}\left(C{\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{3}\right)\ <\infty.

On the other hand, assuming there is a blow-up at

, we
can suppose that
\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}=C/(T^{*}-t)^{\alpha}
,

\alpha>0
. Plugging this ansatz in expression (

), we
then have that

and

\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}=\frac{C}{(T^{*}-t)^{1+\alpha}}=C\left[\frac{1}{(T^{*}-t)^{\alpha}}\right]^{\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}}=C\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{\beta},

(23)

where

, cf. (

We therefore conclude that, somewhat counter-intuitively,
for a blow-up to occur in Navier-Stokes flows,

\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}
cannot
be arbitrarily large. In fact, it must be at most proportional to

\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{3(q-1)/(q-3)}

as otherwise the integrand expression in (
17
) would
blow-up before the integral becomes unbounded. In other words, if
blow-up is to occur in a Navier-Stokes flow where the norm

\|{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|_{L^{q}}
grows following relation (
23
),
then the exponent
\beta
must lie within a
q
-dependent range with
the bottom bracket given by the exponents in (
22
)
and the upper bracket given by the exponent in (
19
). These
relations are illustrated in Figure

. Needless
to say, for blow-up to occur, the growth rate in (
23

be sustained sufficiently long and this time grows without bound as the
exponent
\beta
drops towards the bottom bracket in
(
22

how “close” the extreme flows constructed in the present study come
to becoming singular.

4
Optimization Formulations

In this section we formulate a number of optimization problems we
consider in this study. They are motivated by the conditional
regularity results discussed in Section
3
and aim
at finding optimal initial conditions
{\mathbf{u}}_{0}
that will produce
“extreme” flows, in the sense of maximizing the different regularity
indicators involved in the conditional regularity results. The goal is
to see whether a singularity might occur in such flows. With the domain

\Omega
and the viscosity
\nu
fixed, each problem is defined by
three parameters, namely, the function space where the initial data is
sought, the size of the optimal initial data and the length of the time
window. The perspective adopted here is that for a given size of the
initial data we begin with a time window
[0,T]
that is assumed
sufficiently short such that classical solutions exist. The
maximization is then performed on time windows with gradually
increasing length
T
to see whether the quantities controlling the
regularity of the solution might reveal an unbounded growth that would
signal approach to a singularity, cf. (
10
) and
(
17
), as
T
becomes longer than the interval of local
existence. Thus, one has to work with smooth classical solutions only
which facilitates numerical computations (cf. Appendix

9
).

To provide context for the present investigation, we first recall the
optimization problem motivated by the enstrophy condition
(
9

Kang et al. 2020

\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0;{\mathcal{E}}_{0},T}

fixed enstrophy

enstrophy at prescribed times

Given

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{Q}}}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,\colon\,\boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot{\mathbf{u}}_{0}=0,\;{\mathcal{E}}({\mathbf{u}}_{0})={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\right\}.

here

5
Solution Approach

First, we describe the Riemannian gradient method used to solve
constrained optimization problems. A key element of this approach is
evaluation of the gradient of the objective functional
(
25
)–(
26
) with respect to the control variable
which is the initial condition
{\mathbf{u}}_{0}
in (
1
). This needs to
be done in a suitable topology and is described below. Finally, we
introduce additional tools needed to implement the Riemannian gradient
approach.

We follow the “optimize-then-discretize” strategy where the
optimization approach is first formulated in the infinite-dimensional
(continuous) setting and only then the resulting equations and
expressions are discretized for the purpose of numerical solution

Gunzburger 2003
. Some details of the numerical implementation are presented
in Appendix
9
. While the general approach is essentially
the same as used in
Kang and Protas 2022
, the main novelty is that
here we also compute Lebesgue gradients, i.e, gradients of the
objective functional (
25
)–(
26
) defined in Lebesgue
spaces
L^{q}(\Omega)
,
q\geq 3
, without Hilbert structure, which are
needed to solve Problems
2
and
4
. As will be
evident below, this is technically more complicated than in Hilbert
spaces
Golomb and Tampia 1972
;
Protas 2008
. To focus attention, we first describe
solution of Problems
1
and
2

mention adaptations needed to solve Problems
3
and

4
which have a similar structure.

Riemannian Gradient Method

Problem

5.2.2
Gradient in the Lebesgue Space
L^{q}

Determination of the gradient in the Lebesgue space
L^{q}(\Omega)
,
q\geq 3
, needed in Problems
2
and
4
is more
involved as we do not have an inner product in this space and hence
there is no Riesz identity. We thus need to invoke the concept of a

metric
gradient
Golomb and Tampia 1972
, which is a generalization of the
notion of the gradient to normed spaces. It relies on the observation
that the gradient is the element maximizing the directional derivative
of the objective functional under certain constraints. We can thus
define the gradient of the objective functional (
25
)
variationally as a solution of the following optimization subproblem
subject to the suitable constraints
Neuberger 2010
;
Protas 2008

\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L_{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}=\mathop{\arg\max}_{\|\boldsymbol{\Theta}\|_{L^{q}}=1,\,\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Theta}=0}\left[(\Phi_{T}^{q})^{\prime}({\mathbf{u}}_{0};{\mathbf{u}}_{0}^{\prime})=\left\langle\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{2}}\Phi^{q}_{T},\boldsymbol{\Theta}\right\rangle_{(L^{q})^{*}\times L^{q}}\right].

(52)

Optimization problem (
52
) can be converted to an
unconstrained form by introducing the Lagrange multipliers
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}

and
\eta\in L^{q}(\Omega)
associated with each of these two constraints

\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\mu\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\eta\in L^{q}(\Omega)}\mathop{\arg\max}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in L^{q}(\Omega)}\left[\left\langle\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{2}}\Phi^{q}_{T},\boldsymbol{\Theta}\right\rangle_{(L^{q})^{*}\times L^{q}}+\right.\\
\left.+\frac{\mu}{q}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\Theta}\|^{q}_{L^{q}}-1\right)+\int_{\Omega}\eta\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Theta}\right)\,d{\mathbf{x}}\right],

(53)

where the expression in the bracket can be interpreted as the Lagrangian
corresponding to the right-hand side of (
52
). After
performing integration by parts, we arrive at

\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}={\mathop{\arg\min}_{\mu\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\eta\in L^{q}(\Omega)}}\mathop{\arg\max}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in L^{q}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left({\mathbf{u}}^{*}{(0)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Theta}+\frac{\mu}{q}|\boldsymbol{\Theta}|^{q}-\frac{\mu}{q|\Omega|}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\eta\right)\,d{\mathbf{x}}.

(54)

Now, using the optimality conditions requiring the vanishing of the
Gâteaux differentials of the integral expression in
(
54
) with respect to
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
,
\mu

and
\eta
, we obtain the following expressions

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left({\mathbf{u}}^{*}{(0)}+\mu\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}|^{q-2}-\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\eta\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{\prime}\,d{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=0,\;\qquad\forall\;\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{\prime}\in L^{q}(\Omega){,}

(55a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\boldsymbol{\Theta}|^{q}\,d{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=1,\qquad\forall\;\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in L^{q}(\Omega){,}

(55b)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Theta}\right)\,\eta\,d{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\;\eta\in L^{q}(\Omega).

(55c)

Conditions (
55b
) and (
55c
) trivially say
that solutions of the optimization problem (
52
) have a
unit
L^{q}(\Omega)
norm and are divergence-free. Since

\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{\prime}
is arbitrary, condition (
55a
) is
equivalent to the following relation

\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}|^{q-2}=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}{(0)}+\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\eta\right),\quad{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega.

(56)

As observed in
Protas 2008
, the RHS in expression
(
56
) represents the Helmholtz-Weyl decomposition of a
vector field in
L^{q}(\Omega)

Robinson
et al. 2016
, where
such a vector field is written as the sum of a divergence-free
vector field and the gradient of a certain scalar potential. Then,
applying the divergence operator to (

) and noticing
that both
{\mathbf{u}}^{*}(0)
and
\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi_{T}^{q}
are divergence-free,
we obtain an elliptic boundary-value problem which can
be used to determine
\eta

\Delta\eta=\mu\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi_{T}^{q}|^{q-2}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi_{T}^{q},\quad{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega

(57)

Therefore, to determine the metric gradient in the space

L^{q}(\Omega),
it is necessary to solve the following non-linear system
subject to periodic boundary conditions

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi^{q}_{T}|^{q-2}=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}{(0)}+\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\eta\right),

\displaystyle\quad{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega,

(58a)

\displaystyle\Delta\eta=\mu\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi_{T}^{q}|^{q-2}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi_{T}^{q},

\displaystyle\quad{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega{,}

(58b)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{q}}\Phi_{T}^{q}\|_{L^{q}}=1{.}

(58c)

We emphasize that, in contrast to the Hilbert-space setting where the
map between the

gradient and the corresponding Sobolev

is

cf. (

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{L^{2}}\Phi^{q}_{T}=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(-{\mathbf{u}}^{*}{(0)}+\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\,\eta\right),

\displaystyle\qquad{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega

(59a)

\displaystyle\Delta\eta=0,

\displaystyle\qquad{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega.

(59b)

Since in this case

7
Discussion and Conclusions

Here we summarize the main findings of this study and draw some
conclusions. Building on a body of earlier work

Ayala and Protas 2011
;
Ayala and Protas 2014
;
Ayala and Protas 2014
;
Ayala and Protas 2017
;
Yun and Protas 2018
;
Kang et al. 2020
surveyed in

Protas 2022
, this investigation considers what is arguably the most
fundamental family of conditional regularity results, the
Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin conditions (
15
)–(
16
).
Singularity formation in a classical solution of the Navier-Stokes
system (
1
) is signalled by the norm
||{\mathbf{u}}(t)||_{L^{3}}
and
the integral in (
17
) becoming unbounded as the
singularity time is approached. We systematically searched for initial
data
{\mathbf{u}}_{0}
that might lead to such a behavior by solving different
PDE-constrained optimization problems for a broad range of parameters.
While no evidence of unbounded growth of the quantities of interest,
and hence also for singularity formation, was detected, we were able to
quantify the transient behavior arising in such worst-case scenarios.
This observation does not rule out the possibility of blow-up, since
Problems
1
–
4
are nonconvex and therefore
solutions found in Section
6
may only be (possibly
nonunique) suboptimal local maximizers. In other words, it is possible
there may be other, local or global, maximizers producing larger,
possibly unbounded, growth of the quantities in question. Another
possibility is that the values of the constraint parameter
B
we used
were too low such that the resulting flows would have been in the
“small-data” regime where global existence of classical solutions can
be asserted a priori. In this context we note that an analogous
approach based on solution of variational optimization problems was
recently applied to search for singularities in 3D inviscid Euler flows
producing flows consistent with a finite-time blow-up
Zhao and Protas 2023
.

A key methodological innovation of the present study is an approach to
solve PDE optimization problems defined on general Banach spaces
without Hilbert structure. Introduced in Section
5.2.2
, it
made it possible to solve Problems
2
and
4

where the optimal initial data
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0;B,T}
was sought in the Lebesgue spaces

L^{q}(\Omega)
,
q\geq 3
. Solutions of optimization problems defined
on these spaces and on Hilbert-Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\Omega)
embedded
in them, cf. (
27
), are distinct which can be attributed
to the different topologies these spaces are endowed with. In this
regard, we found that local maximizers of Problems
1
and

3
are also local maximizers of Problems
2

and
4
, respectively. However, the converse is not true.
This observation can be rigorously justified as the following theorem

Theorem 1
.

Consider the following optimization problems defined on the Banach spaces
X

and
Y

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathbf{z}}\in X}\boldsymbol{\varphi}({\mathbf{z}})\qquad\text{and}

(P1)

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathbf{z}}\in Y}\boldsymbol{\varphi}({\mathbf{z}}),

(P2)

where
X
is densely embedded in
Y
and

\boldsymbol{\varphi}({\mathbf{z}})
is a Fréchet differentiable objective functional
defined on both
X
and
Y
.
If
{\mathbf{z}}_{0}
is a local solution of (
P1
),
then
{\mathbf{z}}_{0}
is also a local solution of (
P2
).

Although Problems
1
–
4

are constrained, they can be converted to an unconstrained form
using Lagrange multipliers such that one can invoke Theorem
1
.
The proof of this theorem is given in Appendix
10
.

While for smaller values of
q
solutions of Problems
1

and
3
defined on the Sobolev spaces generally yield larger
values of the objective functional than solution of Problems

2
and
4
defined on Lebesgue spaces, these
values are comparable for
q=9
. The first observation is
counterintuitive since Lebesgue spaces can be viewed as “larger” than
the Sobolev spaces embedded in them.

In regard to the transient behavior observed in the extreme flows,
Figures
6
and
11
together with the estimates
on the maximum rate of growth of the norms
||{\mathbf{u}}(t)||_{L^{q}}
and of the
enstrophy
{\mathcal{E}}(t)
from Sections
3.2
and
3.4

indicate that the extreme flows do enter a regime where these
quantities are amplified at a rate consistent with singularity
formation in finite time, but this growth is not sustained long enough
for singularities to form due to depletion of nonlinearity. In the
absence of singularities, quantities (
26
)–(
25
)
appear bounded for all values of
B
and
T
, and were found to scale
in proportion to
(B^{p})^{\gamma}
, where
\gamma
is an increasing
function of
q
, cf. Figure
14
. On the other hand, the
maximum enstrophy in the extreme flows scales as
{\mathcal{O}}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\text{min}}^{3/2}\right)
, cf. Figure
14
, which is
the same behavior as was obtained in
Kang et al. 2020
by
solving Problem
0
. The fact that the extreme flows
obtained by solving Problem
0
and Problems

1
–
4
exhibit the same scaling of the maximum
enstrophy is intriguing since this quantity is not directly controlled
in the latter case and this growth is realized through entirely
different physical mechanisms. We add that the same bound is now
rigorously established for the growth of enstrophy in 1D viscous
Burgers flows
Ayala and Protas 2011
;
Albritton and
Nitti 2023
. Perhaps, if one day
the 3D Navier-Stokes system (
1
) is shown to be globally well
posed, the a priori bound on the maximum growth of enstrophy might have
this form.

A comparison of the results from Sections
6.1
–
6.2

and Figure
14
indicates that solutions of Problems

1
and
2
obtained for increasing values of
q

lead to a more “extreme” behavior. Therefore, an interesting open
question that we intend to consider in the future is what happens for
larger values of
q>9
.

As regards the numerical approach, cf. Appendix
9
, while
pseudo-spectral methods provide excellent accuracy in approximating
PDEs with smooth solutions, their main shortcoming is that they do not
allow for local mesh refinement necessary to resolve very fine flow
structures underlying extreme flow behaviors. Overcoming this
limitation is necessary in order to be able to solve Problems

0
–
4
for larger values of the constraint
parameter
B
or, equivalently, larger values of the Reynolds number
(
