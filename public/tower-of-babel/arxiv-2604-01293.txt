Quantum structure of the chiral vortical effect and boundary-induced vortical pumping.
Boqun Song, Pavan Hosur

Abstract
The chiral vortical effect (CVE) – an axial current driven by rotation in chiral matter – appears in systems ranging from relativistic fluids to Weyl semimetals, yet its quantum origin remains unclear because existing derivations are semiclassical. We present an exact quantum solution of a rotating Weyl fermion in a finite cylinder. We show that the bulk vortical response is entirely a magnetization current while the current density on the rotation axis remains finite and matches semiclassical predictions. For spin-polarized boundary conditions, we uncover an additional effect beyond the known CVE: a robust family of chiral modes that transport axial charge, Δ ​ Q = χ ​ N 2 ​ Δ ​ θ / 4 ​ π \Delta Q=\chi N^{2}\,\Delta\theta/4\pi , under rotation by angle Δ ​ θ \Delta\theta , where χ \chi is the Weyl node chirality and N N is the number of chiral modes. The pump is independent of temperature, Fermi level and Weyl velocities, but depends on the UV-sensitive number N N . These results establish a fully quantum picture of the CVE and reveal a boundary-enforced chiral spectral structure underlying vortical response in Weyl systems.

Chiral modes and charge pumping:

Having recovered the semiclassical result and established the magnetization character of the bulk response, we now turn to a distinct spectral structure that appears in finite systems with spin-polarized boundaries: a family of chiral modes beyond semiclassical and perturbative approaches.

Consider a Weyl fermion on a geometry that is translationally invariant along
z
with length
\ell_{z}
and has a cross-section
D
; the simplest example is a cylinder, in which case
D
is a circle. Suppose the boundary condition is fully spin-polarized:

\psi(\partial D,z)\propto\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}

(10)

up to an overall phase. Physically, such a boundary condition can arise when the Weyl fermion is surrounded by a ferromagnet that polarizes the boundary spin. This boundary condition admits no conventional surface states exponentially pinned to the boundary – at least for a circular cross-section as shown in App.
A.1
because the modified Bessel functions lack nontrivial roots. Nevertheless, the spectrum contains a set of chiral eigenstates for arbitrary boundary shape.

To identify these states, we look for zero modes of the transverse part of the Weyl Hamiltonian (
2
), satisfying
(k_{x}\sigma_{x}+k_{y}\sigma_{y})\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})\\
\psi_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})\end{pmatrix}=0
.
The solutions consist of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic functions for
\psi_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})
and
\psi_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})
, respectively, in the transverse directions in terms of the complex co-ordinate
\zeta=x+iy
. The boundary condition (
10
) suppresses
\psi_{\downarrow}
on the boundary; by the uniqueness theorem,
\psi_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})=0
in the bulk as well. Thus, the chiral modes can be expressed as

\displaystyle\psi_{\uparrow}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\zeta^{n}e^{ik_{z}z};\quad n\geq 0\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\psi_{\downarrow}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=0

(11)

and have a dispersion
E^{\text{ch}}=\chi v_{F}k_{z}-\mu
. Clearly, every
n\geq 0
channel produces one spin-polarized chiral mode with polarization selected by the boundary. If the cross-section is circular, the modes can be understood as the
k_{\perp}\to 0
limits of the bulk modes based on Bessel functions
J_{n}
. In particular, the uniform solution
\psi_{\uparrow}(x,y,z)=\frac{e^{ik_{z}z}}{\sqrt{A\ell_{z}}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
comes from setting
k_{\perp}=0
in the bulk solutions and using the identity
J_{n}(0)=\delta_{n0}
. If the boundary spins had the opposite polarization,
\psi(\partial D,z)\propto(0,1)^{T}
, the chiral mode wavefunctions would be
\psi_{\downarrow}(x,y,z)={\bar{\zeta}}^{-n}e^{ik_{z}z};\quad n\geq 0\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\psi_{\uparrow}(x,y,z)=0
and the dispersion would be
E^{\text{ch}}=-\chi v_{F}k_{z}-\mu
.

Next, we show that such modes are general – they exist for Weyl fermions with general spin
S
and Weyl charge
q
too. To see this, consider the generalized Weyl Hamiltonian
H_{\text{lab}}=2\chi(vk_{z}S_{z}+uk_{-}^{q}S_{+}+uk_{+}^{q}S_{-})-\mu
, describing nodes of arbitrary monopole charge
q
and spin
S
. Suppose the boundary condition fixes the spin projection on the boundary to an extremal value
S_{z}=S
. Because the ladder raising operator annihilates such a state,
S_{+}|S\rangle=0
, chiral eigenstates with dispersion
E^{\text{ch}}=2\chi vk_{z}S-\mu
exist and admit an exactly solvable family of chiral eigenstates whose wavefunction factorizes into spatial and spin parts. Substituting this form into the Schrödinger equation yields the constraint
k_{-}^{q}w(x,y)=0
. The solutions are a family of polyanalytic functions

w(x,y)\equiv w(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})\propto\bar{\zeta}^{m}\zeta^{n},\quad m<q,\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\geq 0

(12)

Each such profile produces a chiral eigenstate with the same dispersion
E^{\text{ch}}=2S\chi v_{F}k_{z}-\mu
and orbital angular momentum
l=n-m
. Because this construction relies only on the algebra of the ladder operators and the spin-polarized boundary condition, it survives in the absence of rotational symmetry and for arbitrary cross-sections. On the other hand, polyanalytic functions for higher spins are not subject to a uniqueness theorem like holomorphic functions do, thus the solutions written above might not exhaust chiral modes. Rather, they provide an elegant exactly solvable class robust to boundary deformations.

Returning to the canonical Weyl node with
S=1/2,q=1
, we show that the chiral branches imply a striking dynamical consequence under rotation. In the rotating frame, their energies shift by the spin contribution to the angular momentum,
E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{rot}}(s)=E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{lab}}(s)-s\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\Omega
, where
s=\pm 1
denotes the boundary spin polarization. The occupation of the chiral branches thereby changes relative to the laboratory frame, producing a net current. Evaluating
I=\int\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\frac{dE}{dk_{z}}[f(E_{\text{rot}})-f(E_{\text{lab}})]
using the identity
\intop_{-\infty}^{\infty}dE[f(E-\epsilon)-f(E)]=\epsilon
gives the universal result

I^{\text{ch}}=\chi\frac{\Omega}{4\pi}N^{2}

(13)

independent of temperature, chemical potential, and band parameters such as the Weyl velocity but dependent on the number of chiral modes
N
allowed by microscopic UV physics such as lattice regularization or boundary structure. On a finite cylinder of radius
R
, for instance, we expect
N\sim R/a
where
a
is the lattice constant, thus (
13
) yields a finite current density due to the chiral modes that is distinct from the transport current density carried by the bulk states; the latter vanishes according to (
9
). Integrating (
13
) over time implies that as the cylinder rotates, spectral flow along the chiral branches transfers axial charge between the ends of the system, yielding the pumping relationship (
1
).

The scaling
I^{\text{ch}}\propto N^{2}
follows because branch
n
is shifted by
(n+\tfrac{1}{2})\Omega
under rotation, so summing the resulting spectral flow over the chiral ladder gives
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(n+\tfrac{1}{2})=N^{2}/2
. While the magnitude of the pump is UV-related, the mechanism is general: the spectral flow of a boundary-supported chiral sector under rotation rather than the exact solvability of the fully spin-polarized boundary condition itself. Thus, we conjecture the existence of analogous boundary-enabled pumping beyond this special exactly solvable point of spin-polarized boundary conditions.

Conclusions:
We have provided a quantum formulation of the CVE, which reveals its precise structure for a Weyl fermion in a finite system and the presence of boundary-induced chiral modes that govern transport. In the bulk, the response is magnetization-like and survives semiclassically as a local current density near the rotation axis. A spin-polarized boundary, however, exposes an exactly solvable chiral sector that supports quantized spectral flow and particle pumping under rotation. These results separate the universal and boundary-sensitive aspects of vortical response: the magnetization character of the bulk current is robust, whereas quantized pumping relies on the existence and occupation of the boundary-induced chiral sector. More broadly, our results show that the quantum structure of vortical response is governed not only by bulk anomaly physics but also by boundary-enforced spectral topology. It would be interesting to explore how this structure is modified in lattice Weyl semimetals with multiple nodes and Fermi-arc boundary conditions.

\displaystyle\intop_{0}^{\mathcal{X}}xdxJ_{n}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{X}^{2}}{2}\left[J_{n}^{2}(\mathcal{X})-J_{n+1}(\mathcal{X})J_{n-1}(\mathcal{X})\right]\approx\frac{\mathcal{X}}{\pi}

(29)

independent of
n
, assuming the asymptotic form,
J_{n}(x)\approx\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\cos\left(x-n\pi/2-\pi/4\right)

for
x\gg|n|
. This gives the normalization

N_{n}=\frac{2\ell_{z}R}{k_{\perp}}

(30)

and the wavefunction can be compactly written as

\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}(\rho,\phi,z)=\sqrt{\frac{k_{\perp}}{2\ell_{z}R}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{\lambda}(k_{\perp},k_{z})J_{n}(k_{\perp}\rho)e^{in\phi}\\
b_{\lambda}(k_{\perp},k_{z})J_{n+1}(k_{\perp}\rho)e^{i(n+1)\phi}\end{array}\right)e^{ik_{z}z}

(31)

where
\ell_{z}
is the length of the cylinder.

A.1.2
Chiral modes

Besides wave-like bulk and evanescent surface states, the spectrum of
H_{\text{lab}}
or
H_{\text{rot}}
on a cylinder contains another set of states. These states can be obtained as the
k_{\perp}\to 0
limit of the bulk states, but are more easily written down directly. Explicitly, they are:

\displaystyle E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{lab},\uparrow}=\chi v_{F}k_{z}-\mu\quad

\displaystyle,\quad\psi_{n,k_{z},\uparrow}^{\text{ch}}(\rho,\phi,z)=\frac{1}{R^{n+1}}\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{\pi\ell_{z}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\rho^{n}e^{in\phi}\\
0\end{array}\right)e^{ik_{z}z}

\displaystyle E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{lab},\downarrow}=-\chi v_{F}k_{z}-\mu\quad

\displaystyle,\quad\psi_{n,k_{z},\downarrow}^{\text{ch}}(\rho,\phi,z)=\frac{1}{R^{n+1}}\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{\pi\ell_{z}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\rho^{n}e^{-in\phi}\\
\end{array}\right)e^{ik_{z}z}

for
n\geq 0
, as can be verified by direct substitution. These states are clearly spin-polarized and chiral. For a given spin-polarized boundary condition (
19
), either
\psi^{\text{ch}}_{n,k_{z},\uparrow}
or
\psi^{\text{ch}}_{n,k_{z},\downarrow}
exist, while more general boundary conditions disallow both sets of states.

Under rotation, the energies change as

E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{rot},s}=E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{lab},s}-s\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Omega

(36)

where
s=\pm 1
corresponds to
\uparrow/\downarrow
. Note the extra factor of
s
above compared to (
21
), which reflects the effect of the boundary. In App.
B
, we will repeatedly use the identity,

\displaystyle s\chi v_{F}\intop_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\left[f_{\text{eq}}(E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{rot},s})-f_{\text{eq}}(E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{lab},s})\right]

\displaystyle=\intop_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\frac{dE^{\text{ch}}_{\text{lab},s}}{dk_{z}}\left[f_{\text{eq}}(E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{lab},s}-s(n+1/2)\Omega)-f_{\text{eq}}(E^{\text{ch}}_{\text{lab},s})\right]

(37)

\displaystyle=\frac{s\chi}{2\pi}\intop_{-\infty}^{\infty}dE\left[f_{\text{eq}}(E-s(n+1/2)\Omega)-f_{\text{eq}}(E)\right]

(38)

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi}{2\pi}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Omega

(39)

where
f_{\text{eq}}
is the equilibrium Fermi-Dirac distribution.

A.1.3
Surface states

Surface states can be derived by invoking recurrence relations for the modified Bessel functions
I_{n}
of the first kind:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{n+1}{\rho}\right)I_{n+1}(\kappa\rho)

\displaystyle=\kappa I_{n}(\kappa\rho)

(40)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{n}{\rho}\right)I_{n}(\kappa\rho)

\displaystyle=\kappa I_{n+1}(\kappa\rho)

(41)

This yields the spectrum

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{lab}}^{\lambda}v_{F}(k_{z},\kappa)

\displaystyle=\lambda\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}-\kappa^{2}}-\mu

(42)

\displaystyle\psi_{\lambda,n,\kappa,k_{z}}^{\text{sf}}(\rho,\phi,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{n}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{\lambda}^{\text{sf}}(\kappa,k_{z})I_{n}(\kappa\rho)e^{in\phi}\\
b_{\lambda}^{\text{sf}}(\kappa,k_{z})I_{n+1}(\kappa\rho)e^{i(n+1)\phi}\end{array}\right)e^{ik_{z}z}

with

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{\lambda}^{\text{sf}}(\kappa,k_{z})\\
b_{\lambda}^{\text{sf}}(\kappa,k_{z})\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k_{z}\left(k_{z}-\lambda\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}-\kappa^{2}}\right)}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}i\kappa\\
k_{z}-\lambda\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}-\kappa^{2}}\end{array}\right)

(45)

Once again, the energies are independent of
n
and wavefunctions based on the modified Bessel functions of the second kind,
K_{n}
are excluded as they diverge at the origin.

Normalization of surface states follows from the identity

\intop_{0}^{\mathcal{X}}xdxI_{n}^{2}(x)=\frac{\mathcal{X}^{2}}{2}\left[I_{n}^{2}(\mathcal{X})-I_{n+1}(\mathcal{X})I_{n-1}(\mathcal{X})\right]\approx\frac{\mathcal{X}^{2}}{2}\frac{e^{2\mathcal{X}}}{2\pi\mathcal{X}^{2}}=\frac{e^{2\mathcal{X}}}{4\pi}

(46)

independent of
n
for
\kappa R\gg n^{2}
using the asymptotic form

I_{n}(x)\approx\frac{e^{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\left[1-\frac{4n^{2}-1}{8x}+\dots\right];x\gg|n^{2}-1/4|

(47)

This yields the normalization constant

N_{n}=\frac{\ell_{z}e^{2\mathcal{\kappa}R}}{2\kappa^{2}}

(48)

and normalized surface states

\psi_{\lambda,n,\kappa,k_{z}}^{\text{sf}}(\rho,\phi,z)=\kappa e^{-\kappa R}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\ell_{z}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{\lambda}^{\text{sf}}(\kappa,k_{z})I_{n}(\kappa\rho)e^{in\phi}\\
b_{\lambda}^{\text{sf}}(\kappa,k_{z})I_{n+1}(\kappa\rho)e^{i(n+1)\phi}\end{array}\right)e^{ik_{z}z}

(49)

However, boundary conditions (
19
) lead to a surprising
consequence – the absence of surface states. This is because (
19
)
requires either
I_{n}(\kappa R)
or
I_{n+1}(\kappa R)
to vanish,
but these Bessel functions vanish only if
\kappa R=0
whereas we have assumed
\kappa\neq 0
at the outset. Thus, for spin-polarized
boundary conditions, there are no conventional exponentially localized surface states. On the other hand, the fact that the chiral modes appear only for spin-polarized boundary conditions suggests that they correspond to a certain critical limit of true surface states achieved by the special boundary conditions.

Appendix B
Rotation-induced current densities

In this appendix we evaluate the current density produced by the bulk modes in three limits: on the axis, on the surface, and averaged over the cross-section. Our basic premise is that the system acquires an equilibrium Fermi-Dirac
distribution
f_{\text{eq}}
in the rotating frame:

\hat{\rho}=f_{\text{eq}}(H_{\text{rot}})

(50)

Using
\hat{\rho}
, we will write down the current density
j_{z}(\rho,\phi,z)

and evaluate it on the axis
(\rho=0)
, on the cylinder surface
(\rho=R)

and averaged over the cylindrical cross-section. We restrict to polarized
boundary conditions (
19
) which results in the absence
of surface states. Therefore, it suffices to calculate current densities
only using the bulk states. Importantly, an intensive quantity like
the current density should not depend on the boundary conditions,
so we expect the results in this section to hold for arbitrary boundary
conditions.

The current density along
z
is

\displaystyle j_{z}(\rho,\phi,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}\chi\text{tr}\left[\sigma_{z}\hat{\rho}(\rho,\phi,z)\right]

(51)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}f_{\text{eq}}\left(E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}\right)\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}^{*}(\rho,\phi,z)v_{F}\sigma_{z}\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}(\rho,\phi,z)

(52)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,n}\frac{\ell_{z}}{2\pi}\int dk_{z}\frac{R}{\pi}\int dk_{\perp}f_{\text{eq}}\left(E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}\right)\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}^{*}(\rho,\phi,z)v_{F}\sigma_{z}\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}(\rho,\phi,z)

(53)

Due to translational symmetry along
z
and rotational symmetry about
the
z
axis,
j_{z}(\rho,\phi,z)
is expected to depend only on

\rho
, and will be denoted
j_{z}(\rho)
henceforth.

B.1
On the axis

B.1.1
From bulk states

At
\rho=0
, i.e., on the rotation axis, the current due to the bulk, wave-like states is

\displaystyle j_{z}^{\text{ax,bulk}}=\chi{v_{F}}\sum_{\lambda,n}\intop_{\mathbf{k}}f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}(n,k_{\perp},k_{z})\right]\left(\left|a_{\lambda}(k_{\perp},k_{z})\right|^{2}J_{n}^{2}(0)-\left|b_{\lambda}(k_{\perp},k_{z})\right|^{2}J_{n+1}^{2}(0)\right)

(54)

where
\intop_{\mathbf{k}}=\int\frac{dk_{z}k_{\perp}dk_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}
. Note that

\displaystyle\left|a_{\lambda}(k_{\perp},k_{z})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}\left|\frac{1+\sigma_{z}}{2}\right|\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\lambda k_{z}}{\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\left|b_{\lambda}(k_{\perp},k_{z})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp},k_{z}}\left|\frac{1-\sigma_{z}}{2}\right|\psi_{\lambda,n,k_{\perp}^{2},k_{z}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda k_{z}}{\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}}}\right)

(55)

Since
J_{n}(0)=\delta_{n0}
, the only terms that survive the summation
over
n
in (
54
) are
n=0
and
n=-1
. Thus,

\displaystyle j_{z}^{\text{ax,bulk}}

\displaystyle=\chi{v_{F}}\sum_{\lambda}\intop_{\mathbf{k}}f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}(k_{z},k_{\perp},0)\right]\left|a_{\lambda}(k_{\perp},k_{z})\right|^{2}-f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}(k_{z},k_{\perp},-1)\right]\left|b_{\lambda}(k_{\perp},k_{z})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi}{2}v_{F}\sum_{\lambda}\intop_{\mathbf{k}}\left\{f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}(k_{z},k_{\perp},0)\right]-f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}(k_{z},k_{\perp},-1)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{\chi}{2}v_{F}\sum_{\lambda}\intop_{\mathbf{k}}\left\{f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}(k_{z},k_{\perp},0)\right]+f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{rot}}^{\lambda}(k_{z},k_{\perp},-1)\right]\right\}\frac{\lambda k_{z}}{\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}}}

(56)

The second term above is odd in
k_{z}
and vanishes, leaving behind

j_{z}^{\text{ax,bulk}}=\frac{\chi}{2}v_{F}\sum_{\lambda}\intop_{\mathbf{k}}\left\{f_{\text{eq}}\left[\lambda{v_{F}}\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}}-\mu-\frac{1}{2}\Omega\right]-f_{\text{eq}}\left[\lambda{v_{F}}\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}}-\mu+\frac{1}{2}\Omega\right]\right\}

(57)

At this stage, the connection to semiclassical treatments becomes
transparent. The integrand in
j_{z}^{\rm ax}
depends only on the
rotationally invariant combination
k_{z}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}
and contains no
residual dependence on the angular-momentum quantum number
n
, which
has been eliminated by evaluating the current on the rotation axis.
Writing
\intop_{\mathbf{k}}=\int dk_{z}\,k_{\perp}dk_{\perp}/(2\pi)^{2}
as

\int d^{3}k/(2\pi)^{3}
after trivial angular averaging in the transverse
plane, one recovers precisely the semiclassical phase-space expression
for the chiral vortical current density
36
;
5
upon Taylor-expanding in
\Omega
. Thus, evaluating the exact quantum current density at
\rho=0
implements the same projection as the usual semiclassical limit: it removes sensitivity to angular-momentum quantization and quantum interference, leaving a smooth phase-space integral.

On the other hand, the present quantum treatment yields exact expressions in
\Omega
without Taylor expansion. Specifically, at low
T
, Sommerfeld expansion simplifies (
57
) to

\displaystyle j_{z}^{\text{ax,bulk}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi}{2}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}v_{F}^{2}}\left\{V_{|\mu+\Omega/2|}-V_{|\mu-\Omega/2|}\right\}+\frac{\chi}{2}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{6}\left\{S_{|\mu+\Omega/2|}^{\prime}-S_{|\mu-\Omega/2|}^{\prime}\right\}

(58)

where
V_{a}
and
S_{a}
are the volume and surface area of a sphere
of radius
a
. Explicitly,

\displaystyle j_{z}^{\text{ax,bulk}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi}{3}\frac{|\mu+\Omega/2|^{3}-|\mu-\Omega/2|^{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}v_{F}^{2}}+\chi\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{6}8\pi\left\{|\mu+\Omega/2|-|\mu-\Omega/2|\right\}

\displaystyle=\chi\left(\frac{\mu^{2}+\Omega^{2}/12}{4\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\right)\Omega+\frac{T^{2}}{6}\text{sgn}(\Omega)\min\left(|\mu|,\frac{|\Omega|}{2}\right)

(59)

The well-known result for the CVE corresponds to the
|\mu|>|\Omega|/2
above, in which case the second term reduces to
T^{2}\Omega/12
.

B.1.2
From chiral modes

The contribution to the on-axis current from the chiral modes (
A.1.2

\displaystyle j_{z}^{\text{ax,ch}}

\displaystyle=s\chi v_{F}\ell_{z}\int\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\left(f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{rot}}^{\text{ch}}(n=0,k_{z})\right]-f_{\text{eq}}\left[E_{\text{lab}}^{\text{ch}}(k_{z})\right]\right)\left|\psi_{n=0,k_{z},s}^{\text{ch}}(0,\phi,z)\right|^{2}

B.2.2
From chiral modes

\displaystyle j_{z}^{\text{avg,ch}}

Appendix C
Topology of the chiral modes

In this Appendix, we show that the chiral modes are remarkably general and robust – existing for arbitrary spin, Weyl monopole charge, and shape of the cross-section. They are unique for a canonical Weyl node, while they constitute an exactly solvable family of solutions for general spin
S
and Weyl charge
q
but may not be unique. Under rotation, all these chiral modes pump charge by an amount that ties together quantum geometry in momentum, real and spin space.

C.1
Canonical Weyl case: exact chiral modes and their uniqueness

We begin with the ordinary Weyl fermion of spin
S=\frac{1}{2}
and unit
Weyl charge
q=1
, for which the structure of the chiral modes can be
derived completely and uniquely. This is the physically most relevant
case and also the one in which the fully spin-polarized chiral family
is fixed uniquely by the boundary condition.

Consider the Weyl Hamiltonian (
2
) in the laboratory frame. We look for eigenstates with the chiral dispersion

E^{\rm ch}=s\chi v_{F}k_{z}-\mu.

(83)

where
s=\pm 1
. Substituting this into the Schrödinger equation and writing the spinor as

\psi(\mathbf{r})=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})\\
\psi_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})\end{pmatrix},

(84)

gives a solution for
s=1
that is consistent with the boundary being spin-up:

(k_{x}-ik_{y})\psi_{\uparrow}=0,\qquad\psi_{\downarrow}=0.

(85)

Thus, the chiral mode is fully spin polarized: the down-spin component
vanishes identically, while the up-spin component obeys a first-order
holomorphicity condition.

Introducing

\zeta=x+iy

and using

k_{x}-ik_{y}=-i(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{y})=-2i\,\partial_{\bar{\zeta}},

Eq. (
85
) becomes

\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}\psi_{\uparrow}=0.

(86)

Hence
\psi_{\uparrow}
is holomorphic in the transverse coordinates. On
a simply connected cross-section
D
, every regular holomorphic
function generates a chiral solution,

\psi(\mathbf{r})=\frac{e^{ik_{z}z}}{\sqrt{\ell_{z}}}\begin{pmatrix}f(\zeta)\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}f=0.

(87)

For the circular cross-section, a convenient basis is provided by the
monomials

f(\zeta)=\zeta^{n}=(x+iy)^{n},\qquad n\geq 0,

(88)

which are precisely the modes quoted in the main text. Each
nonnegative integer
n
produces one chiral branch. In polar
coordinates,

\zeta^{n}=\rho^{n}e^{in\phi},

(89)

so these states carry spatial angular momentum
l=n
. They can be
understood as the
k_{\perp}\to 0
limits of the bulk modes built from
Bessel functions
J_{n}
; in particular, the uniform chiral mode
corresponds to
n=0
and follows from
J_{n}(0)=\delta_{n0}
:

\psi(\mathbf{r})=\frac{e^{ik_{z}z}}{\sqrt{A\ell_{z}}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

(90)

We now show that this fully spin-up chiral family is in fact unique.
Indeed, one may view the two spin components as satisfying decoupled
first-order equations, with the down-spin component obeying a
holomorphic or anti-holomorphic equation together with the boundary
condition
\psi_{\downarrow}=0
on the boundary. By analyticity, a function of this type that vanishes on the entire boundary must vanish identically in
the interior. Therefore,

\psi_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})\equiv 0\qquad\text{throughout }D.

(91)

It follows that every chiral mode consistent with the fully
spin-polarized boundary condition must be of the form
(
87
). In this canonical case

S=\frac{1}{2}
,
q=1
, the exactly solvable family of chiral modes is
therefore unique.

This clean uniqueness is special to the ordinary Weyl case. As we show
next, exact fully spin-polarized chiral families continue to exist for
more general spin
S
, Weyl charge
q
, and cross-section shape, but
their uniqueness is no longer guaranteed.

C.2
Beyond
S=1/2
, unit Weyl charge and circular symmetry

We first show that the structure responsible for the chiral branches
does not rely on the special assumptions of spin-
1/2
, unit Weyl
charge, or circular symmetry of the boundary. The argument applies to
a general cross–section and to Weyl nodes with arbitrary charge
q

and spin
S
.

Consider a generalized Weyl Hamiltonian

H_{\rm lab}=2\chi\!\left(vk_{z}S_{z}+uk_{-}^{\,q}S_{+}+uk_{+}^{\,q}S_{-}\right)-\mu,

(92)

where
k_{\pm}=k_{x}\pm ik_{y}
and
S_{\pm}
are spin ladder operators.
This form captures Weyl nodes of arbitrary topological charge
q

as well as higher–spin generalizations protected by crystalline
symmetry. We impose a spin–polarized boundary condition that fixes the spin
projection on the boundary
\partial D
of the transverse region
D
,

S_{z}\psi|_{\partial D}=s\,\psi|_{\partial D}.

(93)

In particular, when
s=\pm S
the boundary selects the extremal spin
state. Importantly, this boundary condition constrains only the spin
degree of freedom and places no restriction on the spatial dependence
of the wavefunction along the boundary.

We now look for eigenstates whose dispersion is linear in
k_{z}
:

E_{\rm ch}=2\chi Svk_{z}-\mu.

(94)

Substituting this form into the Schrödinger equation shows that the
transverse wavefunction must satisfy

\left(k_{-}^{\,q}S_{+}+k_{+}^{\,q}S_{-}\right)\psi(\mathbf{r}_{\perp})=0.

(95)

If the boundary selects the extremal spin state
|S\rangle
, the
ladder operator annihilates it,

S_{+}|S\rangle=0.

(96)

Consequently there exists solutions of the form

\psi(\mathbf{r}_{\perp})=w(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})\,|S\rangle,\qquad\zeta=x+iy.

(97)

where the spatial and spin parts have factorized. The spatial part satisfies

k_{-}^{\,q}w(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})=\left[-i(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{y})\right]^{q}w(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})=[-i(\partial_{\bar{\zeta}})]^{q}w(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})=0

(98)

The general normalizable solution is therefore a function of the form

\displaystyle w(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})

\displaystyle=\bar{\zeta}^{m}\zeta^{n}\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq m<q,\quad\max(0,-N+m)\leq n<N+m,\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}

(99)

\displaystyle=\rho^{m+n}e^{i(n-m)\phi}

(100)

upto normalization, which has spatial angular momentum
l=n-m
. Regularity requires
m+n\geq 0
while a spatial angular momentum cutoff
N
implies
l=n-m\in(-N,N)
. Each such transverse profile produces a chiral eigenstate

\psi_{\rm ch}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{e^{ik_{z}z}}{\sqrt{\ell_{z}}}\,w(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})\,|S\rangle,

(101)

with dispersion
E_{\rm ch}=2S\chi vk_{z}-\mu
.

Thus the existence of chiral branches follows directly from the
structure of the Hamiltonian and the spin–polarized boundary
condition, without requiring circular symmetry of the boundary or
restricting to
S=1/2
or
q=1
. The simplest member of this family for given
S,q
corresponds to the spatially uniform solution
w=\mathrm{const}
, while more general functions generate additional chiral modes distinguished by their transverse structure.

C.3
Quantized charge pumping

We showed in App.
B.2
that the chiral modes in a unit Weyl fermion with
q=1
and spin
S=1/2
produce a spatially averaged current

j_{z}^{\text{avg,ch}}=\frac{\chi\Omega N^{2}}{4\pi A}

(102)

where we have replaced
\pi R^{2}
by the cross-sectional area
A
. The arguments of the previous subsection imply that this result does not depend on the shape of the cross-section but only depends on its area
A
. The total current is

I^{\text{ch}}=Aj_{z}^{\text{avg,ch}}=\frac{\chi\Omega N^{2}}{4\pi}

(103)

Furthermore, integrating over time gives the charge pumped by rotation:

\Delta Q^{\text{ch}}=\chi\frac{\Delta\theta}{4\pi}N^{2}

(104)

where
N\geq 1
is the number of chiral modes. This is a remarkable result, stating that a minimum of one particle is pumped along
z
for every two full rotations. Although it is a quantum phenomenon, it is independent of
\hbar
. The result is solely dependent on the periodicity of spatial rotation and intrinsic nature of the chiral fermions. In general, however, the charge pumped depends on the UV cutoff on angular momentum
N
.

The results in App.
C.2
allow us to immediately compute the contribution to the charge pumped by the exactly solvable modes for general
S
and
q
. Explicitly,

\displaystyle I^{\text{ch}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi\Omega S}{\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{q-1}\sum_{n=\max(0,-N+m)}^{N+m}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)

(105)

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi\Omega qS}{12\pi}\left[6N^{2}+6N(q-1)+(2q-1)(q-1)\right]

(106)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\chi\Omega qSN^{2}}{2\pi}

(107)

for
N\gg q
. Thus, the pump simply gets multiplied by factors
q
and
S
and pumps charge

\Delta Q^{\text{ch}}=\chi qS\frac{\Delta\theta}{2\pi}N^{2}

(108)
