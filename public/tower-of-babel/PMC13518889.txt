Hybrid electricity management system for residential power block applications
Authors: Rabia Tehseen, Uzma Omer, Abdul Razzaq Hamraz, Anam Mustaqeem, Nosheen Sabahat, Nosheen Qamar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDeveloping countries have been facing electricity crisis for decades, mainly due to insufficient electricity generating capacity as compared to its demand and extensive growth in population. This research outlines Electric_Fed, a decentralized electricity management model that incorporates blockchain and federated learning (FL). This model allows multiple clients, defined as houses or blocks, to privately and efficiently collaborate on electricity consumption and production forecasting without needing to exchange raw, shared data. The model achieves collaboration on a global model. Automated electricity trading is made possible through the use of Blockchain and smart contracts, which allows clients to securely and transparently participate in real-time peer-to-peer energy exchange. In this article, electricity production model using cross device FL technique and blockchain has been presented. In the proposed model, FL has been used to provide a secured client server model to facilitate clients having surplus electricity or facing electricity shortfall within the network. Central Server issues alert to the clients having surplus electricity to start Smart contract with the client facing electricity shortfall implementing blockchain. Blockchain ensures safe transaction and reliable transfer of payments to the client selling electricity. Electricity production and consumption dataset from Jan 2024 to Dec 2024 has been processed. Experimental validation proved that the proposed model is capable enough to fulfill 89.2% electricity need of the selected region. The experimental setup results (from June 2024) validate the model’s effectiveness. Client A reached a surplus of + 909 kWh, Client B + 12 kWh, Client C + 230 kWh and Client D fell short by − 693 kWh. The system automatically recognized surpluses and deficits, trading energy to balance the needs of Clients A and C, which allowed them to reach full self-sufficiency. This model lowered national grid dependency and reduced overall electricity costs. As compared to other models presented for electricity supply, our proposed model is not only more effective in fulfilling the regional electricity demands but it also suggested a secured mechanism to compensate the electricity selling client participating in electricity trade. This demonstrates, in and of itself, the effectiveness of the model proposed while providing intelligent decentralized energy management in a system that is, as a whole, efficient, scalable, and privacy preserving.Keywords: Client, Electric power, Machine learning, Server, Smart contractSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Physics

Introduction

The economies of numerous developing nations are being impacted, leading to instability in their national security environments, due to a severe energy crisis across various challenging situations1. Within these developing countries, a significant number of factories have been compelled to shut down due to the high cost of non-renewable energy in hub of industries2. The ongoing usage and escalating need for energy underscore the fact that energy has evolved into one of the most pressing global issues3. This has led to difficulties for power generation, transmission, and distribution enterprises as the escalating cost of power generation, driven by the soaring prices of fossil fuels and increased line losses, has resulted in higher tariffs. Consequently, there is a compelling need to address the energy crisis through the integration of novel electricity production units. As rate of inflation is surging in developing nations, it amplifies urgency of adopting such energy sources that could be renewed4. The electricity shortfall challenge currently confronted by developing nations could be alleviated through the utilization of renewable energy sources. Planners and policymakers are struggling hard to discover alternative energy resources as natural resources are depleting and the demand for electricity is increasing with extravagant growth in population5. To meet consumer demand, developing nations should consider the implementation of solar photovoltaic cells that would boost the country’s proportion of renewable energy6. Solar energy technologies (SETs) offer evident environmental advantages, facilitating sustainable human activities and the reduction of reliance on non-renewable resources7. Moreover, electricity theft can also be controlled to some extent by installation of local photovoltaic panels8.

Classical machine learning (ML) necessitates training of data in centralized manner9. Despite its strong convergence, this training method introduces vulnerabilities as participants send data to central server. In response, Federated Learning (FL) technique has been adopted due to its emphasis on distributed training of data, enabling collaborative local model training on individual data without revealing private information to the central cloud server9. The concept of Federated Learning, encompassing a distributed machine learning approach10, enables training on a substantial dataset of decentralized data residing on devices like mobile phones. To handle the problems related to data privacy, security, and ownership, FL employs a broader approach by “taking the code to the data” rather than the reverse, as shown in Fig. 1. FL enables clients to keep their raw data on their devices and utilizes localized ML based model training process to reduce the burden of data transmission. The resulting models, which are acquired and shared, are subsequently sent to a central server for temporary storage, consolidation, global model training, and distribution to all clients11. In spite of a centralized forecasting model and a private database might seem easier in managing residential power, the model presupposes that all the prosumers are eager to regularly exchange fine-grained information on the electricity generation and use with one reliable operator. In realistic residential power blocks, the assumption is frequently impractical since realistic load profiles can expose delicate behavioral habits, including occupancy charts and appliance use, routine routines of day-to-day actions. Due to this reason, Federated Learning (FL) is implemented in this work to allow collaborative forecasting without submitting unprocessed household data to a central repository. Nonetheless, FL is not sufficient in an energy peer-to-peer environment with multiple prosumers that engage in decentralized coordination and trading. Blockchain in these situations also offers an extra layer of trust and verification by assuring tamper-proof logging, open and transparent records of transactions, and secure smart-contract execution without using a single intermediary. Thus, FL and Blockchain are not integrated with one another due to being new technologies, instead, they are applied to two different needs of the suggested Electric_Fed framework privacy-preserving predicting and reliable decentralized energy trade.

The authors recognize that the joint FL-Blockchain architecture adds more communication, computation, and latency overhead along with energy overhead in comparison to a traditional centralized solution. Specifically, federated round reuse can add to training delay, and Blockchain operation can introduce overhead in processing transactions. Due to this reason, the offered Electric_Fed framework is not supposed to be universally superior to centralized forecasting. Rather, it is aimed at privacy-aware and multi-stakeholder resident energy environments in which the advantages of local data storage, auditability, and decentralized trust supersede incremental system cost. Moreover, the given architecture does not compel severely resource-constrained IoT meters to perform heavy computations on their own; local training and coordination may be done by utilizing edge-capable home gateways or neighborhood-level controllers, whereas only lightweight metadata (model hashes, update proofs, transaction references) are stored on-chain. Such design minimizes storage load and does not keep all the computational power of end IoT devices.

In the current research, “Power Block” describes the hybrid intelligent energy management unit that employs FL and Blockchain technologies to oversee, forecast, and manage electricity distribution among clients (prosumers) in our network. As the system’s primary computational and decision-making unit, it enables privacy-preserving learning, local optimization, and decentralized energy trading. Each Power Block functions as an independent unit comprising of a local data processing unit that documents electricity generation and consumption in real time, and a Blockchain interface that conducts electricity trading among clients through encrypted, automated smart contracts without a centralized intermediary.

In essence, the Power Block is a hybrid control entity that bridges traditional energy management systems with modern distributed intelligence. It combines computational analytics, energy forecasting, and secure peer-to-peer transactions within a single integrated framework. The term “Power Block” symbolizes the fusion of physical energy infrastructure and digital intelligence, forming the foundation of the Electric_Fed-based decentralized energy ecosystem proposed in this research.

Energy management system facilitates the decentralized synergy among multiple clients (prosumers). Proposed system employs Blockchain technology to ensure secure, transparent, and tamper-proof transactions while FL enables dispersed predictive modeling to ascertain energy surplus and demand across varying nodes. In the proposed model, unlike traditional systems, energy coordination is decentralized and clients can engage in energy exchanges with one another in a way that keeps personal information private. The main input for energy generation remains renewable sources, specifically solar photovoltaic (PV) panels. However, the true innovation is the seamless and secure energy trading, coordination, and management made possible through the combined use of Federated Learning (FL) and Blockchain technology. The Power Block is described as a hybrid computational–transactional functional unit that implements Federated Learning for decentralized energy forecasting and blockchains for automated secure trading between prosumers. The block is the main operational unit of the proposed system and is in charge of the energy trading, forecasting, and flow management system. Therefore, this innovative nature of Electric_Fed does not consist of just the combination of FL and Blockchain, but the functionally complementary use of both towards decentralized prediction, secure coordination, and peer-to-peer management of residential power blocks of renewable sources.

Even though Blockchain enhances the transparency and integrity of the transactions, it does not prevent adversarial attacks on the Federated Learning layer completely. In ElectricFed, malicious houses/power blocks can endeavor to poison models or label flip by broadcasting corrupted local updates to manipulate the global forecasting model to gain unfair trading advantage. Thus, dishonest participants in the learning layer should be taken into consideration in a threat model. The effect of abnormal or malicious updates can be minimized by introducing strong aggregation techniques like Krum or Bulyan, which enhance reliability. By doing so, Blockchain will ensure the security of the transaction layer, and the AI layer is insured by powerful FL mechanisms.

Literature review

Over time different solutions to electricity shortfall challenge addressing scarcity of fossil-fuel based energy sources and their environmental impact, have been sought. Renewable sources are becoming increasingly popular due to their accessibility and cost-effectiveness. Energy trading and sharing, particularly using blockchain technology, have gained attention among researchers16,17. Existing models have emphasized the use of blockchain to ensure secure and encrypted trading and sharing between producers and consumers18–20. Blockchain’s scalability, security, and decentralization are important considerations. In the context of blockchain technology, Kumar and Chopra21, Zhang et al.22 assessed these factors and proposed strategies to enhance capacity while maintaining security and decentralization. While previous models have facilitated secure trading and sharing of energy, they often overlook the calculation of future consumer demand. Consumer participation is crucial to embrace renewable energy and the shift from fossil-fuels23–26. Blockchain-based peer-to-peer power trading systems have been introduced, allowing users to contribute renewable energy to the grid and enabling system functionality even when disconnected from the national grid27–29.

Mohammed et al.30 presented various frameworks for trading energy among peers via blockchain have been proposed, including dual auction Vickrey–Clark–Groves (VCG) procedures to promote honest bidding and social welfare. However, these models often lack accurate estimation of future Client demand31. These models do not incorporate techniques or algorithms for predicting future consumption and efficient energy management. Comparison with existing work.

Research has been conducted to examine blockchain-based energy trading and machine learning-based electricity forecasting, individually. Nevertheless, the literature is sparse about using privacy-preserving federated learning in conjunction with blockchain-based peer-to-peer electricity trading of residential energy networks. This paper fills this gap by suggesting a hybrid electricity management system which integrates federated learning of decentralized forecasting with blockchain-based smart contracts of the secure energy transactions.

Proposed study introduces a novel approach that leverages solar energy for electricity production through Photovoltaic panels (PV) and utilizes FL and blockchain technology for secure and efficient energy management. Unlike previous works, our model integrates these technologies to accurately predict future energy generation and demand while ensuring privacy and secure transactions. The proposed model contributes to resolving the energy crisis and shifting toward renewable energy sources.

Recent studies have examined some of the intelligent technologies to enhance management of energy and smart grid functions. Research thereof, like61, indicates the role of advanced information and communication technologies at the realization of effective demand-side management in current smart grids. Similarly, smart forecasting methods of photovoltaic systems have been explored in order to enhance integration of renewable energy and market sustainability62. The combination of blockchain, IoT, and machine learning technologies has also been used to develop security and reliability of smart micro-grids63. Additionally, it has been suggested that decentralized electrical trading systems and profit optimization algorithms on electric vehicles can be implemented and based on blockchain and machine learning systems64. Moreover, the strategies of charging and discharging of the electric vehicles have been devised as flexible to ensure grid stability and efficiency of the energy utilisation65. Even with these developments, the majority of current solutions are based on centralized data processing or a particular application case, e.g. EV management or photovoltaic predictions. However, the proposed Federated Learning-based Hybrid Electricity Management System will allow distributed learning on residential blocks of power and maintain data privacy and lessen centralized computational resources. Such solution can be used in residential settings to offer intelligent electricity management with privacy-preserving and scalable approach.

More in-depth research has been conducted into the subject of advanced optimization and energy management measures to enhance the efficiency and reliability of the latest smart grid systems. A bi-level multi-energy scheduling framework of virtual power plants in a competitive electricity market was proposed by66, which showed how coordinated scheduling can improve operational flexibility and economic performance. In a similar paper, Samadi Gharehveran67, provided an overview of energy management techniques in multi-microgrid systems with particular focus on the complications brought about by uncertainty in distributed generation resources including the solar and wind power. In addition, Karimi and Heydarian-Forushani68 proposed a multi-objective bi-level optimization model to examine the action of distribution system operators that are involved in the wholesale and local transactive energy markets. The problem of reliable monitoring of distributed power systems has also been studied by Esmaili and Haddadi69 who presented an optimal placement strategy of the measurement devices over the distribution networks to enhance the accuracy of the state estimation without conflicting the reliability issues. Moreover, Pirmohammad Talatape et al.70 examined the operations of distributed energy sources aimed at, at the same time, minimizing the pollution of the environment and enhancing economic performance, which explained the significant role of smart coordination between the units of renewable generation. All these works indicate the increasing popularity of advanced optimization, monitoring, and management methods of distributed energy systems, which is why intelligent and decentralized systems can be designed, e.g., the Federated Learning and Blockchain-based electricity management system, offered as the solution to the problem of residential power block applications.

Materials and methods

Energy is an essential component of economic activities for a country. In this research, we proposed a FL-based electricity management system to address electricity shortfall problem in developing countries. Main objective of this research is to develop a model that urges on the installation of photovoltaic panels on rooftop of houses located in the selected region for production of electricity locally. The proposed system is composed of two modules namely electricity management system (EMT) and electricity selling system (ESS).

A. Electricity management system (EMS)

Electricity management system (EMS)

Each participating house can consume self-produced electricity to fulfil its needs and sell surplus electricity to the local grid station for earning money. Local grid station receives electricity declared as surplus from each house and digitalizer system installed at local grid will maintains a database to record daily electricity sold and daily electricity demanded by the particular house to calculate net benefit at the end of every month. Digitalizer will also determine the amount of actual surplus electricity for this local station after fulfilling all electricity demands per month. Digitalizer system will alert the central server informing about surplus electricity available for sale to other Clients. Central server in the proposed model is responsible to send an initial model to all digitalizer systems installed at local grid station of all Clients. Digitalizer system will receive initial model from server. Every digitalizer system is responsible for triggering the central server about surplus electricity or electricity shortfall within the service area by training multiple parameters of initial data model received from server. Local data model updates are transmitted to central server from each digitalizer system hence completing cross device FL circle. Central server receives model updates from all grid stations located in the selected region indicating surplus electricity or electricity shortfall. Central server sends an alert to the local stations that have declared surplus energy to sell electricity to the grid station who has intimated about electricity shortfall. Central server will also trigger the Client facing electricity shortfall to get required electricity from the adjacent Client willing to supply its surplus electricity as presented in Fig. 2.

B. Electricity selling system (ESS)

Electricity selling system (ESS)

Blockchain is involved in selling and buying the surplus electricity among Clients. This research presents a secure, transparent, and efficient framework to manage electricity within the Client and among Clients having surplus electricity by employing a state-of-the-art technology blockchain. In this scenario, each individual client is considered a residential prosumer with a small smart energy meter and rooftop photovoltaic (PV) panels, as well as a prosumer within a distributed grid of blood. As with well-designed prosumer blocks within smart grids, the power blocks manage the production, consumption, and trading of the (decentralized) energy with the prosumers using a combination of Federated Learning and Blockchain.

This technology adds transparency in the system by allowing the prosumer to see status of surplus units by using blockchain traceability characteristic and get notified when it reaches the consumers. Once a prosumer sells surplus electricity, its payment is locked by smart contracts that provides a secure transaction with proof of work. Payment locked by a smart contract cannot be tampered and the prosumer will keep track of the units sold by finding a trace of the supplier and eventual consumers. Once the consumer gets units, prosumer gets notified by blockchain platform and the blockchain nodes are updated against each entry. Meanwhile, payments are locked using a smart contract., whereby a smart contract is nothing but a computer program that enable the blockchain to perform credible transactions without any third parties in a distributed system. Payments remain locked and cannot be handed over until approved by the authorized sources. It can be simply defined as payment on delivery. The whole system is transparent and keeps track of every transaction for external authorities such as government, if they notice any unusual event, they can take action against the person responsible for that transaction. ii. Network layer

Network layer

Working of the network layer is 2-fold. In first fold, it is responsible for secured two-way transfer of local model updates towards central server or updated global model from central server to Clients. Blockchain module

The blockchain layer of the hybrid electricity management system will be provided to offer secure, transparent, and tampered transactions among residential power blocks. A permissioned blockchain network topology is deployed since the actors (residential units, local energy managers, and grid operators) are familiar actors in the smart grid system. Permissioned networks offer reduced latency and increased throughput and additional access control compared to public blockchains, which are more appropriate in energy management applications.

The system utilises Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) consensus mechanism that validates transactions among the participating nodes. The reason why PBFT is chosen is that it can reach consensus quickly in contrast to Proof-of-Work (PoW) mechanisms that have a high computational cost. There is a set of validator nodes that are the representatives of the energy management controllers in the proposed architecture that are involved in the mechanism of the consensus to verify the records of the energy trading, the actions of the demand-response, and choices of the electricity allocation. In residential distributed systems, PBFT can support up to (n − 1)/3 faulty nodes, and hence the reliability of the system.

To carry out transaction processing, a light weight transaction cost model is deployed. It utilizes a micro-transaction cost of a fixed cost as opposed to cryptocurrency-based gas fees that would be paid in the public blockchain to record energy exchange events. This will minimize the overhead of operation and enable the blockchain to operate as a secure accounting and verification layer, not as a financial trading platform. The computational resources, storage requirements, and the overhead of the communication in the network are the principal cost of a single transaction.

Scalability wise, the permissioned blockchain with PBFT consensus has both minimal confirmation latency and computation demands than PoW-based networks. This is because, the only nodes that are involved in the consensus process are authorized and hence, the system can facilitate the high frequency transactions of energy involving residential power blocks with low processing delay and resource efficient utilization. This renders the proposed architecture as being fit in the real-time smart grid electricity management applications.

Even though smart contracts based on Ethereum allow a fully automated and transparent peer-to-peer energy trading, the practical implementation in residential power blocks should consider the scalability constraints of transaction throughput and cost of execution. The high frequency trading environment can result in a substantial cumulative gas cost due to many daily micro-transactions that can be more than the economic value of small-scale energy transactions. Hence, the suggested framework cannot be understood as being based on an open on-chain implementation of each micro-transactions. Rather, scalability can be enhanced through a private or permissioned Ethereum network, aggregating many trades together, or storing only aggregated settlement data on-chain and keeping a fine-grained trading logic off-chain. This saves gas money and enhances throughput rendering the ledger more applicable in energy trading situations of residential usage.

Even though blockchain has immutability and transparency of recorded transactions, it alone cannot confirm whether the physical electricity recorded by a hardware meter is accurate. The system proposed thus presupposes a hardware-to-blockchain trust anchor, according to which smart meters and digitalizer gateways are recognized as trusted sources of data concerning reporting on the generation, consumption, and transfer of electricity. Assuming one of the local meters is compromised and sends incorrect readings, a smart contract can transfer a fraudful payment even with the integrity of the blockchain ledger. To mitigate this threat, the framework ought to have secure smart meters with hardware that is difficult to tamper with, validated communication between the meter and digitalizer, verification of device identity, and periodically verify the reported energy values at the regional grid station. By doing so, blockchain insures the records of transactions once data is entered whereas the metering infrastructure is trusted to be able to verify the validity of the off-chain physical measurements before they are announced on-chain.

Therefore, the security of a smart contract in electricity trading is not the only determinant of the reliability of a blockchain-based electricity trading system, but the credibility of the metering and gateway systems that provide the data that the blockchain-based electricity trading system uses.

D. Buyer–seller selection mechanism in multi-client scenarios

Buyer–seller selection mechanism in multi-client scenarios

During the months when a higher number of clients are coupled with surplus energy production while the other clients are under energy deficits, the Power Block coordinates trading through a dynamic selection algorithm which is integrated within the Electric_Fed federated network. The selection dynamics are based on a triage of priorities, which is as follows:

i. Energy balance calculation

Energy balance calculation

Each client node (prosumer) under federated updates will predict and then periodically supply the Power Block aggregator with their predicted consumption and production balance data. Clients with a surplus (production > consumption) are categorized as potential sellers, while clients with a deficit (consumption > production) are categorized as potential buyers.

ii. Priority ranking via federated model prediction

Priority ranking via federated model prediction

Using data from local clients, the federated model calculates the severity of local demand and the reliability of local supply. Buyers are given a rank according to their deficit ratio (kWh shortfall ÷ average monthly consumption), while sellers are ranked according to their surplus ratio (kWh surplus ÷ installed PV capacity). This process guarantees that the clients experiencing the worst shortages will be prioritized and provided energy from the clients with the most surplus energy.

iii. Blockchain-based matching and transaction formation

Blockchain-based matching and transaction formation

After ranking the clients, the Power Block runs the smart contracts to match the highest ranked buyers and sellers. In the matching process, we first apply a lowest-cost, highest surplus policy to ensure equity and maximize the efficiency of the trade. All the transactions are captured by the blockchain to guarantee transparency and accountability. In the case of conflicting orders, the smart contract enforces timestamp arbitration, and the earliest energy request is prioritized.

Application scenario (Based on Table 6)

During June and July, when clients A and C had energy surpluses and clients B and D had energy deficits. Client C, with the surplus ratio over Client D, was chosen to be the primary seller. Since Client D had the larger deficit, the surplus energy was first allocated to her, while clients B and D were the buyers. Once satisfied, the surplus energy left with Client C was offered to Client B. The implementer of the buyer-seller selection mechanism guarantees equal access to energy resources within a fully decentralized energy trading system.

ix.Computational complexity analysis.

Computational complexity analysis.

The suggested electricity management framework uses a Federated Learning (FL) architecture whereby various residential power blocks can train their own local models and send only model parameters to a central server. The local model training and the aggregation process mainly define the computational complexity of the FL model.

Suppose every client has N training samples and d model parameters, local training by gradient-based learning has computational complexity of about O(N d) per training round. The central server does parameter aggregation, which is normally by federated averaging (fedavg) algorithm, which has complexity of O(K d) where K is the number of interacting clients.

The FL-based approach spreads the load of the training process across clients compared with centralized machine learning schemes, where all the data should be sent and processed in one central server with a complexity of about O ((KN) d ). This saves the central server computation and vastly minimizes the overheads of communication.

The FL framework is more scalable than deep reinforcement learning (DRL) or metaheuristic optimization algorithms (e.g., genetic algorithms or particle swarm optimization) that typically involve several iterative steps and massive data sets, usually requiring many iterations and a large training set to achieve accuracy. DRL-based frameworks can have complexity of the order of O(T·N·d) with T being the number of training episodes, which is computationally expensive on large-scale residential systems. The model in general has a reasonable forecasting accuracy in conditions of heterogeneous energy consumption and generation. E. Economic implications

Economic implications

The Electric_Fed Power Block system allows trading of electricity which results in grid-locked households saving 60–65% of electricity bills in addition to what is paid for electricity to the national grid. Surplus energy prosumers on the other hand may sell their excess electricity to clients to earn, if the profit margin is PKR 27–30 per kwh sold. The client, in return, is paying 40% less than the national grid rate.

F. Economic feasibility analysis

Economic feasibility analysis

To implement the Electric_Fed system in practice, one also will have to consider the economic viability of the proposed system. In the electric world, the structure of tariffs differs according to the peak seasons and off-peak seasons. In the suggested system, peer-to-peer electricity trading is possible at a rate slightly below the grid rate and this will be advantageous to buyers and sellers. An example is given by the fact that in the case the national grid tariff is 0.18/kwh at peak hours, in-block trading can take place at 0.15/kwh and consumer can save money on electricity bills and producers can make extra money on the excess production.

Cryptocurrency volatility may affect the value of a transaction since the payment is done using blockchain-based payment. To alleviate this problem, the system can use stablecoins or energy-backed tokens whose value can be fixed to local electricity tariffs where the payment will be stable and predictable. Conclusion

The aim of this study is to fulfill the electricity demand of consumer by producing electricity locally using PV panels. In this article, FL technique has been applied to determine the total regional need of electricity. Blockchain technology has been used to trade surplus electricity to electricity deficient Clients through digitalizer system installed on region grid station. Our proposed system contains smart meters that collect data of electricity consumption from every house. Digitalizer system installed at regional grid station keeps record of the surplus units applied by each digitalized house. Cross device federated learning technique has been used in this research in which all Clients receive central model with initial parameters from the central server. Every Client is responsible to update the initial model and set status to inform server about surplus electricity or shortfall. Server collects all local updates and after aggregating all local updates it triggers Clients with surplus electricity to connect with Client reported electricity shortfall. Smart contract is initiated for secure energy transaction between both agreeing Clients for selling and buying electricity. Payments are added as bitcoins to the wallet maintained by digitalizer system installed at Client’s grid station from where it will be shifted to the wallets of prosumer maintained by the digitalizer system. Electricity produced per year has been calculated to forecast electricity requirement for the upcoming year. More PV panels might be installed, and more house could be digitalized for producing more electricity to meet demand or trade the excessive energy produced to regional grid station for earning money. It has also been concluded that profit rate is high in June, July and August in summer season, as these are very hot months in the selected region. Although more electricity is consumed due to rise in regional temperature but production rate is also very high. Proposed model fulfilled 88.9% electricity demand of the selected region. Even though the records of transactions are secure with the help of Blockchain, the FL layer needs to be specifically safeguarded in case of adversarial interference, like model poisoning and label flipping, by means of robust aggregation and monitoring of client behavior. In future, more Clients can be added in the proposed electricity production and trading system to earn more profit. In future work, scalability challenges must be addressed by investigating efficient protocols for federated learning and blockchain that can handle large-scale networks of PV panels and extensive energy trading volumes.
