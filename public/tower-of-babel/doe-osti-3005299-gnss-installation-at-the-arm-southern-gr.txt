1.0 Summary
 GRUAN, the GCOS Reference Upper Air Network, is an international reference observing network of
 sites measuring essential climate variables above Earth's surface, designed to fill an important gap in the
 current global observing system. GRUAN’s objectives are to (1) provide long-term, high-quality climate
 records, (2) constrain and calibrate data from more spatially comprehensive global observing systems
 (including satellites and current radiosonde networks), and (3) fully characterize the properties of the
 atmospheric column.

 In support of these goals, Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) precipitable water (GNSS-PW)
 measurement has been included as a high-priority measurement of the essential climate variable water
 vapor. Since 2013, the GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences (GFZ) has hosted a GRUAN central
 processing facility for the GNSS-PW. GFZ is responsible for the installation of GNSS hardware, for raw
 data transfer, for processing and archiving, for derivation of GNSS-PW products according to GRUAN
 requirements, including PW uncertainty estimation, and for quality checks and archiving of the
 GNSS-PW products. To provide GNSS-PW for the U.S. Department of Energy’s Atmospheric Radiation
 Measurement (ARM) Southern Great Plains (SGP) observatory in Oklahoma, the GNSS station SGPO
 was installed in February 2018 (see Figure 1).

 Station SGPO is officially registered under DOMES 49368M001 and is part of the International GNSS
 Service (IGS) tracking network. GFZ provides official coordinate solutions for SGPO annually.

 The GNSS-PW data products are compared with PW derived from ERA5, the atmospheric reanalysis of
 the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF). These comparisons are performed
 on a routine basis. Daily updates (sliding window of 30 days) are available through:

 ftp://ftp.gfz-potsdam.de/GNSS/products/nrttrop/MONITORING_IFS/

 As an example, Figure 3 shows the comparison of GNSS-derived PW with ECMWF Reanalysis v5
 (ERA)-5 PW for 30 days in October-November 2025. The small bias of -0.1 kg/m2 and the standard
 deviation of 1 kg/m2 indicates a high accuracy of the GNSS-PW data product.
