A Berry-Esseen Bound for Quantum Lattice Systems
Marcus Cramer, Fernando G.S.L. Brandão, Mădălin Guţă, Álvaro M. Alhambra, Matteo Scandi

Abstract
It is expected that the statistical fluctuations of local observables in large quantum systems obey the central limit theorem, and approximate a normal distribution as their size grows. Here, we prove a version of the Berry-Esseen theorem for quantum lattice systems, which strengthens that central limit theorem by providing a rigorous convergence estimate towards the normal distribution for large but finite system size. Given a local quantum Hamiltonian on N N particles and a quantum state with a finite correlation length, the result states that the measurement of local observables such as the energy follows a normal distribution, up to an error scaling as 𝒪 ⁡ ( N − 1 2 ​ polylog ​ ( N ) ) \mathcal{O}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\text{polylog}(N)\right) , which is optimal up to logarithmic factors.



I
Introduction

Quantum many-body systems are typically characterized via Hamiltonians with local interactions among the individual particles.
This includes numerous spin models, which can describe a wealth of physical and computational scenarios of wide interest. However, the fact that their Hilbert space dimension grows exponentially with their size prevents us from having exact descriptions as soon as they reach just a few dozens of particles. Still, what physically relevant models have in common is the locality of the interactions between the individual particles, so that quantum Hamiltonians are a very particular class of matrices within that Hilbert space. It is thus of great interest to understand which physical features can be inferred directly from locality alone. Prominent examples include the approximate light-cone structure of the dynamics through Lieb-Robinson bounds
Lieb and Robinson 1972
or many properties at equilibrium such as bounds on their correlation structure
Hastings and Koma 2006
;
Nachtergaele and Sims 2006
;
Hastings 2007
;
Wolf et al. 2008
;
Eisert et al. 2010
;
Kliesch et al. 2014
.

Another important series of features has to do with the statistical fluctuations of macroscopic observables, such as the energy or magnetization. Intuitively, locality implies that distant particles are roughly uncorrelated, and as such, approximately satisfy the assumptions behind the
central limit theorem
. One can thus expect that the statistics of measuring local observables approach increasingly peaked normal distributions as the system size grows. This has been shown to be the case in the thermodynamic limit in previous works, under different sets of conditions (see
Hartmann et al. 2004
;
Erdös and Schröder 2014
;
Keating et al. 2015
for some examples). However, in this context, it is also important to understand how these normal distributions emerge in large but finite systems, and what is the most general set of conditions under which they do.

In statistics, for finite but large sample sizes of independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables, the Berry-Esseen theorem
Berry 1941
;
Esseen 1942
provides a refinement of the central limit theorem, with an explicit rate of convergence towards the normal distribution as the sample size grows (see
Feller 1991
;
Vershynin 2026
for more pedagogical proofs). Specifically, the result states that cumulative distribution functions approach the corresponding error function in Kolmogorov distance as
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}})
. This has also been generalized to settings of weakly correlated classical variables
Tikhomirov 1981
;
Sunklodas 1984
.

In this manuscript, we show that this theorem also applies to the statistical fluctuations of finite but large interacting many-body systems, even when the underlying state
\rho
has fast-decaying spatial correlations. The result thus significantly strengthens previous works
Hartmann et al. 2004
;
Erdös and Schröder 2014
;
Keating et al. 2015
in two concrete ways: by considering the finite-size scaling with system size, and by considering states in arbitrary spatial dimension with decaying but non-zero correlations. Other previous related works largely focus on the concentration around the average of observable statistics in similar settings
Anshu 2016
;
Kuwahara 2016
;
Kuwahara and Saito 2020a
;
De Palma and Rouzé 2022
;
Anshu and Metger 2023
;
Wild and Alhambra 2023
, rather than on the approach to Gaussianity.

This manuscript is based on the previously unpublished results in
Brandao et al. 2015
, and we here extend the proof to weaker notions of correlation decay, explicitly including algebraic decay. The proof closely follows classical works on the statistics of weakly correlated random variables
Tikhomirov 1981
;
Sunklodas 1984
, since it also relies on showing that the corresponding characteristic function is close to a Gaussian function. It also involves intermediate lemmas controlling exponentials of non-commuting local operators, which might be of independent interest.

The result deals with universal features of the statistics of macroscopic quantum observables in a wide variety of physically relevant situations. As such, we expect it to have a range of implications in any context in which the observable statistics of interacting quantum systems are relevant, including statistical physics, many-body and complexity theory, classical and quantum algorithms, or quantum statistics and estimation theory
Hayashi 2006
;
Guţă and Jenčová 2007
;
Kahn and Guţă 2009
.

Since the initial reporting of the main result here in
Brandao et al. 2015
(Theorem
), the statement has already been used in technical proofs of several follow-up works on a variety of topics in quantum many-body and statistical physics. A (possibly non-exhaustive) summary of direct existing applications is the following:

•

It is one of key ingredients of the proof of the equivalence of the canonical and microcanonical ensembles in
Brandao and Cramer 2015
, which holds under the only assumption of correlation decay in the canonical ensemble. See
Tasaki 2018
;
Kuwahara and Saito 2020a
;
Kuwahara and Saito 2020b
for later related results. These arguments were later extended in
Bertoni et al. 2025
to prove thermalization of low-entangled states under random energy smoothings.

•

It can be used to upper bound the effective dimension of diagonal ensembles, thus bounding the average fluctuations of a quantum system around equilibrium and thermalization
Farrelly et al. 2017
(see also
Hovhannisyan et al. 2020
for subsequent applications in quantum thermodynamics).

•

It is also a key technical ingredient of the feature that many-body dynamics with periodic revivals contain anomalous “scarred" eigenstates in their spectrum
Alhambra et al. 2020
, since the Gaussianity implies that the initial state of the dynamics has well distributed support within those eigenstates (see
Schecter and Iadecola 2019
for an example of a model for which the bound is tight).

•

It is used in the proof of the efficiency bounds of coarse-grained many-body thermometry
Hovhannisyan et al. 2021
, to compute the relevant Fisher information using the approximate Gaussianity of the distribution.

•

The approximate Gaussianity of the wavefunction of product states is also leveraged in
Rai et al. 2024
to give rigorous bounds on the efficiency of energy filtering algorithms (both classical and quantum) for probing non-equilibrium dynamics
Lu et al. 2021
;
Schuckert et al. 2023
;
Yang et al. 2022
;
Irmejs et al. 2024
.

•

The Gaussianity of the density of states (when setting
\rho=\mathbb{I}/d
) is also used in
Huang 2024
to characterize the entanglement of eigenstates of chaotic Hamiltonians.

The paper is structured as follows. In Sec.
we first explain the setting, main assumptions, and technical preliminaries. In Sec.
we then prove the main result for the particular case of the states being product, for illustrative purposes. In Sec.
we state and give the proof of the main result, except for that of the main technical Lemma, which we refer to Sec.
. We conclude in Sec.
with some open questions. In the Appendix we show the full proof of some of the more technical lemmas, including a proof of Esseen’s original inequality and the results on series approximations to matrix exponentials.

II
Preliminaries

We consider
\mathcal{X}
to be a lattice of
N
sites, and let
\mathcal{H}:=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal{X}}\;\mathcal{H}_{i}
be the associated Hilbert space. Assume
\mathcal{X}
is equipped with a metric
d:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
. This induces a canonical notion of semidistance between subsets
\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{X}
given by:

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{A}\\
j\in\mathcal{B}\end{subarray}}\,d(i,j)\,.

(1)

We also define the dimension
D
of
\mathcal{X}
to be the smallest
D\geq 1
such that there exists a constant
c_{D}>0
satisfying:

\displaystyle\left|\{i\in\mathcal{X}|d(i,j)=\ell\}\right|\leq c_{D}\,\ell^{D-1}

(2)

for all
\ell
.

Then, let
\rho
be a state acting on
\mathcal{H}
. We use the notation
\left\langle X\right\rangle_{\rho}:=\mathrm{Tr}\left[X\rho\right]
to denote the averages with respect to
\rho
. We say that
\rho
satisfies decay of correlations with correlation function
\alpha:\mathbb{N}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
, if for all pairs of operators
A:=\hat{A}\otimes\mathds{I}_{\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{A}}
and
B:=\hat{B}\otimes\mathds{I}_{\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{B}}
it holds that:

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle AB\right\rangle_{\rho}-\left\langle A\right\rangle_{\rho}\left\langle B\right\rangle_{\rho}\right|\leq\left\|A\right\|\left\|B\right\|\min\{|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\}\,\alpha\left(d(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\right)\,.

(3)

This condition with
\alpha(l)
exponentially decaying is known to hold in a variety of settings, including gapped ground states
Hastings and Koma 2006
;
Nachtergaele and Sims 2006
, matrix product and finitely correlated states
Fannes et al. 1992
;
Perez-Garcia et al. 2007
, thermal states in 1D
Araki 1969
;
Bluhm et al. 2022
;
Bergamaschi and Chen 2026
and at high temperatures
Kliesch et al. 2014
and fixed points of rapidly mixing Lindbladians
Kastoryano and Eisert 2013
;
Roon and Sims 2024
among others. In most of these situations, it even holds without the pre-factor
\min\{|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\}
. In long-range systems, the condition is also expected to hold if
\alpha(l)
is a suitably fast-decaying polynomial
Kimura and Kuwahara 2025
;
Kim et al. 2025
;
Möbus et al. 2026
. A similar polynomial decay is also expected to hold in critical systems at zero temperature.

In order to assess the effect of decaying terms, we also introduce the function:

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}(\ell):=c_{D}\,\sum_{r=\ell}^{\infty}\,\alpha(r)\,{(r+1)^{D-1}}\,,

(4)

which we assume to be well defined for all
\ell\geq 1
. We will consider the case of
\rho
being a product state separately.

We focus on the statistics of a Hamiltonian defined as:

\displaystyle H:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{X}}\;h_{j}=\sum_{n}\;e_{n}\left.\left|{n}\right.\right\rangle\!\!\left.\left\langle{n}\right.\right|\,,

(5)

where
h_{i}
are
R
-local (in the sense that they act trivially on sites that are at a distance larger than
R
), and bounded as
\|h_{i}\|\leq E
. Then, we define the cumulative distribution as:

\displaystyle F_{H}(y):=\sum_{e_{n}\leq y}\;\left\langle n\right|\rho\left|n\right\rangle\,.

(6)

This corresponds to a probability distribution with average
\mu_{H}:=\left\langle H\right\rangle_{\rho}
and variance
\sigma^{2}_{H}:=\left\langle(H-\mu)^{2}\right\rangle_{\rho}
. A central assumption in the derivation is the following:

\displaystyle\sigma_{H}^{2}\geq c_{0}E^{2}N\,,

(7)

where
c_{0}
is some arbitrary
\Theta(1)
constant.
This assumption on the variance of the state is necessary for the normal distribution to appear. It guarantees that we are away from exceptional cases such as
\rho
being an eigenstate of
H
(with zero variance), or the fluctuations being confined to a small spatial region such that
\sigma_{H}=o(N)
. It is automatically satisfied in i.i.d. scenarios, it follows from translational invariance, and it can be efficiently checked given some standard classical description of
\rho
(e.g. as a product state or an MPS) and
H
. Finally, let us also note that since the states we consider are short-range correlated we have that
\sigma^{2}_{H}=\mathcal{O}(N)
, so this is the fastest scaling possible too.

It is also useful to introduce the re-normalized Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}
given by:

\displaystyle\widehat{H}:=\frac{1}{\sigma_{H}}\,\sum_{j\in\mathcal{X}}\;\widehat{h}_{j}\,,

(8)

where
\widehat{h}_{i}:=h_{i}-\left\langle h_{i}\right\rangle_{\rho}
. This transformation allows us to have a standardized distribution with
\mu_{\widehat{H}}=0
and
\sigma^{2}_{\widehat{H}}=1
. The cumulative distribution of
\widehat{H}
is related to the one of the original Hamiltonian as:

\displaystyle F_{\widehat{H}}(y)=F_{H}\left(\sigma_{H}\,y+\mu_{H}\right)\,.

(9)

Finally, let us introduce the cumulative distribution of a standard Gaussian
G(y)
as:

\displaystyle G(y):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{y}{\rm d}x\;e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\,.

(10)

This is what ultimately allows us to define a notion of distance between two cumulative distributions. In particular, let us define our figure of merit
\Delta
to be:

\displaystyle\Delta:=\sup_{y}\,\left|F_{\widehat{H}}(y)-G(y)\right|\,.

(11)

An important Lemma we will use is given by Esseen’s inequality
Esseen 1945
:

\displaystyle\Delta=\sup_{y}\,\left|F_{\widehat{H}}(y)-G(y)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\Omega}+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\Omega}{\rm d}\omega\,\,\frac{|\varphi_{\widehat{H}}({\omega})-e^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}}|}{{|\omega|}}\,,

(12)

where
C
is some numerical constant.
This allows us to estimate the convergence of
F_{\widehat{H}}(y)
to
G(y)
as the size of the lattice
N:=|\mathcal{X}|
grows, and the main results of the paper will be stated as upper bounds on
\Delta
in terms of
N
. We refer to App.
for a precise statement and a proof of this Lemma.

III
Product states

Before moving to the main result, we cover a narrower case with a significantly simpler proof for illustrative purposes: that in which the correlations are exactly zero and the state is product.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\rho=\bigotimes_{i}^{N}\rho_{i}
be a product state among all sites, and
H
be a
R-
local Hamiltonian as above. Then

\Delta\leq\frac{K}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}},

(13)

where
K=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Proof.

The proof, previously sketched in
Rai et al. 2024
, is simplified by using the results from
Wild and Alhambra 2023
bounding the cumulant series via the cluster expansion.
Let
\varphi_{\widehat{H}}({\omega}):=\langle e^{i\omega\widehat{H}}\rangle_{\rho}
with
\widehat{H}
as in Eq. (
8
), and let
\omega^{*}=\frac{1}{2e^{2}R^{2D}(R^{2D}+1)E}=\mathcal{O}(1)
. It follows from
(
Wild and Alhambra 2023
, Theorem 10)
that, for
\omega\leq\frac{\omega^{*}\sigma_{H}}{2}
,

\displaystyle\left|\log\varphi_{\widehat{H}}({\omega})+\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}\right|\leq\frac{2N}{\sigma_{H}^{3}}\frac{\omega^{3}}{(\omega^{*})^{3}}\leq\tilde{B}_{1}\,\frac{\omega^{3}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,,

(14)

where we implicitly used the bound
\sigma_{H}^{2}\geq c_{0}E^{2}N
and we defined
\tilde{B_{1}}:=(c_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{*}E)^{-3}
. Eq. (
14
) follows from the fact that the Taylor series of
\log\varphi_{\widehat{H}}({\omega})
has a convergence radius given by
\omega^{*}
, as it can be by directly bounding the coefficients of the series (i.e. the cumulants of the distribution). Applying
|e^{z}-1|\leq|z|e^{|z|}
to Eq. (
14
), and assuming that
\omega\leq\min\{\frac{\omega^{*}\sigma_{H}}{2},\frac{\sqrt{N}}{4\tilde{B}_{1}}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\varphi_{\widehat{H}}({\omega})-e^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}}\left|e^{-(\log\varphi_{\widehat{H}}({\omega})+\frac{\omega^{2}}{2})}-1\right|\leq\tilde{B}_{1}\,\frac{\omega^{3}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,e^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}(1-(2\tilde{B}_{1}N^{-1/2})\,\omega)}\leq\tilde{B}_{1}\,\frac{\omega^{3}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,e^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{4}}\,.

(15)

We can now combine this with Esseen’s inequality Eq.
12
as follows (see Appendix
or
Kolassa 1997
). Let
\Omega\leq\min\{\frac{\omega^{*}\sigma_{H}}{2},\frac{\sqrt{N}}{4\tilde{B}_{1}}\}
,

\displaystyle\left|F_{\widehat{H}}(y)-G(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\Omega}+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\Omega}{\rm d}\omega\,\,\frac{|\varphi_{\widehat{H}}({\omega})-e^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}}|}{{|\omega|}}\leq\frac{C}{\Omega}+\frac{2\tilde{B}_{1}}{\pi\,N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\Omega}|\omega|^{2}e^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{4}}{\rm d}\omega\leq\frac{C}{\Omega}+\frac{4\tilde{B}_{1}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,.

(16)

Then, choosing
\Omega=C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}
with
C^{\prime}=\min\{\frac{\omega^{*}\sigma_{H}}{2\sqrt{N}},\frac{1}{4\tilde{B}_{1}}\}
, we obtain
\Delta\leq\frac{C+4\tilde{B}_{1}}{C^{\prime}\sqrt{N}}
.

∎

Here, the decay is as the inverse of a square root, without polylogarithmic factors, which matches (up to constant factors) the scaling of the standard case of classical i.i.d. variables.
This result for product states also extends to Hamiltonians with polynomially decaying interactions
Sanchez-Segovia et al. 2025
, for which the cumulant series converges in the same way as in Eq. (
14
).

IV
Main results

Our main technical result is an upper bound on
\Delta
for the cumulative distribution function of states under the weaker assumption of correlation decay. It is stated as follows:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\rho
be a state on a
D

R

1.

V.1
The choice of the
{\tilde{R}}_{m,j}(\omega)
and
{\tilde{S}}_{m,j}(\omega)

There are two possible limiting choices. First, one can choose
{\tilde{R}}_{m,j}(\omega)\equiv\mathds{I}
. In this case we have:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\xi_{j}^{0}(\omega):=\mathds{I}\,;\\
\xi_{j}^{m}(\omega):=\left(e^{i\omega(z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)-z_{j}^{\ell}(m))}-\mathds{I}\right)=\left(e^{i\omega\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(m)}-\mathds{I}\right)\,;\\
\Xi_{j}^{m}(\omega):=\left(e^{i\omega z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)}-e^{i\omega(z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)-z_{j}^{\ell}(m))}e^{i\omega z_{j}^{\ell}(m)}\right)\,.\end{cases}\qquad\text{For ${\tilde{R}}_{m,j}(\omega)=\mathds{I}$}\,,

(77)

meaning that
\xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)
becomes completely localized in the region
d(k,j)\in[2R\ell(m-1),2R\ell m]
, while
\Xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)
exactly accounts for the non-commutativity between
\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(m)
and
z_{j}^{\ell}(m)
. In the case of commuting Hamiltonian, this choice gives the desired result, since
\Xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)=0
(a similar discussion also applies to
\tilde{S}_{m,j}(\omega)
and
\Gamma_{j}^{m}(\omega)
). Still, for non-commuting models this correction is finite, and it does not decay sufficiently fast to be a useful choice in the derivation.

In the opposite limit, one can choose
{\tilde{R}}_{m,j}(\omega)\equiv R^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)
and
{\tilde{S}}_{m,j}(\omega)\equiv S^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)
, where we introduced the two operators:

\displaystyle R^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega):=e^{-i\omega(z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)-z_{j}^{\ell}(m))}e^{i\omega z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)}e^{-i\omega z_{j}^{\ell}(m)}\,;\qquad S^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega):=e^{i\omega(\widehat{H}-z^{\ell}_{j}(m))}e^{i\omega z^{\ell}_{j}(m)}e^{-i\omega\widehat{H}}\,,

(78)

Then, if we consider the effect of this choice on
\xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)
and
\Xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)
, it follows that:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\xi_{j}^{0}(\omega):=\mathds{I}\,;\\
\xi_{j}^{m}(\omega):=\left(e^{i\omega z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)}e^{-i\omega z_{j}^{\ell}(m)}-\mathds{I}\right)\,;\\
\Xi_{j}^{m}(\omega):=0\,.\end{cases}\qquad\text{For ${\tilde{R}}_{m,j}(\omega)=R^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)$}\,.

(79)

This means that for commuting and non-commuting models the correction arising from
\Xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)
disappears. On the other hand, though,
\xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)
becomes completely delocalized, which prevents the error arising from the decomposition in Eq. (
69
) to be small.

For this reason, we look for a choice of
{\tilde{R}}_{m,j}(\omega)
that makes
\Xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)
sufficiently small, while keeping the locality of
\xi_{j}^{m}(\omega)
. This is achieved with the definition:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\tilde{R}}_{m,j}(\omega)=R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)=\mathds{I}+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\;\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\,R^{(n)}_{m,j}(0)\\
{\tilde{S}}_{m,j}(\omega)=S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)=\mathds{I}+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\;\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\,S^{(n)}_{m,j}(0)\end{cases}\,,

(80)

where
R^{(m)}_{m,j}(0):=\partial^{m}_{\omega}R^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)\big|_{\omega=0}
and
S^{(m)}_{m,j}(0):=\partial^{m}_{\omega}S^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)\big|_{\omega=0}
. That is, we choose
{\tilde{R}}_{m,j}(\omega)
and
{\tilde{S}}_{m,j}(\omega)
to be the Taylor expansion of
R^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)
and
S^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)
truncated at order
M
. Choosing
M=0
or
M=\infty
gives the two limiting behaviors discussed above in Eq. (
77
) and Eq. (
79
).

As it will be clear in the following, fixing
M
in an intermediate regime between the two will allow us to achieve the correct scaling. Indeed, we can prove the following:

Lemma 4
.

Let
R^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)
and
S^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega)
be the two operators defined in Eq. (
78
), and let
R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)
and
S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)
be their truncated Taylor expansion at order
M
. the following properties hold:

1.

R^{\infty}_{m,j}(0)=S^{\infty}_{m,j}(0)=\mathds{I}
and
R^{(1)}_{m,j}(0)=S^{(1)}_{m,j}(0)=0
;

2.

Their support satisfies:

\displaystyle{\rm supp}(R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega))\subseteq\{i|d(j,i)\in[2R(\ell(m-1)-M),2R(\ell\,m+M)]\}\,;

(81)

\displaystyle{\rm supp}(S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega))\subseteq\{i|d(j,i)\in[2R(\ell(m-1)-M),2R(\ell\,m+M)]\}\,.

(82)

3.

Their
n
-th derivative is upper bounded by:

\displaystyle\|R^{(m)}_{m,j}(0)\|,\,\|S^{(m)}_{m,j}(0)\|\leq\left(\frac{E\,\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}\right)^{n}(n)!\,.

(83)

where we introduced the constant
\Gamma=\max\{(4c_{D}(2R)^{D}),(2c_{D}^{2}(2R)^{D})\}
. This implies that:

\displaystyle\|R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|,\,\|S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq\frac{1-\left(\frac{\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}(E|\omega|)\right)^{M+1}}{1-\left(\frac{\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}(E|\omega|)\right)}\,.

(84)

Now, let
m\leq K
, and let
\omega\in[0,\Omega_{1})
, where
\Omega_{1}:=\left(\frac{\sigma_{H}}{2E\,\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}K^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\right)
. Then, it also holds that:

\displaystyle\|R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{M+1}}\right)\,;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\|S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{M+1}}\right)\,;

\displaystyle\|R_{m,j}^{\infty}(\omega)-R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(\frac{\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}(E|\omega|)\right)^{M+1}\,;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\|S_{m,j}^{\infty}(\omega)-R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(\frac{\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}(E|\omega|)\right)^{M+1}\,;

(85)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{(k)}_{\omega}R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\,(k!)\,\left(\frac{E\,\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}\right)^{k}\,;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\|\partial^{(k)}_{\omega}S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\,(k!)\,\left(\frac{E\,\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}\right)^{k}\,.

The proof is based on the cluster expansion, and it is deferred to App.

VI
Conclusion

The main open technical question of this work is whether a proof similar to that of Theorem
can be found also for Theorem
, which will simplify the arguments dramatically. This requires proving the convergence of the cluster expansion of the characteristic function of a state with correlation decay, for which none of the existing techniques directly apply
Kuwahara and Saito 2020a
;
Haah et al. 2024
;
Wild and Alhambra 2023
;
Mann and Minko 2024
. The proof of the concentration bound for states with decaying correlations in
Anshu 2016
might yield some insights along these lines. It would also be interesting to understand whether an alternative set of conditions similar to the classical of Lindeberg’s
Lindeberg 1922
can also yield a similar result. It is also of relevance to extend this type of result to the infinite dimensional bosonic setting
Cramer and Eisert 2010
;
Arous et al. 2013
and to long-range Hamiltonians beyond
Sanchez-Segovia et al. 2025
.

It is understood already from simple classical i.i.d. variables that the bound
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}})
derived here is tight up to polylogarithmic factors, and there are also explicit examples of that tightness in quantum many-body systems
Alhambra et al. 2020
. However, chaotic quantum systems, with the well-established level repulsion, have much smoother spectra than discrete classical random variables, so it is possible that there is a large class of interacting quantum systems presenting a much faster rate of convergence, up to exponentially fast (see
Rai et al. 2024
for numerical evidence for small system sizes). This in itself could have relevant consequences for the thermalization of chaotic quantum systems
Wilming et al. 2018
.

Appendix A
The Esseen inequality

For completeness, in this section we present a proof of Esseen’s inequality (see Eq. (
12
)). The presentation closely follows
Esseen 1945
. A similar proof can also be found in
(
Kolassa 1997
, Theorem 2.5.2)
.

Theorem 3

(Esseen’s inequality)
.

Let
F(y)
be a real, non-decreasing function, and let
G(y)
be a real function of bounded variation. Suppose
F(-\infty)=G(-\infty)=0
and
F(\infty)=G(\infty)
. Moreover, denote by
\hat{f}(\omega)
and
\hat{g}(\omega)
their corresponding Fourier transform, and assume that there exist three positive finite constants
A
,
\Omega
and
\varepsilon
such that:

1.

for all
y
, it holds that
|G^{\prime}(y)|\leq A
;

2.

the following integral is bounded:

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\int_{0}^{\Omega}{\rm d}\omega\;\frac{|\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)|}{|\omega|}\,.

(86)

Then, for every
k>1
there exists a finite constant
c(k)
(which only depends on
k
) such that:

\displaystyle\Delta=\sup_{y}\,|F(y)-G(y)|\leq\frac{c(k)A}{\Omega}+\left(\frac{k}{k-1}\right)\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}\,.

(87)

We can apply this Theorem to prove Eq. (
12
) since
F_{\widehat{H}}(y)
is non-decreasing (being a cumulative function),
G(y)
defined in Eq. (
10
) is of bounded variation, and satisfies
|G^{\prime}(y)|\leq(2\pi)^{-1/2}
. Then, setting
k=2
and
C:=((2\pi)^{-1/2}c(2))
gives Eq. (
12
).

Proof.

We begin by rewriting the difference
\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)
as:

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}(F(y)-G(y))\;e^{i\omega y}=-i\omega\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\;(F(y)-G(y))e^{i\omega y}\,,

(88)

where in the last step we integrated by parts and used the fact that
F(-\infty)=G(-\infty)=0
and
F(\infty)=G(\infty)
. Then, by Fourier inversion, we obtain that:

\displaystyle F(y)-G(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega\;e^{-i\omega y}\,\frac{\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)}{-i\omega}\,.

(89)

Then, if one could assume that the integrand in the right-hand side was absolutely integrable, the result would follow by setting
\Omega=\infty
. For this reason, the rest of the proof can be considered as an estimation of the error made by restricting Eq. (
89
) to a finite interval.

To this end, without loss of generality, consider
|F(y)-G(y)|
to reach its maximum at zero (if this is not the case, one can traslate the origin to the point, which only affects the Fourier transform by a phase factor). Moreover, for the moment we assume that
A=\Omega=1
.
Then, let us introduce a filter function
H(y)
with Fourier transform
\hat{h}(\omega)
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

H(y)
and
\hat{h}(\omega)
are real and non-negative;

2.

\hat{h}(0)=1
(that is, the integral of
H(y)
is normalized);

3.

there exists a finite constant
b
such that:

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\;|y|H(y)=b\,;

(90)

4.

\hat{h}(\omega)
has support on
[-1,1]
and it is bounded by
1
, i.e.,
\hat{h}(\omega)\leq 1
.

We present here a choice of such a function, but the argument is independent of the specific choice. Define
\hat{k}(\omega)
as:

\displaystyle\hat{k}(\omega):=\begin{cases}1-|\omega|&{\text{if $\omega\in[-1,1]$}}\\
0&{\text{otherwise}}\end{cases}\,,

(91)

and let
K(y)
be its Fourier transform:

\displaystyle K(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega\;e^{-i\omega y}\hat{k}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{\sin(y/2)}{(y/2)}\right)^{2}\,.

(92)

Then, we define:

\displaystyle H(y):=\frac{2}{3\pi}\,K(y/2)^{2}\;;\qquad\qquad\qquad\hat{h}(\omega)=\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tilde{\omega}\;\hat{k}(2\omega-\tilde{\omega})\hat{k}(\tilde{\omega})}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tilde{\omega}\;\hat{k}(\tilde{\omega})^{2}}\,,

(93)

Due to the connection with
\hat{k}(\omega)
, one can verify the properties of
\hat{h}(\omega)
by inspection, while the property
of
H(y)
follows from the fact that
(yK(y)^{2})
decays as
y^{-3}
at infinity.

Consider the convolution between
(F(y)-G(y))
and
H(y)
. This satisfies the property that, for arbitrary
x
:

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\;H(x-y)

\displaystyle(F(y)-G(y))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega\;H(x-y)\,\left(e^{-i\omega y}\,\frac{\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)}{-i\omega}\right)=

(94)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega\;e^{-i\omega x}\,\hat{h}(\omega)\left(\frac{\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)}{-i\omega}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\omega\;e^{-i\omega x}\,\hat{h}(\omega)\left(\frac{\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)}{-i\omega}\right)\,,

(95)

where in the first step we used the identity in Eq. (
89
), then the fact that
\hat{h}(\omega)
is the Fourier transform of
H(y)
, and finally the fact that
\hat{h}(\omega)
has support only on
[-1,1]
(see condition
). The relation just obtained allows us to prove the upper-bound:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\;H(x-y)(F(y)-G(y))\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm d}\omega\;|\hat{h}(\omega)|\frac{\left|\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)\right|}{|\omega|}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}\omega\;\frac{\left|\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega)\right|}{|\omega|}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}\,,

(96)

where in the second inequality we used the fact that
\hat{h}(\omega)\leq 1
(condition
), together with the fact that
\hat{f}(\omega)
and
\hat{g}(\omega)
are Fourier transform of real functions, which implies that
(\hat{f}(-\omega)-\hat{g}(-\omega))=\overline{(\hat{f}(\omega)-\hat{g}(\omega))}
.

Suppose now that
F(0)\geq G(0)
(the other case can be treated symmetrically). Then, by definition
F(0)-G(0)=\Delta
. Moreover, since
F(y)
is non-decreasing and
G(y)
has derivative bounded by
A=1
, in the interval
y\in[0,\Delta]
, it also holds that:

\displaystyle F(y)-G(y)\geq\Delta-y\geq 0\,,\qquad\qquad{\text{for $y\in[0,\Delta]$}}\,.

(97)

Then, we can also lower bound Eq. (
96
) as:

\displaystyle\left|\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}+\int^{\Delta}_{0}+\int_{\Delta}^{\infty}\right){\rm d}y\;H(x-y)(F(y)-G(y))\right|\geq

(98)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\int^{\Delta}_{0}{\rm d}y\;H(x-y)(F(y)-G(y))-\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}+\int_{\Delta}^{\infty}\right){\rm d}y\;H(x-y)|F(y)-G(y)|\geq

(99)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\int^{\Delta}_{0}{\rm d}y\;H(x-y)(\Delta-y)-\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}+\int_{\Delta}^{\infty}\right){\rm d}y\;H(x-y)\Delta\,,

(100)

where in the last line we used the relation in Eq. (
97
) together with the property
|F(y)-G(y)|\leq\Delta
. It should also be noticed that thanks to condition
, we can rewrite Eq. (
100
) as:

Eq. (
100
)

\displaystyle=\left(\int^{\Delta}_{0}{\rm d}y\;H(x-y)(2\Delta-y)\right)-\Delta=\left(\int^{\Delta-x}_{-x}{\rm d}y\;H(-y)(2\Delta-x-y)\right)-\Delta\geq

(101)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int^{\Delta-x}_{-x}{\rm d}y\;H(-y)(2\Delta-x)\right)-\Delta-b\,,

(102)

where in the last step we used the assumption
. At this point, introduce
m\in[0,1]
, such that
x=m\Delta
. Putting everything together, it holds that:

\displaystyle\left(\int^{\Delta-x}_{-x}{\rm d}y\;H(-y)(2\Delta-x)\right)-\Delta=\Delta\left(\left((2-m)\int^{\Delta(1-m)}_{-\Delta m}{\rm d}y\;H(-y)\right)-1\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}+b\,.

(103)

Let us focus in the expression of the integral in the second inequality. It should be noticed that thanks to the normalization of
H(y)
, for any
k>1
we can find a
m(k)
small enough and a
\alpha(k)
large enough such that:

\displaystyle\left((2-m(k))\int^{\alpha(k)(1-m(k))}_{-\alpha(k)m(k)}{\rm d}y\;H(-y)\right)-1=1-\frac{1}{k}=\frac{k-1}{k}\,.

(104)

Then, for a fixed
k
, there are two possibilities: if
\Delta>\alpha(k)
, we have:

\displaystyle\left((2-m(k))\int^{\Delta(1-m(k))}_{-\Delta m(k)}{\rm d}y\;H(-y)\right)-1\geq\left((2-m(k))\int^{\alpha(k)(1-m(k))}_{-\alpha(k)m(k)}{\rm d}y\;H(-y)\right)-1=\frac{1}{k}\,,

(105)

where we implicitly used the positivity of
H(y)
. Plugging this inequality in Eq. (
103
), and rearranging, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}+b\qquad\implies\qquad\Delta\leq\left(\frac{k}{k-1}\right)\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}+b\right)\,.

(106)

On the other hand, if
\Delta\leq\alpha(k)
, we can use a trivial upper-bound. In this way, we have:

\displaystyle\Delta\leq\max\left\{\left(\frac{k}{k-1}\right)\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}+b\right),\,\alpha(k)\right\}\leq\left(\frac{k}{k-1}\right)\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}+\left(b\left(\frac{k}{k-1}\right)+\alpha(k)\right)\,.

(107)

This proves the claim for
A=\Omega=1
with
c(k):=\left(bk/(k-1)+\alpha(k)\right)
.

In order to prove the general case, let us introduce the two rescaled functions:

\displaystyle F_{1}(y):=\frac{\Omega}{A}F\left(\frac{y}{\Omega}\right)\,;\qquad\qquad\qquad G_{1}(y):=\frac{\Omega}{A}G\left(\frac{y}{\Omega}\right)

(108)

with Fourier transform
\hat{f}_{1}(\omega)
and
\hat{g}_{1}(\omega)
which satisfy:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}\omega\;\frac{|\hat{f}_{1}(\omega)-\hat{g}_{1}(\omega)|}{|\omega|}=\frac{\Omega\,\varepsilon}{A}\,,

(109)

and
|G_{1}^{\prime}(y)|\leq 1
. Then, we can apply Eq. (
107
), which gives us:

\displaystyle|F_{1}(y)-G_{1}(y)|\leq c(k)+\frac{k\,\Omega\,\varepsilon}{A\pi}\qquad\implies\qquad|F(y)-G(y)|\leq\frac{c(k)A}{\Omega}+\frac{k\varepsilon}{\pi}\,.

(110)

This proves the claim for arbitrary
A
and
\Omega
.
∎

C.1

Our starting point is the derivative of Eq. (

[A,B]_{n}
for the nested commutator, inductively defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}[A,B]_{0}=B\\
[A,B]_{n}=[A,[A,B]_{n-1}]\end{cases}\,.

(189)

From its explicit expression in Eq. (
186
) it can be noticed that
\theta(0)=0
, which implies condition
, i.e.,
\zeta^{(1)}(0)=0
, Moreover, for
m\geq 1
one has:

\displaystyle\theta^{(m)}(\omega)=(i)^{m-1}e^{i\omega X}[X,Y]_{m}e^{-i\omega X}\,.

(190)

Then, by deriving Eq. (
186
), we get an expression for the
(n+1)
-th derivative of
\zeta(\omega)
:

\displaystyle\zeta^{(n+1)}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\,\zeta^{(n-k)}(\omega)\Theta^{(k)}(\omega)=

(191)

\displaystyle=\zeta^{(n)}\Theta(\omega)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(i)^{k}\zeta^{(n-k)}(\omega)[Y,(e^{i\omega Y}\theta(\omega)e^{-i\omega Y})]_{k}+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{m}\,(i)^{k-1}\zeta^{(n-k)}(\omega)[Y,(e^{i\omega Y}e^{i\omega X}[X,Y]_{m}e^{-i\omega X}e^{-i\omega Y})]_{k-m}\,,

(192)

This relation allows us to recursively compute
\zeta^{(n+1)}(\omega)
in terms of derivatives of at most order
n
. In particular, the
(n+1)
-th derivative evaluated in
0
is given by:

\displaystyle\zeta^{(n+1)}(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\binom{n}{n-k,k-m,m}\,(i)^{k-1}\zeta^{(n-k)}(0)\;[Y,[X,Y]_{m}]_{k-m}\,.

(193)

where we used the fact that
\Theta(0)=\theta(0)=0
. This expression is the main result of this section.

C.2
Support of
\zeta^{M}(\omega)

In this section, we use Eq. (
193
) to characterize the support of
\zeta^{M}(\omega)
as:

\displaystyle{\rm supp}(\zeta^{M}(\omega))\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,\mathcal{S})\leq 2R(M-2)\}\,;

(194)

where
\mathcal{S}
is the support of
[X,\,Y]
. This is done by induction. First, it should be noticed that for
M=2
, we have:

\displaystyle\zeta^{M=2}(\omega)=\mathds{I}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}\zeta^{(2)}(0)=\mathds{I}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}\,[X,Y]\,,

(195)

which proves the induction basis. Then, suppose that Eq. (
194
) holds for some
M
. Then, for
M+1
, since
\zeta^{M+1}(\omega)=\zeta^{M}(\omega)+\frac{\omega^{M+1}}{(M+1)!}\zeta^{(M+1)}(0)
we have:

\displaystyle{\rm supp}(\zeta^{M+1}(\omega))\subseteq{\rm supp}(\zeta^{M}(\omega))\cup{\rm supp}(\zeta^{(M+1)}(\omega))\,.

(196)

Then, the claim would follow if we could prove that
{\rm supp}(\zeta^{(M+1)}(\omega))\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,\mathcal{S})\leq 2(M-1)R\}
. To this end, it is sufficient to show that:

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\left([Y,[X,Y]_{m}]_{n-m}\right)\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,\mathcal{S})\leq 2R(n-1)\}\,.

(197)

Then, using Eq. (
193
) gives the claim in
, since:

\displaystyle\zeta^{(M+1)}(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\binom{n}{n-k,k-m,m}\,(i)^{k-1}\zeta^{(n-k)}(0)\;[Y,[X,Y]_{m}]_{k-m}

(198)

contains nested commutator of order at most
M
.

It remains to prove Eq. (
197
), which we do by induction. In particular, let
Z=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{X}}Z_{i}
and
H=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{X}}H_{i}
be a sum of
R
-local observables. Then, it holds that:

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\left([Z,H]_{n}\right)\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,{\rm supp}\left(H\right))\leq 2R\,n\}\,.

(199)

Indeed, for
n=0
this is trivial. Then, suppose it holds for some integer
n
, and we want to prove it for
n+1
. It should be noticed that we can rewrite
[Z,H]_{n+1}
as:

\displaystyle[Z,H]_{n+1}=[Z,[Z,H]_{n}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{X}\\
d(i,\mathcal{S}_{n}^{H})\leq 2R\end{subarray}}[Z_{i},[Z,H]_{n}]\,,

(200)

where we introduced the notation
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{H}
for the support of
[Z,H]_{n}
, and we used the fact that the only non-commuting terms can arise when
Z_{i}
is at most at distance
2R
from
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{H}
(each of the terms in
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{H}
acts on a ball of at most radius
R
around its site, and the same holds for
Z_{i}
). Then, we obtain:

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\left([Z,H]_{n+1}\right)\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,\mathcal{S}_{n}^{H})\leq 2\,R\}\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,{\rm supp}\left(H\right))\leq 2R\,(n+1)\}\,,

(201)

where in the last step we applied the triangle inequality. This proves Eq. (
199
). With this result in hand, it directly follows that:

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\left([X,Y]_{m}\right)={\rm supp}\left([X,[X,Y]]_{m-1}\right)\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,\mathcal{S})\leq 2R(m-1)\}\,,

(202)

and that:

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\left([Y,[X,Y]_{m}]_{n-m}\right)\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,{\rm supp}\left([X,Y]_{m}\right))\leq 2R(n-m)\}\subseteq\{i|\,d(i,\mathcal{S})\leq 2R(n-1)\}\,,

(203)

where, once again, we used the triangle inequality to prove the last implication.

C.3
Operator norm

The main ingredient of this section is the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|[Y,[X,Y]_{m}]_{n-m}\|\leq 2BC_{X\cap Y}\,(n!\,\lambda^{n})\,.

(204)

where
\lambda:=(2c_{D}(2R)^{D}B)
. This allows us to recursively estimate the norm of
\zeta^{(n)}(0)
. Indeed, starting from Eq. (
193
), we have:

\displaystyle\|\zeta^{(n+1)}(0)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\binom{n}{n-k,k-m,m}\,\|\zeta^{(n-k)}(0)\|\;\|[Y,[X,Y]_{m}]_{k-m}\|\leq

(205)

\displaystyle\leq 2BC_{X\cap Y}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\|\zeta^{(n-k)}(0)\|\,\lambda^{k}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{k}\binom{k}{m}\right)\leq

(206)

\displaystyle\leq((n+1)!(2\lambda)^{n+1})\,\left(\frac{BC_{X\cap Y}}{(2\lambda)(n+1)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\|\zeta^{(n-k)}(0)\|}{((n-k)!(2\lambda)^{n-k})}\right)\,,

(207)

which can be rearranged as:

\displaystyle\frac{\|\zeta^{(n+1)}(0)\|}{((n+1)!(2\lambda)^{n+1})}\leq\frac{BC_{X\cap Y}}{(2\lambda)(n+1)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\|\zeta^{(n-k)}(0)\|}{((n-k)!(2\lambda)^{n-k})}\right)\,.

(208)

Let us introduce the constant
\gamma:=\max\{1,\frac{C_{X\cap Y}}{(4c_{D}(2R)^{D})}\}
. The expression just obtained allows us to derive the upper bound:

\displaystyle\|\zeta^{(n)}(0)\|\leq((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma})^{n}(n!)\,.

(209)

First, notice that
\|\zeta^{(0)}(0)\|=1
and
\|\zeta^{(1)}(0)\|=0
. Then, by induction on Eq. (
208
) we have:

\displaystyle\frac{\|\zeta^{(n+1)}(0)\|}{((n+1)!(2\lambda)^{n+1})}\leq\frac{\gamma}{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\|\zeta^{(n-k)}(0)\|}{((n-k)!(2\lambda)^{n-k})}\leq\frac{\gamma}{n+1}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\,\gamma^{\frac{n-k}{2}}\right)\leq\frac{\gamma^{1+\frac{n-1}{2}}(n-1)}{n+1}\leq\gamma^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\,,

(210)

where we implicitly used the fact that
1\leq\gamma\implies\gamma^{\frac{n-k}{2}}\leq\gamma^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
. This proves Eq. (
182
). Then, as a direct consequence we also have:

\displaystyle\|\zeta^{M}(\omega)\|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{M}\;\frac{|\omega|^{n}}{n!}\|\zeta^{(n)}(0)\|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{M}\;((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}|\omega|)^{n}\leq\frac{1-((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}|\omega|)^{M+1}}{1-((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}|\omega|)}\,,

(211)

which proves Eq. (
183
).

At this point, let us assume that
\omega\in[0,\,\frac{1}{2(2\lambda)\,\sqrt{\gamma}})
. In this regime,
((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}\omega)\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then, it follows from Eq. (
211
) that:

\displaystyle\|\zeta^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{M+1}}\right)\,,

(212)

and that:

\displaystyle\|\zeta(\omega)-\zeta^{M}(\omega)\|\leq\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\;\frac{|\omega|^{n}}{n!}\|\zeta^{(n)}(0)\|\leq\frac{((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}\omega)^{M+1}}{1-((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}\,\omega)}\leq 2((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}\,\omega)^{M+1}\,.

(213)

This proves the first two items of Eq. (
184
). Moreover, consider now the
k
-th derivative of
\zeta^{M}(\omega)
. Then, in the same range, it also holds that:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\omega}^{(k)}\,(\zeta^{M}(\omega))\|\leq\sum_{n=k}^{M}\frac{|\omega|^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}\|\zeta^{(n)}(0)\|\leq k!((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}\,)^{k}\,\sum_{n=k}^{M}\binom{n}{k}((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}\omega)^{n-k}\leq 2\,(k!)\,((2\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma}\,)^{k}\,

(214)

where in the last step we used the identity
\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}(1/2)^{n-k}=2^{k+1}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
.

Then, it remains to prove Eq. (
204
).
As in the previous section, let
Z^{1}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{X}}Z_{i}
,
Z^{2}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{X}}Z_{i}
and
H=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{X}}H_{i}
to be three arbitrary operators that are sum of
R
-local observables, which satisfy
\|Z^{1}_{i}\|,\,\|Z^{2}_{i}\|\leq B
and
\|H_{i}\|\leq\tilde{B}
. Then, we can give a first estimate:

\displaystyle\|[Z^{1},[Z^{2},H]_{\tilde{m}}]_{\tilde{n}}\|\leq\sum_{i_{0},\dots,i_{n}}\|[Z^{1}_{i_{\tilde{m}+\tilde{n}}},[Z^{1}_{i_{\tilde{m}+\tilde{n}-1}},\dots[Z^{1}_{i_{\tilde{m}+1}},[Z^{2}_{i_{\tilde{m}}},\dots,[Z^{2}_{i_{1}},H_{i_{0}}]]]\|\leq 2^{n+1}\tilde{B}\,B^{\tilde{m}+\tilde{n}}\#_{\tilde{m}+\tilde{n}}\,.

(215)

where we denote by
\#_{n}
is the number of non-zero commutators of local terms at order
n
. This number can be estimated by using the locality of
Z^{1}
,
Z^{2}
and
H
. Let
\tilde{Z}_{i_{k}}
be either
Z^{1}_{i_{k}}
or
Z^{2}_{i_{k}}
, depending on whether
k>\tilde{m}
or not. Then, it should be noticed that the terms in the sum are non-zero if and only if for all
k\in\{1,\dots,\tilde{m}+\tilde{n}\}
, the support of
\tilde{Z}_{i_{k}}
intersects the support of
[\tilde{Z}_{i_{k-1}},\dots[\tilde{Z}_{i_{1}},H_{i_{0}}]]
. This allows to estimate the number of terms in the sum by reducing it to a combinatorial problem. Indeed, suppose that we have a sequence of indices
\{i_{k},\dots,i_{0}\}
corresponding to a non-zero commutator. This corresponds to at most
(k+1)
different points over the lattice. When adding an additional term, say
i_{k+1}
, in order for the total commutator not to be zero,
i_{k+1}
must be inside one of the
2R
-ball around any of the (at most)
k+1
different points already present in the lattice. Then, this means that:

\displaystyle\#_{k+1}\leq(k+1)c_{D}(2R)^{D}\,\#_{k}\,\qquad\implies\qquad\#_{k+1}\leq((k+1)!)(c_{D}(2R)^{D})^{k}\,\#_{0}\,.

(216)

Finally, it should be noticed that
\#_{0}
simply corresponds to the number of sites in the support of
H
. This prove that:

\displaystyle\|[Z,H]_{n}\|\leq 2\tilde{B}(({\tilde{m}+\tilde{n}})!)(2c_{D}(2R)^{D}B)^{\tilde{m}+\tilde{n}}|{\rm supp}\left(H\right)|\,.

(217)

Applying this expression to Eq. (
204
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\|[Y,[X,Y]_{m}]_{n-m}\|

\displaystyle=\|[Y,[X,([X,Y])]_{m-1}]_{n-m}\|\leq\|[X,Y]\|((n-1)!)\lambda^{n-1}|{\rm supp}\left([X,Y]\right)|\leq

(218)

\displaystyle\leq 2BC_{X\cap Y}((n-1)!)\lambda^{n}\leq 2BC_{X\cap Y}(n!)\lambda^{n}\,.

(219)

This concludes the proof.

C.4
Proof of Lemma

We are now ready to show how Lemma
is a direct consequence of Lemma
. Indeed, both
R_{m,j}^{\infty}(\omega)
and
S_{m,j}^{\infty}(\omega)
are of the form in Eq. (

\displaystyle R^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega):=e^{-i\omega(z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)-z_{j}^{\ell}(m))}e^{i\omega z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)}e^{-i\omega z_{j}^{\ell}(m)}\,;\qquad S^{\infty}_{m,j}(\omega):=e^{i\omega(\widehat{H}-z^{\ell}_{j}(m))}e^{i\omega z^{\ell}_{j}(m)}e^{-i\omega\widehat{H}}\,.

(220)

Then, let us define
X_{R}:=-z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)
and
Y_{R}=z_{j}^{\ell}(m)
, together with
X_{S}:=-z_{j}^{\ell}(m)
and
Y_{S}=\widehat{H}
. In order to apply Lemma

B=\frac{E}{\sigma_{H}}

\mathcal{S}_{R}:={\rm supp}([X_{R},Y_{R}])
and
\mathcal{S}_{S}:={\rm supp}([X_{S},Y_{S}])

In the first case we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{R}

\displaystyle={\rm supp}([\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(m)+z_{j}^{\ell}(m),z_{j}^{\ell}(m)])={\rm supp}([\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(m),z_{j}^{\ell}(m)])\subseteq

(221)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\subseteq\{i|\,\exists k,\,d(j,k)\in[2R\ell(m-1),2R\ell m],\,d(i,k)\leq 2R\}\,,

(222)

where we used the fact that
z_{j}^{\ell}(m-1)=\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(m)+z_{j}^{\ell}(m)
. In other words, the set in Eq. (
222
) comprises all the points that are at a distance at most
2R
from the support of
\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(m)
. This expression can be further simplified using the triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{R}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{i|d(j,i)\in[2R(\ell(m-1)-1),2R(\ell m+1)]\}\,,

(223)

Then, following Sec.

\displaystyle{\rm supp}(R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega))\subseteq\{i|d(j,i)\in[2R(\ell(m-1)-M),2R(\ell\,m+M)]\}\,.

(224)

A similar discussion also gives:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{S}

\displaystyle={\rm supp}\left(\left[z_{j}^{\ell}(m),\sum_{k=1}^{m}\,\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(k)+z_{j}^{\ell}(m)\right]\right)={\rm supp}\left(\left[z_{j}^{\ell}(m),\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(m)\right]\right)\subseteq

(225)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\subseteq\{i|d(j,i)\in[2R(\ell(m-1)-1),2R(\ell m+1)]\}\,,

(226)

where we implicitly used the fact that
d({\rm supp}(\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(k)),{\rm supp}(z_{j}^{\ell}(m)))>2R\ell(m-k)
to commute the elements in the sum with
k\neq m
. This shows that
\mathcal{S}_{S}=\mathcal{S}_{R}
. Then, this also means that
C_{X\cap Y}=C_{X_{R}\cap Y_{R}}=C_{X_{S}\cap Y_{S}}
. Using directly Eq. (
217
), we get
C_{X\cap Y}=|\widehat{H}_{j}^{\ell}(m)|\leq c_{D}(2R\ell)^{D}m^{D-1}
.

We are now ready to use Lemma
. First, it should be noticed that:

\displaystyle\|R^{(m)}_{m,j}(0)\|,\,\|S^{(m)}_{m,j}(0)\|\leq\left(\frac{\Gamma E\,\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}\right)^{n}(n!)

(227)

where we introduced the constant
\Gamma=\max\{(4c_{D}(2R)^{D}),(2c_{D}^{2}(2R)^{D})\}
. This expression directly implies that:

\displaystyle\|R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|,\,\|S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq\frac{1-\left(\frac{\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}(E|\omega|)\right)^{M+1}}{1-\left(\frac{\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}(E|\omega|)\right)}\,.

(228)

Moreover, let
\omega\leq\Omega_{1}=\left(\frac{\sigma_{H}}{2E\,\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}K^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\right)
. Carrying out the same computations as in Sec.
, in this regime we have:

\displaystyle\|R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{M+1}}\right)\,;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\|S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{M+1}}\right)\,;

(229)

\displaystyle\|R_{m,j}^{\infty}(\omega)-R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(\frac{\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}(E|\omega|)\right)^{M+1}\,;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\|S_{m,j}^{\infty}(\omega)-R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\left(\frac{\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}(E|\omega|)\right)^{M+1}\,;

(230)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{(k)}_{\omega}R_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\,(k!)\,\left(\frac{E\,\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}\right)^{k}\,;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\|\partial^{(k)}_{\omega}S_{m,j}^{M}(\omega)\|\leq 2\,(k!)\,\left(\frac{E\,\Gamma\ell^{\frac{D}{2}}m^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}{\sigma_{H}}\right)^{k}\,.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
