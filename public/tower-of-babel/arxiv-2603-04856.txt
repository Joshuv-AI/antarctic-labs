Generalized Gorenstein Categories
1

Abstract




Abstract

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category and let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
be additive subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
.
As a generalization of Gorenstein categories, we introduce one-sided
n
-
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
-Gorenstein categories with
n\geq 0
.
Under certain conditions, we give some equivalent characterizations of one-sided
n
-
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
-Gorenstein categories
in term of the finiteness of projective and injective dimensions relative to one-sided Gorenstein subcategories, which
induce some new equivalent characterizations of Gorenstein categories. Then we apply these results to categories of interest.
In particular, a necessary condition is obtained for the validity of the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture.

Key Words:
One-sided Gorenstein categories, One-sided Gorenstein subcategories,
Relative projective dimension, Relative injective dimension, Auslander classes, Bass classes.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
18G25, 16E10.

Introduction

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category and
\mathscr{C}
an additive subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
. As a common generalization
of Gorenstein projective and Gorenstein injective modules, Sather-Wagstaff, Sharif and White
[
31
]
introduced the
Gorenstein subcategory
\mathcal{G}(\mathscr{C})
of
\mathscr{A}
relative to
\mathscr{C}
.
From the definition of the Gorenstein subcategory
\mathcal{G}(\mathscr{C})
,
it is known that
\mathscr{C}
should be simultaneously a generator and a cogenerator for
\mathcal{G}(\mathscr{C})

and both functors
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{C},-)
and
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathscr{A}}(-,\mathscr{C})
should possess certain exactness.
It leads to some limitations. To overcome such limitations, Song, Zhao and Huang
[
33
]
introduced
one-sided Gorenstein subcategories by modifying the definition of Gorenstein subcategories, and then
Gao and Wu
[
13
]
generalized them to a more general setting. On the other hand, Beligiannis and Reiten
[
3
]

introduced Gorenstein categories by categorizing Gorenstein rings, and compare the relevant results about Gorenstein rings
to Gorenstein categories. It is a natural topic to study the relation between Gorenstein categories and
(one-sided) Gorenstein subcategories, we will study it in a more general setting.

In homological theory, homological dimensions are important and fundamental invariants,
which play a crucial role in studying the structures of modules and rings.
The properties of modules and rings have been studied when certain homological dimensions
relative to some special one-sided Gorenstein subcategories of a given module or all modules
are finite, see
[
8
,
13
,
14
,
19
,
22
,
33
]
, and so on.
In this paper, we will introduce generalized Gorenstein categories and give them some equivalent characterizations
in term of the finiteness of certain homological dimensions relative to one-sided Gorenstein subcategories,
which provide a unified framework for some related results.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we give some notions and preliminary results which will be used in the sequel.

In this section,
R
is an arbitrary ring and
{}_{R}C
is Wakamatsu tilting module with
S=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(_{R}C)
.
For any subcategory
\mathscr{X}
of
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
and
n\geq 0
, we use
\mathscr{X}\text{-}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits^{\leq n}(R)
and
\mathscr{X}\text{-}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits^{\leq n}(R)

to denote the subcategories of
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
consisting of modules with
\mathscr{X}
-projective and
\mathscr{X}
-injective dimensions
at most
n
, respectively. We write

(-)^{+}:=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Z}}(-,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),

where
\mathbb{Z}
is the additive group of integers and
\mathbb{Q}
is the additive group of rational numbers.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a coresolving subcategory of
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
closed under direct summands, and
let
\mathscr{Y}
be a resolving subcategory of
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R^{op}
closed under direct summands.
Assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

For a module
X\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
, it holds that
X\in\mathscr{X}
if and only if
X^{+}\in\mathscr{Y}
;

(b)

For a module
Y\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R^{op}
, it holds that
Y\in\mathscr{Y}
if and only if
Y^{+}\in\mathscr{X}
.

Then we have

(1)

\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M=\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M^{+}
for any
M\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
.

(2)

\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits N=\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits N^{+}
for any
N\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R^{op}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
M\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
. We first prove
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M^{+}\leq\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M
. If
\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M=\infty
,
then the assertion follows trivially. Now suppose that
\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M=n<\infty
and

0\to M\to X^{0}\to X^{1}\to\cdots\to X^{n}\to 0

is an exact sequence in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
with all
X^{i}
in
\mathscr{X}
. It yields the following exact sequence

0\to{X^{n}}^{+}\to\cdots\to{X^{1}}^{+}\to{X^{0}}^{+}\to M^{+}\to 0

in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R^{op}
. By assumption, we have that all
{X^{i}}^{+}
are in
\mathscr{Y}
and

\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M^{+}\leq n=\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M
.

In the following, we prove
\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M\leq\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M^{+}
. If
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M^{+}=\infty
,
then the assertion follows trivially. Now suppose that
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M^{+}=n<\infty
and

0\to M\to I^{0}\to I^{1}\to\cdots\to I^{n-1}\to X^{n}\to 0

is an exact sequence in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
with all
I^{i}
injective. It yields the following exact sequence

0\to{X^{n}}^{+}\to{I^{n-1}}^{+}\to\cdots\to{I^{1}}^{+}\to{I^{0}}^{+}\to M^{+}\to 0

in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R^{op}
. Since all
I^{i}
are in
\mathscr{X}
,
we have that all
{I^{i}}^{+}
are in
\mathscr{Y}
.
Since
\mathscr{Y}
is resolving and closed under direct summands, we have
{X^{n}}^{+}\in\mathscr{Y}

by
[
19
, Lemma 3.1(1)]
. Then
X^{n}\in\mathscr{X}
by assumption, and thus

\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M\leq n=\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M^{+}
.

(2) It is dual to (1).
∎

Recall from
[
8
,
14
]
an exact sequence

0\to K\to M\to L\to 0

in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
is called
pure exact
if

0\to A\otimes_{R}K\to A\otimes_{R}M\to A\otimes_{R}L\to 0

is exact for any
A\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R^{op}
. In this case,
K
and
L
are called a
pure submodule

and a
pure quotient
of
M
, respectively. As a consequence of Proposition
4.1
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.2
.

It holds that

(1)

Under the assumptions in Proposition
4.1
,
for any
n\geq 0
, both
\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits^{\leq n}(R)
and
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits^{\leq n}(R^{op})

are closed under pure submodules and pure quotients.

(2)

For any
n\geq 0
, both
\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits^{\leq n}
and
\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits^{\leq n}

are closed under pure submodules and pure quotients.

Proof.

(1) Let

0\to K\to M\to L\to 0

be a pure exact sequence in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
with
\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M\leq n
.
Then by
[
8
, Proposition 5.3.8]
, the induced exact sequence

0\to L^{+}\to M^{+}\to K^{+}\to 0

splits and both
K^{+}
and
L^{+}
are direct summands of
M^{+}
.
By Proposition
4.1
(1), we have
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M^{+}\leq n
.
Since
\mathscr{Y}
is resolving and closed under direct summands,
we have that
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits^{\leq n}
is closed under direct summands by Lemma
2.1
(1).
It follows that
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits K^{+}\leq n
and
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits L^{+}\leq n
.
Thus
\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits K\leq n
and
\mathscr{X}
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits L\leq n

by Proposition
4.1
(1) again.

Similarly, we have that
\mathscr{Y}
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits^{\leq n}(R^{op})
is closed under pure submodules and pure quotients.

(2) By
[
16
, Theorem 6.2 and Proposition 4.2(a)]
, we have that
both
\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)
and
\mathcal{B}_{C}(S^{op})
are coresolving and closed under direct summands, and
both
\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)
and
\mathcal{A}_{C}(R^{op})
are resolving and closed under direct summands.
On the other hand, by
[
20
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have

(a)

a module
B\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
(respectively,
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S^{op}
), it holds that
B\in\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)

(respectively,
\mathcal{B}_{C}(S^{op})
)
if and only if
B^{+}\in\mathcal{A}_{C}(R^{op})
(respectively,
\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)
);

(b)

a module
A\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R^{op}
(respectively,
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S
), it holds that
A\in\mathcal{A}_{C}(R^{op})

(respectively,
\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)
)
if and only if
A^{+}\in\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)
(respectively,
\mathcal{B}_{C}(S^{op})
).

Now the assertion follows from (1).
∎

For a left
R
-module
M
, we use
E(M)

to denote the injective envelope of
M
, and write

\mathop{\rm FP\text{-}id}\nolimits_{R}M:=\mathcal{FI}(R){\text{-}}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits M.

The following result shows that the assumption “
R
is a left coherent ring”
in
[
21
, Proposition 3.3 and Theorem 3.4]
is superfluous.

Proposition 4.3
.

It holds that

(1)

If
M\in\mathcal{FI}(R)
, then
M^{+}
is a direct summand of
E(M)^{+}
and

{M_{*}}^{+}
is a direct summand of
{E(M)_{*}}^{+}
.

(2)

\mathcal{FI}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)
and
\mathcal{FI}_{C}(S)
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits^{<\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)\subseteq{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)}
.

(3)

\mathop{\rm FP\text{-}id}\nolimits_{R}C\otimes_{S}N\leq\mathcal{FI}_{C}(S)
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits N
for any
N\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S
; the equality holds true if
N\in\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)
.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathcal{FI}(R)
. Then
M
is a pure submodule of
E(M)
.

(1) By using an argument similar to that in the proof of Corollary
4.2
(1),
we get that
M^{+}
is a direct summand of
E(M)^{+}
,
and hence
M^{+}\otimes_{R}C
is a direct summand of
E(M)^{+}\otimes_{R}C
. Since

{M_{*}}^{+}\cong M^{+}\otimes_{R}C
and
{E(M)_{*}}^{+}\cong E(M)^{+}\otimes_{R}C
by

[
10
, Lemma 1.16(c)]
, it follows that
{M_{*}}^{+}
is a direct summand of
{E(M)_{*}}^{+}
.

(2) Since
E(M)\in\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)
by
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
, we have
M\in\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)

by Corollary
4.2
(2), and thus
\mathcal{FI}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)
. Then by
[
16
, Proposition 4.1]
,
we have
\mathcal{FI}_{C}(S)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)
. It follows from
[
16
, Theorem 6.2]

and
[
38
, Corollary 3.5(2)]
that

\mathcal{FI}_{C}(S)
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits^{<\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)\subseteq{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)}
.

(3) When
R
is a left coherent ring, the assertion (3) was proved in
[
21
, Theorem 3.4]
and its proof
depends on that the containment
\mathcal{FI}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)
holds true. In view that

\mathcal{FI}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)
over arbitrary rings by (2),
the argument in the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 3.4]
is valid in our setting.
∎

The following observation is useful.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a subclass of
\widetilde{\mathop{\rm res}\nolimits\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)}
,
and let
\mathscr{T}
be a subclass of
\widetilde{\mathop{\rm cores}\nolimits\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)}
. It holds that

(1)

\mathcal{P}(R)\subseteq r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathscr{H})
and
\mathcal{I}(S)\subseteq l\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S),\mathscr{T})
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)\subseteq\mathscr{H}
, then
r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathscr{H})
is resolving.
If
\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)\subseteq\mathscr{T}
, then
l\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S),\mathscr{T})
is coresolving.

Proof.

(1) Let
P\in\mathcal{P}(R)
and
I\in\mathcal{I}(S)
. Since
r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R))
is resolving
and
l\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S))
is coresolving (cf.
[
19
, Remark 4.4(3)]
),
there exists a
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{R}(-,\mathcal{P}_{C}(R))
-exact exact sequence

0\to P\to Q^{0}\to Q^{1}\to\cdots\to\cdots Q^{i}\to\cdots

[formula]

in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
with all
Q^{i}
in
\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)
, and there exists a
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{S}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S),-)
-exact exact sequence

\cdots\to E_{i}\to\cdots\to E_{1}\to E_{0}\to I\to 0

[formula]

in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S
with all
E_{i}
in
\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)
.
On the other hand, since
\mathscr{H}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathop{\rm res}\nolimits\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)}

and
\mathscr{T}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathop{\rm cores}\nolimits\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)}
by assumption,
we have that for any
H\in\mathscr{H}
and
T\in\mathscr{T}
,
there exists a
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{R}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),-)
-exact exact sequence

\cdots\to Q_{i}\to\cdots\to Q_{1}\to Q_{0}\to H\to 0

[formula]

in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
with all
Q_{i}
in
\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)
, and there exists a
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{S}(-,\mathcal{I}_{C}(S))
-exact exact sequence

0\to T\to E^{0}\to E^{1}\to\cdots\to\cdots E^{i}\to\cdots

[formula]

in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S
with all
E^{i}
in
\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)
.
It is trivial that (4.3) is
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{R}(P,-)
-exact and (4.4) is
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{S}(-,I)
-exact.
Then (4.1) is
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{R}(-,H)
-exact and (4.2) is
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{S}(T,-)
-exact by Lemma
2.3
.
So
P\in\widetilde{\mathop{\rm cores}\nolimits_{\mathscr{H}}\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)}
and
I\in\widetilde{\mathop{\rm res}\nolimits_{\mathscr{T}}\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)}
, and hence

P\in{{}^{\bot}\mathscr{H}}\cap\widetilde{\mathop{\rm cores}\nolimits_{\mathscr{H}}\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)}=r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathscr{H})

and

I\in{\mathscr{T}^{\bot}}\cap\widetilde{\mathop{\rm res}\nolimits_{\mathscr{T}}\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)}=l\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S),\mathscr{T}).

(2) If
\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)\subseteq\mathscr{H}
and
\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)\subseteq\mathscr{T}
, then

r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathscr{H})
is closed under extensions and kernels of epimorphisms
and
l\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S),\mathscr{T})
is closed under extensions and cokernels of monomorphisms
by
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
and its dual result, respectively. Now both assertions follow from (1).
∎

We write

\mathcal{I}_{C}(R^{op})^{+}:=\{E^{+}\mid E\in\mathcal{I}_{C}(R^{op})\}.

For simplicity, we write

\mathcal{GF}_{C}(R):=r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{F}_{C}(R),\mathcal{I}_{C}(R^{op})^{+})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{PGF}_{C}(R):=r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathcal{I}_{C}(R^{op})^{+}),

\mathcal{SGF}_{C}(R):=r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathcal{F}_{C}(R))\ \text{(the class of $C$-strong Gorenstein flat left $R$-modules)},

\mathcal{GFI}_{C}(S):=l\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S),\mathcal{FI}_{C}(S))\ \text{(the class of $C$-Gorenstein FP-injective left $S$-modules)}.

Corollary 4.5
.

It holds that

(1)

\mathcal{P}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{PGF}_{C}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{GF}_{C}(R)
.

(2)

Both
\mathcal{SGF}_{C}(R)
and
r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathcal{B}_{C}(R))
are resolving.

(3)

Both
\mathcal{GFI}_{C}(S)
and
l\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S),\mathcal{A}_{C}(S))
are coresolving.

Proof.

We have the following facts:

(i)

Since
\mathcal{I}_{C}(R^{op})\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{C}(R^{op})
by
[
16
, Corollary 6.1]
,
it follows from
[
20
, Proposition 3.2(1)]
and
[
16
, Theorem 6.1]
that

\mathcal{I}_{C}(R^{op})^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathop{\rm res}\nolimits\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{C}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathop{\rm res}\nolimits\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)}

by
[
16
, Corollary 6.1 and Theorem 6.1]
.

(iii)

\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)\subseteq\mathcal{FI}_{C}(S)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathop{\rm cores}\nolimits\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)}
by
Proposition
4.3
(2) and
[
16
, Theorem 2]
.

Now all assertions follow from Proposition
4.4
.
∎

Note that
\mathcal{GF}_{C}(R):=r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{F}_{C}(R),\mathcal{I}_{C}(R^{op})^{+})
and

\mathcal{PGF}_{C}(R):=r\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathcal{I}_{C}(R^{op})^{+})

C
-Gorenstein flat

and
C
-projectively coresolved Gorenstein flat
left
R
-modules, respectively (
[
22
]
).
When

-modules, respectively (

8
,
30
]
).

4.1
Usual
C
-Gorenstein subcategories

Following the usual customary notation, we write

\mathop{\rm G_{\it C}\text{-}pd}\nolimits_{R}M:=r{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)){\text{-}}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathop{\rm G_{\it C}\text{-}id}\nolimits_{S}N:=l{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)){\text{-}}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits N,

\mathop{\rm SGF_{\it C}\text{-}pd}\nolimits_{R}M:=\mathcal{SGF}_{C}(R){\text{-}}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits M\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathop{\rm GFP_{\it C}\text{-}id}\nolimits_{S}N:=\mathcal{GFI}_{C}(S){\text{-}}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits N.

A Wakamatsu tilting module
{}_{R}C
with
S=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(_{R}C)
(equivalently, a semidualizing bimodule
{{}_{R}C_{S}}
)
is called
faithful
if the following conditions are satisfied: (i) if
M\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
satisfies
M_{*}=0
, then
M=0
;
and (ii) if
N\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S^{op}
satisfies
N_{*}=0
, then
N=0
(
[
16
]
).
In the following result, the implications
(1.1)\Longleftrightarrow(1.2)\Longleftrightarrow(2.1)\Longleftrightarrow(2.2)

have been obtained in
[
22
, Theorem 5.4(1)]
.

Theorem 4.6
.

It holds that

(i)

For any
n\geq 0
, the following statements are equivalent.

(1.1)

\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
is right
n
-
\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)
-Gorenstein

(
that is,
\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits\mathcal{I}(R)\leq n
and
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)\leq n)
.

\mathop{\rm G_{\it C}\text{-}pd}\nolimits_{R}M\leq n

.

(1.3)

\mathop{\rm G_{\it C}\text{-}pd}\nolimits\mathcal{I}(R)\leq n
and
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)\leq n
.

(2.1)

\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S
is left
n
-
\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)
-Gorenstein

(
that is,
\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)
-
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits\mathcal{P}(S)\leq n
and
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)\leq n)
.

(2.2)

\mathop{\rm G_{\it C}\text{-}id}\nolimits_{S}N\leq n

N\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S
.

(2.3)

\mathop{\rm G_{\it C}\text{-}id}\nolimits\mathcal{P}(S)\leq n
and
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)\leq n
.

(3.1)

\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits R
is right
n
-
(\mathcal{P}_{C}(R),\mathcal{F}_{C}(R))
-Gorenstein

(

\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)
-
\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits\mathcal{I}(R)\leq n
and

.

\mathop{\rm SGF_{\it C}\text{-}pd}\nolimits_{R}M\leq n

.

(3.3)

\mathop{\rm SGF_{\it C}\text{-}pd}\nolimits\mathcal{I}(R)\leq n

.

\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits S

-

(

\mathcal{I}_{C}(S)

\mathop{\rm GFP_{\it C}\text{-}id}\nolimits_{S}N\leq n

.

(4.3)

\mathop{\rm GFP_{\it C}\text{-}id}\nolimits\mathcal{P}(S)\leq n

If the Wakamatsu tilting module

(5)

If the Wakamatsu tilting module
{}_{R}C
with

\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)

Putting
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{D}=\mathcal{P}_{C}(R)
in Theorem

we get
(1.1)\Longleftrightarrow(1.2)\Longleftrightarrow(1.3)\ (\Longleftrightarrow(1.4)

\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)\text{-}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits R/J(R)=\mathcal{B}_{C}(S^{op})\text{-}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits S/J(S)=\mathcal{A}_{C}(R^{op})\text{-}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits R/J(R)=\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)\text{-}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits S/J(S).

[formula]

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)\text{-}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits R/J(R)=n<\infty
. Then

\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits_{R}C=\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits_{S^{op}}C=n
by assumption and Proposition
4.19
(1),
and thus

\mathcal{B}_{C}(S^{op})\text{-}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits S/J(S)=\mathcal{A}_{C}(R^{op})\text{-}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits R/J(R)=\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)\text{-}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits S/J(S)=n

by Proposition
4.19
. This shows that the the first quantity in (4.6)
is at least the last three ones. Similarly, we get that any quantity in (4.6)
is at least the other three ones.
∎

As a consequence, we get the following result.

Corollary 4.21
.

Let
R
be a left coherent semilocal ring and
S
a right coherent semilocal ring.
Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits_{R}C=\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits_{S^{op}}C<\infty
.

(2)

\mathcal{B}_{C}(R)\text{-}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits R/J(R)<\infty
and
\mathcal{B}_{C}(S^{op})\text{-}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits S/J(S)<\infty
.

(3)

\mathcal{A}_{C}(R^{op})\text{-}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits R/J(R)<\infty
and
\mathcal{A}_{C}(S)\text{-}\mathop{\rm pd}\nolimits S/J(S)<\infty
.

If one of the above conditions is satisfied, then all these six quantities are identical.

Proof.

The implications that
(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
and
(1)\Longrightarrow(3)
follow from Theorem
4.15
, and
the implications that
(2)\Longrightarrow(1)
and
(3)\Longrightarrow(1)
follow from Proposition
4.19
.
The last assertion follows from Corollary
4.20
.
∎
