Asymmetry of ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -cohomology via skewed Følner geometry
Nachi Avraham-Re’em, Zemer Kosloff

Abstract
. We study the two canonical ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -Dirichlet structures on a finitely generated group G G , arising from the left and right regular actions on ℝ G \mathbb{R}^{G} . Although the left and right regular representations are unitarily equivalent, their ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -Dirichlet subspaces of ℝ G \mathbb{R}^{G} need not coincide. We prove that for finitely generated nilpotent groups this ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -asymmetry is governed by virtual commutativity: 𝒟 2 ( G , λ ) = 𝒟 2 ( G , ρ ) ⟺ G is virtually abelian . \mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)=\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\text{ is virtually abelian}. The proof introduces a skewed Følner-geometric mechanism, called a left scheme , combining summability of left boundaries with displacement under right translation. By refining this mechanism into recurrent left schemes , we further show that every non-virtually abelian finitely generated nilpotent group admits Bernoulli schemes whose left shift is nonsingular and weakly mixing whereas the right shift is singular. These are the first constructions of such Bernoulli schemes over amenable groups. In addition to nilpotent groups, our techniques are robust enough to cover all amenable wreath products over ℤ \mathbb{Z} and solvable Baumslag–Solitar groups. We also classify the virtually cyclic case, where ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -asymmetry arises from one-sided commensurated ends rather than from left schemes.

Introduction and results

1.1.
Background

A theorem of Cheeger–Gromov
[
6
]
asserts that for amenable groups the reduced
\ell^{2}
-cohomology vanishes; see also
[
17
, Cor. 5.13]
,
[
16
]
. Thus the phenomena studied in this paper are invisible from the reduced point of view, and in particular from
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers. The purpose of this work is to show that before passing to reduced classes, the first
\ell^{2}
-cohomology can be intrinsically sensitive to noncommutativity: the left and right
\ell^{2}
-Dirichlet spaces may exhibit asymmetry already in the simplest nonabelian groups. The central question of the paper is whether the two canonical Dirichlet structures induced by left and right translation agree as subspaces of the same function space
\mathbb{R}^{G}
.

A central object in first
\ell^{2}
-cohomology of groups is the
\ell^{2}
-Dirichlet space. For a countable group
G
, this is the space of real-valued functions defining
\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
-valued
1
-cocycles for the regular representation. Beyond its intrinsic interest in geometric group theory and representation theory (see e.g.
[
10
, §8]
,
[
3
,
18
,
9
]
), it also lies at the heart of the nonsingular ergodic theory of Bernoulli schemes through Kakutani’s classical
\ell^{2}
-type criterion for equivalence of product measures
[
12
]
; see
[
23
,
5
,
4
]
.

We now define these two Dirichlet structures precisely. Fix a countably infinite discrete group
G
. Denote by
\lambda
and
\rho
the left and right translation actions of
G
on the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{G}
, defined by

\lambda\left(g\right)\xi\left(h\right)=\xi\left(g^{-1}h\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\rho\left(g\right)\xi\left(h\right)=\xi\left(hg\right).

Define the
left
\ell^{2}
-Dirichlet space
of
G
by

\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right):=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{G}:\forall g\in G,\ \lambda\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\right\},

and the
right
\ell^{2}
-Dirichlet space
of
G
by

\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right):=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{G}:\forall g\in G,\ \rho\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\right\}.

Inside
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\cap\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
one always finds the trivial subspace

\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}.

We will use the following slightly abused but convenient terminology:

•

the elements of
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)
are called
left
\ell^{2}
-cocycles
;

•

the elements of
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
are called
right
\ell^{2}
-cocycles
;

•

the elements of
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\cap\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
are called
symmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles
;

•

the elements of
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\triangle\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
are called
asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles
;

•

the elements of
\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}
are called
\ell^{2}
-coboundaries
, which are always symmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles.

Although the left and right regular representations are unitarily equivalent as abstract unitary representations
G\to\mathrm{U}\left(\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\right)
(via variable inversion), their
\ell^{2}
-Dirichlet spaces need not agree as subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{G}
. This discrepancy is not accidental: the unreduced
\ell^{2}
-cohomology can retain directional structure.

There are two general obstructions to
\ell^{2}
-asymmetry. The first is algebraic. Abelian groups, and more generally FC-groups, admit no asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles (see
section

7
). Thus, for FC-groups,

\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)=\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right).

The second obstruction is geometric or potential-theoretic: for nonamenable groups, the space of
\ell^{2}
-cocycles has a particularly restrictive form
[
3
]
. One implication in the following theorem is due
to Stefaan Vaes, who kindly allowed us to include his proof; see Appendix
A
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every countably infinite group
G
,

\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\cap\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)=\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}\iff G\text{ is nonamenable.}

Among nonamenable groups, some exhibit additional cohomological obstructions. For instance, for groups with Kazhdan’s property (T), all
\ell^{2}
-cocycles are
\ell^{2}
-coboundaries. Other nonamenable groups, such as free groups, have positive first
\ell^{2}
-Betti number, and therefore admit asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles that are not
\ell^{2}
-coboundaries. In light of
1.1
, this work is concerned with the following problem.

Problem 1.2
.

Which finitely generated amenable groups admit asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles?

This question was raised in a MathOverflow discussion
[
19
]
, where it was noted that amenable groups appear to be the interesting case; this is now substantiated by
1.1
. In the same discussion, M. Kapovich observed that the infinite dihedral group
D_{\infty}
admits an asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycle, a case that we discuss in more detail below.

1.2.
Summary of main results

Our first main result is a complete classification for finitely generated nilpotent groups, where we show that the only obstruction to
\ell^{2}
-asymmetry is virtual commutativity.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every finitely generated nilpotent group
G
,

\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)=\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)\iff G\text{ is virtually abelian.}

The proof of
1.3
introduces the main technical object of this work, that we call
left scheme
. Informally, a left scheme is a sequence of finite subsets with two competing features: summability of normalized left boundaries, and strong displacement under right translation by a distinguished infinite order element. Thus a left scheme is a skewed Følner configuration: the sets are almost invariant from the left, in a summable sense, but are separated from a prescribed right translate.

The proof of existence of left schemes in all non-virtually abelian finitely generated nilpotent groups goes by proving two key properties of left schemes:

(a)

If a group
G
is an amenable extension of a group
H
and
H
admits a left scheme, then
G
also admits a left scheme; see
3.1
.

(b)

Establishing a sufficient criterion for existence of a left scheme in semidirect products of the form
G=H\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
with
H
amenable.

The first property allows us to reduce the question from general nilpotent groups to torsion-free
2
-step nilpotent groups by passing to a suitable quotient. There, by carefully choosing a Malcev basis exhibiting a
Heisenberg direction
, we are able to build a left scheme using the criterion for semidirect products. See
section

5
for the details and
section

2.3
for a direct construction of a left scheme in the Heisenberg group which is the model of this construction.

With regards to
1.3
one should observe that the nilpotency assumption is crucial, as the infinite dihedral group is virtually abelian (cyclic), and yet it admits an asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycle as observed by M. Kapovich
[
19
]
. We discuss the virtually cyclic case in details in
section

7
. There we show that virtually cyclic groups never admit left schemes, and thus their
\ell^{2}
-asymmetry cannot arise by the same geometric mechanism as nilpotent groups. Still, a major source for
\ell^{2}
-asymmetry there is the geometry of multiple ends, and more specifically left-commensurated but not bi-commensurated end in the sense of Cornulier
[
7
, §1.4]
. Indeed, in
section

7
we show that a virtually cyclic group admits an asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycle if and only if it is not an FC-group.

The criterion we obtain for left schemes in semidirect products is quite general, and in particular it covers also the following classes of groups.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a group belonging to one of the following classes:

•

Amenable wreath products
A\wr\mathbb{Z}
for a nontrivial finitely generated amenable group
A
.

•

Solvable Baumslag–Solitar groups
\mathrm{BS}\left(1,d\right)
with
d\geq 2
.

Then
G
admits an asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycle.

The general nature of
\ell^{2}
-asymmetry remains a question worth studying. Let us first note that being an FC-group is not the only obstruction to having an asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycle. The group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
is far from being an FC-group, and it has Kazhdan’s property (T). Thus it admits no asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycle by the Delorme–Guichardet theorem
[
2
, Prop. 2.2.10]
. In amenable groups the situation is unclear:

Problem 1.5
.

Classify the finitely generated amenable groups
G
with the property

\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)=\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right).

In particular, are FC-groups the only finitely generated amenable groups with this property?

The present work solves this problem for finitely generated nilpotent groups (
1.3
,
appendix

B
), and for virtually cyclic groups (
section

7
).

1.3.
Motivations for searching for asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles

Recall the first (unreduced)
\ell^{2}
-cohomology of a group
G
for the left regular representation,

H^{1}\left(G,\lambda\right):=Z^{1}\left(G,\lambda\right)\big/B^{1}\left(G,\lambda\right),

where
Z^{1}\left(G,\lambda\right)
is the space of
\ell^{2}
-cocycles for
\lambda
, and
B^{1}\left(G,\lambda\right)
is the subspace of
\ell^{2}
-coboundaries. Constructing nonzero classes in
H^{1}\left(G,\lambda\right)
is generally challenging. Since asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles are never
\ell^{2}
-coboundaries, they provide a new source of nonzero classes in
H^{1}\left(G,\lambda\right)
. Moreover, since we work with amenable groups, these cocycles are further proper by the Peterson–Thom theorem
[
20
, Thm. 2.5]
.

We now turn to dynamical aspects of this asymmetry phenomenon. Consider the product space

\Omega_{G}:=\{0,1\}^{G}

with the two canonical actions of
G
, namely the left shift and the right shift:

L\left(g\right)\left(\omega_{h}\right)_{h\in G}=\left(\omega_{g^{-1}h}\right)_{h\in G}\quad\text{and}\quad R\left(g\right)\left(\omega_{h}\right)_{h\in G}=\left(\omega_{hg}\right)_{h\in G}.

For a
\left(0,1\right)
-valued vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{G}
, define a probability product measure
\mu_{\xi}
on
\Omega_{G}
by

\mu_{\xi}:=\bigotimes\nolimits_{h\in G}\left(\xi\left(h\right),1-\xi\left(h\right)\right).

Such a probability space
\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is called a
Bernoulli scheme
, and it is a fundamental question in ergodic theory when a Bernoulli scheme is nonsingular with respect to the shift actions. By a classical theorem of Kakutani
[
12
]
, a Bernoulli scheme
\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is left nonsingular precisely when both

\sqrt{\xi}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{\mathbf{1}-\xi}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right).

A similar criterion holds with right nonsingularity. Thus the same
\ell^{2}
-asymmetry that governs our cohomological constructions also governs the nonsingularity of Bernoulli shifts; see
[
23
,
5
,
4
]
.

From a dynamical point of view, a nonsingular system is nontrivial when it is
conservative
, meaning that it exhibits recurrence in the measurable sense. While conservativity is automatic for probability preserving actions (this is the Poincaré recurrence theorem), it is often highly nontrivial to check for nonsingular systems. A manifestation of the importance of conservativity in Bernoulli schemes is a theorem of Danilenko
[
8
, Thm. 0.2]
, which states that a conservative Bernoulli scheme over an amenable group whose marginals stay away from
0
is weakly mixing (cf.
[
14
]
,
[
4
, §3]
).

Left schemes can be naturally used to produce Bernoulli schemes which are left nonsingular but not right nonsingular. However, to ensure conservativity one must include a dynamical input that challenges the basic properties of left schemes. By considering
recurrent left schemes
, we control the growth of the Radon–Nikodym cocycle, and so a criterion due to Vaes–Wahl
[
23
, §4]
is applicable. Combined with the aforementioned theorem of Danilenko, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Every non-virtually abelian finitely generated nilpotent group
G
admits a Bernoulli scheme
\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
such that the left shift action is nonsingular and weakly mixing, while the right shift action is singular. This Bernoulli scheme can be constructed with a distinguished element
g_{o}\in G
of infinite order such that the left shift by
g_{o}
is conservative while the right shift by
g_{o}
is singular.

The conclusion of
1.6
also holds for the classes of groups appearing in
1.4
.

2.
Left schemes

The purpose of this section is to introduce left schemes and explain how they are used in asymmetric constructions. Before that, we will start with a general construction of symmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles.

2.1.
Balanced Følner geometry

The following construction of symmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles uses balanced (two-sided) Følner geometry, and it will inspire the later construction of asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles.

Proposition 2.1
.

Every countably infinite amenable group
G
admits a symmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycle which is not an
\ell^{2}
-coboundary.

The following is an elementary lemma on amenability.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a countably infinite amenable group with an exhaustion by finite symmetric sets
K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}\subseteq\dotsm
. There is a sequence
\left(F_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
of pairwise disjoint finite sets with
\left|F_{n}\right|\to\infty
, such that

\max_{g\in K_{n}}\frac{\left|gF_{n}\triangle F_{n}\right|}{\left|F_{n}\right|}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{g\in K_{n}}\frac{\left|F_{n}g\triangle F_{n}\right|}{\left|F_{n}\right|}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\quad\text{for every }n\geq 1.

Proof of
section

2.1
.

Since
G
is amenable, it admits a two-sided Følner sequence (see e.g.
[
13
, Thm. 4.10]
). Fix such a Følner sequence
\left(S_{m}\right)_{m\geq 1}
and define
\left(F_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
inductively as follows. Define
F_{0}:=\emptyset
and put
E_{1}:=\emptyset
. Assume
F_{0},\dotsc,F_{n-1}
are defined and put
E_{n}:=F_{1}\cup\dotsm\cup F_{n-1}
. Choose
m>n
large so that

\max_{g\in K_{n}}\frac{\left|gS_{m}\triangle S_{m}\right|}{\left|S_{m}\right|}<\frac{1}{4n^{2}},\quad\max_{g\in K_{n}}\frac{\left|S_{m}g\triangle S_{m}\right|}{\left|S_{m}\right|}<\frac{1}{4n^{2}},\quad\frac{\left|S_{m}\cap E_{n}\right|}{\left|S_{m}\right|}<\frac{1}{4n^{2}},\quad\left|S_{m}\right|>n,

where the two last conditions are simply because
\left|S_{m}\right|\to\infty
. Define now
F_{n}:=S_{m}\backslash E_{n}
.

The sets
\left(F_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
are pairwise disjoint by construction, and for every
n
we have

\left|F_{n}\right|=\left|S_{m}\right|-\left|S_{m}\cap E_{n}\right|>\left(1-1/4n^{2}\right)\cdot\left|S_{m}\right|\geq 3/4\cdot\left|S_{m}\right|.

In particular, since
\left|S_{m}\right|>n
, this shows that
\left|F_{n}\right|\to\infty
. We show the left boundary condition, and the right boundary condition is similar. Fix
g\in K_{n}
. Then

gF_{n}\triangle F_{n}\subseteq\left(gS_{m}\triangle S_{m}\right)\cup g\left(S_{m}\cap E_{n}\right)\cup\left(S_{m}\cap E_{n}\right),

we get

\left|gF_{n}\triangle F_{n}\right|\leq\left|gS_{m}\triangle S_{m}\right|+2\left|S_{m}\cap E_{n}\right|<\left(1/4n^{2}+2/4n^{2}\right)\cdot\left|S_{m}\right|=3/4n^{2}\cdot\left|S_{m}\right|.

Then both estimates combined give the left boundary condition.
∎

Proof of
section

2.1
.

Fix a sequence
\left(F_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
as in
section

2.1
, and define

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{G},\quad\xi\left(h\right):=\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{n}\right|}}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{F_{n}}\left(h\right),\quad h\in G.

Fix
g\in G
and let
n\left(g\right):=\min\left\{n\geq 1:g\in K_{n}\right\}
. For every
n\geq n\left(g\right)
, since
g\in K_{n}
we have

\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{n}\right|}}\left(\mathbf{1}_{gF_{n}}-\mathbf{1}_{F_{n}}\right)\Big\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}^{2}=\frac{1}{\left|F_{n}\right|}\cdot\left|gF_{n}\triangle F_{n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Since the sets
\left(F_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
are pairwise disjoint, so are the sets

\left(gF_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
. Therefore, for each
h\in G
,

\Big|\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{n}\right|}}\left(\mathbf{1}_{gF_{n}}\left(h\right)-\mathbf{1}_{F_{n}}\left(h\right)\right)\Big|=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{i}\right|}}&\exists!\,i,\,h\in gF_{i}\triangle F_{i}\\
\big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{i}\right|}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{i}\right|}}\big|&\exists!\,i\neq j,\,h\in\left(gF_{i}\cap F_{i}\right)\cap\left(gF_{j}\cap F_{j}\right)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Then using
\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}\big)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}
, we obtain that

\Big|\sum\nolimits_{n\geq n\left(g\right)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{n}\right|}}\left(\mathbf{1}_{gF_{n}}\left(h\right)-\mathbf{1}_{F_{n}}\left(h\right)\right)\Big|^{2}\leq\sum\nolimits_{n\geq n\left(g\right)}\frac{1}{\left|F_{n}\right|}\mathbf{1}_{gF_{n}\triangle F_{n}}\left(h\right).

Summing over
h\in G
we get

\Big\|\sum\nolimits_{n\geq n\left(g\right)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{n}\right|}}\left(\mathbf{1}_{gF_{n}}-\mathbf{1}_{F_{n}}\right)\Big\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}^{2}\leq\sum\nolimits_{n\geq n\left(g\right)}\frac{\left|gF_{n}\triangle F_{n}\right|}{\left|F_{n}\right|}\leq\sum\nolimits_{n\geq n\left(g\right)}\frac{1}{n^{2}}<+\infty.

Since there are only finitely many terms with
n<n\left(g\right)
, we found that
\lambda\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
. A similar reasoning shows that also
\rho\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
. As
g
is arbitrary, it follows that
\xi\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\cap\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
.

Let us show that
\xi\notin\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}
. Assume otherwise, so that
\xi-p\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
. If
p=0
, since
\left(F_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
are pairwise disjoint we get

\left\|\xi\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}^{2}=\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\left|F_{n}\right|}\cdot\left|F_{n}\right|=\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}1=+\infty,

contrary to the assumption. If
p\neq 0
, since
\left|F_{n}\right|\to\infty
we may pick
N\geq 1
such that
\big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{n}\right|}}-p\big|>\left|p\right|/2
for all
n\geq N
. Then using that
\xi\mid_{F_{n}}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{n}\right|}}
we obtain

\left\|\xi-p\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}^{2}\geq\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\sum\nolimits_{h\in F_{n}}\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|F_{n}\right|}}-p\Big|^{2}\geq\left|p\right|^{2}/4\cdot\sum\nolimits_{n\geq N}\left|F_{n}\right|=+\infty,

which is again contrary to the assumption. Hence
\xi\notin\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}
.
∎

2.2.
Skewed Følner geometry

Here we present a general method of constructing asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles and asymmetric weakly mixing Bernoulli schemes. As a convention, our constructions are formulated leftwise, that is, we aim to construct elements of
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\setminus\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
. All of our constructions have obvious rightwise versions.

We start by defining (recurrent) left schemes in a form that makes the underlying skewed Følner geometry transparent. In this way, finding (recurrent) left schemes is greatly simplified and is more understood geometrically. For the actual asymmetric constructions, we will use a combinatorial refinement of (recurrent) left schemes (
section

2.2
) which is somewhat more intricate.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a group with a finite symmetric generating set
S
and an element
s_{o}\in S
of infinite order. An
s_{o}
-left scheme
is a sequence
\left(E_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
of finite subsets of
G
such that:

(1)

E_{n}s_{o}\cap E_{n}=\emptyset
for every
n
.

(2)

\Phi\left(g\right):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left|gE_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}<+\infty
for every
g\in G
.

Put
\Gamma:=\left\langle s_{o}\right\rangle<G
. The
s_{o}
-left scheme
\left(E_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is called
recurrent
if, in addition to (1) and (2):

(3)

\left(\Gamma E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right)\cap\left(\Gamma E_{m}\triangle E_{m}\right)=\emptyset
for all
n\neq m
.

(4)

There exists
\kappa>0
such that
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\exp\left(-\kappa\cdot\Phi\left(\gamma\right)\right)=+\infty
.

Remark 2.4
.

To verify condition (2) of left scheme, it suffices to check that
\Phi\left(s\right)<+\infty
for
s\in S
. Indeed, let
g\in G
, and write
g=s_{1}\dotsm s_{k}
with
s_{i}\in S
,
1\leq i\leq k

2.3.
The model case of the Heisenberg group

We now record a concrete geometric model for left schemes in the Heisenberg group. This example was the starting point for their definition and study, but it is subsumed by the general construction for torsion-free
2
-step nilpotent groups in
section

5.1
.

Consider the Heisenberg group

H=H_{3}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)=\left\langle x,y,z:\left[y,x\right]=z,\left[z,x\right]=\left[z,y\right]=e_{H}\right\rangle,

where commutators are taken with the convention

\left[g,h\right]=g^{-1}h^{-1}gh
. Thus the multiplication rule is

x^{a}y^{b}z^{c}\cdot x^{a^{\prime}}y^{b^{\prime}}z^{c^{\prime}}=x^{a+a^{\prime}}y^{b+b^{\prime}}z^{c+c^{\prime}+ba^{\prime}},\quad a,b,c,a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}.

We work with the finite symmetric generating set
S:=\left\{x,x^{-1},y,y^{-1}\right\}
and put
\Gamma:=\left\langle x\right\rangle
.

Choose positive integer sequences
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}
such that

\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{A_{n}}<+\infty,\quad\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{B_{n}}<+\infty,\quad\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}<+\infty.

Define an integer sequence
b_{n}
inductively by

b_{1}:=C_{1}+1,\quad b_{n+1}:=\max\left\{C_{n+1}+1,\,b_{n}+B_{n}+1\right\},

and denote

I_{n}:=\left[b_{n},b_{n}+B_{n}\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}.

The choice of
b_{n}
ensures the properties

b_{n}>C_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{n}\cap I_{m}=\emptyset\quad\text{for all }n\neq m.

Define

E_{n}:=\left\{x^{a}y^{b}z^{c}:0\leq a<A_{n},\ b\in I_{n},\ 0\leq c<C_{n}\right\}.

We then claim that
\left(E_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is an
x
-left scheme. Indeed, right multiplication by
x
gives

x^{a}y^{b}z^{c}x=x^{a+1}y^{b}z^{c+b}.

Since
b\in I_{n}
implies
b\geq b_{n}>C_{n}
, the new
z
-coordinate cannot lie in
\left[0,C_{n}\right)
. Therefore
E_{n}x\cap E_{n}=\emptyset
, verifying condition (1) of
x
-left scheme. On the other hand, left multiplication produces relatively small boundary. We check this for the generators:

•

Left multiplication by
x
gives
xx^{a}y^{b}z^{c}=x^{a+1}y^{b}z^{c}
. Therefore,

\frac{\left|xE_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}=\frac{2}{A_{n}}.

The same estimate holds for
x^{-1}
.

•

Left multiplication by
y
gives
yx^{a}y^{b}z^{c}=x^{a}y^{b+1}z^{c+a}
. Since

\left|yE_{n}\setminus E_{n}\right|=\left|E_{n}\setminus yE_{n}\right|
, it suffices to estimate the second. If
g=x^{a}y^{b}z^{c}\in E_{n}\setminus yE_{n}
then
y^{-1}g=x^{a}y^{b-1}z^{c-a}
. Since
g\in E_{n}
but
y^{-1}g\notin E_{n}
, either
b-1\notin I_{n}
or
c-a\notin\left[0,C_{n}\right)
. As
b\in I_{n}
and
0\leq c<C_{n}
, this means that either
b=b_{n}
or
c<a
. Therefore

\left|E_{n}\setminus yE_{n}\right|\leq A_{n}C_{n}+B_{n}\sum\nolimits_{a=0}^{A_{n}-1}\min\left\{a,C_{n}\right\}\leq A_{n}C_{n}+B_{n}\sum\nolimits_{a=0}^{A_{n}-1}a\leq A_{n}C_{n}+\frac{1}{2}A_{n}^{2}B_{n}.

Using that
\left|E_{n}\right|=A_{n}B_{n}C_{n}
, it follows that

\frac{\left|yE_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}\leq\frac{2A_{n}C_{n}+A_{n}^{2}B_{n}}{A_{n}B_{n}C_{n}}=\frac{2}{B_{n}}+\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}.

The same estimate holds for
y^{-1}
.

Therefore, by the choice of
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}
(and using
section

2.2
), condition (2) of
x
-left schemes holds.

Finally, we mention that this construction produces a recurrent
x
-left scheme if, in addition, one chooses
A_{n}\geq q^{n}
for some
q>1
. Indeed, left multiplication by powers of
x
preserves the
y
-coordinate,
while the intervals
I_{n}
are pairwise disjoint. Thus

\left(\Gamma E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right)\cap\left(\Gamma E_{m}\triangle E_{m}\right)=\emptyset\quad\text{for all }n\neq m.

Moreover, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, left multiplication by
x^{k}
replaces the
a
-interval
\left[0,A_{n}\right)
by
\left[k,A_{n}+k\right)
, without changing the
b
- and
c
-coordinates. Then similarly to the above,

\left|x^{k}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|=2\min\left\{\left|k\right|,A_{n}\right\}B_{n}C_{n}.

For
\left|k\right|\geq 1
, put
N_{k}:=\left\lfloor\log_{q}\left|k\right|\right\rfloor
. Using
A_{n}\geq q^{n}
, by dividing the sum for
n\leq N_{k}
and
n>N_{k}
we get

\Phi\left(x^{k}\right)\leq 2\cdot\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\min\Big\{\frac{\left|k\right|}{q^{n}},1\Big\}\leq 2N_{k}+2\left|k\right|\cdot\frac{q^{-N_{k}-1}}{1-q^{-1}}\leq 2\log_{q}\left|k\right|+\frac{2q}{q-1}.

Taking
\kappa:=\log q/2
, one finds a constant
C\left(q\right)>0
so that

\sum\nolimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\exp\left(-\kappa\Phi\left(\gamma\right)\right)\geq C\left(q\right)\cdot\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}=+\infty.

2.4.
Asymmetric Bernoulli schemes

We now turn to the dynamical aspect of our construction. First we recall some fundamental properties of nonsingular actions. Let
G\curvearrowright\left(\Omega,\mu\right)
be a nonsingular action of a countable discrete group on a probability space. The action is called
conservative
if it is Poincaré recurrent: for every measurable set
A
in
\Omega
with
\mu\left(A\right)>0
, there is
e_{G}\neq g\in G
such that
\mu\left(g.A\cap A\right)>0
. This is the simplest of many other equivalent forms of conservativity, and for comprehensive treatments see
[
15
, §1.3]
,
[
1
, §1.1-1.6]
. The action is called
ergodic
if for every measurable set
A
satisfying
\mu\left(G.A\triangle A\right)=0
, it holds that
\mu\left(A\right)\in\{0,1\}
. The action is called
weakly mixing
if for every ergodic probability preserving action
G\curvearrowright\left(\Omega^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\right)
, the diagonal nonsingular action
G\curvearrowright\left(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\mu\otimes\mu^{\prime}\right)
is ergodic. Weak mixing implies ergodicity, and as long as the underlying probability space is nonatomic, ergodicity implies conservativity.

A
Bernoulli scheme
on
G
is a probability space of the form

\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right):=\big(\{0,1\}^{G},\bigotimes\nolimits_{h\in G}\left(\xi\left(h\right),1-\xi\left(h\right)\right)\big),

where
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{G}
is a
\left(0,1\right)
-valued vector. By Kakutani’s dichotomy
[
12
]
, for every pair of
\left(0,1\right)
-valued vectors
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{G}
, the product measures
\mu_{\xi}
and
\mu_{\eta}
are either equivalent (i.e. mutually absolutely continuous) or mutually singular. Moreover, Kakutani’s criterion asserts that the measures
\mu_{\xi}
and
\mu_{\eta}
are equivalent if and only if both

\sqrt{\xi}-\sqrt{\eta}\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{\mathbf{1}-\xi}-\sqrt{\mathbf{1}-\eta}\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right).

Equip
\Omega_{G}
with the left shift and the right shift actions of
G
, given by

L\left(g\right)\left(\omega_{h}\right)_{h\in G}=\left(\omega_{g^{-1}h}\right)_{h\in G}\quad\text{and}\quad R\left(g\right)\left(\omega_{h}\right)_{h\in G}=\left(\omega_{hg}\right)_{h\in G}.

A Bernoulli scheme
\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is said to be
left
(
right
)
nonsingular
if for every
g\in G
,

L\left(g\right)_{\ast}\mu_{\xi}\sim\mu_{\xi}\quad(R\left(g\right)_{\ast}\mu_{\xi}\sim\mu_{\xi}\text{, respectively}),

where
\sim
stands for equivalence of measures. Here, for a transformation
T
we denote by
T_{\ast}\mu_{\xi}=\mu_{\xi}\circ T^{-1}
the pushforward of
\mu_{\xi}
by
T
. Note that the pushforward of product measures by left shifts and right shifts are still product measures:

L\left(g\right)_{\ast}\mu_{\xi}=\mu_{\lambda\left(g\right)\xi}\quad\text{and}\quad R\left(g\right)_{\ast}\mu_{\xi}=\mu_{\rho\left(g\right)\xi}.

Using Kakutani’s criterion, one has that
\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is left (right) nonsingular precisely when both

\sqrt{\xi}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\text{ and }\sqrt{\mathbf{1}-\xi}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\quad(\sqrt{\xi}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)\text{ and }\sqrt{\mathbf{1}-\xi}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)\text{, respectively)}.

In the following we use recurrent left schemes to construct left nonsingular weakly mixing Bernoulli schemes that fail to be right nonsingular. We will ensure the conservativity using a criterion on the growth of the associated Radon–Nikodym cocycle due to Vaes–Wahl
[
23
, §4]
, and then the weak mixing property will be ensured by Danilenko’s theorem
[
8
]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
G
be an amenable group admitting a recurrent
s_{o}
-left scheme. There exists a
\left(0,1\right)
-valued

\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)

(1)

\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)

(2)

The left shift by
s_{o}
on
\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is conservative.

(3)

is singular.

. Since
\delta\leq\xi\left(h\right)\leq 1-\delta
for all
h\in G
, and
\delta^{-2}+\delta^{-1}\left(1-\delta\right)^{-2}=63/4<16
, by
[
23
, Prop. 4.1]
the left action of
\Gamma\curvearrowright\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is conservative. Finally, since the left shift
\Gamma\curvearrowright\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is conservative, then a fortiori the left shift
G\curvearrowright\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is conservative. Since
0<\inf\xi\leq\sup\xi<1
and
G
is amenable, by
[
8
, Thm. 0.2]
the left shift
G\curvearrowright\left(\Omega_{G},\mu_{\xi}\right)
is weakly mixing.
∎

3.
Lifting left schemes through amenable extensions

The following structural result says that left schemes lift from a quotient across an amenable kernel.

Theorem 3.1
.

Admitting a recurrent left scheme is preserved under extensions by amenable groups: if

1\to N\to G\to Q\to 1

is an exact sequence, where
G
is finitely generated and
N
is amenable, then every recurrent left scheme on
Q
lifts to a recurrent left scheme on
G
.

The key to proving
3.1
is a general lemma on equivariant lifts from Følner thickenings.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q:G\to Q:=G/N
be a quotient homomorphism with normal amenable kernel
N\triangleleft G
. Let
S\subset G
be a finite symmetric set with a fixed element
s_{o}\in S
so that
\overline{s_{o}}:=q\left(s_{o}\right)
has infinite order. Let
D\subset Q
be a finite set so that
D\overline{s_{o}}\cap D=\emptyset
. Then for every
\epsilon>0
there is a finite set
E\subset G
with:

(1)

q\left(E\right)=D
.

(2)

Es_{o}\cap E=\emptyset
.

(3)

\frac{\left|sE\triangle E\right|}{\left|E\right|}\leq\frac{\left|q\left(s\right)D\triangle D\right|}{\left|D\right|}+\epsilon
for every
s\in S
.

(4)

\frac{\left|\gamma E\triangle E\right|}{\left|E\right|}=\frac{\left|q\left(\gamma\right)D\triangle D\right|}{\left|D\right|}
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma:=\left\langle s_{o}\right\rangle
.

(5)

q\left(\Gamma E\triangle E\right)=\overline{\Gamma}D\triangle D
, where
\overline{\Gamma}:=\left\langle\overline{s_{o}}\right\rangle
.

Proof of
section

3
.

Let
\overline{S}:=q\left(S\right)
and
\overline{\Gamma}:=\left\langle\overline{s_{o}}\right\rangle
. Since
\overline{s_{o}}
has infinite order then so does
s_{o}
. It follows that
q\mid_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\to\overline{\Gamma}
is a surjective homomorphism of the abstract group
\mathbb{Z}
, hence it is an isomorphism. Put

Y:=\overline{\Gamma}\left(D\cup\overline{S}D\right).

Choose a set
R\subseteq D\cup\overline{S}D
containing exactly one representative from each
\overline{\Gamma}
-orbit in
Y
. For every
r\in R
choose a lift
\dot{r}\in q^{-1}\left(r\right)
. Since the action of
\overline{\Gamma}
on
Q
is free, every
y\in Y
can be written uniquely as
y=\overline{\gamma}r
with
\overline{\gamma}\in\overline{\Gamma}
and
r\in R
. Define then

\tau:Y\to G,\quad\tau\left(\overline{\gamma}r\right):=q\mid_{\Gamma}^{-1}\left(\overline{\gamma}\right)\dot{r}.

Then
q\circ\tau=\operatorname{id}_{Y}
. Additionally,
\tau
is
\Gamma
-equivariant under the isomorphism
q\mid_{\Gamma}
, in the sense that

\tau\left(\overline{\gamma}y\right)=q\mid_{\Gamma}^{-1}\left(\overline{\gamma}\right)\tau\left(y\right)\quad\text{for all }\overline{\gamma}\in\overline{\Gamma}\text{ and }y\in Y.

For every
s\in S
and
d\in D
, since
q\left(s\right)d\in\overline{S}D\subseteq Y
, the element
\tau\left(q\left(s\right)d\right)
is well-defined. Moreover, using that
q\circ\tau=\operatorname{id}_{Y}
we have

q\left(s\tau\left(d\right)\right)=q\left(s\right)d=q\left(\tau\left(q\left(s\right)d\right)\right).

Hence
s\tau\left(d\right)
and
\tau\left(q\left(s\right)d\right)
lie in the same
N
-coset, so there exists a unique
\alpha\left(s,d\right)\in N
such that

s\tau\left(d\right)=\tau\left(q\left(s\right)d\right)\alpha\left(s,d\right).

We now use the amenability of
N
: given
\epsilon>0
, find a finite set
K\subseteq N
such that

\frac{\left|\alpha\left(s,d\right)K\triangle K\right|}{\left|K\right|}\leq\epsilon\,\text{ for all }s\in S\text{ and }d\in D.

Finally, we will show that the desired set is given by

E:=\tau\left(D\right)K.

Since
\tau
is a section and
K\subseteq N
, the sets
\tau\left(d\right)K
,
d\in D
, are pairwise disjoint. Then

E=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{d\in D}\tau\left(d\right)K,

in particular
\left|E\right|=\left|D\right|\cdot\left|K\right|
and
q\left(E\right)=D
, proving (1). Since
D\overline{s_{o}}\cap D=\emptyset
we have that

q\left(Es_{o}\cap E\right)\subseteq D\overline{s_{o}}\cap D=\emptyset,

so
Es_{o}\cap E=\emptyset
, proving (2). Fix
s\in S
. From the definition of
\alpha\left(s,d\right)
,

sE=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{d\in D}\tau\left(q\left(s\right)d\right)\alpha\left(s,d\right)K.

Comparing the fiber decompositions of
E
and
sE
, the fibers over
q\left(s\right)D\triangle D
contribute whole copies of
K
, while on
D\cap q\left(s\right)^{-1}D
one compares
K
with
\alpha\left(s,d\right)K
. Hence

\left|sE\triangle E\right|\leq\left|q\left(s\right)D\triangle D\right|\left|K\right|+\sum\nolimits_{d\in D\cap q\left(s\right)^{-1}D}\left|\alpha\left(s,d\right)K\triangle K\right|.

Since
\left|E\right|=\left|D\right|\cdot\left|K\right|
and
\left|\alpha\left(s,d\right)K\triangle K\right|\leq\epsilon\left|K\right|
for all
s\in S
and
d\in D
, (3) follows. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have

\gamma E=\tau\left(q\left(\gamma\right)D\right)K,\,\text{ hence }\,\gamma E\triangle E=\tau\left(q\left(\gamma\right)D\triangle D\right)K,

by the
\Gamma
-equivariance of
\tau
. Since
\tau\left(y\right)K
,
y\in Y
, are pairwise disjoint, (4) follows. In the same way,

\Gamma E=\tau\left(\overline{\Gamma}D\right)K,\quad\text{hence}\quad\Gamma E\triangle E=\tau\left(\overline{\Gamma}D\triangle D\right)K.

Applying
q
we obtain
q\left(\Gamma E\triangle E\right)=\overline{\Gamma}D\triangle D
, and (5) follows.
∎

Proof of
3.1
.

Let
q:G\to Q:=G/N
be the quotient map. Let
\overline{T}
be a finite symmetric generating set of
Q
, let
\overline{s_{o}}\in\overline{T}
be of infinite order, and let
\left(D_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
be a recurrent
\overline{s_{o}}
-left scheme for
Q
. Choose a lift
s_{o}\in q^{-1}\left(\overline{s_{o}}\right)
, and choose a finite symmetric generating set
S
of
G
with
s_{o}\in S
. For every
n\geq 1
, apply
section

3
to
D=D_{n}
and
\epsilon=1/n^{2}
, and let
E_{n}\subset G
be the resulting finite set. We then check that
\left(E_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is a recurrent
s_{o}
-left scheme for
G
. Let
\Phi_{E}
and
\Phi_{D}
denote the functions associated with

\left(E_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
and
\left(D_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
, respectively.

(1)

This follows from
section

3
(2).

(2)

Fix
s\in S
and write
\overline{s}:=q\left(s\right)
. By
section

3
(3) we get

\frac{\left|sE_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}\leq\frac{\left|\overline{s}D_{n}\triangle D_{n}\right|}{\left|D_{n}\right|}+\frac{1}{n^{2}},

and therefore

\Phi_{E}\left(s\right)\leq\Phi_{D}\left(\overline{s}\right)+\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{2}}<+\infty.

Thus
\Phi_{E}\left(s\right)<+\infty
for every
s\in S
, and hence
\Phi_{E}\left(g\right)<+\infty
for every
g\in G
by
section

2.2
.

(3)

Put
\Gamma:=\left\langle s_{o}\right\rangle
and
\overline{\Gamma}:=\left\langle\overline{s_{o}}\right\rangle
. By
section

3
(5), one has

q\left(\Gamma E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right)=\overline{\Gamma}D_{n}\triangle D_{n}.

Therefore, if for some
n\neq m
one had

\left(\Gamma E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right)\cap\left(\Gamma E_{m}\triangle E_{m}\right)\neq\emptyset,

then applying
q
would give

\left(\overline{\Gamma}D_{n}\triangle D_{n}\right)\cap\left(\overline{\Gamma}D_{m}\triangle D_{m}\right)\neq\emptyset,

contrary to the recurrence of
\left(D_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
.

(4)

By
section

3
(4), for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

\frac{\left|\gamma E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}=\frac{\left|q\left(\gamma\right)D_{n}\triangle D_{n}\right|}{\left|D_{n}\right|}.

Hence,
\Phi_{E}\left(\gamma\right)=\Phi_{D}\left(q\left(\gamma\right)\right)
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since
q\mid_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\to\overline{\Gamma}
is an isomorphism, the recurrence of
\left(D_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
gives some
\kappa>0
such that

\sum\nolimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\exp\left(-\kappa\cdot\Phi_{E}\left(\gamma\right)\right)=\sum\nolimits_{\overline{\gamma}\in\overline{\Gamma}}\exp\left(-\kappa\cdot\Phi_{D}\left(\overline{\gamma}\right)\right)=+\infty.\qed

4.
Constructing left schemes on semidirect products

Throughout this section we isolate a criterion for recurrent left schemes in semidirect products

G=H\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z},

where
H
is countable nontrivial (typically amenable, potentially not finitely generated) and
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}\left(H\right)
. Fix a cyclic generator
s_{o}\in\mathbb{Z}<G
. Then every element of
G
has a unique normal form

hs_{o}^{a}

for some
h\in H
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, and the multiplication in
G
is given by

\left(h_{1}s_{o}^{a}\right)\left(h_{2}s_{o}^{b}\right)=h_{1}\phi^{a}\left(h_{2}\right)s_{o}^{a+b},\quad h_{1},h_{2}\in H,\,a,b\in\mathbb{Z}.

In particular, we have the useful relation

s_{o}^{a}hs_{o}^{-a}=\phi^{a}\left(h\right),\quad h\in H,\,a\in\mathbb{Z}.

Assume that there is a finite symmetric set
T\subseteq H
such that
G
is generated by

S:=T\cup\{s_{o}^{\pm 1}\}.

Note that
H
may not be finitely generated, and in particular
T
need not generate
H
(as it is indeed the case of amenable wreath products discussed below).

Proposition 4.1
.

For
G=H\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
as above, suppose there exist a sequence
\left(R_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
of finite sets in
H
, an integer sequence
\left(A_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
with
A_{n}\geq q^{n}
for some
q>1
, and a positive sequence
\left(\epsilon_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
, such that:

(1)

\sum_{n\geq 1}\epsilon_{n}<+\infty
.

(2)

The sets
\phi^{m}\left(R_{n}\right)
, for
n\geq 1
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, are pairwise disjoint.

(3)

For every
t\in T
and
n\geq 1
,

\frac{\left|\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|}{\left|R_{n}\right|}\leq\epsilon_{n},\quad 0\leq l<A_{n}.

Then
G
admits a recurrent
s_{o}
-left scheme.

Proof of
section

4
.

For every
n\geq 1
, define

F_{n}:=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{l=0}^{A_{n}-1}\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)\subset H,

and let
u_{n}:F_{n}\to\{0,\dotsc,A_{n}-1\}
be defined by
u_{n}\mid_{\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)}\equiv l
for
0\leq l<A_{n}
. Then put

E_{n}:=\big\{hs_{o}^{u_{n}\left(h\right)}:h\in F_{n}\big\}\subset G.

We show that
\left(E_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is a recurrent
s_{o}
-left scheme. First, since normal form in
G\cong H\rtimes_{\phi}\left\langle s_{o}\right\rangle
is unique,

E_{n}s_{o}=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{l=0}^{A_{n}-1}\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)s_{o}^{l+1},

and the potential common
s_{o}
-levels of
E_{n}s_{o}
and
E_{n}
are
1,\dotsc,A_{n}-1
. For every such
1\leq l<A_{n}
, the
H
-coordinates coming
from
E_{n}s_{o}
lie in
\phi^{l-1}\left(R_{n}\right)
, while the
H
-coordinates coming from
E_{n}
lie in
\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)
. These two sets are disjoint by condition (2), and hence
E_{n}s_{o}\cap E_{n}=\emptyset
. Now fix
t\in T
. For
h\in R_{n}
and
0\leq l<A_{n}
, since
\phi^{l}\in\mathrm{Aut}\left(H\right)
,

t\left(\phi^{l}\left(h\right)s_{o}^{l}\right)=\phi^{l}\left(\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)h\right)s_{o}^{l}.

Thus for each
l
, left multiplication by
t
sends
\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)s_{o}^{l}
onto
\phi^{l}\left(\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)R_{n}\right)s_{o}^{l}
. Since the levels
\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)
,
0\leq l<A_{n}
, are pairwise disjoint, comparing the symmetric difference in each level gives

\left|tE_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|=\sum\nolimits_{l=0}^{A_{n}-1}\left|\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|\leq\epsilon_{n}A_{n}\left|R_{n}\right|=\epsilon_{n}\left|E_{n}\right|.

It follows that

\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left|tE_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}\leq\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\epsilon_{n}<+\infty.

It remains to verify the summability condition for
s_{o}^{\pm 1}
. Since

s_{o}\left(\phi^{l}\left(h\right)s_{o}^{l}\right)=\phi^{l+1}\left(h\right)s_{o}^{l+1},

we get

s_{o}E_{n}=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{l=0}^{A_{n}-1}\phi^{l+1}\left(R_{n}\right)s_{o}^{l+1}.

Thus the contribution to the symmetric difference of
E_{n}
and
s_{o}E_{n}
comes only from the first level of
E_{n}
and the last level of
s_{o}E_{n}
. Hence, by the disjointness of the sets
\phi^{m}\left(R_{n}\right)
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\left|s_{o}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|=2\left|R_{n}\right|,\,\text{ hence }\,\frac{\left|s_{o}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}=\frac{2\left|R_{n}\right|}{A_{n}\left|R_{n}\right|}=\frac{2}{A_{n}}.

The same argument gives the same bound with
s_{o}^{-1}
. Therefore, using that
A_{n}\geq q^{n}
we get

\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left|s_{o}^{\pm 1}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}\leq 2\cdot\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{q^{n}}<+\infty.

Thus condition (2) of
s_{o}
-left scheme holds for every
g\in S
, and hence for every
g\in G
by
section

2.2
.

We will now verify the conditions for recurrent left scheme. Let
\Gamma:=\left\langle s_{o}\right\rangle
. For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
r\in R_{n}
, and
0\leq l<A_{n}
, one has

s_{o}^{a}\left(\phi^{l}\left(r\right)s_{o}^{l}\right)=\phi^{a+l}\left(r\right)s_{o}^{a+l}.

It follows that

\Gamma E_{n}=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)s_{o}^{l}.

Since
E_{n}=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{l=0}^{A_{n}-1}\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)s_{o}^{l}
, we get

\Gamma E_{n}\triangle E_{n}=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\dotsc,A_{n}-1\}}\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)s_{o}^{l}.

By condition (2), the sets
\phi^{l}\left(R_{n}\right)
, with

n\geq 1
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, are pairwise disjoint. Therefore, using uniqueness of normal form in
G=H\rtimes_{\phi}\left\langle s_{o}\right\rangle
, we get

\left(\Gamma E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right)\cap\left(\Gamma E_{m}\triangle E_{m}\right)=\emptyset\quad\text{for all }n\neq m.

This proves condition (3) of recurrent
s_{o}
-left schemes. Finally, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

s_{o}^{k}E_{n}=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{l=0}^{A_{n}-1}\phi^{l+k}\left(R_{n}\right)s_{o}^{l+k}.

Since the sets
\phi^{m}\left(R_{n}\right)
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, are pairwise disjoint, the overlap between
E_{n}
and
s_{o}^{k}E_{n}
occurs only on the levels with indices belonging to both
\{0,\dotsc,A_{n}-1\}
and
\{k,\dotsc,k+A_{n}-1\}
. We then have

\left|s_{o}^{k}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|=2\min\left\{\left|k\right|,A_{n}\right\}\left|R_{n}\right|,

and therefore

\frac{\left|s_{o}^{k}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}=2\min\Big\{\frac{\left|k\right|}{A_{n}},1\Big\}.

We then obtain the formula

\Phi\left(s_{o}^{k}\right)=\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left|s_{o}^{k}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}=2\cdot\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\min\Big\{\frac{\left|k\right|}{A_{n}},1\Big\}.

For
\left|k\right|\geq 1
, put
N_{k}:=\left\lfloor\log_{q}\left|k\right|\right\rfloor
. Using
A_{n}\geq q^{n}
, by dividing the sum for
n\leq N_{k}
and
n>N_{k}
we get

\Phi\left(s_{o}^{k}\right)\leq 2\cdot\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\min\Big\{\frac{\left|k\right|}{q^{n}},1\Big\}\leq 2N_{k}+2\left|k\right|\cdot\frac{q^{-N_{k}-1}}{1-q^{-1}}\leq 2\log_{q}\left|k\right|+\frac{2q}{q-1}.

Taking
\kappa:=\log q/2
, one finds a constant
C\left(q\right)>0
so that

\sum\nolimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\exp\left(-\kappa\Phi\left(\gamma\right)\right)\geq C\left(q\right)\cdot\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}=+\infty.\qed

4.1.
Amenable wreath products

For a nontrivial finitely generated amenable group
A
, consider the wreath product

A\wr\mathbb{Z}:=H\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z},\quad H:=\bigoplus\nolimits_{\mathbb{Z}}A,

where
\phi
is the left shift automorphism, acting on
H
by

\phi\left(\left(a_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\right)=\left(a_{k+1}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

For
a\in A
, denote by
\delta_{0}\left(a\right)\in H
the configuration with
a
at
0
and
e_{A}
everywhere else. Fix a finite symmetric generating set
Q\subset A
, and define

T:=\left\{\delta_{0}\left(q\right):q\in Q\right\}\subset H.

Fix a generator
s_{o}
of the cyclic factor
\mathbb{Z}
. Then a finite symmetric generating set of
A\wr\mathbb{Z}
is

S:=T\cup\left\{s_{o}^{\pm 1}\right\}.

We aim to use
section

4
. Since
A
is amenable, it has a sequence of finite sets
\left(K_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
with

\max_{q\in Q}\frac{\left|qK_{n}\triangle K_{n}\right|}{\left|K_{n}\right|}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

Fix
a_{o}\in A\setminus\left\{e_{A}\right\}
. For
n\geq 1
, put
A_{n}:=2^{n}
and define the set

R_{n}:=\left\{\eta\in H:\operatorname{supp}\left(\eta\right)\subseteq\left[-1,A_{n}\right],\,\eta\left(-1\right)=\eta\left(A_{n}\right)=a_{o},\,\eta\left(i\right)\in K_{n}\,\text{ for }i=0,1,\dotsc,A_{n}-1\right\}.

We then verify the conditions of
section

4
with
A_{n}
and
\epsilon_{n}=2^{-n}
.

Condition (1) is clear. For condition (2), let
n,n^{\prime}\geq 1
and
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and suppose

\eta\in\phi^{m}\left(R_{n}\right)\cap\phi^{m^{\prime}}\left(R_{n^{\prime}}\right)\neq\emptyset.

Then
\operatorname{supp}\left(\eta\right)
is contained in
\left[-m-1,A_{n}-m\right]\cap\left[-m^{\prime}-1,A_{n^{\prime}}-m^{\prime}\right]
, and additionally

\eta\left(-m-1\right)=a_{o}=\eta\left(A_{n}-m\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\eta\left(-m^{\prime}-1\right)=a_{o}=\eta\left(A_{n^{\prime}}-m^{\prime}\right).

Thus
-m-1
and
A_{n}-m
are the minimal and maximal elements of
\operatorname{supp}\left(\eta\right)
, respectively, and the same holds for
-m^{\prime}-1
and
A_{n^{\prime}}-m^{\prime}
. It follows that

-m-1=-m^{\prime}-1\quad\text{and}\quad A_{n}-m=A_{n^{\prime}}-m^{\prime},

so that
m=m^{\prime}
and
A_{n}=A_{n^{\prime}}
. Since
A_{n}=2^{n}
, we get
n=n^{\prime}
. This proves condition (2).

Finally we verify condition (3). Fix
t=\delta_{0}\left(q\right)\in T
,
n\geq 1
, and
0\leq l<A_{n}
. Then

\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)=\delta_{l}\left(q\right).

Multiplication of every element of
H
by
\delta_{l}\left(q\right)
changes only the
l
-th coordinate, and in that coordinate it replaces
K_{n}
by
qK_{n}
. Since the free coordinates of
R_{n}
are
0,1,\dotsc,A_{n}-1
, one verifies that

\left|\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|=\left|qK_{n}\triangle K_{n}\right|\left|K_{n}\right|^{A_{n}-1}.

Since
\left|R_{n}\right|=\left|K_{n}\right|^{A_{n}}
, it follows that

\frac{\left|\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|}{\left|R_{n}\right|}=\frac{\left|qK_{n}\triangle K_{n}\right|}{\left|K_{n}\right|}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

Thus all conditions of
section

4
hold, and therefore
A\wr\mathbb{Z}
admits a recurrent
s_{o}
-left scheme.

4.2.
Solvable Baumslag–Solitar groups

For every integer
d\geq 2
, let the Baumslag–Solitar group

\mathrm{BS}\left(1,d\right)\cong H\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z},\quad H:=\mathbb{Z}\left[\tfrac{1}{d}\right],

where
H
is written additively, and
\phi
is the automorphism
\phi\left(h\right)=dh
. Define

T:=\left\{\pm 1\right\}\subset H.

Fix a generator
s_{o}
of the cyclic factor
\mathbb{Z}
. Then a finite symmetric generating set of
\mathrm{BS}\left(1,d\right)
is

S:=T\cup\left\{s_{o}^{\pm 1}\right\}.

We aim to use
section

4
. For
n\geq 1
put
A_{n}:=2^{n}
. Fix a sequence
\left(I_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
of pairwise disjoint arithmetic progressions

I_{n}:=\left\{a_{n}+dj:0\leq j<d^{2A_{n}}\right\},

where
a_{n}\equiv 1\mod d
, and thus
I_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\setminus d\mathbb{Z}
and
\left|I_{n}\right|=d^{2A_{n}}
. Define

R_{n}:=\left\{rd^{-A_{n}}:r\in I_{n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{Z}\left[\tfrac{1}{d}\right].

We then verify the conditions of
section

4
with
A_{n}
and
\epsilon_{n}=2d^{-A_{n}}
.

Condition (1) is clear. For condition (2), note that every
0\neq x\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\tfrac{1}{d}\right]
has a unique representation

x=rd^{j}\,\text{ for some }r\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus d\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Suppose
x\in\phi^{m}\left(R_{n}\right)\cap\phi^{m^{\prime}}\left(R_{n^{\prime}}\right)
, and so
x=rd^{m-A_{n}}=r^{\prime}d^{m^{\prime}-A_{n^{\prime}}}
with
r\in I_{n}
and
r^{\prime}\in I_{n^{\prime}}
. Then

r=r^{\prime}\in I_{n}\cap I_{n^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad m-A_{n}=m^{\prime}-A_{n^{\prime}}.

By the disjointness of the
I_{n}
’s we get
n=n^{\prime}
, and then also
m=m^{\prime}
. This proves condition (2). Finally we verify condition (3). Look at
1\in T
, and fix
n\geq 1
and
0\leq l<A_{n}
. For every
rd^{-A_{n}}\in R_{n}
write

rd^{-A_{n}}+d^{-l}=\left(r+d^{A_{n}-l}\right)d^{-A_{n}}.

Because
d\nmid r
and
A_{n}-l\geq 1
, we also have
d\nmid r+d^{A_{n}-l}
. Therefore,

\phi^{-l}\left(1\right)+R_{n}=d^{-l}+R_{n}=\left\{sd^{-A_{n}}:s\in I_{n}+d^{A_{n}-l}\right\}.

Since
I_{n}
is a
d
-step arithmetic progression, we get

\left|\left(\phi^{-l}\left(1\right)+R_{n}\right)\triangle R_{n}\right|=\left|\left(I_{n}+d^{A_{n}-l}\right)\triangle I_{n}\right|\leq 2d^{A_{n}-l-1}\leq 2d^{A_{n}}.

Since
\left|R_{n}\right|=\left|I_{n}\right|=d^{2A_{n}}
, it follows that

\frac{\left|\left(\phi^{-l}\left(1\right)+R_{n}\right)\triangle R_{n}\right|}{\left|R_{n}\right|}\leq 2d^{-A_{n}}.

The same argument shows that the same bound holds also for
-1\in T
. Thus all conditions of
section

4
hold, and therefore

\mathrm{BS}\left(1,d\right)
admits a recurrent
s_{o}
-left scheme.

5.
Constructing left schemes on nilpotent groups

The following discussion is dedicated to constructing recurrent left schemes on general torsion-free
2
-step nilpotent groups, which is a significant part of the proof of our main theorems.

5.1.
Torsion-free 2-step nilpotent groups

We will use Hall–Malcev theory of torsion-free nilpotent groups in order to generalize the Heisenberg construction presented in
section

2.3
. This will be done using the semidirect product construction of
section

4
.

Recall that a Malcev basis of a group
G
is a tuple

\bm{B}=\left(g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\right),

so that every
g\in G
has a unique presentation of the form
g=g_{1}^{a_{1}}\dotsm g_{m}^{a_{m}}
for some
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Accordingly,
\bm{B}
is associated with the coordinate map

\chi:G\to\mathbb{Z}^{m},\quad\chi\left(g_{1}^{a_{1}}\dotsm g_{m}^{a_{m}}\right)=\left(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{m}\right).

We may sometimes write
\chi_{g_{j}}\left(g_{1}^{a_{1}}\dotsm g_{m}^{a_{m}}\right)=a_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq m
.

5.1.1.
A Heisenberg direction in torsion-free 2-step nilpotent groups

In the following we will freely use the basic facts mentioned at the beginning of appendix
B
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated torsion-free nonabelian
2
-step nilpotent group, and put

Z:=Z\left(G\right)\quad\text{and}\quad A:=G/Z.

Since
G
is torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent,
Z
is torsion-free finitely generated abelian, hence is a free abelian group of finite rank. We claim that
A
is also torsion-free; indeed, if
g^{m}\in Z
for some
g\in G
and
m\geq 1
, then because commutators are central in
2
-step nilpotent groups, for every
h\in G
one has

\left[g,h\right]^{m}=\left[g^{m},h\right]=e_{G}.

Since
G
is torsion-free,
\left[g,h\right]=e_{G}
for every
h\in G
, so
g\in Z
. Thus
A
is torsion-free. Since
G
is
2
-step nilpotent
\left[G,G\right]\subseteq Z\left(G\right)
, hence
A=G/Z
is abelian, and since
G
is finitely generated, so is
A
. Hence
A
is a free abelian group of finite rank.

Definition 5.1

(Malcev basis with a Heisenberg direction)
.

A Malcev basis

\bm{B}:=\left(x,x_{3},\dotsc,x_{r},y,z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right)

of
G
will be called a
Malcev basis with a Heisenberg direction
\left(x,y,z\right)
, if the following hold:

(1)

Z=\left\langle z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle
forms a basis of a free abelian group.

(2)

A=\left\langle\overline{x},\overline{x_{3}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{r}},\overline{y}\right\rangle
forms a basis of a free abelian group, where
\overline{\bullet}
is the image of
\bullet\in G
in
A
.

(3)

There exists
0<\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\left[y,x\right]=z^{\mu}
.

(4)

\left[x_{j},x\right]\in\left\langle w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle
for every
j=3,\dotsc,r
.

The power
\mu
will be referred to as the
height
of the Heisenberg direction
\left(x,y,z\right)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Every finitely generated torsion-free nonabelian
2
-step nilpotent group
G
admits a Malcev basis with a Heisenberg direction.

Proof of
section

5.1.1
.

Since
G
is
2
-step nilpotent we have
\left[G,G\right]\subseteq Z
, and since
Z
is central, changing representatives modulo
Z
does not change commutators. Hence the commutator form

\omega:A\times A\to Z,\quad\omega\left(\overline{g},\overline{h}\right):=\left[g,h\right]

is well-defined and bi-additive. Since
G
is nonabelian,
\omega
is nonzero. Therefore, after choosing a basis of
A
in an appropriate order, we can decompose

A=\left\langle\overline{x}\right\rangle\oplus A^{\prime},

where
A^{\prime}
is also a free abelian group, in such a way that the homomorphism

\beta:A^{\prime}\to Z,\quad\beta\left(\overline{g}\right):=\omega\left(\overline{g},\overline{x}\right)

is nonzero. Now
\beta
is a homomorphism between free abelian groups, and so using Smith normal form for
\beta
, after changing the bases of
A^{\prime}
and
Z
, we can write

A^{\prime}=\left\langle\overline{x_{3}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{r}},\overline{y}\right\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad Z=\left\langle z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle,

and find
0<\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\beta\left(\overline{y}\right)=z^{\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta\left(\overline{x_{j}}\right)\in\left\langle w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle\,\text{ for }j=3,\dotsc,r.

Choose lifts
x,x_{3},\dotsc,x_{r},y\in G
of
\overline{x},\overline{x_{3}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{r}},\overline{y}\in A
. Then we have

\left[y,x\right]=z^{\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad\left[x_{j},x\right]\in\left\langle w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle\,\text{ for }j=3,\dotsc,r.

Finally, we claim that the desired Malcev basis is

\bm{B}:=\left(x,x_{3},\dotsc,x_{r},y,z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right).

It only remains to check that
\bm{B}
is indeed a Malcev basis. To see this, for every
g\in G
, since

A=G/Z=\left\langle\overline{x},\overline{x_{3}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{r}},\overline{y}\right\rangle,

we may write

\overline{g}=\overline{x}^{a}\overline{x_{3}}^{a_{3}}\dotsm\overline{x_{r}}^{a_{r}}\overline{y}^{b}\text{ with unique exponents }a,a_{3},\dotsc,a_{r},b\in\mathbb{Z}.

Define
h:=x^{a}x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}
. Then
h^{-1}g\in Z
, and since
Z=\left\langle z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle
, we may write

h^{-1}g=z^{c}w_{2}^{d_{2}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}}\text{ with unique exponents }c,d_{2},\dotsc,d_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore
g=x^{a}x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c}w_{2}^{d_{2}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}}
. The uniqueness of this expression follows from the uniqueness of the image of
g
in
A
, and then the uniqueness of the coordinates of
h^{-1}g
in the basis of
Z
.
∎

Let us record how the coordinate map behaves under right multiplication by powers of
x
, and under left multiplication by the remaining basis elements.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\bm{B}=\left(x,x_{3},\dotsc,x_{r},y,z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right)
be a Malcev basis of
G
with a Heisenberg direction
\left(x,y,z\right)
of height
\mu
, and coordinate map
\chi=\left(\chi_{x},\chi_{x_{3}},\dotsc,\chi_{x_{r}},\chi_{y},\chi_{z},\chi_{w_{2}},\dotsc,\chi_{w_{s}}\right):G\to\mathbb{Z}^{r+s}
. Put

H:=\left\langle x_{3},\dotsc,x_{r},y,z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle\leq G.

Then for every
h=x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c}w_{2}^{d_{2}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}}\in H
, the following relations hold.

(1)

For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\chi_{y}\left(hx^{k}\right)=\chi_{y}\left(h\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{z}\left(hx^{k}\right)=\chi_{z}\left(h\right)+\mu k\chi_{y}\left(h\right).

(2)

For
t=x_{j}^{\pm 1}
with
3\leq j\leq r
,

th=x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{j}^{a_{j}\pm 1}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c^{\prime}}w_{2}^{d_{2}^{\prime}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}^{\prime}},

where each of
c^{\prime}-c,d_{2}^{\prime}-d_{2},\dotsc,d_{s}^{\prime}-d_{s}
depends linearly, with integer coefficients, on
a_{3},\dotsc,a_{j-1}
.

(3)

For
t=y^{\pm 1}
,

th=x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b\pm 1}z^{c^{\prime}}w_{2}^{d_{2}^{\prime}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}^{\prime}},

where each of
c^{\prime}-c,d_{2}^{\prime}-d_{2},\dotsc,d_{s}^{\prime}-d_{s}
depends linearly, with integer coefficients, on
a_{3},\dotsc,a_{r}
.

(4)

For
t=z^{\pm 1}
,

th=x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c\pm 1}w_{2}^{d_{2}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}}.

(5)

For
t=w_{j}^{\pm 1}
with
2\leq j\leq s
,

th=x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c}w_{2}^{d_{2}}\dotsm w_{j}^{d_{j}\pm 1}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}}.

Proof of
section

5.1.1
.

Fix
h=x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c}w_{2}^{d_{2}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}}\in H
. Since
G
is
2
-step nilpotent, we repeatedly use the identity
uv=vu\left[u,v\right]
, noting that
\left[u,v\right]
is always central.

For (1), as
\left[y,x\right]=z^{\mu}
and
\left[x_{j},x\right]\in\left\langle w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle
for
j=3,\dotsc,r
, moving
x^{k}
to the left gives

hx^{k}=x^{k}x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c+\mu kb}w\,\text{ for some }w\in\left\langle w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle.

Then we get
\chi_{y}\left(hx^{k}\right)=b=\chi_{y}\left(h\right)
and
\chi_{z}\left(hx^{k}\right)=c+\mu kb=\chi_{z}\left(h\right)+\mu k\chi_{y}\left(h\right)
.

For (2), let
t=x_{j}^{\pm 1}
with
3\leq j\leq r
. To write
th
in normal form,
x_{j}^{\pm 1}
only needs to commute with
x_{3}^{a_{3}},\dotsc,x_{j-1}^{a_{j-1}}
. Since
G
is
2
-step nilpotent, the only effect is multiplication by the central element

\prod\nolimits_{i=3}^{j-1}\left[x_{j},x_{i}\right]^{\pm a_{i}}.

The coordinates of each fixed commutator
\left[x_{j},x_{i}\right]
in the basis
\left(z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right)
are fixed integers. Hence the resulting changes in the exponents of
z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}
depend linearly on
a_{3},\dotsc,a_{j-1}
.

For (3), let
t=y^{\pm 1}
. To write
th
in normal form, one has to commute
y^{\pm 1}
with
x_{3}^{a_{3}},\dotsc,x_{r}^{a_{r}}
. The resulting commutator contribution is

\prod\nolimits_{i=3}^{r}\left[y^{\pm 1},x_{i}^{a_{i}}\right]=\prod\nolimits_{i=3}^{r}\left[y,x_{i}\right]^{\pm a_{i}},

which is central. Again, when this product is written in the basis
\left(z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right)
, each exponent depends linearly on
a_{3},\dotsc,a_{r}
.

For (4) and (5), if
t=z^{\pm 1}
or
t=w_{j}^{\pm 1}
with
2\leq j\leq s
then
t
is central, so the relations are clear.
∎

5.1.2.
Construction of a Heisenberg-like recurrent left scheme

Let
G
be a finitely generated torsion-free nonabelian
2
-step nilpotent group. Using
section

5.1.1
, fix a Malcev basis

\bm{B}=\left(x,x_{3},\dotsc,x_{r},y,z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right)

with a Heisenberg direction
\left(x,y,z\right)
of height
\mu
. Let
\pi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
be the homomorphism defined by

\pi\left(x\right)=1,\quad\pi\left(x_{3}\right)=\dotsb=\pi\left(x_{r}\right)=\pi\left(y\right)=\pi\left(z\right)=\pi\left(w_{2}\right)=\dotsb=\pi\left(w_{s}\right)=0,

and consider its kernel

H:=\ker\pi=\left\langle x_{3},\dotsc,x_{r},y,z,w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle.

Since
G
is torsion-free, the element
x
has infinite order, and

G\cong H\rtimes_{\phi}\left\langle x\right\rangle,\quad\text{where }\phi\left(h\right):=xhx^{-1}.

Set

s_{o}:=x,\quad T:=\left\{x_{3}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,x_{r}^{\pm 1},y^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 1},w_{2}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,w_{s}^{\pm 1}\right\}\subset H.

Then
T\cup\left\{x^{\pm 1}\right\}
generates
G
.

Choose positive integer sequences
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}
such that

\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{A_{n}}<+\infty,\quad\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{B_{n}}<+\infty,\quad\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}<+\infty.

For the recurrent divergence condition we further assume that
A_{n}
has at least exponential growth,

A_{n}\geq q^{n}\quad\text{for some }q>1.

Define an integer sequence
b_{n}
inductively by

b_{1}:=\big\lceil\tfrac{C_{1}+1}{\mu}\big\rceil,\quad b_{n+1}:=\max\Big\{\big\lceil\tfrac{C_{n+1}+1}{\mu}\big\rceil,\,b_{n}+B_{n}+1\Big\},

and denote the integer intervals

I_{n}:=\left[b_{n},b_{n}+B_{n}\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}.

The choice of
b_{n}
ensures the properties

\mu b_{n}>C_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{n}\cap I_{m}=\emptyset\,\text{ for }n\neq m.

Define the sets
R_{n}\subset H
by

R_{n}:=\Bigg\{x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c}w_{2}^{d_{2}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}}:\begin{array}[]{l}0\leq a_{3},\dotsc,a_{r}<A_{n},\\
b\in I_{n},\\
0\leq c,d_{2},\dotsc,d_{s}<C_{n}\end{array}\Bigg\}.

By uniqueness of coordinates in the Malcev basis,

\left|R_{n}\right|=A_{n}^{r-2}B_{n}C_{n}^{s}.

We verify the hypotheses of
section

4
(with the above
A_{n}
, and
\epsilon_{n}
that will be specified later).

First we verify condition (2). Let

h=x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c}w_{2}^{d_{2}}\dotsm w_{s}^{d_{s}}\in R_{n}.

Let
0\neq m\in\mathbb{Z}
be fixed. By
section

5.1.1
(1) applied to
hx^{-m}
, one has

hx^{-m}=x^{-m}x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c-\mu mb}w

for some
w\in\left\langle w_{2},\dotsc,w_{s}\right\rangle
, and therefore

\phi^{m}\left(h\right)=x^{m}hx^{-m}=x_{3}^{a_{3}}\dotsm x_{r}^{a_{r}}y^{b}z^{c-\mu mb}w.

In particular,
\phi^{m}
preserves the
y
-coordinate. Since
h\in R_{n}
we have
b\geq b_{n}
, and therefore

\left|\mu mb\right|=\mu\left|m\right|b\geq\mu b_{n}>C_{n}>c\geq 0.

If
m>0
then
c-\mu mb<0
, while if
m<0
then

c-\mu mb>C_{n}
. In either case,
c-\mu mb\notin\left[0,C_{n}\right)
. This shows that for every
m\neq 0
and every
n
,

\phi^{m}\left(R_{n}\right)\cap R_{n}=\emptyset.

Since
\phi
is an automorphism, it follows that for every fixed
n
, the sets

\phi^{m}\left(R_{n}\right)
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, are pairwise disjoint. Since
\phi^{m}
preserves the
y
-coordinate and since the intervals
I_{n}
are pairwise disjoint, the sets

\phi^{m}\left(R_{n}\right),\quad n\geq 1,\ m\in\mathbb{Z},

are pairwise disjoint. This proves condition (2) of
section

4
.

In order to verify condition (3) of
section

4
, we establish the following estimate.

Claim 5.4
.

There exists a constant
K>0
such that for every
t\in T
,
n\geq 1
, and
0\leq l<A_{n}
,

\frac{\left|\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|}{\left|R_{n}\right|}\leq K\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}}+\frac{1}{B_{n}}+\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right).

Proof of
section

5.1.2
.

We first estimate
tR_{n}\triangle R_{n}
uniformly for
t\in T
. By
section

5.1.1
(2)–(5), left multiplication by
t
changes at most one of the exponents
a_{3},\dotsc,a_{r},b
by
\pm 1
, and additionally, the changes in the exponents
c,d_{2},\dotsc,d_{s}
are bounded by a constant multiple of
A_{n}
. Indeed, for
t=x_{j}^{\pm 1}
or
t=y^{\pm 1}
, these changes depend linearly on some of
a_{3},\dotsc,a_{r}
, and these exponents lie in
\left[0,A_{n}\right)
; for
t=z^{\pm 1}
or
t=w_{i}^{\pm 1}
, only one of
c,d_{2},\dotsc,d_{s}
changes by
\pm 1
. Since
T
is finite, there exists
M>0
, independent of
n
and
t
, such that all these changes are bounded by
MA_{n}
.

Therefore, if
g\in R_{n}\setminus t^{-1}R_{n}
, then one of the following occurs: one of the coordinates
a_{3},\dotsc,a_{r}
of
g
is within distance
1
from an endpoint of
\left[0,A_{n}\right)
; or the coordinate
b
of
g
is within distance
1
from an endpoint of
I_{n}
; or one of the coordinates
c,d_{2},\dotsc,d_{s}
of
g
is within distance
MA_{n}
from an endpoint of
\left[0,C_{n}\right)
. Hence, there is a constant
K^{\prime}>0
, independent of
t
and
n
, such that

\left|R_{n}\setminus t^{-1}R_{n}\right|\leq K^{\prime}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}}+\frac{1}{B_{n}}+\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right)\cdot\left|R_{n}\right|.

Since
T
is symmetric, the same estimate holds with
t^{-1}
in place of
t
, and hence also for
\left|R_{n}\setminus tR_{n}\right|
. Then

(5.1)

\left|tR_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|=\left|tR_{n}\setminus R_{n}\right|+\left|R_{n}\setminus tR_{n}\right|\leq 2K^{\prime}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}}+\frac{1}{B_{n}}+\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right)\cdot\left|R_{n}\right|.

Now let
0\leq l<A_{n}
. Since
G
is
2
-step nilpotent, we may write

\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)=x^{-l}tx^{l}=t\left[t,x\right]^{l}=tc_{t}^{l},\quad c_{t}:=\left[t,x\right]\in Z\leq H.

Since
T
is finite, multiplication by
c_{t}^{l}
changes each of the coordinates
c,d_{2},\dotsc,d_{s}
by at most a constant multiple of
l
, for all
t\in T
uniformly. As
0\leq l<A_{n}
, the same counting argument as above gives a constant
K^{\prime\prime}>0
, independent of
n,t,l
, such that

\frac{\left|c_{t}^{l}R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|}{\left|R_{n}\right|}\leq K^{\prime\prime}\cdot\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}.

Using the triangle inequality for symmetric differences, we get

(5.2)

\left|\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|\leq\left|tc_{t}^{l}R_{n}\triangle tR_{n}\right|+\left|tR_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|=\left|c_{t}^{l}R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|+\left|tR_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|.

Combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we find a constant
K>0
, independent of
t
,
n
, and
0\leq l<A_{n}
, such that

\frac{\left|\phi^{-l}\left(t\right)R_{n}\triangle R_{n}\right|}{\left|R_{n}\right|}\leq K\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}}+\frac{1}{B_{n}}+\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right).\qed

Finally, by virtue of
section

5.1.2
, condition (3) of
section

4
holds with

\epsilon_{n}:=K\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}}+\frac{1}{B_{n}}+\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right),

and the sequence

7.
Asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles on virtually cyclic groups

We start by noting that the commutativity obstruction to
\ell^{2}
-asymmetry generalizes to the class of FC-groups, which by definition are groups whose every element has finite conjugacy class.

Claim 7.1
.

If
G
is an FC-group then
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)=\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
.

Proof of
section

7
.

For
g,h\in G
write
g^{h}:=hgh^{-1}
. For every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{G}
and every
g,h\in G
one has

\left(\rho\left(g^{-1}\right)\xi-\xi\right)\left(h\right)=\xi\left(hg^{-1}\right)-\xi\left(h\right)=\left(\lambda\left(g^{h}\right)\xi-\xi\right)\left(h\right),

and therefore

\left\|\rho\left(g^{-1}\right)\xi-\xi\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}^{2}\leq\sum\nolimits_{c\in g^{G}}\left\|\lambda\left(c\right)\xi-\xi\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}^{2}.

Now if
G
is an FC-group then
g^{G}
is finite, and hence
\xi\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)
implies that
\rho\left(g^{-1}\right)\xi-\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
. This shows that
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
. The converse follows symmetrically.
∎

Nevertheless, in the broader class of virtually abelian groups, or even virtually cyclic groups, there is no general obstruction for
\ell^{2}
-asymmetry. Indeed, it was observed by M. Kapovich
[
19
]
that the infinite dihedral group, which is virtually cyclic but not an FC-group, admits asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles. We shall explain this asymmetry from the viewpoint of multiple ends in groups. The relevant feature is not merely the existence of more than one end
(
\mathbb{Z}
is
2
-ended), but rather the existence of an end represented by
a subset which is left-commensurated but not bi-commensurated, in the sense of
Cornulier
[
7
, §1.4]
.

Let us start with the main instance of this phenomenon. Consider the infinite dihedral group

D_{\infty}=\left\langle r,s\mid s^{2}=e,\ srs=r^{-1}\right\rangle.

It is virtually abelian since

\left\langle r\right\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}
has index
2
, and it is still not an FC-group since
r^{n}sr^{-n}=r^{2n}s
, and so the conjugacy class of
s
is infinite. Since every element is of the form
r^{n}
or
r^{n}s
with some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is the decomposition

D_{\infty}=E_{+}\sqcup E_{-},

where

E_{+}:=\left\{r^{n}:n>0\right\}\cup\left\{r^{n}s:n<0\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{-}:=\left\{r^{n}:n\leq 0\right\}\cup\left\{r^{n}s:n\geq 0\right\}.

The set
E_{+}
is almost invariant under left translations by the generators:

sE_{+}\triangle E_{+}=\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad rE_{+}\triangle E_{+}\text{ is finite}.

Thus
E_{+}
forms one of the two ends of
D_{\infty}
. However,
E_{+}
is
not almost invariant under right translations:

E_{+}s\triangle E_{+}=\left(E_{-}\setminus\{e,s\}\right)\triangle E_{+}\,\,\,\text{is infinite}.

Thus the end represented by
E_{+}
is not bi-commensurated in the sense of
[
7
]
.

As we shall see, this one-sided commensuration produces asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles. This mechanism is different from the one coming from left schemes: in the virtually cyclic non-FC case, left schemes do not exist, while asymmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles do. The following proposition isolates this
phenomenon.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
G
be an infinite finitely generated virtually cyclic group. Then:

(1)

G
admits no left scheme.

(2)

\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)=\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)\iff G
is an FC-group.

Proof of
section

7
.

Since
G
is virtually cyclic, by an alternative of Wall
[
21
, Thm. 5.12]
,
[
7
, §1.4]
there is a finite normal subgroup
K\triangleleft G
such that either
G/K\cong\mathbb{Z}
or
G/K\cong D_{\infty}
. If
G/K\cong\mathbb{Z}
then
G
is an FC-group; indeed, if
q:G\to\mathbb{Z}
is the quotient map, then
q\left(hgh^{-1}\right)=q\left(g\right)
for all
g,h\in G
, so the conjugacy class of
g
is contained in the finite coset
gK
. Then
\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)=\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
by
section

7
, and so
G
cannot admit a left scheme by
section

2.2
. Thus both
assertions are proved in this case. Then for the rest of the proof we assume that
G/K\cong D_{\infty}
, and denote the quotient map
q:G\to D_{\infty}
.

We first prove (1). Assume
towards a contradiction that
\left(E_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is an
s_{o}
-left scheme for an infinite order element
s_{o}\in G
. Since
K
is finite,
q\left(s_{o}\right)\in D_{\infty}
has infinite order, and so necessarily
q\left(s_{o}\right)\in\left\langle r\right\rangle
. Then for every
g\in G
,

q\left(s_{o}g\right)=q\left(s_{o}\right)q\left(g\right)=q\left(g\right)q\left(s_{o}\right)^{\epsilon\left(g\right)}\,\text{ for some }\epsilon\left(g\right)\in\{1,-1\},

depending on whether
q\left(g\right)\in\left\langle r\right\rangle
or
q\left(g\right)\in\left\langle r\right\rangle s
. Hence there is
k\left(g\right)\in K
such that
s_{o}g=gs_{o}^{\epsilon\left(g\right)}k\left(g\right)
. It follows that for
every finite set
E
in
G
,

s_{o}E\cap E\subseteq\left(Es_{o}K\cap E\right)\cup\left(Es_{o}^{-1}K\cap E\right).

Now assume that
E
satisfies
Es_{o}\cap E=\emptyset
, and then also

Es_{o}^{-1}\cap E=\emptyset
. For every
k\in K
we have

\left(Es_{o}^{\pm 1}k\cap E\right)k^{-1}\subseteq Es_{o}^{\pm 1}\cap Ek^{-1}\subseteq Ek^{-1}\setminus E\subseteq KE\setminus E\subseteq KE\triangle E,

where the second containment follows from
Es_{o}^{\pm 1}\cap E=\emptyset
, and the
third is because
K\triangleleft G
. Therefore,

\left|Es_{o}^{\pm 1}k\cap E\right|=\left|\left(Es_{o}^{\pm 1}k\cap E\right)k^{-1}\right|\leq\sum\nolimits_{c\in K}\left|cE\triangle E\right|.

Altogether we obtain

\left|s_{o}E\cap E\right|\leq\sum\nolimits_{k\in K,\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}}\left|Es_{o}^{\epsilon}k\cap E\right|=\sum\nolimits_{k\in K,\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}}\left|\left(Es_{o}^{\epsilon}k\cap E\right)k^{-1}\right|\leq 2\left|K\right|\cdot\sum\nolimits_{c\in K}\left|cE\triangle E\right|.

Then for every
n\geq 1
, applying this to
E=E_{n}
and using that
E_{n}s_{o}\cap E_{n}=\emptyset
, we get

\left|s_{o}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|=2\left|E_{n}\right|-2\left|s_{o}E_{n}\cap E_{n}\right|\geq 2\left|E_{n}\right|-4\left|K\right|\cdot\sum\nolimits_{c\in K}\left|cE_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|.

It then follows that

\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left|s_{o}E_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}\geq 2\cdot\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}1-4\left|K\right|\cdot\sum\nolimits_{c\in K}\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left|cE_{n}\triangle E_{n}\right|}{\left|E_{n}\right|}=+\infty,

because the second term is finite by the definition of left scheme for each
c\in K
. This contradicts the left scheme condition for
s_{o}
, and hence
G
admits no left scheme.

We now prove (2). One implication is
section

7
. For the converse, define

\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{\infty}}
by

\eta\left(r^{m}\right)=\begin{cases}0&m\leq 0\\
1/\sqrt{m}&m>0\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta\left(r^{m}s\right)=\eta\left(r^{-m}\right).

We will leave it to the reader to verify that
\eta\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(D_{\infty},\lambda\right)
(because
\sum_{m\geq 1}\big|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\big|^{2}<\infty
) while
\eta\notin\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(D_{\infty},\rho\right)
(in fact
\left\|\rho\left(s\right)\eta-\eta\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(D_{\infty}\right)}^{2}\asymp\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{1}{\left|m\right|}=\infty
). Define
\xi:=\eta\circ q\in\mathbb{R}^{G}
. As
K
is finite, one verifies that
\xi\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\lambda\right)
, and
\rho\left(s_{o}\right)\xi-\xi\notin\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
for any
s_{o}\in q^{-1}\left(s\right)
, so
\xi\notin\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(G,\rho\right)
.
∎

Appendix A
Symmetric
\ell^{2}
-cocycles on nonamenable groups

The following result with its proof are due to Stefaan Vaes, who kindly allowed us to include it here.

Theorem A.1

(S. Vaes)
.

Let
G
be a countable nonamenable group, and suppose
\xi:G\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\lambda\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\rho\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\quad\text{ for every }g\in G.

Then
\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}
.

The following is a type of Reiter/Kesten spectral gap reformulation of nonamenability.

Lemma A.2
.

For every nonamenable group
G
there is a finite set
F\subset G
, such that
the operator

T:=\frac{1}{\left|F\right|}\sum\nolimits_{g\in F}\left(\lambda\left(g\right)-\mathrm{Id}\right)

is invertible on
\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
.

Proof of Lemma
A
.

Since
G
is nonamenable, the trivial representation is not weakly contained in the left-regular representation
[
2
, §G.3]
. Therefore, there exist a finite set
F\subset G
and
\epsilon>0
such that

\max_{g\in F}\left\|\lambda\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}\geq\epsilon\quad\text{whenever }\left\|\xi\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}=1.

Replacing
F
by
F\cup F^{-1}
, we may assume that
F
is symmetric. Set

U:=\frac{1}{\left|F\right|}\sum\nolimits_{g\in F}\lambda\left(g\right),\text{ so that }T=U-\mathrm{Id}.

Since
F
is symmetric
U
is self-adjoint. For every unit vector
\xi\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
we have

2-2\left\langle U\xi,\xi\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\left|F\right|}\sum\nolimits_{g\in F}\left\|\lambda\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}^{2}\geq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\left|F\right|},\quad\text{hence}\quad\left\langle U\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leq 1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2\left|F\right|}.

Letting
\alpha:=1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2\left|F\right|}<1
, we get that
\sup\sigma\left(U\right)\leq\alpha<1
hence
\sup\sigma\left(T\right)=\sup\sigma\left(U\right)-1\leq\alpha-1<0
. Thus
0\notin\sigma\left(T\right)
so that
T
is invertible on
\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

Consider the invertible operator
T
produced in Lemma
A
. Then

T\xi=\frac{1}{\left|F\right|}\sum\nolimits_{g\in F}\left(\lambda\left(g\right)\xi-\xi\right)\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right).

Using that
T
is invertible, define the vectors

\eta:=T^{-1}\left(T\xi\right)\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta:=\xi-\eta,

and by construction
T\zeta=0
. Now fix an arbitrary
h\in G
, and we have

\rho\left(h\right)\zeta-\zeta=\left(\rho\left(h\right)\xi-\xi\right)-\left(\rho\left(h\right)\eta-\eta\right)\in\ell^{2}\left(G\right).

Since
\lambda
and
\rho
commute, the operator
T
commutes with
\rho\left(h\right)
, hence

T\left(\rho\left(h\right)\zeta-\zeta\right)=\rho\left(h\right)T\zeta-T\zeta=0.

As
T
is injective on
\ell^{2}\left(G\right)
, this implies that
\rho\left(h\right)\zeta=\zeta
. Since
h
is arbitrary,
\zeta
is a constant vector, so let
p\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\zeta\left(h\right)=p
for all
h\in G
. Then
\left\|\xi-p\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}=\left\|\xi-\zeta\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}=\left\|\eta\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(G\right)}<+\infty
.
∎

Appendix B
Basic facts about nilpotent groups

following are some elementary facts for which we could not find direct proofs in the literature.

Fix a finitely generated
c
-step nilpotent group
G
, with its lower central series

G=\gamma_{1}\left(G\right)\vartriangleright\gamma_{2}\left(G\right)\vartriangleright\dotsm\vartriangleright\gamma_{c}\left(G\right)\vartriangleright\gamma_{c+1}\left(G\right)=\left\{e_{G}\right\},\qquad\gamma_{i+1}\left(G\right):=\left[G,\gamma_{i}\left(G\right)\right],\quad i=1,\dotsc,c.

Since the abelianization
G/\left[G,G\right]
is finitely generated abelian,
G
is polycyclic by
[
22
, Cor. 8, p. 13]
. Since nilpotent groups are solvable, every subgroup of
G
is finitely generated by
[
22
, Prop. 4, p. 4]
. Additionally, each quotient
\gamma_{i}\left(G\right)/\gamma_{i+1}\left(G\right)
is a finitely generated abelian group by
[
22
, Cor. 7, p. 13]
.

Lemma B.1
.

A finitely generated nilpotent group
G
is virtually abelian if and only if it is an FC-group.

Proof of
appendix

B
.

For
g\in G
, let

C_{G}\left(g\right):=\left\{h\in G:hg=gh\right\}
and
g^{G}:=\left\{g^{h}=hgh^{-1}:h\in G\right\}
. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem for the conjugation action of
G
on itself, for every
g\in G
one has

\left[G:C_{G}\left(g\right)\right]=\left|g^{G}\right|.

For one implication, every finitely generated FC-group
G
is virtually abelian; indeed, fix a finite generating set
S
, and for every
s\in S
one has

\left[G:C_{G}\left(s\right)\right]=\left|s^{G}\right|<\infty.

At the same time,

Z\left(G\right)=\left\{z\in G:zg=gz\,\,\,\forall g\in G\right\}=\bigcap\nolimits_{s\in S}C_{G}\left(s\right).

Since
S
is finite and each
C_{G}\left(s\right)
has finite index, it follows that
Z\left(G\right)
is abelian and of finite index.

For the converse, assume
G
is finitely generated virtually abelian nilpotent. Let
A\leq G
be abelian and of finite index. We first claim that we may assume that
A\triangleleft G
is torsion-free. To see this, recall that since
A
is finitely generated abelian, its torsion subgroup
\tau\left(A\right)
is finite, so let
m
be the exponent of
\tau\left(A\right)
, and define

B:=mA.

Then
B
is finitely generated abelian torsion-free with finite index in
A
. Since
A
has finite index in
G
, then
B
has finite index in
G
. Then
B
has finitely many conjugacy classes in
G
, hence its normal core

N:=\bigcap\nolimits_{g\in G}gBg^{-1}

is a finite intersection of finite index subgroups of
G
, and so
N
has finite index in
G
. Moreover,
N\leq B
is torsion-free abelian. Then after replacing
A
by
N
, we may assume that the finite index abelian group
A\leq G
is also torsion-free and normal in
G
.

As
A
is finitely generated torsion-free abelian, identify
A
with
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for some
r\geq 0
. Since
A\triangleleft G
, we consider conjugation by elements of
G
as automorphisms of
A
:

\chi_{A}:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\left(A\right)\cong\operatorname{GL}_{r}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right).

For every
g\in G
, set

T_{g}:=\chi_{A}\left(g\right)-I\in\operatorname{End}\left(A\right).

Since
A\leq\ker\left(\chi_{A}\right)
, the group
\chi_{A}\left(G\right)
is a quotient of the finite group
G/A
. In particular,
\chi_{A}\left(G\right)
is finite, and so every
\chi_{A}\left(g\right)
has finite order. Define

A_{i}:=A\cap\gamma_{i}\left(G\right),\quad 1\leq i\leq c+1,

and we claim that
T_{g}\left(A_{i}\right)\leq A_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq c
;
2
2

2

This means that the conjugation action of
G
on
A
is nilpotent; see
[
22
, §1B, p. 9]
.
indeed, if
a\in A_{i}
and
g\in G
, then

T_{g}\left(a\right)=\chi_{A}\left(g\right)\left(a\right)-a=\left[g^{-1},a^{-1}\right]\in A\cap\left[\gamma_{i}\left(G\right),G\right]=A\cap\gamma_{i+1}\left(G\right)=A_{i+1}.

Then by induction we get

T_{g}^{c}\left(A\right)=T_{g}^{c}\left(A_{1}\right)\leq A_{c+1}=\left\{0\right\}.

This means that
\chi_{A}\left(g\right)
is unipotent for every

. As every finite-order unipotent matrix over

is trivial,
\chi_{A}
is the trivial homomorphism. Then the conjugation action of
G
on

is trivial, so

. Since

has finite index, also

has finite index. Then

is an FC-group, since for every

\left|g^{G}\right|=\left[G:C_{G}\left(g\right)\right]\leq\left[G:Z\left(G\right)\right]<\infty.\qed

Lemma B.2
.

Every finitely generated non-virtually abelian nilpotent group
G
is an extension of a non-virtually abelian torsion-free
2
-step nilpotent group by an amenable group.

In the proof we will use the following observation. If
T\triangleleft G
is amenable and
G/T
is an extension of a group
Q
by an amenable group, then
G
is also an extension of
Q
by an amenable group. Indeed, if

is an exact sequence with
A
,
Q
, and
G/T
all amenable, then let
\pi:G\to G/T
be the quotient map, and by composing
\pi
with the quotient map

, we obtain the exact sequence

1\to\pi^{-1}\left(A\right)\to G\to Q\to 1.

Moreover,
\pi^{-1}\left(A\right)
is amenable, for we have the exact sequence

1\to T\to\pi^{-1}\left(A\right)\to A\to 1,

and amenability is preserved under extensions by amenable groups (see e.g.
[

).

appendix

B
.

Let
T:=\tau\left(G\right)
be the torsion subgroup, which is finite by

. Let

\left\{e_{G}\right\}=Z_{0}\left(G\right)\triangleleft Z_{1}\left(G\right)\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft Z_{c}\left(G\right)=G,\qquad Z_{i}\left(G\right)/Z_{i-1}\left(G\right)=Z\left(G/Z_{i-1}\left(G\right)\right),

be the upper central series of

. Define

Then

, and therefore

is abelian. Hence

is

is torsion-free, since

is torsion-free, so by

are torsion-free. We claim that

is torsion-free; indeed, if

is torsion-free and

G
is nilpotent and so

with
