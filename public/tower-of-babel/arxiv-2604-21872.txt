Quark and gluon production in the presence of the time-varying chiral magnetic current
Kirill Tuchin

Abstract
The chiral magnetic effect (CME) consists in the induction of the electric current along the direction of the magnetic field. The corresponding transport coefficient b 0 b_{0} , known as the chiral magnetic conductivity, is proportional to the chiral imbalance in the medium. In many systems, such as quark-gluon plasma, b 0 b_{0} is time-dependent. This paper studies the effect of the time variation of b 0 b_{0} on the particle spectra and energy loss produced through the chiral Cherenkov and associated processes in Abelian and non-Abelian systems. The rates of all processes are derived in the ultra-relativistic approximation. The results are applied to the relativistic heavy-ion collisions utilizing a specific model describing the relaxation of the initial P P -odd domain within the quark-gluon plasma. The corresponding energy loss is computed. The results suggest strong polarization of jets in quark-gluon plasma.



I
Introduction

An ultra-relativistic particle losses most of its energy in a medium due to radiation. The amount of energy lost and the spectrum of the emitted radiation reflect the medium properties. This paper investigates novel mechanisms of energy loss in chiral medium, which contains chiral fermions. The peculiar properties of energy loss in a chiral medium are due to the chiral anomaly, which induces P and CP-odd transport phenomena
Kharzeev 2014
. One such phenomenon is the chiral magnetic effect (CME), whereby electric current is induced in the direction of an external magnetic field:
{\bm{j}}=b_{0}{\bm{B}}

Kharzeev 2006
;
Kharzeev et al. 2008
;
Kharzeev 2010
;
Kharzeev and Zhitnitsky 2007
;
Fukushima et al. 2008
;
Barredo-Alamilla et al. 2023
;
von Dossow et al. 2026
. Another closely related phenomenon is the anomalous Hall effect, where a current is induced in the direction perpendicular to an external electric field:
{\bm{j}}={\bm{b}}\times{\bm{E}}
. The chiral magnetic conductivity
b_{0}
is proportional to the chiral chemical potential
\mu_{5}
, which reflects the net chirality in the medium. The non-Abelian version of the chiral magnetic effect was investigated in
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
;
Duari et al. 2026
.

As a result of the chiral magnetic effect, new channels of energy loss are opened in empty space due to the momentum supplied by the chiral magnetic current. These processes include the chiral Cherenkov radiation and its cross channels, which have been studied in Ref.
Tuchin 2018a
;
Huang and Tuchin 2018
;
Tuchin 2018b
;
Hansen and Tuchin 2021
;
Hansen and Tuchin 2022
;
Hansen and Tuchin 2023
;
Hansen et al. 2025
, and recently reviewed in
Hansen and Tuchin 2024a
. The cascade induced by these novel channels were studied in
Hansen and Tuchin 2025
. Additionally, the non-Abelian version of the chiral Cherenkov radiation was explored in
Hansen and Tuchin 2024b
. While the anomalous Hall current also contributes to energy loss, as discussed in
Huang and Tuchin 2018
;
Tuchin 2018b
;
Hansen et al. 2025
;
Tuchin 2025
, the present paper assumes a spatially uniform medium, so we disregard the anomalous Hall currents in our analysis.

The references mentioned above ignore the time-variation of the chiral magnetic conductivity
b_{0}
. However, in the quark-gluon plasma produced in relativistic heavy-ion collisions the chirality is sourced by the parallel chromoelectric and chromomagnetic fields in the initial stage following a heavy-ion collision
Lappi and Schlichting 2018
or by sphaleron transitions during the plasma’a evolution
Fukushima et al. 2008
;
Arnold et al. 1997
;
Arnold et al. 1999
;
Bodeker 1998
. These processes introduce the time-dependence of
b_{0}
. The time-dependence of
b_{0}
can also be driven by the chiral plasma instability
Joyce and Shaposhnikov 1997
;
Boyarsky et al. 2012
;
Hirono et al. 2015
;
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
;
Tuchin 2015
;
Buividovich and Ulybyshev 2016
;
Manuel and Torres-Rincon 2015
;
Kirilin and Sadofyev 2017
. This motivated my recent study of photon production in the presence of time-dependent chiral imbalance
Tuchin 2025
;
Tuchin 2026
. This paper builds upon that work and presents a comprehensive calculation of all processes contributing to energy loss in both Abelian and non-Abelian media. I derive general expressions for the rates of all parton splitting processes at any
b_{0}(t)
and then apply these expressions to a specific model describing particle production in the presence of a decaying
P
-odd domain in heavy-ion collisions.

II
Photon and gluon wave functions in the presence of CME

II.1
QED

Perturbative calculations in chiral medium in the presence of the chiral magnetic current are facilitated by an effective field theory obtained by adding the Chern-Simons term to the Maxwell Lagrangian
Wilczek 1987
;
Carroll et al. 1990
;
Sikivie 1984
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}\left(F_{\mu\nu}^{2}+c_{1}\theta F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)\,,

(1)

where
F_{\mu\nu}
is the electromagnetic field tensor,
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}F^{\lambda\rho}
is its dual and
c_{1}
is the chiral anomaly coefficient given by
c_{1}=\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}N_{c}\sum_{f}q_{f}^{2}
. The Chern-Simons term is coupled to an external pseudo-scalar field
\theta
chosen to reproduce the chiral magnetic and anomalous Hall currents. Namely,
c_{1}\partial_{\mu}\theta=b_{\mu}=(b_{0},-{\bm{b}})
, where
b_{\mu}
is a function of time but not spatial coordinates. The axial chemical potential
\mu_{5}
is identified with
\dot{\theta}
, so that
b_{0}=c_{1}\mu_{5}
.
The medium is assumed to be isotropic, implying that
{\bm{b}}=0
. Perturbation theory in the presence of finite
{\bm{b}}
has been discussed in
Huang and Tuchin 2018
;
Tuchin 2018b
;
Hansen et al. 2025
;
Tuchin 2025
.

The electromagnetic field, in the radiation gauge
A^{0}=0
,
{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}}=0
, is described by the vector potential
{\bm{A}}
that satisfies the equation:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}{\bm{A}}-\partial_{t}^{2}{\bm{A}}+b_{0}(t){\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{A}}=0\,.

(2)

It is assumed that the chiral magnetic conductivity
b_{0}
is a given function of time that is unaffected by the radiation. The wave function of a photon of energy
\omega
, momentum
{\bm{k}}
and polarization
\lambda
is a plane wave solution to (
2
):

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}(t,{\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}a(t){\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}\,.

(3)

The photons must be circularly polarized with the polarization vector
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda=\pm 1
, which obeys the equation
i\hat{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}=\lambda{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}
. The product of the photon polarization vectors satisfies the following identity:

\displaystyle{\bm{\epsilon}}^{i*}_{\lambda}{\bm{\epsilon}}^{j}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)+i\lambda\varepsilon^{ij\ell}\frac{k^{\ell}}{k}\right]\,,

(4)

where no summation over
\lambda
is implied.

Substituting (
3
) into (
1
) one finds that the amplitude
a(t)
is governed by the equation

\displaystyle\ddot{a}(t)+\Omega^{2}(t)a(t)=0\,,

(5)

where

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}(t)=k^{2}-\lambda b_{0}(t)k\,.

(6)

Eq. (
5
) can be solved in a general form, assuming that
\Omega(t)
is slowly varying function (see e.g.
Tuchin 2025
):

\displaystyle a(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\Omega}}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}\Omega(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\,.

(7)

The amplitude
a
is normalized such that the wave function (
3
) represents one photon per unit volume. Expanding
\Omega
in the ultra-relativistic limit
k\gg b_{0}
:

\displaystyle\Omega(t)\approx k-\frac{\lambda b_{0}(t)}{2}

(8)

leads to the final result for the amplitude:

\displaystyle a(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}e^{-ikt+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\,.

(9)

The validity of (
9
) is restricted to photon momenta
k
that satisfy the condition:

\displaystyle\left|\dot{b}_{0}\right|\ll k^{2}\,,

(10)

where the dot indicates the time-derivative.

The scattering matrix elements are proportional to the Fourier image of the amplitude:

\displaystyle\tilde{a}(q)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{i(q+i\epsilon\sgn t)t}a(t)dt\,.

(11)

The positive infinitesimal parameter
\epsilon
ensures convergence of the integral. Substituting (
9
) into (
11
) one obtains:

\displaystyle\tilde{a}(q)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon t+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\cos\left[(k-q)t-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right]dt\,,

(12)

where the functions

\displaystyle b_{0}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{0}(t)+b_{0}(-t)}{2}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle b_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{0}(t)-b_{0}(-t)}{2}\,,

(14)

are respectively the even and odd components of
b_{0}(t)
.

The wave functions of fermions are not modified and given by the standard expressions. Hence the wave function of an incident fermion with momentum
{\bm{p}}
and spin
s
reads:

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bm{p}}s}(t,{\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2EV}}u_{s}({\bm{p}})e^{-iEt+i{\bm{p}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}\,,

(15)

where
E=\sqrt{p^{2}+m^{2}}
.

Another formulation of the effective theory is possible if one uses the expression for the divergence of the axial vector current
j^{\mu 5}=\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi
to obtain the effective Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}+\theta\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu 5}\,.

(16)

Integrating the second term by parts yields a term proportional to
b_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi
. In this formulation, the chiral anomaly modifies only the fermion sector. The two formulations (
1
) and (
16
) appear to be equivalent in the chiral limit, as they transform into each other through the chiral rotation. However, this contention has not been rigorously verified.

II.2
QCD

The color version of the chiral magnetic effect, which describes the induction of the color current in the direction of the color magnetic field, can be similarly described by adding the corresponding non-Abelian Chern-Simons term to the Yang-Mills Lagrangian. The effective Lagrangian is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\Tr\left(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right)-\frac{c_{2}}{2}\theta\Tr\left(F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)\,,

(17)

The field tensor and the potentials are defined as usual:

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}-ig[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]\,,

(18)

A_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}
,
F_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\nu}^{a}t^{a}
, where
t^{a}
are the SU(3) generators and the anomaly coefficient is
c_{2}=\frac{g^{2}N_{f}}{4\pi^{2}}
.

The linearized equations of motion clearly have the same form as those discussed in the preceding section for the Abelian case. The corresponding plane wave solutions are given by

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{b}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}(t,{\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}a(t)e^{b}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}\,.

(19)

Here,
e^{b}
is a unit vector in the adjoint color representation, which we will omit from the equations henceforth, as per usual convention. The amplitude
a(t)
is given by (
9
) in the ultra-relativistic approximation.

Unlike the Abelian Chern-Simons term, the non-Abelian one includes a cubic term that contributes to the triple-gluon interaction vertex. Substituting the identity

\displaystyle\Tr\left(F^{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)=2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_{\mu}\Tr\left(A_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}A_{\sigma}-g\frac{2i}{3}A_{\nu}A_{\rho}A_{\sigma}\right)

(20)

into the second term in (
17
) and integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\theta}=c_{2}\partial_{\mu}\theta\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{2}A^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}A^{a}_{\sigma}-g\frac{2i}{3}\frac{1}{4}if^{abc}A^{a}_{\nu}A^{b}_{\rho}A^{c}_{\sigma}\right)\,.

(21)

The first term in (
20
) contributes to the equation of motion, while the second one to the triple-gluon vertex. The corresponding Feynman rule for the anomalous contribution to the triple-gluon vertex in momentum space is:

\displaystyle\text{anomalous triple-gluon vertex}=gb_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}f^{abc}\,,

(22)

where we follow the conventions of
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
. It was shown in
Hansen and Tuchin 2024b
that this contribution is suppressed in the ultra-relativistic limit by the inverse power of particles’ energy.

In summary, in the ultra-relativistic limit the effect of anomaly is to impart a time- and helicity-dependent phase

\displaystyle e^{\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}

(23)

to the photon and gluon fields, as compared to their expressions in empty space. Note also that all medium effects other than the anomalous chiral magnetic and Hall currents are omitted for simplicity.

II.3
Plasma oscillations

Thus far, we ignored the collective medium effects on the wave functions of gauge bosons. In the ultra-relativistic limit discussed in this work, these effects can be described by the plasma frequency
\omega_{p}
. In the quark-gluon plasma at temperature
T
it is given by
\omega_{p}^{2}=\frac{g^{2}T^{2}}{18}(2N_{c}+N_{f})

Rischke 2004
.

The plasma oscillations modify Eq. (
2
), which is obeyed by the electromagnetic vector potential, as follows:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}{\bm{A}}-\partial_{t}^{2}{\bm{A}}+b_{0}(t){\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{A}}-\omega_{p}^{2}{\bm{A}}=0\,.

(24)

As a result, the function
\Omega(t)
reads:

\displaystyle\Omega(t)=\sqrt{k^{2}-\lambda b_{0}(t)k+\omega_{p}^{2}}\approx k-\frac{\lambda b_{0}}{2}+\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{2k}\,,

(25)

where we assumed that
k\gg\omega_{p}\gg|b_{0}|
as favored by the phenomenological applications. A similar equation holds for the gluon field. In place of (
9
), we obtain for the amplitude:

\displaystyle a(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}e^{-ikt+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}-\frac{i\omega_{p}^{2}t}{2k}}\,.

(26)

To keep equations less bulky, the plasma frequency will be omitted in all expressions unless otherwise indicated.

III
Parton spectra

Now, I move on to the main section of the paper, where the expressions for the photon and gluon fields derived in the previous section are utilized to calculate the differential probabilities of photon, gluon, and quark production.

III.1
q\to q+\gamma
and
q\to q+g

First, consider photon radiation by an ultra-relativistic quark. The initial and final quark momenta are
p_{z}\hat{{\bm{z}}}
and
{\bm{p}}’
, respectively. The photon’s momentum is
{\bm{k}}=xp_{z}\hat{{\bm{z}}}+{\bm{k}}_{\bot}
, where
x
is the longitudinal momentum fraction it carries and
{\bm{k}}_{\bot}
its component in the
xy
-plane. The corresponding energies are

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=p_{z}\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{2p_{z}^{2}}\right)\,,

(27)

\displaystyle E^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(1-x)p_{z}\left(1+\frac{m^{2}+k_{\bot}^{2}}{2(1-x)^{2}p_{z}^{2}}\right)\,,

(28)

where the terms of the order
k_{\bot}^{2}/p_{z}^{2}
and higher are neglected. The energy is not conserved unless
b_{0}
is constant.

The element of the scattering matrix describing photon radiation reads:

\displaystyle S(q\to q\gamma)

\displaystyle=e\int\bar{\psi}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}s^{\prime}}{\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{A}}^{*}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}\psi_{{\bm{p}}s}d^{3}xdt=e\frac{\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}({\bm{p}}^{\prime}){\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}^{*}_{\lambda}u_{s}({\bm{p}})}{2V^{3/2}\sqrt{EE^{\prime}}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bm{p}}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime}-{\bm{k}})\tilde{a}^{*}(E-E^{\prime})\,,

(29)

where
\tilde{a}
is given by (
12
) and
e
is the quark’s electric charge. The photon radiation probability is then calculated as

\displaystyle dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda,s,s^{\prime}}|S(q\to q\gamma)|^{2}\frac{Vd^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{Vd^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}=\frac{e^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{3}E}\sum_{\lambda}|\tilde{a}(E-E^{\prime})|^{2}d^{2}k_{\bot}\frac{dx}{1-x}\Tr[(\not{p}+m)\not{\epsilon}^{*}_{\lambda}(\not{p}^{\prime}+m)\not{\epsilon}_{\lambda}]\,,

(30)

which, upon using (
27
),(
28
) and (
4
), yields the differential probability
Tuchin 2025
:

\displaystyle\frac{dw(q\to q\gamma)}{d^{2}k_{\bot}dx}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8(2\pi)^{3}E}\sum_{\lambda}|\tilde{a}(E-E^{\prime})|^{2}\frac{2e^{2}}{x(1-x)^{2}}\left\{k_{\bot}^{2}\frac{1+(1-x)^{2}}{x}+m^{2}x^{3}\right\}\,.

(31)

Using (
12
) and taking into account that

\displaystyle k-E+E^{\prime}=\frac{k_{\bot}^{2}+x^{2}m^{2}}{2x(1-x)p_{z}}\,,

(32)

one obtains:

\displaystyle\tilde{a}(E-E^{\prime})=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon t+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\cos\left[\frac{k_{\bot}^{2}+x^{2}m^{2}}{2x(1-x)p_{z}}t-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right]dt\,.

(33)

The gluon spectrum emitted by an ultra-relativistic quark differs from (
31
) only by a color factor and the replacement
e\to g
:

\displaystyle\frac{dw(q\to qg)}{d^{2}k_{\bot}dx}

\displaystyle=C_{F}\frac{dw(q\to q\gamma)}{d^{2}k_{\bot}dx}\bigg|_{e\to g}\,,

(34)

where
x
and
k_{\bot}
represent the longitudinal momentum fraction and the transverse momentum of the appropriate gauge boson.

Constant

III.1.2

In this specific model,

\displaystyle I=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon\tau}e^{\frac{i\Lambda}{2}B\tau\log\cosh\frac{t}{\tau}}\cos\left(\alpha t-\frac{\Lambda}{2}At\right)dt\,.

\displaystyle I=\frac{\tau 2^{-i\Lambda B\tau/2-2}}{\Gamma(-i\Lambda B\tau/2)}\Gamma\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda\tau}{2}(A+B)+i\alpha\tau+\epsilon\tau\right)\right]\Gamma\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{i\Lambda\tau}{2}(A-B)-i\alpha\tau+\epsilon\tau\right)\right]\,.

In particular, replacing

\displaystyle\tilde{a}(E-E^{\prime})=\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{2k_{z}}}\frac{2^{-i\lambda B\tau/2-1}}{\Gamma(-i\lambda B\tau/2)}\Gamma\left[\frac{i\tau}{2}\left(-\frac{\lambda}{2}(A+B)+\frac{k_{\bot}^{2}+x^{2}m^{2}}{2x(1-x)p_{z}}-i\epsilon\right)\right]

III.2
\gamma\to q+\bar{q}
and

The quark and antiquark pair production process is the cross-channel of photon production. Given the incident photon’s momentum
{\bm{k}}
and the momenta of the quark and antiquark as

and
{\bm{p}}’
respectively, the corresponding

\displaystyle S(\gamma\to q\bar{q})

\displaystyle=e\int\bar{\psi}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}s^{\prime}}{\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{A}}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}\psi_{{\bm{p}}s}d^{3}xdt=e\frac{\bar{u}_{s}({\bm{p}}){\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}v_{s^{\prime}}({\bm{p}}^{\prime})}{2V^{3/2}\sqrt{EE^{\prime}}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bm{p}}+{\bm{p}}^{\prime}-{\bm{k}})\tilde{a}(E+E^{\prime})\,,

The pair-production probability reads

\displaystyle dw(\gamma\to q\bar{q})

Using (

E

V
Conclusions

I computed the spectra of quarks, gluons and photons produced by ultra-relativistic particles in the presence of an arbitrary time-dependent chiral magnetic current. Eqs. (
31
),(
33
),(
34
) describe the photon and gluon spectra produced by an ultra-relativistic quark in the chiral Cherenkov processes
q\to q+\gamma
and
q\to q+g
. Eqs. (
44
),(
40
),(
47
) describe the quark spectrum produced by an ultra-relativistic gluon of photon in the pair production processes
\gamma\to q+\bar{q}
and
g\to q+\bar{q}
. Eqs. (
65
),(
63
) describe the gluon spectrum produced by an ultra-relativistic gluon in the process
g\to g+g
. These formulas only include contributions proportional to
b_{0}
, which arise due to the chiral magnetic effect, whereas the well-known conventional contributions are omitted.

Using a specific model
b_{0}(t)=A+B\tanh\frac{t}{\tau}
that describes the net chirality relaxation in the original
P
-odd domain, I derived the analytical expressions for the corresponding spectra given by (
III.1.2
),(
III.2.2
) and (
III.3.2
). The essential feature of this model is that it provides a smooth approximation of the step-function. The details of the transition between the two limiting values
b_{0}\to\pm\infty
are not essential. For practical illustration, the spectra (
III.1.2
),(
III.2.2
) and (
III.3.2
) were applied to relativistic heavy-ion collisions, assuming an initial chiral magnetic conductivity of
15
MeV and a relaxation time of 5 fm/
c
. The results are presented in Figs.
1
,
2
and
3
. As expected for chiral Cherenkov radiation, the spectra exhibit a strong chirality dependence.

The spectra show a characteristic resonance structure that was noted in previous publications
Tuchin 2025
;
Tuchin 2026
. The sensitivity of the spectra, in general, and this structure in particular, to collective plasma oscillations was studied by incorporating a finite plasma frequency
\omega_{p}
into the gluon spectrum. As shown in Figs. Fig.
1
,Fig.
2
and Fig.
3
, the gluon spectrum is suppressed, while the quark spectrum is enhanced due to the dependence of the phase space volume on
\omega_{p}
.

The amount of energy lost by a particle in the relaxing
P
-odd domain due to the chiral magnetic effect is shown in Fig.
4
for each process. The strong variation of the energy loss with gluon polarization implies significant jet polarization in quark-gluon plasma. This observation may serve as an effective tool for studying parity-violating processes in hot nuclear matter.
