Topological singular set of manifold-valued maps weakly approximable by smooth maps
Giacomo Canevari and Giandomenico Orlandi

Abstract
Given a positive integer p p , we consider W 1 , p W^{1,p} -maps from a Euclidean domain of dimension p + 1 p+1 into a closed Riemannian manifold 𝒩 \mathscr{N} . The target manifold is required to satisfy suitable topological conditions; in particular, the action of π 1 ​ ( 𝒩 ) \pi_{1}(\mathscr{N}) over the π p ​ ( 𝒩 ) \pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}) must be trivial. However, we do not assume that 𝒩 \mathscr{N} is ( p − 1 ) (p-1) -connected. Using tools from geometric measure theory — namely, flat chains with coefficients in π p ​ ( 𝒩 ) \pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}) — we associate to each map u u in the weak sequential closure of smooth maps an object that captures its point singularities. The vanishing of this object characterizes local strong approximability by smooth maps.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathscr{N}
be a smooth, compact, connected manifold
without boundary, which we identify with a submanifold
of some Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
and let
p\geq 1
. We consider the Sobolev space of
\mathscr{N}
-valued maps on
the domain
\Omega
,

W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N}):=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{R}^{m})\colon u(x)\in\mathscr{N}\textrm{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\right\}\!.

Sobolev spaces of manifold-valued maps arise naturally in many contexts
in which admissible configurations are constrained to lie on a nonlinear target,
such as harmonic maps
[
29
,
38
]
, condensed matter physics
(more precisely, in the modelling of partially ordered materials)
[
40
]
,
and numerical graphics (in cross-fields algorithms for domain meshing)
[
25
]
.

A natural question is whether maps in
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

can be approximated, in a suitable sense, by maps in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N})
— that is,
\mathscr{N}
-valued maps that can be smoothly extended to a neighbourhood of
\overline{\Omega}
.
Classical regularisation techniques based on convolution
are not enough to address this question, because
the nonlinear constraint
u(x)\in\mathscr{N}
is not preserved by convolution
with a mollifying kernel. In fact, the answer to this question
depends on the topology of
\Omega
and
\mathscr{N}
, as well as the Sobolev exponent
p
.
For instance, in case the domain
\Omega
is contractible, smooth maps are dense in
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
with respect to the strong
W^{1,p}
-topology if and only if
p\geq m
or the homotopy group
\pi_{\lfloor p\rfloor}(\mathscr{N})
is trivial
[
47
,
4
]
(here
\lfloor p\rfloor
denotes the largest integer not greater than
p
).
Indeed, when
p<m
and
\pi_{\lfloor p\rfloor}(\mathscr{N})
is nontrivial, elements of
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
may exhibit topological singularities that act as an obstruction toward approximability by smooth maps.
Further work on this problem (see e.g.
[
34
,
5
,
13
,
43
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
18
]
) eventually led to a complete characterisation of fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
, with arbitrary
s>0
,
p\geq 1
, and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in which smooth maps are strongly dense, see
[
27
]
.

When strong density of smooth maps fails, it is natural to investigate weaker notion of approximability. In this paper, we are interested in the sequential weak closure of smooth maps,
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
. More precisely, we will say that a function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
belongs to
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
if and only if there exists a sequence of maps
\varphi_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N})
such that
\varphi_{j}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
as
j\to+\infty
and
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<+\infty
. (This notion of weak convergence in
W^{1,p}
coincides with the standard one for
p>1
, but actually corresponds to weak
∗
convergence in
\BV
when
p=1
).
When
p
is
not
an integer, this approach does not provide any further information, for
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
coincides with the strong closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N})

[
4
, Theorem 3]
.
On the other hand, for integer values of
p
, the set
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
may be strictly larger than the strong closure of smooth maps — in fact, this is always the case if
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
is nontrivial, see
[
4
, Theorem 5]
,
[
35
, Theorem 5.5]
— and we may have weak sequential density of smooth maps even if strong density fails.
For instance, the equality
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})=W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
holds for any integer
p\geq 1
and any
(p-1)
-connected target manifold
\mathscr{N}
, that is, any
\mathscr{N}
such that
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N})\simeq\dotsb\simeq\pi_{p-1}(\mathscr{N})\simeq\{0\}
(see
[
4
, Theorem 6]
and
[
34
]
), and for a wider class of targets
\mathscr{N}
, including some non-simply connected ones, when
p=2

[
44
]
.
However, the equality need not hold in general.
In a groundbreaking result, Bethuel
[
7
]
showed that for any domain
\Omega
of dimension
n\geq 4
, there holds

(1)

H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,3}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{S}^{2})\subsetneq W^{1,3}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{S}^{2}).

More generally, for any integer
p\geq 2
Detaille and Van Schaftingen
[
28
]
constructed a manifold
\mathscr{N}_{p}
such that, for any domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of dimension
n\geq p+1
, there holds

(2)

H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N}_{p})\subsetneq W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N}_{p}).

In the same paper, the authors also proved that

(3)

H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,4k-1}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{S}^{2k})\subsetneq W^{1,4k-1}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{S}^{2k})

for any integer
k\geq 1
and any domain
\Omega
of dimension
n\geq 4k
.
These examples reveal the presence of analytical obstructions to weak sequential density in addition to topological ones, as previously observed in lifting problems
[
12
,
8
,
41
]
and trace extension problems
[
10
,
6
,
42
]
.

In this work, we aim at describing the topological singularities of maps in
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
, in case
p\geq 1
is an integer and
\Omega
is a bounded, Lipschitz domain of dimension
p+1
.
In other words, to each map
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
, we associate a geometric object
\mathbf{S}(u)
— roughly speaking, a countable collection of points equipped with multiplicities in a suitable group — that describe the singularities of
u
and satisfies appropriate notions of continuity.
Such an object acts as a (local) topological obstruction to strong approximability by smooth maps — that is, if
\Omega
is contractible, then
u
belongs to the strong closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N})
if and only if
\mathbf{S}(u)=0
.
Moreover,
\mathbf{S}
turns out to be useful in variational problems featuring energy concentration and emergence of topological singularities (see e.g.
[
2
,
23
,
24
,
21
]
.
In the case
u\in W^{1,2}(B^{3},\mathbb{S}^{2})
, Bethuel first identified
\mathbf{S}(u)
with the distributional Jacobian
[
3
]
, i.e., the exterior differential
\mathrm{d}(u^{*}\omega_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})
of the pull-back of the volume form
\omega_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
.
This approach, based on differential forms, was later adopted by Bethuel, Coron, Démengel and Hélein
[
9
]
, who considered maps
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
for a larger class of exponents
p
and manifolds
\mathscr{N}
, and by Detaille, Mironescu and Xiao, who considered fractional Sobolev maps
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
with
s>0
possibly smaller than one.
However, differential forms cannot describe topological obstructions carried by finite-order elements of the homotopy groups of
\mathscr{N}
.
Nonetheless,
\mathbf{S}(u)
is well-defined for any
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
so long as
\mathscr{N}
satisfies suitable topological conditions — namely,
\mathscr{N}
is
(\lfloor p\rfloor-1)
-connected and, in case
1\leq p<2
,
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N})
is Abelian.
This construction is carried out by Pakzad and Rivière
[
44
]
, under the additional assumption that
1\leq p<2
or
n-1\leq p<n
, and then again in
[
22
]
.
For manifolds
\mathscr{N}
that do not necessarily satisfy these topological conditions, Hardt and Rivière
[
37
]
constructed an object that is well-defined for (all integer
p
and) all
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{p+1},\,\mathscr{N})
and describes the rational homotopy singularities of
u
— that is, those described by
\mathrm{Hom}\,(\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}),\,\mathbb{R})
.

Our setting is reminiscent of that of
[
37
]
, as we consider maps
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
for integer
p
and a domain
\Omega
of dimension
n=p+1
. However, our construction differs from the one in
[
37
]
because we aim at describing all singularities of
u
, including those that are associated with finite-order elements of
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
.
Contrary to
[
44
,
22
]
, we do
not
assume that
\mathscr{N}
is
(p-1)
-connected.
Instead, we make the following assumptions:

(H
1
)

the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N})
acts trivially on
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
;

(H
2
)

for all
\Lambda>0
, only finitely
many homotopy classes
\sigma\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
contain
maps
v\in\sigma\cap W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{p}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}\leq\Lambda.

For a thorough discussion on these assumptions, we refer the reader to Section
3.1
.
(In Section
2
, we introduce the relevant topological notions assuming little or no prior knowledge on the reader’s part.)
Here, we only remark that these assumptions, contrarily to the ones in
[
44
,
22
]
, are compatible with settings in which
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})\subsetneq W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
— for instance, the case
p=3
,
\mathscr{N}=\mathbb{S}^{2}
, which is studied in
[
46
,
7
]
.

We define the topological singular set
\mathbf{S}(u)
of a map
u
as a (
0
-dimensional) flat chains with coefficients in a normed Abelian group, as introduced in
[
32
]
(see also Section
2.1
for more details).
The coefficient group is the homotopy group
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
, which is Abelian, as a consequence of assumption
H1
.
In Section
2.4.2
, we define a suitable norm on
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})

(depending on the choice of a basepoint
z_{0}\in\mathscr{N}
, although all possible choices of
z_{0}
lead to equivalent norms).
This norm induces the discrete topology on
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
— in fact, there exists a constant
\alpha_{p}>0
such that

(4)

\left|\sigma\right|\geq\alpha_{p}\qquad\textrm{for all }\sigma\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}),\,\sigma\neq 0.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p+1}
be a bounded, Lipschitz domain.
A flat chain
S\in\mathbb{F}_{0}(\Omega;\,\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}))
is a finite or countably infinite (formal) sum of points in
\Omega
, with coefficients in
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
, that can be written in the form

S=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\llbracket z_{i}\rrbracket+\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\sigma_{j}\left(\llbracket x_{j}\rrbracket-\llbracket y_{j}\rrbracket\right)+S_{\partial\Omega},

where
\gamma_{i}\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
,
\sigma_{j}\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
,
z_{i}
,
x_{j}
,
y_{j}
are points in
\overline{\Omega}
, and
S_{\partial\Omega}
is a combination of points of
\partial\Omega
, in such a way that the quantity

(5)

\begin{split}\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(S)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|\gamma_{i}\right|+\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left|\sigma_{j}\right|\left|x_{j}-y_{j}\right|\colon\gamma_{i},\,\sigma_{j},\,z_{i},\,x_{j},\,y_{j}\textrm{ as above}\right\}\!.\end{split}

We also define

(6)

\begin{split}\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(S)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|\gamma_{i}\right|+\frac{1}{\alpha_{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left|x_{j}-y_{j}\right|\colon\gamma_{i},\,\sigma_{j},\,z_{i},\,x_{j},\,y_{j}\textrm{ as above}\right\}\end{split}

for all chains
S
, where
\alpha_{p}
is the (optimal) constant as in (
4
). By definition, we have
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(S)\leq\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(S)
for all chains
S
.
The norms
\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}
and
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}
are equivalent to one another if
\Omega
is finite, but in general
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}
is weaker.

For a map
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
that is continuous out of a finite set
\Sigma=\Sigma(u)
of point discontinuities, it is natural to define

(7)

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u):=\sum_{x\in\Sigma}\sigma(u,\,x)\llbracket x\rrbracket,

where
\sigma(u,\,x)\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
is the free homotopy class
of
u
on any sufficiently small sphere centred at
u
,
whose interior contains no singular point of
u
other than
x
.
(Thanks to Assumption (
H1
), we can identify
free homotopy classes of maps
\mathbb{S}^{p}\to\mathscr{N}
with elements
of
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
.)
A classical result by Bethuel
[
4
, Theorem 2]
shows that that maps with finitely many point discontinuities are indeed strongly dense in
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
.
We claim that
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}
can be extended by continuity to an operator
\mathbf{S}
defined on all of
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
, and that the
\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}
-norm of
\mathbf{S}(u)
can be estimated from above in terms of the “relaxed energy”,

(8)

\overline{D}_{p}(u):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\varphi_{j}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\colon(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N}),\ \varphi_{j}\to u\textrm{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)\right\}\!.

More precisely, we have the following result.
We denote by
H_{\mathrm{strong,loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
the set of maps
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
such that the restriction of
u
to any ball
B
whose closure is contained in
\Omega
belongs to the strong
W^{1,p}
-closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{B},\,\mathscr{N})
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p\geq 1
be an integer,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p+1}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and
\mathscr{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a closed, connected manifold satisfying the conditions (
H1
)–(
H2
).
Then,
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}
extends, in a unique way, to a map

\mathbf{S}\colon H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})\to\mathbb{F}_{0}(\Omega;\,\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}))

that is continuous in the following sense:
if
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

such that
u_{j}\to u
strongly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)

for some
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

and
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{D}_{p}(u_{j},\Omega)<+\infty
,
then
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}(u_{j})-\mathbf{S}(u))\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
.
For all
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
we have

(9)

\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}(u))\leq C\,\overline{D}_{p}(u),

for some constant
C
that depends only
on
\Omega
,
\mathscr{N}
,
p
.
Moreover, a map
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
belongs
to
H_{\mathrm{strong,loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
if and only if
\mathbf{S}(u)=0
.

While the estimate (
9
) provides a bound for
\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}
, the continuity is formulated in terms of the weaker norm
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}
.
We have not investigated whether this notion of continuity is optimal.
Another possible question, inspired by the counterexamples in
[
7
,
28
]
, is whether one can construct a further extension of the operator
\mathbf{S}
, defined for all maps in
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
for instance, whose
\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}
is finite exactly over the maps in
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
.
We do not have an answer to that question.
Contrary to the proofs of
[
44
,
37
]
— which rely on the use of differential forms — and that of
[
22
]
— which relies on a projection argument, inspired by
[
36
]
—, the proof of Theorem
1
is based on an approximation of
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}
using suitable grids.
Some of the proofs (see for instance Lemma
3.2
) rely on extension arguments for manifold-valued maps that are reminiscent of
[
39
, Lemma 1]
.
As a preliminary result (see Proposition
4.2
), we prove that any map
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
can be strongly approximated by a sequence of maps
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with finitely many point discontinuities, in such a way that the relaxed energy
\overline{D}_{p}(u_{j})
remains bounded.

The paper is organised as follows.
Section
2
introduces some preliminary notions such as zero-dimensional flat chains with coefficients in a group (Subsection
2.1
), notation for grids and the deformation theorem for zero-dimensional chains (Subsection
2.2
), Sobolev spaces of manifold-valued maps (Subsection
2.3
), and (free) homotopy classes of maps
\mathbb{S}^{p}\to\mathscr{N}
(Subsection
2.4
).
Section
3
introduces an approximation of
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}
based on grids and contains some of the key technical lemmas.
Section
4.1
contains the proof that any element of
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
can be approximated by a sequence of maps wt finitely many point singularities, in such a way that the relaxed energy remains bounded.
Finally, Section
5
contains the proof of Theorem
1
.

2
Notation and preliminaries

2.1
Zero-dimensional flat chains

Let
\mathbb{G}
be an Abelian group. We write the operation on
\mathbb{G}

using additive notation. Let
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|

be a norm on
\mathbb{G}
, that is, a function
\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
the following conditions:

(i)

\left|\sigma\right|\geq 0
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{G}
,
with equality if and only if
\sigma=0
;

(ii)

\left|\sigma\right|=\left|-\sigma\right|
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{G}
;

(iii)

\left|\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\right|\leq\left|\sigma_{1}\right|+\left|\sigma_{2}\right|

for all
\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{G}
,
\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
.

We assume that the norm satisfies the additional condition

(2.1)

\alpha:=\inf\left\{\left|\sigma\right|\colon\sigma\in\mathbb{G},\ \sigma\neq 0\right\}>0.

In particular,
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|
induces the discrete topology on
\mathbb{G}
.
Let
n\geq 2
be an integer.
We define the Abelian group
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\,\mathbb{G})

of polyhedral
0
-chains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with coefficient in
\mathbb{G}

as the tensor product between
\mathbb{G}

and the free Abelian group generated by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The elements of
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\,\mathbb{G})

are (formal) finite sums of the form

(2.2)

S=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sigma_{j}\llbracket x_{j}\rrbracket,

where all the coefficients
\sigma_{j}
are elements of
\mathbb{G}

and the
x_{j}
’s are points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
(The brackets
\llbracket\,\cdot\,\rrbracket
do not have any
particular meaning here, but they would be useful
in defining higher-dimensional chains.
We write them in the
0
-dimensional case, too,
just to keep the notation uniform.)
The operation on
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\mathbb{G})
is induced by
the one in
\mathbb{G}
and obeys the property

\sigma_{1}\llbracket x\rrbracket+\sigma_{2}\llbracket x\rrbracket=(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})\llbracket x\rrbracket
,
for all
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
and
x\in\Omega
.
By applying this property repeatedly, we can write
any polyhedral chain
S
in the form (
2.2
)
in such a way that the points
x_{i}
are
distinct
.
Given a chain
S
in the form (
2.2
),
and assuming with no loss of generality
that
\sigma_{j}\neq 0
for all
j
,
we define the support of
S
as the set

\spt S:=\{x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{N}\}
.
Given a bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we write
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega;\,\mathbb{G})
for the subgroup of
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega;\,\mathbb{G})

whose elements are the chains
S
such that
\spt S\subseteq\Omega
.
Given chains
S
and
T
, we write
S=T
in
\Omega

as a synonym for
\spt(S-T)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.

Given a chain
S
in the form (
2.2
)
with distinct
x_{i}
, we define its
mass
as

(2.3)

\mathbb{M}(S):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left|\sigma_{j}\right|.

The mass is a norm on
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega;\,\mathbb{G})

(and roughly corresponds to the
L^{1}
-norm of a function
or the total variation of a measure).
However, for our purposes we will need to introduce
weaker norms — namely, the flat (
\flat
) norm
and a variation thereof. The flat norm was introduced by Whitney
[
51
]

and later played an important role in geometric measure
theory (see e.g.
[
30
,
32
,
50
]
).
We will call monopoles the
0
-chains of the
form
M=\sigma\llbracket x\rrbracket
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{G}
and
x\in\Omega
.
We will say that a
0
-chain
D
is a simple dipole
(or just a dipole, for brevity)
if and only if it can be written in the form

D=\sigma\llbracket x+v\rrbracket-\sigma\llbracket x\rrbracket

for some
\sigma\in\mathbb{G}
,
x\in\Omega
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
\left|v\right|\leq 1
.
Monopoles and dipoles are uniquely defined by
\sigma
,
x
,
respectively by
\sigma
,
x
,
v
, so
it makes sense to define

\left|M\right|:=\left|\sigma\right|,\qquad\left|D\right|:=\left|\sigma\right|\left|v\right|,\qquad\size(D):=\left|v\right|\!.

A dipolar decomposition relative to
\Omega

of a chain
S\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega;\,\mathbb{G})

is a decomposition of
S
of the form

(2.4)

S=\sum_{i=1}^{m}M_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{j}\qquad\textrm{in }\Omega,

where each
M_{i}
is a monopole and each
D_{j}
is a dipole.
Given
S\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega;\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
we define its flat norm relative to
\Omega
as

(2.5)

Remark 2.1
.

Without loss of generality, we can compute the infima

only with respect to dipolar decompositions (

such that the support of each dipole

In fact, letting

be the straight line segment

for otherwise, we can replace

The definitions (

immediately imply that

is bounded (as a metric space with respect to

Lemma 2.1
.

We have
\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(S)>0
and
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(S)>0

for all nonzero

.

In the proof, we will make
use of the augmentation homomorphism

, defined by

\chi(S):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}

Proof of Lemma

The result can be proved, e.g. by reasoning
as in
[

we present here a proof, based on ideas similar to those in
[
32
]
.
We provide some details for the reader’s convenience.
In light of (

to show that
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(S)>0
for any nonzero
S

Let
S\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega;\,\mathbb{G})
be such that
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(S)=0
.
If the support of

is empty, there is nothing left to prove.
Otherwise, we take

and write

,
where

is a chain with support in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}
.
We also define

\rho:=\min\left\{\alpha,\,\dist(x,\,\partial\Omega),\,\min_{y\in\spt T}\left|y-x\right|\right\}\!.

Since
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(S)=0
, there exists a dipole
decomposition

such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|M_{i}\right|+\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{d}\size(D_{j})\leq\frac{\rho}{2}.

As
\rho/2<\alpha
, the inequalities (
2.1
)
and (

) imply that

for all

.
Let
\overline{D}_{j}
be the straight line segment
between the points in
\spt D_{j}

The co-area formula implies that

\int_{0}^{\rho}\#\!\left(\partial B_{r}(x)\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{d}\overline{D}_{j}\right)\!\mathrm{d}r\leq\frac{\rho}{2},

where

denotes the number of elements in a set.
Therefore, there exists

such that
\partial B_{r}(x)

contains no point of
\cup_{j=1}^{d}\overline{D}_{j}
.
We relabel the indices in such a way
that the dipoles

are supported in

Then, we must have

is contained in
\Omega
and does not

. However,
by applying the homomorphism

defined in (

)
to both sides of (

), we obtain

.
By applying the same reasoning to all the points in

.
∎

We define

The

Lemma 2.3

Assume that the conditions (
2.1
)
and (

Proof.

First, we can assume without loss of generality that each

Next, we write each

and

In case some coefficient

We also define

\sigma_{j,k}:=0
,

for all integers

By Lemma
2.2

The inequality (

)
imply that

for all indices
j
,

.
Then, by a diagonal argument, we can extract a further

Upon applying another diagonal argument, we can

for all

\displaystyle\left|v_{j,k}-v_{j}\right|+\left|x_{j,k}-x_{j}\right|\leq 2^{-j}\qquad

that is, no dipole
D_{j,k}
connects a point of
V+y

with a point of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(\overline{V}+y)
.
Since the points in a dipole have opposite multiplicities,
we conclude that
\mathbb{I}(S_{k},\,V+y)=0
. This contradicts (
2.25
).
∎

It would also be possible to define an intersection index
for more general chains satisfying the assumption (
2.23
)
(see e.g.
[
33
]
), but we will not need such generality
for our purposes.

2.2
Grids and deformation of chains

In this section, we introduce some notations
for cubical grids in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which will be used later.
Let
Q^{n}_{h}:=[-h/2,\,h/2]^{n}
for
h>0
and
Q^{n}:=Q^{n}_{1}
.
Given
h>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the grid
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

of size
h>0
translated by
hy
with respect to the origin
as a collection of closed cubes of the form

(2.28)

\mathscr{G}(h,\,y):=\left\{hy+hz+Q^{n}_{h}\colon\colon z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right\}\!.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq j\leq n
, we denote by
\mathscr{G}_{j}(h,\,y)

the collection of the (closed)
j
-cells of
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
,
namely, the
j
-dimensional faces of the cubes in
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
.
We define the
j
-skeleton as
R_{j}(h,\,y):=\cup_{K\in\mathscr{G}_{j}(h,\,y)}K
.
Given an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
by
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)
the set of (
n
-dimensional)
cubes
k\in\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)
such
that
K\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing
.
We denote by
\mathscr{G}_{j}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)

the collection of
j
-cells of
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)

and by
R_{j}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)
its
j
-skeleton.

R_{k}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)\supseteq R_{k}(h,\,y)\cap\Omega
.
Given a grid
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
, we define its dual as

\mathscr{G}^{\prime}(h,\,y):=\left\{hy+hz+[0,\,h]^{n}\colon\colon z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right\}\!.

The dual grid has the following property:
for each
j
-cell
K
of
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

there exists a unique
n-j
-cell
K^{\prime}
of
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}(h,\,y)
,
called the dual cell of
K
, such that
K\cap K^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
.
We will denote by
R^{\prime}_{j}(h,\,y)
the
j
-skeleton of
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
.
The following statement is a well-known observation,
but we recall it because it will be useful later.
Set
Q^{n}_{h}:=[-h/2,\,h/2]^{n}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, any integrable function
f\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
,
any integer
j\in\{0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,n\}
and any
h>0
, there holds

\int_{Q^{n}}h^{n-j}\left(\int_{R^{j}(h,\,y)\cap U}f\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{j}\right)\mathrm{d}y=\binom{n}{j}\int_{U}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Let
P
be a
j
-dimensional coordinate subspace,
that is, a vector
j
-subspace spanned by a subset of
the canonical basis
\{e_{1},\,\ldots,\,e_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and let
P^{\perp}
be its orthogonal complement.
Let
R_{P}^{j}(h,\,y)
be the subset of
R^{j}(h,\,y)

containing the
j
-cells that are parallel to
P
.
We have
R_{P}^{j}(h,\,y)=R_{P}^{j}(h,\,y^{\prime})

if
y-y^{\prime}
is parallel to
P
and, hence,

\begin{split}\int_{Q^{n}}h^{n-j}\left(\int_{R_{P}^{j}(h,\,y)\cap U}f\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{j}\right)\mathrm{d}y&=\int_{P^{\perp}\cap Q^{n}}h^{n-j}\left(\int_{R_{P}^{j}(h,\,y)\cap U}f\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{j}\right)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\int_{P^{\perp}\cap Q^{n}_{h}}\left(\int_{(P+\xi\mathbb{Z})\cap U}f\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{j}\right)\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{U}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

where we have made the change of variable
hy=\xi

and applied Fubini theorem.
The lemma follows by taking the sum over
all
j
-dimensional coordinate subspaces
P
.
∎

Let
(\mathbb{G},\,\left|\,\cdot\,\right|)
be a normed Abelian group,
where the norm satisfies (
2.1
) and (
2.11
).
Let
\Omega
,
\Omega^{\prime}
be bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
\overline{\Omega}\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}

and
h_{0}:=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\dist(\Omega,\,\partial\Omega^{\prime})>0
.
The number
h_{0}
is defined in such a way that
cube of side length
h\leq h_{0}
that intersects
\Omega

is contained inside
\Omega^{\prime}
.
Let
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
be a grid, and
let
S\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime};\,\mathbb{G})
be a chain such that

(2.29)

\spt S\cap R_{n-1}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)=\varnothing.

We define the approximation of
S
induced by
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

as follows. Write

S=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{j}\llbracket x_{j}\rrbracket
,
where
\sigma_{j}\in\mathbb{G}
and the points
x_{j}\in\Omega^{\prime}

are distinct. For each
K\in\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)
,,
let
j_{1}
, …,
j_{k}
be the collection of indices
such that
x_{j}\in K
and let

\sigma(S,\,K):=\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sigma_{j_{m}}
.
We define

(2.30)

P(S,\,h,\,y):=\sum_{K\in\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)}\sigma(S,\,K)\llbracket c_{K}\rrbracket,

where
c_{K}
is the centre of
K
.
The operator
S\mapsto P(S,\,h,\,y)
is linear
and extends by continuity
(with respect to suitable norms), so that
P(S,\,h,\,y)

exists for all flat chains
S\in\mathbb{F}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime},\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
all
h>0
and almost all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(see
[
31
,
49
]
).
The following statement is a particular case of the deformation
theorem for flat chains. The theorem holds true for chains
of arbitrary dimension and actually provides more information
than what we recall here. However, this simplified
statement is enough for our purposes.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
31
,
49
]
)
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

For all
S\in\mathbb{FS}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime},\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there holds

\int_{Q^{n}}\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}\!\left(P(S,\,h,\,y)\right)\mathrm{d}y\leq C\,\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega^{\prime}}(S),

for some constant
C
depending only on
\mathbb{G}
and
n
.

(ii)

For
all
S\in\mathbb{F}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime},\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
there holds

\int_{Q^{n}}\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}\!\left(S-P(S,\,h,\,y)\right)\mathrm{d}y\leq Ch\,\mathbb{M}(S).

(iii)

For every
S\in\mathbb{F}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime},\,\mathbb{G})

and every sequence of positive numbers
h_{k}\to 0
,
there exists a (non-relabelled) subsequence such that

\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}\!\left(S-P(S,\,h_{k},\,y)\right)\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }k\to+\infty

for almost all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In particular, if
P(S,\,h,\,y)=0
for
all
h\in(0,\,h_{0}]
and almost every
y\in Q^{p+1}
,
then
S=0
.

Proof.

Statements (ii) and (iii) follow, respectively,
from Corollary 1.2 and Corollary 1.3 in
[
49
]
,
so we only have to prove Statement (i).
By linearity of
P(\cdot,\,S,\,y)
,
it is enough to consider the case
S
is a monopole or a dipole.
If
S=\sigma\llbracket x\rrbracket
is a monopole and
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

is any grid satisfying (
2.29
),
we have
P(S,\,h,\,y)=\sigma\llbracket c_{K}\rrbracket

where
c_{K}
is the centre of the cube
K\in\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

containing
x
. Therefore, we immediately have

(2.31)

\int_{Q^{n}}\left|P(S,\,h,\,y)\right|\mathrm{d}y=\left|\sigma\right|=\left|S\right|\!.

Suppose now
S=\sigma\llbracket x_{2}\rrbracket-\sigma\llbracket x_{1}\rrbracket

is a dipole. Then,
P(S,\,h,\,y)=\sigma\llbracket c_{2}\rrbracket-\sigma\llbracket c_{1}\rrbracket

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are the centres of the cubes of
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

containing
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
respectively.
If
\left|x_{2}-x_{1}\right|\geq\lambda h
for some
\lambda>0
, then

\left|c_{2}-c_{1}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{n}h+\left|x_{2}-x_{1}\right|\leq\left(\frac{2\sqrt{n}}{\lambda}+1\right)\left|x_{2}-x_{1}\right|

and we immediately obtain

(2.32)

\int_{Q^{n}}\size(P(S,\,h,\,y))\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C\size(S),

with
C=2\sqrt{n}/\lambda+1
.
Suppose now that
\left|x_{2}-x_{1}\right|\leq\lambda h
. Up to a
translation, we also assume that
x_{1}=0
, so that
c_{1}=hy

for any choice of
y\in Q^{n}
. For any
y
such that

hy\in Q^{n}_{h}\cap(Q^{n}_{h}+x_{2})
, we have
c_{2}=hy
,
so
P(S,\,h,\,y)=0
. For
y
such that

hy\in Q^{n}_{h}\setminus(Q^{n}_{h}+x_{2})
,
the points
c_{1}=hy
,
c_{2}
are the centres of two cubes
that have at least a vertex in common,
so
\size(P(S,\,h,\,y))\leq\sqrt{n}h
.
If
\lambda
is small enough, say for instance
\lambda=1/100
,
we have

\int_{Q^{n}}\size(P(S,\,h,\,y))\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\sqrt{n}h\left|Q^{n}\setminus\left(Q^{n}+\frac{x_{2}}{h}\right)\right|\leq C_{n}\left|x_{2}\right|=C_{n}\size(S),

for some constant
C_{n}
depending on
n
only.
Therefore, the inequality (
2.32
) holds in this case, too.
Statement (i) follows from (
2.31
) and (
2.32
).
∎

2.3
Manifold-valued Sobolev maps

Let
p\geq 1
be
an integer
and
let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p+1}
be a bounded, Lipschitz domain.
In order to simplify some statements, we will
adopt a rather
non-standard terminology

and say that a sequence

(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{R}^{m})

converges weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to some limit
map
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{R}^{m})
if and only if

(2.33)

u_{j}\to u\textrm{ strongly in }L^{p}(\Omega)\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\nabla u_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<+\infty.

This definition agrees with the usual one when
p>1
,
but for
p=1
corresponds instead to weak
∗
convergence in BV.
However, this definition allows us to treat the cases
p>1

and
p=1
at the same time.

Let
\mathscr{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a smooth, compact,
connected submanifold (without boundary) of some Euclidean space.
We define

W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N}):=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{R}^{m})\colon u(x)\in\mathscr{N}\ \textrm{for a.e. }x\in\Omega\right\}\!.

We also write
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N})

for the set of maps
\varphi\colon\overline{\Omega}\to\mathscr{N}

that admit a smooth extension defined on an open neighbourhood
of
\overline{\Omega}
.
We write
D_{p}
for the
p
-Dirichlet functional, i.e.

(2.34)

D_{p}(u)=D_{p}(u,\,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

if
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
. We also consider the relaxed
p
-Dirichlet functional, given by

(2.35)

\begin{split}\overline{D}_{p}(u)=D_{p}(u,\,\Omega):=\inf\!\big\{&\liminf_{j\to+\infty}D_{p}(\varphi_{j},\,\Omega)\colon\\
&(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N}),\ u_{j}\to u\textrm{ strongly in }L^{p}(\Omega)\big\}\end{split}

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
.
(As usual, we define
\inf\varnothing=+\infty
.)
By definition, the functional
\overline{D}_{p}

is sequentially lower semicontinuous with respect to
weak
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-convergence. We write

(2.36)

H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N}):=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})\colon\overline{D}_{p}(u)<+\infty\right\}\!.

The set
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
is the sequential
closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N})

with respect to weak
W^{1,p}
-convergence. We also write

H_{\mathrm{strong}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
for the closure
of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N})
with respect
to the
strong

W^{1,p}
-topology.
We will write
u\in H_{\mathrm{strong,loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
if and only if,
for any ball
B
with
B\overline{B}\subseteq\Omega
,
the restriction
u_{|B}
belongs to
H_{\mathrm{strong}}^{1,p}(B,\,\mathscr{N})
.

We will say that a map
u\colon\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

belongs to
R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
if and only if there exist
a finite set
\Sigma=\Sigma(u)
and a constant
C
such
that
u
is locally Lipschitz
in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Sigma
and

(2.37)

\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\dist(x,\,\Sigma)}\qquad\textrm{for a.e. }x\in\Omega\setminus\Sigma.

In the terminology of
[
1
,
2
]
,
maps in
R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
have “nice singularities”.
Any
\mathscr{N}
-valued Sobolev map can be strongly approximated
by maps with nice singularities.

Theorem 2.7
.

The set
R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
is dense
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
, with respect to the strong topology.

Theorem
2.7
was proved by Bethuel
in
[
4
, Theorem 2]
.
Generalisations of this result to
W^{s,p}
-spaces
are given in
[
14
,
16
,
27
]
.

2.4
Free homotopy classes of maps
\mathbb{S}^{p}\to\mathscr{N}

From now on,
p\geq 1
will denote
an integer
.
Given continuous maps
h_{1}
,
h_{2}\colon\mathbb{S}^{p}\to\mathscr{N}
,
we will say that
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
are freely homotopic
(or just homotopic, for short) to one another
if and only if there exists a continuous map

H\colon\overline{B}^{p+1}_{2}\setminus B^{p+1}\to\mathscr{N}
such that

H(x)=h_{1}(x),\quad H(2x)=h_{2}(x)\qquad\textrm{for any }x\in\partial B^{p+1}.

Since the annulus
\overline{B}^{p+1}_{2}\setminus B^{p+1}

is homeomorphic to the cylinder
[0,\,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{p}
,
this definition of free homotopy is equivalent
to the usual one, in which the map
H
is defined
on
[0,\,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{p}
. However, free homotopy
differs substantially from, say, based homotopy,
which is used to define the homotopy groups,
in that the curves
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
are not required to pass
through a given base point in
\mathscr{N}
.
Free homotopy is an equivalence relation
on
C(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
,
and we denote the set of equivalence classes as
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
.

Let
\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N}))
denote the power set of
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
.
There is a natural binary
multi-valued

operation in
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
, i.e. a map

+\colon\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})\times\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})\to\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})),

which is defined as follows.
Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p+1}
be a closed ball with two holes,
say for instance
D:=D_{0}\setminus(D_{1}\cup D_{2})
, where

(2.38)

D_{0}:=\overline{B}^{p+1}_{2},\qquad D_{1}:=B^{p+1}_{1/2}(-1,\,0,\,\ldots,\,0),\qquad D_{2}:=B^{p+1}_{1/2}(1,\,0,\,\ldots,\,0).

We identify all the boundary components of
D
,
i.e.
\partial D_{0}
,
\partial D_{1}
,
\partial D_{2}
,
with the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{p}
, up to translation and dilations.
In particular, we give all the boundary components the same
orientation, the same we give to
\mathbb{S}^{p}
.
Given free homotopy classes
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
in
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
,
we define
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
as the set of homotopy
classes
\sigma\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
for which
there exists a continuous map
G\colon D\to\mathscr{N}
such that

G
restricted to
\partial D_{1}
,
\partial D_{2}
,
\partial D_{0}

respectively belongs to the homotopy
classes
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
,
\sigma
.
We extend
+
to an
n
-ary operation,
for any integer
n\geq 2
, by setting e.g.

(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})+\sigma_{3}:=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}}\left(\sigma+\sigma_{3}\right)

and so on.

Lemma 2.8
.

The operation
+
on
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
satisfies the following properties:

(0)

\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
is nonempty for all
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
;

(1)

for all
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
and
\sigma_{3}\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
, there holds

(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})+\sigma_{3}=\sigma_{1}+(\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3})
;

(2)

there exists
0\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
such that
\sigma+0=\{\sigma\}

for all
\sigma\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
;

(3)

for all
\sigma\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
there exists
-\sigma\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})

such that
\sigma+(-\sigma)\ni 0
;

(4)

for all
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
, there holds

\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{1}
.

Sketch of the proof.

The proof of Lemma
2.8

follows from elementary arguments in topology.
We sketch the arguments, but omit the details
for the sake of brevity.
Property (0) depends on the assumption
that
\mathscr{N}
is (path-)connected, which allows us to extend
any continuous map
g\colon\partial D_{1}\cup\partial D_{2}\to\mathscr{N}

to a continuous map
G\colon D\to\mathscr{N}
.
For instance, we consider a small “tube”
\Sigma
,
homeomorphic to
[0,\,1]\times\overline{B}^{p-1}
,
joining a small geodesic disk
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{1})\subseteq D_{1}

with a geodesic disk in
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})\subseteq D_{2}
.
We define
G
inside
\Sigma
by travelling along geodesic paths
in
\mathscr{N}
that join
g_{|B_{\varepsilon}(x_{1})}
with
g_{|B_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})}
.
Then, we extend
G
to the whole of
D
by observing
that
D
retracts onto
\Sigma\cup\partial D_{1}\cup\partial D_{2}
,
that is, there is a continuous
map
D\to\Sigma\cup\partial D_{1}\cup\partial D_{2}
which is equal
to the identity on
\Sigma\cup\partial D_{1}\cup\partial D_{2}
.
The class
0\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
is the homotopy class of constant maps,
and
-\sigma
is the homotopy class that contains
the continuous map
\overline{h}\colon\mathbb{S}^{p}\to\mathscr{N}

obtained from an arbitrary
h\in\sigma
by reversing the orientation.
Property (4) follows from the fact that there exists
a diffeomorphism
D\to D
that coincides with the identity
on
\partial D_{0}
but exchanges
\partial D_{1}
with
\partial D_{2}
.
Such a diffeomorphism can be constructed, for instance, by
rotating each sphere
\partial B^{p+1}_{r}

about a given axis
e\in\mathbb{S}^{k}
by an angle
\theta(r)
,
which increases continuously from
0
to
\pi

as
r
decreases from
2
to
3/2
.
∎

Lemma
2.8
shows that
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})

is a (commutative) polygroup, that is,
it enjoys algebraic properties that are similar to
those of groups, except that the operation
is multi-valued. Note that we may have

\sigma+(-\sigma)\supsetneq\{0\}
,
as shown in Remark
2.3
.
In view of Lemma
2.8
, we can adopt
some notation that is commonly used for the operation of group,
such as dropping the parenthesis in
n
-ary sums and
writing
\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}
instead of
\sigma_{1}+(-\sigma_{2})
.

Remark 2.3
.

The set
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
can be described in terms of more standard
objects in algebraic topology, namely homotopy groups.
Indeed, let
z_{0}\in\mathscr{N}
be an arbitrary point.
Then, there is a natural bijection between elements of
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})

and orbits of the action of the fundamental
group
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N},\,z_{0})
on
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N},\,z_{0})

(see e.g.
[
40
, Sections VI and VII.D]
).
In case
k=2
,
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N},\,z_{0})
acts on itself by conjugation,
so elements in
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
are in bijection with conjugacy classes
in
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N},\,z_{0})
. By identifying elements of
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})

with orbits in
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N},\,z_{0})

under the action of
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N},\,z_{0})
,
we can express the operation
+
on
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
as

\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}=\left\{(\textrm{orbit of }s_{1}s_{2})\colon s_{1}\in\sigma_{1},\ s_{2}\in\sigma_{2}\right\}\!.

Now suppose, for instance, that
p=1
and
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N})

is not Abelian. Then,
\sigma+(-\sigma)
contains all the
conjugacy classes of elements of the form
sgs^{-1}g^{-1}

with
s\in\sigma
and
g\in\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N},\,z_{0})
,
and if
s
does not commute with all the other elements
of
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N},\,z_{0})
then
\sigma+(-\sigma)
contains
more than one homotopy class.

Remark 2.4
.

If
X
is a topological manifold
and
\phi\colon\mathbb{S}^{p}\to X
an homeomorphism,
it makes sense to define the homotopy class
of a map
u\colon X\to\mathscr{N}
as the homotopy class
of
u\circ\phi\colon\mathbb{S}^{p}\to\mathscr{N}
.
However, these classes
could
depend on
the choice of
\phi
. To avoid this ambiguity,
whenever
X
is a smooth manifold

For later convenience, we state a few

Let

Lemma 2.9
.

For each boundary
(p+1)
-face

,

In the next lemma, we show that “changing the base point”

2.4.1
Homotopy classes in critical Sobolev spaces

The notion of homotopy class extends to Sobolev maps
in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
, although those are not
necessarily continuous. (In fact, Brezis and Nirenberg
[
19
]

showed that homotopy classes are well-defined
in an even larger class, i.e. the space
\mathrm{VMO}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
,
but we will not need such generality here.)
The construction is based on the tubular neighbourhood theorem,
which implies the existence of a nearest-point projection onto
\mathscr{N}

defined in a neighbourhood of
\mathscr{N}
of uniform thickness.
More precisely, there exists
\delta_{*}=\delta_{*}(\mathscr{N})>0

with the following properties: for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

with
\dist(y,\,\mathscr{N})\leq\delta_{*}
,
there exists a unique
\pi_{\mathscr{N}}(y)\in\mathscr{N}
such that

(2.41)

\left|y-\pi_{\mathscr{N}}(y)\right|<\left|y-z\right|\qquad\textrm{for all }z\in\mathscr{N},\,y\neq z,

and the map
y\mapsto\pi_{\mathscr{N}}(y)
is smooth.

The projection
\pi_{\mathscr{N}}
allows us to define homotopy
classes of maps in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
, as follows.
For all
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
, all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{p}

and all
\varepsilon>0
, let

(2.42)

\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\mathbb{S}^{p}}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}(y),

where the integral average is taken component-wise.
The map
x\in\mathbb{S}^{p}\mapsto\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)
is continuous,
by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem.
While
\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)
is not necessarily an element of
\mathscr{N}
,
its distance from
\mathscr{N}
can be estimated using the Poincaré inequality.
More precisely, since
u(y)\in\mathscr{N}
for a.e.
y\in\mathbb{S}^{p}
, we have

(2.43)

\begin{split}\dist(\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}(x),\,\mathscr{N})^{p}&\leq\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\mathbb{S}^{p}}\left|u(y)-\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}\leq C\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\mathbb{S}^{p}}\left|\nabla u(y)\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}.\end{split}

The constant
C
in front of the right-hand side
can be chosen independently of (
x
and)
\varepsilon
,
by scaling reasons. As a consequence,
there exists
\varepsilon_{*}(u)>0
such that

\dist(\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}(x),\,\mathscr{N})^{p}\leq\delta_{*}

for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{p}
and all
\varepsilon\in(0,\,\varepsilon_{*}(u)]
,
so that the projection
u_{\varepsilon}:=\pi_{\mathscr{N}}\circ\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}

is well-defined and continuous.
For all
\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\,\varepsilon_{*}(u)]
,
the maps
u_{\varepsilon}
,
u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
are homotopic to one another;
a homotopy (in the sense previously defined) is given by

H_{\varepsilon}(r\omega):=u_{(r-1)\varepsilon+(2-r)\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\omega)

for all
r\in[1,\,2]
,
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{p}
.
We define the homotopy class of
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p}\,\,\mathscr{N})

as the homotopy class of
u_{\varepsilon}
, for arbitrary
\varepsilon\in(0,\,\varepsilon_{*}(u)]
.
The theory of homotopy classes for Sobolev
and, more generally, VMO maps
is carried out in
[
19
,
20
]
.
Here, we recall only two properties.

Proposition 2.12

(
[
48
]
,
[
19
, Theorem 1]
)
.

For any
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
, there
exists a positive value
\eta=\eta(u,\,p,\,\mathscr{N})

such that any map
v\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
satisfying

\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{p})}+\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{p})}\leq\eta

belongs to the same homotopy class as
u
.
In particular, there exists
\alpha_{p}=\alpha_{p}(\mathscr{N})>0

such that any map
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})

with
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{p})}^{p}\geq\alpha_{p}

belongs to the trivial homotopy class.

Proposition 2.13

(
[
47
, Proposition p. 267]
)
.

The set
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
is dense
in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
, with respect to the strong topology.

2.4.2
Norms of homotopy classes

We now define a family of functions on
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})

that satisfy “norm-like” properties (and are norms
if the operation on
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
is single-valued).
These functions we use depends on the choice of a base point
in
\mathscr{N}
, but they are all equivalent to one another.
Let
N_{p}:=(1,\,0,\,\ldots,\,0)
be the North
pole of the sphere, and let
z_{0}
be a given point in
\mathscr{N}
.
For any
\sigma\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
, we define

(2.44)

\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}:=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{p}}\left|\nabla_{\top}v\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}\colon v\in(W^{1,p}\cap C)(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N}),\ v\in\sigma,\ v(N_{p})=z_{0}\right\}\!.

Here
\nabla_{\top}
denotes the Riemannian gradient on
\mathbb{S}^{p}
,
applied component-wise to
v=(v^{1},\,\ldots,\,v^{m})
.
Before proving that (
2.44
) defines a norm,
we reformulate slightly the infimum at the right-hand side.
Without loss of generality, we can further restrict
our attention to maps
v
that are equal to
z_{0}

in a neighbourhood of the North pole.
Then, since the
p
-Dirichlet energy is conformally invariant,
up to composition with the stereographic
projection
\mathbb{S}^{p}\setminus\{N_{0}\}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
we can write

(2.45)

\begin{split}\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}=\inf\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\colon&v\in C(\mathbb{R}^{p},\,\mathscr{N}),\ \nabla v\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{p},\,\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}),\ v\in\sigma,\\[-8.53581pt]
&v=z_{0}\textrm{ in a neighbourhood of infinity}\bigg\}.\end{split}

By rescaling, we also have

(2.46)

\begin{split}\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}=\inf\bigg\{\int_{B^{p}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\colon&v\in(W^{1,p}\cap C)(B^{p},\,\mathscr{N}),\ v\in\sigma,\ v=z_{0}\textrm{ on }\partial B^{n}\bigg\}.\end{split}

These reformulations will be useful in proving
the following result.

Lemma 2.14
.

The function
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|_{z_{0}}\colon\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
the following properties:

(i)

\left|-\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}=\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}

for all
\sigma\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
;

(ii)

for all
\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma_{2}\in\mathscr{N}
,
there exists
\sigma\in\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}

such that
\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}\leq\left|\sigma_{1}\right|_{z_{0}}+\left|\sigma_{2}\right|_{z_{0}}
;

(iii)

there exists a constant
\alpha_{p}=\alpha_{p}(\mathscr{N})>0
,
depending only on
p
and
\mathscr{N}
, such that

\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}\geq\alpha_{p}\qquad\textrm{for all }\sigma\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}),\ \alpha\neq 0,\ z_{0}\in\mathscr{N};

(iv)

there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\mathscr{N}

and
p
, such that

\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{1}}\leq C\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}

for all
z_{0}\in\mathscr{N}
,
z_{1}\in\mathscr{N}
, and all
\sigma\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
.

Proof.

We prove each statement separately.

Proof of (i).

This property follows immediately from the definition (
2.44
)
of
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|_{z_{0}}
.

Proof of (ii).

We use the characterisation (
2.46
) of
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|_{z_{0}}
.
Let
v_{1}\in(W^{1,p}\cap C)(B^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
,

v_{2}(W^{1,p}\cap C)(B^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
be maps, with trace
on
\partial B^{n}
equal to
z_{0}

belonging to the homotopy classes
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
, respectively.
Let
\overline{B}_{1}(x_{1})
,
\overline{B}_{1}(x_{1})
be disjoint,
closed balls in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Let
v\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathscr{N}
be defined
as
v(x):=v_{1}(x-x_{1})
for
x\in B_{1}(x_{1})
,

v(x):=v_{2}(x-x_{2})
for
x\in B_{1}(x_{2})
, and
v(x):=v_{0}
otherwise.
Lemma
2.10
shows that the
homotopy class
\sigma
of
v
belongs to
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
,
so (
2.45
) implies

\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\nabla v_{1}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\nabla v_{2}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

Statement (iii) follows by taking
the infimum over all admissible
v_{1}\in\sigma_{1}
,

v_{2}\in\sigma_{2}
.

Proof of (iii).
This is an immediate consequence
of Proposition
2.12
.

Proof of (iv).

We use again the formulation (
2.46
) of the problem.
Let
z_{0}\in\mathscr{N}
,
z_{1}\in\mathscr{N}
be given points in
\mathscr{N}
.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,\,1]\to\mathscr{N}
a minimising geodetic
(parametrised by multiples of arclength) from
z_{0}
to
z_{1}
.
Let
v_{0}\in(W^{1,p}\cap C)(B^{p},\,\mathscr{N})

be a map equal to
z_{0}
on
\partial B^{n}

and belonging to the homotopy class
\sigma
.
We define
v_{1}
as in (
2.40
).
The map
v_{1}
, too, belongs to the homotopy class
\sigma
,
because of Lemma
2.11
. Then, we have

\begin{split}\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{1}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\nabla v_{0}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\mathscr{H}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{p})\int_{0}^{1}\left|\gamma^{\prime}(r)\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}r=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\nabla v_{0}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\mathscr{H}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{p})\dist_{\mathscr{N}}(z_{0},\,z_{1})^{p},\end{split}

where
\dist_{\mathscr{N}}(z_{0},\,z_{1})
denotes the geodesic distance
between
z_{0}
and
z_{1}
, i.e. the length of a minimising
geodesic
\gamma
joining them. (The last equality
follows from the fact that
\left|\gamma^{\prime}\right|
is constant.)
By taking the infimum over all admissible
v_{0}

in
\gamma
, we obtain

(2.47)

\begin{split}\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{1}}\leq\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}+C,\end{split}

where
C
depends only on
p
and (the geodesic
diameter of)
\mathscr{N}
. Combining (
2.47
)
with (
iii
), we deduce (

).
∎

Since spheres and boundaries of cubes are homeomorphic,
homotopy classes are also defined for

\partial[0,\,h]^{p+1}\to\mathscr{N}
.
In fact, we have the following property:

Lemma 2.15
.

There exists a constant
C
, depending only
on
p
and
\mathscr{N}
, that satisfies

(2.48)

\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}\leq C\int_{\partial[0,\,h]^{p+1}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}

for all
\sigma\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
, all
z_{0}\in\mathscr{N}
, all
h>0
, and all

in
the homotopy class
\sigma
.

Note that in Lemma
2.15
, we are
not

requiring
v
to take the value
z_{0}
anywhere.

Proof of Lemma
2.15
.

The right-hand side of (
2.48
) is invariant by rescaling,

.
We can further suppose that

Let

The desired inequality follows from (

)

.

3
The singular set

3.1
Assumptions on the target manifold

Let
\mathscr{N}
be a smooth, compact, connected submanifold
without boundary of some Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
We take
an integer

p
such that
1\leq p<n
.
Henceforth, we will always assume that

(H
1
)

for all
\sigma_{1}\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
,
\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
, the set

\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
contains no more than one element.

Then, Lemma
2.8
implies
that
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}

contains exactly one element for all
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
,
and moreover, the single-valued operation
+
induces
an Abelian group structure on
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
.
We will abuse of notations slightly
and write, e.g.
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}=\sigma

instead of
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}=\{\sigma\}
, and so on.
In fact, under assumption (
H1
) we have

\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})=\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N}),

where
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
is the
p
-th homotopy group of
\mathscr{N}
.
(This equality, or rather a group homomorphism,
follows from Remark
2.3
; see also
Lemma
2.10
).
In practice, we will continue working with the group
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})

as defined above, but write
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
instead of
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})

to keep the notation consistent with the standard one.
We will also assume that

(H
2
)

for all
\Lambda>0
, there are only finitely
many homotopy classes
\sigma\in\pi_{1}(\mathscr{N})
that contain
maps
v\in\sigma\cap W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{p},\,\mathscr{N})
with

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{p}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}\leq\Lambda.

In particular, this condition implies that
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})

satisfies the assumption (
H2
)
in Lemma
2.2
and
2.3
,
that is,

(\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}))_{z_{0},\,\Lambda}:=\left\{\sigma\in\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})\colon\left|\sigma\right|_{z_{0}}\leq\Lambda\right\}

is finite for all
z_{0}\in\mathscr{N}
and
\Lambda>0
.
To check whether the condition (
H2
)
is satisfied, one could attempt using concentration
compactness methods for minimising sequences of (
2.45
)
and a bubbling analysis, along the lines of
[
45
]
.
However, we do not pursue this direction.

Remark 3.1
.

Contrary to
[
22
,
23
]
, we do
not

assume that
\mathscr{N}
is
(p-1)
-connected.
For instance, the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
satisfies both (
H1
)
and (
H2
) with
p=3
, because
\pi_{3}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\simeq\mathbb{Z}

and the norm satisfies

\left|d\right|_{z_{0}}\leq C\left|d\right|^{3/4}

for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}
(see
[
46
]
).

In the sequel, we consider chains with coefficients in
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
,
where
\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
is equipped with the norm
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|_{z_{0}}

for a given point
z_{0}\in\mathscr{N}
. Different
choices of
z_{0}
correspond to equivalent norms,
by Lemma
2.14
. Since the basepoint
z_{0}

is fixed, we will omit it from the notation and
write
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|
instead of
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|_{z_{0}}
.

3.2
Approximating the singular set on grids

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p+1}
be a bounded, Lipschitz domain.
We recall the definition of the topological singular
set
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u)
of a map
u\in R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

with nice singularities.
Let
\Sigma=\Sigma(u)\subseteq\Omega

be a finite set such that
u
is locally Lipschitz
in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Sigma
.
Let
\rho>0
be a small enough radius,
such that the closed balls
B_{\rho}(x)
, with
x\in\Sigma
,
are pairwise disjoint and contained in
\Omega
.
Let
\sigma(u,\,x)\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})=\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
denote the
(free) homotopy class of
u
restricted
to
\partial B_{\rho}(x)
. By definition of free homotopy,

\sigma(u,\,x)
is independent of the choice of
\rho
.
We define

(3.1)

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u):=\sum_{x\in\Sigma}\sigma(u,\,x)\llbracket x\rrbracket\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega;\,\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})).

Recalling the definition of the operation on
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})=\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})

and the definition of the intersection index
\mathbb{I}
in (
2.24
),
we obtain the following property: if
V
is a Lipschitz domain,
homeomorphic to a disk, such that
\overline{V}\subseteq\Omega

and
\spt\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u)\cap\partial V=\varnothing
, then

(3.2)

\mathbb{I}(u,\,V)=(\textrm{homotopy class
of the restriction }u_{|\partial V}).

Our goal is to show that
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}
extends to
arbitrary maps in
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
.
As a first step, we define the singular set of
u

restricted to a
grid
.
We will denote by
C
several different constants that only
depend on
\Omega
,
\mathscr{N}
,
p
and may change from line to line,
and write
A\lesssim B
as a synonym of
A\leq CB
.

First of all, we recall that there exists a
(nonlinear, but continuous) extension operator

W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime},\,\mathscr{N})
,
where
\Omega^{\prime}\supseteq\overline{\Omega}
is a larger
(but still bounded) domain, such that

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
.
Here and in what follows, we denote with the same symbol
the map
u\colon\Omega\to\mathscr{N}
and its extension
to
\Omega^{\prime}
. Such an operator can be defined
for instance, by composition with a reflection across
the boundary
\partial\Omega
, since
\Omega
is bounded
and has Lipschitz boundary (see e.g.
[
2
, Proposition 8.1]
).
Next, let
h_{0}:=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\dist(\Omega,\,\partial\Omega^{\prime})>0
.
We work with grids
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

of size
h\leq h_{0}
, so that any
cell of the grids that intersects
\Omega

is contained in
\Omega^{\prime}
.
Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
,

h\in(0,\,h_{0}]
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be given.
For almost every choice of
y
, the restriction of
u

to the
p
-skeleton
R^{p}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)
is well-defined
and belongs to
W^{1,p}(R^{p}(h,\,y),\,\mathscr{N})
.
Therefore, given any
(p+1)
-cell
K\in\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)
,

u_{|\partial K}
has a well-defined (free)
homotopy class
\sigma(u,\,\partial K)\in\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})=\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N})
,
as defined in Section
2.4.1
.
Let
c_{K}
be the centre of
K
. We define

(3.4)

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y):=\sum_{K\in\mathscr{G}^{p+1}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)}\sigma(u,\,\partial K)\llbracket c_{K}\rrbracket.

In case
u\in R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
,
the homotopy class
\sigma(u,\,\partial K)

is the sum of the classes
\sigma(u,\,x)

among all the singular points
x
of
u
contained in
K
.
This claim follows from the very definition of the
operation in
\Gamma_{p}(\mathscr{N})
. Therefore, recalling the
definition (
2.30
) of the
approximation
P(S,\,h,\,y)
for a polyhedral
chain
S
, we immediately obtain

(3.5)

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)=P(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u),\,h,\,y)

for all
u\in R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
, all
h\in(0,\,h_{0}]

and almost all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In light of
(ii)

in Proposition
2.6
and (
3.5
),
we deduce

(3.6)

\begin{split}\fint_{Q^{p+1}_{h}}\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u)-\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y))\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim h\,\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u))\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }\lambda\to 0.\end{split}

In particular, when
u
has finitely many singularities,

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)
is indeed an approximation of
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u)
.

Our next task is to find bounds on
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)
,
uniformly with respect to the parameter
h
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
u\in R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

and
h\in(0,\,h_{0}]
, we have

(3.7)

\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u))\lesssim\overline{D}_{p}(u).

The proof of this lemma is based on ideas in
[
39
, Lemma 1]
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

For any
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

and all
h\in(0,\,h_{0}]
, we claim that

(3.8)

\fint_{Q^{p+1}_{h}}\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y))\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\overline{D}_{p}(u).

Once (
3.8
) is proved, the estimate (
3.7
)
will follow, thanks to Statement
(ii)
in Proposition
2.6
.
Let
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
and
let
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence
in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\,\mathscr{N})

that converges to
u
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
We extend each
\varphi_{j}
to the larger domain
\Omega^{\prime}
,
by reflection across the boundary of
\Omega
.
Let
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
be a grid, with
0<h\leq h_{0}

and
y\in Q^{p+1}_{h}
. By applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg
interpolation inequality on each
(p-1)
-cell
of
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
, we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|u-\varphi_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(R_{p-1})}\lesssim\left\|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(R_{p-1})}^{1-1/p}\left\|u-\varphi_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(R_{p-1})}^{1/p}+h^{1/p-1}\left\|u-\varphi_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(R_{p-1})}\end{split}

where
R_{p-1}:=R_{p-1}(h,\,y)
is
the
(p-1)
-skeleton of
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
.
Since
\varphi_{j}\to u
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)

and hence, by Fubini theorem,
also weakly in
W^{1,p}(R_{p-1})

(except for a negligible set of
y\in Q^{p+1}_{h}
),
we can choose
j=j(h,\,y)
large enough, in such a way that

(3.9)

\left\|u-\varphi_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(R_{p-1})}\leq\delta_{*}.

Here
\delta_{*}>0
is small enough, so that
the nearest-point projection onto
\mathscr{N}

(see (
2.41
)) is well-defined
in a
\delta_{*}
-neighbourhood of
\mathscr{N}
.

Next, we extend
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

to a higher-dimensional grid, of the form

(3.10)

\mathscr{E}:=\left\{K\times[0,\,h]\colon K\in\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)\right\}\!.

Let
\mathscr{E}_{p}
be the collection of
p
-dimensional
cells of
\mathscr{E}
, and
let
X_{p}:=\cup_{K\in\mathscr{E}_{p}}K
be the
p
-skeleton of
\mathscr{E}
.
We define a map
U\colon X_{p}\to\mathscr{N}
as follows.
On the
p
-cells of
X_{p}
contained
in
\Omega^{\prime}\times\{0\}
, we define
U:=u
.
On
X_{p}\cap\left(\Omega^{\prime}\times\{h\}\right)
,
we define
U:=\varphi_{j}
, where
j=j(h,\,y)

has been chosen above.
Finally, on the cells of the form
K\times[0,\,h]

for
K\in\mathscr{G}_{p-1}
, we define

U(x,\,t):=\pi_{\mathscr{N}}\!\left(\left(1-\frac{t}{h}\right)u(x)+\frac{t}{h}\varphi_{j}(x)\right)\!,\qquad\textrm{for }(x,\,t)\in K\times[0,\,h]

This map is well-defined, thanks to (
3.9
).
Since the projection
\pi_{\mathscr{N}}
is Lipschitz-continuous
on the
\delta_{*}
-neighbourhood of
\mathscr{N}
, we have

(3.11)

\begin{split}\int_{X_{p}}\left|\nabla U\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}&\leq\int_{R_{p}}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla\varphi_{j}\right|^{p}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+Ch\int_{R_{p-1}}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla\varphi_{j}\right|^{p}+\frac{\left|u-\varphi_{j}\right|^{p}}{h^{p}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p-1}.\end{split}

Next, we use the map
U
to construct a dipole
decomposition for
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)
, which will
lead to (
3.8
). For each

p
-cell
H\in\mathscr{G}_{p}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)
,
let
H^{\prime}
be the dual
1
-cell
and let
a_{H}
,
b_{H}
be the endpoints of
H^{\prime}
.
We consider an orientation of
H
and label
the endpoints
a_{H}
,
b_{H}
in such a way that,
for any positive basis
(\tau_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{p})

of the
p
-plane containing
H
,

(\tau_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{p},b_{H}-a_{H})

is a positive basis of
\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\sigma(U,\,H)
be the (free) homotopy class
of
U
restricted to
\partial(H\times[0,\,h])
,
where the latter is given the orientation induced
by
H\times[0,\,h]
. We claim that

(3.12)

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)=\sum_{H\in\mathscr{G}_{p}(h,\,y)\colon H\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset}\left(\sigma(U,\,H)\llbracket b_{H}\rrbracket-\sigma(U,\,H)\llbracket a_{H}\rrbracket\right)\!.

First, we observe that changing the orientation of
H

switches the order of
a_{H}
and
b_{H}
, but also
change
\sigma(U,\,H)
into
-\sigma(U,\,H)
,
because
\sigma(U,\,H)
depends on the choice
of an orientation-preserving
homeomorphism
\partial(H\times[0,\,h])\to\mathbb{S}^{p}

(see Remark
2.4
).
Therefore, the right-hand side of (
3.12
)
does not depend on the orientation of the cells.
Next, let
K
be a (
(p+1)
-dimensional) cube
in
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)
, and let
c_{K}
be its centre.
The
1
-cells
H^{\prime}\in\mathscr{G}_{1}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)

that are incident to
c_{K}
are in one-to-one
correspondence with the boundary faces
H
of
K
.
The boundary
(p+1)
-faces of
K\times[0,\,h]

are exactly those of the form
H\times[0,\,h]
,

K\times\{0\}
, and
K\times\{h\}
.
The homotopy class of
U
restricted
to
\partial(K\times\{0\})
is the homotopy class
of
u
on
\partial K
,
\sigma(u,\,\partial K)
.
The homotopy class of
U
restricted
to
\partial(K\times\{h\})
is trivial, because
\varphi_{j}

is smooth. Therefore, Lemma
2.9

implies

(3.13)

\sigma(u,\,\partial K)=\sum_{H^{\prime}\ni c_{K}}\sigma(U,\,H),

where the sum is taken over all
1
-cells
H^{\prime}

of the dual grid that are incident to
c_{K}
.
Since (
3.13
) holds for any
K
,
the claim (
3.12
) follows.
Using the definition (
2.5
) of
\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}

and (
3.12
), we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(u,\,h,\,y)\leq h\sum_{H\in\mathscr{G}_{p-1}}\left|\sigma(U,\,H)\right|\leq Ch\int_{X_{p}}\left|\nabla U\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}.\end{split}

The second inequality is a consequence of
Lemma
2.15
.
From (
3.11
), Lemma
2.5

and (
3.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}\fint_{Q^{p+1}_{h}}\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(u,\,h,\,y)\,\mathrm{d}y&\lesssim h\fint_{Q^{p+1}_{h}}\int_{R_{p}(h,\,y)}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla\varphi_{j}\right|^{p}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\hskip 8.5359pt+h^{2}\fint_{Q^{p+1}_{h}}\int_{R_{p-1}(h,\,y)}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla\varphi_{j}\right|^{p}+\frac{\left|u-\varphi_{j}\right|^{p}}{h^{p}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p-1}\mathrm{d}y\\
&\lesssim D_{p}(u)+D_{p}(\varphi_{j})+\frac{1}{h^{p}}\int_{\Omega}\left|u-\varphi_{j}\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

By passing to the limit as
j\to+\infty

in the right-hand side, and then taking the infimum
over all sequences of smooth maps that
converge
W^{1,p}
-weakly to
u
,
we obtain (
3.1
).
∎

By the same line of reasoning we used
in the previous lemma, we can prove that
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}

is stable with respect to strong
W^{1,p}
-convergence.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence that converges strongly
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to a map
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
.
For all
h\in(0,\,h_{0}]
and almost all
y\in Q^{p+1}
,
there exists
n_{*}=n_{*}(u,\,h,\,y)
such that

(3.14)

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)=\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u_{n},\,h,\,y)

for all
n\geq n_{*}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
we consider a sequence of smooth maps
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

that converge to
u
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)

and we extend it to a larger
domain
\Omega^{\prime}\supseteq\overline{\Omega}

by reflection about
\partial\Omega
.
By applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality,
as in the proof of (
3.9
), we obtain

(3.15)

\left\|u_{n}-\varphi_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(R_{p-1}}\leq\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(R_{p-1}}+\left\|u-\varphi_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(R_{p-1}}\leq\delta_{*},

for sufficiently large values of
n
and
j
.
Here we have written
R_{p-1}
instead of
R_{p-1}(h,\,y)
.
Now, we consider the grid
\mathscr{E}
given by (
3.10
)
along with its
p
-skeleton
X_{p}
. In addition to the
map
U\colon X_{p}\to\mathscr{N}
we have already defined in the proof
of Lemma
3.1
, we define
U_{n}\colon X_{p}\to\mathscr{N}

as
U_{n}(x,\,0):=u_{n}(x)
for
x\in R_{p-1}
,

U_{n}(x,\,h):=U(x,\,h)=\varphi_{j}(x)
for
x\in R_{p-1}
, and

U_{n}(x,\,t):=\pi_{\mathscr{N}}\!\left(\left(1-\frac{t}{h}\right)u_{j}(x)+\frac{t}{h}\varphi_{j}(x)\right)\!,\qquad\textrm{for }(x,\,t)\in R_{p-2}\times[0,\,h].

The map
U_{n}
is well-defined because of (
3.15
).
For almost every
y\in Q^{p-1}
, Fubini theorem implies
that the restrictions of
u_{n}
to the
(p-1)
-
and the
(p-2)
-skeletons converge to the restrictions of
u
,
strongly in
W^{1,p}(R_{p-1})
,
W^{1,p}(R_{p-2})
, respectively.
As a consequence, for almost all
y\in Q^{p+1}

we have
U_{n}\to U
strongly in
W^{1,p}(X_{p-1})
.
Given a
p
-cell
H
of
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

with
H\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing
, let
\sigma(U_{n},\,H)

be the homotopy class of
U_{n}
on
\partial(H\times[0,\,h])
.
Stability of the homotopy classes with respect
to strong
W^{1,p}
-convergence
(Proposition
2.12
)
implies that there exists
n_{*}=n_{*}(u,\,h,\,y)
large enough
that, for all
p
-cells
H
intersecting
\Omega
and
all
n\geq n_{*}
, there holds

(3.16)

\sigma(U_{n},\,H)=\sigma(U,\,H).

(We can choose
n_{*}
uniformly with respect to
H

because there are only finitely many cells of the given grid
that intersect
\Omega
.)
Let
a_{H}
,
b_{H}
be the endpoints of the
cell
H^{\prime}
dual to
H
, oriented in such a way that

(\tau_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{p},b_{H}-a_{H})

is a positive basis of
\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}

whenever
(\tau_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{p})
is
a positive basis of the
p
-plane containing
H
.
Because of (
3.12
) and (

),
we have

\begin{split}\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u_{n},\,h,\,y)&=\sum_{H\in\mathscr{G}_{p}(h,\,y)\colon H\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset}\left(\sigma(U_{n},\,H)\llbracket b_{H}\rrbracket-\sigma(U_{n},\,H)\llbracket a_{H}\rrbracket\right)\\
&=\sum_{H\in\mathscr{G}_{p}(h,\,y)\colon H\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset}\left(\sigma(U,\,H)\llbracket b_{H}\rrbracket-\sigma(U,\,H)\llbracket a_{H}\rrbracket\right)=\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)\end{split}

for any

, and the lemma follows.
∎

4
Approximation by maps with nice singularities
and bounded relaxed energy

The goal of this section is to prove the following result:

Proposition 4.1
.

For all
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
,
there exists a sequence
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

in

that converges to

strongly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and satisfies

\overline{D}_{p}(u_{j})\leq C\overline{D}_{p}(u)

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and some constant
C
that depends
only on
\Omega
,
\mathscr{N}
,
p
.

This will be achieved by combining two propositions:

Proposition 4.2
.

For all
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
,
there exists a sequence
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

in
R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
that converges to
u

strongly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and satisfies

\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j}))\leq C\overline{D}_{p}(u)

for some constant
C
that depends only on
\Omega
,
\mathscr{N}
,
p
.

.

For all
u\in R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
, there holds

\overline{D}_{p}(u)\leq D_{p}(u)+C\,\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u)),

for some constant
C
that depends only on
\Omega
,
\mathscr{N}
,

.

Proposition

is an immediate consequence
of Propositions

and

,
which will be proved in Subsections

and

, respectively.

4.1
Proof of Proposition
4.2

Proposition

is a variant of
[
4
, Theorem 2]

and can be proved by reasoning along the lines
of
[

, Theorem 3]
.
The first tool in the proof is the following proposition,
which is a variant of
[

, Proposition 2.1]
.
Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
be a given map.
Using reflection across the boundary of
\Omega
, as before,
we construct an extension of

(denoted by the same symbol)

and satisfies (

).
Let
h_{0}:=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\dist(\Omega,\,\partial\Omega^{\prime})>0

Let
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)

and

. By Lemma

and an averaging argument,
we can select

in such a way that, for

on

(4.1)

\int_{R^{j}(h,\,y)\cap\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{j}\leq Ch^{j-n}D_{p}(u,\,\Omega)\qquad\textrm{for all }j\in\{0,\,1,\,\ldots,p\},

where the constant

depends only on
\Omega
and
p
.
Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)

be a collection of cubes of the grid
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
,
all of them having non-empty intersection with
\Omega
.
We denote by

and
R_{\partial\mathscr{C}}
,
respectively, the
(p+1)
-dimensional skeleton of
\mathscr{C}

and the

-dimensional one, that is,

(4.2)

R_{\mathscr{C}}:=\bigcup_{K\in\mathscr{C}}K,\qquad R_{\partial\mathscr{C}}:=\bigcup_{K\in\mathscr{C}}\partial K.

For all

and all

, we define

(4.3)

U_{t}(X):=\left\{x\in\Omega^{\prime}\colon\dist(x,\,X)<t\right\}\!.

In particular, we have
U_{t}(R_{\partial\mathscr{C}})\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}

if
h\leq h_{0}
and

.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
0<h\leq h_{0}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p+1}
,
0<\theta<1/2

and
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{G}(h,\,y,\,\Omega)

The proof of this result is based on the so-called

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
g\colon\partial B^{p+1}_{r}\to\mathscr{N}
be a Lipschitz map that
is homotopic to a constant. Then, there exists
a Lipschitz extension
v\colon\overline{B}^{p+1}_{r}\to\mathscr{N}

of

that satisfies

(4.31)

D_{p}\!\left(v,\,B^{p}_{r}\right)\leq Cr\int_{\partial B^{p+1}_{r}}\left|\nabla g\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{p}

for some constant
C_{p}
that depends only on
p
.

Proof.

Since the estimate (
4.31
) is scale-invariant,
up to a rescaling we can assume without loss of
generality that
r=1
. By a regularisation argument
(using convolution and the projection (
2.41
) onto
\mathscr{N}
),
we can also assume that
g
is smooth.
Let
\hat{g}\colon\overline{B}^{p+1}\to\mathscr{N}

be defined as
\hat{g}(x):=g(x/\left|x\right|)
,

x\in\overline{B}^{p+1}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then,
[
11
, Lemma 5]
yields

\inf\left\{D_{p}(v)\colon v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{B}^{p+1},\,\mathscr{N}),\,u=g\ \textrm{ on }\partial B^{p+1}\right\}\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(3^{p}+1\right)D_{p}(\hat{g})

and the lemma follows.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

If

is smooth, then we can construct a

that satisfies (

For the next lemma, we set

Let

(

Step 5

.

Smooth functions are dense in

).
Therefore, we can replace

with a smooth function

that still satisfies (

), (
4.45
),

4.46

It only remain to extend

5
Proof of Theorem
1

We are finally ready to prove Theorem
1
.
The bulk of the argument is the proof of the following statement.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Cauchy sequence
in
R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

such that
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{D}_{p}(u_{j})<+\infty
.
Then,
(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy
sequence in
\mathbb{FS}_{0}(\Omega;\,\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}))
.

Proof.

The estimate (
3.7
) in Lemma
3.1

and the assumption that
\overline{D}_{p}(u_{j})
is bounded
imply that
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j}))<+\infty
.
By Lemma
2.3
, we can extract a
subsequence
(j_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and a find a
chain
S\in\mathbb{F}_{0}(\Omega;\,\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}))
in such a way that

(5.1)

\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j_{n}})-S)\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }n\to+\infty.

In order to complete the proof, it suffices to
show that
all
converging subsequences
have the same limit
S
. To this end, let
(j^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

be another subsequence and
S^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{0}(\Omega;\,\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}))

be another chain, such that

(5.2)

\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j^{\prime}_{n}})-S^{\prime})\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }n\to+\infty.

We claim that
S=S^{\prime}
.
Because of (
3.5
), we know that

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u_{j_{n}},\,h,\,y)=P(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j_{n}}),\,h,\,y)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in(0,\,h_{0}]
and almost
all
y\in Q^{p+1}
, where
P(\cdot,\,h,\,y)
is
the approximation operator induced by the grid
\mathscr{G}(h,\,y)
,
as in (
2.30
). Using Fatou lemma,
Statement (i) in Proposition
2.6
,
and (
5.1
), we deduce

\begin{split}&\int_{Q^{p+1}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u_{j_{n}},\,h,\,y)-P(S,\,h,\,y))\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{Q^{p+1}}\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(P(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j_{n}})-S,\,h,\,y))\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j_{n}})-S)=0.\end{split}

As a consequence, upon extraction of a (non-relabelled)
subsequence, we have the pointwise convergence

(5.3)

\begin{split}\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u_{j_{n}},\,h,\,y)-P(S,\,h,\,y))\to 0\qquad\textrm{ for all }h\in(0,\,h_{0}],\textrm{ a.e. }y\in Q^{p+1}.\end{split}

In a similar way, passing to a (non-relabelled) subsequence,
we have

(5.4)

\begin{split}\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u_{j^{\prime}_{n}},\,h,\,y)-P(S^{\prime},\,h,\,y))\to 0\qquad\textrm{ for all }h\in(0,\,h_{0}],\textrm{ a.e. }y\in Q^{p+1}.\end{split}

Since
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence,
it converges
W^{1,p}
-strongly to a
map
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
.
By Lemma
3.2
, for all
h\in(0,\,h_{0}]

and almost all
y\in Q^{p+1}
there is
n_{*}(u,\,h,\,y)

such that

(5.5)

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u_{j_{n}},\,h,\,y)=\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u_{j^{\prime}_{n}},\,h,\,y)=\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)\qquad\textrm{for all }n\geq n_{*}(u,\,h,\,y).

Combining (
5.3
), (
5.4
),
and (
5.5
), we obtain

P(S,\,h,\,y)=P(S^{\prime},\,h,\,y)\qquad\textrm{for all }h\in(0,\,h_{0}],\textrm{ a.e. }y\in Q^{p+1}.

From Statement
(iii)

in Proposition
2.6
, we conclude
that
S=S^{\prime}
, as claimed. The lemma follows.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

As we will do in the sequel, let us write
\mathbf{S}(u)
for the unique limit
S

(with respect to the
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}
-norm) of the sequence
(\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}(u_{j}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Combining (
5.3
) with (
5.5
), we
also obtain

\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{grid}}(u,\,h,\,y)=P(\mathbf{S}(u),\,h,\,y)\qquad\textrm{for all }h\in(0,\,h_{0}],\textrm{ a.e. }y\in Q^{p+1}.

Then, Statement (ii) in Proposition
2.6

implies that for any sequence
h_{k}\to 0
there exists a (non-relabelled)
subsequence such that
\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}(u,h_{k},y)-\mathbf{S}(u))\to 0

as

for almost every

.

In the proof of Theorem
1
, we will also
use the following characterisation of strong approximability
by smooth maps:

Proposition 5.2
.

A map
u\in W^{1,p}(B^{p+1},\,\mathscr{N})
belongs to
H_{\mathrm{strong}}^{1,p}(B^{p+1},\,\mathscr{N})

if and only if, for all
(p+1)
-dimensional closed
simplex
T\subseteq B^{p+1}
and almost all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p+1}

such that
\left|y\right|<\dist(T,\,\partial B^{p+1})
,
the restriction
u_{|y+\partial T}
is homotopic to a constant
(in the sense defined in Section
2.4.1
).

Proposition
5.2
follows, e.g., from
[
17
, Theorems 1.10 and 1.12]
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
5.1
,
combined with standard density arguments, imply that
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}

can be extended to an operator

\mathbf{S}\colon H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})\to\mathbb{F}_{0}(\Omega;\,\pi_{p}(\mathscr{N}))

that is continuous in the following sense:
if
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

such that
u_{j}\to u
strongly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)

for some
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

and
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{D}_{p}(u_{j},\Omega)<+\infty
,
then
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}(u_{j})-\mathbf{S}(u))\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
.
The estimate (
9
) follows from Lemma
3.1
,
because the
\mathbb{F}_{\Omega}
-norm is lower semicontinuous with respect
to
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}
-convergence (Lemma
2.3
).
For later convenience, let us also note that
the operator
\mathbf{S}
is local, that is, for any Lipschitz
subdomain
V\subseteq\Omega
and any
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
,
there holds

(5.6)

\mathbf{S}(u_{|V})=\mathbf{S}(u)\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}V.

Equality (
5.6
) is an immediate consequence
of (
2.21
) and (
3.1
) in case
u\in R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
,
and remains true in general, by a density argument.

It only remains to prove that a map
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

belongs to
H_{\mathrm{strong,loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
if and only if
\mathbf{S}(u)=0
.
Let
u\in H_{\mathrm{strong,loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
, let
B
be a ball
such that
\overline{B}\subseteq\Omega
,
an let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of smooth maps

\overline{B}\to\mathscr{N}
that converges
W^{1,p}(B)
-strongly to
u_{|B}
.
Since
C^{\infty}(\overline{B},\,\mathscr{N})\subseteq R^{1,p}(B,\,\mathscr{N})
,
the definition (
3.1
) of
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{nice}}
immediately
gives
\mathbf{S}(\varphi_{k})=0
. By continuity of
\mathbf{S}
and (
5.6
),
we obtain
\mathbf{S}(u)\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B=\mathbf{S}(u_{|B})=0
and, since the ball
B

is arbitrary, we conclude that
\mathbf{S}(u)=0
.
For the opposite implication, assume
that
u\in H_{\mathrm{weak}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})
satisfies
\mathbf{S}(u)=0
.
In light of Proposition
5.2
, proving that
u\in H_{\mathrm{strong,loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

is equivalent to proving that, for any ball
B

compactly contained in
\Omega
, any
(p+1)
-dimensional
simplex
T\subseteq B
and any vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p+1}

with
\left|y\right|<\dist(T,\,\partial B)
,
the restriction
u_{|y+\partial T}
is homotopic to a constant.
Let
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
R^{1,p}(\Omega,\,\mathscr{N})

such that
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)

and
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{D}_{p}(u_{k})<+\infty
;
such a sequence exists, by Proposition
4.1
.
By continuity of
\mathbf{S}
, we have
\mathbb{FS}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{S}(u_{k}))\to 0

as
k\to+\infty
. Given a simplex
T
as above, Lemma
2.4

implies that

\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{S}(u_{k}),\,T+y)=0

for all large enough
k
and almost all small enough
y
.
(Here
\mathbb{I}
is the intersection index, defined by (
2.24
).)
For any such
k
and
y
, the restriction of
u_{k}

to
\partial T+y
is homotopic to a constant, due to (

).
Since the restriction
u_{k|\partial T+y}

converges

-strongly to
u_{|\partial T+y}

,
