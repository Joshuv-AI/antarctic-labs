Karhunen Loève Expansions of Hilbert Space-Valued Random Elements
Trajan Murphy

Abstract
The Karhunen-Loève Expansion (KLE) of a stochastic process is a well understood eigenfunction expansion used widely in time series analysis, stochastic PDEs, and signal processing. Karhunen-Loève expansions have also been proven to exist for other types of stochastic elements whose values lie in certain L 2 L^{2} spaces. This article provides a concise proof about the necessary and sufficient conditions for a function v v defined on some sample space Ω \Omega and whose values lie in some Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} to admit an eigenfunction expansion like the well-known KLE. We draw on the existing theory of Bochner spaces and Hilbert-Schmidt spaces and construct an isomorphism between them. Furthermore, this isomorphism is natural, which has important computational consequences. Finally, we demonstrate with an example the computational advantages conferred by considering the KLE in this generalized setting.

2.1
The Space

Let

Definition 2.1

(Simple Function)

A function

We say that

The following are equivalent

1.

2.

2.3
The Space
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},L^{2}(\Omega))

From this point on, unless specified otherwise,
\mathcal{H}
denotes a Hilbert space. Let
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G}
be two Hilbert spaces. Let
H\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
.
H
is said to be
Hilbert-Schmidt
if

\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\|He\|^{2}_{\mathcal{G}}<\infty

for arbitrary orthonormal basis (ONB)
\mathscr{E}
of
\mathcal{H}
. Of course, this sum is defined iff
He=0
for all but at most countably many
e\in\mathscr{E}
. We denote the set of all Hilbert-Schmidt operators
\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
by
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
. It is routine to prove that
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
is a vector subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
(and is proven so in
[
5
]
). Much of the current literature on Hilbert-Schmidt operators focuses on those which are self-adjoint and positive (
[
18
,
20
,
25
]
), endomorphisms on separable Hilbert spaces (
[
20
,
27
,
22
,
17
,
25
]
), or integral operators (
[
10
]
). Eventually, we will produce a natural isomorphism between
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
and
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},L^{2}(\Omega))
, but in order to do so, we must generalize the properties of Hilbert-Schmidt operators here.

Theorem 2.2
.

\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
is a vector subspace of
\mathcal{B}_{0}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
where the latter denotes the space of compact linear operators
\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let
H\in\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G}
), let
\mathscr{E}
be an ONB of
\mathcal{H}
. If
He\neq 0
for only finitely many
e\in\mathscr{E}
, then
H
is finite rank and therefore compact. For the more interesting case, suppose
\left\{e_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an enumeration of the unit vectors in
\mathscr{E}
for which
He\neq 0
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
define
H_{N}
by
H_{N}x=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\langle x,e_{n}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}He_{n}
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Each
H_{N}
is of finite rank, its image lying in
\mathrm{Span}\left\{He_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
. We demonstrate that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|H-H_{N}\|_{\mathcal{G}}=0
(
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{G}}
denotes the operator norm on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
) as follows:

Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
be a unit vector. We have

\displaystyle\|(H-H_{N})x\|_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n>N}\left\langle x,e_{n}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}He_{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n>N}\left\langle x,e_{n}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n>N}\|He_{n}\|_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n>N}\|He_{n}\|_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

The final term goes to zero independently of
x
as
N\to\infty
. Thus,
\left\{H_{N}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of finite rank operators approximating
H
in the operator norm, which makes
H
compact.
∎

By the spectral theorem, we can write the singular value decomposition (SVD) of each
H\in\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
as
H=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sigma_{r}g_{r}\widehat{\otimes}h_{r}
where
R=\text{rank}(H)
;
\left\{\sigma_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}
is a decreasing, positive, sequence which approaches 0 if
R=\aleph_{0}
;
\left\{g_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}
is an orthonormal set in
\mathcal{G}
; and
\left\{h_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}
is an orthonormal set in
\mathcal{H}
. Here
\widehat{\otimes}
denotes the outer product of two vectors, i.e.
g\widehat{\otimes}h
is defined to be the rank one operator
\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
given by
x\mapsto\left\langle x,h\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}g
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
. The SVD of
H
allows us to make the following claim:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H\in\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
. Then for any two ONB
\mathscr{E}
,
\mathscr{F}
of
\mathcal{H}
,
\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\|He\|_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}=\sum_{f\in\mathscr{F}}\|Hf\|_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}

Proof.

Let
\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sigma_{r}g_{r}\widehat{\otimes}h_{r}
be the SVD of
H
. The statement of Lemma
2.1
holds if, for arbitrarily chosen ONB
\mathscr{E}
of
\mathcal{H}
,
\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sigma_{r}^{2}=\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\|He\|_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}
. Let

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\left\{e\in\mathscr{E}:\left\langle e,h_{r}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\neq 0\text{ for some }h_{r}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{r=1}^{R}\left\{e\in\mathscr{E}:\left\langle e,h_{r}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\neq 0\right\}

\mathscr{E}_{0}
is countable. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\|He\|_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\left\|\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sigma_{r}\left\langle e,h_{r}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}g_{r}\right\|_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{0}}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sigma_{r}^{2}\left\langle e,h_{r}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{0}}\sigma_{r}^{2}\left\langle e,h_{r}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sigma_{r}^{2}

where the interchange of sums is justified since the terms
\sigma_{r}^{2}\left\langle e,h_{r}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
are non-negative.

∎

Lemma
2.1
demonstrates that the value of
\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\|He\|^{2}_{\mathcal{G}}
does not depend on the ONB
\mathscr{E}
. Thus we may unambiguously define the
Hilbert-Schmidt norm
of a Hilbert-Schmidt operator as
\|H\|_{\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})}:=\left(\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\|He\|^{2}_{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{1/2}
for any choice of orthonormal basis
\mathscr{E}
of
\mathcal{H}
. This norm can be easily seen to be generated by the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product
\left\langle H_{1},H_{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})}=\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\left\langle H_{1}e,H_{2}e\right\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Corollary 2.1
.

If
H\in\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
, then
\|H\|^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})}
equals the sum of the squares of the singular values of
H
.

The following properties are proven in
[
5
]

Proposition 2.1
.

1.

\left\langle H_{1},H_{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})}:=\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\left\langle H_{1}e,H_{2}e\right\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}
defines an inner product on
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
which generates the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

2.

\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})
is complete w.r.t. this inner product.

With the above information, we can identify
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
with a subspace of
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},L^{2}(\Omega))
from Theorem

Theorem 2.3
.

If
v\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
, then the operator
H_{v}:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
defined by
(H_{v}x)(\omega)=\left\langle v(\omega),x\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
is Hilbert-Schmidt.

Proof.

As
v
is ESV, we may, if necessary, redefine
v
on a null set such that
v(\Omega)
is separable. Put
\left\{e_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{V}:=\overline{\mathrm{Span}\left\{v(\Omega)\right\}}
where
N=\dim(\mathcal{V})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\aleph_{0}\right\}
. Let
\mathscr{E}
be an extension of
\left\{e_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
to an ONB of
\mathcal{H}
. (This could be done by letting
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
be an ONB of
\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
and then letting
\mathscr{E}=\left\{e_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}\cup\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
.)

By the weak measurability of
v
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
H_{v}
does actually map each element of
\mathcal{H}
to an element of
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}}\|H_{v}e\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\left\langle v,e_{n}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\left\langle v(\omega),e_{n}\right\rangle^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}}\;d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\langle v(\omega),e_{n}\right\rangle^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}}\;d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\|v(\omega)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}}\;d\mathbb{P}

If
N=\aleph_{0}
, then the interchange of integration and summation is justified by the Dominated Convergence since Bessel’s inequality implies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle v(\omega),e_{i}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\|v(\omega)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\mathbb{P}-
a.e. for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle v(\omega),e_{i}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\|v(\omega)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\mathbb{P}-
a.e. Thus
\|H_{v}\|_{\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},L^{2}(\Omega))}=\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}<\infty
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

The correspondence

is an isometry from

to
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},L^{2}(\Omega))

2.4
Properties of the KL-Expansion

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose
v\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
, then
v
has a KLE.

Proof.

Define
v_{0}:=v-\mathbb{E}(v)
and
H_{v_{0}}
as in Theorem
2.3
. Let
H_{v_{0}}
have SVD
\sum_{r=1}^{R}\lambda_{r}^{1/2}Y_{r}\widehat{\otimes}\phi_{r}
, with
\{\lambda_{r}^{1/2}\}_{r=1}^{R}\subset\mathbb{R}
the singular values,
\left\{Y_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
the left singular vectors and
\left\{\phi_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}\subset\mathcal{H}
the right singular vectors. Now let
v^{\prime}:=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\lambda_{r}^{1/2}Y_{r}\phi_{r}
. We claim that
v^{\prime}
defines an element of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
.

Since
v^{\prime}
takes all its values in
\mathrm{Span}\left\{\phi_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}
, it’s ESV. As
\left\langle v^{\prime},x\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\lambda_{r}^{1/2}Y_{r}\left\langle\phi_{r},x\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
describes an
L^{2}(\Omega)
-convergent sum of measurable random variables for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
,
v^{\prime}
is weakly measurable. Thus
v^{\prime}
is Bochner measurable by Pettis’ Theorem. Finally, the orthonormality of
\left\{Y_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}
and
\left\{\phi_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}
guarantee that the
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
norm of
v^{\prime}
equals
\sum_{r=1}^{R}\lambda_{r}=\|H_{v_{0}}\|^{2}_{\text{HS}(\mathcal{H},L^{2}(\Omega))}=\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}<\infty
.

Now consider the Hilbert-Schmidt operator
H_{v^{\prime}}
. For any
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{v^{\prime}}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\lambda_{r}^{1/2}Y_{r}\left\langle\phi_{r},x\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\lambda_{r}^{1/2}(Y_{r}\widehat{\otimes}\phi_{r})x

\displaystyle=H_{v}x

By Cor.
2.2
, the correspondence
v\mapsto H_{v}
is an isometry; we must have
v_{0}=v^{\prime}
, which is to say
v=\mathbb{E}(v)+\sum_{r=1}^{R}\lambda_{r}^{1/2}Y_{r}\phi_{r}
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

The correspondence
v\mapsto H_{v}
represents an isometric isomorphism between
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
and
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H},L^{2}(\Omega))

Corollary 2.4
.

Both

2.5
Borel Measurability

Since
\mathcal{H}
constitutes a topogical space, a question of interest arises: What if we merely assume

2.6

Consider the category

\text{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{M},L^{2}(\Omega))

and

the projection onto

\ell^{2}(\mathcal{I},\mathbb{R}^{5})
.) We will denote truncation (
4
) as the
vector field KL truncation
of
v
.

By Theorem
2.6
, the mean-squared error incurred by truncation (
4
) must be less than that of (
3
), i.e.
\|v-\tilde{v}_{R_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}\leq\|v-v_{R_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}
. We confirm this with experiments, observing that the relative squared error of the component-wise KL truncation - i.e. the quotient
\dfrac{\|v-v_{R_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}}{\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}}
- is considerably less than that of the vector field KL truncation - i.e. the quotient
\dfrac{\|v-\tilde{v}_{R_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}}{\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}}
(see Figure
1
). In fact the component-wise KL truncation requires 30 terms to obtain a similar relative squared error as a 5 term vector field KL truncation. Our results demonstrate that the component-wise expansion implemented by Gao in
[
9
]
is suboptimal in the
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}^{2}
sense.

4
Conclusion

The Karhunen-Loève expansion of a function
v:\Omega\to L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
is a well known method for decomposing a square-summable stochastic process into a countable number of simple components with provable optimality properties. While most of the generalizations of the KLE focus on square-summable functions of the form
v:\Omega\to\mathcal{H}
where
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space of scalar or
\mathbb{R}^{Q}
-valued functions, the existence of an expansion for arbitrary - even non-separable -
\mathcal{H}
can be demonstrated by appealing to the isomorphism between the spaces
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
and
\text{HS}(\mathcal{H},L^{2}(\Omega))
induced by the natural isomorphism between the functors
L^{2}(\Omega,-)
and
\text{HS}(-,L^{2}(\Omega))
allowing for a more complete, cohesive theory. We furthermore demonstrate that the separability assumptions usually placed on the image of
v
isn’t just a computational convenience, but a necessary condition for the KLE of
v
to hold. The usefulness of such a general view of the KLE holds in the capability to represent a multitude data types corresponding to different Hilbert spaces, from lists, to time series, to images, to solutions of PDEs.

Appendix A
The Failure of the Categorical Tensor Product

We demonstrate in this section that the “tensor product” as described in
2.4
fails to be a tensor product in the category
Hilbert
. We adopt the example from
[
11
]
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space with ONB
\left\{e_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and suppose we have the commutative diagram as below

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

(5)

where
b
is the continuous bilinear map
(u,v)\mapsto\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\otimes
is the bilinear map
(u,v)\mapsto u\otimes v
, and
\varphi
is the linear map
u\otimes v\mapsto\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Diagram
5
exists and commutes in the category of vector spaces over
\mathbb{R}
. However, when each object in
5
is endowed with its inner product structure, the maps
\otimes
and
\varphi
fail to be continuous.

To demonstrate that
\otimes
is not continuous, define for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
T_{N}\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
by
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}(e_{n},e_{n})
. While
\lim_{N\to\infty}T_{N}\to\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}(e_{n},e_{n})
, a well-defined element of
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
, we have that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\varphi(T_{N})\|_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}=\infty
.

Further, define for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
S_{N}\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
by
S_{N}:=\ \sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}e_{n}\otimes e_{n}
. Then
S_{N}
converges to the well-defined element of
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
given by
\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}e_{n}\otimes e_{n}
. However,
\lim_{N\to\infty}|\varphi(S_{N})|=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}=\infty
.
