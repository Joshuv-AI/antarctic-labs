Two to Tango: Coupled Task–Reference Selection for Safe LLM Fine-tuning
Xinrui Chen, Jianhao Zhang, Ou Wu, Di Gao, wuou@ucas.ac.cn

Abstract
Fine-tuning safety aligned large language models (LLMs) on downstream data improves adaptation but may erode learned safety behavior. Existing methods use fixed safety examples, global constraints, or one-sided task filtering. Our diagnostics show task updates expose different safety constraints, motivating joint selection of relevant references and compatible task samples. We propose DualSelect , a coupled framework for task and reference selection that refreshes task conditioned safety references before filtering whole task samples compatible with the induced reference direction. Under a minimax view, DualSelect selects safety references with high preservation loss and task conflict, together with compatible task samples, through entropy-regularized scoring surrogates, lazy reference refresh, and gradient correction. On 1B–8B LLMs, DualSelect preserves safety without losing task utility; using the REDORCA judge, it improves Safety Avg. over the strongest baseline by at least 5.10 5.10 points and remains highest in Safety Avg. across judges with moderate overhead. This view extends to retention focused continual learning. Code at: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/DualSelect-D814 .

Introduction

Fine-tuning safety-aligned LLMs on downstream data improves task adaptation but may erode safety behavior learned during alignment

Recent methods filter/reweight task data, mix safety examples, or impose global safety constraints

;
17
;

, but still use static/global safety evidence.
Our diagnostics reveal update dependence: different task directions activate different safety constraints, making task-conditioned references more effective than static ones for safety preservation.

Paradigm shift in safety-reference use: from static subsets that may miss update-specific constraints to task-conditioned active constraints.

This shift reframes safety preservation as selecting references that constrain each task update. Prior work shows safety data are not uniformly useful, motivating safety-subset selection
35
, sample-level risk estimation
17
;
47
, and safe/harmful reference-aware distributional reweighting
45
. Yet these methods still assign global scores to samples, references, or distributions, rather than pairing each update with its active safety constraints.
Thus, the missing step is not another global safety-data score, but update-conditioned reference selection.

Building on this update-conditioned view, we introduce
DualSelect
, a coupled task–reference selection framework for safe fine-tuning.
Its key distinction is not simply using safety references or filtering task data, but conditioning both decisions on update geometry.
DualSelect selects safety references with high preservation loss and task conflict, exposing active constraints for the current task direction. It then filters whole task samples compatible with the induced reference direction and applies reference-gradient correction to convert selected references into an update-level constraint. Gradient interaction provides the shared signal linking reference discovery, task filtering, and correction. This reference-constrained view also extends to continual learning by treating old-task examples as retention references.

Theoretically, we formulate fine-tuning as a bilevel style minimax selection problem and derive entropy regularized surrogates that yield alternating scoring rules. DualSelect couples selection with correction: selection exposes task conditioned safety constraints, while correction analyzes alignment between realized updates and reference gradients. Unlike fixed pool reweighting or projection based methods
42
;
4
, DualSelect treats task samples and safety references as coupled variables, not separate scoring targets. Empirically, across 1B to 8B LLMs on REDORCA and GSM8K, DualSelect better preserves safety with comparable or best utility; under the primary REDORCA judge, it improves Safety Avg. by
5.10
+ points over the strongest baseline. Ablations support reference relevance, compatibility, and correction.

Our contributions are fourfold:

•

We identify a shift from static safety-reference use to task-conditioned reference selection, and show that selected references are structured and preserve held-out safety better than static ones.

•

We formulate
DualSelect
as coupled min–max selection over high-loss, task-conflicting safety references and compatible whole task samples.

•

We derive entropy-regularized surrogates whose scores induce hard selection, with lazy reference refresh, reference-gradient correction, and direction-level update-alignment analysis.

•

Across two datasets and 1B–8B LLMs, we show stronger safety, comparable utility, moderate overhead, and ablation-backed mechanisms.

3.1
Problem Setting

For safe fine-tuning, let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the model parameters and
\theta_{0}
the aligned initialization. The task data are
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{new}}=\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
. The reference pool is
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}=\{(x_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}},y_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}})\}_{j=1}^{M}
, disjoint from customization and evaluation data. In the main safety setting, each reference is a safety prompt paired with an aligned safe response,
y_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}=y_{j}^{\mathrm{safe}}
. Generally,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}
can be any retention reference pool; in continual learning,
y_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}
is the old-task target. We use length-normalized supervised loss

\ell_{\theta}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{|y|}\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{|y|}\log p_{\theta}(y_{t}\mid x,y_{<t}).

(1)

Task and reference losses are
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{new},i}=\ell_{\theta}(x_{i},y_{i})
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}=\ell_{\theta}(x_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}},y_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}})
, with gradients
\mathbf{g}_{i}^{\mathrm{new}}
and
\mathbf{g}_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}
. Appendix
B
gives a prompt-only KL-to-
\theta_{0}
variant. We use hard selectors
\mathbf{w}\in\{0,1\}^{N}
and
\mathbf{p}\in\{0,1\}^{M}
for task and reference candidates. The reference selector finds poorly preserved, task-conflicting references, while the task selector keeps whole samples gradient-compatible with the selected reference direction.

We write cosine similarity as

\phi(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=\frac{\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|\,\|\mathbf{v}\|}.

(2)

All cosine-based scores use the stabilized denominator

(\|\mathbf{u}\|+\varepsilon_{\phi})(\|\mathbf{v}\|+\varepsilon_{\phi})

in implementation.

3.2
Min–Max Selection Objective and Simplex Scoring Surrogate

Given task and reference selectors
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{p}
, where
w_{i}
and
p_{j}
denote selection weights, we form the aggregate task and reference gradients as

\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{\sum_{i}w_{i}\mathbf{g}_{i}^{\mathrm{new}}}{\sum_{i}w_{i}+\varepsilon},\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})=\frac{\sum_{j}p_{j}\mathbf{g}_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}}{\sum_{j}p_{j}+\varepsilon},

(3)

where
\varepsilon>0
ensures numerical stability. The reference-corrected descent-gradient vector is

\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w})+\rho\,\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p}),

(4)

where
\rho>0
controls the correction strength.

The selection problem balances adaptation to fine-tuning data with behavior preservation on references. In the safety setting, these references are safe responses; in the continual-learning extension, they are old-task examples. We formulate this as a coupled min–max objective, where the outer minimizer selects task samples and the inner maximizer selects poorly fitted reference examples that maximally oppose the task direction.

Hard Selection Formulation.

Let
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{P}
denote budget-feasible task and reference sets. For clarity, we present cardinality budgets:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{K_{\mathrm{new}}}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{w}\in\{0,1\}^{N}:\|\mathbf{w}\|_{0}=K_{\mathrm{new}}\},

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{K_{\mathrm{ref}}}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{p}\in\{0,1\}^{M}:\|\mathbf{p}\|_{0}=K_{\mathrm{ref}}\}.

In implementation,
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{K_{\mathrm{ref}}}
, while
\mathcal{W}
selects whole task samples per mini-batch. Its budget matches the total number of target tokens contributing to the supervised loss. The objective is

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i}w_{i}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{new},i}+\lambda\sum\nolimits_{j}p_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}

(6)

\displaystyle-\mu\,\phi\!\left(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})\right),

where
\lambda,\mu>0
are weights balancing preservation loss and task-reference conflict. The inner maximizer identifies poorly preserved, task-conflicting references, exposing update-relevant constraints. The outer minimizer favors samples compatible with the reference direction. References expose constraints, and samples are filtered accordingly.

Entropy-Regularized Simplex Surrogate.

For tractability, we replace discrete search with an entropy-regularized scoring surrogate:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{N}}

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{M}}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{new},i}-\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{w})

(7)

\displaystyle+\lambda\left(\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{M}p_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}+\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p})\right)

\displaystyle-\mu\,\phi\bigl(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})\bigr),

where
\Delta_{N}
and
\Delta_{M}
are probability simplexes,
\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{w})=-\sum_{i}w_{i}\log w_{i}
,
\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p})=-\sum_{j}p_{j}\log p_{j}
, and
\gamma_{\mathrm{new}},\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}>0
are temperatures.

Opposite optimization directions set the entropy signs:
-\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{w})
gives soft-min task scores;
+\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p})
gives soft-max reference scores. Their scores drive Algorithm
1
’s hard selection.

3.3
Alternating Optimization Algorithm

Since Eq. (
7
) couples
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{p}
through aggregate cosine, block updates are nonlinear. We use frozen-direction scores: fix latest opposite-side direction and compute task–reference interactions.

Time-Scale Separation.

References refresh per epoch with the current model and cached task direction, while task selection runs per mini-batch with the cached reference direction. This keeps filtering responsive while amortizing scoring.

Reference Scoring.

Given a fixed task direction
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w})
, the reference selector uses

c_{j}=-\phi\bigl(\mathbf{g}_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}},\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w})\bigr),a_{j}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}+\frac{\mu}{\lambda}c_{j}.

(8)

High-loss, conflicting references get larger
a_{j}
. The surrogate assigns

p_{j}^{*}=\frac{\exp(a_{j}/\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}})}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\exp(a_{\ell}/\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}})},

(9)

while the implemented selector uses
a_{j}
for budgeted hard Top-
K
selection.

Task Scoring.

Given a fixed reference direction
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})
, the task selector uses

\displaystyle\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=\phi\bigl(\mathbf{g}_{i}^{\mathrm{new}},\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})\bigr),

(10)

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{new},i}-\mu\xi_{i}.

Smaller
b_{i}
means lower cost and stronger safety compatibility.
For a candidate set
\mathcal{S}
, the entropy-regularized surrogate assigns

w_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{S})=\frac{\exp(-b_{i}/\gamma_{\mathrm{new}})}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}}\exp(-b_{k}/\gamma_{\mathrm{new}})},\quad i\in\mathcal{S},

(11)

while the implemented selector ranks whole samples by
b_{i}
and applies the target-token budget.

Algorithm
1
applies these scores per mini-batch, forming a whole-sample subset whose target tokens match the budget specified in experiments.

\mathbf{z}=\Pi(\mathbf{g})

\mathbf{g}

3.5
Direction-Level Analysis of Correction

The selection stage determines the task and reference directions that define the batch-level constraint, while the correction stage controls how strongly the selected reference direction influences the realized descent step. We therefore analyze the local geometry of one corrected step, asking when the selected reference direction is aligned with the final update. This is the quantity directly controlled by
\rho
and later measured by the RawCos/
+
Cos diagnostics.

Let selected task and reference gradients define

\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}^{*}+\rho\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{*}.

(13)

When
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{*}
is evaluated at the checkpoint, the parameter displacement
-\eta\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}
yields a first-order decrease of the aggregate selected-reference loss whenever
\langle\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{*},\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}\rangle>0
. Lazy refresh keeps the interpretation tied to the reference-selection checkpoint and cached reference direction.

Proposition 1

(Correction alignment condition; proof in Appendix
A.5
)
.

Let
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}^{*}
and
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{*}
satisfy
\|\mathbf{u}\|,\|\mathbf{v}\|>0
. Let
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}=\mathbf{u}+\rho\mathbf{v}
with
\rho>0
, and assume
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Suppose
\phi(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\geq c
for some
c\in[-1,1]
, and

\rho\geq-c\,\frac{\|\mathbf{u}\|}{\|\mathbf{v}\|}.

(14)

Then

\phi(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}},\mathbf{v})\geq\frac{c\|\mathbf{u}\|+\rho\|\mathbf{v}\|}{\|\mathbf{u}\|+\rho\|\mathbf{v}\|}\geq 0,

(15)

with strict positivity when Eq. (
14
) is strict.

Proposition
1
gives a local alignment criterion: sufficient correction moves the realized descent-gradient direction into the selected reference gradient’s positive half-space, yielding one-step first-order descent on selected-reference loss. When task and reference directions conflict, the raw cosine
c
decreases, so Eq. (
14
) requires stronger correction. Thus, the selection stages determine the active reference constraint and compatible task direction, while
\rho
controls how strongly this constraint is realized in the update. Appendix
D.5
validates this mechanism through correction-strength sweeps and RawCos/
+
Cos diagnostics.

5
Conclusion

We presented DualSelect, coupled task-reference selection for safe fine-tuning. It selects task samples and safe-response references under task conditioned update geometry, with lightweight safety direction correction. Surrogate derived scoring, fixed budget controls, and lazy reference refresh make DualSelect practical, while direction-level analysis explains correction. On REDORCA and GSM8K, DualSelect better preserves safety while maintaining utility with moderate overhead and limited over-refusal, with ablations confirming roles of reference selection, compatibility, and correction. A continual learning extension uses old task examples as references. Future work includes adaptive refresh, finer grained token selection, and continual tuning with evolving constraints.

Limitations

DualSelect assumes access to a reference signal. Our main experiments use held-out safe-response references; Appendix
B
provides a prompt-only KL variant when safe responses are unavailable. Performance can therefore depend on reference coverage, but this dependence is inspectable and can be improved by retrieval or curation. DualSelect adds selection and correction cost. Streamed scoring and lazy refresh keep overhead moderate in our experiments, while extremely large reference pools may benefit from caching or approximate retrieval. Finally, safety evaluation partly uses automatic judges. We reduce judge-specific artifacts through anonymized pairwise judging, absolute safety metrics, and cross-judge analysis; human evaluation is left for future work.

This appendix provides the supporting derivations for Sec.
3
, including entropy-regularized scoring, score concentration, budgeted hard selection, corrected-update alignment, and selection overhead. Unless otherwise specified,
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w})
and
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})
follow the aggregate-gradient definitions in Sec.
3
, with reference gradients computed from supervised reference losses: safe-response losses for safety fine-tuning and old-task losses for continual learning. The prompt-only KL variant is given in Appendix
B
. For analysis, we use the standard cosine
\phi

A.1
Entropy-Regularized Scoring Rules

The min–max selection objective contains the nonlinear coupling term

-\mu\,\phi\bigl(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})\bigr),

(16)

which makes direct block optimization expensive. DualSelect therefore uses frozen-direction block scores: each block fixes the latest aggregate direction from the other side and computes pairwise task-reference interactions. These frozen-direction pairwise objectives are the scoring surrogates used by Algorithm
1
, and the closed-form rules below optimize them exactly.

Reference scoring rule.

Fix the current task direction
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w})
. For each reference sample
j
, define the conflict score

c_{j}:=-\phi\bigl(\mathbf{g}_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}},\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}(\mathbf{w})\bigr),\quad j=1,\dots,M.

(17)

A larger
c_{j}
indicates stronger conflict with the current task direction. Define the reference score

a_{j}:=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}+\frac{\mu}{\lambda}c_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,M.

(18)

The corresponding entropy-regularized reference scoring problem is

\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{M}}\left\{\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{M}p_{j}a_{j}+\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p})\right\},

(19)

where
\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p})=-\sum_{j}p_{j}\log p_{j}
.

Proposition 2

(Reference scoring weights)
.

Assume
\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}>0
. The objective in Eq. (
19
) is strictly concave on the interior of
\Delta_{M}
, and its unique simplex optimizer is

p_{j}^{*}=\frac{\exp(a_{j}/\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}})}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\exp(a_{\ell}/\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}})},\quad j=1,\dots,M.

(20)

Proof.

The entropy
\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p})
is strictly concave on the interior of
\Delta_{M}
, so the objective is strictly concave. The Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{p},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M}p_{j}a_{j}-\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}p_{j}\log p_{j}

(21)

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{M}p_{j}\right).

The stationarity condition gives

a_{j}-\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}(1+\log p_{j})-\alpha=0.

(22)

Thus
p_{j}=C\exp(a_{j}/\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}})
, and normalization yields Eq. (
20
).
∎

Task scoring rule.

Fix the current reference direction
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})
. For each task sample
i
, define the compatibility score

\xi_{i}:=\phi\bigl(\mathbf{g}_{i}^{\mathrm{new}},\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{p})\bigr),\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

(23)

Larger
\xi_{i}
indicates stronger compatibility with the selected reference direction. Define the task score

b_{i}:=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{new},i}-\mu\xi_{i},\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

(24)

For a candidate task set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
, the entropy-regularized task scoring problem is

\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}}\left\{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}w_{i}b_{i}-\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{w})\right\},

(25)

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}
is the probability simplex on
\mathcal{S}
.

Proposition 3

(Task scoring weights)
.

Assume
\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}>0
. The objective in Eq. (
25
) is strictly convex on the interior of
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}
, and its unique simplex optimizer is

w_{i}^{*}=\frac{\exp(-b_{i}/\gamma_{\mathrm{new}})}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}}\exp(-b_{k}/\gamma_{\mathrm{new}})},\quad i\in\mathcal{S}.

(26)

Proof.

Since
-\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{w})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}w_{i}\log w_{i}
is strictly convex on the interior of
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}
, the objective in Eq. (
25
) is strictly convex. Its Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{w},\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}w_{i}b_{i}+\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}w_{i}\log w_{i}

(27)

\displaystyle+\beta\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}w_{i}-1\right).

The stationarity condition gives

b_{i}+\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}(1+\log w_{i})+\beta=0,\qquad i\in\mathcal{S}.

(28)

Thus
w_{i}=C^{\prime}\exp(-b_{i}/\gamma_{\mathrm{new}})
. Enforcing
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}w_{i}=1
gives Eq. (
26
).
∎

The reference score
a_{j}
favors high-loss, task-conflicting references; in our safety setting, these are safe-response references. The task score
b_{i}
favors low-loss task samples compatible with the selected reference direction. Algorithm
1
uses both scores for budgeted hard selection.

A.2
Concentration of Entropy-Regularized Scores

The entropy coefficients smooth the selection scores. As
\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}
or
\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}
decreases, the corresponding entropy-regularized weights concentrate on the best-scoring candidates.

Proposition 4

(Concentration of reference scores)
.

For
\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}>0
, let
\mathbf{p}^{(\gamma)}
be the optimizer in Proposition
2
, and let

\mathcal{A}_{\max}:=\arg\max_{j}a_{j}.

(29)

Then, as
\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\to 0^{+}
,

\sum_{j\notin\mathcal{A}_{\max}}p_{j}^{(\gamma)}\to 0.

(30)

If
\mathcal{A}_{\max}=\{j^{\star}\}
, then

\mathbf{p}^{(\gamma)}\to\mathbf{e}_{j^{\star}}.

(31)

Proof.

For any
j\notin\mathcal{A}_{\max}
and any
j^{\star}\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}
,

\frac{p_{j}^{(\gamma)}}{p_{j^{\star}}^{(\gamma)}}=\exp\left(\frac{a_{j}-a_{j^{\star}}}{\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right).

(32)

Since
a_{j}-a_{j^{\star}}<0
, the ratio vanishes as
\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\to 0
. Summing over
j\notin\mathcal{A}_{\max}
proves this.
∎

Proposition 5

(Concentration of task scores)
.

For
\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}>0
, let
\mathbf{w}^{(\gamma)}
be the optimizer in Proposition
3
on
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}
, and let

\mathcal{A}_{\min}^{\mathcal{S}}:=\arg\min_{i\in\mathcal{S}}b_{i}.

(33)

Then, as
\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}\to 0^{+}
,

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\min}^{\mathcal{S}}}w_{i}^{(\gamma)}\to 0.

(34)

If
\mathcal{A}_{\min}^{\mathcal{S}}=\{i^{\star}\}
, then

\mathbf{w}^{(\gamma)}\to\mathbf{e}_{i^{\star}}\quad\text{on }\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}.

(35)

Proof.

For any
i\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\min}^{\mathcal{S}}
and
i^{\star}\in\mathcal{A}_{\min}^{\mathcal{S}}
,

\frac{w_{i}^{(\gamma)}}{w_{i^{\star}}^{(\gamma)}}=\exp\left(-\frac{b_{i}-b_{i^{\star}}}{\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}}\right).

(36)

Since
b_{i}-b_{i^{\star}}>0
, the ratio converges to zero as
\gamma_{\mathrm{new}}\to 0
.
∎

A.3
Budgeted Hard Selection Operators

Algorithm
1
applies hard selection to the scores derived above. For references, given scores
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
, the selected reference set is

R_{e}=\operatorname{TopK}_{j}(a_{j},K_{\mathrm{ref}}),

(37)

where
\operatorname{TopK}
returns the
K_{\mathrm{ref}}
largest-score references. The selected reference direction is

\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{*}=\frac{1}{|R_{e}|}\sum_{j\in R_{e}}\mathbf{g}_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}.

(38)

For task samples, selection is sample-level, while the budget is matched by target tokens within each mini-batch. Given task scores
\{b_{i}:i\in\mathcal{B}\}
and target lengths
\{|y_{i}|:i\in\mathcal{B}\}
, define

S_{\mathcal{B}}=\operatorname{BTop}_{\tau_{\mathrm{tok}}}\{(b_{i},|y_{i}|):i\in\mathcal{B}\}.

(39)

This operator sorts samples in ascending order of
b_{i}
and keeps whole samples until

\sum_{i\in S_{\mathcal{B}}}|y_{i}|\geq\tau_{\mathrm{tok}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}}|y_{i}|.

(40)

Thus, DualSelect selects whole samples, while
\tau_{\mathrm{tok}}
controls the target-token budget up to the final retained sample.

A.4
Selective Reference Scoring versus Uniform Usage

We next compare selective reference scoring with a uniform reference distribution under the reference scoring objective. Let

\mathbf{u}=\left(\frac{1}{M},\dots,\frac{1}{M}\right)\in\Delta_{M}

(41)

denote the uniform reference distribution.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mathbf{p}^{*}
be the optimizer of Eq. (
19
);

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{M}p_{j}^{*}a_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}a_{j}+\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\bigl(\log M-\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p}^{*})\bigr)

(42)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}a_{j}.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{p}^{*}
maximizes Eq. (
19
), it matches or exceeds the feasible uniform distribution
\mathbf{u}
:

\sum_{j=1}^{M}p_{j}^{*}a_{j}+\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p}^{*})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{M}u_{j}a_{j}+\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{u}).

(43)

Using
u_{j}=1/M
and
\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{u})=\log M
gives Eq. (
42
). Since
\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{p}^{*})\leq\log M
, the additional term is nonnegative.
∎

Proposition
6
is an objective-level statement for the entropy-regularized reference-scoring surrogate. It shows soft adaptive reference weights improve, or at least match, uniform weighting on this surrogate. Since
a_{j}
combines supervised reference loss and task-reference conflict, the soft optimum emphasizes references that are poorly preserved and constraining for the current task direction. This result does not guarantee that the hard Top-
K
subset improves downstream safety over the full reference pool; this is empirically evaluated by the controlled reference-selection ablation.

A.5
Alignment of the Final Reference-Corrected Update

We analyze one corrected descent-gradient step

\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}^{*}+\rho\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{*},\qquad\rho>0.

(44)

The following result restates Proposition
1
and gives a sufficient condition for positive alignment with the selected reference direction.

Proposition 7

(Final alignment lower bound; restatement)
.

Let
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}^{*}
and
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{*}
satisfy
\|\mathbf{u}\|,\|\mathbf{v}\|>0
. Let
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}=\mathbf{u}+\rho\mathbf{v}
with
\rho>0
, and assume
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Suppose
\phi(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\geq c
for
c\in[-1,1]
, and

\rho\geq-c\,\frac{\|\mathbf{u}\|}{\|\mathbf{v}\|}.

(45)

Then

\phi(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}},\mathbf{v})\geq\frac{c\|\mathbf{u}\|+\rho\|\mathbf{v}\|}{\|\mathbf{u}\|+\rho\|\mathbf{v}\|}\geq 0,

(46)

with strict positivity whenever Eq. (
45
) holds strictly.

Proof.

By bilinearity and
\phi(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\geq c
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}},\mathbf{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle+\rho\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}

(47)

\displaystyle\geq\bigl(c\|\mathbf{u}\|+\rho\|\mathbf{v}\|\bigr)\|\mathbf{v}\|.

The triangle inequality gives

\|\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}\|\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|+\rho\|\mathbf{v}\|.

(48)

Under Eq. (
45
), the numerator lower bound is nonnegative; dividing it by the denominator upper bound yields Eq. (
46
). Strict positivity follows when Eq. (
45
) is strict.
∎

Proposition
7
gives a single-step, direction-level characterization of correction. When the raw task-reference cosine is negative, larger
\rho
is required to move the corrected descent-gradient direction into the positive half-space of the selected reference gradient. Evaluated at the current refresh checkpoint, positive alignment yields one-step first-order decrease of the selected-reference loss under a small update; between refreshes, the same geometry is interpreted with respect to the cached reference direction. This motivates the correction-strength sweep and RawCos/
+
Cos diagnostics in Appendix
D.5
.

Appendix B
Prompt-Only Reference Variant

The main experiments use safe-response references

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}=\{(x_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}},y_{j}^{\mathrm{safe}})\}
.
When safe responses are unavailable, DualSelect can instead use a prompt-only reference pool

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{prompt}}=\{x_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}\}
.
In this case, the reference loss is defined by KL preservation to the aligned initialization:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}^{\mathrm{KL}}(\theta)=\mathrm{KL}\left(p_{\theta_{0}}(\cdot|x_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}})\|p_{\theta}(\cdot|x_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}})\right),

(50)

and

\mathbf{g}_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}=\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}^{\mathrm{KL}}(\theta).

(51)

All selection scores remain unchanged after replacing

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}
with

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}^{\mathrm{KL}}
. Since this KL gradient is zero at
\theta=\theta_{0}
, the prompt-only variant activates reference scoring after a short task-only warm-up or after the first nontrivial model update.

This variant is useful when only safety-sensitive prompts are available. It preserves the aligned behavior on these prompts without explicit safe-response supervision. We therefore treat it as a robustness variant rather than the main setting.

Appendix C
When Harmful Data Are Appropriate

Harmful instruction–response pairs should not be used as positive safe-response references. Minimizing their supervised loss would train the model toward harmful behavior. They are appropriate in three different roles.

Safety-degraded reference modeling.

Harmful data can train a safety-degraded model that recognizes unsafe patterns, as in methods that compare harmful and utility-oriented reference models
18
. In this role, harmful data provide a detector or scorer, not a preservation target.

Repulsive constraints.

If harmful responses are used directly in an update objective, their gradients should enter with a repulsive sign. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{harm},j}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{|y_{j}^{\mathrm{harm}}|}

\displaystyle\sum_{t}\log p_{\theta}\bigl(y_{j,t}^{\mathrm{harm}}

(52)

\displaystyle\mid x_{j}^{\mathrm{harm}},y_{j,<t}^{\mathrm{harm}}\bigr).

A repulsive correction can use

\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final}}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new}}+\rho_{\mathrm{safe}}\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{safe}}-\rho_{\mathrm{harm}}\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{harm}},

(53)

so that the parameter update descends on safe-response loss but ascends on harmful-response likelihood.

Evaluation and diagnostics.

Harmful or red-teaming prompts are appropriate for stress testing, held-out safety probes, risk-category analysis, and training safety classifiers
11
;
38
. These uses should remain disjoint from the safe-response reference pool and final safety evaluation when needed to avoid leakage.

In this work, the main DualSelect setting uses safe-response references. Harmful data are used only for comparison, diagnostics, or optional variants, and are not treated as positive safety targets.

Pairwise win rate.

Unless stated otherwise, each method is compared with Standard SFT from the same aligned initialization. For method

\mathrm{WinRate}(m)=\frac{n_{\mathrm{win}}+0.5\,n_{\mathrm{tie}}}{n_{\mathrm{win}}+n_{\mathrm{tie}}+n_{\mathrm{loss}}}.

(54)

If ties are disallowed, this becomes the preferred-response proportion. Standard SFT obtains
0.5
, reported as
50.00
, when compared with itself.

Safety metrics.

HH and HEx-PHI are pairwise win rates on the Anthropic HH and HEx-PHI evaluation sets. HH measures helpful-harmless dialogue behavior, and HEx-PHI emphasizes harmful-instruction robustness. We report

\mathrm{SafetyAvg}=\frac{\mathrm{HH}+\mathrm{HEx\text{-}PHI}}{2},

(55)

where higher is better.

Task utility.

For REDORCA, utility is the SlimOrca pairwise win rate against Standard SFT. For GSM8K, utility is exact-match accuracy after answer extraction:

\mathrm{Acc}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{eval}}|}\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{eval}}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\operatorname{extract}(\widehat{y}(x))=y\right].

(56)

Absolute safety metrics.

Harmfulness Score is the mean harmfulness score on harmful prompts,

\mathrm{HmS}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{harm}}|}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{harm}}}h(x),

(57)

where lower is safer. With binary labels, HmS becomes the harmful-response rate. Attack Success Rate and False-Refusal Rate are

\mathrm{ASR}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{harm}}|}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{harm}}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\operatorname{Unsafe}(\widehat{y}(x))=1\right],

(58)

\mathrm{FRR}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{benign}}|}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{benign}}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\operatorname{Refusal}(\widehat{y}(x))=1\right].

(59)

Lower ASR and FRR indicate fewer unsafe completions and unnecessary refusals.

Update-geometry diagnostics.

Diagnostics are used only for mechanism analysis. RawCos is the mean pre-correction cosine between selected task and reference gradients:

\mathrm{RawCos}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\cos\left(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{new},t},\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref},t}\right).

(60)

The positive-cosine ratio, reported as
+
Cos, is

\mathrm{+Cos}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\cos\left(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{final},t},\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{ref},t}\right)>0\right].

(61)

RawCos measures pre-correction task-reference geometry, while
+
Cos measures how often the corrected update aligns with the reference direction.

Task batches and labels.

Warm-up and gradients.

We first run a short task-only warm-up from
\theta_{0}
to obtain
\theta_{\mathrm{warm}}

b

\mathbf{g}_{b}^{\mathrm{new}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_{b}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}_{b}}\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{new},i}(\theta_{\mathrm{warm}}).

(63)

For each safe-response reference
j
, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref},j}=-\frac{1}{|y_{j}^{\mathrm{safe}}|}

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{|y_{j}^{\mathrm{safe}}|}\log p_{\theta_{\mathrm{warm}}}\bigl(y_{j,t}^{\mathrm{safe}}

(64)

\displaystyle\mid x_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}},y_{j,<t}^{\mathrm{safe}}\bigr),

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}

D.7
Case Study

We provide a qualitative case study to illustrate how DualSelect differs from sample-level and token-level selection baselines.
Following prior safe fine-tuning case studies, we compare SEAL, TOSS, and DualSelect under the same held-out red-teaming prompt and decoding setting.
The prompt is disjoint from training, reference selection, validation, and diagnostic probes.
Unsafe operational details are redacted when necessary, and minor repetitions are omitted for readability.

Table
13
shows a representative safety case.
SEAL avoids explicit endorsement of illegal activity, but still gives access-oriented information by describing specialized tools and hidden-service addresses.
TOSS refuses the request, yet the response contains a residual intent-following fragment and provides only a short refusal.
DualSelect gives a more stable refusal, avoids access-enabling details, and redirects the user to benign cybersecurity and online-safety topics.
This behavior is consistent with DualSelect’s mechanism: the selected reference subset forms a task-conditioned safety direction, suppressing unsafe task updates while preserving helpful non-actionable assistance.

Appendix E
Ethical Considerations

This work studies safety preservation during LLM fine-tuning and therefore involves safety-sensitive prompts and potentially harmful model outputs. Our goal is to analyze and mitigate safety degradation caused by customization, rather than to promote or disseminate harmful content. Red-teaming prompts and qualitative examples are used solely for evaluation and illustration; when they contain access-enabling or operationally harmful details, we manually redact or replace such details to avoid spreading actionable harm. We keep training, reference selection, validation, diagnostic probes, and evaluation data disjoint to reduce leakage, and we report only aggregate statistics rather than instance-level personal information. We do not attempt to identify individuals in any dataset. Finally, improved benchmark safety should be interpreted as evidence of better safety-preserving fine-tuning under the evaluated settings, not as a guarantee of safe deployment in all real-world scenarios. AI assistants were used only for language polishing, formatting, and code debugging.
