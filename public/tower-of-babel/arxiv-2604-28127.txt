Nodal algebraic curves and entropy diagnostics in degenerate two-dimensional harmonic-oscillator shells
C A Escobar Ruiz, H Olivares-Pilon, A M Escobar-Ruiz

Abstract
Degenerate quantum eigenspaces can support substantial changes in nodal geometry at fixed energy. We show that, for the two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator, this restructuring is organized by the Hermite-constrained algebraic curve P N ​ ( x , y ) = 0 P_{N}(x,y)=0 appearing in every real shell state, ψ N = e − α r 2 / 2 P N ( x , y ) \psi_{N}=e^{-\alpha r^{2}/2}P_{N}(x,y) . Finite singularities, P N = ∇ P N = 0 P_{N}=\nabla P_{N}=0 , and projective degeneracies of the leading homogeneous part identify the strata where topology-changing events can occur. We combine these criteria with entropy diagnostics: the nodal-domain entropy S dom S_{\mathrm{dom}} , Cartesian mutual information I ⁡ ( x , y ) I(x;y) , and the entropic uncertainty sum S r + S p S_{r}+S_{p} . The first three shells reveal a hierarchy: N = 1 N=1 only rotates a nodal line; N = 2 N=2 has a conic transition at b 2 = 2 ​ a ​ c b^{2}=2ac , sharply detected by S dom S_{\mathrm{dom}} but not by global entropies; and N = 3 N=3 supports cubic close-branch regimes organized by the projective discriminant, with enhanced responses in S dom S_{\mathrm{dom}} and I ⁡ ( x , y ) I(x;y) . Thus algebraic stratification, rather than spectral ordering, organizes nodal geometry inside a degenerate eigenspace, while entropy diagnostics quantify probability redistribution and correlation. The framework suggests experimentally reconstructible signatures for real-phase Hermite–Gaussian structured light and approximately isotropic trapped motional systems.



I
Introduction

In one-dimensional quantum mechanics, the zeros of an eigenfunction partition
configuration space into nodal domains, and Sturm–Liouville theory fixes this
partition from the spectral ordering
7
. More broadly, the relation
between eigenfunctions and nodal sets goes back to the classical work of
Courant and Hilbert, where nodal partitions appear as fundamental geometric
objects associated with spectral problems
7
. If
\psi_{j}
, defined on
(a,b)
, denotes the
j
th normalized
eigenfunction in the one-dimensional Sturm–Liouville ordering,
with
j=1,2,\ldots
, then its nodal partition may be written as

(a,b)\setminus\{x:\psi_{j}(x)=0\}=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{j}I^{(j)}_{k},

(1)

where the intervals
I^{(j)}_{k}
are the connected components on
which
\psi_{j}
has definite sign. Thus, in one dimension, the
number and ordering of nodal intervals are rigidly tied to the
spectral ordering.

This rigidity admits a natural probabilistic refinement. With

\rho_{j}(x)=|\psi_{j}(x)|^{2},

(2)

the probability carried by the
k
th nodal interval is

p^{(j)}_{k}=\int_{I^{(j)}_{k}}\rho_{j}(x)\,dx,\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{j}p^{(j)}_{k}=1.

(3)

The usual configuration-space Shannon entropy is

S^{(j)}_{r}=-\int_{a}^{b}\rho_{j}(x)\ln\rho_{j}(x)\,dx,

(4)

while the corresponding nodal-domain entropy is

S^{(j)}_{\rm dom}=-\sum_{k=1}^{j}p^{(j)}_{k}\ln p^{(j)}_{k}.

(5)

These two quantities encode distinct information:

S^{(j)}_{r}
measures global spatial spread, whereas

S^{(j)}_{\rm dom}
measures how probability is distributed
across the nodal partition itself.

The one-dimensional picture is useful precisely because it fails in higher
dimensions. In two dimensions, nodal sets are generically curves, and spectral
ordering alone does not determine either their number of domains or their
topology. At most, general nodal theorems impose constraints such as Courant
bounds; they do not provide a rigid nodal classification. The freedom is even
larger inside degenerate eigenspaces, where coefficient variation can reshape
the nodal set without changing the eigenvalue
7
;
6
;
13
.
Thus, nodal geometry becomes an internal degree of freedom within a fixed energy
shell. This shifts the problem from one-dimensional nodal counting to the
geometry of nodal curves, singularities, and degenerations, themes closely
connected with the theory of real algebraic curves and singularity theory
developed by Arnol’d and others
2
;
1
.

Degenerate eigenspaces therefore provide a natural setting in which to separate
spectral information from nodal geometry. A fixed eigenvalue determines the
energy, but not a unique nodal pattern. Varying the superposition coefficients
can produce topology-changing deformations of the nodal set, redistribute
probability among nodal domains, induce coordinate-space correlations, and
alter global delocalization. The central problem is to identify the geometric
loci where nodal restructuring can occur and to quantify the accompanying
probabilistic reorganization.

We address this problem for the two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator.
At fixed shell number
N=n_{x}+n_{y}
, the energy

E_{N}=\hbar\omega(N+1)

(6)

is
(N+1)
-fold degenerate. Every real state in this shell can be written as
a Gaussian envelope multiplied by a real polynomial,

\psi_{N}(x,y)=e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})/2}P_{N}(x,y).

(7)

Since the Gaussian factor is strictly positive, the nodal set is exactly the
real algebraic curve

P_{N}(x,y)=0.

This representation turns nodal restructuring into an algebraic-geometry
problem. Finite singular candidates satisfy

P_{N}(x_{c},y_{c})=0,\qquad\nabla P_{N}(x_{c},y_{c})=0,

while additional topology changes may arise from projective or asymptotic
degeneracies of the highest-degree homogeneous component of
P_{N}
. The
Gaussian–polynomial form therefore provides the geometric backbone of the
analysis: fixed-shell nodal sets are affine real algebraic curves, and their
possible bifurcations are organized by finite and projective degeneracy
conditions
11
;
9
;
15
.

Information-theoretic diagnostics provide a complementary quantitative layer. Recent work by Schürger and Engel has shown that differential Shannon entropies and mutual information can resolve correlations in coupled quantum systems and distinguish coordinate-space from momentum-space behavior
17
;
16
. They also studied how nodal structures in two-dimensional quantum densities affect particle
correlations. These results motivate the fixed-energy question considered here: how does coefficient-driven nodal restructuring inside a degenerate
eigenspace appear in entropy and mutual-information diagnostics?

The nodal structure of harmonic-oscillator eigenstates and their superpositions has been studied from geometric, spectral, and semiclassical viewpoints
4
;
3
, while entropic and information-theoretic diagnostics have been developed independently
19
;
8
. Here we connect these directions by showing that, within a fixed degenerate oscillator shell, nodal restructuring is organized by the algebraic degeneracy strata of the Hermite-constrained polynomial
P_{N}
. The energy fixes the shell, but not the topology of the real algebraic curve
P_{N}(x,y)=0
.

The key point is that fixed oscillator shells do not realize arbitrary real algebraic curves of degree
N
. They generate a restricted Hermite hierarchy: lines for
N=1
, centered conics for
N=2
, constrained odd cubics for
N=3
, and constrained even quartics for
N=4
. The explicit low-shell families, including the cubic projective discriminant, are collected in Appendix
C
. Within this hierarchy, finite singularities and projective degeneracies identify the loci where nodal curves can reorganize.

We use this algebraic stratification to compare three diagnostic roles:
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
, which is directly sensitive to the nodal partition;

I(x;y)
, which measures correlations in the fixed Cartesian oscillator basis; and the global differential entropies
S_{r}
and
S_{r}+S_{p}
, which track spatial and position–momentum delocalization
18
;
14
.
The shell-resolved hierarchy is explicit:
N=1
permits only rotations of a nodal line,
N=2
exhibits the first topology-changing conic transition at
b^{2}=2ac
, and
N=3
supports cubic branch-approach regimes controlled by projective degeneracy. Thus, algebraic stratification, rather than spectral ordering, becomes the organizing principle for nodal geometry inside a degenerate quantum eigenspace, while entropy diagnostics refine this stratification by quantifying probability redistribution and correlation.

II
Two-Dimensional Isotropic Harmonic Oscillator

For the two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator, the fixed shell

N=n_{x}+n_{y}\ ,

(8)

has energy

E_{N}=\hbar\omega(N+1)\ ,

(9)

and dimension
N+1
. A convenient basis is the Cartesian product basis

\displaystyle\Phi_{n,N-n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{N-n}(y),

(10)

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=0,1,\ldots,N.

where
\phi_{n}
are the one-dimensional oscillator eigenfunctions.

Any real state in this shell can be written as

\psi_{N}(x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}\,\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{N-n}(y),\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}^{2}=1,

(11)

with real coefficients
c_{n}
defined up to an overall sign. This restriction to real coefficients is essential in the present
work: it ensures that the nodal set is described by the single real
algebraic equation
P_{N}(x,y)=0
. Generic complex superpositions
would require the simultaneous vanishing of the real and imaginary
parts of the wavefunction and may contain phase singularities. Using the Hermite form

\phi_{n}(x)=\mathcal{N}_{n}\,H_{n}(\sqrt{\alpha}\,x)\,e^{-\alpha x^{2}/2},\qquad\alpha=\frac{m\omega}{\hbar},

(12)

one obtains

\psi_{N}(x,y)=e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})/2}\,P_{N}(x,y),

(13)

where
P_{N}(x,y)
is a real polynomial of total degree
N
. Hence, the shell dependence enters through the polynomial factor
P_{N}
, while the Gaussian envelope is fixed.

Hidden
SU(2)
structure.

The isotropic oscillator carries a dynamical
SU(2)
symmetry within each degenerate shell. Writing

H=\hbar\omega(a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{x}+a_{y}^{\dagger}a_{y}+1)\ ,

and introducing the Schwinger generators

J_{+}=a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{y},\qquad J_{-}=a_{y}^{\dagger}a_{x},\qquad J_{z}=\tfrac{1}{2}(a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{x}-a_{y}^{\dagger}a_{y}),

with
J_{x}=(J_{+}+J_{-})/2
and
J_{y}=(J_{+}-J_{-})/(2i)
, one has
[H,J_{i}]=0
, and the operators
J_{i}
satisfy the
su(2)
commutation relations. The shell
N
therefore realizes the spin-
j=N/2
irreducible representation of
SU(2)
, with the product basis
\{\Phi_{n_{x},n_{y}}\}
identified with
\{|j,m\rangle\}
through

m=\frac{n_{x}-n_{y}}{2}.

From this viewpoint, varying the coefficients
\{c_{n}\}
amounts to moving within an
SU(2)
multiplet, which provides a symmetry interpretation of the one-parameter families considered below.

III
Fixed-shell geometry and information measures

III.1
Nodal set and degeneracy criteria

Since the Gaussian envelope in Eq. (
13
) is strictly positive, the zeros of
\psi_{N}
coincide with those of the polynomial factor:

P_{N}(x,y)=0.

(14)

For generic coefficients
\{c_{n}\}
, this defines a real algebraic curve of degree at most
N
. Qualitative changes in the nodal geometry can occur when the curve develops a
finite singular point,

P_{N}(x_{c},y_{c})=0,\qquad\nabla P_{N}(x_{c},y_{c})=\mathbf{0},

(15)

or when the asymptotic nodal directions of the highest-degree homogeneous
part become non-simple.

III.2
Configuration-space information measures

In two dimensions, the normalized spatial density is given by

\rho(x,y)=\frac{|\psi_{N}(x,y)|^{2}}{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi_{N}(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy}.

(16)

To quantify coefficient-driven restructuring, we use four closely related information measures, grouped into three diagnostic roles. We shall use differential entropies with a fixed oscillator
reference scale. Equivalently, one may work with the dimensionless
variables

X=\sqrt{\alpha}\,x,\quad Y=\sqrt{\alpha}\,y,\quad\Pi_{x}=\frac{p_{x}}{\sqrt{m\hbar\omega}},\quad\Pi_{y}=\frac{p_{y}}{\sqrt{m\hbar\omega}}.

With this convention the logarithms appearing in the differential
entropies are dimensionless. Additive constants associated with
the choice of length or momentum units are therefore fixed once
and for all. Consequently, the coefficient dependence of

S_{r}
,
S_{p}
, and
S_{r}+S_{p}
is unambiguous, and all numerical
comparisons below are performed in dimensionless oscillator units.

1.

Configuration-space Shannon entropy
S_{r}

S_{r}=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(x,y)\ln\rho(x,y)\,dx\,dy.

(17)

2.

The Cartesian mutual information given by

I(x;y)=S_{x}+S_{y}-S_{r}\geq 0,

(18)

is defined in terms of
S_{x}
and
S_{y}
,

\displaystyle S_{x}

\displaystyle=-\int\rho_{x}\ln\rho_{x}\,dx,

(19)

\displaystyle S_{y}

\displaystyle=-\int\rho_{y}\ln\rho_{y}\,dy,

where the marginal distributions are

\displaystyle\rho_{x}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho(x,y)\,dy,

(20)

\displaystyle\rho_{y}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho(x,y)\,dx,

respectively. For a single particle in two dimensions,
I(x;y)
is not an
entanglement measure between two particles. Rather, it quantifies
statistical correlations between the two Cartesian degrees of freedom
in the position representation. In the oscillator basis, these correlations are induced by coherent superpositions inside the degenerate shell. Therefore,
I(x;y)
provides a basis-dependent, but physically transparent, measure of coordinate nonseparability within the fixed Cartesian oscillator representation.

3.

Nodal-domain entropy
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
.
Let
\{\Omega_{k}\}
be the connected components of

\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(x,y):\psi_{N}(x,y)=0\},

(21)

that is, the maximal connected open sets on which
\psi_{N}
has definite sign. Their probability weights are

p_{k}=\int_{\Omega_{k}}\rho(x,y)\,dx\,dy,\qquad\sum_{k}p_{k}=1,

(22)

and the associated entropy is

S_{\mathrm{dom}}=-\sum_{k}p_{k}\ln p_{k}.

(23)

Unlike
S_{r}
, this quantity is tied directly to the nodal partition, since a change in nodal topology reorganizes the set
\{p_{k}\}
itself. The integrals in Eq. (
22
) converge for all fixed-shell states because
\rho
has Gaussian decay.

4.

Entropic uncertainty sum
S_{r}+S_{p}
.
Given the momentum-space wavefunction
\tilde{\psi}_{N}(p_{x},p_{y})
, the normalized momentum density

\tilde{\rho}(p_{x},p_{y})
is

\tilde{\rho}(p_{x},p_{y})=\frac{|\tilde{\psi}_{N}(p_{x},p_{y})|^{2}}{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\tilde{\psi}_{N}(p_{x},p_{y})|^{2}\,dp_{x}\,dp_{y}},

(24)

and the corresponding momentum-space Shannon entropy takes the form

S_{p}=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{\rho}(p_{x},p_{y})\ln\tilde{\rho}(p_{x},p_{y})\,dp_{x}\,dp_{y}.

(25)

The entropic uncertainty sum

S_{r}+S_{p},

(26)

measures overall delocalization across configuration and momentum space.

Taken together, these diagnostics separate different aspects of restructuring:
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
resolves the nodal partition itself,
I(x;y)
measures coordinate-space correlations, and
S_{r}+S_{p}
tracks global spread.

IV
Regular one-parameter families: nodal stability and entropy regularity

The shell-by-shell examples below display two qualitatively distinct regimes.
Away from singular parameter values, the nodal curve deforms smoothly, its
topology is unchanged, and the entropy diagnostics vary regularly. Topology-changing nodal restructuring can occur only when this regularity fails: either the polynomial develops a finite singular point, or its highest-degree homogeneous part develops a non-simple real direction at infinity. We first isolate the regular regime for an arbitrary fixed oscillator shell.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval, and let

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}(t)\,\Phi_{n,N-n}(x,y),

(27)

\displaystyle c_{n}

\displaystyle\in C^{1}(J;\mathbb{R}),\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}(t)^{2}=1,

be a
C^{1}
family of normalized real states in the fixed
N
shell of the
two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator. Writing

\psi_{t}(x,y)=e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})/2}P_{t}(x,y),

(28)

let
P_{t}^{(N)}
denote the homogeneous degree-
N
part of
P_{t}
, and define

Z_{t}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:P_{t}(x,y)=0\},\qquad\rho_{t}=|\psi_{t}|^{2}.

(29)

Since the coefficients in (
27
) are normalized and
the shell basis is orthonormal,
\rho_{t}
is a normalized probability density.

Assume that, for every
t\in J
, the following two regularity conditions hold:

(i)

the affine singularity equations

P_{t}(x,y)=0,\qquad\nabla P_{t}(x,y)=0

(30)

have no common real solution;

(ii)

the angular function

f_{t}(\theta):=P_{t}^{(N)}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)

(31)

has only simple real zeros.

Condition (i) says that the affine curve
P_{t}=0
is nonsingular in

\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Condition (ii) is the corresponding transversality condition at
infinity: it prevents the asymptotic nodal directions of the highest-degree
part from merging or becoming tangent. Together, these assumptions exclude
both finite and projective degeneracies of the nodal curve.

Under assumptions

(32)

(33)

(34)

where

(b)

V
The
N=1
shell: linear nodal geometry and coefficient-independent domain entropy

V.1
Wavefunction and fixed-shell polynomial

The
N=1
shell is spanned by
\Phi_{10}=\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{0}(y)
and

\Phi_{01}=\phi_{0}(x)\phi_{1}(y)
. A general real state is

\psi_{1}(x,y)=a\,\Phi_{10}+b\,\Phi_{01},\qquad a^{2}+b^{2}=1.

(39)

Using

\phi_{0}(x)=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}e^{-\alpha x^{2}/2},\qquad\phi_{1}(x)=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}\sqrt{2\alpha}\,x\,e^{-\alpha x^{2}/2},

(40)

one finds the Gaussian–polynomial form
\psi_{1}=e^{-\alpha r^{2}/2}P_{1}(x,y)
, where

P_{1}(x,y)=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}\sqrt{2\alpha}\,(ax+by),

(41)

so the polynomial factor is linear.

V.2
Nodal set and absence of bifurcation

Since the Gaussian envelope is strictly positive, the nodal set is determined
by

ax+by=0.

(42)

Thus every nontrivial
N=1
state has a single straight nodal line through
the origin. Up to an irrelevant nonzero factor,
P_{1}\propto ax+by
, so

\nabla P_{1}\propto(a,b)
. Since
a^{2}+b^{2}=1
, the gradient never vanishes,
and the conditions

P_{1}=0,\qquad\nabla P_{1}=\mathbf{0}

(43)

cannot be satisfied. Hence the
N=1
shell contains no nodal bifurcation;
coefficient variation only rotates the line.

V.3
Exact density and normalization

The normalized density is

\rho_{1}(x,y)=|\psi_{1}(x,y)|^{2}=\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\pi}(ax+by)^{2}e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})}.

(44)

Introducing rotated coordinates

u=ax+by,\qquad v=-bx+ay,

(45)

gives

\rho_{1}(u,v)=\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\pi}u^{2}e^{-\alpha(u^{2}+v^{2})},

(46)

showing that all dependence on
(a,b)
amounts to a rigid rotation of a single
universal profile.

V.4
Shannon entropy and entropic sum

The configuration-space Shannon entropy

S_{r}=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho_{1}\ln\rho_{1}\,dx\,dy

(47)

is rotation-invariant and therefore independent of
(a,b)
. Direct evaluation
gives

S_{r}=\ln\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}\right)+\gamma,

(48)

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant.

In momentum space, the Fourier transform of
Eq. (
39
) has the same fixed-shell structure. Writing

p_{0}=\sqrt{m\hbar\omega}
, the normalized momentum density is

\rho_{p}(p_{x},p_{y})=\frac{2}{\pi p_{0}^{4}}(ap_{x}+bp_{y})^{2}e^{-(p_{x}^{2}+p_{y}^{2})/p_{0}^{2}},

(49)

which is again reduced by the rotation (
45
) to a
coefficient-independent form. Hence

S_{p}=-\iint\rho_{p}\ln\rho_{p}\,dp_{x}\,dp_{y}=\ln(2\pi p_{0}^{2})+\gamma.

(50)

Therefore

S_{r}+S_{p}=2\gamma+2\ln(2\pi\hbar),

(51)

or, in the dimensionless convention
\hbar=1
,

S_{r}+S_{p}=2\gamma+2\ln(2\pi).

(52)

Along the path
a=\sqrt{1-t^{2}}
,
b=t
, this sum is constant: varying the
coefficients only rotates the state.

V.5
Nodal-domain entropy

The line
ax+by=0
divides the plane into two nodal domains. In the coordinates
(
45
), these are
u>0
and
u<0
. Because

\rho_{1}(u,v)\propto u^{2}e^{-\alpha(u^{2}+v^{2})}
is even in

weight
1/2

p_{+}=\int_{u>0}\rho_{1}\,du\,dv=\frac{1}{2},\qquad p_{-}=\int_{u<0}\rho_{1}\,du\,dv=\frac{1}{2}.

(53)

Thus

S_{\mathrm{dom},\,N=1}=-\sum_{k=\pm}p_{k}\ln p_{k}=\ln 2,

(54)

independent of
(a,b)
.

V.6
Mutual information and axis dependence

The mutual information depends on the choice of coordinates. In the rotated

(u,v)
, the density factorizes and

; in the fixed Cartesian

For later numerical use, the marginals are

\displaystyle\rho_{1,x}(x)

Hence

\phi_{2}(x)=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(2\alpha x^{2}-1)\,e^{-\alpha x^{2}/2},\\

(60)

the expression for
\psi_{2}(x,y)
is

\psi_{2}(x,y)=e^{-\alpha r^{2}/2}\,P_{2}(x,y),\qquad r^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2},

(61)

where

P_{2}(x,y)=\sqrt{2}\,\alpha\,a\,x^{2}+2\alpha\,b\,xy+\sqrt{2}\,\alpha\,c\,y^{2}-\frac{a+c}{\sqrt{2}}.

(62)

Up to an overall nonzero factor, this polynomial contains the information
relevant to both the nodal curve and the normalized density.

VI.2
Nodal set, conic degeneracies, and bifurcation surface

The nodal curve is the conic

P_{2}(x,y)=0,

(63)

with

P_{2}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix}Q\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}+D,\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}A&B/2\\
B/2&C\end{pmatrix},

(64)

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\alpha\,a,\qquad B=2\alpha\,b,\qquad C=\sqrt{2}\,\alpha\,c,

(65)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=-\frac{a+c}{\sqrt{2}}=-\frac{A+C}{2\alpha}.

This representation separates finite affine singularities from projective
degeneracies of the conic.

A finite singular point must satisfy

P_{2}(x_{c},y_{c})=0,\qquad\nabla P_{2}(x_{c},y_{c})=\mathbf{0}.

(66)

Since

\nabla P_{2}(\mathbf{x})=2Q\mathbf{x},\qquad\mathbf{x}=\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix},

(67)

a nonsingular
Q
leaves only the critical point
\mathbf{x}=0
. Hence the
finite affine singular condition is

D=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad a+c=0.

(68)

Generically, this gives two intersecting lines through the origin.

Independently, the quadratic part may lose rank:

\det Q=AC-\frac{B^{2}}{4}=\alpha^{2}(2ac-b^{2}).

(69)

Thus the rank-degeneracy condition is

b^{2}=2ac.

(70)

When
D\neq 0
, this is not a finite singularity; it gives a projectively
degenerate conic, realized in the affine plane as two parallel lines. Indeed,
after homogenization,

F(X,Y,Z)=AX^{2}+BXY+CY^{2}+DZ^{2},

the projective degeneracy condition is

D\det Q=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad(a+c)(2ac-b^{2})=0.

(71)

The two factors correspond respectively to finite crossing-line degeneracies
and rank-degenerate parallel-line degeneracies.

Accordingly, the sign of
\det Q
distinguishes ellipse-type

(\det Q>0)
, hyperbola-type
(\det Q<0)
, and rank-degenerate

(\det Q=0)
conics, as shown in Figure
2
. To probe
the transition across Eq. (
70
), we use

a=c=\sqrt{\frac{1-t^{2}}{2}},\qquad b=t,\qquad t\in[0,1].

(72)

Along this path,
\det Q=0
occurs at
t^{2}=1/2
. The nodal geometry is
(see Figure
2
):

•

t^{2}<1/2
: closed conic, two nodal domains;

•

t^{2}=1/2
: two parallel lines, three nodal domains;

•

t^{2}>1/2
: hyperbola-type curve, three nodal domains;

•

t=1
:
P_{2}\propto xy
, the coordinate cross, four nodal domains.

Thus
t=1/\sqrt{2}
is the rank-degenerate conic transition, whereas
t=1

is the finite affine singularity.

VI.3
Exact normalization of the density

Orthogonality of the shell basis, equivalently direct Gaussian-moment
evaluation, gives

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\alpha r^{2}}\,P_{2}(x,y)^{2}\,dx\,dy=\frac{\pi}{\alpha}.

(73)

Therefore

\rho_{2}(x,y)=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})}\,P_{2}(x,y)^{2}.

(74)

VI.4
Shannon entropy and smoothness

From Eq. (
74
),

S_{r}=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho_{2}(x,y)\ln\rho_{2}(x,y)\,dx\,dy,

(75)

\ln\rho_{2}(x,y)
can be written as

\displaystyle\ln\rho_{2}=\ln\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)-\alpha r^{2}+2\ln|P_{2}|,

(76)

therefore

S_{r}=-\ln\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)+\alpha\langle r^{2}\rangle-2\langle\ln|P_{2}|\rangle,

(77)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes expectation with respect to
\rho_{2}
.
The quadratic moment
\alpha\langle r^{2}\rangle
is fixed by the shell index.
From the virial theorem
\langle T\rangle=\langle V\rangle=E_{N}/2
with

E_{N}
from (
9
), and
for any state in the
N
th shell,

\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}\langle r^{2}\rangle=\frac{E_{N}}{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\alpha\,\langle r^{2}\rangle=N+1.

(78)

For
N=2
,

\alpha\,\langle r^{2}\rangle=3,

(79)

and all coefficient dependence of
S_{r}
is therefore carried by
the last term
\langle\ln|P_{2}|\rangle
.

Smoothness across the rank-degenerate conic transition
\det Q=0
.

Although the nodal curve changes topology at
\det Q=0
, the entropy
S_{r}

does not develop a singularity there. The zero set has measure zero in

\mathbb{R}^{2}
; near a regular zero one has
P_{2}\sim\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{r}
,
while at an isolated critical point
P_{2}\sim\mathbf{r}^{T}H\mathbf{r}
. Thus

\ln|P_{2}|
has only an integrable logarithmic singularity against the
Gaussian-weighted density, so
\langle\ln|P_{2}|\rangle
remains finite and
continuous throughout coefficient space.

Analytic checkpoints.

Two special points are useful for benchmarks.

(i) Product state
\Phi_{11}
. At the endpoint
t=1
of the symmetric
path (
72
), the state is proportional to

\Phi_{11}=\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{1}(y)
, so the density factorizes:

\rho_{1}(x)=|\phi_{1}(x)|^{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\pi}}\,(2\alpha x^{2})e^{-\alpha x^{2}}.

(80)

Hence
S_{r}=2S_{1\mathrm{D},n=1}
, with

S_{1\mathrm{D},n=1}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\right)+\gamma+\ln 2-\frac{1}{2},

(81)

so

S_{r}\big|_{\Phi_{11}}=\ln\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\right)+2\gamma+2\ln 2-1.

(82)

This is consistent with
I(x;y)=0
.

(ii) Quadratic-moment identity.

Equation (
78
) supplies a check for all coefficient
choices: any numerical evaluation of
S_{r}
in the
N=2
shell must reproduce

\alpha\langle r^{2}\rangle=3
.

Thus the conic degeneracy governs the nodal transition along this path, while
the global Shannon entropy remains a smooth function of the coefficients.

VI.5
Entropic sum

For fixed-shell oscillator states, the two-dimensional Fourier transform acts
diagonally on the Cartesian basis, and every basis vector in the
N=2
shell
acquires the same phase factor
(-i)^{2}=-1
. Consequently, the momentum-space wave

\tilde{\psi}_{2}(p_{x},p_{y})

\tilde{\psi}_{2}(p_{x},p_{y})=-\frac{1}{m\omega}\,\psi_{2}\!\left(\frac{p_{x}}{m\omega},\frac{p_{y}}{m\omega}\right).

(83)

Thus

\tilde{\rho}_{2}(p_{x},p_{y})=\frac{1}{(m\omega)^{2}}\,\rho_{2}\!\left(\frac{p_{x}}{m\omega},\frac{p_{y}}{m\omega}\right),

(84)

and a change of variables yields

S_{p}=S_{r}+2\ln(m\omega).

(85)

Hence

S_{r}+S_{p}=2S_{r}+2\ln(m\omega).

(86)

The entropic sum therefore inherits the same regularity as
S_{r}
and remains
nonsingular across
\det Q=0
(see Figure
3
).
In the dimensionless convention

m=\omega=\hbar=1
, one has simply
S_{p}=S_{r}
and
S_{r}+S_{p}=2S_{r}
.

VII
The
N=3
shell: cubic nodal curves and enhanced restructuring

The
N=3
shell is fourfold degenerate, with Cartesian basis

\Phi_{30},\;\Phi_{21},\;\Phi_{12},\;\Phi_{03}.

A general real state is

\displaystyle\psi_{3}(x,y)=a\,\Phi_{30}(x,y)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle b\,\Phi_{21}(x,y)

(91)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c\,\Phi_{12}(x,y)+d\,\Phi_{03}(x,y),

with

a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}=1.

Using the Hermite representation of the one-dimensional oscillator eigenfunctions, one again obtains the Gaussian–polynomial form

\psi_{3}(x,y)=e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})/2}P_{3}(x,y),

where
P_{3}(x,y)
is a Hermite-constrained real cubic,

P_{3}(x,y)=A\,x^{3}+B\,x^{2}y+C\,xy^{2}+D\,y^{3}+E\,x+F\,y.

Here the coefficients
(A,\ldots,F)
are linear functions of the shell amplitudes
(a,b,c,d)
and depend on
\alpha
. In particular, the lower-order coefficients
E
and
F
are not independent of the cubic part; they arise from the lower-order terms in the Hermite polynomials.

The nodal set is therefore the real cubic curve

P_{3}(x,y)=0.

Unlike the linear and quadratic shells, the cubic shell admits a richer geometry of branches and asymptotic directions. Finite-affine restructuring is detected by the singularity condition

P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c})=0,\qquad\nabla P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c})=0.

Equivalently, the point
(x_{c},y_{c})
must satisfy

\begin{cases}Ax^{3}+Bx^{2}y+Cxy^{2}+Dy^{3}+Ex+Fy=0,\\[4.0pt]
3Ax^{2}+2Bxy+Cy^{2}+E=0,\\[4.0pt]
Bx^{2}+2Cxy+3Dy^{2}+F=0.\end{cases}

(92)

Eliminating
(x,y)
gives the finite singularity locus in coefficient space,

\Delta^{\mathrm{fin}}_{3}(A,B,C,D,E,F)=0.

(93)

This locus contains the parameter values at which cusp formation, self-intersection, or tangential finite-branch contact can occur.

Finite singularities, however, do not exhaust the relevant restructuring mechanisms. The homogeneous degree-three part of
P_{3}
controls the asymptotic directions of the cubic. Repeated real asymptotic directions define a projective, or asymptotic, degeneracy and can reorganize the arrangement of unbounded branches without producing a finite affine singularity. Thus the cubic shell naturally separates finite-affine degeneracies from projective ones.

The same organizing principle as for
N=2
therefore persists, but
with a richer geometric repertoire. The nodal-domain entropy
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
is expected to respond most directly to changes in
the nodal partition,
I(x;y)
tracks the induced Cartesian correlations, and the global differential entropies remain less
sensitive because the nodal set has zero measure with respect to the Gaussian-weighted density.

VII.1
Shannon entropy and entropic sum

A closed form for
S_{r}
is not available throughout the full

N=3
coefficient space, but the regularity mechanism is the same as
in the quadratic shell. The nodal set
\{P_{3}=0\}
has measure zero
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Near a regular zero,
P_{3}
vanishes linearly,
so
\ln|P_{3}|
is locally integrable against the Gaussian-weighted
density. At an isolated finite singularity, the logarithmic singularity
is still integrable. Thus the configuration-space entropy

S_{r}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho_{3}(x,y)\ln\rho_{3}(x,y)\,dx\,dy

remains finite across the cubic families considered below.

What changes across degeneracy strata is not the integrability of

S_{r}
, but the nodal partition itself. Consequently,
S_{r}
and the
entropic uncertainty sum are expected to be much less sensitive to
finite-affine or projective restructuring than
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
.
Possible nonsmooth dependence of
S_{r}
on the coefficients can occur
only when the algebraic degeneracy conditions are met, but the global
entropy need not display a sharp signature of the nodal transition.

The momentum-space structure is fixed by the shell index. Since every
Cartesian basis state in the
N=3
shell acquires the same Fourier
phase
(-i)^{3}=i
, the momentum-space wavefunction satisfies

\widetilde{\psi}_{3}(p_{x},p_{y})=\frac{i}{m\omega}\,\psi_{3}\!\left(\frac{p_{x}}{m\omega},\frac{p_{y}}{m\omega}\right).

Therefore

\widetilde{\rho}_{3}(p_{x},p_{y})=\frac{1}{(m\omega)^{2}}\,\rho_{3}\!\left(\frac{p_{x}}{m\omega},\frac{p_{y}}{m\omega}\right),

and a change of variables gives

S_{p}=S_{r}+2\ln(m\omega).

Hence

S_{r}+S_{p}=2S_{r}+2\ln(m\omega).

In dimensionless oscillator variables,
m=\omega=\hbar=1
, this
reduces to

S_{r}+S_{p}=2S_{r}.

Thus, as in the
N=2
shell, the entropic uncertainty sum tracks the
global differential entropy rather than the detailed topology of the
nodal partition.

VII.2
Nodal-domain entropy and mutual information: explicit
N=3
results

To probe a representative family of cubic deformations, we consider

\psi_{3}(x,y)=a\,\Phi_{30}(x,y)+b\,\Phi_{21}(x,y)+c\,\Phi_{12}(x,y),

(94)

with
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=1
, parametrized by

a=c=\sqrt{\frac{1-t^{2}}{2}},\qquad b=t,\qquad t\in[0,1].

(95)

This path interpolates between a nontrivial cubic configuration at
t=0
and the
pure
\Phi_{21}
state at
t=1
. Because
\Phi_{03}
is absent, it is not
generally symmetric under

and should be viewed as a
structured deformation inside a restricted three-state subspace.

Along this path the nodal domains evolve as a function of
t
as
follows (see Figure
6
):

•

at
t=0
, the polynomial factorizes into the product of the line

x=0
and an ellipse, as written explicitly below;

•

for small nonzero
t

branch and the plane splits into two nodal domains;

•

near the projective-discriminant regime, distinct smooth branches approach

•

•

at
t=1

so the nodal set consists of the horizontal line
y=0
together with the two
vertical lines

Along (
95

\displaystyle P_{3}(x,y)

\displaystyle=A_{1}(t)\,x^{3}+A_{2}(t)\,x^{2}y+A_{3}(t)\,xy^{2}

(96)

\displaystyle+B_{1}(t)\,x+B_{2}(t)\,y.

where
A_{1}(t),A_{2}(t),A_{3}(t),B_{1}(t)

are smooth functions of
t

and
\alpha

Finite-affine singular events along this path are detected by

P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c};t)=0,\qquad\nabla P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c};t)=0.

(97)

Unlike the symmetric conic case, the candidate singular

97

the cubic coefficients

and the lower-order Hermite coefficients

Appendix
D.1
. Here

. Along the interior branch

C.1
. For the path (
95

To extend the low-shell analysis without introducing the full

(N+1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}\{\Phi_{n,N-n}:n=0,1,\dots,N\},

\displaystyle\psi_{N}^{(t)}

\displaystyle 0\leq t\leq 1.

(N+1)

\psi_{N}^{(t)}(x,y)=\mathcal{N}_{N}e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})/2}Q_{N}^{(t)}(\xi,\eta),

\displaystyle Q_{N}^{(t)}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\bigl[H_{N}(\xi)+H_{N}(\eta)\bigr]

(100)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t\sqrt{\binom{N}{n_{-}}}\,H_{n_{+}}(\xi)H_{n_{-}}(\eta).

The nodal curve is therefore given by

Q_{N}^{(t)}(\xi,\eta)=0.

(101)

At the endpoint

\psi_{N}^{(1)}(x,y)=\Phi_{n_{+},n_{-}}(x,y)=\phi_{n_{+}}(x)\phi_{n_{-}}(y),

(102)

so the nodal set becomes a rectangular grid of Hermite zeros and the plane is
partitioned into

\displaystyle\nu_{N}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{+}+1)(n_{-}+1)

\displaystyle=

IX
Physical implementation

The fixed-shell structure analyzed here has direct realizations in structured light and trapped-particle motion. In paraxial optics, Hermite–Gaussian modes are transverse oscillator eigenmodes; coherent superpositions with fixed order

N=n_{x}+n_{y}
and real relative phases at a fixed propagation plane
therefore have the real Gaussian–polynomial form

u_{N}(x,y)=e^{-r^{2}/w^{2}}P_{N}(x,y),

(113)

up to an overall scale and phase. In this real-field setting, the
zero-amplitude curves, and hence the corresponding dark intensity
curves, are precisely determined by

P_{N}(x,y)=0.

(114)

Recent structured-light platforms allow programmable control of
transverse amplitude and phase, including higher-order free-space
modes, making coefficient-space paths of the type studied here
experimentally accessible
10
;
5
.
In this setting,
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
is obtained by integrating the measured intensity over connected bright domains,
I(x;y)
follows from the transverse intensity marginals, and
S_{p}
is accessed from the far-field distribution.

A complementary AMO realization is a single atom or ion in an approximately isotropic two-dimensional harmonic trap. The states
\Phi_{n,N-n}
form a degenerate motional shell when the transverse frequencies are equal, and coherent control of two-dimensional motional
states has been demonstrated in trapped ions
12
. Thus the finite and projective degeneracy strata discussed above correspond to
tunable regions of modal-coefficient space where nodal domains, probability weights, and coordinate correlations reorganize at fixed energy or fixed transverse order.

X
Conclusion

We have developed a geometric and information-theoretic framework for nodal
restructuring in degenerate shells of the two-dimensional isotropic harmonic
oscillator. Unlike the one-dimensional Sturm–Liouville setting, where spectral
order fixes the nodal partition, a degenerate two-dimensional shell admits
coefficient-driven changes of nodal geometry at fixed energy. The
Gaussian–polynomial representation

\psi_{N}(x,y)=e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})/2}P_{N}(x,y)

makes this structure explicit: the nodal set is the real algebraic curve

P_{N}(x,y)=0
. Finite singularities and projective/asymptotic degeneracies of
the leading homogeneous part therefore identify possible topology-changing
events, while
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
,
I(x;y)
,
S_{r}
, and
S_{r}+S_{p}

quantify the associated redistribution of probability, correlation, and global
delocalization.

The regularity theorem establishes the off-bifurcation regime. Away from finite
singularities and non-simple asymptotic directions, the nodal set moves by
ambient isotopy, the number of nodal domains is unchanged, the nodal weights
vary smoothly, and
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
is smooth, while
S_{r}
and

S_{r}+S_{p}
remain continuous. The shell-by-shell analysis shows how this
regular behavior fails at algebraic degeneracy strata. For
N=1
, coefficient
variation only rotates a single nodal line; hence
S_{\mathrm{dom}}=\ln 2

and
S_{r}+S_{p}=2\gamma+2\ln(2\pi\hbar)
remain constant, while nonzero

I(x;y)
reflects axis misalignment. For
N=2
, the symmetric path exhibits
the first topology-changing transition through the rank-degenerate conic
condition
b^{2}=2ac
, where
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
responds sharply although
the global entropies remain smooth. For
N=3
, Hermite-constrained cubic
curves display richer close-branch approaches organized by the projective
discriminant; these are accompanied by larger variations in

S_{\mathrm{dom}}
and
I(x;y)
, while
S_{r}
and
S_{r}+S_{p}
remain
regular.

These results show that finite and projective algebraic degeneracies organize
nodal-topology change in degenerate oscillator shells. The nodal-domain entropy
is the most direct probe of probability redistribution across nodal domains,
the mutual information captures induced Cartesian correlations, and the
entropic uncertainty sum provides a complementary measure of global
delocalization. In this sense, the construction gives a fixed-energy analogue
of the Sturm–Liouville nodal picture: degeneracy strata replace spectral order
as the organizing principle, and entropy diagnostics refine the resulting
nodal stratification.

Several extensions follow naturally. For the hydrogen atom, a fixed principal
shell has
n^{2}
-fold spatial degeneracy and real superpositions of the form

\psi_{n}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}c_{\ell m}R_{n\ell}(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi).

Although the nodal factor is no longer a single Cartesian polynomial times a
Gaussian, the same organizing principle should apply: fixed-energy
superpositions generate nodal surfaces, singularity structure organizes
possible topology changes, and entropy diagnostics quantify the resulting
probability redistribution.

The diagnostics are also experimentally reconstructible in
principle. In optical Hermite–Gaussian realizations with real
relative phases, the transverse field profile at a fixed propagation
plane may be modeled by the same real Gaussian–polynomial form
used here. Transverse intensity images then provide

|\psi(x,y)|^{2}
, while the nodal set is obtained from the dark
curves of the real field profile. From these data one can estimate
the nodal-domain weights

p_{k}=\int_{\Omega_{k}}\rho(x,y)\,dx\,dy,\qquad S_{\rm dom}=-\sum_{k}p_{k}\ln p_{k}.

(115)

The mutual information follows from the measured Cartesian
marginals, whereas momentum-space distributions, accessible for
example through far-field optical measurements or time-of-flight
methods in cold-atom realizations, give
S_{p}
and hence

S_{r}+S_{p}
. A sharp response of
S_{\rm dom}
together with smooth
global entropies would therefore provide an experimentally
reconstructible signature of nodal restructuring.

Future work should characterize higher-shell discriminants more systematically,
relate singularity type to the behavior of
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
and
I(x;y)
,
and extend the framework to complex superpositions, where vortices and phase
singularities enter. More broadly, the same approach should apply to
anisotropic and higher-dimensional oscillators, degenerate manifolds with
nontrivial dynamical symmetry, and semiclassical regimes where the leading
homogeneous component controls the global nodal pattern.

Appendix C
Low-shell nodal curves for
N=1,2,3,4

This appendix records the algebraic curve classes arising in the first few
fixed oscillator shells. The aim is not to classify all real curves of a given
degree, but to identify the restricted curve families generated by
fixed-shell oscillator superpositions.

We use dimensionless variables

\xi=\sqrt{\alpha}\,x,\qquad\eta=\sqrt{\alpha}\,y,

and write a general real state in shell
N
as

\psi_{N}(x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}\Phi_{n,N-n}(x,y),\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}^{2}=1.

Since

\phi_{n}(x)\propto H_{n}(\xi)e^{-\xi^{2}/2},

the nodal set is determined, up to an irrelevant nonzero factor, by the
dimensionless polynomial

Q_{N}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}\sqrt{\binom{N}{n}}\,H_{n}(\xi)H_{N-n}(\eta).

Thus the physical nodal set is equivalent, under the rescaling

(x,y)\mapsto(\xi,\eta)
, to

\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{N}=\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:Q_{N}(\xi,\eta)=0\}.

The parity of the Hermite polynomials gives

Q_{N}(-\xi,-\eta)=(-1)^{N}Q_{N}(\xi,\eta).

Hence odd shells produce odd algebraic curves, while even shells produce even
ones. This parity restriction is one reason fixed-shell nodal curves form
proper subfamilies of all real algebraic curves of degree
N
.

Finite singular points satisfy

Q_{N}(\xi_{c},\eta_{c})=0,\qquad\partial_{\xi}Q_{N}(\xi_{c},\eta_{c})=0,\qquad\partial_{\eta}Q_{N}(\xi_{c},\eta_{c})=0.

Asymptotic degeneracies are controlled instead by the highest-degree
homogeneous part. If

Q_{N,\mathrm{top}}(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)=r^{N}f_{N}(\theta),

then repeated asymptotic directions occur when

f_{N}(\theta)=0,\qquad f_{N}^{\prime}(\theta)=0.

This separates finite affine singularities from degeneracies associated with
the line at infinity.

N=1
: linear nodal curves

For
N=1
,

Q_{1}(\xi,\eta)=2(c_{1}\xi+c_{0}\eta).

Thus the nodal curve is the line

c_{1}\xi+c_{0}\eta=0.

Since

\nabla Q_{1}=(2c_{1},2c_{0})

is nonzero for every normalized state, the conditions

Q_{1}=0,\qquad\nabla Q_{1}=0

cannot be satisfied. The
N=1
shell therefore has no nodal bifurcation:
coefficient variation only rotates the nodal line.

N=2
: centered conics

For
N=2
, an equivalent polynomial is

Q_{2}(\xi,\eta)=\sqrt{2}\,c_{2}\xi^{2}+2c_{1}\xi\eta+\sqrt{2}\,c_{0}\eta^{2}-\frac{c_{0}+c_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}.

With
c_{2}=a
,
c_{1}=b
, and
c_{0}=c
, this is the dimensionless form of
the quadratic polynomial used in the main text. It can be written as

Q_{2}(\xi,\eta)=\begin{pmatrix}\xi&\eta\end{pmatrix}M\begin{pmatrix}\xi\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}+D,

where

M=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}\,c_{2}&c_{1}\\
c_{1}&\sqrt{2}\,c_{0}\end{pmatrix},\qquad D=-\frac{c_{0}+c_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},

and

\det M=2c_{0}c_{2}-c_{1}^{2}.

The finite affine singular condition is

D=0,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad c_{0}+c_{2}=0.

In this case the conic is homogeneous and generically factors into two
intersecting lines through the origin.

A separate degeneracy occurs when the quadratic part loses rank:

\det M=0,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad c_{1}^{2}=2c_{0}c_{2}.

When
D\neq 0
, this gives a projectively degenerate conic, realized in the
affine plane as two parallel lines. Along the symmetric path

c_{2}=c_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{1-t^{2}}{2}},\qquad c_{1}=t,

this occurs at

t=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},

where the closed conic passes through a parallel-line configuration before
becoming hyperbola-type.

Equivalently, the projective conic discriminant factors as

D\,\det M=0,

or

(c_{0}+c_{2})(2c_{0}c_{2}-c_{1}^{2})=0.

The first factor gives finite affine singularities; the second gives the
rank-degenerate quadratic part controlling the ellipse-to-hyperbola
transition.

N=3
: constrained cubic curves

For
N=3
, after division by an irrelevant nonzero factor,

\displaystyle Q_{3}(\xi,\eta)={}

\displaystyle 2c_{3}\xi^{3}+2\sqrt{3}\,c_{2}\xi^{2}\eta+2\sqrt{3}\,c_{1}\xi\eta^{2}+2c_{0}\eta^{3}

\displaystyle-(3c_{3}+\sqrt{3}\,c_{1})\xi-(\sqrt{3}\,c_{2}+3c_{0})\eta.

Thus the
N=3
shell produces an odd cubic of the restricted form

Q_{3}(\xi,\eta)=A\xi^{3}+B\xi^{2}\eta+C\xi\eta^{2}+D\eta^{3}+E\xi+F\eta,

where the linear and cubic coefficients are not independent. Hence the shell
does not realize an arbitrary real cubic.

Since
Q_{3}
is odd,

Q_{3}(0,0)=0,

so the origin always lies on the nodal curve. It is regular unless

3c_{3}+\sqrt{3}\,c_{1}=0,\qquad\sqrt{3}\,c_{2}+3c_{0}=0.

More generally, finite singularities are obtained from

Q_{3}=0,\qquad\partial_{\xi}Q_{3}=0,\qquad\partial_{\eta}Q_{3}=0.

Eliminating
(\xi,\eta)
gives the finite cubic discriminant surface in
coefficient space.

The cubic shell also has asymptotic degeneracies controlled by

Q_{3,\mathrm{top}}(\xi,\eta)=2c_{3}\xi^{3}+2\sqrt{3}\,c_{2}\xi^{2}\eta+2\sqrt{3}\,c_{1}\xi\eta^{2}+2c_{0}\eta^{3}.

Repeated real zeros of

Q_{3,\mathrm{top}}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=0

change the arrangement of unbounded branches and should be distinguished from
finite singularities.

Product states give useful reducible checkpoints. For example,

\Phi_{21}:\qquad Q_{3}(\xi,\eta)\propto H_{2}(\xi)H_{1}(\eta)\propto\eta(2\xi^{2}-1),

so the nodal set consists of

\eta=0,\qquad\xi=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

N=4
: constrained quartic curves

For
N=4
, the shell gives an even quartic. A compact expression is

\displaystyle Q_{4}(\xi,\eta)={}

\displaystyle c_{4}H_{4}(\xi)+2c_{3}H_{3}(\xi)H_{1}(\eta)+\sqrt{6}\,c_{2}H_{2}(\xi)H_{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle+2c_{1}H_{1}(\xi)H_{3}(\eta)+c_{0}H_{4}(\eta).

Using

\displaystyle H_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=2z,\qquad H_{2}(z)=4z^{2}-2,

\displaystyle H_{3}(z)

\displaystyle=8z^{3}-12z,\qquad H_{4}(z)=16z^{4}-48z^{2}+12,

and dividing by an irrelevant nonzero factor gives

\displaystyle Q_{4}(\xi,\eta)={}

\displaystyle 4c_{4}\xi^{4}+8c_{3}\xi^{3}\eta+4\sqrt{6}\,c_{2}\xi^{2}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle+8c_{1}\xi\eta^{3}+4c_{0}\eta^{4}-(12c_{4}+2\sqrt{6}\,c_{2})\xi^{2}

\displaystyle-12(c_{3}+c_{1})\xi\eta-(2\sqrt{6}\,c_{2}+12c_{0})\eta^{2}

\displaystyle+3(c_{4}+c_{0})+\sqrt{6}\,c_{2}.

Thus
N=4
produces a five-parameter family of even quartics:

Q_{4}(-\xi,-\eta)=Q_{4}(\xi,\eta).

These quartics contain fourth-degree, second-degree, and constant terms, but
no cubic or linear terms. Therefore the
N=4
shell is again a restricted
algebraic family.

Possible nodal geometries include smooth quartics with closed oval
components, unbounded quartic branches, reducible conic–conic
configurations, products involving symmetric pairs of lines, and singular
quartics with double points or tangencies. Finite singularities are found from

Q_{4}=0,\qquad\partial_{\xi}Q_{4}=0,\qquad\partial_{\eta}Q_{4}=0,

while the arrangement of unbounded branches is controlled by

Q_{4,\mathrm{top}}(\xi,\eta)=4c_{4}\xi^{4}+8c_{3}\xi^{3}\eta+4\sqrt{6}\,c_{2}\xi^{2}\eta^{2}+8c_{1}\xi\eta^{3}+4c_{0}\eta^{4}.

The product state
\Phi_{22}
gives

Q_{4}(\xi,\eta)\propto H_{2}(\xi)H_{2}(\eta)\propto(2\xi^{2}-1)(2\eta^{2}-1).

Hence the nodal set is the rectangular grid

\xi=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad\eta=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},

which partitions the plane into

(2+1)(2+1)=9

nodal domains. This agrees with the separable-endpoint count

\nu_{N}=(n_{+}+1)(n_{-}+1)

for
N=4
, where
n_{+}=n_{-}=2
.

Relevance to the entropy diagnostics

The low-shell hierarchy is

\displaystyle N=1:

\displaystyle\text{lines},

\displaystyle N=2:

\displaystyle\text{centered conics},

\displaystyle N=3:

\displaystyle\text{constrained odd cubics},

\displaystyle N=4:

\displaystyle\text{constrained even quartics}.

This hierarchy is the geometric input behind the entropy diagnostics used in
the main text. Away from finite singularities and repeated asymptotic
directions, the nodal set deforms by ambient isotopy and the nodal weights
vary smoothly. Sharp changes in
S_{\mathrm{dom}}
can occur only when the
nodal partition changes, while the global entropies remain regular because
the nodal set has measure zero with respect to the Gaussian-weighted
probability density.

Cubic-shell discriminants for

\nabla^{2}=\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}

(131)

is the two-dimensional Laplacian. We reserve the symbol

\Delta_{\infty}
for the projective discriminant of the leading
binary cubic.
Thus the cubic-shell nodal set is controlled by the highest homogeneous part

H_{3}
, together with the lower-order Hermite correction.

There are two distinct degeneracy mechanisms. The asymptotic directions are
the real projective roots of
H_{3}=0
. They become non-simple when the binary
cubic discriminant vanishes:

\Delta_{\infty}=B^{2}C^{2}-4AC^{3}-4B^{3}D-27A^{2}D^{2}+18ABCD=0.

(132)

This is the projective, or asymptotic, discriminant. It controls changes in
the arrangement of unbounded branches.

Finite affine singularities instead require

P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c})=0,\qquad\nabla P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c})=0.

(133)

Define

p=3A+C,\qquad q=B+3D,\qquad\ell(x,y)=px+qy.

Using Euler’s identity for
H_{3}
, any finite singular point must satisfy

H_{3}(x_{c},y_{c})=0,\qquad\ell(x_{c},y_{c})=0.

Hence finite singular candidates occur only when
H_{3}
and
\ell
have a
common projective root, equivalently when

R_{\mathrm{fin}}=Aq^{3}-Bpq^{2}+Cp^{2}q-Dp^{3}=0.

(134)

This resultant gives the finite-singularity locus of the Hermite-constrained
cubic shell; real affine singularities occur only on the real sublocus where
the corresponding line–conic intersections are real.

For the three-state path used in Sec. VII,

a(t)=c(t)=\sqrt{\frac{1-t^{2}}{2}},\qquad b(t)=t,\qquad d(t)=0,

one has, up to a common nonzero factor,

A=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\alpha^{3/2}a(t),\quad B=2\alpha^{3/2}t,\quad C=2\alpha^{3/2}a(t),\quad D=0.

The projective discriminant reduces to

\Delta_{\infty}=C^{2}(B^{2}-4AC),

(135)

and its nontrivial zero is

t_{\infty}^{2}=4-2\sqrt{3},\qquad t_{\infty}\simeq 0.732.

(136)

This value lies in the same range where Fig.
6
shows the
closest approach of smooth nodal branches.

The finite resultant has endpoint zeros at
t=0
and
t=1
, corresponding
to the line–ellipse configuration and the separable
\Phi_{21}
endpoint,
respectively. It also has an interior reducible candidate at

t_{\mathrm{red}}^{2}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{1+\sqrt{3}}=\frac{3-\sqrt{3}}{2},\qquad t_{\mathrm{red}}\simeq 0.796.

(137)

A direct real check shows that this interior reducible point does not produce
real affine singularities. This is consistent with the critical-value
diagnostic
\Delta_{\mathrm{crit}}
, which remains nonzero on the interior
branch
0<t<1
.

Thus, along the path studied in Sec. VII, the observed close branch approach is
controlled by the nearby projective discriminant

\Delta_{\infty}=0
, not by a real finite singularity. The real finite
singular configurations occur at the degenerate endpoints.

Appendix D
Numerical implementation

This appendix summarizes the numerical procedure used for the representative

N=3
nodal geometries and nodal-domain entropy in
Figs.
6
and
7
. All computations were performed
in dimensionless oscillator units,
\alpha=1
.

Along the path used in Sec. VII,

a(t)=c(t)=\sqrt{\frac{1-t^{2}}{2}},\qquad b(t)=t,\qquad d(t)=0,

the cubic polynomial was taken, up to an irrelevant overall factor, as

\displaystyle P_{3}(x,y;t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\alpha^{3/2}a(t)x^{3}+2\alpha^{3/2}b(t)x^{2}y+2\alpha^{3/2}c(t)xy^{2}

(138)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\alpha}\bigl(\sqrt{3}\,a(t)+c(t)\bigr)x-\sqrt{\alpha}\,b(t)y.

For the computation of domain weights we used the Gaussian-weighted
polynomial density

w_{3}(x,y;t)=e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})}P_{3}(x,y;t)^{2},

and normalized the weights by dividing by the total integral over the computational domain.

The nodal plots in Fig.
6
were generated by plotting the zero
contour
P_{3}(x,y;t)=0
on
[-3.2,3.2]^{2}
for

t=0.10,\quad 0.70,\quad 0.85,\quad 1.00.

The background shading represents the value of the polynomial field
P_{3}
,
clipped and rescaled for visual contrast; it is not the probability density.

For the entropy curve in Fig.
7
, the interior values

0<t<1
were computed on a Cartesian grid on
[-L,L]^{2}
, with
L=8
and

n=180
subdivisions in each direction. At each grid point, the sign of

P_{3}
was recorded using the tolerance
\varepsilon=10^{-12}
:

\sigma_{ij}=\begin{cases}+1,&P_{3}(x_{i},y_{j};t)>\varepsilon,\\
-1,&P_{3}(x_{i},y_{j};t)<-\varepsilon,\\
0,&|P_{3}(x_{i},y_{j};t)|\leq\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Grid points with
\sigma_{ij}=0
were treated as nodal points. Connected
nodal domains were then identified as nearest-neighbor connected components of
the sign field
\sigma_{ij}=\pm 1
. For each component
C
, the unnormalized
weight was computed by

w_{C}(t)=\sum_{(i,j)\in C}\rho_{3}(x_{i},y_{j};t)\,\Delta x\,\Delta y.

(139)

After discarding components with
w_{C}<10^{-14}
, the weights were normalized,

p_{C}(t)=\frac{w_{C}(t)}{\sum_{C^{\prime}}w_{C^{\prime}}(t)},

and the nodal-domain entropy was evaluated as

S_{\mathrm{dom}}(t)=-\sum_{C}p_{C}(t)\ln p_{C}(t).

(140)

The endpoints were treated separately from their analytic nodal
configurations. At
t=0
,

P_{3}(x,y;0)\propto x\left[\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\alpha x^{2}+2\alpha y^{2}-(\sqrt{3}+1)\right],

(141)

so the nodal set is the union of the line
x=0
and the ellipse

\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\alpha x^{2}+2\alpha y^{2}=\sqrt{3}+1.

At
t=1
,

P_{3}(x,y;1)\propto y(2\alpha x^{2}-1),

(142)

so the nodal set consists of
y=0
and

x=\pm 1/\sqrt{2\alpha}
, giving six nodal domains. Since this endpoint is the
separable product state
\Phi_{21}
, its nodal-domain entropy factorizes:

S_{\mathrm{dom}}(1)=S_{\mathrm{dom}}^{1D}(2)+S_{\mathrm{dom}}^{1D}(1).

(143)

As checks, the endpoint factorizations reproduce the plotted nodal geometries,
the separable endpoint satisfies
I(x;y)=0
, and the Gaussian tail outside

[-8,8]^{2}
is negligible in the units used here. Grid refinement was also used
to verify the stability of the detected domain count and of

S_{\mathrm{dom}}
away from the immediate vicinity of topology-changing
events.

D.1
Critical-value diagnostic

To distinguish an exact finite singularity from a close approach of smooth
nodal branches, we also computed a critical-value diagnostic for the
N=3

path. For each sampled value of
t
, the real critical points of the cubic
were obtained by solving

\partial_{x}P_{3}(x,y;t)=0,\qquad\partial_{y}P_{3}(x,y;t)=0.

At such a point, an actual finite singularity of the nodal curve occurs only if
the critical value also vanishes,
P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c};t)=0
. We therefore define

\Delta_{\mathrm{crit}}(t)=\min_{\nabla P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c};t)=0}\frac{|P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c};t)|}{\|P_{3}(\cdot,\cdot;t)\|_{G}},

(144)

where the Gaussian norm is

\|P_{3}(\cdot,\cdot;t)\|_{G}=\left[\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\alpha(x^{2}+y^{2})}P_{3}(x,y;t)^{2}\,dx\,dy\right]^{1/2}.

(145)

Thus
\Delta_{\mathrm{crit}}(t)=0
precisely when the finite singularity
condition

P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c};t)=0,\qquad\nabla P_{3}(x_{c},y_{c};t)=0

is satisfied.

Numerically, the minimum in (
144
) was taken over the real
critical points lying inside the computational window. Along the interior
branch
0<t<1
, this diagnostic remains nonzero, confirming that the
close-branch regime shown in Fig.
6
is not an exact finite singularity. It is instead a close approach of smooth nodal branches.
Small values of
\Delta_{\mathrm{crit}}
occur near the degenerate endpoints,
which are treated separately from the regular interior branch.
