The complete chloroplast genome sequence and phylogenetic position of Causonis japonica (Thunb.) Raf. (Vitaceae)
Authors: Can Zheng, Tianzhe Sun, Jiangwei Zheng, Siyuan Yu, Wenjun Zhu, Chao Xiong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCausonis japonica (Thunb.) Raf. 1838 (Vitaceae), the type species of the genus Causonis, holds notable medicinal and systematic value. In this study, we present the complete chloroplast genome of C. japonica (158,414 bp), which exhibits the conserved four-part structure typical of angiosperms—comprising a large single-copy (LSC, 87,629 bp) region, a small single-copy (SSC, 19,101 bp) region, and two inverted repeats (IRa/IRb, 25,842 bp each). The chloroplast genome contains 139 annotated genes, including 92 protein-coding genes, 39 tRNA genes, and 8 rRNA genes, with an overall GC proportion of 37%. Phylogenomic inference based on complete chloroplast sequences of Vitaceae confirms C. japonica as an independent evolutionary lineage, distinct from Cissus and Tetrastigma.Keywords: Causonis japonica, chloroplast genome, phylogenetic analysis, Vitaceae

Introduction

Causonis japonica (Thunb.) Raf. (Rafinesque. 1838) exhibits a wide distribution across East Asia and marked morphological variability (representative features illustrated in Figure 1). Historically, this high variability has complicated its taxonomic placement, frequently leading to confusion with sympatric Cayratia species.

Morphological characteristics of Causonis japonica. (A) Whole plants; (B) Inflorescences with flowers; (C) Pedately 5-foliolate leaflets; (D) Infructescences with globose to oblate berries. Photos by Can Zheng in Zhangjiajie, Hunan Province, China (29°23′44″N, 110°10′36″E). Key features include longitudinally ridged twigs, 2–3 forked tendrils, and 5-foliolate pedate leaves. Reproductive structures exhibit compound dichasial cymes, ovate flower buds, disc-shaped calyces (with entire or shallowly lobed margins), and subglobose fruits.

Chloroplast genomes have emerged as highly informative markers for phylogenetic reconstruction and species delimitation in plants. Their conserved quadripartite structure, moderate sequence variation, and predominantly maternal inheritance make them robust for resolving genus- and species-level relationships (Daniell et al. 2016, 2021)—a critical need for clarifying Causonis’ status within Vitaceae, especially in the Asia-Pacific context (Lu et al. 2018). Specifically, recent studies have effectively employed chloroplast genomes to resolve complex plant phylogenies, such as reconstructing the evolutionary history of the Cayratia/Causonis complex (Parmar et al. 2021; Yu et al. 2023) and other related Vitaceae lineages (e.g. Tetrastigma, Dong et al. 2022). To address gaps in molecular data for C. japonica and reinforce the taxonomic validity of Causonis, this study reports the sequencing, annotation, and comparative evaluation of its complete chloroplast DNA. The findings aim to provide robust molecular evidence for its phylogenetic position, support Causonis’ evolutionary distinctiveness, and contribute to Asia-Pacific Vitaceae biodiversity research.

Material and methods

Plant material collection

Fresh leaf samples of C. japonica were obtained from Zhangjiajie, Hunan Province, China (29°23′44″N, 110°10′36″E) (Figure 1). The verified plant specimen (voucher ID: WHPU-2024-004) is deposited in the herbarium of the School of Life Science and Technology, Wuhan Polytechnic University (contact person: Chao Xiong; email: xiongchao080190@126.com).

DNA extraction and sequencing

Total genomic DNA was extracted from silica-dried tissue using a modified CTAB method (Doyle and Doyle 1987). Subsequently, high-quality DNA was fragmented to an average insert size of approximately 350 bp for the creation of the sequencing library using the Illumina TruSeq DNA PCR-Free Kit. Paired-end sequencing (2 × 150 bp) was performed on the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform. Raw sequencing data were processed with Trimmomatic v0.39 (Bolger et al. 2014) to remove adapters and low-quality reads.

Genome assembly and annotation

High-quality sequences were assembled de novo into the chloroplast genome utilizing NOVOPlasty v4.3 (Dierckxsens et al. 2017). The circularity and authenticity of the constructed chloroplast genome were confirmed by checking the overlap between the contig ends. Gene annotation was conducted utilizing GeSeq (Tillich et al. 2017), followed by manual curation and structural verification using Sequin v15.50 and MAFFT v7 (Katoh and Standley 2013). Nucleotide composition and gene content were calculated using Geneious Prime (Kearse et al. 2012). The completed chloroplast genome map of C. japonica was illustrated using CPGView (Liu et al. 2023).

Phylogenetic analysis

To resolve the phylogenetic position of C. japonica within Vitaceae, complete chloroplast genomes of 23 taxa were compiled. Following Parmar et al. (2021), the dataset comprised 21 Vitaceae species representing major lineages (e.g. Cissus, Tetrastigma, Vitis) and two Rosaceae outgroups (Fragaria moupinensis, GenBank: OQ992645.1; Pyracantha fortuneana, GenBank: NC_059101.1). Sequences were aligned using the MAFFT v7.526 L-INS-i algorithm (Katoh and Standley 2013) and trimmed with BMGE v1.1 (gap threshold = 0.5, entropy threshold = 0.8; Criscuolo and Gribaldo 2010). Maximum-likelihood (ML) analysis was performed in IQ-TREE v1.6.12 (Nguyen et al. 2015) under the TVM+F + I + G4 substitution model, which was selected by ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017) according to the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). Branch support was assessed using 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates (Hoang et al. 2018), with values ≥95% considered well-supported. The resulting ML topology was visualized in FigTree v1.4.4 (Rambaut, 2012).

Results

The sequencing data were assembled with a minimum read depth of 519× and an average depth of 1,140× (Supplementary Figure 1). The C. japonica chloroplast genome exhibits a typical angiosperm quadripartite structure (Figure 2), consisting of the LSC, the SSC, and two IRs. The complete genome is 158,414 bp in length with an overall GC content of 37.0%. GC content varies across regions: highest in the IRs (43.2%), moderate in the LSC (35.1%), and lowest in the SSC (31.8%). A total of 139 genes were annotated, encompassing 92 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 39 tRNAs, and 8 rRNAs.

The IR regions fully duplicate 17 genes: six protein-coding genes (PCGs) (rpl2, rpl23, ycf2, ndhB, rps7, rps12), seven tRNAs (trnI [two copies], trnL, trnV, trnA, trnR, trnN), and four rRNAs (rrn16, rrn23, rrn4.5, rrn5). Intron-containing PCGs include nine with a single intron (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, ndhA) and two with double introns (clpP1, pafI). Furthermore, the rps12 gene is trans-spliced, with its 5′ exon located in the LSC region and the two 3′ exons duplicated in the IRs (Supplementary Figure 2).

To clarify the phylogenetic position of C. japonica, we inferred a maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogeny using complete chloroplast genomes from 21 Vitaceae taxa and two Rosaceae outgroups (Figure 3). In the resulting phylogeny, three C. japonica accessions formed a fully supported monophyletic clade (ML Boot = 100%), and were shown to be more closely related to Tetrastigma than to Cissus (Figure 3).

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we successfully assembled the complete chloroplast genome of C. japonica. Historically, the taxonomic position of this species was ambiguous. It was traditionally placed within the genus Cayratia (as Cayratia japonica) or occasionally confused with Cissus due to striking morphological homoplasy in vegetative traits, such as leaf morphology and inflorescence architecture (Ren et al. 2011; Parmar et al. 2021).

The C. japonica chloroplast genome provides high-resolution molecular evidence that resolves this historical controversy. Our phylogenetic analysis robustly corroborates its revised taxonomic circumscription, with C. japonica and its allies forming a distinct monophyletic clade. Importantly, this topology aligns perfectly with prior molecular studies that uncovered the polyphyly of Cayratia sensu lato (s.l.)—an assemblage that historically conflated Causonis with other lineages—and strongly supports the reinstatement of Causonis as an independent genus (Lu et al. 2013; Wen et al. 2018; Parmar et al. 2021). Furthermore, our phylogeny demonstrates that C. japonica is more closely related to Tetrastigma than to Cissus. This close evolutionary relationship between Causonis and Tetrastigma within the core vitaceous clade is consistent with previous broad-scale plastid phylogenomic analyses of the grape family (Ren et al. 2011; Habib et al. 2017).

In conclusion, this study substantially enhances the chloroplast genomic resources of C. japonica, addressing a critical gap in the genetic information of this type species. It provides a solid foundation for investigating the genetic variation, species delimitation, and broader phylogenetic relationships within the genus Causonis and the family Vitaceae. The newly obtained chloroplast genome sequence offers valuable support for future species identification, population genomics research, and evolutionary studies.
