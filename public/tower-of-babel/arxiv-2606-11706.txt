Multivariate Period Rings
Rohit Pokhrel 1,2

Abstract
. In this article, we present a new approach to studying multivariate period rings that is more consistent with classical theory and provides a clearer description of their structure. We also prove that the category of B B -admissible representations forms a Tannakian subcategory of the category of representations of G K , Δ \G_{K,\Delta} by defining an analogue of ( F , G ) (F,G) -regular rings, which is central to the classification of representations in multivariate p p -adic Hodge theory.



1.
Introduction

The classical
p
-adic Hodge theory of Fontaine uses various period rings to classify continuous Galois representations of a
p
-adic field
K
. A period ring
B
is a commutative ring equipped with a continuous action of
G_{K}
such that
g(b_{1}+b_{2})=g(b_{1})+g(b_{2})
and
g(b_{1}b_{2})=g(b_{1})g(b_{2})
for all
g\in G_{K}
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. These rings are constructed to satisfy specific properties. Let
F
be a closed subfield of
B^{G_{K}}
; we then have the following definitions:

Definition 1.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

(i)

A
B
-representation

X
of
G_{K}
is a
B
-module of finite type equipped with a continuous action of
G_{K}
satisfying:

•

g(x_{1}+x_{2})=g(x_{1})+g(x_{2})
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
and
g\in G_{K}
;

•

g(\lambda x)=g(\lambda)g(x)
for all
\lambda\in B,g\in G_{K}
and
x\in X
.

(ii)

A
free
B
-representation
of
G_{K}
is a
B
-representation such that the underlying
B
-module
X
is free.

(iii)

A free
B
-representation
X
of
G_{K}
is
trivial
if there exists a basis of
X
consisting of elements fixed by
G_{K}
(i.e., elements in
X^{G_{K}}
).

(iv)

Let
V
be an
F
-representation of
G_{K}
. Then
V
is called
B
-admissible
if
B\otimes_{F}V
is a trivial
B
-representation of
G_{K}
.

A fundamental result of the theory is that the category
\Rep^{B}_{F}(G_{K})
of
B
-admissible
F
-representations of
G_{K}
forms a Tannakian subcategory of the category
\Rep_{F}(G_{K})
of all
F
-representations of
G_{K}
. Consequently, we can restrict our attention to such subcategories; different period rings thus yield different classes of representations. We aim to establish a similar framework in a multivariate setup. However, the classical definitions of
(F,G)
-regular rings given by Fontaine
[
5
]
are not applicable in the multivariate case, as we must depart from the class of integral domains. To overcome this challenge, we utilise the properties of von Neumann regular rings and modules over them to formulate a definition suited for proving analogous results in the multivariate setup.

Let us briefly recall the core ideas of multivariate
p
-adic Hodge theory. For details, see foundational articles such as
[
12
,
13
,
9
,
4
,
2
]
. Let
K_{1},\dots,K_{t}
be
p
-adic fields over a
p
-adic field
K_{0}
, and write
\G_{K,\Delta}=\G_{K_{1}}\times\dots\times\G_{K_{t}}
, which is a profinite group. Let
F
be a
p
-adic field. The goal is to study the structure of the category
\Rep_{F}(\G_{K,\Delta})
by examining its relevant Tannakian subcategories. These Tannakian subcategories arise from a commutative ring
B_{\Delta}
equipped with a continuous action of
\G_{K,\Delta}
such that
g(b_{1}+b_{2})=g(b_{1})+g(b_{2})
and
g(b_{1}b_{2})=g(b_{1})g(b_{2})
for all
g\in\G_{K,\Delta}
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B_{\Delta}
and contains

Consider the perfectoid ring

\C_{\Delta}:=\C\hat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}\C

where the completion is with respect to the semi-norm

This ring is an example of the perfectoid
\Delta

[

(g_{1},\cdots,g_{t})\left(\sum x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{t}\right):=\sum g_{1}\cdot x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{t}\cdot x_{t}.

In multivariate
p
-adic Hodge theory,
\C_{\Delta}
is an analogue of
\C

[
2
]
, the authors consider
\C_{\Delta}
with

The period rings defined in this article are slightly different from those of
[

]
and can be related to them via profinite completion. However, this reformulation ensures that these period rings share many properties with the usual period rings, which is necessary for establishing the Tannakian structure. These new period rings are restricted to classical period rings when

\displaystyle x^{2}y

\displaystyle=x^{2}(z^{2}x)=(x^{2}z)zx=xzx=x,\text{ and}

\displaystyle xy^{2}

\displaystyle=x(z^{2}x)^{2}=xz^{4}x^{2}=(x^{2}z)(xz^{3})=(x^{2}z)z^{2}=xz^{2}=y.

\displaystyle y_{1}=xy_{1}^{2}=(x^{2}y)y_{1}^{2}=y(x^{2}y_{1})y_{1}=yxy_{1}=y(x^{2}y)y_{1}=y^{2}(x^{2}y_{1})=y^{2}x=y.

Corollary 4.11

\gr B^{(p)}_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}=B^{(p)}_{HT,\Delta}

\spec B\to\spec E
is surjective; thus, every
\mathfrak{P}\in\spec E
can be written as
\mathfrak{Q}\cap E
for some prime ideal
\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B
, use

\displaystyle\alpha_{V,\mathfrak{Q}}:B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{E_{\mathfrak{P}}}\bD_{B_{\mathfrak{Q}}}(V)\to B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{F}\bD_{B_{\mathfrak{Q}}}(V)

is injective, where
\bD_{B_{\mathfrak{Q}}}(V):=(B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{F}V)^{G_{K_{s(\mathfrak{Q})}}}

\displaystyle B\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V)\to\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B}(B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{E_{\mathfrak{Q}\cap E}}\bD_{B_{\mathfrak{Q}}}(V))

and

\displaystyle B\otimes_{F}V\to\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B}(B_{\mathfrak{P}}\otimes_{F}V)

are injective. Note that

is injective. As

is a finitely presented
F

Taking
\G_{K,\Delta}

\displaystyle\bD_{B}(V)\to\left(\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B}(B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{F}V)\right)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}

But we have
\left(\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B}B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{F}V\right)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}\to\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B}(B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{F}V)^{\G_{K_{s(\mathfrak{Q})}}}

, the map

is an injective. Taking the tensor product functor

\displaystyle B\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V)\to B\otimes_{E}\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B}\bD_{B_{\mathfrak{Q}}}(V)\to\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B}(B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{E_{\mathfrak{Q}\cap E}}\bD_{B_{\mathfrak{Q}}}(V))

because

is a VNR ring and hence every module over

is flat. Therefore, the map
B\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V)\to\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}\in\spec B}\left(B_{\mathfrak{Q}}\otimes_{E_{\mathfrak{Q}\cap E}}\bD_{B_{\mathfrak{Q}}}(V)\right)
is injective which is what we want to prove. If
\bD_{B}(V)
is free, then from Theorem
5.10
, it follows that the determinant
b
of
\alpha_{V}
is a non-zero divisor.

We will now prove that
\alpha_{V}
is an isomorphism if
\rank_{E}\bD_{B}(V)=\dim_{F}V
, which is equivalent to
b
being a unit in
B
. Let
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{d}\}
be a basis of
V
and
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d}\}
be a basis of
\bD_{B}(V)

\displaystyle e_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}b_{ij}v_{i}

such that

. Let
v=v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{d}\in\bigwedge^{d}_{F}V=Fv
. Then
g(v)=\eta(g)v
, where
\eta:G_{K,\Delta}\to F^{\times}
is a homomorphism. Similarly, let
e=e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{d}\in\wedge^{d}_{E}\bD_{B}(V)
. We then have
e=bv
, and thus

. Consequently,
g(b)=\eta(g)^{-1}b
for all

. By the

-regularity of

The rest of the proof is very similar to that of Theorem 3.14 of

5

(1)

\displaystyle 0\to V^{\prime}\to V\to V^{\prime\prime}\to 0

be an exact sequence of

\displaystyle 0\to B\otimes_{F}V^{\prime}\to B\otimes_{F}V\to B\otimes_{F}V^{\prime\prime}

an exact sequence of
B

\G_{K,\Delta}
-fixed points we get an exact sequence

(2)

\displaystyle 0\to\bD_{B}(V^{\prime})\to\bD_{B}(V)\to\bD_{B}(V^{\prime\prime})

of

\bD_{B}(V^{\prime}),\bD_{B}(V)
and

and

\displaystyle\rank_{E}\bD_{B}(V)=\dim_{F}V=\dim_{F}V^{\prime}+\dim_{F}V^{\prime\prime}\geq\rank_{E}\bD_{B}(V^{\prime})+\rank_{E}\bD_{B}(V^{\prime\prime})\geq\rank_{E}\bD_{B}(V).

Therefore, we have

and

. But

\bD_{B}(-)
is an exact functor when restricted to
\Rep^{B}_{F}(\G_{K,\Delta})
.

We now prove that
\Rep^{B}_{F}(\G_{K,\Delta})
is closed under tensor products. Let

and

be two

-admissible representations of
\G_{K,\Delta}
. Then we have the isomorphisms:

\displaystyle B\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{1})

\displaystyle\cong B\otimes_{F}V_{1}

\displaystyle B\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{2})

\displaystyle\cong B\otimes_{F}V_{2}

It follows that:

\displaystyle\bD_{B}(V_{1}\otimes_{F}V_{2})

\displaystyle=(B\otimes_{F}(V_{1}\otimes_{F}V_{2}))^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=((B\otimes_{F}V_{1})\otimes_{B}(B\otimes_{F}V_{2}))^{\G_{K,\Delta}}

\displaystyle=((B\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{1}))\otimes_{B}(B\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{2})))^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=(B\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{1})\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{2}))^{\G_{K,\Delta}}

\displaystyle=B^{\G_{K,\Delta}}\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{1})\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{2})=\bD_{B}(V_{1})\otimes_{E}\bD_{B}(V_{2})

6.
Hodge-Tate and de Rham Representations

We start this section by proving the classical Ax-Sen-Tate theorem in multivariate setups. For that, let us prove a lemma that is needed for the proof.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
K_{1},\cdots,K_{t}\subset\C_{\Delta}
are fields over a p-adic field
K_{0}
which may not be finite over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
\G_{K,\Delta}=\G_{K_{1}}\times\cdots\times\G_{K_{t}}
be the direct product of the absolute Galois groups. Then the completion of
(\C\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}\C)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
and
\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}
are isomorphic with respect to the semi-norm
|\bullet|=\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}|\beta_{s}(\bullet)|
.

Proof.

Note that
\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}
is complete with respect to
|\bullet|
. Indeed, if
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\C^{G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}
which converges to
x\in\C_{\Delta}
, then for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
g\in\G_{K,\Delta}
we have
|x_{n}-g(x)|=|g(x_{n})-g(x)|=|x_{n}-x|<\epsilon
. This is true for all
\epsilon>0
, we have
g(x)=x
and hence
x\in\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}
. Also,
\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}\cap(\C\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}\C)=(\C\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}\C)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
is dense in
\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}
. Thus its completion must be equal to
\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}
.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain the Ax-Sen-Tate Theorem (Proposition 4.46 of
[
5
]
) in the multivariate case

Proposition 6.2
.

Set up as in Lemma
6.1
, we have

\displaystyle\C_{\Delta}(i)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=\begin{cases}K_{\Delta}&\text{ if }i=0\\
0&\text{ if }i\neq 0\end{cases}

where
K_{\Delta}=K_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}K_{t}
is the completed tensor product, which equals the completion of the usual tensor product with respect to the seminorm
|\bullet|
.

Proof.

First, suppose that
i=0
, then we will first show that
(\C\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots{\otimes}_{K_{0}}\C)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=K_{1}\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}K_{t}
. First, we will show for
t=2
. From Lemma
5.11
and
[
5
]
Proposition 4.9 we get

\displaystyle(\C\otimes_{K_{0}}\C)^{\G_{K_{1}}\times\G_{K_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left((\C\otimes_{K_{0}}\C)^{\G_{K_{1}}}\right)^{\G_{K_{2}}}

\displaystyle=(\C^{\G_{K_{1}}}\otimes_{K}\C)^{\G_{K_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\C^{K_{1}}\otimes_{K_{0}}\C^{K_{2}}=\hat{K}_{1}\otimes_{K_{0}}\hat{K}_{2}

From induction, we get

\displaystyle(\C\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}\C)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=\widehat{K}_{1}\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}\hat{K}_{t}

From Lemma
6.1
we get
\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}=K_{\Delta}
.
For
i\neq 0
, note that its localisation at a prime ideal corresponding to
s\in\mathfrak{S}
is
\C(i)^{\G_{K_{s}}}
, which is zero by the classical Ax-Sen-Tate Theorem. This is because the image of
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\textbf{p}^{\textbf{n}}}
is a system of compatible roots of unity under the map
\beta_{s}
, since
\C_{\Delta}(i)=\C_{\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)
is the tate twist for
\G_{K,\Delta}
which maps to the tate twist
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)
for
\G_{K_{s}}
for every
s\in\mathfrak{S}
. Therefore,
\C_{\Delta}(i)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=0
for
i\neq 0
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

With the above setup, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\text{ closure of $K_{\Delta}$ inside $\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}$}&\text{ if }i=0\\
0&\text{ if }i\neq 0\end{cases}.

Proof.

The result is easy because
\C_{\Delta}
is dense in
\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}
. Note that if
K_{1},\cdots,K_{t}
are finite extensions of
K_{0}
, then the closure of
K_{\Delta}
is equal to
K_{\Delta}
.
∎

Now we will prove the multivariate period rings
B_{HT,\Delta}
and
B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
are
(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\G_{K,\Delta})
-regular. From now on, we will assume
K_{1},\cdots,K_{t}
are finite extensions of
K_{0}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

The rings
B_{HT,\Delta}
and
B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
are
(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\G_{K,\Delta})_{\Delta}
-regular.

Proof.

Let us first show that
B_{HT,\Delta}
is
(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\G_{K,\Delta})_{\Delta}
-regular. The total ring of quotients of
B_{HT,\Delta}
has
t_{\Delta}
-adic completion
C_{HT,\Delta}
, which is a VNR ring. Since
C_{HT,\Delta}
is the profinite completion of
\C_{\Delta}(\!(t_{\Delta})\!)
, it is enough to show that
\C_{\Delta}^{\G_{K,\Delta}}(\!(t_{\Delta})\!)=K_{\Delta}
. Suppose
b=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}t^{i}
, where
c_{i}\in\C_{\Delta}
, is an arbitrary element in
\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}((t))
. Then for
g\in\G_{K,\Delta}
, we have

\displaystyle g(b)=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g(c_{i})\chi_{\Delta}^{i}(g)t^{i}=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}t^{i}.

Therefore, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
c_{i}t^{i}\in\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
, and using Corollary
6.3
, we obtain
\C^{\G_{K,\Delta}}_{\Delta}(\!(t_{\Delta})\!)=K_{\Delta}
(We will prove the result only for this type of element; for others, we must take the limits). It is obvious that
C_{HT,\Delta}
is faithfully flat over
K_{\Delta}
. Finally, assume
b=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}t^{i}\in B_{HT,\Delta}
is not a zero divisor such that
g(b)=\alpha b
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then we get
g(b)=\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}g(b_{i})\chi^{i}_{\Delta}(g)t^{i}=\sum\limits_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha b_{i}t^{i}
. Then in the total ring of fractions of
B_{HT,\Delta}
, we have
g\left(\frac{b_{i}}{b_{j}}\right)
and thus
g\left(\frac{b_{j}}{b_{i}}\right)=\chi_{\Delta}^{i-j}\frac{b_{j}}{b_{i}}
. Therefore,
\frac{b_{j}}{b_{i}}\in\left(\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(j-i)\right)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }i\neq j,\\
K_{\Delta}&\text{ if }i=j\end{cases}
. This shows that
b=b_{i}t^{i}
, where
b_{i}\in\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}
is a non-zero divisor.

Next, we want to prove that
B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
is
(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\G_{K,\Delta})
-admissible. We have already seen that
B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
is a VNR ring. Thus, we next want to show that
B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=K_{\Delta}
. We have the following chain of subrings:
K_{\Delta}\subset\overline{K}_{\Delta}\subset B^{+}_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\subset B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
. Let
b\neq 0
be an element of
B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
; then there exists
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
b\in\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
but
b\notin\Fil^{i+1}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
. Let
\overline{b}\in\gr^{i}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}=\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)
be its image; then
\overline{b}\in\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }i\neq 0,\\
K_{\Delta}&\text{ if }i=0\end{cases}
is non-zero. Therefore,
i=0
and hence
\overline{b}\in K_{\Delta}\subset B^{+}_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
. Now
b-\overline{b}\in B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
and
b-\overline{b}\in(\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta})^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
for some
i\geq 1
. This shows that
b-\overline{b}=0
, proving that
b=\overline{b}\in K_{\Delta}
. We also have that
\spec B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\to\spec K_{\Delta}
is surjective; thus,
B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
is faithfully flat over
K_{\Delta}
.
∎

Definition 6.5
.

(i)

A
p
-adic representation
V
of
\G_{K,\Delta}
is called
Hodge-Tate
if it is
B_{HT,\Delta}
admissible.

(ii)

A
p
-adic representation
V
of
\G_{K,\Delta}
is called

if it is
B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}
admissible.

For any
p
-adic representation, define

\displaystyle\textbf{D}_{HT,\Delta}(V):=(B_{HT,\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}

Then if

is a Hodge-Tate representation then
\textbf{D}_{HT,\Delta}(V)
is a free
K_{\Delta}
-module of rank
\dim_{F}V
, and the canonical map

\displaystyle\alpha_{\text{HT},\Delta}(V):B_{HT,\Delta}\otimes_{K}\textbf{D}_{HT,\Delta}(V)\to B_{HT,\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V

is an isomorphism. The
K_{\Delta}
-module
\textbf{D}_{HT,\Delta}(V)
is a graded
K_{\Delta}
-module

\displaystyle\textbf{D}_{HT,\Delta}(V)=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}

and clearly
(\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
are free
K_{\Delta}
-modules.

We also define for any
p
-adic representation
V

\displaystyle\textbf{D}_{dR,\Delta}(V):=(B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}

and the map

\displaystyle\alpha_{\text{dR},\Delta}(V):B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{K}\textbf{D}_{dR,\Delta}(V)\to B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V

If the representation
V
is de Rham, then we have
\alpha_{\text{dR},\Delta}(V)
is an isomorphism and
\textbf{D}_{dR,\Delta}(V)
is a free
K_{\Delta}
-module of rank
\dim_{F}(V)
.

If
V
is any
p
-adic representation of
\G_{K,\Delta}
, then
\textbf{D}_{dR,\Delta}(V)
is a filtered
K_{\Delta}
-module with

\displaystyle\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}\textbf{D}_{dR,\Delta}(V):=(\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}.

Consider the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\Fil^{i+1}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\to\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\to\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)\to 0

Tensoring with
V
and taking the
\G_{K,\Delta}
-fixed points, we get

\displaystyle\gr^{i}\textbf{D}_{dR,\Delta}(V)\hookrightarrow\textbf{D}_{HT,\Delta}(V).

Proposition 6.6
.

If a
p
-adic representation
V
of
\G_{K,\Delta}
is de Rham, then
V
is Hodge-Tate.

Proof.

To show that
V
is Hodge-Tate, it suffices to prove that
(\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
is a free
K_{\Delta}
-module when
V
is de Rham. Consider the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\Fil^{i+1}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\to\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\to\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)\to 0

of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector spaces. Tensoring with
V
and taking the
\G_{K,\Delta}
-fixed points, we obtain the exact sequence

(3)

\displaystyle 0\to(\Fil^{i+1}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}\to(\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}\to(\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}

The de Rham representation
V
of
\G_{K,\Delta}
can be viewed as a representation of
\G_{K_{s}}
through the map
\eta_{s}:\G_{K_{s}}\to\G_{K,\Delta}
for each

. Because free modules are locally free of constant rank,

is a de Rham representation of
\G_{K_{s}}
. Locally, the sequence obtained from (

) is right-exact since

is de Rham as a representation of
\G_{K_{s}}
; hence, the sequence (
3
) is right-exact globally. Therefore, when

is de Rham, we obtain the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to(\Fil^{i+1}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}\to(\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}\to(\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}\to 0

of

\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}\textbf{D}_{dR,\Delta}(V)=(\Fil^{i}_{\Delta}B_{\mathrm{dR},\Delta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
is finitely presented and

is finitely generated,
\textbf{D}_{HT,\Delta}(V)=(\widehat{\C}_{\Delta}(i)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)^{\G_{K,\Delta}}
is finitely presented. Therefore, Conjecture
5.14
holds in this case. Furthermore, because it has a constant rank at each localization, it is free.
∎
