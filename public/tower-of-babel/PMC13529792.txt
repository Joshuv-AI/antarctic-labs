Research and application of production release process optimization for low permeability condensate gas reservoirs in the East China Sea
Authors: Junrui Ge, Yanfei Li, Bin Cai, Hao Fu, Shunshui Li, Penglei Tang, Jian Wu, Zhengkang Li, Lei Zuo, Yuqing Zhu, Weian Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aiming at the problem that the production capacity of the NB19-X well in the NB19 block of the East China Sea was significantly lower than the design value of the development plan after its commissioning, this study systematically carried out the research on the diagnosis of reservoir damage mechanism and process optimization. Firstly, a productivity evaluation model for condensate gas reservoirs is established, with a calculation accuracy of the established productivity model being ≥ 90%. Through the analysis of the P-T phase state of the reservoir fluid, it was found that the amount of condensate precipitate from the formation accounted for only 0.6%-6%, confirming that condensate is not the main controlling factor. The scaling prediction based on the Scalechem4.0 software shows that the formation risk of CaCO3 scale is relatively high under the pressure reduction conditions of 135℃/5-50 MPa. However, the scaling amount in the near-well area is only 21.69 mg/L, indicating that the impact of scaling on production capacity is limited. The core discovery comes from dynamic contamination experiments: The permeability damage rate caused by cement slurry and perforating fluid reaches 74.4%-79.9%, among which the permeability after contamination by the mixed liquid is only 22.3%. To this end, an innovative low-harm working fluid system with the synergistic effect of “water lock removal - filter loss suppression - backflow promotion” was constructed: the surface tension of the completion fluid system was reduced to 28.00 mN/m; The filtration loss of the perforated liquid system API was reduced to 10mL, and the temperature resistance performance was improved to 140℃. Field applications show that the permeability damage rate of the optimized completion fluid has dropped to 63%, and the permeability recovery rate after backflow has reached 95%, which is 36% points higher than that of the conventional system.

Introduction

With the deepening of exploration and development, the development pace of the East China Sea oil and gas fields has gradually accelerated, and the development of the NB-19 block is steadily advancing. Recently, after the test production of the NB19-X well, it was found that the production capacity did not meet the calculated values in the development plan. It severely impacting the block’s production capacity assessment, subsequent development plans and construction design. The reservoir in this block is characterized as a low-porosity, low-permeability formation. The natural gas is primarily composed of light hydrocarbons, free of H2S, and belongs to a medium-CO2, micro-N2 gas reservoir. The gas-oil ratio in the well fluids is 2191 m³/m³, containing condensate oil components, with overall features of low density, low viscosity, and slight sulfur content. The formation water output is minimal, mostly a mixture of mud filtrate and condensate water, with a total salinity of 3682.6 mg/L. In similar oil and gas reservoirs both domestically and internationally, retro-condensation pollution typically occurs during the mid-to-late stages of development. When the pressure drops below the dew point, the gaseous condensate oil precipitates and transitions to a liquid state, accumulating near the wellbore and causing blockage of the formation flow channels, which reduces the well’s production capacity. Reservoir damage in condensate gas reservoirs mainly includes retrograde condensation damage, water lock damage, sensitivity damage, solid phase damage, scaling, emulsion blockage, and wettability reversal. Particularly for low-porosity, low-permeability, low-production condensate gas wells, liquid lock and retro-condensation cause more severe reservoir damage. Additionally, current research on scaling in wellbores and near-well zones primarily focuses on injection wells, oil wells, and some gas wells, with limited studies on condensate gas wells. This paper addresses the productivity decline of Well NB19-X and conducts a study on the evaluation method for condensate gas reservoirs. Based on the Besson formula, a dual radial flow model is introduced, which comprehensively considers the influences of multiple factors on productivity, including perforation depth, perforation density, radius and damage degree of the contaminated zone, and well deviation angle. The OLGA and Scalechem 4.0 software are adopted to analyze retrograde condensation damage and scaling blockage. Key problems are identified through laboratory experiments on formation damage caused by working fluids. On the basis of formation contamination analysis, targeted formation protection and improvement measures are proposed, which significantly enhance the productivity of the test Well NB19-X and form a comprehensive productivity stimulation technology. Research on capacity evaluation method for gas condensation reservoir

Productivity evaluation model for casing perforation directional well

To calculate the productivity of perforated directional wells, many scholars have established a variety of productivity models.

Thompson et al. proposed a two-region flow model1. Compared with the blockage effect, the two-region flow model more accurately describes the influence of the precipitation of retrograde condensate in the formation on the seepage, but does not take into account the transition between the two flow regions. Through the numerical simulation of the production dynamics of the condensate gas reservoir, Fevang and Whitson proposed a three-region model based on the change in the production gas/oil ratio: (1) the inner region, where the oil and gas phases can flow at different speeds; (2) the transition region, where condensate oil exists but cannot flow, and only gas phase can flow; and (3) the outer region, where only the gas phase flows2. This model more accurately shows the distribution of the oil and gas phases in condensate gas reservoirs, and is widely used in calculation and numerical simulation3–7. Some scholars have further established the four-region model8–12, which takes into account the capillary effect and is the most comprehensive method; however, it increases the difficulty of modeling. Zhang Yufeng et al. established a three-phase productivity calculation model for horizontal wells in water-bearing condensate gas reservoirs considering the non-Darcy effect13. This model provides a practical and convenient tool for the productivity analysis of condensate gas reservoirs considering multiphase flow, but does not independently consider the influence of the perforated zone.

Based on the review of previous scholars’ research, we decided to introduce a dual radial flow model on the basis of the Besson formula14,15, and derive the productivity calculation method for perforated deviated wells under two scenarios: perforations penetrating the damaged zone and perforations not penetrating the damaged zone. This method can consider the influences of factors such as perforation depth, perforation density, perforation diameter, phase angle, radius and damage degree of the damaged zone, thickness and compaction degree of the compacted zone, and well deviation angle on productivity.

In this case, the model (Fig. 2) is described as follows: ① Zone I - the uncontaminated area of the reservoir, where fluid flow is similar to the presence of an open hole directional well in the reservoir. The radius of the contaminated zone is considered as the inner boundary radius of the radial flow, and the permeability is the original permeability; ② Zone II - contamination zone without perforation, fluid flow is the same as Zone I. The outer boundary radius is the radius of the contamination zone, the inner boundary radius is the depth of the hole plus the wellbore radius, and the permeability is the permeability of the contamination zone (depending on the degree of damage caused by the drilling fluid to the reservoir); ③ Zone III - Polluted zone penetrated by boreholes, with radial flow around the boreholes. The outer boundary radius is half the distance between two adjacent boreholes with the same phase, the inner boundary radius is the radius of the compacted zone, and the permeability is the permeability of the polluted zone; ④ Zone IV - Perforation compaction zone, the flow is radial flow around the perforation, the outer boundary radius is the compaction zone radius (the thickness of the compaction zone is generally 6.4–12.7 mm), the inner boundary radius is the perforation radius, and the permeability is the permeability after the perforation causes compaction damage around the perforation (only 7–20% of the original undamaged value).

Geological and seepage characteristics of the target area

Geological features

According to the multi layer DST and RCI testing data of well NB19-X, the comprehensive analysis shows that the geothermal gradient in this area is 2.91 ℃/100 m. The pore type of NB19 structure is mainly secondary pores, mainly dissolution intergranular pores, with a pore size of mesopores and micro throat characteristics in the throat.

Percolation characteristics

The flow channels of low-permeability and breathable gas reservoirs are very narrow, and hydration films are easily formed at small pore throats. The gas in the formation pores must overcome the constraints of the hydration membrane from static to flowing. The pressure gradient acting on both sides of the hydration membrane surface must exceed a critical value before the gas can flow. This critical value is the starting pressure gradient during gas permeation. When there is a starting pressure gradient, the well test curve is no longer a horizontal line during the radial flow stage, but gradually rises, and the larger the starting pressure gradient, the greater the amplitude of the rise (as shown in Fig. 4). Comparing the effect of starting pressure gradient on the theoretical curve (Fig. 5), it can be seen that there is no obvious upward curve segment on the well test curve, so the impact of starting pressure gradient on productivity can be ignored.

According to reservoir sensitivity experiments, the study block has weak stress sensitivity, so the influence of stress sensitivity is ignored in productivity evaluation. According to the analysis of anti condensation pollution, the impact of anti condensation mainly exists in the wellbore, and the effect of two-phase flow on reservoir productivity can be ignored.

Reservoir pollution analysis

Gas condensate reservoirs are different from the conventional reservoir systems; when the gas condensate reservoir pressure drops below the dew point pressure, the heavy components of the condensate gas begin to condense and become the liquid phase16–19. Therefore, liquid condensate will accumulate near the wellbore, which will seriously damage the productivity of gas well20–23. It is of great significance to study the production performance well in gas condensate reservoirs.

Analysis of retrograde condensation pollution

Using OLGA software for simulation analysis, it was found that the error between the simulated and actual oil production values under different production dates remained within 5%. The maximum error in wellhead temperature was 5.68%, and the degree of oil pressure coincidence was the highest, with an error of less than 1%.

The distribution of reservoir wellbore temperature and pressure lines in the P-T phase diagram under different gas production rates in well NB19-X is shown in Fig. 624–26. At a reservoir temperature of 150.99 ℃, the dew point pressure of the formation fluid is 34.34 MPa. According to the simulation results of wellbore temperature distribution with depth at gas production rates of 89,400 cubic meters, 161,600 cubic meters, 182,200 cubic meters, and 212,100 cubic meters in well NB19-X, it was found that the wellhead temperature was the highest at high gas production rates of 212,100 cubic meters, and the change in bottomhole temperature and wellhead temperature was smaller than that at daily gas production rates of 89,400 cubic meters. At the same time, it was found that the wellbore pressure distribution with high gas production of 212,100 cubic meters was lower than that with 89,400 cubic meters. By placing the wellbore temperature and pressure lines at different production rates in the phase diagram, it was found that the reservoir fluid has two stages from the formation to the wellhead:The first stage is the temperature and pressure variation stage from the reservoir to the bottom hole. It is found that the reservoir–bottom hole temperature-pressure line crosses the dew-point line, and condensate oil gradually precipitates. At a gas production rate of 182,200 cubic meters per day, the volume fraction of precipitated condensate oil changes from 0% to 0.6%.The second stage is the temperature and pressure variation stage from the bottom hole to the wellhead within the wellbore. It is observed that the wellbore temperature-pressure line lies below the dew-point line, and all condensate precipitation occurs inside the wellbore. At a gas production rate of 271,300 cubic meters per day, the volume fraction of precipitated condensate oil increases from 0.6% at the bottom hole to 6% at the wellhead, with a difference of 5.4% between the bottom hole and the wellhead. Therefore, the condensate oil in Well NB19-X is mainly generated by wellbore retrograde condensation, while the amount of retrograde condensate liquid in the formation is extremely small and can be neglected (Fig. 6).

Analysis of scaling and blockage

Types of scaling and sensitivity factors

Based on the formation water composition of Well NB19-X, Scalechem 4.0 was used to predict the scaling tendency under two conditions: isothermal pressure depletion (135 °C, 5–45 MPa) and isobaric temperature reduction (30 MPa, 20–140 °C). The results are shown in Fig. 7.The prediction indicates that the scaling product is CaCO₃. The scaling amount increases during isothermal pressure depletion, whereas the scaling tendency weakens or even disappears during isobaric temperature reduction. Temperature reduction has a positive effect on scaling inhibition.

Fig. 7Scaling behavior at various temperatures and pressures. (a) Isobaric cooling, (b) isothermal pressure depletion.

Scaling behavior at various temperatures and pressures. (a) Isobaric cooling, (b) isothermal pressure depletion.

Scaling trend in wellbore

Based on formation water analysis data and gas well production data, the wellbore scaling zones under two scenarios—maximum water production and maximum gas production—were simulated, as shown in Fig. 8. Considering the maximum risk, the scaling zone of Well NB19‑X is considered to extend from the bottom hole to a depth of 200 m.

Using production data including gas and water rates from currently available operational records, simulations were performed to predict wellbore scaling mass under both maximum gas production and maximum water production conditions, as well as the corresponding deposit thickness after one year of simulated production. The results show that both the maximum daily deposition rate and maximum annual deposit thickness occur at the bottom hole, with a daily wellbore scale deposition rate of 3.89 kg/d and an annual deposit thickness of 0.795 mm. It is concluded that the effect of temperature on scaling is greater than that of pressure, which explains why the maximum scale deposition mass and thickness both appear at the bottom of the wellbore.

Scaling trend near wellbore

Based on the bottomhole and formation temperature-pressure calculated by the OLGA software, the variation of temperature and pressure in the near-well zone with the distance from the bottomhole was linearly fitted, as shown in Fig. 9. Combined with the formation water analysis data of Well NB19-X, the scaling trend in the near-well zone of Well NB19-X was calculated, as presented in Fig. 10. The simulation results show that the maximum scaling trend in the near-well zone reaches 21.69 mg/L.

Combining the above analysis of the daily scaling deposition amount and annual deposition thickness in the wellbore, as well as the scaling trends in the near-well zone and formation of Well NB19-X, it can be concluded that the scaling risk of pure formation water induced by temperature and pressure variation is relatively low, exerting a minor impact on the wellbore productivity of the condensate gas well. Therefore, it is inferred that formation water scaling caused by temperature and pressure changes is not the dominant factor leading to the productivity decline of Well NB19-X, and scaling has a slight influence on the productivity of this well.

Assessment of reservoir damage caused by working fluid

Evaluation of reservoir damage caused by a single working fluid

Reservoir cores from the NB19-X block were selected. With reference to SY/T 6540 − 2002 Laboratory Evaluation Method for Formation Damage Induced by Drilling and Completion Fluids, dynamic core contamination flow experiments were conducted using simulated formation water as the medium to evaluate the permeability damage of cores caused by the currently used working fluids. The test results are presented in Table 1.

It was found that drilling fluid and cement slurry caused relatively low damage to core permeability. After contamination by completion fluid, perforation fluid, and the mixed solution of the four working fluids, the core permeability recovery values were all below 30%, indicating severe reservoir damage. Evaluation of reservoir damage by series working fluids

Reservoir cores from the NB19-X Block were selected, and sequential multi-cycle contamination experiments were conducted in accordance with SY/T 6540 − 2002 Laboratory Evaluation Method for Formation Damage Caused by Drilling and Completion Fluids to evaluate permeability changes of cores after repeated contamination by wellbore fluids. Formation water permeability was taken as the initial permeability. A sequential contamination experiment was performed in the order of drilling fluid → cement slurry (primary slurry) → completion fluid → perforating fluid to investigate permeability variations after core contamination. The experimental results are listed in Table 1.

The results show that permeability damage was most severe due to contamination by drilling fluid and cement slurry. After contamination by drilling fluid, core permeability decreased from 1.712 mD to 0.937 mD, with a permeability recovery of only 54.72%. Permeability declined further after cement slurry contamination, resulting in a permeability recovery of merely 27.62%. Although the cement slurry contamination layer on the core surface was largely washed away during completion fluid contamination, core permeability was not effectively restored. This indicates that cement slurry caused deep pore-throat structure damage, which was difficult to remediate. Finally, core permeability decreased further after perforating fluid contamination. The core surface was covered by gel-like substances that were hard to remove; under pressure, these gels tended to deform and invade core pores, resulting in permanent permeability damage.

After four sequential contamination cycles with wellbore fluids, the final core permeability recovery was only 13.12%, representing severe formation damage. Drilling fluid, cement slurry and perforating fluid all caused significant damage to the reservoir. Although completion fluid exhibited a relatively good cleaning effect on the core surface, drilling fluid and cement slurry altered the internal pore-throat structure of the core, making effective permeability recovery difficult by removing only the external mud cake (Table 2).

Measures for productivity stimulation and release

Measures against condensate blockage

Producing through small-sized chokes can elevate bottomhole pressure and mitigate retrograde condensation within the reservoir. For severe retrograde condensate contamination, the following measures can be adopted subsequently27,28:Hydraulic fracturing can increase the near-wellbore formation flow pressure, delay condensate liquid accumulation around the wellbore and reduce the skin factor. However, fracture closure tends to occur, inducing a reverse imbibition water-blocking effect that impairs fracturing performance; this phenomenon is particularly prominent in low-permeability reservoirs.Nitrogen injection can replenish formation energy and displace retrograde condensate oil in low-permeability zones, providing the functions of plug removal, flowback enhancement and gas lifting.Methanol injection accelerates the evaporation of invading fluid, reduces interfacial tension, and more effectively mitigates water blocking. It also lowers the threshold pressure required to overcome the water-blocking effect during injection, which is of particular importance for low-permeability gas reservoirs.Propane injection effectively reduces retrograde condensate liquid saturation, decreases condensate oil volume, improves gas-phase permeability and enhances productivity. It features advantages such as continuous separation, low energy consumption and simple operation.

Scale inhibition countermeasures

The scaling products in Block NB19 are predominantly calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) scale, which has a minor impact on gas well productivity. However, considering the increase in water production in the late production stage, the scaling risk of gas wells will rise. To ensure safe and efficient production throughout the entire life cycle of gas wells, corresponding prevention and control methods are proposed for potential scaling risks29–31.Application of coating anti-scaling technology. The use of anti-scaling coatings with low surface tension, poor wettability and anti-adhesion properties can effectively reduce the deposition rate of scale on the wall surface and decrease the thickness of the scale layer on the wellbore wall. Commonly used anti-scaling coatings at present include ceramic coatings and organic polymer coatings.Installation of anti-scaling devices to alter the electrostatic potential of the fluid, causing fluid polarization and inhibiting the formation of wax and scale. Formulation of a rational production plan to control the production flow rate and drawdown pressure of gas wells, reduce the deposition rate of scale on the wellbore wall, prevent the accelerated precipitation of CO₂ from formation water, and slow down the precipitation rate of CaCO₃.

Low-damage working fluid measures

Low-damage completion fluid system and evaluation of its reservoir protection performance

Construction of a low-damage completion fluid system

Spontaneous water absorption under the action of capillary force will increase the water saturation of low-permeability reservoirs, and the water film will block the pore throats, seriously hindering the flow of oil and gas. Selecting optimal surfactants to effectively reduce the surface and interfacial tension is the core method to relieve water blocking and promote fluid flowback32–36.

Since the completion fluid contains a large amount of inorganic salts, resulting in high surface tension, it is difficult to dissolve the surfactant when added directly (Table 3). Small-molecule alcohols can be used as co-surfactants to dissolve surfactants in high surface tension systems. At the same time, alcohols themselves have a certain effect of reducing surface tension. Therefore, it is designed to use small-molecule alcohols compounded with surfactants to synergistically reduce the surface tension performance of the optimized completion fluid system.

Conclusion

This study conducts an in-depth analysis of the productivity evaluation method for low-permeability condensate gas reservoirs, as well as the geological and seepage flow characteristics of the target block. Reservoir contamination analysis is also carried out to identify the core causes of below-expected productivity. Through analyses of retrograde condensation damage, reservoir damage evaluation induced by working fluids, and scaling plugging analysis, targeted countermeasures for productivity recovery are finally proposed and verified by field application comparison.
