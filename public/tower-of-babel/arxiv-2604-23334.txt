A Note on Interdiction of Linear Minimization Problems
Yu Cong, Kangyi Tian

Abstract
Motivated by the FPTAS for connectivity interdiction of Huang et al. [ 4 ] , we isolate the part of the argument that does not use cuts. The setting is a minimization problem over a feasible-set family ℱ \mathcal{F} with a linear objective w ⁡ ( S ) = ∑ e ∈ S w ⁡ ( e ) w(S)=\sum_{e\in S}w(e) . After dualizing the interdiction budget, deletion can be absorbed into truncated weights w � ​ ( e ) = min ⁡ { w ⁡ ( e ) , � ​ c ​ ( e ) } w_{\lambda}(e)=\min\{w(e),\lambda c(e)\} . At an optimal Lagrange multiplier � ∗ \lambda^{*} , the unknown optimal interdiction witness is a strict 2 2 -approximate minimizer of the reweighted problem. Thus an exact algorithm can be obtained whenever one can optimize w � ∗ w_{\lambda^{*}} over ℱ \mathcal{F} , enumerate all its 2 2 -approximate minimizers, and solve the remaining knapsack problem.



1
Model

Let
E
be a finite ground set and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{E}
be the family
of feasible sets of an underlying linear minimization problem

\min_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w(S),

where
w(S)=\sum_{e\in S}w(e)
for weights
w:E\to\mdmathbb{Z}_{+}
. We are also given
an interdiction cost
c:E\to\mdmathbb{Z}_{+}
and a budget
b\in\mdmathbb{Z}_{+}
.

The linear minimization interdiction problem considered here is

\opt=\min\left\{w(S\setminus R)\;|\;S\in\mathcal{F},\;R\subseteq S,c(R)\leq b\right\},

(1)

where
c(R)=\sum_{e\in R}c(e)
. This is equivalent to first deleting an
arbitrary set
R
with
c(R)\leq b
and then minimizing
w(S\setminus R)
over

S\in\mathcal{F}
, because only
R\cap S
affects the value of a chosen feasible
set
S
.

2
Lagrangian relaxation

It is helpful to first look at (
1
) as an integer program,
even though we do not write variables for the feasible-set constraints:

\displaystyle\opt=\min

\displaystyle w(S\setminus R)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle S\in\mathcal{F},\quad R\subseteq S,

\displaystyle c(R)\leq b.

The set
S
is the object chosen by the original minimization problem, and

R
is the part of
S
removed by the interdiction budget. For a fixed
S
,
choosing
R
is a knapsack-like deletion problem; the global difficulty is that
we do not know which feasible set
S
will be optimal after deletion.

The Lagrangian relaxation prices the single budget constraint. For a multiplier

\lambda\geq 0
, move the constraint
c(R)\leq b
into the objective:

\Phi(\lambda)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{F}\\
R\subseteq S\end{subarray}}\bigl(w(S\setminus R)+\lambda(c(R)-b)\bigr).

For every fixed
\lambda
, this is a lower bound on the interdiction optimum:
budget-feasible pairs get a non-positive correction term

\lambda(c(R)-b)
. The Lagrangian dual is
\Lambda=\max_{\lambda\geq 0}\Phi(\lambda)
and we denote a maximizer by
\lambda^{*}
. We assume
\Lambda>0
since otherwise every feasible set
S
would satisfy
c(S)\leq b
, so the interdiction problem would reduce to the original optimization problem.

Now remove the constant term
-\lambda b
from
\Phi(\lambda)
and focus on the
inner minimization

L(\lambda)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{F}\\
R\subseteq S\end{subarray}}\bigl(w(S\setminus R)+\lambda c(R)\bigr),

so that
\Phi(\lambda)=L(\lambda)-\lambda b
. For a fixed
S
and
\lambda
,
each element
e\in S
has only two relevant choices: If
e
is included in
R
, its cost will be
\lambda c(e)
; Otherwise the cost is
w(e)
.
Thus the best truncated weight of whether including
e
in
R
is
w_{\lambda}(e)=\min\{w(e),\lambda c(e)\}
.
Because both
w
and
c
are linear,

\displaystyle\min_{R\subseteq S}\bigl(w(S\setminus R)+\lambda c(R)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in S}\min\{w(e),\lambda c(e)\}

\displaystyle=w_{\lambda}(S).

Therefore we have
L(\lambda)=\min_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{\lambda}(S)
, which is the original linear optimization problem under the truncated weight.

The function
\Phi
is the lower envelope of finitely many lines in
\lambda
,
so it is piecewise-linear and concave.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
\lambda\geq 0
,
\Phi(\lambda)\leq\opt
. Hence

\Lambda\leq\opt
.

Let
(S,R)
be feasible for (
1
). Since
c(R)\leq b
,
w(S\setminus R)+\lambda(c(R)-b)\leq w(S\setminus R)
.
Minimizing the left-hand side over all pairs
(S,R)
, and then minimizing the
right-hand side only over budget-feasible pairs, gives

\Phi(\lambda)\leq\opt
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
\lambda^{*}
is a finite maximizer of
\Phi
. Then there is a pair

(S^{LD},R^{LD})
attaining
L(\lambda^{*})
such that
c(R^{LD})\leq b
.
Consequently,

L(\lambda^{*})\geq\opt.

If every pair attaining
L(\lambda^{*})
had
c(R)>b
, then every active line in
the lower envelope defining
\Phi
would have positive slope at
\lambda^{*}
.
For sufficiently small
\delta>0
, the value of the lower envelope would then
increase from
\lambda^{*}
to
\lambda^{*}+\delta
, contradicting the optimality of

\lambda^{*}
.

Thus some active pair
(S^{LD},R^{LD})
has
c(R^{LD})\leq b
. This pair is
feasible for (
1
), so

w(S^{LD}\setminus R^{LD})\geq\opt
. Therefore

L(\lambda^{*})=w(S^{LD}\setminus R^{LD})+\lambda^{*}c(R^{LD})\geq\opt.

3
The main observation

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(S^{*},R^{*})
be an optimal solution to (
1
). If

\Lambda>0
, then

L(\lambda^{*})\leq w_{\lambda^{*}}(S^{*})\leq L(\lambda^{*})+b\lambda^{*}<2L(\lambda^{*}).

In particular,
S^{*}
is a strict
2
-approximate minimizer of

\min_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{\lambda^{*}}(S)
.

The lower bound follows immediately from the definition of
L(\lambda^{*})
:

L(\lambda^{*})=\min_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{\lambda^{*}}(S)\leq w_{\lambda^{*}}(S^{*}).

For the upper bound, use the particular deletion set
R^{*}
inside the definition
of
w_{\lambda^{*}}(S^{*})
:

\displaystyle w_{\lambda^{*}}(S^{*})

\displaystyle\leq w(S^{*}\setminus R^{*})+\lambda^{*}c(R^{*})

\displaystyle=\opt+\lambda^{*}c(R^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\opt+\lambda^{*}b

\displaystyle\leq L(\lambda^{*})+\lambda^{*}b,

where the last inequality is
section 2
. Finally,

\Lambda=L(\lambda^{*})-\lambda^{*}b>0
, so

L(\lambda^{*})+\lambda^{*}b<2L(\lambda^{*})
.

The strict
2
-approximation analysis generalizes to non-negative set function objectives.
For connectivity interdiction,
S^{*}
is the optimal interdiction cut, so it is
among the strict
2
-approximate min-cuts in the graph with capacities

w_{\lambda^{*}}
.

Lemma 4.1
(

Let

4.1
Application on Connectivity Interdiction

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected multigraph, and let
\mathcal{F}
be the family of
all nontrivial cuts
\delta(U)
, where
\emptyset\neq U\subsetneq V
. The
interdiction problem is

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\mathcal{F},\;R\subseteq C\\
c(R)\leq b\end{subarray}}w(C\setminus R).

If there is a cut
C
with
c(C)\leq b
, then the optimum is
0
: remove all
edges in
C
. This case is detected by one min-cut computation under capacities

c
, so below assume every cut has
c
-cost larger than
b
.

For a fixed
\lambda
, put capacity
w_{\lambda}(e)=\min\{w(e),\lambda c(e)\}

on every edge. Then

L(\lambda)=\min_{C\in\mathcal{F}}w_{\lambda}(C),

so evaluating
L(\lambda)
is just a global min-cut computation in

(G,w_{\lambda})
. Using the deterministic almost-linear time min-cut algorithm
of Li
[
6
]
, this takes
\tilde{O}(m)
time. Therefore,

section 4
gives a
\tilde{O}(m^{2})
-time algorithm for computing
\lambda^{*}
.
If randomization is allowed, we can find
\lambda^{*}
in near linear time.
Min-cut can be computed with high probability in
O(m\log^{2}n)
work and
O(\log^{3}n)
depth
[
1
]
. Brent’s law
[
3
]
shows that the running time is
T(n,P)=O(\frac{m\log^{2}n}{P}+\log^{3}n)
with
P
processors. Setting
P=O(m)
and plugging the parallel algorithm into
section 4
give a randomized
\tilde{O}(m)
-time algorithm for
\lambda^{*}
.

Let
L^{*}=L(\lambda^{*})
. By
Theorem 3.1
, the optimal
interdiction cut
C^{*}
satisfies

w_{\lambda^{*}}(C^{*})<2L^{*}
.
Thus
C^{*}
is one of the strict
2
-approximate min-cuts in

(G,w_{\lambda^{*}})
. Karger
[
5
]
showed that the number of

\alpha
-approximate min-cuts is
O(n^{\left\lfloor 2\alpha\right\rfloor})
. Hence the number of
cuts with value strictly smaller than
2L^{*}
is
O(n^{3})
, and they can be
enumerated in randomized
O(n^{3})
time
