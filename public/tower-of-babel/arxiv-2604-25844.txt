(Super-)renormalizable hairy meronic black holes
Luis Avilés, Borja Diez

Abstract
We construct an analytical black hole solution in the Einstein–Maxwell–Yang–Mills theory with a conformally coupled scalar field in four dimensions, which generalizes the charged Martínez–Troncoso–Zanelli (MTZ) black hole in the presence of self–gravitating non–Abelian gauge fields. The internal gauge group is determined by the horizon curvature, becoming S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) in the case of positive curvature and S ​ U ​ ( N − 1 , 1 ) SU(N-1,1) when the curvature is negative. Moreover, this solution is employed as a conformal seed to obtain new meronic spacetimes dressed with all (super–)renormalizable contributions of the scalar field, which provides the generalization of the Anabalón–Cisterna (AC) solution when self–gravitating non–Abelian gauge fields are included. Finally, we consider the non–Noetherian extension of the conformal scalar fields, which still yields a second–order conformally invariant scalar equation, even though the action is not. In that case, we show that static black hole solutions can also be charged with Yang–Mills fields.

Introduction

In modern physics, Yang–Mills theory stands as one of the most successful theoretical frameworks, providing the gauge structure underlying quantum chromodynamics and the electroweak sector of the Standard Model. Beyond the perturbative regime, non–Abelian gauge fields exhibit nontrivial configurations that establish profound links between topology and quantum field theory. When coupled to gravity, Yang–Mills fields acquire additional significance, as they are known to evade the no–hair conjecture

SU(N)

On the other hand, the inclusion of scalar fields in non–Abelian gauge theories is of fundamental importance, as exemplified by the Higgs mechanism, which implements spontaneous symmetry breaking and determines the mass spectrum of the gauge sector. Since classical Yang–Mills theories are conformally invariant, the introduction of conformally coupled scalar fields arises as a natural extension that preserves the symmetry properties of the matter sector, particularly when gravity is taken into account. II
The theory

In this work, we focus on the Yang-Mills theory coupled to Einstein-Maxwell gravity with a cosmological constant
\Lambda
and to a conformally coupled scalar field. The dynamics of the system is described by the following action principle

I[g_{\mu\nu},\mathcal{A}_{\mu},A_{\mu},\phi]=I_{\rm G}+I_{\rm Max}+I_{\rm YM}+I_{\rm SF}\,,

(1)

where

\displaystyle I_{\rm G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\differential^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\left(R-2\Lambda\right)\,,

(2a)

\displaystyle I_{\rm Max}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\differential^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}\,,

(2b)

\displaystyle I_{\rm YM}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\differential^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\Tr(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})\,,

(2c)

\displaystyle I_{\rm SF}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\differential^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{12}R\phi^{2}+\lambda\phi^{4}\right)\,.

(2d)

Here,
G
and
\Lambda
are the gravitational and cosmological constants, respectively,
\lambda
is the coupling of the conformal potential, and
(\nabla\phi)^{2}:=\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\mu}\phi
corresponds to the kinetic term for the scalar field. From now on, Latin indices will denote
SU(2)
indices, while Greek indices will refer to spacetime indices. The non–Abelian field strength is given in terms of the non–Abelian gauge connection in the adjoint representation of the group, i.e.,
A_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{a}t_{a}
, according to

F_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\nu}^{a}t_{a}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}+e[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]\,,

(3)

where
e
corresponds to the Yang-Mills coupling, while the Abelian field strength is given by

\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\,.

(4)

The field equations of the theory are obtained by performing arbitrary variations of the action with respect to the metric, the Maxwell and Yang–Mills gauge potentials, and the scalar field, yielding

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=8\pi G\left(T^{(\rm Max)}_{\mu\nu}+T^{(\rm YM)}_{\mu\nu}+T^{(\rm SF)}_{\mu\nu}\right)\,,

(5a)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(5b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}+e[A_{\mu},F^{\mu\nu}]

\displaystyle=0\,,

(5c)

\displaystyle\Box\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}R\phi+4\lambda\phi^{3}\,,

(5d)

respectively, where
G_{\mu\nu}:=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R
denotes the Einstein tensor, and we have defined the stress–energy momentum tensor for the Maxwell, Yang–Mills and scalar fields, as

\displaystyle T^{(\rm Max)}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\mu\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\nu}^{\penalty\ \lambda}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{F}^{\alpha\beta}\,,

(6a)

\displaystyle T^{(\rm YM)}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-2\Tr\left(F_{\mu\lambda}F_{\nu}^{\penalty\ \lambda}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}\right)\,,

(6b)

\displaystyle T^{(\rm SF)}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}+\lambda\phi^{4}\right)

(6c)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ +\frac{1}{6}(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}+G_{\mu\nu})\phi^{2}\,.

Since both Maxwell and Yang–Mills theory are invariant under conformal rescaling of the metric,
g_{\mu\nu}\mapsto\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu}
, with
\Omega=\Omega(x)
an arbitrary local function, the corresponding stress–energy tensors
T_{\mu\nu}^{(\rm Max)}
and
T_{\mu\nu}^{(\rm YM)}
are traceless. On the other hand, the action for the scalar field (
2d

(g_{\mu\nu},\phi)\mapsto(\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu},\Omega^{-1}\phi)

5a

Hairy meronic black hole solution

In order to seek static black hole solutions within the theory defined by the action (
1

\displaystyle\differential s^{2}

\displaystyle=-f(r)\differential t^{2}+\frac{\differential r^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}\differential\Omega^{2}_{k}\,,

\displaystyle t_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\sqrt{(j-1)(N-j+1)}(E_{j-1,j}+E_{j,j-1})\,,

(11a)

\displaystyle t_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\sqrt{(j-1)(N-j+1)}(E_{j-1,j}-E_{j,j-1})\,,

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{N+1}{2}-j\right)E_{j,j}\,,

For horizons of positive curvature,

\displaystyle r_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell}{2}\left(-1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4MG}{\ell}}\right)\,,

(21a)

IV
(Super-)renormalizable meronic spacetimes

In the previous section, the dyonic MTZ black hole was generalized to include self-gravitating Yang-Mills fields. In this section, we construct a new solution by employing the strategy proposed in Ref.
Ayón-Beato et al. 2015
. There, the authors devised a method to generate solutions by mapping any self-gravitating conformal scalar field with non-trivial self-interaction and a non-vanishing cosmological constant into a one-parameter family of a related theory, supplemented by a (super-)renormalizable potential. When the MTZ black hole is used as the seed, the resulting geometry is nothing but the solution previously reported by Anabalón and Cisterna (AC) in Ref.
Anabalon and Cisterna 2012
. This family of solutions includes regular black holes, wormholes, and bouncing cosmologies, thereby significantly enhancing the solution space compared to the original seed. This strategy was extended to incorporate Maxwell fields as well
Ayón-Beato et al. 2015
, which is possible thanks to their conformal invariance.
This technique has also been implemented using geometries with NUT charge
Bardoux et al. 2014
as seed in Ref.
Barrientos et al. 2022
. Here, we apply a similar procedure to generate a new solution using the meronic MTZ black hole obtained in the previous section as the conformal seed.

The idea is to map the fields of the theory, together with the coupling constants, in such a way to obtain a new action that is proportional to the original one, namely,

\bar{I}[\bar{g}_{\mu\nu},\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{\mu},\bar{A}_{\mu},\bar{\phi}]=(1-a^{2})I[g_{\mu\nu},\mathcal{A}_{\mu},A_{\mu},\phi]\,,

(25)

where

is the parameter that controls the mapping and is restricted by
a^{2}<1
in order to preserve unitarity in both theories.
As a consequence, the solutions of the equations of motion (
5
), obtained by performing stationary variations of the right-hand side of Eq. (
25

V
Non-Noetherian conformal meronic black hole

Recently, in Ref.
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2024
, the most general action for a real scalar field leading to a conformally invariant second-order equation of motion was constructed. This corresponds to a generalization of the theory presented in Ref.
Fernandes 2021
. The associated scalar field equation includes a new non-minimal coupling that breaks the conformal invariance of the action, while preserving it at the level of the equations of motion. Such a symmetry was dubbed non-Noetherian conformal symmetry
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2024
, since it is not realized at the level of the action principle (see also Refs.
Jackiw 2006
;
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2023
for the analogous construction in lower dimensions). This theory has given rise to several solutions of physical interest, such as black holes
Fernandes 2021
;
Ayón-Beato et al. 2024
;
Babichev et al. 2022
, cosmological models
Fernandes 2021
, stealth configurations
Ayón-Beato et al. 2025
, and exact wave solutions
Sánchez 2025
, among others. In addition, its DC conductivity properties have been explored in Ref.
Hernandez-Vera 2025
(see Ref.
Fernandes et al. 2022
for a comprehensive review).

In this section we consider an extension of the theory presented in Sec.
II
, in which the conformal non-Noetherian sector associated with the scalar field is taken into account. The action principle to be considered is given by

I[g_{\mu\nu},\mathcal{A}_{\mu},A_{\mu},\phi]=I_{\rm G}+I_{\rm Max}+I_{\rm YM}+I_{\rm SF}+I_{\rm nN}\,,

(33)

where the first four terms are defined in Eq. (
2

;
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2024

\displaystyle I_{\rm nN}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\differential^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\left(\log(\phi)\mathcal{G}-\frac{4}{\phi^{2}}G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi\right.

\displaystyle\left.\penalty\ \penalty\ -\frac{4}{\phi^{3}}(\nabla\phi)^{2}\Box\phi+\frac{2}{\phi^{4}}(\nabla\phi)^{4}\right)\,,

(34)

where
\mathcal{G}=R^{2}-4R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}+R_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}R^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}
is the Gauss-Bonnet term. This additional contribution to the action belongs to the Horndeski class

;

;
Babichev et al. 2023a

The field equation associated with the scalar field is corrected with respect to Eq. (

VI
Discussion

In this work, we have constructed a broad class of exact solutions within Einstein–Maxwell–Yang–Mills theory with cosmological constant, and non-minimally coupled scalar fields. The gauge configurations considered correspond to merons, reflecting the intrinsically non–Abelian nature of these solutions. Moreover, the choice of gauge group depends explicitly on the topology of the horizon under consideration. For horizons of positive curvature, the group is
SU(N)
, whereas for negative curvature it is
SU(N-1,1)
. To parametrize the generators of these groups, we employ the maximal embedding of
SU(2)
into
SU(N)
(or
SU(1,1)
into
SU(N-1,1)
), together with the Euler angle parametrization
Bertini et al. 2006
;
Cacciatori et al. 2017
;
Tilma and Sudarshan 2004
, which allows us to construct solutions for arbitrary values of
N
.

We begin by considering the case of a conformally coupled scalar field, presenting the non–Abelian extension of the MTZ black hole
Martinez et al. 2003
;
Martinez et al. 2006
. Subsequently, using this configuration as a conformal seed and applying an extension of the mappings proposed in Ref.
Ayón-Beato et al. 2015
, we obtain a generalization of the AC
Anabalon and Cisterna 2012
solution in the presence of self-gravitating merons. Depending on the values of the coupling constants, this geometry admits particular limits describing black holes, inhomogeneous bouncing cosmologies, and wormholes. Finally, we analyze the non-Noetherian conformal extension of scalar fields
Fernandes 2021
;
Ayón-Beato and Hassaine 2024
, which breaks conformal invariance at the level of the action while preserving it in the scalar field equation. We show that the black hole solutions presented in Ref.
Fernandes 2021
can be consistently extended to include self-gravitating Yang–Mills fields.

Several generalizations of the results presented in this work are conceivable. First of all, it is natural to ask whether our construction can be extended to higher dimensions, for instance to
D=5
, where a self-gravitating meronic black hole solution is known to exist and carries a nontrivial topological charge
Canfora et al. 2019
, in contrast to the solutions discussed in this work. It would also be interesting to explore the possibility of constructing self-gravitating Yang–Mills configurations on spacetimes endowed with NUT charge and self-gravitating scalar conformal scalar fields, as in, e.g., Ref.
Bardoux et al. 2014
. If feasible, such configurations would provide a one-parameter extensions of the solutions obtained here, allowing for a more detailed study of their thermodynamic and holographic properties. Extensions incorporating the recently proposed non–Abelian generalizations of ModMax electrodynamics
Bandos et al. 2020
;
Canfora et al. 2025b
;
Cirilo-Lombardo 2023
, which preserve conformal invariance, could also be of interest, and we expect that they reduce to the solutions presented in this work in the limit where the dimensionless non–Abelian ModMax parameter vanishes. Furthermore, exploring the compatibility of meronic configurations with geometries with nontrivial torsion (see, for example, Ref.
Avilés et al. 2024
) would undoubtedly constitute an interesting extension of these results. Finally, it is worth emphasizing that since the non–Noetherian sector lacks conformal invariance at the level of the action, it is not clear whether a mapping analogous to that discussed in Sec.
IV
can be implemented when this sector is taken into account which remains an open problem that is certainly worth exploring. We leave these questions for future work.
