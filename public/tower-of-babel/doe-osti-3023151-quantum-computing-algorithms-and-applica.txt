Topical Area: Quantum Computing in Chemical and Materials Sciences.

Executive Summary
This project advanced quantum algorithms, quantum information theory, strongly correlated electronic
structure methods, molecular quantum materials, and exciton transport imaging in coherent condensed-
phase systems. Across the award period, the team developed new methods for open-quantum-system
simulation, Hamiltonian learning, state tomography, reduced-density-matrix and contracted-quantum-
eigensolver approaches, and quantum diagnostics for device capability and openness.
The project also established new molecular and materials platforms relevant to quantum sensing and
quantum photonics, including radicaloid tetrathiafulvalene tetrathiolate compounds with bright near-
infrared fluorescence, microsecond-scale solution lifetimes, and early optically detected magnetic
resonance signatures. In parallel, the team demonstrated environment-assisted exciton transport and
quantum-interference control of exciton-exciton annihilation in molecular aggregates using advanced
optical microscopy.
These results produced a broad set of peer-reviewed publications, review articles, preprints, and patent
activity, while training undergraduate students, graduate students, and postdoctoral researchers in
quantum algorithms, synthesis, spectroscopy, microscopy, and theory. The work strengthened the interface
of quantum information science with chemistry, materials, and biology and delivered new conceptual and
methodological tools for coherent and strongly correlated chemical systems.

Project Goals
1. Development of quantum algorithms for open quantum dynamics with application in understanding
 chemical and biological quantum systems and processes.
2. Development of the mathematical theory of the qubit/qudit quantum space and with potential
 applications.
3. Use quantum mechanical properties for coherent control of chemical reactions.
4. Predict and design strongly correlated molecules and materials that are energy and information efficient.
5. Design quantum-computing algorithms for strongly correlated molecules and open quantum systems.
6. Generate new quantum molecules and materials for molecular qubit candidates
7. Perform a comparative study of different ansatzes used for finding the ground state energy of simple
 molecules.
8. Synthesize multi-qubit arrays as well as stacked arrays
9. Develop super-resolution and temperature-dependent transient absorption microscopy to image exciton
 and polariton transport

Major Accomplishments
Open quantum systems and algorithm development
developing quantum algorithms for simulating open quantum dynamics, we have applied our home-
developed quantum algorithm to simulating the radical pair dynamics of the avian compass on the IBM
quantum simulator. This work not only demonstrates the generality of the quantum algorithm but also

 Page 2

helps bridging the gap between applying the tools of quantum information science to the investigation of
new areas of quantum biology.
We developed a hybrid quantum-classical approach for a class of dynamics called random unitary channels,
which allows us to classically sample a set of unitary dynamics via a probability distribution, and run the
selected unitaries on a quantum computer. These require no environmental overhead and allow us to
characterize the state and associated quantities via standard statistical techniques. The method is a
generalization of classical sampling techniques related to state vector or density matrix simulation, but
circumvents the exponential overhead of these techniques by carrying out those computations on a
quantum device. We show these can be adapted for simulating large channels, as well as propagating
Markovian dynamics as a classical product of i.i.d. channels with constant sampling cost.
We developed open-system quantum sensing and simulation methods through collaboration between the
Kais and Mazziotti groups and coworkers, using reduced observables and device noise to detect and
emulate environmental dynamics without full tomography.
We synthesized the emerging field of quantum algorithms for open quantum systems in an extensive
review article in Chemical Reviews through collaboration among the Kais, Narang, and Mazziotti groups
and coworkers, connecting theory, methods, and applications across chemistry, materials, and biology.

Quantum information theory, tomography, and learning
The project further advanced mathematical structures of the qubit/qudit quantum space, we have the
following discoveries:
The wave-particle duality of the qudit space and the quantum wave gates. Extending the “quantum
condition space” study to more general cases on the qudit space, we found the wave-particle duality of the
qudit space, which is analogous to the wave-particle duality in fundamental quantum physics. This then
allows us to classify all conventional quantum gates as wave-like objects, and then define a “quantum
version” of the quantum gates, which we call the “quantum wave gates”. The wave-particle duality of the
qudit space and the quantum wave gates open entirely new ways of understanding the mathematical
structure of quantum computing space.
The “qubit functional configuration theory” and the “unitary dependence theory” as originally developed
last year have been refined and the results have been improved. The papers have been published in peer-
reviewed journals.
Hamiltonian learning from time dynamics using variational algorithms. We present a general and efficient
scheme for reconstructing the Hamiltonian of a quantum system using variational algorithms and time
series data of observables. The Hamiltonian is reconstructed in the Pauli basis, and the time propagation is
implemented through Trotterization and optimized variationally with gradients computed on a quantum
circuit. The authors validate their approach by reproducing the dynamics of unseen observables on a
randomly chosen state not used for optimization. Additionally, they extend their protocol to quantum state
learning, solving the reverse problem of learning the state given time series data of observables generated
against multiple Hamiltonian dynamics.
Dimension reduction and redundancy removal through successive Schmidt decompositions. We explore the
use of successive Schmidt decompositions for dimension reduction and redundancy removal in the context
of quantum computers, aiming to map classical data onto quantum states efficiently and approximate
quantum operations in forms easier to simulate on classical computers.
Variational approach to quantum state tomography based on maximal entropy formalism. We present a
variational approach to quantum state tomography using the maximal entropy formalism, aiming to
reconstruct the quantum states of a quantum system with high fidelity based on mean measurement values
 Page 3

of different operators obtained from real quantum devices. The approach not only constructs a least-biased
quantum state but also replicates the exact state prepared experimentally within a preset.
Quantum machine learning for chemistry and physics. We provide an overview of the applications of
machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) in the field of physical sciences, particularly chemistry and
physics, highlighting the advancements made in classical ML as well as ML algorithms trainable on near-
term quantum hardware. ML algorithms trained on near-term quantum hardware have shown promising
results, revolutionizing material design, photo-voltaics, electronic structure calculations, force-field
computation, chemical reaction dynamics, drug design strategies, and phase classification. This review
article emphasizes the contributions made by both classical and quantum computing enhancements in
achieving these advancements.
We construct a classical analog of bipartite entanglement in terms of the mutual information between
phase-space distributions of subsystems and find completely analogous signatures of chaos as those found
in entanglement for the kicked top Hamiltonian.
We have introduced the qubit information logic (QIL) theory that uses the qubit information equation (QIE)
and logic to describe correlations in multi-qubit entanglements. Starting with the Bell states, we introduced
the global information status and local information availability that provided an alternative and intuitive
interpretation of the spooky action and the quantum no-communication theorem.
We propose a modulator-assisted scheme that enables indirect control of energy transfer using only local
operations on a third system, the modulator, which interacts only with the charger. The charger-battery
interaction remains always on and requires no direct control during the charging cycle. Instead, repeated
unitary pulses applied to the modulator exploit the quantum Zeno effect to dynamically modulate the
effective coupling strength, thereby controlling the battery's charging speed. Our protocol enables the
potential of remote, platform-independent control of energy flow and provides a framework for
engineering interactions in spatially distributed quantum technologies.

Strongly correlated quantum simulation and reduced-density-matrix methods
We developed a new excited-state algorithm which extends a ground-state approach (the contracted
quantum eigensolver) for excited states. We generalize this by projecting out the obtained states at each
step, allowing us to iteratively solve for states. The primary result applies this to a model system with
challenging electron correlation, rectangular hydrogen, allowing us to probe the limits of the approach.
Importantly, our method allowed for high-accuracy results with low iterations across a wide range of
states, with scaling dependent on only the two-body reduced density matrix (i.e. quartic in the number of
atomic orbitals). Because our method allows us to accurately locate each state, we are able to identify
challenges of the two-body unitary ansatz even in a relatively small system, providing insights for algorithm
design.
We advanced contracted-quantum-eigensolver methods for strongly correlated quantum simulation in the
Mazziotti group and with collaborators, including algorithms for excited states, bosons, fermion-boson
systems, conical intersections, and polaritonic chemistry.
We advanced scalable reduced-density-matrix and shadow-tomography methods for quantum simulation
in the Mazziotti group and with collaborators, enabling fewer measurements and shallower circuits for
molecular calculations on quantum devices.
We demonstrated that reduced density matrices reveal emergent quantum order and criticality through
work led by the Mazziotti group and collaborators on exciton condensation, superconducting-to-excitonic
transitions, and topological phase transitions.

We introduced new quantum diagnostics of device capability and openness in the Mazziotti group and with
collaborators, including qubit condensation for benchmarking entanglement and generalized-Pauli-
constraint violations for detecting open-system effects.

Molecular qubit candidates and quantum sensing materials
we have made significant progress in this area. Initially, and through collaborations with the Mazziotti
group, we have predicted that the diradical character for TTFtt compounds which we previously observed
only for Fe is in fact quite general across a wide variety of dinuclear compounds. This is an important result,
as it suggests that we, in principle, can generate a large family of tunable qubit candidates. The project
further examined whether there was any luminescence from these systems. Surprisingly, in the previous
reporting period we have discovered that Pt and Pd complexes of TTFtt display bright NIR fluorescence,
among the very best yet reported. The NIR region is particularly exciting as it is generally tissue
transparent, opening up exciting new possibilities in biological or medical imaging. More importantly, the
bright luminescence of these compounds opens up the possibility of using them as optically gated qubits.
A key step in evaluating qubit suitability was examining the lifetimes of these compounds. Here too, we
have been thrilled to find that these systems have remarkably good lifetimes, up to microseconds in
solution at room-temperature. This suggests that not only should these molecules be good qubit candidates
at cryogenic temperatures, but that they should also be viable candidates in solution and at room
temperature. In fact, we have also found that these compounds are air and water stable. Excitingly, we have
been acquiring initial optically-detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) data on these compounds. This data
tentatively shows an ODMR response and we are following up on this initial hit. This is the first time that
ODMR has been successfully observed in solution at ambient conditions.
In separate work, we have been investigating how the TTFtt unit imbues unique materials properties on
coordination polymers. Interestingly, we have found that the material NiTTFtt exhibits unusual charge
transport behavior, most consistent with it being a glassy metal (Figure 2). Importantly, this material is
completely amorphous, so the existence of high conductivity is surprising. Theory collaborations with
Mazziotti suggest that the origin of this metallic character is promiscuous pi bonding even with a high
degree of structural disorder. Excitingly, we have collaborated with Mazziotti to also predict evidence of
exciton condensation or even superconductivity in these materials, areas which we are now investigating.
Finally, we have also shown that the properties of these materials can be tuned by redox doping which
turns on exciting photothermal applications. Another area of investigation, again undertaken
collaboratively, will be to investigate how doping with other metals tunes physical properties.

Exciton transport and quantum optical microscopy
We have demonstrated environment-assisted quantum transport of exciton in molecular aggregates. The
transport of excitation energy through interacting systems plays a pivotal role in various critical processes,
ranging from light harvesting in photosynthesis to transport in networks of qubits. In this study, we
demonstrate the transport of delocalized excitons in molecular aggregates with unambiguous signatures of
environment-assisted quantum transport. By directly imaging exciton propagation with high spatial and
temporal resolutions over a wide temperature range, we elucidated the interplay between coupling,
Anderson localization, and dephasing.
We have successfully demonstrated the remarkable suppression of exciton-exciton annihilation (EEA)
through the implementation of quantum interference effects in two-exciton pathways. Traditionally, EEA
has been perceived as an incoherent process limited by diffusion, and it has significant implications for
optoelectronic devices and photosynthetic complexes. However, we challenge this conventional assumption
by experimentally showcasing our ability to control EEA in molecular aggregates by leveraging the
quantum phase relationships of excitons. Our approach involved utilizing time-resolved photoluminescence

 Page 5

microscopy to independently determine the diffusion constants and annihilation rates of two substituted
perylene diimide aggregates with distinct excitonic phase envelopes. Remarkably, we observed a difference
of over two orders of magnitude in the low-temperature EEA rates between the two compounds, despite
their similar diffusion constants. By employing a microscopic theory and performing simulations that align
excellently with our experimental results, we provide a rational explanation for this behavior. The origin of
this unique EEA modulation arises from quantum interference resulting from the presence or absence of
spatial phase oscillations of delocalized excitons. These findings introduce an entirely novel avenue for
designing molecular materials using quantum phase engineering, where low annihilation rates can coexist
with high exciton concentrations and mobilities, thereby opening up exciting possibilities for future
applications.

Impact
The quantum simulation of the radical pair dynamics of the avian compass not only demonstrates the
generality of our self-developed quantum algorithm but also helps bridging the gap between applying the
tools of quantum information science to the investigation of new areas of quantum biology.
The wave-particle duality of the qudit space and the quantum wave gates open entirely new ways of
understanding and characterizing the mathematical structure of quantum computing space.
The studies on variational quantum algorithms and more generally on quantum machine learning
techniques lead to important advancements in Hamiltonian simulation and quantum state tomography.
 Page 12

On a fundamental level, we are revealing the principles that are required for quantum sensing in the
solution phase. This is an exciting new direction in the area of quantum technologies, but our complexes are
the first that have demonstrated this exciting principle. On a broader level, if we can successfully apply our
samples, we have the chance to transform imaging in biological systems through the sensitivity of qubit
sensors.
Our results establish an understanding of exciton transport in natural and biomimetic photosynthetic
systems. These results will build the fundamental knowledge base necessary for realizing the potential of
these systems for solar energy harvesting.
Development of quantum light microscopy provides a new method to measure exciton transport.
We demonstrate that for the generalized depolarizing channel, our hybrid sampling method outperforms
traditional ancilla-based methods in both accuracy and scalability, as well as the simulation of non-trivial
states.
