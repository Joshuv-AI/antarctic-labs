I
Introduction

The charmonium states below the open-charm threshold have been studied extensively, but questions about their spin-singlet states, for example, the P-wave state
h_{c}
and the S-wave ground state
\eta_{c}
, remain unresolved. Although the
\eta_{c}
meson has been investigated for nearly four decades since its discovery
Ref_etacdis
, most of its decay modes remain unidentified. Even for the known channels, some of their measured branching fractions (BFs) suffer from considerable uncertainties. Early measurements of the
\eta_{c}
BFs in
J/\psi
and
\psi(3686)
decays did not sufficiently take into account the interference effects between resonant and non-resonant processes. More recent studies, such as the coherent amplitude analysis of
\eta_{c}\to 2(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})\eta

Ref_yxq
, have partially addressed this limitation.

Experimental studies on exclusive charmonium decays play an important role in testing the perturbative quantum chromodynamics (pQCD). The complexity of QCD remains unsolved in the charmonium mass region. There are still many contradictions between pQCD calculations and experimental measurements. In particular, the pQCD helicity selection rule
Ref_pqrd1
;
Ref_pqrd2
;
Ref_pqrd3
is violated in many exclusive charmonium decay processes. Charmonium decay processes with baryon anti-baryon final states, such as
\eta_{c}/h_{c}\to B_{8}{\bar{B}_{8}}
, where
B_{8}{\bar{B}_{8}}
denotes the octet baryon anti-baryon pair, are supposed to be highly suppressed according to the pQCD helicity selection rule. Theoretical predictions on the BFs of
\eta_{c}/h_{c}\to B_{8}\bar{B}_{8}
have been made based on a model consideringthe long distance contribution via charmed hadron loops
Ref_bb
. Some experimental measurements agree with the theoretical predictions, e.g.
\eta_{c}\to p\bar{p},\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
and
\Xi^{-}\bar{\Xi}^{+}
; while others do not, e.g.
\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{+}\Sigma^{-}
. Meanwhile, its isospin partner
\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
has not been observed. A study of this decay channel would allow for a test of isospin invariance. In addition, theoretical predictions for the
h_{c}
have not been tested due to the lack of experimental information, since its predominant decay mode is the electric dipole (E1) transition of
h_{c}\to\gamma\eta_{c}
. Recently, BESIII measured this E1 transition BF to be
(57.66^{+3.62}_{-3.50}\pm 0.58)\%

Ref_htoeta
, which is reasonably consistent with some theoretical predictions
Ref_llh1
;
Ref_llh2
. This implies that nearly half of the
h_{c}
decays proceed via a non-E1 transition. However, the decay modes of
h_{c}
remain poorly understood experimentally, even after decades of study. Currently, the sum of measured BFs, excluding the
\gamma\eta_{c}
channel, amounts to only about
6\%

Ref_pdg
. Up to now, no
B_{8}\bar{B}_{8}
final state has ever been observed.

In this paper, we present the first observation of
\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
via
\psi(3686)\to\gamma\eta_{c}
and search for
h_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
via
\psi(3686)\to\pi^{0}h_{c}
by analyzing
(2712.4\pm 14.3)\times 10^{6}~\psi(3686)
events collected by the BESIII detector
Ref_total
.

EVENT SELECTION

The decays of interest in this analysis are the cascade processes
\psi(3686)\to\gamma\eta_{c}
with
\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
, and
\psi(3686)\to\pi^{0}h_{c}
with
h_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}

\Sigma^{0}\to\gamma\Lambda
(

) and
\bar{\Sigma}^{0}\to\gamma\bar{\Lambda}
(
\bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{p}\pi^{+}
). Charged tracks with the polar angle (
\theta
) in the range of
|\cos\theta|<0.93
are detected in the MDC. Since
\Lambda/\bar{\Lambda}
contains secondary vertices, no interaction vertex requirement is applied on candidate tracks. Each charged track is identified using particle identification (PID), which combines measurements of the energy deposited in the MDC (d
E
/d
x
) and the flight time in the TOF to form likelihoods
\mathcal{L}(h)~(h=p,K,\pi)
for each hadron
h
hypothesis. Tracks are identified as protons when the proton hypothesis has the greatest likelihood (
\mathcal{L}(p)>\mathcal{L}(K)
and
\mathcal{L}(p)>\mathcal{L}(\pi)
), while charged kaons and pions are identified by comparing the likelihoods for the kaon and pion hypotheses,
\mathcal{L}(K)>\mathcal{L}(\pi)
and
\mathcal{L}(\pi)>\mathcal{L}(K)
, respectively. At least four charged particles,
p\bar{p}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, need to be identified.

Photon candidates are reconstructed from neutral showers in the EMC. The deposited energy of each candidate is required to be greater than 25 MeV for the barrel region
(|\cos\theta|<0.80)
and 50 MeV for the end cap region
(0.86<|\cos\theta|<0.92)
.To suppress electronic noise and showers unrelated to the event, the difference between the EMC time and the event start time is required to be within
[0, 700] ns.

For the process
\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
, it is required that there are at least three photons. A four-constraint (4C) kinematic fit is performed
under the
\psi(3686)\to 3\gamma p\bar{p}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
hypothesis. If there are multiple combinations of
3\gamma p\bar{p}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

\chi^{2}_{\rm 4C}
is chosen.
Furthermore,

is required, which is obtained by optimizing the figure-of-merit (FOM), defined as
S/\sqrt{S+B}
. Here,
S
denotes the number of signal MC events scaled with the expected branching fraction, while
B
denotes the number of background events from the inclusive MC sample.

MC statistics

The uncertainties due to MC statistics are assigned by

\epsilon

VI.8
Mass fit

For the
\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
decay mode, the uncertainties caused by mass fit include the components listed below.

Damping function
: The systematic uncertainty in the damping function is estimated by changing the KEDR form
\frac{E_{0}^{2}}{E_{\gamma}E_{0}+(E_{\gamma}-E_{0})^{2}}

Ref_kedr
to the CLEO form written as
e^{-\frac{E_{\gamma}^{2}}{8\beta^{2}}}

Ref_cleo
.

NRB shape
: The uncertainty from NRB shape is estimated by varying the parameters of the PDF, modeled by a third-order Chebyshev polynomial, from fixed to floating values.

Sideband region
: The systematic uncertainty associated with the 2D
\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
sideband background is estimated by zooming in and out the sideband regions by 10 MeV/
c^{2}
around the
\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
mass window.

Mass and width of
\eta_{c}
: The uncertainty caused by the
\eta_{c}
mass and width is determined by changing the nominal value by one standard deviation. The difference relative to the nominal result is taken as the systematic uncertainty.

For the
h_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
decay mode, the uncertainties caused by mass fit include the sources listed below.

Signal shape
: The uncertainty from signal shape is estimated by convolving the MC-determined shape with a Gaussian function characterized by free parameters. The difference in the final results is taken as the systematic uncertainty.

Background shape
: To estimate the uncertainty associated with the description of the background, its shape in the fit is changed from the Argus function to a second-order polynomial function with free parameters. The difference in the result is assigned as the systematic uncertainty.

\Sigma^{0}

h_{c}

1+\cos^{2}\theta/1-\cos^{2}\theta

VII
Summary

Using
(2712.4\pm 14.3)\times 10^{6}~\psi(3686)
events collected by the BESIII detector, the
\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
decay is observed for the first time. Its BF is measured to be
\mathcal{B}(\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0})=(2.59\pm 0.14(stat)\pm 0.44(syst))\times 10^{-3}
for destructive interference and
(1.18\pm 0.12(stat)\pm 0.21(syst))\times 10^{-3}
for constructive interference. In addition, we search for the decay
h_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
for the first time and no significant signal is observed. The upper limit of its BF is determined to be
\mathcal{B}(h_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0})<1.02\times 10^{-4}
at the 90% C.L. The obtained results are inconsistent with theoretical calculations, which give
(4.82\sim 9.56)\times 10^{-4}
for
\mathcal{B}(\eta_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0})
and
(5.57\sim 7.08)\times 10^{-4}
for
\mathcal{B}(h_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0})

Ref_bb
, assuming the isospin conservation between charged and neutral modes.
