Proof of the Finiteness of the Chromatic Number of Two-Dimensional Lacunary Integer Distance Graphs
Nabh Singh

Abstract
We extend the one-dimensional lonely set method to two dimensions for the purpose of studying the chromatic number of integer distance graphs in two dimensions. Given a lacunary sequence of displacement vectors in ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} , we use a lacunary matrix theorem given by Broderick, Fishman and Kleinbock, to prove the existence of a satisfactory multiplier vector. We then give an explicit geometric colouring argument. This proves that any integer distance graph generated by a lacunary sequence of vectors in two dimensions has finite chromatic number.



1
Introduction

Let

D=\{\mathbf{d}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}

be a sequence of distinct displacement vectors. The integer distance graph
G(\mathbb{Z}^{2},D)
is defined as the graph with vertex set
V=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where an undirected edge exists between
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
if and only if

\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\in D\quad\text{or}\quad\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}\in D.

In the one-dimensional setting, where
V=\mathbb{Z}
, it is a celebrated result of Katznelson
[
1
]
and Peres–Schlag
[
2
]
that integer distance graphs associated with lacunary distance sets have finite chromatic number. In this note, we prove an analogous finiteness statement for displacement vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
satisfying exponential growth in Euclidean norm.

Definition 1

(2D lacunary sequence)
.

A sequence of vectors

D=\{\mathbf{d}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}

is said to be lacunary if there exists a constant
r>1
such that, for all
k\geq 1
,

\left\|\mathbf{d}_{k+1}\right\|_{2}\geq r\left\|\mathbf{d}_{k}\right\|_{2},

(1)

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
denotes the Euclidean norm.

2
Lonely vector sets and torus-bin colourings

Let

\left\|x\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}=\min_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|x-m\right|

denote the distance from
x\in\mathbb{R}
to the nearest integer.

Definition 2

(Lonely vector set)
.

A distance set
D\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is called
lonely
if there exist a multiplier vector

\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in[0,1)^{2}

and a constant
\delta>0
such that

\left\|\langle\mathbf{d},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\geq\delta\qquad\forall\mathbf{d}\in D,

(2)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard inner product.

We now show that loneliness implies finite colourability. The proof uses a two-dimensional torus-bin construction.

Lemma 1

(Torus-bin colouring lemma)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
be a lonely vector set with gap
\delta>0
. Then

\chi(G(\mathbb{Z}^{2},D))\leq\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2}{\delta}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2}.

(3)

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in[0,1)^{2}
satisfy

\left\|\langle\mathbf{d},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\geq\delta\qquad\forall\mathbf{d}\in D.

Define the coordinate-wise torus map

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}

by

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v})=(v_{1}\alpha_{1}\bmod 1,\;v_{2}\alpha_{2}\bmod 1).

(4)

We use the
\ell_{\infty}
metric on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, defined by

\rho_{\infty}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\max_{i=1,2}\left\|x_{i}-y_{i}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}.

Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be adjacent vertices in
G(\mathbb{Z}^{2},D)
. Then

\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}=\pm\mathbf{d}

for some
\mathbf{d}\in D
. Write

a_{i}=(u_{i}-v_{i})\alpha_{i}\qquad(i=1,2).

Then

\langle\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle=a_{1}+a_{2}.

By the triangle inequality on
\mathbb{T}
,

\left\|a_{1}+a_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\left\|a_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}+\left\|a_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}.

Therefore

\left\|a_{1}+a_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq 2\max\{\left\|a_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}},\left\|a_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\}.

\implies\max\{\left\|a_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}},\left\|a_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|a_{1}+a_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}.

(5)

Since

a_{1}+a_{2}=\langle\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle,

we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}\!\left(\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{u}),\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v})\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{i=1,2}\left\|(u_{i}-v_{i})\alpha_{i}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}

(6)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\langle\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\langle\mathbf{d},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}

(8)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.

(9)

Thus adjacent vertices are separated by at least
\delta/2
in the
\ell_{\infty}
torus metric.

Now choose

M=\left\lfloor\frac{2}{\delta}\right\rfloor+1.

Then,

\frac{1}{M}<\frac{\delta}{2}.

Partition
[0,1)^{2}
into
M^{2}
half-open square bins

Q_{ij}=\left[\frac{i}{M},\frac{i+1}{M}\right)\times\left[\frac{j}{M},\frac{j+1}{M}\right),

where
0\leq i,j\leq M-1
.

Assign one colour to each square bin. Namely, define

c:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{0,\dots,M-1\}^{2}

by

c(\mathbf{v})=(i,j)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v})\in Q_{ij}

If two vertices
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
receive the same colour, then their images lie in the same square bin. In other words,

\rho_{\infty}\!\left(\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{u}),\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v})\right)<\frac{1}{M}<\frac{\delta}{2}

However, adjacent vertices satisfy

\rho_{\infty}\!\left(\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{u}),\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v})\right)\geq\frac{\delta}{2}

which is a contradiction.
Thus
c
is a proper colouring of
G(\mathbb{Z}^{2},D)
using
M^{2}
colours, and

\chi(G(\mathbb{Z}^{2},D))\leq M^{2}=\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2}{\delta}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2}

∎

3
Existence of a lonely multiplier

We now use an existing theorem on lacunary matrix sequences to obtain the multiplier vector
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
.

Theorem 1

(Broderick–Fishman–Kleinbock, lacunary matrix case)
.

Let
(M_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a lacunary sequence of real
m\times n
matrices. Let
(Z_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of uniformly discrete subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then, the set

\widetilde{E}(M,Z)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\inf_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{dist}(M_{k}\mathbf{x},Z_{k})>0\right\}

is nonempty.

Proposition 1
.

Let

D=\{\mathbf{d}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}

be lacunary. Then
D
is lonely.

Proof.

Write

\mathbf{d}_{k}=(x_{k},y_{k}).

Regard
\mathbf{d}_{k}
as a
1\times 2
real matrix

M_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}&y_{k}\end{pmatrix}.

For
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

M_{k}\boldsymbol{\alpha}=x_{k}\alpha_{1}+y_{k}\alpha_{2}=\langle\mathbf{d}_{k},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle.

Moreover, the operator norm of
M_{k}
is exactly

\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}=\left\|\mathbf{d}_{k}\right\|_{2}.

Since
D
is lacunary, there exists
r>1
such that

\left\|\mathbf{d}_{k+1}\right\|_{2}\geq r\left\|\mathbf{d}_{k}\right\|_{2}

for every
k
. Hence

\left\|M_{k+1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\geq r\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}.

Thus
(M_{k})
is a lacunary sequence of
1\times 2
real matrices.

Now take

Z_{k}=\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}

for all
k
. The set
\mathbb{Z}
is uniformly discrete. By the Broderick–Fishman–Kleinbock theorem, there exists

\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

such that

\inf_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{dist}(M_{k}\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathbb{Z})>0.

Therefore there exists
\delta>0
such that

\operatorname{dist}(M_{k}\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathbb{Z})\geq\delta\qquad\forall k

Equivalently,

\left\|\langle\mathbf{d}_{k},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\geq\delta\qquad\forall k

Replace
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
by its coordinate-wise reduction modulo
1
, which lies in
[0,1)^{2}
. Since each
\mathbf{d}_{k}
has integer coordinates, this does not change

\langle\mathbf{d}_{k},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\pmod{1}

Thus there exist
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in[0,1)^{2}
and
\delta>0
such that

\left\|\langle\mathbf{d}_{k},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\geq\delta\qquad\forall k

That is,
D
is lonely.
∎

4
Main theorem

Theorem 2
.

Let

D=\{\mathbf{d}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}

be a lacunary sequence of vectors. Then the integer distance graph
G(\mathbb{Z}^{2},D)
has finite chromatic number.

Proof.

By the above Proposition 1, the lacunary sequence
D
is lonely. Hence there exist

\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in[0,1)^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta>0

such that

\left\|\langle\mathbf{d},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\geq\delta\qquad\forall\mathbf{d}\in D.

Using the torus-bin colouring lemma, we get

\chi(G(\mathbb{Z}^{2},D))\leq\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2}{\delta}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2}

Therefore
G(\mathbb{Z}^{2},D)
has finite chromatic number.
∎
