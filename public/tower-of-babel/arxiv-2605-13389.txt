We establish the optimal convergence of solutions to integro-differential equations (IDEs) governed by symmetric integrodifferential p p -Lévy operators, 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , in the presence of nonlocal Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions. For illustrative purposes, consider the particular case of the (fractional) p p -Laplacian ( − Δ ) p s (-\Delta)^{s}_{p} with 0 < s ≤ 1 0<s\leq 1 . Another subsequent goal underpinning our approach is the robustness of the nonlocal trace spaces; specifically, we also show that the nonlocal trace spaces converge, in an appropriate sense, to the local trace space.

2.
Convergence of nonlocal forms

In this section, we discuss in various perspectives the convergence of nonlocal energy forms. More importantly,are interested in the asymptotic of the energy forms: max form (or special form)
\cE^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
, plus form
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\cdot,\cdot)
, Gagliardo form
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(\cdot,\cdot)
and boundary crossing form
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(\cdot,\cdot)
and local gradient form
\cE^{0}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\iint_{(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})^{c}}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x))\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{+}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega\R^{d}}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))(v(x)-v(y))\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega\Omega}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x))\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega\Omega^{c}}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))(v(x)-v(y))\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{0}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla v(x)\mathrm{d}x.

Note in passing that if when
\Omega=\R^{d}
then
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)=\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{+}(u,v)=\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u,v)
. For a general measurable kernel
k:\R^{d}\times\R^{d}\setminus\diag\to[0\,,\infty)
, we introduce the nonlinear nonlocal form

\displaystyle\cE_{k}(u,v):=\iint_{\R^{d}\R^{d}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))k(x,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x.

It is important to keep in mind that Hölder’s inequality implies that

(2.1)

\displaystyle|\cE_{k}(u,v)|\leq\cE_{k}(u,u)^{1/p^{\prime}}\cE_{k}(v,v)^{1/p}.

Remark 2.1
.

The nonlocal forms
\cE^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(\cdot,\cdot)
merely appear as special instances of the nonlocal form
\cE_{k}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Indeed one easily verifies that

\displaystyle\cE_{k}(u,v)=\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,v)\quad\text{when}\quad k(x,y)

\displaystyle=\max(\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(x),\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(y))\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y),

\displaystyle\cE_{k}(u,v)=\cE^{\varepsilon}_{+}(u,v)\quad\text{when}\quad k(x,y)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(x)+\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(y))\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y),

\displaystyle\cE_{k}(u,v)=\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u,v)\quad\text{when}\quad k(x,y)

\displaystyle=\min(\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(x),\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(y))\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y),

\displaystyle\cE_{k}(u,v)=\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u,v)\quad\text{when}\quad k(x,y)

\displaystyle=\min(\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(x),\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(y))\nu(x-y).

Furthermore, in each of these four examples, one obtains the following uniform estimate

(2.2)

\displaystyle\cE_{k}(u,u)\leq\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\R^{d}}(u,u)\leq 2^{p}\|u\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\R^{d})},\qquad\text{$u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})$},

independently on
\varepsilon
. This is readily implied by the fact that
\int_{\R^{d}}(1\land|h|^{p})\nu_{\varepsilon}(h)\mathrm{d}h=1
and the following Taylor expansion

\displaystyle\int_{\R^{d}}|u(x)-u(x+h)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{p}(1\land|h|^{p})\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\R^{d})}.

Let us recall that an open set (not necessarily bounded)

\displaystyle Eu\mid_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=u\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|Eu\|_{W^{1,p}(\R^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}\quad u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

Examples of extension domains include, the half space
\R^{d}_{+}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\R^{d}\,:x_{d}>0\}
and bounded Lipschitz domains. In particular euclidean balls, rectangles in

are extension domains. The geometrical characterization of extension domains has been extensively studied in the last decades. It turns out that, the

[

W^{1,p}
-extension domain
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is a
d
-set, i.e., there exists a constant

such that

\displaystyle|\Omega\cap B(x,r)|\geq cr^{d}\qquad\text{for all $x\in\partial\Omega$ and $0<r<1$.}

Several references on extension domains for Sobolev spaces can be found in
[
56
]
.
It becomes clear in the sequel, thanks to the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem, that if
\Omega
is a
d
-set, then it is a Jordan set

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is a

-set then
\partial\Omega
has Lebesgue measure zero, i.e.,
|\partial\Omega|=0.

Proof.

Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Omega=\partial\Omega
and consider
(x_{j})_{j}\subset\Omega
such that
x_{j}\to x
as

so that

\displaystyle|\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)|=\lim_{j\to\infty}|\Omega\cap B_{r}(x_{j})|\geq cr^{d}.

Thereby, the

we have

\displaystyle 1>\frac{|\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}\geq\frac{cr^{d}}{|B_{r}(x)|}=\frac{c}{|B_{1}(0)|}=:c_{1}>0.

Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Omega)\cap B(x,r)|}{|B(x,r)|}=\frac{|\overline{\Omega}\cap B(x,r)|}{|B(x,r)|}-\frac{|\Omega\cap B(x,r)|}{|B(x,r)|}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\mathds{1}_{\partial\Omega}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Omega)\cap B(x,r)|}{|B(x,r)|}\leq 1-c_{1}<1.

It turns out that each

However it is well-known that, the set of non-Lebesgue points of

\displaystyle 2\cE_{cr}^{\varepsilon}(u+t\sigma v,u+t\sigma v)-2\cE_{cr}^{\varepsilon}(u,u)-2pt\cE_{cr}^{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma v)\geq 0.

Accordingly, passing to the
\lim\hskip-2.15277pt\sup
as
\varepsilon\to 0
yields

\displaystyle\frac{K_{d,p}}{t}\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(|\nabla(u+t\sigma v)(x)|^{p}-|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\geq 2p\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE_{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma v).

Letting

and taking
\sigma=\pm 1
imply

\sigma pK_{d,p}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla v(x)\mathrm{d}x\geq 2p\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sigma\cE_{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}(u,v),

yielding that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}2\cE_{cr}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)=K_{d,p}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla v(x)\mathrm{d}x.

In particular the right hand side vanishes when
|\partial\Omega|=0
.
Now in the case
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, by density argument, it is sufficient to assume that
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. To this end, consider
\delta=\dist(\partial\Omega,\supp v)>0
so that by using (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u,v)\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u,u)^{1/p^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(v,v)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\|u\|^{p/p^{\prime}}_{W^{1,p}(\R^{d})}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big(\int_{|h|>\delta}\nu_{\varepsilon}(h)\mathrm{d}h\Big)^{1/p}=0.

∎

Remark 2.9
.

The condition
\partial\Omega=\partial\overline{\Omega}
prevents
\Omega
from being on both sides of the domain; and hence rules out domains with slits on both
\Omega
and
\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. A simple example of domain with a slit is

\Omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in B_{1}(0):x_{d}\neq 0\}
whose slit is the set
S=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in B_{1}(0)\,:\,x_{d}=0\}
and for which
\partial(\R^{d}\setminus\Omega)=\partial\Omega=\partial B_{1}(0)\cup S
, whereas
\partial\overline{\Omega}=\partial\overline{B_{1}(0)}=\partial B_{1}(0)
.

As a consequence of Lemma

, we obtain the next result.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
v\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
. Assume that
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
or that
|\partial\Omega|=0
.
Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
,
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
be an asymptotically bounded sequence, that is,

\displaystyle M:=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})<\infty.

Then
(\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u_{\varepsilon},v))_{\varepsilon}
and
(\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(v,u_{\varepsilon}))_{\varepsilon}
collapse across the boundary, meaning that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u_{\varepsilon},v)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(v,u_{\varepsilon})=0.

Proof.

Put
\psi(t)=|t|^{p-2}t
. Using the inequality (

) and Lemma
2.8
we get

\displaystyle\Big(\iint_{\Omega\Omega^{c}}\big|\psi(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(y))(v(x)-v(y))\big|\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x)^{p}

\displaystyle+\Big(\iint_{\Omega\Omega^{c}}\big|\psi(

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))(v(x)-v(y))\big|\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(M^{p^{\prime}/p}+M^{p/p^{\prime}})\Big(\iint_{\Omega\Omega^{c}}|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0\,.

∎

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
be open. Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\big)<\infty.

If

for

for

\displaystyle K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for}\quad 1<p<\infty

\displaystyle K_{d,1}|u|_{BV(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for}\quad p=1.

Proof.

By Fatou’s Lemma,
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, i.e.
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Let

[

[

\displaystyle K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\iint_{\Omega_{j}\Omega_{j}}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{p}\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

Letting

Theorem 2.12

Let

Assume either that one of the following conditions

Remark 2.13

It is worth mentioning that, the convergence above is achieved when either

is sufficiently regular, or when

As illustrated in

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{+}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u,v)+\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u,v).

Thence, it is sufficient to establish the result solely for

\displaystyle\cE_{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}(u+t\sigma v,u+t\sigma v)-\cE_{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}(u,u)-pt\cE_{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma v)\geq 0\qquad\text{$t>0$,\, $\sigma\in\R$}.

2.5.
Asymptotic demi-convergence

Now we investigate the asymptotics of the nonlocal forms
\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,v)
and
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u,v)
when
u
or
v
also depends on
\varepsilon
. Let us begin with the following elementary result.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
a,b\in\R
,
t\neq 0
and
R>0
.The following assertions are true.

(1)

For all
a,b\in\R
, there holds that

\displaystyle|a+b|^{p}=|a|^{p}+p|a|^{p-2}ab+O(|b|^{p})+\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}|a|^{p-2k}a^{k}b^{k},

where
\lfloor p\rfloor=\max\{m\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,m\leq p\}
and
\binom{p}{k}=\frac{\Gamma(p+1)}{k!\Gamma(p-k+1)}
.

(2)

If
1<p<2
then for all
|b|\leq R
and
a,t\in\R
we have

\displaystyle|a+tb|^{p}=|a|^{p}+pt|a|^{p-2}ab+O(|tR|^{p}).

If
p\geq 2
then for all
|a|\leq R
,
|b|\leq R
and all
|t|\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle|a+tb|^{p}=|a|^{p}+pt|a|^{p-2}ab+O(|t|^{2}R^{p}).

(3)

For all
|a|\leq R
,
|b|\leq R
and
|t|\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle|a+tb|^{p}-|a|^{p}-pt|a|^{p-2}ab=O(t^{\min(p,2)}R^{p}).

Note that the constant behind
O(\cdot)
only depends on
p
.

Proof.

Note that
(2)
and
(3)
are implied by
(1)
or can also be easily retrieved from Corollary
2.3
. Now, consider the continuous function
\zeta:\R\to\R
with

\displaystyle\zeta(t)=\frac{|1+t|^{p}-\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}t^{k}}{|t|^{p}}.

Note that, if
\lfloor p\rfloor<p
then
\zeta(t)\xrightarrow{|t|\to\infty}1
and if
p=\lfloor p\rfloor
then
\zeta(t)\xrightarrow{|t|\to\infty}0
,
whereas, using Taylor’s expansion for
|t|<1
we deduce

\displaystyle\zeta(t)=\frac{(1+t)^{p}-\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}t^{k}}{|t|^{p}}=\frac{\sum_{k=\lfloor p\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\binom{p}{k}t^{k}}{|t|^{p}}\xrightarrow{|t|\to 0}0.

Therefore, the map
t\mapsto\zeta(t)
is bounded say
|\zeta(t)|\leq C
for some
C>1
, yielding

\displaystyle|1+t|^{p}\leq C|t|^{p}+\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}t^{k}.

In other words we have

\displaystyle|1+t|^{p}=1+pt+\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}t^{k}+O(|t|^{p}).

From this, it is a routine to deduce
(1)
, that is, for all
a,b\in\R
we have

\displaystyle|a+b|^{p}=|a|^{p}+pb|a|^{p-2}a+O(|b|^{p})+\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}|a|^{p-2k}a^{k}b^{k}.

∎

The following result generalizes that of

[
22
, Lemma 2.8]
, yielding a sort of nonlocal-to-local asymptotic demi-convergence of the forms
\cE^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\cdot,\cdot)
to
\cE^{0}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
u,v\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
and the sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
v_{\varepsilon}\in W_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}^{p}(\Omega|\R^{d})
be such that
v_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\to v|_{\Omega}
in
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\big(\|v_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\cE^{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\big)<\infty.

Assume either that one of the following conditions
(i)
,
(ii)
or
(iii)
holds.

(1)

\Omega
is an
W^{1,p}
-extension domain.

(2)

\partial\Omega=\partial\overline{\Omega}
and
\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is an
W^{1,p}
-extension domain.

(3)

We have
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Then the following convergences hold true

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,v_{\varepsilon})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}_{+}(u,v_{\varepsilon})=K_{d,p}\cE^{0}(u,v)\quad\text{for all $u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})$}.

Proof.

We only prove for
\cE^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
since the one for
\cE^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\cdot,\cdot)
is analogous. We apply Lemma
2.15
with
a=U(x,y)=u(x)-u(y)
and
b=t\sigma V_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=t\sigma(v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y))
with
|t|\leq 1
and
\sigma=\pm 1
yielding

\displaystyle 0\leq\cE^{\varepsilon}(u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon},u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon})-\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)-p\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,t\sigma v_{\varepsilon})=O(t^{p}\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u))+F^{\varepsilon}_{p}(u,tv_{\varepsilon}).

The remainder
F^{\varepsilon}_{p}(u,tv_{\varepsilon})
is given by:
F^{\varepsilon}_{p}(u,tv_{\varepsilon})=0
for
1<p<2
or else by

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}(u,tv_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}t^{k}\iint_{(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})^{c}}\hskip-8.61108pt|U(x,y)|^{p-2k}U(x,y)^{k}V_{\varepsilon}(x,y)^{k}\nu_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x,

Using the Hölder inequality with
q=\frac{p}{p-k}
and
q^{\prime}=\frac{p}{k}
we obtain

\displaystyle|F^{\varepsilon}(u,tv_{\varepsilon})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}|t|^{k}\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)^{p-k}\cE^{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})^{k}.

Next, by (
2.2
) we have
\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)\leq 2^{p}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\R^{d})}
, while
(\cE^{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}))_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded. Thus, there exists
M>0
such that for
|t|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\cE^{\varepsilon}(u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon},u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon})\leq 2^{p}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\big(\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)+\cE^{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\big)\leq M.

From this and the penultimate estimate we deduce

\displaystyle|F^{\varepsilon}(u,tv_{\varepsilon})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\binom{p}{k}|t|^{k}\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)^{p-k}\cE^{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})^{k}\leq C|t|^{2},

for some generic constant
C>0
only depending on
M
and
p.
It follows that

\displaystyle 0\leq\cE^{\varepsilon}(u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon},u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle-\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)-pt\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma v_{\varepsilon})\leq C|t|^{p}+C|t|^{2}.

Therefore, for all
0<t<1
, we get that

\displaystyle\cE^{\varepsilon}(u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon},u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon})-\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)=pt\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma v_{\varepsilon})+O(t^{\min(p,2)}).

Observing that
u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon}\to u+t\sigma v
as
\varepsilon\to 0
in
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
and
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\big(\cE^{\varepsilon}(u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon},u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon})\big)\leq 2^{p}M
, we apply
Theorem
2.11
and Theorem
2.12
so that

\displaystyle p\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma v_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\cE^{\varepsilon}(u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon},u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon})-\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)}{t}-O(t^{\min(p-1,1)})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon},u+t\sigma v_{\varepsilon})-\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,u)}{t}-O(t^{\min(p-1,1)})

\displaystyle\geq K_{d,p}\frac{\cE^{0}(u+t\sigma v,u+t\sigma v)-\cE^{0}(u,u)}{t}-O(t^{\min(p-1,1)}).

Letting
t\to 0
yields
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,v_{\varepsilon})=K_{d,p}\cE^{0}(u,v)
since
\sigma=\pm 1
and

\displaystyle p\sigma\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}(u,v_{\varepsilon})\geq p\sigma K_{d,p}\cE^{0}(u,v).

∎

Theorem 2.18

(\nu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following uniform lower bound condition:

\displaystyle\text{There exist $c_{0},\theta_{0}\in(0,1)$ such that}\quad\inf_{0<\varepsilon<1}\inf_{\theta_{0}\leq\theta\leq 1}\nu_{\varepsilon}(\theta x)\geq c_{0}\quad\text{for all }x\in\R.

Assume that
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1\leq p<\infty
satisfies

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\big)<\infty.

Then there exist
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with,
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
if
1<p<\infty
and
u\in BV(\Omega)
if
p=1
, and a subsequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n}
with
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
such that
u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\to u
in
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\geq K_{d,p}\cE^{0}(u,u),\quad 1<p<\infty,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\geq K_{d,1}|u|_{BV(\Omega)},\quad p=1.

In addition, the following assertions hold.

(1)

If
\Omega
is a
W^{1,p}
-extension set then
\cE^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{\Omega}(v,u_{\varepsilon_{n}})\to K_{d,p}\cE^{0}(v,u)
,
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

(2)

If
\Omega=\R^{d}
then
\|u_{\varepsilon_{n}}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
for
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\R^{d}
with
|\Omega^{\prime}|<\infty
.

(3)

If
\Omega
is bounded Lipschitz then
\|u_{\varepsilon_{n}}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
.

(4)

Further if
\Omega
is bounded Lipschitz and,
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\R^{d})
satisfies
u_{\varepsilon}=0
a.e. on
\Omega^{c}
for each
\varepsilon
, and
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\R^{d})}<\infty
then
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
.

Note that
(iv)
follows the upcoming Theorem
3.5
.

3.
Robustness of nonlocal trace spaces

In this section we establish the robustness of our nonlocal trace space of
W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
toward the trace space of the trace space
W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)
with
1\leq p<\infty
. Note that in the case
d=1
we require the additional condition:

\displaystyle\text{There exist $c_{0},\theta_{0}\in(0,1)$ such that}\quad\inf_{0<\varepsilon<1}\inf_{\theta_{0}\leq\theta\leq 1}\nu_{\varepsilon}(\theta x)\geq c_{0}\quad\text{for all }x\in\R.

3.1.
Local trace theorem

Let us quickly recall the following version of classical trace theorem, which will be useful later.

Theorem 3.1

(Classical trace theorem, see
[
8
, Chap III]
)
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is bounded Lipschitz. There exists a linear and continuous trace operator
\gamma_{0}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
such that
\gamma_{0}u=u|_{\partial\Omega}
for all
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{0}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|^{1-1/p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|u\|^{1/p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Moreover, the following assertions are true

(1)

\ker(\gamma_{0})=W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, and
\gamma_{0}(W^{1,p}(\Omega))\overset{def}{=}W^{1,1-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
.

(2)

The mappings
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\xrightarrow{\gamma_{0}}W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Id}}L^{p}(\partial\Omega)

are continuous.

(3)

The operator
\gamma_{0}
has a right lifting, i.e., there is
R_{0}:W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\Omega)
bounded and continuous mapping such that
\gamma_{0}\circ R_{0}=Id
.

It is important to emphasize that a lifting operator
R_{0}
can be chosen to be linear (though not necessarily) only when

1<p<\infty
, whereas
R_{0}
can never be linear in the case
p=1
. This is known as the Peetre’s theorem
[
46
]
; a more recent and more accessible proof can be found
[
47
]
. For obvious and practical reasons, we equip the local trace space
W^{1-1/p,p}(\Omega)
the following norm

\displaystyle\|g\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}=\inf\big\{\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\,\,:\,\,\gamma_{0}(u)=g\quad\text{a.e on $\partial\Omega$}\quad u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\big\}

where we consider the Sobolev norm

\displaystyle\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\Big)^{1/p}.

Let us recall that when
\Omega
is bounded Lipschitz and
1<p<\infty
, it is possible to obtain the explicit equivalence of the following trace norm, see for instance
[
44
,
43
]
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}\asymp\Big(\int_{\partial\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\sigma(x)+\iint_{\partial\Omega\,\partial\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+p-2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(x)\Big)^{1/p}.

Interestingly, according to
[
30
]
, it turns out that in the case
p=1
, the trace space
W^{0,1}(\partial\Omega)
of
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
coincides with
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, i.e.
\gamma_{0}(W^{1,1}(\Omega))=L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
; we strongly recommend see
[
42
]
for elegant proof of the latter statement.

3.2.
Nonlocal trace theorem

In the nonlocal situation, given
\nu
a symmetric
p
-Lévy kernel, i.e.,
\nu\geq 0
,
\nu(h)=\nu(-h)
and
\nu\in L^{1}(\R^{d},1\land|h|^{p})
, it is much natural to consider the nonlocal Sobolev space

\displaystyle W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})=\big\{u:\R^{d}\to\R\text{ meas.}\,:\,u|_{\Omega}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\,\!\text{ and }\,\!\cE(u,u)<\infty\big\},

where
\cE(\cdot,\cdot)
is the nonlinear nonlocal form

\displaystyle\cE(u,u)

\displaystyle=\iint_{(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})^{c}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\nu(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x.

In addition we also consider
W_{\nu,0}^{p}(\Omega|\R^{d})
the space of elements
u\in W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
such that
u=0
a.e. on
\Omega^{c}
that is,

\displaystyle W_{\nu,0}^{p}(\Omega|\R^{d})

\displaystyle=\{u\in W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})\penalty\ :\penalty\ u=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{a.e. on }\R^{d}\setminus\Omega\}.

To be rigorous, it is natural to identify
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})\equiv W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})
, where
\Omega_{\nu}=\Omega+\supp\nu
and
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})
is the space of measurable functions
u:\Omega_{\nu}^{*}=\Omega_{\nu}\cup\Omega\to\R
such that
u|_{\Omega}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\cE_{+}(u,u)<\infty
with

\displaystyle\cE_{+}(u,u)=\iint_{\Omega\Omega_{\nu}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\nu(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x=\iint_{\Omega\R^{d}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\nu(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x.

In this way th space
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
can be endowed with one the equivalent seminorms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\cE(u,u)\Big)^{1/p}\hskip-4.30554pt\asymp\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\cE_{+}(u,u)\Big)^{1/p}\hskip-4.30554pt=:\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})}.

Interested reader is referred to
[
24
,
27
,
26
,
23
]
for further details on these spaces.
Due to its nonlocal character, the nonlocal trace space of
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
assumes functions defined on
\R^{d}\setminus\Omega
, or strictly speaking on the nonlocal boundary
\Omega_{\nu}^{e}=\Omega_{\nu}\setminus\Omega
of
\Omega
with respect to
\nu
. The main reason is that elements of
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
are essentially defined on
\R^{d}
, or strictly speaking on the nonlocal hull

\Omega_{\nu}^{*}=\Omega_{\nu}\cup\Omega
when taking into account the support of
\nu
; see
[
24
]
.

Definition 3.2

(Trace space of
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
)
.

The trace space of
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
denoted
T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})
is the space of restrictions to
\R^{d}\setminus\Omega
of functions of
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
. More precisely,

\displaystyle T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})=\{v=u|_{\Omega^{c}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \hbox{with }\penalty\ \penalty\ u\in W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})\}.

By identifying
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})\equiv W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})
leads to the identification
T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})\equiv T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega_{\nu}^{e})
where
\Omega_{\nu}^{e}=\Omega_{\nu}\setminus\Omega
is the nonlocal boundary of
\Omega
with respect to
\nu
and

\displaystyle T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega_{\nu}^{e})=\{v=u|_{\Omega_{\nu}^{e}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \hbox{with }\penalty\ \penalty\ u\in W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})\}.

We endow
T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})
with its natural seminorm,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})}:\penalty\ \penalty\ u\in W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})\penalty\ \penalty\ \hbox{ with }\penalty\ \penalty\ v=u|_{\Omega^{c}}\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})}:\penalty\ \penalty\ u\in W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})\penalty\ \penalty\ \hbox{ with }\penalty\ \penalty\ v=u|_{\Omega^{e}_{\nu}}\}.

Of course, if the space
(W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})})
is a Banach space then so is the space
(T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c}),\|\cdot\|_{T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})})
. Let us state the nonlocal trace theorem in connection with the trace space
T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})
. Further detailed explanation can be found in
[
24
,
26
]
.

Theorem 3.3

(Nonlocal trace theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
be open and
\omega_{\nu}\in\{\widetilde{\nu},\overline{\nu}\}
where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(1\land\nu(x-y))\mathrm{d}y\qquad\text{and}\qquad\overline{\nu}(x)=\operatorname{ess}\inf_{y\in\Omega}\nu(x-y).

The embedding
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\R^{d},\omega_{\nu})
is continuous. Furthermore, the nonlocal trace operator
u\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(u)=u\mid_{\Omega^{c}}
fulfills the following properties.

(1)

\ker(\operatorname{Tr})=W_{\nu,0}^{p}(\Omega|\R^{d})
and
\operatorname{Tr}(W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d}))=T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})
.

(2)

The mappings
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Tr}}T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Id}}L^{p}(\Omega^{c},\omega_{\nu})

are continuous.

(3)

If
\Omega
is bounded in one direction, then there is a bounded and continuous mapping
R:T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})\to W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
such that
\operatorname{Tr}\circ R=Id
.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that the kernel
\nu
is not necessarily fully supported in
\R^{d}
. Consequently, the sets
\Omega^{c}
and

\Omega^{e}_{\nu}:=\Omega_{\nu}\setminus\Omega
do not necessarily coincide.
Using the rigorous identifications

W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})\equiv W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})
and
T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})\equiv T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{e}_{\nu}),

we also have
L^{p}(\Omega^{c},\omega_{\nu})\equiv L^{p}(\Omega^{e}_{\nu},\omega_{\nu}),

since one readily verifies that
\omega_{\nu}=0
on
\Omega^{c}\setminus\Omega_{\nu}
. In this setting, the nonlocal trace operator
\operatorname{Tr}
reduces to the restriction
\operatorname{Tr}(u)=u\big|_{\Omega^{e}_{\nu}},
and therefore
\operatorname{Tr}\big(W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})\big)=T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{e}_{\nu}).

Moreover,
W_{\nu,0}^{p}(\Omega|\R^{d})\equiv W^{p}_{\nu,0}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})

is precisely the subspace of
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\Omega_{\nu})
consisting of functions vanishing on

\Omega^{e}_{\nu}=\Omega_{\nu}\setminus\Omega
.
For simplicity of notation, we shall continue to write
L^{p}(\Omega^{c},\omega_{\nu})
,
T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})
,
W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
,
W^{p}_{\nu,\Omega}(\Omega|\R^{d})
, as well as
\operatorname{Tr}
and
R
, as if
\nu
were fully supported. This slight abuse of notation does not affect any of the arguments or results presented here, all statements remain true in both settings (see
[
26
,
24
]
for further details).

3.3.
Robustness of the nonlocal trace space

In this section, we prove that our formulation of the nonlocal trace space is understood in the robust sense. In other words, prove the convergence of the nonlocal trace space
T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})\equiv T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{e}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}})
, with
1\leq p<\infty
, to the local trace space
W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
including the case
p=1
.
Furthermore, we also establish the existence of a robust right lifting
R_{\varepsilon}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\varepsilon}
for the nonlocal trace operator
\operatorname{Tr}
, i.e.,
R_{\varepsilon}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\varepsilon}
strongly converges to
R_{0}=\operatorname{Ext}
, which is also a right lifting of the local trace operator
\gamma_{0}
. Let us recall that the (semi)norms we consider on the (non)local trace spaces are given by

\displaystyle\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}

\displaystyle=\inf\big\{\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}\,\,:\,\,u=g\quad\text{a.e on $\Omega_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}\setminus\Omega$}\quad u\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})\big\},

\displaystyle\|g\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\inf\big\{\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\,\,:\,\,\gamma_{0}(u)=\gamma_{0}(g)\quad\text{a.e on $\partial\Omega$}\quad u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\big\},

where we consider the Sobolev norm

\displaystyle\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\Big)^{1/p}.

Theorem 3.5

(Robust trace spaces)
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is open with a compact Lipschitz boundary. The nonlocal trace spaces
(T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c}))_{\varepsilon}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, converge to the local trace
W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
in the following sense:

If
\|\operatorname{Tr}(g_{\varepsilon}-g)\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for
g\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
and
g_{\varepsilon}\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\operatorname{Tr}(g_{\varepsilon})\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}=\|\gamma_{0}(g)\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

This is true in particular if
g_{\varepsilon}\to g
in
W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
or merely
g_{\varepsilon}=g
.

Proof.

Since
\big|\|\operatorname{Tr}(g_{\varepsilon})\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}-\|\operatorname{Tr}(g)\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\big|\to 0
, it is sufficient to prove for the case
g_{\varepsilon}=g
. Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
\gamma_{0}(u)=\gamma_{0}(g)
. Define
E_{g}u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})\subset W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
by

\displaystyle E_{g}u(x)=\begin{cases}u(x)&x\in\Omega,\\
g(x)&x\in\R^{d}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

By convergence of forms
\cE^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
from Theorem
2.12
we find that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE^{\varepsilon}(E_{g}u,E_{g}u)=K_{d,p}\cE^{0}(E_{g}u,E_{g}u)=K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Inasmuch as
E_{g}u|_{\Omega^{c}}=\operatorname{Tr}(g)
and
\|g\|^{p}_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\leq\|E_{g}u\|^{p}_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}
we get

\displaystyle\|g\|^{p}_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}

\displaystyle\leq\cE^{\varepsilon}(E_{g}u,E_{g}u)+\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

In other words, it follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|E_{g}u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}=\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},

wherefrom we deduce the limsup inequality

(3.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\leq\|\gamma_{0}(g)\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Next for each
0<\varepsilon<1
consider
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
such that
u_{\varepsilon}=g
a.e. on
\Omega^{c}
and

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}\leq\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}+\varepsilon.

Note that, the uniform estimate (
2.2
) implies that

\displaystyle\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\leq\|g\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}\leq\|g\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\R^{d})}\leq 2^{1+1/p}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\R^{d})}.

By definition of
u_{\varepsilon}
and the fact that
u_{\varepsilon}=g
on
\Omega^{c}
we find that

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle=\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}+\|g\|^{p}_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}+\varepsilon+\|g\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\R^{d})}.

From this we deduce that, there is
M>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\R^{d})}\leq M.

According to Theorem
2.18
, there exist
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
and
u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon_{j}}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{j}\to 0}0,\qquad\text{for every bounded set $\Omega^{\prime}\subset\R^{d}$,}

\displaystyle K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE_{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}),

\displaystyle K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\cE_{\R^{d}}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}).

In particular, the above liminf implies

\displaystyle\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}.

Next, take a large ball
B\subset\R^{d}
, neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
, i.e.,
\partial\Omega\subset B
. Then
\Omega^{\prime}=B\setminus\Omega
(with
\partial\Omega^{\prime}=\partial\Omega\cup\partial B
) is also bounded Lipschitz. The trace Theorem
3.1
implies

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{0}(g)-\gamma_{0}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\gamma_{0}(g)-\gamma_{0}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g-u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|g-u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime})}=0.

Inasmuch as
u_{\varepsilon_{j}}=g
a.e. on
\Omega^{c}
.
Hence
\gamma_{0}(g)=\gamma_{0}(u)
with
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{0}(g)\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}.

This together with (
3.1
) yields the sought result

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}=\|\gamma_{0}(g)\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

It is worth recalling that any open set
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
with a compact Lipschitz boundary is a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain in the sense of (
2.13
), i.e., there is a continuous extension operator
E:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
. Consider the lifting of the trace operator

R_{0}:W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\Omega)
as given in Theorem
3.1
. It clearly appears that the extension operator
\widehat{E}=E\circ R_{0}:W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
is also continuous and bounded. In addition we find that

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}(\widehat{E}(g))

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\circ R_{0}(g)=g\quad\text{for all $g\in W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)$}.

Moreover, since the continuous embeddings
W^{1,p}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow W^{p}_{\nu}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow W^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega|\R^{d})
clearly hold, it follows that
\operatorname{Tr}\circ E\circ R_{0}:W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)\to T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})
with
g\mapsto\widehat{E}(g)|_{\Omega^{c}}
is also continuous and bounded.
On the other hand, if
\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is
W^{1,p}
-extension domain, i.e., there is a continuous extension operator
E^{*}:W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\to W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
continuous then one readily obtains the continuous embedding
\operatorname{Tr}\circ E^{*}:W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\to T^{p}_{\nu}(\Omega^{c})
with
\operatorname{Tr}\circ E^{*}(g)=g

for all
g\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
.

Using the notations of Remark
3.6
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
be open with compact Lipschitz boundary.
Then the nonlocal trace spaces
(T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c}))_{\varepsilon}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, converge to the classical local trace space

W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
in the following sense: for all
g\in W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\widehat{E}(g)\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}=\|g\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Moreover, this does not depend on the choice of the extension of
g
. Namely, if
\widehat{g}\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
is another extension of
g
such that
\gamma_{0}(\widehat{g})=g
then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\widehat{E}(g)-\widehat{g}\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}=0.

Proof.

Note in passing that, for
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
we have
\gamma_{0}(Eu)=\gamma_{0}(u)
since
Eu=u
a.e. on
\Omega
and
Eu\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
. Since
R_{0}
is the right lifting of
\gamma_{0}
we find that

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\circ\widehat{E}(g)=\gamma_{0}(E(R_{0}(g)))=\gamma_{0}\circ R_{0}(g)=g.

Therefore, Theorem
3.5
implies

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\widehat{E}(g)\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}=\|\gamma_{0}\circ\widehat{E}(g)\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}=\|g\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Now observing that
\gamma_{0}(\widehat{E}(g)-\widehat{g})=0
, Theorem
3.5
also implies

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\widehat{E}(g)-\widehat{g}\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}=0.

∎

Next, we establish the existence and the robustness of a right lifting to the nonlocal trace operator
\operatorname{Tr}
. It is worth mentioning that, in general, the right lifting may not be uniquely determined.

Theorem 3.8

(Robust right lifting)
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
be open bounded with Lipschitz boundary. There exist
R_{\varepsilon}:T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})\to W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
and
R_{0}:W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\Omega)

right lifting of the (non)local trace operators
\operatorname{Tr}
and
\gamma_{0}
respectively such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|R_{\varepsilon}(g)-R_{0}(g)\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|R_{\varepsilon}(g)-R_{0,g}(g)\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}=0,

for all
g\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
. Here
u_{g}=R_{0,g}(g)\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
is the
g
-extension of
u
outside
\Omega
, that is
u_{g}=R_{0,g}(g)=g
a.e. on
\Omega^{c}
. In fact, we merely take
u_{\varepsilon}=R_{\varepsilon}(g)
and
u=R_{0}(g)
to be respectively weak solutions to the (non)local Dirichlet problems

\displaystyle L_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in $\,\Omega$ \, and }\quad u_{\varepsilon}=g\quad\text{on $\,\Omega^{c}$},

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in $\,\Omega$\,\,\, and }\quad u=g\quad\text{on $\,\partial\Omega$}.

Proof.

Clearly by definition of non(local) traces we have
\operatorname{Tr}(u_{\varepsilon})=g
on
\Omega^{c}
and
\gamma_{0}(u)=\gamma_{0}(g)
on
\partial\Omega
. In particular,
\operatorname{Tr}\circ R_{\varepsilon}=Id
and
\gamma_{0}\circ R_{0}=Id
. Repeating the arguments from
[
24
, Sec. 8, & Sec. 11]
, reveals that each mapping
g\mapsto R_{\varepsilon}(g)
and, analogously
g\mapsto R_{0}(g)
are continuous and bounded, in fact,

\displaystyle\|R_{\varepsilon}(g)\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}\leq C\|g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})},

\displaystyle\|R_{0}(g)\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)},

for some generic constant
C>0
not depending on
\varepsilon
.
We conclude that
R_{\varepsilon}
and
R_{0}
are right lifting for the operator
\operatorname{Tr}
and
\gamma_{0}
respectively. Finally, as we show in the upcoming
Section
5
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}=0.

∎

Finally, using the norm
\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}
above we obtain the convergence of dual spaces.

Theorem 3.9

(Robust dual spaces)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
be open. For
f\in(L^{p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}}.

If
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is an
W^{1,p}
-extension domain
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{\prime}}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}.

However, if in addition
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is bounded Lipschitz then we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{\prime}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}}.

Proof.

If
\Omega
is a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain then, see
[
27
,
28
]
and Theorem
2.12
,
(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}\subset(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
and
\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)}\to\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}
,
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
so that

\displaystyle|\langle f,u\rangle|\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\big(\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}\cdot\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)}\big)=\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}.

Thus,
\|f\|_{(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{\prime}}\leq\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}.
The first
\operatorname{liminf}
follows analogously. Now assume
\Omega
is bounded Lipschitz. Consider
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)
such that
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)}=1
and
\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}-\varepsilon\leq|\langle f,u_{\varepsilon}\rangle|
. By the compactness Theorem
2.18
we can assume there is
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
\|u\|^{*}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)}=1
and
\|u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0
. The latter implies
\langle f,u_{\varepsilon}-u\rangle\to 0
since
f\in(L^{p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
and hence we get

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}\leq|\langle f,u\rangle|\leq\|f\|_{(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{\prime}}.

Likewise, if we instead selected

u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega|\R^{d})
, i.e.
u_{\varepsilon}=0
a.e. on
\Omega^{c}
, such that
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\R^{d})}=\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega|\R^{d})}=1
and
\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}}-\varepsilon\leq|\langle f,u_{\varepsilon}\rangle|
, then
u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
and
u=0
on a.e.
\Omega
, so that Theorem
3.5
implies
\gamma_{0}(u)=0
, i.e.,
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. From this we also deduce that

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}}\leq|\langle f,u\rangle|\leq\|f\|_{(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}}.

∎

4.
Definition: Asymptotic weak convergence

Given Banach spaces
X
and
X_{\varepsilon}
, we aim to define a form of weak convergence for elements

\ell_{\varepsilon}\in X^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
converging to an element
\ell\in X^{\prime}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The primary challenge arises from the dependence of
X_{\varepsilon}
on
\varepsilon
, where of course
X_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime}
are respectively the dual space of
X_{\varepsilon}
and
X
. Our objective is to preserve key features of standard weak convergence in a fixed Banach space, including: (a) boundedness of the sequence, (b) the weak convergence property, and (c) the weak-strong type lemma.

To alleviate the notations we simply adopt the following notional conventions. For simplicity we write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
) to denote the dual pairing between
X_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
and
X_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
X^{\prime}
and
X
). Moreover, it should become clear from the context that
X_{\varepsilon}
denotes one of the spaces
W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)
,
W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
,
W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},\Omega}(\Omega|\R^{d})
or
T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})
, while
X
denotes one of the spaces
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
W^{1,p}_{0}\Omega)
or
W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
. Furthermore, the restriction of functions on
\Omega^{c}
or on
\partial\Omega
is understood in the sense of the traces. In other words, we merely write
\langle g,v\rangle
instead of
\langle g,\gamma_{0}(v)\rangle
for
v\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
and
g\in(W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega))^{\prime}
and
\langle g_{\varepsilon},v\rangle_{\varepsilon}
instead of
\langle g,\operatorname{Tr}(v)\rangle_{\varepsilon}
if
v\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
and
g\in(T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c}))^{\prime}
.

Let us recall that
W^{1,p}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
, while, if
\Omega
is an
W^{1,p}
-extension domain we have
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)
. Hence
(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}\subset(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}\subset(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
.This motivates the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
f_{\varepsilon}\in(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}
(resp.
f_{\varepsilon}\in(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}
) asymptotically weakly converges to
f\in(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
if the following conditions hold

(1)

Asymptotically bounded:

(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is asymptotically bounded, that is,

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}}<\infty\quad\big(\text{resp.}\quad\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}<\infty\big).

(2)

L^{p}
-Weak-strong type lemma:
For a sequence
v
,
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
v_{\varepsilon}\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
and
v\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
(resp.
v_{\varepsilon}\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)
and
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
) such that
v_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\to v|_{\Omega}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\langle f_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\varepsilon}=\langle f,v\rangle.

In particular
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the usual
W^{1,p}
-weak convergence
, that is, for any
v\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
(resp.
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
) we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\langle f_{\varepsilon},v\rangle_{\varepsilon}=\langle f,v\rangle.

The asymptotic weak convergence of
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
f_{\varepsilon}\in(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},\Omega}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}
to
f\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined,
mutatis mutandis
, in a similar manner.

Example 4.2
.

The conditions of Definition
4.1
hold true in the situation where
f,f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
(in particular if
f_{\varepsilon}=f
) and

f_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup f
weakly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, that is

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{for all $v\in L^{p}(\Omega)$}.

By the boundedness principle, the weak convergence of the sequence
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
implies the boundedness of the sequence
(\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)})_{\varepsilon}
and hence it is readily seen that

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d}))^{\prime}}\leq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{(W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega))^{\prime}}\leq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}<\infty.

Now if
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is a sequence such that
v_{\varepsilon}\to v
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, then the standard weak-strong lemma implies
\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}(x)v_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\Omega}f(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}x
since

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|f_{\varepsilon}(x)(v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v(x))|\mathrm{d}x\leq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|v_{\varepsilon}-v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that a sequence
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
g_{\varepsilon}\in T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})
converges asymptotically strongly to
g\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
, if

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|g_{\varepsilon}-g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}=0.

If
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is bounded Lipschitz, we say that
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
g_{\varepsilon}\in T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})
converges asymptotically strongly to
g\in W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
if

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|g_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{E}(g)\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}=0.

where
\widehat{E}(g)
is defined as in Remark
3.6
. It is worth mentioning that, the asymptotic boundedness of
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is automatic under mild regularity assumptions on
\Omega
; for example, it holds whenever
\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is a
W^{1,p}-
extension domain.

Example 4.4
.

Let
g_{\varepsilon},g\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
. Assume
\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain. If
g_{\varepsilon}\to g
strongly in
W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
. By Remark
3.6
there is
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|g_{\varepsilon}-g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\leq C\|g_{\varepsilon}-g\|_{W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}\to 0

Namely
\|g_{\varepsilon}-g\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0
.
Likewise, if
g\in W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
,
\Omega
bounded Lipschitz and
g_{\varepsilon}\to\widehat{E}(g)
strongly in
W^{1,p}(\R^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})

we also have
\|g_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{E}(g)\|_{T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

Definition 4.5
.

We say that a sequence
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
g_{\varepsilon}\in(T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c}))^{\prime}
asymptotically weakly converges to
g\in(W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega))^{\prime}
if
the following conditions hold.

(1)

Asymptotically bounded:
The sequence
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{(T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c}))^{\prime}}<\infty.

(2)

L^{p}
-Weak-strong type lemma:

For
v\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
and a sequence
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
v_{\varepsilon}\in T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c})
such that
v_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\to v|_{\Omega}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\langle g_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\varepsilon}=\langle g,v\rangle

In particular, the sequence
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
satisfies
W^{1,p}
-weak convergence
, that is, for all
v\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
we have
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\langle g_{\varepsilon},v\rangle_{\varepsilon}=\langle g.v\rangle.

Note that by duality Theorem
3.1
implies
L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow(W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega))^{\prime}
, while Theorem
3.3
implies
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega^{c},\omega_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}^{1-p^{\prime}})\hookrightarrow(T^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega^{c}))^{\prime}
.
In particular the case where
g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega^{c},\omega_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}^{1-p^{\prime}})
and

g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)
the dual pairings
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\varepsilon}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
respectively boil down to

\displaystyle\langle g_{\varepsilon},v\rangle_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{c}}g_{\varepsilon}(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad\langle g,v\rangle=\int_{\partial\Omega}g(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}\sigma(x).

Example 4.6
.

The asymptotic weak convergence in the sense of Definition
4.5

holds clearly for the simple case
g_{\varepsilon}=0
and
g=0
.
The upcoming Theorem
4.8
, reminiscent of

[
24
, Lemma 11.1]
, provides a nontrivial example given for some
\varphi\in C_{b}^{2}(\R^{d})
by

\displaystyle g_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad g=K_{d,p}\,\partial_{n,p}\varphi:=K_{d,p}\,|\nabla\varphi|^{p-2}\,\nabla\varphi\cdot n,

where for
1<p<2
, we assume
\kappa_{\varphi}:=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|L_{\varepsilon}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty.
According to Theorem
4.7
the later is merely implied when
|\nabla\varphi(x))|\neq 0
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

The following theorem provides pointwise estimates for the
p
-Lévy operator when the corresponding kernel is radial, thereby also improving
[
24
, Proposition B.2]
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\nu\in L^{1}(\R^{d},\,1\wedge|h|^{p})
be a non-negative
p
-Lévy radial kernel, and whose corresponding
p
-Lévy operator is given by

\displaystyle Lu(x)=\pv 2\int_{\R^{d}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,\nu(x-y)\mathrm{d}y.

Let
u\in C^{2}(B_{2}(x))\cap L^{\infty}(\R^{d})
with
x\in\R^{d}
. Assume that
|\nabla u(x)|\neq 0
only when
1<p<2
.
Then
Lu(x)
is well defined, and for
0<\delta<1
small

\displaystyle|Lu(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(\R^{d})}\int_{|h|\geq\delta}\nu(h)\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2^{p+1}p\Big(\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{\delta}(x))}\frac{K_{d,p-2}}{|\nabla u(x)|^{2-p}}+\|u\|^{\max(1,p-1)}_{C^{2}(B_{\delta}(x))}\Big)\int_{|h|<\delta}|h|^{p}\,\nu(h)\mathrm{d}h

with
K_{d,p-2}=\tfrac{d+p-2}{p-1}K_{d,p}.
In particular, for some constant
c_{d,p}>0
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}|Lu(x)|\leq c_{d,p}\Big(\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}+\|u\|^{\max(1,p-1)}_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}+\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(\R^{d})}\Big)\|\nu\|_{L^{1}(\R^{d},1\land|h|^{p})}.\end{split}

Furthermore, if
u\in C^{2}_{b}(U)\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
where
U\subset\R^{d}
is open, then for
p\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\|Lu\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq c_{d,p}\Big(\|u\|^{p-1}_{C^{2}_{b}(U)}+\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(\R^{d})}\Big)\|\nu\|_{L^{1}(\R^{d},1\land|h|^{p})}.

Meanwhile, for the case
1<p<2
, if in addition
\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\in L^{\infty}(U)
then we have

\displaystyle\|Lu\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq c_{d,p}\Big(\|\,|\nabla u|^{-1}\|^{2-p}_{L^{\infty}(U)}\|u\|_{C^{2}_{b}(U)}+\|u\|_{C^{2}_{b}(U)}+\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(\R^{d})}\Big)\|\nu\|_{L^{1}(\R^{d},1\land|h|^{p})}.

Proof.

We prove only the first estimate, as the remaining ones follow directly from it. Let us put
a=\nabla u(x)\cdot h
and
b=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u(x+th)\cdot h\,\mathrm{d}t
so that
u(x+h)-u(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u(x+th)\cdot h\mathrm{d}t
. Since
\pv\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}\psi(\nabla u(x)\cdot h)\nu(h)\mathrm{d}h=0
and
\psi(t)=|t|^{p-2}t
, by the fundamental theorem of calculus we obtain

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\int_{|h|<\delta}&\psi(u(x+h)-u(x))\,\nu(h)\,\mathrm{d}h\\
&=2\int_{|h|<\delta}\psi^{\prime}(a)(b-a)+[\psi(b)-\psi(a)-\psi^{\prime}(a)(b-a)]\,\nu(h)\,\mathrm{d}h\end{split}

where we emphasize that
\psi^{\prime}(t)=(p-1)|t|^{p-2}
. It is worth noticing that

\displaystyle 2|b-a|

\displaystyle=2\Big|\int_{0}^{1}t\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}u(x+sth)\cdot h\cdot h\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|\leq\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}|h|^{2}.

While, since
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
for
1<p<2
, using polar coordinates we find that

\displaystyle 2\Big|\int_{|h|<\delta}\psi^{\prime}(a)

\displaystyle(b-a)\,\nu(h)\,\mathrm{d}h\Big|\leq(p-1)\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}\int_{|h|<\delta}|\nabla u(x)\cdot h|^{p-2}|h|^{2}\nu(h)\,\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle=(p-1)\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\hskip-8.61108pt|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(\xi)\,\int_{0}^{\delta}r^{p+d-1}\nu(r)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=(p-1)\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}K_{d,p-2}\int_{|h|<\delta}|h|^{p}\nu(h)\,\mathrm{d}h,

where we recall that

\displaystyle K_{d,p-2}=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|w_{d}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w)=\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{d}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{p-1}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{d+p-2}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)}=\frac{d+p-2}{p-1}K_{d,p}.

Next, for
1<p<2
with
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
, note that
\psi(t)=|t|^{p-2}t
is
C^{1}
on
\R\setminus\{0\}
so that

\displaystyle\lim_{b\to a}\frac{\psi(b)-\psi(a)-\psi^{\prime}(a)(b-a)}{b-a}=0.

Since
b-a\to 0
as
|h|\to 0
, for
0<\delta<1
small enough we have

\displaystyle|\psi(b)-\psi(a)-\psi^{\prime}(a)(b-a)|\leq|b-a|\leq\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}|h|^{2}\leq\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}|h|^{p}.

For the case
p\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle|\psi(b)-\psi(a)-\psi^{\prime}(a)(b-a)|

\displaystyle\leq|b-a|\int_{0}^{1}|\psi^{\prime}(a+\tau(b-a))-\psi^{\prime}(a)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p+1}(p-1)\|u\|_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}\|\nabla u\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}|h|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p+1}(p-1)\|u\|^{p-1}_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}|h|^{p}.

Thus in both cases
1<p<2
and
p\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\footnotesize 2\Big|\int_{|h|<\delta}\hskip-6.88889pt|\psi(b)-\psi(a)-\psi^{\prime}(a)(b-a)|\nu(h)\mathrm{d}h\Big|\leq 2^{p+1}(p-1)\|u\|^{\max(1,p-1)}_{C^{2}(B_{1}(x))}\int_{|h|<\delta}\hskip-6.88889pt|h|^{p}\nu(h)\mathrm{d}h.

Inserting altogether in (
4.1
) for
0<\delta<1
sufficiently small we obtain

\displaystyle 2\Big|\int_{|h|<\delta}\psi(u(x+h)-u(x))\,\nu(h)\,\mathrm{d}h\Big|

Remark 4.10

In view of Theorem

Moreover, recalling that
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is asymptotically bounded in
W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}-g)|\leq\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})^{1/p^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(g_{\varepsilon}-g,g_{\varepsilon}-g)^{1/p}\leq C\|g_{\varepsilon}-g\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

Thence, since
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
is a weak solution and
g-u_{g}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})
, it follows by linearity and by
u_{\varepsilon}-g_{\varepsilon},u_{g}-g\in W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon},0}(\Omega|\R^{d})
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}-g)+\langle f_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}-g_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\varepsilon}+\langle f_{\varepsilon},g-u_{g}\rangle_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

Altogether, we have obtained the convergences

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u_{g},u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g})\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g})\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

Accordingly, Theorem
2.6
implies
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g},u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g})\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0
and hence we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

Finally, let
\overline{u}\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
be another
W^{1,p}
-extension of
u
, then we have
u_{g}-\overline{u}\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
and
u_{g}-\overline{u}=0
a.e. on
\Omega
. Accordingly, since
\Omega
is bounded Lipschitz, by Lemma
2.8
we get

\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u_{g}-\overline{u},u_{g}-\overline{u})^{1/p}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-\overline{u}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}+\|u_{g}-\overline{u}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{g}\|_{W^{p}_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega|\R^{d})}+\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u_{g}-\overline{u},u_{g}-\overline{u})^{1/p}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0,

while also Lemma
2.8
implies that

\displaystyle\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})^{1/p}\leq\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(\overline{u},\overline{u})^{1/p}+\cE^{\varepsilon}_{cr}(u_{\varepsilon}-\overline{u},u_{\varepsilon}-\overline{u})^{1/p}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0.

∎
