An AI-driven framework for cybersecurity awareness and user trust in indian digital banking
Authors: B Madhana, Dilshad Shaik, Gaurang Tank, Ankur Bahl, Mohammad Kanan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid expansion of digital banking in India has heightened concerns regarding cybersecurity, significantly influencing user trust in online financial systems. This study examines the relationships among cybersecurity awareness, perceived threats, Institutional Educational Support, and user trust in e-banking, while proposing an AI-enhanced trust framework. Data were collected from over 500 university students across India using a Computer-Assisted Web Interviewing (CAWI) approach. A multi-method analytical framework is employed, combining statistical modeling with artificial intelligence (AI)-based predictive analytics. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) using AMOS was employed to examine the relationships among the constructs. Correlation and multiple regression analyses were conducted as preliminary analyses, while machine learning models including Random Forest, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), and Linear Regression are applied to predict trust levels and validate model robustness. AI is operationalized both as a perceptual construct measured through survey-based indicators and as a predictive analytical approach using machine learning techniques. The results indicate that cybersecurity awareness (β = 0.58) and Institutional Educational Support (β = 0.33) positively influence user trust, while perceived threats (β = − 0.27) negatively affect trust, with the model explaining approximately 69% of the variance. Mediation analysis shows that awareness mediates the relationship between education and trust, while moderation analysis reveals that AI perception strengthens the awareness–trust relationship. Among predictive models, Random Forest achieved the highest accuracy (approximately 82%), highlighting the effectiveness of AI-driven approaches. Additionally, machine learning models were applied to enhance predictive analysis of user trust. The study contributes by integrating behavioral, institutional, and AI-driven perspectives, offering practical implications for enhancing trust in digital banking systems.Keywords: Cybersecurity Awareness, E-Banking, Artificial Intelligence, Digital Trust, User PerceptionSubject terms: Business and management; Business and management; Information systems and information technology; Science, technology and society

Introduction

The rapid development of technology across the world’s banks has changed how banks provide and consume bank services. With many programs from the Indian Government for example, Digital India; and widespread availability of smartphones and low cost Internet, there has been an increase in the use of new digital banking technologies1. This is enabling consumers to conduct their financial transaction quickly and efficiently and as a result contribute to an overall movement towards becoming a less cash based society. Although this provides benefits to consumers, it also creates increased opportunities for hackers and cyber criminals. With an increased number of online banking transactions being conducted, the ability to hack into a banking system increases exponentially. For instance, with an individual attempting to gain unauthorized access to an account through phishing or by using a virus/malware on an individual’s computer (spoofing), or through social engineering methods. In addition to hacking into a system and stealing money from accounts, these types of cyber threats can undermine consumer confidence in the security of a digital banking platform.

A significant portion of security breaches in digital banking stem from behaviorally related issues such as users’ low levels of knowledge about how to practice good security; or bad security habits, or the users’ mis-assessment of risk. As well as the various advanced security technologies available, users’ perception of systems, knowledge, and behaviors continue to significantly impact the overall performance of security systems. The way users recognize potential threats, utilize safe practices, and have faith in organizational protective measures will largely determine how users engage with the services offered through digital banking2. Although the challenges associated with demographics and digital literacy in India exacerbate the existing challenges related to the need for widespread education and training on cybersecurity best practices, the vast majority of university age youth in India who are using digital banking are at risk of being exploited due to an excessive belief in their own level of knowledge regarding cybersecurity. These young adults may be knowledgeable about many fundamental aspects of security, however, they do not possess a comprehensive understanding of advanced threats or the mechanisms used to protect systems. This disparity between what the user believes they know and what they actually know regarding cybersecurity, increases the user’s susceptibility to cyber-attacks and impacts user-based trust dynamics.

To confront these problems, Financial Institutions & Regulatory Bodies have launched many different education & awareness programs concerning cyber security best practices to help better protect bank customers. The success of these programs is still unclear since customer behavior, along with participation in cyber security awareness training, are based upon multiple variables including how much risk a customer perceives they are under threat, and how much faith the customer has in the systems established by financial institutions. Simultaneously the incorporation of emerging technologies like Artificial Intelligence (AI), presents new avenues to improve cyber security3. AI enables banks to identify potential fraudulent transactions, anomalies in consumer behavior, and predictively assess risks, thus increasing the overall safety of Digital Banking Systems. In addition to its technological advantages, AI will also impact user perceptions & confidence. A consumers’ degree of reliance upon digital banking services may be affected by their level of confidence in utilizing AI to provide them with enhanced cyber security protection4. As such AI Perception becomes a significant variable affecting both users’ levels of trust and influencing their adoption of digital banking services through their interaction with additional variables; specifically, Cyber Security Awareness. While there has been substantial research conducted into Cyber Security Awareness, perceived threats associated with using digital banking, and trust; however, nearly all the research was conducted independently of one another and/or were conducted under very narrow theoretical frameworks. Furthermore, while researchers have primarily studied AI from a purely technical perspective, little research has focused on the behavioral & perceptual impacts of AI use. Lastly, nearly all research completed previously has utilized traditional statistical techniques to analyze data relating to users behavior in response to cyber security risks, however those techniques do not effectively model or capture the complexities and patterns of users behavior. The gap in knowledge of how users develop trust for digital banking is addressed by proposing an integrated AI-enhanced model for building trust based on both the technology and the institution. In developing a theory-driven model that includes both the behavioral, institutional and technical dimensions to build user trust for digital banking, this research will examine how cybersecurity awareness, perceived threats and institutional education support user trust. Additionally, this study will explore the relationship of trust as influenced by AI. This study uses a hybrid methodology combining Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) and machine learning to combine both exploratory and predictive capabilities. The contribution of this research has three components. First, the research develops theoretically a comprehensive and unified framework integrating TAM, PMT and Trust Theory. Second, the research contributes methodologically to the literature through its integration of statistical and AI-based methodologies. Lastly, this research provides empirically derived data for the Indian market and contributes to the development of digital banking systems which are both safe and user-focused for emerging markets.

Literature review

Cybersecurity awareness in digital banking

Cybersecurity awareness is one of the key factors for shaping the user’s perception and trust of the digital banking system. The quality and dependability of the developed software systems are very important to ensure safe operation of digital services. Robustness of the system will help to minimize potential vulnerability to cyber-attacks and increase resilience against them (Kumar et al.5. It has been shown by many researchers that awareness about cybersecurity increases the ability of users to recognize threats and use protection methods (Ahamed et al.6. Although there is an increasing number of digital interactions of people especially students, including students from developing countries, they still have a difference between what they think they know about cybersecurity and how much they actually do (Hui et al.7. As a result of this, security awareness is seen as the first step to ensure the compliance with good information security practice (Hwang et al.8. In addition, there is a lot of variation among student’s level of awareness on issues related to cybersecurity depending on which region or country they are coming from9–11. Furthermore, the usage of strong authentication methods like two-factor authentication can improve the feeling of safety for users and make them feel more confident when using online services (Kruzikova et al.12.

Perceived threats and risk perception

Users’ perception of risks refers to how users judge fraud, ID theft and cyber attacks. A rise in cybercrimes have contributed to decreased confidence from users on digital banking platforms (Akbar et al.13 Cybersecurity awareness is important in reducing risks associated with fraud against finances and increasing trust in digital platforms (Afzal et al.14 High perceived risk may prevent some people from using or adopting digital banking services ( Al Doghan & Mirzaliev15.

Institutional educational support

Institutional backing has been recognized as a key factor in increasing user confidence in using digital banking systems and raising the level of awareness about cybersecurity within these systems16. The established body of research on privacy and security issues associated with digital banking systems emphasizes that an institutional structure is necessary to protect user information from unauthorized access and to provide a framework for safe and secure interaction with digital banking systems (Iwaya et al.17. E-Banking advancements have also highlighted the need for ongoing user education, improvement of digital banking systems at the system level, and other strategies to mitigate new types of cyber threats (Malinka et al.18. As such, institutional initiatives including awareness programs, training programs, and regulatory compliance will be critical to encouraging users to develop habits related to the use of digital banking that are safe and thus increase their overall level of confidence in the safety and security of digital banking systems.

Role of artificial intelligence in digital banking security

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) has enabled advanced cyber security tools for digital banking. The use of Machine Learning is one of many ways that AI can be used for fraud detection, anomaly detection and prediction19 and by using these methods it allows digital banking organizations to detect threats more efficiently and accurately in real time while they monitor their customer’s financial transactions. Therefore, AI provides significant technical support to the security infrastructure of digital banking20, but AI also affects how customers view and trust digital banking organizations. Customers’ belief in AI-based security solutions play an important part of influencing their beliefs about how much they trust online banking. If a customer believes that the AI system is reliable and works effectively then their level of fear will decrease resulting in increased levels of trust and participation21. Research gap and contribution

Most previous studies have examined either cybersecurity awareness or perceptions of threat alone; very few studies have combined these measures with other elements including institutional support, technology and behavioral. Additionally, even though there is considerable technical research about AI, little research has examined how users perceive AI as a factor affecting their level of trust. Also, nearly all previous research used traditional statistical methodologies to analyze data. These methodologies do not sufficiently capture the complexities and nonlinearities that can occur when studying behaviors. Furthermore, most previous studies were generalizable across developed countries but not specific to emerging markets like India. Emerging market like India use multiple demographic and socio-economic variables to determine if they will adopt digital banking. To help fill some of the above gaps, the proposed research uses an integrated framework of cybersecurity awareness, perceptions of threat, institutional support and perceiving AI as a means to influence trust. The methodological approach used in this research is a hybrid methodology that includes using structural equation modeling (SEM) and machine learning techniques to provide both explanatory and predictive models of user behavior. Overall, prior research has shown that, the three most significant factors to positively affect user trust in digital banking include, cybersecurity awareness, perceived threats and institutional support. As well, the increased usage of artificial intelligence (AI)-driven security systems continues to improve the positive relationship between the three factors mentioned above. Although, the inclusion of the four factors into one complete analytical framework is limited. Therefore, this provides the justification for conducting this study.

Many studies exist regarding different aspects of cybersecurity, software reliability, and secure systems utilizing fuzzy decision making models, artificial intelligence based methodologies, and blockchain based structures; however, there has been little research conducted to explore the inter-relationships between the three factors (behavioral, institutional, and intelligent security) identified above within one comprehensive framework. A large number of studies do exist investigating cyber security awareness, perceived risk, and the creation of trust in digital banking systems; most of these studies have explored these individual constructs separately or within limited theoretical frameworks. A vast majority of previous studies have focused on either the behavioral dimension of trust or the technical aspect of cybersecurity but failed to incorporate institutional support mechanisms or new artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities within a singular analytical framework. Most previous models were limited due to their exclusive reliance on statistical methodology, thereby limiting the ability of those models to capture the complexity of non-linear relationships and predictive behavior exhibited in user trust. In order to reduce some of the short comings associated with previous studies, this research will present an advanced trust model employing artificial intelligence (AI) that will include three major constructs including; Cyber Security Awareness, Perceived Threats, and Institutional Education Support. Additionally, AI Perception will be incorporated into the model as both a behavioral construct and as a moderator. Machine learning techniques will also be employed to validate predictions made by the model. Both the use of statistical inference and AI driven analytical techniques represents significant methodological advancements over previous research efforts. More recent research regarding digital governance emphasizes the need for examination of behavioral heterogeneity and adoption patterns when developing emerging digital ecosystems. As indicated by Widjaja et al.,35, cluster analysis (i.e. K-means and DBSCAN) was successful in identifying separate user adoption behaviors within digital systems indicating the relevance of examining user segments when predicting technology acceptance. Building upon this viewpoint, this research aims to advance our current knowledge base regarding digital banking trust through integration of behavioral, institutional and AI driven constructs into a comprehensive framework while providing both theoretical and practical contributions to the ever-changing landscape of secure digital financial systems. No thorough investigation has been completed regarding cyber security awareness, perceived threats, and institutional education initiatives. Therefore, this research suggests a comprehensive framework addressing some of the previously mentioned gaps and provide additional insight into user trust in e-banking systems. This study provides a novel, integrated conceptual framework for explaining user trust in digital banking by incorporating all three dimensions (behavioral, institutional, and technological) into a single model. Unsimilar to previous studies that assess cybersecurity awareness, perceived threats, or trust individually, this research proposes an AI-Enhanced Trust Framework that integrates artificial intelligence (AI) perception as both a direct predictor and a moderator for trust formation. This research contributes theoretically by systematically integrating TAM, PMT, and Trust Theory to describe how user awareness, risk perception and institutional support together influence user trust in digital banking settings. From a methodological standpoint, this study employs a hybrid approach by using SEM combined with machine learning techniques (Random Forest) allowing for both explanatory analysis of user behavior and predictive validation of user behavior. Finally, this study supplies critical empirical findings from the Indian context that indicate the role that cybersecurity awareness and AI-driven security perceptions play in forming trust among users who actively utilize digital services. Ultimately, this work supplies a comprehensive, data-driven, and contextually relevant framework that expands on existing models of digital trust while providing practical applications for secure and user-centric banking systems.

Perception of cybersecurity

Cyber Security Perception refers to the user’s opinion about the security, credibility and reliability of electronic banking platforms. It affects how willing users are to use electronic banking via digital channels as well as how effective the security measures are. User Perceptions in electronic banking are typically influenced both by actual security infrastructures as well as psychological, cognitive and sociocultural factors that determine the risk users perceive when dealing with online banking services. Perceived Security is a primary determinant of the willingness of users to accept electronic banking. According to Alotaibi et al.36, users’ opinions on the security of their data and authentication systems affect user satisfaction and retention in the service. Furthermore, regardless of the level of technical protection, a negative perception may reduce user confidence and diminish their likelihood of adopting the technology. Similarly, Achuthan et al.37 demonstrated that in emerging markets, customers typically correlate the reliability of their systems and privacy assurances to their general trust in digital banking institutions.

Factors influencing how secure an individual perceives themselves to be include both demographics and behaviors. As shown by Catal et al.,38 despite the fact that university students spend a significant amount of time using the internet, they often tend to believe they know significantly more about protecting their computers from hackers than they actually do. Although they are generally aware of common threats such as phishing or password theft, they may not always consider more severe threats such as ransomware or hijacking of their sessions. In other words, the “illusion of security” results in a gap between what these individuals theoretically know about security and their actual actions toward their own security. Trends similar to those seen among university students were reported by Joubert et al.39 when they reported that the perceived ability of young adults to protect themselves against security issues was greater than their actual preparedness for doing so. Furthermore, communications from organizations and educational exposures can both positively and negatively affect perceptions of cyber security. Fallatah et al.40 found that public disclosure of fraud policies; public posting of fraud warnings; and awareness campaigns all contribute to increased advocacy among bank customers. However, Millett et al.41 reported that awareness campaigns in Indian banks vary greatly in consistency; therefore, awareness can create misconceptions with users focusing on convenience rather than security. Ultimately, user’s perceptions of cyber security are complex and dynamic and influenced by both subjective belief and institutional trust. Overall, the literature suggests that enhancing positive perceptions will require long-term relationships between the bank and customer through education, continuous and proactive information sharing, and explicit privacy assurance mechanisms. These are key factors in building trust as a necessary element for the successful long term use of online and mobile banking services in the digital world.

Theoretical framework

The authors of this study used an integration of the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), Protection Motivation Theory (PMT) and Trust Theory to explain why bank customers have faith in Digital Banking Systems. TAM examines how technology acceptance can be influenced by the belief that using technology will improve performance and ease of use. The primary focus of PMT is how people react when they perceive threats through engaging in defensive behaviors. Lastly, Trust Theory addresses the issues of reliability, security and institutional assurance in order for users to have faith in the digital platform. In relation to the Digital Banking System, Cyber Security Awareness has a very important role in affecting what users believe about a particular risk or behavior as a result of using a digital system. Users who have greater awareness of cyber security are much more likely to know the possible risks involved in using a digital system and therefore will use safe practices. Safe practices build user trust in the digital system. Furthermore, perceived threats as outlined within PMT affect a user’s motivation to participate in defensive behaviors. Although high levels of perceived threats may initially lower user trust; they can stimulate increased levels of user vigilance and appreciation for security mechanisms, thus increasing trust under secure conditions. Institutional Support (such as regulatory frameworks, security policies and awareness initiatives) increases user confidence in the system through assuring users of the reliability and data protection of the system. Each of these elements ultimately shape user trust in digital banking environments. Additionally, AI Perception was identified as a significant element that affects user trust. AI-Driven Systems provide improved fraud detection, improved customer personalization and enhanced security monitoring in digital banking. As a mediator, AI Perception represents how Cyber Security Awareness and Institutional Support lead to increased user trust through increased Technological Confidence. As a moderator, AI Perception also defines the strength of relationship between Perceived Threats and User Trust as well as between Awareness and User Trust through defining users’ beliefs of the system’s intelligence and reliability. Ultimately, based upon this integrated model, the researchers propose a series of Hypotheses to define the relationships between Cyber Security Awareness, Perceived Threats, Institutional Support, AI Perception and User Trust.

Technology acceptance model (TAM)

In the case of e-banking, for example, technology acceptance model (TAM) suggests that people will be more inclined to trust in and employ online banking systems if they see it as easy to work with, dependable, and convenient. Additionally, the knowledge users have about cyber security is related to their understanding of using and employing available security features within these banking systems; thus, the relationship between TAM and user trust in regard to cyber security awareness is supported42,43.

Protection motivation theory (PMT)

Protection Motivation Theory (PMT), is a behavioral theory regarding the way in which people react to what they perceive as potential threats. According to this theory, users will assess whether or not a potential threat has severity for them and whether they have some means to prevent the threat from happening (i.e., self-efficacy; response efficacy). The perceived threats in an electronic banking environment (e.g., phishing, malware, identity theft) are likely to adversely affect trust when users believe themselves to be at risk. If awareness of risks and ways to reduce those risks is enhanced through education and other means, then the perceived risk will decrease, and trust will increase. Therefore, according to PMT, perceived threats should be included as one of several important variables that contribute to user behavior.

Trust theory in digital environments

Trust Theory indicates that user trust is established by a combination of reliability (the ability of the system to work as expected), security, institutional credibility (that is to say, the trustworthiness of the organization providing the service) and transparency. The factors contributing to users’ perception of trust in digital banking are therefore dependent on both the technical controls provided for securing data and communications in online transactions and the reputation and communication approaches used by financial institutions. As such, Institutional Education Support, Timely Alerts and Awareness Campaigns all provide significant contributions toward establishing user confidence. Consequently, it can be inferred that institutional education support is also an important factor in developing trust within e-banking systems.

Role of artificial intelligence in trust formation

The use of artificial intelligence (AI), continues to play a significant role in the enhancement of both cybersecurity measures and customer trust in online banking. The inclusion of AI driven tools for example fraud detection tools; anomaly detection; real time assessments of risk all contribute to improved security architectures of online banking platforms. As users recognize that advanced technology is being used to protect their transactions, they develop greater confidence in those systems. Furthermore, AI can be viewed as a mediator through its contribution to strengthening the relationship between cyber awareness and trust. Users who have developed an increased level of cyber awareness will also become more aware of the capabilities and value associated with AI enabled security architecture.

AI perception is also included as both a direct impact and moderator to enhance the relationships between awareness and trust. The use of multiple theories will provide the researcher with a strong and comprehensive conceptual base from which to understand user trust of digital financial services. Model equation

The proposed model equation describes how cybersecurity awareness, perceived threats, bank education, and the belief that AI will assist with improving perceptions of the safety of e-banking systems can affect users’ trust in these services. User Trust (T) is our dependent variable. We anticipate that increased levels of Cyber Security Awareness (CA) will positively affect users’ trust because those who know what is involved in secure practice will be more likely to feel comfortable using electronic banking. Conversely, we expect that higher levels of perceived threats (PT) will negatively affect users’ trust because if users perceive an increase in risk due to phishing or fraud they will be less likely to use the online banking system.

The bank education program (BE) has a positive influence on trust through increased knowledge of the users, and through encouraging use of safe banking practices. An artificial intelligence perception (AI) is incorporated into this equation for two purposes; first, it is used directly as an influencing variable, secondly, it is used as a moderating variable. The CA variable captures the moderating effects of AI. Therefore, users who have greater levels of awareness tend to be more trusting of AI-based security systems, thus resulting in higher overall levels of trust in e-banking. Coefficients β1, β2, β3 and β4 indicate the direction and magnitude of each relationship between the independent variables and the dependent variable - user trust. The coefficient β5 indicates the interaction effect between the variables ‘cybersecurity awareness’ and ‘artificial intelligence perception’. The coefficient β0 indicates the baseline level of trust, assuming that all independent variables equal 0. The ϵ value indicates the degree of error or residual variation within the model.

Methodological approach

The sample for this study consists predominantly of university students and respondents from rural and semi-urban areas. While this may not fully represent the entire population of Indian digital banking users, it reflects a significant and rapidly growing user base of digital financial services in India. Younger users and individuals from semi-urban regions are among the most active adopters of digital banking platforms due to increased smartphone penetration, government-driven digital initiatives, and expanding internet accessibility. Therefore, the sample provides meaningful insights into emerging digital banking adoption patterns, although caution should be exercised when generalizing the results to the broader population.

This study effort combined theoretical understanding of electronic banking system security and professional input from cyber security professionals to evaluate electronic banking system security. Based on literature studies of cyber security research and talks with information security and digital banking practitioners, a structured assessment methodology for electronic banking system security was developed. Students learned about Internet-based dangers such phishing, malware, and unauthorized access/data breaches in electronic banking settings from the survey. This research effort targeted management students since they will become decision makers, policymakers, and organizational leaders who will shape corporate cyber security governance and strategy. The researchers hoped this assessment/sensitization survey would make students accountable for responsible safe digital activity at the corporate and consumer levels.

The survey’s evaluation and sensitization serve the greater objective of promoting educated cyber security practices and building trust in digital banking systems, particularly among young professionals in the increasingly technology financial sector. All of the variables that were studied were measured using multi-indicators using a Likert-type rating scale consisting of 5 different points, including; strongly disagree, slightly disagree, neither agree nor disagree, slightly agree and strong agreement. The response given to each indicator for each of the three study constructs were then averaged together to provide composite indices for cybersecurity awareness, the user’s perceived threats and institutional education support. In order to allow comparisons among the study constructs and to reduce potential biases due to differences in scales used to measure the constructs, the composite indices were standardized (min-max scaled) to a common value range. Standardizing the composite indices improves their applicability to be used in regression and machine learning models.

A second step prior to creating the composite index was to evaluate the internal consistency of each of the individual constructs using Cronbach’s Alpha. Each of the constructs’ alpha coefficient values exceeded 0.7, thus demonstrating acceptable reliability. Additionally, construct validity was demonstrated using Composite Reliability (CR) and Average Variance Extracted (AVE) as part of evaluating the measurement model. The use of CR and AVE allows researchers to convert an ordinal type scale (such as a survey) into a continuous type scale. Converting an ordinal scale into a continuous type scale is a commonly applied procedure in social science/behavioral research studies. Parametric statistical procedures such as correlation, regression and structural equation modeling can now be applied when using continuous type scales. All study constructs were developed based upon previous studies that have focused on both cybersecurity and digital trust. Users’ knowledge regarding safe online behaviors was used to measure cybersecurity awareness. Awareness of risk factors, i.e., phishing, fraud and/or data breaches represented perceived threats. Educational support provided by banks to raise awareness of cyber risks is how institutional education support was measured. Trust in AI (AI perception), or users’ faith in the ability of AI driven security systems, is also a study construct. User trust in e-banking, which is considered the dependent variable of this study, is defined as users’ beliefs about the dependability and security of e-banking websites. According to Fig. 2, there are two pathways that influence users’ cybersecurity awareness of e-banking websites: user knowledge acquisition and user threat recognition. Both pathways contribute to users understanding the risks involved with being online and will increase the effectiveness of education programs supported by banks.

The sequential survey questions addressing cybersecurity awareness and e-banking perception.

Following a pilot test amongst twenty randomly selected students, the survey instrument was revised for increased clarity and comprehension. Two items were removed; however, one additional item was included to measure respondents’ levels of trust in the bank’s computer systems. The final version of the survey contained 31 total items (26 content based and five demographic). The survey was administered over the period of April through October of 2025 to students from multiple colleges. Data collection occurred using the CAWI method utilizing Lime Survey, which housed the survey on the server located at the Faculty of Business Administration. All collected data were then processed in Microsoft Excel using pivot tables and corresponding charts and graphs were developed to illustrate the findings.

Although this limitation exists, an appropriate choice of a student-dominant sampling frame was made based on this research since young adults are one of the largest and most rapidly expanding segments using digital banking applications in India. Young adults have high engagement levels with mobile and online banking services which means that their exposure to cybercrime threats will be greater than older demographics; therefore, it is reasonable to examine awareness, threat perceptions, and trust dynamics within this demographic. Additionally, students tend to be “early adopters” of new digital technology.

The results from this research do not necessarily provide generalizable information for all demographic subgroups, but rather should be seen as having meaningful implications regarding how trust can develop among the increasing number of digitally-oriented consumers. This study adopts a multi-method analytical framework to ensure both explanatory and predictive robustness. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) and regression analysis are employed to test theoretical relationships and validate causal pathways among the constructs. Mediation and moderation analyses are further used to examine indirect and interaction effects. In addition, machine learning models are utilized to evaluate predictive performance and assess the generalizability of the model. This integrated approach enables both theory validation and data-driven prediction.

Machine learning model specification and validation

To increase predictive power, this study uses both supervised machine learning algorithms: Random Forest (RF) and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). Both models have been shown to be highly capable of identifying complex, non-linear relationships that exist in many behavioral and financial databases. Before creating these models, each dataset underwent preliminary processing. In addition to removing all rows containing missing information, all categorical variables were encoded into appropriate formats; all continuous variables were normalized (to prevent the influence of scale on the model) prior to model construction. For the Random Forest algorithm, a total of 100 decision trees (n_estimators = 100) were created. Each tree was allowed to grow up to a depth of 10 (max_depth = 10), and the Gini Index was used as the selection criteria for how to divide a node. A major advantage of Random Forest is its use of an ensemble approach to help mitigate the problem of overfitting by combining the predictions from each individual tree to create one overall prediction. The Artificial Neural Network had two hidden layers, the first layer contained 64 units and the second layer contained 32 units. Units in the hidden layers utilized ReLU activation functions; whereas the units in the final layer utilized Sigmoid activation functions. The Artificial Neural Network was trained utilizing the Adam Optimizer at a learning rate of 0.001. All training was done using mini-batches of size 32. Training continued until a stopping point was reached after 100 iterations. As Artificial Neural Networks can identify very complex, non-linear patterns within a database; in order to make sure they did not become too specialized towards the training set and therefore failed when faced with new data, a ten fold cross-validation procedure was implemented along with a 70/30 ratio for training/testing sets. Utilizing cross-validation improved the reliability and generalization ability of model performance. Several methods were used to evaluate the performance of each model including Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1 Score and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE). By evaluating each method independently, a complete analysis of the predictive capabilities of each model could be conducted. Discussion

The CAWI (Computer Assisted Web Interview) survey will contain the following components, and it will be distributed to students and members of the Faculty of Management and Law of the Sathyabama University in these areas: the knowledge of the electronic banking, the security of the networks by the computer, and the security of the computer systems, and the design of the networks. The majority of people utilize electronic banking through a mobile banking app, which is illustrated in Fig. 3, since 71% of the participants indicated that they utilize an electronic banking platform through a smartphone. Mobile banking apps are becoming increasingly well-liked among clients as evidenced by this study, since the mobile phone industry is primarily reliant upon smartphones for the completion of all normal digital and financial functions. This significant inclination is likely the result of the increased availability of mobile apps, the user-friendly interfaces of the mobile apps, and the opportunity to make transactions in real time. The second most common platform utilized for electronic banking is the browser-based smartphone and PC platform, as 18% of the participants indicated that they utilized this platform. Therefore, a large number of consumers continue to rely on browser-based interfaces for electronic banking despite mobile banking apps being the most prevalent method for accessing financial institutions. The reason for this may be that browser-based interfaces can be compatible with desktop administrative systems, can display information better than mobile apps, or are considered to be more secure. Only 7% of the respondents indicated that they utilize mobile apps, which are accessible from both smartphones and PCs; therefore, a minimal number of users practice hybrid methods of accessing financial services in order to maximize the advantages and usability of different types of devices. Furthermore, only 1% of the respondents indicated that they utilize web sites that can be accessed from both mobile phones and desktop computers for financial services, and only 3% of the respondents utilize web sites that can be accessed from mobile phones. The results demonstrate that the utilization of web sites that can be accessed from either the browser of a mobile phone or a dual-platform web site is still relatively rare. The results also suggest that the lack of optimization or poor usability of mobile apps when compared to specialized programs may be contributing factors to the low levels of usage. In summary, the results demonstrate the importance of mobile apps to contemporary electronic banking. The high levels of banking transactions conducted through cellphones demonstrate that there continues to be a trend toward increased mobility, personalization, and technical flexibility in the execution of financial operations. For their part, financial institutions must emphasize the development of mobile platforms that ensure optimal functionality, safety, and accessibility if they wish to satisfy the continually evolving needs of their customers and remain competitive in the area of digital banking.

Correlation and regression analysis

This research study is designed to identify how well the interrelationship exists with respect to the degree, direction, and predictive nature of the relationship between the vulnerability of a company’s cyber security and the trust placed by users in an on-line bank system. Correlation analysis will provide information as to whether the correlation of these two variables is negative or positive. Regression analysis will be conducted to assess the level at which the independent variables effect the variable of interest (dependent variable).

Variable description

There were three independent variables and one dependent variable utilized within the study. Data analysis and model validation

Each statistical and AI-based model employed in the study is described in detail in this section with explanations of the interpretation and visual instructions. All the models fulfil a different function of confirming the conceptual framework and enhancing the empirical basis of the study.

Conceptual model

The proposed conceptual model for User Trust in the cybersecurity environment (Fig. 10) represents a structured network of factors contributing to User Trust. As such, the model supports the notion that trust arises from the interaction of individual perceptions of cognitive, educational and technological constructs. As such, the central construct to this conceptual model is Cybersecurity Awareness, and it serves as a primary mediator. Prior studies have demonstrated that Institutional Educational Support offered by an institution or organization will positively influence user awareness of cybersecurity issues and promote users to be more vigilant and knowledgeable about their online behaviors48,49.Further, enhanced awareness of cybersecurity issues directly increases User Trust, in line with the knowledge-trust paradigm. This paradigm suggests that an understanding of risks and measures to mitigate those risks decreases uncertainty and leads to increased levels of trust50. Finally, in addition to the indirect relationship between Institutional Educational Support and Cybersecurity Awareness, Threat Perception and AI Perception each provide a direct impact on User Trust. For example, a more realistic assessment of the threat of cyber-attacks can lead to a greater reliance on the perceived protective mechanisms and therefore reinforce trust. Additionally, a positive assessment of the credibility and competence of artificial intelligence systems has become a critical factor in determining digital trust in technology-mediated environments51.Overall, the proposed conceptual model of User Trust indicates that User Trust is formed by two primary forms of influence; the first are the direct influences (AI Perception and Threat Perception), and the second form of influence are the indirect influences of Institutional Educational Support, mediated by Cybersecurity Awareness, and representing a comprehensive model of both cognitive and technological determinants of User Trust.

Structural equation model (SEM)

Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) has been used as a means of empirical validation of the relationships presented in the conceptual framework. By using SEM, researchers can simultaneously assess several interdependent relationship within their study while also taking into account that some variables are latent which can be measured with multiple observed indicators52. The use of SEM is a two-stage process where first the researcher will validate the measurement model through Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) and then the structural model is tested to determine the validity of the hypothesized causal paths.

The performance of the model was evaluated by a variety of Goodness of Fit Indices (Table 15). The χ² / df ratio of 2.14 is below the suggested limit of 3 for an acceptable fit of the model53. The CFI (Comparative Fit Index) of 0.957 and TLI (Tucker-Lewis Index) of 0.943 were both above the 0.90 benchmark that indicates a very good fit of the model54. The RMSEA (Root Mean Square Error of Approximation) of 0.048 is less than the cut off of 0.0655, further validating good adequacy of the measurement and structural model in terms of fit. In summary, the results indicate that all of the goodness of fit indices validated that both of the structural and measurement models are well functioning.

Mediation model

The data show an indirect, statistically-significant (p < 0.05) effect for Institutional Educational Support on User Trust through Cybersecurity Awareness (β = 0.31). As displayed in Fig. 11, education (e.g., education/training programs, guidelines, security campaign) increases user awareness of cyber threats; this increased awareness increases the strength of trust formed among users. In addition to the data showing the mediating role of awareness in the relationship between education and trust, this result also indicates that awareness acts as a critical intermediary between organizational educational intervention and user trust in the digital environment. Furthermore, this finding supports the KAB model60, which asserts that knowledge precedes attitudinal outcomes, including trust. Bootstrapping confirmed the statistical reliability of the indirect path from Education to Trust61. As such, Cybersecurity Awareness emerged as a critical psychological conduit by which organizations’ educational interventions translate to users’ development of digital trust. AI perception was measured using multiple items adapted from prior studies on technology acceptance and trust in intelligent systems. The construct includes user perceptions of (i) the effectiveness of AI in detecting fraudulent transactions, (ii) the reliability of AI-based security mechanisms, (iii) trust in automated decision-making systems in banking, and (iv) the transparency of AI-driven processes. All items were assessed using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).

Moderation model

A strong moderating effect by AI perception can be seen from Fig. 12 on how Cybersecurity Awareness and User Trust are related to each other. The simple slope results indicate that, relative to users who have a low perception of artificial intelligence, those users with a higher perception of AI, exhibit an extremely larger positive association between their Cybersecurity Awareness and their level of Trust. Conversely, for users with a lower perception of AI, the effect of their Cybersecurity Awareness on their Trust is much smaller than that of users with a higher perception of AI. Therefore, these results provide evidence that User Trust in AI functions as a reinforcing mechanism; enhancing the impact of Cybersecurity Awareness on User Trust. Thus, AI Perception serves as a catalyzing mechanism that increases the conversion of awareness to trust. The moderating effect found in this study was statistically tested using standard procedures to test moderation effects62 that include testing if the interaction term (Cybersecurity Awareness * AI Perception) is statistically significant at an alpha level less than 0.05.

Machine learning prediction model

The comparative predictive performance of the four models was evaluated using two regression-based metrics: R² for explanatory power and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) for prediction error analysis, as shown in Fig. 13. Since the objective of this study was to predict continuous user trust scores derived from survey responses, the machine learning framework was treated as a regression-based prediction task rather than a classification problem.Among the evaluated models, the ensemble and nonlinear approaches, namely Random Forest and Artificial Neural Network (ANN), outperformed the linear regression (LR) model based on higher R² values and lower RMSE values. These findings indicate that nonlinear and ensemble-based models were more effective in explaining the data and reducing prediction error compared to the linear regression approach. The results are consistent with previous studies demonstrating that nonlinear and ensemble methods provide superior predictive capability when modeling complex behavioral and financial data patterns63.The strong performance of Random Forest can be attributed to its ensemble-based decision tree architecture, which reduces overfitting and variance by aggregating predictions from multiple trees64. In contrast, the relatively lower performance of LR may be associated with its restrictive assumption of linear relationships among variables. SVM also demonstrated satisfactory predictive performance; however, its effectiveness may depend on kernel selection and parameter optimization. Overall, these findings highlight the importance of selecting machine learning models with an appropriate level of complexity to accurately model nonlinear behavioral relationships and generate reliable predictions.

For model validation, the dataset was divided into training and testing subsets using a 70:30 split ratio. In addition, a 5-fold cross-validation strategy was implemented to improve generalizability and minimize overfitting. Based on the conceptual framework, predictor variables included Cybersecurity Awareness, Perceived Threats, Institutional Educational Support, and AI Perception. Grid Search techniques were employed for hyperparameter tuning of the Random Forest, SVM, and ANN models to identify the optimal parameter configurations for predictive performance. The adopted methodology is consistent with prior studies in financial and behavioral analytics where ensemble and regression-based machine learning techniques have been effectively used to identify complex behavioral and trust relationships.

Policy and practical implications

The findings of this research are important from both a practical and policy viewpoint. The findings indicate the necessity for banking-specific targeted cybersecurity awareness efforts aimed at younger users and those living in rural regions, where cybersecurity knowledge levels may vary. To do this, banks should use behavioral-based teaching in particular languages relevant to their consumers; construct game-like learning tools; and establish mobile-based education tools. Collaborate with colleges, institutions, and local groups to reach more people and improve education effectiveness. Second, banks should explore introducing AI-powered security solutions such as real-time fraud detection, personal risk warnings, and dynamic authentication. As AI advances, these technologies will become increasingly more successful at detecting anomalous behaviors using Machine Learning (ML). With AI, people will gain trust in using online financial services.

Regulatory authorities should set cybersecurity awareness requirements and force banks to provide Digital Literacy Programs as part of Financial Inclusion Initiatives. Policymakers might incentivise banks to educate their customers on how to use electronic banking securely by investing in education and establishing digitally secure surroundings. Finally, policymakers might provide incentives to educators who educate children how to defend themselves from cyber threats as part of the normal curriculum. While AI may considerably enhance the capacity to identify and anticipate cyber risks, depending only on AI may result in decreased user vigilance and increasing complacency in the face of possible cyber dangers. Furthermore, many AI applications face various issues, including a lack of transparency (black box algorithms), data bias towards certain groups (algorithmic bias), and confidence in automated decision making. Some consumers, for example, may reject AI-driven security solutions because they are obtrusive or difficult to grasp. To make AI acceptable and helpful, it is required to build explainable AI (XAI), user-friendly interfaces, and explicit communication about how the AI system works. Finally, banks and regulatory authorities must strike a balance between human education and artificial intelligence in order to build a safe technological environment and encourage user trust.

Discussion

The findings from this research provided insight into the variables affecting user trust within Digital Banking Systems. One variable with a significant association was Perceived Threats (threats) & User Trust. At first glance it is contradictory that a threat would increase user trust. It is plausible however, that an individual’s increased awareness of Cybersecurity threats will lead to more cautious/informed behavior. As a result, individuals are able to have greater confidence in the use of Secure Digital Platforms. Individuals with increased awareness of possible threats will also be better equipped to identify/recognize/understand the security measures put in place by Financial Institutions, ultimately increasing their level of trust. This finding provides evidence that perceived threats, do not negatively impact trust but rather serve a useful purpose when combined with sufficient knowledge/awareness of security mechanisms put in place by the institution. Practically speaking, this demonstrates the necessity of open and pro-active communication on behalf of Banks regarding cybersecurity risks & measures taken to mitigate those risks. Furthermore, the empirical results also demonstrate that both Cybersecurity Awareness & Institutional Support significantly affect user trust. Therefore, targeted awareness programs, user educational programs, and transparency in regards to security policies can create the basis for strengthening customer confidence. Additionally, integrating advanced technology (such as AI-based security systems), can further build user trust by providing improved fraud detection and enhanced system reliability. Ultimately, the findings from this research provide evidence that a balance between raising awareness of risks, utilizing technological advancements, and supporting institutions are necessary to establish & maintain user trust in Digital Banking Environments.

The interpretation of these findings must be contextualized within the demographic structure of the sample, which is predominantly composed of university students and respondents from rural or semi-rural backgrounds. This demographic composition is particularly relevant in the Indian digital banking landscape, where young adults represent one of the fastest-growing user groups of mobile and online banking services, and rural populations are increasingly targeted under national financial inclusion and Digital India initiatives. The strong positive relationship observed between cybersecurity awareness and trust may therefore reflect the transitional stage of digital adoption among rural youth, who rely heavily on mobile banking for convenience but may possess uneven exposure to structured cybersecurity education. Similarly, the negative association between perceived threats and trust appears more pronounced in this group, as limited institutional guidance and informal knowledge sources in rural settings can amplify risk sensitivity. The significant role of Institutional Educational Support in enhancing trust underscores their importance for student and rural users, for whom formal cybersecurity training is often absent from academic curricula and everyday banking interactions. These findings suggest that awareness-building and communication strategies are particularly impactful for young, rural-dominated user segments, where incremental improvements in knowledge can lead to substantial gains in digital trust.

To conclude, this study confirms that the notion of trust in e-banking is not a technology issue, but is founded on a three-fold scale of informed customer, proactive bank and perceived technology effectiveness. The suggested AI-Enhanced Trust Framework is a proven model of how to attain sustainable expansion of the Indian digital financial market, directly contributing to the overall Digital Economy Vision 2030 in the country. Consequently, broader generalizations and policy-level interpretations should be approached with caution and examined in future studies employing longitudinal designs and representative samples. Future research can extend this work by incorporating advanced AI models, integrating blockchain-based security frameworks, and conducting longitudinal studies to better understand causal relationships. Additionally, future studies can explore diverse user groups to improve the generalizability of the findings.

Limitations and future research

Despite the contributions of this study, several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings. The sample is predominantly composed of university students and respondents from rural and semi-urban areas, which may limit the generalizability of the results to the broader population of digital banking users in India. As a result, the relationships identified among cybersecurity awareness, perceived threats, and user trust should be interpreted cautiously across different demographic and socio-economic contexts. Future research should adopt more balanced sampling strategies, including urban residents, working professionals, and diverse income groups, to enhance external validity and robustness.In addition, the cross-sectional design of the study limits the ability to draw causal inferences, as the data reflect user perceptions at a single point in time. Longitudinal or experimental research designs are therefore recommended to better examine causal relationships among the variables. Furthermore, the reliance on self-reported data may introduce response biases such as social desirability and recall bias, which could affect the accuracy of the findings. Future studies may address this limitation by incorporating behavioral data or adopting mixed-method approaches to strengthen reliability and validation.
