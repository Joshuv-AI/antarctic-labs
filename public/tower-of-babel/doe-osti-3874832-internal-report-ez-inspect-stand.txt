Internal Report: EZ-Inspect Stand

Introduction:

 In the warehouses of the Transuranic Waste Facilities (TWF) of the Los Alamos
National Laboratory, 55-gallon drums containing nuclear waste are held for storage before
shipping to permanent storage. During this period of storage at TWF, the drums are
currently inspected by workers to check for defects or damage to the waste drums. If there
is a defect when the drums arrive at the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP) for permanent
storage, LANL must pay a fine to the New Mexico Environmental Protection Agency
depending on how long the defect has existed. Currently there is no visual history of the
drums to establish a time for when damage occurred, meaning that even if the defect
occurred recently, it will be assumed that the defect has existed since the barrel began
storage at TWF, thus incurring high fees.

 To avoid paying greater fees than warranted, a database of pictures will be compiled
and analyzed with software to screen for defects and create a record for each drum. Having
photo documentation of each drum will make it possible to track when a defect formed,
eliminating the assumption that any defect found when arriving at TWF has existed since
the start of storage at LANL. This report outlines the progress made in the summer of 2026
on the EZ-Inspect Stand for the purpose of taking consistent and reliable photographs of
waste drums.

Objectives:

 In the summer of 2025, a camera stand design was made to help ensure positioning
of the camera is consistent when taking pictures for the database. The aim of the design is
to minimize workers’ time with drums and be easily deployable. This initial design was
adapted for manufacturing and assembly of the first prototype has begun.

Design:

 The camera stand has four arms with two cameras per arm to eliminate blind spots
(see Figure 1). One camera is placed 1/3 of the way down the side of the drum and another
2/3 of the way down the side of the drum to cover the full length of the drum with the four
arms covering the radius of the drum.

 Figure 1. Location of cameras on the Ez-Inspect Stand

 All four arms are connected to a center fixture so that as one arm is extended, all
other arms extend simultaneously. Cables will attach to both the ends of each arm and the
central moving fixture that connects the arms to the center stalk (see Figure 2). The arm will
extend vertically downwards in a telescopic motion directly proportionally to the vertical
movement of the center fixture as the stand folds and unfolds. This mechanism will ensure
that the cameras are able to extend far enough down the drum to take pictures in the
desired locations without hitting the rim as the arms unfold.

 Figure 2. Cable routing

Assembly:

 When assembling the first prototype, a few necessary adjustments in the design
became apparent. Firstly, the size of the cable used to lengthen and shorten the cable
needed to be reduced to allow for a small enough bend radius to fit the design. The original
cable size could not bend enough to conform to the pulleys used to guide the cable.

 The second issue that arose was with the center fixtures that attach the legs to the
center stalk which were 3D printed using Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM). These initial

parts were suitable to determine if the other parts worked well together and if the general
design seemed viable but ultimately were not strong enough. Additionally, the tolerance of
the center hole was not tight enough and the fixtures had to be attached temporarily with
tape instead of clamped like intended. Another 3D print was made, this time with nylon,
but this version also proved unsuitable. Although the tolerances were better, the material
was too brittle and cracked after a reasonable amount of force was applied during
assembly. For the next iteration of the center fixtures, aluminum will be used to increase
the strength of the parts and add stiffness which will reduce the amount of slop in the arms
of the stand when opening and closing the stand.

 3D print fixtures

 Figure 3. Assembled prototype with 3D printed center fixtures

 Another issue to be addressed is the ease of opening and closing the stand.
Currently, to close the stand opposite legs of the stand must be pulled upwards
simultaneously, which can be difficult especially because the weight of all four arms must
be lifted simultaneously by the operator. To make it easier to open and close the stand, a
spring can be added to help support the weight of the arms. This spring would be around
the center stalk of the stand, pushing upwards on the moveable clamp enough to assist in
closing the stand without needing to push the arms down to fully open the stand.

 Figure 4. Left: Prototype at fully extended position. Right: Prototype at fully retracted position.

Remaining Work:

 After the above alterations are made to the first prototype, mounts for the camera
need to be designed so that the cameras are a suitable distance from the drum and will not
collide with the drums at any point during operation. Additionally, the placement of wiring
and any other required hardware must be decided. After the first prototype is completed, it
should be tested and feedback should be requested from the customer so that additional
adjustments can be made and implemented in the design until a final design is reached.
