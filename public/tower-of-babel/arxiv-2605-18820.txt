Emergence of Frontier Superposition: Möbius attractor and Cascade Supervision
Hongyu Gu, Jingwen Fu

Abstract
Superposition is a class of hidden-state representations that lets a Transformer reason in depth : instead of unrolling a reasoning trajectory into a long serial chain of chain-of-thought tokens, an entire reasoning frontier is carried in parallel through one bounded-depth forward pass. The instantiation we study, and the one realised by Zhu et al. 2025 ’s hand-crafted construction on graph reachability, is the equal-weight mixture of a breadth-first frontier in a single residual stream, whether gradient descent ever finds such a target in a landscape riddled with permutation-symmetric saddles was left open. We close that gap on Reachability-by-Superposition over Erdős–Rényi graphs by separating the contributions of architecture and supervision. Architecturally, we identify a Möbius attractor : under S n S_{n} -symmetry in the tree regime, the layerwise dynamics reduce to a one-dimensional Möbius map whose zero set is a codimension-one manifold of global optima containing the equal-weight superposition state; residual stream plus per-layer MLP is one sufficient instance, and the conclusions transfer to any architecture realising the same attractor. On the supervision side, we identify Cascade Supervision : the loss class whose backward pass simultaneously delivers (A) selectivity bootstrap, (B) gradient persistence across depth, and (C) per-step discrimination, of which ℒ sup \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}} and ℒ node \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{node}} are instances. End-to-end supervision fails to satisfy condition (B) and is provably insufficient: internal-layer gradients at layer c c decay as ( n p ) − ( D − c − 2 ) / 2 (np)^{-(D-c-2)/2} in the graph fan-out and stall before the manifold is reached. The thesis of the paper is the equality Möbius attractor + + Cascade Supervision = = emergence of superposition reasoning : the parameter-free decay law predicts final-step cosine 0.35 0.35 vs. 0.71 0.71 (end-to-end vs. cascade) at depth D = 3 D{=}3 ; experiment confirms 0.37 0.37 vs. 0.69 0.69 , matching within 0.02 0.02 at every step.

\mathbb{E}\!\left[-\nabla_{W^{\mathrm{emb}}}\mathcal{L}_{0}\right]=\frac{|g_{\mathrm{diag}}|+|g_{\mathrm{off}}|}{n}\,I_{n}\;-\;\frac{|g_{\mathrm{off}}|}{n}\,J_{n},\qquad\boxed{\;\frac{|g_{\mathrm{diag}}|}{|g_{\mathrm{off}}|}=\Theta\!\left(\frac{n^{3/2}}{p}\right).\;}

5
Cascade Supervision: the loss class that locks gradient flow onto the Möbius attractor

Section
4
established that the Möbius
attractor
\mathcal{M}=\{A=0\}
is the unique attractor of the
layer-wise dynamics, conditional on the per-layer gradient
actually arriving.
Knowing the target is not the same as
reaching it.
We abstract the supervision side into a loss class

Cascade Supervision
: any depth-additive objective whose
backward pass satisfies, at every layer
c
,
(A) a selectivity bootstrap driving
S_{c}\to 1
,
(B) gradient persistence with a per-layer budget that does not
decay in depth, and
(C) per-step discrimination separating the on-frontier target
from off-frontier mixing. The triple (A)
\wedge
(B)
\wedge
(C)
is the loss-level diagnostic formalised in §
6
and
App.
M
;
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{node}}
are instances,
not the definition.

The dichotomy is sharp. Members of Cascade Supervision keep the
budget at
\Theta(1)
uniformly in depth and drive every layer
onto the Möbius attractor in BFS order
(Theorem
5
). End-to-end supervision is provably

not
a member: its budget decays super-polynomially in
depth, so intermediate layers stall near initialisation
(Proposition
7
). A three-mode empirical
comparison (§
5.4
) anchors the gap.

5.1
Cascade Supervision drives layerwise convergence to the Möbius attractor

On-manifold single-step recursion.

We first compute the one-step update assuming the previous layer has
already reached the manifold; this single-step formula is the
inductive step of Theorem
5
. Suppose

and the layer-
c
softmax is saturated,

. The

\cos(z_{c+1},z_{c+1}^{*})\;=\;\frac{\sqrt{k_{c}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}}{\sqrt{k_{c+1}}\sqrt{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{2}/m_{f}^{(c)}}},

depth-dependent: a single scalar
\alpha
shared across layers cannot
match
\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*}
at every step. This is exactly the

\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}

Theorem 5

(
D
-step cascade convergence)
.

Fix

5.2

5.3

6
Conclusion and Discussion

The open problem we close.

Zhu et al. 2025
proved by construction that a depth-
D

cascade transformer
can
carry the BFS reachable frontier
as the equal-weight superposition

z_{c}^{*}=k_{c}^{-1/2}\sum_{v\in V_{c}}u_{v}
. Whether gradient descent

discovers
this state, and under which supervision, was
the direction (2) they left open. On

Reachability-by-Superposition
over Erdős–Rényi
graphs in the tree regime, we close both halves: the trained
network reaches the construction along the four-stage trajectory

\text{init}\xrightarrow{\text{diag.\ adv.}}\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{inv}}\xrightarrow{\text{M\"{o}b.\ collapse}}\{A{=}0\}\xrightarrow{\text{cascade ladder}}\text{IDEAL}.

Architecture sets the destination; supervision decides
whether the trajectory arrives.

Contributions.

(i)

The Möbius attractor.
Under
S_{n}
-symmetry
the layerwise dynamics reduces—
a priori from init, not
by ansatz
—to a one-dimensional Möbius map, whose zero set

\{A=0\}
is a codimension-one manifold of global optima
containing Zhu’s construction (Prop.
3
,
Thm.
5
).

(ii)

Spontaneous
S_{n}
-diagonalisation.

From a generic init, gradient flow contracts
W^{\mathrm{emb}}

onto
\mathrm{span}\{I_{n},J_{n}\}
at exponential rate, so the
symmetry the attractor presumes is a learned conclusion, not an
assumption (Thm.
4
).

(iii)

Cascade Supervision.
A three-condition
characterisation—(A) selectivity bootstrap, (B) gradient
persistence, (C) per-step discrimination—of the losses that
drive every layer to IDEAL, with the parameter-free decay

(np)^{-(D-c-2)/2}
pinning end-to-end failure to a single rate
(Thm.
5
, Prop.
7
).

Limitations and Outlook.

The picture identified here—a Möbius attractor on the
architecture side, paired with three loss-level conditions on
the supervision—suggests an organising lens for the
learnability of depth-bounded reasoning more broadly: each task
should induce its own attractor–supervision pair through its
symmetry. Pursuing that programme, and a finite-batch refinement
of the diagonal-advantage SNR, are the natural next steps.

Lemma 8

(
S_{n}
-equivariant commutant; restated)
.

Let
S_{n}
act on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by permuting coordinates and on

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
by simultaneous conjugation

M\mapsto P_{\sigma}MP_{\sigma}^{\!\top}
. Then

1.

The space of
S_{n}
-equivariant linear maps

\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the two-dimensional commutant

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathrm{span}\{I_{n},J_{n}\}
, where

J_{n}=\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}
.

2.

The space of
S_{n}
-fixed matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

under simultaneous conjugation coincides with

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{inv}}
.

3.

Any
S_{n}
-equivariant nonlinear map of the form

x\mapsto T_{2}\,\sigma(T_{1}x+b_{1})+b_{2}
with

T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{inv}}
, biases
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1})

and coordinate-wise
\sigma
cannot inject mass into the

\mathrm{ker}\,T_{1}\cap\mathrm{ker}\,T_{2}
subspace; in particular
no equivariant MLP can recover an embedding direction that the
preceding linear stage projected to zero.

Proof.

Step 1: isotypic decomposition.

The standard permutation representation

(\mathbb{R}^{n},\rho_{\mathrm{perm}})
splits as

\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}\,\oplus\,\mathbf{1}^{\perp}=:\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{S},

where
\mathcal{T}
is the trivial representation
(spanned by
\mathbf{1}
) and
\mathcal{S}=\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
is the

(n-1)
-dimensional standard representation
(standard fact from
S_{n}
representation theory). Both summands are
irreducible and non-isomorphic.

Step 2: Schur’s lemma.

By Schur’s lemma, an equivariant map between irreducibles is zero
or scalar. Hence any equivariant
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
acts as

\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}\,\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}+\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}\,\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}

where
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}=J_{n}/n
and

\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}=I_{n}-J_{n}/n
are the two isotypic
projections. Re-writing,

T=\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}I_{n}+\frac{\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}-\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}}{n}J_{n}\in\mathrm{span}\{I_{n},J_{n}\}.

This proves (1).

Step 3: matrices fixed under conjugation.

By the same argument applied to

\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n}=(\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{S})^{\otimes 2}
,
the only
S_{n}
-fixed elements are spanned by the two rank-one
projectors onto the isotypic components,
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}=J_{n}/n

and
\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}=I_{n}-J_{n}/n
, equivalently

\mathrm{span}\{I_{n},J_{n}\}
. This proves (2).

Step 4: nullspace lock.

Suppose
v\in\mathrm{ker}\,T_{1}
. Coordinate-wise nonlinearity

\sigma
commutes with the standard basis (apply pointwise), so

\sigma(T_{1}x+b_{1})
depends on
x
only through

P_{\mathrm{im}\,T_{1}}x
. Since
T_{2}
is also commutant,

\mathrm{ker}\,T_{2}\supseteq(\mathrm{im}\,T_{1}\cap\mathcal{S})^{\perp}

restricted to
\mathcal{S}
, and the composition

T_{2}\circ\sigma\circ T_{1}
has range contained in

\mathrm{im}\,T_{1}+\mathrm{im}\,T_{2}\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{I_{n},J_{n}\}

acting on the same isotypic class as
T_{1}x
. Thus mass on

v\in\mathrm{ker}\,T_{1}\cap\mathrm{ker}\,T_{2}
remains zero
throughout the layer. This proves (3).
∎

Step 1:

Lemma 10

.

8

Bernstein tail

In particular
TB=\Omega(n\log n)
implies
\mathrm{SNR}\gg 1
and

\|\Delta W_{\mathrm{off}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\|\mathbb{E}[\Delta W_{\mathrm{diag}}]\|_{\mathrm{op}}
with probability

.

Step 1: decomposition.

Population
S_{n}
-symmetry (Lem.
8

\mathbb{E}[g_{t}]\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{inv}}

\mathbb{E}[g_{t,\mathrm{off}}]=0

Step 2: diagonal signal (ii).

At any

\gamma

\mathbb{E}[-\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\gamma]\geq c_{0}/\sqrt{d}
(Stage 1,

). Since

, accumulating

in the
\gamma
-direction gives

\|\mathbb{E}[\Delta W_{\mathrm{diag}}]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq Tc_{0}/\sqrt{d}
.

).

For a single minibatch sample
(G,r)
, the per-entry population

\partial\ell/\partial W^{\mathrm{emb}}_{ij}
has magnitude
at most
\alpha/\sqrt{d}
, and in the tree regime the gradient is
non-zero only on the rank-one block

\{(r,v):v\in\mathrm{Nbr}(r)\}\cup\{(s,v):s\in V_{c},\,v\in V_{c+1}\}

of total support
\leq c^{\prime}_{1}\,n

(Lem.

A.5
Lemma
11
: Frontier concentration

Lemma 11

(Frontier concentration; restated)
.

For
G\sim G(n,p)
in the tree regime
(np)^{D}\leq n^{1-\varepsilon}
,

1.

\mathbb{E}[k_{c}]=(1+o(1))(np)^{c}
for
c\leq D
.

2.

For any
t\in(0,1)
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl[\,|k_{c}-(np)^{c}|>t\,(np)^{c}\,\bigr]\leq 2\exp(-t^{2}(np)^{c}/3)
.

3.

In particular,
|V_{c}|=(np)^{c}(1\pm O(\sqrt{\log n/(np)^{c}}))
with
probability
1-O(n^{-1})
, uniformly in
c\leq D
.

4.

m_{f}^{(c)}=k_{c+1}-k_{c}=k_{c}(np-1)(1+o(1))
in the tree regime, so

m_{f}^{(c)}=(np)^{c}(np-1)(1\pm O(\sqrt{\log n/(np)^{c}}))
.

Proof.

Step 1: Galton–Watson coupling.

Couple BFS exploration of
G(n,p)
from
r
with a Galton–Watson
tree of offspring
\mathrm{Binomial}(n-1,p)
, mean
np-p
. In the
tree regime
(np)^{D}\leq n^{1-\varepsilon}
, the total number of vertices
visited up to depth
D
is at most
\sum_{c\leq D}(np)^{c}\leq n^{1-\varepsilon}/(1-(np)^{-1})
,
so the probability of revisiting a vertex by depth
D
is

O(n^{-\varepsilon})=o(1)
by a union bound. The
coupling matches the BFS exactly until the first revisit; on the
high-probability event there is no revisit, and BFS equals the
GW tree restricted to the first
n
offspring.

Step 2: GW expectation and variance.

Let
Z_{c}
be the size of generation
c
in the GW tree. Then

\mathbb{E}[Z_{c}]=(np-p)^{c}=(np)^{c}(1-1/n)^{c}=(np)^{c}(1-c/n+O(c^{2}/n^{2}))
,
so
\mathbb{E}[Z_{c}]=(1+o(1))(np)^{c}
provided
c\leq D=O(\log n)
.

\mathrm{Var}(Z_{c})\leq\mathbb{E}[Z_{c}]\cdot(np)^{c}/(np-1)
by the standard
GW variance formula (standard branching-process identity).

Step 3: Chernoff for conditional binomial sums.

Conditional on
V_{c}
,
|V_{c+1}\setminus V_{c}|
is a sum of

k_{c}\cdot(n-k_{c})
Bernoulli
(p)
thinned to remove duplicates;
in the tree regime the duplicate fraction is
o(1)
.
Standard Chernoff bound for sums of independent Bernoulli random variables
gives

\mathbb{P}[|S-\mathbb{E}S|>t\,\mathbb{E}S]\leq 2\exp(-t^{2}\,\mathbb{E}S/3)

for
t\in(0,1)
. Iterating in
c
and unioning over
c\leq D

introduces an
O(D)=O(\log n)
factor absorbed into the

O(n^{-1})
bound on the failure event.

Step 4: frontier-edge count.

m_{f}^{(c)}=k_{c+1}-k_{c}
by definition. Substituting the
expansion of Step 2,

m_{f}^{(c)}=(np-1)(np)^{c}(1+o(1))
on the same high-probability
event.
∎

A.6
Auxiliary lemma: softmax saturation

Lemma 12

(Softmax saturation lemma)
.

Let
\sigma_{i}=e^{a_{i}}/\sum_{j}e^{a_{j}}
on
K
classes, and

S\subset[K]
. If
\min_{i\in S}a_{i}-\max_{j\notin S}a_{j}\geq\Delta
, then

1.

\sum_{j\notin S}\sigma_{j}\leq(K-|S|)e^{-\Delta}/|S|
.

2.

For
i\in S
and
k\notin S
,

|\partial\sigma_{i}/\partial a_{k}|=\sigma_{i}\sigma_{k}\leq e^{-\Delta}/|S|^{2}
.

3.

Restricted to perturbations
\delta a
with
\delta a|_{S}=0
,
the softmax Jacobian has spectral norm
O(e^{-\Delta})
.

This implies that once selectivity
S_{c}\to 1
at step
c
, the
gradient pulled back through the softmax decays like

e^{-\beta R_{\min}}
, locking the cascade variable
\gamma_{c}
in
place against perturbations supported on non-frontier edges.

Proof.

(1) From
a_{i}\geq a_{j}+\Delta
for any
i\in S
,
j\notin S
, we have

\sigma_{j}\leq e^{-\Delta}\sigma_{i}
, hence

\sigma_{j}\leq e^{-\Delta}\min_{i\in S}\sigma_{i}\leq e^{-\Delta}|S|^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\sigma_{i}\leq e^{-\Delta}/|S|
.
Summing over
j\notin S
gives

\sum_{j\notin S}\sigma_{j}\leq(K-|S|)e^{-\Delta}/|S|
.
(2)
\partial\sigma_{i}/\partial a_{k}=-\sigma_{i}\sigma_{k}
for
i\neq k
, and
from (1),
\sigma_{k}\leq e^{-\Delta}/|S|
while
\sigma_{i}\leq 1/|S|
for

i\in S
(taking the upper end of the saturation), hence

|\sigma_{i}\sigma_{k}|\leq e^{-\Delta}/|S|^{2}
.
(3) The softmax Jacobian is

J=\diag(\sigma)-\sigma\sigma^{\!\top}
. For perturbations supported
off-
S
, the action
J\delta a
on the
S
-block is governed by part (2)
entries, hence has
\ell_{\infty}
bound

|S|^{-1}(K-|S|)e^{-\Delta}\|\delta a\|_{\infty}
.
∎

Loss equivalence and learnability conditions

This appendix supplies the proofs deferred from the main text linking

end-point is Proposition

5

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}}
satisfies
(A)
\wedge
(B)
\wedge
(C) and is therefore the unique

Setup and notation.

Write

for a supervision objective. For each

let

2
,
§
F
) and let

(Prop.
2
,
A=0

B.1
Operational definitions of A/B/C

Following
Zhu et al. 2025
, enhanced with the quantitative

(A) Selectivity bootstrap:

(independent of

Appendix E
Proof of Prop.
1

Setup.

We compare two architectures on the cascade task:

Under (A2) the attention weights satisfy

-equivariant MLP

\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}(0,B)=\frac{m_{f}^{(c)}B}{\sqrt{k_{c}}\,B}=\frac{m_{f}^{(c)}}{\sqrt{k_{c}}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*},\quad\forall c\in\{0,\dots,D-1\},\;B\neq 0.

Lemma 26

(Removable singularity at

)
.

View

as a rational map

On every slice

Proof.

Direct substitution. For (iii), set

\lambda\to 0^{+}

\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}(\lambda A_{0},\lambda B_{0})=(\alpha A_{0}+m_{f}^{(c)}B_{0})/(A_{0}+\sqrt{k_{c}}B_{0})

depends on the ratio

.
∎

Step 2: uniqueness of

Suppose

Step 3: quadratic floor at

Write

(Lem.

second-order expansion at the maximum,

\cos_{c}(\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*}+\delta)=1-\delta^{2}/(2\,m_{f}^{(c)}\,k_{c+1})+O(\delta^{3})

Lemma 27

(Cosine unimodality)
.

For each fixed

\cos_{c}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{k_{c}}+x}{\sqrt{k_{c+1}}\sqrt{1+x^{2}/m_{f}^{(c)}}}

\cos_{c}^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})=-1/(m_{f}^{(c)}\,k_{c+1})

Proof.

\cos_{c}^{\prime}(x)=(m_{f}^{(c)}-\sqrt{k_{c}}\,x)/\bigl(m_{f}^{(c)}\sqrt{k_{c+1}}(1+x^{2}/m_{f}^{(c)})^{3/2}\bigr)

vanishes precisely at

.
The value at the maximum is

\cos_{c}(\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})=(\sqrt{k_{c}}+m_{f}^{(c)}/\sqrt{k_{c}})/(\sqrt{k_{c+1}}\sqrt{1+m_{f}^{(c)}/k_{c}})=\sqrt{(k_{c}+m_{f}^{(c)})/k_{c+1}}=1

The second derivative at the optimum is

-\sqrt{k_{c}}/(m_{f}^{(c)}\sqrt{k_{c+1}}(1+(\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})^{2}/m_{f}^{(c)})^{3/2})

which simplifies to
-1/(m_{f}^{(c)}k_{c+1})

∎

We prove: starting from any

Lemma 28

(Reduced coordinate; flat
|B|

.

Let

,

Lemma 29

(Strict monotonicity in

)
.

For

\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}/\partial t>0

\frac{\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}{\partial t}=\sum_{c=0}^{D-1}\frac{k_{c}\,(\alpha-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})\,(\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})}{m_{f}^{(c)}\sqrt{k_{c+1}}\,(1+\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{2}/m_{f}^{(c)})^{3/2}\,(t+\sqrt{k_{c}})^{2}}\;>\;0.

Proof.

Chain rule

\partial\mathcal{L}_{c}/\partial t=-(\partial\cos_{c}/\partial\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c})(\partial\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}/\partial t)
.
By Lem.

,

\partial\cos_{c}/\partial\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}=\sqrt{k_{c}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*}-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c})/(m_{f}^{(c)}\sqrt{k_{c+1}}(1+\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{2}/m_{f}^{(c)})^{3/2})

Direct computation

\partial\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}/\partial t=\sqrt{k_{c}}(\alpha-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})/(t+\sqrt{k_{c}})^{2}

Multiplying and noting both factors share the sign of

(\alpha-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})\cdot(\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})
,
which is positive when
\alpha>\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*}

\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}(t)-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*}=t(\alpha-\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}^{*})/\sqrt{k_{c}}\cdot(1+O(t/\sqrt{k_{c}}))

Lemma 30

(
b_{1}^{\prime}<0

.

If

|b_{1}^{\prime}(\tau)|\geq|b_{1}^{\prime}(0)|

(A,B)=(0,0)

Proof.

\dot{b_{1}^{\prime}}=-(\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}/\partial t)(\partial t/\partial b_{1}^{\prime})=-g(\tau)t(\tau)/b_{1}^{\prime}(\tau)
where

g:=\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}/\partial t>0
(Lem.
29
). With

,

,

, the right-hand side has the sign of

-gt/b_{1}^{\prime}>0
, so
\dot{b_{1}^{\prime}}>0
, meaning

increases

towards

from below.
However the rate vanishes near zero: as

,

|\dot{b_{1}^{\prime}}|=gt/|b_{1}^{\prime}|\to\infty
but the

-flow simultaneously
drives

(Lem.

); a Nagumo
tangent-cone argument on the closed half-space

then shows that the trajectory cannot cross

in finite

\tau
. Moreover the conserved quantity

∎

Lemma 31

(Exponential convergence of

)
.

There exist

(explicit in the proof) such that

Proof.

Step 1: Taylor expansion at

.

The Lyapunov function

satisfies, by

29

Step 2:

From Lem.
30
and its proof,

|b_{1}^{\prime}(\tau)|\geq|b_{1}^{\prime}(0)|>0
for all
\tau\geq 0

|b_{1}^{\prime}(\tau)|\leq|b_{1}^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{0}^{\tau}|\dot{b_{1}^{\prime}}|\,ds\leq|b_{1}^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{0}^{\tau}g\,t/|b_{1}^{\prime}|\,ds

Step 3: Grönwall.

On the reduced coordinate

, the ambient gradient flow
projected onto
t
-direction obeys (Lem.
28
)

\dot{t}=-\|\nabla_{\theta}t\|^{2}\,g(t)

\|\nabla_{\theta}t\|^{2}=1/|b_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}\geq 1/M_{\infty}^{2}

(since

(absorbing the

with

\nu=\kappa/(2M_{\infty}^{2})
.

Step 4: explicit rate.

For

and
\alpha_{\infty}
bounded, the constants in

and

, hence
\nu=\Omega(1/n^{2})
.
∎

Putting it together.

.
Lem.

is preserved, and the Hessian gap rescales as

\lambda_{\perp}^{\mathrm{n.o.}}\geq\bar{\rho}/(8dn^{3})
.

Proof.

With non-orthogonal
u_{v}
the inner products are

\langle u_{u},u_{v}\rangle=\delta_{uv}+\rho_{uv}
with
|\rho_{uv}|\leq\bar{\rho}
.
The QK-conjugated weight
\widetilde{W}:=U^{\!\top}W_{QK}U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

decomposes under the
S_{n}
-isotypic decomposition of

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
into invariant and orthogonal pieces:

\widetilde{W}\;=\;A\,I_{n}\;+\;B\,J_{n}\;+\;W_{\perp},\qquad A,B\in\mathbb{R},\ \ W_{\perp}\perp\mathrm{span}\{I_{n},J_{n}\}\ \text{in Frobenius IP},

so
A=\langle\widetilde{W},I_{n}\rangle/n
and

B=(\langle\widetilde{W},J_{n}\rangle-nA)/(n^{2}-n)
are the two

S_{n}
-invariant scalars (Möbius coordinates) and the
orthogonal complement
W_{\perp}
is the trace-zero,
row-and-column-mean-zero residual. This decomposition is purely
matricial in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and does
not
reference the

\mathbb{R}^{d}
inner-product structure on
\{u_{v}\}
; non-orthogonality
of the embeddings enters only through the maps

U^{\!\top}(\cdot)U
that produce
\widetilde{W}
from
W_{QK}
.

Numerator perturbation.
Substitute into the attention logit

\ell_{i}=\alpha\langle u_{t},W_{QK}u_{s_{i}}\rangle/\sqrt{d}=\alpha[\widetilde{W}]_{t,s_{i}}/\sqrt{d}
. Reading the entry

[\widetilde{W}]_{t,s_{i}}=A\,\delta_{t,s_{i}}+B+[W_{\perp}]_{t,s_{i}}
, the
diagonal contribution is
A
if
t=s_{i}
and
0
otherwise, the
off-diagonal contribution is
B
, and the residual contributes

[W_{\perp}]_{t,s_{i}}
. With the embedding cross-products

\rho_{uv}
, the realised attention logit acquires a

\bar{\rho}
-multiplicative correction on the
B
-coefficient (since

B
couples
\sum_{v}u_{v}u_{v}^{\!\top}\neq I_{d}
), while the

A
-coefficient is preserved up to a
1+O(\bar{\rho})
overall
constant. Thus the Möbius reduction holds with the substitution

B\mapsto B_{\mathrm{eff}}=\bar{\rho}\,B\,(1+o(1))
.

Denominator preservation.
The denominator

A+\sqrt{k_{c}}B
comes from the IDEAL cosine normalisation

\|z_{c}\|=1
, which depends only on the unit-norm constraint

\|u_{v}\|=1
(assumed throughout) and is independent of cross-inner-products.
Hence
A+\sqrt{k_{c}}B
is unchanged, and the substitution

B\mapsto\bar{\rho}B(1+o(1))
is the only modification.

Optimum preservation.
The fixed-point equation

\partial_{\alpha}\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}=0
at IDEAL becomes

A=0
in the non-orthogonal case as well (the denominator is
non-vanishing on the trajectory by Lem.
30
, so
the substitution
B\mapsto\bar{\rho}B
does not introduce new zeros).

Hessian gap.
The Hessian gap calculation of
Lem.
9
relied on the orthogonality bound

\|\sum_{v}u_{v}u_{v}^{\top}-I_{d}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=0
. Replacing this by

\|\sum_{v}u_{v}u_{v}^{\top}-I_{d}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq n\bar{\rho}
(Gershgorin
applied to the off-diagonal Gram matrix

[\rho_{uv}]_{u\neq v}
), the spectral gap of the
W_{\perp}

Hessian block is reduced by a factor
1-n\bar{\rho}
. For

\bar{\rho}=o(1/n)
this is
1-o(1)
and the qualitative
bound holds; in the regime

\bar{\rho}=\Theta(1/\sqrt{d})
relevant to JL-random embeddings with

d\gtrsim\log n
this gives

\lambda_{\perp}^{\mathrm{n.o.}}\geq(1-n/\sqrt{d})/(8dn^{3})\geq 1/(8d^{3/2}n^{3})

for
d\geq 4n^{2}
.
∎

Putting it together.

The OV channel and non-orthogonality
both rescale the Möbius slope but preserve the codim-
1
optimum

\{A=0\}
. The diagonal advantage of §
4.4
continues to
drive
W_{V}
toward
aI+bJ
form during training, which is the
empirical
48.8
:
1
diag/off ratio measured at convergence
(empirically observed).

Appendix L
RoPE compatibility

Setup.

RoPE
[
Su et al. 2024
]
replaces absolute
positional encodings with rotation matrices
R(\ell)\in\mathrm{SO}(2)

applied to query and key vectors at position
\ell
. We show that the
Möbius reduction of §
4
is invariant under this
transformation: the
\{A=0\}
optimum and the cascade ladder

\gamma_{c}^{*}
persist verbatim.

Definitions.

Group the
d
dimensions into pairs and let

R(\ell)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{d/2}R_{\theta_{j}\ell}
act block-diagonally,

R_{\theta}=\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]
.
The rotated query and key are
q_{\ell}=R(\ell)\,W_{Q}z
and

k_{\ell^{\prime}}=R(\ell^{\prime})\,W_{K}u_{s_{i}}
. Their inner product is

\langle q_{\ell},k_{\ell^{\prime}}\rangle\,=\,z^{\top}W_{Q}^{\top}R(\ell)^{\top}R(\ell^{\prime})W_{K}u_{s_{i}}\,=\,z^{\top}W_{Q}^{\top}R(\ell^{\prime}-\ell)W_{K}u_{s_{i}}.

(20)

Define the relative-rotated weight

W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}(\Delta\ell)\,:=\,W_{Q}^{\top}R(\Delta\ell)W_{K},\qquad\Delta\ell:=\ell^{\prime}-\ell.

(21)

Lemma 42

(RoPE–Möbius compatibility)
.

For any fixed
\Delta\ell
,
W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}(\Delta\ell)
is an
element of
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with the same
S_{n}
-equivariance under
node permutations as
W_{QK}
. The Möbius reduction of
§
4
applies to
W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}
with the
same Möbius coordinates
(A,B)
and the same global optimum
\{A=0\}
.

Proof.

S_{n}
-equivariance.
Permutations of node identities act on

U=[u_{1},\dots,u_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
as right-multiplication by a
permutation matrix
P\in S_{n}
,
U\mapsto UP
. RoPE’s rotation

R(\Delta\ell)
acts on the
embedding axis

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is
disjoint from the node-permutation axis. Hence the action of
S_{n}

commutes with the action of RoPE:

\sigma\!\cdot\!W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}(\Delta\ell)\,=\,W_{Q}^{\top}R(\Delta\ell)W_{K}\,P_{\sigma}\,=\,W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}(\Delta\ell)\,P_{\sigma}.

The commutant decomposition of Lem.
8
applies to

U^{\top}W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
in the same way as
to
U^{\top}W_{QK}U
.

Möbius reduction.
The proof of Möbius reduction
(Prop.
2
) used only (i) the linearity of attention logits
in the QK weight and (ii) the
S_{n}
-equivariance of the embedding
basis. Both properties carry over to
W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}
. Define
the rotated
S_{n}
-isotypic scalars

\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{rot}}(\Delta\ell)

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{1}{n}\tr\bigl(U^{\top}W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}(\Delta\ell)\,U\bigr),

(22)

\displaystyle B^{\mathrm{rot}}(\Delta\ell)

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{u\neq v}(U^{\top}W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}(\Delta\ell)\,U)_{uv},

(23)

so that the
S_{n}
-projection of
U^{\top}W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}U

equals
A^{\mathrm{rot}}I_{n}+B^{\mathrm{rot}}(J_{n}-I_{n})
(mean of
off-diagonal entries). The Möbius effective coupling is then

\alpha_{\mathrm{eff},c}(\Delta\ell)=\frac{\alpha\,A^{\mathrm{rot}}+m_{f}^{(c)}\,B^{\mathrm{rot}}}{A^{\mathrm{rot}}+\sqrt{k_{c}}\,B^{\mathrm{rot}}},

with the same scalar
(A^{\mathrm{rot}},B^{\mathrm{rot}})
structure
as the absolute-encoding case.
The optimum manifold is
\{A^{\mathrm{rot}}=0\}
, and the cascade
ladder of Lem.
20
produces a relative-rotation
ladder
\Delta\ell_{c}^{*}
matching the BFS depth.

Hessian gap.
The Hessian gap calculation of
Lem.
9
depends only on the spectral norm of the
rotated weight,
\|W_{QK}^{\,\mathrm{rot}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|R(\Delta\ell)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|W_{QK}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|W_{QK}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

since
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
is orthogonal. Hence

\lambda_{\perp}^{\mathrm{rot}}=\lambda_{\perp}\geq 1/(8dn^{3})
.
∎

Lemma 43

(RoPE preserves the cascade attractor)
.

With RoPE positional encodings, Thm.
5
and
Thm.
4
hold verbatim, with all rates and constants
unchanged from the absolute-encoding case.

Proof.

By Lem.
42
, the Möbius optimum and Hessian gap

5

(selectivity bootstrap, ladder, error contraction, Möbius locking)

-equivariant operations and the Möbius scalars

4

∎

Reproducibility: full experimental setup

This appendix gives a complete recipe for reproducing every

0.37/0.69/0.35/0.71

,

Task and data.

Erdős–Rényi directed graphs
G\sim G(n,p)
with

n{=}50
,
p{=}0.04
(so
np{=}2
), reasoning depth

D{=}3
, in the tree regime
(np)^{D}=8\leq n^{1-\varepsilon}
.
Each instance
(G,r)
pairs a graph with a uniformly
random root
r\in[n]
. The target is the lexicographically
minimum element of
V_{D}
, the
D
-hop reachable set;
intermediate supervision targets are the indicator
distributions
\mathbf{1}_{V_{c}}/k_{c}
for
c=1,\dots,D-1

(node loss) or the IDEAL embedding

z_{c}^{*}=k_{c}^{-1/2}\sum_{v\in V_{c}}u_{v}
(superposition
loss). Data is generated
online
: every minibatch
samples fresh graphs and roots, so train and test
distributions coincide and there is no held-out

utils/graph_gen.py

seeded by the global
seed
below.

Architecture.

The Cascade Transformer of §
3
:
embedding dimension
d{=}64
, single attention head,
single width-
d_{\mathrm{mlp}}{=}256
ReLU MLP per
layer, residual stream, parameter-free LayerNorm
(
\mathrm{LN}(x)=x/\|x\|_{2}
), depth
D{=}3
.
Attention temperature
\beta_{c}
and projection scale

\alpha_{c}
are reduced order parameters of
§
3
. Implementation:

models/pmrs.py
.

Optimisation.

Adam, learning rate
10^{-3}
, batch size
64
,

400
epochs of
\sim
1k steps each (
\sim

2.5{\times}10^{5}

gradient updates total). No warm-up, no LR schedule,
no weight decay, no gradient clipping. Random seed

\mathtt{seed}{=}42
throughout (Python, NumPy,
PyTorch CPU and CUDA generators), single seed per
configuration; robustness is established by the
quantitative
\pm 0.02
-band agreement between the
parameter-free prediction and the measurement

experiments/pmrs/train_supervision_modes.py

,

,

).

results/pmrs/supervision_modes/<mode>/config.json
.
