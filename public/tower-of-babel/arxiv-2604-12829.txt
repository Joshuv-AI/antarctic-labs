Variable Bregman Majorization–Minimization algorithms for nonconvex nonsmooth optimization, with application to Poisson imaging
Maxence Adly, Alix Chazottes, Émilie Chouzenoux, Jean-Christophe Pesquet & Florent Sureau, [1ex] Université Paris-Saclay, CentraleSupélec, Inria, Centre pour la Vision Numérique, Gif-sur-Yvette, France

Abstract
In this work, we introduce a unifying Bregman-based majorization-minimization (MM) framework for solving nonconvex nonsmooth optimization problems. The proposed approach leverages Bregman divergences, possibly varying across iterations, to construct tailored surrogate functions that majorize the objective. We establish the convergence of the iterates of the resulting variable Bregman MM algorithm to critical points under the Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz property, relaxing standard assumptions such as the Lipschitz smoothness of the nonconvex objective function. We derive a constructive methodology to build a broad class of variable Bregman majorants with tractable minimizers. Our study encapsulates various existing majorization techniques, in particular those derived for Poisson data fidelity terms in imaging inverse problems. Numerical experiments on Positron Emission Tomography (PET) image reconstruction with a nonconvex regularizer showcase the practical feasibility of the proposed scheme.



Keywords.

majorization–minimization, Bregman divergence, variable metric, Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz property, global convergence, nonsmooth analysis, PET image reconstruction.

Over the last decades,

By iteratively constructing convex surrogates (or majorants) of the nonconvex objective, these methods provide a stable descent mechanism at each iteration. Furthermore, as shown for instance in
[

,
37
]
, MM techniques can benefit from proven convergence of their iterates toward a critical point in the challenging nonconvex setting, by leveraging powerful proof mechanisms based on Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz (KŁ) assumption introduced in

Convergence results highly depend on the structure of the majorant functions, and most existing theoretical results on MM methods assume majorization inequalities that hold within a Euclidean geometry (i.e., quadratic majorant functions), with an underlying metric possibly varying along iterations
[

,
52

However, such an Euclidean structure does not fit well classes of problems where the objective function is not Lipschitz differentiable on its domain. A typical example, widely encountered in computational imaging, arises with functions containing (minus) logarithmic terms, possibly composed with linear operators. The barrier behavior of the logarithm around zero makes the objective function and its gradient unbounded at the border of its domain, preventing the construction of meaningful quadratic majorant functions. Various MM techniques have been proposed in the past to account for this situation, motivated by the applicative problem of image restoration/reconstruction problems under Poisson noise
[
8
]
, starting from the historical maximum likelihood expectation maximization (ML-EM) algorithm
[
27
,
26
]
and its multiple extensions
[
31
,
29
]
. Convergence has been studied for instance in
[
28
,
10
,
40
]
, leveraging properties of the Kullback-Leibler divergence and MM update rules. However, there remains a crucial need to unify these methods and to better characterize their convergence guarantees in the general nonconvex nonsmooth case, in order to address a wider class of penalized optimization problems.

A key concept adopted in this work is the use of
Bregman divergences
whose fundamental role in optimization have been pointed out in main contributions

[
5
,
6
,
48
,
23
]
,
rooted in the literature of variational analysis
[
54
,
55
]
. Bregman majorant functions varying along iterations, and the consequent variable Bregman MM algorithms, were introduced in our preliminary study
[
56
]
. Bregman divergences provide a more general framework than Euclidean metrics, including in particular the Kullback-Leibler divergence. They hence lead to more flexible choice of majorant approximations, beyond classical quadratic bounds, that not only better fit singular functions such as logarithmic barriers, but naturally account for positivity constraints arising in image processing. The recent paper
[
46
]
proves the subsequential convergence of the variable Bregman MM method in the convex case, and displays its application to maximum likelihood estimation.

In this work, our first contribution is to show the convergence of variable Bregman MM algorithm iterates in the nonconvex nonsmooth setting under KŁ assumption. To establish our convergence theorem, we demonstrate that the proposed scheme fits into the broader
gradient-like descent
framework from
[

Furthermore, we provide in this paper a constructive and unifying analysis of the design of variable Bregman majorant functions, with particular emphasis on logarithmic objectives, an important use case widely explored in the computational imaging literature. We show that our framework encompasses several practical MM algorithms providing state-of-the-art solutions of Poisson imaging inverse problems. Our findings are illustrated through numerical benchmarking on a tomographic image reconstruction problem under Poisson noise, with a nonconvex regularization.

Related concepts can be found in the works of

In contrast, our approach considers variable Bregman metrics, which naturally extends variable metric Euclidean-based algorithms such as

2.1
The Bregman Framework

We begin by providing useful reminders on the framework of Bregman divergences induced by Legendre functions, as defined for example in
[
54
]
,
[

]

{definition}

(Legendre function)

Let
h\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Function
h
is called

\bullet

Essentially smooth
if
h
is differentiable on
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h))
and, for all sequences
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, converging to some
x^{\ast}\in\operatorname{bd}(\operatorname{dom}(h))
, we have
\|\nabla h(x^{k})\|\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}+\infty
;

\bullet

Of

if

is essentially smooth and strictly convex on
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h))
.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the class of Legendre functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The class of Legendre functions benefits from properties that will be useful for the analysis. In particular, let us remind the gradient bijection property.

{proposition}

(Gradient of a Legendre function)

[
54
]

Let
h\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we denote by
h^{\ast}
its Legendre-Fenchel conjugate. Then
\nabla h
is a bijection from
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h))
to
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h^{\ast}))
and its inverse is the gradient of the conjugate:
(\nabla h)^{-1}=\nabla h^{\ast}
.

Given the class of Legendre functions, the Bregman distance can be defined as follows.

{definition}

(Bregman Distance)

Let
h\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The
Bregman distance
associated with
h
evaluated at
(x,y)\in\operatorname{dom}(h)\times\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h))
reads

D_{h}(x,y)=h(x)-h(y)-\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle.

(2.1)

The above definition basically expresses the Bregman distance, as the difference at
x
between
h
at
x
and its first order Taylor expansion around
y
.

Example 2.1
.

Let us now give a few important examples of Bregman distances that are widely used in the optimization literature, as well as their associated domain of validity.

\bullet

Euclidean distance:
The Bregman distance generated by
h:x\longmapsto\|x\|^{2}
is equivalent to the regular Euclidean distance.

\bullet

Itakura–Saito distance:
The Bregman distance generated by
h:x=(x_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}\in(0,+\infty)^{N}\longmapsto-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log(x_{n})

(Burg’s entropy)
is sometimes refereed to, as the Itakura–Saito distance (or divergence)
[
3
,
35
]
.

\bullet

Kullback-Leibler divergence:
The Bregman distance generated by
h:x=(x_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}\in[0,+\infty)^{N}\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{n}\log(x_{n})
is
(Shannon’s entropy)
.

The above function is usually named as the Kullback-Leibler divergence, and will play a fundamental role in our analysis in
section

5
, and in our experiments in
section

6
.

These examples illustrate the flexibility of Bregman divergences, which can be tailored to different data structures and problems.

2.2
Variational Analysis

We now present the subdifferentiation tools necessary for our convergence proof.
The classical definition of the limiting subdifferential
\partial f
of a proper lower-semincontinuous function

f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
will be used.

A fundamental property of the limiting subdifferential is its
closedness
.
For any sequence
(x^{k},u^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\text{graph}(\partial f)^{\mathbb{N}}
converging to
(\bar{x},\bar{u})
, if
(f(x^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f(\bar{x})
, then

(\bar{x},\bar{u})\in\text{graph}(\partial f).

The above result is useful to prove that the limit point of a generated sequence is a critical point, that is an element of the set
defined as

\text{crit}(f)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid 0\in\partial f(x)\}.

(2.2)

2.3
Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz Property

Finally, we remind here an essential property that will be key in the derivation of the global convergence of the algorithm.

{definition}

[
11
]

•

For every
\eta>0
, let

\Phi_{\eta}=\Big\{\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,\eta))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}((0,\eta))\mid\phi(0)=0,\;\phi\text{ concave },\;\phi^{\prime}>0\text{ on }[0,\eta)\Big\}

(2.3)

be the class
desingularizing
functions
on
[0,\eta)
.

•

A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is said to satisfy the
Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz
(KŁ) property at
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}(\partial f)
if there exist
\eta>0
, a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
,
and
\phi\in\Phi_{\eta}

such that, for every
x\in U\cap[f(\bar{x})<f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\eta]
, the
KŁ inequality
hereafter holds:

\phi^{\prime}(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))d(0,\partial f(x))\geqslant 1,

(2.4)

where
d(0,\partial f(x))
the least norm element of
\partial f(x)
, itself defined as the projection of 0 on the closed convex set
\partial f(x)
.

One can see that the KŁ property is trivially verified at non critical points of a lower-semicontinuous proper function
f
(by choosing
\phi
linear).

{theorem}

(Uniformized KŁ property)
[
11
, Lemma 3.6]

Let
K
be a nonempty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower-semicontinuous function. Assume that
f
is constant on
K
and that it satisfies the KŁ property at each point of
K
. Then there exist
\eta>0
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
\phi\in\Phi_{\eta}
such that

\displaystyle(\forall\bar{x}\in K)(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})\begin{cases}d(x,K)<\varepsilon\\
f(\bar{x})<f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\eta\end{cases}\;\Longrightarrow\;\phi^{\prime}(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))d(0,\partial f(x))\geqslant 1.

(2.5)

Mathematical Model

We are now ready to present the problem formulation, our assumptions, and the proposed algorithm.

3.1
Problem Formulation

In what follows, we will be interested in the resolution of the following non necessarily convex optimization problem:

\quad\operatorname*{minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x)=f(x)+g(x).

(3.1)

We start by stating below our first set of assumptions on the involved functions in (
3.1
).

Assumption 3.1
.

In problem (
3.1
),

(a)

f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a continuously differentiable function on a non-empty open set
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
;

(b)

g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a proper lower semi-continuous convex function with
\operatorname{dom}g\subset\mathcal{D}
;

(c)

Function
F
is coercive, continuous on its domain and lower bounded by
\underline{F}>-\infty
.

3.1(a)
and
3.1(b)
are standard when solving composite continuous optimization problems with proximal gradient approaches.
3.1(c)
is a typical hypothesis ensuring the well posedness of the minimization problem, and, in particular, the existence of solutions.

{definition}

(Bregman Tangent Majorant)

Let
f
be a differentiable function on an open set
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
z\in\mathcal{D}
a reference point and
h_{z}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let us define

(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad Q_{f}(x,z)=f(z)+\langle\nabla f(z),x-z\rangle+D_{h_{z}}(x,z).

(3.2)

Q_{f}(\cdot,z):\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
in (
3.2
) is a
Bregman Tangent Majorant
of
f
at
z\in\mathcal{D}
, associated with
h_{z}
, if the following majorizing inequality holds:

(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad f(x)\leqslant Q_{f}(x,z).

(3.3)

The key original element of our construction is that the Bregman metric function
h_{z}
can vary according to the reference point
z
, allowing the majorant to dynamically adjust, so encapsulating more complex local geometrical constraints of the function we wish to majorize. This is in relation with the previous work in
[
22
]
, also based on variable metrics, although it was restricted to the Euclidean case. See
section

3.3
for more discussion.

To guarantee the applicability of our framework, we introduce the following assumption.

Assumption 3.2
.

Function
f
in problem (
3.1
), admits a Bregman tangent majorant at any element
z\in\mathcal{D}
, associated with Legendre functions
(h_{z})_{z\in\mathcal{D}}
that satisfies

(\forall z\in\mathcal{D})\quad\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h_{z})).

Section

5
will provide examples of constructions and properties of Bregman tangent majorants, for an important class of functions in image recovery, involving a Kullback-Leibler divergence composed with a linear operator.

3.2
Proposed algorithm

Under
3.1
and
3.2
, we can construct our proposed MM algorithm to solve problem (
3.1
). Starting from an element in
\operatorname{dom}g\subset\mathcal{D}
, we iteratively define the next iterate as the minimizer of a Bregman majorant approximation to
F=f+g
at the current point. To do so, we rely on the following inequality, direct consequence of (
3.3

z\in\operatorname{dom}g

(\forall x\in\operatorname{dom}g)\quad F(x)\leq Q_{f}(x,z)+g(x).

(3.4)

The resulting numerical solution is presented in

Algorithm 1

Variable Bregman MM Algorithm to solve problem (
3.1
)

Input:

Initial point
x^{0}\in\operatorname{dom}{g}

for

k=0,1,\ldots

do

x^{k+1}=\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(Q_{f}(x,x^{k})+g(x)\right)

end

for

.

For generic choices of

and

, the optimization problem to solve at each iteration may remain challenging. In

5
and
6

Remark 3.2
.

Let us then provide equivalent forms of the

1
that will be useful in our analysis, using (

) and (

):

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(Q_{f}(x,x^{k})+g(x)\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in\operatorname{dom}g}\left(f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle+D_{h_{x^{k}}}(x,x^{k})+g(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in\operatorname{dom}g}\left(\langle\nabla f(x^{k})-\langle\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x^{k}),x\rangle+h_{x^{k}}(x)+g(x)\right).

We now establish the well definedness of the proposed algorithm.
{proposition}

(Well-definedness)

Under
3.1
and
3.2
,

1
is well defined and generates a sequence
(x^{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
\operatorname{dom}g\subset\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, the iteration satisfies

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\;\left(\exists v^{k+1}\in\partial g(x^{k+1})\right)\quad x^{k+1}=\nabla h_{x^{k}}^{\ast}(\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x^{k})-\nabla f(x^{k})-v^{k+1}).

(3.6)

Proof.

We proceed by induction. The initial point
x^{0}\in\operatorname{dom}{g}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
x^{k}\in\mathcal{D}
. The function

\gamma_{x^{k}}:x\longmapsto\langle\nabla f(x^{k})-\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x^{k}),x\rangle+h_{x^{k}}(x)+g(x)
, which appears in
the final expression in

3.2
, is well defined as
\operatorname{dom}g\subset\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}h_{x^{k}})
. Moreover, it is proper and lower semi-continuous (lsc) by

. Finally, up to an additive constant independent on

,
\gamma_{x^{k}}
majorizes

, which is coercive by

. Hence
\gamma_{x^{k}}
is coercive, and admits a minimizer belonging to
\operatorname{dom}g\subset\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, since

is of Legendre type, it is strictly convex on
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}h_{x^{k}})

\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}h_{x^{k}})\supset\mathcal{D}\supset\operatorname{dom}g

(3.7)

Hence,

(\exists v^{k+1}\in\partial g(x^{k+1}))\quad\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x^{k+1})=\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x^{k})-\nabla f(x^{k})-v^{k+1}.

(3.8)

Using

3.3
Relation with existing algorithms

To illustrate the versatility of
algorithm

1
, in this section we show how it encompasses several other well‑known methods.

3.3.1
Variable metric forward-backward algorithm

Let us first consider the case when
3.2
holds with the following Legendre generator:

(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})(\forall z\in\mathcal{D})\quad h_{z}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}M(z)x.

(3.9)

where
M(z)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is symmetric positive definite.
The Bregman distance associated with this generator is given by the squared norm in the metric induced by
M(z)
:

(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})(\forall z\in\mathcal{D})\quad D_{h_{z}}(x,z)=\frac{1}{2}(x-z)^{\top}M(z)(x-z)=\frac{1}{2}\|x-z\|^{2}_{M(z)}.

(3.10)

Plugging the corresponding Bregman tangent majorant
into
algorithm

1
and defining
M_{k}:=M(x_{k})
yields the following iteration, known as the
Variable Metric Proximal Gradient algorithm
(VMFB)
[
22
]
:

x^{k+1}=\arg\min_{x}\left\{\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),\,x-x^{k}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|x-x^{k}\|^{2}_{M_{k}}+g(x)\right\}=\text{prox}_{g}^{M_{k}}\left(x^{k}-M_{k}^{-1}\nabla f(x^{k})\right).

In particular, if
f
is
L_{f}
-Lipschitz smooth, one can set
M_{k}\equiv\alpha_{k}\mathbf{I}_{N}
, with
\alpha_{k}<\frac{2}{L_{f}}
, and recover the classic
forward-backward
(a.k.a. proximal gradient) algorithm.
The convergence of the above algorithms has been extensively studied
[
15
,
22
,
25
,
24
,
13
]
, in convex and non-convex scenarios.

3.3.2
Bregman Proximal Gradient algorithm

In practice, the above Euclidean majorant construction might not exist when the gradient Lipschitz continuity condition on
f
is not met. The Bregman minimization methods
[
48
,
39
]
allow this issue to be addressed by relying on
Lipschitz-like convexity condition
. For a given Legendre function
h
, the pair
(f,h)
satisfies this condition if there exists
L>0
such that

Lh-f\text{ is convex}.

(3.11)

Under this condition, the extended descent lemma in
[
4
]
shows that

\eqref{eq:LC}\Longleftrightarrow(\forall(x,y)\in\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}h)^{2})\quad f(x)\leqslant f(y)+\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle+LD_{h}(x,y)=Q_{f}(x,y).

Using the above Bregman tangent majorant,
algorithm

1
reduces to the Bregman Proximal Gradient (BPG) algorithm.
In practice, BPG iteration is used with a stepsize
\eta_{k}
that satisfies, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\eta_{k}\in(0,1/L)
.
The BPG update with stepsize, is given by

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad x^{k+1}=\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big\{\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}D_{h}(x,x^{k})+g(x)\Big\}.

(3.12)

By setting
h_{x^{k}}=\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}h
in
algorithm

1
, one recovers the BPG algorithm. This shows that this form of the BPG emerges as a special case of VBMM where all the generating functions
(h_{x^{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are proportional to each other. Our proposed
algorithm

1
can thus be viewed as a generalization of the
BPG Algorithm
. The difference is that in BPG, the control over the stepsize from
x^{k}
to
x^{k+1}
is given by the scalar parameter
\eta_{k}
whilst in our scheme, it is given by the choice of our point-dependent generator
h_{x^{k}}
. This adaptability will be illustrated in our experimental scenario, coping with image reconstruction under Poisson statistics.

Remark that, in the study of
[
4
]
, the stepsize range for BPG convergence is extended, by exploiting the symmetry of the Legendre function
h
, leading to the condition
\eta_{k}\in(0,((1+\alpha)-\delta)/L)
, with
\delta\in(0,1+\alpha)
and
\alpha
the symmetry coefficient of
h
. As emphasized in
[
46
, Rem.3.2]
, such exploitation of the symmetry properties can also be made in the case of varying Legendre metrics. In the present paper, for ease of presentation, we have opted for a versatile convergence analysis, not depending on symmetry properties of the Legendre functions
(h_{x^{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

3.3.3

Let us now consider problem (

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad h_{x^{k}}=\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}h

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad x^{k+1}=\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big\{\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}D_{h}(x,x^{k})\Big\}.

(3.13)

In
[
60
]
, the authors show the convergence of MD, by relaxing the standard Lipschitz smoothness assumption into (
3.11
), and by assuming, in addition, the following inequality:

(\forall\eta>0)\;(\exists G>0)\;(\forall x\in\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h))\quad\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle-\eta D_{h}(x,x^{k})\leqslant\frac{\eta}{2}G^{2}.

(3.14)

Our
algorithm

1
with
g\equiv 0
can be understood as a variable metric extension of MD, where the Legendre functions can vary along iterations in a very flexible manner (i.e., non necessarily based on a proportionality rule). Convergence of this scheme under convexity assumptions was studied in
[
46
]
applied to Dirichlet MLE. In this work, we extend these convergence results to non-convex cases, under Lipschitz smoothness assumption.

4
Convergence Analysis

In this section we establish the convergence properties of the proposed Variable Bregman MM algorithm, stated in
algorithm

1
. We will show that under suitable assumptions, the sequence
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by
algorithm

1
forms a
gradient-like descent sequence
for minimizing
F
in the sense of
[
12
, Definition 6.1]
. As shown in
[
12
, Theorem 6.2]
, this property coupled with a KŁ assumption on function
F
in problem (
3.1
), yields the desired global convergence result.

Let
x^{0}\in\operatorname{dom}{g}
be an initial point. In what follows, we will denote by
\Omega(x^{0})
the set of cluster points of
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
starting from point
x^{0}
. Note that by virtue
of
section

3.2
, for all
k\geq 1
we have
x^{k}\in\operatorname{dom}{g}
.

{proposition}

(Objective convergence)

Suppose that
3.1
and
3.2
hold true. Then the sequence
(F(x^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
produced by
algorithm

1
is decreasing, and converges. Moreover, the sequence
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
lies in a compact subset of
\operatorname{dom}g
, and there exists a subsequence
(x^{k_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges to some
\bar{x}\in\Omega(x^{0})
.

Proof.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x^{k+1}=\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{f}(x,x^{k})+g(x)
, therefore

Q_{f}(x^{k+1},x^{k})+g(x^{k+1})\leqslant Q_{f}(x^{k},x^{k})+g(x^{k})=F(x^{k}).

(4.1)

Function
Q_{f}
is a majorant of
f
, hence, by (
3.3
),

F(x^{k+1})=f(x^{k+1})+g(x^{k+1})\leqslant Q_{f}(x^{k+1},x^{k})+g(x^{k+1}).

(4.2)

Combining the above inequalities, it follows that
F(x^{k+1})\leqslant F(x^{k})
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence the sequence
(F(x^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is non increasing, bounded from below by
\underline{F}
(by
3.1(c)
), therefore it converges. Moreover,
(F(x^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is decreasing, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{k}
belongs to the sub-level set
\text{lev}_{\leq F(x_{0})}
, that is bounded and closed, hence compact, by
3.1(c)
and Heine–Borel property. The end of the proof follows by the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem.
∎

We now make the following assumptions on the smooth part of the cost function, i.e.
f
, and on its majorants to ensure that the generated sequence
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the conditions to be a gradient-like descent sequence for solving problem (
3.1
).

Assumption 4.1
.

Consider
algorithm

1
applied to problem (
3.1
) under
3.1
and
3.2
.

(a)

There exists
\underline{\gamma}>0
such that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, for every
x\in\operatorname{dom}{g}
,
Q_{f}(x,x^{k})-f(x)\geq\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2}\|x-x^{k}\|^{2}
, with
\gamma_{k}\in[\underline{\gamma},+\infty)
.

(b)

There exists
\overline{L}>0
such that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\nabla f-\nabla h_{x^{k}}
is
L_{k}
-Lipschitz on every compact subset of
\operatorname{dom}{g}
, with
L_{k}\leq\overline{L}
.

We now provide some examples of iterative algorithms in which
4.1
is satisfied.

Example 4.1
.

(i)

Let us consider the VMFB algorithm
[
22
]
, where the update relies on the majorant function given by

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad Q_{f}(x,x^{k})=f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}(x-x^{k})^{\top}M_{k}(x-x^{k}),

(4.3)

with
M_{k}
symmetric positive definite. For
\nabla f

L_{f}
-Lipschitz, direct calculations, combined with the descent lemma on
f
, show that,

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})(\forall x\in\operatorname{dom}{g})\quad Q_{f}(x,x^{k})-f(x)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}(x-x^{k})^{\top}(M_{k}-L_{f}\mathbf{I}_{N})(x-x^{k}),

which shows that
4.1(a)
is satisfied as soon as

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad M_{k}\succ L_{f}\mathbf{I}_{N},

(4.4)

and
\underline{\gamma}=\min_{k}(\eta_{\rm min}(M_{k})-L_{f})>0
. Moreover,
4.1(b)
holds if
\overline{L}=\max_{k}({L_{f}}+\eta_{\rm max}(M_{k}))<+\infty
.
The standard forward-backward algorithm arises as a special case when, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
M_{k}=(\alpha_{k})^{-1}\mathbf{I}_{N}
where
\alpha_{k}
is the algorithm stepsize assumed to be bounded.

(ii)

In the BPG algorithm, the majorant allowing us to build the iterates is given by

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad Q_{f}(x,x^{k})=f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}D_{h}(x,x^{k}).

(4.5)

Under the Lipschitz-like condition (
3.11
) with constant
L>0
, using the generalized descent lemma,

Q_{f}(x,x^{k})-f(x)\geqslant\Big(\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}-L\Big)D_{h}(x,x^{k}).

(4.6)

Hence in this case,
4.1(a)
holds if
h
is strongly convex and
1/\eta_{k}-L>\epsilon
, with some constant
\epsilon>0
.

4.1(b)
holds
if, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\nabla f-\eta_{k}^{-1}\nabla h
is Lipschitz on every compact of
\operatorname{dom}g
, with a Lipschitz constant independent from
k
. In particular, this is satisfied if both
\nabla f

and
\nabla h
are Lipschitz continuous on
\operatorname{dom}g
, and
\eta_{k}
is bounded.

Other examples are discussed in
section

5
, presenting a constructive framework for majorants tailored for Poisson data fidelity terms.

4.1
Sufficient decrease condition

We derive here the first required condition for
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to be a
gradient-like descent sequence
for minimizing
F
.
{proposition}

(Sufficient decrease condition)

Suppose that
3.1
,
3.2
, and
4.1
hold true. Then it follows that:

(
i
)

The sequence
(F(x^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is non increasing, and we have

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad F(x^{k})-F(x^{k+1})\geqslant({\underline{\gamma}}/{2})\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.

(4.7)

(
ii
)

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}<+\infty
, and, in particular,
\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(
i
)

By the definition of the update in
algorithm

1
,

Q_{f}(x^{k+1},x^{k})+g(x^{k+1})-\left(Q_{f}(x^{k},x^{k})+g(x^{k})\right)\leqslant 0.

Moreover, by
4.1(a)
:

Q_{f}(x^{k+1},x^{k})-f(x^{k+1})-(\underbrace{Q_{f}(x^{k},x^{k})-f(x^{k})}_{=0})\geqslant({\underline{\gamma}}/{2})\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.

Subtracting the second inequality from the first yields the desired sufficient decrease condition

F(x^{k})-F(x^{k+1})\geqslant({\underline{\gamma}}/{2})\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.

(4.8)

(
ii
)

Summing the last inequality, and using
3.1(c)
and
4.1(a)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{1}{\underline{\gamma}}(F(x^{k})-F(x^{k+1}))

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{\underline{\gamma}}(F(x^{0})-F(x^{N+1}))

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{\underline{\gamma}}(F(x^{0})-\underline{F})<+\infty.

The positive-term series
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}
, having bounded partial sums, converges. ∎

4.2
Relative error condition

We now derive the second condition for
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to be a
gradient-like descent sequence
for minimizing
F
(
[
12
, Definition 6.1]
).

{proposition}

(Relative error condition)

Suppose that
3.1
,
3.2
, and
4.1
hold true. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

w^{k+1}=\nabla f(x^{k+1})-\nabla Q_{f}(x^{k+1},x^{k}).

(4.9)

Then the following holds true:

(i)

For every
k\in\mathbb{N},\;w^{k+1}\in\partial F(x^{k+1})
;

(ii)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N},\;\|w^{k+1}\|\leqslant{\overline{L}}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

Using the optimality condition:
0\in\nabla Q_{f}(x^{k+1},x^{k})+\partial g(x^{k+1})
,

(\exists v^{k+1}\in\partial g(x^{k+1}))\quad\;\nabla Q_{f}(x^{k+1},x^{k})+v^{k+1}=0.

Adding and subtracting
\nabla f(x^{k+1})
yields
w^{k+1}=\nabla f(x^{k+1})+v^{k+1}\in\partial F(x^{k+1})
.

(ii)

By construction of the Bregman tangent majorant,
for every
x\in\operatorname{dom}g
,

\nabla Q_{f}(x,x^{k})=\nabla f(x^{k})+\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x)-\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x^{k}).

(4.10)

We have thus

\displaystyle\|w^{k+1}\|

\displaystyle=\|\nabla f(x^{k+1})-\nabla Q_{f}(x^{k+1},x^{k})\|

\displaystyle=\|\nabla f(x^{k+1})-\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x^{k+1})-(\nabla f(x^{k})-\nabla h_{x^{k}}(x^{k}))\|.

(4.11)

By
section

4
,
x^{k+1}
lies in a compact set, hence using
4.1(b)
,

\|w^{k+1}\|\leqslant\overline{L}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|.

(4.12)

By
section

4.1
, we conclude that
\|w^{k+1}\|\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0
, and that the relative error condition is met.

∎

Note also that
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded by
section

4
.
Leveraging the above results, we now show that all cluster points of
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are critical points of
F
, and that the set
\Omega(x^{0})
satisfies the assumptions for
section

2.3
.

{proposition}

(Subsequential convergence)

Under
3.1
,
3.2
, and
4.1
,
\Omega(x^{0})
is a non empty compact subset of
\operatorname{crit}(F)
and

d(x^{k},\Omega(x^{0}))\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0.

(4.13)

Moreover,
F
is constant and finite on
\Omega(x^{0})
.

Proof.

The proof of this standard lemma in the context of
gradient-like descent sequences
can be found in
[
12
, Lemma 6.1]
.
∎

4.3
Main convergence result

We now introduce our final assumption, to establish our convergence result on the algorithm’s iterates, without requiring convexity of
f
.

Assumption 4.2
.

In problem (
3.1
),
f
satisfies the KŁ property at each point of
\operatorname{dom}(\partial F)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\operatorname{dom}(\partial g)
.

We are now ready to establish the finite‑length property of the iterates and guarantee convergence to a single critical point. By combining
3.1
,
3.2
,
4.1
, and
4.2
, we can apply the uniformized KŁ result from
section

2.3
to the compact set
K=\Omega(x^{0})
, yielding the existence of
\eta>0
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
\phi\in\Phi_{\eta}
such that

(\forall\bar{x}\in\Omega(x^{0}))\quad(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\begin{cases}d(x,\Omega(x^{0}))<\varepsilon\\
F(\bar{x})<F(x)<F(\bar{x})+\eta\end{cases}

(4.14)

implies

\phi^{\prime}(F(x)-F(\bar{x}))\,d(0,\partial F(x))\geqslant 1.

(4.15)

In the following result we combine this inequality with the sufficient decrease condition established in
section

4.1
as well as the bounds on the subgradients given by the relative error condition in
section

4.2
to show that under our assumptions, the sequence produced by
algorithm

1
is a Cauchy sequence that converges to a critical point of
F
.

{theorem}

(Global Convergence)

Under
3.1
,
3.2
,
4.1
, and
4.2
, the sequence
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
produced by
algorithm

1
has finite length, i.e.,

\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|<+\infty.

(4.16)

Moreover,
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a critical point of
F
.

Proof.

Combining all
3.1
,
3.2
, and
4.1
ensures that the sequence
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by
algorithm

1
satisfies all the axioms for being a
gradient-like descent sequence for minimizing
F
, as defined in
[
12
, Def. 6.1]
. Moreover,
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded by
section

4
. The proof for the convergence result is then given in
[
12
, Thm 6.2]
.
∎

4.4
Convergence Rate

We finally derive the convergence rate of
algorithm

1
, using the following standard KŁ result.

{theorem}

(Convergence Rate)

Assume that
3.1
,
3.2
,
4.1
, and
4.2
with desingularizing function
\phi:t\longmapsto ct^{1-\theta}
with

and

5
A unifying framework for Bregman majorization methods

In this section, we present several useful Bregman majorant properties as well as majorization techniques that we then apply to give an inventory of Bregman tangent majorants for the Poisson data fidelity term.

5.1
Bregman majorants and their properties

We refer the reader to
eq.

3.3
, for the notion of Bregman tangent majorant.
Let us start this section by defining the following order, allowing to compare different choices of Bregman metrics when constructing majorants.

{definition}

(Order relation between Bregman metrics)

Let
(h_{1},h_{2})\in(\mathcal{L}\big(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)^{2}
.We define the following tightness order relation:

\displaystyle h_{1}\preceq h_{2}\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left(\forall\,(x,y)\in\bigl(\operatorname{dom}(h_{1})\cap\operatorname{dom}(h_{2})\bigr)\times\Bigl(\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h_{1}))\cap\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h_{2}))\Bigr)\right)

\displaystyle\quad D_{h_{1}}(x,y)\leqslant D_{h_{2}}(x,y).

We can now state the following lemma.

{lemma}

(Bregman tangent majorants characterization from order)

Let
f
satisfying
3.1(a)
.
Let
z\in\mathcal{D}
. Assume that there exists
Q_{1,f}(\cdot,z)
, Bregman tangent majorant of
f
at
z
, associated with
h_{1,z}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with

\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}h_{1,z})
. Let
h_{2,z}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that

\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}h_{2,z})
and

h_{1,z}\preceq h_{2,z}
. Then,
Q_{2,f}(\cdot,z)

is also a Bregman tangent majorant of
f
at
z
, associated with
h_{2,z}
.

Proof.

By
section

5.1
and (
3.2
),
h_{1,z}\preceq h_{2,z}
yields that, for all
x\in\mathcal{D}
,
Q_{1,f}(x,z)\leq Q_{2,f}(x,z)
, hence the result using (
3.3
).
∎

We now provide the following lemma, that will allow to construct majorants for linear composite functions, by linearly combining majorants of each individual terms.
{lemma}

(Linearity in Bregman metrics)

Let
(\alpha,\beta)\in[0,+\infty)^{2}
and
(h_{1},h_{2})\in(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{2}
. For all
(x,y)\in(\operatorname{dom}(h_{1})\cap\operatorname{dom}(h_{2}))\times(\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}(h_{1})\cap\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(h_{2}))
, we have

D_{\alpha h_{1}+\beta h_{2}}(x,y)=\alpha D_{h_{1}}(x,y)+\beta D_{h_{2}}(x,y).

Proof.

Direct consequence of the bilinearity of the scalar product in (
2.1
).
∎

{corollary}

(Additivity property of the Majorants)

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
satisfy
3.1(a)
on the open set
\mathcal{D}

with Bregman tangent majorants
Q_{f_{1}}(\cdot,z),Q_{f_{2}}(\cdot,z)
at
z\in\mathcal{D}
, associated respectively with
h_{1,z}
and
h_{2,z}

with
\mathcal{D}\subset\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}h_{1,z})\cap\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}h_{2,z})
. Let
(\alpha,\beta)\in[0,+\infty)^{2}
.
Then, the function
\alpha f_{1}+\beta f_{2}
satisfies
3.1(a)
, and a Bregman tangent majorant of it at
z
is
\alpha Q_{f_{1}}(\cdot,z)+\beta Q_{f_{2}}(\cdot,z)
, associated with
\alpha h_{1,z}+\beta h_{2,z}
.

Proof.

Consequence of
section

5.1
.
∎

We will exploit this result in our constructions of majorant functions, in
section

6
.

We now present a result regarding the construction of Bregman majorant functions, relying on second-order properties of function
f
.

{proposition}

(Hessian Characterization)

Let
f
be a twice-continuously differentiable function on an open set
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

and let
z\in\mathcal{D}
. Let
h_{z}
be a twice-continuously differentiable Legendre function on
\mathcal{D}
.

(i)

Q_{f}(\cdot,z)
is a Bregman tangent majorant of
f
at
z
associated with
h_{z}
if and only if

(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad(x-z)^{\top}C_{h_{z}-f}(x,z)(x-z)\geq 0,

(5.1)

where
C_{h}
is defined, for every function
h
twice-differentiable on
\mathcal{D}
, as

(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad C_{h}(x,z)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\nabla^{2}h\big((1-t)z+tx)\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

(5.2)

(ii)

Let
h^{\prime}_{z}
be another twice-differentiable Legendre function on
\mathcal{D}
. Then,
h_{z}
is tighter than
h^{\prime}_{z}
if and only if

(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad(x-z)^{\top}C_{h^{\prime}_{z}-h_{z}}(x,z)(x-z)\geq 0.

(5.3)

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathcal{D}
.

(i)

By definition,
Q_{f}(\cdot,z)
is a Bregman tangent majorant of
f
at
z
associated with
h_{z}
if and only if,
for every
x\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle Q_{f}(x,z)-f(x)\geq 0

(5.4)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle f(z)+\langle\nabla f(z),x-z\rangle+D_{h_{z}}(x,z)-f(x)\geq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle f(z)+\langle\nabla f(z),x-z\rangle+(h_{z}(x)-h_{z}(z)-\langle\nabla h_{z}(z),x-z\rangle-f(x)\geq 0.

(5.5)

On the other hand, according to second-order Taylor formula with Laplace integral remainder, for all
x
in
\mathcal{D}
,

f(x)=f(z)+\langle\nabla f(z),x-z\rangle+(x-z)^{\top}C_{f}(x,z)(x-z).

(5.6)

Furthermore, for all
x
in
\mathcal{D}
,

h_{z}(x)=h_{z}(z)+\langle\nabla h_{z}(z),x-z\rangle+(x-z)^{\top}C_{h_{z}}(x,z)(x-z).

(5.7)

Plugging the last two relations in (
5.5
) and using the fact that

C_{h_{z}-f}=C_{h_{z}}-C_{f}
by linearity of the integral and the Hessian yields the necessary and sufficient condition (
5.1
).

(ii)

According to
section

5.1
,
h_{z}\preceq h^{\prime}_{z}
if and only if, for every
x\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle D_{h^{\prime}_{z}}(x,z)-D_{h_{z}}(x,z)\geq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(h^{\prime}_{z}(x)-h^{\prime}_{z}(y)-\langle\nabla h^{\prime}_{z}(z),x-z\rangle)-(h_{z}(x)-h_{z}(z)-\langle\nabla h_{z}(z),x-z\rangle)\geq 0.

The result again follows from second-order Taylor expansions with Laplace integral remainder and the convexity of
h_{z}
.∎

We recall hereafter a result stemming from the literature of image reconstruction, for building quadratic majorant functions.

{lemma}

(Quadratic majorization)
[
29
]

Let
\psi
be a twice-continuously differentiable function on
[-\tau,+\infty)
with
\tau>0
, and let

(\forall z\in[-\tau,+\infty))\quad c_{\psi,\tau}(z)=\begin{cases}\ddot{\psi}(-\tau)&\mbox{if $z=-\tau$}\\
\displaystyle 2\frac{\psi(-\tau)-\psi(z)+\dot{\psi}(z)(z+\tau)}{(z+\tau)^{2}}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(5.8)

Let us assume that
\ddot{\psi}
is decreasing on
[-\tau,+\infty)
and that
\forall z\in[-\tau,+\infty),c_{\psi,\tau}(z)>0
.
For every
z\in[-\tau,+\infty)
,
let

(\forall x\in\mathbb{R})\quad h_{z}(x)=\frac{1}{2}c_{\psi,\tau}(z)x^{2}.

(5.9)

Then,
for every
z\in\mathcal{D}=(-\tau,+\infty)
,
Q_{\psi}(\cdot,z)
given by (
3.3
) with
h_{z}
defined as in (
5.9
), is a Bregman tangent majorant of
\psi
at
z
associated with
h_{z}
. In addition, (
3.3
) with

f=\psi
extends to
(x,z)\in[-\tau,+\infty)^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
z\in[-\tau,+\infty)
. Let us first notice that as
c_{\psi,\tau}
is strictly positive-valued,
h_{z}
is a twice-differentiable Legendre function.
We first establish the majorizing property in the open set
\mathcal{D}
.
By performing a second-order Taylor expansion, we have for every

z>-\tau
:

\psi(-\tau)=\psi(z)+\dot{\psi}(z)(-\tau-z)+(z+\tau)^{2}C_{\psi}(-\tau,z).

(5.10)

Hence,

C_{\psi}(-\tau,z)=\frac{\psi(-\tau)-\psi(z)+\dot{\psi}(z)(z+\tau)}{(z+\tau)^{2}}.

(5.11)

On the other hand, since
\ddot{\psi}
is a decreasing function,

\displaystyle(\forall x\in[-\tau,+\infty))\quad C_{\psi}(x,z)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,\ddot{\psi}((1-t)z+tx)\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,\ddot{\psi}((1-t)z-t\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=C_{\psi}(-\tau,z)

\displaystyle=2C_{\psi}(-\tau,z)\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=2C_{\psi}(-\tau,z)C_{(\cdot)^{2}/2}(x,z).

(5.12)

When
z=-\tau
, using similar arguments,

\displaystyle(\forall x\in[-\tau,+\infty))\quad C_{\psi}(x,-\tau)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,\ddot{\psi}(tx-\tau(1-t))\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,\ddot{\psi}(-\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\ddot{\psi}(-\tau)C_{(\cdot)^{2}/2}(x,z).

(5.13)

In summary,

C_{\frac{1}{2}c_{\psi,\tau}(z)(\cdot)^{2}-\psi}(x,z)=C_{c_{\psi,\tau}(z)(\cdot)^{2}/2}(x,z)-C_{\psi}(x,z)\geq 0,

(5.14)

which, according to

section

5.1
5.0(i)
shows that

Q_{\psi}(\cdot,z)
is a Bregman tangent majorant of
\psi
at

z
associated with
h_{z}
.
The majorization property
(
3.3
)
with
f=\psi
extends by continuity to
(x,z)\in[-\tau,+\infty)^{2}
.
∎

We finish this subsection with the following lemma, consequence of Jensen’s inequality for convex functions (see
[
21
, Lemma 1.9]
[
38
]
, for similar results, although not formulated under the Bregman majorizing framework).

{lemma}

(Logarithmic separable majorization)

Let
\theta=(\theta_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}\in[0,+\infty)^{N}\setminus\{0_{N}\}
,
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\in[0,+\infty)^{2}
and let

(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad\psi(x)=-\ln(\theta^{\top}x+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})

(5.15)

where
\mathcal{D}=(-\zeta\delta_{1},+\infty)^{N}
with
\zeta=1/\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}
.
For every
z=(z_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}\in\mathcal{D}
,
let

(\forall x=(x_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}\in\mathcal{D})\quad h_{z}(x)=-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{n}(z_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1})}{\theta^{\top}z+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}\ln(x_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1}).

(5.16)

Then, for all
z\in\mathcal{D}
, function
Q_{\psi}(\cdot,z)
with
h_{z}
given in (
5.16
), is a Bregman tangent majorant of
\psi
at
z
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathcal{D}
. First notice that
h_{z}
, as defined in (
5.16
), is a Legendre function, and that it is also twice differentiable on
\mathcal{D}
. Then, using (
5.15
), for every
x=(x_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle=-\ln\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}(x_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1})+\delta_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi(z)-\ln\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{n}(z_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1})}{\theta^{\top}z+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}\frac{x_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1}}{z_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1}}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{\theta^{\top}z+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\psi(z)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{n}(z_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1})}{\theta^{\top}z+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}\ln\left(\frac{x_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1}}{z_{n}+\zeta\delta_{1}}\right)-\frac{\delta_{2}}{\theta^{\top}z+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}\ln 1

(5.17)

\displaystyle=\psi(z)-\frac{\theta^{\top}(x-z)}{\theta^{\top}z+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}+h_{z}(x)-h_{z}(z)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{n}(x_{n}-z_{n})}{\theta^{\top}z+\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}

\displaystyle=\psi(z)+\langle\nabla\psi(z),x-z\rangle+D_{h_{z}}(x,z),

(5.18)

where Jensen’s inequality is used to get (
5.17
).
This yields the sought majorant.
∎

5.2.4
Relation with majorant construction in the literature

There is a significant amount of literature addressing the construction of separable majorant functions for imaging inverse problems under Poisson noise, and more generally for Kullback-Leibler minimization problems. This methodology likely originates from the EM-based approach
[
27
]
which gave rise to the MLEM method
[
57
]
, equivalently known as the Richardson-Lucy algorithm
[
53
,
45
]
, for minimizing the Poisson data fidelity term under positivity constraints. Convergence proofs for this algorithm can be found in
[
50
,
10
]
. The majorant function
Maj
-
6
is identical to the one used to build the aforementioned MLEM algorithm, and our proof follows the same construction mechanism as the one detailed for instance in
[
26
,
10
]
. Due to its form, which includes a logarithmic function, this majorant function promotes positivity of the iterates of the resulting MM scheme. It has subsequently been used in many works in the field of image restoration: for instance, let us cite
[
62
]
for maximum-a-posteriori PET image reconstruction, and
[
36
,
47
]
for AI-based PET reconstruction methods. As emphasized in several works, including
[
14
,
63
,
42
]
, the construction of the majorant function
Maj
-
6
is also tightly linked to split gradient algorithms, that propose multiplicative updates for positivity-constrained optimization problems. Similar majorant constructions are also at the core of the alternating multiplicative algorithms for nonnegative matrix factorization
[
43
,
33
]
. The approach we use in
Maj
-
3
, decomposing the majorizing terms depending on derivative signs, was also employed in
[
19
]
in the context of barrier function majorization and in
[
14
]
for building accelerating variable metrics in proximal algorithms.

Paraboloidal (i.e., quadratic) surrogates for the Poisson data fidelity term, such as those presented in
section

5.2.3
, were originally proposed in
[
30
]
and applied to PET dynamic imaging
[
61
]
. The quadratic majorant
Maj
-
7
is retrieved by applying the same technique as in
[
30
]
after Jensen’s inequality. Majorant
Maj
-
9
can be traced back to the separable paraboloid algorithm of
[
61
]
. Similar constructions for quadratic separable surrogates have also been used to approximate non-separable regularization terms, for instance in a patch-based regularization in PET imaging
[
62
]
.

The Log-shift majorant
Maj
-
1
from
section

5.2.1
was introduced in
[
32
]
to derive a penalized MLEM algorithm accounting for a non-negative background term, building upon the statistical framework of generalized EM
[
31
]
. The Log-shift construction was shown in
[
32
]
to yield significant acceleration of the reconstruction algorithm compared to the Log-0 majorant
Maj
-
6
. From a statistical viewpoint, the Log-shift approach exploits a more informative complete-data space than the Log-0 majorant, for the estimates related to background events.

In a nutshell, our constructive approach allows us to recover many majorants found in the literature. However, up to the best of our knowledge, this section is the first attempt to present a unified view of the many majorizing constructions. Moreover, our propositions provide the first formal comparison of the various majorant approximations, thanks to the introduced Bregman-based order relation.

Building on the theoretical VBMM framework and Bregman Majorizing techniques presented in previous sections, we now address the challenging problem of Positron Emission Tomography (PET) image reconstruction.

6.1
Problem Statement

In PET, the goal is to recover an image of the radiotracer activity concentration

7
Conclusion

In this work, we established new theoretical guarantees for the variable Bregman majorization–minimization algorithm. The proposed proof
is grounded on general
assumptions which apply to a wide class
of nonsmooth nonconvex objectives. Iterates convergence to a critical point was established under the Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz property. We illustrated the versatility of the proposed framework by deriving constructive rules for Bregman majorant design, and we derived a broad class of separable majorants for Poisson data fidelity term. Finally, we provided a numerical experiment demonstrating the practical feasibility of the proposed scheme in the nonconvex setting. The proposed approach should be applicable to other domains than PET and allow the development of data-driven strategies grounded on its use.

. Then, it is enough to prove that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\nabla f-\nabla h_{x^{k}}=\nabla\mathcal{L}+\nabla\mathcal{R}-\nabla h_{x^{k}}
is Lipschitz continuous on any compact subset of
[\epsilon_{0},+\infty)^{N}
, with a constant independent from
k
.

Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla\mathcal{L}

We have

(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad\nabla\mathcal{L}(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(1-\frac{y_{m}}{H_{m}x+b_{m}}\right)H_{m}^{\top}=H^{\top}\!\left(1-y\oslash(Hx+b),\right)

(C.1)

where
\oslash
denotes
the componentwise vector division.
This yields

(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{y_{m}}{(H_{m}x+b_{m})^{2}}H_{m}^{\top}H_{m}=H^{\top}\,\mathrm{Diag}\!\left(y\oslash(Hx+b)^{.2}\right)H,

(C.2)

where
(\cdot)^{.2}
is the square componentwise operation.
Since
b\in(0,+\infty)^{M}
,
\epsilon_{0}\geq 0
, and
H\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}_{+}
, we have

(\forall x\in[\epsilon_{0},+\infty)^{N})\quad(Hx+b)_{m}\geq b_{m}>0

which yields

(\forall x\in[\epsilon_{0},+\infty)^{N})\quad\left|\!\left|\!\left|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}(x)\right|\!\right|\!\right|\leq\left|\!\left|\!\left|H\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2}\underset{1\leq m\leq M}{\text{max}}\frac{y_{m}}{b_{m}^{2}}\triangleq L_{\mathcal{L}}.

(C.3)

This shows that
\mathcal{L}
has a Lipschitz continuous gradient on
[\epsilon_{0},+\infty)^{N}
with a Lipschitz constant
L_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Lipchitz continuity of
\nabla\mathcal{R}

Let us denote
\omega:t\mapsto\dot{\vartheta}(t)/t=(4\delta^{2})/(2\delta^{2}+t^{2})^{2}
. Then, the gradient of the regularization term (
6.3
) is expressed as

(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\nabla\mathcal{R}(x)=\lambda\,\Delta^{\top}\text{Diag}\left(\Big(\omega(\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|){\mathbf{I}_{2}}\Big)_{1\leq n\leq N}\right)\Delta x+\varepsilon x.

(C.4)

Moreover,
the Hessian of
\mathcal{R}
reads

(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})\;\;\nabla^{2}\mathcal{R}(x)=\lambda\Delta^{\top}\text{Diag}\left(\Big({\omega(\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|)\mathbf{I}_{2}+\frac{\dot{\omega}(\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|)}{\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|}[\Delta x]_{n}[\Delta x]_{n}^{\top}}\Big)_{1\leq n\leq N}\right)\Delta+\varepsilon\mathbf{I}_{N}.

(C.5)

Since the eigenvalues of
[\Delta x]_{n}[\Delta x]_{n}^{\top}
with
n\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket

are
\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|^{2}
and

0
, and, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\dot{\omega}(t)t+\omega(t)=\ddot{\vartheta}(t)
, we have,
for each
2\times 2
block diagonal term,

\left|\!\left|\!\left|\omega(\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|)\mathbf{I}_{2}+\frac{\dot{\omega}(\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|)}{\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|}[\Delta x]_{n}[\Delta x]_{n}^{\top}\right|\!\right|\!\right|=\max\left(\omega(\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|),|\ddot{\vartheta}(\|[\Delta x]_{n}\|)|\right).

(C.6)

Moreover,

\displaystyle(\forall t\in\mathbb{R})\quad\ddot{\vartheta}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\delta^{2}(2\delta^{2}-3t^{2})}{(2\delta^{2}+t^{2})^{3}}\in\left[-\frac{1}{4\delta^{2}},\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right],\quad\text{and}\quad\omega(t)\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\Big],

(C.7)

Hence,

(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\left|\!\left|\!\left|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{R}(x)\right|\!\right|\!\right|\leq\frac{\lambda}{\delta^{2}}\left|\!\left|\!\left|\Delta\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2}+\varepsilon\triangleq L_{\mathcal{R}}.

(C.8)

This shows that
\mathcal{R}
has a Lipschitz continuous gradient on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(and thus on
[\epsilon_{0},+\infty)^{N}
) with Lipschitz constant
L_{\mathcal{R}}
.

Log-shift Majorant

Similarly, let us now consider the separable Log-shift majorant
Maj-
1
(
5.21
) for
\mathcal{L}
. We have, for all
z\in\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad D_{h_{1,z}}(x,z)=-\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{1,n}(z)\ln\left(\frac{x_{n}+\rho}{z_{n}+\rho}\right)-\frac{a_{1,n}(z)}{z_{n}+\rho}(x_{n}-z_{n}).

Then, for every
n\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
,

\displaystyle(\forall x\in\mathcal{D})\quad[\nabla_{x}Q_{f}(x,z)]_{n}

\displaystyle=\nabla f(z_{n})-\frac{a_{1,n}(z_{n})}{x_{n}+\rho}+\frac{a_{1,n}(z_{n})}{z_{n}+\rho}+M_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{n}-z_{n})

\displaystyle=d_{n}(z_{n})-\frac{a_{1,n}(z_{n})}{x_{n}+\rho}+M_{\mathcal{R}}x_{n}

where
d_{n}(z_{n})=\nabla f(z_{n})+\frac{a_{1,n}(z_{n})}{z_{n}+\rho}-M_{\mathcal{R}}z_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle[\nabla_{x}Q_{f}(x,z)]_{n}=0

\displaystyle\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;M_{\mathcal{R}}x_{n}^{2}+(d_{n}(z_{n})+M_{\mathcal{R}}\rho)x_{n}+\rho d_{n}(z_{n})-a_{1,n}(z_{n})=0,

\boxed{(\forall k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast})\quad x^{k+1}=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}\left(\Big(\frac{\sqrt{(d_{n}(x^{k})-M_{\mathcal{R}}\rho)^{2}+4M_{\mathcal{R}}a_{1,n}(x^{k})}-d_{n}(x^{k})-M_{\mathcal{R}}\rho}{2M_{\mathcal{R}}}\Big)_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}\right).}

(D.1)
