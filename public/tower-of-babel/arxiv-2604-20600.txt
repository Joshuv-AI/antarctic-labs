Abstract
. We investigate the geometric behavior of τ ⁡ ( E ) \tau(E) for bounded finite-perimeter sets E ⊂ ℝ n E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} , where τ ⁡ ( E ) \tau(E) is the trace constant introduced by Figalli–Maggi–Pratelli [Invent. Math. 2010]. This quantity is a key ingredient in proving a quantitative isoperimetric inequality with the optimal exponent. We first show that for every ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 one can find a bounded open set Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} that is very close to the unit ball 𝔹 n \mathbb{B}^{n} in the sense that τ ⁡ ( 𝔹 n ) > τ ⁡ ( Ω ) > τ ⁡ ( 𝔹 n ) − ϵ and P ⁡ ( Ω ​ Δ ​ 𝔹 n ) ≤ C ⁡ ( n ) ​ ϵ , \tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})>\tau(\Omega)>\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad P(\Omega\Delta\mathbb{B}^{n})\leq C(n)\epsilon, while at the same time the complement of Ω \Omega has infinitely many connected components. Thus, τ ⁡ ( Ω ) \tau(\Omega) can be made arbitrarily close to τ ⁡ ( 𝔹 n ) \tau(\mathbb{B}^{n}) even when Ω \Omega has highly intricate geometry. We then establish, under a mild additional hypothesis, the equivalence between a condition formulated in terms of τ \tau and two classical criteria from the literature for open sets that admit trace inequalities. As a consequence, we obtain the John-type characterization of domains that support a trace inequality, assuming the ball separation property.

1.
Introduction

In their seminal work
[
8
]
, Figalli, Maggi and Pratelli applied a mass transportation approach to show a quantitative version of the isoperimetric inequality, a high-profile topic, stated as follows:
For any set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|E|=|\mathbb{B}^{n}|
, where
\mathbb{B}^{n}
denotes the standard unit Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it holds that

(1.1)

P(E)-P(\mathbb{B}^{n})\geq c(n)\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|E\Delta(x+\mathbb{B}^{n})|^{2}.

Remarkably, the constant
c(n)>0
is independent of the set
E
.
Here the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
of the set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
|E|
, and the symmetric difference between
E
and
x+\mathbb{B}^{n}
is denoted by
E\Delta(x+\mathbb{B}^{n})
. For any function
u
that is locally integrable on
E
and whose distributional derivative is a Radon measure, we denote the total variation measure of
u
by
\|Du\|(E)
, and define the perimeter of
E
as

P(E):=\|D\chi_{E}\|(\mathbb{R}^{n});

see Section
2
for preliminaries and more specific definitions.

A crucial step in their proof is to show that, any set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be modified to a new (open) set
G
, which in particular satisfies

\tau(G)\geq 1+\delta_{0},

where the definition of
\tau
is given as follows; we refer to
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
for more detailed information.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter with
0<|E|<+\infty
. The trace constant
\tau(E)
of
E
is defined by setting

\tau(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{P(F)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial^{*}F)}:F\subset E,0<|F|\leq\frac{|E|}{2}\right\}\geq 1.

The constant
\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})
has already been studied in
[
5
]
. To be more specific, let
O
be the origin and
B(P,r)
be the ball with radius
r>0
and center
P
located at the negative
x_{1}
-half-axis, such that

\partial B(P,r)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\neq\emptyset
. Choose a point
Q\in\partial B(P,r)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Let

E_{\varphi,\vartheta}=\mathbb{B}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}(P,\,r)

be the half-moon shaped set as in Figure
1
, where
\vartheta\in(0,\pi)
denotes the angle between the vector
\overrightarrow{PO}
and the vector
\overrightarrow{OQ}
, and

\varphi\in(0,\vartheta)
denotes the angle between

\overrightarrow{PO}
and the vector
\overrightarrow{PQ}
.

Observe that the operator
K_{\rm med}
defined in
[
5
, (2.25)]
satisfies

\tau(E)=\frac{1}{K_{\rm med}(E)}+1.

Then
[
5
, Theorem 2.3]
together with
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
implies the following.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

The infimum in the definition of
\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is attained when
F
is exactly taken as
E_{\varphi,\vartheta}
, where
\varphi
and
\vartheta
satisfy the condition
[
5
, (4.2)]
with
\varphi_{\rho}=\varphi,\,\vartheta_{\rho}=\vartheta
and
\rho=1/2
there. In particular, one has

(1.2)

\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})=\frac{P(E_{\varphi,\vartheta})}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E_{\varphi,\vartheta}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}>1,

and via
[
5
, Lemma 4.3]
,

(1.3)

|E_{\varphi,\vartheta}|=|\mathbb{B}^{n}\setminus E_{\varphi,\vartheta}|.

Figure 1.
The half-moon shaped set
E_{\varphi,\vartheta}
is colored in grey.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. We say that
\Omega
supports a
(1,1)
-trace inequality for functions of bounded variation if there exists a bounded linear operator
T:BV(\Omega)\to L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, such that

(1.4)

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|Tu-c|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C_{T}\|Du\|(\Omega)\quad\text{for any }u\in BV(\Omega)

holds for some positive constant
C_{T}
independent of
u
. Then thanks to
\tau(G)\geq 1+\delta_{0}
, one gets that
G
supports a trace inequality of the following form:

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\partial^{*}G}|tr_{G}u-c|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n){\delta_{0}}^{-1}\|Du\|(G^{(1)})\quad\text{for any }u\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
tr_{G}u
, called the inner trace of
u
on
G
, is defined on the reduced boundary
\partial^{*}G
; see
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
.
The construction of
G
is rather subtle and is obtained via finding a maximal critical set in
E
(see
[
8
, Lemma 3.2]
). According to the trace inequality above, with some nontrivial effort one can finally deduce that the original set
E
satisfies (
1.1
).

For classical treatments of traces of
BV
-functions in Euclidean spaces, we refer to
[
1
, Chapter 3]
and
[
7
, Chapter 5]
. Recent developments in metric measure spaces can be found in works such as
[
4
,
13
,
15
,
17
]
.
The aim of the current manuscript is to further study the geometric properties of the trace constant
\tau
.

We start with the following example, telling that without any further assumptions,
the geometry of a set could be bad even if the trace constant of such a set is close to
\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})
. This example was briefly mentioned in
[
3
, Page 578]
for Sobolev–Poincaré inequalities in planar domains.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\,n\geq 2
of finite perimeter,
|\Omega|<+\infty
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial^{*}\Omega)=0
, close to the unit ball in the sense that

\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{n}\setminus D,\quad P(\Omega\Delta\mathbb{B}^{n})\leq C(n)\epsilon,

together with

\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})>\tau(\Omega)>\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-\epsilon.

Moreover, up to a modification on a set of
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measure zero,

D
consists of countably infinitely many balls.

Regarding the geometric characterization of domains supporting the
(1,1)
-trace inequality (
1.4
), a lot of pioneering work has already been done. For instance, under the additional assumptions

(1.5)

P(\Omega)<+\infty,\quad 0<|\Omega|<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial^{*}\Omega)=0,

Maz’ya established a sufficient and necessary condition, in a similar manner to

\tau(\Omega)\geq 1+\delta_{0},

for the open set
\Omega
supporting
(1,1)
-trace inequality (
1.4
)
[
18
, Theorem 9.6.4]
; see also Lemma
2.3
below.

Meanwhile, analogous to
[
18
, Theorem 9.6.4]
, Ziemer introduced another sufficient condition for those bounded domains supporting (
1.4
); see
[
22
, Theorem 5.10.7]
. Such domains are called
admissible domains
; see Definition
2.4
for detailed information.

For any set
\Omega
satisfying the conditions (
1.5
), we show that
\Omega
can be assumed to be open up to a modification on a set of
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measure zero; see Lemma
4.1
for a detailed explanation. This allows us to study the equivalence between the following three conditions under the assumption (
1.5
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\,n\geq 2
be a set of finite perimeter satisfying (
1.5
). Then up to a modification on a set of
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measure zero of
\Omega
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The trace constant
\tau(\Omega)\geq 1+\delta_{0}
for some constant
\delta_{0}>0
;

(ii)

\Omega
is an open set satisfying (
1.4
) with constant
C_{T}>0
;

(iii)

\Omega
is a
\Theta
-admissible domain.

The dependence of the constants is given as follows: If
\Omega
satisfies (i), then
\Omega
meets (ii) with constant
C_{T}=(\tau(\Omega)-1)^{-1}
; if
\Omega
satisfies (ii), then
\Omega
meets (iii) with constant
\Theta=C_{T}
; if
\Omega
satisfies (iii), then
\Omega
meets (i), with constant
\tau(\Omega)
(or more precisely, the lower bound of
\delta_{0}
) depending on both
n
and
\Omega
itself.

Remark 1.5
.

In (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) of Theorem
1.4
, the dependence of the constant
\tau(\Omega)
cannot be determined solely by
\Theta
. An example is given as follows. Consider the rectangle

R_{l}:=(-l,l)\times(-1,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},

which is a
\sqrt{2}
-admissible domain for any
l\gg 1
. However, as
l\to\infty
,
\tau(R_{l})\to 1^{+}
.

Let us recall the definition of a John domain.

Definition 1.6
.

For
J\geq 1
, a (bounded) domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
J
-John if there exists a distinguished point
x_{0}\in\Omega
so that, for any
x\in\Omega
, there exists a curve
\gamma\subset\Omega
joining
x
to
x_{0}
satisfying the following condition:

(1.6)

\ell(\gamma[x,\,y])\leq J{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(y,\,\partial\Omega)\quad\text{ for any $y\in\gamma$},

where
\ell(\gamma[x,\,y])
denotes the length of the subcurve of
\gamma
joining
x
to
y
with respect to the metric induced by the standard Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
.
We usually call
x_{0}
the John center of
\Omega
and
\gamma
the John curve joining
x_{0}
and
x
.

Partially motivated by
[
8
]
, the authors presented an alternative proof of (
1.1
) (for the general anisotropic perimeter) in a recent manuscript
[
21
]
, by utilizing the John property of (almost) minimal surfaces
[
6
]
. Specifically speaking, the authors modified each set
E
sufficiently close to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
via a variational method, known as selection principle, to obtain a new set
\Omega
with uniformly bounded John constant
J
independent of
E
. The set
\Omega
also satisfies a uniform volume density estimate and supports the
(1,1)
-trace inequality (
1.4
) with the constant only depending on the dimension
n
and
J
, thus supporting (
1.1
); see
[
21
, Theorem 1.3]
for detailed discussion.

Partially motivated by this, we delve into examining the relationship between an open set that supports the
(1,1)
-trace inequality (
1.4
) and its John property.
Observe that, Theorem
1.3
already yields that sets satisfying both (
1.5
) and supporting the
(1,1)
-trace inequality (
1.4
) could have overly complicated geometric properties.
Nevertheless, when
\Omega
satisfies some extra mild geometric assumption, such as ball separation property, then one can show that
\Omega
is a John domain, together with an upper density bound on the perimeter.

Let us firstly recall the definition of the ball separation property.

Definition 1.7
.

We say that a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has a ball separation property with respect to a distinguished point
x_{0}\in\Omega
if there exists a constant
S>0
such that
for each
z\in\Omega
, one can find a curve
\gamma\subset\Omega
joining
z
to
x_{0}
so that, for any point
\eta\in\gamma
, either

\gamma[z,\eta]\subset B(\eta,S{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\eta,\partial\Omega))=:B,

or each point
y\in\gamma[z,\eta]\setminus B
belongs to a different component of
\Omega\setminus\partial B
than
x_{0}
.

Note that, each finitely connected planar domain has a ball separation property; see e.g.
[
3
]
(and further in
[
2
, Page 75]
and
[
12
, Page 191]
). Now we can state the following result, as a byproduct of the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Corollary 1.8
.

Suppose that the open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies
(
1.5
).

(i)

Assume that
\Omega
satisfies the ball separation property with respect to a distinguished point
x_{0}\in\Omega
and the constant
S>0
. Moreover, for any
u\in BV(\Omega)
, the trace
Tu
exists on
\partial\Omega
and (
1.4
) holds. Then by letting

r_{0}:={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x_{0},\Omega^{c}),

\Omega
is a
J
-John domain with John center
x_{0}
, where
C_{T}
is the same constant in (
1.4
) and
J=J(n,C_{T},S,|\Omega|/r_{0}^{n})
. In addition,
\partial\Omega
satisfies the upper density estimate

(1.7)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\cap B(x,r))\leq C_{H}r^{n-1}\quad\text{ for any }r\in(0,r_{0})\text{ and }x\in\partial\Omega,

where
C_{H}=C_{H}(n,C_{T},r_{0},P(\Omega))
is a positive constant.

(ii)

Conversely, if
\Omega
is a
J
-John domain with John center
x_{0}\in\Omega
and satisfies (
1.7
), then
\Omega
has a ball separation property with a distinguished point
x_{0}
and constant
S>0
, where
S=J
. Furthermore, for each
u\in BV(\Omega)
, the trace
Tu(x)
exists for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega
, and there exists a constant
C_{T}=C_{T}(n,J,C_{H},r_{0})>0
such that (
1.4
) holds.

Remark 1.9
.

The ball separation property is necessary for the equivalence of John property and trace inequality. In fact, the example, constructed in Chapter 3 to prove Theorem
1.3
, is precisely a domain that supports the
(1,1)
-trace inequality yet fails to have the ball separation property; see Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
together with Theorem
1.4
.

Our paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
, we recall some elementary results, especially the definition and properties of admissible domains.
Subsequently, in Section 3, we construct the set
\Omega
stated in Theorem
1.3
and then verify (
1.5
) for
\Omega
in Lemma
3.2
. Furthermore, we prove in Proposition
3.3
that
\tau(\Omega)
is sufficiently close to
\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})
, thereby concluding Theorem
1.3
. Finally, in Section 4, we show that each open set satisfying (
1.5
) and the
(1,1)
-trace inequality (
1.4
) necessarily have connectivity and boundedness, thus obtaining the equivalence of three types of sets referred to in Theorem
1.4
. In addition, Corollary
1.8
follows as a consequence of (the proof of) Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Notations and some elementary lemmas.

To begin with, we establish the notation as follows:
For a (rectifiable) curve
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote the length of
\gamma
by
\ell(\gamma)
. For any pair of points
x,y\in\gamma
, we denote a subcurve of
\gamma
joining
x
to
y
by
\gamma[x,y]
.

For any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the closure of
E
with respect to the Euclidean topology is denoted as
\overline{E}
or
cl(E)
and its complement is denoted as
E^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E
for brevity. The interior of
E
is denoted as
{\operatorname{int}}(E)
. The Lebesgue measure of
E
is denoted by
|E|
and the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
E
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)
. A general constant is denoted by
C
, which may vary between different estimates, and we include all the constants it depends on within the parentheses, denoted as
C(\cdot)
.

In addition, the standard Euclidean norm is denoted by
|\cdot|
. With this notation, for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x,E):=\inf_{y\in E}|x-y|.

We use

B(x,r):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|z-x|<r\}

to represent the ball with center
x
and radius
r
. The closure of
B(x,r)
is denoted by
\overline{B}(x,r)
. For brevity, we write
B_{r}=B(0,r)
. In particular, we denote by
\mathbb{B}^{n}
the unit ball
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Its boundary and its Lebesgue measure are denoted by
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\omega_{n}
, respectively.

For any
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any function
u
integrable on
E
, the integral average of
u
over
E
is denoted by

u_{E}:={\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 5.17493pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{E}u(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}u(x)\,dx.

A function
f\in L^{1}(U)
has bounded variation on the open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
if its total variation is finite, i.e.,
\|Df\|(U)<+\infty
. We denote the space of functions of bounded variation in
U
by
BV(U)
. This space is equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{BV(U)}:=\|f\|_{L^{1}(U)}+\|Df\|(U).

For any measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the distributional gradient
D\chi_{E}
of its characteristic function
\chi_{E}
induces the relative
perimeter of
E
with respect to the measurable set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is defined as

P(E;V)=\|D\chi_{E}\|(V).

In particular, when
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
P(E;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the so-called the
perimeter
of
E
, denoted by
P(E)
for simplicity.

A measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

is said to be of
finite perimeter
provided
that
D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}
is a Radon measure with finite total variation, i.e.,
\|D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}\|(\mathbb{R}^{n})<\infty
.
According to the Lebesgue-Besicovitch differentiation theorem for measures (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 1.34]
), for
\|D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}\|
-a.e.
x
, it holds that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}-\frac{D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}(x+rB^{n})}{\|D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}\|(x+rB^{n})}=\nu_{E}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad|\nu_{E}(x)|=1.

The set of points
x
where this condition holds is called the
reduced boundary
of
E
and is denoted by
\partial^{*}E
.
At points on the reduced boundary,
\nu_{E}(x)
represents the
measure-theoretic outward unit normal
to
E
at
x
.
Also, up to modifying
E
in a set of Lebesgue measure zero, one can assume that
\overline{\partial^{*}E}=\partial E

[
16
, Proposition 12.19 & (15.3)]
.
Additionally,
\|D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}\|
coincides with
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial^{*}E
, the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
restricted on
\partial^{*}E
.

For any sets
E
of finite perimeter with
|E|<\infty
, the isoperimetric inequality

(2.1)

P(E)\geq n\omega_{n}^{1/n}|E|^{(n-1)/n}

holds. Analogously, the relative isoperimetric inequality states that for each ball
B(x,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(2.2)

P(E;B(x,r))\geq C_{re}\min\{|B(x,r)\cap E|,|B(x,r)\setminus E|\}^{1-\frac{1}{n}},

where
C_{re}=C_{re}(n)>0
; see
[
7
, Theorem 5.11]
for these two inequalities. For more details on sets of finite perimeter, we refer the interested reader to
[
16
, Sections 12 and 15]
. Besides, for any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Fleming-Rishel Coarea Formula tells that

(2.3)

\|Df\|(E)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}P(\{f>t\};E)\,dt\quad\text{for any }f\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{n});

see
[
8
, (2.22)]
.

For a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\lambda\in[0,1]
, we denote by
E^{(\lambda)}
the set

(2.4)

E^{(\lambda)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|E\cap B(x,r)|}{|B(x,r)|}=\lambda\right\}.

Then the
measure-theoretic boundary

\partial_{M}E
of
E
is the set defined as
\partial_{M}E:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(E^{(0)}\cup E^{(1)})
.
Federer’s theorem characterizes the relation among
\partial^{*}E,\,\partial_{M}E
and
E^{(1/2)}
.

Lemma 2.1

(Federer’s theorem)
.

If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of locally finite perimeter, then

\partial^{*}E\subset E^{(1/2)}\subset\partial_{M}E\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial_{M}E\setminus\partial^{*}E)=0.

In addition, we record the following lemma regarding set operations; see
[
16
, Theorem 16.3]
and
[
16
, Exercise 16.6]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
E
and
F
are sets of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For brevity, we let

\{\nu_{E}=\nu_{F}\}:=\{x\in\partial^{*}E\cap\partial^{*}F:\nu_{E}=\nu_{F}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\nu_{E}=-\nu_{F}\}:=\{x\in\partial^{*}E\cap\partial^{*}F:\nu_{E}=-\nu_{F}\}.

Then
E\cap F
,
E\setminus F
and
E\cup F
are sets of finite perimeter, satisfying that for every Borel set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

P(E\cap F;G)=P(E;F^{(1)}\cap G)+P(F;E^{(1)}\cap G)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{\nu_{E}=\nu_{F}\}\cap G),

P(E\setminus F;G)=P(E;F^{(0)}\cap G)+P(F;E^{(1)}\cap G)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{\nu_{E}=-\nu_{F}\}\cap G),

P(E\cup F;G)=P(E;F^{(0)}\cap G)+P(F;E^{(0)}\cap G)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{\nu_{E}=\nu_{F}\}\cap G).

Moreover,

\partial^{*}(E\cap F)=(\partial^{*}E\cap F^{(1)})\cup(\partial^{*}F\cap E^{(1)})\cup\{\nu_{E}=\nu_{F}\}.

In particular, if

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\mathbb{B}^{n})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega^{\delta})-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq C_{1}(k_{0}!)^{-(n-1)/\delta},

\mu_{1}(\delta)=C_{1}a^{-1}(k_{0}!)^{-(n-1)/\delta}\quad\text{ for }\ \delta>0,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\partial^{*}F)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\mathbb{B}^{n})<a\mu_{1}(\delta).

Thanks to
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\partial^{*}F)>a=a(n)
, the definition of
\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})
and
|F|<\frac{1}{2}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|
yield

a<\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\partial^{*}F)\leq\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{-1}P(F).

This together with (
3.24
) implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)

\displaystyle<\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\partial^{*}F)+a\mu_{1}(\delta)\leq\tau(\mathbb{B})^{-1}(1+\mu_{1}(\delta))P(F).

Thus, we conclude (
3.22
) with

(3.25)

\mu(\delta)=\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})\mu_{1}(\delta)\geq\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})\mu_{1}(\delta)/(1+\mu_{1}(\delta))

where
\mu
depends only on
n
.

In what follows, we assume that

(3.26)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\partial^{*}F)\leq a=a(n).

Step 2: Replace

by a regular set
U
under (
3.26

Let

small (and particularly independent of

(3.28)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)\leq(1+b)\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{F}).

In particular,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{F})\neq 0.

Next, we consider a set of finite perimeter

minimizing the Plateau problem:

\inf\{P(G):G\setminus\Omega^{\delta}=\widetilde{F}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}\}.

From

coming from

, Proof of Proposition 12.29]

(3.29)

P(U)\leq P(\widetilde{F}),\quad P(U;\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})\leq P(\widetilde{F};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap U)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{F})\neq 0,

where the second inequality is given by
[
16

Moreover,

c(n)\rho^{n-1}\leq P(U;B(y,\rho))\leq n\omega_{n}\rho^{n-1}\quad\text{ for any $B(y,2\rho)\subset\subset\Omega^{\delta}$ with $y\in\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial U$}.

We now consider two subcases

|U\cap\Omega^{\delta}|>\lambda_{0}a^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad|U\cap\Omega^{\delta}|\leq\lambda_{0}a^{\frac{n}{n-1}}.

Step 3: Show (
3.22
) when
|U\cap\Omega^{\delta}|>\lambda_{0}a^{\frac{n}{n-1}}
and (
3.26
).

Recall that

Under the assumption (
3.26

(3.31)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)\leq a+\mu_{1}(\delta)\leq 2a.

Since

Thus, combining this with (

\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)<P(U)\leq P(\widetilde{F})\leq(1+b)P(F).

Hence, by eventually sending

, we establish (
3.22
) with

(3.32)

\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(\delta)\text{ taken to be any modulus of continuity.}

Step 4: Show (
3.22
) when
|U\cap\Omega^{\delta}|\leq\lambda_{0}a^{\frac{n}{n-1}}
and (
3.26
).

,

Thus, via (

P(U;\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})\leq P(\widetilde{F};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})\leq(1+b)P(F;\Omega^{\delta})=(1+b)(P(F)-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)).

Furthermore, combining (
3.28

(1+b)^{-1}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{F})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap U).

This coupled with (
3.33
), tells that

(3.34)

\left[(1+b)^{-2}(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1-\hat{\mu}(\delta))+1\right]\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)\\
\leq(1+b)^{-1}P(U;\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap\partial^{*}F)\leq P(F).

We next show the existence of a modulus of continuity
\hat{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, independent of

(3.35)

(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1-\hat{\mu}(\delta))\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap U)\leq P(U;\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})\quad\text{for any }0<\delta\leq\epsilon_{0}.

Once this holds, then by letting

(3.36)

\mu(\delta)=\frac{b^{2}+2b}{(1+b)^{2}}(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1)+\frac{\hat{\mu}(\delta)}{(1+b)^{2}},

and eventually sending

3.34

3.22
) with

.

Step 4.1: Estimate on
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap U)
.

In order to estimate
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap U)
, we divide it into four parts.
For any set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote

I_{\epsilon}(G):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(z,G)<\epsilon\}.

Let
a_{k}=(k!)^{-1/\delta}2^{-k-9},\,k\geq k_{0},
and set

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}:=\left\{(x,k):I_{a_{k}}(\partial B_{x,k})\subset{\operatorname{int}}(U),\,x\in E_{k},\,k\geq k_{0}\right\},

(3.37)

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}:=\left\{(x,k):I_{a_{k}}(\partial B_{x,k})\cap\partial U\neq\emptyset,\,x\in E_{k},\,k\geq k_{0}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}:=\left\{(x,k):I_{a_{k}}(\partial B_{x,k})\cap\overline{U}=\emptyset,\,x\in E_{k},\,k\geq k_{0}\right\}.

We further set

(3.38)

\widetilde{U}:=U\setminus\bigcup_{(x,k)\in\mathscr{D}}B_{x,k}\quad\text{and}\quad U_{x,k}:=U\cap B_{x,k}\quad\text{for any }(x,k)\in\mathscr{D}.

For any
k\geq k_{0}
and
x\in E_{k}
, since
I_{a_{k}}(\partial B_{x,k})
is connected, it follows that if
I_{a_{k}}(\partial B_{x,k})\cap\overline{U}\not=\emptyset
, then either
(x,k)\in\mathscr{A}
or
(x,k)\in\mathscr{K}
. Therefore,

\mathscr{A}\cup\mathscr{K}\cup\mathscr{D}=\{(x,k):x\in E_{k},\ k\geq k_{0}\}.

In addition, since all the balls
2B_{x,k}
are disjoint from
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, it follows from (
3.18
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap U)\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\widetilde{U})+\sum_{(x,k)\in\mathscr{A}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B_{x,k}^{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{(x,k)\in\mathscr{K}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B_{x,k}^{\delta}\right)+\sum_{(x,k)\in\mathscr{D}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B_{x,k}^{\delta}\cap U\right)

(3.39)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}.

In what follows we estimate these four terms separately.

Step 4.2: Estimate on

.

Applying the fact
U\setminus\Omega^{\delta}=\widetilde{F}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}
, it follows from the definition of
\widetilde{U}
in (
3.38

\widetilde{U}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n}=U\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n}=\widetilde{F}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n}.

Due to the openness of

, for any

, when the radius
\rho\ll 1

Thus, by letting
\rho\to 0^{+}
, we have

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{|B(z,\rho)\cap(\widetilde{U}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n})|}{|B(z,\rho)|}=\frac{1}{2}.

Analogously, recalling (
3.38
), we also have

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{|B(z,\rho)\cap(U_{x,k}\cap B_{x,k}^{\delta})|}{|B(z,\rho)|}=\frac{1}{2}

for any
(x,k)\in\mathscr{D}
and
z\in\partial B_{x,k}^{\delta}\cap U
. Therefore,

(3.40)

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\widetilde{U}\subset(\widetilde{U}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n})^{(\frac{1}{2})}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial B_{x,k}^{\delta}\cap U\subset(U_{x,k}\cap B_{x,k}^{\delta})^{(\frac{1}{2})}\cap(B_{x,k}^{\delta})^{c};

recall (
2.4
).
By Federer’s Theorem and applying Lemma
2.2
to both
\widetilde{U}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{U}
, (
3.40
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\widetilde{U})\leq

\displaystyle P(\widetilde{U}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{\nu_{\widetilde{U}}=-\nu_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\})+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\widetilde{U}^{(1)}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

(3.41)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{\nu_{\widetilde{U}}=-\nu_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\})+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}(\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{U})\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

Furthermore, due to
|\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{U}|\leq\lambda_{0}a^{\frac{n}{n-1}}
and (
3.21
), the definition of
\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})
yields

(3.42)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}(\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{U})\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1)^{-1}P(\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{U};\mathbb{B}^{n}).

We let

B_{half}:=\mathbb{B}^{n}\cap\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{1}<0\}.

By the definition of
\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})
and applying Lemma
2.2
to

(3.43)

(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1)^{-1}\geq\left(\frac{P(B_{half})}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\partial^{*}B_{half})}-1\right)^{-1}=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\partial^{*}B_{half})}{P(B_{half};\mathbb{B}^{n})}>1.

Therefore, we obtain from (
3
), (
3.42
)
and Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\widetilde{U})\leq P(\widetilde{U};\mathbb{S}^{n-1})+(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1)^{-1}P(\Omega^{\delta}\cap\widetilde{U};\mathbb{B}^{n})

(3.44)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1)^{-1}P(\widetilde{U};\mathbb{S}^{n-1})+(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1)^{-1}(P(\widetilde{U};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta}\cap\mathbb{B}^{n})+P(\Omega^{\delta};\mathbb{B}^{n}\cap\widetilde{U}^{(1)})).

Moreover, (
3.15

\widetilde{U}
in (

in (

Thus, (

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\widetilde{U})\leq(\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1)^{-1}(P(\widetilde{U};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})+T_{2}+T_{3}).

Step 4.3: Estimate on
T_{2}
.

Let

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq k_{U}}\sum_{x\in E_{k}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial B_{x,k}^{\delta}\right)=\sum_{k\geq k_{U}}\sum_{x\in E_{k}}n\omega_{n}\left(\frac{2^{-k}}{(k!)^{1/\delta}}\right)^{n-1}\leq C(n)(k_{U}!)^{(n-1)(1-1/\delta)}\sum_{k\geq k_{U}}2^{-(n-1)k}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)(k_{0}!)^{(n-1)(n+1-1/\delta)}|B_{x_{U},k_{U}}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq C(n)(k_{0}!)^{(n-1)(n+1-1/\delta)}|B_{x_{U},k_{U}}\cup(\widetilde{U}\cap\Omega^{\delta})|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

\displaystyle T_{2}\leq\mu_{2}(\delta)\left(P(\widetilde{U};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})+P(\Omega^{\delta};\mathbb{B}^{n}\cap\widetilde{U}^{(1)})\right)\leq\mu_{2}(\delta)\left(P(\widetilde{U};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})+T_{2}+T_{3}\right).

.

(x,k)\in\mathscr{D}
. Using Federer’s theorem and the second inclusion in (

\displaystyle=P(U_{x,k};(B_{x,k}^{\delta})^{c})+P(U_{x,k}\cap B_{x,k}^{\delta})-P(U_{x,k}).

P(U_{x,k};B_{x,k}\setminus B_{x,k}^{\delta}))=P(U\setminus\widetilde{U};B_{x,k}\setminus B_{x,k}^{\delta}),

T_{4}\leq\sum_{(x,k)\in\mathscr{D}}P(U\setminus\widetilde{U};B_{x,k}\setminus B_{x,k}^{\delta})\leq P(U\setminus\widetilde{U};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta}).

Step 4.6: Conclude (

) when

As a consequence, by gathering (

(3.56)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\delta}\cap U)\leq\left((\tau(\mathbb{B}^{n})-1)^{-1}+\omega_{1}(\delta)\right)P(\widetilde{U};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})+P(U\setminus\widetilde{U};\overline{\Omega}^{\delta})+\omega_{2}(\delta)P(U;\overline{\Omega}^{\delta}),

are

Recall (
3.38

. Then since (

Hence, due to the inclusion

cl(\widetilde{U})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(\bigcup_{(x,k)\in\mathscr{D}}I_{a_{k}}(B_{x,k}))

following from the definition of
\mathscr{D}
in (

Step 2.3: The decay estimate of

.

Set

Observe that by (

4.5
to the open set
\Omega
, for a.e.

,
\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}
is a Borel set satisfying

(4.24)

\displaystyle 0\leq f(r)\leq P(\Omega;(B_{r})^{c})<2C_{M}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}).

Now, by Lemma
4.4
and (
4.23
), for a.e.

, there exists

for some

, such that

(4.25)

-f^{\prime}(r)\geq P_{\partial B_{r}}(B_{\partial B_{r}}(q_{r},\rho_{r}))\quad\text{ for a.e. }r\in[\hat{R},+\infty).

By gathering (
4.1

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B_{\partial B_{r}}(q_{r},\rho_{r}))=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap\partial B_{r})\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\overline{\Omega}\cap\partial B_{r})<P(\Omega;(B_{r})^{c})<\frac{1}{2}P(B_{r}),

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B_{\partial B_{r}}(q_{r},\rho_{r}))\leq C(n)\rho_{r}^{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\partial B_{r}}(B_{\partial B_{r}}(q_{r},\rho_{r}))\geq C(n)\rho_{r}^{n-2}.

Hence, via (
4.25
) and (

-f^{\prime}(r)\geq C(n)\rho_{r}^{n-2}\geq C(n)\left(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B_{\partial B_{r}}(q_{r},\rho_{r}))\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}=C(n)\left(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap\partial B_{r})\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

Step 2.4: Leading to a contradiction.

Recalling (
4.24
), we obtain

(4.27)

-f^{\prime}(r)>C(n,C_{M})(f(r))^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\geq 0\quad\text{ for a.e. }r\in[\hat{R},+\infty).

Moreover, it follows from (

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}f(r)=0,

which, together with (

for all

and applying (

(4.28)

By letting

, which leads to a contradiction. Hence,
\Omega
must be bounded.
