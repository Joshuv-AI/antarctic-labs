Good Integers: ( T , k ) (T,k) -Subclasses and Applications to Galois Duality in Coding Theory
Somphong Jitman, Panthakan Boonsuriyatham

Abstract
The notion of good integers, namely the divisors of the sequence ( a s + b s ) s ≥ 1 (a^{s}+b^{s})_{s\geq 1} for nonzero coprime integers a a and b b , together with their subfamilies such as oddly-good and evenly-good integers, has become an important arithmetic tool in the study of Euclidean and Hermitian dualities for abelian and cyclic codes. Building on this perspective, this paper introduces and studies another interesting subclass of good integers arising from the sequence ( a k ​ s + T + b k ​ s + T ) s ≥ 1 \bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)_{s\geq 1} for some integers 0 ≤ T < k 0\leq T<k , whose divisors are called ( T , k ) (T,k) - good integers with respect to ( a , b ) (a,b) . An arithmetic theory of these integers is developed, including a characterization at odd prime powers, a general characterization for odd integers in terms of 2 2 -adic valuations, and a treatment of even integers. An explicit algorithm is also given for deciding whether a given integer d d is ( T , k ) (T,k) -good with respect to ( a , b ) (a,b) and, when it is, for computing an exponent s s such that d | ( a k ​ s + T + b k ​ s + T ) d\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr) . Applications in coding theory are then obtained from the specialization ( a , b ) = ( q , 1 ) (a,b)=(q,1) , where q q is a prime power. In particular, the q k q^{k} -cyclotomic classes of the cyclic group ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} characterize the Galois self-reciprocal irreducible factors of x n − 1 x^{n}-1 over 𝔽 q k \mathbb{F}_{q^{k}} , give a description and enumeration of Galois LCD cyclic codes of length n n over 𝔽 q k \mathbb{F}_{q^{k}} , and lead to a characterization of Galois self-dual cyclic codes.

Introduction

The classical notion of a good integer
Moree1997
is formulated for a pair of nonzero coprime integers
a
and
b
: a positive integer
d
is said to be
good with respect to

a
and
b
if it is a divisor of some term of the sequence
(a^{s}+b^{s})_{s\geq 1}
. Equivalently,
d\mid(a^{s}+b^{s})

for some positive integer
s
. The set of all such integers is denoted by
G(a,b)
. In
J2018
, two important subclasses of good integers were also investigated. A positive integer
d
is said to be
oddly-good with respect to

a
and
b
if
d\mid(a^{s}+b^{s})

for some odd positive integer
s
, and
evenly-good with respect to

a
and
b
if
d\mid(a^{s}+b^{s})

for some even positive integer
s
. The sets of all oddly-good and evenly-good integers with respect to
a
and
b
are denoted by
OG(a,b)
and
EG(a,b)
, respectively.
These arithmetic notions have been studied from several directions, especially through multiplicative orders modulo primes and prime powers; see, for example,
J2018
;
Jitman2020
;
Moree1997
.

For applications in coding theory, the relevant specialization is the case
(a,b)=(q,1)
, where
q
is a prime power. In this case, the problem is to determine whether
d
is a divisor of some term of the sequence
(q^{s}+1)_{s\geq 1}
. This divisibility condition is used in the characterization of the reciprocal behavior of irreducible monic factors of
x^{n}-1
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Consequently, it plays an important role in the study of Euclidean duals of cyclic codes
JLX2011
, negacyclic codes
Jitman2020
, and abelian codes
JLLX2013
. For example, it arises in the characterization and enumeration of important families of codes such as Euclidean self-dual codes, Euclidean linear complementary dual (LCD) codes, and Euclidean hulls of codes in
J2018
;
JLLX2013
;
JLX2011
. The motivation becomes even stronger when one moves from Euclidean duality to Hermitian duality, where oddly-good integers arise naturally in the characterization and enumeration of Hermitian self-dual cyclic, abelian, and quasi-abelian codes; see, for example,
BJU2018
;
J2018
;
JLS2014
.

More generally, Galois self-dual and Galois complementary dual codes provide a common framework that contains the Euclidean and Hermitian cases as special instances. This general setting has been investigated for cyclic, constacyclic, and related classes of codes; see, for example, the work on Galois self-dual constacyclic and quasi constacyclic codes in
FanZhang2017
;
FL2024
, Galois linear complementary dual codes in
LiuLiu2018
;

, and two-sided Galois duals in

.
Motivated by these developments, for fixed nonzero coprime integers
a
and
b
, and integers

and
T
with
0\leq T<k
, this paper introduces and investigates the family of positive integers
d
satisfying

d\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)

for some positive integer
s
. More specifically,
d
is called a
(T,k)
-good integer with respect to
a
and
b
. The set of all such integers is denoted by
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
. From the definitions, it is clear that
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)\subseteq G(a,b)
.
Since
\gcd(a,b)=1
, no prime divisor of

can divide a term of the form
a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}
. Consequently, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
a
and
b
be nonzero coprime integers and let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
. Then every element of
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
is coprime to

When

which corresponds to the classical notion of oddly-good integers with respect to

, and
G_{(1,2)}(a,b)=OG(a,b)

extends the classical theory of good integers and provides an arithmetic framework for the study of Euclidean, Hermitian, and Galois dualities.

The purpose of this paper is twofold. First, a self-contained arithmetic theory of

is developed. This includes local criteria at odd prime powers, a general characterization of odd integers in terms of the
2
-adic valuations, a treatment of the even case, and an explicit algorithm for deciding whether a given integer belongs to

and, when it does, for computing an exponent

. Second, applications in coding theory are obtained by specializing to
(a,b)=(q,1)

-cyclotomic classes on finite cyclic and abelian groups. For cyclic groups, the resulting orbit conditions determine when an irreducible factor of

\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
is

The paper is organized as follows. Section

(T,k)
-good integers with respect to
(a,b)

It also provides a characterization of Galois self-dual cyclic codes, which reduces to a simpler pairwise form in the involutory case. The paper ends with concluding remarks and possible directions for further research in Section

2
Characterization of
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

In this section, the arithmetic structure of
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
is developed. The defining condition

d\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)\quad\text{for some }s\geq 1

naturally leads to multiplicative orders of
ab^{-1}
modulo divisors of
d
. Basic arithmetic properties required for the study of good integers are recalled. The local problem at odd prime powers is treated first, then combined into general criteria for odd and even integers.

2.1
Basic arithmetic properties

For a positive integer
m
coprime to an integer
i
, the multiplicative order of
i
modulo
m
is denoted by
\operatorname{ord}_{m}(i)
. For a prime
p
and an integer
i\neq 0
, the
p
-adic valuation
\nu_{p}(i)
is the largest integer
e\geq 0
such that
p^{e}\mid i
. Let
a
and
b
be nonzero coprime integers. For each positive integer
d
coprime to
ab
, the inverse of
b
modulo
d
exists and it is denoted by
b^{-1}
. This allows the divisibility condition
d\mid(a^{s}+b^{s})
to be translated into a congruence involving the residue class
ab^{-1}
modulo
d
in
Moree1997
, thereby reducing the problem to the multiplicative order of
ab^{-1}
modulo
d
.

The following lemma translates the divisibility condition
d\mid(a^{N}+b^{N})
into a statement about the multiplicative order of
ab^{-1}
modulo
d
. It is a basic arithmetic tool in the later study of
(T,k)
-good integers with respect to
a
and
b
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
d
be an integer such that
d\geq 3
and
\gcd(d,ab)=1
and let
N
be a positive integer. Then
a^{N}+b^{N}\equiv 0\pmod{d}

if and only if
\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})
is even and
N\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})}
.

Proof.

Assume that

a^{N}+b^{N}\equiv 0\pmod{d}
. Then
(ab^{-1})^{N}\equiv-1\pmod{d}
which implies that
(ab^{-1})^{2N}\equiv 1\pmod{d}
.
Equivalently,

\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})\mid 2N
.
Since

(ab^{-1})^{N}\not\equiv 1\pmod{d}
,
we have

\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})\nmid N
.
It follows that
\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})
is even and

N\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})}.

Conversely, assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})
is even and

N\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})}.

Then
(ab^{-1})^{N}\equiv(ab^{-1})^{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})/2}\pmod{d}
.
The residue class

(ab^{-1})^{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})/2}

has order
2
, and hence,

(ab^{-1})^{N}\equiv(ab^{-1})^{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(ab^{-1})/2}\equiv-1\pmod{d}.

Equivalently,

a^{N}+b^{N}\equiv 0\pmod{d}
.
∎

The characterization of odd good integers is given in terms of the
2
-adic valuations of the orders
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})
for primes
p\mid m
in
Moree1997
.

Theorem 3

(
(
Moree1997
, Theorem 1)
)
.

Let
d>1
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
. Then

d\in G(a,b)

if and only if there exists a positive integer
\alpha
such that

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\alpha

for all primes
p\mid d
.

2.2
Odd prime power characterization

In this subsection, we focus on the local problem at a single odd prime power. The corresponding prime power conditions will later be combined, via the Chinese Remainder Theorem, to obtain criteria for general odd integers in Section
2.3
.

Proposition 4

(
(
N2000
, Theorem 3.6)
)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime such that
p\nmid ab
and
e
be a positive integer. Let
b^{-1}
denote the inverse of
b
modulo
p^{e}
and let
\lambda_{p}=\nu_{p}\bigl((ab^{-1})^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})}-1\bigr)
.
Then

\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})=\begin{cases}\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})&\text{ if }e\leq\lambda_{p},\\[2.84526pt]
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})p^{e-\lambda_{p}}&\text{ if }e>\lambda_{p}.\end{cases}

In particular,

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)
for all
e\geq 1
.

The characterization of a
(T,k)
-good odd prime power with respect to
a
and
b
is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Let
p
be an odd prime such that
p\nmid ab
, let
e
be a positive integer, and let
0\leq T<k
be integers. Then

p^{e}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})
is even and

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))}.

Proof.

Assume that

p^{e}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
Then there exists a positive integer
s
such that

a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{e}}
, which is equivalent to
(ab^{-1})^{ks+T}\equiv-1\pmod{p^{e}}
.
By Lemma
2
, it follows that
\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})
is even and

ks+T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}.

Hence, the congruence

ks\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}-T\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}

is solvable, which implies that

\gcd(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))\mid\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}-T\right).

Consequently,

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))}.

By Proposition
4
,

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)
, which implies that
\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})
is even if and only if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})
is even.

Conversely, assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})
is even and
T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))}
.
Then
\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})
is even by Proposition
4
. Moreover,

\gcd(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))\mid\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}-T\right),

which implies that the linear congruence

ks\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}-T\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}

has an integer solution. Hence, there exists a positive integer
s
such that

ks+T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}.

Since
\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})
is even,

(ab^{-1})^{ks+T}\equiv-1\pmod{p^{e}}
by Lemma
2
. Multiplying by
b^{ks+T}
, we obtain
a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{e}}
.
Therefore,
p^{e}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
as desired.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
p
be an odd prime with
p\nmid ab
, let
e
be a positive integer.
Then
p^{e}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if there exists a positive integer
\alpha
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\alpha
.

(2)

T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{\nu_{2}(k),\alpha\}}},

(3)

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right)\mid T.

Proof.

Assume that

p^{e}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
By Theorem
5
,
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})
is even and

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))}.

Let
\alpha=\nu_{2}(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1}))
. Then
\alpha
is positive and satisfies (1).
Since

\nu_{2}(\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))=\nu_{2}(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1}))=\alpha
,
we have

\gcd(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))=2^{\min\{\nu_{2}(k),\alpha\}}\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right).

This implies

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{\nu_{2}(k),\alpha\}}},

which is (2), and also

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right)\mid T,

which is (3).

Conversely, assume that there exists a positive integer
\alpha
satisfying (1)–(3). Then
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})
is even, which implies that
\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})
is also even. Since

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right)\mid\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2},

condition (3) gives

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right)}.

Combining this with (2) and using the coprimality of the two moduli, we obtain

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1}))}.

Hence, by Theorem
5
,
p^{e}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
∎

2.3
Odd integers:
2
-adic characterization

We now turn to arbitrary odd integers. In this setting, the local conditions at the prime power divisors of
d
combine into a particularly global criterion expressed in terms of the
2
-adic valuations of the orders
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})
for all primes
p\mid d
.

For a positive integer
d
with distinct prime power factorization

\displaystyle d=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{e_{i}},

(1)

we write

\operatorname{lcm}_{p^{e}\parallel d}\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})=\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p_{1}^{e_{1}}}(ab^{-1}),\dots,\operatorname{ord}_{p_{m}^{e_{m}}}(ab^{-1})\bigr).

Then we have the following results.

Lemma 7
.

Let
d
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
decomposed as in (
1
). If there exists an integer
N
such that

N\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}

for all
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, then

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p_{1}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p_{2}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\cdots=\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p_{m}}(ab^{-1})\bigr).

Proof.

For each
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, write

\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})=2^{\alpha_{j}}w_{j},

where
w_{j}
is odd. By Proposition
4
,

\alpha_{j}=\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}}(ab^{-1})\bigr).

Assume that
\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{j}
for some
i\neq j
. Then

N\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{i}^{e_{i}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}=2^{\alpha_{i}-1}w_{i}\pmod{2^{\alpha_{i}}}.

Since
w_{i}
is odd,

N\equiv 2^{\alpha_{i}-1}\pmod{2^{\alpha_{i}}}.

On the other hand,

N\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}=2^{\alpha_{j}-1}w_{j}\pmod{2^{\alpha_{j}}}.

Since
\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{j}
, this implies

N\equiv 0\pmod{2^{\alpha_{i}}},

a contradiction. Hence, all
\alpha_{j}
are equal.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
d
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
decomposed as in (
1
). Assume that, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,

\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})=2^{\alpha}w_{j}
,
where
\alpha
is a positive integer and each
w_{j}
is odd. If

T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}}

and

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\right)\mid T
,
then the system

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\equiv T\pmod{k},

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}\quad(j=1,\dots,m),

is solvable.

Proof.

Assume that
T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}}

and

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\right)\mid T
.

First, we show that the congruences

N\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}\quad(j=1,\dots,m)

are pairwise compatible. Let
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Then

\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{i}^{e_{i}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}-\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}=2^{\alpha-1}(w_{i}-w_{j}).

Since
w_{i}
and
w_{j}
are odd, the difference
w_{i}-w_{j}
is even and
\gcd(w_{i},w_{j})\mid(w_{i}-w_{j})
.
Hence,

2^{\alpha}\gcd(w_{i},w_{j})\mid\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{i}^{e_{i}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}-\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\right).

Since

\gcd\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p_{i}^{e_{i}}}(ab^{-1}),\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=2^{\alpha}\gcd(w_{i},w_{j})
,
it follows that the congruences are pairwise compatible.

Next, we show that

N\equiv T\pmod{k}

is compatible with each local congruence. Let
j
be fixed in
\{1,\dots,m\}
. Since

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},w_{j}\right)\mid\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\right),

the hypothesis implies that

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},w_{j}\right)\mid T.

We note that

\gcd\bigl(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},w_{j}\right)

and the two factors on the right hand side are coprime. Since

\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}=2^{\alpha-1}w_{j}

with
w_{j}
odd, the congruence
T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}}

implies that

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}}.

Since

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},w_{j}\right)\mid\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}
,
the divisibility

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},w_{j}\right)\mid T

is equivalent to

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},w_{j}\right)}.

Therefore,

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd\bigl(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)}.

Thus, the congruence

N\equiv T\pmod{k}

is compatible with the
j
th local congruence.

Hence, all congruences in the system are pairwise compatible. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the system is solvable.
∎

Theorem 9
.

Let
d>1
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
decomposed as in (
1
). Then

d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if there exists a positive integer
\alpha
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\alpha

for every prime
p\mid d
.

(2)

T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}}

(3)

\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},\frac{\operatorname{lcm}_{p^{e}\parallel d}\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right)\mid T
.

Proof.

Assume first that
d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
Then there exists a positive integer
s
such that
d\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
.
Hence,

p_{j}^{e_{j}}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)

for every
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.
By Lemma
2
, we have

ks+T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}

for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.
Hence, by Lemma
7
, condition (1) holds for some positive integer
\alpha
.

For each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, write

\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})=2^{\alpha}w_{j}
with odd
w_{j}
. Since

ks+T\equiv T\pmod{k}\quad\text{and}\quad ks+T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})},

we obtain

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd\bigl(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)}

for every
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.
It follows that

\gcd\bigl(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},w_{j}\right),

with coprime factors. Therefore, for every
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
,

T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\gcd\left(\frac{k}{2^{\nu_{2}(k)}},w_{j}\right)\mid T.

The first is exactly condition (2). Since

\operatorname{lcm}(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})=\frac{\operatorname{lcm}_{p^{e}\parallel d}\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}},

the second family of divisibility conditions is equivalent to (3).

Conversely, assume that (1)–(3) hold. For each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, define

w_{j}=\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}
.
Then each
w_{j}
is odd and the system

\displaystyle N\equiv T\pmod{k},\quad N\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}\quad(j=1,\dots,m)

(2)

is solvable by Lemma
8
. Let
M
be a solution of (
2
). Since
M\equiv T\pmod{k}
, we may write

M=ks+T

for some integer
s
. Replacing
M
by a sufficiently large positive integer in the same congruence class modulo the least common multiple of the moduli, we may assume that

M=ks+T

with
s\geq 1
. Then

ks+T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}(ab^{-1})}\quad\text{for every }j=1,\dots,m.

By Lemma
2
, we have

p_{j}^{e_{j}}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)

for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.
Since the prime powers
p_{j}^{e_{j}}
are pairwise coprime, it follows that

d\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr).

Hence,

d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

2.4
The even case

We now turn to even integers. If exactly one of
a
and
b
is even, then

G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

contains no even integers by Lemma
1
. Throughout this subsection,
a
and
b
are assumed to be odd coprime integers.

Write
d=2^{\varepsilon}d_{0}
with
\varepsilon\geq 1
and
d_{0}
is odd. Here, the odd part is still controlled by the preceding theorem, while the additional restriction comes from the
2
-adic valuation of
a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
a
and
b
be odd nonzero coprime integers. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

2\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

for all integers
k\geq 1
and
0\leq T<k
.

(2)

For every integer
\varepsilon\geq 2
,

2^{\varepsilon}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if
\varepsilon\leq\nu_{2}(a+b)
and there exists an integer
s\geq 1
such that

ks+T
is odd.

Proof.

Since
a
and
b
are odd, it follows that, for every positive integer
N
,

\displaystyle\nu_{2}\bigl(a^{N}+b^{N}\bigr)=\begin{cases}\nu_{2}(a+b)&\text{if }N\text{ is odd},\\[2.84526pt]
1&\text{if }N\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

(3)

Consequently,
a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}
is even for all positive integers
s
, which implies that

2\mid(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T})
. Therefore,

2\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
which proves (1).

To prove (2), assume that
\varepsilon\geq 2
. By the definition,

2^{\varepsilon}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if there exists an integer
s\geq 1
such that

2^{\varepsilon}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
. Assume that

2^{\varepsilon}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
. Then there exists a positive integer
s
such that
2^{\varepsilon}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
,
and hence,

\varepsilon\leq\nu_{2}\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
.
Since
\varepsilon\geq 2
,
ks+T
must be odd by (
3
). It follows that

\varepsilon\leq\nu_{2}\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)=\nu_{2}(a+b).

Conversely, assume that
\varepsilon\leq\nu_{2}(a+b)
and there exists a positive integer
s
such that

ks+T
is odd.
Since
ks+T
is odd, it follows that

\nu_{2}\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)=\nu_{2}(a+b).

Hence, we have

\varepsilon\leq\nu_{2}\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
,
which implies that

2^{\varepsilon}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
.
Therefore,
2^{\varepsilon}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
as desired.
∎

Proposition 11
.

Let
a
and
b
be odd nonzero integers and let
d_{0}
be an odd positive integer. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

2d_{0}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if

d_{0}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.

(2)

For every integer
\varepsilon\geq 2
,

2^{\varepsilon}d_{0}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if
\varepsilon\leq\nu_{2}(a+b)
and there exists a positive integer
s
such that

d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
and
ks+T
is odd.

Proof.

By the definition,

2d_{0}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if there exists a positive integer
s
such that

2d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
.
Since
a
and
b
are odd,

2\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)

for every integer
s\geq 1
. Therefore,
2d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)

if and only if

d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
.
This proves (1).

For (2), let
\varepsilon\geq 2
be an integer. Assume that

2^{\varepsilon}d_{0}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
Then there exists a positive integer
s
such that

2^{\varepsilon}d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
.
Since
\gcd(2^{\varepsilon},d_{0})=1
,
it follows that
2^{\varepsilon}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
and
d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
.
By Proposition
10
, the first divisibility implies that
\varepsilon\leq\nu_{2}(a+b)
and
ks+T
is odd.

Conversely, assume that
\varepsilon\leq\nu_{2}(a+b)
and there exists a positive integer
s
such that
d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
and
ks+T
is odd.
Since
\varepsilon\geq 2
and
ks+T
is odd, we have
2^{\varepsilon}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
by Proposition
10
.
Together with
d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)

and
\gcd(2^{\varepsilon},d_{0})=1
,
we obtain

2^{\varepsilon}d_{0}\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr)
.
Therefore,

2^{\varepsilon}d_{0}\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
This proves (2).
∎

2.5
Special cases

In this subsection, several useful refinements of Theorem
9
are presented in the cases where
k
is odd and where
k
is a power of
2
. Precisely, the
2
-adic congruence condition imposes no restriction when
k
is odd, and the odd-part divisibility condition disappears if
k
is a power of
2
.

2.5.1
The case where
k
is odd

When
k
is odd, the congruence condition in Theorem
9
disappears. Thus, membership in
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
for odd integers is determined by a common positive
2
-adic valuation of the local orders and an odd part divisibility condition on
T
.

Corollary 12
.

Let
k
be an odd positive integer and let
d>1
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
. Then
d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if there exists a positive integer
\alpha\geq 1
such that

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\alpha
for all primes
p\mid d
,
and
\gcd\left(k,\frac{\operatorname{lcm}_{p^{e}\parallel d}\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right)\mid T
.

Proof.

Since
k
is odd, we have
\nu_{2}(k)=0
. The congruence condition
T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{\alpha,\nu_{2}(k)\}}}
in Theorem
9
becomes

2^{\min\{\alpha,0\}}=1
which is always true. The result follows immediately.
∎

Under the additional assumption
\gcd(T,k)=1
, the divisibility condition in Corollary
12
is equivalent to

\gcd\left(k,\frac{\operatorname{lcm}_{p^{e}\parallel d}\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right)=1
.
Thus, the next corollary follows immediately from Corollary
12
.

Corollary 13
.

Let
k
be an odd positive integer and let
d>1
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
. If

\gcd(T,k)=1
,
then

d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if there exists a positive integer
\alpha\geq 1
such that

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\alpha
for all primes
p\mid d
,
and

\gcd\left(k,\frac{\operatorname{lcm}_{p^{e}\parallel d}\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2^{\alpha}}\right)=1
.

2.5.2
The case where
k
is a power of
2

We now turn to the complementary situation where
k=2^{r}
for some positive integer
r
.
In this case, the odd-part divisibility condition in Theorem
9
disappears completely, and the odd part of membership is determined by the common
2
-adic valuation of the orders
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})
for primes dividing
d
.

Corollary 14
.

Let
r
be a positive integer and let
d>1
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
. Then
d\in G_{(T,2^{r})}(a,b)

if and only if there exists a positive integer
\alpha
such that
\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\alpha

for all primes
p\mid d
,
and

T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{r,\alpha\}}}
.

Proof.

Since
\frac{2^{r}}{2^{\nu_{2}(2^{r})}}=1
, the result follows immediately from Theorem
9
.
∎

Corollary 15
.

Let
r
be a positive integer and let
d>1
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
. Then the following statements hold.

1.

If
T=0
, then

d\in G_{(0,2^{r})}(a,b)

if and only if there exists an integer
\alpha\geq r+1
such that

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=\alpha

for all primes
p\mid d
.

2.

If
1\leq T<2^{r}
and
\nu_{2}(T)=t
, then

d\in G_{(T,2^{r})}(a,b)

if and only if

\nu_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{p}(ab^{-1})\bigr)=t+1

for all primes
p\mid d
.

Proof.

If
T=0
, then the congruence in Corollary
14
becomes

0\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{r,\alpha\}}}
,
which holds if and only if
\min\{r,\alpha\}\leq\alpha-1
. Equivalently,
\alpha\geq r+1
. This proves (1).

Now assume that
1\leq T<2^{r}
and write

\nu_{2}(T)=t,

where

T=2^{t}u

for some odd integer
u
and
0\leq t<r
. By Corollary
14
,

T\equiv 2^{\alpha-1}\pmod{2^{\min\{r,\alpha\}}}
.
Since
T
has exact
2
-adic valuation
t
, this congruence holds if and only if

\alpha-1=t
.
Hence, necessarily
\alpha=t+1
.

Conversely, if
\alpha=t+1
, then

2^{\alpha-1}=2^{t}
.
Since
T=2^{t}u
with
u
odd, we have

T\equiv 2^{t}\pmod{2^{t+1}}
. Since

\min\{r,\alpha\}=t+1
,
the required congruence follows. This proves (2).
∎

2.6
Relations among sets of
(T,k)
-good integers

In this subsection, we study the relations among sets of odd
(T,k)
-good integers as the parameter
T
varies. For odd integers
d
, Theorem
5
shows that the admissible values of
T
are determined by congruence conditions modulo suitable divisors of
k
. This leads naturally to a family of subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
that control how membership in
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
behaves as
T
varies.

For each integer
T
with
0\leq T<k
, define

C_{k}(T):=\{(2i+1)T\bmod k:i\geq 0\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{k}.

The set
C_{k}(T)
collects the residue classes modulo
k
obtained from odd multiples of
T
. It plays a key role in the study of relations among the sets of odd
(T,k)
-good integers as the parameter
T
varies. The following result describes
C_{k}(T)
explicitly.

Proposition 16
.

Let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If

\frac{k}{\gcd(T,k)}

is odd, then

C_{k}(T)=\left\{iT\bmod k:0\leq i\leq\frac{k}{\gcd(T,k)}-1\right\}\quad\text{and }|C_{k}(T)|=\frac{k}{\gcd(T,k)}.

(2)

If

\frac{k}{\gcd(T,k)}

is even, then

C_{k}(T)=\left\{(2i+1)T\bmod k:0\leq i\leq\frac{k}{2\gcd(T,k)}-1\right\}\quad\text{and }|C_{k}(T)|=\frac{k}{2\gcd(T,k)}.

In particular,

|C_{k}(T)|=\frac{k}{\gcd(k,2\gcd(T,k))}
.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{ord}(T)
denote the additive order of
T
in
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. Then

\operatorname{ord}(T)=\frac{k}{\gcd(T,k)}
.

Assume that
\operatorname{ord}(T)
is odd. Since
\gcd(2,\operatorname{ord}(T))=1
, multiplication by
2
is a permutation of
\mathbb{Z}_{\operatorname{ord}(T)}
. Hence, for every integer
i
, there exists an integer
j
such that

i\equiv 2j+1\pmod{\operatorname{ord}(T)}
.
Since
\operatorname{ord}(T)
is the additive order of
T
in
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, it follows that

iT\equiv(2j+1)T\pmod{k}
.
Therefore, every multiple
iT
belongs to
C_{k}(T)
which implies that

C_{k}(T)=\{iT\bmod k:0\leq i\leq\operatorname{ord}(T)-1\}\quad\text{and }|C_{k}(T)|=\operatorname{ord}(T).

Assume next that
\operatorname{ord}(T)
is even. By definition,
\{(2i+1)T\bmod k:0\leq i\leq\tfrac{\operatorname{ord}(T)}{2}-1\}\subseteq C_{k}(T)
.
This set has exactly
\operatorname{ord}(T)/2
distinct elements. Conversely, let
\ell\in C_{k}(T)
. Then

\ell\equiv(2j+1)T\pmod{k}

for some integer
j\geq 0
. Since
\operatorname{ord}(T)
is even, there exists an integer
j^{\prime}
with

0\leq j^{\prime}<\frac{\operatorname{ord}(T)}{2}

such that

2j\equiv 2j^{\prime}\pmod{\operatorname{ord}(T)}
.
Hence,

\ell\equiv(2j^{\prime}+1)T\pmod{k}
,
which proves the required description of
C_{k}(T)
and its cardinality.

Finally, we have

|C_{k}(T)|=\frac{\operatorname{ord}(T)}{\gcd(\operatorname{ord}(T),2)}=\frac{k}{\gcd(T,k)\,\gcd\!\left(\frac{k}{\gcd(T,k)},2\right)}=\frac{k}{\gcd(k,2\gcd(T,k))}

as desired.
∎

The next lemma gives a basic symmetry of the sets
C_{k}(T)
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
. Then

C_{k}(T)=C_{k}(k-T)
.

Proof.

Since
k-T\equiv-T\pmod{k}
, we have

C_{k}(k-T)=\{(2i+1)(k-T)\bmod k:i\geq 0\}=\{-(2i+1)T\bmod k:i\geq 0\}.

As
i
runs over the nonnegative integers, the odd multiples of
-T
modulo
k
coincide with the odd multiples of
T
modulo
k
. Therefore

C_{k}(k-T)=C_{k}(T)
.
∎

The next result gives the corresponding arithmetic propagation for the sets
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.

Proposition 18
.

Let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
and let
d>1
be an odd integer such that
\gcd(d,ab)=1
. Then

d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)

if and only if

d\in G_{(i,k)}(a,b)
for every
i\in C_{k}(T)
.

Proof.

Assume that

d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
Let
p^{e}\parallel d
. By Theorem
5
,

\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})
is even
and

T\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd\bigl(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)}.

Let
i\in C_{k}(T)
. Then
i\equiv(2r+1)T\pmod{k}

for some integer
r\geq 0
. Reducing modulo
\gcd\bigl(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)
, we obtain

i\equiv(2r+1)\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}=\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}+r\,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})\equiv\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})}{2}\pmod{\gcd\bigl(k,\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e}}(ab^{-1})\bigr)}.

Therefore, by Theorem
5
,

p^{e}\in G_{(i,k)}(a,b)

for every
p^{e}\parallel d
.
Hence,

d\in G_{(i,k)}(a,b)
for every
i\in C_{k}(T)
.

Conversely, the reverse implication is immediate since
T\in C_{k}(T)
.
∎

For convenience, let

G^{O}_{(T,k)}(a,b)=\{d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b):d\text{ is odd}\}

denote the set of odd integers in
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
.
The following results describe how these sets are related as
T
varies.

Corollary 19
.

Let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
. Then

G^{O}_{(T,k)}(a,b)=G^{O}_{(k-T,k)}(a,b)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
18
, if

d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
,
then

d\in G_{(i,k)}(a,b)

for every
i\in C_{k}(T)
.
By Lemma
17
, we have
k-T\in C_{k}(T)
which implies that
d\in G_{(k-T,k)}(a,b)
.
The converse is proved in the same way.
Therefore,
G^{O}_{(T,k)}(a,b)=G^{O}_{(k-T,k)}(a,b)
.
∎

Corollary 20
.

Let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
.
If
k
is odd, then

G^{O}_{(T,k)}(a,b)\subseteq G^{O}_{(0,k)}(a,b)
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{ord}(T)
divides
k
, it follows that
\operatorname{ord}(T)
is odd. Hence, by Proposition
16
, we have
0\in C_{k}(T)
.
The result now follows from Proposition
18
.
∎

Theorem 21
.

Let
\ell
be an odd prime. Then

G^{O}_{(1,\ell)}(a,b)=G^{O}_{(2,\ell)}(a,b)=\cdots=G^{O}_{(\ell-1,\ell)}(a,b)\subseteq G^{O}_{(0,\ell)}(a,b).

Proof.

Let
1\leq T\leq\ell-1
. Since
\ell
is prime, the additive order of
T
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
is equal to
\ell
which is odd. By Proposition
16
, we have
C_{\ell}(T)=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
.
By Proposition
18
, for every odd integer
d
with
\gcd(d,ab)=1
,

d\in G_{(T,\ell)}(a,b)
if and only if
d\in G_{(i,\ell)}(a,b)
for all
i\in\{0,1,\dots,\ell-1\}
.
In particular, the membership of
d
in
G_{(T,\ell)}(a,b)
is independent of the choice of

T\in\{1,\dots,\ell-1\}
,
which implies that

d\in G_{(0,\ell)}(a,b)
.
Therefore,

G^{O}_{(1,\ell)}(a,b)=G^{O}_{(2,\ell)}(a,b)=\cdots=G^{O}_{(\ell-1,\ell)}(a,b)\subseteq G^{O}_{(0,\ell)}(a,b).

∎

Remark 1
.

The preceding relations are formulated only for the odd parts

G^{O}_{(T,k)}(a,b),

and they do not extend automatically to the full sets
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
. Indeed, for even integers, membership depends not only on the odd prime-power conditions but also on the
2
-adic divisibility of

a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}
,
which is determined by the parity of the exponent
ks+T
.

3
An Explicit Algorithm and Examples

Combining the local characterization at odd prime powers, the global
2
-adic criterion for odd integers, and the analysis of the even case from the previous section, we present a practical procedure for deciding whether a given integer belongs to
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
and, when it does, for constructing a corresponding exponent. The procedure is presented in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Determine whether
d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
and compute an exponent

1:

Coprime nonzero integers
a,b
, integers
k\geq 1
,
0\leq T<k
, and
d\geq 1
.

2:

Decide whether
d\in G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
; if yes, output one integer
s\geq 1
such that

d\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr).

is applied

.

Let

. If

, then

.

4.1
Characterization

The characterization of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes of type
T
is given in this subsection. It shows that the type of a class is determined entirely by the order of its elements through membership in the set
G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
.

Theorem 23
.

Let
q
be a prime power and let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
.
Let
G
be a finite abelian group such that
\gcd(q,|G|)=1
and let
g\in G
be such that
\operatorname{ord}(g)=d
. Then
S_{q^{k}}(g)
is of type
T

if and only if
d\in G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
.
In particular, the type of
S_{q^{k}}(g)
depends only on the order of
g
.

Proof.

Assume first that
S_{q^{k}}(g)
is of type
T
. Then

S_{q^{k}}(g)=S_{q^{k}}(-q^{T}g)
.
Since
g\in S_{q^{k}}(g)
, it follows that

g\in S_{q^{k}}(-q^{T}g).

Hence, there exists an integer
j\geq 0
such that

q^{kj}(-q^{T}g)=g
.
Equivalently,
(q^{kj+T}+1)g=0
.
Since
g
has order
d
, this holds if and only if
d\mid(q^{kj+T}+1)
.
If
j\geq 1
, then
d\in G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
. Assume that
j=0
. Then
d\mid(q^{T}+1)
.
Let
m=\operatorname{ord}_{d}(q^{k})
. Then
m\geq 1
and

q^{km+T}\equiv q^{T}\equiv-1\pmod{d}
.
Hence,

d\mid(q^{km+T}+1)
,
which implies that
d\in G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
.

Conversely, assume that
d\in G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
.
Then there exists an integer
s\geq 1
such that
d\mid(q^{ks+T}+1)
.
Equivalently,

(q^{ks+T}+1)g=0
.
Thus,
q^{ks}(-q^{T}g)=g
,
which implies that

g\in S_{q^{k}}(-q^{T}g)
.
Hence,
S_{q^{k}}(g)=S_{q^{k}}(-q^{T}g)
.
Therefore,
S_{q^{k}}(g)
is of type
T
.

The final statement is immediate since the right hand side depends only on
d=\operatorname{ord}(g)
.
∎

4.2
Enumeration

The characterization obtained above leads directly to enumeration formulas for
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes of type
T
and type
T^{\prime}
. Since the type of a class is determined by the order of its elements, it is enough to count how many classes arise from each divisor of the exponent of
G
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group of exponent
N
. For each divisor
d
of
N
, let

\mathcal{N}_{G}(d)=\bigl|\{g\in G:\operatorname{ord}(g)=d\}\bigr|
and let
\varphi(d)
denote the
Euler phi function
.
The quantity
\mathcal{N}_{G}(d)
is well known; see, for example,
B1997
. Especially, we have
\mathcal{N}_{G}(d)=\varphi(d)
if
G
is cyclic.

Proposition 26
.

Let
q
be a prime power and let
G
be a finite abelian group of exponent
N
such that
\gcd(q,N)=1
. Let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
. Then the number of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes of type
T
is

\mathfrak{N}_{T}(G,q^{k})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid N\\
d\in G_{(T,k)}(q,1)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{G}(d)}{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(q^{k})},

(4)

and the number of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes of type
T^{\prime}
is

\mathfrak{N}_{T^{\prime}}(G,q^{k})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid N\\
d\notin G_{(T,k)}(q,1)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{G}(d)}{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(q^{k})}.

(5)

Proof.

Let
d
be a divisor of
N
. By Theorem
23
, a
q^{k}
-cyclotomic class containing an element of order
d
is of type
T
if and only if

d\in G_{(T,k)}(q,1).

Moreover, by Lemma
22
, every such class has size
\operatorname{ord}_{d}(q^{k})
. Since there are
\mathcal{N}_{G}(d)
elements of order
d
, the number of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes whose elements have order
d
is

\frac{\mathcal{N}_{G}(d)}{\operatorname{ord}_{d}(q^{k})}.

Summing over all divisors
d
of
N
such that
d\in G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
gives (
4
), while summing over all divisors
d
of
N
such that
d\notin G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
gives (
5
).
∎

5
Applications: Galois Duality of Cyclic Codes

In this section, the arithmetic and orbit theoretic results developed in Section
4
are applied to cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. The main point is that the action

i\mapsto-q^{T}i

on
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes modulo
n
describes the
\theta
-reciprocal behavior of the irreducible factors of
x^{n}-1
. We first study Galois linear complementary dual (LCD) cyclic codes in the general setting, including repeated-root codes. We then turn to Galois self-dual repeated-root cyclic codes, where the correct description is orbit-wise in general and specializes to the familiar pairwise form in the involutory case. These families of codes are significant because they extend the classical Euclidean and Hermitian settings and play an important role in the study of algebraic duality and symmetry in coding theory. The applications developed here are general theory, and it would be of further interest to investigate specific code lengths and classes of codes for which the arithmetic properties of
G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
give more explicit structural descriptions and sharper enumeration formulas.

5.1
Cyclic codes over finite fields

In this subsection, we recall the basic definitions and notation for cyclic codes over finite fields required in the study of Galois duality of cyclic codes. In particular, we review their description in terms of ideals in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
, together with the notions of generator polynomial, check polynomial, and Galois duality.

A
linear code

C
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
is a vector subspace
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{n}
.
A linear code
C
is called
cyclic
if

(c_{n-1},c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-2})\in C\text{ for all }(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\in C.

Via the linear isomorphism

\pi:\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{n}\rightarrow\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}[x]}{\langle x^{n}-1\rangle}

defined by

(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\mapsto c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1},

cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
are precisely the ideals of the quotient ring

\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}[x]}{\langle x^{n}-1\rangle}
.
Hence, every cyclic code has the form
C\cong\langle g(x)\rangle
,
where
g(x)
is the unique monic divisor of
x^{n}-1
of minimal degree in
\pi(C)
. This polynomial is called the
generator polynomial
of
C
; see, for example,
JLX2011
.

For integers
0\leq T<k
, let

\theta(a)=a^{q^{T}}

be the corresponding
Galois automorphism
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. The associated
Galois form
, or specifically the
\theta
-form
, on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{n}
is defined by

[u,v]_{\theta}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}u_{i}\,\theta(v_{i}),

for all

u=(u_{0},\dots,u_{n-1})
and
v=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{n}
.
The corresponding dual code is called the
Galois dual
, or specifically the
\theta
-dual
of
C
and it is given by

C^{\perp_{\theta}}=\{v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{n}:[v,c]_{\theta}=0\text{ for all }c\in C\}.

For a cyclic code
C=\langle g(x)\rangle
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
and

h(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{g(x)}
, we have that
C^{\perp_{\theta}}
is again a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
and

C^{\perp_{\theta}}=\langle h^{\ast,\theta}(x)\rangle.

A cyclic code
C
is called
Galois LCD
, or specifically the
\theta
-LCD
if

C\cap C^{\perp_{\theta}}=\{0\},

and it is called
Galois self-dual
, or specifically the
\theta
-self-dual
if

C=C^{\perp_{\theta}}.

It follows that
C
is
\theta
-LCD if and only if

\displaystyle\gcd(g(x),h^{\ast,\theta}(x))=1,

(6)

and
C
is
\theta
-self-dual if and only if

\displaystyle g(x)=h^{\ast,\theta}(x),

(7)

5.2
\theta
-self-reciprocal irreducible factors of
x^{n}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}

We consider the simple root case
\gcd(n,q)=1
. For integers
0\leq T<k
, let
\theta
be the Galois automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
defined by

\theta(\alpha)=\alpha^{q^{T}}.

For a polynomial

f(x)=f_{0}+f_{1}x+\cdots+f_{m}x^{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}[x]

with
f_{0},f_{m}\neq 0
, define its
\theta
-reciprocal
by

f^{\ast,\theta}(x)=\theta(f_{0})^{-1}x^{m}\theta\bigl(f(x^{-1})\bigr).

A monic polynomial
f
with nonzero constant term is called
\theta
-self-reciprocal
if

f^{\ast,\theta}(x)=f(x)
.

Fix
n\geq 1
with
\gcd(n,q)=1
, let
\zeta
be a primitive
n
th root of unity in an extension field of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. For each
q^{k}
-cyclotomic class
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, define

f_{Q}(x)=\prod_{i\in Q}(x-\zeta^{i}).

Then
f_{Q}(x)
is a monic irreducible factor of
x^{n}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
, and every monic irreducible factor of
x^{n}-1
is obtained in this way.

Theorem 27
.

For every
q^{k}
-cyclotomic class
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,

f_{Q}^{\ast,\theta}(x)=f_{-q^{T}Q}(x),

where

-q^{T}Q=\{-q^{T}i\bmod n:i\in Q\}
.
Consequently,
f_{Q}
is
\theta
-self-reciprocal if and only if

Q=-q^{T}Q,

or equivalently, if and only if every element of
Q
has additive order in
G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
.

Proof.

The roots of
f_{Q}(x)
are precisely the elements
\zeta^{i}
with
i\in Q
. Passing to
x^{-1}
replaces each root
\zeta^{i}
by
\zeta^{-i}
, and applying
\theta
to the coefficients raises the roots to the
q^{T}
th power. Hence, the roots of
f_{Q}^{\ast,\theta}(x)
are exactly

(\zeta^{-i})^{q^{T}}=\zeta^{-q^{T}i}
for all
i\in Q
,
which is precisely the root set attached to
-q^{T}Q
. Therefore,

f_{Q}^{\ast,\theta}(x)=f_{-q^{T}Q}(x).

The final statement follows from Theorem
23
with
G=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.
∎

To describe the full factorization of
x^{n}-1
, let
\sigma
be the permutation of the set of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes modulo
n
defined by

\sigma(Q)=-q^{T}Q.

Corollary 28
.

Let
\Omega
be a set of representatives of the
\sigma
-orbits on the set of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes modulo
n
. For each
Q\in\Omega
, let
\mathcal{O}(Q)=\{Q,\sigma(Q),\sigma^{2}(Q),\dots,\sigma^{r_{Q}-1}(Q)\}

be the
\sigma
-orbit of
Q
, where
r_{Q}=|\mathcal{O}(Q)|
. Then

x^{n}-1=\prod_{Q\in\Omega}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x).

Moreover,
f_{Q}(x)
is
\theta
-self-reciprocal if and only if
r_{Q}=1
.

Proof.

By Theorem
27
,

f_{Q}^{\ast,\theta}(x)=f_{\sigma(Q)}(x)
.
Hence, repeated application of
\ast,\theta
permutes the irreducible factors according to the
\sigma
-orbits on the set of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes modulo
n
. Partitioning the set of cyclotomic classes into these orbits allows the stated factorization. The final assertion is immediate from Theorem
27
.
∎

Corollary 29
.

Let
\Omega_{1}
be the set of representatives
Q
of the
\sigma
-orbits on the set of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes modulo
n
such that
|\mathcal{O}(Q)|=1
, and let
\Omega_{2}
be the set of representatives
Q
such that
|\mathcal{O}(Q)|>1
. Then

x^{n}-1=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)\right).

Moreover, the factors
f_{Q}(x)
with
Q\in\Omega_{1}
are precisely the monic irreducible
\theta
-self-reciprocal factors of
x^{n}-1
, whereas each factor belonging to an orbit represented by some
Q\in\Omega_{2}
is not
\theta
-self-reciprocal.

Proof.

By Corollary
28
,

x^{n}-1=\prod_{Q\in\Omega}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x),

where
\Omega
is a set of representatives of the
\sigma
-orbits. Partition
\Omega
as

\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2},

where
\Omega_{1}
consists of the representatives of the orbits of size
1
, and
\Omega_{2}
consists of the representatives of the orbits of size greater than
1
. This gives the stated factorization.

If
Q\in\Omega_{1}
, then
|\mathcal{O}(Q)|=1
, so
\sigma(Q)=Q
. Hence, by Theorem
27
,

f_{Q}^{\ast,\theta}(x)=f_{\sigma(Q)}(x)=f_{Q}(x),

and
f_{Q}(x)
is therefore
\theta
-self-reciprocal.

Conversely, if
Q\in\Omega_{2}
, then
|\mathcal{O}(Q)|>1
, so
\sigma(Q)\neq Q
. Again by Theorem
27
,

f_{Q}^{\ast,\theta}(x)=f_{\sigma(Q)}(x)\neq f_{Q}(x),

and hence,
f_{Q}(x)
is not
\theta
-self-reciprocal. The same argument applies to every factor
f_{R}(x)
with
R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)
.
∎

Remark 2
.

By Theorem
27
, a
q^{k}
-cyclotomic class
Q
belongs to
\Omega_{1}
if and only if every element of
Q
has additive order in
G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
. Hence, the self-reciprocal part of the factorization of
x^{n}-1
is determined precisely by the arithmetic set
G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
.

The following example illustrates how the factorization of
x^{17}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{4}}
is organized by the
\sigma
-orbits corresponding to the values
T=0,1,2,
and
3
.

Example 6
.

Let
\zeta
be a primitive
17
th root of unity in some extension field of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{4}}
. The
2^{4}
-cyclotomic classes modulo
17
are

Q_{0}=\{0\}
,
Q_{1}=\{1,16\}
,

Q_{2}=\{2,15\}
,

Q_{3}=\{3,14\}
,

Q_{4}=\{4,13\}
,

Q_{5}=\{5,12\}
,

Q_{6}=\{6,11\}
,

Q_{7}=\{7,10\}
,

Q_{8}=\{8,9\}
.
For each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,8\}
, let

f_{i}(x)=f_{Q_{i}}(x)=\prod_{j\in Q_{i}}(x-\zeta^{j})=(x-\zeta^{i})(x-\zeta^{-i}).

Then each
f_{i}(x)
is an irreducible quadratic polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{4}}
, and

x^{17}-1=(x-1)\prod_{i=1}^{8}f_{i}(x).

We now group this factorization according to the action

Q\longmapsto-2^{T}Q
.

Case
T=0
.

Then
\sigma_{0}(Q_{i})=-Q_{i}=Q_{i}
for all
i\in\{0,1,\dots,8\}
. Hence, every
\sigma_{0}
-orbits have size
1
, namely

\{Q_{0}\},\{Q_{1}\},\{Q_{2}\},\{Q_{3}\},\{Q_{4}\},\{Q_{5}\},\{Q_{6}\},\{Q_{7}\},\{Q_{8}\}.

Therefore,

x^{17}-1=(x-1)f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)f_{3}(x)f_{4}(x)f_{5}(x)f_{6}(x)f_{7}(x)f_{8}(x),

and every irreducible factor is
\theta
-self-reciprocal.

Case
T=1
.
Then

\sigma_{1}(Q)=-2Q=2Q
. Thus

Q_{1}\mapsto Q_{2}\mapsto Q_{4}\mapsto Q_{8}\mapsto Q_{1}
,
and

Q_{3}\mapsto Q_{6}\mapsto Q_{5}\mapsto Q_{7}\mapsto Q_{3}
.
Hence, the
\sigma_{1}
-orbits are

\{Q_{0}\},\quad\{Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{4},Q_{8}\},\quad\{Q_{3},Q_{5},Q_{6},Q_{7}\}.

Therefore,

x^{17}-1=(x-1)\,\bigl(f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)f_{4}(x)f_{8}(x)\bigr)\,\bigl(f_{3}(x)f_{5}(x)f_{6}(x)f_{7}(x)\bigr).

In particular, the only
\theta
-self-reciprocal irreducible factor is
x-1
.

Case
T=2
.

Then
\sigma_{2}(Q)=-2^{2}Q=-4Q=4Q
.
Thus

Q_{1}\leftrightarrow Q_{4},Q_{2}\leftrightarrow Q_{8},Q_{3}\leftrightarrow Q_{5},Q_{6}\leftrightarrow Q_{7}
.
Hence, the
\sigma_{2}
-orbits are

\{Q_{0}\},\{Q_{1},Q_{4}\},\{Q_{2},Q_{8}\},\{Q_{3},Q_{5}\},\{Q_{6},Q_{7}\}.

Therefore,

x^{17}-1=(x-1)\,\left(f_{1}(x)f_{4}(x)\right)\,\left(f_{2}(x)f_{8}(x)\right)\,\left(f_{3}(x)f_{5}(x)\right)\,\left(f_{6}(x)f_{7}(x)\right).

Thus, the only
\theta
-self-reciprocal irreducible factor is again
x-1
.

Case
T=3
.

Then

\sigma_{3}(Q)=-2^{3}Q=-8Q=8Q
.
Thus,

Q_{1}\mapsto Q_{8}\mapsto Q_{4}\mapsto Q_{2}\mapsto Q_{1}

and

Q_{3}\mapsto Q_{7}\mapsto Q_{5}\mapsto Q_{6}\mapsto Q_{3}
.
Hence, the
\sigma_{3}

\{Q_{0}\},\{Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{4},Q_{8}\},\{Q_{3},Q_{5},Q_{6},Q_{7}\}.

Therefore,

x^{17}-1=(x-1)\,\bigl(f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)f_{4}(x)f_{8}(x)\bigr)\,\bigl(f_{3}(x)f_{5}(x)f_{6}(x)f_{7}(x)\bigr).

\theta

5.3
Galois LCD cyclic codes

Let

q=p^{m}

be a prime power and let
n=n_{0}p^{r}

with
r\geq 0
and
\gcd(n_{0},p)=1
. Then

x^{n}-1=\bigl(x^{n_{0}}-1\bigr)^{p^{r}}.

Let
\sigma
be the permutation of the set of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes modulo
n_{0}
defined by

\sigma(Q)=-q^{T}Q
.
Using Corollary
29
, write

x^{n_{0}}-1=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)\right),

where
\Omega_{1}
is the set of representatives of the
\sigma
-orbits of size
1
, and
\Omega_{2}
is the set of representatives of the
\sigma
-orbits of size greater than
1
. Hence,

\displaystyle x^{n}-1=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)^{p^{r}}\right).

(8)

For each
Q\in\Omega_{2}
, define

F_{Q}(x):=\prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x).

Then

F_{Q}^{\ast,\theta}(x)=F_{Q}(x)

and

\displaystyle x^{n}-1=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}F_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right).

(9)

We have the following characterization of
Galois LCD codes.

Theorem 30
.

A cyclic code
C=\langle g(x)\rangle
of length
n=n_{0}p^{r}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
is Galois LCD if and only if

\displaystyle g(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in J_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in J_{2}}F_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)

(10)

for some subsets
J_{1}\subseteq\Omega_{1}
and
J_{2}\subseteq\Omega_{2}
. Consequently, the number of Galois LCD cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
is

2^{|\Omega_{1}|+|\Omega_{2}|}.

Proof.

Write

g(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{a_{Q}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)^{a_{R}}\right),

where
0\leq a_{Q},a_{R}\leq p^{r}
.
Then

h(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{g(x)}=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}-a_{Q}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)^{p^{r}-a_{R}}\right).

Since each
Q\in\Omega_{1}
is fixed by
\sigma
, by Theorem
27

h^{\ast,\theta}(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}-a_{Q}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)^{p^{r}-a_{\sigma^{-1}(R)}}\right).

By (
6
), the code
C
is
\theta
-LCD if and only if

\gcd\bigl(g(x),h^{\ast,\theta}(x)\bigr)=1
.
Since the irreducible factors
f_{R}(x)
are pairwise coprime, this holds if and only if

\min\{a_{R},\;p^{r}-a_{\sigma^{-1}(R)}\}=0
for every
R
.
Equivalently,

a_{R}=0\quad\text{or}\quad a_{\sigma^{-1}(R)}=p^{r}

for every
R
.

We now consider the two parts separately.
First, let
Q\in\Omega_{1}
. Since
\sigma(Q)=Q
, the above condition becomes

a_{Q}=0\text{ or }a_{Q}=p^{r}
.
Hence, each factor
f_{Q}(x)
with
Q\in\Omega_{1}
occurs either with multiplicity
0
or with multiplicity
p^{r}
.

Next, let
Q\in\Omega_{2}
, and write

\mathcal{O}(Q)=\{Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}\}
,
\sigma(Q_{i})=Q_{i+1}

where the indices are computed modulo
m
.
Then the condition above becomes

a_{Q_{i}}=0
or
a_{Q_{i-1}}=p^{r}

for all
i\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
.
If all exponents on the orbit are zero, then the orbit contributes nothing to
g(x)
. Suppose now that at least one exponent is
a_{Q_{i}}>0

for some
i
. Then necessarily

a_{Q_{i-1}}=p^{r}>0
. Using the same argument, we conclude that
a_{Q_{i-2}}=p^{r}
.
Repeating this process around the orbit, we obtain

a_{Q_{0}}=a_{Q_{1}}=\cdots=a_{Q_{m-1}}=p^{r}.

Therefore, for each orbit represented by
Q\in\Omega_{2}
, either all exponents are
0
or all exponents are
p^{r}
. Hence, the contribution of such an orbit is either
1
or

F_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}
, where

F_{Q}(x)=\prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)
.
It follows that

g(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in J_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in J_{2}}F_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)

for some subsets
J_{1}\subseteq\Omega_{1}
and
J_{2}\subseteq\Omega_{2}
.

Conversely, assume that

g(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in J_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in J_{2}}F_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)

for some subsets
J_{1}\subseteq\Omega_{1}
and
J_{2}\subseteq\Omega_{2}
. Then

h(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}\setminus J_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}\setminus J_{2}}F_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right).

Since each
f_{Q}(x)
with
Q\in\Omega_{1}
and each
F_{Q}(x)
with
Q\in\Omega_{2}
is
\theta
-self-reciprocal, we have

h^{\ast,\theta}(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}\setminus J_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}\setminus J_{2}}F_{Q}(x)^{p^{r}}\right).

Thus,
g(x)
and
h^{\ast,\theta}(x)
have no common irreducible factor, which implies that
\gcd\bigl(g(x),h^{\ast,\theta}(x)\bigr)=1
.
By (
6
), the code
C
is
\theta
-LCD.

Finally, each factor indexed by
\Omega_{1}
and each orbit product indexed by
\Omega_{2}
can be chosen independently. Hence, the total number of
\theta
-LCD cyclic codes is

2^{|\Omega_{1}|+|\Omega_{2}|}

as desired.
∎

From Theorem
30
, it is equivalent to, each
\theta
-self-reciprocal irreducible factor
f_{Q}(x)
with
Q\in\Omega_{1}
, and each orbit product
F_{Q}(x)
with
Q\in\Omega_{2}
, occurs either with multiplicity
0
or with multiplicity
p^{r}
.

Example 7
.

Let

,

, and

. Then

\theta(a)=a^{q^{T}}=a^{2}
for all

.
By Example

,

x^{17}-1=(x-1)\,A(x)\,B(x),

where

A(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)f_{4}(x)f_{8}(x)
and
B(x)=f_{3}(x)f_{5}(x)f_{6}(x)f_{7}(x)
.
Hence,

x^{34}-1=(x^{17}-1)^{2}=(x-1)^{2}A(x)^{2}B(x)^{2}.

For
T=1
, the
\sigma_{1}
-orbits are

\{Q_{0}\}
,
\{Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{4},Q_{8}\}
, and
\{Q_{3},Q_{5},Q_{6},Q_{7}\}
which implies that

\Omega_{1}=\{Q_{0}\}
and
\Omega_{2}=\{Q_{1},Q_{3}\}
.
Therefore, by Theorem
30
, a cyclic code of length
34
over
\mathbb{F}_{16}
is
\theta
-LCD if and only if its generator polynomial has the form

g(x)=(x-1)^{\varepsilon_{0}}A(x)^{\varepsilon_{1}}B(x)^{\varepsilon_{2}},

where
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\{0,2\}
.

For instance, the cyclic codes of length

over

generated by

(x-1)^{2}\bigl(f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)f_{4}(x)f_{8}(x)\bigr)^{2}\text{ and }\bigl(f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)f_{4}(x)f_{8}(x)\bigr)^{2}\bigl(f_{3}(x)f_{5}(x)f_{6}(x)f_{7}(x)\bigr)^{2}

are

Moreover, the number of

since

and

.

5.4
Galois self-dual cyclic codes

For integers
0\leq T<k
, let
\theta(a)=a^{q^{T}}

be the corresponding Galois automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. Recall that a cyclic code
C
is Galois self-dual, or simply
\theta
-self-dual if
C=C^{\perp_{\theta}}
.

Let

n=n_{0}p^{r}

with
r\geq 0
and
\gcd(n_{0},p)=1
. The following lemma shows that
\theta
-self-dual cyclic codes can occur only in the repeated-root case over fields of even characteristic.

Lemma 31
.

There exists a
\theta
-self-dual cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
if and only if both
q
and
n
are even. In particular, simple root
\theta
-self-dual cyclic codes do not exist.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a
\theta
-self-dual cyclic code
C=\langle g(x)\rangle

of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. Let

h(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{g(x)}
.
By (
7
), we have

g(x)=h^{\ast,\theta}(x)
. Since the factor
x-1
is always
\theta
-self-reciprocal, the exponent of
x-1
in
g(x)
must be equal to the exponent of
x-1
in
h^{\ast,\theta}(x)
which is

\frac{p^{r}}{2}.

Therefore,
p^{r}
must be even. Preciselt,
p=2
and
r\geq 1
. Since
q
is a power of
p=2
, it follows that
q
is even. Therefore,
n=n_{0}p^{r}
is even. In particular, simple root
\theta
-self-dual cyclic codes do not exist.

Conversely, assume that both
q
and
n
are even. Then
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
has characteristic
2
. Since
n
is even, the polynomial

g(x)=x^{n/2}-1

is a well-defined monic divisor of
x^{n}-1
.
Since the characteristic is
2
, we have

x^{n}-1=x^{n}+1=(x^{n/2}+1)^{2}=(x^{n/2}-1)^{2}

and

h(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{g(x)}=x^{n/2}-1=g(x).

Sine
\theta
acts trivially on the subfield
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

h^{\ast,\theta}(x)=g^{\ast,\theta}(x)=g(x).

By (
7
), the cyclic code
C=\langle g(x)
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
is
\theta
-self-dual.
∎

We now restrict the attention to repeated-root cyclic codes. By Lemma
31
,
\theta
-self-dual cyclic codes can exist only when
q
is even. Hence, throughout this subsection, we write
q=2^{\nu}
and
n=n_{0}2^{r}
,
where
\nu\geq 1
,
r\geq 1
and
n_{0}
is odd.
The next theorem gives a characterization for the generator polynomial of
\theta
-self-dual cyclic codes.

Theorem 32
.

Let
q=2^{\nu}
and
n=n_{0}2^{r}
for some
\nu\geq 1
,
r\geq 1
and
n_{0}
is odd.
Let
g(x)
be a monic divisor of
x^{n}-1
over in (
8
) of the form

g(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{a_{Q}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)^{a_{R}}\right),

where
0\leq a_{Q},a_{R}\leq 2^{r}
.
Then the cyclic code

C=\langle g(x)\rangle
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}

is
\theta
-self-dual if and only if

a_{Q}=2^{r-1},

for every
Q\in\Omega_{1}
, and

a_{R}=2^{r}-a_{\sigma^{-1}(R)}

for every
R
in
a
\sigma
-orbit represented by some element of
\Omega_{2}
.

Proof.

Let

h(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{g(x)}.

Then

h(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{2^{r}-a_{Q}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)^{2^{r}-a_{R}}\right).

By Theorem
27
,

h^{\ast,\theta}(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{2^{r}-a_{Q}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)^{2^{r}-a_{\sigma^{-1}(R)}}\right),

since every
Q\in\Omega_{1}
is fixed by
\sigma
.

First, assume first that
C
is
\theta
-self-dual. By (
7
),

g(x)=h^{\ast,\theta}(x).

Comparing exponents of the pairwise coprime irreducible factors, we obtain

a_{Q}=2^{r}-a_{Q}
for every
Q\in\Omega_{1}
, and

a_{R}=2^{r}-a_{\sigma^{-1}(R)}

for every
R

in a
\sigma
-orbit represented by some element of
\Omega_{2}
. From the first equality, we have

a_{Q}=2^{r-1}
for every
Q\in\Omega_{1}.

Conversely, assume that

a_{Q}=2^{r-1}
for every
Q\in\Omega_{1}
,
and

a_{R}=2^{r}-a_{\sigma^{-1}(R)}

for every
R

in a
\sigma
-orbit represented by some element of
\Omega_{2}
. Then, substituting these equalities into the expression for
h^{\ast,\theta}(x)
, we obtain

h^{\ast,\theta}(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{a_{Q}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}\ \prod_{R\in\mathcal{O}(Q)}f_{R}(x)^{a_{R}}\right)=g(x).

Hence, by (
7
), the cyclic code
C
is
\theta
-self-dual.
∎

Remark 3
.

Let
Q\in\Omega_{2}
and write

\mathcal{O}(Q)=\{Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}\}

with

\sigma(Q_{i})=Q_{i+1}
, where the subscripts are computed modulo
m
.
Then Theorem
32
is equivalent to

a_{Q_{i}}=2^{r}-a_{Q_{i-1}}

for all
i\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
.
Thus, if
m
is even, the exponents alternate between two complementary values

b,\ 2^{r}-b,\ b,\ 2^{r}-b,\dots,b,\ 2^{r}-b.

If
m
is odd, then going once around the orbit forces

a_{Q_{i}}=2^{r-1}

for all
i\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
.

The following corollary makes the generator polynomial explicit in the involutory case.

Corollary 33
.

Let
q=2^{\nu}
for some
nu\geq 1
and let
k
and
T
be integers such that
0\leq T<k
.
Assume that the map

Q\mapsto-q^{T}Q

is an involution on the set of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes modulo
n_{0}
. Then every orbit represented by an element of
\Omega_{2}
has size
2
. A repeated-root cyclic code
C=\langle g(x)\rangle

of length
n=n_{0}2^{r}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
is Galois self-dual if and only if

g(x)=\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{1}}f_{Q}(x)^{2^{r-1}}\right)\left(\prod_{Q\in\Omega_{2}}f_{Q}(x)^{b_{Q}}f_{\sigma(Q)}(x)^{2^{r}-b_{Q}}\right),

where
0\leq b_{Q}\leq 2^{r}
.
Consequently, the number of Galois self-dual repeated-root cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
is

(2^{r}+1)^{|\Omega_{2}|}
.

Proof.

Since
Q\mapsto-q^{T}Q

is an involution, it is clear that
every orbit represented by an element of
\Omega_{2}
has size
2
. The general condition in Theorem
32
reduces to
a_{Q}=2^{r-1}
for all
Q\in\Omega_{1}
,
and
a_{\sigma(Q)}=2^{r}-a_{Q}
for all
Q\in\Omega_{2}
.
Writing
b_{Q}=a_{Q}
gives the asserted form of the generator polynomial.

Conversely, any polynomial of the stated form satisfies

g(x)=h^{\ast,\theta}(x),

and hence, the corresponding code is Galois self-dual by (
7
).

Finally, each orbit represented by an element of
\Omega_{2}
contributes exactly
2^{r}+1
choices for the exponent
b_{Q}
. Therefore, the total number of such codes is
(2^{r}+1)^{|\Omega_{2}|}
.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this paper, a new family of good integers, namely the
(T,k)
-good integers with respect to
(a,b)
, has been introduced and studied. This family extends the classical notions of good, oddly-good, and evenly-good integers, and provides a arithmetic framework for divisibility conditions of the form

d\mid\bigl(a^{ks+T}+b^{ks+T}\bigr).

A self-contained arithmetic theory has been developed, including local criteria at odd prime powers, a global characterization of odd integers in terms of
2
-adic valuations of multiplicative orders, a treatment of the even case, and an explicit algorithm for deciding membership in
G_{(T,k)}(a,b)
and constructing a corresponding exponent.

The specialization
(a,b)=(q,1)
shows that this arithmetic is closely connected with the structure of
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes. In particular, the membership condition

d\in G_{(T,k)}(q,1)

determines when a
q^{k}
-cyclotomic class is stable under the action
a\mapsto-q^{T}a
.
This orbit interpretation leads naturally to applications in coding theory. In the cyclic case, we present a characterization of
\theta
-self-reciprocal irreducible factors of
x^{n}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
, and consequently explicit generator-polynomial descriptions and enumeration formulas for simple root
\theta
-self-dual and
\theta
-LCD cyclic codes. These applications have been developed at the level of general theory. It would be of further interest to focus on specific code lengths and classes of codes for which the arithmetic properties of
G_{(T,k)}(q,1)
give more explicit structural descriptions and sharper enumeration formulas.

The abelian group formulation developed in this paper indicates that the same arithmetic framework extends beyond cyclic codes. This suggests a natural direction for further work on abelian codes in group algebras
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}[G]
, where
q^{k}
-cyclotomic classes in finite abelian groups play the role of the corresponding cyclotomic classes modulo
n
. It would also be of interest to investigate analogous questions for repeated-root settings, constacyclic and quasi-abelian codes, and other families of Galois dual codes.
