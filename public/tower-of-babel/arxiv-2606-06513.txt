An Alternative Proof of Dilworth’s Theorem via Local Chain Merges
Tao Zhang

Abstract
Several elegant proofs of Dilworth’s theorem for finite posets already exist in the literature. In this note, we present an alternative inductive proof using a local “legal merge” lemma on chain ends. By combining this lemma with the bound established by Dilworth’s theorem, we observe a direct consequence: every chain decomposition can be transformed into a minimal chain decomposition through a finite sequence of such legal merges.



Coloring and Legal Merges

Let
S
be a finite poset with an initial
k
-chain decomposition
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}
. We define a
coloring scheme
on
S
by assigning color
i
to every element
x\in C_{i}
. Thus, elements share a color if and only if they belong to the same chain in
\mathcal{C}
.

For an arbitrary chain decomposition
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of
S
, a
legal merge
is an operation defined as follows: if two chains in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
have minimal elements of the same color, we merge their monochromatic bottom segments. Since all elements of a given color belong to the same chain in
\mathcal{C}
and are thus pairwise comparable, the union of these segments forms a linear order. Specifically, the segment whose own maximal element is smaller is merged into the chain containing the segment with the larger maximal element, ensuring the merged set remains a valid chain.

Lemma
.

For any chain decomposition
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of
S
, there exists a finite sequence of legal merges starting from
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and resulting in a decomposition
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
where the minimal elements of the chains have pairwise distinct colors.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be the total number of monochromatic segments in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Since
S
is finite,
\Phi
must be finite. Each legal merge reduces
\Phi
by exactly
1
. Since
\Phi\geq k
,
\Phi
cannot decrease indefinitely. Therefore, the process must terminate.
∎

Theorem

(Dilworth)
.

The minimum number of chains needed to cover a finite poset
S
is equal to the maximum size of an antichain in
S
.

Proof.

Clearly, the minimum number of chains is at least the maximum size of an antichain. It remains to show that equality holds, which we prove by induction on
|S|
.

The base case
|S|=1
is trivial. Let
e
be a maximal element of
S
, and let
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{e\}
. Let
k
be the size of the maximum antichain in
S^{\prime}
. By the inductive hypothesis,
S^{\prime}
admits a
k
-chain decomposition
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}
. Assign to each chain
C_{i}
a distinct color
i
, and color all elements within
C_{i}
with this color. Define

L_{i}=\{c\in C_{i}\mid c\preceq e\},\quad U_{i}=\{c\in C_{i}\mid c\npreceq e\}.

Let
U=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}
. Since
e
is a maximal element of
S
,
e
is incomparable to all elements in
U
. By induction,
U
decomposes into
r^{\prime}
chains
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
, where
r^{\prime}
is the size of the largest antichain in
U
. By the Lemma,
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
can be adjusted to a decomposition
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}
with
r^{\prime\prime}
chains of distinct minimal element colors, where
r^{\prime\prime}\leq r^{\prime}\leq k
.

Each chain in
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}
pairs with exactly one
L_{i}
by the color of its minimal element, with the paired two concatenated end-to-end to form a single chain. If
r^{\prime\prime}<k
, we append
e
to any unpaired
L_{i}
to form a chain, yielding a partition of
S
into
k
chains. If
r^{\prime\prime}=k
, let the element
e
form a separate chain; however, since
e
is incomparable to all elements in
U
, any maximum antichain of
U
together with
e
forms an antichain of size
k+1
. The equality remains satisfied.
∎

Combining the merging lemma with Dilworth’s theorem, we immediately obtain the following structural corollary.

Corollary
.

Every chain decomposition can be transformed into a minimal decomposition after finitely many legal merges, where the final number of chains equals the width of the poset.

Proof.

Let
w
be the width of the poset
S
. By Dilworth’s theorem, there exists a minimal chain decomposition
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{w}\}
of
S
. We apply the coloring scheme defined above to
S
based on
\mathcal{C}
, using exactly
w
distinct colors.

Now, let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be an arbitrary chain decomposition of
S
. According to the Lemma, a finite sequence of legal merges transforms
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
into a decomposition
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
where the minimal elements of all chains have pairwise distinct colors. Since there are only
w
colors available in total, the number of chains in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
cannot exceed
w
. On the other hand, by Dilworth’s theorem, no chain decomposition of
S
can have fewer than
w
chains. It follows that the final number of chains in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
is precisely
w
.
∎
