From 𝑵 N - to ( 𝒑 , 𝑵 ) (p,N) -Inflationary Attractors in view of ACT


Abstract
We review two types of fractional Kähler potentials K K which reduce, along the inflationary path, to the form N / ( 1 − ϕ q M ) p N/(1-\phi^{q_{\rm M}})^{p} with q M = 1 q_{\rm M}=1 or 2 2 and 0.1 ≤ p ≤ 10 0.1\leq p\leq 10 . Their coexistence, within a non-linear sigma model, with chaotic inflationary potentials of the form ϕ n \phi^{n} (where n = 2 n=2 or 4 4 ) determines, independently from q M q_{\rm M} and n n , a class of ( p , N ) (p,N) -inflationary attractors which leads to observables compatible with the ACT DR6. An implementation of these models in the context of supergravity can be also achieved by introducing two chiral superfields and a monomial superpotential, linear with respect to the inflaton-accompanying field, and supplementing the K K ’s above with a shift symmetry. Although inflation is attained for subplanckian inflaton values, the tensor-to-scalar ratio obtained for certain N N values can be possibly observable in the near future. [

n_{\rm s}=0.9743\pm 0.0068,~a_{\rm s}=0.0062\pm 0.0104~~\mbox{and}~~r\leq 0.038\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{at

\Phi-\Phi^{*}

{2(1+2p|\Phi|^{2})}/{(1-2|\Phi|^{2})^{p+2}}&\mbox{for $K=K_{\rm T}$.}\end{cases}

3
SUGRA Framework

To achieve a SUGRA incarnation of our set-up we consider two
gauge-singlet chiral superfields, i.e.,
z^{\alpha}=\Phi,S
, with

\Phi
(
{\alpha}=1
) and
S
(
{\alpha}=2)
being the inflaton and a
“stabilizer” field respectively following Ref.
[
15
,
16
]
. The
relevant part of the SUGRA Lagrangian density for
z^{\alpha}
’s can
be written as

{\cal L}=\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}+K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}\partial_{\mu}z^{\alpha}\partial^{\mu}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}-V_{\rm F}\right),

(17a)

where summation is taken over the scalar fields
z^{\alpha}
and the
Kähler potential
K
obeys Eq. (
7
). Also
V_{\rm F}
is the F–term SUGRA
potential given by

V_{\rm F}=e^{K}\left(K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}(D_{\alpha}W)(D^{*}_{\bar{\beta}}W^{*})-3{|W|^{2}}\right),

(17b)

where
D_{\alpha}W=W_{,z^{\alpha}}+K_{,z^{\alpha}}W
with
W_{\rm B}
being the
superpotential.

We consider the most general
W
consistent with the
R
symmetry
under which
R(S)=R(W)
and apply some specific hierarchies to
simplify it. Namely,

W=S(\lambda_{1}\Phi+\lambda_{2}\Phi^{2}-M^{2})~~\Rightarrow~~W=\begin{cases}\lambda S\Phi&\mbox{with}~\lambda=\lambda_{1}~~\mbox{for}~~\lambda_{2}/\lambda_{1}\ll 0.001\\
\lambda S\Phi^{2}&\mbox{with}~\lambda=\lambda_{2}~~\mbox{for}~~\lambda_{1}/\lambda_{2}\ll 0.001\end{cases}

(18)

and
M\ll m_{\rm P}
. Therefore,
W
assumes the following monomial form

W=\lambda S\Phi^{n/2}~~\mbox{with}~~n=2,~4

(19)

which may reproduce
V_{\rm I}
in Eq. (
1
) via Eq. (
17b
) for
suitably selected
K
’s. These same
K
’s have to reproduce the

\phi-\widehat{\phi}
relation in Eq. (
13

Both objectives can be achieved, as we show below, if we adopt the
the following
K
’s,
\widetilde{K}_{\rm Ms}
, which include two contributions
without mixing between
\Phi
and
S
, i.e.,

\widetilde{K}_{\rm Ms}=\widetilde{K}_{\rm M}+K_{\rm st}\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\rm M=\rm E,\>\rm T

(20)

where the indices “s” and “st” are just descriptive, i.e.,
they do not take symbolic values as M. From the contributions of

\widetilde{K}_{\rm Ms}
,
K_{\rm st}
successfully stabilizes
S
along the
inflationary path

\langle{S}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{\theta}\rangle_{\rm I}=0,

(21)

without invoking higher order terms. We adopt the form
[
16
]

K_{\rm st}=N_{\rm st}\ln\left(1+{|S|^{2}/N_{\rm st}}\right)\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>0<N_{\rm st}<6,

(22)

which parameterizes
[
16
]
the compact manifold
SU(2)/U(1)

with curvature
2/N_{\rm st}

\widetilde{K}_{\rm M}
contains not
only
K_{\rm M}
in Eq. (
9

\widetilde{K}_{\rm M}=K_{\rm M}+K_{\rm Msh}\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\rm M=\rm E,\>\rm T

(23a)

where
K_{\rm Msh}
, with the index “sh” being again

K_{\rm Msh}=-(N/2)\left(1-(\sqrt{2}\Phi)^{q_{\rm M}}\right)^{-p}-(N/2)\left(1-(\sqrt{2}\Phi^{*})^{q_{\rm M}}\right)^{-p},

(23b)

which can be specified as follows

K_{\rm Msh}=\begin{cases}-(N/2)\left(1-\sqrt{2}\Phi\right)^{-p}-(N/2)\left(1-\sqrt{2}\Phi^{*}\right)^{-p}\>\>&\mbox{for \rm M=\rm E,}\\
-(N/2)\left(1-2\Phi^{2}\right)^{-p}-(N/2)\left(1-2\Phi^{*2}\right)^{-p}&\mbox{for \rm M=\rm T.}\end{cases}

(23c)

With these ingredients, we can easily confirm that

\langle{K_{\Phi\Phi^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=J^{2}\>\>\mbox{for}\>\>K=\widetilde{K}_{\rm Ms}\>\>\mbox{with
$J$ given in Eq.~(\ref{VJe}).}

(24)

/T

4
Inflation Analysis

The establishment of a period of slow-roll E
p
/T
p
MI is controlled
by the condition

{\footnotesize\sf max}\{\epsilon(\phi),|\eta(\phi)|\}\leq 1,

(27)

where the slow-roll parameter
\epsilon

K_{\rm M}

On the other hand,

and

$K=K_{\rm T}$.}\end{cases}

(28b)

in Eq. (
4

large enough

. This fact is welcome from the point of view of
the effective theory since it assists us to stabilize our
inflationary scenario against higher order terms in
W
and/or

K_{\rm M}
– see Eqs. (
18
) and (
20
). Taking also into account that

V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\star})^{1/4}\leq 1
– see Fig.
1
-
(b)
–, we expect that
corrections from quantum gravity may be, in principle, under
control.

The amplitude
A_{\rm s}
of the power spectrum of the curvature
perturbations generated by
\phi
can be calculated at
\phi=\phi_{\star}

as a function of
\lambda
. With given
A_{\rm s}
we can also derive
\lambda

as follows

\sqrt{A_{\rm s}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}\pi}\;\frac{V_{\rm I}^{3/2}(\widehat{\phi}_{\star})}{|V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{\phi}_{\star})|}\>\>\Rightarrow\>\>\lambda\simeq 2^{\frac{5-2q_{\rm M}}{2}+\frac{n}{4}}\frac{\sqrt{3A_{\rm s}}n\pi f_{\rm M\star}^{p/2+1}}{\sqrt{pN\phi_{\star}^{n+q_{\rm M}}j_{{\rm M}\star}}}\>\>\mbox{for}\>\>K=K_{\rm M},

(32)

where
\phi_{\star}
is given by Eq. (
31
). We did not replace it in the
expression above to avoid the exposition of the lengthly final
result.

The remaining inflationary observables (i.e.
n_{\rm s}
, its running

a_{\rm s}
and
r
) are calculated via the relations

\displaystyle n_{\rm s}=\>1-6\epsilon_{\star}\ +\ 2\eta_{\star}\simeq 1-\frac{p+2}{(p+1){N_{\star}}},

(33a)

\displaystyle a_{\rm s}=\>{2\over 3}\left(4\eta_{\star}^{2}-(n_{\rm s}-1)^{2}\right)-2\xi_{\star}\simeq-\frac{p+2}{(p+1)N^{2}_{\star}},

(33b)

\displaystyle r=16\epsilon_{\star}\simeq 2^{{(p(4-q_{\rm M})+2)}/(p+1)}\frac{(pn^{p}N)^{1/(p+1)}}{(p+1)N_{\star}^{(p+2)/(p+1)}},

(33c)

where
\xi={V_{\rm I,\widehat{}\phi}V_{\rm I,\widehat{}\phi\widehat{}\phi\widehat{}\phi}/V_{\rm I}^{2}}
and the
approximate expressions are obtained by expanding the exact result
for all possible
K_{\rm M}
in powers of
1/{N_{\star}}
and keeping the
lowest order term. The common expressions for
\epsilon\gg\eta
and

\phi_{\star}
in Eqs. (
28a
) and (
31
) justify the “unified” results obtained
for
n_{\rm s}
,
a_{\rm s}
and
r
for all possible
K_{\rm M}
. Note that
n_{\rm s}

and
a_{\rm s}
are independent of
N
,
n
and
q_{\rm M}
at the lowest
order as in the case of E/TMI
[
3
,
11
]
. Also the
dependence of
r
on
N
is similar to that obtained for E/TMI whereas the one on
n
and
q_{\rm M}
is very faint. These prominent
features of our outputs signal the attractor behavior of our
solutions which are influenced only by the parameters
N
and
p
.

6
Conclusions

We proposed a variant of E/TMI (i.e., E- and T- model inflation)
named E
p
/T
p
MI using a gauge-singlet inflaton. In the non-SUSY
regime our setting was established as a non-linear sigma model
using chaotic potentials shown in Eq. (
1
) and rational Kähler potentials
with a 1st or 2nd order pole and an overall exponent
p
– see
Eq. (
9
). Within SUGRA we employ two chiral superfields, a
monomial superpotential,
W
, consistent with an
R
symmetry –
see Eq. (
19
) – and a shift-symmetric rational
K
for the
inflaton – see Eq. (
23a
). In both regimes our models develop an
attractor behavior – i.e., an independence from
n
and
q_{\rm M}
,
e.g., in Eqs. (
19
) and (
23b
) – towards the
P-ACT-LB-BK18
results for natural

(p,N)
values with possibly detectable primordial GWs. T
p
MI can
be further realized by a gauge non-singlet chiral superfield as
shown in Ref.
[
20
,
21
]
.

Despite the fact that our proposal employs one extra parameter
(
p
) compared to the original model of E/TMI and lacks any
string-theoretical motivation, the convergence of the results
towards the currently observational preferred values of
P-ACT-LB-BK18
data is really impressive – see Fig.
2
and
3
.
Moreover, within our scheme, the reheating phase is not
constrained, as e.g. in Ref.
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
,
deformations of the simple chaotic potential is not applied, as
e.g. in Ref.
[
25
,
26
]
, and corrections to the
inflationary potential are not required, as e.g. in Ref.
[
18
,
29
,
28
,
30
]
.
