Deep learning-based myocardial infarction detection using echocardiography
Authors: Shamal Bulbule, Shridevi Soma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMyocardial infarction (MI) is a life-threatening condition caused by reduced oxygen supply to the heart muscle due to blockage of the coronary arteries. Delayed or missed diagnosis increases the risk of mortality and heart failure, making early detection critical. Echocardiography is a non-invasive imaging technique that uses real-time ultrasound to examine the heart’s structure and function, including the evaluation of coronary artery disease and detection of regional wall motion abnormalities linked to MI. However, current approaches encounter limitations such as susceptibility to noise, restricted motion analysis capabilities, and significant computational demands. Hence, this work proposes EfficientNet_SpinalNet (Efficient_SpinalNet) for MI detection using echocardiography video. The proposed model utilizes the Hamad Medical Corporation Heart Hospital & Qatar University (HMC-QC) dataset and the Cardiac Acquisitions for Multi-structure Ultrasound Segmentation (CAMUS) dataset. The process begins by retrieving a video sample from the dataset, which is then decomposed into individual image frames. A hybrid filtering approach, integrating both median and Gaussian techniques, is first applied to the frames to suppress noise and enhance image quality. Following this preprocessing step, the Left Ventricle (LV) wall is fully extracted using the Fuzzy Local Information C-Means Clustering (FLICM) method, which further enables precise identification of the endocardial border. Following segmentation, displacement metrics and area variation curves are computed and passed into the feature extraction module. The extracted features, along with the displacement and area data, are then utilized for MI detection using the Efficient_SpinalNet architecture, which synergistically merges EfficientNet-B3-attn-2 with SpinalNet for enhanced diagnostic accuracy. Moreover, the experimental result reveals that the Efficient_SpinalNet functioned efficiently, attaining accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity values of 91.19%, 92.01%, and 92.435%. These results indicate that Efficient_SpinalNet is a reliable and efficient approach for real-time MI detection, offering potential improvements in clinical decision-making and patient outcomes.Keywords: Myocardial Infarction, EfficientNet, SpinalNet, Fuzzy Local Information C-Means Clustering, Echocardiography, EfficientNet-B3-attn-2

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) rank among the top causes of death globally. CVDs refer to cardiovascular and blood vessel illnesses that include angina, arrhythmia, and MI. Moreover, MI is the most dangerous and prevalent CVD, with symptoms such as sudden onset and fast alterations that endanger patients' lives [1]. A significant number of deaths worldwide are caused by myocardial infarction (MI). About 4 million individuals in the United States alone experience cardiac pain yearly [2]. MI is a life-threatening heart disorder resulting from blockage in the coronary arteries, which limits blood flow to cardiac tissue. Infarction is defined as tissue death due to inadequate blood supply [3]. Myocardial necrosis is irreversible and can result in permanent cardiac damage [4]. MI symptoms include extreme chest discomfort, breathing difficulties, and rapid heartbeats, which necessitate immediate therapy, such as endoscopic myocardial recanalization or bypass grafting of the coronary arteries. Precise assessment of the global and regional motion of the Left Ventricle (LV) of the heart is required for the early diagnosis of MI [5]. Early indications of MI are typically reflected in variations of the ventricular wall motion. As the condition progresses, the abnormalities are classified as hypokinesia, dyskinesia, and akinesia. In modern imaging methods, these irregularities are difficult to detect because the final interpretation is highly subjective [1]. Such conditions can significantly affect the patient’s cardiac function and overall health adversely [6].

Cardiac imaging tools have evolved over the past few years and produce extensive and precise information, such as real-time images of the heart valves and chambers. These recordings allow cardiologists to detect Regional Wall Motion Abnormalities (RWMA) in the LV, which are associated with MI [7]. Conventional heart attack diagnosis schemes rely primarily on electrocardiography (ECG) analysis, which measures the electrical activity of the heart [8]. Although ECG can help in diagnosing MI, the symptoms are often slight in the early stage, and the findings may overlap with myocardial ischemia. Echocardiography, on the other hand, can be used to assess the heart functions and detect RWMA [1]. Echocardiography is the most widely utilized tool for diagnosing MI due to its non-invasive nature, high safety and performance, low cost and easy availability [9]. Echocardiography functions on the principles of ultrasound imaging, capturing images of cardiac structures from multiple perspectives by varying the probe orientation. Echocardiography devices can offer a detailed view of the chambers of the heart and their walls, thus assisting in identifying the early signs of MI through RWMA [1]. Meanwhile, the accuracy of the detection is influenced by the sonographer's experience [5]. As a result, there is a critical demand for an automatic, more robust, and precise diagnostic method to address this problem [10]. This motivates scientists and cardiac specialists to create novel techniques that seek to identify and evaluate cardiac problems based on this data [7].

In the past few years, Machine Learning (ML) has become recognized as an efficient and precise technology that aids in the resolution of complex medical tasks such as MI [10]. Moreover, Artificial Intelligence (AI) methods [11], like Deep Learning (DL), have demonstrated high processing power. DL has been frequently employed for video dataset-based multi-classification. It can perform a broad range of analyses, including visual quality evaluation, view categorization, boundary separation, and disease detection. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are effective for forecasting and categorizing sequential information such as waveform signals, video, and natural language. Furthermore, the DL method, like Long Short Term Memory (LSTM), is widely used for multiple classifications of video images in massive video datasets with excellent precision [5]. Other DL methods, like Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), learn the features without the need for mining the unique characteristics. The precision of image categorization schemes is determined by the fundamental concepts of Artificial Intelligence (AI). The detection efficiency is primarily enhanced by various deep Convolutional networks (ConvNet). This is done by extracting the improved features and identifying the images using the inbuilt Convolutional networks [12]. The Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (B-LSTM), Multilead CNN (MLCNN), DL-based Least Square Support Vector Machine (DL-LSSVM), and other techniques are also utilized for MI identification [13].

Literature review

Kiranyaz, S., et al. [5] developed the active polynomial model for MI detection. It provided the clinicians with increased viewing capabilities of echo images via the color-coded segments. This model showed a high recall while reducing the number of parameters. Nonetheless, it did not depend on real-time data and failed to assess each section of the heart independently. Mohan, H.M., et al. [12]. devised a Faster Region-based Convolution Neural Network (RCNN) for real-time MI detection. It was a sophisticated non-invasive algorithm for identifying MI. This model needed minimal computing cost and resulted in the least amount of loss. However, it did not utilize advanced data augmentation techniques to boost the performance. Meanwhile, Degerli, A., et al. [1] introduced the Support Vector Machine (SVM) based early detection model for detecting MI. The process was lightweight, power-efficient, and energy-efficient. This approach required the largest collection of data for MI detection of both males and females of various ages to attain better performance. Mohan, H.M., et al. [14] devised a Deep Neural Network (DNN)-based real-time MI detection model. This approach was dependable and did not necessitate a large amount of memory. Nonetheless, this strategy demanded greater processing power.

Hamila, O., et al. [7]. developed the 3-Dimensional CNN (3D CNN) for video segmentation and MI detection. The model followed a pipeline structure, using a 2D CNN in the preprocessing stage to segment the LV chamber from apical four-chamber (A4C) data. This approach was dependable and did not necessitate more memory, but it required a lot of computer power. Lin, X., et al. [15] developed the LSTM-Unet for detecting the abnormalities in regional wall motion and MI. This model calculated the dimensions of the heart chambers and the LV ejection fraction automatically. Furthermore, it was efficient and required minimal training time. Nonetheless, it encountered the black box issue and failed to identify the degree of movement variations. Uchiyama, R., et al. [4] devised the End-to-end CNN model for MI detection. This model learned waveform properties of an unprocessed raw ECG image obtained from 12-lead ECG signals. This method did not necessitate any complicated preprocessing and it was very generalizable. However, it encountered a class imbalance issue and generated misclassified outcomes. Li, W., et al. [10] devised the Single-Lead ECG data -Generative Adversarial Networks (SLC-GAN) for MI detection. Here, issues such as insufficient data and class imbalance were effectively mitigated using the SLC-GAN approach. However, it failed to construct a more robust generative model that was capable of dealing with diverse forms of medical information. Bulbule, S. and Soma, S., et al. [16] introduced the parallel SqueezeNet (PSq-Net) for the detection of MI from echocardiography videos. By leveraging a parallel architecture of multiple SqueezeNet branches, the model effectively captured diverse spatial features from echocardiographic images, enhancing its sensitivity to subtle myocardial abnormalities. However, the parallel design of the model introduced a redundancy in feature extraction, potentially leading to increased training time and complexity in model optimization. Mingxi Han, et al. [17] introduced MSRC-TransBLSTM, a deep learning-based hierarchical hybrid model for automatic myocardial infarction (MI) detection. This model employed a hierarchical modeling strategy that combined spatial and temporal features for enhancing both local and global representations. This model effectively captured complex ECG signal patterns. However, a lack of validation on large-scale datasets restricts the model’s overall generalization capability. Chen, Y., et al. [18] proposed a multi-domain feature fusion convolutional neural network (MFF–CNN) that integrated time-domain, frequency-domain, and time–frequency domain features of ECG signals for automatic MI detection and localization. The model delivered robust outcomes, demonstrating the benefit of multi-domain feature fusion for improving generalization in complex medical diagnostic signals. However, using only Lead II data for training and testing could limit its ability to represent frequency and time–frequency interactions across all 12 leads.

Problem statement and objective

Despite significant advancements in automated MI detection, current approaches face several limitations. Most methods struggle to capture fine-grained temporal and spatial variations in cardiac motion, which are essential for accurate diagnosis. Many existing approaches are computationally intensive, reducing their suitability for deployment in real-time clinical settings. Furthermore, challenges such as noisy echocardiography data, limited or imbalanced datasets, and variability in patient conditions reduce the robustness and generalizability of current techniques. DL–based approaches often act as black boxes, limiting clinical interpretability and failing to quantify important functional variations in the heart. To address these limitations, this study aims to design an efficient, accurate, and robust framework for myocardial infarction detection using echocardiography.

Proposed Efficient_SpinalNet for MI detection using echocardiography

This study aims to identify myocardial infarction (MI) using the proposed Efficient_SpinalNet architecture, whose overall framework is illustrated in Fig. 1. The workflow starts with acquiring input video data from a selected dataset. After that, video frame extraction is carried out to convert a video into a number of image frames, and the image frame is fed to the preprocessing module. Preprocessing is performed using a combination of a median filter [19] and a Gaussian filter [20], which effectively reduces speckle noise while preserving important structural boundaries, thereby improving overall image clarity. Next, the LV wall is segmented using the FLICM [21] algorithm, which is well-suited for ultrasound data due to its ability to incorporate local spatial information and produce smooth, accurate segmentations. The same algorithm is subsequently applied to segment the endocardial boundary, enabling precise identification of the inner LV contour. The segmented results are used to derive displacement and area variation curves, which help characterize ventricular motion patterns associated with MI. From endocardial boundary segmentation, feature extraction is performed, where the features, like LOOP [22], LBP [23], and statistical features [24], like mean, variance, standard deviation, kurtosis, and energy, are extracted to provide complementary texture and shape characteristics. The extracted features, along with the displacement and segmented area curves, are subsequently input into the MI detection module. Finally, MI detection is executed using the proposed hybrid deep learning Efficient-SpinalNet, which is obtained by the combination of EfficientNet-B3-attn-2 [25] and SpinalNet [26]. This hybrid network enhances feature representation while maintaining computational efficiency, thereby enabling accurate and robust MI classification.

SpinalNet

The MI layer output and segmentation curve are forwarded to the SpinalNet [26]. SpinalNet is a specialized type of Artificial Neural Network (ANN) based on the structural and functional characteristics of the human spinal cord. It processes input data in a sequential, layered fashion, simulating the progressive transmission of signals across spinal segments. The architecture evaluates both localized and aggregated responses. Each segment generates a distinct local output, while an expanded version of the input contributes to a broader global representation. The network's parameters are optimized using training data to adjust its internal weights. In its conventional designThe architecture of SpinalNet comprises three main functional components: an input layer for segmented data, an intermediate processing unit, and an output layer responsible for final prediction. In the neural network (NN), the initial layer does not include an intermediate split; however, subsequent intermediate splits contain two elements: the output of the previous intermediary split and the current output of the standard input segment. These intermediate outputs are weighted and passed to the output split of each layer, where they are aggregated. The overall two inputs exist in every hidden layer of the intermediate piece. Dataset description

Dataset 1

The echocardiography videos are acquired from the HMC-QC dataset [28], which is created by the collaboration between Hamad Medical Corporation (HMC), Tampere University, and Qatar University. The dataset comprises 2D echocardiography recordings in A4C and A2C views. These recordings were acquired using ultrasound devices from different vendors, specifically Philips and GE Vivid (GE-Health-USA) systems. The temporal resolution of the recordings is 25 fps, while the spatial resolution ranges from 422 × 636 to 768 × 1024 pixels.

Dataset 2

The CAMUS dataset [29] consists of clinical echocardiographic examinations from 500 patients 500 patients, acquired at the University Hospital of St Etienne (France). To ensure clinical realism, no prerequisite processing or data selection is applied to the acquisitions, which are optimized for left ventricle ejection fraction measurement. The dataset contains challenging cases characterized by difficult tracing, significant variability in acquisition settings, and instances of incomplete wall visibility in the images. It is organized into two subsets: a training set including 450 patients with manual references based on the evaluation of a clinical expert, and a testing set comprising 50 patients. All raw input images are provided in.mhd file format. Discussion

The effectiveness of Efficient_SpinalNet for MI detection is determined by considering its performance with existing methods. The results noted from an experiment by schemes utilizing altered training data and K-fold data are illustrated in Table 5. The Efficient_SpinalNet achieved a superior process with a greater accuracy of 91.19%, sensitivity of 92.01%, and specificity of 92.435%. In addition, the accuracy achieved by Active Polynomials, PSq-Net, Conv-BiLSTM-Attn, Faster RCNN, SSS-DRN, MFF–CNN, SVM, and DNN is 81.64%, 82.518%, 82.768%, 83.11%, 83.896%, 84.051%, 84.98%, and 86.86%, which also attained the sensitivity of 81.43%, 82.545%, 82.713%, 83.05%, 83.876%, 83.878%, 84.97%, and 86.87%. Also, the techniques received a specificity of 81.925% by Active Polynomials, 82.716% by PSq-Net, 83.346% by Conv-BiLSTM-Attn, 83.389% by Faster RCNN, 84.089% by SSS-DRN, 84.453% by MFF–CNN, 85.544% by SVM, and 89.662% by DNN. Efficient_SpinalNet performs better than other conventional methods, such as Active Polynomials, PSq-Net, Conv-BiLSTM-Attn, Faster RCNN, SSS-DRN, MFF–CNN, SVM, and DNN, during MI detection by attaining superior accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. Specifically, while traditional models primarily rely on raw image inputs or limited feature representations, the proposed method enhances feature quality by incorporating both spatial and temporal information, namely, segmented area curves and displacement curves extracted from precisely segmented LV regions using FLICM. Additionally, the fusion of EfficientNet-B3-attn-2, known for its strong feature extraction capabilities, with SpinalNet, recognized for its efficient classification accuracy, leads to a more robust and accurate detection model. This integrated design enables Efficient_SpinalNet to achieve the highest accuracy and the lowest computational time. From a clinical perspective, the proposed Efficient_SpinalNet model can serve as a CAD tool to assist cardiologists in improving the speed, consistency, and reliability of myocardial infarction detection from echocardiography videos. By automatically analyzing left ventricular motion and structural changes, the system has the potential to reduce observer variability and support early clinical decision-making in routine cardiac assessment.

Conclusion

Early detection of MI, often driven by lifestyle-related risk factors, is crucial as delayed diagnosis can lead to chronic heart failure or sudden cardiac death. Here, Efficient_SpinalNet is introduced for MI detection using echocardiography. The process begins by selecting a video sample from the dataset. This input undergoes enhancement through a combination of median and Gaussian filtering techniques to reduce noise and improve image clarity. The refined frames are then passed into the segmentation module, where the complete LV wall is delineated using the FLICM algorithm. Subsequently, the same algorithm is applied to isolate the endocardial boundary within the segmented LV region. From this, displacement profiles and area-based segmentation curves are derived. Upon completion of boundary extraction, the workflow advances to the feature extraction stage. Then, MI detection is done by Efficient_SpinalNet, which is designed by a combination of EfficientNet-B3-attn-2 and SpinalNet. The results show that superior performance is achieved by Efficient_SpinalNet with a maximum of 91.19% accuracy, 92.01% sensitivity, and 92.435% specificity. The evaluation is conducted on publicly available datasets, which may not fully represent the complete diversity of real-world clinical scenarios. In addition, variations in echocardiography data quality, patient-specific cardiac motion patterns, and acquisition conditions can still influence model performance. While cross-dataset testing improves robustness, further validation on large-scale multi-center clinical data is required to confirm consistent performance across different hospital environments, imaging devices, and operator settings. Moreover, the current framework is computationally efficient but may still require optimization for deployment in low-resource or real-time embedded system. In future work, the model can be extended toward real-time implementation and integration into ultrasound imaging systems or portable point-of-care devices. However, further validation on large-scale multi-center clinical datasets is required before routine clinical deployment to ensure robustness and generalizability across different clinical settings, imaging conditions, and patient variability.
