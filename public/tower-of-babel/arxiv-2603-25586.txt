An explicit presentation for asymptotically rigid mapping class groups
Sergio Domingo-Zubiaga

Abstract
. We show that several families of asymptotically rigid mapping class groups arise as explicit quotients of the fundamental group of a graph of groups, with mapping class groups as vertex and edge stabilizers. Using this description, and building on the work of Labruère and Paris [ 17 ] , we compute explicit presentations for asymptotically rigid mapping class groups of surfaces.



In recent years, asymptotically rigid mapping class groups of manifolds have been in the spotlight for a variety of reasons (see
[
9
,
11
,
10
,
13
]
). First, they sometimes appear as extensions of Higman-Thompson groups by direct limits of mapping class groups of compact manifolds. In certain cases, this implies that their homology coincides with the stable homology of the underlying manifold (see
[
1
, Section 10]
and
[
7
, Section 5]
). Additionally, in the context of surfaces, these groups form countable subgroups of the so-called big mapping class groups, an active area of research at the intersection of geometric group theory, low-dimensional topology, topological groups, etc.

One of the main sources of motivation for the study of asymptotically rigid mapping class groups stems from the investigation of their finiteness properties (see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
10
,
13
,
15
]
). In particular, these properties are often closely related to the space of ends of the underlying manifold. For instance, for the families of groups considered in
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
15
]
, the associated asymptotically rigid mapping class group is finitely presented whenever the space of ends contains at least three points. These results are non-constructive, which naturally raises the question of how to obtain explicit finite presentations for this class of groups.

For surfaces of genus zero, this had previously been settled by the work of Funar and Kapoudjian
[
9
]
. More recently, Genevois, Lonjou and Urech
[
14
, Theorem 3.20]
gave a finite presentation for the asymptotically rigid mapping class group of the planar surfaces with punctures defined in
[
13
]
; and Hill, Kwan, Udall, and West
[
15
]
provided a finite presentation for the
pure graph Houghton group
, a finite-index subgroup of the asymptotically rigid mapping class group of the graph.

1.
Preliminaries

Mapping class groups are defined differently depending on the underlying space. For an orientable manifold
M
,
\textnormal{Map}(M)
is the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of
M
fixing
\partial M
, up to isotopy; for a locally finite graph
\Gamma
,
\textnormal{Map}(\Gamma)
consists of homotopy equivalences of
\Gamma
modulo homotopy; and for a handlebody
H
,
\textnormal{Map}(H)
is the group of homeomorphisms of
\partial H
that extend to
H
, up to isotopy.

Throughout this paper, we assume the reader is familiar with the standard literature on mapping class groups, and refer to
[
8
]
for further background. For the sake of consistency and clarity, throughout this section we define the relevant objects in the surface case. The constructions for the other cases are analogous, and we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
15
]
for the precise definitions. Nevertheless, when there are key differences we will say so explicitly.

Henceforth, all surfaces are assumed to be connected and orientable, and for such a surface
\Sigma
we define

\textnormal{Map}(\Sigma)=\textnormal{Homeo}^{+}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)/\textnormal{Homeo}^{+}_{0}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma),

where
\textnormal{Homeo}^{+}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
is the group of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms fixing the boundary pointwise, and
\textnormal{Homeo}^{+}_{0}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
is the group of isotopies fixing the boundary pointwise.

1.1.
Boundary permuting mapping class group

Given a surface
\Sigma
with
b
boundary components
\{A_{1},...,A_{b}\}
, fix a set of parametrizations for the
A_{i}
, i.e. a set of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms
\{\mu_{i}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow A_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq b}
. The
boundary permuting mapping class group

\textnormal{Map}_{\{\mu_{i}\}}(\Sigma)
is the group of isotopy classes (relative to
\partial\Sigma
) of homeomorphisms of
\Sigma
that respect the
\mu_{i}
, meaning that for any
\phi\in\textnormal{Map}_{\{\mu_{i}\}}(\Sigma)
, there exists a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,...,b\}
such that
\phi\circ\mu_{i}=\mu_{\sigma(i)}

\forall i
. For any two sets of parametrizations
\{\mu_{i}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow A_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq b}
and
\{\nu_{i}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow A_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq b}
there is an isomorphism between
\textnormal{Map}_{\{\mu_{i}\}}(\Sigma)
and
\textnormal{Map}_{\{\nu_{i}\}}(\Sigma)
given by conjugation by a homeomorphism
\psi
of
\Sigma
satisfying
\psi(\mu_{i}(s))=\nu_{i}(s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
i\in\{1,...,b\}
. Hence we denote
\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma):=\textnormal{Map}_{\{\mu_{i}\}}(\Sigma)
to simplify notation. Note that
\textnormal{Map}(\Sigma)
is a finite index subgroup of
\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
.

1.2.
Tree surfaces and rigid structures

Let
d\geq 1
and
r\geq 1
be integers,
O
and
Y
compact surfaces without boundary. Remove
r
open discs from
O
, giving rise to a surface
O^{r}
with
r
boundary components which we denote by
\{A_{1},...,A_{r}\}
. Similarly, remove
d+1
open discs from
Y
, giving rise to a surface
Y^{d}
with
d+1
boundary components which we denote by
\{B_{0},B_{1},...,B_{d}\}
. Fix orientation-reversing homeomorphisms
\mu_{i}:A_{i}\rightarrow B_{0}
for
i\in[1,...,r]
and
\lambda_{j}:B_{j}\rightarrow B_{0}
for
i\in[1,...,d]
. We define a set of surfaces
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
as follows:

•

M_{1}=O^{r}
,

•

M_{2}
is the result of gluing a copy of
Y^{d}
to each
\{A_{1},...,A_{r}\}
using the maps
\mu_{i}:A_{i}\rightarrow B_{0}
.

•

For
k\geq 3
,
M_{k}
is the result of gluing a copy of
Y^{d}
to each boundary component in
M_{k-1}\setminus M_{k-2}
using the maps
\lambda_{j}:B_{j}\rightarrow B_{0}
.

Figure 2
.
The tree surface
\mathcal{S}_{2,1}(O,Y)
, with
O
of genus 3 and
Y
of genus 1.

We define the
tree surface

\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
as
\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}M_{k}
. Each of the connected components of
\overline{M_{k}\setminus M_{k-1}}
for
k\geq 1
is called a
piece
. By construction, each piece is homeomorphic to
Y^{d}
. A subsurface
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
is
suited
if it is connected and is the union of
O^{r}
and finitely many pieces. A piece
P
is
adjacent
to a suited subsurface
\Sigma
if
P\not\subset\Sigma
and
P\cap\Sigma\neq\emptyset
. For each piece
P
of
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
, choose a homeomorphism

that agrees with the
\mu_{i}
’s and the
\lambda_{i}
’s used in defining
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
.

Remark 1.1
.

When
d\geq 2
, the surfaces
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
correspond to
Cantor surfaces
as introduced in
[
1
]
, whose end space is a Cantor set. On the other hand, the surfaces
\mathcal{S}_{1,r}(O,Y)
include the surfaces
\Sigma_{r}
in
[
2
]
, whose end space consists on
r
isolated points. In this sense, tree surfaces encompass both constructions.

Definition 1.2
.

(Preferred rigid structure). The set
\{i_{P}\>:\>P\text{ is a piece}\}
is called the
preferred rigid structure
on
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
.

1.3.
Asymptotically rigid mapping class groups

Let
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
be a tree surface equipped with its preferred rigid structure. A homeomorphism
f:\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
is
asymptotically rigid
if there exists a suited subsurface
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
such that

(1)

f(\Sigma)
is suited,

(2)

f
is
rigid outside of
\Sigma
, that is, for every piece
P
outside of
\Sigma
,
f(P)
is also a piece and
i_{f(P)}\circ f_{|P}\circ i^{-1}_{P}=\textnormal{id}_{Y^{d}}
.

Definition 1.3
.

(Asymptotically rigid mapping class group)
The
asymptotically rigid mapping class group

\mathcal{B}_{d,r}(O,Y)
is the group of asymptotically rigid maps of
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
, up to isotopy.

Remark 1.4
.

The asymptotically rigid mapping class group is analogously defined for every group in Theorem
I
, and we still denote it by
\mathcal{B}_{d,r}(O,Y)
, where
O
and
Y
are either specific compact manifolds without boundary, finite graphs or compact handlebodies. Although the asymptotically rigid mapping class group of a manifold is generally defined up to
proper isotopy
(see
[
1
, Subsection 3.3, page 14]
), we use isotopy as both definitions are equivalent in the surface case by
[
1
, Proposition 3.11]
.

1.4.
Stein-Farley cube complex

Consider all ordered pairs
(\Sigma,f)
where
\Sigma
is a suited subsurface of
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
and
f\in\mathcal{B}_{d,r}(O,Y)
. We deem two such pairs
(\Sigma_{1},f_{1})
and
(\Sigma_{2},f_{2})
to be equivalent, and write
(\Sigma_{1},f_{1})\sim(\Sigma_{2},f_{2})
, if there are representing homeomorphisms (abusing notation)
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
such that
f_{2}^{-1}\circ f_{1}
maps
\Sigma_{1}
onto
\Sigma_{2}
and is rigid outside of
\Sigma_{1}
. We denote by
[(\Sigma,f)]
the equivalence class of the pair
(\Sigma,f)
with respect to this relation, and write
\mathcal{P}
for the set of equivalence classes. Observe that
\mathcal{B}_{d,r}(O,Y)
acts on
\mathcal{P}
by left multiplication, namely
g\cdot[(\Sigma,f)]=[(\Sigma,g\circ f)]
.

Consider a pair
(\Sigma,f)
. Since
\Sigma
is a suited subsurface, it is the union of finitely many pieces and
O^{r}
. We define the
height

h((\Sigma,f))
of the pair
(\Sigma,f)
as the number of pieces in
\Sigma
. Note that if
(\Sigma_{1},f_{1})\sim(\Sigma_{2},f_{2})
then
h((\Sigma_{1},f_{1}))=h((\Sigma_{2},f_{2}))
, and thus
h
descends to a map (abusing notation)
h:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, setting the height
h([(\Sigma,f)])
to be the height of any representative.

We introduce a relation
\preceq
on the elements of
\mathcal{P}
by declaring
x_{1}\preceq x_{2}
if and only if
x_{1}=[\Sigma_{1},f]
and
x_{2}=[\Sigma_{2},f]
for suited subsurfaces
\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{2}
such that
\overline{\Sigma_{2}\setminus\Sigma_{1}}
is a disjoint union of pieces.

Define
a closed interval

[x,y]
to be the set of elements
z\in\mathcal{P}
such that
x\preceq z\preceq y
. The relation
\preceq
can be used to construct a cube complex
\mathfrak{X}_{d,r}(O,Y)
with
\mathcal{P}
as its 0-skeleton and every
[x,y]
as a
d
-cube with
d=h(y)-h(x)
(See
[
1
, Proposition 5.11]
or
[
2
, Section 4]
for details). We will refer to the cube complex
\mathfrak{X}_{d,r}(O,Y)
as the
Stein-Farley cube complex
associated to the tree surface
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
.

The Stein-Farley cube complex can be analogously defined for any of the asymptotically rigid groups in Theorem
I
. The fact that
\mathfrak{X}_{d,r}(O,Y)
is a cube complex is not obvious, and we want to remark that it relies on the fact that the corresponding underlying manifolds satisfy the
inclusion
and
intersection
properties. We refer the reader to
[
1
, Subsection 3.1]
for the precise definitions of these properties.

1.5.
Graphs of groups and fundamental groups

Let
\Gamma
be a finite undirected graph with vertex set
V(\Gamma)
and edge set
E(\Gamma)
. A
graph of groups

\mathcal{G}
over a finite graph
\Gamma
consists of

•

a group
G_{v}
for each vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
,

•

a group
G_{e}
for each directed edge
e=(v_{0},v_{1})\in E(\Gamma)
, together with a pair of monomorphisms:

\varphi_{e,0}:G_{e}\rightarrow G_{v_{0}},\quad\varphi_{e,1}:G_{e}\rightarrow G_{v_{1}}.

Let
T
be a tree formed by edges in
E(\Gamma)
containing every vertex in
V(\Gamma)
. Assign a symbol
y_{e}
to each
e=(v_{0},v_{1})\in E(\Gamma)
. The
fundamental group
of
\mathcal{G}
is the free product

(\ast_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\;G_{v})*(\ast_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\;\langle y_{e}\rangle)

modulo the relations:

•

y_{\bar{e}}=y^{-1}_{e}
for all
e\in E(\Gamma)
, where if
e=(v_{i},v_{j})
then
\bar{e}=(v_{j},v_{i})
,

•

y_{e}=1
for all
e\in T
,

•

y_{e}\varphi_{e,0}(x)y_{e}^{-1}=\varphi_{e,1}(x)
for all
e\in T
and every
x\in G_{e}
.

This definition does not depend on the choice of
T
, see
[
18
, Proposition 20]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
I

To prove Theorem
I
, we introduce a theorem of Brown which provides a method for expressing a group as the quotient of the fundamental group of a graph of groups, given that the group acts on a simplicial complex under suitable conditions.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
5
, Theorem
1^{\prime}
]

Let
G
be a group acting on a simplicial complex
K
, and
W_{K}
a subcomplex of
K
such that:

(1)

K
is simply-connected,

(2)

Every simplex of
K
is equivalent modulo
G
to a unique simplex of
W_{K}
,

(3)

W_{K}
has finite 2-skeleton,

(4)

The stabilizer
G_{v}
of every vertex
v\in K
is finitely presented,

(5)

The stabilizer
G_{e}
of every edge
e\in K
is finitely generated.

Let
\hat{G}
be the fundamental group of the graph of groups over the 1-skeleton of
W_{K}
, with stabilizers of vertices
G_{v}
as vertex groups, stabilizers of edges
G_{e}
as edge groups, and with the inclusion as the maps
\varphi_{e,0}
and
\varphi_{e,1}.
Then
G
is isomorphic to the quotient of
\hat{G}
by the relations:

y_{e}=1\textit{ for all }e\in E(W_{k}).

Whenever a subcomplex
W_{K}\subset K
satisfies hypotheses (2) and (3) in the theorem, we say that
W_{K}
is a
fundamental domain
of the action of
G
on
K
. We dedicate the rest of the section to introducing a simplicial complex where each of groups in Theorem
I
act satisfying every hypothesis of Theorem
2.1
, hence proving Theorem
I
. In contrast to the previous section, the definitions and proofs presented are formulated in the general setting, in order to be as precise as possible.

Let

2.2.

Let
\mathcal{B}

4.
Vertex and edge stabilizers in the surface case

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the asymptotically rigid mapping class group of a tree surface and

be a suited subsurface. Recall from Subsection

\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
denotes the boundary permuting mapping class group of
\Sigma
. For any

\textnormal{Map}^{A_{1}}_{o}(\Sigma)
denote the subgroup of
\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
of elements fixing

, and
\textnormal{Map}^{\{A_{1},A_{2}\}}_{o}(\Sigma)
the subgroup of elements fixing
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
as a set. For a simplex
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
, we call
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}
the stabilizer of
\sigma
by the action of
\mathcal{B}
. The next lemma goes along the lines of
[
1
, Lemma 6.3]
, and describes vertex and edge stabilizers for the action of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{K}
in the tree surface case.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider the action of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{K}
:

(1)

\mathcal{B}_{[\Sigma,\phi]}\simeq\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
.

(2)

Let
P_{1}
be a piece adjacent to
\Sigma
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{\{[\Sigma,\phi],[\Sigma\cup P_{1},\phi]\}}\simeq\textnormal{Map}_{o}^{A_{1}}(\Sigma)
, for
A_{1}
any boundary of
\Sigma
.

(3)

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be distinct pieces, both adjacent to
\Sigma
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{\{[\Sigma,\phi],[\Sigma\cup P_{1}\cup P_{2},\phi]\}}\simeq\textnormal{Map}_{o}^{\{A_{1},A_{2}\}}(\Sigma)
, for
A_{1},A_{2}
any couple of distinct boundaries of
\Sigma
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

We start by proving point (1). After multiplying by
\phi^{-1}
we may suppose the vertex is
[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}]
. If
\psi\in\mathcal{B}_{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}]}
then it must satisfy
\psi(\Sigma)=\Sigma
, and must be rigid away from
\Sigma
. This can be used to define maps:

\Phi:\mathcal{B}_{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}]}\rightarrow\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma),

\Psi:\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}]},

where, for
\psi\in B_{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}]}
, its image
\Phi(\psi)
is defined by choosing a representative that is rigid away from
\Sigma
and restricting it to
\Sigma
, which defines an element of
\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
; similarly, for
f\in\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
, the image
\Psi(f)
is the unique rigid extension of any representative of
f
to a homeomorphism of
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
. We will first check that these maps are well defined, and then see that they are inverses of each other.

The map
\Psi
is well defined, since representatives of
f\in\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
are isotopic so are their extensions. The map
\Phi
is well defined: if
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
homeomorphisms of
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
are isotopic, and are rigid away from
\Sigma
, then restriction to any suited surface
Y
containing the support of the isotopy defines the same element of
\textnormal{Map}_{o}(Y)
, and because of the
inclusion property
of tree manifolds (see the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 3.6]
, which works for tree manifolds), restriction to
\Sigma
defines the same element of
\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
. Hence the maps are well defined.

The maps satisfy
\Psi\circ\Phi=\textnormal{id}
, since taking a homeomorphism
\psi
of
\mathcal{S}_{d,r}(O,Y)
that is rigid away from
\Sigma
, restricting it to
\Sigma
and then extending it, gives back
\psi
. Analogously,
\Phi\circ\Psi=\textnormal{id}
. Hence the maps establish an isomorphism, since composition is preserved, and we conclude that
\mathcal{B}_{[\Sigma,\phi]}\simeq\textnormal{Map}_{o}(\Sigma)
.

To compute the stabilizer of the edge
\{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}],[\Sigma\cup P_{1},\textnormal{id}]\}
, we remark that

\mathcal{B}_{\{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}],[\Sigma\cup P_{1},\textnormal{id}]\}}\leq\mathcal{B}_{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}]}.

Hence we simply have to study the image of
\mathcal{B}_{\{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}],[\Sigma\cup P_{1},\textnormal{id}]\}}
under
\Phi
, which corresponds to the image of elements
\psi
that fix
P_{1}
. The image
\Phi(\psi)
is an element of
\textnormal{Map}^{A_{1}}_{o}(\Sigma)
for
A_{1}
the boundary component that is in
P_{1}
, and any element of
\textnormal{Map}^{A_{1}}_{o}(\Sigma)
is extended to an element in the stabilizer, hence
\mathcal{B}_{\{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}],[\Sigma\cup P_{1},\textnormal{id}]\}}\simeq\textnormal{Map}_{o}^{A_{1}}(\Sigma)
.

Analogously it can be proved that
\mathcal{B}_{\{[\Sigma,\textnormal{id}],[\Sigma\cup P_{1}\cup P_{2},\textnormal{id}]\}}\simeq\textnormal{Map}_{o}^{\{A_{1},A_{2}\}}(\Sigma)
.

∎

5.
Presentation of the boundary permuting mapping class group of surfaces

In
[
17
]
, Labruère and Paris find a presentation of the mapping class group of a finite type surface, possibly with boundary. Building on this work, in this section we will find a presentation of the boundary permuting mapping class group of a surface, and a set of generators of some of its subgroups.

We first give some definitions. By a
curve
on a surface
\Sigma
we mean the isotopy class of a closed simple curve on
\Sigma
, and by an
arc
we mean the isotopy class of an simple arc that connects either two boundary components of
\Sigma
, or two punctures of
\Sigma
. We will blur the difference between isotopy classes and their representatives.

Let
S_{g,b,p}
be a surface of genus
g
, with
p
punctures and
b
boundary components. For the rest of the paper, we adopt the notation:

S=S_{g,n,0}\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad S^{\prime}=S_{g,0,n}

for
g\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
.

By selecting
n
curves each bounding a punctured disc
D_{i}\subset S^{\prime}
, we determine an inclusion
S\hookrightarrow S^{\prime}
where the image of each of the
n
boundary components of
S
is one of the chosen curves. In other words, the surface
S^{\prime}
is the result of
capping
every boundary component of
S
with a punctured disc. This inclusion in turn induces a surjective homomorphism

\textnormal{Cap}:\textnormal{Map}_{o}(S)\rightarrow\textnormal{Map}(S^{\prime})

by, after permuting the boundary components, extending each homeomorphism as the identity in each
D_{i}
. More precisely, fix a set of parametrizations
\xi_{i}:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow D_{i}
. Each map
\phi\in\textnormal{Map}_{o}(S)
, induces a permutation
\sigma=\sigma(\phi)
of the
n
boundary components of
S
. We define

\textnormal{Cap}(\phi):=\begin{cases}\phi&\text{in }S,\\
\xi_{\sigma(i)}\circ\xi_{i}^{-1}&\text{in each }D_{i},\end{cases}

The notation
\textnormal{Cap}(\cdot)
reflects the fact that the map is the result of iterating the capping homeomorphism introduced in
[
8
, Proposition 3.19]
, adapted to the boundary
-
permuting setting.

Let
U_{S}
be the subgroup of
\textnormal{Map}_{o}(S)
generated by the Dehn twists
\{u_{1},...,u_{n}\}
in Figure
8
, where each
u_{i}
is the Dehn twist along
\partial D_{i}
. The group
U_{S}
is abelian and has a presentation

\langle u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{n}|R_{U}:=\{u_{i}u_{j}=u_{j}u_{i}\;\forall i,j\}\rangle.

The following lemma relates the groups
\textnormal{Map}_{o}(S)
and
\textnormal{Map}(S^{\prime})
through a short exact sequence.

Lemma 5.1
.

There is an exact sequence:

1\rightarrow U_{S}\rightarrow\textnormal{Map}_{o}(S)\overset{\textnormal{Cap}}{\rightarrow}\textnormal{Map}(S^{\prime})\rightarrow 1.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

We verify that the kernel of
Cap
is precisely
U_{S}
. The inclusion
U_{S}\subset\ker(\textnormal{Cap})
is direct. For the inclusion
\ker(\textnormal{Cap})\subset U_{S}
, we observe that elements in the kernel do not permute boundary components, and hence belong to
\textnormal{Map}(S)
. Because of
[
8
, Theorem 3.18]
, any element in
\ker(\textnormal{Cap})\cap\textnormal{Map}(S)
is in
U_{S}
.

∎

We will use this short exact sequence in order to compute a presentation of the boundary permuting mapping class group. The text subsection introduces one of the various tools needed for this.

5.1.
Exact sequences and presentations

Through their work, Labruère and Paris use a well-known method for crafting a presentation from a short exact sequence (see
[
17
, Subsection 2.2]
).

Let
1\rightarrow K\hookrightarrow G\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}H\rightarrow 1
be a short exact sequence of groups. Given presentations
H=\langle X_{H}|R_{H}\rangle
and
K=\langle X_{K}|R_{K}\rangle
, for each
x\in X_{H}
, we choose
\tilde{x}\in\pi^{-1}(x)
, and write

\tilde{X}_{H}:=\{\tilde{x}:x\in X_{H}\}.

Given any relation
r=x_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}...\,x_{l}^{\varepsilon_{l}}\in R_{H}
, if we substitute each
x_{i}
for
\tilde{x}_{i}\in\tilde{X}_{H}
we get an element
\tilde{r}=\tilde{x_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1}}...\,\tilde{x_{l}}^{\varepsilon_{l}}\in G
, which is in the kernel of
\pi
as
\pi(\tilde{r})=r
is a relation of
H
. Treat the group
K
as a subgroup of
G
. By the exactness of the sequence, one can choose a word
w_{r}
with letters in
X_{K}\subset G
that represents the same element as
\tilde{r}
. Set

R_{1}:=\{\tilde{r}w_{r}^{-1}:r\in R_{H}\}.

Since
K
is a normal subgroup of
G
, for each
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{X}_{H}
and
y\in X_{K}
the conjugate
\tilde{x}y\tilde{x}^{-1}
is also in
