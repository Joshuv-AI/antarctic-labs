Catastrophic pelvic-perineal injuries with traumatic hemipelvectomy following a motorcycle collision: a multidisciplinary reconstruction case report

Background

Pelvic and perineal trauma resulting from high-energy mechanisms such as motorcycle collisions often presents an extraordinary clinical challenge. Open pelvic fractures are rare, accounting for only 2%–4% of all pelvic fractures, yet they carry high mortality rates of 40% to 50%, with some series reporting rates as high as 70% in cases involving associated rectal or perineal injuries [1–3]. Traumatic hemipelvectomy (THP) is one of the most severe forms of pelvic trauma, characterized by disruption of the pelvic ring with associated vascular and soft tissue injury, and can be classified into complete and partial forms [4, 5]. These injuries show considerable variability across age groups and clinical settings and are often accompanied by severe multisystem damage, including perineal avulsion, bladder rupture, rectal transection, vascular compromise, pelvic instability, and even limb loss [1, 6]. Survivors are uncommon, but standardized management protocols remain limited, particularly for staged reconstruction and functional rehabilitation [7–10]. Managing such cases requires a coordinated multidisciplinary surgical strategy, frequently involving trauma surgery, orthopedic surgery, vascular surgery, urogynecology, colorectal surgery, plastic surgery, and burn care as part of a staged reconstruction plan [1, 8–11].

In this report, we present a unique case of a 52-year-old man who survived complete THP with devastating degloving pelvic-perineal trauma and hemorrhagic shock following a motorcycle crash. His injuries included complete avulsion of the left hemipelvis, disruption of the genitourinary and anorectal systems, and severe soft tissue and musculoskeletal loss. He underwent prolonged hospitalization lasting nearly four months, involving multiple staged surgeries performed by a highly coordinated multidisciplinary surgical team. Remarkably, this case occurred in Vietnam, a developing country with limited access to microsurgical techniques and advanced trauma systems. The patient’s successful recovery was achieved through strategic use of staged reconstruction, meticulous wound care, and close interdisciplinary collaboration. This highlights the feasibility of performing complex reconstruction even in resource-limited settings.

To our knowledge, few published cases have described such a comprehensive recovery from major pelvic-perineal trauma associated with traumatic hemipelvectomy, particularly in low- to middle-income healthcare settings. This case offers valuable insights into staged surgical reconstruction and multidisciplinary management of catastrophic pelvic-perineal injuries.

Case presentation

A 52-year-old man was admitted in 2022 to our tertiary care center following a high-energy motorcycle collision. He initially received first aid at a district hospital and was urgently transferred to our facility within 40 min. Upon arrival, he was in critical condition, presenting with severe polytrauma and profound hemorrhagic shock, characterized by a non-palpable pulse and unrecordable blood pressure. Initial assessment revealed extensive injuries involving the genitoperineal region and pelvis. His injuries included crush injuries to the left thigh and groin, extensive perineal lacerations, scrotal degloving with exposure of both testes, complete penile avulsion, full-thickness transection of the rectum, bladder rupture, and complete loss of the left hemipelvis, accompanied by an open fracture of the right femur and a fracture of the left clavicle.

The patient underwent immediate resuscitation in the emergency department. Invasive arterial blood pressure measured 50/30 mmHg. A multidisciplinary team was promptly consulted, and the patient was transferred to the operating room within 30 min of arrival for emergency surgical intervention.

Reconstructive surgical interventions

Within the first postoperative week, the patient developed extensive wound necrosis with foul-smelling purulent discharge. The necrosis extended from the left hemipelvic stump to the lower back and right buttock. Debridement revealed complete loss of skin, fascia, and muscle over the left abdominal quadrant and lower back, with full exposure of the peritoneum and sacral bone. The abdominal wall and left groin were widely exposed; therefore, a Parietex mesh was placed to temporarily reinforce the abdominal wall and groin defect (Fig. 2A). Three weeks later, additional necrotic areas were identified and surgically debrided (Fig. 2B, C). Urological re-evaluation revealed near-total necrosis of the anterior bladder wall, with spontaneous urinary leakage and bladder exposure due to the loss of pelvic soft tissue. The bladder had an estimated volume of around 50 mL, with edematous mucosa and unidentifiable bilateral ureteral orifices. In coordination with the urology team, the bladder was debrided and repaired using 2 − 0 Vicryl sutures, reinforced with 1 − 0 Vicryl. After reconstruction, the bladder capacity was estimated at around 30 mL. A Pezzer 24 Fr suprapubic catheter was reinserted, and no leakage was observed. The bladder was irrigated to remove all clots, and a drain was placed adjacent to the bladder. In collaboration with the burn and plastic surgery team, the previously placed Parietex mesh was replaced with a new one, partial skin closure over the exposed abdominal organs was achieved, and the surgical site was drained using a Penrose drain (Fig 2D).

Roughly three and a half months after admission, the patient underwent additional reconstructive surgery to address persistent soft tissue defects at the left pelvic stump. During hospitalization, the patient received meticulous wound care, with all debridements and dressing changes performed exclusively in the operating room under sterile conditions. Temporary wound coverage was achieved using serial debridement, mesh reinforcement, conventional dressings, and drainage, without the use of negative pressure wound therapy. He was discharged about four months post-injury with stable vital signs. The suprapubic bladder catheter and the colostomy were functioning properly. All pelvic reconstruction sites were healing steadily and showed good functional recovery. Mobilization was achieved with assisted rehabilitation, progressing to wheelchair ambulation. Given the extent of injury and soft tissue loss, prosthetic fitting was not feasible at the time of discharge. However, the follow-up was limited to the hospitalization period, and long-term outcomes were not assessed.

Discussion

Severe pelvic and perineal trauma, though rare, represents one of the most devastating and life-threatening injuries in trauma surgery [3]. Its severity arises from extensive soft tissue disruption, major vascular injury, high risk of contamination, and complex reconstructive requirements. While much of the literature focuses on obstetric or military injuries, motor vehicle collisions, particularly motorcycle crashes, represent an intermediate-energy mechanism that can result in multisystem trauma, complex pelvic fractures, and extensive soft tissue and organ damage [8, 11–13].

Effective management requires a multidisciplinary approach that prioritizes the patient’s physiological status and the specific anatomical characteristics of the injuries [7, 8, 11, 14]. The patient in this case presented with an exceptionally rare and severe constellation of injuries, including complete avulsion of the left hemipelvis, traumatic lower limb amputation, disruption of the genitourinary and anorectal systems, and extensive soft tissue and musculoskeletal loss. Due to the complexity of this injury, conventional classification systems have limited applicability. Despite the extent of trauma and profound hemorrhagic shock, the patient survived through a carefully coordinated multidisciplinary management.

Early, coordinated trauma care was critical to this patient’s survival, even within resource-limited settings. After initial stabilization at a district hospital, he was transferred to our tertiary center within 40 min. Immediate resuscitation was initiated, and he was taken to the operating room within 30 min by a multidisciplinary team. This rapid response aligns with trauma principles emphasizing early hemorrhage control and timely surgical intervention, which have been shown to improve survival and outcomes. A recent systematic review supports direct-to-operating-room strategies, emphasizing that timely surgical access significantly improves survival and functional outcomes in severely injured patients [15].

The value of fecal diversion in reducing septic complications is well documented, especially in cases involving extensive perineal injuries with significant soft tissue loss, as seen in this patient [11, 14, 16, 17]. Glasgow et al. reported that the mortality rate was significantly higher for rectal injuries managed without fecal diversion (10.8% versus 3.7%, p < 0.0001) [18]. Recent evidence also suggests that diversion reduces the need for repeated debridement and facilitates wound healing and reconstruction [19]. In this case, a colostomy was created at the transverse colon to divert the fecal stream away from the severely injured anorectal region. The transverse colon offers sufficient mobility and accessibility, permitting safe exteriorization through an upper abdominal stoma site. This approach is particularly advantageous in situations of extensive pelvic contamination or when lower abdominal access is limited due to soft tissue destruction and concurrent reconstructive procedures. By diverting stool away from the injured rectum and perineum, the transverse colostomy facilitated wound healing, reduced the risk of infection, and created a stable environment for future definitive reconstruction.

Management of severe pelvic and perineal trauma follows ATLS and damage control principles, prioritizing rapid hemorrhage control and stabilization, followed by treatment of other life-threatening injuries. Once the patient is stabilized, surgical management includes meticulous wound debridement and infection control. All nonviable tissues must be sharply excised, while preserving viable muscle and fasciocutaneous tissue for potential use in later reconstruction. When limb salvage is not feasible, early amputation is preferred, although no standardized scoring system guides this decision [20]. In this case, complete hemipelvic avulsion with a nonviable, insensate limb necessitated hemipelvectomy.

Although negative pressure wound therapy can promote granulation tissue formation and wound healing, its use in extensive pelvic-perineal defects may be limited by difficulty in achieving an effective seal, especially in the presence of contamination or exposed intra-abdominal structures. Reconstructing extensive perineal trauma presents a formidable surgical challenge due to the region’s complex anatomy and critical functional components. Following repeated debridements and necrotic tissue loss, the staged reconstructive strategy was employed using a combination of autologous skin grafting, a local rotational skin flap, and a right PAP flap. The utilization of the local and the right PAP flaps provided durable, well-vascularized coverage with relatively low donor site morbidity. These procedures were adapted to the available resources in Vietnam, a developing country where access to advanced reconstructive tools is limited.

Various reconstructive approaches have been reported. Saleh Al-Wageeh et al. [21] described successful use of an anterolateral thigh flap in a pediatric bilateral hemipelvectomy, while Brown and Rubayi [22] used combined local muscle and fasciocutaneous flaps for penetrating perineal trauma. Waszyn and Fortunato [23] reported coverage with an inferior gluteal artery perforator flap. More recent reports have emphasized multidisciplinary strategies and the potential for limb salvage or partial reconstruction in selected cases [9, 10, 24].

Conclusion

This case illustrates that successful outcomes are possible in resource-limited settings by applying pragmatic strategies such as skin flaps, autologous grafts, and coordinated interdisciplinary care. It emphasizes the importance of teamwork across specialties in managing complex trauma and provides valuable insights for future cases with similar challenges.
