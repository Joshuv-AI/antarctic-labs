1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of analytic functions defined in the open unit disk

\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}.

(1.1)

The space
\mathcal{H}
is a locally convex topological vector space equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of functions normalized by the conditions
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=1
. Every function
f
in the subclass
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{A}
of univalent functions admits a Taylor series expansion of the form

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(1.2)

A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be starlike if the image domain
f(\mathbb{D})
is starlike with respect to the origin. The corresponding subclass of
\mathcal{S}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
. These functions are characterized by the analytical condition

f\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}\iff\Re\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(1.3)

Let
\Omega
denote the class of Schwarz functions
\omega
, which are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and satisfy
\omega(0)=0
and
|\omega(z)|<1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Every such function admits a series representation
\omega(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\omega_{n}z^{n}
. An analytic function
f
is subordinate to an analytic function
g
(written as
f\prec g
) if there exists a function
\omega\in\Omega
such that
f(z)=g(\omega(z))
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. In particular, if
g
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f\prec g
if and only if
f(0)=g(0)
and
f(\mathbb{D})\subset g(\mathbb{D})
.

Using the subordination principle, Ma and Minda
[
34
]
introduced a unified framework to study starlike and convex functions by defining the class

\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(\phi):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\phi(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\right\},

(1.4)

where
\phi
is an analytic and univalent function mapping
\mathbb{D}
onto a domain that is symmetric with respect to the real axis, satisfying
\Re(\phi(z))>0
,
\phi(0)=1
, and
\phi^{\prime}(0)>0
.

In recent years, subclasses of starlike functions associated with exponential mappings have been extensively investigated. Motivated by these geometric properties, Panja et al.
[
37
]
introduced the subclasses associated with the function

\phi(z)=e^{\alpha z},\qquad 0<\alpha\leq 1.

(1.5)

A normalized analytic function
f\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to the family
\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
if it satisfies the geometric subordination condition

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec e^{\alpha z},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(1.6)

The mapping
\phi(z)=e^{\alpha z}
satisfies the classical Ma–Minda conditions; it is univalent, possesses a positive real part in
\mathbb{D}
, is symmetric with respect to the real axis, and maps the open unit disk onto a convex, oval-shaped target domain. As
\alpha
varies from
0
toward
1
, the boundary expands outwards symmetrically along the real axis, scaling from a localized neighborhood around
w=1
into a asymmetric convex domain.

1.1.
Inverse Logarithmic Coefficients

For a function
f\in\mathcal{S}
, the inverse logarithmic coefficients
\Gamma_{n}
, introduced by Ponnusamy et al.
[
40
]
, are defined by the inverse function
f^{-1}
through the expansion

F_{f^{-1}}(w):=\log\frac{f^{-1}(w)}{w}=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{n}w^{n},\quad|w|<\frac{1}{4}.

(1.7)

By explicit inversion, the first four inverse logarithmic coefficients are related to the Taylor coefficients
a_{n}
as follows:

\begin{cases}\Gamma_{1}=-\dfrac{1}{2}a_{2},\\[8.0pt]
\Gamma_{2}=-\dfrac{1}{2}a_{3}+\dfrac{3}{4}a_{2}^{2},\\[8.0pt]
\Gamma_{3}=-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(a_{4}-4a_{2}a_{3}+\dfrac{10}{3}a_{2}^{3}\right),\\[8.0pt]
\Gamma_{4}=\dfrac{35}{8}a_{2}^{4}-\dfrac{15}{2}a_{2}^{2}a_{3}+\dfrac{5}{2}a_{2}a_{4}+\dfrac{5}{4}a_{3}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}a_{5}.\end{cases}

(1.8)

Ponnusamy et al.
[
40
]
proved that for the univalent class
\mathcal{S}
, the inverse logarithmic coefficients satisfy the sharp bound
|\Gamma_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{2n}\binom{2n}{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with equality holding for the Koebe function and its rotations.

Recently, Hankel and Toeplitz determinants containing logarithmic coefficients have received considerable attention. Kowalczyk and Lecko
[
19
,
20
]
initiated the study of logarithmic Hankel determinants, defined for
f\in\mathcal{S}
by

H_{q,n}\left(F_{f}/2\right)=\begin{vmatrix}\gamma_{n}&\gamma_{n+1}&\cdots&\gamma_{n+q-1}\\
\gamma_{n+1}&\gamma_{n+2}&\cdots&\gamma_{n+q}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\gamma_{n+q-1}&\gamma_{n+q}&\cdots&\gamma_{n+2(q-1)}\end{vmatrix}.

(1.9)

The inverse analogue
H_{q,n}\left(F_{f^{-1}}/2\right)
, where the entries are the logarithmic coefficients of the inverse function
f^{-1}
with
f\in\mathcal{S}
, was introduced in
[
12
,
28
]
:

H_{q,n}\left(F_{f^{-1}}/2\right)=\begin{vmatrix}\Gamma_{n}&\Gamma_{n+1}&\cdots&\Gamma_{n+q-1}\\
\Gamma_{n+1}&\Gamma_{n+2}&\cdots&\Gamma_{n+q}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\Gamma_{n+q-1}&\Gamma_{n+q}&\cdots&\Gamma_{n+2(q-1)}\end{vmatrix}.

(1.10)

While logarithmic coefficient structures and Hankel determinant bounds for standard exponential cases have been studied
[
37
]
, inverse logarithmic configurations for the general class
\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
remain largely unexplored.

To resolve this problem, this paper provides a unified treatment of the parametric class
\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
governed by
\phi(z)=e^{\alpha z}
for
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Specifically, we present the following sharp findings in a clear sequence:

(1)

We determine sharp upper bounds for the initial inverse logarithmic coefficients
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2}
, and
\Gamma_{3}
.

(2)

We examine the sharp bounds for the consecutive difference
|\Gamma_{2}|-|\Gamma_{1}|
.

(3)

We derive sharp estimates for the second-order inverse logarithmic Hankel determinant
H_{2,1}\left(F_{f^{-1}}/2\right)=\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{3}-\Gamma_{2}^{2}
.

(4)

We obtain sharp estimates for the third-order Hermitian–Toeplitz determinant
T_{3,1}(f)
within this class.

(5)

We provide a complete solution for the generalized Fekete–Szegő functional
|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}|-\mu|a_{2}|
.

3.
Sharp Bounds for the Inverse Logarithmic Coefficients

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
and define the unique positive real root of the polynomial equation
1421\alpha^{4}+1456\alpha^{3}+368\alpha^{2}-96\alpha-48=0
as the critical transition threshold
\alpha_{0}\approx 0.2889
. For any function
f\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
, the initial inverse logarithmic coefficients satisfy the following sharp upper bounds:

|\Gamma_{1}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2},

(3.1)

|\Gamma_{2}|\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{\alpha}{4},&\text{if }\;0<\alpha\leq\dfrac{2}{3},\\[12.0pt]
\dfrac{3\alpha^{2}}{8},&\text{if }\;\dfrac{2}{3}<\alpha\leq 1,\end{cases}

(3.2)

|\Gamma_{3}|\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{\alpha}{6},&\text{if }\;0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0},\\[12.0pt]
\dfrac{29\alpha^{3}}{72},&\text{if }\;\alpha_{0}<\alpha\leq 1.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Equality is attained in each case for the corresponding extremal functions
f_{\text{Koebe}}(z)
,
f_{\text{odd}}(z)
, or
f_{\text{tri}}(z)
.

Proof.

By definition, a function
f\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
satisfies the geometric subordination condition

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=e^{\alpha\omega(z)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

(3.4)

where
\omega\in\Omega
. The function
\omega(z)
can be expressed in terms of a Carathéodory function
p\in\mathcal{P}
by setting
\omega(z)=\frac{p(z)-1}{p(z)+1}
, where
p(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}
.

Expanding the right-hand side of (
3.4
) into a Taylor series and equating coefficients yields:

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p_{1}}{2},

(3.5)

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p_{2}}{4}+\frac{3\alpha^{2}-2\alpha}{16}p_{1}^{2},

(3.6)

\displaystyle a_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p_{3}}{6}+\frac{5\alpha^{2}-4\alpha}{24}p_{1}p_{2}+\frac{17\alpha^{3}-30\alpha^{2}+12\alpha}{288}p_{1}^{3}.

(3.7)

Estimation of
|\Gamma_{1}|

From the relation connecting the inverse logarithmic coefficients to the direct coefficients,
\Gamma_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}a_{2}
. Substituting the parametric representation (
3.5

|\Gamma_{1}|=\left|-\frac{\alpha p_{1}}{4}\right|=\frac{\alpha}{4}|p_{1}|.

(3.8)

Applying the classical bound

yields the sharp bound
|\Gamma_{1}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}
.

The second inverse logarithmic coefficient is defined by

Factoring out

Also, since

Hence near

Since there are no critical points inside the interval, the derivative stays negative throughout the interval. So the function

Now consider the second case
\alpha_{c}<\alpha\leq 1

\tau_{1}^{*}<\delta_{a}(\alpha)

The right endpoint gives

\Psi_{a}(\alpha)=\lim_{\tau_{1}\to\left(\frac{4}{7\alpha+4}\right)^{-}}M(\tau_{1}).

Substituting the boundary value and simplifying we get

\Psi_{a}(\alpha)=\frac{7\alpha^{4}+4\alpha^{3}+4\alpha^{2}+2\alpha}{3(7\alpha+4)^{2}}.

Finally, we compare this branch with the cubic branch

\frac{29\alpha^{3}}{72}.

Their difference is

\Psi_{a}(\alpha)-\frac{29\alpha^{3}}{72}=\frac{-(1421\alpha^{4}+1456\alpha^{3}+368\alpha^{2}-96\alpha-48)}{72(7\alpha+4)^{2}}.

Since the denominator is always positive, the sign depends only on the polynomial in the numerator. The detailed sign analysis and the intersection point will be studied later in the global phase transition analysis.

Subcase (b).

This case applies when
|B|<2(1-|C|)

Subcase (c).

In this subcase, Lemma

\tau_{1}\geq\delta(\alpha)=\sqrt{\frac{84}{203\alpha^{2}-232\alpha+84}}
.
For this subcase, Lemma
2.2
gives

.
Substituting this into (

To determine the extremal value, we differentiate:

After cross multiplication and simplification, this reduces to

To study the sign of this polynomial, we compute

Solving

\alpha_{1}\approx 0.1387,\qquad\alpha_{2}\approx 0.9089.

Therefore, this subcase does not produce any new extremal value, and the maximal contribution from this region is bounded above by

Subcase (d).

In this subcase, Lemma
2.2
requires that the inequalities

To determine the extremal behavior, we differentiate with respect to
\tau_{1}

Since the derivative changes sign from positive to negative, this critical point corresponds to a local maximum.

We now compare this critical point with the upper boundary of the admissible interval. The inequality

Subcase (e).

This subcase corresponds to the intermediate admissible interval lying between the regions obtained in Subcases (c) and (d), namely:

Phase Transition Analysis

To determine the sharp global bound, we compare the boundary extremal function obtained in Subcase (a), namely

\Psi_{a}(\alpha)=\frac{7\alpha^{4}+4\alpha^{3}+4\alpha^{2}+2\alpha}{3(7\alpha+4)^{2}},

.

Equivalently,

More precisely, for

On the other hand, for

|\Gamma_{3}|\leq\frac{29\alpha^{3}}{72}.

4.
Bounds for the differences of inverse logarithmic coefficients

The Bieberbach conjecture was proved in 1985 by de Branges
[
10
]
, who established that the Taylor coefficients of any function
f\in\mathcal{S}
of the form (
1.2
) satisfy the inequality
|a_{n}|\leq n
for all
n\geq 2
. For the starlike subclass
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
, the coefficients are bounded by
n
, a result proved by Leung
[
31
]
. Within the class of convex functions, Li and Sugawa
[
33
]
investigated the difference of adjacent coefficients, while further results on successive differences were obtained in
[
38
,
3
,
2
,
4
]
.

Recently, attention has shifted toward the difference of logarithmic coefficients. In 2024, Lecko and Partyka
[
29
]
utilized the Loewner differential equation to establish sharp bounds for
|\gamma_{2}|-|\gamma_{1}|
within the class
\mathcal{S}
. Following this direction, Kumar and Cho
[
25
]
derived sharp estimates for the coefficient difference for certain subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
. Motivated by these investigations, this section focuses on establishing sharp upper and lower bounds for the difference of the initial inverse logarithmic coefficients, namely
|\Gamma_{2}|-|\Gamma_{1}|
, for functions belonging to the subclass
\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
. If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
and the inverse logarithmic coefficients
\Gamma_{n}
(
n=1,2
) are defined by (
1.8
), then

-\frac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha+2}}\leq|\Gamma_{2}|-|\Gamma_{1}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}.

(4.1)

These inequalities are sharp for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
. Using the expressions for
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
in terms of the parameters
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, the difference is given by:

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{2}|-|\Gamma_{1}|

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{\alpha}{8}p_{2}+\frac{3\alpha^{2}+2\alpha}{32}p_{1}^{2}\right|-\left|-\frac{\alpha}{4}p_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{32}\left(|(3\alpha+2)p_{1}^{2}-4p_{2}|-8|p_{1}|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{32}\Phi(p_{1},p_{2}).

(4.2)

The expression
\Phi(p_{1},p_{2})=|Kp_{1}^{2}+Lp_{2}|-|Jp_{1}|
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
2.4
with parameters
K=3\alpha+2
,
L=-4
, and
J=8
. It follows that
M=|4K+2L|=12\alpha
and
|2K+L|=6\alpha
.

To determine the upper bound, we check the condition
|2K+L|\geq|L|+J
, which requires
6\alpha\geq 12
, or
\alpha\geq 2
. Since
0<\alpha\leq 1
, this condition is not satisfied, and the alternative branch of Lemma
2.4
applies, giving
\Phi(p_{1},p_{2})\leq 2|L|=8
. Substituting this value into (
4.2
) yields the upper bound
|\Gamma_{2}|-|\Gamma_{1}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}
.

To determine the lower bound, we apply the condition
J^{2}\leq 2|L|(M+2|L|)
, which simplifies to
64\leq 8(12\alpha+8)
, or
12\alpha\geq 0
. This inequality holds for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and the corresponding branch of Lemma
2.4
gives
-\Phi(p_{1},p_{2})\leq 2J\sqrt{\frac{2|L|}{M+2|L|}}=16\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha+2}}
. Substituting this into (
4.2
) yields the lower bound.

Sharpness of the Estimates and Extremal Functions

•

The upper bound is reached for the odd starlike function
f_{\text{odd}}\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
defined by:

f_{\text{odd}}(z)=z\exp\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{e^{\alpha t^{2}}-1}{t}dt\right),

which corresponds to
p_{1}=0
and
p_{2}=2
.

•

The lower bound is attained by the function

defined by:

f_{\text{spiral}}(z)=z\exp\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{e^{\alpha\omega(t)}-1}{t}dt\right),\quad\text{where}\quad\omega(z)=\frac{z\left(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3\alpha+2}z\right)}{\sqrt{3\alpha+2}+\sqrt{2}z},

which corresponds to
p_{1}=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha+2}}
and
p_{2}=2
.

∎

5.
Hankel Determinants for the Inverse Logarithmic Coefficients

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
. If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
and the inverse logarithmic coefficients
\Gamma_{n}

(n=1,2,3)
are defined by (
1.8

\left|H_{2,1}\left(F_{f^{-1}}/2\right)\right|\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{\alpha^{2}}{16},&\text{if }\;0<\alpha\leq\dfrac{2}{5},\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{\alpha^{2}(15\alpha^{2}+10\alpha+4)}{4(35\alpha^{2}+60\alpha+12)},&\text{if }\;\dfrac{2}{5}<\alpha\leq 1.\end{cases}

(5.1)

These inequalities are sharp for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
, expanding the subordination relation
\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=e^{\alpha\omega(z)}
with
\omega(z)=\frac{p(z)-1}{p(z)+1}
for
p\in\mathcal{P}
expresses the direct coefficients in terms of
p_{n}

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p_{1}}{2},

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p_{2}}{4}+\frac{\alpha(3\alpha-2)p_{1}^{2}}{16},

\displaystyle a_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p_{3}}{6}+\frac{\alpha(5\alpha-4)p_{1}p_{2}}{24}+\frac{\alpha(17\alpha^{2}-30\alpha+12)p_{1}^{3}}{288}.

Substituting these expressions into the definition of the inverse logarithmic functional
H_{2,1}\left(F_{f^{-1}}/2\right)=\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{3}-\Gamma_{2}^{2}
yields:

\displaystyle H_{2,1}\left(F_{f^{-1}}/2\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}(35\alpha^{2}+60\alpha+12)}{9216}p_{1}^{4}-\frac{\alpha^{2}(5\alpha+2)}{384}p_{1}^{2}p_{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{48}p_{1}p_{3}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{64}p_{2}^{2}.

(5.2)

By the rotational invariance of the class
\mathcal{P}
, we assume
p_{1}\in[0,2]
and set
p_{1}=2x
with
x\in[0,1]
. Applying Lemma
2.1
to express
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
in terms of
\tau_{2},\tau_{3}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}

5

\displaystyle H_{2,1}\left(F_{f^{-1}}/2\right)=\frac{35\alpha^{4}}{576}x^{4}

\displaystyle-\frac{5\alpha^{3}}{48}x^{2}(1-x^{2})\tau_{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{48}(1-x^{2})(x^{2}+3)\tau_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{12}x(1-x^{2})(1-|\tau_{2}|^{2})\tau_{3}.

(5.3)

Applying the triangle inequality together with the estimate
|\tau_{3}|\leq 1
in (
5

\left|H_{2,1}\left(F_{f^{-1}}/2\right)\right|\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}x(1-x^{2})}{12}\left(|A+B\tau_{2}+C\tau_{2}^{2}|+1-|\tau_{2}|^{2}\right),

(5.4)

where
A=\frac{35\alpha^{2}x^{3}}{48(1-x^{2})}>0
,
B=-\frac{5\alpha}{4}x
, and
C=-\frac{3+x^{2}}{4x}<0
. Since
AC<0
, Case (ii) of Lemma
2.2
applies. Direct calculation shows that subcases (a), (b), and (c) are excluded for our interval. We evaluate the remaining cases:

(d)

The conditions
|B|\geq 2(1-|C|)
and
|AB|\leq|C|(|B|-4|A|)
reduce to the polynomial inequality
25\alpha x^{4}+36x^{2}-12\leq 0
. In this range, Lemma
2.2
states that
Y(A,B,C)=-|A|+|B|+|C|
. Substituting this into (
5.4
) eliminates the factor

from the denominator, leaving the function:

\phi(x)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{576}\left[36+(60\alpha-24)x^{2}-(35\alpha^{2}+60\alpha+12)x^{4}\right].

Differentiating
\phi(x)
with respect to

For

For

6.
Hermitian–Toeplitz Determinants for the Class
\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}

For a sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=2}^{\infty}
of coefficients of a normalized analytic function
f\in\mathcal{A}
of the form (
1.2
), the Hermitian–Toeplitz determinant of order
q
starting at index
n
is defined by:

T_{q,n}(f):=\begin{vmatrix}a_{n}&a_{n+1}&\cdots&a_{n+q-1}\\
\overline{a_{n+1}}&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n+q-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\overline{a_{n+q-1}}&\overline{a_{n+q-2}}&\cdots&a_{n}\end{vmatrix}.

(6.1)

Setting
q=3
and
n=1
in (
6.1
), and noting that
a_{1}=1
for normalized functions, the third-order Hermitian–Toeplitz determinant
T_{3,1}(f)
simplifies explicitly to the functional form:

T_{3,1}(f):=2\Re\left(a_{2}^{2}\overline{a_{3}}\right)-2|a_{2}|^{2}-|a_{3}|^{2}+1.

(6.2)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
. If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
is given by (
1.2
), then the third-order Hermitian–Toeplitz determinant satisfies the sharp inequalities:

\mathcal{B}_{L}(\alpha)\leq T_{3,1}(f)\leq 1,

(6.3)

where the sharp lower bound
\mathcal{B}_{L}(\alpha)
is defined piecewise by:

\mathcal{B}_{L}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}1-2\alpha^{2}+\frac{15\alpha^{4}}{16},&\text{if }\;0<\alpha<\frac{-2+\sqrt{964}}{30},\\[10.0pt]
1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}-\frac{\alpha^{2}(\alpha+6)^{2}}{4(15\alpha^{2}+4\alpha-4)},&\text{if }\;\frac{-2+\sqrt{964}}{30}\leq\alpha\leq 1.\end{cases}

(6.4)

Both bounds are sharp for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

By the definition of the class
\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
, there exists a Schwarz function
\omega
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
\omega(0)=0
and
|\omega(z)|<1
such that
\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=e^{\alpha\omega(z)}
. Expressing
\omega
in terms of a Carathéodory function

via
\omega(z)=\frac{p(z)-1}{p(z)+1}
, where
p(z)=1+p_{1}z+p_{2}z^{2}+\dots
, and equating coefficients, we obtain:

a_{2}=\frac{\alpha p_{1}}{2},\qquad a_{3}=\frac{\alpha p_{2}}{4}+\left(\frac{3\alpha^{2}-2\alpha}{16}\right)p_{1}^{2}.

(6.5)

Owing to the rotational invariance of the class
\mathcal{P}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
p_{1}\in[0,2]
. By applying the Libera–Złotkiewicz parameterization
2p_{2}=p_{1}^{2}+(4-p_{1}^{2})\xi
for some

, the coefficient

expands to:

a_{3}=\frac{3\alpha^{2}}{16}p_{1}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{8}(4-p_{1}^{2})\xi.

Substituting these coefficient expressions into the individual segments of the determinant functional (

) yields:

\displaystyle 2\Re\left(a_{2}^{2}\overline{a_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\alpha^{4}}{32}p_{1}^{4}+\frac{\alpha^{3}}{16}p_{1}^{2}(4-p_{1}^{2})\Re\xi,

(6.6)

\displaystyle-2|a_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}p_{1}^{2},

(6.7)

\displaystyle-|a_{3}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{9\alpha^{4}}{256}p_{1}^{4}-\frac{3\alpha^{3}}{64}p_{1}^{2}(4-p_{1}^{2})\Re\xi-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{64}(4-p_{1}^{2})^{2}|\xi|^{2}.

Combining these components and mapping the parameter to the real variable

with

T_{3,1}(f)=\frac{1}{256}\left(256-128\alpha^{2}x+15\alpha^{4}x^{2}+4\alpha^{3}x(4-x)\Re\xi-4\alpha^{2}(4-x)^{2}|\xi|^{2}\right).

We optimize this expression over the domain

7.

In 2024, Lecko and Partyka

(7.1)

\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}

Theorem 7.1
.

0<\alpha\leq 1
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and

\mathcal{B}_{L}\leq|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}|-\mu|a_{2}|\leq\mathcal{B}_{U},

where the upper bound
\mathcal{B}_{U}

\mathcal{B}_{U}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\alpha}{2},&\text{if }\;\alpha|3-4\lambda|<2+4\mu,\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{\alpha^{2}|3-4\lambda|-4\alpha\mu}{4},&\text{if }\;\alpha|3-4\lambda|\geq 2+4\mu,\end{cases}

(7.3)

and the lower bound
\mathcal{B}_{L}
is given by

\mathcal{B}_{L}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{\alpha}{4}(4\mu-\alpha|3-4\lambda|),&\text{if }\;\alpha|3-4\lambda|\leq 2\mu-2,\\[10.0pt]
-\alpha\mu\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\alpha|3-4\lambda|+2}},&\text{if }\;\alpha|3-4\lambda|\geq 2\mu^{2}-2,\\[12.0pt]
-\dfrac{\alpha}{4}\left(\dfrac{\alpha|3-4\lambda|+4\mu^{2}+1}{\alpha|3-4\lambda|+2}\right),&\text{if }\;2\mu-2<\alpha|3-4\lambda|<2\mu^{2}-2,\end{cases}

(7.4)

whenever the corresponding parameter region is non-empty. Both bounds are sharp for all admissible parameter selections.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
. Utilizing the structural expansions derived via the subordination relation for the class
\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
, the Taylor coefficients
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
are represented in terms of the Carathéodory parameters

and

by:

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p_{1}}{2},

(7.5)

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha p_{2}}{4}+\left(\frac{3\alpha^{2}-2\alpha}{16}\right)p_{1}^{2}.

(7.6)

Substituting equations (
7.5
) and (

\displaystyle|a_{3}-\lambda a_{2}^{2}|-\mu|a_{2}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\alpha p_{2}}{4}+\left(\frac{3\alpha^{2}-2\alpha-4\lambda\alpha^{2}}{16}\right)p_{1}^{2}\right|-\frac{\alpha\mu}{2}|p_{1}|

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{4}\left(\left|p_{2}+\left[\frac{\alpha(3-4\lambda)-2}{4}\right]p_{1}^{2}\right|-2\mu|p_{1}|\right)

(7.7)

The optimization function

2.4

(7.8)

These parameter choices give the base evaluation metrics

1. Upper Bound Estimation

Applying the corresponding upper branches from Lemma
2.4

When

delivers the upper branch of

2. Lower Bound Optimization

To obtain the sharp lower bounds, we apply Lemma

to establish the maximum values for

2.4
yields:

-\Phi(p_{1},p_{2})\leq 2J\sqrt{\frac{2|L|}{M+2|L|}}=4\mu\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha|3-4\lambda|+2}}.

Multiplying by
-\frac{\alpha}{4}
reduces the expression to
-\alpha\mu\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha|3-4\lambda|+2}}
, establishing the second lower bound branch.

•

When
2\mu-2<\alpha|3-4\lambda|<2\mu^{2}-2
, Lemma
2.4
yields:

-\Phi(p_{1},p_{2})\leq\frac{J^{2}+L^{2}+M|L|}{M+2|L|}=\frac{4\mu^{2}+1+\alpha|3-4\lambda|}{\alpha|3-4\lambda|+2}.

Multiplying by
-\frac{\alpha}{4}
delivers the final lower bound branch of the system.

3. Sharpness and Extremal Mappings

The bounds are sharp and are achieved by the following extremal functions:

•

For the first upper branch, equality holds for the odd starlike function
f_{\text{odd}}(z)
defined by:

f_{\text{odd}}(z)=z\exp\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{e^{\alpha t^{2}}-1}{t}\,dt\right)=z+\frac{\alpha}{2}z^{3}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8}z^{5}+\dots,

corresponding to
p_{1}=0
and
p_{2}=2
, which gives
a_{2}=0
and
a_{3}=\frac{\alpha}{2}
.

•

For the second upper branch and the first lower bound branch, sharpness is attained by the Koebe-type function
f_{\text{Koebe}}(z)
defined by:

f_{\text{Koebe}}(z)=z\exp\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{e^{\alpha t}-1}{t}\,dt\right)=z+\alpha z^{2}+\left(\frac{3\alpha^{2}+2\alpha}{4}\right)z^{3}+\dots,

corresponding to
p_{1}=2
and
p_{2}=2
.

•

For the remaining lower bound branches, equality is verified by selecting the Carathéodory parameters for the variational starlike function mapping
f_{\delta}(z)=z\exp\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{e^{\alpha\omega_{0}(t)}-1}{t}dt\right)
associated with the specific Schwarz function sequence:

\omega_{0}(z)=z\left(\frac{z+\delta}{1+\delta z}\right).

When
K=0
, the choice
p_{1}=0,p_{2}=0
trivially satisfies the minimum condition. In this case, the functional reduces to the corresponding extremal value directly. For
K\neq 0
, we choose the complex parameter
\delta=\frac{K}{|K|}
such that
|\delta|=1
. Setting
p_{1}=2t_{1}
and
p_{2}=2t_{1}^{2}+2(1-t_{1}^{2})t_{2}
with
t_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha|3-4\lambda|+2}}
and
t_{2}=-\delta
, the arguments of
Lp_{2}
and
Kp_{1}^{2}
become opposite. This choice produces exact cancellation, yielding
|Lp_{2}+Kp_{1}^{2}|=0
and producing the minimum value of the functional.

Hence, the estimates are sharp, completing the proof.

Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated several classical coefficient problems for the geometric subclass
\mathcal{S}_{ex}^{\ast}
of normalized analytic starlike functions defined by exponential subordination. By establishing an explicit link to Carathéodory functions and applying optimization lemmas, we determined sharp upper bounds for the initial inverse logarithmic coefficients
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
, and
\Gamma_{3}
, isolating a unique parameter transition threshold at
\alpha_{0}\approx 0.2889
.

Furthermore, we obtained sharp estimates for the consecutive difference
|\Gamma_{2}|-|\Gamma_{1}|
, the second-order inverse logarithmic Hankel determinant
H_{2,1}(F_{f^{-1}}/2)
, and the third-order Hermitian–Toeplitz determinant
T_{3,1}(f)
. Finally, we provided a complete solution to the generalized Fekete–Szegő problem within this class. Every bound was proved to be sharp through the construction of corresponding extremal functions, including
f_{\text{Koebe}}(z)
,
f_{\text{odd}}(z)
,
f_{\text{tri}}(z)
, and specialized variational functions, directly extending known benchmarks where
\alpha=1
. This work opens up possibilities for studying higher-order determinant inequalities within other parametric Ma–Minda subclasses.
