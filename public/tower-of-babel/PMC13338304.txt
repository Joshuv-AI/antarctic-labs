Ecology, host plant resistance, and management of paddy black beetle (Heteronychus lioderes) in rice under north-western Himalayan conditions
Authors: Vasu Mehta, Khushwinder Singh, Abdul Ahad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe paddy black beetle, Heteronychus lioderes Redtenbacher, has emerged as an important soil-dwelling pest in rice ecosystems of the north-western Himalayas. This study evaluated its population dynamics, host plant resistance, and management under mid-hills field conditions. The pest appeared in late July and peaked in early August, causing up to 48.4% tiller infestation. Population build-up showed significant positive correlation with minimum temperature, relative humidity, and rainfall. Screening of 60 rice genotypes revealed no highly resistant entries, although T-23 and Vallabh Basmati-24 exhibited moderate resistance. Among management options, Chlorpyrifos was most effective, recording lowest infestation (13.18%) and highest grain yield (35.33 q ha⁻¹), followed by carbofuran and clothianidin. Botanical treatments were less effective but offered eco-friendly alternatives. The study highlights the importance of integrating climatic monitoring, host resistance, and timely interventions for sustainable management of H. lioderes.Keywords: Heteronychus lioderes, Rice, Population dynamics, Pest management, Abiotic factorsSubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Plant sciences

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is one of the most important food crops worldwide and serves as the principal source of dietary energy for a large proportion of the population, particularly in Asian countries. Despite continuous improvements in crop production technologies, rice productivity is still substantially affected by insect pests and diseases. Crop losses caused by pests remain a major challenge to global food security1,2, and the increasing demand for food under changing climatic conditions further emphasizes the need for effective and sustainable pest management strategies3. In addition, rising temperatures and altered rainfall patterns are expected to influence insect pest abundance, distribution, and outbreak frequency in agricultural ecosystems4.

Among the different insect groups associated with rice ecosystems, soil-dwelling scarabaeid beetles are gaining importance because of their concealed feeding behavior and destructive nature. Both larvae and adults damage underground plant parts, resulting in poor plant establishment, reduced tillering, and yield decline5. In upland and rainfed rice ecosystems, such pests are often difficult to detect during the early stages of infestation, making their management challenging6. Although stem borers, leaf folders, planthoppers, and gundhi bug are considered major insect pests of rice in Himachal Pradesh, recent field observations in certain mid-hill and rainfed rice ecosystems have indicated increasing incidence of paddy black beetle, Heteronychus lioderes Redtenbacher, causing localized tiller damage and poor crop establishment under favorable soil moisture conditions. The concealed soil-dwelling habit of the pest makes early detection and management difficult, and limited published information is available regarding its ecology and management under north-western Himalayan conditions. Farmers in affected areas have reported increasing incidences of tiller damage, particularly under conditions favourable for soil moisture retention and reduced flooding. However, information regarding the ecology, seasonal incidence, and field-level management of this pest under Himalayan agro-climatic conditions remains limited.

Environmental factors such as temperature, relative humidity, and rainfall play an important role in regulating insect survival, development, and seasonal population build-up7–9. Understanding the relationship between abiotic factors and pest incidence is therefore essential for predicting outbreaks and developing timely management strategies. Climate-associated shifts in insect phenology and distribution have already been documented in several cropping systems, highlighting the importance of region-specific ecological studies10.

Host plant resistance forms an important component of integrated pest management because resistant cultivars can reduce dependence on insecticides and provide relatively stable pest suppression. However, resistant sources against many emerging insect pests remain poorly characterized, necessitating continuous screening of available rice germplasm11–13. Chemical insecticides continue to play a major role in pest suppression under severe infestation conditions, although concerns regarding environmental safety, non-target effects, and resistance development have increased in recent years14,15. Consequently, interest in botanical products and integrated pest management approaches has increased as safer and more sustainable alternatives for crop protection16–18.

Despite the increasing occurrence of Heteronychus lioderes in rice-growing areas of the north-western Himalayas, limited information is available regarding its seasonal ecology, host plant resistance, and management under field conditions. Therefore, the present study was undertaken to: (i) investigate the seasonal population dynamics of H. lioderes, (ii) determine the influence of abiotic factors on pest incidence, (iii) evaluate rice genotypes for resistance, and (iv) assess the efficacy of insecticides and botanicals for its management under field conditions.

Materials and methods

Study area and experimental conditions

Field investigations were conducted during the kharif season of 2019–2020 at the Rice and Wheat Research Centre, Malan, Himachal Pradesh, India (32°07′ N, 76°25′ E; 961 m above mean sea level). The experimental site is situated in the mid-hill sub-humid agro-climatic zone of the north-western Himalayas and is characterized by humid temperate conditions with an average annual rainfall of approximately 2500 mm. The region receives most of its rainfall during the monsoon season, creating warm and humid field conditions favorable for the development of soil-dwelling insect pests. The experimental field consisted of silty clay loam soil and had a history of continuous rice cultivation under irrigated conditions. The experimental field covered approximately 0.12 ha under rice cultivation during the study period.

Crop establishment and agronomic practices

Rice cultivar HPR 2143 was used for field experiments related to population dynamics and treatment evaluation. Seedlings were raised in a nursery following standard agronomic recommendations for the region. Transplanting was carried out during the first fortnight of July in plots measuring 13.5 m², maintaining a spacing of 20 × 15 cm between rows and plants. Experimental plots were maintained under recommended agronomic practices throughout the cropping season. Uniform irrigation, fertilizer application, intercultural operations, and weed management practices were followed across all plots to minimize variation arising from crop management practices. No insecticidal treatment was applied prior to the initiation of observations in order to allow natural establishment of Heteronychus lioderes populations.

Population dynamics of Heteronychus lioderes

Seasonal incidence and population dynamics of Heteronychus lioderes were monitored from transplanting until crop maturity under natural field conditions. Observations were recorded at weekly intervals using three complementary approaches: pit sampling, assessment of tiller infestation, and light trap monitoring. These methods were used to evaluate both below-ground and above-ground pest activity during the cropping season.

Pit sampling

Population density of adults and grubs was assessed using pit sampling, which is considered suitable for monitoring soil-dwelling scarabaeid pests occurring below the soil surface. Five sampling locations were selected randomly within the experimental field at each observation interval. At each location, soil samples measuring approximately 30 × 30 × 30 cm were excavated carefully, and the number of adult beetles and grubs present in the soil sample was recorded manually. Weekly mean population per five pits was subsequently calculated. Pit sampling was preferred over pitfall trapping because H. lioderes remains predominantly subterranean during most of its life cycle, and direct soil excavation allows recovery of both adults and immature stages occurring within the root zone.

Light trap monitoring

Adult beetle activity was monitored using a light trap fitted with a 125 W mercury vapor bulb installed near the experimental field. The trap was operated daily from 19:30 h to 05:30 h throughout the cropping season. Adult beetles collected in the trap were counted every morning, and weekly mean trap catches were calculated to determine seasonal activity patterns.

Influence of abiotic factors

Meteorological data including maximum temperature (°C), minimum temperature (°C), relative humidity (%), and rainfall (mm) were obtained from the meteorological observatory located at the experimental site during the cropping season. Weekly mean values of weather parameters were correlated with pest population parameters, including pit population, light trap catches, and percent tiller infestation, to determine the influence of abiotic factors on seasonal pest incidence. Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r) was used to assess the relationship between pest incidence and weather variables.

Screening of rice genotypes for resistance

A total of 60 rice genotypes, including aromatic and basmati lines, were evaluated for resistance against Heteronychus lioderes under natural field infestation conditions at a farmer’s field located near the research station (32°16′ N, 77°01′ E) (Table 1). The experiment was laid out in a randomized block design (RBD) with three replications. Each genotype was grown in separate plots following recommended agronomic practices for the region. Observations on pest incidence were recorded at 60 days after transplanting (DAT) by assessing percent tiller infestation in ten randomly selected hills from each plot. Based on the level of infestation, genotypes were classified according to a standard evaluation scale: 0 = immune, 1 = resistant, 3 = moderately resistant, 5 = moderately susceptible, 7 = susceptible, and 9 = highly susceptible (Table 2).

Evaluation of insecticides and botanicals

Field experiments to evaluate the efficacy of insecticides and botanical treatments against Heteronychus lioderes were conducted under field conditions using a randomized block design (RBD) with three replications. Each treatment plot measured 13.5 m². The treatments evaluated included Chlorpyrifos 20 EC, Carbofuran 3G, Clothianidin 50 WDG, Cartap hydrochloride 4G, Eucalyptus oil, Eupatorium extract, and an untreated control (Table 3). The insecticides were applied at recommended field doses using standard plant protection equipment, while botanical treatments were prepared freshly prior to application. Two applications were carried out at appropriate crop stages based on pest incidence. Pre-treatment observations on tiller infestation were recorded one day before treatment application to ensure uniformity of pest population across treatments. Post-treatment observations were recorded at 1, 7, and 15 days after spray (DAS) by assessing percent tiller infestation in randomly selected hills from each plot. Grain yield from each treatment plot was recorded at harvest and converted to q ha⁻¹. Economic analysis of different treatments was performed using prevailing local market prices of inputs and grain yield obtained under each treatment. Net returns were calculated by subtracting the total cost of cultivation and plant protection measures from gross returns, and the benefit–cost (B: C) ratio was computed as the ratio of net returns to total treatment cost.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA appropriate to randomized block design. Prior to analysis, percentage data were subjected to arcsine transformation to stabilize variance. Assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance were examined using Shapiro–Wilk and Levene’s tests, respectively. Treatment means were separated using Duncan’s Multiple Range Test (DMRT) at p ≤ 0.05. Correlation between pest population parameters and abiotic factors was determined using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r). Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS statistical software version 25.0.

Results

Seasonal incidence and population dynamics of Heteronychus lioderes

The incidence of Heteronychus lioderes was first observed during the 29th standard meteorological week (third week of July) under field conditions. The pest population increased gradually and reached peak activity during the 31st standard meteorological week, after which a declining trend was observed toward crop maturity (Table 4; Figs. 1 and 2). Pit sampling revealed that the adult population increased from 1.4 beetles per five pits during the 29th standard week to a peak population of 3.8 beetles during the 31st standard week. Thereafter, the population declined progressively and reached 1.4 beetles per five pits during the 39th standard week.

Influence of abiotic factors on population dynamics of Heteronychus lioderes

Correlation analysis revealed that the population parameters of Heteronychus lioderes were variably influenced by prevailing abiotic factors during the cropping season (Table 5). Light trap catches exhibited a significant positive correlation with minimum temperature (r = 0.61) and rainfall (r = 0.50), whereas correlation with maximum temperature and relative humidity was non-significant. Pit population showed a significant positive correlation with minimum temperature (r = 0.57) and relative humidity (r = 0.67). However, the association between pit population and rainfall was non-significant. Similarly, tiller infestation exhibited a significant positive correlation with minimum temperature (r = 0.45) and rainfall (r = 0.62). The results indicated that comparatively higher humidity, rainfall, and moderate temperature conditions prevailing during the monsoon period favored pest activity and infestation under field conditions.

Screening of rice genotypes for resistance against Heteronychus lioderes

Considerable variation in susceptibility among rice genotypes against Heteronychus lioderes was observed under natural field infestation conditions (Table 6). Based on the standard evaluation scale, none of the evaluated genotypes exhibited immune or resistant reactions against the pest. Among the tested genotypes, T-23 and Vallabh Basmati-24 were categorized as moderately resistant, recording comparatively lower tiller infestation levels. Eleven genotypes were categorized as moderately susceptible, whereas the majority of the evaluated genotypes fell under the susceptible category (Table 7). Twelve genotypes exhibited highly susceptible reactions with infestation levels exceeding 60%. Several traditional basmati genotypes, including Basmati 370, Pusa Basmati 1121, and Ranbir Basmati, showed comparatively higher susceptibility under field conditions.

Effect of insecticidal and botanical treatments against Heteronychus lioderes

Significant differences among insecticidal and botanical treatments were observed with respect to reduction in tiller infestation caused by Heteronychus lioderes under field conditions (Table 8; Fig. 3). Pre-treatment infestation levels recorded one day before treatment application were statistically comparable across treatments, indicating uniform pest population before initiation of treatments. Among the evaluated treatments, Chlorpyrifos 20 EC recorded the lowest mean tiller infestation (13.18%), followed by Carbofuran 3G (13.80%) and Clothianidin 50 WDG (14.45%). These treatments were significantly superior to the untreated control. Cartap hydrochloride 4G provided moderate suppression of infestation, whereas botanical treatments including eucalyptus oil and Eupatorium extract were comparatively less effective but still reduced infestation compared to untreated plots. The untreated control recorded the highest infestation level (31.75%) throughout the observation period.

Effect of treatments on grain yield and economic returns

Application of insecticidal and botanical treatments significantly influenced grain yield and economic returns under field conditions (Table 9). Among the evaluated treatments, Chlorpyrifos 20 EC recorded the highest grain yield (35.33 q ha⁻¹), followed by Carbofuran 3G (34.00 q ha⁻¹) and Clothianidin 50 WDG (32.25 q ha⁻¹). These treatments produced significantly higher yields compared to the untreated control. Economic analysis revealed that Chlorpyrifos 20 EC resulted in the highest net returns (₹42,860 ha⁻¹) with a benefit–cost ratio of 3.22, followed by Carbofuran 3G and Clothianidin 50 WDG. Botanical treatments recorded comparatively lower economic returns; however, they performed better than the untreated control. The untreated control recorded the lowest grain yield (15.65 q ha⁻¹) and economic returns among all treatments.

Discussion

The present investigation demonstrated that Heteronychus lioderes remained active throughout the cropping season, with peak infestation occurring during August under humid monsoon conditions. The increase in pest population during the early vegetative stage of the crop may be associated with favorable soil moisture and moderate temperature conditions that support adult activity and feeding. Similar observations on enhanced activity of soil-dwelling scarabaeid pests under warm and humid conditions have been reported in rice and other cereal ecosystems7,6.

Correlation analysis indicated positive associations between pest incidence and minimum temperature, relative humidity, and rainfall. These environmental factors likely influenced adult emergence, movement, and feeding activity in the field. Increased soil moisture during the monsoon period may also favor survival of subterranean stages, thereby contributing to higher infestation levels. Comparable relationships between climatic factors and insect pest incidence have been documented in earlier ecological studies on rice insect pests8,9.

Considerable variation in susceptibility among rice genotypes against H. lioderes was observed under natural field conditions. None of the evaluated genotypes exhibited complete resistance; however, T-23 and Vallabh Basmati-24 showed comparatively lower infestation and were categorized as moderately resistant. The observed variation in host response may be associated with differences in morphological and biochemical plant characteristics that influence pest feeding and establishment. Host plant resistance has long been recognized as an important component of sustainable insect pest management because it reduces dependence on chemical insecticides and contributes to stable pest suppression under field conditions11–13. Most traditional basmati genotypes were categorized as susceptible to highly susceptible under field conditions. The comparatively higher susceptibility of aromatic cultivars may possibly be associated with varietal differences in plant vigor, tissue characteristics, or attractiveness to the pest, although these aspects were not examined in the present study. The identification of moderately resistant genotypes under natural infestation conditions provides useful preliminary information for future resistance breeding and integrated pest management programmes in the region.

Among the evaluated management treatments, Chlorpyrifos 20 EC, Carbofuran 3G, and Clothianidin 50 WDG recorded comparatively lower tiller infestation and higher grain yield under field conditions. Earlier studies have also reported the effectiveness of organophosphate, carbamate, and neonicotinoid insecticides against soil-dwelling insect pests in rice ecosystems15. However, the environmental and toxicological concerns associated with certain conventional insecticides necessitate their careful and judicious use within an integrated pest management framework14,18.

Botanical treatments including eucalyptus oil and Eupatorium extract were comparatively less effective than synthetic insecticides; nevertheless, they reduced infestation compared to untreated plots. Plant-derived products are generally considered environmentally safer alternatives because of their lower persistence and comparatively reduced non-target effects17. Although the level of protection observed in the present study was moderate, botanicals may still have value as supplementary components within integrated pest management programmes. Economic analysis indicated comparatively higher net returns and benefit–cost ratios in plots treated with Chlorpyrifos 20 EC and Carbofuran 3G. Improved economic returns in treated plots were primarily associated with reduced pest infestation and increased grain yield. The grain yield recorded under different treatments in the present study was within the productivity range generally reported for rice cultivation under mid-hill conditions of Himachal Pradesh, where rice productivity is influenced by varietal performance, climatic conditions, soil characteristics, and crop management practices19,20. Nevertheless, management decisions should not rely solely on economic effectiveness, and future pest management strategies should emphasize environmentally sustainable approaches integrating host plant resistance, cultural practices, biological regulation, and need-based insecticide application16.

The present study provides baseline information on the ecology, host response, and management of H. lioderes under north-western Himalayan conditions. However, the study was conducted during a single cropping season and under a limited range of agroecological conditions. Therefore, broader conclusions regarding long-term pest dynamics and management efficacy should be interpreted cautiously. Further multi-season and multi-location studies incorporating detailed ecological, behavioral, and economic analyses would strengthen understanding of this emerging rice pest and support development of sustainable management strategies.

Conclusion

The study demonstrated that Heteronychus lioderes remained active throughout the rice-growing season under north-western Himalayan conditions, with peak infestation observed during August under humid monsoon conditions. Screening of rice germplasm revealed only limited sources of moderate resistance against the pest. Among the evaluated management treatments, Chlorpyrifos 20 EC and Carbofuran 3G provided comparatively better suppression of infestation and higher grain yield under field conditions. The findings provide useful baseline information for developing integrated management strategies against this emerging rice pest in rice ecosystems of the region.
