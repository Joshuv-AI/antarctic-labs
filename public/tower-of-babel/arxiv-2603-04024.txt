Volumetric Directional Diffusion: Anchoring Uncertainty Quantification in Anatomical Consensus for Ambiguous Medical Image Segmentation
Chao Wu, Kangxian Xie, Mingchen Gao

Abstract
Equivocal 3D lesion segmentation exhibits high inter-observer variability. Conventional deterministic models ignore this aleatoric uncertainty, producing over-confident masks that obscure clinical risks. Conversely, while generative methods (e.g., standard diffusion) capture sample diversity, recovering complex topology from pure noise frequently leads to severe structural fractures and out-of-distribution anatomical hallucinations. To resolve this fidelity-diversity trade-off, we propose Volumetric Directional Diffusion (VDD). Unlike standard diffusion models that denoise isotropic Gaussian noise, VDD mathematically anchors the generative trajectory to a deterministic consensus prior. By restricting the generative search space to iteratively predict a 3D boundary residual field, VDD accurately explores the fine-grained geometric variations inherent in expert disagreements without risking topological collapse. Extensive validation on three multi-rater datasets (LIDC-IDRI, KiTS21, and ISBI 2015) demonstrates that VDD achieves state-of-the-art uncertainty quantification (significantly improving GED and CI) while remaining highly competitive in segmentation accuracy against deterministic upper bounds. Ultimately, VDD provides clinicians with anatomically coherent uncertainty maps, enabling safer decision-making and mitigating risks in downstream tasks (e.g., radiotherapy planning or surgical margin assessment).

Introduction

Accurate boundary delineation in volumetric medical imaging is notoriously challenging. For equivocal structures (e.g., ground-glass opacities, infiltrative boundaries), pathology admits a distribution of clinically plausible contours, exhibiting high inter-observer variability. Capturing aleatoric uncertainty is critical for downstream tasks like radiotherapy planning and surgical margin assessment
[
19
]
, where misestimation carries severe risks.
However, state-of-the-art deterministic pipelines (e.g., nnU-Net
[
12
]
) inherently collapse this spatial uncertainty into a single, over-confident prediction. To model inter-reader variability, generative models were introduced, pioneering with VAE-based approaches such as the Probabilistic U-Net
[
14
]
. Recently, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)
[
10
]
have gained significant traction as general medical segmentation solvers
[
24
,
15
]
. By formulating segmentation as a conditional generation task, diffusion models can produce a diverse set of segmentation masks by sampling from a learned distribution
[
1
,
21
,
23
,
13
]
. This stochastic capability has been successfully exploited to model aleatoric uncertainty from divergent annotations
[
18
,
25
]
and to establish multi-expert consensus
[
26
]
. Diffusion processes have even demonstrated potential in refining noisy or incomplete 3D clinical labels
[
7
,
27
]
.

However, applying standard diffusion models to 3D medical image segmentation reveals a critical bottleneck: the inherent degradation of anatomical fidelity. Standard diffusion models recover data from isotropic, independently distributed Gaussian noise (
x_{T}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
). In high-dimensional 3D voxel space, recovering complex volumetric topology from pure noise is an extremely unconstrained inverse problem
[
8
,
22
]
. Consequently, the sampling trajectory is highly susceptible to local mode mismatch across slices, converging to an expansive contour on one slice while collapsing to a conservative contour on the adjacent slice. This phenomenon generates fragmented structures that break the vital slice-to-slice volume consistency
[
6
,
5
]
, and can even induce machine-generated “hallucinations”
[
20
]
. Generating a complete organ from pure noise is an over-complicated and ill-posed formulation when the sole objective is to capture fine-grained boundary uncertainty.

To resolve this trade-off, we propose
Volumetric Directional Diffusion (VDD)
shifting the generative paradigm from
ab initio
generation to “residual exploration”[cite: 30]. Our main contributions are threefold:

•

First
, to the best of our knowledge, VDD is the first work to investigate aleatoric uncertainty quantification in 3D volumes using diffusion models for ambiguous medical image segmentation.

•

Second
, we introduce
Anatomical Anchoring
, which mathematically reformulates the diffusion trajectory
[
11
]
to incorporate a deterministic structural prior. By restricting the generative search space to
residual exploration
, VDD mitigates topological fractures and explicitly enforces slice-to-slice volumetric consistency.

•

Third
, extensive experiments on three multi-rater datasets (LIDC-IDRI, KiTS21, ISBI 2015) demonstrate that VDD acts as a plug-and-play refiner, achieving state-of-the-art uncertainty metrics (GED and CI) while preserving macroscopic structural accuracy.

Methodology

2.1
Preliminaries and Forward Process of VDD

Standard DDPMs progressively add Gaussian noise to the ground truth label
y_{0}
via a Markov chain:

q(y_{t}\mid y_{0})=\mathcal{N}\!\left(y_{t};\ \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}^{\text{std}}}\,y_{0},\ \left(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}^{\text{std}}\right)\mathbf{I}\right)

(1)

As
t\to T
, the generative space ultimately collapses into isotropic pure noise
\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})
. To explicitly accommodate basic anatomical structures without relying on perfect initial predictions, we propose a directional diffusion process. We define
\hat{y}
as a straightforward, coarse anatomical prior generated by a standard baseline network (e.g., nnU-Net). It is important to note that
\hat{y}
is not a perfect ground truth; while it provides a reasonable macroscopic location, it inherently contains boundary errors and over-confident delineations in equivocal regions.

2.2
Preliminaries and Forward Process of VDD

Standard DDPMs
[
10
]
progressively add Gaussian noise to the ground truth label
y_{0}
via a Markov chain:

q(y_{t}\mid y_{0})=\mathcal{N}\!\left(y_{t};\ \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}^{\text{std}}}\,y_{0},\ \left(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}^{\text{std}}\right)\mathbf{I}\right)

(2)

As
t\to T
, the generative space ultimately collapses into isotropic pure noise
\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})
. To explicitly accommodate basic anatomical structures without relying on perfect initial predictions, we propose a directional diffusion process. We define
\hat{y}
as a straightforward, coarse anatomical prior generated by a standard baseline network (e.g., nnU-Net). It is important to note that
\hat{y}
is not a perfect ground truth; while it provides a reasonable macroscopic location, it inherently contains boundary errors and over-confident delineations in equivocal regions.

We decouple the process to guide the initial segmentation
y_{0}\in[-1,1]^{D\times H\times W}
towards this coarse anatomical direction
\hat{y}
, rather than unstructured noise. The single-step transition is defined as:

y_{t}=\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}y_{t-1}+(1-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}})\hat{y}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\epsilon_{t},\quad\epsilon_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})

(3)

By accumulating the recurrence over the Markov chain (where
\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\alpha_{i}
and
\bar{\beta}_{t}=\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}
), the marginal distribution
q(y_{t}|y_{0},\hat{y})
can be formulated as a linear combination:

y_{t}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}y_{0}+(1-\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}})\hat{y}+\bar{\beta}_{t}\epsilon

(4)

Proof of Expected Convergence:
To demonstrate that this process macroscopically anchors to the heuristic topology at the terminal timestep
T
, we take the conditional expectation of the marginal distribution:

\mathbb{E}_{q(y_{t}|y_{0},\hat{y})}[y_{t}]=\sqrt{\overline{\alpha}_{t}}y_{0}+(1-\sqrt{\overline{\alpha}_{t}})\hat{y}

(5)

Since
\mathbb{E}[\epsilon]=0
, by strictly constraining the cumulative schedule to satisfy
\bar{\alpha}_{T}=0
, it yields:

\lim_{t\to T}\mathbb{E}[y_{t}]=\hat{y}

(6)

This ensures that the generative space of VDD is not a disordered high-dimensional vacuum, but a structured neighborhood roughly bounded by the baseline prior
\hat{y}
. This provides a safe search space where the diffusion model can interpretably diverge from the coarse prior to recover fine-grained details.

\epsilon_{\theta}

\hat{y}_{0}^{\theta}

2.3
Reverse Trajectory and
y_{0}
-Parameterization

The reverse generative process aims to recover
y_{0}
from the noisy state
y_{t}
. We use a network
\epsilon_{\theta}
to predict the noise and directly parameterize the predicted boundary
\hat{y}_{0}^{\theta}
by inverting the marginal distribution:

\hat{y}_{0}^{\theta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}\left(y_{t}-(1-\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}})\hat{y}-\bar{\beta}_{t}\epsilon_{\theta}(y_{t},x,\hat{y},t)\right)

(7)

Consequently, the reverse sampling step is updated to:

y_{t-1}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\hat{y}_{0}^{\theta}+(1-\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})\hat{y}+\sigma_{t}\hat{\epsilon}_{\theta}

(8)

The coarse prior
\hat{y}
acts as a continuous spatial bias in every denoising iteration. It prevents the generation from deviating from the macroscopic anatomical skeleton, allowing the network to focus exclusively on refining microscopic boundary ambiguities.

3.2
Results

Balancing Anatomical Fidelity and Generative Diversity

As shown in Table
1
, while deterministic baselines (e.g., nnU-Net 3D) achieve high structural precision (e.g., 0.7671 Dice on LIDC-IDRI ), they fail to capture inter-reader variability. Conversely, slice-wise 2D diffusion models exhibit catastrophic topological degradation, with HD95 scores soaring to 18.05 on LIDC-IDRI and 21.34 on KiTS21. By anchoring the generative trajectory to a 3D structural prior, VDD successfully resolves this trade-off, restoring consistency and slashing HD95 to 1.36 on LIDC-IDRI. Remarkably, VDD remains highly competitive in Dice accuracy and even outperforms nnU-Net 3D on ISBI 2015 (0.6989 vs. 0.6914), proving that residual exploration can refine macroscopic consensus.

VDD consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance across all uncertainty metrics (GED, CI, and SNCC) on the three multi-rater datasets. Compared to the VAE-based Probabilistic U-Net. VDD significantly reduces Generalized Energy Distance (GED), indicating that our residual exploration mechanism more accurately maps the probabilistic space to the true clinical distribution. Superior Collective Insight (CI) scores further emphasize that VDD captures clinically meaningful variations rather than random noise. While VDD exhibits slightly higher HD95 on infiltrative datasets (e.g., KiTS21), this reflects the model’s intentional exploration of ambiguous boundaries to encompass the full spectrum of plausible expert annotations—a critical requirement for downstream risk assessment.

Figure 3 confirms the fidelity-diversity trade-off across Axial, Coronal, and Sagittal planes. While Prob U-Net achieves competitive accuracy, its latent space suffers from mode collapse; the generated masks remain largely static, leading to under-calibrated uncertainty that fails to capture true inter-rater ambiguity. By anchoring the generative trajectory to the structural prior, VDD successfully preserves macroscopic topology while effectively allocating its capacity to explore uncertain boundary residuals. Conclusion

We proposed Volumetric Directional Diffusion (VDD) to resolve the trade-off between uncertainty quantification and topological degradation in ambiguous 3D medical segmentation. By introducing
Anatomical Anchoring
, VDD grounds the diffusion trajectory to a deterministic prior, restricting generation to boundary “residual exploration.” This mathematically mitigates structural fractures and explicitly enforces slice-to-slice consistency. Evaluations on three multi-rater datasets demonstrate that VDD significantly outperforms baselines in both anatomical fidelity and distribution alignment, providing clinicians with anatomically coherent uncertainty maps for safer downstream decision-making.
