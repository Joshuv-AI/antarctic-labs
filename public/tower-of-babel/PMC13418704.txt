Passive surveillance of adverse events following immunization for National Immunization Program vaccines in Shenyang, China, 2009–2024
Authors: Nina Jia, Wei Zhong, Dongmei Gao, Lijun Zhu, Xuanyi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBased on the Chinese National Adverse Events Following Immunization Surveillance Information System (CNAEFIS), a descriptive epidemiological analysis was conducted on Adverse Events Following Immunization (AEFI) of National Immunization Program (NIP) vaccines in Shenyang from 2009 to 2024, to understand the distribution characteristics and reporting status of AEFI using passive surveillance data in the region. Reaction types were classified according to national unified surveillance standards. Descriptive epidemiological analysis was carried out from temporal, regional, demographic and vaccine-specific distributions, and AEFI reporting rate was calculated. From 2009 to 2024, a total of 3,420 cases of AEFI associated with NIP vaccines were reported, with an annual average reporting rate of 15.8 per 100,000 doses. Among these, the reporting rate of common vaccine reactions was 14.87 per 100,000, and that of rare vaccine reactions was 0.54 per 100,000. Differences in reporting rates between districts were statistically significant (χ2 = 618.9, p < .05). Most reported events were common reactions, and rare reactions were infrequently reported. There were obvious regional disparities in AEFI surveillance performance. Surveillance training and management should be further strengthened in low-reporting districts to reduce regional gaps and improve overall reporting performance.KEYWORDS: Immunization, adverse events following immunization, vaccine, surveillance

Vaccination is an economical and effective measure for preventing infectious diseases. Over the past four decades, the national immunization program has achieved remarkable results in controlling vaccine-preventable diseases.1 As the incidence of vaccine-preventable diseases has declined, public concern regarding adverse events following immunization has grown in recent years.2 On December 1, 2019, China implemented the Vaccine Administration Law of the People’s Republic of China, mandating strengthened surveillance of Adverse Events Following Immunization(AEFI). Surveillance for suspected adverse events following immunization enables monitoring of vaccination safety and helps maintain public confidence in immunization programs.3

Shenyang, a major central city in Northeast China and the capital of Liaoning Province, has a population of approximately 9 million. With 50,000 newborns each year, the city administers an average of 1.35 million doses of National Immunization Program (NIP) vaccines. Shenyang initiated its Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) in 1978, initially covering only four vaccines. By the end of 2007, the program had been expanded to include all 11 vaccines in the NIP, protecting against 12 vaccine-preventable diseases (VPDs). Since 2009, Shenyang has formally participated in the Chinese National Adverse Events Following Immunization Information System (CNAEFIS)4 and has reported AEFI through this system. Although routine AEFI surveillance has been conducted in Shenyang for many years, most local studies have been limited to short-term summaries or cross-sectional analyses, lacking systematic epidemiological assessment based on long-term consecutive surveillance data. Existing national or regional reports rarely provide continuous observation over such an extended period, making it difficult to reflect stable long-term trends and regional characteristics of AEFI. This study systematically analyzed the distribution characteristics of AEFI cases reported after NIP vaccination in Shenyang from 2009 to 2024, filling the local research gap in long-term continuous AEFI monitoring and providing a reference for similar surveillance analyses in other regions.

Data and methods

Data sources

Data on AEFI cases following the National Immunization Program vaccinations from January 2009 to December 2024 were collected from the CNAEFIS. CNAEFIS is the primary official surveillance system in China developed in 2007 to detect new, unusual or rare AEFIs, assess the safety of licensed vaccines and identify potential risk factors for AEFIs.5

Inclusion Criteria: ① The reported vaccine was the NIP vaccine; ② The reported vaccination date was between January 1, 2009, and December 31, 2024; ③ The case report had been confirmed and classified through expert diagnosis.

Exclusion Criteria: ① Duplicate reports were identified and removed based on key information such as name, national identification number, vaccination date, and vaccine type; ② Cases with missing key variables, logical inconsistencies, or unverifiable information were excluded.

All case reports underwent tiered review by disease control centers at all levels, ensuring data authenticity and validity. Reported information included name, gender, age, vaccine type, dose number, vaccine lot number, manufacturer, vaccination date, occurrence of adverse reactions, and clinical outcomes.

Denominator data (number of administered vaccine doses) for calculating AEFI reporting rates were obtained from annual regional vaccination summaries. These data were stratified by year and vaccine type for subgroup analyses.

Surveillance of vaccines

From 2009 to 2024, AEFI surveillance was conducted for 14 vaccines included in the immunization program in Shenyang City. These include: Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG), hepatitis B vaccine (HepB), oral live attenuated polio vaccine (OPV), inactivated polio vaccine (IPV), bivalent oral live attenuated polio vaccine (bOPV), diphtheria, tetanus and acellular pertussis combined vaccine (DTaP), diphtheria – tetanus combined vaccine (DT), measles live attenuated vaccine (MV), measles-rubella combined live attenuated vaccine (MR), measles-mumps-rubella combined live attenuated vaccine (MMR), hepatitis A live attenuated vaccine (HepA-l), meningococcal polysaccharide vaccine type A (MPV-A), meningococcal polysaccharide vaccine types A and C (MPV-AC), and Japanese encephalitis attenuated live vaccine (JEV-L). Among these, OPV, MV and MR were vaccines used during the vaccine transition process and are currently discontinued.

Classification of AEFI

In June 2010, China’s Ministry of Health and the State Food and Drug Administration jointly issued the National AEFI Guidelines.6 According to these guidelines, AEFI (including fatal cases) is defined as reactions or events suspected to be related to vaccination and is categorized as: (1) vaccine reaction or vaccine-associated reaction (including common vaccine reactions and rare vaccine reactions); (2) vaccine quality incidents; (3) vaccination accidents; (4) coincidental events, and (5) psychogenic reactions. Common vaccine reactions refer to transient physiological dysfunction. Rare reactions refer to reactions that cause damage to body tissues, organs or functions. Coincidental events refer to the coincidental onset of a disease after vaccination when the recipient was already in its latent or prodromal stage.

According to severity, AEFI needs to be categorized into non-serious AEFI and serious AEFI. Non-serious AEFI refers to cases that generally do not require hospitalization. Serious AEFI refers to an adverse event following immunization that results in death, is life-threatening, causes permanent or significant disability, or leads to organ dysfunction.

Expert panels consisting of multidisciplinary experts in epidemiology, clinical medicine, pharmacy, and other related fields were established at provincial, municipal, and district-level centers for disease control and prevention (CDC). These panels were responsible for the investigation, diagnosis, and classification of AEFI. Most cases were diagnosed and classified by district-level expert panels. For cases involving suspected vaccination-associated death, severe disability, clustered AEFI, or AEFI with significant social impact, the diagnosis and classification were performed by municipal or provincial-level expert panels.

Data processing

Data cleaning and verification were performed using Excel. Descriptive statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 22.0, and chi-square test was applied for hypothesis testing. The significance level α was set at .05, and p-value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

In this study, the annual percent change (APC) was defined as the average annual change rate obtained by fitting the trend using a log-linear regression model. A regression equation was established with year as the independent variable and the annual AEFI reporting rate as the dependent variable. The APC value was calculated from the regression coefficient to quantify the long-term temporal trend of AEFI reporting rates from 2009 to 2024 and to directly reflect the dynamic evolution of adverse reaction levels over the years. The formula for APC is as follows: APC = 100 × (eβ1 −1) where β1 is the regression coefficient for year. p < .05 was considered statistically significant for the trend. Meanwhile, the trend χ2 test was used to analyze the linear trend of AEFI reporting rates over the years. To evaluate the robustness of temporal trend estimates and reduce potential bias from outlier years, a sensitivity analysis was conducted by excluding 2009 (the initial year of surveillance implementation) and 2021 (a year affected by the COVID-19 pandemic). The annual percent change (APC) was recalculated.using the same log-linear trend model with chi-square test for the restricted study period.

Results

Basic information on AEFI reports

From 2009 to 2024, a total of 21,645,609 doses of vaccines under the national immunization program were administered in Shenyang City. A total of 3,420 adverse events following immunization (AEFI) were reported. Among these, vaccine reactions accounted for 3,334 cases (97.49%), including 3,218 common vaccine reactions and 116 rare vaccine reactions. Coincidental events constituted 86 cases (2.51%). No psychogenic reactions, vaccine quality incidents, or vaccination accidents were reported. The male-to-female case ratio was 1.25:1. Age distribution was as follows: 0–1 y (41.18%), 1–4 y (47.27%), 5–9 y (11.06%), and 10 y and above (0.49%). AEFI cases were reported in every month from 2009 to 2024, with relatively higher numbers observed from May to July.

The average annual reporting rate of AEFI reports for NIP vaccines was 15.8 per 100,000, comprising 14.87 per 100,000 for common vaccine reactions, 0.54 per 100,000 for rare vaccine reactions, and 0.40 per 100,000 for coincidental events. The overall AEFI APC from 2009 to 2024 was 19.87% (χ2 = 491.8, p < .05), with the reporting rate increasing from 1.70 per 100,000 in 2009 to 24.69 per 100,000 in 2024. The rate showed an increasing trend with fluctuations from 2009 to 2014 and remained volatile thereafter, with a notable decline in 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The reporting rate of common vaccine reactions exhibited an increasing trend, while the rates for rare vaccine reactions and coincidental events showed no significant change. See Table 1.

Regional distribution

There are 13 districts in Shenyang City, with 100% coverage of AEFI surveillance districts from 2009 to 2024. The regions with the highest overall AEFI reporting rates were observed for Liaozhong (28.01 per 100,000) and Dadong (26.89 per 100,000). These were followed by Heping (22.76 per 100,000), Sujiatun (21.44 per 100,000), Faku (20.85 per 100,000), Huanggu (19.99 per 100,000) and Xinmin (17.92 per 100,000). The lowest AEFI reporting rates were observed for Shenbei (6.08 per 100,000) and Yuhong (6.34 per 100,000). The differences in AEFI reporting rates among regions were statistically significant (χ2 = 618.9, p < .001). See Table 2.

Interval between vaccination and onset and case outcomes

Among 3,420 AEFI cases, 2,433 (71.14%) occurred within 1 d post-vaccination; 458 (13.39%) occurred 2–3 d post-vaccination; 309 (9.04%) occurred 4–7 d post-vaccination; 162 (4.74%) occurred 8–14 d post-vaccination; and 58 cases (1.69%) occurred more than 15 d post-vaccination (see Table 2). Among these, 603 cases (17.63%) recovered, 2,565 cases (75.00%) improved, 6 (0.18%) worsened, 107 (3.13%) had unknown outcomes, 126 (3.68%) required treatment, 3 (0.09%) developed sequelae, and 10 cases (0.29%) resulted in death. Among the 10 deaths, 3 were attributed to rare vaccine reactions, while the remaining 7 were considered coincidental events.

Discussion

Since AEFI surveillance was initiated in 2009, the reporting rate in Shenyang showed an overall increasing trend with some fluctuations until 2014 and fluctuated notably thereafter, while the long-term trend remained generally upward. A temporary decline in the 2021 AEFI reporting rate was associated with the COVID-19 pandemic. Routine vaccination services were reduced, and the redeployment of primary health staff affected AEFI reporting. This was a temporary fluctuation that did not change the overall upward trend. Sensitivity analysis excluding the years 2009 and 2021 yielded consistent findings, confirming the robustness of the observed temporal trend. The overall AEFI reporting rate for NIP vaccines in Shenyang from 2009 to 2024 was 15.8 cases per 100,000 doses, which was lower than the 2017 national average of 38.94 per 100,000 doses,7 but slightly higher than the rate of 12.75 per 100,000 doses reported in Fengtai District, Beijing, from 2010 to 2020.8 This rate is comparable to Australia’s 2017 rate of 15.8 per 100,000 population,9 and slightly higher than Italy’s 2017 rate of 11.1 per 100,000 population and Canada’s 2017 rate of 12.6 per 100,000 doses (equivalent to 8.1 per 100,000 population).10,11 However, international variations in AEFI reporting rates should be interpreted with great caution, as rates based on administered doses and those based on population are not directly comparable. Differences in surveillance systems, reporting policies, and case definitions across countries may also contribute to inconsistencies. These international comparisons are presented for illustrative purposes only and do not imply direct statistical equivalence.

The serious AEFI reporting rate for Shenyang’s NIP vaccines from 2009 to 2024 was 0.26 per 100,000 doses, approaching the Chinese national serious AEFI reporting rate of 0.27 per 100,000 doses in 2019.12 This rate was lower than Chile’s 2014–2016 rate (1.22 per 100,000 doses) and the 2017 rate in Tuscany, Italy (1–20 per 100,000 doses).13,14 From 2009 to 2024, Shenyang reported 10 fatal AEFI cases following NIP vaccine administration, accounting for 0.29% of all cases. This proportion was slightly higher than the Chinese national average of 0.07% in 2016.15 This finding may reflect more complete ascertainment of severe or fatal cases, whereas milder non-serious AEFIs may be underreported, thereby influencing the proportion of fatal cases among all reported events. Importantly, most of these deaths were determined to be coincidental to vaccination, not caused by the vaccines themselves.

The top five districts in total AEFI reports were Dadong, Huanggu, Hunnan, Heping, and Tiexi, accounting for 57.7% of the total. This may be related to higher vaccination volumes in these jurisdictions. The AEFI reporting rates across Shenyang’s 13 districts ranged from 6.08 to 26.89 per 100,000 doses, indicating significant variation. This indicates considerable heterogeneity in AEFI reporting rates across districts, which may reflect uneven implementation of AEFI surveillance across regions. The uneven development of citywide AEFI surveillance primarily stems from varying levels of awareness and emphasis among relevant personnel, inconsistent monitoring intensity, and insufficient staffing in some districts. Children aged ≤ 1 y accounted for the highest proportion of reported cases (41.18%), consistent with the age distribution of the NIP. This is attributed to the higher number of vaccine doses administered to this age group. Cases were concentrated between May and July. One possible explanation is that warmer temperatures may result in lighter clothing among children, making local reactions at the injection site more likely to be noticed by parents.

Our data showed that the vaccines with the highest overall AEFI reporting rates during 2009–2024 were MR, DTaP, DT, MMR, and MV. For rare vaccine reactions, the top five vaccines were MR, BCG, MMR, MV, and HepA-l. Allergic reactions were the most common type of rare adverse reactions, with allergic rashes being the most frequent manifestation. Anaphylactic shock was extremely rare, and the highest rate was associated with MV at 0.26 per 100,000 doses, which was lower than the WHO range of 3.5–10 cases per million doses.16 Allergic events may be triggered by vaccine antigens, residual animal proteins, antibiotics, preservatives, or stabilizers.17

The next most frequently reported rare vaccine reactions were thrombocytopenic purpura(TP) and BCG lymphadenitis. The onset interval of thrombocytopenic purpura (TP) was relatively long. To date, evidence linking measles-containing vaccines to TP has been established. Caution is warranted when inferring causal relationships between other vaccines and TP. This study indicates a reporting rate of TP following MR vaccination of 0.41 per 100,000 doses. This rate is higher than the national reporting rate of TP after MR vaccination in 2018 (0.16 per 100,000 doses) but remains below WHO estimates for thrombocytopenia following MR vaccination (1 per 30,000 to 1 per 40,000 doses).16 The immunological mechanism hypothesis for MR-associated TP suggests an association with anti-measles and anti-rubella IgG antibodies.18 Although studies indicate an increased risk of TP onset following MMR vaccination,19,20 the risk of vaccine-induced TP remains lower than that of wild-virus infection.21 Moreover, most TP cases exhibit mild clinical manifestations and have a favorable prognosis.

BCG is one of the vaccines with a relatively high incidence of vaccine reactions; lymphadenitis and local abscesses are the most common AEFIs caused by BCG. In our study, the reported rate of BCG lymphadenitis was 0.05 per 100,000 doses, substantially lower than the international reference range of 1–10 cases per 10,000 doses.22 This discrepancy not only suggests high-quality vaccine administration practices in Shenyang but may also be partly attributed to differences in surveillance systems and reporting thresholds across regions. The occurrence of local abscesses can be influenced by multiple factors, including vaccine dosage, injection depth and site, recipient immune status, and vaccine dilution. Since freeze-dried BCG is a multi-dose vaccine, it must be thoroughly shaken and administered at the standard dose; improved administration quality can effectively reduce the incidence of local adverse reactions to BCG. This report describes three fatal cases of rare vaccine reactions, all of which were associated with BCG vaccination. All three pediatric patients had underlying immunodeficiency. One case was confirmed as primary immunodeficiency (PID) due to an IL‑12β1 receptor defect, while the other two cases presented with axillary lymphadenitis complicated by severe systemic multi-organ infection. No autopsies were performed on any of the three cases. All three cases were reviewed and evaluated by provincial and municipal expert panels. The reporting rate of disseminated BCG infection in our study was 0.1 cases per 100,000 doses administered (1 case per million doses), which is close to the range of 1.56–4.29 cases per million doses reported in the general population and cited in previous studies.23 This disease primarily affects immunocompromised children in developing countries. Although its incidence is low, it has severe outcomes and a high mortality rate. China is one of the countries with a high tuberculosis burden, and BCG remains the only available vaccine against tuberculosis. Targeted screening for PID in high-risk infants (e.g., those with a family history of PID or recurrent infections) and avoiding BCG vaccination in immunocompromised individuals may help prevent severe or fatal BCG-related adverse events.23

Several rare reactions were observed, such as 5 cases of anaphylactic shock, 4 cases of sterile abscess, and 3 cases of febrile convulsions. The reporting rates of anaphylactic shock and sterile abscess were both 0.02 per 100,000 doses, consistent with the 2018 national level. The rate of febrile convulsions was 0.01 per 100,000 doses, slightly lower than the national rate of 0.05 per 100,000 doses in 2018. Anaphylactic shock is a rapid-onset, life-threatening severe adverse reaction that requires prompt clinical recognition and management. China implements a 30-minute on-site observation period after vaccination and requires all vaccination sites to have emergency personnel and equipment available. Vaccination sites must enhance emergency response capabilities to promptly treat suspected cases and minimize severe outcomes. Sterile abscesses primarily result from vaccines containing DTaP components, related to the characteristics of the vaccine. The risk of febrile seizures correlates with age, with viral infections and genetics being common risk factors. However, febrile seizures do not adversely affect children’s subsequent developmental progress.24,25

In summary, this 16 y surveillance study indicates that the epidemiological characteristics of reported AEFIs following NIP vaccination in Shenyang were generally consistent with national and other provincial reports in China. Reported AEFIs were predominantly common vaccine reactions, while the proportion of rare vaccine reactions was quite low. However, surveillance sensitivity remained relatively low, with significant regional variations observed, suggesting room for improvement in local surveillance capacity. Targeted training, standardized reporting, and focused supervision in underperforming areas will help further enhance the sensitivity and quality of AEFI surveillance. Maintaining continuous and systematic AEFI monitoring is essential for ensuring vaccine safety, enabling early detection of adverse events, and sustaining public confidence in the national immunization program.
