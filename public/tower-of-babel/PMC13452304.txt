Efficacy and safety of trastuzumab deruxtecan in patients with HER2-expressing biliary tract or pancreatic tumors: a subgroup analysis of DESTINY-PanTumor02
Authors: D-Y Oh, I Ługowska, D Stroyakovskiy, KH Jung, O Dumas, K Penkov, A Dechaphunkul, A Oaknin, ST Kim, N Starling, B Chewaskulyong, C Charonpongsuntorn, D Doroshow, S-Y Hsiao, Y-P Hung, L Jung, N Kuptsova-Clarkson, F Michelini, S Puvvada, F Meric-Bernstam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn DESTINY-PanTumor02 Part 1, trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) demonstrated a clinically meaningful benefit in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-expressing tumors. In this article, we report subgroup analyses in the biliary tract cancer (BTC) and pancreatic cancer (PC) cohorts from DESTINY-PanTumor02 Part 1.Patients and methodsDESTINY-PanTumor02 (NCT04482309) Part 1 evaluated T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg in locally advanced/metastatic HER2 immunohistochemistry (IHC) 3+/2+ (local/central testing) tumors that had progressed after systemic treatment or without treatment options. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR) by investigator assessment (INV). Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival, overall survival, and safety. Exploratory endpoints included outcomes by biomarker status and treatment history.ResultsOverall, 41 patients with BTC and 25 patients with PC had received T-DXd; median (range) follow-up duration was 6.01 (0.7-29.1) months and 4.99 (1.1-27.2) months, respectively. By INV, ORR was 22.0% [95% confidence interval (CI) 10.6%-37.6%] in the BTC cohort and 4.0% (95% CI 0.1%-20.4%) in the PC cohort; by independent central review, ORR was 26.8% (95% CI 14.2%-42.9%) and 12.0% (95% CI 2.5%-31.2%), respectively. Responses were seen across most patient subgroups in the BTC cohort; the highest ORR (56.3%, 95% CI 29.9%-80.2%) by INV was observed for those with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors (central test). Best objective response (by INV) of stable disease was observed in 61.0% of the BTC cohort and 68.0% of the PC cohort; median overall survival was 7.0 (95% CI 4.6-10.2) and 5.0 (95% CI 3.8-14.2) months, respectively. Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis occurred in 17.1% of patients with BTC and 4.0% of patients with PC.ConclusionsThese data further support T-DXd as a recommended treatment in pretreated HER2 IHC 3+ tumors. Interpretation of PC cohort findings was limited by sample size and potentially confounded by the low number of patients with IHC 3+ PC. Safety was consistent with the known profile.Key words: advanced, biliary tract cancer, pancreatic cancer, T-DXd, trastuzumab deruxtecan

Introduction

Patients with biliary tract cancer (BTC) or pancreatic cancer (PC) have poor prognoses. The estimated 5-year survival rate at diagnosis, across all stages of disease, is ≤11% for BTC and 13% for PC, with rates as low as 2% and 3% in patients diagnosed at metastatic stages, respectively.1,2 Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a transmembrane tyrosine kinase receptor that is overexpressed in a range of solid tumors and has been linked with worse survival outcomes in multiple tumor types.3 Tumors with HER2 immunohistochemistry (IHC) 3+ expression have been reported in 1% to 19% of patients with BTC and 1% to 7% of patients with PC.3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 Given the current prognoses associated with BTC and PC,1,2 HER2-directed therapy provides an opportunity to improve the current standard of care and patient outcomes.

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) is an antibody-drug conjugate composed of a humanized immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody specifically targeting HER2, a tetrapeptide-based cleavable linker, and a potent topoisomerase I (TOP1) inhibitor payload.9,10 T-DXd is approved in multiple countries for the treatment of HER2-positive, HER2-low, and HER2-ultralow breast cancer, HER2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma, and HER2-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer.11,12 T-DXd is also approved in multiple countries, including the United States and the United Kingdom, for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive (IHC 3+) solid tumors that have progressed after prior treatment and/or who have no satisfactory alternative therapy.12, 13, 14 This approval was based, in part, on results from Part 1 of the phase II DESTINY-PanTumor02 study, which demonstrated durable clinical benefit and meaningful survival outcomes in pretreated patients with HER2-expressing tumors who were receiving T-DXd.15 This analysis further explores the efficacy and safety of T-DXd in the BTC and PC cohorts from DESTINY-PanTumor02 Part 1.

Patients and methods

Study design

DESTINY-PanTumor02 is a two-part, open-label, multicenter, phase II study evaluating the efficacy and safety of T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg in HER2-expressing, locally advanced, or metastatic solid tumors after ≥1 systemic treatment or without alternative treatments. The study design of Part 1 has been published previously.15 This study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov as NCT04482309 and is active.

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board at each site and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines defined by the International Conference on Harmonisation, and local regulations on the conduct of clinical research. All patients provided written informed consent.

Patients

In Part 1, eligible patients were aged ≥18 years with locally advanced, unresectable, or metastatic biliary tract, bladder, cervical, endometrial, ovarian, pancreatic, or other cancer (excluding breast cancer, non-small-cell lung cancer, gastric cancer, and colorectal cancer) that had progressed after prior systemic treatment or without satisfactory alternative treatment options.15 HER2 expression for enrollment was based on local testing, where available, and otherwise determined by central HER2 testing (HercepTest; Dako Agilent, Glostrup, Denmark). Retrospective central HER2 testing was carried out for patients enrolled based on local HER2 test results. The protocol did not specify how sex or gender and race or ethnicity should be defined; these data were collected during patient visits or from patient records, as appropriate. Detailed inclusion/exclusion criteria are provided in the Supplementary Appendix, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108344.

Procedures

Patients received T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg i.v. once every 3 weeks. RECIST 1.1 scans were carried out at screening and every 6 weeks (±1 week) relative to the date of the first dose of T-DXd until documented disease progression, death by any cause, or withdrawal of consent. Treatment continued until documented disease progression per RECIST 1.1, withdrawal of consent, or when discontinuation criteria were met.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was investigator-assessed confirmed objective response rate (ORR) per RECIST 1.1. Secondary efficacy endpoints included investigator-assessed duration of response (DOR), disease control rate (DCR), and progression-free survival (PFS) per RECIST 1.1 and overall survival (OS). Best objective responses (BORs) were also reported; a BOR of stable disease required confirmation at ≥5 weeks. This threshold for determining stable disease was selected to correspond with the planned RECIST 1.1 scan interval (every 6 ± 1 weeks). An independent central review (ICR) per RECIST 1.1 was conducted to support the investigator-assessed results for primary and secondary outcomes. Secondary safety endpoints included the occurrence of adverse events (AEs), serious AEs, drug-related AEs, and AEs of special interest, including interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis.

Exploratory endpoints included subgroup analyses of efficacy endpoints according to biomarker status and treatment history. Subgroup analyses by HER2 status were determined by central test results, which included HER2 IHC expression by HercepTest (Dako Agilent) in a LabCorp laboratory, tissue [dual in situ hybridization (ISH); Ventana, Tuscon, AZ] HER2 amplification in a CellCarta laboratory, and plasma [circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA)] HER2 amplification using the GuardantOMNI assay (Guardant Health, Redwood City, CA). Biomarker analyses included programmed death-ligand 1 [PD-L1] immune cell prevalence, and Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog (KRAS) gene alterations. PD-L1 positivity (≥1% positive immune cells relative to the tumor area (IC ≥1%; tumor area positivity score) was evaluated using the Ventana PD-L1 (SP263) assay) in a CellCarta laboratory. KRAS alterations were evaluated in baseline ctDNA using the GuardantOMNI assay (Guardant Health); mutations were considered based on prior evidence on actionability.16

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses in Part 1 of DESTINY-PanTumor02 have been reported previously.15

Role of the funding source

The study was designed and led by AstraZeneca, who were involved in the management and analysis of the data. AstraZeneca and Daiichi Sankyo were involved in the conduct of the study; collection and interpretation of the data; preparation, review, or approval of the manuscript; and the decision to submit the manuscript for publication.

Results

Between 7 October 2020 and 7 July 2022,15 41 patients with BTC and 25 patients with PC were enrolled at 32 centers across 13 countries. According to the prespecified futility criterion, on 24 March 2022, the PC cohort was closed for further recruitment because no objective responses (ORs) were observed in the first 15 patients, by which time 25 patients had been enrolled.15 All assigned patients received ≥1 dose of T-DXd and were included in the full analysis set (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108344). At data cut-off (8 June 2023), two (4.9%) patients with BTC and no patients with PC continued to receive T-DXd, with objective disease progression (46.3% and 52.0%, respectively) the most common reason for treatment discontinuation. The median follow-up duration was 6.01 (range 0.7-29.1) and 4.99 (range 1.1-27.2) months in the BTC and PC cohorts, respectively.

Patient demographics and clinical characteristics

Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics of all patients and patients with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors in the BTC and PC cohorts are presented in Table 1 and Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108344, respectively. In the BTC cohort, 34 patients (82.9%) were enrolled based on HER2 expression determined by local testing and seven (17.1%) by central testing; according to enrollment test results, 22 (53.7%) and 19 (46.3%) patients had HER2 IHC 3+ and IHC 2+ BTC, respectively. Of those in the PC cohort, 15 (60.0%) patients were enrolled based on HER2 status determined by local IHC testing and 10 (40.0%) by central testing; five (20.0%) patients and 20 (80.0%) patients had HER2 IHC 3+ and IHC 2+ tumors according to enrollment test results, respectively. The percentage positive agreement of centrally determined HER2 test results (including for patients enrolled by local testing) with paired local test results was 66.7% (12 of 18) for IHC 3+ and 46.7% (7 of 15) for IHC 2+ biliary tract tumors, and 25.0% (1 of 4) for IHC 3+ and 72.7% (8 of 11) for IHC 2+ pancreatic tumors (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108344). By central testing, a similar proportion of patients in the BTC cohort had IHC 3+ (39.0%; n = 16) compared with IHC 2+ tumors (34.1%; n = 14). In contrast, few patients in the PC cohort had centrally determined IHC 3+ tumors (8.0%; n = 2), while the majority of patients had IHC 2+ tumors (76.0%; n = 19) by central testing. The proportion of patients with tissue or plasma HER2 amplification by central HER2 IHC status is presented in Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108344. Median number of prior treatment regimens was two (range 1-5) among patients with BTC and two (range 1-4) among patients with PC. Patient demographics and baseline characteristics of those with IHC 3+ tumors were generally similar to the full population in each cohort.

Efficacy

Efficacy outcomes by investigator assessment and ICR and HER2 status are presented in Tables 2 and 3, respectively. As reported previously, the primary endpoint of investigator-assessed ORR was 22.0% (9 of 41; 95% CI 10.6%-37.6%) for the BTC cohort;15 all patients who achieved an investigator-assessed OR had HER2 IHC 3+ tumors (central testing). The confirmed ORR by ICR among patients with BTC was 26.8% (11 of 41, 95% CI 14.2%-42.9%). Investigator-assessed ORRs according to HER2 IHC (at enrollment and centrally confirmed) and ISH status, plasma HER2 amplification, prior therapy, and biomarker status for each cohort are presented in Figure 1; median investigator-assessed PFS and median OS are presented in Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108344. In the BTC cohort, the highest investigator-assessed confirmed ORR across all evaluated subgroups was observed in patients with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors by central testing (56.3%; 9 of 16); investigator-assessed ORR was 40.9% (9 of 22) in patients with HER2 IHC 3+ BTC by enrollment HER2 test result. Higher investigator-assessed ORRs were observed in patients with HER2 ISH-positive than in those with ISH-negative BTC [42.1% (8 of 19) versus 0% (0 of 15)] and in patients with plasma HER2 amplification compared with those without amplification [42.9% (6 of 14) versus 11.5% (3 of 26)]. Duration of treatment and investigator-assessed BOR by central HER2 IHC testing for each patient with BTC are presented in Figure 2A. A BOR of stable disease (≥5 weeks) by investigator assessment was observed in 61.0% (25 of 41) of patients in the BTC cohort. Furthermore, investigator-assessed disease control was achieved by 82.9% (34 of 41; 95% CI 67.9%-92.8%) and 65.9% (27 of 41; 95% CI 49.4%-79.9%) of these patients at weeks 6 and 12, respectively. Among those with IHC 3+ and IHC 2+ BTC (by central testing), the investigator-assessed DCR at 12 weeks was 68.8% (11 of 16; 95% CI 41.3%-89.0%) and 71.4% (10 of 14; 95% CI 41.9%-91.6%), respectively.

In the PC cohort, an OR was observed in one patient (1 of 25; 4.0%, 95% CI 0.1%-20.4%) by investigator assessment and three patients (3 of 25; 12.0%, 95% CI 2.5%-31.2%) by ICR.15 The patient with an OR by both investigator and ICR assessments had a HER2 status of IHC 2+ by central testing, a BOR of partial response, and an investigator- and ICR-assessed DOR of 6 and 9 months, respectively. The two additional patients identified by ICR both had a HER2 status of IHC 2+ by central testing, a BOR of partial response, and an investigator-assessed DOR of 21 and 6 months, respectively. Duration of treatment and investigator-assessed BOR by central HER2 IHC test result for each patient with PC are presented in Figure 2B; most patients (76%) had IHC 2+ tumors by central testing. An investigator-assessed BOR of stable disease (≥5 weeks) was observed in 68.0% (17 of 25) of patients with PC. Additionally, investigator-assessed disease control was achieved by 72.0% (18 of 25; 95% CI 50.6%-87.9%) and 36.0% (9 of 25; 95% CI 18.0%-57.5%) of patients in this cohort at weeks 6 and 12, respectively. The DCR among patients with PC at 12 weeks was 50.0% (1 of 2; 95% CI 1.3%-98.7%) and 36.8% (7 of 19; 95% CI 16.3%-61.6%) in those with IHC 3+ and IHC 2+ tumors (by central testing), respectively.

Investigator-assessed ORRs for patients with no detectable KRAS alterations in baseline ctDNA in the BTC and PC cohorts were 25.0% (9 of 36) and 12.5% (1 of 8), respectively, whereas no ORs occurred in patients with detected KRAS alterations (BTC cohort: 0 of 4; PC cohort: 0 of 15). Median investigator-assessed PFS and median OS were also greater in those without KRAS alterations versus those with alterations. One patient with BTC and no patients with PC with KRAS alterations had IHC 3+ tumors by central testing (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108344). ORRs for the BTC and PC cohorts were 19.1% (4 of 21; 95% CI 5.4%-41.9%) and 0% (0 of 7) in the PD-L1 IC ≥1% subgroups, and 35.7% (5 of 14; 95% CI 12.8%-64.9%) and 6.7% (1 of 15; 95% CI 0.2%-32.0%) in the PD-L1 IC <1% subgroups, respectively.

Safety

Of the 41 patients with BTC and 25 patients with PC who received T-DXd, 33 (80.5%) and 15 (60.0%) had drug-related AEs, respectively.15 A summary of drug-related AEs by cohort is presented in Supplementary Table S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108344. Drug-related ILD/pneumonitis, as adjudicated by an independent committee, occurred in seven patients (17.1%) in the BTC cohort (grade 2: n = 5; grade 3: n = 1; grade 5: n = 1) and one patient (4.0%) in the PC cohort (grade 1). The adjudicated grade 5 ILD/pneumonitis case occurred in a patient with HER2 IHC 3+ (by central testing) BTC who had received one prior treatment regimen, T-DXd treatment for 2.9 months, and achieved an OR (partial response); the patient had lung metastases at baseline and received prednisolone 1 mg/kg once daily from day 1 of ILD/pneumonitis diagnosis. Per the reporting investigator, the cause of death was considered to be related to the disease under investigation.

Discussion

In Part 1 of DESTINY-PanTumor02, T-DXd demonstrated clinically meaningful benefit and durable responses in pretreated patients with HER2-expressing BTC, with the greatest clinical benefit observed in those with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors.15 Results from this exploratory analysis further support the use of T-DXd as a recommended treatment for pretreated patients with HER2 IHC 3+ advanced tumors. Furthermore, these data show the therapeutic potential of T-DXd across diverse patient populations with varied treatment histories; however, assessment of clinical benefit in PC was limited by the low number of patients enrolled.

Although first-line treatment with cisplatin–gemcitabine with or without immunotherapy (durvalumab or pembrolizumab) has improved survival for patients with advanced BTC, outcomes remain poor; the median OS reported in the phase III TOPAZ-1 study for patients with advanced BTC receiving first-line chemotherapy with durvalumab was 12.9 months (n = 341; median follow-up, 41.3 months), with a 2-year OS rate of 22.9%.17,18 Better clinical outcomes are also critical for patients with PC, considering this population has one of the lowest survival rates reported among common cancers.1 First-line treatment recommendations for advanced PC include gemcitabine with nab-paclitaxel, 5-fluorouracil–folinic acid–irinotecan–oxaliplatin (FOLFIRINOX), and most recently, 5-fluorouracil–folinic acid–nanoliposomal irinotecan–oxaliplatin (NALIRIFOX).19 In the phase III NAPOLI 3 study (median follow-up, 16.1 months), first-line NALIRIFOX demonstrated improved median OS (11.1 months; n = 383) compared with first-line gemcitabine with nab-paclitaxel (9.2 months; n = 387) in advanced PC.20 Results of the subgroup analyses presented in this article are pertinent given the poor clinical outcomes in late-stage BTC and PC and further support the recommendation of T-DXd in pretreated patients with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors.12, 13, 14

Consistent with the overall population of DESTINY-PanTumor02, the greatest responses in the BTC cohort were observed in patients with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors, regardless of whether HER2 status was identified by the test result used for study enrollment or by central testing.15,21 Moreover, T-DXd elicited durable ORs in these patients; a median DOR of 8.6 months was observed for those with centrally determined HER2 IHC 3+ BTC. Although no ORs were observed by investigator assessment in patients with IHC 2+/1+/0 BTC (central testing), the majority of these patients (82.9%) achieved an investigator-assessed BOR of stable disease (≥5 weeks), partial response, or complete response, and >55% of patients in each IHC category (excluding those with unknown IHC status) had investigator-assessed disease control at 12 weeks. These findings are important considering that only 27 of 81 (33.3%) patients with advanced BTC who received second-line chemotherapy in the ABC-06 study achieved disease control; 23 (28.4%) had a BOR of stable disease.22 Therefore, our results highlight the potential benefit of T-DXd in pretreated patients with IHC 2+ advanced tumors and without alternative treatment options, and a clinically meaningful benefit in those with IHC 3+ tumors, as reported previously.15 The ongoing, phase III DESTINY-BTC01 study (NCT06467357) is further investigating T-DXd with or without rilvegostomig (monovalent, fragment crystallizable-reduced, dual checkpoint bispecific immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody against programmed cell death 1 and T cell immunoglobulin and immunoreceptor with tyrosine-based inhibitor motif domain) as first-line therapy versus standard of care in HER2-expressing (IHC 3+/2+) locally advanced or metastatic BTC.23

Although only one patient in the PC cohort had an investigator-assessed OR, when assessed by ICR, three ORs were observed (12.0%).15 Most patients (68.0%) with PC had a BOR of stable disease (≥5 weeks), and investigator-assessed disease control at 12 weeks was achieved by 9 of 25 patients (36.0%). Among the 25 patients with PC, 76% had IHC 2+ tumors by central testing, with a DCR at 12 weeks of 36.8%. Given that there are currently no specific treatment recommendations for HER2-expressing, advanced PC, these findings show potential benefit with T-DXd for patients with HER2 IHC 3+/2+ advanced/metastatic PC. However, interpretation of findings from the PC cohort is limited by low patient numbers due to early cohort closure per the prespecified futility criterion. Furthermore, results may be confounded by the low proportion of patients with PC enrolled with IHC 3+ tumors. Limited efficacy observed in this cohort may also reflect the aggressive nature of the disease and the prominent stromal microenvironment observed in PC that may impact treatment response.1,24 Indeed, a negative correlation between patient survival and extracellular matrix deposition in PC has been observed, and both primary and metastatic lesions exhibit desmoplasia.25 Overall, considering the poor survival rates and limited treatment options in the second-line setting for advanced PC,1,19 these data highlight the potential use of T-DXd in late-line PC.

Across both cohorts, patients had varied characteristics and treatment histories, which may impact responses to T-DXd treatment. Of note, antitumor activity was observed in patients with (ORR, 40.0%; 2 of 5) or without (ORR, 19.4%; 7 of 36) prior immunotherapy in the BTC cohort; this finding is important considering chemotherapy with immunotherapy has since been recommended as the first-line treatment for metastatic BTC.17,18 Additionally, ORs in the BTC cohort occurred only in patients with no prior HER2 therapy (ORR, 26.5%; 9 of 34); however, how prior HER2 treatment may impact T-DXd treatment response remains unclear, as there were only seven patients in this subgroup. As expected, the majority of patients (72.0%) in the PC cohort had prior exposure to TOP1 inhibitors, reflecting the use of irinotecan-containing regimens (e.g. NALIRIFOX and FOLFIRINOX) in the first-line setting.19 In contrast, prior TOP1-inhibitor therapy was less frequent in the BTC cohort, consistent with the use of gemcitabine/cisplatin-based regimens in the first-line advanced setting, although irinotecan-containing therapies are used in later lines of treatment.17 Although numerically lower ORRs were observed between patients with or without prior TOP1-inhibitor therapy in the PC and BTC cohorts, it is not possible to draw any firm conclusions given the disparity in patient numbers between subgroups and overlapping 95% CIs. However, it is interesting to note that the antitumor activity of T-DXd was observed in patients with BTC with or without prior TOP1-inhibitor therapy; T-DXd antitumor activity has also been observed in patients with prior TOP1-inhibitor therapy in a phase I study in advanced gastric cancer and the phase II DESTINY-CRC02 study.26,27 Nevertheless, further study is needed to determine any resistance mechanisms that may limit T-DXd antitumor activity for patients with BTC or PC in this setting.

Another observation was the potential impact of KRAS alterations. Based on a recent retrospective study in 5883 patients with BTC in Japan, approximately 23% of patients had mutations in KRAS28; in contrast, only 9.8% of the BTC cohort in this study had KRAS alterations detected in baseline ctDNA. Among those with KRAS alterations, we observed lower ORRs (0.0% versus 25.0%) and shorter median PFS (1.3 versus 4.6 months) and median OS (2.1 versus 7.6 months) than in those without KRAS alterations. However, these results may be confounded by the proportion of patients with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors in each subgroup; in the BTC cohort, only one of four patients (25.0%) with detected KRAS alterations had IHC 3+ tumors compared with 15 of 36 patients (41.7%) without detected KRAS alterations. In a recent retrospective study of patients in the United States with PC, KRAS mutations were identified in approximately 82% (n = 578 of 703) of cases,29 which is higher than the 60% of patients with PC in this study with KRAS alterations detected in baseline ctDNA. In the PC cohort, no ORs were observed in those with detected KRAS alterations, whereas an OR was observed in one patient (12.5%) without detected KRAS alterations. Similar to the BTC cohort, shorter median PFS (2.4 versus 9.1 months) and OS (3.8 versus 15.9 months) were observed for patients with PC and detected KRAS alterations versus those without KRAS alterations, and no patients with detected KRAS alterations had IHC 3+ tumors compared with two patients (25.0%) among those without detected alterations. Overall, the lower antitumor activity observed among patients with KRAS alterations may reflect a more aggressive tumor phenotype, given that poorer outcomes have been reported for patients with KRAS-altered BTC/PC.28,29 However, interpretation of these results is limited by sample size, proportions of patients with IHC 3+ tumors between subgroups, and the fact that the presence/absence of KRAS alterations in ctDNA was not validated in tumor tissue. Additionally, the prevalence of KRAS mutations was lower in the BTC and PC cohorts compared with other studies,28,29 which may reflect limited sensitivity of the GuardantOMNI assay (Guardant Health) or reduced tumor shedding in the enrolled patient population. Interestingly, a negative association between HER2 amplification and KRAS mutations has been reported in gastric and colorectal cancers,30,31 which could further explain the lower prevalence observed in this study. Taken together, these findings warrant careful consideration, particularly in PC, where KRAS mutations are highly prevalent and differences in tumor biology and prognosis have been observed across specific KRAS mutation subtypes.29,32,33 Further research is therefore needed to understand the role of KRAS in determining treatment strategies for this population, which carries a remarkably poor prognosis.

Other clinical trials have similarly demonstrated that HER2-directed therapies are promising treatment options in BTC. The phase II HERB study (NCCH1805) reported an ORR of 36.4% (8 of 22; 95% CI 19.6%-56.1%) in T-DXd-treated patients from Japan with HER2 IHC 3+ or IHC 2+/ISH+, unresectable, or recurrent BTC.34 In addition, the primary analysis of the phase IIb HERIZON-BTC-01 study (NCT04466891) of zanidatamab, a bispecific antibody targeting HER2, in pretreated patients with HER2-amplified, HER2 IHC 3+/2+, advanced BTC showed an ORR of 41.3% (33 of 80; 95% CI 30.4%-52.8%); ORR in HER2 IHC 3+ tumors was 51.6% (32 of 62; 95% CI 38.6%-64.5%).35 These findings were consistent with the final analysis.36 Based on results from HERIZON-BTC-01, zanidatamab was granted accelerated approval in the United States in 2024 and conditional approval by the European Medicines Agency in 2025 for pretreated patients with HER2-positive (IHC 3+) locally advanced or metastatic BTC.37,38 Other studies of HER2-directed therapy include the phase II SGNTUC-019 (NCT04579380) and phase IIb MyPathway (NCT02091141) trials, which reported therapeutic potential for trastuzumab with tucatinib (SGNTUC-019) or pertuzumab (MyPathway) in patients with pretreated metastatic BTC.39,40 There is limited insight on the benefit of HER2-directed therapy in pretreated metastatic PC; however, treatment with trastuzumab and chemotherapy in the first-line setting previously showed limited clinical benefit.41 Additional insight into T-DXd efficacy in solid tumors is expected from Part 2 of DESTINY-PanTumor02, which is further evaluating T-DXd in pretreated patients with HER2-expressing/-amplified solid tumors.42

The safety findings in the BTC and PC cohorts were consistent with the established profile of T-DXd and reflective of a pretreated advanced cancer population (median of two prior treatment regimens in each cohort).12,15 ILD/pneumonitis remains an important identified risk among patients with cancer receiving T-DXd.43 The majority of ILD/pneumonitis cases observed across the BTC and PC cohorts were low-grade and manageable and generally consistent with previously reported incidence rates;13,15 one adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis-related death occurred in the BTC cohort. Proactive monitoring, early detection, and active management are pertinent in preventing high-grade ILD/pneumonitis; multidisciplinary guidelines for the diagnosis and management of T-DXd-related ILD/pneumonitis have previously been published.43

Limitations of DESTINY-PanTumor02 have been reported previously.15 Each tumor cohort comprised multiple histology types, which may have affected treatment response. Interpretation of the exploratory analyses may have been further limited by the sensitivity of ctDNA detection and small sample sizes, particularly in the IHC 3+ subgroup of the PC cohort. The heterogeneity of the patient population, with reference to varying HER2 status, prior therapy, and genetic alterations, introduced a further challenge in identifying specific biomarkers or prior treatments that may affect treatment response. Additionally, HER2 IHC/ISH testing was carried out on archival tissue, while ctDNA analyses used baseline blood samples. Therefore, exploratory analyses should be interpreted with caution and with consideration of disease complexity and possible confounding factors. Moderate discordance between local and central HER2 results was also highlighted in this analysis, which, based on insight from HER2 testing in other tumor types, may be due to tumor heterogeneity, interpathologist variability, or quality of fixation and HER2 staining.44 As real-world HER2 IHC testing is typically carried out locally, and there are no validated HER2 scoring guidelines for BTC or PC, this discordance should be carefully considered when interpreting the findings.45,46 These data also emphasize the importance of using validated HER2 tests and ensuring comprehensive training at local testing sites to ensure patients who may benefit from HER2-directed therapy are accurately identified. However, despite the observed discordance between HER2 test results, a post hoc analysis of all patients in DESTINY-PanTumor02 Part 1 demonstrated activity for T-DXd irrespective of stratification by locally or centrally determined HER2 IHC status.21

Overall, this analysis of DESTINY-PanTumor02 Part 1 demonstrated clinically meaningful and durable benefit for T-DXd in pretreated patients with HER2-expressing BTC, consistent with that observed in the full trial population,15 and a safety profile consistent with previously reported data for T-DXd. These results further support the use of T-DXd in pretreated, locally advanced, unresectable, or metastatic HER2-positive (IHC 3+) cancers. Interpretation of results from the PC cohort was limited; however, given the especially poor outcomes and limited treatment options for this population, the data provide important hypothesis-generating insights into the potential use of HER2-directed therapy in advanced PC.
