A higher homological approach to the q q -characters of representations of quantum affine algebras
Élie Casbi

Abstract
. For any acyclic quiver Q Q without multiple edges, we construct a monoidal category ℛ Q \mathcal{R}_{Q} whose indecomposable objects are tensor products (over the base field) of finite-dimensional modules over the path algebra of Q Q . We show the existence and uniqueness up to homotopy of certain distinguished chain complexes satisfying good homological properties (higher almost split complexes) preserved under tensoring by objects in ℛ Q \mathcal{R}_{Q} . As a crucial ingredient for this construction, we establish the existence of a family of complete exceptional sequences in mod ​ 𝐤 ​ Q \mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q satisfying many good properties, which we believe might be of independent interest. We then prove that when Q Q admits a height function, the Euler characteristics of (the images under certain additive functor of) these complexes coincide with the truncated q q -characters of the standard modules in Hernandez-Leclerc’s category 𝒞 ( 1 ) \mathcal{C}^{(1)} . Applying our results to the case where the underlying graph of Q Q is a Dynkin diagram of type A n , n ≥ 1 A_{n},n\geq 1 , we also interpret the cluster characters of all cluster variables in the finite type cluster algebra 𝒜 Q \mathcal{A}_{Q} as Euler characteristics of certain chain complexes in ℛ Q \mathcal{R}_{Q} .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivations

Let
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
denote the quantum affine algebra associated to an affine Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The understanding of simple
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules is a non-trivial problem that has led to many important advances over the past decades. Chari-Pressley
[
9
]
gave a classification of all simple finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules (up to isomorphism) in terms of dominant monomials. On the other hand the attempts to describe their
q
-characters as defined by Frenkel-Reshetikhin’s
[
15
]
led to the discovery of deep connections with the theory of Fomin-Zelevinsky’s cluster algebras
[
12
]
, via the notion of monoidal categorification introduced by Hernandez-Leclerc
[
22
]
. More precisely, when
\mathfrak{g}
is an affine Kac-Moody algebra, certain discrete versions of the category of finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules called Hernandez-Leclerc categories have been shown to categorify various interesting cluster structures in such a way that the set of cluster monomials is contained into the basis of isomorphism classes of simple objects in the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}

[
22
,
24
,
28
,
29
,
4
]
. This allows to relate certain invariants from cluster theory (
g
-vectors,
F
-polynomials) to representation-theoretic features (loop weights,
q
-characters). Recently, monoidal categorifications have also been obtained using representations of shifted quantum affine algebras
[
17
]
. In a different direction, when
\mathfrak{g}
is an arbitrary symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra, the
q
-characters of certain modules over a Borel subalgebra of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
have been studied by Hernandez-Jimbo
[
21
]
, and Negut
[
31
]
recently provided uniform formulas using the theory of shuffle algebras.

Among the irreducible representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, the fundamental representations play a special role as they generate
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}
as a monoidal category. More algebraically, the isomorphism classes of suitably ordered tensor products of fundamental representations (called standard modules) form a basis of the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. We will respectively denote by
L(\mathfrak{m})
and
\Delta(\mathfrak{m})
the simple and the standard
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module corresponding to a dominant monomial
\mathfrak{m}
.
When
\mathfrak{g}
is an affine simply-laced Kac-Moody algebra, the
q
-characters of fundamental representations admit interesting combinatorial descriptions as well as non-trivial geometric interpretations
[
30
,
24
]
. For more general Kac-Moody algebras, the fundamental representations are not anymore finite-dimensional, but still admit interesting combinatorial descriptions in certain cases.

The main motivation for the construction proposed in the present work stems from our desire to provide a conceptual interpretation of some remarkable rational identities obtained in our recent joint work with Jian-Rong Li
[
6
]
.
For a Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is simply-laced, and for any orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, we defined (originally in
[
7
]
, and then under its definite form in
[
6
]
) an algebra homomorphism
{\widetilde{D}}_{Q}:\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{k}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
which is intimately related to the map
\overline{D}
defined in
[
2
]
by Baumann-Kamnitzer-Knutson in their study of the equivariant homology of Mirković-Vilonen cycles. Here
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the ring of Laurent polynomials containing the
q
-characters of all objects in the Hernandez-Leclerc’s
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, while
\mathfrak{t}
stands for a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. In
[
6
]
, we used the map
{\widetilde{D}}_{Q}
to exhibit new families of non-trivial rational identities out of the representation theory of quantum affine algebras. More precisely, we showed that for any standard module
M
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

{\widetilde{D}}_{Q}\left(\chi_{q}(M)\right)=0.

The starting point of the present work consists in viewing these identities as the vanishing of certain alternate sums of dimension vectors (after getting rid of the denominators). In other words, to each object in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
should correspond a chain complex satisfying some kind of exactness property, and whose objects should keep track (in a bijective way) of the Laurent monomials appearing in the
q
-character of
M
.
Before presenting our results, we briefly describe the categories that will serve as underlying framework for the constructions proposed in this paper.

1.2.
The hammock category

Given a finite acyclic quiver
Q
, let us denote by
\mathbb{Z}Q
the repetition quiver of
Q
,
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
the category of finite-dimensional modules over the path algebra of
Q
, and by
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
its bounded derived category. For each
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
(viewed as the isomorphism class of an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
via Happel’s theorem), we consider a pair
Y(x):=(H(x),h_{x})
where
H(x)
is a collection (or multiset) consisting of all isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(x,y)\neq 0
, and
h_{x}:(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is an integer-valued function on the repetition quiver of
Q
satisfying a certain property called
quasi-additivity
(cf. Definition
4.1
). Taking unions of multisets and sums of quasi-additive functions, we obtain a family of pairs
(X,h)
that we call
dominant objects
. We then consider a certain operation on such pairs
(X,h)
, called
Serre tilting
, which consists in replacing finitely many elements of
X
by their image under the standard Serre functor of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
, as well as modifying
h
in a suitable way. The pairs obtained this way form the class of objects of a monoidal category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
. Given two multisets
X=\{\tau^{-1}x_{k},k\in K\}
and
Y=\{y_{l},l\in L\}
and two quasi-additive functions
f
and
g
, the space of morphisms between
(X,f)
and
(Y,g)
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
is given by

(1.1)

\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)\\
\sharp\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma)<\infty\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{k\in\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma)}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(\tau^{-1}x_{k},y_{\sigma(k)})

where
\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)
denotes the set of bijections from
K
to
L
and
\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma):=\{k\in K\mid y_{\sigma(k)}\neq\tau^{-1}x_{k}\}
for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)
.
We call
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
the
hammock category
, as it is essentially governed by the supports of
\mathrm{Hom}(x,-)
for
x\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
, which have been extensively studied via the hammock functions considered by Gabriel, Brenner and Ringel
[
16
,
3
,
35
]
. Eventually, the correspondence at the combinatorial level between our construction and the theory of
q
-characters will hinge on the hammock category: for instance, the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
can be essentially viewed as a subring of the ring
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
containing the
q
-characters of objects in the Hernandez-Leclerc’s category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The classes of the dominant pairs in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
can then be identified dominant monomials, while Serre tiltings provide an analogue of Chari’s braid group action on
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}

[
8
]
.

1.3.
Higher almost split complexes

One of the main desired outcomes motivating this work consists in constructing well-behaved chain complexes in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
whose objects shall keep track of the
q
-characters of distinguished objects in Hernandez-Leclerc’s categories, such as simple or standard objects. A sensible way to tackle this question is to construct these complexes inductively by some iterated mapping cone procedure. Classically, such method works well provided the complexes constructed at each step satisfy good homological properties. Important instances of such properties include the notion of higher exact or almost split complexes, higher Auslander-Reiten sequences, that have been the subject to numerous major works in the past few years (for example
[
26
,
27
,
18
]
).
In order to establish the existence of chain complexes satisfying similar homological properties, it will turn out being necessary to avoid working directly in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
, but rather in the (abelian) category

\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q^{\otimes n}.

The indecomposable objects in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
are tensor products (over the base field
\mathbf{k}
) of indecomposable
\mathbf{k}Q
-modules.
Note that certain special families of higher Auslander-Reiten sequences living in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
have been considered in
[
32
]
. However, they do not seem to be appropriate for our needs.
In a sense,
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
may feel more convenient than the hammock category, as it is nothing but a concrete category of modules over (tensor product of) path algebras, and also its monoidal structure is more refined. However, several significant obstructions appear:

•

naively replacing objects of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
by tensor products of
\mathbf{k}Q
-modules becomes problematic outside Dynkin types, as the hammocks then become infinite.

•

the chain complexes that we wish to construct might involve morphisms in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
.

•

the class of morphisms in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
is gigantic, which makes it a priori difficult to construct exact or almost split sequences.

The way to circumvent these obstructions is to work inside a well-chosen monoidal (but not full !) subcategory
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
. Note that in the literature, subcategories of great interest are provided by cluster tilting subcategories. However at this moment it does not seem clear how
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
can fit into this framework. Rather, we construct
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
somewhat explicitly, by proving the existence of certain distinguished classes of objects and morphisms in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
that satisfy all the properties necessary for our needs. We achieve this using the theory of exceptional sequences in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
.

1.4.
A special family of exceptional sequences in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q

Exceptional sequences were introduced by Crawley-Boevey
[
11
]
, generalizing Rudakov’s exceptional bundles
[
36
]
. They have been extensively studied and generalized since then (e.g.
[
33
,
34
,
25
,
5
]
) and have become an important notion in particular in the theory of additive categorifications of cluster algebras. Crawley-Boevey’s braid group action on exceptional sequences provides an inductive way of constructing indecomposable exceptional (i.e. without self extensions) objects in
\mathrm{mod}\,\,\mathbf{k}Q
, which is particularly useful in wild types where these admit no combinatorial classification in general. In the present paper, we use exceptional sequences to select in a non-trivial way certain
finite
subcollections of the hammocks
H(P_{i}),i\in Q_{0}
(here
P_{i}
denotes the indecomposable projective
\mathbf{k}Q
-modules). This is done by proving the existence of a remarkable family of exceptional sequences in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
satisfying strong properties. We refer to Theorem
5.1
below for a detailed statement.

1.5.
Purely exact complexes in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}

As mentioned above, we are going to construct certain chain complexes with objects in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
satisfying some higher exactness properties. However, we will need a slightly stronger property, called
pure exactness
, essentially guaranteeing some compatibility with the monoidal structure of
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
. More precisely, a chain complex
C_{\bullet}
will be called purely
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
-exact if
X\otimes C_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
-exact for every object
X
in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
. The following is the first main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Q
be an acyclic quiver without multiple edges. Then for every indecomposable object
M
in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
, there exists a (unique up to homotopy) purely
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
-exact almost split complex
C_{\bullet}(M)
such that
C_{0}\simeq M
.

1.6.
Truncated
q
-characters as Euler characteristics

Given a chain complex
C_{\bullet}=\cdots\rightarrow C_{0}\rightarrow C_{1}\rightarrow\cdots
with objects in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
(resp.
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
), we will denote by
\chi(C_{\bullet})
its Euler characteristics, which is the element of the split Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{R}_{Q})
(resp.
K_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{Q})
) given by

\chi\left(C_{\bullet}\right):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}[C_{n}].

We can now state the second main result of this paper, which provides an interpretation of the (truncated)
q
-characters of the standard modules
\Delta(\mathfrak{m})
in Hernandez-Leclerc’s category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
as Euler characteristics of the (images under
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
of the) chain complexes given by Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
Q
admits a height function. Let
M
be an object in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
and let
\mathfrak{m}
denote the dominant monomial in
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
given by the class of
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)
in
K_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{Q})
. Then we have

\chi\left(\mathcal{D}_{Q}\left(C_{\bullet}(M)\right)\right)_{\mid F_{i}:=-1}={\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}\left(\Delta(\mathfrak{m})\right).

The condition that
Q
admits a height function is always satisfied if the underlying graph of
Q
is a tree (in particular if
Q
is a Dynkin quiver or a tame quiver of type
D_{n}^{(1)}
or
E_{n}^{(1)}
) and is also satisfied for certain quivers of tame type
A_{2n+1}^{(1)},n\geq 1
. For other kinds of quivers, we believe the Laurent polynomials provided by the left hand side in the identity of Theorem
1.2
may be of independent interest.

The strong homological properties satisfied by the chain complexes
C_{\bullet}(M)
make them a powerful tool to exhibit interesting objects and morphisms in the bounded homotopy category of
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
. In particular, one may wonder whether this approach may allow to also realize the truncated
q
-characters of (at least some)
simple

U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules as Euler characteristics of certain well-behaved chain complexes in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
. The next result illustrates that this is indeed the case. We focus on the case where
\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}}
and
Q
is an arbitrary orientation of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
, leaving more general situations for future investigation.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
Q
is a Dynkin quiver of type
A_{n},n\geq 1
. Then for each positive root
\beta\in\Delta_{+}
, there exists a chain complex
C_{\bullet}^{\beta}
in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
such that

\chi\left(\mathcal{D}_{Q}(C_{\bullet}^{\beta})\right)_{\mid F_{i}:=-1}={\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L[\beta])

where
L[\beta]
is the (unique up to isomorphism) simple module in Hernandez-Leclerc’s category
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}
categorifying the cluster variable of denominator vector
\beta
in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
.

In view of the monoidal categorification results by Brito-Chari
[
4
]
and Kashiwara-Kim-Oh-Park
[
28
]
, all simple modules in
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}
are cluster monomials and thus simple truncated
q
-characters can be essentially identified with cluster characters. Thus the proof of Theorem
1.3
will be carried out purely in the language of cluster algebras.

1.7.
Perspectives

The framework proposed in this paper allows to construct new instances of complexes satisfying higher exactness (or almost splitness) properties, in a compatible way with the monoidal structure of the underlying category. Thus, our construction may allow to produce large families of new non-trivial examples of
n
-exact sequences (potentially including the case
n=0
recently developed in
[
19
]
).
The fact that their Euler characteristics coincide with certain distinguished (standard or simple)
q
-characters hence builds a new bridge between the representation theory of quantum affine algebras on the one hand and the theory of
n
-exact complexes on the other hand.

We would also like to outline some upshot from a cluster-theoretic perspective. Indeed, while quantum affine algebras are involved in the theory of monoidal categorifications of cluster algebras, all the key features making possible the construction of our chain complexes are controlled by objects typical from the theory of additive categorifications of cluster algebras, above all exceptional sequences. Thus we believe our approach may possibly yield to a better understanding of the connections between these two theories at a categorical level.

Finally, though the cluster-related result of this paper (Theorem
1.3
) focus on the type
A
case, we expect the framework developed in this paper to provide powerful tools to realize more general cluster characters as Euler characteristics, raising the question of the potential existence of monoidal categorifications of other cluster structures than the ones coming from Lie theory.

1.8.
Outline of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. Sections
2
and
3
are respectively devoted to the necessary recollections on Auslander-Reiten theory and Hernandez-Leclerc’s categories of modules over quantum affine algebras. Section
4
introduces the hammock category and relates it to the theory of
q
-characters. In Section
5
, we present a result on complete exceptional sequences in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
which is the crucial technical feature upon which our constructions hinge. In Section
6
we define the category
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
which will serve as underlying category for the main results of this paper. Section
7
is devoted to the main homological definitions as well as the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Finally, in Section
8
we prove Theorem
1.3
after a brief recollection on cluster theory.

2.2.

C_{\bullet}
, defined as

\chi(C_{\bullet}):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}[C_{n}]\kern 5.0pt\in K_{0}(\mathcal{H})

where
K_{0}(\mathcal{H})
stands for the split Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{H}
. Given two objects
C_{\bullet}
and
D_{\bullet}
and a morphism
u_{\bullet}:C_{\bullet}\rightarrow D_{\bullet}
in
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{H})
, we will denote by
\mathrm{Cone}(u_{\bullet})
the mapping cone of
u_{\bullet}
, which is the object of
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{H})
whose degree
n
object is
C_{n+1}\oplus D_{n}
and whose differentials are given by

d_{n}^{\mathrm{Cone}(u_{\bullet})}:=\begin{pmatrix}d_{n+1}^{C_{\bullet}}&u_{n+1}\\
0&-d_{n}^{D_{\bullet}}\end{pmatrix}

Classically, we get a distinguished triangle in
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{H})
:

C_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{u_{\bullet}}D_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathrm{Cone}(u_{\bullet})\rightarrow C_{\bullet}[1].

Assuming now that
\mathcal{H}
comes with a symmetric monoidal structure
\otimes
,
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{H})
can then be naturally endowed with a structure of (symmetric) triangulated monoidal category. We do not provide its explicit description here as it will not be needed. We simply mention that if
X
is an object in
\mathcal{H}
and
C_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{H})
, we will denote by
X\otimes C_{\bullet}
the chain complex whose degree
n
object is
X\otimes C_{n}
and whose
n
-th differential is
\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}}
.

3.
Representations of quantum affine algebras

This section provides necessary reminders about the representation theory of quantum affine algebras. We will mostly focus on the symmetric affine case and will recall the definitions and main features of the Hernandez-Leclerc categories.

3.1.
Representations of quantum affine algebras and their
q
-characters

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simply-laced type simple complex Lie algebra and let
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
denote the associated quantum affine algebra. The category
\mathcal{C}
of finite-dimensional representations of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
is an abelian monoidal category which is not semisimple, not braided, and that contains infinitely (and even uncountably) many simple objects. More precisely, it was proved by Chari-Pressley
[
10
]
that the irreducible representations in
\mathcal{C}
are classified (up to isomorphism) by a monoid
\mathcal{P}^{+}
whose elements are called dominant monomials and which is defined as the free monoid generated by indeterminates
Y_{i,a},i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Here
I
denotes an indexing set of the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
. We will denote by
L(\mathfrak{m})
the simple module corresponding to
\mathfrak{m}
under this classification. The simple modules of the form
L(Y_{i,a})
are called
fundamental representations
. Once a certain order has been fixed on
I\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, the ordered tensor products of fundamental representations are called
standard modules
. If
\mathfrak{m}=\prod_{i,a}Y_{i,a}^{u_{i,a}}
, we will denote by
\Delta(\mathfrak{m})
the standard module given by the ordered tensor product of the
L(Y_{i,a})^{\otimes u_{i,a}}
. It is then known that for any dominant monomial
\mathfrak{m}
, the simple module
L(\mathfrak{m})
is isomorphic to a quotient of
\Delta(\mathfrak{m})
. In this sense, the fundamental representations generate
\mathfrak{C}
as a monoidal category. Although
\mathcal{C}
is not symmetric, its Grothendieck ring is commutative. Frenkel-Reshetikhin
[
15
]
introduced an injective ring homomorphism

\chi_{q}:\kern 5.0ptK_{0}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}:=\mathbb{Z}[Y_{i,a}^{\pm},i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}]

called the
q
-character morphism. Essentially, the Laurent monomials appearing in the
q
-character of a given object
M
in
\mathcal{C}
represent the eigenvalues of the action of the (loop) Cartan subalgebra of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
on
M
and the coefficients of
\chi_{q}(M)
encode the dimensions of the corresponding eigenspaces. It is known that the
renormalized
q
-character
of
M
defined as
\mathfrak{m}^{-1}\chi_{q}(M)
is a polynomial in the
A_{i,a}^{-1},i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
with constant term
1
, where

(3.1)

\forall i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C^{*}},\kern 5.0ptA_{i,a}:=Y_{i,aq^{-1}}Y_{i,aq}\prod_{j\sim i}Y_{i,a}^{-1}.

3.2.
Hernandez-Leclerc categories

Motivated by exhibiting deep connections between the representation theory of quantum affine algebras and the combinatorics of cluster algebras introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
12
]
, Hernandez-Leclerc defined several monoidal full subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
, which we now briefly recall.
Let
\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the set of pairs

\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\{(i,p),i\in I,p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}\}

and let
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the ring

\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\mathbb{Z}[Y_{i,p}^{\pm 1},(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}].

Following
[
23
]
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
the smallest monoidal full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
containing the fundamental representations
L(Y_{i,q^{p}})
for
p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}
. Following the common use, we will be writing
Y_{i,p}
(resp.
A_{i,p}
) as a shorthand notation for
Y_{i,q^{p}}
(resp.
A_{i,q^{p}}
) in all what follows. The simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
are the modules
L(\mathfrak{m})
where
\mathfrak{m}
is a monomial in the
Y_{i,p}
. The category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
admits various kinds of well-known short exact sequences that play an important role in the theory of monoidal categorifications of cluster algebras
[
24
,
29
]
. The following special case will be useful to us later

(3.2)

0\longrightarrow\bigotimes_{j\sim i}L(Y_{j,p+1})\longrightarrow L(Y_{i,p})\otimes L(Y_{i,p+2})\longrightarrow L(Y_{i,p}Y_{i,p+2})\longrightarrow 0.

Note that the middle term is the standard module
\Delta(Y_{i,p}Y_{i,p+2})
.
Frenkel-Reshetikhin’s
q
-character morphism restricts to an injective ring homomorphism

\chi_{q}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}.

The polynomial identity obtained by applying
\chi_{q}
to (
3.2
) is an instance of the so-called
T
-systems.

We now fix an orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi
a height function adapted to
Q
. Then we set, for every positive integer
l\geq 1
,

\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}^{(l)}:=\{(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}},\kern 5.0pt\xi(i)-2l\leq p\leq\xi(i)\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}:=\{(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}},p\leq\xi(i)\}

and we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(l)}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
) the smallest full monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
containing all the fundamental representations
L(Y_{i,p})
such that
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}^{(l)}
(resp.
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}
).
Finally, following
[
22
,
24
]
, we define for each
M\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
the
truncated
q
-character

{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(M)
of
M
as the element of
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
obtained from
\chi_{q}(M)
by dropping all the terms involving variables
Y_{i,p}
with
p>\xi(i)
. This gives another injective ring homomorphism

{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}:=\mathbb{Z}[Y_{i,p}^{\pm 1},(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\leq\xi}].

3.3.
The case of the category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}

In this paper, we will be particularly interested in the category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
. The following truncated
q
-characters are known:

{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L(Y_{i,\xi(i)}))=Y_{i,\xi(i)}\qquad{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L(Y_{i,\xi(i)-2}Y_{i,\xi(i)}))=Y_{i,\xi(i)-2}Y_{i,\xi(i)}

and furthermore, the renormalized truncated
q
-character of any object in
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
is a polynomial (with constant term
1
) in the
A_{i,\xi(i)-1}^{-1},i\in I
. For that reason we will often use the shorthand notation
A_{i}:=A_{i,\xi(i)-1}
for each
i\in I
.
When
\mathfrak{g}
is not finite-dimensional, the fundamental representations of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
are not anymore finite-dimensional. Nonetheless, they can still be studied by considering the Hernandez-Jimbo category
\mathcal{O}

[
21
]
, and the category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
can then be defined as a subcategory of
\mathcal{O}
in the same way as above.

4.
The hammock category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}

4.1.
Quasi-additive functions

Extending Gabriel’s terminology of additive functions from
[
16
]
, we consider the following class of integer-valued functions on
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A function
f:(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
will be called quasi-additive if
\widetilde{f}
has finite support, i.e.
\widetilde{f}(x)=0
on all but finitely many vertices of
\mathbb{Z}Q
.

For each
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
, we consider the unique quasi-additive function
h_{x}
determined by the requirements

(1)

h_{x}(y)=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(x,y)
for every vertex
y
on the section
Q_{x}
of
\mathbb{Z}Q
,

(2)

\widetilde{h_{x}}=\delta_{x}
,

where
\delta_{x}
denotes the function given by
\delta_{x}(y):=1
if
x=y
and
0
otherwise. For a given
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
, the function
h_{x}
should be viewed as a semi-infinite version of the
hammock functions
considered by Gabriel
[
16
]
and Brenner
[
3
]
. With the notations above, a function is additive in the sense of
[
16
]
if
\widetilde{f}=0
, hence the terminology "quasi-additive".

Lemma 4.3
.

The functions
h_{x},x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Assume
r\geq 1
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
are such that
a_{1}h_{x_{1}}+\cdots+a_{r}h_{x_{r}}=0
. We can choose
i
such that
\tau^{-1}x_{i}
is to the left of the other
\tau^{-1}x_{j}
. Wlog
i=1
. Hence we have
h_{\tau^{-1}x_{k}}(x_{1})=0
for all
k>1
and
h_{x_{1}}(x_{1})=1
by definition of the functions
h_{x}
. This implies
a_{1}=0
. The lemma thus follows by a straightforward induction.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
we have that

h_{x}+h_{\tau^{-1}x}=\delta_{x}+\sum_{x\rightarrow y}h_{y}.

Proof.

Let us fix
x\in(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
. It is then straightforward to check that

\widetilde{\delta_{x}}=\delta_{x}+\delta_{\tau^{-1}x}-\sum_{x\rightarrow y}\delta_{y}.

It follows from this that the function

(4.1)

h_{x}+h_{\tau^{-1}x}-\sum_{x\rightarrow y}h_{y}-\delta_{x}

is additive and hence is entirely determined by its values on any section of
\mathbb{Z}Q
(cf.
[
16
]
). Let us consider the section starting at
x
. For any
z
on that section, we have that
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{Hom}(x,z)=1
so that
h_{x}(z)=1
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(z,x)=0
which implies that
h_{\tau^{-1}x}(z)=0
using the Auslander-Reiten formula. On the other hand,
z
belongs to exactly one of the sections starting at each
y
such that there is an arrow
x\rightarrow y
in
\mathbb{Z}Q
, unless
z
is
x
itself. In other words we have that
\sum_{x\rightarrow y}h_{y}(z)=1
if
z\neq x
, and
0
if
z=x
. Putting all of this together we see that the function (
4.1
) vanishes on all the vertices of the section starting at
x
. The lemma is proved.
∎

4.2.
The category
\mathcal{M}_{Q}

We denote by
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
the set of all isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
. Recall that a multiset of elements of
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
is a collection
\{\tau^{-1}x_{k},k\in K\}
where
K
is a set and
\tau^{-1}x_{k}
is an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
for each
k\in K
(equivalently, it can be viewed as a function from
K
to
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
). Note that such a function shall usually not be injective; in other words, any given indecomposable object in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
may appear several times (up to isomorphism) in the same collection (but only finitely many times !). In this work, we will exclusively be considering at most countable multisets, i.e. functions from a finite or countable set
K
to
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
.
Given two multisets
X
and
Y
we can define their intersection
X\cap Y
as the largest multiset contained in both
X
and
Y
.

We define a
\mathbf{k}
-category
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
as follows. The class of objects of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
consists of all pairs
(X,h)
where
X
is an at most countable multiset of elements of
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
and
h
is a quasi-additive function on
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
. We then define the notion of Serre tilting of such pairs as follows. Recall that
S
denotes the Serre functor
S=\tau\Sigma=\Sigma\tau
in the category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
.

Definition 4.5
.

Given any indecomposable object
(X,h)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
and any
finite
subset
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\}\subseteq X
, we define the
Serre tilting
of
(X,h)
at
Y
as the object of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
given by

\mu_{Y}(X,h):=\left((X\setminus Y)\sqcup SY\kern 5.0pt,\kern 5.0pth-\sum_{y\in Y}\delta_{y}\right)

where
SY
stands for
SY:=\{Sy_{1},\ldots,Sy_{r}\}
. For simplicity we will write
\mu_{y}(X)
for
\mu_{\{y\}}(X)
.

We now define the class of morphisms of the category
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
.
Given two at most countable sets
K
and
L
, we denote by
\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)
the set of all bijections from
K
to
L
. In particular
\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)=\emptyset
if
K
and
L
are finite of different cardinalities. Moreover for any function
\sigma:K\rightarrow L
, we set

\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma):=\{k\in K\mid y_{\sigma(k)}\neq\tau^{-1}x_{k}\}.

Given two multisets
X=\{\tau^{-1}x_{k},k\in K\}
and
Y=\{y_{l},l\in L\}
and two quasi-additive functions
f
and
g
on
(\mathbb{Z}Q)_{0}
, we then define the morphism space from
(X,f)
to
(Y,g)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
as follows:

(4.2)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}}\left((X,f),(Y,g)\right):=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)\\
\sharp\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma)<\infty\end{subarray}}\bigotimes_{k\in\mathrm{Supp}_{X,Y}(\sigma)}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(\tau^{-1}x_{k},y_{\sigma(k)})

Note that each summand on the right hand side is a tensor product of finitely many
\mathbf{k}
-vector spaces (as
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
is a
\mathbf{k}
-category) so that
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
is a
\mathbf{k}
-category.

Remark 4.6
.

In most of what follows we will be considering only the case where
X
and
Y
are finite with same cardinality so that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}}\left((X,f),(Y,g)\right):=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Bij}(K,L)}\bigotimes_{k\in K}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(\tau^{-1}x_{k},y_{\sigma(k)}).

The category
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
can be endowed with a symmetric monoidal structure as follows.
Given two objects
(X,f)
and
(Y,g)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
with
X=\{\tau^{-1}x_{k},k\in K\}
and
Y=\{y_{l},l\in L\}
, we set

(4.3)

(X,f)\otimes(Y,g):=\left(X\sqcup Y,f+g\right).

where
X\sqcup Y
is the multiset defined by

X\sqcup Y:=\{z_{p},p\in K\sqcup L\},\kern 5.0ptz_{p}:=\begin{cases}\tau^{-1}x_{p}&\text{if $p\in K$,}\\
y_{p}&\text{if $p\in L$. }\end{cases}

Moreover, given two non-trivial morphisms
\varphi_{1}:(X_{1},f_{1})\rightarrow(Y_{1},g_{1})
and
\varphi_{2}:(X_{2},f_{2})\rightarrow(Y_{2},g_{2})
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
, we get a canonical non-trivial morphism
\varphi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{2}:(X_{1},f_{1})\otimes(X_{2},f_{2})\rightarrow(Y_{1},g_{1})\otimes(Y_{2},g_{2})
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
as
(Y_{1}\sqcup Y_{2},g_{1}+g_{2})=\mu_{Z_{1}\sqcup Z_{2}}(X_{1}\sqcup X_{2},f_{1}+f_{2})
where
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are such that
(Y_{i},g_{i})=\mu_{Z_{i}}(\tau^{-1}x_{i},f_{i})
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
. It is straightforward to check that this construction is natural and hence (
4.3
) endows
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
with a symmetric monoidal structure.
Finally, we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
the additive envelope of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
. It follows from what precedes that
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
is a
\mathbf{k}
-linear symmetric monoidal category.

Remark 4.7
.

We believe the objects of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{Q}}
can be shown to be indecomposable in
\mathcal{M}_{Q}
. We can prove it in the case where
Q
is a Dynkin quiver but do not know how to extend this to arbitrary acyclic quivers.

Proposition 4.11
.

For each
x\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
we have that

\mu_{x}Y(x)\otimes Y(\tau^{-1}x)\simeq F(x)\otimes\bigotimes_{x\rightarrow z}Y(z).

Proof.

In view of Lemma
4.4
, the desired statement is equivalent to the following identity of multisets

(4.7)

H(x)\sqcup H(\tau^{-1}x)=\{x,\Sigma x\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{x\rightarrow z}H(z).

This will follow from the fact that
x\xrightarrow{\iota}\bigoplus_{x\rightarrow z}z\xrightarrow{\pi}\tau^{-1}x\rightarrow\Sigma x
is an Auslander-Reiten triangle in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
. Indeed, let
y\in\mathcal{I}_{Q}
and assume
y
is not isomorphic neither to
x
nor
\Sigma x
. We are going to check that the sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\tau^{-1}x,y)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{x\rightarrow z}\mathrm{Hom}(z,y)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(x,y)\longrightarrow 0

is then a short exact sequence in the category of
\mathbf{k}
-vector spaces.
As
y\not\simeq x
, the fact that the above triangle is left almost split implies that any morphism from
x
to
y
factors through
\iota
, hence the surjectivity of the right most non trivial map in the above sequence. Let us now take
f
a non trivial morphism in
\mathrm{Hom}(\tau^{-1}x,y)
and assume
f\circ\pi=0
. By definition of Serre functors, we can consider a non trivial morphism
\overline{f}:S^{-1}y\rightarrow\tau^{-1}x
such that
f\circ\overline{f}\neq 0
. Note that
\overline{f}
is not an isomorphism, because in that case we would have
\tau^{-1}x\simeq S^{-1}y=\tau^{-1}\Sigma^{-1}y
which contradicts our assumption that
y\not\simeq\Sigma x
. Therefore, as the triangle
x\rightarrow\bigoplus_{x\rightarrow z}z\rightarrow\tau^{-1}x\rightarrow\Sigma x
is right almost split,
\overline{f}
has to factor through
\pi
. But this implies
f\circ\overline{f}=0
(as
f\circ\pi=0
by assumption) which is a contradiction. This shows the injectivity of the left most non trivial map in the desired short sequence. Finally the exactness of the middle part follows from the fact that
\mathrm{Hom}(-,y)
is cohomological (equivalently,
\pi
is a weak cokernel of
\iota
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
).
As a consequence of the exactness of the above short exact sequence, we obtain

\forall y\neq x,\Sigma x\kern 5.0pt\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(x,y)+\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(\tau^{-1}x,y)=\sum_{x\rightarrow z}\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(z,y).

Therefore, in order to conclude that (
4.7
) holds, it remains to observe that
x
appears once in
H(x)
(as
\dim\mathrm{Hom}(x,x)=1
) and does not appear in
H(\tau^{-1}x)
, while
\Sigma x
appears once in
H(\tau^{-1}x)
because

\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(\tau^{-1}x,\Sigma x)=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(x,Sx)=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(x,x)=1

and does not appear in
H(x)
as
\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)}(x,\Sigma x)=0
. This finishes the proof of the proposition.

4.4.
The subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{Q}

We now define a full monoidal subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}Q}
by allowing only Serre tiltings on a well-chosen section of
\mathbb{Z}Q
. More precisely, let us fix an embedding of
\mathrm{mod}\,(\mathbf{k}Q)
into
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
and fix isomorphism classes
\{x_{i},i\in I\}
of the indecomposable projective
\mathbf{k}Q
modules. As the irreducible morphisms between the
x_{i}
form a copy of
Q^{op}
, it yields a section in
\mathbb{Z}Q
that we also denote
\{x_{i},i\in I\}
. We then define
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
as the smallest
k
-linear monoidal full subcategory of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}Q}
whose class of objects contains
Y(x_{i}),Y(\tau^{-1}x_{i})
as well as
F(x_{i})
for every
i\in I
, and that is stable under Serre tiltings
\mu_{x_{j}},j\in I
. We will often use the shorthand notation
\mu_{j}:=\mu_{x_{j}}
. We denote by
K_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{Q})
the split Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
, and set the following notations:

(4.8)

\forall i\in I,\quad Y_{x_{i}}:=[Y(x_{i})]\quad Y_{\tau^{-1}x_{i}}:=[Y(\tau^{-1}x_{i})]\quad F_{i}:=[F(x_{i})].

5.
A result on complete exceptional sequences in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q

This section is devoted to the proof of the following result, which we believe may be of independent interest from the perspective of the representation theory of quivers.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a finite acyclic quiver, and denote
n:=\sharp Q_{0}
and
m:=\sharp Q_{1}
. There exists a family
\{E^{(0)},\ldots,E^{(m)}\}
of complete exceptional sequences in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

E^{(0)}
(resp.
E^{(m)}
) consists only of the indecomposable projectives (resp. injectives).

(2)

If we denote
E^{(t)}:=(x_{1}^{(t)},\ldots,x_{n}^{(t)})
for each
0\leq t\leq m
, then

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q}\left(x_{i}^{(t)},x_{i}^{(t^{\prime})}\right)\neq 0\quad\text{for every $0\leq t\leq t^{\prime}\leq m$ and $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$.}

(3)

There is a bijection
\alpha\in Q_{1}\longmapsto t_{\alpha}\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
such that

\alpha:=i\rightarrow j\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0ptx_{j}^{(t_{\alpha}+1)}\simeq x_{i}^{(t_{\alpha})}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q}\left(x_{j}^{(t_{\alpha})},x_{i}^{(t_{\alpha}+1)}\right)=0.

(4)

For each
i\in I
and every arrow
\alpha
whose target (resp. source) is
i
, we have

\forall 0\leq t,t^{\prime}\leq m,\qquad\text{$t\leq t_{\alpha}<t^{\prime}$ (resp. $t<t_{\alpha}\leq t^{\prime}$)}\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0ptx_{i}^{(t)}\not\simeq x_{i}^{(t^{\prime})}.

Let us choose a complete exceptional sequence
E^{(0)}
in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
consisting only of the indecomposable projectives, and let us consider
i\in Q_{0}
such that
x_{n}^{(0)}\simeq P_{i}
. Note that in particular
i
is a source in
Q
. Let
n>k_{1}>\cdots>k_{r}\geq 1
be such that
x_{k_{1}}^{(0)},\ldots,x_{k_{r}}^{(0)}
are respectively isomorphic to the indecomposable projectives at each of the neighbors of
i
in
Q
. It will also be convenient to set
k_{0}:=n
.
We introduce the following piece of notation:

\forall 0\leq t\leq r,\kern 5.0pt\beta_{t}:=\boldsymbol{\dim}P_{i}-\sum_{1\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t^{\prime}}}^{(0)}.

Let us first establish a couple of straightforward technicalities.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
t\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and
l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then we have:

(1)

If
l<k_{t}
, then
\langle\beta_{t},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)}\rangle=0
and
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)},\beta_{t}\rangle\geq 0
. Moreover, if
l=k_{t+1}
, then

\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)},\beta_{t}\rangle=1
.

(2)

If
l\geq k_{t}
, then
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)},\beta_{t}\rangle=0
and
\langle\beta_{t},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)}\rangle\leq 0
. Moreover, if
l=k_{t}
, then
\langle\beta_{t},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(0)}\rangle=-1
.

Proof.

Proof of (1).

If
l<k_{t}
then
n>k_{s}\geq k_{t}>l
for all
1\leq s\leq t
and hence
\langle P_{i},x_{l}^{(0)}\rangle=\langle x_{k_{s}}^{(0)},x_{l}^{(0)}\rangle
for all
1\leq s\leq t
as
E^{(0)}
is an exceptional sequence. Thus
\langle\beta_{t},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)}\rangle=0
. In addition to that, as
i
is a source in
Q
, we have

(5.1)

\beta_{t}=\alpha_{i}+\sum_{t^{\prime}>t}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t^{\prime}}}^{(0)}

which implies
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)},\beta_{t}\rangle\geq 0
because as
x_{l}^{(0)}
is projective, the Euler form
\langle x_{l}^{(0)},Y\rangle
is non negative for any object
Y
in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
. Finally,

\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)},\beta_{t},\rangle=\langle x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)},S_{i}\rangle+\sum_{t^{\prime}\geq t+1}\langle x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)},x_{k_{t^{\prime}}}^{(0)}\rangle=1

because
\langle x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)},S_{i}\rangle=0
while
\langle x_{u}^{(0)},x_{v}^{(0)}\rangle=0
for all
1\leq v<u\leq r
as
E^{(0)}
is an exceptional sequence.

Proof of (2).

If
l\geq k_{t}
then
k_{s}<k_{t}\leq l
for all
r\geq s>t
and hence
\langle\tau^{-1}\tau^{-1}x_{l}^{(0)},x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle=0
for all such
s
as
E^{(0)}
is an exceptional sequence. As
\langle\tau^{-1}\tau^{-1}x_{l}^{(0)},S_{i}\rangle=0
, we obtain
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}\tau^{-1}\tau^{-1}x_{l}^{(0)},\beta_{t}\rangle=0
using (
5.1
). On the other hand, we have
\langle P_{i},\tau^{-1}\tau^{-1}x_{l}^{(0)}\rangle=0
and hence from the definition of
\beta_{t}
it immediately follows that
\langle\beta_{t},\boldsymbol{\dim}\tau^{-1}\tau^{-1}x_{l}^{(0)}\rangle\leq 0
as the Euler form
\langle\tau^{-1}\tau^{-1}x_{l}^{(0)},Y\rangle
is non negative for any object
Y
in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
. Finally,

\langle\beta_{t},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(0)}\rangle=\langle\beta_{t-1}-\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(0)},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(0)}\rangle=\langle\beta_{t-1},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(0)}\rangle-1=-1

applying the previous bullet point with
t-1
instead of
t
, and
l=k_{t}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
1\leq t\leq t^{\prime}\leq r
, let
l\in\{k_{t},\ldots,n\}
and let
Y
be an indecomposable object in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
such that
\mathrm{Hom}(x_{l}^{(0)},Y)\neq 0
and
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}Y,\beta_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=0
. Then the Euler form
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)}+\lambda\beta_{t},s_{\beta_{t^{\prime}}}\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle
is positive for any
\lambda\geq 0
.

Proof.

Denote
\mu:=-\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle
. Then

\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)},s_{\beta_{t^{\prime}}}\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle=\langle x_{l}^{(0)},Y\rangle+\mu\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)},\beta_{t^{\prime}}\rangle>0

as
\mathrm{Hom}\left(x_{l}^{(0)},Y\right)\neq 0
by assumption and
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)},\beta_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=0
by Lemma
5.2
(2) given that
l\geq k_{t}\geq k_{t^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, using Lemma
5.2
, a straightforward induction shows that
\langle\beta_{t},\beta_{t}\rangle=1
and hence (again using Lemma
5.2
)
\langle\beta_{t},\beta_{s}\rangle=1
for all
1\leq t\leq s\leq r
. Thus we get

\langle\beta_{t},\boldsymbol{\dim}Y+\mu\beta_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=\langle\beta_{t},\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle+\mu=\langle\beta_{t}-\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle=\sum_{t<s\leq t^{\prime}}\langle x_{k_{s}}^{(0)},Y\rangle\geq 0.

All together, we conclude that the desired Euler form is positive.

∎

Proposition 5.4
.

For every
0\leq t\leq r
, there is a complete exceptional sequence
E^{(t)}=(x_{1}^{(t)},\ldots,x_{n}^{(t)})
such that:

\forall 1\leq l\leq n,\kern 5.0pt\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(t)}=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)}&\text{if $1\leq l<k_{t}$,}\\
s_{\beta_{t}}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{l}^{(0)}&\text{if $k_{t}\leq l<n$,}\\
\beta_{t}&\text{if $l=n$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof goes by induction on
t
. For
t=0
there us nothing to prove (recall that
k_{0}:=n
). Let us now assume it holds at rank
t
. We shall perform
\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{k_{t+1}+1}^{-1}\sigma_{k_{t+1}}\cdots\sigma_{n-1}
on
E^{(t)}
. First, we perform
\sigma_{k_{t+1}+1}\cdots\sigma_{n-1}
on
E^{(t)}
: we obtain a complete exceptional sequence
E^{\prime}=(X^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,X^{\prime}_{n})
whose objects are such that

\boldsymbol{\dim}X^{\prime}_{j}\simeq\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{j}^{(0)}&\text{if $j\leq k_{t+1}$,}\\
\beta_{t}&\text{if $j=k_{t+1}+1$,}\\
X_{j-1}^{(0)}&\text{if $j>k_{t+1}+1$. }\end{cases}

Then, performing the action of the elementary generator
\sigma_{k_{t+1}}
on
E^{\prime}
yields a complete exceptional sequence
E^{\prime\prime}
which is the same as
E^{\prime}
except that
\boldsymbol{\dim}X_{k_{t+1}}^{\prime\prime}=\beta_{t}
while

\boldsymbol{\dim}X_{k_{t+1}+1}^{\prime\prime}=\beta_{t}-\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)},\beta_{t},\rangle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)}=\beta_{t}-\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)}=\beta_{t+1}

using Lemma
5.2
(1).
Finally, we perform the action of
\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{k_{t+1}+1}^{-1}
on
E^{\prime\prime}
and we define
E^{(t+1)}
as the resulting (complete) exceptional sequence. We obtain

\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{j}^{(t+1)}=\begin{cases}x_{j}^{(0)}&\text{it $j<k_{t+1}$,}\\
\beta_{t}&\text{if $j=k_{t+1}$},\\
s_{\beta_{t+1}}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{j}^{(0)}&\text{if $k_{t+1}<j<n$,}\\
\beta_{t+1}&\text{if $j=n$.}\end{cases}

Thus to conclude, it suffices to note that by definition we have
\beta_{t}=\beta_{t+1}+\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)}
, which coincides with
s_{\beta_{t+1}}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t+1}}^{(0)}
by Lemma
5.2
(1).
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

For every
1\leq j\leq n
, we have
\mathrm{Hom}\left(x_{j}^{(t)},x_{j}^{(t^{\prime})}\right)\neq 0
for all
1\leq t\leq t^{\prime}\leq r
. Moreover, for every
1\leq t\leq r
, we have
x_{k_{t}}^{(t)}\simeq x_{n}^{(t-1)}
and
\mathrm{Hom}\left(x_{k_{t}}^{(t-1)},x_{n}^{(t)}\right)=0
.

Proof.

We have already noted (cf. the proof of Lemma
5.3
) that
\langle\beta_{t},\beta_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=1
if
t\leq t^{\prime}
, so the first statement holds for
j=n
. Let us now assume
j<n
. Combining Proposition
5.4
together with Lemma
5.2
(2), we see that
\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{j}^{(t)}
can be written as
\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{j}^{(0)}+\lambda_{j,t}\beta_{t}
with
\lambda_{j,t}\geq 0
. If
j<k_{t^{\prime}}
then
j<k_{t}
as well and hence
\mathrm{Hom}\left(x_{j}^{(t)},x_{j}^{(t^{\prime})}\right)=\mathrm{Hom}\left(x_{j}^{(0)},x_{j}^{(0)}\right)\neq 0
. If
l\geq k_{t^{\prime}}
, then by Lemma
5.2
(2) the equality
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{j}^{(0)},\beta_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=0
holds and hence applying Lemma
5.3
with
l=j,Y=x_{j}^{(0)}
and
\lambda=\lambda_{j,t}
we obtain

\langle x_{j}^{(t)},x_{j}^{(t^{\prime})}\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{j}^{(0)}+\lambda_{j,t}\beta_{t},s_{\beta_{t^{\prime}}}x_{j}^{(0)}\rangle>0

which establishes the first desired statement.
For the second statement, by Proposition
5.4
together with Lemma
5.2
(1), we have

\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(t)}=s_{\beta_{t}}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(0)}=\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(0)}+\beta_{t}=\beta_{t-1}=\boldsymbol{\dim}X_{n}^{(t-1)}.

We also have
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t}}^{(0)},\beta_{t}\rangle=0
by Lemma
5.2
(1), which implies
\mathrm{Hom}\left(x_{k_{t}}^{(t-1)},x_{n}^{(t)}\right)=0
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.
.

We obviously have
\beta_{r}=\alpha_{i}=\boldsymbol{\dim}S_{i}=\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}
as
i
is a source in
Q
. Therefore Proposition
5.4
implies that the exceptional sequence
E^{(r)}
takes the form
(\Sigma_{i}x_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,\Sigma_{i}x_{n-1}^{(0)},I_{i})
where
\Sigma_{i}
denotes the BGP reflection functor at
i
. Note also that
x_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{(0)}
are the indecomposable projectives other than
P_{i}
, so denoting by
Q^{\prime}
the full subquiver of
Q
obtained by deleting the node
i
as well as all the arrows incident to
i
, one can view
(x_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{(0)})
as a complete exceptional sequence in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q^{\prime}
consisting of the indecomposable projectives. By induction on the number of vertices, we have a family
E^{\prime(r)},\ldots,E^{\prime(m)}
of complete exceptional sequences in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q^{\prime}
satisfying all the desired properties. Then for each
r<t\leq m
, we define
E^{(t)}
as the sequence whose
n-1
first objects are the images of those of
E^{\prime(t)}
under the reflection functor
\Sigma_{i}
, and whose
n
th object is the injective
I_{i}
. In the case
t=r
this procedure gives
E^{(r)}
by what has just been said before. We can already note that
E^{(r+1)},\ldots,E^{(m)}
are (complete) exceptional sequences, because the proof of Proposition
5.4
shows that each of them can be obtained from
E^{(r)}
by performing a sequences of braids involving only
\sigma_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,\sigma_{n-2}^{\pm}
(so
I_{i}
will always remain the last object of all of these sequences).

Let us now verify that putting together the sequences
E^{(0)},\ldots,E^{(m)}
, Properties (1)-(5) hold. Property (1) holds because by the induction assumption,
E^{\prime(r)}
consists of all the indecomposable injectives
I_{j},j\neq i
; as
\Sigma_{i}I_{j}\simeq I_{j}
in that case, it follows that
E^{(m)}
consists of all the indecomposable injectives of
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
. Property (3) immediately follows from the induction assumption together with Corollary
5.5
. Property (2) follows from the induction assumption if
r<t\leq t^{\prime}\leq m
and from Corollary
5.5
if
0\leq t\leq t^{\prime}\leq r
so that it remains only to check it if
t\leq r<t^{\prime}
. Keeping the notations of the previous Corollary, we need to show that
\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}\tau^{-1}x_{j}^{(0)}+\lambda_{j,t}\beta_{t},s_{i}\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle>0
where
Y
is an indecomposable object in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
supported on
Q^{\prime}
and such that
\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\geq\boldsymbol{\dim}\tau^{-1}x_{j}^{(0)}
. In particular
\langle Y,S_{i}\rangle=0
and
\langle\tau^{-1}x_{j}^{(0)},Y\rangle>0
. Using (
5.1
) and the fact that
\langle P,X\rangle
is non negative for any object
X
if
P
is projective, we get

\displaystyle\langle x_{j}^{(0)}+\lambda_{j,t}\beta_{t},s_{i}\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle

\displaystyle>\lambda_{j,t}\langle\beta_{t},s_{i}\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle\geq\lambda_{j,t}\langle\alpha_{i},s_{i}\boldsymbol{\dim}Y\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j,t}\langle\alpha_{i},\boldsymbol{\dim}Y-\langle S_{i},Y\rangle\alpha_{i}\rangle=0.

Finally Let us check Property (4). Keeping the same notations as in Proposition
5.4
, we first need to check that
x_{i}^{(t)}\not\simeq x_{i}^{(t^{\prime})}
for all
0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq r
. This is equivalent to showing that
\beta_{t}\neq\beta_{t^{\prime}}
if
0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq r
. We shall show that
\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\beta_{t}\rangle\leq 0
, which will in particular imply that
\beta_{t}\neq\beta_{t^{\prime}}
. Recall that as
t<t^{\prime}
we have
k_{t}>k_{t^{\prime}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\beta_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\alpha_{i}+\sum_{s>t}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle=1+\sum_{s>t}\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle=1+\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t^{\prime}}}^{(0)}\rangle+\sum_{t<s<t^{\prime}}\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle\leq 0

because
\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle=0
if
s\geq t^{\prime}
by Lemma
5.2
(1), and
\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{t^{\prime}}}^{(0)}\rangle=-1
and
\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle\leq 0
for each
t<s<t^{\prime}
, by Lemma
5.2
(2). Thus Property (4) holds at the vertex
i
. Let us now fix
1\leq s\leq r
and show that
x_{k_{s}}^{(t)}\not\simeq x_{k_{s}}^{(t^{\prime})}
is
t\leq s-1<t^{\prime}
(recall that by construction,
t_{i\rightarrow k_{s}}=s-1
for every
1\leq s\leq r
). First it is worth noting that in view of the property (1), it is enough to assume
r^{\prime}\leq r
. We then have
k_{t}<k_{s}\geq k_{t^{\prime}}
and thus by Proposition
5.4
:

\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(t)}=\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(t^{\prime})}=s_{\beta_{t^{\prime}}}\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}.

So we simply need to check that the Cartan pairing of
\beta^{\prime}
with
\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}
is non zero. As
k_{s}\geq k_{t^{\prime}}
, Lemma
5.2
(2) implies that
\langle x_{k_{s}}^{(0)},\beta_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=0
, while the other Euler form is easily seen to be negative:

\displaystyle\langle\beta_{t^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\dim}x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{i},x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle-\sum_{1\leq u\leq t^{\prime}}\langle x_{k_{u}}^{(0)},x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle\leq\langle x_{k_{s}}^{(0)},x_{k_{s}}^{(0)}\rangle=-1

because
s\leq t^{\prime}
. So we have shown that Property (4) holds for all arrows incident to
i
. The remaining cases depend only on the subquiver
Q^{\prime}
and hence follow by induction.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

6.
The category
\mathcal{R}_{Q}

In this section, we construct the category
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
that will serve as underlying category for the construction of exact complexes in the next section. The definition and properties of the objects and morphisms in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
crucially rely on the family of exceptional sequences provided by Theorem
5.1
. We also define an additive functor
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
allowing to go from
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
back to the hammock category defined in Section
4
.

6.1.
Inclusions of multisets

For each
i\in I
and
0\leq t<m
, we set

\pi_{i}:=\{x_{i}^{(s)},0\leq s<m\}\quad\pi_{i}^{\leq t}:=\{x_{i}^{(s)},0\leq s\leq t\}\quad\pi_{i}^{<t}:=\{x_{i}^{(s)},0\leq s<t\}

viewed as sets not multisets, i.e. the elements of
\pi_{i}
(resp.
\pi_{i}^{\leq t},\pi_{i}^{<t}
) are
pairwise distinct
. Here by convention
\pi_{i}^{<0}:=\emptyset
. Denoting
m_{i}:=\sharp\pi_{i}
we write
\pi_{i}:=\{x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,m_{i}}\}
. In particular by construction we have
x_{i,1}\simeq x_{i}^{(0)}\simeq x_{i}
and
x_{i,m_{i}}\simeq x_{i}^{(m-1)}\simeq Sx_{i}
. Recall the bijection
\alpha\in Q_{1}\mapsto t_{\alpha}\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
provided by Theorem
5.1
(3). For the sake of legibility of certain notations used below, it will be convenient to consider the quiver
\overline{Q}
whose set of vertices is
I\sqcup\{\ast\}
and whose set of arrows consist of that of
Q
together with one arrow
i\rightarrow\ast
for every
i
such that
i
is a sink in
Q
. We then define an integer
t_{\alpha}\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
for each arrow
\alpha
in
\overline{Q}
by setting

\forall\alpha\in\overline{Q}_{1},\kern 5.0ptt_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}t_{\alpha}&\text{if $\alpha\in Q_{1}$,}\\
m&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We then extend this notation to any
non lazy
path
p
in
\overline{Q}
by setting
t_{p}:=t_{i\rightarrow j}
if
p:=i\rightarrow j\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow k
with
k\in I\sqcup\{\ast\}
. In what follows
\mathrm{in}_{Q}(i)
(resp.
\mathrm{out}_{Q}(i)
) will stand for the set of all oriented paths in
\overline{Q}
arriving at
i
(resp. starting at
i
). We then introduce the following multisets:

(6.1)

\forall\alpha\in\overline{Q}_{1},\kern 5.0pt\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}:=\pi_{s(\alpha)}^{\leq t_{\alpha}}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathrm{in}(s(\alpha))\\
\text{$p$ non lazy}\end{subarray}}\pi_{s(p)}^{<t_{p}}.

Example 6.1
.

In the situation of Example

, we have for example

. Then taking for instance

\displaystyle\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}=\pi_{4}\sqcup\pi_{2}^{<t_{2\rightarrow 4}}\sqcup\pi_{3}^{<t_{3\rightarrow 4}}\sqcup\pi_{1}^{<t_{1\rightarrow 3}}\sqcup\pi_{1}^{<t_{1\rightarrow 2}}=\{P_{4},x,I_{4},P_{2},P_{1},P_{3},y,P_{1}\}.

Note that

appears twice in

, which is due to the fact there are two oriented paths from

to

in

(cf. Corollary
6.3
below).

The next proposition will be crucial in what follows.

Proposition 6.2
.

For every arrow
\alpha\in\overline{Q}_{1}
, the following inclusions of multisets hold:

\{x_{s(\alpha)}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(s(\alpha))}\overline{\pi}_{\beta}\kern 5.0pt\subseteq\kern 5.0pt\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}\kern 5.0pt\subseteq\kern 5.0pt\pi_{s(\alpha)}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathrm{in}(s(\alpha))\\
\text{$p$ non lazy}\end{subarray}}\pi_{s(p)}^{<t_{p}}\kern 5.0pt\subseteq\kern 5.0ptH(x_{s(\alpha)}).

Proof.

The middle inclusion is trivial. Let us then deal with the leftmost inclusion. Let us fix an arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
and denote
i:=s(\alpha)
. We can decompose the set of all non lazy paths arriving at
i
as those of length one i.e. the arrows of target
i
on the one hand, and the non lazy paths arriving at one of the sources of these arrows on the other hand. Consequently, the desired inclusion boils down to

\{x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)}\pi_{s(\beta)}^{\leq t_{\beta}}\subseteq\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\alpha}}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)}\pi_{s(\beta)}^{<t_{\beta}}.

Now for any arrow
\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\mathrm{in}(i)
, denoting
j:=s(\beta)
we have
t_{\beta}<t_{\alpha}
by Remark
5.6
so that
x_{i}^{(t_{\beta}+1)}\in\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\alpha}}
and moreover
x_{i}^{(t_{\beta}+1)}\simeq x_{j}^{(t_{\beta})}

by Theorem
5.1
(3). Note also that Theorem
5.1
(4) implies the two following things: firstly,
x_{j}^{(t_{\beta})}\not\simeq x_{j}^{(t_{\beta}+1)}
, which implies that
x_{j}^{(t_{\beta})}\sqcup\pi_{j}^{<t_{\beta}}=\pi_{j}^{\leq t_{\beta}}
;
secondly, the
x_{i}^{(t_{\beta}+1)},\beta\in Q_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)
are pairwise non isomorphic and also not isomorphic to
x_{i}^{(0)}\simeq x_{i}
, so that
\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\alpha}}\supseteq\{x_{i}\}\sqcup\{x_{i}^{(t_{\beta}+1)},\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)\}
.
Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\alpha}}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)}\pi_{s(\beta)}^{<t_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\supseteq\{x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)}\left(x_{i}^{(t_{\beta}+1)}\sqcup\pi_{s(\beta)}^{<t_{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\{x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)}\left(x_{s(\beta)}^{(t_{\beta})}\sqcup\pi_{s(\beta)}^{<t_{\beta}}\right)=\{x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\overline{Q}_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)}\pi_{s(\beta)}^{\leq t_{\beta}}.

We now move on to the rightmost inclusion. Still denoting
i:=s(\alpha)
, we prove the desired inclusion by induction on the total dimension of the indecomposable projective
\mathbf{k}Q
-module at
i
. The initial step of the induction corresponds to the case where
i
is a sink in
Q
(this number is then equal to
1
). In that case by construction there is a single arrow
\alpha:i\rightarrow\ast
in
\overline{Q}_{1}
and we have
\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}=\pi_{i}^{\leq m}=\{x_{i}^{(t)},0\leq t\leq m\}
. The fact that this set is included into
H(x_{i})
simply follows from Theorem
5.1
(2) with
t=0
and
t^{\prime}
running over
0,\ldots,m\}
. We now assume
i
is not a sink in
Q
and suppose the claimed inclusion holds for all vertices
j
whose indecomposable projective has a total dimension strictly less than that of
i
. In particular, the induction assumption can be applied to all vertices
j
such that there is an arrow from
i
to
j
in
Q
. Therefore, applying Proposition
4.11
we get

\displaystyle H(x_{i})\sqcup H(\tau^{-1}x_{i})

\displaystyle=\{x_{i},\Sigma x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{j\rightarrow i}H(x_{j})\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i\rightarrow k}H(\tau^{-1}x_{k})

\displaystyle\supseteq\{x_{i},\Sigma x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{j\rightarrow i}\left(\pi_{j}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathrm{in}(j)\\
\text{$p$ non lazy}\end{subarray}}\pi_{s(p)}^{<t_{p}}\right)\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i\rightarrow k}H(\tau^{-1}x_{k})

\displaystyle\supseteq\{x_{i},\Sigma x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathrm{in}(i)\\
\text{$p$ non lazy}\end{subarray}}\pi_{s(p)}^{<t_{p}}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i\rightarrow k}H(\tau^{-1}x_{k}).

Thus to conclude it suffices to prove that

\pi_{i}\sqcup H(\tau^{-1}x_{i})\subseteq\{x_{i},\Sigma x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i\rightarrow k}H(\tau^{-1}x_{k}).

First of all we can note that
x_{i}\simeq x_{i}^{(0)}\in\pi_{i}
and of course
x_{i}\notin H(\tau^{-1}x_{i})
hence the multiset on the left hand side contains exactly one copy of
x_{i}
. Similarly,
\Sigma x_{i}\notin\pi_{i}
(as it does not even belong to
\mathrm{mod}\,(\mathbf{k}Q)
) and appears exactly once in
H(\tau^{-1}x_{i})
as
\Sigma x_{i}\simeq S\tau^{-1}x_{i}
.
So we now fix
y\in\pi\sqcup H(\tau^{-1}x_{i})
and assume
y\not\simeq x_{i}
and
y\not\simeq\Sigma x_{i}
. In particular the Auslander-Reiten formula (equivalently the fact that
S=\tau\Sigma
is a Serre functor in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q)
) yields

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\tau^{-1}x_{i},y)\simeq D\mathrm{Hom}(y,x_{i})=0.

Consequently the number of times
y
may appear in the multiset of the left hand side is

\displaystyle\delta_{y\in\pi_{i}}+\dim\mathrm{Hom}(\tau^{-1}x_{i},y)

\displaystyle=\delta_{y\in\pi_{i}}+\langle\tau^{-1}x_{i},y\rangle=\delta_{y\in\pi_{i}}+\langle\tau^{-1}I_{i},y\rangle+\sum_{i\rightarrow k}\langle\tau^{-1}x_{k},y\rangle.

If
y\in\pi_{i}
, then
y\simeq x_{i}^{(t)}
with
t>0
because by assumption
y\not\simeq x_{i}\simeq x_{i}^{(0)}
. Hence Theorem
5.1
(2) together with the Auslander-Reiten formula implies

\langle\tau^{-1}I_{i},y\rangle=-\langle y,I_{i}\rangle=-\langle x_{i},y\rangle<0.

Therefore the number of times
y
appears on the left hand side is

1+\langle\tau^{-1}I_{i},y\rangle+\sum_{i\rightarrow k}\langle\tau^{-1}x_{k},y\rangle\leq\sum_{i\rightarrow k}\langle\tau^{-1}x_{k},y\rangle\leq\sum_{i\rightarrow k}\dim\mathrm{Hom}\left(\tau^{-1}x_{k},y\right)

which is the number of times
y
appears in the multiset
\bigsqcup_{i\rightarrow k}H(\tau^{-1}x_{k})
. If now
y\notin\pi_{i}
then
\delta_{y\in\pi_{i}}=0
while
\langle\tau^{-1}I_{i},y\rangle\leq\mathrm{\dim}\mathrm{Hom}(\tau^{-1}I_{i},y)=0
as
y\in H(\tau^{-1}x_{i})
and
y\not\simeq\tau^{-1}I_{i}\simeq\tau^{-1}Sx_{i}=S\tau^{-1}x_{i}=\Sigma x_{i}
. The conclusion thus follows identically as in the case
y\in\pi_{i}
. This concludes the proof of the proposition.

∎

We now give a couple of useful corollaries. Recall that if
\gamma:=\sum_{i}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
with
a_{i}\geq 0
for each

Z_{\gamma}
the multiset consisting of
a_{i}
copies of
x_{i}
for every

.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{Q_{1}}
and
i:=s(\alpha)
. Then
(\overline{\pi}_{\alpha})_{\mid\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Proj}\mathbf{k}Q)}=Z_{\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}}
.

Proof.

For each
j\in I
, the indecomposable projective
x_{j}
appears exactly
p(j,i)
times in the hammock multiset
H(x_{i})
. Consequently,
Z_{\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}}=H(x_{i})_{\mid\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Proj}\mathbf{k}Q)}
. Hence the inclusion
(\overline{\pi}_{\alpha})_{\mid\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Proj}\mathbf{k}Q)}\subseteq Z_{\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}}
follows from the right most inclusion of Proposition
6.2
. The converse inclusion is proved by induction on the total dimension of the indecomposable injective
\mathbf{k}Q
module at
i
. Namely, if
i
is a source in
Q
then the inclusion is trivial because
Z_{\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}}=\{x_{i}\}
while it follows from (
6.1
) that
\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}\supseteq\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\alpha}}\ni x_{i}^{(0)}\simeq x_{i}
. If
i
is not a source then we can write

Z_{\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}}=\{x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{j\rightarrow i}Z_{\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{j}}=\{x_{i}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\beta\in Q_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)}Z_{\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{s(\beta)}}

so the desired inclusion immediately follows from the induction assumption together with the leftmost inclusion of Proposition
6.2
.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s}
be arrows in
\overline{Q_{1}}
and let
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
be multisets having empty intsersection with
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Proj}\mathbf{k}Q)
. Assume
Z\sqcup\overline{\pi}_{\alpha_{1}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\overline{\pi}_{\alpha_{r}}=Z^{\prime}\sqcup\overline{\pi}_{\beta_{1}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\overline{\pi}_{\beta_{s}}
. Then
Z=Z^{\prime},r=s
and
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s}\}
.

Proof.

Taking the intersection with
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Proj}(\mathbf{k}Q))
, Corollary
6.3
yields
\sum_{k=1}^{r}\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{s(\alpha_{k})}=\sum_{l=1}^{s}\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{s(\beta_{l})}
. Thus it follows from the linear independence of the dimension vectors of the indecomposable injective
\mathbf{k}Q
-modules that
r=s
and (up to relabeling)
s(\alpha_{k})=s(\beta_{k}):=i_{k}
for each
1\leq k\leq r
. Let
i
(without loss of generality
i=i_{1}
) be a sink in the smallest (full) subquiver
Q^{\prime}
of
Q
containing
i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}
. Then it follows from the right most inclusion of Proposition
6.2
that the projective
x_{i}
appears exactly once in each
\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}
such that
s(\alpha)=i
and does not appear in of the
\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}
such that
s(\alpha)\neq i
. Thus we get
\overline{\pi}_{\alpha_{1}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\overline{\pi}_{\alpha_{r^{\prime}}}=\overline{\pi}_{\beta_{1}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\overline{\pi}_{\beta_{r^{\prime}}}
where all the arrows involved here have the same source
i
. From (
6.1
), this can be obviously rewritten as
\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\alpha_{1}}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\alpha_{r^{\prime}}}}=\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\beta_{1}}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\pi_{i}^{\leq t_{\beta_{r^{\prime}}}}
. Now it follows from the construction of the exceptional sequences given by Theorem
5.1
that
\pi_{i}^{t_{\alpha}}
is strictly contained in
\pi_{i}^{t_{\beta}}
if
s(\alpha)=s(\beta)=i
and
t_{\alpha}<t_{\beta}
. From this we see that
\max_{\alpha}t_{\alpha}=\max_{\beta}t_{\beta}
so that this equality of multisets can be simplified. Continuing inductively we get (up to relabeling)
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r^{\prime}}=\beta_{r^{\prime}}
. In other words we proved that
\alpha_{k}=\beta_{k}
for every

such that
s(\alpha_{k})=s(\beta_{k})=i
. Repeating the same arguments inductively, we conclude that
\alpha_{k}=\beta_{k}
for all

. The equality

then follows trivially.
∎

6.2.
The category
\mathcal{R}_{Q}

We now introduce the category that will serve as underlying category for the construction of exact chain complexes in the next section. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
denote the monoidal
\mathbf{k}
-category given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}:=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathrm{mod}\,\left(\mathbf{k}Q^{\otimes n}\right).

The category
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
is an abelian symmetric monoidal
\mathbf{k}
-category whose indecomposable objects are all the tensor products of the form
z_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}z_{r}
where
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
are indecomposable objects in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
. We outline on the fact that the tensor product is taken over the base field. We now fix an arbitrary total order on the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbf{k}Q
and introduce a distinguished family
\{M_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
of indecomposable objects in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
:

(6.2)

\forall\alpha\in Q_{1},\kern 5.0ptM_{\alpha}:=\overrightarrow{\bigotimes_{z\in\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}}}z

where the
\overline{\pi}_{\alpha},\alpha\in Q_{1}
are the multisets given by (
6.1
) and the arrow means that the tensor product is taken following the increasing order with respect to the chosen order
<
. Any object isomorphic to a tensor product of the form
M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{\alpha_{r}}
will be called a dominant object. On the other hand, an indecomposable object in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
of the form

will be called

if
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}\}\cap\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Proj\mathbf{k}Q})=\emptyset
.
We now define, for each
\alpha\in\overline{Q_{1}}
a distinguished morphism
\eta_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}}\left(M_{\alpha},\mu_{s(\alpha)}M_{\alpha}\right)
. Namely, writing
\pi^{\leq\alpha}:=\{x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,m}\}
, by Theorem
5.1
we can choose a non-trivial morphism
f_{k,\alpha}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}Q}(x_{i,k},x_{i,k+1})
for each
1\leq k<m
where
i:=s(\alpha)
, as well as a non trivial morphism
f_{m,\alpha}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}Q}(x_{i,m},Sx_{i})
. Then the pure tensor

\eta_{\alpha}:=(f_{1,\alpha}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{m,\alpha})\otimes\cdots

where the
\cdots
stand for tensor products of identity morphisms, defines a non trivial morphism in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}

\eta_{\alpha}^{M_{\alpha}}:\kern 5.0ptM_{\alpha}\simeq(x_{i,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i,m})\otimes\cdots\longrightarrow(x_{i,2}\otimes\cdots x_{i,m}\otimes Sx_{i})\otimes\cdots\simeq\mu_{i}M_{\alpha}.

In other terms, using the diagrammatic description from Example
4.9
, the morphism
\eta_{\alpha}^{M_{\alpha}}
can be represented as follows:
x_{i,1}
x_{i,2}
\cdots
\cdots
x_{i,m}
\cdots
Sx_{i,1}
x_{i,2}
\cdots
\cdots
x_{i,m}
\cdots
\cdots

We now define the category
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
as the smallest
\mathbf{k}
-linear monoidal subcategory of
\widetilde{\mathcal{R_{Q}}}
whose class of objects contains all the objects isomorphic to
M_{\alpha},\alpha\in\overline{Q}_{1}
as well as all the neutral objects, and whose class of morphisms contains the morphism
\eta_{\alpha}^{M_{\alpha}}
for every
\alpha\in\overline{Q_{1}}
as well as all possible braiding isomorphisms. In particular, if
X
is a neutral object then there is no (non zero, non iso) morphism of domain
X
in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{Q}_{1}
, let
j:=s(\alpha)
and denote by
M^{\prime}_{\alpha}
the codomain of
\eta_{\alpha}^{M_{\alpha}}
. Then we have
M^{\prime}_{\alpha}\simeq X\otimes\bigotimes_{\beta\in Q_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)}M_{\beta}
where
X
is a neutral object. Moreover, we have
\eta_{\beta}^{M^{\prime}_{\alpha}}\circ\eta_{\alpha}^{M_{\alpha}}=0
for every
\beta\in Q_{1}\cap\mathrm{in}(i)
.

Proof.

The first statement is a straightforward consequence of the leftmost inclusion of Proposition
6.2
. Concerning the second claim, let us denote by
i:=s(\beta)
and also
t:=t_{\beta}
; then when writing the desired composition as a pure tensor, then by Theorem
5.1
(3) and (4), one of the factors will be of the form
g_{l,\beta}\circ f_{k,\alpha}
with

x_{j}^{(t)}\xrightarrow{f_{k,\alpha}}x_{j}^{(t+1)}\simeq x_{i}^{(t)}\xrightarrow{g_{l,\beta}}x_{i}^{(t+1)}

which is zero by Theorem
5.1
(3).
∎

7.
Exact complexes and standard
q
-characters

This section contains the most important results of this paper. Relying on the materials from Sections
8
and
6
, we show the existence for each indecomposable object
M
in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
of a chain complex

satisfying strong homological properties that determine it uniquely up to homotopy. We then prove that (after a mild specialization), the Euler characteristics of the complexes in the hammock category obtained by applying the functor
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
to the

coincide with the (truncated)
q
-characters of standard modules in the HL category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
.

7.1.
Definitions and classical properties

We fix a
\mathbf{k}
-linear category
\mathcal{A}
together with a
\mathbf{k}
-linear subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{A}
. Let
C_{\bullet}
be a chain complex in
\mathcal{C}
, assumed to be of the form

C_{\bullet}:=\quad\cdots\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow C_{0}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow C_{m}\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\cdots\quad

For such a complex
C_{\bullet}
, the integer

will be called the length of
C_{\bullet}
.

Definition 7.1
.

We say that
C_{\bullet}
is left (resp. right)
\mathcal{C}
-exact if

is a weak cokernel of
d_{n-1}
in
\mathcal{C}
for every

(resp.

is a weak kernel of

in
\mathcal{C}
for every

). We say that

is
\mathcal{C}
-exact if it is both left and right
\mathcal{C}
-exact.

Note in particular that with this definition, if

is left (resp. right)

-exact then

(resp.

) is an epimorphism (resp. a monomorphism).
The following is a classical fact (cf. for example
[
27
]
).

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
and
D_{\bullet}
be left
\mathcal{C}
-exact chain complexes. Assume moreover that
C_{0}\simeq D_{0}
and
d_{0}^{C_{\bullet}}=d_{0}^{D_{\bullet}}
. Then
C_{\bullet}
and
D_{\bullet}
are isomorphic up to homotopy.

Definition 7.3
.

We say that

is
\mathcal{C}
-left almost split (resp.
\mathcal{C}
-right almost split) if

is
\mathcal{C}
-left (resp. right) exact and moreover
C_{\bullet}
does not split,
C_{0}
(resp.
C_{m}
) is indecomposable in
\mathcal{C}
and any morphism in
\mathcal{C}
of domain

(resp. of codomain

) that is not a split mono (resp. split epi) factors through

(resp.
d_{m-1}
) in
\mathcal{C}
. We say that
C_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{C}
-almost split if it is both left and right
\mathcal{C}
-almost split.

The following is a direct consequence of the above definitions.

Lemma 7.4
.

Assume
C_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{C}
-left exact and
D_{\bullet}
is an arbitrary chain complex in
\mathcal{C}
. Assume also that there are non trivial morphisms
u:C_{0}\rightarrow D_{0}
and
u_{1}:C_{1}\rightarrow D_{1}
in
\mathcal{C}
such that
d_{0}^{D_{\bullet}}u_{0}=u_{1}d_{0}^{C_{\bullet}}
. Then there is a non trivial morphism of chain complexes
u_{\bullet}:C_{\bullet}\rightarrow D_{\bullet}
with

.

Proof.

The composition
v_{1}:=d_{1}^{D_{\bullet}}u_{1}
is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}
of domain

, and

v_{1}d_{0}^{C_{\bullet}}=d_{1}^{D_{\bullet}}u_{1}d_{0}^{C_{\bullet}}=d_{1}^{D_{\bullet}}d_{0}^{D_{\bullet}}u_{0}=0.

Hence by left
\mathcal{C}
-exactness of
C_{\bullet}
,
v_{1}
factors through
d_{1}^{C_{\bullet}}
which gives a morphism
u_{2}:C_{2}\rightarrow D_{2}
making the obvious square commute. Continuing inductively, we obtain the desired morphism
u_{\bullet}:C_{\bullet}\rightarrow D_{\bullet}
, and
u_{\bullet}
is non trivial as
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
are.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
and

be two complexes such that

is left

-left exact and

-left almost split. Assume

does not factor through

-left exact.

Proof.

Let

, let us fix

and assume

so writing

the condition

f_{2}d_{n-2}^{D_{\bullet}}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{1}d_{n-1}^{C_{\bullet}}+f_{2}u_{n-1}=0.

If

, then by
\mathcal{C}
-left exactness of

, the first condition implies that

so plugging into the second condition we get

Thus by
\mathcal{C}
-left exactness of
C_{\bullet}
, we can write
f_{1}+f^{\prime}_{2}u_{n}=f^{\prime}_{1}d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}}
. All together we have

(f_{1}\kern 5.0ptf_{2})=(f^{\prime}_{1}\kern 5.0pt-f^{\prime}_{2})\begin{pmatrix}d_{n}^{C_{\bullet}}&0\\
u_{n}&-d_{n-1}^{D_{\bullet}}\end{pmatrix}

which shows that

. Assume now

, so that the first condition becomes empty as

does not factor through

\mathcal{C}
-left almost split, we can write

∎

We now assume that
\mathcal{A}
is endowed with a symmetric monoidal structure

7.2.

For each indecomposable object

\mathcal{R}_{Q}
,
\mathrm{id}_{T}\otimes u_{0}
does not factor through
\mathrm{id}_{T}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{M_{\alpha_{1}}}\otimes d_{0}^{C_{\bullet}}=\mathrm{id}_{T}\otimes d_{0}^{M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes C_{\bullet}}
. Hence by Lemma
7.5
, the complex
T\otimes\mathrm{Cone}(u_{\bullet}[-1])
is
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
-left exact i.e.
E_{\bullet}:=\mathrm{Cone}(u_{\bullet}[-1])
is
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
-purely left exact. Moreover we have
E_{n}\simeq(M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes C_{n})\oplus D_{n-1}
so that
E_{n}\simeq 0
if
n<0
and
E_{0}\simeq M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes C_{0}\simeq M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes M^{\prime}\simeq M
. Thus to conclude it only remains to check that
E_{\bullet}
is
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
-left almost split. But this follows from the fact that by the mapping cone construction,

d_{0}^{E_{\bullet}}=^{t}(\mathrm{id}_{M_{\alpha_{1}}}\otimes d_{0}^{C_{\bullet}}\quad u_{0})=^{t}(g_{1}\kern 5.0ptg_{2}\kern 5.0pt\ldots\kern 5.0ptg_{r})

where
g_{1}=\eta_{M_{\alpha_{1}}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{M_{\alpha_{2}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{id}_{M_{\alpha_{r}}}
, so that any non split mono in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
having domain
M
necessarily factors through
d_{0}^{E_{\bullet}}
. This shows the existence of the desired complex
C_{\bullet}(M)
, while the uniqueness up to homotopy follows from Lemma
7.2
. Note that all the arguments above were done with the notions of left exactness, left almost splitness etc but can be carried out in the same way with the dual notions replacing left by right. This concludes the proof of Theorem
7.7
.

7.3.
Standard
q
-characters as Euler characteristics

We conclude this section by constructing chain complexes in the hammock category
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
from those obtained in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
, and computing their Euler characteristics. This is done by applying a functor
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
that we now define, and whose existence is essentially a consequence of Proposition
6.2
. Let us consider
\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
the smallest full monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
containing all the objects (isomorphic to objects) of the chain complexes
C_{\bullet}(M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{\alpha_{l}})
for all collections
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
in
\overline{Q}_{1}
.

Proposition 7.8
.

There is an additive monoidal functor
\mathcal{D}_{Q}:\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{Q}
satisfying the following:

(1)

For each arrow
\alpha\in\overline{Q}_{1}
, we have
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M_{\alpha})=Y(x_{s(\alpha)})
.

(2)

The functor
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
commutes with Serre tiltings, i.e. If
M:=X\otimes M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots M_{\alpha_{r}}
is an (indecomposable) object in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
, then for each
1\leq k\leq r
, the image under
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
of the codomain of
\eta_{\alpha_{k}}^{M}
is the Serre tilting
\mu_{s(\alpha_{k})}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)
in
\mathcal{H}_{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{Q}_{1}
, denote
i:=s(\alpha)
and let us write
M_{\alpha}:=z_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes z_{l}
where
z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}
are the elements of
\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}
. Let us denote by
M^{\prime}_{\alpha}
the codomain of
\eta_{\alpha}^{M_{\alpha}}
. By construction,
M^{\prime}_{\alpha}\simeq z^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes z^{\prime}_{l}
where
\{z^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,z^{\prime}_{l}\}=\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}\setminus\{x_{i}\}\sqcup\{Sx_{i}\}
. Moreover by Proposition
6.2
, we have
\overline{\pi}_{\alpha}\subseteq H(x_{i})
. Combining these two facts, we see that
\eta_{\alpha}^{M_{\alpha}}
can be naturally viewed as an element in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}}(Y(x_{i}),\mu_{i}Y(x_{i}))
. In other words, we obtain an (injective) linear map

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{R}_{Q}}(M_{\alpha},M^{\prime}_{\alpha})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}}\left(Y(x_{i}),\mu_{i}Y(x_{i})\right).

Now this argument can be applied inductively to every indecomposable object in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
, as these are all (up to isomorphism) of the form
X\otimes M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{\alpha_{r}}
.
∎

We can now establish the second main result of this paper.

Theorem 7.9
.

Assume there exists a height function
\xi:I\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
compatible with
Q
, i.e.
\xi(j)=\xi(i)-1
if there is an arrow
i\rightarrow j
in
Q
. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
in
\overline{Q}_{1}

and
M:=M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{\alpha_{r}}
. Let also
\mathfrak{m}
denote the dominant monomial given by

. Then we have

\chi\left(\mathcal{D}_{Q}(C_{\bullet}(M)\right)_{\mid F_{i}:=1}={\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(\Delta(\mathfrak{m})).

Proof.

The proof goes by induction on
\boldsymbol{d}(M)
. The initial step corresponds to
\boldsymbol{d}(M)=\alpha_{i}
which is possible only if
r=1
i.e.

with

is a source in

. In that case the complex
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(C_{\bullet}(M))
is of the form

so by Proposition

its Euler characteristics is given by

\chi\left(\mathcal{D}_{Q}(C_{\bullet}(M)\right)=Y_{x_{i}}-F_{i}Y_{\tau^{-1}x_{i}}^{-1}\prod_{i\rightarrow j}Y_{\tau^{-1}x_{j}}=Y_{x_{i}}(1-F_{i}A_{i}^{-1}).

On the other hand the truncated
q
-character of the fundamental representation

, the desired identity holds in that case. Now by construction if
\boldsymbol{d}(M)
is not a simple root then

is isomorphic to the (shifted) mapping cone of a morphism of chain complexes

M_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes C_{\bullet}(M^{\prime})\kern 5.0pt\longrightarrow\kern 5.0ptC_{\bullet}(M^{\prime}_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes M^{\prime})

where

8.
Cluster characters as Euler characteristics

The purpose of this section is to show that the methods proposed in this paper can be used not only to recover the
q
-characters of standard modules in the HL categories, but also (at least some) simple modules. Though we will leave a more systematic investigation of this problem for future works, we show here an instance of this when
Q
is an arbitrary orientation of a type
A_{n}
Dynkin graph. Let us choose a height function
\xi I\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
adapted to
Q
. In view of the monoidal categorification results due to Brito-Chari
[
4
]
and Kashiwara-Kim-Oh-Park
[
28
]
, we know that all (the classes of) simple modules in the HL category
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{(1)}
are cluster monomials, and that their truncated
q
-characters can be identified (up to a mild change of variables) with their cluster expansion with respect to an appropriately chosen initial seed. Therefore in what follows we will essentially work exclusively using the language of cluster algebras and will not rely on any representation-theoretic tool throughout the proofs. Let us begin with a brief reminder of the combinatorics of finite type cluster algebras.

8.1.
Identities between
F
-polynomials in cluster algebras of type
A_{n}

Given a Dynkin quiver
Q
(in this section, we will mostly focus on the case where
Q
is of type
A_{n},n\geq 1
), we will be denoting by
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
the cluster algebra with initial seed

((u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}),Q)
.
It follows from Fomin-Zelevinsky’s results
[
13
]
that
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
is a finite type cluster algebra, i.e.
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
contains finitely many clusters (equivalently finitely many cluster variables). Consequently, the non-frozen cluster variables in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
are in bijection with the set of almost positive roots which is defined as
\Delta_{+}\cup\Pi_{-}
. We will denote by
u[\beta]
the cluster variable corresponding to
\beta
under this bijection, for each almost positive root
\beta
. In particular, the cluster variables
u[-\alpha_{i}],i\in I
respectively coincide with the cluster variables
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
belonging to the initial cluster. Fomin-Zelevinsky
[
14
]
proved that the cluster expansion of
u[\beta]
with respect to the initial cluster can be written under the form

x[\beta]=\mathbf{x}^{g_{\beta}}\cdot F[\beta](\widehat{y_{1}},\ldots,\widehat{y_{n}})

where
g_{\beta}:=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
is an integer-valued vector called the
g
-vector (here
\mathbf{u}^{g}:=u_{1}^{g_{1}}\cdots u_{n}^{g_{n}}
) and
F[\beta]
is a polynomial in the variables
\widehat{y}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{y}_{n}
which are defined by

\widehat{y}_{i}=\prod_{i\rightarrow j}u_{j}\cdot\prod_{i\leftarrow j}u_{j}^{-1}.

The polynomial
F[\beta]
is called the
F
-polynomial of
u[\beta]
. It is known that
F[\beta]
has constant term
1
, and that its highest degree term is given by
\mathbf{\widehat{y}}^{\beta}:=\widehat{y}_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\widehat{y}_{n}^{a_{n}}
where
\beta:=\sum_{i}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Assume
Q
is an arbitrary orientation of a type
A_{n}
Dynkin diagram, with
n\geq 1
. Let
\beta\in\Delta_{+}
and
i
be a sink in
Q_{\beta}
. Then we have
F[\beta]=F[\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}]+\widehat{y_{i}}F[\beta-\alpha_{i}]
.

Proof.

Let us choose a triangulation of a regular
2n
-gon corresponding to the chosen orientation
Q
. The fact that
i
belongs to
(Q_{\beta})_{0}
tells us that that the diagonal corresponding to the negative simple root
-\alpha_{i}
crosses the diagonal corresponding to the positive root
\beta
. Hence they are not compatible, and thus the Ptolemy relation

u[\beta]u[-\alpha_{i}]=M_{1}+M_{2}

is an exchange relation corresponding to a cluster mutation in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
. Let us now describe
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
explicitly. Let us write
\beta
as a segment
[k,l]
with
k\leq i\leq l
. Also let us denote by
(a,b)
(resp.
(c,d)
) the diagonals respectively corresponding to
u[\beta]
and
u[-\alpha_{i}]
. It is convenient to distinguish two cases, whether
i
is strictly inside
[k,l]
or on the border.

In the former case, as
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
, the situation looks like

k\cdots\leftarrow k^{\prime}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow i\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow l^{\prime}\rightarrow\cdots l.

We see that (up to renaming)
(a,d)
crosses the same diagonals corresponding to the negative simple roots as
(a,b)
except the one corresponding to
u[-\alpha_{i}]
(i.e.
(c,d)
), while
(b,c)
is an edge of the
2n
-gon. Thus
u[a,d]u[b,c]=u[\beta-\alpha_{i}]
. On the other hand, we see that
(b,d)
(resp.
(a,c)
) crosses the diagonals corresponding to
u[\alpha_{p}],k\leq p<k^{\prime}
(resp.
l^{\prime}<p\leq l
) so that
u[b,d]u[a,c]=u[\alpha_{k}+\cdots\alpha_{k^{\prime}-1}]u[\alpha_{l^{\prime}+1}+\cdots\alpha_{l}]
. But these two roots being obviously compatible, this product is a cluster monomial, whose denominator vector is given by

\beta-(\alpha_{k^{\prime}}+\cdots+\alpha_{l^{\prime}})=\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}.

In the case where
i
is on the border of
[k,l]
(without loss of generality
i=l
) the situation looks like

k\cdots\leftarrow k^{\prime}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow i.

We can repeat the same arguments as in the previous case. The only difference is if
i
is not a sink in
Q
, i.e. there is an arrow
i\rightarrow i+1
in
Q
; then
M_{1}
will be given by
u[-\alpha_{i+1}]u[\beta-\alpha_{i}]
(which is a cluster monomial as
-\alpha_{i+1}
and
\beta-\alpha_{i}
are compatible). Summarizing the two cases, this exchange relation can be written uniformly as

(8.1)

u[\beta]u[-\alpha_{i}]=u[\beta^{\prime}]+\prod_{i\rightarrow j}u_{j}\cdot u[\beta-\alpha_{i}]

where
\beta^{\prime}:=\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}
and the second term on the right hand side is a cluster monomial because
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
. Let us write
u[\gamma]=\mathbf{u}^{g_{\gamma}}F[\gamma]
for each root
\gamma
. As
\beta-\alpha_{i}\geq\beta^{\prime}
, the term of highest degree in the
\widehat{y}_{l}
on the right hand side comes from
F[\beta-\alpha_{i}]
. Hence comparing with that coming from the left hand side, we get

\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta}}\mathbf{\widehat{y}}^{\beta}u_{i}=\prod_{i\rightarrow j}u_{j}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta-\alpha_{i}}}\mathbf{\widehat{y}}^{\beta-\alpha_{i}}

which yields

\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta}}u_{i}\widehat{y_{i}}=\prod_{i\rightarrow j}u_{j}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta-\alpha_{i}}}.

Thus, dividing (
8.1
) by
\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta}}u_{i}
we get

F[\beta]=\frac{\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta^{\prime}}}}{\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta}}u_{i}}F[\beta^{\prime}]+\widehat{y_{i}}F[\beta-\alpha_{i}].

Now recalling that the constant term of all
F
-polynomials are
1
, this identity also implies

1=\frac{\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta^{\prime}}}}{\mathbf{u}^{g_{\beta}}u_{i}}

so in conclusion we obtain

F[\beta]=F[\beta^{\prime}]+\widehat{y_{i}}F[\beta-\alpha_{i}].

∎

8.2.
Combinatorial preliminaries

This subsection provides necessary technicalities that will be needed to prove the last main result of this paper. Note that all the results in this subsection hold for any acyclic quiver without multiple edges.
Recall that for any
\gamma:=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
with
a_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in I
, we will denote by
Z_{\gamma}
the multiset containing
a_{i}
copies of

for each

, i.e.
Z_{\gamma}:=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\{x_{i}\}^{\sqcup a_{i}}
. We now associate to any quasi-additive function

on

an element

as follows. For each vertex

, we define a non-negative integer

by induction on the total dimension of the indecomposable projective

-module at

. Namely, we set

and

\forall i\in Q_{0},\kern 5.0pta_{i}:=\max\left(0,\widetilde{h}(x_{i})-\widetilde{h}(\tau^{-1}x_{i})+\sum_{i\rightarrow j}a_{j}\right).

We then set

\boldsymbol{\omega}(h):=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\alpha_{i}\qquad Z(h):=Z_{\boldsymbol{\omega}(h)}.

We will sometimes refer to

.
Recall the notations

from Section

.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let

be a dominant quasi-additive function on

\forall i\in(Q_{\beta})_{0},\kern 5.0pt\beta-\boldsymbol{\dim}I_{i}^{\beta}\leq\boldsymbol{\omega}\left(h+h_{\tau^{-1}x_{i}}\right)\leq\beta-\alpha_{i}.

Moreover the left most inequality is an equality if there is at most one oriented path between any two vertices in
Q
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\beta=\sum_{i}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
and
c_{k}:=\widetilde{h}(x_{k}),d_{k}:=\widetilde{h}(\tau^{-1}x_{k})
for every
k\in I
. Let

and
\boldsymbol{\omega}(h^{\prime}):=\sum_{k}a^{\prime}_{k}\alpha_{k}
. Note that
\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(x_{k})=c_{k}
and
\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau^{-1}x_{k})=d_{k}+\delta_{i,k}
for all

. We prove by induction on

that
a_{k}-p_{Q_{\beta}}(k,i)\leq a^{\prime}_{k}\leq a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}
for all
k\in I
. If

p_{Q_{\beta}}(k,i)=\delta_{i,k}
so we need to prove that
a^{\prime}_{k}=a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}
. By definition we have
a^{\prime}_{k}=\max(0,c_{k}-d_{k}-\delta_{i,k})
. If
k\neq i
then we get
a^{\prime}_{k}=a_{k}
. If
k=i
then as

we have
0<a_{i}=c_{i}-d_{i}
(as
i=k
is a sink) and hence
a^{\prime}_{k}=a_{i}-1
. Thus the claim holds in that case. Let us fix
k\in I
and assume the desired inequalities hold for all

such that

. Then we have

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{k}-d_{k}-\delta_{i,k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a^{\prime}_{j}\right).

If
k\notin(Q_{\beta})_{0}
then in particular
k\neq i
and moreover
a_{k}=0
so
c_{k}-d_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}\leq 0
. Hence using the induction assumption we get that

c_{k}-d_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a^{\prime}_{j}\leq c_{k}-d_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}(a_{j}-\delta_{i,j})\leq c_{k}-d_{k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}\leq 0

and hence
a^{\prime}_{k}=0
. So we get
a_{k}-p_{Q_{\beta}}(k,i)=-p_{Q_{\beta}}(k,i)=\leq 0=a^{\prime}_{k}\leq a_{k}=a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}
as desired. If on the other hand
k\in(Q_{\beta})_{0}
then
a_{k}>0
so in particular
a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}\geq 0
and moreover using the induction assumption we have

a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}=c_{k}-d_{k}-\delta_{i,k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}\geq c_{k}-d_{k}-\delta_{i,k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a^{\prime}_{j}.

Therefore
a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}\geq a^{\prime}_{k}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle\geq c_{k}-d_{k}-\delta_{i,k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle\geq c_{k}-d_{k}-\delta_{i,k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}(a_{j}-p_{Q_{\beta}}(j,i))

\displaystyle=a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}-\sum_{k\rightarrow j}p_{Q_{\beta}}(j,i)=a_{k}-p_{Q_{\beta}}(k,i).

Thus we get the desired inequalities. If now we furthermore assume that there is at most one oriented path between any two vertices in
Q
, then in the proof above we can use the fact that if
j\in(Q_{\beta})_{0}
i.e.
a_{j}>0
then
a_{j}-p_{Q_{\beta}}(j,i)\geq 0
so in the induction we can plug-in
a^{\prime}_{j}=a_{j}-p_{Q_{\beta}}(j,i)
and conclude using the fact that
p_{Q_{\beta}}(k,i)=\delta_{i,k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}p_{Q_{\beta}}(j,i)
.
This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
h
be a dominant quasi-additive function, let
\beta:=\boldsymbol{\omega}(h)
and let
i
be a sink in
Q_{\beta}
. Then we have
\boldsymbol{\omega}(h+h_{\tau^{-1}x_{i}}-\delta_{x_{i}})=\beta-\alpha_{i}
.

Proof.

Denote
c_{k}:=\widetilde{h}(x_{k})
and
d_{k}:=\widetilde{h}(\tau^{-1}x_{k})
for every
k\in I
. Let also denote
h^{\prime}:=h+h_{\tau^{-1}x_{i}}-\delta_{x_{i}}
. As
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
, one can check by a straightforward induction that
c_{j}=d_{j}=0
for all
j
such that
i\rightsquigarrow j
. Thus we have
0<a_{i}=c_{i}-d_{i}
so that
c_{i}>d_{i}\geq 0
. Consequently, we can write
h=h_{x_{i}}+f
and thus applying Lemma
4.4
we get

h^{\prime}=f+\sum_{k\rightarrow i}h_{x_{k}}+\sum_{i\rightarrow k}h_{\tau^{-1}x_{k}}

so that
where

c^{\prime}_{k}:=\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(x_{k})=c_{k}-\delta_{i,k}+\delta_{i\leftarrow k}\qquad d^{\prime}_{k}:=\widetilde{h^{\prime}}(\tau^{-1}x_{k})=d_{k}+\delta_{i\rightarrow k}

as there is an arrow
x_{i}\rightarrow x_{k}
(resp.
x_{i}\rightarrow\tau^{-1}x_{k}
) in
\mathbb{Z}Q
if
i\leftarrow k
(resp.
i\rightarrow k
) in
Q
. Note that
h^{\prime}
is dominant. We set
\boldsymbol{\omega}(h^{\prime}):=\sum_{k}a^{\prime}_{k}\alpha_{k}
and we prove by induction on
r_{Q}(k)
that
a^{\prime}_{k}=a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}
for all
k\in I
. If
r_{Q}(k)=0
i.e.
k
is a sink in
Q
then

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}_{k}=c_{k}-\delta_{i,k}+\delta_{i\leftarrow k}=c_{k}-\delta_{i,k}=a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}

as
k
is a sink in
Q
. If
k\in I
is such that the claim holds for all
j
with
r_{Q}(j)<r_{Q}(k)
then

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{k}-d_{k}-\delta_{i,k}+\delta_{i\leftarrow k}-\delta_{i\rightarrow k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}(a_{j}-\delta_{i,j})\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,c_{k}-d_{k}-\delta_{i,k}-\delta_{i\rightarrow k}+\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}\right)

so that
a^{\prime}_{k}=a_{k}-\delta_{i,k}
if there is no arrow from
i
to
k
in
Q
. If on the contrary we have
i\rightarrow k
then in particular
k\notin(Q_{\beta})_{0}
as
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
, and hence necessarily
a_{k}=0
which means that
c_{k}-d_{k}-\sum_{k\rightarrow j}a_{j}\leq 0
and thus we get
a^{\prime}_{k}=0
. Thus we have
\boldsymbol{\omega}(h^{\prime})=\beta-\alpha_{i}
as desired.
∎

8.3.
Cluster variables of type
A_{n}
as Euler characteristics

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
\beta\in\Delta_{+}
and
h
be a dominant quasi-additive function such that
\boldsymbol{\omega}(h)=\beta
. Then there exists a chain complex
C_{\bullet}(h)\in\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{H}_{Q})
of length
|\beta|

whose renormalized Euler characteristics coincides with the
F
-polynomial
F[\beta]
.

Proof.

Let us first outline the strategy of the proof. Recall the chain complex
C_{\bullet}(N)
given by Theorem
7.7
for each object
N
in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
. We begin by establishing the following claim. Let
h:=\sum_{k}(c_{k}h_{x_{k}}+d_{k}h_{\tau^{-1}x_{k}}
) be a dominant quasi-additive function. Then for every
M\in\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)=Y(x_{1})^{\otimes c_{1}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{Q}}Y(x_{n})^{\otimes c_{n}}
, there exists a chain complex
C_{\bullet}(M,h)
such that
C_{0}(M,h)=M
and such that for any
Z\subseteq\bigsqcup_{k}\{x_{k}\}^{\sqcup\min(c_{k},d_{k})}
, there is a morphism
C_{\bullet}(M,h)\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N)[1-d]
where
N
is such that
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)=\mu_{Z}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)
and
d:=\sharp Z
.

Assuming the claim holds, we will then show that the image of
C_{\bullet}(M,h)
under the functor
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
satisfies all the desired properties.

Let us first prove the claim. We proceed by induction on both
d(M)
and
\beta:=\boldsymbol{\omega}(h)
. If
\beta=0
then it suffices to define
C_{\bullet}(M,h):=M\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots
. If
\beta\neq 0
, then let
i
be a sink in
Q_{\beta}
. Note that as
i
is a sink in
Q_{\beta}
, we have
0<a_{i}=c_{i}-d_{i}
. Then denoting
h^{\prime}:=h+h_{\tau^{-1}x_{i}}
, we still have
h^{\prime}\sim f
and
\min(c^{\prime}_{i},d^{\prime}_{i})\geq 1
. As
\boldsymbol{\omega}(h^{\prime})<\boldsymbol{\omega}(h)
by Lemma
8.2
the induction assumption with
h^{\prime}
and
Z:=\{x_{i}\}
, yields a morphism
C_{\bullet}(M,h^{\prime})\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N)
where as above
N\in\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
is such that
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)=\mu_{i}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)
. Note that
d(N)=d(M)-\alpha_{i}
so we can apply the induction assumption with
N
and
g:=h^{\prime}-\delta_{x_{i}}
. Indeed,
g\sim f-\delta_{x_{i}}
while by
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)=\mu_{i}Y(f)
. The induction assumption provides a chain complex
C_{\bullet}(N,g)
whose degree
0
object is
N
. Thus Lemma
7.4
yields a morphism

C_{\bullet}(N)\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N,g)
. We then construct
C_{\bullet}(M,h)
as the mapping cone of the composition of the two previous morphisms:

C_{\bullet}(M,h):=\mathrm{Cone}\left(C_{\bullet}(M,h^{\prime})[-1]\kern 5.0pt\longrightarrow\kern 5.0ptC_{\bullet}(N,g)[-1]\right).

It remains to check that
C_{\bullet}(M,h)
satisfies the property stated in the claim. Obviously,
C_{0}(M,h)=C_{0}(M,h^{\prime})=M
.
Thus, let us take
Z\subseteq\bigsqcup_{k}\{x_{k}\}^{\sqcup\min(c_{k},d_{k})}
arbitrary. If
d_{i}\geq c_{i}
then the induction assumption gives us a morphism

C_{\bullet}(M,h^{\prime})\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N)

so composing with the morphism
C_{\bullet}(M,h)\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(M,h^{\prime})
coming from the definition of
C_{\bullet}(M,h)
we get the desired conclusion. If on the other hand
d_{i}<c_{i}
then the induction assumption applied with
h^{\prime}
and
Z^{\prime}:=Z\sqcup\{x_{i}\}
gives a morphism

C_{\bullet}(M,h^{\prime})\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N^{\prime})[1-(d+1)]=C_{\bullet}(N^{\prime})[-d]

where
N^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
is such that
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N^{\prime})=\mu_{Z^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)
. But looking at the construction of the complexes
C_{\bullet}(M)
carried out in the previous section, we may find an object
N\in\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
such that
C_{\bullet}(N)
is constructed as
C_{\bullet}(N)=\mathrm{Cone}(\cdots\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N^{\prime})[-1])
with
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N^{\prime})=\mu_{i}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)
. Thus there is a morphism
C_{\bullet}(N^{\prime})\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N)[1]
in
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime})
. All together we have

C_{\bullet}(M,h)\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(M,h^{\prime})\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N^{\prime})[-d]\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(N)[-d+1]

with
d:=\sharp Z
and
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)=\mu_{Z}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)
given that

\mu_{i}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)=\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N^{\prime})=\mu_{Z^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)=\mu_{i}\mu_{Z}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M).

This concludes the proof of the claim.

We can now finish the proof of Theorem
8.4
. We set

C_{\bullet}(h):=\mathcal{D}_{Q}(C_{\bullet}(M,h))

where
M
is an object in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}^{\prime}
chosen as in the claim. In particular if
\beta=0
then
C_{\bullet}(h):=Y(h)\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots
so its renormalized Euler characteristics is
1
.
Now if
\beta\neq 0
then by construction of
C_{\bullet}(M,h)
we get a distinguished triangle in
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{H}_{Q})
:

\mathcal{D_{Q}}(C_{\bullet}(N,g))[-1]\longrightarrow C_{\bullet}(h)\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{Q}(C_{\bullet}(M,h^{\prime}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{D_{Q}}(C_{\bullet}(N,g)).

Taking the Euler characteristics we get using the induction assumption:

\chi(C_{\bullet}(h))=[\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)]F[\beta^{\prime}]-[\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)]F[\beta-\alpha_{i}].

Now recall that
\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)=\mu_{i}\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)
, so by Proposition
4.11
(cf. also Remark
4.12
) we have

[\mathcal{D}_{Q}(N)]=F_{i}\cdot A_{i}^{-1}\cdot[\mathcal{D}_{Q}(M)]

which allows to rewrite the above identity as

\chi^{ren}(C_{\bullet}(h))_{\mid F_{i}:=-1}=F[\beta^{\prime}]+A_{i}^{-1}F[\beta-\alpha_{i}].

Thus, up to identifying
A_{i}^{-1}
with
\widehat{y}_{i}
(which is standard from
[
22
,
24
]
), we obtain the desired equality, as the relations obtained in Lemma
8.1
together with
F[0]=1
determine the
F[\beta]
uniquely.
∎
