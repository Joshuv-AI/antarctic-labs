ABSTRACT
 The ZPPR-15 experiments [1] were mockups of a 330 MWe Integral Fast Reactor (IFR).
The ZPPR-15 assembly consisted of a clean, two zone, approximately circular core surrounded
by a thin depleted uranium (DU) blanket with sodium (Na) cooling and a thick stainless steel
reflector. The ZPPR-15 program was conducted in four phases: A, B, C, and D. Each phase was
marked by a particular composition of the reference assembly, with the last three being
representative of the three stages of the IFR fuel cycle.
 This report documents all MCNP [2] runs of the ZPPR-15 loadings that were included in
the ZPRD database on GitLab. In the present work, eigenvalues and associated standard
deviations were computed for each ZPPR-15 loading with the use of the three data libraries
(ENDF/B-VII.0 [3], ENDF/B-VII.1 [4] and ENDF/B-VIII.0 [5]). Relevant results from the
obtained outputs are also discussed in this report with two main objectives:

 a) provide and evaluate updated calculated values with respect to previous reports,
notably Refs. [6] and [7] that were based on the use of the ENDF/B-VII.0 library.

 b) address any change in the observed reactivity effects relevant to the analysis of the
experimental data when a different data library is used (previously reported results were mostly
based on the use of ENDF/B-VII.0 data only).

1. Introduction
 The ZPPR-15 experiments [1] were mockups of a 330 MWe Integral Fast Reactor (IFR).
The ZPPR-15 assembly consisted of a clean, two zone, approximately circular core surrounded
by a thin depleted uranium (DU) blanket with sodium (Na) cooling and a thick stainless steel
reflector. The ZPPR-15 program was conducted in four phases: A, B, C, and D. Each phase was
marked by a particular composition of the reference assembly, with the last three being
representative of the three stages of the IFR fuel cycle.
 This report documents all MCNP [2] runs of the ZPPR-15 loadings that were included in
the ZPRD database on GitLab. In the present work, eigenvalues and associated standard
deviations were computed for each ZPPR-15 loading with the use of the three data libraries
(ENDF/B-VII.0 [3], ENDF/B-VII.1 [4] and ENDF/B-VIII.0 [5]). Relevant results from the
obtained outputs are also discussed in this report. Section 2 discusses the details of the MCNP
execution (code version, library data, statistical uncertainty, modifications/corrections of original
input decks, etc.) of all input files associated with the ZPPR-15 loadings included in the ZPRD
database on GitLab. Section 3 shows the calculated keff values and associated standard deviations
for all ZPPR-15 loadings along with relevant reactivity effects due to the use of different library
data. The impact on computed experimental uncertainties due to the new obtained results is also
discussed in Section 3. Section 4 presents how the results of previous reports based of the use of
ENDF/B-VII.0 would be updated for consistency with the new MCNP output files uploaded in
GitLab and how those results would be affected by the use of a different data library. Conclusions
are given in Section 5.

2. ZPRD MCNP Execution of ZPPR-15 Loadings
 All MCNP runs were executed on the VTR cluster at ANL with version 6.2 of MCNP and
three data libraries: ENDF/B-VII.0, ENDF/B-VII.1 and ENDF/B-VIII.0. The ENDF/B-VIII.0
library data were initially missing on the cluster and were properly installed for this work. For
this, the ENDF/B-VIII.0 cross-section and s(alpha,beta) data files (“ACE” files) that were
released by LANL in 2018 were downloaded directly from the following webpages:

https://nucleardata.lanl.gov/ace/lib80x
https://nucleardata.lanl.gov/ace/endf80sab2

These were installed in the approved shared location (“/software/mcnp62/MCNP_DATA/xdata”
folder) on the VTR cluster (in the “Lib80x” and “ENDF80SaB2” subfolders, respectively). The
location paths of these data files were consistently added to the MCNP6.2 XSDIR file (the official
ASCII text file that acts as a directory or index for MCNP's nuclear cross-section data), which
was renamed as "xsdir_mcnp6.2_endfb.viii.0.add" and placed in the
“/software/mcnp62/MCNP_DATA” folder.

 In the original ENDF/B-VIII.0 data released in 2018, some inconsistencies were
successively found in 2022 in particular with regard to the B-10 total and elastic cross section
data. The details of these inconsistencies can be found in https://www.nndc.bnl.gov/endf-
b8.0/errata.html. The corrected B-10 cross-section data were downloaded from the same LANL
webpage (https://nucleardata.lanl.gov/ace/lib80x) given above and uploaded in the designated
location on the VTR cluster (“/software/mcnp62/MCNP_DATA/xdata/B-10-Lib80x” subfolder).

 It is noted that with the implemented corrections, the “conventional” library identifier for
the B-10 cross-sections (.00c to .06c depending on the temperature) can no longer be used in the
MCNP6.2 input decks that are executed on the VTR cluster. Therefore, in all MCNP inputs of the
ZPPR-15 loadings based on the use of the ENDF/B-VIII.0 library, the designator of the B-10
cross-section data at room temperature was modified from “5010.00c” to “5010.20c” at any
occurrence. The MCNP6.2 inputs and outputs uploaded in GitLab are consistent with this change.

 For the MCNP6.2 execution of the ZPPR-15 loadings, the following “kcode”
specifications (number of source histories per cycle, initial keff guess, number of cycles to be
disregarded, total number of cycles, number of source points for which to allocate storage [2])
were adopted across all input decks:
        kcode    1000000    0.990000   100     1100       9900000

The total number (1,000,000,000) of active histories was chosen to reach a one sigma standard
deviation of ~2 pcm on the obtained eigenvalue across all cases. This was selected as a
compromise between a good result accuracy and the computation times. Having small standard
deviations is particularly useful when reactivity variations associated with the computed
eigenvalues have to be calculated. If the standard deviation on the computed eigenvalues is 2
pcm, the derived error on the associated Δρ would be ~3 pcm.

 It is also noted that in the case of a single loading (ZPPR-15B L90) an error was identified
in the original input decks consisting in duplicate numbers of some geometry cells. With more
details, the cell numbers “10001” to “10007” of those input files appear twice in the geometry
data causing the MCNP execution to fail because of “fatal errors”, such as:
 fatal error. data symbol 10001 is used more than once.

3.1 Impact on Computed Eigenvalues due to Different Data Libraries

Table 2 shows a summary of the observed changes in the eigenvalue results obtained with the use
of the ENDF/B-VII.1 and ENDF/B-VIII.0 library data with respect to ENDF/B-VII.0. The choice
to express the keff variation with respect to ENDF/B-VII.0 is not dictated by the selection of a
refence library. ENDF/B-VII.0 is the library that was mostly used for previous published results,
therefore Table 2 is meant to address the changes that to some extent would have been observed
if a different data library was used in previous reports. In Table 2 the observed changes due to the
data libraries are expressed separately for each ZPPR-15 loading phase (A, B, C and D) by
providing the average, minimum and maximum variation across all assemblies in that phase. A
graphical view of the observed change for each single assembly of each phase is provided in
Figure 1 to Figure 8 (note that these figures are a graphical representation of the numerical
variation expressed in the last two columns of Table 1).

The use of both ENDF/B-VII.1 and ENDF/B-VIII.0 data results in a decrease of the MCNP
predicted eigenvalues compared to the ENDF/B-VII.0 library for all ZPPR-15 loadings, with the
 7

ENDF/B-VIII.0 library bringing a larger effect. Particularly, with the use of ENDF/B-VII.1
compared to ENDF/B-VII.0 the calculated keff values are smaller by an average of ~90 pcm for
the ZPPR-15A and ZPPR-15B loadings and an average of ~75 pcm for the ZPPR-15C and ZPPR-
15D loadings. On the other hand, with the use of ENDF/B-VIII.0 compared to ENDF/B-VII.0 the
calculated keff values are smaller by an average of ~140 pcm for the ZPPR-15A and ZPPR-15C
loadings, an average of ~120 pcm for the ZPPR-15B loadings and an average of ~200 pcm for
the ZPPR-15D loadings.

 Separate MCNP runs were performed to identify the major isotopic contributions to the
observed changes on calculated keff values with the three data libraries. For the four reference

the order of 2 - 3 pcm. Therefore, this type of MCNP runs are not suitable to capture cross section
effects on the order of just few pcm. For the same reason, these runs were not meant to perform
an exact breakdown of the observed reactivity changes with the use of the three data libraries, but
to identify the nuclide data that are the major contributors to such differences. From Table 3 to
Table 6 it is noted that the ENDF/B-VII.1 data of most individual isotopes bring only minor
changes to the keff values obtained with a consistent use of ENDF/B-VII.0 data across all isotopes.
The dominant contributions to the keff change from the use of ENDF/B-VII.1 compared to
ENDF/B-VII.0 are the due to Cr-52, Ni-58, Mn-55 and Pu-240 data. On the other hand, the use
of ENDF/B-VIII.0 brings large keff variations to the calculated keff values with ENDF/B-VII.0,
especially with the U-238 (+200 to 300 pcm depending on the loading) and Fe-56 (-250 to -350
pcm) data. Noticeable effects of ~-90 pcm are also associated with the ENDF/B-VIII.0 U-235
data for the loadings where this isotope is present in larger amounts (ZPPR-15C and especially
ZPPR-15D). Important effects are also observed for Cr-52 and Mn-55 cross section data.

3.2 Impact on Computed Experimental Uncertainty due to Different Data Libraries
 The total uncertainty on the measured keff values of the reference ZPPR-15 loadings was
derived by combining several effects that were evaluated through MCNP and DIF3D runs as
discussed in Ref. [7]. In Ref. [7], the reactivity bias due to some minor model simplifications was
also estimated. For each ZPPR-15 reference loading, Table 7 to Table 10 show the details of the
uncertainty and bias components that were derived in Ref. [7] (columns 2 and 3) with MCNP
calculations and how these components would change with a consistent use of the results obtained
in the present work with ENDF/B-VII.0 data (columns 4 and 5). In Table 7 to Table 10,
uncertainty and bias components were also assessed based on the results obtained with ENDF/B-
VII.1 (columns 6 and 7) and ENDF/B-VIII.0 (columns 8 and 9) library data. As can be seen, the
use of different data libraries has only a minor impact, generally within few pcm, on the
uncertainty components assessed on the measured keff values or on the bias components obtained
for the calculated keff values of the reference ZPPR-15 loadings.

4. Updated ZPPR-15 Eigenvalues with MCNP and ENDF/B-VII.0 with respect to
 Previously Reported Results.
 This section shows all eigenvalue and reactivity worths (Δρ) with associated comparison
to both experimental data (C-E or C/E) and MC2-3/DIF3D/VARIANT [8][9][10][11][12]
calculated values that were obtained in previous work with MCNP6.1 and ENDF/B-VII.0 data
and documented in Ref. [6] in a tabular format. For each table, the corresponding ENDF/B-VII.0
values obtained in the present work with the MCNP6.2 runs uploaded in the ZPPRD database in
GitLab are presented in red font for an easier identification.

 As noted in in Ref. [6], the previous results were also obtained with a statistical uncertainty
of 2 pcm. Therefore, the keff values obtained in this work were found to be within a maximum
difference of ~8 pcm (accounting for the combined 3 sigma uncertainty on each value) of the
previously reported values, as expected, with most differences being in the range of only 2-3 pcm.
The small differences on the keff values lead to meaningless changes on the C-Es as well. In the
case of the (C/Es-1) reported for the Δρ, a noticeable change may be found for small Δρ values
with absolute variations close to zero so that the results obtained in this and past work can be
proved very close in all cases. Therefore, a full consistency was found with the results already
documented in Ref. [6] given the standard deviations of the computed values. In particular, the
small changes identified with the results of the present work do not affect any statement or
conclusion provided in Ref. [6] except for the specific numeric values eventually mentioned.

4.1 Impact on Computed Eigenvalues due to Different Data Libraries
 In the previous section, Table 11 to Table 44 showed the eigenvalue and reactivity worth
values presented in Ref. [6] along with the updated results from the MCNP output files uploaded
in the ZPRD database in GitLab. All calculated values in those tables are based on the use of the
ENDF/B-VII.0 data library and represents the impact of using a newer version of MCNP and
corrections to some models.
 For the MCNP values presented in Table 11 to Table 44, Table 45 to Table 49 compare
the updated results obtained in the present work with the three data libraries (ENDF/B-VII.0,
ENDF/B-VII.1 and ENDF/B-VIII.0) along with the associated discrepancy with the experimental
data (C-E or C/E). For the predicted keff values (Table 45 to Table 47), the absolute (C-E)
variations between calculation and measurements obtained with the three data libraries reflect the
effect already discussed in Section 3.1. In average, it appears that the best comparison to the
experimental values is obtained with the ENDF/B-VII.0 data. Of particular interest is Table 45
that compares the keff values and discrepancy with the measurements obtained with the three data
libraries for the four ZPPR-15 reference loadings.
 In the case of reactivity worths, the calculated values reflect the variation between two
predicted eigenvalues k1 and k2 which mitigates the effect of using different library data.

5. Conclusions
 This report documents all MCNP [2] runs of the ZPPR-15 loadings that were included in
the ZPRD database on GitLab. In the present work, eigenvalues and associated standard
deviations were computed for each ZPPR-15 loading with the use of three data libraries: ENDF/B-
VII.0 [3], ENDF/B-VII.1 [4] and ENDF/B-VIII.0 [5]. Relevant results from the obtained outputs
were discussed in this report detailing the updated calculated values with respect to previous
reports, notably Refs. [6] and [7], and address any change in the observed reactivity worths
calculated relative to the experimental results.

 A total of 345 MCNP calculations were performed and corresponding (revised) input and
output files are accessible through the GitLab ZPRD database. It was observed that the use of
both ENDF/B-VII.1 and ENDF/B-VIII.0 data resulted in a decrease of the MCNP predicted
eigenvalues compared to the ENDF/B-VII.0 library for all ZPPR-15 loadings, with the ENDF/B-
VIII.0 library bringing a larger effect. Particularly, with the use of ENDF/B-VII.1 compared to
ENDF/B-VII.0 the calculated keff values are smaller by an average of ~90 pcm for the ZPPR-15A
and ZPPR-15B loadings and an average of ~75 pcm for the ZPPR-15C and ZPPR-15D loadings.
On the other hand, with ENDF/B-VIII.0, the calculated keff values are smaller than the ENDF/B-
VII.0 ones by an average of ~140 pcm for the ZPPR-15A and ZPPR-15C loadings, an average of
~120 pcm for the ZPPR-15B loadings and an average of ~200 pcm for the ZPPR-15D loadings.

 Separate MCNP runs were performed to identify the major isotopic contributions to the
observed changes on calculated keff values with the three data libraries. For the four reference
ZPPR-15 loadings, these library effects were evaluated through the change on the calculated keff
value with ENDF/B-VII.0 data by replacing the cross section data of a single isotope with those
from ENDF/B-VII.1 or ENDF/B-VIII.0. The dominant contributions to the keff change from the
use of ENDF/B-VII.1 compared to ENDF/B-VII.0 are the due to Cr-52, Ni-58, Mn-55 and Pu-
240 data. On the other hand, the use of ENDF/B-VIII.0 brings large keff variations to the calculated
keff values with ENDF/B-VII.0, especially with the U-238 (+200 to 300 pcm depending on the
loading) and Fe-56 (-250 to -350 pcm) data. Noticeable effects of ~-90 pcm are also associated
with the ENDF/B-VIII.0 U-235 data for the loadings where this isotope is present in larger
amounts (ZPPR-15C and especially ZPPR-15D). Important effects were also observed for Cr-52
and Mn-55 cross section data.

 Eigenvalue and reactivity worths (Δρ) with associated comparison to both experimental
data (C-E or C/E) and MC2-3/DIF3D/VARIANT [8][9][10][11][12] calculated values were
obtained in previous work with MCNP6.1 and ENDF/B-VII.0 data and documented in Ref. [6] in
a tabular format. For each table, the updated ENDF/B-VII.0 values obtained from the MCNP6.2
runs performed in this work and uploaded in the ZPPRD database in GitLab were derived in this
report. As noted in in Ref. [6], the previous results were also obtained with a statistical uncertainty
of 2 pcm. Therefore, the keff values obtained in this work were found to be within a maximum
difference of ~8 pcm (accounting for the combined 3 sigma uncertainty on each value) of the
previously reported values, as expected, with most differences being in the range of only 2-3 pcm.
The small differences on the keff values lead to slight and meaningless changes on the C-Es as
well. In the case of the (C/Es-1) reported previously for Δρ, a noticeable change may be found
for small Δρ measured values with absolute variations close to zero. Overall, a full consistency
was also found in Δρ with the results already documented in Ref. [6] given the standard deviations
of the computed values. In particular, the small changes identified with the results of the present

 37

work do not affect any statement or conclusion provided in Ref. [6] except for the specific numeric
values eventually mentioned.
 For the updated MCNP values, the change in the associated discrepancy with the
experimental data (C-E or C/E) due to use of the ENDF/B-VII.0, ENDF/B-VII.1 and ENDF/B-
VIII.0 data libraries was also discussed. For the predicted keff value, it was found that in average
the best comparison to the experimental values is obtained with the ENDF/B-VII.0 data. In the
case of reactivity worths, the calculated values reflect the variation between two predicted
eigenvalues k1 and k2 which mitigates the effect of using different library data. Therefore, the
three data libraries show only minor variations on the calculated Δρ (in most cases within 10
pcm).
