Color symmetry in the Potts spin glass at high temperature
Heejune Kim

Abstract
We show that color symmetry is preserved at high temperatures in the Potts spin glass model with κ ≥ 3 \kappa\geq 3 colors. Our proof employs the second moment method applied to the balanced model with a suitable centering of the Hamiltonian, while incorporating results from the non-disordered Potts model [ 12 ] . For κ = 2 \kappa=2 , we exploit the model’s gauge symmetry to show that unbalanced configurations occur with exponentially small probability at all temperatures β ∈ [ 0 , ∞ ] \beta\in[0,\infty] .

Introduction and main results

The Potts spin glass is a
\kappa
-color generalization of the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick (SK) model.
For a system of
N
spins, let
\Sigma_{\kappa}=[\kappa]^{N}
denote the set of configurations. For any
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa}
, the magnetization vector
d(\sigma)\in\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{d\in[0,1]^{\kappa}:d_{1}+\dots+d_{\kappa}=1\}
is given by

d(\sigma)=\bigl(N^{-1}|\{i\in[N]:\sigma_{i}=a\}|\bigr)_{a\in[\kappa]}.

We define the set of constrained configurations by
\Sigma^{d}_{\kappa}=\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa}:d(\sigma)=d\}
for
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}
.
In particular, a configuration is called balanced if
d(\sigma)=\kappa^{-1}\mymathbb{1}
, and we let
\Sigma^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}
denote the collection of all such configurations.
We identify each configuration
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N})
with a
\kappa\times N
matrix whose columns are standard basis vectors
(e_{a})_{a\in[\kappa]}
in
\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
.
By a slight abuse of notation, we will use
\sigma_{i}=e_{a}
and
\sigma_{i}=a
interchangeably.
The Hamiltonian of the Potts spin glass model is given by

H_{N}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i,j\in[N]}g_{ij}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i,j\in[N]}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}^{\intercal}\sigma_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa},

(1.1)

where
(g_{ij})_{i,j\in[N]}
are i.i.d. standard normal random variables.
Associated with (
1.1
) are the ground state energy
\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}=N^{-1}\mathbb{E}\max_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa}}H_{N}(\sigma)
and the free energy at inverse temperature
\beta\in[0,\infty)
,

F_{N,\beta}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log Z_{N,\beta},\quad\text{where}\quad Z_{N,\beta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa}}\exp(\beta H_{N}(\sigma))

is called the partition function.
The corresponding Gibbs measure
G_{N,\beta}
is specified by its probability mass function

G_{N,\beta}(\sigma)=\frac{\exp(\beta H_{N}(\sigma))}{Z_{N,\beta}}\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa},

and we denote the average with respect to the product measure
G_{N,\beta}^{\otimes\infty}
by
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N,\beta}.

In the zero-temperature limit
\beta=\infty
,
G_{N,\infty}
denotes the uniform distribution on the set of configurations maximizing
H_{N}(\sigma)
.

We similarly define the balanced ground state energy
\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa}
, the balanced free energy
F^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}
, and the balanced partition function
Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}
by restricting to the balanced configurations
\Sigma^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}
and assuming
N\in\kappa\mathbb{N}
.
More generally, the model constrained at
\Sigma^{d}_{\kappa}
can be defined for a magnetization vector
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{\kappa}
and
N\in\operatorname{lcm}(d)\mathbb{N}
, where
\operatorname{lcm}(d)
is the least common multiple of the denominators of
d
in reduced form.
Henceforth, we assume that
N
satisfies the necessary divisibility conditions whenever a constraint is imposed on the model.

Panchenko
[
21
]
established the Parisi formula for the limiting free energy
\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\beta}
as a supremum of the Parisi formula for the model constrained around
\Sigma^{d}_{\kappa}
for each
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}
.
Building on this work, Bates–Sohn
[
1
, Theorem 1.7]
demonstrated that this constrained Parisi formula indeed coincides with the limiting constrained free energy
\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\beta}^{d}
which is uniformly continuous in
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{\kappa}
(see
[
1
, Lemma E.4]
).
We can summarize these findings as

\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\beta}=\max_{d\in\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}}\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\beta}^{d},

(1.2)

where we extended
\lim_{N\to\infty}F^{d}_{N,\beta}
to all
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}
by uniform continuity.
In the special case of balanced configurations, Bates–Sohn
[
1
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.8]
showed that the Parisi formula for the limiting balanced free energy
\lim_{N\to\infty}F^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}
simplifies considerably in terms of its order parameter, resembling the SK model.
They further suggested that this limiting balanced free energy always achieves the maximum in (
1.2
), that is, color symmetry holds at any
\beta\in[0,\infty)
.
We formalize this notion as follows, including the case of limiting ground state energies.

Definition 1.1

(Color symmetry)
.

We say that the Potts spin glass model preserves color symmetry at inverse temperature
\beta\in[0,\infty)
if
\lim_{N\to\infty}F^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}=\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\beta}
, and at zero-temperature
\beta=\infty
if
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}
1
1

1

The existence of the limits
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa}
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}
easily follows from that of the corresponding free energies. Indeed, from the elementary inequalities
\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}\leq\beta^{-1}F_{N,\beta}\leq\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}+\beta^{-1}\log\kappa
and the existence of the limit
\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\beta}
, we conclude that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}=\inf_{\beta>0}(\beta^{-1}\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\beta})
. The balanced case is similar.
.
Otherwise, the model breaks color symmetry at
\beta.

Remark 1.2
.

By the concentration of Gaussian measures, color symmetry breaking at
\beta
is equivalent to the statement that the balanced configurations occur with exponentially small probability under
\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}
(see, e.g.,
[
23

).

Regarding the aforementioned prediction by Bates–Sohn
[
1
]
, Mourrat
[
18
]
showed, contrary to the prediction, that color symmetry breaks on an
O(\sqrt{\kappa})
-window of
\beta
centered at
2\kappa/(3\sqrt{\pi})
whenever
\kappa\geq 58
.
We expect that color symmetry breaks at zero temperature for all
\kappa\geq 3
(see the first item in the open problems below), as predicted in the physics literature
[
2
,
8
,
11
,
10
]
.

In the present work, we establish color symmetry at high temperatures for all
\kappa\geq 3.

To state our main result, we define a high-temperature threshold for color symmetry breaking, for
\kappa\geq 3
, as

\beta_{\kappa}=\sqrt{\kappa(\kappa-1)\log(\kappa-1)}\cdot\min\Bigl\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa-2}},\,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\kappa-2}\Bigr\}.

(1.3)

For
\kappa\geq 4
, this minimum is achieved by the second term, which is obtained from the critical temperature of a corresponding non-disordered ferromagnetic Potts model in
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Our main theorem characterizes the high-temperature behavior for all
\kappa\geq 3
within the regime
\beta\in[0,\beta_{\kappa}).

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
\kappa\geq 3
and
\beta\in[0,\beta_{\kappa})
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}F^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}F_{N,\beta}=\log\kappa+\frac{\beta^{2}(\kappa-1)}{2\kappa^{2}}.

In particular, color symmetry is preserved in this high temperature regime.

Remark 1.4
.

The right-hand side can be identified with the replica symmetric solution of the balanced model, where the order parameter is given by the constant path
\pi(t)=\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}
for all
0<t\leq 1
in the notation of
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
.
Notably, this coincides with the predicted high-temperature value of the unconstrained limiting free energy in the physics literature
[
8
,
17
]
.

We complement Theorem
1.3
with a result for
\kappa=2
, showing that unbalanced configurations occur with exponentially small probability for all
\beta\in[0,\infty]
.
Denote the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Assume
\kappa=2
. Then, for any
\beta\in[0,\infty]
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}\bigl(\|d(\sigma)-2^{-1}\mymathbb{1}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon\bigr)\leq 2e^{-\varepsilon^{2}N}.

Consequently, color symmetry is preserved for all
\beta\in[0,\infty]
.

Open problems.

With the above results, we list a few related questions.

1.

Does color symmetry break at zero temperature for all
\kappa\geq 3
?
Noticing that
\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}^{\sf bal}\leq\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}\leq\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa+1}
due to the trivial inclusions
\Sigma^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa+1}
, this would follow if one could show the following strict inequality,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa}>\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa+1}\quad\text{for}\quad\kappa\geq 2,

which we conjecture to be true.
We refer the reader to Appendix
C
for a proof of color symmetry breaking at zero temperature for the restricted range
\kappa\geq 56
.

2.

For
\kappa\geq 3,
is there a critical temperature
\hat{\beta}_{\kappa}\in(0,\infty]
of color symmetry breaking, where color symmetry is preserved for
\beta\leq\hat{\beta}_{\kappa}
and broken for
\beta>\hat{\beta}_{\kappa}
?
If so, Theorem
1.3
provides a lower bound
\hat{\beta}_{\kappa}\geq\beta_{\kappa}
.

3.

For
\kappa\geq 3
, find the maximizers
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}
, possibly depending on
\beta
, that achieves the supremum in (
1.2
).
Is the maximizer unique modulo the permutation symmetry of colors?

1.1
Proof sketch

For
\kappa\geq 3
, the balanced free energy is analyzed via the second moment method. The centering of the Hamiltonian is critical to this approach; without such an adjustment, the second moment method is inherently bound to fail, as demonstrated in Appendix
A
.
Most of the work lies in controlling the ratio (
2.7
), which compares the second moment of the centered and balanced partition function to the square of its first moment. To manage this, the ratio is decomposed into two distinct components: the first is addressed via a local expansion of the Kullback–Leibler divergence (see Lemma
2.5
), while the second is controlled by applying large deviation results from the non-disordered Potts model
[
12
]
(see Lemma
2.6
).
This framework allows us to establish the limiting balanced free energy as in Proposition
2.1
, and it turns out to coincide with the annealed limiting free energy of the unconstrained model from
[
12
]
, thereby proving Theorem
1.3
.

For
\kappa=2
, the Potts spin glass model reduces to the SK model by a simple change of variable
\mymathbb{1}\{\tau_{i}=\tau_{j}\}=2^{-1}(\tau_{i}\tau_{j}+1)
for
\tau\in\{-1,+1\}^{N}
, where Jagannath–Sen
[
16
, Appendix C]
established color symmetry at zero temperature
\beta=\infty
.
Their method can be extended to
\beta<\infty
, which together with some additional properties of the Parisi formula would imply Proposition
1.5
.
Nevertheless, we present an elementary proof without the Parisi formula by directly controlling the multi-spin correlations via the gauge symmetry of the SK model, namely, the invariance of the distribution of the Hamiltonian under the map
\tau\mapsto(a_{i}\tau_{i})_{i\in[N]}
for any
a,\tau\in\{-1,+1\}^{N}
(see Lemma
3.1
).

Related work.

Chen
[
5
]
demonstrated that color symmetry is preserved when a sufficiently strong antiferromagnetic interaction is integrated into the Hamiltonian.
Relatedly, Chen
[
4
]
established that this “self-overlap correction” simplifies the Parisi formula to the one equivalent to the balanced Potts spin glass
[
1
]
, which was subsequently generalized to permutation-invariant models by Issa
[
14
]
.

The connection between the SK model and the Max-cut problem, initially explored in
[
9
]
, was generalized by Sen
[
22
]
who established that the balanced ground state energy of the Potts spin glass serves as the leading-order correction term for the Max
\kappa
-cut problem on sparse random graphs.
This relationship was later extended to inhomogeneous sparse random graphs in
[
15
]
.

Finally, while gauge symmetry has long been known in the physics literature (cf.
[
19
, Chapter 4]
), it has recently been rediscovered as a key component in the proof of the disorder chaos phenomenon in the Edwards–Anderson model
[
3
]
and diluted spin glass models
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.2

\mathbb{E}H_{N}(\sigma)H_{N}(\tau)=N\|R(\sigma,\tau)\|_{F}^{2}.

Proof.

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa})^{2}}{(\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa})^{2}}<\infty.

Proof.

Consider the uniform probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{\sigma,\tau}
on
\Sigma_{\kappa}^{\sf bal}\times\Sigma_{\kappa}^{\sf bal}
.
Condition on a fixed
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa}^{\sf bal}
and denote the conditional probability measure by
\mathbb{P}_{\tau}
.
The matrix
R(\sigma,\tau)
is then a random
\kappa
-by-
\kappa
matrix with a fixed row and columns sums
1/\kappa
, and its conditional distribution is given by

\mathbb{P}_{\tau}(R(\sigma,\tau)=(r_{ab})_{a,b\in[\kappa]})=\bigl(\frac{N!}{((N/\kappa)!)^{\kappa}}\bigr)^{-1}\prod_{a\in[\kappa]}\frac{(N/\kappa)!}{\prod_{b\in[\kappa]}(Nr_{ab})!}\quad\text{for}\quad(r_{ab})_{a,b\in[\kappa]}\in\mathcal{A}^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}.

Using the Stirling’s formula
\log n!=n\log n-n+2^{-1}\log n+O(1)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\log\mathbb{P}_{\tau}(R(\sigma,\tau)=(r_{ab})_{a,b\in[\kappa]})=-N\sum_{a,b\in[\kappa]}r_{ab}\log(\kappa^{2}r_{ab})-\frac{(\kappa-1)^{2}}{2}\log N-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(a,b)\in I_{r}}\log r_{ab}+O(1),

(2.8)

where

I_{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(a,b)\in[\kappa]\times[\kappa]:r_{ab}\neq 0\}.

Note that this holds uniformly in
\sigma.

We can identify the first term on the right-hand side (
2.8
) (without the prefactor
N
) as a relative entropy (Kullback–Leibler divergence) with respect to the uniform measure on
[\kappa]\times[\kappa]
, which we write as
D(r\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}).

Combining (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa})^{2}}{(\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa})^{2}}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{A}^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}}N^{-(\kappa-1)^{2}/2}

\displaystyle\exp\Bigl(-N\bigl(D(r\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal})-\beta^{2}\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}\bigr)

(2.9)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b\in I_{r}}\log r_{ab}+O(1)\Bigr)

The following is a local expansion of the Kullback–Leibler divergence whose reference measure has strictly positive mass, and we defer its proof to Appendix
B
(see also
[
7
, Theorem 4.1]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
p\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(p_{i})_{i\in[n]}
and
q\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(q_{i})_{i\in[n]}
be two discrete probability distributions on
[n]
.
Suppose
\min_{1\leq i\leq n}q_{i}>0
and
\max_{i\in[n]}|p-q|\leq 2^{-1}\min_{i\in[n]}q_{i}.

We have

\Bigl|D(p\|q)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in[n]}\frac{(p_{i}-q_{i})^{2}}{q_{i}}\Bigr|\leq 5\bigl(\min_{i\in[n]}q_{i}\bigr)^{-2}\sum_{i\in[n]}|p_{i}-q_{i}|^{3}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0.

From Lemma
2.5
, there exists
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that if
\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}<\delta^{\prime}
,

D(r\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}.

(2.10)

Moreover, there exists
K=K(\kappa)<\infty
such that for a small enough
\delta^{\prime\prime}=\delta^{\prime\prime}(\kappa)>0
, we have

\sum_{a,b\in[\kappa]}|\log r_{ab}|\leq K\quad\text{whenever}\quad\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}<\delta^{\prime\prime}.

(2.11)

Take
\delta=2^{-1}\min\{\delta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime}\}
.
From (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we can bound (
2.9
) by separating it into two parts:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{A}^{\sf bal}_{\kappa},\\
\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\delta\end{subarray}}N^{-(\kappa-1)^{2}/2}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{(1-\varepsilon)\kappa^{2}-2\beta^{2}}{2}N\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}+K+O(1)\Bigr)

(2.12)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{A}^{\sf bal}_{\kappa},\\
\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}\geq\delta\end{subarray}}N^{-(\kappa-1)^{2}/2}\exp\Bigl(-N\bigl(D(r\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal})-\beta^{2}\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}\bigr)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b\in I_{r}}\log r_{ab}+O(1)\Bigr).

(2.13)

Moreover, we have the following volume estimate, for
l\in[N]
,

\Bigl|\Bigl\{(r_{ab})_{a,b\in[\kappa]}\in\mathcal{A}^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}:\frac{l-1}{N}\leq\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}<\frac{l}{N}\Bigr\}\Bigr|\leq C^{\prime}(lN)^{(\kappa-1)^{2}/2}

(2.14)

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
depending only on
\kappa
.
To see this, note that any admissible matrix in (
2.14
) is uniquely determined by its leading principal submatrix of order
\kappa-1
and each entry in this submatrix can take at most
O(1)\sqrt{lN}
many values.
As a result, (
2.12
) is bounded by

O(1)\sum_{1\leq l\leq\delta N}l^{(\kappa-1)^{2}/2}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{(1-\varepsilon)\kappa^{2}-2\beta^{2}}{2}(l-1)+K\Bigr).

(2.15)

If
2\beta^{2}<(1-\varepsilon)\kappa^{2}
, then (
2.15
) is uniformly bounded in
N
as it is dominated by a convergent series
\sum_{l\geq 1}l^{(\kappa-1)^{2}/2}e^{-cl}<\infty
for some
c>0
.

It remains to estimate (
2.13
).
To this end, note the following application of
[
12
]
, whose proof is deferred to Appendix
B
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
\delta>0
, there exists
\eta>0
such that if

\beta^{2}<\frac{\kappa(\kappa-1)\log(\kappa-1)}{\kappa-2},

then

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{A}^{\sf bal}_{\kappa},\\
\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}\geq\delta\end{subarray}}\bigl(D(r\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal})-\beta^{2}\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}\bigr)>\eta.

If
\beta^{2}<\kappa(\kappa-1)\log(\kappa-1)/(\kappa-2)
, Lemma
2.6
guarantees an
\eta>0
such that (
2.13
) is bounded by, in view of (
2.14
),

O(1)\sum_{\delta N<l\leq N}l^{(\kappa-1)^{2}/2}\exp\bigl(-\eta N+\kappa^{2}\log N\bigr).

(2.16)

The limit superior of (
2.16
) in
N
is zero as the sum over
l
is at most polynomial in
N
.
Combining the previous estimates (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa})^{2}}{(\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa})^{2}}<\infty

whenever

\beta^{2}<\min\Bigl\{\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2},\frac{\kappa(\kappa-1)\log(\kappa-1)}{\kappa-2}\Bigr\}=\frac{\kappa(\kappa-1)\log(\kappa-1)}{\kappa-2}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

We are now in a position to complete the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Lemma
2.4
and the Paley–Zygmund inequality show that

\mathbb{P}(Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}\geq 2^{-1}\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa})>c>0

for some constant
c>0
depending only on
\kappa
and
\beta.

On this event of positive probability, we have

\frac{1}{N}\log Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}\geq-\frac{\log 2}{N}+\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}.

On the other hand, from the concentration of Gaussian measures (see
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
), it holds that
N^{-1}\log Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}\to N^{-1}\mathbb{E}\log Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}
in probability as
N\to\infty.

Therefore, given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an event of positive probability such that for sufficiently large
N,

\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}\geq\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}\geq-\varepsilon-\frac{\log 2}{N}+\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa},

(2.17)

where the first inequality is due to Jensen.
Note that
\|P_{\kappa}\|_{F}^{2}=\kappa^{-2}(\kappa-1)
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\log|\Sigma^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}|=\log\kappa
.
From (
2.6
), we can then calculate the limit of the last term as

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}=\log\kappa+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa^{2}}.

Since every quantity in (
2.17
) is deterministic, taking
N\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
shows that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta,\kappa}=\log\kappa+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa^{2}}.

This finishes the proof.

∎

3
Proof of Proposition
1.5

This section is devoted to the proof of color symmetry of the Potts model with two colors
\kappa=2
.
As mentioned in the proof sketch, our main tool will be the gauge symmetry of the SK model.
We first identify the SK model with the Potts model for
\kappa=2
as follows.
For
\sigma\in[2]^{N}
, consider the change of variable

\tau_{j}=2\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{j}=1\}-1\quad\text{for}\quad j\in[N].

Note that
\tau\in\{-1,+1\}^{N}
, and it is easy to check
\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=2\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\}-1.

The SK Hamiltonian is then obtained by this change of variable

H_{N}^{\text{SK}}(\tau)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i,j\in[N]}g_{ij}\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=2H_{N}(\sigma)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i,j\in[N]}g_{ij}.

(3.1)

For
\beta\in[0,\infty]
, denote the corresponding Gibbs measure by
G_{N,\beta}^{\text{SK}}
, and its Gibbs average by
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\text{SK}}
.
Since the last term in (
3.1
) does not affect the Gibbs measure, we have

G_{N,\beta}^{\text{SK}}=G_{N,2\beta}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\cdot\rangle_{N,\beta}^{\text{SK}}=\langle\cdot\rangle_{N,2\beta}.

(3.2)

For a finite sequence
(i_{1},\dots i_{m})
in
[N]
, define the degree of an index
l
as
d_{l}=|\{l^{\prime}\in[m]:i_{l^{\prime}}=i_{l}\}|
.
Gauge symmetry of the SK model controls the multi-spin correlation of the Potts model as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\beta\in[0,\infty]
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
For any sequence
(i_{1},\dots i_{m})
in
[N]
, if there exists
l_{0}\in[m]
such that
d_{l_{0}}
is odd, then
\mathbb{E}\langle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\tau_{i_{l}}\rangle_{N,\beta}=0
.

Proof.

It is a standard fact that
G_{N,\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}G_{N,\infty}
as
\beta\to\infty
.
Therefore, it suffices to consider the case
\beta<\infty
.
Using (
3.2
), we may rewrite the expectation as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\tau_{i_{l}}\Bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\tau_{i_{l}}\Big\rangle_{N,\beta/2}^{\text{SK}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{\tau\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\exp(2^{-1}\beta H^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\tau))\bigr)^{-1}\sum_{\tau\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\tau_{i_{l}}\cdot\exp(2^{-1}\beta H^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\tau)).

Consider the bijection
\tau\mapsto\hat{\tau}
where
\hat{\tau}_{i_{l_{0}}}=-\tau_{i_{l_{0}}}
and
\hat{\tau}_{j}=\tau_{j}
for all
j\in[N]\setminus\{i_{l_{0}}\}
.
Since this map is a bijection on
\{-1,+1\}^{N}
, the preceding display is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sum_{\tau\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\exp(2^{-1}\beta H^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\hat{\tau}))\Bigr)^{-1}\sum_{\tau\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\hat{\tau}_{i_{l}}\cdot\exp(2^{-1}\beta H^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\hat{\tau}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sum_{\tau\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\exp(2^{-1}\beta\widetilde{H}^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\tau))\Bigr)^{-1}\sum_{\tau\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\Bigl((-1)^{d_{l_{0}}}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\tau_{i_{l}}\Bigr)\cdot\exp(2^{-1}\beta\widetilde{H}^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\tau)),

where
\sqrt{N}\widetilde{H}^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\tau)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\sum_{l\in[N]\setminus\{i_{l_{0}}\}}(g_{i_{l_{0}},l}+g_{l,i_{l_{0}}})\tau_{i_{l_{0}}}\tau_{l}+\sum_{l,l^{\prime}\in[N]\setminus\{i_{l_{0}}\}}g_{ll^{\prime}}\tau_{l}\tau_{l^{\prime}}+g_{i_{l_{0}},i_{l_{0}}}
.
Notice that we have the equality in distribution of the Gaussian processes,
(\widetilde{H}^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\tau))_{\tau\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(H^{\text{SK}}_{N}(\tau))_{\tau\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}
,
since
\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{SK}}_{N}
is obtained from
H^{\mathrm{SK}}_{N}
by flipping the signs of the independent centered Gaussian couplings incident to
i_{l_{0}}
, which leaves the joint law invariant.
Hence, the last display is unchanged if we replace the process
\widetilde{H}^{\text{SK}}_{N}
by
H^{\text{SK}}_{N}
.
Combining this with (
3.2
) and the assumption that
d_{l_{0}}
is odd, we conclude that (
3.3
) is equal to

-\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\tau_{i_{l}}\Bigr\rangle_{N,\beta},

which implies that it is indeed zero, thereby finishing the proof.
∎

With the preceding lemma, we bound the moments of the centered magnetization vector.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\beta\in[0,\infty]
.
For any even
m\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=1\}-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)^{m}\Bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}\leq\frac{m!}{2^{m}(m/2)!}\cdot\frac{1}{N^{m/2}}.

For odd
m
, the left-hand side of the preceding display is zero.

Proof.

Fix
m\geq 1.

We write

\displaystyle 2^{m}\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=1\}-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)^{m}\Bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}=\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}(2\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=1\}-1)\Bigr)^{m}\Bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{m}}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\in[N]}\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\tau_{i_{l}}\Bigr\rangle_{N,\beta}.

(3.4)

Lemma
3.1
readily implies the desired result in the case of odd
m
, as there exists an index
l
such that
d_{i_{l}}
is odd.
It remains to check the case of even
m
.
Again by Lemma
3.1
, the only contributing terms in the sum (
3.4
) are the ones such that all
d_{l}
’s are even for
1\leq l\leq m
, and each contribution is equal to
1
.
Therefore, we only need to count the number of ways to form a sequence
(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})
in
[N]
such that all
d_{l}
’s are even.
An elementary counting shows that it is bounded by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}=m/2\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq N}(2x_{i})!}\leq\frac{m!}{(m/2)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}=m/2\end{subarray}}\frac{(m/2)!}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq N}x_{i}!}=\frac{m!}{(m/2)!}N^{m/2}.

Here, we used
(2x_{i})!\geq x_{i}!
and the multinomial theorem.
We obtain the desired result by combining this with the prefactor
N^{-m}
in (
3.4
).

∎

An immediate corollary is a control of the moment-generating function, from which we deduce the proof of Proposition
1.5
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\beta\in[0,\infty]
.
For any
\lambda>0
, we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl\langle\exp\Bigl(\lambda\bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=1\}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\Bigr)\Big\rangle_{N,\beta}\leq e^{\lambda^{2}/(4N)}.

Proof.

Denote
X_{N}=N^{-1}\sum_{i\in[N]}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=1\}-2^{-1}
.
Let
\lambda>0
.
From the Taylor expansion
e^{x}=\sum_{m\geq 0}x^{m}/m!
, Lemma
3.2
shows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle e^{\lambda X_{N}}\rangle_{N,\beta}=\sum_{\text{even }m}\lambda^{m}\frac{\mathbb{E}\langle X_{N}^{m}\rangle_{N,\beta}}{m!}\leq\sum_{\text{even }m}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{2^{m}(m/2)!}\cdot\frac{1}{N^{m/2}}=e^{\lambda^{2}/(4N)}.

∎

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

By a Chernoff bound, Lemma
3.3
yields

\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}\Bigl(\,\Bigl|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=1\}-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr|>\varepsilon\Bigr)\leq 2e^{-\varepsilon^{2}N}.

(3.5)

It remains to establish color symmetry.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and some
d\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{2}
satisfying
\|d-d_{\sf bal}\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon
, which is equivalent to
|d_{1}-2^{-1}|>\varepsilon
.
The proof is now finished from Jensen’s inequality and (
3.5
):

\mathbb{E}F^{d}_{N,\beta}-\mathbb{E}F_{N,\beta}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log G_{N,\beta}(d(\sigma)=d)\leq\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}G_{N,\beta}(|d_{1}-2^{-1}|>\varepsilon)\leq-\varepsilon^{2}+o(1).

∎

Appendix A
Necessity of Hamiltonian centering

In this section, we show that a suitable centering is required in the Hamiltonian for the second moment method to be effective in the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Define the annealed Gibbs measure at inverse temperature
\beta>0
with the probability mass function

G_{\mathrm{ann},N,\beta}(\sigma)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\exp(\beta H_{N}(\sigma))}{\mathbb{E}Z_{N,\beta}}=\frac{\exp\bigl((2N)^{-1}\beta^{2}\sum_{i,j\in[N]}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\}\bigr)}{\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa}}\exp\bigl((2N)^{-1}\beta^{2}\sum_{i,j\in[N]}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\}\bigr)}\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa},

and denote the corresponding annealed Gibbs average by
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{ann},N,\beta}
.
The following is in a sharp contrast with Lemma
2.4
, which justifies the consideration of the centered Hamiltonian (
2.1
).

Lemma A.1
.

For any
\beta\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}Z_{N,\beta}^{2}}{(\mathbb{E}Z_{N,\beta})^{2}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z^{{\sf bal}}_{N,\beta})^{2}}{(\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta})^{2}}=\infty.

Proof.

From the identity

Z_{N,\beta}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa}}e^{\beta H_{N}(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\kappa}}\prod_{i,j\in[N]}e^{\beta g_{ij}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\}/\sqrt{N}},

we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathbb{E}Z_{N,\beta})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in\Sigma_{\kappa}}\prod_{i,j\in[N]}e^{\beta^{2}(\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\}+\mymathbb{1}\{\tau_{i}=\tau_{j}\})/(2N)}\quad\text{and}

(A.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z_{N,\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in\Sigma_{\kappa}}\prod_{i,j\in[N]}e^{\beta^{2}(\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\}+\mymathbb{1}\{\tau_{i}=\tau_{j}\})^{2}/(2N)}.

By the Jensen’s inequality and the symmetry among the sites,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}Z_{N,\beta}^{2}}{(\mathbb{E}Z_{N,\beta})^{2}}=\langle e^{\beta^{2}\sum_{i,j}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j},\tau_{i}=\tau_{j}\}/N}\rangle_{\mathrm{ann},N,\beta}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\bigl(\beta^{2}(N-1)G_{\mathrm{ann},N,\beta}(\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2})^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\bigl(\beta^{2}(N-1)\kappa^{-2}\bigr),

where the last inequality follows from the coupling of the non-disordered Potts Gibbs measure and the random cluster measure; see
[
13
, Theorem 1.16]
.
This implies the desired divergence for the unconstrained model upon sending
N\to\infty
.
On the other hand, if we restrict to the balanced configurations
\Sigma^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}
in (
A.1
), we have an analogous inequality

\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta})^{2}}{(\mathbb{E}Z^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta})^{2}}=\langle e^{\beta^{2}\sum_{i,j}\mymathbb{1}\{\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j},\tau_{i}=\tau_{j}\}/N}\rangle_{{\sf bal}}\geq\exp\bigl(\beta^{2}(N-1)\mathbb{P}^{\sf bal}_{\operatorname{unif}}(\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2})^{2}\bigr),

where
\mathbb{P}^{\sf bal}_{\operatorname{unif}}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\sf bal}
denote the uniform probability measure on
\Sigma^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}
and uniform average on
\Sigma^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}\times\Sigma^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}
, respectively.
Since
\mathbb{P}^{\sf bal}_{\operatorname{unif}}(\sigma_{2}=a\mid\sigma_{1}=a)=(N-1)^{-1}(\kappa^{-1}N-1)
for any
a\in[\kappa]
, we have

\mathbb{P}^{\sf bal}_{\operatorname{unif}}(\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2})=\sum_{a\in[\kappa]}\mathbb{P}^{\sf bal}_{\operatorname{unif}}(\sigma_{1}=a)\mathbb{P}^{\sf bal}_{\operatorname{unif}}(\sigma_{2}=a\mid\sigma_{1}=a)=\frac{N-\kappa}{(N-1)\kappa}.

Combining the last two displays yield a diverging lower bound of the ratio for the balanced model.
This finishes the proof.
∎

.

Proof of Lemma

.

Using
\sum_{a,b}r_{ab}=1

\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}=\|r\|_{F}^{2}-\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}.

(B.1)

Therefore, our objective function is equivalent to

D(r\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal})-\beta^{2}\|r\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\sum_{a\in[\kappa]}\Bigl(\sum_{b\in[\kappa]}v_{ab}\log(\kappa v_{ab})-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\kappa}\sum_{b\in[\kappa]}v_{ab}^{2}\Bigr),

(B.2)

where we employed a change of variable
v_{ab}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\kappa r_{ab}
.

Fix
a\in[\kappa]
and denote each row sum by

S_{a}(v_{a,\square})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{b\in[\kappa]}v_{ab}\log(\kappa v_{ab})-\kappa^{-1}\beta^{2}\sum_{b\in[\kappa]}v_{ab}^{2},

where
v_{a,\square}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(v_{ab})_{b\in[\kappa]}
.
Note that
\sum_{b\in[\kappa]}v_{ab}=1
, so we can precisely identify
S_{a}
with the negative of
[
12
, Equation (2.5)]
.
By
[
12
, Theorem 2.1-(b)]
, if

\beta^{2}<\frac{\kappa(\kappa-1)\log(\kappa-1)}{\kappa-2},

then the uniform distribution
v_{a,\square}=\kappa^{-1}\mymathbb{1}
is the unique minimizer of
S_{a}
.
Noticing
S_{a}(\kappa^{-1}\mymathbb{1})=-\kappa^{-2}\beta^{2}
,
we deduce that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\eta>0
such that

\inf_{v_{a,\square}\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}}S_{a}(v_{a,\square})\geq-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}+\eta,

(B.3)

where

\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Bigl\{x\in[0,1]^{\kappa}:\sum_{b\in[\kappa]}x_{b}=1,\,\|x-\kappa^{-1}\mymathbb{1}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\varepsilon\Bigr\}.

(We understand
\eta=0
if
\varepsilon=0
.)
Indeed, this follows from the compactness of
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
and the uniqueness of the minimizer of
S_{a}.

Now, let
\delta>0
and suppose
\|r-\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}\geq\delta
for some
r\in\mathcal{A}^{\sf bal}_{\kappa}
.
The pigeonhole principle shows that there exists
\hat{a}\in[\kappa]
such that
\sum_{b\in[\kappa]}(r_{\hat{a}b}-\kappa^{-2})^{2}\geq\kappa^{-1}\delta,
i.e.,

\sum_{b\in[\kappa]}(v_{\hat{a}b}-\kappa^{-1})^{2}\geq\kappa\delta,

which together with (
B.3
) guarantees that, for some
\eta>0
,

S_{\hat{a}}(v_{\hat{a},\square})\geq-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}+\eta.

On the other hand, since (
B.3
) continues to hold in the case
\varepsilon=0
with
\eta=0
, we have that for any
a\neq\hat{a}
,

S_{a}(v_{a,\square})\geq-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}.

Combining the last two displays with (
B.2
), we arrive at

\displaystyle D(r\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal})-\beta^{2}\|r\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\Bigl(S_{\hat{a}}(v_{\hat{a},\square})+\sum_{a\neq\hat{a}}S_{a}(v_{a,\square})\Bigr)\geq-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}+\frac{\eta}{\kappa}=-\beta^{2}\|\kappa^{-2}\mymathbb{1}\mymathbb{1}^{\intercal}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta}{\kappa},

which together with (
B.1
) yields the desired result.
∎

Remark B.1
.

Without resorting to the row decomposition (
B.2
), one may directly apply
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
by “flattening” a matrix
r
to a
\kappa^{2}
-dimensional vector.
However, this will yield an upper bound on
\beta
with worse dependence on
\kappa
.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

For convenience, define
x=p-q
.
From the assumption
\max_{i\in[n]}|p-q|\leq 2^{-1}\min_{i\in[n]}q_{i}
,

\max_{i\in[n]}|q_{i}^{-1}x_{i}|\leq 2^{-1}.

(B.4)

Then, Taylor’s expansion
|\log(1+a)-a+2^{-1}a^{2}|\leq 3|a|^{3}
for
|a|\leq 2^{-1}
gives

\displaystyle D(p\|q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n]}(q_{i}+x_{i})\log(1+q^{-1}_{i}x_{i})=\sum_{i\in[n]}(q_{i}+x_{i})(q^{-1}_{i}x_{i}-2^{-1}q^{-2}_{i}x_{i}^{2}+r_{i})

\displaystyle=2^{-1}\sum_{i\in[n]}q_{i}^{-1}x_{i}^{2}-2^{-1}\sum_{i\in[n]}q_{i}^{-2}x_{i}^{3}+\sum_{i\in[n]}(q_{i}+x_{i})r_{i}

for some
|r_{i}|\leq 3q_{i}^{-3}|x_{i}|^{3}
.
Here, we used
\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}=0
in the last equality.
The proof is now finished by the following control of the error terms,

\Bigl|-2^{-1}\sum_{i\in[n]}q_{i}^{-2}x_{i}^{3}+\sum_{i\in[n]}(q_{i}+x_{i})r_{i}\Bigr|\leq\frac{7}{2}\sum_{i\in[n]}q_{i}^{-2}|x_{i}|^{3}+3\sum_{i\in[n]}q_{i}^{-3}|x_{i}|^{4}\leq 5(\min_{i\in[n]}q_{i})^{-2}\sum_{i\in[n]}|x_{i}|^{3},

where we used (
B.4
) for the last inequality.

∎

Appendix C
Color symmetry breaking at zero temperature

In this section, we verify color symmetry breaking at zero temperature for
\kappa\geq 56
, essentially by refining Mourrat’s approach in
[
18
]
.
From the inequalities
\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa}\leq\beta^{-1}F^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}\leq\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa}+\beta^{-1}\log\kappa
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa}=\inf_{\beta>0}\frac{1}{\beta}\lim_{N\to\infty}F^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}.

Since the right-hand side in Theorem
1.3
is an upper bound of
\lim_{N\to\infty}F^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}
for any
\beta>0
per the Parisi formula
[
1
]
, the preceding display implies

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}^{\sf bal}_{N,\kappa}=\inf_{\beta>0}\frac{1}{\beta}\lim_{N\to\infty}F^{\sf bal}_{N,\beta}\leq\inf_{\beta>0}\Bigl(\frac{\log\kappa}{\beta}+\frac{\beta(\kappa-1)}{2\kappa^{2}}\Bigr)=\Bigl(\frac{2(\kappa-1)\log\kappa}{\kappa^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

(C.1)

We remark that this bound can also be obtained without the Parisi formula, by applying a well-known upper bound of the maximum of Gaussian processes as in
[
24
, Proposition 1.1.3]
to the centered Hamiltonian (
2.1
).
Now, recall the lower bound
[
18
, Inequality (0.6)]
for the limiting unconstrained ground state energy,

\frac{2}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{GSE}_{N,\kappa}.

Combining the last two displays, we obtain color symmetry breaking at zero temperature whenever

\Bigl(\frac{2(\kappa-1)\log\kappa}{\kappa^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/2}<\frac{2}{3\sqrt{\pi}}.

One can numerically check that
\kappa\geq 56
guarantees the last inequality and, hence, color symmetry breaking at zero temperature.
