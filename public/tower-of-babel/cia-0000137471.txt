1. Report prepared on ll December 1961

a. The National Revolutionary Movement had hoped to stir up
serious street disturbances on the arrival of President Kennedy in
Bogota. However, these plans will be hampered because almoet all
members of the Department of Administrative Security (DAS) in
Bogota have been assigned to securing the personal safety of the °
President. Suspicious leftists will be rounded up before the President's
arrival. ;

b. General Gustavo ROJAS Pinilla has issued orders to the
National Popular Alliance (ANP) to study the possibility of assembling
10,000 ROJAS sympathizers at El Dorado Airport, the American
Embassy, or the American Embassy residence to welcome President
Kennedy. The crowd is to shout “yivas" to President Kennedy and
ROJAS and “abajo” to the oligarchy. Some members of the National
Command of the ANP are opposing the demonstration, because they feel
it would be prejudicial to the ANP. Proponents of the demondration
believe it would be beneficial because it would be an obvious expression
of sentiment by the people that President Nennedy should not support
the oppressive oligarchy.

c.’ A zone committee of the Communist Party of Colombia (PCC)
in Bogota will hold a meeting on 14 or 15 December to effect distribution
of 5,000 leaflets calling attention to President Kennedy's visit. Pre-
sumably, the leaflets will be in derogation of Pregident Kennedy per-
sonally or of the GOC.

d. Because there will be a large group of U.S. police, FBI agents,
Colombian police, detectives and soldiers in Bogota, the PCC has decided
that demonstrations would not be to the advantage of the PCC. The PCC
has decided to hold meetings in public squares, streets and factories to
give the Party opinion on the significance of the President's visit.

e. The director of Prensa Latina (PL) in Bogota and representatives
of the Communist Youth of Colombia tos CC} printed leaflets to incite
members of the UJCC to create disturbances in the bull ring and the
football stadium on 10 December. Placards and banners were to be dis-
played. The National Police have the PL office under aurvelllance and
have restricted admittance to the office to 3 persons.

Zz. Report prepared on 12 December 1961

a. An official Colombian Government statement which appeared
in the 1} December issue of El Siglo suggeated a warning to those who
would plan hostile action during President Kennedy's visit but appeared
as & mandate for the people to welceme the President. The announce-
ment said that the state of siege is still in effect, but guaranteed the
public the right to peaceful participation in the coming political cam-
paigns. The statement specified that strict jail sentences would be en-
forced for those causing disorders.

b. According to the 1! December issue of Vanguardia Liberal, of
Bucaramanga, Alfonso LOPEZ Michelsen, head ol the Liberal Revolu-
tionary Movement (MRL}, changed the international policy of the MRL
with respect to the United States. In a speech at the MRL convention
in Bucaramanga, LOPEZ was emphatic in calling the Alliance for
Progress an admirable step. Following LOPEZ's endorsement of
Alliance for Progress a proposal declaring President Kennedy
persona non grata was rejected.

c. Fidel CASTRO's provocation for a break in diplomatic relations
between Colombia and Cuba was seen as an attempt to disrupt President
Kennedy's visit. Ali major media are in full support of the Colombian
Government's position. Media quoted leaders of industry, labor, the Church,
and professions as supporting the Government action. Leftist reaction
to the break was slow as avidenced by the long silence of Alfonse LOPEZ
Michelsen, leader of the MRL, before his statement acknowledging
CASTRO's provocation but interpreting the break in relations as weaken-
ing the Colombian Government's authority and uncommitted position at
the Council of Ministers meeting.

d. The Bogota and provincial papers report that security prepara-
tions for President Kennedy's visit have been completed. The papers
report that no disturbances are expected. El Espectador reports pre-
parations have been made for an “unprecedented public welcome" for
President Kennedy. Official, sem{-official, and private sources report
receiving numerous requests in organising a welcome for the President.
Large gatherings are anticipated at Cuidad Techo, a self-help develop-
ment project, which President Kennedy will inaugurate and along the
entire route into Bogota. Delegations from outlying provinces are
expected to participate in the welcome.
