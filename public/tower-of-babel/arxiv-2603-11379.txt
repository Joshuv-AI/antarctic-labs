Proof Outline.

The proofs of all main theorems begin with a reduction from the class of all
K_{t,t}
-induced-minor-free graphs to the subclass of such graphs with degeneracy bounded by a constant.
To obtain this reduction, we show that every
K_{t,t}
-induced-minor-free graph

admits a partition of
V(G)
into a constant number of parts such that each connected component of every part is contained in a ball of radius four in

(this is Theorem

G^{\prime}
of

with bounded chromatic number, and hence bounded clique number. Since

remains

-induced-minor-free, by a theorem in
[
7

By construction, separators consisting of a bounded number of balls, as well as collections of vertex-disjoint and pairwise anticomplete paths in

-induced-minor-free graphs whose degeneracy is bounded by a constant.

A number of recent

consider the problem of efficiently finding (or proving the existence of) separators that can be covered by a small number of sets from a prescribed family
\mathcal{F}
, by rounding the corresponding linear programming relaxation. In general, these linear programs may have large integrality gaps. However, positive results are known when
\mathcal{F}
has additional structure – for example when the sets are cliques
[

]
or when they have poly-logarithmic independence number
[
12
]
, where poly-logarithmic upper bounds on the integrality gaps can be obtained. We identify another type of
\mathcal{F}
, that we call layered families, which is amenable to the techniques used in these papers.

Let
G
be a graph and
\Pi
be an ordered partition of
V(G)
. We define the layered family of
G
corresponding to
\Pi
as follows. First, remove all edges whose endpoints lie in the same part of
\Pi

For each source, that is, each vertex of indegree zero in the resulting orientation, include in the family the set of all vertices reachable from it by a directed path. In graphs of bounded degeneracy, there exists an ordered partition
\Pi
for which the layered family satisfy the following useful properties: (1) the integrality gap of the linear programming relaxation for separators using sets of this family is at most poly-logarithmic, (2) for every vertex

, and (3) every set in

is contained in a ball of radius

For Theorem

For Theorem

Finally, for Theorem

.
We use

to denote the set of centers of elements of

. If

, then we use

to denote the element of

.
Finally, for a vertex subset

, we define its

\displaystyle\widehat{S}\;

\displaystyle:=\;\{\,u\in V(G)\mid\exists v\in S\text{ such that }u\text{ is an ancestor of }v\,\},

\displaystyle\widecheck{S}\;

\displaystyle:=\;\{\,u\in V(G)\mid\exists v\in S\text{ such that }u\text{ is a descendant of }v\,\}.

We say that a vertex set is

(resp.,

)

if the upward (resp., downward) closure of the set is itself.
For vertices

, a

Now we are ready to discuss some key properties of layered families. It follows from definition that
\mathcal{F}
covers

and that the center of each set in

is unique to that set. Furthermore, elements of

are downward closed by definition.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph and

be a positive integer. If

is a layered family of

with thickness at most

, then for every

and every

, there exists a path in

from

that contains at most

or

.

Proof.

Let
\Pi
be the ordered partition of

such that
\mathcal{F}
is the layered family corresponding to

. Let

be a set with center

. By definition, there exists a directed path from

to

in the layer graph of
(G,\Pi)
, contained within

. Let

be such a path, where

and

. Each vertex

is a child of

, and a parent of a vertex must lie in a part of

. Since

, and therefore

. Thus,

is a path from

to

.

Now let

be two vertices in

and let

and
P_{v}
be the paths in

from

and

to the center of

and

, respectively. These two paths imply the existence of a

–

of length at most

contained within the set of ancestors of

or

. Hence, the shortest

has length at most

∎

Finally, we prove the following property of upward minimal paths.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a graph and

is a layered family of

Suppose for contradiction that there exists

. Let

. We construct a

, since we removed at least

. Note that

Lemma 4.2

Let

Lemma 4.3

5
Using the Dual of A–B Separator LP

The main theorem that we prove in this section is the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be a graph,
k
be a positive integer and
\mathcal{F}
be a layered family of
G
with thickness at most
k
. Let
A,B\subseteq V(G)
be vertex subsets of

.
Then, for every
\ell\geq\frac{1}{6}\cdot\log 4|\mathcal{F}|
, there exists a multiset

of induced

–

paths in

of cardinality at least

, such that

•

For every

, the number of paths in
\mathcal{Q}
that intersect

is at most

•

Each path in
\mathcal{Q}
intersects

at most

where

denotes the optimum value of LP
1
corresponding to
(G,\mathcal{F},A,B)
.

Proof.

Since
f=0
is trivially true, let us assume that
f
is positive. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of all induced paths

–
B
in
G
. We describe the dual of LP

using non-negative real variables
\{y_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}

Maximize :

\displaystyle\quad\sum\limits_{P\in\mathcal{P}}y_{P}

(3)

Subject To :

\displaystyle\quad\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
P\cap F\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}y_{P}\leq 1

\displaystyle\forall F\in\mathcal{F}

Observe that, by strong duality
[

]
, the dual LP also has the same optimum value

as the primal. We begin the proof of the theorem, with the following claim:

Claim 5.1.1
.

There exists an optimal solution to LP

where, for every
P\in\mathcal{P}
we have

only if
P
is upward minimal.

Proof of the Claim. .

Consider the optimal solution
\{y_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
to LP
3
with the smallest number of variables that have a non-zero assignment to upward non-minimal paths. If there exists an upward non-minimal path
P^{\prime}
with
y_{P^{\prime}}>0
, then let

be any path that witness its upward non-minimality, i.e., let
P_{o}
satisfy
\widehat{P}_{o}\subsetneq\widehat{P}^{\prime}
, or
\widehat{P}_{o}=\widehat{P}^{\prime}
and
|V(P_{o})|<|V(P^{\prime})|
.
We claim that the assignment
\{y^{\prime}_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
, defined by

y^{\prime}_{P}=\begin{cases}y_{P_{o}}+y_{P^{\prime}},&\text{if }P=P_{o},\\
0,&\text{if }P=P^{\prime},\\
y_{P},&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

is a feasible solution. This contradicts the definition of
\{y_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
, since
\{y^{\prime}_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
attains the same (optimal) objective function value but has one fewer nonzero variable that corresponds to an upward non-minimal path.

To this end, observe that any constraint of the LP that does not involve
y^{\prime}_{P_{o}}
remains satisfied, since the values of all other variables in
\{y^{\prime}_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
do not exceed those in
\{y_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
. Now consider a constraint that involves
y^{\prime}_{P_{o}}
and let

be the element of
\mathcal{F}
it corresponds to. If

, then
\widehat{P}_{o}\cap F\neq\emptyset
, and consequently
\widehat{P}^{\prime}\cap F\neq\emptyset
as well. Moreover, if
\widehat{P}^{\prime}\cap F\neq\emptyset
, then as elements of
\mathcal{F}
are downward closed,

must contain a vertex of

, and hence

. Therefore, any constraint that contains
y^{\prime}_{P_{o}}
also contains
y^{\prime}_{P^{\prime}}
, and since
y^{\prime}_{P_{o}}+y^{\prime}_{P^{\prime}}=y_{P^{\prime}}+y_{P^{o}}
, these constraints also remain satisfied. Thus, all constraints remain satisfied under
\{y^{\prime}_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
, which concludes the proof of the claim.
∎

Let
\{y_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
be an optimal solution to LP

, satisfying

only if
P
is minimal. Observe that
\mathcal{D}:=\{\,y_{P}/f\,\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
is a probability distribution over
\mathcal{P}
, as every

take non-negative values and satisfy
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}y_{P}=f
. Let
\mathcal{Q}:=\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in[\,f\cdot\ell\,]}
be

\mathcal{D}
over the set
\mathcal{P}
. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
, and let
\chi_{F}
denote the number of paths in
\mathcal{Q}
that intersect
F
. We define
\chi_{\mathcal{F}}:=\max\{\chi_{F}\,|\,F\in\mathcal{F}\}
. For every
i\in[\,f\cdot\ell\,]
, we have that,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}\end{subarray}}\left[\,Q_{i}\cap F\neq\emptyset\,\right]\quad=\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
P\cap F\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}\end{subarray}}\left[\,Q_{i}=P\,\right]\quad=\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
P\cap F\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{y_{P}}{f}\quad\leq\quad\frac{1}{f}

By linearity of expectation, it follows that the expected value of
\chi_{F}
is at most
\ell
. Applying union bound over all
F\in\mathcal{F}
and the Chernoff bound from Theorem
2.1
to
\chi_{F}
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we get :

\mathbb{P}\left[\,\chi_{\mathcal{F}}\;<\;6\cdot\ell\,\right]\quad\geq\quad 1\ -\ \sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{P}\left[\,\chi_{F}\;\geq\;6\cdot\ell\,\right]\quad\geq\quad 1\ -\ |\mathcal{F}|\cdot 2^{-6\cdot\ell}\quad\geq\quad\frac{3}{4}

Hence, with probability at least
\frac{3}{4}
, the sampled multiset has cardinality at least
f\cdot\ell
, consists of induced
A
–
B
paths in
G
, and satisfies that for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, at most
6\ell
of these paths intersect
F
. Therefore, there must exist some multiset
\mathcal{Q}
with these properties. Finally, observe that
\mathcal{Q}
can only contain upward minimal paths since
\mathcal{D}
assigns non-zero probability only to them. This combined with Lemma
3.2
, implies the statement of the theorem.
∎

6
Rounding Balanced Separator LP

We begin this section by defining the
balanced separator LP
. Let
G
be a graph,
\mathcal{F}
be a layered family of
G
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}
be the set of centers of elements in
\mathcal{F}
and
X\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}
. For each pair
u,v\in X
, let
\mathcal{P}_{u,v}
denote the set of all induced paths from
u
to
v
in
G
. We describe the balanced separator LP corresponding to the instance
(G,X,\mathcal{F})
, using non-negative real variables
x_{F}
and
d_{u,v}
, defined for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
u,v\in X
.

Minimize:

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}x_{F}

(4)

Subject to:

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in X}d_{u,v}\geq\frac{|X|}{10}

\displaystyle\forall u\in X

\displaystyle d_{u,v}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap P\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x_{F}

\displaystyle\forall u,v\in X,\ P\in\mathcal{P}_{u,v}

\displaystyle d_{u,v}\leq 1

\displaystyle\forall u,v\in X

We remark that the above linear program is a variant of the one used in the Leighton–Rao rounding scheme
[
25
]
, adapted to operate on vertex sets rather than individual vertices, as in
[
24
,
12
]
. We are now ready to state the main theorem proved in this section:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
G
be a graph,
k
be a positive integer and
\mathcal{F}
be a layered family of
G
with thickness at most
k
. Then for every subset
X\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}
denotes the set of centers of the elements of
\mathcal{F}
, there exists an
(X,\tfrac{95}{100})
-balanced separator
S
in
G
such that
S
is downward closed and

\mathrm{fcov}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)\ \leq\ 5000k\cdot\log 2n\cdot\log(f+4)\cdot f

where
f
is the optimum value of LP
4
corresponding to
(G,X,\mathcal{F})
.

For the remainder of this section, we fix a graph
G
, the layered family
\mathcal{F}
and the set
X\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}
. We also fix an optimal solution to LP
4
corresponding to
(G,X,\mathcal{F})
. Let
x_{F}
and
d_{u,v}
, for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
u,v\in X
, denote the values assigned to the corresponding variables in this solution. Let
f
denote the objective function value of this solution.

For
U\subseteq V(G)
, we define
\mu(U):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap U\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x_{F}
.
For every
v\in V(G)
, we define
x_{v}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\ni v\end{subarray}}x_{F}
. Also, for every
u,v\in V(G)
, we define
d(u,v):=\min\,\{\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap P\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x_{F}\,|\,P\in\mathcal{P}_{u,v}\,\}
. Now, we make some observations about the function
d
.

Obervation 6.2
.

If
u,v\in X
, then
\min\{d(\bar{u},v),1\}\geq d_{u,v}

Obervation 6.3
.

If
u,v,w\in V(G)
, then
d(u,w)\leq d(u,v)+d(v,w)
. In particular, if
(v,w)\in E(G)
and
u\in V(G)
, then
d(u,w)\leq d(u,v)+x_{w}
.

Observation
6.2
follows immediately from the LP constraints:
d_{u,v}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap P\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x_{F}
and
d_{u,v}\leq 1
for every
u,v\in X
and
P\in\mathcal{P}_{u,v}
. Furthermore, note that
d(u,v)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap Q\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x_{F}
for any
u
–
v
walk
Q
in
G
, as we can always find an induced
u
–
v
path in
G[Q]
. Hence, Observation
6.3
follows from the fact that if
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are induced
u
–
v
and
v
–
w
paths realizing
d(u,v)
and
d(v,w)
respectively, then appending
P_{2}
to
P_{1}
yields a
u
–
w
walk
Q
satisfying
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap Q\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x_{F}\leq d(u,v)+d(v,w)
.

Let
\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{500\log(f+4)}
,
\ell_{\max}=\lceil\log\frac{f+4}{\varepsilon}\rceil
and define
r_{i}:=3i\cdot\varepsilon
, where
i
is a positive integer. Let
Z_{0}:=\{\,v\in V(G)\mid x_{v}\geq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2k}\,\}
, and for every
u\in V(G)
,
C\subseteq V(G)
and positive real number
r
, define

B_{C}(u,r):=\{v\in C\mid d(u,v)\leq r\}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{C}(u,r):=B_{C}(u,r+3\varepsilon)\setminus B_{C}(u,r+\varepsilon)

We note that every
F\in\mathcal{F}
that contains a vertex
u\in Z_{0}
also contains every descendant of
u
. This implies that
x_{v}>x_{u}
and consequently
v\in Z_{0}
for every descendant
v
of
u
. Hence
Z_{0}
is downward closed.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
u
and
v
be vertices in
V(G)\setminus Z_{0}
that satisfy
d(u,v)\geq\varepsilon
. Then there does not exist
F\in\mathcal{F}
that contains both
u
and
v
.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction, suppose there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
, that contains both
u
and
v
. Let
P
be the
u
–
v
path in
G[F]
of length at most
2k-1
that use only ancestors of
u
or
v
, whose existence is asserted by Lemma
3.1
. Let
\bar{v}
be a vertex of this path and without loss of generality let
\bar{v}
be an ancestor of
v
. Since the
v
belongs to every set in
\mathcal{F}
that contains
\bar{v}
, we have
x_{\bar{v}}\leq x_{v}\leq\max\,\{x_{u},x_{v}\}
. Hence
\bar{v}\in V(G)\setminus Z_{0}
, and consequently
P
is a path in
G-Z_{0}
. Thus, we have

d(u,v)\ \ \leq\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap P\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x_{F}\ \ <\ \ (2k-1)\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2k}\ \ <\ \ \varepsilon

which gives the desired contradiction.
∎

Now we state and prove three properties in Lemma
6.5
, Lemma
6.6
and Lemma
6.7
that we require to implement a version of the Leighton-Rao rounding scheme
[
25
]
and prove Theorem
6.1
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
Z\subseteq V(G)
be a superset of
Z_{0}
. Suppose that
G-Z
contains a connected component
C
such that
|C\cap X|>\frac{95|X|}{100}
, and let
\bar{u}\in X\cap C
and
\ell\in[\,\ell_{\max}\,]
. Then we have,

\left|X\setminus B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1})\right|\geq\frac{5|X|}{100}

Proof.

Since
\ell\in[\,\ell_{\max}\,]
, we have

r_{\ell+1}\ \ \leq\ \ r_{\ell_{\max}+1}\ \ =\ \ 3\varepsilon\bigl(\lceil\log\tfrac{f+4}{\varepsilon}\rceil+1\bigr)\ \ \leq\ \ \frac{3\log\bigl(2000\cdot(f+4)\cdot\log(f+4)\bigr)}{500\log(f+4)}\ \ \leq\ \ \frac{1}{20}

where the third inequality upper bounds
\lceil\log\tfrac{f+4}{\varepsilon}\rceil+1
by
\log\tfrac{4(f+4)}{\varepsilon}
and substitutes the value of
\varepsilon
and the final inequality follows from the facts that for
x\geq 4
we have
\log x\geq 2
and
\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\leq\tfrac{3}{4}
. Now, assume, for the sake of contradiction, that
\left|X\setminus B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1})\right|<\frac{5|X|}{100}
.
Then we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in X}\min\{d(\bar{u},v),1\}

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{v\in X\cap B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1})}\min\{d(\bar{u},v),1\}\quad+\sum_{v\in X\setminus B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1})}\min\{d(\bar{u},v),1\}

\displaystyle<\quad\frac{95|X|\cdot r_{\ell+1}}{100}\ +\ \frac{5|X|\cdot 1}{100}

\displaystyle<\quad\frac{|X|}{10}.

But, Observation
6.2
implies the inequality
\sum_{v\in X}\min\{d(\bar{u},v),1\}\geq\sum_{v\in X}d_{\bar{u},v}
which contradicts the constraint
\sum_{v\in X}d_{\bar{u},v}\geq\frac{|X|}{10}
of LP
4
. Thus, it follows that
|X\setminus B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1})|\geq\frac{5|X|}{100}
.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
Z\subseteq V(G)
be a superset of
Z_{0}
. Suppose that
G-Z
contains a connected component
C
such that
|C\cap X|>\frac{95|X|}{100}
, and let
\bar{u}\in X\cap C
. Then there exists
\ell\in[\,\ell_{\max}\,]
such that
\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))\leq\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
.

Proof.

For this, we need the following claims:

Claim 6.6.1
.

\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{1}))\geq 2\varepsilon

Proof of the Claim. .

Consider a path
P
in
G[C]
from
\bar{u}
to a vertex
w
such that
w\in X\cap(C\setminus B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell_{\max}+1}))
. Note that such a vertex exists by Lemma
6.5
. Let
P^{\prime}:=(\bar{u},v_{1},\dots,v_{p})
be the subpath of
P
such that the successor of
v_{p}
in
P
(say
v_{p+1}
) is the first vertex in
P
that does not belong to
B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{1})
.
P^{\prime}
is well defined, since
w
does not belong to
B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{1})
and is non-empty since
\bar{u}\in B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{1})
. Hence, by Observation
6.3
, we get:

\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{1}))\ \ \geq\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap P^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x_{F}\ \ \geq\ \ d(\bar{u},v_{p})\ \ \geq\ \ d(\bar{u},v_{p+1})-x_{v_{p+1}}\ \ \geq\ \ 3\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{2k}\ \ >\ \ 2\varepsilon

∎

Claim 6.6.2
.

\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1}))\geq\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))+\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
for every
\ell\in[\,\ell_{\max}\,]
.

Proof of the Claim. .

Let
v\in\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell})
, and let
F\in\mathcal{F}
be a set containing
v
, with
w
denoting the center of
F
. Observe that
d(\bar{u},v)>r_{\ell}+\varepsilon
while
d(\bar{u},v^{\prime})\leq r_{\ell}
for every
v^{\prime}\in B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell})
. Hence by Observation
6.3
, we get
d(v^{\prime},v)\geq\varepsilon
, and by Lemma
6.4
we conclude that there does not exist an
F\in\mathcal{F}
that contains both
v
and
v^{\prime}
. As every set
F\in\mathcal{F}
that intersects
\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell})
has an empty intersection with
B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell})
, it follows that no set in
\mathcal{F}
contributes to both
\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
and
\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
. Moreover, from the definitions it follows that every set
F\in\mathcal{F}
contributing to
\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))+\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
also contributes to
\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1}))
. These two observations together imply the lemma.
∎

Now, combining Claims
6.6.1
and
6.6.2
, we conclude that if
\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))>\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
for every
\ell\in[\,\ell_{\max}\,]
, then
\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell_{\max}}))>2^{\ell_{\max}-1}\cdot\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{1}))\geq f
, while
\mu(G)\leq f
, which yields a contradiction. Hence we conclude that there exists some
\ell\in[\,\ell_{\max}\,]
for which we have
\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))\leq\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
, which proves Lemma
6.6
.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
Z\subseteq V(G)
be a downward closed superset of
Z_{0}
. Suppose that
G-Z
contains a connected component
C
such that
|C\cap X|>\frac{95|X|}{100}
, and let
\bar{u}\in X\cap C
and
\ell\in[\,\ell_{\max}\,]
. If
A^{\prime}:=B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}+\varepsilon)
,
B^{\prime}:=C\setminus B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1})
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\{\,F\cap C\mid F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }F\cap C\neq\emptyset\,\}
, then there exists a fractional
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
-separator using
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
in
G[C]
of weight at most
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
.

Proof.

Consider the assignment
\{x^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}}\}_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
defined as follows. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
and let
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the set corresponding to
F\cap C
. If
F\cap\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell})
is nonempty, define
x^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}}:=\frac{x_{F}}{\varepsilon}
; otherwise, define
x^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}}:=0
. We claim that
\{x^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}}\}_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
is a fractional
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
-separator using
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
in
G[C]
.

Let
P:=(v_{1},\dots,v_{p})
be an
A^{\prime}
–
B^{\prime}
path in
G[C]
. Let
P^{\prime}:=(v^{\prime}_{1},\dots,v^{\prime}_{q})
be the contiguous subpath of
P
with the smallest number of vertices such that the predecessor of
v^{\prime}_{1}
in
P
(denoted
v^{\prime}_{0}
) satisfies
d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{0})\leq r_{\ell}+\varepsilon
(that is,
v^{\prime}_{0}\in A^{\prime}
), and the successor of
v^{\prime}_{q}
in
P
(denoted
v^{\prime}_{q+1}
) satisfies
d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{q+1})>r_{\ell}+3\varepsilon
(that is,
v^{\prime}_{q+1}\in B^{\prime}
). Note that
P^{\prime}
is well-defined, since the subpath obtained by removing the first and last vertices of
P
is a path that satisfies the requirements. Moreover, by definition, we have
V(P^{\prime})\subseteq\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell})
. Finally,
P^{\prime}
must be non-empty; otherwise, applying Observation
6.3
to
\{v^{\prime}_{0},v^{\prime}_{q+1},\bar{u}\}
yields
d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{0})\geq d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{q+1})-x_{v^{\prime}_{q+1}}\geq r_{\ell}+3\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{2k}
, which contradicts the assumption that
d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{0})\leq r_{\ell}+\varepsilon
. Hence:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\\
F^{\prime}\cap P\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
F\cap P^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{x_{F}}{\varepsilon}\quad\geq\quad\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot d(v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{q})

\displaystyle\geq\ \quad\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot\bigl(d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{q+1})-x_{v^{\prime}_{q+1}}-d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\ \quad\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot\left(r_{\ell}+3\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{2k}-(r_{\ell}+\varepsilon+\frac{\varepsilon}{2k})\right)\quad\geq\quad 1

where the first inequality follows from the fact that any set in
\mathcal{F}
that intersects
P^{\prime}
must intersect
\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell})
. The second line follows from applying Observation
6.3
to the triples
\{v_{1},v^{\prime}_{q},\bar{u}\}
and
\{\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{q},v^{\prime}_{q+1}\}
. The last uses
d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{1})\leq d(\bar{u},v^{\prime}_{0})+x_{v^{\prime}_{0}}
and
v^{\prime}_{0},v^{\prime}_{q+1}\not\in Z_{0}
. The claimed upper bound on the weight of the fractional separator follows from the fact that
\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}x^{\prime}_{F}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
.
∎

With this, we prove the key lemma used in the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
Z\subseteq V(G)
be a downward closed superset of
Z_{0}
. If
G-Z
has a connected component
C
such that
|C\cap X|>\frac{95|X|}{100}
then there exists a partition
A\cup S\cup B
of
C
such that:

1.

S
is downward closed.

2.

S
is an
A
–
B
separator in
G-Z
.

3.

B\subsetneq C
.

4.

|A\cap X|\leq\frac{95|X|}{100}
.

5.

\mathrm{fcov}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)\leq 4000k\cdot\log 2n\cdot\log(f+4)\cdot(\mu(C)-\mu(B))
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{u}\in C\cap X
. We apply Lemma
6.6
to obtain some
\ell\in[\,\ell_{\max}\,]
such that
\mu(\delta_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))\leq\mu(B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}))
. Now, let
A^{\prime}:=B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell}+\varepsilon)
and
B^{\prime}:=C\setminus B_{C}(\bar{u},r_{\ell+1})
. We define
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
to be
\{F\cap C\ |\ F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }F\cap C\neq\emptyset\}
and prove the following claim:

Claim 6.8.1
.

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a layered family of
G[C]
with thickness at most
k
.

Proof of the Claim. .

Let
\Pi:=(L_{1},\dots,L_{k})
be an ordered partition of
V(G)
such that
\mathcal{F}
is the layered family corresponding to
(G,\Pi)
. Define
L^{\prime}_{i}:=L_{i}\cap C
for every

. We claim that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the layered family corresponding to
(G[C],\Pi^{\prime})
, where
\Pi^{\prime}:=(L^{\prime}_{1},\dots,L^{\prime}_{k})
.
To this end,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
denote the layered family of
(G[C],\Pi^{\prime})
; we prove that it equals
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. We begin by showing that
w\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}}
if and only if
w
is the center of some
F\in\mathcal{F}
that has a non-empty intersection with
C
. Observe that this implies
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}}
. For the
only if
direction, let
w\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}}
. If
w
has a parent
w^{\prime}
in
G
according to
\mathcal{F}
, it would imply that
w^{\prime}\notin C
, since otherwise,
w^{\prime}
would also be a parent of
w
in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
. But as
w\in C
and
N(C)\subseteq Z
, this now implies that
w^{\prime}\in Z
. This contradicts the assumption that
Z
is downward closed and hence
w
is the center of some
F\in\mathcal{F}
that has a non-empty intersection with
C
. For the
if
direction, let
w
be the center of some
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that
F\cap C
contains a vertex
w^{\prime}
. If
w\notin C
, then considering the
w^{\prime}
–
w
path in
G[F]
, whose existence is asserted by Lemma

, yields a vertex in
F
whose parent is not in
C
. But, by the same argument as above, this contradicts the assumption that
Z
is downward closed. Hence, we have that
w\in C
and consequently
w\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}}
.

Now, we need only show that any element
F^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
and
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
that share the same vertex

as the center, are equal. For this, let
F\in\mathcal{F}
be the element with center
w
that satisfies
F^{\prime}=F\cap C
. To see

let

be a vertex of
F^{\prime\prime}
. By definition,

can reach

in the layer graph of
(G[C],\Pi^{\prime})
, which implies that

can reach
w
in the layer graph of
(G,\Pi)
, and hence

. Conversely, let

be a vertex of

. All ancestors of

must lie in

, since otherwise

must contain a vertex whose parent is not in

and the downward-closed property of

would be violated. Thus any vertex

that can reach

in the layer graph of
(G,\Pi)
can also reach

7
Using the Dual of Balanced Separator LP

Let
G
be a graph, let
\mathcal{F}
be a layered family of
G
, and let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}
be the set of centers of elements in
\mathcal{F}
. Let
X\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}
and for each pair
u,v\in X
, let
\mathcal{P}_{u,v}
denote the set of all induced paths from
u
to

in
G
. We now describe the dual of LP
4
, corresponding to the instance
(G,X,\mathcal{F})
. It uses non-negative real variables
\rho_{u}
,
\eta_{u,v}
, and
\gamma_{u,v,P}

and path

Maximize :

\displaystyle\quad\frac{|X|}{10}\sum\limits_{u\in X}\rho_{u}-\sum\limits_{u,v\in X}\eta_{u,v}

(5)

Subject To :

\displaystyle\quad\rho_{u}-\eta_{u,v}-\sum\limits_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{u,v}}\gamma_{u,v,P}\leq 0

\displaystyle\forall u,v\in X

\displaystyle\quad\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in X\\
P\in\mathcal{P}_{u,v}\\
P\cap F\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\gamma_{u,v,P}\leq 1

\displaystyle\forall F\in\mathcal{F}

It is easy to verify that the above is indeed the dual of LP
4
. Having established this, we are now ready to state the main theorem proved in this section. We remark that the general framework of this section closely follows that of Section 7 in
[
12
]
. In that work, whenever the optimum value of LP
5
is high, one can sub-sample a set of paths that induce a subgraph of large treewidth. The main difference in this section is that we show it is possible to sample

paths that induce a subgraph of large treewidth. The upward minimality additionally guarantees that, for every sampled path

and every set

, the intersection

, which is a property we require for our main results.

Theorem 7.1

Let

be a graph,

, where

denotes the set of centers of the elements of

•

•

For every

.

whenever

For the remainder of this section, we fix a graph

\mathcal{F}
and the set

. We begin with the following Lemma:

Lemma 7.2

There exists an optimal solution to LP
5
where, for every

Proof.

Let
\rho_{u}
,
\eta_{u,v}
, and
\gamma_{u,v,P}
, for every
u,v\in X

P\in\mathcal{P}_{u,v}

v^{\prime}

be any path that witness its upward non-minimality, i.e., let

\widehat{P}_{o}\subsetneq\widehat{P}^{\prime}

\eta^{\prime}_{u,v}=\eta_{u,v}

\gamma^{\prime}_{u,v,P}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{u,v,P_{o}}+\gamma_{u,v,P^{\prime}},&\text{if }u=u^{\prime},v=v^{\prime},P=P_{o}\\

\gamma_{u,v,P},&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

is a feasible solution. This leads to a contradiction as the new solution attains the same (optimal) objective function value but has one fewer non-zero variable corresponding to an upward non-minimal path.

To this end, let us consider the constraint in LP
5
. For ease of presentation, we refer to the constraints corresponding to vertex pairs in

u^{\prime}
and

contains both
\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P_{o}}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P^{\prime}}
, and since
\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P_{o}}+\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P^{\prime}}=\gamma_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P_{o}}+\gamma_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P^{\prime}}
its left-hand side remains unchanged. Consequently, this constraint also remains satisfied, which completes the case of type-one constraints.
Now considering type-two, we note that any type-two constraint that does not involve
\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P_{o}}
remains satisfied, since the values of all other
\gamma^{\prime}
variables do not exceed those of
\gamma

that involves

, and consequently

whose ancestor lies in

P^{\prime}
, and hence

is non-empty. Therefore, any constraint that contains
\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P_{o}}

\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P^{\prime}}
, and since
\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P_{o}}+\gamma^{\prime}_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P^{\prime}}=\gamma_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P_{o}}+\gamma_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},P^{\prime}}

Let us fix an optimal solution to LP

Lemma 7.3
.

The optimal solution has the following properties:

\sum_{v\in X}\eta_{u,v}\leq\frac{|X|}{10}\rho_{u}

Given any feasible solution to the LP, we can always obtain a new assignment by choosing a

We define

Lemma 8.1

Theorem 8.5

be a graph in
\mathcal{C}^{c}_{d}

be a positive integer and
\mathcal{F}

8.3
, we conclude that
\mathrm{cov}_{\mathcal{F}}(S^{\prime})\,\leq\,50k\cdot g\cdot\log^{3}2n^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}
be a cover of
S^{\prime}
of size at most
50k\cdot g\cdot\log^{3}2n^{\prime}
, and redefine
S^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}F
. Let
S:=\{v\in V(G)\mid v\in C,\text{ for some }v_{C}\in S^{\prime}\}
. We claim that
S
is an
A
–
B
separator with the desired properties.
First, observe that if it is not an
A
–
B
separator, then
G-S
must contain an
A
–
B
path
P
. We obtain a walk
P^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}-S^{\prime}
by replacing each vertex
v\in V(P)
with the vertex
v_{C}
, where for some
i\in[\pi_{4}(G)]
,
C
is the connected component of
G[X_{i}]
containing
v
. By construction of
G^{\prime}
, consecutive vertices of
P
map to vertices in
G^{\prime}
that are the same or adjacent, and since
P
avoids
S
, the walk
P^{\prime}
avoids
S^{\prime}
. Moreover, as
P
starts in
A
and ends in
B
,
P^{\prime}
starts in
A^{\prime}
and ends in
B^{\prime}
. But this contradicts the assumption that
S^{\prime}
is an
A^{\prime}
–
B^{\prime}
separator in
G^{\prime}
.
Second, observe that since every connected component
C
of
G[X_{i}]
for each
i\in[\pi_{4}(G)]
is
(1,4)
–coverable in
G
, for every pair of vertices
u,v\in C
there exists a
u
–
v
path on at most eight vertices in
G
. Furthermore, letting
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
denote the set of centers of the elements of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, every vertex
v_{C}\in S^{\prime}
has a path on at most
\log 2n^{\prime}
vertices in
G^{\prime}
to some vertex of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
. Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq V(G)
be a subset that contains one arbitrary vertex of
v_{C}
for every
v_{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
. Combining the above two observations, we conclude that every vertex of
S
has a path on at most
8\log 2n^{\prime}\leq 8\log 2n
vertices to some vertex of
\mathcal{C}
. Therefore, the maximum size of a distance
16\log 2n
-independent set in
S
is
|\mathcal{C}|\leq 50k\cdot g\cdot\log^{3}2n
, which completes the proof of the theorem.
