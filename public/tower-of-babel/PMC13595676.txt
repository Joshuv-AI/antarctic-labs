Subclinical myocardial changes after hematopoietic stem cell transplantation: limited predictive value of early biomarkers and strain imaging
Authors: Ana Carolina Oliver, Federico Superchi, Roberto Superchi, Matilde Canabal, Carolina Errecarte, Ricardo Mosquera, Ada Caneiro, Silvia Pierri, Sebastian Galeano, Eloisa Riva, Laura Bello, Regis Gai, Silvana Jubin, Pablo Muxi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCardiotoxicity is a relevant late complication after hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. Early identification of subclinical myocardial injury could allow preventive interventions, yet the optimal diagnostic approach in this setting remains undefined.ObjectivesTo evaluate the incidence of subclinical myocardial damage through cardiac biomarkers and echocardiography in adult HSCT recipients, and to determine their association with subsequent clinical cardiotoxicity.MethodsThis prospective, single-center study enrolled 158 adult patients undergoing HSCT between 2017 and 2020. NT-proBNP, troponins, and CK-MB were measured at baseline, post-infusion, day 14, and day 30. Echocardiography was performed at baseline and day 30. Subclinical myocardial damage was defined as the presence of asymptomatic myocardial injury during chemotherapy, as evidenced by new biomarker elevation and/or a relative decline > 15% in global longitudinal strain from baseline. Clinical cardiotoxicity was assessed at 1 year.ResultsDuring the first 100 days, 79.7% of patients exhibited NT-proBNP elevation, whereas troponins and CK-MB remained unchanged. A ≥ 15% GLS reduction occurred in 14.6% of cases, without correlation to NT-proBNP changes (p = 0.3). Subclinical myocardial damage was detected in 83.5% of patients and was associated with older age and females but not with prior anthracycline exposure or conditioning regimen. At 1 year, the cumulative incidence of clinical cardiotoxicity was 4.4%, with no predictive association with early biomarker or strain alterations.ConclusionsSubclinical myocardial alterations are frequent during the early post-HSCT period but lack predictive value for subsequent clinical cardiotoxicity. NT-proBNP elevation and GLS reduction likely represent transient myocardial stress rather than irreversible injury. Routine early post-transplant biomarker and strain monitoring may have limited clinical utility.Keywords: Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, Cardiotoxicity, Subclinical myocardial damage

Introduction

Cardiovascular toxicity is an increasingly relevant complication in cancer survivorship and may be particularly challenging in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT), where multiple treatment and transplant-related factors may contribute to myocardial injury. Despite this, early cardiac dysfunction after HSCT remains poorly characterized, especially in adults [1–3].

Although biomarkers and myocardial deformation imaging have emerged as promising tools for the early detection of cancer therapy–related cardiac injury, their value in the HSCT setting is not well established. Whether early subclinical abnormalities identify patients at greater risk for subsequent clinical cardiotoxicity remains uncertain [4–10].

Accordingly, we assessed subclinical myocardial damage using cardiac biomarkers and echocardiography in adult HSCT recipients and examined its association with clinical cardiotoxicity during the first year after transplantation.

Methods

Study design and participants

We performed a prospective, single-center cohort study at the British Hospital’s Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation Unit in Montevideo, Uruguay. Inclusion criteria: Adults (≥ 18 years) undergoing autologous or allogeneic HSCT with Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0–1. Exclusion criteria included: baseline left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) < 50% and cardiac amyloidosis (In patients with systemic amyloidosis, cardiac involvement was excluded based on cardiac magnetic resonance imaging findings). All participants provided written informed consent; the protocol was approved by the British Hospital Review Board and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki.

The design of the study included serial assessments of cardiac biomarkers and echocardiography to identify early changes that could be indicative of future cardiotoxicity.

Biomarkers

Venous blood samples were collected in all patients for NT-proBNP, cardiac troponin I (TnI), troponin T (TnT), and creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB) at pre-conditioning (baseline), day + 1 (post-infusion), day + 14, and day + 30. Patients with an abnormal biomarker value at day + 30 underwent an additional measurement at day + 100; this targeted re-testing was prespecified to distinguish transient elevations from persistent myocardial injury, whereas biomarkers were not repeated at day + 100 when day + 30 values were normal. Reference ranges were: NT-proBNP 0–125 pg/mL, TnI < 64 pg/mL, TnT 1–50 pg/mL, and CK-MB < 5 ng/mL.

Echocardiography

Transthoracic echocardiography was performed at baseline and day + 30 by the same operator using the same ultrasound system (Vivid series, GE HealthCare). Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was measured by two-dimensional (2D) echocardiography using the biplane method of disks (modified Simpson’s rule). Global longitudinal strain (GLS) was assessed by 2D speckle-tracking echocardiography using vendor-specific software (EchoPAC, GE HealthCare). Patients with a relative GLS reduction at day + 30 underwent a repeat study at day + 100, applying the same rationale used for biomarkers.

All patients underwent clinical follow-up until June 30, 2025.

Definitions

Cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction (CTRCD)

was defined according to the American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) and European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging (EACVI) consensus as an absolute decrease in LVEF of ≥ 10% to a value below 53%, confirmed on repeat assessment [11].

Abnormal strain

was defined as a relative reduction of ≥ 15% from baseline in global longitudinal strain (GLS), indicating subclinical left ventricular systolic dysfunction.

Subclinical myocardial damage

was operationalized as asymptomatic myocardial injury during the early post-transplant period evidenced by a new significant biomarker elevation and/or a relative decline > 15% from baseline in GLS, in the absence of overt heart failure symptoms or a clinically significant reduction in left ventricular ejection fraction. This endpoint was evaluated during the first 100 days after transplantation: echocardiography at baseline and day + 30 (repeated at day + 100 in abnormal cases) and biomarkers at baseline, day + 1, day + 14 and day + 30 (repeated at day + 100 in abnormal cases).

Clinical cardiac complications

were prospectively ascertained during follow-up and included acute coronary syndrome or myocardial infarction, clinically significant arrhythmias requiring medical evaluation or treatment, new or worsening hypertension, symptomatic heart failure, pericardial disease, and cardiovascular death. 11 Patients were followed for clinical cardiovascular events from transplantation to the last available clinical contact or until June 30, 2025 (median 69 months, range 0.9–99 months); the prespecified clinical endpoint was assessed at 1 year, and the 3-year cumulative incidence is also reported.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was to determine the incidence and pattern of subclinical myocardial damage during the first 100 days after stem cell transplant. The secondary endpoints were: 1.- to explore the relationship of subclinical myocardial damage with type of conditioning regimen, age, cardiac comorbidities and prior use of anthracycline; and 2.- to explore if the development of subclinical myocardial damage during transplant is related with clinical cardiac complications at 1 year.

Statistics

Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± SD or median (IQR) and compared using t-tests, ANOVA, or nonparametric equivalents; categorical variables used χ² or Fisher’s exact tests. Associations between early measures and clinical cardiotoxicity were explored using logistic regression. Analyses were conducted with SPSS; two-sided α = 0.05.

Study assessments were highly compliant: biomarkers were available at > 86% of planned time points, and echocardiography was completed in 100% at baseline and 92.4% at day + 30.

Biomarker Kinetics: During the first 30 days, 137 patients (86.7%) showed elevation of at least one biomarker. NT-proBNP was the most frequently elevated, reaching 79.7% of patients. The proportion of patients with elevated NT-proBNP peaked at day + 14 (67.1%) and decreased to 44.3% at day + 30. TnT, TnI, and CK-MB showed no meaningful change. The kinetics of all biomarkers are shown in Fig. 2. Among the 70 patients with an elevated NT-proBNP at day + 30, 28 (40%) underwent re-testing at day + 100, of whom 19 (68%) remained elevated. One patient developed atrial fibrillation, but no patient developed CTRCD within one year. To sum up, NT-proBNP: had a significant elevation during the first 100 days after transplant (p < 0.001,) in contrast with the other biomarkers, however it was not related with clinical cardiac complications.

Echocardiography and Strain: The LVEF had a slight but significant decrease between the pre transplant value and D30: Δ% ≈ −1.9%, p = 0.0044). However, no patients met the strict criteria for CTRCD. Hence, 16 patients (10%) experienced a 10% LVEF decrease between baseline and day 30, 61.5% of whom had elevated NT-proBNP. One patient develop an infectious endocarditis within 1 year.

Paired GLS measurements were available in 116/158 patients (73.4%); the main reason for missing values was a poor acoustic window. GLS worsened by a mean relative change of − 4.7% between baseline and day + 30 (p < 0.001). A relative GLS reduction ≥ 15% between baseline and day + 30 occurred in 23 patients, corresponding to 19.8% of those with paired GLS (n = 116) and 14.6% of the total cohort (n = 158). Of these 23 patients, 74% had concurrently elevated NT-proBNP, although no significant association between GLS reduction and biomarker elevation was found (p = 0.3). No significant correlations were observed between LVEF, GLS and NT-proBNP at any time point; the relationships between GLS and LVEF, and between LVEF and NT-proBNP, were weak and not statistically significant.

Subclinical myocardial damage, taking into account elevation of NT-proBNP from initial to D100 or 15% reduction of strain at D30, was presented in 132 patients (83.5%). It was associated with older age (mean 56.1 ± 11.2 vs. 44.3 ± 13.8 years, p < 0.0001) and it was more common in woman versus man (OR 3.83 (1.03–14.24)), p = 0.045. No significant associations were found with comorbidities, prior anthracycline use, or conditioning regimen. No association was observed between subclinical myocardial damage and the development of clinical cardiotoxicity at 1 year (p = 0.579).

Clinical Outcomes: With a median follow-up of 69 months (range 0.9–99), 13 patients (8.2%) developed clinical cardiac complications during follow-up (hypertension n = 6, arrhythmia n = 4, pulmonary embolism n = 1, sudden death n = 1, myocardial infarction n = 1; Table 2). Seven events occurred within the first year and six between years one and three. Eleven of these patients (85%) had presented subclinical myocardial damage within the first 100 days. Among the 7 patients who developed clinical cardiac complications within 1 year, all (100%) had subclinical myocardial damage; in contrast, 86.8% of the patients who did not develop cardiac complications within 1 year had also presented subclinical myocardial damage within the first 100 days after transplantation (p = 0.165). The 1-year cumulative incidence of cardiac complications was 4.43% and the 3-year cumulative incidence was 8.22%. Seventeen patients (10.75%) died within the first year after HSCT (disease-related 53%, infection 41%, cardiovascular 6%). After excluding patients who died within the first year from non-cardiovascular causes, the incidence of cardiac complications was 4.6%.

Discussion

Cardiovascular toxicity after HSCT remains incompletely characterized, although prior studies suggest a clinically relevant burden, with reported incidences of 5–10% [12, 13]. Arrhythmias and heart failure are the most frequent complications, likely driven by multiple transplant-related exposures, including conditioning regimens, prior anthracyclines, and infectious complications. Prior studies have identified age as a major predictor of short-term cardiovascular events, while older age, allogeneic transplantation, higher body mass index, lower baseline left ventricular ejection fraction, chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease have been associated with longer-term risk [6]. In this setting, early biomarker and echocardiographic abnormalities may reflect subclinical myocardial injury, but their link with subsequent overt clinical cardiotoxicity remains uncertain [13–15].

To our knowledge, this is one of the largest studies worldwide to comprehensively evaluate subclinical myocardial damage after HSCT. Consistent with the biomarker kinetics described above, TnI, TnT and CK-MB remained essentially unchanged during the first 100 days, whereas NT-proBNP showed a transient rise. Although a high proportion of patients exhibited NT-proBNP elevation within the first 100 days, these changes were not predictive of subsequent clinical cardiotoxicity at 1 year. Similarly, although a relative reduction in global longitudinal strain has been proposed as an important predictor of clinical cardiotoxicity [16], the 19.8% rate observed in our cohort was not associated with a higher incidence of clinical cardiovascular events. Previous studies have reported significant and persistent elevations of NT-proBNP and TnT up to day + 30, with simultaneous elevations beyond 14 days in 29.7% of patients. In contrast with our findings, those studies suggested that biomarker elevation preceded clinical cardiotoxicity, as 5 of 37 patients (13.5%) experienced an event; however, these results should be interpreted with caution given the small sample sizes and the consequent limitations in statistical robustness and external validity [17, 18]. More recently, Rotz et al. reported that, although cardiac biomarkers rise soon after transplantation, they did not correlate with subsequent cardiotoxicity in a younger population [19].

In this prospective adult HSCT cohort, early NT-proBNP elevations and strain reductions were frequent yet non-predictive of subsequent clinical cardiotoxicity. The dissociation between transient biomarker, strain changes and later events suggests reversible myocardial stress, potentially driven by inflammatory burden, hemodynamic shifts, transfusion exposure, or infection, rather than structural injury. Our findings contrast with smaller earlier studies that reported associations between biomarker rises and events, likely reflecting differences in age, timing of sampling, definitions, and statistical power.

These findings should be interpreted primarily within the autologous HSCT setting, as autologous recipients comprised 93.6% of the study population. In addition, the cohort reflects an earlier transplant era at our center, when allogeneic transplant activity was limited to 3 to 5 procedures annually. Since 2022, following implementation of a haploidentical transplant program, annual allogeneic transplant volume has increased to 10 to 12 procedures. Therefore, the study population may not fully represent the current allogeneic transplant case mix at our institution.

The main limitations of this study include its single-center design, the lack of paired strain measurements in a subset of patients due to suboptimal acoustic windows, and limited power to detect relatively infrequent events. Notwithstanding these limitations, the prospective design, standardized timing of imaging and biomarker assessments, and long-term follow-up strengthen the validity of the observations. Multicenter validation using harmonized imaging and biomarker protocols will be important to confirm the generalizability of these findings.

Taken together, our findings highlight the importance of thorough cardiovascular assessment before HSCT, while suggesting that early biomarker and echocardiographic abnormalities may have limited incremental value for predicting 1-year clinical cardiotoxicity in unselected patients. In this context, routine intensive early surveillance with biomarkers and strain imaging in all recipients may not be warranted, and a more individualized strategy based on baseline cardiovascular risk and clinical context may be more appropriate.

Conclusions

In this prospective study, we characterized the early pattern of biomarker and echocardiographic changes after HSCT and found that subclinical myocardial abnormalities were frequent during the early post-transplant period. NT-proBNP elevation was observed in 79.7% of patients and a ≥ 15% relative reduction in GLS in 14.6%; however, neither was independently associated with clinical cardiotoxicity at 1 year. These findings suggest that early biomarker and strain abnormalities may predominantly reflect transient myocardial stress rather than persistent injury. Overall, our data support the value of comprehensive pre-transplant cardiovascular assessment, while suggesting a more selective rather than routine approach to early serial biomarker and strain surveillance in unselected HSCT recipients.
