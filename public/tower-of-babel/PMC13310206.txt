The Canonical Wnt Surrogate Agonist scFv-Dkk1c Ameliorates Spinal Cord Injury in Rats
Authors: Pau González, Alfredo Maqueda, Carlos González-Fernández, Tomas Sánchez-Gutiérrez, Virginia Pérez, Francisco Javier Rodríguez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite the therapeutic potential of canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling activation in spinal cord injury (SCI), the development of Wnt-based therapeutic approaches is severely hampered by difficulties in Wnt ligand production, their high receptor cross-reactivity and the unspecificity of glycogen synthase kinase-3β inhibitors. Consequently, the generation of chimeric canonical Wnt surrogates that can overcome the previously detailed limitations has been of paramount importance for the development of Wnt-based therapeutic approaches, although their potential beneficial role in SCI has not been assessed to date. For this purpose, we have evaluated the effects exerted by the overexpression of the canonical Wnt surrogate scFv-Dkk1c in a clinically relevant model of SCI in critical injury-related aspects such as motor functional recovery, myelin preservation/loss, neuronal cell density, microglia/macrophage and astroglial reactivity, expression of markers related to astroglial and microglia/macrophage polarization, vascularization and descending serotonergic innervation. Briefly, we found that scFv-Dkk1c overexpression during the progression of SCI significantly improved motor functional recovery concomitant with changes in the SCI-associated microglia/macrophage reactivity, expression of markers related to microglia/macrophage polarization, astroglial reactivity and tissue vascularization, without affecting myelin preservation/loss, neuronal cell density, expression of markers related to astroglial polarization, NG2 + cell presence and descending serotonergic innervation. In conclusion, the present study provides experimental evidence for the potential role of the canonical Wnt surrogate scFv-Dkk1c in the context of SCI.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12035-026-06007-3.Keywords: Wnt, Wnt surrogate, Wnt mimetic, Canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling, Spinal cord injury, Spinal cord therapy

Introduction

Traumatic spinal cord injury (SCI) is a devastating neuropathological condition that frequently results in severe and irreversible lifelong disabilities, placing a significant financial strain on healthcare systems and imposing a substantial economic, social, psychosocial and physical burden on patients and their families [1]. Unfortunately, despite the wide range of potential therapeutic approaches that have been evaluated to date and the significant advances made over the last decades in the understanding of the complex SCI pathophysiology and in its early medical and surgical management, there is no currently accepted treatment for this neuropathological condition [2, 3]. Consequently, a great effort is being done to identify new targets for effective therapeutic intervention in SCI.

Among the different potential therapeutic targets, the Wnt family of proteins has attracted significant interest due to its pivotal role in a wide range of neuropathological conditions, including SCI [4]. More specifically, it has been previously shown that the expression patterns of most Wnt ligands, receptors, co-receptors and soluble modulators suffered significant alterations during the temporal progression of this neuropathological condition [5], suggesting its potential implication in the spinal cord response to an injury. Accordingly, this family of proteins and its related signaling pathways are involved in the regulation of a wide range of pivotal biological processes in the lesioned spinal cord such as: neuron survival/death [6, 7], microglia/macrophage reactivity [4], astroglial reactivity and glial scar formation [4], NG2 + cell response [4], axonal regeneration [4], myelin preservation and/or remyelination [6, 8], pro-inflammatory cytokine and enzyme expression [8], differentiation of neural stem cells [9], synapse formation/preservation [10], fibrosis [4] and neutrophil infiltration [11].

Although the therapeutic potential of modulating the Wnt family of proteins and its related signaling pathways in SCI is still far away from being well understood, it can be extrapolated from the currently published studies that the activation of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is able to exert a beneficial role in the lesioned spinal cord. More specifically, the administration or overexpression of prototypic canonical Wnt ligands such as Wnt3a [12] and Wnt1 [6], the pharmacological inhibition of glycogen synthase kinase (GSK)−3β [13] and the blockade of Dkk1 [8], one of the main endogenous inhibitors of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, significantly improved the histopathological and functional progression and outcome of SCI.

Despite the observations detailed above, the potential use of canonical Wnt ligands as therapeutic agents is severely hampered by the fact that these molecules undergo various post-translational modifications that make them difficult to produce consistently [14]. Moreover, canonical Wnt ligands also exhibit a high degree of cross-reactivity with the different Fz receptors and the capacity of modulating β-catenin-independent signaling pathways [15], which increases the risk of adverse side effects. In addition, although pharmacological GSK-3β inhibitors can partially overcome the aforementioned limitations, it has been described that these molecules do not only activate the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, but are usually able to modulate other signaling pathways, which in turn can lead to unwanted deleterious off-target consequences [16]. Consequently, the development of new Wnt surrogates with the capacity to activate the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is of paramount importance, in order to overcome the previously detailed limitations in the use of Wnt-based therapeutic approaches in those pathologies that may benefit from the activation of this signaling pathway, such as SCI.

Intriguingly, in a groundbreaking study published in 2017, Janda et al. developed an easily produced water soluble canonical Wnt surrogate (scFv-Dkk1c) composed by the single-chain variable fragment (scFv) of the Wnt antagonizing antibody vantictumab (which binds Fz1, 2, 5, 7 and 8) linked to the C-terminal domain of the human Wnt antagonist Dkk1 (Dkk1c), which is able to bind LRP5/LRP6 [17]. In this work, the authors demonstrated that this Wnt surrogate is capable of inducing Fz/LRP heterodimerization similarly to that observed for Wnt ligands, activating the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in a Fz-specific manner, and exerting equal functions than those observed for canonical Wnt ligands in diverse biological aspects such as osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal cells and organoid generation in vitro [17]. In addition, subsequent experimental studies have shown that scFv-Dkk1c is also able to increase bone mass density in vivo [18], a currently well-known biological activity of canonical Wnt signaling [19]. Surprisingly, despite the lack of an effective clinically approved treatment for SCI, the therapeutic potential of the activation of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in this neuropathological condition, the critical limitations that hinder the generation and clinical translation of potential Wnt-based therapeutic approaches and the recent development of chimeric canonical Wnt surrogates that can overcome them, there are no available studies aiming to evaluate the use of these molecules for the treatment of SCI to date. Therefore, we here aimed to assess, in a clinically relevant model of contusive SCI, the effects exerted by scFv-Dkk1c overexpression on motor functional recovery and on different critical SCI-related histopathological parameters such as myelin preservation, neuron cell density, microglia/macrophage and astroglial reactivity, expression of markers related to microglia/macrophage and astroglial polarization, NG2 + cell response, vascularization and descending serotonergic innervation. For this purpose, we have used the same lentiviral-based approach that we used in previous reports [6, 7], which allows for the long-lasting expression of the transgene specifically in the affected areas.

Methods

Production of Lentiviral Vectors

The self-inactivating lentiviruses employed in the present study were generated using the bicistronic pWPI transfer plasmid (12,254, Addgene), which allows for the simultaneous expression of the transgene and the green fluorescent protein (GFP). In particular, two distinct lentiviral vectors were produced, one that solely induces the expression of GFP (Lv-GFP), and another one designed to induce the expression of both GFP and scFv-Dkk1c (Lv-scFv-Dkk1c). To this end, the nucleotide sequence encoding scFv-Dkk1c (obtained from [20]), with the implementation of the leading peptide of rat Dkk1 at 5’ to improve its secretion (obtained from UniProt #A6I0Z0) and the hemagglutinin tag at 3’ for subsequent identification, was cloned into the pWPI plasmid by GenScript Biotech Corporation (nucleotide and amino acid sequence can be found in Online Resource 1). Finally, VSV-G pseudotyped lentiviral particles were produced by Vectalys (Flash Therapeutics) and stored at − 80 °C until use.

All experimental procedures involving the use of lentiviral vectors were conducted in accordance with Spanish regulations governing the use of genetically modified organisms (9/2003 and Royal Decree 178/2004) and biological agents (31/1995) and received approval from the Biosafety Committee (Permit number CB6/2020) of the National Hospital of Paraplegics (Toledo, Spain).

In vitro Evaluation of Lentiviral Vector Generated to Overexpress scFv-Dkk1c

Cell Cultures

All in vitro experiments conducted to assess the proper functionality of Lv-scFv-Dkk1c were carried out in rat B1a cells. For this purpose, three different B1a cell lines were produced. Firstly and as previously detailed [8], we generated a canonical Wnt signaling reporter cell line (B1a/7TGC) by transducing B1a cells with the 7TGC lentivirus (24,304, Addgene) (multiplicity of infection [MOI] = 10), which allows for the constitutive expression of mCherry and induces the expression of GFP due to the activation of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. Additionally, the B1a/scFv-Dkk1c (scFv-Dkk1c overexpressing cells) and the B1a/GFP (as control for B1a/scFv-Dkk1c cells) cell lines were generated by transducing B1a cells with Lv-scFv-Dkk1c or Lv-GFP respectively (MOI = 50). Cell transductions were performed in accordance with the previously described methodology [6].

In all cases, cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (41,966–029, Gibco) supplemented with 10% of fetal bovine serum (FBS) (10,270, Gibco), 0.4% of Tylosin (T3397, Sigma Aldrich), 7.5 mM of HEPES buffer (15,630–056, Gibco), 100 IU/mL of penicillin and 100 µg/mL of streptomycin (15,070–083, Gibco).

Assessment of scFv-Dkk1c Expression and Secretion

Western blot analyses were conducted to ascertain whether cell transduction with Lv-scFv-Dkk1c led to an efficient protein expression and secretion of scFv-Dkk1c. For this purpose, B1a, B1a/GFP and B1a/scFv-Dkk1 cells were cultured in non-coated 6-wells culture plates (140,675, Nunc). After three days, cells were lysed using RIPA buffer (R0278, Sigma Aldrich) containing a cocktail of proteases (11,873,580,001, Roche) and phosphatases (04906837001, Roche). At the same time, the conditioned medium was recovered and filtered through a 0.2 µm filter to eliminate any potential cross-contaminating cell. Both cell lysates and conditioned mediums were stored in working aliquots at − 20 °C until use. Thereafter, 40 µg of protein from cell lysates or 100 µg of protein from conditioned mediums from each group (n = 1 per group) were analyzed by Western blot following the method that we have already described [21, 22] with slight modifications since, in this case, TBS with 0.5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) (A7906, Sigma-Aldrich) and 0.1% Tween 20 (P5927, Sigma-Aldrich) was employed for membrane blocking, antibody visualization was conducted using the SuperSignal West Pico PLUS Chemiluminiscence Substrate (34,580, Thermo Scientific) and the Amersham Imager 600 (GE Healthcare) was used to obtain membrane images. The following primary and secondary antibodies were used: rabbit monoclonal anti-Dkk1 (1:1000) (MA5-32,229, Thermo Scientific), mouse monoclonal anti-GAPDH (1:500) (ab8245, Abcam), horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-linked goat anti-rabbit (1:10,000) (31,460, Invitrogen) and HRP-linked goat anti-mouse (31,430, Thermo Scientific).

Evaluation of scFv-Dkk1c Bioactivity

As previously described [8], we firstly evaluated the proper functioning of the B1a/7TGC reporter cell line. For this purpose, B1a/7TGC were cultured as described above and subsequently incubated with or without the GSK-3β inhibitor CHIR99021 (5 µM) (SML1046, Sigma-Aldrich) for 72 h (n = 5 per group). Subsequently, cells were detached using trypsin–EDTA 0.25% (252,200–056, Gibco) and resuspended in PBS supplemented with 25 mM of HEPES buffer (15,630–056, Gibco), 2% of FBS (10,270, Gibco), 5 mM of EDTA (E7889, Sigma-Aldrich), 200 IU/mL of penicillin and 200 µg/mL of streptomycin (15,070–083, Gibco). Finally, the percentage of mCherry +/GFP + cells and the GFP mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) were obtained using a CytoFLEX S Flow Cytometer (Beckman Coulter) and the FlowJo 10.8 analysis software (BD Biosciences). Once we confirmed the functionality of the B1a/7TGC cell line, we next aimed to evaluate whether overexpressed scFv-Dkk1c was biologically active. To this end, B1a/7TGC cells were cultured alone or in combination with B1a/GFP or B1a/scFv-Dkk1c at a 1:1 ratio (n = 5 per group). Once again, the percentage of mCherry +/GFP + cells and the GFP MFI were analyzed after 72 h following the same experimental method used to evaluate the proper functioning of the B1a/7TGC cell line.

Animals, Surgical Procedures and Experimental Design

To evaluate the effects exerted by scFv-Dkk1c overexpression on histopathological and functional SCI progression and outcome in vivo, a total of 37 adult female Wistar rats were used (Charles River) (3 months; ≃ 250 g). Spinal cord contusions were performed at the T10 spinal level as we have previously described [23], using an Infinite Horizon Impactor (Precision Systems and Instrumentation LLC) and a contusion force of 200 Kdyn. Immediately after SCI, the corresponding lentiviral vectors were intraparenchymatically administered following the injection method that we have described in detail in previous publications [6] with slight modifications since, in this study, a total of 5 × 106 lentiviral particles were injected in each injection point. Briefly, two injections per rostro-caudal level (stereotaxic coordinates: 0.6/− 0.6 mm lateral and 1 mm depth) were performed in three rostro-caudal levels corresponding to the lesion epicenter, 1.5 mm rostral and 1.5 mm caudal. In all injection points, a volume of 1 µl was injected at a rate of 0.5 µl/min. The postoperative cares were the same that we have detailed in previous works [21], although marbofloxacin (2.5 mg/kg) (Marbocyl, Vetoquinol) was administered instead of enrofloxacin during the first five days post-injury (dpi). In all cases, animal housing and experimental procedures were carried out in accordance with the Spanish (Royal Decree 53/2013) and the European Union (2010/63/EU) legislation, and were approved by the Bioethics Committee at The National Hospital of Paraplegics (Toledo, Spain) (Permit number 6/2021). All efforts were made throughout the experimental process to minimize animal suffering. Finally, the previously detailed animals were subdivided into two experimental groups: (1) Lv-GFP control group, composed by animals subjected to spinal cord contusion and injected with Lv-GFP (sacrificed for histological evaluation at 7 [n = 8] and 70 dpi [n = 11]) and (2) Lv-scFv-Dkk1c group, composed by animals subjected to spinal cord contusion and injected with Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (sacrificed for histological evaluation at 7 [n = 7] and 70 dpi [n = 11]).

Histology

Tissue Processing

Animal sacrifice, intraaortical perfusion with 4% paraformaldehyde (P6148, Sigma-Aldrich) and spinal cord extraction, cryoprotection, freezing and sectioning were carried out as detailed in previous reports [21] with slight modifications since, in the present study, a 1.5 cm spinal cord stretch containing the lesion was extracted. Finally, obtained parallel transversal spinal cord sections (30 μm thick) were mounted on slides (J1800AMNZ, Thermo Scientific) and stored at − 20 °C until use.

Evaluation of Transduction In vivo

A set of parallel sections per animal was used to corroborate cell transduction in vivo. For this purpose, after several washes in TBS and TBS + 0.3% Triton, sections were incubated during 1 h at room temperature (RT) with blocking buffer (BB) (4% BSA [A7906, Sigma-Aldrich] in TBS). Subsequently, sections were incubated during 1 h at RT with ChromoTek GFP-Booster ATTO488 (gba488, Proteintech) diluted to 1:200 in BB. Thereafter, sections were washed in TBS and TB, incubated with Sudan Black as previously described [6] and counterstained with DAPI (1:5000) (#62,247, Thermo Scientific). Finally, the sections were coverslipped with Immumount (9,990,402, Thermo Scientific).

Eriochrome Cyanine

To ascertain whether overexpression of scFv-Dkk1c induced alterations in myelin preservation after SCI, eriochrome cyanine (Ecy) staining was performed in a set of parallel sections per animal following the same experimental protocol that we have used in previous reports [24].

Simple and Double Immunofluorescence

Simple immunofluorescence was carried out in a set of parallel sections per animal to visualize the expression of neuronal nuclei (NeuN), ionized calcium binding adaptor molecule 1 (Iba1), glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), RECA1, NG2 and 5-HT in order to evaluate the potential variations induced by scFv-Dkk1c overexpression in the presence of neurons, microglia/macrophages, astroglial cells, blood vessels, NG2 + cells and descending serotonergic (5-HT +) axons after SCI, respectively. To perform the different simple immunofluorescences used, we have followed the same experimental procedure that has been previously detailed by our group [6]. The following primary and secondary antibodies were used: rabbit monoclonal anti-NeuN (1:150) (MABN140, Millipore), rabbit polyclonal anti-Iba1 (1:400) (019–19741, Wako), mouse monoclonal anti-GFAP (1:100) (G3893, Sigma-Aldrich), mouse monoclonal anti-RECA1 (1:100) (MCA970GA, AbD Serotec), rabbit polyclonal anti-NG2 (1:150) (AB5320, Millipore), rabbit polyclonal anti-5-HT (1:500) (S5545, Sigma-Aldrich), Dylight 594-linked goat anti-rabbit (1:500) (ab96897, Abcam) and Dylight 594-linked goat anti-mouse (1:500) (ab96881, Abcam).

Double immunofluorescence was performed in a set of parallel sections per animal to co-visualize OX-42 and CD206, as well as GFAP and C3 or S100A10, to assess the potential changes induced by scFv-Dkk1c overexpression in the expression of markers related to “M2” microglia/macrophage as well as to “A1” and “A2” astroglial polarization, respectively. The experimental method used to perform the different double immunofluorescences has been already described [24]. The following primary and secondary antibodies were used: mouse monoclonal anti-OX42 (1:100) (ab1211, Abcam), mouse monoclonal anti-GFAP (1:100) (G3893, Sigma-Aldrich), rabbit polyclonal anti-CD206 (1:200) (ab64693, Abcam), rabbit polyclonal anti-C3 (1:50) (A13283, Abclonal), rabbit polyclonal anti-S100A10 (1:100) (11,250–1-AP, Proteintech), Dylight 594-linked goat anti-mouse (1:500) (ab96881, Abcam), Dylight 650-linked donkey anti-rabbit (1:500) (ab96922), Dylight 594-linked goat anti-rabbit (ab96897, Abcam) (1:500) and Dylight 650-linked donkey anti-mouse (ab98797) (1:200).

Both sections processed without the primary antibodies and/or sections processed without the second primary antibody were used as controls to confirm a lack of undesired cross-reactivity. No non-specific staining was observed in any case.

Image Acquisition

Composite 10 × images from whole spinal cord sections processed for the visualization of Ecy, GFAP, Iba1, NeuN, RECA1, NG2, OX-42/CD206, GFAP/C3 and GFAP/S100A10 were obtained using a UPLXAPO 10 ×/0.4 objective (Olympus) in an IX83 Motorized Microscope (Olympus) attached to an Orca 4.0 camera (Hamamatsu). Moreover, 20× images from ventral horns in sections processed for the visualization of 5-HT were obtained using the same microscope and camera detailed above although, in this case, an UPLXAPO 20 ×/0.8 (Olympus) was used. In addition, an UPlanSApo 10 ×/0.4 (Olympus) and a DP71 camera (Olympus) attached to a BX61 Motorized Research Microscope (Olympus) were used to obtain composite 10 × images from sections processed for the visualization of GFP. Finally, higher magnification images from sections processed for the visualization of GFAP, Iba1, NeuN, NG2, GFP, OX-42/CD206, GFAP/C3 and GFAP/S100A10 were obtained using a TCS SP5 confocal microscope (Leica Microsystems) and a HCX PL APO 40 ×/1.25–0.75.

Densitometric Analysis

Following the methods that we have used in previous studies [6], densitometric analyses were performed in images obtained from sections processed for the visualization of Ecy, GFAP, Iba1, RECA1, NG2 and 5-HT in order to ascertain whether scFv-Dkk1c overexpression induced variations in SCI-associated myelin loss, astroglial and microglia/macrophage reactivity, vascularization, NG2 + cell density and descending serotonergic innervation. All previously detailed densitometric analysis were performed in composite images from whole spinal cord sections except for the densitometric analysis of 5-HT, which was carried out in a selected ROI of 150 × 150 µm that was positioned in the lower limit of the ventral horns. Moreover and to determine whether scFv-Dkk1c overexpression led to changes in the expression of markers related to microglia/macrophage and astroglial polarization, densitometric analysis of sections processed for the visualization of OX-42/CD206, GFAP/C3 or GFAP/S100A10 was performed to quantify OX-42 +/CD206 +, GFAP +/C3 + or GFAP +/S100A10 + areas. To this end, we have used the experimental protocol previously described by our group [25] with slight modifications since, in this case, the region of interest was the whole spinal cord section and the densitometric analysis was performed in images obtained using an IX83 Motorized Microscope (Olympus). In all cases, thresholds were qualitatively established according to the histological signal for each immunohistochemical method, corroborating that no background signal was detected in control spinal cord sections processed without the primary antibody. The selected threshold as well as the settings used to obtain the analyzed images were maintained in all analyzed sections for each immunohistochemical method and at each evaluated time post-injury. All densitometric analyses were conducted using the Fiji software (version 1.53q).

Cell Count

Neuronal cell count was conducted in a set of parallel sections per animal processed for the visualization of NeuN. Automatized NeuN + cell count was performed in composite images from whole spinal cord sections using the Olympus TruAI™ and the Fiji software (version 1.53q) following the method that has been recently detailed by our group [8].

Functional Evaluation

To evaluate whether scFv-Dkk1c overexpression resulted in significant variations in motor functional recovery, the 21-point Basso Beattie and Bresnahan (BBB) open-field test was carried out at 1, 5, 7, 14, 21, 28, 42, 56 and 70 dpi as it has been previously described [6]. The BBB subscore was calculated following the method described in previous reports [26]. Moreover, the potential existence of between-groups differences in motor functional recovery was also evaluated using the Catwalk® gait analysis system (version 7.1, Noldus), both before SCI (to obtain pre-injury [PI] values) and at the end of the study (70 dpi), following the previously described procedure [6]. The following gait parameters were evaluated: crossing velocity, regularity index, AB step pattern, front paws base of support (BOS), hind paws BOS, print positions, front paws stride length (SL), hind paws SL and duty cycle.

Statistical Analysis

Repeated measures two-way ANOVA followed by Bonferroni post hoc test was used to determine the existence of statistically significant differences in data obtained from the assessment of the BBB score, BBB subscore and in individual BBB parameters. Non-repeated measures two-way ANOVA followed by Bonferroni post-hoc test was used to assess the existence of statistically significant differences in data obtained from the evaluation of myelin preservation/loss, neuronal cell density, microglia/macrophage and astroglial reactivity, expression of markers related to microglia/macrophage and astroglial polarization, NG2 + cell response, vascularization and descending serotonergic innervation. Two-tailed t test was used to evaluate the existence of statistically significant differences in in vitro experiments performed to analyze the existence of statistically significant differences in GFP expression due to CHIR99021 treatment in B1a/7TGC cells. Finally, one-way ANOVA followed by Bonferroni post hoc test was carried out to evaluate the existence of statistically significant differences in data obtained from the analysis of the different gait parameters evaluated and from the assessment of GFP expression in vitro in B1a/7TGC cells co-cultured or not with B1a/GFP or B1a/scFv-Dkk1c cells.

Results

In vitro Determination of Lv-scFv-Dkk1c Bioactivity

As a first essential step to determine the proper functionality of Lv-scFv-Dkk1c, we sought to evaluate whether cell transduction with this lentiviral vector was able to induce the expression and secretion of scFv-Dkk1c. Corroborating an efficient expression of the transgene, western blot analysis of cell lysates showed a robust protein expression of scFv-Dkk1c in B1a cells transduced with Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (B1a/scFv-Dkk1c), in contrast to that observed in non-transduced B1a cells (B1a) or transduced with Lv-GFP (B1a/GFP) where no protein expression of scFv-Dkk1c can be found (Online Resource 2a). Moreover and confirming an adequate secretion of the overexpressed protein to the extracellular medium we found that scFv-Dkk1c is present in conditioned culture medium from B1a/scFv-Dkk1c cells, but not in conditioned culture medium from non-transduced B1a cells or B1a/GFP cells (Online Resource 2b). Finally and to ascertain whether the overexpressed scFv-Dkk1c is biologically active, we generated a reporter cell line (B1a/7TGC) which induce GFP expression upon activation of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. As shown (Online Resource 2c and d), incubation of these reporter cells with a GSK-3β inhibitor (CHIR99021) for 72 h induced the expression of GFP in approximately 95% of B1a/7TGC cells, thereby corroborating the utility of this cell line for evaluating the adequate functionality of overexpressed scFv-Dkk1c. In addition, co-culture of B1a/7TGC with B1a/scFv-Dkk1c cells (scFv-Dkk1 overexpressing cells) for 72 h led to GFP expression in approximately 95% of B1a/7TGC cells (Online Resource 2e and f), while nearly absent GFP expression was observed in B1a/7TGC cells cultured alone or co-cultured with B1a/GFP cells, although a slight but significant increase in the percentage of GFP + cells was observed in B1a/7TGC cells when co-cultured with B1A/GFP cells (Online Resource 2e). In summary, the data obtained from this experimental set demonstrated that cell transduction with Lv-scFv-Dkk1c conveniently induced scFv-Dkk1c protein expression and secretion and that overexpressed scFv-Dkk1c was able to exert its previously described biological function as an agonist of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway [17].

Evaluation of Transduction In vivo

Confirming an efficient in vivo transduction and in consistence with previous reports published by our group using the same injection system [6, 7], transduced GFP + cells at 7 dpi were located in close relationship with the injured tissue. In brief, cell transduction was predominantly observed in rostral and caudal levels adjacent to the lesion epicenter, particularly in the lesioned dorsal columns and in the grey matter (in the dorsal horns and intermediate columns and, to a lesser extent, in the ventral horns) (Online Resource 3a, c, c1, e, e1, h, h1, j and j1). Moreover, transduced GFP + cells were also identified in the lesioned dorsal columns and surrounding the central canal at rostral and caudal spinal cord levels distant from the lesion epicenter (Online Resource 3a, b, b1, f, f1, g, g1, k and k1). Additionally, transduced GFP + cells were also observed within the lesioned tissue and in the dorsal meninges in those spinal cord levels corresponding to the lesion epicenter (Online Resource 3a, d, d1, i and i1). By the end of the study (70 dpi), the cell transduction pattern remained largely consistent with that observed at 7 dpi. However, at this time point, the presence of transduced GFP + cells was nearly absent in spinal cord levels corresponding to the lesion epicenter (Online Resource 4a-k1). At both evaluated time points, only few GFP + cells were present in ventrolateral white matter areas of rostro-caudal spinal cord levels corresponding and adjacent to the lesion epicenter. No GFP + cells were observed in these areas in rostro-caudal spinal cord levels separated from the injury epicenter. Finally, no evident qualitative differences in GFP expression and transduction area were observed between animals.

scFv-Dkk1c Overexpression Improves Motor Functional Recovery

The analysis of motor functional recovery using the BBB open-field test demonstrated that those animals injected with Lv-scFv-Dkk1c exhibited a significantly higher BBB score at 56 and 70 dpi in comparison with the Lv-GFP control group (Fig. 1a and Online Resource 5) [56 dpi: Lv-GFP (12.45 ± 0.24), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (14 ± 0.56); 70 dpi: Lv-GFP (12.63 ± 0.24), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (14.4 ± 2.1); * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01; repeated measures two-way ANOVA]. Moreover, evaluation of the BBB subscore revealed that those animals belonging to the Lv-scFv-Dkk1c group displayed a non-significant trend to increase this parameter from 14 dpi to the end of the study (70 dpi), reaching statistical significance at 28 and 56 dpi (Fig. 1b and Online Resource 5) [28 dpi: Lv-GFP (9.36 ± 1.08), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (12.72 ± 0.99); 56 dpi: Lv-GFP (12.9 ± 0.85), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (15.9 ± 0.56); * p < 0.05; repeated measures two-way ANOVA]. Finally, when the different individual parameters that comprise the BBB score and subscore were evaluated separately, we found that those animals that overexpressed scFv-Dkk1c exhibited a significantly higher stepping value at 14 dpi (Fig. 1c and Online Resource 5) [Lv-GFP (2.63 ± 0.83), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (4.72 ± 0.58); *** p < 0.001; repeated measures two-way ANOVA] and also a significantly better coordination at 28, 56 and 70 dpi (Fig. 1d and Online Resource 5) [28 dpi: Lv-GFP (0.27 ± 0.14), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (1 ± 0.26); 56 dpi: Lv-GFP (1.45 ± 0.24), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (2.18 ± 0.26); 70 dpi: Lv-GFP (1.63 ± 0.24), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (2.36 ± 0.24); * p < 0.05; repeated measures two-way ANOVA], being the latter a crucial aspect of locomotion [27, 28]. No differences were observed in the remaining individual BBB parameters analyzed (paw positions and toe clearance), with the exception of a transient increase in tail position at 28 dpi in Lv-scFv-Dkk1 injected animals (Online Resource 5). On the other hand, assessment of motor functional recovery at 70 dpi using the Catwalk® gait analysis system showed that, in agreement with the enhanced coordination capacity found in the BBB open-field test, those animals injected with Lv-scFv-Dkk1c exhibited a significantly higher regularity index (Fig. 1e and Online Resource 7) [Lv-GFP (94.69 ± 0.94), Lv-scFv-Dkk1c (97.78 ± 0.77), * p < 0.05 and *** p < 0.001 vs PI, ##p < 0.01 vs Lv-GFP, one-way ANOVA]. No statistically significant differences were found in the rest of evaluated gait parameters (AB step pattern, front paws BOS, hind paws BOS, print positions, front paws SL, hind paws SL and duty cycle) (Online Resources 6 and 7).

Assessment of scFv-Dkk1c overexpression in motor functional recovery. scFv-Dkk1c Overexpression Increases early NG2 + Cell Responce

As widely described, the NG2 + cell response is another significant factor in the progression and outcome of SCI [37] that can be significantly influenced by the Wnt family of proteins and its related signaling pathways in this neuropathological condition [6, 7, 38]. In this regard, the assessment of the NG2 + cell response at 7 dpi showed that the overexpression of scFv-Dkk1c led to a slight but significant increase in the presence of NG2 + cells in spinal cord levels caudal to the lesion epicenter (Fig. 5d–f). scFv-Dkk1c Overexpression Induces a Time-Dependent Biphasic Effect on Vascular Loss and/or Revascularization

Otherwise, traumatic injury to the spinal cord induce a significant vascular damage, leading to a compromised blood flow and blood-spinal cord barrier integrity, spinal cord ischemia and extravasation of blood components in the lesioned tissue, thereby contributing to the secondary injury phase that take place after SCI and increasing the severity of this neuropathological condition [39]. Owing to these observations, together with the growing number of studies showing the involvement of the Wnt family of proteins and its related signaling pathways in pathological vascularization in the damaged CNS [40–42], we next sought to ascertain whether scFv-Dkk1c overexpression induced alterations in tissue vascularization after SCI. Interestingly, we found that those animals injected with Lv-scFv-Dkk1c displayed a significant increase in vascularized area at 7 dpi in the injury epicenter and the adjacent rostro-caudal levels when compared to the Lv-GFP control group (Fig. 6a–c). On the contrary, at 70 dpi, scFv-Dkk1c overexpression resulted in a significant reduction in tissue vascularization in the lesion epicenter and its adjacent rostro-caudal spinal cord levels (Fig. 6d–f).

scFv-Dkk1c Overexpression does not Influence Descending Serotonergic Innervation

Finally, it has been extensively demonstrated that, in addition to its pivotal regulatory activity on axonal growth during development, different components of the Wnt family of proteins and its related signaling pathways are clearly involved in axonal regeneration in the damaged CNS, including in the lesioned spinal cord [4]. Due to these interesting observations, we next aimed to evaluate whether scFv-Dkk1c overexpression led to differences in axonal regeneration and/or preservation in the descending serotonergic system after SCI, since it plays a major role in the recovery of motor function, is particularly affected [43] and can be modulated by the Wnt family of proteins in this neuropathological condition [7, 44]. As illustrated in Fig. 6g–j, no differences were observed due to scFv-Dkk1c overexpression in descending serotonergic innervation in ventral horns caudal to the lesion site at 70 dpi.

Discussion

In the present study, we have induced the lentiviral-mediated overexpression of the Wnt canonical agonist named scFv-Dkk1c along the injury progression in a clinically relevant model of contusive SCI. Accordingly with the previously detailed therapeutic role displayed by canonical Wnt surrogates in other pathological conditions [45] and the beneficial function of canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway activation in the lesioned spinal cord [6, 8, 12, 13], we here found that scFv-Dkk1c overexpression during the progression of the injury is able to induce a significant improvement in motor functional recovery after SCI, mainly due to an enhancement of limb coordination capacity which, as already described, is a major factor in both quadrupedal and bipedal locomotion [27, 28].

Regarding the effects exerted by scFv-Dkk1c overexpression in key SCI-associated histopathological aspects, one of the most important hallmarks of SCI is the massive and long-lasting death of oligodendrocytes and consequent loss of myelin sheaths which, ultimately, lead to functional worsening [29]. Moreover, during the progression of SCI an endogenous remyelination response can be observed which, unfortunately, does not result in an effective histopathological and functional recovery [46]. Interestingly, a growing number of studies have demonstrated that the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway critically influences remyelination in the damaged CNS showing apparently disparate results. More specifically, it has been shown that its activation significantly hindered oligodendrocyte precursor cell (OPC) differentiation and remyelination [47, 48] while, on the other hand, different studies have observed that canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway activation promotes OPC differentiation and remyelination [47, 48]. Specifically in the context of SCI, the overexpression of the prototypic canonical Wnt ligand Wnt1, the blockade of Dkk1 and the inhibition of GSK-3β resulted in higher myelination in the lesioned spinal cord [6, 8, 13], without affecting the presence of mature oligodendrocytes in the damaged areas at least after Wnt1-overexpression [6]. These observations together with the fact that, as we show here, the overexpression of the canonical Wnt surrogate scFv-Dkk1c did not induce variations in the myelinated area after SCI, support that the potential inhibitory effect of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in OPC differentiation and remyelination in this neuropathological condition, if exists, is not sufficient to lead to a reduction of myelination. To further complete this picture, we also evaluated whether scFv-Dkk1c overexpression led to variations in the presence of NG2 + cells, since this cell type greatly underlie the SCI-related endogenous remyelination response [46]. We found that scFv-Dkk1c overexpression did not induce evident changes in the presence of NG2 + cells in the lesioned areas. Accordingly, different studies have demonstrated that the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway did not influence the proliferation and/or survival of this cell type, although some discrepancies can be found [47, 48]. These apparent conflicting results can be also observed in the specific context of SCI, since the deletion of the β-catenin gene in OPCs led to a reduced proliferation of this cell type [38], while the overexpression of the canonical Wnt ligand Wnt1 resulted in a significant reduction in the presence of NG2 + cells in the lesioned areas [6]. Altogether, these observations emphasize the great complexity of the involvement of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in OPC biology and remyelination, pointing to the necessity of further studies in order to ascertain the experimental and/or biological issues underlying the previously detailed divergent findings.

In addition, SCI also encompasses a substantial neuronal death [30] which, as shown in different neuropathological conditions [49–51], can be reduced through the activation of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. This neuroprotective role has been also observed in SCI, since the administration of the prototypic canonical Wnt ligands Wnt1 [6] and Wnt3a [52] and the pharmacological inhibition of GSK-3β [53] increased neuronal survival. In contrast, scFv-Dkk1c overexpression during the progression of SCI did not induce variations in neuronal cell density. Although we cannot conclude the reasons underlying this observation, it is worth noting that most neuronal loss take place during the first 24 h after SCI [54] and that, as previously described, cell transduction with lentiviral vectors led to low transgene expression levels during the first 24 h after transduction [55]. Therefore, it is tempting to hypothesize that, if this also occurs in our experimental conditions, the low scFv-Dkk1c expression levels during the first 24 h after SCI might be underlying the lack of effects on neuronal cell density. Accordingly, we have recently shown that the pharmacological inhibition of Dkk1 by the administration of a blocking antibody at 24 h after SCI resulted in functional and histopathological improvement without affecting neuron cell density [8].

Otherwise, reactive microglia/macrophages exert many different beneficial and detrimental functions that greatly influence the progression and outcome of SCI [31]. Different studies have shown that the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is involved in the modulation of microglia/macrophage response in neuropathological conditions being able to exert either a pro-inflammatory [38, 56, 57] or anti-inflammatory role [8, 40, 58, 59], suggesting that its specific role in these cell types do not merely depend on the activation or inactivation of this signaling pathway, but also on different specific experimental and biological aspects. Specifically, we here show that scFv-Dkk1c overexpression induced a significant increase in microglia/macrophage reactivity at 7 days after SCI. Of note, the classical view of the injury-related microglia/macrophage response as a deleterious process in the CNS has been challenged, since this cellular response also elicits a wide range of beneficial functions, especially during the acute phase of CNS injury [31]. More specifically, depending on the lesion environment reactive microglia/macrophages can polarize into a pro-inflammatory “M1” or an anti-inflammatory “M2” phenotype after SCI, being thus able to play a deleterious or a pro-regenerative role, respectively [32]. However, recent data have demonstrated the existence of a range of intermediate microglia/macrophage phenotypes, demonstrating that this classification is oversimplified and that microglia/macrophage transformation is a continuum rather than all-or-nothing states [60–62]. Despite this, a growing amount of experimental evidence has pointed out that the induction of a “M2” phenotype in microglia/macrophages has a positive impact on SCI progression [61–63] and, therefore, that the assessment of markers related to specific functional states in these cell types, such as CD206 [32, 64], is useful when evaluating the effects exerted by a potential beneficial approach for this neuropathological condition. Moreover, it has been previously shown that the activation of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway induced a “M2” microglia/macrophage polarization [40, 58, 59]. In this regard, we found that scFv-Dkk1c overexpression led to an increase in the microglia/macrophage expression of the “M2”-related marker CD206, which might suggest that this canonical Wnt surrogate could be able to potentiate the beneficial aspects of the early SCI-associated microglia/macrophage response. However, it should be noted that these variations in microglia/macrophage expression of CD206 does not necessarily reflect functional polarization and, as a result, constraint for the establishment of a direct causal or mechanistic correlation with the improvement found in motor functional recovery. Besides, another major feature of the SCI-related microglia/macrophage is that their activation persist at the injury indefinitely, leading to a dysfunctional wound healing [65]. Interestingly, we found that scFv-Dkk1c overexpression results in a robust reduction of microglia/macrophage reactivity during the chronic phase of the injury (70 dpi), suggesting that this canonical Wnt surrogate could be able to reduce the chronification of the microglia/macrophage response after SCI.

Another critical cell response in the lesioned spinal cord is astroglial reactivity, since injury-activated astrocytes are involved in a wide range of either beneficial or deleterious processes [34], and are the main cellular component of the glial scar [66]. Different studies have demonstrated that the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is involved in the modulation of the astroglial cell response in the damaged CNS [67]. However, apparently divergent results have been obtained to date, even in the context of the spinal cord. More specifically, the overexpression of the canonical Wnt ligand Wnt3a in the healthy spinal cord led to astroglial activation [38], the inhibition of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in animal models of neuropathic pain reduced the associated astroglial activation [57, 68] and the activation of this signaling pathway led to an accelerated migration and morphological polarization of astroglial cells in order to generate the glial scar and seclude inflammatory cells after SCI [13]. However, Wnt1 overexpression significantly reduced astroglial reactivity in the subacute phase of SCI [6] while, in the present study, scFv-Dkk1c overexpression was able to induce a robust decrease in astroglial reactivity during the chronic phase of SCI. Besides, it is interesting to note that reactive astroglial cells are functionally heterogeneous, ranging from a deleterious “A1” to a pro-regenerative “A2” phenotype [35] and that, as previously described [59], the administration of the canonical Wnt ligand Wnt3a after brain ischemic stroke led to an increased “A2” and reduced “A1” astroglial polarization. In contrast, we did not find alterations in the astroglial expression of the “A1”-related marker C3 or the “A2”-related marker S100A10 due to scFv-Dkk1c overexpression after SCI. However, it should be noted that the lack of changes in the astroglial expression of these markers does not necessarily reflect a lack of functional polarization in this cell type. Finally, these observations again suggest that the effects exerted by this signaling pathway in SCI-associated astroglial reactivity exhibit a high context-dependence, and that further studies will be needed to highlight the specific mechanisms underlying these apparent divergences.

Moreover, vascular damage and loss is one of the major factors contributing to the secondary tissue injury that occurs after SCI [39]. Unfortunately, although an endogenous angiogenesis take place during the subacute phase of SCI, it is not sufficient to result in the proper vascularization of the damaged tissue [39]. Interestingly, the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is able to play a proangiogenic role in different neuropathological conditions [40–42]. In agreement, we found that the overexpression of the canonical Wnt surrogate scFv-Dkk1c led to a significant increase in the vascularization of the lesioned spinal cord tissue after SCI at 7 dpi, coinciding with the endogenous angiogenic process [39]. However, it should be noted that most of the newly formed blood vessels do not became functional and, thus, are conveniently removed later on [39]. Surprisingly, none of the previously detailed studies have evaluated whether the increased angiogenesis observed at earlier times post-injury resulted in a higher vascularization during the chronic phase of the injury. In this line, we found that scFv-Dkk1c overexpression led to a significant reduction in the vascularization of the lesioned spinal cord tissue at 70 dpi, strongly highlighting the necessity of evaluating whether the potential changes observed in angiogenesis at earlier time points remain during the chronic phase of the injury. Moreover, this time-dependent biphasic effect in tissue vascularization suggest that scFv-Dkk1c overexpression is able to induce an early beneficial and a late detrimental effect in vascularization of the affected tissue after SCI. Otherwise, it should be noted that reactive microglia/macrophages are able to play a significant proangiogenic role after SCI [69]. In addition, the proangiogenic effect exerted by the administration of a GSK-3β inhibitor after brain ischemic stroke seems to be dependent on the changes elicited in microglia/macrophage reactivity [40]. Furthermore, macrophage treatment with the canonical Wnt ligand Wnt3a induced a proangiogenic profile in this cell type in vitro [70]. Considering these observations, together with the fact that the changes observed in the present study in microglia/macrophage reactivity seems to correlate with those found in vascularization, we cannot conclude whether the changes observed in vascularization due to scFv-Dkk1 overexpression after SCI depend on a direct action on endothelial cells or is indirectly induced by scFv-Dkk1c modulation of microglia/macrophage reactivity. Finally, it is worth noting that RECA1 immunostaining not only stain functional but also collapsed or non-perfused blood vessels, impeding us to conclude whether the variations observed in the vascularized area reflect changes in functional vascularization.

Lastly, the Wnt family of proteins is also a pivotal regulator of CNS axonal degeneration/regeneration in the injured CNS [71]. More specifically, it has been demonstrated that the administration of the canonical Wnt ligand Wnt3a [52] and the pharmacological inhibition of GSK-3β [44, 72] significantly increased axonal regeneration, while the inactivation of the canonical Wnt/β-signaling pathway led to the opposite effect in animal models of SCI [73, 74], pointing to a pro-regenerative role of this signaling pathway. Consequently, we here aimed to evaluate whether the overexpression of scFv-Dkk1c induced variations in axonal regeneration, focusing in the descending serotonergic innervation since, in the lesioned spinal cord, it plays a major role in the recovery of motor function [43], is particularly affected [43] and its regeneration is increased due to pharmacological GSK-3β inhibition [44]. In contrast, we found that the canonical Wnt surrogate scFv-Dkk1c did not affect the regeneration of descending serotonergic axons. In this regard, although we cannot conclude the specific reasons underlying this apparent divergence, it is worth noting that the overexpression of the canonical Wnt ligand Wnt1 did not influence the regeneration and/or degeneration of the descending serotonergic system after SCI [6], and that the effects elicited by the pharmacological inhibition of GSK-3β in axonal regeneration seems to depend on different experimental aspects such as the degree of GSK-3β inhibition, even observing an inhibitory effect on neurite outgrowth when high concentrations of GSK-3β inhibitors are used [44], thereby highlighting the great complexity of the involvement of the Wnt family of proteins in axonal regeneration.

Finally, although some of the histopathological variations found in the present study could be, at least in part, underlying the improvement observed in functional performance (i.e. increase in the microglia/macrophage expression of the “M2”-related marker CD206, chronic microglia/macrophage reactivity resolution, early increase in tissue vascularization and reduced chronic astroglial reactivity), it is surprising that these changes were not accompanied by variations in myelin preservation, neuronal cell density or axonal preservation/regeneration. Future studies will be needed to ascertain whether variations in other histopathological aspects that can be influenced by the Wnt family of proteins and its related signaling pathways (i.e. preservation/regeneration of specific axonal tracts, synaptic remodeling, ECM/glial scar composition …) could account for the improvement observed in motor functional recovery. Accordingly, it also should be noted that we cannot conclude whether the changes observed in the different histological parameters were sufficient to explain the improvement found in functional performance.

In conclusion, we here demonstrate that the overexpression of the canonical Wnt surrogate scFv-Dkk1c in a clinically relevant model of SCI is able to significantly improve motor functional recovery, concomitantly with changes in microglia/macrophage reactivity and expression of CD206, astroglial reactivity and vascularization.
