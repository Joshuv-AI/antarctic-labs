Negative heat capacities in spherically symmetric sectors of d d -matrix quantum mechanics. Preprint: DIAS-STP-26-10 QMUL-PH-26-19
Denjoe O’Connor, Sanjaye Ramgoolam

Abstract
We consider the S ​ O ​ ( d ) SO(d) and O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) invariant sectors of the bosonic d d -matrix harmonic oscillator with U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) gauge symmetry. The micro-canonical degeneracy 𝒵 ⁡ ( N , d , k ) {\cal Z}(N,d,k) for fixed energy k k is expressed as a pairing between an N N -dependent vector and a d d -dependent vector in the space of partitions of the integer k k . This pairing formula is derived by counting invariant words in multi-matrix variables X j , a i X^{i}_{j,a} , using properties of Clebsch-Gordan multiplicities (Kronecker coefficients) for the symmetric group S k S_{k} , Schur-Weyl duality and harmonic analysis on the homogeneous space U ⁡ ( d ) / S ​ O ​ ( d ) U(d)/SO(d) . Analytic formulae for large N N and k k with k ≤ N k\leq N are obtained using group integrals over U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) and S ​ O ​ ( d ) SO(d) (or O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) ). The micro-canonical heat capacity in this regime is negative and turns positive, at a critical value k crit k_{{\rm crit}} , due to finite N N modifications to the counting, thus forming what we denote as a characteristic caloric fold in the E E versus T T curve. Data from the pairing formula is well fitted by k crit ∼ N 2 4 k_{{\rm crit}}\sim{N^{2}\over 4} for small values of d d . A derivation of this large N N formula is given using a matrix model approximation and semi-classical analysis of the eigenvalue density. The large N , d N,d limit of the degeneracies reveals a key role for ribbon graph combinatorics. The caloric fold is also notably a property of black hole thermodynamics in anti-de-Sitter spaces. We propose the spherically symmetric S ​ O ​ ( d ) SO(d) and O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) invariant sectors of d d -matrix quantum mechanics as tractable matrix systems for capturing key features of dual descriptions of black-hole thermodynamics.

In the classic paper of Hawking and Page

The lower branch describes the small black hole with negative heat capacity, while the upper branch

SO(d)
in
U(N)
-gauged harmonic oscillator quantum mechanics is equivalent to the counting of the
SO(d)\times U(N)
invariant polynomials in

of degree

.
We will describe a representation theoretic approach to this counting using an approach based on

, where this approach was used to construct orthogonal bases for these matrix invariants in the free-field inner product, which is also the harmonic oscillator quantum mechanics inner product. Related results on the counting of gauge invariants for multi-matrix theories are in
BDHO
;
Dolan
;
DutGop
.

We will explain below the derivation, along the lines of
BHR2
, of the following counting formula

\boxed{{\cal Z}(N,d,k)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\vdash k\\
l(T)\leq d\end{subarray}}C(R,R,T)\,{\rm{Dim}}(V_{\Lambda_{0},T})}

(112)

where
C(R,R,T)
is the Kronecker coefficient for the tensor product
R\otimes R\otimes T
for irreducible representations of
S_{k}
. This is the multiplicity of the trivial representation in the decomposition of the tensor product

R\otimes R\otimes T
, where elements
\sigma\in S_{k}
acts diagonally as
\sigma\otimes\sigma\otimes\sigma
, into irreducible representations. Equivalently it is the multiplicity of
T
in the decomposition of
R\otimes R
, with diagonal action of
\sigma\in S_{k}
as
\sigma\otimes\sigma
.
{\rm{Dim}}(V_{\Lambda_{0},T})
is dimension of the
SO(d)
invariant subspace of
V_{d}^{\otimes k}
into irreps of
SO(d)
, or equivalently the multiplicity of the trivial representation of
SO(d)
in the decomposition of
V_{d}^{\otimes k}
in terms of irreducible representations.

We have an elementary field
X_{a,j}^{i}
where

a
runs over
\{1,\cdots,d\}
and
i,j
run over
\{1,\cdots,N\}
. The invariants of degree
k
are built from appropriate index contractions of

\displaystyle\displaystyle X_{a_{1},j_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{a_{k},j_{k}}^{i_{k}}

(113)

Equivalently we are counting invariants of both
SO(d)
and
U(N)
in
{\rm{Sym}}^{k}(V_{N}\otimes\bar{V}_{N}\otimes V_{d})
. The
k
-fold symmetrisation comes from the fact that the
X
-variables above are commutative.

Using the standard Schur-Weyl duality, we have

\displaystyle V_{N}^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}V_{R}^{U(N)}\otimes V_{R}^{S_{k}}

(114)

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{N}^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\vdash k\\
l(S)\leq N\end{subarray}}\bar{V}_{S}^{U(N)}\otimes V_{S}^{S_{k}}

(115)

We have a direct sum over Young diagrams with
k
boxes and no more than
N
rows.
Projecting to
U(N)
invariants sets
R=S
in the expansions (
114
), and for each such pair there is a unique invariant.
We therefore have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm Invt}_{U(N)}(V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes\bar{V}_{N}^{\otimes k})=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}V_{R}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{R}^{S_{k}}

(116)

The
k
’th tensor power of the
d
-dimensional representation of
SO(d)
can be decomposed into irreps of
SO(d)
in two steps.

\displaystyle V_{d}^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\vdash k\\
l(T)\leq d\end{subarray}}V_{T}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{d}^{U(d)}

(117)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in{\rm Rep}(SO(d))}V_{T}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{\Lambda}^{SO(d)}\otimes V_{T.\Lambda}^{U(d)\rightarrow SO(d)}

(118)

In the first step we consider it as a representation of
U(d)
and decompose into irreps of
U(d)
labelled by Young diagrams with
k
boxes and no more than
d
rows.
In the second step we decompose the irreps
V_{T}^{U(d)}
into irreps of
V_{\Lambda}^{SO(d)}
with a multiplicity space

V_{\Lambda,T}

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm{Dim}}(V_{\Lambda,T}^{U(d)\rightarrow SO(d)})={\rm{Mult}}(V_{T}^{U(d)}\rightarrow V_{\Lambda}^{SO(d)})

(119)

The irreps
\Lambda
of
SO(d)
appearing in this decomposition are the tensor representations, which are indexed by Young diagrams with
k
or fewer boxes, and no more than
\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
rows. In the case of even
d
, there is the additional subtlety that for Young diagrams with exactly
\frac{d}{2}
rows, there is a pair of distinct representations with plus and minus chiralities
FultonHarris
. The general rules for the reduction multiplicities in (
119
) are available in the mathematical literature

Littlewood
;
King
;
KoikeTerada
;
HoweTanWillenbring
.

Thus

\displaystyle{\rm Invt}_{U(N)}(V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes V_{d}^{\otimes k})=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in{\rm Rep}(SO(d))}V_{R}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{R}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{T}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{\Lambda}^{SO(d)}\otimes V_{T.\Lambda}^{U(d)\rightarrow SO(d)}

(120)

Projecting to
U(N)
invariants in the
k
-fold symmetrised tensor power is equivalent to projecting to
U(N)\times S_{k}
invariant subspace in the tensor product

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm Invt}_{U(N)}({\rm{Sym}}^{k}(V_{N}\otimes V_{N}\otimes V_{d}))={\rm Invt}_{U(N)\times S_{k}}(V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes V_{d}^{\otimes k})

(122)

Hence

\displaystyle{\rm Invt}_{U(N)\times S_{k}}(V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes V_{d}^{\otimes k})=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in{\rm Rep}(SO(d))}V_{RRT}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{\Lambda}^{SO(d)}\otimes V_{T.\Lambda}^{U(d)\rightarrow SO(d)}

(123)

where
V_{RRT}^{S_{k}}
is the
S_{k}
invariant subspace of
V_{R}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{R}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{T}^{S_{k}}
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle V_{RRT}^{S_{k}}={\rm Invt}_{S_{k}}(V_{R}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{R}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{T}^{S_{k}})

(125)

The dimension of the invariant subspace is given by the Kronecker coefficient
C(R,R,T)

for the triple
(R,R,T)
, which is also the Clebsch-Gordan multiplicity for
V_{T}{(S_{k})}
in the
decomposition of the tensor product representation
V_{R}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{R}^{S_{k}}
into irreducibles

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm{Dim}}(V^{S_{k}}_{RRT})=C(R,R,T)

(126)

The Kronecker coefficient can be expressed in terms of characters of
S_{k}
.
The result is that the number of states in the irrep

\Lambda
, denoting it as
{\cal Z}(N,d,k;\Lambda)
is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal Z}(N,d,k;\Lambda)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\vdash k\\
l(T)\leq d\end{subarray}}C(R,R,T){\rm{Dim}}(V^{U(d)\rightarrow SO(d)}_{\Lambda,T})

(127)

Since we are after trivial irreps of
SO(d)
, we specialise
\Lambda
on the right hand side of (
123
) to
\Lambda_{0}
and obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Invt}_{U(N)\times SO(d)\times S_{k}}(V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes V_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes V_{d}^{\otimes k})=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in{\rm Rep}(SO(d))}V_{RRT}^{S_{k}}\otimes V_{\Lambda_{0}}^{SO(d)}\otimes V_{\Lambda_{0},T}^{U(d)\rightarrow SO(d)}

(128)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal Z}(N,d,k)

\displaystyle={\cal Z}(N,d,k;\Lambda_{0})

(131)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\vdash k\\
l(T)\leq d\end{subarray}}C(R,R,T){\rm{Dim}}(V^{U(d)\rightarrow SO(d)}_{\Lambda_{0},T})

Changing the invariance group from
SO(d)
modifies the derivation above in a straightforward way, leading to

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{O}(N,d,k;\Lambda)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\vdash k\\
l(T)\leq d\end{subarray}}C(R,R,T){\rm{Dim}}(V^{U(d)\rightarrow O(d)}_{\Lambda,T})

(132)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{O}(N,d,k)

\displaystyle={\cal Z}(N,d,k;\Lambda_{0})

(134)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\vdash k\\
l(T)\leq d\end{subarray}}C(R,R,T){\rm{Dim}}(V^{U(d)\rightarrow O(d)}_{\Lambda_{0},T})

While these formulae are useful for general representations
\Lambda
and the algorithms for general
\Lambda
can be specialised to
\Lambda_{0}
, it is possible to obtain more efficient algorithms for (
112
) and (
134
) by taking advantage of results on harmonic analysis on spaces

U(d)/SO(d)
and

.

5.2
SO(d)
and
O(d)
invariant sectors for general

using homogeneous spaces

In this section we use results from harmonic analysis on the homogeneous space
U(d)/SO(d)
to derive

\boxed{\begin{aligned} {\rm{Dim}}(V^{U(d)\rightarrow SO(d)}_{\Lambda_{0},T})&=1~~\hbox{if}~~T\in{\cal A}(k,d)\cr&=0~~\hbox{if}~~T\notin{\cal A}(k,d)\end{aligned}}

(135)

where

\displaystyle{\cal A}(k,d)={\cal A}_{+}(k,d)\cup{\cal A}_{-}(k,d)

with

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)=\hbox{Set of Young diagrams with $k$ boxes, even row lengths and up to $d$ rows. }

(137)

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{-}(k,d)=\hbox{Set of Young diagrams with $k$ boxes, odd row lengths and exactly $d$ rows. }

(138)

The result for

invariant sector is

\boxed{\begin{aligned} {\rm{Dim}}(V^{U(d)\rightarrow O(d)}_{\Lambda_{0},T})&=1~~\hbox{if}~~T\in{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)\cr&=0~~\hbox{if}~~T\notin{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)\end{aligned}}

(140)

Comparing
O(d)
and
SO(d)

For

odd, diagrams in
{\cal A}_{-}(k,d)
have an odd number of boxes, since they have, by definition, exactly
d
rows of odd lengths. This means that
{\cal A}_{-}(k,d)
is empty for even
k
.
Furthermore,
{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)
for odd
d
and odd
k
is empty, since diagrams in this set must have even row lengths.
This means that

Odd
d
case

(141)

\displaystyle~~\hbox{For }\hbox{ even energies, }k=2n~:~{\cal A}(2n,d)

\displaystyle={\cal A}_{+}(2n,d)

(142)

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{SO}(N,d,2n)

\displaystyle={\cal Z}_{O}(N,d,2n)

(143)

\displaystyle~~\hbox{For }\hbox{ odd energies, }k=(2n+1)~:~{\cal A}(2n+1,d)

\displaystyle={\cal A}_{-}(2n+1,d)

(144)

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{O}(N,d,2n+1)

\displaystyle=0

(145)

Now consider even
d
,
{\cal A}_{-}(k,d)
as well as
{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)
are empty for odd

.
Hence in this case we have

Even

case

(146)

\displaystyle\hbox{Odd energies, }k

\displaystyle=(2n+1)~:~{\cal Z}_{SO}(N,d,2n+1)={\cal Z}_{O}(N,d,2n+1)=0

In this case, for even

, generically

and

are both non-zero and different.

5.2.1
Cartan-Helgason theorem and
SO(d)
invariant subspace of
V_{d}^{\otimes k}

In (
117
) we described the
SO(d)
invariant subspace of the

k
-fold tensor product
V_{d}^{\otimes k}
by first decomposing it into

U(d)
irreducible representations using Schur–Weyl duality, and then
reducing these
U(d)
irreps to
SO(d)
irreps. Specialising this
reduction to the trivial
SO(d)
representation
\Lambda_{0}
leads to
(
128
).

A general result from harmonic analysis on homogeneous spaces gives the
decomposition of the space of functions on a coset as

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm Functions}(G/H)=\bigoplus_{T\in{\rm Rep}(G)}V^{G}_{T}\otimes V^{G\rightarrow H}_{T,\Lambda_{0}}

(149)

in terms of the multiplicity spaces
V^{G\rightarrow H}_{T,\Lambda_{0}}
,
which describe the decomposition of the irreducible representation
T
of
G

into representations of
H
, with
\Lambda_{0}
denoting the trivial representation of
H
.

In the present context we restrict attention to polynomial
(regular) functions on
G/H
. These provide an algebraic basis for the
representation-theoretic decomposition of functions on
G/H
, modulo the
analytic completion appearing in the Peter–Weyl theorem. The
decomposition above is a standard consequence of harmonic analysis on
compact homogeneous spaces. See for example Helgason
Helgason
for
a systematic treatment of functions on
G/H
, and Fulton–Harris

FultonHarris
for background on polynomial representations of

U(d)
.

Interesting instances of these homogeneous spaces are ones where the multiplicity spaces have dimension zero or one.
We will find the Cartan–Helgason result for the coset
SU(d)/SO(d)
to be useful:

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm Functions}(SU(d)/SO(d))=\bigoplus_{k}\bigoplus_{T\in{\cal B}(k,d)}V_{T}^{SU(d)}

(150)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal B}(k,d)=\hbox{ Set of Young diagrams with $k$ boxes, up $d-1$ rows, all of even length. }

(151)

Equivalently

\displaystyle{\rm{Dim}}(V^{SU(d)\rightarrow SO(d)}_{\Lambda_{0},T})

\displaystyle=1~~\hbox{if}~~T\in{\cal B}(k,d)

(152)

\displaystyle=0~~\hbox{if}~~T\notin{\cal B}(k,d)

(153)

Irreducible representations of
U(d)
can be obtained from those of
SU(d)

by tensoring with powers of the determinant representation.

We can therefore relate the
SU(d)
result above to the
U(d)
result we are after.
The Young diagrams labelling irreducible representations of
U(d)
appearing
in the decomposition of the tensor space
V_{d}^{\otimes k}
can be obtained
from Young diagrams in
{\cal B}(k^{\prime}=k-md,d)
by attaching
m
columns of
height
d
. This corresponds to tensoring with
m
copies of
\Lambda^{d}(V_{d})
,
which is the determinant representation and is
SO(d)
invariant.
The set
{\cal A}(k,d)
is precisely the set of Young diagrams obtained from

{\cal B}(k^{\prime},d)
with
k^{\prime}=k-md
by attaching
m
columns of length
d
.

Attaching a column of height
d
increases each row length by one and
introduces a
d
-th row if it was previously absent. Starting from Young
diagrams in
{\cal B}(k^{\prime},d)
with even row lengths and at most
d-1
rows,
attaching
m
such columns produces diagrams whose row lengths have
parity determined by
m
. If
m
is even the resulting diagrams have
even row lengths and at most
d
rows, giving the family
{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)
.
If
m
is odd the resulting diagrams have odd row lengths and exactly

d
rows, giving the family
{\cal A}_{-}(k,d)
.

5.4
Simplification from Kronecker coefficients to character sums

For the regime,
k\leq N
, it is useful to use the character expansion of the Kronecker coefficient

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal Z}(N,d,k)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{p\vdash k}{1\over{\rm{Sym}}~p}(\chi^{R}_{p})^{2}\sum_{S\in{\cal A}(k,d)}\chi^{S}_{p}

(156)

and change the order of sums, to do the
R
-sum first

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal Z}(N,d,k)=\sum_{p\vdash k}{1\over{\rm{Sym}}~p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}(\chi^{R}_{p})^{2}\left(\sum_{S\in{\cal A}(k,d)}\chi^{S}_{p}\right)\,.

(157)

It is useful to define two functions on partitions
p\vdash k
, which carry information about finite
N
and
d
cut-offs respectively

\displaystyle\Psi(N,k;p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}(\chi^{R}_{p})^{2}

(158)

\displaystyle\Phi(d,k;p)=\sum_{S\in{\cal A}(k,d)}\chi^{S}_{p}

(159)

This allows us to write

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal Z}(N,d,k)=\sum_{p\vdash k}{\Psi(N,k;p)\Phi(d,k;p)\over{\rm{Sym}}~p}

(160)

We may think of
\Psi(N,k;p)
as the coefficients of a vector
\Psi(N,k)
in the vector space of partitions
p
of the integer
k
, and likewise

\Phi(d,k;p)
as the coefficients of a vector
\Phi(d,k)
:

\displaystyle\Psi(N,k)=\sum_{p\vdash k}\Psi(N,k;p)~p

(161)

\displaystyle\Phi(d,k)=\sum_{p\vdash k}\Phi(d,k;p)~p

(162)

and

\displaystyle\boxed{{\cal Z}(N,d,k)=\sum_{p\vdash k}{\Psi(N,k;p)\Phi(d,k;p)\over{\rm{Sym}}~p}=\langle\Psi(N,k),\Phi(d,k)\rangle}

(163)

For the
O(d)
case, we have

{\cal Z}_{O}(N,d,k)=\sum_{p\vdash k}{1\over{\rm{Sym}}~p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\vdash k\\
l(R)\leq N\end{subarray}}(\chi^{R}_{p})^{2}\sum_{S\in{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)}\chi^{S}_{p}

(164)

and defining

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{+}(d,k;p)=\sum_{S\in{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)}\chi^{S}_{p}

(165)

we have

\displaystyle\boxed{{\cal Z}_{O}(N,d,k)=\sum_{p\vdash k}{\Psi(N,k;p)\Phi_{+}(d,k;p)\over{\rm{Sym}}~p}=\langle\Psi(N,k),\Phi_{+}(d,k)\rangle}

(166)

The equations (
163
) and (
166
) give our most efficient formulae for the computation of the micro-canonical partition functions. The sage implementation is described in Appendix
B
.

We can arrive at the same expression (

treat the existing vertices of the graph as black nodes of a new embedded graph. The new graph is also endowed with white vertices in the middle of the edges of the original graph, thus dividing each edge of the original graph into two half-edges. We can assign labels
\{1,2,\cdots,2n\}

\sigma_{1}\in S_{2n}

q\vdash 2n

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\rightarrow\alpha\sigma_{1}\alpha^{-1}

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}\rightarrow\alpha\sigma_{2}\alpha^{-1}

(229)

Caloric fold at large

We now consider the partition function

From the discussion above,

The large

is known

{\cal Z}^{\rm conn}(\infty,\infty,k=2n)\sim\sqrt{2}\,(2n)^{n}e^{-n}.

The corresponding disconnected count
{\cal Z}(\infty,\infty,k=2n)
is obtained by allowing
arbitrary finite collections of connected components.
A disconnected object with total size
n
is assembled from connected
components whose sizes sum to
n
. Thus the possible component-size
decompositions include

n,\qquad(n-1)+1,\qquad(n-2)+2,\qquad(n-2)+1+1,\qquad(n/2)+(n/2),\ldots.

The leading term comes from the single connected component of size
n
,
namely
{\cal Z}^{{\rm conn}}(\infty,\infty,2n)
. This dominance follows from the super-exponential growth

{\cal Z}^{{\rm conn}}(\infty,\infty,2n)\sim\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{2n}{e}\right)^{n}

For example, the contribution from two components of comparable size,
schematically
n/2+n/2
, is of order

({\cal Z}^{{\rm conn}}(\infty,\infty,n))^{2}\sim 2\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n},

which is exponentially suppressed relative to the
single-component term.

The entropy at large

is thus

\displaystyle\displaystyle S=\log({\cal Z}^{{\rm conn}}(\infty,\infty,2n))=n\log n-(1-\log 2)n

(242)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\partial^{2}S\over\partial n^{2}}\sim{1\over n}

(243)

which is positive and gives negative heat capacity.

N
,
d=\infty

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal Z}(N,\infty,2k)

derived earlier. Computations with

9
Summary, Discussion and Outlook

We start with a summary of the results of the paper accompanied by a discussion of natural generalisations and related literature. This includes a conjecture motivated by AdS/CFT and the results of the present paper.

9.1
Summary, discussion and related literature

In this paper we have studied the states of quantum mechanical
matrices
X_{a}
with
a=1,\cdots,d
invariant under both the adjoint
action of
U(N)
and the vector action of
SO(d)
. These state form
subsectors of the full Hilbert space of multi-matrix models where only

U(N)
and
SO(d)
(or
O(d)
) singlets are permitted. Our purpose here
was to study these subsystem from a microcanonical point of view. For
this we count the number of states at each level,
k
, of the Hilbert
space where
k
counts the number of matrix creation operators acting
on the vacuum. The generating function for such counting is the
thermal canonical ensemble partition function of an
U(N)\times SO(d)

(or
U(N)\times O(d)
) gauged gaussian matrix model.
Gauging the orthogonal group imposes the
further constraint that only rotationally invariant
combinations of the matrices are permitted. In the
U(N)\times O(d)

case we further demand invariance under reflection of any individual
matrix, thus excluding contractions with the
\epsilon
-tensor of
SO(d)
.

We found that, in the micro-canonical ensemble, these systems naturally have negative heat capacity at
low energies which turns over in what we term a “caloric fold” to
positive heat capacity at high energies. At large
N
they have a
Hagedorn transition at
T_{H}=\frac{1}{\ln d}
, i.e. at the same
temerature as the
U(N)
gauged models. There is equivalence of
ensembles in the high energy regime but not for low energies. The
characteristic thermal behaviour of these systems is very reminiscent
of black holes in anti De Sitter spacetime and we conjecture that the
gauge invariant states that are in addition rotationally invariant
should be identified with black hole states in the dual gravity
picture. At low energies the subsector of rotationally invariant
states has negative heat capacity and is the natural candidate for the
small black hole configurations which are unstable and decay. But in
the larger system, below the Hawking-Page/confining-deconfining
transition, they are in thermal equilibrium with the thermal radiation
and fluctuate in and out of existence as one would expect in a finite
system undergoing a first order phase transition. In the terminology of

Nagle1973
;
BhattacharjeeNagleHuseFisher
the transition is
‘a
3/2
-order transition’ i.e. it appears first order as seen from the
low temperature side as the internal energy in the canonical ensemble
jumps from zero to
\frac{1}{4}
but on cooling from the high
temperature side it appears to be 2nd order (or continuous) with
a divergent heat capacity see Eqn (
187
).

This work is the starting point for a larger research programme. A
natural next step is to consider fermionic matrices. Here there are
options for how the fermions transform under the orthogonal
group. They might transform as either a vector or as a spinor, both are natural for matrix models, e.g. fermions transform under the spinor representation of
SO(9)
for the BFSS model. When transforming
under the vector representation the analysis would be very similar to
that performed here and one can anticipate based on the large
N

connection with the

A strong coupling AdS/CFT conjecture for the minimum black hole temperature in AdS

Our results establishing the caloric fold as a generic feature of

Review of Black Hole formulae

Here we recall the black hole formulae in
d
-dimensions, first in asymptotically flat spacetime and then we consider Schwarzschild-AdS.

C.1
Schwarzschild Black Holes in
D
Dimensions and Negative Specific Heat

In
D
spacetime dimensions, the Schwarzschild–Tangherlini metric is

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\left(\frac{r_{h}}{r}\right)^{D-3}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\left(1-\left(\frac{r_{h}}{r}\right)^{D-3}\right)}+r^{2}d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}\,.

(271)

describes a black hole in asymptotically flat spacetime. Such black holes only exist for dimensions
D>3
.

The ADM mass,
M
related to horizon radius
r_{h}
by

M=\frac{(D-2)\,\Omega_{D-2}}{16\pi G_{D}}\,r_{h}^{D-3}\,,

(272)

where
\Omega_{D-2}
, the hypersurface area of the unit
(D-2)
-sphere, is

\Omega_{D-2}=\frac{2\pi^{(D-1)/2}}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{D-1}{2}\right)}\,.

(273)

The Bekenstein–Hawking entropy is

S=\frac{A}{4G_{D}}=\frac{\Omega_{D-2}\,r_{h}^{D-2}}{4G_{D}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad M=\frac{D-2}{4\pi r_{h}}\frac{A}{4G_{D}}\,,

(274)

These imply the scaling relations

M\propto r_{h}^{D-3},\qquad S\propto r_{h}^{D-2}\propto M^{\frac{D-3}{D-2}},\qquad\implies M\frac{dS}{dM}=\frac{D-2}{D-3}S\,.

(275)

The ADM mass is the natural energy for this system and the microcanonical temperature obtained from the entropy eqn (
52
) gives the microcanonical temperature as the Hawking temperature

T=\frac{D-3}{4\pi r_{h}}

(276)

Using
M\propto T^{-(D-3)}
so that
T\frac{dM}{dT}=-(D-3)M
we then see that
the specific heat is defined by

\boxed{C=\frac{dM}{dT}=-(D-3)\frac{M}{T}}\,.

(277)

Thus Schwarzschild type black holes in asymptotically flat spacetime have negative specific heat in any spacetime dimension
D\geq 4
, reflecting the fact that they become hotter as they lose energy.

Low Energy and High Energy regimes:

For

the equation

•

(291)

(292)

•

\boxed{~~{\cal Z}_{(H;G)}\equiv\sum_{C\in{\mathrm{Classes}}(G)}\Phi_{(H;G)}(C)=\sum_{\tilde{C}\in{\mathrm{Classes}}(G)}\Phi{{\raisebox{0.08612pt}{\Large$\check{}$}}}_{(H;G)}(\tilde{C})~~}

Appendix F
An identity relating dimensions of symmetric group representations to special functions

We have approached the counting of
U(N)\times SO(d)
and
U(N)\times O(d)
polynomial
invariants of multi-matrices in two ways in this paper. One approach in section
4
works directly with the Molien-Weyl integration formula for the invariants, and the other in section
5
approaches the counting using the combinatorics of words in
X^{i}_{a,j}
approached using the representation theory of tensor product spaces. Combining these two perspectives gives some interesting identities relating the special functions in equations (
68
) and (
101
) to sums of dimensions of irreducible
symmetric group representations. These sums are taken over the subsets
{\cal A}(k,d)
and
{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)
defined in (
136
) and (
137
).

Let
V_{d}
denote the vector representation of
SO(d)
. The stable
generating function
\widehat{Z}_{d}(x)
counts the dimensions of the
invariant subspaces

\left(V_{d}^{\otimes k}\right)^{SO(d)}.

Schur–Weyl duality gives

V_{d}^{\otimes k}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\vdash k\\
\ell(T)\leq d\end{subarray}}V_{T}^{U(d)}\otimes V_{T}^{S_{k}},

where
T
is a Young diagram with
k
boxes. We write

d_{T}:=\dim V_{T}^{S_{k}}

for the dimension of the corresponding symmetric-group representation.
Taking
SO(d)
-invariants gives

\dim\left(V_{d}^{\otimes k}\right)^{SO(d)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\vdash k\\
\ell(T)\leq d\end{subarray}}d_{T}\,\dim\left(V_{T}^{U(d)}\right)^{SO(d)}.

The Cartan–Helgason selection rule gives

\dim\left(V_{T}^{U(d)}\right)^{SO(d)}=\begin{cases}1,&T\in{\cal A}(k,d),\\
0,&T\notin{\cal A}(k,d),\end{cases}

where

{\cal A}(k,d)={\cal A}_{+}(k,d)\cup\mathcal{A}_{-}(k,d).

Here

{\cal A}_{+}(k,d)=\{\,T\vdash k:\ell(T)\leq d,\ \hbox{all row lengths of }T\hbox{ are even}\,\},

and

{\cal A}_{-}(k,d)=\{\,T\vdash k:\ell(T)=d,\ \hbox{all row lengths of }T\hbox{ are odd}\,\}.

It follows that the coefficient of
x^{k}
in the
SO(d)

\displaystyle\boxed{~~{\rm Coefficient}(\widehat{\cal Z}_{d}(x),x^{k})=\hat{{\cal Z}}_{d,k}=\sum_{T\in{\cal A}(k,d)}d_{T}~~}

(322)

Each allowed diagram in
{\cal A}(k,d)
contributes with its Schur–Weyl
multiplicity
d_{T}
.

For the
O(d)
-invariant tensor space, the epsilon-tensor sector is
removed. Equivalently, only the even-row sector survives:

\dim\left(V_{T}^{U(d)}\right)^{O(d)}=\begin{cases}1,&T\in\mathcal{A}_{+}(k,d),\\
0,&T\notin\mathcal{A}_{+}(k,d).\end{cases}

Hence the corresponding identity following from the counting of
O(d)
invariants os

\boxed{~~{\rm Coefficient}(\widehat{\cal Z}_{Od}(x),x^{k})=\hat{{\cal Z}}_{Od,k}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{A}_{+}(k,d)}d_{T}~~}

(323)

This gives a direct combinatorial verification of the closed-form
generating functions for
d=2,3,4,5
in the
SO(d)
case, and for

d=2,3,4
in the
O(d)
case. We have checked these identities (
322
) and (
323
) for selected ranges of
k
with Mathematica.
