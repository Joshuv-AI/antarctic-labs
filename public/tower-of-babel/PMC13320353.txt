Comprehensive analysis of antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) targets in adenoid cystic carcinoma: examining antigen expression in relation to clinicopathological features, prognosis, and initial therapeutic outcomes
Authors: Yu Wang, Shengjin Dou, Wen Jiang, Ling Zhu, Shengnan Liu, Ting Gu, Guopei Zhu, Jiang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Comprehensive analysis of five ADC targets in a relatively large ACC cohort.•EGFR and TROP2 are highly expressed in non-solid subtype ACC.•B7-H4 and Nectin-4 are highly expressed in solid subtype ACC.•High B7-H4 and Nectin-4 expression levels predict poor overall survival.•Preliminary clinical evidence may inform biomarker-guided ADC therapy.Keywords: Adenoid cystic carcinoma, Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), Non-solid subtype, Solid subtype

Introduction

Adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) is the most common malignant tumor originating from salivary glands, making up about 30.42% of all salivary gland cancers in the Chinese population [1]. Histologically, ACC is characterized by its dual differentiation into myoepithelial and luminal epithelial cells arranged in cribriform, tubular, and solid growth patterns [2]. Tumors with a solid component comprising 30% of the total area are classified as the solid subtype, which is consistently associated with more aggressive clinical behavior and a poorer prognosis than the cribriform-tubular subtype (also called the non-solid subtype) [3].

ACC is characterized by perineural invasion, a high propensity for distant metastasis, and persistent progression despite local control [4,5]. Current treatment options are limited to surgical resection with or without adjuvant radiotherapy [6]. Cytotoxic chemotherapy shows poor response rates, and the “immune-desert” tumor microenvironment of ACC presents a major obstacle to the effectiveness of modern immunotherapies [7,8]. Therefore, therapeutic options for refractory and recurrent/metastatic ACCs are limited, with no FDA-approved systemic treatments currently available.

The arrival of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) has marked a new chapter in cancer treatment, combining the targeting precision of monoclonal antibodies with the powerful cell-killing ability of chemotherapeutic drugs [9]. The effectiveness of ADCs depends on several factors, such as antibody specificity, the type of cytotoxic payload, linker design, and conjugation techniques, with the expression level of tumor-associated antigens (TAAs) on the cell surface being especially important [10,11]. ADCs can further impact neighboring cells that lack the target antigen, exerting notable bystander effects [12]. To date, 21 ADCs targeting 13 TAAs have been approved for the treatment of both solid and blood cancers. Among these, four targets, HER2, EGFR, Nectin-4, and TROP2, are mainly found in solid tumors. However, ACC is known to almost completely lack HER2 expression (0.037% - 5.4%), leaving EGFR, Nectin-4, and TROP2 as the remaining potential options [13]. In addition, two other targets, AXL and B7-H4, have emerged as promising ADC targets in clinical trials for other solid tumors due to their roles in treatment resistance [14] and immune evasion [15] respectively. Collectively, EGFR, Nectin-4, TROP2, AXL, and B7-H4 have become important ADC target candidates for study in adenoid cystic carcinoma.

As ADC research progresses, therapeutic expression thresholds differ across various cancers, antigens, and drugs. For instance, TDXd effectively treats HER2-low/negative breast cancer [16], whereas TROP2 ADC (ASCENT trial) benefits patients with metastatic triple-negative breast cancer only when they have moderate to high TROP2 expression [17]. These context-dependent thresholds highlight the importance of defining not only the presence but also the level and clinical significance of ADC target expression in ACC.

In ACC, although B7-H4 and TROP2 have been previously studied [18,19], these investigations focused on individual targets, and a comprehensive head-to-head evaluation of all five targets in a large ACC cohort has not yet been conducted. Furthermore, biomarker-guided ADC therapy in ACC patients has not yet been investigated clinically. To fill this gap, we extensively analyzed the expression of all five ADC targets in a large cohort of 265 ACC specimens. We systematically linked their expression to different histological subtypes and clinicopathological features, and offered initial clinical validation with two representative cases of biomarker-guided ADC therapy.

Materials and methods

Patient cohort and tissue samples

A total of 265 cases, including surgical and consultation cases diagnosed with ACC, were selected from the archives of the Ninth People’s Hospital affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University from 2009 to 2025. Based on histological criteria, the study comprised 193 non-solid ACCs and 72 solid-type ACCs in which the solid component exceeded 30%. Follow-up data were available for 129 patients. This study was approved by the Medicine Ethics Committee of the Ninth People’s Hospital affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SH9H-2025-T580–2).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Immunohistochemistry was conducted using the EnVisionTM detection kit (Dako, USA) as previously described. The primary antibodies, clones, and dilutions included: anti-EGFR (EP22, ZSGB-Bio); anti-TROP2 (MRX033, MXB, prediluted); anti-B7-H4 (D1M8I, Cell Signaling Technology, 1:200); anti-AXL (EPR19880, Abcam, 1:2000); and anti-Nectin-4 (1:500, Ab155692, Abcam). Since binding to membrane-bound antigen is essential for ADC effectiveness, only membranous immunoreactivity was evaluated, even though cytoplasmic presence was also observed. Staining intensity was first scored as weak (1+), moderate (2+), or strong (3+). Based on the combination of intensity and percentage of positive cells, Samples were classified as negative (<1% of any intensity), low (≤30% of moderate), moderate (>70% of weak or >30% of moderate or ≤30% of strong), and high (>70% of moderate or >30% of strong). This semiquantitative system was adapted from published criteria [18,20] and applied uniformly to all five targets. For binary analyses, “high” was compared to “non-high” (negative/low/moderate). HER2 expression was evaluated using the anti-HER2 antibody (clone 4B5, Ventana) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and scored as 0, 1+, 2+, or 3+ following the ASCO/CAP guideline: negative (score 0, 1 +, with < 10%, incomplete/fainth membrane staining), weakly positive/ equivocal (score 2 +, with circumferential membrane staining that is incomplete or weak in > 10% of cells, or complete strong membranous staining in < 10% of cells), positive (score 3 +, with homogeneous dark “chicken-wire” staining in10% of carcinoma cells); only cases with score 3 + were considered. Three pathologists independently examined the samples, with a consensus required from at least two to finalize the assessment.

Clinical case evaluation

To assess the translational potential of our biomarker profile, we performed an exploratory evaluation of ADC therapy in two patients with ACC who showed high expression of the relevant target. The treatment strategies included: (1) EGFR-directed ADC MRG003 (Becotatug vedotin) given at 2 mg/kg intravenously every 3 weeks for three cycles, combined with stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT, 5.5 Gy × 5 fractions) to the primary tumor; and (2) TROP2-directed ADC Sac-TMT (Sacituzumab Tirumotecan, SKB264) administered at 5 mg/kg intravenously every 2 weeks for six cycles. Treatment responses were monitored via contrast-enhanced MRI in line with RECIST v1.1 criteria. When available, surgical specimens were examined for pathological response, with pathological complete response (pCR) indicating no viable tumor cells. Adverse events were recorded and graded using the CTCAE v5.0.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted with SPSS (version 26.0; IBM). Categorical associations were assessed using Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. Overall survival was estimated by the Kaplan–Meier method (log-rank test) and Cox proportional hazards regression (univariate and multivariate) to calculate hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. To adjust for multiple comparisons, the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (q = 0.05) was applied to the association analyses; Bonferroni correction was used for survival analyses (log-rank tests for four targets: α = 0.05/4 = 0.0125; multivariate Cox for four targets: α = 0.05/4 = 0.0125) and for histological subtype comparisons (five targets: α = 0.05/5 = 0.01).

Results

Patient characteristics and HER2 negative control data

The study cohort comprised 265 patients diagnosed with ACC, with a median age of 48 years and a female predominance (151 females vs. 114 males). Of these, 245 were primary or locally recurrent tumors, and the remaining 20 were distant metastases (to the bone or lung). Based on histologic grading [21,22], 193 cases were classified as cribriform and tubular (non-solid) ACC, and 72 as solid-type ACC. The overall rate of distant metastasis was 49.4% (131 out of 265), with metastases present in 49.7% (96/193) of non-solid ACC and 48.6% (35/72) of solid-type ACC, a difference that was not statistically significant. Regarding the primary tumor sites, 109 cases originated in the major salivary glands, 130 in the minor salivary glands, and 26 in other locations, including the lacrimal glands.

To verify the reported extremely low HER2 expression in ACC, we performed HER2 IHC on 125 randomly selected cases from the cohort. Following the ASCO/CAP guidelines, 124 cases (99.2%) were scored as 0 or 1+ (negative), and only one case (0.8%) showed equivocal (2+) weak incomplete membranous staining. No case exhibited 3+ positive staining (Supplementary Figure 1). These results confirm that HER2 expression is virtually absent in ACC, consistent with previous reports.

EGFR and TROP2 expression profiles in ACC

The expression levels of EGFR and TROP2 were assessed in 237 ACC samples. EGFR was negative in 8 cases (3.4%), low in 37 (15.6%), moderate in 58 (24.5%), and high in 134 (56.5%). TROP2 was negative in 6 cases (2.5%), low in 48 (20.3%), moderate in 71 (29.9%), and high in 112 (47.3%). Overall, moderate to high expression was observed in 81% of EGFR and 77% of TROP2 samples, highlighting the frequent presence of both markers in ACC (Fig. 1A and 1B). Importantly, strong and diffuse TROP2 expression was seen in the acinar and ductal cells of normal salivary glands, whereas EGFR was only expressed in the ductal cells (Figure Ca and Ea).

Clinicopathological analysis revealed that EGFR expression was not significantly linked to age, sex, tumor site, perineural invasion, or distant metastasis (all p > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 1). Conversely, high TROP2 expression was notably associated with a reduced likelihood of distant metastasis (raw p value= 0.027). Specifically, high TROP2 expression was present in 38.6% of metastatic cases, compared to 55.3% of non-metastatic cases. However, after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction for multiple comparisons (q = 0.05), this association was no longer statistically significant. Other clinical features showed no statistically significant associations (Supplementary Table 2).

Given their widespread expression in ACC, we further explored their expression patterns across different histologic subtypes: high levels of both EGFR and TROP2 were mainly observed in non-solid ACC (72.1% and 55.2%, respectively), while solid-type ACC showed overall downregulation (with high expression rates of only 15.4% for EGFR and 26.2% for TROP2, raw p < 0.0001, adjusted p < 0.01) (Fig. 1D 1F).

In addition to the inter-tumoral heterogeneity described above, namely differences between classical and solid-type ACC, we also observed intratumoral heterogeneity in several ACC cases with mixed histological components. As shown in Supplementary Figure 2, the solid component of a mixed-type ACC was completely negative for TROP2, whereas the cribriform non-solid component showed strong membranous staining. This heterogeneous expression pattern reflects intratumoral heterogeneity and highlights the potential relevance of bystander effects in ADC therapy, as membrane-permeable payloads may eliminate adjacent antigen-negative cells.

B7-H4 and Nectin-4 expression profiles in ACC

B7-H4 and Nectin-4 were also commonly expressed in ACC, but their distribution patterns differed from those of EGFR and TROP2. For B7-H4 (n = 228) and Nectin-4 (n = 174), the expression levels were as follows: B7-H4 was negative in 4.4% (10/228), low in 26.8% (61/228), moderate in 31.6% (72/228), and high in 37.3% (85/228); Nectin-4 displayed a similar pattern with 3.4% negative (6/174), 31.0% low (54/174), 32.2% moderate (56/174), and 33.3% high (58/174) (Fig. 2A, 2B, 2C 2E). High expression levels of B7-H4 (65.6%) and Nectin-4 (74.5%) were mainly found in solid-type ACC, with significantly lower frequencies in the non-solid subtype (26.2% and 16.3%, respectively; Figs. 2D and 2F). Both differences remained significant after Bonferroni correction (raw p < 0.0001, adjusted p < 0.01). In normal salivary gland tissues, both markers were either absent or showed only faint expression (Figs. 2Ca and 2Ea). Analysis of key clinicopathological parameters, including age, sex, primary tumor site, perineural invasion, and distant metastasis, showed no significant correlations with either marker (all p > 0.05; Supplementary Table 3 and Table 4).

AXL expression profile in ACC

Analysis of AXL expression in ACC showed a distribution pattern different from that of previously identified markers. In the total cohort of 169 cases, AXL was most commonly expressed at low levels (45.6%,77/169), followed by negative (37.9%, 64/169), moderate (14.8%, 25/169), and high (1.8%, 3/169) levels (Fig. 3A, B, C). No significant relationships were found between AXL levels and key clinical or pathological parameters such as age (p = 0.589), sex (p = 0.745), tumor site (p = 0.304), perineural invasion (p = 0.505), tumor origin (p = 0.751), or distant metastasis (p = 0.089) (Supplementary Table 5). AXL expression patterns showed no significant difference between non-solid and solid ACC histological subtypes (p = 0.61) (Fig. 3A, B, C). Overall, AXL expression in ACC was mainly low or negative and did not strongly correlate with clinicopathological features or the major histological subtypes.

Comparison of biomarker expression in primary/recurrent and metastatic tumors

The biomarker expression profiles were analyzed and compared between primary/recurrent tumors and distant metastases. EGFR, B7-H4, and Nectin-4 maintained relatively high and consistent levels across both groups, with no significant differences in their distribution patterns (p = 0.418, p = 0.476, and p = 0.255, respectively) (Supplementary Table 1–3). Conversely, TROP2 expression showed potential heterogeneity between primary and metastatic sites, with a notably lower rate of high expression in metastases (25%) compared to primary tumors (48.9%) (Supplementary Table 2). However, this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.095) and required validation in an independent ACC cohort. AXL expression, primarily low or negative, did not vary significantly based on tumor origin (p = 0.751) (Supplementary Table 5).

Prognostic significance of EGFR, TROP2, B7-H4, and Nectin-4 expression in ACC

Survival analysis was conducted for four key biomarkers (EGFR, TROP2, B7-H4, and Nectin-4). For the log-rank tests and Cox regressions, Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons was applied (α = 0.05/4 = 0.0125). AXL was excluded from this analysis due to an insufficient number of high-expression cases to allow for robust statistical evaluation. The results of the log-rank tests showed distinct prognostic effects for the markers examined. Both B7-H4 and Nectin-4 expression levels were significantly linked to patient outcomes. Patients with high Nectin-4 expression experienced a notably shorter median survival (48.0 months) compared to those with low expression (183.0 months), with a hazard ratio (HR) of 4.794 (95% CI: 2.409–9.54, log-rank p < 0.0001, adjusted p < 0.0125). Similarly, high B7-H4 expression was associated with significantly worse survival (median survival of 65.0 vs. 212.0 months; HR = 4.046, 95% CI: 2.098–7.803, log-rank p < 0.0001, adjusted p < 0.0125) (Fig. 4C, 4D). In contrast, neither EGFR nor TROP2 expression was significantly linked to overall survival. The median survival times and hazard ratios for both EGFR (HR = 0.7877, p = 0.4310) and TROP2 (HR = 0.697, p = 0.2419) did not differ significantly between the high and low expression groups (Fig. 4A, 4B).

Additional clinical factors that could affect prognosis, including age, gender, primary tumor site, neural invasion, distant metastasis (DM), and histologic subtype, were also evaluated. Univariate analysis showed that, in addition to high B7-H4 and Nectin-4 expression, histologic subtype (solid vs. non-solid) was strongly associated with shorter overall survival (HR = 5.653, 95% CI: 3.082–10.370, p < 0.0001). By contrast, high TROP2 expression (HR = 0.506, 95% CI: 0.334–0.766, p = 0.001) and high EGFR expression (HR = 0.541, 95% CI: 0.353–0.830, p = 0.005) appeared to be associated with favorable survival outcomes (Supplementary Table 6A). In multivariate analyses of the four targets using a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold of α = 0.0125, after adjustment for age, histologic subtype, and distant metastasis, neither TROP2 nor EGFR was independently associated with survival (both p > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 6B, 6C). Conversely, B7-H4 (adjusted HR = 1.56, p = 0.012) and Nectin-4 (adjusted HR = 1.89, p = 0.005) remained significant independent predictors of poorer overall survival (Fig. 4E and 4F).

Co-Expression and complementary expression patterns among TAAs

To investigate the potential of combined ADC strategies, we examined the co-expression patterns of all five targets in a subset of 97 ACC cases (comprising 31 solid-type and 66 non-solid). These cases were successfully evaluated for all targets despite limited tissue availability. It was common to see high expression across multiple targets: 17 cases (17.5%) showed high levels of three or more targets, including 11 (11%) with three, five (5%) with four, and one (1%) with all five targets. (Fig. 4G). At the pairwise level, the most common high-expression combination was EGFRhigh/TROP2high, found in 37 cases (38.1% of the cohort). This pattern was strongly associated with the non-solid histologic subtype, which accounted for 50% of non-solid ACC cases but only 12.9% of solid-type cases. Conversely, a different correlated pattern involved B7-H4high/Nectin-4high coexpression, mainly observed in the solid-type subtype, present in 48.4% of solid cases compared to just 6.1% of non-solid cases (Fig. 4H). These results highlight two major co-expression architectures associated with specific subtypes of ACC.

Preliminary clinical evidence of ADC efficacy in two ACC patients

As an initial effort, we investigated ADC therapy in the difficult setting of refractory or recurrent/metastatic ACC. Both patients were selected for ADC therapy based on high expression of the corresponding target according to our IHC scoring criteria (EGFRHigh for MRG003; TROP2 High for Sac-TMT).

EGFR-Directed ADC (MRG003) achieves pathological complete response in neoadjuvant setting

A 54-year-old woman was diagnosed with primary, locally advanced adenoid cystic carcinoma at the right tongue base, initially detected via laryngoscopy and confirmed through biopsy in December 2025. Contrast-enhanced MRI prior to treatment showed an ill-defined, heterogeneously enhancing mass measuring about 3.8 × 3.5 × 3.4 cm, crossing the midline and involving the right oropharynx, along with cervical lymph node metastases (Fig. 5Aa). Given the tumor's location and the patient’s strong wish to preserve her larynx, a neoadjuvant approach was chosen. Immunohistochemical analysis of the biopsy revealed a multi-target high-expression profile, including EGFR (high), TROP2 (moderate), B7-H4 (high), and Nectin-4 (high) (Fig. 5Ba-f). As an exploratory proof-of-concept, the patient received three cycles of the EGFR-directed ADC MRG003 (2 mg/kg, 50 kg, q3W) concurrently with stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT, 27.5 Gy in 5 fractions). A follow-up MRI on January 26, 2026 showed a significant tumor regression compared to the baseline, allowing for a reassessment of surgical resection (Fig. 5Ab). The patient then underwent surgery, and postoperative pathology revealed a pathologic complete response (pCR) at both the primary tumor site and level II cervical lymph nodes, with no viable tumor cells seen (Fig. 5Bg). Nonetheless, residual tumor cells were found in a level-III lymph node (Fig. 5Bh). Immunohistochemical analysis of the residual level-III lymph node showed persistent strong EGFR expression (3+), similar to the pre-treatment primary tumor biopsy (Fig. 5B). No reduction in EGFR expression was observed after therapy (Supplementary Figure 3).

TROP2-Targeted ADC (Sac-TMT) in recurrent ACC with skull base involvement

In August 2022, a 27-year-old man visited the outpatient clinic with a one-year history of swelling and pain on the left side of his face. Contrast-enhanced MRI showed a mass in the left buccal area that involved the skull base and extended into the intracranial space, raising suspicion of metastasis to level II cervical lymph nodes (data not shown). The patient underwent a comprehensive surgical procedure, including left craniomaxillofacial radical resection, left suprahyoid neck dissection, and reconstruction using an anterolateral thigh flap. Postoperative pathological examination confirmed a diagnosis of solid-type adenoid cystic carcinoma. Perineural invasion was observed, and metastases in the cervical lymph nodes with extranodal extension were identified. The patient completed adjuvant radiotherapy in November 2022. Subsequently, lenvatinib maintenance therapy (8 mg) was administered from February 2023 to February 2024.

In June 2025, the patient experienced diplopia, and imaging confirmed a local recurrence involving the skull base. He then underwent salvage surgery in August 2025. Pathology of the recurrent skull base tumor confirmed a solid-type ACC recurrence (see Fig. 5Ea). Immunohistochemistry showed strong, diffuse positivity for TROP2, B7-H4, and Nectin-4, with weak EGFR expression and negative P63 staining (Figure Eb-f). Postoperative imaging on September 16, 2025, after salvage surgery, showed residual tumor involving the cavernous sinus and meningeal layers of the temporal lobe (Fig. 5Ca and Da). Due to the tumor's high TROP2 expression and its aggressive, treatment-resistant behavior, the patient was started on the TROP2-targeted ADC Sac-TMT (SKB264, 5 mg/kg, every two weeks) in September 2025 as an exploratory proof-of-concept. He received a total of six cycles, with the final one in January 2026. Grade III skin rashes that occurred were managed appropriately and supportively.

Follow-up MRI in February 2026 showed a significant reduction, indicating a partial response, in the residual lesion compared to the pre-treatment scan from September 16, 2025 (Fig. 5Cb and Db). Throughout the treatment, hepatic, renal, and pancreatic functions stayed within normal limits. Surveillance ultrasonography of the neck and salivary glands revealed no new or enlarging abnormalities or lesions.

Discussion

Adenoid cystic carcinoma typically follows an aggressive yet slow-progressing course, with a well-documented tendency for locoregional recurrence and distant metastasis, which are the primary reasons for treatment failure [23]. The success of ADCs in treating solid tumors has naturally generated significant optimism for similar progress in ACC. Notably, antigen expression plays a crucial role in ADC effectiveness. Our study aimed to assess the expression of five potential TAAs (EGFR, TROP2, B7-H4, Nectin-4, and AXL) in a large cohort of 265 ACC patients, thereby mapping a detailed expression landscape characterized by prevalence, heterogeneity, and a strong correlation with histology, which has important implications for patient selection and the development of targeted therapies for ADCs.

Several prior studies have identified links between ADC targets and histology in ACC, such as TROP2 with non-solid patterns [18] and B7-H4 with the solid subtype [24,25], which aligns with our current findings. Although EGFR [26] and Nectin-4 [27] expression has been observed in ACC, their correlation with histological subtypes and prognosis remains unexplored. In our cohort, moderate-to-high expression of EGFR and TROP2 was highly prevalent (81% and 77%, respectively), and both were predominantly enriched in non-solid ACC. Conversely, B7-H4 and Nectin-4 showed strong association with the solid subtype. High expression of B7-H4 and Nectin-4 was also significantly associated with shorter overall survival. Although the prognostic value of these markers does not directly imply predictive value for ADC response, their high expression still supports their potential as rational therapeutic targets for ADC development, given that sufficient antigen expression is a prerequisite for ADC binding and internalization. The observed variability in TAA expression further supports the potential for innovative strategies such as bispecific ADCs, and provides a foundation for biomarker-driven clinical trial design and personalized treatment selection. Unlike these four targets, AXL expression was generally low to negative and showed no correlation with any clinicopathological features or histological subtypes. This suggests that AXL may have limited utility as a single primary ADC target in most patients with ACC. However, given its established role in drug resistance and tumor adaptation, AXL remains a candidate for combination ADC-based approaches or for targeting specific molecular subsets, rather than serving as a standalone biomarker for patient selection [28].

Another key finding of our study was the relative consistency of target expression between primary and metastatic sites for markers like EGFR, B7-H4, and Nectin-4. This suggests that assessing target expression on a primary or locally recurrent biopsy can serve as a reliable reference for current or future metastatic disease. Regarding TROP2, the rate of high expression was lower in metastatic lesions than in primary tumors (25%vs. 48.9%). However, this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.095). Validation in an independent cohort is therefore required to determine whether this pattern reflects genuine biological heterogeneity, such as clonal evolution or therapy-induced selection pressure, or represents a chance finding.

Currently, >20 ADCs have already been approved, and dozens more are in clinical trials for solid tumors. Among the targets we examined, drugs targeting EGFR (such as MRG003), TROP2 (e.g., Sacituzumab govitecan/datopotamab deruxtecan), and Nectin-4 (e.g., Enfortumab vedotin) are commercially available, while agents like HS-20,089 targeting B7-H4 are in advanced stages of development. In our study, we demonstrate the potential of this biomarker-driven approach through two representative exploratory proof-of-concept cases. To our knowledge, this is the first report of ADCs achieving a complete response (CR) with MRG003 (EGFR-targeted, combined with low-dose SBRT) and a partial response with Sac-TMT (TROP2-targeted) in patients with refractory or recurrent/metastatic ACC. A contemporaneous phase II study of another TROP2-targeted ADC, SHR-A1921, reported only stable disease in ACC [29]. Notably, although Case 1 received a combination of SBRT and an EGFR-directed ADC, we considered that concurrent low-dose SBRT (27.5 Gy in 5 fractions) may primarily play an adjuvant radiosensitizing role. ACC is intrinsically radioresistant, and conventional photon-based radiotherapy provides only modest local control [30]. The delivered dose was well below the established ablative dose thresholds for pulmonary metastases, typically 30–60 Gy in 3–8 fractions, and far below definitive doses used for primary head and neck tumors (≥45 Gy in 5 fractions). Published evidence regarding SBRT in ACC is mostly derived from studies of pulmonary metastases. The largest study [31] reported a 12-month local control rate of only 57.5%, with no clear overall survival advantage over palliative radiotherapy. Given this modest efficacy in metastatic disease, together with the low dose used in our case, SBRT alone would be expected to have minimal direct tumoricidal activity. Furthermore, preclinical studies have shown that subtherapeutic radiation doses can enhance ADC activity through immune-mediated mechanisms and upregulation of target antigens [32]. Therefore, the EGFR-directed ADC was considered the primary contributor to the observed response, with SBRT serving as a sensitizing modality. These findings require validation in prospective clinical trials. In Case 2, the limited tumor shrinkage observed with the TROP2-directed ADC may be due to two main factors: first, the heterogeneity of TROP2 expression within tumors, which our data suggest is even more pronounced in metastatic sites, allowing some TROP2-low or TROP2-negative tumor cells to escape treatment. Second, the tumor's biological aggressiveness or the characteristics of the ADC itself. This initial experience not only proves the feasibility of biomarker-guided therapy in this setting but also provides a rational foundation and preliminary safety and efficacy data for developing future prospective clinical trials of ADC therapies in ACC.

However, several important aspects should be considered when interpreting these promising findings. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) is a semi-quantitative method that does not directly measure absolute antigen density, defined as molecules per cell, or capture the spatial uniformity of expression across the tumor, namely homogeneous tumor distribution, both of which are critical for ADC efficacy. Direct comparison of staining intensities between different antibodies using the same ordinal scale, such as EGFR in Fig. 1C and TROP2 in Fig. 1E, is not valid because antibody affinity, epitope accessibility, and detection protocols may vary. The stronger TROP2 signal is therefore more likely attributable to assay performance than to higher antigen density. Quantitative techniques, such as quantitative immunofluorescence, mass spectrometry-based proteomics, or radioligand binding assays, are required for accurate cross-target comparisons and for establishing objective thresholds for ADC-based patient selection. In addition to antigen expression and density, ADC efficacy is critically influenced by internalization efficiency and intracellular trafficking, linker-payload pharmacology, bystander effects, and normal tissue toxicity [33]. For example, TROP2 internalizes rapidly, whereas some EGFR variants are recycled more slowly [34,35]. Accordingly, the two ADCs used in our clinical cases have distinct pharmacological designs. MRG003, an EGFR-directed ADC, carries a highly potent MMAE payload with a cleavable Val-Cit linker and acts as a tubulin inhibitor [36]. Sac-TMT, a TROP2-directed ADC, employs a less potent camptothecin-based topoisomerase I inhibitor conjugated through a carbonate linker. Both payloads are membrane-permeable, enabling bystander killing of adjacent antigen-negative tumor cells. This property is particularly relevant given the intratumoral heterogeneity shown in Supplementary Figure 2. Toxicity also warrants consideration, as it depends on antigen expression in normal tissues and payload-related off-target effects [37]. TROP2 expression in salivary glands may increase the risk of xerostomia, whereas B7-H4 and Nectin-4 show more restricted normal tissue expression, suggesting a wider therapeutic window. Payload-dependent toxicities, such as myelosuppression and interstitial lung disease, may also occur independently of tumor target expression.

Moreover, our study has several methodological limitations. First, its retrospective design, combined with the enrichment of consultation cases, introduces potential selection bias due to the inherent clinical complexity of the patient population. Second, because of limited tissue availability in consultation archives, comprehensive co-expression analysis of all five targets was conducted on only a subset of 97 cases (36.7% of the total cohort), which may limit the generalizability of the co-expression findings. Third, although the clinical responses observed in this case series are encouraging and represent a valuable initial step, they are derived from a small number of patients and should be considered preliminary, proof-of-concept data. Therefore, these findings require validation in prospective, biomarker-driven clinical trials with larger cohorts before definitive conclusions about ADC efficacy and safety in ACC can be established.

This paper offers a clear and comprehensive analysis of the key ADC targets in ACC. It goes beyond simply reporting prevalence to define a subtype-driven expression pattern: non-solid ACC is enriched in EGFR and TROP2, while solid-type ACC is enriched in B7-H4 and Nectin-4. Early clinical studies further demonstrated that the EGFR-targeted ADC MRG003 and the TROP2-targeted ADC Sac-TMT showed notable efficacy in patients with refractory ACC and high expression of these targets. Therefore, we support a histology-informed, biomarker-driven approach to ADC therapy in ACC, which provides a direct pathway toward personalized treatment for patients.
