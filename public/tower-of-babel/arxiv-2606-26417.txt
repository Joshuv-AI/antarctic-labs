Preservation of primariness under ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -, c 0 c_{0} -, and ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} -sums of Banach spaces
Antonio Acuaviva

Abstract
. We prove transfer principles for the uniform primary factorisation property (UPFP) from a Banach space X X to the vector-valued sequence spaces ℓ 1 ​ ( X ) \ell_{1}(X) , c 0 ​ ( X ) c_{0}(X) and ℓ ∞ ​ ( X ) \ell_{\infty}(X) . The hypotheses are either finite-cotype assumptions on X X or X ∗ X^{*} , or natural self-similarity assumptions on X X . Consequently, under these conditions, the resulting vector-valued sequence spaces are primary. As applications, we recover the primariness of ℓ ∞ ​ ( L p ) \ell_{\infty}(L_{p}) for 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty without using Bourgain’s localisation method, and obtain the primariness of c 0 ​ ( L 1 ) c_{0}(L_{1}) . We also show that ℓ 1 ​ ( Γ , L 1 ​ [ 0 , 1 ] ) \ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1]) has the UPFP for every set Γ \Gamma , and consequently that C ​ [ 0 , 1 ] ∗ C[0,1]^{*} has the UPFP and is primary.

1.
Introduction and organisation

A Banach space is called
primary
if every decomposition of it as a direct sum has one summand already isomorphic to the whole space. Equivalently, whenever
P\colon X\to X
is a projection, at least one of the spaces
PX
and
(I_{X}-P)X
is isomorphic to
X
. The primariness problem for classical Banach spaces has played an important role in the isomorphic theory of Banach spaces, and many of its solutions rely on factorisation methods.

A typical feature of such proofs is that they establish a stronger factorisation dichotomy for arbitrary operators. This is captured by the (uniform) primary factorisation property, abbreviated (U)PFP; see
Section

2
. Roughly speaking, the PFP asks that, for every operator
T\colon X\to X
, the identity on
X
factors through either
T
or
I_{X}-T
, while the UPFP asks for uniform control of the corresponding factorisation constants. Thus (U)PFP is a natural property to explore in the study of primariness, and is often the more flexible property in permanence arguments. This point of view was used explicitly in the recent work of the present author and Kania on primariness of uncountable
\ell_{p}
-sums
[
3
]
; we refer to that article for a more detailed discussion of primariness and the PFP.

In this paper, we prove transfer principles showing that, under finite-cotype assumptions on
X
or
X^{*}
, or under natural self-similarity assumptions on
X
, the UPFP passes from
X
to the vector-valued sequence spaces
\ell_{1}(X)
,
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
. The proofs are surprisingly elementary and recover, by a unified method, several known primariness results for spaces of the form
\ell_{\infty}(X)
. Our first main results is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with the UPFP. Suppose that at least one of the following holds:

(i)

The space
X
is self-similar, in the sense that
X\simeq\ell_{p}(X)
for some
1\leq p\leq\infty
, or
X\simeq c_{0}(X)
.

(ii)

The dual space
X^{*}
has finite cotype.

Then
\ell_{1}(X)
has the UPFP. In particular,
\ell_{1}(X)
is primary.

We also obtain a similar conclusion in the case of
c_{0}
and
\ell_{\infty}
-sums.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with the UPFP. Suppose that at least one of the following holds:

(i)

The space
X
is self-similar, in the sense that
X\simeq\ell_{p}(X)
for some
1\leq p<\infty
.

(ii)

The space
X
has finite cotype.

Then both
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
have the UPFP. In particular,
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
are primary.

As an immediate consequence, we recover the primariness of
\ell_{\infty}(L_{p})
for
1\leq p<\infty
by elementary methods. The original proofs of Wark
[
16
]
for
1<p<\infty
and Müller
[
14
]
for the full range, rely on substantially more technical methods, in particular Bourgain’s localisation method. We also record the case of
c_{0}(L_{1})
, which we could not find explicitly stated in the literature, although it is presumably known.

Corollary 1.3
.

The spaces
\ell_{\infty}(L_{p})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
c_{0}(L_{1})
have the UPFP and thus are primary.

Proof.

By
Theorem

1.2
, it is enough to note that
L_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, has the UPFP and satisfies the self-similarity condition
L_{p}\simeq\ell_{p}(L_{p})
. For
1<p<\infty
, the UPFP can be extracted from the primariness argument of Alspach, Enflo and Odell
[
5
]
. For
p=1
, the corresponding quantitative statement can be extracted from Capon’s argument
[
6
]
, see
Section

6
. It is plausible that a quantitative statement of this kind can also be extracted from Enflo’s proof, via Maurey’s exposition, of the primariness of
L_{p}

[
13
]
, although we have not verified this. Therefore
Theorem

1.2
applies and gives the UPFP. Primariness follows directly from the UPFP together with Pełczyński’s decomposition method.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

Lechner and Speckhofer recently proved a much broader factorisation theorem for Haar system Hardy spaces
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
. In particular, their result also implies the UPFP for
L_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and for
\ell_{\infty}(L_{p})
when
1<p<\infty
. The point of the corollary above is that, once the UPFP of a Banach space X is known, the passage to
\ell_{\infty}(X)
follows from the elementary triangular-reduction argument developed here.

We also record a consequence for spaces of continuous functions on ordinal intervals. Combining our
\ell_{1}
-sum transfer theorem with the known factorisation properties of
C(\alpha)
, we obtain the remaining
\ell_{1}
-case in the classification of primariness of the spaces
\ell_{p}(C(\alpha))
begun in
[
2
]
.

Corollary 1.5
.

For every ordinal
\alpha
, the space
\ell_{1}(C(\alpha))
has the UPFP. In particular,
\ell_{1}(C(\alpha))
is primary.

Proof.

Suppose first that
C(\alpha)
is not isomorphic to
C(\xi\cdot n)
for any uncountable regular ordinal
\xi
and any integer
n\geq 2
. By the Alspach–Benyamini theorem
[
4
]
, in the form stated in
[
1
, Proposition 1.9]
, the space
C(\alpha)
has the PFP; observing that the estimates in the proof are quantitative gives the UPFP. Since the ordinal interval
[0,\alpha]
is scattered, we have
C(\alpha)^{*}\simeq\ell_{1}([0,\alpha])
.
In particular,
C(\alpha)^{*}
has finite cotype and therefore
Theorem

1.1
implies that
\ell_{1}(C(\alpha))
has the UPFP.

Suppose now that
C(\alpha)\simeq C(\xi\cdot n)
for some uncountable regular ordinal
\xi
and some integer
n\geq 2
. Since
C(\xi\cdot n)
is isomorphic to the direct sum of
n
copies of
C(\xi)
, we have
\ell_{1}(C(\alpha))\simeq\ell_{1}(C(\xi))
. By the first part of the proof, applied to
\xi
, and by the isomorphic invariance of the UPFP,
\ell_{1}(C(\alpha))
has the UPFP. The primariness follows from the UPFP together with Pełczyński’s decomposition method, since
\ell_{1}(C(\alpha))\simeq\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}(C(\alpha)))
.
∎

Finally, although the transfer principles in
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
apply only under the stated abstract hypotheses on the summand
X
, the diagonal-reduction method can sometimes be implemented beyond those hypotheses by using additional structure of
X
. We illustrate this phenomenon for the summand
L_{1}[0,1]
, obtaining the following result. It generalises a previous result of the author and Kania
[
3
]
, which assumed the negation of
\mathrm{CH}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

For every set
\Gamma
, the space
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
has the UPFP. Consequently,
C[0,1]^{*}
has the UPFP and, in particular, is primary.

1.1.
Organisation and proof strategy

In
Section

2
, we introduce the notation and basic facts used throughout the paper, including the matrix representation of operators on vector-valued sequence spaces. Once this framework is in place, the proofs of
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
are fairly straightforward, we present them in
Section

5
. The main idea is to reduce, up to a small perturbation, an operator
T\colon E(X)\to E(X)
to diagonal form. We then use the UPFP of
X
to factor the identity through either the resulting diagonal operator
D
or through
I_{E(X)}-D
; see
Section

5.2
. Finally, we use the fact that factorisation of the identity is stable under sufficiently small perturbations; see
Section

5.2
.

The diagonal reduction is obtained in two steps: an upper-triangular reduction, proved in
Section

3
, and a lower-triangular reduction, proved in
Section

4
. There are two types of hypotheses. In the finite-cotype case, the relevant finite-dimensional geometry is sufficiently far from that of the scalar sequence spaces involved: finite cotype of
X^{*}
is used for
\ell_{1}(X)
, while finite cotype of
X
is used for
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
. In the self-similar case, the reductions are obtained by contrasting incompatible sequence-space geometries: for
\ell_{1}
-sums, the internal
\ell_{p}
,
1<p\leq\infty
, or
c_{0}
structure is contrasted with the external
\ell_{1}
structure, while for
c_{0}
- and
\ell_{\infty}
-sums, the internal
\ell_{p}
structure is contrasted with the external
\ell_{\infty}
norm structure.

In
Section

6
, we apply a variant of the same diagonal method to uncountable
\ell_{1}
-sums with summand
L_{1}[0,1]
. The additional ingredients are a finite-coordinate localisation lemma for operators into
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
and a finite free-set theorem of Hajnal. This gives the UPFP for
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
for every set
\Gamma
, and hence the UPFP and primariness of
C[0,1]^{*}
.

Finally,
Section

7
contains some additional remarks concerning the relationship between the PFP and UPFP of
X
and those of
\ell_{p}(X)
,
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
, together with some open questions.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

We use standard notation and conventions, unless explicitly stated otherwise. The scalar field is denoted by
\mathbb{K}
and may be either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. By an
operator
we mean a bounded linear map between Banach spaces. If
X
is a Banach space, then
I_{X}\colon X\to X
denotes the identity operator on
X
,
B_{X}
denotes its closed unit ball, and
\mathscr{B}(X)
denotes the space of operators on
X
. If
X
has cotype
q
, we write
C_{q}(X)
for its cotype
q
constant. Further notation will be introduced as needed. We shall use the following terminology.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T\colon X\to Y
and
S\colon Z\to W
be operators between Banach spaces. We say that
S

factors through

T
with constant
C
if there are operators
V\colon Z\to X
and
U\colon Y\to W
such that

UTV=S\quad\text{and}\quad\|U\|\|V\|\leq C.

If such a constant
C\geq 1
exists, we simply say that
S
factors through
T
.

The uniform primary factorisation property will play a central role throughout the paper. We recall its definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space.

(a)

We say that
X
has the
primary factorisation property
(PFP) if, for every operator
T\colon X\to X
, the identity operator
I_{X}
factors through either
T
or
I_{X}-T
.

(b)

Let
C\geq 1
. We say that
X
has the
C
-primary factorisation property
(
C
-PFP) if, for every operator
T\colon X\to X
, the identity operator
I_{X}
factors through either
T
or
I_{X}-T
with constant
C
.

(c)

We say that
X
has the
uniform primary factorisation property
(UPFP) if
X
has the
C
-PFP for some
C\geq 1
.

Throughout the paper, we shall use without further comment that the UPFP is invariant under isomorphisms; see, for instance,
[
3
, Proposition 2.5]
.

2.1.
Vector-valued sequence spaces

Given a Banach space
X
, we shall often consider vector-valued sequence spaces. Let
E
be a Banach space with a fixed normalized
1
-unconditional basis
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We define
E(X)
to be the Banach space of all sequences
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}\|e_{n}

norm converges in
E
, equipped with the norm

\|x\|=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}\|e_{n}\right\|.

This construction depends on the chosen unconditional basis of
E
, which will be fixed throughout and left implicit in the notation.

For
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in E(X)
, we define the
support
of
x
by

\supp(x)=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:x_{n}\neq 0\}.

If
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we set

E(M,X)=\{x\in E(X):\supp(x)\subseteq M\}.

We denote by

J_{M}\colon E(M,X)\to E(X)\quad\text{and}\quad P_{M}\colon E(X)\to E(M,X)

the canonical inclusion and projection, respectively. Thus
P_{M}J_{M}=I_{E(M,X)}
. If
M=\{n\}
is a singleton, we write
J_{n}
and
P_{n}
in place of
J_{\{n\}}
and
P_{\{n\}}
; under the natural identification of
E(\{n\},X)
with
X
, these are precisely the canonical coordinate embedding and projection.

If
E
and
F
are Banach spaces with normalized
1
-unconditional bases, and
T\colon E(X)\to F(Y)
is an operator, then for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
we set

T_{n,m}=P_{n}TJ_{m}\in\mathscr{B}(X,Y).

We call
(T_{n,m})_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
the
matrix of
of
T
. Since the finitely supported vectors are dense in
E(X)
, this matrix determines
T
. More precisely, for every
x=(x_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in E(X)
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

P_{n}Tx=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}T_{n,m}x_{m},

where the series converges in
Y
. If
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a uniformly bounded sequence in
\mathscr{B}(X)
, then the formula

\diag(T_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=(T_{n}x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

defines an operator on
E(X)
, called a
diagonal operator
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
T\colon E(X)\to E(X)
be an operator with matrix of
(T_{n,m})_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
. We define the
diagonal part
of
T
to be the diagonal operator
D\colon E(X)\to E(X)
given by

D(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=(T_{n,n}x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

Equivalently,
D=\diag(T_{n,n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
.

Observe that since
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|T_{n,n}\|\leq\|T\|
, the diagonal part is indeed always an operator.

Let
c_{00}(X)
denote the subspace of
E(X)
consisting of finitely supported vectors.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
T\colon E(X)\to E(X)
be an operator with matrix of
(T_{n,m})_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
. We define the
upper triangular part
and the
lower triangular part
of
T
to be the linear maps initially defined on
c_{00}(X)
by the matrices

U_{n,m}=\begin{cases}T_{n,m},&n\leq m,\\
0,&n>m,\end{cases}\qquad L_{n,m}=\begin{cases}T_{n,m},&n\geq m,\\
0,&n<m.\end{cases}

These maps are well defined on
c_{00}(X)
and take values in
E(X)
. They need not, in general, extend to operators on
E(X)
. Since
c_{00}(X)
is dense in
E(X)
, when one of them is bounded on
c_{00}(X)
, we use the same notation for its unique bounded extension to an operator on
E(X)
; in that case the other one also extends boundedly, since on
c_{00}(X)
we have

T=U+L-D,

where
D
denotes the diagonal part of
T
. Analogous considerations apply to the strictly upper and strictly lower triangular parts, obtained by keeping only the entries with
n<m
and
n>m
, respectively.

The same terminology applies to operators on
E(M,X)
, with the order inherited from
\mathbb{N}
. Thus, if
M=\{m_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is written in increasing order, the upper triangular part keeps only the entries
P_{m_{i}}TJ_{m_{j}}
with
i\leq j
, while the lower triangular part keeps only those with
i\geq j
.

Remark 2.5
.

Throughout the paper, we leave the extension from
c_{00}(X)
to
E(X)
implicit and focus on the only problematic point, namely the boundedness of the corresponding triangular truncation. Equivalently, one may first regard these truncations as coordinatewise defined linear maps from
E(X)
into
\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X
. In each case where such a truncation is used, the estimates show that it actually takes values in
E(X)
and, in fact, defines a bounded operator on
E(X)
.

We shall also use the notation
\ell_{\infty}(X)
in the usual sense, with norm

\|(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(X)}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|x_{n}\|.

For
\ell_{\infty}(X)
we keep the notation
P_{n},J_{n},P_{M},J_{M}
and
T_{n,m}=P_{n}TJ_{m}
. However, one must be more careful: the matrix
(T_{n,m})_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
does not, in general, determine the operator
T\in\mathscr{B}(\ell_{\infty}(X))
; see
[
2
, Example 2.3]
for an easy example.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
T\colon\ell_{\infty}(X)\to\ell_{\infty}(X)
be an operator with operator matrix
(T_{n,m})_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
. We say that
T

acts according to its matrix
if, for every
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{\infty}(X)
and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{m,n}x_{n}

norm converges in
X
and

P_{m}Tx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{m,n}x_{n}.

The same coordinatewise definition of diagonal operators will be used on
\ell_{\infty}(X)
. Thus, if
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a uniformly bounded sequence in
\mathscr{B}(X)
, then

\diag(T_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=(T_{n}x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

defines an operator on
\ell_{\infty}(X)
, which acts according to its matrix.

We have a similar notion of diagonal and lower triangular parts for operators on
\ell_{\infty}(X)
. In this case these parts are genuine operators and act according to their matrices in the sense just defined.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
T\colon\ell_{\infty}(X)\to\ell_{\infty}(X)
be an operator, and let
(T_{n,m})_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
be its matrix of. We define the
diagonal part

D
of
T
to be the operator
D\colon\ell_{\infty}(X)\to\ell_{\infty}(X)
defined, for each
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{\infty}(X)
and each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, by

P_{m}Dx=T_{m,m}x_{m}.

Similarly, we define the
lower triangular part

L
of
T
to be the operator
L\colon\ell_{\infty}(X)\to\ell_{\infty}(X)
defined, for each
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{\infty}(X)
and each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, by

P_{m}Lx=\sum_{n=1}^{m}T_{m,n}x_{n}.

Indeed, these formulae define operators, with
\|D\|\leq\|T\|
and
\|L\|\leq\|T\|
. For the latter, observe that, for every
x\in\ell_{\infty}(X)
and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

P_{m}Lx=P_{m}TJ_{\{1,\ldots,m\}}P_{\{1,\ldots,m\}}x.

Moreover, both
D
and
L
act according to their matrices in the sense defined above. We shall not use the upper triangular part of an arbitrary operator on
\ell_{\infty}(X)
.

2.2.
Iterated vector-valued sequence spaces

When working with iterated sequence spaces
\ell_{1}(E(X))
or
E(\ell_{p}(X))
, we distinguish the outer and inner coordinates. The coordinate projections and embeddings associated with the outer sequence space will be denoted by

\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{P}_{M},\widehat{J}_{n},\widehat{J}_{M},

whereas the unadorned symbols
P_{n},P_{M},J_{n},J_{M}
refer to the inner sequence space. Thus, for example, if
T\colon\ell_{1}(E(X))\to\ell_{1}(E(X))
, then
\widehat{P}_{m}T\widehat{J}_{n}
is an operator from
E(X)
into
E(X)
.

Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces belonging to the family
\{c_{0}\}\cup\{\ell_{p}:1\leq p\leq\infty\}
. If
\mathbf{n}=(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
are increasing sequences of positive integers, we define

J_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}\colon F(X)\to F(E(X))

as follows. Given
x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in F(X)
, the vector
J_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}x
has outer coordinate
J_{m_{j}}x_{j}
in position
n_{j}
, and zero in all other outer coordinates. That is,

\widehat{P}_{n_{j}}J_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}x=J_{m_{j}}x_{j}

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, while
\widehat{P}_{n}J_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}x=0
whenever
n\notin\{n_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

We also define

P_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}\colon F(E(X))\to F(X)

by extracting the corresponding coordinates. Thus, if
y=(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in F(E(X))
, then

P_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}y=(P_{m_{j}}y_{n_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}.

Equivalently,

P_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}y=(P_{m_{j}}\widehat{P}_{n_{j}}y)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}.

3.
Upper triangular reduction

In this section we prove the upper-triangular reductions needed later for the diagonal reductions. The general mechanism is the same throughout: first one proves a forward reduction lemma, which allows us, after passing to suitable infinite subsets, to choose a coordinate whose image has arbitrarily small projection onto future coordinates; then a standard recursive selection turns these one-step estimates into an upper-triangular form.

We begin with the case of
\ell_{1}(X)
. The finite dual-cotype case is recorded in
Section

3.1.1
, while the self-similar case is obtained from the forward reduction in
Section

3.1.2
and the inductive construction in
Section

3.1.2
. We then treat the
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
cases: finite cotype is handled in
Section

3.2.1
and
Section

3.2.1
, and self-similarity in
Section

3.2.2
and
Section

3.2.2
.

3.1.
The
\ell_{1}
case

3.1.1.
Finite cotype of the dual space

We first consider operators on
\ell_{1}(X)
under the assumption that
X^{*}
has finite cotype. The required upper-triangular reduction follows from the same compactness mechanism used in
[
2
]
; for completeness, we record the precise form needed here.

Proposition 3.1

(Reduction to upper-triangular operators from dual cotype of the dual space)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{*}
has finite cotype. Then, for every operator
T\colon\ell_{1}(X)\to\ell_{1}(X)
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an infinite subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\|P_{M}TJ_{M}-U\|<\varepsilon,

where
U\colon\ell_{1}(M,X)\to\ell_{1}(M,X)
is the upper triangular part of
P_{M}TJ_{M}
.

Proof.

Since
X^{*}
has finite cotype, it contains no copy of
c_{0}
. Hence, by a classical theorem of Pełczyński
[
15
]
, every operator from
\ell_{\infty}=C(\beta\mathbb{N})
into
X^{*}
is weakly compact. Therefore
S^{*}\colon\ell_{\infty}\to X^{*}
is weakly compact for any
S\colon X\to\ell_{1}
. By Gantmacher’s theorem,
S
is weakly compact. Since
\ell_{1}
has the Schur property, weakly compact subsets of
\ell_{1}
are norm compact. Hence
S
is compact. The result is now a particular instance of
[
2
, Proposition 3.5]
.
∎

3.1.2.
Self-similarity of the space

Now we move to the case when
X\simeq\ell_{p}(X)
for some
1<p\leq\infty
or
X\simeq c_{0}(X)
. Before we can prove the upper-triangular reduction, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(Forward reduction from self-similarity)
.

Let
E=\ell_{p}
for
1<p\leq\infty
or
E=c_{0}
,
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and
T\colon E(X)\to\ell_{1}(Y)
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a pair of infinite sets
A,M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\|P_{M}TJ_{A}\|<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We first do the case
E=\ell_{p}
for
1<p<\infty
. Proceed by contradiction and suppose that the statement is false. Then there is
\varepsilon>0
such that, for every pair of infinite sets
A,M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we have
\|P_{M}TJ_{A}\|\geq\varepsilon
. Choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N^{(p-1)/p}\varepsilon>2\|T\|
, and partition
\mathbb{N}
into infinite pairwise disjoint sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{N}
and
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
respectively.

By assumption, for each
n=1,\dots,N
we have
\|P_{M_{n}}TJ_{A_{n}}\|\geq\varepsilon
. Hence, we may choose
x_{n}\in B_{\ell_{p}(A_{n},X)}
and
y^{*}_{n}\in B_{\ell_{1}(M_{n},Y)^{*}}
such that

|y^{*}_{n}P_{M_{n}}TJ_{A_{n}}x_{n}|>\varepsilon/2.

For each choice of signs
\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N})\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, set

x_{\theta}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}J_{A_{n}}x_{n}.

Since the vectors
J_{A_{n}}x_{n}
have disjoint supports in
\ell_{p}(X)
, we have
\|x_{\theta}\|\leq N^{1/p}
. Thus
\|Tx_{\theta}\|\leq\|T\|N^{1/p}
for every choice of signs, and averaging gives

\|T\|N^{1/p}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Tx_{\theta}\|].

Since the sets
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
form a partition of
\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Tx_{\theta}\|]=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|P_{M_{n}}Tx_{\theta}\|]\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}y_{n}^{*}P_{M_{n}}Tx_{\theta}]|.

For each fixed
n
, since
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}\theta_{m}]=0
if
n\not=m
and
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}^{2}]=1
, we have

|\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}y_{n}^{*}P_{M_{n}}Tx_{\theta}]|=|y^{*}_{n}P_{M_{n}}TJ_{A_{n}}x_{n}|>\varepsilon/2.

Therefore

\|T\|N^{1/p}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Tx_{\theta}\|]>N\varepsilon/2,

contradicting the choice of
N
. This contradiction proves the result.

The proofs for the cases
E=\ell_{\infty}
and
E=c_{0}
are identical, now choosing
N
so that
N\varepsilon>2\|T\|
and observing that
\|x_{\theta}\|\leq 1
in this case.
∎

We are now ready to prove an upper-triangular reduction in the case of
\ell_{p}
or
c_{0}
self-similarity.

Proposition 3.3

(Reduction to upper-triangular operators from self-similarity)
.

Let
E=\ell_{p}
for
1<p\leq\infty
or
E=c_{0}
,
X
be a Banach space and
T\colon\ell_{1}(E(X))\to\ell_{1}(E(X))
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist an increasing sequence
\mathbf{n}=(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and infinite sets
(A_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
so that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\|\widehat{P}_{M_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{j+1},

where
M_{j}=\{n_{i}:i>j\}
.

Proof.

We recursively construct an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and infinite subsets
(A_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\widetilde{M}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

n_{j}\in\widetilde{M}_{j-1},\qquad\widetilde{M}_{j}\subseteq\widetilde{M}_{j-1}\cap\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n>n_{j}\},

and

\|\widehat{P}_{\widetilde{M}_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{j+1},

where
\widetilde{M}_{0}=\mathbb{N}
.

Suppose that the construction has been carried out to stage
j-1
, in particular
\widetilde{M}_{j-1}
has been constructed. Choose
n_{j}\in\widetilde{M}_{j-1}
, and set

L_{j}=\widetilde{M}_{j-1}\cap\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n>n_{j}\}.

Then
L_{j}
is infinite. Applying
Section

3.1.2
to the operator

\widehat{P}_{L_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}\colon E(X)\to\ell_{1}(L_{j},E(X)),

after identifying
\ell_{1}(L_{j},E(X))
with
\ell_{1}(E(X))
, we obtain infinite sets
A_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
\widetilde{M}_{j}\subseteq L_{j}
such that

\|\widehat{P}_{\widetilde{M}_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{j+1}.

This completes the recursive construction.

By construction,
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is strictly increasing. Moreover, if

M_{j}=\{n_{i}:i>j\},

then
M_{j}\subseteq\widetilde{M}_{j}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence

\widehat{P}_{M_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}}=\widehat{P}_{M_{j}}\widehat{P}_{\widetilde{M}_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}},

and therefore

\|\widehat{P}_{M_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}}\|\leq\|\widehat{P}_{\widetilde{M}_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{j+1}.

This proves the result.
∎

3.2.
The
\ell_{\infty}
and
c_{0}
cases

3.2.1.
Finite cotype

We consider now the upper-triangular reduction in the case of
c_{0}
and
\ell_{\infty}
sums, we start with the case of finite cotype. We shall need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4

(Forward reduction from cotype)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with cotype
q<\infty
,
E
denote either
c_{0}
or
\ell_{\infty}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
T\colon\ell_{\infty}^{N}(X)\to E(X)
be an operator. Denote by
C_{q}(X)
the cotype constant of X and suppose that

N^{1/q}\varepsilon>2C_{q}(X)\|T\|.

Then there exist
1\leq n\leq N
and an infinite subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\|P_{m}TJ_{n}\|<\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }m\in M.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction and assume that the statement is false. Then, for every
1\leq n\leq N
, the set

\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\|P_{m}TJ_{n}\|<\varepsilon\}

is finite. Hence we may choose
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
1\leq n\leq N
we have
\|P_{m}TJ_{n}\|\geq\varepsilon
. For each
n
, choose
x_{n}\in B_{X}
such that

\|P_{m}TJ_{n}x_{n}\|>\varepsilon/2.

For each choice of signs
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N})\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, put

x_{\theta}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}J_{n}x_{n}.

Naturally
\|x_{\theta}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{N}(X)}\leq 1
so that

\|T\|^{q}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Tx_{\theta}\|^{q}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|P_{m}Tx_{\theta}\|^{q}].

Using the cotype
q
estimate for
X
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|P_{m}Tx_{\theta}\|^{q}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}P_{m}TJ_{n}x_{n}\right\|^{q}\right]\geq C_{q}(X)^{-q}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|P_{m}TJ_{n}x_{n}\|^{q}

\displaystyle>C_{q}(X)^{-q}N(\varepsilon/2)^{q}.

Thus

N^{1/q}\varepsilon<2C_{q}(X)\|T\|,

contradicting the hypothesis. This contradiction proves the result.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The point of the previous lemma is not merely the coordinatewise conclusion, but the fact that for
E=c_{0}
or
E=\ell_{\infty}
one has

\|P_{M}TJ_{n}\|=\sup_{m\in M}\|P_{m}TJ_{n}\|.

Thus, coordinatewise smallness on
M
gives smallness of
P_{M}TJ_{n}
. Similar coordinatewise forward reductions can be proved for other
\ell_{p}
-sums, but they do not by themselves give the required estimate for
P_{M}TJ_{n}
, since the target norm is no longer a supremum norm.

We can now give the upper triangular reduction in the case of finite cotype.

Proposition 3.6

(Reduction to upper-triangular operators from cotype)
.

Let
E=c_{0}
or
E=\ell_{\infty}
,
X
be a Banach space with finite cotype, and
T\colon E(X)\to E(X)
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an infinite subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
m\in M
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<m\\
n\in M\end{subarray}}\|P_{m}TJ_{n}\|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
q<\infty
be such that
X
has cotype
q
, and let
C_{q}(X)
denote the cotype
q
constant of
X
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, choose
N_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

N_{j}^{1/q}\varepsilon/2^{j+1}>2C_{q}(X)\|T\|.

Using
Section

3.2.1
, we recursively construct an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and a decreasing sequence
(M_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
, with
M_{0}=\mathbb{N}
, such that
n_{j}\in M_{j-1}
,
M_{j}\subseteq M_{j-1}\cap\{m\in\mathbb{N}:m>n_{j}\}
, and for all
m\in M_{j}
we have

\|P_{m}TJ_{n_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{j+1}.

Suppose that
M_{j-1}
has been constructed. Choose distinct elements
a_{1}<\ldots<a_{N_{j}}
of
M_{j-1}
, and set

L_{j}=M_{j-1}\cap\{m\in\mathbb{N}:m>a_{N_{j}}\}.

Put
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N_{j}}\}
, and define
J_{A}\colon\ell_{\infty}^{N_{j}}(X)\to E(X)
by

J_{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N_{j}})=\sum_{s=1}^{N_{j}}J_{a_{s}}x_{s}.

Apply
Section

3.2.1
to the operator

P_{L_{j}}TJ_{A}\colon\ell_{\infty}^{N_{j}}(X)\to E(L_{j},X),

where we identify
E(L_{j},X)
with
E(X)
. Since
\|P_{L_{j}}TJ_{A}\|\leq\|T\|
, we obtain
1\leq s_{j}\leq N_{j}
and an infinite set
M_{j}\subseteq L_{j}
such that, for all
m\in M_{j}
, we have

\|P_{m}TJ_{a_{s_{j}}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{j+1}.

Set
n_{j}=a_{s_{j}}
. This completes the recursive construction.

Let
M=\{n_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
. If
i<j
, then
n_{j}\in M_{i}
, and therefore

\|P_{n_{j}}TJ_{n_{i}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+1}.

Hence

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<n_{j}\\
n\in M\end{subarray}}\|P_{n_{j}}TJ_{n}\|=\sum_{i<j}\|P_{n_{j}}TJ_{n_{i}}\|<\sum_{i<j}\varepsilon/2^{i+1}<\varepsilon.

Since
j\in\mathbb{N}
was arbitrary, the proof is complete.
∎

The conclusion of
Section

3.2.1
should be compared with the upper-triangular reduction obtained for
\ell_{1}(X)
in
Section

3.1.1
. There, one obtains norm approximation of a suitable compression by its upper triangular part. In the present setting, and especially for
\ell_{\infty}(X)
, matrix representations require more care; for this reason we only record the weaker entry-wise estimate above. This is the form of upper-triangular reduction that will be needed.

3.2.2.
Self-similarity of the space

We now argue that we can get a similar upper-triangular reduction in the case of
\ell_{p}
-self-similarity. We need the following preliminary result.

Lemma 3.7

(Forward reduction from self-similarity)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
E=c_{0}
or
E=\ell_{\infty}
,
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces, and
T\colon\ell_{\infty}^{N}(Y)\to E(\ell_{p}(X))
be an operator. Suppose that

N^{1/p}\varepsilon>2\|T\|.

Then there exist
1\leq n\leq N
, an infinite set
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, and, for each
m\in M
, an infinite set
A_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\|P_{A_{m}}\widehat{P}_{m}TJ_{n}\|<\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }m\in M.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction and suppose that the conclusion fails. For each
1\leq n\leq N
, set

M(n)=\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\|P_{A}\widehat{P}_{m}TJ_{n}\|<\varepsilon\text{ for some infinite }A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\}.

By assumption each
M(n)
is finite so that we may choose
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}M(n)
. By the choice of
m
, for every
1\leq n\leq N
and every infinite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
we have

\|P_{A}\widehat{P}_{m}TJ_{n}\|\geq\varepsilon.

Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{N}
be a partition of
\mathbb{N}
into infinite sets. For each
1\leq n\leq N
, choose
y_{n}\in B_{Y}
and
x_{n}^{*}\in B_{\ell_{p}(B_{n},X)^{*}}
such that

|x_{n}^{*}P_{B_{n}}\widehat{P}_{m}TJ_{n}y_{n}|>\varepsilon/2.

For each choice of signs
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N})\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, put

y_{\theta}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}J_{n}y_{n}.

Since the domain is
\ell_{\infty}^{N}(Y)
, we have
\|y_{\theta}\|\leq 1
. Therefore

\|T\|^{p}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|\widehat{P}_{m}Ty_{\theta}\|_{\ell_{p}(X)}^{p}].

Since the sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{N}
are disjoint, we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|\widehat{P}_{m}Ty_{\theta}\|_{\ell_{p}(X)}^{p}]\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|P_{B_{n}}\widehat{P}_{m}Ty_{\theta}\|^{p}]\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[|x_{n}^{*}P_{B_{n}}\widehat{P}_{m}Ty_{\theta}|^{p}].

By Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[|x_{n}^{*}P_{B_{n}}\widehat{P}_{m}Ty_{\theta}|^{p}]\geq\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}x_{n}^{*}P_{B_{n}}\widehat{P}_{m}Ty_{\theta}]\right|^{p}.

Since
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}\theta_{r}]=0
whenever
n\neq r
and
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}^{2}]=1
, we get

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}x_{n}^{*}P_{B_{n}}\widehat{P}_{m}Ty_{\theta}]\right|=|x_{n}^{*}P_{B_{n}}\widehat{P}_{m}TJ_{n}y_{n}|>\varepsilon/2.

Hence

\|T\|^{p}>N(\varepsilon/2)^{p},

contradicting the hypothesis.
∎

Using this, we can give the desired upper triangular reduction.

Proposition 3.8

(Reduction to upper-triangular operators from self-similarity)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
E=c_{0}
or
E=\ell_{\infty}
,
X
be a Banach space, and
T\colon E(\ell_{p}(X))\to E(\ell_{p}(X))
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is an infinite set
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and, for each
m\in M
, an infinite set
A_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<m\\
n\in M\end{subarray}}\|P_{A_{m}}\widehat{P}_{m}T\widehat{J}_{n}\|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, choose
N_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

N_{j}^{1/p}\varepsilon/2^{j+1}>2\|T\|.

Using
Section

3.2.2
, we recursively construct an increasing sequence
(m_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, a decreasing sequence
(M_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
, with
M_{0}=\mathbb{N}
, and, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
and each
m\in M_{j}
, an infinite set
B_{m}^{j}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. The construction will ensure that
m_{j}\in M_{j-1}
,
M_{j}\subseteq M_{j-1}\cap\{m\in\mathbb{N}:m>m_{j}\}
, and, whenever
1\leq i\leq j
and
m\in M_{j}
, we have

\|P_{B_{m}^{j}}\widehat{P}_{m}T\widehat{J}_{m_{i}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+1}.

Set
B_{m}^{0}=\mathbb{N}
for every
m\in M_{0}
.

Suppose that the construction has been carried out up to stage
j-1
. Choose distinct elements
a_{1}<\ldots<a_{N_{j}}
of
M_{j-1}
, let
F=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N_{j}}\}
and define

\widehat{J}_{F}\colon\ell_{\infty}^{N_{j}}(\ell_{p}(X))\to E(\ell_{p}(X))

by

\widehat{J}_{F}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N_{j}})=\sum_{s=1}^{N_{j}}\widehat{J}_{a_{s}}x_{s}.

Put

L_{j}=M_{j-1}\cap\{m\in\mathbb{N}:m>a_{N_{j}}\}.

After identifying each
\ell_{p}(B_{m}^{j-1},X)
with
\ell_{p}(X)
, define
R_{j}\colon\ell_{\infty}^{N_{j}}(\ell_{p}(X))\to E(L_{j},\ell_{p}(X))
coordinate-wise by

\widehat{P}_{m}R_{j}x=P_{B_{m}^{j-1}}\widehat{P}_{m}T\widehat{J}_{F}x\qquad\text{for }m\in L_{j}.

Then
\|R_{j}\|\leq\|T\|
. Applying
Section

3.2.2
to
R_{j}
, after identifying
E(L_{j},\ell_{p}(X))
with
E(\ell_{p}(X))
and translating the outer coordinates and inner coordinate sets back through the previous identifications, we obtain
1\leq s_{j}\leq N_{j}
, an infinite set
M_{j}\subseteq L_{j}
, and, for each
m\in M_{j}
, an infinite set
B_{m}^{j}\subseteq B_{m}^{j-1}
such that

\|P_{B_{m}^{j}}\widehat{P}_{m}T\widehat{J}_{a_{s_{j}}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{j+1}.

Set
m_{j}=a_{s_{j}}
. Since
B_{m}^{j}\subseteq B_{m}^{j-1}
, all previous estimates are preserved. This completes the recursive construction.

Let
M=\{m_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
and for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, define
A_{m_{j}}=B_{m_{j}}^{j-1}
.

If
i<j
, then
m_{j}\in M_{i}
, and since
A_{m_{j}}\subseteq B_{m_{j}}^{i}
, the estimate constructed at stage
i
gives

\|P_{A_{m_{j}}}\widehat{P}_{m_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{m_{i}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+1}.

Therefore, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<m_{j}\\
n\in M\end{subarray}}\|P_{A_{m_{j}}}\widehat{P}_{m_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{n}\|=\sum_{i<j}\|P_{A_{m_{j}}}\widehat{P}_{m_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{m_{i}}\|<\sum_{i<j}\varepsilon/2^{i+1}<\varepsilon.

This proves the result.
∎

4.
Lower triangular reduction

In this section we prove the corresponding lower-triangular reductions. The general mechanism is again based on a one-step reduction: a backward reduction lemma allows us, after passing to suitable infinite subsets, to choose a coordinate whose image has arbitrarily small projection onto prescribed earlier coordinates. A standard recursive selection then turns these estimates into a lower-triangular form.

We begin with the case of
\ell_{1}(X)
. The finite cotype of the dual space case is obtained from
Section

4.1.1
and
Section

4.1.1
, while the self-similar case is obtained from
Section

4.1.2
and
Section

4.1.2
. We then treat the
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
cases: finite cotype is handled in
Section

4.2.1
and
Section

4.2.1
, and self-similarity in
Section

4.2.2
and
Section

4.2.2
.

4.1.
The
\ell_{1}
case

4.1.1.
Finite cotype of the dual space

We begin by considering the operators on
\ell_{1}(X)
, again under the assumption that
X^{*}
has finite cotype. The following lemma is the adjoint counterpart of the finite cotype estimate used in the proof of
Section

3.2.1
.

Lemma 4.1

(Backward reduction from dual cotype)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces, suppose that
X^{*}
has cotype
q<\infty
, and let
C_{q}(X^{*})
denote its cotype
q
constant. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
T\colon X\to\ell_{1}^{N}(Y)
be an operator. Suppose that

N^{1/q}\varepsilon>2C_{q}(X^{*})\|T\|.

Then there exists
1\leq n\leq N
such that

\|P_{n}T\|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction and suppose that
\|P_{n}T\|\geq\varepsilon
for every
1\leq n\leq N
. For each
n
, choose
y_{n}^{*}\in B_{Y^{*}}
such that

\|(P_{n}T)^{*}y_{n}^{*}\|>\varepsilon/2.

Identifying
(\ell_{1}^{N}(Y))^{*}
with
\ell_{\infty}^{N}(Y^{*})
, we have
(P_{n}T)^{*}y_{n}^{*}=T^{*}J_{n}y_{n}^{*}
, where
J_{n}y_{n}^{*}
denotes the element of
\ell_{\infty}^{N}(Y^{*})
supported in the
n
-th coordinate.

For each choice of signs
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N})\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, put

y_{\theta}^{*}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}J_{n}y_{n}^{*}.

Then
\|y_{\theta}^{*}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{N}(Y^{*})}\leq 1
, and therefore

\|T\|^{q}=\|T^{*}\|^{q}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|T^{*}y_{\theta}^{*}\|^{q}].

By the cotype
q
estimate in
X^{*}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|T^{*}y_{\theta}^{*}\|^{q}]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}T^{*}J_{n}y_{n}^{*}\right\|^{q}\right]\geq C_{q}(X^{*})^{-q}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|T^{*}J_{n}y_{n}^{*}\|^{q}.

Hence

\|T\|^{q}>C_{q}(X^{*})^{-q}N(\varepsilon/2)^{q},

and so

N^{1/q}\varepsilon<2C_{q}(X^{*})\|T\|,

contradicting the hypothesis.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The reason this reduction is effective for
\ell_{1}
-sums is that restrictions to coordinate sets are controlled by the individual coordinate restrictions. Indeed, if
S\colon\ell_{1}(X)\to Y
is bounded and
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, then

\|SJ_{M}\|=\sup_{m\in M}\|SJ_{m}\|.

Hence, coordinatewise smallness on
M
immediately yields smallness of the restricted operator
SJ_{M}
. One may prove analogous coordinatewise backward reductions for other
\ell_{p}
-sums, but such estimates no longer control
SJ_{M}
itself, since the domain norm is not the
\ell_{1}
-norm.

Using the previous result, we can give the lower-triangular reduction in the case of Banach spaces
X
so that
X^{*}
has finite cotype.

Proposition 4.3

(Reduction to lower-triangular operators from dual cotype)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{*}
has finite cotype. Then, for every operator
T\colon\ell_{1}(X)\to\ell_{1}(X)
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an infinite subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\|P_{M}TJ_{M}-L\|<\varepsilon,

where
L\colon\ell_{1}(M,X)\to\ell_{1}(M,X)
is the lower triangular part of
P_{M}TJ_{M}
.

Proof.

Let
q<\infty
be such that
X^{*}
has cotype
q
, and let
C_{q}(X^{*})
denote its cotype
q
constant. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, choose
N_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

N_{k}^{1/q}\varepsilon/2^{k+1}>2C_{q}(X^{*})\|T\|.

Using
Section

4.1.1
, we recursively construct an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and infinite sets

\mathbb{N}=A_{0}\supseteq A_{1}\supseteq A_{2}\supseteq\ldots

such that
n_{k}\in A_{k-1}
,
A_{k}\subseteq A_{k-1}\cap\{j\in\mathbb{N}:j>n_{k}\}
, and, whenever
1\leq i\leq k
and
j\in A_{k}
, we have

\|P_{n_{i}}TJ_{j}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+1}.

Suppose the construction has been carried out up to stage
k-1
. Choose distinct elements
a_{1}<\ldots<a_{N_{k}}
of
A_{k-1}
, and put

L_{k}=\{j\in A_{k-1}:j>a_{N_{k}}\}.

For each
j\in L_{k}
, define
T_{j}\colon X\to\ell_{1}^{N_{k}}(X)
by

T_{j}x=(P_{a_{s}}TJ_{j}x)_{s=1}^{N_{k}}.

Then
\|T_{j}\|\leq\|T\|
. Hence, by
Section

4.1.1
, there exists
1\leq s(j)\leq N_{k}
such that

\|P_{a_{s(j)}}TJ_{j}\|<\varepsilon/2^{k+1}.

By the pigeonhole principle, there are
1\leq s_{k}\leq N_{k}
and an infinite set
A_{k}\subseteq L_{k}
such that
s(j)=s_{k}
for every
j\in A_{k}
. Define
n_{k}=a_{s_{k}}
. Since
A_{k}\subseteq A_{k-1}
, all previous estimates are preserved, and the new estimate follows from the choice of
A_{k}
. This completes the recursive construction.

Set
M=\{n_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and let
S=P_{M}TJ_{M}
. Write
S=(S_{i,j})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to the enumeration
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
M
, so that

S_{i,j}=P_{n_{i}}TJ_{n_{j}}.

If
i<j
, then
n_{j}\in A_{i}
, and therefore

\|S_{i,j}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+1}.

Define
H\colon\ell_{1}(M,X)\to\ell_{1}(M,X)
to be the strictly upper triangular part of
S
. Then, for every
x\in\ell_{1}(M,X)
,

\|Hx\|\leq\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i+1}}\right)\|x_{n_{j}}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|x\|.

Hence
H
is bounded and
\|H\|<\varepsilon
. Now set
L=S-H
, so that
L
is the lower triangular part of
S=P_{M}TJ_{M}
, and

\|P_{M}TJ_{M}-L\|=\|H\|<\varepsilon.

∎

4.1.2.
Self-similarity of the space

We obtain an analogous lower triangular reduction in the cases that the space has
\ell_{p}
or
c_{0}
-self-similarity. First, we shall need the following preliminary result.

Lemma 4.4

(Backward reduction from self-similarity)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces,
\varepsilon>0
, and
T\colon\ell_{p}(X)\to\ell_{1}^{N}(Y)
be an operator. Suppose that

N^{(p-1)/p}\varepsilon>2\|T\|.

Then there exists
1\leq n\leq N
and
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
infinite such that

\|P_{n}TJ_{M}\|<\varepsilon.

The same result holds for operators from
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
provided
N\varepsilon>2\|T\|
.

Proof.

As before, we proceed by contradiction and assume this is not the case. Let
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
be a partition of
\mathbb{N}
into infinite sets. By assumption
\|P_{n}TJ_{M_{n}}\|\geq\varepsilon
for
1\leq n\leq N
, so we can choose
x_{n}\in B_{\ell_{p}(M_{n},X)}
and
y_{n}^{*}\in B_{Y^{*}}
such that

|y_{n}^{*}P_{n}TJ_{M_{n}}x_{n}|>\varepsilon/2.

For each choice of signs
\theta\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
let

x_{\theta}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}J_{M_{n}}x_{n}.

Since
J_{M_{1}}x_{1},\dots J_{M_{N}}x_{N}
are disjointly supported vectors of norm at most one, we have that
\|x_{\theta}\|\leq N^{1/p}
, so that

\|Tx_{\theta}\|\leq\|T\|N^{1/p}

for any choice of signs
\theta
. On the other hand, averaging, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Tx_{\theta}\|]=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|P_{n}Tx_{\theta}\|]\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[y^{*}_{n}\theta_{n}P_{n}Tx_{\theta}]|.

Since
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}\theta_{m}]=0
whenever
n\neq m
and
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}^{2}]=1
, we get

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}y_{n}^{*}P_{n}Tx_{\theta}]\right|=|y_{n}^{*}P_{n}TJ_{M_{n}}x_{n}|>\varepsilon/2.

It follows that

N\varepsilon/2<\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Tx_{\theta}\|]\leq\|T\|N^{1/p},

in other words
N^{(p-1)/p}\varepsilon<2\|T\|
, which gives the desired contradiction.

The proof for
c_{0}
and
\ell_{\infty}
sums is identical, but now
\|x_{\theta}\|\leq 1
, yielding

N\varepsilon/2<\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Tx_{\theta}\|]\leq\|T\|,

which contradicts
N\varepsilon>2\|T\|
.
∎

We can now give a version of lower triangular reduction in the case of
\ell_{p}
and
c_{0}
self-similarity.

Proposition 4.5

(Reduction to lower-triangular operators from self-similarity)
.

Let
E=\ell_{p}
for
1<p\leq\infty
or
E=c_{0}
,
X
be a Banach space and
T\colon\ell_{1}(E(X))\to\ell_{1}(E(X))
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist an increasing sequence
\mathbf{n}=(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and infinite sets
(A_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
so that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have that whenever
j>i
then

\|\widehat{P}_{n_{i}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+1}.

Proof.

We first do the case
E=\ell_{p}
for
1<p<\infty
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, choose
N_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

N_{k}^{(p-1)/p}\varepsilon/2^{k+1}>2\|T\|.

Using
Section

4.1.2
, we recursively construct an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, a decreasing sequence
(M_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
with
M_{0}=\mathbb{N}
, and, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and each
m\in M_{k}
, an infinite set
B_{m}^{k}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. The construction will ensure that
n_{k}\in M_{k-1}
,
M_{k}\subseteq M_{k-1}\cap\{m\in\mathbb{N}:m>n_{k}\}
, and, whenever
1\leq i\leq k
and
m\in M_{k}
, we have

\|\widehat{P}_{n_{i}}T\widehat{J}_{m}J_{B_{m}^{k}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+1}.

Set
B_{m}^{0}=\mathbb{N}
for every
m\in M_{0}=\mathbb{N}
.

Suppose the construction has been carried out up to stage
k-1
. Choose distinct elements
a_{1}<\ldots<a_{N_{k}}
of
M_{k-1}
, and let
B=\{m\in M_{k-1}:m>a_{N_{k}}\}
. For each
m\in B
, define

T_{m}\colon\ell_{p}(B_{m}^{k-1},X)\to\ell_{1}^{N_{k}}(\ell_{p}(X)),\qquad T_{m}x=(\widehat{P}_{a_{s}}T\widehat{J}_{m}J_{B_{m}^{k-1}}x)_{s=1}^{N_{k}}.

Then
\|T_{m}\|\leq\|T\|
. Hence, by
Section

4.1.2
, there exist
1\leq s(m)\leq N_{k}
and an infinite set
L_{m}\subseteq B_{m}^{k-1}
such that

\|\widehat{P}_{a_{s(m)}}T\widehat{J}_{m}J_{L_{m}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{k+1}.

By the pigeonhole principle, there exist
1\leq s_{k}\leq N_{k}
and an infinite set
M_{k}\subseteq B
such that
s(m)=s_{k}
for every
m\in M_{k}
. Define
n_{k}=a_{s_{k}}
. For
m\in M_{k}
, set
B_{m}^{k}=L_{m}
. Since
B_{m}^{k}\subseteq B_{m}^{k-1}
, all estimates obtained at previous stages are preserved, and the estimate for the new row follows from the choice of
M_{k}
. This completes the recursive construction.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, define

A_{j}=B_{n_{j}}^{j-1}.

If
j>i
, then
n_{j}\in M_{i}
, and since
A_{j}\subseteq B_{n_{j}}^{i}
, the estimate constructed at stage
i
gives

\|\widehat{P}_{n_{i}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{A_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+1}.

The cases
E=\ell_{\infty}
and
E=c_{0}
are identical, except that at the beginning we choose
N_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

N_{k}\varepsilon/2^{k+1}>2\|T\|,

and we apply the
c_{0}
or
\ell_{\infty}
part of
Section

4.1.2
.
∎

4.2.
The
\ell_{\infty}
and
c_{0}
cases

4.2.1.
Finite cotype

We now turn to the
c_{0}(X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(X)
cases under the assumption that
X
has finite cotype. The backward reduction is simpler than in the
\ell_{1}
case: finite cotype allows us to make the restriction of an operator to a suitable infinite set of coordinates arbitrarily small. This is the content of the next lemma.

Lemma 4.6

(Backward reduction from cotype)
.

Let
X
have cotype
q<\infty
,
E
be either
c_{0}
or
\ell_{\infty}
, and
T\colon E(X)\to X
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an infinite subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\|TJ_{M}\|<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction and suppose this is not the case. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
so that

\|TJ_{M}\|\geq\varepsilon

for every infinite subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. Choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

N^{1/q}\varepsilon>2C_{q}(X)\|T\|.

Let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{N}
be pairwise disjoint infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
. By the assumption, for each
1\leq n\leq N
we may choose
x_{n}\in B_{E(M_{n},X)}
such that

\|TJ_{M_{n}}x_{n}\|>\varepsilon/2.

For each choice of signs
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N})\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, put

x_{\theta}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}J_{M_{n}}x_{n}.

Since the sets
M_{1},\ldots,M_{N}
are pairwise disjoint, we have
\|x_{\theta}\|_{E(X)}\leq 1
. Therefore

\|T\|^{q}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Tx_{\theta}\|^{q}]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}TJ_{M_{n}}x_{n}\right\|^{q}\right].

By cotype
q
of
X
,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}TJ_{M_{n}}x_{n}\right\|^{q}\right]\geq C_{q}(X)^{-q}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|TJ_{M_{n}}x_{n}\|^{q}>C_{q}(X)^{-q}N(\varepsilon/2)^{q}.

Hence

N^{1/q}\varepsilon<2C_{q}(X)\|T\|,

contradicting the choice of
N
.
∎

The lower-triangular reduction will now follow from the same recursive selection argument.

Proposition 4.7

(Reduction to lower-triangular operators from cotype)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with finite cotype
q
,
E=c_{0}
or
E=\ell_{\infty}
and
T:E(X)\to E(X)
be an operator. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an infinite subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\|P_{M}TJ_{M}-L\|<\varepsilon,

where
L\colon E(M,X)\to E(M,X)
is the lower triangular part of
P_{M}TJ_{M}
.

Proof.

Using
Section

4.2.1
, we recursively construct an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and infinite sets
(A_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, with
A_{0}=\mathbb{N}
, such that
n_{k}\in A_{k-1}
,
A_{k}\subseteq A_{k-1}\cap\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n>n_{k}\}
, and

\|P_{n_{k}}TJ_{A_{k}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{k+1}.

We now do the recursive construction. Assume that
A_{k-1}
has been constructed. Choose
n_{k}\in A_{k-1}
, and put

B_{k}=A_{k-1}\cap\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n>n_{k}\}.

Applying
Section

4.2.1
to the operator

P_{n_{k}}TJ_{B_{k}}\colon E(B_{k},X)\to X,

after identifying
E(B_{k},X)
with
E(X)
, we obtain an infinite set
A_{k}\subseteq B_{k}
such that

\|P_{n_{k}}TJ_{A_{k}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{k+1}.

This completes the recursive construction.

Set
M=\{n_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, put
M_{k}=\{n_{j}:j>k\}
. Since
M_{k}\subseteq A_{k}
, we have

\|P_{n_{k}}TJ_{M_{k}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{k+1}

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
S=P_{M}TJ_{M}
. For
x\in E(M,X)
, define
Hx
coordinatewise by

P_{n_{k}}Hx=P_{n_{k}}TJ_{M_{k}}P_{M_{k}}x

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for every
x\in E(M,X)
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\|P_{n_{k}}Hx\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}}\|x\|.

Hence
H
is a bounded operator on
\ell_{\infty}(M,X)
with
\|H\|<\varepsilon
. In the case
E=c_{0}
, the same estimate also shows that
Hx\in c_{0}(M,X)
whenever
x\in c_{0}(M,X)
. Define
L=S-H
. Then

\|P_{M}TJ_{M}-L\|=\|H\|<\varepsilon.

Finally, for every
x\in E(M,X)
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

P_{n_{k}}Lx=P_{n_{k}}TJ_{\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\}}P_{\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\}}x=\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{n_{k}}TJ_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}}.

Thus
L
has the lower triangular matrix representation of
P_{M}TJ_{M}
and acts according to this matrix. Hence, also in the case
E=\ell_{\infty}
,
L
is the lower triangular part of
P_{M}TJ_{M}
.
∎

4.2.2.
Self-similarity of the space

It remains to treat the self-similar case for
c_{0}
and
\ell_{\infty}
sums. Here the backward reduction follows from the incompatibility between the sup norm in the domain and the
\ell_{p}
-structure in the target; the lower-triangular reduction then follows by the usual recursive selection.

Lemma 4.8

(Backward reduction from self-similarity)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces,
E=c_{0}
or
E=\ell_{\infty}
and
T\colon E(Y)\to\ell_{p}(X)
be an operator where
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist infinite sets
A,M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\|P_{A}TJ_{M}\|<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction and suppose that the conclusion fails. Then there is
\varepsilon>0
such that

\|P_{A}TJ_{M}\|\geq\varepsilon

for every pair of infinite sets
A,M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. Choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

N^{1/p}\varepsilon>2\|T\|.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{N}
and
M_{1},\ldots,M_{N}
be partitions of
\mathbb{N}
into infinite sets. By assumption, for each
1\leq n\leq N
we have

\|P_{A_{n}}TJ_{M_{n}}\|\geq\varepsilon.

Hence we may choose
y_{n}\in B_{E(M_{n},Y)}
and
x_{n}^{*}\in B_{\ell_{p}(A_{n},X)^{*}}
such that

|x_{n}^{*}P_{A_{n}}TJ_{M_{n}}y_{n}|>\varepsilon/2.

For each choice of signs
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N})\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, put

y_{\theta}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{n}J_{M_{n}}y_{n}.

Since
E=c_{0}
or
E=\ell_{\infty}
and the vectors
J_{M_{n}}y_{n}
have pairwise disjoint supports, we have
\|y_{\theta}\|\leq 1
. Therefore

\|T\|^{p}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Ty_{\theta}\|^{p}].

Since the sets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{N}
are pairwise disjoint, we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Ty_{\theta}\|^{p}]\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|P_{A_{n}}Ty_{\theta}\|^{p}]\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[|x_{n}^{*}P_{A_{n}}Ty_{\theta}|^{p}].

By Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[|x_{n}^{*}P_{A_{n}}Ty_{\theta}|^{p}]\geq\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}x_{n}^{*}P_{A_{n}}Ty_{\theta}]\right|^{p}.

Since
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}\theta_{m}]=0
whenever
n\neq m
and
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}^{2}]=1
, we have

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\theta_{n}x_{n}^{*}P_{A_{n}}Ty_{\theta}]\right|=|x_{n}^{*}P_{A_{n}}TJ_{M_{n}}y_{n}|>\varepsilon/2.

It follows that

\|T\|^{p}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|Ty_{\theta}\|^{p}]>N(\varepsilon/2)^{p},

contradicting the choice of
N
. This contradiction proves the result.
∎

Lastly, we use the previous lemma to give the desired version of lower-triangular reduction.

Proposition 4.9

(Reduction to lower-triangular operators from self-similarity)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
E=c_{0}
or
E=\ell_{\infty}
,
X
be a Banach space, and
T\colon E(\ell_{p}(X))\to E(\ell_{p}(X))
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an infinite set
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and, for each
m\in M
, an infinite set
A_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
m\in M
,

\|P_{A_{m}}\widehat{P}_{m}T\widehat{J}_{M_{m}}\|<\varepsilon,

where
M_{m}=\{n\in M:n>m\}
.

Proof.

Using
Section

4.2.2
, we recursively construct an increasing sequence
(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, a decreasing sequence
(B_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
, with
B_{0}=\mathbb{N}
, and infinite sets
(A_{m_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
m_{k}\in B_{k-1}
,
B_{k}\subseteq B_{k-1}\cap\{m\in\mathbb{N}:m>m_{k}\}
, and

\|P_{A_{m_{k}}}\widehat{P}_{m_{k}}T\widehat{J}_{B_{k}}\|<\varepsilon.

Assume that
B_{k-1}
has been constructed. Choose
m_{k}\in B_{k-1}
, and put

L_{k}=B_{k-1}\cap\{m\in\mathbb{N}:m>m_{k}\}.

Then
L_{k}
is infinite. Applying
Section

4.2.2
to the operator

\widehat{P}_{m_{k}}T\widehat{J}_{L_{k}}\colon E(L_{k},\ell_{p}(X))\to\ell_{p}(X),

after identifying
E(L_{k},\ell_{p}(X))
with
E(\ell_{p}(X))
, we obtain an infinite set
A_{m_{k}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and, after translating back through the identification, an infinite set
B_{k}\subseteq L_{k}
such that

\|P_{A_{m_{k}}}\widehat{P}_{m_{k}}T\widehat{J}_{B_{k}}\|<\varepsilon.

This completes the recursive construction.

Set
M=\{m_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Let
m\in M
, say
m=m_{k}
. Since

M_{m}=\{n\in M:n>m\}=\{m_{j}:j>k\}\subseteq B_{k},

we have

\|P_{A_{m}}\widehat{P}_{m}T\widehat{J}_{M_{m}}\|\leq\|P_{A_{m_{k}}}\widehat{P}_{m_{k}}T\widehat{J}_{B_{k}}\|<\varepsilon.

This proves the result.
∎

5.
Diagonal reductions and proof of the main theorems

In this section we combine the upper- and lower-triangular reductions from the previous sections. Applying both reduction allow us to pass, up to an arbitrarily small perturbation, to the diagonal part. These diagonal reductions are the final structural step before the factorisation argument proving the main theorems.

5.1.
Diagonal reductions

We prove the diagonal reductions. The proofs are obtained by applying the lower-triangular reductions of
Section

4
and then the upper-triangular reductions of
Section

3
, which gives the desired diagonal form.

5.1.1.
The
\ell_{1}
case

We begin with the
\ell_{1}
case, treating first the finite dual-cotype hypothesis and then the self-similar hypothesis.

Proposition 5.1

(Diagonal reduction from dual cotype)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{*}
has finite cotype, and let
T\colon\ell_{1}(X)\to\ell_{1}(X)
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an infinite subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\|P_{M}TJ_{M}-D\|<\varepsilon,

where
D\colon\ell_{1}(M,X)\to\ell_{1}(M,X)
is the diagonal part of
P_{M}TJ_{M}
.

Proof.

Apply
Section

4.1.1
to
T
with
\varepsilon/2
in place of
\varepsilon
. We obtain an infinite subset
M_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\|P_{M_{0}}TJ_{M_{0}}-L\|<\varepsilon/2,

where
L\colon\ell_{1}(M_{0},X)\to\ell_{1}(M_{0},X)
is the lower triangular part of
P_{M_{0}}TJ_{M_{0}}
.

Identifying
\ell_{1}(M_{0},X)
with
\ell_{1}(X)
, apply
Section

3.1.1
to
L
with
\varepsilon/2
in place of
\varepsilon
. Thus, after passing to an infinite subset
M\subseteq M_{0}
, we have

\|P_{M}LJ_{M}-D\|<\varepsilon/2,

where
D
is the upper triangular part of
P_{M}LJ_{M}
.

Since
L
is lower triangular,
P_{M}LJ_{M}
is lower triangular. Hence its upper triangular part
D
is precisely its diagonal part. Moreover,
L
has the same diagonal as
P_{M_{0}}TJ_{M_{0}}
, and therefore
D
is also the diagonal part of
P_{M}TJ_{M}
. Here, in the terms involving
L
, the projection and inclusion are taken relative to
\ell_{1}(M_{0},X)
. Finally, using the two estimates above and the fact that passing to the smaller set
M\subseteq M_{0}
is contractive, we obtain

\|P_{M}TJ_{M}-D\|\leq\|P_{M_{0}}TJ_{M_{0}}-L\|+\|P_{M}LJ_{M}-D\|<\varepsilon.

This proves the result.
∎

We now have the analogous statement in the case of
\ell_{p}
or
c_{0}
self-similarity.

Proposition 5.2

(Diagonal reduction from self-similarity)
.

Let
E=\ell_{p}
for
1<p\leq\infty
or
E=c_{0}
and
T\colon\ell_{1}(E(X))\to\ell_{1}(E(X))
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist infinite increasing sequences
\mathbf{n}=(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\|P_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}TJ_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}-D\|<\varepsilon,

where
D\colon\ell_{1}(X)\to\ell_{1}(X)
is the diagonal part of
P_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}TJ_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}
.

Proof.

Applying
Section

3.1.2
to
T
, we obtain an increasing sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and infinite sets
(B_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, writing
M_{j}=\{a_{i}:i>j\}
, we have

\|\widehat{P}_{M_{j}}T\widehat{J}_{a_{j}}J_{B_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{j+2}

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

We shall work on the same inner copies on both the domain and range sides. After identifying each
E(B_{j},X)
isometrically with
E(X)
, let

V\colon\ell_{1}(E(X))\to\ell_{1}(E(X))

be the contraction which sends the
j
-th outer coordinate into the
a_{j}
-th outer coordinate and, inside that coordinate, into
E(B_{j},X)
. Let

U\colon\ell_{1}(E(X))\to\ell_{1}(E(X))

be the contraction which projects onto the outer coordinates
\{a_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
and, in the
a_{i}
-th outer coordinate, onto
E(B_{i},X)
, followed by the natural reindexing. Set
S=UTV
. Then
S\colon\ell_{1}(E(X))\to\ell_{1}(E(X))
is bounded, and, under the above identifications, its matrix entries are

S_{i,j}=P_{B_{i}}\widehat{P}_{a_{i}}T\widehat{J}_{a_{j}}J_{B_{j}}.

Applying
Section

4.1.2
to
S
with
\varepsilon/2
in place of
\varepsilon
, we obtain an increasing sequence
(b_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and infinite sets which, after translating back through the identifications
E(B_{b_{j}},X)\simeq E(X)
, we denote by
A_{j}\subseteq B_{b_{j}}
, such that whenever
j>i
,

\|P_{B_{b_{i}}}\widehat{P}_{a_{b_{i}}}T\widehat{J}_{a_{b_{j}}}J_{A_{j}}\|<\varepsilon/2^{i+2}.

Set
n_{j}=a_{b_{j}}
. Since each
A_{j}
is infinite, choose
m_{j}\in A_{j}
recursively so that
(m_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing.

Let
D
be the diagonal part of
P_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}TJ_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}
. We estimate the strictly upper and strictly lower triangular parts separately. Let
x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{1}(X)
. For the strictly upper triangular part, the second reduction gives

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{j>i}P_{m_{i}}\widehat{P}_{n_{i}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{m_{j}}x_{j}\right\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j>i}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i+2}}\|x_{j}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\|x\|.

For the strictly lower triangular part, note that
\{n_{i}:i>j\}\subseteq\{a_{i}:i>b_{j}\}=M_{b_{j}}
and
A_{j}\subseteq B_{b_{j}}
. Hence, by the first reduction,

\sum_{i>j}\|P_{m_{i}}\widehat{P}_{n_{i}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{m_{j}}x_{j}\|\leq\|\widehat{P}_{M_{b_{j}}}T\widehat{J}_{a_{b_{j}}}J_{B_{b_{j}}}\|\|x_{j}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{b_{j}+2}}\|x_{j}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}\|x_{j}\|.

Therefore

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i>j}\|P_{m_{i}}\widehat{P}_{n_{i}}T\widehat{J}_{n_{j}}J_{m_{j}}x_{j}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\|x\|.

Combining the two estimates gives

\|(P_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}TJ_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}-D)x\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|x\|.

Hence

\|P_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}TJ_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}-D\|<\varepsilon,

which finishes the proof.
∎

(Diagonal reduction from cotype)

4.2.1

Proposition 5.5
.

Let

,

factors the identity on

with constant

.

If instead

Lemma 5.6

(Stability of factorisation under perturbations)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
T,S\in\mathscr{B}(X)
. Suppose that
T
factors the identity on
X
with constant
C
and that

C\|S-T\|<1/2.

Then
S
factors the identity on
X
with constant
2C
.

Proof.

Since
T
factors the identity with constant
C
, there exist operators
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathscr{B}(X)
such that

\Phi T\Psi=I_{X}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\Phi\|\|\Psi\|\leq C.

Hence

\|\Phi(S-T)\Psi\|\leq C\|S-T\|<1/2.

Therefore

\Phi S\Psi=I_{X}+\Phi(S-T)\Psi

is invertible, and

\|(\Phi S\Psi)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\|\Phi(S-T)\Psi\|}<2.

Thus

I_{X}=(\Phi S\Psi)^{-1}\Phi S\Psi.

Consequently, the identity factors through
S
, and the factorisation constant is at most

\|(\Phi S\Psi)^{-1}\Phi\|\|\Psi\|\leq\|(\Phi S\Psi)^{-1}\|\|\Phi\|\|\Psi\|<2C.\qed

6.
An uncountable
\ell_{1}
-sum

Although the preceding transfer principles are stated for countable vector-valued sequence spaces, the diagonal-reduction arguments are not inherently countable. With the evident modifications, the same methods apply to spaces indexed by an arbitrary set
\Gamma
, such as
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,X)
,
c_{0}(\Gamma,X)
and
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma,X)
. The only caveat is that for
X\simeq\ell_{1}(X)
, the UPFP of
X
does not automatically transfer to the UPFP of
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,X)
when
\Gamma
is uncountable.

The uncountable setting allows an additional diagonal-reduction mechanism. In the countable arguments above, both upper- and lower-triangular reductions are needed in order to reach a diagonal operator. For uncountable index sets, one can sometimes replace this two-sided selection by a one-sided finite-interference estimate, followed by a free-set argument which removes the finitely many exceptional coordinates on a large subset of the index set.

We now apply this mechanism to the summand
L_{1}[0,1]
. Although
L_{1}[0,1]
is not covered by the finite-dual-cotype hypothesis in
Theorem

1.1
, it has enough additional measure-theoretic structure to recover the required diagonal reduction. Capon’s localisation argument gives the UPFP for
L_{1}[0,1]
, while Kalton’s representation theorem gives the finite-interference estimate for operators from
L_{1}[0,1]
into
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
. Combining this estimate with Hajnal’s free set theorem yields the diagonal reduction for every uncountable
\Gamma
. Together with the countable case, this proves
Theorem

1.6
. The application to
C[0,1]^{*}
then follows from its classical representation as an
\ell_{1}
-sum of copies of
L_{1}[0,1]
.

We begin by recalling the localisation argument of Capon. If
B\subseteq[0,1]
is measurable, we write

L_{1}(B)=\{f\in L_{1}[0,1]:f=0\text{ a.e. on }[0,1]\setminus B\},

and we denote by
M_{B}\colon L_{1}[0,1]\to L_{1}(B)
the multiplication operator
f\mapsto\mathbf{1}_{B}f
and by
\jmath_{B}\colon L_{1}(B)\to L_{1}[0,1]
the canonical inclusion. Thus, when
R\colon L_{1}[0,1]\to L_{1}[0,1]
is an operator,
M_{B}R
denotes the map
f\mapsto\mathbf{1}_{B}Rf
. Finally, for any operator
S\colon L_{1}[0,1]\to L_{1}[0,1]
we can associate to it a family of measures
(\nu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
according to Kalton’s representation theorem
[
9
, Theorem 3.1 (b)]
.

Lemma 6.1

(Capon localisation)
.

Let
S\colon L_{1}[0,1]\to L_{1}[0,1]
be an operator, and let
(\nu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be the family of measures associated with
S
by Kalton’s representation theorem. Suppose that, for some
a>0
, the set

E_{a}(S)=\{t\in[0,1]:|\nu_{t}(\{t\})|>a\}

has positive measure. Then, for every
0<\varepsilon<a
, there is a measurable set
A\subseteq[0,1]
of positive measure such that, whenever
B\subseteq A
has positive measure, the operator

M_{B}S\jmath_{B}\colon L_{1}(B)\to L_{1}(B)

is an isomorphism onto
L_{1}(B)
, and

\|M_{B}S\jmath_{B}f\|\geq(a-\varepsilon)\|f\|\qquad(f\in L_{1}(B)).

Consequently,

\|(M_{B}S\jmath_{B})^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{a-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

This is Capon’s localisation argument
[
6
, Proposition 1.2]
. Capon stated the result for the value
a=1/2
; the proof is unchanged with
1/2
replaced by an arbitrary
a>0
.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following.

Corollary 6.2
.

The space
L_{1}[0,1]
has the UPFP.

Proof.

Let
T\colon L_{1}[0,1]\to L_{1}[0,1]
be an operator, and let
(\nu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be the associated family of measures. The operator
I_{L_{1}[0,1]}-T
is associated with the family
(\delta_{t}-\nu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
. For every
t\in[0,1]
, either

|\nu_{t}(\{t\})|>\frac{1}{3}

or

|(\delta_{t}-\nu_{t})(\{t\})|>\frac{1}{3}.

Hence one of these two alternatives holds on a set of positive measure.

Applying
Section

6
with
a=1/3
and
\varepsilon=1/6
, either to
T
or to
I_{L_{1}[0,1]}-T
, we obtain a positive-measure set
B\subseteq[0,1]
such that the corresponding operator

M_{B}T\jmath_{B}\colon L_{1}(B)\to L_{1}(B)

or

M_{B}(I_{L_{1}[0,1]}-T)\jmath_{B}\colon L_{1}(B)\to L_{1}(B)

is an isomorphism onto
L_{1}(B)
, whose inverse has norm at most
6
. Since
L_{1}(B)
is isometric to
L_{1}[0,1]
, the identity on
L_{1}[0,1]
factors through either
T
or
I_{L_{1}[0,1]}-T
with a constant independent of
T
. Therefore,
L_{1}[0,1]
has the UPFP.
∎

The next lemma is the one-sided localisation step used in the uncountable diagonal reduction. It is analogous in spirit to the forward reductions from
Section

3
: after passing to a suitable positive-measure part of the domain, all but finitely many target coordinates of the operator become small. In the uncountable setting, this finite exceptional set can later be removed simultaneously along a large set of coordinates by Hajnal’s free set theorem.

Lemma 6.3

(Finite interference)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a set and
S\colon L_{1}[0,1]\to\ell_{1}(\Lambda,L_{1}[0,1])
be an operator. Then, for every
\eta>0
, there are a finite set
F\subseteq\Lambda
and a measurable set
B\subseteq[0,1]
of positive measure such that

\|P_{\Lambda\setminus F}S\jmath_{B}\|<\eta.

Proof.

We first prove the result when
\Lambda
is countable and thus we may assume without loss of generality that
\Lambda=\mathbb{N}
. Identify
\ell_{1}(\mathbb{N},L_{1}[0,1])
with
L_{1}(\Omega,m)
, where
\Omega=\mathbb{N}\times[0,1]
and
m
is the product of the counting measure on
\mathbb{N}
and the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
. Let
\mu
denote Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
. By Kalton’s representation theorem
[
9
, Theorem 3.1(b)]
, there is a measurable family
(\nu_{y})_{y\in\Omega}
of finite measures on
[0,1]
such that

(Sf)(y)=\int_{[0,1]}f\,d\nu_{y}

for almost every
y\in\Omega
.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, put
F_{N}=\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
\Omega_{N}=(\mathbb{N}\setminus F_{N})\times[0,1]
. Define a finite positive measure
\rho_{N}
on
[0,1]
by

\rho_{N}(E)=\int_{\Omega_{N}}|\nu_{y}|(E)\,dm(y).

By Kalton’s representation theorem
[
9
, Theorem 3.1(b)]
, applied to
P_{\mathbb{N}\setminus F_{N}}S
, and using the estimate
[
9
, (3.1.1)]
, we have

\rho_{N}(E)\leq\|P_{\mathbb{N}\setminus F_{N}}S\|\mu(E)\leq\|S\|\mu(E)

for every measurable
E\subseteq[0,1]
. Hence
\rho_{N}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
.

Let
g_{N}
be the Radon–Nikodym derivative of
\rho_{N}
with respect to
\mu
. Since
g_{N}
is finite almost everywhere, the set

B=\{t\in[0,1]:g_{N}(t)<\eta/2\}

has positive measure for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, otherwise
g_{N}\geq\eta/2
almost everywhere for every
N
, and hence

\rho_{N}([0,1])=\int_{0}^{1}g_{N}\,d\mu\geq\eta/2\qquad(N\in\mathbb{N}),

which contradicts that
\rho_{N}([0,1])\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. The latter convergence follows from
\Omega_{N}\downarrow\varnothing
and the integrability of
y\mapsto|\nu_{y}|([0,1])
over
\Omega
.

It remains to estimate the norm of
P_{\mathbb{N}\setminus F_{N}}S\jmath_{B}
. Let
f\in L_{1}(B)
. Then, for almost all
y\in\Omega_{N}
, we have

(P_{\mathbb{N}\setminus F_{N}}S\jmath_{B}f)(y)=\int_{B}f\,d\nu_{y}.

Hence, by Tonelli’s theorem and the definition of
\rho_{N}
,

\|P_{\mathbb{N}\setminus F_{N}}S\jmath_{B}f\|\leq\int_{\Omega_{N}}\int_{B}|f|\,d|\nu_{y}|\,dm(y)=\int_{B}|f|\,d\rho_{N}.

Since
d\rho_{N}=g_{N}\,d\mu
and
g_{N}<\eta/2
on
B
, we get

\int_{B}|f|\,d\rho_{N}=\int_{B}|f|g_{N}\,d\mu\leq(\eta/2)\|f\|.

Therefore

\|P_{\mathbb{N}\setminus F_{N}}S\jmath_{B}\|<\eta.

Now let
\Lambda
be arbitrary. Since
L_{1}[0,1]
is separable, choose a dense sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L_{1}[0,1]
. For each
n
, the vector
Sf_{n}
has countable support in
\Lambda
, so

\Lambda_{0}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\supp(Sf_{n})

is countable. If
\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{0}
, then
P_{\lambda}Sf_{n}=0
for every
n
, and hence
P_{\lambda}S=0
by continuity. Thus

S(L_{1}[0,1])\subseteq\ell_{1}(\Lambda_{0},L_{1}[0,1]).

Applying the countable case to
S
regarded as an operator into
\ell_{1}(\Lambda_{0},L_{1}[0,1])
, we obtain a finite set
F\subseteq\Lambda_{0}
and a measurable set
B\subseteq[0,1]
of positive measure such that

\|P_{\Lambda_{0}\setminus F}S\jmath_{B}\|<\eta.

Since
S
has no coordinates outside
\Lambda_{0}
, this is the same as

\|P_{\Lambda\setminus F}S\jmath_{B}\|<\eta.

The proof is complete.
∎

Before we proceed to the diagonal reduction, we recall the following finite-valued form of Hajnal’s free set theorem
[
8
]
.

Lemma 6.4

(Finite free set)
.

Let
\Gamma
be an uncountable set, and let
F_{\gamma}\subseteq\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma\}
be finite for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then there is a set
H\subseteq\Gamma
such that
|H|=|\Gamma|
and, for every
\gamma\in H
, we have

F_{\gamma}\cap H=\varnothing.

We are now ready to obtain the analogue, in the present uncountable setting, of the diagonal reductions proved in
Section

5
. The key point is that the finite interference lemma gives a one-sided reduction for each column, while Hajnal’s free set theorem removes the finitely many exceptional coordinates simultaneously on a large subset of
\Gamma
.

Lemma 6.5

(Diagonal reduction)
.

Let
\Gamma
be an uncountable set, and let
T\colon\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])\to\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
be an operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there are a set
H\subseteq\Gamma
with
|H|=|\Gamma|
, measurable sets
B_{\gamma}\subseteq[0,1]
of positive measure for
\gamma\in H
, contractions

V\colon\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in H}L_{1}(B_{\gamma})\right)_{\ell_{1}}\to\ell_{1}(H,L_{1}[0,1])

and

U\colon\ell_{1}(H,L_{1}[0,1])\to\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in H}L_{1}(B_{\gamma})\right)_{\ell_{1}}

such that

UV=I_{\bigl(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in H}L_{1}(B_{\gamma})\bigr)_{\ell_{1}}},

and a diagonal operator
D
on
\bigl(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in H}L_{1}(B_{\gamma})\bigr)_{\ell_{1}}
such that

\|UP_{H}TJ_{H}V-D\|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, define

S_{\gamma}=P_{\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma\}}TJ_{\gamma}\colon L_{1}[0,1]\to\ell_{1}(\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma\},L_{1}[0,1]).

Apply
Section

6
to
S_{\gamma}
, with
\eta=\varepsilon
. We obtain a finite set
F_{\gamma}\subseteq\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma\}
and a measurable set
B_{\gamma}\subseteq[0,1]
of positive measure such that

\|P_{\Gamma\setminus(\{\gamma\}\cup F_{\gamma})}TJ_{\gamma}\jmath_{B_{\gamma}}\|<\varepsilon.

By
Section

6
, there is a set
H\subseteq\Gamma
with
|H|=|\Gamma|
such that, for all
\gamma\in H
, we have

F_{\gamma}\cap H=\varnothing.

Hence, for every
\gamma\in H
,

\|P_{H\setminus\{\gamma\}}TJ_{\gamma}\jmath_{B_{\gamma}}\|<\varepsilon.

Put

Y=\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in H}L_{1}(B_{\gamma})\right)_{\ell_{1}}.

Define
V\colon Y\to\ell_{1}(H,L_{1}[0,1])
and
U\colon\ell_{1}(H,L_{1}[0,1])\to Y
coordinatewise, for each
\gamma\in H
, by

(Vy)_{\gamma}=\jmath_{B_{\gamma}}y_{\gamma},\qquad(Ux)_{\gamma}=M_{B_{\gamma}}x_{\gamma}.

Then
U
and
V
are contractions, and
UV=I_{Y}
.

Let
D\colon Y\to Y
be the diagonal operator given, for each
\gamma\in H
, by

(Dy)_{\gamma}=M_{B_{\gamma}}P_{\gamma}TJ_{\gamma}\jmath_{B_{\gamma}}y_{\gamma}.

Estimating by columns and using
F_{\beta}\cap H=\varnothing
, we obtain, for
y=(y_{\beta})_{\beta\in H}\in Y
,

\|(UP_{H}TJ_{H}V-D)y\|\leq\sum_{\beta\in H}\|P_{H\setminus\{\beta\}}TJ_{\beta}\jmath_{B_{\beta}}y_{\beta}\|\leq\varepsilon\|y\|.

Therefore

\|UP_{H}TJ_{H}V-D\|\leq\varepsilon.

∎

Once the operator has been reduced to diagonal form up to a small perturbation, the factorisation argument is the same as in the proof of the main transfer theorems. We first record the corresponding diagonal dichotomy for the localised
\ell_{1}
-sum.

Proposition 6.6

(Diagonal dichotomy)
.

Let
C\geq 1
, let
H
be an uncountable set, and for each
\alpha\in H
let
B_{\alpha}\subseteq[0,1]
be measurable with positive measure. Suppose that
L_{1}[0,1]
has the
C
-PFP. Put

Y=\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in H}L_{1}(B_{\alpha})\right)_{\ell_{1}}.

Then, for every diagonal operator
D\colon Y\to Y
, either
D
or
I_{Y}-D
factors the identity on
Y
with constant
C
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of
Section

5.2
, using that each
L_{1}(B_{\alpha})
is isometric to
L_{1}[0,1]
.
∎

We are finally ready for the proof of
Theorem

1.6
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.6
.

If
\Gamma
is finite or countably infinite, the result follows from
Section

6
, since
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
is isomorphic to
L_{1}[0,1]
. We may therefore suppose that
\Gamma
is uncountable.

Let
C\geq 1
be such that
L_{1}[0,1]
has the
C
-PFP, and choose
\delta>0
such that
C\delta<1/2
. Let
T\colon\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])\to\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
be an operator.

Applying
Section

6
with
\varepsilon=\delta/2
, we obtain a set
H\subseteq\Gamma
with
|H|=|\Gamma|
, measurable sets
B_{\gamma}\subseteq[0,1]
of positive measure for
\gamma\in H
, contractions

V\colon Y\to\ell_{1}(H,L_{1}[0,1]),\qquad U\colon\ell_{1}(H,L_{1}[0,1])\to Y,

where

Y=\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in H}L_{1}(B_{\gamma})\right)_{\ell_{1}},

such that
UV=I_{Y}
, and a diagonal operator
D\colon Y\to Y
satisfying

\|UP_{H}TJ_{H}V-D\|\leq\delta/2<\delta.

By
Section

6
, either
D
or
I_{Y}-D
factors the identity on
Y
with constant
C
.

Suppose first that
D
factors the identity on
Y
with constant
C
. Since

C\|UP_{H}TJ_{H}V-D\|<1/2,

Section

5.2
implies that
UP_{H}TJ_{H}V
factors the identity on
Y
with constant
2C
. Choose an isometric isomorphism

W\colon\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])\to Y,

which exists because
|H|=|\Gamma|
and each
L_{1}(B_{\gamma})
is isometric to
L_{1}[0,1]
. Since
W
and
W^{-1}
are isometries, it follows that
T
factors the identity on
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
with constant
2C
.

Suppose instead that
I_{Y}-D
factors the identity on
Y
with constant
C
. Since
UV=I_{Y}
, we have

I_{Y}-UP_{H}TJ_{H}V=UP_{H}(I_{\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])}-T)J_{H}V.

Moreover,

\|UP_{H}(I_{\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])}-T)J_{H}V-(I_{Y}-D)\|<\delta.

Thus, arguing as before we conclude that
I_{\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])}-T
factors the identity with constant
2C
, which proves that
\ell_{1}(\Gamma,L_{1}[0,1])
has the UPFP.

Lastly, by a classical result
[
12
, Proposition 5.6]
, the space
C[0,1]^{*}
is isomorphic to
\ell_{1}(2^{\aleph_{0}},L_{1}[0,1])
. Hence, by the previous argument,
C[0,1]^{*}
has the UPFP, and therefore
C[0,1]^{*}
is primary.
∎

7.
Final discussion and open questions

The spaces
\ell_{1}
,
c_{0}
and
\ell_{\infty}
, especially the latter, have historically been among the more delicate settings for factorisation and primariness arguments, often requiring arguments different from those used for
\ell_{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, or even for sums with respect to arbitrary symmetric bases. Somewhat surprisingly, the methods presented here can only be directly applied in these cases. The reason is that the geometry of
\ell_{1}
and of the supremum norm gives enough control over coordinate restrictions to obtain both upper- and lower-triangular reductions.

For
\ell_{p}
-sums with
1<p<\infty
, the situation is less clear. One can often obtain one-sided reductions, either upper or lower triangular, under natural hypotheses, but obtaining both simultaneously seems to require substantially stronger assumptions. This obstruction is not absolute: in
[
2
]
, a two-sided reduction was obtained for
\ell_{p}
-sums of certain
C(K)
spaces. There, the upper-triangular reduction used the
c_{0}
-structure of the spaces, while the lower-triangular reduction relied on the special structure of
C(K)
, in particular on reproducibility properties of
K
which force an
\ell_{1}
-type behaviour.

It is natural to ask whether there are general hypotheses under which analogous two-sided reductions can be proved for
\ell_{p}(X)
,
1<p<\infty
.

Question 7.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Are there natural and sufficiently broad conditions on a Banach space
X
under which the UPFP of
X
passes to
\ell_{p}(X)
?

Remark 7.2
.

In the uncountable setting, the need for a full two-sided reduction can sometimes be avoided. Indeed, once a one-sided finite-interference estimate is available, Hajnal’s free set theorem can remove the finitely many exceptional coordinates on a large subset of the index set. Thus the cotype and self-similarity mechanisms used in the countable reductions above are expected to give corresponding UPFP results for uncountable sums
\ell_{p}(\Gamma,X)
,
1<p<\infty
, under the analogous hypotheses.

The PFP and UPFP originate in the study of primariness, so it is natural to ask whether these properties, or primariness itself, are preserved by vector-valued sequence-space constructions.

Question 7.3
.

Is there a Banach space
X
with the PFP, respectively the UPFP, such that
\ell_{p}(X)
, for some
1\leq p\leq\infty
, or
c_{0}(X)
fails the PFP, respectively the UPFP?

Question 7.4
.

Is there a primary Banach space
X
such that
\ell_{p}(X)
, for some
1\leq p\leq\infty
, or
c_{0}(X)
is not primary?

A counterexample to either question would likely have to be rather pathological, while a positive answer seems far beyond the reach of the present methods.
