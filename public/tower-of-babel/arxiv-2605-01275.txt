Symplectic small covers in dimension four Thanks: This work was supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea Grant funded by the Korean Government (RS-2025-00521982).
Suyoung Choi

Abstract
. We study symplectic structures on four-dimensional small covers. Our main result shows that every symplectic four-dimensional small cover is aspherical. We then classify symplectic small covers over products of two polygons, proving that symplecticity is equivalent to factor-compatibility. We also classify them up to diffeomorphism. Finally, we construct a symplectic four-dimensional small cover whose orbit polytope is not combinatorially equivalent to a product of two polygons.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 57S12; Secondary 53D05, 57K43, 52B11

1.
Introduction

Small covers
, introduced by Davis and Januszkiewicz
[
11
]
, are real analogues of quasitoric manifolds.
They are closed manifolds equipped with locally standard
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-actions whose orbit spaces are simple convex polytopes.
They form one of the basic classes of manifolds in toric topology, and their topology is controlled by a simple polytope together with a mod
2
characteristic map.
For general background on toric topology, we refer the reader to
[
4
]
.

From the viewpoint of symplectic geometry, small covers are often regarded as real or Lagrangian objects associated with toric geometry.
This point of view is natural, but it leaves open the question of when a small cover itself carries a symplectic structure.
A fundamental positive result in this direction is due to Ishida
[
15
]
.
For
real Bott manifolds
, equivalently small covers over cubes, he proved that cohomological symplecticity is equivalent to the existence of a symplectic structure.

The goal of the present paper is to study symplectic structures on four-dimensional small covers.
In dimension four, c-symplecticity is easy to detect.
For a closed connected smooth
4
-manifold, it is equivalent to orientability together with
b_{2}>0
.
Thus c-symplecticity is flexible, whereas symplecticity is much more rigid.

The symplecticity problem for four-dimensional small covers is related to the geography of aspherical and hyperbolic four-manifolds.
For instance, small covers of the right-angled
120
-cell give many closed hyperbolic four-manifolds, but it is unknown whether any of them is symplectic.
LeBrun conjectured that closed hyperbolic four-manifolds have vanishing Seiberg–Witten invariants
[
18
]
; together with Taubes’ non-vanishing theorem
[
28
]
, this would rule out symplectic structures on such manifolds.
This viewpoint also motivates recent work on hyperbolic four-manifolds with vanishing Seiberg–Witten invariants
[
1
,
26
]
.

The main results of the paper may be summarized as follows.
Our first main result is a flagness obstruction.
We prove that if a four-dimensional small cover is symplectic, then the dual simplicial sphere of its orbit polytope must be flag.
Consequently, every symplectic four-dimensional small cover is aspherical.
See Corollary
4.8
.

We also obtain a corresponding statement for real moment-angle manifolds.
If a four dimensional real moment-angle manifold
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is symplectic, then either
K
is flag or
K\cong\partial(P_{3}\times P_{m-3})^{\ast}
.
Equivalently, apart from the exceptional case
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong S^{2}\times\Sigma
, where
\Sigma
is a closed orientable surface, symplecticity forces
K
to be flag.
See Theorem
4.7
.

We then give a sharp classification for products of two polygons.
A small cover over a product of two polygons is symplectic if and only if it is factor-compatible.
See Theorem
5.7
.
We further enumerate the symplectic small covers over products of two polygons, and show that their diffeomorphism type is determiend by their mod
2
cohomology rings.
See Theorem
6.5
and Corollary
6.6
.

Finally, we show that symplectic four-dimensional small covers are not exhausted by the examples over products of two polygons.
We construct a symplectic four-dimensional small cover whose orbit polytope is not combinatorially equivalent to a product of two polygons.
See Theorem
7.6
.

We also include a computer-assisted verification showing that, among small covers whose orbit polytopes have at most ten facets, every symplectic example arises from a product of two polygons and is factor-compatible.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect the basic definitions and facts that will be used throughout the paper.
Throughout the paper, we write
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\{0,1\}
.
For a field
\Bbbk
and a topological space
X
, we write
b_{i}(X;\Bbbk)=\dim_{\Bbbk}H^{i}(X;\Bbbk)
.
When
\Bbbk=\mathbb{Q}
, we simply write
b_{i}(X)
.
All vector spaces over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
are written additively.

If
K
is a simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}
and
\omega\subset[m]
, then
K_{\omega}
denotes the full subcomplex of
K

on the vertex set
\omega
.
Throughout the paper, we identify a subset of
[m]
with its indicator vector in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
, and also identify a vector
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
with its support

\supp(u)=\{i\in[m]\mid u_{i}=1\}.

Thus
K_{u}
means
K_{\supp(u)}
for
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
.
We also write
u^{c}
for the complementary subset
[m]\setminus\supp(u)
, or equivalently for the vector
\mathbf{1}_{m}+u
.

2.1.
Small covers and characteristic maps

Let
P
be a simple polytope of dimension
n
, and let
\mathcal{F}(P)=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\}
be the set of its facets.
A mod
2

characteristic map
on
P
is a map

\lambda\colon\mathcal{F}(P)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}

such that whenever
F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{k}}\neq\varnothing
, the vectors
\lambda(F_{i_{1}}),\dots,\lambda(F_{i_{k}})
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
We use the same symbol
\lambda
for the associated matrix

\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda(F_{1})&\cdots&\lambda(F_{m})\end{pmatrix}\in M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

We also write
\row(\lambda)\subset\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
for the row space of
\lambda
.
With this convention, the defining condition means that the columns corresponding to the facets meeting at each vertex form a basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
.

The
small cover

M(P,\lambda)
over
P
determined by
\lambda
is defined by

M(P,\lambda):=(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\times P)/\sim,

where
(g,p)\sim(h,q)
if
p=q
and
g+h\in G_{F(p)}
.
Here
F(p)
denotes the unique face of
P
whose relative interior contains
p
, and

G_{F}:=\langle\lambda(F_{i})\mid F\subset F_{i}\rangle\subset\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}

is the subgroup generated by the characteristic vectors of the facets containing
F
.
See
[
11
]
for details.

The group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
acts naturally on
M(P,\lambda)
by
a\cdot[g,p]=[a+g,p]
, and the orbit space of this action is naturally identified with
P
.
Two small covers over
P
are said to be
Davis–Januszkiewicz equivalent
(or simply
D–J equivalent
) if there is a homeomorphism between them
which is weakly
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-equivariant with respect to an automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
and which covers the identity on
P
.
Throughout the paper, unless otherwise specified, small covers are considered up to D–J equivalence.

Elementary row operations on
\lambda
correspond to changing the basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
, and hence do not change the small cover up to D–J equivalence.
After relabeling the facets so that
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
meet at a chosen vertex and then applying elementary row operations over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we may assume that

\lambda=(I_{n}\mid A).

We also recall the Davis–Januszkiewicz description of the mod
2
cohomology ring.
Let
v_{i}\in H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
be the Poincaré dual of the characteristic submanifold over the facet
F_{i}
.
Then

(2.1)

H^{\ast}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]/(I_{P}+J_{\lambda}),

where
\deg v_{i}=1
for all
i
.
Here,
I_{P}
denotes the Stanley–Reisner ideal of
P
, that is, the ideal generated by all square-free monomials
v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{r}}
for which the corresponding facets
F_{i_{1}},\dots,F_{i_{r}}
have empty intersection, and
J_{\lambda}
is the ideal generated by the linear forms

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{ji}v_{i}\qquad(j=1,\dots,n),

where
\lambda_{ji}
denotes the
(j,i)
-entry of the characteristic matrix
\lambda
.

Let
f_{i}
denote the number of codimension-
(i+1)
faces of
P
.
Thus
f_{-1}=1
corresponds to the unique codimension-
0
face, namely
P
itself.
Its
h
-vector
h(P)=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{n})
is defined by

\sum_{i=0}^{n}h_{i}t^{n-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i-1}(t-1)^{n-i}.

The
i
th mod
2
Betti number
b_{i}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is completely determined by face numbers of
P
, that is,

(2.2)

b_{i}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=h_{i}(P),

where
h(P)=(h_{0},\dots,h_{n})
is the
h
-vector of
P
; see
[
11
]
.

2.2.
Real moment-angle complexes and real toric spaces

Let
K
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]
.
The
real moment-angle complex

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is defined by

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in K}(D^{1},S^{0})^{\sigma}\subset(D^{1})^{m},

where

(D^{1},S^{0})^{\sigma}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in(D^{1})^{m}\mid x_{i}\in S^{0}\text{ for }i\notin\sigma\}.

When
K
is a piecewise linear (PL)
(n-1)
-sphere,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a simplicial
n
-manifold; see
[
5
, Theorem 2.3]
.
In particular, if
n\leq 7
,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
admits a smooth structure.
Throughout this paper, all simplicial spheres are finite.
In dimension three, every simplicial sphere is PL.

Example 2.1
.

Let
K=\partial P_{m}^{\ast}
be the boundary complex dual to an
m
-gon.

We use the cubewise cell structure on
(D^{1})^{m}=[-1,1]^{m}
.
A cell of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is determined by a simplex
\sigma\in K
together with a choice of signs on the complementary coordinates.
Since
K
has one empty simplex,
m
vertices, and
m
edges, the induced cell decomposition on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
has
f_{0}=2^{m}
,
f_{1}=m2^{m-1}
,
f_{2}=m2^{m-2}
cells in dimensions
0,1,2
, respectively.

Since
K\cong S^{1}
,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a closed surface.
Since
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a smooth complete intersection of real quadrics in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, its normal bundle is canonically trivialized by the gradients of the defining equations, and hence,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is stably parallelizable, in particular orientable.

Let
\mathfrak{g}(m)
denote the genus of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
Since the Euler characteristic is
2^{m-2}(4-m)
, we obtain
\mathfrak{g}(m)=1+(m-4)2^{m-3}
.
In particular,
m=3
gives
S^{2}
,
m=4
gives
T^{2}
, and
m\geq 5
gives a higher-genus surface.

If
K=\partial(P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}})^{\ast}=\partial P_{m_{1}}^{\ast}\join\partial P_{m_{2}}^{\ast}
, then

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial P_{m_{1}}^{\ast}}\times\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial P_{m_{2}}^{\ast}},

thus
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a product of two closed orientable surfaces.

We say that a linear map
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\ell}
is
nonsingular
over
K
if, for every simplex
\sigma\in K
, the vectors
\lambda(e_{i})
for
i\in\sigma
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Such a map
\lambda
is also called a
mod
2
characteristic
map over
K
.
If
P
is a simple
n
-polytope and
K=\partial P^{\ast}
, then the vertices of
K
are naturally identified with the facets of
P
.
With this identification and with
\ell=n
, a mod-
2
characteristic map over
K
is precisely a mod-
2
characteristic map over
P
in the sense of Subsection
2.1
.

If
\lambda
is a mod
2
characteristic map over
K
, then
\ker\lambda
acts freely on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
by
[
6
]
.
The quotient

M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda):=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}/\ker\lambda

is called a
real toric space
.

When
\lambda=\id_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}}
, one has
M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda)=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
When
K=\partial P^{\ast}
is the dual complex of a simple polytope
P
and
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
is a mod
2
characteristic map on
P
, this construction recovers the small cover
M(P,\lambda)
.

Note that not every simplicial sphere is the dual complex of a simple polytope.
Thus the above construction contains all small covers in the classical sense.
However, the mod
2
cohomology presentation (
2.1
) and the mod
2
Betti number formula (
2.2
) extend to the generalized Davis–Januszkiewicz setting; see
[
11
, Theorem 5.12]
.
Therefore, when
K
is a simplicial
(n-1)
-sphere and
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
is nonsingular over
K
, we also call
M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda)
a
small cover over
K
.
This agrees with the classical small cover when
K=\partial P^{\ast}
for a simple polytope
P
.

One of the main cohomological tools in this paper is the cohomology formula for real toric spaces.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a commutative ring in which
2
is a unit.
There is a
(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\row(\lambda))
-graded
\Bbbk
-algebra isomorphism

H^{\ast}\bigl(M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda);\Bbbk\bigr)\cong\bigoplus_{\omega\in\row(\lambda)}\widetilde{H}^{\ast-1}(K_{\omega};\Bbbk).

More precisely, the product structure on
\bigoplus_{\omega\in\row(\lambda)}\widetilde{H}^{\ast}(K_{\omega};\Bbbk)
is given by the canonical maps

\widetilde{H}^{i-1}(K_{\omega_{1}};\Bbbk)\otimes\widetilde{H}^{j-1}(K_{\omega_{2}};\Bbbk)\to\widetilde{H}^{i+j-1}(K_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}};\Bbbk),

which are induced by the simplicial maps

K_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}\to K_{\omega_{1}}\join K_{\omega_{2}},

where
\join
denotes the simplicial join.

In the special case
\lambda=\id_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}}
, this gives the corresponding formula for
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
For this case, Cai
[
5
]
proved the formula with integral coefficients.

In particular, for a real toric space
M=M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda)
, we have

(2.3)

H^{i}(M;\mathbb{Q})\cong\bigoplus_{\omega\in\row(\lambda)}\widetilde{H}^{i-1}(K_{\omega};\mathbb{Q}),

and the multiplicative structure is given by Theorem
2.2
.
We will refer to (
2.3
) as the
Hochster-type decomposition
.

We now recall the orientability criterion for real toric spaces over simplicial spheres.
Let
\mathbf{1}_{m}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
.
If
M=M(P,\lambda)
is a small cover, then
K=\partial P^{\ast}
is an
(n-1)
-sphere, so
\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(K;\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
.
By Theorem
2.2
, this top class contributes to
H^{n}(M;\mathbb{Q})
exactly when
\mathbf{1}_{m}\in\row(\lambda)
.
Since
\mathbf{1}_{m}
is the only weight that can contribute in top degree, one recovers the following criterion, originally due to
[
23
]
in the classical case.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
K
be a simplicial sphere on
[m]
, and let
M=M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda)
be a real toric space.
Then
M
is orientable if and only if the all-one vector

\mathbf{1}_{m}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}

belongs to
\row(\lambda)
.
In particular,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is orientable.

The following is an elementary linear-algebra observation.
We state it separately because it will be used several times in the sequel.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\ell}
be a linear map, and let
G_{\lambda}=\ker\lambda
.
For
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
,
u\not\in\row(\lambda)
if and only if there exists
g\in G_{\lambda}
such that

\langle u,g\rangle=1,

where
\langle-,-\rangle
denotes the standard pairing on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
.

Proof.

This follows from the elementary identity

\row(\lambda)=(\ker\lambda)^{\perp}

with respect to the standard pairing on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
.
∎

2.3.
Symplectic and basic complex-geometric terminology

Let
N
be a closed smooth manifold of real dimension
2n
.
A
symplectic structure
on
N
is a closed nondegenerate differential
2
-form
\Omega
on
N
, and a manifold equipped with such a form is called

symplectic
.
If
(N,\Omega)
is symplectic, then

[\Omega]^{n}\neq 0\in H^{2n}(N;\mathbb{R}),

since
\Omega^{n}
is a nowhere vanishing top-degree form.
Accordingly,
N
is called
cohomologically symplectic
, or
c-symplectic
, if there exists a class
a\in H^{2}(N;\mathbb{Q})
such that

a^{n}\neq 0\in H^{2n}(N;\mathbb{Q}).

Since rational classes are dense in
H^{2}(N;\mathbb{R})
and the condition
x^{n}\neq 0
is open, every symplectic manifold is c-symplectic.
The converse is false in general.

We begin with the elementary criterion for c-symplecticity in dimension four.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
N
be a closed connected smooth
4
-manifold.
Then
N
is c-symplectic if and only if
N
is orientable and
b_{2}(N)>0
.

In particular, for a
4
-dimensional small cover
M=M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda)
over a simplicial
3
-sphere
K
,
M
is c-symplectic if and only if
\mathbf{1}_{m}\in\row(\lambda)
and there exists
\omega\in\row(\lambda)
such that
\widetilde{H}^{1}(K_{\omega};\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
.

Proof.

The “only if” part is clear.
Indeed, a c-symplectic class
a
and
a^{2}
are nonzero in
H^{2}(N;\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{4}(N;\mathbb{Q})
, respectively.
Conversely, assume that
N
is orientable and
b_{2}(N)>0
.
By Poincaré duality, the intersection pairing

Q(x,y)=\langle xy,[N]\rangle

on
H^{2}(N;\mathbb{Q})
is nondegenerate.
If every
x\in H^{2}(N;\mathbb{Q})
satisfied
x^{2}=0
, then the polarization identity would give

Q(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle(x+y)^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2},[N]\right\rangle=0

for all
x,y
, contradicting nondegeneracy.
Thus there exists
x\in H^{2}(N;\mathbb{Q})
such that
x^{2}\neq 0
.
The final assertion follows by combining the first statement with Theorem
2.2
and Corollary
2.3
.
∎

3.
Basic obstructions

Throughout this section,
K
is a simplicial
3
-sphere on the vertex set
[m]
, and
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}
a mod
2
characteristic map over
K
.
Let
M=M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda)=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}/{G_{\lambda}}
be the corresponding small cover, where
G_{\lambda}=\ker\lambda
.

The purpose of this section is to collect several basic obstructions to the existence of symplectic structures on
M
or
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
Some of them depend only on
K
, while others depend on the canonical cover
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
or on the action of
G_{\lambda}
.

The first obstruction is elementary but fundamental.
Since every small cover is a finite quotient of its real moment-angle cover, symplecticity of the small cover forces symplecticity of the canonical cover.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}/G_{\lambda}
be a
4
-dimensional small cover.
If
M
admits a symplectic structure, then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
admits a
G_{\lambda}
-invariant symplectic structure.
Conversely, if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
admits a
G_{\lambda}
-invariant symplectic structure, then
M
is symplectic.

In particular, if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is not symplectic, then no small cover over
K
is symplectic.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\to M
be the finite covering map.
If
\Omega
is a symplectic form on
M
, then
\widetilde{\Omega}:=\pi^{\ast}\Omega
is a closed nondegenerate
2
-form on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
Moreover,
\widetilde{\Omega}
is
G_{\lambda}
-invariant because
\pi
is the quotient map by
G_{\lambda}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
carries a
G_{\lambda}
-invariant symplectic form
\widetilde{\Omega}
.
Since the action of
G_{\lambda}
is free and finite,
G_{\lambda}
-invariant differential forms on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
are naturally identified with differential forms on the quotient
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}/G_{\lambda}=M
.
Hence
\widetilde{\Omega}
descends to a closed nondegenerate
2
-form on
M
.
Thus
M
is symplectic.
∎

We shall also use the following consequence.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
M=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}/G_{\lambda}
is symplectic, and let
\widetilde{\Omega}=\pi^{\ast}\Omega
be the pulled-back symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
Then every element of
G_{\lambda}
preserves the symplectic canonical class

K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Every
g\in G_{\lambda}
is a symplectomorphism of
(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K},\widetilde{\Omega})
.
Choose an almost complex structure
J
compatible with
\widetilde{\Omega}
.
Then
g^{\ast}J
is also compatible with
\widetilde{\Omega}
.
Since the space of compatible almost complex structures is contractible, the class
K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}:=-c_{1}(T\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K},J)
is independent of the choice of
J
.
Therefore
g^{\ast}K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}=K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
for every
g\in G_{\lambda}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Proposition
3.1
separates two different questions.
First, one may ask whether the canonical cover
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is symplectic at all.
Second, even if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is symplectic, one must ask whether it carries a
G_{\lambda}
-invariant symplectic form.
This distinction will be important later.

The next obstruction depends only on the face numbers of
K
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If a
4
-dimensional small cover
M=M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda)
admits a symplectic structure, then

\chi(M)\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

Equivalently, the number
f_{3}(K)
of facets of
K
is divisible by
4
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a symplectic form on
M
, and choose an
\Omega
-compatible almost complex structure
J
.
Then the first Chern class
c_{1}(TM,J)
is a characteristic element for the intersection form, and the Hirzebruch–Hopf formula
[
14
]
gives

(3.1)

c_{1}(TM,J)^{2}=2\chi(M)+3\sigma(M),

where
\sigma(M)
is the signature of
M
.

Since
M
is symplectic, it is orientable.
Hence
\mathbf{1}_{m}\in\row(\lambda)
by Corollary
2.3
.
By the Hochster-type product structure in Theorem
2.2
, the intersection pairing on
H^{2}(M;\mathbb{Q})
pairs the summand of weight
\omega
only with the summand of weight
\omega^{c}=\mathbf{1}_{m}+\omega
.
Since no weight satisfies
\omega=\omega^{c}
, the intersection form is a direct sum of hyperbolic pairs.
Thus
\sigma(M)=0
.
Thus

c_{1}(TM,J)^{2}=2\chi(M).

By van der Blij’s congruence
[
30
]
, the square of a characteristic element is congruent to the signature modulo
8
.
Therefore

2\chi(M)=c_{1}(TM,J)^{2}\equiv 0\pmod{8},

which proves the first statement.

For a small cover over a simplicial
3
-sphere, by (
2.2
) we have

\chi(M)=h_{0}-h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{3}+h_{4}=f_{1}-5f_{0}+16.

For a simplicial
3
-sphere, we have
f_{1}-f_{0}=f_{3}
.
These prove the equivalent form.
∎

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere on the vertex set
[m]
.
If
K_{\omega}\cong S^{1}
, then, after fixing signs on the complementary coordinates, we obtain an embedded coordinate surface
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{\omega}}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere on
[m]
, and let
\omega\subset[m]
be such that
K_{\omega}\cong S^{1}
.
Then the coordinate surface
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{\omega}}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
represents a nonzero rational homology class.
Moreover, its rational self-intersection is zero.

In particular, for
q\geq 3
, if
K_{\omega}\cong\partial P_{q}^{\ast}
, then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{\omega}}\cong\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}(q)}
is an embedded closed orientable surface of square zero in
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.

Proof.

The rational cohomology of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is given by the Hochster-type decomposition (
2.3
).
Under Poincaré duality for
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, the fundamental class of the coordinate submanifold
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{\omega}}
has rational Poincaré dual in the summand indexed by the complementary subset
\omega^{c}
.

Since
K_{\omega}\cong S^{1}
, combinatorial Alexander duality for the simplicial
3
-sphere
K
gives
\widetilde{H}^{1}(K_{\omega^{c}};\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}
.
Thus the Poincaré dual of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{\omega}}
is nonzero, and hence
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{\omega}}
represents a nonzero rational homology class.

The square of this Poincaré dual has Hochster weight
\omega^{c}+\omega^{c}=0
.
The weight-zero summand in degree four is
\widetilde{H}^{3}(K_{\varnothing};\mathbb{Q})=0
.
Therefore the square of the Poincaré dual is zero by Theorem
2.2
.
Equivalently, the coordinate surface has rational self-intersection zero.
∎

The following obstruction depends only on the real moment-angle complex.
It is especially useful for non-flag spheres with induced missing triangles.
The source of this obstruction is Li’s theorem on embedded spheres in symplectic
4
-manifolds
[
19
]
, which states that a symplectic
4
-manifold containing an infinite-order smoothly embedded sphere of nonnegative self-intersection must be rational or ruled.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere.
Suppose that there is a subset
\omega\subset[m]
such that
K_{\omega}\cong\partial P^{\ast}_{3}
.
Assume also that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is neither rational nor ruled.
Then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is not symplectic.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is symplectic.
Since
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{\omega}}\cong S^{2}
, Lemma
3.5
gives a smoothly embedded sphere in
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
which represents a nonzero rational homology class and has self-intersection zero.
Li’s theorem
[
19
]
would then imply that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is rational or ruled, which is a contradiction.
∎

We also prepare one obstruction used later.
A closed manifold is called
algebraically simply connected
if its fundamental group has no nontrivial finite quotients.

Proposition 3.7

(
[
17
, Proposition 1]
)
.

Let
X
be a closed symplectic
4
-manifold.
Suppose that
X
decomposes as a nontrivial smooth connected sum.
Then one of the summands has negative definite intersection form and is algebraically simply connected.

4.
The flagness obstruction in dimension four

In this section, we prove the main flagness obstruction in dimension four.
The only exceptional non-flag case for the real moment-angle cover is
\partial(P_{3}\times P_{m-3})^{\ast}
, and this case will be excluded later for
small covers by a c-symplectic argument.

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere.
A simplicial complex
K
is called
flag
if every finite set of vertices which are pairwise joined by edges spans a simplex of
K
.
Equivalently,
K
is flag if and only if every missing face of
K
has cardinality two.
Here a subset
\omega\subset V(K)
is called a missing face if
\omega\notin K
and every proper subset of
\omega
belongs to
K
.

Since
K
is a simplicial
3
-sphere, a non-flag
K
has a missing face of cardinality
3
,
4
, or
5
.
If
K
has a missing face of cardinality
5
, then
K=\partial\Delta^{4}
.
In this case
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong S^{4}
, so
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is not symplectic.
Moreover, every small cover over
K
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}P^{4}
, hence is non-orientable and not symplectic.
Therefore, it remains to consider the case where
K
has a missing triangle or a missing tetrahedron.

4.1.
Missing-triangle case

We first prepare two elementary combinatorial facts.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
L
be a finite simplicial complex on the vertex set
V
.
Assume that
\widetilde{H}_{1}(L;\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}
and that
\widetilde{H}_{1}(L_{\upsilon};\mathbb{Q})=0
for every proper subset
\upsilon\subsetneq V
.
Then
L
is the boundary of a polygon.

Proof.

Choose a simple edge cycle
C
in
L
which represents a nonzero class in
H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Q})
.
Let
V(C)
be the vertex set of
C
.
If
V(C)\subsetneq V
, then the class of
C
is nonzero in
H_{1}(L_{V(C)};\mathbb{Q})
, since otherwise it would also vanish in
H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Q})
.
This contradicts the assumption.
Hence
V(C)=V
.

The cycle
C
has no chord.
Indeed, a chord would split
C
into two cycles supported on proper subsets of
V
.
Each of those cycles would bound in the corresponding full subcomplex, and hence
C
would also bound in
L
, a contradiction.

Thus the
1
-skeleton of
L
is exactly the cycle
C
.
Moreover
L
has no higher-dimensional simplices.
If
|V|>3
, any
2
-simplex would give a chord of
C
.
If
|V|=3
, filling the triangle would kill
H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Q})
.
Therefore
L=C
, the boundary of a polygon.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere on
[m]
.
Suppose that there exists a subset
\omega\subset[m]
such that
K_{\omega}\cong\partial P_{3}^{\ast}
.
Then
b_{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})\geq 2
.
Moreover, if
b_{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})=2
, then
K\cong\partial(P_{3}\times P_{m-3})^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Recall the Hochster-type decomposition (
2.3
) for
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}

H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{Q})\cong\bigoplus_{I\subset[m]}\widetilde{H}^{1}(K_{I};\mathbb{Q}).

Since
K_{\omega}\cong\partial P_{3}^{\ast}\cong S^{1}
, the summand indexed by
\omega
contributes one dimension.
By Alexander duality for full subcomplexes of the simplicial
3
-sphere
K
, the complementary full subcomplex
K_{\omega^{c}}
also satisfies
\widetilde{H}^{1}(K_{\omega^{c}};\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}
.
Hence
b_{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})\geq 2
.

Assume now that
b_{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})=2
.
Then the only nonzero Hochster summands in degree
2
are those indexed by
\omega
and
{\omega^{c}}
.
In particular,
\widetilde{H}_{1}(K_{I};\mathbb{Q})=0
for every
I\neq\omega,\omega^{c}
.

Applying Lemma
4.1
to
L=K_{\omega^{c}}
, we obtain
K_{\omega^{c}}\cong\partial P_{q}^{\ast}
for
q=|{\omega^{c}}|
.

It remains to see that
K
is the join of
K_{\omega}
and
K_{\omega^{c}}
.
For
x\in\omega^{c}
, we have
\widetilde{H}_{1}(K_{\omega\cup\{x\}};\mathbb{Q})=0
.
Since
K_{\omega}
is the boundary of a missing triangle, the only possible
2
-simplices in
K_{\omega\cup\{x\}}
which can bound this cycle are the three triangles containing
x
.
Thus all these triangles must be present.
Hence, for every edge
e\subset\omega
, the triangle
e\cup\{x\}
is a face of
K
.

Therefore, for every edge
e\subset\omega
, the link
\link_{K}(e)
is a simplicial
1
-sphere whose vertex set is exactly
\omega^{c}
.
Since
K_{\omega^{c}}
is a polygonal cycle, and since every edge of
\link_{K}(e)
is an edge of
K_{\omega^{c}}
, we must have
\link_{K}(e)=K_{\omega^{c}}
.
Thus
e\cup f
is a simplex of
K
for every edge
e\subset\omega
and every edge
f\subset\omega^{c}
of
K_{\omega^{c}}
.
Equivalently, all facets of
K_{\omega}\join K_{\omega^{c}}
belong to
K
.

Since
K_{\omega}
and
K_{\omega^{c}}
are full subcomplexes and both are
1
-dimensional cycles, no additional simplex can occur.
Hence
K=K_{\omega}\join K_{\omega^{c}}\cong\partial P_{3}^{\ast}\join\partial P_{m-3}^{\ast}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The following hold.

(1)

An orientable four-dimensional small cover is neither rational nor ruled.

(2)

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere.
If
b_{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})>2
, then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is neither rational nor ruled.

Proof.

(1) Let
M
be an orientable four-dimensional small cover.
By (
2.1
), the ring
H^{\ast}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is generated by
H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
This is impossible for a rational surface, since then
H^{1}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=0
but
H^{2}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\neq 0
.
It is also impossible for a ruled surface over a positive-genus base, since the subring generated by degree-one classes comes from the base and does not contain the fiber class or the exceptional classes.

(2) Since a real moment-angle manifold is stably parallelizable by
[
4
]
, its rational Pontryagin classes vanish.
Hence, by the Hirzebruch signature theorem, its signature is zero.
A rational or ruled four-manifold with signature zero has
b_{2}=2
, so the condition
b_{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})>2
excludes both possibilities.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere with a missing triangle.
If
K
is not isomorphic to
\partial(P_{3}\times P_{m-3})^{\ast}
, then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is not symplectic.

Proof.

Since
K
has a missing triangle, by Lemma
4.2
together with the assumption, we have
b_{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})\geq 3
.
Then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is neither rational nor ruled by Lemma
4.3
.
By Corollary
3.6
,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is not symplectic.
∎

4.2.
Missing-tetrahedron case

We recall the connected-sum formula for real moment-angle manifolds under a simplicial connected sum.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
be simplicial
(n-1)
-spheres, and suppose that they contain a common facet
\sigma
with
|\sigma|=n
.
Let
K=K_{1}\#_{\sigma}K_{2}
be their simplicial connected sum along
\sigma
.
Put
a=|V(K_{1})\setminus\sigma|
and
b=|V(K_{2})\setminus\sigma|
.
Then

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong\left(\#_{2^{b}}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{1}}\right)\#\left(\#_{2^{a}}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{2}}\right)\#\left(\#_{(2^{a}-1)(2^{b}-1)}S^{1}\times S^{n-1}\right).

We now apply this to missing tetrahedra.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere with a missing tetrahedron.
Then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is not symplectic.

Proof.

Let
\omega=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}\subset V(K)
be a missing tetrahedron of
K
.
The subcomplex
K_{\omega}\cong S^{2}
is a simplicial embedded
2
-sphere in
|K|\cong S^{3}
.
Hence it separates
|K|
into two simplicial
3
-balls.
Filling
K_{\omega}
by the simplex
\Delta_{\omega}^{3}
on each side gives two simplicial
3
-spheres, say
K_{+}
and
K_{-}
, such that

K=K_{+}\#_{\omega}K_{-}.

Put
A=V(K_{+})\setminus\omega
and
B=V(K_{-})\setminus\omega
.
Both
A
and
B
are non-empty.
Indeed, if one side had no vertices outside
\omega
, then that side would be a triangulated
3
-ball with boundary
\partial\Delta_{\omega}^{3}
and with no vertices other than those of
\omega
.
The only such simplicial
3
-ball is
\Delta_{\omega}^{3}
, which would imply
\omega\in K
, contradicting the assumption that
\omega
is a missing tetrahedron.

Applying Lemma
4.5
with
n=4
,
a=|A|\geq 1
, and
b=|B|\geq 1
, we obtain

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong\left(\#_{2^{b}}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{+}}\right)\#\left(\#_{2^{a}}\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K_{-}}\right)\#\left(\#_{(2^{a}-1)(2^{b}-1)}S^{1}\times S^{3}\right)\cong Y\#(S^{1}\times S^{3}),

for some closed oriented smooth
4
-manifold
Y
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is symplectic with symplectic form
\Omega
.
Then
[\Omega]^{2}>0
, so
b_{2}(Y)>0
, since
H^{2}(S^{1}\times S^{3};\mathbb{R})=0
.
By Proposition
3.7
, one summand in this connected-sum decomposition must be negative definite and algebraically simply connected.

The summand
S^{1}\times S^{3}
is not algebraically simply connected, since
\pi_{1}(S^{1}\times S^{3})\cong\mathbb{Z}
has nontrivial finite quotients.
On the other hand,
Y
cannot be negative definite, because the symplectic class has positive square and
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{R})\cong H^{2}(Y;\mathbb{R})
.
This is a contradiction.
∎

Combining Propositions
4.4
and
4.6
, together with the case
K=\partial\Delta^{4}
discussed above, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
K
be a simplicial
3
-sphere on
[m]
.
If
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is symplectic, then
K
is either isomorphic to
\partial(P_{3}\times P_{m-3})^{\ast}
or flag.

Corollary 4.8
.

Every symplectic four-dimensional small cover is aspherical.

Proof.

Let
M
be a symplectic four-dimensional small cover over a simplicial
3
-sphere
K
.

By Proposition
3.1
,
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is symplectic.
Hence Theorem
4.7
implies that
K
is either flag or isomorphic to
\partial(P_{3}\times P_{m-3})^{\ast}
.

The second case is excluded by Proposition 5.2 below, which shows that
\partial(P_{3}\times P_{m-3})^{\ast}
supports no c-symplectic small cover.
Since every symplectic manifold is c-symplectic, this rules out the exceptional case.

Therefore
K
is flag.
Hence
M
is aspherical by
[
10
]
.
∎

5.
Four-dimensional small covers over products of two polygons

Throughout this section we consider a simple polytope of the form

P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}},

where
P_{q}
is a
q
-gon.
We may assume that
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
.
We write
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
, and decompose the facet set of
P
as

\mathcal{F}(P)=\{F_{1,1},\dots,F_{1,m_{1}}\}\sqcup\{F_{2,1},\dots,F_{2,m_{2}}\}

where the block
\{F_{i,1},\dots,F_{i,m_{i}}\}
comes from the
i
th polygon factor.

We identify each facet
F_{i,j}
with the corresponding standard basis vector of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
.
With this convention, for each
i=1,2
, we set the factor weights

\chi_{i}=F_{i,1}+\dots+F_{i,m_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}.

We also write
\mathbf{1}_{m}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}F=\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
.

Let
K=\partial P^{\ast}
.
As shown in Example
2.1
,

(5.1)

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial P_{m_{1}}^{\ast}}\times\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial P_{m_{2}}^{\ast}}\cong\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}(m_{1})}\times\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}(m_{2})}=:\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}

where
\Sigma_{i}=\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}(m_{i})}
is a closed orientable surface of genus
\mathfrak{g}(m_{i})=1+(m_{i}-4)2^{m_{i}-3}
.

Let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}
be a mod
2
characteristic map on
K
, and let

M=M(P,\lambda)=M^{\mathbb{R}}(K,\lambda)=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}/G_{\lambda}

be the corresponding small cover, where
G_{\lambda}=\ker\lambda
.
Moreover, by Corollary
2.3
,
M
is orientable if and only if
\mathbf{1}_{m}\in\row(\lambda)
.

We first show that triangle factors cannot occur in c-symplectic small covers over products of polygons.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
be a product of two polygons, and let
\lambda
be a mod
2
characteristic map over
P
.
If
\chi_{i}\in\row(\lambda)
, then
m_{i}
is even.

Proof.

Since
\chi_{i}\in\row(\lambda)
, there is a linear functional

\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}

whose values on the columns of
\lambda
are prescribed by
\chi_{i}
.

Choose a vertex in the other polygon factor.
It is given by two adjacent facets.
Let
W\subset\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}
be the span of their characteristic vectors.
By the characteristic condition,
\dim W=2
, and by the definition of
\chi_{i}
we have
W\subset\ker\varphi
.

In the quotient
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}/W
, the images of the characteristic vectors from the
i
th polygon all lie in the affine line

\{v\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}/W\mid\varphi(v)=1\},

which has two elements.
Adjacent facets of the
i
th polygon have distinct image, again by the characteristic condition.
Hence the two possible images must alternate around the polygon.
This is possible only when
m_{i}
is even.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
P=P_{3}\times P_{m-3}
be a product of two polygons.
Then no small cover over
P
is c-symplectic.

Proof.

Let
K=\partial P^{\ast}
and let
M=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}/G_{\lambda}
be a c-symplectic small cover over
K
.
By Proposition
2.5
, there exists
\omega\in\row(\lambda)
such that
\widetilde{H}^{1}(K_{\omega};\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
.

Since
K=\partial P_{3}^{\ast}\join\partial P_{m-3}^{\ast}
, every full subcomplex of
K
is a join of full subcomplexes of the two factors.
The triangle factor
\partial P_{3}^{\ast}
has no disconnected proper full subcomplex. Hence the only weights
\omega
with
\widetilde{H}^{1}(K_{\omega};\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
are
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
.
Thus either
\chi_{1}\in\row(\lambda)
or
\chi_{2}\in\row(\lambda)
.
Since
\mathbf{1}_{m}=\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}\in\row(\lambda)
, it follows that both
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
belong to
\row(\lambda)
.

This contradicts Lemma
5.1
.
Therefore
M
is not c-symplectic.
∎

For
P=P_{4}\times P_{4}
, set

\delta_{1,1}=F_{1,1}+F_{1,3}\quad\text{ and }\quad\delta_{1,2}=F_{1,2}+F_{1,4},

and

\delta_{2,1}=F_{2,1}+F_{2,3}\quad\text{ and }\quad\delta_{2,2}=F_{2,2}+F_{2,4}.

These are the four opposite-pair vectors.

Definition 5.3
.

Fix the product decomposition
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
.
With notation as above, we call the small cover
M(P,\lambda)

factor-compatible
if the following two conditions hold.

(1)

If
(m_{1},m_{2})=(4,4)
, then the four vectors
\delta_{1,1},\delta_{1,2},\delta_{2,1},\delta_{2,2}
can be divided into two pairs whose sums both lie in
\row(\lambda)
.

(2)

If
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(4,4)
, then both
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
belong to
\row(\lambda)
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
be a product of polygons, and let
M=M(P,\lambda)
be a factor-compatible small cover.
Then
M
is symplectic.

Proof.

If
(m_{1},m_{2})=(4,4)
, then the factor-compatible condition is exactly the c-symplectic condition in Ishida’s theorem, written in the facet notation of the cube.
Indeed, for
K=\partial(P_{4}\times P_{4})^{\ast}=(S^{0})^{\join 4}
, the full subcomplexes
K_{\omega}
with
\widetilde{H}^{1}(K_{\omega};\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
are precisely those indexed by the sums of two opposite-pair vectors.
Hence the above condition is equivalent to c-symplecticity by Proposition
2.5
.
By Ishida’s theorem
[
15
]
., it is also equivalent to symplecticity.

Assume that
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(4,4)
.
Let
K=\partial P^{\ast}
.
Under the identification
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}
as in (
5.1
), the canonical action splits by the two factors.
Write
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m_{2}}
.
For
g\in G_{\lambda}
, write
g=(g^{(1)},g^{(2)})
according to this decomposition, and let
\rho_{i}(g)\colon\Sigma_{i}\to\Sigma_{i}
be the diffeomorphism induced by
g^{(i)}
on the
i
th surface factor.

The orientation character of
\rho_{i}(g)
is
(-1)^{\langle\chi_{i},g\rangle}
.
Since
\chi_{i}\in\row(\lambda)
and
g\in\ker\lambda
, we have
\langle\chi_{i},g\rangle=0
.
Thus every
\rho_{i}(g)
is orientation-preserving.

Choose positive area forms
\eta_{1}^{0}
and
\eta_{2}^{0}
on
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
.
Define

\eta_{i}=\sum_{g\in G_{\lambda}}\rho_{i}(g)^{\ast}\eta_{i}^{0}\qquad(i=1,2).

Since every
\rho_{i}(g)
is orientation-preserving, each
\eta_{i}
is again a positive area form.
By construction,
\eta_{i}
is invariant under the induced
G_{\lambda}
-action on
\Sigma_{i}
.

Let
\pi_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\to\Sigma_{i}
be the projection.
Then

\widetilde{\Omega}=\pi_{1}^{\ast}\eta_{1}+\pi_{2}^{\ast}\eta_{2}

is closed and
G_{\lambda}
-invariant.
Moreover,
\widetilde{\Omega}^{2}=2\pi_{1}^{\ast}\eta_{1}\wedge\pi_{2}^{\ast}\eta_{2}
is a positive volume form on
\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}
.
Hence
\widetilde{\Omega}
is a
G_{\lambda}
-invariant symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, and it descends to a symplectic form on
M
by Proposition
3.1
.
∎

We now turn to the converse direction.
From now on, we prove that every symplectic small cover over a product of two polygons is factor-compatible.
We introduce the geometric obstruction that controls symplectic small covers over products of two polygons.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
be a product of two polygons with
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 4
and
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(4,4)
, and put
K=\partial P^{\ast}
.
Let
M=\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}/G_{\lambda}
be an oriented small cover.
If some element of
G_{\lambda}
reverses the orientations of both surface factors of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, then
M
is not symplectic.

Proof.

Suppose that
M
is symplectic.
Let
\Omega
be a symplectic form on
M
.
Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\to M
be the finite covering map, and let
\widetilde{\Omega}=\pi^{\ast}\Omega
be the pullback symplectic form.
Every element of
G_{\lambda}
acts as a symplectomorphism of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}
with respect to
\widetilde{\Omega}
.

Choose an almost complex structure
J
compatible with
\widetilde{\Omega}
, and let

K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}:=-c_{1}(T\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K},J)

be the symplectic canonical class of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
Since
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(4,4)
, one of
\mathfrak{g}(m_{1})
and
\mathfrak{g}(m_{2})
is greater than
1
.
Hence

(5.2)

b_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})=1+2\mathfrak{g}(m_{1})\mathfrak{g}(m_{2})>3.

Let
\gamma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{2}
be embedded loops representing nonzero integral homology classes.
Then

T_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}:=\gamma_{1}\times\gamma_{2}

is an embedded torus in
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
Its homology class is non-torsion, and its self-intersection is zero.

By Taubes’ non-vanishing theorem and the standard Seiberg–Witten adjunction inequality for
b_{2}^{+}>1

[
28
,
24
]
, applied to the symplectic canonical class
K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
, we have

0=2\mathfrak{g}(T_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}})-2\geq[T_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}]^{2}+\left|K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\cdot[T_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}]\right|=\left|K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\cdot[T_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}]\right|.

Therefore
K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\cdot[T_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}]=0
for every product torus of this form.

As
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
vary, these product tori span the mixed Künneth summand

H_{1}(\Sigma_{1};\mathbb{Q})\otimes H_{1}(\Sigma_{2};\mathbb{Q})\subset H_{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{Q}).

Hence, by Künneth duality, the component of
K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
in
H^{1}(\Sigma_{1};\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^{1}(\Sigma_{2};\mathbb{Q})
vanishes.
Thus the rational canonical class lies in
\mathbb{Q}u_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Q}u_{2}
, where
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are the orientation classes of the two surface factors.

Let
g\in G_{\lambda}
be an element reversing the orientations of both surface factors.
Then
g
acts by multiplication by
-1
on each of the two factor orientation classes.
Therefore

g^{\ast}K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}=-K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}.

On the other hand,
g
is a symplectomorphism of the pulled-back symplectic form, so it preserves the symplectic canonical class.
Thus

g^{\ast}K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}=K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}.

It follows that
K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}=0
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{Q})
.
Since
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a product of surfaces, its integral cohomology is torsion-free.
Thus
K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}=0
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{Z})
.
Equivalently,
c_{1}(T\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K},J)=0
.
In particular,
c_{1}(T\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K},J)
is torsion.
By Bauer’s theorem
[
2
, Corollary 1.2]
, applied to the closed symplectic four-manifold
(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K},\widetilde{\Omega})
, we have

b_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K})\leq 3.

This contradicts (
5.2
).
Hence
M
is not symplectic.
∎

We now apply the obstruction to the failure of factor-compatibility.
For a product of two polygons, write
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
for the vectors supported on the facets of the two factors.
When at least one factor is not a square, factor-compatibility is equivalent to the condition that both
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
lie in
\row(\lambda)
.
When both factors are squares, this is the real Bott case.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
with
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(4,4)
and let
M=M(P,\lambda)
be orientable.
Assume that
M
is not factor-compatible.
Then there exists
g\in\ker\lambda
which reverses the orientations of both surface factors of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial P^{\ast}}\cong\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
M
is orientable, Corollary
2.3
gives
\mathbf{1}_{m}=\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}\in\row(\lambda)
.
By the assumption, one of
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
does not lie in
\row(\lambda)
.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\chi_{1}\notin\row(\lambda)
.
By Lemma
2.4
, there exists
g\in\ker\lambda
such that
\langle\chi_{1},g\rangle=1
.
Since

0=\langle\mathbf{1}_{m},g\rangle=\langle\chi_{1}+\chi_{2},g\rangle=1+\langle\chi_{2},g\rangle,

we have
\langle\chi_{2},g\rangle=1
.
The orientation character of the action of
g
on the
i
th surface factor is
(-1)^{\langle\chi_{i},g\rangle}
.
Therefore
\langle\chi_{i},g\rangle=1
means that
g
reverses the orientation of the
i
th surface factor.
∎

Combining the previous results gives the four-dimensional classification for products of two polygons.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
P
be a product of two polygons.
A small cover over
P
is symplectic if and only if it is factor-compatible.

Proof.

Let
M
be a small cover over
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
.
If one of
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
is equal to
3
, then Proposition
5.2
shows that no small cover over
P
is c-symplectic, hence none is symplectic.
On the other hand, Lemma
5.1
shows that no such small cover is factor-compatible.
Thus the equivalence holds in this case.
We may therefore assume
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 4
.

If the small cover is factor-compatible, then it is symplectic by Proposition
5.4
.

Conversely, assume that
M
is symplectic.
If
(m_{1},m_{2})=(4,4)
, then
M
is a real Bott manifold.
By
[
15
]
, c-symplecticity and symplecticity are equivalent for real Bott manifolds.
Since factor-compatibility is defined as c-symplecticity in this case, the claim follows.

Now assume
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(4,4)
.
If
M
is not factor-compatible, Lemma
5.6
would give an element of
\ker\lambda
reversing both surface factors.
Proposition
5.5
would then rule out symplectic structures on
M
, a contradiction.
Hence
M
is factor-compatible.
∎

Example 5.8
.

Let
P=P_{5}\times P_{4}
.
Consider the characteristic matrix

\lambda=\begin{pNiceArray}.

It is straightforward to check the characteristic condition.

Since
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{4}=\mathbf{1}_{9}\in\row(\lambda)
, the corresponding small cover
M=M(P,\lambda)
is orientable by Corollary
2.3
.
Moreover, the row space contains

\omega=F_{1,1}+F_{1,3}+F_{2,1}+F_{2,3}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}.

The induced full subcomplex is
K_{\omega}\cong S^{0}\ast S^{0}\cong S^{1}
, and hence
\widetilde{H}^{1}(K_{\omega};\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}
.
Therefore, by Proposition
2.5
,
M
is c-symplectic.

On the other hand, since the first factor has five sides, Lemma
5.1
gives
\chi_{1}\notin\row(\lambda)
.
Hence
M
is not factor-compatible.
Therefore Theorem
5.7
implies that
M
is not symplectic.

This example shows that the equivalence between c-symplecticity and symplecticity for real Bott manifolds
[
15
]
does not extend to small covers over products of two polygons.

6.
Smooth classification of factor-compatible small covers

In this section, we discuss the smooth type of a symplectic small covers
M
over a product
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
of two polygons.
First, we shall classify
M
up to D–J equivalence.

For an even integer
m
, set

\mathbf{1}_{m}=(1,\ldots,1)\quad\text{ and }\quad\varepsilon_{m}=(1,0,1,0,\ldots,1,0)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Every two-stage factor-compatible small cover is, after permuting the two polygon factors, and up to
D–J equivalence, represented by a characteristic matrix of the form

(6.1)

\lambda_{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}&0\\
\varepsilon_{m_{1}}&0\\
0&\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}\\
\beta&\varepsilon_{m_{2}}\end{pmatrix},

where
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m_{1}}
.

Proof.

By factor-compatibility, after elementary row operations, we may assume that the first and third rows are the factor weights
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
, respectively.
With respect to this choice of row basis, the second and fourth rows can be written as
\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{m_{1}}&\beta_{m_{2}}\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}\beta_{m_{1}}&\varepsilon_{m_{2}}\end{pmatrix}
, respectively.
Hence,

\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}}&0\\
\varepsilon_{m_{1}}&\beta_{m_{2}}\\
0&\mathbf{1}_{m_{2}}\\
\beta_{m_{1}}&\varepsilon_{m_{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Suppose both
\beta_{m_{2}}
and
\beta_{m_{1}}
are nonzero.

For adjacent pairs in
P_{m_{1}}
and
P_{m_{2}}
, set

A_{i}=\beta_{m_{1}}(F_{1,i})+\beta_{m_{1}}(F_{1,i+1})\qquad B_{j}=\beta_{m_{2}}(F_{2,j})+\beta_{m_{2}}(F_{2,j+1}).

At the vertex determined by
F_{1,i}\cap F_{1,i+1}\cap F_{2,j}\cap F_{2,j+1}
, the four corresponding columns must form a basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}
.
After adding the first of the two
P_{m_{1}}
-columns to the second, and the first of the two
P_{m_{2}}
-columns to the second, the determinant becomes

\det\begin{pmatrix}1&B_{j}\\
A_{i}&1\end{pmatrix}=1+A_{i}B_{j}.

Hence the characteristic condition forces
A_{i}B_{j}=0
for all
i,j
.
Therefore either
A_{i}=0
for all
i
, or
B_{j}=0
for all
j
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be even integers with
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 4
, and put
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
.
Up to D–J equivalence, the number of symplectic small covers over
P
is

2^{m_{1}-2}+2^{m_{2}-2}-1.

Proof.

A small cover over
P
is symplectic if and only if it is factor-compatible by Theorem
5.7
.
By Lemma
6.1
, the only off-diagonal datum is a class in
[\beta]\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m_{i}}/\langle\mathbf{1}_{m_{i}},\varepsilon_{m_{i}}\rangle
, so each direction contributes
2^{m_{i}-2}-1
nonzero classes, while the zero class gives the unique product case.
∎

Since each diagonal block is an orientable characteristic matrix over a polygon, the diagonal small covers over
P_{m_{1}}
and
P_{m_{2}}
are the orientable surfaces

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}=\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}_{sc}(m_{1})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}=\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}_{sc}(m_{2})},

respectively, where
\mathfrak{g}_{sc}(m)=\frac{m-2}{2}
.

On the other hand, Since
H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m_{1}}/\langle\mathbf{1}_{m_{1}},\varepsilon_{m_{1}}\rangle
, the class
[\beta]
may be regarded as an element
\alpha\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, where

\alpha=[\beta]\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

We denote by
M_{\alpha}
the small cover represented by (
6.1
).

Proposition 6.3
.

For fixed
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, the diffeomorphism type of
M_{\alpha}
depends only on whether
\alpha
is zero or nonzero.
More precisely,

M_{\alpha}\cong\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}\times\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}\quad\text{if }\alpha=0,

whereas all small covers
M_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\neq 0
are mutually diffeomorphic.

Proof.

If
\alpha=0
, then
\lambda_{\beta}
is D–J equivalent to the block diagonal matrix.
Thus
M_{\alpha}\cong\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}\times\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}
.

Now suppose that
\alpha\neq 0
.
The projection
P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}\to P_{m_{1}}
induces an equivariant bundle

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}\longrightarrow M_{\alpha}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}.

The off-diagonal row
\beta
records the monodromy of this bundle.
More precisely,
M_{\alpha}
is the associated bundle

M_{\alpha}\cong L_{\alpha}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2},

where
L_{\alpha}\to\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}
is the principal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-bundle classified by
\alpha\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, and the nontrivial element of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
acts on the fiber
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}
by the fixed involution induced by the second generator of the standard small cover over
P_{m_{2}}
.

It remains to show that all nonzero
\alpha
give diffeomorphic total spaces.
Let
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
be nonzero.
The mapping class group of
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}
acts transitively on the nonzero elements of
H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
Indeed, by Poincaré duality, nonzero cohomology classes correspond to nonzero mod
2
homology classes, and such classes can be represented by nonseparating simple closed curves.
The mapping class group is transitive on nonseparating simple closed curves.

Hence there exists a diffeomorphism
f\colon\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}\to\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}
such that
f^{\ast}\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
.
Therefore the principal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-bundle
L_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to
f^{\ast}L_{\alpha^{\prime}}
.
Taking the associated bundle with the same fiber action on
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}
gives a diffeomorphism

L_{\alpha}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}\cong L_{\alpha^{\prime}}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}.

Thus
M_{\alpha}\cong M_{\alpha^{\prime}}
for all nonzero
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
.
∎

We now show that the mod
2
cohomology ring detects the two cases in Proposition
6.3
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
R_{\alpha}=H^{\ast}(M_{\alpha};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, and let
q_{\alpha}\colon R_{\alpha}^{1}\to R_{\alpha}^{2}
be the square map on degree-one classes, that is,
q_{\alpha}(z)=z^{2}
. Then

\rank q_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}0,&\alpha=0,\\
m_{2}-2,&\alpha\neq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.

By (
2.1
), one can see that
u^{2}=0
for every
u\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, and
v^{2}=\alpha v
for every
v\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.

Since the coefficient field is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the square map on degree-one classes is linear.
If
z=u+v\in R_{\alpha}^{1}
, then

z^{2}=\alpha v.

Consequently, if
\alpha=0
, then
q_{\alpha}=0
.

Assume now that
\alpha\neq 0
. The image of
q_{\alpha}
is
\alpha\cdot H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
Apply again (
2.1
), one can see that multiplication by
\alpha
is injective on the fiber degree-one part, as desired.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be symplectic small covers over products of two polygons.
If

H^{\ast}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong H^{\ast}(M^{\prime};\mathbb{Z}_{2})

as graded rings, then
M
and
M^{\prime}
are diffeomorphic.

Proof.

Since products of two polgyons are distinct by their face numbers, they are decided by
H^{\ast}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
by (
2.2
).
Put
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
.
By Lemma
6.1
, we may write
M=M_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, where
M_{\alpha}
is represented by the matrix (
6.1
).
Then, the diffeomorphism type is determined by the nonzeroness of
\alpha
by Proposition
6.3
, and it is detected by the
H^{\ast}(M_{\alpha};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
by Lemma
6.4
.
If
\alpha\neq 0
, the rank
q
only depends on
m_{2}
.
Hence, one can even decide the which factor is base.

By (
2.2
), the graded mod
2
cohomology ring determines the
h
-vector of the orbit polytope.
For a product of two polygons
P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
, this
h
-vector determines the unordered pair
\{m_{1},m_{2}\}
.
Hence, after possibly interchanging the two factors, we may assume that both
M
and
M^{\prime}
lie over the same product
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
.

By Lemma
6.1
, after possibly interchanging the two factors, we may write
M=M_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, where
M_{\alpha}
is represented by the normal form (
6.1
).
Similarly, write
M^{\prime}=M_{\alpha^{\prime}}
.

By Proposition
6.3
, for fixed
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, the diffeomorphism type of
M_{\alpha}
depends only on whether

\alpha
is zero or nonzero.
By Lemma
6.4
, this condition is detected by the graded ring
H^{\ast}(M_{\alpha};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, namely by the rank of the square map on degree-one classes.
Thus
\alpha=0
if and only if
\alpha^{\prime}=0
.

If
\alpha=0
, then both manifolds are diffeomorphic to
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}\times\widetilde{\Sigma}_{2}
.
If
\alpha\neq 0
, then Lemma
6.4
gives
\rank(x\mapsto x^{2})=m_{\mathrm{fiber}}-2
so the graded ring also determines which factor is the base, except in the case
m_{1}=m_{2}
, where the distinction is irrelevant.
Proposition
6.3
then implies that all nonzero choices of
\alpha
give diffeomorphic total spaces.
Therefore
M
and
M^{\prime}
are diffeomorphic.
∎

The property that the diffeomorphism type of a small cover is determined by its mod
2
cohomology ring is called a
mod
2
cohomology rigidity
.
It does not hold for general small covers
[
22
]
, but it holds for some specific cases such as real Bott manifolds
[
16
,
7
]
.
See
[
8
]
for details.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be even integers with
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 4
, and put
P=P_{m_{1}}\times P_{m_{2}}
.
Up to diffeomorphism, the number of symplectic small covers over
P
is

\begin{cases}2,&m_{1}=m_{2},\\
3,&m_{1}\neq m_{2}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The zero off-diagonal class gives the product type, and for each ordered base-fiber choice all nonzero classes give one non-product type.
Two choices are identified when
m_{1}=m_{2}
, while for
m_{1}\neq m_{2}
they are distinguished by
\rank(x\mapsto x^{2})
.
∎

7.
Symplectic small covers beyond products of two polygons

In the previous sections, we proved that the flag condition is an important obstruction for four-dimensional small covers and real moment-angle complexes.
At present, however, we do not know any flag simplicial
3
-sphere
K
for which
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is not symplectic.
This suggests that flagness may be close to the right combinatorial condition.

On the other hand, explicit symplectic examples are still quite limited.
So far, the most accessible family comes from products of two polygons.
It is not clear a priori whether there exist symplectic small covers over flag spheres which are not dual to products of two polygons.
The goal of this section is to show that such examples do exist.

Our main tool is the following criterion for products with a circle.
For a closed oriented
3
-manifold
N
, the manifold
S^{1}\times N
is symplectic if and only if
N
fibers over
S^{1}
.
The “if” direction goes back to Thurston
[
29
]
, and the converse was proved by Friedl and Vidussi
[
12
]
.

This leads us naturally to the case

K=\partial(I\times Q)^{\ast},

where
Q
is a simple
3
-polytope.
Indeed,
\partial(I\times Q)^{\ast}=\partial I^{\ast}\join\partial Q^{\ast}
, and hence

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}\cong\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial I^{\ast}}\times\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial Q^{\ast}}\cong S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial Q^{\ast}}.

Therefore, if
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{\partial Q^{\ast}}
fibers over
S^{1}
, then
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is symplectic.

If
Q
is decomposable, then since
\dim Q=3
, it must be of the form
I\times P_{m}
for some polygon
P_{m}
.
In that case,
I\times Q\cong P_{4}\times P_{m}
, which is again a product of two polygons.
Thus, in order to find genuinely new examples, it is enough to consider the case where
Q
is indecomposable.

We begin with a brief review of the general background on fibrations of
3
-manifolds over the circle.
After that, we introduce a specific indecomposable simple
3
-polytope
Q
, let
L=\partial Q^{\ast}
, and construct a symplectic small cover over
I\times Q
.

We recall a standard way to prove that a closed
3
-manifold fibers over the circle.
Let
N
be a closed orientable irreducible
3
-manifold.
By Stallings’ fibering theorem
[
25
]
, if a primitive class
\phi\in H^{1}(N;\mathbb{Z})
has finitely generated kernel
\ker\phi\subset\pi_{1}(N)
, then
\phi
is represented by a fibration

N\to S^{1}.

Thus, in our setting, it is enough to construct such a class and prove that its kernel is finitely generated.

We shall use Bestvina–Brady Morse theory
[
3
]
for this purpose.
Let
L
be a flag simplicial
2
-sphere on the vertex set
[m]
, and let
\mu\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
be a mod
2
characteristic map over
L
.
Write
\mu_{i}:=\mu(e_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
for
i\in[m]
.
Let
N=M^{\mathbb{R}}(L,\mu)
be the corresponding orientable small cover.
We use the cubewise cell structure on
N
.
Its vertices are indexed by elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
, and the oriented edge in the
i
th direction at a vertex
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
goes from
g
to
g+\mu_{i}
.

Let
c
be an integral cubewise
1
-cocycle on
N
.
Assume that
c
is nonzero on every oriented edge and is affine on every square.
Equivalently, opposite parallel edges in each square have the same value under
c
.
Then
c
lifts to a cubewise Morse function on the universal cover of
N
and defines a homomorphism

[c]\colon\pi_{1}(N)\to\mathbb{Z}.

For each vertex
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
, define

P_{g}=\{i\mid c(g,i)>0\}\quad\text{ and }\quad N_{g}=\{i\mid c(g,i)<0\}.

The ascending and descending links at
g
are the induced subcomplexes
L_{P_{g}}
and
L_{N_{g}}
, respectively.

The following criterion is a standard consequence of Stallings’ fibering theorem and Bestvina–Brady Morse theory.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
L
be a flag simplicial
2
-sphere, and let

The small cover

Proof.
