Bohr phenomenon for certain integral operators and transforms in complex Banach spaces
Vasudevarao Allu, Raju Biswas, Rajib Mandal and Hiroshi Yanagihara

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate several Bohr radii associated with the Cesáro operator, Bernardi integral operator, β \beta -Cesáro operator, and discrete Fourier transform, all defined on a set of holomorphic mappings from the unit ball of a complex Banach space into the closure of the unit polydisc 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} within the space ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} .

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be a holomorphic mapping in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. The classical Bohr’s theorem states that if

|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for all}\quad|z|=r\leq 1/3.

(1.1)

The number
1/3
cannot be improved and is known as the Bohr radius (see
[
13
]
). The inequality (
1.1
) is known as the classical Bohr inequality.
In fact, Bohr’s
[
13
]
original proof of the inequality (
1.1
) was limited to the case when
r
was no greater than
1/6
. Subsequently, Wiener, Schur, and Riesz (see
[
42
,
43
]
) independently proved that the inequality (
1.1
) holds for
r\leq 1/3
.
Bohr’s and Wiener’s proofs can be found in
[
13
]
.

In the recent years, there has been a significant increase in research studies concerning Bohr-type theorems in various abstract settings, including refinements, ramifications, and extensions. For a detailed study on the Bohr radius in one complex variable, we refer to

,

,

]
and the references therein.
A natural question arises: “Is it possible to prove the Bohr-type inequality for certain complex integral operators and integral transforms defined on different function spaces?”
The idea was initially proposed in the context of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
for the classical Cesáro operator in
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
and for the Bernardi integral operator in
[
30
]
. The Cesáro operator is studied in
[
21
]
and is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}f(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}\;dt

(1.2)

for a holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.
Evidently,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}f(z)|=\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\quad\text{for each}\quad|z|=r<1.

For the Cesáro operator, Kayumov
et al.
[
25
]
have established the following Bohr-type inequality.

Theorem A
.

[
25
, Theorem 1, p. 616]
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq R_{1},

where
R_{1}(\approx 0.5335)
is the positive root of the equation
2x=3(1-x)\log(1/(1-x))
. The number
R_{1}
cannot be improved.

For a holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the Bernardi integral operator (see
[
36
, p. 11]
) is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\beta}f(z):=\frac{1+\beta}{z^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{z}f(\eta)\eta^{\beta-1}d\eta=(1+\beta)\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\beta+n}z^{n},

(1.3)

where
\beta>-m
and
m\geq 0
is an integer.
In 2021, Kumar and Sahoo
[
30
]
studied the following Bohr-type inequality for the Bernardi integral operator.

Theorem B
.

[
30
]
Let
\beta>-m
. If
f(z)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{\beta+n}r^{n}\leq\frac{r^{m}}{m+\beta}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq R_{2}(\beta),

where
R_{2}(\beta)
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{x^{m}}{m+\beta}-2\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n+\beta}=0.

The number
R_{2}(\beta)
cannot be improved.

Let
\left\{x_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
be a sequence of complex numbers. The discrete Fourier transform (see
[
11
, Chapter 6]
) is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x_{n})=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x_{n}e^{-2\pi ink/N}.

For a holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, we perform the discrete
Fourier transform on the coefficients
a_{k}
from
k=0
to
n
which gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f](z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}e^{-2\pi ink/(n+1)}\right)z^{n}.

To obtain the Bohr-type inequality, we denote the majorant series of
\mathcal{F}[f](z)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n},\quad\text{where}\quad|z|=r<1.

(1.4)

For the discrete Fourier transform, Ong and Ng
[
39
]
have established the following Bohr-type inequality.

Theorem C
.

[
39
]

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(r)\leq\frac{1}{1-r}\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

The constant
1/3
cannot be improved.

Motivated by Theorem A, Kumar and Sahoo
[
30
]
have considered the
\beta
-Cesáro operator (
\beta>0
), which is defined as

\mathcal{C}_{\beta}^{f}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}a_{k}\right)z^{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{(1-tz)^{\beta}}dt=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{f(t)}{(1-t)^{\beta}}dt

(1.5)

for a holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.
Indeed, Kumar and Sahoo
[
30
]
have established the following Bohr-type inequality for the
\beta
-Cesáro operator.

Theorem D
.

[
30
]

Let
0<\beta(\not=1)
. If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}\right)

for
|z|=r\leq R_{3}(\beta)
, where
R_{3}(\beta)
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{3\left(1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}\right)}{1-\beta}-\frac{2\left((1-r)^{-\beta}-1\right)}{\beta}=0.

The number
R_{3}(\beta)
cannot be improved.

In particular, if we put
f(t)\equiv 1
,
f(t)\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n}
and
f(t)\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}
in (
1.5
), then we obtain, respectively, the following relations:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\right)z^{n}=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\beta}}dt,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\right)z^{n}=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{t}{(1-t)^{\beta+1}}dt

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\right)z^{n}=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\beta+1}}dt.

(1.8)

From (
1.6
) and (
1.8
), we obtain the following identity

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}.

(1.9)

Before proceeding with the discussion, it is essential to introduce the requisite notations.
For
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
\|z\|_{\infty}=\max\limits_{j=1,2,\cdots,n}|z_{j}|
denote the maximum norm.
A ploydisc in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the product of discs. The open polydisc with center
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
and poly-radius
r=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})
will be denoted by
\mathbb{D}^{n}(\alpha;r)
and defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{n}(\alpha;r)=\left\{z=(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{j}-\alpha_{j}|<r_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n\right\}.

Let
\alpha=0=(0,0,\ldots,0)
and
r=1=(1,1,\ldots,1)
. Then,
\mathbb{D}^{n}(0;1)\equiv\mathbb{D}^{n}
denote the unit polydisc in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The set

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\mathbb{D}^{n}(\alpha;r):=\left\{z=(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{j}-\alpha_{j}|=r_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n\right\}

is contained within the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}(\alpha;r)
of
\mathbb{D}^{n}(\alpha;r)
and it is called the distinguished boundary of
\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}(\alpha;r)
.

. Kayumov and Ponnusamy

holomorphic functions with lacunary series in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}

In their study, Liu and Liu
[

made significant contributions to the field by extending the results of Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
24

to holomorphic mappings
f
with lacunary series from the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
into
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and from the unit ball
B
of a complex Banach space into
B
.
Lin
et al.

[
31
]
have studied other types of Bohr’s inequality for holomorphic mappings
f
with lacunary series from the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
into
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Let
X
and
Y
be complex Banach spaces. A mapping
P:X\to Y
is called a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k\in\mathbb{N}
if there exists a
k
-linear mapping

u:X^{k}\to Y
such that
P(x)=u(x,x,\ldots,x)
for every
x\in X
. Note that if
P
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
, then
P(\mu x)=\mu^{k}P(x)
for all
x\in X
and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
.

In this paper, the degree of a homogeneous polynomial is denoted by a subscript. Note that if
P_{k}
is a
k
-homogeneous polynomial from
X
into
Y
, there exists a unique symmetric
k
-linear mapping
u
such that
P_{k}(x)=u(x,...,x)
(see
[
37
, Chapter 1 and 2]
). For a domain
\mathcal{D}\subset X
, let
F
be a holomorphic mapping from
\mathcal{D}
into
Y
. For
z\in\mathcal{D}
,
let
D^{k}F(z)
denote the
k
-th Fréchet derivative of
F
at
z
. If
\mathcal{D}
contains the origin, then
any holomorphic mapping
F:\mathcal{D}\to Y
can be expanded into the series

\displaystyle F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}D^{k}F(0)(z^{k}),

(1.10)

for all
z
in some neighborhood of the origin (see
[
16
, Chapter 6, p. 197]
). Since
(1/k!)D^{k}F(0)(z^{k})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
, we use the notation
P_{k}(z)=(1/k!)D^{k}F(0)(z^{k})
all along this paper. Note that if
\mathcal{D}
is a bounded balanced domain in a complex Banach space
X
and
F(\mathcal{D})
is bounded, then (
1.10
) converges uniformly on
r\mathcal{D}
for each
r\in(0,1)
.

Let
L(X,\mathbb{C})
denote the set of continuous linear operators from
X
into
\mathbb{C}
. For each
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
, let

\displaystyle T(x)=\left\{l_{x}\in L(X,\mathbb{C}):l_{x}(x)=\|x\|,~\|l_{x}\|=1\right\},

where
\|l_{x}\|=\sup\{|l_{x}(w)|:\|w\|=1\}
.
In view of the Hahn-Banach theorem,
T(x)
is nonempty.
The following question naturally arises:

Question 1.1
.

Let
B_{X}
be the unit ball of the complex Banach space
X
. Is it possible to establish the Bohr-type inequalities for the Cesáro operator, Bernardi integral operator,
\beta
-Cesáro operator
(\beta\not=1)
, and discrete Fourier transform defined on a set of holomorphic mappings
F:B_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
?

This paper primarily provides an affirmative answer to Question
1.1
.

2.
Main results

The following are key lemmas of this paper and will be used to prove the main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
15
]
If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(z)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{(1-|z|)^{n-1}(1-|z|^{2})}\quad\text{and}\quad|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1,\;|z|<1.

Before proceeding to the main result, it is necessary to establish the majorant series corresponding to the Cesáro operator for a holomorphic mapping
F:B_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
with

\displaystyle F(z)=a+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}P_{s}(z),\quad z\in B_{X},

where
P_{s}(z)=(1/s!)D^{s}F(0)(z^{s})
and
F(z)=(F_{1}(z),F_{2}(z),\ldots,F_{n}(z))
.
We consider the mapping
F_{j}:B_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
defined by

\displaystyle F_{j}(z)=a_{j}+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}(P_{s})_{j}(z),\quad\text{where}\quad z\in B_{X}\quad\text{and}\quad j\in\{1,2\ldots,n\}.

In view of the relation (
1.2
), we consider the following integral for the holomorphic mapping
F_{j}
with values in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F_{j}(tz)}{1-tz}\;dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{a_{j}}{1-tz}\;dt+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}(P_{s})_{j}(z)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{s}}{1-tz}\;dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{j}\left(1+\frac{z}{2}+\frac{z^{2}}{3}+\cdots\right)+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}(P_{s})_{j}(z)\int_{0}^{1}t^{s}\left(1+tz+t^{2}z^{2}+\cdots\right)\;dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}z^{n}}{n+1}+(P_{1})_{j}(z)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{3}+\frac{z^{2}}{4}+\cdots\right)+(P_{2})_{j}(z)\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{z}{4}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{z^{2}}{5}+\cdots\right)+(P_{3})_{j}(z)\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{z}{5}+\frac{z^{2}}{6}+\cdots\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}z^{n}}{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}(P_{1})_{j}(z)+\frac{1}{3}\left((P_{2})_{j}(z)+z(P_{1})_{j}(z)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left((P_{3})_{j}(z)+z^{2}(P_{2})_{j}(z)+z^{3}(P_{1})_{j}(z)\right)+\cdots.

Therefore, the majorant series corresponding to the Cesáro operator for a holomorphic mapping
F_{j}
is as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{j}|\|z\|^{n}}{n+1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|z\|^{k-1}\left|\left(P_{n-k+1}\right)_{j}(z)\right|\right).

As
\|P_{s}(z)\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|(P_{s})_{j}(z)|
, thus, the majorant series corresponding to the Cesáro operator for a holomorphic mapping
F:B_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{F}(\|z\|):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|a\|_{\infty}\|z\|^{n}}{n+1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|z\|^{k-1}\left\|\left(P_{n-k+1}\right)(z)\right\|_{\infty}\right).

In the following result, we establish the Bohr-type inequality for the Cesáro operator defined on a set of holomorphic mappings
F:B_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
B_{X}
be the unit ball of a complex Banach space
X
. Let
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\in\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
and let
F:B_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
be a holomorphic mapping with

\displaystyle F(z)=a+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}P_{s}(z),\quad z\in B_{X},

where
P_{s}(z)=(1/s!)D^{s}F(0)(z^{s})
. Assume that
|a_{j}|=\|a\|_{\infty}
for all
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{F}(\|z\|)\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\quad\text{for}\quad\|z\|=r\leq R,

(2.1)

where
R\in(0,1)
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle 3(1-r)\log\frac{1}{1-r}-2r=0.

The number
R
cannot be improved.

Proof.

Let
z_{0}\in\partial B_{X}
be fixed. Since
F=(F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{n})
, we consider
f_{j}(t)=F_{j}(tz_{0})
for
t\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f_{j}\in H(\mathbb{D},\overline{\mathbb{D}})
and

\displaystyle f_{j}(t)=a_{j}+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}(P_{s})_{j}(z_{0})t^{s},\quad t\in\mathbb{D}.

In view of Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle|(P_{s})_{j}(z_{0})|\leq 1-|a_{j}|^{2}=1-\|a\|^{2}_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad s\geq 1

for each fixed
j

(j=1,2,\cdots)
.
Since
\|P_{s}(z_{0})\|_{\infty}=\max\limits_{j}|(P_{s})_{j}(z_{0})|
, thus, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{s}(z_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq 1-\|a\|^{2}_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad s\geq 1.

(2.2)

Let
x=\|a\|_{\infty}\in[0,1]
. It is evident that, if
x=1
, then
P_{s}(z_{0})=0
for
s\geq 1
. Therefore, the inequality (
2.1
) holds for
z=rz_{0}
and
r\in[0,1)
. Thus,
we may assume that
x\in[0,1)
. Using (
2.2
), for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x}{n+1}\|rz_{0}\|^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|rz_{0}\|^{k-1}\|P_{n-k+1}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left((1-x^{2})\sum_{k=1}^{n}r^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}+(1-x^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{nr^{n}}{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}+(1-x^{2})\left(\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\right):=G(x,r).

Differentiating partially
G(x,r)
twice with respect to
x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G(x,r)=\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}-2x\left(\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\right)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}G(x,r)=-2\left(\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\leq 0,

which shows that
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G(x,r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
x\in[0,1)
and it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G(x,r)\geq\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G(x,r)=\frac{1}{r(1-r)}\left(-2r+3(1-r)\log\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\geq 0

for
r\leq R
, where
R(\approx 0.533589)\in(0,1)
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle G_{1}(r):=-2r+3(1-r)\log\frac{1}{1-r}.

Figure 1.
The graph of
G_{1}(r)
in (0, 1)

Thus,
G(x,r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
x\in[0,1)
for
r\leq R
, and it follows that

\displaystyle G(x,r)\leq\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}G(x,r)=\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\quad\text{for all}\quad r\leq R.

To prove the sharpness of the constant
R
, we consider the function

\displaystyle F_{1}(z)=f(l_{z_{0}}(z))u,\quad z\in B_{X},

where
z_{0}\in\partial B_{X}
,
l_{z_{0}}\in T(z_{0})
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n})\in\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
|u_{1}|=|u_{2}|=\dots=|u_{n}|=1
, and

\displaystyle f(t)=\frac{\lambda-t}{1-\lambda t},\quad t\in\mathbb{D},~\lambda\in(0,1).

Then, we have

\displaystyle F_{1}(rz_{0})=f\left(l_{z_{0}}(rz_{0})\right)u=f\left(r\;l_{z_{0}}(z_{0})\right)u=f(r)u=a+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}P_{s}(rz_{0}),

where
a=\lambda u
and
P_{s}(z_{0})=\lambda^{s-1}(\lambda^{2}-1)u
for
s\geq 1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\lambda u\|_{\infty}}{n+1}\|rz_{0}\|^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|rz_{0}\|^{k-1}\|P_{n-k+1}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{r}\log{\frac{1}{1-r}}+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda^{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{r}\log{\frac{1}{1-r}}+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\cdot\frac{1-\lambda^{n}}{1-\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{r}\log{\frac{1}{1-r}}+(1+\lambda)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}-\frac{(\lambda r)^{n}}{n+1}\right)\hskip 85.35826pt

(2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\log{\frac{1}{1-r}}+\frac{2\lambda}{r}\log{\frac{1}{1-r}}-\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda r}\log{\frac{1}{1-\lambda r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\log{\frac{1}{1-r}}+(1-\lambda)\frac{3(1-r)\log(1-r)+2r}{r(1-r)}+D_{\lambda}(r),

where

\displaystyle D_{\lambda}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3-\lambda}{r}\log{\frac{1}{1-r}}-\frac{2(1-\lambda)}{1-r}-\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda r}\log{\frac{1}{1-\lambda r}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{3-\lambda}{n}-2(1-\lambda)-\frac{(1+\lambda)\lambda^{n-1}}{n}\right)r^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left((1-\lambda)^{2}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad\lambda\to 1^{-}.

Furthermore, it is evident that for

\displaystyle\frac{3(1-r)\log(1-r)+2r}{r(1-r)}>0.

(2.5)

From (
2.3
), (

cannot be improved. This completes the proof.

Theorem 2.2
.

X

\beta>-m

\displaystyle\sum_{s=m}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|P_{s}(z)\right\|_{\infty}}{\beta+s}\leq\frac{r^{m}}{m+\beta}

(2.6)

for

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta+m}-2\sum_{s=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta+s}r^{s-m}=0.

Proof.

z_{0}\in\partial B_{X}

\displaystyle f_{j}(t)=t^{m}\left((P_{m})_{j}(z_{0})+\sum_{s=m+1}^{\infty}(P_{s})_{j}(z_{0})t^{s-m}\right),\quad t\in\mathbb{D}.

g_{j}(t)=(P_{m})_{j}(z_{0})+\sum_{s=m+1}^{\infty}(P_{s})_{j}(z_{0})t^{s-m}
for

g_{j}(t)\in H(\mathbb{D},\overline{\mathbb{D}})
.
In view of Lemma
2.1
, for each fixed
j

(j=1,2,\cdots,n)

\displaystyle|(P_{s})_{j}(z_{0})|\leq 1-|(P_{m})_{j}(z_{0})|^{2}=1-\|P_{m}(z_{0})\|^{2}_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad s\geq m+1.

\displaystyle\|P_{s}(z_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq 1-\|P_{m}(z_{0})\|^{2}_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad s\geq m+1.

\displaystyle\frac{\|P_{m}(z_{0})\|_{\infty}}{\beta+m}r^{m}+(1-\|P_{m}(z_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2})\sum_{s=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta+s}r^{s}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G_{2}(x,r)=\frac{1}{\beta+m}r^{m}-2x\sum_{s=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta+s}r^{s}

and

.
Therefore,

is a monotonically increasing function of

for

and it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=m}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|P_{s}(rz_{0})\right\|_{\infty}}{\beta+s}\leq G_{2}(x,r)\leq\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}G_{2}(x,r)=\frac{1}{m+\beta}r^{m}\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq R(\beta).

To prove the sharpness of the constant

\displaystyle F_{2}(z)=f(l_{z_{0}}(z))u,\quad z\in B_{X},

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\beta+m}r^{m}+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{s=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{s-1}}{\beta+s}r^{s}

R_{\beta}\in(0,1)
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{3\left(1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}\right)}{1-\beta}-\frac{2\left((1-r)^{-\beta}-1\right)}{\beta}=0.

Thus,
G_{6}(x,r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
x\in[0,1)
for
r\leq R_{\beta}
and it follows that

\displaystyle G_{6}(x,r)\leq\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}G_{6}(x,r)=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq R_{\beta}.

To prove the sharpness of the constant
R_{\beta}
, we consider the function

\displaystyle F_{4}(z)=f(l_{z_{0}}(z))u,\quad z\in B_{X},

where
z_{0}\in\partial B_{X}
,
l_{z_{0}}\in T(z_{0})
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n})\in\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
|u_{1}|=|u_{2}|=\dots=|u_{n}|=1
, and

\displaystyle f(t)=\frac{\lambda-t}{1-\lambda t},\quad t\in\mathbb{D},~\lambda\in(0,1).

Then, we have
F_{4}(rz_{0})=f(l_{z_{0}}(rz_{0}))u=a+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}P_{s}(rz_{0})
,
where
a=\lambda u
and
P_{s}(z_{0})=\lambda^{s-1}(\lambda^{2}-1)u
for
s\geq 1
.
Using (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), we have

\displaystyle\|\lambda u\|_{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\|rz_{0}\|^{n}\hskip 85.35826pt

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(k-1+\beta)}{\Gamma(k)\Gamma(\beta)}\|rz_{0}\|^{k-1}\|P_{n-k+1}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\beta)}r^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(k-1+\beta)}{\Gamma(k)\Gamma(\beta)}r^{n}\lambda^{n-k}(1-\lambda^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\beta)}r^{n}+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(k-1+\beta)}{\Gamma(k)\Gamma(\beta)}\lambda^{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\beta}}dt+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\lambda^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{r}\left(\frac{1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}\right)+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\lambda^{k-1}\right)

(2.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}\right)-\frac{1-\lambda}{r}\left(\frac{1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\lambda^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}\right)-\frac{1-\lambda}{r}\left(\frac{3\left(1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}\right)}{1-\beta}-\frac{2\left((1-r)^{-\beta}-1\right)}{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle+G_{7}(\lambda,r),

where

\displaystyle G_{7}(\lambda,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda}{r}\left(\frac{2\left(1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}\right)}{1-\beta}-\frac{2\left((1-r)^{-\beta}-1\right)}{\beta}\right)\hskip 113.81102pt

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\lambda^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1-\lambda)\left(\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\beta}}dt-\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\beta+1}}dt\right)

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\lambda^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1-\lambda)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\beta)}r^{n}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\right)r^{n}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-\lambda^{2})}{\lambda}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\lambda^{k}-\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(-\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}}{\lambda}\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\beta)}-2(1-\lambda)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{(1-\lambda^{2})}{\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\lambda^{k}\right)r^{n}.

Using the identity (
1.9
) and letting
\lambda\to 1^{-}
, we have

\displaystyle G_{7}(\lambda,r)=O\left((1-\lambda)^{2}\right).

(2.17)

Furthermore, it is evident that for
r>R_{\beta}
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\frac{3\left(1-(1-r)^{1-\beta}\right)}{1-\beta}-\frac{2\left((1-r)^{-\beta}-1\right)}{\beta}<0.

(2.18)

From (
2.16
), (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), we conclude that
R_{\beta}
cannot be improved. This completes the proof.
∎
