Linker-directed lipid-antigen conjugates co-enhance lymph node targeting and antigen presentation for potent T cell immunity
Authors: Junfei Zhu, Licheng Bai, Chun Wang, Jiating Liu, Yuhua Chen, Wenxia Zhang, Jun Weng, Lin Wei, Zigong Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe clinical efficacy of peptide-based cancer vaccines is limited by inefficient lymphatic delivery and suboptimal antigen presentation. Here, we report a rationally engineered class of lipid-antigen conjugates that co-enhance lymph node (LN) targeting and antigen-presenting cell (APC) uptake through molecular-level structural optimization. By systematically varying lipid tail composition and linker chemistry, we identify a lead construct (dOA-K-O) incorporating a dioleic acid (dOA) lipid tail and an L-lysine (K) linker, which outperforms the clinically used DSPE-PEG2000 conjugate (DSPE-O). Mechanistically, the positively charged L-lysine linker promotes albumin binding while reducing excessive self-assembly, enhancing lymphatic trafficking and facilitating APC internalization. In vivo, dOA-K-O elicits robust antigen cross-presentation and induces an 8-fold increase in antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses compared to unmodified antigenic peptide and 3-fold higher than DSPE-O. This enhanced cellular immunity translates into marked tumor suppression in both prophylactic and therapeutic B16-OVA melanoma models. Our results establish a design paradigm in which linker chemistry and lipid tail composition are synergistically optimized to boost the immunogenicity of peptide vaccines for cancer immunotherapy, which may provide a chemical and structural basis for the optimization of peptide-based vaccine delivery systems.Keywords: Lipid-antigen conjugates, Lymph node targeting, Vaccine delivery, Cellular immunity, Albumin hitchhiking

Introduction

Peptide-based neoantigen vaccines offer a convenient and safe strategy for eliciting antigen-specific T cell responses and show great clinical application prospects in personalized tumor immunotherapy [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]]. Despite clinical successes in priming cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), there remains substantial room for improvement in generating robust and durable T-cell immunity in vivo due to modest lymph node (LN) targeting and suboptimal antigen presentation [[6], [7], [8], [9]]. Indeed, to trigger effective T cell responses, antigenic peptide must be efficiently internalized by the key antigen-presenting cells (APCs) upon reaching LNs, proteolytically processed, and subsequently presented to specific T cells via major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules [[10], [11], [12]]. Therefore, an ideal neoantigen vaccine delivery system must coordinate both efficient LN targeting and effective antigen presentation by APCs.

Under the premise of LN targeting, synthetic nanovaccines have been engineered to facilitate LN trafficking by tuning physicochemical properties such as particle size, surface charge, and lipophilicity [[13], [14], [15], [16]]. However, vehicle properties that favor LN drainage often hinder APC internalization [17,18]. For instance, PEGylation can enhance interstitial mobility but suppresses phagocytic uptake by APCs [[19], [20], [21], [22]]. Similarly, positively charged carriers may improve cellular internalization but risk entrapment in the interstitial matrix, reducing lymphatic transport [[23], [24], [25]]. In this context, although ligand modification on the carrier surface can promote the cascade responses over specific timespans to a certain extent, this is usually achieved through sophisticated delivery system structure design, which is not conducive to clinical translation [[26], [27], [28]]. In contrast, lipid-antigen conjugates that target draining LNs using an in vivo albumin hitchhiking strategy offer a chemically defined and clinically translatable platform for LN-targeted vaccine delivery [[29], [30], [31], [32], [33], [34]]. Upon interstitial administration, the conjugated lipid tail can non-covalently bind to endogenous albumin, promoting improved pharmacokinetics and lymphatic transport of antigenic peptides [[35], [36], [37]]. Moreover, this conjugation does not interfere with the antigen-processing machinery of APCs [38,39].

The lipid tails with different chemical structures have different binding affinities to albumin, resulting in different accumulation amounts in LNs [40,41]. Beyond the lipid tail, the chemical linker connecting the lipid tail to the antigen backbone has a profound effect on albumin binding and in vivo half-life as well as the self-assembly behavior of amphiphilic vaccine constructs [[42], [43], [44]]. Clinically approved peptide therapeutics highlight the impact of linker chemistry on systemic exposure and efficacy. For example, the L-γ-glutamoyl (L-γGlu) linker in zilucoplan (approved for generalized myasthenia gravis) and the butanoyl-sulfonamide linker in the long-acting human growth hormone somapacitan illustrate how tailored linker designs enhance pharmacokinetics and bioactivity [[45], [46], [47], [48]]. Despite this, few studies have systematically investigated how linker chemistry influences lymphatic transport and APC uptake of lipid-antigen conjugates. Building on these insights, we hypothesized that rational optimization of lipid structure, especially orchestration of the linker, could improve systemic and cell-level pharmacokinetics of lipid-antigen conjugates, achieving co-enhanced lymphatic trafficking and APC uptake, leading to robust antigen-specific CD8+ T cell priming.

Herein, we designed and fabricated a series of lipid-antigen conjugates to identify structural features that co-enhance LN targeting activity and antigen presentation in vivo. These lipid moieties systematically varied the charge properties and length of the linker, the number and the degree of unsaturation of the fatty acid (Fig. 1). To evaluate the structural optimization of the lipid moiety in order to enhance the immune response of the peptide vaccine by simultaneously promoting LN targeting and APC internalization, we selected the MHC-I restricted short peptide OVA257-264 (SIINFEKL), which has poor antigen presentation ability, as the model antigen [49,50]. The epitope peptide was conjugated to lipid moieties through an N-terminal extended cysteine residue (CSIINFEKL) [51,52], and then the LN targeting and APC uptake of each conjugate were evaluated in vitro and in vivo. The evaluation of whole-body distribution, pharmacokinetics, and uptake patterns by APCs, along with the evocation of the adaptive immunity, indicated that the linker structure of lipid-antigen conjugates was crucial for improving their drainage through the lymphatic system, and this improved targeting ability was influenced by their lipid tail as well. Among the variants, the optimized construct incorporating a dioleic acid lipid tail and an L-lysine linker (denoted as dOA-K-O) showed superior LN accumulation, efficient APC internalization, and sustained antigen retention for over 5 days in LNs. Structure-function analyses reveal that the positively charged L-lysine linker enhances albumin binding and reduces micelle formation, thereby facilitating lymphatic transport and biodistribution of amphiphilic vaccine constructs. In vivo, dOA-K-O induced 8-fold greater antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses compared to free OVA257-264 and 3-fold higher than commercially available 1,2-distearoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine-N-PEG2000 (DSPE-PEG2000) conjugated counterpart (denoted as DSPE-O), significantly delaying tumor growth in both prophylactic and therapeutic B16-OVA melanoma models. Collectively, our findings demonstrate that structural optimization of lipid-antigen conjugates, especially orchestration of the linker, provides a powerful design axis to control antigen trafficking, cellular uptake, and immunogenicity. This approach offers a broadly applicable strategy for enhancing peptide vaccine performance against cancer and infectious diseases.

Materials and methods

Materials

OVA257-264 (CSIINFEKL) was purchased from GL Biochem Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Maleimide (polyethylene glycol) Carboxylic acid (Mal-PEG2000-COOH) and 1,2-distearoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine-N-PEG2000 amphiphile (DSPE) were purchased from Shanghai Ponsure Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). 1,1′-Dioctadecyl-3,3,3′,3′-Tetramethylindodicarbocyanine, 4-Chlorobenzenesulfonate Salt (DiD) was purchased from Beyotime Biotech Inc. (Shanghai, China). Oleoyl chloride and 5-Carboxyfluorescein N-succinimidyl ester were purchased from Energy-Chemical (Shanghai, China). Amino acids and other reagents used for the synthesis of small molecules were purchased from Bidepharm (Shanghai, China). All chemicals were directly used without any further purification. GM-CSF protein (mouse, HY-P7361A) and IL-4 protein (mouse) were purchased from MedChemExpress LLC (Shanghai, China). Antibodies used in this study are listed in Table S1.

Animals and cell lines

SPF grade female C57BL/6 mice aged 6-8 weeks were purchased from the Hubei Laboratory Animal Research Center (Wuhan, China). Animals were randomly allocated to experimental groups prior to treatment. Tumor volume measurements were performed using standardized caliper measurements. No formal blinding was used during outcome assessment. Mice were housed in a stable 12/12 h day/night environment with free access to food and water and allowed to acclimate to the new environment for at least one week before the start of the experiments. B16-OVA (cat. CTCC-007-0623) cell lines were purchased from Zhejiang Meisen Cell Technology Co., Ltd and cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM) medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin solution at 37 °C in 5% CO2.

In vitro cellular uptake

To evaluate the lipid-antigen conjugates uptake by BMDCs, cells were incubated with fluorescein-labeled conjugates for 4 h. Then cells were washed three times with PBS and incubated with DiD (10 μg/mL) for 30 min at 37 °C to stain cell membrane. Finally, the cells were washed three times with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 20 min before observing the internalization by BMDCs using confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM, Leica TCS SP8).

In vitro cross-presentation and DCs maturation assay

To evaluate DC activation, after 24 h of incubation with the lipid-antigen conjugates (0.5 μM), the cells were collected and stained by antibody cocktail (anti-CD11c, CD80, CD86 and SIINFEKL-H-2Kb antibodies) for 25 min at 4 °C, washed once and fixed with 4% PFA for further FACS analysis.

For the studies of intracellular processing pathways of lipid-antigen conjugates, BMDCs were treated with dOA-K-O with or without various inhibitors (20 μM chloroquine, 15 μM MG132, 100 μM ERAP1-IN-1 or 15 μM brefeldin A) for 12 h. After treatment, cells were collected and stained by antibody cocktail (anti-CD11c and SIINFEKL-H-2Kb antibodies) for 25 min at 4 °C, washed once and fixed with 4% PFA for further FACS analysis.

Lymph node imaging

The mice were subcutaneously injected with fluorescein-labeled lipid-antigen conjugates (100 nmol, 100 μL) on both sides of the tail base. At the indicated time points, mice were euthanized, and the inguinal and axillary LNs, heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney were harvested, followed by ex vivo imaging by IVIS Spectrum Imaging System (PerkinElmer, Ex: 465 nm, Em: 520 nm). A region of interest tool was used to measure the radiant efficiency from each lymph node. Identical ROI sizes, exposure parameters, and analysis settings were applied to all samples, and background-subtracted total radiant efficiency was used for quantitative comparison.

Bio-layer interferometry (BLI) measurements

The binding affinity of lipid-antigen conjugates to albumin was measured by an Octet® R8 protein analysis system (Sartorius) at 25 °C. The lipid-antigen conjugates was first biotinylated with Biotin-PEG4-NHS ester (Bidepharm, Shanghai, China), then diluted to 1 mg/mL in PBS and loaded on a streptavidin-coated sensor tip for 600 s. Biosensor association to human serum albumin in PBS was measured for 150 s, followed by measurements of dissociation for 90 s. The binding values were measured using Octet Analysis Studio v.13.0.1.35.

Immunization

C57BL/6 mice (6-8 weeks) were vaccinated with lipid-antigen conjugates (100 nmol OVA257-264 peptide equivalents) in PBS on days 0,7 and 14. All vaccines were injected in a volume of 100 μL, half on each side at the base of the tail. On the 21st day, the mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation, and the spleen, lymph nodes and peripheral blood of mice in each group were collected for flow cytometry analysis.

IFNγ+ T cells in the spleen

For detection of IFNγ+ T cells in spleen, splenocytes (1 × 106 cells per well) were restimulated with OVA257-264 peptide (10 μg/mL) for 8 h in the presence of brefeldin A (10 μg/mL) and monensin (2 μM). Cells were then stained for surface markers (CD4, CD8), fixed, permeabilized, and stained intracellularly with anti-IFNγ antibody before flow cytometric analysis.

DC activation in LNs

To analyze activated DC in LNs, inguinal LNs were removed and ground to single cell suspensions on the 7th day after the third vaccination. The collected LN cells were labeled with antibody cocktail (anti-CD11c, MHC-II, CD86 and SIINFEKL-H-2Kb antibodies) for 25 min at 4 °C, washed twice and fixed with 4% PFA for further FACS analysis.

Prophylactic and therapeutic studies on B16-OVA tumor model

For the prophylactic study, four groups of mice (6-7 mice per group) were respectively vaccinated with (1) PBS, (2) OVA257-264, (3) DSPE-O, and (4) dOA-K-O (100 nmol OVA257-264 peptide equivalents in each group), one week apart, for a total of three inoculations, with 100 μL injected subcutaneously at the base of the tail, half on each side. Seven days after the final vaccination, the vaccinated mice were subcutaneously challenged with 1 x 106 B16-OVA cells. The tumor volumes were measured with vernier caliper every two days and calculated as follows: tumor volume (V) = a × b2/2, where a represents the major axis, and b represents the minor axis of the tumor. Mice were euthanized when the tumor volume reached 1000 mm3.

For the therapeutic study, female C57BL/6 mice (6-8 weeks old) were subcutaneously injected with 2 × 106 B16-OVA cells. On day 5 post tumor inoculation, when established tumors were palpable, mice were randomly divided into four groups (6-7 mice per group) and subcutaneously vaccinated with (1) PBS, (2) OVA257-264, (3) DSPE-O, and (4) dOA-K-O (100 nmol OVA257-264 peptide equivalents in each group) on days 5, 12 and 19 post tumor cell inoculation. The tumor volumes were measured with vernier caliper and calculated as follows: tumor volume (V) = a × b2/2, where a represents the major axis, and b represents the minor axis of the tumor. Mice were euthanized when the tumor volume reached 1500 mm3.

Results and discussion

L-lysine linker facilitated LN targeting

To investigate how linkers and lipid tails influence LN targeting, we synthesized a panel of lipid-antigen conjugates differing in linker chemistry and lipid tail structure (Fig. 2a; for synthesis details see Scheme S1 to S9). As a reference, we included a previously reported conjugate using 1,2-distearoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine-N-PEG2000 amphiphile (DSPE-PEG2000) (Fig. 1a). Following conjugation with OVA257-264 epitope peptide (Figs. S1–S7), lipid-antigen conjugates are labeled with fluorescein and inoculated subcutaneously at the base of the tail in mice.

We first evaluated LN accumulation at 24 h post-injection. Compared with the unmodified antigenic peptide OVA257-264, lipid-antigen conjugates showed varying degrees of increased accumulation in inguinal LNs. Notably, conjugates containing chemical linkers showed significantly higher fluorescence intensity than the linker-free construct (dOA-O), indicating an important role of linker chemistry in promoting efficient LN targeting (Fig. 2b). In particular, the conjugate containing the L-lysine linker (dOA-K-O) accumulated the most in inguinal LNs, with fluorescence intensity 6-fold higher than that of the L-γGlu linker conjugate (dOA-E-O) and 2-fold higher than that of DSPE-O (Fig. 2c). Interestingly, increasing the number of linker residues (e.g., dOA-3E-O or dOA-3K-O) led to diminished LN accumulation, suggesting that linker bulk or charge density may interfere with lymphatic trafficking.

In the axillary LNs, dOA-K-O also showed the highest fluorescence signal among all tested groups, although consistently lower than that observed in inguinal LNs, reflecting lymphatic drainage gradients (Fig. 2d and e). To assess off-target distribution, we examined fluorescence in major organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney) at 24 h post-injection. With the exception of weak renal fluorescence, systemic biodistribution was minimal across all constructs (Fig. S8), indicating favorable LN specificity of the conjugates.

To further elucidate the behavioral characteristics of lymphatic transport, we performed a time-resolved analysis of LN accumulation at early time points (2, 6, and 12 h) following subcutaneous injection. Distinct kinetic profiles were observed among the different constructs (Fig. 2f and g). Notably, dOA-K-O exhibited both rapid lymphatic access and sustained accumulation, reaching substantial LN levels as early as 2 h and maintaining high signals over time. In contrast, dOA-O showed no appreciable accumulation at any time point, indicating extremely low lymphatic transport efficiency. The dOA-E-O and DSPE-O groups displayed intermediate behaviors, characterized by slower accumulation and reduced overall retention. These findings suggest that linker chemistry not only governs the magnitude of LN accumulation but also critically regulates lymphatic transport kinetics, including the balance between drainage efficiency and retention within the LN microenvironment.

Dioleic acid lipid tail promotes LN targeting and intranodal APC accessibility

Next, we examined whether the number and saturation level of C18 lipid tails influenced LN targeting. Using dOA-K-O as the base structure, we synthesized two analogs: dSA-K-O (saturated stearic acid) and dLA-K-O (polyunsaturated linoleic acid) (Fig. 3a). To investigate the impact of tail number, we also generated single-tail variants (OA-K-O, SA-K-O, and LA-K-O), which are expected to exhibit higher hydrophilicity and weaker self-assembly. Ex vivo fluorescence imaging of inguinal LNs revealed that all single-tail variants showed reduced LN accumulation compared to their double-tail counterparts, confirming the importance of amphiphilic structure in facilitating lymphatic transport (Fig. 3b and c). Among all tested constructs, dOA-K-O bearing two oleic acid chains exhibited the highest fluorescence intensity in draining LNs. Notably, dSA-K-O also showed slightly higher LN fluorescence than DSPE-O, indicating that linker optimization remains a major determinant of lymphatic transport across different lipid-tail chemistries.

Given its superior LN targeting performance, we next investigated the retention and intranodal distribution of dOA-K-O. Time-course fluorescence imaging demonstrated progressive accumulation of dOA-K-O within draining inguinal LNs, reaching maximal levels at approximately 48 h post-injection, with robust fluorescence still detectable at 120 h (Fig. 3d and e), indicating sustained LN retention. To further clarify intranodal localization and APC accessibility, we analyzed LN cryosections after vaccination using immunofluorescence staining for CD11c+ DCs, F4/80+ macrophages, and CD45+ immune cells (Fig. 3f). At 24 h post-immunization, free OVA showed minimal signal within draining LNs, whereas dOA-K-O generated strong fluorescence predominantly localized near the LN periphery. By 72 h, dOA-K-O fluorescence became more broadly distributed throughout the LN parenchyma, accompanied by increased overlap with APC-rich immune compartments. Quantitative image analysis demonstrated an increase in FITC+CD11c+ signal within draining LNs from 8.87% at 24 h to 13.69% at 72 h, supporting progressive accessibility of the conjugate to APC populations over time. Together, these findings indicate that the dioleic acid lipid tail not only enhances LN accumulation and retention but also promotes intranodal distribution and APC access, thereby providing a favorable spatial basis for efficient antigen presentation and downstream T-cell priming.

L-Lysine linker enhances albumin-mediated lymphatic transport

To elucidate how linker engineering regulates the lymphatic transport of lipid-antigen conjugates, we systematically investigated the effects of linker charge, molecular assembly behavior, and albumin interaction on LN targeting. We hypothesized that the efficient lymphatic trafficking of lipid-antigen conjugates is primarily mediated through dynamic albumin association rather than classical nanoparticle-mediated drainage. We first examined the contribution of the positively charged ε-amino group of the L-lysine linker to LN targeting by acetylating the ε-amino group to generate dOA-AcK-O (Fig. 4a and Scheme S8). Strikingly, acetylation of the ε-amino group markedly reduced LN accumulation, resulting in fluorescence intensity approximately 20-fold lower than dOA-K-O and 10-fold lower than DSPE-O (Fig. 4b and c). This finding underscores the critical role of the positively charged ε-amino group in promoting efficient lymphatic drainage through its interaction with albumin.

To elucidate whether LN targeting originated from nanoparticle formation, we next characterized their hydrodynamic size, surface charge, and colloidal behavior in PBS buffer and albumin-containing media (Table S2). Dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis revealed broad and heterogeneous size distributions for all conjugates in PBS, indicating the absence of stable, well-defined nanoscale assemblies typically associated with nanoparticle-mediated LN delivery. Incubation with human serum albumin (HSA) did not generate uniform nanoparticles but instead altered the apparent hydrodynamic profiles of the conjugates, consistent with dynamic molecular association with albumin rather than colloidal self-assembly. Notably, dOA-3K-O exhibited an increased tendency toward aggregation in albumin-containing media, suggesting that excessive charge density perturbs optimal transport behavior. Zeta potential measurements further confirmed that linker composition substantially modulates the electrostatic properties of the conjugates (Table S2). Together, these findings indicate that LN targeting in this system is mechanistically distinct from conventional nanovaccine drainage and instead likely relies on molecular transport mediated by endogenous albumin.

Because molecular assembly directly influences albumin accessibility, we next evaluated the self-assembly propensity of the conjugates by measuring their critical micelle concentrations (CMC). Amphiphilic lipid-antigen conjugates can self-assemble into micellar structures that bury hydrophobic lipid domains, thereby limiting albumin accessibility and impairing lymphatic trafficking [40,42]. Among all tested constructs, dOA-K-O exhibited the highest CMC (∼92.9 μM), which was significantly higher than dOA-O (∼28.1 μM), dOA-E-O (∼42.6 μM), and DSPE-O (∼43.9 μM) (Fig. 4d and Fig. S9). These findings indicate that the L-lysine linker suppresses excessive micellization and promotes a more dispersed molecular state favorable for albumin accessibility. Conversely, increasing the number of L-lysine or L-γGlu residues reduced the CMC significantly, potentially promoting micelle formation that sequesters hydrophobic regions and reduces albumin interaction.

We next quantified the binding affinity of various lipid-antigen conjugates to HSA using Bio-Layer Interferometry (BLI). Consistent with the LN accumulation data, dOA-K-O exhibited a significantly higher affinity for HSA (KD = ∼30 nM) compared to dOA-O (KD = ∼255.6 nM) and dOA-AcK-O (KD = ∼621.5 nM) (Fig. 4e and Fig. S10), indicating that the positively charged L-lysine linker promotes productive albumin interaction. Interestingly, despite exhibiting the highest albumin-binding affinity (KD = ∼24.4 pM), dOA-3K-O failed to accumulate effectively in draining LNs. To further understand this paradoxical behavior, we monitored fluorescence retention at the injection site. Notably, dOA-3K-O displayed substantially higher residual fluorescence than dOA-K-O at 72 h post-administration (Fig. S11), indicating prolonged local retention and inefficient interstitial clearance. Combined with its elevated aggregation tendency in albumin-containing media, these findings suggest that excessively strong albumin association and high charge density impair efficient transport from the injection site into lymphatic vessels. Thus, optimal LN targeting requires a balanced physicochemical profile that coordinates albumin accessibility, transport mobility, and efficient lymphatic entry, rather than simply maximizing albumin affinity. Similar binding trends were also observed using mouse serum albumin (MSA) (Fig. S12).

To directly validate albumin-mediated transport, we performed native PAGE analysis of conjugate-albumin interactions. Following incubation with albumin, dOA-K-O displayed complete co-migration with albumin, whereas negligible co-migration was observed for dOA-O (Fig. S13), confirming stable albumin association. Furthermore, competitive inhibition using excess oleic acid significantly reduced the early LN accumulation of dOA-K-O, particularly at 2 and 6 h post-injection (Fig. 4f and g), providing direct evidence for albumin-dependent lymphatic transport.

Collectively, these findings indicate that dOA-K-O primarily undergoes albumin-mediated molecular transport rather than conventional nanoparticle-mediated lymphatic drainage. Linker engineering therefore represents an effective strategy for regulating the dynamic biointerface between amphiphilic vaccine conjugates and endogenous albumin, thereby coordinating molecular assembly and lymphatic trafficking.

L-lysine linker promotes antigen internalization and cross-presentation

Beyond efficient lymphatic drainage, successful induction of T cell-mediated immunity requires effective uptake and presentation of antigenic peptides by APCs. We therefore systematically investigated how linker engineering influences DC uptake, intracellular trafficking, and antigen cross-presentation.

To assess the impact of linker structure on antigen internalization, we incubated bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) with fluorescein-labeled OVA257-264 peptides conjugated to various lipid structures. As shown in Fig. 5a and Fig. S14, free OVA257-264 exhibited barely visible fluorescence in the cytoplasm, indicating poor cellular uptake. Linker-free dOA-O led to membrane-associated fluorescence, but little cytoplasmic signal, suggesting limited internalization. Strikingly, introduction of a single L-lysine residue as a linker (dOA-K-O) markedly enhanced intracellular fluorescence, indicating improved cytoplasmic delivery. Increasing the number of L-lysine residues further intensified the cytoplasmic signal and reduced membrane retention, suggesting that multiple positive charges enhance internalization efficiency. Interestingly, BMDCs treated with the negatively charged dOA-E-O also displayed cytoplasmic fluorescence levels comparable to dOA-K-O, and notably greater than DSPE-O, potentially due to the enhanced membrane interaction and membrane dynamics associated with unsaturated dioleic acid tails, as previously reported [53,54]. In contrast, increasing the number of negatively charged L-γGlu residues impaired cellular uptake, likely due to electrostatic repulsion with the negatively charged cell membrane. Overall, these findings highlight that both electrostatic and lipid-tail-driven effects contribute to enhanced cellular internalization.

To investigate whether the enhanced uptake of dOA-K-O depends on canonical endocytic pathways, BMDCs were pretreated with pharmacological inhibitors targeting dynamin-dependent endocytosis (dynasore), caveolae-associated uptake (filipin III), macropinocytosis (EIPA), and clathrin-mediated endocytosis (chlorpromazine). None of these inhibitors reduced dOA-K-O uptake (Fig. S15), suggesting that dOA-K-O internalization is unlikely to be predominantly mediated by any single classical endocytic pathway. In addition, efficient intracellular delivery of dOA-K-O was maintained even at a 10-fold lower concentration (Fig. S16), indicating that its enhanced uptake is an intrinsic feature of the molecular design rather than a concentration-dependent artifact. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that linker engineering enhances cellular uptake through coordinated optimization of physicochemical properties rather than preferential engagement of a specific canonical endocytic pathway.

We further investigated the intracellular trafficking behavior of the conjugates by assessing colocalization with lysosomal markers. Confocal imaging revealed that dOA-K-O displayed significantly reduced lysosomal colocalization (Fig. 5b and Fig. S17), suggesting decreased lysosomal sequestration and improved intracellular accessibility for antigen processing, thereby favoring MHC-I antigen presentation and cross-presentation. To evaluate the downstream functional consequences of antigen uptake, we examined DC maturation and antigen cross-presentation following exposure to the lipid-antigen conjugates. After 24 h of incubation, dOA-K-O treated BMDCs exhibited markedly increased expression of the co-stimulatory molecules CD80 and CD86 compared to cells treated with linker-free dOA-O (Fig. 5c and d), indicating enhanced expression of costimulatory markers. Importantly, dOA-K-O induced the highest level of SIINFEKL-MHC-I complex presentation on the BMDCs surface (Fig. 5e and Fig. S18), demonstrating efficient cross-presentation of the antigenic peptide. To determine whether the enhanced intracellular delivery observed in vitro translates into improved APC acquisition in vivo, we next quantified antigen uptake by APC populations in draining LNs following vaccination. Compared with DSPE-O, dOA-K-O exhibited consistently higher uptake by CD11c+ DCs, F4/80+ macrophages, and B220+ B cells across early post-vaccination time points (Fig. S19), providing a cellular basis for the potent in vivo immune responses induced by dOA-K-O. The relative mRNA expression levels of inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β) remained largely comparable among lipid-antigen conjugates (Fig. S20), suggesting that the superior immune activity of dOA-K-O primarily derives from enhanced antigen delivery and cross-presentation rather than generalized inflammatory stimulation.

To quantitatively integrate the multiple physicochemical and biological parameters governing vaccine performance, we further constructed a radar plot incorporating LN accumulation, albumin-binding affinity, self-assembly propensity (CMC), and APC uptake (Fig. 5f). Notably, dOA-K-O displayed the most balanced multidimensional profile among all tested conjugates, whereas other constructs exhibited clear trade-offs between lymphatic transport and intracellular delivery. A time-course study showed that cross-presentation commenced as early as 2 h post-addition of dOA-K-O (Fig. 5g), benefiting from its efficient intracellular internalization capability. To delineate how cross-presentation operates in APCs, BMDCs were treated with pharmacological inhibitors targeting distinct endocytic and intracellular processing pathways prior to exposure to dOA-K-O. The blockade of lysosomes, proteasome, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) or Golgi apparatus abolished SIINFEKL presentation (Fig. 5h), indicating the participation of these compartments as essential mediators in cross-presentation.

Collectively, these data suggest that optimal vaccine performance is achieved through coordinated regulation of molecular assembly, albumin accessibility, and cellular processing rather than optimization of a single parameter. Importantly, linker engineering enables simultaneous enhancement of lymphatic transport and antigen cross-presentation, thereby mechanistically bridging extracellular trafficking with intracellular immune activation.

dOA-K-O coordinates antigen cross-presentation and T-cell immunity in vivo

Given the superior performance of dOA-K-O in LN targeting and antigen presentation, we further evaluated its capacity to elicit antigen-specific T cell responses in vivo. C57BL/6 mice were subcutaneously immunized at the tail base using a repeated-dose immunization schedule on days 0, 7, and 14, and immune analysis was performed on day 21 using flow cytometry (Fig. 6a). Since adaptive immune responses are primarily initiated in LNs, we first analyzed maturation and antigen presentation of DCs in LNs. Vaccination with dOA-K-O triggered the highest proportion of CD11c+CD86+ cells, significantly surpassing responses induced by free OVA257-264 or DSPE-O (Fig. 6b and c). Furthermore, dOA-K-O led to the highest frequency of CD11c+SIINFEKL-H-2Kb+ cells in draining LNs, confirming efficient cross-presentation of the antigen in vivo (Fig. 6d and e). In addition, there was no significant difference in the proportion of CD11c+MHC-II+ cells among the groups, likely due to OVA257-264 being an MHC-I restricted epitope (Fig. S21). Next, we assessed the levels of SIINFEKL-specific T cell in peripheral blood using H-2Kb-SIINFEKL tetramer. Mice vaccinated with dOA-K-O exhibited an 8-fold increase tetramer+CD8+ T cells compared to free OVA257-264, and a 3-fold increase relative to DSPE-O (Fig. 6f, g and Fig. S22). It is worth mentioning that free OVA257-264 failed to generate detectable antigen-specific T cells, indicating the necessity of effective LN targeting for eliciting robust cellular immunity.

Interferon gamma (IFNγ) is a critical cytokine in cell-mediated immunity and can enhance the anti-tumor and anti-viral effects of CD8 T cells [55,56]. We assessed cytokine-producing T cells in spleens and peripheral blood from C57BL/6 mice one week after the final immunization. Mice immunized with dOA-K-O exhibited significantly higher proportions of CD8+ T cells and IFNγ+CD8+ T cells compared to other treatment groups (Fig. 6h and Fig. S23), indicating enhanced effector differentiation following LN-targeted vaccination. Similar trends were observed in peripheral blood, where dOA-K-O induced substantially stronger systemic IFNγ+CD8+ T-cell responses than free peptide vaccination (Fig. S24). In addition to promoting antigen cross-presentation, dOA-K-O also enhanced immune-stimulatory activation of APCs in vivo. Specifically, vaccination with dOA-K-O significantly increased the proportion of CD40+IL-12+CD11c+ cells in draining LNs compared with free peptide and DSPE-O formulations (Fig. 6i and Fig. S25), indicating enhanced activation of APC-associated immune signaling pathways that support T-cell priming and effector differentiation.

To further determine whether the day 0/7/14 immunization regimen provides optimal T-cell priming and boosting, we compared prime-only, prime-boost, and prime-boost-boost vaccination strategies using dOA-K-O. As shown in Fig. 6j, k and Fig. S26, sequential booster immunizations progressively amplified antigen-specific CD8+ T-cell responses and promoted memory-associated T-cell differentiation. In particular, the prime-boost-boost regimen generated the highest frequencies of SIINFEKL+CD8+ T cells and CD44+CD62L+ memory-associated CD8+ T cells in the spleen, indicating that repeated antigen exposure is required for sustained expansion and functional maturation of vaccine-induced T cells. These findings experimentally validate the rationale for the 0/7/14 immunization schedule and demonstrate that repeated booster immunization enhances both effector and memory-associated cellular immunity.

To evaluate the systemic safety of repeated administration, mice receiving the complete prime-boost-boost regimen were subjected to comprehensive biosafety assessment. No vaccine-related weight abnormalities, hematologic abnormalities, hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity were observed in the dOA-K-O vaccine group versus the PBS controls (Figs. S27 and S28). To further assess whether repeated immunization induced cumulative or systemic toxicities, major organs were collected 7 days after the final booster dose for histopathological examination. H&E staining revealed no apparent pathological damage, inflammatory infiltration, or structural abnormalities in the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, or kidneys of dOA-K-O-treated mice (Fig. S29). In addition, serum inflammatory cytokine profiling revealed no evidence of excessive systemic immune activation or inflammatory toxicity associated with dOA-K-O administration (Fig. S30), collectively supporting the favorable biosafety profile of the lipid-antigen conjugates under repeated immunization conditions.

In addition, to verify the general applicability of our findings to other antigens, we conjugated the MHC-I-restricted immunogenic neoantigen Adpgk peptide (ASMTNMELM) from the mouse colorectal tumor model MC38 to dOA-K, and then evaluated the LN-targeting and immune activation of the conjugate in vivo. The results showed that the dOA-K modified Adpgk peptide (denoted as dOA-K-Adpgk) exhibited excellent LN accumulation at 24 h post-injection, with low to negligible levels detectable in the kidney, liver, and other organs (Fig. S31a). The proportions of the CD80+CD86+CD11c+ cell and IFNγ+CD8+ T cells population in the dOA-K-Adpgk treatment group were 2.5 and 5.8-fold that in the free Adpgk treatment group, respectively (Fig. S31b and c). This demonstrates that linker engineering, as a general chemical strategy, can overcome key delivery limitations associated with peptide vaccines. By successfully extending the dOA-K platform from the model antigen OVA to the endogenous MC38-derived neoantigen Adpgk while preserving its favorable lymphatic trafficking and T-cell priming properties, our findings support the broader applicability of this design strategy for neoantigen-based cancer vaccines.

dOA-K-O remodels the tumor immune microenvironment and suppresses melanoma progression

To determine whether the enhanced LN targeting and antigen cross-presentation mediated by dOA-K-O translate into functional antitumor immunity, we first evaluated its prophylactic efficacy using a B16-OVA melanoma model. C57BL/6 mice were immunized three times, one week apart, and then challenged with OVA-expressing B16-OVA melanoma cells 7 days after the final dose (Fig. 7a). Tumor progression was monitored for 40 days following tumor inoculation. Compared with PBS and free OVA257-264 controls, lipid-antigen conjugate vaccines significantly delayed tumor progression, with LN-targeting formulations (DSPE-O and dOA-K-O) exhibiting markedly improved prophylactic efficacy (Fig. 7b–d). Among them, dOA-K-O achieved the most pronounced suppression of tumor growth.

To further assess the translational therapeutic potential of dOA-K-O, we evaluated its efficacy in an established B16-OVA melanoma model (Fig. 7e). Following establishment of palpable tumors, mice received therapeutic vaccination with different formulations. Consistent with the prophylactic setting, therapeutic vaccination with dOA-K-O markedly suppressed established tumor progression, whereas free OVA257-264 exhibited limited therapeutic benefit (Fig. 7f and Fig. S32). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis further demonstrated that dOA-K-O provided the greatest survival benefit among all treatment groups (Fig. 7g), whereas all mice receiving PBS or free peptide succumbed to tumor burden within 25 days.

To elucidate the immunological basis underlying tumor control, we next analyzed remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment following therapeutic vaccination. Flow cytometric analysis revealed significantly increased infiltration of CD3+CD8+ T cells in tumors from dOA-K-O-treated mice compared with control groups (Fig. 7h and Fig. S33), indicating enhanced accumulation of cytotoxic T lymphocytes within tumors. Importantly, dOA-K-O also generated substantially higher frequencies of intratumoral IFNγ+CD8+ T cells (Fig. 7i), demonstrating enhanced local effector function of tumor-infiltrating T cells. In parallel, vaccination with dOA-K-O significantly reduced the abundance of immunosuppressive CD4+Foxp3+ regulatory T (Treg) cells within tumors (Fig. 7j). Furthermore, analysis of exhaustion-associated phenotypes revealed that dOA-K-O did not induce a significant increase in PD-1+TIM-3+ CD8+ T cells within tumors (Fig. S34), suggesting that enhanced antitumor immunity was accompanied by preserved CD8+ T-cell functionality without an apparent exhaustion-associated phenotype at the analyzed time point. Together, these findings demonstrate that dOA-K-O converts enhanced LN-mediated antigen priming into productive remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment, characterized by increased cytotoxic T-cell infiltration, elevated effector activity, and reduced immune suppression.

Collectively, these results confirm that optimized linker engineering enables dOA-K-O to translate potent antigen-specific T-cell immunity into durable antitumor efficacy. By coordinating efficient LN targeting, antigen cross-presentation, and tumor immune microenvironment remodeling, dOA-K-O effectively suppresses melanoma progression and prolongs survival, highlighting the promise of chemically defined lipid-conjugated peptide vaccines for cancer immunotherapy.

Conclusion

In this study, we established a rational molecular engineering strategy for lipid-antigen conjugates that simultaneously optimizes lymphatic trafficking and intracellular antigen processing to enhance peptide vaccine immunogenicity. Through systematic modulation of linker chemistry and lipid tail structure, we identified a dioleic acid/L-lysine construct (dOA-K-O) that achieves a favorable physicochemical balance between albumin binding, self-assembly behavior, and cellular delivery.

Mechanistically, the positively charged L-lysine linker enhances albumin binding while reducing micelle propensity, thereby promoting efficient lymphatic transport and accumulation within draining LNs. Beyond extracellular delivery, dOA-K-O facilitates antigen internalization, intracellular trafficking, and efficient DC cross-presentation, ultimately leading to enhanced APC activation and robust antigen-specific CD8+ T-cell immunity. The optimized lipid-conjugated peptide vaccine further promotes memory-associated T-cell responses and remodels the tumor immune microenvironment by increasing intratumoral cytotoxic T-cell infiltration while reducing immune suppression. Functionally, these coordinated improvements translate into potent antitumor efficacy in both prophylactic and therapeutic melanoma models and are extendable to an independent neoantigen system, supporting the general applicability of the design strategy.

Collectively, our findings highlight linker engineering as a powerful yet underexplored chemically tunable design axis for lipid-antigen conjugates and provide a chemically defined framework for synchronizing LN targeting with intracellular antigen processing. This work establishes generalizable molecular design principles for next-generation peptide vaccines and offers broad implications for cancer immunotherapy and vaccination against infectious diseases.

Statistical analysis

Statistical comparisons between two groups were performed using two-tail unpaired Student's t-tests after assessment of data normality. Survival curves were analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test.
