Abstract
. Using the characterizations in terms of various differential operators including partial, normal, and tangential derivatives, we extend the family of Bergman spaces of ℋ \mathcal{H} -harmonic functions on the real hyperbolic ball from α > − 1 \alpha>-1 to all α ∈ ℝ \alpha\in\mathbb{R} . We then generalize several properties of Bergman spaces such as projection, duality, and inclusion relations, to this extended family.

1.
Introduction

For
n\geq 2
, let
\mathbb{B}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the unit ball and

\mathbb{S}=\partial\mathbb{B}
the unit sphere.
The real hyperbolic ball is
\mathbb{B}
equipped with the hyperbolic metric

ds^{2}=\frac{4}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}^{2}.

For
a\in\mathbb{B}
,

\varphi_{a}(x)=\frac{a|x-a|^{2}+(1-|a|^{2})(a-x)}{1-2\langle x,a\rangle+|a|^{2}|x|^{2}}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{B})

is a Möbius transformation mapping
\mathbb{B}
to
\mathbb{B}
, is
involutive and exchanges
a
and
0
.
The group of Möbius transformations of
\mathbb{B}
is generated
by orthogonal transformations and
\varphi_{a}

(a\in\mathbb{B})
.
The Laplacian
\Delta_{h}
with respect to the hyperbolic metric is given by

\Delta_{h}f(a)=\Delta(f\circ\varphi_{a})(0)\qquad(f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B})),

and a straightforward calculation shows

\Delta_{h}f(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{2}\Delta f(x)+2(n-2)(1-|x|^{2})\langle x,\nabla f(x)\rangle.

Here,
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial^{2}/\partial x_{i}^{2}
is the Euclidean Laplacian,

\nabla=(\partial/\partial x_{1},\ldots,\partial/\partial x_{n})
is the Euclidean
gradient and
\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
is the Euclidean inner product.

Functions that are annihilated by
\Delta_{h}
are called hyperbolic harmonic or

\mathcal{H}
-harmonic.
The space of all
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions is denoted
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
.
For properties of
\Delta_{h}
and
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions
we refer the reader to
[
12
]
.

Let
\nu
ad
\sigma
be the volume and surface measures on
\mathbb{B}

and
\mathbb{S}
normalized so that
\nu(\mathbb{B})=1
and
\sigma(\mathbb{S})=1
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the weighted measures

d\nu_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{V_{\alpha}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}d\nu(x)

which are finite only when
\alpha>-1
and in this case
we set

V_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(n/2+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n/2+\alpha+1)},

so that
\nu_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B})=1
.
When
\alpha\leq-1
, we set
V_{\alpha}=1
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote the Lebesgue spaces
with respect to
\nu_{\alpha}
by
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B})=L^{p}_{\alpha}
.

For
\alpha>-1
and
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman
space
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
is
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})\cap L^{p}_{\alpha}
,
that is,

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}=\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})\colon\|f\|^{p}_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(x)<\infty\bigr\}.

These are Banach spaces and point evaluations are bounded on them.
We refer to
[
12
, Chapter 10]
for basic properties of these spaces.

The first aim of this paper is to characterize
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic
Bergman functions in terms of various differential operators including
partial, normal and tangential derivatives.
This will allow us to extend the Bergman space family from
\alpha>-1
to
all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
This extended family will be called the Bergman-Besov spaces.
We will then generalize some known properties of Bergman spaces to
this extended family.

For partial derivatives we use multi-index notation.
For
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n})
with
\kappa_{i}
non-negative
integer, we write

\partial^{\kappa}f=\frac{\partial^{|\kappa|}f}{\partial x_{1}^{\kappa_{i}}\cdots\partial x_{n}^{\kappa_{n}}},

where
|\kappa|=\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{n}
.
We also write
x^{\kappa}=x_{1}^{\kappa_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\kappa_{n}}
.
The normal (radial) derivative
N
is defined by

Nf(x)=\langle x,\nabla f(x)\rangle

and for
k\geq 2
, we define
N^{k}f=N(N^{k-1}f)
.
For
1\leq i<j\leq n
, the tangential derivative
T_{i,j}
is
defined by

T_{i,j}f(x)=x_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}-x_{j}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}.

For
k\geq 1
, we denote the set of all tangential derivatives of
order
k
by
\mathcal{T}^{k}
,

\mathcal{T}^{k}=\{T_{i_{1},j_{1}}\circ\dots\circ T_{i_{k},j_{k}}\colon 1\leq i_{l}<j_{l}\leq n\}.

For
k=0
we set
\mathcal{T}^{0}=\{\textup{Id.}\}
.

We will also employ another type of differential operator
denoted by
D^{t}_{s}
(
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
) which act as coefficient
multipliers on the series expansion of
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
.
Let
H_{m}=H_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the space of (Euclidean) harmonic
polynomials homogeneous of degree
m
.
By
[
12
, Theorem 6.3.1]
, every
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
has a unique
series expansion

(1)

f(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}(x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{B}),

where
f_{m}\in H_{m}
and
S_{m}
is the hypergeometric function

S_{m}(r)=\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(m,1-\frac{n}{2};m+\frac{n}{2};r^{2})}{{}_{2}F_{1}(m,1-\frac{n}{2};m+\frac{n}{2};1)}\qquad(0\leq r\leq 1)

normalized so that
S_{m}(1)=1
.
For
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
with series expansion (
1
),
we define

D^{t}_{s}f(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}d_{m}(s,t)S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}(x),

where the coefficients
d_{m}(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are defined in
terms of the coefficients of the reproducing kernels as explained
in Section
3
.
We have
d_{m}(s,t)\sim m^{t}
and
D^{t}_{s}
acts as a differential operator of
order
t
(integral if
t<0
).
The operators
D^{t}_{s}
are compatible with reproducing kernels and
compared to other derivative operators, they are more convenient
to use in understanding the properties of Bergman and Bergman-Besov spaces.

For
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman functions the following characterization holds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
.
The following are equivalent:

(a)

f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
.

(b)

For some (equivalently, all)
k\geq 1
, we have

T^{k}f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha+pk}
for all
T^{k}\in\mathcal{T}^{k}
.

(c)

For some (equivalently, all)
k\geq 1
, we have

\partial^{\kappa}f\in L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}
for all multi-indices
\kappa

with
|\kappa|=k
.

(d)

For some (equivalently, all)
k\geq 1
, we have

N^{k}f\in L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}
.

(e)

For some (equivalently, all)
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha+pt>-1
,
we have
D^{t}_{s}f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha+pt}
.

Moreover, all the induced norms are equivalent.
That is,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{T^{k}\in\mathcal{T}^{k}}\|T^{k}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}+|f(0)|\sim\sum_{|\kappa|=k}\|\partial^{\kappa}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}+\sum_{|\kappa|\leq k-1}|\partial^{\kappa}f(0)|

\displaystyle\sim\|N^{k}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}+|f(0)|\sim\|D^{t}_{s}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pt}}.

This theorem is mostly known.
The equivalence (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b)
\Leftrightarrow
(d) is shown
in
[
7
]
.
For
k=1
, the equivalence (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(c) is
[
12
, Theorem 10.3.3]
.
In
[
9
]
, a result similar to (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(e) is proved,
where different coefficient multipliers are used.
We will give an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
that differs
from these sources.
It will be based on reproducing formulas and estimates of reproducing kernels.
For an analogous characterization of Euclidean harmonic Bergman functions,
see
[
4
]
.

The main purpose of this paper is to extend the above result to
the range
\alpha\leq-1
.
We note that if
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
, then
T_{i,j}f
and
D^{t}_{s}f
are also
in
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
.
However, this is not true for partial and normal derivatives.
Correspondingly, while Theorem
1.1
extends to all

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
for tangential derivatives and the operators
D^{t}_{s}
,
for partial and normal derivatives it is necessary to restrict
\alpha
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
.
The following are equivalent:

(a)

For some (equivalently, all)
k\geq 0
with
\alpha+pk>-1
,
we have
T^{k}f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha+pk}
for all
T^{k}\in\mathcal{T}^{k}
.

(b)

For some (equivalently, all)
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha+pt>-1
,
we have
D^{t}_{s}f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha+pt}
.

Moreover,

\sum_{T^{k}\in\mathcal{T}^{k}}\|T^{k}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}+|f(0)|\sim\|D^{t}_{s}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pt}}

for every
k\geq 0
with
\alpha+pk>-1
and for every
t,s\in\mathbb{R}

with
\alpha+pt>-1
.

Using this theorem we extend the spaces
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
from
\alpha>-1

to all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 1.3
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, the Bergman-Besov space

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
that satisfy
the equivalent conditions given in Theorem
1.2
.

For every
k
with
\alpha+pk>-1
,

\sum_{T^{k}\in\mathcal{T}^{k}}\|T^{k}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}+|f(0)|

is a norm on
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
.
Similarly, for every
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha+pt>-1
,

\|D^{t}_{s}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pt}}
is a norm.
All these norms are equivalent and we denote any one of these
by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}
.

If
\alpha>-1
, then one can take
k=0
(or
t=0
) and obtain the Bergman spaces.
Special cases of the Besov zone
\alpha\leq-1
have been investigated before.
When
\alpha=-1
and
p=2
,
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{-1}
is the Hardy space

\mathcal{H}^{2}
which is studied in
[
13
]
.
When
\alpha>-p-1
, first order derivatives suffice and

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
coincides with the Dirichlet type spaces

\mathcal{D}^{p}_{\gamma}
(with
\gamma=\alpha+n
) considered
in
[
12
, Chapter 10]
.
The case
\alpha=-n
is especially important as the measure

d\nu_{-n}(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{-n}d\nu(x)
is Möbius invariant.
When
p=2
, the space
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{-n}
is extensively studied in

[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, where it is shown that
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{-n}
is the unique
Möbius invariant Hilbert space of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions.
It is true that the spaces
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{-n}
are Möbius
invariant for all
1\leq p<\infty
, but this will be considered in a
different work.

Characterizations in terms of partial and
normal derivatives holds only for restricted
\alpha
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

(2)

\alpha+p(n-1)>-1.

For
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
.

(b)

For some (equivalently, all)
k\geq 0
with
\alpha+pk>-1
,
we have
\partial^{\kappa}f\in L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}
for all multi-indices
\kappa

with
|\kappa|=k
.

(c)

For some (equivalently, all)
k\geq 0
with
\alpha+pk>-1
,
we have
N^{k}f\in L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}
.

In addition,

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}\sim\sum_{|\kappa|=k}\|\partial^{\kappa}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}+\sum_{|\kappa|\leq k-1}|\partial^{\kappa}f(0)|\sim\|N^{k}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}+|f(0)|.

We note that (
2
) is needed only for the parts
(a)
\Rightarrow
(b) and (a)
\Rightarrow
(c), but not for the reverse
implications.
That is, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, if there exists a
k
with

\alpha+pk>-1
such that
\partial^{\kappa}f\in L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}
for all

|\kappa|=k
(or

then

.

For the Möbius invariant space
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{-n}
,
Theorem
1.4
is proved in
[
2
, Theorem 14]

under the condition
k\leq n-2
.
Theorem
1.4
shows that no restriction on the order of
the derivative is needed as long as the space parameter
\alpha
satisfies
(
2
).

We next extend some known properties (projection, duality,
inclusion relations) of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman spaces
to the whole range
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
.

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha_{2}}

for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Indeed, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
t=(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})/p
,

D^{t}_{s}\colon\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}\to\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha_{2}}
is an
isomorphism.
Thus, all
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
are Banach spaces.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)
be the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
,

\alpha>-1
.
We will review the properties of these kernels and extend them
to
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
in Section
3
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, define the projection operator

P_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)\,d\nu_{\alpha}(y).

In the Bergman zone the following theorem is proved in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
and
1\leq p<\infty
.
Then
P_{\beta}\colon L^{p}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
is bounded if and only if

\alpha+1<p(\beta+1)
.
Under this condition
P_{\beta}f=f
for
f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
.

The following theorem generalizes the projection theorem to all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
.
Then
P_{\beta}\colon L^{p}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
is bounded if and only if

\alpha+1<p(\beta+1)
.
Under this condition,
P_{\beta}
is right invertible.
That is, for every
f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
, there exists
g\in L^{p}_{\alpha}

with
\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}\sim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}
such that
P_{\beta}g=f
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Point evaluations are bounded on
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
for all

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
.

We next consider dual spaces and generalize
[
14
, Corollary 1.4]
and

[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
1<p<\infty
, the dual of
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
is isomorphic
to
\mathcal{B}^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
, where
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
is the conjugate
exponent of
p
.
More precisely, pick any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha+pt>-1
.
Set
t^{\prime}=(p-1)t
so that
\alpha+p^{\prime}t^{\prime}=\alpha+pt
.
Define the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha,p,s,t}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha,p}
by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha,p}=\int_{\mathbb{B}}D^{t}_{s}f(x)D^{t^{\prime}}_{s}g(x)\,d\nu_{\alpha+pt}(x).

Then, to each
\Lambda\in(\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha})^{*}
, there corresponds
a unique
g\in\mathcal{B}^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
with

\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}}\sim\|\Lambda\|
such that

\Lambda(f)=\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha,p}
.

The dual of
\mathcal{B}^{1}_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to the

\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bloch space

\mathcal{B}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})\colon\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}}(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})\lvert\nabla f(x)\rvert<\infty\}.

More precisely, pick any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha+t>-1
and define
the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha,s,t}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
by

(3)

\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha}=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\int_{r\mathbb{B}}D^{t}_{s}f(x)g(x)\,d\nu_{\alpha+t}(x).

Then, to each
\Lambda\in(\mathcal{B}^{1}_{\alpha})^{*}
, there corresponds
a unique
g\in\mathcal{B}
with

\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}}\sim\|\Lambda\|
such that

\Lambda(f)=\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha}
.

Similarly the pre-dual of
\mathcal{B}^{1}_{\alpha}
can be identified with the

\mathcal{H}
-harmonic little Bloch space

\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})\colon\lim_{\lvert x\rvert\to 1^{-}}(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})\lvert\nabla f(x)\rvert=0\}

under the pairing (
3
).

The next theorem generalizes the inclusion relations in
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,q<\infty
.

(a)

If
q\geq p
, then

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\frac{\alpha+n}{p}\leq\frac{\beta+n}{q}

(b)

If

, then

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\frac{\alpha+1}{p}<\frac{\beta+1}{q}

In both cases the inclusion

2.
Preliminaries

We denote positive constants whose exact values are inessential by
the letter
C
.
For two positive expressions
X
and
Y
, we write
X\lesssim Y

to mean
X\leq CY
.
If both
X\leq CY
and
Y\leq CX
, we write
X\sim Y
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{B}
, we write

[x,y]:=\sqrt{1-2\langle x,y\rangle+|x|^{2}|y|^{2}}.

Clearly,
[x,y]
is symmetric; when
y=0
,
[x,0]=1
and otherwise

(4)

[x,y]=\bigl||y|x-\frac{y}{|y|}\bigr|.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
x,y\in\mathbb{B}
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, we have

[tx,y]\geq\frac{[x,y]}{2}.

Proof.

By (
4
), it suffices to show that

|tx-\zeta|\geq|x-\zeta|/2
for all

x\in\mathbb{B}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{S}
and
0\leq t\leq 1
.
This follows from the triangle inequality

|x-\zeta|\leq|x-tx|+|tx-\zeta|
and the fact that

|x-tx|=|x|-t|x|\leq 1-t|x|\leq|tx-\zeta|
.
∎

The following lemma follows from
[
14
, Lemma 2.15]
and (
4
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
b>-1
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
There exists
C=C(n,b,c)
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{B}
,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-t)^{b}}{[tx,y]^{1+b+c}}\,dt\leq C\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{[x,y]^{c}},&\text{if $c>0$};\\
1+\log\dfrac{1}{[x,y]},&\text{if $c=0$};\\
1,&\text{if $c<0$}.\end{cases}

For
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
, define the operator

E_{s,t}f(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{t}\int_{\mathbb{B}}f(y)\frac{(1-|y|^{2})^{s}}{[x,y]^{n+s+t}}\,d\nu(y).

For a proof of the following lemma see, for example,
[
5
, Theorem 1.6]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha,s,t\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
.
Then
E_{s,t}\colon L^{p}_{\alpha}\to L^{p}_{\alpha}
is bounded
if and only if
-pt<\alpha+1<p(s+1)
.

3.
Reproducing Kernels and the Operators
D^{t}_{s}

For
\alpha>-1
, point evaluations are bounded on the Hilbert space

\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
and so, for each
x\in\mathbb{B}
, there exists

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
such that

f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}}f(y)\overline{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(y)\qquad(f\in\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}).

The reproducing kernel
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
is real valued (so
conjugation can be deleted), symmetric, and
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic
in each variable.
It has the series expansion

(5)

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{m}(\alpha)S_{m}(|x|)S_{m}(|y|)Z_{m}(x,y),

where the coefficient
c_{m}(\alpha)
is determined by

(6)

\frac{1}{c_{m}(\alpha)}=\frac{n}{V_{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2m+n-1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}S_{m}^{2}(r)\,dr.

Here,
Z_{m}(\cdot,\cdot)
, the zonal harmonic of degree
m
, is the reproducing kernel
of
H_{m}(\mathbb{S})
extended to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
by homogeneity
(see
[
1
, Chapter 5]
).
The series in (
5
) converges absolutely and uniformly on

K\times\mathbb{B}
for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{B}
.

In
[
14
]
, a series expansion of the integral in (
6
) is obtained and it is
shown that
c_{m}(\alpha)
has the asymptotic expansion (
[
14
, Corollary 3.4]
)

(7)

c_{m}(\alpha)\approx\frac{\Gamma(m+\alpha+n)}{\Gamma(m+n-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{D_{k}}{m^{k}}\qquad(m\to\infty),

where
D_{k}=D_{k}(\alpha,n)
are constants with
D_{0}>0
.
That is, for each
K\geq 1
,

c_{m}(\alpha)=\frac{\Gamma(m+\alpha+n)}{\Gamma(m+n-1)}\biggl(\,\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{D_{k}}{m^{k}}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{m^{K}}\Bigr)\biggr)\qquad(m\to\infty).

Thus, by Stirling’s formula,
c_{m}(\alpha)\sim m^{\alpha+1}
for
m\geq 1
.
Integrating in polar coordinates shows that for

f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}
,

g=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}(|x|)g_{m}
,

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}}f\bar{g}\,d\nu_{\alpha}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{c_{m}(\alpha)}\langle f_{m},g_{m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S})},

and so, for
\alpha>-1
,

\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}=\Bigl\{f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})\colon\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{\alpha+1}}\|f_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S})}<\infty\Bigr\}.

This characterization extends to all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
in agreement with
Definition
1.3
.

We next extend the reproducing kernels
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
to all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
We first mention two special cases that have been studied in detail before.
When
\alpha=-1
,
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{-1}=\mathcal{H}^{2}
is the Hardy space whose
reproducing kernel is the extended hyperbolic Poisson kernel

\mathcal{R}_{-1}(x,y)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}(|x|)S_{m}(|y|)Z_{m}(x,y)

(see
[
13
, Theorem 2.5]
).
When
\alpha=-n
,
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{-n}
is Möbius invariant
and with respect to the Möbius invariant (semi-)inner product, the
reproducing kernel is (see
[
2
]
)

\mathcal{R}_{-n}(x,y)=1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(m)}{\Gamma(m+n-1)}S_{m}(|x|)S_{m}(|y|)Z_{m}(x,y).

Definition 3.1
.

For
\alpha\leq-1
and
x,y\in\mathbb{B}
, we define

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{m}(\alpha)S_{m}(|x|)S_{m}(|y|)Z_{m}(x,y),

where
c_{0}(\alpha)=1
and

(8)

c_{m}(\alpha)=\frac{\Gamma(m)}{\Gamma(m-\alpha-1)},\qquad m\geq 1.

The reason for the choice of the formula (
8
) is
to incorporate the two special cases above.
The only properties of
c_{m}(\alpha)
we will employ later are

c_{m}(\alpha)>0
and

(9)

c_{m}(\alpha)\sim m^{\alpha+1}\qquad(m\geq 1),

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
When
\alpha\leq-1
,
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
is indeed reproducing kernel of

\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
given in Definition
1.3
with respect
to a suitable inner product.

Using the above coefficients
c_{m}(\alpha)

(\alpha\in\mathbb{R})
,
we define differential operators
D^{t}_{s}
which act as coefficient
multipliers on the series expansion
of
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
with

f(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}(x)
, we define

D^{t}_{s}f(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{m}(s+t)}{c_{m}(s)}S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}(x).

By (
9
),
c_{m}(s+t)/c_{m}(s)\sim m^{t}
and
D^{t}_{s}
acts as a
differential (integral if
t<0
) operator of order
t
.
The role of the parameter
s
is minor and its main role is to have
exact formula in part (ii) of the lemma below.
The idea of first extending the reproducing kernels to
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
then defining the operators
D^{t}_{s}
in the above form is taken from
[
8
]
,
where holomorphic (Bergman-)Besov spaces on the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
are
considered.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
D^{t}_{s}
satisfies the following
properties.

(i)

D^{t}_{s}:\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})\to\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})

is continuous, where
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
is endowed with the topology
of uniform convergence on compact subsets.

(ii)

D^{t}_{s}\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,y)=\mathcal{R}_{s+t}(x,y)
,
acting on the variable
x
(or
y
).

(iii)

D^{u}_{s+t}\circ D^{t}_{s}=D^{u+t}_{s}
.

(iv)

D^{0}_{s}=\textup{Id}.
and
D^{t}_{s}
has the two-sided inverse

D^{-t}_{s+t}
, i.e.,
D^{-t}_{s+t}\circ D^{t}_{s}=\textup{Id}.
and

D^{t}_{s}\circ D^{-t}_{s+t}=\textup{Id}.

Parts (ii)–(iv) follow from the definition.
For part (i) see
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 4.1

∎

We next estimate

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

such that for all

Proof.

We have

D^{t}_{s}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{m}(s+t)}{c_{m}(s)}c_{m}(\alpha)S_{m}(|x|)S_{m}(|y|)Z_{m}(x,y),

7

)

(

We next estimate partial derivatives of the reproducing kernels.
For simplicity we consider only the case

which is
sufficient for our purposes.
The estimate takes a different form when the order of the derivative

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
\kappa
be a multi-index.
There exists a constant
C=C(n,\alpha,\kappa)
such that for all

x,y\in\mathbb{B}
,

(i)

If
|\kappa|\leq n-2
, then

|\partial^{\kappa}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+|\kappa|}}.

(ii)

If
|\kappa|=n-1
, then

(a)

\displaystyle\bigl|\partial^{\kappa}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)\bigr|\leq C\Bigl(1+\log\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}\Bigr)\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1}},

(b)

for every
0<\varepsilon<1
(with
C
depending also on
\varepsilon
),

\bigl|\partial^{\kappa}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)\bigr|\leq C\frac{1}{(1-|x|^{2})^{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1-\varepsilon}}.

(iii)

If
|\kappa|\geq n
, then

\bigl|\partial^{\kappa}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)\bigr|\leq C\frac{1}{(1-|x|^{2})^{|\kappa|-(n-1)}}\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1}}.

Here, differentiation is with respect to
x
.

Proof.

The proof follows the proof of Theorem 1.2(b) of
[
14
]
and we will be
brief about some of the details.
To estimate
\partial^{\kappa}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)
, it suffices to estimate

G(x,y):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{m}(\alpha)\partial^{\lambda}S_{m}(|x|)S_{m}(|y|)\partial^{\mu}Z_{m}(x,y),

where
\lambda+\mu=\kappa
.
Since (
[
14
, Eqn. (22)]
)

S_{m}(|x|)=\frac{1}{B(m,n-1)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{m-1}(1-t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-|x|^{2}t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\,dt,

we have

\partial^{\lambda}S_{m}(|x|)=\frac{1}{B(m,n-1)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{m-1}(1-t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}P_{\lambda}(x,t)(1-|x|^{2}t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1-|\lambda|}\,dt,

where
P_{\lambda}
is a polynomial.
Thus

\displaystyle G(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle(1-t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}P_{\lambda}(x,t)(1-|x|^{2}t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1-|\lambda|}(1-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-|y|^{2}\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{m}(\alpha)}{B^{2}(m,n-1)}t^{m-1}\tau^{m-1}\partial^{\mu}Z_{m}(x,y)\,d\tau dt.

Since
\partial^{\mu}Z_{m}(x,y)
is homogeneous of degree
m-|\mu|
in
x

and of degree
m
in
y
, writing

t^{m-1}\tau^{m-1}\partial^{\mu}Z_{m}(x,y)=t^{|\mu|}\partial^{\mu}Z_{m}(tx,\tau y)/t\tau
, and estimating the above
series with Proposition
4.2
using (
7
) we obtain

|G(x,y)|\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-|x|^{2}t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1-|\lambda|}(1-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-|y|^{2}\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{[tx,\tau y]^{n+\alpha+2(n-1)+|\mu|}}\,d\tau dt,

where we also use
|P_{\lambda}|\lesssim 1
, and delete the term
1/t\tau

which can be justified as done in the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 4.4]
.
Using the inequality
1-|y|^{2}\tau\leq 2[tx,\tau y]
(
[
14
, Eqn. (36)]
)
and then integrating
with respect to
\tau
, Lemma
2.2
yields

|G(x,y)|\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-|x|^{2}t)^{\frac{n}{2}-1-|\lambda|}}{[tx,y]^{n+\alpha+(n-1)+|\mu|}}\,dt.

Finally using the inequality (
[
14
, Eqn. (35)]
with
\tau=1
)

(13)

1-t\leq 1-|x|^{2}t\leq 2[tx,y]

and
|\lambda|+|\mu|=|\kappa|
, we obtain

|G(x,y)|\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|x|^{2}t)^{n-2-|\kappa|}}{[tx,y]^{n+\alpha+(n-1)}}\,dt.

We now consider the three cases.

(i) If
|\kappa|\leq n-2
, then using the second inequality
in (
13
), and then Lemma
2.2
shows

|G(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+|\kappa|}}.

(ii) If
|\kappa|=n-1
, then by Lemma
2.1
,

(14)

\displaystyle|G(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{(1-|x|^{2}t)[tx,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{1-|x|^{2}t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(1+\log\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}\Bigr)\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1}}.

This proves part (a).
Alternatively, for
0<\varepsilon<1
, let
p=1/\varepsilon
and apply
Hölder’s inequality to the first integral in (
14
).
This gives

\displaystyle|G(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{(1-|x|^{2}t)^{p^{\prime}}}\biggr)^{1/p^{\prime}}\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{[tx,y]^{(n+\alpha+n-1)p}}\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|x|^{2})^{1-1/p^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1-1/p}},

where in the last inequality we use Lemma
2.2
.
This proves part (b).

(iii) If
|\kappa|\geq n
, then by Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle|G(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-|x|^{2}t)^{n-2-|\kappa|}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|x|^{2})^{|\kappa|-(n-1)}}\frac{1}{[x,y]^{n+\alpha+n-1}}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(a)
\Leftrightarrow

Suppose

Then

since
(\beta-\alpha/p)p^{\prime}>-1
by (

),
and the reproducing formula

(19)

f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}}\mathcal{R}_{\beta}(x,y)f(y)\,d\nu_{\beta}(y)

Applying

to both sides and pushing it into the integral

Proof of Theorem

.

\Bigl\|\frac{Nf(x)}{|x|}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+p}}\lesssim\sum_{i<j}\|T_{i,j}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha+p}}.

For

Lemma 6.1

Let

\lambda
with
|\lambda|\leq k-1

and for every
0<R<1
, we have

\int_{B_{R}}\partial^{\lambda}f(x)\,d\nu(x)=0.

Proof.

In the proof we use the fact that if
q\in H_{m}
and
p
is a polynomial
of degree less than
m
, then
\int_{\mathbb{S}}pq\,d\sigma=0

(see
[
1
, Proposition 5.9]
).

Let
\lambda
be a multi-index with
|\lambda|\leq k-1
.
By uniform convergence of the series expansion of
f
and its partial
derivatives, we can differentiate term by term.
We don’t need the exact formula of the derivative

\partial^{\lambda}(S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}(x))
.
Only terms of special form will occur in the derivative and we only mention these.
Because
S_{m}(|x|)=C\,{}_{2}F_{1}(m,1-n/2;m+n/2;|x|^{2})
,
for any multi-index
\beta
,
\partial^{\beta}S_{m}(|x|)
is a
sum of multiples of terms of the form

x^{\gamma}G(|x|^{2})

with

|\gamma|\leq|\beta|
and
G
is some function which
also depends on
n,m,\beta,\gamma
.
Therefore,
\partial^{\lambda}(S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}(x))
is a sum of terms
of the form

x^{\gamma}G(|x|^{2})\partial^{\delta}f_{m}(x),

with
|\gamma|+|\delta|\leq|\lambda|\leq k-1
.
Integrating in polar coordinates

\int_{B_{R}}x^{\gamma}G(|x|^{2})\partial^{\delta}f_{m}(x)\,d\nu(x)=\int_{0}^{R}nr^{n-1}r^{|\gamma|}G(r^{2})r^{m-|\delta|}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\zeta^{\gamma}\partial^{\delta}f_{m}(\zeta)\,d\sigma(\zeta)\,dr.

For
m\geq k
, the integral over
\mathbb{S}
vanishes since

\partial^{\delta}f_{m}\in H_{m-|\delta|}
and

|\gamma|<m-|\delta|
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exists a constant
C=C(n,\alpha,p,k)
such that for all

f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
with

f(x)=\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}S_{m}(|x|)f_{m}(x)
,
the series starting from
k
, we have

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}\leq C\sum_{|\kappa|=k}\|\partial^{\kappa}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}.

Proof.

Pick an
R
with
0<R<1
, say
R=1/2
.
Let
\lambda
be a multi-index with
|\lambda|=k-1
.
Then, by Lemmas
6.2
and
6.3
,

\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\partial^{\lambda}f(x)|^{p}(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+p(k-1)}\,d\nu(x)\lesssim\sum_{|\kappa|=k}\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\partial^{\kappa}f(x)|^{p}(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+pk}\,d\nu(x),

i.e.,

\|\partial^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+p(k-1)}}\lesssim\sum_{|\kappa|=k}\|\partial^{\kappa}f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+pk}}
.
Repeating this for multi-indices
|\lambda|=k-2,k-3,\dots
,
we obtain the desired result.
∎

We remove the restriction on
f
with the following two elementary lemmas.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
p_{m}\in H_{m}
.
Then, for every multi-index
\lambda
with
|\lambda|<m
, we have

\partial^{\lambda}\bigl(S_{m}(|x|)p_{m}(x)\bigr)_{|_{x=0}}=0
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\partial^{\lambda}(S_{m}(|x|)p_{m}(x))=\sum_{\gamma\leq\lambda}\binom{\lambda}{\gamma}\partial^{\lambda-\gamma}S_{m}(|x|)\partial^{\gamma}p_{m}(x)
,
and
\partial^{\gamma}p_{m}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree

m-|\gamma|
.
Because
m-|\gamma|\geq 1
, we have
\partial^{\gamma}p_{m}(0)=0

and the result follows.
∎

For
k\geq 0
,

\mathcal{P}_{k}=\bigl\{\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{m}(|x|)p_{m}(x):p_{m}\in H_{m}\bigr\}

is a finite dimensional vector space and

\|p\|_{0}:=\sum_{|\lambda|\leq k}|\partial^{\lambda}p(0)|\qquad(p\in\mathcal{P}_{k})

is a norm on
\mathcal{P}_{k}
.
The only non-trivial part to check is
\|p\|_{0}=0
implies

p\equiv 0
which is done in the next lemma.

Lemma 6.6
.

If
p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
and
\|p\|_{0}=0
, then
p\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Suppose
p(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{m}(|x|)p_{m}(x)
and
\|p\|_{0}=0
.
We use induction to show that each
p_{m}=0
.
First,
p(0)=0
implies
p_{0}=0
.
Next, assume that
p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{j-1}=0

(j\leq k)
.
Let
\lambda
be a multi-index with
|\lambda|=j
.
Then, by the induction hypothesis and Lemma
6.5
,

\displaystyle 0=\partial^{\lambda}p(0)

\displaystyle=\partial^{\lambda}\Bigl(\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{m}(|x|)p_{m}(x)\Bigr)_{|_{x=0}}=\partial^{\lambda}\bigl(S_{j}(|x|)p_{j}(x)\bigr)_{|_{x=0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\leq\lambda}\binom{\lambda}{\gamma}\bigl(\partial^{\lambda-\gamma}S_{j}(|x|)\partial^{\gamma}p_{j}(x)\bigr)_{|_{x=0}}=S_{j}(0)\partial^{\lambda}p_{j}(0),

since
\partial^{\gamma}p_{j}(0)=0
for
|\gamma|\leq|\lambda|-1
.
Because
S_{j}(0)\neq 0
, we see that
\partial^{\lambda}p_{j}(0)=0
, and since this is true
for all
|\lambda|=j
, we conclude that
p_{j}=0
.
∎

We now show part (d)

.
For this we will use two lemmas.
First one is a mean-value inequality of Grellier-Jaming
for partial derivatives of

of an

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
,
0<p<\infty
and
k,d\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exists a constant
C=C(n,\varepsilon,p,k,d)
such that
for all
x\in\mathbb{B}
,
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
and
multi-indices
\lambda
with
|\lambda|\leq d
,
we have

|\partial^{\lambda}N^{k}f(x)|^{p}\leq\frac{C}{(1-|x|)^{n+pd}}\int_{B(x,(1-|x|)\varepsilon)}|N^{k}f(y)|^{p}\,d\nu(y).

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
1\leq p<\infty
.
There exists
C=C(n,\alpha,p)
such that for all
g\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B})
,
we have

\|g-g(0)\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}\leq C\Bigl(\|Ng\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+p}}+\sup_{|x|\leq 1/2}|\nabla Ng(x)|\Bigr).

Proof.

For
0<r<1
ad
\zeta\in\mathbb{S}
, we have

g(r\zeta)-g(0)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{Ng(t\zeta)}{t}\,dt.

Integrating in polar coordinates and using Hardy’s inequality (
33
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\|g-g(0)\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\int_{0}^{1}nr^{n-1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}|g(r\zeta)-g(0)|^{p}\,dr\,d\sigma(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\alpha}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{|Ng(t\zeta)|}{t}\,dt\Bigr)^{p}\,dr\,d\sigma(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\alpha+p}\frac{|Ng(r\zeta)|^{p}}{r^{p}}\,dr\,d\sigma(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{|Ng(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{p+n-1}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+p}\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|Ng(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{p+n-1}}\,d\nu_{\alpha+p}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{B}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|Ng(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{p+n-1}}\,d\nu_{\alpha+p}(x)=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

We have
I_{2}\lesssim\|Ng\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha+p}}
.
For the first integral note that by Lagrange’s mean-value inequality,
for
|x|\leq 1/2
,

|Ng(x)|\leq|x|\sup_{|y|\leq 1/2}|\nabla Ng(y)|,

and so,

I_{1}\leq\sup_{|y|\leq 1/2}|\nabla Ng(y)|^{p}\int_{0}^{1/2}nr^{n-1}\frac{r^{p}}{r^{p+n-1}}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+p}\,dr\lesssim\sup_{|y|\leq 1/2}|\nabla Ng(y)|^{p}.\qed
