Feasibility of the effectiveness of a psychoeducational intervention on the physical and psychosexual functioning of women being treated for cervical cancer in a tertiary hospital in Southwest, Nigeria: a quasi-experimental study
Authors: Temitayo Moyosore Adebisi, Timothy Abiola Olusesan Oluwasola, Chizoma Millicent Ndikom, Bolaji Lateef Salako
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCervical cancer is a global public health concern with significant morbidity and mortality even with advancement in treatment strategies. While efforts have been directed primarily to prevention and elimination, there is need to improve the health indices of those who have already been diagnosed. This study was designed to assess the feasibility of the effectiveness of a psycho-educational intervention on the physical and psychosexual functioning as well as health-related quality of life of women with cervical cancer.MethodsA quasi-experimental single group pre- and post-test design was used to assess the effectiveness of a single-module psychoeducational intervention developed by the researcher through a guide developed for the design of psychoeducational interventions for women with gynaecological cancers. This was carried out among twenty (20) women (> 18years) attending the radiation and clinical oncology unit of Federal Medical Centre, Abeokuta, Nigeria. Women’s functioning levels were assessed using two (2) standardized tools at baseline and four (4) weeks after the intervention. Data was analyzed with SPSS version 26 and hypotheses were tested using Wilcoxon Signed Rank test at a significance level of ≥ 0.05.Result60% were between 46 to 56years with a mean age of 51.7 ± 7.6; 70% were Yoruba, 45% were married and a higher percentage (35%) were traders. None of the respondents was ever vaccinated against Human Papilloma Virus and were on chemotherapy. Only 45% had partner support and 5 to 6 children with majority (55%) being post-menopausal. The intervention showed evidence of clinical relevance for all domains but did not yield a statistical significance for the physical (d=-0.016, p=0.954), psychological (d=-0.015, p=0.678) and sexual functioning (d=-0.098, p=0.678). However, HRQoL was significant (d=-0.702, p=0.011). Pain and partner support were the most significant socio-economic predictors of quality of life.ConclusionsPsychoeducational intervention can help improve the physical and psychosexual domains of functioning as well as the health-related quality of life of women with cervical carcinoma.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16288-5.

Background

Cervical cancer tops the list of death related to cancer in Africa [1]. Although, advances in treatment strategies have improved survival, the disease coupled with its treatment have profound effect on quality of life [2]. Therefore, survivorship care and improvement of quality of life for women being diagnosed and treated for cervical cancer have become an important discuss [3, 4].

Treatment for cervical carcinoma usually consists of surgery, chemotherapy and radiotherapy or a combination of these options. However, the choice of treatment depends on several factors including stage of the disease, age, lymph node involvement, co-existing / chronic diseases and the probability of occurrence [5]. The side effects of treatment also vary from woman to woman and may include: weight loss, hair loss, skin changes, loss of self-esteem, fatigue, anxiety and depression [6, 7]. These are usually in addition to the physical and psychological effects brought about by the disease in the first place and will usually increase women’s vulnerability with significant influence on their health-related quality of life [8].

Physical impact of cancer and its treatment is usually the most obvious and frequently discussed, while the effects on the psychological and mental health do not receive as much attention despite the fact that many studies have been able to establish the increased vulnerability of this group of women to anxiety, depression and low quality of life [9]. In addition, due to the location of the disease, there are changes in the female reproductive system which leads to significant sexual, psychological and body image concerns often influencing the sexual relationship between these women and their partners.

Psycho-educational and psychotherapeutic approaches have been utilized by researchers to deliver interventions to improve the physical and psychosexual functioning of women being treated for cervical cancer [10]. However, Psycho-educational interventions (PEI) have been suggested by many researchers to improve sexual functioning, survival benefits and better quality of life [11]. Most hospitals rendering oncology care in this part of the world cater more to amelioration of disease than these aspects [12]. While all patients reported receiving health education during hospital appointments, only a few reported having contacts with a psycho-oncologist despite the high prevalence of psychological dysfunction and distress among people living with cancer.

Therefore, considering the impact of cervical cancer and its treatment on quality of life and most especially, on the psychosexual and physical domains of functioning during before, during and after completion of treatment, the researcher designed this study to introduce and evaluate the feasibility of the effectiveness of a newly developed psychoeducational intervention on the health-related quality of life, physical and psychosexual functioning of women being treated for cervical cancer in the radiation and clinical oncology unit of the Federal Medical Centre, Abeokuta, Nigeria.

Methods

A quasi-experimental single group pre-and post-test design was utilized. The baseline functioning of women were assessed before commencement of treatment and four weeks after the newly-developed psycho-educational intervention package was delivered using the same data collection tools. Abeokuta, which is one of the six (6) states in Southwest Nigeria, served as the study area. The state is being inhabited by various ethnic groups but its primary inhabitants are the Yoruba tribe. The city houses a number of private and government owned health care facilities with Federal Medical Centre (FMC), Abeokuta being one of the two federal government owned institutions in Ogun State.

The study population were women with cervical cancer attending the radiation and clinical oncology clinic of Federal Medical Centre, Abeokuta. The sample size was determined using the sample size formula for an intervention study [13] which gave rise to 202 women (inclusive of attrition). However, since this study was designed to evaluate the feasibility of the main study, sample size was determined using 10% of 202. Therefore, 20 women were selected for this study. These women were selected using simple ballot technique and informed consent was obtained before data collection began. Only women (≥ 18years) that were diagnosed with cervical cancer with a histological evidence and will be receiving any of the prescribed treatment: surgery, chemotherapy or radiotherapy (to enable assessment of the impact of the intervention) while those who had been diagnosed of dementia or any other cognitive disorder/condition which may impair the ability to recall, participated in a similar intervention study within the frame of 6 months prior recruitment for this study or already undergoing any form of psychotherapy were excluded from the study.

Two validated tools were adapted for data collection. The first is the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer-Quality of life core Questionnaire version 3 (EORTC)-QLQC-30, a standardized and generally accepted tool for assessing the HRQoL in oncology with a test-retest reliability score of 0.927 in Sub Saharan Africa [14, 15] and the second tool is the Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI)-6 which is a widely-used measure of sexual dysfunction in women that evaluates desire, arousal, lubrication, orgasm, satisfaction and pain in the preceding four weeks has a test-retest reliability score of 0.94 [16]. These, together with some researcher-structured items made up the data collection instrument and the result of the reliability testing was a Cronbach alpha of 0.87. This instrument was translated to Yoruba (for Yoruba-only speaking respondents) and back translated to English to ensure accuracy.

The psycho-educational intervention package for this study was designed according to the recommendations derived from the systematic review of psycho-educational interventions to improve the quality of life and psychological outcomes in women being treated for gynaecological cancers [17], the report of the program designed to test the feasibility of implementing psycho-educational intervention for women with gynaecological cancers by [18], and the result of a trial to assess the effects of a theory driven psychoeducational intervention for women with gynaecological cancers [19]. These authors suggested that in order for the intervention to be effective, its design must be theory-based and incorporate psychological support, behavior therapy and information provision. Also, the Nurse is the most effective provider.

For the intervention evaluated in this study, it was administered by a Registered Nurse/Midwife who also has a Master’s degree (M.Sc.) in Maternal and Child Health Nursing. Psychological support was given in the form of counseling, behavioural therapy was given by teaching breathing exercises, coping skills and relaxation techniques while information provision was achieved by teaching patients about self-care, cervical cancer and its treatment. This package was given in a in a room over a single session, individually and/or with spouse and lasted between 40 and 60 min. Respondents were given opportunities to make clarifications before terminating each session. On completion of each session, women were given a hard copy of the intervention (in form of a pamphlet) together with weekly reinforcement of the same intervention via telephone.

Data collection took place in a total of four phases: in the first phase, there was a purposive selection of study site while the second phase entailed obtaining informed consent and recruitment of study participants which lasted for 4 weeks. Phase three involved assessment of the baseline physical and psychosexual functioning and delivery of the psychoeducational intervention being assessed. Four (4) weeks after phase three, women were interviewed with the same questionnaire to obtain their post intervention HRQoL which was the last phase of the study. The study was approved by an ethics committee with approval number (UI/EC/24/0511) and all data collection processes were conducted in accordance with the ethical principles guiding human research and took place from September 10th, 2024 to October 31st, 2024. Data was analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 26 and presented using frequency tables and hypotheses tested using one sample t-test at a significance level of ≤ 0.05.

Discussion

The mean age was 51.7 ± 7.6 with majority being Christians, of Yoruba tribe, married and with stage 2 or 3 disease. Although, they all have some level of education, many had only secondary school education with majority being artisans. All women that participated in this study were only on chemotherapy. A higher number of women had either 3 or 4 children and none of them had ever took vaccine against Human Papilloma Virus (HPV). This further highlights the disparities in HPV vaccination coverage as stated in the literature on the “Global strategy to accelerate the elimination of cervical cancer as a public health problem”, that the vaccination coverage in LMICs is still low when compared to the High-income countries (HICs) [20]. Strikingly, few of them had undergone a routine pap smear test before onset of symptoms. This could imply that the Cervical cancer screening (cervical cancer screening) uptake is improving. However, the ultimate goal is regular screening [21]. Although, less than half of these women were married, a significant number of them had sexual partners with satisfactory sexual life before onset of symptoms and diagnosis while some still have partners who made sexual advances towards them. A bit more than half of them were post-menopausal.

About half of the women had good physical functioning pre intervention. Although, there appeared to be no significant difference in physical functioning post intervention. The most commonly reported physical symptoms ranged from pain, tiredness to nausea which significantly influenced their daily activities and subsequently, psychological functioning which have also been reported by previous studies [6, 22, 23]. A particular study on the impact of CC on the sexual and physical health of women diagnosed with CC among women in Ghana stated that women had pain and obvious physical changes that included emaciation and weakness which affected basic activity like walking [6]. Furthermore, the authors included that the physical symptoms led to an impairment of their psychological functioning.

Women psychological functioning scores were significantly lower than their physical functioning scores pre and post intervention. Although, the proportion of women with good psychological functioning did not increase post intervention, many women had improved psychological functioning score post intervention when compared with their pre intervention. This could be associated with the lower sexual functioning scores post intervention which is not uncommon among women receiving chemotherapy. One study reported that the side effects of chemotherapy may become more debilitating which may make women need more assistance with their daily activities thereby worsening their distress levels [24]. This could only explain the not so significant difference between the pre and post intervention psychological functioning scores but does not infer that the psychoeducational intervention is ineffective.

Sexual functioning appears to reduce significantly post intervention with many women having lower functioning score compared with their pre intervention scores which was statistically different. This may be due to the fact that sexual dysfunction requires a combination of evidenced based practices consisting of psycho-education, dilator use, hormone replacement therapy and couple therapy [25] whereas only psychoeducation was offered to the participants in this study.

Over all, women had good HRQoL at baseline when compared to post intervention which was statistically significant. This could be attributed to the small study population and may also be as a result of the fact that women who participated in this study had mainly stage 2 and 3 disease which have been reported to significantly influence HRQoL [26, 27]. Also, it may be attributed to their young age and low level of education which have been associated with good quality of life [28]. Family support, pain from treatment, other concurrent condition and study intervention were identified as the factors that influenced women’s HRQOL pre and post intervention as identified by several studies [28]. Therefore, this implies the need to increase the power of the study and modify the intervention under investigation to bridge the gaps identified in this study.

Conclusions

Psychoeducational intervention showed some clinical relevance in improving the functioning of women with cervical cancer. Therefore any psychoeducation aimed at improving women’s functioning post treatment for cervical cancer should be planned and commenced right from the beginning of treatment and should continue through treatment for an effective result. Also, these interventions should focus on improving the physical, psychological, emotional and sexual functioning of women with focus on the debilitating symptom in each domain: pain, anxiety, sexual and body image concerns. Also, when planning intervention to improve women’s sexual functioning, partner’s involvement and other interventions like hormone replacement therapy should be considered.
