miR-1911-3p Regulates Malignant Biological Behaviors and Glycolytic Activity of Triple-Negative Breast Cancer via FBLN5
Authors: Linna Kong, Huihui Zhang, Xufang Sui, Xueping Peng, Tiantian Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectivesBased on previous reports demonstrating the clinical potential of miR-1911-3p in breast cancer, its function and mechanisms in the development of TNBC were investigated, aiming to identify a novel biomarker.Materials and MethodsPaired tissues were sampled from 128 TNBC patients, and miR-1911-3p levels were compared between tumor and normal tissues. The clinical significance of miR-1911-3p was evaluated from the perspective of disease severity and patients’ prognosis. In vitro, the regulatory effects of miR-1911-3p on the malignant biological behaviors were assessed based on cell growth, motility, and glycolytic activity in MDA-MB-231 and HCC1806 cells. The mechanism evaluation was performed focusing on FBLN5 through luciferase reporter assay and related reversal experiments by co-regulating miR-1911-3p and FBLN5.ResultsSignificant upregulation of miR-1911-3p was observed in tumor tissues of TNBC patients (fold change of 1.35), which was associated with advanced TNM stage, higher Ki67 levels, and occurrence of lymph node metastasis. miR-1911-3p (HR = 3.036) was identified as an independent prognostic factor predicting 5-year recurrence-free survival (log‑rank P = 0.036) of TNBC patients. Consistent elevation of miR-1911-3p was also observed in TNBC cells, and its knockdown dramatically suppressed cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Glycolytic activity was revealed to mediate the regulation of cell biological functions. FBLN5 was identified as a downstream target of miR-1911-3p, and negative regulation of its expression by miR-1911-3p was observed. Silencing FBLN5 could reverse the tumor‑suppressive effect of miR-1911-3p on TNBC cells, which was considered the major molecular mechanism.ConclusionmiR-1911-3p was identified as an independent prognostic biomarker for TNBC. Different from previously reporter miRNAs, miR-1911-3p regulated glycolytic activity-dependent cell growth and motility by targeting FBLN5, indicating its potential in serving as therapeutic target for TNBC.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most prevalent malignant tumor in women, with a high incidence rate. Estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) are major molecules assisting the early screening of breast cancer and the classification of subtypes, which is critical for the selection of therapeutic strategies.1,2 Among the various subtypes of breast cancer, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), with a negative status of all indicators, is the most invasive and lacks effective screening biomarkers. From the perspective of molecular mechanisms, the high molecular heterogeneity of TNBC causes complex pathological processes, involving a variety of genes and signaling, which directly leads to the unsatisfactory effect of a single treatment.3,4 Although great progress has been made in the targeted treatment, immunotherapy, and various precision medicines, the prognosis of patients with TNBC, especially those with metastasis, is unsatisfactory. The recurrence has also been considered a critical adverse prognostic factor. Therefore, it is of urgent need to explore reliable biomarkers for the assessment of tumor onset, progression, and outcomes of TNBC, improving patients’ prognosis.

Glycolytic metabolism is one of the most important metabolic changes of tumor cells, providing an energetic basis for cell growth, migration, and invasion.5,6 Normal cells mainly rely on the tricarboxylic acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation pathways for energy supply, while tumor cells tend to rely on glycolysis to obtain energy, whether in an aerobic environment or an anaerobic environment.7 This difference in energy transformation precisely meets the demands of cancer cells for rapid proliferation. Hence, glycolytic metabolism has gradually become a therapeutic strategy, and related molecules have been considered promising therapeutic targets for human cancers.

The non-coding RNA family has attracted special attention in past decades, and the significance of microRNAs (miRNAs) in human cancers has been highlighted. Based on the dysregulation of miRNAs, their function is mainly defined as oncogenes and anti-oncogenes. Oncogenes are usually upregulated in tumors and could promote the malignancy of tumor cells, which further accelerates tumor progression.8,9 In contrast, anti-oncogenes were a series of downregulated molecules, inhibiting the biological function of tumor cells and suppressing the development of cancers. Hence, there have been a number of investigations attempting to identify candidate miRNAs with the help of sequencing, revealing their aberrant expression. miR-1911-3p was noticed in these studies, which was identified to be dysregulated in chemotherapy-resistant breast cancer.10 More importantly, miR-1911-3p was also revealed to have great diagnostic potential associated with the onset of breast cancer.11 miR-1911-3p shows significant potential in serving as a biomarker for TNBC, but there was a lack of data available to directly demonstrate its clinical value.

Downstream target is the critical for unclosing the regulatory mechanism of miRNAs. FBLN5, a predicted target of miR-1911-3p with high score and binding energy, was previously reported to participate in the progression of breast cancer. For instance, it was identified as a tumor suppressor of breast cancer, inhibiting cell proliferation and invasion through downregulating Ki67.12,13 Additionally, it was identified as a critical mediator for the regulator role of other ncRNAs in breast cancer, such as miR-370-3p and LINC01089, implying its potential involvement in the regulatory effect of miR-1911-3p in TNBC.14,15

This study focused on the significance of miR-1911-3p in the development of TNBC. From the clinical perspective, the significance of miR-1911-3p in evaluating disease severity and prognosis was evaluated. On the cell level, the regulatory effect of miR-1911-3p on the malignant function of TNBC cells based on glycolytic metabolism was evaluated. Additionally, the regulatory mechanism was investigated with the hypothesis of involving FBLN5. This study was proposed to identify a promising biomarker for TNBC and complete its regulatory mechanism during tumor development.

Materials and Methods

Patients and Sampling

A total of 128 TNBC patients were included from Zibo Central Hospital from January 2020 to December 2022. All patients were pathologically diagnosed through biopsy and met the following inclusion criteria: 1) patients received surgical treatment; 2) patients had never received any neoadjuvant therapies before surgery; 3) patients were primarily diagnosed with TNBC, excluding recurrence; 4) patients without any other complications, especially for malignancies; 5) patients had never received any anti-cancer therapy. The clinicopathological features of all study subjects were obtained from their electronic medical records.

A 5-year follow-up survey was conducted for all study subjects to collect their prognosis information. The survey started from the date of pathological diagnosis and was performed through telephone or outpatient review every 3 months. All-cause deaths and recurrence were recorded as endpoint events. The median follow-up period was 31.5 months (ranging from 3 to 60 months). This study had been approved by the Ethics Committee of Zibo Central Hospital (No. 2019117), and all participants had signed informed consent.

Sampling was conducted during surgery, collecting tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues (over 5 cm from the lesion). Collected tissues were frozen with liquid nitrogen and further stored at −80°C until the following analysis.

Cell Lines and Incubation

Four TNBC cells, MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, HCC1806, and a normal human breast tissue cell line, MCF-10A, were employed in the present study. All cells were purchased from ATCC (USA). Cell incubation was conducted with DMEM culture medium (cat. 31885–049, Gibco, USA). The culture medium was supplemented with 10% FBS (cat. 16000–044) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (cat. 15140148, all from Gibco, USA). Cells were maintained at 37°C with 5% CO2 and were available for the following experiments after reaching the logarithmic growth stage (fusion of 80–90%).

Cell Transfection

Cells were transfected with miR-1911-3p mimic (30 nM, 5’-CACCAGGCAUUGUGGUCUCC-3’), inhibitor (30 nM, 5’-GGAGACCACAAUGCCUGGUG-3’), or negative control (NC, 30 nM, 5’-GGACCAAATCTCGAGATTTGG-3’) to regulate its expression. While for the regulation of FBLN5, cells were transfected with corresponding small interference RNA (siRNA, 50 nM, targeting sequence 5’-CUGGUUUUACCCUCAAUGA-3’), and co-transfection was also conducted to evaluate the relationship between miR-1911-3p and FBLN5. Cell transfection was performed at room temperature with the help of Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA). Transfection efficiency was evaluated based on the expression of miR-1911-3p and FBLN5. Transfected cells with successful regulation were available for the following experiments after 48 h of cell transfection.

Real-Time Quantitative PCR

Total RNA was extracted from tissue samples and cell lines using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Extracted RNA was assessed using NanoDrop 2000 based on the ratio of OD260/280 and OD260/230. High-quality RNA (OD260/280 ranging 1.8–2.2 and OD260/230 no less than 2.0) was further reverse‑transcribed into cDNA using TaqMan MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystem, USA) or mRNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Qiagen, China). PCR amplification was conducted with cDNA and SYBR Green kit on the LightCycler Real-Time PCR system (both from Roche, France). The relative expression levels were calculated by the 2−∆∆CT method with U6 and GAPDH as internal references.

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay

The downstream targets of miR-1911-3p were predicted from public databases, including TargetScan, miRDB, and miRWalk. The intersection of the three databases was obtained through Venn plotting. The binding sites between miR-1911-3p and FBLN5 were predicted from TargetScan, and wild-type vectors were established by the cloning of the predicted sites into pGL3 plasmid (Promega, USA). Site mutation was conducted for the construction of mutant-type vectors. The established vectors were co-transfected with miR-1911-3p mimic, inhibitor, or NC in TNBC cells, and the relative luciferase activity of FBLN5 was detected with the employment of a luciferase reporter system with Renilla as internal reference.

Cell Proliferation Assay

Cells were seeded into 96-well plates and supplied with the complete DMEM culture medium. The plates were incubated at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 24, 48, and 72 h followed by adding the cell counting kit-8. Another 2-h incubation was performed, and then, the absorbance at 450 nm of the plates was detected using a microplate reader. The time-OD450 curve was plotted to indicate cell proliferation.

Cell Motility Assay

Cells were seeded into the upper chamber of the 24-well Transwell plates and supplied with the complete culture medium. The bottom chamber was filled with FBS-free culture medium to induce cell motility. Cells were allowed to move to the bottom chamber for 48 h. Then, unmoved cells were removed with cotton, and cells on the subsurface of the upper chamber were fixed and stained. Cells were counted under an optical microscope. For the assessment of cell invasion, the upper chambers were pre-coated with Matrigel, and the other processes were the same with the migration assessment.

Quantification of Pyruvic Acid and Lactic Acid Assay

Incubated cells were centrifuged, and the supernatant was collected and filtered through 0.22 μm filters. The content of pyruvate and lactic acid was analyzed with corresponding kits (pyruvate assay kit and lactic acid assay kit, both from Nanjing Jiancheng, China). Quantification was conducted based on the absorbance at 530 and 505 nm, respectively, according to the corresponding standard curves.

Quantification of Glucose Consumption and ATP

The concentration of glucose was detected in the supernatant of cell culture with the help of Glucose Content Detection kit (Solarbio, China) based on the absorbance at 505 nm. While the ATP levels were analyzed with the chemiluminescence method after cell lysis. Quantification was also displayed with corresponding standard curves.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses for clinical and cellular experiments were conducted using SPSS 26.0 software and GraphPad Prism 10.0 software. All data has been assessed meeting normal distribution based on the P–P plot. The homogeneity of variances was assessed using Levene’s test before statistical analysis. The assumption of homogeneity of variance was met for all groups. The evaluation of the clinical significance of miR-1911-3p was performed from the perspectives of disease severity and prognosis, through the chi-square test (for the difference comparison of counting data), Kaplan-Meier curve with log-rank test for difference comparison (for the comparison of recurrence-free survival), and Cox regression analysis (for the evaluation of prognostic factors). Comparison between normal and tumor tissues was conducted with the paired Student’s t-test. While for the cellular experiments, triplicate biological repetitions were conducted for all assays with three technical repetitions of each, and a difference comparison was conducted with the help of one-way ANOVA, followed by the LSD post-hoc test. Statistical significance was indicated by P < 0.05.

Cellular miR-1911-3p levels were compared between MCF-10A cells and four TNBC cells, and a significant increase of miR-1911-3p was observed in MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and HCC1806 cells (Figure 2a). MDA-MB-231 and HCC1806 cells were selected for the following functional evaluation due to their relatively high expression. miR-1911-3p was knocked down by the transfection of its inhibitors in both MDA-MB-231 (Figure 2b) and HCC1806 (Figure 2c) cells. After silencing miR-1911-3p, the proliferation (Figure 3a), migration (Figure 3b), and invasion (Figure 3c) of TNBC cells were significantly suppressed. Meanwhile, the knockdown of miR-1911-3p also suppressed the production of PA and LA, reduced the consumption of glucose, and inhibited cellular ATP levels, indicating the inhibition of glycolytic activity (Figure 3d). To evaluate the association of glycolytic activity with the inhibition of biological functions of TNBC cells, 2-Deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG), a competitive inhibitor of hexokinase, was employed to treat MDA-MB-231 and HCC1806 cells. 2-DG exerted dramatic inhibition on the glycolytic activity of MDA-MB-231 and HCC1806 cells (Figure 3d) but did not significantly affect the expression of miR-1911-3p in TNBC cells (Figure 2b and c). Additionally, 2-DG exerted a significant inhibitory effect on cell proliferation, migration, and invasion (Figure 3a–c). Simultaneous silencing of miR-1911-3p with 2-DG showed no enhancement of the suppressed miR-1911-3p levels and biological functions of TNBC cells (Figure 3a–d), indicating no additive or synergistic effects. It suggested that miR-1911-3p inhibitor and 2-DG converge on overlapping downstream effector pathways, further supporting the critical role of glycolysis in miR-1911-3p-mediated oncogenic functions.

Targeting FBLN5 Mediating the Regulatory Effect of miR-1911-3p

Downstream targets of miR-1911-3p were predicted from TargetScan, miRDB, and miRWalk databases. There were 29 genes predicted from all three databases, where FBLN5 showed the highest scoring (Figure 4a). FBLN5 was found to be downregulated in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (Figure S1a), which was negatively correlated with the tissue miR-1911-3p levels (r = −0.711, Figure S1b). Overexpressing FBLN5 could significantly suppress glycolytic activity in MDA-MB-231 cells (Figure S1c and d), indicating the association between FBLN5 and glycolytic phenotype. Discussion

miR-1911-3p is a novel cancer-related miRNA, which has been demonstrated to participate in the progression of various human cancers. For example, miR-1911-3p was revealed to target mEAK-7 in lung cancer cells, which further inhibits cancer development via the mTOR signaling.16 As the cognate miRNA of miR-1911-3p, miR-1911-5p was reported to regulate cisplatin resistance in gastric cancer and was identified as a potential biomarker in neuroglioma.17,18 In breast cancer, miR-1911-3p was predicted to have diagnostic potential and showed a potential association with chemotherapy sensitivity.10,11 Herein, significant upregulation of miR-1911-3p was observed in tumor tissues of TNBC patients, consistent with its dysregulation in chemotherapy-resistant breast cancer cells.10 The severity and development of TNBC were mainly evaluated based on TNM stage, a systemic indicator involving tumor size, the occurrence of LNM, and distant metastasis.19 The occurrence of LNM is also closely associated with the development and prognosis of TNBC patients, which could indicate the high tumor invasiveness and provide a basis for therapeutic strategies.19 Ki67 is another important indicator associated with the proliferative and invasive capacity of tumor cells.20 miR-1911-3p was revealed to show close association with the TNM stage, LNM incidence, and Ki67 levels in TNBC patients, indicating its critical role in the TNBC development and the significant prognostic potential. Recurrence is a crucial issue in the prognosis research of TNBC, especially in patients in the early and middle stages. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) is a commonly used endpoint event in prognosis assessment of human cancers, especially in TNBC.21–23 Considering the high recurrence rate within 2–3 years after surgery, this study performed a 5-year follow-up and highlighted the significant predictive value of miR-1911-3p. It was found that the higher miR-1911-3p was significantly associated with the 5-year RFS and was identified as an independent prognostic factor. Hence, miR-1911-3p was suggested to have great potential in indicating disease severity and prognosis. In the clinical evaluation, the mean expression of miR-1911-3p in tumor tissues was employed as the cutoff, which could represent the central tendency of the entire population, making it a statistically appropriate threshold to distinguish biologically distinct subgroups. On the other hand, the employment of mean expression level as the cutoff aligns with routine clinical practice where patients are stratified based on deviation from the population normal. However, to promote the clinical application of miR-1911-3p in clinical management of TNBC, the efficiency of more cutoffs should be evaluated. Median and ROC-derived cutoffs are promising indicators in previous cancer-related research, which should attract special attention in future investigations. Moreover, limited to the study period and hospital scale, the sample size of the present study is relatively small, limiting the robustness of the clinical evaluation. Except for expanding sample size, external validation with other datasets is also necessary. Therefore, future investigations would pay attention to multiple research centers with larger sample sizes.

During the progression of tumors, abnormal changes occurred in the characteristics of tumor cells, involving the proliferative, migrative, and invasive capacities. Cell proliferation is closely associated with tumor growth. Although there is no direct association between tumor size and patients’ prognosis, the proliferation of tumor cells provides a basis for further metastasis.24,25 Metastasis is the main cause of most cancer-related deaths. Tumor cells at the primary site gradually invade deeper tissues and colonize in distant organs through the bloodstream, lymphatic vessels, or direct penetration of adjacent tissues. Eventually, cells regain proliferative capacity in those distant locations.26 Cell migration and invasion play a critical role in driving this process. In TNBC cells, increasing miR-1911-3p levels were also observed, and its knockdown significantly inhibited cell growth and motility. Therefore, miR-1911-3p was identified as a potential tumor promoter, and its silencing was hypothesized as a potential therapeutic strategy for TNBC. Reversed regulations of miRNAs are commonly employed methods for functional validation. Significant upregulation of miR-1911-3p was observed in both TNBC tissues and cell lines. Overexpressing miR-1911-3p might be accompanied by systemic errors in evaluating its expression and function, and therefore, only knockdown of miR-1911-3p was conducted in the present study. However, gain-of‑function experiments have not been performed to confirm the effect of miR-1911-3p overexpression on TNBC cells, which should be comprehensively evaluated in future investigation.

Considering the important role of metabolic reprogramming in tumor cells, aerobic glycolysis, a key metabolic pathway, attracts special attention in various mechanisms investigations on tumor cells. Aerobic glycolysis provides energy supplies for tumor cells, and its metabolic intermediates play significant roles in the synthesis of biological macromolecules.27 In-depth investigations on aerobic glycolysis could provide an important theoretical foundation for early diagnosis, precise treatment, and overcoming drug resistance.28–30 Lactic acid (LA) and pyruvic acid (PA) are key metabolic products of aerobic glycolysis, forming an acidic environment and further causing damage to T cells, which would inactivate the immune system and benefit the immune escape of tumor cells.31 Glucose consumption is a direct indicator evaluating aerobic glycolysis, providing raw material input and a rate basis. ATP levels were linked to glucose consumption through PA, providing a basis for the rapid division of tumor cells.32,33 Hence, aerobic glycolysis is the basis for malignant tumor cell behaviors. However, except for the mentioned indicators, the evaluation aerobic glycolysis needs more evidence, involving ECAR and related enzymes. The suppression of PA, LA, glucose consumption, and ATP by miR-1911-3p knockdown could indicate the reduced entire glycolytic pathway flux at the functional level, which is a hallmark of suppressed glycolytic metabolism in cancer cells, and this inhibitory effect has not been enhanced by the glycolysis inhibitor, suggesting that miR-1911-3p inhibitor and 2-DG operate along the same functional axis. In other words, the tumor-suppressive function of miR-1911-3p knockdown was primarily achieved through modulating glycolytic pathway activity. Therefore, miR-1911-3p was hypothesized to regulate glycolytic activity-dependent growth and motility of tumor cells, and further investigations for evaluating aerobic glycolysis are needed.

In molecular mechanisms, miRNAs could target the 3’UTR of functional mRNAs, regulating their functional role in tumor progression, which further mediates disease development. Among predicted downstream targets of miR-1911-3p, FBLN5 was previously identified as a cancer regulator and mediates the progression of breast cancer.15,34–37 FBLN5 was revealed to regulate Ki67 levels and further inhibit cell proliferation and invasion in breast cancer.13 Herein, FBLN5 could reverse the suppressive effect of miR-1911-3p knockdown on TNBC cells, indicating its involvement in the regulation. Regarding the regulation of glycolytic activity, silencing FBLN5 also alleviated the inhibitory effect of miR-1911-3p knockdown, and the typical glycolysis inhibitor could reverse the effect of FBLN5. Therefore, miR-1911-3p/FBLN5 was identified as a promoting axis in TNBC cellular progression, and the knockdown of this axis could suppressed glycolytic activity-dependent cell growth and motility. miRNA was accepted to target mRNA and regulate their function at posttranscriptional levels. The regulation of FBLN5 by miR-1911-3p was only confirmed on the mRNA level, where evidence on the protein level was lacked. Further validation would be performed to confirmed the regulation of FBLN5 protein by miR-1911-3p. Additionally, although FBLN5 was demonstrated to reverse miR-1911-3p-mediated suppression of glycolytic activity, the molecular mechanism by which FBLN5 modulates glycolysis is not fully resolved. The regulation of critical signaling pathways related with glycolysis and specific rate-limiting enzymes should be fully investigated.

The identified functional molecules have been demonstrated as therapeutic targets for TNBC. For example, the IRF5/SLC7A5/IDO1 was demonstrated to modulate TNBC cell biological function via regulating the tryptophan metabolism, and it was identified as the molecular mechanism mediating the inhibitory effect of isoliquiritigenin on TNBC.38 The activation of CDK16-JAK1 axis was also suggested to mediate the alleviation of TNBC by copper.39 Therefore, the demonstrated regulatory effect of the miR-1911-3p/FBLN5 axis on TNBC cells shows their great potential in serving as novel therapeutic targets.

In conclusion, increasing miR-1911-3p was revealed to evaluate malignant disease development and predict unfavorable 5-year recurrence-free survival, indicating its potential in serving as a prognostic biomarker of TNBC. miR-1911-3p regulates the biological functions and glycolytic activity of tumor cells. FBLN5 was revealed to show a close association with glycolytic activity of TNBC cells, which was considered the promising molecular mechanisms underlying the function of miR-1911-3p.
