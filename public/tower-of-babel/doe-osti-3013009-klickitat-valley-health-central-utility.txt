Executive Summary

This project replaced legacy heating and cooling equipment at Klickitat Valley Health (KVH), a
rural hospital in Goldendale, Washington. Work included constructing a new chiller building,
installing modern hydronic piping systems, replacing the main Emergency Department air
handler (Air Handlining Unit (AHU-1)), upgrading electrical systems, and converting the
Emergency Department wing from electric reheat to hydronic heating and cooling.
Commissioning, testing, adjusting, and balancing were completed under this grant.​
​
These upgrades improve energy efficiency, operational resilience, and system reliability while
preparing the campus for future electrification and renewable energy integration. They also
support growth in healthcare services for a rural population. The project directly reduces energy
costs and provides a foundation for future decarbonization measures, including a FEMA-funded
microgrid and a large-scale ground source heat pump (GSHP) project. These follow-on
initiatives would not have been possible without the hydronic backbone built through this
project.

Introduction

Klickitat Valley Health operates critical healthcare infrastructure for a rural community in
south-central Washington State. Prior to this project, much of the HVAC and central plant
infrastructure relied on outdated, inefficient, and less resilient technology. This modernization
addressed aging infrastructure while enabling future decarbonization, resiliency, and efficiency
goals.​
​
The project focused on:​
- Replacing obsolete chiller equipment and converting portions of the heating distribution system
from electric reheat to hydronic.​
- Installing an efficient, flexible hydronic backbone to enable future electrification and
low-carbon heating and cooling.​
- Improving reliability and patient comfort while supporting growth in patient services.

Background

The legacy central utility plant did not meet modern efficiency or reliability standards and
constrained future expansion and carbon reduction. KVH sought to optimize heating and cooling
operations and create infrastructure capable of integrating advanced clean energy technologies.​
​
This project was part of KVH’s broader strategy to:​
- Achieve carbon neutrality and climate resilience in healthcare operations.​
- Electrify heating and cooling loads using modern, flexible systems.​
- Prepare for integration with on-site renewable generation microgrid and a ground source heat
pump system.

Results and Discussion

1. Finalize System Design and Permitting – 100% design drawings approved, City and DOH
permits secured.​
2. Procurement & Delivery of Equipment – Air handler, chillers, hydronic piping, and associated
electrical components procured.​
3. Installation – New chiller plant, cooling towers, and piping installed; hydronic distribution tied
to AHU-1.​
4. Start-up & Commissioning – All systems tested, balanced, and commissioned.​
5. Permit Close-out – All inspections passed, permits closed by June 16, 2025.

Key Achievements

- Constructed new chiller building and installed modern chillers.​
- Converted Emergency Department wing from electric reheat to hydronic heating.​
- Upgraded main Emergency Department air handler (AHU-1).​
- Installed a campus-wide hydronic backbone enabling future electrification.
- Integrated the new equipment into our Building Automation System.​
- Commissioned and balanced all new systems for operational readiness.

Lessons Learned

- Supply chain challenges required creative scheduling and patience.​
- System integration between old and new equipment is challenging, requiring operational
adjustments to prioritize patient comfort over maximum efficiency.​
- Rural hospital electrification is possible when planned around hydronic systems and
future-ready equipment.

Conclusions

The Central Utility Plant Modernization improved efficiency, reliability, and resilience at
Klickitat Valley Health. It enables additional decarbonization projects, including a fully designed
microgrid (ready for construction despite loss of BRIC funding) and a future ground source heat
pump system. The work completed under this grant laid the necessary groundwork for these
transformative projects.

Future Work/Recommendations

- Microgrid: KVH has completed the design of a campus-wide microgrid, initially planned for
FEMA BRIC funding. Although federal funding was canceled, the design remains ready for
construction when alternative funding is secured. We are using the WA State Climate
Commitment Act matching grant to complete the solar carport portion of the microgrid during
the summer of 2026. ​
- Ground Source Heat Pump (GSHP): A planned GSHP system will significantly reduce carbon
emissions by replacing natural gas heating loads. This project would not be feasible without the
hydronic backbone installed under this grant. KVH recently secured 100% funding for the GSHP
and will be breaking ground in Q4-2025.
