Emergence of a correlated insulating state in bulk 1T- NbSe 2 \text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} via metal intercalation
M. Tomlinson, AKM A. Rahman, S. Devi, R. Tuchikawa, M. Ishigami, D. Le, Md Z. Mohayman, A. Kushima, Y. Nakajima

Abstract
The 1T polymorph of NbSe 2 \text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , long confined to the monolayer limit, has remained inaccessible in bulk. Here, we report the realization of bulk 1T- NbSe 2 \text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} via electrochemical Sn intercalation. Transmission electron microscopy directly reveals the formation of the 1T structure induced by Sn intercalation. The intercalated samples exhibit insulating transport behavior, in stark contrast to metallic 2H- NbSe 2 \text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} . Density functional theory calculations, however, predict a metallic band structure, highlighting the crucial role of emergent electronic correlations in the observed insulating state. Raman spectroscopy further reveals vibrational modes associated with Sn intercalation and possible charge density wave order. Our results establish electrochemical intercalation as an effective route to stabilize otherwise inaccessible bulk polytypes, positioning bulk 1T- NbSe 2 \text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} as a new platform for investigating correlated electronic states.



I
Introduction

Transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) host a wide range of emergent electronic phases enabled by their structural polymorphism. Distinct coordinations—trigonal prismatic H phase and octahedral T phase—stabilize markedly different electronic states, ranging from superconductivity to correlation-driven insulating behavior. Among these materials,
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a prototypical system in which intertwined orders emerge. The bulk 2H phase [FIG.1 (a)] undergoes a charge density wave (CDW) transition at 33 K
Moncton et al. 1977
;
Kiss et al. 2007
, followed by superconductivity below 7.2 K
Harper et al. 1975
. The monolayer 1H phase hosts Ising superconductivity, in which electron spins are locked in the out-of-plane direction due to spin-orbit coupling, protected against in-plane magnetic fields
Xi et al. 2016
. Additional stacking variants, such as 3R and 4H phases, further enrich this phase space with noncentrosymmetric superconductivity
Li et al.
and possible bulk Ising superconductivity
Patra et al. 2025
;
Martino et al. 2026
.

In contrast, the octahedrally coordinated 1T phase of
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
[FIG.1 (b)]—believed to harbor strong correlation effects—has remained inaccessible in bulk form and has only been observed in monolayer islands
Nakata et al. 2016
;
Nakata et al. 2021
;
Liu et al. 2021a
;
Liu et al. 2021b
;
Zhang et al. 2024
. In the molecular-beam-eptaxy-grown monolayer
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) reveals a
\sqrt{13}\times\sqrt{13}
star-of-David CDW accompanied by a Mott gap, suggesting a correlated insulating state
Nakata et al. 2016
;
Nakata et al. 2021
;
Liu et al. 2021a
;
Liu et al. 2021b
. Another STM study of magnetic molecules deposited on the monolayer 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
elucidate the spinon-Kondo effect, pointing toward the realization of a quantum spin liquid state
Zhang et al. 2024
. Thus, realizing the 1T phase in bulk
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
would provide a new platform for exploring correlation-driven phenomena beyond the two-dimensional limit. A promising route to this regime is electrochemical intercalation, which can drive structural transformations, such as the 2H-to-1T transition in
\text{MoS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Shi et al. 2024
.

Here we report that electrochemical Sn intercalation stabilizes the 1T phase of
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in bulk form. X-ray diffraction (XRD) reveals a systematic expansion of the interlayer spacing, consistent with Sn intercalation in
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) directly confirms the emergence of the 1T structure in the Sn-intercalated
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Transport measurements show insulating behavior at low temperatures without a clear phase transition between 2 and 300 K, reminiscent of Mott insulating TMDs such as 1T-
\text{TaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. In contrast, density functional theory (DFT) predicts a metallic band structure in the bulk 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, indicating that electronic correlations are essential to understanding the observed insulating state. Raman spectroscopy further identifies additional vibrational modes, associated with intercalant-induced distortions and/or a possible CDW formation. These results establish electrochemical Sn intercalation as an effective route to stabilize otherwise inaccessible bulk polytypes in TMDs. The realization of bulk 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
opens a pathway to investigate correlated insulating states and related quantum phases in a tunable, three-dimensional platform.

II
Methods

Polycrystalline
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
was synthesized via a solid-state reaction. Elemental Nb and Se in a stoichiometric ratio were heated in a sealed quartz ampule at 850
∘
C for 5 days. High-quality single crystals were subsequently grown by chemical vapor transport using I
2
as the transport agent. The polycrystalline material sealed in a quartz ampule with 3 mg/cm
3
of I
2
was placed in a two-zone furnace at 850
∘
C and 800
∘
C for 21 days.

For electrochemical intercalation, the as-grown
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
crystals were wrapped in high-purity Sn wire serving as the working electrode, with a second Sn wire as the counter electrode. A leak-free Ag/AgCl electrode was used as the reference. Intercalation was performed at a constant potential of
-0.7
V at room temperature for 2 h, using 0.1% HCl as the electrolyte. The Sn content in the intercalated samples was determined by X-ray fluorescence (XRF) spectroscopy.

Electrical resistivity was measured in the ab-plane with a standard four-wire configuration using an AC resistance bridge. An excitation current of 316
\mu
A was applied. Raman spectra were collected using a 532 nm laser.

High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) analysis was performed using Tecnai F30 (FEI) operated at 300 kV acceleration voltage. TEM sample was prepared by attaching a piece of synthesized NbSe
2
sample on a Cu TEM grid using conductive epoxy. Peeling off the sample after curing the epoxy leaves thin flakes of NbSe
2
on the grid for the observation.

Spin-polarized DFT calculations are performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP 5.4.4)
Kresse and Hafner 1993
;
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
, employing the projector-augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotential method and a plane wave basis set
Blöchl 1994
;
Kresse and Joubert 1999
. We use the generalized-gradient approximation (GGA) in the form of Perdew-Berke-Enzerhoff (PBE) functionals
Perdew et al. 1996
;
Perdew et al. 1997
with a Hubbard
U
of 3 eV for Nb 4
d
electrons for describing the exchange correlation
Liu et al. 2021b
;
Liu et al. 2024
. DFT-D3 correction was used for accounting for the van der Waals (vdW) interactions
Grimme et al. 2010
. We set a cutoff energy of 500 eV for plane-wave expansion. All electronic iterations are converged with 0.01 meV threshold. The internal coordinate of atoms and lattice constant of the bulk structures are optimized so that forces acting on each atom are less than 5 meV/Å and the stress is minimized to less than
0.2k_{\mathrm{B}}
. We model bulk structures
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with lateral dimension of
\sqrt{13}\times\sqrt{13}
which include two layers of
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(26 Nb and 52 Se atoms). We chose this supercell to accommodate the potential
\sqrt{13}\times\sqrt{13}
CDW reconstruction of 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. For modeling Sn intercalation, we used one Sn between the
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layers, i.e.,
\sim
7.7% Sn intercalation. We use Gaussian smearing method with
\sigma
= 0.1 eV and sample Brillouin Zone with a 3
\times
3
\times
3
\Gamma
-centered grid.

III
Results and Discussion

We first confirm that the crystal structure of pristine
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is consistent with the 2H polytype (space group
P6_{3}/mmc
), as shown in Fig.
1
(c). The refined lattice constant is
c=12.5668(7)
Å, in good agreement with previous reports
Meyer et al. 1975
;
Naik et al. 2019
. Notably, while the XRD pattern of the Sn-intercalated sample closely resembles that of pristine
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, all peaks shift systematically toward lower
2\theta
, as highlighted in the inset of Fig.
1
(c). This shift indicates a significant expansion of the interlayer spacing, suggesting the successful intercalation of Sn in the vdW gap.

XRF measurements determine the intercalated Sn concentration to be approximately 6%. Assuming that the intercalated sample retains the 2H structure, we obtain an effective lattice constant of
c=13.0340(8)
Å, corresponding to an expansion rate of
\sim 0.08
Å/%. This value is nearly an order of magnitude larger than the previously reported expansion rate of
\sim 0.01
Å/% for Sn intercalation in polycrystalline 2H-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Naik et al. 2019
. The anomalously large lattice expansion observed in our sample strongly suggests that Sn is not simply intercalated within the 2H phase. Instead, it points to an intercalation-driven structural transformation into a distinct phase.

\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
has a pronounced impact on its charge transport. Pristine
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the 2H structure exhibits metallic behavior upon cooling, as shown in FIG.

\sim
290
\mu\Omega
cm and
\sim
10
\mu\Omega

\rho(290\,\mathrm{K})/\rho(T_{c})\approx 28

In contrast, the Sn-intercalated
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
exhibits dramatically enhanced resistivity, reaching
\sim

\Omega
cm at 290 K, approximately 34 times larger than that of the pristine sample. Upon cooling, the resistivity shows insulating behavior below
\sim
30 K, after showing a broad hump centered around 150 K. A small peak is observed near 7 K, indicated by the blue arrow, which we attribute to a superconducting transition arising from a minor fraction of remaining 2H-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

The observed insulating transport cannot be attributed to disorder-induced localization. In particular, Anderson localization arising from impurity scattering would require sufficiently strong disorder within the conducting TMD planes
Di Salvo et al. 1976
. However, the Sn intercalants reside in the vdW gaps between layers and therefore couple only weakly to in-plane electronic motion. Consistent with this picture, in Sn-intercalated 2H-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
a comparable intercalation level (4%) increases residual resistivity but preserves metallic transport without any indication of localization
Naik et al. 2019
. The stark contrast with the insulating behavior observed here thus rules out disorder as the primary origin and instead points to other effects.

Rather, the insulating behavior observed in Sn-intercalated
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is reminiscent of the Mott insulating state in 1T-
\text{TaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. In 1T-
\text{TaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, the resistivity increases upon cooling from room temperature and exhibits a pronounced jump at the commensurate CDW transition, followed by insulating behavior at low temperatures
Wilson et al. 1975
. This behavior is attributed to electron localization associated with the formation of a star-of-David CDW superstructure with
\sqrt{13}\times\sqrt{13}
periodicity
Law and Lee 2017
. In contrast, although Sn-intercalated
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
shows a similar overall insulating behavior, no clear anomaly associated with such a transition is observed within the measured temperature range up to 300 K, suggesting that the CDW transition may occur at higher temperatures. Our observations suggests the emergence of a correlated insulating state realized in the 1T-phase of bulk Sn-intercalated
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

Despite the observed insulating behavior, DFT calculations predict a metallic band structure for Sn-intercalated 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. As shown in Fig.
4
(a), bulk pristine 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
without the
\sqrt{13}\times\sqrt{13}
CDW exhibits a metallic band structure. It has been suggested that the stacking of the star-of-David CDW plays a crucial role in determining the ground state of 1T-TMDs
Zhang and Wu 2023
. To investigate this effect, we construct several stacking configurations based on the relative alignment of the star-of-David pattern. Specifically, we consider three geometries: (i) AA stacking, where the centers A of the star-of-David units are vertically aligned; (ii) AB stacking, where an inner vertex B of one star-of-David lies above the center A of a star-of-David in the adjacent layer; and (iii) AC stacking, where an outer vertex C lies above the center A in the neighboring layer, as illustrated in Fig.
4
(a). Among these, AC stacking has the lowest total energy for bulk pristine 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. As shown in Fig.
4
(b), the reconstructed band structure with the AC stacking CDW order is either metallic or semiconducting, depending on the position of the Fermi level. This suggests that a shift in the Fermi energy induced by Sn intercalation could, in principle, render the system semiconducting.

However, Sn intercalation does not open a gap, and the system remains metallic, according to our calculations. To calculate the band structure of Sn-intercalated
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, we start with AC stacking, which has the lowest total energy. We model Sn intercalation by placing one Sn atom above the center A of a star-of-David unit in one
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layer, while the second Sn atom is positioned above either the center A, an inner vertex B, or an outer vertex C of a star-of-David unit in the adjacent layer. Among these three intercalation configurations, the lowest-energy structure corresponds to the case in which the second Sn atom is located above the inner vertex B of the star of David. Notably, as shown in FIG.
4
(c), the band structure with the Sn-intercalated 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with the CDW is metallic. This discrepancy between experiments and calculations indicates that electronic correlations may play a crucial role in stabilizing the observed insulating state. Further theoretical work is required to clarify the nature of these correlation effects.

IV
Summary

We have synthesized the Sn-intercalated
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. The TEM measurements clearly reveal the presence of the 1T phase of
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the bulk form. The resistivity measurements show insulating behavior at low temperatures, similar to 1T-
\text{TaSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. In spite of the insulating behavior, DFT calculations predict that the 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a metal, suggesting that electron correlations are key ingredients to the observed insulating behavior in Sn-intercalated
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Raman spectroscopy suggests the additional peaks to the 1T-phase due to the vibrational modes of intercalants and/or a possible CDW phase. Our results suggest that electrochemical Sn intercalation stabilizes the unrealized bulk 1T-
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, providing a new platform for correlated physics.
