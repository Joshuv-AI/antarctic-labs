Folate-directed morin nano-gold decorated with chitosan: a multifunctional arsenal against triple-negative breast cancer and AMR superbugs
Authors: Suvadeep Mal, Thakur Gurjeet Singh, Ajit Kumar Bishoyi, Kakarla Pakeeraiah, Preetesh Kumar Panda, Monalisa Mahapatra, Suvadra Das, Roshan Kumar, Sudhir Kumar Paidesetty, Partha Roy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
TNBC defying conventional therapies and the rapid increase in antimicrobial resistance disarming powerful antibiotics demand urgent clinical intervention. The current research study explored a strategy to target TNBC and drug-resistant pathogens using flavonoid (morin)-induced gold nanoparticles (MorAunps) tethered with folic acid and chitosan (CH/FA-MorAunps) for enabling targeting approach and induced cellular uptake. The synthesized nanosystems with a size of 21.87 nm exhibited a spherical morphology with utmost stability. The synthesized nanoformulations were assessed for in vitro antioxidant, antimicrobial and anticancer activity. The CH/FA-MorAunps were found to be excellent antioxidants with scavenging capacity ranging from 83% to 96%. The tagging of chitosan also dramatically enhanced the antimicrobial potency (MIC ranging from 8 to 32 µg mL−1), outperforming standard's MICs. The nanosystems exhibited significant cytotoxicity against both BC and TNBC cells (IC50 values of 4.29 and 8.32 µg mL−1, respectively). The enhanced anticancer potential of the nanosystems was most likely driven by rapid internalization, G2/M phase cell cycle arrest, and ROS-mediated apoptotic and necrotic deaths, as evidenced in MDA-MB-231 cells. In addition, the nanoparticles did not confer significant toxicity against non-malignant cells, reinforcing their biosafety and translational viability. Further docking analysis of the flavonoid with potential cancer, microbial and oxidative stress-related targets revealed significant docking scores in the range of 7–9 kcal mol−1 and specific interactions with active pockets, providing a strong mechanistic view of the nanosystems as a multifaceted bioactive nanoplatform. This pioneering study establishes morin-conjugated gold nanoparticles as a powerful targeted nanotherapeutic platform against triple-negative breast cancer and AMR pathogens.

Introduction

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a clinically diverse and aggressive breast cancer (BC) variant, further crippled by worse prognosis, recurrent relapses, truncated survival rate and absence of site-specific treatment strategies to manage off-target toxicities of chemotherapeutics. The TNBC survival rate varies from 1 to 5 years with just over 10% chances of life subsistence in metastasized cases.1 As a targeted check point, the WHO Global Breast Cancer Initiative intends to reduce breast cancer incidences by 2.5% per year, saving almost 2.5 million lives during a span of two decades.2 Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) ranks in the top-tier global health challenges of this century, impacting 4.71 million deaths in 2021 coupled with an alarming statistic of 3 million paediatric mortality cases in 2022 (ref. 3). Although mortality among children under the age of five has declined by 50% over three decades, rates among the elderly have increased by over 80% (ref. 4). Without urgent intervention, the deaths caused by AMR could rise up to 10 million annually by the year of 2050 with major pathogenic stakeholders like Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa.5 Interestingly, drug resistance extends beyond microbes, and therapeutic resistance is implicated in 80–90% of cancer deaths.6 Shared resistance mechanisms, namely, efflux pump overexpression, gene mutations, biofilm formation, and tumour microenvironment shielding muddle both antimicrobial and cancer treatment portfolios.7,8 Over 60% of cancer patients receive antibiotics during therapy, cementing infections as the second leading mortality stimulus and warranting an immediate integrated game plan.9,10

Gold nanoparticles (Aunps) have emerged as multidimensional platforms for antimicrobial and anticancer applications due to their high cargo capacity, photothermal and SERS activity, minimal toxicity, facile synthesis and potential for targeted delivery.11,12 Moreover, ease of conjugation of nano-gold with bioactives as well as diverse ligands for drug delivery with an added edge of exploring light and heat as external triggers for drug release makes it an obvious choice for clinical applications. Aunps conjugated with polyherbal formulations and doxorubicin have demonstrated efficacy in degrading ferritin and eliminating drug-resistant BC stem cells via autophagy-necroptosis axis activation.13 Ciprofloxacin-loaded Aunps significantly reduced E. faecalis colony counts in murine kidney infections with minimal toxicity.14 Surface-modified Aunps with cationic alkanes disrupt bacterial membranes, sustaining activity across 20 bacterial generations without inducing resistance.15 Similarly, size-tuned DAPT-capped Aunps restored efficacy against MDR E. coli by leveraging enhanced surface area.16

Morin is a yellow pentahydroxyflavone associated with diverse families like Rosaceae, Fagaceae, and Moraceae. It has a myriad of scientific reports confirming its anti-inflammatory, anti-Alzheimer's, anticancer and anti-AMR properties. In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) models, morin inhibited MMP-9, N-cadherin, and Akt signaling while upregulating p21 and ERK phosphorylation.17,18 Morin reversed paclitaxel resistance via ABCB1 suppression and induced G2/M arrest and apoptosis when combined with AuNPs.19 Recent studies involving morin-loaded Au/Ag-graphene oxide nanohybrids have revealed potent TNBC suppression via p53 and Bax activation, reduced glucose uptake, and inhibition of lactate dehydrogenase.20 In context of our present work, Mor also ruptured biofilms, disoriented motility and disrupted quorum sensing in S. aureus, thus blocking resistance among pathogens.21 It is reported to enhance S. aureus susceptibility to oxacillin via PBP-2a downregulation,22 and synergize with imipenem or methicillin to reduce MICs in resistant strains.23 The study by Zayed et al. (2024) underscores the synergistic power of nanotechnology and phytochemistry, showing remarkable pathogen-specific antimicrobial action, especially against S. aureus and K. pneumoniae by morin-conjugated silver nanoparticles fabricated via an eco-friendly route.24

Folate receptor alpha (FR-α) is overexpressed in 71% of TNBC incidences compared to normal tissues, making it an ideal therapeutic target.25 Targeting FR-α not only improves delivery to cancer cells but also modulates tumor-associated macrophages, reducing immunosuppression and drug resistance. This strategic ploy is a boon in AMR therapy by targeting bacterial folate pathways.26 FA-tagged morin-nanoceria induced ROS-mediated apoptosis and inhibited migration in BC.27 Vanamala et al. (2021) developed a folate-decorated lipid nanocarrier, which not only enhanced vancomycin accumulation in MRSA-infected thigh tissues but also disrupted the biofilm produced by the superbug, providing strong evidence for targeting folate to mitigate AMR.28 FR-targeted drugs like vitafolitide and farletuzumab have reached Phase-III trials cementing their emerging clinical relevance.29

Chitosan, a biocompatible polymer from chitin, adds further value in drug delivery. It facilitates sustained release, mucoadhesion, and cellular uptake via electrostatic interaction with negatively charged membranes, and enhances therapeutic bioavailability. FA-conjugated chitosan-Aunps have been shown to promote mitochondrial depolarization, caspase activation, and pH-responsive release in MCF-7 cells.30 Chitosan-tailored Aunps also exhibited a broad antimicrobial spectrum, particularly against drug-resistant S. aureus, E. coli, and P. aeruginosa, along with biofilm disruption.31,32 Its additional roles in apoptosis induction, immune stimulation, and angiogenesis inhibition support its active participation in multifunctional nanosystem design.33

Considering these facts in backdrop, we propose a novel integrative nanoplatform interweaving morin, folic acid, and chitosan-functionalized gold nanoparticles with dual action against triple-negative breast cancer and antimicrobial-resistant pathogens. To our knowledge, this is the first report exploring the combinatorial efficacy of these agents within a single nanosystem. Engineered nanosystem design with multimodal action is expected to be a next-generation chemotherapeutics coupled with resistance-reversing potential.

Materials and methods

Materials

Chloroauric acid trihydrate (HAuCl4, 3H2O), used as a precursor for nanogold, was purchased from Alfa Aesar (Haverhill, United States). From the same company, 1-ethyl-3-[3-dimethylaminopropyl]carbodiimide hydrochloride (EDC) was also procured. 2′,3,4′,5,7-Pentahydroxyflavone (morin, Mor), chitosan (CH; 50 to 190 kDa molecular weight), folic acid (FA) and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) were obtained from CDH Fine chemicals (New Delhi, India). N-Hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) was purchased from TCI (Tokyo, Japan). FTIR-grade potassium bromide (KBr) was purchased from Merck (Burlington, United States). 2,2′-Azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS, 98.5%) and 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH, ≥95%) were procured from SRL Chemicals (Mumbai, Maharashtra, India).

For biological evaluation, human triple-negative breast cancer cell line (MDA-MB-231), human breast cancer cell line (MCF-7), and mouse fibroblast cell line (NIH/3T3) were purchased from the National Centre for Cell Sciences (Pune, India). Dulbecco's modified eagle medium (DMEM), fetal bovine serum (FBS), and antibiotic–antimycotic mix were obtained from Himedia (India). MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyl tetrazolium bromide), fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC), Annexin V-FITC early apoptosis detection kit, 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI), propidium iodide (PI) and Triton X-100 were procured from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Paraformaldehyde, phosphate buffer saline (PBS) and RNaseA were acquired from MERCK (USA). ProLong™ antifade reagent and 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) were procured from Thermo Fisher Scientific (USA).

All the solvents used in the experiment were of HPLC grade and were obtained from Spectrochem (Mumbai, India). Glassware (Borosil®, Mumbai, India) used for the synthetic and analytical purposes was cleaned using aqua regia prior to the experimentation. The chemicals and reagents used were of analytical grade. Standard tissue culture plates and cell strainers from HIMEDIA were used in determining biological activities.

Methods

Synthesis of morin-reduced gold nanoparticles

Morin-engineered gold nanoparticles (MorAunps) were synthesized following a facile, ultrasonic method previously developed by our team with slight modifications.34 Briefly, a gold precursor (HAuCl4, 3H2O) was dissolved in HPLC-grade water. Then 1 mL methanolic solution of Mor (conc. 1 mM) was rapidly admixed with previously prepared 3 mL 1 mM chloroauric acid solution under sonication. A bath sonicator (LMUC 3, Labman, Chennai, India) was set at a power of 100 W with a frequency of 40 kHz preconditioned at 30 °C. The progression of the reaction was tracked visually until the characteristic wine color nano-gold appeared. The synthesis route was optimized via varying the molar ratio and reaction time-length, where Mor aided in dual mode, first ensuring the reduction of the gold precursor to nano-gold followed by speedy stabilization of the Aunps within 3 min.

After the synthesis, MorAunps were purified by ultracentrifugation at 11 800 rpm (∼24 150 g) for 25 min at 4 °C ± 2 °C using Himac CS 120 GHXL, Hitachi-Koki, Tokyo, Japan. The obtained pallets were re-dispersed in deionized water and were centrifuged two more times to remove potential impurities like unreduced Au3+ and un-oxidized Mor if present. The resultant nanogold was re-dispersed in an aqueous environment and refrigerated until further experimentation.

Surface engineering of MorAunps with folic acid and chitosan

Following MorAunp synthesis, folic acid (FA) and chitosan (CH) were tagged via carbodiimide coupling and electrostatic adsorption or covalent grafting.11,35 The functionalization of FA to the MorAunp was achieved following two steps, namely, carboxylation and coupling. Sequential addition of succinic anhydride (0.5 g, 5.0 mM) and triethylamine (TEA, 0.5 g, 5.0 mM) onto MorAunps under constant stirring, followed by 24 h of incubation first achieve carboxylation. After COOH groups were introduced, the activation of COOH groups occurred for the smooth tagging of FA. An aliquot of 250 µL EDC (in PBS pH 7.4; conc. 1.5 mg mL−1) was added dropwise to the carboxylated nanoparticle and stirred for 5 min, followed by the drop-wise addition of 250 µL NHS (in PBS pH 7.4; conc. 1.5 mg mL−1), and the mixture was stirred continuously for further 4 h. After the specified period, 100 µL of FA solution (in PBS 7.4; conc. of 1 mg mL−1) was added dropwise to the activated nanoparticle under continuous stirring for the next 2 h under dark conditions and the whole mixture was incubated overnight at 4 °C ± 2 °C to stabilize the covalent attachment (amide bond formation). Finally, the FA-conjugated MorAunps (FA-MorAunps) were purified by centrifugation, re-dispersed in HPLC-water, and stored at 4 °C ± 2 °C until further use.

The developed FA-MorAunp was further coated with CH in 1% (v/v) acetic acid under constant stirring for 1 h at r.t. The resulting chitosan-coated nanoparticles (CH/FA-MorAunps) were stabilized by acidifying with 0.1 M HCl. The resultant nanoconjugates were then processed to remove unbound CH by ultracentrifugation and stored at 4 °C ± 2 °C for further use.

Fluorescein 5-isothiocyanate (FITC)-labelled nanosystems were synthesized following a previously reported protocol with slight modifications.34 Briefly, 15 µL of a 1 mM FITC solution was introduced into 5 mL of freshly prepared CH/FA-MorAunp suspension under continuous stirring in the dark for 45 min to facilitate fluorescence labeling. The reaction mixture was subsequently incubated overnight at 4 °C ± 2 °C under light-protected conditions. The resulting FITC-conjugated CH/FA-MorAunps were purified by ultracentrifugation at 11 800 r.c.f. for 30 min under cold conditions to remove unbound dye molecules. Finally, the purified fluorescence-tagged nanoparticles were stored under refrigerated and light-protected conditions until further experimental use.

Characterization of the nanoparticles

Tailored gold nanoparticles synthesized were characterized by a combination of spectroscopic, microscopic, and elemental methods. Initially, the optical properties were analysed by UV-visible spectroscopy (UV-1780, Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) over a wavelength range of 200–800 nm. The surface plasmon resonance (SPR) peak of gold nanoparticles synthesized by varying the molar ratios and reaction conditions was recorded in the range between 500 and 600 nm. The hydrodynamic diameter, polydispersity index (PDI) and zeta potential were measured using a Malvern Zetasizer Advance Pro (Malvern Instruments, UK) exploring dynamic light scattering (DLS) methods. The morphological and structural characteristics were investigated through transmission electron microscopy (TEM) using a Talos L120C G2 (Thermo Fisher) with a magnification range of 34× to 650 000×. Imaging was conducted using a LaB6 filament at 120 kV operating in the bright-field mode after the drop-casting method on carbon-coated copper grids (Ted Pella, USA) followed by air-drying at room temperature. The particle size distribution was determined by analysing ∼70 particles on three representative micrographs. The elemental composition was assessed using energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) integrated with the FESEM. Elemental mapping and spectral analysis were performed using the dedicated software Origin 6 Professional®.

Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was used to validate the interaction of flavonoids, folic acid, and chitosan on the particle surface. Spectra were obtained using a PerkinElmer Spectrum™ 10 spectrometer in the ATR mode employing the KBr pellet method. The samples were scanned 32 times at 4 cm−1 resolution referenced against air. The data were processed using the Origin-6 Professional® and Spectrum™ 10 software. The crystalline nature and phase purity were identified by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using a D8 Advance Powder X-ray diffractometer (Bruker) with a Ni-filtered CuKα radiation source (λ = 1.5418 Å) at an operating voltage of 40 kV and a current intensity of 40 mA. Data were recorded between 2θ values of 20° and 100° with a step size of 0.02° and with 0.5 s per step. Lastly, the gold molar concentration in the optimized MorAunps was measured by high-resolution inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (HR-ICP-MS; Element XR™, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) after digestion with aqua regia and diluted up to a conc. of <1 ppm with HPLC water. Quantification was also performed using the 197Au isotope with the equipment run at 27 MHz radio frequency and 2 kW power, employing a concentric nebulizer with 50–100 µL min−1 flow rate and triple argon gas mass flow controllers for plasma and nebulization regulation.

Biological evaluation

Antioxidant study

ABTS radical scavenging assay

The antioxidant activity of the prepared nanoparticles was also determined spectro-photometrically using an ABTS free radical decolorization test with slight modifications.36 Initially, for the generation of ABTS radical cations, an equal volume of ABTS (7.2 mM) and potassium persulfate (2.5 mM) were mixed and incubated under dark conditions for at least 16–18 h, and the temperature was maintained at 30 °C ± 2 °C. The radical solution was diluted with 70% ethanol to arrive at an absorbance of 0.75 ± 0.05 at 743 nm and used as blank. For the samples, different concentrations of Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps were mixed with 2 mL of ABTS radical solution and stabilized under dark conditions for 1–2 h for recording the absorbance. Trolox was used as the ABS control for the experimental study. Experiments were performed in triplicates, and the mean ABTS radical scavenging activity in percentage was evaluated using the following formula:1% Inhibition (I) = [(ABSControl − ABSSample)/ABSControl] × 100,where ABSControl is the absorbance of the control and ABSSample is the absorbance of the sample.

DPPH radical scavenging assay

The antioxidant activity of the prepared nanoparticles was assessed via DPPH assay according to the procedure followed by Jin Li and team with slight modifications.37 Different concentrations (50, 100 and 150 µg mL−1) of Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps were prepared to perform the assay at different concentrations. An equal volume of these samples and 0.1 mM ethanolic solution of DPPH were mixed together. The whole mixtures were then incubated under dark conditions with vigorous shaking at 25 °C for 30–45 min followed by the measurement of absorbance at 517 nm. A DPPH solution without the nanocomponents was used as the negative control, whereas ascorbic acid was used as the positive control. All the experiments were performed in triplicates, and the mean DPPH radical scavenging activity in percentage was determined using eqn (1) where ABSControl is the absorbance of the control and ABSSample is the absorbance of the sample.

Antimicrobial study

Microorganisms and culture conditions

The antibacterial properties of the synthesized compounds were assessed against four resistant bacterial strains: Klebsiella pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pyogenes, and Escherichia coli. Prior to testing, all strains were cultivated in suitable nutrient media and incubated at a standard temperature of 37 °C for 24 hours. The tested formulations are Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps.

Agar well diffusion assay

The antimicrobial potential of morin-based formulations was evaluated using the agar well diffusion technique by determining the ZOI against the resistant strains K. pneumoniae, S. aureus, S. pyogenes, and E. coli. Initially, the bacterial strains were cultured in Mueller-Hinton broth (MHB) and incubated overnight at 37 °C in a shaker incubator (REMI). The cultures were then standardized to 0.5 McFarland turbidity (approximately 1 × 108 CFUs per mL) and evenly spread onto Mueller-Hinton agar (MHA) plates (HIMEDIA, LOT0000457117). Subsequently, 50 µL of the test compounds, prepared at a concentration of 100 µg mL−1 in 10% DMSO, was dispensed into each sterile well. Novobiocin served as the positive control and DMSO as the negative control. The plates were incubated at 37 °C ± 2 °C for 24 h. followed assessment of inhibition zones.38

Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) determination

The minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of the nanoconjugates were determined using a microbroth dilution method. In this approach, bacterial cultures were exposed to a series of compound dilutions prepared in a 96-well flat-bottom microtiter plate (Polystyrene, Eppendorf). Following the zone of inhibition assay, the most promising antibacterial agents, namely, Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps were assessed for their MICs against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacterial strains, using Novobiocin as a reference control.39

The dilution series began by adding 100 µL of a bacterial suspension (0.5 McFarland standard) to the first well, which also contained 100 µL of the compound stock solution (1024 µg mL−1 in 10% DMSO), resulting in a starting concentration of 512 µg mL−1. Serial two-fold dilutions were carried out across wells 1 to 10 by transferring 100 µL from one well to the next, with the final dilution reaching 1 µg mL−1. Well 11 was filled with 200 µL of media (MHB) as the negative control, while well 12 received 200 µL of the bacterial inoculum as the positive control. The plates were incubated at 37 °C ± 2 °C for 18–24 h. After incubation, 5 µL of a 0.5 M solution of 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) was added to each well as a colorimetric growth indicator. The MIC was defined as the lowest concentration of the sample that prevented visible bacterial growth, confirmed by the absence of color change after 24 h.5

Anticancer evaluation

Cell culture

MDA-MB-231 (triple negative breast cancer), MCF-7 (breast cancer), and NIH/3T3 (mouse fibroblast) cell lines were cultured in Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium (DMEM; Gibco, USA) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, USA) as a supplement for nutrients, hormones and growth factors with 1% antibiotic–antimycotic solution (Gibco, USA) for preventing any possible microbial contamination. The cells were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2 changing the culture media every alternate days. A 0.05% trypsin-0.02% EDTA (Gibco, USA) solution was employed to enzymatically detach cells upon reaching confluence. Actively proliferating cells in the logarithmic growth phase were subsequently passaged for downstream experimental applications.34

Cytotoxicity study

Malignant as well as non-malignant cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well and allowed to adhere overnight under standard culture conditions. Next day, Mor (dissolved in DMSO), Mor-reduced gold nanoparticles (MorAunps), folic acid-functionalized MorAunp (FA-MorAunps) and chitosan-coated FA-functionalized morin-gold nanoparticles (CH/FA-MorAunps) were dispersed in HPLC-grade water and introduced to the respective wells in triplicate at 5-fold varying concentrations ranging from 1 to 32 µg mL−1 (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 µg mL−1). The wells containing only the culture medium served as untreated controls and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) served as the positive control.

After 48 h of drug exposure, the cells were gently washed with 100 µL of sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove residual particles and to ensure negligible interference with the MTT. Following this, the medium was discarded, and 5 mg mL−1 MTT reagent (prepared in PBS, pH 7.4) was added to each well. The plates were then incubated under dark conditions at 37 °C for 4 h to allow formazan crystal formation. Subsequently, the crystals were solubilized using 100 µL of DMSO per well, and the absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader. The cell viability was expressed as the ratio of the mean absorbance of treated wells to that of control wells and calculated using the following formula:2Cell viability (%) = AbsTest/AbsControl × 100,where AbsTest is the mean absorbance of treated cells (with samples) and AbsControl is the mean absorbance of untreated cells.34

Mode of cell death analysis by flow cytometry

MDA-MB-231 cells were collected following 24 h of treatment with Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps, and subsequently resuspended in an Annexin V binding buffer. The cells were then exposed to Annexin V-FITC and a propidium iodide (PI) solution followed by incubation for 10 minutes at 37 °C in a dark environment. Fluorescence signals were acquired using a flow cytometer, and the proportions of apoptotic and necrotic cell populations were analyzed using the BD FACS Diva software.34

Cell cycle analysis

MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well and exposed to Mor and gold nanoconjugates for 48 h. Cells were washed with ice-cold PBS (pH 7.4) after treatment, resuspended, and fixed in ethanol for 15 min at 4 °C. After fixation, cells were washed, treated with RNase (100 µg mL−1) and stained with propidium iodide (PI) (50 µg mL−1 PI in 0.1% sodium citrate and 0.05% Triton X-100) under dark conditions for 15 min. The DNA content was examined using a FACS Aria III flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA), and cell cycle distribution was assessed employing the BD FACS Diva software.34

In vitro ROS generation assay

To further check whether the nanosystems induce ROS-mediated cytotoxicity in cancer cells, the intracellular ROS levels were quantified via a DCFH-DA assay following the methodology reported by Shamprasad et al. (2022), with minor modifications.40 TNBC cells (MDA-MB-231) were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 104 cells per well containing DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin antibiotic solution in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2 at 37 °C and incubated with 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) for 1 h. After the dye was loaded, the culture medium was removed, and the cells were washed with PBS followed by treatment with Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps along with the standard drug ascorbic acid, while hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) served as the positive control. Following 6 h of incubation, the intracellular fluorescence was measured using a fluorescence spectrophotometer (RF-6000, Shimadzu, Japan) at excitation and emission wavelengths of 485 and 527 nm, respectively. The fluorescence intensity of oxidized DCF was used as an indirect measure of intracellular ROS generation. The quantitative analysis of the fluorescence pattern corresponding to oxidized DCF was determined by integrating the emission peak (485–527 nm) after baseline subtraction. The obtained values were expressed as mean fluorescence intensity (MFI). All experiments were performed in triplicate, and the results are presented as mean ± SD.

Cellular uptake study

For the cellular uptake study, TNBC (MDA-MB-231) cells were grown and seeded in a 6-well plate at a density of 5 × 104 cells per well and allowed to adhere overnight. Subsequently, the cells were treated with FITC-labeled nanosystems i.e. MorAunps, FA-MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps at a sub-IC50 concentration and incubated for up to 12 h at 37 °C. Following incubation, the cells were washed thrice with PBS, followed by fixing in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature before the study. The cellular uptake was assessed by monitoring fluorescence at time intervals of 2 and 4 h, with free FITC serving as the control and fluorescence images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope (Cilika, BT-P-FL).

In silico molecular docking studies

The molecular docking analysis was performed taking five protein targets, namely, cytochrome c peroxidase enzyme responsible for duplicating the ascorbate peroxidase binding properties (PDB: 2x08), epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) responsible for cell growth and division with MTX-531 (PDB: 8SC7), unphosphorylated maternal embryonic leucine zipper kinase (MELK) in complex with a pyrrolopyrazole inhibitor (PDB: 4BKY), crystal structures of DNA gyrase from two distinct organisms, S. aureus and E. coli, viz. PDB: 5D7R and 7P2M, respectively.41–44 The protein structures were retrieved from the RSCB Protein Data Bank (PDB). After downloading (.cif) protein files, they were made free from water molecules and other ligands or heteroatoms to achieve the whole surface area of proteins for a blind docking approach. The ligand (morin) was initially drawn using ChemDraw Ultra Tool 19.0. Further, it was 3D optimized and brought to its ground state by minimizing the energy using ACD Labs tool 2015 and Avogadro 1.1.1. To obtain a precise and accurate docking pocket, this study was performed using AutoDock Tools 4.2 developed by The Scrips Research Institute. Before procedure, both the ligand and the protein were saved as (.) pdb files using Pymol. Furthermore, protein preparation was done by adding polar hydrogen atoms and Kollman charges to facilitate a hassle-free docking protocol. Suitable spacing was set according to the targets at the time of grid box preparation. Similarly, 100 genetic algorithm runs were set for all proteins taking a population size of 150. Lamarkian genetic algorithm was employed to compute a flawless docking study. To visualize the binding pockets of residue interactions made by ligands with the protein target, Pymol and BIOVIA Discovery Studio Visualiser version 4.5 (BIOVIA DSV) were used.44

Result and discussion

Synthesis of gold nanoparticles

Aunp synthesis followed a facile, robust, reproducible, environment-compatible ultrasonic approach for controlled nano-size formation under ambient conditions with tailored properties. The synthesis route explored tetrachloroauric acid as a gold precursor with morin playing a dual role as reducing agent and stabilizer. The ultrasonic irradiation in aqueous solutions induces the phenomenon of acoustic cavitation, a process which denotes the formation, growth, and violent collapse of micrometrical bubbles.45 Upon collapse, these bubbles generate localized hot spots with extreme temperatures (reaching up to 5000 K) and pressures (∼1000 atm), with rapid heating and cooling rates (∼1010 K s−1), leading to unique transient conditions that drastically accelerate nucleation and growth kinetics favourable for nanoformation.46 The gold precursor undergoes stepwise reduction, initiated by the formation of the intermediate complex AuCl2−, which gets disproportionated under ultrasonic irradiation. The localized high energy driven by acoustic cavitation facilitates electron transfer between gold ions and morin. The electrons from the reducing agent (Mor) initiates faster reduction from Au3+ to Au0, resulting in the nucleation of gold seeds. Cavitation-generated primary radicals (˙H and ˙OH) further contribute to redox cycling via continuous regeneration of AuCl4− with the deposition of gold atoms on these nuclei, leading to the self-sustaining growth of nanoparticles.47 The completion of reaction and complete gold reduction were confirmed by the transient colour change from pale yellow to stable wine and the appearance of a steady SPR peak at 530 nm.

Classical nano-gold formation could be achieved primarily by Turkevich's method; however, modified Fren's method with varying molar gold precursor-to-reducing agent ratios propagates different size and shape orientations.11 In the present study, Fren's method involving rapid nucleation followed by diffusion-controlled growth was systematically studied using differential molar ratio of the reducing agent Mor to a fixed concentration of HAuCl4 ranging from 1 : 0.167 to 1 : 0.4. The characteristic gold colour could be observed with the molar concentration of 1 : 0.26 (HAuCl4 : Mor). The highest SPR intensity, with a λmax value of 530 nm, was observed at an optimal molar ratio of 1 : 0.34 (HAuCl4 : Mor) when the conc. of Mor increased to 1 mM. At suboptimal Mor concentrations (0.5–0.7 mM), although reduction initiated rapidly, insufficient stabilizing agent led to early-stage nanoparticle aggregation due to van der Waals forces, resulting in polydispersed nanoclusters.48 As the reaction proceeded, aggregation subsided and particle growth transitioned to a diffusion-controlled phase, producing larger particles at lower Mor ratios. Enhancing the Mor concentration promoted complete reduction of gold ions, evident from the increasing SPR peak intensity at 530 nm. Adequate Mor attachment to the nanoparticle surface prevented further aggregation through electrostatic repulsion, in agreement with the classical nucleation-growth model, and facilitated the formation of uniformly spherical Aunps. However, exceeding the optimal Mor concentration (>1 mM) led to a red-shift in the UV-vis spectrum with a reduction in peak intensity, suggesting aggregation reversal and irregular Aunp morphology under these conditions (Fig. 1).

To determine the optimal duration of MorAunps synthesis, a series of sonication times ranging from 1 to 4 min were systematically evaluated. Notably, at the shortest sonication interval of 1 min, no SPR peak appeared, suggesting the absence of reduction initiation. The gold precursor undergoes hydrolysis to form Au(iii), which, upon exposure to acoustic energy, enters a redox process. Concurrently, morin is oxidized, donating electrons that facilitate the reduction of Au(iii) to an intermediate Au(i) complex. Prolonged sonication promotes the accumulation of this complex, ultimately reducing it to Au(0). However, it is worth to mention that during the initial process, the cessation of sonication favored random aggregation over controlled nucleation, resulting in polydispersed larger nanoparticles.34 The continued irradiation helps nucleation followed by seed growth up to a certain point. Maximum SPR intensity was observed at 530 nm after 3 min of sonication, indicating optimal nanoparticle formation. Beyond this point, continued sonication caused a red shift to 537 nm and 550 nm with reduced intensity, likely due to interparticle collisions leading to fusion and loss of monodispersity49 (Fig. 1).

Interestingly, unlike classical thermal routes, the sonochemical system did not reach bulk boiling temperatures. Instead, latent heat accumulation during cavitation created microenvironments sufficient to sustain reaction progress without thermal degradation. Additionally, the ultrasound power intensity can be tuned to modulate the cavitation dynamics, influencing the particle size, crystallinity, and surface properties of the Aunps, which deeply affect the SPR, optical and even photodynamic activity of Aunps.34 The current method stands tall while comparing with the synthesis method previously reported by Kondath et al. (2014) due to its unreliability upon system pH and usage of simple solvents.50 The stability of the synthesised MorAunp was assessed by UV-vis spectroscopy over 3 to 30 days, showing a consistent λmax value at 531 nm, indicating negligible size variation and minimal agglomeration. Thus, the optimized route for the sonochemical synthesis of MorAunp was chosen with a molar ratio of 1 : 0.33 (HAuCl4 : Mor) and with a sonication time for 3 min. Mor initiates gold ion reduction by abstracting labile hydrogen atoms from its 3-OH and 4′-OH groups, leading to its oxidation into chromane-3,4-dione via a free radical pathway, which gets stabilized by resonance within the chromene ring and diene system (Fig. 2).51,52 Additionally, Mor binds to AuNP facets, preventing agglomeration by sterically stabilizing the particles, and thus, gets involved as a stabilizer. Subsequently, free hydroxyl groups on MorAunps were carboxylated with succinic anhydride, enabling FA conjugation through carbodiimide-mediated coupling, followed by chitosan tagging via static absorption (Fig. 2).

Characterization of gold nanoparticles

Particle morphology and zeta potential studies

The primary characterization after visual and UV-vis spectroscopic evaluation was carried out for determining different morphological analysis including particle size, size distribution, polydispersity index (PDI), and surface charge via dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis following multiple rounds of optimization to ensure reproducibility and precision. DLS, also known as photon correlation spectroscopy (PCS), measures fluctuations in light scattering caused by Brownian motion to determine the diffusion coefficient and subsequently calculate the hydrodynamic diameter using the Stokes–Einstein equation.53 MorAunps exhibited a mean hydrodynamic diameter of 21.93 nm with a lowered PDI of 0.309. The size distribution profiles of the nanoconjugates revealed a Gaussian pattern with a moderate PDI, suggesting a reasonably uniform particle population (Fig. 3). Upon conjugation with FA and subsequent coating with chitosan, a progressive increase in hydrodynamic diameter was observed, reaching ∼80.71 nm for CH/FA-MorAunps.

The zeta potential measurements offered further insights into colloidal stability by quantifying the net surface charge. MorAunps displayed a high negative zeta potential value of −34.11 mV reflected by a characteristic V-shaped plot, indicating strong electrostatic repulsion and excellent dispersion stability. After chitosan tagging, CH/FA-MorAunps exhibited a strong positive zeta value of +55.63 mV and an inverse V-shaped phase plot. Notably, the conjugation of cationic chitosan to the nanoparticle surface concomitantly shifted the surface charge from a net negative value to a highly positive value, corroborating earlier findings that chitosan functionalization enhances surface potential due to the abundance of cationic residues onto the nano surface.35 These surface modifications not only improve colloidal stability but also enhance the potential for receptor-specific interactions, critical for targeted therapeutic applications. This systematic evolution in the hydrodynamic diameter and zeta potential substantiated the sequential surface engineering of the nanoparticles.

TEM analysis

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) is an advanced imaging technique that employs a focused electron beam to visualize the internal architecture of a sample, offering detailed insights into its morphology, elemental composition, and crystalline structure at the nanometer scale. Representative TEM images of MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps (Fig. 4) revealed predominantly spherical particles with no discernible aggregation. MorAunps exhibited an average diameter of 15.85 nm; however, rod and triangular shapes with side lengths around 27 and 14 nm, respectively, were also observed. These anisotropic shapes are likely driven by chloride ions occasionally released during the reduction of AuCl4−, which preferentially adsorb onto specific crystallographic facets, thereby directing anisotropic growth and promoting non-spherical geometries, which was observed in the study by Rai et al. (2006) and in our previous study also.34,54 The anisotropic shape of the nanosystem was also found in the tagged nanoparticles, which can significantly influence the biological potential of the nanosystem. These anisotropic structures are known to possess distinct surface energies and enhanced interaction profiles, which may contribute to improved biological activity.55 However, given their low abundance relative to spherical particles, their influence on the overall system behaviour can be considered limited but noteworthy.56

Size distribution histograms constructed from ∼90 individual particle counts showed an average diameter of 15.85 ± 5.66 nm for MorAunps and 21.87 ± 8.29 nm for CH/FA-MorAunps (Fig. 4). Both nanoparticle populations appeared monodisperse, consistent with the observed histogram tails. These findings affirm the uniformity of particle synthesis with a moderate increase in the overall particle size after FA and CH tagging.

EDX, ICP-MS and XRD analysis of the nanoparticles

Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) was employed to elucidate the elemental composition of MorAunps. The EDX spectrum confirmed the prominent presence of gold (Au), along with carbon (C) and oxygen (O), indicating successful capping of the nanoparticles by Mor molecules through organic functional groups. A minor chlorine (Cl) signal was also detected, likely originating from residual chloride ions retained during the gold precursor reduction process (Fig. 5A). High-resolution inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (HR-ICP-MS) analysis quantified the elemental gold (Au0) content in the aqueous dispersion of MorAunps at 167 µg mL−1, confirming efficient reduction and nanoparticle formation. The crystalline nature and structural orientation of the synthesized nano-gold were further elucidated by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis, as presented in Fig. 5B. The observed Bragg reflection peaks at 37.68°, 44.54°, 64.89°, 77.69° and 81.56° correspond to the (1 1 1), (2 0 0), (2 2 0), (3 1 1) and (2 2 2) planes, respectively, confirming the formation of a face-centered cubic (fcc) lattice typical of metallic Aunps.57

Nanoparticle characterization. (A) EDX spectrum and (B) XRD pattern of MorAunps.

Notably, the (111) diffraction plane exhibited a distinctly sharp and intense peak, indicating its predominance in the crystal structure. The preferential exposure of the (111) facet is of particular significance, as this crystallographic plane is thermodynamically favorable and exhibits the lowest surface energy among all fcc gold facets. The dominance of the (111) orientation contributes to enhanced catalytic activity, increased surface reactivity, and greater structural integrity, which are critically advantageous for biomedical applications such as targeted drug delivery, photothermal therapy, and biosensing.58 The high degree of (111) facet expression thus reinforces the physicochemical robustness and functional utility of the MorAunp system.

FTIR spectroscopy

FTIR spectroscopy was employed to validate the stepwise surface functionalization of the synthesized nanoconjugates, including Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps, and CH/FA-MorAunps. In nanoparticle systems, FTIR spectroscopy aids in verifying the successful attachment of surface-binding agents or biomolecules by detecting shifts in characteristic vibrational peaks, indicating interactions such as hydrogen bonding or coordination with metal surfaces like gold. The FTIR spectra of Mor, MorAunps, CH/FA-MorAunps were recorded and overlaid (Fig. 6), with wavenumber (cm−1) on the x-axis and transmittance (a.u.) on the y-axis, to analyse potential interactions between Mor, FA, CH, and the Aunp surface.

Biological evaluations

Antioxidant study

To comprehensively evaluate the redox-modulatory capacity of morin (Mor), and its nanoformulations along with free FA and CH, the antioxidant activity was assessed using both DPPH and ABTS free radical scavenging assays at incremental concentrations (50, 100, and 150 µg mL−1). These assays, although both are widely accepted for determining the radical scavenging efficacy, differ mechanistically, i.e., DPPH relies on electron/hydrogen donation primarily in organic solvents, while ABTS is more versatile, functioning in both aqueous and lipid environments and reflecting broader radical scavenging capabilities. Trolox and ascorbic acid were used as the standard drugs in ABTS and DPPH assays due to their consistent and high antioxidant efficacy.

The ABTS assay with crude morin exhibited a concentration-dependent increase in scavenging activity (33.96% at 50 µg mL−1 to 70.70% at 150 µg mL−1). Functionalization with gold nanoparticles (MorAunps) and subsequent tagging with folic acid (FA-MorAunps) modestly enhanced antioxidant performance, with RSA values reaching 72.41% and 77.35%, respectively, at the final concentration (Fig. 7A). The most pronounced improvement was observed with chitosan-coated and folic acid-functionalized MorAunps (CH/FA-MorAunps), which recorded 83.17% RSA at the highest dose, nearly matching the antioxidant capacity of the standard (96.98%) (Table 1). However, neither free folic acid nor chitosan exhibited significant scavenging activity against the ABTS assay. Notably, the ABTS assay revealed greater sensitivity to functionalization effects, particularly at lower concentrations, highlighting the broad-spectrum antioxidant potential of these nanoconjugates in physiological and hydrophilic environments.

Previous studies with FA and CH, when taken as individual entities, suggested relatively limited antioxidant potential in comparison with established antioxidant standards. Gliszczyńska-Świgło et al. (2007) reported that FA displayed approximately 16-fold and 49-fold lower radical-scavenging potency than ascorbic acid in ABTS and FRAP assays, respectively, while its DPPH radical-scavenging efficacy was nearly 17-fold lower than that of the standard.61 Similarly, CH has been shown to possess only moderate antioxidant activity, with reported radical-scavenging efficiencies ranging from 3.7% to 16.8% with a dose increment from 1 to 2 mg mL; relative to the synthetic antioxidant BHT scoring 18–29% scavenging potency upon same concentration increment.62 Similar observations have been documented in a recent study by Wang et al. (2024), further supporting the inherent modest antioxidant characteristics of chitosan.63

The observed progressive antioxidant enhancement exhibited by CH/FA-MorAunps is predominantly synergistic rather than purely additive. Though the induced activity is primarily contributed by the flavonoid as free FA and CH failed to exhibit significant activity, the strong scavenging potential of CH/FA-MorAunps could stem from multiple molecular mechanisms, viz. the electron transfer facilitation by Aunp SPR, hydrogen-donating ability and ROS neutralization capability provided by the abundant hydroxyl and amine groups available in morin and chitosan, resonance stabilization provided by conjugated systems of morin, and FA and CH in the nanosystem resulting in increased solubility after nano-conjugation.63–66 A dramatic increase in scavenging activity could also be a result of attaching low-molecular-weight chitosan onto gold nanoparticles as suggested by Pu et al. (2019).37 These factors may collectively lead to the superior radical-scavenging efficacy of the tagged nanosystems compared to the individual components alone.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS), while playing vital roles in cellular signalling and homeostasis, are implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple diseases, including cancer, through the induction of DNA damage, lipid peroxidation, and chronic inflammation. In the tumor microenvironment, oxidative stress serves as both a promoter of cancer progression and a therapeutic target.67 Therefore, antioxidant agents that can finely modulate redox balance hold immense promise in oncology. Given that tumor cells often exhibit elevated basal ROS levels, these redox-active nanoparticles could selectively amplify oxidative stress beyond the cytotoxic threshold, thereby promoting apoptosis while concurrently safeguarding normal cells. Importantly, the dual action of radical scavengers-protecting healthy cells from oxidative insult while selectively destabilizing ROS-dependent cancer cell survival pathways-positions redox-active nanotherapeutics at the forefront of anticancer strategy development. Taken together, these findings establish CH/FA-MorAunps as potent antioxidant constructs, which could be therapeutic agents where oxidative stress plays a pivotal role in onco-progression and resistance.

Antimicrobial activity

Zone of inhibition (ZOI)

Mor alone exhibited the least antimicrobial activity, with ZOI ranging from 8 to 10 mm. The introduction of gold nanoparticles (MorAunps) moderately improved activity, as observed by larger inhibition zones (12–14 mm.). Further conjugation with folic acid (FA-MorAunps) led to significantly enhanced activity, particularly against E. coli (24 mm.) and S. pyogenes (23 mm.), indicating the role of targeting moieties in enhancing bacterial uptake. The CH/FA-MorAunp formulation demonstrated the highest antimicrobial activity across all tested strains. The ZOI reached a maximum of 45 mm against S. aureus, followed by 41 mm against S. pyogenes, indicating strong bactericidal potential (Fig. 8).

Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC)

The MIC results further supported the ZOI findings. While Mor and MorAunps maintained a MIC of 128 µg mL−1, FA-MorAunps showed improved potency with MICs ranging from 32 to 64 µg mL−1. The CH/FA-MorAunps displayed the lowest MIC values, notably 8 µg mL−1 against S. pyogenes and 16 µg mL−1 against S. aureus, suggesting significantly enhanced antimicrobial efficacy. Against K. pneumoniae and E. coli, chitosan coated FA-Mor-nanogold demonstrated a MIC value of 32 µg mL−1. When compared with Novobiocin, interestingly, CH/FA-MorAunps outperformed the standard antimicrobial agent in terms of both ZOI and MIC against S. aureus and S. pyogenes. However, the chitosan- and FA-tagged nanosystem did not surpass the zone of inhibition and MIC values that the standard exhibited against K. pneumoniae and E. coli. Overall, from the antimicrobial activity observed against MDR strains, CH/FA-MorAunps appears to be a broad-spectrum antimicrobial nanosystem (Table 3).

Anticancer activity

Cytotoxicity assay

The in vitro cytotoxicity of crude Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps and CH/FA-MorAunps was evaluated against human cancer cell lines: MDAMB-231 (TNBC) and MCF-7 (breast) using MTT assay. 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) was used as the reference standard. A dose-dependent evaluation was conducted based on the gold content in each formulation to determine their respective IC50 values. The results indicated a significant decrease in cell viability in both malignant cell lines. Among the tested compounds, CH/FA-MorAunps demonstrated the most potent anti-proliferative activity across all cancer cell lines, with an IC50 value of 4.29 µg mL−1 in MDA-MB-231 and 8.32 µg mL−1 in MCF-7 (Fig. 9A and B). Against TNBC and MCF-7 cell lines, respectively, the IC50 values for FA-tagged nano-gold were found to be 10.82 ± 0.24 and 12.17 ± 0.44 µg mL−1, while untagged MorAunp and crude Mor followed least activity with an IC50 value > 12 µg mL−1 (Table 4). In addition, the nanosystems exhibited no significant cytotoxicity against non-malignant NIH/3T3 cells, comparable to that of untreated controls up to 32 µg mL−1, suggesting biocompatibility (Fig. 9C).

Targeting TNBC is clinically challenging due to the absence of ER, PR, and HER2; however, the overexpression of folate receptor alpha (FR-α) in aggressive and chemoresistant TNBC makes it a promising biomarker and therapeutic target. FR-α-directed strategies such as MOv18-IgG1–Src inhibitor conjugates have shown effective tumor suppression in TNBC models.70 Thus, the present study deals with the assessment whether FA-MorAunp could enable folate targeting in TNBC as well as BC, where chitosan coating could be beneficial in inducing cellular uptake. The enhanced cytotoxicity of chitosan-coated folic acid-tethered MorAunps (∼3 fold to MorAunp and crude Mor) is attributable to the synergistic contribution of chitosan and folic acid, which promote active targeting via folate receptor-mediated endocytosis and facilitate efficient cellular internalization of the drug-loaded nanoconjugate. Notably, FA-MorAunps also showed significant cytotoxic effects against BC and TNBC, supporting the targeting capability imparted by folic acid functionalization, which was in line with the research findings suggested by Akinyelu et al. (2019).60 In contrast, the unmodified morin exhibited the least cytotoxic potential (IC50 > 13 µg mL−1), highlighting the limitations of native flavonoids due to poor solubility and cellular uptake, which were in line with the findings by Kondath et al. (2014).50 Nanoparticle integration (MorAunp) slightly improved cytotoxicity, but functionalization with bioactive ligands markedly improved the efficacy. The cytotoxic performance of CH/FA-MorAunps approaches that of the reference drug 5-FU, especially against triple-negative breast cancer cells (MDA-MB-231), emphasizing its therapeutic promise, which could be attributed to the improved bioavailability and anticancer activity possessed by low-molecular-weight chitosan.71,72 In contrast, NIH/3T3 murine fibroblast cells exhibited no significant reduction in viability following treatment with morin-reduced nanoconjugates, indicating minimal cytotoxicity toward non-malignant cells. This selective toxicity towards malignant cells underscores the therapeutic specificity of the nanoparticle design and supports their entry in clinical translational investigations; however, a detailed study accordingly with in vivo models would further strengthen the translational potential.

Mode of cell death analysis

The pro-apoptotic effects of morin-based gold nanoconjugates were evaluated in MDA-MB-231 triple-negative breast cancer cells by Annexin V-FITC/PI flow cytometry after 24 h of treatment. The concentrations used for flow cytometry were selected based on sub-IC50 values observed from the 48 h of viability treatment (MTT assay) to ensure sufficient viable cell populations for mechanistic evaluation. The formulations tested included free Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps, and CH/FA-MorAunps, with untreated cells serving as the control. The control cells exhibited high viability (72.9%) with minimal early (15.0%) or late (0.1%) apoptosis and low necrosis (4.2%). Free Mor induced only a marginal increase in early apoptosis (16.2%) with negligible late apoptosis and necrosis, indicating limited intrinsic pro-apoptotic potential. Morin-Aunps moderately increased necrotic (11.0%) and apoptotic (early: 15.6%; late: 0.4%) populations.

Folic acid-functionalized MorAunps elicited a significant apoptotic shift, with early apoptosis rising to 34.0%, late apoptosis to 4.8%, and necrosis to 12.8%, reducing viable cells to 48.3%. This indicates efficient receptor-mediated uptake via folate receptor targeting. The chitosan/folic acid-functionalized MorAunps demonstrated the most potent cytotoxicity, with 39.4% early apoptosis, 4.8% late apoptosis, and 13.1% necrosis, reducing viability to 42.5% (Fig. 10). The enhanced activity of CH/FA-MorAunps can be attributed to the synergistic contributions of folic acid for active targeting and chitosan for improved stability, mucoadhesion, and endosomal escape, collectively driving apoptosis induction in TNBC cells.

These results clearly demonstrate that surface functionalization of MorAunps with chitosan and folic acid significantly augments their anticancer efficacy, primarily via the activation of apoptotic pathways and targeted cytotoxicity, as reported by Akinyelu et al. (2022).30 The sharp rise in apoptotic indices with FA-MorAunp and CH/FA-MorAunp nanoformulations underscores their therapeutic potential and supports further investigation into their in vivo anticancer performance and mechanistic underpinnings.

Cell cycle analysis

To further investigate the anti-proliferative mechanism of morin-based gold nanoconjugates, cell cycle analysis was conducted on MDA-MB-231 cells using flow cytometry after 24 hours of treatment. The tested formulations included free Mor, MorAunps, FA-MorAunps, and CH/FA-MorAunps with untreated cells serving as control. Control cells exhibited normal phase distribution with 60.3% in G0/G1, 23.4% in S, and 15.9% in G2/M. Treatment with free morin induced a mild increase in the G2/M phase (18.3%), while morin-Aunps caused more pronounced arrest at G2/M (24.6%), indicating enhanced interference with cell cycle progression upon nanoparticle delivery (Fig. 11).

The lowest ROS scavenging activity was exhibited by CH/FA-MorAunps, which suggest that these nanosystems could enhance ROS-driven cell death in TNBC cells. Statistical quantification of DCF fluorescence demonstrated a highly significant enhancement of intracellular ROS generation in TNBC cells following CH/FA-MorAunps exposure exhibiting the highest mean fluorescence intensity, closely approximating the response elicited by H2O2 treatment (Fig. 12B). The marked enhancement in ROS production highlights the superior pro-oxidant property of multifunctional nanosystem and provides compelling evidence of the involvement of ROS-mediated oxidative stress in potent anticancer efficacy exhibited by CH-FA/MorAunps against TNBC cells.

Although morin and the associated nanoparticles exhibited significant radical scavenging behavior in the DPPH and ABTS assays, the observed intracellular ROS elevation in TNBC cells is not contradictory. Rather it suggests environment-dependent redox switching of flavonoid-functionalized metal nanoparticles. Chemical antioxidant assays reflect the direct electron donating capacity of the compounds in a cell-free environment directed by the radical neutralizing capability of morin's phenolic OH groups. In contrast, the intracellular environment is considered to be more complex. The redox behavior of the nanoparticles primarily depends on cellular uptake, subcellular localization, interaction with cell organelles or macromolecules, mitochondrial dysfunction, depletion of glutathione reserves and interactions with endogenous redox pathways.74,75 The ROS insults inside cancer cells often compromise due to the elevated basal ROS levels and altered redox homeostasis in a tumor microenvironment. This allow further ROS generation beyond the threshold limit and trigger mitochondrial membrane depolarization, cytochrome c release, caspase activation, and apoptotic cell death.76 The switching of the redox potential of flavonoids is well documented in previous studies.77 A similar trend in the breast cancer environment has been recently shown by Iqbal et al. (2025).78 The folate and chitosan decoration onto morin-gold nanoparticles further enables active targeting and receptor-mediated smooth entry inside the malignant cells, followed by redox insults and ROS generation.

The DCFH-DA assay provides a reliable measure of total intracellular ROS that were generated; however, it lacks specificity toward individual ROS. The fluorescence signal primarily reflects oxidation by ROS such as H2O2 and OH radicals, while superoxide and singlet oxygen contribute indirectly through the formation of secondary reactive species.79 However, despite the limitations, overall, the findings from ROS detection assay demonstrate that CH/FA-MorAunps substantially enhanced ROS production in breast cancer cells relative to free morin and other nanosystems, highlighting the beneficial impact of chitosan and folic acid functionalization in cellular interaction and ROS-mediated cellular damage.

Cellular uptake study in TNBC cells

Further assessment of the induced activity of the conjugated nanosystems was done by a cellular uptake study using a fluorescence microscope. The intracellular uptake of FITC-loaded nanosystems on TNBC cells was observed during the 2nd and 4th hours of incubation, as demonstrated in Fig. 13. As depicted in Fig. 13, a time-dependent increment in fluorescence was observed, as the nanoparticles enter inside the TNBC cells. In case of unmodified MorAunps, the cellular uptake was found to be slowest and reduced in comparison to FA-MorAunps after 2 and 4 h of treatment, suggesting active targeting via FA tagging. On the other hand, within 2 h of the treatment, cytoplasmic accumulation was well observed in the case of CH/FA-MorAunps, suggesting an efficient and rapid internalization, most likely due to the synergistic effect of active targeting by FA and smooth internalization exhibited by CH. The active targeting and rapid internalization of CH/FA-MorAunps might be linked to the improved cytotoxic behaviour of the nanosystem, making it clinically dominant in breast cancer variants.

In silico docking analysis

Docking study with BC targets

Molecular docking and target protein analysis provide mechanistic insights into the observed biological activity by revealing the favorable interactions between substrate and the target protein. Herein, morin – a flavonoid targeted for its proven efficiency towards cancer cells – is, however, more prone to lung and prostate cancer. The synthesized nanoparticles show their efficacy towards the notorious metastasis of breast, i.e., TNBC, while diving deeper into the cancer cell's enzymatic role. Hence, the target 4BKY (maternal leucine zipper kinase; MLZK) has been chosen for the computational studies.43 Morin has interacted well into the active site of 4BKY with a binding energy of −8.5 kcal mol−1, making H-bonds with N227, K145, Y73, and R65, which support the in vitro results of CH/FA-MorAunps and FA-MorAunps inhibiting the proliferation of the TNBC cancer cells (Fig. 14A and B). The hydroxyl groups expected to interact with the polymers have been active by preparing bonds with the amino acid residues of the active site, which could be a better indication of their affinity towards the cancer cell lines. As the flavonoids have already been established for their anticancer potential and EGFR is a receptor tyrosine kinase of ErbB family, the target 8SC7 (EGFR structure in complex with an inhibitor MTX-531) was chosen. Morin suggested an attractive docking score of −8.1 kcal mol−1 and displayed five conventional H-bond interactions with E762, T854, C797, A743, and K745 (Fig. 14C and D). The other non-bonding interactions were with L844, V726, and T790, providing a strong background for the BC studies. Interestingly, three of the major interacting amino acid residues, viz. C797, K745 and T790, have also displayed interaction with the co-crystallized ligand, i.e., methotrexate, proving its affinity towards the active site. These interactions block the ATP-binding site of EGFR, thereby inhibiting the kinase activity, which further blocks the cancer-related downstream signalling pathways.80 All these findings are supportive of the significant in vitro anticancer potency observed due to the nanoparticle design.

Docking study with antioxidant defenders

Further, the morin and their nanoparticles were evaluated for their antioxidant activities, which were confirmed by in silico methods along with in vitro studies. Herein, the selected target is cytochrome c peroxidase (PDB ID 2X08), the enzyme involved in the defense mechanism of the antioxidant pathway.81 The crystallography study of the enzyme claimed for its duplication of ascorbate peroxidase in the substrate binding, when Y36 was replaced by N184 as a one-point change. Mor was found to interact strongly in the active site of 2X08 with a docking score of −9.2 kcal mol−1 by making a H-bond with V47 and other pi–pi interactions with W51 and R48, which could also support the proficiency of Mor and the nanoparticle design of the enzyme involved in the catalysis of hydrogen peroxide to water for sequestering the reactive oxygen species82 (Fig. 15).

Docking study with AMR-associated targets

The CH/FA-MorAunp and FA-MorAunp could potentially be favourable inhibitors of Staphylococcus aureus DNA gyrase due to their high affinity towards the active site of the target 5D7R through morin-target interaction. The binding energy was demonstrated as −7.8 kcal mol−1 by specific interactions with the key amino acid residues, viz., R84, G85, D81, S55 and I51 of the target (Fig. 16A and B). All the interactions were made via conventional H-bonds, giving a dense proof for the antibacterial activity. The above-mentioned target has been reported by Durgadasheemi and Klageri (2023) for its potential affinity towards amoxicillin with a binding score of −7.11 kcal mol−1 and later has been cited by Roney et al. (2023).83,84 Therefore, morin could be a potential substrate for the enzyme, as all the hydroxyl groups have been occupied with hydrogen generation. E. coli DNA gyrase (PDB ID: 7P2M) was chosen as another target, as it is frequently isolated in the case of food-borne diseases.44 Herein, Mor has shown a strong binding efficiency towards the protein, proving it to be a potential inhibitor of the E. coli DNA gyrase active sites. The amino acid residues at G77 and N46 were shown to have conventional H-bond interactions with the active sites, and many other non-bond interactions could be validated through the provided ligplot (Fig. 16C and D), viz., Q50, D73, I78, and P79 through either pi–anionic bonds or pi–pi stacking bonds with the hydroxyl groups of morin, through which it will attach to the nanoconjugates as well as the macromolecule. This finding of molecular docking studies gives the mechanistic perspective of gyrase inhibition, leading to biofilm destruction, linking the enhanced anti AMR activity of the nanoparticulate design.

Conclusion

The present study explored the multifaceted bio-potency of a novel chitosan-coated folic acid-functionalized morin nano-gold formulation as a dual-action platform against TNBC and AMR. The study integrated three distinct biomolecular domains, viz. redox active morin, tumor targeting biomolecule folic acid, and the bioadhesive, anti-AMR scaffold chitosan, as an expected synergism strategy. The eco-friendly and scalable approach was explored for the nanoparticulate design, which demonstrated a robust physicochemical profile, including remarkable colloidal stability with precise control of particle dimensions (around 21 nm.). The nanoplatform exhibited a broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity with surpassing ZOIs and MICs shown by the standard antibiotic Novobiocin, conferring a MIC with a low score of 8 µg mL−1. The cytotoxic profile of the nanosystem suggested selective TNBC and BC targeting with significantly low IC50 (ranging from 4–8 µg mL−1), without affecting healthy fibroblast cells, confirming its biocompatibility. Mechanistically, the nanoparticles induced orchestrated cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase and triggered a shift toward apoptotic and necrotic cell death, underscoring their potential to bypass classical drug-resistance mechanisms. The presence of chitosan and folic acid in the nanosystems triggered ROS generation in a tumor microenvironment and enabled rapid internalization inside cancer cells, which might be linked to the improved anticancer potency of the dual-tagged nanosystems. Additionally, similar trends in the ligand–receptor interaction with multiple microbial and oncogenic targets were found with a strong docking range of −7 to −9 kcal mol−1, which affirmed the multifaceted efficacy of the flavonoid core. The convergence of antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anticancer functionalities in a single, biocompatible nanoparticulate design offered a compelling solution to the interlinked challenges of tumor drug resistance and bacterial superinfections. By addressing oxidative stress, immune evasion, and efflux-driven resistance concurrently, CH/FA-MorAunps redefined the paradigm of integrated nanotherapeutics in the treatment of TNBC and AMR-related issues. Though these findings warrant further in vivo studies to assess the biodistribution, systemic toxicity, and therapeutic efficiency in relevant tumor models, chitosan/folic acid-based morin nanogold displays translational potential to foster its further clinical investigation.
