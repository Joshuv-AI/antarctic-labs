ABSTRACT
Sandia National Laboratories (SNL) first became interested in creating biofuels as part of its mission
to address vital national challenges in clean energy, the environment, and national security. By
creating energy security from biofuels, this will reduce reliance on fossil fuels. Previously, SNL
successfully converted hydrolyzed biomass into C4 and C5 alcohols, which are longer-chain
fermentation products. As fermentation progresses, alcohols accumulate to concentrations that
become toxic to the cells themselves. This accumulation inhibits cell growth and limits the cells’
ability to continue producing alcohol. To prevent inhibition, extractant overlays have been found to
successfully allow alcohols to be redirected elsewhere while also allowing cell growth and alcohol
production to continue. For biological purposes, scientists are looking for overlays that are non-
toxic while keeping costs low. By researching past literature, overlays were selected, and the project
found promising evidence that selected overlays could be non-toxic while allowing continued cell
growth and alcohol production.

Introduction
How do we make flying more sustainable without giving up on airplanes? Aviation is one of the
most difficult industries to decarbonize because aircraft require fuel with extremely high energy
densities. While electric and hydrogen power aircrafts are being researched, they aren’t yet
practical for most commercial long-distance flights.

This has created growing interest in sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) as a way to reduce aviation’s
environmental impact while continuing to use existing aircraft. SAF can be produced from
renewable resources such as waste oils, biomass, and other carbon sources. Sustainable aviation
fuel could contribute up to 65% of the emission reductions needed for aviation to reach net zero
CO2 emissions by 2050. Depending on the production pathway, SAF can reduce life-cycle
carbon emissions by around 80% compared with conventional aviation fuel, according to the
International Air Transport Association.

One of the biggest limitations is whether enough feedstock can be produced to meet the demand
of the aviation industry. Many current SAF pathways rely on waste oils and other lipid-based
feedstocks, but the global supply of these materials is limited. Other pathways, such as producing
SAF from ethanol, face additional technical challenges because ethanol contains only two carbon
atoms and must undergo significant chemical upgrading to produce the longer hydrocarbons
required for jet fuel. These processes can reduce efficiency and increase production costs.

Strain Selection
 Strain selection is an important factor in determining the efficiency and overall success of
a biological process. Scientists at SNL previously created BFL2 and AY3 strains. These strains
targeted production of five major alcohols: isobutanol, ethanol, 2-methyl-1-butanol, 3-methyl-1-
butanol, and phenylethanol. These alcohols were targeted because the C4 and C5 fusel alcohols
have energy densities closer to that of gasoline.
Extractant Overlay Selection

 5

 When selecting an extractant overlay, scientists have to keep a couple of factors in mind.
Since this extractive overlay will be in contact with live cells, the overlay has to be non-toxic,
allowing the cells to continue growing without inhibiting production of alcohol and cell growth.
The extractant has to have an affinity for the compounds that are being extracted. When choosing
an overlay, the majority of the fusel alcohols should eventually move into the overlay, though a
small concentration of fusel alcohols remaining in the aqueous layer should not inhibit cell
growth. The two phases remain largely separated because the overlay and fermentation broth
have different physical and chemical properties. Once the extraction is complete, the extract
containing the fusel alcohols can be separated from the fermentation broth and analyzed for
further processing.

Experiment Types
 Two types of experiments were set up. In vitro partition experiments (without cells) were
set up to test the effectiveness of overlays and to create standards. Tests were also done with
mixtures of two alcohols to determine whether the presence of one alcohol affected another
alcohol’s partitioning into the overlay. The second type of experiment consisted of cell growth
and overlay experiments. Three tests were set up, 15% and 20% dodecane overlays, 20% ethyl
hexanol and 20% durasyn overlay (experiment failed because of long lag phase leading to a
second Durasyn overlay experiment), and 20% durasyn and 20% FAMEs (Fatty Acid Methyl
Ester) Overlay. These experiments were set up in triplicates.

In Vitro Setup
 When creating standards, pure alcohol (isobutanol, ethanol, 2-methyl-1-butanol, 3-
methyl-1-butanol, and phenylethanol) was combined in pure ethyl acetate in increments of
0.2(0.2=40 μL alcohol + 160μL ethyl acetate, 0.4=80μL alcohol +120μL ethyl acetate, etc.).
These standards were put into gas chromatography (GC) inserts which were in GC vials. These
standards were taken to Emery Station East (ESE) and run on an Agilent 8890 GC-FID with a
DB-WAX column. Standards were created to create a known value for a specific amount of our
alcohol to overlay ratio. If the GC read that the overlay extracted 0.5 g/L of the alcohol, scientists
would know that 1 g/L was actually produced.

 6

 When creating single alcohol partitions, 1g/L of an alcohol was added into 15 mL M9-
yeast media(M9-YE). 1 mL of an alcohol was then added to 1 mL of pure ethyl acetate, mixed,
and allowed to sit for five minutes before 40 μL of the organic layer was removed and put into a
GC insert. This insert also had 160μL of pure ethyl acetate in it.
 For mixed alcohol partitions, there were ten possible combinations of alcohol pairs
(ethanol+isobutanol, ethanol+2-methyl-1-butanol, etc.). The purpose of this experiment was to
see if alcohols would inhibit each others movement to the organic phase. 0.5 g/L of each alcohol
were created by taking 5 mL of the 1g/L alcohol standard that was previously made and
combining it with 5mL of M9-YE. Two 0.5 g/L alcohols were selected, 0.5 mL of each were
added to a vial with 1mL of ethyl acetate, mixed, then allowed to sit for five minutes before 40
μL of the organic layer was removed and put into a GC insert. This insert also had 160μL of pure
ethyl acetate in it.

Cell Growth and Overlay Experiment Set Ups
 AY3 cells were streaked from a glycerol stock onto a lysogeny broth (LB) plate with
antibiotic (ampicillin, chloramphenicol, and spectinomycin) and grown for 2 days at 37 °C. Then
single colonies were inoculated from the plate into 4 mL of LB media (with the same antibiotics)
in tubes and grow at 37 °C, 250 rotations per minute (RPM) overnight. Optical density (OD) was
measured and used to calculate the amount needed to spin down to get the starting OD wanted.
Samples are spun down, resuspend in M9 media, and added to the flasks (with each seed culture
tube split between the three flask conditions). After resuspended cells were put in the flask, the
flask was given a couple swirls. Cubettes were set up with 950μL of Milli-Q water and 50μL of
flask solution. The OD was measured at 600nm. The flasks were then put into an incubator at 37
°C and 250 RPM. OD measurements were taken around every 24 hours. Overlay would be added
to flasks when OD measurements reach 0.8 undiluted. 5mL of overlay was added to each flask
(or 3mL for a 15% alcohol experiment) along with 200μL of isopropyl β-D-1-
thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) to induce alcohol production, except for the control flasks. These
flasks went back into the incubator, temperature was brought down to 30 °C and allowed to
grow. OD measurements were then taken after 24 hours.

Gas Chromatography
 To analyze the alcohols produced and extracted during our experiments, gas
chromatography with flame ionization detection (GC-FID) was used to analyze the alcohols
produced and extracted during the experiments. In general, gas chromatography separates the
compounds as an inert carrier gas sweeps them through the column. Different compounds
interact with the column differently, and therefore come out at different retention times. The FID
then detects the compounds as they leave the column. Samples were run for 15 minutes. Analysis
for certain overlays were more difficult than others to identify. Overlapping peaks made it

Analysis
 Results shown in Figure 2 from the 1 g/L single-alcohol partition experiment showed that
2-ethyl-1-hexanol extracted the greatest amount of alcohol among the overlays that could be
analyzed. The FAMEs’ peaks were too difficult to analyze reliably because of extensive peak
overlap. Until final analysis can be completed, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol was most able to extract
alcohol.

Conclusions
 Overall, this project provided a promising starting point for developing an in situ method
for fusel alcohol extraction during fermentation. There are many techniques and possibilities to
best optimize the projects’ results. Many experiments would need to confirm the data that was
discovered accurately represented the overlays, though this has many promising data points. The
main conclusions from the project are as follows:
A. Dodecane showed no apparent inhibition of cell growth under the tested conditions and
was able to extract isobutanol and methyl butanols.
B. 2-ethyl-1-hexanol showed the highest extraction among the overlays that could be
analyzed in the partition experiment
 In conclusion, the project made a significant discovery for future alcohol extraction
during fermentation. Previously, only a method for converting hydrolyzed biomass into alcohols
and extraction with a toxic overlay was feasible, and these results demonstrate the potential for
using non-toxic overlays for in situ alcohol extraction.
