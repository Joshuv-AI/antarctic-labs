No involutions in the missing Moore graph
Yawara Ishida

Abstract
The Moore graph of degree 57 57 , if one exists, is the remaining open case of the Hoffman–Singleton classification in diameter two. Although its existence remains open, substantial restrictions on the automorphism group of such a graph are known. In this paper we prove that a Moore graph of degree 57 57 has no involutory automorphisms. The proof combines the known fixed-point structure of an involution with a module-theoretic obstruction. More precisely, we consider the vertex module over the ring of 2-adic integers and the direct summand given by the image of the spectral idempotent for the eigenvalue − 8 -8 . Comparing the ordinary trace of the involution on this summand with the dimension of its Brauer quotient gives a contradiction.

Introduction

Moore graphs are the extremal graphs for the degree–diameter problem: regular graphs whose order attains the Moore bound for their degree and diameter. We refer to the surveys of Miller–Širáň
[
13
]
and Dalfó
[
6

2,\quad 3,\quad 7,\quad 57;

the first three cases are the pentagon, the Petersen graph, and the Hoffman–Singleton graph, while the case of degree

remains open
[

. For diameter greater than two, Bannai–Ito
[
1

and Damerell
[
7

independently proved that the only Moore graphs are the odd cycles, so the degree-
57
graph is the unique Moore graph whose existence is undecided. The hypothetical graph in the last case is often called the missing Moore graph. It would be a strongly regular graph with parameters
[

,
4
]

(3250,57,0,1).

Even in the absence of an existence proof, the possible automorphisms of the missing Moore graph have been studied extensively. Higman’s unpublished argument, as recorded in Brouwer–Haemers and discussed in Cameron’s text on permutation groups, shows that such a graph cannot be vertex-transitive

4
,
5

,

2
Known facts on the missing Moore graph

Throughout the paper,
\Gamma
denotes a Moore graph of diameter two and degree
57
, and
V(\Gamma)
denotes its vertex set. The graph is finite, simple, undirected, and regular.

2.1
Parameters and spectrum

The Moore bound gives

|V(\Gamma)|=1+57^{2}=3250,

and the diameter-two Moore graph condition implies that
\Gamma
is strongly regular. A strongly regular graph with parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
is a
k
-regular graph on
v
vertices in which any two adjacent vertices have exactly
\lambda
common neighbours and any two distinct non-adjacent vertices have exactly
\mu
common neighbours. For
\Gamma
these parameters are
[
8
,
4
]

(v,k,\lambda,\mu)=(3250,57,0,1);

here
\lambda=0
because
\Gamma
is triangle-free, and
\mu=1
because any two non-adjacent vertices have a unique common neighbour.
Let
A
be its adjacency matrix and let
J
be the all-one matrix. Then

A^{2}+A=56I+J.

(1)

Consequently the spectrum of
A
is

\operatorname{Spec}(A)=\{57^{1},\ 7^{1729},\ (-8)^{1520}\}.

(2)

This spectral calculation is standard for Moore graphs and strongly regular graphs with these parameters
[
8
,
4
]
.

2.2
The fixed-point theorem for involutions

The fixed-point calculation for involutions is part of Higman’s unpublished argument and was further used by Makhnev–Paduchikh in their study of
\Gamma
. For an automorphism
g
, write
\operatorname{Fix}(g)
for the subgraph induced by the vertices fixed by
g
. We need the following precise form.

Theorem 2.1

(Higman–Makhnev–Paduchikh)
.

Let
t\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
be an involution. Then

\operatorname{Fix}(t)\cong K_{1,55}.

In particular,
t
fixes exactly
56
vertices.

The assertion that an involution fixes
56
vertices is contained in the proof of Brouwer–Haemers
[
4
, Proposition 11.5.2]
. The stronger star statement is stated by Makhnev–Paduchikh, who say that the fixed points of every involutory automorphism of
\Gamma
form a star with
56
vertices
[
11
, Lemma 4]
; the same alternative is restated in their later paper
[
12
, Proposition 1]
. We use only this fixed-point statement and not any classification of possible automorphism groups.

3
A 2-adic trace formula

We record the module-theoretic terminology used below. Let
O
be a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field
k
of characteristic
p
, and let
G
be a finite group. An
OG
-module is a module over the group algebra
OG
. An
OG
-lattice is an
OG
-module which is finitely generated and free as an
O
-module. A permutation
OG
-lattice is an
OG
-lattice admitting an
O
-basis permuted by
G
.

An
OG
-lattice is called
p
-permutation if its restriction to a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
is a permutation lattice. Equivalently, it admits an
O
-basis permuted by a Sylow
p
-subgroup. We shall use the standard characterization that direct summands of permutation
OG
-lattices are
p
-permutation; see Lassueur
[
9
, Proposition–Definition 4.2]
. The Brauer quotient and its basis description for permutation modules are used in the form given by Broué and by Bouc–Thévenaz
[
3
,
2
]
.

We now specialize to

O=\mathbb{Z}_{2},\qquad k=\mathbb{F}_{2},\qquad G=C_{2}=\langle t\rangle=\{1,t\},\qquad t^{2}=1.

In this special case, a
2
-permutation
\mathbb{Z}_{2}C_{2}
-lattice is simply a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}C_{2}
-lattice admitting a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-basis permuted by
C_{2}
.
For a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}C_{2}
-lattice
N
, put

\overline{N}=\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}N\cong N/2N.

We write

N(C_{2})=\overline{N}^{C_{2}}\big/\operatorname{Tr}_{1}^{C_{2}}(\overline{N}),

(3)

where

\operatorname{Tr}_{1}^{C_{2}}(v)=v+t(v)

is the relative trace from the trivial subgroup to
C_{2}
. Thus
N(C_{2})
is the usual Brauer quotient of the
\mathbb{F}_{2}C_{2}
-module
\overline{N}
at the subgroup
C_{2}
, written so as to record the lattice
N
from which it comes.

The following observation shows that, for a
C_{2}
-permutation basis, the ordinary trace in characteristic zero and the Brauer quotient in characteristic two count the same fixed basis elements.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N
be a
2
-permutation
\mathbb{Z}_{2}C_{2}
-lattice. Then

\operatorname{tr}\left(t\mid\mathbb{Q}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}N\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}N(C_{2}),

where the integer on the right is regarded as an element of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
N
is
2
-permutation and
C_{2}
is its own Sylow
2
-subgroup,
N
has a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-basis
X
permuted by
C_{2}
. The
C_{2}
-orbits on
X
have size one or two.

A fixed basis element contributes
1
to the trace of
t
on
\mathbb{Q}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}N
. A two-element orbit contributes the trace of the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

which is
0
. Hence the left-hand side counts the fixed elements of
X
.

After reduction modulo
2
, the image
\overline{X}
of
X
is an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-basis of
\overline{N}
permuted by
C_{2}
. If
\{x,tx\}
is a two-element orbit in
X
, then

\overline{x}+t(\overline{x})=\operatorname{Tr}_{1}^{C_{2}}(\overline{x})

vanishes in
N(C_{2})
. If
x\in X
is fixed by
C_{2}
, its image survives in the quotient. The images of the fixed elements of
X
therefore form a basis of
N(C_{2})
, as in the standard basis description of the Brauer quotient for permutation modules
[
2
, Proposition 2.5(4)]
. Hence the right-hand side also counts the fixed elements of
X
.
∎

4
The spectral summand for the eigenvalue
-8

Throughout this section and the next, let
t\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
be an involution and write

C_{2}=\langle t\rangle=\{1,t\},\qquad t^{2}=1.

The action of
C_{2}
on the vertex set
V(\Gamma)
makes the vertex lattice

M=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{V(\Gamma)}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}C_{2}

4.1
The 2-adic spectral idempotent

The spectral idempotent for the eigenvalue
-8

E=\frac{(A-57I)(A-7I)}{(-8-57)(-8-7)}=\frac{(A-57I)(A-7I)}{975}.

(4)

Using (

\displaystyle(A-57I)(A-7I)

\displaystyle=A^{2}-64A+399I

Corollary 5.1
.

If a Moore graph of diameter two and degree
57
exists, then its automorphism group has odd order.

Proof.

By Cauchy’s theorem, a finite group of even order contains an element of order
2

1.1
