A twisted Hecke algebra, then and now, and a Klein bottle of tempered representations
Anne-Marie Aubert, Roger Plymen

Abstract
. Let F F be a non-archimedean local field such that 4 | q − 1 4|q-1 , with q q the order of the residue field of F F , and let ( M 0 , σ 0 ) (M^{0},\sigma^{0}) be the depth-zero cuspidal pair for the twisted Levi subgroup G 0 G^{0} of SL 8 \mathrm{SL}_{8} arising from quadratic and quartic field extensions, as defined in [ AFO ] . Then the corresponding Bernstein block is described by a twisted Hecke algebra ℋ 0 \mathcal{H}^{0} . We describe ℋ 0 \mathcal{H}^{0} explicitly as a noncommutative ℂ \mathbb{C} -algebra with generators and relations. We describe explicitly the simple modules of ℋ 0 \mathcal{H}^{0} . All the simple modules are 2 2 -dimensional. The primitive spectrum of ℋ 0 \mathcal{H}^{0} is then an explicit complex algebraic variety 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} . The maximal compact real form of 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} is homeomorphic to a Klein bottle. This Klein bottle is a model of the unitary principal series of G 0 G^{0} attached to the cuspidal pair ( M 0 , σ 0 ) (M^{0},\sigma^{0}) . In addition, we make a full comparison with the classical situation in which G = SL 8 G=\mathrm{SL}_{8} and ( M , σ ) (M,\sigma) is a cuspidal pair for G G . The supercuspidal representantion σ \sigma is constructed from the same quadratic and quartic extensions of F F . Let 𝔰 \mathfrak{s} be the point in the Bernstein spectrum 𝔅 ​ G \mathfrak{B}G determined by ( M , σ ) (M,\sigma) . We compare the two points 𝔰 \mathfrak{s} and 𝔰 0 \mathfrak{s}^{0} and show explicitly that the corresponding Bernstein varieties are isomorphic. In that case, the Klein bottle re-appears, this time floating in the tempered dual of SL 8 \mathrm{SL}_{8} .

1.0.1.
Geometric meaning of the cocycle.

The group
W_{\mathrm{aff}}(\widetilde{A}_{1})
is the infinite dihedral group

\mathrm{D}_{\infty}:=\left\langle s,t\,:\,s^{2}=t^{2}=1\right\rangle\,\simeq\,\mathbb{Z}/2\ast\mathbb{Z}/2,

which also admits the following presentation

\mathrm{D}_{\infty}=\left\langle r,s\,:\,s^{2}=1,srs=r^{-1}\right\rangle\,\simeq\,\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2.

It is instructive to compare the untwisted group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, where
\Gamma:=(\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2)\times\mathbb{Z}
,
with the twisted algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma,\mu]
with
\mu\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
a non-trivial
2
-cocycle.

In the absence of the cocycle, the induced
\mathbb{Z}/2
–action on the
character torus
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
is

(w,z)\longmapsto(w,z^{-1}),

whose fixed points give rise to one–dimensional simple modules.
After twisting, the commutation relation between the
\mathbb{Z}/2
–generator
and the
\mathbb{Z}
–direction is modified, and the involution becomes

(w,z)\longmapsto(-w,z^{-1}),

which acts freely on
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
.
Consequently all simple modules become two–dimensional, and on the
maximal compact real form
S^{1}\times S^{1}
the resulting free
orientation-reversing quotient is a Klein bottle.
In this sense, the Klein bottle appearing in the tempered dual is the
topological manifestation of the nontrivial cohomology class

[\mu]\in H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
.

In summary, we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{0},\sigma^{0})
be the depth-zero
cuspidal pair for
G^{0}
arising from quadratic and quartic field extensions of
F
.
Then the Bernstein block
\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathfrak{s}^{0}}(G^{0}(F))
is described by a twisted Hecke algebra whose primitive spectrum is the irreducible complex algebraic variety
\mathfrak{X}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times})/(w,z)\sim(-w,z^{-1})
.
Its maximal compact real form is a Klein bottle. This Klein bottle is a model of the unitary principal series of
G^{0}
attached to the cuspidal pair
(M^{0},\sigma^{0})
.

Let
G=\mathrm{SL}_{8}
and let
\mathfrak{s}
be the point in the Bernstein spectrum
\mathfrak{B}G
determined by
(M,\sigma)
.
The Bernstein varieties associated to the points
\mathfrak{s}
and
\mathfrak{s}^{0}
are isomorphic. In that case, the Klein bottle re-appears, this time floating in the tempered dual of
\mathrm{SL}_{8}
.

In both cases of
G^{0}
and
G
, a
non-trivial
cocycle in the Hecke algebra is associated with a compact connected
non-orientable
component in the tempered dual.
We are tempted to ask whether this phenomenon arises in other examples.

Proposition 7.1

Presentation.

In the group
\Gamma

(inversion on

View as a crossed product.

Let

\alpha

Remark 7.2

(Effect of the cocycle on simple modules and topology)
.

The passage from the untwisted group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
to the twisted algebra

\mathbb{C}[\Gamma,\mu^{T}]
has two decisive consequences.

First, the cocycle modifies the crossed–product relation between the
\mathbb{Z}/2
–generator
s

and the
\mathbb{Z}_{B}
–generator
Y
from
sY=Ys
to
sY=-Ys
.
As a result,
1
–dimensional representations disappear: in dimension
1
the relation

sY=-Ys
would force
Y=0
, contradicting invertibility.
Thus every simple module of the twisted algebra becomes
2
–dimensional
(Theorem
6.1
).

Second, this altered commutation relation changes the induced action of
\mathbb{Z}/2

on the character torus
\mathrm{Spec}\mathbb{C}[X^{\pm 1},Y^{\pm 1}]\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
.
In the untwisted case the involution is

(w,z)\longmapsto(w,z^{-1}),

whose fixed points
z=\pm 1
give rise precisely to the
1
–dimensional simple modules.
After twisting, the involution becomes

(w,z)\longmapsto(-w,z^{-1}),

which has no fixed points on
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
and therefore yields only

2
–dimensional simple modules.

On the compact real form
S^{1}\times S^{1}
, this change replaces the quotient
of the torus by a reflection with boundary (compatible with the presence of

1
–dimensional representations) by a free, orientation–reversing involution.
The resulting compact quotient is non–orientable and closed, hence a Klein bottle.
In this precise sense, the Klein bottle geometry is the topological manifestation
of the nontrivial cocycle
\mu^{\mathcal{T}}
.

Lemma 8.1

:

\mathbf{Kb}\subset\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{t}}(\mathrm{SL}_{8})

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
denote the reduced
C^{\ast}
-algebra of
G
. We have the Bernstein decomposition

\mathfrak{C}=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathfrak{B}G}\,\mathfrak{C}^{\mathfrak{t}}

as a
C^{\ast}
-direct sum. Define

\mathfrak{A}:=\mathfrak{C}^{\mathfrak{s}}

and let
X_{\mathrm{unr}}(M)
denote the group of unramified unitary characters of
M
.

Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{A}}
denote the spectrum of
\mathfrak{A}
, equipped with its standard Jacobson topology on the set of its primitive ideals, see
[
D
, §3.1]
.

According to
[
R
, §4.1]
, each induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{MN}^{G}\,\psi\otimes\sigma
with
\psi\in X_{\mathrm{unr}}(M^{)}
is an irreducible unitary representation of
G
, a
tempered

representation of
G
. Therefore, the map

(15)

\displaystyle X_{\mathrm{unr}}(M)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{Ind}_{MN}^{G}\,\psi\otimes\sigma

is well-defined and surjective. The map (
15
) determines a bijective map

X_{\mathrm{unr}}(M)/W^{\mathfrak{s}}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{A}}

that is to say, a bijective map

\mathbf{Kb}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{A}}.

As in the proof of Theorem
8.2
, the
C^{*}
-Plancherel Theorem now secures a homeomorphism

\widehat{\mathfrak{A}}\cong\mathbf{Kb}.

∎

The Klein bottle
\mathbf{Kb}
therefore appears as a connected compact Hausdorff subspace of the tempered dual of
G
.
