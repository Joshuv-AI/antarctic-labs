Daniela Kraus, Annika Moucha †, Oliver Roth

Abstract
. Forward iteration of holomorphic self-maps generalizes the iteration of a single function in a natural way. This framework arises in complex dynamics, for instance in the study of wandering domains and in seeking suitable extensions of the Denjoy–Wolff theorem. Here, we consider forward iteration of Blaschke products. We prove that the classes of indestructible and maximal Blaschke products are stable under forward iteration.

\sum_{n\geq 1}\big(1-D_{h}f_{n}(a)\big)<\infty,\qquad

Lemma 2.1

Let
(f_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
, and suppose the forward iterates
F_{n}:=f_{n}\circ\dots\circ f_{1}
converge to a nonconstant limit

(2.1a)

Z_{n}\subseteq Z_{n+1}\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},\qquad Z=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}Z_{n},

and

(2.1b)

\prod_{z\in Z\setminus\{0\}}|z|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{z\in Z_{n}\setminus\{0\}}|z|.

Proof.

Only (

) requires proof, since (
2.1a
) follows as in

If

Enumerate

), for each

\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}\subseteq Z_{N(k)}\setminus\{0\}

\prod_{j=1}^{k}|c_{j}|\geq\prod_{z\in Z_{N(k)}\setminus\{0\}}|z|\geq\prod_{z\in Z\setminus\{0\}}|z|\,.

This implies

\prod_{z\in Z\setminus\{0\}}|z|=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\prod_{z\in Z_{N(k)}\setminus\{0\}}|z|

and (

\prod\limits_{z\in Z_{n}\setminus\{0\}}|z|
is monotonic.

We denote the

Lemma 2.2

Let
(f_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
, and suppose the forward iterates

converge to a nonconstant limit

Assume moreover that

at

Then the sequence

is nondecreasing and eventually constant with value

.

Proof.

We first assume that
f_{n}(0)=0
for all

. Then also
F_{n}(0)=F(0)=0
.
Let

denote the order of the zero of

at

K_{n}=j_{n}\cdot K_{n-1},

so the sequence
(K_{n})
is nondecreasing.
Since

such that

. This implies

for each

is eventually constant with value

. By increasing

By Theorem

For the general case, in which the functions

do not necessarily fix the origin, we apply the previous argument to

\prod_{z\in Z_{B}(c)}|z|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{z\in Z_{B_{n}}(c)}|z|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{B_{n}(0)-B_{n}(c)}{1-\overline{B_{n}(0)}B_{n}(c)}\right|=\left|\frac{B(0)-a}{1-\overline{B(0)}a}\right|.

Step 2: Verification of (
3.1b
).

Let
K\geq 1
denote the order of the zero of
B-B(0)
at
0
. Then Lemma
2.2
shows that
B_{n}-B_{n}(0)
has a zero of order
K
at
0
for sufficiently large
n
.
Combining this with (
2.1b
) and the validity of (
3.1b
) for each
B_{n}
,
n
sufficiently large, we find

\frac{|\widehat{B}(K)|}{1-|B(0)|^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\widehat{B_{n}}(K)|}{1-|B_{n}(0)|^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{z\in Z_{B_{n}}(0)\setminus\{0\}}|z|=\prod_{z\in Z_{B}(0)\setminus\{0\}}|z|.

Hence
B
satisfies both conditions of Theorem
C
and is therefore an indestructible Blaschke product.
∎

4.
Maximal Blaschke products – Proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

We begin with a brief discussion of maximal Blaschke products and some of their basic properties, which will be needed later. For further details, see, for instance,
[
28
,
30
]
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic. We denote by
\mathscr{C}_{f}
the
critical set
of
f
, that is, the multiset of its critical points counted with multiplicities. For
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
, we write
\mathscr{C}_{f}\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{g}
if each critical point of
f
is also a critical point of
g
with at least the same multiplicity. Postcomposition with automorphisms of the disk preserves critical sets, that is,

\mathscr{C}_{T\circ f}=\mathscr{C}_{f},\qquad T\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D}).

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
be the critical set of a nonconstant function in
\mathcal{H}
. A function
F\in\mathcal{H}
is said to be
maximal for
\mathscr{C}
if
\mathscr{C}_{F}=\mathscr{C}
and the Schwarz–Pick type inequality

(4.1)

\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|F^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|F(z)|^{2}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
whose critical set contains
\mathscr{C}
.

The following properties are fundamental.

(P1)

Conformal invariance

If
F\in\mathcal{H}
is maximal for
\mathscr{C}
and
T\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, then
T\circ F
is also maximal for
\mathscr{C}
.

(P2)

Uniqueness up to automorphisms

If
F,F_{*}\in\mathcal{H}
are maximal for
\mathscr{C}
, then
F_{*}=T\circ F
for some
T\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})
; see
[
30
, Theorem 2.5]
.

(P3)

Existence

A maximal function for
\mathscr{C}
exists; see Heins
[
20
, Theorem 13.1]
and also
[
30
, Theorem 2.2(a)]
.

(P4)

Finite critical sets and finite Blaschke products

If
\mathscr{C}
is finite with
N
points, then every maximal function for
\mathscr{C}
is a finite Blaschke product of degree
N+1
. Conversely, every finite Blaschke product is maximal; see Nehari
[
33
]
and Heins
[
20
]
.

(P5)

Maximal functions are Blaschke products

Every maximal function for
\mathcal{C}
is a Blaschke product; see
[
27
, Theorem 1.2]
.

In summary, if
\mathscr{C}
is the critical set of a nonconstant holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
, then there exists a Blaschke product
B
whose critical set is precisely
\mathscr{C}
and such that

(4.2)

\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|B^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B(z)|^{2}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\mathscr{C}_{f}\supseteq\mathscr{C}
.
Such a Blaschke product is unique up to postcomposition with an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
and is called
maximal Blaschke product for
\mathscrbf{C}
. In particular, every maximal Blaschke product is maximal for its own critical set.

An immediate consequence of property
(P1)

and
(P5)
is that every maximal Blaschke product is indestructible.
Moreover, property
(P4)
characterizes the finite Blaschke products as precisely those maximal Blaschke products whose set of critical points is finite.

For our proof of Theorem
1.3
, the defining inequality (
4.2
) for a maximal Blaschke product is not, by itself, sufficient. Our approach is based on the insight that the quantity

\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}

appearing in (
4.2
) may be replaced by any conformal pseudometric
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
on
\mathbb{D}
whose curvature satisfies an appropriate upper bound and whose zero set contains the critical set of
B
. This observation, originating in Ahlfors’ extension of the Schwarz–Pick lemma
[
3
]
and developed further by Nehari
[
33
]
and Heins
[
20
]
, is the source of the additional flexibility that enables the arguments to follow. The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.3
is the following extended maximality property of maximal Blaschke products. Recall that
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
is said to be a conformal pseudometric on
\mathbb{D}
with zero set

\mathscr{C}=\{\underbrace{z_{1},\dots,z_{1}}_{m_{1}\text{-times}},\underbrace{z_{2},\dots,z_{2}}_{m_{2}\text{-times}},\dots\}

and curvature
\kappa_{\lambda}\leq-4
, if

(1)

\lambda
is a nonnegative and twice continuously differentiable function on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathscr{C}
,

(2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{z\to z_{j}}\frac{\lambda(z)}{|z-z_{j}|^{m_{j}}}<\infty
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3)

and

\kappa_{\lambda}(z):=-\frac{\Delta(\log\lambda)(z)}{\lambda(z)^{2}}\leq-4

for
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathscr{C}
.

Theorem D

(
[
27
, Cor. 1.5]
and
[
30
, Th. 1.1]
)

Let
\mathscr{C}
be the critical set of a nonconstant function in
\mathcal{H}
and
B
a maximal Blaschke product for
\mathscr{C}
.
Suppose that
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
is a conformal pseudometric on
\mathbb{D}
with zero set
\mathscr{C}
and curvature
\kappa_{\lambda}\leq-4
.
Then

\lambda(z)\leq\frac{|B^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B(z)|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Remark 4.1
.

If
f\in\mathcal{H}
, then the quantity

\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}\,|dz|

defines a conformal pseudometric on
\mathbb{D}
with zero set
\mathscr{C}_{f}
and constant curvature
-4
, that is,

\kappa_{\lambda}(z)=-4\quad\text{for }z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{f}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

In view of Remark
1.2
, we only need to prove that if
A=A_{1}\circ A_{2}
is a maximal Blaschke product, then so is
A_{2}
. Suppose
A=A_{1}\circ A_{2}
is a maximal Blaschke product.
Obviously,
A_{2}
is a nonconstant function in
\mathcal{H}
.
Therefore, in view of property
(P3)
and property
(P5)
, there is a maximal Blaschke product
B
for
\mathscr{C}_{A_{2}}
. In particular,

(4.3)

\frac{|A_{2}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|A_{2}(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|B^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B(z)|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\,.

For convenience, we use the short-hand notation

\lambda_{A}(z)=\frac{|A^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|A(z)|^{2}},\quad\lambda_{A_{2}}=\frac{|A_{2}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|A_{2}(z)|^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{B}(z)=\frac{|B^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B(z)|^{2}}\,.

Since
\mathscr{C}_{A_{2}}=\mathscr{C}_{B}
, which means that
A_{2}^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
have the same zeros (counting multiplicities), the function
z\mapsto\lambda_{B}(z)/\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)
, which is originally defined on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{A_{2}}
, has a continuous extension to
\mathbb{D}
, and
(
4.3
) shows

(4.4)

1\leq\frac{\lambda_{B}(z)}{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}<\infty\quad\text{for }z\in\mathbb{D}\,.

In order to establish that
A_{2}
is a maximal Blaschke product we aim to prove the reverse inequality
\lambda_{B}\leq\lambda_{A_{2}}
. The main idea is to introduce the auxiliary function

\sigma(z):=\lambda_{A}(z)\cdot\frac{\lambda_{B}(z)}{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}\,.

Then, in view of (
4.4
),
\sigma(z)\,|dz|
is a conformal pseudometric on
\mathbb{D}
with zero set
\mathscr{C}_{A}
. We proceed to show that
\kappa_{\sigma}\leq-4
on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{A}
.
Taking into account the definition of curvature and Remark
4.1
, we have

\Delta(\log\sigma)=\Delta(\log\lambda_{A})+\Delta(\log\lambda_{B})-\Delta(\log\lambda_{A_{2}})=4\lambda_{A}^{2}+4\lambda_{B}^{2}-4\lambda_{A_{2}}^{2}.

Therefore

(4.5)

\begin{split}\kappa_{\sigma}(z)=-\frac{\Delta(\log\sigma)(z)}{\sigma(z)^{2}}&=-4\left(\left(\frac{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}{\lambda_{B}(z)}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}{\lambda_{A}(z)}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}{\lambda_{A}(z)}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}{\lambda_{B}(z)}\right)^{2}\right)\\
&=-4\left(\left(\frac{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}{\lambda_{B}(z)}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}{\lambda_{A}(z)}\right)^{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}{\lambda_{B}(z)}\right)^{2}\right)\right)\,.\end{split}

In order to estimate
\kappa_{\sigma}
on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{A}
from above, we notice that

(4.6)

\frac{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}{\lambda_{A}(z)}\geq 1\quad\text{for }z\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathscr{C}_{A}\,.

This follows at once from the Schwarz–Pick lemma applied to
A_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
, which leads to

\lambda_{A}(z)=\frac{|A^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|A(z)|^{2}}=\frac{|A_{1}^{\prime}(A_{2}(z))|\cdot|A_{2}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|A_{1}(A_{2}(z))|^{2}}\leq\frac{|A_{2}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|A_{2}(z)|^{2}}=\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)\,,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\,.

Combining (
4.4
) in the form
1-(\lambda_{A_{2}}/\lambda_{B})^{2}\geq 0
and (
4.6
), we see that the right-hand side of (
4.5
) is bounded above by
-4
. We are therefore in a position to apply Theorem
D
to the conformal pseudometric
\sigma(z)\,|dz|
, which leads to

\lambda_{A}(z)\cdot\frac{\lambda_{B}(z)}{\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)}=\sigma(z)\leq\frac{|A^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|A(z)|^{2}}=\lambda_{A}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Thus
\lambda_{B}(z)\leq\lambda_{A_{2}}(z)
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Combining this with (
4.3
) gives

\frac{|A_{2}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|A_{2}(z)|^{2}}=\frac{|B^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B(z)|^{2}}\quad\text{for }z\in\mathbb{D}\,\,.

Therefore,
A_{2}
is maximal for
\mathcal{C}_{A_{2}}
, and hence it is a maximal Blaschke product by property
(P5)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Having Theorem
1.3
at hand, the proof of the implication
(b)

\implies

(a)
follows by the same argument as in the case of indestructible Blaschke products, see the proof of Theorem
1.1

(b)

\Longrightarrow

(a)
.

It remains to prove
(a)

\implies

(b)
. We denote by
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}_{B}
the set of critical points of
B
. We wish to show that
B
is a maximal Blaschke product for
\mathscr{C}
. In view of our preliminary discussion, see
(P3)
and
(P5)
, there exists a maximal Blaschke product
M
for
\mathscr{C}
. In particular,

(4.7)

\frac{|B^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|M^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|M(z)|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Further, denote
\mathscr{C}_{n}=\mathscr{C}_{B_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In view of Theorem
1.3

(a)

\Longrightarrow

(b)
, the function
B_{n}
is a maximal Blaschke product for
\mathscr{C}_{n}
, and clearly
\mathscr{C}_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Together, this implies by (
4.2
)

(4.8)

\frac{|M^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|M(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|B_{n}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B_{n}(z)|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
B_{n}\to B
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
and hence
B_{n}^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
, we deduce that

(4.9)

\frac{|B^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|M^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|M(z)|^{2}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|B_{n}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B_{n}(z)|^{2}}=\frac{|B^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|B(z)|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\,.

Hence

B
is a maximal Blaschke product by properties
(P1)
,
(P2)
and
(P5)
.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
A

By hypothesis, the forward iterates
F_{n}
of
(f_{n})
converge to a limit
F\in\mathcal{H}
.
Define

\lambda_{n}(z):=\frac{|F_{n}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|F_{n}(z)|^{2}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

We first assume
f_{n}(0)=0
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

By the Schwarz–Pick lemma applied to
f_{n+1}\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\lambda_{n+1}(z)=\frac{|f_{n+1}^{\prime}(F_{n}(z))|}{1-|f_{n+1}(F_{n}(z))|^{2}}\,|F_{n}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{|F_{n}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|F_{n}(z)|^{2}}=\lambda_{n}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Hence
(\lambda_{n})
is a pointwise nonincreasing sequence of nonnegative functions, and thus converges to

\frac{|F^{\prime}|}{1-|F|^{2}},

where either
F
is identically zero or
F
has a zero of order
K\in\mathbb{N}
at
z=0
.

In the latter case, since
F
is the limit of the forward iterates of
(f_{n})
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{n}^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
for all
n\geq N
, and we can write

F=H_{N}\circ F_{N}

for some nonconstant
H_{N}\in\mathcal{H}
(see Remark
2.1
), satisfying

\prod_{j=N+1}^{\infty}|f_{j}^{\prime}(0)|=|H_{N}^{\prime}(0)|\neq 0.

Consequently,

(5.1)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-|f_{j}^{\prime}(0)|)<\infty.

In the first case,
F
is identically zero, condition (
5.1
) cannot hold: it would imply that for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\prod_{j=M+1}^{\infty}|f_{j}^{\prime}(0)|\geq\varepsilon.

As in Remark
2.1
, setting
h_{M,n}:=f_{M+n}\circ\cdots\circ f_{M+1}\in\mathcal{H}
, we would have
F_{M+n}=h_{M,n}\circ F_{M}
with
|h_{M,n}^{\prime}(0)|\geq\varepsilon
for each
n\geq 1
.
This allows us to write
F=H_{M}\circ F_{M}
with
H_{M}\in\mathcal{H}
and
|H_{M}^{\prime}(0)|\geq\varepsilon
, contradicting
F_{M}\not\equiv 0
.

Finally, we indicate how to deal with the general case when
f_{n}(0)
is not necessarily
0
. Fix
a\in\mathbb{D}
and consider the sequence

(\tilde{f}_{n})
defined by (
1.2
) and its forward iterates
\tilde{F}_{n}
. Note that
\tilde{f}_{n}(0)=0
. Then
F
is nonconstant if and only if
(\tilde{F}_{n})
converges to a nonconstant limit
\tilde{F}
. In view of

\frac{1-D_{h}\tilde{f}_{n}(0)}{1-D_{h}f_{n}(a)}=\frac{1-D_{h}f_{n}(F_{n-1}(a))}{1-D_{h}f_{n}(a)},

the estimate
[
17
, (3.4)]

e^{-2\mathop{{\rm d}_{\mathbb{D}}}(a,F_{n-1}(a))}\leq\frac{1-D_{h}f_{n}(F_{n-1}(a))}{1-D_{h}f_{n}(a)}\leq e^{2\mathop{{\rm d}_{\mathbb{D}}}(a,F_{n-1}(a))},

where
\mathop{{\rm d}_{\mathbb{D}}}
denotes the hyperbolic distance,
and the assumption that
(F_{n})
converges to an element in
\mathcal{H}
,
we see that the two series
\sum(1-D_{h}f_{n}(a))
and
\sum(1-D_{h}\tilde{f}_{n}(0))
are equisummable.
Hence, the general case is reduced to the special case
f_{n}(0)=0
, and this completes the proof of Theorem
A
.
