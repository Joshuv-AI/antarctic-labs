Multi-layer feature aggregation network with residual module and attention mechanism for jaw cyst image segmentation
Authors: Huixia Zheng, Xiaoliang Jiang, Xu Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMedical image segmentation plays a critical role in diverse healthcare applications, providing essential support for early disease detection and comprehensive treatment planning. However, due to blurred boundaries, low contrast between lesions and surrounding tissues, and variations in lesion morphology and scale, existing segmentation methods still face significant challenges in enlarging the receptive field and extracting discriminative image features. To address these challenges, we propose MFA-Net, an innovative architecture specifically designed for accurate medical image segmentation. During the encoding stage, we employ convolutional kernel structures composed of 31 followed by 13 and 91 followed by 19 to construct the residual module, aiming to mitigate issues associated with the gradient vanishing and network performance degradation. In addition, we introduce a sophisticated channel-spatial attention mechanism in the last encoder to improve the capacity for feature extraction and representation. Furthermore, feature maps from adjacent encoder units are utilized to construct a multi-layer feature aggregation block, which allows features from different scales to synergistically contribute to the overall representation. Finally, to comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness and robustness of our approach, we conduct extensive experiments on two clinically significant jaw cyst datasets and the publicly available ISIC-2018 dataset. Comparative results demonstrate that MFA-Net outperforms current state-of-the-art methods across multiple evaluation metrics, achieving F1 of 0.9411, IoU of 0.9444, Mcc of 0.9406, and Jaccard of 0.8892 on the original jaw cyst dataset. Similarly, on the augmented jaw cyst dataset derived from the original jaw cyst images, MFA-Net maintains superior performance, achieving F1 of 0.9228, IoU of 0.9280, Mcc of 0.9221, and Jaccard of 0.8579. On the ISIC-2018 dataset, MFA-Net also achieves F1 of 0.8678, IoU of 0.8388, Mcc of 0.8238, and Jaccard of 0.7783. Moreover, the ablation study demonstrates the important contributions of the residual structure, channel-spatial attention mechanism, and multi-layer feature aggregation block to the overall performance of MFA-Net.Keywords: Image segmentation, jaw cyst, residual module, channel-spatial attention mechanism, multi-layer feature aggregation blockSubject terms: Image processing, Machine learning

Introduction

Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) has evolved into a widely used imaging modality in stomatology, providing detailed visualization of complex oral and maxillofacial structures. Therefore, accurate segmentation of jaw cysts in CBCT images is essential for preoperative prediction, postoperative evaluation, and treatment planning. However, owing to the large amount of information contained in medical images and the dependence on specialist expertise for manual delineation, oral jaw cyst segmentation still faces two major challenges. Firstly, the boundary between the cyst and surrounding tissues is often blurred, especially in CBCT images where the tooth root and adjacent alveolar bone may exhibit similar intensity distributions. Secondly, oral jaw cysts present diverse morphological characteristics, including variations in shape, size, and appearance, and their morphology may vary considerably during clinical diagnosis. Recently, the application of artificial intelligence to CBCT image segmentation and diagnosis has shown great potential for improving diagnostic efficiency, which has attracted increasing attention from researchers. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop a fully automated jaw cyst segmentation algorithm to significantly reduce the burden on medical specialists while improving the consistency and reliability of diagnostic results.

Classical medical image segmentation algorithms encompass a variety of approaches such as edge detection1,2, statistical shape models3,4, active contour models5,6, and Markov random fields7,8. These methods generally rely heavily on hand-crafted features and prior knowledge, which limits their adaptability and accuracy, particularly when dealing with the diversity and complexity of medical images. With the widespread application of deep learning, convolutional neural networks have demonstrated considerable potential in medical image processing tasks, including thyroid nodule segmentation9,10, retinal vessel segmentation11,12, esophageal cancer lesion segmentation13,14. Since its introduction by Ronneberger et al., U-Net15 has become a widely adopted and effective framework for medical image segmentation. The encoder of U-Net progressively downsamples the input image, thereby reducing the spatial resolution of feature maps while expanding the receptive field. Through this hierarchical downsampling process, the encoder can capture increasingly abstract and high-level semantic features at lower resolutions. Although U-Net has achieved remarkable success, its conventional convolutional blocks remain limited in capturing both broad contextual dependencies and fine-grained local details. Moreover, the direct fusion of shallow spatial features and deep semantic features may introduce redundant or irrelevant information, which can weaken feature discrimination and reduce segmentation robustness. Therefore, it is necessary to design more effective feature extraction and fusion strategies for accurate medical image segmentation.

To address these challenges, researchers have continuously explored enhancements and modifications to the U-Net framework, including advanced attention mechanisms16,17, multi-scale feature extraction modules18,19, and more sophisticated encoder-decoder structures20,21, which are better suited for capturing intricate details and broad contextual information required for accurate medical image segmentation. For example, Xie et al.22 introduced two novel components: a dense expansion convolution module and a stacked residual pooling module. These components hierarchically extract a wide range of complementary features and enhance the network’s ability to distinguish different semantic regions. Guo et al.23 proposed a hybrid framework named UCTNet to model long-range dependencies among global features, thereby learning more robust feature representations, particularly for challenging cases. Feng et al.24 utilized pyramid structures and attention mechanisms to effectively integrate detailed local features with broad global context, enabling the model to obtain more accurate and comprehensive feature representations. Che et al.25 enhanced the traditional encoder-decoder architecture by introducing a multi-scale context extraction module at each encoding stage. In addition, they designed a sophisticated decoder that emphasizes deep feature aggregation to achieve a more coherent and comprehensive representation of input information. Ding et al.26 implemented a coordinate attention block to capture more comprehensive contextual information from the focal region. They also introduced neighbor attention converters to replace conventional skip connections, thereby mitigating the negative impact of shallow features on network efficiency and segmentation performance. Xie et al.27 designed a dual-network framework composed of a feature-oriented module and a boundary-refinement module. The former focuses on capturing rich contextual representations, whereas the latter improves the accuracy of boundary localization.

Therefore, we develop an effective framework, named MFA-Net, to extract richer semantic features and improve skip connections for jaw cyst segmentation. Based on the encoder-decoder framework, MFA-Net first adopts residual modules to replace the traditional double convolutional layers in the encoding stage. This replacement strategy is designed to mitigate gradient vanishing and performance degradation commonly encountered in deep networks, thereby ensuring a more stable and efficient training process. In the last encoder, an advanced channel-spatial attention mechanism is employed to enhance feature extraction and representation by focusing on both channel-wise and spatial information, which significantly improves the network’s ability to capture complex patterns in medical images. Additionally, we construct a multi-layer feature aggregation block to facilitate the integration of features at different scales. This design enables multi-scale features to contribute synergistically to the overall representation, providing richer and more detailed information about image content. Finally, extensive experiments are conducted on two jaw cyst datasets from Quzhou People’s Hospital and the publicly available ISIC-2018 dataset. The contributions of our MFA-Net is summarized as:

Methods

In this section, we present a detailed description of the proposed MFA-Net, focusing on its overall architecture, residual module, channel-spatial attention mechanism, and multi-layer feature aggregation block.

Overview architecture of MFA-Net

Traditional U-Net architectures and their variants often suffer from inherent limitations, including restricted receptive fields, insufficient ability to model long-range dependencies, and feature interference during cross-level feature fusion. These limitations may lead to suboptimal segmentation performance, particularly in complex medical image segmentation scenarios. To address these issues, we propose MFA-Net, a multi-layer feature aggregation network based on residual modules and an attention mechanism. As shown in Fig. 1, MFA-Net is built upon an encoder-decoder framework, which consists of five encoder stages and four decoder stages. Specifically, given an input image with a size of 3256256, the encoder first extracts hierarchical features through four consecutive residual modules. The feature maps generated by these four encoder stages have resolutions of 16256256, 32128128, 646464, and 1283232. Each residual module is designed to replace the conventional double convolutional block, which helps alleviate gradient vanishing and performance degradation while improving the stability of feature learning. After each residual module, downsampling is performed to progressively reduce the spatial resolution and enlarge the receptive field. At the deepest encoding stage, the feature map is further transformed into a high-level semantic representation with a size of 2561616. To enhance the discriminative ability of this deep feature, a channel-spatial attention mechanism is introduced. This mechanism adaptively emphasizes informative channel responses and spatial regions, allowing the network to focus more effectively on cyst-related features and suppress irrelevant background information. Unlike the traditional U-Net, which directly concatenates encoder features with decoder features through skip connections, MFA-Net introduces multi-layer feature aggregation blocks into the skip paths. As illustrated in Fig. 1 encoder features from multiple levels are fed into these aggregation blocks. Each block integrates information from different encoder stages and generates an aggregated feature map that matches the corresponding decoder scale, including 1283232, 646464, 32128128, and 16256256. In this way, the skip connections provide not only shallow spatial details but also multi-scale contextual information. In the decoder, transposed convolution is employed to progressively upsample the high-level features and recover spatial resolution. At each decoding stage, the upsampled decoder feature is concatenated with the corresponding aggregated feature generated by the multi-layer feature aggregation block. The concatenated feature is then refined by traditional double convolutional layers. This decoding strategy enables the network to effectively integrate deep semantic information with shallow boundary details, thereby improving the representation of cyst regions and their blurred boundaries. Finally, a 11 convolution followed by a sigmoid activation function is applied to the final decoder output to generate the segmentation prediction.

Residual module

The feature maps generated by the encoder contain hierarchical information with different spatial resolutions. In conventional U-Net-like architectures, encoder stages usually employ stacked 33 convolutional layers to extract local features. Although such convolutional operations are effective for local feature representation, their fixed kernel size restricts the receptive field and limits the ability of the encoder to capture contextual information at multiple scales. Inspired by the architecture of InceptionNet28, we design a residual module based on asymmetric convolution and feature concatenation, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Specifically, the input feature is first fed into an asymmetric convolutional unit consisting of a 31 convolution, batch normalization, a 13 convolution, and batch normalization. This factorized convolutional structure can approximate the effect of a standard 33 convolution while reducing computational redundancy. After this operation, the extracted feature is concatenated with the shortcut feature from the input, allowing the module to preserve the original information and enhance feature reuse. The concatenated feature is then passed to the second asymmetric convolutional unit, which consists of a 91 convolution, batch normalization, a 19 convolution, and batch normalization. Compared with the previous 31 and 13 convolutions, the larger 91 and 19 kernels further expand the receptive field and help the network capture broader contextual information. The kernel size of 9 was adopted to achieve a balanced trade-off between receptive-field enlargement and computational efficiency. Jaw cyst regions in CBCT images often exhibit irregular shapes, blurred boundaries, and similar intensity distributions to adjacent anatomical structures. Therefore, relying only on small convolutional kernels may be insufficient to capture the surrounding contextual cues required for accurate boundary discrimination. Compared with standard 33 convolutions, the 91 followed by 19 convolutional structure provides a broader receptive field, enabling the network to capture more extensive contextual information. Meanwhile, compared with a standard 99 convolution, this factorized asymmetric design approximates a large-kernel receptive field with substantially fewer parameters and lower computational cost. Moreover, the sequential use of 91 and 19 convolutions allows contextual information to be modeled along both vertical and horizontal directions, rather than emphasizing only a single spatial direction. Although large asymmetric kernels may theoretically introduce excessive feature smoothing or affect small lesion details, this potential limitation is alleviated by the structure of the proposed residual module. First, the preceding 31 and 13 convolutions preserve local structural information and fine-grained details. Second, the shortcut-based concatenation retains the original input feature and intermediate feature representations, preventing small lesion-related information from being directly overwritten by large-kernel responses. Finally, the output of the large-kernel asymmetric convolutional unit is concatenated with the shortcut features, including the original input feature and the intermediate feature generated after the first concatenation. Overall, the proposed residual module integrates local feature extraction, large-receptive-field contextual modeling, and shortcut-based feature fusion. This design enables the encoder to learn more discriminative feature representations while alleviating gradient vanishing and performance degradation in deeper networks, thereby improving the segmentation performance of MFA-Net for jaw cyst regions.

Multi-layer feature aggregation block

Shallow encoder features usually contain rich spatial details and boundary information, whereas deeper encoder features provide stronger semantic representations with larger receptive fields. However, the traditional skip connection in U-Net only transfers the encoder feature at the same scale to the corresponding decoder stage, which limits the interaction among features from adjacent levels. To better integrate local details and semantic context, we introduce a multi-layer feature aggregation block into the skip connections of MFA-Net. As shown in Fig. 4, we take the multi-layer feature aggregation block-4 as an example to describe the feature alignment and fusion process. The input features of this block are obtained from residual module-3, residual module-4, and the channel-spatial attention mechanism. Their feature dimensions are 646464, 1283232, and 2561616. Since these features have different spatial resolutions and channel numbers, they are first aligned to the same size before feature fusion. Specifically, the feature from residual module-3 is downsampled from 646464 to 1283232, while the feature from the channel-spatial attention mechanism is upsampled from 2561616 to 1283232. The feature from residual module-4 is kept unchanged because it already has the target resolution of 1283232. In this process, channel alignment is performed when necessary to ensure that all feature maps have the same channel dimension before fusion. After spatial and channel alignment, each feature is passed through a squeeze and excitation module29,30 (as show in Fig. 5) to recalibrate channel-wise responses and emphasize informative features. The three aligned features are then fused by element-wise addition. This fusion strategy enables the current skip connection to incorporate detailed information from the shallower layer, semantic information from the deeper layer, and the current-level representation simultaneously. To further enrich the contextual representation, the fused feature is fed into three parallel 33 dilated convolutions with dilation rates of 1, 4, and 5. These dilated convolution branches capture contextual information at different receptive fields. The outputs of the three branches are then combined by element-wise addition and refined by a 33 convolution to generate the final aggregated feature map. This aggregated feature is subsequently used as the enhanced skip feature and concatenated with the corresponding decoder feature. Through this design, the multi-layer feature aggregation block can effectively integrate shallow boundary details, current-level structural information, and deep semantic context. Therefore, it provides more discriminative and comprehensive feature representations for the decoder, which is beneficial for segmenting jaw cyst regions with blurred boundaries and irregular shapes.

Loss function

We use a combined loss function consisting of Dice loss31 and binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss32 to optimize the proposed network. Dice loss is employed to measure the overlap between the predicted segmentation map and the ground-truth mask, which is particularly suitable for medical image segmentation tasks with imbalanced foreground and background regions. Meanwhile, BCE loss evaluates the pixel-wise classification error and encourages each pixel to be accurately classified as either lesion or background. By combining these two losses, the model can simultaneously improve region-level overlap and pixel-level prediction accuracy, thereby enhancing the segmentation performance, especially around lesion boundaries. Detailed specifications of the three datasets.

Original jaw cyst dataset: The jaw cyst segmentation dataset was collected from the Department of Stomatology at Quzhou People’s Hospital. It consists of CBCT images from 58 patients, with a total of 2,592 images. The dataset was divided into training, validation, and testing sets at a ratio of 3:1:1, resulting in 1,558 images for training, 517 images for validation, and 517 images for testing. This division provides a consistent data split for model training, parameter optimization, and performance evaluation. The segmentation task is particularly challenging because the average cyst area is approximately 400 pixels, accounting for only about 0.6% of the total image area.

Augmented jaw cyst dataset: Due to the characteristics of medical images, including anatomical variations among patients, differences in imaging devices and acquisition conditions, and the limited availability of manually annotated images, the original dataset size is relatively small, which may restrict the generalization ability of the model. To address this issue, data augmentation techniques were employed to increase the number and diversity of jaw cyst images. Through these augmentation strategies, an augmented dataset containing 7,773 images was generated. This dataset was divided into 4,673 images for training, 1,550 images for validation, and 1,550 images for testing. By increasing data diversity, the augmented dataset helps improve the robustness and generalization capability of the proposed model.

ISIC-2018 dataset: The ISIC-2018 dataset33, released by the International Skin Imaging Collaboration, is a publicly available benchmark dataset designed to promote research on automated skin lesion analysis. It contains 3,694 dermoscopic images of various skin lesions, each paired with an expert-annotated segmentation mask. In this study, the dataset was divided into 2,594 training images, 100 validation images, and 1,000 testing images.

The jaw cyst dataset from Quzhou People’s Hospital is private. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. All experimental protocols were approved by Quzhou People’s Hospital. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects and their legal guardian.

Implementation details

All models were implemented using the PyTorch framework and trained on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU with 24 GB of memory. In our experimental setup, we employed a combined loss function consisting of Dice loss and BCE loss to optimize segmentation performance. The Adam optimizer was used to update the network parameters, with an initial learning rate of 0.001. Each model was trained for 250 epochs with a batch size of 16, balancing convergence efficiency and GPU memory consumption. Considering the complexity of the proposed network and the memory limitations of the hardware, all input images were resized to 256256 pixels. Moreover, all comparative experiments were conducted under the same experimental settings and computational environment to ensure fairness. The Loss, Accuracy, and Dice curves during the training and validation stages are illustrated in Fig. 7. On the original jaw cyst dataset, both training and validation losses decrease significantly, indicating effective convergence. Although a slight spike appears in the validation loss around epoch 75, the overall downward trend suggests stable training and limited overfitting. Both training and validation accuracies approach 1.0, reflecting strong segmentation performance. The small gap between the two curves indicates good generalization ability. In addition, the Dice scores for both training and validation remain consistently high, exceeding 0.9, which demonstrates that the model can accurately segment jaw cyst regions. The slight decrease in the validation Dice curve corresponds to the temporary increase in validation loss, but the model quickly recovers in subsequent epochs. Similarly, on the augmented jaw cyst dataset, MFA-Net exhibits stable and consistent trends in the Loss, Accuracy, and Dice curves, further indicating its robustness under expanded data conditions. On the ISIC-2018 dataset, the training process is relatively more challenging, and the generalization performance is slightly weaker, mainly due to the greater complexity and visual variability of skin lesion images. After training, MFA-Net was evaluated on the test datasets, and the segmentation results are shown in Fig. 8. The visual results show that MFA-Net effectively captures complete lesion shapes and boundary contours, further validating its effectiveness in medical image segmentation tasks.

Original jaw cyst dataset

To rigorously evaluate the segmentation performance of the proposed MFA-Net on the original jaw cyst dataset, we conducted comparative experiments with several representative segmentation methods, including U-Net, FF-Net, CANet, BSNet, MDA-Net, HFENet, LMFFNet, CSCA-U-Net, ERDUnet, Transunet, TransRUPNet, and RBMDC-Net. The quantitative results are summarized in Table 3. As a widely used baseline model, U-Net achieved F1 of 0.9136, IoU of 0.9205, Mcc of 0.9141, and Jaccard of 0.8427. Compared with U-Net, several improved convolutional networks, such as FF-Net, CANet, MDA-Net, CSCA-U-Net, and ERDUnet, obtained better results, indicating that enhanced feature extraction and feature fusion strategies are beneficial for jaw cyst segmentation. Among them, MDA-Net achieved F1 of 0.9337, IoU of 0.9378, Mcc of 0.9334, and Jaccard of 0.8770, while RBMDC-Net obtained competitive results with F1 score of 0.9344, IoU of 0.9380, Mcc of 0.9340, and Jaccard of 0.8771. In contrast, the proposed MFA-Net achieved the best performance across all evaluation metrics, with F1 of 0.9411, IoU of 0.9440, Mcc of 0.9406, and Jaccard of 0.8892. Compared with the strongest competing method RBMDC-Net, MFA-Net improved the F1 score, IoU, Mcc, and Jaccard index by 0.0067, 0.0060, 0.0066, and 0.0121, respectively. These improvements demonstrate that the proposed residual module, channel-spatial attention mechanism, and multi-layer feature aggregation block can effectively enhance feature representation and contextual information integration. Therefore, MFA-Net shows superior capability in accurately segmenting jaw cyst regions with blurred boundaries and complex morphological variations.

The visualization results in Fig. 9 provide a qualitative comparison of different methods on randomly selected test samples from the original jaw cyst dataset. The results demonstrate that the proposed MFA-Net achieves more accurate segmentation results than the comparison methods, showing better consistency with the ground-truth masks and fewer false segmentation regions. Specifically, U-Net and TransRUPNet produce relatively unsatisfactory results, with obvious missegmented regions and missed cyst areas. This indicates that these methods have limited capability in handling complex anatomical structures and subtle boundaries in jaw cyst images. CANet, HFENet, ERDUnet, and TransUNet show certain improvements over U-Net, but their segmentation results are still not sufficiently accurate. These methods have difficulty delineating precise boundaries and still exhibit several missegmented regions. FF-Net, MDA-Net, and RBMDC-Net perform better in this segmentation task, particularly in preserving boundary details. However, they still show varying degrees of false segmentation and incomplete lesion delineation. In contrast, MFA-Net produces the most accurate segmentation results, with better alignment with the ground-truth masks and fewer false-positive regions. This improved performance can be attributed to the effective combination of residual modules, the channel-spatial attention mechanism, and the multi-layer feature aggregation block, which enhance feature extraction, contextual modeling, and multi-scale feature fusion. These qualitative results further demonstrate the effectiveness of MFA-Net for accurate jaw cyst segmentation.

Ablation experiments

As shown in Table 6, a series of ablation experiments were conducted on the original jaw cyst dataset to evaluate the effectiveness and computational cost of each component in MFA-Net, with U-Net used as the baseline model. Model1, corresponding to the original U-Net, achieves F1 of 0.9136, IoU of 0.9205, Mcc of 0.9141, and Jaccard of 0.8427, with 1.94M parameters and an inference speed of 874.80 FPS. After introducing only the multi-layer feature aggregation block, Model2 improves the F1 , IoU, Mcc, and Jaccard to 0.9280, 0.9328, 0.9275, and 0.8670. This indicates that multi-level feature aggregation can effectively enhance contextual representation and improve segmentation accuracy. However, due to the additional feature alignment and fusion operations, the number of parameters increases to 11.17M, and the FPS decreases to 216.30. When only the residual module is introduced, Model3 achieves F1 of 0.9244, IoU of 0.9299, Mcc of 0.9244, and Jaccard of 0.8613, while maintaining a relatively small model size of 2.18M parameters and a high inference speed of 637.37 FPS. This demonstrates that the residual module enhances feature extraction capability with limited computational overhead. Similarly, Model4, which only incorporates the channel-spatial attention mechanism, achieves F1 of 0.9272, IoU of 0.9320, Mcc of 0.9272, and Jaccard of 0.8655, with 2.06M parameters and 707.61 FPS. These results suggest that the attention mechanism can improve the discriminative representation of deep features while preserving relatively high inference efficiency. Furthermore, combining different modules leads to more substantial performance improvements. Model5, which integrates the multi-layer feature aggregation block and residual module, achieves F1 of 0.9374, IoU of 0.9409, Mcc of 0.9371, and Jaccard of 0.8831, with 11.40M parameters and 197.32 FPS. Model6, which combines the multi-layer feature aggregation block and channel-spatial attention mechanism, obtains F1 of 0.9377, IoU of 0.9410, Mcc of 0.9371, and Jaccard of 0.8831, with 11.28M parameters and 205.29 FPS. Model7, which integrates the residual module and channel-spatial attention mechanism, achieves F1 of 0.9363, IoU of 0.9400, Mcc of 0.9360, and Jaccard of 0.8813, while requiring only 2.29M parameters and reaching 549.59 FPS. These results show that the proposed modules are complementary and can jointly enhance lesion feature representation and boundary delineation. Finally, Model8, which incorporates all three components, achieves the best performance across all evaluation metrics, with F1 of 0.9411, IoU of 0.9440, Mcc of 0.9406, and Jaccard of 0.8892. Although the full model contains 11.51M parameters and achieves 173.99 FPS, it still maintains acceptable inference efficiency while obtaining the highest segmentation accuracy. These results demonstrate that the multi-layer feature aggregation block, residual module, and channel-spatial attention mechanism each contribute to the performance improvement of MFA-Net, and their combination further enhances the ability of the network to segment jaw cyst regions with irregular shapes and blurred boundaries. The selection of the loss function

To investigate the influence of different loss functions on the segmentation performance of MFA-Net, we conducted additional experiments on the original jaw cyst dataset. As shown in Table 10, Dice loss, BCE loss, and different weighted combinations of Dice loss and BCE loss were compared under the same experimental settings. The experimental results show that Dice loss achieves better performance than BCE loss, with F1 of 0.9407, IoU of 0.9438, Mcc of 0.9402, and Jaccard of 0.8887. This indicates that Dice loss is effective for jaw cyst segmentation because it directly optimizes the overlap between the predicted mask and the ground-truth mask, which is particularly suitable for segmentation tasks with imbalanced foreground and background regions. In contrast, BCE loss obtains relatively lower results, with F1 of 0.9308, IoU of 0.9350, Mcc of 0.9304, and Jaccard of 0.8715, suggesting that pixel-wise classification loss alone is insufficient for accurately segmenting small cyst regions. Among all evaluated loss settings, the combined Dice+BCE loss achieves the best performance when =0.9 and =0.1, with F1 of 0.9411, IoU of 0.9440, Mcc of 0.9406, and Jaccard of 0.8892. This result demonstrates that combining Dice loss with BCE loss can simultaneously enhance region-level overlap and pixel-level classification accuracy. Therefore, the weighted Dice+BCE loss is adopted as the final loss function for training MFA-Net.

Failed segmentation

Figure 13 presents several representative examples of segmentation failures by MFA-Net across the three evaluated datasets. In both the jaw cyst and ISIC-2018 datasets, certain lesion regions exhibit low contrast against adjacent tissues, posing a considerable challenge for accurate boundary delineation. In addition, some images are affected by imaging noise, motion-induced blur, and artifacts, which degrade feature clarity and compromise the model’s ability to perform reliable segmentation. These observed limitations suggest that although MFA-Net achieves competitive overall performance, its segmentation accuracy may decrease when image quality is degraded or when lesion structures are ambiguous. To address these challenges, future work could incorporate more advanced attention mechanisms to better emphasize subtle features and blurred boundaries. In addition, boundary-sensitive loss functions or post-processing refinement modules could be introduced to improve contour delineation. Finally, enriching the training dataset with more difficult and atypical cases may further enhance the generalization ability and robustness of MFA-Net in practical clinical applications.

Conclusion

In this study, we proposed MFA-Net, a multi-layer feature aggregation network for medical image segmentation, with a particular focus on jaw cyst segmentation in CBCT images. To address the challenges caused by blurred lesion boundaries, low contrast between cyst regions and surrounding tissues, and large variations in lesion morphology and scale, MFA-Net integrates a residual module, a channel-spatial attention mechanism, and a multi-layer feature aggregation block to enhance feature extraction and representation capability. Specifically, the residual module replaces conventional double convolutional layers in the encoding stage, enabling the network to capture both local details and broader contextual information while alleviating gradient vanishing and performance degradation. In addition, the channel-spatial attention mechanism is introduced in the final encoder layer to strengthen the discriminative representation of deep semantic features. Furthermore, the multi-layer feature aggregation block is designed to integrate features from adjacent encoder levels, allowing multi-scale information to collaboratively contribute to the final feature representation. Extensive experiments were conducted on two jaw cyst datasets and the publicly available ISIC-2018 dataset. On the original jaw cyst dataset, MFA-Net achieved F1 of 0.9411, IoU of 0.9444, Mcc of 0.9406, and Jaccard of 0.8892. On the augmented jaw cyst dataset, MFA-Net maintained strong performance, achieving F1 of 0.9228, IoU of 0.9280, Mcc of 0.9221, and Jaccard of 0.8579. In addition, on the ISIC-2018 dataset, MFA-Net achieved F1 of 0.8678, IoU of 0.8388, Mcc of 0.8238, and Jaccard of 0.7783. These results demonstrate that MFA-Net can effectively improve segmentation accuracy and robustness across different medical image segmentation tasks. Moreover, the ablation experiments further verified the effectiveness of each proposed component. The residual module improves feature extraction capability, the channel-spatial attention mechanism enhances deep feature representation, and the multi-layer feature aggregation block strengthens multi-scale feature fusion. Overall, MFA-Net provides an effective and robust segmentation framework for jaw cyst regions with irregular shapes and blurred boundaries, and it also shows promising generalization ability on other medical image segmentation datasets.
