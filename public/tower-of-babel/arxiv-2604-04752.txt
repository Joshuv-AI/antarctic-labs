DAG Projections: Reducing Distance and Flow Problems to DAGs
Bernhard Haeupler, Yonggang Jiang, Thatchaphol Saranurak

Abstract
We show that every directed graph G G with n n vertices and m m edges admits a directed acyclic graph (DAG) with m 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) m^{1+o(1)} edges, called a DAG projection , that can either ( 1 + 1 / polylog ⁡ ( n ) ) (1+1/\mathrm{polylog}(n)) -approximate distances between all pairs of vertices ( s , t ) (s,t) in G G , or n o ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{o(1)} -approximate maximum flow between all pairs of vertex subsets ( S , T ) (S,T) in G G . Previous similar results suffer a Ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \Omega(\log n) approximation factor for distances [ AHW25 , Fil25 ] and, for maximum flow, no prior result of this type is known. Our DAG projections admit m 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) m^{1+o(1)} -time constructions. Further, they admit almost-optimal parallel constructions, i.e., algorithms with m 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) m^{1+o(1)} work and m o ⁡ ( 1 ) m^{o(1)} depth, assuming the ones for approximate shortest path or maximum flow on DAGs , even when the input G G is not a DAG. DAG projections immediately transfer results on DAGs, usually simpler and more efficient, to directed graphs. As examples, we improve the state-of-the-art of ( 1 + ϵ ) (1+\epsilon) -approximate distance preservers [ HXX25 ] and single-source minimum cut [ CLL13 ] , and obtain simpler construction of ( n 1 / 3 , ϵ ) (n^{1/3},\epsilon) -hop-set [ KP22 , BW23 ] and combinatorial max flow algorithms [ BBST24 , BBL+25 ] . Finally, via DAG projections, we reduce major open problems on almost-optimal parallel algorithms for exact single-source shortest paths (SSSP) and maximum flow to easier settings: • From exact directed SSSP to exact undirected ones, • From exact directed SSSP to ( 1 + 1 / polylog ⁡ ( n ) ) (1+1/\mathrm{polylog}(n)) -approximation on DAGs, and • From exact directed maximum flow to n o ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{o(1)} -approximation on DAGs.

1.3
Transferring Results from DAGs to Directed Graphs

Lastly, our DAG projections immediately transfer algorithms on DAGs, usually simpler and more efficient, to directed graphs. Below, we list problems that we improve the state-of-the-art or obtain simpler algorithms via DAG projections.

.

1.4
Related Work

There has also been a line of work on preserving distances in
undirected
graphs by tree-like graphs with multiple copies of the original vertices.
This includes the
multi-embeddings
of Bartal and Mendel
[
BM03
]
, the

of Filtser and Le
[
FL21
]
, and

[

]

However, these works incur at least logarithmic distortion and are tailored to undirected graphs.

In contrast, our distance DAG projections apply to directed graphs and achieve

-approximation with almost-linear size.
Moreover, for our congestion DAG projection, we are not aware of prior copy-based constructions aimed at preserving congestion or flow.

, we give a formal definition of all DAG projection-related concepts, and show their applications assuming efficient construction. In
Section

5
, we will show an efficient algorithm for constructing distance DAG projections assuming SSSP oracles on DAGs. In
Section

6
, we will show an efficient algorithm for constructing congestion DAG projections assuming max flow oracles on DAGs.

2
Overview

In this section, we sketch the existence of our distance and congestion DAG projections, ignoring running time and focusing only on the idea and existence.

2.1
Distance DAG Projections

Our construction is inspired by the
(n^{o(1)},\epsilon)
-hop-sets of
[
Coh00
]
in
undirected
graphs.

Given a directed graph
G=(V,E)
with edge lengths
\ell_{G}:E\to{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 1}
, the goal is to construct a
(1+\epsilon)
-distance-preserving DAG projection
D
onto
G
.
For convenience, when we say that
D

preserves the
(s,t)
-distance
for
s,t\in V(G)
, we mean: there exist
s^{\prime},t^{\prime}\in V(D)
with
\pi(s^{\prime})=s
and
\pi(t^{\prime})=t
such that

\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)\leq\mathrm{dist}_{D}(\pi^{-1}(s),\pi^{-1}(t))\;\leq\;(1+\epsilon)\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t).

We will use the following directed low-diameter decomposition (LDD) as a subroutine.

2.1
[
BCF23
,
BNW25
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a directed graph with edge lengths and let
d
be a positive integer. There exists a random set of edges
{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
(a
low-diameter decomposition
) satisfying:

•

each SCC of
G-{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
has diameter at most
d
,

•

for every
e\in E
,
\Pr[e\in{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}]\leq s_{\mathrm{LDD}}{}\cdot\ell_{G}(e)/d
,

where
s_{\mathrm{LDD}}{}=\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)
.

Now, the construction is as follows.

Step 1 (LDD).

Compute LDDs of
G
with diameters
2^{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq O(\log n)
.
Fix one such LDD for a given
i
with parameter
d=2^{i}
, yielding an edge set
{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
as in
Lemma

2.1
. We show how to build a DAG projection that preserves
(s,t)
-distances in
G
whenever
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)
is on the order of
d/\epsilon
. The final projection
D
will be the union of the
O(\log n)
projections over all
i
, thereby handling all pairs (we assume edge lengths are polynomial on
n
).

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the family of SCCs of
G-{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
. By construction, each
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
has
\operatorname{diam}(G[C])\leq d
.
Let
\sigma=n^{o(1)}
(to be fixed later), and classify clusters by size:

•

large
if
|C|\geq n/\sigma
,

•

small
if
|C|<n/\sigma
.

Step 2 (recursive construction for small clusters).

For each small
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
, recursively construct a DAG projection
D_{C}
of
G[C]
that
(1+\epsilon)
-approximately preserves distances for
all pairs
of vertices in
G[C]
(instead of just length bounded pairs).

Step 3 (shortest-path trees for large clusters).

For each large
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
, pick an arbitrary root
r_{C}\in C
. In
G
, compute a shortest-path tree
T^{\mathrm{out}}_{C}
rooted at
r_{C}
and a reversed shortest-path tree
T^{\mathrm{in}}_{C}
rooted at
r_{C}
(i.e., every
(s,r_{C})
-path in
T^{\mathrm{in}}_{C}
is a shortest
(s,r_{C})
-path). Define
D_{C}
to be the DAG projection that combines
T^{\mathrm{out}}_{C}
and
T^{\mathrm{in}}_{C}
(as
disjoint copies
of subgraphs of
G
) by merging the common root
r_{C}
.

Step 4 (combining everything).

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{z})
be a topological ordering of the SCCs in
G-{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
. Form
D^{\prime}
by concatenating the
D_{C_{i}}
using copies of edges in
G
: for every
1\leq i<j\leq z
, every
u^{\prime}\in V(D_{C_{i}})
, and every
v^{\prime}\in V(D_{C_{j}})
, if
(\pi(u^{\prime}),\pi(v^{\prime}))\in E(G)
then add the edge
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
to
D^{\prime}
.

Finally, let
x=s_{\mathrm{LDD}}{}/\epsilon
. Create
x
copies
D^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,D^{\prime}_{x}
of
D^{\prime}
and concatenate them in the same manner: for every
1\leq i<j\leq x
,
u^{\prime}\in V(D^{\prime}_{i})
, and
v^{\prime}\in V(D^{\prime}_{j})
, if
(\pi(u^{\prime}),\pi(v^{\prime}))\in E(G)
then add
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
. The resulting DAG is
D
.

Distance preservation.

It is easy to see that

\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)\leq\mathrm{dist}_{D}\bigl(\pi^{-1}(s),\pi^{-1}(t)\bigr)

since
\pi
is a (weight-preserving) graph homomorphism (the only edges we added to
D
are in Step 3 and 4, which are copies of edges in the original graph): Every path in
D
connecting a vertex in
\pi^{-1}(s)
to a vertex in
\pi^{-1}(t)
can be mapped to a path in
G
connecting
s
to
t
with the same length. Hence the distance in
G
is at most the distance in
D
.

In what remains, we will only prove the harder direction

\mathrm{dist}_{D}\bigl(\pi^{-1}(s),\pi^{-1}(t)\bigr)\leq(1+\epsilon)\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t).

Fix
s,t\in V(G)
with
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)=d/\epsilon
. Let
p
be an
s{\to}t
path in
G
of length
\ell_{G}(p)=d/\epsilon
. By
Lemma

2.1
, in expectation there are
s_{\mathrm{LDD}}{}/\epsilon
edges of
p
lie in
{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
. Hence we can partition
p
into subpaths
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots
, each entirely contained in
G-{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
. Notice that edges connecting
p_{i}
to
p_{i+1}
is preserved in
D
according to Step 4.

Let
a,b\in V
be the endpoints of some subpath
p_{i}
. Let
p^{\prime}
be a shortest
a{\to}b
path in
G-{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
. Decompose
p^{\prime}
into maximal subpaths
p^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{y^{\prime}}
so that each
p^{\prime}_{j}
lies within a single SCC of
G-{E^{\mathrm{rem}}}
. Notice that edges connecting
p^{\prime}_{i}
to
p^{\prime}_{i+1}
is preserved in
D^{\prime}
according to Step 4.
If that SCC is a small cluster, then the endpoints’ distance is preserved by the recursive construction. Otherwise, it is a large cluster.

Let
C_{L}
be the first large cluster meeting
p
(
not
p^{\prime}
, we switch our attention to the whole path
p
) and, similarly,
C_{R}
be the last. Let
\tilde{p}
be the subpath of
p
from its first vertex in
C_{L}
to its last vertex in
C_{R}
. Denote these endpoints by
\mathrm{Start}(\tilde{p})
and
\mathrm{End}(\tilde{p})
. By the definition of
D_{C}
for a large cluster
C
,
D_{C}
contains
T^{\mathrm{in}}_{C}
followed by
T^{\mathrm{out}}_{C}
, both rooted at
r_{C}
. Thus a copy of
\mathrm{Start}(\tilde{p})
in
T^{\mathrm{in}}_{C_{L}}
reaches
r_{C_{L}}
within distance at most
d
(since
G[C_{L}]
has diameter
d
and
T^{\mathrm{in}}_{C_{L}}
is a shortest path tree), and from
r_{C_{L}}
we can reach a copy of
\mathrm{End}(\tilde{p})
within distance
\ell_{G}(\tilde{p})
plus at most
d
additional length via
T^{\mathrm{out}}_{C_{R}}
(this is because the original distance from
c_{L}
to
\mathrm{End}(\tilde{p})
is at most
d+\ell_{G}(\tilde{p})
: from
c_{L}
we can reach
\mathrm{Start}(\tilde{p})
whithin distance
d
, the we follow
\tilde{p}
to reach
\mathrm{End}(\tilde{p})
). Hence, the total distance is at most

\ell_{G}(\tilde{p})+2d,

which has an additive
O(d)
overhead. Since
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)=d/\epsilon
, this additive term is negligible and absorbed in the
(1+\Theta(\epsilon))
multiplicative guarantee.

Size of the projection.

If we write
f(n)
as the width (maximum number of copies) of the DAG projection returned by the above construction on an
n
-vertex graph, then
f(n)
can be calculated as follows.

(1) In Step 1 and Step 4, we know that
D
contains
x=\widetilde{O}\left(1/\epsilon\right)
copies of
D^{\prime}
.

(2) In Step 2, each vertex is copied
f(n/\sigma)
times in
D^{\prime}
.

(3) In Step 3, each vertex is copied
2\cdot\sigma^{2}
times in
D^{\prime}
, where the factor
2
comes from the forward and reversed shortest path trees, and the factor
\sigma^{2}
comes from the fact that there are at most
\sigma
large clusters (because each large cluster has at least
n/\sigma
vertices).

To summarize, we get the following recursive inequality:

f(n)\leq\widetilde{O}\left(1/\epsilon\right)\cdot\bigl(f(n/\sigma)+2\sigma^{2}\bigr).

Notice that
\epsilon=1/\mathrm{polylog}(n)
according to our assumption. By choosing an appropriate parameter
\sigma=n^{o(1)}
, the desired bound
f(n)=n^{o(1)}
follows.

Algorithmic Aspect.

The algorithm above runs in
m^{1+o(1)}
time; it takes near-linear time in the size of the output, which is
m^{1+o(1)}
, because we simply call LDD which can be computed using SSSP as a subroutine.

In the parallel setting, our goal is to reduce DAG projection construction to approximate SSSP on DAGs.
But, currently, the algorithm needs to call as a subroutine the
SSSP algorithm on general graphs, which does not admit efficient parallel algorithms yet.
Our strategy is to reduce SSSP on general graphs to SSSP on DAGs using our distance DAG projection itself.
This leads to the ‘chicken-and-egg’ situation: (When we are given an SSSP on DAGs oracle), we can reduce distance DAG projection to SSSP on general graphs, and we can reduce SSSP on general graphs to distance DAG projection.

Our strategy (detailed in
Section

5
) is to find a spiral recursion: We define
h
-length DAG projection that only preserves distances up to
h
, and we reduce it to
h/n^{o(1)}
-length SSSP on general graphs which computes distance up to
h/n^{o(1)}
, reducing to
h/n^{o(1)}
-length DAG projection. This spiral recursion will finally make
h
small enough so the construction is trivial.

2.2
Congestion DAG Projections

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
with edge capacities denoted by
U_{G}:E\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, we will show how to construct a
n^{o(1)}
-congestion-preserving DAG projection.

We consider
expander decomposition
as the analogue of LDD in the congestion setting. We first give some necessary definitions.

For a flow
\mathit{f}
, we represent the demand routed by
\mathit{f}
as a pair of vectors
(\bm{\mathit{\Delta}},\bm{\mathit{\nabla}})
, where
\bm{\mathit{\Delta}},\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
denote the source demands and sink demands routed by
\mathit{f}
, respectively; see
Section

3.2
for the formal definition.

We say a demand
(\bm{\mathit{\Delta}},\bm{\mathit{\nabla}})
is
\mathbf{d}
-respecting
for some function
\mathbf{d}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
if
\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v),\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v)\leq\mathbf{d}(v)
for every
v\in V
. For an edge set
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
, define

\mathrm{vol}_{E^{\prime}}(v)\;=\;\sum\{\,U_{G}(u,v)\mid(u,v)\in E^{\prime}\text{ or }(v,u)\in E^{\prime}\,\},

and for a vertex set
C\subseteq V
write
\mathrm{vol}_{E^{\prime}}\!\mid_{C}
for the restriction of function
\mathrm{vol}_{E^{\prime}}
to
C
.

2.2
Terminal Expanding.

Given a directed graph
G=(V,E)
with edge capacities and a function
\mathbf{d}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, we say
\mathbf{d}
is
\phi
-expanding
on
G
if every
\mathbf{d}
-respecting demand is routable via a flow
\mathit{f}
in
G
with congestion at most
1/\phi
.

We next formalize the hierarchical structure we will use.

2.3
Expander Hierarchy.

Given a directed graph
G=(V,E)
with edge capacities, an
\phi
-expander hierarchy
with
t
layers consists of edge sets
E_{1},\ldots,E_{t}
with
E_{1}=E
and
E_{t}=\emptyset
. Let
E_{>i}=\bigcup_{t\geq j>i}E_{j}
. For every
1\leq i<t
and every strongly connected component (SCC)
C
of
G-E_{>i}
, the function
\mathrm{vol}_{E_{i}}\!\mid_{C}
is
\phi
-expanding in
G[C]
.

By
[
BBST24
]
, such a hierarchy exists with expansion
\phi=2^{-O(\sqrt{\log n})}
and
t=\sqrt{\log n}
. In our actual efficient construction, we will instead use a weaker hierarchy (
Definition

6.4
) that admit fast construction.

Constructing the congestion DAG projection
D
.

Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{t}
be the expander hierarchy of
G
with expansion
\phi=2^{-O(\sqrt{\log n})}
and
t=\sqrt{\log n}
. The construction proceeds recursively from top to bottom using the hierarchy. We will only describe the top level.

Fix an SCC
C
of
G
. By definition,
\mathrm{vol}_{E_{t-1}}\!\mid_{C}
is
\phi
-expanding in
G[C]
. Let
D_{C}
be a congestion DAG embedding of
G[C]-E_{t-1}
obtained
recursively
. We build a congestion DAG embedding
D^{\prime}_{C}
of
G[C]
as follows:

•

Create two disjoint copies
D_{C}^{(1)}
and
D_{C}^{(2)}
of
D_{C}
, and add a dummy vertex
w_{C}
.

•

For every
v\in V(D_{C}^{(1)})
, add an edge
(v,w_{C})
with capacity
\mathrm{vol}_{E_{t-1}}(\pi(v))
.

•

For every
v\in V(D_{C}^{(2)})
, add an edge
(w_{C},v)
with capacity
\mathrm{vol}_{E_{t-1}}(\pi(v))
.

Here
\pi
denotes the projection map from the vertices of
D_{C}
to
G
.

Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{z}
be the SCCs of
G
in a topological order. We obtain
D
by concatenating the graphs
D^{\prime}_{C_{1}},\ldots,D^{\prime}_{C_{z}}
: for every
1\leq i<j\leq z
and for every
u\in V(D^{\prime}_{C_{i}})
,
v\in V(D^{\prime}_{C_{j}})
, add an edge
(u,v)
in
D
whenever
(\pi(u),\pi(v))\in E(G)
.

Size of
D
.

There are
t=O(\sqrt{\log n})
layers, and in each layer we make two copies of every vertex in the next layer. Hence, every vertex of
G
(and every dummy vertex
w_{C}
) is copied at most
2^{t}=n^{o(1)}
times overall. For edges, we only add edges
(u,v)
in
D
if
(\pi(u),\pi(v))\in E
or one of
u,v
is a dummy vertex. Since the number of vertex copies is
n^{o(1)}
, it follows that
|E(D)|\leq m^{1+o(1)}
.

Correctness.

We first show that
\mathrm{maxflow}_{G}(S,T)\leq\mathrm{maxflow}_{G^{\prime}}(\pi^{-1}(S),\pi^{-1}(T))
.
Given a flow
\mathit{f}
in
G
from
S
to
T
, we show how to route
\mathit{f}
in
D
from
\pi^{-1}(S)
to
\pi^{-1}(T)
with the same value. Because we concatenate
D^{\prime}_{C_{1}},\ldots,D^{\prime}_{C_{z}}
using all edges of
G
respecting the topological order, it suffices to show that the restriction
\mathit{f}_{C}
of
\mathit{f}
to a fixed SCC
C
can be routed in
D^{\prime}_{C}
. Consider a flow path
p
of
\mathit{f}_{C}
; we decompose it into three segments:

1.

The prefix of
p
before it uses any edge of
E_{t-1}
. This segment is routed inside
D_{C}^{(1)}
by the recursive embedding.

2.

The subsequence of
p
from its first edge
(u_{1},v_{1})\in E_{t-1}
to its last edge
(u_{2},v_{2})\in E_{t-1}
. We replace this by the two-hop path
(u^{\prime}_{1},w_{C},v^{\prime}_{2})
where
\pi(u^{\prime}_{1})=u_{1}
and
\pi(v^{\prime}_{2})=v_{2}
. Feasibility holds because the capacity of
(u^{\prime}_{1},w_{C})
is
\mathrm{vol}_{E_{t-1}}(u_{1})
, and analogously for
(w_{C},v^{\prime}_{2})
so no congestion larger than
1
is introduced when considering all flow paths of
\mathit{f}
(a feasible flow in
G
) passing through
E_{t-1}
.

3.

The suffix of
p
after its last edge in
E_{t-1}
, which is routed inside
D_{C}^{(2)}
by recursion.

Then we show that
\mathrm{maxflow}_{G^{\prime}}(\pi^{-1}(S),\pi^{-1}(T))\leq n^{o(1)}\mathrm{maxflow}_{G}(S,T)
.
Given a flow
\mathit{f}^{D}
in
D
, we map it back to
G
with congestion at most
n^{o(1)}
. For all portions of
\mathit{f}^{D}
that do not traverse dummy vertices, we use the projection
\pi
; since each original vertex has at most
n^{o(1)}
copies, this incurs at most an
n^{o(1)}
congestion blow-up.

Next, consider every subpath of flow paths of
\mathit{f}^{D}
that are incident to the dummy vertex
w_{C}
.
This induces a demand that is
n^{o(1)}\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{E_{t-1}}
-respecting in
G[C]
(the
n^{o(1)}
factor comes from the number of vertex copies), hence is routable in
G[C]
with congestion at most
1/\phi=2^{O(\sqrt{\log n})}=n^{o(1)}
. Although each
w_{C}
may itself be replicated
n^{o(1)}
times across lower layers, the resulting multiplicative factors still yield total congestion
n^{o(1)}
overall.

Algorithmic Aspect.

Excluding the time for computing the expander hierarchy in
Definition

2.3
, the construction of congestion DAG projection take near-linear time in the output-size, which is
m^{1+o(1)}
time.
To compute the expander hierarchy efficiently, we employ a weaker version as in stated
Definition

6.4
which admit simpler construction by performing expander decomposition in a bottom-up manner.

In the sequential setting, by using almost-linear time maximum flow algorithm to compute expander decomposition, this gives
m^{1+o(1)}
total time.

Unfortunately, to obtain efficient parallel reduction, this suffers from the similar bottleneck as in the distance DAG projection algorithm: Expander decomposition usually requires maximum flow on a general graph as a subroutine (which does not admit fast parallel algorithms), and we intend to reduce max flow on general graphs to max flow on DAGs.

The idea is again to design a spiral recursion (detailed in
Section

6
): (Given a max flow on DAGs oracle) We define
\delta
additive-error congestion DAG projection to tolerate some
\delta
additive error, and reduce it to
\delta\cdot n^{o(1)}
additive-error maximum flow on general graphs, which reduces to
\delta\cdot n^{o(1)}
additive-error congestion DAG projection. The additive error will be finally large enough so the problem is trivial.

3
Preliminaries

We use the following notation. For
a\in\mathbb{N}
, let
[a]=\{1,2,\ldots,a\}
. We write
\widetilde{O}\left(f\right)=f\cdot\mathrm{polylog}n
and
\hat{O}\left(f\right)=f\cdot n^{o(1)}
. Unless stated otherwise, we use
n,m
to denote the numbers of nodes and edges of the input graph, respectively. A
randomized algorithm
succeeds with high probability (w.h.p.), meaning that it fails to output the correct answer with probability at most
1/n^{c}
for some constant
c
.

We use
[E]
to denote the indicator variable of the event
E
, i.e.,
[E]=1
if
E
happens and
[E]=0
otherwise. We overload
[\cdot]
for both ranges and indicators; context will be clear. For functions with the same domain (e.g.
d_{1},d_{2}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
), we write
d_{1}\leq d_{2}
to denote
d_{1}(v)\leq d_{2}(v)
for every
v\in V
. Other relations
d_{1}=d_{2}
,
d_{1}<d_{2}
, etc. are defined similarly. For a function
d:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we write
d+a
to denote the function
(d+a)(v)=d(v)+a
for every
v\in V
. For
S\subseteq V
, we write
d(S)=\sum_{v\in S}d(v)
. We write
d\mid_{S}
to denote the function
d
restricted to
S
.

An algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}
with input size
t
in this paper may make oracle calls to another algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. We say the oracle calls are
f
-work-efficient
if the sum of all input sizes to
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
over all oracle calls is at most
f\cdot t
, and
f
-depth-efficient
if the longest dependency chain among all calls has length at most
f
.

Graph.

All graphs in this paper, unless specified otherwise, are directed graphs
G=(V,E)
possibly with edge lengths and capacities. We assume edge lengths and capacities are positive integers bounded by
W
, where
W
is a polynomial in
n
, for simplicity. We treat edge lengths and capacities as attributes of edges
e\in E
, so we use
\ell(e)
and
U(e)
to denote the length and capacity of
e
, respectively, instead of writing
G=(V,E,\ell,U)
. We write
\ell_{G}(e)
and
U_{G}(e)
if we want to stress that the length and capacity are associated with
G
. For an edge set
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
and vertex sets
S,T\subseteq V
, we write

E^{\prime}(S,T)=\{(s,t)\mid s\in S,\,t\in T,\,(s,t)\in E^{\prime}\}.

We use standard definitions of graph terminology for directed graphs, including path, distance, strongly connected components (SCCs), and shortest-path tree, which can be found in textbooks. For a path
H
, we use
\ell(H)
and
U(H)
to denote the sum of the lengths or capacities, respectively, of the edges on the path, and we use
|H|
to denote the number of edges (edges can repeat, in which case they are counted multiple times). We use

\delta^{+}(S)=\{(u,v)\mid u\in S,\,v\in V\setminus S\}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta^{-}(S)=\{(u,v)\mid v\in S,\,u\in V\setminus S\}

to denote the outgoing and incoming edge sets of
S
, respectively. We use
\mathrm{Start}(p)
and
\mathrm{End}(p)
to denote the starting vertex and the ending vertex of a path
p
.

Projection map.

A graph
G=(V,E)
can be assigned a projection map
\pi:V\to U
or
\pi:V\to U\cup\{\bot\}
(where
U
can be the vertex set of another graph). We write
\pi_{G}
to stress that
\pi_{G}
is the labeling function for
G
. We write
\pi^{-1}:U\to 2^{V}
for the inverse mapping, where
\pi^{-1}(u)=\{v\in V\mid\pi(v)=u\}
. For a set
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
, we write
\pi^{-1}(U^{\prime})=\bigcup_{u\in U^{\prime}}\pi^{-1}(u)
.

For a set
S\subseteq V
, we write

\pi(S)=\{\pi(s)\mid s\in S\}\setminus\{\bot\},

and we write

\hat{\pi}(S)=\{u\in U\mid\pi^{-1}(u)\subseteq S\}.

In words,
\pi(S)
contains vertices
u
of
G
for which some copy of
u
is in
S
, and
\hat{\pi}(S)
contains vertices
u
of
G
for which all copies of
u
are in
S
.

Remark on DAG as an input.

A DAG refers to a
directed acyclic graph
. In this paper, when we say a DAG is an input, we assume its vertex set is given in a topological order.

3.1
Distances and Shortest Paths

Graph distance.

We use
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)

Given a directed graph

Single-source shortest path (SSSP).

The single-source shortest path problem is: given a graph with edge lengths and a source

We use

-length SSSP to denote the problem of

3.2
Flows and Cuts

Cuts.

Given a directed graph
G=(V,E)
, a cut is a partition of the vertex set into two sets
(S,\bar{S})
where
\bar{S}:=V\setminus S
(when
V
is clear from the context). For convenience, we also use
S
to denote the cut
(S,\bar{S})
when there is no ambiguity. The value of a cut
(S,\bar{S})
is defined as

\mathrm{val}_{G}(S):=U_{G}(\delta^{+}(S)):=\sum_{e\in\delta^{+}(S)}U_{G}(e),

i.e., the total capacity of edges going from
S
to
\bar{S}
. We allow a cut to be
\emptyset
or
V
, in which case the value is defined to be
0
.

Flows.

Given a directed graph
G=(V,E)
, a (single-commodity) flow
\mathit{f}
is represented as a collection of paths in
G
associated with positive flow values. We call each path
p\in\mathit{f}
a
flow path
, and we denote its value by
\mathit{f}(p)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
. If
p\notin\mathit{f}
, we write
\mathit{f}(p)=0
. For an edge
e\in E
, we define

\mathit{f}(e)=\sum_{p:e\in p}\mathit{f}(p).

(That is, the flow on an edge is the sum of the flows of all paths that use that edge.) By default, we assume a flow is represented by flows on each edge, so every flow can be represented in
O(m)
space.

The
congestion
of a flow
\mathit{f}
in
G
is

\mathrm{Cong}(\mathit{f})=\max_{e\in E}\frac{\mathit{f}(e)}{U_{G}(e)}.

If
\mathrm{Cong}(\mathit{f})\leq 1
, we say
\mathit{f}
is
feasible
. Let

{\mathit{f}}^{\mathrm{last}}(u)=\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\mathit{f}(u,v)\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathit{f}}^{\mathrm{first}}(u)=\sum_{(v,u)\in E}\mathit{f}(v,u)

be the outgoing and incoming flow at a vertex
u\in V
, and let

\mathit{f}(u)={\mathit{f}}^{\mathrm{last}}(u)-{\mathit{f}}^{\mathrm{first}}(u)

be the net outgoing flow at
u
.

The following lemma shows it is possible to convert from flow path decomposition and edge representation. It is implied by Section 8 of

Demands.

A
(single-commodity) demand

The

\mathrm{val}(\bm{\mathit{\Delta}},\bm{\mathit{\nabla}})=\sum_{v\in V}\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v).

The value of a flow is
\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f})=\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{Dem}(\mathit{f}))

is an
(s,t)
-flow with value

Similarly, we say

is an
(S,T)
-flow with value

\mathit{f}

A

Let

for every

.

Volume.

Consider

A demand

is

\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v),\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v)\leq\mathrm{vol}_{F}(v)\quad\text{for every }v\in V.

The classical max-flow problem is: given a directed graph with edge capacities
G=(V,E)
and two vertices

(s,t)
-flow

In many cases, however, we need to route a demand instead of fixing the source and sink. Thus, we define the

and a super sink connected from every node

with capacity
\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v)
.

We also define the
\alpha

.

It is well known that

that routes a sub-demand of

and a cut

with inputs restricted to DAGs.

is an

and a super sink connected from every node with capacity

4.1
Graph Projections.

Let

A

together with a projection map

4.2
.

4.3
.

If

\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)\leq\mathrm{dist}_{D}(\pi^{-1}(s),\pi^{-1}(t)).

.

projects to a path of the same length in

.

Now let

\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,v)\leq\lambda h
, and let

be a shortest
(s,v)
-path in

. Partition

\lambda

such that every

(this is possible because the total length is at most
\lambda h
). By the definition of an

(1+\epsilon)
-distance-preserving DAG projection, for each

there is a corresponding path

in
D
with

\pi(\mathrm{Start}(p_{i}^{\prime}))=\mathrm{Start}(p_{i}),\quad\pi(\mathrm{End}(p_{i}^{\prime}))=\mathrm{End}(p_{i}),\quad\text{and}\quad\ell_{D}(p_{i}^{\prime})\leq(1+\epsilon)\cdot\ell_{G}(p_{i}).

Let
p_{i}^{\prime\prime}
be the copy of
p_{i}^{\prime}
in the
i
-th copy of
D
inside
D^{\prime}
. By the construction of the induced projection (we connect later copies after earlier ones when their projections match), the concatenation

is a valid path in
D^{\prime}
from some
s^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(s)
in the first copy to some
v^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(v)
in the last copy. Its length is

\ell_{D^{\prime}}(p^{\prime\prime})\leq(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i}\ell_{G}(p_{i})=(1+\epsilon)\cdot\ell_{G}(p)=(1+\epsilon)\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,v).

The oracle gives a
(1+\epsilon^{\prime})
-approximation to this distance in

, so

∎

Since exact SSSP can be reduced to approximate SSSP, we can now reduce exact SSSP to DAGs.

5.3
Analysis: Approximation

It is immediate that
D
is an DAG projection onto
G
, since

is constructed only by applying

to a sequence consisting of (1) DAG projections onto subgraphs of
G
and (2) subgraphs of
G
. Thus, it remains to show that
D
is an
h
-length
(1+\epsilon)
-distance-preserving DAG projection of
G
. By
Lemma

4.3
and
Definition

4.2
, it is enough to prove that for every
s,t\in V(G)
with
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)\leq h
,

\min\{\mathrm{dist}_{D}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\mid\pi(s^{\prime})=s,\ \pi(t^{\prime})=t\}\;\leq\;(1+\epsilon)\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t).

We prove this by induction on
|V(G)|
and on
h
. In the base case, when
|V(G)|
is constant or
h
is constant, any shortest path of length at most
h
has at most
\max(|V(G)|,h)
vertices (the path is simple and edge lengths are positive). Because we stacked
\max(|V(G)|,h)
copies of
G
, the induced DAG contains a path from the copy of
s
in the first layer to the copy of
t
in the last layer with exactly the same length, so the base case holds.

5.6
Induction.

Assume all recursive calls in
D\leftarrow\textsc{DistDAGProj}(G,\epsilon,h)
are correct. Then, with high probability, for every
s,t\in V(G)
with
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)\leq h
, we have

\min\{\mathrm{dist}_{D}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\mid\pi(s^{\prime})=s,\ \pi(t^{\prime})=t\}\;\leq\;(1+\epsilon)\cdot\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t).

Proof.

Fix
s,t\in V(G)
with
\mathrm{dist}_{G}(s,t)\leq h
. We show the inequality holds w.h.p. for this pair; a union bound over all
s,t
gives the lemma.

Let
p
be a shortest
s
–
t
path in
G
. We need the following.

5.7
.

With high probability, there exists
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
such that

1.

every cluster in
S
has diameter at most
\frac{\epsilon\cdot\ell_{G}(p)}{9\log n}
, and

2.

contains at most
50(\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon
reversed edges in
S
(see
Definition

4.8
).

Proof.

If
\ell_{G}(p)=O((\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon)
, then
|p|=O((\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon)
(edge lengths are positive), and taking the LDD with diameter
1
satisfies the claim: every cluster is a singleton, so diameter
0
, and the number of reversed edges is
O((\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon)
.

Otherwise,
\ell_{G}(p)=\omega((\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon)
. Among the
O(\log n)
diameter scales
2^{i}
we took (up to at least
h\geq\ell_{G}(p)
), there is one scale
d
in

For that scale we sampled
O(\log n)
independent LDDs. By
Definition

4.8
, the expected number of reversed edges on

in such an LDD is at most

s_{\mathrm{LDD}}\cdot\frac{\ell_{G}(p)}{d}\;\leq\;36(\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon.

By Markov’s inequality and independence across the

trials at that scale, w.h.p. one of them has at most
50(\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon
reversed edges and diameter at most
\epsilon\cdot\ell_{G}(p)/(9\log n)
.
∎

Let
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
be as in
Lemma

5.7
, and let
d\leq\epsilon\cdot\ell_{G}(p)/(9\log n)
be its diameter parameter. Let

(C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{q})

be the sequence of clusters of

p_{i}
be the subpath of
p
inside
C_{i}
. By
Lemma

5.7
, there are at most

q_{\text{rev}}:=50(\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon

reversed edges along

. For each

, define

to be the number of reversed edges on

p_{i}
, and
\mathrm{Suf}(p_{i})
the number after
p_{i}
.

Let

be the first large cluster along this sequence, and

the last large cluster (it is possible that
C_{x}=C_{y}
; the one-large-cluster case is handled similarly, so we assume

for clarity). Let

be the portion of

from the first to the last large cluster.

For every
i<x

C_{i}
is a small cluster. By the induction hypothesis,
D_{C_{i}}
contains a path

that projects to
p_{i}

\ell_{D_{C_{i}}}(p^{\prime}_{i})\leq\bigl(1+(1-\tfrac{1}{\log n})\epsilon\bigr)\cdot\ell_{G}(p_{i}).

Recall that in Step 4 we made

z:=\frac{50(\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}}{\epsilon}

D_{S}
, so we can place
p^{\prime}_{i}
in the
\mathrm{Pre}(p_{i})

. We do the symmetric construction for every

•

•

It remains to replace

in

Similarly, in

Between the two large clusters, in

We place

in

\ell_{D}(p_{\mathrm{mid}}^{\prime\prime})\leq 2\cdot\frac{\epsilon\cdot\ell_{G}(p)}{8\log n}+(1+(1-5/\log n)\epsilon)\cdot(\ell_{G}(p_{\mathrm{mid}})+2d)

\leq(1+(1-5/\log n)\epsilon)\cdot\ell_{G}(p_{\mathrm{mid}})+\frac{\epsilon\cdot\ell_{G}(p)}{2\log n}.

Finally, concatenating the replaced prefix, the middle

, and the replaced suffix gives a path in

from some

with

to some

whose length is at most

\bigl(1+(1-\tfrac{1}{\log n})\epsilon\bigr)\cdot(\ell_{G}(p)-\ell_{G}(p_{\mathrm{mid}}))\;+\;(1+(1-\tfrac{5}{\log n})\epsilon)\cdot\ell_{G}(p_{\mathrm{mid}})\;+\;\frac{\epsilon\cdot\ell_{G}(p)}{2\log n}\;\leq\;(1+\epsilon)\cdot\ell_{G}(p),

for

5.4
Analysis: Size and Complexity

Width of the DAG projection.

The width of the DAG projection is bounded as follows.

5.8
.

Suppose
D\leftarrow\textsc{DistDAGProj}(G,\epsilon,h)
where
G
has
n
vertices. Then
D
has width

f(n,\epsilon)=\bigl(\widetilde{O}\left(1/\epsilon\right)\bigr)^{\log^{\delta}n}\cdot n^{1/\log^{\delta}n}

Proof.

The base case is trivial since each vertex is duplicated only a constant number of times.

Recall the structure of
D
. The final DAG
D
is induced from two copies of
D^{\prime}
. Each
D^{\prime}
is the union (over
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
) of
D^{\prime}_{S}
. Each
D^{\prime}_{S}
is obtained from

z:=50(\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon

copies of
D_{S}
. Each
D_{S}
contains one copy of
D_{C}
for every cluster
C
in
S
.

For a cluster
C
we have two cases.

•

If
C
is a
small
cluster, then by the induction hypothesis every vertex in
C
is duplicated

f\bigl(|C|,(1-1/\log n)\epsilon\bigr)

times in
D_{C}
.

•

If
C
is a
large
cluster, then we build
D_{C}
just from the two trees, so every vertex of
G
participates in
D_{C}
at most twice.

Large clusters have size at least
n/\sigma
and are disjoint, so there are at most
\sigma
large clusters. Thus, across all large clusters, each vertex is duplicated at most
2\sigma
times. Small clusters are disjoint and have size at most
n/\sigma
, so across all small clusters, each vertex is duplicated at most

f\bigl(n/\sigma,(1-1/\log n)\epsilon\bigr)

times.

We have
O(\log^{2}n)
LDDs in
{\mathcal{S}}
(because we take
O(\log n)
scales and
O(\log n)
independent LDDs per scale). Every such LDD is blown up by a factor of
O((\log n)s_{\mathrm{LDD}}/\epsilon)
. Putting this together, we obtain the recursion

f(n,\epsilon)\;\leq\;O\bigl((\log n)^{5}/\epsilon\bigr)\cdot\bigl(f\bigl(n/\sigma,(1-1/\log n)\epsilon\bigr)+2\sigma\bigr).

Taking

\sigma=2^{\log^{1-\delta}n}

and unwinding for at most
\log^{\delta}n
levels (since
n\mapsto n/\sigma
shrinks by
2^{\log^{1-\delta}n}
each time), we get

f(n,\epsilon)=\bigl(\widetilde{O}\left(1/\epsilon\right)\bigr)^{\log^{\delta}n}\cdot n^{1/\log^{\delta}n}.

∎

Complexity.

Let
T(m,n,\epsilon,h)
be the work of
\textsc{DistDAGProj}(G,\epsilon,h)
on a graph
G
with
m
edges and
n
nodes. Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{DAG}}(m)
denote the work of one call to the DAG-SSSP oracle on a graph with
m
edges.

Step 1 runs
O(\log^{2}n)
LDD constructions. By
Lemma

4.9
and
Lemma

5.5
, this costs

\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)\text{ calls to }(1+\epsilon/\log n)\text{-}\mathsf{ApxSSSP}\text{-}\mathsf{DAG}\text{ on graphs with }O(\lambda wm)\text{ edges,}

plus

\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)\text{ recursive calls }T(m,n,\epsilon,h/\lambda),

Step 2 makes recursive calls on all small clusters. All such calls are in parallel. Since the clusters of a fixed LDD partition
V
, and we have only
O(\log^{2}n)
LDDs, we have

\sum_{C\in S,\,S\in{\mathcal{S}}}|E(G[C])|\;\leq\;O(m\log^{2}n).

So we can upper-bound Step 2 by

O(\log^{2}n)\cdot T\bigl(m,n/\sigma,(1-1/\log n)\epsilon,nW\bigr).

If these recursive calls internally make more oracle calls, we can batch them (by taking the union of the graphs and adding a super source), so the stated number of oracle calls in
Theorem

5.1
still holds.

Step 3 makes one recursive call

T\bigl(m,n,(1-10/\log n)\epsilon,h/\lambda\bigr)

and, since there are at most
\sigma
large clusters, makes
O(\sigma)
calls to the
(1+\epsilon/\log n)\text{-}\mathsf{ApxSSSP}\text{-}\mathsf{DAG}
oracle on graphs with
\hat{O}\left(m\right)
edges.

Step 4 costs time proportional to the size of the final DAG, which is
O(w\cdot m)
by
Lemma

5.8
.

Recall that
\lambda=\sigma=2^{\log^{1-\delta}n}
.
Putting these together, we get the recurrence

T(m,n,\epsilon,h)\;\leq\;\widetilde{O}\left(\,T(m,n,\epsilon,h/\lambda)\;\right)+\sum_{i}\widetilde{O}\left(\,T\bigl(m_{i},n/\sigma,(1-1/\log n)\epsilon,nW\bigr)\;\right)+{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{DAG}}(O(\lambda wm)).

Where
m_{i}
is the number of edges in the
i
-th small cluster. Observe that, since
\lambda=\sigma=2^{\log^{1-\delta}n}
, the recursion depth is at most
\log^{2\delta}n
for expanding both the
n
part and
h
part, so the parameter
\epsilon
passed down the recursion never shrinks below

(1-1/\log n)^{\log^{2\delta}n}\epsilon\;\geq\;\epsilon/2

for
n
large enough. Moreover, the total number of oracle calls is

\bigl(\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)\bigr)^{\log^{2\delta}n}=\hat{O}\left(1\right),

and each is on a graph with

\widetilde{O}\left(\lambda wm\right)=\hat{O}\left(wm\right)

edges (since
w
is a function of
n,\epsilon
and
\epsilon
stays within a constant factor of the root value). Thus the total extra work from oracle calls is

\bigl(\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)\bigr)^{\log^{2\delta}n}\cdot\lambda wm=\hat{O}\left(wm\right).

Finally, since the recursion depth is only
\log^{2\delta}n
and all work inside a level can be parallelized, the overall depth is
\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)
.

6

In this section, we provide an efficient parallel algorithm for constructing congestion DAG projections using oracle calls to only approximate max-flow/min-cut on DAGs.

See
4.17

High-Level Strategy.

Assuming an oracle for
n^{o(1)}
-approximate MFMC on DAGs, our strategy for constructing a

-congestion-preserving DAG projection is as follows.

Similar to the distance-preserving DAG projection case, the difficulty is that, if we follow the idea from the overview, then an algorithm for DAG projection requires solving max flow. Our idea is to define a relaxed notion of
(\kappa,\delta)
-congestion-preserving DAG projection as follows.

6.1
.

Let
D
be a DAG projection of
G=(V,E)
. A
(\kappa,\delta)
-congestion-preserving projection algorithm
associated with
D
takes as input either

•

a flow
\mathit{f}^{D}
in
D
with
\pi(\mathrm{Supp}(\mathrm{Dem}(\mathit{f}^{D})))\subseteq V
, and returns a flow
\mathit{f}

6.1

Although we did

Basic definitions of ordered vertex-partition.

Given

6.2
Congestion DAG Projections via Expander Hierarchy

In this section, we assume we are given an expander hierarchy and we show how to construct a congestion DAG projection.

6.5
.

There is an algorithm that takes as input

•

a directed graph
G=(V,E)
with edge capacities,

•

an oracle that constructs an expander hierarchy of
G
with
t
layers and expansion
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{t})
where
\phi_{i}=\phi
for all

12
12

12

Algorithm (constructing

Let

and

Define

(since

). For every

. Since

For every original vertex

Base layer.
When

. We let

Inductive step.
Suppose

Since

(1) Concatenating the child DAGs.

(2) Replicating to allow congestion projection.

We then add the following edges:

1.

For every

2.

For every

with

3.

Analysis.

The following lemma bounds the size of the DAG projection.

6.6
.

D
has size at most
O(2^{t}\sigma\,|E(G)|)
.

Proof.

For
i\geq 1
, each

(for

) is composed of
2\sigma_{i}
copies of
\tilde{D}_{i}(C)
, and

is the concatenation of

over the partition

of

|E(D_{i}(C))|\;\leq\;2\sigma_{i}\cdot\Bigl(|E(G[C])|+\sum_{j=1}^{z}|E(D_{i-1}(Y_{j}))|\Bigr).

Summing this recurrence up the hierarchy (and using that
\sum_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{i}}|E(G[C])|\leq|E(G)|
and
\sigma_{i}=\sigma
for only layer

\sigma_{i}=1

∎

Projection algorithm (flow).

For an edge
(u,v)\in E(G)
, define its

\sum_{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in E(D)\,:\,\pi(u^{\prime})=u,\ \pi(v^{\prime})=v}U_{D}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}).

6.7
.

For every
(u,v)\in E(G)
, the

-congestion of
(u,v)
is at most
2^{t}
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on

that, for every
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
, the

-congestion of any edge
(u,v)
is at most
2^{i}
.

For
i=0

D_{0}(C)
has no edges.

For
i\geq 1
,
\tilde{D}_{i}(C)
is a concatenation of
D_{i-1}(Y_{j})

that crosses between two different

, with its original capacity, so such edges contribute congestion

at this level. Edges internal to a

come from

, and by the induction hypothesis they contribute at most

per copy.

Now

consists of

copies of
\tilde{D}_{i}(C)
, and finally we scale down capacities by
\sigma_{i}
. Hence, for edges not in

, the total congestion is

2^{i-1}\cdot\frac{2\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}=2^{i}.

For edges in
E_{i}
, we add one between every consecutive pair of copies, so there are
2\sigma_{i}
of them, each scaled by

, giving congestion

. Taking the maximum over these two cases yields the desired bound

.
∎

Next, we need to control the demands created at dummy vertices. For every

The

-source-demands over all dummy centers

6.8

For all

For

To route the dummy-hop demands, for each

. This gives congestion at most

, and
- the expander-routed part, whose congestion is at most

6.4
Expander Decomposition via MFMC Oracle

In this section, we describe how to compute an expander decomposition given access to a congestion DAG projection. By

See
6.12

The algorithm follows the same high-level structure as Section 7 of
[

]
(a directed non-stop cut–matching framework), but we restate the pieces here for completeness.

6.14
Matching.

Let
(P,Q)
be a partition of
V

\mathrm{vol}_{M}\leq\mathbf{d}

[

]

[

]

[
FLL25
]

Applying the cut–matching game.

Correctness (expansion).

We prove by induction on the size of the instance passed to

When

Assume now that

First situation (cut–matching game finishes).

In this case we obtain

-expanding in

where

6.16

The congestion of

is at most

Proof.

Each
\mathit{f}_{i}

. In Case 1 of

Hence the number of iterations inside

using

. Since the cut–matching game makes

∎

We store all these flows. Now let a demand be given that is

-respecting and
(V)

\frac{9}{\phi\log^{2}n}\cdot O(\log n)\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\phi},

Second situation (early cut, Case 2).

Here the algorithm returns the union of two recursive calls:

(F^{\prime}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{1})=\mathsf{ED}(G[\bar{S}],\widehat{F}[\bar{S}],\phi),\qquad(F^{\prime}_{2},{\mathcal{C}}_{2})=\mathsf{ED}(G[S],\widehat{F}[S],\phi).

By the induction hypothesis,

By the definition of

(we keep a cross edge only if both corresponding self-loops survived in the two recursive calls), any

-respecting

\mathrm{vol}_{F^{\prime}_{2}}

Correctness (slack).

We now show that the total capacity of reversed edges in the final decomposition is at most
\phi\gamma\cdot U_{G}(F)

{\mathcal{C}}=(V)
and
U_{G}(E^{\mathrm{rev}}({\mathcal{C}}))=0

So assume we stopped in Case 2 on some cut

6.17
.

The cut

Proof.

The condition for Case 2 is

\displaystyle\mathrm{val}(S)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\cdot\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f})+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(P)\cdot\frac{\phi\gamma}{4\log n},

where the last inequality uses that the additive term is dominated by the main term once we plug in

and the lower bound
\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(P)\geq\epsilon\mathbf{d}^{\prime}(P)\geq\epsilon\mathbf{d}(P)/2

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{F}(S)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(S)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(P)-\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{S}\cap P)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(P)-\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(P)\cdot\frac{\phi\gamma}{4\log n}

Combining the two displays gives

\mathrm{val}(S)\leq\mathrm{vol}_{F}(S)\cdot\frac{\phi\gamma}{\log n}.

A symmetric argument, swapping

and

\mathrm{vol}_{F}(\bar{S})\geq\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(Q)/2\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{val}(S)\leq\mathrm{vol}_{F}(\bar{S})\cdot\frac{\phi\gamma}{\log n}.

∎

Therefore, whenever we split on

, the amount of capacity we “cut off” is at most

\min\bigl(\mathrm{vol}_{F}(S),\mathrm{vol}_{F}(\bar{S})\bigr)\cdot\frac{\phi\gamma}{\log n}.

So for the final decomposition

we have the recursion

U_{G}(E^{\mathrm{rev}}({\mathcal{C}}))\;\leq\;\min\bigl(\mathrm{vol}_{F}(S),\mathrm{vol}_{F}(\bar{S})\bigr)\cdot\frac{\phi\gamma}{\log n}+U_{G}(E^{\mathrm{rev}}({\mathcal{C}}_{1}))+U_{G}(E^{\mathrm{rev}}({\mathcal{C}}_{2})),

and

Correctness (size of

).

We will show that

We first show it for the first situation where the cut-matching game finishes.

.

If the cut-matching game finishes, we have
U_{G}(F^{\prime})\geq(1-O(\phi\gamma{}/\log n))\cdot U_{G}(F)
.

Proof.

We have
\tilde{\mathbf{d}}(V)\geq(1-O(\epsilon\log^{2}n))\cdot\mathbf{d}(V)

contain all edges

such that both

(u,v)\in F-F^{\prime}

such that
\tilde{\mathbf{d}}(x)<\mathbf{d}(x)/2
. We charge

to

.

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in K}\mathrm{vol}_{F}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\bigl(\mathrm{vol}_{F}(V)-\mathrm{vol}_{F^{\prime}}(V)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq O(\epsilon\log^{2}n)\cdot\mathbf{d}(V)

\displaystyle=O(\phi\gamma{}/\log^{2}n)\cdot\mathbf{d}(V),

because
\epsilon=\phi\gamma{}/\log^{3}n
. Each

can receive at most
\mathrm{vol}_{F}(x)
total charge (since we charge edges incident to

), so the total charge, which equals

O(\phi\gamma{}/\log^{2}n)\cdot\mathbf{d}(V)=O(\phi\gamma{}/\log^{2}n)\cdot U_{G}(F).

This implies

U_{G}(F^{\prime})\geq\bigl(1-O(\phi\gamma{}/\log n)\bigr)\cdot U_{G}(F),

where we relaxed
1-O(\phi\gamma{}/\log^{2}n)
to
1-O(\phi\gamma{}/\log n)
.
∎

Now we consider the second situation where the cut-matching game stops due to Case 2. We get the following recursive inequality

U_{G}(F-F^{\prime})\leq U_{G}(\hat{F}[\bar{S}]-F^{\prime}_{1})+U_{G}(\hat{F}[S]-F^{\prime}_{2}),

where recall that
\hat{F}[\bar{S}]
and
\hat{F}[S]
add self-loops so that
\mathrm{vol}_{\hat{F}[\bar{S}]}=\mathrm{vol}_{F}\mid_{\bar{S}}
and
\mathrm{vol}_{\hat{F}[S]}=\mathrm{vol}_{F}\mid_{S}
. Note that
\hat{F}[\bar{S}]+\hat{F}[S]
double-counts edges in

(edges of

with one endpoint in
S
and the other in
\bar{S}
). However, each such edge is double-counted at most once in the recursion, because once we cut along
(S,\bar{S})

Thus, unrolling the recursion and using

6.17
(which shows each cut removes only a

-fraction of the relevant volume), we get

U_{G}(F-F^{\prime})\leq O(\phi\gamma{}/\log n)\cdot U_{G}(F).

Finally, by taking

U_{G}(F^{\prime})\geq(1-\phi\gamma{})\cdot U_{G}(F).

Complexity.

In the first situation (the cut-matching game finishes), the algorithm uses
\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)
calls to

and
\widetilde{O}\left(m\right)
work and
\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)

In the second situation (the algorithm makes recursive calls to

we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{F}(S)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(P)/2\geq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{d}(P)/4,

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{F}(\bar{S})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}(P)/2\geq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{d}(P)/4.

So each time we recurse, both sides keep at least an
\epsilon
-fraction (up to constants) of the volume, and the recursion depth is
O(1/\epsilon)
. Each level of recursion takes
\widetilde{O}\left(m\right)
work and
\widetilde{O}\left(1\right)
depth in total and makes

calls only on induced subgraphs (whose vertex sets are a partition of

), so the calls to
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{MFMC}}
are

-efficient. Hence the total additional work is

and the depth is
\widetilde{O}\left(1/\epsilon\right)

6.5
MFMC on General Graphs via MFMC on DAGs and DAG Projections

The next lemma shows that such a projection algorithm reduces MFMC on general graphs to MFMC on DAGs, with both a multiplicative and an additive loss that match the DAG oracle.

6.19
.

Let
D
be a DAG projection of
G=(V,E)
with an
(\kappa,\delta)
-congestion-preserving efficient projection algorithm. Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an oracle solving
\alpha\text{-}\mathsf{ApxMFMC}\text{-}\mathsf{DAG}
. Then there is an algorithm solving
(\alpha\kappa,\alpha\delta)\text{-}\mathsf{ApxMFMC}
on
G
with complexity proportional to one efficient call to
{\mathcal{O}}
plus the projection algorithm.

Proof.

Build
D^{\prime}
from
D
exactly as in the standard super-source/super-sink reduction: for every
v\in V
, add a vertex
s_{v}
and edges
(s_{v},v^{\prime})
for every
v^{\prime}\in V(D)
with
\pi(v^{\prime})=v
, each with infinite capacity; also add a vertex
t_{v}
and edges
(v^{\prime},t_{v})
for every such
v^{\prime}
, each with infinite capacity. Then add a super source
s
and edges
(s,s_{v})
of capacity
\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v)
for every
v\in V
, and add a super sink
t
and edges
(t_{v},t)
of capacity
\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v)
for every
v\in V
. Let the resulting graph be
D^{\prime}
.

Run the oracle
{\mathcal{O}}
on
D^{\prime}
with source
s
and sink
t
, and let it return a flow
\mathit{f}^{\prime}
and a cut
S^{\prime}
satisfying

\mathrm{val}(S^{\prime})\leq\alpha\cdot\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f}^{\prime}).

Restrict
\mathit{f}^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime}
to
D
to obtain
\mathit{f}^{D}
and
S^{D}
. By construction,
\pi(\mathrm{Supp}(\mathrm{Dem}(\mathit{f}^{D})))\subseteq V
, so we can invoke the projection algorithm to get a flow
\mathit{f}
in
G
and a cut
S
in
G
such that

\mathrm{Cong}(\mathit{f})\leq\kappa\cdot\mathrm{Cong}(\mathit{f}^{D}),\qquad\mathrm{Dem}(\mathit{f})\preceq\pi(\mathrm{Dem}(\mathit{f}^{D})),\qquad\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f})\geq\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f}^{D})-\delta,

and

\mathrm{val}(S)\leq\mathrm{val}(S^{D}),\qquad\hat{\pi}(S^{D})\setminus\{\bot\}\subseteq S\subseteq\pi(S^{D}).

Since
\mathrm{Cong}(\mathit{f})\leq\kappa
, the scaled flow
\mathit{f}/\kappa
is feasible in
G
; we will output
\mathit{f}/\kappa
together with
S
.

Now we relate the cut values. As in the usual argument, for any
v\in V
for which there exists
v^{\prime}\in V(D)
with
v^{\prime}\notin S^{D}
, we must have
s_{v}\notin S^{\prime}
; otherwise, some infinite-capacity edge
(s_{v},v^{\prime})
would cross the cut. Thus
(s,s_{v})
contributes
\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v)
to
\mathrm{val}(S^{\prime})
. Symmetrically, for any
v\in V
for which there exists
v^{\prime}\in V(D)
with
v^{\prime}\in S^{D}
, we must have
t_{v}\in S^{\prime}
; otherwise, some infinite-capacity edge
(v^{\prime},t_{v})
would cross the cut, and so
(t_{v},t)
contributes
\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v)
to
\mathrm{val}(S^{\prime})
. All other contributions to
\mathrm{val}(S^{\prime})
come from edges of
D
, i.e.

\mathrm{val}(S^{D})\leq\mathrm{val}(S^{\prime})-\sum_{v\notin\hat{\pi}(S^{D})}\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v)-\sum_{v\in\pi(S^{D})}\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v).

We also have
\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f}^{D})=\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f}^{\prime})
(restriction does not reduce the flow through
s,t
in
D^{\prime}
), and the projection algorithm gives
\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f})\geq\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f}^{\prime})-\delta
. Using
\mathrm{val}(S^{\prime})\leq\alpha\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f}^{\prime})
, we obtain

\mathrm{val}(S^{D})+\sum_{v\in\pi(S^{D})}\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v)+\sum_{v\notin\hat{\pi}(S^{D})}\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v)\;\leq\;\alpha\bigl(\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f})+\delta\bigr).

Since

\hat{\pi}(S^{D})\setminus\{\bot\}\subseteq S\subseteq\pi(S^{D}),

we can replace the sums over
\pi(S^{D})
and over
V\setminus\hat{\pi}(S^{D})
by sums over
S
and
V\setminus S
, respectively, and also use
\mathrm{val}(S)\leq\mathrm{val}(S^{D})
, to get

\mathrm{val}(S)+\sum_{v\in S}\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v)+\sum_{v\notin S}\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v)\;\leq\;\alpha\bigl(\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f})+\delta\bigr).

Finally, we output the feasible flow
\mathit{f}/\kappa
and the cut
S
. Multiplying the right-hand side by
\kappa
to account for scaling gives

\mathrm{val}(S)+\sum_{v\in S}\bm{\mathit{\nabla}}(v)+\sum_{v\notin S}\bm{\mathit{\Delta}}(v)\;\leq\;\alpha\kappa\cdot\mathrm{val}(\mathit{f}/\kappa)+\alpha\delta,

so
(\mathit{f}/\kappa,S)
is an
(\alpha\kappa,\alpha\delta)\text{-}\mathsf{ApxMFMC}
pair.
∎
