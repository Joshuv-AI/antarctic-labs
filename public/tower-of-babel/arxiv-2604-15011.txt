Glauber–Lachs formula-based analysis of three-pion Bose–Einstein correlation data at 7 TeV from the LHCb Collaboration
Takuya Mizoguchi, Seiji Matsumoto, Minoru Biyajima

Abstract
We combine the Glauber–Lachs formula from quantum optics and the two-component picture for pion production to analyze data on two- and three-pion Bose–Einstein correlation at 7 TeV from the LHCb Collaboration. For the pion exchange function E 2 ​ B E_{\rm 2B} , we chose a dipole form and an inverse one-and-a-half pole form. The extensions are computed in the configuration space of 4-dimensional Euclidean space ( ξ = | 𝒓 1 − 𝒓 2 | 2 + ( t 1 − t 2 ) 2 \xi=\sqrt{|\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2}|^{2}+(t_{1}-t_{2})^{2}} ).

4
Concluding remarks

C1)
The authors of Refs.
[
1
,
17
]
proposed the following hypothesis for their formulation. For two terms in the GL formulation, the same function is assigned as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}p^{2}e^{-RQ}=(1-p_{c})^{2}e^{-RQ}\\
2p(1-p)e^{-RQ}=2(1-p_{c})p_{c}e^{-RQ}.\end{array}\right.

with the role of the second assignment being the same as that of the pure chaotic part. In other words, the effect of the coherent part
(1-p)
is not reflected in the above expression.

Therefore, their formula requires an additional correction factor
f_{c}
to compensate. Moreover, it is difficult to estimate
\sqrt{\langle\xi^{2}\rangle}
for the exponential distribution in the configuration space (see Appendix
A
).

C2)
We have analyzed data on two- and three-pion BEC data at 7 TeV from the LHCb Collaboration
[
1
]
using a theoretical formula based on quantum optics
[
4
,
5
]
as proposed in
[
10
]
. For the geometrical picture, we assumed a dipole expression in 4-dimensional space-time. The estimated
R(2\pi)
values in Table
2
are almost the same as those from the LHCb Collaboration (Table
1
).

C3)
We introduced a one-and-a-half pole for the mixed part
2p(1-p)
, which has a weaker power than that of the dipole, to improve the
\chi^{2}
values in Table
2
. Specifically,
1/(1+(R_{2}Q)^{2})^{1.5}
was introduced for the mixed term in
2p(1-p)
. For the coherent part, we assumed the half-pole
1/(1+(R_{2}Q)^{2})^{0.5}
. This gave the results shown in Table
3
. Moreover, when
R_{1}(2\pi)
and
p(2\pi)
from Table
3
were used as inputs to the working hypothesis of the LHCb Collaboration, we obtained improved
R_{2}(3\pi)
and
\chi^{2}
values that match closely with
R_{2}(2\pi)
and
\chi^{2}
in Table
1
. In conclusion, the role of the one-and-a-half pole
1/(1+(R_{2}Q)^{2})^{1.5}
seems to be important in addition to the dipole
1/(1+(R_{2}Q)^{2})^{2.0}
.

C4)
We compared two kinds of LRC’s in our analysis. The values of
{\rm LRC_{(Gauss)}}
at
Q=2
GeV are almost the normalization factor
C
’s, which are the asymptotic values. On the other hand,
{\rm LRC_{(linear)}}
’s are increasing as
Q>2
GeV.

R_{1}=1.28

\displaystyle\left.\frac{N^{(4\pi)}}{N^{\rm BG}}\right|_{\rm GL}

\displaystyle\!\![1+6(p^{2}\times{\rm D}+2p(1-p)\times{\rm FD})+8(p^{3}\times{\rm D}^{1.5}+p\times{\rm D}^{1/2}\times 3p(1-p)\times{\rm FD})

\displaystyle\!\!+6(p^{4}\times{\rm D}^{2}+2p^{2}\times{\rm D}\times 2p(1-p)\times{\rm FD})+3(p^{2}\times{\rm D}+2p(1-p)\times{\rm FD})^{2}]\times\left({\rm LRC_{(Gauss)}}\right)^{2},
